Hochschild and cotangent complexes of operadic algebras
Truong Hoang

Abstract
. We make use of the cotangent complex formalism developed by Lurie to formulate Quillen cohomology of algebras over an enriched operad. Additionally, we introduce a spectral Hochschild cohomology theory for enriched operads and algebras over them. We prove that both the Quillen and Hochschild cohomologies of algebras over an operad can be controlled by the corresponding cohomologies of the operad itself. When passing to the category of simplicial sets, we assert that both these cohomology theories for operads, as well as their associated algebras, can be calculated in the same framework of spectrum valued functors on the twisted arrow ∞ \infty -category of the operad of interest. Moreover, we provide a convenient cofiber sequence relating the Hochschild and cotangent complexes of an E n \E_{n} -space, establishing an unstable analogue of a significant result obtained by Francis and Lurie. Our strategy introduces a novel perspective, focusing solely on the intrinsic properties of the operadic twisted arrow ∞ \infty -categories.

1.
Introduction

In
[
15
]
, we established the Quillen cohomology of operads, inspired by Harpaz-Nuiten-Prasma’s framework for enriched categories (
[
12
]
). In the present paper, we further extend these ideas to construct the Quillen cohomology of algebras over an operad, as well as a spectral version of Hochschild cohomology for such objects, with the aim of providing a broader framework for classical Hochschild cohomology.

1.1.
From tangent categories to spectral cotangent complexes

Let us first recall the definitions of the classical
Hochschild cohomology
(
[
17
]
) and
Quillen cohomology
(
[
25
]
).

Let
k
be a commutative ring and let
A
be a
k
-algebra. Note that the structure of a bimodule over
A
is equivalent to that of a (left) module over
A\otimes A^{\op}
. Conceptually, the
n
-th
Hochschild cohomology group
of
A
with coefficients in an
A
-bimodule
N
is given by

\HHH^{n}(A;N):=\Ext^{n}_{A\otimes A^{\op}}(A,N).

Quillen cohomology is more universal. Let
X
be an object in a model category
\mathcal{M}
. We denote by
\Ab(\mathcal{M}_{/X})
the category of
abelian group objects
over
X
, and assume that it comes equipped with a model structure transferred along the free-forgetful adjunction
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{M}_{/X}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\Ab(\mathcal{M}_{/X}):\mathcal{U}
. Then one defines the
cotangent complex
of
X
to be
\rL_{X}:=\mathbb{L}\mathcal{F}(X)
the
derived abelianization
of
X
. Now the
n
-th Quillen cohomology group
of
X
with coefficients in some
M\in\Ab(\mathcal{M}_{/X})
is given by the formula

\HHQ^{n}(X;M):=\pi_{0}\Map^{\h}_{\Ab(\mathcal{M}_{/X})}(\rL_{X},M[n])

in which
M[n]
signifies the
n
-suspension of
M
in
\Ab(\mathcal{M}_{/X})
.

Despite the successes, there are certain constraints within Quillen’s theory (cf.
[
12
,
15
]
). In response, Lurie contributes to the further advancement of Quillen’s abelianization through the stabilization of
\infty
-categories.

Let
X
be an object living within a presentable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
. The
tangent category to
\mathcal{C}
at
X
is by definition
\mathcal{T}_{X}\mathcal{C}:=\textrm{Stab}((\mathcal{C}_{/X})_{*})
the
stabilization
of
(\mathcal{C}_{/X})_{*}
, i.e. the pointed
\infty
-category associated to the over category
\mathcal{C}_{/X}
. Due to the presentability of
\mathcal{C}
, the canonical functor
\mathcal{T}_{X}\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{/X}
admits a left adjoint called the
suspension spectrum functor
, and denoted by

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:\mathcal{C}_{/X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X}\mathcal{C}.

In light of this, the
(spectral) cotangent complex
of
X
is defined to be

\rL_{X}:=\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(X).

Now the
Quillen cohomology of

X
with coefficients in some
M\in\mathcal{T}_{X}\mathcal{C}
is the space

\HHQ^{\star}(X;M):=\Map_{\mathcal{T}_{X}\mathcal{C}}(\rL_{X},M),

and the
n
-th

Quillen cohomology group
is given by the formula

\HHQ^{n}(X;M):=\pi_{0}\Map_{\mathcal{T}_{X}\mathcal{C}}(\rL_{X},M[n]).

For more details about the above constructions, we refer the reader to
[
21
]
. Additionally, according to
[
14
,
12
]
, one may opt to work within the framework of model categories.

1.2.
Cohomologies of enriched operads and algebras over them

Suppose we are given a sufficiently nice symmetric monoidal model category
\mathcal{S}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a
C
-colored operad enriched over
\mathcal{S}
, where
C
is a fixed set of colors. As per Quillen-Lurie thinking, a cohomology theory for
\mathcal{P}
depends on only the choice of a model structure on the category containing
\mathcal{P}
. What makes the situation intriguing is that
\mathcal{P}
resides in various noteworthy categories. The following are of interest (see
\lx@sectionsign
2.1
,
2.2
):

\bullet

\mathcal{P}\in\Op(\mathcal{S})
: the category of
\mathcal{S}
-enriched operads with non-fixed sets of colors,

\bullet

\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
: the category of
C
-colored operads in
\mathcal{S}
,

\bullet

{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\in\BMod(\mathcal{P})
: the category of
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules, and

\bullet

{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
: the category of infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules.

Here
\mathcal{P}^{\sB}
(resp.
\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}
) denotes
\mathcal{P}
itself considered as an object of
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
(resp.
\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
). In each case, an important invariant specific to
\mathcal{P}
will emerge, as discussed below.

We endow
\Op(\mathcal{S})
with the
Dwyer-Kan model structure
, while the others are all equipped with the standard model structure transferred from that on
\mathcal{S}
(cf.
\lx@sectionsign
2.4
). First, we refer to the Quillen cohomology of
\mathcal{P}\in\Op(\mathcal{S})
as its
(proper) Quillen cohomology
, while the corresponding cohomology of
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
is called its
reduced Quillen cohomology
.

Next, due to the fact that infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules are exactly the
linearization
of the usual notion of
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules, it is natural to let the Hochschild complex of the operad
\mathcal{P}
be classified by
\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
. Nonetheless, note that the category
\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
remains unstable unless
\mathcal{S}
is stable. Therefore, to assure that the resulting cohomology theory is
abelian
(i.e. the cohomology groups are all abelian), we will let the cotangent complex of
\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}
, denoted by
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
, represent the
Hochschild complex of the operad

\mathcal{P}
. More precisely, the Hochschild cohomology of
\mathcal{P}
with coefficients in some
M\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
is the space

\HHH^{\star}(\mathcal{P};M):=\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})}(\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}},M).

The above definition has full generality because when the category
\mathcal{S}
is stable then we obtain an equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})\simeq\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
, which identifies
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
with
{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
itself.

Now let
A\in\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
be a
\mathcal{P}
-algebra. Note that
A
may also be regarded as an object of
\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
the category of
A
-
modules over

\mathcal{P}
(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
). We will write
A^{\me}
to denote
A
itself in this role. First, since we are mainly interested in the
\mathcal{P}
-algebra structure on
A
, it is obvious to exhibit the Quillen cohomology of
A\in\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
as its
(proper) Quillen cohomology
. Moreover, according to the logic as in the above paragraph, we will exhibit
\rL_{A^{\me}}\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
, i.e. the cotangent complex of
A^{\me}
, as the
Hochschild complex of the

\mathcal{P}
-
algebra

A
. Now the Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in some
N\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
is the space

\HHH^{\star}(A;N):=\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}}(\rL_{A^{\me}},N).

1.3.
Main statements

We shall now summarize our main results, divided into three parts as follows.

1.3.1.
Formulations and comparison theorem

It is noteworthy that there exists a natural passage between infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules (resp.
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules) and
A
-modules over
\mathcal{P}
(resp.
\mathcal{P}
-algebras) performed by the adjunction
(-)\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\dashv\textrm{End}_{A,-}
(see
\lx@sectionsign
5.2
). Moreover, there is a commutative square of adjunctions between tangent categories:

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\BMod(\mathcal{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textrm{End}_{A,-}^{\st}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\downvdash}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdash}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textrm{End}_{A,-}^{\st}}

in which the vertical pairs are all induced by the adjunction
(-)\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\dashv\textrm{End}_{A,-}
(see Notation
5.23
), and the horizontal pairs are given by the induction-restriction adjunctions.

Under some mild assumptions on the triple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{P},A)
, we obtain the following results.

In Proposition
5.25
, we establish the weak equivalences

\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\rL_{A}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\rL_{A^{\me}}

in
\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
respectively, where
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}
denotes the cotangent complex of
\mathcal{P}
as a bimodule over itself. These lead to the following
comparison theorem
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
5.2
,
5.3
) Suppose given a fibrant object
N\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
.

(i) There is a natural weak equivalence

\HHQ^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\Omega\HHQ^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N})

between the Quillen cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
N
and the loop space of Quillen cohomology of
\mathcal{P}
with coefficients in
\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
.

(ii) There is a natural weak equivalence

\HHH^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\HHH^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N})

between the Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
N
and Hochschild cohomology of
\mathcal{P}
with coefficients in
\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N}
.

Remark 1.1
.

When
\mathcal{S}
is stable, the cotangent complex
\rL_{A}\in\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
can be represented as an
A
-module over
\mathcal{P}
. We then obtain a weak equivalence in
\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
of the form

\overline{\rL}_{\mathcal{P}}\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\rL_{A}

where
\overline{\rL}_{\mathcal{P}}\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
is given at each
C
-sequence
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m};c)
by the formula

\overline{\rL}_{\mathcal{P}}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m};c)=\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m};c)\otimes\hocolim_{n}\Omega^{n}[\,(\sS^{n})^{\otimes m}\times_{1_{\mathcal{S}}}^{\h}0\,].

Here
\sS^{n}
refers to the
n
-sphere
in
\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, we obtain weak equivalences, similar to those above:

\displaystyle\HHQ^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\Omega\HHQ^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}_{A,N}),\;\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\HHH^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\HHH^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}_{A,N})

with the only difference being that
N
is now an object of
\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
.

1.3.2.
Simplicial operads and algebras over them

We will now assume that
\mathcal{S}
is the category
\Set_{\Delta}
of simplicial sets, and that
\mathcal{P}
is a fibrant and
\Sigma
-cofibrant
C
-colored operad in
\Set_{\Delta}
, for some fixed set of colors
C
. Recall from
[
15
]
that there is a chain of equivalences of
\infty
-categories

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}\Op(\Set_{\Delta})_{\infty}\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}\Op_{C}(\Set_{\Delta})_{\infty}\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\infty}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{\infty}\simeq\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)

where
\Tw(\mathcal{P})
signifies the
twisted arrow
\infty
-
category
of
\mathcal{P}
and
\Sp
denotes the
\infty
-
category of spectra
.

Due to the above, both Quillen and Hochschild cohomologies of
\mathcal{P}
can be calculated in the framework of functors
\Tw(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\Sp
. In
[
15
]
, we have proved that
\rL_{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}\Op(\Set_{\Delta})_{\infty}
is identified with
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}[-1]\in\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)
where the functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}:\Tw(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\Sp
takes any operation
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
of arity
m
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}(\mu)=\mathbb{S}^{\oplus m}
, and while the Hochschild complex
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{\infty}
is identified with
\overline{\mathbb{S}}:\Tw(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\Sp
the constant functor on the
sphere spectrum
(see Proposition
6.13
). We further obtain the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
6.4
) Suppose that
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\Set_{\Delta})
is fibrant and
\Sigma
-cofibrant. Let
A
be a cofibrant
\mathcal{P}
-algebra and let
N\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
be a fibrant object.

(i) The Quillen cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
N
is computed by

\HHQ^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\Omega\HHQ^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N})\simeq\Map_{\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}},\widetilde{\textrm{End}}^{\st}_{A,N})

where
\widetilde{\textrm{End}}^{\st}_{A,N}:\Tw(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\Sp
is the functor naturally associated with the pair
(A,N)
(see Construction
6.14
).

(ii) The Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
N
is computed by

\HHH^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\HHH^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N})\simeq\Map_{\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)}(\overline{\mathbb{S}},\widetilde{\textrm{End}}^{\st}_{A,N}).

In particular, the
n
-th Hochschild cohomology group is given by

\HHH^{n}(A;N)\cong\pi_{-n}\,\lim\widetilde{\textrm{End}}^{\st}_{A,N}.

1.3.3.
Quillen principle for
\E_{n}
-spaces

While Quillen cohomology plays a central role in the study of deformation theory and obstruction theory, its actual computation is far from straightforward. In addressing this complexity, one approach is to establish a connection between Quillen cohomology and the notably more accessible Hochschild cohomology. We will say
that a given object of interest is subject to the
Quillen principle
if there exists a convenient (co)fiber
sequence relating the cotangent and Hochschild complexes of that object.

Let
A
be an
\E_{n}
-algebra in
\Set_{\Delta}
. We let
\Free_{A}(*)
denote the free
A
-module over
\E_{n}
generated by a singleton, and let
\eta_{A}:\Free_{A}(*)\longrightarrow A^{\me}
be the map in
\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
classified by the unit of
A
. We have a canonical map
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})\longrightarrow\rL_{A^{\me}}
where
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})
refers to the image of
\eta_{A}
through the functor

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:(\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A})_{/A^{\me}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
6.6
,
6.31
) Suppose that
A
is a cofibrant
\E_{n}
-algebra. Then there is a cofiber sequence in
\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
of the form

(1.2)

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})\longrightarrow\rL_{A^{\me}}\longrightarrow\rL_{A}[n]

where we use the same notation for the derived image of
\rL_{A}\in\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\E_{n}}(\Set_{\Delta})
under the equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\E_{n}}(\Set_{\Delta})\simeq\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
. Consequently, for a given fibrant object
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
, we obtain a fiber sequence connecting the two cohomologies

\Omega^{n}\HHQ^{\star}(A;\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow\HHH^{\star}(A;\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow|\mathcal{N}|

in which
|\mathcal{N}|:=\{1_{A}\}\times^{\h}_{A}\mathcal{N}_{0,0}
, i.e. the homotopy fiber over
1_{A}
of the structure map
\mathcal{N}_{0,0}\longrightarrow A^{\me}
.

This assertion provides an unstable analogue of a result obtained by Francis and Lurie, which establishes the Quillen principle for
\E_{n}
-algebras in stable categories, such as chain complexes or symmetric spectra (cf.
[
9
, Theorem 2.26]
and
[
21
, Theorem 7.3.5.1]
).

2.
Background and conventions

This section is devoted to some preliminaries relevant to the theory of enriched operads and their associated modules, as well as the standard homotopy theories of such objects. We also revisit the tangent category formalism, from which we may obtain the central concepts of the paper including the spectral cotangent complex and Quillen cohomology.

2.1.
Enriched operads

Let
(\mathcal{S},\otimes,1_{\mathcal{S}})
be a
symmetric monoidal category
whose underlying category
\mathcal{S}
is cocomplete and whose monoidal product
-\otimes-
distributes over colimits. We will refer to
\mathcal{S}
as the
base category
.

Let
C\in\Sets
be a given set, regarded as the
set of colors
. By definition, a
symmetric

C
-
collection
in
\mathcal{S}
consists of a collection

M=\{M(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\,|\,c_{i},c\in C,n\geqslant 0\}

of objects in
\mathcal{S}
such that for each sequence
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)
and each permutation
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
, there is a map of the form

\sigma^{*}:M(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\longrightarrow M(c_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,c_{\sigma(n)};c).

Such maps are required to establish a
right
\Sigma_{n}
-action
, meaning that for every
\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma_{n}
, one has
\sigma^{*}\tau^{*}=(\tau\sigma)^{*}
and
\iota_{n}^{*}=\Id
where
\iota_{n}\in\Sigma_{n}
denotes the trivial permutation. We let
\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})
denote the
category of symmetric
C
-collections in
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 2.1
.

When
C
is a singleton then a symmetric
C
-collection will be called a
\Sigma_{*}
-
object
. More concretely, a
\Sigma_{*}
-object
M
consists of a collection
\{M(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
such that each
M(n)
is a right
\Sigma_{n}
-object in
\mathcal{S}
. The
category of
\Sigma_{*}
-objects in

\mathcal{S}
will be denoted by
\Sigma_{*}(\mathcal{S})
.

The category
\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})
admits a monoidal structure given by the
composite product

-\circ-:\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\times\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\longrightarrow\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})

(see, e.g.,
[
13
]
). We will denote by
\mathcal{I}_{C}
the monoidal unit which agrees with
\emptyset_{\mathcal{S}}
(i.e. the initial object of
\mathcal{S}
) on all the levels except that
\mathcal{I}_{C}(c;c)=1_{\mathcal{S}}
for every
c\in C
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
symmetric

C
-
colored operad
in
\mathcal{S}
is a monoid in the monoidal category
(\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S}),-\circ-,\mathcal{I}_{C})
. We will write
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
to denote the
category of symmetric
C
-colored operads in

\mathcal{S}
. When
C
is a singleton, we will write
\Op_{*}(\mathcal{S})
for the corresponding category.

Convention 2.3
.

All operads considered in this paper are assumed to be symmetric. Henceforth, we will omit the term
``
symmetric” when referring to an object within
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
(or
\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})
).

Remark 2.4
.

An
\mathcal{S}
-enriched category
\mathcal{C}
can be identified with an
\mathcal{S}
-enriched operad concentrated in arity
1
, i.e. defined by letting
\mathcal{C}(c;d):=\Map_{\mathcal{C}}(c,d)
. On the other hand, every
\mathcal{S}
-enriched operad
\mathcal{P}
has an
underlying category

\mathcal{P}_{1}
such that
\Map_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(c,d):=\mathcal{P}(c;d)
.

The following provides two typical examples of operads.

Definition 2.5
.

(1) The
associative operad

\Ass\in\Op_{*}(\mathcal{S})
is defined by, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, letting
\Ass(n)=\underset{\Sigma_{n}}{\bigsqcup}1_{\mathcal{S}}
with the symmetric-action being induced by the right regular representation of
\Sigma_{n}
and with the composition induced by the concatenation of linear orders.

(2) The
commutative operad

\Com\in\Op_{*}(\mathcal{S})
is defined by letting
\Com(n)=1_{\mathcal{S}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, endowed with the trivial action by the symmetric groups, and such that the composition is induced by the canonical isomorphisms
1_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\cdots\otimes 1_{\mathcal{S}}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}1_{\mathcal{S}}
.

One can organize all the colored operads in
\mathcal{S}
, for arbitrary sets of colors, into a single category as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

The
category of
\mathcal{S}
-enriched operads
, denoted by
\Op(\mathcal{S})
, is the Grothendieck construction

\Op(\mathcal{S}):=\int_{C\in\Sets}\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})

where for each map
\alpha:C\longrightarrow D
of sets, the corresponding functor
\alpha^{*}:\Op_{D}(\mathcal{S})\longrightarrow\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
is defined by sending
\mathcal{Q}\in\Op_{D}(\mathcal{S})
to
\alpha^{*}\mathcal{Q}
with

\alpha^{*}\mathcal{Q}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c):=\mathcal{Q}(\alpha(c_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(c_{n});\alpha(c)).

More precisely, an object of
\Op(\mathcal{S})
is the choice of a pair
(C,\mathcal{P})
with
C\in\Sets
and
\mathcal{P}\in\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
. Moreover, a morphism
(C,\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow(D,\mathcal{Q})
consists of a map
\alpha:C\longrightarrow D
of sets and a map
f:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\alpha^{*}\mathcal{Q}
in
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
.

2.2.
Modules over an operad

Let
(\mathcal{S},\otimes,1_{\mathcal{S}})
be as in the previous subsection, and let
\mathcal{P}
be a
C
-colored operad in
\mathcal{S}
, for some fixed set of colors
C
.

Various types of operadic modules come naturally when considering
\mathcal{P}
as a monoid in the monoidal category of
C
-collections, as expressed in the following.

Definition 2.7
.

(i) A
left
\mathcal{P}
-module
is a
C
-collection
M
that is equipped with a left
\mathcal{P}
-action
\mathcal{P}\circ M\longrightarrow M
whose data consist of
\Sigma_{*}
-equivariant maps of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes M(d_{1,1},\cdots,d_{1,k_{1}};c_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes M(d_{n,1},\cdots,d_{n,k_{n}};c_{n})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M(d_{1,1},\cdots,d_{1,k_{1}},\cdots,d_{n,1},\cdots,d_{n,k_{n}};c).

These are required to satisfy the classical axioms of associativity and unitality for left modules.

(ii) Dually, a
right
\mathcal{P}
-module
is a
C
-collection
M
equipped with a right
\mathcal{P}
-action
M\circ\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow M
whose data consist of
\Sigma_{*}
-equivariant maps of the form

\displaystyle M(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\mathcal{P}(d_{1,1},\cdots,d_{1,k_{1}};c_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{P}(d_{n,1},\cdots,d_{n,k_{n}};c_{n})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M(d_{1,1},\cdots,d_{1,k_{1}},\cdots,d_{n,1},\cdots,d_{n,k_{n}};c)

satisfying the classical axioms of associativity and unitality for right modules.

(iii) A
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule
is a
C
-collection
M
equipped with both a left and a right
\mathcal{P}
-module structure, which must satisfy the essential axiom of compatibility.

We will write
\LMod(\mathcal{P})
,
\RMod(\mathcal{P})
and
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
respectively for the categories of
left

\mathcal{P}
-
modules
,
right

\mathcal{P}
-
modules
and
\mathcal{P}
-
bimodules
.

The structure of right
\mathcal{P}
-modules is
linear
in the sense that the forgetful functor

\RMod(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})

preserves all colimits. However, an analogue in general does not hold for left
\mathcal{P}
-modules, and thus, does not hold for
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules as well. This promotes the development of
infinitesimal left modules (bimodules)
over an operad (cf.
[
22
]
), essentially coming as the linearization of the former.

We write
-\circ_{(1)}-:\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\times\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\longrightarrow\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})
for the
infinitesimal composite product
that is characterized as the
right linearization
of the composite product
-\circ-
(cf.
[
20
,
15
]
).

Definition 2.8
.

(i) An
infinitesimal left

\mathcal{P}
-
module
is a
C
-collection
M
endowed with a map
\mathcal{P}\circ_{(1)}M\longrightarrow M
whose data consist of
\Sigma_{*}
-equivariant maps of the form

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes M(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};c_{i})\longrightarrow M(c_{1},\cdots,c_{i-1},d_{1},\cdots,d_{m},c_{i+1},\cdots,c_{n};c).

These maps must satisfy the classical axioms of associativity and unitality for left modules.

(ii) An
infinitesimal

\mathcal{P}
-
bimodule
is a
C
-collection
M
equipped with both an infinitesimal left
\mathcal{P}
-module and a right
\mathcal{P}
-module structure. Moreover, these two structures are subject to the usual compatibility axiom. (See
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.2]
for more details).

We denote by
\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
(resp.
\ILMod(\mathcal{P})
) the
category of infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules
(resp.
infinitesimal left
\mathcal{P}
-modules
). We will revisit these two notions in
\lx@sectionsign
4
.

Remark 2.9
.

It is noteworthy that each of the categories
\LMod(\mathcal{P})
,
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
,
\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
and
\RMod(\mathcal{P})
can be represented as the category of algebras over an enriched operad (or
\mathcal{S}
-valued functors on an enriched category). For more details about these, we refer the reader to
[
15
,
2
,
8
]
. Moreover, according to
[
11
]
, there exists a discrete operad, which we denote by
\textbf{O}_{C}
, that encodes the category of
C
-colored operads in
\mathcal{S}
.

One of the main interests in the study of operads is the notion of algebras over an operad.

Definition 2.10
.

A
\mathcal{P}
-
algebra
is a left
\mathcal{P}
-module concentrated in level
0
. More explicitly, a
\mathcal{P}
-algebra
A
consists of a collection
\{A(c)\}_{c\in C}
of objects in
\mathcal{S}
equipped, for each tuple
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)
, with an action map

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes A(c_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(c_{n})\longrightarrow A(c).

These maps must satisfy the essential axioms of associativity, unitality and equivariance. We denote by
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
the
category of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras
.

Example 2.11
.

In line with the terminologies, the operad
\Ass
(resp.
\Com
) encodes the category of associative monoids (resp. commutative monoids) in
\mathcal{S}
.

Next, the notion of modules over a
\mathcal{P}
-algebra
A
essentially provides
representations
for
A
.

Definition 2.12
.

An
A
-module over

\mathcal{P}
is a collection
M=\{M(c)\}_{c\in C}
of objects in
\mathcal{S}
endowed, for each tuple
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)
and
k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, with an action map of the form

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\;\bigotimes_{i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\setminus\{k\}}\;A(c_{i})\otimes M(c_{k})\longrightarrow M(c).

These maps are required to satisfy the essential axioms of associativity, unitality and equivariance. The
category of
A
-modules over

\mathcal{P}
will be denoted by
\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
.

Remark 2.13
.

As in
[
5
]
,
\mathcal{P}_{A}
denotes the
enveloping operad
of
A\in\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
. A key feature of this construction is the existence of a categorical isomorphism

(2.1)

\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}\cong\Fun((\mathcal{P}_{A})_{1},\mathcal{S})

between
A
-modules over
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{S}
-valued enriched functors on the underlying category of
\mathcal{P}_{A}
(cf.
[
5
, Theorem 1.10]
).

In particular, when
\mathcal{P}
is a single-colored operad then
(\mathcal{P}_{A})_{1}
forms an associative monoid in
\mathcal{S}
. In this situation, we will write
\sU_{\mathcal{P}}(A):=(\mathcal{P}_{A})_{1}
and as usual, refer to it as the
universal enveloping algebra
of
A
(cf. e.g.,
[
20
,
10
]
). The following is a fundamental result.

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is a single-colored operad. Then
\sU_{\mathcal{P}}(A)
can be modeled by
\Free_{A}(1_{\mathcal{S}})
the free
A
-module over
\mathcal{P}
generated by
1_{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

As discussed above, the category
\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
is identified with the category of left modules over
\sU_{\mathcal{P}}(A)
. Note that
\sU_{\mathcal{P}}(A)
is the free left module over itself that is generated by
1_{\mathcal{S}}
. Thus we obtain an identification
\sU_{\mathcal{P}}(A)\cong\Free_{A}(1_{\mathcal{S}})
of
A
-modules over
\mathcal{P}
.
∎

2.3.
Semi-model categories

Let us now recall briefly some preliminaries relevant to semi-model categories. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
30
,
10
]
.

Definition 2.15
.

(
[
30
]
) The data of a
semi-model category
consist of a bicomplete category
M
and together with three subcategories of
weak equivalences
,
fibrations
and
cofibrations
such that the following conditions are satisfied.

(SM1) The class of weak equivalences satisfies the
two-out-of-three
axiom.

(SM2) The three classes of morphisms are all closed under retracts.

(SM3) (i) The cofibrations have the left lifting property with respect to
trivial fibrations
; and (ii) the
trivial cofibrations
with
cofibrant
domains have the left lifting property with respect to fibrations. Here, a morphism in
M
is called a trivial (co)fibration if it is both a weak equivalence and a (co)fibration; and an object
X\in\textbf{M}
is called cofibrant if the unique morphism
\emptyset_{\textbf{M}}\longrightarrow X
is a cofibration, where
\emptyset_{\textbf{M}}
denotes the initial object of
M
.

(SM4) (i) Every morphism in
M
can be functorially factored into a cofibration followed by a
trivial fibration, and (ii) every morphism in
M
whose domain is cofibrant can be functorially factored into a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration.

(SM5) The two classes of fibrations and trivial fibrations are closed under pullbacks.

(SM6) The initial object
\emptyset_{\textbf{M}}
is cofibrant.

Definition 2.16
.

(
[
30
]
) A semi-model category
M
is said to be
cofibrantly generated
if there are sets of morphisms
I
and
J
such that the following conditions are fulfilled:

(i) The class of morphisms which have the right lifting property with respect to
J
(resp.
I
) coincides with that of fibrations (resp. trivial fibrations).

(ii) The domains of maps in
I
are small relative to
I
-
cell
, and the domains of maps in
J
are
small relative to maps in
J
-
cell
whose domains are cofibrant. Here,
I
-cell (resp.
J
-cell) refers to the class of maps which are transfinite compositions of pushouts of maps from
I
(resp.
J
).

We will refer to
I
(resp.
J
) as the set of
generating cofibrations
(resp.
trivial cofibrations
).

Moreover, a semi-model category
M
is
combinatorial
if it is cofibrantly generated, and in addition, the underlying category
M
is locally presentable.

Remark 2.17
.

As in (full) model categories, the
homotopy category

\Ho(\textbf{M})
of a semi-model category
M
can be modeled by
\pi\textbf{M}_{cf}
the category whose objects are
bifibrant
(i.e. both fibrant and cofibrant) objects and whose morphisms are given by the homotopy classes of maps in
M
. Moreover, one may also define
function complexes
between objects using
(co)simplicial frames
of objects in
M
. As usual, these serve as models for the
derived mapping space

\Map^{\h}_{\textbf{M}}(-,-)
in
M
. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
30
]
once again.

Definition 2.18
.

An adjunction
\mathcal{L}:\textbf{M}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\textbf{N}:\mathcal{R}
between semi-model categories is called a
Quillen adjunction
if the right adjoint
\mathcal{R}
preserves fibrations and trivial fibrations.

For a Quillen adjunction
\mathcal{L}\dashv\mathcal{R}
between semi-model categories, one can show that the left adjoint
\mathcal{L}
(resp. the right adjoint
\mathcal{R}
) preserves weak equivalences between cofibrant (resp. bifibrant) objects, due to a version of
K. Brown’s lemma
. In light of this, the adjunction
\mathcal{L}\dashv\mathcal{R}
induces an adjunction
\mathbb{L}\mathcal{L}:\Ho(\textbf{M})\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\Ho(\textbf{N}):\mathbb{R}\mathcal{R}
between homotopy categories by taking the corresponding
left and right derived functors
.

Definition 2.19
.

A Quillen adjunction
\mathcal{L}:\textbf{M}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\textbf{N}:\mathcal{R}
between semi-model categories is a
Quillen equivalence
if the induced adjunction
\mathbb{L}\mathcal{L}\dashv\mathbb{R}\mathcal{R}
is an equivalence between homotopy categories.

The reader may refer to
[
10
]
for various equivalent characterizations of a Quillen equivalence between semi-model categories, which are quite analogous to those in the context of (full) model categories.

2.4.
Operadic model structures

We assume that
(\mathcal{S},\otimes,1_{\mathcal{S}})
is a
symmetric monoidal model category
(cf.
[
18
]
). Let
\mathcal{P}
be a
C
-colored operad in
\mathcal{S}
, where
C
is a fixed set of colors. We will usually require the existence of a
transferred model (or semi-model) structure
on the category
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
. This is based on the result below. By definition, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is
\Sigma
-
cofibrant
if it is cofibrant as an object in
\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})
with respect to the model structure transferred from
\mathcal{S}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Spitzweck
[
30
]
, Fresse
[
10
]
) Suppose that the model structure on
\mathcal{S}
is cofibrantly generated, that the monoidal unit
1_{\mathcal{S}}
is cofibrant, and that
\mathcal{P}
is
\Sigma
-cofibrant. Then the category
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
carries a cofibrantly generated semi-model structure transferred from the model structure on
\mathcal{S}^{\times C}
. Namely, a map
A\longrightarrow B
in
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
is a weak equivalence (resp. fibration) if and only if for every
c\in C
the map
A(c)\longrightarrow B(c)
is a weak equivalence (resp. fibration) in
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 2.20
.

In particular, with the same assumptions on
\mathcal{S}
, the transferred semi-model structure on the category
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
does exist. Additionally, with the same assumptions on
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{P}
, the categories
\LMod(\mathcal{P})
and
\BMod(\mathcal{P})
admit a transferred semi-model structure as well, while
\RMod(\mathcal{P})
and
\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
inherit a transferred (full) model structure. These observations follow from the above theorem and Remark
2.9
.

We now recall the Dwyer-Kan model structure on
\mathcal{S}
-enriched operads, according to the work of Caviglia
[
7
]
. For
\mathcal{C}\in\Cat(\mathcal{S})
an
\mathcal{S}
-
enriched category
, the
homotopy category
of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted
\Ho(\mathcal{C})
, is the ordinary category whose objects are the same as those of
\mathcal{C}
and such that, for
x,y\in\Ob(\mathcal{C})
, we have

\textrm{Hom}_{\Ho(\mathcal{C})}(x,y):=\textrm{Hom}_{\Ho(\mathcal{S})}(1_{\mathcal{S}},\Map_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)).

By convention, the
homotopy category of an operad

\mathcal{P}
is
\Ho(\mathcal{P}):=\Ho(\mathcal{P}_{1})
.
Moreover, a map
f:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
in
\Op(\mathcal{S})
is called a
levelwise weak equivalence
(resp.
fibration, trivial fibration
, etc) if for every sequence
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)
of colors of
\mathcal{P}
, the induced map

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}(f(c_{1}),\cdots,f(c_{n});f(c))

is a weak equivalence (resp. fibration, trivial fibration, etc) in
\mathcal{S}
.

Definition 2.21
.

A map
f:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}
in
\Op(\mathcal{S})
is called a
Dwyer-Kan equivalence
if it is a levelwise weak equivalence and such that the induced functor

\Ho(f):\Ho(\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{Q})

between homotopy categories is essentially surjective.

Remark 2.22
.

Under mild assumptions on
\mathcal{S}
(in particular, combinatoriality), there is a combinatorial
Dwyer-Kan model structure
on
\Op(\mathcal{S})
, in which the weak
equivalences are the Dwyer-Kan equivalences and the trivial fibrations are the
levelwise trivial fibrations that are surjective on colors. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
7
,
\lx@sectionsign
8.6]
.

Examples 2.23
.

Here are some base categories that we are concerned with in this paper. Let
k
be a (unital) commutative ring.

(i) Firstly, we let
\Set_{\Delta}
denote the category of
simplicial sets
. This category comes equipped with the Cartesian monoidal structure and with the standard (Kan-Quillen) model structure.

(ii) Another instance is the category of
simplicial

k
-
modules
, denoted
\sMod(\textbf{k})
, and equipped with the (degreewise) tensor product over
k
, and with the standard model structure transferred from that on
\Set_{\Delta}
.

(iii) We will denote by
\mathcal{C}(\textbf{k})
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})
) the category of
dg
k
-modules
(resp.
connective dg
k
-modules
), endowed with the usual tensor product, and with the projective model structure.

Remark 2.24
.

When
\mathcal{S}=\Set_{\Delta}
or
\sMod(\textbf{k})
, then for every operad
\mathcal{P}
the transferred homotopy theory on
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
exists as a (full) model category. Moreover, the Dwyer-Kan model structure on
\Op(\mathcal{S})
does exist as well.

Remark 2.25
.

For the case
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{C}(\textbf{k})
or
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}(\textbf{k})
, due to Theorem
2.1
the transferred homotopy theory on
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
exists as a semi-model category, provided that
\mathcal{P}
is
\Sigma
-cofibrant. Additionally, it can be shown that the same classes of weak equivalences and generating (trivial) cofibrations as in
[
7
, Theorem 8.6]
define a cofibrantly generated semi-model structure on
\Op(\mathcal{S})
. We will refer to this as the
Dwyer-Kan semi-model structure
on
\Op(\mathcal{S})
. (This is in fact made up together by the Dwyer-Kan model structure on
\Cat(\mathcal{S})
and by the transferred semi-model structure on the categories
\Op_{C}(\mathcal{S})
for

). In particular, in this situation
\mathcal{S}
is not
sufficient
in the sense of
[

,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
. In spite of this, the results needed from that paper remain valid if we bring them in the framework of semi-model categories.

2.5.
Spectral cotangent complex and Quillen cohomology

We recall briefly some fundamental notions relevant to the
spectral cotangent complex
and
Quillen cohomology
, according to the works of Harpaz-Nuiten-Prasma
[
14
,
12
]
. Nevertheless, our setting is a bit more flexible, so that we may define
tangent categories
of (semi) model categories of interest without requiring the left properness. This enables us to cover a wider range of examples. We also refer the reader to
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.5]
for more details.

By definition, a semi-model category
M
is
weakly pointed
if it contains a
weak zero object

0
, i.e.
0
is both homotopy initial and terminal. When
M
is weakly pointed, we may define the
suspension
and
desuspension
of an object
X\in\Ob(\textbf{M})
respectively as

\Sigma(X):=0\,\overset{\h}{\underset{X}{\bigsqcup}}\,0\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\Omega(X):=0\,\overset{\h}{\underset{X}{\times}}\,0.

Moreover, when passing to the homotopy category, these constructions determine an adjunction

\Sigma:\Ho(\textbf{M})\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\Ho(\textbf{M}):\Omega.

Definition 2.26
.

A semi-model category
M
is said to be
stable
if the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1) The
underlying

\infty
-
category

\textbf{M}_{\infty}
is stable in the sense of
[
21
]
.

(2)
M
is weakly pointed and such that a square in
M
is homotopy coCartesian if and only if it is homotopy Cartesian.

(3)
M
is weakly pointed and such that the self-adjunction
\Sigma\dashv\Omega
on
\Ho(\textbf{M})
is an adjoint equivalence.

We now recall the procedure of taking
stabilizations
. Let
M
be a
weakly pointed semi-model category
and let
X
be an (
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
)-diagram in
M
. A square of
X
that takes the form

\textstyle{X_{n,n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{n,n+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{n+1,n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{n+1,n+1}}

will be called a
diagonal square
.

Definition 2.27
.

An (
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
)-diagram in
M
is called

(1) a
prespectrum
if all its off-diagonal entries are weak zero objects in
M
,

(2) an
\Omega
-spectrum
if it is a prespectrum and all its diagonal squares are homotopy Cartesian,

(3) a
suspension spectrum
if it is a prespectrum and all its diagonal squares are homotopy coCartesian.

Definition 2.28
.

A map
f:X\longrightarrow Y
in
\textbf{M}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
is called a
stable equivalence
if for every
\Omega
-spectrum
Z
the induced map

\Map^{\der}_{\textbf{M}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}_{\proj}}(Y,Z)\longrightarrow\Map^{\der}_{\textbf{M}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}_{\proj}}(X,Z)

is a weak equivalence, where
\textbf{M}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}_{\proj}
signifies the projective semi-model category of (
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
)-diagrams in
M
.

We will assume further that
M
is a
weakly pointed combinatorial semi-model category
such that the domains of generating cofibrations are cofibrant.

Definition 2.29
.

The
stabilization
of
M
, denoted
\Sp(\textbf{M})
, is the left Bousfield localization of
\textbf{M}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}_{\proj}
with
\Omega
-spectra as the local objects. More precisely,
\Sp(\textbf{M})
is a cofibrantly generated semi-model category such that

\bullet
weak equivalences are the stable equivalences, and

\bullet
(generating) cofibrations are the same as those of
\textbf{M}^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}_{\proj}
.

In particular, fibrant objects of
\Sp(\textbf{M})
are precisely the levelwise fibrant
\Omega
-spectra.

Remark 2.30
.

The above definition is valid due to the main result of
[
3
]
, and together with the fact that the
\Omega
-spectra in
M
can be characterized as local objects against a certain set of maps (cf.

Definition 2.35

Definition 2.37

Let

A basic fact is that

In what follows, we shall recall from

\lx@sectionsign

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet
We will write

Constructions 4.2

We construct three categories in
\mathcal{S}
, denoted
\textbf{Ib}^{\mathcal{P}}
,
\textbf{R}^{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\textbf{I}\ell}^{\mathcal{P}}
, as follows. Firstly, the three categories have the same objects given by
C

\{(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\,|\,c_{i},c\in C,n\geqslant 0\}
.

\bullet
The mapping spaces of
\textbf{Ib}^{\mathcal{P}}
are defined as follows. For each map

\Fin_{*}

\Map^{f}_{\textbf{Ib}^{\mathcal{P}}}((c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c),(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};d)):=\mathcal{P}\left(c,\{d_{j}\}_{j\in f^{-1}(0)};d\right)\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1,\cdots,n}\mathcal{P}\left(\{d_{j}\}_{j\in f^{-1}(i)};c_{i}\right).

Then we define

\Map_{\textbf{Ib}^{\mathcal{P}}}((c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c),(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};d)):=\bigsqcup_{\left\langle m\right\rangle\overset{f}{\rightarrow}\left\langle n\right\rangle}\Map^{f}_{\textbf{Ib}^{\mathcal{P}}}((c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c),(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};d))

where the coproduct ranges over

\textbf{Ib}^{\mathcal{P}}

\mathcal{P}
, and moreover, the categorical structure maps are induced by the composition in
\mathcal{P}

\bullet
The mapping spaces of

\Map_{\textbf{R}^{\mathcal{P}}}(\,(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c),(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};c)\,):=\bigsqcup_{\left\langle m\right\rangle\underset{\text{active}}{\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}}\left\langle n\right\rangle}\left[\bigotimes_{i=1,\cdots,n}\mathcal{P}\left(\{d_{j}\}_{j\in f^{-1}(i)};c_{i}\right)\right]

where the coproduct ranges over those maps

\id_{c}

\mathcal{P}(c;c)

\textbf{Ib}^{\mathcal{P}}

\bullet
The mapping spaces of

\Map_{\textbf{I}\ell^{\mathcal{P}}}((c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c),(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};d)):=\bigsqcup_{f}\mathcal{P}\left(c,\{d_{j}\}_{j\in f^{-1}(0)};d\right)

induced by the canonical embedding
\Fin_{*}^{\ine}\longrightarrow\Fin_{*}
and by inserting the identity operation
\id_{c_{i}}
into the factor
\mathcal{P}(c_{i};c_{i})
for

There are natural isomorphisms of categories

5.
Hochschild and Quillen cohomologies of operadic algebras

In this section, we assume that the base category
\mathcal{S}
is (at least)
sufficient
in the sense of
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
, and we let
\mathcal{P}
be a
C
-colored operad in
\mathcal{S}
, for some fixed set of colors
C
.

We will define
spectral Hochschild cohomology
of operads and their algebras, and describe the passage between various categories of operadic bimodules and algebras. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the cohomologies of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras are governed by those of the operad
\mathcal{P}
itself.

5.1.
Spectral Hochschild cohomology

Let us start with the notion of spectral Hochschild cohomology of operads.

Definition 5.1
.

The
Hochschild complex of
\mathcal{P}
is defined to be the cotangent complex
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
of
\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.6
). Moreover, the
(spectral) Hochschild cohomology
of
\mathcal{P}
with coefficients in a given object
M\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
is the space

\HHH^{\star}(\mathcal{P};M):=\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})}(\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}},M).

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
n
-th Hochschild cohomology group of
\mathcal{P}
with coefficients in
M
is

\HHH^{n}(\mathcal{P};M):=\pi_{0}\Map^{\der}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})}(\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}},M[n]).

Remark 5.2
.

By definition, the Hochschild cohomology of
\mathcal{P}
coincides with Quillen cohomology of
\mathcal{P}
considered as an infinitesimal bimodule over itself.

Let
A\in\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
be an algebra over
\mathcal{P}
. The corresponding cohomology of
A
is defined as follows.

Definition 5.3
.

The
Hochschild complex of
A
is defined to be the cotangent complex
\rL_{A^{\me}}\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
of
A^{\me}\in\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
. Moreover, the
(spectral) Hochschild cohomology
of
A
with coefficients in a given object
N\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
is the space

\HHH^{\star}(A;N):=\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}}(\rL_{A^{\me}},N).

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
n
-th Hochschild cohomology group of
A
with coefficients in
N
is given by

\HHH^{n}(A;N):=\pi_{0}\Map^{\der}_{\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}}(\rL_{A^{\me}},N[n]).

Notations 5.4
.

By abuse of notation, we let
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathbb{S}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
and
A\otimes\mathbb{S}\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
denote the suspension spectra respectively given by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathbb{S})_{n,n}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m};c):=\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m};c)\otimes\sS^{n},\;\text{and}

\displaystyle(A\otimes\mathbb{S})_{n,n}(c):=A(c)\otimes\sS^{n}

where
\sS^{n}
refers to the
n
-sphere in
\mathcal{S}
(see Notations
3.2
).

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is levelwise cofibrant. Then
\mathcal{P}\otimes\,\mathbb{S}
is a suspension spectrum model for the Hochschild complex
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
. Likewise, when
A
is levelwise cofibrant then
A\otimes\mathbb{S}
is a suspension spectrum model for
\rL_{A^{\me}}
.

Proof.

When
\mathcal{P}
is levelwise cofibrant then the coproduct
\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
has already the right homotopy type. Thus we may exhibit the constant spectrum
\Sigma^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\Inf})
as a model for
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
. Moreover, observe that the
n
-suspension
\Sigma^{n}(\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\Inf})\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}//\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
is given on each level by

\Sigma^{n}(\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\Inf})(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m};c)\simeq\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m};c)\otimes\sS^{n}.

This follows from the fact that colimits of infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules can be taken levelwise. We just showed that
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathbb{S}
is a suspension spectrum model for
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
(see also Remark
2.34
). The second claim is very similar.
∎

Example 5.6
.

Recall from Remark
2.9
that there exists an operad
\textbf{O}_{C}
such that the category of
\textbf{O}_{C}
-algebras is isomorphic to the category of
C
-colored operads in
\mathcal{S}
. Thus we may consider
\mathcal{P}
as an object of
\Alg_{\textbf{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{S})
. It can be proved further that there is a categorical isomorphism

\Mod_{\textbf{O}_{C}}^{\mathcal{P}}\cong\IbMod(\mathcal{P})

between
\mathcal{P}
-modules over
\textbf{O}_{C}
and infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules, which identifies
\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
with
\mathcal{P}
itself as an object of
\Mod_{\textbf{O}_{C}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. Accordingly, we obtain that Hochschild cohomology of the operad
\mathcal{P}
agrees with Hochschild cohomology of
\mathcal{P}\in\Alg_{\textbf{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{S})
. (On other hand, we can see that Quillen cohomology of
\mathcal{P}\in\Alg_{\textbf{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{S})
coincides with the reduced Quillen cohomology of the operad
\mathcal{P}
).

Remark 5.7
.

Hochschild cohomology of the operad
\Com
is trivial for any base category
\mathcal{S}
. This is because of the fact that
\Com^{\Inf}\in\IbMod(\Com)
is a free object generated by

Remark 5.8
.

Suppose that the pair

is sufficient, so that we obtain Quillen equivalences

(5.1)

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\simeq\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)

Remark 5.9

Similarly, when the triple

Example 5.10

When the pair

5.2.
Endomorphism constructions

It is known that the composite product

-\circ-:\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\times\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\longrightarrow\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})

is
left linear
, i.e. for every
A\in\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})
the functor
(-)\circ A:\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\longrightarrow\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})
preserves all colimits. Consider the case where
A\in\mathcal{S}^{\times C}
regarded as a
C
-collection concentrated in level
0
. Taking composite product with
A
determines a functor

(-)\circ A:\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\times C}.

Of course, this functor admits a right adjoint, as described in the following construction.

Construction 5.14
.

Let
A=\{A(c)\}_{c\in C}
and
B=\{B(c)\}_{c\in C}
be two objects of
\mathcal{S}^{\times C}
. The
endomorphism object
associated to the pair
(A,B)
is a
C
-collection denoted by
\textrm{End}_{A,B}
, and defined by letting

\textrm{End}_{A,B}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c):=\Map_{\mathcal{S}}(A(c_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(c_{n}),B(c)).

The right
\Sigma_{n}
-action is naturally given by the permutation on the factors
A(c_{i})
’s. In particular, the
endomorphism operad associated to
A
is given by
\textrm{End}_{A}:=\textrm{End}_{A,A}
. (See also
[
10
,
26
]
).

We have indeed the following result, which can be readily verified using definition.

Proposition 5.15
.

The functors
(-)\circ A
and
\textrm{End}_{A,-}
determine an adjunction

(5.2)

(-)\circ A:\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)

Another important adjunction, which interposes the two above, is given as follows.

Proposition 5.17
.

Let
A
be a
\mathcal{P}
-algebra. Then the constructions
(-)\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A
and
\textrm{End}_{A,-}
determine an adjunction:

(5.5)

(-)\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A:\IbMod(\mathcal{P})\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{\hfil$#$\hfil\cr\scriptstyle\cr\kern 0.43057pt\cr\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\begin{picture}\roundcap\polyline(0,0)(1,0)
\polyline(0.5,0)(0.5,1)
\end{picture}\cr\leftharpoondown\joinrel\relbar\cr\scriptstyle\cr}}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}:\textrm{End}_{A,-}.

Proof.

For an
A
-module
N
, the canonical infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule structure on
\textrm{End}_{A,N}
is defined as follows. Since
\textrm{End}_{A,N}
already inherits a right
\mathcal{P}
-module structure, it suffices to establish a compatible infinitesimal left
\mathcal{P}
-module structure, whose data consist of
\Sigma_{*}
-equivariant maps of the form

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\textrm{End}_{A,N}(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};c_{i})\longrightarrow\textrm{End}_{A,N}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{i-1},d_{1},\cdots,d_{m},c_{i+1},\cdots,c_{n};c)

(see Definition
2.8
). This is equivalent to giving a map of the form

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\textbf{A}^{\otimes\hat{i}}\otimes[A(d_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(d_{m})]\otimes\Map_{\mathcal{S}}(A(d_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(d_{m}),N(c_{i}))\longrightarrow N(c)

where
\textbf{A}^{\otimes\hat{i}}:=\bigotimes_{k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}A(c_{k})
. This map is defined by first inserting the canonical map

[A(d_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(d_{m})]\otimes\Map_{\mathcal{S}}(A(d_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(d_{m}),N(c_{i}))\longrightarrow N(c_{i}),

and then applying the structure map
\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\textbf{A}^{\otimes\hat{i}}\otimes N(c_{i})\longrightarrow N(c)
of
N
. The axioms of an infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodule structure follow from those of the
A
-module structure on
N
.

Next, the object
M\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A
for some
M\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
carries a canonical
A
-module structure, described as follows. By definition, we need to establish
\Sigma_{*}
-equivariant maps of the form

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\textbf{A}^{\otimes\hat{i}}\otimes(M\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A)(c_{i})\longrightarrow(M\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A)(c).

This can be constructed from maps of the form

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\textbf{A}^{\otimes\hat{i}}\otimes M(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};c_{i})\otimes[A(d_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(d_{m})]

\longrightarrow M(c_{1},\cdots,c_{i-1},d_{1},\cdots,d_{m},c_{i+1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes\textbf{A}^{\otimes\hat{i}}\otimes[A(d_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(d_{m})]

that satisfy a compatibility condition governed by the coequalizer
M\circ\mathcal{P}\circ A\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
\longrightarrow\end{subarray}}M\circ A
. This is simply induced by the structure map of
M
:

\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\otimes M(d_{1},\cdots,d_{m};c_{i})\longrightarrow M(c_{1},\cdots,c_{i-1},d_{1},\cdots,d_{m},c_{i+1},\cdots,c_{n};c).

Finally, to verify that the obtained functors form an adjoint pair, we use the adjunction (
5.3
), reducing the task to showing that the unit
M\longrightarrow\textrm{End}_{A,M\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A}
(resp. counit
\textrm{End}_{A,N}\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\longrightarrow N
) of (
5.3
) is a map of infinitesimal
\mathcal{P}
-bimodules (resp.
A
-modules) for
M\in\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
(resp.
N\in\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
). This follows straightforwardly from the definitions.
∎

Corollary 5.18

In particular, when

we obtain that
\Free_{A}(1_{\mathcal{S}})\cong\mathcal{P}[1]\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A
(see

Proof.

\textstyle{\Coll_{C}(\mathcal{S})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathcal{S}^{\times C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\IbMod(\mathcal{P})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The following plays a pivotal role in the study of various cohomologies of operadic algebras.

Proposition 5.25
.

Suppose that
A\in\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
is levelwise cofibrant. There are weak equivalences

\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\rL_{A}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\rL_{A^{\me}}

in
\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\mathcal{P}}^{A}
respectively.

Proof.

We will prove the first weak equivalence. The other can then be verified in the same fashion. By definition,
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}
is represented by a constant spectrum of the form
\Sigma^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\sB}\sqcup(\mathcal{P}^{\sB})^{\cof}\right)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\BMod(\mathcal{P})
in which
(\mathcal{P}^{\sB})^{\cof}
refers to any cofibrant model for
\mathcal{P}^{\sB}\in\BMod(\mathcal{P})
, and the coproduct
\mathcal{P}^{\sB}\sqcup(\mathcal{P}^{\sB})^{\cof}
is considered as an object over and under
\mathcal{P}^{\sB}
. We have a chain of weak equivalences

\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\sB}\sqcup(\mathcal{P}^{\sB})^{\cof}\right)\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\;\overset{\defi}{=}\;\Sigma^{\infty}\left((\mathcal{P}^{\sB}\sqcup(\mathcal{P}^{\sB})^{\cof})\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\right)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}(A\sqcup A^{\cof})

where
A^{\cof}:=(\mathcal{P}^{\sB})^{\cof}\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A
represents a cofibrant model for
A\in\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
. As above,
\Sigma^{\infty}(A\sqcup A^{\cof})
is a model for
\rL_{A}
. So we obtain a weak equivalence
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\rL_{A}
as expected.
∎

According to
\lx@sectionsign
3
,
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\BMod(\mathcal{P})
admits a suspension spectrum replacement given by
\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{\bullet}
with
(\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{\bullet})_{n,n}=\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{n}
, provided that
\mathcal{P}
is
\Sigma
-cofibrant. Due to this, we can describe the cotangent complex
\rL_{A}
as follows.

Proposition 5.26
.

Suppose that the triple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{P},A)
is sufficient. Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have a weak equivalence in
\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})_{A//A}
:

(\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{n})\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\Sigma^{n}(A\sqcup A)

in which the right hand side is the
n
-suspension of
A\sqcup A\in\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})_{A//A}
. Consequently, the cotangent complex
\rL_{A}\in\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})
admits a suspension spectrum replacement:

(\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{\bullet})\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\rL_{A}.

Proof.

As discussed in
\lx@sectionsign
3
, we have a weak equivalence
\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{n}\simeq\Sigma^{n}(\mathcal{P}^{\sB}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\sB})
in
\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}//\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}
. By the assumption that
\mathcal{P}
is
\Sigma
-cofibrant and that
A
is cofibrant, the relative composite product
(\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{n})\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A
has the right homotopy type (cf.
[
10
]
,
\lx@sectionsign
15.2). Thus we obtain a chain of natural weak equivalences

(\mathcal{P}\circ\sS_{C}^{n})\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\left(\Sigma^{n}(\mathcal{P}^{\sB}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\sB})\right)\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A\simeq\Sigma^{n}(A\sqcup A)

where the latter weak equivalence is due to the left Quillen functor

(-)\circ_{\mathcal{P}}A:\BMod(\mathcal{P})_{\mathcal{P}^{\sB}//\mathcal{P}^{\sB}}\longrightarrow\Alg_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{S})_{A//A}.

The second statement follows from the above, because the functor
(-)\circ^{\st}_{\mathcal{P}}A
is computed degreewise. (Of course, it can also be immediately derived from Proposition
5.25
).
∎

In what follows, we shall provide some comparison theorems asserting that Hochschild and Quillen cohomologies of

Theorem 5.2

Suppose that the triple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{P},A)
is sufficient and suppose given a fibrant object

(ii) There is a natural weak equivalence

\HHH^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\HHH^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N})

between the Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
N
and Hochschild cohomology of
\mathcal{P}
with coefficients in
\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N}

Proof.

First, by assumption the horizontal adjunctions of (

and while by Theorem

We now arrive at the main result as follows.

Theorem 5.3

Suppose that the triple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{P},A)
is abundant. For a fibrant object

\HHQ^{\star}(A;N)\simeq\Omega\HHQ^{\star}(\mathcal{P};\textrm{End}^{\st}_{A,N})

∎

\mathcal{Q}\circ\sS_{D}^{\bullet}

\textstyle{\mathcal{P}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n};c)\times(\sS^{m})^{\times n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Definition 6.16

Typical examples of adequate simplicial operads include the operads

Notations 6.17

For any object

Remark 6.18

Unwinding the definitions, the functor

Proof.

Firstly, since

has a single color, we may identify objects of

with those of

and each

with value

) There is a fiber sequence in
\Fun(\Tw(\E_{n}),\Sp)
of the form

(6.7)

(\mu_{0})_{!}[\mathbb{S}]\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{\E_{n}}[n].

As we have seen, the functor
\mathcal{F}_{\E_{n}}
represents the cotangent complex of
\E_{n}
, and while
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
represents the Hochschild complex of
\E_{n}
(see Proposition
6.13
). In light of this, we will refer to the fiber sequence (
6.7
) as the
Quillen principle for the operad

\E_{n}
.

We now explain how the Quillen principle for
\E_{n}
-algebras can be formulated, relying on the following lemma. For a single-colored simplicial operad
\mathcal{P}
, we write
\eta_{\mathcal{P}}:\Free_{\mathcal{P}}^{\Inf}(\mathcal{E}_{*})\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}
for the map in
\IbMod(\mathcal{P})
classified by a given nullary operation
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(0)
(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.6
for notations).

Lemma 6.28
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is fibrant. Under the equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{\infty},

the map
(\mu_{0})_{!}[\mathbb{S}]\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{S}}
is identified with the canonical map
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{\mathcal{P}})\longrightarrow\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
in which as usual
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{\mathcal{P}})
denotes the image of
\eta_{\mathcal{P}}
through the functor

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{/\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P}).

Proof.

We let
\textbf{S}:=(\Set_{\Delta})_{\infty}
denote the
\infty
-category of spaces. We also write
(\mu_{0})_{!}:\textbf{S}\longrightarrow\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\textbf{S})
for the induction along the inclusion
\{\mu_{0}\}\longrightarrow\Tw(\mathcal{P})
. We have a commutative diagram of the form

S
\scriptstyle{(\mu_{0})_{!}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\textbf{S})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\;\,}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{*}(\textbf{S})_{/\mathcal{E}_{*}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Free_{\mathcal{P}}^{\Inf}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;}
\textstyle{{\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{\infty}}_{/\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\;\;}
\textstyle{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{\infty}}

in which the left vertical functor is the obvious equivalence sending each space
X
to the
\Sigma_{*}
-object given by
X
itself concentrated in level
0
and equipped with an evident map to
\mathcal{E}_{*}
, and while the middle vertical functor is the equivalence induced by the straightening-unstraightening constructions (cf.
[
15
,
\lx@sectionsign
6.3]
). Let us start with the terminal object
\Delta^{0}\in\textbf{S}
. Clearly the image of
\Delta^{0}
through the composed horizontal functor is identified with
(\mu_{0})_{!}[\mathbb{S}]\in\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)
. On the other hand, observe that the left vertical functor sends
\Delta^{0}
to nothing but
\Id_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\in\Sigma_{*}(\textbf{S})_{/\mathcal{E}_{*}}
, whose image through
\Free_{\mathcal{P}}^{\Inf}
is exactly the map
\eta_{\mathcal{P}}
. Now, the commutativity of the diagram proves that
(\mu_{0})_{!}[\mathbb{S}]
is identified with
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{\mathcal{P}})
under the equivalence
\Fun(\Tw(\mathcal{P}),\Sp)\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\mathcal{P})_{\infty}
.

Moreover, by Proposition
6.13
, we obtained that
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
is identified with the Hochschild complex
\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
. To complete the proof, we argue that the two maps
(\mu_{0})_{!}[\mathbb{S}]\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{S}}
and
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{\mathcal{P}})\longrightarrow\rL_{\mathcal{P}^{\Inf}}
are both induced by the operation
\mu_{0}
.
∎

Combining the above lemma with the fiber sequence (
6.7
), and along with Theorem
6.2
we obtain a corollary as follows.

Corollary 6.29
.

There is a fiber sequence in
\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\E_{n})
of the form

(6.8)

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{\E_{n}})\longrightarrow\rL_{\E_{n}^{\Inf}}\longrightarrow\rL_{\E_{n}}[n+1].

Here we use the same notation for the derived image of
\rL_{\E_{n}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}}\Op(\Set_{\Delta})
under the equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}}\Op(\Set_{\Delta})\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\E_{n})
.

Remark 6.30
.

The fiber sequence (
6.8
) is in fact the operadic source of (
6.7
), and in particular, can be viewed as the standard version of the Quillen principle for
\E_{n}
. One might think of another proof of the existence of (
6.8
) that is more direct; nevertheless, it may be much more complicated.

We are now in position to formulate the
Quillen principle for
\E_{n}
-algebras
. Let
A
be an
\E_{n}
-algebra. We let
\Free_{A}(*)
denote the free
A
-module over
\E_{n}
generated by a singleton, and let
\eta_{A}:\Free_{A}(*)\longrightarrow A^{\me}
be the map in
\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
classified by the unit of
A
. As usual, we have a canonical map
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})\longrightarrow\rL_{A^{\me}}
where
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})
refers to the image of
\eta_{A}
through the functor

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:(\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A})_{/A^{\me}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}.

Theorem 6.6
.

Suppose that
A\in\Alg_{\E_{n}}(\Set_{\Delta})
is cofibrant. There is a fiber sequence in
\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
of the form

(6.9)

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})\longrightarrow\rL_{A^{\me}}\longrightarrow\rL_{A}[n]

where we use the same notation for the derived image of
\rL_{A}\in\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\E_{n}}(\Set_{\Delta})
under the equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{A}\Alg_{\E_{n}}(\Set_{\Delta})\simeq\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
. Equivalently,
\rL_{A}[n]
is weakly equivalent to
\rL_{A^{\me}/\Free_{A}(*)}
the relative cotangent complex of
\eta_{A}
.

Proof.

First, note that the fiber sequence (
6.8
) can be equivalently rewritten as

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{\E_{n}})\longrightarrow\rL_{\E_{n}^{\Inf}}\longrightarrow\rL_{\E_{n}^{\sB}}[n]

where we use the same notation for the derived image of
\rL_{\E_{n}^{\sB}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}^{\sB}}\BMod(\E_{n})
under the equivalence
\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}^{\sB}}\BMod(\E_{n})\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\E_{n})
. Let us now apply the left Quillen functor

(-)\circ^{\st}_{\E_{n}}A:\mathcal{T}_{\E_{n}^{\Inf}}\IbMod(\E_{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}

(see Notation
5.23
) to that fiber sequence. Then we may complete the proof by using Corollary
5.18
, which proves that
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{\E_{n}})\circ^{\st}_{\E_{n}}A\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})
; and along with Proposition
5.25
, from which we obtain the following weak equivalences:
\rL_{\E_{n}^{\Inf}}\circ^{\st}_{\E_{n}}A\simeq\rL_{A^{\me}}
and
\rL_{\E_{n}^{\sB}}[n]\circ^{\st}_{\E_{n}}A\simeq\rL_{A}[n]
.
∎

Corollary 6.31
.

Suppose that
A\in\Alg_{\E_{n}}(\Set_{\Delta})
is cofibrant and suppose given a fibrant object
\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
. Then there is a fiber sequence relating the two cohomologies of
A
of the form

\Omega^{n}\HHQ^{\star}(A;\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow\HHH^{\star}(A;\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow|\mathcal{N}|

in which
|\mathcal{N}|:=\{1_{A}\}\times^{\h}_{A}\mathcal{N}_{0,0}
, i.e. the homotopy fiber over
1_{A}
of the structure map
\mathcal{N}_{0,0}\longrightarrow A^{\me}
.

Proof.

We have in fact a commutative diagram of the form

\textstyle{\Map^{\h}(\rL_{A}[n],\mathcal{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Map^{\h}(\rL_{A^{\me}},\mathcal{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Map^{\h}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A}),\mathcal{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\Omega^{n}\HHQ^{\star}(A;\mathcal{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\HHH^{\star}(A;\mathcal{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{|\mathcal{N}|}

where the derived mapping spaces are taken in
\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}
, and the first two weak equivalences follow immediately from the definitions. To verify the third weak equivalence, we argue as follows. First, by adjunction we have a weak equivalence

\Map^{\h}_{\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A}),\mathcal{N})\simeq\Map^{\h}_{(\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A})_{/A^{\me}}}(\eta_{A},\Omega^{\infty}_{+}\mathcal{N}).

Next, the space on the right is weakly equivalent to the homotopy fiber over
\eta_{A}
of the map

\Map^{\h}_{\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}}(\Free_{A}(*),\mathcal{N}_{0,0})\longrightarrow\Map^{\h}_{\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}}(\Free_{A}(*),A^{\me}).

Moreover, clearly this homotopy fiber is weakly equivalent to
|\mathcal{N}|
.
∎

-algebras in a stable category. Suppose we are given a base category

that is sufficient in the sense of

,

, and equipped with a

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
A\in\Alg_{\E_{n}}(\mathcal{S})
be a cofibrant
\E_{n}
-algebra in
\mathcal{S}
. There is a fiber sequence in
\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}(\mathcal{S})
of the form

(6.10)

\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})\longrightarrow\rL_{A^{\me}}\longrightarrow\rL_{A}[n]

(i.e. the same as in Theorem

). When
\mathcal{S}
is in addition strictly pointed stable, then there is a fiber sequence in
\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}(\mathcal{S})
of the form

(6.11)

\Free_{A}(*)\overset{\eta_{A}}{\longrightarrow}A^{\me}\longrightarrow\rL_{A}[n].

are preserved through the changing-bases functor
\Set_{\Delta}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}
. It implies that the same fiber sequence as in Corollary
6.29
holds for the
\mathcal{S}
-enriched version of
\E_{n}
. Then the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
6.6
can be applied to obtain the fiber sequence (

).

Now assume further that
\mathcal{S}
is stable. In particular, the composed functor

\ker\circ\,\Omega^{\infty}:\mathcal{T}_{A^{\me}}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}(\mathcal{S})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Mod_{\E_{n}}^{A}(\mathcal{S})

) by using

, Corollary 2.2.4]
, which demonstrates that the derived image of the map
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}(\eta_{A})\longrightarrow\rL_{A^{\me}}
through the functor

is nothing but the map
\Free_{A}(*)\overset{\eta_{A}}{\longrightarrow}A^{\me}
.
∎

Examples 6.32
.

The most basic example is the case of
symmetric spectra
. Another noteworthy example is the case
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{C}(\textbf{k})
the category of dg modules over some commutative ring
k
. Nonetheless, it is a bit subtle that
\mathcal{C}(\textbf{k})
does not come equipped with a lax monoidal left Quillen functor from
\Set_{\Delta}
. Instead, we have a composed functor

\Set_{\Delta}\overset{\textbf{k}[-]}{\longrightarrow}\sMod_{\textbf{k}}\overset{\sN}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(\textbf{k})\overset{\iota}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}(\textbf{k})

in which
\textbf{k}[-]
refers to the free
k
-module functor, while
\sN
denotes the normalization functor, and
\iota
is the obvious embedding. This composed functor takes
\E_{n}
to its
differential graded (dg) version
. In the above composition, while
\textbf{k}[-]
and
\iota
are left adjoints of weak monoidal Quillen adjunctions,
\sN
is the only functor that does not satisfy the expected property. Despite this, the latter induces a right Quillen equivalence between operadic infinitesimal bimodules, as presented in
[
16
]
. Due to this, we may show that an analogue of (
6.8
) holds for the dg version of
\E_{n}
. Then we may deduce the existence of (
6.11
) for
dg
\E_{n}
-algebras
, as in Theorem
6.7
.

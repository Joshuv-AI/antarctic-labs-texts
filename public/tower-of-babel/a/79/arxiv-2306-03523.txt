Inconsistency Handling in Prioritized Databases with Universal Constraints: Complexity Analysis and Links with Active Integrity Constraints
Meghyn Bienvenu, Camille Bourgaux \affiliations CNRS, University of Bordeaux, Bordeaux INP, LaBRI, Talence, France

Abstract
This paper revisits the problem of repairing and querying inconsistent databases equipped with universal constraints. We adopt symmetric difference repairs, in which both deletions and additions of facts can be used to restore consistency, and suppose that preferred repair actions are specified via a binary priority relation over (negated) facts. Our first contribution is to show how existing notions of optimal repairs, defined for simpler denial constraints and repairs solely based on fact deletion, can be suitably extended to our richer setting. We next study the computational properties of the resulting repair notions, in particular, the data complexity of repair checking and inconsistency-tolerant query answering. Finally, we clarify the relationship between optimal repairs of prioritized databases and repair notions introduced in the framework of active integrity constraints. In particular, we show that Pareto-optimal repairs in our setting correspond to founded, grounded and justified repairs w.r.t. the active integrity constraints obtained by translating the prioritized database. Our study also yields useful insights into the behavior of active integrity constraints.

Introduction

When a database is inconsistent w.r.t. the integrity constraints,
it is possible to obtain meaningful query answers by adopting the
consistent query answering
(CQA) approach
[
1999
]
.
In a nutshell, the idea is to consider a set of

repairs
, which correspond to those databases that satisfy the constraints and are as close as possible to the original database.
An answer is then considered true w.r.t.
CQA semantics
if it holds no matter which repair is chosen, thus embodying
the cautious mode of reasoning employed in many KR contexts.
The CQA approach was subsequently extended to the setting of ontology-mediated query answering,
which led to the proposal of other natural repair-based semantics, such as the
brave semantics
,
which considers as true those answers that hold in at least one repair
[
2013
]
,
and the
intersection (or IAR) semantics
,
which evaluates queries w.r.t. the intersection of the repairs
[
2010
]
.
There is now an extensive literature on CQA and other forms of inconsistency-tolerant query answering,
[
2019
]
and
[
2020
]
provide recent surveys for the database and ontology settings.

Several different notions of
repair have been considered, depending on the considered class of constraints and the allowed repair actions.
For denial constraints (such as functional dependencies, FDs)
and constraints given by ontologies, consistency can be restored only by removing information,
so subset repairs based upon fact deletions are the most common choice. For richer
classes of constraints, however, it makes sense to consider

symmetric difference repairs
obtained using both fact additions and deletions. This is the case for the
universal constraints
considered in the present paper, which can be used to express data completeness assumptions and other kinds of domain knowledge.
For example, in a hospital setting, a universal constraint can be used to capture expert knowledge
that a patient cannot receive a certain treatment without a positive test for a given mutation,
with violations indicating either an erroneous treatment record or missing test result.
Universal constraints are one of the most expressive classes of first-order constraints
for which CQA with symmetric difference repairs is decidable, albeit intractable:
\Pi^{p}_{2}
-complete w.r.t. data complexity

[
2010
,
2016
]
.
Despite this high complexity, there have been some prototype implementations using logic programming
[
2008
,
2010
]
.

Repairs can be further refined by taking into account information about the relative reliability of the database facts.
In the framework of
prioritized databases

[
2012
]
, a binary

priority relation
indicates preferences between pair of facts involved in some violation of a denial constraint.
Three kinds of
optimal repair
(Pareto-, globally-, and completion-optimal) are then
defined to select the most preferred subset repairs according to the priority relation.
The complexity of reasoning with these three kinds of optimal repair has been investigated, primarily focusing on databases with FDs
[
2015
,
2017
,
2017
]
, but also in the context of description logic knowledge bases
[
2020
]
.
A recent system implements SAT-based algorithms for optimal repair-based semantics having (co)
NP
-complete data complexity

[
2022
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, there has been no work addressing how to define fact-level preferences for databases with universal constraints and how to exploit such preferences to single out the optimal symmetric difference repairs.
Our first contribution is thus an extension of the framework of prioritized databases to the case of universal constraints and symmetric difference repairs.
By carefully defining a suitable notion of conflict (which may involve negative facts),
we are able to faithfully lift existing notions of optimal repairs and optimal repair-based semantics, while retaining many properties of the original framework.

We next study the computational properties of optimal repairs of prioritized databases with universal constraints.
We provide an almost-complete picture of the data complexity of repair checking and inconsistency-tolerant query answering
for each of the three notions of optimal repair (Pareto, global, and completion) and
three repair-based semantics (CQA, brave, and intersection).
Our results show that adopting optimal repairs does not increase the complexity of inconsistency-tolerant query answering.

Our third contribution is to establish connections with active integrity constraints (AICs), a framework in which universal constraints are enriched with information on what are the allowed update actions (fact deletions or additions) to resolve a given constraint violation
[
2004
,
2006
,
2009
]
.
More precisely, we provide a natural translation from prioritized databases to AICs and observe that Pareto-optimal repairs
coincide with three kinds of repairs (founded, grounded and justified) that have been defined for AICs.
This leads us to explore more general conditions under which AIC repair notions coincide, which we subsequently exploit to exhibit
a translation of certain ‘well-behaved’ sets of AICs into prioritized databases.

in the appendix.

Preliminaries

We assume familiarity with propositional and first-order logic (FOL) and provide here terminology and notation for databases, conjunctive queries, constraints, and repairs.

Relational databases

Let
{\bf{C}}
and
{\bf{V}}
be two disjoint countably infinite sets of constants and variables respectively. A (relational)
schema

{\bf{S}}
is a finite set of relation names (or
predicates
), each with an associated arity
n>0
. A
fact
over
{\bf{S}}
is an expression of the form
P(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})
where
P\in{\bf{S}}
has arity
n
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\in{\bf{C}}
. A
database (instance)
over
{\bf{S}}
is a finite set
\mathcal{D}
of facts over
{\bf{S}}
. The
active domain
of
\mathcal{D}
, denoted
\mathsf{dom}(\mathcal{D})
, is the set of constants occurring in
\mathcal{D}
.

A database
\mathcal{D}
can also be viewed as a finite relational structure whose domain is
\mathsf{dom}(\mathcal{D})
and which interprets each predicate
P\in{\bf{S}}
as the set
\{\mathbf{c}\mid P(\mathbf{c})\in\mathcal{D}\}
. We shall use the standard notation
\mathcal{D}\models\Phi
to indicate that a (set of) FOL sentence(s)
\Phi

is satisfied in this structure.

Conjunctive queries

A
conjunctive query
(CQ) is a conjunction of
relational atoms

P(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
(with each
t_{i}\in{\bf{V}}\cup{\bf{C}}
), where some variables may be existentially quantified. A
Boolean
CQ (BCQ) has no free variables.
Given a query
q(\mathbf{x})
, with free variables
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
,
and a tuple of constants
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
,

q(\mathbf{a})
denotes the BCQ obtained by replacing each variable in
\mathbf{x}

by the corresponding constant in
\mathbf{a}
. An
answer
to
q(\mathbf{x})

over a database
\mathcal{D}
is a tuple of constants
\mathbf{a}
from
\mathsf{dom}(\mathcal{D})

such that
\mathcal{D}\models q(\mathbf{a})
.

Constraints

A
universal constraint
over a schema
{\bf{S}}
is a FOL sentence of the form

\forall\mathbf{x}(R_{1}(\mathbf{t_{1}})\wedge\dots\wedge R_{n}(\mathbf{t_{n}})\wedge\neg P_{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})\wedge\dots\wedge\neg P_{m}(\mathbf{u_{m}})\wedge\varepsilon\rightarrow\bot)
,
where each
R_{i}(\mathbf{t_{i}})
(resp.
P_{i}(\mathbf{u_{i}})
) is a relational atom over
{\bf{S}}
,

\varepsilon
is a (possibly empty) conjunction of inequality atoms,
and
\mathbf{u_{1}}\cup\dots\cup\mathbf{u_{m}}\subseteq\mathbf{t_{1}}\cup\dots\cup\mathbf{t_{n}}
(safety condition).
Universal constraints can also be written in the form

\forall\mathbf{x}(R_{1}(\mathbf{t_{1}})\wedge\dots\wedge R_{n}(\mathbf{t_{n}})\wedge\varepsilon\rightarrow P_{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})\vee\dots\vee P_{m}(\mathbf{u_{m}}))
. For simplicity, we shall often omit the universal quantification
and will sometimes use the generic term
constraint
to mean universal constraint.

Denial constraints
are universal constraints of the form
\forall\mathbf{x}(R_{1}(\mathbf{t_{1}})\wedge\dots\wedge R_{n}(\mathbf{t_{n}})\wedge\varepsilon\rightarrow\bot)
, which capture the well-known class of functional dependencies.

We say that a database
\mathcal{D}
is
consistent
w.r.t. a set of constraints
\mathcal{C}
if
\mathcal{D}\models\mathcal{C}
. Otherwise,
\mathcal{D}
is
inconsistent
(w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
).

A constraint is
ground
if it contains no variables.
Given a constraint
\tau
and database
\mathcal{D}
, we use

gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\tau)
for the set of
all ground constraints obtained by (i) replacing variables with constants from
\mathsf{dom}(\mathcal{D})
, (ii) removing all true
c\neq d
atoms, and (iii)
removing all constraints that contain an atom
c\neq c
. We let
gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}):=\bigcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}}gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\tau)
, and
note that
\mathcal{D}\models\tau
iff
\mathcal{D}\models\tau_{g}
for every
\tau_{g}\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\tau)
.

Repairs

A
symmetric difference repair
, or
\Delta
-repair, of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
is a database
\mathcal{R}
such that (i)
\mathcal{R}\models\mathcal{C}
and (ii) there is no
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\models\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\Delta\mathcal{D}\subsetneq\mathcal{R}\Delta\mathcal{D}
, where
\Delta
is the symmetric difference operator:
S_{1}\Delta S_{2}=(S_{1}\setminus S_{2})\cup(S_{2}\setminus S_{1})
.
If only fact deletions are permitted, we obtain
subset repairs
(
\subseteq
-repairs),
and if only fact additions are permitted,
superset repairs
(
\supseteq
-repairs).
We denote the set of
\Delta
-repairs of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
by
\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
.

Because of the safety condition, an empty database satisfies any set of universal constraints, so every database has at least one
\subseteq
-repair (which is also a
\Delta
-repair), while
it may be the case that no
\supseteq
-repair exists.
Moreover, for the subclass of denial constraints,
\Delta
-repairs and
\subseteq
-repairs coincide since adding facts cannot resolve a violation of a denial constraint.

3
Optimal Repairs for Universal Constraints

In this section, we show how existing notions of optimal repairs, defined for
\subseteq
-repairs w.r.t. denial constraints,
can be lifted to the broader setting of
\Delta
-repairs w.r.t. universal constraints. We then use the resulting repair notions
to define inconsistency-tolerant semantics for query answering.

3.1
Conflicts for Universal Constraints

In the setting of denial constraints, a conflict is a minimal subset of the database
that is inconsistent w.r.t. the constraints.
Conflicts and the associated notion of conflict (hyper)graph
underpin many results and algorithms for consistent query answering, and in particular,
they appear in the definition of prioritized databases
[
2012
]
.
Our first task will thus be to
define a suitable
notion of conflict for universal constraints.

An important observation is that the absence of a fact may contribute
to the violation of a universal constraint. For this reason,
conflicts will contain
both facts and negated facts, where
\neg P(\mathbf{c})
indicates
that
P(\mathbf{c})
is absent.
We use
\mathit{Facts}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}
for the set of facts over
{\bf{S}}
with constants from
\mathsf{dom}(\mathcal{D})
,
and let
\mathit{Lits}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}\cup\{\neg\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathit{Facts}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}\setminus\mathcal{D}\}
be the set of
literals
of
\mathcal{D}
.
Conflicts can then be defined as minimal sets of literals that necessarily lead to a constraint violation.

Definition 1
.

Given a database
\mathcal{D}
and set of (universal) constraints
\mathcal{C}
,
the set

\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
of
conflicts of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
contains all
\subseteq
-minimal sets
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathit{Lits}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}
such that for every database
\mathcal{I}
, if
\mathcal{I}\models\mathcal{E}
, then
\mathcal{I}\not\models\mathcal{C}
.

Example 1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{A(a),B(a)\}
and
\mathcal{C}=\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3}\}
, where

\tau_{1}:=A(x)\rightarrow C(x)
,
\tau_{2}:=B(x)\rightarrow D(x)
, and
\tau_{3}:=C(x)\wedge D(x)\rightarrow\bot
.
It can be verified that

\displaystyle\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})=\{

\displaystyle\emptyset,\{A(a),C(a)\},\,\{B(a),D(a)\}\}

and that the set
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
is as follows:

\{\{A(a),\neg C(a)\},\{B(a),\neg D(a)\},\{A(a),B(a)\}\}

The first (resp. second) conflict directly violates
\tau_{1}
(resp.
\tau_{2}
).
To see why
\{A(a),B(a)\}
is also a conflict, consider any database
\mathcal{I}
such that
\{A(a),B(a)\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
.
Then either
C(a)\notin\mathcal{I}
or
D(a)\notin\mathcal{I}
, in which case
\mathcal{I}
violates
\tau_{1}
or
\tau_{2}
, or
\mathcal{I}
contains both
C(a)
and
D(a)
,
hence violates
\tau_{3}
.

We also provide two alternative characterizations of conflicts,
in terms of the hitting sets of literals removed from
\Delta
-repairs and the prime implicants

For every database

Given a database

For every candidate repair

Furthermore, with every subset

\mathit{Dat}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B})=\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{D}\cup\{\alpha\mid\neg\alpha\in\mathit{Lits}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}\setminus\mathcal{B}\}
.
Note that if
\mathcal{B}
is a candidate repair,
\mathit{Dat}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B}))=\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a database,
\mathcal{C}
a set of universal constraints, and
\mathcal{R}
a candidate repair for
\mathcal{D}
.

1.

\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
iff
\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})
is a maximal subset of
\mathit{Lits}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\mathit{Dat}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R}))\models\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}\models\mathcal{C}
.

2.

\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
iff
\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})
is a maximal subset of
\mathit{Lits}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\mathcal{E}\not\subseteq\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})
for every
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})

3.

\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})

,

The third generalizes a well-known hypergraph-based characterization of

.

Let

3.2
Prioritized Databases & Optimal Repairs

With the definition of conflicts in place, we can extend the notion of prioritized database
[
2012
]
to the setting of universal constraints.

Definition 2
.

A
priority relation

\succ
for a database
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t. a set of universal constraints
\mathcal{C}
is an acyclic binary relation over the literals of
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
such that if
\lambda\succ\mu
, then there exists
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
such that
\{\lambda,\mu\}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
.
We say that
\succ
is
total
if for every pair
\lambda\neq\mu
such that
\{\lambda,\mu\}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
for some
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
, either
\lambda\succ\mu
or
\mu\succ\lambda
.
A
completion
of
\succ
is a total priority relation
\succ^{\prime}\ \supseteq\ \,\succ
.

A priority relation
\succ
is
score-structured
if there is
a scoring function
s:\bigcup_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
for every

\{\lambda,\mu\}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
,

\lambda\succ\mu
iff
s(\lambda)>s(\mu)
.

Definition 3
.

A
prioritized database

\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}=(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},\succ)
consists of a database
\mathcal{D}
, a set of universal constraints
\mathcal{C}
, and a priority relation
\succ
for
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
.

We now extend the definitions of optimal repairs to the case of universal constraints.

Definition 4
.

Consider a prioritized database
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}=(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},\succ)
, and let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
.

•

A
Pareto improvement
of
\mathcal{R}
is a database
\mathcal{B}
consistent w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
such that
there is
\mu\in\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})
with
\mu\succ\lambda
for every
\lambda\in\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})\setminus\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B})
.

•

A
global improvement
of
\mathcal{R}

We briefly recall the basics of

Update actions

An
update atom
is of the form
+P(\mathbf{x})
or
-P(\mathbf{x})
where

is a relational atom.
We use
\mathit{fix}

\mathit{fix}(P(\mathbf{x}))=-P(\mathbf{x})

and
\mathit{fix}(\neg P(\mathbf{x}))=+P(\mathbf{x})
.
An
update action
is a ground update atom, i.e., is of the form
-\alpha
or
+\alpha
with
\alpha
a fact.
A set of update actions
\mathcal{U}
is

if
\mathcal{U}
does not contain both
-\alpha
and
+\alpha
for some fact
\alpha
.
The result of applying a consistent set of update actions
\mathcal{U}
on a database
\mathcal{D}
is
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{D}\setminus\{\alpha\mid-\alpha\in\mathcal{U}\}\cup\{\alpha\mid+\alpha\in\mathcal{U}\}
.

An
active integrity constraint
(AIC) takes the form
r=\ell_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\ell_{n}\rightarrow\{A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\}

\mathsf{body}(r)=\ell_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\ell_{n}

\mathit{fix}(\ell_{i})

\ell_{i}

A set of AICs is

A

5.2
From Prioritized Databases to AICs

Given a prioritized database
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
we define the following set of ground AICs:
\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}=\{r_{\mathcal{E}}\mid\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})\}
where

r_{\mathcal{E}}:=\bigwedge_{\lambda\in\mathcal{E}}\lambda\rightarrow\{\mathit{fix}(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\mathcal{E},\forall\mu\in\mathcal{E},\lambda\not\succ\mu\}.

Intuitively,
\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
expresses that conflicts of
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
should be fixed by modifying the least preferred literals
according to
\succ
.

We can prove that Pareto-optimal repairs of
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
coincide with several kinds of repairs of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
.

Proposition 7
.

For every prioritized database
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
,

\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ})=\mathit{JustRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ})=\mathit{GroundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ})=\mathit{FoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ})\subseteq\mathit{WellFoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ})
.

This result is interesting not only because it provides additional evidence for the naturalness of Pareto-optimal repairs, but also because it identifies a class of AICs for which justified, grounded, and founded r-updates coincide. The proof in fact shows that these three notions coincide for every set of ground AICs
\eta
that is
monotone
, i.e. does not contain both a fact
\alpha
and the complementary literal
\neg\alpha
.

We remark that the final inclusion in Proposition
7
may be strict.
This is demonstrated on the next example, which suggests that
well-founded repairs may be too permissive:

Example 5
.

It is possible to construct a prioritized database
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
where

\mathcal{D}=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\}
and
\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}=\{\alpha\wedge\beta\rightarrow\{-\beta\},\ \alpha\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\alpha\},\ \gamma\wedge\delta\rightarrow\{-\gamma\}\}
.
For the AICs
\eta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}
, the r-update
\{-\alpha,-\gamma\}
is well-founded, but not founded,
as the only founded r-update is
\{-\beta,-\gamma\}
.
We argue that
\{-\beta,-\gamma\}
should indeed be preferred to
\{-\alpha,-\gamma\}
, since the first AIC expresses that it is better to remove
\beta
than
\alpha
.

The reduction used to show Proposition
7

is data-dependent and requires us to create potentially exponentially
many ground AICs, one for every conflict.
In the case of
denial constraints
, however, we can give
an alternative data-independent reduction, provided that the priority relation
\succ
is specified in the database.
We thus assume for the next result that
P_{\succ}
is a predicate in
{\bf{S}}
,
that the first attribute of each relation in
{\bf{S}}\setminus\{P_{\succ}\}
stores a unique fact identifier,
and
P_{\succ}
stores pairs of such identifiers.
Then given a set
of denial constraints
\mathcal{C}
over
{\bf{S}}\setminus\{P_{\succ}\}
, we
build a set
\mathit{min}(\mathcal{C})
that is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}
but has
the property that the conflicts of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
are precisely
the images of constraint bodies of
\mathit{min}(\mathcal{C})
on
\mathcal{D}
.
This can be achieved by replacing each
\varphi\rightarrow\bot\in\mathcal{C}
with all refinements
obtaining by (dis)equating variables in
\varphi
with each other, or with constants mentioned in
\mathcal{C}
,
then removing any subsumed constraints.
For example, if
\mathcal{C}=\{R(x,x)\rightarrow\bot,R(x,y)\wedge S(y)\rightarrow\bot\}
,
then
\mathit{min}(\mathcal{C})
contains
R(x,x)\rightarrow\bot
and

R(x,y)\wedge S(y)\wedge x\neq y\rightarrow\bot
, so
\{R(a,a),S(a)\}

is no longer an image of a constraint body.
We then define
\eta^{\mathcal{C}}
as the set of all AICs

\bigl(\ell_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\ell_{n}\wedge\varepsilon\wedge\bigwedge_{\ell_{j}\neq\ell_{i}}\neg P_{\succ}(id_{i},id_{j})\bigr)\rightarrow\{-\ell_{i}\}.

such that
\ell_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\ell_{n}\wedge\varepsilon\rightarrow\bot\in\mathit{min}(\mathcal{C})
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
and for every
1\leq k\leq n
,
\ell_{k}=R(id_{k},\mathbf{t})
for some
R,\mathbf{t}
.

Proposition 8
.

For every set of denial constraints
\mathcal{C}
, database
\mathcal{D}
and priority relation
\succ
of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ})=\mathit{JustRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}})=\mathit{GroundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}})=\mathit{FoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}})\subseteq\mathit{WellFoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}})
.

This reduction could be used for example to transfer data complexity lower bounds for prioritized databases with denial constraints to the setting of AICs.

5.3
Towards Well-Behaved AICs

When translating a prioritized database into AICs, we obtained monotone sets of AICs,
for which most of the different kinds of r-update coincide. Can we generalize this idea to obtain
larger classes of ‘well-behaved’ sets of AICs which share this desirable behavior? This subsection explores
this question and provides some first insights.

We start by defining the following condition, which serves to ensure that
all constraints are made explicit:

Definition 8
.

We say that a set
\eta
of ground AICs is
closed under resolution
if it is consistent,
and for every pair of AICs
r_{1},r_{2}\in\eta
, if there exists
\alpha\in\mathsf{lits}(r_{1})
such that
\neg\alpha\in\mathsf{lits}(r_{2})
,
and
\mathsf{lits}(r_{1})\cup\mathsf{lits}(r_{2})\setminus\{\alpha,\neg\alpha\}
is a consistent set of literals,
then there exists
r_{3}\in\eta
with
\mathsf{lits}(r_{3})=\mathsf{lits}(r_{1})\cup\mathsf{lits}(r_{2})\setminus\{\alpha,\neg\alpha\}
.
A set of AICs
\eta
is closed under resolution if so is
gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
for every database
\mathcal{D}
.

The name ‘closure under resolution’ comes from considering the clauses that correspond to the negation of the rule bodies:
if we have AICs whose clauses are
\neg\alpha\vee\varphi
and
\alpha\vee\psi
, then
we should also have an AIC for their resolvent

\varphi\vee\psi^{\prime}
, corresponding to the implied constraint
\neg\varphi\wedge\neg\psi\rightarrow\bot
.
This property ensures that
\eta
captures all potential conflicts: for every
\mathcal{D}
, if

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}=\{\tau_{r}\mid r\in\eta\}
, then
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\eta})=\{\mathsf{lits}(r)\mid r\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta),\mathcal{D}\not\models r,\text{ and there is no }r^{\prime}\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)\text{ with }\mathsf{lits}(r^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathsf{lits}(r)\}
.

The following example, given by

Example 6

Consider

and
\eta
that contains the AICs:

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle:\neg\alpha\wedge\neg\beta\rightarrow\{+\alpha\}

\displaystyle r_{4}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\neg\gamma\rightarrow\{+\gamma\}

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\neg\beta\rightarrow\{+\beta\}

\displaystyle r_{6}

\displaystyle:\neg\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{+\alpha\}

It is always possible to transform a set of ground AICs into one that is closed under resolution by adding the required AICs. However this may result in an exponential blowup.
Moreover, we need to choose the update actions of the added AICs. We advocate for this to be done by propagating the relevant update actions of the rules on which the resolution is done.
A set of ground AICs obtained in this way will be closed under resolution and will
preserve actions under resolution
according to the following definition.

Definition 9
.

We say that a set
\eta
of ground AICs
preserves actions under resolution
if for every triple of AICs

,
r_{2}
,
r_{3}

\in\eta
,
if there exists
\alpha
such that
\alpha\in\mathsf{lits}(r_{1})
,
\neg\alpha\in\mathsf{lits}(r_{2})
, and
\mathsf{lits}(r_{3})=\mathsf{lits}(r_{1})\cup\mathsf{lits}(r_{2})\setminus\{\alpha,\neg\alpha\}
,
then
\mathsf{upd}(r_{1})\cup\mathsf{upd}(r_{2})\setminus\{+\alpha,-\alpha\}\subseteq\mathsf{upd}(r_{3})
.
A set of AICs
\eta
preserves actions under resolution if so does
gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
for every database
\mathcal{D}
.

The next example shows that a set of AICs which does not preserve actions under resolution may be ambiguous.

Example 7

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}

\eta

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\beta\rightarrow\{-\alpha\}

\displaystyle r_{3}

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle:\beta\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\gamma\}

\displaystyle r_{4}

\displaystyle:\beta\wedge\delta\rightarrow\{-\beta\}

This set of AICs is closed under resolution but does not preserve actions under resolution: due to

and

should be an update action of

should be removed when

Sets of AICs that are closed under resolution and preserve actions under resolution are well behaved in the sense that they make most of the r-update notions coincide. The monotone sets of AICs mentioned in relation to Proposition
7
trivially satisfy these two conditions.

Proposition 9
.

If
\eta
is closed under resolution and preserves actions under resolution, then for every database
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathit{JustRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)=\mathit{GroundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)=\mathit{FoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)\subseteq\mathit{WellFoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)
.

The next example
shows that
both conditions are necessary for obtaining Proposition
9
.

Example 8
.

Consider
\mathcal{D}=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
and the two sets

\displaystyle\eta_{1}=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\wedge\neg\beta\rightarrow\{-\alpha\},\quad\neg\alpha\wedge\beta\rightarrow\{-\beta\},

\displaystyle\quad\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\gamma\}\}

\displaystyle\eta_{2}=

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\cup\{\alpha\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\gamma\},\quad\beta\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\gamma\}\}.

\eta_{1}
is not closed under resolution but (trivially) preserves actions under resolution, while
\eta_{2}
is closed under resolution but does not preserve actions under resolution.

In both cases, there are two founded r-updates:
\{-\gamma\}
and
\{-\alpha,-\beta\}
. However,
\{-\alpha,-\beta\}
is not well-founded, hence not grounded nor justified.
Indeed,
\mathcal{D}
violates only AICs whose only update action is
-\gamma
.

Even if a set of AICs is such that justified, grounded and founded repairs are guaranteed to exist and coincide, its behavior may still be puzzling, as illustrated next.

Example 9
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\}
and
\eta
be the monotone set of AICs comprising the following AICs:

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\delta\rightarrow\{-\delta\}

\displaystyle r_{3}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma\wedge\delta\rightarrow\{-\beta\}

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\delta\rightarrow\{-\alpha\}

\displaystyle r_{4}

\displaystyle:\beta\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\gamma\}

There are four r-updates:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{1}=

\displaystyle\{-\alpha,-\gamma\}\text{ and }\mathcal{U}_{2}=\{-\delta,-\gamma\}\text{ are founded}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{3}=

\displaystyle\{-\delta,-\beta\}\text{ is not founded but is well-founded}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{4}=

\displaystyle\{-\alpha,-\beta\}\text{ is not founded nor well-founded}

There are two conflicts:
\{\alpha,\delta\}
and
\{\beta,\gamma\}
. It is natural to prefer removing
\gamma
rather than
\beta
to resolve the latter conflict (due to
r_{4}
), which would justify to preferring
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
over
\mathcal{U}_{4}
and
\mathcal{U}_{3}
respectively. However, the exact same argument applied to
r_{1}
should lead us to prefer removing
\delta
to solve the first conflict, thus to prefer
\mathcal{U}_{2}
over
\mathcal{U}_{1}
. It is therefore not clear why both
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
should be the preferred r-updates.
The intention of a user specifying the preceding AICs is probably quite far from their actual behavior.

We thus believe that a reasonable property for sets of AICs is to respect the principle that adding atoms to a rule body can only restrict the possible update actions. We call the
anti-normalization
of a set
\eta
of AICs the set
AN(\eta)
of AICs that replace all the AICs
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\in\eta
that share the same body by a single AIC whose update actions are the union of the update actions of
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
.

Definition 10
.

We say that a set
\eta
of ground AICs
preserves actions under strengthening
if for every pair of AICs
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
in
AN(\eta)
, if

\mathsf{lits}(r_{1})\subseteq\mathsf{lits}(r_{2})
, then
\mathsf{upd}(r_{2})\subseteq\mathsf{upd}(r_{1})
.
A set of AICs
\eta
preserves actions under strengthening if so does
gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
for every database
\mathcal{D}
.

The following proposition shows that if
\eta
preserves actions under strengthening, then constraints that have non-minimal bodies have no influence on the r-updates.

Proposition 10
.

Let
\eta
be a set of ground AICs and
\mathit{min}(\eta)
be the set of AICs from
AN(\eta)
that have (subset-)minimal bodies.
If
\eta

preserves actions under strengthening, then for every
\mathcal{D}
, for
X\in\{\mathit{Found},\mathit{WellFound},\mathit{Ground}\}

\mathit{XUp}(\mathcal{D},\eta)=\mathit{XUp}(\mathcal{D},AN(\eta))=\mathit{XUp}(\mathcal{D},\mathit{min}(\eta))
, and
\mathit{JustUp}(\mathcal{D},AN(\eta))=\mathit{JustUp}(\mathcal{D},\mathit{min}(\eta))
.

5.4
From AICs to Prioritized Databases

We next study the possibility of reducing well-behaved sets of AICs to prioritized databases and discuss the differences between the two settings.

Binary conflicts case

We first consider the case where the size of the conflicts is at most two (this covers, for example, AIC bodies corresponding to functional dependencies or class disjointness).
In this case, given a set
\eta
of AICs
closed under resolution that preserves actions under resolution and under strengthening
and a database
\mathcal{D}
, we build a set of constraints
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
and a binary relation
\succ_{\eta}
such that if
\succ_{\eta}
is acyclic, the Pareto-optimal repairs of
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}}_{\succ_{\eta}}
coincide with the founded, grounded and justified repairs of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\eta
.
We take
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}=\{\tau_{r}\mid r\in\eta\}
and define
\succ_{\eta}
so that

\lambda\succ_{\eta}\mu
iff

•

there exists
r\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
such that
\mathcal{D}\not\models r
,
\{\lambda,\mu\}\subseteq\mathsf{lits}(r)
, and
\mathit{fix}(\mu)\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
;
and

•

for every
r\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
such that
\mathcal{D}\not\models r
and
\{\lambda,\mu\}\subseteq\mathsf{lits}(r)
,
\mathit{fix}(\lambda)\not\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
,

where
\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)=\{r\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)\mid\text{ there is no }r^{\prime}\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)

\text{with }\mathsf{lits}(r^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathsf{lits}(r)\}
.
As
\eta
is closed under resolution,
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\eta})=\{\mathsf{lits}(r)\mid r\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta),\mathcal{D}\not\models r\}
.

Proposition 11
.

If
\eta
is closed under resolution, preserves actions under resolution and under strengthening, the size of the conflicts of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\eta
is bounded by 2, and
\succ_{\eta}
is acyclic, then

\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}}_{\succ_{\eta}})=\mathit{JustRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)=\mathit{GroundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)

=\mathit{FoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)\subseteq\mathit{WellFoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)
.

The following examples show that the three first conditions are necessary.

Example 10
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
and
\eta=\{\alpha\wedge\beta\rightarrow\{-\beta\},

\neg\beta\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\gamma\}\}
, which preserves actions under resolution and strengthening but is not closed under resolution. We have
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\eta})=\{\{\alpha,\beta\},\{\alpha,\gamma\}\}
and
\alpha\succ_{\eta}\beta
.
Both
\{\alpha\}
and
\{\beta,\gamma\}
are Pareto-optimal, but the only founded r-update (which is also grounded and justified) is
\{-\beta,-\gamma\}
.

Example 11

(Example
7
cont’d)
.

In Example
7
,
\eta
is closed under resolution and preserves actions under strengthening but not under resolution. We have
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\eta})=\{\{\alpha,\beta\},\{\beta,\gamma\},\{\alpha,\neg\delta\}\}
and

\beta\succ_{\eta}\alpha
,
\beta\succ_{\eta}\gamma
,
\alpha\succ_{\eta}\neg\delta
.
The only Pareto-optimal repair is
\{\beta\}
,
but
\{-\beta,+\delta\}
is a founded, grounded and justified r-update.

Example 12

(Example
9
cont’d)
.

In Example
9
,
\eta
is closed under resolution and
preserves actions under resolution but not under strengthening. We have
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\eta})=\{\{\alpha,\delta\},\{\beta,\gamma\}\}
and

\alpha\succ_{\eta}\delta
,
\beta\succ_{\eta}\gamma
.
The only Pareto-optimal repair is
\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
but
\{-\alpha,-\gamma\}
is a founded, grounded and justified r-update.

Note that
\succ_{\eta}
may be cyclic: if
\eta=\{A(x)\wedge B(x)\rightarrow\{-A(x)\},B(x)\wedge C(x)\rightarrow\{-B(x)\},C(x)\wedge A(x)\rightarrow\{-C(x)\}\}
and
\mathcal{D}=\{A(a),B(a),C(a)\}
, we obtain
A(a)\succ_{\eta}C(a)\succ_{\eta}B(a)\succ_{\eta}A(a)
.

General case

Let us now consider the case where the size of the conflicts is not bounded. If we apply the same reduction, we can only show the following inclusions between repairs of
\mathcal{D}
w.r.t.
\eta
and Pareto-optimal repairs of
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}}_{\succ_{\eta}}
:

Proposition 12
.

If
\eta
is closed under resolution, preserves actions under resolution and under strengthening, and
\succ_{\eta}
is acyclic, then

\mathit{JustRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)=\mathit{GroundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)=\mathit{FoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta)\subseteq\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}}_{\succ_{\eta}})
.

The next example shows that the inclusion may be strict.

Example 13
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\epsilon\}
and
\eta
consist of:

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma\rightarrow\{-\beta\}

\displaystyle r_{3}

\displaystyle:\delta\wedge\epsilon\rightarrow\{-\delta\}

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle:\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\delta\rightarrow\{-\alpha,-\beta\}

We obtain
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\eta})=\{\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\},\{\alpha,\beta,\delta\},\{\delta,\epsilon\}\}
and

\gamma\succ_{\eta}\beta
,
\delta\succ_{\eta}\alpha
,
\delta\succ_{\eta}\beta
and
\epsilon\succ_{\eta}\delta
(note that
\alpha\not\succ_{\eta}\beta
because
-\alpha
is an update action of
r_{2}
).
The repair
\{\beta,\gamma,\epsilon\}
is Pareto-optimal, but the corresponding r-update
\{-\alpha,-\delta\}
is not founded, as
-\alpha
appears only in
r_{2}
and
\mathcal{D}\circ\{-\delta\}\models r_{2}
.

One might try to modify the definition of
\succ_{\eta}
by
dropping the second condition and adding
\mathit{fix}(\lambda)\not\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
to the first. In this case,

\{\beta,\gamma,\epsilon\}
is no longer Pareto-optimal. However, now if we take

\eta^{\prime}=\eta\setminus\{r_{3}\}
, then
\{-\alpha\}
would be founded, but the corresponding repair

\{\beta,\gamma,\delta,\epsilon\}
would not be Pareto-optimal, violating the inclusion of Proposition
12
.

This example shows that even for AICs corresponding to denial constraints,
there is no clear way to define a priority relation
that captures the preferences expressed by the AICs.

6
Conclusion and Future Work

We studied how to incorporate preferences into repair-based query answering
for an expressive setting in which databases are equipped with universal constraints,
and both fact additions and deletions are used to restore consistency.
We showed that the existing framework of prioritized databases could be faithfully adapted to this richer setting, although the proofs are more involved and crucially rely upon finding
the right definition of what constitutes a conflict.
While these results focus on databases, we expect that they will also
prove useful for exploring symmetric difference repairs in related KR settings,
e.g. ontologies with closed predicates.

Our complexity analysis showed that adopting optimal repairs in place of
symmetric difference repairs does not increase the complexity of repair-based
query answering. A major difference between denial and universal constraints
is that the latter may lead to conflicts of unbounded size. We showed that
it is intractable to recognize a conflict and that several problems
drop in complexity if we assume that the conflicts are available. This suggests
the interest of developing structural conditions on constraint sets that ensure
easy-to-compute conflicts, as well as practical algorithms for computing
and updating the set of conflicts, which could enable an integration with
existing SAT-based approaches.

Intrigued by the high-level similarities between prioritized databases and active integrity constraints,
we explored how the two formalisms relate. We exhibited a natural translation
of prioritized databases into AICs whereby Pareto-optimal repairs coincide with founded, grounded and justified repairs w.r.t. the generated set of AICs.
We take this as further evidence that Pareto-optimal repairs are an especially natural
notion (we previously showed that Pareto-optimal (subset) repairs
correspond to stable extensions in argumentation
[
2020
]
). It would be of interest to extend our comparison to other more recent notions of repair updates
for AICs (

,

?

?
;
?
).

Our work also provided new insights into AICs. Existing examples used to
distinguish different notions of r-update often
seem unnatural in some respect. This led us to devise a set of criteria for ‘well-behaved’ AICs,
which provide sufficient conditions for founded, grounded and justified repairs to coincide
(Example

suggests that well-founded repairs
are too permissive).
Even for such restricted AICs, it is not always clear what user intentions are being captured.

Data-independent reduction in denial constraints case

Recall that for the next result, we assume that the

\succ

Concretely, we add a fresh predicate

to

and increase the arity of the predicates in

first argument of each

now stores a unique fact identifier,
while
P_{\succ}
stores pairs of such identifiers.

We now take a set of denial constraints
\mathcal{C}
over
{\bf{S}}\setminus\{P_{\succ}\}

and explain how to build the new set of constraints
\mathit{min}(\mathcal{C})
that allow us to more easily
identify conflicts. The construction of

is done in two steps.

First, we shall transform every

into a set of more specific constraints. It will be
convenient here to have some notations for referring to parts of (sets of) denial constraints: we will use
\mathsf{body}(\tau)
and
\mathsf{lits}(\tau)
for the body and set of literals of
\tau
,

\mathsf{vars}(\tau)
and
\mathsf{terms}(\tau)

\tau

\mathsf{const}(\mathcal{C})
for the constants occurring in
\mathcal{C}
.
Then the set
\textit{refine}(\tau)
of
refinements
of
\tau
contains
all denial constraints that can be obtained
from
\tau
by applying the following operations in order:

1.

choose some partition
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}
of
\mathsf{vars}(\tau)\cup\mathsf{const}(\mathcal{C})
such that each set
T_{i}

in the partition contains at most one constant

2.

for each
T_{i}
that contains a constant

T_{i}
with
c

3.

for each
T_{i}
that does not contain any constant, choose some variable

and replace all occurrences of variables in
T_{i}\setminus\{v\}

v

4.

for every pair of distinct variables

in the modified
\tau

v\neq z

5.

for every variable
v
in the modified
\tau

c\in\mathsf{const}(\mathcal{C})

We then let

\mathcal{D}

.

We show that for every prioritized database

).
Note that for every

and

.

(\mathit{FoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}})\subseteq\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ}))
Let

•

Let

, there exists an injective homomorphism

.

Since

•

.

.

(\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\succ})\subseteq\mathit{FoundRep}(\mathcal{D},\eta^{\mathcal{C}}))
Let

•

Since

and
\ell_{i}

\tau

Since

,
\mathcal{R}

\mathcal{D}
that does not contain any

of

\mathcal{U}
hence

•

Let

,

.

h:\mathsf{terms}(\tau)\rightarrow\mathsf{dom}(\mathcal{D})

\mathcal{E}=h(\tau)

Lemma 11
.

If
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\models\eta
, then
ne(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U})\cup\mathcal{U}
is closed under
\eta
.

Proof.

Let
r\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
be such that
ne(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U})\cup\mathcal{U}
satisfies all non-updatable literals of
r
.
By definition of
ne(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U})
, this implies that
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}
satisfies all non-updatable literals of
r
.
Since
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\models r
, it follows that
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}
does not satisfy some updatable literal
\ell
of
r
.
If
\ell
is positive,
\ell=\beta
, then since
\ell
is updatable
-\beta\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
. Since
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}
does not satisfy
\ell
,
\beta\notin\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}
. If
\beta\notin\mathcal{D}
, then
-\beta\in ne(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U})
. Otherwise
-\beta\in\mathcal{U}
. In both cases,
-\beta\in ne(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U})\cup\mathcal{U}
. If
\ell
is negative,
\ell=\neg\beta
, then since
\ell
is updatable
+\beta\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
. Since
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}
does not satisfy
\ell
,
\beta\in\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}
. If
\beta\in\mathcal{D}
, then
+\beta\in ne(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U})
. Otherwise
+\beta\in\mathcal{U}
. In both cases,
+\beta\in ne(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U})\cup\mathcal{U}
.
Hence

is closed under

.
∎

Lemma 12
.

If
\eta
is closed under resolution, then for every database
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}_{\eta})=\{\mathsf{lits}(r)\mid r\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta),\mathcal{D}\not\models r\}
where
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
is the set of universal constraints that correspond to AICs of
\eta
and
\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
is the subset of
gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
that contains AICs whose bodies are subset-minimal.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{D}\not\models r
iff
\mathcal{D}\models\mathsf{body}(r)
iff
\mathsf{lits}(r)\subseteq\mathit{Lits}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}
.
Hence by Proposition
1
, it is sufficient to show that
\{\mathsf{lits}(r)\mid r\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)\}=\{\mathsf{lits}(r)\mid\mathsf{body}(r)\text{ is a prime implicant of}\bigvee_{\varphi\rightarrow\bot\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{\eta})}\varphi\}

Since
\eta
is closed under resolution, for every implicant
\psi
of
\bigvee_{\varphi\rightarrow\bot\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}_{\eta})}\varphi
, there is an AIC

such that

. Since
\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
retains only the body minimal AICs in

∎

Lemma 13
.

For every set of constraints
\mathcal{C}
, database
\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
,
\mathcal{B}\Delta\mathcal{D}
is a minimal hitting set of
\mathit{MHS}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
, where
\mathit{MHS}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}\in\mathit{SRep}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
.
For every
\mathcal{H}\in\mathit{MHS}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
, since
\mathcal{H}
is a hitting set of

,
(\mathcal{B}\Delta\mathcal{D})\cap\mathcal{H}\neq\emptyset

is a hitting set of
\mathit{MHS}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be a database such that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\Delta\mathcal{D}\subsetneq\mathcal{B}\Delta\mathcal{D}
.
By Lemma
4
,
\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B})\subsetneq\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
.
Moreover, by Proposition
2
,
\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B})
is a maximal subset of
\mathit{Lits}^{\bf{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}
that does not include any conflicts of
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
. Hence there exists
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
such that
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
.
By Proposition
1
,
\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})=\{\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{D}\cup\{\neg\alpha\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{D}\}\mid\mathcal{H}\in\mathit{MHS}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})\}

\mathcal{E}
.
It is easy to check that

, then either

,
\alpha\notin\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}
, or

.
∎

Lemma 14
.

If
\eta
is closed under resolution and preserves actions under resolution, then for every database
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathit{FoundUp}(\mathcal{D},\eta)
,
for every
A\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists
r_{A}\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
such that
A\in\mathsf{upd}(r_{A})
,
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\}\not\models r_{A}
and
\mathcal{D}\not\models r_{A}
.

Proof.

We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
the set of universal constraints that corresponds to
\eta
.
Let
\mathcal{U}\in\mathit{FoundUp}(\mathcal{D},\eta)
.
Let
A\in\mathcal{U}
and let
\ell_{A}
be the literal that is set to true by
A
.

•

Since
\mathcal{U}
is founded and
A\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists
r\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
such that
A\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
(hence
\overline{\ell_{A}}
is a literal of
r
) and
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\}\not\models r
.

•

If
\mathcal{D}\not\models r

\mathcal{D}\models r
.

•

Since
\mathcal{D}\models r
while
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\}\not\models r
, then

does not satisfy all literals of

•

Since
\eta

•

Since

\mathcal{D}
satisfies all literals of

. Thus there is no literal

occur in some

r\in gr_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
such that
A\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
,
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\}\models r
. Let
\ell_{A}
denote the literal that
A
falsifies.

–

Let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}})
such that
\ell_{A}\in\mathcal{E}
and
\ell_{A}\not{\succ_{\eta}}\lambda
for every
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}
. Since
\eta
is closed under resolution, by Lemma
12
,
\mathcal{E}=\mathsf{lits}(r_{\mathcal{E}})
for some
r_{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
such that
\mathcal{D}\not\models r_{\mathcal{E}}
.

*

If
A\in\mathsf{upd}(r_{\mathcal{E}})
, then
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\}\models r_{\mathcal{E}}
by assumption on
A
.

*

Otherwise, if
A\notin\mathsf{upd}(r_{\mathcal{E}})
, let
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathit{fix}(\lambda)\in\mathsf{upd}(r_{\mathcal{E}})
(there must be at least one such
\lambda
otherwise
r_{\mathcal{E}}
has no update actions).
Since
\ell_{A}\not{\succ_{\eta}}\lambda
, by construction of
{\succ_{\eta}}
:

1.

either for every
r\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
such that
\{\ell_{A},\lambda\}\subseteq\mathsf{lits}(r)
and
\mathcal{D}\not\models r
,
\mathit{fix}(\lambda)\notin\mathsf{upd}(r)
;

2.

or there is
r\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
such that
\{\ell_{A},\lambda\}\subseteq\mathsf{lits}(r)
,
\mathcal{D}\not\models r
, and
A\in\mathsf{upd}(r)
.

Since
\{\ell_{A},\lambda\}\subseteq\mathsf{lits}(r_{\mathcal{E}})
,
\mathcal{D}\not\models r_{\mathcal{E}}
, and
\mathit{fix}(\lambda)\in\mathsf{upd}(r_{\mathcal{E}}
), we are not in case (1) so we are in case (2).
Hence, there is
r_{\lambda}\in\mathit{min}_{g}(\eta)
such that
\{\ell_{A},\lambda\}\subseteq\mathsf{lits}(r_{\lambda})
,
\mathcal{D}\not\models r_{\lambda}
, and
A\in\mathsf{upd}(r_{\lambda})
.

Since the size of the conflicts is bounded by
2
,
r_{\lambda}
and
r_{\mathcal{E}}
have the same body:
\ell_{A}\wedge\lambda
.
Hence, since
A\in\mathsf{upd}(r_{\lambda})
,
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\}\models r_{\lambda}
by assumption on
A
.
Hence
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\}\models r_{\mathcal{E}}
.

It follows that
\mathcal{E}=\mathsf{lits}(r_{\mathcal{E}})\not\subseteq\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{U}\setminus\{A\})=\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})\cup\{\ell_{A}\}
.

–

We have thus shown that for every
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}})
such that
\ell_{A}\in\mathcal{E}
, either
\mathcal{E}
contains some
\lambda
such that
\ell_{A}{\succ_{\eta}}\lambda
, or
\mathcal{E}\not\subseteq\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})\cup\{\ell_{A}\}
.

–

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\mathit{Dat}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})\cup\{\ell_{A}\}\setminus\{\mu\mid\ell_{A}{\succ_{\eta}}\mu\})
, so that
\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})=\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})\cup\{\ell_{A}\}\setminus\{\mu\mid\ell_{A}{\succ_{\eta}}\mu\}
by Lemma
3
.
Since
\{\mu\mid\ell_{A}{\succ_{\eta}}\mu\}
intersects every conflict
\mathcal{E}\in\mathit{Conf}(\mathcal{D},{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}})
such that
\ell_{A}\in\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R})\cup\{\ell_{A}\}
, then there is no conflict included in
\mathit{Int}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})
.
By Lemma
7
, it follows that
\mathcal{R}\notin\mathit{PRep}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}}_{\succ_{\eta}})
.

Hence
\mathcal{U}\in\mathit{FoundUp}(\mathcal{D},\eta)
.∎

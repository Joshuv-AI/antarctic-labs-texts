1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold, and let
(F,h_{F})
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
endowed with a Hermitian metric
h_{F}
. If the curvature form

\displaystyle i\Theta(F,h_{F})\geqslant 0
is semi-positive, then for every
(n,q)
-form
v
with values in
F

such that

\bar{\partial}v=0,\qquad\int_{X}\langle[i\Theta(F,h_{F}),\Lambda_{\omega_{X}}]^{-1}v,v\rangle dV<\infty

and whose
L^{2}
norm is finite one can solve the equation

(1.0.1)

\bar{\partial}u=v,

where
u
is an
F
-valued
(n,q-1)
-form whose
L^{2}
–norm is finite. Moreover, the solution
u
of (
1.0.1
) which is orthogonal to

\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\partial})
satisfies very precise
L^{2}
estimates, cf.
[
6
]
.

Consider next an snc divisor
E
on
X
, and the corresponding space of forms with log poles along
E
. Hodge theory results in
[
8
]
,
[
10
]

show that for any logarithmic
(n,q)
-form
v

\bar{\partial}v=0,\qquad v=\partial w

(where the first equality holds pointwise in the complement of the support of
E
and
w
is a
(n,q-1)
log form) there exists a form
u
with log poles along
E
such that

(1.0.2)

\bar{\partial}u=v,

holds on
X\setminus E
. This is the log-version of the familiar
\partial\bar{\partial}
lemma in Hodge theory.

We remark that one could try to obtain (
1.0.2
) as consequence of the
L^{2}
-theory, i.e. by interpreting

v
as
(n,q)
-form with values in
F:=\mathcal{O}(E)
, the line bundle corresponding to
E
. However, the only natural, semi-positively curved metric
h_{F}
we have at hand is singular along
E
, and the
L^{2}
condition we have mentioned above means that we impose
v
to vanish on
E
–in conclusion, we would get a much weaker result.

In this article we are aiming at the following general vanishing theorem, which loosely speaking interpolates between Hodge and
L^{2}
-theory, respectively.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and let
E
be a snc divisor on
X
,
s_{E}
be the canonical section of
E
. Let
(L,h_{L})
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum q_{i}[Y_{i}]+\theta_{L},

where
q_{i}\in]0,1[\cap\mathbb{Q}
,
E+\sum Y_{i}
is snc, and the form
\theta_{L}
is smooth, semi-positive.
Let
\lambda
be a
\bar{\partial}
-closed smooth
(n,q)
-form with value in
L+E
. If there exists
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
, two
L
-valued
(n-1,q)
-form and
(n-1,q-1)
-form with log poles along
E+\sum Y_{i}
respectively, such that

(1.1.1)

\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\beta_{1}+\theta_{L}\wedge\beta_{2}\qquad\text{ on }X\setminus(E+\sum Y_{i}),

then the form
\lambda
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact, i.e,
the class
[\lambda]=0\in H^{q}(X,K_{X}+L+E)
.

As consequence of the methods developed in this paper (especially Theorem
1.1
) and in
[
4
]
we obtain the following injectivity statement, conjectured by O. Fujino long ago (cf.
[
12
]
).

Theorem 1.2
.

[
12
, Conj 2.21]

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
E=\sum E_{i}
be a simple normal crossing divisor on
X
. We consider a semi-positive line
F

bundle on
X
and a holomorphic section
s
of
mF
(for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
). We assume moreover that the zero locus of
s
contains no lc centres of the lc pair
(X,E)
. Then the multiplication map
induced by the tensor product with
s

(1.2.1)

s\otimes:\qquad H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+F)\rightarrow H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+(m+1)F)

is injective for every
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

We refer to
[
16
,
5
]
for some special cases of the above result.

Given the results obtained in
[
15
]
, it would be natural to adapt our method to the case of a singular metric
h_{F}
(with arbitrary singularities). More precisely, we propose the following question.

Question 1.3
.

Let
E=\sum E_{i}
be a simple normal crossing divisor on a compact Kähler manifold
X
and let
F
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
, endowed with a possible singular metric
h_{F}
such that
\displaystyle i\Theta_{h_{F}}(F)\geqslant 0
. We suppose that
\mathcal{I}(h_{F})=\mathcal{I}(h_{F}\cdot e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\log|s_{E}|^{2}})
for every
\varepsilon>0
.

Let
s
be a (holomorphic) section of
mF
(for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
\displaystyle|s|_{h^{m}_{F}}<+\infty
point-wise and the zero locus of
s
contains no lc centres of the lc pair
(X,E)
. Then the multiplication map
induced by the tensor product with
s

(1.3.1)

s\otimes:\qquad H^{q}\big(X,K_{X}\otimes E\otimes F\otimes\mathcal{I}(h)\big)\rightarrow H^{q}\big(X,K_{X}\otimes E\otimes(m+1)F\otimes\mathcal{I}(h^{m+1})\big)

is injective for every
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem
1.1
can be seen as a generalisation of the familiar
\partial\bar{\partial}
lemma. Hence it should be no surprise that Hodge theory plays a key role in its proof.
The corresponding versions of Hodge decomposition theorem needed here, for metrics with Poincaré or conic singularities, respectively will be established in Section
2
. We refer to
[
17
]
and the references therein for statements with a similar flavour.

In this paper we introduce and study the notion of a
(p,q)
-current with values in a singular Hermitian bundle

(L,h_{L})
, cf. Definition
2.30
in Section
3
. The terminology refers to the fact that under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
we are forced to work with a Kähler metric with conic singularities on
X
; moreover, the model for the type of currents we have to manipulate here is provided by the RHS of (
1.1.1
).

The Green operator of such a current
T
is defined by duality

(1.3.2)

\int_{X}\mathcal{G}(T)\wedge\overline{\star\phi}:=\int_{X}T\wedge\overline{\star\mathcal{G}(\phi)},

for any
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form
\phi
, smooth in conic sense –for this important notion, as well for the definition of
\mathcal{G}(\phi)
we refer to
Sections
2
and
3
.

As consequence of our version of Hodge decomposition in Section hodge, we have a decomposition of the current
T
which parallels the case of differential forms

(1.3.3)

T=\mathcal{H}(T)+\Delta^{\prime\prime}\big(\mathcal{G}(T)\big).

where
\mathcal{H}(T)
is the projection of
T
on the space of harmonic,
L
-valued forms of type
(p,q)
.
We call this equality

Kodaira-de Rham decomposition of the current

T
, by analogy with the untwisted case treated in
[
13
]
. Together with the
properties of the current
\mathcal{G}(T)
and an important lemma from
[
14
]
, this is a key technique in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
and interesting in its own right.

A few results from
L^{2}

2.1.
Hodge decomposition for metrics with Poincaré singularities

Let
X
be a
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold, and let
(L,h_{L})
be a
line bundle endowed with a (singular) metric
h_{L}=e^{-\varphi_{L}}
. The terminology in this section is as follows.

(a)

We say that the metric
h_{L}
has log poles, if its local weights can be written as follows

\varphi_{L}\equiv\sum a_{i}\log|f_{i}|^{2}

modulo a smooth function, where
a_{i}
are rational numbers and
f_{i}
are holomorphic functions.

(b)

If the metric
h_{L}
has log poles, then we will consistently write

\displaystyle i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum a_{i}[Y_{i}]+\theta_{L}

where
\theta_{L}
is a smooth form.

We consider a modification
\pi:\widehat{X}\to X
of
X
such that the support of the singularities of

\varphi_{L}\circ\pi
is a simple normal crossing divisor
E
. As usual, we can construct
\pi
such that its restriction to
\widehat{X}\setminus E
is a biholomorphism . We write

(2.0.1)

\varphi_{L}\circ\pi|_{\Omega}\equiv\sum_{\alpha=1}^{p}e_{\alpha}\log|z_{\alpha}|^{2}

modulo a smooth function. Here
\Omega\subset\widehat{X}
is a coordinate subset, and

(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha=1,\dots,n}
are coordinates such that

E\cap\Omega=(z_{1}\dots z_{p}=0)
.

Let
\widehat{\omega}_{E}
be a complete Kähler metric on
\widehat{X}\setminus E
,
with Poincaré singularities along
E
, and let

(2.0.2)

\omega_{E}:=\pi_{\star}(\widehat{\omega}_{E})

be the direct image metric. We note that in this way
(X_{0},\omega_{E})
becomes a
complete Kähler manifold, where
X_{0}:=X\setminus(h_{L}=\infty)
.

The following statement is well-known, but we recall the precise version we need here.
We denote by
s
the section of a line bundle for which the support of its zero divisor coincides with the support of
(h_{L}=\infty)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist a family of smooth functions
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
with the following properties.

(a)

For each
\varepsilon>0
, the function
\mu_{\varepsilon}
has compact support in
X_{0}
, and

0\leqslant\mu_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1
.

(b)

The sets
(\mu_{\varepsilon}=1)
are providing an exhaustion of
X_{0}
.

(c)

There exists a positive constant
C>0
such that
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{X_{0}}\left(|\partial\mu_{\varepsilon}|_{\omega_{E}}^{2}+|\partial\bar{\partial}\mu_{\varepsilon}|^{2}_{\omega_{E}}\right)\leqslant C.

Proof.

It is enough to obtain the corresponding statement on
\widehat{X}
, so that the divisor
E
is snc. Then we take

(2.1.1)

\mu_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}\Big(\log\log\frac{1}{|\widehat{s}|^{2}}\Big)

where
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is equal to 1 on the interval
[1,\varepsilon^{-1}]
and is equal to zero on

[1+\varepsilon^{-1},\infty]
. Also, we denote by
|\widehat{s}|
the inverse image of the norm
of the section
s
with respect to an arbitrary, smooth metric.
Actually we can also impose the condition that

(2.1.2)

\max_{j=1,\dots N}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\rho^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}|<C_{N}<\infty,

for any positive integer
N
.
The properties (a)-(c) are then verified by a simple computation.
∎

In this context we have the following statement, which is a slight
generalization of the usual result in
L^{2}
Hodge theory.

Theorem 2.2
.

Consider a line bundle
(L,h_{L})\to X
endowed
with a metric
h_{L}
such that the requirement
(a)
above is satisfied and assume that
\theta_{L}\geqslant 0
on
X
. Let
(X_{0},\omega_{E})
be the corresponding complete Kähler manifold
cf. (
2.0.2
). Then the following assertions are true.

(i)

We have the Hodge decomposition for
(n,q)
forms, i.e.

L^{2}_{n,q}(X_{0},L)={\mathcal{H}}_{n,q}(X_{0},L)\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}^{\star},

where
{\mathcal{H}}_{n,q}(X_{0},L)
is the space of
L^{2}

\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
-harmonic
(n,q)
-forms with respect to
(\omega_{E},h_{L})
.

(ii)

Let
u
be an
L
-valued
\bar{\partial}
-closed
L^{2}
-form of type
(n,q)
on
X_{0}
, and
assume that we
have
u=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}w
, where
w\in L^{2}_{n-1,q}(X_{0},L)
. Then there exists
v\in L^{2}_{n,q-1}(X_{0},L)
such
that
u=\bar{\partial}v
.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
, which we give below, makes use of the
following statement which is the
\bar{\partial}
-version of the Poincaré inequality established in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
p\leqslant n
be an integer. There exists a positive constant
C>0
such
that the following inequality holds

(2.3.1)

\int_{X_{0}}|u|^{2}_{\omega_{E}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\omega_{E}}\leqslant C\int_{X_{0}}|\bar{\partial}u|^{2}_{\omega_{E}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\omega_{E}}

for any
L
-valued form
u
of type
(p,0)
which belongs to the domain of
\bar{\partial}
and which is orthogonal to the
space of
L^{2}
holomorphic
(p,0)
–forms. Note that here
\theta_{L}
is arbitrary.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

The first observation is that we can assume from the very beginning that the polar set

h_{L}=\infty
has snc support. Indeed, via the map
\pi
the hypothesis and the conclusion of
our Poincaré inequality transform as follows.

\bullet
The form

(2.3.2)

\widehat{u}=\pi^{\star}u

on
\widehat{X}
is
\pi^{\star}L
-valued and
L^{2}
with respect to
\widehat{\omega}_{E}
and
\pi^{\star}h_{L}
.

\bullet
The form
\widehat{u}
is orthogonal on the space of
L^{2}
–harmonic
(p,0)
forms with values in
\pi^{\star}L
. Indeed, given any
\pi^{\star}L
-valued form of

(p,0)
-type
\widehat{\gamma}
on
\widehat{X}\setminus E
there exists a form
\gamma
on

X\setminus Z
such that

(2.3.3)

\widehat{\gamma}=p^{\star}\gamma

because
p:\widehat{X}\setminus E\to X\setminus Z
is a biholomorphism. Also,
p
establish an
isometry between
(\widehat{X}\setminus E,\widehat{\omega}_{E})

and
(X\setminus Z,\omega_{E})
respectively. The same is true for the pairs

(p^{\star}L,p^{\star}h_{L})
and
(L,h_{L})
, so our claim is clear.

This means that it is enough to assume that
\widehat{X}=X
, i.e. the metric
\omega_{E}
has
Poincaré singularities along an snc divisor
E
which coincides with the support of the analytic set
h_{L}=\infty
.

The following statement represents an important step towards the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be some co-ordinate open set of
X
and
let
\tau
be a
L
-valued
(p,0)
-form with compact support
in
\Omega
. Then we have

(2.4.1)

\int_{\Omega}|\tau|^{2}_{\omega_{E}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\omega_{E}}\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}|\bar{\partial}\tau|^{2}_{\omega_{E}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\omega_{E}}

where
C
is a constant.

The arguments in
[
3
]
, Lemma 1.10 correspond to the case
p=0
and
\varphi_{L}=0
. For the general case we need the
following auxiliary statement.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\delta
be an arbitrary real number. Then there exists
x_{\delta}<1
such that the following holds.
For any smooth function
f
defined on the closure of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathbb{C}
, such that
there exists
\varepsilon>0
with the property that
f(z)=0
on the set
\{|z|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cup\{|z|\geqslant 1-x_{\delta}\}
, we have

(2.5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{2}\frac{d\lambda(z)}{|z|^{2\delta}}\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{z}}\right|^{2}|z|^{2-2\delta}\log^{2}|z|d\lambda(z)

as well as

(2.5.2)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{2}\frac{d\lambda(z)}{|z|^{2+2\delta}\log^{2}|z|}\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{z}}\right|^{2}\frac{d\lambda(z)}{|z|^{2\delta}}.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Let
x_{\delta}<1
be a positive real number whose expression will be determined in what follows. Let
v:[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function whose support is contained in the interval
[\varepsilon,x_{\delta}]
for some positive, small enough
\varepsilon
.

We claim that the following inequalities hold

(2.5.3)

\int_{0}^{1}|v|^{2}t^{k}dt\leqslant C\int_{0}^{1}|v^{\prime}|^{2}t^{k+2}\log^{2}tdt

as well as

(2.5.4)

\int_{0}^{1}|v|^{2}t^{k}\frac{dt}{\log^{2}t}\leqslant C\int_{0}^{1}|v^{\prime}|^{2}t^{k+2}dt

where
k
is any element of the set
\delta+\mathbb{Z}
, and
C
is a numerical constant (in particular independent of
v,\varepsilon,k,...
).

We first check that (
2.5.3
) holds true: we have

(2.5.5)

-\int_{0}^{1}|v|^{2}t^{k+1}\log^{2}d\left(\frac{1}{\log t}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\log t}\frac{d}{dt}\left(v^{2}t^{k+1}\log^{2}t\right)dt

and this equals

(2.5.6)

2\int_{0}^{1}(\overline{v}v^{\prime}+v\overline{v^{\prime}})t^{k+1}\log tdt+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{k}v^{2}}{\log t}\left((k+1)\log^{2}t+2\log t\right)dt.

Now two things can happen: either
k+1=0
, and then the second term in (
2.5.6
) is equal to twice the quantity we are interested in, or
k+1\neq 0
. Note that in the latter case it is possible to fix
x_{\delta}
uniformly with respect to
k
so that

(2.5.7)

|(k+1)\log^{2}t+2\log t|\geqslant 2|\log t|

for any
t\in[0,x_{\delta}]
precisely because
k\in\delta+\mathbb{Z}
.

As a consequence, we have

(2.5.8)

\left|\int_{0}^{1}(\overline{v}v^{\prime}+v\overline{v^{\prime}})t^{k+1}\log tdt\right|\geqslant\int_{0}^{1}t^{k}v^{2}dt.

for any
v
of compact support in
]0,x_{\delta}[
.

Moreover, we have

(2.5.9)

\left|\int_{0}^{1}(\overline{v}v^{\prime}+v\overline{v^{\prime}})t^{k+1}\log tdt\right|^{2}\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}|v^{\prime}|^{2}t^{k+2}\log^{2}tdt\int_{0}^{1}|v|^{2}t^{k}dt.

by Cauchy-Schwarz, so the inequality (
2.5.3
) is settled.

The arguments for (
2.5.4
) are completely similar: we have

(2.5.10)

\int_{0}^{1}|v|^{2}t^{k}\frac{dt}{\log^{2}t}=\int_{0}^{1}|v|^{2}\frac{t^{k+1}}{\log^{2}t}d\log t

which equals

(2.5.11)

-\int_{0}^{1}{\log t}\frac{d}{dt}\left(|v|^{2}\frac{t^{k+1}}{\log^{2}t}\right)dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|v|^{2}t^{k}}{\log^{2}t}\left(2+(k+1)\log\frac{1}{t}\right)dt-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{k+1}}{\log t}(v^{\prime}\overline{v}+v\overline{v^{\prime}})dt.

and two things can happen. If
k+1\geqslant 0
, then we have
\displaystyle 2+(k+1)\log\frac{1}{t}\geqslant 2
for any
t\in[0,1]
and (
2.5.4
) follows.
For each element
k\in\delta+\mathbb{Z}
such that
k+1<0
we can assume that

(2.5.12)

(k+1)\log\frac{1}{t}+2\leqslant\frac{1}{2}

for any
t\in[0,x_{\delta}]
by imposing a supplementary (but uniform) condition on
x_{\delta}
if necessary, and we proceed exactly as we did for (
2.5.3
).

Therefore, we obtain (
2.5.3
) and (
2.5.4
) for any

k\in\delta+\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma
2.5
follows from this: we consider the Fourier series

(2.5.13)

f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}(t)e^{\sqrt{-1}k\theta}

of
f
, and then we have

(2.5.14)

\frac{\partial v}{\bar{\partial}z}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(a_{k}^{\prime}(t)-\frac{k}{t}a_{k}(t)\right)e^{\sqrt{-1}k\theta}.

The identity

(2.5.15)

t^{k}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{a_{k}}{t^{k}}\right)=a_{k}^{\prime}(t)-\frac{k}{t}a_{k}(t)

reduces the proof of our statement to inequalities of type (
2.5.3
) and (
2.5.4
) which are already established. In conclusion, Lemma
2.5
is completely established.
∎

Proof of Proposition

We can suppose that the intersection

In (

Now for the

\displaystyle\frac{|\bar{\partial}\tau_{I}|_{g}^{2}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{g}}{d\lambda}=

(2.5.18)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\left|\frac{\partial f_{I}}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}\right|^{2}\frac{e^{-\varphi_{L}}}{\prod_{\alpha=p+1}^{r}|z_{\alpha}|^{2}\log^{2}|z_{\alpha}|^{2}}

and the observation is that for every
\xi\in\Omega
there exist some index

for which

We use (
2.5.3
) or (
2.5.4

In general, if
\tau

\mu_{\varepsilon}
in Lemma

. We apply the above estimation for
\mu_{\varepsilon}\tau
. Note that the constant
C

Now we finish the proof of Theorem

: if a positive constant as in (

It follows that the weak limit

The last step in the proof is to notice that the considerations above contradict the fact that the

Corollary 2.6
.

In the setting of Theorem

2.2.
Hodge decomposition for metrics with conic singularities, I

We discuss in this section the analog of Theorem

2.2
for metrics with conic singularities. This will be important for the applications we are aiming at.
There are two possibilities to deal with this sort of problems: either we approximate our metrics with complete ones (e.g. with Poincaré
singularities), or directly, by using local uniformisations. In this subsection we use the former approach and in the next subsection we present the later.

Since most of the arguments in the proof of Theorem
2.2
will be used here, we will skip a few details. In the last part of this section we offer a few comments about the
L^{2}
estimates in this class of singularities. The setting of this subsection is as follows.

Setting

),

(independent of
\varepsilon
) such that for any
L
-valued
(p,0)
form
u
with support in
U
we have the inequality

(2.12.6)

\frac{1}{C}\int_{U}|u|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}\leqslant\int_{U}|\bar{\partial}u|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}+\int_{U\setminus\frac{1}{2}U}|u|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}.

We will not prove (
2.12.6
) here, because it follows directly from the
arguments invoked for Proposition
2.4
.

The second fact is that the space of
L^{2}
holomorphic

p
-forms is independent of
\varepsilon\geqslant 0
.
Let
\Phi
be a holomorphic
p
–form on
X_{0}
with values in
L
.
If
\Phi
is
L^{2}
with respect to
(\omega_{Y,\varepsilon},h_{L})
, it is also
L^{2}
integrable with respect to
(\omega_{Y},h_{L})
because of the inequality

(2.12.7)

\|\Phi\|_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}^{2}\geqslant\|\Phi\|_{\omega_{Y}}^{2}.

We next assume that
\Phi
is
L^{2}
with respect to
(\omega_{Y},h_{L})
. In local coordinates this writes as

(2.12.8)

\int_{\Omega}\sum|\Phi_{I}(z)|^{2}\prod_{j\not\in I}\lambda_{j}(z)e^{-\varphi_{L}(z)}d\lambda(z)<\infty

where
\lambda_{j}
are the eigenvalues of
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to the Euclidean metric on
\big(\Omega,(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\big)
. We take the coordinates
z
so that they are adapted to
(X,Y)
, i.e.
\Omega\cap Y=z_{1}\dots z_{r}=0
.
It follows that the holomorphic functions
\Phi_{I}
are divisible by

(2.12.9)

\prod_{i\leqslant r,i\in I}z_{i}^{\lfloor q_{i}\rfloor}\prod_{i\leqslant r,i\not\in I}z_{i}^{\lfloor 1+q_{i}-\frac{1}{m}\rfloor}

cf. notations at the beginning.
Recall that we have chosen
m
so that

(2.12.10)

\left\lfloor q_{i}-\frac{1}{m}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor q_{i}\right\rfloor,

for each non-integer coefficient
q_{i}
which appears in the expression of the metric
h_{L}
.

So summing up we see that by the careful choice of the singularities of the metric
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
cf.
(ii)
, for any holomorphic form
\Phi
satisfying
\|\Phi\|_{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}^{2}<\infty
, we have

(2.12.11)

\|\Phi\|_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}^{2}<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|\Phi\|_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}^{2}=\|\Phi\|_{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}^{2}.

The rest of the proof is absolutely similar (i.e. by contradiction) to the case
of Theorem
2.3
: we suppose by contradiction that there exists a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
such that
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}=1
,
u_{\varepsilon}
is orthogonal to the space of
L^{2}
-holomorphic forms with respect to
\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}
and

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|\bar{\partial}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}=0
. By passing to some subsequence,
u_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly to some
(p,0)
-form
u
on
X_{0}
. Then
\|u\|_{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}\leqslant 1
and
\bar{\partial}u=0
. Therefore
u
is a holomorphic
L^{2}
-form on
X
. Since
u_{\varepsilon}
is orthogonal to the holomorphic forms, we have
\int_{X}\langle u,u_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}=0
. Together with (
2.12.11
), we obtain
u=0
. Then we obtain a contradiction by the same arguments as in Theorem
2.3
combined with (
2.12.6
).
∎

We prove next Theorem
2.11
.

Proof.

Let
\xi
be any
L^{2}
-form of type
(n,q)
with values in
(L,h_{L})
(with respect to the metric
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
). Then we also have

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}<\infty
and thus we write

(2.12.12)

\xi=\xi_{\varepsilon}+\bar{\partial}(\eta_{\varepsilon})+\bar{\partial}^{\star}(\tau_{\varepsilon}),

where
\xi_{\varepsilon}
is harmonic,
\eta_{\varepsilon}
is orthogonal to
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\partial})
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}
orthogonal on
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{\star})
. We have

(2.12.13)

\|\xi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}\leqslant\|\xi\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}\leqslant\|\xi\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y}}

and therefore the limit of
(\xi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
will be harmonic with respect to

\omega_{\mathcal{C}},h_{L}
. Moreover, thanks to Theorem
2.12
, we have

(2.12.14)

\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}\leqslant C\|\bar{\partial}\eta_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}\leqslant C\|\xi\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y}}

and thus we can extract a limit of
(\eta_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
on compacts of
X_{0}
.

In order to deal with the family
(\tau_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
, thanks to the uniform estimate of Theorem
2.12
, the proof of Corollary
2.6
applies. We get thus a constant
C>0
so that

(2.12.15)

\|\tau_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}\leqslant C\|\bar{\partial}^{\star}(\tau_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}}

and the RHS of (
2.12.15
) is bounded uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon
. We can therefore take the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
and we get
\xi_{0}
harmonic,
\eta_{0}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\bar{\partial})
and
\tau_{0}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\bar{\partial}^{\star})
such that

(2.12.16)

\xi=\xi_{0}+\bar{\partial}\eta_{0}+\bar{\partial}^{\star}\!(\tau_{0}).

To prove that
\mathcal{H}_{n,q}(X_{0},L),\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}
and
\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}^{*}
are orthogonal with each other, we use the partition of unity and the local covering. For example, let
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{n,q}(X_{0},L)
and
\eta_{0}\in L^{2}
. Let
\theta_{i}
be a partition of unity. We have

\int_{X_{0}}\langle\xi_{0},\bar{\partial}\eta_{0}\rangle=\sum_{i}\int_{X_{0}}\langle\xi_{0},\bar{\partial}(\theta_{i}\eta_{0})\rangle.

Let
\pi_{i}:V_{i}\rightarrow U_{i}
be a local ramified cover such that
\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
is smooth and

Supp\theta_{i}\Subset U_{i}
. Then

\int_{X_{0}}\langle\xi_{0},\bar{\partial}(\theta_{i}\eta_{0})\rangle=\int_{U_{i}}\langle\xi_{0},\bar{\partial}(\theta_{i}\eta_{0})\rangle=\frac{1}{r}\int_{V_{i}}\langle\pi_{i}^{*}\xi_{0},\bar{\partial}(\pi_{i}^{*}(\theta_{i}\eta_{0}))\rangle_{\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}=0,

where the last equality comes from the fact that
\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
is smooth on
V_{i}
and

Supp\theta_{i}\Subset V_{i}
.

To conclude the proof, it remains to check (
(ii)
). Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{n,q}(X_{0},L)
(with respect to the conic metric). Then
\xi
is still
L^{2}
with respect to
\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon
. We consider the Hodge decomposition of
\xi
with respect to
\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}
:

\xi=\xi_{\varepsilon}+\bar{\partial}\eta_{\varepsilon}+\bar{\partial}^{\star_{\varepsilon}}\tau_{\varepsilon}.

Then
\xi_{\varepsilon}
is harmonic with respect to the complete metric
\omega_{Y,\varepsilon}
.
In particular, by using Bochner, we know that

(2.12.17)

\bar{\partial}\xi_{\varepsilon}=\bar{\partial}^{\star_{\varepsilon}}\xi_{\varepsilon}=(D^{\prime})^{\star_{\varepsilon}}\xi_{\varepsilon}=0.

By passing to some subsequence, the above argument implies that
\xi_{\varepsilon},\eta_{\varepsilon}
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}
converge to some
\xi_{0},\eta_{0}
and
\tau_{0}
which are
L^{2}
with respect to
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
and we have

\xi=\xi_{0}+\bar{\partial}\eta_{0}+\bar{\partial}^{\star}\tau_{0}.

As we proved that
\mathcal{H}_{n,q}(X_{0},L),\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}
and
\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{\partial}^{*}
are orthogonal with each other, we obtain that
\xi=\xi_{0}
. Together with (
2.12.17
), (
(ii)
) is proved.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

The decomposition

L^{2}_{n,q}=\mathrm{Ker}\Delta^{\prime\prime}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta^{\prime\prime}

with respect to the Poincaré type/conic metrics, still holds, as we will see next. Given our previous results, all we need to prove is the image of
\Delta^{\prime\prime}

is closed.

We prove it for the Poincaré case, and the conic case is similar.
If (by contradiction) this is not true, then there exists a sequence of smooth
f_{i}
of compact support such that

(2.13.1)

\|f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=1,\qquad\|\Delta^{\prime\prime}f_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0.

Moreover, each of the forms
f_{i}
is orthogonal to the space of harmonic forms, i.e. to
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\partial})
and
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{\star})
.

As consequence of (
2.13.1
) we have

(2.13.2)

\langle f_{i},\Delta^{\prime\prime}f_{i}\rangle\rightarrow 0

as
i\to\infty
. On the other hand, we have
\langle f_{i},\Delta^{\prime\prime}f_{i}\rangle=\|\bar{\partial}f_{i}\|^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}^{*}f_{i}\|^{2}
, and this leads to a contradiction as follows. We can write

(2.13.3)

f_{i}=\bar{\partial}\alpha_{i}+\bar{\partial}^{\star}\beta_{i},

according to Theorem
2.2
. Moreover, it follows from the proof of Theorem
2.2
that

(2.13.4)

\|f_{i}-\bar{\partial}\alpha_{i}\|^{2}\leqslant C\|\bar{\partial}f_{i}\|^{2},\qquad\|f_{i}-\bar{\partial}^{\star}\beta_{i}\|^{2}\leqslant C\|\bar{\partial}^{\star}f_{i}\|^{2}

since
f_{i}-\bar{\partial}\alpha_{i}
is orthogonal to
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\partial})
and
f_{i}-\bar{\partial}^{\star}\beta_{i}
is orthogonal to
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{\star})
. Adding the two inequalities shows that

(2.13.5)

\|f_{i}\|^{2}\leqslant C(\|\bar{\partial}f_{i}\|^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}^{\star}f_{i}\|^{2}),

which is impossible as
i\to\infty
.

2.3.
Hodge decomposition for metrics with conic singularities, II

The setting here will be the same as in the previous subsection
2.2
.
We will use systematically the ramified covers corresponding to the orbifold structure

(2.13.6)

\Delta:=\sum_{i\in I}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)Y_{i}

in order to establish a generalization of Theorem
2.11
.

Let
\displaystyle(V_{i},z_{i})_{i\in I}
be a finite cover of
X
with coordinate charts such that

(2.13.7)

z_{i}^{1}\dots z_{i}^{r}=0

is the local equation of the divisor
Y
when restricted to the
set
V_{i}
. We then consider the local ramified maps

(2.13.8)

\pi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i},\qquad\pi_{i}(w_{i}^{1},\dots,w_{i}^{n}):=\left((w_{i}^{1})^{m},\dots,(w_{i}^{r})^{m},w_{i}^{r+1},\dots w_{i}^{n}\right)

which define the orbifold structure corresponding to
(X,\Delta)
.

We first define the analog of smooth forms in our geometric context.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\phi
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
form of
(p,q)
–type with values in
L
defined on
X_{0}:=X\setminus Y
. We say that
\phi
is smooth in conic
sense if the quotient of the local inverse images

(2.14.1)

\phi_{i}:=\frac{1}{w_{i}^{qm}}\pi_{i}^{\star}(\phi|_{V_{i}})

admit a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-extension to
U_{i}
. In order to simplify the writing, in the equality above we are using the notation

w_{i}^{qm}:=(w_{i}^{1})^{q_{1}m}\cdots(w_{i}^{r})^{q_{r}m}
.

In the absence of the twisting bundle
L
, this corresponds to the usual definition of “orbifold differential forms”. Here in some sense the idea is the same, except that we take into account the singularities of the metric
h_{L}
of
L
. In what follows we will establish a few simple
properties.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\phi
be a smooth
(n,q)
-form on
X
with value in
L
in the standard sense, and we suppose that
q_{i}<1
for every
i
. Then
\phi
is smooth in conic sense.

Proof.

The matter is immediate, via a local calculation. This is based on the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{d(z^{m})}{z^{qm}}

is smooth (recall that
qm
is an integer).
∎

Remark 2.16
.

Let
\phi
be a smooth
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form on
X
. Even if
q_{i}\in]0,1[
for every
i
, it is possible that
\phi
is not smooth in conic sense. But we will show later that it defines a current in conic sense.

The following statement establishes a correspondence between the
intrinsic differential operators associated to the data
(X,\omega_{\mathcal{C}})
and
(L,h_{L})
and the local ones.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\phi
be a
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form, smooth in orbifold sense.
Then its ”natural” derivatives
D^{\prime}_{h_{L}},D_{h_{L}}^{\star},\cdots
are also smooth in the orbifold sense. Moreover, we have:

(1)

\sup_{X\setminus Y}|\phi|_{h_{L},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}}<\infty
, i.e. forms which are smooth in conic sense are bounded.

(2)

The following equalities hold true

\pi_{i}^{\star}(D^{\prime}\phi)=w_{i}^{qm}D^{\prime}\phi_{i};\qquad\pi_{i}^{\star}(\bar{\partial}\phi)=w_{i}^{qm}\bar{\partial}\phi_{i},

as well as

\pi_{i}^{\star}({D^{\prime}}^{\star}\phi)=w_{i}^{qm}{D^{\prime}}^{\star}\phi_{i};\qquad\pi_{i}^{\star}(\bar{\partial}^{\star}\phi)=w_{i}^{qm}\bar{\partial}^{\star}\phi_{i}

where the
D^{\prime}
on the LHS is
\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}
and the rest of the notations are
D^{\prime}\psi:=\partial\psi-\partial(\varphi_{L,0}\circ\pi_{i})\wedge\psi
and
\varphi_{L}=\sum q_{i}\log|z_{i}|^{2}+\varphi_{L,0}
.

(3)

Let
\Delta^{\prime\prime}:=[\bar{\partial},\bar{\partial}^{\star}]
be the Laplace with respect to
(\omega_{\mathcal{C}},h_{L})
. Then we have

\pi_{i}^{\star}\Delta^{\prime\prime}\phi=w_{i}^{qm}\cdot\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}\phi_{i},

where
\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}
is the Laplace for the local, non-singular setting
(\pi_{i}^{\star}\omega_{\mathcal{C}},\varphi_{L,0}\circ\pi_{i})

Proof.

The first inequality is clear; for the rest we will only discuss the first equality of (2), since the verification of all the others is completely analogue. Also, we will drop the index
i
in order to simplify the notations. By definition, locally on
V
we have

(2.17.1)

D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\phi=\partial\phi-\Big(\partial\varphi_{L,0}+\sum q_{\alpha}\frac{dz_{\alpha}}{z_{\alpha}}\Big)\wedge\phi

and its pull-back to
U
via the map
\pi
is equal to

(2.17.2)

\pi^{\star}(D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\phi)=\partial\pi^{\star}\phi-\Big(\partial\varphi_{L,0}\circ\pi+m\sum q_{\alpha}\frac{dw_{\alpha}}{w_{\alpha}}\Big)\wedge\pi^{\star}\phi.

The equality

(2.17.3)

\frac{1}{w^{mq}}\pi^{\star}(D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\phi)=\partial\frac{\pi^{\star}\phi}{w^{mq}}-\partial(\varphi_{L,0}\circ\pi)\wedge\frac{\pi^{\star}\phi}{w^{mq}}

follows immediately and the RHS equals precisely
\displaystyle D^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{w^{qm}}\pi^{\star}\phi\Big)
.
∎

Example 2.18
.

The terminology we are using could be sometimes misleading. Let
L
be the trivial bundle
over
X=\mathbb{D}
, endowed with the metric
\varphi_{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\ln|z|
.
Then the form
\displaystyle\frac{dz}{z}
defined over the pointed disk is smooth in the sense of our Definition
2.14
. Moreover, its
\bar{\partial}
is also smooth in
conic sense: the requirement is that
\displaystyle w\bar{\partial}\Big(\frac{dw}{w}|_{\mathbb{D}^{\star}}\Big)

extends over the disk and this is obviously the case.

A first consequence of the statement
2.17
is that forms which are smooth in conic sense behave well by integration by parts.

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be an
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form and an
L^{\star}
-valued
(n-p-1,n-q)
form, respectively, both smooth in conic sense.
Then the usual integration by parts formula holds true

(2.19.1)

\int_{X}D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{p+q+1}\int_{X}\alpha\wedge D^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{\star}}\beta.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be the family of truncation functions corresponding to
Y
. We obviously have

(2.19.2)

\int_{X}D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}(\mu_{\varepsilon}\alpha)\wedge\beta=(-1)^{p+q+1}\int_{X}\mu_{\varepsilon}\alpha\wedge D^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{\star}}\beta

for each positive
\varepsilon
. The integrals
\displaystyle\int_{X}\mu_{\varepsilon}\alpha\wedge D^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{\star}}\beta
and
\displaystyle\int_{X}\mu_{\varepsilon}D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\alpha\wedge\beta

are converging to the expected value by Lebesgue theorem, since

(2.19.3)

\sup_{X\setminus Y}(|D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\alpha|+|\alpha|+|D^{\prime}_{h_{L}^{\star}}\beta|+|\beta|)<\infty

(cf. Proposition
2.17
above). The remaining term is bounded by

(2.19.4)

\int_{X}|\partial\mu_{\varepsilon}|dV_{\mathcal{C}}

up to a uniform constant –because of (
2.19.3
)– and this converges to zero
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

The following corollary is very useful.

Corollary 2.20
.

Let
\phi
be a
L
-valued
L^{2}
-form on
X
such that
\Delta^{\prime\prime}(\phi)=\psi
holds in the sense of currents on
X
for some form
\psi
smooth in conic sense. Then
\phi
is smooth in conic sense.

Proof.

Let
V\subset X
be a coordinate open subset, such that we have
a local ramified cover
\pi:U\rightarrow V
given by the orbifold structure.
By hypothesis we have

(2.20.1)

\int_{V}\phi\wedge\overline{\Delta^{\prime\prime}\tau}e^{-\varphi_{L}}=\int_{V}\psi\wedge\overline{\tau}e^{-\varphi_{L}}

for any
L
-valued form
\tau
with compact support in
V
, smooth in orbifold sense.
The Laplace operator in (
2.20.1
) is induced by
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
and
h_{L}
.

We first prove that

(2.20.2)

\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\phi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho)}=\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\psi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\rho}

for any smooth form
\rho
of compact support in
U
.

In fact, the
m
-ramified cover
\pi:U\rightarrow V
induces the Galois group
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}
which are the automorphisms
\gamma_{i}:U\rightarrow U
invariant over
V
.
Since
\pi^{*}\psi
is
\gamma_{i}
-invariant, we have

\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\psi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\rho}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\int_{U}\pi^{\star}(\psi)\wedge\gamma_{i}^{*}\frac{\overline{\rho}}{w^{mq}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\int_{U}\pi^{\star}(\psi)\wedge\gamma_{i}^{*}(\overline{w^{mq}\cdot\rho})\cdot e^{-\pi^{*}\varphi_{L}}

=\frac{1}{m}\int_{U}\pi^{\star}(\psi)\wedge\overline{G}\cdot e^{-\pi^{*}\varphi_{L}},

where
G:=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}^{*}(w^{mq}\rho)
is
\pi
-invariant and the quotient
\frac{G}{w^{mq}}
is smooth by construction. By definition,
G
is the pull back of some form
\tau
on
V
, smooth in conic sense.
Therefore we have

(2.20.3)

\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\psi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\rho}=\int_{V}\psi\wedge\overline{\tau}\cdot e^{-\varphi_{L}}.

For the term
\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\phi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho)}
, we do the same argument:

\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\phi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho)}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\int_{U}\pi^{\star}(\phi)\wedge\gamma_{i}^{*}\frac{\overline{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho)}}{w^{mq}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\int_{U}\pi^{\star}(\phi)\wedge\gamma_{i}^{*}(\overline{w^{mq}\cdot\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho)})\cdot e^{-\pi^{*}\varphi_{L}}

=\frac{1}{m}\int_{U}\pi^{\star}(\phi)\wedge\overline{H}\cdot e^{-\pi^{*}\varphi_{L}},

where
H:=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}^{*}(w^{mq}\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho))
is
\pi
-invariant and the quotient
\frac{H}{w^{mq}}
is smooth. Then there exists a form
\theta
on
V
smooth in conic sense such that
H=\pi^{*}\theta
.
Then

(2.20.4)

\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\phi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho)}=\int_{V}\phi\wedge\overline{\theta}\cdot e^{-\varphi_{L}}.

Since
\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{sm}
commute with
\gamma_{i}
, we obtain
H=w^{qm}\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{sm}(\frac{G}{w^{qm}})
. Together with Proposition
2.17
,
we know that
\theta=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\tau
on
V
. Combining this with (
2.20.3
), (
2.20.4
) and (
2.20.1
), we obtain finally

\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\phi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\rho)}=\int_{U}\frac{\pi^{\star}(\psi)}{w^{mq}}\wedge\overline{\rho}.

Therefore, the equality

(2.20.5)

\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\widehat{\phi})=\frac{\pi^{\star}(\psi)}{w^{m}q}

holds in the sense of distributions on
U
, where
\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}:=\frac{\pi^{\star}(\phi)}{w^{mq}}
is moreover
L^{2}
. The corollary follows.
∎

Another consequence is the following version of Bochner formula.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
\phi
be a
L
-valued
(p,q)
-form, smooth in orbifold sense.
Then the equality

(2.21.1)

\Delta^{\prime\prime}\phi=\Delta^{\prime}\phi+[\theta_{L},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}]\phi

holds pointwise on
X\setminus Y
. Moreover the following Bochner formula holds.

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\bar{\partial}\phi|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\mathcal{C}}+\int_{X}|\bar{\partial}^{\star}\phi|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\mathcal{C}}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}|D^{\prime}\phi|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\mathcal{C}}+\int_{X}|D^{\prime\star}\phi|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\mathcal{C}}

(2.21.2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{X}\langle[\theta_{L},\omega_{\mathcal{C}}]\phi,\phi\rangle e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\mathcal{C}}

Proof.

On every local ramified cover
\pi_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow V_{i}
, thanks to Proposition
2.17
, we have

\pi_{i}^{\star}(\Delta^{\prime\prime}\phi)=w_{i}^{qm}\cdot\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}\phi_{i}.

Using the same type of arguments we infer that we have

\pi_{i}^{\star}(\Delta^{\prime}\phi)=w_{i}^{qm}\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{sm}\phi_{i}.

Together with the usual Bochner equality
\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}=\Delta^{\prime}_{sm}+[i\Theta_{\pi^{\star}\varphi_{0}},\pi_{i}^{\star}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}]
, we obtain (
2.21.1
).

In order to obtain the integral identity, we first see that the formula holds if we replace
\phi
with
\mu_{\varepsilon}\phi
, where the cut-off function
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is defined in Lemma
2.1
.
Since the error terms e.g. of type

(2.21.4)

\int_{X}\langle\bar{\partial}\mu_{\varepsilon}\wedge\phi,\bar{\partial}\phi\rangle e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV_{\mathcal{C}}

tend to zero, we are done. Note that here we are using the fact that the function
|\phi||\bar{\partial}\phi|e^{-\varphi_{L}}
is bounded (and not only
L^{1}
).
∎

Yet another corollary states as follows.

Corollary 2.22
.

Let
\tau
be a smooth
(n,q)
-form with values in
(L,h_{L})
and support in
X\setminus Y
.
We consider the (usual) orthogonal decomposition

(2.22.1)

The usual Gårding and Sobolev inequalities together with Rellich embedding theorem still hold in conic setting. They are established by their usual versions via the local uniformisations of

. We will state and comment Gårding inequality, but prior to doing that, we introduce the analogue of Sobolev spaces in our context.

Definition 2.26
.

We denote by
W^{k}_{p,q}(X,L)
the space of
L^{2}
forms
u
of type
(p,q)
with values in
L
, such that its pull back

\displaystyle\frac{\pi_{i}^{\star}u}{w_{i}^{qm}}
via the ramified coverings belongs to the usual Sobolev space
W^{k}(U_{i})
. The Sobolev norm of
W^{k}_{p,q}(X,L)
is obtained by a partition of unit and the usual Sobolev norm of the local forms
\displaystyle\frac{\pi_{i}^{\star}u}{w_{i}^{qm}}
.

Given a form
u\in W^{k}_{p,q}(X,L)
, we can approximated it by smooth forms
u_{\varepsilon}
in the sense of conic. In fact, by using the partition of unity, it is sufficient to consider the case when
u
is supported in some open set
V_{i}
. Let
z
be the coordinate on
U_{i}
. Let
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\|w\|^{2})
be the standard convolution function. Let
G
be the Galois group of the covering
\pi:U_{i}\rightarrow V_{i}
and let
\gamma\in G
. Then
\rho_{\varepsilon}(|w|^{2})
is
G
-invariant. Assume for simplicity that
p=q=0
; we then consider the function

v_{\varepsilon}(w):=\frac{(w^{-qm}\cdot\pi^{\star}u)\star\rho_{\varepsilon}}{w^{-qm}}.

Note that
\gamma(w)^{-qm}=c_{\gamma}\cdot w^{-qm}
for some constant
c_{\gamma}
independent of
w
. Then

v_{\varepsilon}(\gamma(w))=\frac{\int_{U_{i}}(\gamma(w)-\tau)^{-qm}\cdot\pi^{\star}u(\gamma(w)-\tau)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}(\|\tau\|^{2})dV_{\tau}}{\gamma(w)^{-qm}}

=\frac{c_{\gamma}\int_{U_{i}}(w-\gamma^{-1}(\tau))^{-qm}\cdot\pi^{\star}u(w-\gamma^{-1}(\tau))\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}(\|\tau\|^{2})dV_{\tau}}{c_{\gamma}\cdot w^{-qm}}

=\frac{c_{\gamma}\int_{U_{i}}(w-\tau)^{-qm}\cdot\pi^{\star}u(w-\tau)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}(\|\gamma(\tau)\|^{2})dV_{\gamma(\tau)}}{c_{\gamma}\cdot w^{-qm}}=v_{\varepsilon}(w),

where we use the fact that
\pi^{*}u
,
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\|w\|^{2})
and
dV_{w}
are
G
-invariant.
Therefore
v_{\varepsilon}
is
G
-invariant and we can find some form
u_{\varepsilon}
on
U_{i}
such that
v_{\varepsilon}=\pi^{*}u_{\varepsilon}
. Then
u_{\varepsilon}
is smooth in conic sense and
\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{W^{k}_{p,q}}\rightarrow 0
.

The general case of an arbitrary
(p,q)
–form is similar, obtained by regularising locally its coefficients.

We have the following version of Gårding theorem.

Theorem 2.27
.

Let
\Delta^{\prime\prime}:=[\bar{\partial}^{\star},\bar{\partial}]
be the Laplace operator induced by
(h_{L},\omega_{\mathcal{C}})

\|u\|_{k+2}\leqslant C_{k}(\|\Delta^{\prime\prime}u\|_{k}+\|u\|_{0}).

Proof.

We first observe that this is a direct consequence of the usual Gårding inequality if the form

has compact support contained in one of the sets

. Moreover, the “dictionary” established in Proposition
2.17

shows that one could define the space

-valued forms induced by

and
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}
. The general case is obtained by a partition of unit argument.

By Theorem

Since harmonic forms are in particular smooth

Remark 3.4
.

If moreover

We have the following compatibility relation between the notions we have introduced above.

Lemma 3.5

-form smooth in conic sense. We have

\int_{X}\mathcal{G}(s)\wedge t=\int_{X}s\wedge{\mathcal{G}(t)}.

Proof.

Note first that, since

Coming back to the case of a current induced by a form

In order to prove the point (1), our first remark is that

\int_{X}|\mathcal{G}(\Phi)|^{2}e^{-\varphi}\leqslant C\int_{X}|\Phi-\mathcal{H}(\Phi)|^{2}e^{-\varphi}

\mathcal{H}(\Phi)

norm of

. The smoothness of

By the definition of the current

.

The first part of the point (2) (i.e. the equation satisfied by the form

in (

]
, Theorem 7.1 we obtain

To prove the point

, let

The equation (

\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{sm}(\Xi\cdot v_{i})=\Xi\cdot u_{i}+\mathcal{L}(v_{i})

where

By Sobolev embedding theorem we infer that

It follows from (

Finally, the usual argument –by using a Fourier expansion– implies that

Let

We consider the convolution

of

with the usual regularisation kernel and as usual we can assume that this functions are 1-periodic. We then write

and by hypothesis, together with the

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=-N}^{N}f_{\varepsilon,k}(y)\right|^{2}\leqslant 2\sum_{k=-N}^{N}k^{2}|f_{\varepsilon,k}(y)|^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k^{2}}

and integrating.

We next write
\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=f_{\varepsilon}(0,y)+x\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{1}f_{\varepsilon}(tx,y)dt

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\frac{1}{|x|}\left|\partial_{1}f_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\right|^{2}d\lambda.

By Hölder inequality the expression (

(3.6.13)

and on the other hand, for each fixed

(3.6.14)

\frac{1}{C}\left(\int_{|x|<1}\left|\partial_{1}f_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\right|^{2+2\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}\leqslant\int_{|x|<1}\left|\partial_{1}f_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\right|^{2}dx+\int_{|x|<1}\left|\partial_{11}f_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\right|^{2}dx

The arguments for the general case are completely similar –modulo the fact that the number of indexes is greater– and we will not provide any further detail.

d=0
,
L_{I_{0}}\varphi:=\bar{\partial}^{\star}\mathcal{G}(\varphi)
on
X
.
For every sequence
I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}
fixed, the residue
\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta})
is defined by the following way:

(4.2.8)

\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}):=\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}\Big(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k-1}}}\big(\cdots\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{1}}}(\widehat{\Theta})\big)\Big).

Now we can state the key lemma:

Lemma 4.3
.

In the setting of Theorem
4.1
, set
\widehat{\Theta}:=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\beta_{1}+\theta_{L}\wedge\beta_{2}
. Let
\varphi
be a
\bar{\partial}
-closed
(0,n-q)
-form on
X
with values in the dual of
L
, which moreover is smooth in conic sense. We assume that
\varphi
vanishes along
E
.
Then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

(4.3.1)

\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta})\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ L_{I_{k-2}}\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi=0,

and

(4.3.2)

\int_{X}\widehat{\Theta}\wedge\varphi=0.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
k
from large to small. For
k=n+1
, it is automatically true since
E_{I_{n+1}}
is empty. We suppose that the lemma holds for
k+1
. Now we prove the lemma for
k
.

First of all, we have

(4.3.3)

\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta})\wedge\gamma=0

for any harmonic
\gamma\in C^{\infty}_{(0,n-k-1)}(E_{I_{k}},L^{\star})
. Indeed, we know that the residue
\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta})
obtained by iterating the construction we have recalled before the lemma
4.2
can be written as

\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta})=D^{\prime}(\beta_{k})+\theta_{L}\wedge\alpha_{k}

for some forms
\beta_{k},\alpha_{k}
with log poles. Then Lemma
4.2
implies (
4.3.3
).

Then the deRham-Kodaira decomposition implies that the equality

\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta})=\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))+\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))

holds. As a consequence, we obtain

\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta})\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ L_{I_{k-2}}\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi=A+B,

where

A:=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi

and

B:=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi.

We would like to prove that
A=B=0
.

We first estimate
A
. Since
\mathcal{G}
commutes with
\bar{\partial}
, together with the relation (
4.1.8
), we have

A=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}\bar{\partial}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi

=-\sum_{I_{k+1}\subset I_{k}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k+1}}}\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k+1}}}(\widehat{\Theta})\wedge L_{I_{k}}\circ L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi=0.

where the last equality is a consequence of the induction hypothesis.

Next, we estimate the quantity
B
. We have

B=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi

=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi).

By applying the deRham-Kodaira decomposition (
3.2.5
) to
(L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi)|_{E_{I_{k-1}}}
, we obtain

\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi)=\gamma+(L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi)|_{E_{I_{k-1}}}+L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{k-2}}\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi),

where
\gamma
is a
\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{E_{I_{k-1}}}
-harmonic
(0,n-k+1)
-form on
E_{I_{k-1}}
with values in the restriction of the dual of
L
.

We next estimate the integral involving the first two terms.

\bullet

The term involving

\gamma
. Since
\gamma
is a harmonic
(0,n-k+1)
-form on
E_{I_{k-1}}

with values in
L^{\star}
, it follows that
D^{\prime}\gamma=0
and
\displaystyle\theta_{L}|_{E_{I_{k}}}\wedge\gamma=0
on
E_{I_{k-1}}
. These
crucial equalities obviously are still verified by restriction one level deeper, to
E_{I_{k}}
. We infer that the restriction

\gamma|_{E_{I_{k}}}

is still harmonic and
thus

\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge\gamma=0.

\bullet

The term involving

L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi
. The sum

\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge(L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi)

equals zero as soon as
k-1\geqslant 1
: for every
I_{k}\subset I_{k-2}
fixed,
I_{k-2}\setminus I_{k}
contains only two elements. By construction of
\mathrm{Res}_{I_{k}}
(
4.2.8
), the alternate sum for
I_{k-1}
satisfying
I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset I_{k-2}
equals to
0
.

It follows that if
k-1\geqslant 1
, we obtain

B=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi).

Now we can repeat the same argument for
\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi)
as long as
k\geqslant 3
.
The only difference is to treat the integral involving the harmonic forms
\gamma
on
E_{I_{k-2}}
: we use the fact that
\mathcal{G}_{I_{k-1}}(\gamma|_{E_{I_{k-2}}})=0
. We finally get

B=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{0}}\varphi)

\cdots=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{1}}\circ\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{0}}(\varphi)).

Concerning the term
\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{0}}(\varphi))=\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{\star}\mathcal{G}(\varphi)
, we consider the Hodge decomposition

\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{\star}\mathcal{G}(\varphi)=\gamma+\varphi-\bar{\partial}^{\star}\mathcal{G}(\bar{\partial}\varphi)=\gamma+\varphi,

where
\gamma
is harmonic. Since
\varphi=0
on
E
, we have
L_{I_{1}}(\varphi)=0
.

We obtain

\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ L_{I_{k-2}}\circ\cdots\circ\bar{\partial}(L_{I_{0}}(\varphi))

=\sum_{I_{k}\subset I_{k-1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{0}}\int_{E_{I_{k}}}\bar{\partial}^{\star_{I_{k}}}\mathcal{G}_{I_{k}}(\mathrm{Res}_{E_{I_{k}}}(\widehat{\Theta}))\wedge L_{I_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ L_{I_{1}}(\gamma+\varphi)=0.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

It is important and challenging to refine further Theorem
4.1
and
solve the equation
\lambda=\bar{\partial}f

with estimates
. A quick inspection of our arguments shows that one ”only” needs to understand on what the constant
C

in (
4.1.4
) depends. This might not be the easiest thing to do, given the proof (by contradiction) for the existence of this constant.

4.2.
Application: an injectivity theorem

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
E=\sum E_{i}
be a simple normal crossing divisor on a compact Kähler manifold
X
and
F
be a holomorphic line
bundle on
X
with a smooth metric
h_{F}
such that
i\Theta_{h_{F}}(F)\geqslant 0
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
and consider a section
s\in H^{0}(X,mF)
such that the zero locus
s
contains no lc centers of the lc pair
(X,E)
. Then the multiplication map induced by the tensor product with
s

(4.5.1)

s\otimes:\qquad H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+F)\rightarrow H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+(m+1)F)

is injective for every
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
s_{E}
be the canonical section of
E
. After some desingularisation, by the assumption on the zero locus of
s
, we can suppose that

\mathrm{Div}(s)=\sum a_{i}F_{i},

and
\sum F_{i}+E
is snc. Indeed, consider a birational map
\pi:\widehat{X}\to X
such that the inverse image of

E+\mathrm{Div}(s)
is snc. Given a
(n,q)
-form
\lambda

with values in
E+F
, the
\pi
inverse image of
\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}
is a
(n,q)
-form with log-poles along the support of the inverse image of
E
, i.e. we have

(4.5.2)

\pi^{\star}\left(\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}\right)=\frac{\widehat{\lambda}}{s_{\widehat{E}}}

where
\widehat{\lambda}
is a
(n,q)
form on
\widehat{X}
with values in
\widehat{E}+\pi^{\star}(F)
. Another way to express this is the familiar equality

(4.5.3)

G+\pi^{\star}(K_{X}+E)=K_{\widehat{X}}+\widehat{E}

where
G
is effective and the components of
\widehat{E}
are projecting onto the
log-canonical centers of
(X,E)
(since we are only blowing up submanifolds which are transverse to
E
). In particular, the components of the support of
\widehat{E}
is disjoint from the inverse image of
\mathrm{Div}(s)
.

Now if we are able to show that
\widehat{\lambda}
is

\bar{\partial}
-exact, then we write

(4.5.4)

\pi^{\star}\left({\lambda}\right)=\frac{\widehat{\lambda}}{s_{\widehat{E}}}\otimes\pi^{\star}(s_{E}).

The RHS is holomorphic, since
\widehat{E}
is reduced and its support is
contained in the inverse image of
E
. It follows that the inverse image of
\lambda
is zero in cohomology and then
the same is true for
\lambda
.

We suppose for simplicity that
\mathrm{Div}(s)=aF_{1}+bF_{2}
(the general case follows from the same argument, by using the remark after Lemma
4.7
). Let
s_{i}
and
s_{E}

be the canonical section of
F_{i}
and of
E
, respectively.
One of the important points in our proof is to construct a sequence

(a_{k},b_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with the following properties.

(1)

We have
(a_{0},b_{0})=(0,0),(a_{N},b_{N})=(a,b)
and for each
k\geqslant 0
the successive differences
a_{k+1}-a_{k},b_{k+1}-b_{k}
are elements of the set
\{0,1\}
.

(2)

The bundle
F_{k}:=F+a_{k}F_{1}+b_{k}F_{2}
admits a metric
\displaystyle h_{k}
whose local weights can be written as

\varphi_{k}=c\varphi_{F}+c_{1}\log|f_{1}|^{2}+c_{2}\log|f_{2}|^{2}

where
h_{F}=e^{-\varphi_{F}}
is the non-singular, semi-positively curved metric on
F
, the constant
c
is positive and
c_{i}\in]0,1[
for
i=1,2
.

Such sequence will be constructed in Lemma
4.7
. We denote by

(4.5.5)

s_{k}:=s_{1}^{(a_{k+1}-a_{k})}\otimes s_{2}^{(b_{k+1}-b_{k})}\in H^{0}(X,(a_{k+1}-a_{k})F_{1}+(b_{k+1}-b_{k})F_{2}).

The property (1) above shows that (
4.5.1
) can written as composition of the maps

(4.5.6)

s_{k}:H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+F_{k})\rightarrow H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+F_{k+1}).

Thus, in order to conclude it is sufficient to prove that (
4.5.6
) is injective for every
k
.

Let
\lambda
be a smooth form in
H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+F_{k})
such that

\lambda\otimes s_{k}=0\in H^{q}(X,K_{X}+E+F_{k+1}).

In other words there exists a smooth form
\alpha\in C^{\infty}_{(n,q-1)}(X,E+F_{k+1})
such that

s_{k}\otimes\lambda=\bar{\partial}\alpha.

By property (2) we can equip
F_{k}=F+a_{k}F_{1}+b_{k}F_{2}
with the metric
h_{k}
. Now since the weight of the
metric
h_{k}
contains a singular part
c_{i}\log|f_{i}|^{2}
for some rational number
0<c_{i}<1
and it follows from Lemma
4.6
below that we can find forms
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
with log poles along
E+F
and
\theta
with log poles along
E
, such that the equality

(4.5.7)

\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}=\bar{\partial}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{s_{E}s_{k}}\Big)=D^{\prime}_{h_{k}}(\beta_{1})+\bar{\partial}\theta+i\Theta_{h_{k}}\wedge\beta_{2}

holds true on
X\setminus(E+F_{1}+F_{2})
.
Therefore

(4.5.8)

\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}-\bar{\partial}\theta=D^{\prime}_{h_{k}}(\beta_{1})+i\Theta_{h_{k}}\wedge\beta_{2}

in the complement of
E+F_{1}+F_{2}
. Since
\theta
is smooth near a generic point of
F_{1}+F_{2}
, the forms on the LHS of (
4.5.8
) are non-singular at the generic point of
F_{1}+F_{2}
, we are precisely in the framework of Theorem
1.1
.

We infer that

\displaystyle\lambda-s_{E}\bar{\partial}\theta

is
\bar{\partial}
-exact. Since
\theta
has by construction only simple poles along
E
,
\bar{\partial}(\theta)\otimes s_{E}=\bar{\partial}(\theta\cdot s_{E})
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact. Then
\lambda
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact and (
4.5.6
) is injective. The theorem is thus proved, modulo the two lemmas below.
∎

To complete our proof, we establish next the auxiliary statements we used; we start with the existence of forms
\beta_{i},\theta
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
F_{1}+F_{2}+E
be a snc divisor on
X
and let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
with a possible singular metric
h_{L}
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=c_{1}[F_{1}]+c_{2}[F_{2}]+\theta_{L}

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are non zero and
\theta_{L}
is smooth. Let
A
be a
L
-valued smooth
(n,0)
-form on
X\setminus(E+F_{1}+F_{2})
with log poles along
E+F_{1}+F_{2}
. Then we can find
forms
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
with at most log poles along
E+F_{1}+F_{2}
, and
\theta
with at most log poles along
E
, such that the equality

\bar{\partial}A=\bar{\partial}\theta+D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}(\beta_{1})+i\Theta_{h_{L}}\wedge\beta_{2}

holds in the complement of the support of
E+F_{1}+F_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\Omega_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
be a cover of
X
and let
\theta_{\alpha}
be a partition of unity with respect to the covering. We suppose that
F_{1}+F_{2}
on
\Omega_{\alpha}
are defined by
z_{1,\alpha}\cdot z_{2,\alpha}=0
.
We define a smooth vector field

V_{1}:=\sum_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1,\alpha}}\in C^{\infty}(X,T_{X}).

Now on any local open set
\Omega\subset X
, we suppose that
F_{1}+F_{2}
is defined by
z_{1}\cdot z_{2}=0
. Then
V_{1}
is of type:

V_{1}|_{\Omega}=(z_{1}+\mathcal{O}((z_{1})^{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+\mathcal{O}(z_{1}z_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}+\sum_{i\geqslant 3}a_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}.

Then there is a constant
c
such that
\displaystyle A_{1}:=A-cD^{\prime}_{h_{L}}(V_{1}\rfloor A)
is smooth on
X\setminus(E+F_{2})
and simple pole along
E+F_{2}
.
Moreover, by construction,
V_{1}\rfloor A
has no poles along
F_{1}
and it has at most log poles along
E+F_{2}
. Note that we used the fact that
A
is a
(n,0)
-form where
n=\dim X
.

Now

\bar{\partial}(A)=\bar{\partial}(A_{1})+c\bar{\partial}(D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}(V_{1}\rfloor A))=\bar{\partial}(A_{1})+D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\bar{\partial}(cV_{1}\rfloor A)+i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)\wedge(cV_{1}\rfloor A)

pointwise on
X\setminus(E+F_{1}+F_{2})
.
By construction,
\bar{\partial}(cV_{1}\rfloor A)
and
cV_{1}\rfloor A
have log poles
E+F_{2}
.

We repeat the same argument for
A_{1}
to remove its pole along
F_{2}
. The resulting forms are
\theta
with log poles along
E
and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
with log poles along
E+F
so our lemma proved.
∎

Finally, the construction of the sequence
(a_{k},b_{k})_{k=0,\dots,N}
with the two properties (1) and (2) can be done as follows.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
F
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
. Let
s\in H^{0}(X,mF)
such that
\mathrm{Div}(s)=aF_{1}+bF_{2}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
.
We consider a sequence
\{(a_{k},b_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{N}^{2}
constructed as follows:

(i)

We define
(a_{0},b_{0})=(0,0)
. For
k\geqslant 1
, if
(a_{k},b_{k})=(a,b)
, then we stop at this step. If not, we take
c_{k}:=\min\{\frac{a_{k}}{a},\frac{b_{k}}{b}\}
.

(ii)

If
\frac{a_{k}}{a}>c_{k}
, then we set
a_{k+1}:=a_{k}
and if

\frac{a_{k}}{a}=c
, we define
a_{k+1}:=a_{k}+1
.

(iii)

We do the same for
b_{k+1}
: if
\frac{b_{k}}{b}>c_{k}
, then

b_{k+1}:=b_{k}
and if
\frac{b_{k}}{b}=c
, then we set
b_{k+1}:=b_{k}+1
.

Then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(4.7.1)

0=\min\{a_{k}-c_{k}a,b_{k}-c_{k}b\}\leqslant\max\{a_{k}-c_{k}a,b_{k}-c_{k}b\}<1.

In particular, there exist
\displaystyle c_{1,k},c_{2,k}\in]0,1[\cap\mathbb{Q}
and
d_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
such that

(4.7.2)

F+a_{k}F_{1}+b_{k}F_{2}\equiv d_{k}F+c_{1,k}F_{1}+c_{2,k}F_{2}.

Proof.

We prove (
4.7.1
) by induction. By the definition of
c_{k}
, we have

\min\{a_{k}-c_{k}a,b_{k}-c_{k}b\}=0

so it is enough to establish the strict inequality for
\max
.

Assume that (
4.7.1
) holds for
k
. Note first that by construction,
c_{k+1}>c_{k}
. There are three cases to consider.

\bullet

c_{k}=\frac{a_{k}}{a}=\frac{b_{k}}{b}
. Then
a_{k+1}-c_{k}a=b_{k+1}-c_{k}b=1
. As
c_{k+1}>c_{k}
, we know that (
4.7.1
) holds for
k+1
.

\bullet

c_{k}=\frac{a_{k}}{a}<\frac{b_{k}}{b}
. Then
a_{k+1}=a_{k}+1
and
b_{k+1}=b_{k}
. Therefore

a_{k+1}-c_{k+1}a=a_{k}+1-c_{k+1}a=1+(c_{k}-c_{k+1})a<1,

and

b_{k+1}-c_{k+1}b=b_{k}-c_{k+1}b<b_{k}-c_{k}b<1.

Then (
4.7.1
) holds for
k+1
.

\bullet

c_{k}=\frac{b_{k}}{b}<\frac{a_{k}}{a}
. We argue as in the second case.

For the second part of the lemma, thanks to (
4.7.1
), we have

F+a_{k}F_{1}+b_{k}F_{2}\equiv(1+c_{k}m)F+(a_{k}-c_{k}a)F_{1}+(b_{k}-c_{k}b)F_{2}.

Let
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
small enough. Then

F+a_{k}F_{1}+b_{k}F_{2}\equiv(1+c_{k}m-\varepsilon m)F+(a_{k}-c_{k}a+\varepsilon a)F_{1}+(b_{k}-c_{k}b+\varepsilon b)F_{2}.

Thanks to (
4.7.1
), for
\varepsilon
small enough, we know that

(a_{k}-c_{k}a+\varepsilon a)
and
(b_{k}-c_{k}b+\varepsilon b)\in]0,1[
. Moreover
(1+c_{k}m-\varepsilon m)>0
.
Then (
4.7.2
) is proved.
∎

In general
(s=0)
may have several components,

\mathrm{Div}(s)=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant s}a_{i}F_{i}
. The construction of the sequence
\{(a_{1,k},\cdots,a_{s,k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is absolutely identical.

Indeed, suppose that
(a_{1,k},\cdots,a_{s,k})
has already been constructed, for some
k\geqslant 1
. The next element
(a_{1,k+1},\cdots,a_{s,k+1})
of our sequence is obtained as follows:
let

c_{k}=\min\Big\{\frac{a_{1,k}}{a_{1}},\frac{a_{2,k}}{a_{2}},\cdots,\frac{a_{s,k}}{a_{s}}\Big\}.

We define

a_{i,k+1}:=a_{i,k}+1

in case
\displaystyle\frac{a_{i,k}}{a_{i}}=c_{k}
, and

a_{i,k+1}:=a_{i,k}

if
\frac{a_{i,k}}{a_{i}}>c_{k}.
One can easily verify by induction that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
the relation

(4.7.3)

0=\min\{a_{i,k}-c_{k}a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}\leqslant\max\{a_{i,k}-c_{k}a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}<1

holds, precisely as we did in the case

.

5.
Appendix

We detail here the calculations needed for the evaluation of the limit (
4.2.7
) (and discussion of the examples). They are rather long, but after all, nothing more than Taylor expansions. In connection to these topics, we are referring to the very nice article
[
1
]
and the references therein.

As a ”warm-up” we consider the following particular case.

(\diamondsuit)

Assume that we can find a smooth metric
h_{0}
on
\mathcal{O}(Y)
whose local weights
\varphi_{0}
are
independent
on the

z_{1},\dots,z_{r}
, where the

\displaystyle(z_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
are coordinates such that
\varphi_{L}(z)=\sum q_{i}\log|z_{i}|^{2}+\varphi_{L,0}.

In such case we argue as follows.
Consider the following measure

(5.0.1)

d\lambda_{f,q}(z):=\frac{f(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{k_{i}}}\frac{d\lambda(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2-2\delta_{i}}}

where the
\delta_{i}
are strictly positive reals and
k_{i}
are positive integers.

Thanks to
(\diamondsuit)
we immediately see that the quantity

(5.0.2)

\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\left(\prod|z_{i}|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{0}(z^{\prime})}\right)d\lambda_{f,q}(z)

where
z^{\prime}=(z_{r+1},\dots z_{n})

tends to zero, regardless to the size of the multiplicities
q_{i}
. This is the case, since

f
is smooth, so (
5.0.2
) amounts to the evaluation of

(5.0.3)

\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)}g(z^{\prime})\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\left(\prod|z_{i}|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{0}(z^{\prime})}\right)\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{m_{i}}\overline{z}_{i}^{m_{\overline{i}}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{k_{i}}}\frac{d\lambda(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}|z_{i}|^{2-2\delta_{i}}}

where
g
is a smooth function only depending of the variables
z^{\prime}
.

The integral (
5.0.3
) is
equal
to zero -for any
\varepsilon>0
- unless we have

(5.0.4)

m_{i}-k_{i}=m_{\overline{i}}\geqslant 0.

But for such exponents the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of (

k_{i}
disappears, and
\delta_{i}>0
.

5.0.1.
Taylor expansions

In general, i.e. in the absence of the assumption
(\diamondsuit)
, our arguments are virtually the same, modulo a few technicalities that we discuss next.

We will use
Taylor formula with integral reminder. In order to illustrate the type of
statement we are after, we first start with a simple case.

Let
\phi
be a smooth function defined on
\mathbb{R}
, and consider

(5.0.5)

f(x_{1},x_{2}):=\phi(x_{1}+x_{2})

a function defined say in a ball centred at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The Taylor expansion of
f
with respect to
x_{1}
reads as follows

(5.0.6)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\phi(x_{2})+x_{1}\phi^{\prime}(x_{2})+\dots+\frac{x_{1}^{k}}{k!}\phi^{(k)}(x_{2})

(5.0.7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{(k+1)}(tx_{1}+x_{2})(1-t)^{k}dt.

Next we expand each of the functions involved in (
5.0.6
) and (
5.0.7
) with respect to
x_{2}
, and we have:

(5.0.8)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant m_{1},m_{2}\leqslant k}\frac{x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}\phi^{(m_{1}+m_{2})}(0)

(5.0.9)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant m_{1}\leqslant k}\frac{x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{k+1}}{(k+1)!m_{1}!}\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{(m_{1}+k+1)}(tx_{2})(1-t)^{k}dt

(5.0.10)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant m_{2}\leqslant k}\frac{x_{1}^{k+1}x_{2}^{m_{2}}}{(k+1)!m_{2}!}\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{(k+1+m_{2})}(tx_{1})(1-t)^{k}dt

(5.0.11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{k+1}x_{2}^{k+1}}{(k+1)!^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{(2k+2)}(t_{1}x_{1}+t_{2}x_{2})(1-t_{1})^{k}(1-t_{2})^{k}dt_{1}dt_{2}.

Now we remark that in the expressions (
5.0.8
)-(
5.0.11
) we have three type of terms, according to the exponents of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, respectively.

(1)

If both exponents
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are smaller than
k
, then
the function
\phi^{(m_{1}+m_{2})}(0)
is constant with respect to both variables.

(2)

If, say,
m_{1}\leqslant k
and
m_{2}=k+1
, then the function in question is only depending on
x_{2}
. The same is of course true if the roles of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are exchanged.

(3)

If both exponents
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are equal to
k+1
, then the function in (
5.0.11
) is smooth (and in general, depending on both variables).

This is not limited to the case of two variables, and the result is the same: the function which multiplies the monomial

(5.0.12)

x_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots x_{r}^{m_{r}}

is independent on
x_{i}
if we have
m_{i}\leqslant k
, for each
i=1,\dots,r

Coming back to our problem, we will use a variation of this type of considerations in order to obtain an expansion which is adapted to our context.

Assume that the section
s
of
\mathcal{O}(Y)
corresponds to the monomial

z_{1}\dots z_{r}
when restricted to
\Omega

The function we want to expand is

(5.0.14)

\rho_{\varepsilon}\Big(\log\log\frac{1}{|s|^{2}e^{-\phi(z)}}\Big).

To this end, remark that we have

(5.0.15)

\log\frac{1}{|s|^{2}e^{-\phi(z)}}=\log\frac{1}{|s|^{2}e^{-\phi_{\widehat{1}}{(z)}}}+z_{1}\phi_{1}(z)+\overline{z_{1}}\phi_{\overline{1}}(z)

for some functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{\overline{1}}

We consider

\bullet

\bullet

Then we have

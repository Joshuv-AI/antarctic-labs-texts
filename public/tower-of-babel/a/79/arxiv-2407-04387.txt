The rigorous derivation of Vlasov equations with local alignments from moderately interacting particle systems
Jinhuan Wang, Mengdi Zhuang, Hui Huang

Abstract
In this paper, we present a rigorous derivation of the mean-field limit for a moderately interacting particle system in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} ( d ≥ 2 ) (d\geq 2) . For stochastic initial data, we demonstrate that the solution to the interacting particle model, with an appropriately applied cut-off, converges in probabilistic sense to the solution of the characteristics of the regularized Vlasov models featuring local alignments and Newtonian potential. Notably, the cutoff parameter for the singular potential is selected to scale polynomially with the number of particles, representing an improvement over the logarithmic cut-off obtained in [ 38 ] . Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q83 82C22 35Q70 60K35 Keywords: Mean-field limit; Propagation of chaos; Local alignment; Newtonian potential; Cucker-Smale model

Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to study the mean-field limit of a stochastic, moderately interacting particle system in order to derive the following Vlasov type equation with a local alignment term

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{v}\cdot((\gamma v+\lambda(\nabla_{x}V+\nabla_{x}W\star\rho))f)=\nabla_{v}\cdot(\beta(v-u)f)

(1.1)

subject to the initial data
f(t,x,v)|_{t=0}=:f_{0}(x,v)
,
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here
f(t,x,v)
represents the particle density at
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and at time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
u
is the local particle velocity, i.e.

u(t,x):=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}vf(t,x,v)\,dv}{\rho(t,x)}\mbox{ with }\rho(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,v)dv\,,

V(x)
and
W(x)
are the confinement and the interaction potentials respectively, which shall be specified later. The first two terms on the left side of the equation (
1.1
) represent the free transport of the particles. The third term accounts for linear damping with a positive strength parameter
\gamma>0
. In equation (
1.1
), we choose
V(x)=\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}
and
\nabla_{x}W(x)=C_{d}\frac{x}{|x|^{d}}
. Additionally, the particle confinement and interaction forces in position are incorporated through potentials
V(x)
and
W(x)
with a positive strength parameter
\lambda>0
. The right-hand side of (
1.1
) corresponds to the local alignment force for particles, which was introduced in
[
28
]
as part of swarming models, and
\beta>0
is a constant.

Equation (
1.1
) can be traced back to the model introduced by Cucker and Smale
[
15
,
16
]
, which serves as a simple dynamical system to explain the emergence of flocking mechanisms in systems such as birds, fish, etc. The system considers an interacting
N
-particle system taking the form of

\frac{d}{dt}x_{i}=v_{i}\quad\frac{d}{dt}v_{i}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}K_{0}(x_{i},x_{j})(v_{j}-v_{i}),\quad i=1,\dots,N,

(1.2)

where each particle is described by its position
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and velocity
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
, and
K_{0}
is a smooth symmetric kernel. If we consider the empirical distribution function

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}^{N}\delta(x-x_{i})\delta(v-v_{i})\,,

(1.3)

then it will satisfy the following kinetic equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot(fL[f])=0,\quad in\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T),

(1.4)

where the operator
L
has the form

\displaystyle L[f]:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}K_{0}(x,y)f(y,w)(w-v)\,dwdy.

(1.5)

The Cucker-Smale (CS) model has garnered extensive attention in various fields (see for example
[
2
,
4
,

,

,

]
). Especially, the existence of classical solutions to the CS equation has been established in
[
24
]
, and the mean-field limit of the CS particle model has been derived in
[
14
,
23
]
.
In
[
28
]
, in order to prevent mass loss at infinity, a confinement potential
V
is introduced into the equation (
1.4
). This potential is designed such that
V\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
. As a result, the following equation is obtained

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{v}\cdot(f\nabla_{x}V)+\nabla_{v}\cdot(fL[f])=0,\quad in\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T).

In
[
30
]
, Motsch and Tadmor noticed that the normalization factor
\frac{1}{N}
in (
1.2
) can lead to some undesirable characteristics. In particular, when a small group of particles is situated far away from a much larger group of particles, the internal dynamics within the small group become almost halted due to the total large number of particles.
To address this issue, they proposed a modified Cucker-Smale model that replaces the original CS alignment with a normalized non-symmetric alignment

\displaystyle\tilde{L}[f]:=\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\phi(x-y)f(y,w)(w-v)dwdy}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x-y)f(y,w)dwdy}\,.

(1.6)

Subsequently, in
[
28
]
, Karper, Mellet, and Trivisa introduced a new model by additionally considering the singular limit where the Motsch-Tadmor flocking kernel
\phi
in
\tilde{L}[f]
converges a Dirac distribution. The Motsch-Tadmor correction then converges to a local alignment term

\frac{j-\rho v}{\rho}=u-v,

where

j(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}vf(t,x,v)\,dv\,.

By incorporating this additional term, the following CS model with strong local alignment, as introduced in
[
28
]
, is obtained

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{v}\cdot(f\nabla_{x}V)+\nabla_{v}\cdot(fL[f])+\beta\nabla_{v}\cdot(f(u-v))=0\,,

(1.7)

where
\beta>0
is a constant.
When
V
satisfies that
V\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
, and
L
or
\tilde{L}
satisfies (
1.5
) or (
1.6
), the global existence of weak solutions to (
1.7

[
28

. If
V(x)=\lambda\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\,,L[f]=-\gamma v-\lambda(\nabla_{x}W\star\rho)
and
\nabla_{x}W(x)=C_{d}\frac{x}{|x|^{d}}
, the global existence of weak solutions to (

[

As in
[
38
]
, we aim to investigate the mean-field limit of the following moderately interacting particle system which will lead to the Vlasov equation (

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{dx_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}}{dt}=v_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\quad i=1,\dots,N,\\
&\frac{dv_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}}{dt}=-\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-x_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-(\gamma v_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}+\lambda\nabla_{x}V(x_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})+\beta v_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})+\beta u^{\varepsilon,\delta}(x_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

where

\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &C_{d}\frac{x}{|x|^{d}},&&\quad|x|\geq\varepsilon,\\
&C_{d}\varepsilon^{-d}x,&&\quad|x|<\varepsilon\end{aligned}\right.

is the regularization of the singular interaction force
\nabla_{x}W

and

{u}^{\varepsilon,\delta}({x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})=\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{R}({v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\phi^{\varepsilon}({x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})}{\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\phi^{\varepsilon}({x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})+\delta}

(1.9)

is an approximation of the local velocity
u
. Here
\phi^{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\phi(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
with
\phi
being the standard mollifier. Then the function

\phi^{\varepsilon}
is
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{\varepsilon}dx=1
. For the convenience of subsequent calculations, we define
\phi_{R}(v_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta}):=v_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\cdot h(\frac{|v_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta}|}{R})
, where
h(r)\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
satisfying

h(r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&\quad r<1,\\
&0,&&\quad r\geq 2,\end{aligned}\right.\quad\mbox{ and }\quad 0\leq h\leq 1\,.

Note that in the notation
({x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta},{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})
, we omit the superscript

for the particles. This omission is because we will later choose

.

In this article we will show that equation (
1.1
) can be derived from particle system (
1.8
) in the limit of
N\to\infty
,
\varepsilon,\delta\to 0
, with the scaling relation

\varepsilon=N^{-\theta}\mbox{ and }\delta=R^{-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\vartheta\ln(N)}}

(1.10)

for some constants
\theta,\vartheta>0
.
To achieve this, we start by fixing
\varepsilon,\delta>0
. We then undertake a classical mean-field limit from (
1.8
) to the auxiliary intermediate system given as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{d\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}}{dt}=\overline{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\hskip 9.24994pti=1,\dots,N,\\
&\frac{d\overline{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}}{dt}=-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy-(r\overline{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}+\lambda\nabla_{x}V(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})+\beta\overline{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})+\beta\overline{u}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\,,\\
&f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,v,t)=\mbox{Law}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}),\hskip 9.24994pt\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}dv\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

with the same initial data as (
1.8
) and

\overline{u}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})=\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\phi_{R}(v)\phi^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dvdy}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\phi^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dvdy+\delta}\,.

Note here that we consider
N
independent copies
(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})
,
i=1,\dots,N
, and the intermediate system depends on
i
only through the initial datum. Secondly, it is easy to check that the density function
f^{\varepsilon,\delta}
shall satisfy the PDE

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\nabla_{v}\cdot((\gamma v+\lambda(\nabla_{x}V+\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}\ast\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}))f^{\varepsilon,\delta})=\nabla_{v}\cdot(\beta(v-u^{\varepsilon,\delta})f^{\varepsilon,\delta})\,,

(1.12)

which is a regularized version of our original model (
1.1
). Finally, if we let
\varepsilon,\delta\to 0
, one expects that
f^{\varepsilon,\delta}
converges to
f
, which is exactly the solution to (
1.1
) in weak sense (See the literatures
[
3
]
and
[
38
]
for detailed proof).

Lemma 2.1
.

There are several facts for
\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)
and

.

(i)
\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)
is bounded, i.e.,
|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq C\varepsilon^{-(d-1)}
.

(ii)
\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)
satisfies

|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq q^{\varepsilon}(x)|x-y|,\qquad\forall~~|x-y|<2\varepsilon,

where

q^{\varepsilon}(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{C}{|x|^{d}},&&\quad|x|\geq 3\varepsilon,\\
&C\varepsilon^{-d},&&\quad|x|<3\varepsilon.\end{aligned}\right.

(iii)

is Lipschitz continuous, i.e.,

|G(x,v)-G(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})|\leq L(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|).

Proof.

Conclusions
(i)
and
(iii)
can easy be achieved from the definition of
\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)
and
G(x,v)
. Thus, we omit the proof of results

and

As for
(ii)

[

. Indeed for

, by the definition of

it is easy to get

|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(y)|=|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x+\zeta)-\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq|\nabla^{2}_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})||\zeta|\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}|\zeta|.

For the case of
|x|\geq 3\varepsilon
, there exists some with

|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(y)|=|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x+\zeta)-\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq|\nabla^{2}_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x+s\zeta)||\zeta|

holds. One further notices that

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(x+s\zeta)|\leq C_{d}\frac{1}{|x+s\zeta|^{d}}\leq C_{d}\frac{1}{\Big|x-\frac{x}{|x|}|\zeta|\Big|^{d}}=C_{d}\frac{1}{\Big|x(1-\frac{|\zeta|}{|x|})\Big|^{d}}\leq C_{d}\frac{1}{\Big|x(1-\frac{2}{3})\Big|^{d}}=\frac{3^{d}C_{d}}{|x|^{d}}.

Then we have

Theorem 1
.

(\overline{Q}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}))_{i}:=-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy.

Similarly
P^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta})
and
\overline{P}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta})
are understood in the sense of

(P^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}))_{i}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|(\phi^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})|

and correspondingly

(\overline{P}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}))_{i}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(\phi^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)|\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy.

Next, we will prove that the probability of the sets
\mathcal{N}_{\kappa}
,
\mathcal{N}_{\gamma}
,
\mathcal{N}_{\eta}
and

tend to

as

-
3.4
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa})\leq\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-4(d-1)}N^{-(1-4\kappa)}.

Proof.

First, we let the set
\mathcal{N}_{\kappa}
evolve along the characteristics of the kinetic equation

\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}:=\Big\{(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{V}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}):\Big|N^{\kappa}\Psi^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-N^{\kappa}\overline{\Psi}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big|_{\infty}>1\Big\}

and consider the following fact

\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{i},

where

\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{i}:=\Big\{(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}):\Big|N^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\lambda N^{\kappa}(\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}\ast\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta})(t,\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big|_{\infty}>1\Big\}.

So, using the symmetry argument in exchanging any two coordinates, we can get

\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{i})=N\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{1}).

Using Markov inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Big(N^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\lambda N^{\kappa}(\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}\ast\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta})(t,\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big)^{4}\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\lambda N^{\kappa}}{N}\Big)^{4}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-N(\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}\ast\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta})(t,\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big)^{4}\Big].

(3.5)

Let
h_{j}:=\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy
. Then, the each term in the expectation (
3
) takes the form of
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}h_{j}^{k_{j}}
with
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}k_{j}=4
, and the expectation assumes the value of zero whenever there exists a
j
such that
k_{j}=1
, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy\Big]=0.

Then, we simplify the estimate (
3
) to

\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{1})\leq\Big(\frac{\lambda N^{\kappa}}{N}\Big)^{4}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{N}h_{j}^{4}+\sum_{1\leq m<n}^{N}C_{4}^{2}h_{m}^{2}h_{n}^{2}\Big].

Since
\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}
and
\|\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{1}}
are bounded, thus for any fixed
j

|h_{j}|\leq\Big|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big|+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\nabla_{x}W^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\Big|\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy\leq C\varepsilon^{-(d-1)}.

Therefore
|h_{j}|
is bounded to any power and we get

\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[h_{m}^{2}h_{n}^{2}\Big]\leq C\varepsilon^{-4(d-1)}\quad\quad\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[h_{j}^{4}\Big]\leq C\varepsilon^{-4(d-1)},

and consequently

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{1})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-4(d-1)}\Big(\frac{\lambda N^{\kappa}}{N}\Big)^{4}\Big(N+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\Big)\leq\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-4(d-1)}N^{-(2-4\kappa)}.

Then, we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa})=\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t})\leq N\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa,t}^{1})\leq N\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-4(d-1)}N^{-(2-4\kappa)}=\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-4(d-1)}N^{-(1-4\kappa)}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma})\leq\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-2d}N^{-(1-4\gamma)}.

Proof.

Let the set
\mathcal{N}_{\gamma}
evolve along the characteristics of the kinetic equation

\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}:=\Big\{(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{V}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}):\Big|N^{\gamma}Q^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{V}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-N^{\gamma}\overline{Q}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(\overline{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{V}_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big|_{\infty}>1\Big\}

and consider the following fact

\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{i},

where

\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{i}:=\Big\{(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta},\overline{v}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}):\Big|N^{\gamma}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\lambda N^{\gamma}(q^{\varepsilon}\ast\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta})(t,\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big|_{\infty}>1\Big\}.

Due to the symmetry in exchanging any two coordinates, we get

\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{i})=N\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{1}).

Using Markov inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{i})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Big(N^{\gamma}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\lambda N^{\gamma}(q^{\varepsilon}\ast\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta})(t,\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big)^{4}\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\lambda N^{\gamma}}{N}\Big)^{4}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-N(q^{\varepsilon}\ast\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta})(t,\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big)^{4}\Big].

(3.6)

Similar to Lemma
3.1
, we define
h_{j}:=q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy
. With the same argument as in Lemma
3.1
, we get

\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{1})\leq\Big(\frac{\lambda N^{\gamma}}{N}\Big)^{4}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{N}h_{j}^{4}+\sum_{1\leq m<n}^{N}6h_{m}^{2}h_{n}^{2}\Big].

On the other hand, due to the cut-off, we have

\|q^{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}.

Using the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
q^{\varepsilon}
and the integrability of
\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}
, we obtain

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy\Big|\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}.

Then

|h_{j}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}
.
Furthermore, by applying the inequality
\mathbb{E}\Big[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^{2}\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]
for any random variable
X
, we have for any fixed
j

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[h_{j}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Big(q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-y)\rho^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y)dy\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\Big(q^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{1}^{\varepsilon,\delta}-\overline{x}_{j}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}(q^{\varepsilon}(x-y))^{2}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv\Big)f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,x,w)dxdw.

Notice that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}(q^{\varepsilon}(x-y))^{2}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|x-y|<3\varepsilon\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(C\varepsilon^{-d})^{2}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv

\displaystyle~~~+\int_{\{|x-y|\geq 3\varepsilon\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(C\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}})^{2}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(3.7)

Applying
(iii)
of Assumption
2.1
, we get that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=C\varepsilon^{-d}\int_{\{|x-y|<3\varepsilon\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varepsilon^{-d}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}\int_{\{|x-y|<3\varepsilon\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv,

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=C\int_{\{|x-y|\geq 3\varepsilon\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}\int_{\{|x-y|\geq 3\varepsilon\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv.

Taking the above two term into (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}(q^{\varepsilon}(x-y))^{2}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,y,v)dydv\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}.

So, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[h_{j}^{2}]\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,x,w)dxdw\leq C\varepsilon^{-d}.

Therefore for any fixed
j

\mathbb{E}_{t}[h_{j}^{4}]\leq\|h_{j}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{t}[h_{j}^{2}]\leq C\varepsilon^{-2d}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{d}\varepsilon^{-d}=C\varepsilon^{-3d}\quad\quad\quad\mathbb{E}_{t}[h_{m}^{2}h_{n}^{2}]\leq C\varepsilon^{-2d}.

Consequently

\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{1})\leq C\Big(\frac{\lambda N^{\gamma}}{N}\Big)^{4}\Big(\varepsilon^{-3d}N+\varepsilon^{-2d}\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\Big)\leq\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-2d}N^{-(2-4\gamma)}.

Then

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma})=\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t})\leq N\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,t}^{1})\leq N\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-2d}N^{-(2-4\gamma)}=\lambda^{4}C\varepsilon^{-2d}N^{-(1-4\gamma)}.

∎

\displaystyle\leq(C+CR\delta^{-1}+CR\delta^{-1}N^{-\alpha}\varepsilon^{-(d+1)})\mathbb{E}_{0}[S_{t}]+C\Big(N^{-\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-(d-1)}+R+\frac{1}{\delta}\Big)

\displaystyle~~~~~\cdot\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa})N^{\alpha},\,\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma})N^{\alpha},\,\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\eta})N^{\alpha},\,\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\mu})N^{\alpha},\,N^{\alpha-\kappa},\,N^{\alpha-\eta}\Big\}.

Hence, Gronwall’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}[S_{t}]

\displaystyle\leq e^{(C+CR\delta^{-1}+CR\delta^{-1}N^{-\alpha}\varepsilon^{-(d+1)})t}

\displaystyle~~~\cdot\Big[C\Big(N^{-\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-(d-1)}+R+\frac{1}{\delta}\Big)\cdot\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\kappa})N^{\alpha},\,\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\gamma})N^{\alpha},\,\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\eta})N^{\alpha},\,\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\mu})N^{\alpha},\,N^{\alpha-\kappa},\,N^{\alpha-\eta}\Big\}\Big].

We choose
\varepsilon=N^{-\theta},R=\delta^{-1}=\sqrt{\vartheta\ln(N)}
, then we achieve

\mathbb{E}_{0}[S_{t}]\leq C\exp\Big\{\Big(C+C\vartheta\ln(N)\Big)t\Big\}\cdot N^{-n}.

∎

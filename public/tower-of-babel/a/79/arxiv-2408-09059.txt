Abstract
We call a proper edge coloring of a graph G G a B-coloring if every 4-cycle of G G is colored with four different colors. Let q B ​ ( G ) q_{B}(G) denote the smallest number of colors needed for a B-coloring of G G . Motivated by earlier papers on B-colorings, here we consider q B ​ ( G ) q_{B}(G) for planar and outerplanar graphs in terms of the maximum degree Δ = Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta=\Delta(G) . We prove that q B ​ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ Δ + 8 q_{B}(G)\leq 2\Delta+8 for planar graphs, q B ​ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ Δ q_{B}(G)\leq 2\Delta for bipartite planar graphs and q B ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ + 1 q_{B}(G)\leq\Delta+1 for outerplanar graphs with Δ ≥ 4 \Delta\geq 4 . We conjecture that, for Δ \Delta sufficiently large, q B ​ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ Δ ​ ( G ) q_{B}(G)\leq 2\Delta(G) for planar G G and q B ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) q_{B}(G)\leq\Delta(G) for outerplanar G G . Keywords: planar graphs, graph colorings.

1.1
The extended line-graph

The

of a graph

The parameter

we add further edges to

if the corresponding (independent) edges in

the graph induced by this set of new edges. As a result,

Proposition 1.1
.

For any graph

q_{B}(G)=\chi(L^{+}(G))
.

Proposition
1.1
leads to

\omega(L^{+}(G))\leq\chi(L^{+}(G))=q_{B}(G),

(1)

providing a natural lower bound of

.
In the cases of planar and outerplanar graphs

we have some restrictions on

Lemma 1.2

Let

(i)

1.2
B-coloring of planar and outerplanar graphs

Our main results are the estimates of

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

and this is sharp.

Note that (i) and (ii) are also sharp apart from the constant term.
The sharpness of these results is shown by

It is an interesting problem to determine here the true value of

For planar graphs with maximum degree at most 3 (subcubic graphs) we can prove the

Theorem 1.5
.

If

As it is usually the case, for outerplanar graphs we can show stronger results. We state our results for

(i)

(ii)

The sharpness in (i) for

Conjecture 1.7

and all further edges of
H
must meet
e
. However, since
H
has no two independent edges,
H
must be a star, or a triangle. Thus
\omega(L^{+}(G))=|E(H)|\leq\max\{\Delta(H),3\}\leq\Delta
.

Case 2:

|S|=2
. We will show that in this case
|E(H)|\leq 5\leq\Delta
.
Here edges
e_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1}),e_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2})
correspond to
S
and
G
has a 4-cycle
C=(u_{1},u_{2},v_{2},v_{1})
with edges
e_{1},e_{2}
on it.
If all
H
-edges are inside
V(C)
, then we indeed have
|E(H)|\leq 5\leq\Delta
. Thus we may assume that we have additional
H
-edges.
Clearly no edge
e\in E(H)
exists with
|e\cap C|=0
otherwise
|S|>2
, contradicting Lemma
1.3
. Since outerplanar graphs have no
K_{4}
subgraphs, we may assume that the edge
(u_{1},v_{2})
is not in
G
. Moreover, no edge
f\in E(H)
can intersect
C
in exactly the vertex
u_{1}
otherwise the edge
f
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
cannot be in a 4-cycle without forming a
K_{2,3}
subgraph which is also impossible in an outerplanar graph. The same argument works for
v_{2}
, thus all edges of
H
meet
C
in either
v_{1}
on in
u_{2}
. However we claim that there are no two edges
f,g\in E(H)
meeting
C
in
v_{1},u_{2}
, respectively. Indeed,
f,g
must be independent, otherwise we have a
K_{2,3}
. But
f,g
can be in a 4-cycle of
G
only if
(u_{2},v_{1})\in E(G)
and the other endpoints of
f,g
are adjacent in
G
. But then this edge with
f,g
give a path between
u_{2},v_{1}
extending the paths
(u_{2},u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2},v_{1})
to a topological
K_{2,3}
.
Thus, apart from at most two edges all edges of
H
are incident
v_{1}
or to
u_{2}
, assume wlog that to
v_{1}
.

Let
f=(v_{1},z)
be an edge of
H
intersecting
C
in only
v_{1}
. The edges
f,e_{2}\in E(H)
are in a 4-cycle of
G
. The edge
(u_{2},z)\notin E(G)
since there is no
K_{2,3}
in
G
. Thus, for all such
z
the 4-cycle must use the edges
e_{2},(v_{2},z),f,(v_{1},u_{2})
. But then there could be only one such
z
otherwise we have a
K_{2,3}
in
G
, giving us at most 6
H
-edges.
Furthermore, the edge
(u_{1},u_{2})
cannot belong to
E(H)
assuming that this
z
exists, since otherwise the edges
(u_{1},u_{2})
and
f
are in a 4-cycle and then we either get a
K_{4}
or a
K_{2,3}
.
Thus indeed
\omega(L^{+}(G))=|E(H)|\leq 5\leq\Delta
if
\Delta\geq 5
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
\Box

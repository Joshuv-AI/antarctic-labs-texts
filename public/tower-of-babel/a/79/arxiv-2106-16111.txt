Celestial Dual Superconformal Symmetry, MHV Amplitudes and Differential Equations
Yangrui Hu, Lecheng Ren, Akshay Yelleshpur Srikant, Anastasia Volovich

Abstract
Celestial and momentum space amplitudes for massless particles are related to each other by a change of basis provided by the Mellin transform. Therefore properties of celestial amplitudes have counterparts in momentum space amplitudes and vice versa. In this paper, we study the celestial avatar of dual superconformal symmetry of 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 Yang-Mills theory. We also analyze various differential equations known to be satisfied by celestial n n -point tree-level MHV amplitudes and identify their momentum space origins.

3
Dual superconformal symmetry

3.1
Dual superconformal symmetry of momentum space amplitudes

The remarkable simplicity of scattering amplitudes in
\mathcal{N}=4
Yang-Mills theory and our ability to compute them stem partly from the symmetries of the theory. It is now well known that in addition to the conventional superconformal symmetry
PSU(2,2|4)
, tree-level amplitudes of the theory are invariant under dual superconformal symmetry
Drummond:2008vq
;
Drummond:2009fd
which
can be easily seen if we define dual momentum and supermomentum variables

\displaystyle(p_{i})_{\alpha\,\dot{\alpha}}~=~-\lambda_{i\,\alpha}\,\tilde{\lambda}_{i\,\dot{\alpha}}~:=~\left(x_{i}-x_{i+1}\right)_{\alpha\,\dot{\alpha}}\qquad i=1\,\dots,\,n~~,

(12)

\displaystyle q_{i\,\alpha}^{A}~=~\lambda_{i\,\alpha}\eta^{A}_{i}~:=~\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i+1}\right)_{\alpha}^{A}\qquad i=1\,\dots,\,n~~.

(13)

Dual superconformal symmetry is ordinary superconformal symmetry in variables
(x_{i},\theta_{i})
. The amplitudes are covariant under this symmetry and the generators do not annihilate them. However, we can modify these generators such that they annihilate the amplitudes and express them in terms of
\lambda_{i},\,\tilde{\lambda}_{i}
, see
Drummond:2008vq
;
Drummond:2009fd

for more details. All the generators except
K^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
and
S_{\alpha}^{A}
either act trivially on the amplitude or are equal to one of their conformal counterparts. Hence, we only present expressions for
K^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
and
S_{\alpha}^{A}
here.

Let us first rewrite the expression for the generators
K^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
given in
Drummond:2009fd
in a more compact form

\displaystyle{\cal K}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{j}^{\beta}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{\beta}}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{\dot{\beta}}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\beta}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\eta_{j}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{i}^{A}}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{\dot{\alpha}}\right]

(14)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i<j}\left(\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}D_{j,i}+\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\right)~~,

where we have made use of momentum conservation and also introduced the operator

D_{i,j}~=~\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}}~-~\tilde{\lambda}_{i,\dot{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{j,\dot{\beta}}}~-~\sum_{A}\,\eta_{i}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{j}^{A}}~~,

(15)

which will play an additional role in Section
4
.

Similar manipulations on
{\cal S}_{\alpha}^{A}
lead to

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\alpha}^{A}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{j}^{\beta}\eta_{j}^{A}\lambda_{i,\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{\beta}}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j,\alpha}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{\dot{\beta}}\eta_{i}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\beta}}}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j,\alpha}\eta_{j}^{B}\eta_{i}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{i}^{B}}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{j,\alpha}\eta_{j}^{A}\right]

(16)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i<j}\left(\lambda_{j,\alpha}\eta_{i}^{A}D_{j,i}+\lambda_{i,\alpha}\eta_{i}^{A}\right)~~.

Differential equations for celestial MHV amplitudes

4.1
Generalized Banerjee-Ghosh equations

i-1\leftrightarrow i+1
if we look at terms with the prefactor of
{\rm Tr}[T^{a_{1}}\cdots T^{a_{i-1}}T^{a_{i}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}T^{a}}T^{a_{i+1}}\cdots]
. The terms with the prefactor of

{\rm Tr}[T^{a_{1}}\cdots T^{a_{i-1}}T^{a_{i}}T^{a_{i+1}}\cdots T^{a_{j-1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}T^{a}}T^{a_{j}}T^{a_{j+1}}\cdots]
, in which the insertion
T^{a}
isn’t adjacent to
T^{a_{i}}
imply

\begin{split}&\left\{-\,\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\,\frac{\Delta_{j}-J_{j}-1+\bar{z}_{j,i}\bar{\partial}_{j}}{z_{j,i}}\,e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{i}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{j}}}~+~\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j-1}\,\frac{\Delta_{j-1}-J_{j-1}-1+\bar{z}_{j-1,i}\bar{\partial}_{j-1}}{z_{j-1,i}}\,e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{i}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{j-1}}}\right.\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt~+~\left.\left(\,\frac{\Delta_{i}}{z_{j,i}}~-~\frac{\Delta_{i}}{z_{j-1,i}}\,\right)\right\}\,{\tilde{\cal M}}_{n}[1,\cdots,i-1,i,i+1,\dots,j-1,j,\cdots]\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt~+~\left(\,\frac{1}{z_{i-1,i}}~-~\frac{1}{z_{i+1,i}}\,\right)\,{\tilde{\cal M}}_{n}[1,\cdots,i-1,i+1,\dots,j-1,i,j,\cdots]=0~~.\end{split}

(28)

Plugging in the form of the MHV celestial amplitude (

), it is easy to see that (
28
) is satisfied if (
27
) is true. Thus, we will take (
27
) to be the set of independent equations satisfied by the colour stripped amplitude.

4.1.2
Momentum space origin

We can recast (
27
) as an equation acting on the momentum space amplitude
{\cal M}_{n}
to get

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{i-1}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i-1}^{\dot{\alpha}}}\right){\cal M}_{n}=\frac{\langle i-1\,i+1\rangle}{\langle i+1\,i\rangle}{\cal M}_{n}~~,

(29)

where
i
is a positive helicity gluon. We will now demonstrate that this equation follows simply from the properties of
{\cal M}_{n}
under a BCFW shift and that we can generalize this equation to superamplitudes.

Consider the
n
-point MHV amplitude with negative helicity gluons
s
and
t

{\cal M}_{n}[123\dots n]~=~\frac{\langle st\rangle^{4}\,\delta^{(4)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,p_{i}\right)}{\langle 12\rangle\langle 23\rangle\cdots\langle n1\rangle}~~,

(30)

and the BCFW shift

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}~\to~\hat{\lambda}_{i}~=~\lambda_{i}\,+\,z\,\lambda_{j}~~,~~\tilde{\lambda}_{j}~\to~\hat{\tilde{\lambda}}_{j}~=~\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\,-\,z\,\tilde{\lambda}_{i}~~.

(31)

For infinitesimal
z
, this shift is implemented on (
30
) by the operator

D_{i,j}~=~\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}}~-~\tilde{\lambda}_{i,\dot{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{j,\dot{\beta}}}~~~.

(32)

Note that this coincides with the LHS of (
29
) when
j=i-1
and
i
corresponds to a negative helicity gluon. The RHS depends on the helicities of
i,j
and we tabulate the cases below.

1.

When
(J_{i},\,J_{j})=(+,\pm),\,(-,\,-)
,

\begin{split}\hat{\cal M}_{n}[\dots\hat{i}\dots]~=&~{\cal M}_{n}\,\frac{1}{1+z\,\frac{\langle i-1,j\rangle}{\langle i-1,i\rangle}}\,\frac{1}{1+z\,\frac{\langle i+1,j\rangle}{\langle i+1,i\rangle}}\\
~=&~{\cal M}_{n}\,\left(1-z\,\frac{\langle i-1,j\rangle}{\langle i-1,i\rangle}-z\,\frac{\langle i+1,j\rangle}{\langle i+1,i\rangle}\right)~+~{\cal O}(z^{2})~~.\end{split}

(33)

2.

When
(J_{i},\,J_{j})=(-,\,+)
, consider
i=s
,

\begin{split}\hat{\cal M}_{n}[\dots\hat{i}\dots]~=&~\frac{(\langle it\rangle+z\,\langle jt\rangle)^{4}}{\langle 12\rangle\langle 23\rangle\cdots\langle n1\rangle}\,\frac{1}{1+z\,\frac{\langle i-1,j\rangle}{\langle i-1,i\rangle}}\,\frac{1}{1+z\,\frac{\langle i+1,j\rangle}{\langle i+1,i\rangle}}\\
~=&~{\cal M}_{n}\,\left(1-z\,\frac{\langle i-1,j\rangle}{\langle i-1,i\rangle}-z\,\frac{\langle i+1,j\rangle}{\langle i+1,i\rangle}+4z\,\frac{\langle j,t\rangle}{\langle i,t\rangle}\right)~+~{\cal O}(z^{2})~~.\end{split}

(34)

Both of these cases can be compactly summarized as

D_{i,j}\,{\cal M}_{n}~=~{\cal M}_{n}\,\left(-\,\frac{\langle i-1,j\rangle}{\langle i-1,i\rangle}-\frac{\langle i+1,j\rangle}{\langle i+1,i\rangle}+4\,\frac{\langle j,t\rangle}{\langle i,t\rangle}\,\delta_{i,s}+4\,\frac{\langle j,s\rangle}{\langle i,s\rangle}\,\delta_{i,t}\right)~~,

(35)

which is a generalization of (
29
).

We can map this to the celestial sphere. Taking
J_{i}=+
, we get

\begin{split}&\left[-\left(\Delta_{i}+z_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\right)~+~\frac{z_{i-1,j}}{z_{i-1,i}}~+~\frac{z_{i+1,j}}{z_{i+1,i}}~-~1\right]\,\tilde{\cal M}_{n}\\
&\quad\quad~+~\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\left(\Delta_{j}-J_{j}-1+\bar{z}_{ji}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\right)\,e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{i}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{j}}}\tilde{\cal M}_{n}~=~0~~,\end{split}

(36)

which generalizes (
27
).

4.1.3
Supersymmetric case

The discussion above readily generalizes to MHV superamplitudes. The super-BCFW shift

\lambda_{i}~\rightarrow~\lambda_{i}~+~z\,\lambda_{j}~~,~~\tilde{\lambda}_{j}~\rightarrow~\tilde{\lambda}_{j}~-~z\,\tilde{\lambda}_{i}~~,~~\eta^{A}_{j}~\rightarrow~\eta^{A}_{j}~-~z\,\eta^{A}_{i}

(37)

is generated by

D_{i,j}~=~\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}}~-~\tilde{\lambda}_{i,\dot{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{j,\dot{\beta}}}~-~\sum_{A}\,\eta_{i}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{j}^{A}}~~~.

(38)

Recall that

{\cal A}_{n,0}~=~\frac{\frac{1}{2^{4}}\prod_{A=1}^{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle ij\rangle\eta_{i}^{A}\eta_{j}^{A}}{\langle 12\rangle\langle 23\rangle\cdots\langle n1\rangle}\delta^{(4)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\right)~~.

(39)

Under the super-BCFW shift (
37
), the numerator of (
39
) is invariant and we have

D_{i,j}\,{\cal A}_{n,0}~=~-\,{\cal A}_{n,0}\,\left(\frac{\langle i-1,j\rangle}{\langle i-1,i\rangle}+\frac{\langle i+1,j\rangle}{\langle i+1,i\rangle}\right)~~.

(40)

Note that the equation above is valid for arbitrary
i
and
j
.

Transforming (
40
) to the celestial sphere we get

\begin{split}&\left[-\left(\Delta_{i}+J_{i}+z_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\right)~+~\frac{z_{i-1,j}}{z_{i-1,i}}~+~\frac{z_{i+1,j}}{z_{i+1,i}}\right]\,\tilde{\cal A}_{n,0}\\
&\quad\quad~-~\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\,\left(\sum_{A}\,\eta_{i}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{j}^{A}}\right)e^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{j}}}\,\tilde{\cal A}_{n,0}\\
&\quad\quad~+~\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\left(\Delta_{j}-J_{j}-1+\bar{z}_{ji}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\right)\,e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{i}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{j}}}\tilde{\cal A}_{n,0}~=~0~~,\end{split}

(41)

which is a generalization of (
27
) to celestial MHV superamplitudes.

4.2
Hypergeometric equations and their momentum space origin

The tree-level celestial MHV gluon
n
-point amplitudes have been computed in
Schreiber_2018
and have been found to be Aomoto-Gelfand hypergeometric functions. It is well known that these functions satisfy a set of differential equations. We refer the reader to Appendix
A
for more details. In this section, we will motivate these differential equations from properties of amplitudes. The celestial tree-level MHV
n
-point amplitude is given by

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{n}~=&~2\pi\,\frac{z_{st}^{4}}{z_{12}z_{23}\dots z_{n1}}\frac{\delta\left(\sum_{i}(\Delta_{i}-1)\right)}{2^{n-4}}\frac{1}{U}\prod_{a,b}\mathbf{1}\left(x_{a,b}>0\right)\\
&~\times\int\left(\prod_{c=1}^{n-4}\,\mathrm{d}u_{c}\right)\,\delta\left(1-\sum_{d=1}^{n-4}u_{d}\right)\,\left(\prod_{a=1}^{n-4}u_{a}^{\Delta_{a}-J_{a}-1}\right)\prod_{b=n-3}^{n}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n-4}x_{a,b}u_{a}\right)^{\Delta_{b}-J_{b}-1}\\
&~:=\mathcal{N}\,\delta\left(\sum_{i}(\Delta_{i}-1)\right)F\left(x_{a,\,b},\Delta_{i}\right)~~,\end{split}

(42)

where

\displaystyle U=\det\{q_{n-3},q_{n-2},q_{n-1},q_{n}\},\ U_{a,b}=\det\{q_{n-3},q_{n-2},q_{n-1},q_{n}\}|_{b\to a}~~,

(43)

\displaystyle x_{a,b}=-\frac{\epsilon_{a}\,U_{a,b}}{\epsilon_{b}\,U}~~,

and
F
is the Aomoto-Gelfand hypergeometric function
aomoto
. This is of the form given in (
62
) with

\displaystyle Z~=~\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\dots&0&x_{1,\,n-3}&x_{1,\,n-2}&x_{1,\,n-1}&x_{1,\,n}\\
0&1&0&\dots&0&x_{2,\,n-3}&x_{2,\,n-2}&x_{2,\,n-1}&x_{2,\,n}\\
\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&x_{n-4,\,n-3}&x_{n-4,\,n-2}&x_{n-4,\,n-1}&x_{n-4,\,n}\\
\end{pmatrix}~~,

(44)

which corresponds to setting (with
k=n-4
and
m=n
)

\begin{split}&z_{a,b}~=~\delta_{a,b}\qquad 1\leq a,\,b\leq n-4~~,\\
&z_{a,b}~=~x_{a,b}\qquad 1\leq a\leq n-4~~,~~n-3\leq b\leq n~~.\end{split}

(45)

The polynomials
\ell_{b}
are

\begin{split}&\ell_{1}~=~u_{1},\qquad\dots\ ,\qquad\ell_{n-4}~=~u_{n-4}~,\\
&\ell_{b}~=~\sum_{a=1}^{n-4}x_{a,b}u_{a}\,,\qquad b~=~{n-3,\dots,n}~~,\end{split}

(46)

and the exponents are given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}~=~\Delta_{j}-J_{j}-1~~.

(47)

Momentum conservation is manifest in (
9
) due to the presence of the delta function in
{\cal A}_{n}
. However, in arriving at the expression in (
42
), we have used the delta function to eliminate
\omega_{n-3},\,\dots,\omega_{n}
. The statement of momentum conservation can now be written as a differential equation on
F
. Let
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
be the total momentum operator acting on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. The corresponding celestial operator, along the lines of eq (
17
) is

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\,q_{i}^{\mu}e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\,\Delta_{i}}}~~,

(48)

and we must have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\,q_{i}^{\mu}e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\,\Delta_{i}}}\tilde{\cal M}_{n}=0~~.

(49)

We can extract four independent conditions by contracting this with the four vectors
v_{b}^{\mu}
,
b=\left\{n-3,\,n-2,\,n-1,\,n\right\}
defined by

\begin{split}&\epsilon_{n}\,v_{n}^{\mu}=-\,\epsilon^{\mu\,\nu\,\rho\,\sigma}q_{n-3\,\nu}q_{n-2\,\rho}q_{n-1\,\sigma}\qquad,~~~\epsilon_{n-3}\,v_{n-3}^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\,\nu\,\rho\,\sigma}q_{n-2\,\nu}q_{n-1\,\rho}q_{n\,\sigma}~~,\\
&\epsilon_{n-2}\,v_{n-2}^{\mu}=-\,\epsilon^{\mu\,\nu\,\rho\,\sigma}q_{n-1\,\nu}q_{n\,\rho}q_{n-3\,\sigma}~~~,~~~\epsilon_{n-1}\,v_{n-1}^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\,\nu\,\rho\,\sigma}q_{n\,\nu}q_{n-3\,\rho}q_{n-2\,\sigma}~~.\end{split}

(50)

Noting that
v_{b}^{\mu}\,\epsilon_{a}\,q_{a\mu}=-Ux_{a,b}
, we get

\begin{split}&\left[-\sum_{a=1}^{n-4}x_{a,b}\,U\,e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\,\Delta_{a}}}+U\,e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\,\Delta_{b}}}\right]\,\tilde{\cal M}_{n}=0\\
&\implies\mathcal{N}\,\delta\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\Delta_{i}-1\right)\right)e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{b}}}\left(-\sum_{a=1}^{n-4}\frac{1}{\alpha_{b}}x_{a,b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{a,b}}+1\right)F=0~~,\end{split}

(51)

where we have used the fact that

. This gives us a set of differential equations

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n-4}x_{a,b}\frac{\partial\,F}{\partial x_{a,b}}=\alpha_{b}\,F~~,

(52)

which correspond to eq (
69

The other set of differential equations eq (
68

\tilde{\cal M}_{n}
given in (

. A different choice, say solving for
\omega_{k},\dots,\omega_{k+3}
would lead to

\begin{split}\tilde{\cal M}_{n}~=&~2\pi\,\frac{z_{s,t}^{4}}{z_{12}z_{23}\dots z_{n1}}\frac{\delta\left(\sum_{i}(\Delta_{i}-1)\right)}{2^{n-4}}\frac{1}{U^{\prime}}\,\int\left[\prod_{c\neq\{k,\,\dots,\,k+3\}}\,\mathrm{d}u_{c}\right]\,\delta\left(1-\sum_{d\neq\{k,\,\dots,\,k+3\}}u_{d}\right)\\

(53)

with

\displaystyle U^{\prime}=\det\{q_{k},q_{k+1},q_{k+2},q_{k+3}\}~,\ U^{\prime}_{a,b}=\det\{q_{k},q_{k+1},q_{k+2},q_{k+3}\}|_{b\to a}~~,

(54)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}_{a,b}=-\frac{\epsilon_{a}\,U^{\prime}_{a,b}}{\epsilon_{b}\,U^{\prime}}~~.

This corresponds to an integral of the form given in (
62

\displaystyle Z~=~\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&x_{1,\,k}&x_{1,\,k+1}&x_{1,\,k+2}&x_{1,\,k+3}&\dots&0\\
0&1&\dots&x_{2,\,k}&x_{2,\,k+1}&x_{2,\,k+2}&x_{2,\,k+3}&\dots&0\\
\vdots\\

\end{pmatrix}~~.

(55)

Both of these are equivalent representations of the celestial amplitude and

is defined up to

transformations acting on the left. These transformations correspond to a change of solved and unsolved variables. As shown in Appendix
A

gives rise to the differential equation (

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{a,b}\frac{\partial\,F}{\partial z_{c,b}}~=~-\delta_{a,c}\,F\qquad 1\,\leq a,\,c\,\leq n-4~~.

(56)

We need to make sense of the differentials of the gauge fixed

as in (

to the corresponding gauge fixed values. In particular,
z_{a,b}\frac{\partial F}{\partial z_{c,b}}=\delta_{a,b}\,\alpha_{a}e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{c}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{a}}}F
for

\displaystyle\alpha_{a}F+\sum_{b=n-3}^{n}x_{a,b}\frac{\partial\,F}{\partial x_{a,b}}~=~-F~~,

(57)

\displaystyle\alpha_{a}e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{c}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{a}}}F+\sum_{b=n-3}^{n}x_{a,b}\frac{\partial\,F}{\partial\,x_{c,b}}~=~0\qquad c\neq a~~.

It can be shown that (

4.3
Relation between differential equations

Finally, we make a comment here about the relationship between the colour stripped BG equations (
27
) and the hypergeometric PDEs. Recall that the celestial MHV amplitude

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{n}~=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{N}\,\delta\left(\sum_{i}(\Delta_{i}-1)\right)F\left(x_{a,\,b},\Delta_{i}\right)~~,

(59)

where
F
is a hypergeometric function. To compare the two sets of equations, we rewrite the BG equations as equations for
F
in terms of the variables
x_{a,\,b}
in (
43
). Without loss of generality, we choose
i=1
. After some manipulation, it can be brought to the form

\displaystyle\left[\left(\alpha_{1}+1+\sum_{b=n-3}^{n}x_{1,b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1,b}}\right)-\sum_{b}\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\left(x_{2,b}+\bar{z}_{1,2}\frac{\partial x_{2,b}}{\partial\bar{z}_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1,b}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2,b}}e^{\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta_{2}}}\right)\right.

Appendix A
Aomoto-Gelfand hypergeometric functions

The Aomoto-Gelfand hypergeometric functions
aomoto
are associated to Grassmannians. In this section, we will make this connection explicit and derive the differential equations associated to them. For a quick introduction, see
Abe
from which most of this section is adopted (with cosmetic changes).

Let
Z
be a
k\times m
matrix,

\displaystyle Z=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1,1}&z_{1,2}&\dots&z_{1,m}\\
\vdots\\
z_{k,1}&z_{l,2}&\dots&z_{k,m}\end{pmatrix}~~.

(61)

The hypergeometric function associated to this matrix is

\displaystyle F(Z)=\int\prod_{b=1}^{m}\ell_{b}(u)^{\alpha_{b}}\frac{du_{1}\dots du_{k}}{\text{Vol GL(1)}}~~,

(62)

where
\ell_{b}(u)=\sum_{a=1}^{k}u_{a}\,z_{a,b}
and
u=\left(u_{1},\dots u_{k}\right)\in\mathbb{CP}^{k}
. The polynomials
\ell_{1},\,\dots,\,\ell_{m}
can be collectively written as

\displaystyle\ell=\left(\ell_{1},\,\dots\ell_{m}\right)=u\cdot Z~~.

(63)

A point in the Grassmannian
G(k,m)
is defined modulo
GL(k)
transformations. We would like to understand how the function
F(Z)
behaves under such transformations. A
GL(k)
transformation,
G
, acts by left multiplication on the matrix
Z
as
Z\to G\cdot Z
and we have,

\displaystyle\ell\to\ell^{\prime}=u\cdot G\,Z~~.

(64)

We see that the effect on
F(Z)
is a change of variables from
u\to u^{\prime}
leading to

\displaystyle F(G\cdot Z)=\text{det}(G)^{-1}F(Z)~~.

(65)

We can also rescale each column separately. This is effected through right multiplication by an
m\times m
diagonal matrix
S=\text{diag}\left(s_{0},\dots s_{m}\right)
.

\displaystyle Z\to Z\cdot S=\begin{pmatrix}s_{1}\,z_{1,1}&s_{2}\,z_{1,2}&\dots&s_{m}\,z_{1,m}\\
\vdots\\
s_{1}\,z_{k,1}&s_{2}\,z_{l,2}&\dots&s_{m}\,z_{k,m}\end{pmatrix}~~.

(66)

The polynomials transform in a simple manner as
\ell_{i}\to s_{i}\,\ell_{i}
resulting in

\displaystyle F(Z\cdot S)=F(Z)\,\prod_{b=1}^{m}s_{b}^{\alpha_{b}}~~.

(67)

The transformation properties in eqs(
65
,
67
) lead to the following set of differential equations
aomoto
,
Abe

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{m}z_{a,b}\frac{\partial\,F}{\partial z_{c,b}}=-\delta_{a,c}\,F\qquad 1\,\leq a,\,c\,\leq k~~,

(68)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{k}z_{a,b}\frac{\partial\,F}{\partial\,z_{a,b}}=\alpha_{b}\,F\qquad 1\,\leq b\,\leq m~~.

(69)

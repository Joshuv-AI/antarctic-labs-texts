Spectral line shape in the limit of frequent velocity-changing collisions
Nikodem Stolarczyk, Piotr Wcisło, Roman Ciuryło

Abstract
The speed-dependent spectral line profiles collapse into a simple Lorentz profile in the regime dominated by the velocity-changing collisions. We derive general formulas for the effective width and shift of the Lorentzian for arbitrary speed-dependent collisional broadening and shift and velocity-changing collision operators. For a quadratic speed dependence of collisional broadening and shift, and the billiard ball model of velocity-changing collisions, we provide simple analytical expressions for the effective Lorentzian width and shift. We show that the effective Lorentzian width and shift split into components originating from the well-known Dicke-narrowed Doppler width, speed-averaged collisional broadening and shift, their speed dependencies, and a product term that mixes the contributions of the broadening and shift speed dependencies. We show how the components depend on rates of speed-changing and velocity-changing collisions related to the perturber/absorber mass ratio. We validate analytical formulas numerically on the example of H 2 transition perturbed by He. Keywords: molecular line shapes, inhomogeneous broadening and shift, velocity-changing collisions, Dicke narrowing



I
Introduction

The shape of molecular spectral line affected by Doppler broadening and absorber-perturber collisions, in the general case, requires numerical evaluation and cannot be represented by a simple analytical function, unless some simplifications or assumptions are made
Hartmann et al. 2008
. The goal of this work is to show that, in the case when the velocity-changing collisions dominate other line-shape effects, the spectral line shape collapses to an ordinary Lorentz profile and the expressions for its width and shift can be provided analytically.

The Lorentz profile has been used for over a century to describe collisionally-broadened atomic and molecular spectral lines. It is particularly justified at high pressure of perturbers, much lighter than absorbers, in microwave spectral range, where the speed dependence of collisional broadening and shift
Berman 1972
;
Ward et al. 1974
, as well as Doppler broadening, can be neglected. Interestingly, also the Gaussian shape of the spectral line can collapse to the Lorentz profile when the velocity-changing collisions reduce the mean free path of the absorber well below the wavelength of absorbed radiation as predicted by Dicke
Dicke 1953
;
Wittke and Dicke 1956
in the fifties. It was later demonstrated numerically
Ciuryło et al. 2002a
, that the weighted sum of Lorentz profiles (WSL)
Pickett 1980
;
Farrow et al. 1989
, under frequent velocity-changing collisions described by the billiard-ball model
Lindenfeld and Shizgal 1979
;
Lindenfeld 1980
approaches the Lorentz profile and the convergence is faster for lower perturber/absorber mass ratio. It is related to relative contribution of speed change during velocity-changing collisions which is determined by perturber/absorber mass ratio
Hoang et al. 2002
;
Ciuryło et al. 2002b
.
Recently, studies on line shapes for which width is dominated by the speed-dependent collisional shift led to formulating a simple analytical expression
Wcisło et al. 2016
;
Martínez et al. 2018
(see Eq. (15) in Ref.
Wcisło et al. 2016
) for width of Lorentzian profile approximating such line shape when speed-dependent collisional shift is described by a quadratic function
Rohart et al. 1994
and velocity-changing collisions are approximated by the hard-collision model
Nelkin and Ghatak 1964
;
Rautian and Sobelman
. The phenomenological finding from Ref.
Wcisło et al. 2016
got justification in a derivation
Stolarczyk et al. 2022
coming from the speed-dependent hard-collision profile
Lance et al. 1997
;
Pine 1999
.

In this article, we generalize the results from Ref.
Stolarczyk et al. 2022
; we derive general formulas for the effective width and shift of a Lorentzian to which a sophisticated line shape model, based on any arbitrary speed-dependent collisional broadening and shift and velocity-changing collision operator, converges in the limit of frequent velocity-changing collisions. For quadratic speed dependencies of collisional width and shift, and the billiard ball model of velocity-changing collisions, we provide a simple analytical expression for the effective Lorentzian width and shift. We show how their components depend on rates of speed-changing and velocity-changing collisions related to the perturber/absorber mass ratio. We validate the analytical formulas numerically.

II
Algebraic representation of a spectral line shape

In general, the shape of an isolated spectral line affected by Doppler broadening and collisions with perturbers can be evaluated
Blackmore 1987
;
Shapiro et al. 2002
from a function
h(\omega,\vec{v})
,

I(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mbox{\rm Re}\;\left\{(1,h(\omega,\vec{v}))\right\},

(1)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined as a product
(a(\vec{v}),b(\vec{v}))=\int d^{3}\vec{v}f_{m_{A}}(\vec{v})a(\vec{v})b(\vec{v})
of two functions
a(\vec{v})
,
b(\vec{v})
of absorber velocity
\vec{v}
,
f_{m_{A}}(\vec{v})=(\pi v^{2}_{m_{A}})^{-3/2}\exp(-v^{2}/v^{2}_{m_{A}})
is Maxwellian distribution,
v_{m_{A}}=\sqrt{k_{B}T/(2m_{A})}
is the most probable speed of the absorber having mass
m_{A}
at temperature
T
, and
k_{B}
is Boltzmann’s constant. Function
h(\omega,\vec{v})
fulfils transport-relaxation kinetic equation
Shapiro et al. 2002
,

1=-i(\omega-\omega_{0}-\vec{k}\cdot\vec{v})h(\omega,\vec{v})-\hat{S}^{f}h(\omega,\vec{v}),

(2)

where
\omega_{0}
is unperturbed transition frequency,
\vec{k}
is a wavevector of radiation, and operator
\hat{S}^{f}
describes the effect of collisions with perturbers.

Equations (
1
) and (
2
) can be converted into an algebraic form expanding function
h(\omega,\vec{v})
,

h(\omega,\vec{v})=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}c_{s}(\omega)\varphi_{s}(\vec{v}),

(3)

in a set of orthonormal functions fulfilling condition
(\varphi_{s}(\vec{v}),\varphi_{s^{\prime}}(\vec{v}))=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}
. We set
\varphi_{0}(\vec{v})=1
. The expansion coefficients
c_{s}(\omega)
depend only on frequency for given operator
\hat{S}^{f}
. In this basis, any operator
\hat{A}
can be represented by matrix
A
, having matrix elements

[\mbox{\bf A}]_{s,s^{\prime}}=(\varphi_{s}(\vec{v}),\hat{A}\varphi_{s^{\prime}}(\vec{v}))
.

The shape of an isolated spectral line can have algebraic representation by a series of Lorentz profiles. Following the approaches from
Blackmore 1987
;
Lindenfeld 1980
;
Podivilov et al. 1994
;
Robert and Bonamy 1998
and notation described
in Refs.
Shapiro et al. 2002
;
Ciuryło et al. 2002
;
Ciuryło et al. 2002a
, the line shape can be written as
Wehr et al. 2002
;
Ciuryło et al. 2002
;
Ciuryło et al. 2002a
:

I(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mbox{\rm Re}\;\left\{c_{0}(\omega)\right\},

(4)

where the coefficient

c_{0}(\omega)
can be evaluated by solving a set of complex linear equations,

\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{b}}=\mbox{\bf L}(\omega)\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{c}}(\omega),

(5)

for the coefficients
c_{s}(\omega)
, where
s=0,1,...,s_{\rm max}
.
Here the column
b

contains unity in the position 0 and zeros in other positions,
i.e.
[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{b}}]_{s}=\delta_{0,s}
,
and the column
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{c}}(\omega)
consists of
the coefficients
c_{s}(\omega)
,
i.e.
[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{c}}(\omega)]_{s}=c_{s}(\omega)
.
The matrix
\mbox{\bf L}(\omega)
depends on the frequency
\omega

and has the following form:

\mbox{\bf L}(\omega)=-i(\omega-\omega_{0})\mbox{\bf 1}+i\mbox{\bf K}-\mbox{\bf S}^{f},

(6)

where
\omega_{0}
corresponds to the unperturbed frequency of the transition,

1
is the unit matrix,

[\mbox{\bf 1}]_{s,s^{\prime}}=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}
,

K
is the matrix that represents the Doppler shift,

\mbox{\bf S}^{f}=\mbox{\bf S}_{D}^{f}+\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}
is the matrix
which represents the collision operator split into two components:

\mbox{\bf S}_{D}^{f}
is the matrix which represents
the "dephasing and relaxation"
Wcisło et al. 2018
;
Słowiński et al. 2020
collisional width and shift and
\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}
is the matrix which represents the "velocity-changing" collision operator also affected by dephasing and relaxation. The representation used here has a property
[\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}]_{s,0}=[\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}]_{0,s}=0
for any
s
and
[\mbox{\bf K}]_{0,0}=0
. Moreover, all matrices discussed in this work are symmetric.

In practice, the coefficient
c_{0}(\omega)
is calculated using the
diagonalization technique (c.f.
Robert and Bonamy 1998
;
Dolbeau et al. 1999
;
Shapiro and May 2001
). To do it in this way, one needs to find the full set of eigenvectors,
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}
, and corresponding eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{j}
which fulfill the following equation:

(i\mbox{\bf K}-\mbox{\bf S}^{f})\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j},

(7)

where
j=0,1,...,s_{\rm max}
.
Once the eigenvectors and eigenvalues are known, the coefficient
c_{0}(\omega)
can be computed from the following expression:

c_{0}(\omega)=\sum_{j=0}^{s_{\rm max}}\frac{\beta_{j}[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}]_{0}}{\varepsilon_{j}-i(\omega-\omega_{0})},

(8)

where the coefficients
\beta_{j}
fulfill the relation
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{b}}=\sum_{j=0}^{s_{\rm max}}\beta_{j}\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}
. The main advantage of this approach is that the time-consuming diagonalization can be carried out once and this is sufficient to calculate the whole line shape.

III
High frequency of the velocity-changing collisions limit

Our derivation is carried out in the limit where the velocity-changing collisions dominate over the Doppler broadening and collisional broadening and shift. We assume the absolute values of all matrix elements of
K
and
\mbox{\bf S}_{D}^{f}
, as well as detuning,
\omega-\omega_{0}
, to be much smaller than the absolute value of the effective optical frequency of velocity changing collisions,
\nu_{\rm opt}
. Importantly,
\nu_{\rm opt}
can be complex due to the dephasing associated with optical velocity-changing collisions. All nonzero matrix elements of
\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}
are directly proportional to
\nu_{\rm opt}
.

The recognition of
\nu_{\rm opt}
as a complex quantity was originally put forth by Rautian and Sobelmann
Rautian and Sobelman
. Subsequent support for this notion came from the comparison between measurements
Pine 1994
and theoretical estimations
Demeio et al. 1995
. The complex form of
\nu_{\rm opt}
has been justified through both semiclassical approaches
Rautian and Sobelman
;
Nienhuis 1978
;
Loony 1987
;
Ciuryło et al. 2001
and quantum treatments
Hess 1972
;
Kochanov et al.
;
Nienhuis 1978
;
Berman et al. 1982
;
Berman 1984
;
Monchick and Hunter 1986
;
Thibault et al. 2017
, see also the references cited therein.

For the matrix
\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}
we can calculate eigenvectors,
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}
, and eigenvalues,
\varepsilon_{j}^{VC}
, which fulfil the following equation:

-\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}=\varepsilon_{j}^{VC}\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}.

(9)

It is easy to see that one of the eigenvectors of the matrix having property
[\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}]_{s,0}=[\mbox{\bf S}_{VC}^{f}]_{0,s}=0
is vector
b
and its corresponding eigenvalue is zero. Therefore, we can set
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{0}^{VC}=\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{b}}
and
\varepsilon_{0}^{VC}=0
. Consequently, for
j\neq 0
we have
[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}]_{0}=0
and
\varepsilon_{j}^{VC}\sim\nu_{\rm opt}
.

Now we can rewrite Eq. (
7
) in the form:

(-\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}-\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}+i\mbox{\bf K})\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j},

(10)

where the matrix, which we want to diagonalize, is split into the dominating part
\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}
and the perturbation part containing
\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}
and
K
. We take advantage of that and approximate eigenvectors
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}
by
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}
and eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{j}
by
\varepsilon_{j}^{VC}
and improve them by perturbation corrections. Setting
\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}\approx\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}
we get
\beta_{j}=\delta_{j,0}
. In this, way Eq. (
8
) can be approximated with the Lorentz profile:

c_{0}(\omega)\approx\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}-i(\omega-\omega_{0})},

(11)

where the eigenvalue
\varepsilon_{0}
is approximated by second-order perturbation. The eigenvalues,
\varepsilon_{j}
, in the second-order perturbation are given by the following expression:

\varepsilon_{j}\approx\varepsilon_{j}^{VC}+\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=0}^{s_{\rm max}}[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}]_{s}(-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{s,s^{\prime}}+i[\mbox{\bf K}]_{s,s^{\prime}})[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}]_{s^{\prime}}+\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}=0\\
j^{\prime}\neq j\end{subarray}}^{s_{\rm max}}\frac{(\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=0}^{s_{\rm max}}[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}]_{s}(-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{s,s^{\prime}}+i[\mbox{\bf K}]_{s,s^{\prime}})[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j^{\prime}}^{VC}]_{s^{\prime}})^{2}}{\varepsilon_{j}^{VC}-\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}^{VC}}.

(12)

This equation for
\varepsilon_{0}
simplifies to the following form

\varepsilon_{0}\approx-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,0}+\\
+\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{s_{\rm max}}\frac{\{\sum_{s^{\prime}=1}^{s_{\rm max}}(-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,s^{\prime}}+i[\mbox{\bf K}]_{0,s^{\prime}})[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j^{\prime}}^{VC}]_{s^{\prime}}\}^{2}}{-\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}^{VC}},

(13)

remembering that
\varepsilon_{0}^{VC}=0
,
[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{0}^{VC}]_{s}=\delta_{0,s}
and
[\mbox{\bf K}]_{0,0}=0
.
In this way, we get a single Lorentz profile,

I(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma_{\rm eff}}{\Gamma_{\rm eff}^{2}+(\omega-\omega_{0}-\Delta_{\rm eff})^{2}},

(14)

in the limit of frequent velocity-changing collisions, here

\Gamma_{\rm eff}+i\Delta_{\rm eff}=\varepsilon_{0}.

(15)

It should be noted that we can always use the basis in which matrix
\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}
is diagonal. In such case
[\mbox{\bf\lx@text@underline{e}}_{j}^{VC}]_{s}=\delta_{j,s}
,

\varepsilon_{j}^{VC}=-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}]_{j,j}
and Eq. (
13
) takes simple form

\varepsilon_{0}\approx-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,0}+\sum_{s=1}^{s_{\rm max}}\frac{(-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,s}+i[\mbox{\bf K}]_{0,s})^{2}}{[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}]_{s,s}}.

(16)

Furthermore, one can use a basis in which if a matrix element is nonzero,
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,s}\neq 0
, then the corresponding matrix element is zero,
[\mbox{\bf K}]_{0,s}=0
, and, if
[\mbox{\bf K}]_{0,s}\neq 0
then
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,s}=0
. It is a simple consequence of the symmetry of the corresponding operators. The collisional broadening and shift depend on the absolute value of the absorber velocity,
v=|\vec{v}|
, and are not dependent on velocity direction. On the other hand, the Doppler shift is proportional to the scalar product
\vec{k}\cdot\vec{v}
which depends on velocity direction. Taking this into account, we can rewrite Eq. (
16
) in the following form:

\varepsilon_{0}\approx-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,0}-\sum_{s=1}^{s_{\rm max}}\frac{[\mbox{\bf K}]_{0,s}^{2}}{[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}]_{s,s}}+\sum_{s=1}^{s_{\rm max}}\frac{[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{0,s}^{2}}{[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}]_{s,s}}.

(17)

IV
Application in case of quadratic speed-dependent collisional broadening and shift

To get physical insight into the expressions derived above, we consider matrix representation using Burnett functions
Lindenfeld and Shizgal 1979
described in Appendix
A
. We assume quadratic speed dependence of collisional broadening and shift
Rohart et al. 1994
. It means that the operator

\hat{S}_{D}^{f}=-\Gamma(v)-i\Delta(v)

(18)

is determined by
\Gamma(v)
and
\Delta(v)
given in the following form

\Gamma(v)+i\Delta(v)=\Gamma_{0}+i\Delta_{0}+(\Gamma_{2}+i\Delta_{2})\left(\frac{v^{2}}{v_{m}^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right),

(19)

where
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Delta_{0}
are the collisional broadening and shift parameters, averaged over absorber velocity, respectively. Quadratic speed dependencies of collisional broadening and shift are described by
\Gamma_{2}
and
\Delta_{2}
parameters, respectively.

Discussing the velocity-changing collisions, we will focus on the case where matrix
\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}
is diagonal or is approximated by a diagonal matrix.

In the Burnett functions basis representation, instead of the index
s=0,1,...,s_{\rm max}
, we prefer to use two other indices,
n=0,1,...,n_{\rm max}
and
l=0,1,...,l_{\rm max}
. The pair of indices
nl
can be connected with
s=n+(n_{\rm max}+1)l
and
s_{\rm max}=n_{\rm max}+(n_{\rm max}+1)l_{\rm max}
.

The properties of matrix elements in Burnett functions representation are summarized in Appendix
A
. The assumptions made above constrain the number of nonzero matrix elements which contribute to Eq. (
17
) to only a few,

\varepsilon_{0}=-[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{00,00}-\frac{[\mbox{\bf K}]_{00,01}^{2}}{[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}]_{01,01}}+\frac{[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{00,10}^{2}}{[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{VC}]_{10,10}}.

(20)

Now we can explicitly express the matrix elements:
[\mbox{\bf K}]_{00,01}=\omega_{D}\sqrt{1/2}
, where
\omega_{D}=kv_{m_{A}}
,
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{00,00}=-(\Gamma_{0}+i\Delta_{0})
,
and
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{D}]_{00,10}=-(\Gamma_{2}+i\Delta_{2})\sqrt{3/2}
.

IV.1
Hard collision model

To discuss the velocity-changing collisions we first consider the hard collision (HC) model
Nelkin and Ghatak 1964
;
Rautian and Sobelman
, discussed recently in Ref.
Stolarczyk et al. 2022
in a similar context. The HC model is frequently used due to its simplicity, despite its shortcomings. In many situations, it helps to get some analytical results. The operator
\hat{S}_{HC}^{f}
describing hard velocity changing collisions has the following form:

\hat{S}_{HC}^{f}h(\omega,\vec{v})=-\nu_{\rm opt}h(\omega,\vec{v})+\nu_{\rm opt}\int d^{3}\vec{v}^{\prime}f_{m_{A}}(\vec{v}^{\prime})h(\omega,\vec{v}^{\prime}),

(21)

where
\nu_{\rm opt}
is the effective optical frequency of velocity-changing collisions, which, in the general case, can be a complex number
Rautian and Sobelman
;
Hess 1972
;
Pine 1994
;
Demeio et al. 1995
.
It can be shown that in any orthonormal base assuming
\varphi_{0}(\vec{v})=1
, the matrix representation of the
\hat{S}_{HC}^{f}
operator is diagonal,
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{HC}]_{s,s^{\prime}}=-\nu_{\rm opt}\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}(1-\delta_{0,s})
. All its diagonal elements are equal to
-\nu_{\rm opt}
, except
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{HC}]_{0,0}=0
. Therefore, also in the Burnett functions representation, we can write that
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{HC}]_{01,01}=[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{HC}]_{10,10}=-\nu_{\rm opt}
. Inserting these matrix elements into Eqs. (
20
) and (
15
) we got

\Gamma_{\rm eff}^{HC}+i\Delta_{\rm eff}^{HC}=\Gamma_{0}+i\Delta_{0}+\frac{\omega_{D}^{2}}{2\nu_{\rm opt}}-\frac{3\Gamma_{2}^{2}}{2\nu_{\rm opt}}+\frac{3\Delta_{2}^{2}}{2\nu_{\rm opt}}-i\frac{3\Gamma_{2}\Delta_{2}}{\nu_{\rm opt}}.

(22)

The above result has also been obtained in our recent paper with a different method,
see Eqs. (19a)–(19d) in Ref.
Stolarczyk et al. 2022
, where we have shown that speed-dependent hard-collision profile
Pine 1999
collapses into a simple Lorentz profile in the limit of frequent velocity-changing collisions.

IV.2
Soft collision model

In the case when perturbers are much lighter then absorbers, the velocity changing collisions are described by soft collision (SC) model
Chandrasekhar 1943
. This model was introduced by Galatry
Galatry 1961
into the theory of Dicke narrowed spectral line shapes. By that time, the Galatry profile (GP) became one of the most frequently used expression to describe collisionally-narrowed spectra. The exact speed-dependent Galatry profile (SDGP) with quadratic speed dependence of collisional width and shift was given in Ref.
Ciuryło et al. 2001
and should be not confused with its approximated expression provided by Prime
et al

Priem et al. 2000
using appproach from Ref.
Ciuryło and Szudy 1997
. It was shown in Refs.
Lindenfeld and Shizgal 1979
;
Lindenfeld 1980
that the velocity changing soft collisions operator
\hat{S}_{HC}^{f}
is represented with a diagonal matrix operator in the Burnett functions basis. The relevant matrix elements are given by the following expressions:
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{SC}]_{00,00}=0
,
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{SC}]_{01,01}=-\nu_{\rm opt}
,
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{SC}]_{10,10}=-2\nu_{\rm opt}
, see Refs.
Lindenfeld and Shizgal 1979
;
Lindenfeld 1980
. In contrast to the HC model, the matrix elements
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{SC}]_{01,01}
and
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{SC}]_{10,10}
are not identical. Inserting these matrix elements into Eqs. (
20
) and (
15
) yields:

\Gamma_{\rm eff}^{SC}+i\Delta_{\rm eff}^{SC}=\\
\Gamma_{0}+i\Delta_{0}+\frac{\omega_{D}^{2}}{2\nu_{\rm opt}}-\frac{3\Gamma_{2}^{2}}{4\nu_{\rm opt}}+\frac{3\Delta_{2}^{2}}{4\nu_{\rm opt}}-i\frac{3\Gamma_{2}\Delta_{2}}{2\nu_{\rm opt}}.

(23)

The three last terms of this equation are two times smaller then the ones derived in case of the HC model, Eq. (

)

IV.3
Billiard ball model

The billiard ball (BB) model
Lindenfeld 1980
provides a more realistic description of the velocity-changing collisions. This model properly accounts for the perturber/absorber mass ratio,
\alpha=m_{p}/m_{a}
. The matrix describing such velocity-changing collisions
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{BB}]_{nl,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=\nu_{\rm opt}f_{D}M_{nl,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}^{E*}
is determined by coefficients
M_{nl,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}^{E*}

Lindenfeld and Shizgal 1979
;
Lindenfeld 1980
defined in Appendix
A
. This matrix is diagonal and the factor
f_{D}=1
in case of
\alpha=0
, which corresponds to the soft collision (SC) model. For nonzero
\alpha
, the matrix
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{BB}]_{nl,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}
is not diagonal and
f_{D}
becomes greater than one, reaching
32/(9\pi)\approx 1.132
for
\alpha=\infty

Lindenfeld 1980
. Nevertheless, we can approximate the original matrix by its diagonal simplification with the same diagonal elements
[\mbox{\bf S}^{f}_{BB}]_{nl,nl}
. The coefficients
M_{01,01}^{E*}=-1
and
M_{10,10}^{E*}=-2M_{1}

V
Components of effective width and shift

In line with our previous paper
Stolarczyk et al. 2022
, it is important to note that the Dicke parameter can take on complex values,
\nu_{\rm opt}=\nu_{\rm opt}^{r}+i\nu_{\rm opt}^{i}

Rautian and Sobelman
;
Hess 1972
;
Demeio et al. 1995
;
Pine 1994
. Therefore, we can decompose the effective Lorentzian width and shift of the asymptotic quadratic correlated speed-dependent billiard ball profile
Ciuryło et al. 2002a
;
Ciuryło et al. 2002
;
Lisak et al. 2006
;
Wcisło et al. 2018
into several contributions:

\Gamma_{\rm eff}=\Gamma_{0}+\Gamma_{\gamma}+\Gamma_{\delta}+\Gamma_{\gamma\delta}+\Gamma_{\omega_{D}},

(26a)

\Delta_{\rm eff}=\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{\gamma}+\Delta_{\delta}+\Delta_{\gamma\delta}+\Delta_{\omega_{D}}.

(26b)

Assuming quadratic speed-dependent collisional broadening and shift as well as velocity-changing collisions described by the billiard ball model, these contributions are:

\Gamma_{\gamma}=-\frac{f_{v^{2}}}{f_{D}}\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{r}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\Gamma_{2}^{2},

(27a)

\Gamma_{\delta}=\frac{f_{v^{2}}}{f_{D}}\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{r}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\Delta_{2}^{2},

(27b)

\Gamma_{\gamma\delta}=-2\frac{f_{v^{2}}}{f_{D}}\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{i}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\Gamma_{2}\Delta_{2},

(27c)

\Gamma_{\omega_{D}}=\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{r}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\omega_{D}^{2},

(27d)

\Delta_{\gamma}=\frac{f_{v^{2}}}{f_{D}}\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{i}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\Gamma_{2}^{2},

(27e)

\Delta_{\delta}=-\frac{f_{v^{2}}}{f_{D}}\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{i}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\Delta_{2}^{2},

(27f)

\Delta_{\gamma\delta}=-2\frac{f_{v^{2}}}{f_{D}}\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{r}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\Gamma_{2}\Delta_{2},

(27g)

\Delta_{\omega_{D}}=-\frac{\nu_{\rm opt}^{i}}{|\nu_{\rm opt}|^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\omega_{D}^{2}.

(27h)

For real
\nu_{\rm opt}=\nu_{\rm opt}^{r}
(
\nu_{\rm opt}^{i}=0
) the components of effective width are:
\Gamma_{\omega_{D}}

\Gamma_{0}
,
\Gamma_{\gamma}
,
\Gamma_{\delta}
. The first two terms represent collisionally-suppressed Doppler broadening and velocity-averaged collisional width, respectively.
\Gamma_{\gamma}
can be seen as a reduction of line width caused by the speed-dependence of collisional broadening. It is qualitatively coherent with findings in the other context of the speed-dependent Voigt profile (SDVP)
Berman 1972
;
Ward et al. 1974
, where also the narrowing of the spectral line was triggered by speed-dependence of collisional broadening. On the other hand,
\Gamma_{\delta}
represents an additional broadening of the line caused by the speed-dependent spread of collisional shift
Pickett 1980
;
Farrow et al. 1989
;
Wcisło et al. 2015
. The character of these contributions does not depend on the sign of
\Gamma_{2}
and
\Delta_{2}
parameters. The effective shift, in such circumstances, has only two components,
\Delta_{0}
and
\Delta_{\gamma\delta}
. The first one represents an ordinary velocity-averaged collisional shift, however, the second term is less obvious and is related to the product of speed dependencies of collisional broadening and shift or their correlation
Stolarczyk et al. 2022
.

The other contributions, namely
\Gamma_{\gamma\delta}
,
\Delta_{\gamma}
,
\Delta_{\delta}
, and
\Delta_{\omega_{D}}
, only come into play if
\nu_{\rm opt}
has a nonzero imaginary component, i.e.,
\nu_{\rm opt}^{i}\neq 0
. While we will not delve into a detailed discussion of these contributions, we will touch on the last one,
\Delta_{\omega_{D}}
. This shift arises due to Dicke-suppressed Doppler broadening, and it should decrease inversely with pressure, similar to the well-known
\Gamma_{\omega_{D}}

Dicke 1953
. This is because
\nu_{\rm opt}
is proportional to pressure, while
\omega_{D}
remains constant at a given temperature. Therefore, in a moderate range of pressures, this term can potentially impact the precise determination of the line position. In fact, as we demonstrate in the following section, under certain conditions,
\Delta_{\omega_{D}}
can contribute up to 2 MHz to the effective line shift,
\Delta_{\rm eff}
.

To describe the relative contribution of each effect (
x=\gamma,\;\delta,\;\gamma\delta\;{\rm or}\;\omega_{D}
) under specific physical conditions, we introduce dimensionless parameters
\Gamma_{x}/\Gamma_{0}
and
\Delta_{x}/\Delta_{0}
. It is worth noting that all collisional parameters, including
\nu_{\rm opt}
,
\Gamma_{0}
,
\Gamma_{2}
,
\Delta_{0}
, and
\Delta_{2}
, are proportional to gas pressure. Therefore, the dimensionless parameters
\Gamma_{x}/\Gamma_{0}
and
\Delta_{x}/\Delta_{0}
for
x=\gamma,\;\delta,\;{\rm and}\;\gamma\delta
are independent of pressure. In fact, as we show in the following section, under certain conditions,
\Gamma_{\delta}
can account for approximately 35% of
\Gamma_{0}
, and the absolute value of
\Delta_{\gamma\delta}
can reach 5% of
\Delta_{0}
or 20% of
\Gamma_{0}
. For further details, please refer to the next section.

VII
Conclusions

In summary, we demonstrated that under the limit of frequent velocity-changing collisions, the speed-dependent Dicke-narrowed profile of the spectral line collapses to the Lorentz profile. Our work provides formulas for effective Lorentzian width and shift, which take into account the arbitrary speed dependence of collisional broadening and shift, as well as the velocity-changing collision operator. Specifically, for the asymptotic behavior of the quadratic speed-dependent billiard ball profile
Ciuryło et al. 2002a
, we obtained simple analytical expressions for Lorentzian width and shift. Our results generalize those recently reported in Ref.
Stolarczyk et al. 2022
for the speed-dependent hard collision profile
Pine 1999
to the case of arbitrary perturber/absorber mass ratio,
\alpha
. We verified the applicability of our formulas by comparing them with numerical calculations of quadratic speed-dependent billiard ball profiles over a wide range of line-shape parameters.

Finally, our comparison with the numerical calculation of the H
2
spectral line perturbed by He showed good agreement with the profile derived in this work. This is thanks to properly accounting for the perturber/absorber mass ratio, which was not possible with the hard collision model. Our results offer a more accurate and general description of spectral line shapes under the limit of frequent velocity-changing collisions, with potential applications in various fields including atmospheric and astrophysical spectroscopy
involving rovibrational transitions of molecular hydrogen
.

Appendix A
The Burnett functions representation

For evaluation of the speed-dependent Dicke-narrowed spectral line shape from the transport/relaxation equation we use a subset of the Burnett functions having axial symmetry about the wave vector
\vec{k}

Lindenfeld and Shizgal 1979
. A detailed discussion of the application of this set of basis functions, as well as matrix representation of the operators present in the transport/relaxation equation, can be found in Ref.
Ciuryło et al. 2002a
. For convenience we recall these results here, using the original formulation.

We assume the basis functions in the following form
Lindenfeld 1980
:

\varphi_{nl}(\vec{v})=N_{nl}\;(v/v_{m_{A}})^{l}\;L_{n}^{l+1/2}(v^{2}/v_{m_{A}}^{2})\;P_{l}(\vec{e}_{k}\cdot\vec{e}_{v}),

(29)

where a normalization factor,

N_{nl}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{1/2}\;n!\;(2l+1)}{2\;\Gamma(n+l+3/2)}}\;,

(30)

\Gamma(...)
is the gamma-Euler function. The associated Laguerre polynomials are defined as

L_{n}^{l+1/2}(x^{2})=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{m}\;\Gamma(n+l+3/2)}{m!\;(n-m)!\;\Gamma(m+l+3/2)}\;x^{2m}\;,

(31)

where
x=v/v_{m_{A}}
is the reduced speed of the active molecule. The Legendre polynomials are defined as

P_{l}(y)=\frac{1}{2^{l}}\sum_{k=0}^{[l/2]}\frac{(-1)^{k}\;(2l-2k)!}{k!\;(l-k)!\;(l-2k)!}\;y^{l-2k}\;,

(32)

where
y=\vec{e}_{k}\cdot\vec{e}_{v}
is the cosine of the angle between the velocity vector
\vec{v}=v\vec{e}_{v}
and the wave vector
\vec{k}=k\vec{e}_{k}
,
\vec{e}_{v}
and
\vec{e}_{k}
are unit vectors.

The basis functions,
\varphi_{nl}(\vec{v})
, are eigenfunctions of the velocity-changing collision operator in the case of the soft collisions, where perturber/absorber mas ratio
\alpha=0
. Like in
Ciuryło et al. 2002a
, when calculating matrix elements of an operator,
\hat{A}
, we use the following notation
[\mbox{\bf A}]_{nl,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=(\varphi_{nl}(\vec{v}),\hat{A}\varphi_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(\vec{v}))=\langle nl|\hat{A}|n^{\prime}l^{\prime}\rangle
.

The Doppler shift operator,
\vec{k}\cdot\vec{v}=\omega_{D}\vec{e}_{k}\cdot\vec{v}/v_{m_{A}}
, is represented by a matrix,
K
, the elements of which are:
Ciuryło et al. 2002a

[\mbox{\bf K}]_{nl,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=\omega_{D}\langle nl|\vec{e}_{k}\cdot\vec{v}/v_{m}|n^{\prime}l^{\prime}\rangle\;,

(33)

where
\omega_{D}=kv_{m_{A}}
and

\langle nl|\vec{e}_{k}\cdot\vec{v}/v_{m}|n^{\prime}l^{\prime}\rangle=\\
\left[\sqrt{n+l+3/2}\;\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}-\sqrt{n}\;\delta_{n,n^{\prime}+1}\right]\\
\times\sqrt{\frac{(l+1)^{2}}{4(l+1)^{2}-1}}\;\delta_{l,l^{\prime}-1}\\
+\left[\sqrt{n+l+1/2}\;\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}-\sqrt{n+1}\;\delta_{n,n^{\prime}-1}\right]\\
\times\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}}{4l^{2}-1}}\;\delta_{l,l^{\prime}+1}.

(34)

The speed-dependent collisional width and shift operator,

\mbox{\bf S}_{D}^{f}

Ciuryło et al. 2002a
;
Robert and Bonamy 1998

\times L_{n}^{l+1/2}(x^{2})\;L_{n^{\prime}}^{l+1/2}(x^{2})\;\left[\Gamma(xv_{m_{A}})+i\Delta(xv_{m_{A}})\right].

(35)

In the case of quadratic speed-dependent collisional broadening and shift
Rohart et al. 1994

\Gamma(v)+i\Delta(v)=\Gamma_{0}+i\Delta_{0}+(\Gamma_{2}+i\Delta_{2})\left(\frac{v^{2}}{v_{m}^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right),

(36)

the matrix elements can be written as

[\mbox{\bf S}_{D}^{f}]_{nl,n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=-\left[\Gamma_{0}+i\Delta_{0}-(\Gamma_{2}+i\Delta_{2})3/2\right]\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\\
-\left[\Gamma_{2}+i\Delta_{2}\right]\langle nl|v^{2}/v_{m}^{2}|n^{\prime}l^{\prime}\rangle,

(37)

where

\langle nl|v^{2}/v_{m}^{2}|n^{\prime}l^{\prime}\rangle=\\
\left[(2n+l+3/2)\;\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}-\sqrt{(n+l+1/2)n}\;\delta_{n,n^{\prime}+1}\right.\\

(38)

The velocity-changing collisions operator

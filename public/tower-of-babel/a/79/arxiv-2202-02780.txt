A conjecture of Sárközy on quadratic residues. II
Yong-Gao Chen, Ping Xi

1.
Introduction

For each prime
p
, denote by
\mathbb{F}_{p}
the finite field of
p

elements and by
\mathcal{R}_{p}
the set of all quadratic residues modulo

p
. For any subsets
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p},
define the sumset

\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\{a+b:a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}\}.
A. Sárközy
[
Sa12
]

conjectured that for all sufficiently large primes
p
,
\mathcal{R}_{p}

has no
2
-decomposition
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
with

|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2,
which is also believed to be valid for
all odd primes
p
.

There are some partial results towards the above conjecture,
although the full generality seems beyond the current approach.
Sárközy
[
Sa12
]
considered the ternary analogue, and
proved that for all sufficiently large primes
p
, no subsets

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|,|\mathcal{C}|\geqslant 2

satisfy
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
. Recently, Y.-G. Chen and X.-H. Yan

[
CY21
]
proved this claim for all primes
p
. In addition, if
there do exist certain
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that

|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2
and
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p},
Sárközy

[
Sa12
]
showed that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{p}}{3\log p}\leqslant|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\sqrt{p}\log p.

The factor
\log p
was shortly removed by I.E. Shparlinski

[
Shp13
]
and I.D. Shkredov
[
Shk14
]
with some refined
constants. More remarkably, Shkredov
[
Shk14
]
proved the above
conjecture of Sárközy in the case
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B},
i.e.,

\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{A}\neq\mathcal{R}_{p}
holds for any
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
with

|\mathcal{A}|\geqslant 2.
A quantitative refinement on
(
1.1
) was recently given by Y.-G.
Chen and X.-H. Yan
[
CY21
]
, who proved that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{7-\sqrt{17}}{16}\sqrt{p}+1\leqslant|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\frac{7+\sqrt{17}}{4}\sqrt{p}-6.63.

Note that
(7-\sqrt{17})/16\approx 0.1798
and

(7+\sqrt{17})/4\approx 2.7808
. This improves a previous result by
Shkredov
[
Shk14
]
, in which the constants in the lower and
upper bounds are
1/6-o(1)
and
3+o(1)
as
p\rightarrow+\infty.

The main tool of Shparlinski
[
Shp13
]
includes an estimate of
the double character sum

\sum_{a}\sum_{b}\chi(a+b)

due to A.A. Karatsuba
[
Ka92
]
, where
\chi
is a non-trivial
multiplicative character in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}.
Alternatively, Shkredov

[
Shk14
]
and Chen and Yan
[
CY21
]
employed the Weil bound
for complete character sums over finite fields; see Lemmas

2.1
and
2.2
below.

In this paper, we make further refinements on the constants in
(
1.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Assume that
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
is a

2
-decomposition with
|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2.
Then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}+\frac{1}{8}\leqslant|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|<2\sqrt{p}-1.

Our next results involve the representation function

r=r_{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}}
defined by

\displaystyle r(x)=r_{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}}(x):=|\{(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}:a+b=x\}|.

For
\theta\in\mathbb{R},
define the moment of
r
by

\displaystyle M_{\theta}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}}r(x)^{\theta}.

Note that
M_{0}=|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
,
M_{1}=|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|
. Moreover,

M_{2}=E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}),
the additive energy of
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},
i.e.,

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}:a_{1}+b_{1}=a_{2}+b_{2}\}|.

We also introduce

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}):=|\{x\in\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}:r(x)=1\}|

to characterize how many elements in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
have unique
representations in
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}.
Clearly,

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leqslant|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|\leqslant|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\leqslant E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Roughly speaking, the three quantities
|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
,
|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|

and
E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
should be of similar sizes if
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
reasonably large; on the other hand,
|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|
becomes closer
to
2|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
as
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})/|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
decreases to zero (see
Lemma
4.1
for details). It is clear that if

\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|=|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|=E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

We are now ready to state our results.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p
be a prime. Assume that
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
is a

2
-decomposition with
|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2.
Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\geqslant(\log 2)^{-1}\sqrt{p}-1.6.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p
be a prime. Assume that
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
is a

2
-decomposition with
|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2.
If there exists
some
\eta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leqslant\eta(p-1),

then we have

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\geqslant\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2^{2-4\eta}-2^{3-2\eta}\eta+4\eta-1}{2-4\eta}\Big)(p-1)

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^{\eta}}\sqrt{p}.

Theorem
1.2
illustrates that there are suitably many elements in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
which can be
expressed as
a+b
with
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
in a unique way, and however, if there are not too many such elements, Theorem
1.3
shows that
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}

should be of reasonably large sizes.

In principle, there are two novelties in this paper. On one hand, the improvements revealed by Theorem
1.1
rely on Lemma
2.2
on estimates for complete character sums, which allows us to refine the constant derived directly from the Weil bound in the usual shape. In the last section, we will also outline the robustness of this bound in view of equidistributions of Birch
[
Bi68
]
and Katz and Sarnak
[
KS99
]
.
On the other hand, a new quantity
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
has been introduced to characterize the number of elements in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
which have unique representations in
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}
. We hope this should be of independent interests and will receive more attentions in further researches.

By closing this section, we remark that the arguments in this paper admit the bounds

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leqslant p^{3/2}-p,

while the trivial bound would be
E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leqslant|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\min\{|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\}
. We do not know how to beat the exponent
3/2.

2.
Auxiliary results

We start with the following estimate for complete character sums
over finite fields due to A. Weil
[
We48
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character of order
d>1
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Assume that
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
has
k
distinct zeros in the
algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and it is not a constant multiple of
the
d
-th power of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi(f(x))\Big|\leqslant(k-1)\sqrt{p}.

Denote by
\chi_{2}
the quadratic multiplicative character of

\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, i.e., the Legendre symbol mod
p
. For

\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{k}(\mathbf{a};p)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi_{2}((x+a_{1})\cdots(x+a_{k})).

For
k=2
, an elementary argument leads to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{2}(\mathbf{a};p)=-1

if
a_{1}\neq a_{2},
and
\mathfrak{S}_{2}(\mathbf{a};p)=p-1
otherwise. For
k\geqslant 3
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

(2.1)

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{S}_{k}(\mathbf{a};p)|\leqslant(k-1)\sqrt{p}

if the coordinates
a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}
are pairwise distinct. As in Chen and Yan
[
CY21
]
, define

c_{k}(p)
to be the smallest real number
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{k}(\mathbf{a};p)\leqslant c\sqrt{p}

holds for all pairwise distinct elements
a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
c_{k}
be the supremum of
c_{k}(p)
over odd primes
p
.
The following result gives an upper bound for
c_{k}
, which is in fact a special case of D. Wan
[
Wa97
, Corollary 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.2

(Wan
[
Wa97
]
)
.

Let
k>0
be an even integer. With the above notation, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle c_{k}\leqslant k-2.

More precisely, for each odd prime
p
and all pairwise distinct
elements
a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p},
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{k}(\mathbf{a};p)\leqslant(k-2)\sqrt{p}-1.

Proof.

To convince the readers, we now give a completely
elementary argument, plus the original Weil bound (
2.1
),
that leads to (
2.3
).

Making the shift
x\rightarrow x-a_{1},
we may write

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{k}(\mathbf{a};p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\chi_{2}(x(x+a_{2}-a_{1})\cdots(x+a_{k}-a_{1})).

Note that
\chi_{2}(x+h)=\chi_{2}(x)\chi_{2}(1+h\overline{x})
for all

x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}.
We then derive that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{k}(\mathbf{a};p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\chi_{2}((1+(a_{2}-a_{1})\overline{x})\cdots((1+(a_{k}-a_{1})\overline{x}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi_{2}((1+(a_{2}-a_{1})x)\cdots((1+(a_{k}-a_{1})x))-1.

Now the desired inequality follows from Lemma
2.1

immediately.
∎

Remark 1
.

Since
c_{k}(p)
is utilized to bound
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbf{a};p)
for all pairwise distinct
elements
a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p},
it is not possible to improve (
2.2
) with a smaller constant. This will be discussed in detail in the last section.

The following lemma shows an auxiliary bound involving cardinalities
of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B},
which will be our starting point to prove
Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are two subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{p},
then we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}||\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|+2)^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{p}(|\mathcal{A}|^{2}-1)(|\mathcal{A}|-2)

(2.4)

\displaystyle-|\mathcal{A}|^{3}+11|\mathcal{A}|-15+p(3|\mathcal{A}|+2).

Proof.

The proof goes quite similarly to
[
CY21
, Lemma 2.7]
.
Consider the moment

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}:=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{2}(a+b)\Big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{2}(a+b)+2\Big)^{2}.

Since
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{p},
we find that
\chi_{2}(a+b)=1
for
all
a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B},
which gives

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}=|\mathcal{B}||\mathcal{A}|^{2}(|\mathcal{A}|+2)^{2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{2}(x+a)\Big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{2}(a+b)+2\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{4}}\mathfrak{S}_{4}(\mathbf{a};p)+4\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{3}}\mathfrak{S}_{3}(\mathbf{a};p)+4\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{2}(\mathbf{a};p).

We may classify all
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}^{j}

(j=2,3,4)
according to
the multiples in coordinates. From Lemma
2.1
and Lemma

2.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}\leqslant

\displaystyle(2\sqrt{p}-1)|\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|-1)(|\mathcal{A}|-2)(|\mathcal{A}|-3)-6|\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|-1)(|\mathcal{A}|-3)

\displaystyle-4|\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|-1)+3(p-2)|\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|-1)+(p-1)|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle+8\sqrt{p}|\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|-1)(|\mathcal{A}|-2)+12|\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|-1)

\displaystyle-4|\mathcal{A}|(|\mathcal{A}|-1)+4(p-1)|\mathcal{A}|,

from which and (
2.5
) the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are two subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{p},
then

\displaystyle p|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\leqslant

\displaystyle(p-|\mathcal{A}|)(p-|\mathcal{B}|).

Proof.

Using the orthogonality of additive characters, by
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{p},
we derive that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi_{2}(x)\mathop{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}}_{a+b=x}1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\psi(a)\Big)\Big(\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\psi(b)\Big)\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi_{2}(x)\psi(-x)\Big),

where
\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
denotes the group of additive characters of

\mathbb{F}_{p}.
The last sum over
x
vanishes if
\psi
is trivial and is
of modulus
\sqrt{p}
otherwise. Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{1\neq\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Big|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\psi(a)\Big|\Big|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\psi(b)\Big|,

from which and Cauchy’s inequality we find

\displaystyle p(|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{1\neq\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Big|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\psi(a)\Big|^{2}\times\sum_{1\neq\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Big|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\psi(b)\Big|^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\neq\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Big|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\psi(a)\Big|^{2}=\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Big|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\psi(a)\Big|^{2}-|\mathcal{A}|^{2}=p|\mathcal{A}|-|\mathcal{A}|^{2},

and a similar identity can also be derived for the other sum. We
now arrive at

\displaystyle p(|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(p|\mathcal{A}|-|\mathcal{A}|^{2})(p|\mathcal{B}|-|\mathcal{B}|^{2}),

from which Lemma
2.4
follows.
∎

).

.

For any

Remark 2
.

The first inequality illustrates that

2|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
as
\mathfrak{A}/|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
decreases to zero. Noting that

\mathfrak{A}\leqslant|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|\leqslant|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|
, by
(
4.2
) we have

|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|\leqslant|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\leqslant|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|+\sqrt{(E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})-\mathfrak{A})(|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|-\mathfrak{A})}.

It follows that

|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|
approaches to
|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
as
\mathfrak{A}

grows, as long as the additive energy
E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is under
control.

Proof.

From Hölder’s inequality, for any
\theta>0
we have

\displaystyle M_{0}\leqslant M_{1}^{\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}}M_{-\theta}^{\frac{1}{\theta+1}}.

It follows that

(4.3)

\displaystyle 2^{\theta}M_{0}^{\theta+1}\leqslant M_{1}^{\theta}2^{\theta}M_{-\theta}.

Moreover, we also find

\displaystyle M_{-\theta}-\mathfrak{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\\
r(x)\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}r(x)^{-\theta}\leqslant 2^{-\theta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\\
r(x)\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}1=\frac{M_{0}-\mathfrak{A}}{2^{\theta}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle 2^{\theta}M_{-\theta}\leqslant M_{0}+(2^{\theta}-1)\mathfrak{A},

from which and (
4.3
), we may conclude
(
4.1
).

Cauchy’s inequality yields

\displaystyle(M_{1}-\mathfrak{A})^{2}=\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\\
r(x)\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}r(x)\Big)^{2}\leqslant\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\\
r(x)\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}r(x)^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\\
r(x)\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}1\Big),

from which it follows that

\displaystyle(|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|-\mathfrak{A})^{2}\leqslant(E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})-\mathfrak{A})(|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|-\mathfrak{A}).

This proves (
4.2
), and thus completes the proof of
Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If

\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\tau|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2^{1-\tau}|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|.

Proof.

From (
4.1
) it follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\geqslant\frac{2|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|^{1+\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|+(2^{\theta}-1)\tau|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}=2(1+(2^{\theta}-1)\tau)^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\cdot|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|.

Taking
\theta\rightarrow 0^{+},
we arrive at

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2^{1-\tau}|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose
that
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\kappa(p-1)
. Since
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
with

|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2
, it follows that
p\geqslant 37
in
view of Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
. From Lemma

2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle(p-1)|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\leqslant p(p-|\mathcal{A}|-|\mathcal{B}|)\leqslant p(p-2\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|}).

This gives

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\Big(\frac{p}{\sqrt{p}+1}\Big)^{2}.

On the other hand, Lemma
4.2
shows

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2^{1-2\kappa}|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}|=\frac{1}{4^{\kappa}}\cdot(p-1).

We hence infer

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{\kappa}}\cdot(p-1)\leqslant\Big(\frac{p}{\sqrt{p}+1}\Big)^{2},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\kappa\log 2\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\log(p-1)+\log(\sqrt{p}+1)-\log p\geqslant\frac{\sqrt{p}}{p-1}-\frac{1.6\log 2}{p-1}

for
p\geqslant 37.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
in view of
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\kappa(p-1)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We
also assume that
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
with
|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2

and write
\mathfrak{A}=\kappa(p-1)
. By Theorem

1.1
and (
4.4
),
we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|(2\sqrt{p}-1)\geqslant|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\geqslant\frac{1}{4^{\kappa}}\cdot(p-1),

giving

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^{\kappa}}\cdot\frac{p-1}{\sqrt{p}-1/2}\geqslant\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^{\kappa}}\cdot\sqrt{p}.

A similar lower bound for
|\mathcal{B}|
also holds by symmetry.

On the other hand, we infer from (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\leqslant(\kappa+\sqrt{(E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})/(p-1)-\kappa)(1/2-\kappa)})(p-1).

This, together with (
4.4
), yields

\displaystyle E(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\geqslant\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2^{2-4\kappa}-2^{3-2\kappa}\kappa+4\kappa-1}{2-4\kappa}\Big)(p-1)

as desired.

5.
Concluding remarks

5.1.
More bounds for possible decompositions

Theorem
1.1
shows that
|\mathcal{A}|/|\mathcal{B}|

should be between
\frac{1}{8}
and
8
if
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
is a

2
-decomposition with
|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2.

In fact, the above arguments may give better bounds if the size of
|\mathcal{A}|/|\mathcal{B}|
is taken into account.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
p
be a large prime. Assume that
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
is a

2
-decomposition with
|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant|\mathcal{A}|\geqslant 2
and

|\mathcal{A}|=\delta|\mathcal{B}|
with
\delta\in(\frac{1}{8},1].
Then

\displaystyle\sqrt{\delta(p-1)/2}\leqslant|\mathcal{A}|\leqslant\min\{2\delta,\sqrt{\delta}\}\sqrt{p-1},

and

\displaystyle\sqrt{(p-1)/(2\delta)}\leqslant|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\min\{2,\sqrt{\delta^{-1}}\}\sqrt{p-1}.

In fact, from (
3.1
) it
follows that

(5.1)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\geqslant\sqrt{\delta(p-1)/2},\quad|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant\sqrt{(p-1)/(2\delta)}.

By Lemma
2.4
we have
|\mathcal{B}||\mathcal{A}|\leqslant p-1
. Thus,

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\leqslant\sqrt{\delta(p-1)},\quad|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\sqrt{(p-1)/\delta}.

On the other hand, we infer from Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sqrt{p}-1\leqslant 2\sqrt{p-1}

and also

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|=\delta|\mathcal{B}|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\delta\sqrt{p-1}.

Hence Proposition
5.1
follows by collecting the above two inequalities.

In particular, if
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{R}_{p}
is a
2
-decomposition with

|\mathcal{A}|=|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant 2,
then

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{p-1}{2}}\leqslant|\mathcal{A}|=|\mathcal{B}|\leqslant\sqrt{p-1}

as in Proposition
5.1
. The constant

1/\sqrt{2}\approx 0.7071
is much larger than
1/4=0.25
as in Theorem
1.1
.

5.2.
Comments on the character sum
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbf{a};p)

Recall the quantities
c_{k}(p)
and
c_{k}
defined in Section
2
to bound the complete character sum
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbf{a};p)
.
It is clear that
c_{2}(p)=-1/\sqrt{p}
, and the determination of
c_{k}(p)
is no longer elementary for
k\geqslant 4.
Here is our observation.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
k\geqslant 2
be a fixed even integer.
For all sufficiently large primes
p,
we have

c_{k}(p)=k-2+o(1).

In particular, we have
c_{k}=k-2.

The fact that
c_{4}(p)=2+o(1)
follows from a “vertical” version of Sato–Tate conjecture for elliptic curves over finite fields as proven by B.J. Birch
[
Bi68
]
. In general, the value of
c_{k}(p)
corresponds to a related equidistribution of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g:=k/2-1

defined by

\displaystyle E:y^{2}=(1+(a_{2}-a_{1})x)\cdots((1+(a_{k}-a_{1})x)

with pairwise distinct
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.
N.M. Katz and P. Sarnak
[
KS99
]
proved an equidistribution for hyperelliptic curves (in a more general setting) in families. In fact, they showed that the corresponding Frobenius classes become equidistributed in the unitary symplectic group
\mathrm{USp}(k-2)
as
p\rightarrow+\infty
. More precisely, as long as
(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
runs over all possibilities with pairwise distinct coordinates, the
normalized
character sum

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi_{2}((1+(a_{2}-a_{1})x)\cdots((1+(a_{k}-a_{1})x))

becomes equidistributed in
[2-k,k-2]
, as
p\rightarrow+\infty
, with respect to the Haar measure with image in
[0,2\pi)^{g}
given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g!}\Big(\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant g}(2\cos\theta_{i}-2\cos\theta_{j})^{2}\Big)\cdot\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant g}\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta_{j}\mathrm{d}\theta_{j}.

Keeping the uniformality in
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k},
it is not possible to improve the constant
k-2
in Lemma
2.2
.
On the other hand, if relaxing the above uniformality, it is highly desired that one may obtain a much sharper upper bound for
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbf{a};p)
for many
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}.
This is indeed the case when
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
runs over all possibilities as indicated by the equidistribution of Katz–Sarnak. Unfortunately, the range of
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
should be quite restrictive in our applications to Theorem
1.1
, and it seems quite difficult to recover a similar equidistribution when
\mathbf{a}
runs over quite a thin family without friendly structures.

By closing this section, we would like to mention a dual but not quite related problem on behaviours of
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbf{a};p)
. Instead of varying
\mathbf{a}
with a very large
p,
we may alternatively consider the distribution of
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbf{a};p)
, with fixed
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k},
when
p
runs over all large primes.
The case
k=4
can be covered directly by the Sato–Tate conjecture for non-CM elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
as proven in a series of papers by L. Clozel, M. Harris and R. Taylor
[
CHT08
]
, R. Taylor
[
Ta08
]
and M. Harris, N. Shepherd-Barron and R. Taylor
[
HST10
]
, together with the CM case solved much earlier by E. Hecke
[
He18
]
. For
k\geqslant 6
, one should refer to a suitable generalization of Sato–Tate conjecture in the framework of hyperelliptic curves; see
[
KS09
]
for instance.

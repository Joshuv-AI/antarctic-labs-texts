The minimal ramification problem for rational function fields over finite fields Thanks: MSC2010: 11T55 (primary), 11E25, 12F10 (secondary)
Lior Bary-Soroker, Alexei Entin, Arno Fehm

Abstract
. We study the minimal number of ramified primes in Galois extensions of rational function fields over finite fields with prescribed finite Galois group. In particular, we obtain a general conjecture in analogy with the well studied case of number fields, which we establish for abelian, symmetric and alternating groups in many cases.

Based on class field theory, Boston and Markin came to the following conjecture:

The geometric situation

For every nontrivial finite group

For every prime power

There we also discuss the contribution of De Witt

2.
Preliminaries and notation

We start by collecting a few definitions and statements that we will use throughout the paper.

2.1.
Function fields

For a thorough introduction to the subject see
[
Ros02
,
Sti09
]
. Here we only recall some terminology, notation and a few basic facts.
Let
k
be a base field. A function field (of one variable) over
k
is a finite extension of the rational function field
k(T)
.
A
global function field
is a function field over a finite base field
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
(this will be our main case of interest).

A function field
K/k
is
regular

if
K
is linearly disjoint over
k
from the algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of
k
,
which in case that
k
is perfect simply means that

k
is relatively algebraically closed in
K
.
If
K/k
is a regular function field, there exists a unique geometrically integral non-singular projective curve
C/k
such that
K
is the function field of
C
. Conversely the function field of a curve as above is a regular function field over
k
.

Let
K/k
be a regular function field and
C
the corresponding curve.
A
prime

P
of
K/k
is an
equivalence class of nontrivial absolute values on
K
trivial on
k
. Each such absolute value corresponds to a discrete valuation
v
,
and it corresponds to a prime divisor on
C
.
If
P
is a prime of
K
, the residue field
k(P)
at
P
is a finite extension of
k
and
\deg P=[k(P):k]
is called the
degree
of
P
. By a
point
or
geometric point
of
C
we will always mean a closed point of
C\times_{k}\bar{k}
.
For an extension
L/k
we denote by
C(L)
the set of
L
-rational points on
C
.

The rational function field
k(T)
corresponds to the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
and has primes of two types: the
finite primes
of the form
\mathrm{div}(f)_{0}
(the zero divisor of a rational function
f
) where
f\in k[X]
is an irreducible polynomial (such a prime has degree
\deg f
) and the
infinite prime

\infty=\mathrm{div}(1/T)_{0}
which has degree 1.

Let
L/K
be a finite extension of function fields over
k
. It is called
regular
, or
geometric
, if
L
is also a regular function field over
k
. Regular extensions correspond to finite covers of the associated curves.
If
P
is a prime of
K
and
Q
a prime of
L
lying over
P
, we denote by
e(Q/P)
the ramification index of
Q
over
P
.
We say that
L/K
is ramified (resp. wildly ramified) at
Q
if
e(Q/P)>1
(resp.
\mathrm{char}(k)|e(Q/P)
). We say that
L/K
is ramified (resp. wildly ramified)
over

P
if there exists a prime
Q
of
L
lying over
P
at which the extension is ramified (resp. wildly ramified).
Ramification which is not wild is said to be
tame
.
If
L/K
is tamely ramified over at least one prime, then
L/K
is separable.

If
C_{L}
and
C_{K}
are the underlying curves then the extension
L/K
corresponds to a finite morphism
w\colon C_{L}\to C_{K}
.
This correspondence defines an equivalence of categories between regular function fields over
k
and absolutely irreducible non-singular projective curves defined over
k
.
If
P
is a prime divisor of
C_{L}
, and
Q=w(P)
(a prime divisor of
C_{K}
), we say that
w
is ramified (resp. tamely ramified) at
P
whenever the extension
L/K
is ramified (resp. tamely ramified) at the corresponding prime of
L
which we identify with
P
.
In this case we also call the geometric points corresponding to
P

ramification points
of
w
,
and the geometric points corresponding to
Q

branch points
of
w
.

2.2.
Ramification theory

Let
K
be a field. For a separable polynomial
f\in K[X]
of degree
n
we define its Galois group
{\rm Gal}(f/K)
to be the Galois group of its splitting field over
K
.
We will always interpret the Galois group
{\rm Gal}(f/K)
as a subgroup of the symmetric group
S_{n}
via its action on the roots of

. The embedding into
S_{n}
is well-defined up to conjugation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K
be a function field over an algebraically closed field
k

of characteristic
p\geq 0
,
let
f\in K[X]
be a separable irreducible polynomial and let
L=K(\alpha)
where
\alpha
is a root of

.
Let
P
be a prime (finite or infinite) of

and let

be the primes of

lying over
P
.
Let
e_{i}=e(Q_{i}/P)
be the ramification indices.

(i)

Assume that
p\nmid e_{i}
for all

.
Then
{\rm Gal}(f/K)
contains a permutation which is a product of
r
disjoint cycles of lengths
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
.

(ii)

Assume that

p\nmid e_{i}
and
(e_{i},e_{1})=1
for
2\leq i\leq r
,
and either
e_{1}=p
or
p\nmid e_{1}
.
Then
{\rm Gal}(f/K)
contains a cycle of length
e_{1}
.

Proof.

See
[
Abh93
, §3, Generalized Cycle Lemma]
.∎

Lemma 2.2

(Abhyankar’s Lemma)
.

Let

be perfect,

a function field, and

finite separable extensions of

Let

Assume that

2.4.
Discriminants

Let
K
be a field.
For a polynomial
f\in K[X]
of degree
n
we denote its leading coefficient by
\mathrm{lc}(f)
,
and if
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\overline{K}
are the zeros of
f
(listed with multiplicity), the discriminant of
f
is defined by

(2.1)

\Delta(f)\;=\;\mathrm{lc}(f)^{2n-2}\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{2}\in K.

Sometimes we will use the notation
\Delta_{X}(f)
to indicate the variable with respect to which the discriminant is taken.

If
0\neq f,g\in K[X]
have roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
respectively
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
, their resultant is defined by

(2.2)

\mathrm{Res}(f,g)\;=\;\mathrm{lc}(f)^{{\rm deg}(g)}\mathrm{lc}(g)^{{\rm deg}(f)}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j})\;=\;\mathrm{lc}(f)^{{\rm deg}(g)}\prod_{i=1}^{m}g(\alpha_{i})\in K.

We will now summarize some formulae which are useful for computing discriminants, valid in any characteristic. See
[
CCG08
, §3]
for the proofs (but note that there
\Delta(f)
is defined without the factor
\mathrm{lc}(f)^{2n-2}
). First we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{\deg f\cdot(\deg f-1)}{2}+\deg f\cdot\deg f^{\prime}}\mathrm{lc}(f)^{\deg f-\deg f^{\prime}-2}\mathrm{Res}(f^{\prime},f)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{\deg f\cdot(\deg f-1)}{2}+\deg f\cdot\deg f^{\prime}}\mathrm{lc}(f)^{\deg f-\deg f^{\prime}-2}\mathrm{lc}(f^{\prime})^{\deg f}\prod_{f^{\prime}(\rho)=0}f(\rho)

provided
f^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Here
f^{\prime}
is the formal derivative of
f
and the product is over all roots of
f^{\prime}
counted with multiplicity.
Now let
f,g,h\in K[X]
.
From (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) we see that

(2.5)

\Delta(fg)=\Delta(f)\Delta(g)\mathrm{Res}(f,g)^{2}

The resultant is bi-multiplicative, i.e.

(2.6)

\mathrm{Res}(f,gh)\;=\;\mathrm{Res}(f,g)\mathrm{Res}(f,h),\quad\mathrm{Res}(fg,h)\;=\;\mathrm{Res}(f,h)\mathrm{Res}(g,h)

and satisfies a symmetry property

(2.7)

\mathrm{Res}(f,g)=(-1)^{\deg f\cdot\deg g}\mathrm{Res}(g,f).

Finally if
g\equiv h\pmod{f}
then

(2.8)

\mathrm{Res}(f,g)=\mathrm{lc}(f)^{\deg g-\deg h}\mathrm{Res}(f,h).

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\mathrm{char}(K)\neq 2
and
f\in K[X]
is a separable polynomial of degree
n
then
{\rm Gal}(f/K)\subseteq A_{n}
if and only if
\Delta(f)
is a square in
K
.
In particular,
{\rm Gal}(f/K(\sqrt{\Delta(f)}))={\rm Gal}(f/K)\cap A_{n}
.

Proof.

This standard fact is easy to deduce from (
2.1
).∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
K=k(T)
and
f\in k[T,X]
irreducible and monic in
X
.
If
P\in k[T]
is a finite prime which is ramified in the splitting field of

over

, then
P|\Delta(f)
.
Moreover if

exactly once, then in the extension

, where
\alpha
is a root of

there is exactly one ramified prime lying over

Proof.

This standard fact can be easily deduced from the properties of the different and its connection to the discriminant, see e.g.
[
Ros02
, Proposition 7.9 and Corollary 7.10.2]
.

2.5.
Critical points and critical values

Let
k
be a perfect field
and let
w\in k(X)
be a non-constant rational function.
One can view
w
also as a morphism
w\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
k
.
For convenience we will use
X
for the variable in the domain of
w
, which we denote
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}
and
U
in the range of
w
, which we denote
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{U}
and we adopt a similar convention for the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
. A point
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}(\bar{k})
is called a
critical point
of
w
if
w
is ramified at
\alpha
,
i.e. if the corresponding prime of
\bar{k}(X)
is ramified in the extension of function fields
\bar{k}(X)/\bar{k}(U)
induced by
w
.
A point
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{U}(\bar{k})
is called a
critical value
for
w
if there exists a critical point
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}(\bar{k})
such that
\beta=w(\alpha)
, or equivalently, if
w
is branched over
\beta
.

The following lemma gives a useful description of the critical points and values of a rational function in terms of derivatives and discriminants.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
w=\frac{f}{c}
with
f,c\in k[X]
,
(f,c)=1
, be a non-constant rational function.

(i)

Let
\alpha\in\bar{k}\setminus w^{-1}(\infty)
,
let
e
be the ramification index of
w
at
\alpha

and
\nu
the order of the derivative
w^{\prime}
at
\alpha
.
Then
e=\nu+1
if the ramification at
\alpha
is tame,
and
e\leq\nu
otherwise.
In particular,
\alpha
is a critical point of
w
iff
\alpha
is a root of
f^{\prime}c-fc^{\prime}
.

(ii)

Assume additionally that
\deg f>\deg c
,
f^{\prime}\neq 0
, and that
c
is separable.
Then the finite critical values of
w
are the roots of
\Delta_{X}(f-Uc)
, more precisely

\Delta_{X}(f(X)-Uc(X))=a\cdot\prod_{(f^{\prime}c-fc^{\prime})(\alpha)=0}(U-w(\alpha)),

with
a\in k^{\times}
and the product over the roots of
f^{\prime}c-fc^{\prime}
in
\bar{k}
with multiplicity.

Proof.

(i).
We identify
U=w(X)
, viewing
U
as an element of
k(X)
. This gives the extension of function fields
k(X)/k(U)
corresponding to the rational map
w
. Let
\alpha\in\bar{k}
be such that
c(\alpha)\neq 0
and denote
\beta=w(\alpha)
. Then
X-\alpha
is a local parameter for
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}
at the point
\alpha
and
U-\beta
is a local parameter for
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{U}
at the point
\beta
.
We have
U-\beta=w(X)-\beta=(X-\alpha)^{e}u(X)

with
u\in k(X)
which has no pole at
\alpha
.
Taking derivatives we obtain

w^{\prime}(X)=e(X-\alpha)^{e-1}u(X)+(X-\alpha)^{e}u^{\prime}(X).

As
\alpha
is neither a pole nor a zero of
u
, and hence also not a pole of
u^{\prime}
,
the multiplicity
\nu
of the zero
\alpha
of
w^{\prime}

is
\nu=e-1
if
{\rm char}(k)\nmid e
, and
\nu\geq e
if
{\rm char}(k)\mid e
.

(ii).
Denote
h=(f^{\prime},c^{\prime})\in\bar{k}[X]
and write
f^{\prime}=hu,c^{\prime}=hv,u,v\in\bar{k}[X]
.
As
f^{\prime}\neq 0
, also
u\neq 0
.
If
\deg c=0
the
assertion of (ii) is immediate from (i) and (
2.4
), so let us assume
\deg c>0
and then

c^{\prime},v\neq 0
by our assumption that
c
is separable. Using the properties of resultants and
discriminants (
2.2
),(
2.3
),(
2.6
),(
2.7
),(
2.8
) and using
\sim
to denote
equality up to a non-zero multiplicative constant (in
k
),
we calculate:

\displaystyle\Delta_{X}(f-Uc)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:disc})}}{{\sim}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}(f^{\prime}-Uc^{\prime},f-Uc)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:resbimult})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}(h,f-Uc)\cdot\mathrm{Res}(u-Uv,f-Uc)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:resbimult})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}(h,f-Uc)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Res}(u-Uv,fv-Ucv)}{\mathrm{Res}(u-Uv,v)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:ressym}),(\ref{eq:resalt})}}{{\sim}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}(h,f-Uc)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Res}(u-Uv,fv-uc)}{\mathrm{Res}(u,v)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:ressym})}}{{\sim}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}(h,f-Uc)\cdot\mathrm{Res}(uc-fv,u-Uv)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:resdefinition})}}{{\sim}}

\displaystyle\prod_{h(\alpha)=0}\left(c(\alpha)U-f(\alpha)\right)\prod_{(uc-fv)(\alpha)=0}(u(\alpha)-Uv(\alpha))

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\prod_{h(\alpha)=0}\left(U-\frac{f(\alpha)}{c(\alpha)}\right)\prod_{(uc-fv)(\alpha)=0}\left(U-\frac{f^{\prime}(\alpha)}{c^{\prime}(\alpha)}\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\prod_{(f^{\prime}c-fc^{\prime})(\alpha)=0}(U-w(\alpha)).

In the last line we used the fact that for a root
\alpha
of
(f^{\prime}c-fc^{\prime})/h
we have
f^{\prime}(\alpha)/c^{\prime}(\alpha)=w(\alpha)
.
∎

2.6.
Monodromy of rational functions

The definitions and results in this subsection will be used only in Section
4.2
.
Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 0
.
The
(arithmetic) monodromy

\mathrm{Mon}(w)
of a non-constant rational function
w\in k(X)

for which the extension
k(X)/k(w)
is separable
is the Galois group of the Galois closure of
k(X)/k(w)
.
When ambiguity about the base field may arise we write
\mathrm{Mon}_{k}(w)
.
We usually view
\mathrm{Mon}(w)
as a subgroup of
S_{n}
with
n=\deg w
via its action on the generic fiber of the corresponding morphism
w\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
A rational function
w\in k(X)
is called
indecomposable
if it cannot be written as a composition
w=u\circ v
with
u,v\in k(X)
where
\deg u,\deg v>1
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
w\in k(X)
with
k(X)/k(w)
separable.
Then
w
is indecomposable if and only if

\mathrm{Mon}(w)\leqslant S_{n}
is primitive.

Proof.

If
w=u\circ v
with
\deg u>1
,
\deg v>1
,
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in\overline{k(T)}
are the roots of
u-T
,
then
v^{-1}(\alpha_{1}),\dots,v^{-1}(\alpha_{m})
is a nontrivial partition of the roots of
w-T
,
which is preserved by
\mathrm{Mon}(w)
.
The converse implication is proven for example in
[
Fri70
, Lemma 2]
in the case of polynomials,
and the case of rational functions can be proven similarly using Lüroth’s theorem.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

Let
w\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a tamely ramified rational function defined over an algebraically closed field
k
.
The
ramification type
of
w
(called
passport
in
[
AZ15
]
) is the unordered tuple of partitions
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
, where
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
are the distinct branch points of
w
and
\lambda_{i}
is the partition of
n=\deg w
given by the ramification indices over
b_{i}
.
We write partitions of
n
as
n_{1}^{e_{1}}\dots n_{r}^{e_{r}}
with
0\leq n_{1}<\dots<n_{r}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}n_{i}=n
.

We recall that the inertia subgroup of
\mathrm{Mon}(w)\leqslant S_{n}
corresponding to a branch point
b_{i}
of
the tamely ramified rational function
w
is a cyclic subgroup of
S_{n}
with generator of cycle type
precisely the partition of
n
given by the ramification indices over
b_{i}
.
Moreover,
\mathrm{Mon}(w)
is generated by the inertia subgroups.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
w\colon\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
be a tamely ramified rational function defined over an algebraically closed field
k
with monodromy group
G\leqslant S_{n}
and ramification type
\Pi=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
. Then there exists a rational function
W\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
with the same monodromy and ramification type.

Proof.

This is essentially a direct consequence of the comparison theorem for the étale
fundamental group
[
GR71
, §XIII, Corollaire 2.12]
. We use the terminology and notation of

[
GR71
, §XIII]
. Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
be the branch points of
w
and denote
U=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
. By
[
GR71
, §XIII, Corollaire 2.12]
there exist elements

x_{i}\in\pi_{1}^{t}(U),1\leq i\leq r
such that
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
generate
\pi_{1}^{t}(U)
,
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}=1
and each
x_{i}
generates a tame inertia group at
b_{i}
.

The map
w
induces a finite étale cover
V=w^{-1}(U)\to U
and therefore (using the assumption that
w
is tamely ramified) a surjection
\delta\colon\pi_{1}^{t}(U)\twoheadrightarrow G
. Denote
\sigma_{i}=\delta(x_{i})
. The elements
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}
generate
G
, satisfy
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}=1
and each
\sigma_{i}\in G\leqslant S_{n}
has cycle structure
\lambda_{i}
. Now by the Riemann Existence Theorem (see e.g.
[
Völ96
, Corollary 5.11]
) there exists a finite irreducible cover
W\colon Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
with monodromy group
G
and ramification type
\Pi
. The genus of
Y
can be determined by the Riemann-Hurwitz formula from its ramification type and is therefore 0 (since the same calculation gives the genus of the domain of
w
, since
w
is tamely ramified).
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
w\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}

be a rational function of degree
n>1
over the algebraically closed field
k

with monodromy group
\mathrm{Mon}(w)\leqslant S_{n}
.
Assume that
w
has at most two critical values
a,b
,
and that
w^{-1}(a)
contains only one critical point
a^{\prime}
.
If the ramification index of
a^{\prime}
is prime, and all ramification over
b
is
tame, then
\mathrm{Mon}(w)
is primitive.

Proof.

Let
L
be the Galois closure of the extension
k(X)/k(T)
induced by
w
.
Let
k(T)\subseteq M\subsetneq k(X)
be an intermediate extension.
Since there is only one ramified prime of
k(X)
over
T=a
and it has prime ramification index,
the extension
M/k(T)
has to be unramified over
T=a
.
However, it is also tamely ramified over
T=b
and unramified elsewhere,
hence
M=k(T)
by the Riemann-Hurwitz formula.
So
k(X)/k(T)
is a minimal proper extension,
hence by Galois theory
{\rm Gal}(L/k(X))
is maximal in
{\rm Gal}(L/k(T))
,
which means that
\mathrm{Mon}(w)={\rm Gal}(L/k(T))\leqslant S_{n}
is primitive.
∎

3.
Abelian groups

In this section we first prove the group-theoretic fact mentioned in Remark
1.5

and then verify Conjecture
1.4
for abelian groups.

If
G
is a group and
a\in G
is an element we denote by
a^{G}
the conjugacy class of
a
. For a subset
S\subseteq G
we denote
S^{G}=\bigcup_{a\in S}a^{G}
.
Recall that for a finite group
G
we denote by
p(G)
the subgroup generated by its
p
-Sylow subgroups.
A
cyclic-by-
p
group is an extension of a cyclic group by a
p
-group.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group. There exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in G
with
r=\max\{d(G^{\mathrm{ab}}),1\}
such that
G=\langle x_{1}^{G},\ldots,x_{r}^{G}\rangle
.

Proof.

This is a special case of the main theorem in
[
Kut76
]
,
see also
[
Bae64
]
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p
a prime number. There exist subgroups
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}
of
G
such that
r=\max\{d((G/p(G))^{\mathrm{ab}}),1\}
, each
Q_{i}
is cyclic-by-
p
and
G=\langle Q_{1}^{G},\ldots,Q_{r}^{G}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
H=G/p(G)
, so that
r=\max\{d(H^{\mathrm{ab}}),1\}
.
By Lemma
3.1
there exist
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\in H
with
H=\langle y_{1}^{H},\ldots,y_{r}^{H}\rangle
.
Let
P\leqslant G
be a
p
-Sylow subgroup. If
P=1
, i.e.
(|G|,p)=1
, then the claim holds with
Q_{i}=\langle y_{i}\rangle
, so we may assume
P\neq 1
. By Frattini’s argument
[
Gor80
, Theorem 3.7]
we have
p(G)N_{G}(P)=G
, so we can choose
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in N_{G}(P)
such that
y_{i}=p(G)x_{i}
.
Denote
Q_{i}=\langle x_{i}\rangle P
. Each
Q_{i}
is a subgroup of
G
since
x_{i}\in N_{G}(P)
, it is cyclic-by-
p
since it is an extension of
\langle x_{i}\rangle/(\langle x_{i}\rangle\cap P)
by
P
, and finally since

p(G)=\langle P^{G}\rangle
and
G=\langle p(G),x_{1}^{G},\ldots,x_{r}^{G}\rangle
we have
G=\langle P^{G},x_{1}^{G},\ldots,x_{r}^{G}\rangle=\langle Q_{1}^{G},\ldots Q_{r}^{G}\rangle
, as required.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Conjecture
1.4
holds for
G
abelian.

Proof.

Let
q=p^{\nu}
and let
G
be a nontrivial finite abelian group.
The claim is that
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G)=1
if
G
is a
p
-group and
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G)=d(G/p(G))
otherwise.

We first prove the lower bound.
Let
L/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
be an abelian Galois extension with Galois group
G
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
algebraically closed in
L
.
If
G
is a
p
-group, the lower bound
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G)\geq 1
follows from the Riemann-Hurwitz formula.
If
G
is not a
p
-group, then
G/p(G)
is nontrivial,
and since
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G)\geq r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G/p(G))
we can assume that
p(G)=1
.
Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
be all the finite primes of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
ramified in
L
, and let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}
be the corresponding inertia groups (note that
I_{i}
are well defined since the extension is abelian). Then each
I_{i}
is cyclic, as
P_{i}
is tamely ramified in
L
.
As also the infinite prime of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
is tamely ramified in
L
,

G=\left<I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\right>
, cf.
[
Ser08
, Proposition 4.4.6]
.
In particular,
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G)\geq k\geq d(G)
.

To show that the lower bound is tight, we construct a suitable Galois extension.
Write
G=G/p(G)\times p(G)
, let
k
and
p^{\alpha}
be the exponents of
G/p(G)
respectively
p(G)
,
and let
d=\max\{d(G/p(G)),1\}
.
Choose distinct primes
P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}
such that
k(q-1)\mid q^{\deg P_{i}}-1
(i.e. such that the multiplicative order of
q
modulo
k(q-1)
divides
\deg P_{i}
).
Let
R_{i}
be the completion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
at
P_{i}
and
E_{i}
its fraction field.
By
[
Neu99
, Proposition II.5.7]
,
E_{i}^{\times}=\left<\pi\right>\times(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg P_{i}}})^{\times}\times G_{i}
, where

\pi
is a uniformizer and

G_{i}=1+\pi\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg P_{i}}}[[\pi]]\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathbb{N}}.

In particular,
R_{i}^{\times}=(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg P_{i}}})^{\times}\times G_{i}
.
By class field theory (take inverse limit in
[
Hay74
, Proposition 2.2, Theorem 2.3, and Theorem 3.2]
) the maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
which is regular over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, tamely ramified at infinity, and unramified outside
\{P_{i},\infty\}
has Galois group
R_{i}^{\times}
and the tame inertia group at
\infty
corresponds to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Hence
R_{i}^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension that is regular over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and unramified outside
\{P_{i}\}
. As
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
has no unramified extensions regular over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, those extensions for different
i
are linearly disjoint, so the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension that is regular over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and unramified outside
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}\}
is isomorphic to

\prod_{i=1}^{d}R_{i}^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{d}C_{(q^{\deg P_{i}}-1)/(q-1)}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathbb{N}}.

By the assumption that
k\mid(q^{\deg P_{i}}-1)/(q-1)
, we get that
G/p(G)
is a quotient of
\prod_{i}C_{(q^{\deg P_{i}}-1)/(q-1)}
(this is vacantly true if
G/p(G)=1
). Obviously,
p(G)
is a quotient of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Hence
G=G/p(G)\times p(G)
is a quotient of
\prod_{i}R_{i}^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. This implies we can realize
G
as the Galois group of a geometric Galois extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
unramified outside
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}\}
, as needed.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For every prime power
q=p^{\nu}
and every nontrivial finite group

,

4.
S_{n}
and
A_{n}
for
p>n

In this section we obtain Galois extensions of group
S_{n}
and
A_{n}
with ramification over at most two primes
in the case where
n
is smaller than the characteristic
p
.
We start with splitting fields of polynomials
of the form
f(X)-T
in Section
4.1

and
f(X)-Tc(X)
in Section
4.2
,
which produce extensions with one ramified finite prime
and tame ramification over infinity.
In Section
4.3

we then explain how to eliminate the tame ramification at infinity
in many cases.
Finally, in Section
4.4

we present yet another approach to obtain extensions with two ramified primes,
by working with trinomials and applying recent results on small gaps between primes in function fields.

We recall that
if
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T,X]
is monic in
X
,
then the only primes of
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
that possibly ramify in
the splitting field
L
of
f
over
K

are the divisors of the discriminant
\Delta_{X}(f)
, and possibly the infinite prime of
K
(Lemma
2.7
).
In particular, if
\Delta_{X}(f)
is irreducible or, more generally, a prime power, at most two primes of
K
ramify in
L
.

4.1.
Two ramified primes via Morse polynomials

We start with splitting fields of polynomials of the simple from
f(X)-T
,
where in some cases the classical theory of Morse polynomials
produces suitable extensions of group
S_{n}
with two ramified primes.
Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p\neq 2
,
and recall that
a polynomial
f\in k[X]
of degree
n
not divisible by
p
is
Morse
if it has exactly
n-1
distinct critical values, i.e.
the roots
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
of
f^{\prime}
in
\bar{k}
are simple and

f(\alpha_{i})\neq f(\alpha_{j})
for
i\neq j
.
If
f
is Morse, then

{\rm Gal}(f(X)-T/\overline{k}(T))=S_{n}

by
[
Ser08
, Theorem 4.4.5]
,
so the splitting field of
f(X)-T
over
k(T)

is geometric with Galois group
S_{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in k[X]
be Morse of degree
n
with
p\nmid n
.
Then
f^{\prime}
is irreducible if and only if
D(T):=\Delta_{X}(f(X)-T)\in k[T]
is irreducible.

Proof.

Let
A
be the set of roots of
f^{\prime}
, and
G={\rm Gal}(k(A)/k)
.
By (
2.4
) or Lemma
2.8
,
D\sim\prod_{a\in A}(T-f(a))

(where as before
\sim
denotes equality up to a non-zero constant).
The fact that
f
is Morse implies that
f
is injective on
A
,
so
f
induces an isomorphism of
G
-sets
A\rightarrow f(A)
.
In particular,
k(f(a))=k(a)
for every
a\in A
,
hence
D\sim\prod_{a\in A}(T-f(a))
is irreducible
if and only if
f^{\prime}\sim\prod_{a\in A}(X-a)
is.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
q=p^{\nu}
with
p>n
.
There are
\frac{q^{n}}{n-1}+O_{n}(q^{n-1})
many
monic Morse
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree
n

such that the splitting field of
f(X)-T
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
is geometric with Galois group
S_{n}
,
and
\Delta_{X}(f(X)-T)
is irreducible.

Proof.

Let
M_{n}\cong\mathbb{A}^{n}
be the space of monic polynomials of degree
n
(identified with the
n
-tuple of non-leading coefficients) considered as a variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The subset
\mathcal{M}\subseteq M_{n}
of monic Morse polynomials of degree
n

is Zariski-open and dense
with complement
M_{n}\setminus\mathcal{M}
the zero set of a polynomial in the coefficients
a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}

of degree
O_{n}(1)
, see
[
Gey99
, Proposition 4.3 and (2) in its proof]
.
Thus
the elementary estimate
[
LW54
, Lemma 1]
gives that
the set
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of
f\in M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
that are Morse
satisfies

|\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q^{n}+O_{n}(q^{n-1})
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of
f\in M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

for which
f^{\prime}
is irreducible.
As
p>n
, the map
M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rightarrow M_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
f\mapsto\frac{1}{n}f^{\prime}

is surjective with fibers of size
q
,
so by the Prime Polynomial Theorem,

|\mathcal{P}|=\frac{q^{n}}{n-1}+O_{n}(q^{(n-1)/2})
.
It follows that

|\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cap\mathcal{P}|=|\mathcal{P}\setminus(M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))|=\frac{q^{n}}{n-1}+O_{n}(q^{n-1}),

since by the above
|M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=O_{n}(q^{n-1})
.
Now for every
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
the splitting field of
f-T
is geometric with Galois group
S_{n}
(see above),
and

\Delta_{X}(f-T)
is irreducible
if and only if
f\in\mathcal{P}
(Lemma
4.1
),
so the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree
n
where
q=p^{\nu}
,
p\nmid n
and
n-1
is prime.
If
f^{\prime}
is irreducible, then
f
is Morse.

Proof.

Suppose that
f^{\prime}
is irreducible
and that there exist
a\neq b
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
f^{\prime}(a)=f^{\prime}(b)=0
and
f(a)=f(b)
.
As
f^{\prime}
is irreducible of degree
n-1
, we have
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n-1}}

and there exists
1\neq\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n-1}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
b=a^{\sigma}
.
Thus
c:=f(a)=f(b)=f(a)^{\sigma}
is in the fixed field of
\sigma
, which is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
due to the assumption that

n-1
is prime.
So
f-c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
has root
a
, hence
f-c=f^{\prime}g

for some
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree
1
.
Deriving gives

f^{\prime\prime}g=f^{\prime}(1-g^{\prime})
,
so since
f^{\prime}
is irreducible and
{\rm deg}(f^{\prime})>{\rm deg}(g)
,
we conclude that
f^{\prime}|f^{\prime\prime}
, which contradicts the separability of
f^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p\geq 3
and
q=p^{\nu}
. Suppose that
n-1
is prime and
n\in\{2,\dots,p-1\}\cup\{p+1\}
.
Then there exists
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree
n
such that
the splitting field of
f(X)-T
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
is geometric with Galois group
S_{n}
,
and
\Delta_{X}(f(X)-T)
is irreducible.

Proof.

There exists
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree
n
with
f^{\prime}
irreducible:
If
n<p
we can choose
f^{\prime}
to be any irreducible polynomial of degree
n-1
;
if
n=p+1
we can choose

to be the minimal polynomial of any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}

with
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=0
, of which there exist
q^{p-1}-q
many.
By Lemma
4.3
,
f
is Morse,
and therefore
the splitting field of
f-T
is geometric with Galois group
S_{n}
,
and
\Delta_{X}(f-T)
is irreducible by Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.6

For example if

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
w\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a tamely ramified rational function defined over a field
k
.
Assume that
w
has a multiple pole at
\infty
and all other poles over
\overline{k}
are simple. Assume further that the numerator
g\in k[X]
of the derivative
w^{\prime}
is irreducible or the square of an irreducible polynomial.
Then
w
is indecomposable over
k
.

Proof.

It will be convenient to introduce two new variable symbols
T,U
and consider three separate copies of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
which we will denote
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X},\mathbb{P}^{1}_{T},\mathbb{P}^{1}_{U}
.
For the sake of contradiction,
assume that
w=v\circ u
is the composition of two rational functions

u,v\in k(X)
with
\deg u,\deg v>1
.
Since
w(\infty)=\infty
, assume without loss of generality that
u(\infty)=\infty
and
v(\infty)=\infty
.
Consider the corresponding coverings

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}\xrightarrow{u}\mathbb{P}^{1}_{T}\xrightarrow{v}\mathbb{P}^{1}_{U}.

For a divisor
D=\sum_{P}n_{P}P
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}
we denote

D^{0}=\sum_{w(P)\neq\infty}n_{P}P,\quad D^{\infty}=\sum_{w(P)=\infty}n_{P}P

and similarly for a divisor
D
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{T}
we denote

D^{0}=\sum_{v(P)\neq\infty}n_{P}P,\quad D^{\infty}=\sum_{v(P)=\infty}n_{P}P.

Consider the differents
\mathfrak{d}_{w}
,
\mathfrak{d}_{u}
,
\mathfrak{d}_{v}
of
w
,
u
and
v

as divisors on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}
,
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{T}
, respectively.
The Riemann-Hurwitz formula implies that

\deg\mathfrak{d}_{u}=2\deg u-2
and

\deg\mathfrak{d}_{v}=2\deg v-2
.
Since all finite poles of
w
are simple,
the same holds for the finite poles of
u
and
v
.
Moreover, since
w
is tamely ramified, so are
u
and
v
,
which therefore each ramify over at least two geometric primes.
Thus,
\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{0}>0
and
\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0}>0
.
By the basic properties of differents we have

\mathfrak{d}_{w}=u^{*}\mathfrak{d}_{v}+\mathfrak{d}_{u}
.
Therefore, as all divisors in this relation are effective,

(4.1)

\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{0}=u^{*}\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0}+\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{0}.

Note that
\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{0}
is precisely the zero divisor of the numerator of
w^{\prime}
,
which by assumption is of the form

\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{0}=Q
or
\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{0}=2Q
for some prime divisor
Q
.
The first case is already excluded by (
4.1
) (since
u^{*}\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0},\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{0}>0
, their sum cannot be prime),
so assume that
\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{0}=2Q
.
Then (
4.1
) gives that

u^{*}\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0}=\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{0}=Q
.
We have

(\deg u)(\deg\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0})=\deg u^{*}\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0}=\deg Q=\deg\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{0}\leq\deg\mathfrak{d}_{u}=2\deg u-2,

which implies
(\deg u)(\deg\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0}-2)\leq-2
,
hence
\deg u=2
and
\deg\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0}=1
.
Since
\deg\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0}+\deg\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{\infty}=2\deg v-2
, the latter shows that
\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{\infty}>0
.
Since
\deg\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{0}+\deg\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{\infty}=2\deg u-2=2

and
\deg\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{0}=\deg Q=(\deg u)(\deg\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{0})\geq 2
it also follows that
\mathfrak{d}_{u}^{\infty}=0
.
Thus
v
ramifies over
\infty
but
u
does not,
which implies that
w=v\circ u
has
\deg u=2
many
multiple poles over
\overline{k}
, contradicting our assumption.
∎

For a squarefree polynomial

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X],\deg c=m
be a squarefree polynomial and

(g,c)=1

Assume further that

Then there exists

Proof.

Without loss of generality,

is monic.
Write

. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be the

(they are pairwise distinct since

is a
p

It follows using (
4.2

Since both sides of this congruence are of degree less than

we get the following

\frac{v}{c^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{a_{i}}{(X-\alpha_{i})^{2}}.

We therefore can write

-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{a_{i}}{X-\alpha_{i}}=\frac{\psi}{c}\quad\mbox{ with }\quad\psi\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X],\deg\psi<m,

and

\deg u=n-m-1<p-1

4.3.

We will now explain how to eliminate
the tame ramification at infinity of the extensions obtained in the previous subsections,
conditional on a weak consequence of the function field analogue of Schinzel’s hypothesis H

The classical Hypothesis H states the following:

Conjecture 4.12

Let

5.2.
Application to the minimal ramification problem

We note that
L_{1}^{\mathrm{gt}}(q)
-realizability implies
a positive answer to the minimal ramification problem:

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
G
be an
L_{1}^{\mathrm{gt}}(q)
-realizable nontrivial finite group.
Then for infinitely many primes
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
there exists a geometric Galois extension
L/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
with
{\rm Gal}(L/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T))=G
which is ramified only over
h
.
In particular,
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G)=1
and
G
satisfies Conjecture
1.4
.

Proof.

Let
M/\mathbb{F}_{q}(U)
be a geometric realization of
G
ramified over
U=0
and (at most) tamely ramified over
U=\infty
.
Denote by
e
the ramification index over
\infty
,
which by assumption is not divisible by
p
.
Let
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
irreducible of degree divisible by
e

and identify
U=h(T)
.
By Lemma
4.16
,
M
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
are linearly disjoint over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(U)
,
and
M\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
is ramified only over
h
by Lemma
4.17
.
Therefore,
M\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
is geometric Galois of Galois group
G
and ramified only over the single prime

,
hence
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)}(G)=1
.
∎

In this subsection we
extend this to (certain) products of
L_{1}^{\mathrm{gt}}(q)
-realizable groups,
which we then combine with Theorem

to prove Theorem

:

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
G=G_{1}\times\ldots\times G_{m}
be a product with each

being
L_{1}^{\mathrm{gt}}(q)
-realizable.
Assume that there is a prime number

is quasi-

or

holds for

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
.

By a
k
-
variety
(for a field

k
-scheme of finite type.
In what follows all maps are morphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-varieties,
and
\mathbb{A}^{n}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}^{n}

n

\mathbb{F}_{q}
considered as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-variety.
The function field of an irreducible

-variety

We will also use the notion of a Galois cover of

In particular, if

We call

being geometrically irreducible).

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{m}
be an open
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subvariety and
Y\to U
a geometric Galois cover of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-varieties with
{\rm Gal}(Y/U)=G
.
Then
there exists a morphism

\psi\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}\to\mathbb{A}^{m}

such that
\psi^{-1}(U)\neq\emptyset
and
Y\times_{U}\psi^{-1}(U)\to\psi^{-1}(U)
is a geometric Galois cover with Galois group
G
.

Proof.

Denote
\nu=|G|
and
Y^{\prime}=Y\times_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{F}_{q^{\nu}},U^{\prime}=U\times_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{F}_{q^{\nu}}
.
Since
Y\rightarrow U
is geometric,
Y^{\prime}
is irreducible and the cover
Y^{\prime}=Y\times_{U}U^{\prime}\to U
is Galois with
{\rm Gal}(Y^{\prime}/U)=G\times{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\nu}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
. (Generally, if
Z\rightarrow X
and
W\rightarrow X
are Galois covers then
Z\times_{X}W
is a union of isomorphic Galois covers with Galois group a subgroup of
{\rm Gal}(Z/X)\times{\rm Gal}(W/X)
,
and if

is irreducible we have
{\rm Gal}(Z\times_{X}W/X)\cong{\rm Gal}(Z/X)\times{\rm Gal}(W/X)
.
This fact can be deduced from
[
Mil80
, Theorem 5.3]
or by comparing with the Galois groups of the corresponding function field extensions.)
We will show that
one can choose
h_{1},\dots,h_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
such that the corresponding morphism
\psi\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{m}

satisfies
\psi^{-1}(U)\neq\emptyset
and

Y^{\prime}\times_{U}\psi^{-1}(U)\to\psi^{-1}(U)
is a Galois cover with Galois group

{\rm Gal}(Y^{\prime}/U)=G\times{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\nu}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
which will imply that

Y\times_{U}\psi^{-1}(U)\to\psi^{-1}(U)
is a
geometric
Galois cover with Galois group
G
,
since any extension of the field of constants occurring in this cover must be of degree dividing
\nu=|G|
.

First note that

Y^{\prime}\times_{U}\psi^{-1}(U)\to\psi^{-1}(U)
is again étale
for any
\psi
,
so it suffices to show that

h_{1},\dots,h_{m}
can be chosen so that
it is a Galois cover with Galois group
{\rm Gal}(Y^{\prime}/U)
,
for which we are allowed to replace
U
and
Y^{\prime}
by dense open subvarieties.
So since an étale morphism is locally standard étale (see e.g.
[
Mil80
, Proposition I.3.19]
),
we can assume that
U=\{g(T_{1},\ldots,T_{m})\neq 0\}
with
0\neq g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}]
and

Y^{\prime}=\{f(T_{1},\ldots,T_{m},X)=0,g(T_{1},\ldots,T_{m})\neq 0\}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{m+1}

with

, and
Y^{\prime}\to U
is the projection to the first
m
coordinates.
By
[
FJ08
, Theorem 13.3.5 and Proposition 16.1.5]
there exist

h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
with

,
f(h_{1},\dots,h_{m},X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)[X]
is irreducible and

{\rm Gal}(f(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m},X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T))={\rm Gal}(f(T_{1},\ldots,T_{m},X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}))={\rm Gal}(Y^{\prime}/U).

The corresponding
\psi
satisfies

\psi^{-1}(U)\neq\emptyset
,
Y^{\prime}\times_{U}\psi^{-1}(U)
is irreducible, and

{\rm Gal}(Y^{\prime}\times_{U}\psi^{-1}(U)/\psi^{-1}(U))={\rm Gal}(f(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m},X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(T))={\rm Gal}(Y^{\prime}/U),

concluding the proof.
∎

In all cases we will write an explicit polynomial

6.2.
Application to the minimal ramification problem over

Finally, we apply our results for

over

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
h_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
S
a finite set of primes numbers,
and for each
p\in S
let
f_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
be monic of degree
n
.
There exist
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
monic of degree
n
and
c\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
f\equiv f_{p}\pmod{p}
and
c\equiv 1\pmod{p}

for every
p\in S
,
and
f-c^{n}h_{0}
has
n
roots in
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem there exists a monic
f_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]

with
f_{0}\equiv f_{p}\pmod{p}
for every finite
p\in S
.
Let
\pi=\prod_{p\in S}p
,
write
f_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}X^{i}
and
let
f_{\infty}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
be any monic polynomial of degree
n

with
n
roots in
\mathbb{R}
.
Since fractions of the form
\frac{x\pi}{y\pi+1}
with
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
are dense in
\mathbb{R}
,
we can choose

x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\tilde{f}:=X^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(a_{i}+\frac{x_{i}\pi}{y_{i}\pi+1})X^{i}

is arbitrarily close to
f_{\infty}+h_{0}
,
in particular so close that also
\tilde{f}-h_{0}
has
n
roots in
\mathbb{R}
.
Then with
c=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(y_{i}\pi+1)
,
the polynomial

f(X):=c^{n}\tilde{f}(c^{-1}X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
satisfies the claim.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Schinzel’s hypothesis H (Conjecture

) implies that

In case

.
Let

S_{0}
denote the set of prime numbers

By Proposition
4.2
,
if
p
is sufficiently large with respect to

,

of degree

such that

is geometric with Galois group

, and

) and Lemma

).
By Proposition
4.15
(2,c),
if
p
is sufficiently large with respect to

,

with

Fix such a sufficiently large

and

as above,

Then
\Delta_{X}(g_{p})=\mathcal{F}(h_{p}(T))\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[T]

implies that

over

.

Choose

monic of degree

with

and

By Lemma

and

.

and in

and

.

:
Reduction modulo

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(T)

\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(T)

.

In geometric terms, the extension

corresponds
to a branched covering of geometrically irreducible smooth curves

with

.
We wish to apply
[
BS20
, Proposition 1.11]
to
\phi
,
for which we use the notation and definitions from there.
As
\phi
has only the one ramified prime divisor
\mathcal{F}
, its branch locus is the zero locus of an irreducible homogeneous polynomial

of degree
d
.
As in
[
BS20
, p. 923]
,
for
\xi\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})

we denote by

the specialized algebra at
\xi
,
which for

,
where

denotes the integral closure of
\mathbb{Q}[T]_{(T-a)}
in
\mathbb{Q}(C)
.
As
\xi
is unramified in
\mathbb{Q}(C)
,
A^{\phi}_{\xi}
is an étale
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, i.e.

[

\mathbb{Q}

g(X,0)=f-c^{n}h(0)

of

Thus in the language of

[

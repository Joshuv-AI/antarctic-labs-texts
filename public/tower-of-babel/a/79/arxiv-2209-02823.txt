Linear potentials and applications in conformal geometry
Shiguang Ma

Abstract
. In this paper we derive estimates for linear potentials that hold away from thin subsets. And, inspired by the celebrated work of Huber (cf. [ 16 , 22 ] ), we verify that, for a subset that is thin at a point, there is always a geodesic that reaches to the point and avoids the thin subset in general dimensions. As applications of these estimates on linear potentials, we consider the scalar curvature equations and slightly improve the results of Schoen-Yau [ 34 , 35 ] and Carron [ 11 ] on the Hausdorff dimensions of singular sets which represent the ends of complete conformal metrics on domains in manifolds of dimensions greater than 3. We also study Q Q -curvature equations in dimensions greater than 4 and obtain stronger results on the Hausdorff dimensions of the singular sets (cf [ 13 ] ). More interestingly, our approach based on potential theory yields a significantly stronger finiteness theorem on the singular sets for Q Q -curvature equations in dimension 4 (cf. [ 15 , 12 ] ), which is a remarkable analogue of Huber’s theorem [ 16 , 22 ] .

2.1.
Linear potential and the outer capacity in Euclidean spaces

For the purpose to relate potentials on Euclidean spaces to that on manifolds, we want to introduce
potentials that are possibly confined to an open subset
\Omega\subseteq R^{n}

in the Euclidean space. We will use the definition of a Radon measure on locally compact Hausdorff spaces
in
[
31
, page 455]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq R^{n}
be a bounded open subset in the Euclidean space
R^{n}
. Then, for
x\in\Omega
, let

(2.1)

R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu(y)\quad&\text{ when
$\alpha\in(1,n)$}\\
\int_{\Omega}\log\frac{D}{|x-y|}d\mu(y)\quad&\text{ when $\alpha=n$}\end{aligned}\right.

for a Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
, where
D
is the diameter of
\Omega
.

For basic properties of the potential
R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)
,
readers are referred to
[
28
, Chapter 2]
. Most facts, results, and arguments in
[
28
, Chapter 2]
that are
relevant for the discussions in this paper hold with little or slight changes.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E
be a subset in
\Omega
and
\Omega
be a bounded
open subset in
R^{n}
. For
\alpha\in(1,n]
, we define a
capacity by

(2.2)

C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega)=\inf\{\mu(\Omega):\mu\geq 0\text{ on $\Omega$ and }R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)\geq 1\text{ for all $x\in E$}\}.

Because of the choice of the kernel functions in Definition
2.1
, the capacity
C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega)

in Definition
2.2
is not intended to be the same as relative capacity where the kernel function
is the Green function for a so-called Greenian domain
\Omega
. Similar to

[
28
, Theorem 4.1 in Chapter 2]
and
[
28
, Section 2.6]
, we have

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C^{\alpha}
be the capacity defined as in
Definition
2.2
for
\alpha\in(1,n]
.

(1)

C^{\alpha}
is nondecreasing, that is,

C^{\alpha}(E_{1},\Omega)\leq C^{\alpha}(E_{2},\Omega)

when
E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\Omega\subseteq R^{n}
.

(2)

C^{\alpha}
is countably subadditive, that is,

C^{\alpha}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i},\Omega)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}C^{\alpha}(E_{i},\Omega)

for subsets
E_{i}\subseteq\Omega
.

(3)

C^{\alpha}
is an outer capacity, that is,

C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega)=\inf\{C^{\alpha}(U,\Omega):E\subseteq U\text{ and $U\subseteq\Omega$ open}\}.

The immediate and important property of the outer capacity
C^{\alpha}
in Definition
2.2

is the scaling property (cf.
[
4
, page 135]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

For a positive number
\lambda
, let

A_{\lambda}=\{\lambda x:x\in A\}

for any subset
A
in
R^{n}
. Then, for
\alpha\in(1,n]
,

C^{\alpha}(E_{\lambda},\Omega_{\lambda})=\lambda^{n-\alpha}C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega).

Proof.

For a nonnegative Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
, we associate it with a nonnegative
Radon measure

\mu^{*}(A_{\lambda})=\mu(A)

on
\Omega_{\lambda}
. Then

R^{\alpha,\Omega_{\lambda}}_{\mu^{*}}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{\alpha-n}R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)

for
x\in\Omega
. Therefore

\displaystyle C^{\alpha}

\displaystyle(E_{\lambda},\Omega_{\lambda})=\inf\{\mu^{*}(\Omega_{\lambda}):R^{\alpha,\Omega_{\lambda}}_{\mu^{*}}(\lambda x)\geq 1\text{ for all $x\in E$}\}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n-\alpha}\inf\{\lambda^{\alpha-n}\mu(E):R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\lambda^{\alpha-n}\mu}(x)\geq 1\text{ for all $x\in E$}\}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n-\alpha}C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega).

∎

Next important property of the outer capacity
C^{\alpha}
in Definition
2.2

is the contractive property (cf.
[
28
,
2
,
4
]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that

\Phi:\Omega\to\Omega

is a contractive map, that is,

|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|\leq|x-y|

for all
x,y\in\Omega
. Then, for
\alpha\in(1,n]
,

C^{\alpha}(\Phi(E),\Omega)\leq C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega)

for any subset
E\subseteq\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative Radon measure on
\Omega
such that
R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)\geq 1
for all

x\in E
. Then let
\mu^{*}
be a nonnegative Radon measure on
\Omega
such that
\mu^{*}(A)=\mu(\Phi^{-1}(A))
for any
A\subseteq\Omega
and therefore

\int_{\Omega}f(\tilde{y})d\mu^{*}(\tilde{y})=\int_{\Omega}f\circ\Phi(y)d\mu(y).

Notice that

\displaystyle R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu^{*}}(\Phi(x))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|\Phi(x)-\tilde{y}|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu^{*}(\tilde{y})=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu(y)=R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)\geq 1.

Thus

\displaystyle C^{\alpha}(\Phi(E),\Omega)

\displaystyle=\inf\{\nu(\Omega):\nu\geq 0\text{ on $\Omega$ and }R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\nu}(x)\geq 1\text{ for all $x\in\Phi(E)$}\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\{\mu^{*}(\Omega):\mu^{*}\text{ induced from $\mu$ and }R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu^{*}}(\Phi(x))\geq 1\text{ for all $x\in E$}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\mu(\Omega):\mu\geq 0\text{ on $\Omega$ and }R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)\geq 1\text{ for all $x\in E$}\}=C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega).

The argument for
\alpha=n
is similar and the proof is complete.
∎

Before we introduce the notion of thinness by
C^{\alpha}
, for completeness, let us calculate the outer
capacity
C^{\alpha}(S^{n-1},B_{2})
, where

B_{2}=\{x\in R^{n}:|x|<2\}\text{ and }S^{n-1}=\{x\in R^{n}:|x|=1\}.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
28
, Example 5.4.3]
) For
\alpha\in(1,n]
,

C^{\alpha}(S^{n-1},B_{2})=c(n,\alpha)

for some positive constant
c(n,\alpha)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
C^{\alpha}(S^{n-1},B_{2})
is finite and positive. Let
\sigma
be the volume measure for the unit sphere so that the total measure of
S^{n-1}
is
1
. First we realize
that the potential, for
\alpha\in(1,n]
and
x\in S^{n-1}
,

R^{\alpha,B_{2}}_{\sigma}(x)\geq m

for some
m=m(n,\alpha)>0
.
Therefore
C^{\alpha}(S^{n-1},B_{2})\leq\frac{1}{m}<\infty
by Definition
2.2
.
To see that
C^{\alpha}(S^{n-1},B_{2})>0
, for
any
\mu
on
B_{2}
, we use Lemma
2.5
below to pick up a point
p\in S^{n-1}
such that
(
2.3
) holds and calculate, for
\alpha\in(1,n)
,

\displaystyle R^{\alpha,B_{2}}_{\mu}(p)

\displaystyle=(n-\alpha)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{3}>s\}\bigcap B_{2})\frac{1}{(s+\frac{1}{3})^{n-\alpha+1}}ds+\frac{1}{3^{n-\alpha}}\mu(B_{2})

\displaystyle=(n-\alpha)\int_{0}^{3}\mu(B_{r}(p)\bigcap B_{2})r^{\alpha-n-1}dr+\frac{1}{3^{n-\alpha}}\mu(B_{2})

\displaystyle\leq M(n,\alpha)\mu(B_{2})

for some
M(n,\alpha)>0
and
r=|x-p|
. For
\alpha=n
,

\displaystyle R^{\alpha,B_{2}}_{\mu}(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{\frac{3}{r}-1>s\}\bigcap B_{2})\frac{1}{1+s}ds+\log\frac{4}{3}\mu(B_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{3}\mu(B_{r}(p)\bigcap B_{2})\frac{1}{r}dr+\log\frac{4}{3}\,\mu(B_{2})

\displaystyle\leq M(n,n)\mu(B_{2})

for some
M(n,n)>0
. In the above we used
[
32
, Theorem 8.16]
.
This implies
C^{\alpha}(S^{n-1},B_{2})\geq\frac{1}{M(n,\alpha)}>0
by
Definition
2.2
. Thus the proof is complete.
∎

By the Vitali covering lemma, we prove the following fact used in the above.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\mu
be a finite nonnegative Radon measure on

B_{2}\subset R^{n}
. Then there is a point
p\in S^{n-1}
such that

(2.3)

\mu(B_{r}(p)\bigcap B_{2})\leq c(n)\mu(B_{2})r^{n-1}\text{ for all $r>0$}.

for some dimensional constant
c=c(n)
.

Proof.

For convenience, let
\mu(B_{2})=1
. Assume otherwise, for any
q\in S^{n-1}
, there is

r_{q}>0
such that

\mu(B_{r_{q}}(q)\bigcap B_{2})\geq c(n)r_{q}^{n-1}.

Using Vitali covering lemma, we have
\{q_{1},q_{2},\cdots,q_{k}\}\subset S^{n-1}
such that the collection of balls

\{B_{r_{q_{1}}}(q_{1}),B_{r_{q_{2}}}(q_{2}),\cdots B_{r_{q_{k}}}(q_{k})\}

are disjoint but the collection of balls

\{B_{3r_{q_{1}}}(q_{1}),B_{3r_{q_{2}}}(q_{2}),\cdots B_{3r_{q_{k}}}(q_{k})\}

cover the sphere
S^{n-1}
. Therefore, on one hand,

c(n)\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{q_{i}}^{n-1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu(B_{r_{q_{i}}}(q_{i})\bigcap B_{2})\leq\mu(B_{2})=1.

On the other hand,

|S^{n-1}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|B_{3r_{q_{i}}}(q_{i})\bigcap S^{n-1}|<|S^{n-1}|c(n)\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{q_{i}}^{n-1}

when
c(n)
is sufficiently large, where
|\cdot|
stands for the Lebesgue measure on
S^{n-1}
.
Therefore the lemma is proven by contradiction.
∎

Now let us introduce the geometric definition of thinness. For notions of thinness in terms of the fine topology
and Wiener criterion, readers are referred, for instance, to
[
28
,
2
,
4
]
. Let

\omega^{\delta}_{i}(p)=\{x\in R^{n}:|x-p|\in[2^{-i}\delta,2^{-i+1}\delta]\}

and

\Omega^{\delta}_{i}(p)=\{x\in R^{n}:|x-p|\in(2^{-i-1}\delta,2^{-i+2}\delta)\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E
be a subset in the Euclidean space
R^{n}
and
p\in R^{n}
be a point
in
R^{n}
. The subset
E
is said to be
\alpha
-thin at the point
p
for
\alpha\in(1,n)
if

\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{C^{\alpha}(E\bigcap\omega^{\delta}_{i}(p),\Omega^{\delta}_{i}(p))}{C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{2^{-i}\delta}(p),B_{2^{-i+1}\delta}(p))}<\infty

for some small
\delta>0
. The subset
E
is said to be
n
-thin at
p
if

\sum_{i\geq 1}iC^{n}(E\bigcap\omega^{\delta}_{i}(p),\Omega^{\delta}_{i}(p))<\infty

for some small
\delta>0
.

Combining Lemma
2.1
-
2.4
with the above definition, we observe the following important property of
\alpha
-thin sets, inspired by
[
5
]
(see also
[
24
,
25
]
). We recall
Theorem
1.2
from the introduction for readers’ convenience.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
E
be a subset in the Euclidean space
R^{n}
and
p\in R^{n}
be a point. Suppose that
E
is
\alpha
-thin at the
point
p
for
\alpha\in(1,n]
. Then there is a ray from
p
that avoids
E
at least within some small ball at
p
.

Proof.

First of all, due to the translation invariance, we may simply assume
p
is the origin of the Euclidean space.
Then, by the scaling property of the outer capacity
C^{\alpha}
in Lemma
2.2
, one
notices that

\frac{C^{\alpha}(E\bigcap\omega^{\delta}_{i},\Omega^{\delta}_{i})}{C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{2^{-i}\delta},B_{2^{-i+1}\delta})}=\frac{C^{\alpha}(S_{i}(E)\bigcap\omega^{1}_{0},\Omega^{1}_{0})}{C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{1},B_{2})}

where the scaling map:
S_{i}(v)=\frac{2^{i}}{\delta}v
. Then we consider the projection

P(v)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{v}{|v|}\quad&\text{ when $v\in R^{n}$ and $|v|\geq 1$}\\
v\quad&\text{ when $v\in R^{n}$ and $|v|<1$}\end{aligned}\right.,

which is contractive. Therefore, in the light of Lemma
2.3
, we have

C^{\alpha}(P(S_{i}(E)\cap\omega^{1}_{0}),\Omega^{1}_{0})\leq C^{\alpha}(S_{i}(E)\cap\omega^{1}_{0},\Omega^{1}_{0}).

Next, using the countable sub-additivity in Lemma
2.1
, we have

C^{\alpha}(\bigcup_{i\geq k}P(S_{i}(E)\cap\omega^{1}_{0}),\Omega^{1}_{0})\leq\sum_{i\geq k}C^{\alpha}(P(S_{i}(E)\cap\omega^{1}_{0}),\Omega^{1}_{0}).

Thus,

\displaystyle C^{\alpha}

\displaystyle(\bigcup_{i\geq k}P(S_{i}(E)\cap\omega^{1}_{0}),\Omega^{1}_{0})\leq\sum_{i\geq k}C^{\alpha}(S_{i}(E)\cap\omega^{1}_{0},\Omega^{1}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{1},B_{2})\sum_{i\geq k}\frac{C^{\alpha}(S_{i}(E)\bigcap\omega^{1}_{0},\Omega^{1}_{0})}{C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{1},B_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{1},B_{2})\sum_{i\geq k}\frac{C^{\alpha}(E\bigcap\omega^{\delta}_{i},\Omega^{\delta}_{i})}{C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{2^{-i}\delta},B_{2^{-i+1}\delta})}

which is arbitrarily small when
k
is appropriately large using Lemma
2.4

for
C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{1},B_{2})
. And then this implies that

\partial B_{1}\setminus\bigcup_{i\geq k}P(S_{i}(E)\cap\omega^{1}_{0})\neq\emptyset.

The argument for
\alpha=n
is similar and easier. And the proof is complete.
∎

2.2.
Linear potential on manifolds

On a given complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, let
d(\cdot,\cdot)

be the distance function associated with the given Riemannian metric
g
.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold and
U\subseteq M^{n}
is a bounded
open subset. For
\alpha\in(1,n]
, the linear potential on the Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
of order
\alpha

Radon measure
\mu

\mathscr{R}^{\alpha,U}_{\mu}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \int_{U}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{n-\alpha}}d\mu(y)\quad&\text{ when
$\alpha\in(1,n)$}\\
\int_{\Omega}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)\quad&\text{ when $\alpha=n$}\end{aligned}\right.,

where
D
is the diameter of
U
.

From the discussion in the previous subsection, it is easily seen that one may generate an outer capacity

\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(E,U)
for any subset
E\subseteq U\subseteq M^{n}
that behaves like the counter part
in Euclidean spaces. To use
R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)
and
C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega)
on Euclidean spaces
in the previous subsection to study
\mathscr{R}^{\alpha,U}_{\mu}(p)
and
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(A,U)
on manifolds,
we first introduce the correspondence between Radon measures on the tangent space
T_{p}M^{n}
at each point

p\in M^{n}
and those on
(M^{n},g)
. Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold.
Let
p\in M^{n}
and
U
be a convex normal coordinate neighborhood at
p
on
(M^{n},g)
, where
the exponential map serves as the convex normal coordinate

\text{exp}|_{p}:\Omega\to U.

The domain
U
is said to be convex if the unique geodesic joining any two points in
U
stays in
U
. Moreover, we
may assume in the coordinate chart
U
the exponential map be uniformly bi-Lipschitz throughout this paper.

Then, for a Radon measure
\mu
on
U\subseteq M^{n}
, one may introduce the Radon measure

\mu^{*}
on
\Omega\subset T_{p}M^{n}
such that, for a subset
E\subseteq\Omega
,

\mu^{*}(E)=\mu(\text{exp}|_{p}E)\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}f\circ\text{exp}|_{p}d\mu^{*}=\int_{U}fd\mu.

It is then easily seen that the following equivalence between the linear potential
R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu^{*}}
,
the outer capacities
C^{\alpha}(\cdot,\Omega)
and the corresponding
\mathscr{R}^{\alpha,U}_{\mu}
,

\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\cdot,U)
holds. Namely,

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and
p\in M^{n}
. Let

\text{exp}|_{p}:\Omega\to U

be the convex normal coordinate chart, where the exponential map is uniformly bi-Lipschitz. And let

\alpha\in(1,n]
. Then, for

A\subset U
and
E=(\text{exp}|_{p})^{-1}A\subset\Omega
,

\displaystyle C^{-1}R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu^{*}}\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}^{\alpha,U}_{\mu}\leq CR^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu^{*}}

\displaystyle C^{-1}C^{\alpha}(E,\Omega)\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(A,U)\leq CC^{\alpha}(E,\Omega)

for some constant
C=C(M^{n},g,U,p)
. Consequently, a subset
A\subset U
is
\alpha
-thin at
p
if and only if

E=(\text{exp}|_{p})^{-1}(A)\subset\Omega
is
\alpha
-thin at the origin of
T_{p}M^{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward based on the properties of the convex normal coordinate chart at a point in a
complete Riemannain manifold, where the exponential map is bi-Lipschtiz.
∎

2.3.
Estimates of Riesz potential

In this subsection, we introduce our estimates of Riesz potentials on manifolds.
We will recall some well known estimates for Riesz potential in Euclidean spaces
[
28
, Chapter 2]
.

Our estimates on Riesz potentials are designed to help understand the Hausdorff dimensions of singularities of solutions of partial
differential equations on manifolds. Let us start with a general decomposition theorem for nonnegative Radon measures on a complete Riemannian manifold based on
[
23
, Proposition 1.4]
, which is related to Lemma
2.5
and
a broad generalization of the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem in some way.

Lemma 2.7
.

(
[
23
, Proposition 1.4]
)
Let
\mu
be a nonnegative Radon measure on a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
and let

G_{d}^{\infty}=\{x\in M^{n}:\limsup_{r\to 0}r^{-d}\mu(B_{r}(x))=+\infty\}

for any
d\in[0,n]
.
Then

\mathscr{H}_{d}(G^{\infty}_{d})=0

where
\mathscr{H}_{d}
is the Hausdorff measure of dimension
d
.

Proof.

Based on the general decomposition theorem
[
23
, Proposition 1.4]
on the Euclidean space and the
correspondence of Radon measures in Lemma
2.6
, this lemma is easily seen. Specifically, we first
prove the statement for Radon measures supported in a convex normal coordinate chart used in
Lemma
2.6
. Then the lemma follows by using a countable covering for
(M,g)
by convex normal
coordinate charts.
∎

Now we are ready to state and prove one crucial analytic result in this paper on the behavior of the Riesz potentials.
For readers’ convenience, we recall Theorem
1.1
from the introduction.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold and
\mu
is a finite Radon measure on a bounded

be a compact subset in

for some

Proof.

First, due to the assumption that the Hausdorff dimension of

That is to say

\mu(B_{r}(p))\leq Cr^{d+\epsilon}

when

is appropriately small. Secondly, we may confine ourselves to a convex normal coordinate neighborhood

of

and
we may work on the Euclidean space without the loss of generality in the light of the discussion in the previous subsection, particularly, Lemma
2.6

\text{exp}|_{p}:\Omega\to U
and
\text{exp}|_{p}(0)=p
. For convenience, we will not
differentiate

and

if no confusion rises.
Therefore, for
x\in\omega_{i}^{\delta}\subset\Omega
when

is sufficiently small and

is appropriately large,

(2.5)

\displaystyle R^{\alpha,\Omega}_{\mu}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu+\int_{B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu+\int_{\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu,

where
i_{0}\leq i
to be fixed. For the first term in the right hand side of (

I=\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu\leq(\frac{1}{(2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta-2^{-i+1}\delta})^{n-\alpha}\mu(\Omega)\leq(\frac{1}{2^{-i_{0}+1}\delta})^{n-\alpha}\mu(\Omega).

Recall that
2^{-i}\delta\leq|x|\leq 2^{-i+1}\delta

(2.6)

I\leq\mu(\Omega)\frac{(2^{-i+1}\delta)^{n-\alpha-d}}{(2^{-i_{0}+1}\delta)^{n-\alpha}}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-\alpha-d}}\leq C\frac{1}{|x|^{n-\alpha-d}}

where
C=C(n,\alpha,d,\delta,i_{0})
. For the second term in the right hand side of (
2.5
),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}\setminus\Omega_{i}^{\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu=\int_{B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-i+2}\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu+\int_{B_{2^{-i-1}\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-i+2}\delta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu+(\frac{1}{2^{-i-1}\delta})^{n-\alpha}\mu(B_{2^{-i-1}\delta})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{i-1}(\frac{1}{2^{-k}\delta})^{n-\alpha}\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta})+(\frac{1}{2^{-i-1}\delta})^{n-\alpha}\mu(B_{2^{-i-1}\delta}).

Using (
2.4
) for
\epsilon=0

(2.7)

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq C(4^{d}\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{i-1}(\frac{1}{2^{-k}\delta})^{n-\alpha-d}+(\frac{1}{2^{-i-1}\delta})^{n-\alpha-d})

\displaystyle\leq C(\frac{4^{d}}{1-2^{-(n-\alpha-d)}}(\frac{1}{2^{-i+1}\delta})^{n-\alpha-d}+(\frac{1}{2^{-i-1}\delta})^{n-\alpha-d})

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{|x|^{n-\alpha-d}},

where
C=C(n,\alpha,d,\delta,i_{0})
. To handle the third term in the right hand side of (

E_{i}^{\lambda}=\{x\in\omega^{\delta}_{i}:\int_{\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu\geq\lambda 2^{i(n-\alpha-d)}\},

where

to be fixed. By Definition

, we know

C^{\alpha}(E^{\lambda}_{i},\Omega^{\delta}_{i})\leq\frac{\mu(\Omega^{\delta}_{i})}{\lambda 2^{i(n-\alpha-d)}}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\frac{(2^{-i+2}\delta)^{d+\epsilon}}{2^{i(n-\alpha-d)}}=\frac{C4^{d+\epsilon}}{\lambda}2^{-i\epsilon}(2^{-i})^{n-\alpha},

where (

\Omega^{\delta}_{i}\subset B_{2^{-i+2}\delta}
.
Now, from Lemma

C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{2^{-i}\delta},B_{2^{-i+1}\delta})=C(n,\alpha)(2^{-i}\delta)^{n-\alpha}

and

\sum_{i\geq i_{0}}\frac{C^{\alpha}(E^{\lambda}_{i},\Omega^{\delta}_{i})}{C^{\alpha}(\partial B_{2^{-i}\delta},B_{2^{-i+1}\delta})}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\sum_{i\geq i_{0}}2^{-\epsilon i}<\infty.

, the proof is completed.

Estimates of Log potential

First, as stated in

\lim_{x\to p\text{ and }x\in\Omega\setminus E}\frac{U_{n}\mu(x)}{\log\frac{1}{|x-p|}}=\mu(\{p\}).

The following is our version of
[
28
, Theorem 6.3]
on manifolds. For us it is a generalization of
[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
in higher dimensions and linear version of such behaviors for
n
-superharmonic functions (cf.
[
22
,
8
,
9
,
24
,
25

). For convenience, we present a brief but full proof based on the potential theory
developed in previous subsections in this paper.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold.
Let
\mu
be a finite Radon measure on a bounded domain
G\subset M^{n}
.
Then, for
p\in G
, there is a subset
A
that is n-thin at
p
and

\lim_{x\to p\text{ and }x\in M^{n}\setminus A}\frac{\int_{G}\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)}d\mu(x)}{\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)}}=\mu(\{p\}).

Proof.

Let

\text{exp}|_{p}:\Omega\to U

be a convex normal coordinate at
p\in M^{n}
. Clearly, it suffices to show that, there is a subset
A
in
U
, which is

n
-thin at
p
, such that

(2.8)

\lim_{x\to p\text{ and }x\in U\setminus A}\frac{\mathscr{R}^{n,U}_{\mu}(x)}{\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)}}=\mu(\{p\}).

Therefore, for
x\in\omega^{\delta}_{i}(p)
, we write

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}^{n,U}_{\mu}(x)=\int_{U}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{U\setminus B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu+\int_{B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu+\int_{\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu.

Here we omit the center
p
for each ball or annulus for simplicity. For the first term in the right hand side of (
2.9
),

(2.10)

I=\int_{U\setminus B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu\leq\mu(U)\log\frac{D}{2^{-i_{0}+1}\delta}=o(1)\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)}\text{ as $x\to p$}.

For the second term in the right hand side of (
2.9
),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}\setminus\Omega_{i}^{\delta}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)=\int_{B_{2^{-i_{0}+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-i+1}\delta}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu+\int_{B_{2^{-i-2}\delta}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C[\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{i}k\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})]+\mu(B_{2^{-i-2}\delta})\log\frac{D}{2^{-i-2}\delta}.

Due to the regularity of Radon measures and
d(x,p)\in[2^{-i-1}\delta,2^{-i}\delta]
, we know

(2.11)

\mu(B_{2^{-i-2}\delta})\log\frac{D}{2^{-i-2}\delta}=\mu(\{p\})\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)}+o(\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)})\text{ as $x\to p$}

and

(2.12)

\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{i}k\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})=o(1)i=o(1)\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)}

as
i\to\infty
or equivalently
x\to p
. To see (
2.12
), for any
\epsilon>0
, we first find
k_{0}
such that

\mu(B_{2^{-l+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-m+1}\delta})\leq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon

for all
m\geq l\geq k_{0}
due to the regularity of
\mu
. Next, we find
N
such that

\frac{\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{k_{0}}k\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})}{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon

for all
i\geq N
. Together, this gives

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{i}k\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})}{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{k_{0}}k\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})}{i}+\frac{\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{i}k\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})}{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k=i_{0}}^{k_{0}}k\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})}{i}+\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{i}\mu(B_{2^{-k+2}\delta}\setminus B_{2^{-k+1}\delta})

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon

for all
i\geq N
.
Thus we conclude that

(2.13)

II=(\mu(\{p\})+o(1))\log\frac{1}{d(x,p)}\text{as $x\to p$}.

To handle the third term in the right side of (
2.9
), for
\lambda_{i}>0
to be determined, we consider

A^{\lambda_{i}}=\{x\in\omega^{\delta}_{i}:\int_{\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\log\frac{D_{i}}{d(x,y)}d\mu\geq i\lambda_{i}\},

where
D_{i}
is the dimeter of
\Omega_{\delta}^{i}
. By Definition
2.2

\mathscr{C}^{n}(A^{\lambda_{i}},\Omega^{\delta}_{i})\leq\frac{\mu(\Omega^{\delta}_{i})}{i\lambda_{i}}.

In the light of Definition
2.3
, we consider

\sum_{i\geq i_{0}}i\mathscr{C}^{n}(A^{\lambda_{i}},\Omega^{\delta}_{i})\leq\sum_{i\geq i_{0}}\frac{\mu(\Omega^{\delta}_{i})}{\lambda_{i}}

and pick up

as

by the classic Paul du Bois-Reymond Theorem (cf.

, (5) Page 40]
and

) for infinite series such that

converges. This is to say that the third term in the right side of (

(2.14)

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\log\frac{D}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)=\int_{\Omega^{\delta}_{i}}\log\frac{D_{i}}{d(x,y)}d\mu(y)+\log\frac{D}{D_{i}}\mu(\Omega^{i}_{\delta})

Finally, if let

, we have

where

is

-thin at

. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
On scalar curvature equations

In this section we focus on the scalar curvature equations for conformal deformation of metrics. Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be
a compact Riemannian manifold for
n\geq 3
.
Let
R_{ijkl}[\bar{g}]
be the Riemann curvature tensor,
R_{ij}[\bar{g}]=R_{ijkl}\bar{g}^{kl}
be
the Ricci curvature tensor, and
R[\bar{g}]=R_{ij}\bar{g}^{ij}
be the scalar curvature.
The scalar curvature equation in conformal geometry is

(3.1)

-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta[\bar{g}]u+R[\bar{g}]u=R[u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}

for a positive function
u
. The scalar curvature equation describes how the scalar curvature transforms under
conformal change of metrics.
In this section we want to use the estimates for the Newton potential in the previous section to study the Hausdorff
dimensions of the singularities of solutions
u
to the scalar equations which represent the ends of a complete
conformal metric
u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
.

We remark here that all of the results in this section hold if we assume
S
is compact,
D\subset M^{n}
is a bounded domain that
contains
S
, and
(M^{n},\bar{g})
is just complete, because the possible noncompact part
M^{n}\setminus\bar{D}
is not relevant
for the purpose here.

3.1.
Preliminaries

Let us start with
[
25
, Lemma 3.1]
, which
is a slight improvement of
[
13
, Proposition 8.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
25
, Lemma 3.1]
)
Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
S
be a closed subset in
M^{n}
. And let

D
be an open neighborhood of
S
.
Suppose that
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
is a conformal metric on
D\setminus S
and is geodesically complete near
S
. Then

u(x)\to+\infty\text{ as $x\to S$}

if

R^{-}[g]\in L^{p}(D\setminus S,g)
for some
p>n/2
, where
R^{-}[g]=\max\{-R[g],0\}

stands for the negative part of the scalar curvature
R[g]
and
L^{p}(D\setminus S,g)

is the
L^{p}
space with respect to the metric
g
.

For a preliminary estimate on the Hausdorff dimension of
S
, we follow the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
and get

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
S
be a
closed subset in
M^{n}
. And let
D
be an open neighborhood of
S
where the scalar curvature
R[\bar{g}]
is nonpositive.
Suppose that
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
is a conformal metric on
D\setminus S
and is geodesically complete near
S
.
Then the Newton capacity of
S
is zero and therefore the Hausdorff dimension

\text{dim}_{\mathscr{H}}(S)\leq n-2,

R^{-}[g]\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(D\setminus S,g)\bigcap L^{p}(D\setminus S,g)

for some
p>n/2

Proof.

Recall the scalar curvature equation

(3.2)

-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta u=-Ru+R^{+}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}-R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\text{ in $D\setminus S$},

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus S}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol

\displaystyle\leq(\int_{D\setminus S}(R^{-}[g])^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}dvol)^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}\text{vol}(D)^{\frac{n-2}{2n}}

\displaystyle\leq(\int_{D\setminus S}(R^{-}[g])^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}dvol[g])^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}\text{vol}(D)^{\frac{n-2}{2n}}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Here, and from now on in the following, all geometric quantities are under the background metric
\bar{g}
unless
indicated otherwise. And, in the light of Lemma
3.1
, we know

u(x)\to+\infty\text{ as $x\to S$}.

As in the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
(adopted from
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
), we use the following test functions. First we let

u_{\alpha,\beta}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \beta&\quad u\geq\alpha+\beta;\\
u-\alpha&\quad u<\alpha+\beta.\end{aligned}\right.\text{ and }\phi_{\alpha,\beta}=u_{\alpha,\beta}-\beta+\beta(1-\eta),

where
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma_{\alpha})
is a fixed cut-off function that is equal to one in a neighborhood of
S

and
\Sigma_{\alpha}=\{x\in D:u(x)>\alpha\}
. Notice that, for
\beta
sufficiently large,

u_{\alpha,\beta}\in(0,\beta]\text{ in $\Sigma_{\alpha}$ and }\phi_{\alpha,\beta}=0\text{ on $\{x\in D:u(x)=\alpha\}\bigcup\{x\in D:u\geq\alpha+\beta$}\}

and

\nabla\phi_{\alpha,\beta}=\nabla u_{\alpha,\beta}+\beta\nabla\eta\text{ and }\nabla u=\nabla u_{\alpha,\beta}\text{ when }\nabla u_{\alpha,\beta}\neq 0.

We then multiply
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}
to the equation (
3.2
) and get

\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol=\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}(-Ru+R[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}})\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol.

Therefore

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}|\nabla u_{\alpha,\beta}|^{2}dvol

\displaystyle=\beta\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}(\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta+(-Ru+R[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}})(1-\eta))dvol

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}(-Ru+R^{+}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}})(\beta-u_{\alpha,\beta})dvol

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}(\beta-u_{\alpha,\beta})dvol

\displaystyle\leq C\beta,

where
C
depends on
\alpha
and
\eta
but does not depend on
\beta
, due the support of
1-\eta
and (
3.3
).
That is

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}|\nabla\frac{u_{\alpha,\beta}}{\beta}|^{2}dvol\leq\frac{C}{\beta}\to 0

as
\beta\to\infty
, where
\frac{u_{\alpha,\beta}}{\beta}
is a function that is identically one in a neighborhood of
S
. This implies
the Newton capacity
\text{Cap}_{2}(S,D)
of
S
is zero.
Consequently, we know
S
is of the Hausdorff dimensions not greater than
n-2

(cf.
[
3
]
and
[
35
, Theorem 2.10 in Chapter VI]
). So the proof is complete.
∎

3.2.

In order to use the estimates of potentials in the previous section, we need the following lemma (cf.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})

. And let

Proof.

Again, recall the scalar curvature equation

(3.5)

-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta u=-Ru+R^{+}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}-R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}=f\text{ in $D\setminus S$},

where

\int_{D}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol<\infty.

And, in the light of Lemma

, we know

u(x)\to\infty\text{ as $x\to S$}.

Then we claim the right hand side

L^{1}(D)
.
To prove this claim, we follow the argument in the proof of
[
25

(stated as
[
25
, Lemma 3.2]
) . Let

\alpha_{s}(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} t&\quad t\leq s;\\
\text{increasing}&\quad t\in[s,10s];\\
2s&\quad t\geq 10s\end{aligned}\right.

(this functions was used in
[
17
]
).
Notice that one may require
\alpha_{s}^{\prime}\in[0,1]
and
\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
. We calculate

-\Delta\alpha_{s}(u)=-\alpha^{\prime\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}+\alpha_{s}^{\prime}(u)(-\Delta u)

and, for
s>\max\{u(x):x\in\partial D\}
,

\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma=\int_{D}\Delta\alpha_{s}(u)dvol=\int_{D}(-\alpha^{\prime\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}+\alpha^{\prime}(u)\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}f)dvol.

Hence

\int_{D}(-\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}+\alpha_{s}^{\prime}(u)\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}f^{+})\,dvol\leq\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\int_{D}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol

\int_{D}|\Delta\alpha_{s}(u)|dvol=\int_{D}(-\alpha^{\prime\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}+\alpha^{\prime}(u)\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}(f^{+}+f^{-}))dvol.

By Fatou’s lemma, as

, we have

\int_{D}f^{+}dvol\leq\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\int_{D}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol.

So the claim is proven. Moreover,

\int_{D}|\Delta\alpha_{s}(u)|dvol\leq\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\int_{D}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol.

Consequently, for
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
,

\displaystyle|-\Delta

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}(u)(\phi)|=|\int_{D}(-\Delta\alpha_{s}(u))\phi dvol|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{D}|\Delta\alpha_{s}(u)|dvol\|\phi\|_{C^{0}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq(\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\int_{D\setminus S}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol)\|\phi\|_{C^{0}(D)},

for any
s
larger. Before we show
-\Delta u
is a Radon measure, let us state and prove a lemma which is useful
for the proof now and later in the following sections.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold and

be a

. And let

Suppose that

Then

(3.6)

\nabla u\in L^{p}(D)\text{ and }u\in L^{q}(D)

for

and

.

Proof.

In fact, we continue from the above, for

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\int_{D}

\displaystyle\leq(\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\int_{D\setminus S}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol)\|\phi\|_{C^{0}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\int_{D\setminus S}R^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}dvol)\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\lambda}(D)}

and

∎

Back to the proof of Lemma

(3.10)

\displaystyle(-\Delta u)(\phi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to\infty}(-\Delta\alpha_{s}(u))(\phi))

3.3.
Main result on the Hausdorff dimensions

Now we are ready to state and prove our result on the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set
S
, which is a significant
improvement of Proposition
3.1
. For convenience of readers, we recall
Theorem
1.3
from the introduction.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
S
be a
closed subset in
M^{n}
. And let
D
be an open neighborhood of
S
.
Suppose that
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
is a conformal metric on
D\setminus S
and is geodesically complete near
S
.
Then the Hausdorff dimension

(3.11)

\text{dim}_{\mathscr{H}}(S)\leq\frac{n-2}{2}

provided
R^{-}[g]\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(D\setminus S,g)\bigcap L^{p}(D\setminus S,g)

for some
p>n/2
. Consequently, (
3.11
) holds when the scalar curvature
R[g]
of the
conformal metric
g
is nonnegative.

Proof.

The outline of the proof is as follows: We first show that one may assume the scalar curvature
R[\bar{g}]

is nonpositive without loss of generality for our purpose. Then we use the Green function to construct the integral representation of the
solution to the Laplace equation. Finally we apply Lemma
3.2
, Theorem
2.2
,
and the geodesic completeness to complete the proof .

Step I
In this step, we find a conformal change
\bar{h}=v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
such that the scalar curvature
R[\bar{h}]
is
nonpositive (or even negative) in
D
, based on the similar idea used in the proof of
[
25
, Lemma 3.1]
. This is trivial if the
Yamabe constant
of
(M^{n},\bar{g})
is nonpositive. Otherwise, take a point
p\in M^{n}\setminus D
and consider a connected sum of
M^{n}
with
another compact Riemannian manifold

(M^{n}_{1},\bar{g}_{1})
with very negative Yamabe constant in such way that the conformal structure on the connected sum

M^{n}\sharp M^{n}_{1}
is unchanged in
D\subset M^{n}\sharp M^{n}_{1}
. Then, by
[
19
, Theorem 5]
, the Yamabe constant of
such connected sum is negative. Therefore one easily finds a conformal metric
\bar{h}=v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
whose scalar curvature
is negative in
D
, where the function
v\in C^{\infty}(\bar{D})
and

(3.12)

C^{-1}\leq v\leq C\text{ in $\bar{D}$}

for some positive constant
C
. In any cases, we have
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}=(\frac{u}{v})^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{h}

and the scalar curvature
R[\bar{h}]
is nonpositive. In conclusion, due to (
3.12
),
we may simply assume
R[\bar{g}]
is nonpositive (or even negative) in
D
without
loss of any generality for the purpose of obtaining the growth estimate like the one
given in Theorem
2.2
.

Step II
In this step, we use the Green function to construct the integral representation of the solution
u
. In the light of
Lemma
3.2
, we may write

-\Delta u=\mu\text{ in $D$}

for a Radon measure
\mu
on
D
. Let
G(x,y)
be the Green function on
D
given by
[
6
, Theorem 4.17]
. Then

u=\int_{D}G(x,y)d\mu(y)+h

for a smooth function
h
that is harmonic in
D
. By
[
6
, Theorem 4.17 (c)]
, we have

0<G(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{d(x,y)^{n-2}}

for some constant
C
and
x,y\in D
. We therefore arrive at, for
x\in D
,

(3.13)

u(x)\leq\int_{D}G(x,y)d\mu^{+}+h(x)\leq C\mathscr{R}^{2,D}_{\mu^{+}}(x)+h(x).

Step III
Assume otherwise that
\text{dim}_{\mathscr{H}}(S)=d>\frac{n-2}{2}
.
From Theorem
2.2
, there is a point
p\in S
such that

\mathscr{R}^{2,D}_{\mu^{+}}(x)\leq\frac{C}{d(x,p)^{n-2-d}}

at least for
x
along a short geodesic ray
\gamma
from
p
, which implies

(3.14)

u(x)^{\frac{2}{n-2}}\leq\frac{C}{d(x,p)^{\frac{2(n-2-d)}{n-2}}}

at least for
x
along a short geodesic ray
\gamma
from
p
, where

\frac{2(n-2-d)}{n-2}=2-\frac{2d}{n-2}<1

when
d>\frac{n-2}{2}
. Now the length of the curve
\gamma
with respect to the conformal metric
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
is

L(\gamma,g)\leq C\int_{0}^{l_{0}}\frac{1}{s^{\frac{2(n-2-d)}{n-2}}}ds<\infty

when
d>\frac{n-2}{2}
, which contradicts with the geodesic completeness of the conformal metric
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\bar{g}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

\displaystyle\Delta u\Delta\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol-\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\alpha}}\Delta u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma-\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Delta ud\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}(4A(\nabla u,\nabla\phi_{\alpha,\beta})-(n-2)J\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi_{\alpha,\beta})dvol

\displaystyle+\frac{n-4}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}Qu\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol=\frac{n-4}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}Q_{4}[g]u^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol,

where
\nu
is the outward normal direction at the boundary and the boundary term

\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(-\Delta u)d\sigma
is nonnegative due
to (
4.2
) and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}=|\nabla u|
.
Therefore,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle(\Delta u)^{2}dvol+\beta\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}(\Delta u)(\Delta(1-\eta))dvol

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\alpha}}(-\Delta u)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+C\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\nabla u|^{2}dvol

\displaystyle-\beta\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}(4A(\nabla u,\nabla(1-\eta))-(n-2)J\nabla u\cdot\nabla(1-\eta))dvol

\displaystyle+C\beta\int_{D}u\,dvol+C\beta\int_{D}Q_{4}^{-}[g]u^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}}dvol,

where we use (
4.7
) and

|\phi|\leq\beta
in
\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}
.
After applying integral by parts, we get,

(4.10)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\Delta u|^{2}dvol\leq C\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\nabla u|^{2}dvol+C\beta

for some constant
C
depending on the cut-off function
\eta
,
u
at
\partial\Sigma_{\alpha}
, and
\|u\|_{L^{1}(D)}
, because

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}\Delta u\Delta\eta dvol=\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}u\Delta^{2}\eta dvol

and similarly we may unload all derivatives from
u
by integral by parts for the other terms in the above
(
4.9
). Now, to get a priori estimate, we calculate

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\nabla u|^{2}dvol

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(n-4)C}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}dvol+\frac{(n-4)C}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}u^{2}dvol

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2C}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\Delta u|^{2}dvol+\frac{(n-4)C}{4}(\alpha+\beta)\int_{D}u\,dvol,

due to (
4.2
), which implies, from (
4.10
),

(4.11)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\Delta u|^{2}dvol\leq C(\alpha+\beta).

We claim that (
4.11
) implies

(4.12)

\Delta u\in L^{p}(D)

for all
p\in[1,\frac{n}{n-2})
. To prove (
4.12
), we first derive from (
4.11
),

2^{-i}\int_{\Sigma_{2^{i-1}}\setminus\Sigma_{2^{i}}}|\Delta u|^{2}dvol\leq C,

for
i\geq i_{0}
large, which implies

\int_{\Sigma_{2^{i-1}}\setminus\Sigma_{2^{i}}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{u}dvol\leq 2C

and, for
s>0
appropriately small for any
p\in[1,\frac{n}{n-2})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{2^{i_{0}-1}}\setminus S}

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{u^{1+s}}dvol=\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{2^{i-1}}\setminus\Sigma_{2^{i}}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{u^{1+s}}dvol

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}2^{s(-i+1)}\int_{\Sigma_{2^{i-1}}\setminus\Sigma_{2^{i}}}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{u}dvol<\infty.

Thus

\int_{D\setminus S}|\Delta u|^{p}dvol\leq(\int_{D\setminus S}\frac{|\Delta u|^{2}}{u^{1+s}}dvol)^{\frac{p}{2}}(\int_{D\setminus S}u^{\frac{(1+s)p}{2-p}}dvol)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}<\infty

where

\frac{(1+s)p}{2-p}<\frac{n}{n-4}.

∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Under the same assumptions as in Lemma
4.1
we have

\text{dim}_{\mathscr{H}}(S)\leq n-4.

Proof.

Consequently from (
4.2
) and (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\nabla\frac{u_{\alpha,\beta}}{\beta}|^{4}dvol

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{4}}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}dvol

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{4}}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}|\Delta u|^{2}dvol

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^{3}}{\beta^{4}}

for some
\alpha
appropriately large and
\beta\to\infty
,
which leads to
\text{Cap}_{4}(S)=0
and completes the proof similar to the proof of Proposition
3.1

(cf.
[
3
]
and
[
35
, Theorem 2.10 in Chapter VI]
).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold for

and

be a
closed subset in
M^{n}
. And let

be an open neighborhood of
S
where the scalar curvature

.

with nonnegative scalar curvature

and is geodesically complete near

. And suppose also that

Then
\Delta^{2}u
is a Radon measure on

and
\Delta^{2}u|_{S}\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
v=-\Delta u
. We will follow the proof of Lemma
3.2
to show that

is a Radon

using Lemma
4.1
.
We continue to use the notations from the proof of Lemma

. We calculate

-\Delta\alpha_{s}(v)=\alpha^{\prime}(v)(-\Delta v)-\alpha^{\prime\prime}(v)|\nabla v|^{2},

where, by the

), we have

-\Delta v=-\text{div}(4A(\nabla u)-(n-2)J\nabla u)-\frac{n-4}{2}Q_{4}u+Q_{4}[g]u^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}}\text{ in $D\setminus S$}

and

-\Delta\alpha_{s}(v)=-\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}(v)|\nabla v|^{2}+\alpha^{\prime}(v)(-\text{div}(4A(\nabla u)-(n-2)J\nabla u)-\frac{n-4}{2}Q_{4}u+Q_{4}[g]u^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}})

in

. In the light of Lemma

, terms in the right hand side of the above equation are all integrable

∎

, we may assume the scalar curvature

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

. And let

and is geodesically complete near

. And suppose also that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\text{as a Radon measure, }-\Delta u|_{S}=0;

\displaystyle\text{in fact, }\Delta u\in L^{p}(D)\text{ for any $p\in[1,\frac{4}{3})$}.

Proof.

First, by Lemma
3.1
for

, we have

u(x)\to\infty\text{ as $x\to S$}.

Then, by the proof of Lemma

and (

), we know that

•

;

•

\nabla u\in L^{p}(D)
for any

and
u\in L^{p}(D)
for any

•

|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{6}(-R+R[g]e^{2u})\in L^{1}(D)

The same argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.1

as a Radon measure.
Also, for the

Next we multiple
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}
to both sides of

) and integrate

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle\Delta u\Delta\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol-\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\alpha}}\Delta u\frac{\partial\phi_{\alpha,\beta}}{\partial\nu}d\sigma-\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}\Delta u\frac{\partial\phi_{\alpha,\beta}}{\partial\nu}d\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}(4A(\nabla u,\nabla\phi_{\alpha})-2J\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}Q_{4}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol=\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}\setminus\Sigma_{\alpha+\beta}}Q_{4}[g]e^{4u}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}dvol

and, again, the boundary term at

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(M^{4},\bar{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
S
be a
closed subset in

. And let
D
be an open neighborhood of

. Suppose that
g=e^{2u}\bar{g}
is a conformal metric on

with nonnegative scalar curvature

and is geodesically complete near

. And suppose that

\int_{D}Q_{4}^{-}[g]dvol[g]<\infty.

Proof.

As before, we use the argument in Step I on Theorem
3.1
to assume that the scalar curvature
of the background metric
\bar{g}
is less than a negative number, i.e.
R\leq-c_{0}<0
, without loss of any generality for our

v=-\Delta u+u

4.3
and (
4.14

•

v(x)\to\infty

;

•

All terms in the right side of (
4.17

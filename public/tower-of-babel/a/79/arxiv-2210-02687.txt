Abstract

∎

5
Odd-sum Chromatic Number of Surfaces

In this short section, for each orientable surface
\Sigma_{g}
we consider

\chi_{\textrm{\scriptsize{os}}}(\Sigma_{g})
, which is the maximum value of
\chi_{\textrm{\scriptsize{os}}}(G)
taken over all

. Caro et al. posed the problem: “Determine

\chi_{\textrm{\scriptsize{os}}}(\Sigma_{g})
, where

is the Euler genus.” They continued “It is our

H(\Sigma_{g})=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+48g}}{2}\right\rfloor
is the Heawood number of the surface
\Sigma_{g}

We disprove this belief as follows.

Theorem 4
.

\chi_{\textrm{\scriptsize{os}}}(\Sigma_{g})\geq-3+\sqrt{24g-67}
. In particular,
\lim_{g\to\infty}\chi_{\textrm{\scriptsize{os}}}(\Sigma_{g})-H(\Sigma_{g})=\infty
.

For our proof, we will simply bound (from above) the genus of the product

K_{2}\square K_{n}
. Recall
[
1
, Proposition 3.12]
that
\chi_{\textrm{\scriptsize{os}}}(K_{2}\square K_{n})=2n=\chi_{\textrm{\scriptsize{os}}}(K_{2n})
, when

is
odd.
Intuitively we should expect that if a given surface admits an embedding of

K_{2n}

K_{2}\square K_{n^{\prime}}
for some

n^{\prime}>n
, since the latter graph is less dense than the former. To formalize this
intuition, we need a bound on the genus of a cartesian product.
In the next theorem, the
first Betti number
of a graph
H

\mathcal{B}(H)
is given by
\mathcal{B}(H):=|E(H)|-|V(H)|+1

(
[
3
]
)
.

The genus
\gamma(G_{1}\square G_{2})
of

satisfies the inequality:

\gamma(G_{1}\square G_{2})\leq|V(G_{1})|\gamma(G_{2})+|V(G_{2})|\gamma(G_{1})+\mathcal{B}(K_{|V(G_{1})|,|V(G_{2})|}).

We will also need the following well-known result.

Theorem C

(
[
2
]
)
.

The genus
\gamma(K_{n})
of the complete graph

is given by

\gamma(K_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{(n-3)(n-4)}{12}\right\rceil.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We consider the graph
K_{2}\square K_{n}
. By Theorem

, we have

\gamma(K_{2}\square K_{n})\leq 2\lceil\frac{(n-3)(n-4)}{12}\rceil+n(0)+(2n-(2+n)+1)\leq(n-3)(n-4)/6+11/6+n-1

So there exists odd

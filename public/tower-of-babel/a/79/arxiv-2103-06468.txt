Symbolic powers of multipartite hypergraphs and Waldschmidt constant
Ali Alilooee, Arindam Banerjee

Abstract
. In this paper, we find a criterion to answer when the second symbolic and ordinary powers of the edge ideal of a 3-partite hypergraph are equal. Also, we give the formula to compute the Waldschmidt constant of the path ideals of a cycle.



1.
introduction

In this paper, we study the symbolic power of some classes of squarefree monomial ideals.
Let
I
be an ideal in a Noetherian domain
R
, its
n^{\text{th}}
symbolic power is defined as follow

I^{(n)}=\displaystyle\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Ass}_{R}(R/I)}(I^{n}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap R).

Two of the most challenging questions about the symbolic powers are:

(1)

In general, the symbolic and ordinary powers are not equal. The question is for what classes of ideals, we have the equality of the symbolic and the ordinary powers. It is a too broad question. Another less overall problem here is for what powers
n
we have

I^{n}=I^{(n)}
?

(2)

Another interesting questions is to find the generators of the symbolic powers of some classes of ideals. More precisely, if
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, we know there is a combinatorial presentation of the generators of
I
. The question is what the combinatorial character can describe the generators of
I^{(n)}
for some
n
?

In this paper, we address these questions for a particular class of cubic squarefree monomial ideal. More accurately, we consider the class of 3-uniform, 3-partite hypergraph, and we will give a combinatorial description of the generators of the second symbolic power of these hypergraphs.
We define the notion of bad hypergraphs and classify all 3-uniform 3-partite hypergraphs for which second symbolic power and second ordinary powers are the same:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a 3-uniform and 3-partite hypergraph and
I=I(\mathcal{H})
be its edge ideal. Then
I^{(2)}=I^{2}
if and only if one of the following holds

(1)

\mathcal{H}
has no bad subhypergraph of length 3;

(2)

If
\mathcal{H}
has a bad subhypergraph of length 3 say
\mathcal{B}
then there is a hyperedge
E
of
\mathcal{B}
such that
V(\mathcal{B})\backslash E
is a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}

The “ideal containment problem”, given a nontrivial homogeneous ideal I of a polynomial ring over a field, the problem is to determine all positive integer pairs
(m,r)
such that
I^{(m)}\subseteq I^{r}
. This
problem was motivated by the fundamental results of Ein-Lazarsfeld-Smith and Hochster-Huneke showing that containment
holds whenever
m\geq r(n-1)
. In order to capture more precise information about these containments, Bocci and Harbourne introduced the resurgence of
I
, denoted
\rho(I)
and defined as
\rho(I)=\text{sup}\{\frac{m}{r}|I^{(m)}\subsetneq I^{r}\}
.
In general, computing
\rho(I)
is quite difficult. There has been an ongoing
research programme to bound
\rho(I)
in terms of other invariants of
I
that may be easier
to compute. One of the important constant for an ideal is the
Waldschmidt constant
. Let
I
be a nonzero homogenous ideal of the graded ring
R
, we let
\alpha(I)=\min\{d:I_{d}\neq 0\}
. The Waldschmidt constant of
I
is defined as follow

\widehat{\alpha}(I)=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\alpha(I^{(n)})}{n}.

Waldschmidt in
[
16
]
showed that this limit exists for ideals of finite point sets
in the context of complex analysis. He was interested in finding the minimum degree of a hypersurface that passes through the set of points with multiplicities. In other words, he was studying
\alpha(I^{(n)})
when
I
defined a set of points.

We also study this in this paper, and our main result regarding this is as follows:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a simple
r
-uniform and
r
-partite hypergraph and
I=I(\mathcal{H})
be its edge ideal. Then the Waldschmidt constant of
I
(i.e.
\widehat{\alpha}(I)
) is
r
.

We organize the paper as follows. In section (2) we review some necessary preliminaries. Then in section (3), we will consider the
r
-partite hypergraph and we show the Waldschmidt constant of their edge ideals is
r
. In section (4) we shall study cubic path ideals.

\mathcal{H}
be a simple 3-uniform, 3-partite hypergraph and
I=I(\mathcal{H})
be its edge ideal. If
I^{(2)}\neq I^{2}
, then the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
has at least three hyperedges which intersect pairwise and they have at most one variable in common.

Proof.

To show this we consider
e,e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
from the above. If they have at most one variable in common we are done so we assume they have two variables in common say
w_{1},w_{2}
one of
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
(say
w_{1}
) must be squarefree in
X^{a}
(i.e.

w_{1}^{2}
does not divide
X^{a}
). Hence from Lemma
2.6

and by using the fact that
w_{1}
is a squarefree factor of
X^{a}
we can say that there is an edge
e_{0}
dividing
\frac{X^{a}}{w_{1}}

which is not one of
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
(since otherwise
X^{a}\in I^{2}
which is a contradiction). Now note that we can say
e_{0}
intersects
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
and also since
w_{1}\notin e_{0}
they have at most one variable in common. So we just need to replace one of
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
by
e_{0}
and it settles the proof.
∎

Therefore, we have shown
e,e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
have at most one variable in common. We consider the following cases:

(1)

Suppose
e,e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
have no variable in common. If two of them say
e
and
e^{\prime}
have two variables in common we can write
e\cap e^{\prime}=\{x,y\}
. Without loss of generality we assume
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. Since the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is 3-uniform and 3-partite we can conclude that
e=\{x,y,z_{1}\}
and
e^{\prime}=\{x,y,z_{2}\}
such that
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z
. Since we know
e^{\prime\prime}
intersects
e
and
e^{\prime}
and
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
have no vertices in common we can conclude that
z_{1},z_{2}\in e^{\prime\prime}
which is a contradiction because
\mathcal{H}
is 3-partite. Therefore

|e\cap e^{\prime}|=|e\cap e^{\prime\prime}|=|e^{\prime}\cap e^{\prime\prime}|=1

and since
e,e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
have no variable in common we can write
e\cap e^{\prime}\neq e\cap e^{\prime\prime}\neq e^{\prime}\cap e^{\prime\prime}
. So it is clear that the subhypergraph
\left\langle e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\right\rangle
of
\mathcal{H}
is the bad hypergraph of length 3.

(2)

Suppose
e,e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
have exactly one variable in common say
w
. Now we consider the followings

(a)

Suppose
e\cap e^{\prime}=e\cap e^{\prime\prime}=e^{\prime}\cap e^{\prime\prime}=\{w\}.
Again since
X^{a}\notin I^{2}
we have
w^{2}
doesn’t divide
X^{a}
and since
X^{a}\in I^{(2)}
from Lemma
2.6
there is
e_{0}\in I
such that
e_{0}
divides
X^{a}/w
. Since
w^{2}
doesn’t divide
X^{a}
we can write
w\notin e_{0}
and then
e_{0}\notin\{e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\}
. Also since
X^{a}\notin I^{2}
we can conclude that
e_{0}
intersects
e,e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
. Since
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
have just one variable in common (i.e
w
) and
w\notin e_{0}
if we consider
e_{0}
with two of
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
(say
e,e^{\prime}
) we can conclude that
e,e^{\prime},e_{0}
are three hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
which have no variable in common. Therefore, from part (1) we can say the subhypergraph
\left\langle e,e^{\prime},e_{0}\right\rangle
of
\mathcal{H}
is the bad hypergraph of length 3.

(b)

Suppose
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=|e\cap e^{\prime\prime}|=|e^{\prime}\cap e^{\prime\prime}|=2
. Since
\mathcal{H}
is 3-partite and
e,e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
have exactly one vertex in common then this case never happens. So we assume at least one couple of
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
intersecting each other at just
w
(say
e\cap e^{\prime\prime}=\{w\}
). We assume
e\cap e^{\prime}=\{w,z_{1}\}
. Note that
w^{2}
does not divide
X^{a}
because otherwise
ee^{\prime\prime}
divides
X^{a}
and then
X^{a}\in I^{2}
which is a contradiction. Hence, from Lemma
2.6
we can say there is
e_{0}\in I
dividing
\frac{X^{a}}{w}
. Obviously
e_{0},e,e^{\prime\prime}
have no variable in common therefore, from part (1) we can say the subhypergraph
\left\langle e,e^{\prime\prime},e_{0}\right\rangle
of
\mathcal{H}
is the bad hypergraph of length 3.

∎

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a 3-uniform, 3-partite hypergraph and
I=I(\mathcal{H})
be its edge ideal. Suppose
\mathcal{H}
has no bad subhypergraph, then
I^{(2)}=I^{2}
.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a 3-uniform and 3-partite hypergraph and
I=I(\mathcal{H})
be its edge ideal. Then
I^{(2)}=I^{2}
if and only if one of the following holds

(1)

\mathcal{H}
has no bad subhypergraph of length 3;

(2)

If
\mathcal{H}
has a bad subhypergraph of length 3, say
\mathcal{B}
, then there is a hyperedge
E
of
\mathcal{B}
such that
V(\mathcal{B})\backslash E
is a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}

Proof.

If part (1) holds then from Corollary
3.14
we have
I^{(2)}=I^{2}
. Then we assume part(2) holds, we will show
I^{(2)}=I^{2}
. Suppose
I^{(2)}\neq I^{2}
. Then there is a monomial
X^{a}\in I^{(2)}\backslash I^{2}
. From Observation
3.12
we can say there is a bad subhypergraph of length 3, say
\mathcal{B}
, such that

X^{a}=\left(\prod_{x\in V(\mathcal{B})}x\right)u

where
u
is a monomial. From part (2) we know there is a hyperedge
E
in
\mathcal{B}
such that

\prod_{x\in V(\mathcal{B})\backslash V(E)}x\in I.

Then we have

X^{a}=\left(\prod_{x\in V(\mathcal{B})}x\right)u=\left(\prod_{x\in V(E)}x\right)\left(\prod_{x\in V(\mathcal{B})\backslash V(E)}x\right)u\in I^{2}

and it is a contradiction.

Now we suppose
I^{(2)}=I^{2}
. If there is no bad subhypergraph of length 3 we have nothing to show. So we assume
\mathcal{H}
has a bad subhypergraph of length 3 say
\mathcal{B}
. We consider the monomial

.
It is straightforward to see that

. It settles our claim.

4.
Path ideals

Path ideals first were introduced by Conca and De Negri in
[
5
]
. In this section we will study symbolic powers of a cubic path ideal of a graph. First we will recall the definition of the path ideal.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph. A sequence of distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
is called a path if
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
is an edge in
G
for each
1\leq i<n
. The number of edges in a path is defined as the length of that path. We define the
path ideal
of
G
, denoted by
I_{t}(G)
, to be the
ideal of
k[V]
generated by the monomials of the form

x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\dots x_{i_{t}}
where
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{t}}
is
a path in
G
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph and
I_{t}(G)
be a path ideal of length
t-1
when
2\leq t
. Then we define the
path hypergraph
as a hypergraph whose vertices are
V
and whose edges consist of all paths of the length
t-1
. This hypergraph is denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{t}(G)
.

From Theorem
2.8
, [
[
10
]
, Theorem 2.7] and [
[
11
]
, Theorem 3.2] we have the following result.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
t\geq 2
be an integer and
G
be a rooted tree (i.e. A rooted tree is a tree with one vertex chosen as a root). The path hypergraph
\mathcal{H}_{t}(G)
is a simplicial tree (for details about simplicial trees see
[
4
]
). Moreover for the path ideal
J=I_{t}(G)
we have
J^{(n)}=J^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Now we are ready to show when the
t
-path hypergraph of a cycle is
t
-partite.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
2\leq t\leq n
be integers and
C_{n}
be a cycle over
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. Suppose
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
be the
t
-path hypergraph of
C_{n}
. Then we have
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
is a
t
-partite hypergraph if and only if
n\equiv 0\pmod{t}
.

Proof.

We assume
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
is a
t
-partite hypergraph with the
t
-partition
(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})
.
Since
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}\}
is a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
, without loss of generality we can write
x_{i}\in X_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,t
. We will show that for each positive integer
g\leq n
if

g\not\equiv 0\pmod{t}
we have
x_{g}\in X_{i}
when
g\equiv i\pmod{t}
and otherwise
x_{g}\in X_{t}
.

If we assume
g\leq t
, it is obvious. So we assume that
g>t
. We proceed the proof by the induction on
g
. If
g=t+1
since we have
\{x_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{t+1}\}
is a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
and because
x_{i}\in X_{i}
for
i=2,3,\dots,t
then we can conclude that
x_{t+1}\in X_{1}
and it settles the base of the induction.

We assume
g>t+1
and
g\equiv a\pmod{t}
where
1\leq a\leq t
.
We consider the hyperedge
E=\{x_{g-t+1},\dots,x_{g}\}
of
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
. By the induction hypotheses we can write
x_{g-j}\in X_{k_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,t-1
when
g-j\equiv k_{j}\pmod{t}
and
k_{j}\in\{1,2,\dots,t\}=[t]
. Now note that for each
j
we can say
k_{j}\neq a
, because otherwise there is
1\leq j\leq t
such that

a+j\equiv a\pmod{t}
and then
j\equiv 0\pmod{t}
and it is a contradiction.
Then since
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
is
t
-partite and
E
is a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
we can write the
k_{j}
s are distinct and thus
we can conclude that
x_{g}\in X_{a}
and it settles our claim.

On the other hand since
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t-1},x_{n}\}
is a hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
and
x_{i}\in X_{i}
for each
i=1,2,\dots,t-1
then because
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
is
t
-partite we can conclude that
x_{n}\in X_{t}
and then
n\equiv t\equiv 0\pmod{t}
.

∎

Corollary 4.5

Let

Lemma 4.6

Let

be a cycle graph over the vertex set

and
2\leq t\leq n
be an integer and let
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
be the path hypergraph of

\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})

2.4

Proof.

Pick

\pi(x_{i})=x_{a_{i}}

∎

Now we are ready to show the following proposition.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
C_{n}
be a cycle graph over the vertex set
V=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
and
2\leq t\leq n
be an integer. Suppose

and put

\widehat{\alpha}(I)=\begin{cases}t&r=0\\
\displaystyle\frac{n}{q+1}&r\neq 0\end{cases}

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
we have
\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n})
is vertex-transitive (Definition
2.4
), then by using [
[
12
]
, Proposition 3.1.1] we have

\chi^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n}))=\displaystyle\frac{n}{{\mathfrak{a}}}
where
{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathfrak{a}}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n}))

r=0
, then it is straightforward to see that the independent set

W=V\backslash\{x_{ti}:i=1,2,\dots,q\}

|W|=n-q
we can write
{\mathfrak{a}}=n-q
. Hence

(4.8)

\displaystyle\chi^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n}))=\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-q}

\displaystyle\text{if $r=0$}.

If we assume

W=V\backslash\{x_{ti},x_{n}:i=1,2,\dots,q\}

{\mathfrak{a}}=n-q-1

(4.9)

\displaystyle\chi^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{t}(C_{n}))=\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-q-1}

\displaystyle\text{if $r\neq 0$}.

By using (
4.8

4.9
) and by using [
[
3
]

First Law of Black Hole in the Gravitational Electromagnetic System
Jie Jiang ab, Aofei Sang ab, and Ming Zhang c

Abstract
After considering the quantum corrections of Einstein-Maxwell theory, the effective theory will contain some higher-curvature terms and nonminimally coupled electromagnetic fields. In this paper, we study the first law of black holes in the gravitational electromagnetic system with the Lagrangian ℒ ⁡ ( g a ​ b , R a ​ b ​ c ​ d , F a ​ b ) \mathcal{L}(g_{ab},R_{abcd},F_{ab}) . Firstly, we calculate the Noether charge and the variational identity in this theory, and then generically derive the first law of thermodynamics for an asymptotically flat stationary axisymmetrical symmetric black hole without the requirement that the electromagnetic field is smooth on the bifurcation surface. Our results indicate that the first law of black hole thermodynamics might be valid for the Einstein-Maxwell theory with some quantum corrections in the effective region.

Introduction

General relativity is the most successful theory to describe the interaction of gravity. It predicts the existence of the black hole, which is a fundamental object in theoretical physics, astronomy, and cosmology.
Over the past few decades, many studies of general relativity have shown that black holes can be viewed as a thermodynamic system and satisfy the four laws of thermodynamics
A2
;
A3
;
A31
. By considering the semi-classical quantum effect in curved spacetime, Hawking found that the black hole can be regarded as a blackbody system
A4
, which provides a natural provided a natural explanation to the laws of black hole thermodynamics. After that, the thermodynamics of black holes has aroused wide interest among researchers, and people believe that it can give us a deeper understanding of gravity.

The most profound laws of black hole mechanics are the first and second laws. With a straightforward derivation, the first law of the Kerr-Newmann black hole shows the relationship between the variations of black hole mass
M
, angular momentum
J
, electric charge
Q
, and areas, i.e.,

\displaystyle\delta M=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\delta A+\Omega_{H}\delta J+\Phi_{H}\delta Q\,,

(1)

in which
\kappa

\Omega_{H}
, and
\Phi_{H}
are the surface gravity, angular velocity, and electric potential of the event horizon. The original derivation of the first law demands that the perturbation of the spacetime is stationary (“equilibrium state version”)
A31
. Moreover, their calculation is also based on the Einstein equation. After that, the discussion is extended to the “physical process version”, where a stationary black hole is changed by some infinitesimal physical process
Sudarsky:1992ty
;
Iyer:1994ys
. In particular, Iyer and Wald
Iyer:1994ys
show that the above first law of thermodynamic relation is also applicable to any diffeomorphism covariant theories, in which the first law of black holes can be regarded as a straightforward result of the variational identity, and it can be expressed as

\displaystyle\delta M=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\delta S+\Omega_{H}\delta J\,,

(2)

where

\displaystyle S\equiv-2\pi\int_{\mathcal{B}}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta R_{abcd}}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ab}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{cd}

(3)

is the Wald entropy, in which
\bm{\epsilon}_{ab}
is the binormal of the cross-section
\mathcal{B}
of the event horizon. However, it is worth noting that the “potential-charge” term does not appear explicitly in the result derived by Iyer and Wald
Iyer:1994ys
. The different result is caused by the assumption that the asymptotically flat stationary black hole contains a bifurcated Killing horizon and all fields are smooth on the Killing horizon as well as the bifurcation surface. In the gravitational electromagnetic system, because of the gauge covariance of the electromagnetic field, the vector potential is not a real physical quantity in the spacetime, and therefore it is not necessary to demand that it is smooth on the Killing horizon. With this consideration, Gao derived the first law of the asymptotically flat stationary black holes in Einstein-Maxwell and Einstein-Yang-Mills theories without the assumption that the vector potential is smooth on the Killing horizon
Gao:2003ys
. Their result shows the same expression as Eq. (
1
).

The standard Einstein-Maxwell is a good approximation to describe the gravitational and electromagnetic interactions at a low-energy regime. However, at higher-energy regime, the effective theory should be corrected by adding some higher-order derivative terms to take into account the quantum effects

A14
;
A15
;
A16

A natural question is whether the first law of black holes is also satisfied in these effective theories after the quantum corrections are taken into account. Therefore, in this paper, we would like to extend Gao’s discussion
Gao:2003ys
into a more general gravitational electromagnetic system and derive the first law of black holes without the assumption that the vector potential is smooth at the Killing horizon.

The first law of the stationary axisymmetric black holes

In this section, we would like to derive the first law of black holes in the gravitational electromagnetic system with Lagrangian (
4
). Let
(M,g_{ab})
is an asymptotically flat stationary axisymmetric spacetime satisfying the asymptotic condition of “Case I” in Ref.
Wald:1999wa
, and there is a bifurcated Killing horizon
H
with a bifurcated surface
\mathcal{B}
. Assume that the metric
g_{ab}
and electromagnetic strength
F_{ab}
is smooth near the horizon as well as the bifurcation surface. The generated Killing vector field of the Killing horizon can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \xi^{a}=t^{a}+\Omega_{H}\varphi^{a}\,,\end{aligned}

(50)

in which we have denoted
\Omega_{H}\varphi^{a}=\Omega_{H}^{(\mu)}\varphi^{a}_{(\mu)}
. Here
t^{a}
and
\varphi_{(\mu)}^{a}
are the Killing vector fields related to the time transition and axial symmetries of the spacetime,
\Omega_{H}^{(\mu)}
is the velocity of the black hole horizon
\mathcal{H}
. In the following, we consider a one-parameter family
\phi(\lambda)
, any element in which is a stationary axisymmetric black hole as described above. Considering the diffeomorphism invariance of the theory, we can choose a gauge such that
\xi^{a}
and Killing horizon
\mathcal{H}
(including the bifurcation surface
\mathcal{B}
) is independent on
\lambda
, i.e., they are fixed under the variation. Replacing
\zeta^{a}
by
\xi^{a}
and considering the symmetries

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{ab}(\lambda)=0\,,\quad\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\bm{A}(\lambda)=0\,,

(51)

the variational identity (
46
) implies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\xi}-\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)]=0\,.\end{aligned}

(52)

Choose
\Sigma
to a hypersurface connecting the sphere
S_{\infty}
at infinity and a cross-section
S
on the future Killing horizon. Integration of Eq. (
52
) on
\Sigma
, using the Stokes theorem, we can further obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathcal{\infty}}\left[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\xi}-\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)\right]=\int_{S}\left[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\xi}-\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)\right]\,.\end{aligned}

(53)

For the left side of the above expression, using the definition of the mass and angular motion (
48
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathcal{\infty}}\left[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\xi}-\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)\right]&=\int_{\mathcal{\infty}}\left[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\xi}-t\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)\right]\\
&=\int_{\mathcal{\infty}}\left[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{t}-t\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)\right]+\int_{\mathcal{\infty}}\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\Omega_{H}\varphi}\\
&=\delta M-\Omega_{H}\delta J\,.\end{aligned}

(54)

For the right side of Eq. (
53
), considering the gauge choice
\delta\xi^{a}=0
, we can replace
\bar{\delta}
by
\delta
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{S}\left[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\xi}-\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)\right]=&\delta\int_{S}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}\left[E_{F}^{ab}A^{c}\xi_{c}-2\xi_{c}\nabla_{d}E_{R}^{abcd}-E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{[c}\xi_{d]}\right]\\
&-\int_{S}\xi\cdot\left[\bm{\Theta}^{\text{e.m}}(\phi,\delta\bm{A})+\bm{\Theta}^{\text{grav}}(\phi,\delta g)\right]\,.\end{aligned}

(55)

Since we assume that
R_{abcd}
,
g_{ab}
and
F_{ab}
is smooth near the Killing horizon (including the bifurcation surface
\mathcal{B}
),
E_{R}^{abcd}
and
E_{F}^{ab}
would also be the smooth tensor near the horizon. Before deriving the first law, we first prove the following Lemma:

Lemma 2
For a stationary black hole with bifurcated Killing horizon
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\xi^{a}
be a Killing vector field generated the future Killing horizon,
S
be a cross-section of future horizon, and
s^{a}
is another null vector field on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} s^{a}\xi_{a}=1\,,\quad s^{a}s_{a}=0\,,\quad w^{a}_{i}s_{a}=0\,,\end{aligned}

(56)

in which
w_{i}^{a}
is the tangent vector on the cross section
S
. Denote
z_{i}^{a}=\{\xi^{a},s^{a},w^{a}_{j}\}
to the basis on the cross section. Then, for any tensor field
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}
which is smooth on the horizon (including bifurcation surface) and satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}X=X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}
, if
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a}
is not zero, the number of
\xi^{a}
must not be greater than the number of
s^{a}
.

Proof.
Consider a foliation of the horizon
\mathcal{H}
which is obtained from the cross-section
S
by the diffeomorphism generated by the Killing vector field
\xi^{a}
. The vector field
z_{i}^{a}
is also generated by this diffeomorphism, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}z_{i}^{a}=0\,.

(57)

For the slice (cross section)
S
is not the bifurcation surface
\mathcal{B}
,
z_{i}^{a}
would be a finite vector field, i.e., contraction of any finite tensors is finite. Since
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}
is smooth on the Killing horizon
\mathcal{H}
,
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a}
would be finite on any cross section
S
.

When
S
approach the bifurcation surface
\mathcal{B}
, we have
\xi^{a}\to 0
. However, note
s^{a}\xi_{a}
is finite,
s^{a}
must be divergent when
S\to\mathcal{B}
. To show the divergence, we choose another two finite null vector field
k^{a}
and
l^{a}
on the cross-section
S
near the bifurcation surface, which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} k^{a}l_{a}=&-1,\quad\quad k_{a}k^{a}=0,\quad\quad l_{a}l^{a}=0\,,\\
&k^{a}=C\xi^{a},\quad\quad l^{a}=C^{-1}s^{a}\,,\end{aligned}

(58)

in which
C
is a scalar field on the cross section
S
. Since
k^{a}
and
l^{a}
are finite vectors on the bifurcation surface, we have
C\to\infty
when
S\to\mathcal{B}
. Denote
\bar{z}_{i}^{a}=\{k^{a},l^{a},w_{i}^{a}\}
. Since we assume that
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}
is smooth near the bifurcation surface
\mathcal{B}
,
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\bar{z}_{1}^{a}\cdots\bar{z}_{k}^{a}
will be finite on
\mathcal{B}
. Since

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\xi}X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}=0\,,\end{aligned}

(59)

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a})=0\,,

(60)

which implies that
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a}
is invariance along the Killing vector
\xi^{a}
. When the cross section
S
is not the bifurcation surface
\mathcal{B}
,
z^{a}
would be a finite vector. Then, we have
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a}
is finite on whole future horizon
\mathcal{H}
. From Eq. (
58
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\bar{z}_{1}^{a}\cdots\bar{z}_{k}^{a}=C^{m-n}X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\,z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a}\,,\end{aligned}

(61)

in which
m
is the number of
\xi^{a}
and
n
is the number of
s^{a}
. Since
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}
is a smooth tensor near the bifurcation surface,
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\bar{z}_{1}^{a}\cdots\bar{z}_{k}^{a}
should be finite when
C
approaches
\mathcal{B}
. However, from Eq. (
61
), we can see that when
m>n
, if
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\,z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a}
is finite,
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\bar{z}_{1}^{a}\cdots\bar{z}_{k}^{a}
would be divergent, which is in contradiction with the assumption that
X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\bar{z}_{1}^{a}
is smooth near the bifurcation surface. Therefore, when
m>n
, we must have

\displaystyle X_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}\,z_{1}^{a}\cdots z_{k}^{a}=0\,.

(62)

As we desired to show.
\Box

Since we assume that
E_{R}^{abcd}
is smooth near horizon,
\nabla_{a}E_{R}^{abcd}
is also smooth near the horizon (including bifurcation surface). Using
Lemma 2
, Eq. (
55
) can reduce to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{S}\left[\bar{\delta}\bm{Q}_{\xi}-\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}(\phi,\delta\phi)\right]\\
=&\delta\int_{S}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}\left[E_{F}^{ab}A^{c}\xi_{c}-E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{[c}\xi_{d]}\right]-\int_{S}\xi\cdot\left[\bm{\Theta}^{\text{e.m}}(\phi,\delta\bm{A})+\bm{\Theta}^{\text{grav}}(\phi,\delta g)\right]\,.\end{aligned}

(63)

For the first term of the left side in the above equation, considering that
\phi_{H}=-A_{a}\xi^{a}|_{\mathcal{H}}
is a constant on horizon, we have

\displaystyle\int_{S}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{F}^{ab}A^{c}\xi_{c}=-\Phi_{H}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{F}^{ab}\,.

(64)

Using the on-shell condition
\nabla_{a}E_{F}^{ab}=0
, it is not hard to get

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{B}}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{F}^{ab}=-\int_{\mathcal{\infty}}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{F}^{ab}=Q\,.

(65)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \delta\int_{S}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{F}^{ab}A^{c}\xi_{c}=\delta(\Phi_{H}Q)\,.\end{aligned}

(66)

For the third term of Eq.(
63
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{S}\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}^{\text{e.m.}}(\phi,\delta\bm{A})&=2\int_{S}\xi^{c}\bm{\epsilon}_{aca_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{F}^{ab}\delta A_{b}\\
&=2\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\xi^{c}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ac}E_{F}^{ab}\delta A_{b}\,.\end{aligned}

Using

, we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{S}\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}^{\text{e.m.}}(\phi,\delta\bm{A})&=2\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\xi_{a}E_{F}^{ab}\delta A_{b}=2\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\xi_{a}E_{F}^{ab}s_{b}\xi^{c}\delta A_{c}\\
&=\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ab}E_{F}^{ab}\xi^{c}\delta A_{c}=Q\delta\Phi_{H}\,.\end{aligned}

(68)

In the following, we are going to evaluate the gravitational part. Since we choose the gauge such that
\xi^{a}
and
\mathcal{H}
is fixed in the variation, we can use the Gaussian null coordinates

\displaystyle ds^{2}=2(dr-r\alpha dv-r\beta_{i}d\theta^{i})dv+\gamma_{ij}d\theta^{i}d\theta^{j}\,,

in which

,

and

and
\theta^{i}
. The horizon

is determined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \xi^{a}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right)^{a}\,,\quad s^{a}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{a}\,.\end{aligned}

(70)

Note that the gauge choice which fixes the Gaussian null coordinates is the same as the gauge choice to fix
\xi^{a}
and
\mathcal{H}
. In this gauge choice, only

,

and

are dependent on the parameter
\lambda
. Based on the above coordinates, on the horizon

, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\xi_{b}=\kappa\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ab}-\beta_{i}\xi_{[a}(d\theta^{i})_{b]}\,,

(71)

in which

is the surface gravity of the Killing horizon
\mathcal{H}
. For the second term of Eq. (
63
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{S}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{[c}\xi_{d]}&=\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ab}E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{[c}\xi_{d]}\\
&=\kappa\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ab}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{cd}E_{R}^{abcd}+\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\beta_{i}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ab}\xi_{c}(d\theta^{i})_{d}E_{R}^{abcd}\,.\end{aligned}

(72)

Using
Lemma 2
and considering that

is a tangent vector of

, the second term of the above expression vanishes. Thus we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{S}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{[c}\xi_{d]}=\kappa\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{ab}\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{cd}E_{R}^{abcd}=-\frac{\kappa S}{2\pi}\,.\end{aligned}

Therefore, Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\delta\int_{S}\bm{\epsilon}_{aba_{3}\cdots a_{n}}E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{[c}\xi_{d]}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\delta(\kappa S)\,.\end{aligned}

For the last term of Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{S}\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}^{\text{grav}}(\phi,\delta g)&=2\int_{S}\xi^{e}\bm{\epsilon}_{ce\cdots a_{n}}\left(\nabla_{d}E_{R}^{acbd}\delta g_{ab}+E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{b}\delta g_{ad}\right)\\
&=-2\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}\left(\xi_{c}\nabla_{d}E_{R}^{acbd}\delta g_{ab}+\xi_{c}E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{b}\delta g_{ad}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(75)

For the line element (

, in which

For the last term of Eq. (
75

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \xi_{c}E_{R}^{abcd}\nabla_{b}\delta g_{ad}=-E_{R}^{\mu\sigma\rho r}\delta g_{\mu\rho;\sigma}\,.\end{aligned}

(76)

Using the line element (

),
Lemma 2
can be presented as: if

is not zero on horizon, the number of “

” in the index must be larger than number of “

”. Therefore, the nonvanishing contributions in Eq. (

\displaystyle\delta g_{vv;r}=-2\delta\alpha=-2\delta\kappa\,.

(77)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{S}\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}^{\text{grav}}(\phi,\delta g)&=-4\delta\kappa\int_{S}\tilde{\bm{\epsilon}}E_{R}^{vrvr}=\frac{1}{2\pi}S\delta\kappa\,.\end{aligned}

(78)

Summing the above results, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \delta M=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\delta S+\Omega_{H}^{(\mu)}\delta J_{(\mu)}+\Phi_{H}\delta Q\,.\end{aligned}

(79)

Therefore, we derived the first law of black holes in the gravitational electromagnetic system and the expression is the same as the Einstein-Maxwell theory.

4
Conclusion

The first law of black hole in a diffeomorphism covariant theory is generally derived by Iyer and Wald
Iyer:1994ys
. However, their results bases on the requirement that all dynamical fields be smooth near the future Killing horizon as well as the bifurcation surface, and consequently the “potential-charge” term does not appear in their result for the gravitational electromagnetic system. In this paper, we extended their discussion into the gravitational electromagnetic system without the requirement that the vector potential
\bm{A}
is smooth near the horizon since it is not a real physical quantity.

Firstly, we calculated the Noether charge and variational identity of the gravitational electromagnetic theory with the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(g_{ab},R_{abcd},F_{ab})
. Then, using these results, we derived the thermodynamic first law of the asymptotically flat stationary axisymmetric black holes. In contrast to the earlier discussion by Iyer and Wald, we only require that the electromagnetic strength
F_{ab}
and metric
g_{ab}
be smooth near the future horizon (including the bifurcation surface), without making any constraints for the vector potential
\bm{A}
. Under the above conditions, we obtain the first law of thermodynamics for the mass, angular momentum, and charge variation of the black hole. The result is the same as the expression of the first law in Einstein-Maxwell theory. Our investigation shows that the first law of black hole thermodynamics is also universal in an effective theory that takes into account the quantum corrections in Einstein-Maxwell theory, and Wald entropy is still the best choice to describe the entropy of a steady black hole.

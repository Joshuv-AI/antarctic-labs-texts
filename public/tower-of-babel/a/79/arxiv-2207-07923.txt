Composite Fermion Mass
K. A. Villegas Rosales, P. T. Madathil, Y. J. Chung, L. N. Pfeiffer, K. W. West, K. W. Baldwin, M. Shayegan

Abstract
Composite fermions (CFs), exotic quasi-particles formed by pairing an electron and an even number of magnetic flux quanta emerge at high magnetic fields in an interacting electron system, and can explain phenomena such as the fractional quantum Hall state (FQHS) and other many-body phases. CFs possess an effective mass ( m C ​ F m_{CF} ) whose magnitude is inversely related to the most fundamental property of a FQHS, namely its energy gap. We present here experimental measurements of m C ​ F m_{CF} in ultra-high quality two-dimensional electron systems confined to GaAs quantum wells of varying thickness. An advantage of measuring m C ​ F m_{CF} over gap measurements is that mass values are insensitive to disorder and are therefore ideal for comparison with theoretical calculations, especially for high-order FQHS. Our data reveal that m C ​ F m_{CF} increases with increasing well width, reflecting a decrease in the energy gap as the electron layer becomes thicker and the in-plane Coulomb energy softens. Comparing our measured masses with available theoretical results, we find significant quantitative discrepancies, highlighting that more rigorous and accurate calculations are needed to explain the experimental data.

A very fundamental parameter characterizing CFs is their effective mass (

\Lambda

\nu=p/(2p+1)

\tilde{w}
) and finite separation between LL energies,
m_{CF}
can be larger than its ideal value as both the softening of the in-plane Coulomb interaction and the mixing between the LLs can reduce the FQHS energy gaps and thus increase
m_{CF}

Jain.CFbook.2007
;
Park.Activation.1999
;
Morf.PRB.2002

m_{CF}
should be immune to small disorder
Du.PRL.1994

measurements, via a Dingle analysis of the amplitude of the FQHS resistance oscillations, in extremely low-disorder samples as a function of

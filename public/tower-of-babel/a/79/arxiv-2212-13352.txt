Contact non-squeezing and orderability via the shape invariant
Dylan Cant

Abstract
. We prove a contact non-squeezing result for a class of embeddings between starshaped domains in the contactization of the symplectization of the unit cotangent bundle of certain manifolds. The class of embeddings includes embeddings which are not isotopic to the identity. This yields a new proof that there is no positive loop of contactomorphisms in the unit cotangent bundles under consideration. The proof uses the shape invariant introduced by Sikorav and Eliashberg.

2.1.
Positive loops as a squeezing tool

Suppose that
Y
is non-orderable. Let
\varphi_{t}
be a positive loop of contactomorphisms, considered as a positive loop of equivariant symplectomorphisms of
(SY,\lambda)
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
\varphi_{0}=1

Positivity means that
\dot{\varphi}_{t}=X_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}

is the Hamiltonian vector field for a positive homogeneous function

on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times SY

Lemma 2

[

]
)
.

For

\Phi:(t,z)\mapsto(t,e^{h_{t}(z)}\varphi_{t}(z))
is a contact embedding of
Q=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times SY
into the interior of starshaped domain
\left\{(t,z):H_{t}(z)\leq 1\right\}
.
\square

We will use this to prove Lemma
1
.

)

Let
\varphi_{t}

in

2.2.
Review of the shape invariant

Consider
T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n+1}\simeq\mathbb{T}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
-valued spatial coordinates
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
and momentum coordinates
y_{0},\dots,y_{n}
. Let
\lambda=\sum y_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{i}
be the canonical one-form. Say that a Lagrangian embedding
e:\mathbb{T}^{n+1}\to T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n+1}
is
homologically standard
provided
e^{*}\mathrm{pr}^{*}=\mathrm{id}
on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^{n+1})
, where
\mathrm{pr}
is the projection.

Let
U\subset T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n+1}
. Following
[
Sik89
]
,
[
Eli91
]
,
[
Sik91
]
,
[
EP00
]
, define:

\mathrm{Shape}(U):=\left\{[e^{*}\lambda]:e\text{ is homologically standard and $e(\mathbb{T}^{n+1})\subset U$}\right\}\subset H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^{n+1}).

This uses the fact that
e^{*}\lambda
is a closed one-form when
e
is Lagrangian.

Given two subsets
U_{1},U_{2}
of
T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n+1}
, a map
\varphi:U_{1}\to U_{2}
is
homologically standard
provided
\varphi^{*}\mathrm{pr}^{*}=\mathrm{pr}^{*}
on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^{n+1})
. We have the following result for
exact
symplectic embeddings, i.e., those satisfying
\varphi^{*}\lambda=\lambda+\mathrm{d}f
.

Lemma 3
.

If
\varphi:U_{1}\to U_{2}
is a homologically standard and exact symplectic embedding, then
\mathrm{Shape}(U_{1})\subset\mathrm{Shape}(U_{2})
.

Proof
.

Let
e:\mathbb{T}^{n+1}\to U_{1}
be homologically standard. Then
\varphi\circ e
is homologically standard in
U_{2}
, and hence
[e^{*}\lambda]
is in the shape of
U_{2}
. Since
e
was arbitrary, we conclude the desired result.

The constant
1

\sum a_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{i}
form a canonical basis
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^{n+1})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The fundamental theorem about shapes is the following result, originally due to
[
Sik89
]
:

Proposition 4
.

Let
U=\mathbb{T}^{n+1}\times W
where
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then, using the standard basis for
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^{n+1})
, the shape of
U
is equal to
W
.

The proof is a straightforward application of the fact that every closed exact Lagrangian in the cotangent bundle intersects the zero section. See
[
MS12
, Chapter 9]
and the references therein for more details.

Proof of Theorem

\Omega(R)\to\Omega(r)

In order to apply the shape invariant, we will embed

\begin{aligned} x_{0}=t\text{ and }x_{i}&=q_{i}\\

U(r)

\Omega(r)

\Phi:\Omega(R)\to\Omega(r)

\varphi:U(R)\to U(r)
, i.e.,
\varphi(x,e^{s}y)=e^{s}\varphi(x,y)
, lifting
\Phi

If
\Phi

Let
\Phi^{*}([\beta])=[\beta]+k([\beta])\mathrm{d}t

\varphi^{*}[\mathrm{d}x_{0}]=[\mathrm{d}x_{0}]\text{ and }\varphi^{*}[\mathrm{d}x_{i}]=[\mathrm{d}x_{i}]+k_{i}[\mathrm{d}x_{0}].

Consider the canonical transformation:

\delta(x,y)=(x_{0},x_{1}-k_{1}x_{0},\dots,x_{n}-k_{1}x_{0},y_{0}+\sum k_{i}y_{i},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}).

Therefore
\delta\circ\varphi

, and Lemma

It is clear that

\displaystyle W(R)

2.4.
Proof of Theorem
2

Let
Y
be the unit cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times SY
is a star-shaped domain. Consider
SY\subset T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n}
as the complement of the zero section. Without loss of generality, suppose
\Omega\subset\left\{\min(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})>-k\right\}
for some integer
k>0
. As mentioned in §
1
, it suffices to construct a contact embedding
\Omega\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, since
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
has a contact embedding into a Darboux chart by
[
EKP06
, Corollary 1.25]
.

Using the
t,q,p
coordinate system, consider the map
\Omega\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by:

\varphi:(t,q_{1},\dots,q_{n},p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\mapsto(t+k\sum q_{i},f(p_{1})e^{2\pi iq_{1}},\dots,f(p_{n})e^{2\pi iq_{n}}),

where
f(p)=\pi^{-1/2}(p+k)^{1/2}
(this is why we require
\min(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})>-k
). Then
\varphi
is a strict contact embedding. It is easily seen to be injective. We compute:

\varphi^{*}(\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\mathrm{d}y_{i}-y_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{i})=\mathrm{d}t+k\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}q_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi f(p_{i})^{2}\mathrm{d}q_{i}=\mathrm{d}t-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\mathrm{d}q_{i},

as desired. This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

2.5.
Generalization to some other cotangent bundles

Let
\rho
be a fiberwise metric so that the nowhere zero one-form
\beta
satisfies
\rho(\beta)=1
. Let
\Omega(r)=\left\{0<\rho(z)\leq r^{2}\right\}
be considered as starshaped domain in
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times SY
, where
Y
is the unit cotangent bundle of
B
. In search of a contradiction, suppose there is a generalized squeezing
\Phi:\Omega(R)\to\Omega(r)
for
r<R
.

Consider the embedding:

(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times T^{*}B\mapsto(t,1,-z)\in T^{*}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times B)=T^{*}\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times T^{*}B.

Writing
s
for the fiber coordinate on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, so the canonical form is
s\mathrm{d}t
, we conclude that this defines a contact-type hypersurface inside of
T^{*}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times B)
whose induced contact form equals
\mathrm{d}t-\lambda
.

Extending via the Liouville flow, the contact embedding
\Phi:\Omega(R)\to\Omega(r)
lifts to an equivariant exact symplectomorphism
\varphi:U(R)\to U(r)
, where:

U(r):=\mathbb{R}_{+}\Omega(r)=\left\{(t,s,z):0<\rho(z)/s^{2}\leq r^{2}\text{ and }s<0\right\},

and similarly for
U(R)
. Since
\Phi
is assumed to be a generalizing squeezing, it is easy to see that
\varphi^{*}[\mathrm{d}t]=[\mathrm{d}t]
and
\varphi^{*}[\beta]=[\beta]+k[\mathrm{d}t]
.

Consider the Lagrangian graph
L_{a,b}=a\mathrm{d}t+b\beta
for
a>0
. It is clear that
L_{a,b}
lies in the locus where
s=a
and
\rho=b^{2}
, and so
L_{a,b}\in U(R)
if and only if
0<b^{2}/a^{2}\leq R^{2}
.

Let
\Lambda
denote the canonical form on
T^{*}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times B)
. By definition,
L_{a,b}^{*}\Lambda=a\mathrm{d}t+b\beta
. Consider the symplectomorphism
f_{a,b}:T^{*}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times B)\to T^{*}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times B)
which acts on one-forms by subtracting
(a-kb)\mathrm{d}t+b\beta
. It is clear that
f_{a,b}^{*}\Lambda=\Lambda-(a-kb)\mathrm{d}t-b\beta
, and hence:

[(f_{a,b}\circ\varphi\circ L_{a,b})^{*}\Lambda]=[L_{a,b}^{*}\Lambda]-a[\mathrm{d}t]-b[\beta]=0.

Consequently,
f_{a,b}\circ\varphi\circ L_{a,b}
is a compact exact Lagrangian, and
hence intersects the zero section
. In particular,
\varphi\circ L_{a,b}
intersects the graph
f_{a,b}^{-1}(0_{B})
, i.e.,
(a-kb)\mathrm{d}t+b\beta
. Thus
\rho/s^{2}
attains the value
b^{2}/(a-kb)^{2}
at some point on
\varphi(L_{a,b})
.

On the other hand,
\varphi(L_{a,b})
is contained in
U(r)
. Since
L_{a,b}\in U(R)
one has:

a>0\text{ and }b^{2}/a^{2}\leq R^{2}\implies b^{2}/(a-kb)^{2}\leq r^{2}

For
k\geq 0
, pick
a=1
and
b=\min\left\{1/k,R\right\}
to conclude a contradiction. For
k<0
, pick
b=\max\left\{1/k,-R\right\}
, and similarly conclude a contradiction. This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.

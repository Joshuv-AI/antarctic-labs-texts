1.
Introduction

The study of the norm of random matrices has increased significantly over the years, and bounding the operator norm has been proved one central topic in the field
[
1
,
6
,
3
]
. Particularly, several applications coming from statistics require a precise sharp control on the deviations of the empirical covariance problem
[
7
,
9
,
13
]
. For instance, it is well-known
[
11
]
that an i.i.d sample
X_{1},...,X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of isotropic Gaussian random vectors satisfies the following deviation

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{1}X_{1}^{T}\right\|\lesssim\frac{d}{n}\vee\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

Much less is known, however, when the identically distributed condition is removed and we only require independence. Our contribution comes precisely in this direction. In particular, we improve the results in
[
4
]
and we shed light on the
4
th-moment parameter and its graph interpretation that was before unclear.

Let
X
be a random
d\times n
Gaussian matrix with independent entries
X_{ij}=b_{ij}g_{ij}
, where
b_{ij}\geq 0
and
\{g_{ij}:i\in[d],j\in[n]\}
are independent standard Gaussian random variables
N(0,1)
. Our goal is to bound the quantity

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}\right\|,

where
X_{j}=Xe_{j}
is the
j
th column of
X
. One of the first dimension-free results improving bound (
1
) was given in the i.i.d setting in
[
7
]
. Their result states that whenever
Y_{1},...,Y_{n}
are i.i.d Gaussian random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|YY^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}YY^{T}\|\asymp\|\Sigma\|\left(\sqrt{n\text{rk}(\Sigma)},\text{rk}(\Sigma)\right),

where

\displaystyle\text{rk}(\Sigma)=\frac{\text{tr}(\Sigma)}{\|\Sigma\|}

is the effective rank of the covariance matrix
\Sigma=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}Y_{1}Y_{1}^{T}
. The dependency on the sample size
n
is sharp in all i.i.d cases, but much less is evident when the vectors are not identically distributed.

In an orthogonal direction, Bandeira and van Handel
[
3
]
proved that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|\lesssim\sigma_{C}+\sigma_{R}+C\sigma_{*}\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)},

where
\sigma_{C}
is the maximum Euclidean norm of columns of
B=(b_{ij})
,
\sigma_{R}
is the maximum Euclidean norm of rows of
B
and
\sigma_{*}
is the maximum entry of
B
, that is,

\displaystyle\sigma_{C}^{2}=\max_{j\in[n]}\sum_{i\in[d]}b_{ij}^{2};

\displaystyle\sigma_{R}^{2}=\max_{i\in[d]}\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2};

\displaystyle\sigma_{*}=\max_{(i,j)\in[d]\times[n]}|b_{ij}|.

To prove such a result, they compared the moments
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{tr}(XX^{T})^{p}
to the moments of a standard Gaussian matrix
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{tr}(GG^{T})^{p}
with reduced dimensions. This comparison method turned out to be also efficient to prove the estimations for the covariance problem as well. In
[
4
]
, T. Cai, Han and Zhang applied these techniques to
XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}
and they proved that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim\sigma_{C}\sigma_{R}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C(\sigma_{C}\sigma_{*}+\sigma_{R}\sigma_{*})\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+C^{\prime}\sigma_{*}^{2}\log(n\wedge d).

The leading term
\sigma_{C}\sigma_{R}+\sigma_{C}^{2}
is not always sharp. Indeed, studying the case
b_{ij}=b_{j}
, that is, the rows are i.i.d, the authors of
[
4
]
proved that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\asymp\sqrt{d\sum_{j}b_{j}^{4}}+d\max_{j}b_{j}^{2}=\sqrt{d\sum_{j}b_{j}^{4}}+\sigma_{C}^{2}.

Our main contribution shed light on this
4
th-moment parameter and how it appears from the moment method.

We begin our results for the operator norm. Define the parameters:

\displaystyle\bullet\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}^{2}=\max_{i,l:i\neq l}\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}b_{lj}^{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\bar{\sigma}_{\infty}^{2}=\max_{i\in[d]}\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{4};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{\infty}^{2}=\max_{i\in[d]}\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{l:l\neq i}b_{ij}^{2}b_{lj}^{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\beta_{\infty}=\frac{\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\sigma_{C}}{\sigma_{\infty}\sigma_{*}}.

Notice in particular that
\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\leq\bar{\sigma}_{\infty}
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a
d\times n
Gaussian matrix with independent entries such that
X_{ij}=b_{ij}g_{ij}
where
\{g_{ij}:(i,j)\in[d]\times[n]\}
are i.i.d standard Gaussian r.v. Then, if
\beta_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\sum_{j\in[n]}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\{2\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\sigma_{*}\left(\sigma_{C}+\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}\right)\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+C^{2}(\varepsilon)\sigma_{*}^{2}\log(n\wedge d)\right\},

for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/2
. Otherwise,
\beta_{\infty}>1
and we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\{\frac{2\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\sigma_{C}}{\sigma_{*}}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\left(\sigma_{C}\sigma_{*}+\bar{\sigma}_{\infty}\right)\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+C^{2}(\varepsilon)\sigma_{*}^{2}\log(n\wedge d)\right\}.

The constant
C(\varepsilon)
is

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)=\frac{C(1+\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{\log(1+\varepsilon)}},

where
C
is a universal constant.

Theorem
1.1
improves Theorem 2.1 from
[
4
]
. When
\beta_{\infty}\leq 1
, we get the sharp constant
2\sigma_{\infty}
on the right-hand side. Moreover, if
\beta_{\infty}=O(1)
, our result still gives the correct order of magnitude.

Since the method of proof uses the moment method, we can extend Theorem
1.1
to estimate Schatten norms. Recall that the
p
-Schatten norm is defined by

\displaystyle\|A\|_{S_{p}}^{p}=\text{Tr}(A)^{p},

for a positive matrix
A
. It is also the same as the
p
-norm of the singular values of
A
. This time, we define the more involved parameters

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{p}=\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{l\in[d]}b_{ij}^{2}b_{lj}^{2}\right]^{p/2}\right\}^{1/p};

\displaystyle\bullet\overline{\sigma}_{p}=\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{4}\right]^{p/2}\right\}^{1/p};

\displaystyle\bullet b_{p}=\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\max_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2p}\right\}^{1/(2p)};

\displaystyle\bullet\beta_{p}=\frac{\bar{\sigma}_{p}\sigma_{C}}{\sigma_{p}b_{p}}.

Our second main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a
d\times n
Gaussian matrix with independent entries such that
X_{ij}=b_{ij}g_{ij}
where
\{g_{ij}:(i,j)\in[d]\times[n]\}
are i.i.d standard Gaussian r.v. Then, if
\beta_{p}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\emph{Tr}[XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}]^{p})^{1/p}\leq d^{1/p}\left\{2\sigma_{p}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C\sqrt{p}\left(\sigma_{C}\sigma_{*}+\frac{\sigma_{p}\sigma_{*}}{\sigma_{C}}\right)+C^{\prime}pb_{p}^{2}\right\}.

Otherwise,
\beta_{p}>1
and

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\emph{Tr}[XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}]^{p})^{1/p}\leq d^{1/p}\left\{\frac{2\overline{\sigma}_{p}\sigma_{C}}{\sigma_{*}}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C\sqrt{p}\left(\sigma_{C}\sigma_{*}+\overline{\sigma}_{p}\right)+C^{\prime}pb_{p}^{2}\right\}.

1.1.
Main ideas of the proof

The proof relies on the moment method and a careful analysis of paths. We will first remove the diagonal
\text{Diag}(XX^{T})
, so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta XX^{T}\|+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\text{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|,

where
\Delta XX^{T}
is the matrix of off-diagonal elements of
XX^{T}
. It turns out that the contribution of the diagonal is sufficiently small and can be added as an error factor (see Theorem
2.5
). On the other hand, the combinatorics of
\Delta XX^{T}
are much easier to deal with. In particular, all paths in the complete bipartite graph over
[d]\sqcup[n]
have all right vertices with at least two neighbors.

We then proceed with the moment method. Note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|_{S_{p}}\leq d^{1/p}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|,

for any symmetric
d\times d
matrix. Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|\leq(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|Y\|_{S_{p}}^{p})^{1/p},

by Jensen’s Inequality. We apply this for
Y=\Delta XX^{T}
and our goal is to obtain a comparison lemma such as

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta XX^{T})^{p}\leq\kappa\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta GG^{T})^{p},

where
\kappa>0
and
G
is a Gaussian matrix with reduced dimensions as in
[
3
]
.

Outline of the paper

2
, we will provide the main proofs of theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. In section

, we will give examples to illustrate the improvement from the previous results. Finally, in section
4

a\lesssim b
or
a=O(b)
if there exists an absolute constant
C
such that
a\leq Cb
. We also denote it as
b\gtrsim a
. If
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
hold, we denote
a\asymp b
. We write
a\wedge b=\min(a,b)
and
a\vee b=\max(a,b)
. We denote
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}

A\sqcup B

A

C,c,C^{\prime},\ldots

2.
Proofs

2.1.
Preliminaries

We begin by recalling the Gaussian integration by parts lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g\sim N(0,1)
be a standard Gaussian r.v. and
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}gf(g)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}f^{\prime}(g).

The authors of
[
4
]
deduced from this lemma a simple property of the joint moments of
g
and
g^{2}-1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a_{n,m}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}g^{n}(g^{2}-1)^{m}
, where
g\sim N(0,1)
. Then
a_{n,m}\geq 0
and
a_{n,m}=0
if and only if
n
is odd or
(n,m)=(0,1)
.

We also recall the sharp bound on the operator norm for a standard Gaussian matrix shown in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a
d\times n
Gaussian matrix with i.i.d standard Gaussian r.v. entries. Then, for any
p\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|GG^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}GG^{T}\|^{p})^{1/p}\leq 2\sqrt{dn}+d+4\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{n})+2p.

Note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\text{Diag}(GG^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}GG^{T}\|^{p}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\max_{i\in[d]}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}(g^{2}_{ij}-1)\right)^{p}.

Bernstein’s Inequality
[
5
]
implies then that

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\text{Diag}(GG^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}GG^{T}\|^{p})^{1/p}\lesssim\sqrt{pn}+p.

Consequently, we end this subsection with a corollary for the off-diagonal part.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
G
be a
d\times n
Gaussian matrix with i.i.d standard Gaussian entries. Then, for any
p\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta(GG^{T})\|^{p})^{1/p}\leq 2\sqrt{dn}+d+C\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{n})+C^{\prime}p.

2.2.
The diagonal part

In this section, the main result is the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any
p\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\emph{Tr}[\emph{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}]^{p}\right)^{1/p}\asymp\sqrt{p}\overline{\sigma}_{p}+pb_{p}^{2}.

Proof.

For the upper bound, note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\text{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}=\sum_{i\in[d]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}(g_{ij}^{2}-1)\right)^{p}.

Since
g_{ij}^{2}-1
are independent, centered, and subexponential, we can use Bernstein’s Inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}(g_{ij}^{2}-1)\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{a^{2}},\frac{t}{b}\right\}\right),

where

\displaystyle a=\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{4};

\displaystyle b=\max_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2},

hence

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}(g_{ij}^{2}-1)\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{4}\right)^{1/2}+p\max_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}.

We then have

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\text{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\sum_{i\in[d]}\left(\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{4}\right)^{1/2}+p\max_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}.\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{p}\overline{\sigma}_{p}+pb_{p}^{2},

where the last inequality follows by the triangle inequality.

For the lower bound, let
j_{i}
be the index such that

\displaystyle\max_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}=b_{ij_{i}}.

Since the joint moments of
g
and
g^{2}-1
are always positive, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\text{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right]^{1/p}\geq\left[\sum_{i\in[d]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}b_{ij_{i}}^{2p}(g_{ij_{i}}^{2}-1)^{p}\right]^{1/p}.

Now, the estimate

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(g^{2}-1)^{p}\right)^{1/p}\gtrsim p,

that follows the lower bound on the double factorial (see Lemma 5.2 in
[
4
]
) implies that

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\text{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right]^{1/p}\gtrsim p\left(\sum_{i\in[d]}\max_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2p}\right)^{1/p}=pb_{p}^{2}.

On the other hand, Theorem 6 in
[
12
]
yields that
Z=\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}(g_{ij}^{2}-1)
satisfies

\displaystyle\exp(-Ct^{2}/a)\lesssim\mathbb{P}(Z\geq t);

\displaystyle\exp(-Ct^{2}/a)\lesssim\mathbb{P}(Z\leq-t),

for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore, its moments are lower bounded by the ones of the Gaussian
h\sim N(0,a)
, hence

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}|Z|^{p})^{1/p}\gtrsim\sqrt{ap},

so we conclude that

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\text{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right]^{1/p}\gtrsim\sqrt{p}\overline{\sigma}_{p}.

∎

2.3.
The off-diagonal part

The proof of the bounds for the off-diagonal part follows the moment method. First, we open the trace so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta XX^{T})^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in[d]^{p}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}(XX^{T})_{u_{k}u_{k+1}}\mathbf{1}_{u_{k}\neq u_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in[d]^{p}}\sum_{v\in[n]^{p}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}X_{u_{k}v_{k}}X_{u_{k+1}v_{k}}\mathbf{1}_{u_{k}\neq u_{k+1}},

where
u_{p+1}:=u_{1}
. We view the path
u_{1}\to v_{1}\to u_{2}\to\cdots\to u_{p}\to v_{p}\to u_{1}
as a cycle in the complete bipartite graph over
[d]^{(l)}\sqcup[n]^{(r)}
, where
(l)
and
(r)
indicate left and right vertices (we will remove the indexes if the context is clear). For a path
(u,v)
, we define its shape
s(u,v)
as relabelling its vertices in order of appearance. For instance, the path

\displaystyle 3\to 2^{\prime}\to 4\to 1^{\prime}\to 3\to 1^{\prime}\to 4\to 5^{\prime}\to 3

has shape

\displaystyle 1\to 1^{\prime}\to 2\to 2^{\prime}\to 1\to 2^{\prime}\to 2\to 3^{\prime}\to 1.

Note that each edge
u_{k}v_{k}
and
u_{k+1}v_{k}
must appear at least twice in the path
(u,v)
, by the independence of the Gaussian r.v. and symmetry. Call the shapes that satisfy this
even
. Let then
\mathcal{S}
be the set of even shapes
s=(u,v)
such that
u_{k}\neq u_{k+1}
for all
k=1,...,p
. Moreover, the product

(2)

\displaystyle L(s):=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}g_{u_{k}v_{k}}g_{u_{k+1}v_{k}}

only depends on the shape of
(u,v)
, therefore we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta XX^{T})^{p}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}L(s)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in[d]^{p}\times[n]^{p}\\
s(u,v)=s\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}b_{u_{k}v_{k}}b_{u_{k+1}v_{k}}.

Let
(m_{1},m_{2})=(m_{1}(s),m_{2}(s))
be the quantity of right and left vertices that appear in the shape
s
. The key proposition to prove Theorem
1.1
is to bound

(3)

\displaystyle W(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in[d]^{p}\times[n]^{p}\\
s(u,v)=s\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}b_{u_{k}v_{k}}b_{u_{k+1}v_{k}}

according to the number of vertices visited by the path.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume
\sigma_{*}=1
. If
\beta_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle W(s)\leq\left[d\left(\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}\right)^{2m_{1}}\sigma_{C}^{2(m_{2}-1)}\right]\wedge\left[n\left(\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}\right)^{2(m_{1}-1)}\sigma_{C}^{2m_{2}}\right].

Otherwise,
\beta_{\infty}>1
and we have

\displaystyle W(s)\leq\left[d\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}^{2m_{1}}\sigma_{C}^{2(m_{2}-1)}\right]\wedge\left[n\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}^{2(m_{1}-1)}\sigma_{C}^{2m_{2}}\right].

Let us prove Theorem
1.1
given Proposition
2.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume
\sigma_{*}=1
(by homogeneity) and
\beta_{\infty}\leq 1
. Let

\displaystyle a:=\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}};

\displaystyle b:=\sigma_{C}.

Then, using the first bound on Proposition
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta XX^{T})^{p}\leq d\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}L(s)\left(\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}\right)^{2m_{1}}\sigma_{C}^{2(m_{2}-1)}.

On the other hand, for a standard Gaussian
r_{2}\times r_{1}
matrix
G
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta GG^{T})^{p}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}L(s)\frac{r_{1}!}{(r_{1}-m_{1})!}\frac{r_{2}!}{(r_{2}-m_{2})!},

for any
r_{1},r_{2}>p/2
(see
[
4
]
). In particular, if
r_{1}=\lceil a^{2}\rceil+p/2
and
r_{2}=\lceil b^{2}\rceil+p/2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{r_{1}!}{(r_{1}-m_{1})!}\geq r_{1}\cdots(r_{1}-m_{1}+1)^{m_{1}}\geq a^{2m_{1}},

and

\displaystyle\frac{r_{2}!}{(r_{2}-m_{2})!}\geq r_{2}b^{2(m_{2}-1)}.

Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta XX^{T})^{p}\leq\frac{d}{r_{2}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\Delta GG^{T})^{p}\leq d\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta GG^{T}\|^{p}.

Now we estimate the latter by Corollary
2.4
so that

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta GG^{T}\|^{p})^{1/p}\leq 2\sqrt{r_{1}r_{2}}+r_{2}+C\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{r_{1}}+\sqrt{r_{2}})+C^{\prime}p.

Together with Theorem
2.5
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\leq d^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\{2\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C\sqrt{p}\left(\sigma_{C}+\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}+\bar{\sigma}_{p}\right)+Cpb_{p}^{2}\right\}.

Choose
p=\lceil\alpha\log d\rceil
. Since
\beta_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have that

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{p}\leq d^{\frac{1}{p}}\bar{\sigma}_{\infty}\leq d^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}.

Moreover,
b_{p}\leq d^{\frac{1}{2p}}b_{\infty}
, thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\leq e^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left\{2\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+Ce^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sqrt{\alpha\log d}\left(\sigma_{C}+\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}\right)+C\alpha e^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\log d\right\}.

Finally, set
1+\varepsilon=e^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, hence

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{\log(1+\varepsilon)},

and we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\{2\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\log d}\left(\sigma_{C}+\frac{\sigma_{\infty}}{\sigma_{C}}\right)+C^{2}(\varepsilon)\log d\right\}.

This gives the upper bound with
\log d
. The second bound in Proposition
2.6
yields the general bound for
\beta_{\infty}\leq 1
. The case

, we deduce

\displaystyle W(G)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{h=1}^{H}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in[d]^{I_{h}}_{\neq}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[n]^{J_{h}}_{\neq}\end{subarray}}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{w_{u_{h}}j}^{(u_{h}v_{h})}\prod_{a\in N(v_{h})\setminus\{u_{h}\}}\left(\sum_{l\neq w_{u_{h}}}b_{lj}^{(av_{h})}\right)\right]^{q_{h}}\prod_{e=ab\in E(G_{h})}b_{w_{a}t_{b}}^{(ab)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{h}}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{e=uv\in E(G):u\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{(e)}\prod_{a\in N(v)\setminus\{u\}}\left(\sum_{l\neq i}b_{lj}^{(av)}\right)\right]^{p_{v}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{e}}}.

The conclusion of the lemma follows by the renormalization
\alpha_{uv}\leftarrow p_{v}\alpha_{uv}
and splitting the product over
v\in\mathcal{L}
and
v\notin\mathcal{L}
.
∎

Now we can prove Proposition
2.7
.

Proof of Proposition
2.7
.

Let
|k|=\sum_{v}k_{v}=2p
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(G)\cap[m_{1}]
. By Lemma
2.8
and Corollary
2.10
with
p_{v}=|k|/k_{v}
, we get

\displaystyle W^{k}(G)\leq W^{k^{\prime}}(T)\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in\mathcal{L}}\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{k_{v}}\right)^{\frac{|k|}{k_{v}}}\right\}^{\frac{k_{v}}{|k|}}\times

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{k_{uv}}\prod_{a\in N(v)\setminus\{u\}}\left(\sum_{l\neq i}b_{lj}^{k_{av}}\right)\right]^{\frac{|k|}{k_{v}}}\right\}^{\frac{k_{v}}{|k|}},

where
T
is the spanning tree of
G
that maximizes
W^{k^{\prime}}(T^{\prime})
in Lemma
2.8
. Since
k_{v}\geq 4
and
b_{ij}^{k_{uv}}\leq b_{ij}^{2}\sigma_{*}^{k_{uv}-2}
, we get

\displaystyle W^{k}(G)\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{*}^{|k|-4|\mathcal{L}|-2\sum_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}|N(v)|}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{L}}\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{4}\right)^{\frac{|k|}{k_{v}}}\right\}^{\frac{k_{v}}{|k|}}\times

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}\prod_{a\in N(v)\setminus\{u\}}\left(\sum_{l\neq i}b_{lj}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{|k|}{k_{v}}}\right\}^{\frac{k_{v}}{|k|}}.

As
T
is a spanning tree, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}|N(v)|+|\mathcal{L}|=m_{2}+m_{1}-1;

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}|N(v)|-2|\mathcal{L}^{c}|=m_{2}-m_{1}-1+|\mathcal{L}|.

Moreover, we can remove
\sigma_{C}
from each term in the second product to get that

\displaystyle W^{k}(G)\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{*}^{|k|-2(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)-2|\mathcal{L}|}\sigma_{C}^{2(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)+2|\mathcal{L}|}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{L}}\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{4}\right)^{\frac{|k|}{k_{v}}}\right\}^{\frac{k_{v}}{|k|}}\times

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}\left(\sum_{l\neq i}b_{lj}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{|k|}{k_{v}}}\right\}^{\frac{k_{v}}{|k|}}.

Finally, the inequality of the norms in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
implies that

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\frac{|k|}{k_{v}}}\leq d^{\frac{k_{v}-4}{|k|}}\|\cdot\|_{\frac{|k|}{4}},

so we deduce

\displaystyle W^{k}(G)\leq d\sigma_{*}^{|k|-2(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)-2|\mathcal{L}|}\sigma_{C}^{2(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)+2|\mathcal{L}|}\sigma_{p}^{2|\mathcal{L}|}\bar{\sigma}_{p}^{2|\mathcal{L}^{c}|}.

The proof of Proposition
2.7
follows by a straightforward computation and the fact that
0\leq|\mathcal{L}|\leq m_{1}
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Note that we rather proved Theorem
1.2
with a parameter
\sigma_{p}^{\prime}
instead of
\sigma_{p}
, where
\sigma_{p}^{\prime}
only takes
l\neq i
, that is,

\displaystyle\sigma_{p}^{\prime}=\left\{\sum_{i\in[d]}\left[\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{l\neq i}b_{ij}^{2}b_{lj}^{2}\right]^{p/2}\right\}^{1/p}.

This minor change is only important for cases where the contribution of a column
X_{j}
appears only in the diagonal part, that is, when
X_{j}=b_{ij}e_{i}
for some
i
.

3.
Examples

Let us start by recalling the previous known results in
[
4
,
2
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 2.1 in
[
4
]
)
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\{2\sigma_{R}\sigma_{C}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)(\sigma_{C}\sigma_{*}+\sigma_{R}\sigma_{*})\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+C^{2}(\varepsilon)\sigma_{*}^{2}\log(n\wedge d)\right\}.

Theorem 3.2

(Theorem 3.12 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
d\times n
Gaussian matrix with independent entries and
X_{ij}=b_{ij}g_{ij}
, for
b_{ij}\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|X_{\emph{free}}X_{\emph{free}}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\otimes 1\|+C\left\{\sigma(X)\tilde{v}(X)\log^{3/4}(nd)+\tilde{v}^{2}(X)\log^{3/2}(nd)\right\}.

Corollary 3.3
.

In the setting of Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|X_{\emph{free}}X_{\emph{free}}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\otimes 1\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\max_{i\in[d]}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{l\in[d]}b_{ij}^{2}b_{lj}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\sigma_{C}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\sigma(X)=\max(\sigma_{C},\sigma_{R});

\displaystyle\tilde{v}(X)^{2}\asymp\sigma_{*}\sigma(X).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|

(5)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C\left[\sigma_{*}^{1/2}\sigma_{C}^{3/2}+\sigma_{*}^{1/2}\sigma_{R}^{3/2}\right]\log^{3/4}(nd)+C\left[\sigma_{*}\sigma_{C}+\sigma_{*}\sigma_{R}\right]\log^{3/2}(nd).

Proof.

Let
X_{j}
be the
j
th column of
X
. Then

\displaystyle\Sigma_{j}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}=\text{diag}(b_{ij}^{2}).

Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j\in[n]}\Sigma_{j}\right\|=\max_{i\in[d]}\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}=\sigma_{R}^{2};

\displaystyle\max_{j\in[n]}\text{Tr}(\Sigma_{j})=\max_{j\in[n]}\sum_{i\in[d]}b_{ij}^{2}=\sigma_{C}^{2}.

The computation for the parameters
\tilde{v}(X)
and
\sigma(X)
then follows by Lemma 3.8 in
[
2
]
.

On the other hand, denote

\displaystyle X=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}b_{ij}E_{ij}=\sum_{k}g_{k}A_{k},

where
E_{ij}
is the canonical basis of the space of
d\times n
matrices. Then the authors of
[
2
]
computed that
X_{\text{free}}=U+V
, where

\displaystyle U=\sum_{k}A_{k}\otimes l(e_{k});

\displaystyle V=\sum_{k}A_{k}\otimes l^{*}(e_{k}),

and
l
is the creation operator of the free Fock space over
\mathbb{C}^{nd}
. In particular, we have
l^{*}(e_{k})l(e_{j})=\delta_{kj}1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle VV^{*}=\sum_{k}A_{k}A_{k}^{*}\otimes 1=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\otimes 1;

\displaystyle U^{*}U=\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\otimes 1=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X^{T}X\otimes 1.

Hence they deduced that

\displaystyle\|X_{\text{free}}X_{\text{free}}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\otimes 1\|\leq\|UV^{*}+VU^{*}+UU^{*}\|\leq 2\|UV^{*}\|+\|UU^{*}\|.

The second one follows easily as

\displaystyle\|UU^{*}\|=\|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X^{T}X\|=\sigma_{C}^{2}.

On the other hand, in
[
2
]
, they used
\|UV^{*}\|\leq\|U\|\|V\|=\sigma_{R}\sigma_{C}
to bound the first term. However, in the case of independent entries, it is a straightforward computation to check that

\displaystyle\|UV^{*}\|=\sigma_{\infty},

and the result follows.
∎

Now we discuss various examples and present how Theorem
1.1
improves upon Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

Example 3.4
.

Assume the columns of
X
are i.i.d, namely,
b_{ij}=b_{i}
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{C}=\|b\|_{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{R}=\sqrt{n}\|b\|_{\infty};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{*}=\|b\|_{\infty};

\displaystyle\bullet\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\bar{\sigma}_{\infty}=\sqrt{n}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n}\|b\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{2}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\sigma_{C}}{\sigma_{*}}\leq\sqrt{n}\|b\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{2}.

Hence, both bounds shown in Theorem
1.1
yield

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\|b\|_{2}\|b\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{2}^{2}+

\displaystyle C\left[\|b\|_{2}\|b\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{n}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+

\displaystyle C\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}\log(n\wedge d).

The leading term agrees with the sharp bound derived in
[
4
,
7
]
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
X
be a Gaussian matrix with i.i.d rows, that is,
b_{ij}=b_{j}
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{C}=\sqrt{d}\|b\|_{\infty};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{R}=\|b\|_{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{*}=\|b\|_{\infty};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{\infty}=\sqrt{d-1}\|b\|_{4}^{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}=\bar{\sigma}_{\infty}=\|b\|_{4}^{2}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\sigma_{C}}{\sigma_{*}}=\sqrt{d}\|b\|_{4}^{2}.

Hence, Theorem
1.1
implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\|b\|_{4}^{2}+d\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+

\displaystyle C\left[\sqrt{d}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|b\|_{4}^{2}\right]\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+

\displaystyle C\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}\log(n\wedge d).

In this case, the error factor is smaller than the leading one, hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\|b\|_{4}^{2}+d\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}.

This agrees with the sharp result in
[
4
]
. However, they had to derive a different method to prove this case, whereas we deduce directly from our main result that covers all cases.

Moreover, (
3.3
) gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\|b\|_{4}^{2}+d\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+

\displaystyle C\left[\|b\|_{2}^{3/2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{1/2}+d^{3/4}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]\log^{3/4}(nd)+

\displaystyle C\left[\sqrt{d}\|b\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{2}\right]\|b\|_{\infty}\log^{3/2}(nd).

Here, we observe that the error factor is not necessarily smaller than the leading one.

Example 3.6
.

Consider
b_{ij}=a_{i}b_{j}
. Then

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{C}=\|a\|_{2}\|b\|_{\infty};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{R}=\|a\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{*}=\|a\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{\infty};

\displaystyle\bullet\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\leq\|b\|_{4}^{2}\|a\|_{\infty}^{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\bar{\sigma}_{\infty}=\|b\|_{4}^{2}\|a\|_{\infty}^{2};

\displaystyle\bullet\sigma_{\infty}\leq\|b\|_{4}^{2}\|a\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}.

We observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\sigma}_{\infty}\sigma_{C}}{\sigma_{*}}\leq\|b\|_{4}^{2}\|a\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}.

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\|b\|_{4}^{2}\|a\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}+\|a\|_{2}^{2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+

\displaystyle C\left[\|a\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|b\|_{4}^{2}\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+

\displaystyle C\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}\log(n\wedge d).

The result in
[
4
]
yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\|b\|_{2}\|b\|_{\infty}\|a\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}+\|a\|_{2}^{2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+

\displaystyle C\left[\|a\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|b\|_{2}\|b\|_{\infty}\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+

\displaystyle C\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}\log(n\wedge d).

And finally, (
3.3
) gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\|b\|_{4}^{2}\|a\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}+\|a\|_{2}^{2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+

\displaystyle C\left[\|a\|_{2}^{3/2}\|a\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|b\|_{2}^{3/2}\|b\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}\right]\log^{3/4}(nd)+

\displaystyle C\left[\|a\|_{2}\|b\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{2}\|a\|_{\infty}\right]\|a\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{\infty}\log^{3/2}(nd).

In this case, Theorem
1.1
strictly improves both and sheds light on the 4th-moment appearing for
b_{j}
.

Our final example is where all columns have approximately the same norm.

Example 3.7
.

Suppose there exists
K\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\|b_{k}\|_{2}\leq\|b_{j}\|_{2}\leq K\|b_{k}\|_{2},

for all

. Then it is easy to compute

\displaystyle\beta_{\infty}\leq K.

By Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\{2K\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\sigma_{*}\left(\sigma_{C}+\sigma_{R}\right)\sqrt{\log(n\wedge d)}+C^{2}(\varepsilon)\sigma_{*}^{2}\log(n\wedge d)\right\}.

Here, we do not require any additional structure on
B
and the previous known results only show the leading term with
\sigma_{C}\sigma_{R}
in
[

]
and a large error factor in
[

]
.

4.
Lower bounds

We first begin the lower bounds for the
p
-moment of the Schatten norm.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any even
p\geq 2
and
X
satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\emph{Tr}(XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}\gtrsim\sigma_{p}+\sigma_{C}^{2}+\sqrt{p}\bar{\sigma}_{p}+pb_{p}^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, the joint moments of
g
and
g^{2}-1
are always positive, thus it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}\geq\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(\text{Diag}(XX^{T})-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}\gtrsim\sqrt{p}\bar{\sigma}_{p}+pb_{p}^{2}.

For the leading factor, note that the Schatten norm is always lower bounded by the mixed
l_{2}(l_{p})
norm (see Lemma 2.12 in
[
8
]
), then Jensen’s Inequality implies that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\sum_{i\in[d]}\left(\sum_{l\in[d]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{2}_{il}\right)^{p/2}\right]^{1/p}.

The latter can be estimated as

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})_{il}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[n]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X_{j}X_{j}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j})^{2}_{il}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[n]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(b_{ij}b_{lj}(g_{ij}g_{lj}-\mathbf{1}_{i=l})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in[n]}b_{ij}^{2}b_{lj}^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\sum_{i\in[d]}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{l\in[d]}b_{ij}^{2}b_{lj}^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{p}.

Finally, let
j^{*}
be the column with the largest Euclidean norm, that is,
\|b_{j^{*}}\|_{2}=\sigma_{C}
, then

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}\geq\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(X_{j^{*}}X_{j^{*}}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j^{*}}X_{j^{*}}^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

The sharp result for the i.i.d case proved by Koltchinskii and Lounici in
[
7
]
implies that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\text{Tr}(X_{j^{*}}X_{j^{*}}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j^{*}}X_{j^{*}}^{T})^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|X_{j^{*}}X_{j^{*}}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j^{*}}X_{j^{*}}^{T}\|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sigma_{C}^{2},

where we assume
p
is even for the first inequality.
∎

For the operator norm, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a random matrix satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\gtrsim\sigma_{\infty}+\sigma_{C}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
S_{j}=X_{j}X_{j}^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{i}X_{i}^{T}
, then Tropp’s result in
[
10
]
implies that

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|^{2})^{1/2}\gtrsim\left\|\sum_{j\in[n]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}S_{j}S_{j}^{T}\right\|^{1/2}+(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\max_{j\in[n]}\|S_{j}\|^{2})^{1/2}.

The matrix in the first bound can then be computed as

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[n]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}-(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j}^{T})^{2}.

This can easily be seen as a diagonal matrix (a similar argument was proved in
[
4
]
) and lower bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j\in[n]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}X_{j}X_{j}^{T}-(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}X_{j}X_{j}^{T})^{2}\right\|\geq\sigma_{\infty}^{2},

hence

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|XX^{T}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}XX^{T}\|^{2})^{1/2}\gtrsim\sigma_{\infty}.

On the other hand, the second term can be bounded as

\displaystyle(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\max_{j\in[n]}\|S_{j}\|^{2})^{1/2}\geq\max_{j\in[n]}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\|S_{j}\|^{2})^{1/2}\gtrsim\sigma_{C}^{2},

where we again use the lower bound of
[
7
]
.

∎

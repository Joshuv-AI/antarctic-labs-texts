Complex cobordism, Hamiltonian loops, and global Kuranishi charts
Mohammed Abouzaid, Mark McLean, Ivan Smith

Abstract
. Let ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) be a closed symplectic manifold. A loop ϕ : S 1 → Diff ⁡ ( X ) \phi:S^{1}\to\mathrm{Diff}(X) of diffeomorphisms of X X defines a fibration π : P ϕ → S 2 \pi:P_{\phi}\to S^{2} . By applying Gromov-Witten theory to moduli spaces of holomorphic sections of π \pi , Lalonde, McDuff and Polterovich proved that if ϕ \phi lifts to the Hamiltonian group Ham ⁡ ( X , ω ) \mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega) , then the rational cohomology of P ϕ P_{\phi} splits additively. We prove, with the same assumptions, that the 𝔼 \mathbb{E} -generalised cohomology of P ϕ P_{\phi} splits additively for any complex-oriented cohomology theory 𝔼 \mathbb{E} , in particular the integral cohomology splits. This class of examples includes all complex projective varieties equipped with a smooth morphism to ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1} , in which case the analogous rational result was proved by Deligne using Hodge theory. The argument employs virtual fundamental cycles of moduli spaces of sections of π \pi in Morava K K -theory and results from chromatic homotopy theory. 1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold and denote by
\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega)
the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of
X
, equipped with its
C^{\infty}
-topology.
A loop
\phi:S^{1}\to\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega)
defines via the clutching construction a Hamiltonian fibration
P_{\phi}\to S^{2}
, i.e. a smooth fibre bundle with structure group
\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The cohomology
H^{*}(P_{\phi};\mathbb{Z})
splits additively as
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes H^{*}(S^{2};\mathbb{Z})
.

Corollary 1.2
.

The ‘sweepout’ map defined by
S^{1}\times X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}X

(1.1)

\delta_{\phi}:H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{*+1}(X;\mathbb{Z})

vanishes.

The result answers a twenty-year-old question of McDuff
[
47
]
. It is new even for a smooth projective variety equipped with a non-singular map to
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Combining Theorem
1.1
with topological arguments from
[
35
, Section 4]
, it follows that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of a Hamiltonian fibration with base space
B
splits additively when
B
is (for instance) a product of complex projective spaces. For definiteness, we only discuss fibrations over
S^{2}
in this paper. Simple examples such as the Hopf surface
S^{1}\times S^{3}\to S^{2}
and the odd Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1))\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
show respectively that the Hamiltonian hypothesis on
\phi
is essential, and that for Hamiltonian loops the cohomology need not split multiplicatively.

Theorem
1.1
is a special case of a more general result. For a generalised cohomology theory
\mathbb{E}
, we write
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{E}):=\mathbb{E}^{*}(X)
for the
\mathbb{E}
-cohomology groups of
X
, and
\mathbb{E}_{*}
for the coefficients groups
H^{*}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{E})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}
is complex oriented. Then, in the notation of Theorem
1.1
, there is an additive splitting
H^{*}(P_{\phi};\mathbb{E})\cong H^{*}(X;\mathbb{E})\otimes_{\mathbb{E}_{*}}H^{*}(S^{2};\mathbb{E})
.

The complex-oriented hypothesis on
\mathbb{E}
is essential; the real
KO
-theory of the odd Hirzebruch surfaces does not split additively. A striking feature of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is that the geometry of spaces of holomorphic curves gives us access to the case of
\mathbb{E}
a Morava
K
-theory, a class of spectra which are ‘algebraically’ natural, whilst results from chromatic homotopy theory then allow us to pass to the case of
\mathbb{E}=MU
, the geometrically natural theory of complex cobordism. See Section
2
for several examples illustrating Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

Recall that there are elements
Q_{i}
in the Milnor basis
[
49
]
of the Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}_{p}
, determined inductively in terms of the Bockstein
\beta
and power operations
P^{j}
via

(1.2)

Q_{0}=\beta;\ Q_{i+1}=[P^{\,p^{i}},Q_{i}].

The definition applies when
p=2
taking
Q_{i+1}=[Sq^{2^{i}},Q_{i}]
, giving
Q_{0}=Sq^{1}
,
Q_{1}=[Sq^{2},Sq^{1}]
, etc. We use the techniques behind Theorem
1.3
to show:

Corollary 1.4
.

The additive isomorphism
H^{*}(P_{\phi};\mathbb{Z}/p)\cong H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/p}H^{*}(S^{2};\mathbb{Z}/p)
in Theorem
1.3
entwines the action of the subalgebra of the Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}_{p}
generated by the operations
Q_{i}
.

The isomorphism cannot be chosen to entwine the entire Steenrod algebra: the
MU
-modules associated to the product
S^{2}\times S^{2}
and the odd Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1))
are isomorphic, but the operation
Sq^{2}
is trivial in the first case but not in the second as can be easily seen by considering the parity of the intersection form.

Remark 1.5
.

If
\mathbb{E}
is a
p
-local cohomology theory (for example, cohomology with
\mathbb{Z}/p
coefficients as in Corollary
1.4
), then the splitting of Theorem
1.3
depends only on splitting the map of spectra of Theorem
8.10
(this has nothing to do with our particular problem and can be done universally). The additive splitting of cohomology that results in principle depends on this choice. Starting from Remark
8.3
, one can show the splitting is compatible with restriction to the fibre
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{E})
and with the pullback action of
H^{*}(S^{2};\mathbb{E})
. The splitting for a general complex-orientable cohomology theory (including integral cohomology as in Theorem
1.1
) depends on a further choice, because our techniques do not ensure that the various mod
p
choices are ‘consistent’ with the choice for rational cohomology theories and glue together in the arithmetic fracture square.

Theorems
1.1
&
1.3
and Corollaries
1.2
&
1.4
can be viewed as new obstructions to surjectivity of the map
\pi_{1}\mathrm{Ham}(X)\to\pi_{1}\mathrm{Diff}(X)
.

The rational cohomology analogue of Theorem
1.1
was known in two situations classically: if
P_{\phi}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a projective morphism of complex algebraic varieties, the result follows from Deligne’s degeneration of the Leray spectral sequence
[
16
]
; if
\phi
is a Hamiltonian circle action, it follows from Kirwan’s description of cohomology of symplectic quotients
[
32
]
. The statements for rational cohomology were established for a general monotone
X
by Lalonde, McDuff and Polterovich
[
36
]
, and for all
X
by McDuff
[
47
]
, using Gromov-Witten theory. The restriction to rational coefficients from that viewpoint arises because moduli spaces of holomorphic curves are (closed subsets of) orbifolds, and have virtual fundamental cycles only in rational cohomology. McDuff
[
47
]
asked whether the result also holds integrally. Abouzaid and Blumberg have introduced Hamiltonian Floer homology groups with coefficients in Morava
K
-theory
[
1
]
, using crucially the fact that the classifying space of a finite group satisfies Poincaré duality in Morava
K
-theory and hence that compact orbifolds admit fundamental classes in that theory (the ‘ambidexterity’ property). Following that insight, and borrowing heavily from work of Cheng
[
12
]
, Theorem
1.1
gives a positive answer to McDuff’s question.

Remark 1.6
.

The Morava
K
-theories are generalized cohomology theories
K(n)
depending on a choice of prime
p\geqslant 2
(usually suppressed from the notation) and a natural number
n\geqslant 1
, with coefficients
\mathbb{F}_{p}[v^{\pm 1}]
with
|v|=2(p^{n}-1)
. Traditionally, one sets
K(0)=H\mathbb{Q}
;
K(1)
is a wedge summand of mod
p
complex
K
-theory; some authors write
K(\infty)=H\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The theories with
n>1
are very hard to compute, and our argument uses only general properties: most notably, ambidexterity, which is a duality statement for the Morava
K
-theories of classifying spaces of finite groups. Along with suitable Eilenberg-Maclane spectra, the Morava
K
-theories are the ‘only fields’ in the stable homotopy category localised at
p

[
18
,
26
]
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
also employs Morava
K
-theories with coefficients related to the rings
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, which are not completely standard, and which we discuss in Section
7.3
.

A noteworthy feature of our approach to Theorem
1.1
is that we construct
global
Kuranishi charts for moduli spaces of genus zero curves, see Section
6
. This allows us to construct the virtual fundamental cycle in one go, rather than via a local-to-global argument involving a Kuranishi cover. The simplifications that arise from the use of global charts cannot be overstated; not only does it allow us to formulate all constructions for Theorem
1.1
at the level of generalised cohomology theories, but it also makes the paper both independent of
[
1
]
and significantly shorter. The precise result that we prove is the following:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(X,\omega,J)
be a compact symplectic manifold with compatible almost complex structure
J
and fix a class
\beta\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. The moduli space of stable genus zero
J
-holomorphic maps with
n
marked points
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,J,\beta)
in class
\beta
admits a
global Kuranishi chart
: there is a compact Lie group
G
, a topological
G
-manifold
\mathcal{T}
with a
G
-equivariant vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{T}
and a
G
-equivariant section
\mathfrak{s}
of
\mathcal{E}
with
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,J,\beta)=\mathfrak{s}^{-1}(0)/G
.

The case of higher genus curves is substantially more delicate, and will be treated elsewhere.

Remark 1.8
.

Our construction of global Kuranishi charts may be heuristically compared to earlier points of view as follows: one approach to constructing a fundamental chain on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(X,J,\beta)
is to fix, locally on the moduli space, a collection of Donaldson-type divisors in
X
whose inverse images under a stable map stabilise the domain curve. An open subset of the moduli space then maps to a space of pointed curves
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,N}
. Our global chart
\mathcal{T}
for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(X,J,\beta)
comes with a globally defined map to the space of stable maps to projective space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(\mathbb{P}^{d},d)
. Heuristically, we replace the ‘hard’ choice of Donaldson divisors on
X
with a ‘soft’ choice of a connection on a line bundle and universal co-ordinate hyperplanes in projective space. The heart of the matter is that we rigidify a stable map by framing the curve, and not just scattering marked points.

Remark 1.9
.

We do not prove that
\mathcal{T}
is smooth, but we equip it with a ‘fibrewise smooth structure’ over a moduli space of domains; that, coupled with classical smoothing theory, suffices for the applications in this paper. One could circumvent the smoothing theory by reworking some of the results of
[
12
]
for topological manifolds as was done in
[
1
]
; the essential ingredient in this alternative approach is that the tangent spherical fibration of
\mathcal{T}
admits a vector bundle lift, the proof of which uses the fibrewise smooth structure. The use of the spherical fibration in
[
1
]
relied on explicit constructions to globalise local arguments, and it would be difficult to appeal to smoothing theory in that context.

For concreteness, we first give a complete proof of Theorem
1.1
, and then revisit and elaborate on parts of that argument to establish Theorem
1.3
at the end of the paper. We prove the latter result first in the case of complex cobordism,
\mathbb{E}=MU
, by combining the geometric argument underlying Theorem
1.1
with deep results in chromatic homotopy theory; see Section
8
. The proof of the result over
MU
involves showing that a stable
K(n)
-local version of the sweepout map from Corollary
1.2
is actually nullhomotopic, and not just cohomologically trivial. This suffices to conclude Theorem
1.3
for a general complex-oriented
\mathbb{E}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
works directly with certain moduli spaces of sections of
P_{\phi}\to S^{2}
for some fixed almost complex structure, and corresponding Kuranishi charts as obtained from Theorem
1.7
. In particular, we do not need to construct ‘quantum Morava
K
-theory’ or a Seidel representation
[
60
]
into its invertibles. As with quantum
K
-theory for smooth projective varieties
[
41
]
, the associativity of quantum Morava
K
-theory (defined as a module over the Novikov ring
\Lambda_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}
, where
\mathbb{K}=K(n)
is a Morava
K
-theory) relies on working with a deformation of the usual Poincaré pairing on
K^{*}(X;\Lambda_{\mathbb{K}_{*}})
constructed from moduli spaces of curves with two marked points. A sequel to this paper
[
2
]
uses global Kuranishi charts to construct and investigate symplectic quantum (ordinary and Morava)
K
-theory.

Organisation of the paper.
Section
3
reviews and mildly re-interprets the structure of the classical proof (over
\mathbb{Q}
) of Theorem
1.1
in a way which is well-adapted to generalisation. After a background Section
4
on global Kuranishi charts, Section
5
constructs the virtual fundamental class in Morava
K
-theory, and proves Theorem
1.1
assuming the existence of global Kuranishi charts for moduli spaces of genus zero curves. Section
6
then constructs such global charts, proving Theorem
1.7
, and completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Up to this point, we are careful to work only with the language of generalised cohomology theories, avoiding technicalities with spectra. Section
7
reviews background from stable homotopy theory and gives an account of Cheng’s theorems from
[
12
]
. Section
8
gives the proof of Theorem
1.3
. The last two sections appeal to foundations of stable homotopy theory that are formulated in terms of classical spectra, so that the reader can use
[
3
]
as a reference for non-equivariant constructions, and
[
42
]
for equivariant constructions.

2.
Examples and illustrations

We collect a number of examples illustrating Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a smooth
n
-dimensional complex projective variety which is a
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{k}
-bundle over an
(n-k)
-fold
S
such that the
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{k}
-fibres are linearly embedded. If
2k>n
then
X
has ‘positive defect’
[
20
]
, i.e. its dual variety has codimension
>1
, which means that there is a natural algebraic fibration
\Sigma\to X\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
with fibres being smooth hyperplane sections
\Sigma\subset X
. Theorem
1.1
shows that
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
splits (note that
S
, and the hyperplane section
\Sigma
, can have complicated torsion in cohomology).

Suppose a manifold
L
admits a free circle action with quotient
Q
. Viewing the action as a loop of diffeomorphisms, the corresponding fibration
L\to P\to S^{2}
has total space
P=(L\times S^{3})/S^{1}
where
S^{1}
acts diagonally, so there is a second fibration
S^{3}\to P\to Q
with Euler class the square of that defining
L\to Q
. Comparing the Leray-Serre spectral sequences of these fibrations gives many examples of loops of diffeomorphisms for which the cohomology of
P
splits rationally and not integrally. Few symplectic manifolds admit free symplectic circle actions (the quotient is always the mapping torus of a symplectomorphism
[
21
]
), and Hamiltonian circle actions always have fixed points, but the above examples are relevant to loops of diffeomorphisms of a manifold
X
which happen to preserve a submanifold
L
: functoriality of the Leray-Serre spectral sequence and Theorem
1.1
yield constraints on the image of the map
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Ham}(X,L))\to\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(L))
.

Example 2.2
.

The Hopf circle action on
L=\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{3}
yields a fibration
P_{\phi}\to S^{2}
whose cohomology splits over
\mathbb{Q}
but not over
\mathbb{Z}/2
. If
L=\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{3}\subset X
induces a surjection
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H^{*}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{3};\mathbb{Z}/2)
, then an element of
\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Diff}(X,L)
which induces the Hopf rotation on
L
cannot lift to
\pi_{1}\,\mathrm{Ham}(X,L)
.

Example 2.3
.

Consider the loop of diffeomorphisms
\phi
defining the standard Hopf circle action on
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{5}
. Since this is a free circle action, as above we have the dual descriptions of the associated bundle

(2.1)

\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{5}\to P_{\phi}\to S^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad S^{3}\to P_{\phi}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}.

By comparing the Serre spectral sequences of these two fibrations, one sees that the sweepout map for

is non-trivial over

: there is exactly one non-trivial differential

(2.2)

\mathbb{Z}=H^{0}(S^{2};H^{5}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{5}))\to H^{2}(S^{2};H^{4}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{5}))=\mathbb{Z}/2

It follows that the sweepout over

for the

splits additively over

. However, it does not split additively in complex

KU^{i}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{5})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4&i\text{ even}\\

A mildly involved comparison of the two Atiyah-Hirzebruch-Serre spectral sequences associated to (

\mathbb{Z}=H^{0}(S^{2};KU^{1}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{5}))\to H^{2}(S^{2};KU^{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{5}))=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4\to\mathbb{Z}/4

Example 2.4

The additive splitting of

3.
The argument in outline

This section reviews and simplifies the classical argument (for the rational cohomology analogue of Theorem
1.1
) in a format which will guide the translation from classical cohomology to generalized cohomology. Throughout the paper, we will use the following

Notation 3.1
.

For a subspace
A\subset Y
, we set
H^{*}(Y|A;\mathbb{K}):=H^{*}(Y,Y\backslash A;\mathbb{K})
.

In this section,
\mathbb{K}
denotes a ring (in later sections, it will typically be a ring spectrum).

3.1.
Background

Keep the previous notation; a loop
\phi\in\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega))
gives rise to a Hamiltonian fibre bundle
P_{\phi}\to S^{2}
with fibre
X

Lemma 3.2
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The map
\delta_{\phi}:H_{*}(X;\mathbb{K})\to H_{*+1}(X;\mathbb{K})
vanishes.

(2)

The natural map
H_{*}(X;\mathbb{K})\to H_{*}(P_{\phi};\mathbb{K})
is injective.

(3)

The cohomology spectral sequence for the fibration
P_{\phi}\to S^{2}
degenerates.

(4)

The restriction
H^{*}(P_{\phi};\mathbb{K})\to H^{*}(X;\mathbb{K})
is surjective.

If
\mathbb{K}
is a field then the above conditions are equivalent to the condition that

H^{*}(P_{\phi};\mathbb{K})
splits additively as
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{K})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}H^{*}(S^{2};\mathbb{K})
.

The sweepout map
\delta_{\phi}
arises in the Wang sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i+1}(X;\mathbb{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i+1}(P_{\phi};\mathbb{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i-1}(X;\mathbb{K}).}

4.
Global Kuranishi Charts

A global Kuranishi chart presents a space as the zero set of a vector bundle section
over an orbifold, itself presented as a global Lie group quotient.
We will use such global Kuranishi charts of moduli spaces of genus zero curves
to equip them with a virtual fundamental class with respect
to certain generalized cohomology theories.
In order to do this, our global Kuranishi charts need additional structure.
In this section we will define global Kuranishi charts, describe basic operations on them
and give some conditions needed to put additional structures on such charts.

4.1.
Initial definitions

Definition 4.1
.

A
global Kuranishi chart
is a tuple
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
where

(1)

G
is a compact Lie group;

(2)

\mathcal{T}
is a topological manifold admitting a continuous action of
G
with finite stabilisers;

(3)

E\to\mathcal{T}
is a
G
-vector bundle;

(4)

s:\mathcal{T}\to E
is a
G
-equivariant section.

A
global Kuranishi chart
for a metric space
M
is a global Kuranishi chart
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
as above
together with a ‘footprint’ homeomorphism
M\to s^{-1}(0)/G
.
The
virtual dimension
of
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
is defined to be
\mathrm{vdim}(G,\mathcal{T},E,s):=\dim(\mathcal{T})-\mathrm{rank}(E)-\dim(G)
.

A global Kuranishi chart
(G^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime},E^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is
homeomorphic
to
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)

if
G
is isomorphic to
G^{\prime}
and there is a
G
-equivariant homeomorphism
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\prime}

and a
G
-vector bundle isomorphism
E\to E^{\prime}
covering this homeomorphism sending
s
to
s^{\prime}
.

We will refer to
\mathcal{T}
as the
thickening
.
If it is clear what the ‘footprint’ homeomorphism is, then we will omit it from our notation
and just refer to the quadruple
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
as a Kuranishi chart for
M
.
If it is clear which Kuranishi chart is associated to
M
,
then we will just write
\mathrm{vdim}(M)
instead of
\mathrm{vdim}(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
.

Example 4.2
.

If
M
is a manifold then
(0,M,M,0)
is a global Kuranishi chart where
0
is the trivial Lie group
and
M
is viewed as the zero dimensional vector bundle over
M
.

In general moduli spaces of holomorphic curves will be highly singular.
However, for computational purposes, it is helpful to look at nicer
regions of these moduli spaces as in the definitions below. In the following definition, we always lift subsets of
s^{-1}(0)/G
to
s^{-1}(0)
by the natural quotient map.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
be a Kuranishi chart.

•

We say that
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
is
free
along
C\subset s^{-1}(0)/G
if
the
G
-action is free along the preimage of
C
in
s^{-1}(0)
. Such a chart is
free
if it is free everywhere (i.e. along
s^{-1}(0)/G
).

•

A global Kuranishi chart
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)

is
smooth

if both
\mathcal{T}
and
E
are smooth and if the corresponding
G
-actions on
\mathcal{T}
and
E
are smooth. Note that the section
s
need not be smooth.

•

A smooth global Kuranishi chart
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
is
regular

along a subset
C\subset s^{-1}(0)/G
if
s
is smooth and transverse to
0
along the preimage of
C
in
s^{-1}(0)
.
It is
regular
if it is regular along
s^{-1}(0)/G
.

Note that for a smooth global Kuranishi chart,

s^{-1}(0)/G
is naturally a smooth orbifold of dimension
\mathrm{vdim}(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
along its locus of regular points and a smooth manifold along the set of points which are both regular and free.

Example 4.4
.

If
M
is a smooth orbifold with a bounded number of stabilizer groups,
then by
[
55
, Corollary 1.3]
there is a smooth Kuranishi chart

(U(m),\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T},0)
for
M
where
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
In other words, this orbifold is expressed as a global quotient by a Lie group.

Global Kuranishi charts
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
for a given metric space
M

are not unique. We now describe operations
on
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)

which we will later show preserve the virtual fundamental class.

(1)

(Germ equivalence).
Take a
G
-invariant open neighborhood

of

, and replace
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
by
(G,U,E|_{U},s|_{U})
.

(2)

(Stabilization).
Let

4.2.
Microbundles

The global Kuranishi charts for moduli spaces of holomorphic curves that we obtain from geometry are not obviously smooth. We will use the theory of microbundles to show that, by stabilizing, one can equip the moduli space with some smooth global chart.
A microbundle is a generalization of the concept of a vector bundle,
allowing us to speak of the ‘tangent bundle’ of a topological manifold.
A prototype statement is that if the tangent microbundle of a topological manifold admits a vector bundle lift, then the product of that manifold with a Euclidean space
admits a smooth structure.

Remark 4.6
.

We will later use a number of results of Cheng
[
12

on

-duality for compact Lie group actions on smooth manifolds with finite stabilisers. An alternative to appealing to smoothing theory would be to observe that Cheng’s results also apply

to topological manifolds with locally linear actions under the same hypothesis of the microbundle admitting a vector bundle lift. Since the technical ingredients in both approaches are largely the same, and having smooth global charts for moduli spaces of genus zero curves may be of wider interest, we have chosen the former approach.

5.5.
Morava

For background on Morava

By a

Lemma 5.13
.

Let

denote the Morava

(1)

The coefficient ring is

(2)

Any vector bundle with a stable complex structure is
\mathbb{K}
-oriented.

is
\mathbb{K}
-oriented.

(4)

If

\mathbb{K}
-oriented.

\mathbb{K}
-oriented.

Proof.

The coefficients form a graded field, i.e. a graded ring with the property that all non-zero homogeneous elements are invertible, and all graded modules are projective. The Künneth theorem then follows essentially formally. (Note that this involves tensoring left and right

-modules; it is fact that the left and right module structures agree, cf. Lemma

[

43
]
. ∎

In fact, we shall use a slightly more general notion of Morava

:

For a prime

•

•

any vector bundle with a stable complex structure is

∎

5.10.
A homological virtual class

We have constructed the virtual fundamental class for a metric space
M
with a global Kuranishi presentation as an element in the dual of cohomology
H^{*}(M;\mathbb{K})\to\mathbb{K}_{*}
, cf. Definition
5.30
. This fits with the set-up adopted in
[
54
]
, with the usual picture in which moduli spaces of holomorphic curves act by correspondences on cohomology
[
44
]
, and circumvents difficulties arising from the potential pathologies of the space
M
(which would necessitate discussing generalised Steenrod homology
[
19
]
). Nonetheless, for technical reasons it will be useful in Section
8
to formulate the fundamental class as an element of a homology group, which for us will be the homology of the thickening; since the thickening is a manifold, its generalised homology groups are defined using standard methods of algebraic topology. We briefly indicate the set-up here.

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a ring spectrum. The associated generalised cohomology theory has cap products

(5.42)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}(X,A;\mathbb{E})\otimes H_{j}(X,A;\mathbb{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{j-i}(X;\mathbb{E})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frown}

which on representatives
(\alpha:X/A\to\Sigma^{i}\mathbb{E})\in H^{i}(X,A;\mathbb{E})
and
(\beta:S^{j}\to X/A\wedge\mathbb{E})\in H_{j}(X,A;\mathbb{E})
is defined by the composite

(5.43)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\frown\beta:S^{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/A\wedge\mathbb{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/A\wedge X\wedge\mathbb{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\wedge\Sigma^{i}\mathbb{E}\wedge\mathbb{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/A\wedge\Sigma^{i}\mathbb{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{\mathbb{E}}}

where
\Delta
is induced from the diagonal map of
X
and
\mu_{\mathbb{E}}
is multiplication.

Suppose now that
\mathbb{K}
is a complex-oriented
K(n)
-local ring spectrum and
(G,\mathcal{T},E,s)
is a smooth
\mathbb{K}
-oriented global Kuranishi chart.
Up to stabilization and germ equivalence, we can assume that
T_{\mu}\mathcal{T}-\mathfrak{g}
and
E
are
\mathbb{K}
-oriented in a way compatible with the given
\mathbb{K}
-orientation (Lemma
5.11
), and that
\mathcal{T}
is a compact smooth manifold with boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}
. We have the equivariant fundamental class

(5.44)

H^{0}_{G}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{K})\ni 1\mapsto[\mathcal{T},\partial\mathcal{T}]\in H_{\dim(\mathcal{T})-\dim(G)}^{G}(\mathcal{T},\partial\mathcal{T};\mathbb{K})

and the orientation
\mathfrak{o}_{E}\in H^{\operatorname{rk}(E)}_{G}(E|\mathcal{T};\mathbb{K})
. Since by hypothesis

admits a
G
-equivariant section

for which the zero-locus

is supported in the interior, we have a lift
e_{G}(E):=s^{*}\mathfrak{o}_{E}\in H^{*}_{G}(\mathcal{T},\partial\mathcal{T};\mathbb{K})
of the Euler class of
E
, and therefore the cap-product defines an
absolute
homology class,

(5.45)

e_{G}(E)\frown[\mathcal{T},\partial\mathcal{T}]\,:=\,[\mathrm{vfc}]\in H^{G}_{vd}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{K})

in degree the virtual dimension

. The virtual classes for different Kuranishi presentations of

are entwined by the operations on global charts in the obvious sense.

From this starting point, one produces a

splitting of the inclusion map

, rather than a cohomological splitting of the dual restriction map, as formulated in Theorem

. The argument proceeds as follows: we have as before a global chart
\mathcal{K}
for a space of curves

with two marked points lying on

and

; recall that the virtual dimension of the moduli space we consider is

. We consider the composite

(5.46)

H_{i}(\tilde{P};\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Dual}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{d-i}(\tilde{P};\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pull}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{d-i}(\tilde{P}\times(X\times S^{2});\mathbb{K})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\frown[\mathrm{vfc}]}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{i}(\tilde{P}\times(X\times S^{2});\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{project}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{i}(X\times S^{2};\mathbb{K}).

Dualising the argument from Section
5.9
, we see that this splits the inclusion map.

Definition 6.3

An irreducible degree

Lemma 6.4
.

Both
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{C}
are smooth quasi-projective varieties.

Proof.

According to
[
22
, Theorem 2]
, the moduli space

is a projective orbifold whose dimension agrees with its virtual dimension (this holds because projective space is ‘convex’).
This theorem also states that the points on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d},d)

corresponding to curves with trivial automorphism groups
have trivial stabilizer groups.
Therefore it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{F}
includes into the locus of stable maps with trivial automorphism group.

The following reformulation of
\mathcal{F}
will be helpful: fixing the usual homogeneous co-ordinates on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d}
, and sections
s_{i}
vanishing on the co-ordinate hyperplanes, note that
\mathcal{F}
can equivalently be viewed as the Zariski open in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d},d)
of nodal curves
u:\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d}
for which
\{u^{*}s_{0},\ldots,u^{*}s_{d}\}
yields a basis of
H^{0}(u^{*}\mathcal{O}(1))
. More abstractly, let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over
\Sigma
of total degree
d
, and for which the degree of
L
on each unstable component of
\Sigma
is strictly positive. Let
F=(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{d})
be a basis of
H^{0}(L)
;
we define

(6.3)

\phi_{F}:\Sigma\overset{}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{d},\quad\phi_{F}(\sigma):=[f_{0}(\sigma):f_{1}(\sigma):\cdots:f_{d}(\sigma)].

Since no unstable component is contracted, this yields a stable map to projective space, which is manifestly not contained in a hyperplane; thus
\phi_{F}
defines a point of
\mathcal{F}
.

The group of automorphisms of a curve
u
is isomorphic to the group of automorphisms of the corresponding triple
(\Sigma,L,F)
, whose elements are given by an automorphism of
\Sigma
which lifts to
L
and preserves the basis

, hence acts on

trivially. The dual graph of
\Sigma
is a tree; the terminal vertices (leaves) of that tree are unstable components, over which

has strictly positive degree. Over any such component there is a non-zero section of
L
which vanishes on all other components, so an automorphism cannot permute leaves. It follows that it fixes each component setwise, and must fix the unstable components pointwise since it acts trivially on the vector space of sections of an ample line bundle over each such component. This completes our proof that the locus
\mathcal{F}
consists of stable maps with trivial automorphisms, hence is a smooth manifold.
∎

Lemma 6.8

Proof.

For existence, let

Let

6.3.
The ‘Gromov trick’ and shearing almost complex structures

In order to take advantage of existing gluing and transversality results, it will be helpful to recast the moduli spaces of perturbed holomorphic curves appearing in the global Kuranishi chart in terms of unperturbed holomorphic curves. The ‘Gromov graph trick’ is a standard such reduction. The next three subsections discuss general theory, which we then bring to bear on our particular case at the end of the section.

Set-up:
Let
\pi:\check{E}\overset{}{\longrightarrow}\check{X}
be a Hermitian vector bundle over an almost complex manifold
(\check{X},\check{J})
with Hermitian connection
\nabla
.
Let
T^{v}\check{E}:=\mathrm{ker}\,d\pi
be the vertical tangent bundle of
\check{E}
.
If
C^{\infty}(\check{E})
denotes the space of smooth sections of
\check{E}
, set

(6.10)

T^{h}\check{E}:=\{ds(v)\ :\ v\in T\check{X},\ s\in C^{\infty}(\check{E}),\ \nabla_{v}s=0\}

to be the corresponding
horizontal tangent bundle

(i.e. the Ehresmann connection corresponding to the Hermitian structure).

There is a natural lift
\widetilde{J}
of
\check{J}
to the total space of
\check{E}
, characterised by the following three properties:

•

T^{v}\check{E}
and
T^{h}\check{E}
are
\widetilde{J}
-holomorphic subbundles of
(T\check{E},\widetilde{J})
.

•

\pi
is
\widetilde{J}
-holomorphic.

•

The restriction of
\widetilde{J}
to the fibres of
\check{E}
coincides with their natural complex structure.

We will be interested in certain ‘shears’ of this natural lift.

Definition 6.17
.

Given a real linear bundle map
\check{\Psi}:\check{E}\oplus T\check{X}\overset{}{\longrightarrow}T\check{X}
over
\check{X}
,

to be the almost complex structure

(6.12)

\displaystyle\overset{}{\longrightarrow}T^{v}\check{E}\oplus T^{h}\check{E}

\displaystyle:=(v,h+\check{\Psi}(e,h)).

If

\check{J}(\check{\Psi}(e,h))=-\check{\Psi}(e,\check{J}(h)),\textrm{ and }\check{\Psi}(e,\check{\Psi}(e,h))=0,

then we have the following explicit formula for

(6.15)

\widetilde{J}_{\check{\Psi}}(v,h)=(\widetilde{J}(v),\widetilde{J}(h)+2\check{\Psi}(e,\widetilde{J}(h))).

The importance of the shear is explained by the following Lemma. Let
\Sigma
be a nodal curve with complex structure

Suppose that we have a smooth map

(Gromov trick.)

\check{\Psi}

Therefore by Equation (

By unique continuation, we may view all the curves obtained from pregluing applied to a fixed stable map as elements of a space of smooth maps from the fixed domain which is the complement of small balls around the nodes (this viewpoint has been popularised in pseudoholomorphic curve theory by Fukaya, within the more delicate context of constructing a smooth structure). The referenced Proposition shows that, given a
J
-holomorphic neck in the gluing region, the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution near the ends of the neck controls all
C^{k}
-norms throughout the neck region. It follows that the preglued curve is close to the actual solution in any
C^{k}
-norm on this space. Since the preglued curve does not depend on the choice of gluing parameter away from the necks, the comparison between charts associated to different gluing parameters are locally
C^{1}
-smooth with respect to any fixed choice of domain. Unique continuation again implies that the resulting smooth structure does not depend on this choice.
∎

We now recall how Bousfield localisation extends both the notions of localisation and completion of abelian groups: If

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\frown[\mathrm{vfc}]}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\left(P_{\phi}\times(X\times S^{2})_{hor}\right)_{+}\wedge\mathbb{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{project}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X\times S^{2})_{hor,+}\wedge\mathbb{K}.

In order to show that this map gives the desired splitting, one reproduces the framework of Section
3.4
at the level of spectra, replacing the moduli spaces with their global charts, and formulating all results homologically. Briefly, we consider the thickening
\mathcal{K}_{h}
for the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h}
of stable spheres, in the section class, with
2
marked points, one of which is constrained to map to
(S^{2}\times X)_{hor})
and one to
\tilde{P}
. The compatibility of the fundamental classes of
\mathcal{K}_{h}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}

shows that the composition in Equation (

) factors as

(8.18)

P_{\phi,+}\wedge\mathbb{K}\to\tilde{P}_{+}\wedge\mathbb{K}\to(X\times S^{2})_{hor,+}\wedge\mathbb{K}.

On the other hand, the inclusion of
X

P_{\phi}
fits in a homotopy commutative diagram

(8.19)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times S^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{\phi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{P},}

so it suffices to show that the composite

(8.20)

is homotopic to the map induced by the inclusion

as the fibre over
\infty
. This follows from the fact that Diagram (

\mathbb{K}

∎

, we conclude:

Corollary 8.9
.

If
\mathbb{K}
is a
K(n)
-local complex oriented spectrum, then

(8.21)

8.3.
Chromatic splitting

For a fixed prime
p
, the Morava
K
-theories
K(n)=K_{p}(n)
are obtained as quotients of the
p
-local spectrum
BP
which arises as one of the isomorphic wedge summands of the
p
-localization
MU_{(p)}
, see
[
58
]
.

We shall use the following result about the
K(n)
-localisations of these spectra:

Theorem 8.10

(Theorem 3.1 of
[
27
]
)
.

The natural map
BP\to\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{K(n)}BP
is the inclusion of a wedge summand.

The sweepout map associated to
BP
thus factors as

(8.22)

S^{1}\wedge X_{+}\wedge\mathbb{S}\to X_{+}\wedge\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{K(n)}BP\to X_{+}\wedge BP,

so that the null-homotopies from Corollary
8.9
yield a null-homotopy of
\eta_{\phi}\wedge BP_{p}
. Decomposing
MU_{p}
into its wedge summands (Theorem
8.7
), we conclude:

Corollary 8.11
.

For each prime
p
, the stable sweepout
\eta_{\phi}
is nullhomotopic after smashing with
MU_{(p)}
. ∎

8.4.
Null homotopy for complex oriented cohomology theories

We are now ready to complete the proof of our main result:

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
S^{1}\wedge X_{+}
is finite, Lemma
8.6
and Corollary
8.11
imply that the
MU
sweepout map

(8.23)

S^{1}\wedge X_{+}\to X_{+}\wedge MU.

is null homotopic. On the other hand, the assumption that
\mathbb{E}
is a complex oriented cohomology theory includes the unitality statement that the unit map
\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{E}
factors through
MU
, so the result follows from the factorisation

(8.24)

S^{1}\wedge X_{+}\to X_{+}\wedge MU\to X_{+}\wedge\mathbb{E}.

∎

8.5.
Steenrod operations

We conclude by explaining the proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{p}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}}(H\mathbb{Z}/p,H\mathbb{Z}/p)
denote the Steenrod algebra, where
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}}
denotes graded morphisms in the stable homotopy category; equivalently,
\mathcal{A}_{p}=\pi_{*}(F(H\mathbb{Z}/p,H\mathbb{Z}/p))
. We let
\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee}
be its
\mathbb{Z}/p
-linear dual. It is well known that
\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee}=\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{Z}/p\wedge H\mathbb{Z}/p)=(H\mathbb{Z}/p)_{*}(H\mathbb{Z}/p)
. Let
X
be a finite CW complex. The Steenrod action

(8.25)

\mathcal{A}_{p}\otimes H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)\to H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)

dualises to a coaction

(8.26)

H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)\to\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee}\otimes H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)

which is given more explicitly by

(8.27)

(H\mathbb{Z}/p)_{*}(X)\to(H\mathbb{Z}/p)_{*}(\mathbb{S}\wedge X)\to(H\mathbb{Z}/p)_{*}(H\mathbb{Z}/p\wedge X)\to\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee}\otimes(H\mathbb{Z}/p)_{*}(X)

where the second arrow comes from the unit
\mathbb{S}\to H\mathbb{Z}/p
. A map of spaces
X\to Y
gives a map
H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)\to H_{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}/p)
which is a map of coalgebras. We are interested in a situation where we instead have a map
X\to Y\wedge MU
. Let

(8.28)

(\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee})_{MU}=\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{Z}/p\wedge_{MU}H\mathbb{Z}/p)

which comes with a natural quotient map

(8.29)

\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee}=\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{Z}/p\wedge H\mathbb{Z}/p)\to\pi_{*}(H\mathbb{Z}/p\wedge_{MU}H\mathbb{Z}/p)=(\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee})_{MU}.

Essentially from the definition, we then have:

Lemma 8.12
.

Let
X
and
Y
be finite spectra and suppose we have an equivalence
X\wedge MU\simeq Y\wedge MU
of
MU
-modules.
Then the induced equivalence
X\wedge H\mathbb{Z}/p\simeq Y\wedge H\mathbb{Z}/p
coming from the
MU
module structure on
H\mathbb{Z}/p
yields an isomorphism of homotopy groups

(8.30)

H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)\to H_{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}/p)

which respects the Steenrod coaction by the quotient
(\mathcal{A}_{p}^{\vee})_{MU}
. ∎

Remark 8.13
.

The main references
[
38
,
61
]
that we shall use in this section formulate many constructions in terms of a modern category of spectra (orthogonal or symmetric spectra), and others in terms of the classical notions of

and

[

Alternatively, one may note that the computations that we use are established by proving the degeneration of a

Suppose

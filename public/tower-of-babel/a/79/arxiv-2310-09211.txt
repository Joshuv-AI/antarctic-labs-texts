The effect of solar wind on the charged particles’ diffusion coefficients
J. F. Wang, G. Qin

Abstract
The transport of energetic charged particles through magnetized plasmas is ubiquitous in interplanetary space and astrophysics, and the important physical quantities are the along-field and cross-field spatial diffusion coefficients of energetic charged particles. In this paper, the influence of solar wind on particle transport is investigated. Using the focusing equation, we obtain along- and cross-field diffusion coefficient accounting for the solar wind effect. For different conditions, the relative importance of solar wind effect to diffusion are investigated. It is shown that when energetic charged particles are close to the sun, for along-field diffusion the solar wind effect needs to be taken into account. These results are important for studying energetic charged particle transport processes in the vicinity of the sun.

)

The turbulent

in the interplanetary space,

have a significant impact on

.

II
The focusing equation

The Fokker-Planck equation is
formulated as follows

(
Duderstadt & Martin 1979
;
Huang & Ding 2008
;
Zank 2014
)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}+\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{u}f\right)+\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\bm{a}f\right)=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa_{1}\cdot\nabla f\right)+\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\kappa_{1}^{p}\cdot\nabla_{p}f\right)+\nabla\cdot\left[\kappa_{21}\cdot\nabla\left(\kappa_{22}\cdot\nabla f\right)\right]

(4)

\displaystyle+\nabla_{p}\cdot\left[\kappa_{21}^{p}\cdot\nabla_{p}\left(\kappa_{22}^{p}\cdot\nabla_{p}f\right)\right]+\cdots.

Here,
f=f(\bm{r},\bm{p},t)

is the distribution function
of charged energetic particles,

t
is time,

\bm{u}
is particle
velocity, and

\bm{a}
is particle
acceleration.
In addition, the operators

\nabla
and
\nabla_{p}
are
the spatial and momentum Laplacians,
respectively.
For simplicity,
only the first- and second-order
derivative terms
of the Fokker-Planck equation
are usually retained

(
Huang & Ding 2008
)
, thus
Equation (
4
)
becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}+\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{u}f\right)+\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\bm{a}f\right)=\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa\cdot\nabla f\right)+\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\kappa_{p}\cdot\nabla_{p}f\right).

(5)

In general,
the terms of the
first- and second-order derivative
in Equation (
5
)
can describe most of
the important specific physical effects
of energetic particle transport
in solar wind plasmas,
e.g.,
pitch-angle scattering,
cross-field diffusion,
along-field adiabatic focusing,
adiabatic cooling,
along-field spatial
convection, etc.
To encompasses
all of the aforementioned
physical processes,
the focusing
equation becomes the Fokker-Planck
equation,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}=\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa_{\perp}\cdot\nabla f\right)-\nabla\cdot\left[\left(u\mu\hat{\bm{b}}+\bm{V}\right)f\right]+\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(D_{\mu\mu}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\Bigg\{p^{3}\Bigg[\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)+\mu^{2}\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\Bigg]f\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\Bigg\{\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\Bigg[-\frac{u}{L}-\mu\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-3\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)\Bigg]f\Bigg\},

(6)

where

\kappa_{\perp}
is the perpendicular
diffusion coefficient tensor,

u
is the particle speed,

\mu
is pitch-angle cosine,

\hat{\bm{b}}
is the unit vector
along the background magnetic field,

p
is the magnitude of momentum,

D_{\mu\mu}(\mu)
is
the pitch-angle diffusion
coefficient,
L=\left(\hat{\bm{b}}\cdot\nabla\ln B\right)^{-1}
with
solar mean magneitc field

B

is the
characteristic length of
the adiabatic focusing,
and
\bm{V}
is the solar wind
velocity.
The latter equation satisfies
the conservation law of
particle number, and the detailed
derivative is shown in Appendix

A
.
For convenience, the focusing
equation can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}=\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa_{\perp}\cdot\nabla f\right)-\nabla\cdot\left[\left(u\mu\hat{\bm{b}}+\bm{V}\right)f\right]+\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(D_{\mu\mu}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}\right)+\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left[\frac{u\left(1-\mu^{2}\right)}{2L}f\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\Bigg\{p^{3}\Bigg[\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial{V_{x}}}{\partial{x}}+\frac{\partial{V_{y}}}{\partial{y}}\right)+\mu^{2}\frac{\partial{V_{z}}}{\partial{z}}\Bigg]f\Bigg\}-\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\Bigg[\mu\left(\frac{\partial{V_{x}}}{\partial{x}}+\frac{\partial{V_{y}}}{\partial{y}}-2\frac{\partial{V_{z}}}{\partial{z}}\right)f\Bigg].

(7)

The derivation details from Equation
(
6
)
to (
7
)
are shown in Appendix

B
.

III.1.4
The along-field

To derive the formula

\displaystyle T_{2}=\kappa_{tz}\kappa_{z},

\displaystyle T_{3}=\zeta=\left\langle zV_{z}\right\rangle-\langle z\rangle\left\langle V_{z}\right\rangle,

IV.1
The integrating form

\zeta

In order to investigate the relative

effect on along-field diffusion,

solar wind effect,

\zeta
.
Due to the magnetic freezing effect
of plasmas and the solar rotation,
the mean magnetic field of the sun
presents a spiral form, which is called
the Parker spiral.
For simplicity, we consider it in the
ecliptic plane.
As shown in Figure

1
,
we consider a point
A
with the
solar magnetic field line
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MF}}
going through it.
We set a coordinate system

x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}-z^{\prime}
,
with the
y^{\prime}
-axis pointing
from the sun to
point
A
, the
z^{\prime}
-axis
perpendicular to
the ecliptic plane pointing towards
the north,
the
x^{\prime}
-axis defined using the right-hand rule,
and the origin
O
located at
the center of the sun.
We also set a polar coordinate system
r-\theta
,
with the polar axis
r
tangent to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MF}}

at point
O
,
the polar angle
\theta

defined in a clockwise direction.
In addition, another
coordinate system
x^{\prime\prime}-y’^{\prime}-z^{\prime\prime}

is established by rotating

x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}-z^{\prime}
system
with
\pi/2-\theta_{A}

clockwise through point
O
,
where
\theta_{A}
is the polar angle of point
A
.
Additionly, we set a magnetic
coordinate system
x-y-z
,
with the
z
-axis along
the tangent of
magnetic field line
towards its positive direction,
the
y
-axis parallel to
z^{\prime}
,
and the
x
-axis satisfying
right-hand rule.
Moreover, the tangent
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MF}}

at point
A
is
the straight line

y^{\prime}=kx^{\prime}+r_{0}
with the intercept

r_{0}
, i.e., the distance from
O
to
A
.
We suppose the straight line
can be written as
y^{\prime\prime}=k^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime}+l

in
x^{\prime\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}-z^{\prime\prime}
system.
Furthermore,
normal equation
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MF}}

at point
A

is
y^{\prime}=-x^{\prime}/k+r_{0}
.

Additionly,
the angle between
z
-axis
and
y^{\prime}
-axis is denoted as
\psi
.
Therefore,
the component of
solar wind speed along
solar magnetic field can
be expressed as

\displaystyle V_{z}=V\cos\psi,

(46)

where

is the magnitude
of the solar wind velocity

\bm{V}

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\cos\psi=\left(1+\frac{\omega^{2}r^{2}}{V^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}.

(47)

Here,

is the radial distance from
the center of the sun
to the point on
solar magentic field.
Thus,
the influence
\zeta
of
the solar wind
can be written as

\displaystyle\zeta=\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\mathrm{d}zF(z,t)V\left(1+\frac{\omega^{2}r^{2}}{V^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}-\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\mathrm{d}zzF(z,t)\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\mathrm{d}zF(z,t)V\left(1+\frac{\omega^{2}r^{2}}{V^{2}}\right)^{-1/2},

(48)

where
z_{1}
and
z_{2}

The integrals in Equation
(

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\mathrm{d}F(z,t)=1.

(49)

\displaystyle V\left(1+k^{2}\right)\int_{-0.1r_{0}}^{0.1r_{0}}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}x^{\prime}F(x^{\prime},t)\left(1+\frac{\omega^{2}\left[x^{\prime 2}+(kx^{\prime}+r_{0})^{2}\right]}{V^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\zeta=

\displaystyle\frac{2k\frac{V^{2}}{\omega}}{\sqrt{\left(0.1+\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}k\right)^{2}\left(1+k^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\left(1+k^{2}\right)}+\frac{V^{2}}{\omega^{2}r_{0}^{2}}}+\sqrt{\left(-0.1+\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}k\right)^{2}\left(1+k^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\left(1+k^{2}\right)}+\frac{V^{2}}{\omega^{2}r_{0}^{2}}}}

\displaystyle-\frac{5k}{\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}\frac{V^{2}}{\omega}\ln\frac{\left(0.1+\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}k\right)\sqrt{1+k^{2}}+\sqrt{\left(0.1+\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}k\right)^{2}\left(1+k^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\left(1+k^{2}\right)}+\frac{V^{2}}{\omega^{2}r_{0}^{2}}}}{\left(-0.1+\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}k\right)\sqrt{1+k^{2}}+\sqrt{\left(-0.1+\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}k\right)^{2}\left(1+k^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\left(1+k^{2}\right)}+\frac{V^{2}}{\omega^{2}r_{0}^{2}}}}.

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION

In this paper,
starting from the focusing equation,
we derive the formulas of the
mean square displacement definitions
of along- and cross-field diffusion coefficients,

\kappa_{zz}^{DV}

and
\kappa_{xx}^{DV}
,
respectively.
It is demonstrated
that

\kappa_{zz}^{DV}
includes
solar wind effect, i.e.,

\zeta=\left\langle zV_{z}\right\rangle-\left\langle z\right\rangle\left\langle V_{z}\right\rangle
,
and
\kappa_{xx}^{DV}

contains

\eta=\left\langle xV_{x}\right\rangle-\langle x\rangle\langle V_{x}\rangle
.

For different limits of
dimensionless quantities

\alpha_{1}=V/(\omega r_{0})
and
\alpha_{2}=|k|
,
the relative importance of
the solar wind effect
on along-field diffusion,
\zeta
,
is expored, and corresponding
dimensionless quantities
are obtained, which
are summarized
in Table
1
.
For the condition

\alpha_{1}\sim\alpha_{2}\gg 1
,
we find that when the point
A

is close to the sun,
the relative
importance of solar wind effect
on along-field diffusion,
\zeta
,
should be taken into account.
In this condition,
we find that
when
\xi=\lambda/L\geq 1
,
the adiabatic focusing effects
need also to be considered.
Next,
the relative importance of
solar wind effect on
cross-field diffusion,
\eta
,
is investigated
in several extreme
conditions, we find that
the solar wind effect
on cross-field diffusion,

\eta
, might be ignored.

The results obtained in this paper
have certain significance
in the transport of energetic
particles
not only in
the heliosphere, but also
in many other scenarios,
such as, planetary
magnetoshere and ionoshere,
intersteller space,
the spaces close to
neutron stars,
supernova remanents and so on.
It is possible that
in some conditions,
the solar wind
effect on energetic particle diffusion
in the heliosphere is not important, but
the background plasma
speed effect on energetic
particle diffusion
in other scenarios
is not ignorable.

In this article,
the results we get
are not very conclusive.
We only perform the
exploration in
some extreme conditions.
In addition,
we set some
paramters with
typical values as shown in
Section

III
,
which are standard
in
1
AU for energetic
particles. However,
using the typical parameter values,
we discuss solar wind effect
on diffusion coefficients
in some special conditions,
e.g.,
r_{0}\ll 1
AU,
in which the typical parameter
values may be not appropriate
in the condition.
In the future, we will
investigate solar wind effect
on diffusion coefficients
in more general conditions,
using more reasonalble typical
parameter values in special
conditions.

Other physical effects,
e.g.,
solar wind of spatial derivative,
momentum transport, etc.,
on both spatial diffusion
and drift coefficients,
may also be investigated.
In past decades, the non-diffusion,
i.e., subdiffusion
and superdiffusion,
has gained more and more interest
due to its general applications
in numerous research field.
We may also investigate
the influence of
the solar wind and
adiabatic focusing
on non-diffusion of energetic
particles.

Appendix A
The focusing equation
satisfying particle number
conservation law

The focusing equation, which has been
broadly used to research charged particle
transport, is as follow

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa_{\perp}\cdot\nabla f\right)-\left(u\mu\hat{\bm{b}}+\bm{V}\right)\cdot\nabla f+\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(D_{\mu\mu}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}\right)+\frac{dp}{dt}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p}}+\frac{d\mu}{dt}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}

(A-1)

with

\displaystyle\frac{dp}{dt}=p\Bigg[\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)+\mu^{2}\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\Bigg],

(A-2)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu}{dt}=\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\Bigg[-\frac{u}{L}-\mu\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-3\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)\Bigg].

(A-3)

Here,
L=\left(\hat{\bm{b}}\cdot\nabla\ln B\right)^{-1}
.
However, this formulation
does not satisfy particle number
conservation law
which is one of the most important
physical laws. Therefore,
we have to find a focusing equation
which satisfies the particle number
conservation law.

For spherical system
(p,\theta,\phi)
, the nabla operator of momentum
space is

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}f=\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p}}\bm{e}_{p}+\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\theta}}\bm{e}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{p\sin\theta}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\phi}}\bm{e}_{\phi}.

(A-4)

In this paper, we only consider gyrotropic case, and
Equation (
A-4
) becomes

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}f=\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p}}\bm{e}_{p}+\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\theta}}\bm{e}_{\theta}.

(A-5)

Because
\mu=\cos\theta
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\theta}}=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\frac{\partial{\mu}}{\partial{\theta}}=-\sin\theta\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}.

(A-6)

Inserting the latter equation into Equation
(
A-5
) gives

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}f=\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p}}\bm{e}_{p}-\sin\theta\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}\bm{e}_{\theta}.

(A-7)

To proceed, we have

\displaystyle\bm{a}\cdot\nabla_{p}f=\left(a_{p}\bm{e}_{p}+a_{\theta}\bm{e}_{\theta}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p}}\bm{e}_{p}-\frac{\sin\theta}{p}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}\bm{e}_{\theta}\right)=a_{p}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p}}-a_{\theta}\frac{\sin\theta}{p}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}

(A-8)

With the following formula

\displaystyle\bm{a}\cdot\nabla_{p}f=\frac{dp}{dt}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p}}+\frac{d\mu}{dt}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}},

(A-9)

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dp}{dt}=a_{p},

(A-10)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu}{dt}=-a_{\theta}\frac{\sin\theta}{p}.

(A-11)

That is, the latter formulas can be
rewritten as

\displaystyle a_{p}=\frac{dp}{dt},

(A-12)

\displaystyle a_{\theta}=-\frac{d\mu}{dt}\frac{p}{\sin\theta}.

(A-13)

Considering Equations (
A-2
) and (
A-3
), we can find that

\displaystyle a_{p}=p\Bigg[\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)+\mu^{2}\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\Bigg],

(A-14)

\displaystyle a_{\theta}=\frac{p}{\sin\theta}\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\Bigg[\frac{u}{L}+\mu\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-3\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)\Bigg].

(A-15)

Inserting formulas
(
A-12
) and (
A-13
)
into the following equation

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\bm{a}f\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\left(p^{2}a_{p}f\right)-\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(\sin\theta a_{\theta}f\right),

(A-16)

we can obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\bm{a}f\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\Bigg\{p^{3}\Bigg[\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)+\mu^{2}\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\Bigg]f\Bigg\}

(A-17)

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\Bigg\{\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\Bigg[-\frac{u}{L}-\mu\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-3\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)\Bigg]f\Bigg\}.

The Fokker-Planck equation
is shown as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}+\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{v}f\right)+\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\bm{a}f\right)=\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa\cdot\nabla f\right)+\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\kappa\cdot\nabla_{p}f\right),

(A-18)

which satisfies the particle number
conservation law.
By considering the latter formula
and the following fomulas

\displaystyle\bm{v}=u\mu\hat{\bm{b}}+\bm{V},

(A-19)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa\cdot\nabla f\right)=\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa_{\perp}\cdot\nabla f\right),

(A-20)

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\kappa\cdot\nabla_{p}f\right)=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(D_{\mu\mu}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}\right),

(A-21)

Equation (
A-18
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}=\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa_{\perp}\cdot\nabla f\right)-\nabla\cdot\left[\left(u\mu\hat{\bm{b}}+\bm{V}\right)f\right]+\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(D_{\mu\mu}\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\mu}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\Bigg\{p^{3}\Bigg[\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)+\mu^{2}\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\Bigg]f\Bigg\}

(A-22)

which is our starting point of this
paper.

In fact, if the incompressible
condition

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{v}+\nabla_{p}\cdot\bm{a}=0

(A-23)

holds, Equation (
A-1
) can also
satisfy particle number
conservation. Now, the specific
form of the incompressible
condition is derived.
With the divergence formula

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{A}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{r}}\left(r^{2}f_{r}\right)+\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\theta}}\left(\sin\theta f_{\theta}\right)+\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial{f_{\phi}}}{\partial{\phi}},

(A-24)

for gyrotropic case the latter formula
becomes

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{A}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{r}}\left(r^{2}f_{r}\right)+\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\theta}}\left(\sin\theta f_{\theta}\right)

(A-25)

Using
\mu=\cos\theta
, the latter
formula can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{A}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{r}}\left(r^{2}f_{r}\right)-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(\sin\theta f_{\theta}\right).

(A-26)

In addition, the divergence formula
for velocity is shown as

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{v}=\nabla\cdot\left(u\mu\hat{\bm{b}}+\bm{V}\right)=\nabla\cdot\bm{V}.

(A-27)

Similarly,
with Equations (
A-12
)
and (
A-13
),
the divergence formula
for acceleration can be obtained

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}\cdot\bm{a}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\left(p^{2}a_{p}\right)-\frac{1}{p}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{\mu}}\left(\sin\theta a_{\theta}\right)

(A-28)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{p}}\Bigg\{p^{3}\Bigg[\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{V}-\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\right)+\mu^{2}\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}\Bigg]\Bigg\}

can be simplified as

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}\cdot\bm{a}=\nabla\cdot\bm{V}+\left(3\mu^{2}-1\right)\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}+\frac{u\mu}{L}.

(A-29)

Combining Equations
(
A-27
) and
(
A-29

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{v}+\nabla_{p}\cdot\bm{a}=2\nabla\cdot\bm{V}+\left(3\mu^{2}-1\right)\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}+\frac{u\mu}{L}=0

(A-30)

Thus, the condition of particle number
conservation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-\frac{u\mu}{L}=2\nabla\cdot\bm{V}+\left(3\mu^{2}-1\right)\hat{\bm{b}}\hat{\bm{b}}:\nabla\bm{V}

(A-31)

If the latter condition
is satisfied, Equation
(
A-1
)
also satisfies the particle number
conservation law.

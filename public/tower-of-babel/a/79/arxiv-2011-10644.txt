Interacting hard-sphere fluids in an external field
Benaoumeur Bakhti, Gerhard Müller

Abstract
We present a new method for studying equilibrium properties of interacting fluids in an arbitrary external field. The fluid is composed of monodisperse spherical particles with hard-core repulsion and additional interactions of arbitrary shape and limited range. Our method of analysis is exact in one dimension and provides demonstrably good approximations in higher dimensions. It can cope with homogeneous and inhomogeneous environments. We derive an equation for the pair distribution function. The solution, to be evaluated numerically, in general, or analytically for special cases, enters expressions for the entropy and free energy functionals. For some one-dimensional systems, our approach yields analytic solutions, reproducing available exact results from different approaches.

Introduction

Fluids of interacting particles, such as sticky or ionic fluids, exhibit broadly varied and complex phenomena, even more so in the inhomogeneous environment of an external field.
Although several experimental works have well characterized the complex behavior of inhomogeneous interacting fluids, a complete microscopic description of the observed behavior is still missing.

Constructing a good approximation of density functionals for more general interactions remains an open question.

To a large extent, the complex behavior of real fluids stems from (i) the interaction potentials and (ii) the shapes of constituent particles.
In this work we aim to extend the DFT for fluids of hard bodies beyond the hard-sphere models in the line of aspect (i) by introducing nearest-neighbour interactions of arbitrary profile and coupling strength and with a maximum range of two molecular diameters.

Inspired by work of Percus on hard rods

The close connection between radial distribution functions and neutron scattering cross sections makes the former a desirable object of theoretical and computational investigations.

II
Pair distribution function

Direct correlation functions (DCF) are of central interest in DFT.
Generally, the DCF can be determined from the free energy functional via second derivative with respect to the particle density.
If the free energy functional is not known, it can be determined from the Ornstein-Zernike (OZ) equation.
But here a closure relation is required between the two unknowns of the OZ equation, namely the DCF and the total correlation function.
Different closure approximations have been proposed to solve the OZ equation, including the Percus-Yevick
PY58
, hypernetted chain
LGB59
;
RA79
, Born-Green
BG46
and mean-spherical-model approximations
LP66
.
With the DCFs thus calculated, free-energy functionals in various approximations are then being constructed and evaluated.

The route we take in this work is different and does not rely on any closure relation.
It aims instead for a hitherto unused relation between the particle density,

\displaystyle\rho(\mathbf{r})\doteq\left\langle\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{r})\right\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{i})\right\rangle.

(3)

and the PDF
note3
,

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{r})\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\left\langle\sum_{i,j\neq i}\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{i})\delta(\mathbf{r^{\prime}}-\mathbf{r}_{j})\right\rangle,

(4)

where
\langle\hskip 2.56073pt\rangle
denotes the canonical ensemble average.

For the establishment of this relation between
\rho(\mathbf{r})
and
\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})
, we introduce three auxiliary distribution functions (ADFs) defined as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\doteq\left\langle\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\left[1-\int_{V^{\prime}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\hat{\rho}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\right]\right\rangle,

(5)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})\doteq\left\langle\left[1-\int_{V}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\hat{\rho}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1})\right]\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})\right\rangle,

(6)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{0}(\mathbf{r})\doteq\left\langle\!\left[1-\!\!\int_{V}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\hat{\rho}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1})\right]\!\!\left[1-\!\!\int_{V^{\prime}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\hat{\rho}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\right]\!\right\rangle,

where the regions of integration are explained in Fig.

and

.

These expressions can be interpreted as the probability density (
5
) for a particle to be present at position

in region

) for no particle to be present inside the combined region

The ADFs are not independent of each other.
All three can be expressed as functionals of the density (

) and the PDF (

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})=\rho(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})-\int_{V^{\prime}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}),

(8)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})=\rho(\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})-\int_{V}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\rho^{(2)}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}}),

(9)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{0}(\mathbf{r})=1-\int_{V_{T}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}\rho(\bar{\mathbf{r}})

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\int_{V}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\int_{V^{\prime}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rho^{(2)}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1},\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}).

Next we relate the PDF and the three ADFs to the joint probability distribution (JDF) at thermal equilibrium,

\displaystyle p(\mathbf{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{r}_{N})=Z^{-1}e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{r}_{N})},

(12)

is the canonical partition function and

Keeping in mind that here we are using a canonical ensemble, the four distribution functions can be represented as follows:

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z}\frac{1}{N(N-1)}

\displaystyle\hskip-51.21495pt\times e^{-\beta[\phi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})+V_{ex}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})+V_{ex}(\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})]}\int_{\mathcal{V^{\prime}}}\!\!d^{N-2}\mathbf{r}\,e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{1}},

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{0}(\mathbf{r})\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})}{\tilde{\rho}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\tilde{\rho}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})}=e^{-\beta\phi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})}\frac{A(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})}{B(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})},

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})=e^{-\beta\phi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})}\frac{\Bigl[\rho(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})-\int_{V^{\prime}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\Bigr]\Bigl[\rho(\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})-\int_{V}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\rho^{(2)}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})\Bigr]}{\Bigl[1-\int_{V_{T}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}\rho(\bar{\mathbf{r}})+\int_{V}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\int_{V^{\prime}}d\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2}\rho^{(2)}(\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{1},\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{2})\Bigr]}\quad:~\sigma\leq|\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j}|<\xi.

(21)

For specific 1D models this implicit relation can be solved into an explicit expression for the PDF as a functional of the particle density.
In general, we must solve Eq. (
20

The numerical results shown below in Sec.

justify the strength of the approximation

the highly structured oscillations appearing in density profiles can be captured only by an advanced DFT approximation such as the fundamental measure theory (FMT), but not by a simple DFT approximation such as the local density approximation (a mean field theory) and can only be partially captured by the weighted density approximation.

The radial distribution function and total correlation function follow directly:

g(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})=\frac{\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})}{\rho(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\rho(\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})},\quad h(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})=g(\mathbf{r^{\prime}},\mathbf{r^{\prime\prime}})-1.

(22)

Even though the derivation of (

III
Free-energy and entropy functionals

DFT expresses the free energy (grand potential) as a functional,

\tilde{\Omega}=\tilde{\Omega}[\tilde{V}_{ex},\phi],

(23)

of the external potential modified by the chemical potential,

\tilde{V}_{ex}(\mathbf{r})\doteq\mu-V_{ex}(\mathbf{r}),

(24)

and the interaction potential
\phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})
such as introduced in (
2
).
The density
\rho(\mathbf{r})
and the PDF
\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})
are extracted from (
23
) via functional derivatives:

\displaystyle\rho(\mathbf{r})=-\frac{\delta\tilde{\Omega}[\tilde{V}_{ex},\phi]}{\delta\tilde{V}_{ex}},\quad\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\tilde{\Omega}[\tilde{V}_{ex},\phi]}{\delta\phi}.

(25)

The functions
\rho(\mathbf{r})
and
\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})
are conjugate to the functions
\tilde{V}_{ex}(\mathbf{r})
and
\phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})
, respectively, in a thermodynamic sense.
Performing a Legendre transform on (
23
) yields the expression

,

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{r}\,\tilde{V}_{ex}(\mathbf{r})\,\frac{\delta\tilde{\Omega}}{\delta\tilde{V}_{ex}(\mathbf{r})}+\int d\mathbf{r}\int d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}\,\phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\,\frac{\delta\tilde{\Omega}}{\delta\phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})}-\Omega

\displaystyle=-\mu\int d\mathbf{r}\rho(\mathbf{r})+\int d\mathbf{r}\,V_{ex}(\mathbf{r})\rho(\mathbf{r})+\int d\mathbf{r}\int d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}\,\phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})-\tilde{\Omega}=TS[\rho,\rho^{(2)}].

(26)

The last equation in (
III
), which produces the entropy functional, is evident if we identify the first term as
-G
(Gibbs free energy) and the sum of the next two terms as
U
(internal energy).
For what follows, we introduce two kinds of entropy density functionals by writing,

S[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]=\int d\mathbf{r}\int d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}\,\tilde{S}[\rho(\mathbf{r}),\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})],\quad\bar{S}[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]=\int d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}\,\tilde{S}[\rho(\mathbf{r}),\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})].

(27)

The free-energy as a functional of density and PDF now reads,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}=\Omega[\rho(\mathbf{r}),\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})]=\int d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}\rho^{(2)}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})+\int d\mathbf{r}\,\rho(\mathbf{r})V_{ex}(\mathbf{r})-TS[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]-\mu\int d\mathbf{r}\,\rho(\mathbf{r}).

(28)

The external potential and the interaction potential can be extracted from the entropy functional as,

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{ex}(\mathbf{r})=-T\frac{\delta\bar{S}[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]}{\delta\rho},\quad\phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})=T\frac{\delta\tilde{S}[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]}{\delta\rho^{(2)}}.

(29)

An alternative route to Eqs. (
29

28

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Omega[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]}{\delta\rho}=0,\quad\frac{\delta\Omega[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]}{\delta\rho^{(2)}}=0,

(30)

previously employed in different contexts by Gonis et al.

;

and Bakhti

Equations (

Conversely, if the external potential and the interaction potential are known, the density and the PDF can be determined by integrating Eqs. (

We can thus start from the second Eq. (

IV
Exact analysis for 1D systems

The entropy and free-energy expressions developed above are amenable to an exact analysis for 1D systems with specific interactions.
Consider a system of hard rods of length
\sigma

the interaction potential (
2

Equation (

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}(y,y^{\prime})=e^{-\beta\phi(y,y^{\prime})}\,\frac{\Bigl[\rho(y)-\int dy^{\prime}_{1}\,\rho^{(2)}(y,y^{\prime}_{1})\Bigr]\Bigl[\rho(y^{\prime})-\int dy^{\prime}_{1}\,\rho^{(2)}(y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime})\Bigr]}{\Bigl[1-\int dy^{\prime}_{1}\,\rho(y^{\prime}_{1})+\int dy^{\prime}_{1}\int dy^{\prime}_{2}\,\rho^{(2)}(y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2})\Bigr]}

\displaystyle=-\int dy\!\left\{\Bigl[\rho(y)-\int dy^{\prime}\rho^{(2)}(y,y^{\prime})\Bigr]\ln\Bigl(\rho(y)-\int dy^{\prime}\rho^{(2)}(y,y^{\prime})\Bigr)\right.

\displaystyle\beta\Omega[\rho,\rho^{(2)}]

\displaystyle=\int dy\Bigl\{\rho(y)\ln\Bigl(\rho(y)-\int dy^{\prime}\rho^{(2)}(y,y^{\prime})\Bigr)+\rho(y)\ln\Bigl(\rho(y)-\int dy^{\prime}\rho^{(2)}(y^{\prime},y)\Bigr)

The entropy expression (

Given the completely different nature of the two approaches, this is a remarkable convergence.
In addition to the mathematical interest that exact solutions draw quite generally, in the present context they are also of practical interest.
Within the DFT formalism, exact solutions for 1D systems present themselves as ingredients in the construction of approximate functionals in higher dimensions.
Moreover, 3D systems that are inhomogeneous in only one direction are often modeled (with tacit caveats) as effectively 1D systems.

Our approach has a remarkable simplicity for what it is capable to deliver.
It certainly is much simpler than the inverse-problem approach.
There is a straightforward path to extend our method to situations with general nearest-neighbor and next-nearest-neighbor interactions.
This can be accomplished by introducing three-point auxiliary distribution functions.
Contact can then be made with the inverse-problem approach of Percus on a wider scope

Perc97
;
Wert84

Wert86

One strength of our approach is that the higher-dimensional functionals are constructed without the FMT reference to dimensional crossover and zero-dimensional cavity.

V
Applications

First we investigate the sticky-hard-sphere (SHS) model in the presence of an attractive Lennard-Jones (LJ) wall.
Second we investigate the effect of gravity on the SHS system.
These two systems are of great importance for studying wetting transitions, sedimentation, and interfacial phenomena in interacting colloids.
Our focus is on density profiles pertaining to the 3D case.

A more systematic study of this system, including effects of longer-range interactions and lower dimensionality, presently in the works
Bakhti-et-al
, is of great importance for studying glass transition under gravity.
The SHS model
Baxter:1968
has proven to be realistic for many physical phenomena including crystallization of polymers
Hoy/OHern:2010
, micelles
Amokrane/Regnaut:1997
, protein solutions
Braun:2002
, DNA coated colloids
Xu/etal:2011
;
Dreyfus/etal:2009
, and ionic fluids
Stell:1995
.
In the SHS model, the interaction between colloidal particles is limited to an adhesive force upon contact.

It is well known that a significant attractive interaction between colloids results from the depletion forces, which come into play when polymer globules or micelles are added to a colloidal suspension.
The range and strength of the attraction can be varied continuously and independently by adjusting, respectively, the concentration and size of the polymer.
For weak depletion, the colloidal particles are well described by the SHS model.

Consider the square-well interaction potential,

\displaystyle\beta\phi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+\infty&:~0<r<\sigma,\\
-\ln\left[\sigma/12\tau(\sigma-\xi)\right]&:~\sigma<r<\xi,\\
0&:~r>\xi,\end{array}\right.

of width
\xi
, where
\tau
has been named Baxter temperature..
In the limit SHS limit
\xi\rightarrow\sigma
we can write,

\displaystyle e^{-\beta\phi(r)}=\theta(r-\sigma)+\frac{\sigma}{12\tau}\delta(r-\sigma).

(45)

Noninteracting hard spheres are recovered for
\tau\rightarrow\infty
.

V.2
SHS model with hard walls

To make contact with previous results of the density functional formalism,
we switch off the LJ attraction and consider the SHS system confined by two hard walls.
In DFT, the free-energy functional (
III

\displaystyle\Omega[\rho(\mathbf{r})]=F_{1}[\rho(\mathbf{r})]+F_{0}[\rho(\mathbf{r})]+\int d\mathbf{r}\big\{V_{ex}(\mathbf{r})-\mu\big\}\rho(\mathbf{r})

(53)

where

\displaystyle F_{0}[\rho(\mathbf{r})]=\beta^{-1}\int d\mathbf{r}\rho(\mathbf{r})\left\{\ln\left[\rho(\mathbf{r})\Lambda^{3}\right]-1\right\}

(54)

is the ideal part and

The excess part

To determine the density profiles and the radial distribution functions (pair correlation functions), the minimization equation is written in a form more suitable for numerical implementation,

\displaystyle\rho(\mathbf{r})=\rho_{b}\exp\left[-\beta V_{ex}(\mathbf{r})+c^{(1)}[\rho(\mathbf{r}),\mathbf{r}]+\beta\mu_{1}\right],

\displaystyle c^{(1)}[\rho(\mathbf{r}),\mathbf{r}]=-\beta\frac{\delta F_{1}[\rho(\mathbf{r})]}{\delta\rho(\mathbf{r})},

\displaystyle\mu_{1}=c_{0}^{(1)}(\rho_{b})=\mu-\beta^{-1}\ln\left(\rho_{b}\Lambda^{3}\right).

55

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{r})=\exp\left[-\beta\phi(\mathbf{r})+c^{(1)}[\rho_{b}g(\mathbf{r})]+\beta\mu_{1}\right],

There are two well known procedures to get the bulk or inhomogeneous equation of state from the free energy functional Eq. (

\displaystyle\beta p(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}(\mathbf{r})\frac{\delta\Phi[\rho(\mathbf{r})]}{\delta\rho_{i}(\mathbf{r})}-\Phi[\rho(\mathbf{r})],

VI
Conclusion

We have introduced a new approach for studying the thermodynamics of interacting hard-sphere fluids in an arbitrary external field.
The key accomplishment is a functional relation between density and PDF that works for cases of inhomogeneous fluids with general finite-range nearest-neighbor interactions.
We have been able to construct from this functional relation explicit entropy and free-energy functionals.

For the special 1D case of hard rods confined to a narrow channel and interacting upon contact, our results coincide with Percus’ results
Perc89
obtained via rigorous analysis.
Exact solutions in explicit form can then be worked out.
To test the strength of our approximation in higher dimensions, we have considered a system of sticky hard spheres with different external fields.

The results demonstrate that our approach is in good agreement with corresponding results inferred from commonly used approaches such as Percus-Yevick/OZ and the hypernetted chain/OZ equation and also with Monte Carlo simulation.
Extensions of this work to mixtures of interacting fluids in arbitrary, inhomogeneous external fields are in progress.
Extensions to molecules of different sizes and shapes, which require the inclusion of orientational degrees of freedom, are within the range of our methodology.

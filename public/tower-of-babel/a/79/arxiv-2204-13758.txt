Using edt0l systems to solve some equations in the solvable Baumslag-Solitar groups
Andrew Duncan, Alex Evetts, Derek F. Holt, Sarah Rees

Abstract
We investigate the solution sets to equations in the solvable Baumslag-Solitar groups B ​ S ​ ( 1 , k ) BS(1,k) , k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , and show that these sets are represented by edt0l languages in some cases. In particular, we prove that the multiplication table of such a group forms an edt0l language with respect to a specific natural normal form for group elements.

Definition 1

Let

be a finite alphabet. A

of

whose application replaces each instance of

,

We use the conventions that (1) when a table is applied to a word it must be applied to every letter within that word and (2) if a table does not specify a rewrite for some

A table will act on the right of a word, so we write
w{\hbox{\sc t}}
for the result of
applying a table

denoted by concatenation; note that when applying a string of tables that string should be read from left to right.
Note that if the right hand side of each rule in a table contains only one
word, then that table defines a free monoid endomorphism of
\mathcal{V}^{*}
.

2.

3.

4.

The solvable Baumslag-Solitar groups: definition and normal forms

We use the notation
{\mathbb{N}}:=\{n:n\in{\mathbb{Z}},n>0\}
,
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
.

The solvable Baumslag-Solitar groups are the groups defined by the
presentations

\mathsf{BS}(1,k)=\langle a,b\mid b^{-1}ab=a^{k}\rangle

for some fixed
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
. We shall assume here that
k>0
, since
the groups with
k<0
are similar, but with additional minor complications.
Although we have not checked all of the details, we believe that all of
the results proved in this section remain true when
k
is negative, but
their proofs require subdivisions into more cases than when
k>0
.
We shall assume further that
k>1
, since
\mathsf{BS}(1,1)
is free abelian.
For ease of notation, we shall abbreviate
\mathsf{BS}(1,k)
as
\mathsf{BS}
.

Note that
\mathsf{BS}
is a split extension of the infinite abelian group

N=\langle a\rangle^{\mathsf{BS}}
(the normal closure of the subgroup

\langle a\rangle
in
\mathsf{BS}
) by the infinite cyclic group
\langle b\rangle
.
We denote by
a^{\frac{1}{k}}
the element
bab^{-1}
and note that

(a^{\frac{1}{k}})^{k}=a
. Similarly we represent

, where

,
i\neq 0
,

.
We also write
a
for
a^{-1}
, and
b
for
b^{-1}
.

With this notation, we observe that any element

of
\mathsf{BS}
can be represented
as a product

\alpha\in\{a,{\hbox{\sc a}}\}
, where

and we interpret

as the product

\displaystyle(b^{m}\alpha b^{-m})^{i_{m}}(b^{m-1}\alpha b^{-m+1})^{i_{m-1}}\cdots(b\alpha b^{-1})^{i_{1}}\alpha^{s}.

We require that

(that is, the sequence

We shall call

of the

The fractional representation

The group element

\left.\begin{aligned} &\beta\cdots\beta+i_{m}i_{m-1}\cdots i_{1}{\centerdot}s_{0}s_{1}\cdots s_{p}\,{\rm when}\ \alpha=a,\\

Then the suffix

is precisely the representation of

The symbol

We shall call the subwords

We denote the representation of

We can think of

Now the product

When

We denote the representation of

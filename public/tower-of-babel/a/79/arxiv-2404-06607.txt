A sharp bound for the first Robin-Dirichlet eigenvalue
Nunzia Gavitone, Gianpaolo Piscitelli

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian on doubly connected domains when Robin and Dirichlet conditions are imposed on the outer and the inner part of the boundary, respectively. We provide that the spherical shell reaches the maximum of the first eigenvalue of this problem among a suitable class of domains when the measure, the outer perimeter and inner ( n − 1 ) th (n-1)^{\text{th}} quermassintegral are fixed. MSC 2020: 35B40, 35J25, 35P15. Keywords : Laplacian eigenvalues, Robin-Dirichlet boundary conditions, inner parallel method, web-functions. Communicated by Irena Lasiecka.



1.
Introduction

In the recent literature, the study of shape optimization eigenvalue problems for the Laplacian in doubly connected domains, has been widely developed. In this kind of problems, different conditions can be imposed on the two components of the boundary and under suitable geometric constraints, it is worth asking whether the spherical shell is the optimal shape. The study of the shape optimization problems of the first Laplacian eigenvalue in doubly connected domains, strictly depends on the conditions imposed on the two parts of the boundary. More precisely, let
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be two open, connected, bounded sets such that
\Omega_{0}
has Lipschitz boundary and
\Theta\Subset\Omega_{0}
. Then we consider the annular set
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
.

In this paper we study the first Robin-Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian, that is

(1.1)

\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)\\
\psi\nequiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\psi^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi^{2}dx},

where
\beta>0
is a positive parameter and
H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)
denotes the set of the Sobolev functions in
\Omega
which vanish on the boundary of
\Theta
(see
Section

3
for the precise definition). Any minimizer
u\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
) is a solution to the following problem (see
Section

3
for the details)

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda(\beta,\Omega)\,u&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}u+\beta u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{0}\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Theta,\end{cases}

where
\nu
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega_{0}
and
\partial_{\nu}u
denotes the normal derivative of
u
.

We observe that when
\beta=0
and
\beta=+\infty
, (
1.1
) reduces to the so-called first Neumann-Dirichlet and Dirichlet-Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue, respectively.

In the case
\beta=0
, the first Neumann-Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{ND}(\Omega)
was studied for instance in
[
Her62
,
ABD23
,
DPP20
,
AKK21
]
.
When the hole
\Theta
is convex, in
[
ABD23
,
DPP20
]
the authors proved that the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}=B_{R_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{1}}}
maximizes
\lambda_{ND}(\Omega)
among all the doubly connected domains
\Omega
with given volume and such that the hole
\Theta
has fixed
(n-1)^{\text{th}}
quermassintegral
W_{n-1}
(see
Section

2
for the precise definition). This result in the planar case was proved in
[
Her62
]
and we point out that in this case the constraint on the hole
\Theta
corresponds to fix its perimeter and the maximizer is the annulus.

When
\beta=+\infty
,
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
reduces to the first Dirichlet-Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)
studied for instance in
[
HKK01
,
Kes03
,
ESK08
,
GM18
,
HZ19
,
CR20
,
PPT20
,
CG22
,
CPP24
]
.
In particular, in
[
Her63
]
, the author proved that in the planar case, the annulus maximizes
\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)
among the doubly connected plane domains
\Omega
of area
A
and such that
4\pi A=L_{\text{out}}^{2}-L_{\text{int}}^{2}
where
L_{\text{out}}
and
L_{\text{int}}
are the lengths of the outer and inner boundary respectively. Up to our knowledge, in any dimension this result has been proved in
[
Kes03
]
only for eccentric spherical shells, that is when
\Omega_{0}
and
\Theta
are two Euclidean balls not concentric.

The purpose of this paper is to find optimal upper bound for
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
for any
\beta>0
under suitable geometrical constraints on
\Omega
, in the spirit of the quoted results for
\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)
and
\lambda_{ND}(\Omega)
. More precisely, let us consider the following family of admissible doubly connected sets.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the class of doubly connected domains
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
where
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, are two open, bounded, convex sets and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega|}{\omega_{n}}=\displaystyle\left(\frac{P(\Omega_{0})}{n\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}-\displaystyle\left(\frac{(n-1)W_{n-1}(\Theta)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{n}.

Then the main result of this paper is the following upper bound for
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let be
\beta>0
and let be
\Omega\in\mathcal{S}
. Then

(1.4)

\lambda(\beta,\Omega)\leq\lambda(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})

where
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}=B_{R_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{1}}}
is the spherical shell such that,
P(B_{R_{2}})=P(\Omega_{0})
,
W_{n-1}(B_{R_{1}})=W_{n-1}(\Theta)
and
|A_{R_{1},R_{2}}|=|\Omega|
.

To prove
Theorem

1.2
we use the so-called inner parallel method (introduced by Makai
[
Mak59a
,
Mak59b
]
and Pólya
[
Pól60
]
and then refined by Payne and Weinberger in
[
PW61
]
basing on
[
SN59
]
). In our paper, we use a generalized version of this tool for the higher dimension, the so-called web-functions method (see for instance
[
Gaz99
,
CFG02
,
BNT10
,
BFNT19
,
BNNT22
,
AMPS23
]
and
[
DPG14
]
for the anisotropic case). More precisely, we use a suitable test function which is defined by means of the distance function from the boundary.

Moreover, we point out that in order to prove
Theorem

1.2
we need to study the problem (
1.2
) in the radial case, that is when
\Omega=A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
is the spherical shell. We prove that in this case the first eigenfunctions
v
are radially symmetric that is there exists a function
\phi(r)
such that
v(x)=\phi(|x|)
, and satisfying the following problem (see
Section

3
for the details)

\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\left(\phi^{\prime}(r)r^{n-1}\right)^{\prime}=\lambda(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\phi(r)&\text{ for }r\in(R_{1},R_{2})\\
\phi^{\prime}(R_{2})+\beta\phi(R_{2})=0\\
\phi(R_{1})=0.\end{cases}

In particular, we prove that
\phi(r)
, for any
R_{1}<r<R_{2}
, is not globally monotone and has a unique critical point
\bar{R}\in(R_{1},R_{2})
(see
Proposition

3.2
). This result allows us to study the shape optimization problem related to
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
, by merging both the Robin-Neumann and the Neumann-Dirichlet eigenvalue problems by using the inner parallel method.

Furthermore, since we show that

\lim_{\beta\to+\infty}\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\lambda_{DD}(\Omega),

then
Theorem

1.2
also gives

(1.5)

\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{DD}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}}).

The relationship (
1.5
) extends the Hersch’s result in
[
Her63
]
for
\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)
to any dimension and to a larger class of doubly connected domains than the ones considered in
[
Kes03
]
. Indeed eccentric shells belong in the class of admissible sets
\mathcal{S}
.

The plan of the paper is the following. In
Section

2
, we provide the notation and the preliminaries; in
Section

3
, we investigate the foundation of the problem and we study the radial case; in
Section

4
, we compute the first order shape derivative of
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
in order to prove that the spherical shell is a critical set; finally, in
Section

5
, we prove
Theorem

1.2
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we denote by
B_{r}(x_{0})
and
B_{r}
the balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius
r>0
, centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and at the origin, respectively. Moreover by
B
,
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\omega_{n}
we will denote respectively the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its boundary and its volume.
Let
0<r<R
, then the spherical shell with radii
r
and
R
centered at the origin is denoted as

A_{r,R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon r<|x|<R\}.

Moreover, the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
and, the Euclidean scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded set. For any measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the perimeter of
E
in
D
is (see for instance
[
Mag12
]
)

P(E;D)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}{\rm div}\varphi\>dx:\;\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;||\varphi||_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

The perimeter of
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
P(E)
and, if
P(E)<+\infty
, we say that
E
is a set of finite perimeter. Moreover, if
E
has Lipschitz boundary, it holds

P(E)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial E).

The Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
|E|
.
Moreover, we recall that the inradius of
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as follows

(2.1)

\rho(E)=\sup_{x\in E}\;\inf_{y\in\partial E}|x-y|,

while the diameter of
E
is

\text{diam}(E)=\sup_{x,y\in E}|x-y|.

Let us observe that, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded, convex set, it holds (see for instance
[
DPDBG18
,
Sch14
]
)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\rho(E)}{n}\leq\displaystyle\frac{|E|}{P(E)}\leq\rho(E)

In what follows we recall some properties of convex bodies, which will be useful in the following. For more details on these topics we refer for instance to
[
Kes06
,
Sch14
]
.

Let
K
be a bounded convex open set, the outer parallel body of
K
at distance
\delta>0
is the following Minkowski sum

K+\delta B=\{x+\delta y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;|\;x\in K,\;y\in B\}.

The Steiner formulas assert that

|K+\delta B|=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}W_{i}(K)\delta^{i},

and

P(K+\delta B)=n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}W_{i+1}(K)\delta^{i}.

The coefficients
W_{i}(K)
are known as the quermassintegrals of
K
. In particular, it holds that

W_{0}(K)=\left|K\right|,\quad nW_{1}(K)=P(K),\quad W_{n}(K)=\omega_{n}.

Furthermore, the Aleksandrov-Fenchel inequalities state that

(2.3)

\left(\frac{W_{j}(K)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-j}}\geq\left(\frac{W_{i}(K)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-i}},\quad 0\leq i<j\leq n-1,

where the inequality is replaced by an equality if and only if
K
is a ball.

When
i=0
and
j=1
, (
2.3
) reduces to the classical isoperimetric inequality:

(2.4)

P(K)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|K|^{1-\frac{1}{n}}.

It the sequel it will be useful (
2.3
) in the case
i=1
and
j=2
, that is

(2.5)

W_{2}(K)\geq n^{-\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}P(K)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

Let us denote by
K^{*}
a ball such that
W_{n-1}(K)=W_{n-1}(K^{*})
. Then, by Aleksandrov-Fenchel inequalities (
2.3
), we have

\left(\frac{W_{i}(K)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-i}}\leq\frac{W_{n-1}(K)}{\omega_{n}}=\left(\frac{W_{i}(K^{*})}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-i}},

for
0\leq i<n-1
, and hence

W_{i}(K)\leq W_{i}(K^{*})\quad 0\leq i<n-1.

Finally we recall a useful result whose proof can be found for instance in
[
BNT10
]
and
[
BFNT19
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a bounded, open, convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
f:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a non decreasing
C^{1}
function. Let
u(x):=f(d_{o}(x))
, where
d_{o}
is the distance function from the boundary
\partial\Omega_{0}
and

\begin{split}\mathsf{E}_{t}&:=\{x\in\Omega_{0}\;:\;u(x)>t\}.\\
\end{split}

Then, it holds

(2.6)

-\dfrac{d}{dt}P(\mathsf{E}_{t})\geq n(n-1)\dfrac{W_{2}(\mathsf{E}_{t})}{|Du|_{u=t}}\quad\text{ for a.e. }t\in(0,\rho(\Omega_{0})).

3.
The Robin-Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

Let
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be two open, bounded, connected sets such that
\Omega_{0}
has Lipschitz boundary and
\Theta\Subset\Omega_{0}
. Let us consider the doubly connected set
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
. In this section we study the following eigenvalue problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda\,u&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\partial_{\nu}u+\beta u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{0}\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Theta,\end{cases}

where
\beta>0
is a positive parameter,
\nu
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega_{0}
and
\partial_{\nu}u
denotes the normal derivative of
u
.
Let us consider the set
H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)
of the Sobolev functions in
\Omega
vanishing on
\overline{\Theta}
, that is the closure in
H^{1}(\Omega)
of

C^{\infty}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega):=\{u|_{\Omega}\ |\ u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \spt(u)\cap\partial\Theta=\emptyset\}.

We first give the definition of eigenvalue and eigenfunction to the problem (
3.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

The real number
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of (
3.1
) if and only if there exists a function
z\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)
, not identically zero, such that

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla z,\nabla\varphi\rangle\;dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}z\varphi\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}z\varphi\;dx

for every
\varphi\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)
. The function
u
is called an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda
.

3.1.
The first eigenvalue and basic properties

In this section, we study the main properties of the first eigenvalue to the problem (
3.1
) and of the corresponding eigenfunctions. Let us consider the following quantity

(3.3)

\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)\\
\psi\nequiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\psi^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi^{2}dx}.

By using standard argument of the Calculus of Variations, it is possible to prove that
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
is the first eigenvalue to the problem (
3.1
) and we get the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let be
\beta>0
and
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
, where
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, are two open, bounded, connected sets such that
\Omega_{0}
has Lipschitz boundary and
\Theta\Subset\Omega_{0}
. Then
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
is positive and it is actually a minimum. Moreover, any minimizer
u\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)
of (
3.3
) has constant sign and is a weak solution to the problem (
3.1
).

Furthermore, we are able to prove that the first eigenvalue is simple.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let be
\beta>0
and
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
, where
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, are two open, bounded, connected sets such that
\Omega_{0}
has Lipschitz boundary and
\Theta\Subset\Omega_{0}
. Then
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
is simple, that is all the associated eigenfunctions are scalar multiple of each other.

Proof.

Let
u,v
be two minimizers of (
3.3
). By
Proposition

3.2
we can assume that
u
is positive in
\Omega
. Then we have

\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,dx>0.

Therefore, we can find a real constant
\chi
such that

(3.4)

\int_{\Omega}(u(x)-\chi v(x))\,dx=0.

Since
u-\chi v
is still a solution of the problem (
3.3
), then we necessarily have that
u\equiv\chi v
in
\Omega
and the simplicity of
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
follows.
∎

Finally, we prove that all eigenfunctions with constant sign are the first ones.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let be
\beta>0
and
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
, where
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, are two open, bounded, connected sets such that
\Omega_{0}
has Lipschitz boundary and
\Theta\Subset\Omega_{0}
. Then, any positive function
z\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta}(\Omega)
that satisfies (
3.1
), in the sense of Definition
3.1
,
is a first eigenfunction.

Proof.

Let
u
be a positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
; we have

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\;dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\lambda(\beta,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, choosing
\varphi=\displaystyle\frac{u^{2}}{z+\varepsilon}
in (
3.2
), we get

(3.6)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{2u}{z+\varepsilon}\left(\nabla z\cdot\nabla u\right)-\frac{u^{2}}{(z+\varepsilon)^{2}}|\nabla z|^{2}\right]\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\frac{zu^{2}}{z+\varepsilon}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{zu^{2}}{z+\varepsilon}\;dx.\end{split}

Subtracting (
3.5
) by (
3.6
), being
\displaystyle\frac{z}{z+\varepsilon}<1
, we get

0\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u-\frac{u}{z+\varepsilon}\nabla z\right|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\lambda(\beta,\Omega)-\lambda\frac{z}{z+\varepsilon}\right)u^{2}\;dx.

Passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we get

\left(\lambda(\beta,\Omega)-\lambda\right)\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx\geq 0.

Since
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
is the smallest eigenvalue, this implies that
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\lambda
. Therefore, by the simplicity of the first eigenvalue,
z=u
up to a multiplicative constant.
∎

3.2.
The behavior with respect to
\beta

In this section, we study the behavior of the first eigenvalue (
3.3
) with respect to the Robin parameter.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let be
\beta>0
and
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
, where
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, are two open, bounded, connected sets such that
\Omega_{0}
has Lipschitz boundary and
\Theta\Subset\Omega_{0}
. Then,
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
is non-decreasing, continuous and differentiable with respect to
\beta
and it holds

\frac{d}{d\beta}\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\;dx},

where
u
is a minimizer of (
3.3
).

Proof.

The monotonicity with respect to
\beta
is obvious. For the proof of the differentiability refer to
[
DPP20
, Prop. 2.3]
.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

The previous result gives

(3.7)

\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\lambda_{{ND}}(\Omega),

where
\lambda_{{ND}}(\Omega)
is the first Neumann-Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian (see for instance
[
Her62
,
ABD23
,
DPP20
]
).

In what follows, we prove some estimates on
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
in the spirit of the ones contained in
[
GS23
]
for the first Steklov-Robin Laplacian eigenvalue (see also
[
DBG22
]
and
[
KS69
]
).

Theorem 3.7
.

Let be
\beta>0
and
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Theta}
, where
\Omega_{0},\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, are two open, bounded, connected sets such that
\Omega_{0}
has Lipschitz boundary and
\Theta\Subset\Omega_{0}
.
Then, the following estimates hold

(3.8)

\frac{1}{\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda(\beta,\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\beta q(\Omega)},

where
\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega)
is the first Dirichlet-Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\Omega
and
q(\Omega)
is

(3.9)

q(\Omega)=\inf_{\displaystyle\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta w=0\\
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\,dx}.

Proof.

Firstly, let us stress that by the trace embedding theorem the quantity
q(\Omega)
defined in (
3.9
) is strictly positive. Let
u
be a positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
and let
h
be the solution to the following problem

\begin{cases}\Delta h=0&\text{in}\,\Omega\\
h=0&\text{on}\,\partial\Theta\\
\displaystyle h=u&\text{on}\,\partial\Omega_{0}.\end{cases}

Let us set
v:=u-h
, then we immediately get

(3.10)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx.

Denoted by
D(u)=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx
, by using the Minkowski inequality, (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we get

\begin{split}\sqrt{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx}&\leq\sqrt{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\,dx}+\sqrt{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\,dx}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)}\displaystyle D(u)}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{q(\Omega)}\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}.\end{split}

Squaring both sides we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{D(u)}{\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{q(\Omega)}\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+2\sqrt{\frac{D(u)}{\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{q(\Omega)}\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}.\end{split}

Applying the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and the Hölder inequality we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx&\leq\frac{D(u)}{\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{q(\Omega)}\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\frac{D(u)}{\beta q(\Omega)}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)}\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{DD}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\beta q(\Omega)}\bigg)\bigg(D(u)+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\bigg),\end{split}

that gives the conclusion (
3.8
).
∎

Remark 3.8
.

The previous result gives

(3.11)

\lim_{\beta\to+\infty}\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega).

Moreover, taking
w=1
as test function in (
3.9
), we get the following estimate

(3.12)

\frac{1}{\lambda(\beta,\Omega)}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{\beta P(\Omega_{0})}.

Hence, if
\Omega_{0}
is convex, denoted by
d_{M}:=\max\{d(x,\partial\Theta):x\in\partial\Omega_{0}\}
, we get

\lim_{d_{M}\to 0^{+}}\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega).

for any fixed
\beta>0
.

Furthermore, by (
2.2
) and (
3.12
), we have

\frac{1}{\lambda(\beta,\Omega)}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\rho(\Omega_{0})}{\beta}.

Finally, for any fixed
\beta
, we obtain

\lim_{\rho(\Omega_{0})\to 0}\lambda(\beta,\Omega)=\lambda_{{DD}}(\Omega).

3.3.
The problem on the spherical shell

In this section, we study the first Robin-Dirichlet eigenvalue on the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
. In this case, problem (
3.3
) reduces to

(3.13)

\lambda(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in H^{1}_{\partial B_{R_{1}}}(A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\\
\phi\nequiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dx+\beta\int_{\partial B_{R_{2}}}\phi^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}\phi^{2}dx}.

By
Proposition

3.2
, any minimizer of (
3.13
) has constant sign and solves the following problem

(3.14)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=\lambda(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})v&\text{ in }A_{R_{1},R_{2}}\\
\partial_{\nu}v+\beta v=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{R_{2}}\\
v=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{R_{1}}.\end{cases}

The eigenfunctions of (
3.14
) inherit symmetry properties, as proved in the following result.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let be
\beta>0
and
R_{2}>R_{1}>0
. Let
v
be a positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})
. Then
v
is radially symmetric, that is there exists a positive function
\phi(r)
,
r>0
, such that
v(x)=\phi(|x|)
. Moreover, there exists a unique
\bar{R}\in(R_{1},R_{2})
such that
\phi^{\prime}(\bar{R})=0
and
\phi(r)
is strictly increasing in
[R_{1},\bar{R}]
and is strictly decreasing in
[\bar{R},R_{2}]
.

Proof.

The radial symmetry follows from the simplicity of the first eigenvalue and the rotational invariance of the problem. Then if
v
is a positive eigenfunction, we have
v=\phi(|x|)
, where
\phi(r)
has to solve the following ordinary differential equation:

\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\left(\phi^{\prime}(r)r^{n-1}\right)^{\prime}=\lambda(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}})\phi(r)&\text{ for }r\in(R_{1},R_{2})\\
\phi^{\prime}(R_{2})+\beta\phi(R_{2})=0\\
\phi(R_{1})=0.\end{cases}

Since
\phi(r)>0
for
r\in(R_{1},R_{2})
, then we have

(3.15)

\left(\phi^{\prime}(r)r^{n-1}\right)^{\prime}<0,

that means
\phi^{\prime}(r)r^{n-1}
is a strictly decreasing function. Since
\phi(R_{1})=0
and
\phi(r)>0
for
r\in(R_{1},R_{2})
, then

(3.16)

\phi^{\prime}(r)>0\quad r\in[R_{1},R_{1}+\delta],

for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Meanwhile, since we know that
\phi^{\prime}(R_{2})=-\beta\phi(R_{2})<0
, then by continuity

(3.17)

\phi^{\prime}(r)<0\quad r\in[R_{2}-\delta^{\prime},R_{2}],

for sufficiently small
\delta^{\prime}>0
.
The conclusion easily follows from (
3.15
)-(
3.16
)-(
3.17
).
∎

4.
The first order shape derivative

In this section, we compute the first domain derivative of
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
. For the reader convenience, we recall the Hadamard’s formulas (see for instance
[
SZ92
,
HP06
]
).

Let
V(x)
a vector field such that
V\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and, for any
t>0
, let us set

\begin{split}\Omega_{0}(t)&:=\{\mathtt{x}_{t}:=x+tV(x),\,x\in\Omega_{0}\},\\
\Theta(t)&:=\{\mathtt{x}_{t}:=x+tV(x),\,x\in\Theta\},\end{split}

and
\Omega(t):=\Omega_{0}(t)\setminus\overline{\Theta(t)}
.

Let
f(t,x)
be such that
f(t,\cdot)\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and differentiable at
t
, then it holds:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}f(t,x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=&\int_{\Omega(t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[f(t,x)]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\\
&+\int_{+\partial\Omega(t)}f(t,x)\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Moreover, when the integral is defined on the oriented boundary of
\Omega
, if
f(t,\cdot)\in W^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it holds:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{+\partial\Omega(t)}f(t,x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=&\int_{+\partial\Omega(t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[f(t,x)]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\\
&+(n-1)\int_{+\partial\Omega(t)}H_{t}(x)\,f(t,x)\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&+\int_{+\partial\Omega(t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}[f(t,x)]\,\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where
H_{t}
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega(t)
. For further references, see also
[
HP06
, Chap. 5]
and
[
BW15
]
.

At this stage, we are in position to prove the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

The first domain derivative of
\lambda(\beta,\Omega)
in the direction
V
is

(4.3)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}[\lambda(\beta,\Omega(t))]\Big|_{t=0}=&\int_{\partial\Omega}\left\{|\nabla u|^{2}\!\!+\!\!(n-1)\beta Hu^{2}-\!(\lambda(\beta,\Omega)+\!2\beta^{2})u^{2}\right\}\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Proof.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the first Robin-Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\Omega(t)
, that is

(4.4)

\lambda(\beta,\Omega(t)):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta(t)}(\Omega(t))\\
\psi\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\left\{{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(t)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}(t)}\psi^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}\ ,\ \,\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}=1\right\}.

Any minimizer
u_{t}\in H^{1}_{\partial\Theta(t)}(\Omega(t))
of (
4.4
) is a solution to the following perturbed eigenvalue problem

(4.5)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{t}=\lambda(\beta,\Omega(t))u_{t}&\text{ in }\ \Omega(t)\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{t}+\beta u_{t}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{0}(t)\\
u_{t}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Theta(t).\end{cases}

Denoted by
u^{\prime}_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{t}
, and observing that the perturbed hole
\Theta(t)
is the zero-level set of the function
u_{t}
, the inner boundary condition in (
4.5
) implies

(4.6)

u_{t}^{\prime}=-\partial_{\nu}u_{t}(\nu\cdot V)\quad\text{ on }\partial\Theta(t).

By using the boundary conditions in (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and the divergence theorem, we get

(4.7)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega(t)}\langle\nabla u_{t}&,\nabla u_{t}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathtt{x}_{t}=\lambda(\beta,\Omega(t))\int_{\Omega(t)}u_{t}\,u_{t}^{\prime}\,d\mathtt{x}_{t}\\
&-\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}(t)}u_{t}\,u_{t}^{\prime}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial\Theta(t)}\left(\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}\right)^{2}\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Therefore, by using (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.7
), we have:

(4.8)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}[\lambda(\beta,\Omega(t))]=&2\lambda(\beta,\Omega(t))\int_{\Omega(t)}u_{t}\,u_{t}^{\prime}\,d\mathtt{x}_{t}\\
&-\int_{\partial\Theta(t)}\left(\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}\right)^{2}\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}(t)}|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&+(n-1)\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}(t)}H_{t}\,u_{t}^{2}\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}(t)}\frac{\partial u_{t}^{2}}{\partial\nu}\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

It is straightway seen that the normalization condition in (
4.4
) gives

(4.9)

2\int_{\Omega(t)}u_{t}\,u_{t}^{\prime}\,d\mathtt{x}_{t}+\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}(t)}u_{t}^{2}\,\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

Substituting (
4.9
) in (
4.8
) and recalling that
u_{t}
vanishes on
\partial\Theta
, we get the conclusion.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let us observe that the spherical shell
A_{R_{1},R_{2}}
such that
|B_{R_{1}}|=|\Theta|
and
|B_{R_{2}}|=|\Omega_{0}|
is a stationary domain for the first Robin-Dirichlet eigenvalue.

Indeed, if both the volume of the outer domain

Then, by
Proposition

3.9
, when the constraint (

) holds, the first derivative in (

) is zero and this suggest that the spherical shell is an optimal shape candidate under both volume constraints of the hole
\Theta
and
\Omega_{0}
.

(5.6)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathsf{M}_{i}}|\nabla w(x)|^{2}\;dx=&\int_{0}^{v_{M}}g_{i}(t)\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial E_{t}^{i}\cap\mathsf{M}_{i}\right)dt\leq\\
&\leq\int_{v_{m}}^{v_{M}}g_{i}(t)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial F_{t}^{i}\cap A_{R_{1},\bar{R}})\;dt=\int_{A_{R_{1},\bar{R}}}|\nabla v(x)|^{2}\>dx.\end{split}

Now, let us define
\mu^{i}(t)=|E^{i}_{t}\cap\Omega|
and
\eta^{i}(t)=|F^{i}_{t}\cap A_{R_{1},\bar{R}}|
. By using again coarea formula, we obtain

(5.7)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\mu^{i}(t)=&\int_{\{w=t\}\cap\mathsf{M}_{i}}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla w(x)|}\;d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\dfrac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial E^{i}_{t}\cap\mathsf{M}_{i}\right)}{g_{i}(t)}\\
&\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial F^{i}_{t}\cap A_{R_{1},\bar{R}})}{g_{i}(t)}=\int_{\{v=t\}\cap A_{R_{1},\bar{R}}}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla v(x)|}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{d}{dt}\eta^{i}(t),\end{split}

for
0\leq t<v_{M}
.
Since
\mu^{i}(0)=\eta^{i}(0)=0
, by integrating from
0
to
t<v_{M}
, we have

\mu^{i}(t)\leq\eta^{i}(t).

On the other hand, we have

(5.8)

\int_{\mathsf{M}_{i}}w^{2}(x)\;dx=\int_{0}^{v_{M}}2t(|\mathsf{M}_{i}|-\mu^{i}(t))dt\geq\int_{0}^{v_{M}}2t(|A_{R_{1},\bar{R}}|-\eta^{i}(t))\;dt=\int_{A_{R_{1},\bar{R}}}v^{2}(x)\;dx.

Finally, using (
5.2
) and (
5.6
), (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.8
), we achieve

\begin{split}\lambda(\beta,\Omega)&\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\;dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}w^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\;dx}\\
&\leq\frac{\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}|\nabla v|^{2}\;dx+\beta\int_{\partial B_{R_{2}}}v^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\int_{A_{R_{1},R_{2}}}v^{2}\;dx}=\lambda(\beta,A_{R_{1},R_{2}}),\end{split}

and this concludes the proof.
∎

Abstract
. Let 𝔄 {\mathfrak{A}}\, and 𝔄 ′ {\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime} be two alternative ∗ * -algebras with identities 1 𝔄 1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,} and 1 𝔄 ′ 1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}} , respectively, and e 1 e_{1} and e 2 = 1 𝔄 − e 1 e_{2}=1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}-e_{1} nontrivial symmetric idempotents in 𝔄 {\mathfrak{A}}\, . In this paper we study the characterization of multiplicative ∗ * -Jordan-type maps on alternative algebras. Keywords : alternative algebra, multiplicative ∗ * -Jordan-type maps. 2020 MSC : 17D05; 46L05.

Proposition 1.2

Lemma 2.2
.

For any

\displaystyle\ q_{n*}\left(\varphi(1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}),...,\varphi(1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}),\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{2^{n-2}}(e_{i}+a_{ij})\right),\varphi(e_{j}+b_{ij})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ q_{n*}\left(\varphi(1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}),...,\varphi(1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}),\varphi(e_{i})\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{2^{n-2}}a_{ij}\right),\varphi(e_{j})+\varphi(b_{ij})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \varphi(b_{ij})+\varphi(a_{ij}+a_{ij}^{*})+\varphi(a_{ij}b_{ij}+b_{ij}a_{ij}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \varphi(b_{ij})+\varphi(a_{ij})+\varphi(a_{ij}^{*})+\varphi(a_{ij}b_{ij})+\varphi(b_{ij}a_{ij}^{*}),

which implies
\varphi(a_{ij}+b_{ij})=\varphi(b_{ij})+\varphi(a_{ij})
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

For all
a_{ii},b_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{ii}
, we have
\varphi(a_{ii}+b_{ii})=\varphi(a_{ii})+\varphi(b_{ii})
for
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is surjective, given
\varphi(a_{ii})+\varphi(b_{ii})\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}
,
i=1,2
, there exists
t\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
such that

\varphi(t)=\varphi(a_{ii})+\varphi(b_{ii})
, with

. By Proposition
1.2
, for
j\neq i
,

\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{j},...,e_{j},t))=\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{j},...,e_{j},a_{ii}))+\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{j},...,e_{j},b_{ii}))=0.

Then,
t_{ij}=t_{ji}=t_{jj}=0
. We just have to show that
t_{ii}=a_{ii}+b_{ii}
. Given
c_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{ij}
, using Lemma
2.7
and

1.2
we have

\displaystyle\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},t,c_{ij}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},a_{ii},c_{ij}))+\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},b_{ii},c_{ij}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},a_{ii},c_{ij})+q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},b_{ii},c_{ij})).

By injectivity of
\varphi
we obtain

q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},t,c_{ij})=q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},a_{ii},c_{ij})+q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},b_{ii},c_{ij}),

that is,

(t_{ii}-a_{ii}-b_{ii})c_{ij}=0.

Finally, by
\left(\spadesuit\right)
we conclude that

.
∎

Now we are able to show that

preserves
*
-addition.

Using Lemmas

,
2.7
,
2.8

Besides, on the one hand, since

On the other hand, by additivity of
\varphi
,

\displaystyle 2^{n-2}\varphi(a+a^{*})

\displaystyle=\varphi(2^{n-2}(a+a^{*}))=\varphi(q_{n*}(1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},a,1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))

\displaystyle=q_{n*}(1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},\varphi(a),1_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}})=2^{n-2}(\varphi(a)+\varphi(a)^{*}).

Therefore
\varphi(a^{*})=\varphi(a)^{*}

Lemma 2.9

f_{i}=\varphi(e_{i})

Lemma 2.10
.

.
Even more,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\varphi(q_{n*}(e_{i},...,e_{i},x,e_{i}))=q_{n*}(f_{i},...,f_{i},\varphi(x),f_{i})

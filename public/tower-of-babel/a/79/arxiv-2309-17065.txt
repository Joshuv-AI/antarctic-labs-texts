Minimality of the inner automorphism group
Dekui Peng, Menachem Shlossberg

Abstract
. By [ 6 ] , a minimal group G G is called z z -minimal if G / Z ⁡ ( G ) G/Z(G) is minimal. In this paper, we present the z z -Minimality Criterion for dense subgroups with some applications to topological matrix groups. For a locally compact group G G , let Inn ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{Inn}(G) be the group of all inner automorphisms of G , G, endowed with the Birkhoff topology. Using a theorem by Goto [ 14 ] , we obtain our main result which asserts that if G G is a connected Lie group and H ∈ { G / Z ⁡ ( G ) , Inn ⁡ ( G ) } , H\in\{G/Z(G),\operatorname{Inn}(G)\}, then H H is minimal if and only if it is centre-free and topologically isomorphic to Inn ⁡ ( G / Z ⁡ ( G ) ) . \operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)). In particular, if G G is a connected Lie group with discrete centre, then Inn ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{Inn}(G) is minimal. We prove that a connected locally compact nilpotent group is z z -minimal if and only if it is compact abelian. In contrast, we show that there exists a connected metabelian z z -minimal Lie group that is neither compact nor abelian.

For a subgroup

Fact 2.1

Let

be a (not necessarily Hausdorff) topological group and
H
be a subgroup of

If
\gamma_{1}\subseteq\gamma
is a coarser group topology on

\gamma_{1}|_{H}=\gamma|_{H}
and
\gamma_{1}/H=\gamma/H
, then

Recall that a subset

of a group

is called

if

is closed in every Hausdorff group topology of

(see
[

]
).
It is known that the

Lemma 2.2
.

A topological group

is
z
-minimal if and only if both
Z(G)
and
G/Z(G)
are minimal.

Proof.

First we assume that
G
is
z
-minimal. Since minimality is preserved by taking closed central subgroups,
Z(G)
is minimal.
The minimality of
G/Z(G)
follows from the definition of
z
-minimal groups.

For the other direction, it suffices to show that
G
is minimal under the assumption that
Z(G)
and
G/Z(G)
are minimal.
This is an immediate corollary of Merson’s Lemma (Fact
2.1
) as
Z(G)
is unconditionally closed. ∎

Corollary 2.3
.

If
G
is a
z
-minimal group, then
Z_{2}(G)
is precompact. If
G
is also complete, then
Z_{2}(G)
is compact.

Proof.

Both

and
Z(G/Z(G))=Z_{2}(G)/Z(G)
are precompact, being minimal abelian. As precompactness is a three space property (see

]
for example), it follows that

is precompact. If

is also complete, then its unconditionally closed subgroup

must be also complete, hence, compact.
∎

Definition 2.4

Let

be a subgroup of a topological group

for every non-trivial closed normal subgroup

Fact 2.5
.

[

Let

is minimal if and only if

is minimal and

We present here a similar criterion for the

Theorem 2.6

(

-Minimality Criterion)
.

Let
H
be a dense subgroup of a topological group

. Then
H
is
z
-minimal if and only if
G
is
z
-minimal and

(a)

Z(H)
is a dense essential subgroup of
Z(G)
; and

(b)

every closed normal subgroup of
G
properly containing
Z(G)
contains a non-central element of
H
.

Proof.

We first assume that
H
is
z
-minimal.
Then, in particular,
H
is minimal.
By the denseness of
H
in
G
and the Minimality Criterion, we get that
G
is minimal and
Z(H)=Z(G)\cap H
.
Let
q:G\to G/Z(G)
be the canonical mapping. Then the kernel of
q\res_{H}
is
Z(G)\cap H=Z(H)
.
The minimality of
H/Z(H)
implies that the restriction mapping
q\res_{H}:H\to q(H)
is open, in particular,
q(H)
is topologically isomorphic to
H/Z(H)
.
According to
[
9
, 4.3.2 Lemma]
,
Z(H)
must be dense in
Z(G)
.
Since
q(H)\cong H/Z(H)
is a dense minimal subgroup of
G/Z(G)
, the Minimality Criterion implies that
G/Z(G)
is minimal (so
G
is
z
-minimal) and
q(H)
is essential in
G/Z(G)
.
The latter implies (b) since for every closed normal subgroup
N
of
G
properly containing
Z(G)
,
q(G)
is non-trivial and hence
q(N)\cap q(H)\neq\{e\}
, or equivalently,
N\cap H\nsubseteq Z(G)
.
It remains to note that
Z(H)\subseteq Z(G)
, so
N\cap H\nsubseteq Z(H)
.
To see (a), note that minimality is preserved by taking centres (so
Z(H)
is minimal) and we have already proved that
Z(H)
is dense in
Z(G)
, thus the Minimality Criterion applies.

Now we assume that
G
is
z
-minimal and (a) and (b) are satisfied.
Again the centre
Z(G)
of the minimal group
G
is minimal.
By (a) and the Minimality Criterion,
Z(H)
is minimal.
Moreover, as
Z(H)
is dense in

, the image

of

is topologically isomorphic to

It remains to see that

is a minimal group. By the Minimality Criterion, it suffices to show that
q(H)
is essential in
G/Z(G)
.
Let
M
be a closed normal subgroup of

distinct from the trivial group.
Then
q^{-1}(M)
is a closed normal subgroup of
G
properly containing
Z(G)
.
By (b), there exists

such that
x\notin Z(H)
.
As

and

, this implies that

does not belong to
Z(G)
,
thus,

.
In other words,

is essential in

and

is minimal.
Now apply Lemma
2.2
.
∎

-minimal for every prime
p.
Using
[
6
, Theorem 4.8]
again and the fact that
Z(H)=Z(G)
, we establish the minimality of
H
.
∎

3.
The Birkhoff topology

Let
H
be a group of topological automorphisms of a locally compact group
G
.
Equip
H
with a topology
\tau
.
It is known that the action of
H
on
G
is continuous if and only if
\tau
is finer than the compact-open topology.
However, in general the compact-open topology is not a group topology on
H
.
So, we may consider whether there is a coarsest group topology on
H
making this action continuous, i.e., finer than the compact-open topology.
Fortunately, such a topology exists.
It is called the
Birkhoff topology
(see
[
9
, p. 260]
and
[
25
, Remark 1.9]
) and also the
generalized compact-open topology
.This group topology has a local base at the identity formed by the sets

\mathcal{B}(C,U):=\{f\in H|\ f(g)\in Ug,\ f^{-1}(g)\in Ug\ \forall g\in C\},

where

C
runs over compact subsets and
U
runs over neighbourhoods of the identity in
G.

So, if the compact-open topology makes
H
a topological group, then the two topologies coincide.
In the sequel, a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
will always carry the Birkhoff topology. When
G
is a connected Lie group, this topology coincides with the compact-open topology, and makes
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
a Lie group
[
16
, Theorem 1]
.

There is another way to obtain this Lie group topology, see
[
16
,
19
]
.
Let
\varphi:G\to H
be a quotient homomorphism of connected Lie groups.
We say that
\varphi
is a
covering
if
\ker\varphi
is discrete. In this case,
G
is a
covering group
of
H.
If, in addition,
G
is simply connected, then it is the
universal covering group
of
H
.
The universal covering group is unique, up to a topological isomorphism. We denote the universal covering group of
G
by
\widetilde{G}
. Moreover, connected Lie groups have isomorphic universal covering groups if and only if they have isomorphic Lie algebras
[
19
, Theorem 9.5.13]
. In particular, if
G
is a covering group of
H
, then they have isomorphic Lie algebras.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
, then
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
, the group of Lie algebra automorphisms, is a closed subgroup of the general linear group
\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
, where
n
is the dimension of
G
.
It is also known that,
\operatorname{Aut}(\widetilde{G})
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
are isomorphic as groups (see
[
19
, Corollary 9.5.11]
).
Every (topological) automorphism
\varphi
of
G
admits a “lifting” to a unique automorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}
of
\widetilde{G}

[
19
, Remark 9.55(b)]
. That is,
\widetilde{\varphi}
makes the following diagram commutative

\textstyle{\widetilde{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\varphi}}
\textstyle{\widetilde{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{G}

where
q
is the covering mapping. So,
the mapping
\varphi\mapsto\widetilde{\varphi}
gives a group embedding of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
into
\operatorname{Aut}(\widetilde{G})
, with image the setwise stabilizer of
N
, where
N
is the kernel of the covering
\widetilde{G}\to G
. Since the latter group is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
, a Lie subgroup of the linear group
\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
, we obtain a Lie group structure on
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. This topology also coincides with the Birkhoff topology
[
17
, IX. Theorem 1.2]
.

The topological group embedding
\varphi\mapsto\widetilde{\varphi}
maps
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
onto
\operatorname{Inn}(\widetilde{G})
.
So,
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
and
\operatorname{Inn}(\widetilde{G})
are isomorphic as topological groups.
This implies that connected Lie groups with isomorphic Lie algebras have topologically isomorphic inner automorphism groups.

A locally compact group
G
is called a
(CA) group
if
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is closed in
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.
If
G
is a connected Lie group, then
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is a Lie group by the above argument.
So
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is closed in
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
if and only if
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is locally compact.
Therefore, the (CA) property is totally determined by Lie algebras.
In summary, we have:

Fact 3.1
.

Let
G,H
be connected Lie groups with isomorphic Lie algebras, then
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(H)
.
In particular,
G
is (CA) if and only if
H
is (CA).

Let
G
be a locally compact group, then the natural homomorphism

G\to\operatorname{Inn}(G),g\mapsto I_{g}

is continuous.
Indeed, since the kernel of this mapping is
Z(G)
, one may only check the induced group isomorphism
G/Z(G)\to\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is continuous.
Clearly, the action
\alpha:(G/Z(G),\tau_{q})\times G\to G
defined by
\alpha(gZ(G),h)=ghg^{-1}
is continuous, where
\tau_{q}
is the quotient topology on
G/Z(G)
.
Since the Birkhoff topology
\tau_{B}
is the coarsest group topology making the natural action of
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
on
G
continuous,
we get that
\tau_{B}\subseteq\tau_{q}
, which shows that
the mapping is continuous.
So, we have the following:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. If the quotient group
G/Z(G)
is minimal, then it is topologically isomorphic to

\operatorname{Inn}(G).

In particular, if
G
is a locally compact
z
-minimal group, then
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is locally compact minimal.

The Lie algebras of (CA) connected Lie groups are called
(CA) Lie algebras
. They were studied also by van Est
[
12
]
.
The following topological groups are all (CA).

Example 3.3
.

(1)

Compact groups and more generally locally compact
z
-minimal groups in view of Proposition
3.2
;

(2)

[
38
, Theorem 3.3]
every connected Lie group of dimension
\leq 4
;

(3)

[
12
]
connected nilpotent Lie groups;

(4)

[
12
]
semi-simple connected Lie groups. This is because a connected Lie group is (CA) if and only if its radical (the largest closed solvable connected normal subgroup) is (CA).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group, then
G
is (CA) if and only if
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)
.

Proof.

The sufficiency is evident.
To see the converse, suppose that
G
is (CA).
Then
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is a locally compact group.
Since
G
is connected,
we deduce by
[
1
, Theorem 3.1.27]
, in view of the Baire Category Theorem, that the natural mapping
G\to\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is open.
So
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
,
H
be locally compact groups.
Then
\operatorname{Inn}(G\times H)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G)\times\operatorname{Inn}(H)
.

Proof.

Clearly, the mapping

\iota:\operatorname{Inn}(G\times H)\to\operatorname{Inn}(G)\times\operatorname{Inn}(H),I_{(g,h)}\mapsto(I_{g},I_{h})

is an isomorphism of abstract groups.
Now let
K_{1},K_{2}
be compact subsets of
G
and
H
, and
U_{1},U_{2}
be identity neighbourhoods in
G
and
H
, respectively.
To see that
\iota
is continuous, it suffices to check that the preimage of

\mathcal{B}(K_{1},U_{1})\times\mathcal{B}(K_{2},U_{2})

is open in
\operatorname{Inn}(G\times H)
.
While, this follows from that

\iota^{-1}(\mathcal{B}(K_{1},U_{1})\times\mathcal{B}(K_{2},U_{2}))=\mathcal{B}(K_{1}\times K_{2},U_{1}\times U_{2})

immediately.

Now let us see that
\iota
is also open.
Let
K\subseteq G\times H
be compact and
U
be a neighbourhood of the identity in
G\times H
.
Choose compact subsets
K_{1},K_{2}
of
G
and
H
respectively such that
K\subseteq K_{1}\times K_{2}
.
Also take identity neighbourhoods
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
of
G
and
H
respectively with
U_{1}\times U_{2}\subseteq U
.
Then we get that
\mathcal{B}(K_{1}\times K_{2},U_{1}\times U_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(K,U)
and hence

\mathcal{B}(K_{1},U_{1})\times\mathcal{B}(K_{2},U_{2})=\iota(\mathcal{B}(K_{1}\times K_{2},U_{1}\times U_{2}))\subseteq\iota(\mathcal{B}(K,U)).

This proves that
\iota
is open.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2},...,G_{n}
be connected Lie groups. Then
\prod_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}
is (CA) if and only if each
G_{i}
is (CA).

Proof.

We need only to consider the case
n=2
.
If both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are (CA), then by Proposition
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
,

\operatorname{Inn}(G_{1}\times G_{2})\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G_{1})\times\operatorname{Inn}(G_{2})\cong G_{1}/Z(G_{1})\times G_{2}/Z(G_{2})\cong(G_{1}\times G_{2})/Z(G_{1}\times G_{2}).

Using Proposition
3.4
again, we obtain that
G_{1}\times G_{2}
is (CA).

Conversely, if
G_{1}\times G_{2}
is (CA).
Then

\operatorname{Inn}(G_{1})\times\operatorname{Inn}(G_{2})\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G_{1}\times G_{2})\cong(G_{1}\times G_{2})/Z(G_{1}\times G_{2})

is locally compact.
As a closed subgroup,
\operatorname{Inn}(G_{i})
is also locally compact, so closed in
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{i})
, where
i=1,2
.
That is,
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are (CA).
∎

There is a deep connection between the (CA) property and the minimality of a connected Lie group as the following theorem by Goto
[

, Theorem]
shows.

Fact 3.7
.

A connected Lie group is minimal if and only if it is a (CA) group with compact centre.

Combining Proposition
3.4

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then

is minimal if and only if

is compact and
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G).

Motivated by the definition of

Definition 3.9
.

A locally compact minimal group

\operatorname{Inn}
-minimal if
\operatorname{Inn}(G)

Locally compact

\operatorname{Inn}
-minimal. By the isomorphism
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G),

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then

\operatorname{Inn}
-minimal if and only if it is

-minimal.

The following fact is well-known in case

is a Lie group and

is discrete. For the reader sake we provide a proof for a more general case.

Fact 3.11
.

Let
G
be a connected topological group and
N
be a closed normal subgroup. If
N
is totally disconnected, and
q:G\to G/N
is the canonical quotient mapping, then

(1)

N
is central;

(2)

q(Z(G))=Z(G/N)
and
q^{-1}(Z(G/N))=Z(G)
.

Proof.

(1) follows from the second equation in (2).

Now let us check (2).
The first equation is also a consequence of the second.
Fix
x\in q^{-1}(Z(G/N))
, then for any
g\in G
,
q(x)
commutes with
q(g)
.
Hence we have that
gxg^{-1}x^{-1}\in\ker q=N
.
Thus we obtain a continuous mapping

\psi_{x}:G\to N,g\mapsto gxg^{-1}x^{-1}.

As
G
is connected while
N
is totally disconnected,
\psi_{x}(G)
must be a singleton.
Since

, we have that
\psi_{x}(G)
is exactly the trivial group.
Thus,

.
So we have that

.
The inclusion in the other direction is evident.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

[
13

Let

and

(1)

\varphi(G)

.

Let
N
be the kernel of the action in Proposition

on

Recall that the coarsest group topology on a subgroup of

So the natural injective mapping

is continuous.
Thus, we obtain a continuous homomorphism
\sigma^{\prime}
of

into
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
mapping

\sigma(h,-)
.

Theorem 3.14
.

[

Let

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
G
be a centre-free connected Lie group. Then
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal.

Proof.

If
G
is (CA), then it is minimal by Goto’s theorem. Moreover,
G\to\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is bijective.
So
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G
is minimal.

Now assume that
G
is not (CA).
Let
H,\varphi,M,T,V
be as in Theorem
3.14
.
Since
G
is centre-free and
\varphi
is injective it follows that
\varphi(G)
is centre-free. As
M\subseteq\varphi(G),
one obtains that
Z(H)\cap M
is trivial.
So by Theorem
3.14
(iii),
Z(H)
is finite.

Let
\psi:H\to H/Z(H)
be the canonical mapping, which is a covering,
H^{\prime}=\psi(H)
and
M^{\prime}=\psi(M)
(here we consider
M
as a closed subgroup of

in the natural way).
Then
H^{\prime}
is centre-free by Fact
3.11
, and (CA) since

and

have isomorphic Lie algebras, and hence is minimal.
Let
G^{\prime}=\psi(\varphi(G))
. Then
G^{\prime}
is a dense subgroup of

containing
M^{\prime}
.
We first show that
G^{\prime}
is minimal.
By the Minimality Criterion, it suffices to show that
G^{\prime}\cap N^{\prime}\neq\{1\}
for any non-trivial closed normal subgroup
N^{\prime}
of
H^{\prime}
.
Since
M^{\prime}\subseteq G^{\prime}
, we only need to consider the case when
N^{\prime}
trivially meets
M^{\prime}
.
Set
N=\psi^{-1}(N^{\prime})
.
As
M^{\prime}\cap N^{\prime}=\{1\}
and

we deduce that
M\cap N=\{1\}
.
Now, suppose that
(x,t)\in N\trianglelefteq M\rtimes T

,

(3.1)

(s(x),t)=(1,s)(x,t)(1,s^{-1})\in N.

Hence we have

(t^{-1}(x^{-1})t^{-1}(s(x)),1)=(t^{-1}(x^{-1}),t^{-1})(s(x),t)=(x,t)^{-1}(s(x),t)\in N.

On the other hand,
(t^{-1}(x^{-1})t^{-1}(s(x)),1)

(which has been already identified with

So
(t^{-1}(x^{-1})t^{-1}(s(x)),1)\in M\cap N

So

.
In view of Equation (
3.1

(x,t)
commutes with
(1,s)

Since

Now
\mu:=\psi\circ\varphi

By the denseness of

Now, using the minimality of

∎

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. If

is discrete, then
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal.

Proof.

The group

.
So Proposition

Now, by Fact

Recall that a connected Lie group is called

if its Lie algebra is semi-simple, i.e., a direct product of (finitely many) simple Lie algebras.
Evidently, the centre of a semi-simple Lie algebra is trivial.
It follows from
[

that the centre of a semi-simple connected Lie group is discrete.
So we have:

Corollary 3.17
.

For every semi-simple connected Lie group

\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal.

Remark 3.18
.

By
[

]
, a connected semi-simple
Lie group is totally minimal if and only if its centre is finite. In particular, if

is a a connected semi-simple Lie group with finite centre, then

-minimal. Hence,
\operatorname{Inn}(G)

The following example shows that not every centre-free connected Lie group is minimal.

Example 3.19
.

Let
N=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
and
T=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
, where
\mathbb{C}
is the field of complex numbers and
\mathbb{T}
is the unit circle in the complex plane.
Consider the following continuous action

(3.2)

T\times N\to N:((e^{2\pi ir},e^{2\pi is}),(z_{1},z_{2}))\mapsto(e^{2\pi ir}z_{1},e^{2\pi is}z_{2}).

Fixing an irrational number

, we can define another continuous action:

(3.3)

\mathbb{R}\times N\to N:(x,(z_{1},z_{2}))\mapsto(e^{2\pi ix}z_{1},e^{2\pi ihx}z_{2}).

Using the two actions, we get connected Lie groups

and

.
Now let us see that

is centre-free. If
((z_{1},z_{2}),x)\in Z(G)

should be fixed by any

.
However, this happens only when

.
On the other hand, the kernel of the action is trivial since

is irrational. So the only central element of

contained in
\{(0,0)\}\times\mathbb{R}
is the trivial element.
Thus,
G
is centre-free.
Moreover,

is not minimal: the mapping

\varphi:G\to H,((z_{1},z_{2}),x)\mapsto((z_{1},z_{2}),(e^{2\pi ix},e^{2\pi ihx}))

is continuous monomorphism but not open onto its image.

The following corollary provides another instance in which

-minimality and minimality coincide.

Corollary 3.20
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group with finite centre. Then

is minimal if and only if it is

Proof.

If

is finite, then it is both compact and discrete. By Theorem
3.16
,
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal. If
G
is minimal, then by Proposition
3.8
,

We now give a necessary condition for the minimality of

, where
G
is a connected locally compact group.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group such that
G/Z(G)
is minimal. Then
G/Z(G)
is centre-free.

Proof.

We first assume that
Z(G)
is connected and compact.
Let
H=G/Z(G)
and
q:G\to H
be the canonical mapping. Then, by the minimality of
H
, we obtain that
Z(H)
is compact.
By
[
21
, Theorem 13]
, the compact subgroup
Z(H)
of the connected locally compact group
H
is contained in a connected compact subgroup
P
, which can be chosen to be solvable by
[
21
, Theorem 15]
.
Let
K=q^{-1}(P)
, then
K
is a compact connected solvable group.
This implies that
K
is abelian, since compact connected solvable groups are abelian (see, for example,
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
).
Note that
Z_{2}(G)=q^{-1}(Z(H))
is a normal subgroup of
G
contained in

; so it is abelian.
According to a theorem of Iwasawa
[

, Theorem 4]
, compact normal abelian subgroups of a connected topological group must be central.
So,
Z_{2}(G)\subseteq Z(G)
, that is,
Z(H)=q(Z_{2}(G))\subseteq q(Z(G))=\{1\}
and we are done.

Now, let us consider the general case.
Since
Z(G)
is a locally compact abelian group, it has a closed totally disconnected subgroup

such that

is connected and compact, see
[
18
, Proposition 5.43]
.
If
G/Z(G)
is minimal, then its isomorphic copy
(G/D)/(Z(G)/D)
is minimal as well, in view of the
third isomorphism theorem for topological groups

(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.5.18]
and
[
20
, Proposition 3.6]
).
Note that, by Fact
3.11
(2),
Z(G/D)=Z(G)/D
.
So by the above case,
(G/D)/Z(G/D)
is centre-free.
It follows then from

G/Z(G)\cong(G/D)/(Z(G)/D)=(G/D)/Z(G/D)

that
G/Z(G)
is also centre-free.
∎

By Proposition
3.2
, if
G/Z(G)
is minimal, then so is
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
. We will see that, if
G
is also a Lie group, the necessary condition for the minimality of
G/Z(G)
becomes a necessary condition for the minimality of
\operatorname{Inn}(G).

Theorem 3.22
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. If
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal, then it is centre-free.

Proof.

If
G
is (CA), then by Proposition
3.4
,
G/Z(G)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal.
Then Proposition
3.21
can be applied.

Now consider the case when
G
is not (CA).
Let
H,\varphi,M,T,V,\sigma^{\prime}
be as in Theorem
3.14
.
Since
\sigma^{\prime}(H)
contains
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
as a dense subgroup and
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal,
\sigma^{\prime}(H)
must be minimal as well.
By (iv) of Theorem
3.14
,
\sigma^{\prime}(H)
is a closed, hence locally compact subgroup, of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.
Then,
\sigma^{\prime}
is open onto its image, again by
[
1
, Theorem 3.1.27]
.
Thus,
\sigma^{\prime}(H)\cong H/Z(H)
, because
Z(H)
is the kernel of
\sigma^{\prime}
: an element
h\in\ker\sigma^{\prime}
if and only if it commutes with any
g\in\varphi(G)
, this is equivalent to say that
h
commutes with every element in
H
because
\varphi(G)
is dense in
H
.
So, by Proposition
3.21
,
\sigma^{\prime}(H)\cong H/Z(H)
is centre-free.
Then
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is also centre-free.
Indeed, if
x\in\operatorname{Inn}(G)
commutes with every element in
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
, then it commutes with every element in
\sigma^{\prime}(H)
as well, since
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is dense in
\sigma^{\prime}(H)
.
So
x
can only be the identity.
∎

Corollary 3.23
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact nilpotent group. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

G
is abelian;

(2)

G/Z(G)
is minimal.

Moreover, if
G
is also a Lie group, then they are equivalent to

(3)

\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal

as well.

Proof.

The implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is trivial.
Let us consider the other direction.
If
G/Z(G)
is minimal, then by Proposition
3.21
,
G/Z(G)
is centre-free.
Since a centre-free nilpotent group can only be the trivial group,
G=Z(G)
is abelian.

When
G
is additionally assumed to be a Lie group, we have that
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G)
by Example
3.3
(3) and Proposition
3.4
.
So (2) and (3) are equivalent.
∎

Corollary 3.24
.

A connected locally compact nilpotent group is
z
-minimal only if it is compact and abelian.

In contrast, as the following example shows, there exists a totally minimal (so
z
-minimal, in particular) connected solvable Lie group that is neither compact nor abelian.

Example 3.25
.

[
37
, Example 3.13]

By Mayer
[
24
, Examples 2.6(i)]
, the Euclidean motion group
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rtimes\operatorname{SO}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is totally minimal for every
n>1
, where the special orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
acts on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by matrix multiplication.
Fixing
n=2
one obtains the Lie group of orientation-preserving isometries of the complex plane

G:=\ISO_{+}({\mathbb{C}})=\Bigg\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right)\bigg|\ \ a\in{\mathbb{T}},b\in{\mathbb{C}}\Bigg\}\cong{\mathbb{C}}\rtimes{\mathbb{T}},

where
{\mathbb{T}}
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}
by multiplication. Note that the total minimality of the non-compact connected metabelian Lie group
G
can be established in a different way by Banakh
[
2
, Theorem 13]
.

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
q:G\to G/Z(G)
be the quotient map. If
G/Z(G)
is centre-free, then the map

\psi:\operatorname{Inn}(G)\to\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)),I_{g}\mapsto I_{q(g)}

is a continuous group isomorphism.

Proof.

It is easy to see that if
G/Z(G)
is centre-free, then
\psi
is a group isomorphism. Let us prove the continuity of
\psi
. Consider the action

\tilde{\alpha}:\operatorname{Inn}(G)\times G/Z(G)\to G/Z(G)

defined by

\tilde{\alpha}(I_{g},q(h))=(q\circ\alpha)(I_{g},h)\ \forall(g,h)\in G\times G,

where

\alpha
is the natural continuous action of
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
on
G.
We show that
\tilde{\alpha}
is continuos at an arbitrary point
(I_{g},q(h)).
Let
U
be a neighbourhood of

\tilde{\alpha}(I_{g},q(h))=(q\circ\alpha)(I_{g},h).

By the continuity of
q
and
\alpha,
there exist a neighbourhood
V
of
I_{g}
and a neighbourhood
W
of
h
such that

\tilde{\alpha}(V\times q(W))=(q\circ\alpha)(V\times W)\subseteq U.

This proves the continuity of
\tilde{\alpha},
as
q(W)
is a neighbourhood of
q(h).
Now observe that
\tilde{\alpha}(I_{g},q(h))=\beta(I_{q(g)},q(h))
, where
\beta
is the natural action of
\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G))
on
G/Z(G).
Since the Birkhoff topology is the coarsest group topology on
\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G))
for which
\beta
is continuous we deduce that
\psi
is continuous.
∎

Now we are ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 3.27
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal if and only if it is centre-free and topologically isomorphic to
\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).

Proof.

Assume first that
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is centre-free and
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).
Then,
G/Z(G)
is a centre-free connected Lie group. By Proposition
3.15
,

\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G))
is minimal. It follows that its isomorphic copy
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal as well.

Now suppose that
G
is a connected Lie group such that
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal. By Theorem
3.22
,
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is centre-free. By Lemma
3.26
, there exists a continuous group isomorphism
\psi:\operatorname{Inn}(G)\to\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).
As
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal,
\psi
is, in fact, a topological group isomorphism.
∎

Similarly, we have:

Theorem 3.28
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then
G/Z(G)
is minimal if and only if it is centre-free and topologically isomorphic to
\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).

Proof.

Assume first that
G/Z(G)
is centre-free and
G/Z(G)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).
By Proposition
3.15
,

\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G))
and its isomorphic copy
G/Z(G)
are minimal.

Now suppose that
G
is a connected Lie group such that
G/Z(G)
is minimal. By Proposition
3.21
,
G/Z(G)
is centre-free. This and Proposition
3.2
imply that there exists a continuous group isomorphism
\phi:G/Z(G)\to\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).
As
G/Z(G)
is minimal,
\phi
is, in fact, a topological group isomorphism.
∎

As a corollary we obtain a criterion for the
z
-minimality of a connected Lie group.

Corollary 3.29
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

G
is
z
-minimal;

(2)

G
is
\operatorname{Inn}
-minimal;

(3)

(a)

Z(G)
is compact and

(b)

G/Z(G)
is centre-free and topologically isomorphic to
\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).

Proof.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
Use Corollary
3.10
.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(3)
By Lemma
2.2
,
G
is
z
-minimal if and only if
Z(G)
is compact and
G/Z(G)
is minimal. Now use Theorem
3.28
.
∎

4.
Open questions and concluding remarks

Question 4.1
.

Is the condition that
\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G))
in Theorem
3.27
necessary? In other words, can we obtain this isomorphism from the condition that
G/Z(G)
is centre-free?

It suffices to check if the continuous group isomorphism
\psi:\operatorname{Inn}(G)\to\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)),
defined in Lemma
3.26
, is open.

The following question can be viewed as a particular case of
[
6
, Question 7.1]
.

Question 4.2
.

Is an infinite product of locally compact
z
-minimal groups
z
-minimal?

In view of Proposition
3.2
, it is also natural to ask:

Question 4.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group for which
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is locally compact minimal. Is
\operatorname{Inn}(G)^{\kappa}
minimal for every infinite cardinal
\kappa
?

When
G
is a connected Lie group, the answer is “yes”. This is because if
\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is minimal, then it is centre-free, by Theorem
3.22
while arbitrary products of centre-free minimal groups are minimal.

If
G
is a locally compact (not necessarily Lie) connected group such that
G/Z(G)
is minimal, then
G/Z(G)
is centre-free by Proposition
3.21
and the topological isomorphism
G/Z(G)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G))
holds true. So, one may ask if Theorem
3.28
holds for a locally compact connected group even without assuming that it is a Lie group.

Question 4.4
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group such that
G/Z(G)
is centre-free and topologically isomorphic to
\operatorname{Inn}(G/Z(G)).
Must
G/Z(G)
be minimal?

Recall that a complete minimal abelian group must be compact while there exist locally compact minimal nilpotent groups that are not compact.
Indeed, using
[
25
, Theorem 2.11]
one can construct many non-compact minimal (two-step) nilpotent locally compact groups.

Question 4.5
.

Let
G
be a two-step nilpotent locally compact minimal group. Is
G^{\kappa}
minimal for every infinite cardinal
\kappa
?

It is still open whether the Fermat and Mersenne numbers are square-free (see
[
15
]
). The following result follows from Corollary
2.11
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a subfield of a local field,
F_{n}=2^{2^{n}}+1
and
M_{p}=2^{p}-1
be a Fermat number and a Mersenne number, respectively, where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p
is a prime number.

(1)

If
\operatorname{SL}(F_{n},\mathbb{F})
is minimal but not totally minimal, then
F_{n}
is not square-free.

(2)

If
\operatorname{SL}(M_{p},\mathbb{F})
is minimal but not totally minimal, then
M_{p}
is not square-free.

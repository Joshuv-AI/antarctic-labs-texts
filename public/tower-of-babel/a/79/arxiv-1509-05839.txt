Weak solutions of semilinear elliptic equation involving Dirac mass


Abstract




Huyuan Chen Patricio Felmer Jianfu Yang

†
†

AMS Subject Classifications: 35J60, 35J20.
†
†

Key words: Weak solution, Mountain Pass theorem, Dirac mass.

Abstract.
In this paper, we study the following elliptic problem with Dirac mass

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=Vu^{p}+k\delta_{0}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
N>2
,
p>0
,
k>0
,
\delta_{0}
is Dirac mass at the origin, the function
V
is a locally Lipchitz continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

satisfying

V(x)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{|x|^{a_{0}}(1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}})}

with
a_{0}<N,\ a_{\infty}>a_{0}
and
c_{1}>0
.
We obtain two positive solutions of (
1
) with additional conditions for parameters on
a_{\infty},a_{0}
,
p
and
k
. The first solution is
a minimal positive solution and the second solution is constructed by Mountain Pass theorem.

1.
Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to study the existence of multiple weak solutions to the following nonlinear elliptic problem with Dirac mass

\left\{\begin{aligned} -&\Delta u=Vu^{p}+k\delta_{0}&&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}\\
\,\,\,&\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0\end{aligned}\right.

[formula]

where
N>2
,
p>0
,
k>0
,
\delta_{0}
is Dirac mass at the origin, the weight function
V
is locally Lipchitz continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Problem (
P_{k}
) concerns with source term in contrast with
problems with absorption terms. The semi-linear elliptic
equations with absorption terms

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+g(u)=\nu&\rm{in}\quad\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta+g(u)}u=0&\rm{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\nu
is a bounded Radon measure,
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is nondecreasing and
g(0)\geq 0
, has been extensively studied for the last
several decades. A fundamental contribution to the problem is due to Brezis

[
8
]
, Benilan and Brezis
[
5
]
, where they showed the existence and uniqueness of weak solution for problem (
1.1
) if the function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the subcritical assumption:

\int_{1}^{+\infty}(g(s)-g(-s))s^{-1-\frac{N}{N-2}}ds<+\infty.

The method is to approximate the measure
\nu
by a sequence of regular functions, and find classical solutions which
converges to a weak solution of (
1.1
). Meanwhile, it is necessary to establish uniform bounds for the sequence of classical solutions. The uniqueness is then derived by
Kato’s inequality. Such a method has been extended to solve the equations with
boundary measure data in
[
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and other subjects in
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
]
.

In the source term case, one adapts different approaches since it is hard to find uniform bound if one uses the approaching process in
[
5
,
8
]
. Moreover, it seems that the uniqueness is no longer valid in general. Actually, for the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=u^{q}+\lambda\delta_{0}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
q\in(1,\frac{N}{N-2})
,
\lambda>0
and
\Omega
is a bounded domain containing the origin, it was shown in
[
15
]
that there exists
\lambda^{*}>0
such that
(
1.2
) has two solutions for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
. For general Radon measures
\mu
, one weak solution was found in
[
4
]
for (
1.2
) with
\delta_{0}
replaced by
\mu
. If
1<q<\frac{N}{N-2}
, the solutions of (
1.2
) are isolated singular solutions of

-\Delta u=u^{q}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},

(1.3)

such solutions asymptotically behave at the origin like
|x|^{2-N}
.
The nonnegative solutions to (
1.3
) with isolated singularities have been classified in
[
1
]
for
q=\frac{N}{N-2}
, in
[
12
]

for
\frac{N}{N-2}<q<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
and in
[
9
]
for

q=\frac{N+2}{N-2}
. Using this classification of singular solutions, one may construct solutions of the equation like (
1.3
) with many singular points, see for instance
[
20
,
23
]
.

In the whole space, it was proved in
[
22
]
that the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\ \ -\Delta u+u=u^{q}+\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{x_{i}}&{\rm in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

possesses at least two weak solutions for
\kappa>0
small and
q\in(1,\frac{N}{N-2})
. A feature of the operator

-\Delta+id
is that its fundamental solution decays exponentially at infinity, thus the fundamental solution belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This fact plays an essential role in finding solutions of (
1.4
). While in our problem (
P_{k}
), the fundamental solution of
-\Delta
does not belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It brings difficulties in the process of finding solutions of (
P_{k}
).

In this paper, we will find two weak solutions for problem (
P_{k}
). By a
weak solution
of (
P_{k}
) we mean a nonnegative function
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
Vu^{p}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(0)}u(x)=0

and
u
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(-\Delta)\xi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu^{p}\xi dx+k\xi(0),\quad\forall\xi\in C^{1,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We suppose throughout this paper that there exist
a_{0}<N,\ a_{\infty}>a_{0}
,
c_{1}>0
such that the function
V(x)
satisfies

V(x)\leq V_{0}(x):=\frac{c_{1}}{|x|^{a_{0}}(1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}})}.

(1.5)

Condition (
1.5
) implies that the limiting behavior of
V
at the origin is controlled by
|x|^{-a_{0}}
and that of
V(x)
at infinity controlled by
|x|^{-a_{\infty}}
respectively.

The first result is on the minimal solution of (
P_{k}
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose condition (
1.5
) holds and
p>0
satisfies

p\in(\frac{N-a_{\infty}}{N-2},\frac{N-a_{0}}{N-2}).

(1.6)

Then,

(i)
there exists
k^{*}=k^{*}(p,V)\in(0,+\infty]
such that for
k\in(0,k^{*})
, there exists a minimal positive solution
u_{k,V}
of (
P_{k}
)
and for
p>1
and
k>k^{*}
, there is no solution for (
P_{k}
). Moreover, we have

k^{*}<\infty
if
p>1
;
k^{*}=+\infty
if
0<p<1
or
p=1
and
c_{1}>0
small.

(ii)
for
p
fixed, the mapping
V\mapsto k^{*}
is decreasing and the mapping
V\mapsto u_{k,V}
is increasing.

(iii)
if
V
is radially symmetric, the minimal solution
u_{k,V}
is also radially symmetric.

In the sequel, we denote
u_{k,V}
the minimal solution obtained in Theorem
1.1
corresponding to
k
and
V
.

We remark that the minimal solution of (
P_{k}
) is derived by iterating an increasing sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
defined by

v_{0}=k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}],\qquad v_{n}=\mathbb{G}[Vv_{n-1}^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}],

where
\mathbb{G}[\cdot]
is the
Green operator defined as

\mathbb{G}[f](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,y)f(y)dy

and
G
is the Green kernel of
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It is known that
\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]
is the fundamental solution of
-\Delta
.
To insure the convergence of the sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
, we need to construct a suitable barrier function by using the estimate

\mathbb{G}[V\mathbb{G}^{p}[\delta_{0}]]\leq c_{2}\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(1.7)

where
c_{2}>0
. From the estimate (
1.7
), the optimal range of
k
for constructing super solution of (
P_{k}
) is
(0,k_{p}]
, where

k_{p}=(c_{2}p)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{p-1}{p}.

(1.8)

Here we observe that
k^{*}\geq k_{p}
.

Once the minimal solution is found, we explore further properties of the solution. Precisely, we show such a solution
is regular except for the origin, and decays at infinity. These properties allow us to establish the stability of
the minimal solution, whereas this stability plays the role in finding the second solution.

Denote by
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the Sobolev space which is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the norm

\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We say a solution
u
of (
P_{k}
) is
stable (resp. semi-stable)
if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx>p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu^{p-1}\xi^{2}dx,\quad({\rm resp.}\ \geq)\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}.

Let
k_{p}
be given in (
1.8
). The properties of the minimal solution are collected as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that the function
V
satisfies (
1.5
) with
a_{\infty}>a_{0}
and
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p
satisfies (
1.6
).

(i)
If
a_{0}<2
,
p>1
and
k\in(0,k_{p})
, then
u_{k,V}
is a classical solution of the equation

-\Delta u=Vu^{p}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\quad\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0

(1.9)

and satisfies

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}u(x)|x|^{N-2}<+\infty.

(1.10)

Moreover,
u_{k,V}
is stable and there exists
c_{3}>0
independent of
k
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}dx\geq c_{3}[(k^{*})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}-k^{\frac{p-1}{p}}]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}.

(1.11)

(ii)
If

p\in\left(0,\frac{N}{N-2}\right)

(1.12)

and
k\in(0,k^{*})
, the minimal solution
u_{k,V}
is stable and satisfies (
1.11
). Moreover, any positive weak solution
u
of (
P_{k}
) is a classical solution of problem (
1.9
) satisfying (
1.10
).

We note that in
(i)
and
(ii)
of Theorem
1.2
, the parameter
k
is bounded by
k_{p}
and
k^{*}
respectively. It is not clear if
k_{p}<k^{*}
.
We also remark that (
1.10
) implies that the singularity and the decays of
u
at the origin and infinity respectively are the same as the fundamental solution.

The second solution of (
P_{k}
) will be constructed by Mountain Pass Theorem. Indeed, we will look for
critical points of the functional

E(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}VF(u_{k,V},v_{+})\,dx

(1.13)

in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
t_{+}=\max\{0,t\}
,

F(s,t)=\frac{1}{p+1}\left[(s+t_{+})^{p+1}-s^{p+1}-(p+1)s^{p}t_{+}\right].

To assure that the functional
E
is well defined, we establish the
embedding

\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},V_{0}u_{k,V}^{p-1}dx)

(1.14)

and

\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},V_{0}dx),

(1.15)

both of them are compact if

p+1\in(2^{*}(a_{\infty}),2^{*}(a_{0}))\cap[1,2^{*}),

(1.16)

where
2^{*}(t)=\frac{2N-2t}{N-2}
with
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
2^{*}=2^{*}(0)
. Therefore, we may verify that the functional
E

satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
condition. Furthermore, we may build the mountain pass structure by the stability of the minimal solution.

Taking into account the range of
p
for the existence of the minimal solution, we suppose

p\in(\frac{N-a_{\infty}}{N-2},\frac{N-a_{0}}{N-2})\cap(\max\{2^{*}(a_{\infty})-1,0\},\min\{2^{*}(a_{0})-1,2^{*}-1\}).

(1.17)

The intersection of intervals in (
1.17
) is not empty if we assume further that

a_{0}<2,\ a_{\infty}>\max\{0,1+\frac{a_{0}}{2}\}.

(1.18)

Our result of the existence of the second solution is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that the function
V
satisfies (
1.5
) with
a_{0}
and
a_{\infty}
given in (
1.18
),
p>1
satisfies (
1.17
) and
k_{p}
is given by (
1.8
) .
Then, for
k\in(0,k_{p})
, problem (
P_{k}
) admits a weak solution
u>u_{k,V}
.
Moreover, both
u
and
u_{k,V}
are classical solutions of (
1.9
).

Although we are not able to show

, we may prove that if
p
satisfies (

), problem (
P_{k}
) admits a solution
u
such that
u>u_{k,V}
for all
k\in(0,k^{*})
.

If

is radially symmetric, the range of

can be improved to

p\in(\frac{N-a_{\infty}}{N-2},\frac{N-a_{0}}{N-2})\cap(\max\{2^{*}(a_{\infty})-1,0\},2^{*}(a_{0})-1).

(1.19)

Finally, we have the following result for
V
being radial.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that the function
V
is radially symmetric satisfying (
1.5
) with
a_{0}
and

given in (
1.18
),

p>1
satisfies (
1.19
) and

is given by (

).
Then, for
k\in(0,k_{p})
, problem (
P_{k}

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we show the existence of the minimal solution of (
P_{k}
). Section 3 is devoted
the regularity and stability of the minimal solution. Finally, in Section 4 we find the second solution of (

) by the Mountain Pass theorem.

2.
Minimal solution

In this section, we show the existence of the minimal solution for (
P_{k}
). To this end, we construct a monotone sequence of approximating solutions by the iterating technique and bound it by a super-solution. The suitable super-solution will be constructed based on the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that the function
V
satisfies (
1.5
) with
a_{0}<N,\,a_{\infty}>0
, and that

p>0
satisfies (
1.6
). There holds

\mathbb{G}[V\mathbb{G}^{p}[\delta_{0}]]\leq c_{2}\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(2.1)

where
c_{2}
linearly depends on
c_{1}
.

Proof.

Note that

\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}](x)=\frac{c_{N}}{|x|^{N-2}},

(2.2)

by the assumption for
p
, we have

V(x)\mathbb{G}^{p}[\delta_{0}](x)\leq\frac{c_{N}^{p}c_{1}}{|x|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}(1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}})},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(2.3)

where
c_{N}>0
is the normalized constant depending only on
N
. This implies

V\mathbb{G}^{p}[\delta_{0}]\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We deduce by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}[V\mathbb{G}^{p}[\delta_{0}]](x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{N}^{p+1}c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}\frac{1}{1+|y|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}}\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{N}^{p+1}c_{1}|x|^{2-(N-2)p-a_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|e_{x}-y|^{N-2}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}|y|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}}\,dy

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle c_{N}^{p+1}c_{1}|x|^{2-(N-2)p-a_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi(x,y)\,dy,

where
e_{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}
.

Now, we estimate
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi(x,y)dy
. We divide it into two cases
(i)

|x|\geq 1
and
(ii)

|x|\leq 1
to discuss.

In the case
(i)

|x|\geq 1
, by (
1.6
),
(N-2)p+a_{0}<N
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\Phi(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}|y|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}}\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{4}|x|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}-N}\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(0)}\frac{1}{1+|z|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}}\frac{1}{|z|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}|x|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}-N}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\frac{dz}{|z|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}}+\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\frac{dz}{|z|^{(N-2)p+a_{\infty}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{5}\left(|x|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}-N}+|x|^{a_{0}-a_{\infty}}\right),

where
c_{4},c_{5}>0
.

If
y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x})
, we have

\frac{1}{1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}|y|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}}\leq c_{6}|x|^{a_{0}-a_{\infty}},

and then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x})}\Phi(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{7}|x|^{a_{0}-a_{\infty}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x})}\frac{1}{|e_{x}-y|^{N-2}}dy\leq c_{8}|x|^{a_{0}-a_{\infty}},

where
c_{6},c_{7},c_{8}>0
.

If
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x}))
, we have
(N-2)(p+1)+a_{\infty}>N
by (
1.6
). Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x}))}\Phi(x,y)\,dy\leq c_{9}|x|^{a_{0}-a_{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}\frac{dy}{|y|^{(N-2)(p+1)+a_{\infty}}}\leq c_{10}|x|^{a_{0}-a_{\infty}},

where
c_{9},c_{10}>0
.

By the assumption on
p
,
(N-2)p+a_{\infty}\geq N
. We conclude that there exists
c_{11}>0
such that for
|x|\geq 1
,

\mathbb{G}[V\mathbb{G}^{p}[\delta_{0}]](x)\leq c_{11}\max\{|x|^{2-N},|x|^{2-(N-2)p-a_{\infty}}\}\leq c_{11}|x|^{2-N}.

(2.4)

Next, we treat the case
(ii)

|x|\leq 1
.

Apparently, there exist
c_{12},c_{13}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\Phi(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{12}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\frac{1}{|y|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}}}\,dy\leq c_{13}.

By (
1.6
), there are
c_{14},c_{15}>0
such that

\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x})}\Phi(x,y)\,dy\leq c_{14}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x})}\frac{1}{|e_{x}-y|^{N-2}}\,dy\leq c_{15}.

For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x}))
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x}))}\Phi(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{16}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}|y|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}+N-2}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{16}|x|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}\frac{dz}{|z|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}+N-2}(1+|z|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{17}|x|^{(N-2)p+a_{0}-2},

where
c_{16},c_{17}>0
.

Consequently, there exists
c_{18}>0
such that for
|x|\leq 1
,

\mathbb{G}[V\mathbb{G}^{p}[\delta_{0}]](x)\leq c_{18}|x|^{2-N}.

(2.5)

Therefore, the assertion follows by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
First, we prove
(i)
. We define the iterating sequence:

v_{0}:=k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]>0,

and

\displaystyle v_{n}=\mathbb{G}[Vv_{n-1}^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}].

(2.6)

Observing that

v_{1}=\mathbb{G}[Vv_{0}^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]>v_{0},

and assuming that

v_{n-1}(x)\geq v_{n-2}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

we deduce that

\displaystyle v_{n}=\mathbb{G}[Vv_{n-1}^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]\geq\mathbb{G}[Vv_{n-2}^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]=v_{n-1}.

(2.7)

Thus, the sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is increasing with respect to
n
.
Moveover, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}(-\Delta)\xi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vv_{n-1}^{p}\xi dx+k\xi(0),\quad\forall\xi\in C^{1,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(2.8)

Now, we build an upper bound for
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
. For
t>0
, denote

w_{t}=tk^{p}\mathbb{G}[V\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]\leq(c_{2}tk^{p}+k)\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}],

where
c_{2}>0
is from Lemma
2.1
,
then

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}[Vw_{t}^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]\leq(c_{2}tk^{p}+k)^{p}\mathbb{G}[V\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]\leq w_{t}

if

(c_{2}tk^{p-1}+1)^{p}\leq t.

(3.13)

Proof.

We divide the proof into two parts. At first, we show the stability of the minimal solution for
k>0
small; then, we prove this is true for full range
k\in(0,k^{*})
.

Now we show for
k>0
small, the result holds true. By (
2.13
), for
k>0
small,

u_{k,V}(x)\leq c_{35}k|x|^{2-N}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

where
c_{35}>0
is independent of
k
. Therefore,

V(x)u_{k,V}^{p-1}(x)\leq c_{35}^{p-1}c_{1}k^{p-1}\frac{|x|^{(2-N)(p-1)-a_{0}}}{1+|x|^{a_{\infty}-a_{0}}}.

(3.14)

we note for

p\in(\frac{N-a_{\infty}}{N-2},\frac{N-a_{0}}{N-2}),

there holds

(2-N)(p-1)-a_{0}\geq-2\quad{\rm and}\quad(2-N)(p-1)-a_{\infty}<-N.

It implies from (
3.14
) that

V(x)u_{k,V}^{p-1}(x)\leq c_{35}^{p-1}c_{1}\frac{k^{p-1}}{|x|^{2}}.

(3.15)

Hence, for any
\xi\in C^{1,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by (
3.15
) and the Hardy-Sobolev inequality, we deduce for
k>0
small that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}\,dx\leq c_{35}^{p-1}c_{1}k^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\xi^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}\,dx.

(3.16)

Inequality (
3.16
) holds also for
\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which means that

u_{k,V}
is a semi-stable solution of (
P_{k}
) for
k>0
small. Indeed, by a density argument, taking

\{\xi_{n}\}_{n}
in
C^{1,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
so that
\xi_{n}\to\xi
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and replacing
\xi
in (
3.16
) by
\xi_{n}
, the result follows by passing the limit.

Next, we prove the stability of minimal solutions for all
k\in(0,k^{*})
. Suppose that if
u_{k}
is not stable, then we have that

\lambda_{1}:=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}\,dx}{p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}\,dx}\leq 1.

(3.17)

By the compact embedding theorem in section 4,
\lambda_{1}
is achieved by a nonnegative function
\xi_{1}
satisfying

-\Delta\xi_{1}=\lambda_{1}pVu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi_{1}.

Choosing
\hat{k}\in(k,k^{*})
and letting

, we have that

w=\mathbb{G}[Vu_{\hat{k},V}^{p}-Vu_{k,V}^{p}]+(\hat{k}-k)\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}].

By the elementary inequality

(a+b)^{p}\geq a^{p}+pa^{p-1}b\quad{\rm for}\ \ a,b\geq 0,

we infer that

w\geq\mathbb{G}[Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}w]+(\hat{k}-k)\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}].

Choosing
t>0
small, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}pVu_{k,V}^{p-1}w\xi_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)w\xi_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}pVu_{k,V}^{p-1}w\xi_{1}\,dx+(\hat{k}-k)\xi_{1}(0)>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}pVu_{k,V}^{p-1}w\xi_{1}\,dx,

which is impossible. Consequently,

p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}dx<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}.

Finally, we prove (
3.13
). For any
k\in(0,k^{*}),
let
k^{\prime}=\frac{k+k^{*}}{2}>k
and
l_{0}=(\frac{k}{k^{\prime}})^{\frac{1}{p}}<1
. Hence, there exists a minimal solution

of (
P_{k}
) for

, and the minimal solution
u_{k^{\prime},V}
is stable. Noting that
k-k^{\prime}l_{0}^{p}=0
, we deduce

\displaystyle l_{0}u_{k^{\prime},V}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle l_{0}^{p}u_{k^{\prime},V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{0}^{p}\left(\mathbb{G}[Vu_{k^{\prime},V}^{p}]+k^{\prime}\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]\right)+(k-k^{\prime}l_{0}^{p})\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}[V(l_{0}u_{k^{\prime},V})^{p}]+k\mathbb{G}[\delta_{0}],

that is,
l_{0}u_{k^{\prime},V}
is a super-solution of (
P_{k}
) for
k
. Therefore,

l_{0}u_{k^{\prime},V}\geq u_{k,V}.

So for
\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k^{\prime},V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}\,dx-pl_{0}^{1-p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{0}^{1-p}\left[l_{0}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}\,dx-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}dx\right].

It implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}\,dx-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-l_{0}^{p-1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx+\left[l_{0}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}\,dx-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vu_{k,V}^{p-1}\xi^{2}\,dx\right]

which together with the fact

implies (
3.13
).
The proof is complete.

4.
Mountain-Pass solution

In order to find the second solution of (

is a solution of (
P_{k}

u_{k,V}
of (
P_{k}
). We are then led to consider the problem

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=V(u_{k,V}+u_{+})^{p}-Vu_{k,V}^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0.\end{array}

(4.1)

It is adequate to find a nontrivial solution of (
4.1
). Intuitively, the cancelation of the singularity of
u_{k,V}
in the nonlinear term on the right hand side of (

) allows us to find a solution of (

)
as a critical point of the functional

(4.2)

defined on
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(4.3)

Let
V_{0}

1.5
) and
denote by

L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N},V_{0}dx)=\{u:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{0}|u|^{q}\,dx<+\infty\}.

We may verify by the following lemma that the functional

is well-defined on
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
a_{0}<2,\ a_{\infty}>\max\{a_{0},0\}
and
p>1
satisfy (
1.17
). Then
the inclusion
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},V_{0}\,dx)

is continuous and compact.

Proof.

For
\beta\in(0,2)
, it follows by Hölder’s inequality, the Hardy inequality and the Sobolev
inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\xi^{2^{*}(\beta)}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\xi^{2^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{36}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{2-\beta}{2}\frac{2^{*}}{2}}=c_{36}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\xi|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2^{*}(\beta)}{2}}.

We claim that the inclusion
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N},V_{0}dx)

is continuous if

\max\{2^{*}(a_{\infty}),1\}\leq q\leq\min\{2^{*}(a_{0}),2^{*}\}.

For
\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, if
0\leq a_{0}<2
, by (
4.4
),

\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{2^{*}(a_{0})}(B_{1}(0),|x|^{a_{0}}dx)}\leq\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{2^{*}(a_{0})}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{a_{0}}dx)}\leq c_{36}\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

If

, by Sobolev inequality,

\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{2^{*}}(B_{1}(0),|x|^{a_{0}}dx)}\leq\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{2^{*}}(B_{1}(0))}\leq c_{36}\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{1}(0),|x|^{a_{0}}dx)}\leq c_{36}\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{2^{*}(a_{0})}(B_{1}(0),|x|^{a_{0}}dx)}\leq c_{36}\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Moreover, for
a_{\infty}\in(0,2]
, we have
2^{*}(a_{\infty})<q\leq 2^{*}
, by Hölder’s inequality and (
4.4
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}|\xi|^{q}|x|^{-a_{\infty}}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}|\xi|^{q}|x|^{-\tau}\,dx\leq\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{q}{2}},

where

.
The case
a_{\infty}>2
can be reduced to the case
a_{\infty}\in(0,2]
.

In conclusion, for all the cases, we have

\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{q}(B_{1}(0),|x|^{a_{0}}dx)}\leq c_{36}\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0),|x|^{a_{\infty}}dx)}\leq\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

(4.5)

Combining with the fact that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}V_{0}(t)t^{a_{0}}=c_{1}\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{t\to+\infty}V_{0}(t)t^{a_{\infty}}=c_{1},

then the claim is true.

We next show that the inclusion
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N},V_{0}dx)

is compact if

\max\{2^{*}(a_{\infty}),1\}<q<\min\{2^{*}(a_{0}),2^{*}\}.

(4.6)

Let
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n}
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\varepsilon>0
, there exists

(4.7)

By the Sobolev embedding,
\xi_{n}\to\xi
in
H^{1}(B_{R}(0))
up to a subsequence. This, together with (

), yields the result.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u_{k,V}+v_{k})(-\Delta)\xi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(u_{k,V}+v_{k})^{p}\xi dx+k\xi(0),\quad\forall\xi\in C^{1,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(4.12)

This means that
v_{k}+u_{k,V}
is weak solution of (
P_{k}
), and also implies
v_{k}+u_{k,V}
is a classical solution of (
1.9
). The maximum principle yields

, and then

. So we obtain two positive solutions of (
P_{k}
).
\Box

5.
Appendix: Regularities

We recall
G(x,y)=\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}
the Green kernel of
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\mathbb{G}[\cdot]
the
Green operator defined as

\mathbb{G}[f](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,y)f(y)dy.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain and
h\in L^{s}(\Omega)
. Then,
there exists
c_{43}>0
such that

(i)

\|\mathbb{G}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{43}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\quad{\rm if}\quad\frac{1}{s}<\frac{2}{N};

(5.1)

(ii)

\|\mathbb{G}[h]\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq c_{43}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\quad{\rm if}\quad\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{N}\quad\rm{and}\quad s>1;

(5.2)

(iii)

\|\mathbb{G}[h]\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq c_{43}\|h\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\quad{\rm if}\quad 1<\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{N}.

(5.3)

Proof.

First, we prove (
5.1
). By Hölder’s inequality, for any
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)h(y)dy\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg\|\big(\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)^{s^{\prime}}dy\big)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\int_{\Omega}|h(y)|^{s}dy\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{N}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\bigg\|\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-2)s^{\prime}}}dy\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

where
s^{\prime}=\frac{s}{s-1}
. Since
\frac{1}{s}<\frac{2}{N}
,
(N-2)s^{\prime}<N
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-2)s^{\prime}}}dy\leq\int_{B_{d}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{(N-2)s^{\prime}}}dy=c_{44}\int_{0}^{d}r^{N-1-(N-2)s^{\prime}}dr\leq c_{45}d^{N-(N-2)s^{\prime}},

where
c_{44},c_{45}>0
and
d=\sup\{|x-y|:\ x,y\in\Omega\}
. Then (
5.1
) holds.

Next, we prove (
5.2
) for
r\leq s
and (
5.3
) for

r=1
. There holds

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)h(y)dy\bigg]^{r}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,y)h(y)\chi_{\Omega}(x)\chi_{\Omega}(y)dy\bigg]^{r}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{N}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h(y)\chi_{\Omega}(x)\chi_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2}}dy\bigg]^{r}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{N}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h(x-y)\chi_{\Omega}(x)\chi_{\Omega}(x-y)}{|y|^{N-2}}dy\right]^{r}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}.

By the Minkowski’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\bigg\{\int_{\Omega}\bigg[\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)h(y)dy\bigg]^{r}dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h^{r}(x-y)\chi_{\Omega}(x)\chi_{\Omega}(x-y)}{|y|^{(N-2)r}}dx\bigg]^{\frac{1}{r}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq c_{N}\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h^{r}(x-y)\chi_{\Omega}(x)\chi_{\Omega}(x-y)dx\bigg]^{\frac{1}{r}}\frac{1}{|y|^{N-2}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq c_{N}\|h\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq c_{46}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)},

where
c_{46}>0

\tilde{\Omega}=\{x-y:x,y\in\Omega\}
is bounded.

Finally, we prove (
5.2
) in the case

r>s\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{N}
, and (
5.3
) for
r>1,\,\,1<\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{N}

We claim that if
r>s
and
\frac{1}{r^{*}}=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{N}
, the mapping

h\to\mathbb{G}(h)
is of weak-type
(s,r^{*})
in the sense that

|\{x\in\Omega:|\mathbb{G}[h](x)|>t\}|\leq\big(A_{s,r^{*}}\frac{\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}}{t}\big)^{r^{*}},\quad h\in L^{s}(\Omega),\quad\rm{all}\ \ t>0,

(5.4)

where constant
A_{s,r^{*}}>0
.

Denote for
\nu>0
that

G_{0}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}G(x,y),&{\rm if}\quad|x-y|\leq\nu,\\[5.69054pt]
0,&{\rm if}\quad|x-y|>\nu\end{array}\right.

and
G_{\infty}(x,y)=G(x,y)-G_{0}(x,y)
. Then, we have

|\{x\in\Omega:|\mathbb{G}[h](x)|>2t\}|\leq|\{x\in\Omega:|\mathbb{G}_{0}[h](x)|>t\}|+|\{x\in\Omega:|\mathbb{G}_{\infty}[h](x)|>t\}|,

where
\mathbb{G}_{0}[h]
and
\mathbb{G}_{\infty}[h]
are defined
similar to
\mathbb{G}[h]
.

By the Minkowski’s inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle|\{x\in\Omega:|\mathbb{G}_{0}[h](x)|>t\}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbb{G}_{0}(h)\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\int_{\Omega}\chi_{B_{\nu}(x-y)}|x-y|^{2-N}|h(y)|dy\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{[\int_{\Omega}(\int_{\Omega}|h(x-y)|^{s}dx)^{\frac{1}{s}}|y|^{2-N}\chi_{B_{\nu}}(y)dy]^{s}}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\int_{B_{\nu}}|x|^{-N+2}dx}{t^{s}}=c_{47}\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\nu^{2}}{t^{s}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{G}_{\infty}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\int_{\Omega}\chi_{B_{\nu}^{c}}(x-y)|x-y|^{2-N}|h(y)|dy\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\int_{\Omega}|h(y)|^{s}dy)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|(\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\nu}(y)}|x-y|^{(2-N)s^{\prime}}dy)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{48}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\nu^{2-\frac{N}{s}},

where
s^{\prime}=\frac{s}{s-1}
if
s>1
, and if
s=1
,
s^{\prime}=\infty
. Choosing
\nu=(\frac{t}{c_{48}\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}})^{\frac{1}{2-\frac{N}{s}}}
, we obtain

\|\mathbb{G}_{\infty}[h]\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq t,

that means

|\{x\in\Omega:|\mathbb{G}_{\infty}[h](x)|>t\}|=0.

With this choice of
\nu
, we have that

|\{x\in\Omega:|\mathbb{G}[h]|>2t\}|\leq c_{49}\frac{\|h\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\nu^{2s}}{t^{s}}\leq c_{50}\left(\frac{\|h\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}}{t}\right)^{r^{*}}.

The claim
for
r>s
follows by the Marcinkiewicz
interpolation theorem.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain and

.

(5.5)

(5.6)

(iii)

\|\nabla\mathbb{G}[h]\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq c_{51}\|h\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\quad{\rm if}\quad 1<\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{N}.

(5.7)

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathbb{G}[h](x)|=|\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}G(x,y)h(y)\,dy|\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)|\,|h(y)|\,dy

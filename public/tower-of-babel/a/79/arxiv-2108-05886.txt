Geometry of the minimal solutions of a linear Diophantine Equation
Papa A. Sissokho

Abstract
. Let a 1 , … , a n a_{1},\ldots,a_{n} and b 1 , … , b m b_{1},\ldots,b_{m} be fixed positive integers, and let 𝒮 \mathcal{S} denote the set of all nonnegative integer solutions of the equation x 1 ​ a 1 + … + x n ​ a n = y 1 ​ b 1 + … + y m ​ b m x_{1}a_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}a_{n}=y_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+y_{m}b_{m} . A solution ( x 1 , … , x n , y 1 , … , y m ) (x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}) in 𝒮 \mathcal{S} is called minimal if it cannot be expressed as the sum of two nonzero solutions in 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . For each pair ( i , j ) (i,j) with 1 ≤ i ≤ n 1\leq i\leq n and 1 ≤ j ≤ m 1\leq j\leq m , the solution whose only nonzero coordinates are x i = b j x_{i}=b_{j} and y j = a i y_{j}=a_{i} is called a generator . Our main result shows that every minimal solution is a convex combination of the generators and the zero-solution. This proves a conjecture of Henk–Weismantel and, independently, Hosten–Sturmfels.

1.1.
Introduction

For any integer
t
, let
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:\;x\geq t\}
and define
\mathbb{R}_{\geq t}
in a similar manner. Given two vectors
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
and
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{m}
, let
\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
denote the set of all solutions
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
, with
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
, of the linear Diophantine equation

(1)

x_{1}a_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}a_{n}=y_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+y_{m}b_{m}.

For any nonzero solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
, define

(2)

{\rm supp}({\bf x}):=\{i:\,x_{i}>0,\;1\leq i\leq n\};\;{\rm supp}({\bf y}):=\{j:\,y_{j}>0,\;1\leq j\leq m\}.

A solution is called
minimal
if it cannot be
written as the sum of two nonzero solutions in
\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
. The set of all minimal solution of (
1
), denoted by
\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
, is the

Hilbert basis
of the pointed rational cone

\{({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}:\;{\bf a}\cdot{\bf x}={\bf b}\cdot{\bf y}\},

where
{\bf a}\cdot{\bf x}
denotes the dot product of
{\bf a}
and
{\bf x}
(similarly for
{\bf b}\cdot{\bf y}
).

For any integer
k
with
1\leq k\leq n+m
,
let
{\bf e}_{k}
denote the
k
th standard unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. If
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
, then
{\bf g}_{i,j}=(b_{j}{\bf e}_{i},a_{i}{\bf e}_{n+j})
is a solution of (
1
) called
generator
. A generator
{\bf g}_{i,j}
is minimal if and only if
\gcd(a_{i},b_{j})=1
. In particular, if
d_{i,j}=\gcd(a_{i},b_{j})
, then
(1/d_{i,j})\cdot{\bf g}_{i,j}
is minimal.
Let
\mathcal{W}({\bf a},{\bf b})
denote the convex hull of the
zero-solution and the elementary solutions
{\bf g}_{i,j}
,
i.e.,

(3)

\mathcal{W}({\bf a},{\bf b})={\rm conv}\big(\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\left\{{\bf g}_{i,j}:\,\mbox{$1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$}\right\}\big).

[

]
, and, independently

Conjecture 1
.

If
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
and
{\bf b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{m}
, then
\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})\subseteq\mathcal{W}({\bf a},{\bf b})

For
n=1
or

, it was noted in
[

]
that the Conjecture

[

]
(and, independently, by Diaconis–Graham–Sturmfels

||{\bf x}||_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}b_{j}\mbox{ and }||{\bf y}||_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}y_{j}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}.

The above upper bounds have been subsequently

[

(5)

||{\bf x}||_{1}\leq\frac{{\bf y}\cdot{\bf b}}{||{\bf y}||_{1}}\mbox{ and }||{\bf y}||_{1}\leq\frac{{\bf x}\cdot{\bf a}}{||{\bf x}||_{1}}.

1.2.
Main theorem

In this section, we prove the following theorem
and use it to verify Conjecture
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
and
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{m}
. If
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
, with
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
, then
there exist rational numbers
\lambda_{i,j}
such that

(6)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}\cdot b_{j}=x_{i}},\;&\mbox{ for $1\leq i\leq n$}\\
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i,j}\cdot a_{i}=y_{j},\;}&\mbox{ for $1\leq j\leq m$}\\
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}\leq 1}\\
\lambda_{i,j}\geq 0,\;&\mbox{ for $1\leq i\leq n$ and $1\leq j\leq m$}.\end{cases}

Conjecture
1
immediately follows from Theorem
2
by setting the coefficient of

{\bf 0}
to
1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}
. More precisely, we have the following corollary
whose (short) proof is in Section
2
.

Corollary 3
.

Every minimal solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})
is a convex combination of
{\bf 0}
and the generators.
Moreover, one can use up to
m+n-1
nonzero generators for any such combination.

Remark 4
.

Carathéodory’s theorem already predicts a convex combination of
({\bf x},{\bf y})

with at most
d+1
vertices from the convex hull
\mathcal{W}({\bf a},{\bf b})
given in (
3
), where
d=\dim\,\mathcal{W}({\bf a},{\bf b})=n+m-1
.

It was mentioned in
[
4
, Page 54]
that Hosten and Sturmfels found an example for which the convex hull of
{\bf 0}
and the minimal generators
\left((b_{j}/d_{i,j}){\bf e}_{i},(a_{i}/d_{i,j}){\bf e}_{n+j}\right)
, with
d_{i,j}=\gcd(a_{i},b_{j})
, does not contain the Hilbert basis
\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
. Since that “example” was not included in their article, we give another example of that fact below.

Example 5
.

Let
{\bf a}=(6)
and
{\bf b}=(2,3,5)
. Then

d_{1,1}=\gcd(6,2)=2
,
d_{1,2}=\gcd(6,3)=3
, and
d_{1,3}=\gcd(6,5)=1
.
Consider the minimal solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})=\left((2),(2,1,1)\right)
of the equation
6x_{1}=2y_{1}+3y_{2}+5y_{3}
.
To obtain a convex hull that involves the minimal generators, we need to find a solution
\left(\lambda_{1,1},\lambda_{1,2},\lambda_{1,3}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the following linear system:

\begin{cases}\frac{a_{1}}{d_{1,1}}\lambda_{1,1}=y_{1}\cr\frac{a_{1}}{d_{1,2}}\lambda_{1,2}=y_{2}\cr\frac{a_{1}}{d_{1,3}}\lambda_{1,3}=y_{3}\cr\frac{b_{1}}{d_{1,1}}\lambda_{1,1}+\frac{b_{2}}{d_{1,2}}\lambda_{1,2}+\frac{b_{3}}{d_{1,3}}\lambda_{1,3}=x_{1}\cr\lambda_{1,1}+\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{1,3}\leq 1\cr\lambda_{1,1},\,\lambda_{1,2},\,\lambda_{1,3}\geq 0.\end{cases}\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}3\lambda_{1,1}=2\cr 2\lambda_{1,2}=1\cr 6\lambda_{1,3}=1\cr\lambda_{1,1}+\lambda_{1,2}+5\lambda_{1,3}=2\cr\lambda_{1,1}+\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{1,3}\leq 1\cr\lambda_{1,1},\,\lambda_{1,2},\,\lambda_{1,3}\geq 0.\end{cases}

The only solution to the subsystem composed by the first four equations is
(\lambda_{1,1},\lambda_{1,2},\lambda_{1,3})=(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6})
. Thus,
\lambda_{1,1}+\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{1,3}=\frac{4}{3}
, which violates the fifth constraint.

However, in contrast to the situation illustrated in Example
5
, we have the following interesting corollary.

Corollary 6
.

If
\gcd(a_{i},b_{j})=1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
, then the
generators
{\bf g}_{i,j}=(b_{j}{\bf e}_{i},a_{i}{\bf e}_{n+j})
are all minimal and the set of extreme points of
{\rm conv}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})\right)

is

\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\left\{{\bf g}_{i,j}:\,\mbox{$1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$}\right\}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
is the set of minimal solutions, it follows from the first part of Corollary
3
that

\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})\subseteq{\rm conv}\big(\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\left\{{\bf g}_{i,j}:\,\mbox{$1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$}\right\}\big),

which implies that

(7)

{\rm conv}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})\right)\subseteq{\rm conv}\big(\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\left\{{\bf g}_{i,j}:\,\mbox{$1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$}\right\}\big).

Moreover, since
\gcd(a_{i},b_{j})=1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
, the elements of
\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\left\{{\bf g}_{i,j}:\,\mbox{$1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$}\right\}
are minimal and belong to the Hilbert basis
\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
. Thus,

(8)

{\rm conv}\big(\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\left\{{\bf g}_{i,j}:\,\mbox{$1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$}\right\}\big)\subseteq{\rm conv}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})\right).

By combining (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain

{\rm conv}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})\right)={\rm conv}\big(\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\left\{{\bf g}_{i,j}:\,\mbox{$1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$}\right\}\big).

Finally, it follows from the above equality and the minimality of the generators
{\bf g}_{i,j}
and
{\bf 0}
that they are the extreme points of
{\rm conv}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})\right)
.
∎

Before ending this section, we present a useful way of viewing a solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
as a

partition identity
, which is an equality of the form

\underbrace{a_{1}+\ldots+a_{1}}_{\mbox{$x_{1}$ copies}}+\ldots+\underbrace{a_{n}+\ldots+a_{n}}_{\mbox{$x_{n}$ copies}}=\underbrace{b_{1}+\ldots+b_{1}}_{\mbox{$y_{1}$ copies}}+\ldots+\underbrace{b_{m}+\ldots+b_{m}}_{\mbox{$y_{m}$ copies}}

where we skip a term
a_{i}
(resp.
b_{j}
) if
x_{i}=0
(resp.
y_{j}=0
). A partition identity is called
primitive
if it does not contain
a proper nonempty subpartition identity. For instance,
2+2+3=2+5
is not primitive since it contains the subpartition identity
2+3=5
. Primitive Partition identities were introduced by Diaconis–Graham–Sturmfels
[
1
]
, where their relevance and applications to several areas were demonstrated. In the proof of our main theorem (see Section
2
), we sometimes view minimal solutions in
\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
as primitive partition identities.

1.3.
Graver Basis

Let
r
and
k
be positive integers. For each
\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{k})\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
, we can associate the following orthant of
\mathbb{R}^{k}

\mathcal{O}^{(\tau)}=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:\;\tau_{i}x_{i}\geq 0\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq k\}.

Let
A
be an
r\times k
matrix with entries in

\mathbb{Z}
and define the relation
\sqsubseteq
on
D=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:\;A{\bf x}={\bf 0}\mbox{ and }{\bf x}\not={\bf 0}\}
as follows. For any
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
and
{\bf x}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{k})
in
D
, we write
{\bf x}^{\prime}\sqsubseteq{\bf x}
if these two vectors are in the same orthant of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
|x^{\prime}_{i}|\leq|x_{i}|
for
1\leq i\leq k
. For instance
(1,-2,3)\sqsubseteq(1,-3,3)
, but
(1,-2,-3)

and
(2,1,2)
are not comparable since they live in different orthants of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We say that
{\bf x}\in D
is
\sqsubseteq
-minimal if there is no
{\bf x}^{\prime}\in D
such that
{\bf x}^{\prime}\sqsubseteq{\bf x}
.
The
Graver basis
of
A
, denoted by
\mathcal{G}(A)
,
is the set of all
\sqsubseteq
-minimal vectors in
D
.

The concept of a Graver basis was introduced by Graver
[
3
]
as a method for solving certain classes of linear and integer optimization problems. This has since been extended to a wider class of problems along with polynomial-time (in the size of the inputs) algorithms (e.g., see
[
2
, Chapter 3]
).
If
\mathcal{H}^{(\tau)}
denote the Hilbert basis of the
pointed cone
\mathcal{O}^{(\tau)}\cap\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:\;A{\bf x}={\bf 0}\}
,
then it is well-known that

(9)

\mathcal{G}(A)=\bigcup_{\tau\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{(\tau)}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}.

Because of this direct relationship between the Hilbert
basis and the Graver basis, our main theorem yields
the following corollary when
A
has a single row.

Corollary 7
.

Suppose
A
has a single row, i.e.,
A=\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.

(i)
For each orientation
\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{k})\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{(\tau)}\subseteq{\rm conv}\left(\mathcal{F}^{(\tau)}\right)
,
where

\mathcal{F}^{(\tau)}=\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\{\tau_{i}|\alpha_{j}|{\bf e}_{i}+\tau_{j}|\alpha_{i}|{\bf e}_{j}:\;1\leq i\not=j\leq k,\;(\tau_{i}\alpha_{i})(\tau_{j}\alpha_{j})<0\}.

(ii)

\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\subseteq{\rm conv}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
,
where

\mathcal{F}=\{{\bf 0}\}\cup\{\sigma|\alpha_{j}|{\bf e}_{i}+\delta|\alpha_{i}|{\bf e}_{j}:\;1\leq i\not=j\leq k,\;\sigma,\delta\in\{-1,1\},\;\sigma\alpha_{i}\delta\alpha_{j}<0\}.

Corollary

1.4.
Completely Fundamental Solutions

A solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
is called
completely fundamental
if for every decomposition
k({\bf x},{\bf y})=({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})+({\bf x}^{\prime\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime\prime})
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
, and
({\bf x}^{\prime\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
, there exist nonnegative integers
r
and
s
such that

({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})=r({\bf x},{\bf y}),\;({\bf x}^{\prime\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime\prime})=s({\bf x},{\bf y}),\;\mbox{ and }r+s=k.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{c}({\bf a},{\bf b})
denote the set of all completely fundamental solutions in
\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
. In particular

\mathcal{S}_{c}({\bf a},{\bf b})\subseteq\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
.
Completely fundamental solutions were introduced by Stanley
[
7
]
who used them to characterize
a certain generating function associated with
the Hilbert basis of a system of linear homogeneous Diophantine equations. In the case of a single equation,
that this paper is concerned with, the generating function in question is as follows:

(11)

F_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}({\bf z},{\bf w})=F_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})=\sum_{({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{S}}z_{1}^{x_{1}}\ldots z_{n}^{x_{n}}\cdot w_{1}^{y_{1}}\ldots w_{m}^{y_{m}}=\sum_{({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{S}}{\bf z}^{{\bf x}}\,{\bf w}^{{\bf y}},

where
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
,
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
.
In the next corollary (of Theorem
2
), we
determine the completely minimal solutions in
\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
. Note that we only use the fact that
the
\lambda_{i,j}
coefficients in Theorem
2
are rational numbers and not that their sum is at most
1
.

Corollary 10
.

A solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
is completely fundamental if and only if
({\bf x},{\bf y})
is a minimal generator; i.e.,

\mathcal{S}_{c}({\bf a},{\bf b})=\{\left((b_{j}/d_{i,j}){\bf e}_{i},(a_{i}/d_{i,j}){\bf e}_{n+j}\right):\,1\leq i\leq n,\;1\leq j\leq m\},

where
d_{i,j}=\gcd(a_{i},b_{j})
.

We can now deduce the following corollary from Corollary
10
and a result of Stanley
[
7
, Theorem
2.5
]
.

Corollary 11
.

If
\mathcal{S}_{c}=\mathcal{S}_{c}({\bf a},{\bf b})
be the set of all completely fundamental solutions in
\mathcal{S}({\bf a},{\bf b})
, then

F_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}({\bf z},{\bf w})
is a rational function with denominator

D_{{\bf a},{\bf b}}({\bf z},{\bf w})=\prod_{({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}(1-{\bf z}^{{\bf x}}\,{\bf w}^{{\bf y}})=\prod_{\left((b_{j}/d_{i,j}){\bf e}_{i},(a_{i}/d_{i,j}){\bf e}_{n+j}\right)\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}\left(1-z_{i}^{b_{j}/d_{i,j}}\,w_{j}^{a_{i}/d_{i,j}}\right).

2.
Proof of the main results

In this section, we prove Theorem
2
, Corollary
3
, and Corollary
7
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Fix the coefficients
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
and consider a (nonzero) minimal solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
. The proof is by induction on
||{\bf x}||_{1}+||{\bf y}||_{1}
, which is at least
2
.
We first argue that one may assume (without loss of generality) that
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are binary vectors. To see this, first
note that from (
4
) implies
x_{i}\leq||{\bf x}||_{1}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}b_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and
y_{j}\leq||{\bf y}||_{1}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}
for
1\leq j\leq m
. For each
i
such that
x_{i}\geq 1
, let

a^{\prime}_{i,k}=a_{i}
for
1\leq k\leq x_{i}
(i.e., we create
x_{i}
copies of
a_{i}
), and for each
j
such that
y_{j}\geq 1
, let
b^{\prime}_{j,s}=b_{j}
for
j\leq s\leq y_{j}
. Now define
p:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=||{\bf x}||_{1}
,
q:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}y_{j}=||{\bf y}||_{1}
,

{\bf a}^{\prime}=(a^{\prime}_{1,1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{1,x_{1}},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{n,1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{n,x_{n}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{p},

and

{\bf b}^{\prime}=(b^{\prime}_{1,1},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{1,y_{1}},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{m,1},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{m,y_{m}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{q}.

Then the pair
\left((1,\ldots,1),(1,\ldots,1)\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{p+q}
is a minimal nonnegative solution of the Diophantine equation

(12)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{x_{i}}x^{\prime}_{i,k}a^{\prime}_{i,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\ell=1}^{y_{j}}y^{\prime}_{j,\ell}b^{\prime}_{i,k}}\\
x^{\prime}_{i,k},y^{\prime}_{j,\ell}\in\{0,1\},\end{cases}

which is equivalent to the solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})
that we started with.
Since
||{\bf x}||_{1}+||{\bf y}||_{1}
will decrease at each step of the inductive step, the transformed dimension
p+q=||{\bf x}||_{1}+||{\bf y}||_{1}

is also decreasing. Moreover, as we shall see, the maximum entry in any generator
{\bf g}_{i,j}
, used in the convex combination of
({\bf x},{\bf y})
, will not increase at any stage of the inductive step because the coefficients (originally
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
) will not increase.
Thus, it suffices to prove the theorem for linear Diophantine equations with binary variables. In particular, we may assume, without loss of generality, that for any minimal solution
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})
, we have

x_{i}=1
if
i\in{\rm supp}({\bf x})
and
x_{i}=0
if
i\not\in{\rm supp}({\bf x})
(and similarly for
y_{j}
).
Let
s
and
t
be such that

a_{s}=\max_{i\in{\rm supp}({\bf x})}a_{i}\mbox{ and }b_{t}=\max_{j\in{\rm supp}({\bf y})}b_{j}.

For the base case, let
||{\bf x}||_{1}+||{\bf y}||_{1}=2
. Then
||{\bf x}||_{1}=||{\bf y}||_{1}=1
,
{\rm supp}({\bf x})=\{s\}
,
{\rm supp}({\bf y})=\{t\}
,
a_{s}=b_{t}
, and
({\bf x},{\bf y})=({\bf e}_{s},{\bf e}_{t})
. If we set
\lambda_{s,t}=\frac{1}{a_{s}}=\frac{1}{b_{t}}
and
\lambda_{i,j}=0
for
(i,j)\not=(s,t)
,
then
\lambda_{s,t}\leq 1
and the required constraints
in (
6
) are clearly satisfied.

Next, assume that
||{\bf x}||_{1}+||{\bf y}||_{1}>2
. Then, it follows from the minimality of
({\bf x},{\bf y})
that
a_{s}\not=b_{t}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
a_{s}>b_{t}
. Let
a_{n+1}=a_{s}-b_{t}
(thus, the new coefficient
a_{n+1}
is decreasing) and consider the vectors
{\bf x}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n+1})
and
{\bf y}^{\prime}=(y^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,y^{\prime}_{m})
given by

(13)

\begin{cases}\mbox{$x^{\prime}_{n+1}=1$, $x^{\prime}_{s}=0$, $x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}$ for $1\leq i\leq n$ and $i\not=s$},\\
\mbox{$y^{\prime}_{t}=0,\;y_{j}^{\prime}=y_{j}$ for $1\leq j\leq m$ and $j\not=t$}.\end{cases}

Then it can easily be seen that
({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})
is also a minimal solution of the Diophantine equation

\begin{cases}z_{1}a_{1}+\ldots+z_{n+1}a_{n+1}=w_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+\ldots+w_{m}b_{m}\\
z_{i},w_{j}\in\{0,1\}\end{cases}

Moreover, we have

{\rm supp}({\bf x}^{\prime})=\left({\rm supp}({\bf x})\setminus\{s\}\right)\cup\{n+1\},\;{\rm supp}({\bf y}^{\prime})={\rm supp}({\bf y})\setminus\{t\},\;||{\bf x}^{\prime}||_{1}=||{\bf x}||_{1},\mbox{ and }||{\bf y}^{\prime}||_{1}=||{\bf y}||_{1}-1.

Hence,
2\leq||{\bf x}^{\prime}||_{1}+||{\bf y}^{\prime}||_{1}<||{\bf x}||_{1}+||{\bf y}||_{1}
, and it follows from the induction hypothesis that there exist nonnegative rational numbers
\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}
such that,

(14)

\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}=0,\,\mbox{ if }(i,j)\not\in{\rm supp}({\bf x}^{\prime})\times{\rm supp}({\bf y}^{\prime}),

(15)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}b_{j}=x^{\prime}_{i}},\,&\mbox{ if $1\leq i\leq n+1$}\\
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}a_{i}=y^{\prime}_{j}},\,&\mbox{ if $1\leq j\leq m$}.\end{cases}

(16)

\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}\leq 1}.

Since
t\not\in{\rm supp}({\bf y}^{\prime})
, it follows from (
14
) that
\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,t}=0
. This fact, together with (
13
) and (
15
) imply that

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}b_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}b_{j}=x^{\prime}_{n+1}=1.

Since
b_{t}=\max\limits_{j\in{\rm supp}({\bf y})}b_{j}\geq 1
and
{\rm supp}({\bf y}^{\prime})\subseteq{\rm supp}({\bf y})
, it follows from (
17
) that

(18)

\displaystyle b_{t}\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}\geq\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}b_{j}=1

(19)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}\geq\frac{1}{b_{t}}.

We now define
\{\lambda_{i,j}\}_{i=1,j=1}^{n,m}
as follows:

(20)

\begin{cases}\lambda_{s,t}\coloneqq\frac{1}{a_{s}}\\
\lambda_{s,j}\coloneqq\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}+b_{t}}\cdot\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}=\frac{a_{s}-b_{t}}{a_{s}}\cdot\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j},&\mbox{ if $1\leq j\leq m$ and $j\not=t$}\\
\lambda_{i,t}\coloneqq 0=\lambda^{\prime}_{i,t}\mbox{ (since $t\not\in{\rm supp({\bf y}^{\prime})}$)},&\mbox{ if $1\leq i\leq n$ and $i\not=s$}\\
\lambda_{i,j}\coloneqq\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j},&\mbox{ if $1\leq i\leq n$, $1\leq j\leq m$, $i\not=s$, and $j\not=t$}.\end{cases}

Thus, the
\lambda_{i,j}
’s are nonnegative rational numbers. It also follows from (
13
), (
14
), and (
20
) that

(21)

\lambda_{i,j}=0,\,\mbox{ if }(i,j)\not\in{\rm supp}({\bf x})\times{\rm supp}({\bf y}).

By induction hypotheses in (
14
)–(
15
) and the definition of
\lambda_{i,j}

in (
20
), it follows that if
1\leq i\leq n
and
i\not=s
, then

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}b_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}b_{j}=x^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}.

If
1\leq j\leq m
and
j\not=t
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i,j}a_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1,\,i\not=s}^{n}\lambda_{i,j}a_{i}+\lambda_{s,j}a_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1,i\not=s}^{n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}a_{i}+\left(\frac{a_{s}-b_{t}}{a_{s}}\right)\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}a_{s}\quad\mbox{(by~\eqref{eq:al})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1,i\not=s}^{n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}a_{i}+\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}(a_{s}-b_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1,i\not=s}^{n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}a_{i}+\lambda^{\prime}_{s,j}a_{s}+\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}a_{n+1}\quad\mbox{(since $\lambda^{\prime}_{s,j}=0$ and $a_{n+1}=a_{s}-b_{t}$)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}a_{i}

\displaystyle=y^{\prime}_{j}=y_{j}\quad\mbox{(by~\eqref{eq:IH2} and~\eqref{eq:set}).}

Moreover, (
14
) and (
20
) yield

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i,t}a_{i}=\lambda_{s,t}a_{s}=\frac{1}{a_{s}}\cdot a_{s}=1=y_{t},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{s,j}b_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda_{s,j}b_{j}+\lambda_{s,t}\cdot b_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\left(\frac{a_{s}-b_{t}}{a_{s}}\cdot\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}\right)b_{j}+\lambda_{s,t}\cdot b_{t}\quad\mbox{(by~\eqref{eq:al})}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{s}-b_{t}}{a_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}\cdot b_{j}+\frac{b_{t}}{a_{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{s}-b_{t}}{a_{s}}\,x^{\prime}_{n+1}+\frac{b_{t}}{a_{s}}\quad\mbox{ (by~\eqref{eq:IH2})}

\displaystyle=1=x_{s}\quad\mbox{ (since $x^{\prime}_{n+1}=1$ by~\eqref{eq:set})}.

Still using (
14
), (
20
), and (
21
), we infer that

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}

(23)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1,\,i\not=s}^{n}\lambda_{i,t}-\sum_{i=1,\,i\not=s}^{n}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,t}-\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,t}\right)+\left(\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda_{s,j}-\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}\right)+(\lambda_{s,t}-\lambda^{\prime}_{s,t})

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda_{s,j}+\lambda_{s,t}-\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}\quad\mbox{(since $\lambda^{\prime}_{s,t}=0$, $\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,t}=0$, and $\lambda_{i,t}=\lambda^{\prime}_{i,t}$ if $i\not=s$)}

(25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\left(\frac{a_{s}-b_{t}}{a_{s}}\right)\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}+\frac{1}{a_{s}}-\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{s}}\left(1-b_{t}\sum_{j=1,\,j\not=t}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{n+1,j}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

where the last inequality follows from (
18
).

Finally, it follows from (
16
) and (
22
) that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i,j}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{i,j}\leq 1,

which completes the inductive step.
∎

. Since

{\bf x}
of (

x_{i}=\tau_{i}z_{i}

. Moreover, the solution

that
{\bf z}

{\bf 0}

). By definition, these generators are the elements of the set

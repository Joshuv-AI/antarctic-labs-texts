Investigating the soft and hard limits in transverse momentum spectra in pp collisions.
Gábor Bíró, Leonid Serkin, Guy Paić, Gergely Gábor Barnaföldi

Abstract
The transverse momentum spectra and their multiplicity dependence serve as key tools for extracting parameters to be compared with theoretical models. Over the past decade, the scientific community has extensively studied the possibility of a system analogous to quark-gluon plasma, predicted in heavy nuclei collisions, also existing in collisions involving light nuclei and protons. We have reanalysed the data published by the ALICE Collaboration at the LHC. We present the dependence of the mean transverse momenta obtained in the soft and soft+hard (mixed) parts. Finally, we also discuss possible refinements of the analyses concerning the use of statistical parameters of higher order, aimed at a more detailed way of comparing the models with data.

In recent years, the ALICE Collaboration has measured the charged-particle transverse momentum spectra as a function of charged-particle multiplicity at various centre-of-mass energies,
\sqrt{s}
. A parametrization over the full-

Hagedorn 1983

Recent studies showed that traditional, Boltzmann – Gibbs statistics-based effective models are able to take into account the enhancement of soft pions, and therefore give a good description of the experimental data up to 4-5 GeV/
c

data by nature. The left panel of Fig.

\sqrt{s}=13

few GeV/

p_{\rm T}
part representing purely soft interaction, an intermediate part where the hard interactions get mixed with soft ones, and finally beyond around 4 GeV/
c

In the right panel yields in multiplicity classes are presented relative to the inclusive yield. In comparison to the left panel, an interesting observation is: both the best fit for the low-
p_{\rm T}
range and the centrality-class normalized yields select a natural value at
p_{\rm T}\approx 0.6
GeV/

4
Discussion

While reanalysing the experimental charged particles
p_{\rm T}
spectra published by the ALICE Collaboration, as presented in Fig.
2
, a consistent crossing-point at around
\sim
0.6 GeV/
c
transverse momenta value was observed when comparing the ratio of the spectra to the inclusive distribution. This pattern, observed across different
\left<{\mathrm{d}}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/{\mathrm{d}}\eta\right>
classes and over a wide range of collision energies, is also present in the predictions from both
Pythia 8
and
Epos 4
models, as can be seen in Fig.
3
.

The separation of the charged particle
p_{\rm T}
spectra into two regions defined by the observed crossing-point resulted in two markedly different behaviours of
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
in each region. The results shown in Fig.
4
indicate the absence of any variation with
\left<{\mathrm{d}}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/{\mathrm{d}}\eta\right>
and/or collision energy in the values of
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
and
\sigma^{2}
within the soft, low-
p_{\rm T}
region. Our results are consistent with the hypothesis of centre-of-mass energy invariance within the low-
p_{\rm T}
part of the spectra, a phenomenon observed by the CDF collaboration in
pp
collisions at
\sqrt{s}
= 630 GeV and 1.8 TeV
Acosta et al. 2002
. On the other hand, the increase of
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
as a function of multiplicity is moderate for the high-
p_{\rm T}
region
Abelev et al. 2013
.

While based on significantly different underlying assumptions, where
Pythia 8
is rooted in perturbative QCD and string fragmentation and
Epos 4
incorporates collective effects and hydrodynamic considerations, the results in Fig.
5
demonstrate that both Monte Carlo models exhibit the same independence of
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
and
\sigma^{2}
on
\left<{\mathrm{d}}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/{\mathrm{d}}\eta\right>
within the low-
p_{\rm T}
range.

The
p_{\rm T}
value around 0.6 GeV/
c
seems to act as a characteristic momentum scale across different multiplicities. At this momentum, the production rates from various multiplicity classes converge to that of the inclusive one, suggesting a transition in the particle production mechanisms. The region below 0.6 GeV/
c
is predominantly associated with soft processes, while the region beyond comprises a mixture of soft and hard ones. Therefore, this low-
p_{\rm T}
region serves as an excellent laboratory for studying soft physics in
pp
collisions.

We observe that the low-
p_{\rm T}
region yields the same
mean
values.
Figure
4
shows that neither
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
nor
\sigma^{2}
are sensitive to the multiplicity variations in the same region.

Finally, we advocate for the use of statistical parameters of higher order, such as variance and/or skewness
Giacalone et al. 2021
to identify the differences between different models, Monte Carlo simulations, and experimental data. This is particularly important since the weight of soft and hard processes may differ in the models, and yet resulting in the same mean value. The comparison in Fig.
4
does indeed point to difference in the behaviors of the two statistical parameters.

5
Conclusions

The observed trends lead us to claim that indeed we are in presence of two different modes withe fact that the soft part of the interactions are reflecting a a mode of interaction common to all collision energies and multiplicities at the LHC. It is important to underline the importance of this conclusion since there are many attempts to study the collective effects that do extend the range of considered
p_{\rm T}
ranges well above the 0.6 GeV/
c
, risking important contributions of collisions of the hard nature

We have conducted a detailed analysis of the multiplicity dependence of normalised transverse momentum distributions of charged particles, using the extensive dataset of publicly available
pp
collision data at
\sqrt{s}
= 0.9, 5.02, 7 and 13 TeV from the ALICE Collaboration.

We observe that the mean transverse momentum
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
is highly sensitive to the selected
p_{\rm T}
range, and it is not sensitive within the soft region (low-
p_{\rm T}
) region studied of
0.15
GeV/
c

\leq p_{\rm T}\leq 0.6
GeV/
c
. Our investigation reveals a so-far unidentified phenomenon: a universal crossing point of the ratios to the inclusive spectrum at
p_{\rm T}\approx 0.6
GeV/
c
. Below 0.6 GeV/
c
,
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
exhibits an intriguing invariance from the charged-particle pseudorapidity densities and collision energy.

This result is supported by
Pythia 8
with different underlying event and generator settings, as well as by
Epos 4
.
We examine a higher-order statistical parameter, such as variance, to gain further insights into how well the Monte Carlo models agree among themselves and with experimental data.

The absence of any significant
\left<p_{\rm T}\right>
and
\sigma^{2}
variation in the low-
p_{\rm T}
region suggests that the selection of
p_{\rm T}
ranges in analyses demands greater scrutiny and justification to avoid potentially misleading conclusions.
Our study thus contributes to the ongoing controversy regarding the presence of collective effects in small systems.
Finally, we would like to emphasise that the current conclusions apply to the existing
\left<{\mathrm{d}}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/{\mathrm{d}}\eta\right>
range measured with sufficient statistics by the ALICE Collaboration. Future LHC runs should enable us to extend our investigation beyond the current multiplicity reach.

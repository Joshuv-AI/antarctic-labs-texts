2
Symmetry of solutions

Let us study the positive solutions to the integral equation

u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}K_{b}(x,y)y_{n}^{b}f(u(y))dy\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where

\begin{split}&K_{b}(x,y)=D_{b}\int_{0}^{1}(|x-y|^{2}(1-\tau)+|x-y^{*}|^{2}\tau)^{-\frac{n+b-2}{2}}[\tau(1-\tau)]^{\frac{b}{2}-1}d\tau,\\
&D_{b}=2^{b-2}\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+b-2}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{b}{2})},\quad y^{*}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},-y_{n}).\end{split}

(2.1)

Moreover, for
b>0,n\geq 3
, the following estimates for
K_{b}(x,y)
holds

|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}K_{b}(x,y)|\leq C(\gamma,b)|x-y^{*}|^{-b}|x-y|^{2-n-|\gamma|},

(2.2)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
(see Proposition 1-2 of
[
13
]
).
Since we don’t know the behaviors of
u
at infinity, we introduce the Kelvin’s transform of
u
as
v(x)=\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+b-2}}u\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\right)
.

\forall x_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we define
w(x):=u(x-x_{0})
. Since

\displaystyle w(x)

\displaystyle=u(x-x_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}K_{b}(x,y+x_{0})y^{b}_{n}f(u(y))dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}K_{b}(x,y)y^{b}_{n}f(w(y))dy,

so we take
x_{0}=0
. We consider
v(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n+b-2}}u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)
. Then a direct calculation shows that
v(x)
solves

v(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}K_{b}(x,y)y_{n}^{b}f(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})\frac{1}{|y|^{n+b+2}}dy\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

We define
\tilde{\tau}=\frac{n+b+2}{n+b-2}
. By the definition of
g(t)=\frac{f(t)}{t^{\tilde{\tau}}}\displaystyle
, we deduce that
v(x)
satisfies

v(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}K_{b}(x,y)y_{n}^{b}g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v^{\tilde{\tau}}dy.

Since
u
is continuous in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
, we conclude that
v
is continuous and positive in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\setminus\{0\}
with possible singularity at the origin. Moreover,
v
decays at infinity as
u(0)|x|^{2-n-b}
. By the asymptotic behavior of
v
at
\infty
, we get

v(x)\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{r}(0))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{r}(0))

for all
r>0
. Now we use the moving plane method to prove our result.

For a given real number
\lambda
, define

\Sigma_{\lambda}=\{x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\ |\ x_{1}\geq\lambda\},\quad T_{\lambda}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\ |\ x_{1}=\lambda\}

and let
x^{\lambda}=(2\lambda-x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
and
u_{\lambda}(x)=u(x^{\lambda})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

\displaystyle v(x)-v(x^{\lambda})=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}(K_{b}(x,y)-K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y))y^{b}_{n}\times

\displaystyle[g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}-g(v(y^{\lambda})|y^{\lambda}|^{n+b-2})v(y^{\lambda})^{\tilde{\tau}}]dy.

Proof.
By the definition of
K_{b}(x,y)
, we know
K_{b}(x,y)=K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y^{\lambda})
,
K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y)=K_{b}(x,y^{\lambda})
. A direct calculation implies

\displaystyle v(x)=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}K_{b}(x,y)y^{b}_{n}g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}dy+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}K_{b}(x,y^{\lambda})y^{b}_{n}g(v(y^{\lambda})|y^{\lambda}|^{n+b-2})v(y^{\lambda})^{\tilde{\tau}}dy,

and

\displaystyle v(x^{\lambda})=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y)y^{b}_{n}g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}dy+\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y^{\lambda})y^{b}_{n}g(v(y^{\lambda})|y^{\lambda}|^{n+b-2})v(y^{\lambda})^{\tilde{\tau}}dy.

We get the desired result.
\Box

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, we have
v_{\lambda}(x)\geq v(x)
for all
x\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

\forall\lambda>0
, it is easy to see that
|y|>|y^{\lambda}|
,
\forall y\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
. Let

\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}=\{y\in\Sigma_{\lambda}\ |\ v(y)>v_{\lambda}(y)\},

then
\forall y\in\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}
, we have

v(y)|y|^{n+b-2}>v_{\lambda}(y)|y^{\lambda}|^{n+b-2}.

Since
g(t)
is non-increasing (see Theorem
1.1
(ii)), we get

g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})\leq g(v_{\lambda}(y)|y^{\lambda}|^{n+b-2}).

This implies

\displaystyle g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}-g(v_{\lambda}(y)|y^{\lambda}|^{n+b-2})v_{\lambda}(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}\leq g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})(v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}-v_{\lambda}(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}).

(2.3)

As for
y\in\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}
, using the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
we have

\displaystyle g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})}{|y|^{n+b+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{f(v_{\lambda}(y)|y|^{n+b-2})}{|y|^{n+b+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(v_{\lambda}(y)|y|^{n+b-2})}{[|y|^{n+b-2}v_{\lambda}(y)]^{\tilde{\tau}}}v_{\lambda}(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}

\displaystyle=g(v_{\lambda}(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v_{\lambda}(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq g(v_{\lambda}(y)|y^{\lambda}|^{n+b-2})v_{\lambda}(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}.

(2.4)

Then by Lemma
2.1
and the mean value theorem, we know that there exists a
\xi
between
y
and
y^{\lambda}
that makes

v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}-v_{\lambda}(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}=\tilde{\tau}v(\xi)^{\tilde{\tau}-1}(v(y)-v_{\lambda}(y))

(2.5)

true. Combining Lemma
2.1
, (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle v(x)-v_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}}(K_{b}(x,y)-K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y))y^{b}_{n}g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})(v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}}-v_{\lambda}(y)^{\tilde{\tau}})dy

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\tau}\int_{\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}}(K_{b}(x,y)-K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y))y^{b}_{n}g(v(y)|y|^{n+b-2})v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}-1}(v(y)-v_{\lambda}(y))dy.

In getting the above inequality, we also used
\forall x,y\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
,
K_{b}(x,y)\geq K_{b}(x^{\lambda},y)
and
\forall y\in\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}
,
v_{\lambda}(y)\leq v(\xi)\leq v(y)
. Since

|y|^{n+b-2}v(y)=u\left(\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\right)\geq\min_{B_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}}u(x)\geq C_{\lambda}>0,\quad\forall\lambda>0,\ y\in\Sigma_{\lambda},

and
f
is continuous, we conclude that
g(|y|^{n+b-2}v(y))
is bounded for
y\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
. Hence we can deduce from the above inequality that

v(x)-v_{\lambda}(x)\leq C\int_{\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}}K_{b}(x,y)y^{b}_{n}v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}-1}(v(y)-v_{\lambda}(y))dy,\quad\forall\lambda>0,\ x\in\Sigma_{\lambda}.

By (
2.2
) and since
\frac{y^{b}_{n}}{|x-y*|^{b}}\leq 1
, then

v(x)-v_{\lambda}(x)\leq C\int_{\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}v(y)^{\tilde{\tau}-1}(v(y)-v_{\lambda}(y))dy.

By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, it follows that for any
q>\frac{n}{n-2}
,

\|v-v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda})}\leq C\|v^{\tilde{\tau}-1}(v-v_{\lambda})\|_{L^{\frac{qn}{n+2q}}(\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda})}.

By using the generalized H
\ddot{o}
lder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|v-v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v^{\tilde{\tau}-1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{2}}(\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda})}\|v-v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=C\left(\int_{\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}}v^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\|v-v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda})}.

Due to
v\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{r}(0))
, we can choose
\lambda_{0}
sufficiently large, such that for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, we have

C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}v^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then we conclude

\|v-v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda})}=0

for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. Thus,
\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}
must be measure
0
. Since
v
is continuous, we deduce that
\Sigma^{-}_{\lambda}
is empty.
\Box

We define
\lambda_{1}=\inf\{\lambda\ |\ v(x)\leq v_{\mu}(x),\ \forall\mu\geq\lambda,\ x\in\Sigma_{\mu}\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\lambda_{1}>0
, then
v(x)\equiv v_{\lambda_{1}}(x)
for all

.

Proof.
Suppose that conclusion does not hold. We have

, but

in

. We will prove that the plane can be moved further to the left. We infer from the proof of Lemma

(2.6)

If one can show that for

, there holds

then by (

, and therefore

must be measure zero. Since

is continuous, we deduce that

is empty. This contradicts the definition of

.

Now we verify inequality (

). Since

, for any small

we can choose

We fix this

close to

. Since the measure of

It is obviously that

For

Apparently, the measure of

Proof for Theorem 1.1

We define

\mathcal{L}_{b}(u)=-\text{div}(y_{n}^{b}\nabla u).

Take a cutoff function
\varphi_{R}(y)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R})
such that

,
\varphi_{R}=1

. Setting

we clearly have

, where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is the dual space of

. By the result of
[

]

\mathcal{L}_{b}K_{b}(x,y)=\delta(x-y)

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem

, there exists

Proof.
The proof is similar to Lemma
2.2
. We denote by
\Sigma^{w}_{\lambda}=\{y\in\Sigma_{\lambda}\ |\ w(y)>w_{\lambda}(y)\}
and
\Sigma^{z}_{\lambda}=\{y\in\Sigma_{\lambda}\ |\ z(y)>z_{\lambda}(y)\}
. By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|w-w_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{w}_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{z}}z^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\|z-z_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{z}_{\lambda})},

\displaystyle\|z-z_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{z}_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{w}}w^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\|w-w_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{w}_{\lambda})},

where
q>\frac{n}{n-2}
. The above two inequalities imply

\displaystyle\|w-w_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{w}_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{w}}w^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{z}}z^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\|w-w_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{w}_{\lambda})},

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\|z-z_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{z}_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{w}}w^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{z}}z^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\|z-z_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{z}_{\lambda})}.

(4.4)

Since
w(x)
,
z(x)\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{r}(0))
for any
r>0
, we choose
\lambda_{0}
large enough such that

C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}w^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}z^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}

for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. Then we have

\|w-w_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{w}_{\lambda})}=\|z-z_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma^{z}_{\lambda})}=0

for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. So we get the desired result.
\Box

in the same area. If one can show that for

, there holds

C\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{w}}w^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}^{z}}z^{\frac{2n}{n+b-2}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

(4.5)

then by (
4.3

Solving these inequalities, we get for all

(4.14)

Multiplying (

(4.16)

Using the fact that (

(4.19)

F(t)=\int^{t}_{0}f(s)ds

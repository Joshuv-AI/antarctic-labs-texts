The Elephant random walk with gradually increasing memory
Allan Gut, Ulrich Stadtmüller

Abstract
In the simple random walk the steps are independent, viz., the walker has no memory. In contrast, in the elephant random walk (ERW), which was introduced by Schütz and Trimper [ 13 ] in 2004, the next step always depends on the whole path so far. Various authors have studied further properties of the ERW. In [ 8 ] we studied the case when the Elephant remembers only a finite part of the first or last steps. In both cases there was no separation into two different regimes as in the classical ERW. We also posed the question about what happens if she remembers a gradually increasing past. This paper will give some answers to that question. We also discuss related questions for ERW:s with delays.

Introduction

In the classical
simple
random walk the steps are equal to plus or minus one and independent—
P(X=1)=1-P(X=-1)=p
, (
0<p<1
); the walker has no memory. Motivated by applications in physics, although interesting in its own right, is the so called elephant random walk (ERW), for which every step depends on the whole process so far. The ERW was introduced in
[
13
]
in 2004, the name being inspired by the fact that elephants have a very long memory. Limit laws were formally proved in
[
2
,
4
]
using martingale theory. Actually, in the earlier papers
[
11
,
12
]
such results were shown for so-called correlated Bernoulli processes. In
[
8
]
we studied the case when the elephant has a restricted memory; assuming that future steps depend only on some distant past, only a recent past, or a mixture of both. Inspired by a suggestion in
[
3
]
we allowed, in
[
9
]
, the possibility of delays in that the elephant, in addition, always has a choice of staying put.

Formally, the elephant random walk is defined as a random walk in which the first step
X_{1}
equals 1 with probability
s\in[0,1]
and to
-1
with probability
1-s
, where, for convenience, we assume that
s=p
. After
n
steps, at position
S_{n}=\sum^{n}_{k=1}X_{k}
, the next step is defined as

\displaystyle X_{n+1}=\begin{cases}+X_{K},\quad\mbox{ with probability}\quad p\in[0,1],\\
-X_{K},\quad\mbox{ with probability}\quad 1-p,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
K
has a uniform distribution on the integers
1,2,\ldots,n
.

In that notation we studied, in
[
8
]
, the case where
K
in (
1.1
) is uniformly distributed over the set of points constituted by a restricted memory. In particular we considered the case where for some fixed
m
the random variable
K
is uniformly distributed over the memories
{\mathfrak{M}}=\{1,2,\dots,m\}
or
{\mathfrak{M}}=\{n-m+1,n-m+2,\dots,n\}

or a mixture of both. In these cases there was no separation in a diffusion part and a superdiffusion part depending on the value of
p
as in the ordinary ERW, for which the transitional
p
-value equals
3/4
. In
[
8
]
we posed the question what would happen if
m=m_{n}\nearrow\infty
. Some numerical simulations concerning this situation can be found in
[
5
,
14
,
15
]
. The present paper will give some theoretical answers to that question.

The organization of the paper is as follows: After presenting the setup in the following section, we treat the main case, for which
m_{n}\nearrow\infty
, such that
m_{n}/n\to 0
in Section 3. The next Section extends the results to delayed walks. In Section 5 we discuss the number of zeros occuring in this case, and in Section 6 we present moments when
m_{n}/n\to\alpha\in(0,1]
. The paper closes with some comments.

The setup

We begin by assuming that the elephant remembers the first
m=m_{n}
steps, i.e., that
{\cal F}_{n}=\sigma\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{m}\}
, where now the quantity
m
depends on time and
is increasing to infinity but not too fast, i.e., we assume that we have a triangular scheme of random variables
X_{m}=X_{n,m}
and
\sigma
-algebras

{\cal F}_{m_{n}}=\sigma\{X_{n,1},X_{n,2},\dots,X_{n,m_{n}}\},\quad\mbox{ where}\quad m_{n}\to\infty\mbox{ and }\frac{m_{n}}{n}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as}\quad n\to\infty\,.

(2.1)

For convenience we shall suppress the subindex
n
, viz, writing
m=m_{n}
and
S_{m}=S_{m_{n}}
.

We begin by computing moments. Note that the beginning of the walk is following an ERW with full memory, for which we can borrow from, e.g.,
[
2
]
or
[
4
]
. Based on these results we start with the calculation of moments. In contrast to the case of fixed
m
we are now facing three different regimes, namely
0<p<3/4
,
p=3/4
, and
3/4<p\leq 1
, respectively, as in the case with full memory. We also remark that the ERW reduces to a simple, symmetric random walk (a coin-tossing random walk) when
p=1/2
.

Before we start we need to keep the following in mind: When the elephant remembers the whole past the

\sigma
-algebra
{\cal G}_{n}=\sigma\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\}
is relevant for
X_{n+1}
and (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
) the behavior of the next step is governed by the relation
E(X_{n+1}\mid{\cal G}_{n})=(2p-1)\cdot\frac{S_{n}}{n}
, and hence
E(S_{n+1}\,|\,{\cal G}_{n})=\gamma_{n}S_{n}
where
\gamma_{n}=(n+2p-1)/n
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that
M_{n}=a_{n}S_{n}
with
a_{n}=\Pi_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}^{-1}
is a martingale which allows one to prove various limit theorems which, however, depend on the value of
p
with the critical value
p=3/4
.

Our next tool is an analog for ERW:s with a restricted memory. Toward that end, let
\{{\cal F}_{n},\,n\geq 1\}
denote the
\sigma
-algebras generated by the memory
{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}
, i.e.,
{\cal F}_{n}=\sigma\{X_{i}:i\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}\}
. Then,

\displaystyle E(X_{n+1}\mid{\cal F}_{n})=(p-q)\cdot\frac{\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}}X_{i}}{|{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}|}=(2p-1)\cdot\frac{\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}}X_{i}}{|{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}|},

(2.2)

noticing that only the first equality holds in the delayed case.

When we condition on steps that are not contained in the memory it means that the elephant does not remember them, and, hence, cannot choose among them in a following step. Thus, if
A\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
is an arbitrary set of indices, then

\displaystyle E(X_{n+1}\mid\sigma\{X_{i}\,,\,i\in A\cup{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}\})=E(X_{n+1}\mid{\cal F}_{n})=(p-q)\frac{\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}}X_{i}}{|{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}|}=(2p-1)\frac{\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}}X_{i}}{|{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}|},

(2.3)

where, again, the first equality only holds in the delayed case.

Finally, in order to avoid special effects we assume throughout that
0<p<1
; note that
p=1
corresponds to
X_{n}=X_{1}
for all
n
, and
p=0
to the case of alternating summands. Also
\delta_{a}
is the dirac-measure at the point
a
, and
c
and
C
denote numerical constants which may change from line to line.

ERW:s with an increasing memory;
{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,m_{n}\}

We begin with some facts on the asymptotics of mean and variance.

Proposition 3.1

Let
m,n\to\infty
, such that
m/n\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

(a)
If

, then

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{S_{n}\sqrt{m}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2p-1)E(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m}})+2(1-p)\frac{m}{n}E(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m}})\to 0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\Big(\frac{S_{n}\sqrt{m}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2p-1)^{2}\mathrm{Var\,}(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m}})+{\cal O}\Big(\frac{m}{n}\Big)=\frac{(2p-1)^{2}}{3-4p}+o(1).

(b)

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{S_{n}\sqrt{m/\log m}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m\log m}})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m}{n}E(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m\log m}})\to 0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\Big(\frac{S_{n}\sqrt{m/\log m}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Var\,}(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m\log m}})\left(1+{\cal O}\Big(\frac{m}{n}\Big)\right)=\frac{1}{4}+o(1).

(c)
If

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{S_{n}\,m^{2(1-p)}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2p-1)E(\frac{S_{m}}{m^{2p-1}})+2(1-p)\frac{m}{n}E(\frac{S_{m}}{m^{2p-1}})\to(2p-1)E(L),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\Big(\frac{S_{n}\,m^{2(1-p)}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle(2p-1)^{2}\mathrm{Var\,}(\frac{S_{m}}{m^{2p-1}})+{\cal O}\Big(\frac{m}{n}\Big)\sim(2p-1)^{2}\mathrm{Var\,}(L),

If

ERW:s with delays

\displaystyle X_{n+1}=\begin{cases}+X_{K},\quad\mbox{ with probability}\quad p\in[0,1],\\
-X_{K},\quad\mbox{ with probability}\quad q\in[0,1],\\
\phantom{+)}0,\quad\mbox{ \,\, with probability}\quad r\in[0,1],\end{cases}

Proposition 4.2

\displaystyle E((N_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid{\cal F}_{n})=E((N_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid{\cal F}_{m})=

5
Including also the last step;
{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,m_{n},n\}

If the elephant, in addition, remembers the most recent step it turns out that the results of the previous section remain true without change. This is reasonable, since the probability that the most recent step is, indeed, the one that is chosen is
1/m\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. However, since the sum of the probabilities is divergent, the second Borel–Cantelli lemma is not applicable, and some other argument must be exploited.

Toward that end, we create an ERW that selects precisely the steps that are based on the most recent step. We thus define

I_{k}=1\hskip-2.84526pt1\{X_{k}=\pm X_{k-1}\}\quad\mbox{ and}\quad M_{n}=\min\{\ell:\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}I_{k}=n\},

and set

Y_{k}=X_{k}\cdot\{I_{k}=1\}\quad\mbox{ and}\quad T_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{M_{n}}Y_{k}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{k},\quad n\geq 1.

The sequence
\{T_{n},\,n\geq 1\}
thus defined has the desired property, which takes us back to Section 7 of
[
8
]
, according to which
\frac{T_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}{\cal N}_{0,\frac{p}{1-p}}
as
n\to\infty
. Since the additional factors needed for the expressions in Theorem
3.1
all tend to zero as
n
increases (and
m/n\to 0
), we have established the following fact.

Theorem 5.1

The conclusions of Theorem
3.1
remain true for the case
{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,m_{n},n\}
.

Proposition 6.1

Let
m,n\to\infty
, such that
m/n\to\alpha\in(0,1]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3-4p}\cdot\big((2p-1)^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}+2(2p-1)\alpha(1-\alpha)+\alpha^{2}\big)+\alpha(1-\alpha)+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3-4p}\cdot\big((2p-1)(1-\alpha)+\alpha)\big)^{2}+\alpha(1-\alpha)+o(1).

If, in particular,
p=1/2
, then
\mathrm{Var\,}\big(\frac{S_{n}\sqrt{m}}{n}\big)\to\alpha
, or, equivalently,
\mathrm{Var\,}\Big(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\to 1
.

(b)
If
p=3/4
, then

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{S_{n}\sqrt{m/\log m}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m\log m}}\Big)\cdot\big(1+\alpha\big)/2+o(1)\to 0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\Big(\frac{S_{n}\sqrt{m/\log m}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\Big(\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m\log m}}\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{4}(1-\alpha)^{2}+\alpha\Big)+o(1)=\frac{1}{4}(1-\alpha)^{2}+\alpha+o(1).

(c)
If
3/4<p<1
, then

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{S_{n}m^{2(1-p)}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\frac{S_{m}}{m^{2(1-p)}})\cdot\big((2p-1)+2\alpha(1-p)+o(1)\big)\to 0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\Big(\frac{S_{n}m^{2(1-p)}}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\big(L\big)\cdot\big((2p-1)^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}+2(2p-1)\alpha(1-\alpha)+\alpha^{2}\big)+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var\,}\big(L\big)\cdot\big((2p-1)(1-\alpha)+\alpha\big)^{2}+o(1).

We note, in particular, that if

7
Further remarks

•

In
[
8
]
we noted that for fixed, finite, memories
{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}=\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, there was no phase transition, in contrast to the case of full memory (recall
[
2
]
). However there was no simple asymptotic normality. Our present results show, with appropriate variances, that once the memory of the past is gradually increasing (to infinity) asymptotic normality and phase transition occur.

•

In
[
8
]
we have shown that
S_{n}/\sqrt{n}
is asymptotically normal when
{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}=\{n-m,n-m+1,\dots,n\}
with fixed
m
. The asymptotic variance for arbirtrary
m
was calculated in
[
1
]
to be
\sigma^{2}=\frac{m-1+2p}{2(1-p)(2(1-p)m+2p-1)}
. We conjecture that asymptotic normality still holds if
{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}=\{n-m_{n},n-m_{n}+1,\dots,n\}
with increasing
m_{n}
or also if

{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,m_{n},n-k_{n},n-k_{n}+1,\ldots,n\}
whenever
k_{n}/m_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We also conjecture that if
m_{n}
does not increase too rapidly there are no phase transitions and that the asymptotic variance is given by
\sigma^{2}=1/(4(1-p)^{2})
.

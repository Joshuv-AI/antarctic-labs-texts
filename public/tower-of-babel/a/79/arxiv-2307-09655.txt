Heavy quark diffusion coefficients in magnetized quark-gluon plasma
Aritra Bandyopadhyay

Abstract
We evaluate the heavy quark momentum diffusion coefficients in a hot magnetized medium for the most general scenario of any arbitrary values of the external magnetic field. We choose to work with the systematic way of incorporating the effect of the magnetic field, by using the effective gluon and quark propagators, generalized for a hot and magnetized medium. To get gauge independent analytic form factors valid through all Landau levels, we apply the Hard Thermal Loop (HTL) technique for the resummed effective gluon propagator. The derived effective HTL gluon propagator and the generalized version of Schwinger quark propagator subsequently allow us to analytically evaluate the longitudinal and transverse momentum diffusion coefficients for charm and bottom quarks beyond the static limit. Within the static limit we also explore another way of incorporating the effect of the magnetic field, i.e. through the magnetized medium modified Debye mass and compare the results to justify the need for structural changes.

II
Formalism

The present work evaluates the HQ diffusion coefficients in the general most scenario of hot and magnetized medium, also assuming the HQ to be relativistic (i.e. going beyond the static limit). Before going into the details of the evaluation procedure, in this section we will give an overview of the same and clarify the notations to be used throughout the paper.

When a HQ traverses through the medium, it encounters collisions with other partons. Because the HQ has a much higher energy scale than the temperature of the medium, i.e.
P\equiv(M,p)\equiv(E,v)\gg T
, it usually takes a large number of collisions (around
M/T
within and
p/T
beyond the static limit of HQ) to change the HQ momentum by a substantial amount. Hence one can approximate the interaction of the HQ with the medium in a simplified way, such that it becomes a series of uncorrelated momentum kicks. At
T\neq 0
, these uncorrelated momentum kicks can be comprehended as originating from the scatterings faced by the HQ with the thermally populated light quarks and gluons. As a result of that, the transport coefficients, i.e. in our case the momentum diffusion coefficients (
\kappa
) in our case, are directly related to the corresponding scattering/interaction rate (
\Gamma
), as is shown below through explicit expressions.

In the presence of an external magnetic field and considering that the HQ is moving in a particular direction, i.e. going beyond the static limit of HQ, the complex interplay between anisotropies generated from the preferred directions of HQ momentum/velocity and the magnetic field leads to nontrivial scenarios. In this situation it is useful to work with two simple cases, i.e.
\vec{v}\shortparallel\vec{B}
and
\vec{v}\perp\vec{B}
. The first case
\vec{v}\shortparallel\vec{B}
leads to two different diffusion coefficients
\kappa_{L}
and
\kappa_{T}
, related to the HQ scattering rate as

\displaystyle\kappa_{T}(p)=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}q\frac{d~\Gamma(v)}{d^{3}q}q_{\perp}^{2},~~\kappa_{L}(p)=\int d^{3}q\frac{d~\Gamma(v)}{d^{3}q}q_{z}^{2}.

(1)

On the other hand,
\vec{v}\perp\vec{B}
case generates three different diffusion coefficients
\kappa_{j}
’s (
j=\{x,y,z\}\equiv\{1,2,3\}
), i.e.

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}(p)=\int d^{3}q\frac{d~\Gamma(v)}{d^{3}q}q_{j}^{2}.

(2)

It is straightforward to realize that retracing the static limit (
\vec{v}\rightarrow 0
) within a magnetized medium would mean there is only one anisotropy given by the specific direction of
\vec{B}
and would cause the
\vec{v}\perp\vec{B}
case to vanish.

From Eqs.
1
and
2

2\leftrightarrow 2
scatterings between the light quark/gluon and the HQ (i.e.
qH\leftrightarrow qH
and
gH\leftrightarrow gH
). The dominant
t
-channel scattering processes involving them are diagrammatically portrayed in the top half of Fig.
1
. Now, to evaluate the corresponding scattering rates, we use an effective approach, first provided by Weldon

. In this approach we can express the t-channel
2\leftrightarrow 2

1
. Using this technique the expression for the scattering rate (
\Gamma(P)
) comes out to be

\displaystyle\Gamma(P)=-\frac{1}{2E}~\frac{1}{1+e^{-E/T}}~\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M)~{\rm Im}~\Sigma(p_{0}+i\epsilon,{\vec{p}})\right],

(3)

where
\Sigma(P)
’s represent the two-loop HQ self energy diagrams involving second-order quark and gluon loops. The hard contribution of the scattering rate comes from cutting these
\Sigma(P)
’s, depicted by the right hand side of the diagrammatic equation shown in the bottom half of Fig.
1

Q

In this scenario, the hard thermal loop (HTL) corrections to the gluon propagator contribute at the leading order in the strong coupling constant, which in turn suggests that resummation must be taken into account. At this point we will emphasize the advantage of using Eq.
3

\Sigma(P)

\Sigma(P)

1

\displaystyle\Sigma(P)=ig^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}Q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathcal{D}^{\mu\nu}(Q)\gamma_{\mu}S_{m}(P-Q)\gamma_{\nu}.

(4)

Here
\mathcal{D}^{\mu\nu}(Q)
is the effective gluon propagator and
S_{m}(P-Q)
is the heavy fermion propagator in presence of an external magnetic field.

III
Evaluation of the form factors from the one-loop HTL gluon self-energy

In the previous section, we covered the basic formalism and summarized the calculational steps to be followed. As the first step of the same, in this section we will first compute the One loop gluon self energy (
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
) in a hot and arbitrarily magnetized medium, which will then help us to evaluate various form factors (
d_{i}
’s) required to construct the HTL effective gluon propagator (
\mathcal{D}^{\mu\nu}
).

One loop gluon self energy in a hot and magnetized medium can be written as a combination of the pure glue Yang-Mills contribution
\Pi^{g}_{\mu\nu}
and the fermionic loop contribution
\Pi^{m}_{\mu\nu}
, i.e.
\Pi_{\mu\nu}=\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}+\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}
. The pure glue part
\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}
is unaffected in presence of magnetic field and can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}(Q)=-\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3}\int\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}\left(\frac{q_{0}\hat{K}_{\mu}\hat{K}_{\nu}}{\hat{K}\cdot Q}-g_{\mu 0}g_{\nu 0}\right).

(13)

As later we will be using it, at this point we also define the angular factor as
\mathcal{T}_{Q}
, given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{Q}=\int\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}\frac{q_{0}}{\hat{K}\cdot Q}=\frac{q_{0}}{2q}\ln\left(\frac{q_{0}+q}{q_{0}-q}\right).

(14)

On the other hand the fermionic part of the one-loop gluon self-energy can be computed as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}(Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{f}\frac{ig^{2}}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}K}{(2\pi)^{4}}\textsf{Tr}\left\{\gamma_{\mu}S_{m}(K)\gamma_{\nu}S_{m}(R)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f}\frac{ig^{2}}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}K}{(2\pi)^{4}}~e^{-\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}+r_{\perp}^{2}}{q_{f}B}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle~~\left(\frac{1}{K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2lq_{f}B}\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l^{\prime}q_{f}B}\right)

\displaystyle\times\textsf{Tr}\Bigl\{\gamma_{\mu}~D_{l}(q_{f}B,K)~\gamma_{\nu}~D_{l^{\prime}}(q_{f}B,R)\Bigr\},

(15)

where we have used the form of the fermionic propagator from Eq. (
5
), replacing the HQ mass
M
by flavor dependent light quark mass
m_{f}
. We can further simplify the expression by the virtue of the Hard thermal loop approximation, neglecting the external momenta and the quark mass
m_{f}
in the numerator, i.e.

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}(Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{f}\frac{ig^{2}}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}K}{(2\pi)^{4}}~e^{-\frac{2k_{\perp}^{2}}{q_{f}B}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2lq_{f}B}\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l^{\prime}q_{f}B}\right)

\displaystyle\times\textsf{Tr}\Bigl\{\gamma_{\mu}~D_{l}(q_{f}B,K)~\gamma_{\nu}~D_{l^{\prime}}(q_{f}B,R)\Bigr\}.

(16)

The nontrivial trace part can be computed by progressing term by term (see Eq. (
6
)),

\displaystyle\textsf{Tr}\Bigl\{\gamma_{\mu}~D_{l}(K)~\gamma_{\nu}~D_{l^{\prime}}(R)\Bigr\}=T_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{i=1}^{9}(T_{i})_{\mu\nu}.

(17)

We list the explicit expressions for each of the terms in Appendix
A
. Evaluation of the trace finally yields the fermionic part of the one-loop gluon self energy to be

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}(Q)=\sum_{f}\frac{ig^{2}}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}K}{(2\pi)^{4}}~e^{-\frac{2k_{\perp}^{2}}{q_{f}B}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{9}(T_{i})_{\mu\nu}}{\left(K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2lq_{f}B\right)\left(R_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l^{\prime}q_{f}B\right)}.

(18)

Eq. (
18
) along with Eq. (
13
) give the final expression for the one-loop gluon self energy in a hot and arbitrarily magnetized medium.

d_{1}^{YM}
is the contribution for the gluon part, i.e.

\displaystyle d_{1}^{YM}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{1}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}=\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3\bar{u}^{2}}\left[1-\mathcal{T}_{Q}(q_{0},q)\right].

(20)

On the other hand
d_{1}^{m}
is the contribution from the quark loop (See Appendix
B
), given as

\displaystyle d_{1}^{m}=\Delta_{1}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}=-\sum_{f}~\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{2\pi\bar{u}^{2}}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left[(\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}+\delta_{l-1,l^{\prime}-1})\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\left\{(2-\bar{u}_{\shortparallel}^{2})\Phi_{1}+(2k_{3}^{2}+\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.(2-\bar{u}_{\shortparallel}^{2})2l|q_{f}B|)\Phi_{2}\right\}+\bar{u}_{\perp}^{2}(\delta_{l,l^{\prime}-1}+\delta_{l-1,l^{\prime}})\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\left.\left\{\Phi_{1}+2l|q_{f}B|\Phi_{2}\right\}+4\bar{u}^{2}|q_{f}B|l\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\Phi_{2}\right],

(21)

where
\bar{u}_{\shortparallel}^{2}=1-\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\left(2-\frac{Q_{\shortparallel}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\right)
,
\bar{u}_{\perp}^{2}=\frac{q_{0}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}{Q^{4}}
and
\bar{u}^{2}=-q^{2}/Q^{2}
.
In expressing
d_{1}^{m}
we have also used the shorthand notations
\Phi_{i}
’s for the following frequency sums

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{1}{K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l|q_{f}B|}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{1}{k_{0}^{2}-E_{k_{3}}^{2}}=\frac{n_{F}(E_{k_{3}})}{E_{k_{3}}}.

(22)

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{1}{(K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l|q_{f}B|)(R_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l^{\prime}|q_{f}B|)}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{1}{(k_{0}^{2}-E_{k_{3}}^{2})(r_{0}^{2}-E_{r_{3}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm 1}\frac{s_{1}s_{2}}{4E_{k_{3}}E_{r_{3}}}\left(\frac{1-n_{F}(s_{1}E_{k_{3}})-n_{F}(s_{2}E_{r_{3}})}{q_{0}-s_{1}E_{k_{3}}-s_{2}E_{r_{3}}}\right),

(23)

where
E_{k_{3}}=\sqrt{k_{3}^{2}+m_{f}^{2}+2l|q_{f}B|}
and
E_{r_{3}}=\sqrt{r_{3}^{2}+m_{f}^{2}+2l^{\prime}|q_{f}B|}
.

At this point, we should make some comment about the LLL approximation. One can see in a straightforward way that from Eq. (
21
), it is easy to obtain the LLL limit by putting
l=l^{\prime}=0
, i.e.

\displaystyle d_{1}^{LLL}=d_{1}^{YM}-\sum_{f}~\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{2\pi\bar{u}^{2}}\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\left[\Phi_{1}+2k_{3}^{2}\Phi_{2}\right].

(24)

This is similar to what has been obtained in Ref.
Fukushima:2015wck
, where the authors further exploited HTL approximations within
\Phi_{2}
to simplify the expression for
d_{1}
and express it in terms of the medium modified Debye mass
m_{D}
.

III.2
Form factor
d_{2}

Focusing on
d_{2}
, we have (See Eqs. (

) and (

))

\displaystyle d_{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{2}^{\mu\nu}(\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}+\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m})=d_{2}^{YM}+d_{2}^{m}.

The pure-glue part
d_{2}^{YM}
yields

\displaystyle d_{2}^{YM}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{2}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}=\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{q^{2}}-\frac{Q^{2}}{q^{2}}\mathcal{T}_{Q}(q_{0},q)\right].

(26)

For the quark part, we obtain the final expression as

\displaystyle d_{2}^{m}=\Delta_{2}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}=\sum_{f}~\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{2\pi}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left[(\delta_{l,l^{\prime}-1}+\delta_{l-1,l^{\prime}})\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\left\{\Phi_{1}+2l|q_{f}B|\Phi_{2}\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+4|q_{f}B|l\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\Phi_{2}\right].

(27)

III.3
Form factor
d_{3}

Similar to
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
, we can break down
d_{3}
into two parts (See Eqs. (

) and (

\displaystyle d_{3}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{3}^{\mu\nu}(\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}+\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m})=d_{3}^{YM}+d_{3}^{m}

(28)

where the pure glue part yields

\displaystyle d_{3}^{YM}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{3}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}=\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{q^{2}}-\frac{Q^{2}}{q^{2}}\mathcal{T}_{Q}(q_{0},q)\right].

(29)

For the quark part we can subsequently write down

\displaystyle d_{3}^{m}=\Delta_{3}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}=-\sum_{f}~\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{2\pi\bar{n}^{2}}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left[-\bar{n}_{\shortparallel}^{2}(\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}+\delta_{l-1,l^{\prime}-1})\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\left\{\Phi_{1}+(2k_{3}^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2l|q_{f}B|)\Phi_{2}\right\}+\bar{n}_{\perp}^{2}(\delta_{l,l^{\prime}-1}+\delta_{l-1,l^{\prime}})\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\left.\left\{\Phi_{1}+2l|q_{f}B|\Phi_{2}\right\}+4\bar{n}^{2}|q_{f}B|l\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\Phi_{2}\right],

where
\bar{n}_{\shortparallel}^{2}=-\frac{q_{\perp}^{4}}{q^{4}}
,
\bar{n}_{\perp}^{2}=\frac{q_{3}^{2}q_{\perp}^{2}}{Q^{4}}
and
\bar{n}^{2}=-\frac{q_{\perp}^{2}}{q^{2}}
.

III.4
Form Factor
d_{4}

Finally for the last form factor
d_{4}
(See Eqs. (

) and (

\displaystyle d_{4}^{YM}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{4}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{g}=0,

. Subsequently

comes out to be

\displaystyle d_{4}^{m}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{4}^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{m}=-\sum_{f}~\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{2\pi\sqrt{\bar{u}^{2}}\sqrt{\bar{n}^{2}}}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left[(\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}+\delta_{l-1,l^{\prime}-1})\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}\left\{2k_{3}\bar{n}^{2}\Phi_{3}-(\bar{n}\cdot\bar{u})_{\shortparallel}\times\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.(\Phi_{1}+2l|q_{f}B|\Phi_{2})\right\}+(\bar{n}\cdot\bar{u})_{\perp}(\delta_{l,l^{\prime}-1}+\delta_{l-1,l^{\prime}})\int\frac{dk_{3}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\left.\left\{\Phi_{1}+2l|q_{f}B|\Phi_{2}\right\}\right],

(32)

where
(\bar{n}\cdot\bar{u})_{\shortparallel}=(\bar{n}\cdot\bar{u})_{\perp}=\frac{q_{0}q_{3}q_{\perp}^{2}}{q^{2}Q^{2}}
.

Here we have introduced a third frequency sum
\Phi_{3}
as

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{k_{0}}{(K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l|q_{f}B|)(R_{\shortparallel}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}-2l^{\prime}|q_{f}B|)}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{k_{0}}\frac{k_{0}}{(k_{0}^{2}-E_{k_{3}}^{2})(r_{0}^{2}-E_{r_{3}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm 1}\frac{s_{2}}{4E_{r_{3}}}\left(\frac{1-n_{F}(s_{1}E_{k_{3}})-n_{F}(s_{2}E_{r_{3}})}{q_{0}-s_{1}E_{k_{3}}-s_{2}E_{r_{3}}}\right).

(33)

Now that we have all the expressions for the individual form factors

’s, the construction of the effective HTL gluon propagator
\mathcal{D}^{\mu\nu}(Q)
involving quarks residing on any arbitrary Landau levels seems complete (See Eq. (

\Gamma(P)
in a hot magnetized medium, utilizing the HQ effective self energy
\Sigma(P)

IV
HQ scattering rate (
\Gamma
) in a magnetized medium

We start this section with the expression for the HQ effective self energy. Using Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
8
) in Eq. (
4
), we can write down the HQ effective self energy as

\displaystyle\Sigma(P)

\displaystyle=ig^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\int\frac{d^{4}Q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{e^{-{k_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}}{K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-M^{2}-2l|q_{f}B|}

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{J}_{i}\Delta_{i}^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\mu}D_{l}(q_{f}B,K)\gamma_{\nu},

(34)

which also makes it quite evident that we have chosen a gauge with vanishing gauge parameter, i.e.
\xi=0
in Eq. (
8
).

Now to evaluate the scattering rate using Eq. (
3
), one needs to evaluate the imaginary part of
\Sigma(P)
and perform a trace, i.e.
\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M){\rm Im}~\Sigma(P)\right]
. These two actions can commute with each other. Hence we will first perform the trace and then evaluate the imaginary part to obtain the final expression for the HQ scattering rate in a magnetized medium. From Eq. (
34
), one can write the trace as :

\displaystyle\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M)~\Sigma(P)\right]=ig^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}

\displaystyle~~\int\frac{d^{4}Q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{e^{-{k_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}}{K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-M^{2}-2l|q_{f}B|}

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{J}_{i}~\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M)\Delta_{i}^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\mu}D_{l}(q_{f}B,K)\gamma_{\nu}\right].

(35)

We can now proceed to evaluate the individual traces
\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M)\Delta_{i}^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\mu}D_{l}(q_{f}B,K)\gamma_{\nu}\right]
. To this end, we will break down each of the traces into two parts, i.e.
q_{0}
independent
A_{i}
’s and
q_{0}
dependent
B_{i}
’s, i.e.

\displaystyle\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M)\Delta_{i}^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\mu}D_{l}(q_{f}B,K)\gamma_{\nu}\right]=A_{i}+B_{i}(q_{0}).

(36)

The reason for this arrangement can be understood as follows :

1.

We are eventually interested in the imaginary part of
\Sigma(P)
. It has been explicitly shown in Ref.
Braaten:1991jj
for
eB=0
and in Ref.
Bandyopadhyay:2021zlm
for
eB\neq 0
that
q_{0}
dependent terms will not contribute in the imaginary part of
\Sigma(P)
.

2.

We will work on the small energy transfer limit of HQ scattering, which essentially requires
q_{0}\rightarrow 0
. Within this limit, various form factors
d_{i}
s undergoes further simplifications which we eventually use in our numerical evaluation. These simplifications have been explicitly discussed in Appendix
C
.

Hence we can safely neglect the
q_{0}
dependent pieces
B_{i}
’s to proceed further into the calculation. Explicit evaluations of the traces along with the expressions for
A_{i}
’s are given in Appendix
D
.

To compute the sum over
q_{0}
, we introduce the spectral representations for the HQ and effective gluon propagators respectively by using the following relations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-M^{2}-2l|q_{f}B|}=-\frac{1}{2E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}\times

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1/T}d\tau^{\prime}e^{k_{0}\tau^{\prime}}\left[(1-n_{F}(E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}))e^{-E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}\tau^{\prime}}-n_{F}(E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})e^{E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}\tau^{\prime}}\right],

(37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{i}=-\int\limits_{0}^{1/T}d\tau~e^{q_{0}\tau}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}~d\omega~\rho_{i}(\omega,q)~\left[1+n_{B}(\omega)\right]~e^{-\omega\tau}.

(38)

For the spectral representation of the HQ propagator, we have used the dispersion relation
E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}=\sqrt{k_{3}^{2}+M^{2}+2l|q_{f}B|}
. On the other hand for the spectral representation of the effective gluon propagator we have defined the spectral functions as

\displaystyle\rho_{i}(\omega,q)=\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}\left(\mathcal{J}_{i}\Big|_{q_{0}=\omega+i\epsilon}\right),

(39)

which extract the imaginary parts of the corresponding coefficients
\mathcal{J}_{i}
. Explicit expressions of these spectral functions are given in Appendix
E
.

Once we use Eqs. (
36
), (
37
) and (
38
) in Eq. (
35
), the sum over
q_{0}
can be evaluated from the combination of the integrals over
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
, using
1
1

1

Similar formulae have been used in our earlier work
Bandyopadhyay:2021zlm
, which contain typos of missing a factor of
T
.

\displaystyle T\sum_{q_{0}}e^{q_{0}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}=

\displaystyle\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime}).

(40)

This subsequently yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M)~\Sigma(P)\right]=~ig^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}Q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{e^{-{k_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}}{K_{\shortparallel}^{2}-M^{2}-2n|q_{f}B|}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{J}_{i}~A_{i}

\displaystyle=-g^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-{k_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\left[1+n_{B}(\omega)\right]\frac{\rho_{i}(\omega,q)}{2E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}A_{i}~P_{1},

(41)

where

\displaystyle P_{1}=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1/T}d\tau^{\prime}\int\limits_{0}^{1/T}d\tau~e^{p_{0}\tau^{\prime}-\omega\tau}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle~\times\left[(1-n_{F}(E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}))e^{-E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}\tau^{\prime}}-n_{F}(E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})e^{E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}\tau^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\frac{\sigma~n_{F}(\sigma E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})}{p_{0}+\sigma E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-\omega}\left(e^{(p_{0}+\sigma E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-\omega)/T}-1\right).

(42)

After this we can proceed to compute the discontinuity by writing down the evaluation for the trace, now incorporating the imaginary part, as

\displaystyle\operatorname{\text{Tr}}\left[(\not{P}+M)~{\rm Im}\Sigma(p_{0}+i\epsilon,\vec{p})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\pi g^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\left(e^{-E/T}+1\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-{k_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\left[1+n_{B}(\omega)\right]\frac{\rho_{i}(\omega,q)A_{i}}{2E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\sigma~n_{F}(\sigma E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})~\delta(E+\sigma E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-\omega).

(43)

Finally using Eq. (
43
) in Eq. (
3
), we can obtain the scattering rate
\Gamma(P)
(for a particular HQ flavor) as

\displaystyle\Gamma(P)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi g^{2}}{2E}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-{k_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\left[1+n_{B}(\omega)\right]\frac{\rho_{i}(\omega,q)A_{i}}{2E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\sigma~n_{F}(\sigma E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})~\delta(E+\sigma E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-\omega).

(44)

We can further simplify the expression for the scattering rate a bit further using the scale hierarchy
M\gg\sqrt{eB},T
. As
E\sim E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}\sim M
, so the delta function
\delta(E+E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-\omega)
cannot contribute for
\omega\leq T
. Also, the Fermi-Dirac distribution
n_{F}(E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})
will be exponentially suppressed. These changes subsequently simplify the expression of the scattering rate as

\displaystyle\Gamma(P)=\frac{\pi g^{2}}{2E}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-{k_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\left[1+n_{B}(\omega)\right]\frac{\rho_{i}(\omega,q)A_{i}}{2E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}\delta(E-E^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-\omega).

(45)

Eq. (
45
) is the final expression for the HQ scattering rate in a hot and arbitrarily magnetized medium which will be used in the next section to write down the HQ momentum diffusion coefficients.

V

In this section we write down the final expressions for the HQ momentum diffusion coefficients
\kappa_{i}

V.1

\bf{v}\shortparallel\bf{B}

As the magnetic field is considered to be along the

p_{3}

, resulting

A_{i}^{(1)}
’s are given in Appendix

(1+n_{B}(\omega))

\frac{T}{\omega}

\bf{v}\perp\bf{B}

and/or
p_{2}

) will again have modifications in

\displaystyle\kappa_{L}^{(s)}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}~2\pi g^{2}TM}{\sqrt{M^{2}+2l|q_{f}B|}}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}q_{3}^{2}e^{-{q_{\perp}^{2}}/{|q_{f}B|}}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{(m_{D}^{g})^{2}(L_{l}(\xi_{q}^{\perp})-L_{l-1}(\xi_{q}^{\perp}))}{2q(q^{2}+(m_{D}^{\prime})^{2})^{2}}\right],

\displaystyle(m_{D}^{g})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3},

(54)

\displaystyle\delta m_{D,f}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2-\delta_{l,0})\int dk_{3}~\frac{\partial n_{F}(E_{k_{3}})}{\partial E_{k_{3}}},

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{4\pi^{2}T}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2-\delta_{l,0})\int dk_{3}~n_{F}(1-n_{F}).

1.

First and foremost, the structural anisotropy in presence of the magnetic field is not captured in Eqs. (
59

60

2.

From Eq. (

3.

Considering the modifications of the HQ propagator in a magnetized medium provide us with the factor

, comparing their results with QCD kinetic calculation. Similar estimations of

can be tried in magnetized medium through a fitting procedure, comparing the results between
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
.

4.

There is no explicit HQ mass dependence in the expressions of the Debye mass approximated momentum diffusion coefficients, as opposed to the exact expressions for arbitrary Landau levels.

In Fig.

\kappa
and the Debye mass approximated procedure
\kappa^{\prime}
. For evaluating UV finite values of
\kappa^{\prime}

;

VIII
Summary

In the present study we have studied HQ momentum diffusion coefficients in magnetized medium. We have considered the most general cases in both the fronts : (a) arbitrary values of the external magnetic field and (b) going beyond the static limit of the HQ to consider a finite HQ velocity. As far as we know, this is the first calculation which attempts to study the HQ dynamics with arbitrary values of the external magnetic field, i.e. incorporates the contributions from higher Landau levels. Hence the novelty of this work mainly lies in the calculation part, summarized in section
II
and subsequently performed in sections
III
,
IV
along with the various appendices.

We have also discussed an alternate procedure, usually employed in evaluating other HQ observable in a magnetized medium, which assumes the whole medium effect within the Debye screening mass and accordingly replace it with the magnetized medium modified one. By comparing the results from this approximated procedure with our exact results within the static limit of the HQ, we clearly identify the shortcomings of this alternate procedure and emphasise the importance of employing the general structure of the gluon two-point correlation functions in a hot and arbitrarily magnetized medium. Both within and beyond (when we consider the HQ to be moving along the direction of the external magnetic field) the static limit we notice similar pattern in the
eB
dependence of the momentum diffusion coefficients, i.e. the
eB
dependence is rapidly increasing for lower values of
eB
, whereas it becomes saturated (more so for charm quarks) for relatively higher values of
eB
. An opposite trend is observed for the case when the HQ is moving in a perpendicular direction with respect to the external magnetic field, where increasing
eB
dictates more changes in the momentum diffusion coefficients. Soft gluon scattering (which are dominant within the
t
-channel at leading order of strong coupling) governed longitudinal diffusion coefficient dominates over the transverse diffusion coefficient within the static limit of HQ and an opposite trend is observed going beyond the static limit. Competing behaviour of various scales involved, i.e.
M
,
p
,
eB
and
T
, gets well reflected in our results.

At this point we must also mention that the present work is also not completely free from limitations, most of which are being carried over from the limitations of HTL approximation, e.g. assuming the quarks in various Landau levels to be mass less renders into vanishing quark contribution to the longitudinal momentum diffusion coefficient. At the cost of providing completely analytic, gauge independent, simplified expressions there are further scale restrictions appearing because of the HTL approximation, as discussed in section
I
. But apart from the HTL generated limitations, there is another important issue of the UV cutoff
q_{max}
. For
eB=0
, several studies have shown that the UV cutoff is not necessary when one includes the hard contributions along with the soft contribution in the scattering rate. In most of the cases with finite
eB
, the exponential factor
e^{-q_{\perp}^{2}/|q_{f}B|}
acts as a natural UV cutoff. But for the case of HQ moving with sufficiently high transverse momentum the exponential factor is not enough and we need further assurances in the form of a medium dependent
q_{max}(T,eB)
. In the present work, this
q_{max}(T,eB)
has been chosen by choosing a similar form as the
eB=0
case evaluated in Ref
Beraudo:2009pe
by comparing with the full result. But this choice of
q_{max}
is not completely accurate and requires further modifications. One of the way of modifying this
q_{max}
is also discussed in the present work, which requires exact comparison between two alternate procedures within the HQ static limit and extracting the
q_{max}
by appropriate fitting procedure. But the ultimate way of eradicating this
q_{max}
dependence would be proper inclusion of the hard contributions
Braaten:1991jj
;
Braaten:1991we
, which leaves the door open for potential future investigations. The present study creates other avenues to proceed too, e.g. examining the HQ in-medium evolution using a Langevin transport code (e.g.
Li:2019lex
) and their consequences on the experimental observable such as directed and elliptic flow of the open heavy flavor mesons.

\displaystyle+\Bigl(-\Im_{d_{1}}\Im_{d_{3}}+(Q^{2}-\Re_{d_{1}})(Q^{2}-\Re_{d_{3}})\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr].

(125)

Here
\Im_{d_{i}}
and

d_{i}
’s, which we discuss below. The imaginary parts will contribute primarily from
\mathcal{T}_{Q}
’s and the frequency sum

(126a)

\displaystyle\Im(\Phi_{2})\Big|_{q_{0}\rightarrow\omega+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm 1}\frac{1-n_{F}(s_{1}E_{k_{3}})-n_{F}(s_{2}E_{r_{3}})}{4s_{1}s_{2}E_{k_{3}}E_{r_{3}}}

\displaystyle~~\times\pi\delta(\omega-s_{1}E_{k_{3}}-s_{2}E_{r_{3}}),

(126b)

\displaystyle\Im(\Phi_{3})\Big|_{q_{0}\rightarrow\omega+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm 1}\frac{1-n_{F}(s_{1}E_{k_{3}})-n_{F}(s_{2}E_{r_{3}})}{4s_{2}E_{r_{3}}}

\displaystyle~~\times\pi\delta(\omega-s_{1}E_{k_{3}}-s_{2}E_{r_{3}}),

where we have used the identity :

\displaystyle\Im\left(\frac{1}{x+i\epsilon}\right)=-\pi\delta(x).

Let us now specifically discuss the static limit expression for
\left[\frac{1}{\omega}\rho_{1}^{(s)}(\omega,q)\right]_{\omega\rightarrow 0}
. First we write down the expression for
\Im_{d_{1}}
.

\displaystyle\Im_{d_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3}\frac{\pi\omega}{2q}-\sum_{f}~\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2-\delta_{l,0})

\displaystyle\int dk_{3}~E_{k_{3}}^{2}~\Im(\Phi_{2})\Big|_{l^{\prime}=l},

(128)

with

\displaystyle\Im(\Phi_{2})\Big|_{l^{\prime}=l}=\frac{\pi\omega}{4E_{k_{3}}^{2}}\frac{\partial n_{F}(E_{k_{3}})}{\partial E_{k_{3}}}\delta(\omega-q_{3}),

(129)

\omega>0
case only. Subsequently looking at the expression of

in Eq.(
121

\frac{1}{\omega}

\Im_{d_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega}\Im_{d_{1}}\Big|_{\omega\to 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3}\frac{\pi}{2q}-\sum_{f}~\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2-\delta_{l,0})

\displaystyle\times\frac{\pi}{2}\delta(q_{3})\int dk_{3}~\frac{\partial n_{F}(E_{k_{3}})}{\partial E_{k_{3}}}.

Recalling the expressions for the pure glue part
m_{D}^{g}

\delta m_{D}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega}\Im_{d_{1}}\Big|_{\omega\to 0}

\displaystyle=(m_{D}^{g})^{2}\frac{\pi}{2q}+\frac{\pi}{2}\delta(q_{3})\sum_{f}~\delta m_{D,f}^{2},

\displaystyle(m_{D}^{g})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{c}g^{2}T^{2}}{3},

\displaystyle\delta m_{D,f}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}|q_{f}B|}{4\pi^{2}T}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2-\delta_{l,0})\int dk_{3}~n_{F}(1-n_{F}).

Next we proceed to evaluate

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{\omega}\rho_{1}^{(s)}(\omega,q)\right]_{\omega\rightarrow 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\frac{1}{\omega}\Im_{d_{1}}\Big|_{\omega\to 0}}{\left(q^{2}+\Re_{d_{1}}\Big|_{\omega\to 0}\right)^{2}}.

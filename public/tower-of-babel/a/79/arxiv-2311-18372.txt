General features and contact modeling of N N -body isolated resonances near threshold
Ludovic Pricoupenko

Abstract
N N -body non efimovian bound or quasi-bound states for particles with short range interactions are considered in arbitrary dimensions. The different resonance regimes near the threshold are depicted by using a generalization of the effective range approximation. This two-parameter description can be used in various contexts from ultra-cold to hadronic physics. The universal character of these states makes it possible a formulation in terms of a contact model. The singularity at the contact imposes the introduction of a modified scalar product to solve the normalization catastrophe and to restore the self-adjoint character of the model. An equivalence with the standard scalar product used for realistic finite range models is derived.

Introduction

I.1
General context

Near threshold
N
-body bound and quasi-bound states, denoted in short
N
-body resonances play an important role in scattering properties. They are observed and/or predicted for systems with short range interparticle potentials in hadronic and nuclear physics, condensed matter and in ultracold atomic physics
Fed94
;
Jen04
;
Gre17
;
Ham17
;
Nis08
;
Saf13
;
Nai22
;
Hon22
;
Nai23
. In the context of the many-body problem, they induce correlations at large scales in many-body systems
Mak14
;
Lev15
.
Although the huge different scales of energies and the peculiar short distance physics specific to these various systems, they share universal properties. Interestingly, ultracold atoms experiments gives the marvelous opportunity to explore in depth these highly correlated states with the possible tuning of the interactions via magnetic and optical Feshbach resonances
Chi10
. For instance depending on the statistics, the number and the mass of the particles, Efimov states, can be observed or predicted near the unitary limit of the s wave two-body interaction
Efi70
;
Efi71
;
Kra06
;
Cas10
;
Baz17
;
Nai17
.

At the heart of this universality, is the scale invariance in a wide interval of lengths, a property that implies the separability in the hyperangle
{(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}
and hyperradius
{(\rho)}
coordinates for several orders of magnitude. In the separability region, the hyperradial problem can be mapped to a radial two dimensional (2D) Schrödinger equation with an inverse square potential proportional to
{s^{2}/\rho^{2}}
. The strength of this generalized kinetic barrier is obtained from the hyperangular eigenvalue problem by using the behavior of the wave function near the contact of two interacting particles. The Efimov effect, characterized by a discrete scale invariance of the spectrum at vanishing energy emerges when the hyperradial potential is attractive, i.e. when the index
s
is imaginary. The beauty of this effects is that the long range attractive inverse square potential emerges from the short distance behavior of the wave function itself. Besides the Efimov effect which has attracted a lot of interest since its first observation in 2006
Kra06
, isolated
N
-body resonances occur when the tail of this potential is repulsive in the separability region. Such resonances exist for sufficiently attractive interactions in the short hyperradius region where the wave function is no more separable. Instead of using realistic finite range reference Hamiltonians, a way to obtain universal laws in such regime is to use a contact model where by construction, the short distance physics which is not universal, is absent from the formalism. First studies of this regime using a contact model, was done in Refs.
Wer06b
;
Wer08
;
Gao15
. However, due to the normalization catastrophe, i.e. the fact that localized contact states are not square integrable when
{s\geq 1}
, the model was not believed to be tractable for arbitrary resonances and the universality of these states was questionable. Nevertheless, for a large class of resonances, one expects also near threshold universal laws as predicted in Ref.
Mat91
;
Kon17
;
Kon18
;
Son22
.

I.2
First advances obtained in Ref.
Pri23

Part of the answers to these pending issues has been given recently in Ref.
Pri23
in the context of the unitary limit. In this last reference a contact model for isolated
N
-body resonances has been proposed for all real values of the index
{s}
(in what follows
{s}
will be always considered as positive) when part of the particles interact resonantly in the s wave. The present paper permits one to replace the results of Ref.
Pri23
in a more general context. In this introduction, it is thus necessary to recall the main results obtained in Ref.
Pri23
as follows.

In a close analogy with the two-body scattering in high partial wave, it was shown that two parameters are in general needed to describe low energy states. The normalization catastrophe was solved by introducing a modified scalar product which restores also the self-adjoint character of the contact Hamiltonian. Considering the wronskian of two reference states (i.e. eigenstates of the finite range Hamiltonian associated with the contact model), this scalar product was shown to take into account the small hyperradius contribution of the reference states. A two parameter law for the binding energies was given for all values of
{s}
. Moreover, long lived quasi-bound states was predicted when
{s\geq 1}
, for vanishing detuning from the threshold. All the results obtained in Ref.
Pri23
was qualitatively interpreted by what can be called the 3D mapping, meaning the formal equivalence of the hyperradial
N
-body problem for half-integer values of the index
{s=\ell+1/2}
, with the radial problem of a two-body system in 3D with a resonant
\ell
wave interaction.

I.3
The need of a more general contact model

The scope of application of the contact model of Ref.
Pri23
, concerns systems in the vicinity of the unitary regime where part of the pair of particles interact resonantly in the s wave in a three-dimensional (3D) space. It appears that extension of the contact model to situations where there is no unitary limit or in a one-dimensional (1D) or a 2D space is possible. Another issue is how one can conciliate the two-parameter model of a
N
-body resonance in Ref.
Pri23
with the single parameter models of Refs.
Wer06b
;
Wer08
;
Nis08
when
{s<1}
? Always in the region
{s<1}
, when
{s=1/2}
, the 3D mapping for the contact model of Ref.
Pri23
leads to the description of a broad s wave resonance. There is no way to model the narrow resonant limit with this contact model: a generalization is thus expected. To end up this series of issues to solve, perhaps the most important one is to understand the link between the two parameters of the contact model and the reference model. More precisely, one can wonder how to conciliate an energy independent parameterization of the log-derivative of the wave function near the
N
-body contact (see Eq.(6) in Ref.
Pri23
), with the effective range approximation where the energy dependence is explicit, as was done in this last reference.

I.4
Main results and outline of the paper

In Ref.
Pri23
all the low energy properties were parameterized starting directly from a two-parameter contact model. The two parameters, i.e. the detuning from the resonance and the effective radius, were not directly relied on the behavior of the reference states. Here one adopts a more physical point of view in Sec.
II
by considering as a starting point, the properties of the reference states near the threshold. The reference model is introduced in this section together with the notion of separability region and by taking into account the possible occurrence of two-body resonances. The separability region is defined by an interval of the hyperradius
{R_{\rm sep}\leq\rho<R_{\rm sup}}
and also hyperangles such that the particles do not feel the short range interactions. In the separability region the reference state is approximated by the product of a function depending on the hyperradius and of a function of the hyperangles of the
N
-body problem.

All the threshold properties are deduced in the subsequent sections from the log-derivative of the hyperradial function at the radius
{R_{\rm sep}}
which defines the lower bound of the separability region. It is shown that the occupation of the small hyperradius region is related to the energy dependence of this log-derivative.

The hyperradial problem is characterized by an inverse square potential tail. The scattering problem in such a potential is revisited in Sec.
III
in the effective range approximation. The low energy properties are parameterized in terms of the generalized scattering length and of the range parameter. The different non efimovian regimes of resonance are then analyzed in Sec.
IV
.

Using the results of the preceding sections, the contact model is introduced in Sec.
V
. It models
N
-body resonances in the vicinity of the threshold and when few-body interactions are of short range. For
{s>1}
, one recovers the contact model of Ref.
Pri23
but for
{s<1}
, the model of Ref.
Pri23
appears as a particular case where the range parameter is not large. In Sec.
V.7
, the modified scalar product introduced in Ref.
Pri23
is generalized to encompass all the possible resonant regimes. A proof of its equivalence with the usual scalar product associated with the reference model is derived.

To have a qualitative picture of the spectrum when the upper bound of the separability region
R_{\rm sup}
is finite in absence of shallow dimer, a box model is introduced in Sec.
VI
. One obtains a branch similar to that of an Efimov spectrum.

In Sec.
VII
a simple finite range 1D model is used to compare the standard normalization with that obtained by using the modified scalar product in the contact model.

II
Reference model

II.1
Separability region

II.1.1
Generic case

One considers
{N}
point-like particles that evolve in a space of dimension
D
. The mass of the particle
i
is
{m_{i}}
and its spatial coordinates are
{\mathbf{r}_{i}}
. The interparticles interactions are characterized by the radius
R_{\rm pot}
. The Hamiltonian describing the system, denoted as the reference model, is supposed to have only one open channel. More precisely, deeply bound composite particles in the reference model are considered as stable and structureless. In other words, there is no possible break-up and rearrangement of these composite particles in few body collisions. In the generic case of a
N
-body resonance one considers a situation where there is no
M
-body resonance (
{M<N}
) in the system and if a
N
-body state exists it can be considered as brunnian (a generalization of the notion of Borromean states for the
N
-body problem, i.e. no subsystem is a bound state).

In this paper, the systems are considered in the center of mass frame. For convenience, one introduces the dimension of the configuration space of the particles which is given by

d=(N-1)D.

(1)

The positions of the particles are described by the
{d}
-dimensional hyperradius vector
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}

equals to the set of Jacobi coordinates
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}

{(j=1\dots N-1)}
, which are the relative coordinates between the center of mass
{\mathbf{C}_{j}}
of the set of particles
{1\dots j}
of total mass
{M_{j}}
and the particle
{j+1}
:

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{j}}{m_{\rm r}}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{j+1}-\mathbf{C}_{j}\right).

(2)

In Eq. (
2
),
m_{\rm r}
is an arbitrary reference mass and
\mu_{j}
is the reduced mass of the relative particle:

\mu_{j}=\frac{m_{j+1}M_{j}}{M_{j+1}}.

(3)

Other sets of Jacobi coordinates can be defined by permutations of the labels of the particles. In what follows, one will use the separability with respect to the hyperradius
\rho=\|\boldsymbol{\rho}\|
and the hyperangles
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
which parameterize the unit hypervector
{\boldsymbol{\rho}/\rho}
. In this coordinates system, the kinetic operator in the center of mass frame can be expressed in terms of the hyperradial kinetic operator

T_{\rho}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}\left(\partial_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{d-1}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\right)

(4)

and of the Laplacian
{\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}}
acting on the hypersphere of radius unity:

H_{0}=\frac{-\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=T_{\rho}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}\frac{\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}}{\rho^{2}}.

(5)

A standard approach in few-body physics is to expand the reference state in terms of the hyperspherical harmonics
{\Phi^{[\lambda]}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}

Fab83
:

\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{\rm ref}\rangle=\sum_{[\lambda]}\rho^{1-\frac{d}{2}}F^{[\lambda]}(\rho,E)\Phi^{[\lambda]}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}).

(6)

In Eq. (
6
) the notation
{[\lambda]}
gathers all the quantum numbers that define the system. The hyperspherical harmonics are eigenstates of the Laplacian on the unit hypersphere
{\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}}
:

-\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\Phi^{[\lambda]}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})=\Lambda\Phi^{[\lambda]}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}).

(7)

Near the threshold of a resonance, for a large class of systems, one component in the sum of Eq. (
6
) dominates for a sufficiently large hyperradius and one can use a separable approximation for
{\rho>R_{\rm sep}}
where
{R_{\rm sep}}
is of the order of the potential radius
R_{\rm pot}

Fed94
:

\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{\rm ref}\rangle\simeq\rho^{1-\frac{d}{2}}F(\rho,E)\Phi(\boldsymbol{\Omega}).

(8)

Without loss of generality, the hyperspherical function will be always normalized on the hypersphere:
{\langle\Phi|\Phi\rangle=1}
. In the separable approximation Eq. (
8
), one excludes all the configurations where the hyperradius
{\rho_{M}}
of
{M<N}
particles is of the order of
R_{\rm pot}
or smaller.

The radial pre-factor in the right-hand side of Eq. (
8
) has been chosen to have an effective 2D radial equation in the separability region

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}\left(\partial_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{\partial_{\rho}}{\rho}-\frac{s^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)F(\rho,E)=EF(\rho,E).

(9)

In Eq. (
9
), the index
{s}
is defined by

s^{2}=\Lambda+\left({d}/{2}-1\right)^{2},

(10)

where
\Lambda
is the eigenvalue of the dominant component in Eq. (
6
) obtained from Eq. (
7
) and
s
is chosen positive when it is real. In the generic case, the eigenvalues of Eq. (
7
) are positive and given by

\Lambda=K(K+d-2),

(11)

where the integer
{K\geq 0}
is the hypermoment. Negative values of
\Lambda
can be obtained in presence of two-body s wave resonant interactions in a 3D space.

Equation (
9
) coincides with the radial equation (outside the potential radius) of a single-particle in a 2D space with and effective angular momentum
{\hbar s}
. This formal equivalence, Eq. (
8
) can be qualified as a 2D mapping of the initial
N
-body problem. Equivalently, the 3D mapping is obtained with the change of function

F(\rho,E)=\sqrt{\rho}f(\rho,E),

(12)

leading formally to the radial equation (outside the potential radius) of a 3D two-body problem with the angular momentum
{\hbar(s-\frac{1}{2})}
.

The continuity of the log-derivative of the radial reference function at
{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}
permits one to traduce the effect of the short range interactions by a condition at the border of the separable region. Quite generally, for a state of energy
{E=\frac{\hbar^{2}k_{0}^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}}
, it can be written as

\frac{\rho\partial_{\rho}F(\rho,E)}{F(\rho,E)}\biggr|_{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}=-\upsilon(k_{0}^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2}),

(13)

where the function
{\upsilon(k_{0}^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2})}
depends on the details of the reference model at short distance.
It will be assumed in all the subsequent study that the energy of the system
{E}
is small in absolute value with respect to the high energy scale of the reference model:

E_{\rm sep}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}R_{\rm sep}^{2}}.

(14)

Consequently, the right-hand side of Eq. (
13
) can be approximated in the limit
{|k_{0}^{2}|R_{\rm sep}^{2}\ll 1}
by

\upsilon(k_{0}^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2})\simeq\upsilon_{0}+\upsilon_{0}^{\prime}k_{0}^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2}+\dots

(15)

II.1.2
Occurrence of resonant two-body interactions

The presence of a two-body resonant interaction changes radically the structure of the separability region and the eigenvalue problem on the hypersphere in Eq. (
7
). When
{D=3}
, for a s wave resonant interaction between two particles
{(ij)}
of reduced mass
\mu
, with a scattering length
{a_{\rm 3D}}
, there is a boundary condition near the contact of the particles which is given by the Bethe-Peierls condition:

\Psi_{\rm ref}(\mathbf{r}_{1}\dots\mathbf{r}_{N})\simeq A\times\left(\frac{1}{r_{ij}}-\frac{1}{a_{\rm 3D}}\right).

(16)

In Eq. (
16
)
{|a_{\rm 3D}|\gg R_{\rm pot}}
, the relative radius
{r_{ij}}
is small with respect to all the lengths in the system except the potential radius:
{r_{ij}>R_{\rm pot}}
and
{A}
depends on the other coordinates. For a simplified analysis, the two-body behavior in Eq. (
16
) is extended formally to arbitrarily small values of
{r_{ij}}
. In absence of
M
-body resonance in the system (
N>M\geq 3
) one recovers the separability at the unitary limit
{|a_{\rm 3D}|=\infty}
, but the eigenvalue problem on the hypersphere changes due to the singularity at the vicinity of the contact of the two particles. The eigenvalues
{\Lambda}
in Eq. (
7
) are obtained in the zero-range approximation of the two-body interaction by imposing the contact condition
{\Phi(\boldsymbol{\Omega})\propto\frac{\rho}{r_{ij}}}
when
{r_{ij}\to 0}
for each pair
{(ij)}
interacting resonantly in the s wave. Except for
{N=3}
, there is no general solution such as Eq. (
11
) of this eigenvalue problem
Wer08
. For a large but finite value of
{|a_{\rm 3D}|}
, the reference wave function behaves as in the unitary limit for intermediate values of the hyperradius
{R_{\rm pot}<\rho\ll|a_{\rm 3D}|}
. In this interval of the hyperradius and again in absence
M
-body resonance (
{N>M\geq 3}
), for an energy
E
in the interval
{\hbar^{2}/(2\mu a_{\rm 3D}^{2})\ll|E|\ll E_{\rm sep}}
the reference wave function is separable with the same index
{s}
as in the unitary limit. Moreover, when the reference state is brunnian (domain
{a_{\rm 3D}<0}
) with an energy sufficiently near the threshold
{|E|\ll\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu a_{\rm 3D}^{2}}}
, one recovers the separability for
{\rho\gg|a_{\rm 3D}|}
with the index
{s}
obtained this time from Eq. (
11
).
Depending on the system considered, it is also possible to find a small imaginary value for
{s}
in the solutions of Eq. (
7
). This corresponds to the formal occurrence of an Efimov effect with a discrete scaling factor
{e^{\pi/|s|}}
so huge, that it cannot be observable due to the lower bound
{-E_{\rm sep}}
and the finite value of
{a_{\rm 3D}}
. In this case, a
N
-body resonance corresponding to a real value of
s
together with a separability region can be found. This is the case for instance in two neutrons Halo states
Nai23
.

In a 2D space, a two-body scattering resonance in the s wave for a pair of particles
{(ij)}
leads to a logarithmic behavior in the limit of small interparticle radius
{r_{ij}}
and Eq. (
16
) is replaced by

\Psi_{\rm ref}(\mathbf{r}_{1}\dots\mathbf{r}_{N})\simeq A\times\ln\left(\frac{r_{ij}}{a_{\rm 2D}}\right).

(17)

Near the resonance, the 2D scattering length
{a_{2D}}
is large, but contrary to the 3D case, there is no scale invariance. Moreover there is always a shallow dimer in the resonant limit and thus no brunnian state is possible
Pri07
. Consequently, in the resonant limit, there is no separable region. The separability can be recovered only in the non resonant case for an hyperradius
\rho\gg a_{\rm 2D}
and for states of energy
{|E|\ll\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}a_{\rm 2D}^{2}}}
: a situation which corresponds to a value of
{a_{\rm 2D}}
of the order of
R_{\rm pot}
.

In a 1D space, the two-body scattering resonance in the even sector occurs for a vanishing 1D scattering length
{a_{\rm 1D}\simeq 0}
and the behavior of the reference state in the limit of a small
{r_{ij}}
is given by

\Psi_{\rm ref}(\mathbf{r}_{1}\dots\mathbf{r}_{N})\simeq A\times\left(r_{ij}-a_{\rm 1D}\right).

(18)

In the vicinity of the two-body resonance there is no shallow dimer. One recovers at the resonance, the scale invariance and the separability with again a modification of the eigenvalue problem on the hypersphere. An example of state without
N
-body resonance which exhibits this scale invariance is given by the
N
-boson problem in the Tonks regime
Gir60
.

Two-body resonant interactions in a higher partial wave
{\ell\geq 1}
are characterized by a typical length
{L}
linked to the range term which is of the order of, or larger than the potential radius
Pri06a
. Then, for a brunnian state of energy much smaller than
{\hbar^{2}/(m_{\rm r}L^{2})}
, the situation does not basically differs from the case of Sec.
II.1.1
.

In all these situations one can identify two lengths
{R_{\rm sep}}
and
{R_{\rm sup}}
such that there is an hyper-angle/hyperradius separability of the reference wave function as in Eq. (
8
) in a wide range of scales of the hyperradius

R_{\rm sep}<\rho<R_{\rm sup}

(19)

and when also
{r_{ij}>R_{\rm sep}}
for all interacting pairs
{ij}
.

II.2
Resonance condition

In analogy with the two-body s wave resonant scattering in a 3D space, one adopts here the definition of a
N
-body resonance by the threshold at which a
N
-body bound state has a vanishing energy, i.e.
{qR_{\rm sep}\to 0}
in Eq. (
13
). Assuming that the ratio
{R_{\rm sup}/R_{\rm sep}}
is sufficiently large, one can consider the limit
{R_{\rm sup}=\infty}
. The bound state solution of Eq. (
9
) for
{\rho>R_{\rm sep}}
is then well approximated by the Macdonald function in the separability region:

F(\rho,E)=\mathcal{A}K_{s}(q\rho).

(20)

In Eq. (
20
),
{\mathcal{A}}
is a normalization constant and the binding wavenumber

is given by

{q=\sqrt{-2m_{\rm r}E}/\hbar}
. Using the fact that

\frac{x\partial_{x}K_{s}(x)}{K_{s}(x)}\biggr|_{x=0}=-s

(21)

one deduces from Eq. (
13
) that for
{R_{\rm sup}=\infty}
, the zero energy
N
-body resonance i.e. the threshold of the resonance occurs when

\upsilon_{0}=s.

(22)

In what follows the detuning from the resonance will be denoted by

\delta=s-\upsilon_{0}.

(23)

II.3
Occupation of the small hyperradius region

Using the fact that the reference Hamiltonian is self-adjoint, one can deduce
the contribution to the normalization of the small hyperradius region in terms of the wronskian:

\int_{\rho<R_{\rm sep}}d\mu\,\left|\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{\rm ref}(E)\rangle\right|^{2}=\lim_{E^{\prime}\to E}\frac{\hbar^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{d-1}}{2m_{r}}\\
\times\frac{\int d\Omega\,W\left[\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{\rm ref}(E^{\prime})\rangle^{*},\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{\rm ref}(E)\rangle,\rho=R_{\rm sep}\right]}{E^{\prime}-E}.

(24)

To obtain this last equation, one has used the fact that for realistic potentials, the reference wave function and its radial derivative vanish at the origin
{\rho=0}
. In Eq. (
24
),
{d\mu=\rho^{d-1}d\rho d\Omega}
where
{d\Omega}
is the measure on the hypersphere and the notation
{W[f,g,\rho=R_{\rm sep}]=f\partial_{\rho}g-g\partial_{\rho}f}
is the wronskian of the functions
f
and
g
with respect to the variable
\rho
, considered here at
{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}
.

Using the separability approximation of the reference state in Eq. (
8
) at
{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}
and Eq. (
13
), one obtains from Eq. (
24
) the contribution to the norm, of the small hyperradius region in the low energy limit (
|E|\ll E_{\rm sep}
):

\int_{\rho<R_{\rm sep}}d\mu\,\left|\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{\rm ref}(E)\rangle\right|^{2}\simeq\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}\left|R_{\rm sep}F(R_{\rm sep},E)\right|^{2}.

(25)

From Eq. (
25
) one obtains the inequality:

\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}>0.

(26)

Consider now a bound state of energy
E
when
R_{\rm sup}=\infty
. The probability to find the particles in the region
{\rho<R_{\rm sep}}
is

\mathcal{P}_{<R_{\rm sep}}(E)=\frac{\int_{\rho<R_{\rm sep}}d\mu\,\left|\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{\rm ref}(E)\rangle\right|^{2}}{\left|\langle\Psi_{\rm ref}(E)|\Psi_{\rm ref}(E)\rangle\right|^{2}}

(27)

and at the threshold of the resonance, using Eq. (
153
), one finds:

\lim_{E\to 0}\mathcal{P}_{<R_{\rm sep}}(E)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2(s-1)\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}}}&\text{if}\,s>1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(28)

Hence, there is no abrupt change in the occupation of the small hyperradius region at the critical value
{s=1}
. Instead, for a fixed value of
{\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}}
and for increasing values of the index
s
, one has a continuous increases of the occupation in this region.

III
Scattering process in an inverse square potential

III.1
Partial wave amplitude

The wave function in Eq. (
8
) can be interpreted as the eigenstate of a single particle in a symmetric central potential in a
d
-dimensional space. In this point of view, one can consider the scattering problem of the particle on a central potential with a tail having an inverse square law.

At negative energy
{E=-\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}<0}
and in the separability region, the hyperradial function is a linear combination of the two modified Bessel functions

F(\rho,E)={\mathcal{A}}(E)K_{s}(q\rho)+{\mathcal{B}}(E)I_{s}(q\rho).

(29)

When
{R_{\rm sup}=\infty}
, a bound state of binding energy
{E_{B}}
is such that
{\lim_{\rho\to\infty}F(\rho,E_{B})=0}
and thus
{{\mathcal{B}}(E_{B})=0}
. The radial scattering function at energy
{E=\frac{\hbar^{2}k_{0}^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}>0}
of this single-particle system is obtained by performing the analytical continuation of Eq. (
29
) with
{q=-ik_{0}}
for
{\rho>R_{\rm sep}}
. The ratio
{{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{B}}}
can then be redefined in terms of a partial wave amplitude (the other part of the scattering amplitude is a function of the hyperangles) at energy
E
with:

f_{s}(E)=\frac{\pi{\mathcal{A}}(E)e^{i\pi s}k_{0}^{2-d}}{2{\mathcal{B}}(E)}.

(30)

Then for
{E>0}
:

F(\rho,E)=i^{-s}{\mathcal{B}}(E)\left[J_{s}(k_{0}\rho)+ik_{0}^{d-2}f_{s}(E)H_{s}^{(1)}(k_{0}\rho)\right].

(31)

the factor
{k_{0}^{d-2}}
has been inserted for convenience by considering the expansion of the
d
-dimensional plane wave on the hyperspherical harmonics
Fab83
;
Wen85
with a first term corresponding to the lowest hypermoment
{K=0}
, proportional to
{(k_{0}\rho)^{1-d/2}J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(k_{0}\rho)}
, and also the expression of the
d
-dimensional Green’s function of the free Schrödinger equation at energy
{E_{0}}
for a source term
-\delta(\boldsymbol{\rho})
, i.e.

G_{d}(\rho,E)=\frac{-i\pi m_{\rm r}}{(2\pi)^{d/2}\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{k_{0}}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}H_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{(1)}(k_{0}\rho).

(32)

Coming back to the
N
-body problem, one has to be aware that Eq. (
31
) does not take into account the possible
M
-body scattering process (
{M<N}
) but corresponds somehow to a pure
N
-body scattering.

III.2
Scattering phase shift

The link between the partial wave amplitude and the scattering phase shift is obtained by considering another possible expression of the hyperradial function for
{\rho>R_{\rm sep}}
in Eq.(
31
) by using Eq. (
148
):

F(\rho,E)=\frac{i^{-s}{\mathcal{B}}(E)}{2}\left[\left[1+2if_{s}(E)k_{0}^{d-2}\right]H_{s}^{(1)}(k_{0}\rho)\right.\\
\left.+H_{s}^{(2)}(k_{0}\rho)\right].

(33)

In absence of interaction, the partial wave amplitude is zero, so that at low energy (
{k_{0}R_{\rm sep}\ll 1}
) and large distance (
{k_{0}\rho\gg 1}
), the hyperradial function is

F_{\rm free}(\rho,E)\simeq\frac{i^{-s}{\mathcal{B}}(E)}{\sqrt{2\pi k_{0}\rho}}\left(e^{i(k_{0}\rho-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi s}{2})}+e^{-i(k_{0}\rho-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi s}{2})}\right),

(34)

where one has used Eq. (
151
) and Eq. (
150
). The interaction introduces the scattering phase-shift
{\delta_{s}(E)}
such that in the low energy and large distance limit:

F(\rho,E)\simeq\frac{i^{-s}e^{i\delta_{s}(E)}{\mathcal{B}}(E)}{\sqrt{2\pi k_{0}\rho}}\left(e^{i(k_{0}\rho-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi s}{2}+\delta_{s}(E))}\right.\\
\left.+e^{-i(k_{0}\rho-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi s}{2}+\delta_{s}(E))}\right).

(35)

The comparison of Eq. (
35
) with Eq. (
33
) permits one to define the scattering phase shift
{\delta_{s}(E)}
in terms of the partial wave amplitude

e^{2i\delta_{s}(E)}=1+2if_{s}(E)k_{0}^{d-2}

(36)

so that

f_{s}(E)=\frac{k_{0}^{2-d}}{\cot\delta_{s}(E)-i}=-ik_{0}^{2-d}e^{i\delta_{s}(E)}\sin\delta_{s}(E)

(37)

The form of the function
{\cot\delta(E)}
in the low energy limit is deduced from the log-derivative of the reference hyperradial function at
{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}
given in Eq. (
13
). Using Eq. (
149
) and Eq. (
150
), one finds

\cot\delta_{s}(E)=\cot\pi s-k_{0}^{-2s}u_{s}(k_{0}^{2}),

(38)

where the function

u_{s}(k_{0}^{2})=\frac{k_{0}^{2s}}{\sin\pi s}\times\frac{\upsilon(x^{2})J_{-s}(x)+xJ_{-s}^{\prime}(x)}{\upsilon(x^{2})J_{s}(x)+xJ_{s}^{\prime}(x)}\bigg|_{x=k_{0}R_{\rm sep}}

(39)

characterizes the effect of the interactions and is regular at
{k_{0}=0}
for non integer values of
{s}
.

From Eq. (
39
), and the relations in Eqs. (
149
,
147
), one deduces as expected that the poles of the partial wave amplitude in Eq. (
37
) correspond to the binding energies which verify also

\frac{x\partial_{x}K_{s}(x)}{K_{s}(x)}\biggr|_{x=qR_{\rm sep}}=-\upsilon(-q^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2}).

(40)

III.3
Scattering parameters in the low energy limit

In the low energy limit, the function
{u_{s}(k_{0}^{2})}
can be expanded in a series of
{k_{0}^{2}}
. Limiting the low energy expansion of
{u_{s}}
at the order of
k_{0}^{2}
, the partial wave amplitude permits one to define the scattering parameters at low energy:

u_{s}(k_{0}^{2})=\frac{1}{\xi_{s}}+\alpha_{s}k_{0}^{2}+C(s,k_{0}^{2}).

(41)

In this last equation, the term
{C(s,k_{0}^{2})=O(k_{0}^{4})}
is the remainder of the expansion of the function
{u_{s}(k_{0}^{2})}
and the two first terms in the right-hand-side, define the effective range approximation. In what follows, the parameter
{\xi_{s}}
will be denoted as the generalized scattering length and
{\alpha_{s}}
, as the range parameter. From Eq. (
39
), one has

\displaystyle\xi_{s}=\frac{\pi R_{\rm sep}^{2s}}{s4^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}\left(\frac{\upsilon_{0}+s}{\upsilon_{0}-s}\right)

(42)

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}=\frac{s4^{s-1}\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\pi}R_{\rm sep}^{2-2s}\left(\frac{s-\upsilon_{0}}{s+\upsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\biggl[\frac{4\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}+\frac{2+\upsilon_{0}-s}{s-1}}{s-\upsilon_{0}}-(s\rightarrow-s)\biggr].

(43)

The generalized scattering length gives the coefficient of the Wigner’s threshold law for the partial wave amplitude:

f_{s}(E)\underset{k_{0}\to 0}{\simeq}-k_{0}^{2s+2-d}\xi_{s}.

(44)

As expected, using Eq. (
22
), one finds that the generalized scattering length is arbitrarily large at resonance
{|\xi_{s}|=\infty}
whereas the range parameter is

\alpha_{s}^{\rm res}=\frac{4^{s-1}R_{\rm sep}^{2-2s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\pi(s-1)}\left(1+2(s-1)\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}\right).

(45)

In the relevant limit of a small detuning
{|\delta|\ll 1}
, the two scattering parameters in Eq. (
42
,
43
) can be written:

\xi_{s}=-\frac{\pi R_{\rm sep}^{2s}}{s4^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}\left(\frac{2s-\delta}{\delta}\right)\,;\,\alpha_{s}\simeq\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\rm res}}{\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2s}\right)^{2}}.

(46)

For
{s>1}
, the range parameter is always positive and from Eq. (
26
) one obtains the generalization of the width radius inequality already obtained for high partial wave scattering
Pri06a
:

\alpha_{s}^{\rm res}R_{\rm sep}^{2(s-1)}>\frac{\Gamma(s)^{2}}{4^{(1-s)}(s-1)\pi}

(47)

III.4
Mapping to the two-body problem

Let us consider a two-body scattering process in the center of mass frame in a 2D space. For an incident plane of wave vector
\mathbf{k}_{0}
, the scattering state
|\Psi_{\rm 2D}\rangle
can be written asymptotically for
k_{0}r\gg 1

:

\Psi_{\rm 2D}(\mathbf{r})\simeq e^{i\mathbf{k}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{r}}+\sqrt{\frac{i}{k_{0}r}}f_{\rm 2D}(k_{0},\theta)e^{ik_{0}r}

(48)

where
\mathbf{r}
denote the relative coordinates,
{\theta=\angle{(k_{0},\mathbf{r})}}
and the partial wave amplitudes
{f_{\rm 2D}^{[n]}(E)}
for the angular momentum
{n\hbar}
can be defined by

f_{\rm 2D}(k_{0},\theta)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{\rm 2D}^{[n]}(E)e^{in\theta}.

(49)

Using this last definition, the scattering state can be expressed for
r
larger than the 2D potential radius, as:

\Psi_{\rm 2D}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}i^{n}e^{in\theta}\left[J_{n}(k_{0}r)+if_{\rm 2D}^{[n]}(E)H_{n}^{(1)}(k_{0}r)\right].

(50)

For an isotropic interaction, the 2D partial wave amplitudes verify
{f_{\rm 2D}^{[-n]}(E)=f_{\rm 2D}^{[n]}(E)}
and can be related to the partial wave amplitude in Eq. (
37
) with

f_{\rm 2D}^{[n]}(E)=f_{n}(E)k_{0}^{d-2},

(51)

which is a consequence of the 2D mapping.

In the 3D two-body problem with an isotropic interaction potential, the partial wave amplitude is defined from the scattering phase shift by

f_{\rm 3D}^{[\ell]}(E)=\frac{1}{k_{0}\cot\delta(E)-ik_{0}}.

(52)

The partial wave amplitude is thus also proportional to the 3D two-body partial wave amplitude in the
l
-wave when
{s=\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
is an half-integer values with:

f_{\rm 3D}^{[\ell]}(E)=f_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(E)k_{0}^{d-3}.

(53)

IV
Different regimes of resonances

The study of the shallow bound state near the resonance threshold permits one to identify different types of resonant regimes. Using the effective range approximation for
{\upsilon(-q^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2})}
in Eq. (
41
) gives an accurate approximation for the determination of the bound or quasi-bound state energies near threshold with the equation:

\frac{R_{\rm sep}^{2s}}{\xi_{s}}+\alpha_{s}R_{\rm sep}^{2(s-1)}\frac{E}{E_{\rm sep}}+C\left(s,\frac{E}{E_{\rm sep}}\right)=\frac{\left(-\frac{E}{E_{\rm sep}}\right)^{s}}{\sin\pi s}.

(54)

Complex solutions of Eq. (
54
) with a small negative imaginary part and a positive real part correspond to quasi-bound states. Equation (
54
) can be also written in terms of the binding wavenumber as

\frac{1}{\xi_{s}}-\alpha_{s}q^{2}+R_{\rm sep}^{-2s}C(s,-q^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2})=\frac{q^{2s}}{\sin\pi s}.

(55)

Let us make some comments about the singular behavior of the right-hand side of Eqs. (
54
,
55
) near integer values of
{s}
. In the limit
{s=0}
, the singularity is compensated by the one of left-hand side where
{\xi_{s}\sim\pi s}
. In the limit
{s=1}
, the singularity is this time compensated by the range term in the left-hand side where
{\alpha_{s}\sim 1/(\pi(s-1))}
. For higher integer values of
{s}
, the singularity is compensated by the remainder
{C(s,-q^{2})}
which plays the role of a counter term.

For a small detuning, the remainder can be always neglected for
{s<1+\eta}
where
\eta>0
is at an intermediary value between 0 and 1, typically
{\eta\sim 0.2}
. The exact expression of
{C(s,z)}
depends on the
{i-{\rm th}}
derivatives
{\upsilon_{0}^{(i)}}

{i=0\dots n}
which have been neglected for
{i\geq 2}
in the effective range approximation. Then in this respect, it can be chosen arbitrarily with the sole aim of curing the singularity. For example a possible counterterm is
{C(s,z)=0}
for
{s<1+\eta}
and for
{s\geq 1+\eta}
:

C(s,z)=\frac{z^{n}}{\pi(s-n)}\ \text{where}\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n=\lceil s\rceil&\text{if}\ s>\lfloor s\rfloor+\eta\\
n=\lfloor s\rfloor&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(56)

When
s=n\geq 2
is an integer, Eq. (
54
) becomes

\frac{R_{\rm sep}^{2n}}{\xi_{s}}+\alpha_{s}R_{\rm sep}^{2n-2}\frac{E}{E_{\rm sep}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{E}{E_{\rm sep}}\right)^{n}\ln\left(-\frac{E}{E_{\rm sep}}\right).

(57)

For a given value of the index
s
and of the separability radius
{R_{\rm sep}}
, the spectrum is defined from the values of
{\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}}
and of the detuning
\delta
.

IV.1
Regimes of vanishing detuning

If the detuning
{\delta}
is sufficiently small, the generalized scattering length
{\xi_{s}}
is large. Due to the vicinity with the threshold, this corresponds to the situation where one finds a low energy bound (when
{\xi_{s}>0}
) or possibly quasi-bound (when
{\xi_{s}<0}
) state. In this small detuning limit, using Eq. (
46
), the range parameter can be approximated by its value at resonance only when
{|\delta|\ll s}
: a condition which is not verified in the vicinity of the Efimov threshold where
{s\to 0}
.

IV.1.1
One-parameter resonant regime

When
{s<1}
the range term can be negligible in Eqs. (
54
,
55
) in the small energy limit
{qR_{\rm sep}\ll 1}
. This happens in two situations:
i)
for a vanishing value of the detuning when the index is not too close to unity [
{qR_{\rm sep}\ll(qR_{\rm sep})^{s}}
in Eqs. (
54
,
55
)], and/or
ii)
when

\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}=\frac{1}{2(1-s)}.

(58)

In this regime, for a positive generalized scattering length (i.e.
{\delta<0}
), there is one bound state with a one-parameter law for the binding wave number

q\simeq\left(\frac{\sin(\pi s)}{\xi_{s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}

(59)

The binding energy
{E_{\rm 1p}}
is then

E_{\rm 1p}=-4E_{\rm sep}\left(\frac{s\sin(\pi s)\Gamma(s)^{2}\delta}{\pi(\delta-2s)}\right)^{1/s}.

(60)

This regime has been first defined and studied in Refs.
Wer06b
;
Wer08
;
Nis08
. Importantly, there is no quasi-bound state in this regime when
{\delta>0}
.

IV.1.2
Two-parameter resonant regime

The range parameter plays a increasing role for increasing values of the index
s
. For
s\geq 1
it can never be neglected for vanishing values of the detuning and in the low energy limit [
{qR_{\rm sep}\gg(qR_{\rm sep})^{s}}
in Eq. (
54
)]. Starting from the one-parameter law, valid for small values of the index
s
, the spectrum reachs a two-parameter law as
s
increases. In the limit of a large index
s
, the binding wavenumber is given by:

q^{2}\sim\frac{1}{\xi_{s}\alpha_{s}}.

(61)

Equation (
61
) is a very good approximation even when
s
is larger but of the order of the unity. The binding energy is thus

E=E_{r}\simeq\frac{E_{\rm sep}\delta}{\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}+\frac{1}{2(s-1)}}.

(62)

Still for
{s>1}
, aside the shallow bound state solution which exists for a small and negative detuning
\delta
, i.e. for a large and positive generalized scattering length, when
\delta
is small and positive, there is a long-lived quasi-bound state associated with the complex root of Eq. (
54
):

E=E_{r}-i\frac{\Gamma}{2}.

(63)

The resonance energy
{E_{r}>0}
can be approximated by Eq. (
62
) and the width
\Gamma
by

\frac{\Gamma}{E_{r}}\simeq\frac{2\pi(s-1)4^{1-s}}{(1+2(s-1)\upsilon^{\prime}_{0})\Gamma(s)^{2}}\left(\frac{E_{r}}{E_{\rm sep}}\right)^{s-1}.

(64)

This form of asymptotic behavior in
{E_{r}}^{s-1}
was already found in Ref.
Mat91
for half integer values of the index and more recently in Ref.
Son22
by using the Effective Field Theory formalism [see Eq.(1) in this last reference with the identity
{\Delta=s+\frac{5}{2}}
]. Equation (
64

\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}
that characterizes the reference state at small hyperradius.

V
Contact model

V.1
Construction of the contact model

In the contact model, the separability is extended in the region of small hyperradius i.e. for
{0<\rho<R_{\rm sup}}
where the hyperradial functions verify Eq. (
9
).

A contact model defines a set of eigenstates
\{|\Psi\rangle\}
(contact eigenstates) associated with the set of the eigenstates of the reference model
\{|\Psi_{\rm ref}\rangle\}
(reference eigenstates). The contact eigenstates verify the free Schrödinger equation everywhere except at the contact of two or more interacting particles and (almost) coincide with their corresponding reference states for
{\rho>R_{\rm sep}}
. This last property is obtained by imposing appropriate asymptotic condition(s) on the contact states at vanishing inter-particle distances.

In the contact model, the stationary Schrödinger equation for a state
|\Psi\rangle
of energy
E
is:

V.2
Log-derivative condition

In the low energy limit, one expects an equivalence between the contact model and the reference model valid at the order of the effective range approximation. The contact hyperradial function verifies thus a log-derivative condition of the following form:

\frac{\rho\partial_{\rho}F(\rho,E)}{F(\rho,E)}\biggr|_{\rho=R}=\epsilon-s-\theta R^{2}k_{0}^{2}.

(82)

The parameter
{\epsilon}
plays the role of a detuning parameter for the contact model and the length
{R}
is denoted in what follows as the effective radius.

The log-derivative condition in Eq. (
82
) is a simple way to impose the asymptotic behavior of the contact states in the vicinity of the singularity at
{\rho=0}
. This condition generalizes the energy independent condition used in Ref.
Pri23
. It is an alternative way to define a contact model which is more usually defined by using a contact condition. The contact condition equivalent to Eq.(
82
) will be given in Sec.
V.6
.

V.3

The three parameters

and

(83)

More generally, the equivalence of the two models is obtained by identifying the generalized scattering length and respectively the range parameter of the two models. Thus, the parameters

,

\displaystyle\xi_{s}=\frac{\pi(\epsilon-2s)R^{2s}}{\epsilon s4^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}=\frac{4^{s}\Gamma(s+1)^{2}R^{2-2s}}{\pi(s-1)(2s-\epsilon)^{2}}\left(1+2(s-1)\theta\right).

(85)

where

and

are given in Eqs. (

It is worth pointing out that Eqs. (

V.6
Contact Condition

V.6.1
Construction of the condition

The contact condition for the hyperradial function is a way to impose a specific behavior for a vanishing hyperradius such that one recovers the behavior of the radial reference wave function at small but finite hyper radius of the order of
{R_{\rm sep}}
. Due to the universality of the behavior of the contact states in the limit of small hyperradius, one can make a reasoning at negative energy with
{F(\rho,E)=K_{s}(q\rho)}
when
R_{\rm sup}=\infty
. The contact condition at order of the effective range approximation, is then obtained by finding the linear operator that performs the mapping of the small hyperradius limit of the contact hyperradial function
{F(\rho,E)}
to

\frac{1}{\xi_{s}}-\alpha_{s}q^{2}+R_{\rm sep}^{-2s}C(s,-q^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2})-\frac{q^{2s}}{\sin\pi s}.

(101)

Then, the contact states that all verify Eq. (
55
) belong to the kernel of this operator. For this purpose, one uses the behavior of the Macdonald function
{K_{s}(x)}
when
{x\to 0}
:

K_{s}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{2}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-s}\biggl[1-\frac{x^{2}}{4(s-1)}\\
+\dots\frac{\Gamma(s-k)}{\Gamma(s)k!}\left(\frac{-x^{2}}{4}\right)^{k}\\
+\frac{\Gamma(-s)}{\Gamma(s)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2s}+\dots\biggr]

(102)

The mapping is done for each term of Eq. (
55
) as follows

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q\rho}{2}\right)^{-s}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\xi_{s}}

(103)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{s-1}\left(\frac{q\rho}{2}\right)^{-s+2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow-\alpha_{s}q^{2}

(104)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(-s)}{\Gamma(s)}\left(\frac{q\rho}{2}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow-\frac{q^{2s}}{\sin\pi s}

(105)

and if
s>1+\eta
, as discussed previously, it is necessary to introduce a counter-term with

\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(s-k)}{\Gamma(s)k!}\left(\frac{q\rho}{2}\right)^{2k-s}\longrightarrow\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{2k}R^{2(k-s)}}{\pi(s-k)}

(106)

where
k=\lceil s\rceil
if
s>\lfloor s\rfloor+\eta
and
k=\lfloor s\rfloor
otherwise. For convenience one uses the operator already introduced in Ref.
Pri23

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\bigl]\rho^{\beta},F(\rho)\bigr[

(107)

which gives the coefficient of the term
{\rho^{\beta}}
in the expansion of
{F(\rho)}
as
{\rho\to 0}
. In this way, one obtains the following contact condition for
{s<1+\eta}
:

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\bigg]\frac{\rho^{-s}}{\xi_{s}}+4(s-1)\alpha_{s}\rho^{2-s}+\frac{s(4\rho)^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\pi},F(\rho)\bigg[=0.

(108)

and for
{s\geq 1+\eta}
:

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\bigg]\frac{\rho^{-s}}{\xi_{s}}+4(s-1)\alpha_{s}\rho^{2-s}+\frac{s(4\rho)^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\pi}\\
+\frac{4^{k}\Gamma(s)k!R^{2(k-s)}}{\pi\Gamma(s-k+1)}\rho^{2k-s},F(\rho)\bigg[=0,

(109)

where
{k=\lceil s\rceil}
if
{s>\lfloor s\rfloor+\eta}
and
{k=\lfloor s\rfloor}
otherwise.

By construction, the contact condition in Eq. (
108
) (or Eq. (
109
)) is equivalent to the log-derivative condition (
82
) when the calculations are performed at the order of the effective range approximation.

Interestingly, the contact condition in Eq. (
108
) allows for a continuous description of the spectrum at the effective range order as a function of the index
s\in[0,1+\eta[
. This permits to take into account the large range parameter limit for all these values of the index. In this way the transition from the one-parameter regime to the regime of large range parameter of Sec.
IV.2
can be studied accurately beginning from an index in the vicinity of the Efimov threshold
{s=0}
. In contrast, in Ref.
Pri23
, the effective range approximation was not used systematically and the range was neglected for
{s<\eta}
to avoid a spurious divergence near
{s=0}
.

One-parameter resonant regime

In the one-parameter resonant regime defined only for

V.7
Modified scalar product

V.7.1
General expression and properties

Two contact states defined by the domain in Eq. (
82
) are not mutually orthogonal. Moreover the singularity in
{\rho^{-s}}
of any contact state when
{\rho\to 0}
, leads to an arbitrarily large occupation of the small hyperradius region when
{s\geq 1}
. This normalization catastrophe and the non orthogonality problem was solved in the standard resonant regime by introducing a modified scalar product in Ref.
Pri23
, in the same spirit of what was done in the two-body contact model for a high partial wave resonance in Refs.
Pri06a
;
Pri06b
. This method can be extended to the present formalism. The
{\theta}
invariance of the results will be fruitfully used to show the equivalence with the usual scalar product when one considers the reference model.

One begins with the wronskian equality for two contact eigenstates with two distinct energies
{E_{1}\neq E_{2}}
when
{R_{\rm sup}=\infty}
. For this purpose, one uses the 2D radial Schrödinger equation for
{F(\rho,E_{1})}
in Eq. (
9
) multiplied by
{\rho F(\rho,E_{2})^{*}}
and subtracted by the complex conjugate of its analog obtained by the substitution
{E_{1}\leftrightarrow E_{2}}
. Integration of this last equation between
{\rho=R}
and
{\rho=\infty}
gives:

\int_{R}^{\infty}\rho F(\rho,E_{1})F(\rho,E_{2})^{*}d\rho=\frac{\hbar^{2}R}{2m_{\rm r}(E_{2}-E_{1})}\\
\times W\left[F(\rho,E_{1}),F(\rho,E_{2})^{*},\rho=R\right].

(121)

Then, using the log-derivative condition in Eq. (
82
), one finds

\int_{R}^{\infty}\rho F(\rho,E_{1})F(\rho,E_{2})^{*}d\rho\\
+R^{2}\theta F(R,E_{1})F(R,E_{2})^{*}=0.

(122)

The modified scalar product which ensures the orthogonality of two eigenstates of the contact model and solves the normalization catastrophe is deduced directly from Eq. (
122
). For two contact states
(|\Psi_{1}\rangle,|\Psi_{2}\rangle)
with the respective hyperradial functions
{(F_{1}(\rho),F_{2}(\rho))}
it is defined by:

\left(\Psi_{1}|\Psi_{2}\right)_{0}=\int_{\rho>R}d\mu\langle\Psi_{1}|\boldsymbol{\rho}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{2}\rangle\\
+R^{2}\theta F_{1}(R)^{*}F_{2}(R).

(123)

This scalar product is independent of the energy of the eigenstates and can then be used for all states in the domain defined by Eq. (
82
).

In the standard resonant regime of Sec.
V.4.2
, one can choose the parameters
{(R,\epsilon)}
such that
{\theta=0}
and the modified scalar product has the very intuitive form obtained in Ref.
Pri23
where the parameter
{R}
plays the role of a cut-off introduced in the usual scalar product:

\left(\Psi_{1}|\Psi_{2}\right)_{0}=\int_{\rho>R}d\mu\langle\Psi_{1}|\boldsymbol{\rho}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}|\Psi_{2}\rangle.

(124)

It is also of interest to consider the one-parameter regime. It is shown in Ref.
Wer08
(pages 65-66) from a contact condition equivalent to Eq. (
110
), that the contact model is self-adjoint with respect to the usual scalar product in this regime. Thus in this regime, in principle there is no need to use a modified scalar product. Nevertheless, there is no contradiction with the preceding results in this particular case. Indeed, there are two possible conditions for having the one-parameter regime when
{s<1}
. The first one, corresponds to the limit of vanishing value of the detuning
{\delta\simeq 0}
. One finds in this limit a negligible contribution [
{\equiv O((qR_{\rm sep})^{2(1-s)})}
] in the norm, of the small hyperradius region. Then at the lowest order in energy the modified scalar product coincides with the usual one. The second possible condition is given by Eq. (
58
). In this case, the small hyperradius region contribution is even smaller than in the preceding case [
\equiv O((qR_{\rm sep})^{2})
].

The invariance of the modified scalar product in a change of the parameters
{(\epsilon,R,\theta)}
verifying Eqs. (

,

Using Eqs. (

From the definition in Eq. (

(126)

At

is obtained from Eq. (

(127)

\frac{d\left(\Psi|\Psi\right)_{0}}{\left(\Psi|\Psi\right)_{0}}=\frac{dR}{R}\left(\epsilon+2(1-s)(\theta qR)^{2}\right)\times O\left(1\right)

As expected one obtains from Eq. (

V.7.3
Self-adjoint extensions

As one of the basis of quantum mechanics, the self-adjoint character of an Hamiltonian is a key property for any realistic system and the notion of self-adjoint extension is essential in many situations
Bon01
;
Ara04
. In the presence of an inverse square potential, the singular character of the contact solutions at vanishing hyperradius makes this property not trivial
Ess06
and one has to refer to the theory of self-adjoint extensions of operators. For this purpose one considers the solutions of the hyper-radial equation

\left(\partial_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}-\frac{s^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\pm i\tau\right)F_{\pm}(\rho)=0

(129)

where
{\tau>0}
. The solutions are of the form

F_{\pm}(\rho)=\mathcal{A}K_{s}\left(e^{\mp\frac{\pi}{4}}\rho\sqrt{\tau}\right)+\mathcal{B}I_{s}\left(e^{\mp\frac{\pi}{4}}\rho\sqrt{\tau}\right).

(130)

The acceptable solutions are localized and thus must tends to zero for arbitrarily large hyperradius. This implies that
{\mathcal{B}=0}
in Eq. (
130
). The number of linearly independent solutions for the eigenvalue
{+i\tau}
gives the deficiency index
{n_{+}=1}
and for
{-i\tau}
, the deficiency index
{n_{-}=1}
. Thus one has
{n_{+}=n_{-}=1}
, and following the Weyl-von Neumann theorem
Bon01
, there is a one-parameter family of self-adjoint extensions for the contact Hamiltonian. This assertion may appear puzzling as the contact theory of
N
-body resonances is defined through the log-derivative condition in Eq. (
82
) with the three parameters
{\epsilon,R}
and
\theta
. The answer to this apparent paradox relies on the fact that the modified scalar product is also parameterized by the effective radius
R
and the parameter
\theta
. Hence, the way to understand this issue is that for a given metrics, defined by the parameters
{R}
and
{\theta}
, there exists a family of log-derivative conditions in Eq. (
82
) parameterized by only one parameter: the detuning
{\epsilon}
.

Considering the eigenstate
|\Psi(E)\rangle
, the self-adjoint character of the Hamiltonian is proven by showing that the difference

\Delta=\left(\Psi(E_{1})|H_{0}\Psi(E_{2})\right)_{0}-\left(H_{0}\Psi(E_{1})|\Psi(E_{2})\right)_{0}

(131)

is zero for arbitrary values of the energies
{E_{1},E_{2}}
. One finds

\Delta=\frac{\hbar^{2}R}{2m_{\rm r}}W\left[F(\rho,E_{1})^{*},F(\rho,E_{2}),\rho=R\right]\\
+\theta R^{2}(E_{2}-E_{1})F(\rho,E_{1})^{*}F(\rho,E_{2})

(132)

and
{\Delta=0}
thanks to Eq. (
82
).

V.7.4
Equivalence with the usual scalar product in the reference model

Let us consider the contact state
{|\Psi\rangle}
associated with the reference state
{|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle}
. The equivalence between the two scalar products is obtained by making the choice of the parameters in Eq. (
83
) which gives the norm

\left(\Psi|\Psi\right)_{0}=\int_{R_{\rm sep}}^{\infty}\rho|F(\rho)|^{2}d\rho+\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}|R_{\rm sep}F(R_{\rm sep})|^{2}.

(133)

One then recognizes in the right-hand side of this last equation the contribution of Eq. (
25
) so that one obtains

\left(\Psi|\Psi\right)_{0}=\langle\Psi^{\rm ref}|\Psi^{\rm ref}\rangle.

(134)

As shown in the subsection
V.7.2
, this last identity stays valid in the low energy limit at the order of the effective range approximation where the parameters
{(R,\theta)}
verify Eqs. (
84
,
85
). Moreover, two contact eigenstates of different energies being by construction orthogonal with respect to the modified scalar product, one has the equivalence

\left(\Psi_{1}|\Psi_{2}\right)_{0}=\langle\Psi_{1}^{\rm ref}|\Psi_{2}^{\rm ref}\rangle

(135)

where
{(|\Psi_{1}^{\rm ref}\rangle,|\Psi_{2}^{\rm ref}\rangle)}
are the reference states associated with the contact states
(|\Psi_{1}\rangle,|\Psi_{2}\rangle)
. In this equivalence, one has neglected the contributions where the wave function is not separable when
{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}
as in Eq. (
25
).

The norm of the contact state in Eq. (
133
) has been derived for
{R_{\rm sup}=\infty}
. When
{R_{\rm sup}}
is finite, contributions in the modified scalar product for
{\rho>R_{\rm sup}}
coincide with those in the usual scalar product so that, the equivalence between the two scalar products is still valid when
{R_{\rm sup}<\infty}
.

VI
Box model

To model in a simple way the effect of a finite value of
{R_{\rm sup}}
in absence of two-body shallow bound states (typically there is a 3D s wave resonance for part of the interacting pair of particles and
{R_{\rm sup}=|a_{\rm 3D}|}
with a finite but large and negative two-body scattering length
a_{\rm 3D}
), one can consider the picture of one particle in a
d
-dimensional box of hyperradius
{R_{\rm sup}}
with the condition
{F(\rho=R_{\rm sup},E)=0}
. This condition traduces the fact that for increasing values of
\rho
, starting from an hyperradius of the order
{R_{\rm sup}}
, the
N
-body state is no more separable and its component on the hyperspherical harmonic involved in the separability region is gradually depopulated.

The hyperradial function is then

F(\rho,E)={\mathcal{A}}\left[K_{s}(q\rho)-\frac{K_{s}(qR_{\rm sup})}{I_{s}(qR_{\rm sup})}I_{s}(q\rho)\right].

(136)

Using the log-derivative condition in Eq. (
13
) and taking into account that the second term in
{I_{s}(q\rho)}
in the right-hand side of Eq. (
136
) is small with respect to the term in
{K_{s}(q\rho)}
when
{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}
, one finds:

\frac{z\partial_{z}K_{s}(z)}{K_{s}(z)}\bigg|_{z=qR_{\rm sep}}=-s+\delta+\upsilon_{0}^{\prime}q^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2}-sX

(137)

with the small parameter

X=\frac{-K_{s}(qR_{\rm sup})}{qR_{\rm sep}K_{s}(qR_{\rm sep})K^{\prime}_{s}(qR_{\rm sep})I_{s}(qR_{\rm sup})}

(138)

A typical solution is given in Fig. (
3
) with
{s=1}
,
{\delta=-.01}
and
{\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}=.5}
.

, as a function of the inverse of the hyperradius

above which the bound state wave function is no more separable.

One obtains a result qualitatively similar to the behavior of one branch of an Efimov spectrum as a function of
{-1/a_{\rm 3D}>0}
with an endpoint at the three body continuum for a finite and negative value of the 3D scattering length
{a_{\rm 3D}}
.

VII
Square well model

In this section, one considers a simplified reference model of the
N
-body resonance which is separable for all values of the hyperradius. It illustrates the standard regime of resonance.

This model is defined by a square well potential at short distance without the effective centrifugal barrier in the range of the well :

V(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}&\text{for}\ \rho<R_{\rm sep}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}s^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}\rho^{2}}&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(139)

and the hyperradial function for an eigenstate of energy
E
verifies

\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}\left(\partial_{\rho}^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\right)+V(\rho)-E\right]F(\rho,E)=0

(140)

The bound state solution
{E=-\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}}
of Eq. (
140
) is

F(\rho,E)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\mathcal{A}_{\rm in}J_{0}(\kappa\rho)&\text{for}\,\rho<R_{\rm sep}\\
\mathcal{A}_{\rm out}K_{s}(q\rho)&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(141)

where
{\kappa=\sqrt{\kappa_{0}^{2}-q^{2}}}
. The continuity of the log-derivative at
{\rho=R_{\rm sep}}
gives with the notation of Eq. (
13
):

\frac{z\partial_{z}J_{0}(z)}{J_{0}(z)}\biggr|_{z=\kappa R_{\rm sep}}=-\upsilon(-q^{2}R_{\rm sep}^{2}).

(142)

At the
N
-body resonance the value of
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{0}^{\rm res}
is obtained from Eq. (
142
) for
{q=0}
and
{\upsilon_{0}=s}
giving the equation

z_{0}J_{1}(z_{0})=-sJ_{0}(z_{0})

(143)

with
{z_{0}=\kappa_{0}^{\rm res}R_{\rm sep}}
and from Eq. (
143
), one obtains

\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2z_{0}^{2}}.

(144)

Using the fact that the solutions of Eq. (
143
) are such that
{\kappa_{0}^{\rm res}R_{\rm sep}>2.4048\dots}
, one can verify that Eq. (
90
) is always verified and the resonance is in the standard regime for all possible values of
{s}
. By making the choice
{\theta=0}
, the effective radius at resonance is given by:

R=\left(s-\frac{s^{2}}{z_{0}^{2}}+\frac{s^{3}}{z_{0}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-2s}}R_{\rm sep}.

(145)

In Fig. (
4
), the solid black line is an example of a normalized radial bound state solution of Eq. (
140
) computed at the detuning
{\epsilon=-.01}
and for the index
{s=1.3}
. The contact solution is displayed in red in the region
{\rho>R}
and in green for the small hyperradius part
{\rho<R}
. The contact state which is not square integrable has been normalized by using the modified scalar product. The two functions almost coincide for
{\rho>R_{\rm sep}}
.

); red solid line: hyperradial contact function for

A similar analysis can be done if one includes the effective centrifugal barrier for
{\rho<R_{\rm sep}}
with the potential

V(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}}\left(\frac{s^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\kappa_{0}^{2}\right)&\text{for}\ \rho<R_{\rm sep}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}s^{2}}{2m_{\rm r}\rho^{2}}&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(146)

Then,
{F(\rho,E)=\mathcal{A}_{\rm in}J_{s}(\kappa\rho)}
for
{\rho<R_{\rm sep}}
. One finds
{\upsilon^{\prime}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}}
and
{R=\left(s\right)^{\frac{1}{2-2s}}R_{\rm sep}}
, and the system is again in the standard resonant regime for all values of
{s}
.

VIII
Conclusions

Various regimes of non efimovian
{N}
-body resonances near threshold have been explored, when a scale invariance can be identified. All the results are deduced from the properties of a potential with a repulsive tail identical to a kinetic barrier with an arbitrary strength. A contact model is then constructed to encompass all the possible situations. Three regimes emerge in the contact model: the single parameter regime which has been already introduced in Ref.
Wer06b
;
Wer08
when
{s<1}
, the standard resonant regime which has been studied by means of different methods in Refs.
Mat91
;
Kon17
;
Kon18
;
Son22
;
Pri23
, and the anomalous resonant regime. This last regime occurs for a large range parameter when
{s<1}
and can thus occur only in presence of two body s wave scattering resonances. The identification of possible long-lived quasi-bound states in this last regime for small but finite detuning, when
{\frac{1}{2}\lesssim s<1}
, is perhaps the most important physical result of this paper. A possible example for obtaining such values of the index
{s}
is given by the problem of two fermions of mass
{M}
interacting with an impurity of mass
{m}
and a mass ratio smaller than
{\frac{M}{m}<8.6185\dots}
as in Ref.
Nai22
. However, specific systems where the short range interactions are such that the range parameter is large, have not been identified yet.

The threshold laws for bound and quasi-bound states have been obtained when the hyperangle-hyperradius separability and thus the scale invariance is valid for arbitrary large scale. A box model has been introduced to have a semi-quantitative approach when in a 3D space, a two-body scattering length
{a_{\rm 3D}}
is large and negative (implying that the separability breaks down at a large hyperradius
{|a_{\rm 3D}|}
). Further analysis for finite values of
{a_{\rm 3D}}
are needed for a more quantitative approach, following for instance a method analogous to what was done in Ref.
Saf13
.

At the heart of the present paper, the scattering problem of potentials with a tail in an inverse square law appears in various contexts
Jean_College
. In the repulsive case, one thinks at first about the centrifugal barrier in the two-body problem which explains the Wigner’s law at threshold in a scattering process and which has been generalized in the generic regime case of Sec.
II.1.1
in Ref.
Mat91
. It is also at the source of beautiful models initiated by Ref.
Cal71
. The fascinating attractive case leads also to predictions in very different area of physics: the Efimov effect
Efi71
or the bound states of an electron with a polar molecule
Lev67
. This paper deals with the repulsive case where bound or quasi-bound states exist for sufficiently attractive potentials at short distance. In the zero-range model picture, the scale invariance linked to the potential with a pure inverse square law is broken due to the contact condition, giving thus another example of quantum anomaly
Coo02
;
Cam03
;
Ess06
.

The contact model is a self-adjoint extension of the
N
-body Laplacian in association with a modified scalar product that solves the problems of orthogonality and the normalization catastrophe. Beyond its application in the context of
N
-body resonances, this contact model is also interesting in itself as a new example of a way to model a contact interaction leading to non square integrable localized states and thus this enriches the variety of contact models usually considered
Gad21
.

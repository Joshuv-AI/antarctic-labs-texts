6

7

4

Theorem

(Strategy-Stealing)
.

The first player always has a drawing or winning strategy for
n^{d}
Tic-Tac-Toe.

The reasoning for this comes from the classical “Strategy Stealing” argument, due to Nash. Suppose the second player had a winning strategy. Then we can show that the first player can have a first move at random without decreasing the chance of the first player winning or drawing. After the first player having “given up” the first move, the second player becomes the effective “first player” and the first player can adopt whatever winning strategy the second player had to win the game. Since this implies the second player can never have a winning strategy, we have the result.

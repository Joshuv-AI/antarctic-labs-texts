2D Fermion on the Strip with Boundary Defects as a CFT with Excited Spin Fields
Riccardo Finotello, Igor Pesando

Abstract
We consider a two-dimensional fermion on the strip in the presence of an arbitrary number of zero-dimensional boundary changing defects. We show that the theory is still conformal with time dependent stress-energy tensor and that the allowed defects can be understood as excited spin fields. Finally we compute correlation functions involving these excited spin fields without using bosonization.

3
Conserved Product and Charges

In order to have a good quantum formulation, we define a procedure to
build a Fock space of states in the Heisenberg formalism thus equal time
anti-commutation relations must be invariant in time. We therefore need
a time independent internal product to extract the creation and annihilation
operators and expand the fields on the basis of modes.

3.1
Definition of the Conserved Product

Start from a generic conserved current

j(\uptau,\upsigma)=j_{\uptau}(\uptau,\upsigma)\differential{\uptau}+j_{\upsigma}(\uptau,\upsigma)\differential{\upsigma},

and consider

\star j=j_{\upsigma}\differential{\uptau}+j_{\uptau}\differential{\upsigma}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\differential{(\star j)}=\quantity( \partial_{\uptau} j_{\uptau} -
\partial_{\upsigma} j_{\upsigma} )\differential{\uptau}\differential{\upsigma},

where
\star
is the Hodge dual operator. Integrating the 2-form over a
surface
\Upsigma^{\prime}=\quantity[ \uptau_i, \uptau_f ]\times\quantity[ 0, \uppi]

yields:

\begin{split}\int\limits_{\Upsigma^{\prime}}\differential{(\star j)}=\int\limits_{\partial\Upsigma^{\prime}}\star j&=0\Leftrightarrow\\
\Leftrightarrow\int\limits_{0}^{\uppi}\differential{\upsigma}\quantity(
\eval{j_{\uptau}}_{\uptau= \uptau_f} - \eval{j_{\uptau}}_{\uptau=
\uptau_i} )&=\int\limits_{\uptau_{i}}^{\uptau_{f}}\differential{\uptau}\quantity(
\eval{j_{\upsigma}}_{\upsigma= \uppi} - \eval{j_{\upsigma}}_{\upsigma=
0} ).\end{split}

The current
j_{\uptau}(\uptau,\upsigma)
is thus conserved in time if

\int\limits_{\uptau_{i}}^{\uptau_{f}}\differential{\uptau}\quantity(
\eval{j_{\upsigma}}_{\upsigma= \uppi} - \eval{j_{\upsigma}}_{\upsigma=
0} )=0.

(3.1)

If this is the case, then we can say that

Q=\int\limits_{0}^{\uppi}\differential{\upsigma}j_{\uptau}(\uptau,\upsigma)

is conserved (that is
\partial_{\uptau}Q=0
).

is

for

Clearly this does not exclude the possibility to have singularities in

separately:
they are instead deeply
connected to the boundary changing primary operator hidden

singularities will be shown to be in correspondence to the excited spin fields.

Using the definition of the conserved product and defining the fields to
fulfill some basic requirements we therefore moved the focus from finding a
consistent definition of the Fock space to the construction of
the dual basis of modes.
This task is easier to address in a Euclidean
formulation and indeed this is the way we will pursue.

4
Point-like Defect CFT: the Euclidean Formulation

The main motivation behind the Euclidean reformulation of the previous
sections is the fact that the solution to the equations of motion on the
Euclidean strip (or in the complex plane) might be easier to study than its
Lorentzian worldsheet form. This is specifically the case when
\mathrm{R}_{\quantity( t )}\in\mathrm{U}(1)^{\mathrm{N}_{f}}\subset\mathrm{U}(\mathrm{N}_{f})
on which we shall focus in this paper. The
presence of a time dependent Hamiltonian is however not completely standard
and we can neither blindly apply the usual Wick rotation nor the usual CFT
techniques. We will then be a bit pedantic in order not to miss anything.

In the following two subsections we
focus on coordinate changes from the strip to the upper plane not relying on
the CFT properties since we have not shown that the theory is a CFT. We then
find the explicit expression of modes which satisfy the equations of motion and the boundary
conditions and compute the dual modes. Finally we show the algebra of the
creation and annihilation operators.
This step is conceptually separated from the definition of the Fock space
where this algebra is represented: we will in fact take care of it in the
following sections.

\displaystyle 0,\quad m\geq 1-\absolutevalue{n_{\bEs}},

which leads for the same argument to:

\ket{\mathrm{T}}\propto b^{*\,(\overline{\mathrm{E}})}_{0}b^{*\,(\overline{\mathrm{E}})}_{1}\dots b^{*\,(\overline{\mathrm{E}})}_{1-\absolutevalue{n_{\bEs}}}\ket{T_{\Es,\bEs}}.

(6.5)

In order to check the consistency of the definition, we require that:

\ket{T_{\Es,\bEs}}=\quantity( \uppi T \normfac^2 )^{n_{\mathrm{E}}}b^{(\mathrm{E})}_{n_{\mathrm{E}}}b^{(\mathrm{E})}_{n_{\mathrm{E}}-1}\dots b^{(\mathrm{E})}_{1}b^{*\,(\overline{\mathrm{E}})}_{0}b^{*\,(\overline{\mathrm{E}})}_{1}\dots b^{*\,(\overline{\mathrm{E}})}_{1-\absolutevalue{n_{\bEs}}}\ket{T_{\Es,\bEs}},

where the number of
b
operators has to match the number of
b^{*}

operators:

n_{\mathrm{E}}+n_{\overline{\mathrm{E}}}=\mathrm{E}+\overline{\mathrm{E}}=\mathrm{L}=0.

(6.6)

The same procedure applies also in the case
n_{\mathrm{E}}\leq 0
, leading to
the same result.

As a consequence of (
6.6
), we can
express the twisted vacuum as:

\displaystyle b^{(\mathrm{E})}_{n}\ket{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad n\geq 1+n_{\mathrm{E}},

\displaystyle b^{*\,(\overline{\mathrm{E}})}_{m}\ket{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad m\geq 1-n_{\mathrm{E}}.

6.3
Generic Case with Defects

Since the fields in presence of defects behave as NS fields in the
limit
z\to 0
, we can define the vacuum in the usual fashion
by requiring a finite limit

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\Uppsi(z){\ket{\Gexcvac}}

b_{n}{\ket{\Gexcvac}}=b^{*}_{n}{\ket{\Gexcvac}}=0,\quad n\geq 1.

(6.7)

7
Asymptotic Fields and Relation Between Asymptotic
Fields
Vacua and the Vacuum

In this section we define the asymptotic in-field and out-field
and discuss how their vacua are related to that of the theory with
defects.
The relation is “radial time dependent” thus
explicitly showing that an interaction is hidden in the defects.
In particular the vacuum for the theory with defects can be
identified with
{\mathrm{SL}_{2}\quantity( \mathds{R} )}
in-field vacuum while it is connected by a
Bogoliubov transformation to the
{\mathrm{SL}_{2}\quantity( \mathds{R} )}
out-field vacuum.

In the following we use the expansion of

P(z;\{x_{t},\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )}\})=\prod_{t=1}^{N}\quantity( 1- \frac{z}{x_t} )^{E_{t}},

around the origin and infinity with coefficients

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(
0,
\qty{ x_t , \E{t} }
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{k_{t}\}\in\mathds{N}^{N}}\prod\limits_{t=1}^{N}\quantity[
\binom{\E{t}}{k_t} \qty( - \frac{1}{x_t} )^{k_t}
]\quantity( - \frac{1}{x_t} )^{k_{t}}\updelta_{\sum\limits_{t=1}^{N}k_{t},k}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(
\infty,
\qty{ x_t , \E{t} }
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{k_{t}\}\in\mathds{N}^{N}}\prod\limits_{t=1}^{N}\quantity[
\binom{\E{t}}{k_t} \qty( - x_t )^{k_t - \E{t}}
]\quantity( - x_t )^{k_{t}-\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )}}\updelta_{\sum\limits_{t=1}^{N}k_{t},k},

so that we can write

\begin{split}P(z;\{x_{t},\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )}\})&\underset{\absolutevalue{z}<x_{N}}{=}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(
0,
\qty{ x_t , \E{t} }
)z^{k}\\
&\underset{\absolutevalue{z}>x_{1}}{=}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(
\infty,
\qty{ x_t , \E{t} }
)z^{-k+\sum\limits_{t=1}^{N}\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )}}.\end{split}

We do not discuss intermediate fields, i.e. expansions for

x_{t}<\absolutevalue{z}<x_{t-1}
, as it is not possible to clearly disentangle
the effects of defects before and after this range
since, as we will argue, the vacuum in presence of
defects is related to the radial ordering of the operators
associated with the defects as in (
8.3
).

7.1
Asymptotic in-field
and relation between its vacuum and generic case vacuum

Consider the definitions of the basis of solutions
(
5.7
) and
(
5.8
) and expand around

Let us concentrate on the first case since analogous relations can be
written for
b_{n}^{*\,(0)}
with the substitutions of
\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )}

\overline{\mathrm{E}}_{\quantity( t )}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(

\qty{ x_t , \E{t} }

(7.1)

and

as in (

modes of a untwisted fermion, i.e. a plain NS fermion.
The previous expansion connects the asymptotic

We can now relate the operators of the system with defects with

To this purpose we can then substitute the expansion

7.1

By writing

\begin{split}b_{n}&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(

The important point is that annihilation operators of the asymptotic

7.2
Relation between generic case vacuum and asymptotic

As done in the previous section we can
also explicitly compute the expansion

\displaystyle\underset{\absolutevalue{z}>x_{1}}{=}

which connects the asymptotic behavior of the modes of the

This relation can be used to link out-operators with the

\Uppsi(z)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathds{Z}}b_{n}\Uppsi_{n}(z)\underset{|z|>x_{1}}{=}\Uppsi^{(out)}(z)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathds{Z}}b_{n}^{(\infty)}\Uppsi_{n}^{(0)}(z)

b_{n}^{(\infty)}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}b_{n+\mathrm{M}-k}\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(
\infty,
\qty{ x_t , \E{t} }
).

(7.2)

These expressions can also be inverted as

\begin{split}b_{n}&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}_{k}\quantity(
\infty,
\qty{ x_t , -\E{t} }

As we will show later, we must take
\mathrm{M}=0
.
Then the important point is that annihilation operators of the asymptotic
theory, i.e. operators with positive index,
are expressed using both annihilation and creator operators of the theory
with defects while creators, i.e. operators with non negative
index,
are expressed connected with creators only.
It follows from the vacuum definition that

\begin{split}&\quantity( \cmode{0}{\infty}{-\E{t}}{x_t} b^{( \infty)}_{1 }
+ \mbox{creators} ){\ket{\Gexcvac}}=0\\
&\quantity( \cmode{0}{\infty}{-\E{t}}{x_t} b^{( \infty)}_{2 }
+ \cmode{1}{\infty}{-\E{t}}{x_t} b^{( \infty)}_{1 }
+ \mbox{creators} ){\ket{\Gexcvac}}=0\\
&\vdots\end{split},

This means that the vacuum for the asymptotic out-field is non

More explicitly we get the relation

{\ket{\Gexcvac}}={\cal N}_{(out)}(\{x_{t},\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )},\overline{\mathrm{E}}_{\quantity( t )}\})e^{\sum_{m,n\leq 0}{\cal M}_{mn}^{(out)}(\{x_{t},\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )},\overline{\mathrm{E}}_{\quantity( t )}\})b^{(\infty)*}_{m}b^{(\infty)}_{n}}\ket{0_{(out)}}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}\quantity( \mathds{R} )},

so that the two

More precisely we get (see appendix
A

for details)

\displaystyle\quantity{
\Uppsi^{(out, +)}( z )
+
\oint_{|z|,|w|> x_1} \frac{d w}{ 2\uppi i}
\frac{ P(z; \{x_t, \E t\} ) P(w; \{x_t, -\E t\} ) -1
}{
z-w}
\Uppsi^{(out, -)}( w )

(7.3)

and the corresponding equation for

The previous equation can be solved explicitly by

\begin{split}{\ket{\Gexcvac}}=&{\cal N}(\{x_{t},\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )},\overline{\mathrm{E}}_{\quantity( t )}\})\\

In the previous equation there is no need to

Finally deriving the same expression using

8
Contractions and Stress-Energy Tensor

Given the definitions of the in-vacuum of the theory and the algebra of
operators, we can finally define the normal ordering operation and
proceed to compute the contractions and OPEs of the operators: the procedure
ultimately leads to the definition of the stress-energy tensor. This is enough
to show that the theory is a time dependent CFT since the stress-energy tensor
satisfies the canonical OPE.

8.1
NS Complex Fermion

First of all we deal with the simple case of NS fermions and using the
algebra (
5.1
) we compute the OPE of fermion
fields

\Uppsi^{i}(z)\Uppsi^{*}_{j}(w)=\;:\Uppsi^{i}(z)\Uppsi^{*}_{j}(z):+\frac{1}{\uppi T}\frac{\updelta^{i}_{j}}{z-w},\quad\absolutevalue{w}<\absolutevalue{z},

where the operation
\;:\cdot:
is the normal ordering with
respect to the
{\mathrm{SL}_{2}\quantity( \mathds{R} )}
vacuum defined in (
6.1
) .

Secondly we get to the expression of the stress-energy tensor:

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(z)&=\lim\limits_{w\to z}\quantity[
-\frac{ \uppi T }{2}
\qty( \Uppsi^*_i( z ) \partial_w \Uppsi^i( w ) - \partial_z \Uppsi^*_i(
z ) \Uppsi^i( w ) )
+ \frac{\Nf}{ ( z - w )^2 } ]\\
&=-\frac{\uppi T}{2}\;:\Uppsi^{*}_{i}(z)\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial_{z}}\Uppsi^{i}(z):\end{split}

so we are now able to derive the necessary minimal subtraction

\mathfrak{h}(z-w)=\frac{\mathrm{N}_{f}}{(z-w)^{2}},

to get the stress-energy tensor.

8.2
Twisted Fermion

We can now go back to
\mathrm{N}_{f}=1
theories. First of all we consider the
simplest case of the usual twisted fermion with the mode expansion
(
5.5
) and
(
5.6
).
We do not implement beforehand the constraint
(
6.6
) but we recover it in a
different way.
Both excited and twisted vacua can be treated on the same footing
since the their difference amount to choose
n_{\mathrm{E}}
and
n_{\overline{\mathrm{E}}}
.

8.2.1
OPE and Stress-Energy Tensor

Using the anti-commutation relations
(
5.4
) we can compute the OPE

\Uppsi(z)\Uppsi^{*}(w)=\;N_{E,\bar{E}}\left[\Uppsi(z)\Uppsi^{*}(w)\right]+\frac{1}{\uppi T}\quantity( \frac{z}{w} )^{\mathrm{E}}\frac{1}{z-w},\quad\absolutevalue{w}<\absolutevalue{z},

and

\Uppsi^{*}(w)\Uppsi(z)=\;N_{E,\bar{E}}\left[\Uppsi^{*}(w)\Uppsi(z)\right]+\frac{1}{\uppi T}\quantity( \frac{w}{z} )^{\overline{\mathrm{E}}}\frac{1}{w-z},\quad\absolutevalue{w}>\absolutevalue{z}.

If we require that the previous results can be assembled in a
well defined continuous radial ordering

R\quantity[ \Uppsi( z ) \Uppsi^*( w ) ]
we need to set
\mathrm{E}=-\overline{\mathrm{E}}
so we can write

R\quantity[ \Uppsi( z ) \Uppsi^*( w ) ]=\;N_{E,\bar{E}}\left[\Uppsi(z)\Uppsi^{*}(w)\right]+\frac{1}{\uppi T}\quantity( \frac{z}{w} )^{\mathrm{E}}\frac{1}{z-w}.

The same result can be reached by computing the
stress-energy tensor starting from the previous
expressions. We have two ways to construct it depending on the
ordering of the classical expression.
Either as

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(z)&=\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\to z\\
\absolutevalue{w}<\absolutevalue{z}\end{subarray}}\quantity[
-\frac{\uppi T}{2} \qty(
\Uppsi^*( z ) \partial_w \Uppsi( w )
- \partial_z \Uppsi^*( z ) \Uppsi( w ) )
+ \frac{1}{( z- w )^2} ]\\
&=-\frac{\uppi T}{2}\;:\Uppsi^{*}(z)\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial_{z}}\Uppsi(z):+\frac{\mathrm{E}^{2}}{2z^{2}},\end{split}

(8.1)

or

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(z)&=\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\to z\\
\absolutevalue{w}<\absolutevalue{z}\end{subarray}}\quantity[ -\frac{\uppi T}{2}
\qty(
- \partial_z \Uppsi( z ) \Uppsi^*( w )
+\Uppsi( z ) \partial_w \Uppsi^*( w )
)
+ \frac{1}{( z - w )^2} ]\\
&=-\frac{\uppi T}{2}\;:\Uppsi^{*}(z)\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial_{z}}\Uppsi(z):+\frac{\overline{\mathrm{E}}^{2}}{2z^{2}},\end{split}

which however must coincide for consistency. Since

\;:\Uppsi(z)\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial_{z}}\Uppsi^{*}(z):=\;:\Uppsi^{*}(z)\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial_{z}}\Uppsi(z):\,,

then we must then require

\mathrm{E}^{2}=\overline{\mathrm{E}}^{2}
.

We can get a stronger constraint by computing the OPE

\mathcal{T}(z)\mathcal{T}(w)
.
In fact the cancellation of the cubic divergence requires
\mathrm{E}+\overline{\mathrm{E}}=0
.

It the follows that the vacuum
\ket{T_{\Es,\bEs}}
is actually

\ket{T_{\Es}}
, notation we will use from now on.

8.2.2
Virasoro Operators and Conformal Dimensions

From the usual definition of the

Looking back at the analysis of the excited and twisted vacua, we already

{\mathrm{SL}_{2}\quantity( \mathds{R} )}
invariant.
In particular we can see that that the excited vacua

We can further check this idea

Spin Field Correlators

The definitions of the in- and out-vacua and the stress-energy tensor are
critical to compute any correlation function of operators in the presence of
the point-like defects. In fact we need to know both the algebra of the
operators and their representation, usually defined on the in-vacuum (the ket
vector), as well as their Hermitian conjugation in order to build the action
of the operators on the out-vacuum (the bra vector).

Starting from (
8.3
)

\innerproduct{\GGexcvac}{\GGexcvac}={\cal N}(\{x_{t},\mathrm{E}_{\quantity( t )}\})\left\langle R\quantity[
\prodt\spin{\E{t}}{x_t}

At first sight this expression might look incorrect since both

{\ket{\GGexcvac}}
and
{\bra{\GGexcvac}}
seem to contain

R\quantity[ \prodt\spin{\E{t}}{x_t} ]
as if were squaring
the previous radial ordering.
That it is not the case and it can be seen in different ways.
The simplest is to realize that such a square would be
divergent while the product seems to be perfectly finite.
A more sophisticated and rigorous way is to consider what the
previous product is from the point of view of asymptotic out field.
In this case

{\ket{\GGexcvac}}={\cal N}_{(out)}~R\quantity[ \prodt\spin{\E{t}}{x_t} ]\ket{ 0_{(out)}}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}\quantity( \mathds{R} )}

and
{\bra{\GGexcvac}}={}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}\quantity( \mathds{R} )}\bra{ 0_{(out)}}

{\cal N}_{(out)}={\cal N}
.
Moreover

when the two
energy momentum tensors are normal ordered with respect to their
different sets of operators which are related as in (
7.2

Hence all the expressions are surely valid for
\absolutevalue{z}>x_{1}
and can
be analytically extended to the whole plane.
The same result can be obtained from the point of view of asymptotic
in-fields.

Unfortunately it is not completely clear how to fix the
normalization.
Moreover the result depends on the normalization chosen for the
single spin field and this normalization shows only up when we
relate the
N
points correlators to
N-1
points ones and these
recursively down to two points correlators.
Therefore we need to consider quantities where the
normalization cancels.
In particular we can consider

\begin{split}&\partialderivative{x_{\myoverline{t}}}\ln\left\langle R\quantity[
\spin{\E{\myoverline{t}}}{x_{\myoverline{t}}}
\prod\limits_{u = 1,

since

for a quasi-primary operator

From the definition of

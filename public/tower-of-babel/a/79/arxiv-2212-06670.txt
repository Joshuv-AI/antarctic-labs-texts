On the dimension of some union of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties
Arghya Sadhukhan

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider certain union X ⁡ ( μ , b ) X(\mu,b) of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties in the affine flag variety that arises in the study of mod- p p reduction of Rapoport-Zink spaces and moduli spaces of shtukas associated to a connected reductive group. Under a mild hypothesis on μ \mu , but no further restrictions on the group, we compute its dimension in the case where b b is the maximal neutrally acceptable element.

20G25,11G25,20F55

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

A central tool in understanding the reduction modulo
p
of Shimura varieties, and therefore in the study of arithmetic properties encoded in them, is the Newton stratification. Roughly speaking, at least for the Shimura varieties of Hodge type that admits a moduli interpretation, this stratification on the special fiber (of a suitable integral model) is defined by the loci where the
p
-divisible groups attached to the corresponding abelian varieties have fixed isogeny type. Investigation into the unique closed Newton stratum, the so-called basic locus, in the last decades has found fruitful applications in the realm of the Langlands program. We mention the work toward Zhang’s arithmetic fundamental lemma
[
RTZ13
]
, as well as the theory of non-archimedean uniformization of Shimura varieties, first pioneered by Cerednik and Drinfeld, and further developed by Rapoport–Zink
[
RZ96
]
, Fargues
[
Far04
]
, and Howard–Pappas
[
HP17
]
.

The natural range of the index set for the Newton stratification corresponding to a Shimura variety associated to the group-theoretic data
(\mathbf{G},\{\mu\})
is the set
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
of neutral acceptable elements. Using the classification of the Kottwitz set obtained in
[
Kot85
]
and
[
Kot97
]
,
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
can be viewed as a subset of the dominant rational (relative) Weyl chamber.
Thus it becomes a partially ordered set with respect to the usual dominance relation. It is easy to see from the definition that
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
has a unique minimal element; the basic locus corresponds to this minimal element. A nontrivial result due to He and Nie
[
HN18
]
asserts that there is a unique maximal element in
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
; to give an example, when the
\mu
-ordinary locus is nonempty, it is the unique maximal Newton stratum, and then the maximal element of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
is
\mu^{\diamond}
, the twisted Galois-average (see
section

2.4.3
) of a dominant representative in
\{\mu\}
. However, the description of this maximal element is rather mysterious in general.

The primary goal of this article is to investigate the dimension of a certain union of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties associated to this maximal element. Such a geometric object is the group-theoretic model for the maximal Newton stratum. We remark that in the situations where the He-Rapoport axioms in
[
HR17
]
hold, this sought-after dimension is equal to the dimension of the maximal Newton stratum minus the dimension of the corresponding central leaf, see
[
He16
, §2.12]
. The first quantity is
\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle
, where
2\rho
is the sum of the (relative) positive roots, so in that case, we essentially want to compute the latter dimension. The said (union of) affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties can be defined purely in terms of group-theoretic data without any reference to Shimura varieties, which we briefly do in the following subsection. In the remainder of the article, we resort to this latter setup.

1.2.
Main theorem

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over a non-archimedean local field
F
. Denote by
\sigma
the Frobenius of
\breve{F}
over
F
, where
\breve{F}
is the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
. We choose a
\sigma
-invariant Iwahori subgroup
\breve{I}
of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. Fix a geometric
conjugacy class
\{\mu\}
of cocharacters of
\mathbf{G}
over the separable closure
\bar{F}
and choose a dominant representative
\mu
in it. We denote
by
\Adm(\mu)
the
\mu
-admissible set, a finite subset of the Iwahori-Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
, see
section

2
for details. Then for an element
b\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
, we define following Rapoport
[
Rap05
]
the union of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

X(\mu,b)=\{g\breve{}\mathcal{I}\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})/\breve{}\mathcal{I};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}\Adm(\mu)\breve{}\mathcal{I}\}.

This is the set of geometric points of a subscheme, locally of finite type, of the affine flag variety attached to
\breve{F}
(in the usual
sense in equal characteristic; in the sense of Zhu
[
Zhu17
]
and Bhatt-Scholze
[
BS17
]
in mixed characteristics). The interest in such a set comes from the fact that when
F
is
p
-adic and
\mu
is minuscule, sets of this form arise as the set of geometric points of the underlying reduced set of a
Rapoport-Zink formal moduli space of
p
-divisible groups, see
[
RV14
]
. Something analogous holds
in the function field case for formal moduli spaces of shtukas, where the
minuscule hypothesis can be dropped, cf.
[
Vie18
]
. In either case, by He’s work on the Kottwitz-Rapoport conjecture in
[
He16a
]
, the set
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
can be characterized as the set of those
\sigma
-conjugacy classes
[b]
such that
X(\mu,b)\neq\emptyset
. Our main result is then the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the image
\underline{\mu}_{\text{ad}}
of
\mu
in
X_{*}(T_{\text{ad}})_{\Gamma_{0}}
has depth at least
2
in every
\bar{F}
-simple factor of
\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}}
. Let
b=b_{\max}
be the maximal element of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
. Then

(1.1)

\dim X(\mu,b)=\text{rk}_{F}^{\text{ss}}\mathbf{G}^{*}-\text{rk}_{F}^{\text{ss}}\mathbf{G},

where
\mathbf{G}^{*}
is the unique (up to isomorphism) quasi-split inner form of
\mathbf{G}
, and
\text{rk}_{F}^{\text{ss}}
stands for the semisimple
F
-rank.

Here
depth
of a dominant cocharacter is an estimate of its distance from the walls of the dominant Weyl chamber, see
section

2.3
for a precise definition. We remark that, in particular,
\mu
cannot be minuscule because of this restriction on its depth. Hence, by way of application in arithmetic geometry, this result is only of interest in the function field setup.

Let us point out that the quantity on the RHS of
eq.

1.1
is a non-negative integer since every torus in
\mathbf{G}
transfers to
\mathbf{G}^{*}
, cf.
[
Lan89
, lemma 2.1]
, and it is zero precisely when
\mathbf{G}
is quasi-split. On the other hand, by applying Chai’s description of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
in
[
Cha00
, §7.1]
,
[
HN18
, lemma 2.5]
, we can see that the maximal element
b_{\max}
has Newton point
\mu^{\diamond}
exactly when
\mathbf{G}
is quasi-split; also cf.
[
GHN20
, proposition 5.5]
for a different argument. As a result, the dimension on the LHS above can be directly checked to be zero in the quasi-split case. Hence,
eq.

1.1
indeed recovers this trivial case (which is valid even in the absence of the depth hypothesis), and as such this theorem also serves as a geometric way to measure how far
\mathbf{G}
is from being quasi-split.

1.3.
Context of the result and outline of the proof

Let us put
theorem

1.1
into context. For a quasi-split group
\mathbf{G}
, a dimension formula on
X(\mu,b)
for general
b
was first obtained by He and Yu
[
HY21
]
under a
superregularity hypothesis on the depth of
\mu
. In
[
Sad21
, theorem C]
, the same formula was shown to be valid under the weaker assumption that
\mu
is regular, i.e.,
\text{depth}(\mu)\geq 1
. Let us now place ourselves in the context of a general group
\mathbf{G}
, and let
b_{\min}
be the unique minimal (equivalently, basic) element of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
. Then the work of Görtz, He and Rapoport in
[
GHR22
]
gives characterizations of when
X(\mu,b_{\min})
can be of minimal dimension zero, or of maximal dimension
\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle
; the model cases of such phenomena are the Lubin-Tate case and the Drinfeld case, respectively. Let us also remark that in
[
GHN20
]
, Görtz, He and Nie provide a lower bound for the dimension of
X(\mu,b_{\min})
, and they are also able to classify completely when this becomes an equality. Besides these results, as far as the author is aware, there is no dimension formula available for
X(\mu,b)
, nor is there any conjectural description. Standing at this juncture, it is therefore natural to investigate the dimension problem for the other extremal element of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
, namely
b_{\max}
.

It is worth pointing out that our dimension formula can also be obtained more directly, using explicit knowledge of the element
b_{\max}
as discussed in
section

5
. Our method, on the other hand, has the merit of producing all the elements, at least conjecturally, in the Iwahori-Weyl group whose associated Iwahori cells contribute to the maximal Newton stratum; such description would be useful to analyze the (top dimensional) irreducible components of
X(\mu,b_{\max})
in the non quasi-split setup.

We now briefly sketch the strategy of the proof. We first identify
b_{\max}
as the maximum of generic
\sigma
-conjugacy classes associated with the maximal translation elements in the
\mu
-admissible set. Then we proceed to express such generic
\sigma
-conjugacy classes in two distinct ways, first in terms of
\sigma
-twisted Demazure power and then via a reduction to the quasi-split case. Such considerations allow us to express the dimension of individual affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties associated to such generic
\sigma
-conjugacy classes in terms of certain statistics on the quantum Bruhat graph introduced by Brenti, Fomin and Postnikov
[
BFP99
]
. Further analysis in
section

3.5
shows that
\dim X(\mu,b_{\max})
lies between the length and the reflection length of certain minimal length elements of some Frobenius-twisted conjugacy classes in the finite Weyl group. We then show that such minimal length elements are, in fact, partial Coxeter elements via a case-by-case calculation in
section

4
, and hence their length (which is equal to their reflection length) matches the asserted dimension in
theorem

1.1
, thereby finishing the proof.

Let

Let

Note that if

is not quasi-split over

, then there does not exist a

2.2.
Quantum Bruhat graphs

We recall the quantum Bruhat graph introduced by Brenti, Fomin, and Postnikov in the context of quantum cohomology ring of complex flag variety, see
[
BFP99
]
, also
[
FGP97
]
. By definition, a
quantum Bruhat graph

\Gamma_{\Phi}
is a directed graph with

•

vertices given by the elements of
W
;

•

upward edges
w\rightharpoonup ws_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
with
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)+1
;

•

downward edges
w\rightharpoondown ws_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
with
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1
.

The
weight of a path
in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
is defined to be the sum of weights of the edges in the path. For any
x,y\in W
, we denote by
d(x,y)
the minimal length among all paths in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
. Any path between
x
and
y
affording
d(x,y)
as its length is called a
shortest path
between them. Then the following lemma defines a function
\text{wt}:W\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^{\vee}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Pos05
, Lemma 1]
,
[
BFP99
, Lemma 6.7]

Let
x,y\in W
.

(1)

There exists a directed path (consisting of possibly both upward and downward edges) in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
.

(2)

Any two shortest paths in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
have the same weight, which we denote by
\text{wt}(x,y)
.

(3)

Any path in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
has weight
\geqslant\text{wt}(x,y)
.

We need the following results, taken from
[
HY21
, §4.2]
and
[
HN21
, §2.5]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
x,y\in W
. Then

(1)

We have
\langle\text{wt}(a,b),\alpha\rangle\leq 2
, for any simple root
\alpha
.

(2)

\ell(y)-\ell(x)=d(x,y)-\langle 2\rho,\text{wt}(x,y)\rangle
.

2.3.
Demazure product

We follow
[
He09
, §1]
. For any
x,y\in\widetilde{W}
, the subset
\{xy^{\prime};y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}
(resp.
\{x^{\prime}y;x^{\prime}\leqslant x\}
,
\{x^{\prime}y^{\prime};x^{\prime}\leqslant x,y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}
) of
\widetilde{W}
contains a unique maximal element with respect to the Bruhat order. Moreover, we have

\max\{xy^{\prime};y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}=\max\{x^{\prime}y;x^{\prime}\leqslant x\}=\max\{x^{\prime}y^{\prime};x^{\prime}\leqslant x,y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}.

We denote this element by
x\ast y
and we call it the
Demazure product
of
x
and
y
. Then
(\widetilde{W},\ast)
is a monoid and the Demazure product is determined by the following two rules

•

x\ast y=xy
if
x,y\in\widetilde{W}
such that
\ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)
;

•

s\ast w=w
if
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
,
w\in\widetilde{W}
such that
sw<w
.

The Demazure product occurs naturally in the convolution product of Iwahori cells in the sense that for any two elements
x,y\in\widetilde{W}
, we have
\breve{I}\dot{x}\breve{I}\ast\breve{I}\dot{y}\breve{I}=\breve{I}(\dot{x}\ast\dot{y})\breve{I}
. By such connection, it has also found applications in the study of generic Newton points, cf.

.

An interesting connection between the Demazure product on
\widetilde{W}
and the quantum Bruhat graph of
\Phi_{W}
is established in
[
HN21
]
. For any dominant
\underline{\lambda}\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}
, we define the depth
of

to be
\text{depth}(\underline{\lambda})=\min\{\langle\alpha,\underline{\lambda}\rangle:\alpha\in\Delta\}

Theorem 2.3
.

[
HN21

Let

The

2.4.2.
The Newton poset

Given
w\in\widetilde{W}
, define

B(\mathbf{G})_{w}:=\{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G}):[b]\cap\breve{I}w\breve{I}\neq\emptyset\}.

It is easy to see that
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
has an unique maximal element
b_{w}
, which coincides with the generic
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
\breve{I}w\breve{I}
. In the case of a split group, an explicit formula for the Newton point of this maximal element was first obtained in
[
Mil21
]
under certain superregularity hypothesis on the dominant cocharacter associated with
w
. We need the following strengthened formula for the elements in shrunken chambers in the context of quasi-split groups, obtained in
[
HN21
]
. We will write
\nu_{w}
to denote
\nu(b_{w})
.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
HN21
, proposition 3.1]

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is quasi-split and adjoint over
F
. Let
x,y\in W
and
\underline{\mu}\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}^{+}
be such that
\text{depth}(\underline{\mu})\geq 2
. Then
\nu_{xt^{\underline{\mu}}y}
is the average of
\sigma
-orbit of
\underline{\mu}-\text{wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))
.

2.4.3.
Neutrally acceptable elements

Let
\{\mu\}
be a conjugacy class of cocharacters over
\bar{F}
. Choose
\mu
be a dominant representative of
\{\mu\}
and denote by
\underline{\mu}
its image in
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}
. We define the
\mu
-admissible set

\Adm(\mu)=\{w\in\widetilde{W};\ w\leqslant t^{x(\underline{\mu})}\text{ for some }x\in W\}.

We also have the set of
neutrally acceptable elements

B(\mathbf{G},\mu)=\{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})\mid\kappa([b])=\mu^{\natural},\nu([b])\leq\mu^{\diamond}\}.

Here
\mu^{\natural}
denotes the common image of
\mu\in\{\mu\}
in
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})_{\sigma}
, and
\mu^{\diamond}
denotes the average of the
\sigma_{0}
-orbit of
\underline{\mu}
. Here
\sigma_{0}
denotes the
L
-action of
\sigma
, see
[
GHN20
, definition 2.1]
for details. The set
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
inherits a partial order from
B(\mathbf{G})
. Since the Kottwitz map
\kappa
is constant on
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
, we may view it as a subset of
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Q}}
via the
Newton map. The following description of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
is obtained in
[
HN18
, theorem 1.1 & lemma 2.5]
. To state the result, for each
\sigma_{0}
orbit
\mathcal{O}
on
\mathbb{S}
, we set
\varpi_{\mathcal{O}}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\varpi_{i}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

[
HN18
]

(1) Let
v\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Q}}
. Then
v\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
if and only if
\sigma_{0}(v)=v
is dominant, and for any
\sigma_{0}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
on
\mathbb{S}
with
\langle v,\alpha_{i}\rangle\neq 0
for each (or equivalently, some)
i\in\mathcal{O}
, we have that
\langle\mu+\sigma(0)-v,\varpi_{\mathcal{O}}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\langle\mu-v,\varpi_{\mathcal{O}}\rangle\geq 0
.

(2) The set
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
contains a unique maximal element.

2.5.
Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

The
affine Deligne-Lusztig variety
associated to
w\in\tilde{W}
and
b\in\breve{G}
is a locally closed subscheme of the affine flag variety of
\bf G
, locally of finite type over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and of finite dimension, with the set of geometric points given by

X_{w}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})=\{g\breve{I}\in\breve{G}/\breve{I}\mid g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{I}w\breve{I}\}.

If
F
is of equal characteristic, then by affine flag variety we mean the “usual” affine flag variety; in the case of mixed characteristic, this notion should be understood in the sense of perfect schemes, as developed by Zhu
[
Zhu17
]
and by Bhatt and Scholze
[
BS17
]
.

We now define

X(\mu,b)=\bigcup\limits_{w\in\Adm(\mu)}X_{w}(b).

Settling the Kottwitz-Rapoport conjecture made in
[
KR03
]
and
[
Rap05
]
about the non-emptiness pattern for
X(\mu,b)
, He proves the following result in
[
He16
]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
He16a
, theorem A]

X(\mu,b)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
.

Note that by
theorem

2.8
, we have

B(\mathbf{G},\mu)=\bigcup\limits_{w\in\Adm(\mu)}B(\mathbf{G})_{w},

hence
b_{\max}=\max\limits_{w\in\Adm(\mu)}b_{w}
. Since
w[b_{w}]
is an increasing function
\widetilde{W}\rightarrow X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Q}}
, we conclude that

b_{\max}=\max\limits_{x\in W}b_{t^{x(\mu)}}.

3.
Explicating
b_{\max}
via generic Newton point

3.1.
Dimension of a generic ADLV

Following
[
He21
]
, we define the
n
-th
\sigma
-twisted Demazure power of
w
by setting

w^{\sigma,n}=w*\sigma(w)*\sigma^{2}(w)*\cdots*\sigma^{n-1}(w).

We need the following result, which on the one hand describes the dimension of a single affine Deligne-Lusztig variety associated with generic
\sigma
-conjugacy class, and on the other hand quantifies such generic
\sigma
-conjugacy class in terms of
\sigma
-twisted Demazure power. For the first equality below, see
[
He16
, theorem 2.23]
and
[
MV20
, lemma 3.2]
, whereas for the second equality, see
[
He21
, theorem 0.1]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{W}
. Then
\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=\ell(w)-\langle 2\rho,\nu_{w}\rangle=\ell(w)-\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ell(w^{*_{\sigma},n})}{n}
.

Note that there can be elements
w\in\widetilde{W}
that are not pure translations but still satisfy
b_{w}=b_{\max}
; this can be seen e.g. using the Deligne-Lusztig reduction method, see
[
He14
, proposition 4.2]
. However, if
w
contributes to top dimensional components - i.e.,
\dim X(\mu,b_{\max})=\dim X_{w}(b_{\max})
- then
w
is necessarily a translation element of the form
t^{x\underline{\mu}}
for some
x\in W
, by the first equality in
theorem

3.1
.

Now, again by an application of the first equality in
theorem

3.1
we see that
\dim X_{t^{x\underline{\mu}}}(b_{t^{x\underline{\mu}}})
is a decreasing function of
b_{t^{x\underline{\mu}}}
; therefore, finding top dimensional
X_{w}(b_{\max})
inside
X(\mu,b_{\max})
boils down to understanding elements
x\in W
such that
\dim X_{t^{x\underline{\mu}}}(b_{t^{x\underline{\mu}}})
is minimized, and this minimum value of the dimension is indeed the dimension of
X(\mu,b_{\max})
. We approach the problem of computing
\dim X_{t^{x\underline{\mu}}}(b_{t^{x\underline{\mu}}})
below in two different ways. In the rest of the paper, we omit the underline for simplicity and write
\mu
instead of
\underline{\mu}
throughout.

3.2.
A standard reduction

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over
F
, and let
\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}}

be its adjoint group. Let
T_{\text{ad}}
be the image of
T
in
\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}}
, and denote by
\mu_{\text{ad}}
the image of
\mu
in
X_{*}(T_{\text{ad}})_{\Gamma_{0}}
. Similarly
for any
b\in\breve{G}
, we denote by
b_{\text{ad}}
its image in
\breve{G}_{\text{ad}}
. By
[
Kot97
, proposition 4.10]
, we then have an isomorphism of posets

B(\mathbf{G},\mu)\rightarrow B(\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}},\mu_{\text{ad}}),\text{~via~}[b][b_{\text{ad}}].

Next, we have a decomposition

\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}}\simeq\mathbf{G}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{G}_{r}
,
where each
\mathbf{G}_{i}

is adjoint and simple over F. Write
\mu=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r})
, where
\mu_{i}

is a dominant coweight of
\mathbf{G}_{i}
.
This way we can identify

B(\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}},\mu)=\prod B(\mathbf{G}_{i},\mu_{i}).

We also have

\dim X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)=\dim X^{\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}}}(\mu_{\text{ad}},b_{\text{ad}})=\sum\dim X^{\mathbf{G}_{i}}(\mu_{\text{ad},i},b_{\text{ad},i}).

Hence, it suffices to focus on adjoint
F
-simple groups. We work with the absolute
local Dynkin diagram (i.e., the affine Dynkin diagram attached to
\mathbf{G}
over
\breve{F}
), together with the diagram automorphism induced by Frobenius.

We can describe such groups in terms of tuples
(\widetilde{W},\sigma)
, where
\widetilde{W}
is the associated Iwahori-Weyl group with its finite root datum being irreducible of adjoint type, and
\sigma=\text{Ad}(\tau\sigma_{0})
is a length preserving automorphism of
\widetilde{W}
with
\tau\in\Omega,\sigma_{0}(\mathbb{S})=\mathbb{S}
. Thus
\text{Ad}\tau
(resp.
\text{Ad}\sigma_{0}
) corresponds to a symmetry of the affine (resp. finite) Dynkin diagram. More concretely,
\tau
can be taken to be
\tau_{i}=t^{\varpi_{i}^{\vee}}w_{i,0}w_{0}
whenever
\varpi_{i}^{\vee}
is a minuscule fundamental coweight, and
\sigma_{0}
can be nontrivial only when
W
is of type
A_{n},D_{n}(n\geq 5)
and
E_{6}
. Throughout
section

3
and
section

5
, we denote by
\zeta
the finite Weyl group part of
\tau
, i.e.
\zeta=\zeta_{i}:=w_{i,0}w_{0}
for some minuscule fundamental coweight
\varpi_{i}^{\vee}
- often suppressing the label
i
as well. We henceforth assume that
\text{depth}(\mu)\geq 2
.

3.3.
via Demazure power

Here we express
\langle 2\rho,\nu_{t^{x(\mu)}}\rangle
explicitly using Demazure power of
t^{x(\mu)}
.

3.3.1.

To ease notational burden, we separately discuss the case with trivial
\sigma_{0}
first. Suppose that
o(\zeta)=m
. Then we have

\displaystyle(t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},m+1}

\displaystyle=(xt^{\mu}x^{-1})*(\zeta xt^{\mu}x^{-1}\zeta^{-1})*\cdots*(\zeta^{m-1}xt^{\mu}x^{-1}\zeta^{1-m})*(xt^{\mu}x^{-1})

\displaystyle=xt^{\mu_{1}}x^{-1},\text{~with~}\mu_{1}:=(m+1)\mu-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}\text{wt}(\zeta^{i}x,\zeta^{i+1}x).

Here the second equality follows from a repeated application of
theorem

2.3
. It is now easy to see that for any positive integer
k
, we have

(t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},km+1}=xt^{\mu_{k}}x^{-1},\text{~with~}\mu_{k}:=(km+1)\mu-k\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m-1}\text{wt}(\zeta^{j}x,\zeta^{j+1}x).

Note that
\mu_{k}
is dominant under the depth hypothesis on
\mu
and we have
\frac{1}{km+1}\ell((t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},km+1})=\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle-\frac{k}{km+1}\langle 2\rho,\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m-1}\text{wt}(\zeta^{j}x,\zeta^{j+1}x)\rangle
. Since the limit in
theorem

3.1
exists, we deduce

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ell((t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},n})=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{km+1}\ell((t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},km+1})=\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle-\frac{1}{m}\langle 2\rho,\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m-1}\text{wt}(\zeta^{j}x,\zeta^{j+1}x)\rangle.

Thus by
theorem

3.1
,
\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})=\frac{1}{m}\langle 2\rho,\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m-1}\text{wt}(\zeta^{j}x,\zeta^{j+1}x)\rangle
.

3.3.2.

Now we consider the general case, where
\sigma_{0}
may be nontrivial. Then

\sigma^{j}(t^{x\mu})=\text{Ad}((\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j})(t^{x\mu}))=\text{Ad}(\zeta^{\sigma_{0},j})(t^{\sigma_{0}^{j}(x\mu)}),

where we recall
\zeta^{\sigma_{0},j}=\zeta\sigma_{0}(\zeta)\sigma_{0}^{2}(\zeta)\cdots\sigma_{0}^{j-1}(\zeta)
. Let
m^{\prime}
denote the order of
\zeta\sigma_{0}
inside
W\rtimes\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle\subset\Aut(W)
; since the group is adjoint, this is also the order of
\sigma
as an automorphism of the subset of translation elements in
\widetilde{W}
.

Then a similar calculation as above shows for any positive integer
k
,

(t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},km^{\prime}+1}=xt^{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}x^{-1},\text{~with~}\mu_{k}^{\prime}=\mu+k\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m^{\prime}-1}\sigma_{0}^{j}(\mu)-k\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m^{\prime}-1}\text{wt}(\zeta^{\sigma_{0},j}\sigma_{0}^{j}(x),\zeta^{\sigma_{0},j+1}\sigma_{0}^{j+1}(x)).

In other words, the dominant translation part of
(t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},km^{\prime}+1}
is

(3.1)

\mu_{k}^{\prime}:=\mu+km^{\prime}\mu^{\diamond}-k\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m^{\prime}-1}\text{wt}(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j+1}(x)).

Noting that
\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle=\langle 2\rho,\sigma_{0}^{j}(\mu)\rangle
as
2\rho
is
\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle
-invariant, we then proceed in the same way and conclude
\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})=\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}\langle 2\rho,\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m^{\prime}-1}\text{wt}(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j+1}(x))\rangle
.

Rewriting the dimension formula obtained above using
lemma

2.2
, we deduce the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be an adjoint
F
-simple group and
x\in W
be an element in its Weyl group. Suppose that
\sigma=\text{Ad}(\tau\sigma_{0})
is the Frobenius, with
\tau\in\Omega
and
\sigma_{0}(\mathbb{S})=\mathbb{S}
. Let
\zeta
be the finite Weyl group component of
\tau
, and assume that
\text{depth}(\mu)\geq 2
. Finally, let
o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)
denote the order of
\sigma
as an element of
\Aut(X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}})
. Then

\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})=\frac{1}{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)-1}d(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j+1}(x)).

3.4.
via reduction to quasi-split case

Recall that
b_{\max}=\max\limits_{x\in W}b_{t^{x(\mu)}}
. For computation of the generic Newton point, we can further pass on to a quasi-split group in the following way. For an adjoint
F
-simple group
\mathbf{G}
as in the previous subsection, let
\mathbf{G}^{*}
be its quasi-split inner form; then the Frobenius action for
\mathbf{G}^{*}
is given by
\sigma_{\mathbf{G}^{*}}=\sigma_{0}
. By
[
GHN16
, §2.4]
, we can identify
B(\mathbf{G})=B(\mathbf{G}^{*})
via
[b]\rightarrow[b\dot{\tau}]
, and this
bijection preserves the partial order defined via the closure relations in
\breve{G}=\breve{G}^{*}
. Hence, restricting this map to a natural bijection
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}=B(\mathbf{G}^{*})_{w\tau}

for any
w\in\widetilde{W}(\mathbf{G})=\widetilde{W}(\mathbf{G}^{*})
, we then get
\nu^{\mathbf{G}}([b_{w}])=\nu^{\mathbf{G}^{*}}([b_{w}\dot{\tau}])=\nu^{\mathbf{G}^{*}}([b_{w\tau}])
.

We now give alternative (in fact, simpler) formula for the dimension by relating it to the quasi-split case.

Proposition 3.3
.

In the setup of
proposition

3.2
, we have
\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})=d(\zeta^{-1}x,\sigma_{0}(x))
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\text{depth}(\mu)\geq 3
. Note that
t^{x\mu}\tau=t^{x\mu}t^{\varpi_{i}^{\vee}}w_{i,0}w_{0}=xt^{\mu+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}}x^{-1}w_{i,0}w_{0}
. Here
\mu+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}
is dominant with depth at least
2
: for any simple root
\alpha
, we have

\langle\mu+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee},\alpha\rangle=\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle+\langle\varpi_{i}^{\vee},x\alpha\rangle\geq\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle+\langle\varpi_{i}^{\vee},-\theta\rangle=\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle-1\geq 2.

Hence, by
theorem

2.6
we have
\nu^{\mathbf{G}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})=\nu^{\mathbf{G}^{*}}([xt^{\mu+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}}x^{-1}\zeta])=(\mu+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}-\text{wt}(\zeta^{-1}x,\sigma_{0}(x)))^{\diamond}
, giving us

(3.2)

\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})=\langle 2\rho,\mu-\nu(b_{t^{x(\mu)}})\rangle=\langle 2\rho,\text{wt}(\zeta^{-1}x,\sigma_{0}(x))-x^{-1}\varpi_{i}\rangle.

Note that we used
\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle
-invariance of
2\rho
above. Now using the length formula, we get

\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle=\ell(t^{x(\mu)})=\ell(t^{x(\mu)}\tau)=\ell(xt^{\mu+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}}x^{-1}\zeta)=\ell(x)+\langle 2\rho,\mu+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}\rangle-\ell(x^{-1}\zeta),

giving us
\langle 2\rho,x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}\rangle=\ell(x^{-1}\zeta)-\ell(x)
. Plugging this in
eq.

3.2
and applying
lemma

2.2
, we then get

\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})=\langle 2\rho,\text{wt}(\zeta^{-1}x,\sigma_{0}(x))\rangle-\ell(x^{-1}\zeta)+\ell(x)=d(\zeta^{-1}x,\sigma_{0}(x)).

Combining
corollary

3.4
and
proposition

3.2
, we see that this last formula is valid even under the weaker hypothesis that
\text{depth}(\mu)\geq 2
. Hence, we are done.
∎

Let us record an immediate corollary of
proposition

3.3
and
proposition

3.2
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
x\in W
. Suppose that
\sigma_{0}
is an automorphism of
W
with
\sigma_{0}(\mathbb{S})=\mathbb{S}
, and
\zeta=\zeta_{i}=w_{i,0}w_{0}
for some minuscule fundamental coweight
\varpi^{\vee}_{i}
. Finally, let
o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)=\min\{j:\zeta^{\sigma_{0},j}=1\}
. Then

\frac{1}{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)-1}d(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{j+1}(x))=d(\zeta^{-1}x,\sigma_{0}(x)).

3.5.
Finishing the proof

As an consequence of the dimension formulas in the preceeding sections, we obtain the following estimate.

Proposition 3.5
.

In the setup of
proposition

3.2
, we have

\ell(x^{-1}\zeta\sigma_{0}(x))\geq\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})\geq\ell_{R}(x^{-1}\zeta\sigma_{0}(x)).

Proof.

We can prove it using the dimension formula established in either
proposition

3.2
or
proposition

3.3
, but we choose to resort to the latter for notational ease. Then the assertion follows from the easy observation:

(a) for any two elements
a,b\in W
, we have
\ell(a^{-1}b)\geq d(a,b)\geq\ell_{R}(a^{-1}b)
.

Taking any reduced expression for
a^{-1}b=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
and following the edges determined by the simple roots
\alpha_{i_{k}},\cdots,\alpha_{i_{1}}
in order, we obtain a path from
a
to
b
which
has exactly
\ell(a^{-1}b)
edges. This proves the first inequality. For the other one, let
d=d(a,b)
and choose a shortest path
a\rightarrow ar_{1}\rightarrow ar_{1}r_{2}\cdots\rightarrow ar_{1}\cdots r_{d}=b
, where
r_{i}
’s are not necessarily simple roots. Then
a^{-1}b=r_{1}\cdots r_{d}
is a decomposition of
a^{-1}b
into reflections, proving the other inequality.
∎

The reflection length of
w\in W
is the smallest non-negative integer
l
such that
w
can
be written as a product of
l
reflections in
W
. We denote by
\ell_{R}(w)
the reflection
length of
w
. Let
\xi\in W
. Then we denote the
\sigma_{0}
conjugacy class of
\xi
in
W
by
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{0}}(\xi)
. We define
\ell_{R}(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{0}}(\xi))=\min\{\ell_{R}(w):w\in\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{0}}(\xi)\}
. We can define
\ell(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{0}}(\xi))
similarly.

Proof of
theorem

1.1
.

Since
\dim X(\mu,b_{\max})=\min\limits_{x\in W}\dim X_{t^{x\mu}}(b_{t^{x\mu}})
, we see from
proposition

3.5

\ell(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{0}}(\zeta))\geq\dim X(\mu,b_{\max})\geq\ell_{R}(\mathcal{O}_{\sigma_{0}}(\zeta)).

5.
Explicit description of the Newton point of

Note that the existence of
b_{\max}
as in
theorem

2.7
was asserted in
[
HN18
]
via reduction to a similar statement on the associated Iwahori-Weyl group, and then they further reduce it to treat the superbasic case and conclude it from there. However, this is somewhat indirect and does not give an explicit formula for the maximal element. Based on our calculation, in this section we list out
\nu(b_{\max})
for the cases treated in
lemma

4.1
.

5.1.

Recall that the calculation in
section

3
combined with
theorem

3.1
gives us

(5.1)

\langle 2\rho,\nu_{t^{x\mu}}\rangle=\langle 2\rho,\mu^{\diamond}-\text{av}_{\sigma}(x)\rangle,

where we set
\text{av}_{\sigma}(x):=\frac{1}{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)-1}\text{wt}((\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i+1}(x))

However, one can upgrade this.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that we are in the setup of
proposition

3.2
. Let us further assume that
\nu_{t^{x\mu}}
is regular. Then we have

\nu_{t^{x\mu}}=\mu^{\diamond}-\text{av}_{\sigma}(x).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{W}
. Then there is a straight element
w_{s}\in\widetilde{W}
such that
w_{s}\leq w
and
\nu(w_{s})=\nu_{w}
.

Proof.

This essentially follows from the arguments in
[
He21
, section 2.2]
.
∎

Proof of
proposition

5.1
.

Let
w^{\prime}=t^{x(\mu)}
and choose a straight element
w_{s}
corresponding to
w:=w^{\prime}\tau
as given in
lemma

5.2
. By properties of Demazure product and by definition of straight elements,

(5.2)

w_{s}^{\sigma_{0},n-1}\leq w_{s}^{\sigma_{0},n}=w_{s}^{*_{\sigma_{0}},n}\leq w^{*_{\sigma_{0}},n}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that
w^{\prime*_{\sigma},n}=w^{*_{\sigma_{0}},n}
whenever
(\tau\sigma_{0})^{n}=1
, i.e.
o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)\mid n
. Furthermore, for such
n
we have
\sigma^{n}(t^{x\mu})=t^{x\mu},\sigma_{0}^{n}=1
, and hence

(5.3)

w^{*_{\sigma_{0}},n+1}=(w^{\prime}\tau)^{*_{\sigma_{0}},n}*w^{\prime}\tau=(t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},n}*t^{x\mu}\tau=(t^{x\mu})^{*_{\sigma},n+1}\tau.

Let us recall the following well-known fact.

(a) Let
\text{pr}:\widetilde{W}\rightarrow X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}
be the projection map, which sends any element
w
to the unique dominant
coweight
\lambda
with
w\in Wt^{\lambda}W
. Assume that
w_{1},w_{2}\in\widetilde{W}
such that
w_{1}\leq w_{2}
in Bruhat order. Then
(\text{pr}(w_{1}))^{+}\leq(\text{pr}(w_{2}))^{+}
in the dominance order.

Recall now from
section

3.3
that
o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)\mid o(\sigma)
; let
o(\sigma)=\Xi o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)
. Then the calculation in
section

3.3
, combined with
eq.

5.3
, shows that for
n=ko(\sigma)+1
(with
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
), we have

w^{*_{\sigma_{0}},n}=xt^{\mu_{k}^{\prime\prime}}x^{-1}\tau=x^{\mu_{k}^{\prime\prime}+x^{-1}\varpi_{i}^{\vee}}x^{-1}\zeta,\text{ with }\mu_{k}^{\prime\prime}=\mu+ko(\sigma)\mu^{\diamond}-k\Xi\sum\limits_{j=0}^{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)-1}\text{wt}(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i+1}(x)).

Since
\nu(w_{s})
is assumed to be regular, we have by
[
He14
, proposition 2.4]
that
w_{s}=yt^{\gamma}\sigma_{0}(y)^{-1}
for some
y\in W
. Using this in
eq.

5.2
with
n=ko(\sigma)+1
and then applying fact (a), we thus obtain

ko(\sigma)\nu(w_{s})\leq\mu+x^{-1}\varpi^{\vee}+ko(\sigma)\mu^{\diamond}-k\Xi\sum\limits_{j=0}^{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)-1}\text{wt}(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i+1}(x)).

This gives

\nu_{w}=\nu(w_{s})\leq\frac{1}{ko(\sigma)}(\mu+x^{-1}\varpi^{\vee})+\mu^{\diamond}-\frac{1}{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)-1}\text{wt}(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i+1}(x)).

Letting
k
to be sufficiently large and noting that Newton points have bounded denominator, we obtain
\nu_{w}\leq\mu^{\diamond}-\frac{1}{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{o_{\text{tr}}(\sigma)-1}\text{wt}(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i}(x),(\zeta\sigma_{0})^{i+1}(x)).
Hence by
eq.

5.1
, we are done.

∎

To describe
\nu(b_{\max})=\max\limits_{x\in W}\nu_{t^{x\mu}}

5.1
to tabulate

\mu^{\diamond}-\nu(b_{\max})=\min\limits_{x\in W}\text{av}_{\sigma}(x),

x_{n-1}+x_{n}+\frac{2n-2}{4}\in\mathbb{Z}
. From this, we deduce

\Xi_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}&\sum\limits_{j\text{ odd},1\leq j\leq n-2}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}^{\vee},\text{ if $n$ is odd;}\\
&\sum\limits_{j\text{ odd},1\leq j\leq n-2}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}^{\vee}+\frac{1}{4}(\alpha_{n-1}^{\vee}+\alpha_{n}^{\vee}),\text{ if $n$ is even.}\end{cases}

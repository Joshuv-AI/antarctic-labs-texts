Geometric models of soliton vortex dynamics
René Israel García Lara

Abstract
In this work we focus on BPS solutions of the gauged O ⁡ ( 3 ) O(3) Sigma model, originally due to Schroers, and use these ideas to study the geometry of the moduli space. The model has an asymmetry parameter τ \tau breaking the symmetry of vortices and antivortices on the field equations. It is shown that the moduli space is incomplete both on the Euclidean plane and on a compact surface. On the Euclidean plane, the L 2 \mathrm{L}^{2} metric on the moduli space is approximated for well separated cores and results consistent with similar approximations for the Ginzburg-Landau functional are found. The scattering angle of approaching vortex-antivortex pairs of different effective mass is computed numerically and is shown to be different from the well known scattering of approaching Ginzburg-Landau vortices. The volume of the moduli space for general τ \tau is computed for the case of the round sphere and flat tori. The model on a compact surface is deformed introducing a neutral field and a Chern-Simons term. A lower bound for the Chern-Simons constant κ \kappa such that the extended model admits a solution is shown to exist, and if the total number of vortices and antivortices are different, the existence of an upper bound is also shown. Existence of multiple solutions to the governing elliptic problem is established on a compact surface as well as the existence of two limiting behaviours as κ → 0 \kappa\to 0 . A localization formula for the deformation is found for both Ginzburg-Landau and the O ⁡ ( 3 ) O(3) Sigma model vortices and it is shown that it can be extended to the coalescense set. This rules out the possibility that this is Kim-Lee’s term in the case of Ginzburg-Landau vortices, moreover, the deformation term is compared on the plane with the Ricci form of the surface and it is shown they are different, hence also discarding that this is the term proposed by Collie-Tong to model vortex dynamics with Chern-Simons interaction.

Chapter 1
Introduction

This work is about the geometry of moduli spaces of vortices and antivortices
on a Riemann surface
\Sigma
. We are interested mostly in the
gauged
O(3)
Sigma
model,
where the fields are represented by a connection

\gp
and a section
\hf
of a fibre bundle
with fibres
diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the Riemann sphere.
We say
\hf
is a Higgs field with target the Riemann sphere.
Static solutions of the field equations modulo gauge equivalence
form the moduli space of vortices and antivortices, each solution is
determined by the cores of the fields:
the preimages of the north pole (vortex points) and the south pole (antivortex
points). It can be proved the total number of the cores is enumerable and if

\Sigma
is compact, it is finite. We will assume without
loss of generality this is the
case, even though
\Sigma
can be the complex plane. The dynamics
of slowly varying fields can be described by geodesic motion of curves
on the moduli space
[
manton_remark_1982
]
with
a metric called the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
metric. This metric is Kähler and well
understood for the moduli space of vortices of the Ginzburg-Landau
functional, in which case it is known that the moduli space is a complete
metric space and if the ambient surface is compact the moduli space is also
compact, hence of finite volume.

The
O(3)
Sigma model we will study is
asymmetric, vortices and antivortices have different
effective mass, moreover,
the existence of two types of cores
means vortices and
antivortices cannot coalesce, therefore, a natural question is if
the moduli space is still complete.
Another question we address is how the asymmetry
affects the volume of the moduli space. These questions were addressed for the
symmetric case in the
reference
[
romao2018
]
. The techniques used in the reference however
do not apply in general, we developed analytical tools to
extend the results to the asymmetric case.

Later, we add a Chern-Simons deformation to the model and
describe the change in the dynamics of the fields on the moduli space.
The deformation is tuned by means of a deformation constant

\kappa
which we assume small.
It turns out that the dynamics of the theory is described by
geodesic motion perturbed with a connection term proportional to

\kappa
, i.e. a term
dependent on the velocity of the cores. Our model resembles the model of Kim and
Lee
[
kim_first_2002
]
with the difference that the target is the
sphere and there are two types of cores to consider.
It is well known for several related models with Chern-Simons deformations
that multiple solutions of the field equations occur.
We study the problem of existence and multiplicity of
solutions to the field equations of the deformed

O(3)
Sigma model, the main result is that even though
multiple solutions of the equations can exist,
there is a minimal deformation, such that
no matter which configuration of vortices and antivortices on the
moduli space we choose, we can find exactly one
solution close to the undeformed solution of the

O(3)
Sigma model.

We conclude with a description of the chapters of the thesis.

we describe the ideas of localization in
abstract terms. Our approach is general and suits equally well

We also present

In chapter

We compute the metric on the moduli space of vortex-antivortex

The main result is theorem

says that the moduli space is incomplete.

In chapter
we move to a compact ambient

The main results are the incompleteness of the moduli space of

theorem
,
and the computation of the volume of

]

in these cases.

Chapter
is devoted to the study of Chern-Simons
deformations on compact surfaces.
We prove the existence of multiple solutions for small
deformations of the
O(3)
Sigma model if the number of vortices and
antivortices is different and find bounds for
the deformation constant.
We also solve the

The main result is theorem
,
describing the behaviour of the solutions to the field

We finalise the chapter
applying the localization technique to vortices of the Ginzburg-Landau
model and vortices/antivortices of the

Sigma model, both with a
Chern-Simons deformation. We found that dynamics is deviated from

previous results of
Kim-Lee

kim_first_2002

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma,U(1)).

(1.4)

The space of connections
\mathscr{A}
is an affine space: for any two

\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}
,
the difference
\omega-\omega^{\prime}

\mathcal{A}=\Gamma F\times\mathscr{A}

,

,

.

The quotient

\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}
is the configuration space
\mathcal{C}
. If

\rho
has two antipodal fixed points, the north and

N

bundle, the

\rho

represents the unitary group by rotations of the sphere lets us pull

\displaystyle X_{p}=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right\rvert_{s=0}\left({\rho\left({e^{is}}\right)\cdot p}\right),\qquad p\in{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

(1.5)

A section
\phi:\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma\to F

\phi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

Since

\displaystyle\langle{N(x),\phi(x)}\rangle=\langle{N,\phi_{\alpha}(x)}\rangle.

(1.10)

Likewise,

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\phi_{\alpha}-A^{0}_{\alpha}\otimes X_{\phi_{\alpha}},

\displaystyle=d\phi_{\alpha}-A_{\alpha}\otimes X_{\phi},

Localization

The idea of a localization formula originates in the work of
Strachan
[
strachan_lowvelocity_1992
]
. It was later generalised by
Samols
[
samols1992
]
and is based on ideas about geodesic
approximation originating in
[
manton_remark_1982
]
.
From his
work, Strachan and Samols developed approximations to the
dynamics of the Abelian Higgs model in the moduli space of static
solutions of the field equations, later, Stuart proved
in
[
stuart_dynamics_1994
]
that the
moduli space approximation is correct.
The results of Stuart also extended to
other field theories, for example in
[
demoulini_adiabatic_2009
]

Demoulini-Stuart proved a moduli space approximation to the dynamics of
the Chern-Simons-Schrödinger model
proposed by Manton in
[
manton_first_1997
]
. On the other hand,
for some field theories it is possible to find an explicit formula for a
metric on the moduli space governing the dynamics, such that it
only depends of local data, i.e., the position of the cores of the field

\phi
. Over the time, the localization formula has been refined and
extended to other field
theories, e.g. Chern-Simons vortices

[
kim_vortex_1994
,
collie_dynamics_2008
]

or Ginzburg-Landau vortices with electric
and magnetic impurities
[
tong_vortices_2014
]
.
We can describe in an unified way the idea behind localization if we
introduce the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
metric in the space of fields modulo gauge
transformations. By this we mean the space of sections of a given

U(1)
fibre bundle as described on section
Field theory on complex line bundles
.
There are several situations in which this space is
finite dimensional, for example for BPS solitons of the Ginzburg-Landau
functional. In this case, there are rigorous proofs
of this fact
[
taubes1980
,
yang_strings_1999
]
. We make no
assumption on finite dimensionality though, since the theory can be
written in full generality. We restrict the previous field theoretic
setup to the static case and
think of
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}
as an infinite dimensional
principal
\mathscr{G}
-bundle
[
nagy2017
]
. A curve

(\phi_{s},A_{s}):I\to\mathcal{A}
is said to be differentiable, if
for any
x\in\Sigma
, the curves
s\mapsto\phi_{s}(x)
,
s\mapsto A_{s}(x)
,
where,

\displaystyle\phi_{s}(x):I\to F,

\displaystyle A_{s}(x):I\to T_{x}\Sigma,

(1.38)

are differentiable. For a differentiable curve in field space, the variation is
the pair
(\delta\phi,\delta A)
,

\displaystyle\delta\phi:\Sigma\to\phi^{*}TF,

\displaystyle\delta A\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma),

(1.39)

of pointwise derivatives:

\displaystyle\delta\phi(x)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right\rvert_{s=0}\phi_{s}(x),

\displaystyle\delta A(x)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right\rvert_{s=0}A_{s}(x).

(1.40)

We will think of the space of variations as the tangent space of

\mathcal{A}
and denote it as
\mathcal{T}
. If
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}
is a gauge transformation, the fields transform as,

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha}\cdot\phi,

\displaystyle A+d\alpha,

(1.41)

where the product
e^{i\alpha}\cdot\phi
is to be understood as the
action of
e^{i\alpha}
in
\phi
via the representation

\rho
. Equation (
1.41
) defines an action

\alpha*(\phi,A)
, of the gauge group in the space of
fields. This action extends naturally to tangent space. By an abuse in
notation, let us denote by
X

the vector field induced in target space by this action, then

\mathcal{G}
acts in
\mathcal{T}
as,

\displaystyle\alpha*(\delta\phi,\delta A)=(\delta\phi+\alpha X_{\phi},\delta A+d\alpha).

(1.42)

Moreover, the vertical space,

\mathcal{V}_{(\phi,A)}=\left\{(\alpha X_{\phi},d\alpha)\;\left\lvert\;{\alpha}\right.\in\mathcal{G}\right\},

(1.43)

determines a sub-bundle of
\mathcal{T}
whose fibre is in bijection with the
Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}
of gauge transformations
[
nagy2017
]
.

The Riemannian metrics in
\Sigma
and
M
extend to metrics in
the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\Sigma
and
F
respectively, which on
the other hand, extend to a metric in the space of fields: if
(\phi,A)\in\mathcal{A}
and
(\delta\phi,\delta A)\in\mathcal{T}_{(\phi,A)}
, the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-metric is the product of metrics
induced by the Riemannian structure in the domain and the target space,

\lvert\lvert{(\delta\phi,\delta A)}\rvert\rvert_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}=\lvert\lvert{\delta\phi}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Sigma,F)}+\lvert\lvert{\delta A}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Sigma)}.

(1.44)

\mathcal{C}
is the relevant space for applications, as two field
configurations differing by a gauge transformation are regarded as
physically the same. In analogy to a finite dimensional vector bundle,
the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-metric
can be used to split

\mathcal{T}
in a direct sum of the vertical space
\mathcal{V}
and its
orthogonal complement. If the quotient
\mathcal{C}
has a
finite dimensional differentiable structure, this complement can be
identified with its tangent space. This is not necessarily the
case, however we can consider that the orthogonal complement
describes tangent vectors to
\mathcal{C}
, whether this space is finite
dimensional or not. Hence, the orthogonal complement describes the
dynamics of curves
[(\phi_{s},A_{s})]:I\to\mathcal{C}
with a lift to
field space, even if the quotient lacks regularity.

Given
(\delta\phi,\delta{A})\in\mathcal{T}_{(\phi,A)}
, let

\beta\in\mathcal{G}
be the projection onto

\mathcal{V}_{(\phi,A)}
with respect to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
product. If

\alpha\in\mathcal{G}
represents another
arbitrary vertical vector at
(\phi,A)
, then

\langle{(\delta\phi-\beta X_{\phi},\delta A-d\beta),(\alpha X_{\phi},d\alpha)}\rangle=0.

(1.45)

Since
\alpha
is arbitrary, the perpendicularity condition is
equivalent to the equation,

\left(\Delta+\lvert{X_{\phi}}\rvert^{2}\right)\beta=\langle{X_{\phi},\delta\phi}\rangle+d^{*}\delta A,

(1.46)

where
d^{*}:\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\to\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)
is the
codifferential,
d^{*}=-*d*
. What is interesting about
the perpendicularity condition is that it is
independent of the theory because no Lagrangian or functional for
the fields was necessary to deduce it. At the same time, we can
talk of kinetic energy in configuration space, at least
for curves
[\phi_{s},A_{s}]
admitting a lift to
\mathcal{A}
.
For such a curve, we could define its instant energy as,

\mathrm{E}[\delta\phi,\delta{A}]=\frac{1}{2}\lvert\lvert{(\delta\phi^{\perp},\delta{A}^{\perp})}\rvert\rvert_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}.

(1.47)

We think of the kinetic energy of a dynamic pair
(\phi,A)
of
solutions to the field equations slowly varying in time, as
approximated by the energy of the
variation of a static pair of solutions.
In this way, we reduce the full theory in spacetime to variations of
the static solutions to the Bogomolny equations. With this point of
view, the components of the gauge potential are curves defined in some
interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle A_{0}:I\subset\mathbb{R}\to C^{\infty}(\Sigma),

\displaystyle A:I\subset\mathbb{R}\to\Omega^{1}(\Sigma).

(1.48)

Likewise for the electric and magnetic fields. Let us
denote by
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
the subset of
\mathcal{A}
of solutions of the
Bogomolny equations, and by

\mathcal{M}
the quotient space
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}/\mathcal{G}
. There is
a bundle inclusion,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}

(1.49)

and since the Bogomolny equations are gauge invariant, both bundles share
the same vertical space. Therefore, the orthogonal projection onto

\mathcal{V}
is the same.

Lemma 1.0.13

Let
\Sigma
be either the plane or a compact surface.
If

, is a non-negative smooth function
with only finite zeros, such that if

, and all the derivatives

are bounded, then for any

Proof 1.0.14
.

By the hypothesis on the potential function
V
, the operator

\Delta+V
is continuous. Let us define
the bilinear form
B:\mathrm{H}^{1}\times\mathrm{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
and the
linear functional

such that,

\displaystyle B(u,v)

\displaystyle=\langle{\nabla u,\nabla v}\rangle+\langle{Vu,v}\rangle,

(1.82)

\displaystyle A(u)

\displaystyle=\langle{b,u}\rangle,

where
b\in\mathrm{H}^{r}
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
A
and
B
are
continuous.

Firstly, we claim
B
is coercive, i.e., there is a positive
constant
\alpha
such that,

\alpha\,\lvert\lvert{u}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}\leq B(u,u).

(1.83)

Let
\Omega
be either the compact surface
\Sigma
or
an open disk

such that

(1.84)

Assume towards a contradiction the existence of a sequence

\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, such that

\displaystyle\lvert\lvert{u_{n}}\rvert\rvert_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle B(u_{n},u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}.

(1.85)

By the Banach-Aloglu theorem, we can assume
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{*}
in

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, and by the Rellich theorem, we can assume the strong
convergence

in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\lvert\lvert{\nabla u_{n}}\rvert\rvert_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,

(1.86)

hence

is constant almost everywhere, because by the strong convergence

and the convergence,

\displaystyle\langle{u_{n},u_{*}}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\to\lvert\lvert{u_{*}}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)},

(1.87)

we deduce
\langle{\nabla u_{n},\nabla u_{*}}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\to\lvert\lvert{\nabla u_{*}}\rvert\rvert^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
, but
\langle{\nabla u_{n},\nabla u_{*}}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
, hence

almost everywhere. On the

(1.88)

and

is positive except for a finite set, thence

has unit norm. Therefore, there is a

\Sigma
is

\Sigma

(1.89)

Secondly, we prove the basic inequality,

(1.90)

where

Renormalization of singular elliptic stochastic PDEs using flow equation
Paweł Duch

Abstract
We develop a solution theory for singular elliptic stochastic PDEs with fractional Laplacian, additive white noise and cubic non-linearity. The method covers the whole sub-critical regime. It is based on the Wilsonian renormalization group theory and the Polchinski flow equation. MSC classification: 60H17, 81T17

1
Introduction

A general technique that allows to renormalize and prove universality of parabolic singular SPDEs with fractional Laplacian, additive noise and polynomial non-linearity was developed in
[
4
]
. The goal of this paper is to give a different application of this technique. We present a self-contained construction of solutions of the non-local singular elliptic SPDEs

\big(1+(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}\big)\varPhi(x)=\xi(x)+\lambda\varPhi(x)^{3}-\infty\varPhi(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}},

(1.1)

where
\mathsf{d}\in\{1,\ldots,6\}
,
\sigma\in(\mathsf{d}/3,\mathsf{d}/2]
,
(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}
is the fractional Laplacian,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is sufficiently small and
\xi
is the periodization of the white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
with period
2\pi
. Recall that the regularity of the noise
\xi
is slightly worse than
-\mathsf{d}/2
and the expected regularity of the solution is slightly worse than
\sigma-\mathsf{d}/2
. For
\sigma>\mathsf{d}/2
the above equation is not singular and can be solved using classical PDE theory. For
\sigma\leq\mathsf{d}/2
the solution is not a function but only a distribution. As a result, the cubic term is ill-defined and has to be renormalized by subtracting an appropriate mass counterterm (for
\mathsf{d}>6
other counterterms are needed even if one takes into account all the symmetries of the equation). The renormalization problem is tractable only if the equation is sub-critical (super-renormalizable). This is the case if the expected regularity of the renormalized non-linearity is better than the regularity of
\xi
, i.e.
3(\sigma-\mathsf{d}/2)>-\mathsf{d}/2
. Let us remark that for
\mathsf{d}=5
and
\sigma=2
the above equation is sometimes called the elliptic quantization equation of the
\varPhi^{4}_{3}
model (provided
\xi
is replaced by the white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
).

Let
G\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})
be the fundamental solution for the pseudo-differential operator
\mathbf{Q}:=1+(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}
and let
G_{\kappa}
be the smooth approximation of
G
with a spatial UV cutoff of order
[\kappa]:=\kappa^{1/\sigma}
introduced in Def.
2.1
. We rewrite the above singular SPDE in the regularized mild form

\varPhi=G_{\kappa}\ast F_{\kappa}[\varPhi],\qquad\kappa\in(0,1/2].

(1.2)

The functional
F_{\kappa}[\varphi]
, called the force, is defined by

F_{\kappa}[\varphi](x):=\xi(x)+\lambda\,\varphi^{3}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{i_{\sharp}}\lambda^{i}\,c_{\kappa}^{[i]}\varphi(x),

(1.3)

where
i_{\sharp}=\lfloor\sigma/(3\sigma-\mathsf{d})\rfloor
and the parameters
c_{\kappa}^{[i]}\in\mathbb{R}
depending on the UV cutoff
\kappa
are called the counterterms. Let us state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a choice of counterterms and a random variable
\lambda_{0}
such that
\mathbb{E}(\lambda_{0}^{-n})<\infty
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and for every random variable
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}]
and
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
Eq. (
1.2
) has a periodic solution
\varPhi_{\kappa}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})
and for all
\beta<\sigma-\mathsf{d}/2
the limit
\lim_{\kappa\searrow 0}\varPhi_{\kappa}
exists almost surely in the Besov space
\mathscr{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}})
.

Proof.

We first establish bounds for cumulants of the enhanced noise introduced in Def.
7.1
. The bounds are stated in Theorem
16.1
and hold true for an appropriate choice of the counterterms. Using these bounds and a Kolmogorov type argument we deduce bounds for the enhanced noise stated in Theorem
13.1
. This together with the deterministic result of Theorem
11.4
implies the statement.
∎

Remark 1.2
.

There exists a general technique developed in
[
5
,
3
,
2
,
1
]
based on the theory of regularity structures that allows to systematically renormalize virtually all sub-critical singular SPDEs with local differential operators. However, due to the slow decay of the kernel
G
at infinity, this technique does not cover Eq. (
1.2
).

Remark 1.3
.

Our technique requires a small parameter. In the case of the elliptic equations we assume that the prefactor of the non-linear term is sufficiently small. Alternatively, one could introduce a prefactor in front of the noise term and assume that it is sufficiently small. In the case of parabolic equations the technique allows
[
4
]
to construct a solution in a sufficiently small time interval without any assumption about the strength of the non-linearity.

Remark 1.4
.

We study Eq. (
1.2
) with a regularized Green function
G_{\kappa}
and the periodization of the white noise
\xi
. Our technique is also applicable
[
4
]
to the equation with the standard Green function
G
and a regularized noise
\xi_{\kappa}
.

2
Effective force

The basic object of the flow equation approach is the effective force functional

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{M})\ni\varphi\mapsto F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M})

(2.1)

depending on the UV cutoff
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
and the flow parameter
\mu\in[0,1]
, where
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{M})
and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M})
denote the space of Schwartz functions and distributions on
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{d}}
, respectively. Note that
\kappa
is assumed to be strictly positive.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi(r)=0
for
|r|\leq 1
and
\chi(r)=1
for
|r|>2
and let
\chi_{\mu}(r):=\chi(r(1-\mu)/\mu)
for
\mu\in(0,1]
. Let
G\in L^{1}(\mathbb{M})
be the fundamental solution for the pseudo-differential operator
\mathbf{Q}:=1+(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}
. For
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
and
\mu\in(0,1]
the smooth kernels
G_{\kappa},G_{\kappa,\mu}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{M})
are defined by

G_{\kappa}(x):=\chi_{\kappa}(|x|^{\sigma})\,G(x),\qquad G_{\kappa,\mu}(x):=\chi_{\kappa}(|x|^{\sigma})\,\chi_{\mu}(|x|^{\sigma})\,G(x).

(2.2)

Remark 2.2
.

Since
\chi_{\kappa}(r)\chi_{\mu}(r)=\chi_{\kappa}(r)
for
\mu\leq\kappa/2
it holds that
G_{\kappa,\mu}=G_{\kappa}
for
\mu\leq\kappa/2
and
G_{\kappa,\mu}=G_{\mu}
for
\mu\geq 2\kappa
. Moreover,
\lim_{\kappa\searrow 0}G_{\kappa}=G
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{M})
and
G_{\kappa,1}=0
for all
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
. Note that
\kappa=1/2
corresponds to UV cutoff at spatial scale
1
. For arbitrary fixed UV cutoff
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
the family
G_{\kappa,\mu}
,
\mu\in[0,1]
, interpolates between
G_{\kappa}
and
0
. Because of slow decay of
G
for
\sigma\notin 2\mathbb{N}_{+}
the range
\mu\in[1/2,1]
will require some special treatment.

By definition, the effective force satisfies the flow equation

\langle\partial_{\mu}F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi],\psi\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{D}F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi,\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}\ast F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]],\psi\rangle

(2.3)

with the boundary condition
F_{\kappa,0}[\varphi]=F_{\kappa}[\varphi]
, where
\varphi,\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{M})
and the force
F_{\kappa}[\varphi]
is defined by Eq. (
1.3
). The pairing between a distribution
V
and a test function
\psi
is denoted by
\langle V,\psi\rangle
and
\langle\mathrm{D}V[\varphi,\zeta],\psi\rangle
is the derivative of the functional
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{M})\ni\varphi\mapsto\langle V[\varphi],\psi\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
in the direction
\zeta\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{M})
. In contrast to the force
F_{\kappa}[\varphi]
the effective force
F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]
is generically a non-local functional.

We claim that
\varPhi_{\kappa}:=G_{\kappa}\ast F_{\kappa,1}[0]
is a solution of Eq. (
1.2
). The above statement is a consequence of the equalities
G_{\kappa,\mu}=G_{\kappa}
and
F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]=F_{\kappa}[\varphi]
that hold for all
\mu\in[0,\kappa/2]
and the identity

F_{\kappa,1}[0]=F_{\kappa,\mu}[G_{\kappa,\mu}\ast F_{\kappa,1}[0]]

(2.4)

that holds for all
\mu\in[0,1]
. In order to prove the last identity we use the flow equation to show that the difference between the LHS and RHS of Eq. (
2.4
), denoted by
g_{\kappa,\mu}
, satisfies the linear ODE

\partial_{\mu}g_{\kappa,\mu}=-\mathrm{D}F_{\kappa,\mu}[G_{\kappa,\mu}\ast F_{\kappa,1}[0],\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}\ast g_{\kappa,\mu}]

(2.5)

with the boundary condition
g_{\kappa,1}=0
. This implies that
g_{\kappa,\mu}=0
for all
\mu\in[0,1]
.

Remark 2.3
.

The effective force plays also a central role in the approach to singular SPDEs proposed earlier by Kupiainen
[
6
,
7
]
and applied to the dynamical
\varPhi^{4}_{3}
model and the KPZ equation. The method developed by Kupiainen is based on the Wilsonian discrete renormalization group theory
[
11
]
. In this approach one uses the fact that for
\mu\geq\eta
the effective force satisfies the equation

F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]=F_{\kappa,\eta}[(G_{\kappa,\eta}-G_{\kappa,\mu})\ast F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]+\varphi].

(2.6)

Given
F_{\kappa,\eta}[\varphi]
the above equation is viewed as an equation for
F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]
that can be solved using the Banach fixed point theorem. One defines recursively the effective force
F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]
for
\kappa=2L^{-N}
and
\mu\in\{L^{-N},\ldots,1\}
, where
L>1
, starting with
F_{2L^{-N}}[\varphi]=F_{2L^{-N},L^{-N}}[\varphi]
and finishing with
F_{2L^{-N},1}[\varphi]
. In order to prove uniform bounds for
F_{2L^{-N},1}[\varphi]
one has to appropriately adjust the counterterms which are the coefficients of the force
F_{2L^{-N}}[\varphi]
. The fine-tuning problem becomes exceedingly difficult for equations close to criticality. The flow equation provides a different, more efficient and simpler method of constructing the effective force that allows to treat equations arbitrarily close to criticality.

3
Construction of the effective force coefficients

The starting point of the construction of the effective force is the formal ansatz

\langle F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi],\psi\rangle:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{i}\,\langle F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu},\psi\otimes\varphi^{\otimes m}\rangle,

(3.1)

where
\lambda
is the prefactor of the cubic non-linearity in the original equation. The distributions
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M}^{1+m})
,
i,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, are called the effective force coefficients. By definition the expression
\langle F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu},\psi\otimes\varphi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\varphi_{m}\rangle
is invariant under permutations of the test functions
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m}\in\ \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{M})
. The coefficients
F^{i,m}_{\kappa}
of the force
F_{\kappa}[\varphi]
are defined by an equality analogous to Eq. (
3.1
).

Remark 3.1
.

Let us list the non-vanishing force coefficients
F^{i,m}_{\kappa}
:

\displaystyle F^{0,0}_{\kappa}(x)=\xi(x),\qquad F^{1,3}_{\kappa}(x;x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{1})\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{2})\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{3}),

(3.2)

\displaystyle F^{i,1}_{\kappa}(x;x_{1})=c_{\kappa}^{[i]}\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{1}),\quad i\in\{0,\ldots,i_{\sharp}\},

(3.3)

where
\delta_{\mathbb{M}}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M})
is the Dirac delta at
0\in\mathbb{M}
.

The flow equation (
2.3
) for the effective force
F_{\kappa,\mu}[\varphi]
formally implies that the effective force coefficients
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
satisfy the flow equation

\langle\partial_{\mu}^{\phantom{i}}F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}\,,\,\psi\otimes\varphi^{\otimes m}\rangle\\
=-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\,(1+k)\,\big\langle F^{j,1+k}_{\kappa,\mu}\otimes F^{i-j,m-k}_{\kappa,\mu},\psi\otimes\varphi^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathbf{V}\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}\otimes\varphi^{\otimes(m-k)}\big\rangle

(3.4)

with the boundary condition
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,0}=F^{i,m}_{\kappa}
, where
\mathbf{V}\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{M})
is defined by
\mathbf{V}\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}(x,y):=\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}(x-y)
.

The basic idea behind the flow equation approach is a recursive construction of the effective force coefficients
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
:

(0)

We set
F^{0,0}_{\kappa,\mu}=\xi
and
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}=0
if
m>3i
.

(I)

Assuming that all
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
with
i<i_{\circ}
, or
i=i_{\circ}
and
m>m_{\circ}
were constructed we define
\partial_{\mu}F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
with
i=i_{\circ}
and
m=m_{\circ}
with the use of Eq. (
3.4
).

(II)

Subsequently,
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
is defined by
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}=F^{i,m}_{\kappa}+\int_{0}^{\mu}\partial_{\eta}F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\eta}\,\mathrm{d}\eta
.

Using this procedure we construct all the effective force coefficients
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
for arbitrary
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
,
\mu\in[0,1]
.

Remark 3.2
.

One easily shows that the effective force coefficients
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
actually vanish if
i=0
and
m>0
, or
i>0
and
m>2(i-1)+3
. The only non-zero coefficients
F^{i,m}_{\kappa,\mu}
which are independent of the value of the flow parameter
\mu
are
F^{0,0}_{\kappa,\mu}(x)=\xi(x)
and
F^{1,3}_{\kappa,\mu}(x;x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=F^{1,3}_{\kappa}(x;x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
. These coefficients happen to be independent of the UV cutoff
\kappa
. Let us give some further examples:

\begin{gathered}F^{1,2}_{\kappa,\mu}(x;x_{1},x_{2})=3\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}(x)\,\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{1})\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{2}),\\
F^{1,1}_{\kappa,\mu}(x;x_{1})=(3\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}^{2}(x)+c_{\kappa}^{[1]})\,\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{1}),\qquad F^{1,0}_{\kappa,\mu}(x)=\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}^{3}(x)+c_{\kappa}^{[1]}\,\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}(x),\end{gathered}

(3.5)

where
\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}:=G_{\kappa\shortparallel\mu}\ast\xi
and
G_{\kappa\shortparallel\mu}:=G_{\kappa}-G_{\kappa,\mu}
is the so-called fluctuation propagator. The coefficient
F^{2,5}_{\kappa,\mu}(x;x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})
is obtained from the distribution

3\,\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{1})\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{2})\,G_{\kappa\shortparallel\mu}(x-x_{3})\,\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x_{3}-x_{4})\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x_{3}-x_{5})
by symmetrization in variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}
. We also have

F^{2,1}_{\kappa,\mu}(x;x_{1})=(3\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}^{2}(x)+c_{\kappa}^{[1]})\,G_{\kappa\shortparallel\mu}(x-x_{1})\,(3\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}^{2}(x_{1})+c_{\kappa}^{[1]})\\
+\left(\int_{\mathbb{M}}6\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}(x)\,G_{\kappa\shortparallel\mu}(x-x_{2})\,(\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}^{3}(x_{2})+c_{\kappa}^{[1]}\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}(x_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}+c_{\kappa}^{[2]}\right)\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(x-x_{1}),

(3.6)

F^{2,0}_{\kappa,\mu}(x)=(3\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}^{2}(x)+c_{\kappa}^{[1]})\!\int\!G_{\kappa\shortparallel\mu}(x-x_{1})(\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}^{3}(x_{1})+c_{\kappa}^{[1]}\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}(x_{1}))\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}+c_{\kappa}^{[2]}\,\varPsi_{\kappa,\mu}(x).

(3.7)

For the sake of brevity, we did not give expressions for the coefficients
F^{2,4}_{\kappa,\mu}
,
F^{2,3}_{\kappa,\mu}
,
F^{2,2}_{\kappa,\mu}
, which should be constructed after
F^{2,5}_{\kappa,\mu}
and before
F^{2,1}_{\kappa,\mu}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The effective force is an analog of the effective potential in QFT. A recursive construction of the effective potential coefficients based on the flow equation is the backbone of a very simple proof of perturbative renormalizability of QFT models proposed by Polchinski
[
10
]
(see
[
8
]
for a review).

The solution of Eq. (
1.2
) is formally given by the sum

\varPhi_{\kappa}=G_{\kappa}\ast F_{\kappa,1}[0]:=G_{\kappa}\ast\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{i}F^{i,0}_{\kappa,1}.

(3.8)

Assuming that
\lambda
is sufficiently small in Sec.
11
we prove that the above series converges absolutely and
\varPhi_{\kappa}
, as defined above, solves Eq. (
1.2
) and converges almost surely as
\kappa\searrow 0
in the Besov space
\mathscr{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{M})
for every
\beta<\sigma-\mathsf{d}/2
. To this end, we will establish certain bounds for
\partial_{\kappa}^{r}\partial_{\mu}^{s}F_{\kappa,\mu}^{i,m}
which are stated in Sec.
6
. The bounds involve a regularizing kernel
K_{\mu}
, which is introduced in Sec.
4
, and a norm
\|{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\displaystyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\textstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{\hskip 1.42262pt$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$\hskip 1.42262pt}}}}}}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{m}}
, which is introduced in Sec.
5
.

4
Regularizing kernels

Definition 4.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
let
\mathcal{K}^{n}\subset\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M}^{n})
be the space of signed measures
K
on
\mathbb{M}^{n}
with finite total variation
|K|
. We set
\|K\|_{\mathcal{K}^{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{M}^{n}}|K(\mathrm{d}x_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n})|
. If
n=1
, then we write
\mathcal{K}^{1}=\mathcal{K}\subset\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M})
. We denote by
\delta_{\mathbb{M}}\in\mathcal{K}
the Dirac delta at
0\in\mathbb{M}
. Given
K\in\mathcal{K}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
we set
K^{\otimes n}:=K\otimes\ldots\otimes K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
.

Remark 4.2
.

It holds that
\|K\|_{\mathcal{K}^{n}}=\|K\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{M}^{n})}
for all
K\in L^{1}(\mathbb{M}^{n})\subset\mathcal{K}^{n}
.

Definition 4.3
.

For
\mu\geq 0
the kernel
K_{\mu}\in\mathcal{K}
is the unique solution of the equation
\mathbf{P}_{\mu}K_{\mu}=\delta_{\mathbb{M}}
, where
\mathbf{P}_{\mu}:=1-[\mu]^{2}\Delta
and
[\mu]:=\mu^{1/\sigma}
. We set
K_{\mu}^{\ast 0}:=\delta_{\mathbb{M}}
,
\mathbf{P}^{0}_{\mu}:=1
and
K^{\ast({\mathtt{g}}+1)}_{\mu}:=K_{\mu}\ast K^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}_{\mu}
,
\mathbf{P}^{{\mathtt{g}}+1}_{\mu}:=\mathbf{P}_{\mu}\,\mathbf{P}^{{\mathtt{g}}}_{\mu}
for
{\mathtt{g}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We omit the index
\mu
if
\mu=1
.

Remark 4.4
.

It holds that
K_{0}=\delta_{\mathbb{M}}
and
K_{\mu}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{M})
for
\mu>0
. For
{\mathtt{g}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mu\geq 0
the kernel
K_{\mu}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}
is a positive measure with total mass
\|K_{\mu}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}\|_{\mathcal{K}}=1
. We have
\mathbf{P}^{\mathtt{g}}_{\mu}K^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}_{\mu}=\delta_{\mathbb{M}}
. The fact that the regularizing kernel
K_{\mu}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}
is an inverse of a differential operator simplifies the analysis in Sec.
8
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
\mu,\eta>0
it holds that

\begin{gathered}K_{\mu}=\mathbf{P}_{\eta}K_{\mu}\ast K_{\eta},\qquad\|\mathbf{P}_{\eta}K_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{K}}=1\vee(2[\eta/\mu]^{2}-1).\end{gathered}

(4.1)

In particular, if
\mu\geq\eta
, then
\|\mathbf{P}_{\eta}K_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{K}}=1
.

Proof.

We have
\mathbf{P}_{\eta}K_{\mu}=[\eta/\mu]^{2}\delta_{\mathbb{M}}+(1-[\eta/\mu]^{2})K_{\mu}
and
\|\delta_{\mathbb{M}}\|_{\mathcal{K}}=\|K_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{K}}=1
.
∎

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{M}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{\mathsf{d}}
. For
V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{M})
we define
\mathbf{T}V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
by

\mathbf{T}V(x):=\sum_{y\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{\mathsf{d}}}V(x+y).

(4.2)

Remark 4.7
.

For
K\in L^{1}(\mathbb{M})
and periodic
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
it holds that
K\ast f=\mathbf{T}K\star f
, where
\ast
and
\star
are the convolutions in
\mathbb{M}
and
\mathbb{T}
, respectively.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
{\mathtt{g}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathsf{d}}
and
n\in[1,\infty]
. The following is true:

(A)

If
|a|\leq{\mathtt{g}}
, then
\|\partial^{a}K^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{K}}\lesssim[\mu]^{-|a|}
uniformly in
\mu>0
.

(B)

\|\mathbf{T}K^{\ast\mathsf{d}}_{\mu}\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim[\mu]^{-\mathsf{d}(n-1)/n}
uniformly in
\mu\in(0,1]
.

(C)

It holds that
\|\mathbf{P}_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}K_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{K}}\lesssim[\mu]^{-\sigma}
uniformly in
\mu>0
.

Remark 4.9
.

Let
f\in C(\mathbb{M})
,
{\mathtt{g}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathsf{d}}
be such that
|a|\leq{\mathtt{g}}
. Then
K^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}_{\mu}\ast f\in C^{\mathtt{g}}(\mathbb{M})
and
\|\partial^{a}K^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}_{\mu}\ast f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{M})}\lesssim[\mu]^{-|a|}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{M})}
uniformly over
\mu>0
and
f\in C(\mathbb{M})
.

5
Function spaces for the coefficients

Definition 5.1
.

For
\alpha<0
and
\phi\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M})
we define

\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{M})}:=\sup_{\mu\in(0,1]}[\mu]^{-\alpha}\,\|K_{\mu}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}\ast\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{M})},\qquad{\mathtt{g}}=\lceil-\alpha\rceil\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

(5.1)

The space
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{M})
consists of
\phi\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{M})
such that
\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{M})}<\infty
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The vector space
\mathcal{V}^{m}
consists of
V\in C(\mathbb{M}^{1+m})
such that

\|V\|_{\mathcal{V}^{m}}:=\\
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{M}}\int_{\mathbb{M}^{m}}|V(x;y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{m}

(5.2)

is finite and the function
x\mapsto V(x;y_{1}+x,\ldots,y_{m}+x)
is
2\pi
periodic for every
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\in\mathbb{M}
. For
{\mathtt{g}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the space
\mathcal{D}^{m;{\mathtt{g}}}
consists of

Definition 5.5

Definition 5.7
.

Let

,

. We define the trilinear map

by

\mathbf{B}(G,W,U)(x;y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\\
:=\int_{\mathbb{M}^{2}}W(x;y_{0},\ldots,y_{k})\,G(y_{0}-z)\,U(z;y_{1+k},\ldots,y_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y_{0}\mathrm{d}z.

(5.4)

Lemma 5.8
.

The map
\mathbf{B}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{M})\times\mathcal{V}^{1+k}\times\mathcal{V}^{m-k}\to\mathcal{V}^{m}
is well defined and

\|\mathbf{B}(G,W,U)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{m}}\leq\|G\|_{\mathcal{K}}\,\|W\|_{\mathcal{V}^{1+k}}\|U\|_{\mathcal{V}^{m-k}}.

(5.5)

Proof.

To prove well-definedness note that

\mathbf{B}(G,W,U)(x;y_{1}+x,\ldots,y_{m}+x)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{M}^{2}}W(x;y_{0}+x,\ldots,y_{k}+x)\,G(y_{0}-z)\,U(z+x;y_{1+k}+x,\ldots,y_{m}+x)\,\mathrm{d}y_{0}\mathrm{d}z

(5.6)

is
2\pi
periodic. We have

\|\mathbf{B}(G,W,U)\|_{\mathcal{V}^{m}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{M}}\int_{\mathbb{M}^{m}}|\mathbf{B}(G,W,U)(x;y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{m}\\
\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{M}}\int_{\mathbb{M}^{2+m}}|W(x;y_{0},\ldots,y_{k})|\,|G(y_{0}-z)|\,|U(z;y_{1+k},\ldots,y_{m})|\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y_{0}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{m}.

(5.7)

It is easy to see that the last line is bounded by the RHS of (
5.5
).
∎

Definition 6.1

Remark 6.3

3.2
.

Remark 6.4
.

For arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and
i,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
m\leq 3i
,
\varrho_{\varepsilon}(i,m)<\varrho(i,m)
holds. Let
i_{\diamond}
be the smallest integer such that
\varrho(i_{\diamond}+1,0)>0
. Then
i_{\diamond}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Moreover, let
\varrho_{\diamond}>0
be the minimum of
\varrho(i,m)+\mathtt{l}
for

,

,
\mathtt{l}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that
\varrho(i,m)+\mathtt{l}>0
. Define
\varepsilon_{\diamond}:=\dim(\xi)/3\wedge\dim(\lambda)/11\wedge\varrho_{\diamond}/(10+9i_{\diamond})\wedge\sigma
. We claim that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\diamond})
and all

it holds that
\varrho_{\varepsilon}(i,m)+\mathtt{l}>0

\varrho(i,m)+\mathtt{l}>0
. In what follows, we fix some
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\diamond}/3)
.

Lemma 6.5
.

For all
{\mathtt{g}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds that
\partial_{\kappa}^{r}\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{M})
and

\|\mathbf{P}^{{\mathtt{g}}}_{\mu}\partial_{\kappa}^{r}\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}\|_{\mathcal{K}}\lesssim[\kappa]^{(\varepsilon-\sigma)r}\,[\mu]^{-\varepsilon r}

(6.3)

uniformly in
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
,
\mu\in(0,1]
, where
G_{\kappa,\mu}
was introduced in Def.
2.1
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathsf{d}}
. It holds that
|\partial^{a}G(x)|\lesssim|x|^{\sigma-\mathsf{d}-|a|}
uniformly for
|x|\leq 2
. If
\sigma\in 2\mathbb{N}_{+}
, then
\partial^{a}G
decays fast at infinity. In general,
|\partial^{a}G(x)|\lesssim|x|^{-\sigma-\mathsf{d}-|a|}
uniformly for
|x|>1
. Moreover, we have

|\partial^{a}\partial_{\kappa}^{r}\chi_{\kappa}(|x|^{\sigma})|\lesssim[\kappa]^{(\varepsilon-\sigma)r}|x|^{-\varepsilon r-|a|},\qquad|\partial^{a}\partial_{\mu}\chi_{\mu}(|x|^{\sigma})|\lesssim|x|^{\sigma-|a|}/\mu^{2}

(6.4)

uniformly in
\kappa\in(0,1/2]
,
\mu\in(0,1]
and
x\in\mathbb{M}
. Furthermore,
|\partial^{a}\partial_{\mu}\chi_{\mu}(|x|^{\sigma})|
vanishes unless
\mu<(1-\mu)|x|^{\sigma}\leq 2\mu
. Using the above properties and considering separately
\mu\in(0,1/2]
and
\mu\in(1/2,1]
we obtain
\|\partial^{a}\partial_{\kappa}^{r}\partial_{\mu}G_{\kappa,\mu}\|_{\mathcal{K}}\lesssim[\kappa]^{(\varepsilon-\sigma)r}[\mu]^{-\varepsilon r-|a|}
. This implies the lemma since
\mathbf{P}_{\mu}^{\mathtt{g}}=(1-[\mu]^{2}\Delta_{x})^{\mathtt{g}}
.
∎

Remark 6.6

Remark 7.2

Let us list the non-zero force coefficients

(7.3)

Remark 7.3
.

Given

,

such that

for some

Remark 7.4

In the case

For a list of multi-indices

The induction step:

,

,

\leq\|K_{1/2}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}},\otimes(1+m)}\ast\partial_{\kappa}^{r}F_{\kappa,1/2}^{i,m}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{m}}+\int_{1/2}^{\mu}\|K_{\eta}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}},\otimes(1+m)}\ast\partial_{\kappa}^{r}\partial_{\eta}F_{\kappa,\eta}^{i,m}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{m}}\mathrm{d}\eta

s(\mathtt{I})=ns
,

and

(K^{\ast\mathtt{f}}_{\mu}\ast\partial_{\mu}^{s}\partial_{\kappa}^{r}f^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,\mu})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{M}^{m}}\!(K^{\ast\mathtt{f},\otimes(1+m)}_{\mu}\ast\partial_{\mu}^{s}\partial_{\kappa}^{r}F^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,\mu})(x;y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{m}

(13.5)

we obtain for

,
s\in\{0,1\}
the bound

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{M}}|\mathbb{E}(K^{\ast\mathtt{f}}_{\mu}\ast\partial_{\mu}^{s}\partial_{\kappa}^{r}f^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,\mu}(x))^{n}|\lesssim[\kappa]^{n(\varepsilon-\sigma)r}\,[\mu]^{n(\varrho_{\varepsilon}(i,m)+|a|-\sigma s)}

(13.6)

uniform in
x\in\mathbb{M}
,
\kappa,\mu\in(0,1/2]
. Lemma
13.5
and Lemma
4.8
(C) imply

\mathbb{E}\|\partial_{\mu}^{s}\partial_{\kappa}^{r}(K^{\ast\mathtt{h}}_{\mu}\ast f^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,\mu})\|^{n}_{\mathcal{V}}\lesssim[\kappa]^{n(\varepsilon-\sigma)r}\,[\mu]^{n(\varrho_{\varepsilon}(i,m)+|a|-\sigma s-\varepsilon)}

(13.7)

with
\mathtt{h}=\mathtt{f}+\mathsf{d}
. The theorem follows now from Lemma

applied with

\zeta_{2\kappa,2\mu}=K^{\ast\mathtt{h}}_{\mu}\ast\partial_{\kappa}^{r}f^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,\mu},\qquad r\in\{0,1\},

(13.8)

and
\omega=(\varepsilon-\sigma)r
and
\rho=\varrho_{\varepsilon}(i,m)+|a|-2\varepsilon\geq\varrho_{3\varepsilon}(i,m)+|a|
, where the last inequality holds for

(otherwise

∎

Remark 13.4

Lemma 13.5
.

Let
n\in 2\mathbb{N}_{+}
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all random fields
\zeta\in L^{n}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mu\in(0,1/2]
it holds that

\mathbb{E}\|K_{\mu}^{\ast\mathsf{d}}\ast\zeta\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\,[\mu]^{-\mathsf{d}}\,\mathbb{E}\|\zeta\|^{n}_{L^{n}(\mathbb{T})}.

(13.9)

Proof.

Note that
K_{\mu}^{\ast\mathsf{d}}\ast\zeta=\mathbf{T}K_{\mu}^{\ast\mathsf{d}}\star\zeta
, where
\star
is the convolution in
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbf{T}K_{\mu}^{\ast\mathsf{d}}
is the periodization of
K_{\mu}^{\ast\mathsf{d}}
(see Def.
4.6
). Using the Young inequality for convolution we obtain

\mathbb{E}\|K_{\mu}^{\ast\mathsf{d}}\ast\zeta\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\|\mathbf{T}K_{\mu}^{\ast\mathsf{d}}\|_{L^{n/(n-1)}(\mathbb{T})}^{n}\,\mathbb{E}\|\zeta\|^{n}_{L^{n}(\mathbb{T})}.

(13.10)

The lemma follows now from Lemma
4.8
(B).
∎

Lemma 13.6
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that if

\mathbb{E}\|\partial_{\mu}^{s}\partial_{\kappa}^{u}\zeta_{\kappa,\mu}^{\phantom{l}}\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\\
\leq C\,[\kappa]^{n(\omega-\sigma u+\varepsilon u)}[\mu]^{n(\rho-\sigma s+\varepsilon s)}

(13.11)

for some differentiable random function
\zeta:(0,1]^{2}\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, some
C>0
,
\omega,\rho\leq 0
and all
u,s\in\{0,1\}
and
\kappa,\mu\in(0,1]
, then

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{\kappa,\mu\in(0,1]}[\kappa]^{-n\omega}[\mu]^{-n\rho}\,\|\zeta_{\kappa,\mu}\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)\leq c\,C.

(13.12)

Proof.

For all
\kappa,\mu\in(0,1]
we have

[\kappa]^{-\omega}[\mu]^{-\rho}\,\|\zeta_{\kappa,\mu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\|\zeta_{1,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\\
+\int_{\mu}^{1}[\eta]^{-\rho}\,\|\partial_{\eta}\zeta_{1,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{\mu}^{1}\int_{\kappa}^{1}[\nu]^{-\omega}[\eta]^{-\rho}\,\|\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\nu}\zeta_{\nu,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\eta

(13.13)

By the Minkowski inequality we get the bound

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{\kappa,\mu\in(0,1]}[\kappa]^{-n\omega}[\mu]^{-n\rho}\,\|\zeta_{\kappa,\mu}\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)^{\!\frac{1}{n}}\leq\mathbb{E}\Big(\|\zeta_{1,1}\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)^{\!\frac{1}{n}}\\
+\int_{0}^{1}[\mu]^{-\rho}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\|\partial_{\mu}\zeta_{1,\mu}\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)^{\!\frac{1}{n}}\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}[\kappa]^{-\omega}[\mu]^{-\rho}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\|\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\kappa}\zeta_{\kappa,\mu}\|^{n}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)^{\!\frac{1}{n}}\mathrm{d}\kappa\mathrm{d}\mu,

(13.14)

which implies the statement.
∎

Lemma 15.2
.

16
Uniform bounds for cumulants

Theorem 16.1
.

There exists a unique choice of the counterterms
c^{[i]}_{\kappa}
in Eq. (
1.3
) such that:

\mathbb{E}f^{1,3,0}_{\kappa,1/2}=1
,
\mathbb{E}f^{i,1,0}_{\kappa,1/2}=\mathfrak{f}^{[i]}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,i_{\sharp}\}
, and
\mathbb{E}f^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,1/2}=0
for all other
i\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
a\in\mathfrak{M}^{m}
such that
\varrho(i,m)+|a|\leq 0
. Fix arbitrary
\mathfrak{f}^{[i]}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,i_{\sharp}\}
. For all list of indices

\mathtt{I}
there exists
{\mathtt{g}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that the bound

\|K^{\ast{\mathtt{g}},\otimes(n+m)}_{\mu}\ast E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{m}}}\lesssim[\kappa]^{(\varepsilon-\sigma)r(\mathtt{I})}[\mu]^{\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\mathtt{I})-\sigma s(\mathtt{I})+(n-1)\mathsf{d}}

(16.1)

holds uniformly in
\kappa,\mu\in(0,1/2]
, where
n=n(\mathtt{I})
,
\mathsf{m}=\mathsf{m}(\mathtt{I})
,
m=m(\mathtt{I})
. Moreover, the following condition is satisfied

E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};\mathrm{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{y}_{n})=(-1)^{a(\mathtt{I})}E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}(-x_{1},\ldots,-x_{n};-\mathrm{y}_{1},\ldots,-\mathrm{y}_{n}),

(16.2)

where
x_{j}\in\mathbb{M}
,
\mathrm{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{M}^{m_{j}}
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}=0
unless
m+n\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Remark 16.2
.

By stationarity
\mathbb{E}f^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,\mu}(x)
is a constant. Since
\partial_{\mu}F^{1,3}_{\kappa,\mu}=0
it holds that
\mathbb{E}f^{1,3,0}_{\kappa,1/2}=f^{1,3,0}_{\kappa,0}=1
. There is no distinguished value of
\mathbb{E}f^{i,1,0}_{\kappa,1/2}=\mathfrak{f}^{[i]}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,i_{\sharp}\}
, ultimately, because there is no distinguished function
\chi
in Def.
2.1
of the cutoff propagator
G_{\kappa}
. The vanishing of
\mathbb{E}f^{i,m,a}_{\kappa,1/2}
for all other
i\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
a\in\mathfrak{M}^{m}
such that
\varrho(i,m)+|a|\leq 0
is enforced by the properties of the cumulants given in the last sentence of the theorem (here we use the assumption
\mathsf{d}\in\{1,\ldots,6\}
). Observe that these properties are consequences of the following symmetries of Eq. (
1.2
):
\varPhi(x)\mapsto-\varPhi(x)
,
\xi(x)\mapsto-\xi(x)
and
\varPhi(x)\mapsto\varPhi(-x)
,
\xi(x)\mapsto\xi(-x)
, which in particular preserve the law of
\xi
. The counterterms
c^{[i]}_{\kappa}
are related to the renormalization parameters
\mathfrak{f}^{[i]}
by the formula

c^{[i]}_{\kappa}:=f^{i,1,0}_{\kappa}=f^{i,1,0}_{\kappa,0}=\mathfrak{f}^{[i]}-\int_{0}^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\,\partial_{\mu}f^{i,1,0}_{\kappa,\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,i_{\sharp}\}.

(16.3)

The constants
\mathfrak{f}^{[i]}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,i_{\sharp}\}
parametrize the class of solutions of Eq. (
1.2
) constructed in the paper (generically this is an over-parametrization).

Proof.

We first note that the theorem is trivially true for all list of indices
\mathtt{I}
such that
m(\mathtt{I})>3i(\mathtt{I})
since then
E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}=0
. The rest of the proof is by induction.

The base case:

Consider a list of indices
\mathtt{I}
such that
i(\mathtt{I})=0
. In this case the cumulants
E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}
coincide with the cumulants of the white noise
\xi
. The only non-vanishing cumulant is the covariance corresponding to
n(\mathtt{I})=2
,
\mathsf{m}(\mathtt{I})=(0,0)
,
m(\mathtt{I})=0
,
a(\mathtt{I})=0
,
r(\mathtt{I})=0
and
s(\mathtt{I})=0
. It holds that

\|\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-2.33336pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}K_{\mu}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}\ast\xi,K_{\mu}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}}}\ast\xi\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-2.33336pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{m}}}\leq\sup_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\mathbb{E}(\xi(x_{1})\xi(\mathrm{d}x_{2}))|=1.

(16.4)

This finishes the proof of the base case.

Induction step:

Fix
i\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Assume that the theorem is true for all lists of indices
\mathtt{I}
such that either
i(\mathtt{I})<i
, or
i(\mathtt{I})=i
and
m(\mathtt{I})>m
. We shall prove the theorem for all
\mathtt{I}
such that
i(\mathtt{I})=i
and
m(\mathtt{I})=m
.

Consider the case
s(\mathtt{I})>0
. In this case by Lemma
15.2
(A) and (B) the cumulants
E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}
can be expressed in terms of the cumulants for which the statement of the theorem has already been established. As a result, the bound (
16.1
) with
s(\mathtt{I})>0

15.2
(C).

Now consider
\mathtt{I}=((i_{1},m_{1},a_{1},0,r_{1}),\ldots,(i_{n},m_{n},a_{n},0,r_{n}))
,
s(\mathtt{I})=0

12.5
of the cumulants

E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}=E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,0}+\sum_{q=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\mu}E^{\mathtt{I}_{q}}_{\kappa,\eta}\,\mathrm{d}\eta,\quad E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}=E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,1/2}-\sum_{q=1}^{n}\int_{\mu}^{1/2}E^{\mathtt{I}_{q}}_{\kappa,\eta}\,\mathrm{d}\eta,

(16.5)

where
\mathtt{I}_{q}=((i_{1},m_{1},a_{1},0,r_{1}),\ldots,(i_{q},m_{q},a_{q},1,r_{q}),\ldots,(i_{n},m_{n},a_{n},0,r_{n}))

s(\mathtt{I}_{q})=1

16.1

\mathtt{I}

First, let us analyze the irrelevant contributions. If

is a joint cumulant of a list of at least two random distributions. Since

16.5

\|K_{\mu}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}},\otimes(n+m)}\ast E^{\mathtt{I}}_{\kappa,\mu}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{m}}}\leq\sum_{q=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\mu}\|K_{\eta}^{\ast{\mathtt{g}},\otimes(n+m)}\ast E^{\mathtt{I}_{q}}_{\kappa,\eta}\|_{\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{m}}}\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

(16.6)

We used the fact that

4.5

\mathtt{I}=(i,m,a,0,r)

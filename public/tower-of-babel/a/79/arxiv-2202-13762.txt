Marek BOŻEJKO a and Wiktor EJSMONT b

Abstract
In this article, we introduce the notion of a double Fock space of type B. We will show that this new construction is compatible with combinatorics of counting positive and negative inversions on a hyperoctahedral group.

1
Introduction

Several deformations of boson, fermion and full Fock spaces and Brownian motion
have been proposed so far. Bożejko and Speicher used the Coxeter groups of type A (
={}
the symmetric group) to
construct a
q
-deformed Fock space and a
q
-deformed Brownian motion
[
12
]
(
={}
Fock space of type A).
Bożejko, Ejsmont and Hasebe followed this idea
in
[
10
]
and constructed an

(\alpha,q)
-Fock space using the Coxeter groups of type B.
We give an alternative construction to
[
10
]
of a generalized Gaussian process related to the Coxeter groups of type B.
Our motivation is inspired by the following reasoning.
If we start the construction of a deformed probability space by using some symmetrization
operator (with a given statistic on the set of permutations), then we obtain that the joint moments of a Gaussian operator may be expressed by the analogue of statistic on the set of pair partitions.
We explain this by the examples.

First of all, we focus on the work by Bożejko and Speicher about
q
-Gaussian process
[
12
]
on the
q
-deformed Fock space

\mathcal{F}_{q}(H):=(\mathbb{C}\Omega)\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}H^{\otimes n}
, where
-1\leq q\leq 1
, and
\Omega
denotes the vacuum vector and
H
is the complexification of some real separable
Hilbert space
H_{\mathbb{R}}
. On this space the authors introduced
a deformed inner product, using the following symmetrization:

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\SG(n)}q^{\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)}\sigma,

where
\SG(n)
is the set of all the permutations of
\{1,n,\dots,n\}
(the Coxeter groups of type A) and
\operatorname{inv}(\sigma):=\operatorname{card}\{(i,j)\colon i<j,\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\}
is the number of inversions of
\sigma\in\SG(n)
.
On this space we define a creation
a_{q}^{\ast}(x)
and its adjoint, i.e., an annihilation operator
a_{q}(x)
, and a Gaussian operator
G_{q}(x):=a_{q}^{*}(x)+a_{q}(x)
.
The key point of
q
-Gaussian distributions is the methodology of computing their multidimensional moments, which are given by

\begin{split}&\big\langle\Omega,G_{q}(x_{1})\cdots G_{q}(x_{2n})\Omega\big\rangle_{q}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(2n)}q^{cr(\pi)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i,j\}\in\pi\end{subarray}}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle,\end{split}

where
cr(\pi)
is the number of crossings of a pair partition
\pi
(see
[
12
]
).

Bożejko and Yoshida
[
16
]
(see also
[
6
]
) introduced a two-parameter refinement of the
q
-Fock space,
formulated as a
(q,t)
-Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{q,t}(H)
, where
-1\leq q,t\leq 1
.
The corresponding symmetrization
operator has the form

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\SG_{n}}q^{\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)}t^{\operatorname{cinv}(\sigma)}\sigma,

where
\operatorname{cinv}(\sigma)
denotes the number of co-inversions of a permutation
\operatorname{cinv}(\sigma):=\operatorname{card}\{(i,j)\colon{i<j},\allowbreak\sigma(i)<\sigma(j)\}
.
If we denote the creation operator by
a^{\ast}_{q,t}(x)
and the annihilation operator by
a_{q,t}(x)
, then
the moments of
the deformed Gaussian operator
G_{q,t}(x):=a_{q,t}^{*}(x)+a_{q,t}(x)
are encoded by the joint statistics of crossings
and nestings in the set of pair partitions:

\begin{split}&\big\langle\Omega,G_{q,t}(x_{1})\cdots G_{q,t}(x_{2n})\Omega\big\rangle_{q}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(2n)}q^{cr(\pi)}t^{\operatorname{nest}(\pi)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i,j\}\in\pi\end{subarray}}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle,\end{split}

where
\operatorname{nest}(\pi)
denotes the number of nestings in pair partition of
\pi
(see
[
6
, Section 2]
).

From these two examples we see that
the inversions and co-inversions in the symmetric group now become
the joint statistics of crossings and nestings on the set of pair partitions.
The symmetrization operator of type B for
-1\leq\alpha,q\leq 1
, has the form

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in B(n)}\alpha^{\text{number of negative inversions in }\sigma}q^{\text{\,number of positive inversions in }\sigma}\sigma,

where

•

B(n)
is the hyperoctahedral group, see Sections
2
below;

•

the definition of positive and negative inversion is given in Section
4.3
below.

If we apply the above reasoning to the statistic which appears during calculation of moments of type-B Gaussian operator, it should be

\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}^{B}_{2}(2n)}\alpha^{\text{number of negative pairs of }\pi}q^{\text{numbers of crossings of }\pi},

where
\mathcal{P}^{B}_{2}(2n)
is the set of pair partitions of type B (see Definition
4.3
).
It is worth mentioning that probabilistic considerations of type B that first appeared in
[
10
, Corollary 3.9 (2)]
are not quite associated with our present approach.
The goal of this paper is to introduce a double Fock space of type B, such that the Gaussian operator moments are compatible with a statistic symmetrization related to the Coxeter groups of type B. We claim that this approach is more natural than the methodology proposed in
[
10
]
.
In particular, we use this model in order to describe the kernel of the symmetrization operator, that was difficult to achieve with the model from
[
10
]
.

2.1
Coxeter groups of type B

Recall that the Coxeter
group of type B (also known as the hyperoctahedral group) of degree
n
, denoted by
B(n)
, is generated
by the elements
\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{n-1}
subject to the defining relations
\pi_{i}^{2}=e
,
0\leq i\leq n-1
,
(\pi_{0}\pi_{1})^{4}=(\pi_{i}\pi_{i+1})^{3}=e
,
1\leq i<n-1
and
(\pi_{i}\pi_{j})^{2}=e
if
|i-j|\geq 2
,
0\leq i,j\leq n-1
. Note that
\{\pi_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,n-1\}
generate the symmetric group
S(n)
.
The Coxeter diagram for
B(n)
is described in Figure
1
.

\pi_{n-1}
\circ
\dots
\pi_{2}
\circ
\pi_{1}
\circ
\pi_{0}
\circ

Figure 1:
The Coxeter diagram for
B(n)
.

We express
\sigma\in B(n)
in an irreducible form

\sigma=\pi_{i_{1}}\cdots\pi_{i_{k}},\qquad 0\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq n-1,

i.e., in a form with the minimal length, and in this case let

\displaystyle l_{1}(\sigma)=\text{the number of the occurrences of the factor $\pi_{0}$ in $\sigma$},

\displaystyle l_{2}(\sigma)=\text{the number of the occurrences of all the factor of the form $\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{n-1}$ in $\sigma$}.

Remark 2.1
.

These definitions do not depend on the way we express
\sigma
in an irreducible form, and therefore,
l_{1}(\sigma)
and
l_{2}(\sigma)
are well defined (see
[
14
, Proposition 1]
).

2.2
\bm{(q,\alpha)}
-Meixner–Pollaczek orthogonal polynomials

In this subsection, we remind basic facts about the
orthogonal polynomials.

For a probability measure
\mu
with finite moments of all orders, we can assign the orthogonal polynomials
(P_{n}(x))_{n=0}^{\infty}

with
\operatorname{deg}P_{n}(x)=n

and the leading coefficient of each
P_{n}(x)
is
1
, i.e., monic.
It is known that they satisfy the recurrence relation

\displaystyle xP_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+\beta_{n}P_{n}(x)+\gamma_{n-1}P_{n-1}(x),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots,

with the convention that
P_{-1}(x)=0
. The coefficients
\beta_{n}
and
\gamma_{n}
are called
Jacobi parameters
and they satisfy
\beta_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\gamma_{n}\geq 0
.
It is known that

\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|P_{n+1}(x)|^{2}\mu({\rm d}x),\qquad n\geq 0.

Let

•

[n]_{q}
be the
q
-number
[n]_{q}:=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}
,
n\geq 1
;

•

[n]_{q}!
be the
q
-factorial
[n]_{q}!:=[1]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}
,
n\geq 1
;

•

(s;q)_{n}
be the
q
-Pochhammer symbol
(s;q)_{n}:=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\big(1-sq^{k-1}\big)
,

s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
|q|<1
,
n\geq 1
.

Let
{\rm MP}_{\alpha,q}
be the probability measure supported on
\bigl(-2/\sqrt{1-q},2/\sqrt{1-q}\bigr)
.
This measure has density (with respect to the Lebesgue measure)

\frac{{\rm d}{\rm MP}_{\alpha,q}}{{\rm d}t}(t)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}\big(\beta^{2};q\big)_{\infty}}{2\pi\sqrt{4/(1-q)-t^{2}}}\cdot\frac{g(t,1;q)g(t,-1;q)g(t,\sqrt{q};q)g(t,-\sqrt{q};q)}{g(t,{\rm i}\beta;q)g(t,-{\rm i}\beta;q)},

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle g(t,b;q)=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(1-4bt(1-q)^{-1/2}q^{k}+b^{2}q^{2k}\big),\qquad(s;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(1-sq^{k}\big),\qquad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle\beta=\begin{cases}\sqrt{-\alpha},&\alpha\leq 0,\\
{\rm i}\sqrt{\alpha},&\alpha\geq 0.\end{cases}

Remark 2.2
.

In equation (
2.1
), we assume that
({\alpha,q})\in(-1,1)\times(-1,1)
, but
by weak continuity we may allow the parameters
({\alpha,q})
of
{\rm MP}_{\alpha,q}
to take any values in
[-1,1]\times[-1,1]
.

Example 2.3
.

In the special case, we have

1.

The measure
{\rm MP}_{\alpha,1}
is the normal law
(2(1+\alpha)\pi)^{-1/2}{\rm e}^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2(1+\alpha)}}1_{\mathbb{R}}(t)\,{\rm d}t
.

2.

The measure
{\rm MP}_{0,0}
is the standard Wigner’s semicircle law
(1/2\pi)\sqrt{4-t^{2}}1_{(-2,2)}(t)\,{\rm d}t
.

3.

The measure
{\rm MP}_{0,q}
is the
q
-Gaussian law
[
12
]
.

4.

The measure
{\rm MP}_{\alpha,-1}
is the Bernoulli law

(1/2)\big(\delta_{\sqrt{1+\alpha}}+\delta_{-\sqrt{1+\alpha}}\big)
.

5.

The measure
{\rm MP}_{\alpha,0}
is a symmetric free Meixner law
[
1
,
8
,
19
]
.

The orthogonal polynomials
(Q_{n}^{{\alpha,q}}(t))_{n=0}^{\infty}
associated to
the distribution of the

{\rm MP}_{\alpha,q}
are called

(q,\alpha)
-Meixner–Pollaczek polynomials

satisfying the recurrence relation

tQ_{n}^{(\alpha,q)}(t)=Q_{n+1}^{(\alpha,q)}(t)+[n]_{q}\big(1+\alpha q^{n-1}\big)Q_{n-1}^{(\alpha,q)}(t),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots,

(2.2)

where
Q_{-1}^{{\alpha,q}}(t)=0
,
Q_{0}^{{\alpha,q}}(t)=1
and
-1\leq\alpha,q\leq 1
.

3
The double Fock space of type B

Let
H_{\mathbb{R}}
be a separable real Hilbert space and let
H
be its
complexification with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, linear in the right component and anti-linear in the left.
The Hilbert space
{\bf\mathcal{K}}:=H\otimes{H}
is the complexification of its
real subspace
{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}:=H_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
, with the inner product

\langle x\otimes y,\xi\otimes\eta\rangle_{{\bf\mathcal{K}}}=\langle x,\xi\rangle\langle y,\eta\rangle.

We define
{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}:=H^{\otimes n}\otimes{{H}^{\otimes n}}=H^{\otimes 2n}
and instead of
indexing its simple tensors by
\{1,\dots,2n\}
we will index them by
[\pm n]=\{-n,\dots,-1,1,\dots,n\}
and we will use the
typical involution
\bar{n}=-n
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}\ni\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n}=x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\overline{1}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}=x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}.

We use this convention for indexing the elements of
{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}
to define a natural action of the
hyperoctahedral group
B(n)
on
{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}
by setting

\displaystyle\sigma\colon\,

\displaystyle{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}\to{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n},

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\overline{1}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}\mapsto x_{\sigma^{-1}(\overline{n})}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(\overline{1})}\otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}

for any
\sigma\in B(n)
.

Remark 3.1
.

It is worth to mention that in the present paper we act on the indexes, but in the previous one,
[
10
]
, we were acting on the vectors.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\rm fin}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
be the algebraic full Fock space over
{\bf\mathcal{K}}
:

\mathcal{F}_{\rm fin}({\bf\mathcal{K}}):=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{\otimes 2n},

with the convention that
{\bf\mathcal{K}}^{\otimes 0}=H^{\otimes 0}\otimes H^{\otimes 0}={\mathbb{C}}\Omega\otimes\Omega
is the one-dimensional normed space along with the unit vector
\Omega\otimes\Omega
.
We equip
\mathcal{F}_{\rm fin}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
with the inner product

\langle x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\overline{1}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n},y_{\overline{m}}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{\overline{1}}\otimes y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{m}\rangle_{0,0}:=\delta_{m,n}\prod_{i=\overline{n}}^{n}\langle x_{i},y_{i}\rangle.

By definition, the action of the generators
\pi_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
on

\displaystyle\eta=x_{\bar{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\bar{1}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}

is given for
i\geq 1
and
n\geq 2
by

\displaystyle\pi_{i}(\eta)=x_{\bar{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\overline{i}}\otimes x_{\overline{i+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\bar{1}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i+1}\otimes x_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n},

and for
i=0
and
n\geq 1
by

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\eta)=x_{\bar{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{1}\otimes x_{\bar{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}.

For the parameters
-1\leq\alpha,q\leq 1
, we define the symmetrization operators

\displaystyle P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}:=\sum_{\sigma\in B(n)}\alpha^{l_{1}(\sigma)}q^{l_{2}(\sigma)}\,\sigma,\qquad n\geq 1,

\displaystyle P_{\alpha,q}^{(0)}:=I_{H^{\otimes 0}\otimes H^{\otimes 0}}.

Moreover, let

\displaystyle P_{\alpha,q}:=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}

be the
type B symmetrization operator
acting on the algebraic full Fock space.

Remark 3.2
.

From
Bożejko and Speicher
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
, the operator
P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}
is positive for
-1\leq\alpha,q\leq 1
. If
-1<\alpha,q<1
, then
P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}
is a strictly positive operator meaning that it is positive and

\ker P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}=\{0\}
.

For
\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n}\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}
and
\mathbf{y}_{\overline{m}}\otimes\mathbf{y}_{m}\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{m}
, we deform the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{0,0}
by using the type B symmetrization operator:

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n},\mathbf{y}_{\overline{m}}\otimes\mathbf{y}_{m}\rangle_{\alpha,q}:=\delta_{n,m}\langle\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n},P_{\alpha,q}^{(m)}\mathbf{y}_{\overline{m}}\otimes\mathbf{y}_{m}\rangle_{0,0}.

For
x\in H
, let
l(x)
and
r(x)
be the free left and right
annihilation operators on
H^{\otimes n}
, respectively, defined by
the equations

\displaystyle l^{\ast}(x)(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}):=x\otimes x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n},

\displaystyle l(x)(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}):=\langle x,x_{1}\rangle x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n},

\displaystyle r^{\ast}(x)(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}):=x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}\otimes x,

\displaystyle r(x)(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}):=\langle x,x_{n}\rangle x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n-1},

where the adjoint is taken with respect to the free inner product.
The left-right creation and annihilation operators
b^{\ast}(x\otimes y)
,
b(x\otimes y)
on
\mathcal{F}_{\rm fin}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle b^{\ast}(x\otimes y)(\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n}):=l^{\ast}(x)\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes r^{\ast}(y)\mathbf{x}_{n},\qquad

\displaystyle b^{\ast}(x\otimes y)\Omega\otimes\Omega:=x\otimes y,

\displaystyle b(x\otimes y)(\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n}):=l(x)\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes r(y)\mathbf{x}_{n},\qquad

\displaystyle b(x\otimes y)\Omega\otimes\Omega:=0,

where
\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n}\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
.
It holds that
[b^{\ast}(x\otimes y)]^{\ast}=b(x\otimes y)
where the adjoint is taken with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{0,0}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
q,\alpha\in(-1,1)
. The algebraic full Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{\rm fin}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
equipped with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha,q}
is called the
double Fock space of type B
and denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,q}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
.
For
x\otimes y\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}
we define
b_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(x\otimes y):=b^{\ast}(x\otimes y)
and we consider its adjoint operator
b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)
with
respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha,q}

acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,q}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
. The operators
b_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(x\otimes y)
and
b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)
are called
double creation and double annihilation operator of type B, respectively
.

Remark 3.4
.

We would like to emphasize that our double Fock space of type B is different from
[
10
]
.
In the present version of the model, we apply a more natural operation on tensor product, which finally contributes to the more natural combinatorics of calculating the Gaussian moments.
In
[
10
]
, we put the action of the symmetrization on
n
points but in the current approach we give
2n
points which is close to our construction from the articles
[
9
,
20
]
. In
[
10
]
, we defined the creator in the same way as in the case of type A, and then we did not obtain a natural combinatorics which is compatible with partitions of the set
\{\pm 1,\dots,\pm n\}
.
Now if we start our construction with the creator operator of double type B, the situation is completely different from
[
10
]
.

The following proposition can be derived directly
from
[
10
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have the decomposition

P^{(n)}_{\alpha,q}=\big(I\otimes P^{(n-1)}_{\alpha,q}\otimes I\big)R^{(n)}_{\alpha,q}\qquad\text{on}\quad{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n},\qquad n\geq 1,

where

\displaystyle R^{(n)}_{\alpha,q}=I+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}q^{k}\pi_{n-1}\cdots\pi_{n-k}+\alpha q^{n-1}\pi_{n-1}\pi_{n-2}\cdots\pi_{1}\pi_{0}\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}q^{k}\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{k}\bigg),\quad n\geq 2,

\displaystyle R^{(1)}_{\alpha,q}=I+\alpha\pi_{0}.

We can compute the annihilation operator in terms of
R_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}
by using the similar
method to that in
[
10
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For
n\geq 1
and
x\otimes y\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)=b(x\otimes y)R^{(n)}_{\alpha,q}\qquad\text{on}\quad{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}.

Remark 3.7
.

Using the above notation, we can decompose
b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)
into the positive part
p_{q}
and the negative part
n_{q}
as

b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)=p_{q}(x\otimes y)+\alpha n_{q}(x\otimes y),\qquad x\otimes y\in{\bf\mathcal{K}},

where

\displaystyle p_{q}(x\otimes y)\eta=\sum_{k=1}^{n}q^{n-k}\langle x,x_{\bar{k}}\rangle\langle y,x_{k}\rangle\,x_{\bar{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\check{x}_{\bar{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\bar{1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{p_q(x\otimes y)\eta= \sum_{k=1}^n}{}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\check{x}_{k}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n},

(3.1)

\displaystyle n_{q}(x\otimes y)\eta=q^{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q^{k-1}\langle x,x_{{k}}\rangle\langle y,x_{\bar{k}}\rangle\,x_{\bar{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\check{x}_{\bar{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\bar{1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{n_{q}(x\otimes y)\eta=q^{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^n}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\check{x}_{k}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n},

(3.2)

where
\eta=x_{\bar{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}
.
We called the operator
p_{q}
the positive part and
n_{q}
the negative part because in the next section we see that they contribute to positive and negative partitions of type B, respectively.

The following commutation relation is almost the same as in
[
10
, Proposition 2.6]
.
The difference is due to a
twisted
inner product which appears in the second part of the following equation:
\langle x,\eta\rangle\langle y,\xi\rangle
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For
x\otimes y,\xi\otimes\eta\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}
, we have the commutation relation

b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)b_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(\xi\otimes\eta)-qb_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(\xi\otimes\eta)b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)=\langle x,\xi\rangle\langle y,\eta\rangle I+\alpha\langle x,\eta\rangle\langle y,\xi\rangle(q^{2})^{N},

where
(q^{2})^{N}
is the operator on
\mathcal{F}_{\rm fin}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
defined by the linear extension of
(q^{2})^{N}\Omega\otimes\Omega=0
and

\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n}\mapsto q^{2n}\mathbf{x}_{\overline{n}}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{n}
for

.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that

,

1.

If

, where

2.

If

3.

If

4.

Otherwise, if

\frac{\|x\|\|y\|}{\sqrt{1-q}}\leq\|b_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(x\otimes y)\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+|\alpha|}{1-q}}\|x\|\|y\|.

4
The double Gaussian operator of type B

In this non-commutative setting, random variables are understood to be the elements of the

\ast
-algebra generated by
\{b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y),b_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(x\otimes y)\mid x\otimes y\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}\}
. Particularly interesting are their mixed moments.
In order to work effectively on this object we need to define the corresponding statistics. We provide an explicit formula for the combinatorial moments, involving the number of crossings and negative pairs of a partition. First, we need to define the operators, the set of partitions and statistics of type B.

4.1
The orthogonal polynomials

Definition 4.1
.

The operator

G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)=b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)+b_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(x\otimes y),\qquad x\otimes y\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}

on
\mathcal{F}_{\rm fin}({\bf\mathcal{K}})
is called the
double Gaussian operator of type B
.
Denote by
\varphi
the vacuum vector state
\varphi(\cdot)=\langle\Omega\otimes\Omega,\cdot\text{ }\Omega\otimes\Omega\rangle
.

Using a similar argument as in
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
, we can prove the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
\alpha,q\in(-1,1)
and
x\otimes y\in{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
. Let
\mu_{\alpha,q,x,y}
be the probability distribution of
G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)
with respect to the vacuum state. Then
\mu_{\alpha,q,x,y}
is equal to
{\rm MP}_{\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q}
.

Proof.

We observe that for
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)x^{\otimes n}\otimes y^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=b_{\alpha,q}^{\ast}(x\otimes y)x^{\otimes n}\otimes y^{\otimes n}+b_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)x^{\otimes n}\otimes y^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=x^{\otimes(n+1)}\otimes y^{\otimes(n+1)}+[n]_{q}\big(1+\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2}q^{n-1}\big)x^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes y^{\otimes(n-1)},

where Remark
3.7
was used in the second line.
Note that for
n=1

Q_{1}^{(\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q)}(G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y))\Omega\otimes\Omega=G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)\Omega\otimes\Omega=x\otimes y

and by induction

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}^{(\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q)}(G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y))\Omega\otimes\Omega

\displaystyle\qquad{}=G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)Q_{n}^{(\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q)}(G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y))\Omega\otimes\Omega

\displaystyle\qquad\phantom{=}{}-[n]_{q}\big(1+\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2}q^{n-1}\big)Q_{n-1}^{(\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q)}(G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y))\Omega\otimes\Omega

\displaystyle\qquad{}=G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)x^{\otimes n}\otimes y^{\otimes n}-[n]_{q}\big(1+\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2}q^{n-1}\big)x^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes y^{\otimes(n-1)}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=x^{\otimes n+1}\otimes y^{\otimes n+1}.

Therefore, the map
\Phi\colon\bigl(\operatorname{span}\big\{x^{\otimes n}\otimes y^{\otimes n}\mid n\geq 0\big\},\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,q}\bigr)\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\rm MP}_{\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q})
defined by

\Phi\big(x^{\otimes n}\otimes y^{\otimes n}\big)=Q_{n}^{(\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q)}(t)

is an isometry.
Since
\Phi
is an isometry, we get

\langle\Omega\otimes\Omega,G_{\alpha,q}(x\otimes y)^{n}\Omega\rangle_{\alpha,q}=m_{n}({\rm MP}_{\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q})
, where
m_{n}(\mu)
is the
n
-th moment of measure
\mu
.
Since
{\rm MP}_{\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q}
is a compactly supported measure, the Hamburger moment problem has a unique solution and hence
{\rm MP}_{\alpha\langle x,y\rangle^{2},q}=\mu_{\alpha,q,x,y}
.
∎

4.2

Let

For

\pi

\pi

\pi

A block of

Let

The set of partitions of

We denote by

From now on, we will work on a set

From Definition

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cat}(W)=\frac{1}{|W|}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(h+d_{i}),

where
h
is the Coxeter number and
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
are the degrees of
W
, arising
from its ring of polynomial invariants (see
[
2

). The number
\operatorname{Cat}(W)
has
been discovered independently in several different areas and wherever it appears, it
is accompanied by a wealth of new combinatorics.
Armstrong
[
2
, p. 39, Table 2.8]
displays the complete list of Coxeter–Catalan numbers for finite irreducible Coxeter groups;
in type B it is a central binomial coefficient

{2n}\choose{n}
.
This sequence is cataloged as A000984 in Sloane’s database
[
27
]
and the numbers are
called the
Catalan numbers of type B
.

Definition 4.13
.

A type-
B
set partition in a sense of Reiner
[
26

is a set partition
\mathcal{P}_{R}^{B}(n)
of the set
[\pm n]
, which is ordered according to the principle
-1<\dots<-n<1<\dots<n
satisfying the following two conditions:

•

if
B

\mathcal{P}_{R}^{B}(n)
, then
-B
is also a block in
\mathcal{P}_{R}^{B}(n)
;

•

there exists at most one block
B
in
\mathcal{P}_{R}^{B}(n)
for which
B=-B
called the
zero block.

If it is non-crossing then we call it a non-crossing partition. We denote by
\mathcal{NC}^{B}_{R}(n)
the set of all non-crossing partitions in a sense of Reiner.

Our definition of partition in
\mathcal{P}_{1,2}^{B}(n)
is quite similar to a partitions
\mathcal{P}_{R}^{B}(n)
, but in our situation the zero block does not exist.

Reiner
[

\operatorname{Cat}(W)
and in the type-B situation we have:

\#\mathcal{NC}^{{B}}_{R}(n)={{2n}\choose{n}}
.
It is worth to mention that the authors also refer to these numbers as the type-B Catalan numbers, because they count the number of lattice paths of type B from

Finally, we count the number of non-crossing pair partitions of type B, which appear in this article. If we put

\displaystyle tQ_{n}^{(1,0)}(t)

\displaystyle=Q_{n+1}^{(1,0)}(t)+Q_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(t),\qquad n=2,3,\dots.

These are the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind, defined through the identity

5
Fock space in edge cases

In the last section, we explain a situation when
the kernel of the symmetrization
P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}
is nontrivial.
In this case
{P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}}
projects to the space of special symmetric and antisymmetric tensors. In the group algebra
{\mathbb{C}}G
the natural involution is defined by
x^{\ast}=x^{-1}
for
x\in G
and extended by anti-linearity for all groups algebra.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
H\neq\{e\}
is a subgroup of a finite group
G
and function
\varphi
is a character of
H
, then

P_{H}=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{x\in H}\varphi(x)x
is an orthogonal projection.

Proof.

Let us first observe that
P_{H}=P_{H}^{\ast}
because
\overline{\varphi(x)}=\varphi\big(x^{-1}\big)
for
x\in H
. We also have

\displaystyle|H|^{2}P_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in H}\sum_{y\in H}\varphi(x)\varphi(y)xy=\sum_{x\in H}\sum_{y\in H}\varphi(xy)xy

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in H}\sum_{t\in H}\varphi(t)t=|H|\sum_{t\in H}\varphi(t)t=|H|^{2}P_{H}.

From this we see that
P_{H}
is an orthogonal projection.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Assume that
(\alpha,q)\in\{(\pm 1,\pm 1)\}
.
Then we have that
P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}:=\sum_{\sigma\in B(n)}\varphi_{\alpha,q}(\sigma)\sigma
, where
\varphi_{\alpha,q}(\sigma):=\alpha^{l_{1}(\sigma)}q^{l_{2}(\sigma)}

is a character of the group
B(n)
.
Indeed, we can easily see that in each of the four cases
(\alpha,q)\in\{(\pm 1,\pm 1)\}
, we have

\varphi_{\alpha,q}(\sigma\gamma)=\alpha^{l_{1}(\sigma\gamma)}q^{l_{2}(\sigma\gamma)}=\alpha^{l_{1}(\sigma)}q^{l_{2}(\sigma)}\alpha^{l_{1}(\gamma)}q^{l_{2}(\gamma)}=\varphi_{\alpha,q}(\sigma)\varphi_{\alpha,q}(\gamma).

If these cases
(\alpha,q)\in\{(0,\pm 1),(\pm 1,0)\}
, we obtain also two characters of subgroups
H_{0}=gp\{\pi_{0}\}
and two characters of the permutation group
S(n)
generated by
\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{n-1}\}
.

Now we will specify the range of parameters for which the considered operator is invertible.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
|q|<1
,
|\alpha|<1
and
(\alpha,q)\notin\{(\pm 1,\pm 1),(0,\pm 1),(\pm 1,0)\}
, then considered symmetrization
{P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}}
is invertible and
\ker{P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}}=\{0\}
, as operator on
l^{2}(B(n))
and on
{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}=H^{\otimes 2n}
, for arbitrary
Hilbert space
H
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma follows from
[
13
, Theorem 2.4]
.
∎

Our operator
P^{(n)}_{\alpha,q}
has a nontrivial kernel in the following situation.

Theorem 5.4
.

If
\alpha\in[-1,1]
and
q=\pm 1
or
q\in[-1,1]
and
\alpha=\pm 1
, then the operator
P^{(n)}_{\alpha,q}
has a nontrivial kernel.

Proof.

First we consider the case
\alpha\in[-1,1]
and
q=\pm 1
. Then by well-known result from Coxeter groups (see Remark
5.5
below), we have

P_{\alpha,\pm 1}^{(n)}=T_{1}P_{0,\pm 1}^{(n)},

and the operator
\frac{1}{n!}P_{0,\pm 1}^{(n)}
is the orthogonal projection (see Lemma
5.1
). Therefore the space

V_{0,\pm 1}^{(n)}=\bigg(I-\frac{1}{n!}P_{0,\pm 1}^{(n)}\bigg)({\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n})

is the kernel of
P_{\alpha,\pm 1}^{(n)}
since

P_{\alpha,\pm 1}^{(n)}\big(V_{0,\pm 1}^{(n)}\big)=\frac{1}{n!}T_{1}P_{0,\pm 1}^{(n)}\bigg(I-\frac{1}{n!}P_{0,\pm 1}^{(n)}\bigg)({\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n})=\{0\}.

We proceed in a similar manner when
q\in[-1,1]
and
\alpha=\pm 1
. Then we get form Remark
5.5

P_{\pm 1,q}^{(n)}=T_{2}\frac{1}{2}P_{\pm 1,0}^{(n)}.

Since
\frac{1}{2}P_{\pm 1,0}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{2}(e+\pm\pi_{0})
is the orthogonal projection on
l^{2}(H_{0})
, where
H_{0}=gp\{\pi_{0}\}
. Hence we get that on the subspace

W_{\pm 1,0}^{(n)}=\bigg(I-\frac{1}{2}P_{\pm 1,0}^{(n)}\bigg)({\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}),

we have
P_{\pm 1,0}^{(n)}\big(W_{\pm 1,0}^{(n)}\big)=\{0\}
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

(1)

We use very important result form the Coxeter groups
(W,S)
. If
J\subset S
and
W_{J}=gp(J)
, then there exists decomposition

W=W^{J}W_{J}

(see
[
24
]
). Moreover, if we define for the subset
A\subset W

P_{\underline{q}}^{(n)}(A)=\sum_{w\in A}{\underline{q}}^{l(w)}w,

then
P_{\underline{q}}^{(n)}(W)=P_{\underline{q}}^{(n)}(W_{J})P_{\underline{q}}^{(n)}\big(W^{J}\big)
.
In our case,
\underline{q}=(\alpha,q)
and
{\underline{q}}^{l(w)}={\alpha}^{l_{1}(w)}{q}^{l_{2}(w)}
.

(2)

We also observe that if
(\alpha,q)\in\{(\pm 1,\pm 1)\}
, then
P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}
is homomorphism of
B(n)
, i.e.,
P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})=P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}(\sigma_{1})P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}(\sigma_{2})
, Therefore, the natural extension of
P_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}
to tensor product of Hilbert space
\tilde{P}_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}\colon{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}\to{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}
as was presented in beginning of Section 3. Hence we have identification of linear spaces

{\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}/\ker\tilde{P}_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}\cong\tilde{P}_{\alpha,q}^{(n)}({\bf\mathcal{K}}_{n}).

(3)

Finally, we describe four special tensor products.

The type B-tensor product
is of the form

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!2^{n}}\tilde{P}_{\pm 1,\pm 1}^{(n)}x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}.

In particular cases, we distinguish the following tensors:

(I)

If
\alpha=q=1
, then symmetrization covers all
B-symmetric tensor products
, i.e.,

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!2^{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in B(n)}x_{\sigma(\overline{n})}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)},

i.e., we have

\displaystyle\sigma(x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n})

\displaystyle=x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}.

(II)

If
\alpha=-1
and
q=-1
, then we have the antisymmetric tensor product of type B, i.e.,

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}=\frac{1}{n!2^{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in B(n)}(-1)^{\text{number of inversions in }\sigma}x_{\sigma(\overline{n})}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)}.

(III)

If
\alpha=1
and
q=-1
, then we have the
fermionic tensor product of type B
, i.e.,

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}=\frac{1}{n!2^{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in B(n)}(-1)^{\text{number of positive inversions in }\sigma}x_{\sigma(\overline{n})}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)}.

(IV)

If
\alpha=-1
and
q=1
, then we have the
bosonic tensor product of type B
, i.e.,

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}=\frac{1}{n!2^{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in B(n)}(-1)^{\text{number of negative inversions in }\sigma}x_{\sigma(\overline{n})}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)}.

Next we distinguish yet four situations
(\alpha,q)\in\{(0,\pm 1),(\pm 1,0)\}
, namely

(V)

If
q=0
and
\alpha=\pm 1
, then
P_{\alpha,0}^{(n)}=1+\alpha\pi_{0}
and
\frac{1}{2}\tilde{P}_{\alpha,0}^{(n)}
is a projection. From this we see that

(a)

if
q=0
and
\alpha=-1
, then we don’t have a positive inversion and at most one negative inversion
\pi_{0}
and so we obtain

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\bar{1}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{1}\otimes x_{\bar{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}.

This tensor product might be called the
Boolean tensor product of type B
;

(b)

if
q=0
and
\alpha=1
, then we obtain the
free tensor product of type B

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\bar{1}}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}x_{\overline{n}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{1}\otimes x_{\bar{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}.

(VI)

If
q=\pm 1
and
\alpha=0
, then we have two cases namely a negative inversion does not appear and we obtain the classical fermionic relation for
q=-1
and the bosonic relation for
q=1
, namely

\displaystyle x_{\overline{n}}\tilde{\otimes}\cdots\tilde{\otimes}x_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in B(n)\\
\operatorname{ninv}(\sigma)=0\end{subarray}}(\pm 1)^{\text{number of positive inversions in }\sigma}x_{\sigma(\overline{n})}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)}.

The above cases are interesting and will be considered in future work. The most significant situations appear, when
q=0
and
\alpha=1
as it can be a starting point for creating a new kind of type B free probability.

Abstract
Searches are performed for both prompt-like and long-lived dark photons, A ′ A^{\prime} , produced in proton-proton collisions at a center-of-mass energy of 13 Te ​ V \mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV} . These searches look for A ′ → μ + ​ μ − A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-} decays using a data sample corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 5.5 fb − 1 \mbox{\,fb}^{-1} collected with the LHCb detector. Neither search finds evidence for a signal, and 90% confidence-level exclusion limits are placed on the γ \gamma – A ′ A^{\prime} kinetic-mixing strength. The prompt-like A ′ A^{\prime} search explores the mass region from near the dimuon threshold up to 70 Ge ​ V \mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV} , and places the most stringent constraints to date on dark photons with 214 < m ⁡ ( A ′ ) ≲ 740 ​ Me ​ V 214<m(A^{\prime})\lesssim 740\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV} and 10.6 < m ⁡ ( A ′ ) ≲ 30 ​ Ge ​ V 10.6<m(A^{\prime})\lesssim 30\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV} . The search for long-lived A ′ → μ + ​ μ − A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-} decays places world-leading constraints on low-mass dark photons with lifetimes 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) ​ ps \mathcal{O}(1)\mbox{\,ps} .

This Letter presents searches for both prompt-like and long-lived dark photons produced in proton-proton,

The prompt-like

The
LHCb
detector is a single-arm forward spectrometer covering the pseudorapidity range
2<\eta<5
, described in detail in Refs.
[

The prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search is based on a data sample that employs a novel data-storage strategy, made possible by advances in the LHCb data-taking scheme introduced in 2015
[
88
,
89
]
,
where all online-reconstructed particles are stored, but most lower-level information is discarded, greatly reducing the event size.
In contrast, the data sample used in the long-lived
A^{\prime}
search is derived from the standard LHCb data stream.
Simulated data samples, which are used to validate the analysis, are produced using the software described in Refs.
[
90
,
*Sjostrand:2007gs
,
92
,
93

*Agostinelli:2002hh

.

The online event selection is performed by a trigger
[
95
]
consisting of a hardware stage using information from the calorimeter and muon systems, followed by a software stage that performs a full event
reconstruction.
At the hardware stage, events are required to have a muon with momentum transverse to the beam direction
{\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu)\gtrsim 1.8\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}}
,
or a dimuon pair with
\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu^{+})\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu^{-})\gtrsim(1.5\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV})^{2}
.
The long-lived
A^{\prime}
search also uses events selected at the hardware stage due to the presence of a high-
p_{\rm T}
hadron that is not associated to the
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidate.
In the software stage, where the
p_{\rm T}
resolution is substantially improved
cf.
the hardware stage,

A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates are built from two oppositely charged tracks
that form a good-quality vertex and satisfy stringent muon-identification criteria, though these criteria were loosened considerably in the low-mass region during 2017–2018 data taking.
Both searches require
\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(A^{\prime})>1\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and
2<\eta(\mu)<4.5
.
The prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search uses muons that are consistent with originating from the PV,
with
{\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu)>1.0\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}}
and momentum
{p(\mu)>20\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}}
in 2016, and
\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu)>0.5\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
,
{p(\mu)>10\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}}
, and
{\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu^{+})\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu^{-})>(1.0\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV})^{2}}
in 2017–2018.
The long-lived
A^{\prime}
search uses muons that are inconsistent with originating from any PV with
\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu)>0.5\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and
{p(\mu)>10\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}}
,
and requires
2<\eta(A^{\prime})<4.5

The prompt-like
A^{\prime}
sample is contaminated by
prompt
{\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}

various resonant decays to
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

and by the following types of misreconstruction:
(
hh
) two prompt hadrons misidentified as muons;
(

) a misidentified prompt hadron combined with a muon produced in the decay of a heavy-flavor quark,

and
(

Q
-hadron decays that are both misidentified as prompt.
Contamination from a prompt muon and a misidentified prompt hadron is negligible, though it is accounted for automatically by the method used to determine the sum of the
hh
and
h\mu_{Q}
contributions.
The impact of the
\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
background is reduced,
cf.

[
83

This improves the resolution,
\sigma[m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})]

2
for small
m(A^{\prime})
.
The misreconstructed backgrounds are highly suppressed by the stringent requirements applied in the trigger; however, substantial contributions remain for
m(A^{\prime})\gtrsim 1.1\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
In this mass region, dark photons are expected to be predominantly produced in Drell–Yan processes,
from which they would inherit the well-known signature of dimuon pairs that are largely isolated.
Therefore, the signal sensitivity is enhanced by applying the anti-
k_{\rm T}
-based
[
96
,

98

[

99
]
for

The observed prompt-like
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
yields, which are determined from fits to the
m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
spectrum, are normalized using Eq. (
1

.
The
n_{\rm ob}^{\gamma^{*}}[m(A^{\prime})]
values in Eq. (
1

{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
distributions, where
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu)
is defined as the difference in the vertex-fit
\chi^{2}
when the PV is reconstructed with and without the muon. The
{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
distribution provides excellent discrimination between prompt muons and the displaced muons that constitute the
\mu_{Q}\mu_{Q}
background.
The
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu)
quantity approximately follows a
\chi^{2}
probability density function (PDF), with two degrees of freedom, and therefore, the
{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
distributions have minimal dependence on mass for each source of dimuon candidates.
The prompt-dimuon PDFs are taken directly from data at
m({{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}})
and
m(Z)

Small
corrections are applied to obtain these PDFs at all other
m(A^{\prime})
, which are validated near threshold, at
m(\phi)

m[\mathchar 28935\relax(1S)]

Based on these validation studies, a shape uncertainty of 2% is applied in each
{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
bin.
Same-sign

candidates provide estimates for the PDF and yield of the sum of the
hh
and
h\mu_{Q}
contributions, where each involves misidentified prompt hadrons.
The
\mu^{\pm}\mu^{\pm}

\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\pi^{\pm}\pi^{\pm}

since the
hh

\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
pairs where both pions are misidentified.
The uncertainty due to the finite size of the
\mu^{\pm}\mu^{\pm}
sample in each bin is included in the likelihood.
Simulated

\mu_{Q}\mu_{Q}
PDFs, where the dominant uncertainties are from the relative importance of the various
Q
-hadron decay contributions at each mass.
Example

and

For the long-lived
A^{\prime}
search, contamination from prompt particles is negligible due to a stringent criterion applied in the trigger on
{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
that requires muons be inconsistent with originating from any PV.
Therefore, the dominant background contributions are:
photons that convert into
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
in the silicon-strip vertex detector that surrounds the
pp
interaction region, known as the VELO
[
105

;

b

and the low-mass tail from
{K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

A
p

A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

[
106

.
A
m(A^{\prime})
-dependent requirement is applied to these

The remaining backgrounds are highly suppressed by the decay topology requirement applied in the trigger.
Furthermore, since muons produced in
b
-hadron decays are often accompanied by additional displaced tracks,
events are rejected if they are selected by the inclusive heavy-flavor software trigger
[
107
,
108

independently of the presence of the
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidate.
In addition, boosted decision tree classifiers are used to reject events containing tracks consistent with originating from the same
b
-hadron decay as the signal muon candidates
[
109

\epsilon_{{}^{\gamma^{*}}}^{{}_{A^{\prime}}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]

This approach is validated using simulation, where prompt

decays are used to predict the properties of long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays.
The relative uncertainty on
\epsilon_{{}^{\gamma^{*}}}^{{}_{A^{\prime}}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]

pp

-hadron suppression requirements;
and
candidates that fail, but nearly satisfy, the stringent muon-identification requirements.
The profile likelihood is used to obtain the
p
-values and confidence intervals on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]
.
No significant excess is observed in the long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
search (the three-dimensional data distribution and the background-only pull distributions are provided in Ref.
[
99
]
).

Since the relationship between
\tau(A^{\prime})
and
\varepsilon^{2}
is known at each mass
[
76
]
, the upper limits on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]
are easily translated into limits on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{2}]
.
Regions of the
[m(A^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{2}]
parameter space where the upper limit on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{2}]
is less than
n_{\rm ex}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{2}]
are excluded at 90% CL.
Figure
4
shows that sizable regions of
[m(A^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{2}]
parameter space are excluded, which are much larger than those excluded in Ref.
[
83
]
.

In summary, searches are performed for prompt-like and long-lived dark photons produced in
pp
collisions at a center-of-mass energy of 13
\mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
Both searches look for
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays using a data sample corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 5.5
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
collected with the LHCb detector during 2016–2018.
No evidence for a signal is found in either search, and 90% CL exclusion regions are set on the
\gamma
–
A^{\prime}
kinetic-mixing strength.
The prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search is performed from near the dimuon threshold up to 70
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
, and produces the most stringent constraints on dark photons with
214<m(A^{\prime})\lesssim 740\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
and
10.6<m(A^{\prime})\lesssim 30\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
The long-lived
A^{\prime}
search
is restricted to the mass range
214<m(A^{\prime})<350\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
, where the data sample potentially has sensitivity, and places world-leading constraints on low-mass dark photons with lifetimes
\mathcal{O}(1)\mbox{\,ps}
.
The three-fold increase in integrated luminosity, improved trigger efficiency during 2017–2018 data taking, and improvements in the analysis result in the searches presented in this Letter achieving much better sensitivity to dark photons than the previous LHCb results
[
83
]
.
The prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search achieves a factor of 5 (2) better sensitivity to
\varepsilon^{2}
at low (high) masses than Ref.
[
83
]
,
while the long-lived
A^{\prime}
search provides access to much larger regions of
[m(A^{\prime}),\varepsilon^{2}]
parameter space.

These results demonstrate the excellent sensitivity of the LHCb experiment to dark photons, even using a data sample collected with a hardware-trigger stage that is highly inefficient for low-mass
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays.
The removal of this hardware-trigger stage in Run 3, along with the planned increase in luminosity, should increase the potential yield of
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays in the low-mass region by a factor
\mathcal{O}(100)
compared to the 2016–2018 data sample.
Given that most of the parameter space shown in Fig.
4
would have been accessible if the data sample was only three times larger, these upgrades will greatly increase the dark-photon discovery potential of the LHCb experiment.

Additional details and figures for the prompt-like and long-lived searches are presented in this Supplemental Material.

Isolation Criterion

For masses above the
\phi(1020)
meson mass, dark photons are expected to be predominantly produced in Drell–Yan processes in
pp
collisions at the LHC.
A well-known signature of Drell–Yan production is dimuon pairs that are largely isolated, and a high-mass dark photon would inherit this property.
The signal sensitivity for
m(A^{\prime})>1.1\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
is enhanced by applying the jet-based isolation requirement used in Ref.
[
83
]
.
Jet reconstruction is performed by clustering charged and neutral particle-flow candidates
[
98
]

using the anti-
k_{\rm T}
clustering algorithm
[
96
]
with
R=0.5
as implemented in
FastJet

[
97
]
.
Muons with
\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}(\mu)/\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}({\rm jet})<0.7
are rejected, where the contribution to
\mbox{$p_{\rm T}$}({\rm jet})
from the other muon is excluded if both muons are clustered in the same jet,
as this is found to provide nearly optimal sensitivity for all
m(A^{\prime})>m(\phi)
.

Prompt-Like Fits

The prompt-like fit strategy is the same as described in detail in the Supplemental Material of Ref.
[
83
]
.
This strategy was first introduced in Ref.
[
100
]
, where it is denoted by aic-o.
The
m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
spectrum is scanned in steps of
\sigma[m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})]
/2 searching for
{A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
contributions.
At each mass, a binned extended maximum likelihood fit is performed, and the profile likelihood is used to determine the
p
-value and the confidence interval on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime})]
.
The prompt-like-search trials factor is obtained using pseudoexperiments.
As in Ref.
[
100
]
, each fit is performed in a
\pm 12.5\,\sigma[m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})]
window around the scan-mass value using bins with widths of
\sigma[m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})]/20
.
Near threshold, the energy released in the decay,
\sqrt{m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})^{2}-4m(\mu)^{2}}
, is used instead of the mass since it is easier to model.
The confidence intervals are defined using the
bounded likelihood
approach,
which involves taking
\Delta\log{\mathcal{L}}
relative to zero signal, rather than the best-fit value, if the best-fit signal value is negative.
This approach enforces that only physical (nonnegative) upper limits are placed on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime})]
, and prevents defining exclusion regions that are much better than the experimental sensitivity in cases where a large deficit in the background yield is observed.

The signal models are determined at each
m(A^{\prime})
using a combination of simulated
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays and the widths of the large resonance peaks that are clearly visible in the data.
The background models take as input a large set of potential background components, then the data-driven model-selection process of Ref.
[
100
]
is performed, whose uncertainty is included in the profile likelihood following Ref.
[
101
]
.
Specifically, the method labeled
aic-o
in Ref.
[
100
]
is used, where the log-likelihood of each background model is penalized for its complexity (number of parameters).
The confidence intervals are obtained from the profile likelihoods, including the penalty terms, where the model index is treated as a discrete nuisance parameter, as originally proposed in Ref.
[
101
]
. In this analysis, the set of possible background components includes all Legendre modes with
\ell\leq 10
at every
m(A^{\prime})
.
Additionally, dedicated background components are included for sizable narrow SM resonance contributions.
The use of 11 Legendre modes adequately describes every double-misidentified peaking background that contributes at a significant level, and therefore, these do not require dedicated background components.
In mass regions where such complexity is not required, the data-driven model-selection procedure reduces the complexity which increases the sensitivity to a potential signal contribution.
Therefore, the impact of the background-model uncertainty on the size of the confidence intervals is mass dependent, though on average it is about 30%.
As in Ref.
[
100
]
, all fit regions are transformed onto the interval
[-1,1]
, where the scan
m(A^{\prime})
value maps to zero.
After such a transformation, the signal model is (approximately) an even function;
therefore, odd Legendre modes are orthogonal to the signal component, which means that the presence of odd modes has minimal impact on the variance of
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime})]
.
In the prompt-like fits, all odd Legendre modes up to ninth order are included in every background model, while only a subset of the even modes is selected for inclusion in each fit.

Regions in the mass spectrum where large known resonance contributions are observed are vetoed in the prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search.
Furthermore, the regions near the
\eta^{\prime}
meson and the excited
\mathchar 28935\relax
states (beyond the
\mathchar 28935\relax(3S)
meson) are treated specially.
For example, since it is not possible to distinguish between
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
\eta^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
contributions at
m(\eta^{\prime})
, the
p
-values near this mass are ignored.
The small excess at
m(\eta^{\prime})
is treated as signal when setting the limits on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime})]
, which is conservative in that an
\eta^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
contribution will weaken the constraints on
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays.
The same strategy is used near the excited
\mathchar 28935\relax
masses.

Long-Lived Fits

The long-lived fit strategy is also the same as described in the Supplemental Material to Ref.
[
83
]
.
The signal yields are determined from binned extended maximum likelihood fits performed on all long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates using the three-dimensional feature space of
m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
,
t
, and
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}
.
As in the prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search, a scan is performed in discrete steps of
\sigma[m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})]
/2; however, in this case, discrete steps in
\tau(A^{\prime})
are also considered.
The profile likelihood is again used to obtain the
p
-values and the confidence intervals on
n_{\rm ob}^{A^{\prime}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]
.
The binning scheme involves four bins in
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}
: [0,2], [2,4], [4,6], and [6,8].
Eight bins in
t
are used: [0.2,0.6], [0.6,1.1], [1.1,1.6], [1.6,2.2], [2.2,3.0], [3,5], [5,10], and
>\!10\mbox{\,ps}
.
The binning scheme used for
m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
depends on the scan
m(A^{\prime})
value, and is chosen such that the vast majority of the signal falls into a single bin.
Signal decays mostly have small
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}
values, with about 50% (80%) of
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays satisfying
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}<2\,(4)
.
Background from
b
-hadron decays populates the small
t
region and is roughly uniformly distributed in
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}
, whereas background from
K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
decays is signal-like in
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}
and roughly uniformly distributed in
t
.
Figure
S5
shows the three-dimensional distribution of all long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates.

The expected contribution in each bin from photon conversions is derived from the number of candidates rejected by the conversion criterion.
Two large control data samples are used to develop and validate the modeling of the
b
-hadron and
K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
contributions.
Both contributions are well modeled by second-order polynomials in
\sqrt{m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})^{2}-4m(\mu)^{2}}
.
While no evidence for
t
or
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}
dependence is observed for these parameters in either the
b
-hadron or
K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
control sample, all parameters are allowed to vary independently in each
[t,\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}]
region in the fits used in the long-lived
A^{\prime}
search.

Figure
S6
shows the long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates, along with the pull values obtained from fits performed to the data where no signal contributions are included.
These distributions are consistent with those observed in pseudoexperiments where no signal component is generated.
Specifically, the data are consistent with being predominantly due to
b
-hadron decays at small
t
, and due to
K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
decays for large
t
and
m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})\gtrsim 280\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
The remaining few candidates at large
t
and small
m(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
are likely photon conversions.

N.b.
, the same dominant backgrounds are expected in the upgraded LHCb detector in each region of mass and decay time.

Long-Lived Efficiency Determination

The value of
\epsilon_{{}^{\gamma^{*}}}^{{}_{A^{\prime}}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]
is not unity in the long-lived
A^{\prime}
search, in part because the efficiency depends on the decay time,
t
.
The kinematics are identical for
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and prompt
\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays for
m(A^{\prime})=m(\gamma^{*})
;
therefore, the
t
dependence of
\epsilon_{{}^{\gamma^{*}}}^{{}_{A^{\prime}}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]
is obtained by resampling prompt
\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates as long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays, where all
t
-dependent properties,
e.g.

{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
,
are recalculated based on the resampled decay-vertex locations.
The impact of background contamination in the prompt
\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
sample is shown to be negligible by recomputing
\epsilon_{{}^{\gamma^{*}}}^{{}_{A^{\prime}}}[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]
using same-sign
\mu^{\pm}\mu^{\pm}
candidates, which are purely background, instead of prompt
\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates in this procedure.
The high-precision three-dimensional material map produced from a data sample of secondary hadronic interactions in Ref.
[
106
]
, which forms the basis of the photon-conversion criterion applied in the selection, is used here to determine where each muon would hit active sensors, and thus, have recorded hits in the VELO.
The resolution on the vertex location and other
t
-dependent properties varies strongly with the location of the first VELO hit on each muon track, though this dependence is largely geometric, making rescaling the resolution of prompt tracks straightforward.
Finally, the values used for the decay-fit
\chi^{2}_{\rm DF}
are resampled from the observed distribution for
K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
decays in data, which is similar to that expected for an ideal
\chi^{2}
distribution with three degrees of freedom, to ensure that non-Gaussian effects are properly considered.

This approach is validated using simulation, where prompt
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays are used to predict the properties of long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays.
Figure
S1
shows one such example using
A^{\prime}
decays generated with zero lifetime to predict the radial flight distance of a sample produced with
\tau(A^{\prime})=2\mbox{\,ps}
.
Here, the full long-lived selection is applied except for the photon-conversion criterion, which is validated separately as discussed below.
Since this validation study involves only simulated decays, the material map produced in Ref.
[
106
]
is replaced by the geometry used in the simulation.
The integrated long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
yield predictions agree to
\approx 2\%
with the actual values in the
[m(A^{\prime}),\tau(A^{\prime})]
region explored in the long-lived
A^{\prime}
search.
Both the short-distance turn-on (driven by the
{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
criterion) and long-distance turn-off (driven by the minimum number of VELO hits required to form a track) curves are well described.
The dominant uncertainty here arises due to limited knowledge of how radiation damage has affected the performance of the VELO as a function of the distance from the
pp
interaction region.
This uncertainty is estimated to be 5% by rerunning the resampling method many times under different radiation-damage hypotheses.

To validate the efficiency of the photon-conversion criterion, the predicted efficiency for a dark photon with the same mass and lifetime as the
K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
meson is compared to the efficiency observed in a control data sample of
K^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
decays.
The predicted and observed efficiencies agree to 1%.
Additionally, in Ref.
[
106
]
the expected performance of the photon-conversion criterion was shown to agree with the performance observed in an independent control data sample of photon conversions out to the
\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})
level.
The expected performance here was obtained in a similar manner to how the dark photon efficiencies are obtained, except starting from a pure sample of conversions instead of prompt
\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates.

The looser kinematic, muon-identification, and hardware-trigger requirements applied to long-lived
A^{\prime}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidates,
cf.
prompt-like candidates, also increase the efficiency.
This
t
-independent increase in efficiency is determined using a control data sample of dimuon candidates consistent with originating from the PV, but otherwise satisfying the long-lived criteria.
Specifically,
{\rm min}[\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{IP}}(\mu^{\pm})]
fits like those used to determine
n_{\rm ob}^{\gamma^{*}}[m(A^{\prime})]
in the prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search are run on this control sample and on the subset of its candidates that satisfy the more stringent criteria applied in the prompt-like
A^{\prime}
search.
The decrease in the
\gamma^{*}\!\to\!\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
yield observed when applying the more stringent criteria at each mass is used to determine the
t
-independent increase in efficiency.

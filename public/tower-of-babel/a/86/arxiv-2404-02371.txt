Abstract
. We study piecewise injective, but not necessarily globally injective, contracting maps on a compact subset of ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} . We prove that generically the attractor and the set of discontinuities of such a map are disjoint, and hence the attractor consists of periodic orbits. In addition, we prove that piecewise injective contractions are generically topologically stable.



1.
Introduction

Studying the dynamical properties of discontinuous hyperbolic dynamical systems is important to understand many relevant systems (like billiards and optimal control theory etc.) but it is difficult if the system is multi-dimensional. A first step to address this problem can be to divide the problem into two cases: the piecewise expanding case and the piecewise contracting case. Here we address the case of piecewise contracting systems. Since the attractor can differ drastically depending on the kind of piecewise contraction, a first approach is to identify a large class of piecewise contractions that exhibit similar dynamical properties.

4.
Density

We will prove Theorem
2.17
in two steps. First we show that Markov maps are dense in a special class of systems (piecewise strongly contracting) and then we will show that such a class is itself dense in the collection of piecewise smooth contractions.

Definition 4.1

(Piecewise strongly contracting)
.

A piecewise smooth contraction
f
with contraction coefficient
\lambda
and maximal partition
{\boldsymbol{P}}(f)=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{m}\}
is said to be piecewise strongly contracting if there exists
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lambda^{p}m_{p}<1/2
, where
m_{p}=\#{\boldsymbol{P}}(f^{p})
.

We will prove the following results:

Proposition 4.2
.

Markov maps are
d_{2}
-dense in the collection of piecewise strong contractions.

Proposition 4.3
.

Piecewise strong contractions are
d_{2}
-dense in the collection of piecewise smooth contractions.

These two Propositions readily imply our main result.

Proof of Theorem
2.17
.

Let
f
be a piecewise smooth contraction. By Proposition
4.3
, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a piecewise strong contraction
f_{1}
such that
d_{2}(f,f_{1})<\varepsilon/2
. In addition, by Proposition
4.2
, there exists a piecewise smooth Markov contraction
f_{2}
such that
d_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})<\varepsilon/2
, hence the result.
∎

In the rest of the paper, we prove Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
.

The basic idea of the proof is to introduce iterated function systems (IFS) associated with the map. The attractor of the IFS is greater than the one of the map (see section
4.1
for the relation between the two sets) hence if we can prove that the attraction of the IFS is disjoint from the discontinuities of the map, so will be the attractor fo the map. The advantage is that, in this way, the study of the boundaries of the elements of
{\boldsymbol{P}}(f^{n})
is reduced to the study of the pre-images of the discontinuities of
f
under the IFS. Hence we can iterate smooth maps rather than discontinuous ones.

This advantage is first exploited in the section
4.2
, where we prove Proposition
4.2
using an argument that is, essentially, a quantitative version of Sard’s theorem.

To prove Proposition
4.3
, the rough idea is to use a transversality Theorem (see Appendix
B
) to show that if a lot of pre-images intersect, then, generically, their intersection should have smaller and smaller dimensions till no further intersection is generically possible. Unfortunately, if we apply a transversality theorem to a composition of maps of the IFS, we get a perturbation of the composition and not of the single maps. How to perturb the single maps in such a way that the composition has the wanted properties is not obvious.

Our solution to this problem is to make sure that if we perturb the maps in a small neighbourhood
B
, and we consider arbitrary compositions of the perturbed maps, then all the images of
B
along the composition never intersect
B
. Hence, if we restrict the composition to
B
, all the maps, except the first, will behave as their unperturbed version. To ensure this, it suffices to prove that such compositions have no fixed points near the singularity manifolds (such implication is proven in Lemma
4.10
).
To this end, in Propositions
4.11

4.17

Lemma C.1
.

There exists
C_{\#}>0
such that, for given

and

\begin{split}&\left\|h^{-1}-id\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\leq 6\varepsilon\\
&\left\|h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}<C_{\#}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}\varepsilon.\end{split}

Proof.

First we claim that
\left\|h-id\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}<\varepsilon
implies
\left\|h^{-1}-id\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}<2\varepsilon
.
Indeed there there exists a transformation
A
, with
\left\|A\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}<1
, such that we can write

therefore

(Dh)^{-1}=({\mathbbm{1}}+A)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\varepsilon^{k}A^{k}.

That is,

\begin{split}&\left\|(Dh)^{-1}-{\mathbbm{1}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\|A\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}^{k}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\leq 2\varepsilon\\
&\left\|D(Dh)^{-1}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\|A\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}^{k}\leq 2\varepsilon.\end{split}

Moreover, the inverse function theorem implies
h^{-1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
. Accordingly, since
Dh^{-1}=(Dh)^{-1}\circ h^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|Dh^{-1}-{\mathbbm{1}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\left\|((Dh)^{-1}-h)\circ h^{-1}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\left\|((Dh)^{-1}-{\mathbbm{1}})\circ h^{-1}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\left\|(Dh)^{-1}-{\mathbbm{1}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}<2\varepsilon

\displaystyle\left\|Dh^{-1}-{\mathbbm{1}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+\left\|D(Dh)^{-1}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\left\|Dh^{-1}\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq 6\varepsilon.

Hence,

\begin{split}&\bigl\|h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq\varepsilon+\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\varepsilon\\
&\bigl\|D(h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f)\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\bigl\|Dh\circ f\circ h^{-1}\cdot Df\circ h^{-1}\cdot Dh^{-1}-Df)\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\\
&\phantom{\bigl\|D(h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f)\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=}\leq\bigl\|Df\circ h^{-1}-Df\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}+C_{\#}\varepsilon\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\leq C_{\#}\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\varepsilon.\end{split}

Next,

\begin{split}\bigl\|\partial_{x}D(h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f)\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}&=\bigl\|(\partial_{y}Dh)\circ f\circ h^{-1}(\partial_{x}f)_{y}\circ h^{-1}\partial_{x}(h^{-1}_{z})D(f\circ h^{-1})\\
&\phantom{=}+Dh\circ f\circ h^{-1}(\partial_{y}Df)\circ h^{-1}\partial_{x}(h^{-1}_{y})Dh^{-1}\\
&\phantom{=}+Dh\circ f\circ h^{-1}Df\circ h^{-1}\partial_{x}(Dh^{-1})-\partial_{x}Df\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\\
&\leq\bigl\|(\partial_{x}Df)\circ h^{-1}-\partial_{x}Df\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}+C_{\#}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\varepsilon\\
&\leq C_{\#}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}\varepsilon.\end{split}

Thus

\begin{split}\left\|h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}=&\left\|h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}+\left\|D(h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f)\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\\
&+\left\|D^{2}(h\circ f\circ h^{-1}-f)\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq C_{\#}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}\varepsilon.\end{split}

for some constant
C_{\#}>0
. Finally
h\circ f\circ h^{-1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
being the composition of
{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
function.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

For given
\varepsilon>0
, let
g,h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
be such that
g
is invertible,
\left\|g-h\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}<\varepsilon
then
g^{-1}\circ h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
and

\left\|g^{-1}\circ h-id\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\leq C_{\#}\varepsilon\|g^{-1}\|^{3}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}.

Additionally for
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
,
f\circ g^{-1}\circ h,(f\circ g^{-1}\circ h)^{-1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
and

\left\|f\circ g^{-1}\circ h-id\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\leq C_{\#}\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}\varepsilon\|g^{-1}\|^{3}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}

Proof.

Since
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
is invertible, by inverse function theorem
g^{-1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
and therefore being a composition of
{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
functions
g^{-1}\circ h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
.
Next, let
\Psi=g^{-1}\circ h
. Then

\begin{split}&\|\Psi-id\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\|g^{-1}\circ h-g^{-1}\circ g\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\varepsilon\\
&\|D(\Psi-id)\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\|(Dg)^{-1}\circ\Psi\cdot Dh-{\mathbbm{1}}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\varepsilon\\
&\|\partial_{x}D(\Psi-id)\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\|\partial_{y}[(Dg)^{-1}]\circ\Psi\partial_{x}\Psi_{y}\cdot Dh+(Dg)^{-1}\circ\Psi\cdot\partial_{x}Dh\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\\
&\leq\|\partial_{y}[(Dg)^{-1}]\circ\Psi\partial_{x}\Psi_{y}\cdot Dg+(Dg)^{-1}\circ\Psi\cdot\partial_{x}Dg\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}+C_{\#}\varepsilon\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq\|\partial_{x}[(Dg)^{-1}\circ\Psi\cdot Dg]\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}+C_{\#}\varepsilon\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq\|\partial_{x}[(Dg)^{-1}\cdot Dg]\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}+C_{\#}\varepsilon\|(Dg)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}^{3}=C_{\#}\varepsilon\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}^{3}.\end{split}

Accordingly,
\|\Psi-id\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\leq C_{\#}\varepsilon\|(Dg)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}
. Moreover, the first part of Lemma
C.1
implies that
\Psi
is invertible and
\|\Psi^{-1}-id\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\leq C_{\#}\varepsilon\|(Dg)^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}
.
This implies,
f\circ\Psi,\Psi^{-1}\circ f^{-1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{3}
and

\begin{split}&\left\|f\circ\Psi-f\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq C_{\#}\varepsilon\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1}}\\
&\left\|D(f\circ\Psi-f)\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\bigl\|(Df)\circ\Psi\cdot D\Psi-Df\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\leq C_{\#}\varepsilon\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\left\|\partial_{x}D(f\circ\Psi-f)\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}=\left\|[\partial_{y}(Df)]\circ\Psi\cdot\partial_{x}\Psi_{y}\cdot D\Psi+(Df)\circ\Psi\cdot\partial_{x}D\Psi-\partial_{x}Df\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}\\
&\phantom{\left\|\partial_{x}D(f\circ\Psi-f)\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{0}}}\leq C_{\#}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}\|g^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{3}}^{3},\end{split}

from which the Lemma follows.
∎

A Curry-Howard Approach to Church’s Synthesis \rsuper *
Pierre Pradic \rsuper a,b, Colin Riba \rsuper a

Abstract
. Church’s synthesis problem asks whether there exists a finite-state stream transducer satisfying a given input-output specification. For specifications written in Monadic Second-Order Logic ( 𝖬𝖲𝖮 \mathsf{MSO} ) over infinite words, Church’s synthesis can theoretically be solved algorithmically using automata and games. We revisit Church’s synthesis via the Curry-Howard correspondence by introducing 𝖲𝖬𝖲𝖮 \mathsf{SMSO} , an intuitionistic variant of 𝖬𝖲𝖮 \mathsf{MSO} over infinite words, which is shown to be sound and complete w.r.t. synthesis thanks to an automata-based realizability model.

In this work, we propose a Curry-Howard approach to Church’s synthesis.
The Curry-Howard correspondence asserts that, given a suitable
proof system, any proof therein can be interpreted as a program.
This interpretation of proofs as programs

can be formalized using the technique of

which tells

More precisely, realizability can be seen as a relation
between programs (the realizers) and formulae,
usually defined by induction on the latter (see e.g.

Typical clauses state e.g. that realizers of conjunctions

3.1.
A Glivenko Theorem for
\mathsf{MSO}

The limited vocabulary of
\mathsf{MSO}
without primitive universal quantifications
allows for a Glivenko Theorem,
in the sense that
\mathsf{SMSO}
proves
\lnot\lnot\varphi
iff
\mathsf{MSO}
proves
\varphi
.
While Glivenko’s Theorem is often stated for propositional logic,
it also holds in presence of existential quantifications
(see e.g.
[
Kle52
, Thm. 59.(b), §81]
or
[
Koh08
, §10.1]
),
but does not extend to universal quantifications
(see e.g.
[
Koh08
, §11]
).
In particular, we would have relied on
usual recursive negative translations if
\mathsf{MSO}
had primitive universal quantifications.

Theorem 12

(Glivenko’s Theorem for
\mathsf{MSO}
and
\mathsf{SMSO}
)
.

If
\mathsf{MSO}\vdash\varphi
, then
\mathsf{SMSO}\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi
.

Proof 3.1
.

By induction on
\mathsf{MSO}
-derivations, we show that if

\overline{\varphi}\vdash\varphi
is derivable in
\mathsf{MSO}
,
then

\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi

is derivable in
\mathsf{SMSO}
.
This amounts to showing that for every
\mathsf{MSO}
-rule of the form

\dfrac{{(\overline{\varphi}_{i}\vdash\varphi_{i})}_{i\in I}}{\overline{\psi}\vdash\psi}

the following rule is admissible in
\mathsf{SMSO}
:

\dfrac{{(\overline{\varphi}_{i}\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi_{i})}_{i\in I}}{\overline{\psi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\psi}

The logical rules of
\mathsf{MSO}

may be treated exactly as in the usual proof of Glivenko’s Theorem.
It remains to deal with the non-logical rules of
\mathsf{MSO}
.

Comprehension:

We have to prove that the following is admissible in
\mathsf{SMSO}
:

\dfrac{\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\psi[\varphi[y]/X]}{\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\exists X\,\psi}

But this directly follows from the negative comprehension scheme of
\mathsf{SMSO}

together with the last rule of (
9
):

\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\psi[\varphi[y]/X]

\overline{\varphi},\psi[\varphi[y]/X]\vdash\psi[\varphi[y]/X]

\overline{\varphi},\psi[\varphi[y]/X]\vdash\lnot\lnot\exists X\,\psi

\overline{\varphi},\lnot\lnot\psi[\varphi[y]/X]\vdash\lnot\lnot\exists X\,\psi

\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\exists X\,\psi

Induction:

We need to show that the following is admissible in
\mathsf{SMSO}
:

\dfrac{\overline{\varphi},\mathsf{Z}(z)\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[z/x]\qquad\overline{\varphi},\mathsf{S}(y,z),\varphi[y/x]\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[z/x]}{\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi}

But this follows from deterministic induction together
with the last rule of (
9
):

\overline{\varphi},\mathsf{Z}(z)\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[z/x]

\overline{\varphi},\mathsf{S}(y,z),\varphi[y/x]\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[z/x]

\overline{\varphi},\mathsf{S}(y,z),\lnot\lnot\varphi[y/x]\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[z/x]

\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi

Arithmetic Rules (Fig.
5
):

All these rules can be treated the same way.
We only detail the case of elimination of equality.
We have to show that the following rule is admissible in
\mathsf{SMSO}
:

\dfrac{\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[x/z]\qquad\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot(x\doteq y)}{\overline{\varphi}\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[y/z]}

(10)

First, note that elimination of equality in
\mathsf{SMSO}
gives

\dfrac{}{\overline{\varphi},\varphi[x/z],x\doteq y\vdash\varphi[y/z]}

from which (
9
) gives

\dfrac{}{\overline{\varphi},\lnot\lnot\varphi[x/z],\lnot\lnot(x\doteq y)\vdash\lnot\lnot\varphi[y/z]}

We then obtain the rule (
10
) by successively cutting
\lnot\lnot\varphi[x/z]

and
\lnot\lnot(x\doteq y)

with the corresponding premise of (
10
).

3.2.
The Main Result

We are now ready to state the main result of this paper,
which says that
\mathsf{SMSO}
is correct and complete (w.r.t. its provable existentials)

.

Consider a formula
\varphi(\overline{X};\overline{Y})

\overline{X},\overline{Y}

(1)

\mathsf{SMSO}

(2)

If
\varphi(\overline{X};\overline{Y})
admits a (finite-state) synchronous Church-realizer,
then

\exists\overline{Y}\,\lnot\lnot\varphi(\overline{X};\overline{Y})

is provable in
\mathsf{SMSO}
.

The correctness part (

for
\mathsf{SMSO}
based on automata and synchronous finite-state

The completeness part (

of
\mathsf{MSO}
(Thm.
7

of the negative translation
\lnot\lnot(-)
(Thm.

Theorem

The Recursion Theorem

\forall z\forall\overline{Z}\forall\overline{Z}^{\prime}\left(\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq q}\forall y\mathrel{\dot{<}}z\left(Z_{i}y\longleftrightarrow Z^{\prime}_{i}y\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \penalty\ \bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq q}\left(\psi_{i}[\overline{Z}/\overline{X},z/x]\longleftrightarrow\psi_{i}[\overline{Z}^{\prime}/\overline{X},z/x]\right)\right)

\vec{\psi(\overline{X},x)}

\mathsf{MSO}

there are unique

4.4.
Semantically Bounded Formulae

The synchronous comprehension scheme of
\mathsf{MSO}
is motivated by

17

that uniformly bounded formulae induce DMMs.
Recall from Def.

is of the form
\psi\mathord{\upharpoonright}[-\leq x]

Uniform boundedness is a purely syntactic restriction on comprehension,
which has the advantage of being
easy to check and conceptually simple to interpret in a proof relevant semantics.
We present here a more semantic criterion on the formulae for which
comprehension remains sound in a synchronous setting.
We call a formula

\psi(\overline{X},x)

\forall z\forall\overline{Z}\overline{Z^{\prime}}\left(\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq q}\forall y\mathrel{\dot{\leq}}z\big(Z_{i}y\longleftrightarrow Z^{\prime}_{i}y\big)\penalty\ \penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \penalty\ \big(\psi[\overline{Z}/\overline{X},z/x]\longleftrightarrow\psi[\overline{Z^{\prime}}/\overline{X},z/x]\big)\right)

Since all uniformly bounded formulae are obviously semantically bounded, we

5.
The Realizability Interpretation of
\mathsf{SMSO}

We now present our realizability model for
\mathsf{SMSO}
,
and use it to prove Thm.
13
.(
1
). This realizability interpretation bears some similarities
with usual realizability constructions
for the Curry-Howard correspondence
(see e.g.
[

For instance, as in the usual setting,
a realizer of a formula
\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}

is a pair

of a realizer of
R_{1}

of
\varphi_{1}
and a realizer

\varphi_{2}
.
Similarly, a realizer of

is a pair
{\langle B,R\rangle}
of an
\omega

and a realizer

of
\varphi(B)

However, our construction departs from the standard one
on negation (for which we use McNaughton’s Theorem
5
),
and for the fact that there is no primitive notion of implication in
\mathsf{SMSO}
.
In particular, in contrast with the usual settings,
our notion of realizability for sequents of the form

\psi\vdash\varphi
(see Thm.
27

)
is not based on a notion of implication internal to the logic under consideration.

Our approach to Church’s synthesis via realizability uses automata
in two different ways.
First, from a
proof

\mathscr{D}
in
\mathsf{SMSO}
of an existential

\exists{\overline{Y}}\varphi(\overline{X};\overline{Y})

one can compute a finite-state synchronous Church-realizer
\overline{F}
of
\varphi(\overline{X};\overline{Y})
.
Second, the adequacy of realizability
(and in particular the correctness of
\overline{F}
w.r.t.
\varphi(\overline{X};\overline{Y})
)
is
proved
using automata for
\varphi(\overline{X};\overline{Y})

obtained by McNaughton’s Theorem,
but these automata do not have to be built during the extraction procedure.

6.
Indexed Structure on Automata

In §
5
we have defined one category
\mathsf{Aut}_{\Sigma}

for each alphabet
\Sigma
.
These categories are actually related by
substitution functors

arising from
\mathbf{M}
-morphisms, inducing an
indexed

(or
fibred
) structure.
Substitution functors are a basic notion of categorical logic,
which allows for categorical axiomatizations of quantifications.
We refer to e.g.
[
Jac01
, Chap. 1]
for background.

We present here the fibred structure of the categories
\mathsf{Aut}_{(-)}

and show that the existential quantifiers
\exists_{(-)}
and Cartesian
product
(-)\times(-)
of §
5.2

satisfy the expected properties of existential quantifiers and conjunction
in categorical logic.
These properties essentially correspond to the adequacy of the logical
rules of Fig.
3
that do not mention negation (
\lnot
)
nor falsity (
\bot
).
Although the fibred structure is not technically necessary to prove
the adequacy of our realizability model,
following such categorical axiomatization was a guideline in its design.
Besides, categorical logic turns out to be an essential tool
when dealing with generalizations to (say) alternating automata.

6.1.
The Basic Idea

Before entering the details, let us try to explain the main ideas
in the
usual setting of first-order logic
over a manysorted individual language.
The categorical semantics of existential quantifications
is given by an adjunction

\exists x\,\varphi(x)\vdash\psi

(
x
not free in
\psi
)

\varphi(x)\vdash\psi

(18)

This adjunction
induces a bijection
between (the interpretations of) proofs
of the sequents
\varphi(x)\vdash\psi

and
\exists x\,\varphi(x)\vdash\psi
,
that we informally denote

\varphi(x)\vdash\psi\qquad\simeq\qquad\exists x\,\varphi(x)\vdash\psi

Now, in general the variable
x
occurs free in
\varphi
.
As a consequence,
in order to properly formulate (
18
)
one should be able to interpret sequents of the form

\varphi(x)\vdash\psi
with free variables.
More generally, the formulae
\varphi
and
\psi

should be allowed to contain free variables distinct from
x
.

The idea underlying the
general method (see e.g.
[
Jac01
]
for details),
is to first devise a base category
\mathbb{B}
of individuals,
whose objects interpret products of sorts
of the individual language,
and whose maps from say
\iota_{1}\times\cdots\times\iota_{m}

to
o_{1}\times\cdots\times o_{n}

represent
n
-tuples
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
of terms
t_{i}

of sort
o_{i}

whose free variables are among

x_{\iota_{1}},\dots,x_{\iota_{m}}
,
with
x_{\iota_{j}}
of sort
\iota_{j}
.
Then,
for each object
\iota=\iota_{1}\times\cdots\times\iota_{m}
of
\mathbb{B}
,
one devises a category
\mathbb{E}_{\iota}
whose objects represent formulae
with free variables among
x_{\iota_{1}},\dots,x_{\iota_{m}}
,
and whose morphisms interpret proofs.
Furthermore,
\mathbb{B}
-morphisms

t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\quad:\quad\iota_{1}\times\cdots\times\iota_{m}\quad\longrightarrow\quad o_{1}\times\cdots\times o_{n}

induce
substitution functors

{t^{\star}}\quad:\quad\mathbb{E}_{o_{1}\times\cdots\times o_{n}}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\mathbb{E}_{\iota_{1}\times\cdots\times\iota_{m}}

The functor
{t^{\star}}
takes
(the interpretation of) a formula
\varphi

whose free variables are among

y_{o_{1}},\dots,y_{o_{n}}

to (the interpretation of) the formula

\varphi[t_{1}/y_{o_{1}},\dots,t_{n}/y_{o_{n}}]

with free variables among

x_{\iota_{1}},\dots,x_{\iota_{m}}
.
Its action on the morphisms of
\mathbb{E}_{o_{1}\times\cdots\times o_{n}}

allows us to interpret the
substitution rule

\dfrac{\varphi\vdash\psi}{\varphi[t_{1}/y_{o_{1}},\dots,t_{n}/y_{o_{n}}]\vdash\psi[t_{1}/y_{o_{1}},\dots,t_{n}/y_{o_{n}}]}

In very good situations, the operation
{(-)^{\star}}

is itself functorial.
Among the morphisms of
\mathbb{B}
,
one usually requires the existence of projections, say

\pi\quad:\quad o\times\iota\quad\longrightarrow\quad o

Projections induce substitution functors, called
weakening
functors

{\pi^{\star}}\quad:\quad\mathbb{E}_{o}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\mathbb{E}_{o\times\iota}

which simply allow us to see formula
\psi(y_{o})
with free variable
y_{o}

as a formula
\psi(y_{o},x_{\iota})
with free variables among
y_{o},x_{\iota}

(but with no actual occurrence of
x_{\iota}
).
Then the proper formulation of (
18
)
is that existential quantification over
x_{\iota}
is a functor

\exists x_{\iota}\,(-)\quad:\quad\mathbb{E}_{o\times\iota}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\mathbb{E}_{o}

which is left-adjoint to
{\pi^{\star}}
:

\exists x_{\iota}\,\varphi(x_{\iota},y_{o})\vdash\psi(y_{o})

\varphi(x_{\iota},y_{o})\vdash{\pi^{\star}}(\psi)(x_{\iota},y_{o})

(where

x_{\iota}
does not occur free in
\psi

since
\psi
is assumed to be (interpreted as) an object of
\mathbb{E}_{o}
,
thus replacing the usual side condition).
Universal quantifications are dually axiomatized as right adjoints
to weakening functors.
In both cases, the adjunctions are subject to additional conditions
(called the
Beck-Chevalley
conditions) which ensure that
they are preserved by substitution.

Conclusion

In this paper, we
revisited Church’s synthesis via
an automata-based realizability interpretation of
an intuitionistic proof system
\mathsf{SMSO}
for
\mathsf{MSO}
on
\omega

and we
demonstrated that our approach is sound and complete,
in the sense of Thm.
13

As it stands, this approach must still pay the price of the non-elementary
lower-bound for the translation of
\mathsf{MSO}
formulae over finite words
to DFAs
(see Rem.
18
)
and the system
\mathsf{SMSO}

is limited by its set of connectives and its restricted induction scheme.

Further Works

First, the indexed structure (§
6.5
)
induced by the substitution operation of §

suggests that in our context, it may be profitable
to work in a conservative extension of
\mathsf{(S)MSO}

with one function symbol for each Mealy machine
together with defining axioms of the form (

In particular, this could help mitigate Rem.

\mathsf{SMSO}

to give a term for a Mealy machine rather than the
\mathsf{MSO}
-formula
representing it.
We expect this to give better lower bounds w.r.t. completeness (for each solvable instance of Church’s synthesis, to provide proofs
with realizers of a reasonable complexity).

Second,
following the approach of
[
Rib16
]
,
\mathsf{SMSO}
could be extended
with primitive universal quantifications and implications
as soon as one goes
to a
linear

Among outcomes of going to a linear deduction system,

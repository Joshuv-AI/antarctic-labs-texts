Koszul duality, minimal model and L ∞ L_{\infty} -structure for differential algebras with weight
Jun Chen, Li Guo, Kai Wang and Guodong Zhou

Abstract
. A differential algebra with weight is an abstraction of both the derivation (weight zero) and the forward and backward difference operators (weight ± 1 \pm 1 ). In 2010 Loday established the Koszul duality for the operad of differential algebras of weight zero. He did not treat the case of nonzero weight, noting that new techniques are needed since the operad is no longer quadratic. This paper continues Loday’s work and establishes the Koszul duality in the case of nonzero weight. In the process, the minimal model and the Koszul dual homotopy cooperad of the operad governing differential algebras with weight are determined. As a consequence, a notion of homotopy differential algebras with weight is obtained and the deformation complex as well as its L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra structure for differential algebras with weight are deduced.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we develop the Koszul duality theory for differential algebras with nonzero weight by determining the minimal model and the Koszul homotopy dual cooperad of the corresponding operad as well as the
L_{\infty}
-structure on the deformation complex, thereby solving a problem raised by Loday
[
35
]
.

1.1.
Differential algebras with weight

For a fixed scalar
\lambda
, a
differential operator of weight
\lambda

[
20
]
(called
\lambda
-derivation in
[
35
]
) is a linear operator
d=d_{\lambda}
on an associative algebra
A
satisfying the operator identity

(1)

d(uv)=d(u)v+ud(v)+\lambda d(u)d(v),\quad u,v\in A.

When
\lambda=0
, this is the Leibniz rule of the derivation in analysis. When
\lambda\neq 0
, this is the operator identity satisfied by the differential quotient
d_{\lambda}(f)(x):=(f(x+\lambda)-f(x))/\lambda
in the definition of the derivation. The special cases of
\lambda=\pm 1
gives the forward and backward difference operators in numerical analysis and other areas. An associative algebra with a differential operator of weight
\lambda
is called a
differential algebra of weight
\lambda
.

Traditionally, a differential algebra is referring to a differential commutative algebra or a field of weight zero, originated nearly a century ago from the pioneering work of Ritt
[
51
]
in his algebraic study of differential equations.
Through the works of Kaplansky, Kolchin, Magid, Singer and many others
[
25
,
26
,
38
,
50
]
, the subject has evolved into a well-established area in mathematics. In theory its directions include differential Galois theory, differential algebraic geometry and differential algebraic groups. Its importance is also reflected by its connections with broad areas from arithmetic geometry and logic to computer science and mathematical physics, such as noncommutative Geometry, quantum Fields and motives
[
7
]
, differential algebraic geometry
[
11
]
, dynamical systems
[
43
]
, and mechanical theorem-proving
[
58
]
.

A commutative differential algebra naturally gives rise to a Novikov algebra by the well-known work of Gelfand and Dorfman
[
12
]
, see also
[
27
]
. Differential algebras are also closed related to newly emerged structures including Novikov-Poisson algebras
[
59
]
, transposed Poisson algebras
[
3
]
, anti pre-Lie (Poisson) algebras
[
34
]
, and Novikov bialgebras
[
23
]
.

More generally, differential algebras without the commutativity condition have attracted the interest of many recent studies. Derivations on path algebras and universal enveloping algebras of differential Lie algebras were studied in
[
21
,
47
,
48
]
.
From the categorical viewpoint, differential categories were introduced and then studies in
[
4
,
6
,
33
]
.
In
[
2
]
, the notion of a differential algebra was generalized to non(anti)commutative superspace by deformation in the study of instantons in string theory.

1.2.
Cohomology and deformations of differential algebras

Beginning with the pioneering works of Gerstenhaber for associative algebras and of Nijenhuis and Richardson for Lie algebras
[
13
,
14
,
46
]
, deformation, cohomology and homotopy theory have been fundamental in understanding algebraic structures.
A general philosophy, as evolved from ideas of Gerstenhaber, Nijenhuis, Richardson, Deligne, Schlessinger, Stasheff, Goldman, Millson etc, is that the deformation theory of any given
mathematical object can be described by
a certain differential graded (dg) Lie algebra or more generally a
L_{\infty}
-algebra associated to the
mathematical object (whose underlying complex is called the deformation complex). This philosophy has been made into a theorem in characteristic zero by Lurie
[
37
]
and Pridham
[
49
]
, expressed in terms of infinity categories. It is an important problem to explicitly construct the dg Lie algebra or
L_{\infty}
-algebra governing the deformation theory of the given mathematical object. For algebraic structures (operads) with binary operations, especially the binary quadratic operads, there is a general theory in which the deformations and homotopy of an algebraic structure are controlled by a dg Lie algebra naturally coming from the operadic Koszul duality for the given algebraic structure; see
[
18
,
19
]
for the original literature and
[
36
,
42
]
for general treatises.

Studies of cohomology and homotopy for algebra structures with linear operators have become very active recently. The structures include differential operators and Rota-Baxter operators on associative and Lie algebras. The developments began with
[
54
]
which defined the controlling cohomologie as well as the dg Lie algebra for relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras. Subsequently,
the controlling cohomologie as well as the
L_{\infty}
-structure for relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras were found in
[
32
]
;
derivations (=differential operators of weight zero) on Lie algebras were considered and their cohomology theory was introduced in
[
55
]
;
a cohomology theory of crossed homomorphisms (=differential operators of weight
1
) on Lie algebras was established in
[
39
]
, together with a Maurer-Cartan characterisation.
The reference
[
22
]
introduced a cohomology theory of differential associative algebras of arbitrary weight by an ad hoc method. The paper is a predecessor of the present one and whose results will be recalled in Subsection
2.1
.

Due to the complexity of the algebraic structures, the
L_{\infty}
-algebras in these studies so far have been obtained by direct constructions or by using the derived bracket technique without using the language of operads.

The first operadic study of algebras with linear operators is the 2010 work of Loday
[
35
]
on differential algebras of weight zero. Since this operad is quadratic, it can be treated applying the above-mentioned general theory of Koszul duality
[
18
,
19
]
. In particular, Loday obtained the corresponding minimal model and cohomology theory. For further studies, see works
[
9
,
24
,
31
]
.
For the case of nonzero weight, Loday made the following observation in
[
35
]
, where the operad
\lambda
-AsDer is just the operad of differential algebras of weight
\lambda
.

If the parameter
\lambda
is different from 0, then the operad
\lambda
-AsDer is not a quadratic operad since the term
d(a)d(b)
needs three generating operations to
be defined. So one needs new techniques to extend Koszul duality to this case.

The goal of this paper is to address this problem observed by Loday.

1.3.
Our approach and layout of the paper

One of the most important consequences of the general Koszul duality theory for a quadratic operad is that the theory directly gives the minimal model
[
19
,
18
]
, that is, the cobar construction of the Koszul dual cooperad. Then the deformation complex as well as its
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure (in fact a dg Lie algebra in the Koszul case) could be deduced from the minimal model
[
28
]
.

With the absence of the quadratic property for differential algebras with nonzero weight, the existing general Koszul duality theory does not apply, as noted by Loday as above. The purpose of this work is provide such a theory for differential algebras with nonzero weight, in hope to shed light on a general theory beyond the quadratic operads.

To get started, we use a previous work
[
22
]
, in which a cohomology theory of differential algebras with nonzero weight was constructed from a specifically constructed cochain complex, and was shown to control the formal deformations as well as abelian extensions of the differential algebras. We first determine the
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on this deformation complex, in an ad hoc way, such that differential algebras with weight are realized as Maurer-Cartan elements for the
L_{\infty}
-algebra. This suggests that the
L_{\infty}
-algebra should come from the minimal model of the operad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
of differential algebras with weight. Thus instead of verifying the
L_{\infty}
property directly, we put this
L_{\infty}
-algebra in a suitable operadic context that is comparable to the weight zero case obtained in Loday’s work
[
35
]
.
We introduce homotopy differential algebras with nonzero weight and show that their operad is indeed the minimal model of the operad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
. As a byproduct, we obtain the Koszul dual homotopy cooperad of
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
.

Her is an outline of the paper.

In §
2
, after recalling in §
2.1
the needed notions and background on the cohomology theory developed in
[
22
]
, we propose an
L_{\infty}
structure on the deformation complex for differential algebras with nonzero weight (Theorem
2.2
) in §
2.2
. In §
2.3
, we show that this
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure is the right one that realizes differential algebras with weight as the Maurer-Cartan elements (Proposition
2.7
), while holding off the verification of the

property to the end of paper, until the operadic tools are developed.

In §
3
, once the required basics on homotopy (co)operads are collected in §
3.1
, we construct in §
3.2
a homotopy cooperad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}^{\mathrm{\textup{!`}}}
, called the Koszul dual homotopy cooperad of the operad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
of differential algebras with weight because its cobar construction is shown to be the minimal model of
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
(Theorem
4.4
).

In §
4
, we first introduce the dg operad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}_{\infty}}
of homotopy differential algebras with weight in §
4.1
. We establish in §
4.2
that the dg operad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}_{\infty}}

(Theorem

). We give the notion of homotopy differential algebras and its explicit description (Definition

and Proposition

) in §

.

In §

5.1
and

) in §

.

2.
The
L_{\infty}
-algebra controlling deformations of differential algebras

In this section, we first recall the cohomology theory of differential algebras with weight, as developed in
[
22
]
with some modification in sign convention. We then introduce an
L_{\infty}
-algebra such that the cochain complex of differential algebras can be realized as the shift of the underlying complex of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra obtained by the twisting procedure. As we will see in §
5
, this is exactly the
L_{\infty}
-algebra deduced from the minimal model of the operad governing differential algebras with weight.

2.1.
Differential algebras and their cohomology

Let
A=(A,\mu_{A},d_{A})
be a differential algebra of weight
\lambda
, with a multiplication
\mu_{A}
and a differential operator
d_{A}
of weight
\lambda
, as defined in Eq. (
1
).
A bimodule
M
over the associative algebra
(A,\mu_{A})
is called a
differential bimodule over the differential algebra

(A,\mu_{A},d_{A})
if
M
is further endowed with a linear transformation

d_{M}:M\to M
such that for all
a,b\in A
and
x\in M,

\displaystyle d_{M}(ax)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}(a)x+ad_{M}(x)+\lambda d_{A}(a)d_{M}(x),

\displaystyle d_{M}(xa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle xd_{A}(a)+d_{M}(x)a+\lambda d_{M}(x)d_{A}(a).

The regular bimodule
A
is obviously a differential bimodule over itself, called the
regular differential bimodule
of the differential algebra
A
.

Given a differential bimodule
M=(M,d_{M})
over the differential algebra
A=(A,\mu_{A},d_{A})
, a new differential bimodule structure on
M
, with the same derivation
d_{M}
, is given by

(2)

a\vdash v=(a+\lambda d_{A}(a))x,\quad x\dashv a=x(a+\lambda d_{A}(a)),\quad a\in A,x\in M.

For distinction, we let
{}_{\vdash}M_{\dashv}
denote this new differential bimodule structure over
A=(A,\mu_{A},d_{A})
; for details, see
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Recall that the Hochschild cochain complex
{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)
of an associative algebra
A
with coefficients in a bimodule
M
is the cochain complex

({\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M):=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}^{\bullet}),

where for
n\geqslant 0
,
{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)={\rm Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)
(in particular,
{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)=M
) and the coboundary operator

\partial_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}^{n}:{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{C}}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M),n\geqslant 0,

\partial_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}^{n}(f)(a_{1,n+1}):=(-1)^{n+1}a_{1}f(a_{2,n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+1-i}f(a_{1,i-1}\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})+f(a_{1,n})a_{n+1}

for
f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M),~a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1}\in A
, where for
1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant n+1
, we write

a_{i,j}:=a_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}
(by convention,
a_{i,j}=\emptyset
for
i>j
).
The corresponding Hochschild cohomology is denoted by
{\mathrm{H}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)
.
We write
{\mathrm{H}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A):={\mathrm{H}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)
when
M
is taken to be the regular bimodule
A
,

Let
M=(M,d_{M})
be a differential bimodule over a differential algebra
A=(A,\mu_{A},d_{A})
. The Hochschild cochain complex
{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,{}_{\vdash}M_{\dashv})
of the associative algebra
A=(A,\mu_{A})
with coefficients in the new bimodule
{}_{\vdash}M_{\dashv}
is called the
cochain complex of the differential operator
d_{A}
with coefficients in the differential bimodule
M
, denoted by
({\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})
. More precisely, for
g\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M)\coloneqq{\rm Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)
and

,
we have

\begin{split}\partial_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}^{n}g(a_{1,n+1})\coloneqq&(-1)^{n+1}a_{1}\vdash g(a_{2,n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+1-i}g(a_{1,i-1}\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})+g(a_{1,n})\dashv a_{n+1}\\
=&(-1)^{n+1}(a_{1}+\lambda d_{A}(a_{1}))g(a_{2,n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+1-i}g(a_{1,i-1}\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})\\
&+g(a_{1,n})(a_{n+1}+\lambda d_{A}(a_{n+1})).\end{split}

We write
({\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A),\partial_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})\coloneqq({\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})
by taking the differential bimodule
M
to be the regular differential bimodule
A
.
The cohomology of the cochain complex
({\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})
, denoted by
{\mathrm{H}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M)
, is called the
cohomology of the differential operator
d_{A}
with coefficients in the differential bimodule
M
. When the differential bimodule
M
is the regular differential bimodule
A
, write
{\mathrm{H}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A):={\mathrm{H}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M)
.

As in
[
22
, Proposition 3.3]
, define a chain map
\Phi^{\bullet}:{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)\to{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M)
as follows:
for
f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)
with
n\geqslant 1
, define

\begin{split}\Phi^{n}(f)(a_{1,n})\coloneqq&\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda^{k-1}\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leqslant n}f(a_{1,i_{1}-1}\otimes d_{A}(a_{i_{1}})\otimes a_{i_{1}+1,i_{2}-1}\otimes d_{A}(a_{i_{2}})\otimes\cdots\otimes d_{A}(a_{i_{k}})\otimes a_{i_{k}+1,n})\\
&-d_{M}(f(a_{1,n})),\end{split}

and

\Phi^{0}(x)\coloneqq-d_{M}(x),\quad\ x\in{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)=M.

Let
(C_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})
be the negative shift of the mapping cone of
\Phi^{\bullet}
. More precisely,

C_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{n}(A,M)\coloneqq\begin{cases}{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)\oplus{\mathrm{C}}^{n-1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M),&n\geqslant 1,\\
{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)=M,&n=0,\end{cases}

and the differentials
\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{n}:C_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{n}(A,M)\rightarrow C_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{n+1}(A,M)
are defined by

\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{n}(f,g):=(\partial^{n}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(f),-\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}^{n-1}(g)-\Phi^{n}(f)),\quad f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M),\,g\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n-1}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A,M),n\geqslant 1,

\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{0}(x):=(\partial^{0}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(x),-\Phi^{0}(x)),\quad x\in{\mathrm{C}}^{0}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)=M.

The complex
(C_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})
is called the
cochain complex of the differential algebra
A=(A,\mu_{A},d_{A})
with coefficients in the differential bimodule
M
. When the differential bimodule
M
is the regular differential bimodule
A
, we write
({\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}(A),\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet}):=({\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})
.

The cohomology of the cochain complex
(C_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet}(A,M),\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet})
, denoted by
{\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{\bullet}(A,M)
, is called the
cohomology of the differential algebra

A=(A,\mu_{A},d_{A})
with coefficients in the differential bimodule

.

2.2.
L_{\infty}
-algebra on the deformation complex from differential algebras

We first recall some basics on
L_{\infty}
-algebras; for more details, see
[
17
,
29
,
30
,
53
]
.

Let
V=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
be a graded vector space. The graded symmetric algebra
S(V)
of
V
is defined to be the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(V)
by the two-sided ideal
I
generated by

x\otimes y-(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x
for all homogeneous elements
x,y\in V
. For
x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}\in V^{\otimes n}\subseteq T(V)
, write
x_{1}\odot x_{2}\odot\dots\odot x_{n}
for its image in
S(V)
.
For homogeneous elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in V
and
\sigma\in{\rm S}_{n}
, the Koszul sign
\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is defined by the equality

x_{1}\odot x_{2}\odot\dots\odot x_{n}=\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})x_{\sigma(1)}\odot x_{\sigma(2)}\odot\dots\odot x_{\sigma(n)}\in S(V).

We also define

\chi(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})={\mathrm{sgn}}(\sigma)\ \epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

where
{\mathrm{sgn}}(\sigma)
is the sign of the permutation
\sigma
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a graded space
L=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
endowed with a family of linear operators
l_{n}:L^{\otimes n}\rightarrow L,n\geqslant 1
with degree
|l_{n}|=n-2
subject to the following conditions. For
n\geqslant 1
and homogeneous elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in L
,

(i)

(generalized anti-symmetry)

l_{n}(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)})=\chi(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\ l_{n}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}),\ \sigma\in{\rm S}_{n};

(ii)

(generalized Jacobi identity)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}(i,n-i)}\chi(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})(-1)^{i(n-i)}l_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(i)})\otimes x_{\sigma(i+1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)})=0.

Here
{\mathrm{Sh}}(i,n-i)
is the set of
(i,n-i)

shuffles, consisting of
\sigma\in{\rm S}_{n}
such that

\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i)\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \sigma(i+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n).

In particular, if
l_{n}=0
for all
n\geqslant 3
, then
(L,l_{1},l_{2})
is called a
differential graded (=dg) Lie algebra
; if
l_{n}=0
except
n=2
, then
(L,l_{2})
is called a
graded Lie algebra
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}
be a graded space. Let
sV
denote the suspension of
V
and
s^{-1}V
the desuspension.
Denote

\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V):={\rm Hom}(T(sV),sV).

Given homogeneous elements
f\in{\rm Hom}((sV)^{\otimes m},V)
with
m\geqslant 1
and
g\in{\rm Hom}((sV)^{\otimes n},V)
with
n\geqslant 0
, for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
, write

sf\circ_{i}sg:=sf\circ({\rm Id}^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes sg\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes(m-i)}),

and
sf\{sg\}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}sf\circ_{i}sg
; when
m=0
,
sf\{sg\}
is defined to be
0
. The Gerstenhaber bracket
[
13
]
of
sf
and
sg

is defined by

(3)

\displaystyle[sf,sg]_{G}:=sf\{sg\}-(-1)^{(|f|+1)(|g|+1)}sg\{sf\}.

A celebrated result of Gerstenhaber
[
13
]
states that
the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)
endowed with the Gerstenhaber bracket is a graded Lie algebra.
The following facts are quoted for later applications.

(i)

Let
V
be an ungraded space considered as a graded space concentrated in degree 0. Then there is a bijection between the set of Maurer-Cartan elements in the graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)
and the set of associative algebra structures on the space
V
, where the correspondence is induced by

(4)

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}((sV)^{\otimes n},sV)\simeq{\rm Hom}(V^{\otimes n},V),f\mapsto\tilde{f}:=s^{-1}\circ f\circ s^{\otimes n},\quad f\in{\rm Hom}((sV)^{\otimes n},sV).

(ii)

Let
(A,\mu)
be an associative algebra and
\alpha
be the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(A)
. Then the underlying complex of the twisted dg Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(A),l_{1}^{\alpha},l_{2}^{\alpha})
is exactly
s{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A)
, the shift of the Hochschild cochain complex of the associative algebra
A
.

Let
V
be a graded space. Write

\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V):={\rm Hom}(T(sV),V)

and define the graded space

(5)

\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V):=\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)\oplus\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V)={\rm Hom}(T(sV),sV)\oplus{\rm Hom}(T(sV),V).

Now we introduce an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V)
by the following process:

(i)

For
sf,sg\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)
, define

l_{2}(sf\otimes sg)=[sf,sg]_{G}\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V),

where
[-,-]_{G}
is the Gerstenhaber Lie bracket in Eq. (
3
).

(ii)

Let
sf\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)
and
g\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V)
. Define

l_{2}(sf\otimes g)=(-1)^{|f|+1}s^{-1}[sf,sg]_{G}\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V).

(iii)

Let
sf\in{\rm Hom}((sV)^{\otimes n},sV)\subseteq\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V)
with
2\leqslant m\leqslant n
. Define

l_{m+1}(sf\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{m})=\lambda^{m-1}\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm S}_{m}}(-1)^{\xi}s^{-1}\big(sf\{sg_{\sigma(1)},\dots,sg_{\sigma(m)}\}\big)\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V),

where
(-1)^{\xi}=\chi(\sigma;g_{1},\dots,g_{m})(-1)^{m(|f|+1)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}|g_{\sigma(j)}|}
.

(iv)

Let
sf\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V)
with
m\geqslant 1
. For
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
, define

l_{m+1}(g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{k}\otimes sf\otimes g_{k+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{m})\in\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V)

to be

l_{m+1}(g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{k}\otimes sf\otimes g_{k+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{m}):=(-1)^{(|f|+1)(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}|g_{j}|)+k}l_{n+1}(sf\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{m}),

where the right hand side has been defined in (ii) and (iii).

(v)

All other components of the operators
\{l_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
vanish.

Then we can state one of our main results in this paper.

Theorem 2.2
.

Given a graded space
V
and
\lambda\in{\bf k}
, the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V)
endowed with the operations
\{l_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
defined above is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

The theorem can be proved by a direct but lengthy verification. Instead, we will present an operadic proof in the spirit of
[
28
,
56
,
57
]
once we obtain the minimal model. We first sketch the proof below, with details supplied later. We then give an application in the next subsection, realizing differential algebras with weight as the Maurer-Cartan elements in this
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Proof.

(A sketch)
The minimal model of the differential algebra operad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
is obtained in Theorem
4.4
. It is shown in §
5
that
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V)
is exactly the
L_{\infty}
-structure resulting from this minimal model.
∎

2.3.
Realising differential algebra structures as Maurer-Cartan elements

We give an application of Theorem
2.2
.

Definition 2.3
.

An element
\alpha
of degree
-1
in an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(L,\{l_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1})
is called a
Maurer-Cartan element
if it satisfies the
Maurer-Cartan equation
:

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}l_{n}(\alpha^{\otimes n})=0

(in particular, this infinite sum needs to be well defined).

For a dg Lie algebra
(L,l_{1},l_{2})
, Eq. (
6
) reduces to

(7)

\displaystyle l_{1}(\alpha)-\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(\alpha\otimes\alpha)=0.

Proposition 2.4

(twisting procedure
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\alpha
be a Maurer-Cartan element in an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(L,\{l_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1})
. A new
L_{\infty}
-structure
\{l_{n}^{\alpha}:L^{\otimes n}\rightarrow L\}_{n\geqslant 1}
on the graded space
L
is defined by

(8)

l^{\alpha}_{n}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{in+\frac{i(i-1)}{2}}l_{n+i}(\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n}),\ x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in L,

provided the infinite sums exist. The resulting
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(L,\{l_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n\geqslant 1})
is called the
twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra
by the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha
.

For a dg Lie algebra
(L,l_{1},l_{2})
and a Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha\in L_{-1}
, Eq. (
8
) reduces to

(9)

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\alpha}(x)=l_{1}(x)-l_{2}(\alpha\otimes x),\ x\in L,\ \mathrm{and}\ l_{2}^{\alpha}=l_{2}.

We fix the isomorphism

(10)

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}((sV)^{\otimes n},V)\simeq{\rm Hom}(V^{\otimes n},V),g\mapsto\check{g}:=g\circ s^{\otimes n},\,g\in{\rm Hom}((sV)^{\otimes n},V).

The following proposition follows from general facts about
L_{\infty}
-structures obtained from minimal models; see the discussion at the end of §
3.1
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
V
be an ungraded space considered as a graded space concentrated in degree
0
. Then a differential algebra structure of weight
\lambda
on
V
is equivalent to a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V)
in Theorem
2.2
.

The following result justifies the cohomology theory introduced in
[
22
]
and recalled in §
2.1
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(A,\mu,d)
be a differential algebra of weight
\lambda
. Twisting the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
by the Maurer-Cartan element corresponding to the differential algebra structure
(A,\mu,d)
, then its underlying complex is exactly
s{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
, the suspension of the cochain complex of the differential algebra
(A,\mu,d)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.5
, the differential algebra structure
(A,\mu,d)
is equivalent to a Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha=(m,\tau)
in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
with

m=-s\circ\mu\circ(s^{-1})^{\otimes 2}:(sV)^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow sV\ \mathrm{and}\ \tau=d\circ s^{-1}:sV\rightarrow V.

By Proposition
2.4
, the Maurer-Cartan element induces a new
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure
\{l_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
on the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
.

Given
(sf,g)\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n},sA)\oplus{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n-1},sA)\subset\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(A)\oplus\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)=\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
, note that by the isomorphisms (
4
) and (
10
),
we get
(\widetilde{sf},\check{g})\in{\mathrm{C}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{n}(A)
.
By definition, for
sf\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n},sA)\subset\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathrm{Alg}}}(A)
, we have

l_{1}^{\alpha}(sf)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{i+\frac{i(i-1)}{2}}l_{i+1}(\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes sf)=\Big(-l_{2}(m\otimes sf),\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}l_{i+1}(\tau^{\otimes i}\otimes sf)\Big).

The definition of
\{l_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
gives
-l_{2}(m\otimes sf)=-[m,sf]_{G}
.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}l_{i+1}(\tau^{\otimes i}\otimes sf)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-l_{2}(\tau\otimes sf)+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}l_{i+1}(\tau^{\otimes i}\otimes sf)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(iv)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|f|}l_{2}(sf\otimes\tau)+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}(-1)^{i|f|}l_{i+1}(sf\otimes\tau^{\otimes i})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(ii)(iii)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle s^{-1}[sf,s\tau]_{G}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}(-1)^{i|f|}i!\lambda^{i-1}(-1)^{i(|f|+1)+\frac{i(i-1)}{2}}s^{-1}(sf\{\underbrace{s\tau,\dots,s\tau}_{i}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}[sf,s\tau]_{G}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\lambda^{i-1}s^{-1}(sf\{\underbrace{s\tau,\dots,s\tau}_{i}\}).

For
g\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes(n-1)},A)\subset\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)
, we have

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\alpha}(g)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}l_{i+1}(\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-l_{2}(m\otimes g)-\frac{1}{2!}\Big(l_{3}(m\otimes\tau\otimes g)+l_{3}(\tau\otimes m\otimes g)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}[m,sg]+\lambda s^{-1}m\circ(s\tau\otimes sg)+\lambda s^{-1}m\circ(sg\otimes s\tau).

We obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}l_{1}^{\alpha}(sf,g)&=&\Big(-[m,sf]_{G},s^{-1}[sf,s\tau]_{G}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\lambda^{i-1}s^{-1}(sf\{\underbrace{s\tau,\dots,s\tau}_{i}\})\\
&&\hskip 9.24994pt+s^{-1}[m,sg]+\lambda s^{-1}m\circ(s\tau\otimes sg)+\lambda s^{-1}m\circ(sg\otimes s\tau)\Big),\end{array}

which is easily seen to be in
correspondence with
-\partial_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}^{n}(\widetilde{sf},\check{g})
via the fixed isomorphisms (
4
) and (
10
). This completes the proof.
∎

Although
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, once the associative algebra structure
\mu
over
A
is fixed, the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)
is indeed a differential graded Lie algebra, as shown by the following result. Moreover, this result justifies the cohomology theory of differential operators.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(A,\mu)
be an associative algebra. Then the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)
can be endowed with a dg Lie algebra structure, of which the Maurer-Cartan elements are in bijection with the differential operators of weight
\lambda
on
(A,\mu)
.

Given a differential operator
d
of weight
\lambda
on the associative algebra
(A,\mu)
, the dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)
twisted by the Maurer-Cartan element corresponding to
d
has its underlying complex being the cochain complex
{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A)
of the differential operator
d
.

Proof.

Regard
A
as a graded space concentrated in degree 0. Define
m=-s\circ\mu\circ(s^{-1}\otimes s^{-1}):(sA)^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow sA
. Then it is easy to see that
\beta=(m,0)
is naturally a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
. We apply the twisting procedure by
\beta
on
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
.
By the construction of
\{l_{n}\}_{\geqslant 1}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(A)
, the graded subspace
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)
is closed under the action of the operators
\{l_{n}^{\beta}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
.

Since the arity of
m
is 2, the restriction of
l_{n}^{\beta}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)
is
0
for
n\geqslant 3
. Thus
(\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}},\{l_{n}^{\beta}\}_{n=1,2})
forms a dg Lie algebra which can be made explicit as follows. For
g\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n},A)
and
h\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes k},A)
, we have

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\beta}(g)=

\displaystyle-l_{2}(m\otimes g)=s^{-1}[m,sg]_{G},

\displaystyle l_{2}^{\beta}(g\otimes h)=

\displaystyle l_{3}(m\otimes g\otimes h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(-1)^{|g|}s^{-1}m\circ(sg\otimes sh)+\lambda(-1)^{|g||h|+1+|h|}s^{-1}m\circ(sh\otimes sg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(-1)^{n}s^{-1}m\circ(sg\otimes sh)+\lambda(-1)^{nk+k+1}s^{-1}m\circ(sh\otimes sg).

Since
A
is concentrated in degree 0, we have
\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(A)_{-1}={\rm Hom}(sA,A)
. Take an element
\tau\in{\rm Hom}(sA,A)
. Then
\tau
satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation:

l_{1}^{\beta}(\tau)-\frac{1}{2}l_{2}^{\beta}(\tau\otimes\tau)=0

if and only if

s^{-1}[m,s\tau]_{G}+\lambda\ s^{-1}m\circ(s\tau\otimes s\tau)=0.

Define
d=\tau\circ s:A\rightarrow A
. Under the isomorphisms (
4
) and (
10
), the above equation is exactly the statement that
d
is a differential operator of weight
\lambda
on the associative algebra
(A,\mu)
.

Now let
d
be a differential operator of weight
\lambda
on the associative algebra
(A,\mu)
and
\tau=d\circ s^{-1}
be the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element in the dg Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{C}_{{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}},\{l_{n}^{\beta}\}_{n=1,2})
. For
g\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n},A)
, a direct computation shows that

\partial_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(\check{g})
corresponds to
(l_{1}^{\beta})^{\tau}(g)
via the isomorphism (
10
). This proves the last statement.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

In the course of proving the above proposition, the Lie bracket
(l_{2}^{\beta})^{\tau}
on
{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A)
can also be given explicitly:
for
f\in{\mathrm{C}}^{n}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A)
and
g\in{\mathrm{C}}^{k}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A)
,

[f,g](a_{1,n+k})=(-1)^{n}\lambda f(a_{1,n})g(a_{n+1,n+k})+(-1)^{nk+k+1}\lambda g(a_{1,k})f(a_{k+1,n+k}),\quad a_{1},\dots,a_{n+k}\in A.

Note that when
\lambda=0
, the cochain complex
{\mathrm{C}}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}(A)
has the trivial Lie bracket.

3.
The Koszul dual homotopy cooperad

In this section, we will construct a homotopy cooperad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}^{\mathrm{\textup{!`}}}
, which will be called the Koszul dual homotopy cooperad of the operad
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
of differential algebras with weight
\lambda
. In fact, we will show that the cobar construction of
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}^{\mathrm{\textup{!`}}}
is the minimal model of
{{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}
(Theorem
4.4
) which justifies the name.

3.1.
Homotopy (co)operads and
L_{\infty}
-algebras

We first collect needed background on nonsymmetric homotopy (co)operads that are scattered in several references
[
8
,
40
,
44
,
45
]
. We also explain how
L_{\infty}
-structures can be obtained from homotopy operads, in particular from convolution homotopy operads.

A graded collection is a family
{\mathcal{O}}=\{{\mathcal{O}}(n)\}_{n\geqslant 1}
of graded space indexed by positive integers. So each
{\mathcal{O}}(n)
itself is a graded space. Elements in
{\mathcal{O}}(n)
are said to have arity
n
. The suspension of
{\mathcal{O}}
, denoted by
s{\mathcal{O}}
is defined to be the graded collection
\{s{\mathcal{O}}(n)\}_{n\geqslant 1}
. The desuspension
s^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}
of the graded collection
{\mathcal{O}}
is defined in the same way.

We also recall preliminaries about trees. As we only consider planar reduced rooted trees, we will simply call them trees.
For a tree
T\in\mathfrak{T}
, denote the weight (=number of vertices) and arity (=number of leaves) of
T
by
\omega(T)
and
\alpha(T)
respectively. Let
\mathfrak{T}
be the set of all trees with weight
\geqslant 1
and, for
n\geqslant 1
, let
\mathfrak{T}^{(n)}
be the set of trees of weight
n
.
Since the trees are planar, the inputs of each vertex in a tree have total order by going clockwise.
Furthermore by the existence of the root, there is a naturally induced total order on the set of all vertices of the tree
T
, given by counting the vertices starting from the root clockwise along the tree. We call this order the
planar order
.

Let
T^{\prime}
be a divisor of
T
. Let
T/T^{\prime}
denote the tree obtained from
T
in replacing
T^{\prime}
by a corolla (a tree with only one vertex) of arity
\alpha(T^{\prime})
. There is a natural permutation
\sigma=\sigma(T,T^{\prime})\in{\rm S}_{\omega(T)}
associated to the pair
(T,T^{\prime})
defined as follows. Let
\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{n}\}
be the sequence of all vertices of
T
listed in the planar order and let
\omega(T^{\prime})=j
. Let
v^{\prime}
be the vertex in
T/T^{\prime}
corresponding to the divisor
T^{\prime}
of
T
and let
i
be the serial number of
v^{\prime}
in
T/T^{\prime}
in the planar order, that is, there are
i-1
vertices before
T^{\prime}
. Then define
\sigma=\sigma(T,T^{\prime})\in{\rm S}_{n}
to be the unique permutation with the properties that it does not permute the vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1}
, the ordered set
\{v_{\sigma(i)}<\dots<v_{\sigma(i+j-1)}\}
is exactly the planar ordered set of all vertices of
T^{\prime}
, and the ordered set
\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{i-1}<v^{\prime}<v_{\sigma(i+j)}<\dots<v_{\sigma(n)}\}
is exactly the planar ordered set of all vertices in the tree
T/T^{\prime}
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}=\{{\mathcal{P}}(n)\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be a graded collection, let
T\in\mathfrak{T}
and let
\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{n}\}
be the sequence of all vertices of
T
in the planar order. Let
{\mathcal{P}}^{\otimes T}
denote
{\mathcal{P}}(\alpha(v_{1}))\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathcal{P}}(\alpha(v_{n}))
. Its element can be pictured as a linear combination of the tree
T
with each vertex
v_{i}
decorated by an element of
{\mathcal{P}}(\alpha(v_{i}))
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
homotopy operad structure
on a graded collection
{\mathcal{P}}=\{{\mathcal{P}}(n)\}_{n\geqslant 1}
is a family of operations

\{m_{T}:{\mathcal{P}}^{\otimes T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}(\alpha(T))\}_{T\in\mathfrak{T}}

with
|m_{T}|=\omega(T)-2
, such that, for all
T\in\mathfrak{T}
, the equation

\sum\limits_{T^{\prime}\subset{T}}(-1)^{i-1+jk}{\mathrm{sgn}}(\sigma(T,T^{\prime}))\ m_{T/T^{\prime}}\circ({\rm Id}^{\otimes{i-1}}\otimes m_{T^{\prime}}\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes k})\circ r_{\sigma(T,T^{\prime})}=0

holds. Here
T^{\prime}
runs through subtrees of
T
,
i
is the serial number of the vertex
v^{\prime}
in
T/T^{\prime}
as above,
j=\omega(T^{\prime})
,
k=\omega(T)-i-j
, and

r_{\sigma}
denotes the right action by
\sigma=\sigma(T,T^{\prime})
:

r_{\sigma}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=\varepsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)}.

A
strict morphism
between two homotopy operads is a morphism of graded collections that is compatible with all operations
m_{T},T\in\mathfrak{T}
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be the collection with
{\mathcal{I}}(1)=k
and
{\mathcal{I}}(n)=0
for
n\neq 1
. The collection
{\mathcal{I}}
can be naturally endowed with a homotopy operad structure: when
T
is the tree with two vertices and a unique leaf, then
m_{T}:{\mathcal{I}}(1)\otimes{\mathcal{I}}(1)\to{\mathcal{I}}(1)
is given by the identity; for other trees
T
,
m_{T}
vanish.

A homotopy operad
{\mathcal{P}}
is called
strictly unital
if there exists a strict morphism of homotopy operads
\eta:{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}
such that, for
n\geqslant 1
, the compositions

{\mathcal{P}}(n)\cong{\mathcal{P}}(n)\otimes{\mathcal{I}}(1)\xrightarrow{{\rm Id}\otimes\eta}{\mathcal{P}}(n)\otimes{\mathcal{P}}(1)\xrightarrow{m_{T_{1,i}}}{\mathcal{P}}(n)

and

{\mathcal{P}}(n)\cong{\mathcal{I}}(1)\otimes{\mathcal{P}}(n)\xrightarrow{\eta\otimes{\rm Id}}{\mathcal{P}}(1)\otimes{\mathcal{P}}(n)\xrightarrow{m_{T_{2}}}{\mathcal{P}}(n)

are identity maps on
{\mathcal{P}}(n)
, where for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
,
T_{1,i}
is the tree of weight
2
, arity
n
, with its second vertex having arity
1
and connecting to the first vertex on its
i
-th leaf; and
T_{2}
is the tree of weight
2
, arity
n
whose first vertex has arity
1
. Moreover,
for a tree
T
of weight at least three,
m_{T}({\rm Id}^{\otimes i-1}\otimes\eta\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes\omega(T)-i})=0
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant\omega(T)
.

Also a homotopy operad
{\mathcal{P}}
is called
augmented
if there is a strict morphism of homotopy operads
\varepsilon:{\mathcal{P}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}
such that
\varepsilon\circ\eta={\rm Id}_{{\mathcal{I}}}
.

Given a dg operad
{\mathcal{P}}
, for each tree
T
, one can define the composition
m_{\mathcal{P}}^{T}:{\mathcal{P}}^{\otimes T}\to{\mathcal{P}}(\alpha(T))
in
{\mathcal{P}}
along
T
as follows: for
\omega(T)=1
, set
m_{\mathcal{P}}^{T}:={\rm Id}
; for
\omega(T)=2
, set
m_{\mathcal{P}}^{T}:=m_{T}
;
for
\omega(T)\geqslant 3
, write
T
as the grafting of a subtree
T^{\prime}
, whose vertex set is that of
T
except the last one, with the corolla whose unique vertex is just the last vertex of
T
in the planar order, then set

m_{\mathcal{P}}^{T}:=m_{T/T^{\prime}}\circ(m_{\mathcal{P}}^{T^{\prime}}\otimes{\rm Id})
, where
m_{\mathcal{P}}^{T^{\prime}}
is obtained by induction.

Dualizing the definition of homotopy operads, one has the notion of homotopy cooperads.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\{{\mathcal{C}}(n)\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be a graded collection. A
homotopy cooperad structure
on
{\mathcal{C}}
consists of a family of operations
\{\Delta_{T}:{\mathcal{C}}(\alpha(T))\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\otimes T}\}_{T\in\mathfrak{T}}
with
|\Delta_{T}|=\omega(T)-2
such that for
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
\Delta_{T}(c)=0
for all but finitely many
T\in{\mathfrak{T}}
, and the family of operations
\{\Delta_{T}\}_{T\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
satisfies the identity

\sum_{T^{\prime}\subset T}{\mathrm{sgn}}(\sigma(T,T^{\prime})^{-1})(-1)^{i-1+jk}r_{\sigma(T,T^{\prime})^{-1}}\circ({\rm Id}^{\otimes i-1}\otimes\Delta_{T^{\prime}}\otimes{\rm Id}^{\otimes k})\circ\Delta_{T/T^{\prime}}=0

for all
T\in{\mathfrak{T}}
, where
T^{\prime},i,j,k
have the same meaning as for homotopy operads.

The graded collection
{\mathcal{I}}
has a natural homotopy cooperad structure. It is obtained by taking
\Delta_{T}:{\mathcal{I}}(1)\to{\mathcal{I}}(1)\otimes{\mathcal{I}}(1)
to be the identity when
T
is the tree with two vertices and one unique leaf, and
\Delta_{T}=0
for other
T
.
Dualizing the notions of being strictly unital and being augmented gives the notions of being
strictly counital and coaugmented
.
For a coaugmented homotopy cooperad
{\mathcal{C}}
, the graded collection
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}=\ker(\varepsilon)
endowed with operations
\{\overline{\Delta}_{T}\}_{T\in{\mathfrak{T}}}
is naturally a homotopy cooperad, where
\overline{\Delta}_{T}
is the the restriction of operation
\Delta_{T}
on
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}
.

A homotopy cooperad
{\mathcal{E}}=\{{\mathcal{E}}(n)\}_{n\geqslant 1}
such that
\{\Delta_{T}\}
vanish for all
\omega(T)\geqslant 3
is exactly a noncounital dg cooperad in the sense of Markl
[
41
]
.

Definition 4.12

1\leqslant i\leqslant n
,

\ell_{n+1}(\overline{f_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\overline{f_{i}}\otimes\widehat{f_{0}}\otimes\overline{f_{i+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\overline{f_{n}})=(-1)^{i+|\widehat{f_{0}}|(\sum_{k=1}^{i}|\overline{f_{k}}|)}\ell_{n+1}(\widehat{f_{0}}\otimes\overline{f_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\overline{f_{n}});

(v)

All other components of the operators
\{\ell_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
vanish.

Via the bijections
f\mapsto\widehat{f}
and
g\mapsto\overline{g}
, it is readily verified that the above
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\mathbf{Hom}(\mathscr{S}({{}_{\lambda}\!\mathfrak{Dif}}^{\mathrm{\textup{!`}}}),{\rm End}_{sV})
is exactly the one on
{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V)
defined in Eq (
5
), thereby proving Theorem
2.2
.

5.2.
Another description of homotopy differential algebras

We end the paper with a characterization of the key notion of homotopy differential algebras and give a generalization of a work of Kajura and Stasheff
[
24
]
.

By the last paragraph of §
3.1
, there exists another definition of homotopy differential algebras in terms of the Maurer-Cartan elements in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra on the deformation complex. Let us make it precise.

Let
V
be a graded space. Denote

\overline{T}(V)\coloneqq\oplus_{n\geqslant 1}V^{\otimes n},\ \overline{{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V):={\rm Hom}(\overline{T}(sV),sV),\ \overline{{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V):={\rm Hom}(\overline{T}(sV),V).

We consider the subspace

\overline{{{\mathfrak{C}}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}}(V):=\overline{{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)\oplus\overline{{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{DO}_{\lambda}}}(V)

of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}(V)
in Eq. (
5
).
It is easy to see that
\overline{{{\mathfrak{C}}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}}(V)
is an
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V)
.

By the remark at the end of §
3.1
, Definitions
4.12
can be rephrased as follows.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
V
be a graded space. A
homotopy differential algebra structure
of weight
\lambda
on
V
is defined to be a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\overline{{{\mathfrak{C}}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}}(V)
.

Notice that if we take the whole space
{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}(V)
instead of the reduced part
\overline{{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathrm{Alg}}}(V)
, we get the curved homotopy differential algebra structure.

Solving the Maurer-Cartan equation in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\overline{{{\mathfrak{C}}}_{{\mathsf{DA}_{\lambda}}}}(V)
, Proposition
5.1
can be explicated as follows.

Proposition 5.2
.

A homotopy differential algebra structure of weight
\lambda
on a graded space
V
is equivalent to two families of linear maps

b_{n}:(sV)^{\otimes n}\rightarrow sV,\quad R_{n}:(sV)^{\otimes n}\rightarrow V,\quad n\geqslant 1,

both of degree
-1
and subject to the following equations:

\sum_{i+j-1=n\atop i,j\geqslant 1}b_{i}\{b_{j}\}=0\ \mathrm{and}\ \sum_{u+j-1=n\atop u,j\geqslant 1}sR_{u}\{b_{j}\}=\sum_{l_{1}+\dots+l_{q}+p-q=n\atop{l_{1},\dots,l_{q}\geqslant 1\atop 1\leqslant q\leqslant p\leqslant n}}\lambda^{q-1}b_{p}\{sR_{l_{1}},\dots,sR_{l_{q}}\}.

Remark 5.3
.

(i)

The equivalence between Definition
4.13
and Proposition
5.2
is given by

m_{n}:=s^{-1}\circ b_{n}\circ s^{\otimes n}:V^{\otimes n}\rightarrow V,\quad d_{n}:=R_{n}\circ s^{\otimes n}:V^{\otimes n}\rightarrow V,\ n\geqslant 1.

(ii)

Thanks to Proposition
5.2
, Definition
4.13
generalizes the notion of homotopy derivations on
A_{\infty}
-algebras introduced by
Kajura and Stasheff
[
24
]
from zero weight to nonzero weight.

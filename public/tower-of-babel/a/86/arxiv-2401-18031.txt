A note on the density of periodic orbits of Anosov geodesic flow in manifolds of finite volume
Nestor Nina Zarate, Sergio Romaña

Abstract
In this paper, we prove that manifolds of finite volume with Anosov geodesic flow have dense periodic orbits. The same result works for conservative Anosov flows in non-compact cases. Keywords: Geodesic Flow, Shadowing Lemma, Stable(Unstable) Manifolds.

1
Introduction

Let
N
be a complete Riemannian manifold, a flow
\varphi^{t}\colon N\to N
is said to be
Anosov
if the tangent bundle of
N
,
TN
, has a splitting

TN=E^{s}\oplus\langle\Phi\rangle\oplus E^{u}
such that

\displaystyle d\varphi^{t}_{\theta}(E^{s}(\theta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{s}(\varphi^{t}(\theta)),

\displaystyle d\varphi^{t}_{\theta}(E^{u}(\theta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{u}(\varphi^{t}(\theta)),

\displaystyle||d\varphi^{t}_{\theta}\big|_{E^{s}}||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lambda^{t},

\displaystyle||d\varphi^{-t}_{\theta}\big|_{E^{u}}||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lambda^{t},

for all
t\geq 0
with
C>0
and
0<\lambda<1
, where
\Phi
is the vector field derivative of the flow.

If
M
is a complete Riemannanian manifold, we denote
SM
as its unit tangent bundle. Given
\theta=(p,v)\in SM
, we define
\gamma_{{}_{\theta}}(t)
as the unique geodesic with initial conditions
\gamma_{{}_{\theta}}(0)=p
and

\gamma_{{}_{\theta}}^{\prime}(0)=v
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\phi^{t}:SM\to SM
be the diffeomorphism given by

\phi^{{t}}(\theta)=(\gamma_{{}_{\theta}}(t),\gamma_{{}_{\theta}}^{\prime}(t))
. Recall that this family is a flow (called the
geodesic flow
) in the sense that
\phi^{t+s}=\phi^{t}\circ\phi^{s}
for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Geodesic flows provide a wide range of examples of Anosov flows, for example,
the geodesic flow of manifolds with negative pinched curvature (see
[
An69
]
and
[
Kn02
]
. Eberlein in
[
Eb73
]
found in manifolds without conjugate points, necessary and sufficient geometrical conditions such that the geodesic flow is Anosov. Some geometrical conditions to obtain an Anosov geodesic flow can be also found in
[
MR20
]
and
[
CR23
]
.

It is not difficult to prove that Anosov geodesic flows on compact manifolds have a density of periodic orbits. The knowledge of the distribution and density of periodic orbits of Anosov flows and more generally hyperbolic flows is a very interesting problem because it gives us more information about the dynamics of the system. A proof for the density of the periodic orbits of a hyperbolic flow over one compact manifold is using the Spectral Decomposition Theorem for flows (see
[
AK95
]
and
[
FH20
]
). Moreover, the information about the density of periodic orbits of a hyperbolic flow over a compact manifold also guarantees the topological transitivity of the flow, among other good properties.

However, for Anosov geodesic flow on a manifold of finite volume, this result is not easy to see, since some techniques used for the compact case maybe are not valid in the non-compact case, for example, the uniformly size of local stable and unstable manifolds. Everyone believes in this result, even already used this result, but we do not find concrete and clear proof. Thus, the main result of this work is to give a formal proof of this fact.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
M
has finite volume and
\phi^{t}:SM\to SM
is an Anosov geodesic flow, then the periodic orbits of
\phi^{t}
are dense in
SM
.

With the same arguments, we can announce

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\varphi^{t}:N\to N
is an Anosov flow, which preserves a finite smooth measure, then the periodic orbits of
\varphi^{t}
are dense in
N
.

Those results imply the following corollary (valid also for conservative Anosov flow).

Corollary 1.3
.

If
M
has finite volume such that
\phi^{t}:SM\to SM
is an Anosov geodesic flow, then

(a)

For all
x\in SM
,
W^{cu}(x)
and
W^{cs}(x)
are dense in
SM
.

(b)

\phi^{t}
is a transitive flow.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Stable and Unstable Manifolds

Assume that
\varphi^{t}:N\to N
is an Anosov flow. Given
x\in N
, we define the stable, unstable, center stable and center unstable manifolds, respectively, as

\displaystyle W^{ss}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z\in N:\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\varphi^{t}(z),\varphi^{t}(x))=0\},

\displaystyle W^{uu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z\in N:\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\varphi^{-t}(z),\varphi^{-t}(x))=0\},

\displaystyle W^{cs}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}W^{ss}(\varphi^{t}(x)),

\displaystyle W^{cu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}W^{uu}(\varphi^{t}(x)).

Given
\eta>0
, we also consider the local stable and unstable manifolds defined by

\displaystyle W_{\eta}^{ss}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z\in W^{ss}(x):d(\varphi^{t}(x),\varphi^{t}(z))\leq\eta,\,\,\,t\geq 0\},

\displaystyle W_{\eta}^{uu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z\in N:d(\varphi^{-t}(x),\varphi^{-t}(z))\leq\eta,\,t\geq 0\},

\displaystyle W_{\eta}^{cu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{|t|<\eta}W^{uu}_{\eta}(\phi^{t}(x)).

\displaystyle W_{\eta}^{cs}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{|t|<\eta}W^{ss}_{\eta}(\phi^{t}(x)).

When
\varphi^{t}\colon N\to N
is a
C^{r}
-Anosov flow and
N
is a compact manifold, the stable and unstable manifolds theorem states that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that the local stable and unstable manifolds are
C^{r}
-disk embedded tangent to the stable and unstable bundle, respectively. However, the construction of the differentiability of the local stable-unstable manifolds depends on the existence of some special charts depending on the injectivity radius of manifold
M
(see
[
AK95
]
for more details).

In the non-compact case, we can imitate the proof of the stable and unstable manifold theorem to construct the differentiability of the local stable and unstable manifolds on each point
x\in SM
. Thant can be guarantee since in the Sasaki’s metric on
SM
the injectivity radius is positive (cf.
[
El12
, page 41]
). Thus we have a crucial difference with the compact case because in the non-compact case, we have that the sizes of the local stable-unstable manifolds depend on
x\in SM
. In other words, we have

Theorem 2.1
.

[Stable and Unstable Manifolds in the Non-Compact Case]

Let
\phi^{t}:SM\to SM
be a
C^{r}
- Anosov geodesic flow,
r\geq 1
. Then, for each
x\in SM
there is a pair of embedded
C^{r}
-disks
W_{\epsilon(x)}^{ss}
,
W_{\epsilon(x)}^{uu}\subset N
called the
local strong-stable
and
local strong-unstable
manifolds of
x\in N
of size
\epsilon(x)>0
, respectively such that:

(1)

T_{x}W_{\epsilon(x)}^{ss}(x)=E_{x}^{s}
,
T_{x}W_{\epsilon(x)}^{uu}(x)=E_{x}^{u}
.

(2)

For every compact subset
K\subset M
there exists
\epsilon_{K}:=\inf\{\epsilon(x)>0:x\in K\}>0
such that for every
x\in K
we have that
W_{\epsilon_{{}_{K}}}^{ss}(x)
and
W_{\epsilon_{{}_{K}}}^{uu}(x)
are embedded.

(3)

W_{\epsilon(x)}^{ss}(x)\cap W_{\epsilon(x)}^{uu}(x)=\{x\}
.

2.2
Local Product Structure

If
\phi^{t}:N\to N
is an Anosov flow and
N
is compact, then there are
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
such that
d(x,y)<\delta

W_{\epsilon}^{cu}(x)\cap W_{\epsilon}^{ss}(y):=\{[x,y]\},

which is called the
Bowen-Bracket
or also
Local Product Structure
.

The Bowen-Bracket can be defined in the non-compact case, but the size of the local manifolds involved is not uniform. More specifically,

Theorem 2.2

(Local Product Structure )
.

Let
\phi^{t}:N\to N
be an Anosov flow and
\epsilon>0
small enough. Then for each
x\in N
there is a
\delta>0
and
\eta>0
such that

W_{\eta}^{cu}(y)\cap W_{\eta}^{ss}(z):=\{[y,z]\}\in B(x,\epsilon),

for
y,z\in\overline{B(x,\delta)}
. Analogous result holds for
W_{\eta}^{cs}(y)
and
W_{\eta}^{uu}(z)
.

The proof of the last theorem is similar to the compact case, see
[
FH20
]
for more details.

3
Shadowing Lemma

The orbit structure of a hyperbolic dynamical system has a distinctive and iconic richness and complexity, and these features can be derived from what thereby appears as a core feature of hyperbolic dynamics: the shadowing of orbits. In this section, we remember the Shadowing Lemma for flows and the expansivity in the compact case (See
[
FH20
]
and
[
AK95
]
).
We also will give some definitions of the shadowing and expansiveness that we need to prove the density of periodic orbits when we have a geodesic flow on a finite volume manifold.

Let
x\in N
and
t_{0}>0
. We consider the segment of orbit
\varphi^{[0,t_{0}]}(x):=\{\phi^{t}(x):t\in[0,t_{0}]\}
of
x
of size
t_{0}
, the future orbit
O^{+}(x):=\{\phi^{t}(x):t\geq 0\}
of
x
, and the past orbit
O^{-}(x):=\{\phi^{t}(x):t\leq 0\}
.

Definition 3.1

(Shadowing forward and backward)
.

Let
x_{0},y\in SM
,
t_{0}>0
and
\epsilon>0
.

1.

We said that
O^{+}(y)

\epsilon
-shades forward by piecewise the orbit segment
\phi^{[0,t_{0}]}(x_{0})
if

\displaystyle d(\phi^{t}(y),\phi^{t}(x_{0}))\leq\epsilon,\hskip 8.5359pt\forall\hskip 5.69046ptt\in[0,t_{0}],

and there exists a sequence
\{s_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
with
s_{j}\geq 0

(
called
transition times
)
, such that for all
k\geq 1
holds

\displaystyle d(\phi^{t}(\phi^{kt_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}s_{i}}(y)),\phi^{t}(x_{0}))\leq\epsilon,\hskip 8.5359pt\forall\hskip 5.69046ptt\in[0,t_{0}].

2.

Analogously, we said that
O^{-}(y)

\epsilon
-shades backward by piecewise the orbit segment
\phi^{[0,t_{0}]}(x_{0})
if

\displaystyle d(\phi^{-t}(y),\phi^{-t}(x_{0}))\leq\epsilon,\hskip 8.5359pt\forall\hskip 5.69046ptt\in[0,t_{0}],

and there exists a sequence
\{r_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
with
r_{j}\geq 0

(
called
transition times
)
such that for all
k\geq 1
holds

\displaystyle d(\phi^{-t}(\phi^{-kt_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}r_{j}}(y)),\phi^{-t}(x_{0}))\leq\epsilon,\hskip 8.5359pt\forall\hskip 5.69046ptt\in[0,t_{0}].

We said that the orbit
O(y)=O^{+}(y)\cup O^{-}(y)

\epsilon
-shades by piecewise the orbit segment
\phi^{[0,t_{0}]}(x_{0})
if
O^{+}(y)

\epsilon
-shades forward by piecewise and
O^{-}(y)

\epsilon
-shades backward by piecewise, respectively.

The above definition of shadowing forward and backward by piecewise is similar to the specification property for flows (see
[
FH20
]
).

Definition 3.2

Let

In the next proposition, we prove that segments of the orbit of a recurrent point of

\epsilon

Given
t_{0}>0
and a sequence
\underline{c}:=\{c_{j}\}

P_{m,\underline{c}}\colon[0,t_{0}]\to\mathbb{R}
, define by

P_{m,\underline{c}}(t):=\lambda^{t}+\lambda^{t_{0}-t}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{t+jt_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}},

(1)

where
\lambda\in(0,1)
comes from the definition of Anosov flow.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
\eta>0
,
t_{0}>2\eta
, then the family of functions
P_{m,\underline{c}}
, with
|c_{i}|<\eta
is uniformly bounded.

Proof.

For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the sequence

S_{m,\underline{c}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{jt_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}}.

Since
|c_{i}|<\eta
, then for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

t_{0}-\eta<t_{0}+\dfrac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}<t_{0}+\eta.

Moreover, since
t_{0}>2\eta
, then for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

0<\dfrac{t_{0}}{2}<t_{0}-\eta<t_{0}+\dfrac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}.

Thus,

\left(\lambda^{(t_{0}+\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i})}\right)^{j}\leq({\lambda}^{t_{0}-\eta})^{j}\leq(\lambda^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}})^{j},

which implies that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

\displaystyle S_{m,\underline{c}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\lambda^{t_{0}+\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\lambda^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}})^{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\lambda^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}})^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}}}{1-\lambda^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}}}=L.

Note that
P_{m,\underline{c}}(t)=\lambda^{t}+\lambda^{t_{0}-t}+\lambda^{t}S_{m,\underline{c}}

0<\lambda^{t}\leq 1

0<\lambda^{t_{0}-t}\leq 1

, thus

P_{m,\underline{c}}(t)\leq 2+L:=K,

and the proof of the lemma is complete.

Proof of Corollary

In this section, we use Theorem

The main ingredients are the stable and unstable manifolds, which satisfy the following properties (also valid for the stable case).

(a)

For all

(b)

For all

(c)

, then
W^{cu}(y)=W^{cu}(x).

As a consequence of these three properties, we can prove the following:

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
x\in SM

•

W^{cu}(x)=\bigcup_{y\in W^{cu}(x)}W^{cu}(y)
.

•

\overline{W^{cu}(x)}=\bigcup_{y\in\overline{W^{cu}(x)}}W^{cu}(y)
.

Proof.

Since
x\in W^{cu}(x)
, then it is enough to prove that
\bigcup_{y\in W^{cu}(x)}W^{cu}(y)\subset W^{cu}(x)
. In fact: let
z\in\bigcup_{y\in W^{cu}(x)}W^{cu}(y)
, then there is
y\in W^{cu}(x)
such that

from property (c). This concludes the proof of the first item. To proof the second part, note that as
y\in W^{cu}(y)
, then

\overline{W^{cu}(x)}\subset\bigcup_{y\in\overline{W^{cu}(x)}}\{y\}\subset\bigcup_{y\in\overline{W^{cu}(x)}}W^{cu}(y)
. Moreover, if

,

with
\tilde{z}\in W^{uu}(y)
,

. In particular, there is
y_{k}\in W^{cu}(x)
such that
y_{k}\to y
. The continuity of the unstable manifold implies that there is
\tilde{z}_{k}
such that
\tilde{z}_{k}\to\tilde{z}
and
\tilde{z}_{k}\in W^{uu}(y_{k})
. Thus,

. From property (c),

, which allows us to conclude that

.

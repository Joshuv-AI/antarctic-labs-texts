Introduction

In this paper, we study the solutions of

\Delta u+K(x)e^{2u}=0\quad{\rm in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.1)

where
K
is a nonpositive, non trivial and locally bounded function in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Equation (
1.1
) finds its origin from conformal geometry: Given a Riemannian surface
(\mathcal{M},g)
and a function
K
on
\mathcal{M}
, we may ask whether there is a new Riemannian metric
g_{1}
, conformal to
g
, such that
K
is the Gaussian curvature of
g_{1}
. In other words, we need to find a function
u
on
\mathcal{M}
such that
g_{1}=e^{2u}g
and
u
is a solution of the elliptic equation

\Delta_{g}u-k_{g}+Ke^{2u}=0\quad{\rm on}\;\;\mathcal{M}.

(1.2)

Here
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator,
k_{g}
is the Gaussian curvature on
({\mathcal{M}},g)
. In the Euclidean case
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
g=|dx|^{2}
,
(
1.2
) reduces to equation (
1.1
).

The equation (
1.1
) has been studied extensively, especially for the nonpositive, nonconstant curvature case, see
[
5
,
9
,
8
,
6
,
3
]
and the references therein.
The nonexistence of entire solution in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

K\equiv-1
was given by Ahlfors
[
1
]
in 1938, and then improved by Sattinger
[
9
]
, Ni
[
8
]
for

K\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
K(x)\leq-C|x|^{-2}
at infinity with
C>0
. The first general existence result for (
1.1
) is due to Ni
[
8
]
, he proved that if
K\leq 0
,
K\not\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|K(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\ell}
at infinity for some
\ell>2
, then for any
\alpha\in(0,\beta)

with
\beta=\min\{8,\frac{\ell-2}{3}\}
, there exists a solution
u
satisfying
u(x)=\alpha\ln|x|+O(1)
at infinity. Later on, it was proved by Cheng-Ni
[
3
]
that
if
K\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
K(x)\sim-|x|^{-\ell}
at infinity with
\ell>2
, then for any
\alpha\in(0,\,\frac{\ell-2}{2})
, (
1.1
) has a unique solution satisfying

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\ln|x|+c_{\alpha}+o(1)\;\;\mbox{as }\;|x|\to\infty,\quad\mbox{with }c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.3)

The solutions set has layer structure, that is
u_{\beta}<u_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for any
0<\beta<\alpha<\alpha_{1}(K)
.
Moreover, a unique maximal solution
U
exists such that
U(x)=\frac{\ell-2}{2}\ln|x|-\ln\ln|x|+O(1)
at infinity.

For general curvature functions
K
, the following quantity was introduced by Cheng-Ni in
[
4
]
,

\alpha_{1}(K)=\sup\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|(1+|x|)^{2\alpha}dx<+\infty\right\}

(1.4)

with the convention
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
. When
K
is controlled polynomially at infinity, using
\alpha_{1}(K)
,
Cheng-Ni characterized the existence to problem (
1.1
): Assume that
K\leq 0
satisfies

\alpha_{1}(K)>0
and
|K(x)|<C(|x|^{m}+1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for some
m>0
.

[formula]

Then for any
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1}(K))
, (
1.1
) has a unique solution such that

u_{\alpha}=\alpha\ln|x|+O(1)\quad{\rm as}\ \ |x|\to\infty.

(1.5)

Later on, by employing a variational method with weighted Sobolev spaces (firstly applied by McOwen in
[
7
]
),
Cheng-Lin
[
2
]
succeeded to handle the existence of problem (
1.1
) without any growth control for
K
:
Assume that
\alpha_{1}(K)>0
for a nonpositive, locally bounded function
K
.

•

For any
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1}(K))
, equation (
1.1
) possesses a unique solution
u_{\alpha}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Ke^{2u}dx=-2\alpha\pi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{1}}\Big|\nabla(u-\alpha\ln|x|)\Big|^{2}dx<+\infty.

(1.6)

•

Moreover, for any
0<\beta<\alpha<\alpha_{1}(K)
, there holds
u_{\beta}<u_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

•

If
\alpha_{1}(K)<\infty
, then
\lim_{\alpha\to\alpha_{1}(K)}u_{\alpha}=u_{\alpha_{1}}
is a solution to (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.6
) with
\alpha=\alpha_{1}(K)
.

The quantity

\mathcal{C}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Ke^{2u}dx

is called the total curvature of the conformal metric
g_{u}=e^{2u}|dx|^{2}
. It plays an important role in the analysis of (
1.1
) and has a notable meaning in geometry. There are many interesting discussions in
[
2
]
. For example, by Theorem 1.4 in
[
2
]
, when
K\leq 0
, a solution
u
to (
1.1
) is logarithmically up bounded at infinity if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}(u)
is finite and the metric
e^{2u}|dx|^{2}
is complete.

However, to claim the asymptotic behavior like (
1.5
), Cheng-Lin need additional assumptions like
({\mathbb{H}}_{0})
. A naturel question is to understand the asymptotic behavior of solutions provided by variational method in
[
2
]
. We will point out that any solution satisfying (
1.5
) is just a Cheng-Lin type solution, see Proposition
2.1
below. Moreover, we want to prove the existence of solutions satisfying (
1.5
) without any growth assumption on the curvature function
K
.

Another naturel question is to ask if there exist solutions with logarithmic growth but anisotropic behavior at infinity, more precisely, solutions of (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.5
) but not (
1.3
). Our purpose here is to answer these questions.

In fact, we propose to replace the pointwise growth assumption
(\mathbb{H}_{0})
by an integral estimate. To this end, consider the following quantity related to
K
, which generalizes
\alpha_{1}(K)
:

\alpha_{p}(K)=\sup\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|^{p}(1+|x|)^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)}dx<+\infty\right\},\quad\forall\;p\geq 1.

(1.7)

We use always the convention
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
. Our first main result states as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K\leq 0
be locally bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Assume that

\alpha_{1}(K)>0
and there exists
p>1
such that
\alpha_{p}(K)>-\infty
.

[formula]

Then for any
\alpha\in(0,\,\alpha_{1}(K))
, problem (
1.1
) possesses a unique solution
u_{\alpha}

satisfying (
1.5
). Moreover, the total curvature of
u_{\alpha}
is
-2\alpha\pi
; and there exists
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for any
\beta\in(0,\alpha_{1}(K)-\alpha)
,

u_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\ln|x|+c_{\alpha}+o\left(|x|^{-\frac{2\beta}{1+2\beta}}\right)\quad{\rm as}\;|x|\to\infty.

(1.8)

Remark 1.1
.

Combining with Proposition
2.1
below, Theorem
1.1
means that under
(\mathbb{H}_{1})
, for any
\alpha\in(0,\,\alpha_{1}(K))
, the unique solution satisfying (
1.6
) given by
[
2
]
has the asymptotic behavior (
1.5
).

Remark 1.2
.

Assumption
(\mathbb{H}_{1})
is weaker than
(\mathbb{H}_{0})
, see Lemma
2.2
below.

Our construction is based on the understanding of
\alpha_{p}(K)
. Indeed, under
({\mathbb{H}}_{1})
, we can prove that
\alpha_{p}(K)
are positive for
p>1
but nearby. This observation allows us to construct suitable super and sub solutions of (
1.1
). Let
w_{0}
be a positive smooth radial function such that

w_{0}(x)=\ln|x|\quad\ {\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{1},

(1.9)

where
B_{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},|x|<1\}.

In the following,
B_{r}(x)
stands for the standard open ball with center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the radius
r
;
B_{r}
denotes always
B_{r}(0)
. We will look for a solution
u_{\alpha}
to (
1.1
) in the form

u_{\alpha}=\alpha w_{0}+v_{\alpha},

where
v_{\alpha}
resolves

-\Delta v=Ke^{2\alpha w_{0}}e^{2v}+\alpha\Delta w_{0}\quad{\rm in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{2}.

The crucial point is to know whether the above equation has a bounded solution, or equivalently, whether
Ke^{2\alpha w_{0}}e^{2v}+\alpha\Delta w_{0}
belongs to the Kato’s class. If the answer is affirmative, the Perron’s method can be employed to get a solution of (
1.1
).

Our second concern is to understand solutions to (
1.1
) with an exotic
K_{0}\leq 0
such that

\alpha_{p}(K_{0})=-\infty\;\;\mbox{for any }\;p>1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{1}(K_{0})>0.

(1.10)

We construct here an explicit model
K_{0}
as follows. Consider the sequence
a_{n}:=(n,0)_{n\geq 2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let

K_{0}(x)=-\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}r_{n}^{-2}n^{-\ell}\eta_{0}\left(\frac{x-a_{n}}{r_{n}}\right)\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.11)

where

\displaystyle\ell>q>1,\quad r_{n}=e^{-n^{q}}\;\;\mbox{for all }\;n\geq 2

and
\eta_{0}
is a smooth radial cut-off function satisfying

\chi_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\eta_{0}\leq\chi_{B_{1}}.

(1.12)

Here
\chi_{\Omega}
means the usual characteristic function of
\Omega
.
Observe that
r_{n}\leq e^{-2}<\frac{1}{4}
for any
n\geq 2
, so

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K_{0}(x)|^{p}(1+|x|)^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)}dx=\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\int_{B_{r_{n}}(a_{n})}|K_{0}(x)|^{p}(1+|x|)^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)}dx=:\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}I_{n}.

Direct computation implies then: for
p\geq 1
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, (as
n
goes to
\infty
)

\displaystyle I_{n}

\displaystyle:=r_{n}^{-2p}n^{\ell p}\int_{B_{r_{n}}(a_{n})}(1+|x|)^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)}\eta_{0}\left(\frac{x-a_{n}}{r_{n}}\right)^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)}dx

\displaystyle\sim r_{n}^{-2p}n^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)+\ell p}\int_{B_{r_{n}}(a_{n})}\eta_{0}\left(\frac{x-a_{n}}{r_{n}}\right)^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)}dx

\displaystyle=M_{p}r_{n}^{2-2p}n^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)+\ell p}

\displaystyle=M_{p}e^{2(p-1)n^{q}}n^{2(\alpha+1-\ell)p-2},

with
M_{p}=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta_{0}^{p}(z)dz>0
. We can verify easily (
1.10
) and
\alpha_{1}(K_{0})=\frac{\ell-1}{2}
.

The following result exhibits clearly the asymptotic behavior of Cheng-Lin’s solutions to (
1.1
) with
K=K_{0}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\ell>q>1
and
K=K_{0}
given by (
1.11
). By
[
2
]
, for any
0<\alpha<\frac{\ell-1}{2}
, there is a unique solution of (
1.1
)
u_{\alpha}
satisfying (
1.6
). Denote
\alpha_{*}=\frac{\ell-q}{2}
, we have:

(i)

For any
0<\alpha<\alpha_{*}
,
u_{\alpha}
satisfying (
1.3
).

(ii)

For
\alpha=\alpha_{*}
, the solution denoted by
u_{*}
satisfies (
1.5
), but the remainder term
u_{\alpha_{*}}-\alpha_{*}\ln|x|
does not converge to any constant as
|x|\to\infty
.

(iii)

For any
\alpha_{*}<\alpha<\frac{\ell-1}{2}
, the remainder term
u_{\alpha}-\alpha\ln|x|
is unbounded at infinity.

To our best knowledge, this is a first example of
K_{0}
without any symmetry or growth assumption, where we show a complete picture of asymptotic behavior for solutions provided in
[
2
]
. This is also a first example of solution to (
1.1
) with nonpositive Gaussian curvature, logarithmic growth but non uniform behavior at infinity.

The rest of our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we show the link between solutions satisfying (

and the relationship between

or

denote always generic positive constants, their values could be changed from one line to another.

Preliminaries

First, we indicate the relationship between solutions satisfying (
1.5
) and solutions given by
[
2
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u
be a continuous solution to (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.5
) with
\alpha<\alpha_{1}(K)
. Then
u
is the Cheng-Lin’s solution, i.e.
u
satisfies (
1.6
).

Proof.
We involve function
w_{0}
given by (
1.9
). So
\Delta w_{0}(x)=0
for
|x|>1
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Delta w_{0}dx=2\pi.

(2.1)

We claim that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla(u-\alpha w_{0})|^{2}dx<\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Ke^{2u}dx=-2\alpha\pi.

(2.2)

Denote
v=u-\alpha w_{0}
, we prove first
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. To this end, set
\eta_{\rho}(t)=\eta_{0}(t/\rho)
for
\rho>0
,
where
\eta_{0}
is a smooth function satisfying (
1.12
). Observe that

-\Delta v=Ke^{2\alpha w_{0}}e^{2v}+\alpha\Delta w_{0}\quad{\rm in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.3)

As
v
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\alpha<\alpha_{1}(K)
, there holds

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta_{\rho}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta_{\rho}^{2}\left(\Delta v^{2}-2v\Delta v\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}v^{2}\Delta\eta_{\rho}^{2}dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta_{\rho}^{2}v\Delta vdx

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\Delta\eta_{\rho}^{2}|dx+2\|v\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\Delta v|dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}+2\|v\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|K|e^{2\alpha w_{0}+2\|v\|_{\infty}}+|\alpha\Delta w_{0}|\right)dx\leq C_{2}.

Here
C_{i}
are positive constants independent on
\rho
. Passing
\rho\to\infty
, we see that
\nabla v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta_{\rho}\Delta vdx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla\eta_{\rho}\cdot\nabla vdx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{2\rho}\setminus B_{\rho}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{2\rho}\setminus B_{\rho}}|\nabla\eta_{\rho}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\eta_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left(\int_{B_{2\rho}\setminus B_{\rho}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark that
\Delta v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\nabla v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we obtain, taking
\rho\to\infty
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Delta vdx=0.

Combining (
2.3
) and (
2.1
), we obtain the second part of (
2.2
).
\Box

We give some elementary consequences of
(\mathbb{H}_{1})
and
(\mathbb{H}_{0})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
(\mathbb{H}_{1})
holds true with
p_{0}>1
. Then for any
p\in[1,p_{0})
,
\alpha_{p}(K)\geq\alpha_{p_{0}}(K)
and
\alpha_{p}(K)\to\alpha_{1}(K)
as
p\to 1^{+}
.

Proof.
For simplicity, we denote
\alpha_{p}(K)
by
\alpha_{p}
. Take any
\beta<\alpha<\alpha_{p_{0}}
and
p\in[1,\,p_{0})
. Denote
\tau_{\alpha}=-2+\frac{2p_{0}p}{p_{0}-p}(\beta-\alpha)<-2
, then

\tau_{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{p}{p_{0}}\right)+\Big[2\alpha p_{0}+2(p_{0}-1)\Big]\frac{p}{p_{0}}=2\beta p+2(p-1).

By Hölder’s inequality, as
\alpha<\alpha_{p_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|^{p}(1+|x|)^{2\beta p+2(p-1)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|^{p_{0}}(1+|x|)^{2\alpha p_{0}+2(p_{0}-1)}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{0}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|x|)^{\tau_{\alpha}}dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{p_{0}}}<\infty.

It means that
\alpha_{p}\geq\alpha_{p_{0}}
for any
p\in[1,\,p_{0})
. We see also that the function
p\mapsto\alpha_{p}
is non-increasing in
[1,p_{0}]
.

If
\alpha_{p_{0}}=\alpha_{1}
, then
\alpha_{p}\equiv\alpha_{p_{0}}
for
p\in[1,p_{0}]
and we are done. Now assume that
\alpha_{p_{0}}<\alpha_{1}
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{p_{0}})
, take

\lambda=\alpha_{1}-\varepsilon,\quad\beta_{2}<\alpha_{p_{0}}<\lambda<\beta_{1}<\alpha_{1},

and consider

h(p):=\beta_{1}(p_{0}-p)+\beta_{2}p_{0}(p-1)-\lambda p(p_{0}-1)\quad\mbox{in }\;[1,p_{0}].

Readily,
h(1)=(\beta_{1}-\lambda)(p_{0}-1)>0
and
h(p_{0})=(\beta_{2}-\lambda)(p_{0}-1)p_{0}<0.
Hence, there exists
p_{*}\in(1,p_{0})
such that
h(p_{*})=0
, or equivalently

\displaystyle 2\beta_{1}\theta+\Big[2\beta_{2}p_{0}+2(p_{0}-1)\Big](1-\theta)=2\lambda p_{*}+2(p_{*}-1)\quad\mbox{where }\;\theta:=\frac{p_{0}-p_{*}}{p_{0}-1}\in(0,1).

As
\theta+p_{0}(1-\theta)=p_{*}
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|^{p_{*}}(1+|x|)^{2\lambda p_{*}+2(p_{*}-1)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|(1+|x|)^{2\beta_{1}}dx\right)^{\theta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|^{p_{0}}(1+|x|)^{2\beta_{2}p_{0}+2(p_{0}-1)}dx\right)^{1-\theta}<\infty.

Thus we get
\alpha_{p_{*}}\geq\lambda
, so
\alpha_{1}-\varepsilon\leq\alpha_{p}\leq\alpha_{1}
for
p\in[1,p_{*}]
, which means
\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\alpha_{p}=\alpha_{1}
.
\Box

Remark 2.1
.

By Lemmas
2.1
, we see that
({\mathbb{H}}_{1})
is equivalent to
({\mathbb{H}}_{1}^{\prime})
: There exists
p>1
such that
\alpha_{p}(K)>0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
K
satisfies
({\mathbb{H}}_{0})
, then there exists
p>1
such that
\alpha_{p}(K)>0
.

Proof.
Fix some
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1})
and choose
p>1
but nearby such that

\beta:=\alpha+\left(\frac{m}{2}+\alpha+1\right)(p-1)<\alpha_{1}.

Then

\displaystyle|K(x)|^{p-1}(1+|x|)^{2(\alpha+1)(p-1)}\leq C(1+|x|)^{m(p-1)+2(\alpha+1)(p-1)}=C(1+|x|)^{2\beta-2\alpha},

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|^{p}(1+|x|)^{2\alpha p+2(p-1)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|(1+|x|)^{2\alpha}\Big[|K(x)|^{p-1}(1+|x|)^{2(\alpha+1)(p-1)}\Big]dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K(x)|(1+|x|)^{2\beta}dx<\infty.

The proof is completed.
\Box

Lemma 2.3

Let

Let

Denote

, then

\displaystyle-\Delta v^{2}=-2v\Delta v-2|\nabla v|^{2}\leq-2v\Delta v=2Ke^{2u_{\alpha}}v\left(e^{2v}-1\right)\leq 0,

that is,

is bounded and subharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, hence
v^{2}
is a constant. So
0=-\Delta v=Ke^{2u_{\alpha}}(e^{2v}-1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which implies that

and the uniqueness follows.

Proof of Theorem

We begin with a result which plays an important role to obtaining the asymptotic behavior (

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

\displaystyle f\in\dot{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\left\{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)dx=0\right\}.

Assume that
\alpha_{p_{0}}(f)>0
with
p_{0}>1
, then there exists a unique ground state solution to
-\Delta w=f
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover,
w\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}w(x)|x|^{\frac{2\beta}{1+2\beta}}=0\quad{\rm for\ any}\;\beta\in(0,\alpha_{1}(f)).

(3.1)

Proof.
The uniqueness of the ground state solution is a trivial consequence of Liouville’s Theorem.
So we prove only the existence of a solution satisfying (
3.1
). By
\alpha_{p_{0}}(f)>0
and Lemma
2.1
,
we get
\alpha_{1}(f)>0
and
f(z)\ln(2+|z|)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. As
f\in\dot{L}^{1}\cap L^{p_{0}}_{loc}
, consider

\displaystyle w(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|x-y|f(y)dy\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Obviously,
-\Delta w=f
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
w\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and for any
x\neq 0
,

\displaystyle 2\pi w(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}f(y)dy=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\ln\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}f(y)dy=:\sum_{i=1}^{3}J_{i}(x),

where
\Omega_{1}=B_{R}
,
R\geq 2
,
\Omega_{2}=B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)
and
\Omega_{3}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})
. For
|x|\geq 4R>0,

\displaystyle|J_{1}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|y|}{|x|}|f(y)|dy\leq\frac{CR}{|x|}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Let
e_{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}
,
p_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-1}
and fix
0<\beta<\alpha_{p_{0}}(f)
, we get

\displaystyle|J_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega_{2}}\ln\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}f(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}}|f(y)|^{p_{0}}(1+|y|)^{2\beta p_{0}+2(p_{0}-1)}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left|\ln\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}\right|^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(1+|y|)^{-2-2\beta p_{0}^{\prime}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(e_{x})}\big|\ln|e_{x}-z|\big|^{p_{0}^{\prime}}|z|^{-2-2\beta p_{0}^{\prime}}|x|^{-2\beta p_{0}^{\prime}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=C|x|^{-2\beta}.

Let
|x|\geq 4R\geq 8
. In
\Omega_{3}
, as
\min(|x-y|,|x|,|y|)\geq R\geq 2
, we can check that

\forall\;y\in\Omega_{3},\qquad\left|\ln\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}\right|\leq\ln|y|,

since
\frac{1}{|y|}\leq\frac{|x|}{|x-y|}\leq|y|
.
Then there holds

\displaystyle|J_{3}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{3}}|f(y)|\ln|y|dy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{3}}|f(y)|^{p_{0}}(1+|y|)^{2\beta p_{0}+2(p_{0}-1)}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{3}}(\ln|y|)^{p_{0}^{\prime}}|y|^{-2-2\beta p_{0}^{\prime}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{R}}(\ln|y|)^{p_{0}^{\prime}}|y|^{-2-2\beta p_{0}^{\prime}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-2\beta}\ln R.

Notice that all the constants
C
involved are independent of
|x|\geq 4R
and
R\geq 2
. Taking
R=|x|^{\frac{1}{1+2\beta}}
, there holds

|w(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{2\beta}{1+2\beta}}\ln|x|\quad{\rm for}\ |x|\;{\rm large}.

(3.2)

This ends the proof of (
3.1
), because we can get (
3.2
) with any
p\in(1,p_{0})
and any
\beta<\alpha_{p}(f)
.

For the third line, we used the symmetric decreasing rearrangement argument, as

\displaystyle\;Ce^{2\|\xi_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}}(k+1)^{2\alpha}\int_{B_{r_{k}}(a_{k})}\eta_{0}\left(\frac{y-a_{k}}{r_{k}}\right)\big(\ln n+\ln|y|\big)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;Ck^{2\alpha}r_{k}^{2}\big(\ln n+\ln k\big).

Since
\alpha<\frac{\ell-1}{2}
, there exists
C>0
such that
|G_{1}(a_{n})|\leq C\ln n
for
n
large. On the other hand,

\displaystyle-G_{2}(a_{n})

\displaystyle\geq-e^{-2\|\xi_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}}r_{n}^{-2}n^{-\ell}(n-1)^{2\alpha}\int_{B_{r_{n}}(a_{n})}\eta_{0}\left(\frac{y-a_{n}}{r_{n}}\right)\ln|a_{n}-y|dy

\displaystyle\geq-Cn^{-\ell+2\alpha}\ln r_{n}

\displaystyle=C_{3}n^{-\ell+2\alpha+q}.

Combining the estimations of
G_{1}(a_{n})
and
G_{2}(a_{n})
, we get, as
\alpha>\frac{\ell-q}{2}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\eta_{\alpha}(a_{n})}{\ln n}=-\infty.

(4.6)

Moreover, remark that for
x
satisfying (
4.1
) and
y\in B_{r_{n}}(a_{n})
, there holds
|x-y|\leq 3|x|
. Similar estimations as above yield that there exists
C>0
such that

\eta_{\alpha}(x)\leq C\ln(2+|x|)\;\;\mbox{in }\;\mathbb{R}^{2}.

As
\widetilde{v}:=\eta_{\alpha}-u_{\alpha}=\eta_{\alpha}-\alpha w_{0}-\xi_{\alpha}
is harmonic over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
\widetilde{v}(x)\leq C\ln(2+|x|)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\widetilde{v}
is constant by Liouville’s theorem. However,
\eta_{\alpha}=\alpha w_{0}+\xi_{\alpha}+C
with
\xi_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}
contradicts the estimate (
4.6
). This means that
\xi_{\alpha}
cannot be bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
\Box

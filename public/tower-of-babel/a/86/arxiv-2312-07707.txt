Learning for System Identification of NDAE-modeled Power Systems
Wenjie Mei, Muhammad Nadeem, MirSaleh Bahavarnia, Ahmad F. Taha

Abstract
System identification through learning approaches is emerging as a promising strategy for understanding and simulating dynamical systems, which nevertheless faces considerable difficulty when confronted with power systems modeled by differential-algebraic equations (DAEs). This paper introduces a neural network (NN) framework for effectively learning and simulating solution trajectories of DAEs. The proposed framework leverages the synergy between Implicit Runge-Kutta (IRK) time-stepping schemes tailored for DAEs and NNs (including a differential NN (DNN)). The framework enforces an NN to cooperate with the algebraic equation of DAEs as hard constraints and is suitable for the identification of the ordinary differential equation (ODE)-modeled dynamic equation of DAEs using an existing penalty-based algorithm. Finally, the paper demonstrates the efficacy and precision of the proposed NN through the identification and simulation of solution trajectories for the considered DAE-modeled power system.



keywords
Differential-algebraic equations (DAEs), neural network (NN), power systems, differential NN (DNN).

1
Introduction

System identification for power networks is essential in the domain of power systems for many reasons: First, accurate models are necessary for effective design, analysis, and control, offering insights into system behaviors. Identifying model parameters allows engineers to further study power dynamics performance and stability, for example. Second, identification can produce predictive models for planning or optimizing the behaviors of power systems, which is important in advanced power models with renewables and emerging technologies. The identified power models obtained through identification techniques facilitate control strategies for enhancing the efficiency and resilience of the controlled systems.

Differential-algebraic equations (DAEs) are essential in power systems due to their ability to model dynamic behaviors and interconnections. They offer a solid mathematical framework capturing differential equations for dynamic responses and algebraic constraints between causal components. For instance, DAEs enable a well-formed representation of interconnected power system components, facilitating the modeling of generators and transformers. This advantage is beneficial as power grids evolve with renewable energy and advanced integration.

1.1
Existing works

The literature pays considerably less attention to system identification challenges in DAEs compared to, for instance, nonlinear ODE models (refer to
Schön et al. 2015
and
Sjöberg et al. 1995
for more details). Existing methods for DAEs are primarily designed for scenarios of addressing numerical aspects within the optimization problem and involving environments with or without disturbances, under the condition that all inputs are known. In such cases, these methods typically have straightforward formulations, with a focus on addressing numerical aspects within the optimization problem (see, for instance,
Esposito and Floudas 2000
and
Bock et al. 2007
, along with relevant references). Also, there exist some works studying the identification of DAEs with process disturbances,
e.g.
, in
Abdalmoaty et al. 2021
. However, the modeling of DAEs frequently encounters challenges in accurately representing nonlinearities through traditional methods. Neural networks (NNs), famous for their usefulness in capturing complex nonlinear interconnections, exhibit high performance, indicating they are ideal for accurately representing the intricate dynamics in DAE-modeled power systems.

NNs have extensively been investigated for learning dynamical systems that are described by ordinary differential equations (ODEs), considerably in nonlinear ODEs (NODEs), providing us with
an effective alternative to conventional numerical methods with high costs
Lu et al. 2021a
;
Yazdani et al. 2020
;
Meade Jr and Fernandez 1994
.
Although NNs have succeeded in learning solution trajectories or simulating dynamic behaviors for ODEs
Chang et al. 2019
;
Fang and Kincaid 1996
, developing an NN-based framework for learning and simulating solution trajectories in the context of nonlinear DAEs (NDAEs) remains as an unresolved challenge. The difficulty arises from the fact that DAEs exhibit infinite stiffness property
Knorrenschild 1992
;
Kim et al. 2021
, which can result in gradient pathologies
Wang et al. 2021
and optimization problems with poor conditioning. These issues often lead to the failure of many trainings. To that end, observing the general forms of NDAEs (especially the dynamic system), in this paper, we introduce a differential NN (DNN)
Chen et al. 2018
, which is a continuous variant of an artificial NN (ANN). DNNs incorporate a feedback element, imparting a memory effect, enabling them to operate with the historical data of a process—a feature not feasible for static algorithms. The significant attribute of a DNN lies in its ability to identify unknown systems by incorporating established ODEs.

1.2
Main Contributions

The main contributions of the current paper can be summarized as follows:

•

We present an NN scheme for identifying the parameters of an NDAE-modeled power system in which a DNN is included to approximate the dynamic equation of the system. Our approach can also be well-adapted to the general NDAEs with an index of
1
, which is the inherent physical property of the considered power model.

•

To guarantee that the proposed NN architecture is effective, under some standard assumptions (imposed on nonlinearities), we further analyze the error dynamic between the true system and the proposed DNN and give the conditions for bounding the identification error at the infinite-time horizon. This is demonstrated by a numerical simulation. We also illustrate that the imposed conditions for identification error analysis are milder than the existing ones in the literature.

(1a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textit{Power flow equations}:\;\;\;\;\;\;0&=A_{a}x_{a}+C_{a}g(x_{d},x_{a}),\end{split}

(1b)

where

contains the algebraic variables,

,

satisfies

) lumps the dynamic equations (set of ODEs) for all the synchronous generators while (

Nadeem et al. 2022
;

;

That being said,
w.l.o.g.

1

Throughout the paper, we deal with the system identification problem for (
1

identification. To collect data sets for identifications, we also consider the output of the NDAE system (
1
) as follows:

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x_{d}^{T}&x_{a}^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}.

(2)

For system identification, the model (
1
) is further assumed to be smoothly parametrized by a

is known and given by the human operator, while the output

has to be sampled at each time instant denoted by

\displaystyle N_{\eta,T}

\displaystyle:=\{(y(t_{k}),u(s))\mid k=1,\dots,\eta,\;s\in[0,T]\},

for some

. The main goal of this work is to estimate the parameter

by using the data set

. To that end, we first propose an NN architecture for identifying the dynamic and algebraic models and then analyze the corresponding identification error between the true output and the predicted output generated by the proposed NN.

3
Proposed Method

This section presents the proposed method for identifying the dynamic and algebraic equations of the NDAE (
1
). For the ODE-modeled dynamic system, a DNN scheme is employed, while for the algebraic one, a standard feedforward neural network (FNN) is utilized.

3.1
Implicit Runge-Kutta (IRK) method

In the system identification of (
1
), we assume that the data of
\{x_{d}(t_{k}),x_{a}(t_{k})\}_{k=1}^{\eta}
is accessible to the human operator in discrete time. Also, numerical analysis methods can be used to approximate solutions of (
1
). In the current paper, we utilize the Implicit Runge-Kutta (IRK) method with
\nu
stages expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{j}&=x_{d}^{n}+\Delta\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}b_{j,i}\tilde{f}\left(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},u\right),\quad 0=\tilde{g}(\alpha_{j},\;\beta_{j}),\end{split}

(3a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}x_{d}^{n+1}&=x_{d}^{n}+\Delta\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}c_{j}\tilde{f}\left(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j},u\right),~\quad 0=\tilde{g}(x_{d}^{n+1},\;x_{a}^{n+1}),\end{split}

(3b)

where
j=1,\dots,\nu
;
\Delta>0
is sufficiently small,
x_{d}^{n}:=x_{d}(t_{n})
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle:=x_{d}\left(t_{n}+\Delta\sum_{k=1}^{\nu}b_{j,k}\right),\quad\beta_{j}:=x_{a}\left(t_{n}+\Delta\sum_{k=1}^{\nu}b_{j,k}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{d},x_{a},u)

\displaystyle:=A_{d}x_{d}+C_{d}f(x_{d},x_{a})+Bu+hw_{0},\quad\tilde{g}(x_{d},x_{a}):=A_{a}x_{a}+C_{a}g(x_{d},x_{a}).

Note that
\{b_{j,i},c_{j}\}
are known parameters and since
E_{d}=I_{n_{d}}
holds,
E_{d}
is omitted.

For the IRK scheme (
3
), after parametrization (with the denotation
\theta
; for example,
\alpha_{j}
is changed to
\alpha_{j}^{\theta}
), we can denote the dynamic states and algebraic variables by

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1}^{\theta},\;\dots,\;\alpha_{\nu}^{\theta},\;x_{d}^{n+1,\theta}],\quad[\beta_{1}^{\theta},\;\dots,\;\beta_{\nu}^{\theta},\;x_{a}^{n+1,\theta}].

These can be used in the training or testing process.

3.3
Estimation method

In the identification process, the two constraints have to be satisfied (
i.e.
, the NNs are restricted to satisfy the differential and algebraic equations described by (
3
)). At the beginning, we select the initial conditions of NNs in the whole considered domain (the initial conditions are randomly distributed). Also, the output training or testing data (
i.e.
,
x_{d}^{n},x_{a}^{n}
) are measured by the human operator so that we have two training sets of size
\eta>0
for the two NNs (one for the dynamic system (
1a
), and another one for the algebraic Eq. (
1b
)), respectively, that is

\displaystyle D_{\eta}^{d}:=\{x_{d}^{n,1},\dots,x_{d}^{n,\eta}\},\quad D_{\eta}^{a}:=\{x_{a}^{n,1},\dots,x_{a}^{n,\eta}\}.

Then, to analyze the difference between the NNs and the IRK scheme dynamics (
3
), we consider the following loss function:

\mathcal{L}({\theta,\eta}):=l(\mathcal{L}_{d}({\theta,\eta}),\mathcal{L}_{a}({\theta,\eta})),

where
l:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is an operator with
l(0,0)=0
, and the functions

increase as the variable
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
increases, where
\cdot
can be any fixed non-negative real number.

In this paper,
\mathcal{L}_{d}({\theta,\eta})
and
\mathcal{L}_{a}({\theta,\eta})
are specifically defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{d}({\theta,\eta})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\eta(\nu+1)}\sum_{x_{d}^{n}\in D^{d}_{\eta}}\;\sum_{j=1}^{\nu+1}\left\|x_{d}^{n}-\hat{x}_{d}^{n,j}(\theta)\right\|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}({\theta,\eta})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\nu+1}\tilde{\ell}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\|\tilde{g}(\alpha_{j}^{\theta},\beta_{j}^{\theta})\|+\left(\|\tilde{g}(x_{d}^{n+1,\theta},x_{a}^{n+1,\theta})\|\right)\right),

where

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{d}^{n,j}(\theta):=\alpha_{j}^{\theta}-h\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}b_{j,i}\tilde{f}\left(\alpha_{i}^{\theta},\beta_{i}^{\theta},u\right),\quad\hat{x}_{d}^{n,\nu+1}(\theta):=x_{d}^{n+1,\theta}-h\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}c_{j}\tilde{f}(\alpha_{j}^{\theta},\beta_{j}^{\theta},u),

and the function
\tilde{\ell}(s)
increases as the variable
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
increases.

Next, we use the two NNs (as depicted in Fig.
1
) to identify the dynamic and algebraic equations. We train the parameters of the considered NNs by minimizing the loss function using a gradient-based optimizer

;
Dogo et al. 2018

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\arg\min_{\theta}}\;\mathcal{L}({\theta,\eta}).

Note that the selection of the explicit form of the operator
\mathcal{L}
can be tricky since there are two main constraints originating from the DAE system (
1
),
i.e.
, the solution of (
1
) should satisfy the dynamic system (
1a
) and stay within the manifold defined as follows:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle:=\{(x_{d},x_{a})\mid\tilde{g}(x_{d},x_{a})=0\},

which is deduced from (
1b
). For that purpose, we select a recurrent penalty method in the sequel to get a well-conditioned loss function. Also, for simplicity, we select the loss function as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\theta,\eta})

\displaystyle=l(\mathcal{L}_{d}({\theta,\eta}),\mathcal{L}_{a}({\theta,\eta})):=w_{d}\mathcal{L}_{d}({\theta,\eta})+w_{a}\mathcal{L}_{a}({\theta,\eta}).

It is noteworthy that we employ an existing algorithm in the literature (Algorithm

Lu et al. 2021b

3.4
Identification error analysis of DNN

In this section, we consider the following structure of general DNN (the DNN shown in Fig.
1

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{nn}

\displaystyle=A_{nn}x_{nn}+B_{nn}\rho(x_{nn})+C_{nn}\gamma(u_{nn}),

(4)

where
A_{nn}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the state matrix of the DNN;

are also matrices; the function

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle:=x_{d}-x_{nn}.

The resulting error dynamics can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{e}

\displaystyle=Ae+\tilde{\phi}(e,x_{d},x_{a},u,t),

where

\tilde{\phi}(e,x_{d},x_{a},u,t):=A_{d}x_{d}+C_{d}f(x_{d},x_{a})+Bu+hw_{0}-A_{nn}x_{nn}-B_{nn}\rho(x_{nn})-C_{nn}\gamma(u_{nn})-Ae
. In the expression of

, we have the variable

, which is estimated by the previously-proposed FNN (see Fig.
1
for clarification). Furthermore, there exists a function

holds under the condition that the function

is continuously differentiable and its Jacobian is invertible (or the DAE system (

) is with index

). Then, the variable

\displaystyle\phi(e,x_{d},u,t):=~

\displaystyle A_{d}x_{d}+C_{d}f(x_{d},\ell(x_{d}))+Bu+hw_{0}

\displaystyle-A_{nn}x_{d}-(A-A_{nn})e-B_{nn}\rho(x_{d}-e)-C_{nn}\gamma(u).

(6)

For the system (
3.4
), several assumptions are imposed in this work to guarantee that the error

is bounded. We first introduce the following assumption on the nonlinearity

Assumption 1
.

There exist matrices

and
K\succ 0
such that the following inequality:

\displaystyle\phi(e,x_{d},u,t)^{\top}L\phi(e,x_{d},u,t)\leq c_{0}(x_{d},u,t)+c_{1}(x_{d},u,t)e^{\top}Ke,

holds where the real numbers

\displaystyle\sup\;c_{0}(x_{d},u,t)=c^{0}<+\infty,\quad\sup\;c_{1}(x_{d},u,t)=c^{1}<+\infty,

\displaystyle\forall x_{d}\in C^{1}_{n_{d}}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),\;u\in C^{1}_{m}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),\;t\geq 0.

Note that Assumption

is non-restrictive, since the right-hand side terms of (
3.4
) can respectively be upper bounded by

\displaystyle(A_{d}-A_{nn})x_{d}+C_{d}f(x_{d},\ell(x_{d}))+Bu+hw_{0}-B_{nn}\rho(x_{d})-C_{nn}\gamma(u)\leq\;c_{0}(x_{d},u,t),

\displaystyle-(A-A_{nn})e+\left(B_{nn}\rho(x_{d})-B_{nn}\rho(x_{d}-e)\right)\leq\;c_{1}(x_{d},u,t)e^{\top}Ke,

\displaystyle\forall x_{d}\in C^{1}_{n_{d}}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),\;u\in C^{1}_{m}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),\;t\geq 0,

under the conditions that
\rho
is a Lipschitz function, the norm of

<+\infty
holds for

Assumption 2
.

The nonlinearity

is a Lipschitz function, the operator

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sup\|e(t)\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{c^{0}}{\lambda_{\min}(P)\lambda_{\min}(P^{-\frac{1}{2}}WP^{-\frac{1}{2}})}}.

Proof.

Consider the Lyapunov function

\displaystyle V(e)=e^{\top}Pe,\;\text{where}\;P=P^{\top}\succ 0.

\displaystyle\dot{V}(e)=2e^{\top}P\dot{e}=2e^{\top}P\Big(Ae+\phi(e,x_{d},u,t)\Big)=e^{\top}(A^{\top}P+PA)e+2e^{\top}P\phi.

\displaystyle 2e^{\top}P\phi=(e^{\top}P)\phi+\phi^{\top}(Pe)\leq\phi^{\top}L\phi+e^{\top}PL^{-1}Pe,

\displaystyle 2e^{\top}P\phi\leq c_{0}(x_{d},u,t)+c_{1}(x_{d},u,t)e^{\top}Ke+e^{\top}PL^{-1}Pe.

Therefore, it can be deduced that

\displaystyle\dot{V}(e)\leq~

\displaystyle e^{\top}(A^{\top}P+PA)e+c_{0}(x_{d},u,t)+c_{1}(x_{d},u,t)e^{\top}Ke+e^{\top}PL^{-1}Pe

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle e^{\top}\Big(A^{\top}P+PA+PL^{-1}P+c^{1}K\Big)e+c^{0}.

Then, by Assumption

\displaystyle\dot{V}(e)\leq

\displaystyle-e^{\top}We+c^{0}\leq-e^{\top}P^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(P^{-\frac{1}{2}}WP^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)P^{\frac{1}{2}}e+c^{0}\leq-\lambda_{\min}(P^{-\frac{1}{2}}WP^{-\frac{1}{2}})V(e)+c^{0}.

Let
\tilde{W}:=P^{-\frac{1}{2}}WP^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. We solve the above inequality and obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(P)\|e(t)\|^{2}\leq~

\displaystyle V(0)e^{-\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{W})t}+\frac{c^{0}}{\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{W})}e^{-\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{W})t}\Big(e^{\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{W})t}-1\Big)\leq\frac{c^{0}}{\lambda_{\min}(\tilde{W})},

(7)

due to

5
Conclusion

This paper introduces an NN framework designed to effectively identify and simulate solution trajectories of NDAEs, such as those characterizing the dynamics of power networks known for exhibiting infinite stiffness. The proposed framework capitalizes on the synergy between IRK time-stepping schemes tailored for NDAEs and NNs (with a DNN). The framework compels an NN to adhere to the algebraic equation of NDAEs as hard constraints and its DNN component is suitable for simulating the ODE-modeled dynamic equation of NDAEs, using a penalty-based method. The paper showcases the efficacy of the proposed NN via the identification and simulation of solution trajectories for the considered DAE-modeled power system. Future work will consider the effect of disturbances that
are present in models, identifying power systems with partially unknown parameters and extending the proposed results to advanced power networks.

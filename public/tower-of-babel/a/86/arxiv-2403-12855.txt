Uniform boundedness of semistable pure sheaves on projective manifolds
Mihai Pavel, Julius Ross, Matei Toma

Abstract
. We prove uniform boundedness statements for semistable pure sheaves on projective manifolds. For example, we prove that the set of isomorphism classes of pure sheaves of dimension 2 that are slope semistable with respect to ample classes that vary in a compact set K K are bounded. We also prove uniform boundedness for pure sheaves of higher dimension, but with restrictions on the compact set K K . As applications we get new statements about moduli spaces of semistable sheaves, and the wall-chamber structure that governs their variation.

1.
Introduction

When constructing moduli spaces of objects in algebraic geometry one of the most basic properties desired is
boundedness
as this ensures that such moduli spaces will have certain finiteness properties (e.g. be of finite type) and, typically, ensuring such boundedness requires imposing some choice of stability condition.

Once such moduli spaces have been constructed, it is natural to ask how they depend on this choice of stability. In the best cases one can divide the space of stability choices into a (locally) finite number of chambers separated by walls such that the moduli spaces are unchanged within chambers, and undergo some transformation, or
wall-crossing
as the choice of stability passes from one chamber to another.

Typically obtaining such a finite chamber structure requires stronger boundedness results. That is, one requires not just that the set of objects that are semistable with respect to one stability condition is bounded, but that this boundedness remains true uniformly as the stability condition varies within a compact set.

In this paper we address this stronger boundedness in the case of pure sheaves on projective manifolds. The precise statements require some definitions (see Section
2
for more details). On a projective manifold
X
of dimension
n
over an algebraically closed field
k
we let
\Amp^{i}(X)
denote the interior of the nef cone of
i
-codimensional cycles. Given

\alpha_{d-1}\in\Amp^{d-1}(X)\text{ and }\alpha_{d}\in\Amp^{d}(X)

we define the slope of a purely
d
-dimensional sheaf
E
on
X
to be

\mu^{\alpha_{d-1}}_{\alpha_{d}}(E):=\frac{\deg_{\alpha_{d-1}}(E)}{\deg_{\alpha_{d}}(E)},

where the degree of
E
with respect to a class
\beta\in\Amp^{p}(X)
is

\deg_{\beta}(E):=\int_{X}\mbox{ch}(E)\beta\mbox{Todd}_{X}.

When
h\in\Amp^{1}(X)
is an ample class, this gives the classical slope function upon setting
(\alpha_{d},\alpha_{d-1})=(h^{d},h^{d-1})
.

Definition 1.1

(Semistability)
.

We say that a
d
-dimensional sheaf

if it is pure
and for all pure
d
-dimensional quotients

We recall that a set
\mathcal{S}
of isomorphism classes of sheaves on

is said to be

if there is a scheme

of finite type and a coherent

on

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}|_{X\times\{s\}}
for some

HL10
, Definition 1.7.5]
.

Definition 1.2

(Uniform Boundedness)
.

Given a numerical class
\gamma\in K(X)_{\text{num}}
of dimension
d>0
and a compact subset
K\subset\Amp^{d}(X)\times\Amp^{d-1}(X)
, by

we mean the following statement:

\BSS(K,\gamma)

of numerical type
\gamma

X

in

As discussed above, the case of a singleton

is important to ensure the existence of

moduli stacks of pure sheaves, and provides one of the key ingredients in the construction of proper moduli spaces of sheaves. On the other hand, for studying the variation of such moduli spaces with respect to the change of semistability, it is crucial to allow
(\beta,\alpha)
to vary uniformly in a given compact set

\BSS
is already known for one-dimensional pure sheaves (see
[
GRT15

[

Theorem 1.3

(

The statement

Comparison with Other Work:

Boundedness of semistable sheaves was intensively studied in the context of classical slope-semistability, and it took the efforts of many mathematicians to completely settle the case
K=\{(h^{d},h^{d-1})\}
with
h\in\Amp^{1}(X)
rational (e.g.
[
Ati57
,
Kle71
,
Tak72
,
Mar81
,
Gie77
,
Lan04
]
).

Statements about uniform boundedness are more recent, and were obtained for torsion-free sheaves on smooth surfaces by Matsuki-Wentworth
[
MW97
]
and for torsion-free sheaves on higher dimensional base by Greb-Ross-Toma (see
[
GT17
,
GRT16
]
). In both cases the approach is based on two main ingredients: the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality and the Hodge Index Theorem, which are both valid when the underlying base space is smooth. To date, it remains uncertain whether these techniques are applicable in the general setting of pure sheaves, whose support scheme might have very bad singularities (see
[
Wu23
,
Lan23
]
for recent progress in this direction).

Instead, we adopt a different strategy to deal with the uniform boundedness problem in pure dimension two, inspired by Maruyama’s work
[
Mar75
,
Mar81
]
. This way we establish the two-dimensional
\BSS
hypothesis in its broadest scope, valid in arbitrary characteristic.

These results have applications to enumerative geometry through Joyce’s work
[
Joy21
]
, in particular to counts of pure two-dimensional sheaves on a Fano threefold. Specifically Corollary
3.6
gives a positive answer to
[
Joy21
, Problem 7.21]
.

Our approach in higher dimensions is based on an analogue of the Grauert-Mülich-Spindler Theorem, which generalizes the classical result (see
[
HL10
, Theorem 3.1.2]
) to the case of pure sheaves. This is likely to be of independent interest, with the caveat that it only holds in zero characteristic. We emphasise that we obtain here the first uniform boundedness results for pure sheaves in higher dimensions. It remains open if our results are also valid in positive characteristic.

As already noted, several applications of our results to the moduli theory of sheaves are presented in Section
6
. They are closely related to the work in
[
MPT24
]
, which proposes a general framework for studying wall-crossing phenomena in higher dimensions.

Preparations

We start by fixing some notations and conventions. Throughout the paper
X
will be a smooth projective variety of dimension

For

\Pseff^{n-p}(X)

\Amp^{p}(X)

\Nef^{p}(X)
.
The cones

.
The elements of

where

is the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves on

.

We also fix a very ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
on
X
and put

the class of a member of the linear system
|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
.

Let
\alpha_{d}\in\Amp^{d}(X)

\alpha_{d-1}\in\Amp^{d-1}(X)

d

\mu^{\alpha_{d-1}}_{\alpha_{d}}(E):=\frac{\deg_{\alpha_{d-1}}(E)}{\deg_{\alpha_{d}}(E)}.

For simplicity, we write

For any coherent sheaf

\theta_{j}(E)=\sum_{i=j}^{n}\mbox{ch}_{n-i}(E)\mbox{Todd}_{i-j}(X)

In particular if

By the

If

is a coherent subsheaf of a coherent sheaf

of

If

We now introduce a semistability notion with respect to classes which are allowed to vary in a compact set.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

. We say that a

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K=K_{d}\times K_{d-1}
,
K^{\prime}=K_{d}^{\prime}\times K_{d-1}
, where
K_{d-1}
is a compact subset of
\Amp^{d-1}(X)

\theta_{d}\in N^{n-d}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}

\theta_{d-1}\in N^{n-d+1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}

-semistable

-dimensional sheaf on

\theta_{d}(E)=\theta_{d}

\theta_{d-1}(E)=\theta_{d-1}

-semistable.

Proof.

Let
E
be a purely
d
-dimensional sheaf as in the statement, and consider a pure quotient
E\to F
. As
E
is
(a,K)
-semistable, there is some
(\beta,\alpha)\in K
such that

\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta}(F)\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta}(E)-a.

Now for

we have

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)={}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta}(F)\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta}(E)\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}-a\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta^{\prime}}(E)\frac{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(E)}{\deg_{\beta}(E)}\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}-a\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta^{\prime}}(E)-\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta^{\prime}}(E)\left(1-\frac{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(E)}{\deg_{\beta}(E)}\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}\right)-a\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}.

Since
F
is a quotient of

we have
\deg_{\beta}(F)\leq\deg_{\beta}(E)
. So clearly the quantity

\mu^{\alpha}_{\beta^{\prime}}(E)\left(1-\frac{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(E)}{\deg_{\beta}(E)}\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}\right)+a\frac{\deg_{\beta}(F)}{\deg_{\beta^{\prime}}(F)}.

is bounded from above by some

depending on
K,K^{\prime},\theta_{d-1},\theta_{d},a
, and independent of

.
∎

Definition 2.3

(Bounded Stability Parameter)

Let
\gamma

.

We will typically apply Lemma

to the situation where

We will use the next elementary "convexity" result when changing degree functions in our boundedness statements.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be a real vector space of dimension

be a basis of

be a compact set contained in the interior of the convex cone spanned by

If we write elements

for

and we express an element

Z\subset X
is a pure two-dimensional subscheme, we simply write
[Z]:=[\mathcal{O}_{Z}]
.

We will use the definition of the
dual sheaf

E^{D}
of a coherent sheaf of dimension
d
on
X
following
[
HL10
, Definition 1.1.7]
, which is

E^{D}:=\mathcal{E}xt_{X}^{n-d}(E,\omega_{X}).
The sheaf
E
will be called reflexive if the natural morphism
E\to E^{DD}

is an isomorphism. We will also say that a sheaf
E
has property
(S_{2})
if

(S_{2}):\ \depth(E_{x})\geq\min\{2,\mbox{codim}(x,\mbox{Supp}(E)\}\text{ for all }x\in\mbox{Supp}(E).

We recall also the
(S_{2,c})
property (discussed in
[
HL10
, Section 1]
) for coherent sheaves
E
of codimension
c:=n-\dim(E)
on
X
, which is

(S_{2,c}):\ \depth(E_{x})\geq\min\{2,\dim(\mathcal{O}_{X,x})-c\}\text{ for all }x\in\mbox{Supp}(E).

Note that the
(S_{2})
property is equivalent to the
(S_{2,c})
property for pure sheaves of codimension
c
in
X
. Thus by
[
HL10
, Proposition 1.1.10]
a sheaf
E
of codimension
c
on
X
is reflexive if and only if
E
has
(S_{2,c})
if and only if
E
is pure and has
(S_{2})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E
be a 2-dimensional pure sheaf on
X
satisfying
(S_{2})
. Then

H^{i}(X,E^{D})\cong H^{2-i}(X,E)^{\vee}

for
0\leq i\leq 2
.

Proof.

We have seen that
E
is pure and has
(S_{2})
if and only if
E
has
(S_{2,c})
where
c
is the codimension of the support of
E
in
X
. By
[
HL10
, Proposition 1.1.6]
we see that under the
(S_{2,c})
assumption on
E

\mathcal{E}xt^{q}_{X}(E,\omega_{X})=0,\quad\text{for }q\neq c.

By the local-to-global Ext spectral sequence,

H^{p}(X,\mathcal{E}xt^{q}_{X}(E,\omega_{X}))\implies\Ext^{p+q}_{X}(E,\omega_{X}),

we deduce that

H^{i}(X,E^{D})=H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}xt^{c}_{X}(E,\omega_{X}))\cong\Ext^{i+c}_{X}(E,\omega_{X})

for any
i
. By Serre Duality on
X
, the latter space is isomorphic to
H^{2-i}(X,E)^{\vee}
, which proves the claim.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If
E
is a two dimensional reflexive sheaf on
X
, one has
\theta_{i}(E^{D})=(-1)^{i}\theta_{i}(E)

for all
i\in\{0,1,2\}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
we have for any integer ample class
h\in\Amp^{1}(X)
and any integer
m
:

P_{E}^{h}(m)=\chi(E(m))=\chi(E^{D}(-m))=P_{E^{D}}^{h}(-m)

and the equality
\theta_{i}(E^{D})=(-1)^{i}\theta_{i}(E)
follows.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
r,\beta,b,a,K
fixed as above, the two-dimensional support cycles
Y(E)
with
[E]\in\Sigma(r,\beta,b,a,K)
are bounded.

Proof.

For any
E\in\Sigma
, the multiplicity
\mult(E)
is given by

\displaystyle\mult(E)=\sum\nu_{Y,E}\mult(Y).

As the multiplicity of each
E\in\Sigma
is fixed to be
r
, it follows that the multiplicities
\nu_{Y,E}
and each
\mult(Y)
in the sum is bounded.

Let
\mathfrak{Y}
be the set of all prime cycles in the two-dimensional support cycle of
E
with
[E]\in\Sigma
. As the multiplicity function (with respect to
h^{2}
) is bounded on
\mathfrak{Y}
, it follows by Chow’s Lemma (see
[
Gro61
, Lemme 2.4]
) that
\mathfrak{Y}
is a bounded family.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Step 1.
Reduction to the
(S_{2})
case.

If
E^{DD}
is the reflexive hull of

, then there is a short exact sequence

(3.4)

0\to E\to E^{DD}\to T\to 0,

with

supported in codimension
\geq 2
in
\mbox{Supp}(E)
. This gives

\chi(E^{DD})\geq\chi(E)\geq b.

Therefore
[E^{DD}]\in\Sigma(r,\beta,b,a,K)
as soon as
[E]\in\Sigma
, and if we bound the classes of

, this will bound
\chi(E)
and

Hence in what follows we may assume that each

-sheaf. We argue now by induction on

.

Step 2.
Base case

Since

with

. Hence the pure dimensional coherent sheaves

with

are slope semistable with respect to any ample polarization on

and in particular with respect to

, Theorem 1.8]
.

Step 3.
Induction step.

Claim 1.
There is some
m_{0}=m_{0}(r,\beta,b,a,K)
such that for every
(S_{2})
-sheaf

To prove this, first we check that there is some

. Let

be the scheme theoretic support of

.
By Lemma

Suppose there is a non-zero section in

. This section seen as a morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E^{D}(-m)
factors through a closed subscheme

of

and induces injective morphisms
\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to E^{D}(-m)

and
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(m)\to E^{D}
. Denote by

the image of the dual morphism

\mu^{\alpha}(E)-a\leq\mu^{\alpha}(F)

, since

(a,K)
-semistable.
On the other hand from the inclusions

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}(F)\leq{}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}((\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{D}(-m))=-\mu^{\alpha}((\mathcal{O}_{Z})^{DD}(m))\leq-\mu^{\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{Z}(m))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\mu^{\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})-m\frac{\alpha h\theta_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})}{r^{\prime}},

is the multiplicity of

.
Thus

m\frac{\alpha h\theta_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})}{r^{\prime}}\leq-\mu^{\alpha}(E)+a-\mu^{\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{Z}).

By Lemma
3.5

we know that the support cycle of

is bounded, hence also the cycle
[Z]
is bounded. Since

is moreover pure, it follows using Grothendieck’s boundedness result

. Any

may be expressed as a positive combination of finitely many fixed integer ample

. Hence

which leads to the existence of

as desired.

Thus for

, we have

\displaystyle h^{0}(X,E(m))={}

\displaystyle\chi(E(m))+h^{1}(X,E(m))-h^{2}(X,E(m))

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\chi(E(m))\geq r\frac{m^{2}}{2}+m\beta h+b,

hence the claim.

Claim 2.
There is some
m_{1}=m_{1}(r,\beta,b,a,K)
such that for every
(S_{2})
-sheaf
[E]\in\Sigma
there is some irreducible component
Y_{1}
of
\mbox{Supp}(E)
and a non-trivial morphism
\mathcal{O}_{Y_{1}}\to E(m_{1})
.

To prove this, let
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,E(m_{0}))
be some non-zero section, which exists by Claim 1. Let
Y
be the scheme theoretic support of
E
and let
[Y]=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\nu_{i}[Y_{i}]
be its associated two-dimensional cycle (with positive coefficients).
The section
\sigma
seen as a morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E(m_{0})
factors through a closed subscheme

of
Y
and induces an injective morphism
\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to E(m_{0})
. The scheme

is pure two-dimensional, since

is pure. We suppose that
Y_{1}
is an irreducible component of the associated cycle
[Z]
of

.

We first check that the scheme

is bounded.
We have seen that the support cycle
[Z]
of

is bounded when
[E]
belongs to
\Sigma
, by Lemma

. Thus the function
\deg_{\tilde{h}^{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})

By Grothendieck’s Lemma it follows that the function

.
After possibly cutting

We thus get a non-trivial morphism

Putting

of

For the boundedness of

The set of isomorphism classes of sheaves

Next note that the multiplicity

of

4.
A generalized Grauert-Mülich-Spindler Theorem

From now on we assume that
X
is defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. In this section we prove an analogue of the Grauert-Mülich-Spindler Theorem for
(a,\alpha h^{2},\alpha h)
-semistable

-dimensional sheaves on

, where
\alpha\in\Amp^{d-2}(X)
and

are fixed. This is our main ingredient in showing uniform boundedness results in higher dimensions.

Set
\Pi=|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
and consider the
incidence variety
Z=\{([D],x)\in\Pi\times X\mid x\in D\}
with its natural projections:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

One can show that

is a projective bundle. Also, if
[D]\in\Pi
is a closed point, then the fibre
p^{-1}([D])
is identified by
q
with
D\subset X
.

Relative HN filtration.

Let
E
be a purely
d
-dimensional sheaf on
X
. By the analogue
[
MPT24
, Theorem 3.2]
of
[
HL10
, Thm. 2.3.2]
for our notion of

-semistability, there exists a relative Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{F}=q^{*}E

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{F}_{l}=\mathcal{F}

and an open subset
U\subset\Pi
such that

1.
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
is flat over
U
for each
i=1,\ldots,l
,

2.
0=\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{D}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}|_{D}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{F}_{l}|_{D}=E|_{D}
is the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of

for each

, with
\mu_{1}>\ldots>\mu_{l}
, where
\mu_{i}=\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}((\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})|_{D})
. As

is connected and the
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
are flat over

, the

do not depend on

is an intregral smooth divisor on

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the above notation, assume further that
E
is
d
-dimensional and
(a,\alpha h^{2},\alpha h)
-semistable on

. Then, for general

and

, one of the following holds:

(1)

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}((q^{*}E/\mathcal{F}_{i})|_{D})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E)-\frac{1}{2}-a
, or

(2)

\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1}\leq 1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}((q^{*}E/\mathcal{F}_{i})|_{D})<\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E)-\frac{1}{2}-a
for some

, and set
F^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
F^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{F}/F^{\prime}
. See
[
Fle84
, Prop. 1.11]
for the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
Z
,
Y
be
\mathbb{Q}
-schemes and
q:Z\to Y
a surjective smooth mapping of finite presentation with geometrically connected fibers. Let
\mathcal{T}_{Z/Y}
be the relative tangent sheaf. If
E
is a coherent sheaf on
Y
and

is a coherent subsheaf such that

\displaystyle\mbox{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{Z}}(\mathcal{T}_{Z/Y}\otimes F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime})=0,\quad F^{\prime\prime}=q^{*}E/F^{\prime},

then there exists a unique coherent subsheaf
F
of

with
F^{\prime}=q^{*}F
.

We apply this result to the morphism

. Suppose for contradiction that
\mbox{Hom}(\mathcal{T}_{Z/X}\otimes F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime})=0
. Then there exists a subsheaf

such that

, hence there is also a quotient

such that

. As

, we have

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(Q|_{D})<\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E)-\frac{1}{2}-a,

which gives

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(Q)<\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E)-a,

by using Lemma

is

Lemma 4.3
.

We have
\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(F|_{D})=\mu^{\alpha h}_{\alpha h^{2}}(F)-1/2
for general
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
.

Proof.

This uses the additivity of degree functions in short exact sequences. By using the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to F(-1)\to F\to F|_{D}\to 0,

with
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
general, we obtain

\displaystyle\deg_{\alpha h}(F|_{D})

\displaystyle=\deg_{\alpha h^{2}}(F)

\displaystyle\deg_{\alpha}(F|_{D})

\displaystyle=\deg_{\alpha h}(F)-\frac{1}{2}\deg_{\alpha h^{2}}(F).

Hence

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(F|_{D})=\frac{\deg_{\alpha}(F|_{D})}{\deg_{\alpha h}(F|_{D})}=\mu^{\alpha h}_{\alpha h^{2}}(F)-\frac{1}{2}.

∎

Thus we can find a nonzero map
\mathcal{T}_{Z/X}\otimes F^{\prime}\to F^{\prime\prime}
, which restricted to a general fiber of
p
gives a nonzero map

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{Z/X}|_{D}\otimes F^{\prime}|_{D}\to F^{\prime\prime}|_{D}.

Hence

(4.1)

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(\mathcal{T}_{Z/X}|_{D}\otimes F^{\prime}|_{D})\leq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\max}(F^{\prime\prime}|_{D}).

From here the proof follows exactly as in
[
HL10
, p. 66]
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the kernel of the evaluation map

\ev:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(1).

Then
Z=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee})
and we have the Euler sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to q^{*}\mathcal{K}\otimes p^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\to\mathcal{T}_{Z/X}\to 0.

From this and the Koszul complex corresponding to the evaluation map
\ev
above, we obtain a surjection

\left(\Lambda^{2}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\right)|_{D}\to\mathcal{T}_{Z/X}|_{D}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(\mathcal{T}_{Z/X}|_{D}\otimes F^{\prime}|_{D})\geq{}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(\Lambda^{2}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(a))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\otimes F^{\prime}|_{D})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\otimes F^{\prime}|_{D})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(F^{\prime}|_{D})-1.

Putting this inequality together with (
4.1
) yields

\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1}=\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(F^{\prime}|_{D})-\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\max}(F^{\prime\prime}|_{D})\leq 1,

since
\mu_{i}=\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(F^{\prime}|_{D})
and
\mu_{i+1}=\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\max}(F^{\prime\prime}|_{D})
by construction.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
E
be a
d
-dimensional
(a,\alpha h^{2},\alpha h)
-semistable sheaf. Then there is some
a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
depending only on
a
and
\deg_{h^{d}}(E)
such that
E|_{D}
is
(a^{\prime},\alpha h,\alpha)
-semistable for general
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
.

Proof.

We choose a general divisor
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
such that
D
avoids the associated points of
E
and
E|_{D}
remains pure. Also denote by

0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{l}=E|_{D}

the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
E|_{D}
. Now consider a pure quotient
E|_{D}\to F
, and note that by construction
\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(F)\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(E|_{D})
.
By Theorem
4.1
, for general
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
and
i=1,\ldots,l-1
we have

(1)

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E|_{D}/E_{i})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E|_{D})-a
, or

(2)

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{i+1}/E_{i})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{i}/E_{i-1})-1
.

Using the fact that
\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{i+1}/E_{i})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E|_{D}/E_{i})
, we deduce that for each
i

(1’)

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{i+1}/E_{i})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E|_{D})-a
, or

(2)

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{i+1}/E_{i})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{i}/E_{i-1})-1
.

Applying
(1^{\prime})
and
(2)
for
i
starting with
i=l-1
, we find successively
\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(E|_{D})=\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{l}/E_{l-1})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E|_{D})-a
and in this case we stop, or we find

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{l}/E_{l-1})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{l-1}/E_{l-2})-1
and in this case we make
i=l-2
and continue. Eventually we get

\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(F)\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\min}(E|_{D})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E|_{D})-\max(l-1,l-1+a).

We have also used that
\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E_{1}/E_{0})=\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h,\max}(E|_{D})\geq\mu^{\alpha}_{\alpha h}(E|_{D})
.
∎

5.
Boundedness results in higher dimension

We are now ready to prove our main boundedness result for higher dimensional pure sheaves.

Theorem 5.1

(Uniform boundedness of pure sheaves)
.

Let
d\geq 2
, let
K_{1}\subset\Amp^{1}(X)
and
K_{d}\subset\Amp^{d}(X)
be compact sets, and let
K_{d-1}=K_{1}h_{1}\cdots h_{d-2}\subset\Amp^{d-1}(X)
for some fixed
h_{1},\ldots,h_{d-2}\in\Amp^{1}(X)
rational ample divisor classes. Fix
a\in\mathbb{R}
, set
K=K_{d}\times K_{d-1}
, and let
{\mathfrak{E}}
be the set of isomorphism classes of
d
-dimensional sheaves of fixed numerical type on
X
which are
(a,K)
-semistable. Then
{\mathfrak{E}}
is bounded.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and let
h_{1},\ldots,h_{d-2}\in\Amp^{1}(X)
be rational ample divisor classes. Suppose also
K_{1}\subset\Amp^{1}(X)
and
K_{d}\subset\Amp^{d}(X)
are compact sets, and define

K_{d-1}=K_{1}h_{1}\cdots h_{d-2}\subset\Amp^{d-1}(X).

Then the set of isomorphism classes of
d
-dimensional coherent sheaves of fixed numerical type and slope-semistable with respect to some
(\beta,\alpha)\in K_{d}\times K_{d-1}
is bounded.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We assume that all sheaves in
{\mathfrak{E}}
have fixed classes
\theta_{j}
,
0\leq j\leq d
. We shall argue by induction on
d
. If
d=2
, then for any
[E]\in{\mathfrak{E}}
, we have that
E
is
(a,\beta,\alpha)
-semistable for some
\beta\in K_{2}
and some
\alpha\in K_{1}
. We can therefore apply Theorem
3.2
and obtain that
{\mathfrak{E}}
is bounded when
d=2
.

Now let
d>2
. For the induction step, we may assume that the class
h_{1}
corresponds to a very ample divisor
H_{1}
on
X
, by replacing
h_{1}
by some multiple
mh_{1}
if necessary. Let
[E]\in{\mathfrak{E}}
. Then
E
is
(a,\beta,\alpha h_{1}\cdots h_{d-2})
-semistable for some
\beta\in K_{d}
and some
\alpha\in K_{1}
. According to Lemma
2.2
there is some
a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, depending on
K,\theta_{d},\theta_{d-1}
and
a
, such that
E
is
(a^{\prime},\alpha h_{1}^{2}h_{2}\cdots h_{d-2},\alpha h_{1}\cdots h_{d-2})
-semistable.

By Corollary
4.4
we obtain that
E|_{D_{1}}
is
(a^{\prime\prime},\alpha h_{1}\cdots h_{d-2},\alpha h_{2}\cdots h_{d-2})
-semistable for some general
D_{1}\in|H_{1}|
and
a^{\prime\prime}
depending only on
K,\theta_{d},\theta_{d-1}
and
a
.
Using again Lemma
2.2
, we can find
b\in\mathbb{R}
(depending on
K,\theta_{d},\theta_{d-1}
and
a
) such that
E|_{D_{1}}
is
(b,h_{1}^{d-1},\alpha h_{2}\cdots h_{d-2})
-semistable. Also note that the numerical classes of the restricted sheaves
E|_{D_{1}}
, with
[E]\in{\mathfrak{E}}
, depend only on the
\theta_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq d
, and
h_{1}
.
Therefore all restricted sheaves
E|_{D_{1}}
, with
[E]\in{\mathfrak{E}}
, fit inside a suitable family
\Sigma
of
(d-1)
-dimensional pure sheaves of fixed numerical type on
X
which are
(b,K^{\prime})
-semistable, where
K^{\prime}=\{h_{1}^{d-1}\}\times(K_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{d-2})
. By induction we have that
\Sigma
is bounded, and so
\mu^{h_{1}^{d-2}}_{h_{1}^{d-1},\max}
is bounded on
\Sigma
. As

\mu^{h_{1}^{d-2}}_{h_{1}^{d-1}}(E|_{D_{1}})=\mu^{h_{1}^{d-1}}_{h_{1}^{d}}(E)-\frac{1}{2}

for

and general

\mu^{h_{1}^{d-1}}_{h_{1}^{d},\max}
is upper bounded on
{\mathfrak{E}}
. It follows that
{\mathfrak{E}}
is also bounded, cf.
[
Lan04
,
HL10
]
.
∎

6.
Applications

As announced in the introduction, the main application of our boundedness results are to wall-crossing with respect to variation of stability notions. With the exception of Proposition
6.2
and Corollary
6.4
, which are valid in any characteristic, for the results of this section we need to assume that our base field is of characteristic zero. We briefly recall the setup proposed in
[
MPT24
]
.

Fix
d
an integer with
2\leq d\leq n
and let
E
be a
d
-dimensional coherent sheaf on
X
.
For a fixed element
\alpha=(\alpha_{d},\ldots,\alpha_{0})\in\Amp^{d}(X)\times\ldots\times\Amp^{0}(X)
the
\alpha
-Hilbert polynomial
and the
reduced
\alpha
-Hilbert polynomial
of
E
are defined by

P_{\alpha}(E,m)=\sum_{i=r}^{d}\deg_{\alpha_{i}}(E)\frac{m^{i}}{i!}\ \text{and by}\ p_{\alpha}(E)=\frac{P_{\alpha}(E)}{\deg_{\alpha_{d}}(E)}

respectively. The sheaf
E
is then said to be
\alpha
-semistable
if it is pure and
for all pure
d
-dimensional quotients
E\to F
one has

p_{\alpha}(F,m)\geq p_{\alpha}(E,m)\text{ for }m\gg 0.

When
\alpha=(h^{d},\ldots,h^{0})
the usual notion of semistabilty in the sense of Gieseker-Maruyama-Simpson is recovered by the above definition. Clearly an
\alpha
-semi-stable sheaf in the above sense is necessarily
(\alpha_{d},\alpha_{d-1})
-slope-semistable in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Fix now
\gamma
a numerical class of
d
-dimensional coherent sheaves and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{d},\ldots,\alpha_{0})\in\Amp^{d}(X)\times\ldots\times\Amp^{0}(X)
be as above. Then the substack
\mathcal{C}oh_{X}^{\gamma,\alpha ss}
of
\alpha
-semistable coherent sheaves of numerical type
\gamma
on
X
is open in the algebraic stack
\mathcal{C}oh_{X}
of coherent sheaves on
X
,

[
MPT24
, Proposition 4.1]
. Under a suitable boundedness assumption and making use of
[
AHLH23
, Theorem 7.2.7]
it is further proved in
[
MPT24
, Theorem 5.1]
that
\mathcal{C}oh_{X}^{\gamma,\alpha ss}
admits a proper good moduli space
M_{X}^{\gamma,\alpha ss}
in the sense of
[
Alp13
]
. Combining this with our Corollary
5.2
we get

Corollary 6.1
.

If
\gamma
is a numerical class of
d
-dimensional coherent sheaves on
X
and
\alpha\in\Amp^{d}(X)\times\ldots\times\Amp^{0}(X)
is such that
\alpha_{d-1}
is a complete intersection class

Again under suitable boundedness assumptions, the following existence result for locally finite rational chamber structures for the variation of semistability was proved in

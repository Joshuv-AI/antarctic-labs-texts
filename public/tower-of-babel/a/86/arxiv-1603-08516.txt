A blow-up criterion for strong solutions to three-dimensional compressible magnetohydrodynamic equations
Xin Zhong

Abstract
We are concerned with an initial boundary value problem for the compressible magnetohydrodynamic equations with viscosity depending on the density. It is shown that for the initial density away from vacuum, the strong solution to the problem exists globally if the gradient of velocity satisfies ‖ ∇ 𝐮 ‖ L 2 ​ ( 0 , T , L ∞ ) < ∞ \|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty})}<\infty . Our method relies upon the delicate energy estimates and elliptic estimates.



Keywords: Compressible magnetohydrodynamic equations; blow-up criterion; variable viscosity.

Math Subject Classification: 76W05; 35B65

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded smooth domain, we consider the magnetohydrodynamic equations for compressible
fluids in
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\divv(\rho\mathbf{u})=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\mathbf{u})+\divv(\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})+\nabla(A\rho^{\gamma})=\divv(2\nu\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{u}))+\nabla(\lambda\divv\mathbf{u})+(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})\times\mathbf{H},\\
\partial_{t}\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{H}\divv\mathbf{u}=\beta\Delta\mathbf{H},\\
\divv\mathbf{H}=0.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here, the unknown functions
\rho
,
\mathbf{u},\ \mathbf{H}
are the density, velocity of the fluid, and the magnetic field, respectively.
A
is a positive constant, and
\gamma>1
is the specific heat ratio. Since the concrete value of
A
do not play a special role in our discussion, in what follows, we assume

A=1

throughout this paper. In addition,
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{u})
denotes the deformation tensor

\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{u})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{tr}).

We assume that the viscosity coefficients
\nu=\nu(\rho),\lambda=\lambda(\rho)
are twice continuously differentiable functions and satisfy

\nu\geq\underline{\nu}>0,\ \mu\triangleq\nu+\lambda\geq\underline{\mu}>0.

(1.2)

Furthermore, suppose that there is a constant
k>-1
such that

\lambda(\rho)=k\nu(\rho).

(1.3)

The constant
\beta>0
is the magnetic diffusive coefficient.

We study an initial boundary value problem for (
1.1
) with the initial condition

(\rho,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{H})(0,x)=(\rho_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{H}_{0})(x),\ \ x\in\Omega,

(1.4)

and the boundary condition

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0},\ \mathbf{H}=\mathbf{0},\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega.

(1.5)

Throughout this paper, we further assume that the initial density is away from vacuum, that is,

\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(x)\geq\underline{\rho}>0,\ x\in\Omega,

(1.6)

for a positive constant
\underline{\rho}
.

There is huge literature on the studies about the theory of
well-posedness of solutions to the Cauchy problem and initial-boundary-value problem for the system (
1.1
) ) in the case that the viscosity coefficients
\nu
and
\lambda
are both constants, refer to
[
9
,
10
,
20
,
29
]
and references therein.
In particular, non-vacuum small perturbations of a uniform non-vacuum constant state have been shown existing globally in time and remain smooth in
[
22
]
. However, the global existence of the strong solutions for arbitrary initial data remain largely open for the compressible magnetohydrodynamic equations with constant viscosity coefficients, but there is new progress recently
[
18
,
21
]
.

In the last decades, Beale-Kato-Majda
[
1
]
established
a well-known blowup criterion for the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations that a smooth solution exists globally if

\lim_{T\rightarrow T^{*}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

(1.7)

Later, Huang et al.
[
13
,
16
]
extended the Beale-Kato-Majda criterion for the ideal incompressible flows to the viscous compressible barotropic flows (see also
[
4
]
). Furthermore, Sun-Wang-Zhang
[
25
]
established an alternative Beale-Kato-Majda blow-up criterion for the strong solution to the compressible three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations in terms of the density instead of gradient of velocity. Suen
[
24
]
improved the result in
[
25
]
to the compressible magnetohydrodynamic equations, which shows that the mechanism of blow-up is independent of the magnetic field. For more information on the blow-up criteria of compressible flows, we refer to
[
11
,
12
,
14
,
24
,
26
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
and the references therein.

Despite the progress on various blow-up criterion, and the interesting results on global well-posedness of the classical solution to the three-dimensional compressible magnetohydrodynamic equations for initial data with small total energy but possible large oscillations and containing vacuum states
[
18
]
, the behavior near vacuum of solutions to the system (
1.1
) remains to be one of the challenging issues for the global well-posedness of smooth solutions.

It should be noted that all the results mentioned above on the blow-up of smooth solutions of compressible flows are for constant viscosity, i.e., both
\nu
and
\lambda
are constant. Therefore, a natural question arises: how we can describe the mechanism of blow up to ensure the global existence of strong (or classical) solutions to the system (
1.1
)? Recently, Cai-Sun
[
2
]
obtained a blow-up criterion for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with variable viscosity. However, the mechanism of blow-up of strong solutions to the problem (
1.1
) with variable viscosity is still
unknown. In fact, this is the main aim of this paper.

Our main result reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1

Assume that the initial data
(\rho_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{H}_{0})
satisfy (
1.6
) and

\displaystyle(\rho_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{H}_{0})\in H^{3}(\Omega)

(1.8)

with the compatibility conditions on
\partial\Omega
,

\mathbf{u}_{0}=\mathbf{0},\ \mathbf{H}_{0}=\mathbf{0},\ \nabla\rho_{0}^{\gamma}=\divv(2\nu(\rho_{0})\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{u_{0}}))+\nabla(\lambda(\rho_{0})\divv\mathbf{u}_{0}).

(1.9)

Let
(\rho,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{H})\in C([0,T^{*}),H^{3}(\Omega))
be a strong solution to the problem (
1.1
) with the conditions (
1.2
)–(
1.5
).
If
T^{*}<\infty
is the maximal time of existence for that solution,
then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow T^{*}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty})}=\infty.

(1.10)

Several remarks are in order.

Remark 1.1

The local existence of a strong solution with initial data as in Theorem

can be established in the same manner as
[

. Hence the maximal time

is well-defined.

Remark 1.2

It is worth noting that the blow-up criteria (

[
2
, Theorem 1.4]

1.1
may be viewed as an extension of
[
2

Remark 1.3

The techniques in this paper can be easily adapted to the periodic case. And the same criterion will be derived.

Remark 1.4

Similar ideas can be applied to study the case on the viscous, compressible, and heat conducting magnetohydrodynamic equations with variable viscosity.
This will be reported in a forthcoming paper
[
34
]

Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall some known facts and elementary inequalities that will be used frequently later.

We begin with the following Gronwall’s inequality, which plays a central role in proving a priori estimates on strong solutions
(\rho,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{H})
.

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that
h
and
r
are integrable on
(a,b)
and nonnegative a.e. in
(a,b)
. Further assume that
y\in C[a,b],y^{\prime}\in L^{1}(a,b)
, and

y^{\prime}(t)\leq h(t)+r(t)y(t)\ \ \text{for}\ a.e\ t\in(a,b).

Then

y(t)\leq\left[y(a)+\int_{a}^{t}h(s)\exp\left(-\int_{a}^{s}r(\tau)d\tau\right)ds\right]\exp\left(\int_{a}^{t}r(s)ds\right),\ \ t\in[a,b].

Proof.

See
[
27
, pp. 12–13]
.
\Box

Next, the following well-known inequalities will be frequently used later.

Lemma 2.2

For
p\in[2,6],q,m\in[1,\infty),\alpha\in(0,m)
,
\varepsilon>0,a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\begin{split}&\|f\|_{L^{m}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\alpha q}}^{\frac{\alpha}{m}}\|f\|_{L^{(m-\alpha)q}}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{m}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{(H{\"{o}}lder's inequality)}\\
&|ab|\leq\varepsilon|a|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+\left(\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}(1-\theta)|b|^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}},\ \ \ \ \text{(Young's inequality)}\end{split}

and

\|g\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(p,\Omega)\|g\|_{H^{1}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ g\in H^{1}(\Omega).\ \ \text{(Sobolev's inequality)}

Proof.

See
[
17

.
\Box

Finally, we give some regularity results for the following Lamé system with Dirichlet boundary condition

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\mathbf{U}+\nabla\divv\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{F},\ \ x\in\Omega,\\
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{0},\ \ x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Lemma 2.3

Let

and U be a solution of (

depending only on

and

such that the following estimates hold:

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
(\rho,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{H})
be a strong solution described in Theorem
1.1
. Suppose that (
1.10
) were false, that is, there exists a constant
M_{0}>0
such that

\lim_{T\rightarrow T^{*}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty})}\leq M_{0}<\infty.

(3.1)

For simplicity, in what follows, we denote by

\int\cdot\text{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\cdot\text{d}x,

and
C,C_{1},C_{2}
stand for generic positive constants depending only on
M_{0},\beta,T^{*}
, and the initial data.

Lemma 3.1

There exist two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending only on
M_{0}
and the upper and lower bounds of
\rho_{0}
such that

C_{2}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq C_{1}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

(3.2)

Proof.

In accordance with the continuity equation (
1.1
)
1
, the density
\rho
computed along characteristics satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\rho(t,\mathbf{X}(t))=-\rho(t,\mathbf{X}(t))\divv\mathbf{u}(t,\mathbf{X}(t)),\ \mathbf{X}(0)=x,\ t\in[0,T].

Thus, a direct application of Gronwall’s inequality yields that for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
,

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}\rho_{0}(x)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}\|\divv\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}dt\right)\leq\rho(t,x)\leq\sup_{x\in\Omega}\rho_{0}(x)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\divv\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}dt\right),

which combined with (
3.1
) leads to the desired result (
3.2
).

\Box

Remark 3.1

Since
\nu,\lambda
are both twice continuously differentiable functions, we deduce from (
3.2
) that

\|(\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}<\infty.

(3.3)

It will be used frequently in our later analysis.

Lemma 3.2

There exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
,
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\gamma}}
, and
\|\mathbf{H}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
such that for any
T\in[0,T^{*})
, it holds that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\left(\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\rho^{\gamma}+|\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int\left(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dxdt\leq C.

(3.4)

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.1
)
2
and (
1.1
)
3
by
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{H}
respectively, then adding the two resulting equations together, and integrating over
\Omega
, we can derive that

\frac{d}{dt}\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dx+\int\left(2\nu|\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}+\lambda|\divv\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\beta|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dx=0,

(3.5)

where integration by parts was applied. It implies that the inequality (
3.4
) holds and consequently completes the proof.

\Box

Inspired by
[
8
]
,
we have the following higher integrability of the magnetic field
\mathbf{H}
.

Lemma 3.3

Under the condition (
3.1
), it holds that for any
q\in[2,\infty)
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int|\mathbf{H}|^{q}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(3.6)

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.1
)
3
by
q|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}\mathbf{H}
and integrating the resulting equations over
\Omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int|\mathbf{H}|^{q}dx+\beta\int\left(q|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}+q(q-2)|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}|\nabla|\mathbf{H}||^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\int q|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}\left(\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{u}-\frac{q-1}{2}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla|\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{q(q-2)}{2}\int|\mathbf{H}|^{q-4}(\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla|\mathbf{H}|^{2})(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{H})dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\int q|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}dx+C(q)\int|\mathbf{u}|^{2}|\mathbf{H}|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\int q|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}dx+C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int|\mathbf{H}|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\int q|\mathbf{H}|^{q-2}|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}dx+C\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int|\mathbf{H}|^{q}dx,

(3.7)

where in the second inequality one has used

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\ \ \text{for any}\ \ p>3

(3.8)

according to

and Sobolev’s embedding theorem. Then we derive (

The following lemma gives the estimates on the spatial gradients of both the velocity and the magnetic field, which are crucial for deriving the higher order estimates of the solution.

Lemma 3.4

Under the condition (
3.1
), it holds that for any

,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\left(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int\left(\rho|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dxdt\leq C.

(3.9)

Proof.

Note that the momentum equation (
1.1
)
2
can be rewritten as

\rho\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}-\mu\nabla\divv\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{f},

\mathbf{f}=\nabla\nu\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\nu+\divv\mathbf{u}\nabla\lambda-\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\rho^{\gamma}+(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})\times\mathbf{H}.

(3.11)

By Lemma
2.2
and (

\displaystyle\|(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})\times\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\||\mathbf{H}||\nabla\mathbf{H}|\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.12)

Then it follows from (

), (
3
), (
3.2
), (

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\||\nabla\mathbf{u}||\nu^{\prime}||\nabla\rho|\|_{L^{2}}+\||\nabla\mathbf{u}||\lambda^{\prime}||\nabla\rho|\|_{L^{2}}+\||\rho||\mathbf{u}||\nabla\mathbf{u}|\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\rho^{\gamma-1}|\nabla\rho|\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})\times\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\nu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\gamma-1}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\int\mathbf{f}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int\partial_{t}\nu|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx-\int\nabla\nu\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int\partial_{t}\mu|\divv\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx-\int\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\nabla\mu\divv\mathbf{u}dx\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{5}I_{j}.

(3.14)

Employing straightforward calculations, we can estimate each term

as follows

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq

\displaystyle\|\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(1+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\left(1+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+C\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{H^{1}};

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq

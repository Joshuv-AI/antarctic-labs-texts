Several improved adaptive mapped weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme for hyperbolic conservation law
Shuijiang Tang

Abstract
The decisive factor for the calculation accuracy of the mapped weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme is the width of the center region of the mapping function. Through analysis of the classical mapped WENO schemes, the results show the width of the central range of the mapping function determined by the local operator in its denominator. Substituting the local operator in WENO-AIM with a symmetric one and an asymmetric function, we get two new adaptive mapped WENO schemes, WENO-AIMS and WENO-AIMA. Similarly, we improve WENO-RM260 and WENO-PM6 by using these local operators, and composed adaptive WENO-RM260 and adaptive WENO-PM6. Theoretical and numerical results show the present adaptive mapped WENO schemes composed in this paper perform better than WENO-AIM, WENO-RM260, and WENO-PM6 for one- and two-dimensional problems.

1
Introduction

In recent years, the WENO scheme has been widely used in CFD and has continuously improved. Liu et al.
1 1
first proposed the idea of the WENO scheme, to assign a reasonable weight to each flux function involved in the essentially non-oscillation (ENO) scheme
2 2
, and replace it with a weighted convex combination of all fluxes. The basic idea of the WENO scheme is that each flux plays the same role in the smooth region, and only the smoothest flux plays a key role in the region with large gradient changes. In this way, it is possible to maintain the characteristics of substantially essentially no-oscillation and achieve higher accuracy in the smooth region. Jiang and Shu
3 3
found they can further improve the accuracy of the WENO scheme of Liu et al. And they provided a method to assess the smoothness of templates and a framework for composing the WENO scheme (WENO-JS). Since then, many researchers follow with interest in the WENO scheme and got various related results. In 2000, Balsara and Shu
4 4
even constructed the WENO scheme with 7 to 13 order precision. In 2003, Qiu et al.
5 5
got a fifth-order HWENO scheme by using Hermite polynomials. Soon after, they extended the HWENO to two-dimensional problems
6 6
and the non-uniform grid
7 7
.

Henrick et al.
8 8
pointed out the accuracy of the fifth-order WENO-JS scheme will reduce to third-order at critical points in the smooth regions. They reconstructed the weight of the WENO-JS scheme by using a mapping function and got the mapped WENO scheme
——
WENO-M. WENO-M can achieve the optimal convergence order at critical points. After that, Borges et al.
9 9
proposed an original method to calculate weight by incorporating a global smoothness indicator and constructed the WENO-Z scheme that can achieve the optimal convergence order at critical points. Feng et al.
10 10
demonstrated the accuracy of the seventh-order or higher-order WENO-M scheme will lower than the WENO-JS scheme in long-term simulation. To address this issue, Feng et al. proposed a new class of piecewise polynomial mapping function by adding two criteria that can decrease the effect from non-smooth stencils and got a new WENO scheme
——
WENO-PM. Compared with WENO-M, WENO-PM6 performs better on both smooth and discontinuous problems. But there are also difficulties in factorization and increased computational cost. Later, Feng et al.
11 11
gave a new mapping function with two parameters by rewriting the mapping function of the WENO-M method. After selecting suitable parameters, a class of WENO-IM(2,0.1) scheme with less dissipation and high resolution was achieved. Wang et al.
12 12
gave a new non-segmental mapping function that combined the favorable features of WENO-PM and WENO-IM, and composed a new WENO scheme
——
WENO-RM (260). WENO-RM (260) can increase the non-physical oscillations of seventh and ninth-order WENO schemes. Recently, Vevek et al.
13 13
adapted the mapping function of WENO-IM with an adaptive operator, developed a rational mapping function with three parameters. They constructed the WENO-AIM (
n,m,s
) scheme, and
n=4,m=2,s=10000
is subsequently selected. Recently, Hong et al.
14 14
proposed a pre-discrete mapping method to reduce the cost brought by the mapping process, and given the mapping-function-free WENO-M scheme. Hu
15 15
applied the mapping function on the indicators of smoothness to increase the efficiency of WENO-M and get a high order WENO-IM scheme.

In this paper, we analyzed the mapping functions of WENO-M, WENO-IM, and WENO-AIM, and found the decisive factor of the three mapping functions are the local operator
\omega_{k}(1-\omega_{k})
in the denominator. By improving this centrosymmetric local operator, we get a more generalized adaptive mapped WENO scheme
——
WENO-AIMS. We also introduced the asymmetric local operator into the mapping function and getting another adaptive mapped WENO scheme
——
WENO-AIMA. Recommend these local operators in WENO-PM6 and WENO-RM260, we get the adaptive WENO-PM6 and adaptive WENO-RM260 schemes. Many numerical examples show the adaptive WENO-PM and adaptive WENO-RM perform better than WENO-PM and WENO-RM.

This paper organized as: In Section 2, we briefly introduced the construction of the WENO scheme and several mapping functions. In Section 3, we provide many new mapping WENO schemes and determined suitable parameters. We discuss the performance of the new mapping WENO schemes compared with other mapping WENO schemes for linear advection and Euler equations in Section 4. The conclusions of this paper provided in Section 5.

2
Numerical method

In this paper, we study the one-dimensional systems of hyperbolic conservation laws which is to be solved on the domain
x\in[a,b]
for
t>0

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=0,

(1)

where
u(x,t)
is the conservative variable,
f(u)
is the flux,
x
and
t
are respectively, the space and time.
In the uniform grids, let
x_{j}=j\triangle x
,
x_{j+1/2}=(j+1/2)\triangle x,~j=0,1,2,\cdots,N
, where
\triangle x=(b-a)/N
. Eq.(1) at
x_{j}
can be approximated by

\frac{d\bar{u}_{j}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{\Delta x}(\hat{f}_{j+1/2}-\hat{f}_{j-1/2}),

(2)

where
\hat{f}_{j\pm 1/2}
are the numerical fluxes.

The numerical flux function of the fifth-order WENO scheme can be expressed as:

\hat{f}_{j+1/2}=\omega_{0}q_{0}+\omega_{1}q_{1}+\omega_{2}q_{2},

(3)

where
q_{k},~k=0,1,2
are the third-order flux on the substencil
S_{k}={j-2+k,j-1+k,j+k}
and given by

\begin{cases}q_{0}=(2f_{j-2}-7f_{j-1}+11f_{j})/6,\\
q_{1}=(-f_{j-1}+5f_{j}+2f_{j+1})/6,\\
q_{2}=(2f_{j}+5f_{j+1}-f_{j+2})/6.\end{cases}

(4)

2.1
WENO-JS scheme

The weights
\omega_{k},~k=0,1,2
of Jiang and Shu
3 3
are given as

\omega^{JS}_{k}=\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}},\alpha_{k}=\frac{d_{k}}{(\beta_{k}+\epsilon)^{2}},~k=0,1,2,

(5)

where
d_{0}=0.1,~d_{1}=0.6
and
d_{2}=0.3
are the optimal weights, which generate the fifth-order upstream scheme.
\epsilon
is a small positive real number that is utilized to avoid the denominator of zero.
\epsilon=10^{-40}
is recommended by Henrick et al.
8 8
and used in this paper. And the smoothness indicator
\beta_{k}
is given by

\begin{cases}\beta_{0}=\frac{13}{12}(f_{j-2}-2f_{j-1}+f_{j})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(f_{j-2}-4f_{j-1}+3f_{j})^{2},\\
\beta_{1}=\frac{13}{12}(f_{j-1}-2f_{j}+f_{j+1})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(f_{j-1}-f_{j+1})^{2},\\
\beta_{2}=\frac{13}{12}(f_{j}-2f_{j+1}+f_{j+2})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(3f_{j}-4f_{j+1}+f_{j+2})^{2}.\end{cases}

(6)

Expanding the smoothness indicators using Taylor series at
x_{j}
for a smooth solution is given as following

\begin{cases}\beta_{0}=f^{\prime 2}_{j}(\Delta x)^{2}+\left(\frac{13}{12}f_{j}^{\prime\prime 2}-\frac{2}{3}f_{j}^{\prime}f_{j}^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)(\Delta x)^{4}+\left(-\frac{13}{6}f_{j}^{\prime\prime}f_{j}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{2}f_{j}^{\prime}f_{j}^{(4)}\right)(\Delta x)^{5}+O(\Delta x^{6}),\\
\beta_{1}=f^{\prime 2}_{j}(\Delta x)^{2}+\left(\frac{13}{12}f_{j}^{\prime\prime 2}+\frac{1}{3}f_{j}^{\prime}f_{j}^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)(\Delta x)^{4}+O(\Delta x^{6}),\\
\beta_{2}=f^{\prime 2}_{j}(\Delta x)^{2}+\left(\frac{13}{12}f_{j}^{\prime\prime 2}-\frac{2}{3}f_{j}^{\prime}f_{j}^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)(\Delta x)^{4}+\left(\frac{13}{6}f_{j}^{\prime\prime}f_{j}^{\prime\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}f_{j}^{\prime}f_{j}^{(4)}\right)(\Delta x)^{5}+O(\Delta x^{6}).\end{cases}

(7)

The expansion (7) means that

\beta_{k}=\begin{cases}f^{\prime 2}_{j}(1+O(\Delta x)^{2}),&f^{\prime}\neq 0,\\
\frac{13}{12}f^{\prime\prime}_{j}(\Delta x)^{4}(1+O(\Delta x)),&f^{\prime}=0.\end{cases}

(8)

Therefore, WENO-JS suffers from a loss of accuracy near critical points.

2.2
WENO-M scheme

The mapped WENO scheme is proposed by Henrick et al
8 8
by introducing a mapping function. The mapping function is defined as

g_{M}(\omega_{k})=\frac{\omega_{k}(d_{k}+d_{k}^{2}-3d_{k}\omega_{k}+\omega_{k}^{2})}{d_{k}^{2}+\omega_{k}(1-2d_{k})},~k=0,1,2.

(9)

The mapping function (9) has following properties:

\begin{cases}monotonically~increasing~in~[0,~1]~with~finite~slopes,\\
g_{M}(0)=0,g_{M}(d_{k})=d_{k},~g_{M}(1)=1,\\
g_{M}^{\prime}(d_{k})=g_{M}^{\prime\prime}(d_{k})=0.\end{cases}

(10)

By using the mapping function, the classical weights mapped to the new ones as follows

\omega_{k}^{M}=\frac{g_{M}(\omega_{k})}{g_{M}(\omega_{0})+g_{M}(\omega_{1})+g_{M}(\omega_{2})},~k=0,1,2.

(11)

Applying the Taylor expansion of (9) at
d_{k}
, there is

g_{M}(\omega_{k})=d_{k}+O((\Delta x)^{3}).

(12)

Thus, at or near the critical points in smooth regions, the weights of WENO-M satisfies

\omega_{k}^{M}=d_{k}+O((\Delta x)^{3}),

(13)

that is, the WENO-M scheme recovers the optimal order of accuracy.

2.3
WENO-PM scheme

To improve the accuracy near discontinuities, by adding two criteria, Feng et al.
10 10
proposed another mapping function

g_{PM}(\omega_{k};n)=c_{1}(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n+1}(\omega_{k}+c_{2})+d_{k},~n\geq 2,~k=0,1,2,

(14)

where

c_{1}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{n}\frac{n+1}{(d_{k})^{n+1}},~0\leq\omega_{k}\leq d_{k},\\
-\frac{n+1}{(1-d_{k})^{n+1}},~d_{k}\leq\omega_{k}\leq 1,\end{cases}c_{2}=\begin{cases}\frac{d_{k}}{n+1},~0\leq\omega_{k}\leq d_{k},\\
\frac{d_{k}-(n+2)}{n+1},~d_{k}\leq\omega_{k}\leq 1.\end{cases}

(15)

The mapping function (14) satisfies following properties:

\begin{cases}monotonically~increasing~in~[0,~1]~with~finite~slopes,\\
g_{PM}(0;n)=0,g_{PM}(d_{k};n)=d_{k},~g_{PM}(1;n)=1,\\
g_{PM}^{\prime}(d_{k};n)=\cdots=g_{PM}^{(n)}(d_{k};n)=0,\\
g_{PM}(1;n)=1,g_{PM}^{\prime}(1;n)=0.\end{cases}

(16)

By applying the mapping function and using (16), one can verify that the weights meet

\omega^{PM}_{k}=d_{k}+O((\Delta x)^{n+1}),

(17)

this means, WENO-PM scheme can recover the fifth-order accuracy near critical points when
n\geq 2
. Feng et al.
10 10
found that the fifth order WENO-PM method with
n=6
can get better results than WENO-M.

Given a tiny positive number
\epsilon
, we call the interval satisfying
\{\omega_{k}|\left|g(\omega_{k})-d_{k}\right|<\epsilon\}
as a central range of
g(\omega_{k})
. It is clear from Fig. 1 that
g_{PM}(\omega_{k};6)
has a much wider central range compared to
g_{M}(\omega_{k})
.

2.4
WENO-IM scheme

Feng et al.
11 11
developed an improved version of the WENO-M method; i.e., WENO-IM. By rewriting and changing the original mapping function, they got a new type of mapping functions with two parameters,
A
and
n
, as follows:

g_{IM}(\omega_{k};n,A)=d_{k}+\frac{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n+1}A}{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{m}A+\omega_{k}(1-\omega_{k})},

(18)

where
A>0
,
n
is a positive even integer. The mapping function (18) also satisfies the property of (16).
Applying the Taylor expansion of (18) at
d_{k}
, there is

g_{IM}(\omega_{k};n,A)=d_{k}+O((\Delta x)^{n+1}).

(19)

Feng et al.
11 11
found that by taking
n=2
and
A=0.1
, WENO-IM performs better than WENO-M. It is evident from Fig. 2 that
g_{IM}(\omega_{k};2,0.1)
has a much wider central range compared to
g_{M}(\omega_{k})
.

2.5
WENO-RM scheme

Wang et al
12 12
developed an improved version of the WENO-M method, i.e., WENO-RM. Reconstructed the mapping function of WENO-M, they obtained a new mapping function with three parameters,
m
,
n
and
\tau
, as follows

g_{RM}(\omega_{k};m,n,\tau)=d_{k}+\frac{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n+1}}{a_{0}+a_{1}\omega_{k}+\cdots+a_{m+s+1}\omega_{k}^{m+\tau+1}},

(20)

where
m\leq n
, and the choice of coefficients
a_{i}
is related to the value of
\tau
, usually there are

1)~\tau=0,\begin{cases}a_{i}=C_{n+1}^{i}(-d_{k})^{n-i},~i=0,1,2,\cdots,m,\\
a_{m+1}=(1-d_{k})^{n}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}.\end{cases}

(21)

2)~\tau=1,\begin{cases}a_{i}=C_{n+1}^{i}(-d_{k})^{n-i},~i=0,1,2,\cdots,m,\\
a_{m+1}=(m+2)\left[(1-d_{k})^{n}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}\right]+\sum_{i=0}^{m}ia_{i}-(n+1)(1-d_{k})^{n-1},\\
a_{m+2}=(n+1)(1-d_{k})^{n-1}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}ia_{i}-(m+1)\left[(1-d_{k})^{n}-\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}\right].\end{cases}

(22)

The mapping function (20) satisfies following properties:

\begin{cases}monotonically~increasing~in~[0,~1]~with~finite~slopes,\\
g_{RM}(0;m,n,\tau)=0,g^{\prime}_{RM}(0;m,n,\tau)=\cdots=g^{(m)}_{RM}(0;m,n,\tau)=0,\\
g_{RM}(d_{k};m,n,\tau)=d_{k},g^{\prime}_{RM}(d_{k};m,n,\tau)=\cdots=g^{(n)}_{RM}(d_{k};m,n,\tau)=0,\\
g_{RM}(1;m,n,\tau)=1,g^{\prime}_{RM}(1;m,n,\tau)=\cdots=g^{(\tau)}_{RM}(1;m,n,\tau)=0.\end{cases}

(23)

Applying the Taylor expansion of (20) at
d_{k}
, there is

g_{RM}(\omega_{k};m,n,\tau)=d_{k}+O((\Delta x)^{n+1}).

(24)

Wang et al.
12 12
found that, taking
m=2
,
n=6
and
\tau=0
, the fifth and seventh order WENO-RM perform better than WENO-M. It is evident from Fig. 3 that
g_{RM}(\omega_{k};2,6,0)
has a much wider central range compared to

2.6
WENO-AIM scheme

Recently, Vevek et al.
13 13
improved the mapping function of WENO-IM, developed a rational mapping function with three parameters, and constructed the adaptive WENO-AIM (

g_{AIM}(\omega_{k};n,m,s)=d_{k}+\frac{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n+1}}{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n}+s(\omega_{k}(1-\omega_{k}))^{m}},

(25)

where
n
is positive even number,
m
positive number and
s=cd_{k}^{-1}\lambda
with

\lambda=\frac{min(\beta_{k})}{max(\beta_{k})+\epsilon_{m}},

(26)

where,
\epsilon_{m}
is a small number that is provides a threshold of significance for the smoothness indicators, and usually selected
\epsilon_{m}=(\Delta x)^{5}
.

The mapping function (25) satisfies following properties:

\begin{cases}monotonically~increasing~in~[0,~1]~when~n\geq m-1,\\
g_{AIM}(0;n,m,s)=0,~g_{AIM}(d_{k};n,m,s)=d_{k},~g_{AIM}(1;n,m,s)=1,\\
g^{\prime}_{AIM}(0;n,m,s)=\cdots=g^{(n)}_{AIM}(0;n,m,s)=0,\\
g^{\prime}_{AIM}(0;n,m,s)=g^{\prime}_{AIM}(1;n,m,s)=1~for~m>1.\end{cases}

(27)

Applying the Taylor expansion of (25) at
d_{k}
, there is

g_{AIM}(\omega_{k};n,m,s)=d_{k}+O((\Delta x)^{n+1}).

(28)

Vevek et al.
13 13
found that by taking
m=2
,
n=4
and
c=10^{4}
, far better results could be obtained for the seventh order WENO methods compared to WENO-M and WENO-IM(2,0.1). It is evident from Fig. 4 that
g_{AIM}(\omega_{k};4,2,s=1e4)

3
Design and properties of new mapped WENO schemes

3.1
The improvement of WENO-AIM scheme

From the mapping functions of WENO-M, WENO-IM and WENO-AIM, there exists a common formula in these three formulas

\phi(\omega_{k})=(\omega_{k}(1-\omega_{k}))^{\kappa},

(29)

it is the determining factor in determining the center width of the mapping function, and we call it the width operator in this paper. And it meets the following conditions

\begin{cases}\phi~is~a~continuous~positive~function~on~[0,~1],\\
\phi~is~monotone~increase~in~[0,~\frac{1}{2}],~and~decrease~in~[\frac{1}{2},~1],\\
\phi(0)=\phi(1)=0,\\
\phi(\omega_{k})=\phi(1-\omega_{k}),\\
\phi^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(1)=0,~for~\kappa>1.\end{cases}

(30)

As a matter of fact, we can always improve the above conditions (30) as

\begin{cases}\phi~is~a~continuous~positive~function~on~[0,~1],\\
\phi(0)=\phi(1)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(1)=0.\\
There~exist~\omega_{L},~\omega_{R}\in(0,1),~\omega_{L}\leq\omega_{R},~\phi~increases~in~(0,~\omega_{L})~and~decreases~in~(\omega_{R},~1).\end{cases}

(31)

Therefore, we can extend the mapping function of WENO-AIM to the following form

g_{AIM\phi}(\omega_{k};n,s,\phi)=d_{k}+\frac{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n+1}}{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n}+s\phi(\omega_{k})},

(32)

Differentiate the above formula with respect to
\omega_{k}
, we have

g^{\prime}_{AIM\phi}(\omega_{k};n,s,\phi)=\frac{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n}\left[(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n}+s((n+1)\phi(\omega_{k})-\phi^{\prime}(\omega_{k})(\omega_{k}-d_{k}))\right]}{\left[(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n}+s\phi(\omega_{k})\right]^{2}}.

(33)

Obviously, when the local operator
\phi(\omega_{k})
satisfies all conditions in (31), and

(n+1)\phi(\omega_{k})-\phi^{\prime}(\omega_{k})(\omega_{k}-d_{k})\geq 0,

(34)

then,
g_{AIM\phi}(\omega_{k};n,s,\phi)
satisfies the properties of (27). In other words, we can always build any local operator that supports the implementation (31) and (34) so that the function constructed by (32) becomes a mapping function that satisfies the condition (27). The resulting adaptive mapped WENO scheme is called the generalized WENO-AIM scheme.

Next, we will use the different locations of the
\omega_{L},~\omega_{R}
in (31) to create two new types of local operators that are different from those of AIM.

3.1.1
Center symmetric local operator

When
\omega_{L}=1-\omega_{R}
, and the width function
\phi(\omega_{k})
meets the conditions (31), (33) and

\phi(\omega_{k})=\phi(1-\omega_{k}),

(35)

it is called the center symmetric local operator. Obviously, the local operator in AIM is of this kind. In addition, the symmetric local operator can also be developed in the following form.

~\phi(\omega_{k})=(1+\chi(\omega_{k}-0.5)^{2})(\omega_{k}(1-\omega_{k}))^{\kappa}.

(36)

where
\kappa
is an integer greater or equal to 2,
\chi
is undetermined non-negative constant. Easy to verify, the new local operator satisfy the conditions of (31) and (34), and when
\chi=0
, it is the local operator of the WENO-AIM scheme. Figure 5 shows the graph of the function
\phi(\omega_{k})
as

. One can see from the graph, the values of

3.2
The adaptive WENO-PM scheme

Although WENO-PM can achieve the optimal accuracy at the critical points, it will be unstable during long-term calculations. Therefore, we will improve it by using the previously local operator. We can always rewrite the mapping function of WENO-PM into the following form

g_{PM}(\omega_{k};n)=d_{k}+\frac{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n+1}}{\frac{1}{c_{1}(\omega_{k}+c_{2})}}.

(40)

Using the construction idea of
AIM\phi
, we can get an adaptive PM mapping function of the form

g_{APM}(\omega_{k};n,s,\phi)=d_{k}+\frac{(\omega_{k}-d_{k})^{n+1}}{\frac{1}{c_{1}(\omega_{k}+c_{2})}+s\phi(\omega_{k})},

\phi(\omega_{k})
defined as (36) and (37), and

defined as

s=\frac{cd_{k}min(\beta_{k})}{max(\beta_{k})+(\triangle x)^{5}}.

(42)

Easy to verify, the adaptive PM mapping function satisfies the conditions of (18). When the solution is smooth,
s=cd_{k}
, so as
c
tends to infinity, there is
g_{APM}(\omega_{k};n,s,\phi)=d_{k}
. When the solution is discontinuous, there is
g_{APM}(\omega_{k};n,s,\phi)=g_{PM}(\omega_{k};n)
. The improved WENO-PM scheme using (41) will always achieve the optimal accuracy when the solution is continuous. And it will degenerate to WENO-PM when the solution is discontinuous. We call the improved WENO-PM scheme equips with the symmetric local operator (36) as WENO-APMS, and the improved WENO-PM scheme equips with the asymmetric local operator (38) as WENO-APMA.

Conclusions

In this paper, by improving the mapping function of the WENO-AIM scheme with two local operators, we composed two more generalized adaptive mapped WENO schemes, WENO-AIMS and WENO-AIMA. We also introduced these two local operators into the mapping functions of WENO-RM and WENO-PM and composed the adaptive WENO-RM and adaptive WENO-PM schemes. In the numerical examples, we used the WENO scheme by using these kinds of mapping functions to calculate several discontinuous problems, such as one-dimensional SOD’s problem, the interaction of shock-entropy wave, two-dimensional Riemann problem, implosion, and double Mach problem. Compared with the classical WENO-AIM, WENO- RM, and WENO-PM, these new adaptive mapped WENO schemes not only have higher resolution but also have high accuracy and computational stability.

Correcting Delocalization Error in Materials with Localized Orbitals and Linear-Response Screening
Jacob Z. Williams, Weitao Yang

Abstract
Delocalization error prevents density functional theory (DFT) from reaching its full potential, causing problems like systematically underestimated band gaps and misaligned energy levels at interfaces. We introduce lrLOSC to correct delocalization error in materials over a wide range of band gaps. We predict eleven materials’ fundamental gaps to within 0.28 eV, while offering a nonzero correction to the total energy. Molecular properties are improved with the same theory ( arXiv:2406.06345 ). lrLOSC is an essential step toward modeling molecules, materials, and their interfaces within the same DFT framework.



Kohn–Sham density functional theory (DFT)

Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
;
Levy 1979
;
Levy 1982
;
Lieb 1983
is rightly
regarded as the default method for quantum-mechanical calculations in both
chemistry and materials science. The availability of reasonably accurate
density functional approximations (DFAs), implemented efficiently in community
software, remains unparalleled decades later. Frontier orbital (or valence band
maximum and conduction band minimum) energies from (generalized) Kohn–Sham DFAs
have been shown to be the chemical potentials, defined as the derivatives of the
associated DFA’s total energy with respect to the electron number

Cohen et al. 2008a
;
Yang et al. 2012a
; the proof uses Janak’s theorem
Janak 1978
, but
extends it to functionals of the density matrix. DFA frontier orbitals thus
predict the fundamental gaps of molecules and materials based on the exact
conditions for fractional charges
Perdew et al. 1982
;
Yang et al. 2000
. But DFAs suffer
systematic errors, including a characteristic and enduring underestimation of
the fundamental gap
Perdew 1985
. Band structures obtained by (semilocal)
DFAs are also quite inaccurate, and decades of work has been poured into various
methods for improving them

Hybertsen and Louie 1986
;
Dabo et al. 2010
;
Tsuneda et al. 2010
;
Kronik et al. 2012
;
Körzdörfer et al. 2012
;
Puschnig et al. 2017
. Accurately computing band structures, and especially band gaps,
within the efficient DFT framework is a pressing problem for computational
physicists, chemists and materials scientists. Predicting and aligning energy
levels is critical for the development of semiconductor technology

Heyd et al. 2005
;
Xiao et al. 2011
; solar cells
Wang et al. 2018
; and photocatalysts

Li et al. 2017
. Interfacial band structure
Ishii et al. 1999
;
Braun et al. 2009
is
particularly challenging because adsorbates’ bands are renormalized, yet of
outsize importance in predicting the properties of next-generation heterogeneous
materials
Kroemer 2001
;
201 2012
. Behind the band gap and band structure
problems lies
delocalization error

Mori-Sánchez et al. 2008
;
Cohen et al. 2008b
;
Cohen et al. 2012
;
Bryenton et al. 2022
.

Delocalization error in DFAs is a significantly and systematically incorrect
behavior of the energy
E
viewed as a function of the number
N
of electrons as
exhibited for small systems, but its manifestation depends on the size of the
system
Mori-Sánchez et al. 2008
. The exact
E(N)
curve is piecewise linear, with
derivative discontinuities at integer
N

Perdew et al. 1982
;
Yang et al. 2000
; the
magnitude of the discontinuity gives the fundamental gap.
E(N)
is convex in
finite systems when calculated with typical DFAs, underestimating the derivative
discontinuity and thus the gap
Cohen et al. 2008a
. As the size of the system
increases, however,
E(N)
becomes less convex. In periodic boundary conditions,
piecewise linearity of
E(N)
is restored automatically, but deceptively:
delocalization occurs across the entire infinite lattice, the derivative
discontinuity is underestimated just like for finite
N
, and the total energy of
charged bulk systems is incorrect
Mori-Sánchez et al. 2008
. Correcting
delocalization error in both molecules and materials, then, requires that both
the orbital energies and the total energy be corrected, all within the same
approximation scheme.

Two ingredients—orbital localization and screening—are key to delocalization
error corrections. The localized orbital scaling correction (LOSC) initially
featured localization without screening
Li et al. 2018a
;
Su et al. 2020
: it was good
enough to cure delocalization error in atoms’ and small molecules’ valence
orbitals
Mei et al. 2021a
. LOSC orbital energies have accuracy comparable to
molecular
GW

Mei et al. 2019
and can replace
GW
quasiparticle energies as
the starting point for Bethe–Salpeter calculations of neutral excitations

Li et al. 2022
. Screening without localization, on the other hand, is realized
by the global scaling correction (especially GSC2)

Zheng et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2015
;
Mei et al. 2021b
. The screening in GSC2 is based on the
exact second derivatives of the total energy with respect to orbital
occupations
Yang et al. 2012b
. GSC2 improves atomic and molecular photoemission
spectra
Mei et al. 2021b
, as well as valence orbital energies, when
orbital relaxation is included
Zhang et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2018
;
Yang et al. 2020
. Although
screening plays a smaller role in molecular valence ionization, both
localization and screening are necessary to describe core-electron
quasiparticle energies
Yu et al. 2024
.

Correcting delocalization error in bulk materials also requires both
localization and screening. GSC, lacking localization, offers no correction at
all to the total energy of gapped materials, so it cannot restore
size-consistency of the gap
Li et al. 2018a
. On the other hand, the initial
version of LOSC overestimates orbital energy corrections significantly because
the screening of the Coulomb repulsion by other electrons is ignored.

Mahler et al. 2022a
proposed screened LOSC (sLOSC), which included
an empirically screened Hartree repulsion and substantially improved the band
gaps of semiconductors and insulators. But sLOSC’s screening is identical for
all systems, which fundamentally limits its accuracy. In this work, we extend
LOSC to include the linear-response screening of GSC; we call the resulting
method lrLOSC. (A parallel work
Yu et al. 2024
implements lrLOSC in molecular
systems.) lrLOSC is related in spirit to Koopmans-compliant spectral functionals

Nguyen et al. 2018
;
Colonna et al. 2018
;
Colonna et al. 2022
, the Wannier–Koopmans method

Ma and Wang 2016
;
Weng et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2018b
;
Weng et al. 2018
;
Weng et al. 2020
, and Wannier
optimally-tuned screened range-separated hybrid functionals

Wing et al. 2021
;
Ohad et al. 2022
, all of which correct orbital energies using
localized orbitals (in materials, Wannier functions) and screening of the
electron-electron interaction. But unlike LOSC, these functionals do not
correct the total energy of systems with integer orbital occupations—such as
physical systems with a finite band gap
Nguyen et al. 2018
—which requires
mixing of the occcupied and virtual manifolds. Their corrections therefore
cannot restore size-consistency to the DFA
Li et al. 2018a
.

lrLOSC modifies the DFA total energy by

\Delta E=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{ij\mathbf{R}}{\lambda}^{*}_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma}\left(\delta_{\mathbf{R}ij}-\lambda_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma}\right)\kappa_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma},

(1)

where
i,j,\sigma
are respectively the band-like and spin indices of
localized orbitals
\lvert w_{\mathbf{R}i\sigma}\rangle
and
\mathbf{R}
indexes the primitive
cell translation vectors (see below). The local occupations

\lambda_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma}=\langle w_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}|\rho|w_{\mathbf{R}j\sigma}\rangle
are
elements of the density matrix
\rho
in the local orbital basis, and the
curvature
\kappa_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma}
measures the delocalization error between

\lvert w_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}\rangle
and
\lvert w_{\mathbf{R}j\sigma}\rangle
.
\delta_{\mathbf{R}ij}
is the
Kronecker delta, and
{\lambda}^{*}
is the complex conjugate of
\lambda
.
Note that lrLOSC assumes a spin-polarized, collinear DFA calculation, with
orthogonal Bloch spin orbitals:

\langle\psi_{\mathbf{k}m\sigma}|\psi_{\mathbf{q}n\tau}\rangle\propto\delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{q}}\delta_{mn}\delta_{\sigma\tau}
.

LOSC’s localized orbitals are called orbitalets in finite systems
Su et al. 2020

and dually localized Wannier functions (DLWFs) in periodic boundary conditions

Mahler et al. 2022b
. DLWFs are generalized Wannier functions
Wannier 1937

constructed from the Kohn–Sham Bloch orbitals
\lvert\psi_{\mathbf{k}n\sigma}\rangle
,
where
\mathbf{k}
indexes the
N_{k}
points sampled from the irreducible Brillouin
zone,
n
the electronic bands, and
\sigma
the spin, as

\lvert w_{\mathbf{R}i\sigma}\rangle=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{R}}\sum_{n}U_{ni}^{\mathbf{k}}\lvert\psi_{\mathbf{k}n\sigma}\rangle.

(2)

The DLWFs are periodic on a supercell
N_{k}
times larger than the primitive
unit cell and are indexed by a band-like index
i
and a primitive cell
translation vector
\mathbf{R}
. At each
\mathbf{k}
-point, the unitary operator
U^{\mathbf{k}}
is
chosen to minimize the cost function

F=\sum_{i}\left[(1-\gamma)\Delta r_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}^{2}+\gamma\Delta h_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}^{2}\right],

(3)

where
\Delta r_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}^{2}
is the spatial variance

\langle w_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}|\mathbf{r}|w_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}\rangle^{2}-\langle w_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}|r^{2}|w_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}\rangle
and

\Delta h_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}^{2}
is the energy variance. The numerical value of

\gamma
depends on the units chosen, but all LOSC results to date have used
the same value; when
\Delta r^{2}
is computed in
\mathrm{\text{$a$}_{0}}
and
\Delta h^{2}

in
\mathrm{eV}
,
\gamma=0.47714
. (Setting
\gamma=0
recovers
maximally localized Wannier functions
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
.) Despite being
spatially localized, DLWFs retain information about the energy spectrum,
necessary for describing chemical reactivity
Yu et al. 2022
. DLWFs also extend
readily to metals because they combine both the (occupied) valence and
(unoccupied) conduction manifolds in their construction.

Li et al. 2018a
;
Su et al. 2020
;
Mahler et al. 2022b
.

In finite systems, the curvature is

\kappa_{nn\sigma}=\partial^{2}E/\partial f_{n\sigma}^{2}
, where

f_{n\sigma}
is the occupation of the Kohn–Sham orbital

\lvert\psi_{n\sigma}\rangle
. When
\lvert\psi_{n\sigma}\rangle
is a frontier orbital,

\partial^{2}E/\partial f_{n\sigma}^{2}
describes the deviation of
E(N)
,
computed by a DFA, from the correct linear behavior, including the effect of
screening by all other electrons (also called orbital relaxation)

Yang et al. 2012b
. The original GSC method approximated
\kappa_{nn\sigma}
,
treating only the Coulomb interaction, and used Kohn–Sham orbital
densities instead of the Fukui function

\partial\rho_{\sigma}/\partial f_{n\sigma}

Zheng et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2015
.
Yang et al. 2012b
extended the result to
include the exchange-correlation interaction, finding the exact second-order derivatives to be

\begin{split}\kappa_{nn\sigma}&=\langle\rho_{n\sigma}|\sum_{\tau}(\epsilon^{\tau})^{-1}f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\tau\sigma}|\rho_{n\sigma}\rangle\\
&=\langle\rho_{n\sigma}|f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\sigma\sigma}+\sum_{\nu\tau}f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\sigma\nu}\chi^{\nu\tau}f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\tau\sigma}|\rho_{n\sigma}\rangle.\end{split}

(4)

Here,
\rho_{n\sigma}(\mathbf{r})=\lvert\psi_{n\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\rvert^{2}
is the density of the
Kohn–Sham orbital
\lvert\psi_{n\sigma}\rangle
;
(\epsilon^{\tau})^{-1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
is
the inverse static microscopic dielectric function;

\begin{split}f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\sigma\nu}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})&=\frac{\delta^{2}E_{\text{Hxc}}}{\delta\rho^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\delta\rho^{\nu}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\\
&=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\rvert}+\frac{\delta^{2}E_{\text{xc}}}{\delta\rho^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\delta\rho^{\nu}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\end{split}

(5)

is the Hartree–exchange-correlation kernel; and

\chi^{\nu\tau}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\frac{\delta\rho^{\nu}(\mathbf{r})}{\delta v^{\tau}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}=\frac{\delta^{2}E}{\delta v^{\nu}(\mathbf{r})\delta v^{\tau}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}

(6)

is the response of the density to an external perturbing potential
\delta v
,
to linear order. Observe that the curvature is given by a bare kernel

f_{\text{Hxc}}
screened by the inverse dielectric function
\epsilon^{-1}
, or
equivalently by the sum of the bare kernel and a relaxed kernel modulated by

\chi
. This curvature was implemented in the most recent iteration of GSC,
known as GSC2
Mei et al. 2021b
. Compared to the initial implementation, including
orbital relaxation modestly improves the performance of GSC, reducing the error
in ionization energy and electron affinity for small molecules by an average of
about
0.2\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
. GSC2 still gives no correction to the total
energy for materials; its screened interaction must be combined with the
localized DLWFs. In molecules, including both the screening of (
4
)
and localization greatly improves the energies of core-orbital quasiparticles

Yu et al. 2024
.

In LOSC, the GSC2 curvature is transformed to the DLWF basis; its matrix
elements are

\kappa_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma}=\langle\rho_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}|f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\sigma\sigma}+\sum_{\nu\tau}f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\sigma\nu}\chi^{\nu\tau}f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\tau\sigma}|\rho_{\mathbf{R}j\sigma}\rangle,

(7)

where
\rho_{\mathbf{R}j\sigma}(\mathbf{r})=\lvert w_{\mathbf{R}j\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\rvert^{2}
is a DLWF
density. Note that
\kappa
in LOSC is not diagonal because the DLWFs are not
Kohn–Sham eigenfunctions. These off-diagonal elements allow interactions
between pairs of orbitals to affect the total energy, even at long range.
The analogous elements for canonical orbitals were derived as cross-terms

\partial^{2}E/\partial f_{n\sigma}\partial f_{m\sigma}
in the GSC2 method
(see the Supporting Information of
Mei et al. 2021b
). In lrLOSC, we compute

\kappa_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma}
using density functional perturbation theory

Baroni et al. 2001
; this reduces the computational cost by a factor of
N_{k}
,
the number of
\mathbf{k}
-points, because both curvature elements linear in DLWF
densities
Timrov et al. 2018

\kappa_{\mathbf{R}ij\sigma}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}}\times\\
\left[\langle\rho_{\boldsymbol{0}i\sigma}^{\mathbf{q}}|V_{\boldsymbol{0}j\sigma}^{\mathbf{q}}\rangle+\sum_{\tau}\langle\delta\rho_{\boldsymbol{0}i\tau}^{\mathbf{q}}|V_{\boldsymbol{0}j\tau}^{\mathbf{q}}\rangle\right],

(8)

where
\mathbf{q}
samples the Brillouin zone uniformly including its origin; and

\displaystyle V_{\boldsymbol{0}j\tau}^{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,f_{\text{Hxc}}^{\tau\sigma}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\rho_{\boldsymbol{0}j\sigma}^{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),

(9)

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\boldsymbol{0}i\tau}^{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu}\int d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,\chi^{\tau\nu}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})V_{\boldsymbol{0}i\nu}^{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}).

(10)

Because it includes both localization and system-dependent screening, lrLOSC
corrects delocalization error effectively in both molecules and materials. It
provides accurate, size-consistent orbital and band energy corrections in
semiconductors and insulators, matching the performance of many-body
perturbation theory without requiring any many-body observables. In its current
implementation, it is limited to gapped systems, although it is in principle
extensible to metals using similar techniques to those in density functional
perturbation theory
Baroni et al. 2001
. Future work will implement an
approximate, but still system-dependent, curvature, improving LOSC’s
computational efficiency. We also seek to apply LOSC self-consistently,
yielding a correction to the electron density; this feature has already been
demonstrated for molecules
Mei et al. 2020
. With these updates, LOSC will be
applicable to interfaces, including molecules on a solid surface. Modeling
energy level alignment at interfaces is a major challenge for electronic
structure methods
Flores et al. 2009
;
Egger et al. 2015
;
Liu 2020
, and lrLOSC promises a
nearly parameter-free solution entirely within the confines of DFT.

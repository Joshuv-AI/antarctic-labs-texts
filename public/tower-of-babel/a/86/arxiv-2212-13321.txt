Higher Regularity of the Free Boundary in a Semilinear System
Morteza Fotouhi and Herbert Koch

Abstract
. In this paper we are concerned with higher regularity properties of the elliptic system Δ 𝐮 = | 𝐮 | q − 1 𝐮 χ { | 𝐮 | > 0 } , 𝐮 = ( u 1 , … , u m ) \Delta\mathbf{u}=|\mathbf{u}|^{q-1}\mathbf{u}\chi_{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}},\qquad\mathbf{u}=(u^{1},\dots,u^{m}) for 0 ≤ q < 1 0\leq q<1 . We show analyticity of the regular part of the free boundary ∂ { | 𝐮 | > 0 } \partial\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\} , analyticity of | 𝐮 | 1 − q 2 |\mathbf{u}|^{\frac{1-q}{2}} and 𝐮 | 𝐮 | \frac{\mathbf{u}}{|\mathbf{u}|} up to the regular part of the free boundary. Applying a variant of the partial hodograph-Legendre transformation and the implicit function theorem, we arrive at a degenerate equation, which introduces substantial challenges to be dealt with. Along the lines of our study, we also establish a Cauchy-Kowalevski type statement to show the local existence of solution when the free boundary and the restriction of 𝐮 | 𝐮 | \frac{\mathbf{u}}{|\mathbf{u}|} from both sides to the free boundary are given as analytic data.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Problem setting

In this paper we study the higher regularity properties of the free boundary of solutions to the following elliptic system

(1)

\Delta\mathbf{u}=|\mathbf{u}|^{q-1}\mathbf{u}\chi_{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}},\qquad\mathbf{u}=(u^{1},\dots,u^{m}),

where
0\leq q<1
,
\mathbf{u}:B_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
n\geq 2
,
m\geq 1
,
and
|\cdot|
stands for the Euclidian norm on the respective spaces.

Problem (
1
) is studied in
[
3
,
8
]
and in the scalar case (
m=1
) in
[
7
]
,
where an optimal growth rate for the solutions is proven as well as
C^{1,\beta}
-regularity of the free boundary at
regular
points.
In addition, the scalar case with a further assumption of a positive solution has been extensively studied in
[
2
,
17
,
18
]
; for the case of fully nonlinear operators see
[
20
]
, and for the parabolic version see
[
1
]
.

Our aim with this paper is to improve the existing results by showing that the regular part of the free boundary is analytic.

Throughout the paper, we fix the following constants:

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{2}{1-q},

\displaystyle\alpha=(\kappa(\kappa-1))^{-\kappa/2}.

We observe that
\kappa\in[2,\infty)
for
q\in[0,1)
. We denote by
\Gamma(\mathbf{u})=\partial\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}
the free boundary and define the subset
\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})
as follows

\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{z\in\Gamma(\mathbf{u}):\partial^{\mu}\mathbf{u}(z)=0,\text{ for all }|\mu|<\kappa\right\}.

In
[
3
,
8
]
, it is proven that the solution of (
1
) has
\kappa
-growth close to points in
\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})
, indeed
\Arrowvert{\mathbf{u}}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(z))}=O(r^{\kappa})
when
z\in\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})
.
With this regularity, we can get a sense of the blow-up limit of
\mathbf{u}(rx+z)/r^{\kappa}
at each point
z\in\Gamma^{\kappa}
, which is a
\kappa
-homogeneous global solutions of (
1
) (thanks to the energy monotonicity).
When the domain is a plane, i.e.
n=2
, all
\kappa
-homogeneous global solutions are classified (see
[
7
,
8
]
).
An important class of global solutions is the class of

half-space
solutions, defined by

{\mathbb{H}}:=\left\{x\mapsto\alpha\max(x\cdot\nu,0)^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}:\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\hbox{ and }\mathbf{e}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\hbox{ are unit vectors}\right\}.

Definition 1.1
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
the set of all (regular free boundary) points
x_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
such that at least one blow-up limit of
\mathbf{u}
at
x_{0}
is in
{\mathbb{H}}
.

The terminology
regular free boundary points
is justified by the uniqueness of blow-up, as it has been proved in
[
3
,
8
]
. Again by
[
3
,
8
]
the set of regular free boundary points
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
is open relative to
\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
and locally is a
C^{1,\beta}
-manifold. Our first main result in this paper is presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

The set of regular free boundary points
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
is locally an analytic manifold.
In addition,
\mathbf{u}/|\mathbf{u}|
and
|\mathbf{u}|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}
are analytic in
\overline{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}}
in a neighborhood of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
. In particular, when
\kappa
is integer the solution will be analytic.

Our proof is based on changing coordinates with a partial Hodograph-Legendre transform involving dependent and independent variables so that we obtain a degenerate elliptic problem on a fixed domain.
This method first has been introduced in
[
11
]
to show the analyticity of the free boundary
in the classical obstacle problem.
It has been also applied to the thin obstacle problem or the Signorini problem in
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
where it leads to a fully nonlinear Baoundi-Grushin equation.
Our transform is closer to Section 2.1, Theorem 1.4 of Friedman
[
9
]
.

In our case the approach leads to a nonlinear degenerate PDE in a half space
similar to the stationary case of the porous medium equation studied by Daskaloupolos and Hamilton
[
6
]

and with different approach by the second author
[
12
]
.
We need regularity results for such linear and nonlinear problems.
According to
[
6
]
, estimates in Hölder spaces can be found for the parabolic problem of the porous medium equation.
These estimates in Sobolev spaces have been proven by the second author
[
12
]
.
To keep the paper self-contained we will provide a complete proof for the elliptic case in Sobolev spaces.

In this study, we deduce analyticity of the free boundary by an application of the implicit function theorem instead of direct and tedious elliptic estimates.
In doing so we follow the ideas that have been used in
[
4
,
5
,
13
,
15
]
and consider the transformed fully nonlinear equation as a perturbation of a linear degenerate operator between appropriate function spaces (Section
5
).

We also establish a Cauchy-Kowalevski type statement to show the existence of a solution for (
1
) when the free boundary is known and assumed to be analytic.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be any given oriented analytic hypersurface, and

V_{\pm}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
given analytic maps. Then on each side of
\Gamma

there is a unique local solution
\mathbf{u}_{\pm}
, of (
1
), that are analytic up to the boundary and satisfy

\frac{\mathbf{u}_{\pm}(x)}{|\mathbf{u}_{\pm}(x)|}\to V_{\pm}

as
x\to\Gamma
from respective sides.

1.2.
Notation

For clarity of exposition we shall introduce some notation and definitions here that are used frequently in the paper.

Throughout this paper,
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
will be equipped with the Euclidean inner product
x\cdot y
and the induced norm
|x|
,
B_{r}(x_{0})
will denote the open
n
-dimensional ball of center
x_{0}
, radius
r
with the boundary
\partial B_{r}(x_{0})
.
In addition,
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
,
\partial B_{r}=\partial B_{r}(0)
and
B_{r}^{+}=B_{r}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
.
We shall also denote the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure by
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
.

Here we introduce the multi-indices notation that is used in Section
5
.
For a multi-index
\mu=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{+}
, we denote

\mu!=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}!,\qquad|\mu|=\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{n},\qquad x^{\mu}=x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}.

Let
\mu
and
\sigma
be two multi-index, we say
\sigma\leq\mu
when
\sigma_{i}\leq\mu_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,n
.
We also use notation
0<\sigma
to say
0<|\sigma|
as well as
\sigma<\mu
stands for the case
\sigma\leq\mu
and
\sigma\neq\mu
.
When
\sigma\leq\mu
, we will denote

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\\
\sigma\end{array}\right)=\frac{\mu!}{\sigma!(\mu-\sigma)!}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu_{i}\\
\sigma_{i}\end{array}\right).

The following relations is also helpful in our proofs

\sum_{|\sigma|=s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\\
\sigma\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}|\mu|\\
s\end{array}\right),

which has the consequence

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\\
\sigma\end{array}\right)\leq\left(\begin{array}[]{c}|\mu|\\
|\sigma|\end{array}\right).

1.3.
Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
, we obtain some results concerning the regularity of solutions near the regular points of the free boundary, that are essential for the rest of the paper.
In Section
3
we invoke the classical partial hodograph transformation and show that it is a diffeomorphism.
Section
4
is devoted to improve the a priori regularity of the Legendre functions.
Section
5
contains the proof of our main results, Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.
Finally, in Appendix we give a short proof for the regularity of the linear degenerate equation that we obtain after the transformation.

2.
Some regularity results for solutions

In this section, we give a brief overview of some tools and known results about the regularity of
solutions and the free boundary for problem (
1
).
We shall also introduce some new results,
which are crucial in next sections to follow.
Indeed, near the regular parts of the free boundary, solutions behave more smoothly and we can find out some more information.
We start with introducing the monotonicity formula, that are the corner stone in the regularity theory of free boundary.

Proposition 2.1

(Proposition 2.3 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a solution of (
1
) in
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
and let

W(\mathbf{u},x_{0},r)=\frac{1}{r^{n+2\kappa-2}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\frac{2}{1+q}|\mathbf{u}|^{1+q}\big)\,dx-\frac{\kappa}{r^{n+2\kappa-1}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}.

(i) For
0<r<r_{0}
, the energy function
W(\mathbf{u},x_{0},r)
is non-decreasing.

(ii) If
W(\mathbf{u},x_{0},r)
is constant for
r_{1}<r<r_{2}
, then
\mathbf{u}
is
\kappa
-homogeneous with respect to
x_{0}
, i.e.

\mathbf{u}(x_{0}+rx)=(\frac{r}{r_{1}})^{\kappa}\mathbf{u}(x_{0}+r_{1}x),\quad x\in B_{1}(0),\text{ and }r_{1}<r<r_{2}.

(iii) The function
x\mapsto W(\mathbf{u},x,0+)
is upper-semicontinuous.

The next proposition presents the optimal growth for the solution near
\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})
.

Proposition 2.2

(Theorem 1.2 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a solution of (
1
) and
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})
. Then there exists the positive constant
C
such that

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\mathbf{u}|\leq Cr^{\kappa}.

Moreover, the constant
C
is uniform in any compact subset of
\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})
.

As of now, the result for the regularity of the free boundary is as follows.

Proposition 2.3

(Theorem 1.4 in
[
8
]
)
.

The set of regular free boundary points
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
is an open set in
\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
and is locally a
C^{1,\beta}
-manifold for some
\beta\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
m
and
q
.

In what follows, we shall study the behaviour of solutions near the regular parts of the free boundary.
From now on, we assume without loss of generality that
0\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
and that the blowup limit at
x=0
is

(2)

\mathbf{u}_{r}(x)=\frac{\mathbf{u}(rx)}{r^{\kappa}}\rightarrow\alpha(x\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n})_{+}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}_{1}.

We also denote the inner unit normal vector on the free boundary by
\nu
.
According to
C^{1,\beta}
-regularity for the free boundary, Proposition
2.3
, we know that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}\ni x_{0}\mapsto\nu(x_{0})
is
C^{0,\beta}
around the origin on the free boundary.
Moreover, the blowup at a point
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
must be
\alpha(x\cdot\nu(x_{0}))_{+}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}(x_{0})
for some unit vector
\mathbf{e}(x_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, in particular
\nu(0)=\mathbf{e}_{n}
and
\mathbf{e}(0)=\mathbf{e}_{1}
.

As a first step, we prove an auxiliary lemma that appears several times later in the arguments.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a solution of (
1
) in
B_{2}(x_{0})
with
0\leq q<1
and
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
is a regular point of free boundary.
Consider an arbitrary sequence

\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}\cap B_{1}\ni x_{i}\rightarrow x_{0}

and let
d_{i}:={\rm dist}(x_{i},\Gamma)
.
Then we find out

\mathbf{u}_{i}(x):=\mathbf{u}(x_{i}+d_{i}x)/d_{i}^{\kappa}\rightarrow\alpha\left((x+\nu(x_{0}))\cdot\nu(x_{0})\right)_{+}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}(x_{0}),

in
C^{2,\gamma}(B_{1/2})
for any
0<\gamma<1
.

Proof.

For convenience we suppose
x_{0}=0
and that assumption (
2
) holds.
Choose
y_{i}\in\partial B_{d_{i}}(x_{i})\cap\Gamma
.
The regularity of the free boundary implies that

(3)

\nu(y_{i})=(x_{i}-y_{i})/d_{i}.

Since the map to the inner unit normal vector
\nu
is continuous we have (note that
x_{i}\to 0
also
y_{i}\to 0
)

(x_{i}-y_{i})/d_{i}=\nu(y_{i})\rightarrow\nu(0)=\mathbf{e}_{n}.

According to the growth estimate in Proposition
2.2
at the point
y_{i}
, and that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
is open in
\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
, we can apply the growth estimate at
\Gamma^{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})
for large values of
i
, to obtain the estimate

|\mathbf{u}(x_{i}+d_{i}x)|\leq C|x_{i}+d_{i}x-y_{i}|^{\kappa}\leq Cd_{i}^{\kappa}|x+\nu(y_{i})|^{\kappa}.

Thus
\mathbf{u}_{i}
is a bounded sequence of solutions of
\Delta\mathbf{u}_{i}=|\mathbf{u}_{i}|^{q-1}\mathbf{u}_{i}
in
B_{1}\subset\{|\mathbf{u}_{i}|>0\}
.
The classical regularity theory yields that
\{\mathbf{u}_{i}\}
is bounded in
C^{1,\gamma}(B_{1})
and
C^{2,\gamma}(B_{1/2})
for any
0<\gamma<1
.
Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\mathbf{u}_{i}(x)\rightarrow\mathbf{u}_{0}(x),

where the convergence is in
C^{1,\gamma}(B_{1})
and
C^{2,\gamma}(B_{1/2})
.

Now for
\rho
positive and small enough,
B_{\rho}(0)\cap\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
consists only of regular points (
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
is open in
\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
), and hence

(4)

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}W(\mathbf{u},x,r)=\omega_{q},\text{ for }x\in B_{\rho}(0)\cap\Gamma(\mathbf{u}),

where
\omega_{q}
is the energy of half-space solutions which is a universal constant depending only on
n
and
q
.
We know that for every
x
, the function
r\mapsto W(\mathbf{u},x,r)
is nondecreasing in
r
.
Hence by Dini’s monotone convergence theorem, the convergence in (
4
) will be uniform in
x
, for smaller
\rho
. Thus

W(\mathbf{u}_{0},-\mathbf{e}_{n},r)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}W(\mathbf{u}_{i},-\nu(y_{i}),r)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}W(\mathbf{u},y_{i},rd_{i})=\omega_{q},

for any
r>0
.
Hence
\mathbf{u}_{0}
is a homogeneous function with respect to point
-\mathbf{e}_{n}
.

On the other hand,
y_{i}
is a regular point of free boundary for
\mathbf{u}
.
Therefore, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
such that

\{(x-y_{i})\cdot\nu(y_{i})<-\varepsilon|x-y_{i}|\}\cap B_{\delta}(0)\subset\{\mathbf{u}=0\},

when
y_{i}
is close enough to
x_{0}=0
.
Invoking the relation (
3
) and definition of
\mathbf{u}_{i}
to get

\{(x+\nu(y_{i}))\cdot\nu(y_{i})<-\varepsilon|x+\nu(y_{i})|\}\cap B_{\delta/d_{i}}(0)\subset\{\mathbf{u}_{i}=0\},

and passing to the limit,

\{(x+\mathbf{e}_{n})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n}<-\varepsilon|x+\mathbf{e}_{n}|\}\subset\{\mathbf{u}_{0}=0\}.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we conclude that

(5)

\{(x+\mathbf{e}_{n})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n}<0\}\subset\{\mathbf{u}_{0}=0\}.

Now according to Proposition 4.6 in
[
8
]
, we infer that
\mathbf{u}_{0}
must be a half-space solution with respect to the point
-\mathbf{e}_{n}

and (
5
) yields that

\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)=\alpha((x+\mathbf{e}_{n})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n})_{+}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e},

for some unit vector
\mathbf{e}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
(Recall that the nondegeneracy property, Proposition 4.1 in
[
8
]
, necessitates that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\not\equiv 0
.)
We must show that
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
.
To do this note that

(6)

\alpha\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{u}_{0}(0)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{u}_{i}(0)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{u}(x_{i})/d_{i}^{\kappa}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbf{u}(x_{i})}{|x_{i}|^{\kappa}}\cdot\frac{|x_{i}|^{\kappa}}{d_{i}^{\kappa}},

and if
\frac{x_{i}}{|x_{i}|}\rightarrow x_{*}
in a subsequence, by assumption (
2
) we get

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbf{u}(x_{i})}{|x_{i}|^{\kappa}}=\alpha(x_{*}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n})_{+}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}_{1}.

This together with (
6
) implies that
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
.
∎

Now, in the following proposition we find out more information about the behavior of solution near regular free boundary points.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a solution of (
1
) and suppose (
2
) holds.
Then the following properties hold in a neighborhood of
x=0
:

(i)

u^{1}\geq 0
.

(ii)

\frac{u^{j}}{|\mathbf{u}|}\in C^{0}(\overline{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}})
and on the free boundary point
x_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
, we have

\lim_{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}\frac{u^{j}(x)}{|\mathbf{u}(x)|}=\mathbf{e}(x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{j}.

(iii)

\{u^{1}>0\}=\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}
.

(iv)

For all
x_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
, we have

\lim_{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}|\mathbf{u}(x)|^{-\frac{1+q}{2}}\partial_{i}\mathbf{u}(x)=\kappa\alpha^{1/\kappa}(\nu(x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i})\mathbf{e}(x_{0}).

In particular,
\nabla(u^{1})^{1/\kappa}\in C^{0}(\overline{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}})
.

(v)

For all
x_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathbf{u})
and
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we have

\lim_{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}|\mathbf{u}(x)|^{-q}\partial_{ij}\mathbf{u}(x)=(\nu(x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i})(\nu(x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{j})\mathbf{e}(x_{0}).

Proof.

The proof is based on the application of Lemma
2.4
. In all parts, we consider a neighborhood in which the free boundary consists only of regular points.

(i)
Assume that there exists a sequence
x_{i}\rightarrow 0
such that
u^{1}(x_{i})<0
.
For
\mathbf{u}_{i}(x):=\mathbf{u}(x_{i}+d_{i}x)/d_{i}^{\kappa}
we obtain, as in Lemma
2.4
,

\mathbf{u}_{i}(x)\rightarrow\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)=\alpha((x+\mathbf{e}_{n})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n})_{+}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}_{1}.

Thus

\alpha=\mathbf{u}_{0}(0)\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{u}_{i}(0)\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{u}(x_{i})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}/d_{i}^{\kappa}\leq 0,

which is a contradiction.

(ii)

It is obvious that
\frac{u^{j}}{|\mathbf{u}|}
is smooth inside the set
\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}
. We show that it can extend continuously on the free boundary.
If
x_{i}
is a sequence converging to
x_{0}
, a point on the free boundary. Define the corresponding sequence
\mathbf{u}_{i}
and apply Lemma
2.4
, then

\lim_{x_{i}\rightarrow x_{0}}\frac{u^{j}(x_{i})}{|\mathbf{u}(x_{i})|}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{u^{j}_{i}(0)}{|\mathbf{u}_{i}(0)|}=\frac{u^{j}_{0}(0)}{|\mathbf{u}_{0}(0)|}=\mathbf{e}(x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{j}.

(iii)

This statement follows from the fact that
\frac{u^{1}}{|\mathbf{u}|}
is continuous and its value at
x=0
is equals one.

(iv)
For a sequence
x_{l}\rightarrow x_{0}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
, consider the corresponding function
\mathbf{u}_{l}(x):=\mathbf{u}(x_{l}+d_{l}x)/d_{l}^{\kappa}
as defined in Lemma
2.4
converging to
\alpha((x+\nu(x_{0}))\cdot\nu(x_{0}))_{+}^{\kappa}\mathbf{e}(x_{0})
.
Thus we can write (notice that
|\mathbf{u}_{l}(0)|\rightarrow\alpha
and we can assume that
|\mathbf{u}_{l}(0)|\neq 0
for sufficiently large
l
)

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{l}\rightarrow x_{0}}|\mathbf{u}(x_{l})|^{-\frac{1+q}{2}}\partial_{i}\mathbf{u}(x_{l})=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{l}(0)|^{-\frac{1+q}{2}}\partial_{i}\mathbf{u}_{l}(0)=\alpha^{-\frac{1+q}{2}}\kappa\alpha(\nu(x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i})\mathbf{e}(x_{0}).

In addition,

\lim_{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}|\mathbf{u}(x)|^{-\frac{1+q}{2}}\nabla u^{1}(x)=\kappa\alpha^{1/\kappa}\nu(x_{0})(\mathbf{e}(x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}),

which along with
(ii)
implies that
\nabla(u^{1})^{1/\kappa}=\frac{1}{\kappa}(u^{1})^{-\frac{1+q}{2}}\nabla u^{1}
is continuous.

(v)
Proof is similar to previous parts.
∎

3.
Partial Hodograph-Legendre transform

In this section we provide the main tool of our approach, the change of coordinates involving dependent and independent variables. We begin with introducing the transform

(7)

T:x\mapsto y=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},(u^{1}(x)/\alpha)^{1/\kappa}).

In order to show that the transform is well-defined, we need that
u^{1}\geq 0
in a neighborhood of
x=0
which has been shown in Proposition
2.5
.
The following proposition shows that
T
is a local
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
0\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{u}}
and suppose (
2
) holds. Then the transform
T
, defined in (
7
), is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism in a neighborhood of
x=0
in
\overline{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}}
.

Proof.

It easily follows from Proposition
2.5
part
(iv)
that
\nabla(u^{1}/\alpha)^{1/\kappa}
is continuous in
\overline{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}}
and
\nabla(u^{1}/\alpha)^{1/\kappa}(0)=\mathbf{e}_{n}
. Therefore,
DT(0)=I
is identity and invertible.
The proof can now be completed by invoking the inverse function theorem.
∎

We consider the inverse transform
x=T^{-1}(y)
and define the partial Legendre transform of
\mathbf{u}
by

v^{1}(y):=x_{n}-y_{n},\quad v^{j}(y):=\frac{\alpha u^{j}(x)}{u^{1}(x)}\text{ for }2\leq j\leq m.

Since
T^{-1}
is
C^{1}
, the Legendre function

4.
Regularity in a degenerate equation

The key point in our approach is that under assumption (

We will show in this setting the solution of perturbed equation will be analytic as well.
In this section we first review the regularity of solutions for degenerate equations
y_{n}\Delta u+\gamma\partial_{n}u=f
.
Similar results can be found in some literature. We, however, provide a short background for readers’ convenience.

We start with the definition of a quotient Banach space

W^{k,p}_{*}(\Omega):=\{u:\nabla u\in W^{k-2,p}(\Omega)\text{ and }x_{n}D^{k}u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\}/{\mathbb{R}},

which is equipped with norm

\|u\|_{W^{k,p}_{*}(\Omega)}:=\|\nabla u\|_{W^{k-2,p}(\Omega)}+\|x_{n}D^{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Here is the main result for the regularity of the degenerate equation in the clean case.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
\gamma>0
, and for every
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
the equation

(16)

\Delta_{\gamma}u:=y_{n}\Delta u+\gamma\partial_{n}u=f,\qquad\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},

has a unique (up to a constant) solution
u\in W^{2,p}_{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
and the following estimate holds

\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{W^{2,p}_{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}\leq C_{n,p,\gamma}\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})},

where
C_{n,p,\gamma}
is a universal constant.
In addition, there is constant
\tilde{C}_{n,p,\gamma}
such that
if
f\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
, then
u\in W^{3,p}_{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
and

\|u\|_{W^{3,p}_{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}\leq\tilde{C}_{n,p,\gamma}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}.

Similar results for parabolic version of the degenerate operator
\Delta_{\gamma}-\partial_{t}
has been proved earlier; see Theorem 2 in
[
12
]

We shall, however, provide a relatively short and self-contained proof for our case in Appendix.

Lemma 5.2

(i)

Indeed,

5.2.

Here, we prove the first part of Theorem

5.3.
Analyticity of the solution

In previous subsection we proved partial analyticity for the Legendre function
\mathbf{v}
. Indeed, we showed that in a neighborhood of

is analytic in tangential directions.
Although this is sufficient for proving the analyticity of the regular part of free boundary, a natural question is whether a higher degree of regularity can also be obtained for

in the vertical directions

This property is established by virtue of the following proposition.
For the sake of convenience, we assume from now on that the result of previous subsection, namely the analyticity of
\mathbf{v}(y^{\prime},y_{n})
in tangential directions, holds in
B_{1}^{+}

Proposition 5.8
.

If
\varepsilon_{0}
in (

such that for any multi-index
\mu=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})
with

\Arrowvert{\partial^{\mu}\mathbf{v}}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\leq C_{0}R_{0}^{-k}k!.

Before proving this proposition, we explain how this can be used to show the analyticity of solution, the second part of Theorem
1.2

If we consider the Taylor expansion for

at
y=0
, for every
0\leq j\leq m
the remainder term vanishes provided
|y|\leq R_{0}/ne
, because

\displaystyle\Big|v^{j}(y)-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{|\mu|=k}\frac{\partial^{\mu}v^{j}(0)}{\mu!}y^{\mu}\Big|=\Big|\sum_{|\mu|=N}\frac{\partial^{\mu}v^{j}(y^{*})}{\mu!}y^{\mu}\Big|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{N}(\frac{n|y|}{R_{0}})^{N}.

Then
\mathbf{v}
is analytic in a neighborhood of
y=0
, and
the inverse of partial Hodograph-Legendre transform defined in Section
3

, is analytic.
The analytic version of inverse function theorem implies that
T
and so
(u^{1})^{1/\kappa}
is an analytic function.
Thus
u^{j}=u^{1}v^{j}/\alpha
is multiplication of
u^{1}
and the analytic function
v^{j}
.
Hence,
\mathbf{u}/|\mathbf{u}|
and
|\mathbf{u}|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}
have the analytic extension from
\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}
to
\overline{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}}
near

.

The following lemma is crucial for proof of Proposition
5.8
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Suppose
u
satisfies the ordinary differential equation

x_{n}\partial_{n}^{2}u+\gamma\partial_{n}u=f,

for some
\gamma>1
and
f\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
.
If
x_{n}\partial_{n}u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
, then
u\in W_{*}^{2,\infty}(0,1)
and

\begin{split}\Arrowvert{\partial_{n}u}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}},\\
\Arrowvert{x_{n}\partial_{n}^{2}u}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}&\leq 2\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

Proof.

Rewrite the equation as
\partial_{n}(x_{n}^{\gamma}\partial_{n}u)=x_{n}^{\gamma-1}f
and integrate to get

\partial_{n}u(x)=x_{n}^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{x_{n}}t^{\gamma-1}f(x^{\prime},t)dt.

Since
\gamma>1
and
x_{n}\partial_{n}u\in L^{\infty}
, we will have
\lim_{x_{n}\rightarrow 0}x_{n}^{\gamma}\partial_{n}u=0
.
Thus

\Arrowvert{\partial_{n}u}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}\leq x_{n}^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{x_{n}}t^{\gamma-1}\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}dt=\frac{1}{\gamma}\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}},

and

\Arrowvert{x_{n}\partial_{n}^{2}u}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}+\gamma\Arrowvert{\partial_{n}u}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\Arrowvert{f}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}.\qed

Remark 5.10

This lemma shows that operator

is invertible.
If we consider a perturbation operator

Lemma 5.12
.

Assume that
f(s,p):{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is analytic at
(s,p)=(0,0)
and its Taylor series is majorized by the geometric series

f(s,p)\ll\frac{Mr_{0}}{r_{0}-(s+p_{1}+\cdots+p_{n})},

for some constants
M
and

.
Then for any

such that
\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\leq r_{0}/2
, we have

\Arrowvert{f(u,\nabla u)}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}\leq\frac{Mr_{0}}{r_{0}-\left(\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}+\sum_{i}\Arrowvert{\partial_{i}u}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}\right)}.

Proof.

Consider the Taylor expansion of
f

f(s,p)=\sum_{\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n})}F_{\alpha}\,s^{\alpha_{0}}p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

Hence,

\begin{split}&\Arrowvert{f(u,\nabla u)}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}=\sum_{|\mu|+\mu_{n}\leq s}\frac{R^{|\mu|}r^{\mu_{n}}}{|\mu|!}\left(\Arrowvert{y_{n}\partial_{n}\partial^{\mu}f(u,\nabla u)}\Arrowvert_{\infty}+(\mu_{n}+1)\Arrowvert{\partial^{\mu}f(u,\nabla u)}\Arrowvert_{\infty}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{\alpha}|F_{\alpha}|\sum_{|\mu|+\mu_{n}\leq s}\frac{R^{|\mu|}r^{\mu_{n}}}{|\mu|!}\big(\Arrowvert{y_{n}\partial_{n}\partial^{\mu}\left(u^{\alpha_{0}}(\partial_{1}u)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\partial_{n}u)^{\alpha_{n}}\right)}\Arrowvert_{\infty}\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt+(\mu_{n}+1)\Arrowvert{\partial^{\mu}\left(u^{\alpha_{0}}(\partial_{1}u)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\partial_{n}u)^{\alpha_{n}}\right)}\Arrowvert_{\infty}\big)\\
&=\sum_{\alpha}|F_{\alpha}|\sum_{|\mu|+\mu_{n}\leq s}\sum_{\mu=\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{|\alpha|}}\frac{R^{|\mu|}r^{\mu_{n}}}{|\mu|!}\times\frac{\mu!}{\sigma_{1}!\cdots\sigma_{|\alpha|}!}\times\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\Big(\Arrowvert{y_{n}\partial_{n}\big(\partial^{\sigma_{1}}u\cdots\partial^{\sigma_{\alpha_{0}}}u\partial^{\sigma_{\alpha_{0}+1}}(\partial_{1}u)\cdots\partial^{\sigma_{|\alpha|}}(\partial_{n}u)\big)}\Arrowvert_{\infty}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+(\mu_{n}+1)\Arrowvert{\partial^{\sigma_{1}}u\cdots\partial^{\sigma_{\alpha_{0}}}u\partial^{\sigma_{\alpha_{0}+1}}(\partial_{1}u)\cdots\partial^{\sigma_{|\alpha|}}(\partial_{n}u)}\Arrowvert_{\infty}\Big)\\
&\leq\sum_{\alpha}|F_{\alpha}|\left(\sum_{|\mu|+\mu_{n}\leq s}\frac{R^{|\mu|}r^{\mu_{n}}}{|\mu|!}(\Arrowvert{y_{n}\partial_{n}\partial^{\mu}u}\Arrowvert_{\infty}+(\mu_{n}+1)\Arrowvert{\partial^{\mu}u}\Arrowvert_{\infty})\right)^{\alpha_{0}}\times\cdots\times\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\left(\sum_{|\mu|+\mu_{n}\leq s}\frac{R^{|\mu|}r^{\mu_{n}}}{|\mu|!}(\Arrowvert{y_{n}\partial_{n}\partial^{\mu}(\partial_{n}u)}\Arrowvert_{\infty}+(\mu_{n}+1)\Arrowvert{\partial^{\mu}(\partial_{n}u)}\Arrowvert_{\infty})\right)^{\alpha_{n}}\\
&=\sum_{\alpha}|F_{\alpha}|\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots\Arrowvert{\partial_{n}u}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}^{\alpha_{n}}\leq\frac{Mr_{0}}{r_{0}-\left(\Arrowvert{u}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}+\sum_{i}\Arrowvert{\partial_{i}u}\Arrowvert_{s,R,r}\right)}.\end{split}

∎

We do a formal argument, which can be easily justified. First

\int{\rm div\,}(x_{n}^{\gamma}\nabla u)\partial_{n}udx=-\int x_{n}^{\gamma}\nabla u\nabla\partial_{n}udx=\frac{\gamma}{2}\int x_{n}^{\gamma-1}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

and by Cauchy-Schwarz

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(x_{n}^{\gamma-1})}\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}\|x_{n}\Delta u+\gamma u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(x_{n}^{\gamma-1})}

Similarly we multiply with
x_{n}\Delta u
and integrate by parts to arrive at (
38
).

Consider

{\rm div\,}(x_{n}^{\gamma}\nabla u)=x_{n}^{\gamma-1}f

and let
T
by any of the maps

L^{2}(x_{n}^{\gamma/2})\ni f\to\partial_{j}u,x_{n}\partial^{2}_{jk}u\in L^{2}(x_{n}^{\gamma/2}).

It satisfies

\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(x_{n}^{\gamma-1})}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{2}(x_{n}^{\gamma-1})}.

It has an integral kernel
k
which satisfies

|B_{d(x,y)}(x)|_{\gamma-1}\leq C

|B_{d(x,y)}(x)|_{\gamma-1}(|k(x,y)-k(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})|)\leq c\frac{d(x,\tilde{x}),d(y,\tilde{y})}{d(x,y)+d(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})}.

The upper half plane equipped with the metric
d
and the measure
x_{n}^{\gamma-1}dx
is doubling:

|B_{3r}(x)|_{\gamma-1}\leq c|B_{r}(x)|_{\gamma-1}.

Thus
T
and its adjoint are Calderón-Zygmund operators (see

Let us recall the notion of Muckenhoupt weights: Let

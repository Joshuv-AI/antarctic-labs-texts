Quotients of double vector bundles and multigraded bundles
Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract
. We study quotients of multi-graded bundles, including double vector bundles. Among other things, we show that any such quotient fits into a tower of affine bundles. Applications of the theory include a construction of normal bundles for weighted submanifolds, as well as for pairs of submanifolds with clean intersection.



To the memory of Kirill Mackenzie.

1.
Introduction

Double vector bundles are fiber bundles
D\to M
, described by commutative squares

(1)

\textstyle{{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M}

whose sides
A,B
are vector bundles, with a certain compatibility between the horizontal and vertical vector bundle structures.
These objects were introduced by Pradines
[
25
,
26
]
in the 1970s in terms of local fiber bundle charts.
Mackenzie
[
17
]
described the compatibilities more intrinsically, as the requirements that the structure
maps for the horizontal vector bundle should be vector bundle morphisms for the vertical vector bundle structure, and vice versa.
Later, Grabowski and Rotkiewicz
[
6
]
simplified these conditions to the single requirement that the horizontal and vertical scalar multiplications commute.

Double structures, and more generally
n
-fold structures for
n>1
, feature prominently in
Mackenzie’s work
[
15
,
17
]
. Among other things, he discovered a natural action of the symmetric group
S_{3}
on the category of double vector bundles through duality operations. Together with Xu
[
18
]
, he established a canonical isomorphism between
T^{*}V
and
T^{*}V^{*}
, for any vector bundle
V\to M
. Mackenzie also studied Lie algebroid and Poisson structures on double vector bundles, and unearthed a wealth of interesting relationships among these structures. In later work, he and his co-authors explored the more difficult theory of triple vector bundles
[
5
,
9
,
16
]
, and even
n
-fold vector bundles
[
8
]
.

In this note, we will consider
quotients
of double vector bundles
D\to M
by double subvector bundles
D^{\prime}\to M
.
By definition, such a quotient is given by a surjective morphism of double vector bundles
D\to D^{\prime\prime}
having
D^{\prime}
as its
kernel

(the elements mapping to
M\subseteq D^{\prime\prime}
). Equivalently, it is given by an exact sequence of double vector bundles over
M
,

(2)

0_{M}\to D^{\prime}\to D\to D^{\prime\prime}\to 0_{M}

where
0_{M}
indicates
M
regarded as a double vector bundle over
M
. In contrast to ordinary vector bundles, knowledge of the kernel
D^{\prime}
does not suffice to construct the quotient
D^{\prime\prime}
.
The additional information needed may be described in terms of a bundle of bigraded Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}\to M
, with summands in bidegrees
(-1,0),(0,-1),(-1,-1)
given by

(3)

\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{A}\oplus\widehat{B}\oplus C.

Here
C\to M
is the
core
of the double vector bundle, given as the set of elements mapping to
M
under both side projections, while

\widehat{A},\widehat{B}\to M
are the
fat bundles
of Gracia-Saz and Mehta
[
10
]
, given as certain extensions of the side bundles
A,B\to M
by
B^{*}\otimes C
and
A^{*}\otimes C
, respectively.
The fiberwise bracket on (
3
) is defined by the bilinear pairing, due to Flari and Mackenzie
[
5
]
(see also
[
20
]
)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{C}\colon\widehat{A}\times_{M}\widehat{B}\to C.

For degree reasons,
\mathfrak{g}
is a bundle of
nilpotent
Lie algebras; let
G=\exp(\mathfrak{g})
be the corresponding bundle of simply connected nilpotent Lie groups.
We will show that there is a canonical action

G\times_{M}D\to D

of this bundle of Lie groups on the double vector bundle, which in fact is
fiberwise transitive
. We shall prove (cf. Theorem
4.2
):

Theorem.
The quotients of
D
are in bijective correspondence with bigraded Lie subalgebra bundles

\mathfrak{h}=\widehat{A}^{\prime}\oplus\widehat{B}^{\prime}\oplus C^{\prime}

of
\mathfrak{g}
, with the property
\widehat{A}^{\prime}\cap(B^{*}\otimes C)=B^{*}\otimes C^{\prime}
, and similarly for
\widehat{B}^{\prime}
.
Letting
H\subseteq G
be the closed subgroup bundle exponentiating
\mathfrak{h}
, we have that

D^{\prime}=H\cdot M
and
D^{\prime\prime}=D/H
.

We will show that the quotient
D^{\prime\prime}
may also be obtained as an
iterated quotient

D^{\prime\prime}=(D/C^{\prime})/(A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime})

by vector bundle actions: One first takes a quotient by the core action of
C^{\prime}\subseteq D^{\prime}
, followed by an action of the direct sum of side bundles
A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
of
D^{\prime}
. However, one still needs the subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
in order to specify the latter action.

For a concrete context where this construction applies, consider a manifold
M
together with two submanifolds
N_{1},N_{2}\subseteq M
, with clean intersection
N=N_{1}\cap N_{2}
. In
[
20
]
, we defined a

double normal bundle

(4)

\textstyle{{\nu(M,N_{1},N_{2})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\nu(N_{1},N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\nu(N_{2},N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N}

whose side bundles
\nu(N_{i},N),\ i=1,2
are the usual normal bundles of
N\subseteq N_{i}
. A more detailed discussion may be found in the thesis
[
24
]
. We will see that the double normal bundle is a subquotient of the double tangent bundle
TTM
, in direct generalization of the usual
definition of a normal bundle of a submanifold as

\nu(M,N)=TM|_{N}/TN
. Here
D\subseteq TTM
is the pre-image of
TN_{1}|_{N}\times_{N}TN_{2}|_{N}
under the side bundle projection to
TM\times_{M}TM
.
The subalgebra bundle
\mathfrak{h}
defining the quotient
D^{\prime\prime}=\nu(M,N_{1},N_{2})

may be described explicitly in terms of tangent/vertical
lifts of vector fields.

There is a similar discussion for the so-called
graded bundles
of Grabowski and Rotkievicz
[
6
]
. A graded bundle
E\to M
is a fiber bundle, with typical fiber a negatively graded vector space
\mathsf{E}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathsf{E}^{-i}
, and with structure group the polynomial diffeomorphisms of
\mathsf{E}
(not necessarily linear) preserving the grading. Thus, graded bundles may be seen as special cases of non-negatively graded supermanifolds where no odd coordinates are present. Examples of graded bundles include graded
vector
bundles, but also the
r
-th tangent bundle
T_{r}M=J_{0}^{r}(\mathbb{R},M)
.
Grabowski and Rotkiewicz
[
7
]
proved that graded bundles may be defined, much more simply, as manifolds
E
together with a smooth action of the multiplicative monoid
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
. Given a graded subbundle
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
over the same base
M
, one may again ask what additional data are needed in order to define a quotient graded bundle
E^{\prime\prime}
with an exact
sequence of graded bundles

0_{M}\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0_{M}.

Similar to the case of double vector bundles, the answer involves graded Lie subalgebra bundles of a nilpotent graded Lie algebra bundle canonically associated to
E
. Quotient constructions arise, for example,
in the theory of weightings along submanifolds
N\subseteq M
; the so-called
weighted normal bundle

\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)\to N

obtained in
[
14
]
is a graded bundle given as a subquotient of the
r
-th tangent bundle
T_{r}M
.

Rather than treating double vector bundles and graded bundles separately, we will consider both as special cases of
multi-graded bundles
: That is, manifolds
E
with
n
commuting actions of the monoid
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
. The two special cases are most relevant in applications, but the general setting is useful as well – for example, one might
consider notions of normal bundles for cleanly intersecting submanifolds with weightings
[
14
]
, or
n
-fold clean intersections of submanifolds with
n>2
. We will therefore start our discussion with a review of multi-graded bundles.

2.
n
-fold vector bundles and multigraded bundles

We begin with background material on multigraded bundles, as needed in this work. Double vector bundles and graded bundles will be regarded as special cases.

2.1.
n
-fold vector bundles

We shall adopt the following approach to n-fold vector bundles due to Grabowski and Rotkiewicz
[
6
,
7
]
.
Let
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
be the multiplicative monoid of real numbers. Given a vector bundle
E\to M
, the fiberwise scalar multiplication defines a smooth action of this monoid,

\kappa\colon\mathbb{R}\times E\to E,\ (t,v)\mapsto\kappa_{t}(v).

This action has the regularity property that the map
E\to TE,\ v\mapsto\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\kappa_{t}(v)
is injective. By a result of
Nagano
[
23
]
, further developed in
[
6
]
,
any monoid action of
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
on a smooth manifold
E
with
this regularity condition determines a unique vector bundle structure on
E
, with
\kappa_{t}
as its scalar multiplication. In fact, the vector bundle structure on
E
is such that the inclusion
E\to TE
is a morphism of vector bundles.
This implies, for example, that subvector bundles of
E
are exactly the
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
-invariant submanifolds, and that smooth maps between vector bundles are vector bundle morphisms if and only if they are
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
-equivariant.

Using the perspective, it was shown in
[
6
]
that double vector bundles are simply manifolds
D
with two commuting regular
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
-actions, a horizontal action
\kappa^{h}
and a vertical action
\kappa^{v}
.
The side bundles and the base of the double vector bundle are given by

A=\kappa_{0}^{v}(D),\ B=\kappa_{0}^{h}(D),\ M=\kappa_{0}^{h}\kappa_{0}^{v}(D).

More generally,
n
-fold vector bundles are characterized as smooth manifolds
E

with actions
\kappa\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times E\to E
of the multiplicative monoid

(5)

(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)=(\mathbb{R},\cdot)\times\cdots\times(\mathbb{R},\cdot)

such that each component action is regular.
Typical examples of
n
-fold vector bundles include the
n
-fold tangent bundle
T^{n}M=T\cdots TM
, or iterations of tangent and cotangent bundles. As another example, consider
n
submanifolds
N_{1},\ldots,N_{n}
of a manifold
M
with
clean
intersection
: that is, for all
q\in M
, there are local coordinates centered at
q
, in such a way that any
N_{i}
containing
q
is given by the vanishing of a subset of these coordinates.
Then there is an
n
-fold normal bundle

(6)

\nu(M,N_{1},\ldots,N_{n})\to N

with base
N=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}N_{i}
. For
n=2
, this was introduced in
[
20
,
24
]
; see
[
14
]
for general
n
. One of the possible definitions is as a character spectrum:
Give
C^{\infty}(M)
the multi-filtration, where a function has filtration degree
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
if it vanishes to order
i_{1}
on
N_{1}
, to order
i_{2}
on
N_{2}
, and so on. Let
\operatorname{gr}C^{\infty}(M)
be the associated multi-graded algebra; its summand of degree
\mathbf{i}
is the space of functions vanishing to order
\mathbf{i}
, modulo sums of functions vanishing to higher order (using the partial ordering on multi-indices). Then
(
6
) may be defined as
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{gr}C^{\infty}(M),\mathbb{R})
. Using evaluation,

\operatorname{gr}C^{\infty}(M)
is then realized as the algebra of polynomial functions on the
n
-fold normal bundle. We will discuss double normal bundles in Section
5
below.

2.2.
Multi-graded bundles

Grabowski and Rotkiewicz found that it is interesting to drop the regularity assumption:

Definition 2.1
.

An
n
-graded bundle
is a manifold
E
with a smooth action
\kappa\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times E\to E
of the multiplicative monoid
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
, called its
scalar multiplications
. For
n=1
it is called a
graded bundle
. A
morphism of
n
-graded bundles

\phi\colon E^{\prime}\to E
is a smooth map intertwining the scalar multiplications.

By
[
7
]
, an
n
-graded bundle
E
is a smooth fiber bundle over the fixed point set
M\subseteq E
of the action, with
\kappa_{0}
as the bundle projection.

Example 2.2
.

A negatively graded
vector
bundle
E=E^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E^{-r}\to M
is a graded bundle, with
\kappa_{t}
given as multiplication by
t^{i}
on
E^{-i}
.
We may also regard
E
as a 2-graded bundle, where the second
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
-action is the usual scalar multiplication.
More generally,
(\mathbb{Z}_{<0})^{n}
-graded vector bundles

(7)

E=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}E^{-i}

are
n
-graded bundles, and may be regarded as
(n+1)
-graded bundles by including the scalar multiplication for the usual vector bundle structure over
M
.

Conversely, an
n
-graded bundle
E\to M
, together with a vector bundle structure over the same base
M
, is an
n
-graded vector bundle
if and only if the
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
-action is linear, i.e.,
commutes with the scalar multiplication from the vector bundle structure.

Example 2.3
.

The
r
-th tangent bundle (Ehresmann’s bundle of
r
-velocities
[
3
]
)) may be defined as a jet bundle

(8)

T_{r}M=J^{r}_{0}(\mathbb{R},M),

consisting of
r
-jets of paths
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
. It is a graded bundle, with
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
-action defined by linear reparametrization of paths.
Its iterations

T_{r_{1}\cdots r_{n}}M=T_{r_{1}}\cdots T_{r_{n}}M

are examples of
n
-graded bundles. These also admit a more symmetric description: For
r\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathbb{A}_{r}
be the unital graded algebra with a generator
\epsilon
of degree
-1
, and relation
\epsilon^{r+1}=0
. Then
\mathbb{A}_{r_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{r_{n}}
is an
n
-graded algebra, and

(9)

T_{r_{1}\cdots r_{n}}M=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(C^{\infty}(M),\mathbb{A}_{r_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{r_{n}}).

(This is analogous to the characterization of tangent vectors as derivations.) Note that the various ‘flip isomorphisms’
(such as
T_{r_{1}r_{2}}M\cong T_{r_{2}r_{1}}M
) are obvious from this description. Let us also remark that there is a natural inclusion

T_{r}M\to T_{1,\ldots,1}M=T^{r}M
, coming from the inclusion
\mathbb{A}_{r}\to\mathbb{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{1}
as the fixed point set of the action of the symmetric group
S_{r}
on
\mathbb{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{A}_{1}
.
See
[
13
, Chapter VIII]
for more information on the Weil algebra approach to higher tangent bundles.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathfrak{g}^{-i}
be a finite-dimensional
n
-graded Lie algebra, i.e.,
[\mathfrak{g}^{-i_{1}},\mathfrak{g}^{-i_{2}}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{-i_{1}-i_{2}}
.
For degree reasons,
\mathfrak{g}
is necessarily nilpotent; let
G=\exp\mathfrak{g}
be the simply connected nilpotent Lie group exponentiating it.
Since the
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
-action on
\mathfrak{g}
is by Lie algebra morphisms, it exponentiates to an action on
G
by
Lie group morphisms, making
G
into an
n
-graded bundle over
\operatorname{pt}
. If
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is an
n
-graded Lie subalgebra, with corresponding subgroup
H=\exp\mathfrak{h}
, then the
homogeneous space
G/H
is an
n
-graded bundle over
\operatorname{pt}
. Lie groups
G
with an
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)
-action by group morphisms go under various names, for example
homogeneous Lie groups
(for a detailed discussion, see
[
4
]
). This example generalizes to Lie algebroids, and in particular to bundles of
n
-graded Lie algebras.

2.3.
Exact sequences

Let
E,E^{\prime}
be
n
-graded bundles over
M
. For a morphism of
n
-graded bundles
\varphi\colon E^{\prime}\to E
, with base map the identity,
we define the range and kernel by

\operatorname{ran}(\varphi)=\varphi(E^{\prime}),\ \ \ \operatorname{ker}(\varphi)=\varphi^{-1}(M).

If
\varphi
has constant rank,
then these are
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
-invariant submanifolds, and hence are

n
-graded subbundles of
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively. A sequence of morphisms of graded bundles (over a common base
M
, with base maps the identity)

\cdots E_{\nu}\to E_{\nu+1}\to E_{\nu+2}\to\cdots

is called
exact
if for each
\nu
, the range of the map
E_{\nu}\to E_{\nu+1}
equals the kernel of the map
E_{\nu+1}\to E_{\nu+2}
. Below, we will encounter a number of examples of exact sequences of
n
-graded bundles.

2.4.
Linear approximation

Given an
n
-graded bundle
E\to M
, the normal bundle
\nu(E,M)
relative to the base submanifold is again an
n
-graded bundle, with scalar multiplications
obtained by applying the normal bundle functor to
\kappa_{t}
. Since
the action
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
-action on
\nu(E,M)

is linear, the normal bundle is in fact an
n
-fold graded
vector
bundle as in Example
2.2
. We will refer to this bundle as the
linear approximation
of
E
, and use the notation

(10)

\nu(E,M)=E_{\operatorname{lin}}.

For any multi-index
i=(i_{1}\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, the element
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
acts as multiplication by

t^{i}=t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{i_{n}}
on
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}
.

For an
n
-graded vector bundle (
7
), we have
E_{\operatorname{lin}}\cong E
canonically.
For the
r
-th tangent bundle
(
8
) we have that

(T_{r}M)_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}=\begin{cases}TM&i\leq r\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The same description holds for the
iterations (
9
), interpreting
r
as a multi-index

(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
, and using the partial ordering on multi-indices
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, where
k\geq k^{\prime}
if and only if
k_{a}\geq k_{a}^{\prime}
for all
a
. For every morphism of graded bundles
\varphi\colon E^{\prime}\to E
, applying the normal bundle functor gives a morphism of graded vector bundles
\varphi_{\operatorname{lin}}\colon E^{\prime}_{\operatorname{lin}}\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}
called the linear approximation of
\varphi
.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
6
,
7
]
For every graded bundle
E\to M
, there exists an isomorphism of graded bundles

\varphi\colon E_{\operatorname{lin}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}E,

with linear approximation the identity.

The isomorphism is not unique (only its restriction to
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}
, with
i
maximal subject to the condition that
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\neq 0
, is unique). We refer to the choice of
\varphi
as a
linearization
of
E
.

Example 2.6
.

For a double vector bundle
D
, as in (
1
), we have that

D_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1,0}=A,\ \ \ D_{\operatorname{lin}}^{0,-1}=B,\ \ \ D_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1,-1}=C

(where
C
is the core).
Hence, a linearization is a choice of isomorphism of double vector bundles

\varphi\colon A\oplus B\oplus C\to D

whose linear approximation is the identity. The restriction of
\phi
to
C\subseteq D
is uniquely determined by this property. A linearization of a double vector bundle is also known as a
splitting
or
decomposition
. For direct proofs, see e.g.

[
2
,
12
,
20
]
.

2.5.
Polynomial functions

Let
E\to M
be an
n
-graded bundle, with scalar multiplication
\kappa_{t},\ t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
A function

f\in C^{\infty}(E)
is called a
homogeneous polynomial function of degree
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
if

(\kappa_{t})^{*}f=t^{k}f

for all
t\in(\mathbb{R}-\{0\})^{n}
. Let
C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(E)^{k}
be the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
k
, and

C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(E)=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(E)^{k}

of polynomial functions on
E
. Using a linearization of
E
, we see that the space
C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(E)^{k}
is non-trivial only if
k\geq 0
, and is the space of sections of a
vector bundle
\mathcal{P}(E)^{k}
. Taking a direct sum over
k
, we obtain a graded algebra bundle

(11)

\mathcal{P}(E)=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{P}(E)^{k}.

Example 2.7
.

Let
E=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}E^{-i}
be a
(\mathbb{Z}_{<0})^{n}
-graded
vector
bundle. Then

\mathcal{P}(E)^{k}=\bigoplus_{\{k_{i}\}}\bigotimes_{i}\operatorname{Sym}^{k_{i}}(E^{-i})^{*}

where the sum is over sequences
\{k_{i},\ i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
such that
\sum k_{i}\cdot i=k
, and
\operatorname{Sym}
indicates symmetric powers. This describes the situation for a general
n
-graded bundle
E
by choice of a linearization.

Example 2.8
.

For a double vector bundle (
1
), the algebra bundle
\mathcal{P}(D)
is generated (as a
unital
algebra bundle) by its components
\mathcal{P}(D)^{1,0},\ \mathcal{P}(D)^{0,1},\ \mathcal{P}(D)^{1,1}
.
There are canonical isomorphisms
\mathcal{P}(D)^{1,0}=A^{*}
and
\mathcal{P}(D)^{0,1}=B^{*}
, and an exact sequence

0_{M}\to A^{*}\otimes B^{*}\to\mathcal{P}(D)^{1,1}\to C^{*}\to 0_{M},

where the first map is given by multiplication in
\mathcal{P}(D)
. The bundle of double-linear functions
\mathcal{P}(D)^{1,1}
is the ‘fat bundle’ associated to
C^{*}
. This bundle plays an important role in the theory of DVB-sequences of
[
1
]
, where it is shown that a
double vector bundle with side bundles
A,B
and core
C
is equivalent to such an exact sequence. Indeed, the exact sequence allows us to reconstruct the algebra bundle
\mathcal{P}(D)
, which in turn determines
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\mathcal{P}(D),\mathbb{R})
(the fiberwise character spectrum). See also
[
20
]
for further details and related constructions.

Morphisms of graded bundles
E\to E^{\prime}
over
M
give morphisms of graded
algebra bundles
\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime})\to\mathcal{P}(E)
by pullback of functions. Letting
0_{M}=M\times\{0\}
(regarded as an
n
-graded bundle), we have
\mathcal{P}(0_{M})=M\times\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.9
.

The
augmentation ideal

\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)
is the kernel of the map
\mathcal{P}(E)\to\mathcal{P}(0_{M})
given by the inclusion
0_{M}\to E
.

The sections of the augmentation ideal are the polynomial functions vanishing along
M\subseteq E
.

2.6.
Vertical vector fields

The grading on polynomial functions induces gradings on all polynomial tensor fields on
E
. For the quotient construction, we are mainly interested in vertical vector fields.
Let

\mathcal{V}(E)\to M

be the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra bundle of fiberwise graded derivations of
\mathcal{P}(E)
. The sections of

\mathcal{V}(E)^{k}
are the

vertical
vector fields
X
on
E
satisfying
\kappa_{t}^{*}X=t^{k}X
for
t\in(\mathbb{R}-\{0\})^{n}
.
The Lie algebra bundle

\mathcal{V}(E)
is a graded
\mathcal{P}(E)
-module.

Example 2.10
.

Let
E=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}E^{-i}
be a
(\mathbb{Z}_{<0})^{n}
-graded
vector
bundle. Then there is a degree preserving inclusion

E\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}(E)

as
(fiberwise) constant vertical vector fields
. Since these span the vertical tangent space everywhere, we have

\mathcal{V}(E)=\mathcal{P}(E)\otimes E
in this case. For a general
n
-graded bundle, such a decomposition depends on the choice of a linearization.

Example 2.11
.

For a double vector bundle (
1
), the space
\mathcal{V}(D)^{k}
is trivial unless

k\geq(-1,-1)
. A vector field of homogeneity
(-1,-1)
restricts to the core, and the restriction map gives an isomorphism

\mathcal{V}(D)^{-1,-1}\cong C.

Similarly, a vector field of homogeneity
(-1,0)
restricts to the side bundle
A
; the kernel of the restriction map
is
\mathcal{P}(D)^{0,1}\otimes\mathcal{V}(D)^{-1,-1}=B^{*}\otimes C
(linear functions on
B
times
core vector fields). We may take

\mathcal{V}(D)^{-1,0}=\widehat{A},\ \ \ \mathcal{V}(D)^{0,-1}=\widehat{B},

as a definition of the
fat bundles
described in the introduction.

Recall the augmentation ideal
\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)
, and let

\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)=\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\mathcal{V}(E).

Lemma 2.12
.

Restriction to
M\subseteq E
gives an exact sequence of graded bundles

(12)

0_{M}\to\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)\to\mathcal{V}(E)\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}\to 0_{M}.

In particular, the sections of
\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)
are the vertical polynomial vector fields vanishing along
M\subseteq E
.

Proof.

Choosing a linearization
E\cong E_{\operatorname{lin}}
, we see that the restriction map
\mathcal{V}(E)\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}\subseteq TE|_{M}
is surjective, with kernel the vertical vector fields vanishing along
M
.
∎

2.7.
The bundle of nilpotent Lie groups

The
n
-graded Lie algebra bundle
\mathcal{V}(E)
has a subbundle, spanned by the components of strictly negative degree

\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{V}(E)^{<0}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathcal{V}(E)^{-i}

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{+}=\mathfrak{g}\cap\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)
. The sections of
\mathfrak{g}
are polynomial vector fields on
E
of strictly negative degree, and those in

\mathfrak{g}^{+}
are furthermore zero along
M\subseteq E
.

Lemma 2.13
.

The bundle
\mathfrak{g}
has finite rank and generates
\mathcal{V}(E)
as a
\mathcal{P}(E)
-module. Restriction to
M\subseteq E
gives an exact sequence of graded bundles

(13)

0_{M}\to\mathfrak{g}^{+}\to\mathfrak{g}\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}\to 0_{M}.

The action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
E
is fiberwise transitive. (That is, the corresponding vector fields span the vertical subbundle of
TE
everywhere.)

Proof.

Choosing a linearization
E\cong E_{\operatorname{lin}}
, and regarding sections of
E_{\operatorname{lin}}
as fiberwise constant vector fields on
E_{\operatorname{lin}}
, we obtain an isomorphism

\mathcal{V}(E)=\mathcal{P}(E)\otimes E_{\operatorname{lin}}.

The statement is clear from this linearized model; in particular

(14)

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{j-i<0}\mathcal{P}(E)^{j}\otimes E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}

has finite rank, and it generates
\mathcal{V}(E)
since already
E_{\operatorname{lin}}
does. Similarly, the vector fields corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}
span the vertical space of
E\to M
, since already
E_{\operatorname{lin}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
does.
∎

Example 2.14
.

For a double vector bundle (
1
), this is the Lie algebra bundle

\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{A}\oplus\widehat{B}\oplus C

described in the introduction. The bracket restricts to a (possibly degenerate) pairing

\widehat{A}\otimes\widehat{B}=\mathcal{V}^{-1,0}(D)\otimes\mathcal{V}^{0,-1}\to\mathcal{V}^{-1,-1}(D)=C
, which is the warp pairing of Flari-Mackenzie
[
5

. Note that this pairing determines the bracket, for degree reasons. Further details will be given in Section
4.2
below.

For degree reasons, the Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent. By fiberwise exponentiation, we obtain an
n
-graded Lie group bundle (see Example
2.4
)

G=\exp(\mathfrak{g})\to M,

with an
n
-graded subbundle
G^{+}=\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{+})
.

Lemma 2.15
.

The fiberwise
\mathfrak{g}
-action on the
n
-graded bundle
E\to M
exponentiates to a fiberwise action of
G
.

Proof.

Recall that a Lie algebra action exponentiates to an action of the corresponding connected, simply connected Lie group if and only if its generating vector fields are complete. We will use this fact fiberwise, so we may assume
M=\operatorname{pt}
,
with
E
a graded vector space
\mathsf{E}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\mathsf{E}^{-i}
. After passage to the diagonal action of
(\mathbb{R},\cdot)\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
(which does not change the space of polynomial vector fields of negative degree) it suffices to consider the case

n=1
. Letting
X=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}X_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathsf{E})
, with
X_{i}
homogeneous of degree
-i<0
,
we want to show that
X
is complete.
Choose a smooth function

\varrho\colon\mathsf{E}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, homogeneous of degree
1
outside a compact subset.
Then

\mathcal{L}_{X_{i}}\log\varrho
is homogeneous of degree
-i<0
outside a compact subset, and hence is bounded on

\mathsf{E}
. Consequently,
\mathcal{L}_{X}\log(\varrho)

is bounded on
\mathsf{E}
.
This implies that the flow of
X
cannot go to infinity in finite time, so
X
is complete.
∎

Since the
\mathfrak{g}
-action on
E
is fiberwise transitive, with stabilizer of
M
the subbundle
\mathfrak{g}^{+}
, it follows
that the
G
-action on
E
is fiberwise transitive, with stabilizer of
M
the subbundle
G^{+}
.
We see in particular that every
n
-graded bundle is a homogeneous space for an
n
-graded Lie group bundle:

E=G/G^{+}.

See Section
4.2
for a description of the group bundle
G
associated to double vector bundles.

Remark 2.16
.

If
E\to M
is an ordinary vector bundle (regarded as a
1
-graded bundle), we have that
\mathfrak{g}=E
,
where elements of
E
are identified with constant vector fields on fibers, and
\mathfrak{g}^{+}=0
(the zero bundle).
The corresponding group bundle is
G=E
with the group multiplication given by addition, and the

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be maximal subject to the condition
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\neq 0
. Then

is in the center of

and its image under

(with group structure the vector bundle addition). In terms of a linearization of

, its action

(E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i})_{\operatorname{lin}}=\bigoplus_{j\neq i}E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-j}.

2.8.
Splitting short exact sequences

A short exact sequence

(15)

0_{M}\longrightarrow E^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}E^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0_{M}

(where
0_{M}=M\times\{0\}
denotes the zero bundle over
M
, regarded as an
n
-graded bundle) is equivalent to a surjective morphism of graded bundles
\pi\colon E\to E^{\prime\prime}
, with
i\colon E^{\prime}\to E
the inclusion of its kernel. A
splitting
of a short exact sequence is a
morphism
j\colon E^{\prime\prime}\to E
such that
\pi\circ j=\operatorname{id}_{E^{\prime\prime}}
. As in the case of vector bundles, short exact sequences always split:

Proposition 2.17
.

Every short exact sequence (
15
) of
n
-graded bundles admits a splitting.

Proof.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be maximal subject to the condition
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\neq 0
. The exact sequence for

E
restricts to an exact sequence of vector bundles

(16)

0_{M}\to(E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{\prime})^{-i}\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{\prime\prime})^{-i}\to 0_{M}.

Taking the quotient by the respective vector bundle actions, this produces an exact sequence of
n
-graded bundles

(17)

0_{M}\to E^{\prime}/(E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{\prime})^{-i}\to E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}E^{\prime\prime}/(E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{\prime\prime})^{-i}\to 0_{M}.

By induction on the rank of
E
, (
17
) admits a splitting; fix such a splitting and also a splitting of (
16
).
To prove the proposition, it suffices to find isomorphisms

(18)

E\cong(E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i})\times_{M}E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i},\ \ \ \ E^{\prime\prime}\cong(E^{\prime\prime}/(E^{\prime\prime}_{\operatorname{lin}})^{-i})\times_{M}(E^{\prime\prime}_{\operatorname{lin}})^{-i}

that are compatible with the projection
\pi
, in the sense that

\pi(x,w)=(\pi(x),\pi(w))
. Let

q\colon E\to E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i},\ \ \ q^{\prime\prime}\colon E^{\prime\prime}\to E^{\prime\prime}/(E^{\prime\prime}_{\operatorname{lin}})^{-i}

be the two quotient maps. The choice of isomorphisms (
18
) amount to splittings

s\colon E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\to E,\ \ s^{\prime\prime}\colon E^{\prime\prime}/(E^{\prime\prime}_{\operatorname{lin}})^{-i}\to E^{\prime\prime},

i.e.,

q\circ s=\operatorname{id},\ q^{\prime\prime}\circ s^{\prime\prime}=\operatorname{id}
. Compatibility with
\pi
is equivalent to
commutativity of the diagram

\textstyle{{E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{E^{\prime\prime}/(E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{\prime\prime})^{-i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}
\textstyle{E^{\prime\prime}}

Start with arbitrary splittings
s,s^{\prime\prime}
(e.g., defined by linearizations of
E,E^{\prime\prime}
), and
consider the difference

f=\pi\circ s-s^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi\colon E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\to E^{\prime\prime}.

From
q^{\prime\prime}\circ f=0
, we see that
f
takes values in
(E^{\prime\prime}_{\operatorname{lin}})^{-i}\subseteq E^{\prime\prime}
. The given splitting of
(
16
) lifts
f
to a
morphism

\widehat{f}\colon E/E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}.

Then

\widetilde{s}=(-\widehat{f})\cdot s
is again a splitting of
q
(the dot indicates the action of the vector bundle

E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-i}
on
E
), and
\pi\circ\widetilde{s}-s^{\prime\prime}\circ\pi=0
, as desired.
∎

3.
Quotient constructions

In contrast to vector bundles, an
n
-graded subbundle of an
n
-graded bundle does not
determine a quotient in a canonical way. In this section, we will clarify what additional structure is needed. Throughout,
E
will denote an
n
-graded bundle over
M
, with base projection
p\colon E\to M
.

3.1.
Criterion for quotients

Consider a quotient of
E
, described by a short exact sequence

0_{M}\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0_{M}.

The tangent bundle to the quotient map
\pi\colon E\to E^{\prime\prime}
is a
\kappa_{t}
-invariant integrable distribution
T_{\pi}E\subseteq TE
. View the elements of
\mathcal{V}(E)
as vector fields on the respective fiber of
E\to M
, and let

\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(E)

be the subset for which the vector field is tangent to the fibers of
\pi
. Thus, the action map

(19)

\varrho\colon p^{*}\mathcal{V}(E)\to TE

takes
p^{*}\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
to
T_{\pi}E
. Put
\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)_{\pi}=\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)\cap\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The subset
\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(E)
is a bundle of graded Lie subalgebras, and of
\mathcal{P}(E)
-submodules. It has the property

\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)_{\pi}=\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.17
, we may choose a splitting
E\cong E^{\prime}\times_{M}E^{\prime\prime}
(with
\pi
given by projection to the second factor). This
identifies
\mathcal{P}(E)=\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime\prime})
, and determines a direct sum decomposition decomposition as
\mathcal{P}(E)
-submodules

\mathcal{V}(E)=\big(\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{V}(E^{\prime\prime})\big)\oplus\big(\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime\prime})\otimes\mathcal{V}(E^{\prime})\big).

The second summand in this decomposition is
\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
;
in particular,
\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
is a graded subbundle. It is immediate that

\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
is closed under the bracket operation of
\mathcal{V}(E)
. We see furthermore

\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)_{\pi}=(\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime\prime})\otimes\mathcal{V}^{+}(E^{\prime}))+(\mathcal{P}^{+}(E^{\prime\prime})\otimes\mathcal{V}(E^{\prime}));

since
\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)=\mathcal{P}^{+}(E^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime\prime})+\mathcal{P}(E^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{P}^{+}(E^{\prime\prime})
, but this is the same as
\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\cdot\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
.
∎

The following result shows that the property observed in Lemma
3.1
actually
characterizes the subbundles of the form
\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(E)
is a graded Lie subalgebra bundle, which is also a
\mathcal{P}(E)
submodule. Then
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
for a
(unique) quotient map
\pi\colon E\to E^{\prime\prime}
if and only if the submodule
\mathcal{L}^{+}=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)
satisfies

(20)

\mathcal{L}^{+}=\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\,\mathcal{L}.

In terms of the Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{h}=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{V}(E)^{<0}
, and the corresponding Lie group bundle
H
, we have that

E^{\prime}=H\cdot M\subseteq E,\ \ \ \ E^{\prime\prime}=E/H.

Proof.

Lemma
3.1
shows that this condition is satisfied if
\mathcal{L}
is of the form
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{V}(E)_{\pi}
. For the converse, suppose that
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(E)
is a graded
\mathcal{P}(E)
-submodule, closed under brackets, and satisfying (
20
).
Let
S\subseteq\mathcal{L}
be a graded subbundle complementary to
\mathcal{L}^{+}
, so that

\mathcal{L}=S\oplus\mathcal{L}^{+}.

Note that
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{+}
coincide in non-negative degrees (since
\mathcal{V}(E)
and
\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)
agree in non-negative degrees, by the exact sequence (
13
)). Hence
S
is non-trivial only in strictly negative degree, and in particular is of finite rank.
Using (
20
), any
x\in(\mathcal{L}^{+})^{k}
may be written as

(21)

x=\sum_{a}p_{a}x_{a}

where

x_{a}\in\mathcal{L}^{k_{a}}
with
k_{a}<k
, so
p_{a}\in\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)
.
Each
x_{a}
appearing in (
21
) may be decomposed into its
S
and
\mathcal{L}^{+}
components;
to the
\mathcal{L}^{+}
component we may apply (
20
) again, and so on. Lowering the degrees of
x_{a}
in this way, we eventually obtain an expression (
21
) where all
x_{a}\in S
. That is,

\mathcal{L}^{+}=\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\,S.

Hence also
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{P}(E)\,S
, which implies that the image of
p^{*}\mathcal{L}
under the action map (
19
) coincides with the image of
p^{*}S
. Along

M\subseteq E
, the map
\varrho
is the surjection
\mathcal{V}(E)\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}\subseteq TE
, with kernel
\mathcal{V}^{+}(E)
. Hence it restricts to an injective map
S\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}
along
M
. We conclude that
\varrho\big|_{p^{*}S}\colon p^{*}S\to TE
is injective along
M
, hence also on some
open neighborhood of
M
in
E
. By homogeneity, it is injective everywhere.

We conclude that
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
(regarded as a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{X}(E)
by the action
\varrho
)
defines a constant rank,
\kappa_{t}
-invariant distribution on
E
. Since

\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
is involutive, this distribution is Frobenius integrable, and so defines a foliation of
E
. Let

\mathfrak{h}=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{V}(E)^{<0}=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}

be the nilpotent Lie subalgebra bundle given as the summand of strictly negative degree, and
H\subseteq G
the corresponding Lie group bundle. Since
S\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
, the orbits of the Lie algebra action of
\mathcal{L}
coincide with those of the
H
-action,

H\times_{M}E\to E.

Since this action preserves
n
-gradings, the quotient space
E^{\prime\prime}=E/H
is an
n
-graded manifold, with
\pi
as its quotient map. The kernel of the quotient map is the leaf through
M
, i.e.
E^{\prime}=H\cdot M
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The condition (
20
) can be restated in terms of the subalgebra bundle
\mathfrak{h}=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathfrak{g}
(the strictly negative summands of
\mathcal{L}
), as the requirement that

(22)

\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}.

The exact sequence (
13
) restricts to an exact sequence
of graded vector bundles

(23)

0_{M}\to\mathfrak{h}^{+}\to\mathfrak{h}\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{\prime}\to 0_{M}.

Remark 3.4
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a graded Lie subalgebra bundle satisfying the weaker condition

(24)

\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{+}(E)\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\mathfrak{h}=\mathcal{P}(E)\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{g}
satisfies (
22
), and so determines a
quotient
\pi\colon E\to E^{\prime\prime}=E/K=E/H
, with kernel
E^{\prime}
.

3.2.
Iterated quotients

Rather than taking a quotient of
E
by the action of a nilpotent group bundle, we may also take an iterated quotient by vector bundle actions. In other words, every quotient map factors as a tower of affine bundles.

Theorem 3.5
.

Every quotient map
\pi\colon E\to E^{\prime\prime}
of
n
-graded bundles over
M
factors as a sequence of morphisms

E\to E_{1}\to E_{2}\to\cdots\to E^{\prime\prime}

where each map is a quotient by a free and proper vector bundle action.

Proof.

Let
H=\exp\mathfrak{h}\subseteq G
be as in Theorem
3.2
, so that
E^{\prime\prime}=E/H
.
Let
i\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be maximal subject to the condition that
\mathfrak{h}^{-i}\neq 0
. For degree reasons,
\mathfrak{h}^{-i}
is contained in the center of
\mathfrak{h}
. Its image under
\exp\colon\mathfrak{h}\to H
is a central subgroup bundle
\exp\mathfrak{h}^{-i}
, with group structure the vector bundle addition
of
\mathfrak{h}^{-i}
. Let

E_{1}=E/\exp(\mathfrak{h}^{-i})

be the
quotient under the vector bundle action of
\exp\mathfrak{h}^{-i}
.

The quotient by
\mathfrak{h}^{-i}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
is the same as the quotient by
\mathcal{P}(E)\mathfrak{h}^{-i}\cap\mathfrak{h}
, which is a normal Lie subalgebra bundle of
\mathfrak{h}
. Hence, the quotient
\mathfrak{h}_{1}=\mathfrak{h}/(\mathcal{P}(E)\mathfrak{h}^{-i}\cap\mathfrak{h})
acts on
E_{1}
, and
E_{1}/H_{1}=E^{\prime\prime}
.
By induction, the quotient map
E_{1}\to E^{\prime\prime}
factors as a sequence of quotients by free and proper vector bundle actions.
∎

Example 3.6
.

Let
E
be a graded bundle (with
n=1
) of order
r
, i.e.
E^{-i}=0
for
i>r
,
and take
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{V}(E)^{<0}
. The quotient map to
E/G=M
factors as a sequence of quotient maps

E\to E_{1}\to E_{2}\to\ldots\to M

where the first quotient is by a vector bundle action of
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-r}
, the second quotient is by a vector bundle action of
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-r+1}
, and so on. In particular, the tower of higher tangent bundles

T_{r}M\to T_{r-1}M\to\cdots\to TM\to M

is of this form; here each arrow is a quotient map by an action of
TM
.

Example 3.7
.

The bundle projection
D\to M
of a double vector bundle (
1
) may be factored as

D\to A\times_{M}B\to A\to M

where the first map is a quotient by the core
C
, the second map is a quotient by
B
, and the last map is a quotient by
A
. Note that there are no natural actiuons of
A,B
on
D
.

4.
Double vector bundles

In this section, we specialize the general quotient construction to double vector bundles (repeating a few facts already mentioned).
For background on double vector bundles, see e.g.
[
6
,
11
,
17
,
20
]
.

4.1.
Linear approximation

For any double vector bundle

(25)

\textstyle{{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M}

the map
D\to A\times_{M}B
given by the
horizontal and vertical base projections is a surjective submersion, with kernel the core
C\to M
of the double vector bundle.
Thus, by definition there is a short exact sequence of double vector bundles
[
11
, Section 2.2]

0_{M}\to C\to D\to A\times_{M}B\to 0_{M}.

Since the horizontal and vertical scalar multiplications coincide on
C
, the core may be regarded as a vector bundle,
C\to M
. The linear approximation of
D
is a bigraded vector bundle
D_{\operatorname{lin}}\to M
with summands

D_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1,0}=A,\ \ \ D_{\operatorname{lin}}^{0,-1}=B,\ \ \ D_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1,-1}=C.

The scalar multiplications of
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
on
D_{\operatorname{lin}}
are given by

\kappa_{t}(a,b,c)=(t_{1}a,\ t_{2}b,\ t_{1}t_{2}c),\ \ \ (a,b,c)\in A\times_{M}B\times_{M}C;

after a choice of linearization
D_{\operatorname{lin}}\cong D
this serves as a model for
D
.

4.2.
The nilpotent group bundle
G\to M

The Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{V}(D)^{<0}
has components in the same degrees as
D_{\operatorname{lin}}
. The bundle map

\mathfrak{g}\to D_{\operatorname{lin}}
is an isomorphism in bidegree
(-1,-1)
(since
i=(1,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
is the unique maximal index for which
\mathfrak{g}^{-i}\neq 0
); hence we have

\mathfrak{g}^{-1,-1}=C.

Sections of
C
define vector fields on
D
of homogeneity
-1,-1
, these are the
core vector fields
. The action
C\times_{M}D\to D
of the corresponding subgroup bundle
C\cong\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{-1,-1})\subseteq G
on
D
is known as the
core action

[
5
]
. It is a vector bundle action, given in terms of a linearization by

c_{1}\cdot(a,b,c)=(a,b,c+c_{1}).

The other non-zero components of
\mathfrak{g}
are

\mathfrak{g}^{-1,0}=\widehat{A},\ \ \ \mathfrak{g}^{0,-1}=\widehat{B};

these are the so-called
fat bundles
of Gracia-Saz and Mehta
[
10
]
.
The sections of
\widehat{A}
are realized as vector fields on
D
that are
invariant in the vertical direction and constant in the horizontal direction;
the map
\mathfrak{g}^{-1,0}\to D_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1,0}
becomes a bundle map
\widehat{A}\to A
, which is realized on the level of sections by restrictions of vector fields to
A\subseteq M
. The kernel of this bundle map is

(\mathfrak{g}^{+})^{-1,0}=\mathcal{P}(D)^{0,1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{-1,-1}=B^{*}\otimes C,

realized on the level of sections by products of core vector fields with linear functions on
B
(pulled back by the horizontal projection
D\to B
). We obtain the short exact sequence (see
[
11
, Section 4]
)

(26)

0_{M}\to B^{*}\otimes C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{A}\longrightarrow A\to 0_{M}.

A similar discussion applies to
\widehat{B}
, leading to an exact sequence

(27)

0_{M}\to A^{*}\otimes C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{B}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{B}\longrightarrow B\to 0_{M}.

For degree reasons, both
\mathfrak{g}^{-1,0},\mathfrak{g}^{0,-1}
are abelian Lie subalgebra bundles of
\mathfrak{g}
. The Lie bracket between these two summands
defines a bilinear pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\widehat{A}\times_{M}\widehat{B}\to C,

introduced in the work of Flari-Mackenzie
[
5
]
under the name of
warps
(our notation follows
[
20
]
).
Hence,

\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{A}\oplus\widehat{B}\oplus C

is a bundle of Heisenberg Lie algebras for this bilinear form. The group bundle
G\to M
is equal to
\mathfrak{g}\to M
as a fiber bundle,
with the group structure given by

(\widehat{a}_{1},\widehat{b}_{1},c_{1})\cdot(\widehat{a}_{2},\widehat{b}_{2},c_{2})=\big(\widehat{a}_{1}+\widehat{a}_{2},\widehat{b}_{1}+\widehat{b}_{1},c_{1}+c_{2}+\langle\widehat{a}_{1},\widehat{b}_{2}\rangle_{C}-\langle\widehat{a}_{2},\widehat{b}_{1}\rangle_{C}\big)

for
\widehat{a}_{i}\in\widehat{A},\ \widehat{b}_{i}\in\widehat{B},\ c_{i}\in C
(all with the same base point).
For an explicit description of the action
G\times_{M}D\to D
, choose a linearization
D_{\operatorname{lin}}\cong D
, thereby identifying

\widehat{A}\cong A\oplus(B^{*}\otimes C),\ \ \widehat{B}\cong B\oplus(A^{*}\otimes C).

Then the fiberwise group actions of the three summands read as:

\displaystyle(a_{1},\omega)\cdot(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=(a+a_{1},b,c+\omega(b)),

\displaystyle(b_{1},\nu)\cdot(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=(a,b+b_{1},c+\nu(a)),

\displaystyle c_{1}\cdot(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=(a,b,c+c_{1}).

Here
a,a_{1}\in A,\ b,b_{1}\in B,\ c,c_{1}\in C
and
\omega\in B^{*}\otimes C=\operatorname{Hom}(B,C),\ \nu\in A^{*}\otimes C=\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)
, all over the same base point in
M
.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that the
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\cdot)
-action on
G
makes the Lie group bundle
G\to M
into a double vector bundle, with
side bundles
\widehat{A},\widehat{B}
and core
C
. The original double vector bundle
D
is a quotient

D=G/G^{+}
.

4.3.
Quotients

By the general theory (see (
22
)), quotients of a double vector bundle
D\to M
are classified by graded Lie subalgebra bundles
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

\mathcal{P}(D)^{+}\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathcal{P}(D)^{+}\mathfrak{g}
. This boils down to the following:

Theorem 4.2
.

Quotients of a double vector bundle
D\to M
are classified by bigraded Lie subalgebra bundles

\mathfrak{h}=\widehat{A}^{\prime}\oplus\widehat{B}^{\prime}\oplus C^{\prime}

of
\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{A}\oplus\widehat{B}\oplus C
,
such that
\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})=\widehat{A}^{\prime}\cap\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C)
, and similarly

\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{B}}(A^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})=\widehat{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(A^{*}\otimes C)
.
In this case,

D^{\prime}=H\cdot M,\ \ D^{\prime\prime}=D/H

where
H=\exp(\mathfrak{h})
is the subgroup of
G
exponentiating
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

The condition
\mathcal{P}(D)^{+}\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathcal{P}(D)^{+}\mathfrak{g}
is non-trivial only in bidegrees
(-1,0)
and
(0,-1)
.
In bidegree
(-1,0)
, it reduces to

\mathcal{P}(D)^{0,1}\mathfrak{h}^{-1,-1}=\mathfrak{h}^{-1,0}\cap\mathcal{P}(D)^{0,1}\mathfrak{g}^{-1,-1}.

But
\mathcal{P}(D)^{0,1}=B^{*},\ \mathfrak{h}^{-1,-1}=C^{\prime},\ \mathfrak{g}^{-1,-1}=C,\ \mathfrak{h}^{-1,0}=\widehat{A}^{\prime}
, so the condition says

\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})=\widehat{A}^{\prime}\cap\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C),

which is just the same as
\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})\subseteq\widehat{A}^{\prime}
. Similarly, for the condition in bidegree

(0,-1)
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that a bigraded subbundle
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
satisfying these conditions
is automatically closed under brackets.

The condition can be interpreted in several other ways.
For example, given any vector subbundle
C^{\prime}\subseteq C

we may consider the Lie subalgebra bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{C^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
whose sections are the vector fields tangent to
C^{\prime}
. This subbundle is

\mathfrak{g}_{C^{\prime}}=\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})\oplus\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{B}}(A^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})\oplus C^{\prime}.

Comparing with
\mathfrak{h}=\widehat{A}^{\prime}\oplus\widehat{B}^{\prime}\oplus C^{\prime}
, we see:

Proposition 4.4
.

A bigraded subbundle
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the conditions from Theorem
4.2
if and only if

\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{C}=\ \mathfrak{g}_{C^{\prime}}

for some
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
.

4.4.
Quotient in stages

According to Theorem
3.5
, the quotient
D^{\prime\prime}
of
D
may be obtained as an iterated quotient by vector bundle actions. Write
\mathfrak{h}=\widehat{A}^{\prime}\oplus\widehat{B}^{\prime}\oplus C^{\prime},\ H=\exp\mathfrak{h}
as before.

Taking a quotient by the action of

\exp\mathfrak{h}^{-1,-1}\cong\mathfrak{h}^{-1,-1}=C^{\prime}
, one obtains a double vector bundle
D/C^{\prime}
with side bundles
A,B
and core
C^{\prime\prime}=C/C^{\prime}
. The action of
H
descends to an action of
H/\exp\mathfrak{h}^{-1,-1}\cong\widehat{A}^{\prime}\oplus\widehat{B}^{\prime}
(a vector bundle seen as an abelian group bundle). Since
\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})\subseteq\widehat{A}^{\prime}
and

\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{B}}(A^{*}\otimes C^{\prime})\subseteq\widehat{B}^{\prime}
act trivially on the quotient, this is really an action of

A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
.
In conclusion, the quotient
D^{\prime\prime}=D/H
may be written as

D^{\prime\prime}=D/C^{\prime}/(A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}).

We stress that the action of
A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}
on the quotient
D/C^{\prime}
is not canonically determined by
D^{\prime}
alone – this action is encoded by the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.

4.5.
Quotients by wide subbundles

One simple instance of quotients of double vector bundles is
the following. Suppose

and core

(so that

\textstyle{{D_{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Remark 4.5
.

The kernel of the quotient map

is

, seen as a double vector bundle whose horizontal side bundle is the zero bundle. Again, knowledge of the kernel does not suffice to construct the quotient. The subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}^{-1,-1}=C_{1},\ \ \mathfrak{h}^{-1,0}=\mathsf{i}_{\widehat{A}}(B^{*}\otimes C_{1}),\ \ \mathfrak{h}^{0,-1}=\widehat{B_{1}}

where

In a similar way, one may define horizontal quotients

Suppose now that

is vertically wide, and

and the cores by

\textstyle{{D_{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{{D_{2}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

D_{2}/\!_{v}\,(D_{1}\cap D_{2})\subseteq D/\!_{v}\,D_{1},

Theorem 4.6
.

Let

be a double vector bundle, with side bundles

and core

(a)

Every quotient

Proof.

Given a quotient

with kernel

Let

5.
Application: double normal bundles

5.1.
Double tangent bundle

An important example of a double vector bundle is the
double tangent bundle

\textstyle{{TTM}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{TM\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{TM\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M}

As stated in Example
2.3
, this
may be defined as

TTM=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(M),\mathbb{A}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{1}),

where
\mathbb{A}_{1}
is the commutative unital algebra with generator
\epsilon
and relation
\epsilon^{2}=0
.
Both side bundles
A,B
are equal to
TM
, and the core
C
is yet another copy of
TM
. In terms of the identification
TM=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(C^{\infty}(M),\mathbb{A}_{1})
, the inclusion maps for these three subbundles correspond to the algebra morphisms
\mathbb{A}_{1}\to\mathbb{A}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{1}
taking the
generator
\epsilon

\epsilon\otimes 1
,
1\otimes\epsilon
, and
\epsilon\otimes\epsilon

The bigraded Lie algebra bundle
\mathcal{V}(TTM)^{<0}
has components in bidegrees
(-1,0),(0,-1),(-1,-1)
. It
admits a very concrete description in terms of lifts of vector fields. Recall that every vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
has two kinds of lifts to the tangent bundle, the
vertical lift
and the

X_{v}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TM)^{-1},\ \ X_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TM)^{0}.

The vertical lift

\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(V)^{-1}\cong\Gamma(V)
for vector bundles

to fiberwise constant vertical vector fields.
The tangent lift
X_{t}
is the vector field
whose local flow is obtained by applying the tangent lift to the local flow of
X

This vector field is tangent to the zero section, with

X_{t}|_{M}=X

f_{v}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TM)^{0},\ \ f_{t}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TM)^{1}.

The vertical lift
f_{v}
is simply the pullback, and the tangent lift
f_{t}
is the exterior differential regarded as a function on
TM

(fX)_{t}=f_{t}X_{v}+f_{v}X_{t},\ [X,Y]_{v}=[X_{t},Y_{v}]=[X_{v},Y_{t}],\ (Xf)_{t}=X_{t}f_{t}.

For a double tangent bundle

f_{tt}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{0,0},\ \ f_{tv}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{0,1},\ \ f_{vt}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{1,0},\ \ f_{vv}\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{1,1},

and also
four kinds of lifts of vector fields

X_{tt}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{0,0},\ \ X_{tv}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{0,-1},\ \ X_{vt}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{-1,0},\ \ X_{vv}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(TTM)^{-1,-1}.

Let us write
A,B\cong TM
for the horizontal and vertical side bundles of

for the core. The lifts

, respectively. We may describe
\nu(M,N_{1},N_{2})
as the vertical quotient by

D_{1}
followed by the horizontal quotient by
D_{2}/\!_{v}\,(D_{1}\cap D_{2})
; according to Proposition
4.6
this is canonically isomorphic to the horizontal quotient by
D_{2}
followed by the vertical quotient by
D_{1}/\!_{h}\,(D_{1}\cap D_{2})
.

6.
Example: Weighted normal bundles for order
2
weightings

Let
M
be a manifold. In
[
14
]
, we developed a theory of

weightings along submanifolds
N\subseteq M
, a notion introduced in lecture notes of Melrose
[
21
]
under the name of quasi-homogeneous structure. A weighting is described in terms of a suitable filtration

C^{\infty}(M)=C^{\infty}(M)_{(0)}\supseteq C^{\infty}(M)_{(1)}\supseteq\cdots

of the algebra of smooth functions on
M
. Roughly, the filtration amounts to an assignment of weights to coordinate functions, making sense of a weighted order of vanishing along
N
. An upper bound for the weights is called an
order of the weighting
. Weightings determine a filtration

0_{N}=F_{0}\subseteq F_{-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{-r}=\nu(M,N)

of
the normal bundle
\nu(M,N)
, and a main result in
[
14
]
is the construction of a
weighted normal bundle

\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)\to N,

which is a graded bundle over
N
with linear approximation

\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)_{\operatorname{lin}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}F_{-i}/F_{-i+1}.

The weighted normal bundle admits an algebraic description as
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{gr}(C^{\infty}(M)),\mathbb{R})
, where

\operatorname{gr}(C^{\infty}(M))
is the associated graded algebra for the given filtration. But as shown in
[
14
]
, it may also be described as a subquotient of the
r
-th tangent bundle
T_{r}M
.

Let us describe the quotient procedure for the special case of weightings of order
r=2
. Here the notion of
weighting is simpler
[
19
]
, since it is fully determined by the subbundle
F_{-1}=F
. Letting
\tilde{F}\subseteq TM|_{N}
be the pre-image of
F
under the quotient map
TM|_{N}\to\nu(M,N)
, the filtration on functions is generated by letting
C^{\infty}(M)_{(1)}
be all functions vanishing along
N
, and
C^{\infty}(M)_{(2)}\subseteq C^{\infty}(M)_{(1)}
those functions whose differential vanishes on
\tilde{F}
.

Each
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
has three kinds of lifts

f^{(i)}\in C^{\infty}(T_{2}M)^{i},\ i=0,1,2.

In terms of the
definition
T_{2}M=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{alg}}(C^{\infty}(M),\mathbb{A}_{2})
, these are defined by

\mathsf{u}(f)=f^{(0)}(\mathsf{u})+f^{(1)}(\mathsf{u})\epsilon+f^{(2)}(\mathsf{u}){\epsilon^{2}},

for
\mathsf{u}\in T_{2}M
. Alternatively, thinking of elements of
T_{2}M
as 2-jets of curves
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
,

f^{(0)}(j^{2}(\gamma))=f(\gamma(0)),\ \ f^{(1)}(j^{2}(\gamma))=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}f(\gamma(t)),\ \ f^{(2)}(j^{2}(\gamma))=2\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}|_{t=0}f(\gamma(t)).

For vector fields
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
we have lifts
[
22
]

X^{(-i)}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\operatorname{pol}}(T_{2}M)^{-i},\ i=0,1,2,

given in terms of the action on functions by

X^{(-i)}f^{(j)}=(Xf)^{(j-i)}

where the right hand side is taken to be zero if
j-i<0
. The lifts
X^{(-2)}
define a vector bundle action of
TM
on
T_{2}M
.
Now let

Q=T_{2}M\big|_{\widetilde{F}}\subseteq T_{2}M

be the pre-image of
\tilde{F}
under the map
T_{2}M\to TM
. (Note that this is analogous to the definition of
D\subseteq TTM
in the previous section.) Then
Q\to N
is a graded subbundle of
T_{2}M\to M
, with linear approximation
Q_{\operatorname{lin}}=\tilde{F}\oplus TM|_{N}
.

Let us now consider the Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{V}(Q)^{<0}
.
The pairing of homogeneous polynomials of degree
1
on
E
with homogeneous vector fields of degree
-1
, followed by restriction to
M\subseteq E
, defines a non-degenerate pairing between
\mathcal{P}(E)^{1}
and
E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1}
; hence

\mathcal{P}(E)^{1}\cong(E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1})^{*}
.
From the exact sequence
(
13
), we see that

\mathfrak{g}^{-2}=E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-2},

while

\mathfrak{g}^{-1}
fits into an exact sequence

(32)

0_{M}\to(E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1})^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{-2}\to\mathfrak{g}^{-1}\to E_{\operatorname{lin}}^{-1}\to 0_{M}.

In terms of its space of sections,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}^{-2})

\displaystyle=\{X^{(-2)}|_{Q}\colon X\mbox{ arbitrary}\}

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\{\Big(X^{(-1)}+\sum_{\nu}f_{\nu}^{(1)}Y_{\nu}^{(-2)}\Big)|_{Q}\colon X|_{N}\in\Gamma(\tilde{F})\ f_{\nu},Y_{\nu}\mbox{ arbitrary}\}.

In particular, the exact sequence (
32
) splits on the level of sections.
Let
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie subalgebra bundle given on the level of sections by

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{-2})

\displaystyle=\{X^{(-2)}|_{Q}\colon X|_{N}\in\Gamma(\tilde{F})\}

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\{\Big(X^{(-1)}+\sum_{\nu}f_{\nu}^{(1)}Y_{\nu}^{(-2)}\Big)|_{Q}\colon X|_{N}\in\Gamma(TN)\ f_{\nu}\mbox{ arbitrary},\ Y_{\nu}|_{N}\in\Gamma(\tilde{F})\}.

The weighted normal bundle is the quotient
\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)=Q/H
, with kernel

H\cdot M=\widetilde{F}\cdot T_{2}N\subseteq T_{2}M
(using the vector bundle action of
\widetilde{F}\subseteq TM|_{N}
on

T_{2}M|_{N}
). We hence have the exact sequence

0_{N}\to\widetilde{F}\cdot T_{2}N\to T_{2}M\big|_{\widetilde{F}}\to\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)\to 0_{N}

for the weighted normal bundle, generalizing the defining sequence

0_{N}\to TN\to TM|_{N}\to\nu(M,N)\to 0_{N}

for the usual normal bundle. Observe also that

\nu_{\mathcal{W}}(M,N)_{\operatorname{lin}}=(T_{2}M\big|_{\widetilde{F}})_{\operatorname{lin}}/(\widetilde{F}\cdot T_{2}N)_{\operatorname{lin}}=\tilde{F}/TN\oplus TM|_{N}/\tilde{F}=F\oplus\nu(M,N)/F,

as expected.

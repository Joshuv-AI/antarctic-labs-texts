Krasner ( m , n ) (m,n) -hyperring of fractions
M. Anbarloei

Abstract
. The formation of rings of fractions and the associated process of localization are the most important technical tools in commutative algebra. Krasner ( m , n ) (m,n) -hyperrings are a generalization of ( m , n ) (m,n) -rings. Let R R be a commutative Krasner ( m , n ) (m,n) -hyperring. The aim of this research work is to introduce the concept of hyperring of fractions generated by R R and then investigate the basic properties such hyperrings.

In this section we recall some definitions and results about
n

A mapping

n
-ary hyperoperation, where

is the

(H,f)
, where

n
-ary hyperoperation defined on

n
-ary hypergroupoid.

We shall use the following abbreviated notation:

The sequence

will be denoted by

. For
j<i
,
x^{j}_{i}
is the empty symbol. In this convention

will be written as

y_{i+1}=...=y_{j}=y
the last expression will be written in the form
f(x_{1}^{i},y^{(j-i)},z^{n}_{j+1})
.
For non-empty subsets
A_{1},...,A_{n}
of
H
we define

f(A^{n}_{1})=f(A_{1},...,A_{n})=\bigcup\{f(x^{n}_{1})\ |\ x_{i}\in A_{i},i=1,...,n\}
.

An
n
-ary hyperoperation
f
is called associative if

f(x^{i-1}_{1},f(x_{i}^{n+i-1}),x^{2n-1}_{n+i})=f(x^{j-1}_{1},f(x_{j}^{n+j-1}),x_{n+j}^{2n-1}),

hold for every
1\leq i<j\leq n
and all

. An
n
-ary hypergroupoid with the

n
-ary hyperoperation is called an
n
-ary semihypergroup.

An
n
-ary hypergroupoid
(H,f)
in which the equation

has a solution
x_{i}\in H

for every
a_{1}^{i-1},a_{i+1}^{n},b\in H
and
1\leq i\leq n
, is called an
n
-ary quasihypergroup, when
(H,f)
is an
n
-ary
semihypergroup,
(H,f)
is called an
n
-ary hypergroup.

An

-ary hypergroupoid

is commutative if for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
, the group of all permutations of

(2)

G(\frac{a_{i}}{s_{i}},\frac{a_{j}}{s_{j}},\frac{1}{1}^{(n-1)})\in S^{-1}P
. Consequently,
S^{-1}P
is an
n
-ary 2-absorbing hyperideal of
S^{-1}R
.
∎

Let

(m,n)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
R
be a Krasner
(m,n)
-hyperring and
S
be an
n
-ary multiplicative subset of
R
with
1\in S
. Let
I
be a hyperideal of
R
such that
S\cap I=\varnothing
. Then
\bar{S}=\{f(s,I,0^{(m-2)})\ |\ s\in S\}
is an
n
-ary multiplicative subset of

Proof.

Let
f(s_{1},I,0^{(m-2)}),...,f(s_{n},I,0^{(m-2)})\in\bar{S}
, for
s_{1}^{n}\in S
. Then we have

g(f(s_{1},I,0^{(m-2)}),...,f(s_{n},I,0^{(m-2)}))=f(g(s_{1}^{n}),I,0^{m-2)})

. It implies that
g(f(s_{1},I,0^{(m-2)}),...,f(s_{n},I,0^{(m-2)}))\in\bar{S}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
R
be a Krasner
(m,n)
-hyperring and
S
be an
n
-ary multiplicative subset of
R
with
1\in S
. Let
I
be a hyperideal of
R
such that
S\cap I=\varnothing
. If
\bar{S}=\{f(s,I,0^{(m-2)})\ |\ s\in S\}
, then
\bar{S}^{-1}(R/I)\cong S^{-1}R/S^{-1}I
.

Proof.

Define mapping
k:R/I\longrightarrow S^{-1}R/S^{-1}I
as following:

k(f(r,I,0^{(m-2)})=F(\frac{r}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)})
.

It is easy to see the mapping is a homomorphism. Let
f(s,I,0^{(m-2)})\in\bar{S}
. Then
k(f(s,I,0^{(m-2)}))=F(\frac{s}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)}).
Since
F(\frac{1}{s},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)})\in S^{-1}R/S^{-1}I
, then we obtain

G(F(\frac{s}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)}),F(\frac{1}{s},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)}),F(\frac{1}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)})^{(n-2)}))

\hskip 28.45274pt=F(G(\frac{s}{1},\frac{1}{s},\frac{1}{1}^{(n-2)}),S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)})

\hskip 28.45274pt=F(\frac{1}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)})).

Assume that
k(f(r,I,0^{(m-2)})=S^{-1}I.
Then we have
F(\frac{r}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}^{(m-2)})=S^{-1}I
. It means
\frac{r}{1}\in S^{-1}I
. Then there exists
t\in S
such that
g(t,r,1^{(n-2)})\in I
. Clearly,
f(t,I,0^{(m-2)})\in\bar{S}
and we have

g(f(t,I,0^{(m-2)},f(r,I,0^{(m-2)}),f(1,I,0^{(m-2)})^{(n-2)})

\hskip 28.45274pt=f(g(t,r,1^{(n-2)}),I,0^{(m-2)})=I
.

Now, suppose that
F(\frac{r}{s},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R})\in S^{-1}R/S^{-1}I
. Thus we have

F(\frac{r}{s},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R})=G(F(\frac{r}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}),F(\frac{1}{s},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R}),F(\frac{1}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R})^{(n-2)})

\hskip 28.45274pt=G(k(f(r,I,0^{(m-2)}),k(f(r,I,0^{(m-2)}),F(\frac{1}{1},S^{-1}I,0_{S^{-1}R})^{(n-2)})
.

Hence, there exists an isomorphism from
\bar{S}^{-1}(R/I)
to
S^{-1}R/S^{-1}I
, by Corollary
3.7
. It means
\bar{S}^{-1}(R/I)\cong S^{-1}R/S^{-1}I
.
∎

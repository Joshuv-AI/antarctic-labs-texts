Another comment on claims of a transition to the ultimate regime
Erik Lindborg

Abstract




Zhu
et al.

Zhu
carried out DNS of 2D Rayleigh-Bénard convection (RBC) up to Rayleigh number
Ra=10^{14}
and reported evidence of a transition to the ‘ultimate regime’ of heat transfer predicted by Kraichnan
Kraichnan62
for 3D RBC, with Nusselt number dependence
Nu\sim Ra^{1/2}(\ln(Ra))^{-3/2}
, instead of the classical prediction
Nu\sim Ra^{1/3}

Malkus54
. A curve fit made by Zhu
et al.

Zhu
, indicated that
Nu\sim Ra^{0.35}
for the four data points at
Ra\in[10^{13},10^{14}]
, which they interpreted as evidence of a transition to the ultimate regime, despite the fact that the fit is much closer to the classical prediction. Doering
et al.

Doering
analysed the results of
Zhu
and concluded that they should be interpreted as evidence supporting the classical prediction. In a reply,
Zhu
et al.

Zhu2
reported that they had carried out two more simulations at
Ra>10^{14}
and presented a new curve fit showing
Nu\sim Ra^{0.357}
. They claimed that the new results constituted ‘overwhelming evidence’ of a transition and that they had ‘irrefutably settled the issue’.

Lindborg
Lindborg
estimated the convergence time scale for the mean kinetic energy in 2D simulations to more than hundred times larger than the time at which the highest Rayleigh number simulations of
Zhu
were ended, and questioned the conclusions of
Zhu
;
Zhu2
.
In a recent review paper, Lohse & Shishkina
Lohse
repeat the claim that Zhu
et al.

Zhu2
‘irrefutably settled the issue’, without referencing
Lindborg
. They acknowledge that the mean kinetic energy was not converged but claim, without presenting any evidence, that the Nusselt number was converged. The author of this comment seriously questions the validity of this statement. A plot of the time evolution of the Nusselt number from the four simulations at
Ra\in[10^{10},\,10^{11}]
(number 7,8,9 and 10, listed in the supplementary material of
Zhu
) sent to the author by Zhu and Lohse, shows that the Nusselt number in the simulation at
Ra=10^{11}
was far from being converged at the nondimensional time, t=250, at which the simulation at
Ra=10^{14}
was ended. Unfortunately, the plot cannot be shown, because Lohse and Zhu do not grant the author permission to publish it. In recent 2D simulations carried out by He
et al.

He
, which are not referenced by Lohse & Shishkina, the Nusselt number was calculated up to
Ra^{13}
. Up to
Ra^{11}
, the Nusselt number curve of
He
falls on top of the curve of
Zhu
, while it falls above at
Ra\in[10^{11},\,10^{13}]
, where it conforms to

Nu\sim Ra^{1/3}
. Most likely, the reason behind this difference is that the simulations by
He
were better converged than the simulations by
Zhu
;
Zhu2
.

Apart from the curve fit, Zhu
et al.

Zhu2
presented three other pieces of evidence in support of their claim. As one of these three pieces, they observed that the velocity profiles were logarithmic close to the centreline of the convection cell, seemingly in accordance with the assumption under which the prediction of the ‘ultimate regime’ was derived by Kraichnan, who assumed that the profiles conform to the classical boundary layer mean velocity profile

U(z)=u_{\tau}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\left(\frac{u_{\tau}z}{\nu} \right)+B\right)\,.

(1)

Here,
u_{\tau}
is the friction velocity,
\kappa
is the Kármán constant,
\nu
is the kinematic viscosity and
B
is a constant which is
Reynolds number independent
. However, Zhu
et al.
found that the velocity profiles conform to (
1
), with a constant
B
that is
increasing
quite strongly with Reynolds number. As pointed out by
Lindborg
, such an observation does not support the theory of
Kraichnan62
. On the contrary, it invalidates the theory. He
et al.

He
made the same observation and concluded that it was an indication that the asymptotic high
Ra
range had been reached, in which they observed
Nu\sim Ra^{1/3}
.
The two other pieces of evidence given by
Zhu2
are completely circumstantial and rest on a number of
ad hoc
assumptions regarding the dynamics before and after an imagined transition to the ultimate regime.

Based on a review of 2D simulations of RBC, Lindborg
Lindborg
concluded that the Nusselt number dependence, most likely, conforms to the classical prediction
Nu\sim Ra^{1/3}
in the limit of high
Ra
.
There is nothing in the results of
Zhu
;
Zhu2
that contradicts this conclusion. The recent results of
He
add further support to
Nu\sim Ra^{1/3}
.

Critical Sets of Solutions of Elliptic Equations in Periodic Homogenization
Fanghua Lin, Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
In this paper we study critical sets of solutions u ε u_{\varepsilon} of second-order elliptic equations in divergence form with rapidly oscillating and periodic coefficients. We show that the ( d − 2 ) (d-2) -dimensional Hausdorff measures of the critical sets are bounded uniformly with respect to the period ε \varepsilon , provided that doubling indices for solutions are bounded. The key step is an estimate of ”turning” for the projection of a non-constant solution u ε u_{\varepsilon} onto the subspace of spherical harmonics of order ℓ \ell , when the doubling index for u ε u_{\varepsilon} on a sphere ∂ B ⁡ ( 0 , r ) \partial B(0,r) is trapped between ℓ − δ \ell-\delta and ℓ + δ \ell+\delta , for r r between 1 1 and a minimal radius r ∗ ≥ C 0 ​ ε r^{*}\geq C_{0}\varepsilon . This estimate is proved by using harmonic approximation successively. With a suitable L 2 L^{2} renormalization as well as rescaling we are able to control the accumulated errors introduced by homogenization and projection. Our proof also gives uniform bounds for Minkowski contents of the critical sets. Keywords : Critical Set; Homogenization; Hausdorff Measure; Doubling Index. MR (2010) Subject Classification : 35J15, 35B27.

Introduction

3
Doubling Conditions

We introduce a doubling index for a continuous function
u
on a ball
B(x_{0},r)
, defined by

N^{*}(u,x_{0},r)=\log_{4}\frac{\fint_{\partial B(x_{0},r)}(u-u(x_{0}))^{2}}{\fint_{\partial B(x_{0},r/2)}(u-u(x_{0}))^{2}},

(3.1)

assuming
\|u-u(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\partial B(x_{0},t))}\neq 0
for
0<t\leq r
.
If
u
is a (non-constant) harmonic function in
B(x_{0},r)
, then

N^{*}(u,x_{0},r)=\frac{1}{\ln 2}\int_{r/2}^{r}\frac{N(u,x_{0},s)}{s}\,ds,

(3.2)

where
N(u,x_{0},r)
denotes Almgren’s frequency for
u-u(x_{0})
on
B(x_{0},r)
, given by

N(u,x_{0},r)=\frac{r\int_{B(x_{0},r)}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{\partial B(x_{0},r)}(u-u(x_{0}))^{2}}.

(3.3)

Since
N(u,x_{0},r)
is nondecreasing in
r
, it follows that

N(u,x_{0},r/2)\leq N^{*}(u,x_{0},r)\leq N(u,x_{0},r)

(3.4)

if
u
is harmonic in
B(x_{0},r)
.

The doubling index for
u_{\varepsilon}
, in general, may not be monotone in
r
.
The next theorem provides a substitute for the monotonicity, which plays a key role in the study of geometric properties of
solutions of elliptic equations.
We point out that a similar result was proved by the present authors in
[
18
]
, using averages on balls instead of spheres.
The result on the doubling condition in
[
18
]
was the first one for elliptic operators with periodic coefficients.
Subsequent work on doubling inequalities and the closely related three-ball lemma, which focus on the explicit
dependence on
\ell
of doubling constants, may be found in
[
14
,
15
,
1
]
.

Define

\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\Gamma,M)=\Big\{A=A(y):\ A\text{ satisfies \eqref{ellipticity}, \eqref{periodicity}, \eqref{smoothness}, and }\widehat{A}+(\widehat{A})^{T}=2I\Big\}.

(3.5)

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix

.

Note that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{\partial B(0,1)}(v-v(0))^{2}\right)^{1/2}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

Throughout this section we assume

For a function

to denote the homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is
the projection of

onto the subspace of spherical harmonics of order

That is,
P_{k}(f)(r\omega)=r^{k}P_{k}(f)(\omega)
for

and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

P_{k}(f)(\omega)
is the projection of
f
onto the subspace of
spherical harmonics of order
k
.

If

is defined in
B(0,r_{0})
and

, we will use the notation
f(r,\omega)=f(r\omega)
.
Thus, for a fixed

P_{k}(f(r,\cdot))=P_{k}(g)
and
\|f(r,\cdot)\|=\|g\|
, where

and
\|\cdot\|
denotes the norm in

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(B(0,1))
be a non-constant harmonic function in
B(0,1)
.
Assume that
u(0)=0
and

\ell-(1/32)\leq N(u,0,1/2)\leq N(u,0,1)\leq\ell+(1/32)

(4.1)

for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let

\eta=N(u,0,1)-N(u,0,1/2).

Then

a^{2}_{\ell}\geq(1-3\eta)\|u\|^{2},

(4.2)

\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}a_{j}^{2}2^{-2j}\leq 2\eta 2^{-2\ell}\|u\|^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{\infty}a_{j}^{2}\leq 2\eta\|u\|^{2},

(4.3)

where
u(\omega)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}\psi_{j}(\omega)
for
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,

\psi_{j}(\omega)
is a spherical harmonic of order
j
with

\|\psi_{j}\|=1
.

Proof.

A similar result was proved in
[
25
, Lemma 3.18]
.
We provide a more transparent proof here.
Without loss of generality we may assume
\|u\|=1
.
By Weiss type monotonicity formulas for harmonic functions (see
[
29
]
for
\kappa=2
and
[
7
]
for the general case),

\frac{d}{dr}\big\{W_{\kappa}(u,r)\big\}=\frac{2}{r^{d+2\kappa}}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}(x\cdot\nabla u-\kappa u)^{2}\geq 0,

(4.4)

where
\kappa>0
and

\displaystyle W_{\kappa}(u,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{d-2+2\kappa}}\int_{B(0,r)}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{r^{d-1+2\kappa}}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}u^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{N(u,0,r)-\kappa\right\}\cdot\frac{1}{r^{d-1+2\kappa}}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}u^{2}.

Let
\kappa=N(u,0,1/2)
.
By integrating both sides of (
4.4
) in
r
from
1/2
to
1
, we obtain

2\int_{B(0,1)\setminus B(0,1/2)}\frac{(r\partial_{r}u-\kappa u)^{2}}{|x|^{2\kappa}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{d}}=\big\{N(u,0,1)-\kappa\big\}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}u^{2}.

(4.5)

Now, write
u(\omega)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}\psi_{j}(\omega)
, where
\psi_{j}(\omega)
is a spherical harmonic of degree
j
with

\|\psi_{j}\|=1
. Then

\sum_{j}a_{j}^{2}=\|u\|^{2}=1.

Moreover, since
u(x)=u(r\omega)=\sum_{j}a_{j}r^{j}\psi_{j}(\omega)
, it follows from (
4.5
) by a direct computation that

\sum_{j}a_{j}^{2}|j-\kappa||1-2^{2(\kappa-j)}|=N(u,0,1)-N(u,0,1/2)=\eta.

(4.6)

Note that if
j\geq\ell+1
, then
j\geq\kappa+7/8
and

|1-2^{2(\kappa-j)}|\geq 7/8.

By (
4.6
) we see that

\sum_{j\geq\ell+1}a_{j}^{2}\leq(8/7)^{2}\sum_{j\geq\ell+1}a_{j}^{2}|j-\kappa||1-2^{(\kappa-j)}|\leq 2\eta.

(4.7)

In the case
j\leq\ell-1
, we have
j\leq\kappa-7/8
and

|2^{2(\kappa-j)}-1|\geq(7/8)2^{2(\kappa-j)}.

Again, by (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq\ell-1}a_{j}^{2}2^{-2j}

\displaystyle\leq(8/7)^{2}2^{-2\kappa}\sum_{j\leq\ell-1}a_{j}^{2}|j-k||2^{2(\kappa-j)}-1|

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq(8/7)^{2}2^{-2\ell}\eta 2^{-2(\kappa-\ell)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta 2^{-2\ell},

where we have used the fact
|\kappa-\ell|<(1/32)
.
In particular, this gives

\sum_{j\leq\ell-1}a_{j}^{2}\leq\eta.

(4.9)

Since
\sum_{j}a_{j}^{2}=1
, in view of (
4.7
) and (
4.9
), we obtain
a_{\ell}^{2}\geq 1-3\eta
.
The estimates in (
4.3
) are contained in (
4.7
) and (
4.8
).
∎

Lemma 4.4

The next lemma is elementary.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
and
\|f\|,\|g\|\neq 0
.
Suppose

\|f-g\|\leq\delta\|f\|

for some
\delta\in(0,1]
.
Then

\Big\|\frac{f}{\|f\|}-\frac{g}{\|g\|}\Big\|\leq\sqrt{2}\delta.

Proof.

Note that

\|f-g\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}-2\langle f,g\rangle+\|g\|^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\|f\|^{2}.

It follows that

2\langle f,g\rangle\geq(1-\delta^{2})\|f\|^{2}+\|g\|^{2}\geq 2\sqrt{(1-\delta^{2}}\|f\|\|g\|.

Hence.

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{f}{\|f\|}-\frac{g}{\|g\|}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2-\frac{2\langle f,g\rangle}{\|f\|\|g\|}

\displaystyle\leq 2-2\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\delta^{2}}{1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta^{2}.

∎

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma 3.22 in
[
25

We mention that analogous estimates have been obtained earlier in

for stationary harmonic maps,
using monotonicity formulas.

Without loss of generality we assume

By a rotation we may also assume

It follows that

\displaystyle u(x+x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{k}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}(\partial_{1})^{i}\psi_{k}(x)

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(u(x_{1}+\cdot))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{\ell+i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}(\partial_{1})^{i}\psi_{\ell+i}

\displaystyle=a_{\ell}\psi_{\ell}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{\ell+i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}(\partial_{1})^{i}\psi_{\ell+i}.

\displaystyle\|P_{\ell}(u(x_{1}+\cdot))-P_{\ell}(u)\|

\displaystyle\leq|t|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{\ell+i}|\frac{t^{i-1}}{i!}\|(\partial_{1})^{i}\psi_{\ell+i}\|

where we have used the fact that for a homogenous harmonic polynomial

of degree

Since

\displaystyle\|P_{\ell}(u(x_{1}+\cdot))-P_{\ell}(u)\|

\displaystyle\leq C|t|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{\ell+i}|\frac{(e\sqrt{2}|t|)^{i-1}}{\sqrt{i}}

if

, where we have used Lemma
4.1
for the last inequality.

To see (
5.3
), note that

\displaystyle P_{\ell-1}(u(x_{1}+\cdot))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{\ell-1+i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}(\partial_{1})^{i}\psi_{\ell-1+i}

\displaystyle=a_{\ell-1}\psi_{\ell-1}+a_{\ell}t\partial_{1}\psi_{\ell}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}a_{\ell-1+i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}(\partial_{1})^{i}\psi_{\ell-1+i}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|t||a_{\ell}|\|\partial_{1}\psi_{\ell}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\ell-1}(u(x_{1}+\cdot))\|+\|a_{\ell-1}\psi_{\ell-1}\|+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}|a_{\ell-1+i}|\frac{|t|^{i}}{i!}\|(\partial_{1})^{i}\psi_{\ell-1+i}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\eta}(\|u\|+\|u(x_{1}+\cdot)-u(x_{1})\|)

where we have used the observation

∎

0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}(L)
,

|\big\{x:\text{\rm dist}(x,\mathcal{C}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(0,1/4))<t\big\}|\leq Ct^{2}

(7.12)

for
0<t<1
, where
C
depends on
L
.

Proof.

Let
B(x_{i},r_{i}/4)
be a ball constructed in Lemma
7.1
with radius
r_{i}>6\varepsilon^{-1}_{0}\varepsilon
.
We may cover this ball by a finite number of smaller balls
\{B(z_{k},cr_{i})\}
with
z_{k}\in B(x_{i},r_{i})
.
This allows us to replace the third property in Lemma
7.1
by the following: For each ball
B(x_{i},r_{i})
, either
c_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon\leq r_{i}\leq 6\varepsilon^{-1}_{0}\varepsilon
or

6\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon<r_{i}<c
and

N^{*}(u_{\varepsilon},y,r_{i})\leq\ell-1+\delta_{0}\quad\text{ for any }y\in B(x_{i},r_{i})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(0,1/2).

(7.13)

Since
B(x_{i},c)\cap B(0,1/4)\neq\emptyset
implies
B(x_{i},c)\subset B(0,1/2)
, we may remove
B(0,1/2)
in (
7.13
).
This allows us to carry out an induction argument on
\ell
.

Indeed, fix
i
with
r_{i}>6\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon
and property (
7.13
), and let
\phi(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(x_{i}+rx)
.
Then

\text{div}(B(x/(\varepsilon/r_{i}))\nabla\phi)=0\quad\text{ in }B(0,2),

where
B(y)=A(y+(rx_{i})/\varepsilon)
.
Since
B\in\mathcal{A}(\lambda,\Gamma,M,\mu)
and
(\varepsilon/r_{i})<\varepsilon_{0}
,
we may apply Lemma
7.1
to the solution
\phi
to obtain a cover for

\mathcal{C}(\phi)\cap B(0,1/4)
. As a result, we obtain a collection of balls
\{B(x_{i,k},r_{i,k})\}
with the following properties:

\mathcal{C}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(x_{i},r_{i}/4)\subset\bigcup_{k}B(x_{i,k},r_{i,k}/4),

(7.14)

\sum_{k}r_{i,k}^{d-2}\leq Cr_{i}^{d-2},

(7.15)

and for each ball
B(x_{i,k},r_{i,k})
, either
c_{0}\varepsilon\leq r_{i,k}\leq 6\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon
, or
r_{i,k}>6\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon
and

N^{*}(u_{\varepsilon},y,r_{i,k})\leq\ell-2+\delta_{0}\quad\text{ for any }y\in B(x_{i,k},r_{i,k})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

(7.16)

We may continue the process
until we reach
\ell=1
. At each step, we keep those balls whose radii are between
c_{0}\varepsilon
and
6\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon

and decompose those whose radii are greater than
6\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon
.
By Theorem
3.5
, if the right-hand side of the inequality (
7.13
) is bounded by
3/2
, then

\mathcal{C}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(x_{i},r_{i}/4)=\emptyset.

As a result,
we have constructed a finite collection of balls
\{B(z_{i},s_{i})\}
with the properties that

\mathcal{C}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(0,1/4)\subset\bigcup B(z_{i},s_{i}/4),

(7.17)

\sum_{i}s_{i}^{d-2}\leq C,

(7.18)

and
c_{0}\varepsilon\leq s_{i}\leq 6\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(L)
.

We point out the argument above works equally well if we replace
\varepsilon
by any fixed
t\in(\varepsilon,c_{0})
.
More precisely, the same argument also gives a collection of balls
\{B(z_{i},s_{i})\}
with the properties (
7.17
),
(
7.18
), and
c_{0}t\leq s_{i}\leq C_{0}t
for all
i
.
To do this, in the place of (
7.5
), one simply sets

r_{t}^{*}(x)=\sup\big\{\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}t<s\leq 1/2:N^{*}(u_{\varepsilon},x,s)\leq\ell-\delta_{0}\big\}.

(7.19)

If no such
s
exists, i.e.,
N^{*}(u_{\varepsilon},x,s)>\ell-\delta_{0}
for all
\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}t<s\leq 1/2
, define
r_{t}^{*}(x)=\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}t
.
On the other hand, if
t\in(0,\varepsilon)
, we may use the local results in
[
25
]
on each ball
B(z_{i},s_{i})
with
c_{0}\varepsilon\leq s_{i}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon
,
constructed for the case
t=\varepsilon
.
This gives
\{B(z_{i,k},t)\}
with the properties that

\mathcal{C}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(z_{i},s_{i}/4)\subset\bigcup_{k}B(z_{i,k},t/4),

(7.20)

\sum_{k}t^{d-2}\leq Cs_{i}^{d-2}.

(7.21)

In summary, we have proved that for any
t\in(0,c)
, the set
\mathcal{C}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(0,1/4)
can be
covered by a collection of balls
\{B(z_{i},Ct)\}
, and the number of the balls is bounded by
Ct^{2-d}
, where
C
depends on
L
.
This gives the Minkowski estimate (
7.12
).
∎

We are now ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume
A=A(y)
satisfies the conditions (
1.4
), (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
).
Using the change of variables in Remark
2.3
, we may assume that
\widehat{A}+(\widehat{A})^{T}=2I
, and thus

A\in\mathcal{A}(\lambda,\Gamma,M,\mu)
. Note that if
v_{\varepsilon}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}(S^{-1}x)
, as in Remark
2.3
,
the doubling condition (
1.8
) is equivalent to

\fint_{B(0,2)}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq 4^{N}\fint_{B(0,1)}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(B(0,2))
be a non-constant solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
B(0,2)
.
Suppose that

\fint_{B(0,2)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq 4^{N_{0}}\fint_{B(0,1)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2},

(7.22)

for some
N_{0}>1
. It follows that

\int_{B(0,1)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\int_{B(0,2)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C\int_{B(0,1)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C\int_{\partial B(0,1)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2},

where
C=C(N_{0})
depends on
N_{0}
.
Thus,
N(u_{\varepsilon},0,1)\leq C(N_{0})
and
as a result, the case
\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(N_{0})
is covered by the results in
[
9
,
25
]
.
The constants may depend on
\varepsilon_{0}
, and
the periodicity condition is not needed.

To treat the case
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, we note that (
7.22
) implies that for any
0<r<1
,

\fint_{B(0,r)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C(N_{0})\fint_{B(0,r/2)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2},

(7.23)

by Theorem 1.2 in
[
18
]
.
It follows that

\fint_{\partial B(x,1)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C\fint_{B(0,2)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C\fint_{B(0,1/8)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C\int_{B(x,1/2)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C\fint_{\partial B(x,1/2)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2},

if
x\in B(0,1/4)
, where
C
depends on
N_{0}
.
It follows from Lemma
7.2
that

|\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{dist}(x,\mathcal{C}(u_{\varepsilon})\cap B(0,1/8))<t\big\}|\leq C(N_{0})t^{2}.

(7.24)

Finally, using harmonic approximation, one may show that

\fint_{B(x,r)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq C(N_{0})\fint_{B(x,r/2)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}

for any
x\in B(0,3/4)
and
0<r<r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is sufficiently small.
By a simple covering and scaling argument,
this allows us to replace
B(0,1/8)
in (
7.24
) by
B(0,1/2)
and completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

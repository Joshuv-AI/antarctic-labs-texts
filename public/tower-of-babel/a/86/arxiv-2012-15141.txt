The moduli space of quasistable spin curves Thanks: The second author was supported by CNPq-PQ, 301671/2019-2. The third author was supported by Capes.
Alex Abreu, Marco Pacini, and Danny Taboada

Abstract
. We study a compactification of the moduli space of theta characteristics, giving a modular interpretation of the geometric points and describing the boundary stratification. This space is different from the moduli space of spin curves. The modular description and the boundary stratification of the new compactification are encoded by a tropical moduli space. We show that this tropical moduli space is a refinement of the moduli space of spin tropical curves. We describe explicitly the induced decomposition of its cones.



MSC (2010): 14T05, 14H10, 14H40

Key words: spin curve, spin tropical curve, moduli spaces, refinement.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

The goal of this paper is the study of a compactification, denoted
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
, of the moduli space
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
of theta characteristics
on
n
-pointed smooth curves.
A distinguished compatification,
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
, of
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
over the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of
n
-pointed stable curves was introduced by Cornalba in
[
17
]
. The stack
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
is finite over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and parametrizes
spin curves
, which are certain pairs of nodal curve and invertible sheaves on it. The construction was later generalized in
[
9
]
for higher roots of any sheaf. There are at least two other different approaches to this compactification problem. The first uses torsion-free rank-1 sheaves on stable curves and is introduced by Jarvis (see
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
). The second involves the use of twisted curves (nodal curves with a stacky structure at the nodes) and is employed by Abramovich and Jarvis in
[
1
]
and by Chiodo in
[
15
]
.
All these compactifications of
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
are isomorphic to
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
.

We define
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
as the closure of
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
in the Esteves universal compactified Jacobian of quasistable torsion-free rank-1 sheaves on stable curves. We call
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
the moduli space of
quasistable spin curves
.
We give a modular description of the geometric points of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
and study its boundary stratification. The upshot is that
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
is a compactification of
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
which is not isomorphic to
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
. In a way, this is a natural reflection of the existence of two distinguished and different universal Jacobians: Caporaso universal Picard variety, constructed in
[
7
]
, and the Esteves universal compactified Jacobian, introduced by Melo in
[
24
]
bulding upon the work of Esteves
[
18
]
. These compactified Jacobians are different. For instance, using the terminology in
[
8
]
, the first one, in general, is not of Néron type, while the second one is always of Néron type. Caporaso compactified Jacobian contains
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
(at least set-theoretically), so it is natural to expect a difference between
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
.

We achieve our results by resorting to the tropical counterpart of the problem. In the last few years, tropical geometry became a powerful tool to encode moduli problems and describe the boundary stratifications of compactified moduli spaces. The tropical setting often helps in understanding which combinatorial data are relevant to describe the strata. The associated tropical moduli space is a generalized cone complex which can be regarded as a “dual” of the algebro-geometric space. Usually, we have a reverse-inclusion bijection between the strata of the moduli space and the cells of the cone complex switching dimension and codimension.

This phenomenon appears in the study of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, the moduli space of
n
-pointed stable curves, and of its tropical counterpart
M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
, the moduli space of
n
-pointed tropical curves. In fact, a deep connection between
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and
M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
was revealed in
[
5
]
, where Abramovich, Caporaso, and Payne showed that
\overline{M}_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
is identified with the Berkovich skeleton of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. In
[
11
]
, Caporaso, Melo, and the second author, did a similar analysis for the moduli space of spin curves
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
. It is worth mentioning that the new insight of the boundary stratification given by tropical geometry was recently used in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
to give strong results about the rational cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and the
S_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of the top weight cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.

We proceed in a similar way: we define the relevant tropical moduli space and we use it to describe the geometric points and the boundary stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
.

1.2.
Outline of the results

Let us give more details about our results. We start with the tropical setting.
In
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
two universal tropical Jacobians have been introduced. The first one, denoted
J^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
, parametrizes
(\mu,p_{0})
-quasistable divisors on tropical curves of genus
g
. The second one, denoted
P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
, parametrizes
\mu
-polystable divisors on tropical curves of genus
g
. In both cases,
\mu
denotes a universal polarization. The two universal tropical Jacobians are different. In fact, in Theorem
3.7
, we show that
J^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
is a refinement of
P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
, and we describe the induced decomposition of the cones of
P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
. This generalizes to the universal setting a similar result for the Jacobian of a tropical curve, proved in
[
4
, Proposition 3.7]
and
[
16
, Corollay 5.10]
.

Motivated by the algebro-geometric picture, we can consider the locus inside the tropical Jacobian parametrizing square roots of the canonical divisor. This leads to a notion of
tropical theta characteristics
of a tropical curve, first studied by Zharkov in
[
25
]
. A moduli space,
T_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
, of theta characteristics on
n
-pointed tropical curves of genus
g
, is constructed in
[
12
, Section 4]
, based on the existence of certain representatives of theta characteristics that are well-behaved under specializations.
In Proposition
5.18
we show that in the equivalence class of a theta characteristic there is exactly one
\mu
-polystable divisor, where
\mu
is the canonical polarization of degree
g-1
. It is possible to write explicitly this distinguished representative (see equation (
20
)). This gives rise to an explicit injection
T_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}\hookrightarrow P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
.

However
T^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
is not the tropical counterpart of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
of spin curves. In fact,
T^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
is connected, since all tropical theta characteristic admits a specialization to the unique theta characteristic on the tropical curve consisting of one vertex, while the stack
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
is not (it has two connected components, corresponding to the parity of the space of sections of a spin curve). The right tropical counterpart of
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
is the space
S^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
of spin tropical curves constructed in
[
11
]
, parametrizing tropical theta characteristics together with the datum of a sign function. We have a forgetful map
S^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}\rightarrow T^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
, and hence a chain of maps

S^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}\rightarrow T^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}\hookrightarrow P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}.

The idea is to pull-back the refinement
J^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}\rightarrow P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
of
P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
to
S^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
via the chain of maps above. We look for a generalized cone complex,
Q^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
, whose associated poset could encode the data of the induced refinement of
S^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
. This amounts to impose the right condition on the poset associated to
P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
. The last one is the poset of triples
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
where
\Gamma
is a stable graph of genus
g
with
n
legs,
\mathcal{E}
is a subset of the set of edges of
\Gamma
, and
D
is a
\mu
-polystable divisor on the graph
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
obtained from
\Gamma
by inserting a vertex in the interior of any edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
. The key condition is:

(1)

2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}},

where
\Div(\phi)
is the divisor associated to some acyclic flow
\phi
on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
and
K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
is the canonical divisor of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
. The flow
\phi
is uniquely determined if
D
is
\mu
-semistable and plays an important role for us. We refer to Theorem
4.7
for more properties of the flow
\phi
(for example,
|\phi(e)|\leq 1
for every oriented edge
e
of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
).
Using this terminology, the poset underlying
T^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
is given by the triples
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
, where
D
is
\mu
-polystable and satisfies equation (
1
). We can also reinterpret the poset underlying
S^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
simply adding a sign function to these triples. The poset associated to
Q^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
is formed by tuples
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)
where
D
is
\mu
-quasistable and satisfies equation (
1
), and
s
is a sign function. An important step in the construction of the generalized cone complex
Q^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
is the explicit description of the equations of its cones. These are obtained by intersecting the cone
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}_{\geq 0}
with the linear subspace:

\Lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}\colon\sum_{e\in\gamma}\phi(e)x_{e}=0

where
\gamma
runs over the oriented cycles of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
and
x_{e}
is the coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}
corresponding to an edge
e
of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}

(see equation (
11
)).
The following result is Theorem
6.9
.

Theorem A
.

We have a refinement
Q_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}\rightarrow S_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
of generalized cone complexes. There is an explicit description of the associated decomposition of the cones of
S_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
, and of their interiors.

Next, we come back to algebraic geometry. The moduli space,
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
, of theta characteristics on
n
-pointed smooth curves of genus
g
naturally sits inside
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}
, the Esteves universal compactified Jacobian. As we already mentioned, we define
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
as the closure of
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
in
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}
. For our purposes, it is convenient to interpret
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}
as the moduli space of pair
(X,L)
, where
X
is a quasistable curve and
L
is a
(\mu,p_{0})
-quasistable invertible sheaf on
X
with degree
-1
on exceptional components (see Proposition
7.1
). The tropical setting suggests a modular interpretation of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
. In fact, given a triple
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
satisfying equation (
1
),
we can consider the subgraph
P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
over which
\phi
is zero. If
X
is a nodal curve with dual graph
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
, we can consider the subcurves of
X
corresponding to the connected component of
P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
, which we call the
\phi
-subcurves
of
X
. The following result is Theorem
7.10
.

Theorem B
.

Let
(X,L)
be a pair parametrized by a geometric point of
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The point parametrizing
(X,L)
is contained in
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
.

(2)

The combinatorial type
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
of
(X,L)
satisfies equation (
1
) and
for every
\phi
-subcurve
Z
of
X
with corresponding subgraph
P_{Z}
of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
, we have an isomorphism:

\Big(L|_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}\Big(-\sum p_{e}\Big)\Big)^{\otimes 2}\cong\omega_{Z}

where
p_{e}
runs over the nodes of
X
corresponding to the oriented edges
e
of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
with target contained in
P_{Z}
and source not contained in
P_{Z}
, such that
\phi(e)=-1
.

Finally we study the boundary stratification of the open immersion
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
. We define
\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}
as the locus of points of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
whose combinatorial type is a fixed tuple
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)
.

Theorem C
.

The following decomposition:

\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}=\coprod_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}.

is a stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
indexed by the poset underlying
Q^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
.

We have an explicit formula for the codimension of the strata
\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}
. This allows us to show that the natural forgetful map
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is finite exactly over the locus of tree-like curves. In particular,
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
is not isomorphic to
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
. We believe that Theorem A should help in the construction of an explicit blowup
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
, locally given by a toric morphism induced by the decomposition of cones associated to the refinement
Q^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}\rightarrow S^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
. We plan to investigate this problem in a different paper. We can also give a combinatorial characterization of the codimension 1 strata
\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}
: there are more boundary divisors in
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
that in
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
. Finally, we have a reverse-inclusion bijection between the strata of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
and the cells of
Q_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
switching dimension and codimension, proving, as expected, an interesting “duality” between
Q^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
. We refer to Theorem
7.17
for the precise statement of the results.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Graphs

We denote by
\Gamma
a weighted graph (or, simply, a
graph
) with legs, where
V(\Gamma)
and
E(\Gamma)
are the sets of vertices and edges,
w\colon V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is the weight function, and
L_{\Gamma}(v)
(or, simply,
L(v)
) is the number of legs incident to
v\in V(\Gamma)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. For every subset
\mathcal{E}\subset E(\Gamma)
, we let
\deg_{\mathcal{E}}(v)
be the number of edges in
\mathcal{E}
incident to
v
(with loops counting twice). We set
\deg(v)=\deg_{E(\Gamma)}(v)
.
Given subsets
V,W\subset V(\Gamma)
, we let
E(V,W)\subset E(\Gamma)
be the set of edges with one end-vertex in
V
and the other in
W
. If
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
are subgraphs of
\Gamma
, we abuse notation writing
E(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2})=E(V(\Gamma_{1}),V(\Gamma_{2}))
. We set
E(V)=E(V,V)
.
For every subset
V\subset V(\Gamma)
, we let
\Gamma(V)
be the subgraph of
\Gamma
whose set of vertices is
V(\Gamma)
and with set of edges
E(V)
. For
V\subset V(\Gamma)
we set
V^{c}=V(\Gamma)\setminus V
.

We denote by
\textnormal{Aut}(\Gamma)
be the group of automorphism of
\Gamma
. We also let
b_{0}(\Gamma)
be the number of connected components of
\Gamma
and we set
b_{1}(\Gamma)=\#E(\Gamma)-\#V(\Gamma)+b_{0}(\Gamma)
. The
genus
of
\Gamma
is defined as
g(\Gamma)=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}w(v)+b_{1}(\Gamma)
. We say that
\Gamma
is
stable

if
2w(v)-2+\deg_{\Gamma}(v)+L_{\Gamma}(v)>0
for every
v\in V(\Gamma)
.
Given a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset E(\Gamma)
, we let
\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}
be the graph obtained by removing
\mathcal{E}
, so that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}
have the same vertices and the same legs. We say that
\mathcal{E}
is
non-disconnecting
if
\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}
is connected. We denote by
\Gamma_{\mathcal{E},\leg}
the graph obtained by replacing every edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
with end-vertices
v_{1},v_{2}
by a pair of legs, the first incident to
v_{1}
and the second to
v_{2}
.
A
cycle
of
\Gamma
is a connected subgraph of
\Gamma
all of whose vertices
v
satisfy
\deg_{\Gamma}(v)=2
. We will often identify a cycle and its set of edges. A
cyclic subgraph
of
\Gamma
is a (possibly empty) union of cycles of
\Gamma
with no common edges.

We define the set
\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)=E_{1}\sqcup E_{2}
, whose elements are called
oriented edges
, where
E_{i}=E(\Gamma)
. We consider the function
u\colon\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\rightarrow E(\Gamma)
such that
u(e)=e
, and the functions
s,t\colon\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\rightarrow V(\Gamma)
(the
source and target
), such that

(1)

\{s(e),t(e)\}
is the set of vertices incident to
u(e)
,
\forall\;e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
;

(2)

s(e_{1})=t(e_{2})
for oriented edges
e_{1}\in E_{1}
,
e_{2}\in E_{2}
such that
u(e_{1})=u(e_{2})\in E(\Gamma)
.

For every subset
\mathcal{E}\subset E(\Gamma)
, we let
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}=u^{-1}(\mathcal{E})\subset\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
.

An
oriented cycle
of
\Gamma
is a sequence of oriented edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
such that
t(e_{n})=s(e_{1})
and
t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for every
i=1,\dots,n-1
and whose associated set of edges forms a cycle of
\Gamma
.

A flow on a graph
\Gamma
is a function
\phi\colon\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi(e_{1})+\phi(e_{2})=0
for different
e_{1},e_{2}\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
such that
u(e_{1})=u(e_{2})\in E(\Gamma)
. Notice that
\phi(e_{1})=0
if and only if
\phi(e_{2})=0
, so, sometimes, we will abuse notation writing
\phi(e)=0
, where
e=u(e_{1})=u(e_{2})\in E(\Gamma)
. If
v\colon E(\Gamma)\rightarrow\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
is a section of
u
, we will often define
\phi
just defining it over the image of
v
.

Given a flow
\phi
on a graph
\Gamma
and vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(\Gamma)
, a
\phi
-path
from
v_{1}
to
v_{2}
is a sequence
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
such that
s(e_{1})=v_{1}
,
t(e_{n})=v_{2}
,
t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
and
\phi(e_{i})\geq 0
for
i=1,\dots,n
. A
\phi
-path is called
positive
(
null
) if
\phi(e_{i})>0
(if
\phi(e_{i})=0
) for every
i
. A
path
is a
\phi
-path for
\phi=0
. A
\phi
-cycle
is a
\phi
-path whose edges form a cycle of
\Gamma
; it is called
positive
(
null
) if it is so as a
\phi
-path. A
\phi
-loop
is a
\phi
-cycle consisting of a single oriented edge.
A flow
\phi
is
acyclic
if there are no non-null
\phi
-cycles in
\Gamma
. If
\phi
is acyclic and
\phi(e)\neq 0
for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
, then there is at least a
source

v
and a
sink

v^{\prime}
of
\phi
, that is,
\phi(e)>0
if
s(e)=v
and
\phi(e)>0
if
t(e)=v^{\prime}
.

A
\phi
-cut
of
\Gamma
is a subset
E(W_{1},W_{2})\subset E(\Gamma)
for some disjoint subsets
W_{1},W_{2}\subset V(\Gamma)
such that for every
v_{1}\in W_{1}
and
v_{2}\in W_{2}
there is no
\phi
-path from
v_{1}
to
v_{2}
and from
v_{2}
to
v_{1}
whose edges are contained in
\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)\setminus\overrightarrow{E}(W_{1},W_{2})
.

We define the graph
\Gamma/\mathcal{E}
obtained by the contraction of the edges of
\mathcal{E}
. Notice that there is a natural surjection
V(\Gamma)\rightarrow V(\Gamma/\mathcal{E})
and an identification
E(\Gamma/\mathcal{E})=E(\Gamma)\setminus\mathcal{E}
.
A graph
\Gamma

specializes
to a graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
, and we write
\iota\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
, if there is an isomorphism
\Gamma^{\prime}\cong\Gamma/\mathcal{E}
. A specialization
\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
is equipped with a surjective map
\iota\colon V(\Gamma)\rightarrow V(\Gamma^{\prime})
and an injective map
E(\Gamma^{\prime})\rightarrow E(\Gamma)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph.
A
divisor

D
on
\Gamma
is a function
D\colon V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. We often denote a divisor by
D=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}D(v)v
. The degree of
D
is the integer
\deg D=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}D(v)
. We denote by
\Div(\Gamma)
the group of divisors of
\Gamma
. A
polarization

\mu
on
\Gamma
is a function
\mu\colon V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\mu(v)
is an integer, called the
degree
of the polarization. Given a flow
\phi
on
\Gamma
, the
divisor of
\phi
is defined as
\Div(\phi)=\sum_{e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)}\phi(e)t(e)
.
For a vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, we define
the acyclic flow
\phi_{v}
such that for
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
we have

\phi_{v}(e)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\text{ if }s(e)=v\neq t(e)\\
-1&\text{ if }t(e)=v\neq s(e)\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\end{cases}

We set
\Div(v):=\Div(\phi_{v})
. A
principal divisor
on
\Gamma
is a divisor of type
\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}a_{v}\Div(v)
, for
a_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Of course, this divisor is equal to
\Div(\phi)
, where
\phi
is the acyclic flow
\phi=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}a_{v}\phi_{v}
.

Given a specialization
\iota\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
of graphs, a divisor
D
and a polarization
\mu
on
\Gamma
, we define the divisor and polarization
\iota_{*}(D)
and
\iota_{*}(\mu)
on
\Gamma^{\prime}
taking every
v^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma^{\prime})
to

\iota_{*}(D)(v^{\prime})=\sum_{v\in\iota^{-1}(v^{\prime})}D(v)\;\text{ and }\;\iota_{*}(\mu)(v^{\prime})=\sum_{v\in\iota^{-1}(v^{\prime})}\mu(v).

Given a flow
\phi
on
\Gamma
,
we let
\iota_{*}\phi
be the flow on
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
\iota_{*}\phi(e)=\phi(e)
,
\forall\;e\in E(\Gamma^{\prime})\subset E(\Gamma)
.

Let
D
and
\mu
be a divisor and a polarization on
\Gamma
of degree
d
. For every subset
W\subset V(\Gamma)
, we set
\delta_{\Gamma,W}:=\#E(W,W^{c})
(or, simply,
\delta_{W}
) and
\mu(W)=\sum_{v\in W}\mu(v)
, and we define:

\beta_{D}(W):=\deg D|_{W}-\mu(W)+\frac{\delta_{\Gamma,W}}{2}.

Proposition 2.1

Let

be a specialization of graphs. Let

For every subset

The result follows.

2.2.
Tropical curves

An
n
-pointed tropical curve is a pair
X=(\Gamma,\ell)
where
\Gamma
is a graph with
n
legs and
\ell\colon E(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a function (the
length function
). The graph
\Gamma
is called a
model
of
X
. We will consider tropical curves up to isometries. Given a point
p\in X
, we define
\deg_{X}(p)=\deg_{\Gamma}(p)
,
w_{X}(p)=w_{\Gamma}(p)
and
L_{X}(p)=L_{\Gamma}(p)
if
p\in V(\Gamma)
, otherwise we set
\deg_{X}(p)=2
,
w_{X}(p)=0
and
L_{X}(p)=0
. The
genus
of
X
is
g=\sum_{v\in X}w_{X}(v)+b_{1}(\Gamma)
. We say that
X
is
stable
if, for every
p\in X
such that
\deg_{X}(p)\leq 1
, we have
\deg_{X}(p)+2w_{X}(p)+L_{X}(p)\geq 3
.

Let
X
be a tropical curve. A
divisor
on
X
is a function
\mathcal{D}\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathcal{D}(p)\neq 0
only for a finite set of points
p\in X
(called the
support
of
\mathcal{D}
and written
\supp(\mathcal{D})
). We often denote a divisor by
\mathcal{D}=\sum_{p\in X}\mathcal{D}(p)p
. The
degree
of
\mathcal{D}
is the integer
\sum_{p\in X}\mathcal{D}(v)
. The
canonical divisor
of
X
is the divisor
K_{X}=\sum_{p\in X}(w_{X}(p)-2+\deg_{X}(p))p
. The degree of
K_{X}
is
2g-2
. We will implicitly consider a fixed model
\Gamma
of
X
such that the support of
K_{X}
is contained in
V(\Gamma)
.

A divisor
\mathcal{D}
on
X
is
\Gamma
-unitary
(or simply
unitary
), if
for every
e\in E(\Gamma)
we have
\mathcal{D}(p)=0
for each point
p\in e^{\circ}
, except for at most one point
p_{0}\in e^{\circ}
for which
\mathcal{D}(p_{0})=-1
. Given a
\Gamma
-unitary divisor
\mathcal{D}
on
X
, the
combinatorial type
of
\mathcal{D}
is the pseudo-divisor
(\mathcal{E},D)
on
\Gamma
such that

\mathcal{E}=\{e\in E(\Gamma)|D(p)=-1,\text{ for some }p\in e^{\circ}\},

while
D(v)=\mathcal{D}(v)
, for every
v\in V(\Gamma)
, and
D(v)=-1
,
\forall
exceptional vertex
v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
.

One can introduce stability conditions on divisors on a tropical curve (see
[
4
, Section 5]
and
[
4
, Section 5]
). It is easy to see that a quasistable or polystable divisor is always unitary. In this paper we
use the equivalent definition that a unitary divisor
\mathcal{D}
on
X
is
\mu
-stable
,
\mu
-semistable
,
(p_{0},\mu)
-quasistable
for a point
p_{0}\in V(\Gamma)
,
\mu
-polystable
if so is its combinatorial type
(\mathcal{E},D)
, respectively (see
[
2
, Proposition 5.3]
and
[
4
, Definition 5.8]
).

A
rational function
on
X
is a piecewise linear continuous function
f\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with integer slopes. Given a point
p\in X
, we let
\ord_{p}(f)
be the sum of the slopes of
f
outgoing
p
.
A
principal divisor
on
X
is a divisor
\Div(f)=\sum_{p\in X}\ord_{p}(f)p
, for some rational function
f
on
X
. Given divisors
D
and
D^{\prime}
on
X
, we say that
D
is equivalent to
D^{\prime}
, written
D\sim D^{\prime}
, if
D-D^{\prime}
is a principal divisor. Given a rational function
f
on
X
and a model
\Gamma
such that the support of
\Div(f)
is contained in
V(\Gamma)
, we define a flow
\phi_{f}
on
\Gamma
such that
\phi_{f}(e)
is the slope of
f
on
e
, for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
.

Remark 2.3
.

By
[
2
, Theorem 5.6]
and
[
4
, Theorem 5.9]
given a divisor
\mathcal{D}
on

there is a
(p_{0},\mu)
-quasistable divisor

-polystable divisor

3.
Tropical moduli spaces

In this section we recall the tropical moduli space we will deal with. First of all we need to recall the notion of generalized cone complex.

Given a finite set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we let

\cone(S):=\Big\{\sum_{s\in S}\lambda_{s}s|\lambda_{s}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Big\}.

A
polyhedral cone
is a subset
\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\sigma=\cone(S)
for some finite set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A cone
\sigma=\cone(S)
is
rational
if
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Every polyhedral cone
\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the intersection of finitely many closed half spaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote by
\dim(\sigma)
the
dimension
of
\sigma
, that is, the dimension of the minimal linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
\sigma
.
The relative interior
\sigma^{\circ}
of a cone
\sigma
is the interior of
\sigma
inside this minimal linear subspace.
A
face
of a cone
\sigma
is either
\sigma
or the intersection of
\sigma
with some linear subspace
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of codimension
one such that
\sigma
is contained in one of the two closed half-spaces determined by
H
. A
face of
\sigma
is also a polyhedral cone. A
facet
is a face of codimension
1
. We will simply use the terminology
cone
to mean
rational polyhedral cone
.

A morphism
f\colon\sigma\rightarrow\sigma^{\prime}
between cones
\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the restriction to
\sigma

of an integral linear transformation
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
T(\sigma)\subset\sigma^{\prime}
. We say that

f
is an isomorphism if there exists an inverse morphism of cones
f^{-1}\colon\sigma^{\prime}\rightarrow\sigma
.
A morphism
f\colon\sigma\rightarrow\sigma^{\prime}
is called a
face morphism
if
f
is an isomorphism between
\sigma
and a (not
necessarily proper) face of
\sigma^{\prime}
.

A
generalized cone complex

\Sigma
is the colimit (as a topological space) of a finite diagram of cones, denoted
D
, with face morphisms. A generalized cone complex is written as
\Sigma=\underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}\,\sigma_{x}
, where
x\rightarrow\sigma_{x}
is a contravariant functor from a category
\mathbf{C}
to the category of rational polyhedral cones. The group
\textnormal{Aut}(x)
acts on
\sigma_{x}
as a subgroup of automorphisms of
\sigma_{x}
. Notice that the set
\mathcal{P}
of isomorphism classes of objects of
\mathbf{C}
is a poset.

A
morphism

\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma^{\prime}
between generalized cone complexes
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
with diagrams of cones
D
and
D^{\prime}
is a continuous map of topological spaces such that for every cone
\sigma\in D
there exists a cone
\sigma^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
such that the induced map
\sigma\rightarrow\Sigma^{\prime}
factors through a cone morphism
\sigma\rightarrow\sigma^{\prime}
.
We say that a morphism of generalized cone complexes
\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma^{\prime}
is a
refinement
if it is a bijection.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Deligne-Mumford stack and
\mathcal{P}
be a poset. A
stratification of
\mathcal{M}
indexed by
\mathcal{P}
is a decomposition
\mathcal{M}=\coprod_{x\in\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{M}_{x}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{x}
is an irreducible substack of
\mathcal{M}
;

(2)

the closure
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
of
\mathcal{M}_{x}
has a decomposition
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{x}=\coprod_{x^{\prime}\geq x}\mathcal{M}_{x^{\prime}}
.

We call
\mathcal{M}_{x}
a
stratum
of the stratification.

3.1.
The moduli space of tropical curves

Generalized cone complexes are often used to parametrize tropical objects. The prototype example is the moduli space of pointed tropical curve
M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
.
The moduli space
M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
, first constructed
in
[
6
]
, has the structure of a generalized cone complex (see
[
5
]
).

We let
\mathbf{G}_{g,n}
be the category whose objects are stable graphs of genus
g
with
n
legs and whose arrows are specialization of graphs. We let
\mathcal{G}_{g,n}=\mathbf{G}_{g,n}/\sim
be the poset whose elements are isomorphism classes of graphs in
\mathbf{G}_{g,n}
.

Given a graph
\Gamma
we define the cone
\tau_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma)}_{\geq 0}
and its open subcone
\tau_{\Gamma}^{\circ}:=\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma)}_{>0}
. Notice that given a specialization of graphs
\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
, then we have a natural face morphism
\tau_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\rightarrow\tau_{\Gamma}

whose image is the face of
\tau_{\Gamma}
where the coordinates
corresponding to
E(\Gamma)\setminus E(\Gamma^{\prime})
vanish.
We define the generalized cone complex:

M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}=\underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}\;\tau_{\Gamma}=\coprod_{[\Gamma]}\tau^{\circ}_{\Gamma}/\textnormal{Aut}(\Gamma)

where the limit is taken over the category
\mathbf{G}_{g,n}
and the union is taken over all equivalence classes
[\Gamma]
in
\mathcal{G}_{g,n}
.
The points in
M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
are in bijective correspondence with (isomorphism classes of) tropical curves of genus
g
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the moduli stack of
n
-pointed stable curves, and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
be the locus corresponding to smooth curves. Given an
n
-pointed stable curve
X
, we let
\Gamma_{X}
be the usual dual graph of
X
, whose vertices and edges are the components and the nodes of
X
, respectively. For every graph
\Gamma
with
n
legs, we define the locus
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
given by:

\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}:=\{[X]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big|\Gamma_{X}\cong\Gamma\}.

We have a stratification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}=\sqcup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{g,n}}\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
indexed by
\mathcal{G}_{g,n}
.
Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
is irreducible thanks to the existence of a natural étale finite map
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
, where

(2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}=\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\mathcal{M}_{w_{\Gamma}(v),\deg_{\Gamma}(v)+L_{\Gamma}(v)}.

3.2.
Universal tropical Jacobians

A

polarization

(of degree

)
is the datum of a polarization
\mu_{\Gamma}
(of degree

n

\iota_{*}(\mu_{\Gamma})=\mu_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
, for every specialization of graphs
\iota\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
. The
universal genus-

canonical polarization of degree

takes every graph
\Gamma
and a vertex

to:

\mu_{\Gamma}(v):=\frac{d(2w_{\Gamma}(v)-2+\deg_{\Gamma}(v))}{2g-2}=\frac{d}{2g-2}K_{\Gamma}(v).

Let
\mu
be a universal genus-
g
polarization.
We denote by
\mathbf{QD}_{\mu,g,n}
the category whose objects are triples
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
, where
\Gamma
is a stable graph of genus
g
with
n
legs, and
(\mathcal{E},D)
is a
(v_{0},\mu)
-quasistable pseudo-divisor on
\Gamma
(the first leg is incident to

), and whose arrows are given by specialization of triples. We let
\mathcal{QD}_{\mu,g,n}=\mathbf{QD}_{\mu,g,n}/\sim
be the poset of the isomorphism classes of triples
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
.

Given a graph

\Gamma
and a
v_{0}
-quasistable
pseudo-divisor
(\mathcal{E},D)
on
\Gamma
, we define

\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}:=\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}_{\geq 0}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}:=\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}_{>0}.

Notice that, given a specialization

, we have a natural face morphism
\tau_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}\rightarrow\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
.

.

Let
\mu
be a universal genus-
g
polarization. The

\mu
-quasistable divisors
is the generalized cone complex:

J^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}:=\underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}\;\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\coprod_{[\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D]}\tau^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}/\textnormal{Aut}(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D),

where the limit is taken over the category
\mathbf{QD}_{\mu,g,n}
and the union is taken over all equivalence classes
[\Gamma,\mathcal{E},S]
in
\mathcal{QD}_{\mu,g,n}
(see
[
2
, Definition 5.11]
).

By
[
2

the cone
complex
J^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
parametrizes equivalence classes
(X,\mathcal{D})
, where X is a stable
n
-pointed tropical curve of genus
g
and
\mathcal{D}
is a

(p_{0},\mu)
-quasistable divisor on

.
By
[

there is a morphism of generalized cone complexes

\pi^{\textnormal{trop}}_{J}\colon J_{\mu,g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}\rightarrow M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}

taking a class

to the tropical curve
X
. Indeed, we have the linear map:

(3)

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma)}

taking

if

, otherwise. This map induces a morphism of cones
\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}\rightarrow\tau_{\Gamma}
factoring the natural composition

\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}\rightarrow J_{\mu,g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\textnormal{trop}}_{J}}}{{\rightarrow}}M_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}.

Consider a graph
\Gamma
and a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset E(\Gamma)

Remark 3.3

If

By Proposition

Lemma 3.6

Let

be the induced specialization of graphs. If

Proof.

We have specializations

Theorem 3.7
.

The map
\rho^{\textnormal{trop}}_{P}\colon J^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}\rightarrow P^{\textnormal{trop}}_{\mu,g,n}
is a refinement of generalized cone complexes. For every
\mu
-polystable pseudo-divisor

\Gamma
with

\sigma^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\underset{(\mathcal{E},D)=\pol(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}{\coprod_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})\in\mathbf{QD}_{\mu,g,n}}}\tau^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\underset{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}{\coprod_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})\in\mathbf{QD}_{\mu,g,n}}}\tau^{\circ}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}.

Proof.

First of all, if
\mathcal{D}
is a
\mu
-polystable divisor on a tropical curve

equivalent to a
(p_{0},\mu)
-quasistable divisor
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
on

, then the combinatorial type of
\mathcal{D}
is equal to
\pol(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
, where
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is the combinatorial type of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
(see the proof of
[

, Theorem 5.9 (1)]
). Thus we can describe locally the map
\rho^{\textnormal{trop}}_{P}
as follows.
Let
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
be a
(v_{0},\mu)
-quasistable pseudo-divisor on a stable graph

with
n
legs, and set

.
Since

is simple, we have that
\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}=\sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}
. Since

, by Remark
3.3
we have an inclusion
\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}=\sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}\hookrightarrow\sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
which factors the composition

\tau_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}=\sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}\rightarrow J_{\mu,g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho^{\textnormal{trop}}_{P}}}{{\rightarrow}}P_{\mu,g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}.

Denote

\displaystyle\sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}

4.
Root-graphs and their categories

In this section we introduce the notion of pseudo-root and root-graph, and study stability conditions on these objects. We will define the category of root-graphs, which will be important in defining later some tropical moduli spaces.

4.1.
Pseudo-roots on graphs

Let
g
be a nonnegative integer.
From now on,
\mu
will be the canonical polarization of degree
g-1
, defined over a graph
\Gamma
as the polarization taking
v\in V(\Gamma)
to

\mu(v)=\frac{2w(v)-2+\deg(v)}{2}.

All the stability conditions will be taken with respect to
\mu
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. A
pseudo-root
on
\Gamma
is a pseudo-divisor
(\mathcal{E},D)
on
\Gamma
such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
, for some acyclic flow
\phi
on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
.
A pseudo-root
(\mathcal{E},D)
is, respectively,
stable
,
semistable
,
polystable
,
v_{0}
-quasistable
for some vertex
v_{0}
of
\Gamma
, if so is the pseudo-divisor
(\mathcal{E},D)
. (Notice that a pseudo-root has degree
g-1
, where
g
is the genus of its underlying graph.)

We will see in Proposition
4.7
that
\phi
is uniquely determined if
(\mathcal{E},D)
is semistable.
Given a pseudo root
(\mathcal{E},D)
on a graph
\Gamma
with associated acyclic flow
\phi
, we define

(6)

P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}=\{e\in E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})|\phi(e)=0\}.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},D)
be a pseudo-root on a graph
\Gamma
and
\phi
be an acyclic flow on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
.
Consider the specialization
\iota\colon\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
. Since
\iota_{*}
is linear on divisors, we have
2\iota_{*}(D)+\Div(\iota_{*}(\phi))=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}}
. Notice that
\iota_{*}(\phi)
is acyclic by definition. Then
(\emptyset,\iota_{*}(D))
is a pseudo-root on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
.

Definition 4.3
.

A
root-graph
is a triple
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
, where
\Gamma
is a graph and
(\mathcal{E},D)
is a pseudo-root on
\Gamma
. A root-graph is
stable
,
semistable
,
polystable
,
v_{0}
-quasistable
for some vertex
v_{0}
of
\Gamma
, if so is its underlying pseudo-root.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
be a root-graph. Let
\phi
be an acyclic flow on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
. The pseudo-divisor
(\mathcal{E},D)
is stable if and only if
\phi=0
.

Proof.

If
(\mathcal{E},D)
is stable, then
\mathcal{E}=\emptyset
. By Proposition
2.1
and Remark
4.2
, we can assume
\phi(e)\neq 0
,
\forall\;e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
. Assume that
E(\Gamma)\neq\emptyset
and let
v\in V(\Gamma)
be a sink of
\phi
. Thus
\Div(\phi)(v)\geq\deg_{\Gamma}(v)
. The relation
2D(v)+\Div(\phi)(v)=K_{\Gamma}(v)
gives us

D(v)\leq-\frac{\deg_{\Gamma}(v)}{2}+w_{\Gamma}(v)-1+\frac{\deg_{\Gamma}(v)}{2}=\mu(v)-\frac{\deg_{\Gamma}(v)}{2}.

Hence
\beta_{D}(v)\leq 0
, and
D
is not
\mu
-stable, a contradiction.

Conversely, if
\phi=0
, then for every proper subset
W\subset V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
we have

\beta_{D}(W)=\deg D|_{W}-\mu(W)+\frac{\delta_{W}}{2}=\frac{\deg K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}}{2}-\mu(W)+\frac{\delta_{W}}{2}=\frac{\delta_{W}}{2}.

Hence
\beta_{D}(W)>0
and
(\mathcal{E},D)
is stable.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
be a semistable root graph. Let
\phi
be an acyclic flow on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
. Then
|\phi(e)|\leq 1
for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
and Remark
4.2
,
we can assume
\phi(e)\neq 0
, for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
.
By contradiction, suppose that
\phi(e_{0})\geq 2
for some
e_{0}\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
.
We let
V
be the set of vertices
v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
for which
there is a positive
\phi
-path from
s(e_{0})
to
v
.
Then
t(e_{0})\in V
and, since
\phi
is acyclic, we have
s(e_{0})\in V^{c}
, so
V
is a proper nonempty subset of
V(\Gamma)
. By the definition of
V
, we have
\phi(e)\geq 1
for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})
with
t(e)\in V
. Hence:

(7)

\deg(\Div(\phi)|_{V})=\phi(e_{0})+\underset{t(e)\in V}{\sum_{e\in\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})\setminus\{e_{0}\}}}\phi(e)>\#E(V,V^{c})=\delta_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}},V}.

On the other hand,
we have

\deg(\Div(\phi)|_{V})=\deg(K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}|_{V})-2\deg(D|_{V})=2\mu(V)-2\deg(D|_{V})\leq\delta_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}},V},

where we use that
\beta_{D}(V)\geq 0
by the
\mu
-semistability of
D
, and this contradicts equation (
7
).
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
be a semistable root-graph. Let
\phi
be an acyclic flow on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
. Given a subset
V\subset V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
, we have
\beta_{D}(V)=0
if and only if
\phi(e)=1
for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})
such that
t(e)\in V
.

Proof.

First of all, we have

(8)

\deg(\Div(\phi)|_{V})=\deg(K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}|_{V})-2\deg D|_{V}=2\mu(V)-2\deg D|_{V}.

Thanks to equation (
8
), the condition
\beta_{D}(V)=\deg D|_{V}-\mu(V)+\frac{\delta_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}},V}}{2}=0
is equivalent to the condition

\deg(\Div(\phi)|_{V})=\delta_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}},V}
.
We are done by Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
be a semistable root-graph. Let
\phi
be an acyclic flow on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
. Then

(1)

\phi(e)=1
for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
such that
t(e)
is an exceptional vertex of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
;

(2)

\phi
is the unique acyclic flow such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
.

Proof.

Let us prove item (1). Let
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
such that
t(e)
is an exceptional vertex of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
. The relation
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
implies that
\Div(\phi)(t(e))=2
. Since, by Lemma
4.5
, we have
\phi(e)\leq 1
, we deduce that
\phi(e)=1
.

Let us prove item (2). Notice that
(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}},\emptyset,D)
is a semistable root-graph. So assume
\mathcal{E}=\emptyset
. We proceed by induction on
\#E(\Gamma)
, being the statement clear for
\#E(\Gamma)=0
.
If
(\emptyset,D)
is stable, then we are done by Lemma
4.4
. So we can assume that there is a proper subset
V\subset V(\Gamma)
such that
\beta_{D}(V)=0
.
By Lemma
4.6
we know that
\phi(e)=1
for every
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})
such that
t(e)\in V
.
Set
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma(V)
and
\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma(V^{c})
. Define:

(9)

D_{1}:=D|_{\Gamma_{1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad D_{2}:=D|_{\Gamma_{2}}-\sum_{v\in V^{c}}\deg_{E(V,V^{c})}(v)v.

We claim that
(\Gamma_{i},\emptyset,D_{i})
is a semistable root-graph for
i=1,2
. Indeed, consider the flow
\phi_{i}:=\phi|_{\Gamma_{i}}
on
\Gamma_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. First of all,
we have:

\displaystyle 2D_{1}

\displaystyle=2D|_{\Gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle=K_{\Gamma}|_{\Gamma_{1}}-\Div(\phi)|_{\Gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle=K_{\Gamma_{1}}+\sum_{v\in V}\deg_{E(V,V^{c})}(v)v-\Div(\phi_{1})-\underset{t(e)\in V}{\sum_{e\in\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})}}\phi(e)t(e)

\displaystyle=K_{\Gamma_{1}}-\Div(\phi_{1})

and

\displaystyle 2D_{2}

\displaystyle=2D|_{\Gamma_{2}}-2\sum_{v\in V^{c}}\deg_{E(V,V^{c})}(v)v

\displaystyle=K_{\Gamma}|_{\Gamma_{2}}-\Div(\phi)|_{\Gamma_{2}}-2\sum_{v\in V^{c}}\deg_{E(V,V^{c})}(v)v

\displaystyle=K_{\Gamma_{2}}-\sum_{v\in V^{c}}\deg_{E(V,V^{c})}(v)v-\Div(\phi_{2})-\underset{t(e)\in V^{c}}{\sum_{e\in\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})}}\phi(e)t(e)

\displaystyle=K_{\Gamma_{2}}-\Div(\phi_{2}).

We now check the semistability of
(\Gamma_{i},\emptyset,D_{i})
.
Let
\mu_{i}
be the canonical polarization of degree
g_{\Gamma_{i}}-1
on
\Gamma_{i}
, which is the polarization taking a vertex
v\in V(\Gamma_{i})
to

\mu_{i}(v)=\mu(v)-\frac{\deg_{E(V,V^{c})}(v)}{2}.

For every subset
W\subset V(\Gamma_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{D_{1}}(W)

\displaystyle=\deg(D_{1}|_{W})-\mu_{1}(W)+\frac{\delta_{\Gamma_{1},W}}{2}

\displaystyle=\deg(D|_{W})-\mu(W)+\frac{\#E(V^{c},W)}{2}+\frac{\delta_{\Gamma,W}-\#E(V^{c},W)}{2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{D}(W)\geq 0,

where the last inequality follows by the
\mu
-semistability of
D
on
\Gamma
.
So the root-graph
(\Gamma_{1},\emptyset,D_{1})
is

\mu_{1}
-semistable. For every subset
W\subset V(\Gamma_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{D_{2}}(W)

\displaystyle=\deg(D_{2}|_{W})-\mu_{2}(W)+\frac{\delta_{\Gamma_{2},W}}{2}

\displaystyle=\deg(D|_{W})-\#E(V,W)-\mu(W)+\frac{\#E(V,W)}{2}+\frac{\delta_{\Gamma,W}-\#E(V,W)}{2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{D}(W)-\#E(V,W)

\displaystyle=\beta_{D}(V\cup W)-\beta_{D}(V)+\#E(V,W)-\#E(V,W)

\displaystyle=\beta_{D}(V\cup W)\geq 0,

where we use
[
2

and the
\mu
-semistability of
D
on
\Gamma
. So
the root-graph
(\Gamma_{2},\emptyset,D_{2})
is

\mu_{2}
-semistable. This concludes the proof of the claim.

By the induction hypothesis,
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are uniquely determined, so is
\phi
.
∎

4.2.

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},D)
be a polystable pseudo-root on a graph
\Gamma
. We have
\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)}(e)=0
if and only if
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
is a non-exceptional oriented edge. In particular,

is a principal divisor on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}

\Div(\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\Div(w_{e})
, where
w_{e}
is the exceptional vertex in the interior of

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{E},D)
is polystable, we have that the divisor

is
\mu_{\mathcal{E}}
-stable on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}

D_{1}=D_{\mathcal{E}}-\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}\text{deg}_{\mathcal{E}}(v)
is
\mu_{1}
-stable, where
\mu_{1}
is the canonical divisor of degree
g_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}-1
of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
. Set
\phi=\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
. By Theorem
4.7
(1), we have
2D_{1}+\Div(\phi|_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}})=K_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}}
, then by Lemma
4.4
we have that
\phi|_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}}=0
, that is,
\phi(e)=0
for every non-exceptional oriented edge

. We can conclude using Theorem
4.7
(1).
∎

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. The pseudo-divisors
(\mathcal{E}_{P},D_{P})
, for

a cyclic subgraph of
\Gamma

\Gamma
.
In particular,
\Gamma
admits exactly
2^{b_{1}(\Gamma)}
polystable pseudo-roots.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be the acyclic flow associated to the

-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{P}}\Div(w_{e})
. We have:

2D_{P}+\Div(\phi)=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\left(2\deg_{\Gamma}(v)-\deg_{P}(v)-2+2w_{\Gamma}(v)-\deg_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}(v)\right)v=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}.

Moreover, since
\mu
is the canonical polarization, we have:

\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}(v)=\mu(v)+\frac{\deg_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}(v)}{2}=(D_{P})_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}(v)

for every

(D_{P})_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}
is
\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}
-stable on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}_{P}}

(\mathcal{E}_{P},D_{P})

We deduce that
(\mathcal{E}_{P},D_{P})
is a polystable pseudo-root of
\Gamma

Conversely, let
(\mathcal{E},D)
be a polystable pseudo-divisor on
\Gamma
. By Lemma
4.16
:

\displaystyle 2D

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\Div(w_{e})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}(\deg_{\Gamma}(v)-2+2w_{\Gamma}(v)+\deg_{\mathcal{E}}(v))v-2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}w_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}(2\deg_{\Gamma}(v)-\deg_{E(\Gamma)\setminus\mathcal{E}}(v)-2+2w_{\Gamma}(v))v-2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}w_{e}.

If we set

is even for every

is a cyclic subgraph of

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
\iota\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
be a specialization of graphs. Given a cyclic subgraph

\Gamma

Proof.

First of all, notice that
\iota_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{P})=\mathcal{E}_{\iota_{*}(P)}
. We now show that
\iota_{*}(D_{P})=D_{\iota_{*}(P)}
. We can assume that
\iota
is the contraction of just one edge

and

\Gamma

be the vertex of
\Gamma^{\prime}

\iota_{*}(D_{P})(v)=D_{\iota_{*}P}(v)

with

. If
e
is not a loop, then

\displaystyle\iota_{*}(D_{P})(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg_{\Gamma}(v_{1})+\deg_{\Gamma}(v_{2})-\frac{\deg_{P}(v_{1})}{2}-\frac{\deg_{P}(v_{2})}{2}-2+

\displaystyle+w_{\Gamma}(v_{1})+w_{\Gamma}(v_{2})-[e\in\mathcal{E}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(v_{0})-\left(\frac{\deg_{P}(v_{1})}{2}+\frac{\deg_{P}(v_{2})}{2}+[e\in\mathcal{E}]\right)+w_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(v_{0})-\left(\frac{\deg_{\iota_{*}(P)}(v_{0})}{2}+1\right)+w_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\iota_{*}(P)}(v_{0}).

On the other hand, if

\displaystyle\iota_{*}(D_{P})(v_{0})

\displaystyle\deg_{\Gamma}(\overline{v})-\frac{\deg_{P}(\overline{v})}{2}-1+w_{\Gamma}(\overline{v})-[e\in\mathcal{E}]

\displaystyle\deg_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(v_{0})-\left(\frac{\deg_{P}(\overline{v})}{2}+[e\in\mathcal{E}]\right)+\left(1+w_{\Gamma}(\overline{v})\right)

\displaystyle\deg_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(v_{0})-\left(\frac{\deg_{\iota_{*}(P)}(v_{0})}{2}+1\right)+w_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(v_{0})

4.3.
Spin pseudo-roots

In this section we consider an additional structure to root-graphs. This is a sign function which, as we shall see later, is the combinatorial counterpart for the parity of the number of sections of a sheaf on curves.

Definition 4.26
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. A
spin semistable pseudo-root
on
\Gamma

(\mathcal{E},D,s)
, where

\Gamma

, such that
s(v)=0
for every vertex
v\in V(\Gamma/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
of weight

(\mathcal{E},D,s)
is
polystable
(respectively,
v_{0}
-quasistable for some

) if so is
(\mathcal{E},D)
. A
spin semistable
(

v_{0}
-quasistable
)

is a tuple
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)
, where
\Gamma
is a graph and
(\mathcal{E},D,s)
is a spin semistable (polystable,
v_{0}
-quasistable) pseudo-root on
\Gamma
.
We say that

is

or

, respectively, if so is

.

Definition 4.28
.

5.
Moduli of tropical theta characteristics

In this section we introduce and study the moduli space of tropical theta characteristics. We describe an important refinement of this space.

5.1.
Cones associated to semistable pseudo-roots

First of all, we need to introduce the cone associated to a semistable root-graph.

Consider a semistable root-graph
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
. Consider the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}
with coordinates
x_{e}
for
e\in E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
. For every oriented edge
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
, we will abuse notation writing
x_{e}
for the coordinates associated to the unoriented edge associated to
e
.
We define
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}\subset\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}
as the cone obtained by intersecting
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}_{\geq 0}
with the linear subspace
\Lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
defined by the equations:

(11)

\Lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}:\;\;\sum_{e\in\gamma}\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)}(e)x_{e}=0

where
\gamma
runs over the set of oriented cycles of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
.

Remark 5.1
.

Notice that if
(\mathcal{E},D)
is semistable, then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}\subset\Lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
.

We let
\lambda^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
be the interior of
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
, that is,
\lambda^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}\cap\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}_{>0}
.
We also set

(12)

b_{0}(\mathcal{E},D):=b_{0}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})\quad\;\text{ and }\;\quad b_{1}(\mathcal{E},D):=b_{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}).

Notice that the number of independent equations in (
11
) is
b_{1}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
, which is equal to
b_{1}(\mathcal{E},D)-b_{0}(\mathcal{E},D)+1+\#\mathcal{E}
. Hence

(13)

\dim\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\#E(\Gamma)-b_{1}(\mathcal{E},D)+b_{0}(\mathcal{E},D)-1.

We define

\Sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\{\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}|(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})\in\mathbf{SSR}_{g,n}\text{ and }(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})\}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
be a semistable pseudo-root.

(1)

If
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is a specialization of semistable root-graphs, then
we have
a face morphism
\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}\subset\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
.

(2)

Every face of
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
is equal to a cone
\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}
for some specialization of semistable root-graphs
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
.

(3)

We have a decomposition

(14)

\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\underset{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}{\coprod_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})\in{\bf SSR}_{g,n}}}\lambda^{\circ}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}.

(4)

The set
\Sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
is a fan whose support is
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
.

Proof.

We prove (1). If
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is an isomorphism, the result is clear. Otherwise,
consider the hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}
given in the coordinates
(x_{e})_{e\in E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}
of
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}
by the equation

H:\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})\setminus E({\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}})}x_{e}=0.

Notice that
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
in contained in one of the two half spaces determined by
H
. Moreover,
the equations defining the intersection
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}\cap H
are obtained by putting
x_{e}=0
in equation (
11
),
\forall e\in E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})\setminus E({\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}})
. These equations are the equations defining
\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}
, hence
\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}=\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}\cap H.

Let us prove (3). By (1), for every
(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
as in the right hand side of equation (
14
) we have
\lambda^{\circ}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}\subset\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
. Let us show that
\lambda^{\circ}_{(\Gamma_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1},D_{1})}\cap\lambda^{\circ}_{(\Gamma_{2},\mathcal{E}_{2},D_{2})}=\emptyset
for distinct
(\Gamma_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1},D_{1})
,
(\Gamma_{2},\mathcal{E}_{2},D_{2})
as in the right hand side of equation (
14
). This is true if
(\Gamma_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1})\neq(\Gamma_{2},\mathcal{E}_{2})
, because in this case
\mathbb{R}^{E({\Gamma_{1}^{\mathcal{E}_{1}}})}_{>0}\cap\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{E}_{2}})}_{>0}=\emptyset
(as subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}
) and
\lambda^{\circ}_{(\Gamma_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i},D_{i})}\subset\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma_{i}^{\mathcal{E}_{i}})}_{>0}
for
i=1,2
. If
\Gamma^{\prime}:=\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{2}
, then we can assume that
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D_{1})
and
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D_{2})
have the same associated specialization
\widehat{\iota}\colon\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
, otherwise
\mathbb{R}^{E({\Gamma_{1}^{\mathcal{E}_{1}}})}_{>0}\cap\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{E}_{2}})}_{>0}=\emptyset
and we argue as before. Hence
D_{1}=\widehat{\iota}_{*}(D)=D_{2}
, a contradiction. So the right hand side of equation (
14
) is contained in the left hand side.

On the other hand, let
(x_{e})_{e\in E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})}\in\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
. Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
be the specialization of triples determined by the specialization
\widehat{\iota}\colon\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
contracting the edges
e
of
E(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
for which
x_{e}=0
(so
D^{\prime}=\widehat{\iota}_{*}(D)
). Since
2D+\Div(\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
, we have

2D^{\prime}+\Div(\widehat{\iota}_{*}(\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)}))=K_{{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}
and by equation (
11
) the flow
\widehat{\iota}_{*}(\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
is acyclic. Hence
(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is a semistable root-graph and
(x_{e})_{e\in E({\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}})}\in\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}^{\circ}
. This concludes the proof of (3).

Notice that (2) and (4) are consequences of (3) and (1), hence we are done.
∎

Recall the
\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
-graphs
P_{u}
of
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
, for
u\in V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
(see Definition

).

Lemma-Definition 5.3
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
be a specialization of triples where

is a semistable root-graph. Assume that the associated specialization

satisfies one of the following conditions:

(1)

\widehat{\iota}
is the contraction of an edge

.

(2)

\widehat{\iota}
is the contraction of

for distinct vertices

such that

1

, respectively, if (1) or (2) holds).

Proof.

Since

Corollary 5.4
.

The following properties hold.

(1)

For a semistable pseudo-root
(\mathcal{E},D)
on a graph
\Gamma
, a cone
\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}\in\Sigma_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
is a facet of
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
if and only if the associated specialization
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is elementary.

(2)

Every specialization of semistable pseudo-roots can be written (up to isomorphisms) as a chain of elementary specializations.

(3)

An elementary specialization of semistable root-graphs can not be factored as a non-trivial chain of specializations of semistable root-graphs.

Proof.

Consider an elementary specialization
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
. If
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is of type 1, then
\#E(\Gamma^{\prime})=\#E(\Gamma)-1
and
\dim\Lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}=\dim\Lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
. If
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is of type 2, then
\#E(\Gamma^{\prime})=\#E(\Gamma)-\#E(P_{u},P_{u^{\prime}})
and

\dim\Lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}=\dim\Lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}-\#E(P_{v},P_{v^{\prime}})+1.

Hence, in both cases,
\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}
is a facet of
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
. Conversely, assume that
\lambda_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})}
is a facet of
\lambda_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}
and let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
and
\widehat{\iota}\colon\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
be the associated specializations. In particular,
\widehat{\iota}_{*}(\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
is acyclic. If
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)\geq(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is not elementary, by what we have already shown we have that there are distinct vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
and a proper non-empty set

such that
\widehat{\iota}
contracts
E(P_{u},P_{u^{\prime}})\setminus F
and does not contract
F
. Thus
\widehat{\iota}
sends each oriented edge in
\overrightarrow{F}
to a positive
\widehat{\iota}_{*}(\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
-loop in
(\Gamma^{\prime})^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
, which is a contradiction. This proves (1). Notice that (2) and (3) are consequences of (1) and Theorem
5.2
.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{D}

of
\mathcal{D}

.

There is a rational function

. Since
\mathcal{D}
is unitary, we have

Lemma 5.14

Let

(1)

Definition 5.15

T^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}:=\underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}\;\kappa_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}=\coprod_{[\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D]}\kappa^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}/\textnormal{Aut}(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D),

Remark 5.16

It is worth noting that although
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma)}_{\geq 0}
is isomorphic to
\kappa_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}

Proposition 5.17
.

The generalized cone complex
T^{\textnormal{trop}}_{g,n}
parametrizes equivalence classes of pairs
(X,\mathcal{D})
, where
X
is a stable
n
-pointed tropical curve of genus
g
, and
\mathcal{D}
is a polystable root of

, and where

is equivalent to
(X^{\prime},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
if there is an isomorphism
\iota\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}

\iota_{*}(\mathcal{D})

Proof.

Let
X
be a
n
-pointed stable tropical curve of genus
g
and let
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{X}
be the genus-
g
stable graph with
n
legs which is a model of
X

By Lemma
5.11
,

\mathcal{D}
of

has combinatorial type
equal to a polystable pseudo-root
(\mathcal{E},D)
on

corresponds to a point in
\kappa^{\circ}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)}

(X,\mathcal{D})
and

\iota\colon X\to X^{\prime}
such that
\iota_{*}(\mathcal{D})
is equivalent to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.
Consider the three points

.

Let

Proposition 5.24
.

The morphism of generalized cone complexes
\rho_{T}^{\textnormal{trop}}\colon R_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}\rightarrow T_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}

7.1.
Esteves universal Jacobian

A
(pointed) curve
will always mean a reduced connected projective scheme of dimension
1
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
, whose singularities are nodes, together with a tuple of smooth points. We consider curves of genus
g\geq 2
. The dual graph of a curve
X
is denoted
\Gamma_{X}
; we
let
p_{e}
be the node of
X
corresponding to an edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
.

A
chain of rational components
is a curve which is a union of smooth connected rational components
E_{1},\dots E_{n}
such that
E_{i}\cap E_{j}=\emptyset
for
|i-j|>1
and
\#(E_{i}\cap E_{i+1})=1
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. A
semistable modification

X
of a curve
C
is a curve
X
which is the union of the normalization
C_{\Delta}
of
C
at a subset
\Delta\subset C
of nodes and chains
E_{p}=\cup_{1\leq i\leq n_{p}}E_{p,i}
of rational components (called

), for

, such that
\#(E_{p}\cap C_{\Delta})=2
and
\#(E_{p,1}\cap C_{\Delta})=\#(E_{p,n_{p}}\cap C_{\Delta})=1
. A
quasistable modification
is a semistable modification such that
n_{p}=1
for every
p\in\Delta
. There is a map
X\rightarrow C
contracting the exceptional components.

Recall that a pointed curve
X
is
stable

if so is its dual graph.
We say that
X

is
quasistable
if it is a quasistable modification of a stable curve. Given a curve
X
, we denote by

the

of

, obtained by contracting all exceptional components of

. Similarly, we can define the stable model

for a family

of nodal curves.

Let

be a torsion-free rank-

sheaf on a nodal curve

. Consider a smooth point

and a polarization
\mu
on the dual graph

of

. For every subcurve

of

, where

is the set of vertices corresponding to the components of

contained in

, and
\delta_{Y}:=\delta_{V_{Y}}=|Y\cap\overline{X\setminus Y}|

be the restriction of

to

We say that

is
(p_{0},\mu)
-quasistable

for every proper subcurve
Y
where the inequality is strict if

. The definition extends to families: given a family

of nodal curves with a section
\sigma

on

, we say that a sheaf
\mathcal{I}
on

is

-quasistable
if the restriction of
\mathcal{I}
to every fiber

.

7.2.
Spin curves and their moduli

The moduli space of spin curves
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
compactifies the moduli space
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
of isomorphism classes of pairs
(X,L)
, where
X
is a
n
-pointed smooth curve of genus
g
and
L
is an invertible sheaf on
X
such that
L^{\otimes 2}\cong\omega_{X}
.
The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
is finite of degree
2^{2g}
over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, hence
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
has dimension
3g-3+n
.

We use a different, but equivalent, modular description of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
, which better suits our purposes. The closed points of
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
are isomorphism classes of pairs
(X,L)
, where
X
is a
n
-pointed quasistable curve and
L
is an invertible sheaf
with degree
-1
on exceptional components of
X
and
L|_{Y}^{\otimes 2}(-2\sum_{E\in\Ex(X)}\sum_{p\in Y\cap E}p)\cong\omega_{Y}
, where
Y
is the complement of the union of the exceptional components of
X
. Notice that
Y
could be non-conneted. The space
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
has two connected components,
\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{-}_{g,n}
, corresponding to even and odd spin curves.

Remark 7.6
.

If
(X,L)
is a pair parametrized by a point of
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
, then
(X,L)
is a polystable spin curve. In fact, by
[
11
, Theorem 3.4.3]
,
the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}
admits a stratification indexed by the poset
\mathcal{SP}_{g,n}
. Recall that
\mathcal{SP}_{g,n}
is isomorphic to the poset of isomorphism classes of triples
(\Gamma,P,s)
, for a graph
\Gamma
, a cyclic subgraph
P
of
\Gamma
, and a sign function
s\colon V(\Gamma/P)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(see Remark
4.30
). The strata are defined, for every triple
(\Gamma,P,s)
, as:

\mathcal{S}_{(\Gamma,P,s)}=\{[X,L]\in\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}\big|(\Gamma_{\st(X)},\mathcal{E}_{L},D_{L},s_{L})\cong(\Gamma,\mathcal{E}_{P},D_{P},s)\}.

The moduli space
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
of theta characteristics on
n
-pointed smooth curves of genus
g
naturally sits inside
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}
.
We define
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
as the closure of
\mathcal{S}_{g,n}
inside
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}
. By definition, we have inclusions over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
:

\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}\supset\mathcal{S}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}.

In particular, the dimension of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
is
3g-3+n
. Moreover,
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
is a
Deligne-Mumford separated stack, since so is
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\mu,g,n}
.

Our next goal is to give a modular interpretation of the space
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
using root graphs. Before, we need to prove an easy result.

Lemma 7.7
.

A flow
\phi
on a graph is acyclic if and only if there are positive integers
h_{e}
, for each
e\in E(\Gamma)
such that

\sum_{e\in\gamma}\phi(e)h_{e}=0

for every oriented cycle
\gamma
of
\Gamma
. In particular, if
\widehat{\Gamma}
is the refinement of
\Gamma
obtained by inserting
h_{e}-1
vertices over the edge
e
, then

\Div(\phi)
is principal as a divisor on
\widehat{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

Consider the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma)}
with coordinates
\{x_{e}\}_{e\in E(\Gamma)}
. We define the linear subspace
\Lambda
of
\mathbb{R}^{E(\Gamma)}
given by the equations:

(25)

\sum_{e\in\gamma}\phi(e)x_{e}=0,

where
\gamma
runs over the set of oriented cycles of
\Gamma
.
There is a vector
(h_{e})_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\in\Lambda\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{E(\Gamma)}
if and only if
\Div(\phi)
is principal on the refinement of
\Gamma
obtained by inserting exactly
h_{e}-1
vertices in the interior of each edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
.
It is clear that
\Lambda\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{E(\Gamma)}
is empty if
\phi
is not acyclic.

If
\phi
is acyclic and nontrivial, then
|V(\Gamma)|\geq 2
. The space
\Lambda
is given by intersecting at most
b_{1}(\Gamma)
independent linear hyperspaces in a space of dimension
|E(\Gamma)|
, hence
\dim\Lambda\geq|V(\Gamma)|-1\geq 1
.
Since
\phi
is acyclic, for every oriented cycle
\gamma
for which (
25
) is not a trivial equation, there are two nonempty subset

\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}\subset\gamma
such that
\phi(e)\neq 0
for
e\in\gamma^{+}\cup\gamma^{-}
, so equation (
25
) reads as:

\sum_{e\in\gamma^{+}}|\phi(e)|x_{e}=\sum_{e\in\gamma^{-}}|\phi(e)|x_{e}.

We deduce that
\Lambda\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{E(\Gamma)}
is not empty.
∎

.

\mathcal{X}\times_{B}B
at the nodes of
X^{\prime}
corresponding to edges in
E(\Gamma^{\prime})\setminus E(\Gamma)
. Denote by
\varphi\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
the induced map. The dual graph of the special fiber
Y
of
\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow B
is
\widetilde{\Gamma^{\prime}}
. We let
Y_{\widetilde{v}}
be the component of
Y
corresponding to the vertex
\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\prime})
. Define the twister
\widetilde{T}
on
\mathcal{Y}
as:

\widetilde{T}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\Big(\sum_{\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\prime})}b_{\widetilde{v}}Y_{\widetilde{v}}\Big).

Let
\psi\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
be the contraction of the exceptional components of the special fiber of
\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow B
over which
\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{L})\otimes\widetilde{T}
has degree zero. Set
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}:=\psi_{*}(\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{L})\otimes\widetilde{T})
.
Then
(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}},\widetilde{\mathcal{L}})
is a family of spin curves over
B
whose generic fiber is isomorphic to
(X,\mathcal{L}|_{X})
and the special fiber has spin combinatorial type
(\Gamma^{\prime},\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime},\widetilde{D}^{\prime},\pol(s^{\prime}))
. By Remark
7.6
we get a specialization of polystable root-graphs
(\Gamma^{\prime},\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime},\widetilde{D}^{\prime},\pol(s^{\prime}))\geq(\Gamma,\emptyset,D,s)
, as wanted.

Case 2
. Assume that
(\Gamma,\emptyset,D)
is not stable.
We argue by induction on
\#E(\Gamma)
. There is a proper subset
V\subset V(\Gamma)
such that
\beta_{D}(V)=0
. Set
V^{\prime}:=\iota^{-1}(V)
. Let
(\Gamma_{i},\emptyset,D_{i}^{V},s_{i}^{V})
and
(\Gamma^{\prime}_{i},\emptyset,D_{i}^{V^{\prime}},s_{i}^{V^{\prime}})
be the spin semistable root-graphs as in Remark
4.31
.
An edge
e\in\mathcal{E}(V,V^{c})
gives rise to a section
\sigma_{e}\colon B\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
of
\pi
. We let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\colon\mathcal{X}_{i}\rightarrow B
be the two families of curves obtained by taking the normalization of
\mathcal{X}
at the union of
\sigma_{e}(B)
,
\forall\;e\in E(V,V^{c})
. The family
\pi_{i}
could fail to be quasistable. Though, we can and will consider the family to be quasistable simply adding a suitable number of sections to
\pi_{i}
. Set

\mathcal{M}_{1}:=\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}\text{ and }\mathcal{M}_{2}:=\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}\Big(-\sum_{e\in E(V,V^{c})}\sigma_{e}(B)\Big).

If
X_{i}
and
X^{\prime}_{i}
are the generic and special fiber of
\pi_{i}
, respectively, then
(X_{i},\mathcal{M}_{i}|_{X_{i}})
and
(X^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{M}_{i}|_{X^{\prime}_{i}})
are semistable spin curves of spin combinatorial type
(\Gamma_{i},\emptyset,D_{i}^{V},s_{i}^{V})
and
(\Gamma^{\prime}_{i},\emptyset,D_{i}^{V^{\prime}},s_{i}^{V^{\prime}})
.
By the induction hypothesis, there is a specialization of semistable root-graphs
\iota_{i}\colon(\Gamma^{\prime}_{i},\emptyset,D_{i}^{V^{\prime}},s_{i}^{V^{\prime}})\geq(\Gamma_{i},\emptyset,D_{i}^{V},s_{i}^{V})
. We argue that
\iota_{*}(\phi_{(\emptyset,D^{\prime})})=\phi_{(\emptyset,D)}
: this is clear away from
E(V,V^{c})
by the existence of the specialization
\iota_{i}
, while
\iota_{*}(\phi_{(\emptyset,D^{\prime})})(e)=\phi_{(\emptyset,D)}(e)=\pm 1
,
\forall\;e\in\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})=\overrightarrow{E}(V^{\prime},V^{\prime c})
by Theorem
4.7
(we use the natural inclusion
E(\Gamma)\subset E(\Gamma^{\prime})
to identify
\overrightarrow{E}(V,V^{c})
with
\overrightarrow{E}(V^{\prime},V^{\prime c})
, and that
\beta_{D^{\prime}}(V^{\prime})=0
by Proposition
2.1
). If
\widetilde{\iota}\colon\Gamma^{\prime}/P_{(\emptyset,D^{\prime})}\rightarrow\Gamma/P_{(\emptyset,D)}
is the associated specialization, we also deduce that
\widetilde{\iota}_{*}(s^{\prime})=s
, and we are done.
∎

Proposition 7.15
.

Let
(X^{\prime},L^{\prime})
be a semistable spin curves and
(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime},s^{\prime})
be its spin combinatorial type. Let
\iota\colon(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime},s^{\prime})\geq(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)
be a specialization of spin semistable root-graphs.
Then there is a one-parameter deformation
\mathcal{X}\to B
of
X^{\prime}
, with generic fiber
X
, and an invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{X}/B
such that
\mathcal{L}|_{X^{\prime}}\cong L^{\prime}
and
(X,\mathcal{L}|_{X})
is a semistable spin curves with spin combinatorial type
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)
.

Proof.

Adding a suitable number of section, we can make
X^{\prime}
a
n
-pointed stable curve, hence
we can assume
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
Let

\widetilde{\iota}\colon\Gamma^{\prime}/P_{(\emptyset,D^{\prime})}\rightarrow\Gamma/P_{(\emptyset,D)}
be the specializations associated to
(\Gamma^{\prime},\emptyset,D^{\prime},s^{\prime})\geq(\Gamma,\emptyset,D,s)
.

Case 1.
Assume that
(\Gamma,\emptyset,D)
is stable. Then
\phi_{(\emptyset,D)}=0
by Lemma
4.4
, hence
P_{(\emptyset,D)}=\Gamma
and
\#V(\Gamma/P_{(\emptyset,D)})=1
. By Corollary
5.4
, we can assume that
\iota
is elementary. If it is of type
1
, then
\phi_{(\emptyset,D^{\prime})}=0
, so
(\Gamma^{\prime},\emptyset,D^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is stable by Lemma
4.4
, and we are done by Remark
7.6
.

If the specialization
\iota
is elementary of type
2
, then
we can write

V(\Gamma^{\prime})=V(P^{\prime}_{1})\cup V(P^{\prime}_{2})
, where
\iota
is the contraction of
E(P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2})
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\Gamma^{\prime}_{n}
be the connected components of the subgraph of
\Gamma^{\prime}
having
E(P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2})
as a set of edges, and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{n+1},\dots,\Gamma^{\prime}_{m}
be the vertices in
V(\Gamma^{\prime})\setminus\cup_{1\leq i\leq n}V(\Gamma^{\prime}_{i})
. Let
X^{\prime}_{i}
be the subcurve of
X^{\prime}
corresponding to
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
For
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the multidegree of
\omega_{X^{\prime}_{i}}\otimes L^{\prime}|_{X^{\prime}_{i}}^{-2}
is equal to the restriction of
\Div(\phi_{(\emptyset,D^{\prime})})
to
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
, which is a principal divisor on
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
. By
[
19
, Corollary 6.9]
, there is a smoothing
\mathcal{X}_{i}\rightarrow B
of
X^{\prime}_{i}
and a twister
T_{i}
of
\mathcal{X}_{i}
such that
T_{i}|_{X^{\prime}_{i}}\cong\omega_{X^{\prime}_{i}}\otimes L^{\prime}|_{X^{\prime}_{i}}^{-2}
. For
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m\}
, consider the trivial family
\mathcal{X}_{i}=X^{\prime}_{i}\times B
.
Every edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
gives rise to two sections
\sigma^{i}_{e},\sigma^{j}_{e}\colon B\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{i}
, for
i
and
j
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{j}
are incident to
e
(seen as an edge of
\Gamma^{\prime}
). We let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
be the family obtained from
\sqcup\mathcal{X}_{i}
by identifying
\sigma^{i}_{e}(b)
and
\sigma^{j}_{e}(b)
,
\forall\;b\in B
. The generic fiber
X
of
\pi
has
\Gamma
as a dual graph.

We cover
\mathcal{X}
with two open subsets
U
and
V
, where
U\cong(\sqcup_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathcal{X}_{i})\setminus(\sqcup_{i,e}\sigma^{i}_{e}(B))
and
V
is the complement of the singular points of
X^{\prime}
corresponding to edges
e\in E(P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2})
. We let
T_{U}
be the sheaf on
U
restricting to
T_{i}|_{U\cap\mathcal{X}_{i}}
over
U\cap\mathcal{X}_{i}
. Since
\Pic(B)=0
, we have
T_{U}|_{U\cap V}\cong\mathcal{O}_{U\cap V}
, and hence we can glue
T_{U}
and
\mathcal{O}_{V}
along

to get a sheaf
T

\mathcal{X}
such that
T|_{X^{\prime}}\cong\omega_{X^{\prime}}\otimes L^{\prime-2}
and
T
is trivial away from

. By

there is an invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}|_{X^{\prime}}\cong L^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2}\cong\omega_{\mathcal{X}/B}\otimes T^{-1}
. Since
\mathcal{L}|_{X}^{\otimes 2}\cong\omega_{X}
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}|_{X}}=\emptyset
and

.
By Proposition
7.14
we have a specialization

of semistable root-graphs, then

, and we are done.

Case 2.
Assume that
(\Gamma,\emptyset,D)
is not stable. We argue by induction on

. There is a proper subset

such that

. Set

.

By construction,

be the family obtained by the induction hypothesis applied to the specialization

.
Every edge

gives rise to a section

of

. Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
be the family obtained from

. There are exact sequences

Corollary 7.16
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime},s^{\prime})}\cap\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime},s^{\prime})\geq(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)
in the poset
\mathcal{SPQ}_{g,n}
.

(3)

\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},D^{\prime},s^{\prime})}\subset\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}
.

Proof.

The result readily follows from Propositions
7.14
and
7.15
.
∎

We are ready to prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 7.17
.

The following decomposition is a stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
indexed by
\mathcal{SPQ}_{g,n}
:

(30)

\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}=\coprod_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)\in\mathcal{SPQ}_{g,n}}\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}.

In particular,
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
has exactly two connected components
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{+}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{-}_{g,n}
corresponding to even and odd quasistable spin curves and:

(1)

the maps
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{+}_{g,n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

and
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{-}_{g,n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
are finite exactly over the locus of tree-like curves, and hence
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}^{+}
and
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}^{-}
are not isomorphic, respectively, to
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}^{+}
and
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{g,n}^{-}
.

(2)

\codim_{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}}\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}=\#E(\Gamma)-b_{1}(\mathcal{E},D)
.

(3)

\codim_{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}}\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}=1
if and only if one of the following conditions hold:

(a)

\#V(\Gamma)=\#E(\Gamma)=1
.

(b)

\#V(\Gamma)=2
,
\mathcal{E}=\emptyset
, and
P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}=0
.

(4)

we have a reverse-inclusion bijection between the strata of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
and the cells of
Q_{g,n}^{\textnormal{trop}}
switching dimension and codimension.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.13
and Corollary
7.16
we have that the decomposition in equation (
30
) is a stratification indexed by
\mathcal{SPQ}_{g,n}
. The statement about the connected components follows by Remark
6.6
. By Propositions
5.9
and
7.13
we have that (2) and (4) hold.

We prove (1). Assume that
\Gamma
is not a tree-like graph. By upper semicontituity, without loss of generality, we can assume that
V(\Gamma)=2
. Let
e_{1},e_{2}
be edges of
\Gamma
that are not loops and set
\mathcal{E}=E(\Gamma)\setminus\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
.
Let
\phi
be the acyclic flow
on
\Gamma
such that, for
e\in\overrightarrow{E}(\Gamma)
, we have
\phi(e)=1
if either
t(e)
is an exceptional vertex or
s(e)=v_{0}
.
By construction, we have that
K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}(v)-\Div(\phi)(v)
is even for every
v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}})
, hence there is a divisor
D
on
\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}
such that
2D+\Div(\phi)=K_{\Gamma^{\mathcal{E}}}
. It is easy to check that
D
is
v_{0}
-quasistable.
Then
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
is a
v_{0}
-quasistable root-graph with
P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}=0
. If
H
is the subgraph of
\Gamma
with
e_{1},e_{2}
as edges, then we have
b_{1}(\mathcal{E},D)=b_{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=b_{1}(H)=1
. We can easily find an even and an odd sign functions on
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D)
, which we denote by
s^{+}
and
s^{-}
. So, by Proposition
7.13
the maps
\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s^{+})}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s^{-})}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
are not finite.

Conversely, let
\Gamma
be a tree-like grah and
T
be its spanning tree. Let
(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)
be a
v_{0}
-quasistable spin root-graph. Then
\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}
is connected, hence
\mathcal{E}
is formed by loops of
\Gamma
. Since
\phi_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
is acyclic and
P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}
is cyclic, we have that
\mathcal{E}\cup E(P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})
is the set of loops of
\Gamma
. Hence
b_{1}(\mathcal{E},D)=b_{1}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=b_{1}(T)=0
and, by Proposition
7.13
the map
\mathcal{Q}_{(\Gamma,\mathcal{E},D,s)}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is finite.

We prove (3). We have
\#E(\Gamma)-b_{1}(\mathcal{E},D)=1
if and only if
\#\mathcal{E}+\#E(P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})+\#V(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=2
, which is satisfied if either (a) or (b) holds. Moreover,

(i)

if
\#V(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=1
,
\#\mathcal{E}=1
and
P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}=0
, then (a) holds.

(ii)

if
\#V(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=1
,
\mathcal{E}=\emptyset
and
\#E(P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=1
, then (a) holds.

(iii)

if
\#V(\Gamma_{\mathcal{E}}/P_{(\mathcal{E},D)})=2
, then
\mathcal{E}=\emptyset
and
P_{(\mathcal{E},D)}=0
, and hence (b) holds.

The proof is complete.

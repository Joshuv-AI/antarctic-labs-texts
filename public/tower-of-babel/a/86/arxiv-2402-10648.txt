Teresa Yu

Abstract
. We study generalizations of Schur functors from categories consisting of flags of vector spaces. We give different descriptions of the category of such functors in terms of representations of certain combinatorial categories and infinite rank groups, and we apply these descriptions to study polynomial representations and representation stability of parabolic subgroups of general linear groups.



1.
Introduction

Schur functors and polynomial functors are fundamental objects due to their ability to translate properties across fields of math such as representation theory, commutative algebra, algebraic geometry, and combinatorics. There has been recent interest in studying variants of Schur functors from categories of linear algebraic data, such as the category whose objects are vector spaces with symmetric bilinear forms; these variants provide a new perspective for studying representations of classical groups
[
12
,
15
,
20
]
. In this paper, we take a similar approach by studying generalizations of Schur functors from categories where the linear algebraic data consists of flags of vector spaces, and we use these functors to study representations of parabolic subgroups of general linear groups.

1.1.
Main result

In order to state our main result, we introduce three categories. Each will be given the structure of a
tensor
category, meaning it is linear and symmetric monoidal. Fix
n\geq 1
.

Let
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}
be the following category: an object
\{V_{i}\}
is a finite dimensional complex vector space
V
equipped with a flag
0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{n}=V
of length
n
, and a morphism
f:V\to W
is a linear map such that
f(V_{i})\subset W_{i}
for
i\in[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For
a\geq 0
, there is a functor
F_{a}:\mathrm{Flag}_{n}\to\mathrm{Vec}
given by
F_{a}(\{V_{i}\})=V^{\otimes a}
, where
\mathrm{Vec}
denotes the category of finite dimensional complex vector spaces. We say a functor
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}\to\mathrm{Vec}
is
polynomial
if it is a subquotient of a finite direct sum of
F_{a}
’s. The tensor product of such functors is defined by tensoring their values, and it is again polynomial.

Let
\mathbf{GL}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{GL}_{i}(\mathbf{C})
be the infinite general linear group, and let
\mathbf{V}
be the defining representation. Equip
\mathbf{V}
with a flag of length
n
where each graded piece is also infinite dimensional. Let
\mathbf{G}
be the subgroup of
\mathbf{GL}
preserving this flag; this group can be identified with the group of invertible
n\times n
block upper triangular matrices, where each block is an element of
\text{End}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\text{End}(\mathbf{C}^{i})
and blocks along the diagonal are elements of
\mathbf{GL}
. We call
\mathbf{V}
along with the flag the
standard representation
of
\mathbf{G}
, and say a representation of
\mathbf{G}
is
polynomial
if it appears as a subquotient of a finite direct sum of tensor powers of
\mathbf{V}
. The tensor product of polynomial representations as complex vector spaces is again polynomial.

Let
\mathscr{U}
be the following category: an object is a finite set
S
equipped with a weight function
S\to[n]
, and a morphism
f:S\to T
is a bijection such that weights are non-decreasing. A
module
over
\mathscr{U}
is a functor
\mathscr{U}\to\mathrm{Vec}
of finite length, meaning its value at any object of
\mathscr{U}
is a finite dimensional vector space and the functor is supported on finitely many objects of
\mathscr{U}
(up to isomorphism). A morphism of
\mathscr{U}
-modules is a natural transformation of functors, and the tensor product of
\mathscr{U}
-modules is defined by Day convolution (see §
2.4
for a precise statement).

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an equivalence among the following tensor categories:

•

the category of polynomial functors
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}\to\mathrm{Vec}
;

•

the category of polynomial representations of
\mathbf{G}
;

•

the category of
\mathscr{U}
-modules.

The case with
n=1
recovers Schur–Weyl duality in the sense of the equivalence among the categories of polynomial functors
\mathrm{Vec}\to\mathrm{Vec}
, polynomial representations of
\mathbf{GL}
, and finite length
\mathbf{FB}
-modules (see
[
16
]
for an overview of this equivalence). Our result can therefore be seen as a generalization of Schur–Weyl duality for flags.

By studying
\mathscr{U}
-modules, we are able to describe many aspects for the other models of the above category. In particular, the category is self-dual (despite not being semisimple), all objects are of finite length and have both finite projective and injective dimensions, and we give descriptions of all indecomposable projective and injective objects. We also are able to compute the first
Ext
groups among simple objects and describe left-exact tensor functors from the category.

1.2.1.
The infinite symmetric group

Let
\mathbf{FI}^{(n)}
denote the category of
[n]
-weighted finite sets and injections such that the weights are non-decreasing. When

\mathbf{FI}

\mathbf{FI}^{(n)}

Let
R=\mathbf{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]
denote the infinite polynomial ring; it has a natural action of the infinite symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. There has been recent interest in studying modules over
R
that are symmetric in the sense that they have a compatible action of

[

\mathbf{FI}^{(n)}
-modules to the category of modules over

\mathbf{FI}^{(n)}
-modules and symmetric
R/\mathfrak{h}_{n}

[
11

-modules in general.

1.2.2.
Representation stability

The idea behind representation stability is to consider sequences of groups that are naturally indexed in some way and to consider compatible representations of these groups. It has been especially fruitful to study these as functors out of categories, such as
\mathbf{FI}
-modules for the study of symmetric groups,
VI
- and
VIC
-modules for the study of general linear groups, and other variants of Schur functors for the study of orthogonal and symplectic groups
[
3
,
15
,
20
]
.

Representation stability can also be studied using representation theory of infinite rank groups, as done in
[
17
]
with representations of groups such as infinite rank orthogonal and sympletic groups. Such representations have also been studied from the perspective of locally finite Lie algebras
[
4
,
13
,
14
]
.

1.2.3.
Brauer categories and their representations

A useful perspective for studying representations of infinite rank groups is through representations of combinatorial or diagrammatic categories. In
[
17
]
, the authors use categories of various types of Brauer diagrams to study representations of different infinite rank groups, such as algebraic representations of
\mathbf{GL}
; many of the arguments and ideas in this paper are similar to those in that paper. The same authors have also begun to develop a more general theory for representations of Brauer categories in
[
21
]
.

The
n=2
case in this paper is particularly similar to the category of algebraic representations of
\mathbf{GL}
, which is equivalent to the category of modules over the upwards walled Brauer diagram category
[
17
, Corollary 3.2.12]
. Let
\mathbf{W}=\bigcup_{d\geq 0}\mathbf{C}^{d}
be the standard representation of
\mathbf{GL}
. Algebraic representations of
\mathbf{GL}
can also be described in terms of certain representations of
\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{W}\oplus\mathbf{W}_{*})
, where
\mathbf{W}_{*}
is the restricted dual. Then the Lie algebra corresponding to the unipotent radical of the subgroup of block upper triangular matrices can be identified with
\mathbf{W}\otimes\mathbf{W}
, while the corresponding Lie algebra in our case can be identified with
\mathbf{W}\otimes\mathbf{W}_{*}
.

1.2.4.
Twisted commutative algebras and their applications

Let
n=1

\mathbf{GL}
-algebras
. These algebras and their modules have been well-studied
[

18

2.
Representations of categories of weighted finite sets

In this section, we study representations of certain combinatorial categories of weighted finite sets. We refer the reader to
[
17
, §2.1]
and
[
21
, §3]
for further background on representations of combinatorial categories.

2.1.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

The category of
[n]
-weighted finite sets and upwards bijections
, denoted
\mathscr{U}
, is the following category:

•

The objects are
[n]
-weighted finite sets, i.e., finite sets
S=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}
where
S_{i}
has weight
i
; an object is denoted by the
n
-tuple
S=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
.

•

A morphism
S\to T
is a bijection
\varphi:S\to T
of sets such that weights do not decrease, i.e.,
\varphi(S_{i})\subset\bigsqcup_{j=i}^{n}T_{j}
.

For an
n
-tuple of nonnegative integers
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
, there is a weighted finite set
([a_{1}],\ldots,[a_{n}])
, where
[a_{i}]
has weight
i
. Every object of
\mathscr{U}
is isomorphic to a unique such object. We will use
\underline{a}
to denote both the tuple of integers and the corresponding weighted finite set; context should clarify any confusion.

There is also the category of
[n]
-weighted finite sets and downwards bijections
, denoted
\mathscr{D}
. Its definition is the same, except that a morphism is a bijection of finite sets such that weights do not increase, and so
\mathscr{D}
can naturally be identified with
\mathscr{U}^{\text{op}}
.

Both
\mathscr{U}
and
\mathscr{D}
have a symmetric monoidal structure
\amalg
defined by disjoint union: multiplication is defined by
S\amalg T=(S_{1}\sqcup T_{1},\ldots,S_{n}\sqcup T_{n}),
and the empty set
\varnothing
is the unit object. The automorphism group of an object
S
in either category is the product of symmetric groups
\text{Aut}(S_{1})\times\cdots\times\text{Aut}(S_{n})
.

A
\mathscr{U}
-module

M
is a functor
\mathscr{U}\to\mathrm{Vec}
that is supported on finitely many objects of
\mathscr{U}
up to isomorphism.
For a morphism
\varphi:S\to T
in
\mathscr{U}
, we write
\varphi_{*}:M(S)\to M(T)
to denote the linear map
M(\varphi)
. A
morphism
of
\mathscr{U}
-modules is a natural transformation of functors. Let
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
denote the category of
\mathscr{U}
-modules. This is an abelian category. For
\mathscr{U}
-modules
M
and
N
, we write
\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(M,N)
for the space of morphisms
M\to N
. We also write
\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(S,T)
for the space of morphisms
S\to T
for
S,T\in\mathscr{U}
.

Analogous definitions and notation hold for modules over
\mathscr{D}
.

2.2.
Duality

Given an
\mathscr{U}
-module
M
, we define the
\mathscr{D}
-module
M^{\vee}
by
M^{\vee}(S)=M(S)^{*}
, where
(-)^{*}
denotes the dual vector space. This induces an exact functor
(\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}})^{\text{op}}\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
, which gives an equivalence of categories via the canonical isomorphism
M\to(M^{\vee})^{\vee}
.

There is also a (covariant) equivalence of monoidal categories
\mathscr{U}\cong\mathscr{D}
given by changing the weights of elements from
i
to
n-i+1
:

\tau:S=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})\mapsto(S_{n},S_{n-1},\ldots,S_{1}).

A morphism
\varphi:S\to T
in
\mathscr{U}
also gives a morphism
(S_{n},\ldots,S_{1})\to(T_{n},\ldots,T_{1})
in
\mathscr{D}
, since now
\varphi
does not increase weights. For an
n
-tuple
\underline{a}
or
\underline{\lambda}
, we also use
\tau(\underline{a})
or
\tau(\underline{\lambda})
to denote the reverse tuple.

As a consequence, we have a covariant equivalence of categories
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
given by the pushforward and pullback of
\tau
:

\tau_{!}:\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}},\qquad\tau^{*}:\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}.

If
M
is a
\mathscr{U}
-module, then
\tau_{!}M
is defined by
\tau_{!}M(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})=M(S_{n},\ldots,S_{1})
. If
\varphi:S\to T
is a morphism in
\mathscr{D}
, then the linear map
\tau_{!}M(S)\to\tau_{!}M(T)
is defined to be the map
\varphi_{*}=M(\varphi):M(S_{n},\ldots,S_{1})\to M(T_{n},\ldots,T_{1})
. The pullback
\tau^{*}
is defined similarly.

Proposition 2.2
.

The functors
\tau_{!},\tau^{*}
give an equivalence of categories
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
. In particular, this category is self-dual.

2.3.
Simple, projective, and injective modules

For a partition
\lambda
with
\lvert\lambda\rvert=m
, let
M_{\lambda}
denote the Specht module corresponding to
\lambda
; this is a simple representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
. Then, the simple
\mathscr{U}
-modules and
\mathscr{D}
-modules are indexed by
n
-tuples of partitions
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{n})
, and are defined by

S\mapsto\begin{cases}M_{\lambda^{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes M_{\lambda^{n}}&\quad\text{if $\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert=\lvert S_{i}\rvert$ for all $i$,}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We let
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
denote this module, as well as the corresponding irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}=\mathfrak{S}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{S}_{a_{n}}
, where
a_{i}=\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert
.

We define the
\mathscr{U}
-module
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}
, called the
principal projective
at
\underline{a}
, by
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}(T)=\mathbf{C}[\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\underline{a},T)].

We define the
\mathscr{U}
-module
\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}
, called the
principal injective
at
\underline{a}
, by
\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}(T)=\mathbf{C}[\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(T,\underline{a})]^{*}.

Let
M
be a
\mathscr{U}
-module. By
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have that

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}},M)=M(\underline{a}),\qquad\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(M,\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}})=M(\underline{a})^{*}.

In particular, the principal projectives are indeed projective objects, and the principal injectives are injective objects.

We have that
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
acts on
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}
and
\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}
by
\mathscr{U}
-module automorphisms, and so for a tuple of partitions
\underline{\lambda}
with
\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert=a_{i}
for all
i
, we have
\mathscr{U}
-modules
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}
and
\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
defined to be the
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
-isotypic pieces of
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}
and
\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}
:

\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}),\qquad\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}).

Proposition 2.3
.

The module
\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is an indecomposable injective, and its socle is
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is a direct summand of the injective
\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}
, and so it is injective as well.

For a
\mathscr{U}
-module
M
, we have that

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(M,\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}})=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(M(\underline{a}),\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}^{*}).

In particular, taking
M=\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
, we see that
\text{End}_{\mathscr{U}}(\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}})=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}^{*})=\mathbf{C},

where the last equality comes from working over
\mathbf{C}
so Specht moduls are self-dual. We therefore have that there are no nontrivial idempotent endomorphisms of
\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
, and so it is indecomposable.

To show that
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is the socle, it suffices to note that
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}\subset\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
; this can be seen by

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}})=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}^{*})=\mathbf{C}.

Then,
\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is the injective hull of
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

The module
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is an indecomposable projective, and it is the projective cover of
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

It is a direct summand of the projective
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}
, and so it is projective as well. We have that

\text{End}_{\mathscr{U}}(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}})=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}(\underline{a}))=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}})=\mathbf{C},

and so
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is indecomposable.

There is a surjection
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}\to\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
, since

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}})=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}})=\mathbf{C}.

Therefore, by
[
7
, Lemma 3.6]
, the surjection is a projective cover.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

(1)

The
\mathscr{U}
-modules
\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}
form a complete irredundant set of indecomposable injectives in
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
, and the
\mathscr{U}
-modules
\textbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}
form a complete irredundant set of indecomposable projectives in
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
.

(2)

Every object in
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
has finite injective and projective dimension.

Proof.

(1) This follows from all simples being of the form
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
.

(2) Suppose
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{1}\rvert,\ldots,\lvert\lambda^{n}\rvert)
.
The quotient
\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}}/\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is supported on weighted finite sets
\underline{b}
such that
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
and
\underline{b}>\underline{a}
under the dominance order, and the kernel of the surjection
\textbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}\to\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is supported on weighted finite sets
\underline{c}
such that
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{c}\rvert
and
\underline{c}<\underline{a}
. There are only finitely many such sets
\underline{b}
and
\underline{c}
.
Thus, any
\mathscr{U}
-module has a finite injective resolution given by finite direct sums of indecomposable injectives, and a finite projective resolution given by finite direct sums of indecomposable projectives.
∎

The above definitions and results also hold for
\mathscr{D}
-modules; we denote the corresponding projective modules by
\textbf{Q}_{\underline{a}},\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\lambda}}
, and the injective modules by
\textbf{J}_{\underline{a}},\textbf{J}_{\underline{\lambda}}
. Under the equivalence of categories
(\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}})^{\text{op}}\cong\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
, we have that
(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}})^{\vee}=\textbf{J}_{\underline{a}}
and
(\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}})^{\vee}=\textbf{Q}_{\underline{a}}
. Under the equivalence of categories
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
, we have

\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}=\textbf{Q}_{\tau(\underline{a})}
and
\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}=\textbf{J}_{\tau(\underline{a})}.
The analogous identifications hold for the indecomposable injective and projective objects.

2.4.
Tensor products

Using the monoidal structure
\amalg
of
\mathscr{U}
, one can define a tensor product of
\mathscr{U}
-modules (and analogously of
\mathscr{D}
-modules) as follows. Let
M,N
be
\mathscr{U}
-modules. We define
M\otimes N
by

(1)

(M\otimes N)(U)=\bigoplus_{U=S\amalg T}M(S)\otimes_{\mathbf{C}}N(T),

where
U,S,T
are weighted sets, and the weights of
S,T
are induced from those of
U
.

The following lemma shows how this tensor product works on principal projectives. For
\underline{a},\underline{b}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
, let
\underline{a+b}=(a_{1}+b_{1},\ldots,a_{n}+b_{n})
. Note that this is isomorphic to
\underline{a}\amalg\underline{b}
as weighted sets.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\underline{a},\underline{b}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
, there is a natural isomorphism
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{\underline{b}}\cong\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a+b}}
.

Proof.

Let
U\in\mathscr{U}
be a weighted finite set. Given a morphim
\varphi:\underline{a+b}\to U
in
\mathscr{U}
, one obtains morphisms
\varphi_{1}:\underline{a}\to S
,
\varphi_{2}:\underline{b}\to U\setminus S
by restricting
\varphi
via the natural identification
\underline{a+b}=\underline{a}\amalg\underline{b}
. On the other hand, given morphisms
\psi_{1}:\underline{a}\to S
,
\psi_{2}:\underline{b}\to T
such that
S\amalg T=U
, one obtains a unique morphism
\psi:\underline{a}\amalg\underline{b}\to S\amalg T=U
. In particular, we have

\mathbf{C}[\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\underline{a+b},U)]=\bigoplus_{U=S\amalg T}\mathbf{C}[\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\underline{a},S)]\otimes\mathbf{C}[\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\underline{b},T)]

as vector spaces.

Furthermore, this identification is natural in the sense that if
\sigma:U\to V
is a morphism in
\mathscr{U}
, then the maps
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a+b}}(U)\to\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a+b}}(V)
and
(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{\underline{b}})(U)\to(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{\underline{b}})(V)
agree. Thus, we have the desired isomorphism of
\mathscr{U}
-modules.
∎

By (
1
), this tensor product is exact in each variable. In particular, it is right-exact, and so since it satisfies the property on tensor products of principal projectives in Lemma
2.6
, this tensor product agrees with
Day convolution
, or the
convolution tensor product
defined in
[
17
, (2.1.14)]
. Therefore, this tensor product naturally gives
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
the structure of a tensor category with unit object
\mathbf{P}_{\varnothing}
. Note also that the equivalence
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
given by
\tau
is one of tensor categories.

2.5.
Structured
Hom
spaces and tensor products

We now give two constructions using
\mathscr{U}
-modules and
\mathscr{D}
-modules. They will be used in §
4
to describe the equivalence of categories
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
, as well as to give a universal property for this category.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an abelian category, and let
\mathcal{K}
be a functor
\mathscr{U}\to\mathscr{C}
. For a
\mathscr{U}
-module
M
, we define an object
\text{Hom}(M,\mathcal{K})
of
\mathscr{C}
, called the
structured Hom space
, as

\text{Hom}(M,\mathcal{K})=\lim_{f:S\to T}\text{Hom}(M(S),\mathcal{K}(S)),

where the inverse limit is over morphisms in
\mathscr{U}
. Note that this is a finite limit since
M
is of finite length and
\mathscr{U}
decomposes into finite directed categories, and so this Hom space always exists. More explicitly, the object in
\mathscr{C}
is defined by the following mapping property. To give a morphism
f:A\to\text{Hom}(M,\mathcal{K})
in
\mathscr{C}
is the same as giving morphisms
f_{S}:A\to\text{Hom}(M(S),\mathcal{K}(S))
for all
S\in\mathscr{U}
such that for any
x\in A
and morphism
\alpha:S\to T
in
\mathscr{U}
, we have that the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{M(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{S}(x)}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{*}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{K}(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{*}}
\textstyle{M(T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{T}(x)}
\textstyle{\mathcal{K}(T)}

The next result follows immediately from the universal property.

Lemma 2.7
.

We have an identification
\text{Hom}(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}},\mathcal{K})=\mathcal{K}(\underline{a}).

Another useful property regarding this construction is the following. There is a functor
\mathscr{C}\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
defined by

N\mapsto(S\mapsto\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(N,\mathcal{K}(S));

we denote the resulting
\mathscr{U}
-module by
\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(N,\mathcal{K})
.
Then by
[
17
, Proposition 2.1.10]
, the functor
M\mapsto\text{Hom}(M,\mathcal{K})
is adjoint on the right to this functor:

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(M,\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(N,\mathcal{K}))=\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(N,\text{Hom}(M,\mathcal{K})).

There is a covariant version of the structured Hom construction. If
M
is a
\mathscr{D}
-module, then we define the
structured tensor product

M\odot\mathcal{K}
to be

M\odot\mathcal{K}=\text{Hom}(M^{\vee},\mathcal{K}).

It can also be defined by an inverse limit; see
[
17
, (2.1.9)]
. By
[
17
, Proposition 2.1.16]
, we have that the functors

\displaystyle\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\to\mathscr{C}:

\displaystyle\quad M\mapsto\text{Hom}(M,\mathcal{K}),

\displaystyle\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\mathscr{C}:

\displaystyle\quad N\mapsto N\odot\mathcal{K}

are both tensor functors.

3.
Representations of infinite rank parabolic subgroups

In this section, we analyze the category
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
of polynomial representations of
\mathbf{G}
.
Such representations can be analyzed using representation theory of the Levi subgroup
L
of
\mathbf{G}
, since
\textbf{L}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{GL}
.
Many of the arguments in this subsection are similar to those in
[
17
]
, e.g., their argument that the category of polynomial represetnations of the infinite orthogonal group is equivalent to the upwards Brauer category in
[
17
, §4.2]
.

Recall that
\mathbf{V}
is the infinite dimensional vector space with the flag
0=\mathbf{V}_{0}\subset\mathbf{V}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbf{V}_{n}=\mathbf{V}
where
\mathbf{V}_{i}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1}
is also infinite dimensional; this is the standard representation of
\mathbf{G}
. We let
\mathbf{V}_{(i)}
denote the
i^{\text{th}}
graded piece
\mathbf{V}_{i}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1}
.

3.1.
Action of the endomorphism monoid

Let
V
be a polynomial representation of
\mathbf{G}
, and let
\rho:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{GL}(V)
denote the map giving the
\mathbf{G}
-action. Then, after picking a basis for
V
, the entries of
\rho
can be expressed in terms of polynomials.

Let
\text{End}(\mathbf{V})
denote the monoid of endomorphisms of
\mathbf{V}
preserving the flag structure, so an element is a linear map
f:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}
such that
f(\mathbf{V}_{i})\subset\mathbf{V}_{i}
. Then, any polynomial representation
V
of
\mathbf{G}
also has an action of
\text{End}(\mathbf{V})
via the polynomial entries of the map
\rho
. Furthermore, if
V\to W
is a map of polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representations, then the map is equivariant with respect to the
\text{End}(\mathbf{V})
-actions on
V
and
W
.

3.2.
Invariants and specialization

Let
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
. Let
H_{\underline{a}}
denote the subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
consisting of block diagonal matrices such that the
(i,i)
block is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&*\end{pmatrix},

where the top left block is
a_{i}\times a_{i}
and the bottom right block is
(\infty-a_{i})\times(\infty-a_{i})
. Note that
H_{\underline{a}}\cong\textbf{L}
. Let
G_{\underline{a}}
denote the subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
consisting of matrices where the
(i,j)
block is of the form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}*&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\quad\text{if $i=j$},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}*&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{if $i<j$},

where the top left block is
a_{i}\times a_{j}
and the bottom right block is
(\infty-a_{i})\times(\infty-a_{j})
in both cases.
We have that
G_{\underline{a}}
commutes with
H_{\underline{a}}
, and so if
V\in\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
, then
V^{H_{\underline{a}}}
is a representation of
G_{\underline{a}}
. Let
\text{Rep}(G_{\underline{a}})
denote the category of polynomial representations of
G_{\underline{a}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Taking
H_{\underline{a}}
-invariants induces a tensor functor
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})\to\text{Rep}(G_{\underline{a}})
.

Proof.

For
n=1
, this follows from the fact that taking such invariants for a polynomial
\mathbf{GL}
-representation is a tensor functor
[
17
, Proposition 3.4.4]
. For general
n
, we have that a polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representation is also a polynomial
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{GL}
-representation by restricting to the action of the Levi subgroup
L
. The result then follows by considering taking
H_{\underline{a}}
-invariants as a functor from the
n
-fold product of the category of polynomial
\mathbf{GL}
-representations.
∎

3.3.
Principal objects

For an
n
-tuple
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
, we define the polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representations
T_{\underline{a}}
and
U_{\underline{a}}
by

\displaystyle T_{\underline{a}}

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1})^{\otimes a_{i}},\qquad U_{\underline{a}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\otimes a_{i}}.

After showing the equivalence of categories in §
4
, it will follow that
T_{\underline{a}}
is injective and
U_{\underline{a}}
is projective.

For a morphism
\varphi:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
in
\mathscr{U}
, there is a corresponding map of
\mathbf{G}
-representations
T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}}
defined as follows. A tensor factor
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1}
of
T_{\underline{a}}
corresponds to an element
x
of weight
i
in
\underline{a}
, and
\varphi(x)
has weight
j
in
\underline{b}
for some
j\geq i
. Then surject
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1}
onto the tensor factor
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{j-1}
corresponding to
\varphi(x)
.

Now, define the polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representation
K_{\underline{a}}
by

K_{\underline{a}}=\bigcap\ker(T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}}),

where the intersection ranges over all non-isomorphisms in
\mathscr{U}
.
This intersection is finite, as the only such morphisms are if
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
with
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
.

(1)

The
\mathscr{U}
-module given by
\underline{b}\mapsto\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{G}}(T_{\underline{a}},T_{\underline{b}})
is equal to
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}=(\textbf{J}_{\underline{a}})^{\vee}
. In particular,
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{G}}(T_{\underline{a}},T_{\underline{b}})\neq 0
if and only if
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
and
\underline{a}\geq\underline{b}
. When
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
,

\dim(\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{G}}(T_{\underline{a}},T_{\underline{b}}))=b_{1}!b_{2}!\cdots b_{n}!\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i})-(b_{1}+\cdots+b_{i-1})}{b_{i}}.

(2)

K_{\underline{a}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{V}_{(i)}^{\otimes a_{i}}
.

Proof.

(1) We first show that
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{G}}(T_{\underline{a}},T_{\underline{b}})=\mathbf{C}[\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(\underline{a},\underline{b})]
. Let
\{e_{i,j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be a basis for
\mathbf{V}_{(i)}
. Then
T_{\underline{a}}
is generated as a
\mathbf{G}
-module by
x=e_{n,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n,\lvert\underline{a}\rvert}
; this element has weight
(0^{n-1},1^{\lvert\underline{a}\rvert})
under the
L
-action. If
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert\neq\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
, then there is no weight space in
T_{\underline{b}}
of this weight, and so there are no nonzero maps
T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}}
.

Now assume
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
with
\underline{a}\lneq\underline{b}
. Suppose for contradiction
\varphi:T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}}
is a nonzero map. Up to a scalar, the image of
x
must be of the form
\varphi(x)=e_{n,\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n,\sigma^{-1}(\lvert\underline{a}\rvert)}
, where
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\lvert\underline{a}\rvert}
. Then for some
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq n
, there exists
k
with

b_{1}+\cdots+b_{i-1}<k\leq b_{1}+\cdots+b_{i},\qquad a_{1}+\cdots+a_{j-1}<\sigma^{-1}(k)\leq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{j}.

Let
g\in\mathbf{G}
be the element with
I
’s along the diagonal, and
e_{1,\sigma^{-1}(k)}
in the
(i,n)
-block. Then
gx-x=0
in
T_{\underline{a}}
, while
\varphi(gx-x)=g\varphi(x)-\varphi(x)\neq 0
in
T_{\underline{b}}
, since
g(e_{n,\sigma^{-1}(k)})=e_{i,1}+e_{n,\sigma^{-1}(k)}
. Thus, such a nonzero
\varphi
cannot exist.

Now suppose
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
with
\underline{a}\geq\underline{b}
. A nonzero map
\varphi:T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}}
must send
x
to an element of the form (up to scalar)
e_{n,\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n,\sigma^{-1}(\lvert\underline{a}\rvert)}
for some
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\lvert\underline{a}\rvert}
. In addition, we must have that
\sigma^{-1}([b_{1}+\cdots+b_{i}])\subset[a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}]
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. This is because there are no nonzero maps
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{j-1}\to\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1}
for
i<j
by the above argument. Thus,
\varphi
is determined by the morphism
\sigma:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
in
\mathscr{U}
. Furthermore, if
\pi:\underline{b}\to\underline{c}
is a morphism in
\mathscr{U}
, then
\pi_{*}\circ\varphi:T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{c}}
corresponds to the morphism
\pi\circ\sigma
in
\mathscr{U}
. This identifies the
\mathscr{U}
-module
\underline{b}\mapsto\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{G}}(T_{\underline{a}},T_{\underline{b}})
with the principal projective
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}
.

(2) Let
j\in[n-1]
, and let
\underline{b}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1},0,a_{j}+a_{j+1},a_{j+2},\ldots,a_{n})
. Then the kernel of the surjection
\varphi_{j}:T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}}
is

\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{j-1}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1})^{\otimes a_{i}}\right)\otimes\mathbf{V}_{(j)}^{a_{j}}\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=j+1}^{n}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1})^{\otimes a_{i}}\right).

Thus,
K_{\underline{a}}\subset\bigcap_{j}\ker\varphi_{j}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{V}_{(i)}^{\otimes a_{i}}
.

The vector

v=e_{1,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{1,a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n,a_{n}}

generates
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{V}_{(i)}^{\otimes a_{i}}
as a
\mathbf{G}
-module, and
v
has weight
(1^{a_{1}},\ldots,1^{a_{n}})
. For any
T_{\underline{b}}
with
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
, there is no weight space with this weight, so
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{V}_{(i)}^{\otimes a_{i}}\subset K_{\underline{a}}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
\underline{a}>\underline{b}>\underline{c}
with
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert=\lvert\underline{c}\rvert
, then any morphism
f:\underline{a}\to\underline{c}
in
\mathscr{U}
factors through a morphism
g:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
. In particular,
\ker(T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}})\subset\ker(T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{c}})
, and so
K_{\underline{a}}
can equivalently be given by
\bigcap\ker(T_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{b}})
, where
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
is a cover relation in the dominance order.

3.4.
Simple representations

Recall that
\textbf{L}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{GL}
denotes the Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
, and let
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{G}/\textbf{L}
denote the unipotent radical. There is a surjection of groups
\mathbf{G}\to\textbf{L}
, so every simple
L
-module pulls back to a simple
\mathbf{G}
-module. The simple
L
-modules are indexed by tuples of partitions
\underline{\lambda}
, and we denote them by
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
:

S_{\underline{\lambda}}=\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{1}}(\mathbf{V}_{(1)})\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{n}}(\mathbf{V}_{(n)}),

where
\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(-)
denotes the Schur functor corresponding to the partition
\lambda^{i}
. We now show that these are precisely the simple polynomial representations of
\mathbf{G}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Every simple object of
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
is of the form
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that every simple constituent of
T_{\underline{a}}
is of the form
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
. We have an exact sequence

(2)

0\to K_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{a}}\to\bigoplus_{\underline{a}\to\underline{b}}T_{\underline{b}},

where the direct sum is over all non-isomorphisms in
\mathscr{U}
with
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
and
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
a cover relation.
By Lemma
3.2
, the action of
\mathbf{G}
on
K_{\underline{a}}
is given by the action of
L
. The simple constituents of
K_{\underline{a}}
are therefore given by its decomposition as a
L
-module, so they are of the form
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
.

Among the set
\{\underline{b}:\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert\}
, the tuple
(0,\ldots,0,\lvert\underline{a}\rvert)
is minimal in the dominance order, and
T_{(0,\ldots,0,\lvert\underline{a}\rvert)}=(\mathbf{V}_{(n)})^{\otimes\lvert\underline{a}\rvert}
has simple factors given by
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{V}_{(n)})
. Thus, by induction, the simple constituents of
T_{\underline{a}}
are of the form
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
as well.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

A polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representation
V
is semisimple if and only if
\mathbf{U}
acts trivially on
V
.

Proof.

If
V
is semisimple, then since
\mathbf{U}
acts trivially on each simple decomposition factor of
V
,
\mathbf{U}
acts trivially on
V
. If
\mathbf{U}
acts trivially on
V
, then the action of
\mathbf{G}
is given by the action of
L
, and so
V
is semisimple.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The socle of
T_{\underline{a}}
is
K_{\underline{a}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,
K_{\underline{a}}
is semisimple. Any submodule of
T_{\underline{a}}
properly containing
K_{\underline{a}}
has a nontrivial
\mathbf{U}
-action, and so by Corollary
3.5
,
K_{\underline{a}}
is the maximal semisimple submodule of
T_{\underline{a}}
.
∎

3.5.
Injectives and projectives

Let
\underline{\lambda}
be an
n
-tuple of partitions and let
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{1}\rvert,\ldots,\lvert\lambda^{n}\rvert)
.
Define the
\mathbf{G}
-module
T_{\underline{\lambda}}
by
T_{\underline{\lambda}}=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},T_{\underline{a}}).

This is therefore equal to

T_{\underline{\lambda}}=\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{1}}(\mathbf{V})\otimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{2}}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{n}}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-1}).

Similarly, define the
\mathbf{G}
-module
U_{\underline{\lambda}}
by
U_{\underline{\lambda}}=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},U_{\underline{a}})
, so it is equal to

U_{\underline{\lambda}}=\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{1}}(\mathbf{V}_{1})\otimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{2}}(\mathbf{V}_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{n}}(\mathbf{V}_{n}).

It will follow from the equivalence of categories
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})\cong\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
that the
T_{\underline{\lambda}}
’s constitute a complete irredundant set of indecomposable injectives and that the
U_{\underline{\lambda}}
’s constitute a complete irredundant set of indecomposable projectives.

4.
Equivalence of the categories

In this section, we prove the main theorem (Theorem
1.1
) on the equivalence of tensor categories. We then give some consequences of the equivalence, including computing
\text{Ext}^{1}
groups among the simple objects and describing a universal property.

4.1.
Equivalence between
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
and
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}

Recall the representations
T_{\underline{a}}\in\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
defined in §
3.3
. They define a functor
\mathscr{T}:\mathscr{U}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
given by
\underline{a}\mapsto T_{\underline{a}}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have a covariant equivalence of tensor categories
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

We first show that
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
is covariantly equivalent to
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
. Recall from §
2.5
that the structured tensor product
M\mapsto M\odot\mathscr{T}
defines a tensor functor
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
. By
[
17
, Corollary 2.1.12]
, to show that this functor is an equivalence, it suffices to prove the following:

(1)

For any
\underline{\lambda}
with
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
, the polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representation
\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},K_{\underline{a}})
is simple.

(2)

For every simple object
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
of
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
, there exists a unique tuple
\underline{a}
such that
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{G}}(S_{\underline{\lambda}},T_{\underline{a}})
is nonzero.

By Lemma
3.2
,

\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},K_{\underline{a}})=\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{1}}(\mathbf{V}_{(1)})\otimes\cdots\otimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{n}}(\mathbf{V}_{(n)})=S_{\underline{\lambda}}.

By Lemma
3.6
,
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
is a submodule of
T_{\underline{a}}
if and only if
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{1}\rvert,\ldots,\lvert\lambda^{n}\rvert)
.

The covariant equivalence
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
now follows from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

We explicitly describe the equivalence using the constructions from §
2.5
. The equivalence with
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
is given by the functors

\displaystyle\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G}):M\mapsto\text{Hom}(M^{\vee},\mathscr{T})=M\odot\mathscr{T},

\displaystyle\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}:V\mapsto\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{G}}(V,\mathscr{T})^{\vee}.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
and
\mathcal{G}:\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
be the composition of these functors with
\tau_{!}
and
\tau^{*}
respectively. Then
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
give the equivalence
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\cong\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
e_{i}
denote the tuple with
1
in the
i^{\text{th}}
position and
0
’s elsewhere. Then there is a natural isomorphism
\text{Hom}(\textbf{I}_{e_{i}},\mathscr{T})=\mathbf{V}_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be an object of
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
. By the mapping property, giving a map
V\to\text{Hom}(\textbf{I}_{e_{i}},\mathscr{T})
is the same as giving maps
f_{j}:V\to\text{Hom}(\textbf{I}_{e_{i}}(e_{j}),\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{j-1})
for
j=1,\ldots,n
such that the necessary relations hold. In particular, the maps
f_{j}
are determined by
f_{1}
, and since
\textbf{I}_{e_{i}}(e_{j})=0
for
j>i
, giving the map
f_{1}
is the same as giving a map
V\to\ker(\mathbf{V}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{i})=\mathbf{V}_{i}
. Thus,
\mathbf{V}_{i}
satisfies the same universal property as
\text{Hom}(\textbf{I}_{e_{i}},\mathscr{T})
, which gives the desired identification.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
\underline{a},\underline{\lambda}
, we have

\mathcal{F}(\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}})=T_{\tau(\underline{a})},\quad\mathcal{F}(\textbf{I}_{\underline{\lambda}})=T_{\tau(\underline{\lambda})},\quad\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}})=U_{\tau(\underline{a})},\quad\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{\lambda}})=U_{\tau(\underline{\lambda})}.

Proof.

We have that
\mathcal{F}(\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}})=\text{Hom}(\mathbf{P}_{\tau(\underline{a})},\mathscr{T})=\mathscr{T}(\tau(\underline{a}))=T_{\tau(\underline{a})}
by Lemma
2.7
. We also have that
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P}_{e_{i}})=\text{Hom}(\textbf{I}_{e_{n-i+1}},\mathscr{T})=\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1}
by Lemma
4.2
. Then since
\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}_{e_{j}}^{\otimes a_{j}}

and
\mathcal{F}
is a tensor functor, we see that
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P}_{\underline{a}})=U_{\tau(\underline{a})}
.

The other equalities follow from taking
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
-isotypic pieces of
\textbf{I}_{\underline{a}}
and
\mathcal{F}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}})=S_{\tau(\underline{\lambda})}
-isotypic pieces of
T_{\tau(\underline{\lambda})}
, and similarly for the projective objects.
∎

4.2.
Category of polynomial functors on flags

Let
\mathscr{A}
denote the category of polynomial functors
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}\to\mathrm{Vec}
. In this subsection, we show that objects of
\mathscr{A}
correspond to sequences of representations of parabolic subgroups of general linear groups that stabilize. This will be used to show the equivalence
\mathscr{A}\cong\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
.

Suppose that
F\in\mathscr{A}
. For any tuple
\underline{a}
of nonnegative integers, let
\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}}
denote the following flag:

0\subset\mathbf{C}^{a_{1}}\subset\mathbf{C}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbf{C}^{\lvert\underline{a}\rvert}.

Note that every object in
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}
is isomorphic to a unique such
\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}}
. We have that
F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})
is a representation of the parabolic subgroup
G_{\underline{a}}\subset\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{C}^{\lvert\underline{a}\rvert})
of
n\times n
block upper triangular matrices, where the
(i,j)
-block has dimension
a_{i}\times a_{j}
. Note that this group is isomorphic to the group
G_{\underline{a}}
defined in §
3.2
. Furthermore, if
F\to F^{\prime}
is a morphism in
\mathscr{A}
, then the linear map
F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})\to F^{\prime}(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})
is
G_{\underline{a}}
-equivariant.

If
\underline{a},\underline{b}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
with
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
i
, there is a natural morphism
\iota:\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}}\to\mathbf{C}^{\underline{b}}
in
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}
given by inclusion on each graded piece of the flags. Notice that this morphism splits, and so if
F\in\mathscr{A}
, then
F(\iota):F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})\to F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{b}})
is an injective
G_{\underline{a}}
-equivariant linear map, where
G_{\underline{a}}\subset G_{\underline{b}}
is naturally a subgroup via
\iota
. Such maps form a directed system, and so one can consider the direct limit
\varinjlim F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})
. For any
j\gg 0
, there exists one of these natural injections
\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}}\to\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})}
, where
(j^{n})=(j,\ldots,j)
; therefore, one can equivalently consider the direct limit
\varinjlim F(\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
F\in\mathscr{A}
, then
\varinjlim F(\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})})
is a polynomial representation of
\mathbf{G}
.

Proof.

Recall that
F_{a}\in\mathscr{A}
is the functor
\{V_{i}\}\mapsto V^{\otimes a}
. We have that
\varinjlim F_{a}(\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})})\cong\mathbf{V}^{\otimes a}
. The result then follows from direct limits being exact.
∎

4.3.
Equivalence between
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
and
\mathscr{A}

We now show that
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})\cong\mathscr{A}
. By Lemma
4.4
, there is a functor
\Phi:\mathscr{A}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
given by
F\mapsto\varinjlim F(\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})})
. Note that
\Phi
is a tensor functor since it is defined using a direct limit.

We now define a functor
\Psi:\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})\to\mathscr{A}
. Recall the subgroups
H_{\underline{a}}
and
G_{\underline{a}}
of
\mathbf{G}
defined in §
3.2
.
For a polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representation
V
, define
\Psi(V)(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})=V^{H_{\underline{a}}}
to be the
H_{\underline{a}}
-invariants of
V
. This is a representation of
G_{\underline{a}}
, since
G_{\underline{a}}
commutes with
H_{\underline{a}}
. As with
\Phi
, this functor takes polynomial
\mathbf{G}
-representations to polynomial functors. Suppose
f:\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}}\to\mathbf{C}^{\underline{b}}
is a morphism in
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}
. There is an element
g_{f}
in
\text{End}(\mathbf{V})
extending
f
such that
g_{f}
restricts to
f
on
\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}}
and such that for any
h\in H_{\underline{b}}
, there exists an
h^{\prime}\in H_{\underline{a}}
such that the action of
h\circ g_{f}
on the standard representation
\mathbf{V}
is equal to the action of
g_{f}\circ h^{\prime}
. This shows that if
V
is a polynomial representation of
\mathbf{G}
, the image of
V^{H_{\underline{a}}}
from the induced action of
g_{f}
on
V
is contained in
V^{H_{\underline{b}}}
. Define the morphism
\Psi(V)(f)
in
\mathrm{Vec}
by the action of
g_{f}
on
V
. By Lemma
3.1
,
\Psi
is a tensor functor.

Lemma 4.5
.

(\Phi,\Psi)
is an adjoint pair and the counit
\Phi\Psi\to\text{id}
is an equality.

Proof.

Let
F
be an object of
\mathscr{A}
, and
V
an object of
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
. Suppose
f:\Phi(F)\to V
is a
\mathbf{G}
-equivariant map. For any
\underline{a}
, there is a canonical
G_{\underline{a}}
-equivariant map
F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})\to\Phi(F)
, and composing with
f
and then taking
H_{\underline{a}}
-invariants induces a
G_{\underline{a}}
-equivariant map
f_{\underline{a}}:F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})\to V^{H_{\underline{a}}}
. These maps are compatible with all maps induced by morphisms in
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}
since
f
is equivariant under the action of
\text{End}(\mathbf{V})
. We therefore obtain a morphism
F\to\Psi(V)
in
\mathscr{A}
.

Conversely, suppose
g:F\to\Psi(V)
is a map in
\mathscr{A}
, and apply
\Phi
to obtain
\Phi(g):\Phi(F)\to\Phi(\Psi(V))
. Note that
\Phi(\Psi(V))=V
, since every element of
V
is invariant under
H_{(j^{n})}
for
j\gg 0
. This gives a map
\Phi(F)\to V
, and one can check that this construction is inverse to the one above. The claim that the counit is an equality also follows.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

The functors
\Phi,\Psi
give an equivalence of tensor categories
\mathscr{A}\cong\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that both
\Phi,\Psi
are fully faithful. The isomorphism
\Phi\Psi\to\text{id}
shows that
\Psi
is fully faithful.

We claim
\Phi
is faithful. Fix
\underline{a}
, and let
F\in\mathscr{A}
. Then for any
j\gg 0
, there is a natural morphism
\iota_{j}:\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}}\to\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})}
in
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}
that splits, and so
F(\iota_{j})
is injective. Furthermore, the
\iota_{j}
maps are equivariant with the inclusions
\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})}\to\mathbf{C}^{((j+1)^{n})}
. We therefore have an injection
F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})\to\Phi(F)=\varinjlim F(\mathbf{C}^{(j^{n})})
.
Now, suppose
f:F\to G
is a morphism in
\mathscr{A}
. We then obtain the following commutative square with vertical maps that are injections:

\textstyle{F(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})}
\textstyle{G(\mathbf{C}^{\underline{a}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Phi(F)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi(f)}
\textstyle{\Phi(G)}

Thus, if
\Phi(f)=0
, then
f=0
and so
\Phi
is faithful.

We now show
\Phi
is full. Suppose
f:M\to N
is a map in
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
, and let
g=\Psi(f)
. Then
\Phi(g)=\Phi\Psi(f)=f
, since
\Phi\Psi=\text{id}
.
∎

4.4.
Consequences of the equivalence

We now list some immediate consequences of the equivalence of categories.

Proposition 4.7
.

Consider the category
\mathscr{A}
of polynomial functors
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}\to\mathrm{Vec}
. Let
\{V_{i}\}
denote the object
V
in
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}
with flag
0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots\subset V_{n}=V
.

(1)

The functor
F_{\underline{\lambda}}:\{V_{i}\}\mapsto\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(V_{i}/V_{i-1})
is simple, and such functors constitute a complete irredundant set of simple objects in
\mathscr{A}
.

(2)

The injective envelope and projective cover of
F_{\underline{\lambda}}
are

\{V_{i}\}\mapsto\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(V_{n}/V_{i-1}),\qquad\{V_{i}\}\mapsto\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(V_{i}).

(3)

Polynomial functors
\mathrm{Flag}_{n}\to\mathrm{Vec}
are of finite length, and have finite injective and projective dimensions.

(4)

\mathscr{A}
is self-dual.

Proposition 4.8
.

Consider the category
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
of polynomial representations of
\mathbf{G}
.

(1)

The
\mathbf{G}
-modules
T_{\underline{a}}
and
T_{\underline{\lambda}}
are injective.

(2)

T_{\underline{\lambda}}
is the injective envelope of
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
, and the
T_{\underline{\lambda}}
’s constitute a complete irredundant set of indecomposable injective objects in
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
.

(3)

The
\mathbf{G}
-modules
U_{\underline{a}}
and
U_{\underline{\lambda}}
are projective.

(4)

U_{\underline{\lambda}}
is the projective cover of
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
, and the
U_{\underline{\lambda}}
’s constitute a complete irredundant set of indecomposable projective objects in
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
.

(5)

All objects are of finite length, and have finite injective and projective dimensions.

(6)

Tensor powers of the standard representation
\mathbf{V}^{\otimes a}
are both projective and injective.

(7)

The category
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
is self-dual.

4.5.
Extensions of simples

In this subsection, we compute the first Ext groups between simples objects.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\underline{\lambda},\underline{\mu}
be tuples of partitions with
\underline{a}=(\lvert\mu^{i}\rvert)
and
\underline{b}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
.
If
\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{G}}(S_{\underline{\lambda}},S_{\underline{\mu}})\neq 0
, then it must be that
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
and
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
is a cover relation.

Proof.

Recall from (
2
) that we have the exact sequence

0\to K_{\underline{a}}\to T_{\underline{a}}\to\bigoplus_{f:\underline{a}\to\underline{c}}T_{\underline{c}},

where
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{c}\rvert
with
\underline{a}>\underline{c}
a cover relation. Then, take the
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\mu}}
-isotypic piece of this sequence to obtain the exact sequence
0\to S_{\underline{\mu}}\to T_{\underline{\mu}}\to T
, where
T
is a summand of
\bigoplus T_{\underline{c}}
. In particular,
T
is injective and if
T_{\underline{\lambda}}
is a summand of
T
, then it must be that
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
and
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
is a cover relation.
∎

We now give the formula for the first Ext groups by examining projective
\mathscr{D}
-modules. For partitions
\lambda,\mu
, we say that
\lambda/\mu\in\text{HS}_{1}
if
\mu\subset\lambda
as Young diagrams and their difference is a single box.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\underline{\lambda},\underline{\mu}
be tuples of partitions with
\underline{a}=(\lvert\mu^{i}\rvert)
and
\underline{b}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
.
We have
\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{G}}(S_{\underline{\lambda}},S_{\underline{\mu}})\neq 0
if and only if

(1)

\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
and
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
is a cover relation where
a_{i}-1=b_{i}
,

(2)

\mu^{j}=\lambda^{j}
for
j\neq i,i+1
, and

(3)

\mu^{i}/\lambda^{i}
and
\lambda^{i+1}/\mu^{i+1}
are both in
\text{HS}_{1}
.

In this case, its dimension is
1
.

Proof.

First suppose
\underline{\lambda},\underline{\mu}
satisfy the three conditions. Consider the simple
\mathscr{D}
-module
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
, which has projective cover
\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\lambda}}
. Then, if
Q_{\bullet}\to\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}\to 0
is the projective resolution of
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
, we have that
Q_{0}=\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\lambda}}
. We now determine the multiplicity of
\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\mu}}
in
Q_{1}
.

We have that the kernel of
\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\lambda}}\twoheadrightarrow\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
evaluated on the weighted set
\underline{a}
is given by first restricting
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
as a representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{b}}
to a representation of the subgroup

\mathfrak{S}_{b_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{S}_{b_{i}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{1}\times\mathfrak{S}_{b_{i+1}-1}\times\mathfrak{S}_{b_{i+2}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{S}_{b_{n}},

via
\mathfrak{S}_{b_{i+1}-1}\times\mathfrak{S}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{b_{i+1}}
,
and then inducting to
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
via
\mathfrak{S}_{b_{i}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{a_{i}}
. By Pieri’s rule,
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\mu}}
appears with multiplicity one. Since
\underline{a}>\underline{b}
is a cover relation, the projective cover of
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\mu}}
must appear in
Q_{1}
, and it appears with multiplicity one. Therefore,
\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{D}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\textbf{M}_{\underline{\mu}})=\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{G}}(S_{\underline{\lambda}},S_{\underline{\mu}})

has dimension
1
.

By Lemma
4.9
, if condition (1) is not satisfied, then
\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{G}}(S_{\underline{\lambda}},S_{\underline{\mu}})=0
. Now, if condition (1) holds but conditions (2) and (3) are not satisfied, then
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\mu}}
does not appear in the decomposition of
\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\lambda}}(\underline{a})
, and so there is no map from
\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\mu}}\to\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\lambda}}
. Therefore,
\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{G}}(S_{\underline{\lambda}},S_{\underline{\mu}})=0
in this case as well.
∎

An interesting problem is to construct the minimal projective or injective resolution for simple objects in our category, and to then compute all higher Ext groups among simple objects. We expect any formulas for such groups will involve Littlewood–Richardson coefficients.

4.6.
Universal property

Using arguments similar to those in
[
17
, §3.4]
, we now describe left-exact tensor functors from the category
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
to an arbitrary abelian tensor category
\mathscr{C}
. By pre-composing with the pushforward
\tau_{!}:\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}
, this also describes left-exact tensor functors from
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{U}}
. The model for these functors is the one
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
described in §
4.1
given by the structured tensor product with the functor
\mathscr{T}
.

Let
T(\mathscr{C})
be the category whose objects consist of an object
A
of
\mathscr{C}
along with a sequence of morphisms

A=A_{1}\to A_{2}\to\cdots\to A_{n}.

We denote this object by
\{A_{i}\}
. Given
\{A_{i}\}\in T(\mathscr{C})
, define
\mathcal{K}(\{A_{i}\})
to be the functor
\mathscr{U}\to\mathscr{C}
given by

\underline{a}\mapsto\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\otimes a_{i}}.

A morphism
\varphi:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
in
\mathscr{U}
defines a morphism in
\mathscr{C}
given by mapping the tensor factors according to
\varphi
and using the morphisms specified by
\{A_{i}\}
.
One should view this as a generalization of the functor
\mathscr{T}:\mathscr{U}\to\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
giving injective objects in
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{G})
, where the sequence of morphisms are the surjections

\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1}\to\cdots\to\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-1}.

Proposition 4.11
.

To give a left-exact tensor functor
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\mathscr{C}
is equivalent to giving an object of
T(\mathscr{C})
.

Proof.

Let
\text{LEx}(\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}},\mathscr{C})
denote the category of left-exact tensor functors
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\mathscr{C}
. We have a functor
\Phi:\text{LEx}(\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}},\mathscr{C})\to T(\mathscr{C})
defined as follows. Let
F\in\text{LEx}(\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}},\mathscr{C})
. Recall that there are natural maps
\textbf{J}_{e_{1}}\to\textbf{J}_{e_{2}}\to\cdots\to\textbf{J}_{e_{n}}
, where
e_{i}
denotes the
n
-tuple with
1
in the
i^{\text{th}}
position and
0
’s elsewhere.
Then
\{F(\textbf{J}_{e_{i}})\}
gives an object in
T(\mathscr{C})
. We also have a functor
\Psi:T(\mathscr{C})\to\text{LEx}(\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}},\mathscr{C})
given by

\{A_{i}\}\mapsto(N\mapsto N\odot\mathcal{K}(\{A_{i}\}).

By the construction of the structured tensor product, it gives a left-exact tensor functor. We show that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are mutually quasi-inverse equivalences.

Let
\{A_{i}\}
be an object of
T(\mathscr{C})
. Then applying the functor
\Psi(\{A_{i}\})
to
\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}
, we obtain

\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\mapsto\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\odot\mathcal{K}(\{A_{i}\})=\text{Hom}(\mathbf{P}_{e_{i}},\mathcal{K}(\{A_{i}\}))=A_{i}.

Thus,
\Phi\Psi(\{A_{i}\})=\{A_{i}\}
, and so the natural morphism
\text{id}\to\Phi\Psi
is an equality.

Now suppose
F\in\text{LEx}(\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}},\mathscr{C})
. Applying
\Psi\Phi
, we obtain the functor
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{D}}\to\mathscr{C}
given by

N\mapsto N\odot\mathcal{K}(\{F(\textbf{J}_{e_{i}})\})=N\odot F(\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\}))=F(N\odot\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\})),

where the first equality follows from
F
being a tensor functor, and the second equality comes from
F
being left-exact and
\odot
being an inverse limit. It therefore suffices to show that
N\odot\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\})=N
.

Let
M
be a
\mathscr{D}
-module. Then

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,N\odot\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\})=\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,\text{Hom}(N^{\vee},\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\})))=\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(N^{\vee},\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\})));

the second equality comes from the structured Hom being an adjoint functor, as described in §
2.5
.
We have that

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\})(\underline{a}))=\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,\textbf{J}_{\underline{a}})=M^{\vee}(\underline{a}).

In particular,
\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\}))=M^{\vee}
as
\mathscr{U}
-modules, and so

\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,N\odot\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\}))=\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{U}}(N^{\vee},M^{\vee})=\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,N).

Thus, we see that
N\odot\mathcal{K}(\{\textbf{J}_{e_{i}}\})=N
, and this completes the proof.
∎

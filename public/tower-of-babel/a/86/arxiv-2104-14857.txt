Surface plasmonics of Weyl semimetals
Xin Lu, Dibya Kanti Mukherjee, Mark O. Goerbig

Abstract
Smooth interfaces of topological systems are known to host massive surface states along with the topologically protected chiral one. We show that in Weyl semimetals these massive states, along with the chiral Fermi arc, strongly alter the form of the Fermi-arc plasmon, Most saliently, they yield further collective plasmonic modes that are absent in a conventional interfaces. The plasmon modes are completely anisotropic as a consequence of the underlying anisotropy in the surface model and expected to have a clear-cut experimental signature, e.g. in electron-energy loss spectroscopy.



Introduction.
—Weyl semimetals (WSMs) are often considered as a three-dimensional version of graphene since their low-energy
\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{p}
Hamiltonian is described by the massless Weyl equation with linear energy dispersion known as Weyl cones
Armitage et al. 2018
. WSMs must have an even number of Weyl cones: at least four for inversion symmetry broken WSM and two for time-reversal symmetry broken ones. Previous studies have shown that the bulk dielectric properties such as Friedel oscillations and (magneto-)plasmon in WSMs
Das Sarma and Hwang 2009
;
Lv and Zhang 2013
;
Panfilov et al. 2014
;
Hofmann and Das Sarma 2015
;
Giri et al. 2020
are different from those of graphene
Hwang and Das Sarma 2007
;
Wunsch et al. 2006
due to the increased dimensionality. For example, the bulk plasmon’s dispersion in WSMs is gapped and parabolic in momentum while it follows a gapless square-root dispersion in graphene. The chiral anomaly in WSMs
Son and Spivak 2013
may be probed by the bulk plasmon
Zhou et al. 2015
whose dispersion depends on the chirality-resolved chemical potential. As metals, WSMs can also host surface plasmons
Ritchie 1957
and surface plasmon polaritons
Hofmann and Das Sarma 2016
;
Tamaya et al. 2019
by using Maxwell’s equations in the bulk with a topological Chern-Simons
\theta
-term
Zyuzin and Burkov 2012
. In particular, in ferromagnetic WSMs, due to its gapless spectrum and large Berry curvature
Kotov and Lozovik 2018
;
Pellegrino et al. 2015
, electromagnetic waves propagate non-reciprocally, i.e., one direction is preferred.

Localized surface states can also give birth to original surface plasmons. In WSMs, topologically protected Fermi-arc (FA) states connecting two Weyl cones emerge on the surface due to the bulk-edge correspondence: the presence of topologically protected edge states is dictated by the topological invariant of the twisted bulk band structure. The FA states have been shown to induce a chiral linear FA surface plasmon with total non-reciprocity
Song and Rudner 2017
;
Andolina et al. 2018
;
Lošić 2018
;
Adinehvand et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2019
;
Gorbar et al. 2019
;
Ghosh and Timm 2020
,
i.e.
it propagates only in one direction determined by the the chirality of the FA dispersion. Linear dispersion and total non-reciprocity are highly desirable for further plasmonic applications. Furthermore, a smooth surface of a topological material is known to host massive states called
Volkov-Pankratov
(VP) states
Volkov and Pankratov 1985
;
Pankratov et al. 1987
;
Tchoumakov et al. 2017a
;
Inhofer et al. 2017
;
Alspaugh et al. 2020
;
van den Berg et al. 2020a
, along with the protected topological chiral states. These gapped bands can be visualized as pseudo-Landau levels of the system where the smoothness of the interface is modeled as a pseudo-magnetic field
Lu and Goerbig 2020
. Though not protected topologically, their presence may heavily modify the transport
van den Berg et al. 2020b
and magneto-optical properties
Lu and Goerbig 2019
;
Mukherjee et al. 2019
of surfaces of topological materials.

Discussions.
—
We have investigated the effect of surface smoothness on the charge oscillation spectrum of a WSM surface. Within RPA calculations, we observe the emergence of two collective modes stabilized by the inter and intra VP band excitations, in addition to the FA plasmon. The plasmons exhibit anisotropy and non-reciprocity inherited from the underlying surface model. Our findings could be verified experimentally, e.g. in EELS, which in addition to a proof of these plasmons could probe the chirality of the FA. Furthermore,the plasmon gap in Eq. (
8
) gives us a direct experimental measure of the separation between the Weyl nodes.

\displaystyle|F_{1,2}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k+q})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\langle 1,\mathbf{k}|2,\mathbf{k+q}\rangle|^{2}

(S10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}|u_{1}^{*}(k_{y})u_{2}(k_{y}+q_{y})R_{n_{1}-1,n_{2}-1}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}v_{1}^{*}(k_{y})v_{2}(k_{y}+q_{y})R_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})|^{2}

(S11)

where

\displaystyle R_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})

\displaystyle=\langle n_{1},k_{z}|n_{2},k_{z}+q_{z}\rangle

(S12)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}!}{n_{1}!}}\alpha^{n_{1}-n_{2}}L_{n_{2}}^{(n_{1}-n_{2})}(|\alpha|^{2})e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\quad\text{if $n_{1}\geq n_{2}$}

(S13)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}!}{n_{2}!}}\alpha^{n_{2}-n_{1}}L_{n_{1}}^{(n_{2}-n_{1})}(|\alpha|^{2})e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\quad\text{if $n_{2}>n_{1}$},

(S14)

and

\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})=i\sqrt{\frac{l}{8m^{2}v(\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})}}(2k_{z}+q_{z})q_{z}=i\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{2(\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})}\frac{\Delta l}{v}}\frac{(2k_{z}+q_{z})q_{z}}{k_{0}^{2}}.

(S15)

This is precisely a manifestation of the strong anisotropy of the edge states in WSM, and we can already anticipate a highly anisotropic dynamical polarization.

Even if the overlap function is complicated, the expressions can be simplified significantly in the long-wavelength limit. Let us consider
q_{z}/k_{0}\to 0
,
k_{0}
appears to be a cut-off in the long-wavelength limit. When
q_{z}=0
,
R_{n_{1},n_{2}}
is simplified to
\delta_{n_{1},n_{2}}
due to the orthogonality of the wave functions. Therefore,

\displaystyle|F_{1,2}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k+q})|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\delta_{n_{1},n_{2}}|u_{1}^{*}u_{2}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}v_{1}^{*}v_{2}|^{2}

(S16)

and one notices that only excitations
n\to n
are allowed. Suppose that
q_{z}\neq 0
and for example
n_{1}\geq n_{2}

\displaystyle R_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})\sim\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}!}{n_{1}!}}\binom{n_{2}}{n_{1}}\alpha^{n_{1}-n_{2}}\propto q_{z}^{n_{1}-n_{2}}.

(S17)

The
n\to n
excitations are therfore still the leading contribution to the charge susceptibility, and the coupling strength of other excitations decays as
q_{z}^{|n_{1}-n_{2}|}
.

Based on the arguments exposed above, we consider in the rest of this section
q_{z}=0
. Accordingly, the tensor
\chi^{(0)}_{1,2}
is diagonal and can be treated as a scalar. We can thus divide it into contributions from different excitation invoking different bands,

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{n}(q_{y},\omega)+\chi_{0}(q_{y},\omega).

(S18)

For
n=0
,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(q_{y},\omega)=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{f_{D}(E_{0}(\mathbf{k}))-f_{D}(E_{0}(\mathbf{k+q}))}{\omega+E_{0}(\mathbf{k})-E_{0}(\mathbf{k+q})+i\eta},

(S19)

while for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\chi_{n}(q_{y},\omega)=\sum_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{f_{D}(E_{n}^{\lambda_{1}}(\mathbf{k}))-f_{D}(E_{n}^{\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{k+q}))}{\omega+E_{n}^{\lambda_{1}}(\mathbf{k})-E_{n}^{\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{k+q})+i\eta}\times|F_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}^{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k+q})|^{2},

(S20)

where

\displaystyle|F_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}^{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k+q})|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\frac{k_{y}(k_{y}+q_{y})+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\left(k_{y}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}\right)\left((k_{y}+q_{y})^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}\right)}}\right).

(S21)

To obtain the plasmon mode, we need first calculate the imaginary part of each susceptibility for
n=0
and
n\geq 1
from which their real part can then be deduced by the Kramers-Kronig relations. The single-pair excitations (SPE) resides in the domain in the
(q_{y},\omega)
-space where the imaginary part is not vanishing. We assume
T=0
in the following calculations and we can consider only
\omega>0
.

II.1
Fermi arc
n=0

Because of the chirality of the FA state, particle-hole excitations with
\omega>0
require
q_{y}>0
,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(q_{y}>0,\omega>0)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{q_{y}}{\omega-vq_{y}+i\eta}

(S22)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}q_{y}}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{\omega-vq_{y}}{(\omega-vq_{y})^{2}+\eta^{2}}-i\frac{\eta}{(\omega-vq_{y})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\right).

(S23)

Note that this expression is valid for
\omega
and
q_{y}
of arbitrary sign and that
\chi_{0}
is independent of the chemical potential due to the linear dispersion of the FA state and a well-defined SPE
\omega=vq_{y}
. Using RPA and
V(q)=e^{2}/2\epsilon_{0}q
which we will justify in the following section, we can easily find the FA plasmon

\displaystyle\omega=vq_{y}+\frac{k_{0}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon_{0}}\text{sgn}(q_{y}),

(S24)

which is gapped and disperses linearly when
q_{z}=0
.

II.2
Massive states
n\geq 1

The VP states are not chiral such that the sign of
q_{y}
is irrelevant.

II.2.1
Charge neutral point

When the chemical potential is at the charge neutral point, only the interband excitations are possible. Since the VP states are gapped, the following results are also valid for finite chemical potential if only it resides between two band extrema of the VP states. The imaginary part of
\chi_{n}
reads

\displaystyle\Im[\chi_{n}^{-}](q_{y},\omega>0)=-\frac{k_{0}}{\pi v}\frac{\frac{4n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}q_{y}^{2}}{\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}-q_{y}^{2}\right)^{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}-q_{y}^{2}}}}\Theta\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}-q_{y}^{2}-\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}\right)

(S25)

where the minus sign on
\chi_{n}
refers to the contribution from the
\lambda=-
valence band. The particle-hole spectrum
\omega>v\sqrt{q_{y}^{2}+\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}
is above the FA plasmon for realistic value of
k_{0}
. Note that the imaginary part is proportional to
q_{y}^{2}
and vanishing at
q_{y}=0
due to the vanishing overlap function when
q_{y}=0
. This is simply because the eigenstates of Bloch Hamiltonian for a given
k
are orthogonal. The corresponding real part reads

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n}^{-}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{4k_{0}\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}q_{y}^{2}}{\pi^{2}v}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{4n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right)}+\frac{\pi-2\arctan A}{\left|q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}+q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\quad\text{if $q_{y}^{2}\leq\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\leq q_{y}^{2}+\frac{4n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}$}

(S26)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4k_{0}\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}q_{y}^{2}}{\pi^{2}v}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{4n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right)}-\frac{\log|\frac{A+1}{A-1}|}{\left|q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}+q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\quad\text{otherwise}

(S27)

where

\displaystyle A=\sqrt{\left|\frac{\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}+q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}}{q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}}\right|}.

(S28)

Using RPA and
V(q)=e^{2}/2\epsilon_{0}q
, we can prove the existence of a plasmon mode damped by the interband SPE. Let’s check two limits. When
\omega^{2}/v^{2}-q_{y}^{2}\gg 8n/\ell_{S}^{2}
,

\displaystyle 1-V(q_{y})\Re[\chi_{n}^{-}](\omega,q_{y})\to 1^{-}.

(S29)

When
\omega^{2}/v^{2}-q_{y}^{2}\to^{+}8n/\ell_{S}^{2}
,

\displaystyle 1-V(q_{y})\Re[\chi_{n}^{-}](\omega,q_{y})

\displaystyle=1-\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\epsilon_{0}v}\frac{k_{0}|q_{y}|}{\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}},

(S30)

which can only be satisfied for non-zero values of
|q_{y}|
.
This plasmon emerges only at finite
|q_{y}|
for the same reason as the vanishing imaginary part.

II.2.2
Band-crossing chemical potential

Now we consider the case of a chemical potential crossing one of the VP bands. Since VP states are particle-hole symmetric, we consider a chemical potential in the conduction band (
n=+1
),

\displaystyle\mu=v\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}>0,

(S31)

where we define a Fermi wavevector
k_{F}
. Now intraband excitations in the
\lambda=+
conduction band are possible and we have to suppress a part of interband contributions in the susceptibility due to Pauli blocking. Since the latter does not change the boundary of the particle-hole spectrum (checked by numerical calculations), we give here only the expressions for the intraband excitations. The imaginary part for intraband excitations reads

\displaystyle\Im[\chi_{n,\text{intra}}^{+}](q_{y},\omega>0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{0}}{4\pi v}\frac{q_{y}^{2}\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{\left|q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}+q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(S32)

\displaystyle\times\left(\Theta\left(2k_{F}-\left|q_{y}-\frac{\omega}{v}\sqrt{1+\frac{\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}}}\right|\right)-\Theta\left(2k_{F}-\left|q_{y}+\frac{\omega}{v}\sqrt{1+\frac{\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}}}\right|\right)\right).

(S33)

For the intraband excitations in the
n=+1
VP band, we find a particle-hole region between two parallel parabolic-like boundaries excluding a dome from 0 to
2k_{F}
, as it should be for the Lindhard function for a one-dimensional parabolic band, as shown in Figure
S1
where we gather also the other excitations (intra-FA and inter-VP band excitations from
-1
to
+1
). This is due to the one-dimensional dispersion of the massive VP states. We can anticipate that when the chemical potential is high the Dirac linear dispersion of the VP states is probed. So the two parallel parabolic-like boundaries becomes linear and parallel to the line defined by
\omega=vq_{y}
.

Figure S1:
Particle-hole region

The Kramers-Konig relations yield a real part of the intraband contributions that reads

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n,\text{intra}}^{+}]

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}q_{y}^{2}}{\pi^{2}v}\left(g_{1}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\left|\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}-\sqrt{|k_{F}-|q_{y}||^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}\right|\right)-g_{1}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\left|\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}-\sqrt{|k_{F}+|q_{y}||^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}\right|\right)\right)

if
q_{y}^{2}\leq\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\leq q_{y}^{2}+\frac{4n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}

(S34)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}q_{y}^{2}}{\pi^{2}v}\left(g_{2}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\left|\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}-\sqrt{|k_{F}-|q_{y}||^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}\right|\right)-g_{2}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\left|\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}-\sqrt{|k_{F}+|q_{y}||^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}\right|\right)\right)

otherwise

(S35)

where

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{4n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right)}\sqrt{\frac{x+\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{x}}-\frac{2\arctan f(x)}{\left|q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}+q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(S36)

\displaystyle g_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{4n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}\left(q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right)}\sqrt{\frac{x+\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{x}}-\frac{\log|\frac{f(x)+1}{f(x)-1}|}{\left|q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}+q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(S37)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left|\frac{\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}+q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}}{q_{y}^{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}}\right|\cdot\frac{x+\frac{8n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}{x}}.

(S38)

In the long wavelength limit (
q_{y}\to 0
),

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n,\text{intra}}^{+}]=\frac{k_{0}}{\pi^{2}v}\frac{k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\frac{v^{2}q_{y}^{2}}{\omega^{2}-v^{2}q_{y}^{2}}.

(S39)

Recall that for a spinless 1D parabolic band, these expressions may be simplified, and one obtains

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{\text{para}}]=\frac{k_{F}q_{y}^{2}}{\pi m\omega^{2}}.

(S40)

At the band minimum where
k_{F}\to 0
,
\Re[\chi_{n,\text{intra}}^{+}]
becomes indeed

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n,\text{intra}}^{+}]=\frac{2k_{0}}{2\pi}\frac{k_{F}q_{y}^{2}}{\pi m_{D}\omega^{2}},

(S41)

where
m_{D}
is the Dirac mass
\sqrt{2}/v\ell_{S}
. The strong anisotropy is explicitly unveiled in this form: dispersed in the transverse direction and flat in the longitudinal direction, effectively 1D band. However, the prefactor
2k_{0}/2\pi
reminds us that the underlying manifold is 2D. Indeed,
2k_{0}
is the extension of the VP bands in the
k_{z}
-direction delimited by the two Weyl nodes at
k_{z}=\pm k_{0}
, and
2k_{0}/2\pi
is thus its contribution to the density of states. It is also interesting to calculate the dynamical polarization for a 1D linear band
E=\pm vk_{y}
for a positive chemical potential and the excitation momentum
|q_{y}|<k_{F}
, that is

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{\text{lin}}]=\frac{k_{0}}{\pi^{2}v}\frac{v^{2}q_{y}^{2}}{\omega^{2}-v^{2}q_{y}^{2}},

(S42)

which coincides with
\Re[\chi_{n,\text{intra}}^{+}]
when
k_{F}\gg 2n/\ell_{S}^{2}
.

Using RPA and
V(q)=e^{2}/2\epsilon_{0}q
, a square-root intraband plasmon is obtained:

\displaystyle\omega=\sqrt{\frac{e^{2}vk_{0}}{2\pi^{2}\epsilon_{0}}\frac{k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}q_{y}+v^{2}q_{y}^{2}}.

(S43)

The intraband plasmon is pushed up with increasing chemical potential and
k_{F}
.

III
S3. Quasi-two-dimensional random phase approximation

In this section we show how to use the random-phase approximation (RPA) in our quasi-2D Hamiltonian. We show
\ell_{S}
as another length scale cut-off to consider our Hamiltonian as a quasi-2D system. We have already shown the complicated overlap function when
q_{z}\neq 0
. So the matrix element of the Coulomb interaction must be at least as messy as the overlap function. We first show the matrix element of the Coulomb interaction in the most general form without any approximation. Then, we explicitly show how the long-wavelength approximation simplifies the calculations considerably.

III.1
Coulomb interaction matrix element

Consider the wavefunction of the eigenstates

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{r}|\psi_{n,\lambda}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{S}}e^{i\mathbf{k_{\parallel}}\cdot\mathbf{r_{\parallel}}}\chi_{n,\lambda}(x,k_{\parallel})

(S44)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2S}}e^{i\mathbf{k_{\parallel}}\cdot\mathbf{r_{\parallel}}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{n,\lambda}(k_{y})\langle x|n-1\rangle(k_{z})\\
\lambda v_{n,\lambda}(k_{y})\langle x|n\rangle(k_{z})\end{pmatrix},

(S45)

where
S
is the size of the surface and in terms of which the Coulomb interaction operator reads

\displaystyle\hat{V}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}S}\sum_{1,2,3,4}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},q}\iint dxdx^{\prime}\frac{2\pi e^{-|q(x-x^{\prime})|}}{|q|}\chi_{1}^{\dagger}(x,k_{1})\chi_{3}(x,k_{1}+q)\chi_{2}^{\dagger}(x^{\prime},k_{2})\chi_{4}(x^{\prime},k_{2}-q)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{1,k_{1}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{2,k_{2}}\hat{c}_{4,k_{2}-q}\hat{c}_{2,k_{1}+q}.

(S46)

For smooth interfaces with
\ell\gg\ell_{S}
, one can show that the dominant contribution to the Coulomb operator comes from the region where
q(\langle x\rangle-\langle x^{\prime}\rangle)\ll 1
, such that the effective Coulomb potential reads

\displaystyle\hat{V}\approx\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}S|q|}\sum_{1,2,3,4}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},q}F_{1,3}(k_{1},k_{1}+q)F_{2,4}(k_{2},k_{2}-q)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{1,k_{1}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{2,k_{2}}\hat{c}_{4,k_{2}-q}\hat{c}_{2,k_{1}+q}.

(S47)

The complicated form of the overlap function renders further simplification impossible unless we suppose
q_{z}=0
. Then we can define

\displaystyle\hat{V}\approx\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1,2,3,4}\sum_{q}V_{13,24}(q)\frac{1}{S}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}F^{n_{1}}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3}}(k_{1},k_{1}+q)F^{n_{2}}_{\lambda_{2},\lambda_{4}}(k_{2},k_{2}-q)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{1,k_{1}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{2,k_{2}}\hat{c}_{4,k_{2}-q}\hat{c}_{2,k_{1}+q},

(S48)

where the diagonal two-dimensional Coulomb interaction matrix element is defined as

\displaystyle V_{13,24}(q)=\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}|q|}\delta_{n_{1},n_{3}}\delta_{n_{2},n_{4}}.

(S49)

Thus the interaction tensor becomes a scalar, and the RPA polarizability reads

\displaystyle\chi(q_{y},\omega)=\frac{\chi^{(0)}(q_{y},\omega)}{1-V_{\text{2D}}(|q_{y}|)\chi^{(0)}(q_{y},\omega)},

(S50)

where

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{n}(q_{y},\omega)+\chi_{0}(q_{y},\omega)

(S51)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad V_{\text{2D}}(|q_{y}|)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}|q_{y}|}

(S52)

When
q_{z}\neq 0
, we can in general not simplify the Coulomb interaction operator to the previous form. Luckily, when we consider only a few bands, the long-wavelength limit renders the expressions tractable.

III.2
Three-band model

Let us consider a three-band model with the chiral FA state and the two
n=\pm 1
VP states. As simple as it is, this toy model gives already many promising results on plasmon. As shown before, it is difficult to deal with overlap functions when
q_{z}\neq 0
because of
R_{n_{1},n_{2}}\neq 0
for
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
. In the long wavelength limit, i.e.
q/k_{0}\ll 1
, we can write them explicitly for
n_{1},n_{2}\in\{0,1\}
as

\displaystyle R_{0,0}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})

\displaystyle=R_{1,1}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})=1+O(q^{2})\approx 1

(S53)

\displaystyle R_{0,1}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})

\displaystyle=R_{1,0}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})=\alpha(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})+O(q^{3})\approx|\alpha|,

(S54)

where
\alpha\sim q
. Then we can rewrite overlap functions as

\displaystyle|F_{1,2}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k+q})|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}|u_{1}^{*}u_{2}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}v_{1}^{*}v_{2}|^{2}|R_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})|^{2}=|G_{1,2}(k_{y},k_{y}+q_{y})|^{2}|R_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})|^{2},

(S55)

where we decouple the
q_{y}
and
q_{z}
directions. On the RPA level, the final expression for
\chi
reads

\displaystyle\chi(q,\omega)=\frac{\chi^{(0)}(q,\omega)}{1-V_{\text{2D}}(|q|)\chi^{(0)}(q,\omega)},

(S56)

where

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1,2}\chi^{0}_{1,2}(q,\omega),

(S57)

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}_{1,2}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1,2}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{f_{D}(E_{1}(k_{y}))-f_{D}(E_{2}(k_{y}+q_{y}))}{\omega+E_{1}(k_{y})-E_{2}(k_{y}+q_{y})+i\eta}\times|G_{1,2}(k_{y},k_{y}+q_{y})|^{2}|R_{n_{1},n_{2}}(k_{z},k_{z}+q_{z})|^{2},

(S58)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad V_{\text{2D}}(|q|)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}|q|}.

(S59)

Before we calculate the full dynamical polarization including all the contributions in the three-band model, let us write the dispersion for FA plasmon and VP intraband plasmon when
q_{z}\neq 0
. In the long-wavelength limit, the coupling between states of different
n
, for example
n=0
and
n=1
, is negligible because it is at least proportional to
q_{z}^{2}
. Thus, we simply need to replace
q_{y}
by
q\cos\theta
with
\cos\theta=q_{y}/\sqrt{q_{y}^{2}+q_{z}^{2}}
in the previous expressions of dynamical polarization. For the FA state,

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{0}]

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{q\cos\theta}{\omega-vq\cos\theta},

(S60)

and the FA plasmon dispersion reads

\displaystyle\omega=vq\cos\theta+\frac{k_{0}e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon_{0}}\cos\theta.

(S61)

For the
n=1
VP state,

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n,\text{intra}}^{+}]=\frac{k_{0}}{\pi^{2}v}\frac{k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\frac{v^{2}q^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\omega^{2}},

(S62)

and the VP intraband plasmon dispersion reads

\displaystyle\omega=\cos\theta\sqrt{\frac{e^{2}vk_{0}}{2\pi^{2}\epsilon_{0}}\frac{k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}q_{y}}.

(S63)

As shown in the main text of the paper, this expression cannot explain the linear dispersion of the VP intraband plasmon, and we need to take into account the influence of the FA state.

In the long-wavelength limit, assuming
q_{z}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{\text{tot}}]=\frac{k_{0}}{\pi^{2}v}\left(\frac{k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\cdot\frac{q_{y}^{2}}{\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}-q_{y}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{q_{y}}{\frac{\omega}{v}-q_{y}}\right).

(S64)

The first term in the parenthesis comes from the VP intraband excitations and the second one from the intra-FA excitations. Here we do not include
\Re[\chi_{n}^{-}]
since it describes an insulator playing the role of substrate, which only change
\epsilon_{0}
to
\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{r}
. For now, we do not take into the interband part in
\Re[\chi_{n}^{+}]
either. As we will show later, this is essential for VP intraband plasmon. Using RPA and
V(q_{y})=e^{2}/2\epsilon_{0}|q_{y}|
, the dielectric function reads

\displaystyle\epsilon=1-\frac{e^{2}k_{0}}{2\epsilon_{0}\pi^{2}v}\left(\frac{k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\cdot\frac{|q_{y}|}{\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}-q_{y}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\text{sgn}(q_{y})}{\frac{\omega}{v}-q_{y}}\right).

(S65)

To find the plasmon mode, we need to solve the following equation

\displaystyle\omega^{2}-\text{sgn}(q_{y})\gamma\omega-\gamma v|q_{y}|\left(1+\frac{2k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\right)-v^{2}|q_{y}|^{2}=0

(S66)

where
\gamma=e^{2}k_{0}/4\epsilon_{0}\pi^{2}
is the gap of FA plasmon. This equation is readily solved, and one finds

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{\text{sgn}(q_{y})\gamma\pm\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+4\gamma v|q_{y}|\left(1+\frac{2k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\right)+4v^{2}|q_{y}|^{2}}}{2}.

(S67)

As
q_{y}
is small, we have

\displaystyle\omega=\text{sgn}(q_{y})\gamma+\left(1+\frac{2k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\right)vq_{y}\quad\text{or}\quad-\left(1+\frac{2k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\right)vq_{y}.

(S68)

Most saliently, the VP intraband plasmon has linear behavior compared to a square-root-like dispersion expected naively in our previous calculations, where we consider only the VP intraband excitations without taking into account the FA plasmon. For
q_{z}\neq 0
, we can find the plasmon

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\pm\gamma|\cos\theta|}{2}\pm vq\left(|\cos\theta|+\frac{2k_{F}}{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\frac{2n}{\ell_{S}^{2}}}}\right)

(S69)

where we omit the coupling between FA and VP band which is only a correction of
q_{z}^{2}
.

This result can explain the enhanced slope in the dispersion of the FA plasmon at small
|q_{y}|
and the VP intraband plasmon when
q_{y}<0
. However, we do not find an intraband plasmon for
q_{y}>0
, which we observe in our numerical calculations. Indeed, as we have anticipated, the interband part in
\Re[\chi_{n,inter}^{+}]
accounts for this plasmon,

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n,inter}^{+}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{f_{D}(E_{n}^{+}(\mathbf{k}))}{\omega+E_{n}^{+}(\mathbf{k})-E_{n}^{-}(\mathbf{k+q})+i\eta}\times|F_{+,-}^{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k+q})|^{2}

(S70)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{-f_{D}(E_{n}^{+}(\mathbf{k+q}))}{\omega+E_{n}^{-}(\mathbf{k})-E_{n}^{+}(\mathbf{k+q})+i\eta}\times|F_{-,+}^{n}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k+q})|^{2}.

(S71)

To simplify the discussion, let us assume that
k_{F}\gg\sqrt{2n}/\ell_{S}
so that the energy dispersion is
\omega=\pm v|q_{y}|
. Also,
k_{F}/\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+2n/\ell_{S}^{2}}\approx 1
. Neglecting the overlap function which is only a correction of
q_{y}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n,inter}^{+}]

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}}{2\pi^{2}v}\int_{-k_{F}}^{k_{F}}dk_{y}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{\omega}{v}+|k_{y}|+|k_{y}+q_{y}|}-\frac{1}{\frac{\omega}{v}-|k_{y}|-|k_{y}-q_{y}|}\right)

(S72)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}}{2\pi^{2}v}\left(\log\left(\frac{4v^{2}k_{F}^{2}}{v^{2}q_{y}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\right)-\frac{2q_{y}^{2}}{\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}-q_{y}^{2}}\right).

(S73)

where
q_{y}>0
. Then,

\displaystyle\Re[\chi_{n}^{+}]=\Re[\chi_{n,inter}^{+}]+\Re[\chi_{n,intra}^{+}]=\frac{k_{0}}{2\pi^{2}v}\log\left(\frac{4v^{2}k_{F}^{2}}{v^{2}q_{y}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\right)

(S74)

where we set
0<\omega<vq_{y}
as we have observed numerically. Therefore the RPA dielectric function reads

\displaystyle\epsilon=1-\frac{e^{2}k_{0}}{4\epsilon_{0}\pi^{2}v}\left(\frac{1}{|q_{y}|}\log\left(\frac{4v^{2}k_{F}^{2}}{v^{2}q_{y}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\right)+\frac{\text{sgn}(q_{y})}{\frac{\omega}{v}-q_{y}}\right).

(S75)

This is a transcendental equation that yields a plasmon but cannot be solved analytically. If we omit the second term in the parenthesis, we find

\displaystyle\omega=v\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{2v^{2}k_{F}^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\right)q^{2}+4k_{F}^{2}\frac{v}{\gamma}q-4k_{F}^{2}},

(S76)

where we have a velocity smaller than
v
. Although this formula cannot quantitatively match our numerical calculations shown in the main text, it demonstrates qualitatively the importance of
\Re[\chi_{n,inter}^{+}]
.

Surreal fields stable under exponential and logarithmic functions
Olivier Bournez olivier.bournez@lix.polytechnique.fr, Quentin Guilmant quentin.guilmant@lix.polytechnique.fr

Abstract
Surreal numbers, that were initiated by Conway and popularized by Knuth, have a very rich and elegant theory, rooted in strong fundamental nice set theoretic foundations. This class of numbers, denoted by No , includes simultaneously the ordinal numbers and the real numbers, and forms a universal huge real closed field: It is universal in the sense that any real closed field can be embedded in it. Following Gonshor, surreal numbers can also be seen as signs sequences of ordinal length, with some exponential and logarithmic functions that extend the usual functions over the reals. No can actually also be seen as an elegant particular (generalized) power series field with real coefficients, namely Hahn series with exponents in No itself. It can also be considered as a particular field of transseries, providing tools to do some analysis and asymptotic analysis for functions over the continuum, providing natural concepts for discussing hyperexponential or sublogarithm functions, and their asymptotics. In this article, we consider stability of subfields of No under exponential and logarithmic functions. Namely, we consider No λ \textnormal{No}_{\lambda} the set surreal numbers whose signs sequences have length less than λ \lambda where λ \lambda is some ordinal. Extending the discussion from van den Dries and Ehrlich, we show that No λ \textnormal{No}_{\lambda} is stable by exponential and logarithm iff λ \lambda is some ε \varepsilon -number. These authors have also proved that No λ \textnormal{No}_{\lambda} can be seen as a Hahn series with exponents in No λ \textnormal{No}_{\lambda} and length less than λ \lambda iff λ \lambda is some regular cardinal. Motivated in a longer term by computability issues using ordinal machines, we consider subfields stables by exponential and logarithmic functions defined by Hahn series that does not require to go up to cardinal lengths and exponents, i.e. where λ \lambda can be some ordinal and not necessarily a cardinal. We prove that No can be expressed as a strict hierarchy of subfields stable by exponential and logarithmic functions. The definition of each level of this hierarchy is given in terms of Hahn series of length μ \mu , where μ \mu lives in the multiplicative ordinals smaller than λ \lambda . This provides many explicit examples of subfields of No stable by exponential and logarithmic functions, and does not require to go up to a cardinal λ \lambda to provide such examples.

The class of surreal numbers has been introduced by Conway in

The general initial idea from Conway to define the class of surreal numbers, based on a concept of “simplicity”, is rooted on a unification of Dedekind’s construction of real numbers in terms of cuts of the rational numbers, and of von Neumann’s construction of ordinal numbers by transfinite induction in terms of set membership.

Following the alternative presentation from Gonshor in

This class of numbers, denoted by

Following Gonshor

Field operations

Ring operations

Remark 2.0
.

The definitions of sum and product are uniform in the sense of

. Namely the equations that define

It is an early result that these operations, together with the order, give

a structure of ordered field, and even a structure of real closed field (see

).
Consequently, there is a unique embedding of the rational numbers in

so we can

with a subfield of

.
Actually, the subgroup of the dyadic rationals

with

, correspond exactly to the surreal numbers

of finite length

The field

can be isomorphically identified with a subfield of

by sending

to the number

Let

]

\mathbb{K}

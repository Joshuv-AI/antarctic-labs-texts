Abstract
The Bergman tau functions are applied to the study of the Picard group of moduli spaces of quadratic differentials with at most n n simple poles on genus g g complex algebraic curves. This generalizes our previous results on moduli spaces of holomorphic quadratic differentials.

Introduction and statement of results

The theory of the Bergman tau function was applied to the study of geometry of various moduli spaces in

[
3
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
; see
[
8
]
for a review of known results.

The present paper is a continuation of
[
11
]
. Here we use Bergman tau functions to study the geometry of the moduli space
\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}
of quadratic differentials with
n
simple poles on genus
g
complex algebraic curves. More precisely, the space
\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}
is defined as the set of isomorphism classes of pairs
(C,q)
, where
C
is a smooth genus
g
complex curve with
n
labeled distinct marked points, and
q
is a meromorphic quadratic differential on
C
with at most simple poles at the marked points and no other poles (throughout the paper we will assume that
2g+n>3
). It is well known that
\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}
is naturally isomorphic to
T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, the total space of the holomorphic cotangent bundle on the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of pointed complex algebraic curves.

The bundle
T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
can be extended to the Deligne–Mumford boundary of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
in two natural ways: first, as the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
, the Deligne–Mumford moduli space of stable curves, and second, as the total space of the direct image
\pi_{*}\omega_{g,n}^{2}
, where
\omega_{g,n}
is the relative dualizing sheaf on the universal curve
\pi\colon\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{g,n}\to\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
. These two extensions are rather close to each other: namely,
\det T^{*}\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}=\det\pi_{*}\omega_{g,n}^{2}-\delta_{\rm DM}
, where
\delta_{\rm DM}=\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is the Deligne–Mumford boundary class (a detailed analytic treatment of these subjects can be found in
[
13
]
). In this paper we will use the second extension and put
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}=\pi_{*}\omega_{g,n}^{2}
.

The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
has an open dense subset
{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
that consists of isomorphism classes of pairs
(C,q)
, where
C
is a smooth curve, and
q
has exactly
4g-4+n
simple zeros and
n
simple poles. We call
{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
the
principal stratum
.

Denote by
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}=\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
the projectivization of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
, where
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts on quadratic differentials by multiplication. The complement
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\setminus P{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
is a union of divisors that we denote by
D^{0}_{{\rm deg}}
,
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
, and
D_{\rm DM}
(the subscript
{\rm deg}
stands for
degenerate
, as opposed to differentials in
{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
that we call
generic
). The divisor
D^{0}_{{\rm deg}}
is the closure of the set of differentials
q
with multiple zeros. The divisor
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
is the closure of
n
strata
P{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-5+n},0,-1^{n-1}\big)
(one for each pole) parameterizing differentials with
4g-5+n
simple zeros,
n-1
simple poles and one marked ordinary point (where a zero and a pole of a generic differential coalesce). Finally,
D_{\rm DM}
is the pullback to
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
of the Deligne–Mumford boundary of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. The latter consists of the divisor
D_{\rm irr}
of irreducible nodal curves with
n
marked points, and divisors
D_{j,k}
parameterizing reducible curves with components of genera
j
and
g-j
having
k
and
n-k
marked points respectively, where
j=0,\dots,[g/2]
,
k=0,\dots,n
and
2<2j+k<2g+n-2
. (Note that while a quadratic differential may have only simple poles at the
n
marked points, it may have poles up to order
n
at the nodes.)

Denote by
L\to P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
the tautological line bundle associated with the projectivization
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\to P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
,
and put
\phi=c_{1}(L)\in{\rm Pic}\big(P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\big)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
. Denote by
\lambda
the pullback of the Hodge class
\lambda_{1}=\det\pi_{*}\omega_{g,n}
on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
.
Furthermore, denote by
\delta_{{\rm deg}}^{0}
,
\delta_{{\rm deg}}^{\infty}
,
\delta_{\rm irr}
,
\delta_{j,k}
the classes in
{\rm Pic}\big(P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\big)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
of the corresponding divisors. We will also use the standard notation
\psi_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n,
for the tautological classes on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
as well as for their pullbacks to
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
.

Combining the results of
[
1
, Theorem 2]
and, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 3.3(b)]
, we get

Lemma 1.1
.

The rational Picard group
{\rm Pic}\big(P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\big)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
is freely generated over
{\mathbb{Q}}
by the classes
\phi
,
\lambda
,
\psi_{i}
,
\delta_{\rm irr}
,
\delta_{j,k}
, where
i,k=1,\dots,n
,
j=0,\dots,[g/2]
, and
2<2j+k<2g+n-2
.

To each pair
(C,q)
one can canonically associate a twofold branched cover
f\colon\widehat{C}\to C
and an abelian differential
v
on
\widehat{C}
,
where

\widehat{C}=\big\{(x,v(x))\,|\,x\in C,\,v(x)\in T_{x}^{*}C,\,v(x)^{2}=q(x)\big\}
. For a generic
(C,q)\in\mathcal{Q}_{g,n}

the curve
\widehat{C}
is smooth of genus
\widehat{g}=4g-3+n
and
v
is holomorphic.

The covering
f
is invariant under the canonical involution
(x,v(x))\mapsto(x,-v(x))
on
\widehat{C}
that we denote by
\mu
. Zeros and poles of
q
of odd order are branch points
of the covering
f
. The abelian differential
v
has second order zeros at simple zeros of
q
, and at the simple poles of
q
the differential
v
is holomorphic and nonvanishing.

Consider the map
\widehat{p}\colon P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\to\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\widehat{g}}
,
(C,q)\mapsto\widehat{C}
. This map induces a vector bundle
\widehat{p}^{*}\Lambda^{1}_{\widehat{g}}\to\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}

of dimension
\widehat{g}=4g-3+n
, where
\Lambda^{1}_{\widehat{g}}\to\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{\widehat{g}}
is the Hodge vector bundle. The involution
\mu
on

\widehat{C}
induces an involution
\mu^{*}
on the vector bundle
\widehat{p}^{*}\Lambda^{1}_{\widehat{g}}
. Hence we have a decomposition

\displaystyle\widehat{p}^{*}\Lambda^{1}_{\widehat{g}}=\Lambda_{+}\oplus\Lambda_{-},

where
\Lambda_{+}
(resp.
\Lambda_{-}
) is the eigenbundle corresponding to the eigenvalue
+1
(resp.
-1
) of
\mu^{*}
.
Clearly,
\Lambda_{+}=p^{*}\pi_{*}\omega_{g}
is the pullback of the Hodge bundle on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
, where
p\colon P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\to\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
is a natural projection (forgetful map). We call
\Lambda_{-}
the
Prym bundle
. Its fibers are the spaces of Prym differentials on
\widehat{C}
and have dimension
3g-3+n
. We call
\lambda_{\rm P}=c_{1}(\Lambda_{-})\in{\rm Pic}\big(P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\big)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
the
Prym
class
(slightly abusing the notation, we often denote the line bundles and their classes in the Picard group by the same symbols).

In this paper we prove the following generalization of Theorem 1 in
[
11
]
to the case
n>0
:

Theorem 1.2
.

The Hodge class
\lambda
and the Prym class
\lambda_{\rm P}
can be expressed in terms of the tautological class
\phi
and the classes
\delta^{0}_{{\rm deg}}
,
\delta^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
and
\delta_{\rm DM}
by the formulas

\displaystyle\lambda=\left(\frac{5(g-1)}{36}-\frac{n}{36}\right)\phi+\frac{1}{72}\delta^{0}_{{\rm deg}}-\frac{1}{18}{\delta}_{{\rm deg}}^{\infty}+\frac{1}{12}\delta_{\rm DM},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm P}=\left(\frac{11(g-1)}{36}+\frac{5n}{36}\right)\phi+\frac{13}{72}\delta^{0}_{{\rm deg}}+\frac{5}{18}{\delta}_{{\rm deg}}^{\infty}+\frac{1}{12}\delta_{\rm DM}.

On the other hand, according to
[
11
, formula (5.16) and explanations thereafter]
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm P}=\lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(3g-3+n)\phi,

(1.1)

where
\lambda_{2}=\det\pi_{*}\omega^{2}_{g,n}
. This implies

Corollary 1.3
.

The following relation holds in
{\rm Pic}\big(P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\big)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}-13\lambda=n\phi+\delta^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}-\delta_{\rm DM}.

(1.2)

Furthermore, combining (
1.2
) with Mumford’s formula

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}-13\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}-\delta_{\rm DM}

(1.3)

(cf., e.g.,
[
2
]
), formula (7.8)), we get the following

Corollary 1.4
.

The class
\delta^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
is expressed via the sum of
\psi
-classes and class
\phi
as follows:

\displaystyle{\delta}_{{\rm deg}}^{\infty}=-n\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}\,.

(1.4)

Remark 1.5
.

An analytic approach to the classes
\psi_{i}
was outlined, in particular, in
[
12
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce a twofold canonical cover corresponding to
a pair consisting of a complex algebraic curve and a quadratic differential on it with
n
simple poles. The main objective of
this section is to discuss the action of the covering involution on (co)homology of the cover and the associated matrix of
b
-periods. In Section
3
we define two tau functions corresponding to the eigenvalues
\pm 1
of the covering map, discuss their basic properties and interpret them as holomorphic sections of line bundles on the moduli space of quadratic differentials with simple poles. In Section
4
we study the asymptotic behavior of the tau functions near the divisor
{D}_{{\rm deg}}^{\infty}
and use it to express the Hodge and Prym classes via the classes of the boundary divisors and the tautological class (the asymptotics of the tau functions near the divisors
{D}_{{\rm deg}}^{0}
and
D_{j,k}
were thoroughly studied in
[
11
]
).

2
Geometry of the double cover

Let
f\colon\widehat{C}\to C
be the double cover defined by the meromorphic quadratic differential
q
with simple zeros
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{4g-4+n}\}
and simple poles
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}

on a smooth curve
C
, and let
\mu\colon\widehat{C}\to\widehat{C}
be the corresponding involution.
The covering map
f
is ramified over
x_{j}
,
y_{k}
and we put
\hat{x}_{j}=f^{-1}(x_{j})
,
\hat{y}_{k}=f^{-1}(y_{k})
; the points
\hat{x}_{j}
,
\hat{y}_{k}
are exactly
the fixed points of
\mu
. By
\mu_{*}
(resp.
\mu^{*}
) we denote the involution induced by
\mu
in homology (resp. in cohomology) of
\widehat{C}
.
The space
\Lambda^{1}_{\widehat{C}}
of holomorphic abelian differentials on
\widehat{C}
splits into two eigenspaces
\Lambda_{+}
and
\Lambda_{-}
of complex dimension
g
and
3g-3+n
respectively that correspond to the eigenvalues
\pm 1
of
\mu^{*}
. We have a similar decomposition in the real homology of
\widehat{C}
:
H_{1}\big(\widehat{C},{\mathbb{R}}\big)=H_{+}\oplus H_{-}
, where
\dim H_{+}=2g
,
\dim H_{-}=6g-6+2n
.
Following
[
4
]
, we pick
8g-6+2n
smooth 1-cycles on
\widehat{C}

\displaystyle\big\{a_{j},a_{j}^{*},\tilde{a}_{k},b_{j},b_{j}^{*},\tilde{b}_{k}\big\},\qquad j=1,\dots,g,\quad k=1,\dots,2g+n-3,

(2.1)

in such a way that

\mu_{*}a_{j}=a_{j}^{*},\qquad\mu_{*}b_{j}=b_{j}^{*},\qquad\mu_{*}\tilde{a}_{k}+\tilde{a}_{k}=\mu_{*}\tilde{b}_{k}+\tilde{b}_{k}=0,

and the intersection matrix is

\left(\begin{matrix}0&I_{4g-3+n}\\
-I_{4g-3+n}&0\end{matrix}\right)

(here
I_{k}
denotes the
k\times k
identity matrix).

The projections
f_{*}a_{j}=f_{*}(a_{j}^{*})
and
f_{*}b_{j}=f_{*}(b_{j}^{*})
on
C
form a canonical basis in
H_{1}(C)
, while the projections

f_{*}\tilde{a}_{k}
and
f_{*}\tilde{b}_{k}
are trivial in
H_{1}(C)
.

Remark 2.1
.

To avoid complicated notation, we will use the same symbols for the cycles (
2.1
), their homology classes
in
H_{1}\big(\widehat{C}\big)
, and their pushforwards in
H_{1}(C)
. In particular, the images of (
2.1
) give rise to a canonical basis in
H_{1}(C)
.

Denote by
\{u_{j},u^{*}_{j},\tilde{u}_{k}\}
the basis of normalized abelian differentials on
\widehat{C}
associated with (
2.1
), so that the action of
\mu^{*}
on

\Lambda^{1}_{\widehat{C}}
is given by the matrix

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{matrix}0&I_{g}&0\\
I_{g}&0&0\\
0&0&-I_{2g-3+n}\end{matrix}\right).

The differentials
u_{j}^{+}=u_{j}+u_{j}^{*}
,
j=1,\dots,g
,
provide a basis in the space
\Lambda_{+}
, whereas a basis in
\Lambda_{-}
is given by
3g-3+n
Prym differentials
u_{l}^{-}
, where

\displaystyle u_{l}^{-}=\begin{cases}u_{l}-u_{l}^{*},&l=1,\dots,g,\\
\tilde{u}_{l-g},&l=g+1,\dots,3g-3+n.\end{cases}

We also introduce the bases in the spaces
H_{+}
and
H_{-}
.
The classes

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{j}+a_{j}^{*}),\qquad\beta_{j}^{+}=b_{j}+b_{j}^{*},\qquad j=1,\dots,g,

form a symplectic basis in
H_{+}
, whereas the classes

\displaystyle\alpha_{l}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{l}-a_{l}^{*}),\qquad\beta_{l}^{-}=b_{l}-b_{l}^{*},\qquad l=1,\dots,g,

\displaystyle\alpha^{-}_{l}=\tilde{a}_{l-g},\qquad\beta^{-}_{l}=\tilde{b}_{l-g},\qquad l=g+1,\dots,3g-3+n

form a symplectic basis in
H_{-}
.
The basis
\{a_{j}^{+},\alpha_{l}^{-},\beta_{j}^{+},\beta_{l}^{-}\}
,
j=1,\dots,g
,
l=1,\dots,3g-3+n
, is related to the canonical basis (
2.1
) by means of a (non-integer) symplectic matrix

\displaystyle S=\left(\begin{matrix}T&0\\
0&\big(T^{t}\big)^{-1}\end{matrix}\right)

with

\displaystyle T=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{g}&I_{g}&0\\
I_{g}&-I_{g}&0\\
0&0&I_{2g-3+n}\end{matrix}\right)\;.

(2.2)

The differentials
u_{j}^{+}
,
u_{l}^{-}
are normalized relative to the classes
\alpha_{j}^{+}
,
\alpha_{l}^{-}
respectfully in the sense that

\int_{\alpha_{i}^{+}}u_{j}^{+}=\delta_{ij},\qquad\int_{\alpha_{k}^{-}}u_{l}^{-}=\delta_{kl},

where
i,j=1,\dots,g
and
k,l=1,\dots,3g-3+n
.
The corresponding matrices of
\beta
-periods
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
are given by

\displaystyle(\Omega_{+})_{ij}=\int_{\beta_{i}^{+}}u_{j}^{+},\qquad i,j=1,\dots,g,

\displaystyle(\Omega_{-})_{kl}=\int_{\beta_{k}^{-}}u_{l}^{-},\qquad k,l=1,\dots,3g-3+n.

The matrix
\widehat{\Omega}
of
b
-periods of
\{u_{j},\tilde{u}_{k},u^{*}_{j}\}
with respect to the homology basis (
2.1
) on
\widehat{C}
is related to
\Omega^{+}
and
\Omega^{-}
by the formula

\displaystyle\widehat{\Omega}=T^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}\Omega_{+}&0\\
0&\Omega_{-}\end{matrix}\right)\big(T^{t}\big)^{-1}.

We proceed with bidifferentials and projective connections on the double covers.
Let
{\widehat{B}}(x,y)
denote the canonical (Bergman) bidifferential on
\widehat{C}\times\widehat{C}
associated with the homology basis (
2.1
);
{\widehat{B}}(x,y)
is symmetric, has the second order pole on the diagonal
x=y
with biresidue 1, and all
a
-periods of
{\widehat{B}}(x,y)

on
\widehat{C}
vanish. We put

\displaystyle B_{+}(x,y)={\widehat{B}}(x,y)+\mu_{y}^{*}{\widehat{B}}(x,y),\qquad B_{-}(x,y)={\widehat{B}}(x,y)-\mu_{y}^{*}{\widehat{B}}(x,y),

(the subscript
y
at
\mu^{*}
means that we take the pullback with respect to the involution on the second factor in
\widehat{C}\times\widehat{C}
).
The bidifferential
B_{+}(x,y)
is the pullback of the canonical bidifferential
B(x,y)
on
C\times C
(normalized relative to the classes
f_{*}a_{j}
, where
f\colon\widehat{C}\to C
is the covering map). The bidifferential
B_{-}(x,y)
is called
Prym bidifferential
in
[
11
]
.

Properties of the differentials
B_{\pm}
are summarized in
[
11
, Lemma 2]
.
Near the diagonal
x=y
in
\widehat{C}\times\widehat{C}
we have

\displaystyle B_{\pm}(x,y)=\frac{{\rm d}\zeta(x){\rm d}\zeta(y)}{(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}}+\frac{1}{6}S_{B_{\pm}}(\zeta(x))+\cdots,

where
\zeta(x)
is a local parameter at
x\in\widehat{C}
, and
two projective connections
S_{B_{+}}
and
S_{B_{-}}
that are related by

\displaystyle S_{B_{\pm}}(x)=S_{{\widehat{B}}}(x)\pm 6\mu_{y}^{*}{\widehat{B}}(x,y)\big|_{y=x}.

(2.3)

Here
S_{B_{-}}
is the
Prym projective connection
.

Now we describe the dependence of bidifferentials and projective connections on the choice of homology basis.
Let
\sigma
be a symplectic (i.e., preserving the intersection form) transformation of
H_{1}\big(\widehat{C},{\mathbb{Z}}\big)
. In the canonical basis (
2.1
),
\sigma
is represented by an
{\rm Sp}(8g-6+2n,{\mathbb{Z}})
-matrix

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with square blocks of size
4g-3+n
. The canonical bidifferential
{\widehat{B}}^{\sigma}(x,y)
on
\widehat{C}\times\widehat{C}
associated with the new basis is

\displaystyle{\widehat{B}}^{\sigma}(x,y)={\widehat{B}}(x,y)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\boldsymbol{u}(x)^{t}\big(C{\widehat{\Omega}}+D\big)^{-1}C\boldsymbol{u}(y),

where
{\boldsymbol{u}}=\{u_{j},\tilde{u_{k}},u_{j}^{*}\}^{t}
is the vector of holomorphic abelian differentials on
\widehat{C}
normalized with respect to
\{a_{j},\tilde{a_{k}},a_{j}^{*}\}
,
j=1,\dots,g
,
k=1,\dots,2g-3+n
.

If
\sigma
commutes with the involution
\mu_{*}
, then
\sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-})
, where
\sigma_{+}
(resp.
\sigma_{-}
) is a symplectic transformation of
H_{+}
(resp.
H_{-}
) with half-integer coefficients (note that the intersection form on
H_{1}\big(\widehat{C},{\mathbb{R}}\big)
is invariant with respect to
\mu_{*}
). In the bases
\{\alpha_{j}^{+},\beta_{j}^{+}\}
,
j=1,\dots,g
, and
\{\alpha_{l}^{-},\beta_{l}^{-}\}
,
l=1,\dots,3g-3+n
, these transformations can be written as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\pm}:=\left(\begin{matrix}A_{\pm}&B_{\pm}\\
C_{\pm}&D_{\pm}\end{matrix}\right).

(2.4)

Transformation properties of basic holomorphic differentials, Prym matrix, differentials
B_{\pm}
and projective connections
{S_{B}}_{\pm}
are described in
[
11
, Lemma 3]
.

3
Tau functions

Here we define the necessary tau functions and study their basic properties.
Only slight modifications are needed comparing to the case
n=0

[
11
]
.

3.1
Definition of
\boldsymbol{\tau_{\pm}}

Consider two meromorphic quadratic differentials
S_{B_{\pm}}-S_{v}
on
\widehat{C}
, where
S_{B_{\pm}}
are the Bergman projective connections (
2.3
) and
S_{v}
is given by

\displaystyle S_{v}=\left(\frac{v^{\prime}}{v}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{v^{\prime}}{v}\right)^{2}.

where the prime means the derivative with respect to a local parameter
\zeta(x)
(in other words,
S_{v}
is the Schwarzian derivative of the abelian integral
\int_{x_{0}}^{x}v
).
Take the trivial line bundle on the principal stratum
\mathcal{Q}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
and consider two connections

\displaystyle d_{\pm}=d+{\xi}_{\pm}

with

\displaystyle{\xi}_{\pm}=-\sum_{i=1}^{6g-6+2n}\left(\int_{s_{i}^{*}}\varphi_{\pm}\right)d\int_{s_{i}}v,

where

\{s_{i}\}=\{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\}
and
\{s_{i}^{*}\}=\{\beta_{i},-\alpha_{i}\}
,
i=1,\dots,3g-3+n
, being the dual bases in
H_{-}
as above,
and the meromorphic abelian differentials
\varphi_{\pm}
are given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pm}=-\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{-1}}\,\frac{S_{B_{\pm}}-S_{v}}{v}.

The connections
d_{\pm}
are flat, see
[
6
,
8
,
11
]
for details.

Definition 3.1
.

The tau functions
\tau_{\pm}
are (locally) covariant constant sections of the trivial line bundle on the principal stratum

\mathcal{Q}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
with respect to the connections
d_{\pm}
, that is,

\displaystyle d_{\pm}\tau_{\pm}=0.

(3.1)

Explicit formulas for the tau functions
\tau_{\pm}
can be derived similar
to
[
6
,
11
]
, but we are not going to use them here.

Remark 3.2
.

The definition (
3.1
) follows the convention of
[
11
]
. The tau functions defined by (
3.1
)
are equal to the
48
th power of the tau functions defined in
[
6
,
8
]
.

3.2
Transformation properties of
\boldsymbol{\tau_{\pm}}

For our purposes we need to analyze transformation properties of
\tau_{\pm}
.
The group
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
of nonzero complex numbers acts by multiplication on quadratic differential
q
on
C

and, therefore, on the tau functions
\tau_{\pm}
.
Under the action of
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
the tau functions
\tau_{\pm}
transform like
\tau_{\pm}(C,\epsilon q)=\epsilon^{\kappa_{\pm}}\tau_{\pm}(C,q)
, where

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}=\frac{20(g-1)-4n}{3},\qquad\kappa_{-}=\frac{44(g-1)+20n}{3}.

(3.2)

The formulas (
3.2
) follow from formulas (6.54) and (6.55) of
[
8
]
taking into account an extra factor of
48
due to a different definition of
\tau_{\pm}
adopted here, see also (
4.4
).

Let us now describe the behavior of the tau functions under a change of the canonical homology basis that commutes with the action of the involution
\mu
. Note that
\tau_{+}
(resp.
\tau_{-}
) is uniquely determined by the subspace in
H_{+}
(resp.
H_{-}
) generated by the classes
\alpha_{i}^{+}
(resp.
\alpha_{j}^{-}
).

Let
\sigma
be a symplectic transformation of
H_{1}\big(\widehat{C},{\mathbb{R}}\big)
commuting with
\mu_{*}
that is given by the matrices
\sigma_{\pm}
in the basis
\{\alpha_{i}^{+},\beta_{i}^{+},\alpha_{j}^{-},\beta_{j}^{-}\}
,
i=1,\dots,g
,
j=1,\dots,3g-3+n
, as in (
2.4
).
Then the tau functions
\tau_{\pm}
transform under the action of
\sigma
by the formula

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{\pm}^{\sigma}}{\tau_{\pm}}=\gamma_{\pm}(\sigma_{\pm})\det(C_{\pm}\Omega_{\pm}+D_{\pm})^{48},

(3.3)

where
\gamma_{\pm}(\sigma_{\pm})^{3}=1
.

The proof of this statement for
n>0
is similar to the case
n=0

considered in
[
11
, Theorem 3]
. Namely, the transformation property (
3.3
) (with some
constants
\gamma_{\pm}
) follows from the differential equations
(
3.1
) and the transformation rule of the Bergman projective connections
{S_{B}}_{\pm}
under the change of canonical bases in
H_{\pm}
. The proof that
\gamma_{\pm}^{3}=1
uses explicit formulas for
\tau_{\pm}
which use the distinguished local parameters near the ramification points of the cover
C\to\widehat{C}
. The details can be found in
[
8
]
,
see formulas (6.46) and (6.50) therein.

The following statement is an immediate consequence of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
):

Theorem 3.3
.

For the tau function
\tau_{+}

(
resp.
\tau_{-})
its
3
rd power
\tau_{+}^{3}

\big(
resp.
\tau_{-}^{3}\big)
is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic section of the line bundle
\lambda^{144}\otimes L^{{20(g-1)-4n}}

\big(
resp.
\lambda_{\rm P}^{144}\otimes L^{44(g-1)+20n}\big)

on the
(
projectivized
)
principal stratum
P\mathcal{Q}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
, where
L
denotes the tautological line bundle on
P\mathcal{Q}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

The Hodge and Prym classes in the rational Picard group of
P\mathcal{Q}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
satisfy the relations

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{5(g-1)-n}{36}\phi,\qquad\lambda_{\rm P}=\frac{11(g-1)+5n}{36}\phi,

where
\phi=c_{1}(L)
.

4
The Hodge and Prym classes on
\boldsymbol{P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}}

Here we compute the divisor classes of the tau functions
\tau_{\pm}
viewed as holomorphic sections of line bundles on the compactification
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
. The
boundary
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}\setminus P\mathcal{Q}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
consists of divisors

D^{0}_{{\rm deg}}
,
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
and
D_{\rm DM}
. The asymptotic behaviour of
\tau_{\pm}
near
D^{0}_{{\rm deg}}
and
D_{\rm DM}
for
n>0
is the same as for
n=0
, the case considered in detail in
[
11
]
.

Namely, in terms of transversal local coordinate
t
near
D_{\rm DM}
we have

\displaystyle\tau_{\pm}=t^{4}(\text{const}+o(1))\qquad{\rm as}\ t\to 0,

(4.1)

cf. formulas (5.10) and (5.12) in
[
11
]
.

In terms of a local transversal coordinate
t
near
D^{0}_{{\rm deg}}

\displaystyle\tau_{+}=t^{2/3}(\text{const}+o(1)),\qquad\tau_{-}=t^{26/3}(\text{const}+o(1))

(4.2)

as
t\to 0
, cf. Lemmas 8 and 9 in
[
11
]
.

Therefore, it remains to analyze the asymptotics of
\tau_{\pm}
near the divisor

D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
.

4.1
Local behavior of tau functions near
\boldsymbol{D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}}

Recall that the boundary component
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
is the closure in
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
of the union of
n
copies of the set
{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-5+n},0,-1^{n-1}\big)
of equivalence classes of pairs
(C,q)
, where
C
is a smooth curve with a marked point and
q
is a quadratic differential with
n-1
labeled simple poles and
4g-5+n
simple zeros (the marked point is neither zero nor pole of
q
and distinguish the point where a zero and a pole of
q
coalesce).

Consider a one parameter family
(C_{t},q_{t})
of generic quadratic differentials transversal to
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
that converges to
(C_{0},q_{0})\in D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
as
t\to 0
(we can actually keep
C_{t}
fixed and ignore the rational tail of
C_{0}
). Without loss of generality we can assume that under such a degeneration a simple zero
x_{1}^{t}
and a simple pole
y_{1}^{t}
of
q_{t}
on
C_{t}
coalesce as
t\to 0
. Denote by
s_{t}\in H_{-}
the vanishing cycle on
\widehat{C}
going around the path connecting the preimages
\hat{x}_{1}^{t},\hat{y}_{1}^{t}\in\widehat{C}
, and denote by
t=\int_{s_{t}}v_{t}
the corresponding homological coordinate.

Assuming that the zero
x_{1}^{t}
and the pole
y_{1}^{t}
are close to each other, consider a small disk
U_{t}\subset C_{t}
that contains
x_{1}^{t}
,
y_{1}^{t}
and no other singularities of
q_{t}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The local coordinate
t=\int_{s_{t}}v_{t}
is transversal to
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
in a tubular neighborhood of
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma 8 in
[
11
]
. Namely, one can choose a coordinate
\zeta
in
U_{t}
such that

\displaystyle q(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta-\zeta\big(x_{1}^{t}\big)}{\zeta-\zeta\big(y_{1}^{t}\big)}{\rm d}\zeta^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle t=\int_{s_{t}}v_{t}=2\int_{\zeta(x_{1}^{t})}^{\zeta(y_{1}^{t})}\left(\frac{\zeta-\zeta\big(x_{1}^{t}\big)}{\zeta-\zeta\big(y_{1}^{t}\big)}\right)^{1/2}{\rm d}\zeta={\rm const}\big(\zeta\big(x_{1}^{t}\big)-\zeta\big(y_{1}^{t}\big)\big).

Therefore,
t=\int_{s_{t}}v_{t}
is transversal to
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The tau functions
\tau_{\pm}(C_{t},q_{t})
have the following asymptotic behaviour as
t\to 0
:

\displaystyle\tau_{+}(C_{t},q_{t})\sim t^{-8/3}\tau_{+}(C_{0},q_{0}),\qquad\tau_{-}(C_{t},q_{t})\sim t^{40/3}\tau_{-}(C_{0},q_{0}).

(4.3)

Proof.

First, let us notice that for an arbitrary stratum
{\mathcal{Q}}(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
, where
d_{1},\dots,d_{k}
are integers not less than
-1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}=4g-4
, the tau functions
\tau_{\pm}
have the following homogeneity property:

\tau_{\pm}(C,\epsilon q)=\epsilon^{\kappa_{\pm}}\tau_{\pm}(C,q)
with

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}=\sum_{i}\frac{d_{i}(d_{i}+4)}{d_{i}+2},\qquad\kappa_{-}=\kappa_{+}+6\sum_{d_{i}\,{\rm odd}}\frac{1}{d_{i}+2},

(4.4)

(see equations (6.54) and (6.55) of
[
8
]
, as well as Remark
3.2
). Now, in the limit
t\to 0
, we have for some
\gamma_{\pm}

\displaystyle\tau_{\pm}\sim t^{\gamma_{\pm}}\tau_{\pm}(C_{0},q_{0}).

To compute
\gamma_{\pm}
we evaluate the differences of the homogeneity coefficients (
4.4
)
on the strata
{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-4+n},-1^{n}\big)
and
{\mathcal{Q}}\big(1^{4g-5+n},0,-1^{n}\big)
,
i.e., when a pole and a zero annihilate each other (all other contributions remain the same):

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}(q)-\kappa_{+}(q_{0})=\frac{1\cdot 5}{3}+\frac{(-1)\cdot 3}{1}=-\frac{4}{3},

\displaystyle\kappa_{-}(q)-\kappa_{-}(q_{0})=\kappa_{+}(q)-\kappa_{+}(q_{0})+6\left(\frac{1}{3}+1\right)=\frac{20}{3}.

Since the homogeneity coefficient of the coordinate
t
is 1/2,
we have
\gamma_{+}=-8/3
and
\gamma_{-}=40/3
.
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.2
and its consequences

Combining Theorem
3.3
with the asymptotics (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) of
\tau_{\pm}
near
D_{\rm DM}
,
D^{0}_{{\rm deg}}
and
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
, we get the formulas

\displaystyle 48\lambda-\frac{20(g-1)-4n}{3}\phi=\frac{2}{3}\delta^{0}_{{\rm deg}}-\frac{8}{3}{\delta}^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}+4\delta_{\rm DM},

(4.5)

\displaystyle 48\lambda_{\rm P}-\frac{44(g-1)+20n}{3}\phi=\frac{26}{3}\delta^{0}_{{\rm deg}}+\frac{40}{3}{\delta}^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}+4\delta_{\rm DM},

(4.6)

which immediately imply Theorem
1.2
.

Excluding
\delta^{0}_{{\rm deg}}
from (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we obtain

Corollary 4.3
.

The Prym class
\lambda_{\rm P}
on
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
is expressed in terms of the Hodge class
\lambda
, the tautological class
\phi
and
the boundary classes
D_{\rm DM}
,
D^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
by the formula

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm P}-13\lambda={\delta}^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}-\delta_{\rm DM}-\frac{1}{2}(3g-3-n)\phi.

Furthermore, using the formula
\lambda_{\rm P}=\lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(3g-3+n)\phi
that relates the Prym class to the class
\lambda_{2}=\pi_{*}\omega^{2}_{g,n}
(see (
1.1
)), we get

Corollary 4.4
.

The following relation holds:

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}-13\lambda=n\phi+{\delta}^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}-\delta_{\rm DM}.

Combining this relation with the pullback to
P\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n}
of Mumford’s formula (
1.3
), we obtain the equation (
1.4
)

\displaystyle{\delta}^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}=-n\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}.

On the other hand, excluding
{\delta}^{\infty}_{{\rm deg}}
from (
4.5
) and
(
4.6
), we get the formula

\displaystyle\delta^{0}_{{\rm deg}}=72\lambda+4\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}-(10(g-1)+2n)\phi-6\delta_{\rm DM}

that coincides with the formula (1.2) in
[
11
]
for
n=0
.

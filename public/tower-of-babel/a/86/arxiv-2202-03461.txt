The Trace of T 2 T_{2} takes no repeated values
Liubomir Chiriac and Andrei Jorza

Abstract
. We prove that the trace of the Hecke operator T 2 T_{2} acting on the vector space of cusp forms of level one takes no repeated values, except for 0, which only occurs when the space is trivial.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 11F30, Secondary: 11B37, 11F85

1.
Introduction

Given two normalized cuspidal eigenforms of same level
N
but different weights, according to a result of Ghitza
[
Ghi11
]
, there exists an index
n\leq 4(\log N+1)^{2}
where the corresponding Fourier coefficients of the two forms differ. A consequence of this fact is that a newform of level one is determined by the first four Fourier coefficients. Numerical experiments suggest that the threshold can further be improved to
n=2
. In other words, to distinguish two newforms of level one it is enough to compare only their second Fourier coefficients. Since newforms are normalized so that their first coefficients are one,
n=2
is clearly the optimal bound in this case.

In fact, Vilardi and Xue
[
VX18
]
have shown that the
above statement holds under the assumption of Maeda’s conjecture
for the Hecke operator
T_{2}
. A key observation in

[
VX18
]
is that the trace of
T_{2}
does not take the
same value at two distinct weights that are close to each
other. It has been conjectured that this phenomenon happens in general, for any two distinct weights (see
[
XZ21
, Conjecture 3.7]
and
[
VX18
, Remark 3.6]
).

The purpose of this article is to prove the aforementioned conjecture. Throughout the paper, we denote by
{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
the trace of the Hecke operator
T_{2}
acting on the space
\mathcal{S}_{2k}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of cusp forms of weight
2k
and level one. Recall that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{S}_{2k}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))=0
precisely when
k<6
or
k=7
. In particular, the trace of
T_{2}
is trivially zero in those cases.

Theorem 1
.

There are no repeated values in the sequence
\{{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\}_{k\geq 2}
, except for 0, which only occurs trivially when
k<6
or
k=7
.

Our strategy consists of three main steps. First, using the Eichler–Selberg trace formula, we show that the trace is given by a recurrence relation of degree two. Second, we employ
p
-adic methods to reduce the range of possible coincidences for the trace of
T_{2}
, using an explicit formula for its 2-adic valuation, which was originally established by the authors in
[
CJ21
]
in a more general context. Finally, we apply effective results from the theory of linear forms in two logarithms to prove that the recurrence has no repeated values.

T_{2}

Consider the sequence

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Another object that appears in the formula is the polynomial
P_{2k}(t,n)
, defined as the coefficient of
x^{2k-2}
in the power series expansion of
(1-tx+nx^{2})^{-1}

With the above notation, the Eichler–Selberg trace formula (as given in the Appendix by Zagier to
[

]
) reads:

{\rm Tr~T}_{n}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|t|\leq 2\sqrt{n}}P_{2k}(t,n)H(4n-t^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\min(d,d^{\prime})^{2k-1}.

Specializing at
n=2
we prove the following combinatorial formula.

Lemma 3
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Then

{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))=(-2)^{k-2}-1-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k-2-j}{j}2^{j}(1+2^{2k-3-2j}).

Proof.

Given a power series
A(x)
, we denote by
[x^{n}]A(x)
the coefficient corresponding to
x^{n}
. This allows us to write
P_{2k}(t,n)
as follows,

\displaystyle P_{2k}(t,n)

\displaystyle=[x^{2k-2}]\frac{1}{1-tx+nx^{2}}

\displaystyle=[x^{2k-2}]\sum_{m\geq 0}\left(x(t-nx)\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k-1}^{2k-2}[x^{2k-2-m}]\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(-nx)^{j}t^{m-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k-1}^{2k-2}\binom{m}{2k-2-m}(-n)^{2k-2-m}t^{2m-(2k-2)}.

Setting
j=2k-2-m
we obtain

P_{2k}(t,n)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k-2-j}{j}n^{j}t^{2k-2-2j}.

In particular,
P_{2k}(t,n)=P_{2k}(-t,n)
. For
n=2
the trace formula yields

\displaystyle{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}P_{2k}(0,2)H(8)-P_{2k}(1,2)H(7)-P_{2k}(2,2)H(4)-1

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}P_{2k}(0,2)-P_{2k}(1,2)-\frac{1}{2}P_{2k}(2,2)-1.

Using the above explicit description of
P_{2k}(t,n)
we obtain the desired expression for the trace of
T_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

(i)

The terms of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
defined in (
1
) satisfy

a_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{2n-j}{j}2^{j}.

(ii)

For all integers
n\geq 0
we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{2n-j}{j}2^{n-j}=(-1)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.

Proof.

(i) The generating function defined by the sequence on the right-hand side is

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{2n-j}{j}2^{j}x^{n}.

With the change of variables
m=n-j
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\binom{2m+j}{j}(2x)^{j}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{x^{m}}{(1+2x)^{2m+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+2x}\sum_{m\geq 0}\left(\frac{x}{(1+2x)^{2}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+2x}\left(1-\frac{x}{(1+2x)^{2}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+2x}{1+3x+4x^{2}},

where in the first and third equalities we have used the negative binomial series (for integers
-d<0
)

(1+x)^{-d}=\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\binom{d+j-1}{j}x^{j}.

At the same time, the generating function
A(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}x^{n}
of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
can be found from the identity

\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(a_{n}+3a_{n-1}+4a_{n-2}\right)x^{n}=0,

which is equivalent to

(A(x)-1+x)+3x(A(x)-1)+4x^{2}A(x)=0.

This shows that

A(x)=\frac{1+2x}{1+3x+4x^{2}},

so the two sequences that appear in part (i) define the same generating function.

(ii) A similar computation shows that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{2n-j}{j}2^{n-j}x^{n}=\frac{1+x}{1+x^{2}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}x^{n}.

∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
2
.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

By combining Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
(for
n=k-1
) we see that

\displaystyle{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

\displaystyle=(-2)^{k-2}-1-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k-2-j}{j}2^{j}(1+2^{2k-3-2j})

\displaystyle=(-2)^{k-2}-1-a_{k-1}-(-1)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}2^{k-2}

\displaystyle=-a_{k-1}-1+2^{k-2}\left((-1)^{k}+(-1)^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\right),

and the conclusion follows by considering
k
modulo 4.
∎

3.
The 2-adic valuation of the trace of
T_{2}

In this section we give an explicit formula for the 2-adic valuation
v_{2}
of the trace of
T_{2}
. A more general formula, concerning the trace of
\wedge^{n}T_{2}
for
n\leq 15
, appears in
[
CJ21
, Theorem 12]
.

Proposition 5
.

For integers
k\gg 0
we have

v_{2}\left({\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\right)=3+v_{2}\left(k-\Omega\right),

where

\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\log_{2}(1-2\omega)}{\log_{2}(1-\omega)}+1\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Here the function
\log_{2}:1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to 2\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the
2
-adic logarithm, and
\omega=\frac{1-\sqrt{-7}}{2}
is embedded in
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
so that
v_{2}(\omega)=1
.

As stated, Proposition
5
is insufficient for our purposes.
By Proposition
2
,

{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))=-a_{k-1}-1+\varepsilon 2^{k-1},

where

\varepsilon=0,\pm 1
. As the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
is always odd,
the trace of
T_{2}
is always even, but its valuation is hard to
pin down except when
k\gg 0
, because one cannot control the
2-adic expansion of
\Omega
. The strategy of
[
CJ21
, Theorem
12]
is to write explicitly

{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))=\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}^{2k-1}}{\omega-\overline{\omega}}-1\right)-\frac{\omega^{2k-1}}{\omega-\overline{\omega}}+\varepsilon 2^{k-1},

(3)

where
\overline{\omega}=\frac{1+\sqrt{-7}}{2}
is the conjugate
of
\omega
, and to compute each valuation separately. As
k\gg 0
the valuation of the first term dominates. To account for this ineffective condition we need to prove a slightly more general
result.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
t
is a multiple of 8. Then

v_{2}\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}^{2k-1}}{\omega-\overline{\omega}}-1-t\right)=3+v_{2}(k-\Omega_{t}),

where

\Omega_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\log_{2}((1+t)(2\omega-1))}{\log_{2}(1-\omega)}+1\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Proof.

Note that
v_{2}(\overline{\omega})=v_{2}(1-\omega)=0
and

\omega-\overline{\omega}=-(1-2\omega)
is a unit. Therefore

\displaystyle v_{2}\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}^{2k-1}}{\omega-\overline{\omega}}-1-t\right)

\displaystyle=v_{2}\left(\overline{\omega}^{2k-1}+(1+t)(1-2\omega)\right)

\displaystyle=v_{2}\left(\overline{\omega}^{2k-1}-(1-\omega)^{2\Omega_{t}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=v_{2}\left((1-\omega)^{2(k-\Omega_{t})}-1\right)

\displaystyle=v_{2}((1-\omega)^{2}-1)+v_{2}(k-\Omega_{t})=3+v_{2}(k-\Omega_{t})

by the Lifting the Exponent Lemma. We remark that it is in the second equality that we use that
t
is a multiple of 8, for convergence reasons.

∎

6

4.
Repeated values in the sequence
\{a_{n}\}

The first few terms of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
defined in (
1
) are

1,-1,-1,7,-17,23,-1,-89,271,-457,287,\ldots

The purpose of this section is to show that, except for the three occurrences of the value -1, there are no other repeated values. Our main tool is the method of linear forms in logarithms.

If
\alpha
is an algebraic number with minimal polynomial

a_{m}x^{m}+\ldots+a_{0}=a_{m}(x-r_{1})\ldots(x-r_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}[x]

then its (logarithmic) height is given by

h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{m}\left(\log|a_{m}|+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(0,\log|r_{i}|)\right).

One of the sharpest estimates for linear forms in two logarithms is due to Laurent, Mignotte and Nesterenko; we recall here one of their results (
[
LMN95
, Théoréme 3]
).

Lemma 8
.

Let
\alpha
be an algebraic number, which is not a root of unity and
|\alpha|=1
. Let
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
be a positive integers, and

\Lambda=b_{1}i\pi-b_{2}\log\alpha.

Set
D=[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]/2
,
a=\max\{20,10.98|\log\alpha|+Dh(\alpha)\}
, and

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\max\{17,\frac{\sqrt{D}}{10},\log\left(\frac{b_{1}}{2a}+\frac{b_{2}}{68.9}\right)+2.35D+5.03\}.

Then

\log|\Lambda|\geq-8.87aH^{2}.

Remark 1
.

We will be concerned with algebraic numbers
\alpha
of degree 2, i.e., those for which
D=1
. In this setting, an easy consequence of the above result (see
[
Bug18
, Theorem 2.6]
for a proof) is that for all positive integers
n
we have

\log|\alpha^{n}-1|\geq-9aH_{1}^{2},

where
H_{1}=\max\{17,\log\left(\frac{n}{25}\right)+7.45\}
.

Proposition 9
.

Consider the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
defined in (
1
). There are no two distinct integers
5\leq n<m
such that
a_{n}=a_{m}.
In particular, as
k\geq 6
ranges over the integers congruent to 2 or 3 modulo 4, all the values of
{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
are distinct.

Proof.

Since
a_{n}=\frac{1}{\omega-\overline{\omega}}(\omega^{2n+1}-\overline{\omega}^{2n+1})
with
\omega=\frac{1-\sqrt{-7}}{2}
, it is enough to show that the equation

\omega^{n}-\overline{\omega}^{n}=\omega^{m}-\overline{\omega}^{m}

has no integer solutions
11\leq n<m
. Assume, by contradiction, that such a solution exists. Then we consider the 2-adic valuation of both sides of the identity

\omega^{n}(\omega^{m-n}-1)=-\overline{\omega}^{n}(1-\overline{\omega}^{m-n}).

(4)

On the one hand, since
v_{2}(\omega)=1
, it is clear that

v_{2}\left(\omega^{n}(\omega^{m-n}-1)\right)=v_{2}(\omega^{n})+v_{2}(\omega^{m-n}-1)\geq n.

On the other hand,
v_{2}(\overline{w})=0
and
v_{2}(1-\overline{w})=1
, so

\displaystyle v_{2}\left(-\overline{\omega}^{n}(1-\overline{\omega}^{m-n})\right)

\displaystyle=v_{2}(1-\overline{\omega}^{m-n})

\displaystyle\leq v_{2}(m-n)+2

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log(m)}{\log(2)}+2.

Hence (
4
) leads to the following inequality

n\leq\frac{\log(m)}{\log(2)}+2.

In turn, this implies that

\displaystyle|\omega^{m}-\overline{\omega}^{m}|

\displaystyle=|\omega^{n}-\overline{\omega}^{n}|

\displaystyle\leq 2|\omega|^{n}=2^{n/2+1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\log(m)/\log(4)+2},

or that

\log|\omega^{m}-\overline{\omega}^{m}|\leq\frac{\log(m)}{2}+\log(4).

(5)

Consider the algebraic number

\alpha=\frac{\omega}{\overline{\omega}}=-\frac{3}{4}-i\frac{\sqrt{7}}{4}.

The minimal polynomial of
\alpha
is
2x^{2}+3x+2
, so that

h(\alpha)=\log(2)/2
. We also note that
|\log(\alpha)|\approx 2.418858
, thus

10.98|\log\alpha|+h(\alpha)\approx 26.9056>20.
Finally,
\log\left(\frac{m}{25}\right)+7.45>17
for
m\geq 352000
. In this range, the results of Laurent, Mignotte and Nesterenko (see Remark
1
) imply that

\log|\alpha^{m}-1|\geq-9\cdot 26.9056\left(\log\left(\frac{m}{25}\right)+7.45\right)^{2}>-2^{9}(\log m)^{2},

and thus

\log|\omega^{m}-\overline{\omega}^{m}|>\frac{m}{2}\log(2)-2^{9}(\log m)^{2}.

(6)

Therefore, comparing (
6
) and (
5
), we obtain

\frac{m}{2}\log(2)-2^{9}(\log m)^{2}<\frac{\log(m)}{2}+\log(4),

which is false for
m>352000
. In other words, if a solution
11\leq n<m
existed then
m<352000
. However, with the help of a computer it can be easily verified that there are also no solutions in this range.
∎

5.
Repeated values in the sequences
\{a_{n}\pm 2^{n}\}

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 10
.

Consider the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
defined in (
1
) and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. The only pair of distinct positive integers
(n,m)
such that

a_{n}-\varepsilon 2^{n}=a_{m}-\varepsilon 2^{m}

(7)

is
(5,9)
, and this occurs when
\varepsilon=-1
. In particular, as
k\geq 8
ranges over the multiples of
4
, all the values of
{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
are distinct. Similarly, as
k\geq 9
ranges over the integers
\equiv 1\ (\text{mod}\ 4)
, all the values of
{\rm Tr~T_{2}}(2k,\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
are distinct.

Proof.

Suppose that (
7
) has a solution with
n<m
. First, we will show that

n\leq 3+\frac{\log(m)}{\log(2)}.

(8)

As before, set
\omega=\frac{1}{2}-i\frac{\sqrt{7}}{2}
so that
a_{m}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-7}}(\omega^{2m+1}-\overline{\omega}^{2m+1}).
Equation (
7
) implies

\varepsilon(2^{m}-2^{n})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{-7}}(\omega^{2n+1}-\omega^{2m+1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-7}}\overline{\omega}^{2n+1}(1-\overline{\omega}^{2m-2n}).

(9)

The left-hand side of (
9
) can also be expressed as

2^{n}\left(\varepsilon(2^{m-n}-1)+\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{-7}}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}\right)^{n}(1-\omega^{2m-2n})\right).

Since
v_{2}(\omega)=1
and
v_{2}(\overline{\omega})=0
, we find that the 2-adic valuation of the above expression is

\displaystyle n+v_{2}\left(\varepsilon(2^{m-n}-1)+\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{-7}}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}\right)^{n}(1-\omega^{2m-2n})\right)

\displaystyle\geq n+\min\left\{v_{2}(2^{m-n}-1),v_{2}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{-7}}\left(\frac{\omega}{\overline{\omega}}\right)^{n}(1-\omega^{2m-2n})\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=n+\min\{0,1+n\}=n.

At the same time, the 2-adic valuation of the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle v_{2}\left(1-(\overline{\omega}^{2})^{m-n}\right)

\displaystyle=v_{2}\left(1-(\overline{\omega}^{2})\right)+v_{2}(m-n)

\displaystyle=3+v_{2}(m-n)

\displaystyle\leq 3+\frac{\log(m)}{\log(2)}.

Together, the previous two inequalities imply (

and

\displaystyle|a_{n}-\varepsilon 2^{n}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\omega}{\omega-\overline{\omega}}(\omega^{2})^{n}-\frac{\overline{\omega}}{\omega-\overline{\omega}}(\overline{\omega}^{2})^{n}-\varepsilon 2^{n}\right|

\displaystyle<3\cdot 2^{n}

\displaystyle<3\cdot 2^{3+\log(m)/\log(2)},

or equivalently

\log|a_{m}-\varepsilon 2^{m}|=\log|a_{n}-\varepsilon 2^{n}|\leq\log(24m).

(10)

Our next step will be to find a lower bound for
\log|a_{m}-\varepsilon 2^{m}|
. Writing
w=\sqrt{2}e^{i\theta}
, with
\theta\in[\frac{3\pi}{2},2\pi]
, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{m}-\varepsilon 2^{m}

\displaystyle=2^{m}\left(\frac{\sin((2m+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta}-\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=-2^{m}\sqrt{\frac{8}{7}}\left(\sin((2m+1)\theta)-\sin(\varepsilon\theta)\right),

(11)

since
\sin\theta=-\sqrt{\frac{7}{8}}
.

If
\varepsilon=1
we see that

Effective Field Theory of Dipolar Braiding Statistics in Two Dimensions
Yun-Tak Oh, Jintae Kim, Jung Hoon Han

Abstract
A rank-2 toric code (R2TC) Hamiltonian in two dimensions can be constructed as a Higgsed descendant of rank-2 U(1) lattice gauge theory. As noted by the authors recently, [Y.-T. Oh, J. Kim, E.-G. Moon, and J. H. Han, Phys. Rev. B 105 , 045128] the quasiparticles in that model shows unusual braiding statistics that depends on the initial locations of the particles which participate in the braiding. We show that this new kind of statistical phase captures the total dipole moment of quasiparticles encompassed in the braiding, in contrast to the conventional anyonic braiding seeing the total charge. An Aharonov-Bohm interpretation of such dipolar braiding statistics is made in terms of emergent, rank-1 vector potentials that are built out of the underlying rank-2 gauge fields. Pertinent field theories of the quasiparticle dynamics in the R2TC are developed, and the accompanying conservation laws derived. A dipolar BF theory of the rank-2 gauge fields is constructed and shown to correctly capture the dipolar braiding statistics, in contrast to the conventional BF theory capturing the monopolar braiding statistics of anyons in the rank-1 toric code.

II
Review of R2TC

The R2TC model is obtained by Higgsing the rank-2 U(1) LGT in two dimensions employing a pair of canonically conjugate fields
A^{ab}_{i}
and
E^{ab}_{i}
obeying
[A,E]=i
for the same site and indices. There are three independent components
(A^{xx}_{i},A^{xy}_{i},A^{yy}_{i})
per site
i
(similarly for
E
). Several mutually commuting operators called generators can be constructed in the rank-2 LGT as:

\displaystyle G^{x}_{i}=

\displaystyle E_{i+\hat{x}}^{xx}-E_{i}^{xx}+E_{i+\hat{y}}^{xy}-E_{i}^{xy}\equiv\rho_{i}^{x}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\partial_{x}E^{xx}_{i}+\partial_{y}E^{xy}_{i},

\displaystyle G^{y}_{i}=

\displaystyle E_{i+\hat{x}}^{xy}-E_{i}^{xy}+E_{i+\hat{y}}^{yy}-E_{i}^{yy}\equiv\rho_{i}^{y}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\partial_{x}E^{xy}_{i}+\partial_{y}E^{yy}_{i},

\displaystyle B_{i}=

\displaystyle A^{xx}_{i+\hat{y}}+A^{xx}_{i-\hat{y}}-2A^{xx}_{i}+A^{yy}_{i+\hat{x}}+A^{yy}_{i-\hat{x}}-2A^{yy}_{i}

\displaystyle~~-A^{xy}_{i}+A^{xy}_{i-\hat{x}}+A^{xy}_{i-\hat{y}}-A^{xy}_{i-\hat{x}-\hat{y}}\equiv\frac{2\pi}{N}\rho_{i}^{m}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\partial_{y}^{2}A^{xx}_{i}+\partial_{x}^{2}A^{yy}_{i}-\partial_{x}\partial_{y}A^{xy}_{i}.

(1)

The theory contains a vector of electric charges
(\rho_{i}^{x},\rho_{i}^{y})
and a scalar magnetic charge
\rho_{i}^{m}
, tied to the gauge fields through the Gauss law constraints
G_{i}^{a}=\rho_{i}^{a}
(
a=x,y)
and
B_{i}\equiv(2\pi/N)\rho_{i}^{m}
for some integer

. They generate the gauge transformations
A^{ab}_{i}\rightarrow U_{A}^{\dagger}A_{i}^{ab}U_{A}
and
E^{ab}_{i}\rightarrow U_{E}^{\dagger}E_{i}^{ab}U_{E}
which result in

\displaystyle A^{aa}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{aa}_{i}+f^{a}_{i}-f^{a}_{i-\hat{a}}~~(a=x,y)

\displaystyle A^{xy}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{xy}_{i}+f^{y}_{i}-f^{y}_{i-\hat{x}}+f^{x}_{i}-f^{x}_{i-\hat{y}}

\displaystyle E^{xx}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow E^{xx}_{i}+g_{i+\hat{y}}+g_{i-\hat{y}}-2g_{i}

\displaystyle E^{yy}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow E^{yy}_{i}+g_{i+\hat{x}}+g_{i-\hat{x}}-2g_{i}

\displaystyle E^{xy}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow E^{xy}_{i}+g_{i+\hat{x}}+g_{i+\hat{y}}-g_{i}-g_{i+\hat{x}+\hat{y}}.

(2)

The two unitary operators are

\displaystyle U_{A}=e^{i\sum_{i}(f_{i}^{x}G_{i}^{x}+f_{i}^{y}G_{i}^{y})},~~U_{E}=e^{i\sum_{i}g_{i}B_{i}}.

(3)

Generalized Pauli operators are obtained from Higging the gauge fields as
[
24
,
25
]
:

\displaystyle X_{0,i}=e^{iA_{i}^{xy}},

\displaystyle Z_{0,i}=e^{2\pi iE_{i}^{xy}/N},

\displaystyle X_{1,i}=e^{iA_{i}^{xx}},

\displaystyle Z_{1,i}=e^{2\pi iE_{i}^{xx}/N},

\displaystyle X_{2,i}=e^{iA_{i}^{yy}},

\displaystyle Z_{2,i}=e^{2\pi iE_{i}^{yy}/N}.

(4)

They operate on the
N
-dimensional local Hilbert space defined by

\displaystyle X|g\rangle=|g+1\rangle,~Z|g\rangle=\omega^{g}|g\rangle,~ZX=\omega XZ,

(5)

where
|g\rangle=|0\rangle,~|1\rangle,~\cdots,~|N-1\rangle
and
\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}
. Applying the Higgsing formulas in Eq. (
4
) to the three mutually commuting generators in Eq. (
1
) results in three types of commuting spin operators:

\displaystyle a_{i}^{x}

\displaystyle\equiv e^{(2\pi i/N)G^{x}_{i}}=Z_{1,i}^{-1}Z_{1,i+\hat{x}}Z_{0,i}Z_{0,i-\hat{y}}^{-1},

\displaystyle a_{i}^{y}

\displaystyle\equiv e^{(2\pi i/N)G^{y}_{i}}=Z_{2,i}^{-1}Z_{2,i+\hat{x}}Z_{0,i}Z_{0,i-\hat{x}}^{-1},

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv e^{iB_{i}}=X_{0,i}^{-1}X_{0,i-\hat{x}}X_{0,i-\hat{y}}X_{0,i-\hat{x}-\hat{y}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\times X_{2,i-\hat{x}}X_{2,i}^{-2}X_{2,i+\hat{x}}X_{1,i-\hat{y}}X_{1,i}^{-2}X_{1,i+\hat{y}}.

(6)

The R2TC Hamiltonian is constructed as

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{i}^{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(a_{i}^{x})^{j},

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{i}^{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(a_{i}^{y})^{j},

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(b_{i})^{j},

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}(\mathbb{A}^{x}_{i}+\mathbb{A}^{y}_{i}+\mathbb{B}_{i}).

(7)

This model has the ground states characterized by
\mathbb{A}_{i}^{x}|{\rm GS}\rangle=\mathbb{A}_{i}^{y}|{\rm GS}\rangle=\mathbb{B}_{i}|{\rm GS}\rangle=|{\rm GS}\rangle
. An excited state is obtained when one of the stabilizers
a_{i}^{x}
,
a_{i}^{y}
, or
b_{i}
takes on the eigenvalue

with nonzero integer

, and

to denote the two electric and one magnetic charges associated with

,
a_{i}^{y}
, and
b_{i}
excitations, respectively. The

(

) monopole can move freely in the

-(

-) direction, but can hop only by

lattice spacings in the

- (
x
-) direction; the magnetic monopole can only hop by

spacings in both directions

IV
Matter Fields with Minimal Coupling

There are three matter fields in the R2TC, represented by field operators
(\psi_{x},\psi_{y},\psi_{m})
, which are related to the monopole density by
\rho^{a}=\psi_{a}^{\dagger}\psi_{a}

(a=x,y,m)

2

V
Dipolar BF theory

Comparing the temporal derivatives of the constraints in Eq. (
1
) with the continuity equation in Eq. (
26
) allows the identification of several identities:

\displaystyle J_{e}^{ab}=-\partial_{t}{E}^{ab},\frac{2\pi}{N}J_{m}^{xx}=-\partial_{t}{A}^{yy},

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{N}J_{m}^{xy}=\partial_{t}{A}^{xy},\frac{2\pi}{N}J_{m}^{yy}=-\partial_{t}{A}^{xx}.

(30)

The Lagrangian that encodes all these constraints as well as the commutation
[A^{ab}({\bm{r}}),E^{ab}({\bm{r}}^{\prime})]=i\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}^{\prime})
is

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm dBF}

\displaystyle=A^{0x}\left(\partial_{x}E^{xx}\!+\!\partial_{y}E^{xy}-\rho^{x}\right)+A^{0y}\left(\partial_{x}E^{xy}\!+\!\partial_{y}E^{yy}-\rho^{y}\right)+E^{0}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}A^{yy}\!+\!\partial_{y}^{2}A^{xx}\!-\!\partial_{x}\partial_{y}A^{xy}-\frac{2\pi}{N}\rho^{m}\right)

\displaystyle+E^{xx}\left(\partial_{t}{A}^{xx}\!+\!\frac{2\pi}{N}J_{m}^{yy}\right)+E^{xy}\left(\partial_{t}{A}^{xy}\!-\!\frac{2\pi}{N}J_{m}^{xy}\right)+E^{yy}\left(\partial_{t}{A}^{yy}\!+\!\frac{2\pi}{N}J_{m}^{xx}\right)-A^{xx}J_{e}^{xx}\!-\!A^{xy}J_{e}^{xy}\!-\!A^{yy}J_{e}^{yy}.

(31)

This action is the
dipolar BF theory
(dBF) in contrast to the BF theory of the rank-1 TC. The Lagrange multipliers transform as

\displaystyle A^{0a}

\displaystyle\!\rightarrow\!A^{0a}\!+\!\partial_{t}f^{a},

\displaystyle E^{0}\!\rightarrow\!E^{0}\!+\!\partial_{t}g.

(32)

The conservation laws of Eq. (
26
) are recovered from the gauge invariance of the action.

The dipolar AB phase factors follow straightforwardly from the dBF action, but before doing so one must first address the important conceptual question: how can one adiabatically move a particle when its motion is constrained? For instance, the
m
particle cannot move at all in the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
due to the dipole conservation, and
e_{x}
(
e_{y}
) can only move smoothly in the
x
(
y
) direction. Nevertheless, by making use of the translation operators defined in Eqs. (
14
) and (
19
), it seems as though the monopoles can be freely moved around. Upon careful analysis of the lattice translation operators, we realize that such motion becomes possible at the expense of creating an auxiliary dipole that compensates for the changes in the dipole moment during the monopole motion, as explicitly demonstrated in Appendix
B
. The composite particle of a monopole and an auxiliary dipole carries a net dipole moment of zero, and are free from constraint.

We show how to derive the dipolar braiding phase explicitly in the the continuum theory. As an
m
particle initially at
{\bm{r}}_{m0}=(x_{m0},y_{m0})
is moved to
{\bm{r}}_{m}(t)=(x_{m}(t),y_{m}(t))
, the monopole and auxiliary dipole composite has the density

\displaystyle\rho^{m}({\bm{r}})=[1+({\bm{r}}_{m}(t)-{\bm{r}}_{m0})\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}]\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m}(t)).

(33)

The second part reflects the dipole contribution. Details of the derivation can be found in Appendix
B
. The total dipole moment
{\bm{\mu}}_{m}=\int d^{2}{\bm{r}}{\bm{r}}\rho^{m}({\bm{r}})
is indeed conserved. When the position
{\bm{r}}_{m}
varies over time, the time derivative of the total density
\rho^{m}
becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{m}({\bm{r}})=

\displaystyle-\dot{x}_{m}\left[(x_{m}-x_{m0})\partial_{x}^{2}\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m})+(y_{m}-y_{m0})\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m})\right]

\displaystyle-\dot{y}_{m}\left[(x_{m}-x_{m0})\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m})+(y_{m}-y_{m0})\partial_{y}^{2}\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m})\right].

(34)

Invoking the continuity equation for
\rho^{m}
in Eq. (
26
), one can deduce the accompanying current density

\displaystyle J_{m}^{xx}

\displaystyle=\dot{x}_{m}(x_{m}-x_{m0})\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m}),

\displaystyle J_{m}^{xy}

\displaystyle=\left(\dot{x}_{m}(x_{m}-x_{m0})+\dot{y}_{m}(y_{m}-y_{m0})\right)\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m}),

\displaystyle J_{m}^{yy}

\displaystyle=\dot{y}_{m}(y_{m}-y_{m0})\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{m}).

(35)

Finally, inserting the obtained current density into the dBF action gives

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{N}\int dt\int d^{2}{\bm{r}}\left[E^{xx}J_{m}^{yy}-E^{xy}J_{m}^{xy}+E^{yy}J_{m}^{xx}\right]

\displaystyle=\oint d{\bm{r}}_{m}\cdot{\bm{a}}_{m}({\bm{r}_{m}}),

(36)

precisely equal to the statistical phase
\phi_{m,e}
from braiding
m
around
e_{x},e_{y}
quasiparticles.

Likewise when the
e_{x}

{\bm{r}}_{x0}=(x_{x0},y_{x0})

\displaystyle\rho^{x}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle\rho^{y}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}\!-\!{\bm{r}}_{y}(t))+(x_{y}(t)\!-\!x_{y0})\partial_{x}\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}\!-\!{\bm{r}}_{y}(t)).

\displaystyle J^{xx}_{e}=

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{x}\left[1+(y_{x}-y_{x0})\partial_{y}\right]\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{x}),

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{x}(y_{x}-y_{x0})\partial_{y}\delta^{2}({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{x}),

VI
Discussion

Field-theoretic formulation for the dipolar braiding statistics first found in the R2TC model is developed.
The statistical phases count the net dipole moment rather than the charge of the quasiparticles enclosed in the braiding. The adiabatic motion of a quasiparticle is possible when accompanied by the simultaneous motion of auxiliary dipole, which helps conserve the total dipole moment. The dipolar braiding statistics is derived by solving the equation of motion for quasiparticles.

Various field theories of dipolar nature have been proposed in the past in three dimensions
[
18
,
23
]
. Our paper proposes a dipolar field theory in two dimensions along with the accompanying dipolar statistics. While this work was under review, several papers analyzed the ground state degeneracy and the dipolar braiding statistics from a complementary perspective of anyon lattice
[
29
,
30
]
, and proposed a Chern-Simons-type field (Ref.
[
29
]
) or a combination of Chern-Simons and dipolar Chern-Simons theory descriptions (Ref.
[
30
]
). It remains to explore how the dipolar BF theory proposed in this work relates to these other theories. An important part of the specification of the topological field theory is that of the global structure under the so-called large gauge transformation. We have not completely worked out the global structure of the dipolar BF theory or the related ground state degeneracy count in this paper. These problems will be addressed in a forthcoming article
[
31
]
.

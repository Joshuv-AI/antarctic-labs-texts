Milnor and Tjurina numbers for an isolated complete intersection singularity
A.J. Parameswaran, Mohit Upmanyu

Abstract
This paper aims to prove that given a isolated complete intersection singularity, the Milnor number will be bounded by a bound depending only on Tjurina number and dimension of the singularity. The proof uses A 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} AC (introduced in [ PU22 ] ) and as with such methods, the bound is purely existential.

3
Main Theorem

Before we get to the Theorem we would need to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f=(f_{1},...,f_{k}):\Spec A\to\mathbb{A}^{k}
. Let
X:=f^{-1}(0)\subset\Spec(A)
be an isolated complete intersection singularity. Then
\mu(X)\leq
the multiplicity of critical locus w.r.t.
\langle f_{1},...,f_{k}\rangle

Proof.

We use formula from
[
Loo84
, 5.11.a]
, to conclude that

\mu(X)\leq\len(\frac{A}{J_{k}(f)+\langle g_{1},...,g_{k-1}\rangle})

Here
g_{1},...,g_{k-1}
can be any linear combinations of
f_{1},...,f_{k}
.

Now By
[
Loo84
, 4.4]

\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}}
is finite and Cohen-Macaulay over
\mathbb{C}[\![f_{1},...,f_{k}]\!]
of dimension
k-1
. So for
k-1
general linear combinations
g_{1},...,g_{k-1}
,
\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}}
is finite and Cohen-Macaulay over
\mathbb{C}[\![g_{1},...,g_{k-1}]\!]
. Now the projective dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}}
over
\mathbb{C}[\![g_{1},...,g_{k-1}]\!]
is
\dim\mathbb{C}[\![g_{1},...,g_{k-1}]\!]-depth(\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}})
=
k-1-\dim\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}}
=
0
. So
\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}}
is a free module over
\mathbb{C}[\![g_{1},...,g_{k-1}]\!]

We can also assume
e(\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}},\langle f_{1},...,f_{k}\rangle)
=
e(\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}},\langle g_{1},...,g_{k-1}\rangle)
, using
[
Mat89
, Theorem 14.14]
.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}}
is a free module over
\mathbb{C}[\![g_{1},...,g_{k-1}]\!]
.

e(\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}},\langle g_{1},...,g_{k-1}\rangle)=rank(\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}})=\len\frac{\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}}}{\langle g_{1},...,g_{k-1}\rangle}=\len(\frac{A}{J_{k}(f)+\langle g_{1},...,g_{k-1}\rangle})

.

We can finally combine all the above to get

\mu(X)\leq\len(\frac{A}{J_{k}(f)+\langle g_{1},...,g_{k-1}\rangle})=e(\mathcal{O}_{C_{f}},\langle f_{1},...,f_{k}\rangle)

∎

Using the observation that
\mu(X)\leq
the multiplicity of critical locus w.r.t.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f=(f_{1},...,f_{k}):\Spec A\to\mathbb{A}^{k}
. Let
X:=f^{-1}(0)\subset\Spec(A)
be an isolated complete intersection singularity with Tjurina number
\tau
. Then there abstractly exists a constant
c>0
such that, the multiplicity of
C_{f}
w.r.t.
\langle f_{1},...,f_{k}\rangle

\leq c
. This
c
depends only on
\tau
and
n

Proof.

Note That
D(1)=\bigcap_{e}C(e)
.

For this proof we fix
k=\tau
. Since Tjurina number is the dimension of base of a mini-versal deformation
[
Loo84
]
, we see that all possible icis with Tjurina number
\tau
must occur within codimension
k=\tau
. As a consequence
T(\tau)
is a collection of all possible icis which satisfy the hypothesis of the Theorem (albeit with repetition up to isomorphism). (Using this we have shown the bound does not depend on
k
)

Assume for a contradiction that the multiplicity of
C_{f}
w.r.t.
\langle f_{1},...,f_{\tau}\rangle
is not bounded on the set
T(\tau)
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&T(\tau)\cap C(e)\neq\emptyset\quad\forall e\text{ sufficiently larger than }\tau\\
\implies&T(\tau)\cap\bigcap_{e}C(e)\neq\emptyset\quad\text{ (by Lemma \ref{countable intersection lemma})}\\
\implies&T(\tau)\cap D(1)\neq\emptyset\end{split}

This contradicts the fact that any
(f_{1},...,f_{\tau})\in T(\tau)
can only define icis.
∎

.

Proof.

We use
3.1
to conclude

the multiplicity of critical locus w.r.t.
\langle f_{1},...,f_{\tau}\rangle
, so the result follows from
3.4

∎

Lemma 4.1

T(r)
(defined in
3.2
.1) is both A
\mathfrak{m}

\mathfrak{m}
AO

Proof.

We look at
B_{r+2}=(A/\mathfrak{m}^{r+2})^{k}
regarded as an affine space. Define
\pi:A^{k}\to B_{r+2}
to be the canonical quotient map.

Using semi continuity of Tjurina number,
\overline{T}:=\{(\overline{f}_{1},...,\overline{f}_{k})\in B_{r+2}\ |\ \len(T^{1}_{X}/\mathfrak{m}^{r+1}T^{1}_{X})=r\}
is a Zariski constructible subset.

We will prove that

First, let
(f_{1},...,f_{k})\in T(r)

\len(T^{1}_{X})=r

\mathfrak{m}^{r}T^{1}_{X}=0

T^{1}_{X}=T^{1}_{X}/\mathfrak{m}^{r+1}T^{1}_{X}

Let
(f_{1},...,f_{k})\in\pi^{-1}(\overline{T})

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\len(\frac{T^{1}_{X}}{\mathfrak{m}^{r+1}T^{1}_{X}})=r\\
\implies&\mathfrak{m}^{r}(\frac{T^{1}_{X}}{\mathfrak{m}^{r+1}T^{1}_{X}})=0\\
\implies&\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{r}T^{1}_{X}}{\mathfrak{m}^{r+1}T^{1}_{X}}=0\\
\implies&\mathfrak{m}^{r}T^{1}_{X}=\mathfrak{m}^{r+1}T^{1}_{X}\\
\implies&\mathfrak{m}^{r}T^{1}_{X}=0\text{ (By Nakyama lemma)}\\
\implies&T^{1}_{X}=T^{1}_{X}/\mathfrak{m}^{r+1}T^{1}_{X}\\
\end{split}

So we can conclude
\len(T^{1}_{X})=r
, hence
(f_{1},...,f_{k})\in T(r)
.
∎

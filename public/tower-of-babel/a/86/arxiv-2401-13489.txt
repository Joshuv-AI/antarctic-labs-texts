Constructing monoidal structures on fibered categories via factorizations
Luca Terenzi

Abstract
. Let 𝒮 \mathscr{S} be a small category, and suppose that we are given two (non-full) subcategories 𝒮 s ​ m \mathscr{S}^{sm} and 𝒮 c ​ l \mathscr{S}^{cl} that generate all morphisms of 𝒮 \mathscr{S} under composition in the same way as morphisms of quasi-projective algebraic varieties are generated by smooth morphisms and closed immersions. We show that a monoidal structure on a given 𝒮 \mathscr{S} -fibered category is completely determined by its restrictions to 𝒮 s ​ m \mathscr{S}^{sm} and 𝒮 c ​ l \mathscr{S}^{cl} ; in fact, any such pair of monoidal structures satisfying a natural coherence condition uniquely determines a monoidal structure over the whole of 𝒮 \mathscr{S} . The same principle applies to morphisms of 𝒮 \mathscr{S} -fibered categories and monoidality thereof. Under further assumptions on the two subcategories 𝒮 s ​ m \mathscr{S}^{sm} and 𝒮 c ​ l \mathscr{S}^{cl} , and with suitable restrictions on the 𝒮 \mathscr{S} -fibered categories and morphisms involved, we provide a variant of the above method in which inverse images under closed immersion are partially replaced by the corresponding direct images; the latter variant is more adapted to the setting of perverse sheaves.

Introduction

Motivation and goal of the paper

When studying a small category
\mathscr{S}
, it often proves useful to single out some distinguished classes of morphisms that one can control with relatively little effort; if these distinguished classes generate all morphisms in
\mathscr{S}
under composition, one can hope to gain some control on the whole of
\mathscr{S}
in this way. This principle is particularly effective in Algebraic Geometry, where results about the existence of distinguished factorizations for reasonable morphisms of schemes are among the most useful auxiliary tools in several situations.
For the purposes of the present paper, we are interested in the abstract setting of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories, regarded as contravariant pseudo-functors with domain
\mathscr{S}
. There is a general factorization principle to turn collections of categories parametrized by
\mathscr{S}
into actual
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories: roughly speaking, one constructs inverse image functors along arbitrary morphisms of
\mathscr{S}
by first defining inverse images for distinguished classes of morphisms and then pasting the partial constructions together. However, as the reader can guess, this idea cannot really work in practice unless the partial constructions are compatible in a precise sense.

In order to illustrate the general problem with a concrete example, consider the category
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
of quasi-projective algebraic varieties over a field
k
. Among all morphisms in
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
there exist two distinguished classes with particularly nice properties: the class of smooth morphisms and the class of closed immersions. It is known that every morphism
f:T\rightarrow S
in
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
admits some factorization of the form

(1)

f:T\xrightarrow{t}P\xrightarrow{p}S

with
t
a closed immersion and
p
a smooth morphism. We want to use these morphisms as our basic building blocks. So suppose that we are given a collection of categories
\left\{\mathbb{H}(S)\right\}_{S\in\mathsf{Var}_{k}}
that we want to turn into a
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
-fibered category: this amounts to constructing inverse image functors along all morphisms of
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
compatibly with composition in the natural way. Suppose also that we already know how to do this separately for the classes of smooth morphisms and closed immersions. Given a general morphism
f:T\rightarrow S
in
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
, one can try to define the inverse image functor
f^{*}:\mathbb{H}(S)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}(T)
as the composite

f^{*}:\mathbb{H}(S)\xrightarrow{p^{*}}\mathbb{H}(P)\xrightarrow{t^{*}}\mathbb{H}(T)

according to a factorization of the above form (
1
). The whole point is to show that the result is independent of the chosen factorization of
f
and that it is compatible with composition of morphisms in
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
. Assuming to have a positive answer to the former question, let us focus on the latter.
Given a second morphism
g:S\rightarrow V
in
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
, with factorization

g:S\xrightarrow{s}Q\xrightarrow{q}V

into a closed immersion
s
followed by a smooth morphism
q
, one gets a factorization of the composite morphism
gf:T\rightarrow V
as

gf:T\xrightarrow{t}P\xrightarrow{p}S\xrightarrow{s}Q\xrightarrow{q}V.

Since the latter does not quite have the correct form, one is led to choose a further factorization of the composite morphism
sp:P\rightarrow Q
as

sp:P\xrightarrow{h}L\xrightarrow{l}Q

with
h
a closed immersion and
l
a smooth morphism. In this way, one can finally write
gf
as

gf:T\xrightarrow{ht}L\xrightarrow{lq}V

where now, by construction,
ht
is a closed immersion while
lq
is a smooth morphism. At this point, if one wants to compare the composite of inverse images

f^{*}g^{*}:\mathbb{H}(V)\xrightarrow{q^{*}}\mathbb{H}(Q)\xrightarrow{s^{*}}\mathbb{H}(S)\xrightarrow{p^{*}}\mathbb{H}(P)\xrightarrow{t^{*}}\mathbb{H}(T)

with the inverse image of the composite

(gf)^{*}:\mathbb{H}(V)\xrightarrow{q^{*}}\mathbb{H}(Q)\xrightarrow{l^{*}}\mathbb{H}(L)\xrightarrow{p^{*}}\mathbb{H}(P)\xrightarrow{t^{*}}\mathbb{H}(T),

one needs to compare the two possible paths of inverse image functors along the commutative square

(2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}

The theory of
exchange structures
developed in
[
Ayo07a
, § 1.2]
provides a rigorous method for this: it is based on the datum of additional natural isomorphisms attached to squares of the form (
2
) which are required to satisfy a suitable compatibility condition with respect to vertical and horizontal concatenation of such squares.

The main goal of this paper is to adapt the factorization principle to other construction problems within the theory of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories: specifically, we are interested in morphisms of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories, monoidal structures on
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories, and monoidal morphisms of such. Compared to the case of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories just discussed, these questions turn out to be somewhat more elementary, since the task becomes to construct natural transformations between already existing functors rather than to construct functors themselves: coherence of natural transformations is a property rather than an additional structure.

Our main motivation behind this work comes from the theory of perverse sheaves over quasi-projective algebraic varieties, where one wants to exploit the factorization of morphisms into closed immersions and smooth morphisms as displayed above. To fix notation, let
k
be a subfield of the complex numbers, and consider again the category
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
of quasi-projective
k
-varieties. To every
S\in\mathsf{Var}_{k}
one can attach the algebraically constructible
\mathbb{Q}
-linear derived category
D^{b}_{c}(S,\mathbb{Q})
, a tensor-triangulated category. As
S
varies, these enjoy a full six functor formalism and, in particular, assemble into a monoidal
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
-fibered category. The theory of
[
BBD82
]
yields a perverse
t
-structure on
D^{b}_{c}(S,\mathbb{Q})
, whose heart is the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear category of
perverse sheaves
, denoted
\mathsf{Perv}(S)
. Beilinson’s classical result
[
Bei87
, Thm. 1.3]
asserts that the triangulated category
D^{b}_{c}(S,\mathbb{Q})
is canonically equivalent to
D^{b}(\mathsf{Perv}(S))
. This brings the question of how much of the six functor formalism can be reconstructed from the abelian categories
\mathsf{Perv}(S)
.
What makes this question non-trivial is the fact that most functors belonging to the six functor formalism are not
t
-exact with respect to the perverse
t
-structure. For instance, the usual tensor product functor

-\otimes-:D^{b}_{c}(S,\mathbb{Q})\times D^{b}_{c}(S,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(S,\mathbb{Q})

does not respect the perverse
t
-structure unless
\dim(S)=0
. As a remedy, in our paper
[
Ter24T
]
we showed that the entire structure of the constructible derived categories as a monoidal
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
-fibered category can be rephrased in terms of the external tensor product functors

-\boxtimes-:D^{b}_{c}(S_{1},\mathbb{Q})\times D^{b}_{c}(S_{2},\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(S_{1}\times S_{2},\mathbb{Q}),

which are always
t
-exact (see
[
BBD82
, Prop. 4.2.8]
). The usual associativity, symmetry, and (to some extent) unitarity properties of the single tensor product functors can be expressed in terms of
t
-exact functors, so they can be tested directly on the level of perverse sheaves.
However, a fundamental part of the structure of monoidal
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
-fibered category is encoded in the monoidality isomorphisms for inverse image functors. Since the inverse image under a general morphism in
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
does not enjoy any
t
-exactness property, one cannot hope to express monoidality purely in terms of
t
-exact functors just by replacing the usual tensor product by the external one. On the other hand, it turns out that perverse sheaves are sufficiently well-behaved with respect to smooth morphisms and closed immersions: for a smooth morphism
p:P\rightarrow S
, the inverse image
p^{*}
is
t
-exact up to appropriate shiftings (see
[
BBD82
, § 4.2.4]
), while, for a closed immersion
t:T\rightarrow P
, it is the direct image
t_{*}
that is
t
-exact (see
[
BBD82
, Cor. 4.1.3]
). This suggests that, when working with the external tensor product, it should be possible to reconstruct monoidality under a general morphism
f:T\rightarrow S
in terms of natural isomorphisms of
t
-exact functors, by means of a factorization of the form (
1
). This turns out to be the case, but with a caveat: rephrasing monoidality of inverse images under closed immersions as a property of the corresponding direct images is not a purely formal step, since it crucially requires an important property of the six functor formalism known as
localization
.

What we have just explained in the setting of perverse sheaves could be repeated, more generally, for systems of triangulated categories satisfying the six functor formalism and endowed with reasonable perverse
t
-structures. The most prominent example is M. Saito’s celebrated theory of
mixed Hodge modules
, developed in
[
Sai90
]
. In this case, the triangulated categories of coefficients are actually defined as the bounded derived categories of the perverse hearts, and their functoriality is constructed starting from the level of abelian categories. Our work enlightens important category-theoretic features of Saito’s construction, especially around the monoidal structure on mixed Hodge modules, which are not addressed systematically in
[
Sai90
]
.

We ultimately intend to apply our abstract results to the emerging theory of
perverse Nori motives
, a further motivic refinement of Saito’s theory, recently introduced F. Ivorra and S. Morel in
[
IM19
]
. As in the case of mixed Hodge modules, one has a system of abelian categories of enhanced perverse sheaves and one wants to equip their bounded derived categories with a six functor formalism. In this new setting, however, the perverse hearts are defined by a purely categorical procedure, which forces one to be very careful about the formal properties of functors and natural transformations relating them. In our article
[
Ter24N
]
, exploiting the general techniques developed here, we construct a monoidal structure on perverse Nori motives by working directly with the external tensor product on the perverse hearts. The only part of the monoidal structure falling out of the application range of the present paper is the unit constraint; in
[
Ter24E
]
we develop further reduction techniques to treat this specific problem.

Main results

Both for sake of notational clarity and in order not to restrict the application range of our results unnecessarily, we formulate everything in the abstract language of monoidal fibered categories. Throughout the paper, we work over a fixed base category
\mathscr{S}
; for our constructions involving the external tensor product, we need to assume that
\mathscr{S}
admits binary products.
Since our main results are motivated by geometric situations, in the course of the paper we are led to introduce more structure and assumptions on the base category
\mathscr{S}
: we suppose to be given two non-full subcategories
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
and
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
mimicking the behavior of smooth morphisms and closed immersions in
\mathsf{Var}_{k}
; for part of our constructions, we also need to assume the existence of special morphisms in
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
mimicking the behavior of open immersions.

Our final goal is to obtain a factorization method adapted to the theory of perverse sheaves. The argument developed in
[
IM19
, § 4.2]
has served both as an inspiration and as a model for our abstract results. We aim at expressing various structures on
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories in terms of inverse images under
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
and direct images under
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
. In order to make this idea work, we need to introduce suitable assumptions on the
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories considered: roughly speaking, we want to reproduce the setting of stable homotopy
2
-functors from
[
Ayo07a
,
Ayo07b
]
for what concerns the localization axiom, but avoiding any use of the homotopy and stability axioms. We do this by introducing the notion of
localic
triangulated
\mathscr{S}
-fibered category, which takes our setting close to that of geometric pullback formalisms introduced in
[
DG22
]
.

Even if we are mainly interested in applying the factorization method to the construction of monoidal
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories and monoidal morphisms, it is useful to start from the case of morphisms of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories: first, this case is interesting in its own, and also implicitly needed in the study of monoidality for morphisms; second, the argument for monoidal
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories is just a notationally more intricate variant of the argument for morphisms of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories. It seems natural to divide our main construction into two main steps: in the first one, we obtain a general factorization method based on inverse images under
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
and under
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
; in the second one, we explain how to partially replace inverse images under
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
with the corresponding direct images in the localic case.
We describe these two constructions axiomatically via the notions of
\mathscr{S}
-skeleton
and of
\mathscr{S}
-core
of a morphism, respectively. While the first notion makes sense in general, the second one works only for certain morphisms between localic
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories that it seems natural to call
localic
: this covers, for example, all morphisms of stable homotopy
2
-functors in the sense of
[
Ayo10
]
. The skeleton of a morphism of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories consists of its restrictions to the underlying
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
-fibered and
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
-fibered categories, subject to a natural coherence condition with respect to squares of the form (
2
). For the core of a localic morphism, the only difference is that one regards the underlying morphism of
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
-fibered categories as a morphism of
\mathscr{S}^{cl,op}
-fibered categories by replacing inverse images under
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
by the corresponding direct images; in this case, the coherence condition between the two restrictions is slightly less obvious - the rough idea being to decompose a square of the form (
2
) into a Cartesian square and two triangles and analyze the three pieces separately.
After this preamble, we can state our first main result as follows:

Theorem

(
2.6
and
3.10
)
.

Let

be a morphism of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

Suppose that
\mathscr{S}
satisfies
2.1
. Then the morphism
R
is completely determined by its restrictions to the underlying
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
-fibered and
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
-fibered categories. Conversely, such a pair of restrictions gives rise to a morphism over
\mathscr{S}
if and only if it defines an
\mathscr{S}
-skeleton.

(2)

Suppose that
\mathscr{S}
satisfies the stronger
3.1
. In addition, suppose that the
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories
\mathbb{H}_{1}
and
\mathbb{H}_{2}
are localic and that
R
is a localic morphism.

Then the morphism
R
is completely determined by its restrictions to the underlying
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
-fibered and
\mathscr{S}^{cl,op}
-fibered categories. Conversely, such a pair of restrictions gives rise to a (localic) morphism over
\mathscr{S}
if and only if it defines an
\mathscr{S}
-core.

We treat the case of monoidal structures on
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories by applying the same method to monoidality isomorphisms: first of all, we show that these are always determined by those under
\mathscr{S}^{sm}
and under
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
; then, we explain how monoidality isomorphisms under
\mathscr{S}^{cl}
can be partially rephrased in terms of direct images in the localic setting. We formulate this precisely via the notions of
external tensor skeleton
and of
external tensor core
, respectively; the coherence conditions with respect to squares of the form (
2
) needed in the two cases are analogous to those for
\mathscr{S}
-skeleta and
\mathscr{S}
-cores.
As the adjective "external" suggests, we have chosen to work systematically with the external tensor product in place of the usual internal tensor product; this is legitimate in view of our work
[
Ter24T
]
, where the theory of
external tensor structures
is developed in detail.
Compared to the internal tensor product, the external tensor product is better suited to factorization questions, for at least two reasons: first, because it often enjoys better adjointability properties (see
1.10
); second, because it allows us to interpret monoidal structures on
\mathscr{S}
-fibered as suitable
2
-parameter families of morphisms of
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories, so that we can deduce our results for monoidal structures from the analogous results for morphisms quite directly. For sake of brevity, we will not make such an interpretation explicit here; the interested reader can obtain it with little difficulty. Let us mention that the notion of external tensor core is adapted to external tensor structures satisfying suitable adjointability properties analogous to those of localic
\mathscr{S}
-fibered categories and localic morphisms: we call them
localic
external tensor structures. This said, we can state our second main result as follows:

Notation and conventions

•

Unless otherwise dictated, categories are assumed to be small with respect to some fixed universe.

•

Given a category
\mathscr{C}
, the notation
C\in\mathscr{C}
means that
C

\mathscr{C}
.

•

Given categories
\mathscr{C}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{C}_{n}

Adjointability of fibered categories and tensor structures

\mathscr{S}

.

a^{sm}=a^{sm}_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}},\;a^{cl}=a^{cl}_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}:(A_{1}\boxtimes A_{2})\boxtimes A_{3}\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{1}\boxtimes(A_{2}\boxtimes A_{3})

coincide, so that we can indicate both of them with the same symbol
a=a_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}
.

(2)

external commutativity constraint

c
on
(\boxtimes,m^{sm},\bar{m}^{cl})
is the datum of

c^{sm}
on
(\boxtimes,m^{sm})

•

coincide, so that we can indicate both of them with the same symbol

.

Let
(\boxtimes,m^{sm},\bar{m}^{cl})
be an external tensor core on
\mathbb{H}
. Then:

(1)

Giving an external associativity constraint on

is equivalent to giving a triangulated external associativity constraint on the corresponding triangulated external tensor structure
(\boxtimes,m)
.

(2)

Giving an external commutativity constraint on

(\boxtimes,m)

Proof.

(1)

4.6
(1), it suffices to show that giving an external associativity constraint on

(2)

In view of

(2), it suffices to show that giving an external commutativity constraint on

is equivalent to giving an external commutativity constraint on

(2).

∎

5.3.
External tensor cores on morphisms

We conclude by discussing the notion of external tensor core on morphisms of

We fix two localizing triangulated
\mathscr{S}

-fibered categories

4.1
.

.

on
R

β

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we define the spaces
N_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right),

S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right),

w_{p}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
for sequences of
fuzzy numbers using generalized difference operator
\Delta^{m}
and a
lacunary sequence
\theta
and give some relations between them, where
\beta\in\left(0,1\right]
and
p>0
. Furthermore, in the last section of
paper, some inclusion theorems are presented related to the spaces
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
and
w_{p}^{\beta}\left(\theta,f,F,\Delta^{m}\right)
according to modulus function
f
.

Key words and phrases:
Fuzzy number, sequence of fuzzy
numbers, statistical convergence, lacunary sequence, Cesàro summability,
modulus function

The idea of statistical convergence was given by Zygmund

[

]

independently. Over the years and under different names statistical
convergence has been discussed in the theory of Fourier analysis, ergodic
theory, number theory, measure theory, trigonometric series, turnpike theory
and Banach spaces. Later on it was further investigated from the sequence
space point of view and linked with summability theory by Connor
[
7

Statistical convergence and its generalizations are also connected with
subsets of the Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers.
Moreover, statistical convergence is closely related to the concept of
convergence in probability.

A fuzzy number is a mapping
X:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow[0,1]
which
satisfies the following properties:

i)

X
is normal, i.e., there exists an
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
X(t_{0})=1;

ii)

X
is fuzzy convex, i.e., for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
and all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1,X(\lambda s+(1-\lambda)t)\geq\min[X(s),X(t)];

iii)

X
is upper semicontinuous on real numbers set;

iv)
The set
\overline{\{t\in\mathbb{R}:X(t)>0\}},
denoted by
[X]^{0},

is compact, where
\overline{\{t\in\mathbb{R}:X(t)>0\}}
is closure of
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:X(t)>0\}
with usual topology of
\mathbb{R}
.

L(\mathbb{R})
denotes the set of all fuzzy numbers on real
numbers set
\mathbb{R}
and is said to be a fuzzy number space.

For
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right]
, the
\alpha
-level set
[X]^{\alpha}

of fuzzy number
X
is defined by
.

[X]^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\{t\in\mathbb{R}:X(t)\geq\alpha\},&\text{for }\alpha\in\left(0,1\right]\\
\overline{\{t\in\mathbb{R}:X(t)>\alpha\}},&\text{for }\alpha=0\end{array}\right.

The aritmetic operations for
\alpha
-level sets are defined as follows:

For
X,Y\in L(\mathbb{R}),
now and what follows we will denote
\alpha-
level sets by
\left[X\right]^{\alpha}=\left[u_{1}^{\alpha},v_{1}^{\alpha}\right],

\left[Y\right]^{\alpha}=\left[u_{2}^{\alpha},v_{2}^{\alpha}\right],

\alpha\in\left[0,1\right].
Then we have

\displaystyle\left[X+Y\right]^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left[u_{1}^{\alpha}+u_{2}^{\alpha},v_{1}^{\alpha}+v_{2}^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\left[X-Y\right]^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left[u_{1}^{\alpha}-v_{2}^{\alpha},v_{1}^{\alpha}-u_{2}^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\left[X.Y\right]^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left[\min_{i,j\in\left[1,2\right]}u_{i}^{\alpha}v_{j}^{\alpha},\max_{i,j\in\left[1,2\right]}u_{i}^{\alpha}v_{j}^{\alpha}\right].

A sequence
X=(X_{k})
of fuzzy numbers is a function
X
from the set

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\leq n:d\left(X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|=0,

where the vertical bars indicate the number of elements in the enclosed set.
In this case we write
S^{\beta}\left(F\right)-\lim X_{k}=X_{0}.
We
denote the set of all statistically convergent sequences of order
\beta

by
S^{\beta}\left(F\right)

[
3
]
.

Let
w\left(F\right)
be the set of all sequences of fuzzy numbers. The
operator

is

\displaystyle\left(\Delta^{0}X\right)_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{k},\left(\Delta^{1}X\right)_{k}=\Delta^{1}X_{k}=X_{k}-X_{k+1},

\displaystyle\left(\Delta^{m}X\right)_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{1}\left(\Delta^{m-1}X\right)_{k},\left(m\geq 2\right),\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Definition 2.1
Let
\theta=\left(k_{r}\right)
be a
lacunary sequence,
X=\left(X_{k}\right)
be a sequence of fuzzy numbers
and
\beta\in\left(0,1\right]
be given. The sequence
X=\left(X_{k}\right)\in w\left(F\right)
is said to be
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-
statistically convergent (or lacunary
statistically convergent sequence of order
\beta)
if there is a fuzzy
number
X_{0}
such that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|=0,

where
I_{r}=\left(k_{r-1},k_{r}\right]
and
h_{r}^{\beta}
denote the
\beta
th power
\left(h_{r}\right)^{\beta}
of
h_{r},
that is
h^{\beta}=(h_{r}^{\beta})=(h_{1}^{\beta},h_{2}^{\beta},...,h_{r}^{\beta},...).
In this case we write
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim X_{k}=X_{0}
. The set of all
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-
statistically convergent sequences will be denoted by

S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
. For
\theta=\left(2^{r}\right),
we shall write
S^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)

instead of
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
and we shall
write
S\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
instead of
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
in the special case
\beta=1
and
\theta=\left(2^{r}\right).

Lemma 2.2.
Let
\theta=\left(k_{r}\right)
be a
lacunary sequence and
K\subset\mathbb{N}.
Then
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(K\right)\leq S_{\theta}^{\alpha}\left(K\right)
for
0<\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
.

Proof.
Let

0<\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
. We can write

\dfrac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k_{r-1}<k\leq k_{r}:k\in K\right\}\right|\leq\dfrac{1}{h_{r}^{\alpha}}\left|\left\{k_{r-1}<k\leq k_{r}:k\in K\right\}\right|

since
h_{r}^{\alpha}\leq h_{r}^{\beta}
. Hence
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(K\right)\leq

S_{\theta}^{\alpha}\left(K\right)
.

Let
\beta\in\left(0,1\right].
If a sequence of fuzzy numbers is
lacunary statistically convergent of order
\beta
, then it is lacunary
statistically convergent. Really, we obtain

\dfrac{1}{h_{r}^{\alpha}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\geq\dfrac{1}{h_{r}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|

since
h_{r}^{\alpha}\leq h_{r}
for
\beta\in\left(0,1\right].
Taking
limit as
r\rightarrow\infty
we get
X_{k}\rightarrow X_{0}\left(S_{\theta}^{\alpha},\Delta^{m}\right)
and so
X_{k}\rightarrow X_{0}\left(S_{\theta},\Delta^{m}\right).

Note that the lacunary statistical convergence of order
\beta
is well
defined for
\beta\in\left(0,1\right]
, but not well defined for
\beta>1
. To show this, consider the sequence
X=\left(X_{k}\right)
of fuzzy
numbers following:

X_{k}\left(t\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}t,&\text{for }0\leq t\leq 1\\
-t+2,&\text{for }1\leq t\leq 2\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right\}:=X^{\prime},&\begin{array}[]{c}\text{if }k=2r\\
(r=1,2,3,...)\end{array}\\
\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}t-3,&\text{for }3\leq t\leq 4\\
-t+5,&\text{for }4\leq t\leq 5\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right\}:=X^{\prime\prime},&\text{if }k\neq 2r\text{ }\end{array}\right.

We can find the
\alpha-
level sets of sequences
\left(X_{k}\right)
and

\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k}\right)
after some arithmetic operations as follows

\left[X_{k}\right]^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left[\alpha,2-\alpha\right],&\text{if }k=2r\\
\left[3+\alpha,5-\alpha\right],&\text{if }k\neq 2r\text{ }\end{array}\right..

and

\left[\Delta^{m}X_{k}\right]^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left[2^{m}\alpha-2^{m-1}.5,-2^{m}\alpha-2^{m-1}\right]:=X^{\prime},&\text{if }k=2r\\
\left[2^{m}\alpha+2^{m-1},-2^{m}\alpha+2^{m-1}.5\right]:=X^{\prime\prime},&\text{if }k\neq 2r\text{ }\end{array}\right.,\left(m=1,2,...\right)

then we can write

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X^{\prime}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\leq\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{r}-k_{r-1}}{2h_{r}^{\beta}}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h_{r}}{2h_{r}^{\beta}}=0

and

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X^{\prime\prime}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\leq\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{r}-k_{r-1}}{2h_{r}^{\beta}}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h_{r}}{2h_{r}^{\beta}}=0

for
\beta>1.
Hence sequence
\left(X_{k}\right)
is
\Delta^{m}-
lacunary statistically convergent of order
\beta,
both to
X^{\prime}

and
X^{\prime\prime},
i.e.,
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim X_{k}

=X^{\prime}
and
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim X_{k}

=X^{\prime\prime}.
But this is
impossible.

Theorem 2.3
Let
0<\beta\leq 1
and
X=\left(X_{k}\right),

Y=\left(Y_{k}\right)
be sequences of fuzzy numbers
,

then

\left(i\right)
If
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim X_{k}=X_{0}
and
c\in\mathbb{C},
then
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim\left(cx_{k}\right)=cx_{0},

\left(ii\right)
If
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim X_{k}=X_{0}
and
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim Y_{k}=Y_{0},
then
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim\left(X_{k}+Y_{k}\right)=X_{0}+Y_{0}.

Proof.

(i)
It is clear for the case
c=0.
Suppose that
c\neq 0,

then the proof of
\left(i\right)
follows from

\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(c\Delta^{m}X_{k},cX_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|=\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{\left|c\right|}\right\}\right|

and that of
\left(ii\right)
follows from

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}\left(X_{k}+Y_{k}\right),\left(X_{0}+Y_{0}\right)\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}Y_{k},Y_{0}\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}\right|.

Definition 2.4
Let
X=\left(X_{k}\right)
be a
sequence of fuzzy numbers,
\theta=\left(k_{r}\right)

\beta\in\left(0,1\right]

be a
positive real number. A sequence

there is a fuzzy number

\underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\mathop{\textstyle\sum}\limits_{k\in I_{r}}d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)^{p}=0\text{.}

In this case we write
N_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim X_{k}=X_{0}
. The strong
N_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right)-
summability reduces to the strong
N_{\theta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right)-
summability for
\beta=1
. The set of all strongly

N_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right)-
summable sequences will
be denoted by
N_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right)
.

If we take
\theta=\left(2^{r}\right)
in space

then we obtain strongly
p-
Cesaro
summable sequences set of order
\beta
following

This set will be denoted by
w_{p,0}^{\beta}
in case of
X_{0}=\bar{0}

[

Theorem 2.5
If
0<\beta<\gamma\leq 1
then
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)\subset S_{\theta}^{\gamma}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
and the inclusion is strict.

Proof.
Proof can be obtained from the inequality

\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\gamma}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|.

Taking
\theta=\left(2^{r}\right)
we show the strictness of the
inclusion
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)\subset S_{\theta}^{\gamma}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
for a special case. For this,
consider the sequence
X=\left(X_{k}\right)
of fuzzy numbers defined by

X_{k}\left(t\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}2t-\left(2k-1\right),&\text{for }k-\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq k\\
-2t+\left(2k+1\right),&\text{for }k\leq t\leq k+\frac{1}{2}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right\},&\text{if }k=n^{3}\\
\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}2t-1,&\text{for }\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1\\
-2t+3,&\text{for }1\leq t\leq\frac{3}{2}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right\},&\text{if }k\neq n^{3}\end{array}\right.

After calculating
\alpha-
level sets of sequences
\left(X_{k}\right)

and
\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k}\right),
we obtain
S_{\theta}^{\gamma}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-\lim X_{k}=\left[2^{m-1}\left(\alpha-1\right),2^{m-1}\left(1-\alpha\right)\right],
i.e.
X\in S_{\theta}^{\gamma}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
for
\frac{1}{3}<\gamma\leq 1,

but
X\notin S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
for
0<\beta\leq\frac{1}{3}.
This implies that the inclusion
S_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)\subset S_{\theta}^{\gamma}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)

such that

\beta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{3}\right]
and
\gamma\in\left(\frac{1}{3},1\right].

Theorem 2.7
Let
\beta
and
\gamma
be fixed real

0<\beta\leq\gamma\leq 1,

X=\left(X_{k}\right)
be
a sequence of fuzzy numbers and
\theta=\left(k_{r}\right)

N_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right)\subset S_{\theta}^{\gamma}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)
and

\beta
’s and
\gamma
’s
.

Proof.
For any sequence
X=\left(X_{k}\right)
of fuzzy numbers
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\textstyle\sum}\limits_{k\in I_{r}}\left[d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\right]^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\textstyle\sum}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I_{r}\\
d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\left[d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\right]^{p}+\mathop{\textstyle\sum}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I_{r}\\

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathop{\textstyle\sum}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I_{r}\\
d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\left[d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\right]^{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\varepsilon^{p}

and so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\mathop{\textstyle\sum}\limits_{k\in I_{r}}\left[d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\right]^{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\beta}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\varepsilon^{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{r}^{\gamma}}\left|\left\{k\in I_{r}:d\left(\Delta^{m}X_{k},X_{0}\right)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\varepsilon^{p}.

It follows that if
X=\left(X_{k}\right)
is strongly
N_{\theta}^{\beta}\left(p,F,\Delta^{m}\right)-
summable to fuzzy number
X_{0}
, then it
is
S_{\theta}^{\gamma}\left(F,\Delta^{m}\right)-
statistically
convergent to
X_{0}
.

Taking

and

we show

for a

X_{k}\left(t\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{t}{k}+1,&\text{for }-k\leq t\leq 0\\

After calculating
\alpha-
level sets of sequences

\Delta^{m}-
statistically convergent of order

3. Results Related to Modulus Function

We give relation between sets

We first quote the definition of a modulus function:

The concept of modulus function was formally introduced by Nakano
[
19
]
. A mapping
f:[0,\infty)

\rightarrow

[0,\infty)
is said to
be a modulus if

i)

f\left(x\right)=0
iff
x=0
,

ii)

f\left(x+y\right)\leq f\left(x\right)+f\left(y\right)
for
x,y\geq 0,

iii)

f
is increasing,

iv)

f
is right-continuous at
0.

The continuity of
f
everywhere on
[0,\infty)
follows from above
definition. A modulus function can be bounded or unbounded. For example
f\left(x\right)=x^{p},\left(0<p\leq 1\right)
is bounded and
f\left(x\right)=\frac{x}{1+x}
is bounded. The concept of modulus function for
sequences of fuzzy numbers was first investigated by Sarma
[
20
]
.
Later on it was explotied by Talo and Başar
[
24
]
.

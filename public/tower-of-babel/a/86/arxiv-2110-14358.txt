Ferrers Graphs, D-Permutations, and Surjective Staircases
Alexander Lazar

Abstract
. We introduce a new family of hyperplane arrangements inspired by the homogenized Linial arrangement (which was recently introduced by Hetyei), and show that the intersection lattices of these arrangements are isomorphic to the bond lattices of Ferrers graphs. Using recent work of Lazar and Wachs we are able to give combinatorial interpretations of the characteristic polynomials of these arrangements in terms of permutation enumeration. For certain infinite families of these hyperplane arrangements, we are able to give generating function formulas for their characteristic polynomials. To do so, we develop a generalization of Dumont’s surjective staircases, and introduce a polynomial which enumerates these generalized surjective staircases according to several statistics. We prove a recurrence for these polynomials and show that in certain special cases this recurrence can be solved explicitly to yield a generating function. We also prove refined versions of several of these results using the theory of complex hyperplane arrangements.



1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to generalize recent results of Lazar and Wachs
[
14
,
12
]
on the combinatorics of the
homogenized Linial arrangement
in several different directions.

The homogenized Linial arrangement, which was introduced by Hetyei in
[
10
]
, is the real hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}
in

\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1},y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\st x_{i},\,y_{i}\in\R\}=\R^{2n+1}

given by

\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}=\{x_{i}-x_{j}=y_{i}\st 1\leq i<j\leq n+1\}.

Hetyei showed that the number of regions of
\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}
is equal to the
median Genocchi number

h_{n}
. The median Genocchi numbers and their partner sequence the
Genocchi numbers

g_{n}
are classical sequences that appear in several areas of combinatorics (see
[
8
,
10
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
22
]
, among others) and number theory (e.g.,
[
1
]
).

Intersecting
\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}
with the subspace
y_{1}=y_{2}=\cdots=y_{n}=0
yields the braid arrangement

\{x_{i}-x_{j}=0\st 1\leq i<j\leq{n+1}\}

in
\R^{n+1}
, while intersecting it with
y_{1}=y_{2}=\cdots=y_{n}=1
yields the
Linial arrangement

\{x_{i}-x_{j}=1\st 1\leq i<j\leq n+1\}

in
\R^{n+1}
. We can therefore think of
\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}
as being defined by a particular choice of homogenization of the defining equations for the hyperplanes in the braid or Linial arrangements.

In
[
14
]
, Lazar and Wachs studied this arrangement further. They showed that the intersection lattice of
\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}
is isomorphic to the bond lattice of a bipartite graph
\Gamma_{2n}
and used that interpretation to give a combinatorial interpretation of the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{2n-1})}(t)
of
\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}
in terms of a new class of permutations they called
D-permutations
.

Lazar and Wachs showed that the D-permutations on
[2n]
are bijection with certain elements of the class of
surjective staircases
(due to Dumont
[
8
]
). Using a generating function result of Randrianarivony
[
16
]
and Zeng
[
22
]
for surjective staircases, Lazar and Wachs proved (
[
14
, Theorem 5.5]
) a generating function formula for
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{2n-1})}(t)
:

(1)

\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{2n-1})}(t)\,x^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(t-1)_{n-1}(t-1)_{n}\,x^{n}}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-k(t-k)x)},

where
(a)_{n}
denotes the falling factorial
a(a-1)\cdots(a-(n-1))
.

The various perspectives used in
[
14
]
— hyperplane arrangements,
\Gamma_{2n}
and D-permutations, and surjective staircases — suggest several possible avenues to generalize the results of that paper. In this paper, we consider three of them.

In the course of
[
14
]
, the authors found it useful to consider a larger class of graphs
\Gamma_{V}
where
V\subseteq[2n]
for any
n
. One possible direction for generalizing of the results of
[
14
]
would therefore be to study the combinatorics of
\Gamma_{V}
for arbitrary
V
. The study of these
\Gamma_{V}
was initiated in
[
14
]
, where the authors showed that the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of the bond lattice of
\Gamma_{V}
can be interpreted in terms of the D-permutations on
V
. The authors also noted that
\Gamma_{2n}
belongs to a class of graphs known as
Ferrers graphs
, and remarked that the same was true for all
\Gamma_{V}
.

Another possible direction for generalizing
[
14
]
is suggested by the theory of hyperplane arrangements. Beyond the braid and Linial arrangements, there are a number of other
deformations
of the braid arrangement (studied in detail in
[
15
]
), such as the
Shi arrangement

\{x_{i}-x_{j}=0,1\st 1\leq i<j\leq n\},

semiorder arrangement

\{x_{i}-x_{j}=\pm 1\st 1\leq i<j\leq n\},

and
Catalan arrangement

\{x_{i}-x_{j}=-1,0,1\st 1\leq i<j\leq n\}.

Therefore, one might wish to study the combinatorics of homogenizations of these deformations. Let
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})
be a weak composition of
m
. That is,
\nu
is an ordered
n
-tuple of nonnegative integers whose sum is
m
. For all such
\nu
, we define the
homogenized
\nu
-arrangement

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
to be

\{x_{i}-x_{j}=y_{i}^{(\ell)}\st 1\leq i<j\leq n+1,1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}\}.

For instance, taking
\nu=(2,2,\dots,2)
gives us the arrangement

\{x_{i}-x_{j}=y_{i}^{(1)},y_{i}^{(2)}\st 1\leq i<j\leq n+1\}.

Intersecting this arrangement with the subspace where
y_{1}^{(1)}=y_{2}^{(1)}=\cdots=y_{n}^{(1)}=0
and
y_{1}^{(2)}=y_{2}^{(2)}=\cdots=y_{n}^{(2)}=1
yields the Shi arrangement.

The third possible direction is to generalize the notion of surjective staircases. Surjective staircases are surjective functions
F:[2n]\to\{2,4,\dots,2n\}
that satisfy
F(x)\geq x
. A key tool used in
[
14
]
to prove Equation (
1
) is the
generalized Dumont–Foata polynomial

\Lambda_{2n}
, which enumerates the surjective staircases with domain
[2n]
with respect to six statistics. Randrianarivony
[
16
]
and Zeng
[
22
]
proved a recurrence and a generating function formula for the
\Lambda_{2n}
, and it is this formula which eventually yields Equation (
1
). One natural generalization would be to consider surjective functions
F:V\to\{\text{even elements of }V\}
which satisfy
F(x)\geq x
for any finite
V\subset\Z_{>0}
. Given the role that the generalized Dumont–Foata polynomials play in
[
14
]
, we also wish to define analogous polynomials for domains other than
[2n]
.

The pleasant surprise of these three generalizations is that they remain very closely related. In this paper, we show that for any
\nu
the intersection lattice of
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
is isomorphic to the bond lattice of some
\Gamma_{V}
. Hence, the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
have an interpretation in terms of the D-permutations on
V
. We then develop the theory of surjective staircases with domain
V
: we define a generalization
\Lambda_{V}
of the generalized Dumont–Foata polynomials
\Lambda_{2n}
and prove a recurrence for the
\Lambda_{V}
generalizing the Randrianarivony–Zeng recurrence for
\Lambda_{2n}
.

For certain infinite families of homogenized
\nu
-arrangements, we are able to solve our new recurrence explicitly to obtain generating function formulas for their corresponding
\Lambda_{V}
.

For one such family that we denote
\mathcal{H}_{n,k}
, this new machinery lets us prove an analog of Equation (
1
):

(2)

\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})}(t)u^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(t-1)_{n-1}\left((t-1)_{n}\right)^{k}u^{n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-i(t-i)^{k}u\right)},

where
(a)_{n}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-(n-1))
.

These techniques also let us derive a generating function for the chromatic polynomial of the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,k}
:

(3)

\sum_{n\geq 1}\text{ch}(K_{n,k})(t)u^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(t)_{n}\left[(t-n)^{k}+(n-1)(t-(n-1))^{k-1}\right]u^{n}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-iu)}.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperplane Arrangements and Geometric Lattices

A
real hyperplane arrangement
is a finite collection
\mathcal{H}
of hyperplanes in
\R^{d}
. The complement
\R^{d}\setminus\mathcal{H}
consists of finitely many connected components, which are called the
regions of
\mathcal{H}
. The combinatorial data of
\mathcal{H}
can be collected into the
intersection poset

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, which consists of the intersections of hyperplanes in
\mathcal{H}
(viewed as affine subspaces of
\R^{d}
) ordered according to reverse containment. We define the
rank

\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{H}
to be the length of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
.

If all of the hyperplanes in
\mathcal{H}
have a nonempty common intersection, we say
\mathcal{H}
is
central
. The intersection poset of a central arrangement is a geometric lattice, and if
\mathcal{H}
is non-central its intersection poset is a geometric semilattice (in the sense of
[
20
]
).

One important invariant of a hyperplane arrangement is the
characteristic polynomial

\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}(t)
of its intersection poset. If
P
is any ranked poset with bottom element
\hat{0}
, its characteristic polynomial is defined by

\chi_{P}(t)=\sum_{X\in P}\mu_{P}(\hat{0},X)t^{\mathrm{rk}(P)-\mathrm{rk}(X)},

where
\mu_{P}
is the Möbius function of
P
.

A well-known formula of Zaslavsky
[
21
]
relates the number of regions
r(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{H}
to the characteristic polynomial of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
:

(4)

r(\mathcal{H})=(-1)^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{H})}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}(-1).

2.2.
Graphs and Bond Lattices

Associated to any finite graph
G
with vertex set
V
and edge set
E
is the
graphic hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{H}_{G}
, which is given by

\mathcal{H}_{G}=\{x_{i}-x_{j}=0\st\{i,j\}\in E\}.

The intersection lattice of
\mathcal{H}_{G}
is called the
bond lattice of
G
and is denoted
\Pi_{G}
. An equivalent description of
\Pi_{G}
can be given as a subposet of the lattice
\Pi_{V}
of set partitions of
V
. A set partition
\pi=B_{1}|B_{2}|\cdots|B_{k}
of
V
belongs to
\Pi_{G}
if and only if the induced subgraph
G|_{B_{i}}
is connected for all
i
.

The bond lattice of
G
also determines the chromatic polynomial
\mathrm{ch}(G)(t)
in the sense that

\mathrm{ch}(G)(t)=t\cdot\chi_{\Pi_{G}}(t).

2.3.
The Homogenized Linial Arrangement and D-Permutations

In
[

]
, Lazar and Wachs considered a family of bipartite graphs

for

2.4.
Ferrers Graphs

In

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

such that

. Conversely, given a Ferrers graph

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma_{V}
has an edge between

and
2j
if and only if

. We see that
\{2i_{1}-1,2j_{1}\}

\Gamma_{V}
(since
\min V=2i_{1}-1
), and
\{2i_{m}-1,2j_{n}\}
is an edge (since

). Moreover, if
\{2i_{a}-1,2j_{b}\}

\Gamma_{V}
then
\{2i_{k}-1,2j_{\ell}\}

\Gamma_{V}
for all

. Hence,
\Gamma_{V}
is a Ferrers graph with

and

, and moreover the associated partition of
\Gamma_{V}
is

by the definition of
\lambda(V)
.

Conversely, suppose that
G
is a Ferrers graph on vertex set
R=\{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\}
and
C=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{m}\}
. Associated to

is the integer partition
\lambda

. We construct a set

from the Ferrers diagram of

as follows. Let

.

(1)

Start at the southwest corner of the Ferrers diagram for
\lambda
, and walk the path along the lower border of the Ferrers diagram.

(2)

If the
i
th step is to the east, label the edge we have just traversed with the smallest odd integer

and let

(3)

If the

Theorem 4.2
.

For all weak compositions
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})
of
m
, there is an invertible
\Z
-linear transformation from
\R^{m+n+1}
to itself that takes
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
to the graphic hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{G_{\nu}}
. Consequently,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu})\cong\Pi_{G_{\nu}}
.

Proof.

Let
(e_{1},\dots,e_{m+n+1})
be the standard basis for
\R^{m+n+1}
. For notational convenience, we define vectors
u_{i}
and
u_{i}^{(j)}
by setting

\displaystyle(u_{1}^{(1)},u_{1}^{(2)},\dots,u_{1}^{(\nu_{1})},

\displaystyle u_{2},u_{3}^{(1)},\dots,u_{3}^{(\nu_{2})},u_{4},\dots,u_{2n-1}^{(1)},\dots,u_{2n-1}^{(\nu_{n})},u_{2n},u_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle=(e_{1},\dots,e_{m+n+1}),

and also define
v_{i}
and
v_{i}^{(j)}
by setting

\displaystyle(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n+1},v_{n+2}^{(1)},\dots,v_{n+2}^{(\nu_{1})},

\displaystyle v_{n+3}^{(1)},\dots,v_{n+3}^{(\nu_{2})},\dots,v_{2n+1}^{(1)},\dots,v_{2n+1}^{(\nu_{n})})

\displaystyle=(e_{1},\dots,e_{m+n+1}).

For all
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
and all
1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}
, we define the hyperplane

K_{i,j}^{(\ell)}=\{w\in\R^{m+n+1}\st(u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-u_{2j})\cdot w=0\},

and the arrangement

\mathcal{K}=\{K_{i,j}^{\ell}\st 1\leq i\leq j\leq n,\;1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}\}.

We observe that
\mathcal{K}\cong\mathcal{A}_{G_{\nu}}
. Indeed,
\mathcal{K}
is the graphic hyperplane arrangement of the graph on
m+n+1
vertices obtained from
G_{\nu}
by adjoining an isolated vertex
m+n+1
.

Now, for all
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
and
1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}
, we write

H_{i,j}^{\ell}=\{w\in\R^{m+n+1}\st(v_{i}-v_{j+1}-v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)})\cdot w=0\}.

Clearly,

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}=\{H_{i,j}^{\ell}\st 1\leq i\leq j\leq n,\;1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}\}.

Now, consider the linear transformation
\phi
from
\R^{m+n+1}
to itself, given by

•

\phi(u_{2i-1}^{\ell})=v_{i}-v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)}
,

•

\phi(u_{2j})=v_{j+1}
,

•

\phi(u_{m+n+1})=v_{1}
.

To show that this
\Z
-linear transformation is invertible, we define the linear transformation
\tilde{\phi}
from
\R^{m+n+1}
to itself on the standard basis as follows:

•

\tilde{\phi}(v_{1})=u_{m+n+1}
,

•

\tilde{\phi}(v_{i})=u_{2i-2}
for
2\leq i\leq n+1
,

•

\tilde{\phi}(v_{n+2}^{(\ell)})=u_{\lambda_{n}+n+1}-u_{1}^{(\ell)}
for
\ell\in[\nu_{1}]
,

•

\tilde{\phi}(v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)})=u_{2i-2}-u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}
for
1<i\leq n
and
\ell\in[\nu_{i}]
.

We see that

•

\phi(\tilde{\phi}(v_{1}))=\phi(u_{m+n+1})=v_{1}

•

\phi(\tilde{\phi}(v_{i}))=\phi(u_{2i-2})=v_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq n+1

•

\phi(\tilde{\phi}(v_{n+2}^{(\ell)}))=\phi(u_{m+n+1}-u_{1}^{(\ell)})=v_{1}-(v_{1}-v_{n+2}^{(\ell)})=v_{n+2}^{(\ell)}
for
\ell\in[\nu_{1}]

•

\phi(\tilde{\phi}(v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)}))=\phi(u_{2i-2}-u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)})=v_{i}-(v_{i}-v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)})=v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)}
for
2\leq i\leq n
,
\ell\in[\nu_{i}]
.

Hence,
\phi
is invertible, so let
A
be the matrix of
\phi
with respect to the standard basis and let
\psi
be the linear operator on
\R^{m+n+1}
whose matrix in the standard basis is
(A^{-1})^{T}
.

We claim that
\psi
takes each hyperplane
K_{i,j}^{\ell}
to the corresponding hyperplane
H_{i,j}^{\ell}
. Indeed, suppose
w\in K_{i,j}^{\ell}
so
(u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-u_{2j})\cdot w=0
. Then

\phi(u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-u_{2j})\cdot\psi(w)=(v_{i}-v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)}-v_{j+1})\cdot\psi(w),

and

\displaystyle\phi(u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-u_{2j})\cdot\psi(w)

\displaystyle=A(u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-u_{2j})\cdot((A^{-1})^{T}w)

\displaystyle=(A^{-1}A(u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-u_{2j}))\cdot w

\displaystyle=(u_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-u_{2j})\cdot w

\displaystyle=0,

so
(v_{i}-v_{n+i+1}^{(\ell)}-v_{j+1})\cdot\psi(w)=0
. Hence,
\psi(w)\in H_{i,j}^{(\ell)}
, which proves the claim.
∎

It is clear that the Ferrers graph

Theorem 5.5

For all

\sum_{n\geq 1}\text{ch}(K_{n,k})(t)u^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(t)_{n}\left[(t-n)^{k}+(n-1)(t-(n-1))^{k-1}\right]u^{n}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-iu)}.

Let

6.2.

The following lemma was proved in
[
14

:

Lemma 6.7
.

There is a bijection

\phi:\mathcal{D}_{2n}\to\{f\in\mathcal{E}_{2n+2}:f\mbox{ has no even maxima}\}

such that for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{2n}
and

, the following properties hold:

(1)

j
is an even cycle maximum of
\sigma
if and only if it is a fixed point of
\phi(\sigma)
,

(2)

j
is an even fixed point of
\sigma
if and only if it is an isolated fixed point of
\phi(\sigma)
,

(3)

j
is an odd fixed point of
\sigma
if and only if it is an odd maximum of
\phi(\sigma)
.

Our next goal is to generalize Lemma
6.7
. Let
V
be a finite set of positive integers whose maximum is even, and let
R_{k}
be a finite set of
k+1
positive integers consisting of:

•

k
odd numbers larger than
\max V
, and

•

one even number that is larger than the odd numbers.

We will show that the D-permutations on
V
are in bijection with certain of the surjective staircases with domain
V\cup R_{k}
.

Lemma 6.8
.

For any
k
, let
V
and
R_{k}
be as above. There is a bijection

\tilde{\phi}:\mathcal{D}_{V}\to\{f\in\mathcal{E}_{V\cup R_{k}}\st f\text{ has no even maxima}\},

such that for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V}
and all

(1)

j
is an even cycle maximum of
\sigma
if and only if
j
is a fixed point of
\phi(\sigma)
,

(2)

j
is an even fixed point of
\sigma
if and only if
j
is an isolated fixed point of
\phi(\sigma)
,

(3)

j
is an odd fixed point of
\sigma
if and only if
j
is an odd maximum of
\phi(\sigma)
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V}
. We wish to apply Lemma
6.7
. Let
2m=\max V
,
2n=\max R_{k}
and let
\sigma^{\prime}
be the permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{2n-2}
defined by

\sigma^{\prime}(i)=\begin{cases}\sigma(i),&i\in V\\
i,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Notice that the map
\psi:\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{V}\}\to\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n-2}\st\sigma(i)=i\Forall i\notin V\}
given by
\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{\prime}
is a bijection.

We apply the map
\phi
from Lemma
6.7
to
\sigma^{\prime}
to obtain a surjective staircase
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{2n}
with no even maxima. Our next goal is to show that the restriction of
f^{\prime}
to
V\cup R_{k}
is a surjective staircase.

First, recall that for all
i\in[2n-2]\setminus V
,
\sigma^{\prime}(i)=i
. Hence, if
i\in[2n-2]\setminus V
is even,
i
is an isolated fixed point of
f^{\prime}
, and if
i\in[2n-2]\setminus V
is odd,
i
is an odd maximum of
f^{\prime}
. This tells us immediately that for all
i\in V
,
f^{\prime}(i)\in[2n]\setminus([2n-2]\setminus V)=V\cup\{2n-1,2n\}
. Since the image of
f^{\prime}
contains no odd numbers, we see that
f^{\prime}(i)\in V\cup\{2n\}
for all
i\in V
. In other words,

f^{\prime}(V)\subseteq\{\text{even elements of }V\}\cup\{2n\}.

Conversely, we claim that
\{\text{even elements of }V\}\subseteq f^{\prime}(V)
. Indeed, every element of
[2n-2]\setminus V
is either a fixed point or a maximum of
f^{\prime}
, so if

,
f^{\prime}(i)\notin V
. By definition,
f^{\prime}(2n-1)=f^{\prime}(2n)=2n
. Hence,
\{\text{even elements of }V\}\nsubseteq f^{\prime}([2n]\setminus V)
, so the only way for
f^{\prime}
to be surjective is to have

\{\text{even elements of }V\}\subseteq f^{\prime}(V).

We also see that
f^{\prime}(R_{k})=\{2n\}
. Indeed, we know that
f^{\prime}(2n-1)=f^{\prime}(2n)=2n
(whether or not
2n-1\in R_{k}
). Any other element

of
R_{k}
is an odd element of

, and by construction, all such

are odd maxima of
f^{\prime}
. Hence,

f^{\prime}(V\cup R_{k})=\{\text{even elements of }V\}\cup\{2n\}.

Thus, we can define a surjective staircase
f\in\mathcal{E}_{V\cup R_{k}}
by restricting the domain of
f^{\prime}
to
V\cup R_{k}
. Indeed, we have just shown that the image of such an

would be
\{\text{even elements of V }\}\cup\{2n\}
, and such an

would be excedent because
f^{\prime}
was. We also know that
f
would have no even maxima because
f^{\prime}
had no even maxima.

Finally, note that the image of
\phi\circ\psi
consists of surjective staircases
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{2n}
with no even maxima, such that
f^{\prime}(2i-1)=2n
for all

, and such that
2i
is an isolated fixed point of
f^{\prime}
for all
2i\notin V
. We observe that restricting the domain of
f^{\prime}
to
V\cup R_{k}
yields a bijection between the image of
\phi\circ\psi
and
\{f\in\mathcal{E}_{V\cup R_{k}}\st f\text{ has no even maxima}\}
. Indeed, given any
f\in\mathcal{E}_{V\cup R_{k}}
that has no even maxima, we define a

with no even maxima by

g(a)=\begin{cases}f(a),&a\in V\\
2n,&a\in[2n]\setminus V\text{ is odd}\\
a,&a\in[2n]\setminus V\text{ is even}\end{cases}.

Such a

is in the image of
\phi\circ\psi

2i-1
is an odd maximum of
g
for all
2i-1\in[2n-2]\setminus V
and
2i
is an isolated fixed point of
g
for all
2i\in[2n-2]\setminus V
. Moreover, by construction we see that

.

Hence, we define
\tilde{\phi}:\mathcal{D}_{V}\to\mathcal{E}_{V\cup R_{k}}
by
\tilde{\phi}(\sigma)=\left.\phi(\sigma^{\prime})\right|_{V\cup R_{k}}
. Since the three desired properties held for
\phi

\tilde{\phi}
automatically. The map
\tilde{\phi}
is the composition of three maps
\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{\prime}\mapsto f^{\prime}\mapsto f
, each of which is a bijection, so
\tilde{\phi}
is a bijection.
∎

8.1.
Homogenized
\nu
-Dowling Arrangements

As in
[
11
, Chapter 4]
(see also
[
12
]
), we can consider a complex analog of the homogenized
\nu
-arrangements. Many of the results of the previous sections can be extended to the complex case after some technical results are established.

Let
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})
be a weak composition of
m
, and fix a positive integer
q
. Let
\omega
be the primitive
q
th root of unity
e^{\frac{2\pi i}{q}}
. We define the
homogenized
\nu
-Dowling arrangement
to be the hyperplane arrangement in

\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1}^{(1)},\dots,y_{1}^{(\nu_{1})},y_{2}^{(1)}.\dots,y_{2}^{(\nu_{2})},\dots,y_{n}^{(1)},\dots,y_{n}^{(\nu_{n})})\}=\C^{m+n}

given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{q}=

\displaystyle\{x_{i}-\omega^{k}x_{j}=y_{i}^{(\ell)}\st 1\leq i<j\leq n,0\leq k\leq q-1,1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{x_{i}=y_{i}^{(\ell)}\st 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}\}.

The term “Dowling” comes from the fact that the intersection lattice of
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{q}
is a subposet of the
Dowling lattice

Q_{n}(\Z_{q})
, introduced by Dowling in
[
5
]
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q}
be the hyperplane arrangement in
\C^{n+m}
given by:

\displaystyle\{x_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}-\omega^{k}x_{2j-2}=0

\displaystyle\st 1\leq i<j\leq n,0\leq k\leq q-1,1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{x_{2i-1}^{(\ell)}=0\st 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq\ell\leq\nu_{i}\}.

Lemma 8.1
.

There is an invertible linear transformation from
\C^{n+m}
to itself that takes the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{q}
to the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q}
. In particular, we have
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{q})\cong\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q})
.

Proof.

This follows from essentially the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
. See also
[
12
,
11
, Lemma 4.2.3]
.
∎

The results of
[
11
, Section 4.2]
(and of
[
12
]
) allow us to interpret the coefficients of the
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)
(and hence of
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)
) in terms of certain decorated D-permutations.

A
q
-labeled D-cycle
on
V\subseteq[2r]
is a D-cycle
\sigma
on
V
, some of whose entries are labeled with elements of
\{0,\dots,q-1\}
subject to the following conditions.

•

The maximum entry of
\sigma
is labeled
0
.

•

If
2r\in V
and
\sigma
is written in the form
(w\cdot 2r)
then the right-to-left minima of the word
w
are the only unlabeled entries of
\sigma
.

•

If
2r\notin V
then each entry of
\sigma
is labeled.

A
q
-labeled D-permutation
on
V\subseteq[2r]
is a D-permutation
\sigma
on
V
, some of whose entries are labeled with elements of
\{0,\dots,q-1\}
, such that each cycle of
\sigma
is a
q
-labeled D-cycle. We write
\mathcal{D}_{V,r}^{q}
for the set of
q
-labeled D-permutations on
V\subseteq[2r]
.

The combinatorics of
q
-labeled D-permutations is equivalent to the combinatorics of a certain class of edge-decorated forests.

A tree
T
on vertex set
V\subset\Z_{>0}
is
increasing-decreasing
(or
ID
for short) if, when
T
is rooted at its largest vertex, each internal vertex
v
of
T
satisfies:

•

if
v
is odd then
v
is smaller than all of its descendants and all of the children of
v
are even,

•

if
v
is even then
v
is larger than all of its descendants and all of the children of
v
are odd.

An
ID
forest is a forest, each of whose connected components is an ID tree. ID forests were introduced in
[
14
]
, where the authors showed that the ID forests on
V
are exactly the non-broken circuit sets (with respect to a particular edge order) of the graph
\Gamma_{V}

[
14
, Theorem 3.5]
.

A
q
-labeled ID tree
is a tree
T
on vertex set
V\subseteq[2r]
such that each edge of
T
not incident to
2r
is labeled with an element of
\{0,\dots,q-1\}
(so if
2r\notin V
, each edge of
T
is labeled). A
q
-labeled ID forest
is a forest, each of whose components is a
q
-labeled ID tree. We write
\mathcal{F}_{V,r}^{q}
for the set of
q
-labeled ID forests on
V\subseteq[2r]
. As in the unlabeled case, the
q
-labeled ID forests are exactly the non-broken circuit sets of the matroid arising from a complex hyperplane arrangement studied by Lazar and Wachs
[
12
,
11
, Proposition 4.2.8]
.

In
[
12
,
11
, Theorem 4.2.10]
, Lazar and Wachs construct a bijection between the
q
-labeled D-permutations on
V
with
k
cycles and the
q
-labeled ID forests on
V
with
k
components.

Let
V
be any finite subset of
\Z_{>0}
with odd minimum and even maximum such that
\lambda(V)=\lambda(\nu)
, and let
2r=\max V
. Then
V
has
m
odd elements and
n
even elements. We let
2s
be the second-largest even element of
V
and suppose that there are
\ell
odd elements of
V
between
2s
and
2r
. Finally, we let
V^{\prime}\coloneqq V|_{[2s]}
.

By
[
12
,
11
, Proposition 4.2.8]
and
[
12
,
11
, Theorem 4.2.10]
, we have the following interpretation of the coefficients of
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)
.

Proposition 8.2
.

The coefficient of
(-1)^{k}t^{k-1}
in
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)
is equal to the number of
q
-labeled D-permutations on
V
with exactly
k
cycles, and hence is equal to the number of
q
-labeled ID forests on
V
with exactly
k
components.

We can thus use Lemma
6.8
to show the following:

Theorem 8.3
.

For any weak composition
\nu
of
m
of length
n
and any finite
V\subseteq\Z_{>0}
with odd minimum and even maximum such that
\lambda(\nu)=\lambda(V)
, we have

\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)=(-1)^{m+n-1}(1-t)^{\ell}q^{m+n-\ell-1}\Lambda_{V}\left(\frac{1-t}{q},\frac{1-t}{q},1,0,\frac{-t}{q},1\right),

where
\ell
is the number of odd elements of
V
between the largest and second-largest even elements of
V
.

Proof.

We have

\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)=\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)=(-1)^{m+n-1}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{V,r}^{q}}(-t)^{c(F)-1},

where
c(F)
is the number of components of
F
. We let
F^{\prime}
be the forest obtained from
F
by deleting the vertex
2r
. Let
G
is a
q
-labeled ID forest on
V\setminus\{2r\}
such that
F^{\prime}=G
, and let
T
be a component of
G
. If
T
is not an isolated even vertex then either
T
is a component of
F
, or
T
is attached to
2r
in
F
. If
T
is an isolated even vertex then
T
must also be a component of
F
. Hence

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}_{V,r}^{q}\\
F^{\prime}=G\end{subarray}}(-t)^{c(G)-1}=(-t)^{\#\{\text{even isolated nodes of }G\}}(1-t)^{\#\{\text{other components of }G\}}.

Moreover, we know that
G
must have at least
\ell
isolated odd nodes for the
\ell
odd elements of
V
between
2s
and
2r
(since each such vertex can only share an edge with
2r
). Hence we have

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{V,r}^{q}}(-t)^{c(F)-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{F}_{V\setminus\{2r\},r}^{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}_{V,r}^{q}\\
F^{\prime}=G\end{subarray}}(-t)^{c(G)-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{F}_{V\setminus\{2r\},n}^{q}}(-t)^{\#\{\text{even isolated nodes of }G\}}(1-t)^{\#\{\text{other components of }G\}}

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{\ell}\sum_{G\in\mathcal{F}_{V^{\prime},r}^{q}}(-t)^{\#\{\text{even isolated nodes of }G\}}(1-t)^{\#\{\text{other components of }G\}}.

By applying the bijection between
\mathcal{F}_{V^{\prime},r}^{q}
and
\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime},r}^{q}
, this last sum becomes

(1-t)^{\ell}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime},r}^{q}}(-t)^{\#\{\text{even fixed points of }\sigma\}}(1-t)^{\#\{\text{other cycles of }\sigma\}}.

If
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V,r}^{q}
, we let
|\sigma|
be the underlying (unlabeled)
D
-permutation in
\mathcal{D}_{V}
. We thus have

\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)=(-1)^{m+n-1}(1-t)^{\ell}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime},n}^{q}\\
|\tau|=\sigma\end{subarray}}(-t)^{\#\{\text{even fixed points of }\sigma\}}(1-t)^{\#\{\text{other cycles of }\sigma\}}.

Since
2r\notin V^{\prime}
, given any
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime}}
we can construct a
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime},r}^{q}
with
|\tau|=\sigma
by labeling the largest entry of each cycle of
\sigma
with
0
and freely labeling all of the entries of
\sigma
with the elements of
\{0,\dots,q-1\}
. This means that there are exactly
q^{|V^{\prime}|-\#\{\text{cycles of }\sigma\}}
many
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime},r}^{q}
with
|\tau|=\sigma
.

Letting
\text{cyc}(\sigma)
be the number of cycles of
\sigma
, this means that we have

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\nu}^{q})}(t)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+n-1}(1-t)^{\ell}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime}}}q^{|V^{\prime}|-\text{cyc}(\sigma)}(-t)^{\#\{\text{even fixed pts of }\sigma\}}(1-t)^{\#\{\text{other cycles of }\sigma\}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+n-1}(1-t)^{\ell}q^{|V^{\prime}|}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{V^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{-t}{q}\right)^{\#\{\text{even fixed points of }\sigma\}}\left(\frac{1-t}{q}\right)^{\#\{\text{other cycles of }\sigma\}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+n-1}(1-t)^{\ell}q^{m+n-\ell-1}\Lambda_{V}\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right),

as desired, with the last equality following from Lemma
6.8
.
∎

8.2.
Generating Function Proofs

Since
\mathcal{H}_{n,k}^{m}=\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{m}
for
\nu=(\underbrace{k,k,\dots,k}_{n})
, we are now able to prove Theorem
5.4
.

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

By Theorem
8.3
, we have

\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k}^{m})}(t)=(-1)^{nk+n-1}(1-t)^{k}m^{nk+n-k-1}\Lambda_{S_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right),

where

S_{n}^{k}=\{1,3,\dots,2nk-1\}\sqcup\{2k,4k,\dots,2nk\}.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k}^{m})}(t)u^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{nk+n-1}(1-t)^{k}m^{nk+n-k-1}\Lambda_{S_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right)u^{n-1}

\displaystyle=(t-1)^{k}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{S_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right)((-m^{k+1})u)^{n-1}.

By Theorem
7.1
, this last sum is equal to

(t-1)^{k}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left(\frac{1-t}{m}\right)^{(n-1)}\left(\left(\frac{1-t}{m}+1\right)^{(n-1)}\right)^{k}((-m)^{n-1})^{k+1}u^{n-1}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-\left(\frac{1-t}{m}+i\right)^{k-1}\left[\left(\frac{1-t}{m}+i\right)\frac{-1}{m}-\left(\frac{1-t}{m}+i\right)i\right](-m)^{k+1}u\right)}.

Now,

\displaystyle(-m)^{n-1}\left(\frac{1-t}{m}\right)^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle=(-m)^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1-t}{m}+j-1\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}t-1-(j-1)m

\displaystyle=(t-1)_{n-1,m}.

Similarly,

(t-1)(-m)^{n-1}\left(\frac{1-t}{m}+1\right)^{(n-1)}=(t-1)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t-1-jm)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(t-1-jm)=(t-1)_{n,m}.

Thus, the generating function formula above simplifies to

\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k}^{m})}(t)u^{n-1}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(t-1)_{n-1,m}\left((t-1)_{n,m}\right)^{k}u^{n-1}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-(im+1)(t-(im+1))^{k}u\right)},

as desired.
∎

Since
\mathcal{J}_{n,k}^{m}=\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{m}
for
\nu=(k,\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-1})
, a similar argument allows us to prove Theorem
5.6
.

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

By Theorem
8.3
, we have

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{n,k}^{m}}(t)=\begin{cases}(t-1)^{k},&n=1\\
(-m)^{n+k-1}\Lambda_{T_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right),&n\geq 2\end{cases},

so

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{n,k}^{m}}(t)u^{n-1}

\displaystyle=(-m)^{k}\sum_{n\geq 2}\Lambda_{T_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right)(-mu)^{n-1},

and by Theorem
5.5
the last sum is equal to

(-m)^{k}\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{\left(\frac{1-t}{m}\right)^{(n-2)}\Lambda_{T_{2}^{k}}(x+n-2,\bar{x}+n-2)\left|{}_{\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right)}\right.(-m)^{n-1}u^{n-1}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}\left[1-\left(\frac{-1}{m}-i\right)(-m)u\right]}.

Now, we know that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{T_{2}^{k}}(x+i,\bar{x}+i)=

\displaystyle(y+\bar{x}+i)(x+\bar{z}+i)^{k}

\displaystyle+(x+i)^{k-1}\left[(x+i)(\bar{y}-y)-k(\bar{x}+i)(\bar{z}-z)-(x+i)(\bar{x}+i)\right],

so

\displaystyle(-m)

{}^{k+1}\Lambda_{T_{2}^{k}}(x+n-2,\bar{x}+n-2)\left|{}_{\left(\frac{1-t}{m},\frac{1-t}{m},1,0,\frac{-t}{m},1\right)}\right.

\displaystyle=(t-1-m(n-2))\left[(t-1-m(n-1))^{k}+(m(n-2)+1)(t-1-m(n-2))^{k-1}\right].

Since
(-m)^{n-2}\left(\frac{1-t}{m}\right)^{(n-2)}=(t-1)_{n-2,m}
and
(t-1-m(n-2))(t-1)_{n-2,m}=(t-1)_{n-1,m}
, the generating function formula simplifies to

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{n,k}^{m}}(t)u^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(t-1)_{n-1,m}\left[(t-1-m(n-1))^{k}+(m(n-2)+1)(t-1-m(n-2))^{k-1}\right]u^{n-1}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}\left[1-(mi+1)u\right]},

as desired.

∎

9.
Final Remarks and Further Questions

In
[
14
, Corollary 3.9]
, it was shown that
-\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{2n-1})}(0)
is equal to the
(unsigned) Genocchi number

g_{n}
, and Hetyei’s count of the number of regions of
\mathcal{H}_{2n-1}
in
[
10
]
tells us that
-\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{2n-1})}(-1)
is equal to the median Genocchi number
h_{n}
. It therefore seems reasonable to define a family of generalizations
g_{n,k}
and
h_{n,k}
by

(10)

\displaystyle g_{n,k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{nk+n-1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})}(0)

(11)

\displaystyle h_{n,k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{nk+n-1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})}(-1).

Using Corollary
5.3
, we can derive generating function formulas for these sequences as functions of
k
.

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{n,k}u^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{nk+n-1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(n-1)!(n!)^{k}u^{n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+i^{2}u)}

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}h_{n,k}u^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{nk+n-1}\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})}(-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{n!((n+1)!)^{k}u^{n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+i(i+1)u)}

These generating functions reduce to generating functions due to Barsky and Dumont
[
1
]
for the Genocchi and median Genocchi numbers, respectively, when
k=1
.

Specializing the proof of Theorem
8.3
to
m=1
and
V=S_{n}^{k}
(c.f.
[
14
, Theorem 4.14]
), we see that

(14)

\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})}(t)=(1-t)^{k}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{S_{n-1}^{k}}}(-t)^{\#\{\text{even fixed points of }\sigma\}}(1-t)^{\#\{\text{other cycles of }\sigma\}}

By evaluating Equation (
14
) at
t=-1
, we obtain a decomposition of
h_{n,k}
into powers of
2
for all
n\geq 2
:

(15)

h_{n,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k(n-1)}h_{n-1,k}^{j}2^{j+k},

where
h_{n,k}^{j}
is the number of D-permutations on
S_{n}^{k}
with exactly
j
cycles that are not even fixed points.

In
[
14
, Corollary 4.16]
, the authors obtain a decomposition of the Genocchi numbers into powers of
2
that is expected to be the same as a decomposition due to Sundaram
[
18
, Theorem 3.15]
. However, the proof of this decomposition relies on a factorization of
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{2n-1})}(t)

[
14
, Theorem 4.15]
whose proof does not immediately generalize to
\mathcal{H}_{n,k}
.

Question 9.1
.

What is the largest power of
(t-1)
that divides
\chi_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})}(t)
?

Question 9.2
.

Is there a decomposition of
g_{n,k}
analogous to Sundaram’s decomposition of
g_{n}
?

There is a wealth of literature studying the Genocchi numbers and median Genocchi numbers. It would be interesting to see which results about those sequences can be generalized to this broader setting. For example, the Genocchi and median Genocchi numbers can be obtained from one another via a triangular array known as a Seidel triangle (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
).

Question 9.3
.

Is there an analogous relationship between
g_{n,k}
and
h_{n,k}
?

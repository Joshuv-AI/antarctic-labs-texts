New degeneration phenomenon for infinite-type Riemann surfaces Thanks: The author was supported in part by JST, the establishment of the University Fellowship Program for the Creation of Innovation in Science and Technology, Grant Number JPMJFS2123
Ryo Matsuda

Abstract
. Since the Teichmüller space of a surface R R is a deformation space of complex structures defined on R R , its Bers boundary describes the degeneration of complex structures in a certain sense. In this paper, constructing a concrete example, we prove that if S is a Riemann surface of infinite type, a Riemann surface with the marking exists, which is homeomorphic to the surface R R in the Bers boundary. We also show that many such degenerations exist in the Bers boundary.

1.
Introduction

The Teichmüller space
\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
of a Fuchsian group
\Gamma
is the space of quasiconformal deformations of
\Gamma
.
In terms of the corresponding Riemann surface
R:=\mathbb{H}/\Gamma
, the space
\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
can be regarded as the deformation space of the Riemann surface
R
; it is sometimes denoted by
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
.
Furthermore,
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
admits a structure as a complex manifold, which reflects a lot of the properties of the Riemann surface
R
.

As a common technique in complex analysis, one may examine the complex manifold structure of
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
by understanding properties of proper holomorphic maps from the complex disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<1\}
to
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
. On the other hand, a holomorphic function from
\mathbb{D}
to a complex manifold is characterized by the mapping induced by non-tangential limits at almost all points of the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
according to the Fatou–Riesz theorem.
Because the non-tangential limits are contained in the boundary of
\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
, understanding the boundary of
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
is for understanding its complex manifold structure.
The boundary of
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
describes the degeneration of the complex structures of Riemann surfaces, which, in turn, contributes to the understanding of Riemann surfaces.

Various boundaries for
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
have been considered.
We focus on the Bers boundary
\partial_{\mathrm{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
, which is obtained by embedding
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
into a
bounded region of the complex Banach space of all holomorphic quadratic forms. We will denote it
B(\Gamma)
(cf. Subsection
2.1
).
The embedding is called the Bers embedding
\mathcal{B}:\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)\to B(\Gamma)
.
The Teichmüller space so embedded can be seen as the space of deformations of the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
within the category of Kleinian groups.
When
R
is of finite type, it has been extensively studied in relation to three-dimensional hyperbolic geometry in great detail in Bers
[
Ber
]
, Maskit
[
Mas
]
, Abikoff
[
Abi
]
, and others.
When
R
is of finite type, the structure of the Bers boundary has been well investigated; the Bers boundary consists of Kleinian groups corresponding to Riemann surfaces that degenerate topologically
[
Ber
,
Mas
]
if
R
is compact.
When
R
has finitely many punctures, the same can be established by modifying the claim appropriately. This implies that the degeneration of complex structures leads to the degeneration of the topological structure.
The theorem of Bers–Maskit tells us that the degeneration of complex structures induces topological degeneration. Although topological structure is more fundamental than complex structure, breaking the complex structure breaks the base topological structure.
It is known that almost the same holds for the case where the Riemann surfaces have finitely many punctures (more details will be given in Section
2.1
).
From these structures of the Bers boundary, the study by Tanigawa and others has elucidated the structure of
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D},\operatorname{Teich}(R))
, which helps in understanding the complex manifold structure of
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
(Tanigawa
[
Tan1
,
Tan2
]
, Shiga
[
Shi1
,
Shi2
]
).

This paper considers the case when
R
is of infinite type. In this case,
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
becomes an infinite-dimensional complex manifold. We describe a specific construction method to illustrate degeneration phenomena where the complex structure degenerates but topologically remains homeomorphic to
R
.
In this paper, we shall call the Kleinian group corresponding to such a Riemann surface a David-Fuchsian b-group.
A detailed definition is described in Definition
2.1
.
In addition, we show that such degeneration phenomena again form infinite-dimensional complex manifolds within the Bers boundary. The quasiconformal maps are characterized as the solution of the partial differential equation called the Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
considered for a function
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
with
\|\mu\|_{L\infty}<1
.
Since the Teichmüller space is the set of all quasiconformal deformations of the Riemann surface, a deformation by the solution of the degenerate Beltrami equation is expected to exist on the Bers boundary.
“Degenerate” means that we consider the case
\|\mu\|_{L\infty}=1
. David
[
Dav
]
has studied sufficient conditions on
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
for the degenerate Beltrami equation to have a homeomorphic solution; its condition is called David’s condition. We construct the David-Fuchsian b-group by constructing a deformation of the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
to satisfy David’s condition.
To prove that the constructed David deformation of the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
exists on the Bers boundary, it is necessary to construct a sequence in
\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
that converges to the point in
B(\Gamma)
corresponding the constructed David deformation. To show convergence, we apply the estimate given by McMullen
[
McM
, Theorem 1.2]
; we impose as a condition that
\mathrm{short}(R)
is positive, where
\mathrm{short}(R)
is the infimum of lengths of essential simple closed geodesics of
R
.
The main result is as follows:

Theorem

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
R
be a Riemann Surface of analytically infinite type with
\mathrm{short}(R)>0
and infinitely many homotopically independent
essential closed geodesics
\{\gamma_{n}^{\ast}\}_{n\geq 0}
whose lengths are bounded. Then, there exists an infinite-dimensional complex manifold in
\partial_{\mathrm{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(R)
which consists of David-Fuchsian b-group. That is, there exists a unit ball
D
of a Banach space and a holomorphic map
F:D\to B(\Gamma)
, where
F(D)
is contained in the Bers boundary
\partial_{\mathrm{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(R)
, contains only David-Fuchsian b-group, and the dimension of the tangent space of the image is infinite.

Section
2
discusses some necessary results on quasiconformal maps, Kleinian groups, and David maps. In Section
3
, we compute the variants of the injective radius when the cylinders are stretched to apply them to McMullen’s inequality. In Section 4, we construct a Kleinian group using a David map, and we prove that the Kleinian group exists on
\partial_{\mathrm{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
. That is, we prove that David-Fuchsian b-group exists. In Section
4
, we consider how many David-Fuchsian b-groups exist on
\partial_{\mathrm{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
. In conclusion, it is shown that there exist open sets
D
of a certain infinite-dimensional Banach space and an injective holomorphic map
F:D\to B(\Gamma)
such that
F(D)
is contained in the Bers boundary and consists of David-Fuchsian b-group.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Teichmüller theory and Kleinian groups

Let
R
be a Riemann surface whose universal covering surface is the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
,
and let it be represented by a Fuchsian group
\Gamma

acting on
\mathbb{H}
as
R=\mathbb{H}/\Gamma
.
A quasiconformal mapping
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
is called compatible with
\Gamma

if
\Gamma^{f}:=f\Gamma f^{-1}
is again a Fuchsian group,
and the isomorphism
\Gamma\to\Gamma^{f}
given by conjugation with

f
is called a quasiconformal deformation of
\Gamma
.
Note that the deformation of
\Gamma
induces a quasiconformal deformation of the Riemann surface
R
.
Indeed, the map
f
induces a quasiconformal map
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{H}/\Gamma\to\mathbb{H}/\Gamma^{\prime}
.
Denote by
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)

the complex Banach space of all bounded measurable
Beltrami differentials
for
\Gamma

supported on
\mathbb{H}
. Each element
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
satisfies that

\mu\circ\gamma\cdot\overline{\gamma^{\prime}}=\mu\cdot\gamma^{\prime}
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Its open unit ball
\mathrm{Bel}(\Gamma)
is the space of Beltrami coefficients for
\Gamma
.
For each element
\mu\in\mathrm{Bel}(\Gamma)
, the unique quasiconformal homeomorphism

w^{\mu}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
with complex dilatation
\mu

that fixes
0,1
and
\infty
, is compatible for
\Gamma
.
Two elements
\mu
and
\nu
in
\mathrm{Bel}(\Gamma)
are said to be
Teichmüller equivalent

if
w^{\mu}|_{\mathbb{R}}=w^{\nu}|_{\mathbb{R}}
holds.
The
Teichmüller space

\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma

is the quotient space of
\mathrm{Bel}(\Gamma)
by the Teichmüller equivalence relation.

In the following,
we review how to embed
\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
into a bounded domain of the Banach space
B(\Gamma)
by a well-known method. Let
\mathbb{H}^{\ast}
be a lower half plane.

B(\Gamma)
is the complex Banach space of all
L^{\infty}
hyperbolic holomorphic quadratic forms
\varphi:\mathbb{H}^{\ast}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}

for
\Gamma
, that is, the point
\varphi
of
B(\Gamma)
satisfies
\varphi\circ\gamma\cdot(\gamma)^{\prime 2}=\varphi

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, and the
L^{\infty}
hyperbolic norm

\|\varphi\|_{B(\Gamma)}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{H}^{\ast}}|\varphi(z)||\operatorname{Im}z|^{2}
is finite.
Like
\mathrm{Bel}(\Gamma)
,
B(\Gamma)
is sometimes considered a 2-form on the Riemann surface
R
through the covering map
\pi:\mathbb{H}\to R
. It is denoted as
B(R)
in such a sense.
For each
\mu\in\mathrm{Bel}(\Gamma)
, set

\tilde{\mu}=\begin{cases}\mu&\text{on}\ \mathbb{H},\\
0&\text{on}\ \mathbb{H}^{\ast}.\end{cases}

Then, there exists an unique quasiconformal mapping
f_{\mu}:\hat{\mathbb{C}}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
the beltrami differential of
f_{\mu}
is equal to
\tilde{\mu}
, and

f_{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{z+i}+o(1),\ z\to-i.

The Schwarzian derivative of
f_{\mu}
is defined on
\mathbb{H}^{*}
as

S(f_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{H}^{*}})=\left(\frac{f_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{\mu}^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{\mu}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}.

The mapping
\mathcal{B}:\text{Bel}(\Gamma)\ni\mu\mapsto S(f_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{H}^{*}})\in B(\Gamma)
is well-defined and compatible with Teichmüller equivalence. In other words, it establishes a holomorphic map from
\text{Teich}(\Gamma)
to
B(\Gamma)
. The map is a biholomorphic map onto its image, called the
Bers embedding
.
As a known fact, its image is a bounded domain in
B(\Gamma)
.
Therefore, the boundary can be defined via the Bers embedding.
This is called the
Bers boundary

\partial_{\text{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma):=\partial\mathcal{B}(\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma))
.
Next, we summarise the holonomy homomorphism defined at each point in
B(\Gamma)
.
For each a point
\varphi\in B(\Gamma)
,
then there exists a unique, local univalent holomorphic map
W_{\varphi}:\mathbb{H}^{\ast}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
such that

S(W_{\varphi})=\varphi,\ \ \ W_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{1}{z+i}+o(1),\text{near}\ z=-i.

Then, to every an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
, there exists a unique Möbius transformation
g
such that

W_{\varphi}\circ\gamma=g\circ W_{\varphi}.

We denote the mapping
\gamma\mapsto g
by
\chi_{\varphi}
, called a monodromy isomorphism. If
W_{\varphi}

is an univalent,
\chi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
is also discrete. From Nehari’s theorem (
[
Ahl
, §VI Lemma 3]
),
if
\varphi
is contained in
\mathrm{Cl}(\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)):=\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)\cup\partial_{\text{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)

a function
W_{\varphi}
is a univalent function.
Then, we classify
\varphi
according to the properties of
\chi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
.

Definition 2.1

(Classification of elements in
B(\Gamma)
)
.

Let a function
\varphi
in
B(\Gamma)
. The group
\chi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
or the function
\varphi
is called

•

a
quasiFuchsian group

if the function
W_{\varphi}
can be extended to a homeomorphic map on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
,

•

a
cusp
if there exists a hyperbolic element

\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\chi_{\varphi}(\gamma)
is a parabolic element,

•

a
totally degenerate group

if
W_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{\ast})\subset\hat{\mathbb{C}}
is an open and dense subset.

In this paper, we call the group
\chi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
or the function
\varphi

a
topological quasi-Fuchsian b group

if
\varphi\in\partial_{\text{Bers}}\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)

and
\varphi
is a quasiFuchsian group. In particular, when the function
W_{\varphi}
satisfies a David condition
(see Definition
2.3
), we call
\chi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
or the function
\varphi

a
David-Fuchsian b-group
.

Bers
[
Ber
]
and Maskit
[
Mas
]
proved that if a finitely generated Fuchsian group
\Gamma
is of the first kind,
topological quasiFuchsian b-group does not exist in the Bers boundary.
The following are known.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Ber
]
and
[
Mas
]
)
.

Let
R
be a Riemann surface whose Fuchsian group is finitely generated of the first kind.
If a function
\psi\in\partial_{Bers}\operatorname{Teich}(S)
is not a cusp, it is a totally degenerate group.
Moreover, if a function
\varphi\in\mathrm{Cl}(\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)

is a quasiFucsian group, then
\varphi
is in
\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)
.

The others obtained results of Tanigawa
[
Tan1
, Proposition 4.4]
and Yamamoto
[
Yam
, Theorem 3.1]

after the Theorem
2.2
and the comparison and relations with the main results
are summarized in Section
6
.

2.2.
David map

This section summarizes the David map, characterized as a solution to the degenerate Beltrami equation.
In particular, we discuss the results studied by David
[
Dav
]
.
Since the quasiconformal map is characterized as a solution of the Beltrami equation,
the David map can be regarded as a degenerate version of the quasiconformal map.

Definition 2.3

( )
.

Let
\mu
be a measurable function on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{C}

and
|\mu(z)|<1
for almost all
z\in U
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. We say that

\mu
satisfies
a David condition
, if there exist
\alpha>0
,
C>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0

such that for each
\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon_{0}

\left|\{z\in U\mid|\mu(z)|>1-\varepsilon\}\right|_{\mathrm{Leb}}\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon}\right),

where
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Leb}}
is the Lebesgue measure on the plane.
Moreover, if
K_{\mu}(z):=\frac{1+|\mu|(z)}{1-|\mu|(z)}

satisfies

e^{K_{\mu}}\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for some
p\in(0,\infty)
, then
\mu

is said to have
exponentially
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
integrable distortion
.

Remark
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subset\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D})
and
K_{\mu}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
then
\mu
satisfies the David condition by Chebychev’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\{|\mu|>1-\varepsilon\}\right|_{\mathrm{Leb}}

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{K_{\mu}+1\geq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\right\}\right|_{\mathrm{Leb}}=\left|\{\exp(K(z)+1)\geq e^{2/\varepsilon}\}\right|_{\mathrm{Leb}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2/\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\exp(p(K(z)+1)).

Theorem 2.4

(Generalized Riemann mapping theorem,
[
Dav
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{C}
satisfies the David condition,
then the Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu(z)f_{z}(z)

has a homeomorphism solution
f_{\mu}\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{C})

which is unique up to postcomposition with a conformal map.

Definition 2.5

( )
.

Let
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
satisfy
|\mu(z)|<1
for allmost everywhere
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
A homeomorphism
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}

is called
the principal solution
to the Beltrami equation with coefficient
\mu

if the map
f
satisfies the following conditions:

•

f
satisfies the partial differential equation
f_{z}=\mu f_{z}\ \text{a.e.}
,

•

there exists a finite set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
such that

f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{C}\setminus E)
.

•

around the point at infinity,
f
has the Laurent expansion
f(z)=z+\frac{a_{1}}{z}+\cdots
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
AIM
, Theorem 20.4.7]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a measureable function on
\mathbb{C}
with
|\mu|<1\text{a.e.}
.
If there exist
p>1
and a measurable function
K
such that

e^{K}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
|\mu(z)|\leq\frac{K(z)-1}{K(z)+1}\chi_{\mathbb{D}}
,
where
\chi_{\mathbb{D}}
is the characteristic function of
\mathbb{D}
,
the Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu(z)f_{z}(z)

admits a unique principal solution
f\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
\mu
and
(\mu_{n})
satisfy

\exp(K_{\mu})\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for some
p\in(0,\infty)
and
e^{K_{\mu_{n}}}\in L^{p_{n}}_{\mathrm{loc}}

for some
p_{n}\in(0,\infty)
, respectively.
If
(\mu_{n})
satisfies
\|K_{\mu_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})}\to\pi
(as
n\to\infty)
,
the sequence of the normal solution
f_{\mu_{n}}

converges uniformly to the identity map on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

Denote the inverse map of
f_{\mu_{n}}
by
g_{n}
. Then
g_{n}
has the following modulus of continuity: for
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
,

|g_{n}(a)-g_{n}(b)|\leq 16\pi^{2}\frac{1+|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}}{\log\left(e+\frac{1}{|a-b|}\right)}\|K_{\mu_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})},

which is in
[
AIM
, Lemma 20.2.3]
. Therefore, the sequence
(g_{n})
is a normal family.
We obtain a subsequence of
(g_{n})
, converging uniformly in
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
to a mapping
g
.

Moreover, the sequence
(g_{n})
also satisfies the inequality:

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|(g_{n})_{z}-1|^{2}+|(g_{n})_{\bar{z}}|^{2}\leq\|K_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})},

thus
g
is in
W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{C})
.
This same argument and technique are also written in
[
AIM
, Theorem 20.2.1, Lemma 20.2.2]
.

To prove Proposition
2.7
,
we only have to prove that
g
is conformal on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
.
First of all, it is obvious that
g
is conformal on
g^{-1}(\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D}))
,
because
g_{n}
are conformal on
g_{n}^{-1}(\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D}))
and
the subsequence of
(g_{n})
is uniformly converging to
g
. Let
\mathbb{D}_{2}
be the disk of
radius
2
at the center
0
.
Next, from a standard argument,
we get

(2.1)

\int_{g_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{D})}|(g_{n})_{\bar{z}}|^{2}\leq\int_{g_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{D})}(|(g_{n})_{\bar{z}}|+|(g_{n})_{z}|)^{2}\leq\|K_{\mu_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})}.

In addition, we have

\int_{g_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}|(g_{n})_{z}|^{2}\geq\int_{g_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}|(g_{n})_{z}|^{2}\left(1-\left|\frac{(g_{n})_{\bar{z}}}{(g_{n})_{z}}\right|^{2}\right)=4\pi.

Then, extending
K_{\mu_{n}}
by 1 over the set
\{1\leq|z|\leq 2\}
,
we obtain the inequality from (
2.1
):

\int_{g_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}|(g_{n})_{\bar{z}}|^{2}\leq\|K_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}-\int_{g_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}|(g_{n})_{z}|^{2}\leq\|K_{\mu_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}-4\pi.

Therefore, let
n\to\infty
, it follows that

\int_{g^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}|g_{\bar{z}}|^{2}=0,

because
\|K_{\mu_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})}\to 4\pi
as
n\to\infty

from the assumption in Proposition
2.7
.
(Notice
K_{n}\equiv 1
on the set
\{1\leq|z|\leq 2\}
.) From Wely’s lemma,

g
is holomorphic on
g^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{2})
.

Finally, since
g
is holomorphic on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
and
is univalent in the neighborhood of
z=\infty
,
g
is conformal on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
,
using the fact that the degree of the holomorphic function is constant.
Moreover, because the Laurent expansion of
g_{n}
is

g_{n}(z)=z+a_{1,n}/z+\cdots
, we can prove
g\equiv\operatorname{id}
on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
.
That is, the sequence
(f_{\mu_{n}})
has a subsequence that uniformly converges to the identity map on

\hat{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

3.
Deformation of a family of cylinders

3.1.
A result of McMullen

Let
R
be a Riemann surface with
\mathrm{short}(R)>0
, where
\mathrm{short}(R)
is the infimum of the length of the closed geodesic on
R
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
McM
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
[\mu]
be a point in
\operatorname{Teich}(R)
, and
\nu
be a unit-norm Beltrami differential in the tangent space
T_{[\mu]}\operatorname{Teich}(R)
at
[\mu]
.
If the support of
\nu
is contained in the part of
f^{\mu}(R)
of injectivity radius less than
L<1/2
, where the injectivity radius is measured using the hyperbolic metric of
R
.
Then, the image of
\nu
under the derivative of Bers’ embedding
\mathcal{B}:\operatorname{Teich}(\Gamma)\to B(\Gamma)
has norm at most
C(L\log 1/L)^{2}
, where the constant
C
depends only on
\mathrm{short}(R)
.

Remark
.

In the original paper
[
McM
]
, it seems that
R
is assumed to be of analytically finite type, but it is easy to see that his proof works without the assumption that
R
is to be of analytically finite type.

3.2.
Variation of injectivity radius in stretch deformation

We denote by
\operatorname{St}(H):=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<H\}
,
C(H):=\operatorname{St}(H)/\langle x\mapsto x+2\pi\rangle
, where
H>0
. This cylinder is biholomorphic to
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid 1/e^{H}<|z|<e^{H}\}
. In particular, the modulus is
H/\pi
.
The simple closed curve
[0,2\pi]\ni x\mapsto x
in
C(H)
is called the core curve. The core curve of a region
\Omega
biholomorphic to
C(H)
is defined as the image of the conformal map from
C(H)
to
\Omega
.
Let
H>0
and
0<a<1
. In the following, we consider a quasiconformal deformation of
\operatorname{St}(H)
in which the subdomain
\operatorname{St}(aH)
is stretched by a factor of two and
\operatorname{St}(H)\setminus\operatorname{St}(aH)
is shifted in parallel.
This quasiconformal deformation induces a deformation of
C(H)
.
That is, the quasiconformal map and homotopy we are dealing with are as follows:

(3.1)

\psi_{H}:x+iy\mapsto\begin{cases}x+iy+aHi&aH\leq y\leq H\\
x+2iy&|y|\leq aH\\
x+iy-aHi&-H\leq y\leq-aH\end{cases}\in C\left((1+a)H\right),

For each
t\in[0,1]
, we define

(3.2)

Proof.

For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, the homepmprhism
f_{\lambda}
on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
corespond to
\lambda

the quasiconformal map

for

.
Hence its schwarizian derivative
S(f_{\lambda})
is

\frac{-6\lambda}{(z^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}.

We get

\displaystyle\|S(f_{\lambda})-S(f_{1})\|_{B(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D})}|(S(f_{\lambda})-S(f_{1}))(|z|^{2}-1)^{2}|

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D})}\left|\left(\frac{-6\lambda}{(z^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}-\frac{-6}{(z^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)(|z|^{2}-1)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=6\sup_{z\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D})}\left|\left(\frac{\lambda}{(z^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(z^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)(|z|^{2}-1)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=6\sup_{z\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D})}\left|\left(\frac{\lambda(z^{2}-1)^{2}-(z^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}{(z^{2}-\lambda)^{2}(z^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)(|z|^{2}-1)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=6\sup_{z\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D})}\left|\left(\frac{(\lambda-1)(z^{4}-\lambda)}{(z^{2}-\lambda)^{2}(z^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)(|z|^{2}-1)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=6|\lambda-1|\sup_{z\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{D})}\left|\frac{z^{4}-\lambda}{(z^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}\right|\left|\frac{(|z|^{2}-1)^{2}}{(z^{2}-1)^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq 6|\lambda-1|\sup_{x>1}\left|\frac{x^{4}-\lambda}{(x^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}\right|.

(

Summing up the above,

Axioms for Modelling Cubical Type Theory in a Topos
Ian Orton, Andrew M. Pitts

Abstract
. The homotopical approach to intensional type theory views proofs of equality as paths. We explore what is required of an object 𝙸 \mathtt{I} in a topos to give such a path-based model of type theory in which paths are just functions with domain 𝙸 \mathtt{I} . Cohen, Coquand, Huber and Mörtberg give such a model using a particular category of presheaves. We investigate the extent to which their model construction can be expressed in the internal type theory of any topos and identify a collection of quite weak axioms for this purpose. This clarifies the definition and properties of the notion of uniform Kan filling that lies at the heart of their constructive interpretation of Voevodsky’s univalence axiom.

Introduction

Cubical type theory
[
CoquandT:cubttc
]
provides a constructive
justification of Voevodsky’s univalence axiom, an axiom that has
important consequences for the formalisation of mathematics within
Martin-Löf type theory
[
HoTT
]

constructive set theory, Cohen

[
CoquandT:cubttc
]

give a model of their type theory using the category

of

algebra
[

, Chapter XI]
;
see
[

]

de Morgan algebra in

is used as an interval object
\mathtt{I}
in

\hat{\mathcal{C}}

notion of path, that is, by morphisms with domain
\mathtt{I}
. Cohen
et

call the objects of

notions
[

]

by name abstractions
[

, Chapter 4]

with structure that considerably simplifies the definition and
properties of the constructive notion of Kan filling that lies at the
heart of
[

]
. In particular, the filling operation
is obtained from a simple special case that
composes
a filling
at one end of the interval to a filling at the other end.
Coquand
[

]
has suggested that this distinctive
composition operation can be understood in terms of the properties of
partial elements and their extension to total elements, within the
internal higher-order logic of toposes
[

]

particular, the

condition on composition

[

avoid the non-constructive aspects of the classical notion of Kan
filling
[

]
, becomes automatic when the operations
are formulated internally. Our approach has the usual benefit of
axiomatics – helping to clarify exactly which properties of a topos
are sufficient to carry out each of the various constructions used to
model cubical type theory
[
CoquandT:cubttc
]
, clearing the way for
simplifications, generalisations and new examples.

To accomplish this, we find it helpful to work not in the higher-order
predicate logic of toposes, but in an extensional type theory equipped
with an impredicative universe of propositions
\Omega

]

\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Cofibrant propositions}
\par Kan filling and other cofibrancy conditions on collections of
subspaces have to do with extending maps defined on a subspace to maps
defined on the whole space. Here we take ``subspaces of spaces'' to
mean subobjects of objects in toposes. Since subobjects are classified
by morphisms to $\Omega$, it is possible to consider collections of
subobjects that are specified generically by certain propositions.
More specifically, given a property of propositions,
$\cof:\Omega\mathbin{\shortrightarrow}\Omega$, we get a corresponding collection of
propositions
\begin{equation}\mathtt{Cof}\triangleq\{\varphi:\Omega\mid\cof\varphi\}\end{equation}Consider the class of monomorphisms $m:A\rightarrowtail B$ whose
classifying morphism
$$\lambda(y:B)\mathbin{\shortrightarrow}\exists(x:A).\;m\,x=y:B\mathbin{\shortrightarrow}\Omega$$factors through $\mathtt{Cof}\rightarrowtail\Omega$. We call such monomorphisms
\emph{cofibrations}. Kan-like filling properties have to do with when a
morphism $A\rightarrow X$ can be extended along a cofibration
$m:A\rightarrowtail B$. Instead, working in the internal language of $\mathcal{E}$, we
will consider when partial elements whose domains of definition are in
$\mathtt{Cof}$ can be extended to totally defined elements. Recall that in
intuitionistic logic, partial elements of a type $A$ are often
represented by sub-singletons, that is, by functions $s:A\mathbin{\shortrightarrow}\Omega$
satisfying
$$\forall(x\;x^{\prime}:A).\;s\,x\conj s\,x' \Rightarrow x= x'
$$
However, it will be more convenient to work with an extensionally
equivalent representation as dependent pairs $\varphi:\Omega$ and
$f:[\varphi]\mathbin{\shortrightarrow}A$, as in the next definition. The proposition
$\varphi$ is the extent of the partial element; in terms of
sub-singletons it is equal to $\exists(x:A).\;s\,x$.
\par\defi[{Cofibrant partial elements, $\Cpf A$}]
We assume we are given a subobject $\mathtt{Cof}\rightarrowtail\Omega$ satisfying
axioms $\mathtt{ax_{5}}$--$\mathtt{ax_{8}}$ in Figure~\ref{fig:axi}. We call elements
of type $\mathtt{Cof}$ \emph{cofibrant propositions}. Given a type
$A:\mathcal{U}$, we define the type of \emph{cofibrant partial elements}
of $A$ to be
\begin{equation}\Cpf A\triangleq(\varphi:\mathtt{Cof})\times([\varphi]\mathbin{\shortrightarrow}A)\end{equation}An \emph{extension} of such a partial element $(\varphi,f):\Cpf A$
is an element $a:A$ together with a proof of the following relation:
\begin{equation}(\varphi,f)\nearrow a\triangleq\forall(u:[\varphi]).\;f\,u=a\end{equation}\par Note that by taking $i=\mathtt{0}$ in axiom $\mathtt{ax_{5}}$ we have
$\cof(\mathtt{0}=\mathtt{0})$ (that is, $\cof\top$) and $\cof(\mathtt{0}=\mathtt{1})$; and
combining the latter with axiom $\mathtt{ax_{2}}$ we deduce also that
$\cof\bot$ holds. So $A\rightarrowtail A$ and $\emptyset\rightarrowtail A$ are always

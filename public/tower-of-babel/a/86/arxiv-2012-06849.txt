Approximate Generalized Additive-Quadratic Functional Equations on Ternary Banach Algebras
Sedigheh Jahedi

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we introduce the concept of j-hom-derivation,
j\in\{1,2\}
and solve the new generalized additive-quadratic functional equations in the sense of ternary Banach algebras. Moreover, using the fixed point method, we prove its Hyers-Ulam stability.

Keywords:
Hyers-Ulam stability, ternary Banach algebra, additive function, quadratic function, fixed point theorem.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):
17A40, 39B52, 17B40, 47B47.

Introduction and preliminaries

A ternary Banach algebra
X
with
\|.\|
is a complex Banach algebra equipped with a ternary product
(xyz)\rightarrow xyz
of
X^{3}
into
X
. This product is
\mathbb{C}
-linear in the outer variable, conjugate
\mathbb{C}-
linear in the middle variable associative in the sense that

xy(zwv)=x(zyw)v=(xyz)wv
and satisfies
\|xyz\|\leq\|x\|.\|y\|.\|z\|,~\|xxx\|=\|x\|^{3}
(see
[
24
]
). Ternary structures and their extensions, known as n-ary algebras have many applications in mathematical physics and photonics, such as the quark model and Nambu mechanics
[
13
,
17
]
. Today, many physical systems can be modeled as a linear system. The principle of additivity has various applications in physics especially in calculating the internal energy in thermodynamic and also the meaning of the superposition principle.

The Hyers-Ulam stability problem which arises from Ulam’s question says that for two given fixed functions
\varphi
and
\psi,
the functional equation
\mathcal{E}_{1}(F)=\mathcal{E}_{2}(F)
is stable if for a function
f
for which
d(\mathcal{E}_{1}(f),\mathcal{E}_{2}(f))\leq\varphi
holds, there is a function
g
such that
\mathcal{E}_{1}(g)=\mathcal{E}_{2}(g)
and
d(f,g)\leq\psi.

[
9
,
11
,
21
,
23
]
. In 1941
[
11
]
, Hyers solved the approximately additive mappings on the setting of Banach spaces.

The functional equation
f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)
is an additive equation and its solution is called an additive mapping.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
11
]

Let X and Y be Banach spaces. Assume that
f:X\to Y
satisfies

\|f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\varepsilon

for all
x,y,z\in X
and some
\varepsilon\geq 0.
Then there exists a unique additive mapping
T:X\to Y
such that

\|f(x)-T(x)\|\leq\varepsilon

for all
x\in X.

First, T. Rassias
[
21
]
and Aoki
[
1
]
and then a number of authors extended this result by considering the unbounded Cauchy differences in different spaces. For example see
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
14
,
18
]
. F. Skof in 1983
[
22
]
, proved the stability problem of quadratic functional equation between normed and Banach spaces. The functional equation
f(x+y)+f(x-y)=2f(x)+2f(y)
is called quadratic equation. Cholewa
[
4
]
showed that the Skof’s theorem is also true for the mappings defined on abelian groups. Later, a lot of research appeared with various generalization of quadratic functional equations
[
5
,
15
,
20
]
.

Consider the generalized additive-quadratic functional equation

\begin{split}3^{j}f(\frac{x+y+z}{3})&+f(x)+f(y)+(-1)^{j}f(z)-2^{j}f(\frac{x+y}{2})-2^{j}f(\frac{y+z}{2})-(-1)^{j}2^{j}f(\frac{x+z}{2})\\
&=\rho[jf(x+y+z)+jf(x)-f(x+y)-f(x+z)-(j-1)f(y+z)]\end{split}

(1.1)

where
\rho\neq 0,\pm 1
is a complex number and
j\in\{1,2\}
. In this paper, we solve (
1.1
) and show that for
j=1,
a function which satisfies (
1.1
) is additive and for
j=2,
it is quadratic. We also prove its Hyers-Ulam stability by using the fixed point method. To do this, we use the Diaz-Margolis fixed point theorem
[
16
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
16
]

Let
(X,d)
be a complete generalized metric space and let
F:X\rightarrow X
be a strictly contractive mapping
with Lipschitz constant
0<L<1
. Then for each given element
x\in X
, either

d(F^{n}(x),F^{n+1}(x))=\infty

for all nonnegative integers
n
or there exists a positive integer
n_{0}
such that

(1)

d(F^{n}(x),F^{n+1}(x))<\infty,\hskip 17.00024pt\forall n\geq n_{0}
;

(2)
the sequence
\{F^{n}(x)\}
converges to a unique fixed point
y^{*}
of
F
in the set
Y=\{y\in X\mid d(F^{n_{0}}x,y)<\infty\}
;

(4)

d(y,y^{*})\leq\frac{1}{1-L}d(y,F(y))
for all
y\in Y
.

Main Results

Extending the concepts of m-homomorphism,
1\leq m\leq 4,
and hom-derivation which has been introduced by Eshaghi Gordji et. al
[
6
]
and park et. al
[
19
]
, respectively, we have the following definitions in the case of ternary Banach algebras. Throughout the paper, X is a ternary Banach algebra.

We say that a functional
f
is a j-mapping,
j\in\{1,2\}
if for
j=1
it is an additive and for
j=2,
f is a quadratic mapping.

Definition 2.1
.

A mapping
f:X\to X
is a ternary j-homomorphism,
j\in\{1,2\},
if
f
is a j-mapping and

f(xyz)=f(x)f(y)f(z)

for all
x,y,z\in X.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
h:X\to X
be a ternary j-homomorphism,
j\in\{1,2\}.
A mapping
D:X\to X
is called a ternary j-hom-derivation if
D
is a j-mapping and satisfies

D(xyz)=D(x)(h(y))^{j}(h(z))^{j}+(h(x))^{j}D(y)(h(z))^{j}+(h(x))^{j}(h(y))^{j}D(z)

for all
x,y,z\in X
.

For
j\in\{1,2\}
and a given mapping
f:X\to X
consider the equation

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{j}f(x,y,z)=3^{j}f(\frac{x+y+z}{3})&+f(x)+f(y)+(-1)^{j}f(z)-2^{j}f(\frac{x+y}{2})-2^{j}f(\frac{y+z}{2})-(-1)^{j}2^{j}f(\frac{x+z}{2})\\
&-\rho[jf(x+y+z)+jf(x)-f(x+y)-f(x+z)-(j-1)f(y+z)].\end{split}

(2.1)

In the following we give the solution of the functional equation (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f:X\to X
be a mapping satisfies
\mathcal{E}^{j}(f(x,y,z))=0
,
j\in\{1,2\}.
Then
f(0)=0
and

(i)~
for
j=1
, f is an odd and additive mapping;

(ii)~
for
j=2
, f is a quadratic mapping if f is an even function.

Proof.

First of all, note that
\mathcal{E}^{j}f(0,0,0)=0
implies that
f(0)=0.

(i)~
Suppose
j=1
. Let
x=0
and
y=0
in (
2.1
).Then

3f(\frac{z}{3})=f(z)

(2.2)

Put
x=0
and replacing
z
by
y
in (
2.1
) and using (
2.2
), we obtain
f(2y)-2f(y)=\rho[f(2y)-2f(y)].
Since
\rho\neq 0,\pm 1,
then

f(2y)=2f(y).

(2.3)

Again put
x=0
in (
2.1
), use (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we get
\rho[f(y+z)-f(y)-f(z)]=0.
So

f(y+z)=f(y)+f(z)

i.e.,
f
is additive. To show that
f
is an odd function put
z=-y
in the above equation, so
f(-y)=-f(y)
.

(ii)~
Suppose
j=2
and
f
is an even function satisfies
\mathcal{E}^{2}f(x,y,z)=0.
So from
\mathcal{E}^{2}f(x,y,-x)=0
we have

\displaystyle 9f(\frac{y}{3})+f(y)+2f(x)-4f(\frac{x+y}{2})-4f(\frac{y-x}{2})=\rho[2f(y)+2f(x)-f(x+y)-f(y-x)].

(2.4)

Letting
y=0
and

in (
2.4
), then

f(2x)=4f(x).

(2.5)

Again in (
2.4
) put

and use (

9f(\frac{y}{3})=f(y).

(2.6)

Finally, by applying (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) in

f(x+y)+f(x-y)=2f(x)+2f(y).

This completes the proof.
∎

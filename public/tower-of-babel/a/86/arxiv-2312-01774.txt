Non-abelian base change for symmetric power liftings of holomorphic modular forms
Laurent Clozel, James Newton, and Jack A. Thorne

Abstract
. Let f f be a non-CM Hecke eigenform of weight k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . We give a new proof of some cases of Langlands functoriality for the automorphic representation π \pi associated to f f . More precisely, we prove the existence of the base change lifting, with respect to any totally real extension F / 𝐐 F/\mathbf{Q} , of any symmetric power lifting of π \pi .



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a totally real number field. In this paper we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal, regular algebraic automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
without CM, and let
n\geq 1
. Then the base change of the symmetric power
\Sym^{n-1}\pi
to
F
exists. More precisely, there exists a cuspidal, regular algebraic automorphic representation
\mathrm{BC}_{F/\mathbf{Q}}(\Sym^{n-1}\pi)
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{F})
such that for any place
w
of
F
lying above the place
v
of
\mathbf{Q}
, we have

{\operatorname{rec}}_{F_{w}}(\mathrm{BC}_{F/\mathbf{Q}}(\Sym^{n-1}\pi)_{w})\cong\Symm^{n-1}{\operatorname{rec}}_{\mathbf{Q}_{v}}(\pi_{v})|_{W_{F_{w}}}.

More informally, we prove the existence of certain functorial liftings for Hecke eigenforms of weight
k\geq 2
over
\mathbf{Q}
; the functoriality being the composite of ‘
(n-1)
th symmetric power’ and ‘base change with respect to
F/\mathbf{Q}
’.

The case
n=2
of Theorem
1.1
, which is just base change, was established by Dieulefait using modularity lifting theorems and the notion of a ‘safe chain’ of congruences between modular forms
[
Die12
,
Die15
]
. The general case of Theorem
1.1
then follows using the results of two of us on symmetric power functoriality for Hilbert modular forms
[
NT22
]
. However, in this paper we give new proofs of both base change and the existence of the symmetric power lifting for the base changed eigenform. Our strategy is based on the papers
[
NT21a
,
NT21b
]
, which handle the case
F=\mathbf{Q}
. We hope that this work (a first draft of which was written before
[
NT22
]
) is still of interest.

The case
F=\mathbf{Q}
was established in the papers
[
NT21a
,
NT21b
]
using the following two principles as the primary inputs:

•

An ‘analytic continuation of functoriality’ principle, that asserts that the existence of a functorial lifting can be propagated along irreducible components of the eigencurve. This is particularly effective in light of the description by Buzzard–Kilford
[
BK05
]
of a large part of the 2-adic, tame level 1 eigencurve.

•

A ‘functoriality lifting theorem’, giving robust hypotheses under which a congruence
\overline{r}_{\pi,\iota}\cong\overline{r}_{\pi^{\prime},\iota}
of mod
p
residual representations attached to (cuspidal, regular algebraic) automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{F})
implies the equivalence of the existence of the liftings
\Sym^{n-1}\pi
and
\Sym^{n-1}\pi^{\prime}
.

Here we extend these ideas in order to prove Theorem
1.1
. Where possible, we strengthen the arguments used in
[
NT21a
,
NT21b
]
, with one eye towards potential future applications of a similar type. In particular, we prove a more general classicality result (Proposition
3.7
), and replace the appeal to the very intricate level-raising results of
[
NT21a
]
with an appeal to the more generally applicable level-raising theorems proved in
[
Tho24
]
. This gives a streamlined proof even in the case
F={\mathbf{Q}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

We first remark that if
\pi
is a cuspidal, regular algebraic automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
without CM,
n\geq 2
is an integer,
p
is a prime, and
\iota:\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\to\mathbf{C}
is an isomorphism, then
\Sym^{n-1}r_{\pi,\iota}|_{G_{F}}
is irreducible. This explains why the base change of
\Sym^{n-1}\pi
to a totally real field
F
can always be expected to be
cuspidal
. Indeed, if
\pi
is not of CM type, then the Zariski closure of
r_{\pi,\iota}(G_{\mathbf{Q}})
contains
\SL_{2}
, implying that in fact any finite index subgroup of
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
acts irreducibly in any symmetric power of the standard representation. This is quite useful:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal, regular algebraic automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
, let
n\geq 1
, and let
F^{\prime}/F
be a (Galois) soluble extension of totally real number fields. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a polarizable
automorphic representation
\Pi^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{F^{\prime}})
, a prime number
p
, and an isomorphism
\iota:\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\to\mathbf{C}
such that
r_{\Pi^{\prime},\iota}\cong\Sym^{n-1}r_{\pi,\iota}|_{G_{F^{\prime}}}
.

(2)

There exists a polarizable automorphic representation
\Pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{F})
such that for any prime number
p
and isomorphism
\iota:\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\to\mathbf{C}
, we have
r_{\Pi,\iota}\cong\Sym^{n-1}r_{\pi,\iota}|_{G_{F}}
.

Proof.

This follows from the irreducibility of
\Sym^{n-1}r_{\pi,\iota}|_{G_{F^{\prime}}}
and soluble base change for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
(see
[
AC89
]
; a convenient reference for us is
[
BLGHT11
, Lemma 1.3]
).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
F
be a totally real field, and let
\pi
be cuspidal, regular algebraic automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{F})
without CM. Let
n\geq 1
, let
p
be a prime number, and let
\iota:\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\to\mathbf{C}
be an isomorphism. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Symm^{n-1}\pi
exists, in the sense that there is a cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi=\Symm^{n-1}\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{F})
such that for every place
v
of
F
,
{\operatorname{rec}}_{F_{v}}(\Pi_{v})\cong\Symm^{n-1}\,{\operatorname{rec}}_{F_{v}}\pi_{v}
.
\Pi
is then polarizable.

(2)

There exists a polarizable automorphic representation
\Pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{F})
such that
r_{\Pi,\iota}\cong\Symm^{n-1}r_{\pi,\iota}
.

Proof.

If (1) holds then (2) holds, as can be checked at finite unramified places. If (2) holds, then we must show that
\Pi=\Symm^{n-1}\pi
, i.e. that
\Pi_{v}
has the correct local components. If
v
is a finite place then this follows from local-global compatibility for
r_{\pi,\iota}
. If
v
is an infinite place, then we have
{\operatorname{rec}}_{F_{v}}(\Pi_{v})|_{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}\cong\Symm^{n-1}{\operatorname{rec}}_{F_{v}}\pi_{v}|_{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}
(as both sides are a sum of characters, determined by the Hodge–Tate weights of
r_{\Pi,\iota}\cong\Symm^{n-1}r_{\pi,\iota}
). We can now repeat verbatim the argument of
[
Tho24
, Proof of Theorem 8.1]
to conclude that
{\operatorname{rec}}_{F_{v}}(\Pi_{v})\cong\Symm^{n-1}\,{\operatorname{rec}}_{F_{v}}\pi_{v}
.
∎

We next give a useful lemma, which implies that many congruences of residual Galois representations over
\mathbf{Q}
remain robust after base extension to a totally real field
F
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime, and let
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
be a continuous representation such that
\overline{\rho}(G_{\mathbf{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{a}})
for some
a\geq 1
such that
p^{a}\geq 5
. Let
F
be a totally real number field. Then
\overline{\rho}(G_{F})
contains a conjugate of
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{a}})
.

Proof.

After increasing
a
and replacing
\overline{\rho}
by a conjugate, and applying the classification of finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, we can assume that
\operatorname{Proj}\overline{\rho}(G_{\mathbf{Q}})
equals
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{a}})
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{a}})
. In particular, it contains the simple group
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{a}})
as a subgroup of index at most 2. If
\operatorname{Proj}\overline{\rho}(G_{F})
contains
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{a}})
, then we’re done.

We are free to enlarge
F
, so we can replace it by its Galois closure. Let
K/\mathbf{Q}
be the extension cut out by
\operatorname{Proj}\overline{\rho}
. Then
\Gal(K/K\cap F)
is a normal subgroup of
\Gal(K/\mathbf{Q})
which contains each complex conjugation. Since
\operatorname{Proj}\overline{\rho}(G_{F})=\operatorname{Proj}\overline{\rho}(G_{K\cap F})
, we see that if the conclusion of the lemma fails, then
\Gal(K/K\cap F)
must be trivial. This would contradict the fact that since
p
is odd, each complex conjugation is non-trivial as an element of
\Gal(K/\mathbf{Q})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Analytic continuation of base change

3.1.
Definite unitary groups

As in
[
NT21a
]
, our main analytic continuation result will first be proved for definite unitary groups. We adopt the following assumptions and notation, as in
[
NT21a
, §1]
:

•

K
is a CM number field such that
K/K^{+}
is everywhere
unramified (this implies that
[K^{+}:{\mathbf{Q}}]
is even).

•

p
is a prime. We write
S_{p}
for the set of
p
-adic places of

K^{+}
.

•

\iota
is an isomorphism
\iota:\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\to}\limits^{\sim}}\mathbf{C}
.

•

S
is a finite set of finite places of
K^{+}
, all of which split in

K
.
S
contains
S_{p}
.

•

For each
v\in S
, we suppose fixed a factorization
v={\widetilde{v}}{\widetilde{v}}^{c}
in

K
, and write
\widetilde{S}=\{{\widetilde{v}}\mid v\in S\}
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Under the above assumptions we can fix the following data:

•

The unitary group
G_{n}=G
over
K^{+}
with
R
-points given by the formula

(3.1)

G(R)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R\otimes_{K^{+}}K)\mid g=(1\otimes c)(g)^{-t}\}.

G
is quasi-split at all finite places, while for each place
v|\infty
of
K^{+}
,
G(K^{+}_{v})
is compact. We use the same formula to extend
G
to a reductive group scheme over
{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{+}}
.

•

If
L^{+}/K^{+}
is a finite totally real extension, we set
L=L^{+}K
and let
G_{n,L^{+}}
be the extension of scalars of
G_{n}
to
L^{+}
(it can be identified with the unitary group defined relative to
L/L^{+}
by formula
3.1
).

•

If
T
is a set of places of
K^{+}
then we write
T_{L}
for the set of
places of
L^{+}
lying above
T
. If
w\in T_{L}
lies above
v\in T
and
v

splits
v={\widetilde{v}}{\widetilde{v}}^{c}
in
K
(in particular, we suppose that we have made a choice of
{\widetilde{v}}|v
), then we will write
{\widetilde{w}}
for the unique place of

L
which lies above both
w
and
{\widetilde{v}}
(in which case
w
splits
w={\widetilde{w}}{\widetilde{w}}^{c}
in
L
). We write e.g.
\widetilde{S}_{L}
for the set of places of the form
{\widetilde{w}}
(
w\in S_{L}
).

3.2.
Eigenvarieties, their classical points and Galois pseudocharacters

Let
E\subset\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p}
be a coefficient field, which contains the normal closure of
L
, and denote its residue field by
k
. Let
U_{n,L}=\prod_{v}U_{n,L,v}\subset G_{n,L^{+}}(\mathbf{A}_{L^{+}}^{\infty})
be an open compact subgroup with
U_{n,L,v}=G_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{L^{+}_{v}})
for finite places
v\not\in S
. (When
L=K
we will suppress all the subscripts in our notation.)

For
\pi
an automorphic representation of
G_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{L^{+}})
with
\pi^{U_{n,L}}\neq 0
, we have the notion of accessible refinements
\chi=(\chi_{v})_{v\in S_{p,L}}
for
\pi
, where each
\chi_{v}
is a
p
-adic character of the diagonal maximal torus
T_{n}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}})
in
G_{n}(L^{+}_{v})\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}})
which appears in the normalized Jacquet module of
\iota^{-1}\pi_{v}
. See
[
NT21a
, §2.17]
for more details.

We denote by
{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{A}}_{n,L}
the set of pairs
(\pi,\chi)
, where
\pi
is an automorphic representation of
G_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{L^{+}})
such that
(\pi^{\infty})^{U_{n,L}^{p}}\neq 0
and
\chi
is an accessible refinement of
\pi
.

We have an eigenvariety
{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}
of tame level
U_{n,L}^{p}
, a rigid space defined over
E
. See
[
NT21a
, §2.18.1]
. Its (Zariski-dense) subset
Z_{n,L}
of classical points admits a surjective map
\gamma_{n}:{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{A}}_{n,L}\to Z_{n,L}
.

The eigenvariety comes equipped with a finite morphism
\nu:{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}\to{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L}
, where
{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L}=\Hom\left(\prod_{v\in S_{p,L}}T_{n}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}}),\mathbf{G}_{m}\right)
and
\nu(\gamma_{n}(\pi,\chi))=\nu(\pi,\chi)
is given by the formula
[
NT21a
, (2.18.1)]
. It is also natural to consider a renormalisation
\jmath\nu
given by composing
\nu
with an automorphism
\jmath
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L}
(see
[
NT21a
, §2.17]
again). When studying the local geometry of eigenvarieties, we will need to restrict to points
z
where the character
\delta(z):=\jmath\nu(z)
is regular:

Definition 3.3
.

A character

\delta=\prod_{v\in S_{p,L}}\delta_{v}\in\Hom\left(\prod_{v\in S_{p,L}}T_{n}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}}),\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p}^{\times}\right)

is
regular
if for each
v\in S_{p,L}
and
1\leq i<j\leq n
we have
\delta_{v,i}/\delta_{v,j}\neq x^{a_{v}}
for any
a_{v}=(a_{v,\tau})_{\tau}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{\Hom_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}},\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})}
, where
x^{a_{v}}(y)=\prod_{\tau}\tau(y)^{a_{v,\tau}}
.

Regularity defines a Zariski-open condition on characters, so we get a Zariski-open subspace
{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L}^{reg}\subset{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L}
.

For a single place
v
, we also have
{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}^{reg}\subset{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}
where
{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}=\Hom\left(T_{n}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}}),\mathbf{G}_{m}\right)
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}^{reg}
is defined by the condition in Definition
3.3
at the place
v
.

There is a family of conjugate self-dual Galois pseudocharacters
\tau^{\mathrm{univ}}_{n,L}:G_{L,S_{L}}\to{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{E}}_{n,L})
over
{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}
, with the property that the specialization
\tau_{z}
at
z=\gamma(\pi,\chi)\in Z_{n,L}
is equal to
\operatorname{tr}r_{\pi,\iota}
. We fix a residual pseudocharacter
\overline{\tau}:G_{L,S_{L}}\to k
and consider the (open and closed) subspace
{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}(\overline{\tau})\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}
where
\tau^{\mathrm{univ}}_{n,L}
reduces to
\overline{\tau}
. We assume that this subspace is non-empty (in particular,
\overline{\tau}
is then conjugate self-dual). Then we have the space
{\mathcal{X}}_{ps,n,L}
of conjugate self-dual deformations of
\overline{\tau}
, defined in
[
NT21a
, §2.11.1]
.

Now we have a morphism
\lambda:{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}(\overline{\tau})\to{\mathcal{X}}_{ps,n,L}
and a closed immersion
i_{n}=\lambda\times(\jmath\circ\nu):{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}(\overline{\tau})\to{\mathcal{X}}_{ps,n,L}\times{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L}
.

The locus
{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}^{\mathrm{irred}}
where
\tau^{\mathrm{univ}}_{n,L}
is absolutely irreducible is an open subset of
{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}
. If
z\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}^{\mathrm{irred}}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})
, we have an absolutely irreducible representation
r_{z}:G_{L,S_{L}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})
, well-defined up to conjugation, with
\operatorname{tr}r_{z}=\tau_{z}
.

If
\delta_{v}\in\Hom\left(T_{n}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}}),\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p}^{\times}\right)
and
\rho_{v}:G_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})
is a continuous representation, we have the notion of a triangulation of
\rho_{v}
with parameter
\delta_{v}
(cf.
[
NT21a
, §2.3.1]
). When the labelled weights of the triangulation are strictly increasing sequences of integers, we say that the triangulation is non-critical. When the parameter
\delta_{v}
satisfies
\delta_{v,i}(\varpi_{{\widetilde{v}}})\in\overline{{\mathbf{Z}}}_{p}^{\times}
for each
i
we say that it is ordinary.

Definition 3.4
.

Suppose
(\pi,\chi)\in{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{A}}_{n,L}
and let
\delta=\jmath\nu(\pi,\chi)
. The refinement
\chi
is said to be
ordinary
if the parameter
\delta_{v}
is ordinary for each
v\in S_{p,L}
.

Remark 3.5
.

If
(\pi,\chi)\in{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{A}}_{n,L}
and
\chi
is ordinary, then
[
Tho15
, Theorem 2.4]
implies that
r_{\pi,\iota}|_{G_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}}
has a non-critical triangulation with parameter
\delta_{v}
for each
v\in S_{p,L}
.

Definition 3.6
.

A point
z\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}(\overline{\tau})^{\mathrm{irred}}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})
is said to be
non-critical
if for each
v\in S_{p,L}
,
r_{z}|_{G_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}}
admits a non-critical triangulation of parameter
\delta_{v}
, where
\jmath\nu(z)=\prod_{v\in S_{p,L}}\delta_{v}
.

If
z
is a non-critical point, the parameters
\delta_{v}=\delta_{v,alg}\delta_{v,sm}
are necessarily locally algebraic and the local representations
r_{z}|_{G_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}}
are de Rham. The (distinct) labelled Hodge–Tate weights correspond to the strictly dominant character
\delta_{v,alg}
. (See
[
BC09
, Proposition 2.3.4]
and
[
HS16
, Proposition 2.6]
.)

We now prove a simple classicality result for non-critical points, a variant of
[
NT21a
, Lemma 2.30]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
z\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n,L}(\overline{\tau})^{\mathrm{irred}}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})
be a non-critical point with
\delta:=\jmath\nu(z)\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L}^{reg}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})
a regular parameter. Then
z\in Z_{n,L}
.

Before the proof of this proposition, we give two preliminary lemmas on
(\varphi,\Gamma_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}})
-modules. The role of these is to show that a non-critical
z
as in the statement of the proposition cannot have a companion point
z^{\prime}
with
r_{z^{\prime}}\cong r_{z}
and
\jmath_{n}(\nu(z^{\prime}))=\delta^{\prime}_{alg}\delta_{sm}
with the same smooth part as
\delta
but
\delta^{\prime}_{alg}\neq\delta_{alg}
. This is a small part of the companion points conjectures of Breuil
[
Bre15
, Conj. 6.6]
and Hansen
[
Han17
, §6.2]
. Under a stronger regularity hypothesis on
\delta
, it would essentially follow from
[
BHS19
, Thm. 4.2.3]
. We make use of the Robba ring
{\mathcal{R}}_{E,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}
and its
(\varphi,\Gamma_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}})
-modules, as recalled in
[
NT21a
, §2.3.1]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
v\in S_{p,L}
, and let
D
be a
(\varphi,\Gamma_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}})
-module over
{\mathcal{R}}_{E,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}
admitting a triangulation
\Fil_{\bullet}
of locally algebraic parameter
\delta=\delta_{alg}\delta_{sm}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}^{reg}(E)
.

Suppose that for each
\tau\in\Hom_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}(L_{{\widetilde{v}}},E)
we have a strictly increasing sequence of integers
{\operatorname{wt}}_{\tau}(\delta_{1})<{\operatorname{wt}}_{\tau}(\delta_{2})<\cdots{\operatorname{wt}}_{\tau}(\delta_{n})
(i.e.
\delta_{alg}
is strictly dominant).

Suppose that
D
has a triangulation
\Fil^{\prime}_{\bullet}
with a locally algebraic parameter
\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}_{alg}\delta_{sm}
whose smooth part coincides with that of
\delta
. Then
\delta^{\prime}=\delta
.

Proof.

Considering the Sen weights of
D
, we see that
\delta^{\prime}_{alg}=w\delta_{alg}
for an element
w
of the Weyl group of
(\operatorname{Res}_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}/{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\mathrm{GL}_{n})\times_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}E
. Suppose, for a contradiction, that
w\neq 1
. Choose
i\geq 1
such that
{\operatorname{wt}}_{\tau}(\delta_{j})={\operatorname{wt}}_{\tau}(\delta^{\prime}_{j})
for all
\tau
and all
j<i
and
{\operatorname{wt}}_{\tau}(\delta_{i})\leq{\operatorname{wt}}_{\tau}(\delta^{\prime}_{i})
for all
\tau
with strict inequality for some
\tau=\tau_{0}
.

We now reduce to the case
i=1
. If
i>1
, then
\delta_{1}=\delta^{\prime}_{1}
. The fact that
\delta\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}^{reg}(E)
implies that the cohomology group
H^{0}_{\varphi,\gamma_{L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}}(D(\delta_{1}^{-1}))
of the Herr complex (
[
KPX14
, Definition 2.3.3]
) is one-dimensional (cf.
[
NT21a
, Lemma 2.4]
). In particular, there is a unique submodule of
D
which is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{R}}_{E,L_{{\widetilde{v}}}}(\delta_{1})
. We deduce that
\Fil_{1}=\Fil^{\prime}_{1}
. Considering
D/\Fil_{1}
and inducting on
n
, we may assume that
i=1
.

The algebraic character
\delta_{1}^{\prime}/\delta_{1}
satisfies

(3.2)

Since

\iota
-ordinary regular algebraic, cuspidal, conjugate self-dual automorphic representation

.

•

For each prime number

M
-regular.

•

For each

, the automorphic representation

\mathrm{BC}_{F/\mathbf{Q}}(\Sym^{n-1}\pi_{i-1})

This will complete the proof of the Proposition. Indeed, the hypothesis

implies that

r

∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let

be a prime. There exists a regular, cuspidal algebraic automorphic representation

without CM with the following properties:

(2)

\pi_{p}
admits a numerically non-critical,

(3)

\mathrm{BC}_{F/\mathbf{Q}}(\Sym^{n-1}\pi)

Let

be an imaginary quadratic field in which

be an everywhere unramified character of type

such that if

; then
\sigma
is a regular algebraic, cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})

, and an isomorphism

is
\iota_{t}
-ordinary. Let

be the extension cut out by

cut out by
\overline{r}_{\sigma,\iota_{t}}

\sigma_{1}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
of weight
k
and satisfying the following conditions:

•

There is an isomorphism
\overline{r}_{\sigma_{1},\iota_{t}}\cong\overline{r}_{\sigma,\iota_{t}}
.

•

\sigma_{1,q}
is an unramified twist of the Steinberg representation.

•

\sigma_{1,t}

•

For every prime number

admits an

-regular refinement.

In particular,

is
\iota_{t}

∎

Proposition 6.5
.

, then

. Therefore, Theorem

Proof.

Suppose that

holds, and let
\pi
be a regular algebraic, cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
without CM and satisfying the following additional conditions:

•

For every prime
p
,
\pi_{p}
is not supercuspidal.

•

If
p>N+1
is prime, then
\pi_{p}
is unramified.

•

If

\pi_{p}
is
M
-regular.

We need to show that

exists. After replacing

. We can also assume, without loss of generality, that

is prime and

∎

7.
Proof of the main theorem – general case

Fix a totally real number field
F
and integer

. In this section, we will complete the proof of the following theorem:

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\pi
be a regular algebraic automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
, without CM. Then
\mathrm{BC}_{F/\mathbf{Q}}(\Sym^{n-1}\pi)
exists.

We will prove the theorem, as in
[

]
, by induction on the cardinality of the set

of primes
p
such that
\pi_{p}
is supercuspidal, the base case

having been treated already. The proof we give in the case

will rely on the case

(in which case

) since the ‘functoriality lifting theorem’ proved in
[
NT21b
]
requires as an assumption the existence of the base change
\mathrm{BC}_{F/\mathbf{Q}}(\pi)
in order to be able to conclude the existence of
\mathrm{BC}_{F/\mathbf{Q}}(\Sym^{n-1}\pi)
.

In
[
NT21b
]
, Theorem
7.1
in the case

is reduced to the case where
\pi
has no supercuspidal local components by constructing a chain of congruences and using automorphy lifting theorems. Lemma
2.3
shows that this chain of congruences is still robust after base change to

, and as such the arguments here follow those of
[
NT21b
, §3]

Let
\pi
be a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})

. Let

, Proposition 3.7]

Let

.

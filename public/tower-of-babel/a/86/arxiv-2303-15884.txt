Abstract
. Extended affine root systems appear as the root systems of extended affine Lie algebras. A subclass of extended affine root systems, whose elements are called “minimal” turns out to be of special interest mostly because of the geometric properties of their Weyl groups; they possess the so-called “presentation by conjugation”. In this work, we characterize minimal extended affine root systems in terms of “minimal reflectable bases” which resembles the concept of the “base” for finite and affine root systems. As an application, we construct elliptic Lie algebras by means of Serre’s type generators and relations.

Introduction

Motivated by applications in “the construction of a flat structure for the base space of
the universal deformation of a simple elliptic singularity” K. Saito in
[
Sa
]
introduced the concept of an extended affine root system.
Considering
[
Sl
]
, he predicted the “existence of Lie algebras
corresponding to extended affine root systems which would describe the universal deformation of the simple elliptic singularity”. The prediction was partially answered in
[
H-KT
,
P
]
.
Later in 1997, in a systematic study, a class of algebras associated with extended affine root systems was introduced via a set of axioms
[
AABGP
]
. Each element of this class is called an “extended affine Lie algebra”. Since then, the theory of extended affine root systems (Lie algebras) (see Definitions
1.1.1
and
6.1.1
) and related topics have been under intensive investigation.
It turns out that despite the striking similarities with the finite, and affine Lie theory, there are enough interesting differences to make the study of extended affine Lie theory a demanding subject. In what follows, we explain the motivation of the present work in two levels; the level of the root system and Weyl group, and the level of Lie algebra.

Level of Root System and Weyl Group: One of the astonishing privileges of finite and affine Weyl groups is that they are Coxeter groups with respect to
certain appropriate generating sets.
In finite types, these generating sets consist of reflections based on elements of a fundamental system of the corresponding root system
[
C
,
Hu2
,
MP
]
; thus by
[
B
, Chapter IV, §8, Corollary 3]
, these generating sets are “minimal”.
In contrast, it is known that the Weyl group of an extended affine root system
R
is not, in general, a Coxeter group. In fact, one knows that an extended affine Weyl group is a Coxeter group if and only if the corresponding root system is either finite or affine
[
Ho
, Theorem 3.6]
.
Therefore, finding a suitable presentation for an extended affine Weyl group has always been a demanding problem, see for example
[
Kr1
,
Kr2
,
A1
,
A2
,
AS1
,
AS2
,
AS3
,
AS4
,
AS5
,
SaT
]
. A possible approach to achieve such a presentation is as follows. In the theory of root datum, it is known that the Coxeter presentation implies the so-called “presentation by conjugation”
[
MP
, Chapter 5,§3]
, see Definition
2.2.4
. Therefore it is natural to ask if an extended affine Weyl group possesses the presentation by conjugation.
This question was raised first by Y. Krylyuk and was affirmatively answered in
[
Kr2
]
for simply laced extended affine Weyl groups of rank
>1
. Later in a series of works the question was considered for general types,
[
A2
,
Ho
,
AS3
,
AS4
,
AS5
]
. In brief, it is revealed that only elements of a subclass of extended affine Weyl groups enjoy the presentation by conjugation. The root system corresponding to an element of this class is called a “minimal” extended affine root system (see Definition
5.1.1
). For general extended affine Weyl groups, one gives a weaker presentation, called “the generalized presentation by conjugation”
[
A2
]
, which coincides with the presentation by conjugation if the corresponding root system is minimal. This explains our motivation for the study of minimal extended affine root systems.

The main objective of this work is to give a characterization of minimal extended affine root systems of reduced types in terms of minimal reflectable bases, see Theorem
5.1.3
. We achieve this as a by-product of a study of interrelations between three classes of certain subsets of extended affine root systems which we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{r}
,
\mathcal{M}_{m}
and
\mathcal{M}_{c}
, and we explain them and their related terminologies below.
Let
R
be a reduced extended affine root system with the set of non-isotropic roots
R^{\times}
, and the Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
. For a subset
\mathcal{P}
of
R^{\times}
, we denote the subgroup of
{\mathcal{W}}
generated by reflections based on
\mathcal{P}
by
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
. The set

is called a
reflectable base
for

if
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{P}=R^{\times}
and no proper subset of
\mathcal{P}
has this property. The class of all such sets is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{r}
. The class of all sets
\mathcal{P}\subseteq R^{\times}
which are minimal with respect to the property that
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{W}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{m}
, and
the class of all sets
\mathcal{P}\subseteq R^{\times}
which have the minimal cardinality with respect to the property
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{W}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{c}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{rm}=\mathcal{M}_{r}\cap\mathcal{M}_{m}
. In the finite theory, one concludes that

3.2.2
.

Level of Lie Algebra: K. Saito conjectured in
[

that starting from any extended affine root system

there should be a Lie algebra with root system

. This led to many interesting investigations

1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some preliminaries on extended affine root systems, in particular, we describe the structure of a reduced extended affine root system in terms of a finite root system and certain subsets called “semilattices”.

All vector spaces are finite-dimensional and considered over the field
{\mathbb{R}}
. For a vector space
{\mathcal{U}}
, we denote the dual space of
{\mathcal{U}}
by
{\mathcal{U}}^{\ast}
. for a subset
S
of
{\mathcal{U}}
, we denote by
\left\langle S\right\rangle
, the additive subgroup of
{\mathcal{U}}
generated by
S
. The symbol
\uplus
denotes the disjoint union of sets and
|\mathcal{P}|
shows the cardinality of a set
\mathcal{P}
.

For a vector space
{\mathcal{U}}
equipped with a symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
, we denote the radical of the form by
{\mathcal{U}}^{0}
. For a subset
R
of
{\mathcal{U}}
, we set
R^{0}:=R\cap{\mathcal{U}}^{0}
and
R^{\times}:=R\setminus R^{0}
. A subset
\mathcal{P}
of
{\mathcal{U}}^{\times}
is called
connected
(with respect to the form), if

\mathcal{P}
can not be decomposed as
\mathcal{P}_{1}\uplus\mathcal{P}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are non-empty subsets of
\mathcal{P}
satisfying
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})=\{0\}
.

1.1.
Extended affine root systems

Definition 1.1.1
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{V}}
is a finite-dimensional real vector space equipped with a positive semidefinite symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Suppose
R
is a subset of
{\mathcal{V}}
. Following
[
AABGP
, Definition 2.1]
, we say
R
is an
extended affine root system
if

(1)

0\in R
.

(2)

R=-R
.

(3)

R
spans
{\mathcal{V}}
.

(4)

If
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, then
2\alpha\not\in R
.

(5)

R
is discrete in
{\mathcal{V}}
, with respect to the natural topology
of
{\mathcal{V}}
when identified with
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n=\dim{\mathcal{V}}
.

(6)

If
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
\beta\in R
, then there exist non-negative integers
d,u
satisfying
\{\beta+n\alpha\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\cap R=\{\beta-d\alpha,\ldots,\beta+u\alpha\}
and
d-u=\frac{2(\alpha,\beta)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}
.

(7)

R^{\times}
is connected.

(8)

If
\sigma\in R^{0}
, then there exists
\alpha\in R^{\times}
such that
\alpha+\sigma\in R
.

Elements of
R^{\times}
are called
non-isotropic roots
, and elements of
R^{0}
are called
isotropic roots
. The dimension
\nu
of
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
is called the
nullity
of
R
.
Since the form is positive semidefinite, one can easily check that

R^{0}=\{\alpha\in R\mid(\alpha,\alpha)=0\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad R^{\times}=\{\alpha\in R\mid(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0\}.

As it is announced in
[
AF
]
, the definition of an extended affine root system is equivalent to the following.

Definition 1.1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a finite-dimensional real vector space equipped with a positive semidefinite symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
, and
R
a subset of
{\mathcal{V}}
. Then
R
is called an
extended affine root system

if the following axioms hold:

(R1)

\langle R\rangle
is a full lattice in
{\mathcal{V}}
.

(R2)

(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
.

(R3)

w_{\alpha}(\beta)\in R
for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,
\beta\in R
.

(R4)

R^{0}={\mathcal{V}}^{0}\cap(R^{\times}-R^{\times})
.

(R5)

\alpha\in R^{\times}\Rightarrow 2\alpha\not\in R
.

(R6)

R^{\times}
is connected.

Remark 1.1.3
.

(i) The axiom (R1) means that the natural map

\langle R\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
is a vector space isomorphism. In other words
\langle R\rangle
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of an
{\mathbb{R}}
-basis of
{\mathcal{V}}
.

(i) From (R4), we conclude that
R^{\times}\not=\emptyset
.

Let
R
be an extended affine root system in a vector space
{\mathcal{V}}
and let
\bar{\;}:{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathcal{V}}:={\mathcal{V}}/{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
be the canonical map.
It is known that
\bar{R}
, the image of
R
under the map
\bar{\;}
is an irreducible finite root system in
\bar{\mathcal{V}}
, see
[
AABGP
, Proposition II.2.9(d)]
.

Definition 1.1.4
.

The
type
and the
rank
of extended affine root system
R
is defined as the type and the rank of
\bar{R}
. In this work, we always assume that
R
is of
reduced type
, that is
\bar{R}
has one of the types
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
,
E_{6,7,8}
,
F_{4}
or
G_{2}
.

Definition 1.1.5
.

(i) For a real vector space
{\mathcal{U}}
equipped with a symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
, and
\alpha\in{\mathcal{U}}
with
(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0
, the
reflection based on

\alpha
is defined by

\beta\mapsto\beta-\frac{2(\beta,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}{\alpha},\;\beta\in{\mathcal{U}}.

By convention, if
(\alpha,\alpha)=0
, we interpret
w_{\alpha}
as the identity map.

(ii) For a subset
\mathcal{P}\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
the subgroup of
GL({\mathcal{U}})
generated by
w_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}
.

1.2.
Semilattices

Let
R
be an extended affine root system in
{\mathcal{V}}
with corresponding from

(\cdot,\cdot)
. As was mentioned before, we denote the radical of
(\cdot,\cdot)
by
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
. Certain subsets of
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
called “semilattices” play an important role in describing the internal structure
of extended affine root systems.

Definition 1.2.1
.

A
semilattice
in
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
is a spanning subset
S
of
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
which is discrete, contains
0
and satisfies
S\pm 2S\subseteq S
.

Here we record some facts about semilattices needed in the sequel.

1.2.2
.

Let
S
be a semilattice
in
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
and set
\Lambda:=\langle S\rangle
.

(i)
\Lambda
is a lattice in
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
; that is
\Lambda
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of a basis of
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
.

(ii)
2\Lambda+S\subseteq S\subseteq\Lambda
and so

S=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}(\tau_{i}+2\Lambda),
for some

\tau_{i}\in S
, with
\tau_{0}=0
.

(iii)
\Lambda
admits a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
B=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}\}
consisting of elements of
S
.

Definition 1.2.3
.

Assume that
S
is a semilattice
with
\Lambda=\langle S\rangle
.

(i) The
index
of
S
, denoted
\hbox{ind}(S)
, is by definition the least positive integer
m
such that

1.2.2
(ii) holds.

(ii) Assume that
\hbox{ind}(S)=m
, and
\tau_{i}
’s and
B
are as in
1.2.2
(iii).
For
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}n_{i}\sigma_{i}\in\Lambda
, we define
\mbox{supp}(\sigma):=\{i\mid n_{i}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}+1\}.

Then
\sigma=\sum_{i\in\mbox{supp}(\sigma)}\sigma_{i}
(mod
2\Lambda
).
The collection

\mbox{supp}(S)=\{\mbox{supp}(\tau_{i})\mid 0\leq i\leq m\},

is called the
supporting class
of
S
with respect to the basis
B
.

The supporting class determines
S
uniquely. In fact, we have

S=\biguplus_{J\in\mbox{supp(S)}}(\tau_{J}+2\Lambda),

(1)

where
\tau_{J}:=\sum_{r\in J}\sigma_{r}
.

Internal structure of extended affine root systems

In
[
AABGP

As before we assume that

We start with recording the following lemma whose proof for simply laced types is given in

Lemma 1.3.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a reduced irreducible finite root system in a vector space
{\mathcal{U}}
, and
\mathcal{P}
be a minimal subset (with respect to inclusion) of
\Phi^{\times}
such that

{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{P}=\Phi^{\times}

|\mathcal{P}|=\hbox{rank}\;\Phi
.

Proposition 1.3.2
.

Let
R
be an extended affine root system and
\mathcal{P}
a subset of
R^{\times}
satisfying
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{P}=R^{\times}
. Then

\mathcal{P}
contains a subset
\dot{\Pi}
such that the following hold.

(i)
\dot{R}:=({\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\Pi}}\dot{\Pi})\cup\{0\}
is a finite root system
in
\dot{\mathcal{V}}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\dot{R}
,

(ii)
\dot{R}
is isomorphic to
\bar{R}
,

(iii)
{\mathcal{V}}=\dot{\mathcal{V}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{0},

(iv) For a fixed
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, there exists a finite root system
{\dot{R}}_{\alpha}\subseteq R
such that
\alpha\in{\dot{R}}_{\alpha}
and (ii) and (iii) hold with

and
{\dot{\mathcal{V}}}_{\alpha}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\dot{R}_{\alpha}
in place of
\dot{R}
and
\dot{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proof.

Since
R^{\times}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{P}
, we have
\bar{R}^{\times}={\mathcal{W}}_{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}\bar{\mathcal{P}}
. Let
\bar{\Pi}
be a subset of
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\bar{R}^{\times}={\mathcal{W}}_{\bar{\Pi}}\bar{\Pi}
and no proper subset of
\bar{\Pi}
satisfies this property. From Lemma

, we have

is a basis of the vector space

. Let

\dot{\Pi}:=\{{\dot{\alpha}}_{1},\ldots,{\dot{\alpha}}_{\ell}\}

, and

\dot{R}:=({\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\Pi}}\dot{\Pi})\cup\{0\}

induces an isometry
\dot{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathcal{V}}
which maps

. It follows that

. It completes the proof of (i)-(iii).

Next, let

Then

Now

such that
\bar{\alpha}\in{\bar{\Pi}}_{\alpha}
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{{\bar{\Pi}}_{\alpha}}{\bar{\Pi}}_{\alpha}={\bar{R}}^{\times}

in place of
{\bar{\Pi}}
. Then as above we fix a preimage

of

where we pick

. Finally, we construct

in the same manner as

. Then
{\dot{R}}_{\alpha}
and
{\dot{\mathcal{V}}}_{\alpha}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\dot{R}}_{\alpha}
satisfy (ii)-(iii) in place of

\dot{R}
and
\dot{\mathcal{V}}
, and the proof of (iv) is completed.

Extended affine Weyl groups

We proceed with the same notation as
in Section
1

R

{\mathcal{V}}
of type
X
, rank
\ell
and nullity

{\mathcal{V}}^{0}

2.1.
The setting

2.1.1
.

We fix a description of
R
in the form
R=(S+S)\cup(\dot{R}_{sh}+S)\cup(\dot{R}_{lg}+L)
as in
1.3.3
. We recall that
\dot{\mathcal{V}}:=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{R}
and
{\mathcal{V}}=\dot{\mathcal{V}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
. We consider the
\ell+2\nu
-dimensional real vector space
\tilde{{\mathcal{V}}}:=\dot{{\mathcal{V}}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{0}\oplus({\mathcal{V}}^{0})^{\ast}
.
We normalize the form on
\dot{{\mathcal{V}}}
such that

(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha}):=2\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}_{sh}.

We extend the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\mathcal{V}}
to a symmetric form on
\tilde{{\mathcal{V}}}
, denoted again by
(\cdot,\cdot)
, as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}(\dot{{\mathcal{V}}},({\mathcal{V}}^{0})^{\ast})=(({\mathcal{V}}^{0})^{\ast},({\mathcal{V}}^{0})^{\ast}):=\{0\},\\
(\sigma,\lambda)=\lambda(\sigma),\hbox{ for }\sigma\in{\mathcal{V}}^{0},\;\lambda\in(V^{0})^{\star}.\end{array}

This forces the form on
\tilde{{\mathcal{V}}}
to be non-degenerate. For
\alpha\in{\mathcal{V}}
with
(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0
set
\alpha^{\vee}:=2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

The
extended affine Weyl group

{\mathcal{W}}
of
R
(or the Weyl group of extended affine root system
R
) is defined to be the subgroup of
GL(\tilde{\mathcal{V}})
generated by reflections
w_{\alpha}:\beta\mapsto\beta-(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.
Since
0\in L\subseteq S
, we have
\dot{R}\subseteq R
, and so we may identify
\dot{\mathcal{W}}
, the Weyl group of
\dot{R}
, as a subgroup of
{\mathcal{W}}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{W}})
, the center of
{\mathcal{W}}
.

2.2.
Presentation by conjugation

It is known that an extended affine Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
associated with
R
is not, in general, a Coxeter group. It is proved that
{\mathcal{W}}
is a Coxeter group if and only if
R
has nullity
\leq 1
(see
[
Ho
, Theorem 3.6]
).
Nevertheless, extended affine Weyl groups enjoy another interesting presentation called “generalized presentation by conjugation”.
When
R
has some “minimality condition”, then
{\mathcal{W}}
admits even a more delightful presentation, called the “presentation by conjugation”. We record these definitions here, as they play a crucial role in the sequel.

2.2.1
.

We begin by recalling some notation from
[
A2
, Sections 1, 2]
. We fix the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}\}\subseteq S
of
\Lambda=\langle S\rangle
and the integer
k
given in
1.2.2
(iii) and
1.3.4
. For
1\leq i\leq\nu
and
\alpha\in\langle R\rangle
define the linear map
t_{\alpha}^{(i)}:\tilde{{\mathcal{V}}}\rightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{V}}}
by

t_{\alpha}^{(i)}(\lambda):=\lambda-(\lambda,\frac{1}{k}\sigma_{i})\alpha+((\lambda,\alpha)-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha,\alpha)(\lambda,\frac{1}{k})\sigma_{i})\frac{1}{k}\sigma_{i}.

The map
t^{(i)}_{\alpha}
is invertible with the inverse
t^{(i)}_{-\alpha}
, so

t^{(i)}_{\alpha}\in\hbox{Aut}(\tilde{{\mathcal{V}}}).
For
1\leq i,j\leq\nu
set

c_{ij}:=t_{-\sigma_{j}}^{(i)}.

One checks that
(t_{\alpha}^{(i)},t_{\beta}^{(j)})=c_{ij}^{\frac{1}{k}(\alpha,\beta)}
, for
\alpha,\beta\in R
, where
(x,y):=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}
denotes the group commutator.

For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}m_{i}\sigma_{i}\in R^{0}
with
\alpha+\sigma\in R
, we set

c_{(\alpha,\sigma)}:=(w_{\alpha+\sigma}w_{\alpha})(w_{\alpha}w_{\alpha+\sigma_{1}})^{m_{1}}\ldots(w_{\alpha}w_{\alpha+\sigma_{\nu}})^{m_{\nu}}.

(1)

By
[
A2
, (2.4)]
, we have

c_{(\alpha,\sigma)}=\prod_{i<j}c_{ij}^{k(\alpha)m_{i}m_{j}},

(2)

where the integers
k(\alpha)
are defined as follows.

k(\alpha):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1&\hbox{if}&\alpha\in R_{lg},\\
k&\hbox{if}&\alpha\in R_{sh},\end{array}\right.

(If
R
is simply laced, then
k(\alpha)=1
for all
\alpha\in R^{\times}
).

2.2.2
.

Assume that
\theta_{s}
and
\theta_{l}
are the highest short and highest long roots of
\dot{R}
respectively (for simply laced cases
\theta:=\theta_{s}=\theta_{\ell}
), and for
1\leq p\leq\nu
consider a triple

(\varepsilon_{p},\alpha_{p},\eta_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}m_{ip}\sigma_{i})\in(\{\pm 1\}\times\{\theta_{s}\}\times\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{i})\cup(\{\pm 1\}\times\{\theta_{l}\}\times\sum_{i=t+1}^{\nu}{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{i}).

The collection
\{(\varepsilon_{p},\alpha_{p},\eta_{p})\}_{p=1}^{m}
is called a
reduced collection
if

\sum_{p=1}^{m}k(\alpha_{p})\varepsilon_{p}m_{ip}m_{jp}=0~~~\hbox{for all}~~1\leq i<j\leq\nu.

If
\{(\varepsilon_{p},\alpha_{p},\eta_{p})\}_{p=1}^{m}
is a reduced collection, then
c_{(\alpha_{p},\eta_{p})}
is an element of
{\mathcal{W}}
for all
1\leq p\leq m
(see
[
A2
, Section 1]
).

The following theorem is due to
[
A2
, Theorem 2.7]
,
[
Kr1
, Theorem 1.18]
,
[
AS3
, Theorem 5.19(ii)]
and
[
AS5
, Corollaries 2.34, 2.36(b)]
.

Theorem 2.2.3
.

Let
R
be an extended affine root system with Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
and
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}
be the group defined by generators
\widehat{w}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, subject to the relations:

\widehat{w}_{\alpha}^{2}=1\hbox{ for every }\alpha\in R^{\times},\vskip-8.53581pt

(3)

\widehat{w}_{\alpha}\widehat{w}_{\beta}\widehat{w}_{\alpha}=\widehat{w}_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)}\hbox{ for every }\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times},\vskip-8.53581pt

(4)

\prod_{p=1}^{m}\widehat{c}^{\varepsilon_{p}}_{(\alpha_{p},\eta_{p})}=1\hbox{ for any reduced collection }\{(\varepsilon_{p},\alpha_{p},\eta_{p})\}_{p=1}^{m},

(5)

where
\widehat{c}_{(\alpha_{p},\eta_{p})}
is the element in
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}
corresponding to
c_{(\alpha_{p},\eta_{p})},
under the assignment
w_{\alpha}\mapsto\widehat{w}_{\alpha}.

(i) The extended affine Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
is isomorphic to
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}.

(ii) If
R
is simply laced of rank
>1
, or is of type
F_{4}
or
G_{2}
then relations of the form (
5
) are consequences of relations of the forms (
3
) and (
4
); in the sense of Definition
2.2.4
below,
{\mathcal{W}}
has the presentation by conjugation.

(iii) If
\nu\leq 2
, then
{\mathcal{W}}
has the presentation by conjugation (Definition
2.2.4
).

Definition 2.2.4
.

The given presentation in Theorem
2.2.3
is called the
generalized presentation by conjugation
.
If
{\mathcal{W}}
is isomorphic to the presented group defined by generators

\widehat{w}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and relations (
3
) and (
4
), then
{\mathcal{W}}
is said to have the
presentation by conjugation.

2.2.5
.

As a consequence of
[
AS3
, Theorems 4.23, 5.16]
and
[
AS5
, Theorem 2.33]
, the extended affine Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
has the presentation by conjugation if and only if the epimorphism

\displaystyle\psi:\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}

(6)

\displaystyle{\widehat{w}}_{\alpha}\mapsto w_{\alpha},

is an isomorphism.

For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, we denote the orbit of
\alpha
under the action of
{\mathcal{W}}
on
R^{\times}
by
{\mathcal{W}}\alpha
, namely
{\mathcal{W}}\alpha=\{w\alpha\mid w\in{\mathcal{W}}\}
.

Corollary 2.2.6
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{W}}
has the presentation by conjugation and fix
\beta\in R^{\times}
. Then the assignments

w_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{\beta}}}{{\longmapsto}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if}~\alpha\in{\mathcal{W}}\beta,\\
0&\mbox{if}~\alpha\in R^{\times}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\beta,\end{array}\right.\quad\quad\quad w_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Psi}}{{\longmapsto}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\hbox{if }\alpha\in R_{lg},\\
1&\hbox{if }\alpha\in R_{sh}.\end{array}\right.

extend to epimorphisms

\Phi_{\beta}
and
{\Psi}
from
{\mathcal{W}}
onto
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, respectively.

Proof.

First, we consider
\Phi_{\beta}
. Since for
\alpha,\gamma\in R^{\times}
, we have

\gamma\in{\mathcal{W}}\beta
if and only if
w_{\alpha}(\gamma)\in{\mathcal{W}}\beta
, it easily follows that the defining relations of the form

w_{\alpha}w_{\gamma}w_{\alpha}w_{w_{\alpha}(\gamma)}
are mapped to zero under the assignment
\Phi_{\beta}
. The argument for defining relations of the form
w_{\alpha}w_{\alpha}
is obvious.

For the assignment
\Psi
, clearly, the defining relations are mapped to zero under
\Psi
, as reflections preserve the length.∎

Lemma 2.2.7
.

Let
R
be of non-simply laced type. Let
\{w_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}

be a set of generators for
{\mathcal{W}}
. Then
\mathcal{P}\cap R_{sh}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{P}\cap R_{lg}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

First, assume that
{\mathcal{W}}
has the presentation by conjugation and consider the epimorphism
\Psi:{\mathcal{W}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
of Corollary
2.2.6
. Now if
\mathcal{P}\subseteq R_{\lg}
, then for
\alpha\in R_{sh}
, we have

w_{\alpha}=w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{m}}
for some
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in R_{lg}
. Then

1=\Psi(w_{\alpha})=\Psi(w_{\alpha_{1}})+\cdots+\Psi(w_{\alpha_{m}})=0
which is absurd. An analogous argument works for the case
\mathcal{P}\subseteq R_{sh}
, replacing the roles of short and long roots. Thus the result holds if
{\mathcal{W}}
has the presentation by conjugation.

For the general case, we consider the epimorphism

{\mathcal{W}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{\bar{R}}
,
w_{\alpha}\mapsto w_{\bar{\alpha}}
, where

{\mathcal{W}}_{\bar{R}}
denotes the Weyl group of the finite root system
\bar{R}
. Now since
\{w_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}
generates
{\mathcal{W}}
, the set

\{w_{\bar{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}
generates
{\mathcal{W}}_{\bar{R}}
. Since
{\mathcal{W}}_{\bar{R}}
has the presentation by conjugation (see
[
MP
, Proposition 5.3.3]
), we get from the first part of the proof that
{\bar{R}}_{sh}\cap\bar{\mathcal{P}}\not=\emptyset
and

{\bar{R}}_{lg}\cap\bar{\mathcal{P}}\not=\emptyset
.
Since for
\alpha\in R
,
\alpha
, and
\bar{\alpha}
have the same length, we are done.∎

We return to the discussion about the existence of presentation by conjugation for extended affine Weyl groups in future sections.

3.1.4

We denote the class of reflectable bases,

Clearly, we have

(1)

Note that if
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{r}\cup\mathcal{M}_{m}

{\mathcal{V}}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathcal{P}

(2)

Proposition 3.1.5
.

If
\mathcal{P}\subseteq R^{\times}
satisfies
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
, then
\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle=\langle R\rangle
and
\mathcal{P}
is connected. In particular, if
\mathcal{P}
belongs to either of
\mathcal{M}_{c}
,
\mathcal{M}_{m}
and
\mathcal{M}_{r}
, then
\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle=\langle R\rangle
and
\mathcal{P}
is connected.

Proof.

For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, we have
w_{\alpha}=w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{n}}
for some
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{P}
. If there exists
\beta\in R^{\times}
with
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})=-1
, then

\alpha+\beta=w_{\alpha}(\beta)=w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{n}}(\beta)=\beta+k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\alpha_{n}

for some
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus
\alpha\in\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
and so
\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle=\langle R\rangle
. In particular, this holds for the types

X\not=A_{1},\;B_{\ell},\;C_{\ell}
. Assume now that
R
is of type
A_{1}
. Then

-\alpha=w_{\alpha}(\alpha)=w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{n}}(\alpha)=\alpha+2k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+2k_{1}\alpha_{n},

for some
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, which again give
\alpha\in\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
, as required.

Next assume that
R
is of type
B_{\ell}
. If
\alpha\in R_{lg}
, then there exists
\beta\in R_{sh}
,
such that
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})=-1
, and so we have
\alpha\in\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
by the preceding paragraph.
Thus
\langle R_{lg}\rangle\subseteq\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle.
It remains to show that
R_{sh}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
. By
1.3.4
(6),
R_{sh}=\dot{R}_{sh}+S_{1}\oplus\langle S_{2}\rangle
. Since
\langle S_{2}\rangle\subseteq\langle R_{\lg}\rangle
, it only remains to show that
\dot{R}_{sh}+S_{1}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle.
But as
\langle S_{1}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{t}
, we are done if we show that
\dot{R}_{sh}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
and

\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{t}\in\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
.

By Lemma
2.2.7
,
\mathcal{P}
contains a short root
\alpha
. By Proposition
1.3.2
(iv), we may assume that
\alpha\in\dot{R}_{sh}
. Since
\dot{R}_{lg}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
, this implies that
\dot{R}_{sh}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle
and so
\dot{R}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle.
To conclude the proof for type

, it remains to
show that

For this, we fix

We claim that this assignment can be lifted to an epimorphism

we need to show that the defining relations of the generalized presentation by conjugation
vanish in

under this assignment.
The relations of the form

To check that
\psi_{i}(w_{\alpha})\psi_{i}(w_{\beta})\psi_{i}(w_{\alpha})\psi_{i}(w_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)})=0

.
So suppose

\beta=\dot{\beta}+\sigma_{i}+\lambda
for some
\dot{\beta}\in\dot{R}_{sh}

and
\lambda\in\langle L\rangle

w_{\alpha}(\beta)=w_{\alpha}(\dot{\beta})+\sigma_{i}+\lambda\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm\dot{\beta}+2\Lambda+\sigma_{i}+\lambda\subseteq\dot{R}_{sh}+\sigma_{i}+\langle L\rangle&\hbox{if }\alpha\in R_{sh},\\
\dot{R}_{sh}+L+\sigma_{i}+\lambda\subseteq\dot{R}_{sh}+\sigma_{i}+\langle L\rangle&\hbox{if }\alpha\in R_{lg},\end{array}\right.

as required.

Finally, we consider relations of the form

(

Clearly, this holds if

Suppose now that

\psi_{i}(w_{\theta_{s}})=0
and

\psi_{i}(w_{\theta_{s}+\sigma_{j}})=0

\psi_{i}(w_{\theta_{s}+\sigma})+m_{i}\psi_{i}(w_{\alpha+\sigma_{i}})=0

if

, then as
1\leq i\leq t

m_{i}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}+1

\psi_{i}(w_{\theta_{s}+\sigma})=1
and

m_{i}\psi_{i}(\theta_{s}+\sigma_{i})=1
and we are done. Finally, we consider the case

\sigma\not\in\sigma_{i}+\langle L\rangle
. This forces

Then we have

\psi_{i}(w_{\theta_{s}+\sigma})=0

Thus as was claimed, the assignment
\psi_{i}
induces
an epimorphism
\psi_{i}:{\mathcal{W}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Now let

\alpha\in\dot{R}_{sh}
and fix

and so

where

and the proof for type

is completed. The proof for type

is analogous, replacing the roles of short and long roots.

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{P}
is connected. If not, then

\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are nonempty sets and

. Now let

. Since
\mathcal{P}
spans
{\mathcal{V}}
, there exists

with

. Assume without loss of generality that

.
We have
w_{\alpha}=w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{m}}
for some

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathcal{P}
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are orthogonal, it follows that

\beta-(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha=w_{\alpha}(\beta)=w_{\alpha_{j_{1}}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{j_{k}}}(\beta)
where

are in
\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Since

. This argument gives

which is absurd as

is connected.∎

To each subset
\mathcal{P}
of
R^{\times}
, we associate two subsets of
R
as follows.

R_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}:={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{P}\quad\hbox{and}\quad R_{\mathcal{P}}^{i}:=(R_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}-R_{\mathcal{P}}^{n})\cap R^{0}.

(3)

Proposition 3.1.6
.

Let
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{P}\subseteq R^{\times}
be connected.

(i)
The set
R_{\mathcal{P}}=R_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}\cup R_{\mathcal{P}}^{i}
is an extended affine root system in

{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{P}}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R_{\mathcal{P}}
satisfying
R_{\mathcal{P}}^{\times}=R_{\mathcal{P}}^{n}
,

R_{\mathcal{P}}^{0}=R_{\mathcal{P}}^{i}
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{W}}_{R_{\mathcal{P}}}
.

(ii) If
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
, then
R_{\mathcal{P}}
has the same rank, nullity and type of
R
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{P}
belongs to either of the classes

\mathcal{M}_{c}
or
\mathcal{M}_{m}
, then
R_{\mathcal{P}}
has the same rank, nullity and type of
R
.

(iii) If
\mathcal{P}
is a reflectable set, then
R_{\mathcal{P}}=R
.

Proof.

First, we note from the way
R_{\mathcal{P}}
is defined that
R^{0}_{\mathcal{P}}=R^{i}_{\mathcal{P}}
and

R^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}}=R^{n}_{\mathcal{P}}
.

(i) From definition of
R_{\mathcal{P}}
it is clear that axioms (R1)-(R3) and (R5) of an extended affine root system (Definition
1.1.2
) hold for
R_{\mathcal{P}}
.
Since
\mathcal{P}
is connected it follows easily that
R_{\mathcal{P}}
is connected, so (R6) also holds. For (R4), since
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}_{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
, we have

\displaystyle R^{0}_{\mathcal{P}}=R^{i}_{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(R^{n}_{\mathcal{P}}-R^{n}_{\mathcal{P}})\cap R^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(R^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}}-R^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}})\cap\big((R^{\times}-R^{\times})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{0}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(R^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}}-R^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{0}_{\mathcal{P}}.

(ii) If
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
, then
\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathcal{P}={\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{V}}
, so

\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R^{\times}_{\mathcal{P}}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathcal{P}={\mathcal{V}}
and

\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R_{\mathcal{P}}^{0}={\mathcal{V}}^{0}
. Thus

has the same nullity as

. This gives
\hbox{rank}\;R_{\mathcal{P}}=\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{P}}-\dim{\mathcal{V}}^{0}_{\mathcal{P}}=\dim{\mathcal{V}}-\dim{\mathcal{V}}^{0}=\hbox{rank}\;R
. Also by Lemma
2.2.7
,

and

have the same number of root lengths.
Thus both
\bar{R}
and

have the same rank and the same number of root lengths, with

. Now consulting the root data of finite root systems (see
[

), we conclude that
\bar{R}_{\mathcal{P}}=\bar{R}
.

(iii) Since

and

R^{0}_{\mathcal{P}}=(R^{n}_{\mathcal{P}}-R^{n}_{\mathcal{P}})\cap R^{0}=(R^{\times}-R^{\times})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{0}=R^{0}
, we are done.
∎

3.2.
The finite case

We show that
\mathcal{M}_{r}=\mathcal{M}_{c}=\mathcal{M}_{m}
if
R
is finite.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
R
be a reduced irreducible finite root system of rank
\ell
;
then
c=\ell
.

Proof.

Since the rank of a finite root system is equal to the minimal number of generators for the corresponding Weyl group, we get
\ell=c
.

Proposition 3.2.2
.

Let
R
be a reduced irreducible
finite root system, then
\mathcal{M}_{c}=\mathcal{M}_{m}=\mathcal{M}_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a reflectable base for
R
. By Lemma
1.3.1
,

|\mathcal{P}|=\mbox{rank}(R)
. Thus by Lemma
3.2.1
,
\mathcal{M}_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{c}
.
Now let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
; since
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
, we get from Proposition
3.1.6
that
R_{\mathcal{P}}
is a finite subsystem of
R
of the same type and rank of
R
. Thus
R=R_{\mathcal{P}}

and so
\mathcal{P}
is a reflectable set for
R
. This proves that
\mathcal{M}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{r},
and so
\mathcal{M}_{c}=\mathcal{M}_{r}
.

Next, let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
. As we saw in the previous paragraph,

\mathcal{P}
is a reflectable set. If
|\mathcal{P}|>\hbox{rank}\;R=c
, then
\mathcal{P}
contains a proper subset
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
which is a reflectable base, and so
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}={\mathcal{W}}
, contradicting that
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
. Thus

\mathcal{M}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{c},

completing the proof that
\mathcal{M}_{c}=\mathcal{M}_{m}=\mathcal{M}_{r}.
∎

The following example shows that Proposition
3.2.2
may fail if the extended affine root system
R
is not finite.

Example 3.2.3
.

Consider the extended affine root system
R=\Lambda\cup(\pm\epsilon+\Lambda)
of type
A_{1}
, where
\{0,\pm\epsilon\}
is a finite root system of type
A_{1}
and
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{3}
.
Set

\mathcal{P}:=\{\epsilon,\sigma_{1}-\epsilon,\sigma_{2}-\epsilon,\sigma_{3}-\epsilon,\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}-\epsilon,\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon,\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon,\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon\}.

By Proposition
4.2.1
(i),
\mathcal{P}
is a reflectable base for
R
, so
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
. A simple computation shows that

w_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon}=w_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon}w_{\sigma_{1}-\epsilon}w_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon}w_{\epsilon}w_{\sigma_{3}-\epsilon}w_{\epsilon}w_{\sigma_{2}-\epsilon}w_{\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon}w_{\epsilon},

so
w_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon}\in\langle w_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{w_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}-\epsilon}\}\rangle
; thus
\mathcal{P}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
and then by (
1
),
\mathcal{P}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
. That is
\mathcal{P}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{rc}
.

4.
Reflectable bases, the class
\mathcal{M}_{r}

In this section, we consider various aspects of reflectable bases; in particular, we investigate their existences and their possible cardinalities.

4.1.
Characterization theorem for reflectable bases

We recall two recognition theorems for reflectable bases associated with reduced extended affine root systems given in

[

.

(Recognition Theorem for simply laced types) Let

(ii)

\langle R\rangle

>1

Theorem 4.1.2
.

(Recognition Theorem for non-simply laced types) Let

be non-simply laced of type

is a reflectable base for

,

(ii)
R_{sh}=\uplus_{\alpha\in\Pi_{sh}}\big((\alpha+\langle R_{lg}\rangle)\cap R_{sh}\big)
, and

is a minimal set with respect to the property that

-vector space
\langle R_{lg}\rangle/2\langle R_{sh}\rangle

(iii)
R_{lg}=\uplus_{\alpha\in\Pi_{lg}}\big((\alpha+2\langle R_{sh}\rangle)\cap R_{lg}\big)
, and

is minimal with respect to the property that the set

\langle R_{sh}\rangle/\langle R_{lg}\rangle,

-vector space

.

Let

Example 4.4.1
.

Let
R
be a simply laced extended affine root system of type
X
and rank
>1
. Then

, where
\dot{R}
is a finite root system of type

and

Set

We claim that

is a reflectable base for

(ii), it is enough to show that for each

5.
A characterization of minimal root systems

As in the previous sections, we let
R
be an extended affine root system of reduced type
X
with corresponding Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
. It is known that
{\mathcal{W}}
has the presentation by conjugation, see Definition
2.2.4
, if and only if
R
is ”minimal” in the sense of Definition
5.1.1
below; therefore minimality condition reflects the geometric aspects of extended affine root systems. In this section, we characterize minimal root systems in terms of minimal reflectable bases.

5.1.
Minimal extended affine root systems

Definition 5.1.1
.

Following
[
Ho
]
, we say the extended affine root system
R
is
minimal
, if for each
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,

{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}\subsetneq{\mathcal{W}}
, where
\mathcal{P}=R^{\times}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\alpha
.

Lemma 5.1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq R^{\times}
.

(i)
{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
if and only if
S_{\mathcal{P}}=S_{{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}=-\mathcal{P}
.

(ii) The extended affine root system
R
is minimal if and only if whenever
\mathcal{P}\subseteq R^{\times}
with

{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{W}}
and
{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
, then
\mathcal{P}=R^{\times}
.

Proof.

(i) The “if” part is clear.
Now assume that
S_{\mathcal{P}}=S_{{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}=-\mathcal{P}
. Then for
w^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}
we have
w_{w^{\prime}(\alpha)}\in S_{\mathcal{P}}
; thus there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{P}
such that
w_{w^{\prime}(\alpha)}=w_{\beta}
and then we have
w^{\prime}(\alpha)=\pm\beta
. Now as
\mathcal{P}=-\mathcal{P}
, we have
w^{\prime}(\alpha)\in\mathcal{P}
and therefore
{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
.

(ii) Assume first that
R
is minimal and
\mathcal{P}
is a subset of
R^{\times}
such that
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
. We must show
\mathcal{P}=R^{\times}.
If not, we pick
\alpha\in R^{\times}\setminus\mathcal{P}
. Since
{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
, we get
\mathcal{P}\subset R^{\times}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\alpha
. But now the assumption
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
contradicts minimality of
R
.

Next assume that the “only if” part holds, and that
R
is not minimal. So there exists
\alpha\in R^{\times}
such that
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{W}}
for
\mathcal{P}:=R^{\times}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\alpha
. Now since
{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
, we get by the assumption that
R^{\times}=\mathcal{P}
, which is absurd.
∎

Theorem 5.1.3
.

Let
R
be a reduced extended affine root system of type
X
and
\mathcal{P}(X)
be as in Table
1
. Then
\mathcal{P}(X)\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
if and only if
R
is a minimal root system.

Proof.

First, suppose that
R
is of type
A_{1}
or one of the non-simply laced types; then by
[
AS3
, Theorem 5.16]
and
[
AS5
, Theorem 2.33]
,
\mathcal{P}(X)\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
if and only if
R
is a minimal root system. Next, assume that
R
is simply laced of rank
>1
and
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
. If
R
is not minimal, then by
[
Ho
, Theorem 4.5]
,
{\mathcal{W}}
does not have the presentation by conjugation, contradicting Theorem
2.2.3
(iii).

Conversely, assume that
R
is a minimal root system. As
|\mathcal{P}(X)|=\ell+\nu=\mbox{dim}({\mathcal{V}})
, we have
\mathcal{P}(X)\in\mathcal{M}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
.∎

5.2.
Minimality and presentation by conjugation

Let
R
be a reduced extended affine root system with Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
be the group defined by
generators
\widehat{w}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and relations

-
\widehat{w}^{2}=1
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,

-
\widehat{w}_{\alpha}\widehat{w}_{\beta}\widehat{w}_{\alpha}=\widehat{w}_{w_{\alpha}\beta}
,
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
.

We recall from Definition
2.2.4
that if
{\mathcal{W}}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
, then
{\mathcal{W}}
is said to have the presentation by conjugation. We refer to this group as the
conjugation presented group
, associated with
R
.

Proposition 5.2.1
.

Given any reflectable base
\mathcal{P}
for
R
,
the set
\{\widehat{w}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}
is a minimal generating set for the conjugation presented group
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
associated to
R
.

Proof.

If
\nu=0
then
R
is a finite root system and so
{\mathcal{W}}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
. Then we are done by Theorem
3.2.2
.

Assume next that
\nu\geq 1
. If
R
is simply laced of rank
>1
or has one of types
F_{4}
or
G_{2}
, then by Lemma
4.3.3
, Propositions
4.3.1
and
4.4.2
,
\{w_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}
is a minimal generating set for
{\mathcal{W}}
. But by Theorem
2.2.3
, for the types under consideration
{\mathcal{W}}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
and so we are done.

Next, suppose that
R
is of type
A_{1}
; then by Theorem
4.1.1
(i), we have

R^{\times}=\uplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}}(\alpha+2\langle R\rangle)\cap R^{\times}.

(1)

On the other hand, since
R^{\times}=\cup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{W}}\alpha
, and for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
we have
{\mathcal{W}}\alpha\subseteq\alpha+2\langle R\rangle
, we get from
(

),

R^{\times}=\uplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{W}}\alpha.

(2)

If
\{\widehat{w}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}
is not a minimal generating set for
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}
; then there is at least one

\beta\in\mathcal{P}
such that
\widehat{w}_{\beta}=\widehat{w}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\widehat{w}_{\gamma_{n}}
for some
\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{\beta\}
,

. By Corollary
2.2.6
, the assignment

w_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{\beta}}}{{\longmapsto}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if}~\alpha\in{\mathcal{W}}\beta,\\
0&\mbox{if}~\alpha\in R^{\times}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\beta,\end{array}\right.

induces an epimorphism
\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}:{\mathcal{W}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
Now, by (

\mathcal{P}\setminus\{\beta\}\subseteq R^{\times}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\beta
, and so we get
{\Phi}_{\beta}(\widehat{w}_{\beta})=1
and

{\Phi}_{\beta}(\widehat{w}_{\gamma_{1}}\ldots\widehat{w}_{\gamma_{n}})=0

which is a contradiction.

Next, we consider type
B_{2}
. In this case, it is easy to see that

{\mathcal{W}}\alpha\subseteq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha+\langle R_{lg}\rangle&\hbox{if }\alpha\in R_{sh},\\
\alpha+2\langle R_{sh}\rangle&\hbox{if }\alpha\in R_{lg}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(3)

By Theorem
4.1.1
(iii), we have

R_{sh}=\uplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{sh}}\big((\alpha+\langle R_{lg}\rangle)\cap R_{sh}\big)\quad\hbox{and}\quad R_{lg}=\uplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{lg}}\big((\alpha+2\langle R_{sh}\rangle)\cap R_{lg}\big).

Using these equations together with (

R^{\times}=\cup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{W}}\alpha
is disjoint. Then using the same argument as in type
A_{1}
, we achieve the desired result.

Finally, suppose that
R
is of type
B_{\ell}
,
(\ell>2)
, and assume to the contrary that
\{\widehat{w}_{\alpha}|\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\}
is not a minimal generating set for
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}
; then there is at least one
\beta\in\mathcal{P}
such that
\widehat{w}_{\beta}\in\langle\widehat{w}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{\beta\}\rangle
. We proceed with the proof by considering the following two cases.

\beta\in R_{sh}
: Similar to the case
B_{2}
, using Theorem
4.1.1
(iv), we see that
R_{sh}=\uplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{sh}}{\mathcal{W}}\alpha
, then
\mathcal{P}\setminus\{\beta\}\subseteq R^{\times}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}\beta
. Therefore repeating the same argument as in type

leads to a contradiction.

\beta\in R_{lg}
: We have
\widehat{w}_{\beta}=\widehat{w}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\widehat{w}_{\gamma_{n}}
for some
\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{\beta\}
. Applying the epimorphism
\psi
given in
2.2.5
(

)), we get
w_{\beta}=w_{\gamma_{1}}\ldots w_{\gamma_{n}}
. Since

, there exists
\alpha\in R_{lg}
such that
(\alpha,\beta^{\vee})=-1
. If
\{\gamma_{i_{1}},\ldots\gamma_{i_{m}}\}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}\cap R_{lg}
then we have

thus

(4)

which is a contradiction as by Theorem

(iv),

is minimal with respect to the property that the set

As usual, the argument for the case

is analogous to

has the presentation by conjugation. But this is immediate from Theorem
2.2.3
(ii-(iii).

Next, we assume
\hbox{ind}(R)=0
where

X=A_{1}
,
B_{\ell}
,
C_{\ell}
. By
3.1.4
(
1
), it is enough to show that
\mathcal{M}_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{rc}
. Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
.
By Proposition
4.3.1
, we have
|\mathcal{P}|=\ell+\nu
. Now as
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{P}}
, we get
{\mathcal{V}}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathcal{P}
, and since by
3.1.4
(
2
),
\dim({\mathcal{V}})\leq c\leq|\mathcal{P}|
, we get
c=\ell+\nu
. Thus
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{rc}
. ∎

6.
Relations to extended affine Lie algebras

We begin with a brief introduction to the definition of an extended affine Lie algebra and some of its basic properties.
For more details on the subject and further terminologies We refer the reader to
[
AABGP
, Chapter I]
.

6.1.
Extended affine Lie algebras

Definition 6.1.1
.

A triple (
\mathcal{L},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathcal{H})
in which
\mathcal{L}
is complex Lie algebra,
\mathcal{H}
is a subalgebra and
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a bilinear form on
\mathcal{L}
is called an
extended affine Lie algebra
if the following 5 axioms hold:

(E1) The form
(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, invariant, and non-degenerate.

(E2)
\mathcal{H}
is non-trivial, finite-dimensional, and self-centralizing such that

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\alpha\in R}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}

where

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)(x)\hbox{ for all }h\in\mathcal{H}\}

and

R=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\star}\mid\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\not=\{0\}\}.

R
is called the
root system
of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
. The form on
\mathcal{L}
restricted to
\mathcal{H}
is non-degenerate so
the form can be transferred to
\mathcal{H}^{\star}
in a natural way.
We denote by
t_{\alpha}
the unique element in
\mathcal{H}
which represents
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\star}
via the form, namely

\alpha(h)=(t_{\alpha},h),\qquad(h\in\mathcal{H}).

Set

R^{0}:=\{\alpha\in R\mid(\alpha,\alpha)=0\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad R^{\times}:=R\setminus R^{0}.

(E4)
\hbox{ad}(x)
is locally nilpotent for
x\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.

(E5)
R
is irreducible, meaning that
R^{\times}
is connected, and
for
\sigma\in R^{0}
, there exists
\alpha\in R^{\times}
with
\alpha+\sigma\in R
.

6.1.2
.

We assume from now on that
(\mathcal{L},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathcal{H})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. One knows that
R
is an extended affine root system in
{\mathcal{V}}:=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{R}}R
, all non-isotropic root spaces are
1
-dimensional, and that for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, one may choose
e_{\pm\alpha}\in\mathcal{L}_{\pm\alpha}
such that
(e_{\alpha},h_{\alpha}:=[e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha}],e_{-\alpha})
is an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple.
From now on,
we assume that
R
is reduced
.
For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
x_{\pm\alpha}\in\mathcal{L}_{\pm\alpha}
, we define

\Phi_{\alpha}\in\hbox{Aut}(\mathcal{L})
by

\Phi_{\alpha}:=\exp(\hbox{ad}x_{\alpha})\exp(-\hbox{ad}x_{-\alpha})\exp(\hbox{ad}x_{\alpha}).

Then
\Phi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{L}_{\beta})=\mathcal{L}_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)}
for
\beta\in R
, see
[
AABGP
, Proposition I.1.27]
.
As in Definition
2.1.2
, we denote by
{\mathcal{W}}
the extended affine Weyl group of
R
. We also denote by
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{L}}
the Weyl group of
\mathcal{L}
, the subgroup of
GL(\mathcal{H}^{\star})
generated by reflections
\beta\mapsto\beta-(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.
Then
\Phi_{\alpha}
restricted to
\mathcal{H}\equiv\mathcal{H}^{\star}
coincides with the reflection
w_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{W}}
, identifying
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{L}}
with the extended affine Weyl group
{\mathcal{W}}
of
R
.

We recall that the core
\mathcal{L}_{c}
of
\mathcal{L}
is the subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}
generated by the non-isotropic root spaces
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.

6.2.
Reflectable bases and the core

We begin with a lemma that shows, using the concept of a reflectable set, that the root spaces associated with a reflectable set (up to plus-minus sign) generate the core, implying that the core of an extended affine Lie algebra is finitely generated (compare with
[
Neh
, Section 6.12]
and
[
A4
, Corollary 2.14]
). We start with terminology.

Definition 6.2.1
.

We call a subset
\mathcal{P}
of
R^{\times}
, a
root-generating set
for
\mathcal{L}
if the root spaces
\mathcal{L}_{\pm\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}
generate
\mathcal{L}_{c}
.
We call a root-generating set
minimal
if no proper subset of
\mathcal{P}
is a root-generating set.

Lemma 6.2.2
.

Any reflectable set for
R
is a root-generating set for
\mathcal{L}
. In particular,
\mathcal{L}_{c}
is finitely generated.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a reflectable set and
\alpha\in R^{\times}.
Then
\alpha=w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{t}}(\alpha_{t+1})
, for some
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
. Therefore,

\Phi_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\Phi_{\alpha_{t}}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{t+1}})=\mathcal{L}_{w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{t}}(\alpha_{t+1})}=\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}.

Thus
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
contains in the subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}
generated by
\mathcal{L}_{\beta}
,
\beta\in\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
. The second statement now follows from Corollary
4.2.2
.∎

Lemma 6.2.3
.

Let
R
be a simply laced extended affine root system of type
X
and rank
>1
. Let
\mathcal{P}

be a reflectable base for
R
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is a minimal root-generating set for
\mathcal{L}
. In particular the set
\mathcal{P}(X)
given in Table
1
is a minimal root-generating set for

\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
\mathcal{P}
contains a proper root-generating set
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
If
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
is generated by some root spaces

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_{t}}
for some
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
Thus
\alpha\in\langle\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rangle
, and so
\langle\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{P}\rangle=\langle R\rangle.

Then by Theorem 3.2 of
[
ASTY1
]
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a reflectable set for
R
. But this contradicts the minimality of
\mathcal{P}
as a reflectable base.
The second assertion in the statement follows from Proposition
4.2.1
.∎

Lemma 6.2.4
.

If
\hbox{ind}(R)=0
, then any reflectable base
\mathcal{P}
is a minimal root-generating set for
\mathcal{L}
. In particular, if
R
is simply laced of rank
>1
, or is of type
F_{4}
or
G_{2}
, then any reflectable base is a minimal root-generating set for
\mathcal{L}
.∎

Proof.

If
R
is simply laced of rank
>1
, then we are done by Lemma
6.2.3
. For the remaining types, we have from Proposition
4.3.1
that all reflectable bases have the same cardinality. Now if
\hbox{ind}(R)=0
then we see from
1.3.5
that
|\mathcal{P}|=|\mathcal{P}(X)|=\ell+\nu
which is equal to the rank of the free abelian group
\langle R\rangle
. Since the roots corresponding to any generating set of root vectors for
\mathcal{L}_{c}
must generate
\langle R\rangle
, the first assertion in the statement holds. The second assertion is now clear as
\hbox{ind}(R)=0
for the given types.∎

The following example shows that if
\hbox{ind}(R)\not=0
then Lemma
6.2.4
may fail. Also, it shows that an extended affine Lie algebra might have minimal root-generating sets with different cardinalities.

Example 6.2.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
.

(i) Let
R
be of type
A_{1}
and nullity
2
with
\hbox{ind}(R)>0
. We know that (see
1.3.5
) up to isomorphism

R=\Lambda+(\pm\alpha+\Lambda)
where
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{2}
.
We consider the reflectable base

\mathcal{P}(X)=\{\alpha,\sigma_{1}-\alpha,\sigma_{2}-\alpha,\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}-\alpha\}
for
R
, see Table
1
.
From
[
A5
, Remark 1.5(ii)]
, we know that

[[\mathcal{L}_{\sigma_{1}-\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}],\mathcal{L}_{\sigma_{2}-\alpha}]]\not=\{0\}.

Thus the
1
-dimensional space
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}-\alpha}
is generated by root spaces corresponding to roots
\{\alpha,\sigma_{1}-\alpha,\sigma_{2}-\alpha\}
.
Therefore by Lemma
6.2.2
,
\mathcal{L}_{c}
is generated by root spaces

\mathcal{L}_{\pm\beta}
,
\beta\in\{\alpha,\sigma_{1}-\alpha,\sigma_{2}-\alpha\}
. Thus
\mathcal{P}(X)
is not a minimal root-generating set for
\mathcal{L}_{c}
.

(ii) Let
R
be of type
A_{2}
. Then up to isomorphism

R=\Lambda\cup(\dot{R}+\Lambda)
where
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{2}
and
\dot{R}
is a finite root system of type
A_{2}
with a base
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
.
Then
\mathcal{P}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\sigma_{1},\alpha_{2}+\sigma_{2}\}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+2\sigma_{1},\alpha_{2}+3\sigma_{2},\alpha_{2}+\sigma_{2}\}
are reflectable bases for
R
, see Example
4.4.1
. By Lemma
6.2.4
both
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
are minimal root-generating sets with
|\mathcal{P}|\not=|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|
for the corresponding Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
.

6.3.
Elliptic Lie algebras versus
2
-extended affine Lie algebras

We begin with a definition.
By a
2
-extended affine Lie algebra (root system)
we mean an extended affine Lie algebra (root system) of nullity
2
. Throughout this section,
R
is a reduced
2
-extended affine root system.

Definition 6.3.1
.

(i) In the literature, a
2
-extended affine root system is referred to as an
elliptic root system
.

(ii)
Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra containing a subalgebra
\mathcal{H}
with a decomposition
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\star}}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
, where

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in\mathcal{H}\}.

The set
R:=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\star}\mid\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\not=\{0\}\}
is called
the
root system
of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
. We call
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{H})
, or simply
\mathcal{L}
an
elliptic Lie algebra
if
R
is an elliptic root system, with respect to a symmetric positive semi-definite form on the real span of
R
.

Corollary 6.3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
2
-extended affine Lie algebra of type
X
, rank
\ell
and twist number
t
. Let
S
be as in
1.3.3
. Suppose either of the following holds:

-
X
is simply laced of rank
>1
,

-
X=A_{1}
and
\hbox{ind}(S)=2
,

-
X=F_{4},\;G_{2}
,

-
X=B_{2}
with
t=1
; or
t=0,2
and
\hbox{ind}(S)=2
,

-
X=B_{\ell}
(
\ell>2
) with
t=0,1
; or
t=2
and
\hbox{ind}(S)=2
,

-
X=C_{\ell}
with
t=1,2
; or
t=0
and
\hbox{ind}(S)=2
.

Then any reflectable base is a minimal root-generating set for
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

For all the types given in the statement we have
\hbox{ind}(R)=0
and so by Lemma
6.2.4
we are done.∎

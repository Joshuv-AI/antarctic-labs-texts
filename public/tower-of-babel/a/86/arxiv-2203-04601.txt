A Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity bound for threefolds with rational singularities Thanks: J. Park was supported by the National Research Foundation (NRF) funded by the Korea government (MSIT) (NRF-2021R1C1C1005479).
Wenbo Niu, Jinhyung Park

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to establish a Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity bound for threefolds with mild singularities. Let X X be a non-degenerate normal projective threefold in ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} of degree d d and codimension e e . We prove that if X X has rational singularities, then reg ⁡ ( X ) ≤ d − e + 2 \operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+2 . Our bound is very close to a sharp bound conjectured by Eisenbud-Goto. When e = 2 e=2 and X X has Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities, we obtain the conjectured bound reg ⁡ ( X ) ≤ d − 1 \operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-1 , and we also classify the extremal cases. To achieve these results, we bound the regularity of fibers of a generic projection of X X by using Loewy length, and also bound the dimension of the varieties swept out by secant lines through the singular locus of X X .

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers.
Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate projective variety of degree
d
and codimension
e
.
We say that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is
m
-regular
in the sense of Castelnuovo-Mumford if
H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\mathcal{I}_{X|\mathbb{P}^{r}}(m-i))=0
for all
i>0
. It is equivalent to saying that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is
(m-1)
-normal and
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is
(m-1)
-regular, i.e., the natural restriction map
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(m-1))\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(m-1))
is surjective and
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(m-1-i))=0
for
i>0
. The minimal such number
m
is called the
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
of
X
and is denoted by
\operatorname{reg}(X)
. For more details, we refer to
[
L2
, Section 1.8]
.

One of the fundamental problems in projective algebraic geometry is to find a bound for
\operatorname{reg}(X)
in terms of algebraic and geometric data such as the degree
d
and the codimension
e
. A sharp bound was suggested by Eisenbud-Goto
[
EG
]
(cf.
[
GLP
]
) in the
regularity conjecture
, which says that

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+1.

There is also a folklore conjecture which predicts that the maximal degree, denoted by
\max\deg(X)
, of minimal generators of the defining ideal
I_{X|\mathbb{P}^{r}}
of
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is at most
d
. Since
\max\deg(X)\leq\operatorname{reg}(X)
, the regularity conjecture implies this folklore conjecture. The regularity conjecture has only been proved for a few cases: integral curves
[
C
]
,
[
GLP
]
, connected curves
[
G
]
, smooth surfaces
[
P
]
,
[
L1
]
, singular surfaces with rational or Gorenstein elliptic singularities
[
Ni
]
, and smooth threefold in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
with
r\geq 9

[
R1
]
. Slightly weaker bounds for lower dimensional smooth varieties were obtained in
[
Kw1
,
Kw3
]
. A sharp bound for
\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
was shown in
[
KP
]
,
[
No
]
for a smooth variety
X
of arbitrary dimension. This result is generalized in
[
MPS
]
to a variety
X
with
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein isolated singularities.

It turns out that the regularity conjecture is not true. McCullough-Peeva
[
MP
]
constructed counterexamples to the regularity conjecture, but their examples are highly singular. It is then a natural problem to make a distinction between mildly singular varieties satisfying the regularity conjecture and highly singular varieties not satisfying the regularity conjecture. The threefold case is particularly interesting because one of the counterexamples to the regularity conjecture is a projective threefold
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
such that
\dim X_{\operatorname{sing}}=1,\deg(X)=31,
and
\max\deg(X)=\operatorname{reg}(X)=38
(
[
MP
, Example 4.7]
) while the regularity conjecture holds for a smooth projective threefold in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
by
[
Kw2
]
. Notice also that the singular threefold
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
does not even fulfill the folklore conjecture aforementioned.

In this paper, we consider normal projective threefolds with rational or Du Bois singularities. Those singularities are naturally appearing inevitably in many places such as minimal model program, moduli theory, and Hodge theory. Recently, it was shown that the secant varieties of smooth projective curves have normal Du Bois singularities (see
[
ENP
]
). In a variety of flavor, it is highly desirable to have the conjectured bound or a slightly weaker bound for
\operatorname{reg}(X)
, where
X
is a normal projective threefold with rational or Du Bois singularities. It is also interesting to see how local geometric properties of those singularities are related to global algebraic properties of the embedded projective varieties.

The first main result of this paper is to establish the following regularity bound for threefolds with rational singularities, extending Kwak’s result for smooth threefolds in
[
Kw1
]
. Our bound is only off by 1 from the bound in the regularity conjecture, and in particular, the maximal degree can be bounded by the degree.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate normal projective threefold of degree
d
and codimension

. Suppose that

has rational singularities. Then we have

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+2.

Suppose furthermore that
X
has only isolated rational singularities and

(
\star
)

\dim\bigcup_{l\in\Sigma}l\leq 4,\text{ where }\Sigma:=\{\text{lines }l\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}\mid\operatorname{length}(l\cap X)\geq 4\text{ and }l\cap X_{\operatorname{sing}}=\emptyset\}.

In this case, our approach yields the optimal result

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+1.

See Remark
5.2
for more details.
Note that Ran
[
R1
, Theorem 1]
proved that if

and

has non-degenerate tangent variety, then the condition (

[

The codimension two case is specially important as one of the counterexamples to regularity conjecture by McCullough-Peeva appeared in this case. The second main result of this paper is to establish a sharp regularity bound for codimension two normal projective threefolds with Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities and to classify the extremal cases. This generalizes the main theorem of
[
Kw2
]
, which deals with the smooth case. Note that rational singularities are Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a non-degenerate normal projective threefold of degree
d
. Suppose that
X
has Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities. Then we have

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-1.

Furthermore,
\operatorname{reg}(X)=d-1
if and only if

is either a variety of minimal degree or a complete intersection of type

.

If

is a non-degenerate normal projective threefolds with Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities of degree

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+3+{e+1\choose 3}.

To prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we take a
general projection

\pi\colon X\to\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}

of a non-degenerate normal projective threefold

in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
to a hypersurface
\overline{X}
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
, and analyze the complexity of the fibers of
\pi
. It is known that if one has a smaller Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity bound for all fibers of
\pi
, then one would get a better Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity bound for
X
. See Section
5
for more details. This idea essentially goes back to Castelnuovo
[
C
]
(see also
[
P
]
), and is combined with cohomological method by Lazarsfeld
[
L1
]
to show a sharp regularity bound for smooth surfaces. This technique has been successfully applied to lower dimensional smooth varieties as in
[
Kw1
,
Kw2
,
Kw3
]
,
[
R2
]
and singular surfaces as in
[
Ni
]
.

Along this line, our task is to find a reasonably small uniform regularity bound for every fiber
X_{y}=\pi^{-1}(y)
of general projection
\pi\colon X\to\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
with
y\in\overline{X}
(Theorem
4.3
). For this purpose, we essentially classify the fibers of
\pi
(see Remark
4.4
). The situation is well understood if
X
is nonsingular; every
X_{y}
has length at most
4
, and there are only finitely many
y
with
X_{y}
collinear of length
4
(see
[
Kw1
]
).
However, if
X
is singular, then the situation becomes more complicated and new ideas are essentially needed. When
X_{y}
is supported in the smooth locus of
X
, we can control the length of
X_{y}
and consequently the regularity of
X_{y}
by dimension counting arguments and the (dimension+2)-secant lemma
[
R2
, Theorem 1]
(Proposition
4.1
). When
X_{y}
contains a singular point of
X
, the situation becomes quite delicate. It is worth pointing out that the length of
X_{y}
is not useful anymore to bound its regularity at least in our case. To remedy this situation, we use
Loewy length
instead to bound the regularity of fibers.
Since
X
has rational or Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities, we can apply
Briançon-Skoda type theorem
to bound the Loewy length (see Lemma
3.6
and Proposition
3.9
). This is enough to establish the regularity bound for
X
if
X
has only isolated singularities.
However, a normal projective threefold with rational or Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities could have positive dimensional singular locus, and this is actually the major obstruction of bounding regularity of
X
.
The main novelty of this paper is to overcome the difficulty by computing the dimension of the
secant variety
swept out by secant lines passing through the singular locus of
X
(see Theorem
1.3
). This implies that the support of
X_{y}
has at most two points (Proposition
4.2
), and this result leads us to the desired control on the regularity of the fiber
X_{y}
. See Section
4
for more details.

The following theorem describes the secant variety swept out by secant lines passing through the singular locus for an arbitrary projective variety. It might be of independent interest, and can be viewed as complementary to the results on secant variety of smooth locus (see
[
R2
, Theorem 1]
,
[
BDELU
, Theorem A.3]
). We expect that it would have other applications to general projections of higher dimensional singular varieties.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a projective variety of dimension
n
, and let
Z
be a nonempty proper closed subset containing
X_{\operatorname{sing}}
. Denote
\delta:=\operatorname{codim}_{X}Z
and
U_{Z}:=X-Z
. Consider the set of secant lines

\Sigma:=\{\text{lines }l\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}\mid\operatorname{length}(l\cap U_{Z})\geq n+1-\delta\text{ and }l\cap Z\neq\emptyset\}.

Then we have

\dim\overline{S_{\Sigma}(X)}\leq n+1,\text{ where }S_{\Sigma}(X):=\bigcup_{l\in\Sigma}l.

It is worth noting that Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
show a distinction between normal projective threefolds with rational or Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities and highly singular projective threefolds.
There are two geometric reasons for this phenomenon. First, normal projective threefolds with rational or Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities satisfy
Kodaira vanishing theorem
, so the cohomological method can be applied. Second, all fibers of generic projections of these varieties have reasonably small Loewy length, so the general projection method works. There do exist normal projective threefolds with rational or Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities having one dimensional singular locus. The first secant variety
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{1}(C,L)
of a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g
embedded by the complete linear system of a line bundle
L
of degree
\geq 2g+3
has rational singularities when
g=0
and has normal Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities when
g\geq 1
, and the singular locus of
\Sigma_{1}
is
C
(see
[
ENP
]
). If
(g,\deg L)=(0,5),(1,6),(2,7)
, then
\Sigma_{1}
has codimension two. In this case,
\deg(\Sigma_{1})=6,9,13
and
\operatorname{reg}(\Sigma_{1})=3,5,5
, respectively (cf. counterexample of threefold in codimension two
[
MP
, Example 4.7]
).

The paper is organized as follows. We begin in Section
2
with the proof of Theorem
1.3
. In Section
3
, we study local properties of mild singularities using Loewy length.
Section
4
is devoted to the study of complexity of fibers of a generic projection. Finally, in Section
5
, by the classical generic projection method, we complete the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

3.
Loewy Length and Mild Singularities

In this section, we study local properties of mild singularities. Especially, we bound the regularity of the intersection of the singularities with general linear spaces by using Loewy length (see Proposition
3.9
).

Definition 3.1
.

For an Artinian local ring
(A,\mathfrak{m})
, we define the
Loewy length

ll(A)
of
A
to be the nonnegative number
ll(A):=\max\{i\mid\mathfrak{m}^{i}\neq 0\}
. If
\mathfrak{m}=0
, i.e.,
A
is a field, then we put
ll(A)=0
.

The Loewy length of an Artinian local ring is usually much smaller than its length. It provides an efficient way to bound the regularity of zero dimensional schemes, as shown in the following.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Ni
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a zero dimensional subscheme supported at distinct closed points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}
. For each
1\leq i\leq t
, set
\mu_{i}:=ll({\mathcal{O}_{X,p_{i}}})
to be the Loewy length of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,p_{i}}
. Then
X
is
(\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{t}+t)
-regular.

To bound the Loewy length, we need to consider a reduction of an ideal.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local Noetherian ring. An ideal
J\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
is called a
reduction
of
\mathfrak{m}
if
\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}=J\mathfrak{m}^{k}
for some integer
k\geq 0
. Moreover,
J
is called a
minimal reduction
if it is a reduction minimal with respect to inclusion.

As the setting in the definition, if
J
is a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
, then
J
always contains a minimal reduction (
[
HS
, Theorem 8.3.5]
). Furthermore, if
\dim R=n
and
R/\mathfrak{m}=\mathbb{C}
, then there exists a nonempty Zariski open subset
U
in the
n
-th Cartesian product
(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})^{n}
of the cotangent space such that if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{m}
with
(x_{1}+\mathfrak{m}^{2},\ldots,x_{n}+\mathfrak{m}^{2})\in U
, then
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
(
[
HS
, Theorem 8.6.6]
).

Turning to geometric setting, let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a projective variety of dimension
n
, and
p\in X
be a point. Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be an
(r-k)
-dimensional linear subspace passing through

, and assume that

is cut out by linear forms

\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Locally at the point
p
, each form
l_{i}
gives an element
\bar{l}_{i}
of
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
via the quotient
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{P}^{r},p}/I_{X,p}
. Thus we obtain an ideal
(\bar{\l}_{1},\ldots,\bar{l}_{k})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
generated by the elements
\bar{l}_{i}
. This ideal is the localization of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{L}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}
at
p
. We say that
L
is a
reduction linear subspace at
(X,p)
or simply
L
is a
reduction at
(X,p)
if the ideal
(\bar{l}_{1},\ldots,\bar{l}_{k})
is a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
.
If
L
is reduction at
(X,p)
, then the intersection
X\cap L
has dimension zero at
p
and
k\geq n
. For two linear subspaces
L,L^{\prime}
with
p\in L^{\prime}\subseteq L
, if
L
is a reduction at
(X,p)
, then so is
L^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a projective variety of dimension
n
, and
p\in X
be a point. Suppose that
m
is a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}^{m}\subseteq J
for any minimal reduction
J
of
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
. If
L
is a reduction linear subspace at
(X,p)
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, then we have
ll({\mathcal{O}_{X\cap L,p}})\leq m-1
.

Proof.

Let
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}
be linear forms on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
cutting out
L
. Locally at the point
p
, these linear forms generate an ideal
(\bar{\l}_{1},\ldots,\bar{l}_{k})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
which is a reduction ideal of
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
, and hence,
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}^{m}\subseteq(\bar{l}_{1},\ldots,\bar{l}_{k})
since the latter contains a minimal reduction. As
\mathcal{O}_{X\cap L,p}=\mathcal{O}_{X,p}/(\bar{l}_{1},\ldots,\bar{l}_{k})
, we see immediately that
\mathfrak{m}_{X\cap L,p}^{m}=0
as desired.
∎

To apply the above lemma, we need to bound the number
m
. This number turns out to depend on the singularities of the local ring. Let us recall definitions of some mild singularities that provide us reasonable bounds on
m
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a projective variety.

(1)

We say that
X
has
rational singularities
if
X
is normal and
there exists a proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
from a smooth variety
Y
such that
R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=0
for
i>0
. Note that
R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=0
for
i>0
if and only if
X
is locally Cohen-Macaulay and
f_{*}\omega_{Y}=\omega_{X}
(
[
KM
, Theorem 5.10]
).

(2)

Let
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{\bullet}
be the Deligne-Du Bois complex for
X
, which is a generalization of the de Rham complex for a nonsingular variety (see
[
Ko
, Chapter 6]
for detail). We say that
X
has
Du Bois singularities
if the natural map

\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}=Gr_{\text{filt}}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{\bullet}.

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Rational singularities contain a large range of important singularities considered in birational geometry such as terminal, canonical, or log terminal singularities (
[
KM
, Theorem 5.22]
). Du Bois singularities are another important singularities naturally appeared in birational geometry and Hodge theory.
Note that if
X
has rational singularities or log canonical singularities, then
X
has Du Bois singularities (
[
Ko
, Corollary 6.23 and Corollary 6.32]
).
For further information about rational or Du Bois singularities and their relation with birational geometry, we refer to
[
Ko
]
and
[
KM
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a normal projective threefold,
p\in X
be a point, and
L
be a reduction linear subspace at
(X,p)
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

If
X
has rational singularities, then
ll({\mathcal{O}_{X\cap L,p}})\leq 2
.

(2)

If
X
has Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities, then
ll({\mathcal{O}_{X\cap L,p}})\leq 3
.

Proof.

Let
J
be a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
of the local ring
{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}
at
p
. If
(X,p)
is a
3
-dimensional rational singularity, then it follows from Briançon-Skoda type theorem (
[
LT
]
, see also
[
HW
, Theorem 3.2 (1)]
) that
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}^{3}\subseteq J
. If
(X,p)
is a
3
-dimensional normal Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularity, then
[
S
, Lemma 3.5]
implies that
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}^{4}\subseteq J
. Thus the assertions follow from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

For a surface
S
with rational singularities or Gorenstein elliptic singularities and for a closed point
p\in S
, it has been showed in
[
Ni
]
that the inequality
\operatorname{mult}_{p}S\leq\operatorname{embdim}_{p}S
plays a crucial role in bounding the Loewy length of fibers of a general projection of
S
. Ideally, following the method used there, if one could show
\operatorname{mult}_{p}X\leq\operatorname{embdim}_{p}X-1
for a threefold
X
, then Lemma
3.6
(1) can be established. However, this inequality is only known for terminal singularities by
[
Ka
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also
[
TW
, Proposition 3.10]
) and for Gorenstein canonical singularities by
[
HW
, Theorem 5.1]
. In general, if
(X,p)
is a rational singularity, then we only have
\operatorname{mult}_{p}X\leq{{\operatorname{embdim}_{p}X-1}\choose{\dim X-1}}
which is sharp (see
[
HW
, Theorem 3.1 and Example 4.1]
).

Remark 3.8
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate normal projective surface of degree
d
and codimension
e
. Suppose that
S
has Du Bois singularities.
As in the proof of Lemma
3.6
, by applying
[
S
, Lemma 3.5]
, we can show that the Loewy length of any fiber of a general projection
\pi\colon S\to\overline{S}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
is at most 2. Then the arguments in
[
Ni
, Section 3]
show that the regularity conjecture holds for
S
, i.e.,

\operatorname{reg}(S)\leq d-e+1.

Note that all singularities considered in
[
Ni
]
are Du Bois singularities.

In the rest of the paper, we frequently use the notion of (partial) flag varieties. For given
m
integers
1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{m}\leq r-1
, the
(partial) flag variety

\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{P}^{k_{m}},\mathbb{P}^{r})
parameterizes the
m
-tuples
(L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{m})
of the nested linear subspaces
L_{1}\subseteq L_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq L_{m}
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
with
\dim L_{i}=k_{i}
. For a closed point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{r}
, we also consider the subvariety

\mathbb{F}(p,\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{P}^{k_{m}},\mathbb{P}^{r}):=\{(L_{1},\ldots,L_{m})\in\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{P}^{k_{m}},\mathbb{P}^{r})\mid p\in L_{1}\}

of the flag variety
\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{P}^{k_{m}},\mathbb{P}^{r})
. There is a canonical isomorphism

\mathbb{F}(p,\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{P}^{k_{m}},\mathbb{P}^{r})\simeq\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}-1},\ldots,\mathbb{P}^{k_{m}-1},\mathbb{P}^{r-1}).

In the special case when
m=1
, the flag variety
\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\mathbb{P}^{r})
is just the Grassmannian variety
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\mathbb{P}^{r})
, and therefore,
\mathbb{F}(p,\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}},\mathbb{P}^{r})\simeq\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}-1},\mathbb{P}^{r-1})
.

Now we are ready to prove the following main result of this section. The proposition plays an important role in controlling regularity of fibers of a general projection passing through singular locus. The essential idea of the proof is to use the classical dimension counting method.

4.
Complexity of Fibers of a Generic Projection

This section is devoted to the study of the fibers of generic projections of threefolds. Recall the construction of a generic projection for a variety. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate projective variety of dimension
n
. Take an
(r-n-2)
-dimensional linear subspace
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
disjoint with
X
. Blowing up
\mathbb{P}^{r}
along the center
\Lambda
and then projecting to
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
,
we obtain the diagram

\begin{CD}\Bl_{\Lambda}\mathbb{P}^{r}@>{q}>{}>\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\\
@V{p}V{}V\\
\mathbb{P}^{r}.\end{CD}

The variety
X
is naturally a subvariety of
\Bl_{\Lambda}\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Restricting
q
to
X
yields the morphism
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
which is called a
projection of
X
from the center
\Lambda
. We denote the image of
\pi
as
\overline{X}
which is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
. For
y\in\overline{X}
, denote by
X_{y}:=\pi^{-1}(y)
the fiber of the projection and denote by

\mathbb{P}_{y}^{r-n-1}:=q^{-1}(y)=\langle\Lambda,X_{y}\rangle\simeq\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}

the fiber of
q
which is an
(r-n-1)
-dimensional linear subspace in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Note that scheme-theoretically
X_{y}=X\cap\mathbb{P}_{y}^{r-n-1}
and therefore
X_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{y}^{r-n-1}
is a closed subscheme. As there are flexibility on the choice of the projection center
\Lambda
, we expect the projection would have some good properties. We frequently say that a general projection
\pi
satisfies a property
\mathcal{P}
, which means that there exists an open set
U
in the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{P}^{r-n-2},\mathbb{P}^{r})
such that for
\Lambda\in U
the projection from
\Lambda
satisfies the property
\mathcal{P}
. Of course, such open set
U
depends on what kind of property
\mathcal{P}
is involved, and it should be clear from the context.

The main goal of this section is to understand the complexity of a fiber
X_{y}
of a general projection of a threefold
X
, especially the regularity of
X_{y}
.
We first consider the case that
X_{y}
is supported in the smooth locus
X_{\operatorname{reg}}
of
X
. Recall that a zero-dimensional scheme
X
is called
curvilinear
if each local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[x]/(x^{k})
for some
k\geq 1
. It is clear that a dimension zero scheme is curvilinear if and only if it admits an embedding into a smooth curve.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a projective threefold and
\pi\colon X\to\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a general projection from an
(r-5)
-dimensional linear subspace
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

X_{y}\cap X_{\operatorname{reg}}
is curvilinear for all
y\in\overline{X}
.

(2)

If a fiber
X_{y}
over
y\in\overline{X}
is supported in
X_{\operatorname{reg}}
, then
X_{y}
has length at most

\operatorname{reg}(Z)=3
and
\operatorname{reg}(X_{y})=5
for case
(2.c)
with
\operatorname{reg}(Z)=4
.

5.
Castelnuovo-Mumford Regularity Bounds

We prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in this section by using the method of generic projection, which has been used to bound the regularities in
[
L1
]
,
[
Kw1
,
Kw2
,
Kw3
]
and
[
Ni
]
. The paper
[
Kw3
]
gives a very nice explanation of this method, so we refer the reader to there for further information.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate locally Cohen-Macaulay projective variety of dimension
n
, codimension
e
, and degree
d
. As we have introduced in the beginning of Section
4
,
let

\pi\colon X\to\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n+1}

be a general projection from an
(r-n-2)
-dimensional linear subspace
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Denote by
V=H^{0}(\Lambda,\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}(1))
the vector space of linear forms on
\Lambda
.
Let

\mathcal{V}=\{V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}\}

be a collection of linear subspaces
V_{j}\subseteq H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(j))
such that
the natural maps

V_{1}\to V,~~V_{2}\to S^{2}(V)=H^{0}(\Lambda,\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}(2))

are isomorphisms and

V_{j}\to S^{j}(V)=H^{0}(\Lambda,\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}(j))

are injective for all
3\leq j\leq k
.
Then we have a map

w_{\mathcal{V}}\colon V_{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(-k)\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Let
p\colon\Bl_{\Lambda}\mathbb{P}^{r}\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
along the center
\Lambda
, and
q\colon\Bl_{\Lambda}\mathbb{P}^{r}\to\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be the natural projection. Then the map
w_{\mathcal{V}}
is induced by the natural map

q_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(k)\longrightarrow q_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(k)=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(k).

For a point
y\in\overline{X}
, as discussed in Section
4
, the fiber
X_{y}
is a subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y}
. Furthermore, there is a linear form
l_{y}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(1))
which generates
1
in the residue field
\mathbb{C}(y)
of
y
.
Note that
l_{y}
is regarded as a linear form on
\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y}
and
l^{j}_{y}
represents the subspace of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y}}(j))
with the basis
l^{j}_{y}
. Furthermore,
V_{j}
also can be regarded as a subspace of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y}}(j))
in an obvious way.
Therefore, by base change, the map
w_{\mathcal{V}}
is surjective if and only if for all
y\in\overline{X}
the map

w_{\mathcal{V},y}:V_{k}\oplus(V_{k-1}\otimes l_{y})\oplus\cdots\oplus(V_{1}\otimes l_{y}^{k-1})\oplus l_{y}^{k}\longrightarrow H^{0}(X_{y},\mathcal{O}_{X_{y}}(k))

is surjective. Note that the vector space in the left side is a subspace of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y}}(k))
. Therefore one can check the surjectivity of
w_{\mathcal{V},y}
for the subscheme
X_{y}
in the space
\mathbb{P}^{r-n-1}_{y}
.

Suppose that
w_{\mathcal{V}}
is surjective. Since
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is Cohen-Macaulay, it follows that

E:=\ker(w_{\mathcal{V}})

is a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
.
Assume now that the Kodaira vanishing theorem holds on
X
(e.g.,
X
has rational or Du Bois singularities).
By
[
Kw3
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have
\operatorname{reg}(E^{*})\leq-2
and
\operatorname{reg}(E^{*})\leq-3
if and only if
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is linearly normal and
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\mathcal{I}_{X|\mathbb{P}^{r}}(2))=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
. Then by the standard cohomological argument, the regularity of
X
can be bounded as follows.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
Kw3
, Lemma 3.2)]
)
.

Under the same notations as above, we have

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+1+\sum_{j=3}^{k}(j-2)\dim V_{j}.

If moreover
\operatorname{reg}(E^{*})\leq-3
, then we have

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+1-\dim V_{1}-\dim V_{2}+\sum_{j=4}^{k}(j-3)\dim V_{j}.

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Take a general projection
\pi\colon X\to\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
from an
(r-5)
-dimensional linear subspace
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
, and set
V_{1}\subseteq H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(1))
and
V_{2}\subseteq H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(2))
to be any subspaces such that the natural maps
V_{1}\to V=H^{0}(\Lambda,\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}(1))
and
V_{2}\to S^{2}(V)
are isomorphisms.
For
y\in\overline{X}
, denote by
X_{y}:=\pi^{-1}(y)
the fiber of the projection
\pi
and
\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y}:=\langle\Lambda,X_{y}\rangle\simeq\mathbb{P}^{r-4}
.
Let
\overline{X}_{4}:=\{y\in\overline{X}\mid X_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{y}^{r-4}\text{ is not $3$-regular}\}
.
By definition, for every
y\in\overline{X}\setminus\overline{X}_{4}
, the fiber
X_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{y}^{r-4}
is
3
-regular, i.e., it is
2
-normal. Thus the map

(5.1)

V_{2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-2)\oplus V_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}

is surjective over all
y\in\overline{X}\setminus\overline{X}_{4}
(i.e., tensoring with
\mathbb{C}(y)
the resulting map is surjective).

By Theorem
4.3
,
\overline{X}_{4}
is a finite set, and for all
y\in\overline{X}_{4}
, the zero dimensional scheme
X_{y}
can be decomposed into two disjoint subschemes
Z
and
q
such that
q
is a reduced point in
X_{\operatorname{reg}}
and
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{y}^{r-4}
is
3
-regular.
Let
H
be a general linear form on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
which is also a linear form on both
\mathbb{P}_{y}^{r-4}
and
\Lambda
.
By the choice of
H
, it gives a nonzero constant in the residue field
\mathbb{C}(q)
. Let
l_{y}
be a linear form on
\mathbb{P}^{4}
that does not vanish at
y
. Then we have a diagram

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>V_{2}\oplus(V_{1}\otimes l_{y})\oplus l^{2}_{y}@>{\cdot l_{y}}>{}>H^{3}\oplus(V_{2}\otimes l_{y})\oplus(V_{1}\otimes l_{y}^{2})\oplus l^{3}_{y}@>{}>{}>H^{3}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{\theta_{2}}V{}V@V{\theta_{3}}V{}V@V{\theta_{H}}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(2))@>{\cdot l_{y}}>{}>H^{0}(X_{y},\mathcal{O}_{X_{y}}(3))@>{}>{}>k(q)@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

where
H^{3}
represents a subspace of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y}}(3))
with the basis
H^{3}
and the same for the powers of
l_{y}
regarded as a linear form on
\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y}
. Since
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y}
is
3
-regular and
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y}}(2))=V_{2}\oplus(V_{1}\otimes l_{y})\oplus l^{2}_{y}
, it follows that
\theta_{2}
is surjective. By the choice of
H
, the map
\theta_{H}
is also surjective. Hence
\theta_{3}
is surjective as well. Thus if we set
V_{3}:=\langle H^{3}\rangle
and
\mathcal{V}:=\{V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\}
, then the map

w_{\mathcal{V}}\colon V_{3}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-3)\oplus V_{2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-2)\oplus V_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}

is surjective over all
y\in\overline{X}_{4}
. Combining the surjectivity in (
5.1
), we conclude that the map
w_{\mathcal{V}}

is indeed surjective. Finally, by applying Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+1+1=d-e+2,

so we complete the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a non-degenerate normal projective threefold of degree
d
and codimension
e
. Take a general projection
\pi\colon X\to\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
from an
(r-5)
-dimensional linear subspace
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
.

(1)

Suppose that
X
has isolated rational singularities and the condition (

⋆

‣
1
) in Introduction holds, i.e.,

\dim\bigcup_{l\in\Sigma}l\leq 4,\text{ where }~\Sigma=\{\text{lines }l\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}\mid\operatorname{length}(l\cap X)\geq 4\text{ and }l\cap X_{\operatorname{sing}}=\emptyset\}.

Notice that the condition (

⋆

‣
1
) means that there is no
y\in\overline{X}
such that
X_{y}
consists of collinear four reduced points. Thus the case
(1.a)
in Remark
4.4
cannot occur. Moreover, since
\dim X_{\operatorname{sing}}\leq 0
, the case
(1.b)
in Remark
4.4
cannot occur. Thus
X_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r-4}_{y}
is
3
-regular for all
y\in\overline{X}
. Then the map

w\colon V_{2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-2)\oplus V_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}

is surjective. By Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+1.

(2)

Suppose that
X
has Cohen-Macaulay Du Bois singularities. As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can argue that the map

w\colon V_{4}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-4)\oplus V_{3}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-3)\oplus V_{2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-2)\oplus V_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}

is surjective, where we take
V_{3},V_{4}
with
\dim V_{3}={e+1\choose 3},\dim V_{4}=1
. By Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-e+3+{e+1\choose 3}.

On the other hand, consider a general hyperplane section
S
of
X
, which is a non-degenerate normal projective surface with Du Bois singularities in
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
. By Remark
3.8
, we know that
\operatorname{reg}(S)\leq d-e+1
. Then
[
NP
, Lemma 2.2]
(see also
[
KP
, Lemma 2.1]
) implies that

\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq d-e.

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.2
. We shall use the following proposition. It is probably known to experts, but we could not find a suitable reference. So we give a proof here.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a projective variety of codimension
2
. Assume that
\dim X\geq 3
. Then
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is linearly normal.

Proof.

Note that if
X
is contained in a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, then the proposition is obvious. So in the sequel we assume
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is non-degenerate. We first show that the only non-degenerate surface
S
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
which is not linearly normal is obtained by an isomorphic projection of the second Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. Indeed, if
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
is not linearly normal, then it can be projected isomorphically from
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. Thus in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
, the secant variety of
S
should have dimension
4
. By Severi’s theorem for singular surfaces
[
D
]
,
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is either a cone or the second Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. But if
S
is a cone, then the tangent space of
S
at a vertex point is the whole space
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. So in this case,
S
cannot be projected to
\mathbb{P}^{4}
isomorphically. Hence
S
is a projected second Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.

Now let
X
be a threefold in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. Suppose otherwise
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is not linearly normal so that it is obtained by an isomorphic projection of
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{6}
.
Let
Y
be a general hyperplane section of
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
. Then
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
is not linearly normal. So by the above argument,
Y
is a projected second Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. This implies that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{6}
has degree
4
, and therefore, it is a variety of minimal degree. Hence
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{6}
is either a rational normal scroll or a cone over the second Veronese surface. If
X
is a cone, then the tangent space of
X
at a vertex point is the whole
\mathbb{P}^{6}
so that
X
cannot be projected isomorphically to
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. Hence
X
is not a cone, and it is a rational normal scroll. However in this case the tangent variety of
X
fills all
\mathbb{P}^{6}
, so again it cannot be projected isomorphically into
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. Hence
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
has to be linearly normal.

Finally, consider a non-degenerate projective variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
of codimension
2
and dimension
\geq 4
. By taking general hyperplane section which is linearly normal by induction, we see that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is linearly normal as claimed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose first that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is contained in a hyperquadric, i.e.,
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{5},\mathcal{I}_{X|\mathbb{P}^{5}}(2))\neq 0
. If
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a complete intersection, then
\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-1
and the equality holds exactly when
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is of type
(2,2)
. If
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is not a complete intersection, then
\operatorname{reg}(X)=\frac{d+1}{2}
by
[
Kw2
, Proposition 2.9 (2)]
. Thus
\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-1
and the equality holds when
d=3
, i.e.,
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a variety of minimal degree. From now on, we assume that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is not contained in a hyperquadric, i.e.,
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{5},\mathcal{I}_{X|\mathbb{P}^{5}}(2))=0
. We actually prove that
\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-2
, which completes the proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, take a general projection
\pi\colon X\to\overline{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}
from a general point in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
. By Theorem
4.3
,
\pi^{-1}(y)=X_{y}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{y}^{1}
is
5
-regular. Thus the map

w\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-4)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-3)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}

is surjective, and the kernel of
w
is a vector bundle
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. We now claim that

\operatorname{reg}(E^{*})\leq-3.

By
[
Kw3
, Lemma 3.1]
, it is sufficient to show that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is linearly normal and
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
. By Proposition
5.3
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
is linearly normal. On the other hand,
[
FL
, Corollary 5.3]
says that
X
is simply connected and therefore
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})=0
.
Since
X
has Du Bois singularities, it follows that
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is surjective. Thus we obtain
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
. We have shown the claim. Now, by applying Lemma
5.1
, we see that
\operatorname{reg}(X)\leq d-2
.
∎

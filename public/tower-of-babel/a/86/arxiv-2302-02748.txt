Construction of Weighted Clifford Prolate Spheroidal wave Functions
Hamed Baghal Ghaffari, Swanhild Bernstein

Abstract
. We develop some properties and the Bonnet formula for Clifford Gegenbauer polynomials. Then after we define and construct weighted Clifford prolate spheroidal wave functions. We then prove that they are orthogonal in a weighted function space.



1.
Introduction

In 1961,
[
22
]
Slepian, Pollak and Landau observed that Prolate spheroidal wave functions (PSWFs) which have long been used in mathematical physics were the solutions of the spectral concentration problem which is of enormous importance in communications technologies. In 1964,
[
21
]
the multidimensional version of PSWFs as one of the earliest generalizations has been developed. However, the mentioned generalized PSWFs had singularity in the related differential equations causing numerical instability. Moreover, the multidimensional PSWFs were at most complex-valued, i.e., not multichannel, decreasing the flexibility in applications such as image processing. Because of these two problems, the Clifford higher dimension version of PSWFs, i.e., Clifford PSWFs (CPSWFs) has been developed in
[
3
,
4
,
2
]
. In 2010 the weighted version of the one-dimensional PSWFs was constructed by Li-Lian Wang and Jing Zhang
[
25
]
for approximation applications to the class of functions belonging to the weighted function space. A multidimensional version of the latter work in higher dimensions was also developed in
[
26
]
. Some applications and properties of (weighted) PSWFs can be found in

[
24
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
14
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
6
,
16
]
.

Here we continue the work developed in
[
4
]
with the higher dimensional version of the weight given in
[
25
]
. For constructing weighted CPSWFs we find a Bonnet formula for Clifford Gegenbauer polynomials (CGPs). In fact, the Bonnet formula is the key and the innovation part for getting into the construction of weighted CPSWFs and proving they are orthogonal in related space.

This paper is organized as follows. The second section gives background related to Clifford analysis and Jacobi polynomials. In section 3 we recall the definition of the CGPs and investigate some of their properties such as the Bonnet formulas. In section 4 we define the weighted CPSWFs and then we compute them. In section 5, we find the Sturm-Liouville properties of the weighted CPSWFs which enable us to say they are a basis for the weighted function space.

Background

Let

\mathbb{R}^{m}

be

m
-dimensional
euclidean space and let

\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{m}\}

be an orthonormal basis for

\mathbb{R}^{m}.

We endow these vectors with the multiplicative properties

\displaystyle e_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=-1,\;\;j=1,\dots,m,

\displaystyle e_{j}e_{i}

\displaystyle=-e_{i}e_{j},\;\;i\neq j,\;\;i,j=1,\dots,m.

For any subset

A=\{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{h}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}=Q_{m},
with
j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{h}

we consider the formal product

e_{A}=e_{j_{1}}e_{j_{2}}\dots e_{j_{h}}.

Moreover for the empty set

\emptyset

one puts

e_{\emptyset}=1
(the identity element). The Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{m}
is then the
2^{m}
-dimensional associative algebra

{\mathbb{R}}_{m}=\bigg\{\sum\limits_{A\subset Q_{m}}\lambda_{A}e_{A}:\,\lambda_{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}\bigg\}.

Every element
\lambda=\sum\limits_{A\subset Q_{m}}\lambda_{A}e_{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{m}
may be decomposed as

\lambda=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}[\lambda]_{k},

where

[\lambda]_{k}=\sum\limits_{|A|=k}\lambda_{A}e_{A}

is the so-called

k
-vector
part of

\lambda\,(k=0,1,\dots,m).

Denoting by

\mathbb{R}_{m}^{k}

the subspace of all

k
-vectors
in

\mathbb{R}_{m},

i.e., the image of

\mathbb{R}_{m}

under the projection operator

[\cdot]_{k},

one has the multi-vector decomposition

\mathbb{R}_{m}=\mathbb{R}_{m}^{0}\oplus\mathbb{R}_{m}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{R}_{m}^{m},

leading to the identification of

\mathbb{R}

with the subspace of real scalars

\mathbb{R}_{m}^{0}

and of

\mathbb{R}^{m}

with the subspace of real Clifford vectors

\mathbb{R}_{m}^{1}.
The latter identification is achieved by identifying the point

(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

with the Clifford number

x=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}x_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{m}^{1}
.
The Clifford number

e_{M}=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{m}

is called the pseudoscalar; depending on the dimension

m,

the pseudoscalar commutes or anti-commutes with the

k
-vectors
and squares to

\pm 1.

The Hermitian conjugation is the real linear mapping
\lambda\mapsto\bar{\lambda}
of
{\mathbb{R}}_{m}
to itself satisfying

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda\mu}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mu}\bar{\lambda},\;\;\;\;\textnormal{for all}\;\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{m}

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda_{A}e_{A}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{A}\overline{e_{A}},\;\;\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\overline{e_{j}}

\displaystyle=-e_{j},\;\;j,\;\;j=1,\cdots,m.

The Hermitian conjugation leads to a Hermitian inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
and its associated norm
|\cdot|
on

\mathbb{R}_{m}

given respectively by

(\lambda,\mu)=[\bar{\lambda}\mu]_{0}\;\;\;\textnormal{and}\;\;\;|\lambda|^{2}=[\bar{\lambda}\lambda]_{0}=\sum\limits_{A}|\lambda_{A}|^{2}.

The product of two vectors splits up into a scalar part and a 2-vector, also called a bivector:

xy=-\langle x,y\rangle+x\wedge y

where

\langle x,y\rangle=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}y_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{0}_{m}
,
and

x\wedge y=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{j=i+1}^{m}e_{i}e_{j}(x_{j}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{m}
.
Note that the square of a vector variable

x

is scalar-valued and equals the norm squared up to minus sign:

x^{2}=-\langle x,x\rangle=-|x|^{2}.

Clifford analysis offers a function theory which is a higher-dimensional analogue of the theory of holomorphic functions of one complex variable. The functions considered are defined in the Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{m}

and take their values in the Clifford algebra

\mathbb{R}_{m}.

The central notion in Clifford analysis is monogenicity, which is a multidimensional counterpart of holomorphy in the complex plane.
Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}_{m}
and
n
a non-negative integer. We say
f\in C^{n}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}_{m})
if
f
and all its partial derivatives of order less than or equal to

are continuous.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
A function

is said to be left monogenic in that region if

\partial_{x}f=0.

Here

is the Dirac operator in

, i.e.,

\partial_{x}f=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}f
,

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}.

The Euler operator is defined on

by

E=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}
.
If

4.

In this section, we define the weighted CPSWFs and then we will compute using Bonnet formulas.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
c\geq 0
and
\alpha>-1
. The Clifford operator
\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}
acting on
C^{2}(B(1),\mathbb{R}_{m})\subset L_{2}(B(1),(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha})
is defined as follows

(4.1)

\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}f(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{-\alpha}\partial_{x}((1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+1}\partial_{x}f(x))+4\pi^{2}c^{2}|x|^{2}f(x).

Theorem 4.2
.

The operator
L_{c,\alpha}
defined in (
4.1
) is self-adjoint regarding to the weighted inner product, i.e.,

(4.2)

\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}g\rangle_{\alpha}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}f,g\rangle_{\alpha}.

Proof.

We see that
\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}f=L_{0,\alpha}f+4\pi^{2}c^{2}|x|^{2}f(x)
. We prove that
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
is self-adjoint. So using Clifford-Stokes theorem given in
2.2
we have that

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{0,\alpha}g\rangle_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(1)}\overline{f(x)}(L_{0,\alpha}g)(x)(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(1)}\overline{f(x)}\partial_{x}((1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+1}\partial_{x}g(x))\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B(1)}(\overline{f(x)})\frac{x}{|x|}(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+1}\partial_{x}g(x)\,dx-\int_{B(1)}(\overline{f(x)}\partial_{x})((1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+1})\partial_{x}f(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(1)}(\overline{f(x)}\partial_{x})((1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+1})\partial_{x}f(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(1)}(1-|x|^{2})^{-\alpha}((\overline{f(x)}\partial_{x})((1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha+1})\partial_{x}f(x))(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{0,\alpha}f,g\rangle_{\alpha}.

This help to complete the proof. ∎

5.
Sturm-Liouville equation of Weighted CPSWFs

The main goal of this section is to prove the orthogonality of
\{\psi_{n,m,\alpha}^{k,c,j}\}_{j,k,n}
in
L^{2}(B(1),(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha})
using Sturm-Liouville theory in one dimension. This orthogonality enables us to conclude that the set
\{\psi_{n,m,\alpha}^{k,c,i}:\ n\geq 0,\,k\geq 0,\,1\leq i\leq d_{k,m},\alpha>-1\},
is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}_{\alpha}({B(1)},\mathbb{C}_{m})
.

Remark 5.1
.

Using the way of construction weighted CPSWFs and the fact that
C_{2N,m}^{\alpha}(Y_{k}^{j})(x)=P_{N,k,m}^{\alpha}(|x|^{2})Y_{k}^{j}(x)
we can conclude that
\psi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c,j}(x)=P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(|x|^{2})Y_{k}^{j}(x)
. In the same mannar we can write
\psi_{2N+1,m,\alpha}^{k,c,j}(x)=Q_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(|x|^{2})xY_{k}^{j}(x)
.

Now by applying the change of variables
|x|^{2}=t
, and
|x|^{2}=\frac{s+1}{2}
to the equation

(5.1)

\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}\psi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c,j}(x)=\chi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}\psi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c,j}(x),

where
\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}
is defined in (
4.1
) we can obtain the Sturm-Liouville form of the differential equations corresponding to the even and odd weighted CPSWFs.

Lemma 5.2
.

The radial part of
P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(|x|^{2})
of the weighted CPSWF
\psi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c,j}(x),
satisfies

\displaystyle 4t(1-t)\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)

\displaystyle+2[m+2k-t(2+m+2k+2\alpha)]\frac{d}{dt}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)

(5.2)

\displaystyle-4\pi^{2}c^{2}tP_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)+\chi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)=0,

which becomes a Sturm-Liouville differential equation after multiplying by
g(t)=t^{k+\frac{m}{2}-1}(1-t)^{\alpha}
. Therefore,
\{P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}\}_{N=0}^{\infty}
may be normalised so that

\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)P_{M,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)t^{k+\frac{m}{2}-1}(1-t)^{\alpha}\,dt=\langle P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c},P_{M,m,\alpha}^{k,c}\rangle_{g(t)}=\delta_{MN}.

Furthermore, for each fixed integer
k\geq 0
, the collection
\{P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}:\,N\geq 0\}
is complete in the weighted space
L^{2}([0,1],t^{k+\frac{m}{2}-1}(1-t)^{\alpha})
of measurable functions
f:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
for which
\int\limits_{0}^{1}|f(t)|^{2}t^{k+\frac{m}{2}-1}(1-t)^{\alpha}\,dt<\infty
. Moreove the eigenvalues
\{\chi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}\}_{N=0}^{\infty}
may be distinct so that
\chi_{0,m,\alpha}^{k,c}<\chi_{2,m,\alpha}^{k,c}<\dots
.

Remark 5.3
.

By considering
|x|^{2}=\frac{s+1}{2}
we can see that

\displaystyle(1-s^{2})\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\tilde{P}_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(s)

\displaystyle+[(k+\frac{m}{2}-1-\alpha)-s(k+\frac{m}{2}+\alpha+1)]\frac{d}{ds}\tilde{P}_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(s)

(5.3)

\displaystyle-\pi^{2}c^{2}\frac{s+1}{2}\tilde{P}_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(s)=-\frac{\chi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}}{4}\tilde{P}_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(s),

becomes a Sturm-Liouville differential equation after multiplying
y(s)=(s+1)^{k+\frac{m}{2}-1}(1-s)^{\alpha}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For each integer
k\geq 0
, let
\{Y_{k}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{d_{k,m}}
be an orthonormal basis for
M_{l}^{+}(k)
. Then the even eigenfunctions of the operator

L_{c},
i.e,
\{\psi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c,i}:\,k\geq 0,\ 1\leq i\leq d_{k,m}\}
are orthogonal in
L^{2}_{\alpha}(B(1),\mathbb{C}_{m})
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B(1)}\overline{\psi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c,i}(x)}

\displaystyle\,\psi_{2N^{\prime},m,\alpha}^{k^{\prime},c,i^{\prime}}(x)\,(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}dx=\int\limits_{{B(1)}}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(|x|^{2})P_{N^{\prime},m,\alpha}^{k^{\prime},c}(|x|^{2})\overline{Y}_{k}^{i}(x)Y_{k^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime}}(x)\,(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(r^{2})P_{N^{\prime},m,\alpha}^{k^{\prime},c}(r^{2})r^{m+k+k^{\prime}-1}\,(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}\int\limits_{S^{m-1}}\overline{Y_{k}^{i}(\omega)}Y_{k^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime}}(\omega)\,d\omega\,dr

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(r^{2})P_{N^{\prime},m,\alpha}^{k,c}(r^{2})r^{m+2k-1}\,(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}dr\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{N,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)P_{N^{\prime},m,\alpha}^{k,c}(t)t^{k+\frac{m}{2}-1}\,(1-t)^{\alpha}dt\,\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\,\delta_{ii^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{NN^{\prime}}\,\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\,\delta_{ii^{\prime}}.

∎

Remark 5.5
.

We can prove that the radial part of the odd
\psi_{2N+1,m,\alpha}^{k,c}(x)
, i.e.,

We can prove that

2.3

Proposition 5.6
.

The even and odd eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}
, are orthogonal, i.e.,

\int\limits_{B(1)}\overline{\psi_{2N,m}^{k,c,i}(x)}\psi_{2N^{\prime}+1,m}^{k^{\prime},c,i^{\prime}}(x)\,(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}dx=0,

for all

,
k,k^{\prime}\geq 0
,
1\leq i\leq d_{k,m}\,
,

5.4
, remark

, and, proposition

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose
f
is as in (
2.6
) and is supported on
{B(1)}
with
\langle\psi_{2N,m,\alpha}^{k,c,i},f\rangle_{\alpha}=0
for all

where

is the dimension of
M_{l}^{+}(k).
Then
g_{k}^{(i)}=f_{k}^{(i)}=0,
for all
k\geq 0,\;1\leq i\leq d_{k,m}
.

Proof.

Let
n=2N
, and suppose
f\in L^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{C}}_{m})
admits the expansion (
2.6
) and is supported on
{B(1)}.
By corollary
2.3
and lemma
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\langle\psi_{2N,m}^{k,c,i},f\rangle_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{B(1)}}\overline{\psi_{2N,m}^{k,c,i}(x)}f(x)\,(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{B(1)}}P_{N,m}^{k,c}(|x|^{2})\overline{Y_{k}^{i}(x)}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{d_{k^{\prime},m}}f_{k^{\prime}}^{\ell}(|x|)Y_{k^{\prime}}^{\ell}(x)\,(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{k^{\prime},m}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{N,m}^{k,c}(r^{2})f_{k^{\prime}}^{\ell}(r)r^{k+k^{\prime}+m-1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}\int\limits_{S^{m-1}}\overline{Y_{k}^{i}(\omega)}Y_{k^{\prime}}^{\ell}(\omega)\,d\omega\,dr

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{N,m}^{k,c}(r^{2})f_{k}^{(i)}(r)r^{2k+m-1}\,(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}dr

\displaystyle=2\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{N,m}^{k,c}(t)f_{k}^{(i)}(\sqrt{t})t^{k+\frac{m}{2}-1}\,(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt.

Hence by Lemma
5.2

f_{k}^{(i)}=0,
for all
k
and
i
. Now if we let

then we can prove that

for all

and

in a similar way.
∎

Poisson Approximation to Weighted Sums of Locally Dependent Random Variables
Pratima Eknath Kadu

Abstract
This paper deals with Poisson approximation to weighted sums of locally dependent random variables using Stein’s method. The derived result represent a significant improvement of existing results. To illustrate the effectiveness of our findings, a numerical comparison is provided.



Keywords:
Poisson distribution; weighted sums; Stein’s method.

MSC 2020 Subject Classifications:
Primary: 62E17, 60F05; Secondary: 62E20, 60E05.

Introduction and Preliminaries

Weighted sums of random variables (rvs) have been successfully applied in the areas related to least-square estimators, non-parametric regression function estimators, and Jackknife estimates, among many others. These sums also play a crucial role in characterizing various statistics. However, determining the exact distributions of weighted sums of rvs is generally impractical, prompting interest in approximations to study their asymptotic behaviour. The literature has extensively explored limit theorems for weighted sums of rvs, as evidenced by works such as Chow and Lai
[
6
]
, Dolera
[
7
]
, Lai and Robbins
[
13
]
, Zhang
[
18
]
, among many others.

In this paper, we consider the locally dependent set up similar to Kumar
[
9
]
. Let
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,

Y_{n}
be
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variables. For each
i
, let
c_{i}\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
and
i\in\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
be such that
Y_{i}
is independent of
Y_{\mathcal{A}_{i}^{c}}
, where
Y_{\mathcal{A}}
is the collection of random variables
\left\{Y_{i},i\in\mathcal{A}\right\}
and
\mathcal{A}^{c}
denotes the complement of the set
\mathcal{A}
. Also, see Kumar and Vellaisamy
[
11
]
and Röllin [19] for a similar type of locally dependent structure. In addition, if
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{i\}
then our locally dependent structure reduced to the independent collection of random variables. Define

\displaystyle Z_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}Y_{i,n},

(1.1)

the weighted sums of locally dependent rvs. We assume
c_{i}=1
for at least one
i
so that
Z_{n}
becomes
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variable (rv). Throughout the paper, let
Z_{A}:=\sum_{j\in A}c_{i}Y_{i}
. Limit theorems for the weighted sums of rvs have been discussed in the literature if the sum of weights is finite. For example, Chow and Lai
[
6
]
are considered the finite sum of the square of weights, and Bhati and Rattihalli
[
4
]
are considered the geometrically weighted sums. But if the sums of weights are not finite then the study of limit behavior of such distributions becomes challenging. Therefore, in this paper, we consider natural weights and obtain approximations for
Z_{n}
that helps to visualize the behavior of
Z_{n}
.

Next, let
X\sim\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
, the Poisson distribution, then the probability mass function of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X=k)=\frac{e^{-\lambda}{\lambda}^{k}}{k!},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.2)

We obtain error bound for X-approximation to
Z_{n}
. The total variance distance is used as a distance metric. Our results are derived using Stein’s method (Stein
[
16
]
) which can be followed in three steps. First, for a random variable
Y
, find a Stein operator
\mathscr{A}
such that
\mathbb{E}(\mathscr{A}g(Y))=0
, for
g\in G_{Y}
, where
G_{Y}:=\{g\in G|g(0)=0,\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ g(k)=0\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ k\notin S(Y)\}
,
G:=\{f|f:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ is bounded}\}
, and
S(Y)
is the support of random variable
Y
. Second, solve the following Stein equation

\mathscr{A}g(k)=f(k)-\mathbb{E}f(Y),\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\penalty\ {\rm and}\penalty\ f\in G.

(1.3)

Finally, putting a random variable
Z
in place of
k
and taking expectation and supremum, we have

d_{TV}(Y,Z):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\big|\mathbb{E}f(Y)-\mathbb{E}f(Z)\big|=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\big|\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(Z)]\big|,

(1.4)

where
\mathcal{I}=\{{\bf 1}_{A}\penalty\ |\penalty\ A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
and
{\bf 1}_{A}
is the indicator function of
A
. Now, for the random variable X defined in (
1.2
), the Stein operator is given by

\mathscr{A}g(k)=\lambda g(k+1)-kg(k),\quad{\rm for}\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\penalty\ {\rm and}\penalty\ g\in G_{X}

(1.5)

and the solution to the Stein equation (
1.3
), say
g_{f}
, satisfies

\|\Delta g_{f}\|\leq\frac{1}{\max(1,\lambda)},\quad\text{for $f\in\mathcal{I}$, $g\in G_{X}$}.

(1.6)

For additional details, see Barbour and Hall
[
1
]
, Barbour
et. al.

[
2
]
, Chen
et. al.

[
5
]
, Brown and Xia
[
3
]
, and Kumar
et. al.

[
10
,
12
]
. For recent developments, see Ley
et. al.

[
15
]
, Upadhye and Barman
[
17
]
, Kumar
[
9
]
, and references therein.

2
Bounds For Poisson Approximation

In this section, we derive an error bound for the Poisson approximation to weighted sums of locally dependent rvs. Additionally, we present the result for the independent case. Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
follows Poisson distribution with parameter
\lambda>0
and
Z_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}Y_{i}
, where
Y_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, are locally dependent rvs. The subsequent theorem establishes an upper bound for the total variation distance between
Z_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
Z_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
be defined as in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively. Then

\displaystyle d_{TV}(Z_{n},\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})\leq\frac{1}{\max(1,\lambda)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}c_{i}^{2}[\mathbb{E}(Y_{i}Y_{j})+\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\mathbb{E}(Y_{j})]-c_{i}\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\right\},

where
\lambda=\mathbb{E}(Z_{n})
.

Proof.

Replacing
k
by
Z_{n}
in (
1.5
) and taking expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(Z_{n})]

\displaystyle={\lambda}\mathbb{E}[g(Z_{n}+1)]-\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}g(Z_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\mathbb{E}[g(Z_{n}+1)]-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{i}g(Z_{n})\right].

(2.1)

Next, let
Z_{i}^{*}=Z_{n}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}c_{i}Y_{i}=Z_{n}-Z_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
then
Y_{i}
and
Z_{i}^{*}
are independent rvs. Adding and subtracting
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{i}g(Z_{i}^{*}+1)\right]
in (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(Z_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\mathbb{E}[g(Z_{n}+1)-g(Z_{i}^{*}+1)]-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{i}(g(Z_{n})-g(Z_{i}^{*}+1))\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{Z_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}}\Delta g(Z_{i}^{*}+j)\right]-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{Z_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}-1}\Delta g(Z_{i}^{*}+j)\right]

Note that if
Y_{i}\geq 1
, then
Z_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}\geq 1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(Z_{n})]|\leq\|\Delta g\|\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}[\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\mathbb{E}(Z_{\mathcal{A}_{i}})+\mathbb{E}(Y_{i}(Z_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}-1))]\right\}.

Using (
1.6
) the result follows.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
Z_{n}
be the weighted sums of independent rvs and
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
be defined as in (
1.2
) satisfies
\lambda=\mathbb{E}(Z_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle d_{TV}(Z_{n},\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})\leq\frac{1}{\max(1,\lambda)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{c_{i}^{2}[\mathbb{E}(Y_{i}^{2})+(\mathbb{E}(Y_{i}))^{2}]-c_{i}\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\right\}.

Proof.

The result follows by letting
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{i\}
in Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

(i)

If

i=1,2,\ldots,n

\displaystyle d_{TV}(Z_{n},\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}{p}^{2}_{i}}{\max\left(1,\lambda\right)},

(2.2)

(ii)

Note that the bound given in Corollary
2.1

\displaystyle d_{TV}(Z_{n},\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i}^{2}{p}^{2}_{i}+c_{i}(c_{i}-1)p_{i})}{\max\left(1,\lambda\right)},

(2.3)

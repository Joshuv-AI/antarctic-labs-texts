Spectral invariants over the integers
Yusuke Kawamoto, Egor Shelukhin

Abstract
Spectral invariants are quantitative measurements in symplectic topology coming from Floer homology theory. We study their dependence on the choice of coefficients in the context of Hamiltonian Floer homology. We discover phenomena in this setting which hold for ℤ \mathbb{Z} -coefficients and fail for all field coefficients. For example, we prove that the spectral norm, an important metric derived from spectral invariants, is unbounded over ℤ \mathbb{Z} for complex projective spaces, while it is uniformly bounded over all fields. This allows us to answer a symplectic version of a question of Hingston, originally asked in the setting of the energy functional on the loop space. We also provide applications to Hamiltonian dynamics and Hofer’s geometry.

Introduction

1.1
Context

Floer theory has been one of the main tools of study in symplectic topology since it was introduced in the 80s. Although it is regarded as the symplectic analogue of Morse theory, the actual construction is more involved and requires many choices and technical subtleties. One of them is the choice of coefficient ring; indeed, even for one of the most basic Lagrangian submanifolds like
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
in the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, one needs to be careful about the choice of the ground ring in order to get meaningful Floer-theoretic information. The coefficient field
\mathbb{Z}/2
is often chosen in this case. Thus, the choice of the ground ring is not just a minor technical matter, but a crucial point that yields different symplectic consequences. For recent applications of this point of view to Arnol’d’s conjecture we refer to
[
AB
,
BX
,
Rez
]
.

Spectral invariants
are important symplectic measurements coming from Floer homology theory which have found a wide range of application in the field: to Hofer’s geometry and Hamiltonian dynamics in particular. The theme of this paper is to study how the choice of a ground ring to set up Floer homology impacts spectral invariants. We note that usually fields were used as coefficients in this case. We will, however, mostly look at the ring
\mathbb{Z}
of coefficients.

1.3
Application: Question of Hingston

Hingston, in her research on closed geodesics, studied certain quantities that can be regarded as spectral invariants for the energy functional on the loop space. Roughly speaking, to a class of the homology of the loop space of a manifold
M
, say
\alpha\in H_{\ast}(\Lambda M)
, she assigns a real number
c(\alpha)
via a variational procedure and asked the following question.

Question 1.3.1

(Hingston’s question)
.

Does there exist a manifold
M
and a homogeneous non-torsion class
\alpha\in H_{\ast}(\Lambda M)
such that

c(k\cdot\alpha)<c(\alpha)

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
?

We refer the reader to Section
4.3.2
for a more detailed explanation concerning this question. While this question remains open, we consider the following symplectic analogue of it.

Question 1.3.2

(Hingston’s question: symplectic)
.

Does there exist a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
and a Hamiltonian
H
on it such that

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot[M])<c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,[M])?

We consider the fundamental class
[M]\in H_{2n}(M;\mathbb{Z})
since it is never torsion for
M
a closed symplectic manifold and it plays a special role in symplectic topology. However, we could ask if, more generally, there exists a non-torsion quantum cohomology class
a\in QH(M;\mathbb{Z})
for a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
such that
\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot a)<c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,a).

In the setting of Morse theory on a closed manifold, the answer to the analogous question is easily seen to be negative for the fundamental class. Indeed, for a Morse function
f:N\to\mathbb{R}
on a closed orientable manifold
N,

c_{R}(f,[N])=\max f,
regardless of the coefficient ring
R
and similarly
c_{\mathbb{Z}}(f,k[N])=\max f
for all
k\neq 0,
as for every ring
R,

H_{d}(\{f<\max f\};R)=0
for
d=\mathrm{dim}N.

In the case of a general non-torsion class
a\in QH_{*}(M;\mathbb{Z}),
an easy positive answer to this question would follow for
a=b+c,
where
b
is non-torsion,
c
is torsion and
c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,c)>c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,b).
One can construct such examples for Morse theory of closed orientable manifolds
N
for classes of certain degrees in
(0,\mathrm{dim}N).
In fact, it is not hard to make
N
a symplectic manifold, and hence answer this version of the question, since the filtered Floer theory of a
C^{2}
-small Morse function coincides with its filtered Floer homology. Similarly, if we do not require
a
to be degree-homogeneous, then this approach solves Hingston’s question for an arbitrary metric on
S^{n},

n\geqslant 2,
by
[
HR13
]
. However, if
H_{*}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free, like it is in our main case of interest,
M=\mathbb{C}P^{n},
this approach is not accessible.

Let us call the following quantity, the
spectral depth
for a Hamiltonian
H
:

\beta_{\text{spec}}(H):=c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,[M])-\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k[M]).

(1.3.1)

Inspired by Question
1.3.2
, we obtain the following estimate for the spectral norm over
\mathbb{Z}
, which leads to a positive answer to Question
1.3.2
.

Theorem B
.

For every Hamiltonian
H
on
\mathbb{C}P^{n},
we have

\gamma_{\mathbb{Z}}(H)\leqslant 1+\beta_{\mathrm{spec}}(H)+\beta_{\mathrm{spec}}(\overline{H}).

(1.3.2)

As a consequence of Theorems
A
and
B
, we obtain Theorem
1.3.3
, which answers the symplectic version of Hingston’s question, namely Question
1.3.2
, in the positive for
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. In fact, our answer is stronger since it applies to all non-zero quantum cohomology classes.

Theorem 1.3.3
.

For every non-zero class
a\in QH(\mathbb{C}P^{n};\mathbb{Z})
, there is a Hamiltonian
H
such that

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot a)<c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,a).

(1.3.3)

Remark 1.3.4.
The only progress for the original version of Hingston’s question at the time of writing is a result of Chambers–Liokumovich
[
CL19
]
which, in contrast to Theorem
1.3.3
, provides a partial negative answer. See Remark
4.3

Remark 1.3.5.
For a given Hamiltonian
H
on a closed symplectic manifold
M
and a non-torsion class
a\in QH(M;\mathbb{Z}),
for instance
a=[M],
it would be interesting to investigate the quantity

as a function of

For example, clearly if

Theorem

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}c(k)=c_{\mathbb{Q}}:=c_{\mathbb{Q}}(a,H).

In particular,

Moreover, if the infimum is not attained, and

is rational in the sense that

then

Indeed, by the spectrality property of spectral invariants, (

) below, for all

for certain capped orbits

If the infimum is not attained, then there exists a subsequence

and

We may assume that it is strictly decreasing and

Hence the one-periodic orbits

1.4
Poincaré duality over
\mathbb{Z}

In the previous sections, we highlighted results for the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
and in this section, we collect results for general closed monotone symplectic manifolds.

The uniform bound on the spectral norm for
\mathbb{K}
-coefficients, namely (
1.2.1
), is a consequence of the so-called
Poincaré duality formula for spectral invariants
: let
a\in QH_{k}(M;\mathbb{K})
be a degree
k
element of the quantum homology
QH(M;\mathbb{K})
of
M,
then

-c_{\mathbb{K}}(\overline{H},a)=\inf\{c_{\mathbb{K}}(H,b):\Pi(a,b)\neq 0\},

(1.4.1)

where
b
runs over
QH_{2n-k}(M;\mathbb{K}),
for a certain bilinear operation
\Pi(-,-):QH_{k}(M;\mathbb{K})\times QH_{2n-k}(M;\mathbb{K})\to\mathbb{K}.
(See Section
3.5
.) This formula says that the spectral invariants for
\overline{H}
are recovered from the spectral invariants for
H
. Theorem
A
implies that for
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients, this is no longer the case: the spectral invariants for
\overline{H}

cannot
be recovered from the spectral invariants for
H
and this is the reason why the spectral norm exhibits a highly distinct phenomenon (
1.2.2
) with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients. We explore the failure of the Poincaré duality formula for spectral invariants over
\mathbb{Z}
. To establish an appropriate formula for general closed monotone symplectic manifolds over
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients, we introduce the following quantity called the
torsion depth
which, roughly speaking, measures the persistence of the torsion classes in
\{HF^{\tau}(H;\mathbb{Z})\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 1.4.1
.

For each degree
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the following map which is induced by the inclusion map: for
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},\ \tau<\tau^{\prime}
,

\begin{gathered}i_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}^{\ast}:Ext(HF_{k}^{\tau^{\prime}}(H),\mathbb{Z})\to Ext(HF_{k}^{\tau}(H),\mathbb{Z}).\end{gathered}

(1.4.2)

We define the following quantities, which we call the torsion depths:

\displaystyle\beta_{k,\mathrm{tor}}(H)

\displaystyle:=\beta(Ext(HF_{k}^{\tau}(H),\mathbb{Z}))

(1.4.3)

\displaystyle:={\inf\{\kappa:\forall\tau,\mathrm{coker}(i_{\tau}^{\ast})}=\mathrm{coker}(i_{\tau,\tau+\kappa}^{\ast})\},

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{tor}}(H)

\displaystyle:=\max_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{k,\mathrm{tor}}(H).

Remark 1.4.2.
The quantity
\beta_{\mathrm{tor}}(H)
is well-defined even though the maximum is taken over an infinite set, as
\beta_{k,\mathrm{tor}}(H)
is
2N_{M}
-periodic where
N_{M}
is the minimal Chern number of
(M,\omega).

The following is the “corrected version” of the Poincaré duality formula for spectral invariants over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem C
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed monotone symplectic manifold. For a Hamiltonian
H
and a class
a\in QH_{\ast}(M;\mathbb{Z})
, we have the following:

\inf\{c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,b):\Pi(a,b)\neq 0\}-\beta_{\mathrm{tor}}(H)\leqslant-c_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{H},a)\leqslant\inf\{c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,b):\Pi(a,b)\neq 0\}.

(1.4.4)

In other words,

0\leqslant c_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{H},a)+\inf\{c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,b):\Pi(a,b)\neq 0\}\leqslant\beta_{\mathrm{tor}}(H).

(1.4.5)

Remark 1.4.3.
In (

Remark 1.4.4.
Strictly speaking, Inequality (

Then

Question 1.5.1

What does the set

\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)

We prove the first result in this direction, which states that the set

Theorem E

Consider
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
with

\sup_{\phi\in\mathrm{Ham}(\mathbb{C}P^{n})}d_{\mathrm{Hof}}(\phi,PR(\mathbb{C}P^{n}))=+\infty.

Moreover, for any pseudo-rotation
\phi\in\mathrm{Ham}(\mathbb{C}P^{n})

\gamma_{\mathbb{Z}}(\phi)\leqslant 1.

(1.5.2)

There are several definitions for pseudo-rotations, but in Theorem

Remark 1.5.3.

The argument proving Theorem

\gamma_{\mathbb{Z}}(\phi)\leqslant 1.
Conversely, it shows that whenever
\gamma_{\mathbb{Z}}(\phi)>1
then

has at least
n+2

This means that for a suitable prime

Therefore by
[

Triangle inequality.

Spectral invariants satisfy the triangle inequality: for Hamiltonians

Homotopy invariance.

Note that Hamiltonian spectral invariants satisfy the homotopy invariance, i.e. if two normalized Hamiltonians

\begin{gathered}c_{R}:\widetilde{\mathrm{Ham}}(M,\omega)\times QH(M,\omega;R)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\\

where the path

Entov–Polterovich quasimorphisms and (super)heaviness.
Based on spectral invariants, Entov–Polterovich built two theories, namely the theory of (Calabi) quasimorphisms and the theory of (super)heaviness, which we briefly review in this section.

Quasimorphisms.
Entov–Polterovich constructed a special map on
\widetilde{\mathrm{Ham}}(M,\omega)
called a quasimorphism, under some assumptions on the algebraic structure of the quantum homology ring of
(M,\omega)
. Recall that a quasimorphism
\mu

that satisfies the following two properties:

1.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\mu(f\cdot g)-\mu(f)-\mu(g)|<C

for any
f,g\in G
.

2.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
f\in G
, we have

\mu(f^{k})=k\cdot\mu(f).

The following is Entov–Polterovich’s construction of quasimorphisms on
\widetilde{\mathrm{Ham}}(M,\omega)

Theorem 2.2.2

(
[

]

Suppose

has a field factor, i.e.

of

with respect to this split, i.e.

1_{M}=e+a.

Then, the asymptotic spectral invariant of
\widetilde{\phi}

\begin{gathered}\zeta_{e}:\widetilde{\mathrm{Ham}}(M,\omega)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\\
\zeta_{e}(\widetilde{\phi}):=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{c_{R}(\widetilde{\phi}^{k},e)}{k}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{c_{R}(H^{\#k},e)}{k}\end{gathered}

(2.2.5)

where
H

represents the class
\widetilde{\phi}
in
\widetilde{\mathrm{Ham}}(M,\omega)
.

Remark 2.2.3.

From (

Remark 2.2.4.
By slight abuse of notation, we will also see

Superheaviness.
Entov–Polterovich introduced a notion of symplectic rigidity for subsets in

called (super)heaviness.

Definition 2.2.5

(
[
EP09
]
,
[
EP06
]
)

1.

2.

The following is an easy corollary of the definition of superheaviness which is useful.

]

and it follows that

by using the non-triviality and homogeneity of

. We have proven Theorem

The aim of this section is to show that there is a monotone Lagrangian torus in

In

•

It is disjoint from

We do not know whether or not, in the

Proof of Theorem
D
.

Note that by Hofer-continuity of spectral invariants, we can and will assume that
H
is non-degerate.
We first prove
c(H,a_{\mathbb{Q}})\leqslant\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c(H,k\cdot a_{\mathbb{Z}})
. Fix

and take a Floer cycle
x:=\sum_{j}a_{j}\overline{z}_{j}\in CF(H;\mathbb{Z})
that represents

and realizes the spectral invariant
c(H,k\cdot a_{\mathbb{Z}})
, i.e.
[x]=PSS_{H;\mathbb{Z}}(k\cdot a_{\mathbb{Z}})
and

We next show
\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c(H,k\cdot a_{\mathbb{Z}})\leqslant c(H,a_{\mathbb{Q}})

\begin{gathered}x:=\sum_{j}a_{j}\cdot\overline{z}_{j}\in CF(H;\mathbb{Z}),\\

such that

Write

where
{m_{j}},\in\mathbb{Z},\ n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=y+\partial z

\displaystyle c(H,k\cdot a_{\mathbb{Z}})

Note that the last equality is a consequence of our choice of

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c(H,k\cdot a_{\mathbb{Z}})\leqslant c(H,a_{\mathbb{Q}})

We discuss some refinements of Theorems
B
and
D
. First, we will see that the analogous statement of Theorem

for

Proposition 3.4.1
.

Consider
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. Let
p
be a prime.

1.

If
c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)<c_{\mathbb{C}}(H,1)
for a Hamiltonian
H
, then we have

c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)<\inf_{k\notin p\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1).

(3.4.1)

2.

If
c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)>c_{\mathbb{C}}(H,1)
for a Hamiltonian
H
, then we have

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1)<\inf_{k\notin p\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1),

(3.4.2)

i.e. the infimum on the left-hand-side is achieved by an integer that is a multiple of
p

Remark 3.4.2.
The Hamiltonian as in (

Proof for Proposition
3.4.1
.

By Proposition

, we know that

c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)\leqslant\inf_{k\notin p\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1).

(3.4.3)

) for every Hamiltonian. Then,

\displaystyle\inf_{k\notin p\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1)

\displaystyle=c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)

\displaystyle<c_{\mathbb{C}}(H,1)

\displaystyle=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1),

where we used the assumption and Remark

The second statement can be understood as follows, by using (

\displaystyle\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1)

\displaystyle=c_{\mathbb{C}}(H,1)

\displaystyle<c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\inf_{k\notin p\mathbb{N}}c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,k\cdot 1).

∎

We now consider spectral invariants over fields of characteristic

Proposition 3.4.3
.

Consider
\mathbb{C}P^{n}

\inf_{\text{$p$:prime}}c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)\leqslant c_{\mathbb{C}}(H,1)\leqslant\sup_{\text{$p$:prime}}c_{\mathbb{Z}/p}(H,1)\leqslant c_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,1)

Remark 3.4.4.
It would be interesting to figure out whether an equality holds for the third inequality in Proposition

3.4.3
.

We first prove

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbb{Z}}(H)

3.7
Remark on
\mathbb{Z}
-superheaviness

From Proposition
3.1.5
, it follows that

\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(H)\leqslant\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}}(H):=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{c_{\mathbb{Z}}(k\cdot H,[M])}{k}

for any autonomous Hamiltonian
H
, and thus
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}}
-superheaviness is a stronger condition than
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}
-superheaviness for any field
\mathbb{K}
. We point out the following new hierarchy of symplectic rigidity in terms of superheaviness.

Proposition 3.7.1
.

Consider
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. The Clifford torus
T^{n}_{\mathrm{Clif}}
is
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}}
-superheavy while the Chekanov torus
T^{n}_{\mathrm{Chek}}
and
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
are not.

Proof of Proposition
3.7.1
.

In
[
EP09
, Theorem 1.11]
, it was proven that the
special fiber
of a monotone symplectic toric manifold is superheavy with respect to any idempotent of the quantum homology. Although,
[
EP09
, Theorem 1.11]
was formulated for quantum homology over
\mathbb{C}
, the proof is based on the index of Hamiltonian orbits and in particular does not use the Poincaré duality and thus it carries over to
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients. The special fiber in
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
is precisely the Clifford torus and thus, we conclude that the Clifford torus is
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}}
-superheavy. As the Chekanov torus
T^{n}_{\mathrm{Chek}}
and
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
are disjoint (see Proposition
3.2.1
), we conclude that they are not
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}}
-superheavy (even though they are
\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}
-superheavy and
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
-superheavy, respectively, see Proposition
3.1.1
).
∎

4.1
Quantum linking form

In
M=\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, there might be
b\in Tor(HF^{\tau}(H))
, which eventually disappears, such that
c(\overline{H},[M])
corresponds to
-\tau
where
PSS_{H}([pt])+b
appears at
HF^{\tau}(H)
. It would be interesting to study the “filtered quantum linking form” which is the quantum counterpart of the linking form in singular homology theory (an analogue of the Poincaré pairing for torsion classes) by taking the filtration into account. The non-filtered version of this looks as follows:

\mathcal{L}:QH_{\ast}(M;\mathbb{Z})\otimes QH_{2n-1-\ast}(M;\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

which comes from the following operation:

\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 5.0pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\bigcirc$}$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{0.1736pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\ast$}$}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip 5.0pt}}:QH_{i}(M;\mathbb{Z})\otimes Tor(QH_{j}(M;\mathbb{Z}))\to QH_{i+j-2n+1}(M;\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

(a,b)\mapsto a\ast\beta^{-1}(b)

where

\beta:QH_{j}(M;\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to QH_{j-1}(M;\mathbb{Z})

denotes the Bockstein homomorphism.

We have for
a\in QH_{i}(M;\mathbb{Z})
,
b\in QH_{2n-1-i}(M;\mathbb{Z})
,

\mathcal{L}(a,b)=\langle a\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 5.0pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\bigcirc$}$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{0.1736pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\ast$}$}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip 5.0pt}}b,1\rangle,\ 1\in QH^{0}(M;\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

The degeneration of a family of rational surface automorphisms
Qitong Jiang

Abstract
. We consider a one-dimensional family of rational surfaces with automorphisms discussed in [ 8 , 3 , 4 ] . In a degeneration of this family, the limiting map is the identity map on a special fiber. We check that the map on the total space of the family has indeterminacy in the special fiber. However, we show that after blowing-up at an indeterminate curve, there is an induced birational map on the exceptional divisor over the indeterminate curve. Moreover, we show that this map has dynamical degree λ = 16 \lambda=16 .



1.
Introduction

Given an automorphism
f:X\rightarrow X
of an algebraic variety
X
, if we blow-up
X
at a subvariety
V
with
f(V)=V
, then
f
induces an automorphism
\tilde{f}|_{E}:E\rightarrow E
of the exceptional divisor
E
. This map does not have any new dynamical properties, as it fibers over the original map
f|_{V}:V\rightarrow V
. On the other hand, if
f(V)=V^{\prime}\neq V
, then the lift
\tilde{f}
on
\Bl_{V}{X}
simply contracts
E
.

In this paper, we will be concerned with the situation when
f:X\dashrightarrow X
is a birational map and
V
is contained in
\Ind(f)
. In this case, interesting dynamics are possible on
E\subset\Bl_{V}(X)
. In particular, we focus on cases related to degenerations of rational surface automorphisms. Let
T
be an elliptic curve and
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times T
. Given
t\in T
, there is an involution
\iota_{t}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\{t\}
associated with
t
and
T
constructed in
[
8
,
3
,
4
]
. Fix a non-trivial
2
-torsion point
q\in T
. We have the maps
\varphi,\psi,\chi:X\dashrightarrow X
defined by

\varphi(p,t)=(\iota_{q}(p),t),\quad\psi(p,t)=(\iota_{t}(p),t),\quad\chi(p,t)=(\iota_{-t}(p),t),

where
-t
is the inverse of
t
. The map
\vartheta=\varphi\circ\chi\circ\varphi\circ\psi
can be viewed as a family of birational maps of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
:

\{\iota_{q}\circ\iota_{-t}\circ\iota_{q}\circ\iota_{t}:t\in T\}.

For a general
t\in T
, the map
\vartheta_{t}:X_{t}\dashrightarrow X_{t}
, the restriction of
\vartheta
to the fiber
X_{t}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\{t\}
over
t
, can be regularized to an automorphism on a higher birational model of
X_{t}
, and the dynamical degree of
\vartheta_{t}
is
\lambda(\vartheta_{t})=17+12\sqrt{2}
, as computed from its action on divisors (cf. Lemma 3.3 in
[
4
]
; see also
[
5
]
); whereas the dynamical degree of
\vartheta_{q}
is
\lambda(\vartheta_{q})=1
as
\vartheta_{q}=\iota_{q}^{4}=\id_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
. As the family degenerates to the identity on the fiber
X_{q}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\{q\}
,
\vartheta
develops indeterminate curves inside
X_{q}
. In fact, the interesting dynamics do not completely disappear as the family degenerates; rather, they become concentrated along the indeterminate curves. To see this, we blow-up
X
at the indeterminate curves and find that the induced map on the exceptional divisor has dynamical degree
\lambda=16
.

Here is the outline of the discussion:

•

In Section
3
, we exhibit families of birational maps of rational surfaces whose general member can be regularized as an automorphism. We study the indeterminate curves in the fiber over
q
of the corresponding threefold map by factoring the map as a sequence of automorphisms and Atiyah flops.

•

In Section
4
, we find induced self-maps of the exceptional divisors over the identified indeterminate curves in the special fiber over
q
.

•

In Section
5
, we study the dynamical properties of the induced self-maps of the exceptional divisors.

In particular, we show the following result.

Theorem 1
.

There exists a curve
\mathfrak{q}\subset X_{q}
which is indeterminate for the map
\vartheta=\varphi\circ\chi\circ\varphi\circ\psi
. Let
\Bl_{\mathfrak{q}}{X}
be the blow-up of
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times T
at
\mathfrak{q}
with exceptional divisor
E_{\mathfrak{q}}
. There is a birational self-map of
E_{\mathfrak{q}}
induced by
\vartheta
, and the map has dynamical degree
\lambda=16
.

As a warm-up, let us consider several examples of birational self-maps of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
where the induced maps on exceptional divisors can be easily obtained. In particular, we blow-up
\mathbb{P}^{3}
at the line

defined by

to get

, i.e.,
\tilde{f}=\pi^{-1}\circ f\circ\pi
, and the induced maps
\tilde{f}|_{E}
on the exceptional divisor

over

. The exceptional divisor

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
on
\Bl_{L}\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Example 1
.

Given

, let
f:\mathbb{P}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
be the automorphism defined by the formula

[w:x:y:z]\mapstochar\rightarrow[bw:x:y:z].

Then

. The induced map

can be written as

([u:v],[s:t])\mapstochar\rightarrow([bu:v],[s:t]).

In the affine chart
\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}

, this is a dilation in the

-diration:

(u,s)\mapstochar\rightarrow(bu,s).

Example 2
.

For a slightly more complicated example, let

be the birational map given by

[w:x:y:z]\mapstochar\rightarrow[x^{2}w:x^{3}:x^{2}y:x^{2}y-ayw^{2}+x^{2}z],

is defined on a dense open subset of the line

([u:v],[s:t])\mapstochar\rightarrow([u:v],[v^{2}t-au^{2}t+v^{2}s:v^{2}t]).

We note that in the affine chart where

, this is an “area-preserving” bend:

(u,s)\mapstochar\rightarrow(u,1-au^{2}+s).

Example 3
.

In general, given a birational map
f:X\dashrightarrow X
, if

is a subvariety of codimension

and
f(V)\neq V
, then the lifted map on
\Bl_{V}{X}
simply contracts
E
. Let
f:\mathbb{P}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
be the automorphism

[w:x:y:z]\mapstochar\rightarrow[y:z:w:x].

Then the image of the line

is not

will contract the exceptional surface

Example 4
.

Suppose that
V
is a subvariety of codimension
\geq 2
that lies in

, and we blow-up
\mathbb{P}^{3}
at
V
. We consider when there is an induced birational map on the exceptional divisor. For example, let

be the composition of the automorphism:

[w:x:y:z]\mapstochar\rightarrow[y:z:w:x]

[w:x:y:z]\mapstochar\rightarrow[xyz:wyz:wxz:wxy].

The line

is contained in the indeterminacy locus of

, whose formula can be written as

([u:v],[s:t])\mapstochar\rightarrow([s:t],[u:v]).

We note that, in the affine chart where

Example 5
.

We can actually obtain a Hénon map on the exceptional divisor
E
by composing the maps introduced in Examples
1

2

4

is undefined along the line

For our main discussion, we recall some preliminaries.

2.
Groundwork

We assume that the base field
K
is algebraically closed of characteristic
\Char{K}=0
.

2.1.
Relative numerical equivalence

Let
X
be a smooth variety over
K
, and let
p:X\rightarrow S
be a proper morphism onto a variety
S
. We define
Z_{1}(X/S)
to be the free abelian group generated by reduced irreducible curves which are mapped to points by
p
. There is a numerical equivalence
\equiv_{S}
in
\Pic(X)
defined by

D\equiv_{S}D^{\prime}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad D\cdot C=D^{\prime}\cdot C\text{ for all }C\in Z_{1}(X/S).

We write
N^{1}(X/S):=(\Pic(X)/\equiv_{S})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. When
S=\Spec{K}
, we drop
/S
from the notation.

2.2.
Blow-up and exceptional locus

The blow-up of a variety
X
at a subvariety
V
will be written as
\Bl_{V}X
. Let
\pi:\Bl_{V}X\rightarrow X
be the projection map. The exceptional divisor
\pi^{-1}(V)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(N_{V/X})
, where
N_{V/X}
is the normal bundle to
V
in
X
. We denote
\pi^{-1}(V)
by
E_{V}
. We can thus identify the points on
E_{V}
with elements in
\mathbb{P}(N_{V/X})
.

We will frequently consider sequences of blow-ups throughout the paper. To simplify notations, we use the same notation for a subvariety
V
and all of its strict transforms.

2.3.
Dynamical degree and algebraic stability

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
k
, and let
f:X\dashrightarrow X
be a birational map. There exists a smooth projective variety
\widetilde{X}
and proper morphisms
\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
and
\sigma:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
such that the diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

If
D\in\Div(X)
is a divisor, then we define its
pullback
(under
f
) by
f^{*}(D):=\pi_{*}\sigma^{*}(D)
and its
pushforward
by
f_{*}(D):=\sigma_{*}\pi^{*}(D)
. Now let
H\in\Div(X)
be an ample divisor. We define the
(
H
-)degree
of
f
to be

\deg_{H}(f):=H^{k-1}\cdot f^{*}(H).

The
(first) dynamical degree
of
f:X\dashrightarrow X
is defined to be

\lambda(f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\deg_{H}(f^{n})\right)^{1/n}.

The fact that the above limit exists and is independent of the choices of
H
is proved in
[
6
,
12
]
.

We focus on the case when
X
is a smooth projective surface. In this case, we have that
(f^{-1})_{*}=f^{*}
and
(f^{-1})^{*}=f_{*}
and that

(2.1)

\deg_{H}(f)=H\cdot f^{*}(H)=f_{*}(H)\cdot H=\deg_{H}(f^{-1}).

So
\lambda(f)
can also be defined using pushforward and is the same as
\lambda(f^{-1})
. Let us also consider the induced linear maps

f^{*}:N^{1}(X)\rightarrow N^{1}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad f_{*}:N^{1}(X)\rightarrow N^{1}(X).

The birtional map
f:X\dashrightarrow X
is said to be
algebraically stable
if

(f^{n})^{*}=(f^{*})^{n}\quad(\text{or equivalently }(f^{n})_{*}=(f_{*})^{n})

for any integer
n\geq 0
. The following theorem is useful in practice.

Theorem 2

(Proposition 1.13 and Theorem 1.14 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface, and let
f,g:S\dashrightarrow S
be birational maps. Then

(g\circ f)_{*}=g_{*}f_{*}

if and only if there is no irreducible curve
C\subset S
such that
f(C)\subset\Ind(g)
. Moreover,
f:S\dashrightarrow S
is algebraically stable if and only if there is no irreducible curve
C\subset S
and
n\geq 0
such that
f(C)\subset\Ind(f^{n})
.

We use the following theorem to compute the dynamical degree for an algebraically stable birational self-map of a surface.

Theorem 3

(Corollary 1.19 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface, and let
f:S\dashrightarrow S
be an algebraically stable birational map. Then
\lambda(f)
coincides with the spectral radius of
f_{*}:N^{1}(S)\rightarrow N^{1}(S)
.

Proposition 4
.

The normal bundle

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
, and the natural birational map
\phi:\widetilde{X}\dashrightarrow\widetilde{X}^{+}
is an Atiyah flop with the flopping curve

.

Proof.

On
\widehat{X}:=\Bl_{B}{X}
, the curve

is a fiber of the exceptional divisor

. So the self-intersection number of

as a curve on the surface

is
0
. The total transform of

on
\widetilde{X}
is irreducible, so it is also the strict transform of

. In fact, it is isomorphic to the blow-up of
E_{B}\subset\widehat{X}
at a point on the fiber

. Therefore, the strict transform
\mathfrak{b}
of
b
, considered as a curve on the surface
E_{B}\subset\widetilde{X}
(the strict transform of
E_{B}
), has self-intersection number
-1
. If we can show that
K_{\widetilde{X}}\cdot\mathfrak{b}=0
, by the Remark 5.2 (a) in
[
11
]
, there is an exact sequence

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow N_{\mathfrak{b}/\widetilde{X}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0;

and this shows that

.

The canonical divisor of

is

K_{\widetilde{X}}=\pi_{2}^{*}\pi_{1}^{*}K_{X}+\pi_{2}^{*}E_{B}+E_{C}

By the push-pull formula,

is trivial on

, and

. Moreover, since the curve

. To compute the intersection number

.

. In the rest of the paper, we will show that the dynamical complexity persists but is concentrated around the indeterminacy locus. Once we blow-up at the indeterminate curves and then look at the induced maps on the exceptional divisors, the interesting dynamics will be seen again.

As for the composition of four involutions
\vartheta=\varphi\circ\chi\circ\varphi\circ\psi
, i.e., the family

. We will also blow-up at the indeterminate curves of

in the fiber over

. The interesting dynamics will be recovered on the exceptional divisors too.

First- and Second-Moment Constrained Gaussian Channels
Shuai Ma 1 2 3 and Michèle Wigger 1

Abstract
This paper studies the channel capacity of intensity-modulation direct-detection (IM/DD) visible light communication (VLC) systems under both optical and electrical power constraints. Specifically, it derives the asymptotic capacities in the high and low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regimes under peak, first-moment, and second-moment constraints. The results show that first- and second-moment constraints are never simultaneously active in the asymptotic low-SNR regime, and only in few cases in the asymptotic high-SNR regime. Moreover, the second-moment constraint is more stringent in the asymptotic low-SNR regime than in the high-SNR regime.

Introduction

The ever-increasing number of wireless devices and high-speed communication requirements cause a spectrum scarcity of conventional radio-frequencies (RF). A promising solution
is visible light communication (VLC) with its abundant unlicensed spectrum
[
1

2

In particular, when utilizing the simple and practical intensity modulation–direct detection (IM/DD) technology, transmitters directly modulate information
onto the real, non-negative optical intensity of the VLC signals (in contrast to RF signals which modulate the complex field) and receivers apply photodetectors to measure incoming optical intensities.
For eye safety reasons and hardware limitations,
both the
maximum and average optical intensities of VLC transmit signals typically have to be restricted.
Since these apply directly to the intensities, they impose both peak and
first-moment constraints
on the transmit signals.
Additional
second-moment constraints
are imposed by limitations of the electronic circuits that control the transmit signal, such as the boundedness of the linear amplification regime and electric power consumption

A close-form expression for the capacity of such IM/DD systems is still unknown, even when some of the first or second-moment constraints are relaxed. However, bounds and asymptotic results in the high- and low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regimes are known under certain relaxations.
For example,
various upper and lower bounds on the capacity,
as well as its exact high- and low-SNR asymptotics, have been derived under only a first-moment constraint without a second-moment constraint
[
9
,

,

,

In this work, we derive the exact expressions for the asymptotic high- and low-SNR capacities under peak, first-moment,
and
second-moment constraints. Our results show that in the asymptotic low-SNR regime, only one of the two moment constraints is stringent. Specifically, the second-moment constraint is active if the peak-constraint
A
times the first-moment-constraint
\alpha_{1}\textnormal{A}
exceeds this second-moment constraint
\alpha_{2}\textnormal{A}^{2}
, and otherwise the first-moment constraint is active. This can be seen as a consequence of the optimality of on-off keying in the asymptotic low-SNR regime. Our results further show that for most constraint-parameters
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
also in the high-SNR regime, only one of the moment-constraints is active. Interestingly, the second-moment constraint is inactive over a larger region of
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
-pairs in the high-SNR regime than in the low-SNR regime, and the first-moment constraint over a smaller region. An additional second-moment constraint is thus more restrictive in the low-SNR regime than in the high-SNR regime. In the asymptotic high-SNR regime, we further observe a small region of
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
-pairs where both moment-constraints are simultaneously active and limit the asymptotic capacity.

II
Channel Model

Consider a typical VLC communication link, where the transmitter is equipped
with a single LED or laser and the receiver with a single photodetector.
The photodetector measures the incoming light intensity, which can be modeled as

\displaystyle Y={x}+Z,

(1)

where
{x}
denotes the input signal produced by the transmitter’s LED or laser, and
Z
is standard additive white Gaussian noise independent of
{x}
.
Note that, in contrast to the input
{x}
, the output
Y
can be negative.

Inputs
{x}
are subject to both a peak and an
average optical power (average-intensity) constraints:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle[0,\textnormal{A}],

(2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\textnormal{A},

(3)

for some fixed parameters
\textnormal{A}>0
and
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)

Due to battery limitations on the attached RF circuit and power amplifier limitations, the
second moment
of the transmit signal also has to be restricted:

\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{2}\right]\leq\alpha_{2}\textnormal{A}^{2}.

(4)

We denote the capacity of the channel (
1

peak power
A
, maximum average power
\alpha_{1}\textnormal{A}

\alpha_{2}\textnormal{A}^{2}

\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A})

[
16

\displaystyle\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A})=\sup_{P_{X}}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{I}(X;Y),

(5)

where the supremum is over input laws
P_{X}
satisfying (
2
)–(
4
).

Notice that, for any random variable
X\in[0,\textnormal{A}]
, we have
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{2}\right]\leq\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right]\textnormal{A}
and of course
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right]\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{2}\right]}
. Therefore, whenever
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
, the second moment constraint (
4
) is inactive in view of the first moment-constraint (
3
), and whenever
\sqrt{\alpha_{2}}<\alpha_{2}
, the first moment constraint (
3
) is inactive in view of the second moment-constraint (
4
). Moreover, for any
\alpha_{1}\geq 1/2
, the first-moment constraint (
3
) is not active, and for
\alpha_{2}\geq 1/2
, the second-moment constraint (
4
). In fact, by the symmetry of the Gaussian density, for any input
X
, we have
\mathop{}\!\textnormal{I}(X;Y)=\mathop{}\!\textnormal{I}(X^{\prime};Y)
for the derived input
X^{\prime}=\textnormal{A}-X
, which has smaller first and second moments than
X
if
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right]\geq 1/2\textnormal{A}

\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{\prime}\right]=\textnormal{A}-\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right]\leq 1/2\textnormal{A}\leq\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right],

(6)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{\prime 2}\right]=\textnormal{A}^{2}-2\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right]\textnormal{A}+\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{2}\right]\leq\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{2}\right].

(7)

We can thus limit the optimization in (
5
) to random variables
X
with first moments not exceeding
1/2\textnormal{A}
and, by
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X^{2}\right]\leq\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[X\right]\textnormal{A}
, with second moments not exceeding
1/2\textnormal{A}^{2}
.

As a consequence:

\displaystyle\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A})=\textnormal{C}(1,\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A}),\qquad\forall\alpha_{1}\geq\max\{\sqrt{\alpha_{2}},1/2\},

(8)

and

\displaystyle\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A})=\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},1,\textnormal{A}),\qquad\forall\alpha_{2}\geq\max\{\alpha_{1},1/2\}.

(9)

In the remainder of the paper, we present bounds on the capacities, and establish the exact asymptotic results in the high and low SNR regimes, respectively.

The following functions will be used throughout the paper. For
i=0,1,2,3,4
, define:

\zeta_{i}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\int_{0}^{1}y^{i}e^{-\lambda_{1}y-\lambda_{2}y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y.

(10)

IV
The asymptotic low-SNR capacity

Consider now the asymptotic low-SNR regime, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are again kept fixed and
\textnormal{A}\to 0
.

Proposition 2

Given parameters
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\textnormal{A}\downarrow 0}\frac{\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A})}{\textnormal{A}^{2}}=\max_{T\in[0,1]\colon\begin{subarray}{c}\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T\right]\leq\alpha_{1}\\
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{2}\right]\leq\alpha_{2}\end{subarray}}\mathop{}\!\mathsf{Var}\left[T\right].

(16)

Proof:

The achievability follows directly from Prelov’s and Verdú’s classical result on the mutual information of peak-constrained channels
[
17
, Corollary 2]
. The converse follows by the well-known Gaussian max-entropy bound:

\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A})\leq\max\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathsf{Var}\left[X\right]}{\sigma^{2}}\right),

where the maximization is over random variables
X\in[0,1]
satisfying (
2
)–(
4
). Defining
T:=X/\textnormal{A}
and using that
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\log(1+bt)}{t}=b
, for any constant
b>0
, establishes the desired asymptotic converse bound.
∎

Lemma 3

The maximization in Proposition
2
is attained by a binary random variable
T\in\{0,\textnormal{A}\}
:

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in[0,1]\colon\\
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T\right]\leq\alpha_{1}\\
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{2}\right]\leq\alpha_{2}\end{subarray}}\textnormal{Var}(T)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\{0,\textnormal{A}\}\colon\\
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T\right]\leq\alpha_{1}\\
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{2}\right]\leq\alpha_{2}\end{subarray}}\textnormal{Var}(T)

(17)

Proof:

Fix
T
satisfying the conditions in the minimization and construct a new random variable
T^{\prime}\in\{0,\textnormal{A}\}
with
p_{\textnormal{A}}:=\Pr\left[T^{\prime}=\textnormal{A}\right]=\frac{\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{2}\right]}{\textnormal{A}^{2}}
and
\Pr\left[T^{\prime}=0\right]=1-p_{\textnormal{A}}
. Notice that
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[(T^{\prime})^{2}\right]=p_{\textnormal{A}}\textnormal{A}^{2}=\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{2}\right]
and

\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{\prime}\right]=p_{\textnormal{A}}\textnormal{A}=\frac{\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{2}\right]}{\textnormal{A}}\leq\frac{\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T\right]\cdot\textnormal{A}}{\textnormal{A}}=\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T\right].

(18)

The new random variable
T^{\prime}
thus also satisfies the conditions in the maximization, and moreover it has larger objective function (variance) than
T
because
\mathop{}\!\mathsf{Var}\left[T^{\prime}\right]=\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[(T^{\prime})^{2}\right]-(\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{\prime}\right])^{2}\geq\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[(T)^{2}\right]-(\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T\right])^{2}=\mathop{}\!\mathsf{Var}\left[T\right]
.
∎

Combining Proposition
2
with Lemma
3
establishes the desired low-SNR asymptotics.

Theorem 4

For any parameters
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
:

\lim_{\textnormal{A}\downarrow 0}\frac{\textnormal{C}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\textnormal{A})}{\textnormal{A}^{2}}=p^{*}(1-p^{*}),

(19)

where
p^{*}:=\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1/2\}
.

Proof:

By Lemma
3
:

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in[0,1]\colon\\
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T\right]\leq\alpha_{1}\\
\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}\left[T^{2}\right]\leq\alpha_{2}\end{subarray}}\textnormal{Var}(T)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{\textnormal{A}}\in[0,1]\colon\\
p_{\textnormal{A}}\leq\alpha_{1}\\
p_{\textnormal{A}}\leq\alpha_{2}\end{subarray}}p_{\textnormal{A}}(1-p_{\textnormal{A}}).

(20)

Since the function
t\mapsto t(1-t)
is continuous and monotonically increasing over
[0,1/2]
but monotonically decreasing over
[1/2,1]
, the maximum value is obtained for
p_{\textnormal{A}}=\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1/2\}
. Plugging this into Proposition
2
establishes the desired result.
∎

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\int_{0}^{\textnormal{A}}f_{X}^{*}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathop{}\!e^{-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\cdot\log\left(f_{Y}^{(2)}(Y)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathop{}\!e^{-\frac{(y-X)^{2}}{2}}\log\left(f_{Y}^{(2)}(Y)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right]

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\log{\sqrt{2\pi}e}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[\mathop{}\!\mathcal{Q}\left(X\right)+\mathop{}\!\mathcal{Q}\left(\textnormal{A}-X\right)\right].

(44)

Since

\displaystyle P_{f_{Y}^{*}}(\mathcal{I}^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathop{}\!e^{-\frac{(y-X)^{2}}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right]

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[\mathop{}\!\mathcal{Q}\left(X\right)+\mathop{}\!\mathcal{Q}\left(\textnormal{A}-X\right)\right]

(46)

we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 6

For the distribution in (
31
):

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{Y}^{*}}\left[Y|Y\in\mathcal{I}\right]\cdot P_{f_{Y}^{*}}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{Y}^{*}}\left[Y\right]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{\textnormal{A}^{2}}{2}}\right)

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[X\right]+\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{\textnormal{A}^{2}}{2}}\right)

(48)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{Y}^{*}}\left[Y^{2}|Y\in\mathcal{I}\right]\cdot P_{f_{Y}^{*}}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{Y}^{*}}\left[Y^{2}\right]=\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[X^{2}\right]+\sigma^{2}.

(49)

Proof:

The inequality in (
49
) follows simply because
Y^{2}\geq 0
with probability
1
. The inequality in (
47
) is proved as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{Y}^{*}}\left[Y|Y\in\mathcal{I}\right]\cdot P_{f_{Y}^{*}}(\mathcal{I})

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\textnormal{A}}f_{Y}^{*}(y)\cdot y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\textnormal{A}}\int_{0}^{\textnormal{A}}f_{X}^{*}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathop{}\!e^{-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\cdot y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[\int_{0}^{\textnormal{A}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathop{}\!e^{-\frac{(y-X)^{2}}{2}}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right]

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[X\left(1-\mathop{}\!\mathcal{Q}\left(X\right)-\mathop{}\!\mathcal{Q}\left(\textnormal{A}-X\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{X^{2}}{2}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(\textnormal{A}-X)^{2}}{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\operatorname{\textnormal{{E}}}_{f_{X}^{*}}\left[X\right]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\left(1-e^{-\frac{\textnormal{A}^{2}}{2}}\right),

(54)

where the last inequality holds because the
\mathop{}\!\mathcal{Q}\left(\cdot\right)
-function is positive and the exponential function monotonically increasing.
∎

We continue with our upper bound. By plugging these lemmas into (

) and choosing

VI-C

Distinction of the Four Cases

We now show that the case distinction proposed in the theorem partitions the set of all
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
-parameters and that the described choice of
\lambda_{1}^{*},\lambda_{2}^{*}
-parameters exists in each subset.
More specifically, we show that the proposed case distinction coincides with the case distinction that arises when minimizing the right-hand side of (
60
), i.e., the function

\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\log\zeta_{0}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})+\lambda_{1}\alpha_{1}+\lambda_{2}\alpha_{2},

(61)

over the choices
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
, and we show that the
\lambda_{1}^{*},\lambda_{2}^{*}
values given in the theorem are the minimizers of this function.

Consider the partial derivatives of this function:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Gamma}{\partial\lambda_{1}}=-\frac{\zeta_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{\zeta_{0}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}+\alpha_{1}

(62a)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Gamma}{\partial\lambda_{2}}=-\frac{\zeta_{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{\zeta_{0}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}+\alpha_{2},

(62b)

as well as its Hessian matrix

\displaystyle\mathbbm{H}\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{\partial\lambda_{1}^{2}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{\partial\lambda_{1}\partial\lambda_{2}}\\[8.61108pt]
\frac{\partial^{2}\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{\partial\lambda_{1}\partial\lambda_{2}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{\partial\lambda_{2}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})-\zeta_{1}^{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})&c(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\\
c(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})&\zeta_{4}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})-\zeta_{2}^{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\end{pmatrix},

(64)

where

c(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\zeta_{3}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})-\zeta_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\cdot\zeta_{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}).

(65)

Since for any pair
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
the Hessian
\mathbbm{H}\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is a two-by-two matrix with positive trace and determinant, all its eigenvalues are positive, and the Hessian itself is positive definite for all
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
. As a consequence, the function
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is jointly strictly convex in both arguments and the minimizer
(\lambda_{1}^{*},\lambda_{2}^{*})
of
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
, for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0
is accordingly obtained as follows, depending on the values of
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
:

1.

If both partial derivatives of
\Gamma
at the origin are strictly positive, i.e.,

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{1}(0,0)}{\zeta_{0}(0,0)}+\alpha_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}+\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

(66)

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{2}(0,0)}{\zeta_{0}(0,0)}+\alpha_{2}=-\frac{1}{3}+\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

(67)

then
\lambda_{1}^{*}=\lambda_{2}^{*}=0
.

2.

If for some
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}>0
the partial derivatives of
\Gamma
satisfy

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{1}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},0)}{\zeta_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},0)}+\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(68)

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{2}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},0)}{\zeta_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},0)}+\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

(69)

then
\lambda_{1}^{*}=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
and
\lambda_{2}^{*}=0
.

3.

If for some
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}>0
the partial derivatives of
\Gamma
satisfy

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{1}(0,\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}{\zeta_{0}(0,\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}+\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0

(70)

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{2}(0,\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}{\zeta_{0}(0,\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}+\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(71)

then
\lambda_{1}^{*}=0
and
\lambda_{2}^{*}=\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
.

4.

If for some
\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime}>0
the partial derivatives of
\Gamma
at
(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})
are both zero, i.e.,

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{1}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}{\zeta_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}+\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(72)

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta_{2}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}{\zeta_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})}+\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(73)

then
\lambda_{1}^{*}=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
and
\lambda_{2}^{*}=\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
.

Since the strictly convex function
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
has exactly one minimizing pair, combined with continuity considerations, this concludes the proof of the theorem.

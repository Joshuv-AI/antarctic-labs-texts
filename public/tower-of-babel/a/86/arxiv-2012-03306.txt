The possible temperatures for flows on a simple AF algebra
Klaus Thomsen

Abstract




1.
Introduction

A flow on a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is a continuous one-parameter group
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of automorphisms of
A
. In quantum statistical mechanics such a flow is sometimes interpreted as the time evolution of the observables of a physical system and the equilibrium states of the system are then given by states
\phi
of
A
that satisfy the trace-like condition

\phi(ab)=\phi(b\alpha_{i\beta}(a))

for all
a,b\in A
with
a
analytic for
\alpha
,
[
BR
]
. The number
\beta
for which this holds is interpreted as the inverse temperature of the system in the state given by
\phi
, and
\phi
is said to be a
\beta
-KMS state for
\alpha
. The
C^{*}
-algebra
A
of observables in such a model is often a UHF algebra and it is therefore of interest to determine the KMS states and the possible inverse temperatures which can occur for flows on a UHF algebra. It follows from work by Powers and Sakai in the 70’s,
[
PS
]
, that when the flow is approximately inner the set of possible inverse temperatures is the whole real line
\mathbb{R}
, and Powers and Sakai conjectured that all flows on a UHF algebra are approximately inner. That this is not the case was proved by Matui and Sato in
[
MS
]
, following work by Kishimoto on the AF algebra case,
[
Ki2
]
. It is therefore an open question which sets of real numbers can occur as the set of inverse temperatures for a flow on a UHF algebra. The set is closed for any flow and since a UHF algebra has a unique trace state which is automatically a
0
-KMS state, it must contain zero. These are the only known restrictions. It is the purpose with this paper to show that it is also the only restrictions, at least when one only considers non-negative
\beta
, and not only for flows on UHF algebras, but in fact on all simple infinite dimensional unital AF algebras.

In the examples of Kishimoto, and also in the examples of Matui and Sato, the set of possible inverse temperatures is as small as it can be, consisting only of
0
, cf. Remark
3.2
below. Here we will show that a variant of the method developed in
[
Th5
]
can be used to construct a flow on any infinite dimensional simple unital AF algebra such that the set of inverse temperatures is any given lower bounded closed set of real numbers containing zero. For this we use a method which is a natural extension of ideas and methods from work by Bratteli, Elliott, Herman and Kishimoto in
[
BEH
]
,
[
BEK
]
,
[
Ki2
]
, and by Matui and Sato in
[
MS
]
. As in these papers we depend on results from the classification of simple
C^{*}
-algebras. Besides the classification of AF algebras, which was fundamental already in
[
BEH
]
, we apply the recent work by Castillejos, Evington, Tikuisis, White and Winter in
[
CETWW
]
, where the classification results we use in turn are based on work by Elliott, Gong, Lin and Niu,
[
GLN
]
,
[
EGLN
]
, among others. However, when the algebra in question is UHF there is an alternative route to the desired result which we describe Remark
4.12
.

The paper contains some material which appeared in
[
Th5
]
; this is because I now consider
[
Th5
]
a forerunner to the present paper and it will not be submitted for publication. The results of the present paper answer Question 2.6 in
[
Th5
]
positively, but Question 6.2 in
[
Th4
]
only partially. What remains is to determine if it is possible to remove the condition that the set is bounded below. This restriction is annoying and probably not necessary. However, in applications and also to many mathematicians, the (inverse) temperatures are non-negative and then the restriction to the lower bounded case is of no significance.

In relation to this, one should observe the recent work by Christensen and Vaes,
[
CV
]
, in which they connect the theory of KMS states on
C^{*}
-algebras to the theory of group actions on spaces. They also provide two examples of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras on which there are flows with arbitrary KMS spectra. While it is certainly premature to suggest that this applies to all infinite dimensional unital simple
C^{*}
-algebras, it does seem appropriate to point out that we don’t really know.

2.
Statement of results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
be a unital infinite dimensional simple AF
C^{*}
-algebra. For each non-empty closed face
F
in the tracial state space
T(A)
of
A
and for each closed and lower bounded set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of real numbers containing
0
there is a
2\pi
-periodic flow
\alpha
on
A
such that

•

there is a
\beta
-KMS state for
\alpha
if and only if
\beta\in K
,

•

for
\beta\in K\backslash\{0\}
the simplex of
\beta
-KMS states for
\alpha
is affinely homeomorphic to
F
,

•

the simplex of
0
-KMS states for
\alpha
is affinely homeomorphic to
T(A)
, and

•

the fixed point algebra
A^{\alpha}
of
\alpha
is an
AF
algebra.

When
K
is also upper bounded, and hence compact,
\alpha
can be chosen such that the fixed point algebra
A^{\alpha}
of

is a simple
AF
algebra.

When

is not compact it is not possible to arrange that

is simple because when the set of inverse temperatures contains arbitrarily large numbers the flow must have ground states, and this is not possible for periodic flows whose fixed point algebra is simple, cf. Remark 3.5 in
[

]
.

By taking

to consist of a single extremal point in

we get the following

2\pi
-periodic flow
\alpha
on
A
such that

•

there is a
\beta
-KMS state for
\alpha
if and only if
\beta\in K
,

•

•

the simplex of
0
-KMS states for
\alpha

, and

•

the fixed point algebra

of
\alpha

AF

When

In the last section of the paper we combine Corollary

]

]

Recall that two compact Choquet simplexes are strongly affinely isomorphic when there is an affine bijection between them which restricts to a homeomorphism between the sets of extremal points. For Bauer simplexes this is the same as affine homeomorphism, but in general it is a weaker notion. See

]
.

.

Let
U
be a UHF algebra and let
K
be a closed and lower bounded set of real numbers containing

. Let

•

there is
\beta

.

.

Let
U
be a UHF algebra and let

\alpha

3.
Preparations

In this paper all
C^{*}
-algebras are assumed to be separable and all traces and weights on a
C^{*}
-algebra are required to be non-zero, densely defined and lower semi-continuous. Concerning weights and in particular KMS weights we shall use notation and results from Sections 1.1 and 1.3 in
[
KV
]
. Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\theta
a flow on
A
. Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. A
\beta
-KMS weight for
\theta
is a weight
\omega
on
A
such that
\omega\circ\theta_{t}=\omega
for all
t
, and

\omega(a^{*}a)\ =\ \omega\left(\theta_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(a)\theta_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(a)^{*}\right)\ \ \ \forall a\in D(\theta_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}})\ .

(3.1)

In particular, a
0
-KMS weight for
\theta
is a
\theta
-invariant trace. It was shown by Kustermans in Theorem 6.36 of
[
Ku
]
that this definition agrees with the one introduced by Combes in
[
C
]
.
It is because of the formulation given by (
3.1
), which was not available when
[
BEH
]
was written, that we are able to work with KMS weights throughout the present work. A bounded
\beta
-KMS weight is called a
\beta
-KMS functional and a
\beta
-KMS state when it is of norm
1
.

The first lemma can be considered as an updated version of a part of the discussion in Remark 3.3 of
[
BEH
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
an automorphism of
B
. Let
\widehat{\gamma}
be the dual action on
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
considered as a
2\pi
-periodic flow. For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the restriction map
\omega\mapsto\omega|_{B}
is a bijection from the
\beta
-KMS weights for
\widehat{\gamma}
onto the traces
\tau
on
B
with the property that
\tau\circ\gamma=e^{-\beta}\tau
. The inverse is the map
\tau\mapsto\tau\circ P
, where
P:B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}\to B
is the canonical conditional expectation.

Proof.

Let
p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots
be an approximate unit in
B
from the Pedersen ideal
K(B)
of
B
with the additional property that
p_{k}^{2}\leq p^{2}_{k+1}
for all
k
, cf.
[
Pe
]
. Let
u
be the canonical unitary multiplier of
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
such that
ubu^{*}=\gamma(b)
for
b\in B
, and let
\tau
be a trace on
B
with the property that
\tau\circ\gamma=e^{-\beta}\tau
. For
a,b\in B,\ n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that

P(p_{k}au^{n}p_{k}^{2}bu^{m}p_{k})=P(p_{k}bu^{m}p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{i\beta}(au^{n})p_{k})=0

unless
m=-n
. Set
a^{\prime}=p_{k}a\gamma^{n}(p_{k}),\ b^{\prime}=p_{k}b\gamma^{-n}(p_{k})
. Then
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in K(B)
, and by using Proposition 5.5.2 in
[
Pe
]
we find that

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(p_{k}bu^{-n}p_{k}\widehat{\gamma}_{i\beta}(p_{k}au^{n}p_{k}))=e^{-n\beta}\tau(p_{k}bu^{-n}p_{k}^{2}au^{n}p_{k})

\displaystyle=e^{-n\beta}\tau(b^{\prime}u^{-n}a^{\prime}u^{n})=\tau(\gamma^{n}(b^{\prime})a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tau(a^{\prime}\gamma^{n}(b^{\prime}))=\tau\circ P(p_{k}au^{n}p_{k}^{2}bu^{-n}p_{k})\ ,

showing that
\tau\circ P
is a
\beta
-KMS functional on
p_{k}(B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})p_{k}
for the restriction of
\widehat{\gamma}
. We shall use repeatedly that when
b\geq 0
in
B
we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau(p_{k}bp_{k})=\tau(b)\ ,

(3.2)

which follows from the lower semi-continuity of
\tau
since
\tau(p_{k}bp_{k})=\tau(\sqrt{b}p_{k}^{2}\sqrt{b})
and
\sqrt{b}p_{k}^{2}\sqrt{b}
increases to
b
as
k\to\infty
. Let
x\in D(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}})
. Then

\displaystyle\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\tau\left(p_{l}P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)p_{l}\right)\ .

We have shown above that
\tau\circ P
is a
\beta
-KMS functional on
p_{l}(B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})p_{l}
and when
l\geq k
this gives

\displaystyle\tau\left(p_{l}P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)p_{l}\right)=\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{l}xp_{k})\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{l}xp_{k})^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\ \tau\circ P((p_{l}xp_{k})^{*}p_{l}xp_{k})=\tau\circ P(p_{k}x^{*}p_{l}^{2}xp_{k})\leq\tau\left(p_{k}P(x^{*}x)p_{k}\right)\ .

By using (
3.2
) we conclude that

\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)\ \leq\ \tau\circ P(x^{*}x)\ .

Similarly,

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(x^{*}x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau\circ P(x^{*}p_{k}^{2}x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\tau\circ P(p_{l}x^{*}p_{k}^{2}xp_{l})\ .

When
l\geq k
,

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(p_{l}x^{*}p_{k}^{2}xp_{l})=\tau\circ P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{k}xp_{l})\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{k}xp_{l})^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau\circ P(p_{k}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{l}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}p_{k}\ \leq\tau\left(p_{k}P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})p_{k}\right),

and we find therefore that

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(x^{*}x)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau\left(p_{k}P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})p_{k}\right)=\tau\circ P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})\ .

We conclude therefore first that
\tau\circ P(x^{*}x)=\tau\circ P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})
, and then that
\tau\circ P
is a
\beta
-KMS weight.

Let
\omega
be a
\beta
-KMS weight for
\widehat{\gamma}
. Then
\omega(b^{*}b)=\omega(bb^{*})
for all
b\in B
because
\widehat{\gamma}_{-i\frac{\beta}{2}}(b)=b
. Using Riemann sum approximations to the integral

P(a)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\widehat{\gamma}_{t}(a)\ \mathrm{d}t\ ,

it follows from the
\widehat{\gamma}
-invariance and lower semi-continuity of

that

\omega\circ P(a)\leq\omega(a)

(3.3)

when
a\geq 0
in
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
\omega|_{B}
is densely defined since
\omega
is, and we conclude that
\tau=\omega|_{B}
is a trace on
B
. Let
a\geq 0
. Then

\omega\circ\gamma(a)=\omega(uau^{*})=e^{-\beta}\omega\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(u\sqrt{a})\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(u\sqrt{a})^{*}\right)=e^{-\beta}\omega(a)\ .

It follows that
\tau\circ\gamma=e^{-\beta}\tau
. It remains now only to show that
\omega=\omega\circ P
. Note that because
\omega\circ\widehat{\gamma}_{t}=\omega
we find for all

\omega(p_{k}bu^{n}p_{k})=\begin{cases}0,&\ n\neq 0\\
\omega(p_{k}bp_{k}),&\ n=0\end{cases}\ =\ \omega(p_{k}P(bu^{n})p_{k})\ ,

implying that
\omega(p_{k}\ \cdot\ p_{k})=\omega(p_{k}P(\ \cdot\ )p_{k})
. Let

,
a\geq 0
. Since

\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega(p_{k}ap_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega(p_{k}P(a)p_{k})=\ \omega(P(a))\ .

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
B
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
an automorphism of
B
. The restriction map
\omega\mapsto\omega|_{B}
is a bijection from the
\widehat{\gamma}
-invariant traces
\omega
on
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
onto the
\gamma
-invariant traces on
B
. The inverse is the map
\tau\mapsto\tau\circ P
, where
P:B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}\to B
is the canonical conditional expectation.

We remark that in general
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
can have traces that are not invariant under the dual action
\widehat{\gamma}
and hence are not determined by their restriction to
B
; also in cases where
B
is UHF and
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
is simple. For the present purposes it is crucial that we can circumvent this issue thanks to the following lemma which is suggested by the work of Matui and Sato in
[
MS
]
. In fact, most of it appears implicitly in
[
MS
]
.

Recall that a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is stable when
A\otimes\mathbb{K}\simeq A
, where
\mathbb{K}
is the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on an infinite dimensional separable Hilbert space. Recall also that a partially ordered group
(G,G^{+})
has large denominators when the following holds: For any
a\in G^{+}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is an element
b\in G
and an
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
nb\leq a\leq mb
, cf.
[
Ni
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
B
be a stable AF-algebra and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
. Assume that
K_{0}(B)
has large denominators. There is an automorphism
\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
such that

a)

\gamma^{\prime}_{*}=\gamma_{*}
on
K_{0}(B)
,

b)

the restriction map

\mu\ \mapsto\ \mu|_{B}

is a bijection from traces
\mu
on
B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
onto the
\gamma^{\prime}
-invariant traces on
B
,

c)

B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable; i.e
(B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathcal{Z}\simeq B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
where
\mathcal{Z}
denotes the Jiang-Su algebra,
[
JS
]
, and

d)

B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
is stable.

Proof.

The first step is to show that there is an isomorphism
\phi:B\to B\otimes\mathcal{Z}
such that
\phi_{*}={(\operatorname{id}_{B}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}})}_{*}
. Since
\mathcal{Z}
is strongly self-absorbing in the sense of Toms and Winter,
[
TW
]
, it suffices for this to show that
B
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. By Corollary 3.4 in
[
TW
]
it is enough to show that
eBe
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable for any non-zero projection
e\in B
, and hence by Theorem 2.3 in
[
TW
]
it is enough to show that
eBe
is approximately divisible. Now
K_{0}(eBe)
has large denominators since we assume that
K_{0}(B)
has, and it follows therefore from Lemma 4.4 in
[
Th1
]
that for any Bratteli diagram for
eBe
the multiplicity matrices describing the embedding of a given level into the following levels will have the property that the minimum of the non-zero entries increases to infinity. This implies that
eBe
is approximately divisible, cf.
[
BKR
]
. We deduce in this way the existence of
\phi
.

For the second step we use
[
Sa
]
to obtain an automorphism
\theta
of
\mathcal{Z}
with the weak Rohlin property; that is, with the property that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a sequence
\{f_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{Z}
such that

•

0\leq f_{n}\leq 1_{\mathcal{Z}}
for all
n
,

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}a-af_{n}=0
for all
a\in\mathcal{Z}
,

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta^{j}(f_{n})f_{n}=0
for
j=1,2,3,\cdots,k
, and

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau\left(1_{\mathcal{Z}}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\theta^{j}(f_{n})\right)=0
,

where
\tau
is the trace state of
\mathcal{Z}
. From the first step, applied twice, it follows that there is an isomorphism
\phi:B\to B\otimes\mathcal{Z}\otimes\mathcal{Z}
such that
\phi_{*}=(\operatorname{id}_{B}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}})_{*}
. Since
B
is stable there is also a
*
-isomorphism
\psi_{0}:B\to\mathbb{K}\otimes B
such that
{\psi_{0}}_{*}=(e\otimes\operatorname{id}_{B})_{*}
, where
e
is a minimal non-zero projection in
\mathbb{K}
. Set

\psi=\left(\psi_{0}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\right)\circ\phi:B\to\mathbb{K}\otimes B\otimes\mathcal{Z}\otimes\mathcal{Z}\

and

\gamma^{\prime}=\psi^{-1}\circ\left(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{K}}\otimes\gamma\otimes\theta\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\right)\circ\psi\ .

It follows from the Künneth theorem that
\psi_{*}=(e\otimes\operatorname{id}_{B}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}})_{*}
and hence
\left(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{K}}\otimes\gamma\otimes\theta\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{Z}}\right)_{*}\circ\psi_{*}=(e\otimes\gamma\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}})_{*}
. Thus
\gamma^{\prime}_{*}=\gamma
.

In the final step we verify b). For this, set

and similarly

(3.6)

(3.8)

In the following, when

&\leq\ \sqrt{\mu(q_{n}\otimes y^{2})}\|b\|\|z\|\sqrt{\mu(q_{n}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}})}\ .\end{split}

\displaystyle\mu((q_{n}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}})(b\otimes\sqrt{g_{j}}z\sqrt{g_{j}})u^{k}(q_{n}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}))

4.
Proof of Theorem

4.1.
Setting the stage

Let

of

(K_{0}(A),K_{0}(A)^{+},[1])\ =\ (H,H^{+},u)\ .

Each element

\left\{\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathbb{R}):\ \phi(H^{+})\subseteq[0,\infty),\ \phi(u)=1\right\}

[

]
,

•

\theta(H)

, and

•

H^{+}\ =\ \left\{h\in H:\ \theta(h)(x)>0\ \ \forall x\in\Delta\right\}\cup\{0\}
.

Set

and define an automorphism
\rho
of

such that

\rho\left((g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\right)\ =\left(g_{n+1}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\ .

(4.1)

The main difference between the two cases, compact and not compact, is in the definition of the ordering in

4.2.
The first construction

We assume now that the set

\Sigma\left(g\right)(x,t)\ =\ \sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta(g_{m})(x)t^{m}\ .

Let

For

Lemma 4.1
.

\operatorname{Span}\{g_{k,l}:\ (k,l)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
is dense in
\left\{h\in C_{\mathbb{R}}(L):\ h(1)=0\right\}

Proof.

Note that

. Let

satisfy that

, and let
\epsilon>0

.
∎

When

Lemma 4.3

where

and each
\beta

\omega=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{I}_{\beta}}\omega_{I}\

That

is a generalized gauge action means that there is a Bratteli diagram
\operatorname{Br}
and a map

defined on the set
\operatorname{Br}_{Ar}
of arrows in

in the following way. Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}

n

\operatorname{Br}
starting at the top vertex. Extend the map

to

F(\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}F(a_{i})\ ,

when

is made up of the arrows

. The Bratteli diagram

is chosen such that there are finite dimensional

-subalgebras
\mathbb{F}_{n}
in

spanned by a set of matrix units

\mathbb{F}_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{n+1}

The flow
\alpha^{1}
is defined such that

\alpha^{1}_{t}\left(E^{n}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\right)=e^{i(F(\mu)-F(\mu^{\prime}))t}E^{n}_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\ .

See
[
Th4
]
. In order to ensure that

is

-periodic it is necessary to arrange that

only takes integer values. That this is possible follows by inspection of the proof in
[
Th4
]

[
Th4
]
, where some real numbers

are chosen. The only crucial property of these numbers is that they must be sufficiently big and they may therefore be chosen to be natural numbers. The resulting potential
F
will then be integer-valued. Let
\alpha^{2}
be a flow on

with the properties specified for
\alpha
in Corollary
2.2
and set

\alpha_{t}=\alpha^{1}_{t}\otimes\alpha^{2}_{t}\ ,

2.3
. Since a
\beta
-KMS state for
\alpha

\beta
-KMS state for
\alpha^{2}
on the tensor factor

2.2
that there are no
\beta
-KMS states for
\alpha
unless

\beta

. Consider therefore a

-KMS state

,
\beta\neq 0

-analytic. Then

Detectable Abundance of Cyanoacetylene ( HC 3 ​ N \text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N} ) Predicted on Reduced Nitrogen-Rich Super-Earth Atmospheres Journal: ApJL Argo ( Rimmer & Helling 2016 ) , petitRADTRANS ( Mollière et al. 2019 ) , PandExo ( Batalha et al. 2017 )
Paul B. Rimmer, Liton Majumdar, Akshay Priyadarshi, Sam Wright, S. N. Yurchenko

Abstract
We predict that cyanoacetylene ( HC 3 ​ N \text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N} ) is produced photochemically in the atmosphere of GJ 1132 b in abundances detectable by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), assuming that the atmosphere is hydrogen dominated and rich in molecular nitrogen ( N 2 \text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ), methane ( CH 4 \text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} ) and hydrogen cyanide ( HCN ), as described by Swain et al. 2021 . First, we construct line list and cross-sections for HC 3 ​ N \text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N} . Then we apply these cross-sections and the model atmosphere of Swain et al. 2021 to a radiative transfer model in order to simulate the transmission spectrum of GJ 1132 b as it would be seen by JWST, accounting for the uncertainty in the retrieved abundances. We predict that cyanoacetylene features at various wavelengths, with a clear lone feature at 4.5 μ \mu m, observable by JWST after one transit. This feature persists within the 1 − σ 1-\sigma uncertainty of the retrieved abundances of HCN and CH 4 \text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} . The signal is detectable for stratospheric temperatures ≲ 600 \lesssim 600 K and moderate stratospheric mixing ( 10 6 ​ cm 2 ​ s − 1 ≲ K z ​ z ≲ 10 8 ​ cm 2 ​ s − 1 10^{6}\,{\rm cm^{2}\,s^{-1}}\lesssim K_{zz}\lesssim 10^{8}\,{\rm cm^{2}\,s^{-1}} ). Our results also indicate that HC 3 ​ N \text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N} is an important source of opacity that future retrieval models should consider.



I
Introduction

Cyanoacetylene (
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
) is a linear molecule composed of two triple-bonded carbons bound at one end with a hydrogen atom and at the other with a nitrile group: carbon and nitrogen joined by a triple-bond.
{}{}{}{}{}{}{}\mathrm{H}{-}\mathrm{C}\!\equiv\mathrm{\!}\,\mathrm{C}{-}\mathrm{C}\!\equiv\mathrm{\!}\,\mathrm{N}
. Because of its two energetic bonds, it is both physically stable and highly reactive in aqueous media
(
Ferris et al. 1968
)
. It acts as both a molecular backbone and a source of chemical energy for prebiotic chemical synthesis
(
Powner et al. 2009
;
Becker et al. 2019
)
. It can be produced from radical reactions between the cyano radical and acetylene and is a major product of the Miller-Urey synthesis
(
Miller 1953
;
Sanchez et al. 1966
)
. There is extensive discussion among prebiotic chemists about whether
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
is prebiotically plausible
(
Orgel 2002
)
.

\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
has been observed in the atmosphere of Titan from the ground, both in the gas phase
(
Bézard et al. 1992
)
, and condensed into crystalline aerosol
(
Khanna 2005
)
. According to retrieval from ALMA observations, the abundance of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
in the atmosphere of Titan is at about 100 ppb at 0.1 to 1 mbar, and drops by orders of magnitude with increasing pressure
(
Thelen et al. 2019
)
.

Experimentally, the IR intensities of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
was studied in
Bénilan et al. 2006
;
Douin et al. 2015
. High-resolution spectroscopic analyses were reported by
Bizzocchi et al. 2017
;
Jiang et al. 2021
.

Swain et al. 2021
analyzed of Hubble data of GJ 1132 b transmission spectra and find 0.5% concentrations of both
HCN
and
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
in an atmosphere with low mean molecular mass, implying the atmosphere is hydrogen-dominated.
Swain et al. 2021
report that the atmosphere may be volcanic, and their photochemical models predict significant amounts of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
in the upper atmosphere. The analysis of
Swain et al. 2021
has been challenged by
Mugnai et al. 2021
and
Libby-Roberts et al. 2021
, who find no evidence of molecular features in the Hubble data. The analysis of
Swain et al. 2021
and response by
Mugnai et al. 2021
and
Libby-Roberts et al. 2021
are the latest in an elaborate and conflicting literature attempting to determine the presence and nature of an atmosphere of GJ 1132 b
(
Schaefer et al. 2016
;
Southworth et al. 2017
;
Diamond-Lowe et al. 2018
, e.g.)
.

Regardless of whether GJ 1132 b has a reduced atmosphere, there is theoretical support for the persistence of volcanically derived highly reduced atmospheres on rocky exoplanets, owing to the persistence of elemental iron in the mantle
(
Lichtenberg 2021
)
. It is possible that many Super Earth atmospheres resemble a warm Titan composition.

For this paper, we assume that the atmosphere reported by
Swain et al. 2021
is the atmosphere of GJ 1132 b. Based on this assumption, we predict that
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
is present in the upper atmosphere of GJ 1132 b in abundances detectable by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), and is an indication of a reduced atmosphere with a significant fraction of chemically active nitrogen and carbon species. In Section
II
, we describe the method for constructing a line list of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
and the radiative transfer model and synthetic JWST pipeline used to calculate the transmission spectra. We present our results in Section
III
, and discuss the promise and challenges for observing
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
on GJ 1132 b, and the application of this work beyond GJ 1132 b, in Section
IV
.

II
Methods

In order to make predictions about
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
in the atmosphere of GJ 1132 b, we first predict the atmospheric composition as a function of atmospheric height (Section
II.1
) and then develop a line list and cross-sections for
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
(Section
II.2
), to model the transmission spectrum of GJ 1132 b (Section
II.3
). Finally, we simulate how that spectrum will look if observed by JWST (also Section
II.3
).

II.1
Atmospheric Model

Swain et al. 2021
use the
Argo
model
(
Rimmer & Helling 2016
)
with the
Stand2020
chemical network
(
Rimmer & Rugheimer 2019
;
Rimmer et al. 2021
)
and fixed surface conditions to predict atmospheric chemical profiles.
Argo
is a Lagrangian code that solves the photochemistry-transport equation:

\dfrac{dn_{{}\mathrm{X}}}{dt}=P_{X}-L_{X}n_{{}\mathrm{X}}-\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{X}}{\partial z},

(1)

where
n_{{}\mathrm{X}}
(cm
-3
) is the number density of species
X
,
t
(s) is time,
P_{X}
(cm
-3
s
-1
) is the production rate of
X
,
L_{X}
(s
-1
) is the destruction rate of
X
, and
\partial\Phi_{X}/\partial z
(cm
-3
s
-1
) accounts for the vertical transport.

We take the chemical profiles of
Swain et al. 2021
as given. This is a self-consistent photochemical model atmosphere, and so the predictions of each of the species are interconnected. Such an atmosphere cannot be rich in carbon dioxide, for example, because carbon dioxide is not a predicted thermochemical or photochemical product in such a reduced atmosphere. We also explore the sensitivity of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
to the concentrations of
HCN
and
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
. To do this, we take the
\pm 1-\sigma
errors for the retrieved abundances of
HCN
and
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, from
Swain et al. 2021
, and apply or model to predict the effect on
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
.

II.2
Line list and cross sections / k-tables for Cyanoacetylene

\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}

\mu

\nu_{3}+\nu_{6}

,

To study and analyse the detectability of molecular absorption features in the spectrum from JWST both for a clear and cloudy atmosphere, a noise simulator PandExo

IV
Discussion and Conclusion

In this letter, we show that, if GJ 1132 b has observable amounts of
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
HCN
in its atmosphere, as reported by
Swain et al. 2021
, we predict it will have up to 0.5% of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
in its upper atmosphere due to photochemistry. Applying radiative transfer and accounting for the properties of JWST, we predict that cyanoacetylene (
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
) will be observable by JWST, given what we presently know about the molecule.

There is some uncertainty about the
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
line list (limited coverage of vibrational bands), but these uncertainties are small compared to the uncertain composition of the atmosphere, with retrieved
HCN
and
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
abundances spanning more than an order of magnitude and the predicted
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
abundances spanning a factor of 30. These uncertainties were incorporated into the spectra and we found that spectral features of the molecules remain detectable at
\gtrsim 0.1
% mixing ratios of
HCN
and
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, the low end of the
1-\sigma
error bars for these species retrieved abundances. More work will be needed, both experimental and theoretical, in order to make any strong claim about the shape and strength of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
features for temperatures
\gg 500
K, where otherwise energetically prohibitive and therefore yet unknown bands will be accessed, and the spectrum of
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
could be significantly different. As we have found, however,
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
abundance decreases to below detectable levels (concentrations of 10-100 ppm) at temperatures
\gtrsim 600
K, at least in the reducing rocky planet atmosphere we explore. For our bulk atmospheric composition,
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
may not be detectable at such high stratospheric temperatures because it will not survive in those temperatures, see Eq. (
). We found that
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
concentrations were far less sensitive to the eddy diffusion coefficient, remaining detectable between values of
10^{6}
cm
2
s
-1
and
10^{10}
cm
2
s
-1
.

Even if GJ 1132 b does not have
HCN
, there are many planets, including, plausibly, many rocky planets that may host hydrogen-rich atmospheres rich in
HCN

(
Tsiaras et al. 2016
)
. Super Earths also may host reduced atmospheres because of quenched mantle differentiation
(
Lichtenberg 2021
)
. For reduced atmospheres of rocky planets,
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
is an important opacity source that retrieval models should take into account.

By itself,
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
cannot be uniquely identified by observing one or two spectral features. Rather, it will be presence in the context of a hydrogen-rich exoplanet atmosphere. The probability that
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
is an explanation for a given spectral feature must be assessed in the context of other molecular constituents as indicated by the full transmission spectra, such as
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(inferred from the scale height),
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, and
HCN
. In this context,
\text{HC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{N}
would be a further indicator of the presence of
HCN
, and a probe into prebiotically relevant mechanisms taking place on uninhabitable planets. This may give a window into the time when our planet was hot and hydrogen-rich
(
Genda et al. 2017
)
, forming molecules that later may have been essential to the origins of life.

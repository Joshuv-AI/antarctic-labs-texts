Eigenbasis for a weighted adjacency matrix associated with the projective geometry B q ​ ( n ) B_{q}(n)
Murali K. Srinivasan

Abstract
In a recent article Projective geometries, Q Q -polynomial structures, and quantum groups Terwilliger (arXiv:2407.14964) defined a certain weighted adjacency matrix, depending on a free (positive real) parameter, associated with the projective geometry, and showed (among many other results) that it is diagonalizable, with the eigenvalues and their multiplicities explicitly written down, and that it satisfies the Q Q -polynomial property (with respect to the zero subspace). In this note we • Write down an explicit eigenbasis for this matrix. • Evaluate the adjacency matrix-eigenvector products, yielding a new proof for the eigenvalues and their multiplicities. • Evaluate the dual adjacency matrix-eigenvector products and directly show that the action of the dual adjacency matrix on the eigenspaces of the adjacency matrix is block-tridiagonal, yielding a new proof of the Q Q -polynomial property.

Introduction

Let
q
be a prime power and let

denote the

, i.e., the poset (under inclusion)
of all subspaces of
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}^{n}

{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-vector space of all column vectors

The set of
k

is denoted

-binomial coefficient

The

Let

\displaystyle A_{n}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }Y\subseteq X,\dim(X)=\dim(Y)+1,\\

\frac{\varphi-1}{q-1}q^{\dim(X)}&\mbox{if }X=Y,\\

Terwilliger
[

]

[

]
. Note

The topics studied include: diagonalizability, eigenvalues and

-polynomial property, interpretation of

-polynomial structure using the quantum group

.

(Terwilliger

The eigenvalues of

are

\frac{\varphi q^{n-k}-q^{k}}{q-1}\mbox{ with multiplicity }{{{{n}\choose{k}}_{q}}},\;\;k=0,1,\ldots,n.

-submodules is known

-submodule

\displaystyle A_{n}^{*}(E_{n}(k))

In a combinatorial study of vector spaces indexing sets for the objects
studied (bases, eigenvectors etc.) play an important role. The

The Goldman-Rota recurrence
for the Galois numbers (
[

,

]
) is the identity

\displaystyle G_{q}(n+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2G_{q}(n)+(q^{n}-1)G_{q}(n-1),\;n\geq 1,\;\;G_{q}(0)=1,\;G_{q}(1)=2.

(7)

More generally, we have the following identity,

\displaystyle{{{{n+1}\choose{k}}_{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{{{n}\choose{k}}_{q}}}+{{{{n}\choose{k-1}}_{q}}}+(q^{n}-1){{{{n-1}\choose{k-1}}_{q}}},\;n,k\geq 1,

(8)

with
{{{{0}\choose{k}}_{q}}}=\delta(0,k)
and

.
Note that (

Let

denote the set of all distinct irreducible characters (all of

denote the set of all distinct nontrivial irreducible characters

.

For

, inductively define a set

consisting of sequences
as follows (here

denotes the empty sequence):

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{q}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{()\},

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{q}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(0),(1)\},

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{q}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})\;|\;(\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{t-1})\in{\cal E}_{q}(n-1),\;\alpha_{t}\in\{0,1\}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})\;|\;(\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{t-1})\in{\cal E}_{q}(n-2),\;\alpha_{t}\in{\cal N}_{q}(n-1)\},\;n\geq 2.

Given
\alpha\in{\cal E}_{q}(n)
, let

( note that a nonzero entry is either 1 or

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{q}(n,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in{\cal E}_{q}(n)\;|\;N(\alpha)=k\},

\displaystyle e_{q}(n,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\cal E}_{q}(n,k)|.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle e_{q}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{q}(n,k)+e_{q}(n,k-1)+(q^{n}-1)e_{q}(n-1,k-1),\;n,k\geq 1,

with

,
the same recurrence (with the same initial conditions) as (

), Thus

and

.

Another relevant structure in the present situation is an inner product
(different from the standard inner product)
on the (complex) vector space of column vectors
with components indexed by
B_{q}(n)
.

For
X\in B_{q}(n)
with
\dim(X)=k
define

\pi(X)=\frac{\varphi^{k}q^{{{{k}\choose{2}}}}}{P_{q}(n)},

where
P_{q}(n)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+\varphi q^{k})
.
We have

\sum_{X\in B_{q}(n)}\pi(X)\;=\;\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi^{k}q^{{{{k}\choose{2}}}}{{{{n}\choose{k}}_{q}}}}{P_{q}(n)}\;=\;1

where the second equality follows by
the
q
-binomial theorem (so
\pi
is a probability vector on
B_{q}(n)
).
In the statistical literature the distribution
(p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
,
where
p_{k}=\frac{\varphi^{k}q^{{{{k}\choose{2}}}}{{{{n}\choose{k}}_{q}}}}{P_{q}(n)}
, is called Kemp’s distribution, a two parameter
analog of the binomial distribution (which corresponds to

\varphi=1
,
q=1
) (see, for example,
[

]
).

Given vectors

with components indexed by
B_{q}(n)
define

\displaystyle\langle{u},\,{v}\rangle_{\pi}=\sum_{X\in B_{q}(n)}\overline{u(X)}v(X)\pi(X).

(10)

Since
P_{q}(n)
is independent of
k
, for orthogonality of vectors we do not
need the denominator in the definition of
\pi(X)
. It is there only to make

\pi
a probability vector.

It follows from Lemma 10.1 in
[

]
(also see Section 2 of the
present paper) that

is self-adjoint with
respect to the inner product (
10
).

,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varphi^{k}q^{{{{k}\choose{2}}}}}\,A_{n}(X,Y)\,\sqrt{\varphi^{-r}q^{-{{{r}\choose{2}}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\phi^{k}q^{{{{k}\choose{2}}}}}\,q^{k}\,\sqrt{\phi^{-(k+1)q^{-{{{k+1}\choose{2}}}}}}=\sqrt{\varphi q^{k}}&\mbox{if $X\subseteq Y$ and $r=k+1$,}\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\varphi q^{\min\{\dim(X),\dim(Y)\}}}&\mbox{if $X\subseteq Y$ or $Y\subseteq X$, and $|\dim(X)-\dim(Y)|=1$,}\\
\frac{\varphi-1}{q-1}q^{\dim(X)}&\mbox{if $X=Y$,}\\

yielding a symmetric matrix.
\Box

\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{t-1})\in{\cal E}_{q}(n-1)
and
let
\chi\in{\cal N}_{q}(n)
.
Set
\alpha=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{t-1},\chi)\in{\cal E}_{q}(n+1)

and
X=X(\chi)
, where
X\in B_{q}(n,n-1)
. We have

v_{\alpha}=\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)(v_{\beta})
.

Let
Y\in B_{q}(X)
with
\dim(Y)=k
. We have

\langle{Y},\,{Y}\rangle_{n-1}\;=\;\frac{\varphi^{k}q^{{{{k}\choose{2}}}}}{P_{q}(n-1)}.

Now observe the following:
p(\chi)(\widehat{Y})
is a
linear combination of the elements of the orbit

[\widehat{Y}]
, whose cardinality is
q^{n-k}
.
The number of elements
g\in H(n+1,{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})
with
g\cdot\widehat{Y}=\widehat{Y}

is
q^{k}
and by Lemma
2.6
each such
g
satisfies
\chi(g)=1
.
So, for
Z\in[\widehat{Y}]
, if
g_{1}\cdot\widehat{Y}=g_{2}\cdot\widehat{Y}=Z
then

\chi(g_{1})=\chi(g_{2})
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle\langle{\lambda(\chi)(Y)},\,{\lambda(\chi)(Y)}\rangle_{n+1}\;=\;q^{-2k}\frac{\varphi^{k+1}q^{{{{k+1}\choose{2}}}}}{P_{q}(n+1)}\,q^{2k}q^{n-k}\;=\;\frac{\varphi q^{n}}{(1+\varphi q^{n-1})(1+\varphi q^{n})}\langle{Y},\,{Y}\rangle_{n-1}.

It follows from (
23
) that

\displaystyle\langle{\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)(u)},\,{\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)(v)}\rangle_{n+1}\;=\;\frac{\varphi q^{n}}{(1+\varphi q^{n-1})(1+\varphi q^{n})}\langle{u},\,{v}\rangle_{n-1},\;\;u,v\in{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n-1)].

(38)

From the isomorphism
\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)
we now see that

\{v_{\alpha}\;|\;\beta\in{\cal E}_{q}(n-1)\}

is an orthogonal basis of
W(\chi)
.

That completes the proof.
\Box

4
Action of
{\cal A}_{n}^{*}
on the eigenspaces of
{\cal A}_{n}

For
k=0,1,\ldots,n
define
E_{n}(k)\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n)]
to be the eigenspace of

{\cal A}_{n}
with eigenvalue
\frac{\varphi q^{n-k}-q^{k}}{q-1}
. In this section we
consider the action of
{\cal A}_{n}^{*}
on the eigenspaces of
{\cal A}_{n}
and give a
new proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Any homogeneous subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n)]
is closed under the action of

{\cal A}_{n}^{*}
and so the subspaces
{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n)]
,
W(0)
,
W(\chi),\;\chi\in{\cal N}_{q}(n)
of
{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n+1)]
are all closed under
{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}
.

Note the following relations in
{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n)]\oplus W(0)
:

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n}^{*}(X),\;\;X\in B_{q}(n),

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(\theta_{n}(X))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}\theta_{n}({\cal A}_{n}^{*}(X)),\;\;X\in B_{q}(n).

It follows that

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v),\;\;v\in{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n)],

(39)

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(\theta_{n}(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}\theta_{n}({\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v)),\;\;v\in{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n)].

(40)

Let
\chi\in{\cal N}_{q}(n)
and
X=X(\chi)
. Then we have

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)(Y))=q^{-1}\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)({\cal A}_{n-1}^{*}(Y)),\;\;Y\in B_{q}(n-1).

It follows that

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)(v))=q^{-1}\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)({\cal A}_{n-1}^{*}(v)),\;\;v\in{\mathbb{C}}[B_{q}(n-1)].

(41)

We can now give the

Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof is by induction on
n
, the cases

n=0,1
being clear.

Consider a basis element
v_{\beta}\in{\cal B}_{n+1}
with
N(\beta)=k
. Then

v_{\beta}\in E_{n+1}(k)
. There are three cases to consider:

(i) The last element of the sequence
\beta
is 0:
We have
v_{\beta}=q^{k}v_{\alpha}+\theta_{n}(v_{\alpha})
, where

v_{\alpha}\in{\cal B}_{n}
with
N(\alpha)=k
. Then
v_{\alpha}\in E_{n}(k)
.

By the induction hypothesis we have

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k-1}^{k+1}u_{j},\;\;u_{j}\in E_{n}(j),\;j=k-1,k,k+1.

We have, by (
39
) and (
40
),

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(v_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(q^{k}v_{\alpha}+\theta_{n}(v_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{k}{\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v_{\alpha})+q^{-1}\theta_{n}({\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{k}\left\{\sum_{j=k-1}^{k+1}u_{j}\right\}+q^{-1}\left\{\sum_{j=k-1}^{k+1}\theta_{n}(u_{j})\right\}.

We consider the three terms on the right hand side separately.

(a) We have

\displaystyle q^{k}u_{k+1}+q^{-1}\theta_{n}(u_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}(q^{k+1}u_{k+1}+\theta_{n}(u_{k+1}))\in E_{n+1}(k+1),

by Lemma
3.3
(since
u_{k+1}\in E_{n}(k+1)
).

(b) We have

\displaystyle q^{k}u_{k}+q^{-1}\theta_{n}(u_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}(q^{k+1}u_{k}+\theta_{n}(u_{k})).

By the nonsingularity of the
2\times 2
matrix (
3
),
the vector above is in the span of the vectors

q^{k}u_{k}+\theta_{n}(u_{k})\in E_{n+1}(k)
and

\varphi q^{n-k}u_{k}-\theta_{n}(u_{k})\in E_{n+1}(k+1)
.

(c) We have

\displaystyle q^{k}u_{k-1}+q^{-1}\theta_{n}(u_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}(q^{k+1}u_{k-1}+\theta_{n}(u_{k-1})).

Similar to the subcase (b) above, the vector above is in the span of the vectors

q^{k-1}u_{k-1}+\theta_{n}(u_{k-1})\in E_{n+1}(k-1)
and

\varphi q^{n-k+1}u_{k-1}-\theta_{n}(u_{k-1})\in E_{n+1}(k)
.

That completes the proof of the first case.

(ii) The last element of the sequence
\beta
is 1:
We have
v_{\beta}=\varphi q^{n-k+1}v_{\alpha}-\theta_{n}(v_{\alpha})
, where

v_{\alpha}\in{\cal B}_{n}
with
N(\alpha)=k-1
. Then
v_{\alpha}\in E_{n}(k-1)
.

By the induction hypothesis we have

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k-2}^{k}w_{j},\;\;w_{j}\in E_{n}(j),\;j=k-2,k-1,k.

We have, by (
39
) and (
40
),

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(v_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n+1}^{*}(\varphi q^{n-k+1}v_{\alpha}-\theta_{n}(v_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi q^{n-k+1}{\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v_{\alpha})-q^{-1}\theta_{n}({\cal A}_{n}^{*}(v_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi q^{n-k+1}\left\{\sum_{j=k-2}^{k}w_{j}\right\}-q^{-1}\left\{\sum_{j=k-2}^{k}\theta_{n}(w_{j})\right\}.

We consider the three terms on the right hand side separately.

(a) We have

\displaystyle\varphi q^{n-k+1}w_{k-2}-q^{-1}\theta_{n}(w_{k-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}(\varphi q^{n-k+2}w_{k-2}-\theta_{n}(w_{k-2}))\in E_{n+1}(k-1),

by Lemma
3.3
(since
w_{k-2}\in E_{n}(k-2)
).

(b) We have

\displaystyle\varphi q^{n-k+1}w_{k-1}-q^{-1}\theta_{n}(w_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}(\varphi q^{n-k+2}w_{k-1}-\theta_{n}(w_{k-1})).

The vector above is in the span of the vectors

q^{k-1}w_{k-1}+\theta_{n}(w_{k-1})\in E_{n+1}(k-1)
and

\varphi q^{n-k+1}w_{k-1}-\theta_{n}(w_{k-1})\in E_{n+1}(k)
.

(c) We have

\displaystyle\varphi q^{n-k+1}w_{k}-q^{-1}\theta_{n}(w_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-1}(\varphi q^{n-k+2}w_{k}-\theta_{n}(w_{k})).

The vector above is in the span of the vectors

q^{k}w_{k}+\theta_{n}(w_{k})\in E_{n+1}(k)
and

\varphi q^{n-k}w_{k}-\theta_{n}(w_{k})\in E_{n+1}(k+1)
.

That completes the proof of the second case.

(iii) The last element of the sequence
\beta
is
\chi
, for some

\chi\in{\cal N}_{q}(n)
: Let
X=X(\chi)
. Then

v_{\beta}=\lambda(\chi)\mu(X)(v_{\alpha})\in W(\chi)
, where
N(\alpha)=k-1

and
v_{\alpha}\in E_{n-1}(k-1)
. The result now follows from Theorem

3.2
and (
41
).

\Box

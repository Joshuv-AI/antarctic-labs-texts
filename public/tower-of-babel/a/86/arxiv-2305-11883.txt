1.
Introduction

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
and the norm
||\cdot||
. Let
A:H\rightarrow H

be an arbitrary unbounded positive selfadjoint operator acting in
H
with the domain of definition
D(A)
.
Suppose that
A
has a complete orthonormal system of eigenfunctions

\{v_{k}\}
and a countable set of positives
eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
. It is convenient to assume that
the eigenvalues do not decrease as their number increases, i.e.

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\cdot\cdot\cdot\rightarrow+\infty
.

For vector functions (or just functions)

h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow H
fractional integrals and derivatives
are defined similarly with scalar functions and known formulas and
properties are preserved
[
23
]
. Recall that fractional integrals of order
\sigma<0
of the
function
h(t)
defined on
[0,\infty)
has the form (see, for example,
[
1
]
)

(1.1)

J_{t}^{\sigma}h(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\sigma)}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\frac{h(\xi)}{(t-\xi)^{\sigma+1}}d\xi,\hskip 9.24994ptt>0,

provided the right-hand side exists. Here
\Gamma(\sigma)
is
Euler’s gamma function. Using this definition one can define the
Caputo fractional derivative of order
\rho\in(0,1)
:

D_{t}^{\rho}h(t)=J_{t}^{\rho-1}\frac{d}{dt}h(t).

Note that if
\rho=1
, then the fractional derivative coincides with
the ordinary classical derivative of the first order:
D_{t}h(t)=\frac{d}{dt}h(t)
.

Let
C[0,T]
be the set of continuous functions defined on
[0,T]
with the standard max-norm
||\cdot||_{C[0,T]}
and let
C(H)=C([0,T];H)
stand
for a space of continuous
H
-valued functions
h(t)
defined on
[0,T]
, and equipped with the norm

||h||_{C(H)}=\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}||h(t)||.

Let
\rho\in(0,1)
be a fixed number. Consider the following Cauchy problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}&(D_{t}^{\rho})^{2}u(t)+2\alpha D_{t}^{\rho}u(t)+Au(t)=f(t),0<t\leq T;\\
&\underset{t\to 0}{\mathop{\lim}}\,D_{t}^{\rho}u(t)={{\varphi}_{0}},\\
&u(0)={{\varphi}_{1}},\\
\end{cases}

where
f(t)\in C(H)
and
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}
are known elements of
H
.

Definition 1.1
.

If function
u(t)
with the properties

(D_{t}^{\rho})^{2}u(t),Au(t)\in C((0,T];H)
and
u(t),D^{\rho}_{t}u(t)\in C(H)
and satisfying conditions
(
1.2
) is called
the
solution
of the forward problem.

In order to formulate the main results of this paper, for an arbitrary real number
\tau
we introduce the power
of operator
A
, acting in
H
according to the rule

A^{\tau}h=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\tau}h_{k}v_{k}.

The domain of definition of this operator has the
form

D(A^{\tau})=\{h\in H:\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2\tau}|h_{k}|^{2}<\infty\}.

It’s immediate from this definition that
D(A^{\tau})\subset D(A^{\sigma})
for any
\tau\geq\sigma
.

For elements of
D(A^{\tau})
we define the norm

||h||^{2}_{\tau}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2\tau}|h_{k}|^{2}=||A^{\tau}h||^{2},

and together with this norm
D(A^{\tau})
turns into a Hilbert
space.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\varphi_{0}\in H
and
\varphi_{1}\in D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
.Further, let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
be any fixed number
f(t)\in C([0,T];D(A^{\epsilon}))
. Then the forward problem has a unique solution.

Moreover, there is a constant
C>0
such that the following stability estimate

||(D_{t}^{\rho})^{2}u||+||D_{t}^{\rho}u||+||Au||\leq C\bigg[t^{-\rho}\big(||\varphi_{0}||+||\varphi_{1}||_{\frac{1}{2}}\big)+\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}||f(t)||_{\epsilon}\bigg],\hskip 9.24994ptt>0,

holds.

The telegraph equation first appeared in the work of Oliver Heaviside in 1876. When simulating the passage of electrical signals in marine telegraph cables, he obtained the equation

u_{tt}+au_{t}+bu-cu_{xx}=0,

where
a,b
are nonnegative constants and
c
is a positive constant (see, e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
). Then specialists came to this equation when modeling various physical processes. A small overview of various applications of the telegraph equation is given in
[
4
]
.
For example, it appears in the theory of superconducting electrodynamics, where it describes the propagation
electromagnetic waves in superconducting media (see, e.g.
[
5
]
). In
[
4
]
, the propagation of digital and analog signals through media which, in general, are
both dissipative and dispersive is modeled using the telegraph equation. Some applications of the telegraph equation to the theory of random walks are contained in
[
6
]
. Another field of application of the telegraph equation is the biological sciences (see, e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
).

In recent decades, fractional calculus has attracted the attention of many mathematicians and researchers as non-integer derivative operators have come to play a larger role in describing physical phenomena, modeling more accurately and efficiently than classical derivatives
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Various forms of the time-fractional telegraph equation was considered by a number of researchers (see e.g.
[

]
,
[

[

In works

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some information about Mittag-Leffler functions, differential and integral equations, which we will use in the following sections.

For
0<\rho<1
and an arbitrary complex number
\mu
, by
E_{\rho,\mu}(z)
we denote the Mittag-Leffler function of complex argument
z
with two
parameters:

(2.1)

E_{\rho,\mu}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\rho k+\mu)}.

If the parameter
\mu=1
, then we have the classical
Mittag-Leffler function:
E_{\rho}(z)=E_{\rho,1}(z)
.
Prabhakar (see,
[
21
]
) introduced the function
E^{\gamma}_{\rho,\mu}(z)
of the form

(2.2)

E^{\gamma}_{\rho,\mu}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\gamma)_{k}}{\Gamma(\rho k+\mu)}\cdot\frac{z^{k}}{k!},

where
z\in C
,
\rho
,
\mu
and
\gamma
are arbitrary positive constants, and
(\gamma)_{k}
is the Pochhammer
symbol. When
\gamma=1
, one has
E^{1}_{\rho,\mu}(z)=E_{\rho,\mu}(z)
. We also have

(2.3)

E^{2}_{\rho,\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{\rho}\big[E_{\rho,\mu-1}(z)+(1-\rho+\mu)E_{\rho,\mu}(z)\big].

Since
E_{\rho,\mu}(z)
is an analytic function of
z
, then it is bounded for
|z|\leq 1
. On the other hand the well known asymptotic estimate of the
Mittag-Leffler function has the form (see, e.g.,

[
20
]
, p. 133):

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be an arbitrary complex number. Further let
\beta
be a fixed number, such that
\frac{\pi}{2}\rho<\beta<\pi\rho
, and
\beta\leq|\arg z|\leq\pi
. Then the following asymptotic estimate holds

E_{\rho,\mu}(z)=-\frac{z^{-1}}{\Gamma(\rho-\mu)}+O(|z|^{-2}),\,\,|z|>1.

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
2.1
one has

|E_{\rho,\mu}(z)|\leq\frac{M}{1+|z|},\hskip 9.24994pt|z|\geq 0,

where
M
-constant, independent of
z
.

We also use the following estimate for sufficiently large
\lambda>0
and
\alpha>0
,
0<\epsilon<1
:

(2.4)

|t^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\mu}(-(\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-\lambda})t^{\rho})|\leq\frac{t^{\rho-1}M}{1+\sqrt{\lambda}t^{\rho}}\leq M\lambda^{\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}}t^{2\epsilon\rho-1},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0,

which is easy to verify. Indeed, let

\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}t^{-1}=\lambda^{\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{-\epsilon}t^{-1}\leq\lambda^{\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}}t^{2\rho\epsilon-1}.

If
\rho>0

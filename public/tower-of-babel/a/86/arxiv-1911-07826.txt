Non-Hilbert Banach spaces with the self-extension property
Juan Bosco García-Gutiérrez, Francisco Javier García-Pacheco, Paula Piniella, Fernando Rambla-Barreno

Abstract
. In 1992, Kiendi, Adamy and Stelzner investigated under which conditions a certain type of function constituted a Lyapunov function for some time-invariant linear system. Six years later, it was obtained that this property holds if and only if the Banach space enjoys the self-extension property. However, the knowledge of these spaces needed to be extended in order to make useful this characterization, since there were little information on which classic Banach spaces satisfy this property and its relations with other classic properties. We present the self-extension property in a wider frame, relating it to other well-known spaces as the 1 1 -injective or 1 1 -projective ones. We investigate the property in two important low-dimensional classic Banach spaces: ℝ 1 3 \mathbb{R}_{1}^{3} and ℝ 1 4 \mathbb{R}_{1}^{4} . We introduce the concept of k − k- self-extensible spaces and a discussion of the stability of the property. We also show that every real Banach space of dimension greater than or equal to 3 3 can be equivalently renormed to fail the self-extension property. Finally, we summarize some consequences of our study and mention some open questions which appear naturally.

(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\ \|_{1})

Just note that

5.
k-
self-extension and stability

We def
ine a couple of concepts that allow us to study the stability of the self-extension property.

Def
inition 5.1

(
A “self-extension coef
f
icient”).

Let
X
be a Banach space, we say
X
is
k-
self-extensible
if for every subspace
Y\subseteq X
and every linear, continuous operator
T:Y\to Y
there exists a linear, continuous
S:X\to X
such that
S|_{Y}=T
and
\|S\|\leq k\|T\|
. Additionally, we def
ine

se(X)=\inf\{k\geq 1:X\textrm{ is }k-\textrm{self-extensible}\}

We will say
se(X)=\infty
when there is no
k
such that
X
is
k-
self-extensible.

Note that examples
4.2
,
4.3
and
4.4
actually show that
se(\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1})\geq\frac{5}{4}
,
se(\mathbb{R}^{5}_{1})\geq\frac{4}{3}
and
se(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{1})\geq\frac{3}{2}
respectively.

Let us recall that the Banach-Mazur distance between two isomorphic Banach spaces
X
and
Y
can be def
ined as

d(X,Y)=\inf\{\|U^{-1}\|:U\in{\rm ISO}(X,Y),\|U\|=1\}

where
{\rm ISO}(X,Y)
stands for the set of isomorphisms from
X
to
Y
. It is well-known that
d
is symmetric.

Proposition 5.2

(
On stability of
k-
self-extension).

Let
X,Y
be isomorphic Banach spaces. It holds that
se(X)\leq se(Y)d(X,Y)^{2}
.

Proof.

We can assume
se(Y)<\infty
, otherwise it is trivial. Let
U:X\to Y
be an isomorphism such that
\|U\|=1
.
Let
Z\subseteq X
be a subspace and
T:Z\to Z
a linear, continuous operator. Then
W=U(Z)
is a subspace of
Y
and
UTU^{-1}:W\to W
is linear and continuous. Thus, there exists a linear, continuous extension
S:Y\to Y
of
UTU^{-1}
such that
\|S\|\leq se(Y)\|UTU^{-1}\|\leq se(Y)\|U^{-1}\|\|T\|
and
U^{-1}SU:X\to X
is an extension of
T
satisfying

\|U^{-1}SU\|\leq\|U^{-1}\|se(Y)\|U^{-1}\|\|T\|=se(Y)\|U^{-1}\|^{2}\|T\|.

We conclude by taking inf
imum on
U
.
∎

By using
1
-self-extendability, we can discuss some topological properties of the set of self-extensible spaces isomorphic to a given one. Namely,

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of Banach spaces isomorphic to
X
. Assume
\mathcal{C}
is pseudometrized by
\log d
and consider the sets

\mathcal{A}=\{Y\in\mathcal{C}:Y\textrm{ has the self-extension property, i.e. is }1-\textrm{self-extensible}\}

\mathcal{B}=\{Y\in\mathcal{C}:se(Y)=1\}

We have

•

\mathcal{B}
is closed and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
.

•

If
X
is f
inite-dimensional then
\mathcal{B}
is compact and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

The closedness of
\mathcal{B}
is due to Proposition
5.2
, whereas
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
is deduced from the def

Assume now that
X
is f

\mathcal{C}
is a compact metric space and therefore
\mathcal{B}
is compact. Finally, let
Y\in\mathcal{B}
and

be a subspace. If

is a continuous linear mapping then for each

, such that

. Since

is f

has a subsequence converging to some

.
∎

The self-extension property is not inherited to subspaces, not even to hyperplanes (already mentioned after Corollary

).

Although the self-extension property is not inherited to hyperplanes, it is nonetheless inherited to
1

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
Y

q

X

se(Y)\leq qse(X)
. Moreover, if
X

Proof.

Assume
X
is
k
-self-extensible for some
k\geq 1
. Let
Z
be a closed subspace of
Y
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(Z)
. There exists
R\in\mathcal{L}(X)
such that
\|R\|\leq k\|T\|
and
R|_{Z}=T
. If

is a
q
-projection onto
Y
, the mapping
S=P\circ R|_{Y}:Y\to Y
satisf

\|S\|\leq\|P\|\|R\|\leq q\|R\|\leq qk\|T\|
and
Sz=PRz=PTz=Tz
for every

.
∎

Since every
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{1}
is

and these spaces are all

-complemented in

Corollary 6.2
.

The real
\ell_{1}
fails the self-extension property, and moreover

\frac{3}{2}\leq se(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{1})\leq se(\mathbb{R}^{7}_{1})\leq\dots\leq se(\ell_{1})

We think that the previous inequalities are indeed equalities. In other words,

Conjecture 6.3
.

se(\ell_{1})=\frac{3}{2}

The

Theorem 6.4
.

Every real Banach space of dimension greater than or equal to three can be equivalently renormed to fail the self-extension property.

Proof.

Let
X
be a real Banach space with

of

7.
Finite self-extension property

We already know that
\ell_{\infty}
enjoys the self-extension property because it is
1
-injective (it is a
\mathcal{C}(K)
with
K
extremally disconnected). However, extensions of operators def
ined on closed subspaces of
\ell_{\infty}
can be easily constructed explicitly in virtue of the Hahn-Banach Extension Theorem. Indeed, if
Y
is a closed subspace of
\ell_{\infty}
and
S:Y\to Y
is linear and continuous, then it suf
f
ices to def
ine

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}T:&\ell_{\infty}&\to&\ell_{\infty}\\
&x&\mapsto&T(x):=(\gamma_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\end{array}

where, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma_{n}\in\left(\ell_{\infty}\right)^{*}
is a extension of
\delta_{n}|_{Y}\circ S\in Y^{*}
with the same norm, that is,
\gamma_{n}|_{Y}=\delta_{n}|_{Y}\circ S
and
\|\gamma_{n}\|=\left\|\delta_{n}|_{Y}\circ S\right\|
. Here,
\delta_{n}
stands for the coordinate evaluation functional on
\ell_{\infty}

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\delta_{n}:&\ell_{\infty}&\to&\mathbb{K}\\
&x&\mapsto&\delta_{n}(x):=x(n).\end{array}

Def
inition 7.1
.

A Banach space
X
is said to enjoy the f
inite self-extension property if for every f
inite dimensional subspace
Y
of
X
and every linear and continuous
T:Y\to Y
, there exists a continuous linear extension of
T
preserving its norm. In other words, there exists
S:X\to X
linear and continuous such that
S|_{Y}=T
and
\|S\|=\|T\|
.

Following the same ideas described at the beginning of this section, we will prove that
c_{0}
has the f
inite self-extension property.

Def
inition 7.2
.

A subspace
Y
of
\ell_{\infty}
is called
\delta
-null provided that
(\delta_{n}|_{Y})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
0
in
Y^{*}
.

Trivial examples of
\delta
-null subspaces of
\ell_{\infty}
are
\ell_{\infty}^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In fact, a standard compactness argument shows that the
\delta
-null subspaces of
c_{0}
are exactly its f
inite dimensional subspaces.

Theorem 7.3
.

If
Y
is a
\delta
-null subspace of
c_{0}
, then every
T\in\mathcal{L}(Y)
can be extended to a
S\in\mathcal{L}(c_{0})
preserving its norm. In particular,
c_{0}
satisf
ies the f
inite self-extension property.

Proof.

By the Hahn-Banach Theorem, we can extend every
\delta_{n}|_{Y}\circ T
to a
\gamma_{n}\in c_{0}^{*}
such that
\|\gamma_{n}\|=\|\delta_{n}|_{Y}\circ T\|\leq\|\delta_{n}|_{Y}\|\|T\|\leq\|T\|
. Consider

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}S:&c_{0}&\to&c_{0}\\
&x&\mapsto&S(x):=(\gamma_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.\end{array}

Observe the following:

•

S
is well-def
ined. Indeed,
|\gamma_{n}(x)|\leq\|\gamma_{n}\|\|x\|\leq\|\delta_{n}|_{Y}\|\|T\|\|x\|\to 0
.

•

S|_{Y}=T
. Indeed, it holds because
\gamma_{n}|_{Y}=\delta_{n}|_{Y}\circ T
.

•

\|S\|=\|T\|
. Indeed,
\|T\|=\|S|_{Y}\|\leq\|S\|\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\gamma_{n}\|\leq\|T\|
.

∎

Non-perturbative determination of collisional broadening and medium induced radiation in QCD plasmas
Guy D. Moore, Sören Schlichting, Niels Schlusser, Ismail Soudi

Abstract
We supply recently obtained results from lattice EQCD with the correct UV limit to construct the collisional broadening kernel C ⁡ ( b ⟂ ) C(b_{\perp}) in a QCD plasma. We discuss the limiting behavior of C ⁡ ( b ⟂ ) C(b_{\perp}) at small and large impact parameters b ⟂ b_{\perp} , and illustrate how the results can be used to compute medium-induced radiation rates.



1
Introduction

An important signal of quark gluon plasma formation during heavy-ion collisions is the suppression of highly energetic particles while they traverse the medium. These highly energetic partons then generate jets,
and it is the suppression of jets, and modifications in jet properties,
which we hope to use as a tool for understanding the medium produced in heavy ion collisions.

The theory community is in some agreement about the process for jet energy loss.
A high-energy particle traversing the medium undergoes a series of soft
scatterings which exchange transverse momentum with the medium.
(Here and throughout, “transverse” means transverse with respect to
the high energy particle’s propagation direction.)
The particle is constantly emitting virtual collinear radiation;
scattering of the particle or the emitted radiation with the medium
can force the radiation to become real. There are important coherence
effects in this process, originally recognized by Landau and Pomeranchuk

LP1
;
LP2
and by Migdal
Migdal1
.
These were explicated within QCD by Zakharov

Zakharov:1996fv
;
Zakharov:1997uu
;
Zakharov:1998sv

and by Baier et al
Baier:1996kr
;
Baier:1996sk
.

The physical picture which emerges from this analysis does not appear
to be in doubt. As a high-energy particle propagates through a medium
with a density matrix
|p\rangle\langle p|
,
a hard vertex generates an amplitude for the mixed state

|p\rangle\langle p-k,k|
. This state forms at transverse separation

b_{\perp}\hskip-3.0pt=0
but as time evolves,
it undergoes eikonalized propagation in the
transverse plane, diffusing in
{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}
but also receiving damping
due to medium interactions; the strength of this damping at transverse
separation
b_{\perp}
,
C(b_{\perp})
, is the Fourier transform of
the rate of transverse momentum broadening:

\displaystyle C(q_{\perp})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(2\pi)^{2}\mathrm{d}^{3}\Gamma}{\mathrm{d}^{2}q_{\perp}\,\mathrm{d}L}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle C(b_{\perp})

\displaystyle\equiv\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(1-e^{i\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}\right)C(q_{\perp})\,.

(2)

So
C(q_{\perp})
is the rate per unit path length and
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
range to exchange
transverse momentum
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
with the medium, and
C(b_{\perp})
is its
zero-subtracted Fourier transform.
A subsequent hard vertex can convert this mixed state to a real emission.
Almost all literature treatments are based on this framework, see
Ref.
Baier:2000mf
.
However, different treatments

Gyulassy:1999zd
;
Gyulassy:2000er
;
Gyulassy:2003mc

Wiedemann:2000za
;
Salgado:2003gb

Arnold:2002ja

Djordjevic:2008iz

CaronHuot:2010bp

differ dramatically in their simplifying assumptions and their
treatment of the medium interactions, see

Armesto:2011ht
.

Treatments also differ in their description of the medium.
One common approximation is to treat the medium as
many random, static, screened color centers:

C(q_{\perp})\propto\frac{1}{(q_{\perp}^{2}+m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2})^{2}}
.
Dynamical moving charges, treated to lowest order in perturbation
theory, are not much more complicated
Aurenche:2002pd
:

C(q_{\perp})\propto\frac{1}{q_{\perp}^{2}(q_{\perp}^{2}+m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2})}
.
It is also common to make the approximation of many individually
small scatterings, leading to transverse momentum diffusion:

C(b_{\perp})=\hat{q}\,b_{\perp}^{2}/4
.
But these treatments are either assumptions, models,
or low-order perturbative calculations for a medium
which is strongly coupled and where the behavior could have large
nonperturbative contributions even at quite large temperatures.
It would be better to have a treatment of the jet-medium interaction
based more firmly in QCD and with the possibility of including some
genuinely nonperturbative physics.

Recently, this possibility came much closer to reality.
Already more than 10 years ago, Casalderry-Solana and Teaney
showed how the collision kernel
C(b_{\perp})
can be rigorously
defined in terms of the behavior of certain null Wilson loops

CasalderreySolana:2007qw
, and Caron-Huot showed
how such null Wilson loops could be recast, for temperatures
well above
T_{c}
, in terms of modified Wilson loops in the
dimensionally reduced long-distance effective theory for QCD,
3D EQCD (3D QCD with adjoint scalars)
CaronHuot:2008ni
.
This theory can be solved nonperturbatively on the lattice,
allowing for the first time for genuine nonperturbative input
into the form of the jet-medium interaction.
In addition, after some important preliminary work

Panero:2013pla
;
DOnofrio:2014mld
;
Moore:2019lua
,
we recently presented detailed and continuum-extrapolated results
for
C_{\rm EQCD}(b_{\perp})
the impact-parameter space jet-medium interaction
rate within the theory of EQCD
Moore:2020wvy
.

The purpose of this paper is to take this result and to show
how it can be applied in a calculation of jet radiation.
There are two steps which are needed.
First, the work of Ref.
Moore:2020wvy
is within EQCD,
not within 3+1 dimensional real-time QCD. We need to complete
the matching between the theories, with the help of some work
by Arnold and Xiao
Arnold:2008vd
,
to extract
C(b_{\perp})
for QCD.
We do so in Section
2
.
Second, the lattice data for
C(b_{\perp})
covers a finite range
of separations, exists only at discrete
b_{\perp}
values, and has
errors. We need to connect it together with the asymptotic small
and large separation limits into a function which can really be
applied in a calculation.
Then, in Section
3
,
we illustrate how to use the results in a real calculation of
jet modification through medium interaction. For convenience we
carry this out within the AMY formalism

Arnold:2002ja
;
Jeon:2003gi
;
Schenke:2009gb
in a large medium; we will return to its application
in a finite-length medium in a future publication.
We conclude with a summary of our achievements and discuss future research questions that we would like to see addressed in Sec.
4
.
To make this work as helpful to the community as possible,
the arXiv version of this paper includes a simple code to generate our
C(b_{\perp})
function.

2
Non-perturbative broadening kernel

The way to UV-complete
C(b_{\perp})
, starting from an expression of a dimensionally reduced theory, was shown by
Ghiglieri:2018ltw
for
C(b_{\perp})
from
\mathcal{N}{=}4
super-Yang-Mills theory.
Following this example, we use the transverse momentum collision kernel
C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}(b_{\perp})
from lattice EQCD, measured at four different temperatures in
Moore:2019lgw
, and supply it with the correct short-distance behavior in order to promote it to
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
, the transverse collision kernel of the full theory. Subsequently, we provide analytical expressions for the asymptotic behavior in the large- and small-
b_{\perp}
limits, which is then used to construct an interpolation curve of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
that can be used to calculate radiative emission rates.

2.1
Matching for the transverse momentum broadening kernel

Since EQCD is a low-energy effective theory for full QCD, we know that the two of them should agree

In order to ensure the correct short-distance behavior and to keep the fully nonperturbative long-distance information from lattice EQCD at the same time, we schematically express

C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})=\left(C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})-C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}(b_{\perp})\right)+C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}(b_{\perp})\,.

(3)

The quantity in parenthesis is then computed as part

Such matching calculations are free of large IR effects

matching calculation is

2.2
Long-distance limit of
C(b_{\perp})

We first focus on the long-distance limit of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
, which turns out to be the less complex issue. Beyond the known area-law form of
C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}(b_{\perp})
, subleading asymptotic corrections are found to be important to match to the numerical data at the values of
b_{\perp}

Laine:2012ht

\frac{C_{\mathrm{QCD}}}{g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}}(b_{\perp})\xrightarrow{b_{\perp}\gg\;1/g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}}A+\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{EQCD}}}{g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{4}}g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}b_{\perp}+\frac{g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}C_{\mathrm{R}}}{\pi}\left[\frac{y}{4}\left(\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\right)+\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{8\pi^{2}g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}\right]\log(g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}b_{\perp})\,,

(8)

where

is the string tension of EQCD
Laine:2005ai
and

is a scale-setting fitting constant. The (subleading)
\log(\dots)
-part originates from the modified Fourier-transform of the
\frac{1}{q_{\perp}^{2}}
-term in the small-
q_{\perp}
expansion of (
5

Arnold:2008vd
. Since it is introduced by the matching procedure, this term does not show up in the IR limit of
C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}(b_{\perp})

Moore:2019lgw
. Meanwhile, we have re-expressed all occurrences of

in (
8

2.3
Short-distance limit of
C(b_{\perp})

Evaluating
C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}^{\mathrm{latt}}(b_{\perp})
at smaller
b_{\perp}
demands smaller lattice spacings
a
, as discretization errors of the operator occur in powers of
\frac{a}{b_{\perp}}

DOnofrio:2014mld
. As a consequence, the signal-to-noise ratio shrinks due to critical slowing down as one approaches
b_{\perp}\hskip-3.0pt\to 0
. Additionally, the leading term in the small-
b_{\perp}
-expansion of
C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}(b_{\perp})
is precisely an EQCD artifact and therefore cancelled by the matching, diminishing the physically relevant information further without reducing the noise. Thus, the urge for an effective analytical description is clear.

The short-distance limit of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
precisely gives rise to the momentum diffusion coefficient
\hat{q}

\frac{C_{\mathrm{QCD}}}{g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}}(b_{\perp})\xrightarrow{b_{\perp}\ll\;1/m_{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{1}{4}\frac{\hat{q}}{g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{6}}(g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}b_{\perp})^{2}\,,

(9)

where
\hat{q}
consists of a scale-dependent logarithm and constant part. Clearly, at very small
b_{\perp}
, the logarithm is supposed to be the dominant part, and can be extracted by a modified Fourier-transform of the UV limit of (
5
) as described below. However, in keeping only the leading logarithmic terms, we do not find a smooth connection to our data, even at the smallest-
b_{\perp}
data points. There are two ways to resolve this issue: Either computing data points at even smaller
b_{\perp}
from the lattice or improving on the analytical side of the limiting behavior. Option one is certainly possible but numerically extremely costly due to the requirement of finer lattices combined with critical slowing down. Option two is somewhat easier to realize and will therefore be further pursued.

By taking into account the constant part of the momentum diffusion coefficient,
\hat{q}_{0}
, which receives both perturbative and non-perturbative contributions, the short-distance limit of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
reads

\frac{C_{\mathrm{QCD}}}{g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}}(b_{\perp})\xrightarrow{b_{\perp}\ll\;1/m_{\mathrm{D}}}-\frac{C_{\mathrm{R}}}{8\pi}\frac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)}\left(-\frac{1}{2g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}+\frac{3y}{2}\right)(g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}b_{\perp})^{2}\log(g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}b_{\perp})+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\hat{q}_{0}}{g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{6}}(g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}b_{\perp})^{2}\,,

(10)

where we applied the resummation of subleading contributions to the screening mass
m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}
just as in the IR case.

\hat{q}_{0}
would be most straight-forwardly determined from a fit to a few of the smallest-
b_{\perp}
-data-points of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
. However, the cancellations between the EQCD result and the matching contribution make a naive fit numerically quite unstable and badly-constrained. Also, the lattice EQCD results at small
b_{\perp}
are strongly correlated among each other, which translates to
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
.
Determining
\hat{q}_{0}
directly from the lattice-EQCD-data and its correction from
\left(C_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{\rm pert}-C_{\mathrm{subtr}}^{\rm pert}\right)(b_{\perp})
separately turns out to be a numerically more robust procedure, leading to the results quoted in Tab.
1
. We note that the momentum broadening coefficient of EQCD has already been determined from a small-
b_{\perp}
-fit in
Moore:2019lgw
, also taking into account the correlation between the data points. We find that, together with the matching contribution, the values of
\hat{q}_{0}
in Tab.
1
are smaller than the lattice-EQCD results of
Moore:2019lgw
.

in Tab.

to the short-distance asymptotics (

4
Conclusion

Despite small values of the strong coupling
\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}
at large temperature, perturbative calculations of transport phenomena in
the QGP can receive large non-perturbative contributions due to the famous infrared problem of finite temperature QCD. In the present work, we investigated the impact of non-perturbative contributions on jet-medium interactions, by incorporating non-perturbative contributions to the collisional broadening kernel
C(b_{\perp})
, which determines the rate of medium induced splittings. We appended the non-perturbative data from lattice EQCD that dominates in the large- and intermediate-
b_{\perp}
-regime with the
correct small-
b_{\perp}
-limit via a matching calculation, where we subtracted
the perturbative small-
b_{\perp}
limit of
C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}(b_{\perp})
and replaced it with the perturbative
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
of full QCD in Sec.
2
.
Beyond the range of
b_{\perp}
in which lattice data is available, we provide analytical expressions for the short-distance and long-distance limits of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}(b_{\perp})
and reconstruct
C(b_{\perp})
as a function of
b_{\perp}
over the entire range of values, by interpolating between our data points with a sufficiently
smooth spline that recovers the two limiting cases. We find that for
T=250{\rm MeV}
and
500{\rm MeV}
the leading temperature dependence of
C(b_{\perp})
can be scaled out, such that
C(gTb_{\perp})/(g^{2}Tb_{\perp})
is approximately independent of the temperature, in the relevant regime explored in this study. In order to facilitate the use of
C_{\rm QCD}(b_{\perp})
in phenomenological studies of jet quenching, we provide C/C++ routines of the interpolating spline as part of the arXiv submission.

Subsequently, in Sec.
3
, we calculated the medium-induced splitting
rate based on our non-perturbatively determined
C(b_{\perp})
. For the sake of simplicity, we
restrict ourselves to the simpler case of medium induced splittings in an infinite medium, although we see no conceptual problem
in generalizing our approach to a medium of finite extent.
We further compared our non-perturbative splitting rates to a number of other common approximations in the field: the full rates using leading and next-to-leading order
C(b_{\perp})
, the simplified rates in the deep LPM regime,
and the Bethe-Heitler approximation. We find substantial deviations from all these cases in the physically relevant
ranges of energies and momentum fractions even though our rates reproduce all of the mentioned approximations deeper in the
respective limit. Our results make a compelling case for incorporating non-perturbative large-
b_{\perp}
physics into the
computation of medium-induced splitting rates and jet observables in the long run.
In the near future, a generalization of our approach to more realistic finite-medium considerations seems natural.
Furthermore, non-perturbative effects in the
longitudinal
momentum diffusion were recently calculated
Moore:2020wvy
and – after a similar matching
procedure as outlined for
C(b_{\perp})
above – await application in a subsequent, improved non-perturbative calculation of
medium induced splittings rates.

With this in hand, a computation of experimentally measurable quantities like
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
would be in reach,
allowing to see if non-perturbative jet-medium-interactions can indeed explain the suppression of
large-transverse-momentum jets in heavy-ion collisions compared to proton-proton collisions.

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{D}}{4\pi(q_{\perp}^{2}{+}m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2})}\left[\frac{3}{q_{\perp}^{2}{+}4m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}-\frac{2}{(q_{\perp}^{2}{+}m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2})}-\frac{1}{q_{\perp}^{2}}\right]\;.

and
C_{\rm subtr}^{\rm pert}(q_{\perp})
from (
6
) cancels the IR divergence of the hard contribution and the UV behavior of the soft NLO contribution.

Appendix B
Numerical implementation

Several strategies have been developed in the literature to obtain a numerical solution for the calculation of the inelastic splitting rates. We follow the strategy of
Anisimov:2010gy
, and provide a detailed outline of the procedure below.

B.1
Impact parameter space

In order to solve for the splitting function in Eq. (
15
), we switch from transverse momentum
\mathbf{p}_{\perp}
to transverse impact parameter
{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}
space according to

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})=\frac{1}{16P^{2}z^{2}(1{-}z)^{2}}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~e^{i{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}\mathbf{g}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})

(31)

such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})=16P^{2}z^{2}(1{-}z)^{2}~\int\!\mathrm{d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}~e^{-i{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}~\mathbf{f}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})

(32)

where for later convenience we have absorbed a pre-factor
16P^{2}z^{2}(1{-}z)^{2}
into the definition, we need to calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{ij}}{\mathrm{d}z}(P,z)=\alpha_{s}P_{ij}(z)\text{Im}\left[8\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}\cdot\mathbf{f}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})\right]

(33)

we get
\mathbf{p}_{\perp}\to-i\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}
and
\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}\to-\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}^{2}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{-2i}{16P^{2}z^{2}(1{-}z)^{2}}~\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left(M_{\rm eff}(z,P)-\frac{\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}^{2}}{2Pz(1{-}z)}\right)~\mathbf{f}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle+\left[C_{1}\bar{C}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})+C_{z}\bar{C}(z{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})+C_{1-z}\bar{C}((1{-}z){\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})\right]~\mathbf{f}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})\;,

where
\bar{C}(\mathbf{b})
denotes the elastic scattering rate in impact parameter space stripped of its color factor.

B.2
General strategy

Since for
{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}\to 0
the contributions from the collision term vanish, the structure of the solution near the origin is already contained in the ”free” solution
\mathbf{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})
. By matching the most singular terms near the origin one has

\displaystyle\frac{-2i}{16P^{2}z^{2}(1{-}z)^{2}}\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}^{2}}{2Pz(1{-}z)}~\mathbf{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})\;,

(35)

which yields

\displaystyle\lim_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}\to 0}\mathbf{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})=\frac{1}{8\pi Pz(1{-}z)}\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}{|{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}|^{2}}

(36)

as can be easily seen from considering the two dimensional version of Gauss law. Denoting
b_{\perp}=|{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}|
and expressing

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})={\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}f^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})\;,

(37)

such that component wise

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}^{i}\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}^{i}{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}^{j}f^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})={\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}^{j}\left(f^{(0)^{\prime\prime}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})+\frac{3}{b_{\perp}}f^{(0)^{\prime}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})\right)

(38)

we then have to solve

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{b_{\perp}}^{2}+\frac{3}{b_{\perp}}\partial_{b_{\perp}}-2Pz(1{-}z)M_{\rm eff}(z,P)\right]f^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})=0\;,

(39)

for
b_{\perp}>0
with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle\lim_{b_{\perp}\to 0}{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b)=\frac{1}{8\pi Pz(1{-}z)b_{\perp}^{2}}\;,\qquad\lim_{b_{\perp}\to\infty}{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})=0\;,

(40)

to determine the ”free” solution. By implementing the correct boundary conditions, one finds that the appropriate solution to Bessel’s equation is given by

{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})=\frac{1}{8\pi Pz(1{-}z)b_{\perp}^{2}}~\sqrt{2Pz(1{-}z)M(z,P)}b_{\perp}~K_{1}\Big(\sqrt{2Pz(1{-}z)M(z,P)}b_{\perp}\Big)\;.

(41)

where
K_{\nu}(z)
denotes the modified Bessel function of rank
\nu
and argument
z
. Note that the free solution
{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
is entirely real and hence does not contribute to the expression for the radiation rate.

Based on the ”free” solution
{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
, the solution
f_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
to the full evolution equation can be conveniently expressed as

\displaystyle f_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})={f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})+{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})\;,

(42)

where
{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
describes the modifications due to the elastic scattering kernel
\bar{\Gamma}(b_{\perp})
and satisfies the linear inhomogeneous differential equation

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{b_{\perp}}^{2}+\frac{3}{b_{\perp}}\partial_{b_{\perp}}-2Pz(1{-}z)M_{\rm eff}(z,P)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\phantom{\frac{1}{2}}{}+2iPz(1{-}z)\Big[C_{1}\bar{C}(b)+C_{z}\bar{C}(zb_{\perp})+C_{1{-}z}\bar{C}((1{-}z)b_{\perp})\Big]\right]{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2iPz(1{-}z)\Big[C_{1}\bar{C}(b_{\perp})+C_{z}\bar{C}(zb_{\perp})+C_{1-z}\bar{C}((1{-}z)b_{\perp})\Big]{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})\;.

(43)

Since the singular part of
f_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
is already captured by the contribution
{f}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
, one concludes that the function
{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
has to be regular in the vicinity of the origin, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{b_{\perp}\to 0}{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})=c_{1}\,.

(44)

Now as we have already seen from the free case, the above differential equation clearly features diverging solutions in the limit
b_{\perp}\to 0
. Hence the requirement that
{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
remains finite fixes one of the integration constants in the general solution of the differential equation. Similarly, the second integration constant can be determined from the requirement that the solution remains regular in the limit
b_{\perp}\to\infty
, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{b_{\perp}\to\infty}{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})=0\;.

(45)

Expressing the general solution of the linear inhomogeneous ODE as the general solution to the homogeneous ODE plus a special solution to the inhomogenous ODE and choosing the special solution to be regular for
b\to 0
, we can formally write

\displaystyle{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})=c_{\rm div}{f}^{(1){\rm hom,div}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})+c_{\rm reg}{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})+{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})

(46)

where the first coefficient
c_{\rm div}
multiplying the divergent contribution
{f}^{(1){\rm hom,div}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
is fixed as
c_{\rm div}=0
from the requirement that
{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
is regular. Conversely the second coefficient
c_{\rm reg}
can be fixed from the requirement that
{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})
remains finite in the limit
b_{\perp}\to\infty
, i.e.

\displaystyle c_{\rm reg}=-\lim_{b_{\perp}\to\infty}\frac{{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})}{{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp})}

(47)

Hence, if we choose to solve the differential equations with the following initial conditions in the limit
b\to 0

\displaystyle{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp}=0)

\displaystyle=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T\;,

\displaystyle{f^{\prime}}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp}=0)

\displaystyle=0\;,

(48)

\displaystyle{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b=0)

\displaystyle=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T\;,

\displaystyle{f^{\prime}}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp}=0)

\displaystyle=0\;,

(49)

we can exploit the fact that
{f}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(b)
and its derivatives do not diverge in the limit
b\to 0
to determine the relevant derivative at the origin (
\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}=2
) as

\displaystyle\text{Im}\left[8\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}\cdot\mathbf{f}_{(z,P)}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})\right]_{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}=0}=16\text{Im}~c_{1}=16~g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T~\text{Im}~c_{\rm reg}\;,

(50)

with
c_{\rm reg}
given by Eq. (
47
). By combining all the relevant pieces the splitting rate is then determined by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{ij}}{\mathrm{d}z}(P,z)=\frac{4}{\pi}~g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}TP_{ij}(z)~\text{Im}~c_{\rm reg}\,.

(51)

B.3
Numerical implementation

We solve the homogeneous and inhomogeneous ODE for
{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}
and
{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}
numerically using an adaptive fourth order Runge-Kutta scheme. We start at a finite value of
b_{\perp}=b_{\perp}^{\rm min}
, where the initial conditions for
{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}
are determined from the series expansion of the solution around
b_{\perp}=0
as

\displaystyle{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}(b_{\perp}^{\rm min})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T\left(1+\frac{1}{4}M_{\rm eff}Pz\bar{z}b_{\perp}^{\rm min\;2}\right)\;,

(52)

and similarly for the inhomogeneous solution
{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}
. Since the latter is sensitive to the elastic scattering kernel, different expansions have to be employed for the different interaction kernels. Specifically, for the LO potential, the small
b_{\perp}
expansion takes the form

\displaystyle{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}(b_{\perp}^{\rm min})

\displaystyle-{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}(b_{\perp}^{\rm min})=\frac{-ic(T)}{32\pi}(g_{\mathrm{s}}Tb_{\perp}^{\rm min})^{2}\ln(g_{\mathrm{s}}Tb_{\perp}^{\rm min})\left(C_{1}{+}z^{2}C_{z}{+}\bar{z}^{2}C_{\bar{z}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{i(g_{\mathrm{s}}Tb_{\perp}^{\rm min})^{2}}{128\pi}\left(\left[4\frac{\hat{q}_{0}}{4g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}T^{3}}+c(T)(4\ln(2)-3)\right](C_{1}+z^{2}C_{z}+\bar{z}^{2}C_{\bar{z}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\phantom{\frac{i(g_{\mathrm{s}}Tb_{\perp}^{\rm min})^{2}}{128\pi}}\hskip 15.00002pt{}+4c(T)\left(C_{1}\ln\tfrac{1}{2}+z^{2}C_{z}\ln\tfrac{z}{2}+\bar{z}^{2}C_{\bar{z}}\ln\tfrac{\bar{z}}{2}\right)\right)\;,

(53)

where
c(T)=-\frac{C_{\mathrm{R}}}{8\pi}\frac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)}\left(-\frac{1}{2g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}+\frac{3y}{2}\right)(g_{3\mathrm{d}}^{2}b_{\perp})^{2}
.
Based on the terms in the expansion, the starting value
b_{\perp}^{\rm min}
is then chosen as

b_{\perp}^{\rm min}=10^{-4}\text{min}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{M_{\rm eff}Pz\bar{z}}},\frac{1}{8\pi Pz\bar{z}},(g_{\mathrm{s}}T)^{-1},\frac{1}{\sqrt{(C_{1}+C_{z}z^{2}+C_{\bar{z}}\bar{z}^{2})(g_{\mathrm{s}}T)^{2}}}\right)\;,

(54)

such that higher order corrections are effectively negligible at double precision level. Starting from the initial conditions at
b=b_{\rm min}
, we then solve the differential equations for
{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}
and
{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}
, up to a maximal value of
b_{\perp}=b_{\perp}^{\rm max}
, which is chosen similarly according to the criterion that

|f^{(0)}(b_{\perp}^{\rm max})/{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}(b_{\perp}^{\rm max})|<10^{-16}\,.

(55)

Based on the solution, we then determine the constant
c_{\rm reg}
from the ratio

c_{\rm reg}=-\frac{{f}^{(1){\rm inhom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp}^{\rm max})}{{f}^{(1){\rm hom,reg}}_{(z,P)}(b_{\perp}^{\rm max})}\;.

(56)

Such that the differential radiation rate is ultimately given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{ij}}{\mathrm{d}z}(P,z)=\frac{4}{\pi}~g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}TP_{ij}(z)~\text{Im}~c_{\rm reg}\;.

(57)

Appendix C
Bethe-Heitler regime

We solve Eq. (
15
) perturbatively following an opacity expansion in the number of elastic scatterings, such that at leading order

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}^{(0)}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\frac{-2i\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\delta E(z,P,\mathbf{p}_{\perp})}=\frac{-4i\mathbf{p}_{\perp}Pz(1{-}z)}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\;,

(58)

\displaystyle\mu(z)^{2}

\displaystyle=(1{-}z)m^{2}_{\infty,(z)}+zm^{2}_{\infty,(1{-}z)}-z(1{-}z)m^{2}_{\infty,(1)}\;,

(59)

which is entirely imaginary and thus does not contribute to the splitting rate. Hence the first non-trivial contribution comes from

\displaystyle 2\mathbf{p}_{\perp}\mathbf{g}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})=\frac{2i\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\delta E(z,P,\mathbf{p}_{\perp})}\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\bar{C}(\mathbf{q}_{\perp})

\displaystyle\left\{C_{1}\left[\mathbf{g}^{0}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})-\mathbf{g}^{0}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}{-}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})\right]\right.

(60)

\displaystyle\left.{}+C_{z}\left[\mathbf{g}^{0}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})-\mathbf{g}^{0}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}{-}z\mathbf{q}_{\perp})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.{}+C_{1{-}z}\left[\mathbf{g}^{0}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})-\mathbf{g}^{0}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}{-}\bar{z}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})\right]\right\}.

By plugging in the leading order one finds

\displaystyle 2\mathbf{p}_{\perp}\mathbf{g}^{(1)}_{(z,P)}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})=

\displaystyle 16P^{2}z^{2}(1{-}z)^{2}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\bar{C}(\mathbf{q}_{\perp})~\frac{1}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu^{2}(z)}\times

\displaystyle\left\{C_{1}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle+C_{z}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-z\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-z\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\left.+C_{1-z}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right]\right\}\;,

(61)

such that the rate is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{ij}^{BH}}{\mathrm{d}z}(P,z)=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}TP_{ij}(z)\frac{1}{\pi}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T}~\bar{C}(\mathbf{q}_{\perp})~\frac{1}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}~

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt\times\left\{C_{1}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 120.0pt\left.+\,C_{z}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-z\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-z\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 120.0pt\left.+\,C_{1-z}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right]\right\}\,.

(62)

We perform the re-arrangement

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu^{2}(z)}~\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right]

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}-\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\mu(z)^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(64)

To re-write the terms in a manifestly positive definite form, we can re-express the rate as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{ij}^{BH}}{\mathrm{d}z}(P,z)=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}TP_{ij}(z)~Q^{BH}(z,m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2},m_{\infty}^{2})\;,

(65)

where
Q^{BH}(z,m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2},m_{\infty}^{2})
is a dimensionless integral given by

\displaystyle Q^{BH}(z,m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2},m_{\infty}^{2})=\frac{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}{2\pi g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\bar{C}(m_{\mathrm{D}}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})

\displaystyle\hskip 120.0pt\times\left\{C_{1}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}}-\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}}\right]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 140.0pt\left.+\,C_{z}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}}-\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-z\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-z\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}}\right]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 140.0pt\left.+\,C_{1-z}\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}}-\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}}\right]^{2}\right\}\,,

(66)

with
\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}=\mu(z)^{2}/m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}
. By re-scaling
\mathbf{p}_{\perp}
in the second and third term, the three terms can be expressed in terms of a single integral

Q^{BH}(z,m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2},m_{\infty}^{2})=C_{1}Q\Big(\tilde{\mu}^{2}(z)\Big)+C_{z}Q\Big(\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\Big)+C_{z}Q\Big(\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{2}(z)}{(1{-}z)^{2}}\Big)\;,

(67)

where

\displaystyle Q(\tilde{\mu}^{2})=\frac{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}{2\pi g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\bar{C}(m_{\mathrm{D}}\mathbf{q}_{\perp})\left[\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\tilde{\mu}^{2}}-\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})}{(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})^{2}+\tilde{\mu}^{2}}\right]^{2}\;.

(68)

Now evaluating
\tilde{\mu}(z)^{2}
for the different channels, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{g\to gg}(z)=(1{-}z)\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(g)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}+z\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(g)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}-z(1{-}z)\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(g)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}=\frac{1-z(1{-}z)}{2}\;,

(69)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{q\to gq}(z)=(1{-}z)\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(g)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}+z\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(q)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}-z(1{-}z)\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(q)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}=\frac{1-z}{2}+az^{2}\;,

(70)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{g\to qq}(z)=(1{-}z)\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(q)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}+z\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(q)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}-z(1{-}z)\frac{m^{2}_{\infty,(g)}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}=\frac{2a-z(1{-}z)}{2}\;,

(71)

where
a=m^{2}_{\infty,q}/m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}
, such that the rates can be compactly expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{g\to gg}^{BH}}{\mathrm{d}z}(P,z)=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}TP_{g\to gg}(z)\times

(72)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{C_{A}}{2}Q\left(\frac{1-z(1{-}z)}{2}\right)+\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}Q\left(\frac{1-z(1{-}z)}{2z^{2}}\right)+\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}Q\left(\frac{1-z(1{-}z)}{2(1{-}z)^{2}}\right)\right]\;,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{q\to gq}^{BH}}{dz}(P,z)=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}TP_{q\to gq}(z)\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}Q\left(\frac{1-z+2az^{2}}{2}\right)+\left(C_{\mathrm{F}}-\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}\right)Q\left(\frac{1-z+2az^{2}}{2z^{2}}\right)+\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}Q\left(\frac{1-z+2az^{2}}{2(1{-}z)^{2}}\right)\right]\;,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{g\to qq}^{BH}}{dz}(P,z)=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{4}TP_{g\to qq}(z)\times

\displaystyle\left[\left(C_{\mathrm{F}}-\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}\right)Q\left(\frac{2a-z(1{-}z)}{2}\right)+\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}Q\left(\frac{2a-z(1{-}z)}{2z^{2}}\right)+\frac{C_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}Q\left(\frac{2a-z(1{-}z)}{2(1{-}z)^{2}}\right)\right]\;.

C.1
Evaluating the integral in impact-parameter space

In this section we show how to compute the
Q(\tilde{\mu}^{2})
integral in
{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}
-space. We start by rewriting the integral in Eq. (
68
) as follows:

Q(\tilde{\mu}^{2})=\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}T}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\vec{\psi}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\bar{C}(\mathbf{q}_{\perp})\left(\vec{\psi}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})-\vec{\psi}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp}-\mathbf{q}_{\perp})\right)\;,

(73)

where we introduce the function
\vec{\psi}(\mathbf{p}_{\perp})=\frac{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}}{\mathbf{p}_{\perp}^{2}+\tilde{\mu}^{2}}
.
Its Fourier transform is given by

\vec{\psi}({\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})=\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}_{\perp}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}\vec{\psi}(\mathbf{q}_{\perp})=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\tilde{\mu}K_{1}(\tilde{\mu}b_{\perp})\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp}}{b_{\perp}}\,,

(74)

where
K_{1}(x)
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind.
Inserting the Fourier transform to the
Q(\tilde{\mu}^{2})
integral and using the definition of the broadening kernel in Eq. (
2
) we obtain

\displaystyle Q(\tilde{\mu}^{2})

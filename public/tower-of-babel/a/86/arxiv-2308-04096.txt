On fine Mordell-Weil groups over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of an imaginary quadratic field
Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
Let E E be an elliptic curve over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . Greenberg has posed a question whether the structure of the fine Selmer group over the cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of ℚ \mathbb{Q} can be described by cyclotomic polynomials in a certain precise manner. A recent work of Lei has made progress on this problem by proving that the fine Mordell-Weil group (in the sense of Wuthrich) does have this required property. The goal of this paper is to study analogous questions of Greenberg over various ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of an imaginary quadratic field F F . In particular, when the elliptic curve has complex multiplication by the ring of integers of the imaginary quadratic field, we obtain analogous results of Lei over the cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension and anti-cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of F F . In the event that the elliptic curve has good ordinary reduction at the prime p p , we further obtain a result over the ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of F F unramified outside precisely one of the prime of F F above p p . Finally, we study the situation of an elliptic curve over the anticyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension under the generalized Heegner hypothesis. Along the way, we establish an analogous result for the BDP-Selmer group. This latter result is then applied to obtain a relation between the BDP p p -adic L L -function and the Mordell-Weil rank growth in the anticyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension which may be of independent interest. Keywords and Phrases: Fine Selmer groups, fine Mordell-Weil groups, ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 11G05, 11R23, 11S25.

Introduction

Throughout this article,
p
will always denote a fixed prime
\geq 5
. The (
p
-primary)
fine Selmer group of an elliptic curve
E
is a much studied object in Iwasawa theory
and frequently appears in the formulation (and proof) of the Iwasawa main conjecture (for instances, see
[
6
,
7
,
14
,
15
,
31
,
33
]
). Despite this, it was only around 2005, Coates and Sujatha
[
8
]
, and a little later in 2007, Wuthrich
[
34
]
initiated a systematic study on the fine Selmer group of an elliptic curve
E
. Analogous to the usual Selmer group, the fine Selmer group
R(E/F)
sits in the
middle of the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(E/F)\longrightarrow R(E/F)\longrightarrow\mbox{Ж}(E/F)\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{M}(E/F)
and
\mbox{Ж}(E/F)
are the fine Mordell-Weil group and fine Tate-Shafarevich group respectively (defined in the sense of Wuthrich
[
35
]
) which can be thought as the “fine” counterpart of the usual Mordell-Weil group and Tate-Shafarevich group.

When the elliptic curve
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, Greenberg asked whether the following formula

\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\Big(R(E/\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}\Big)=\prod_{n\geq 0}\Phi_{n}^{\max\{0,e_{n}-1\}}

is always valid (see
[
17
, Problem 0.7]
). Here
e_{n}=\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(\mathbb{Q}_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(\mathbb{Q}_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})}
with
\phi(n)
being the Euler totient function,
\Phi_{n}=\frac{(X+1)^{p^{n}}-1}{(X+1)^{p^{n-1}}-1}
is the
p^{n}
th-cyclotomic polynomial,
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{cyc}}
is the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
is the intermediate subextension of
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q}
with
|\mathbb{Q}_{n}:\mathbb{Q}|=p^{n}
. Pertaining to this question of Greenberg, Kurihara and Pollack (see loc. cit.) has performed several numerical computations verifying the truth of Greenberg’s question. Furthermore, when the elliptic curve in question has good supersingular reduction at
p
, they have shown that the validity of Greenberg’s question has implications towards understanding certain finer aspects of the structure of the signed Selmer groups of Kobayashi
[
15
]
(also see
[
21
,
23
]
for some related discussion in this direction).

Recently, Lei
[
19
]
has showed that

\mathcal{M}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}\sim\prod_{n\geq 0}\Phi_{n}^{\max\{0,e_{n}-1\}}

under appropriate assumptions, where
\sim
means pseudo-isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q})\rrbracket
-modules. Prior to this, Wuthrich has performed several numerical computations showing that
\mbox{Ж}(E/\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{cyc}})
is finite which led him to predict that this said group is finite most of the time (see
[
35
]
). Hence the collective works of Lei and Wuthrich provide strong evidence towards the validity of Greenberg’s assertion. Inspired by these prior works, Lei went on to pose an analogous guess on the fine Selmer group of a
p
-supersingular CM elliptic curve over the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension in another paper of his
[
20
]
but he did not provide an analogue of his above result in this context (we will see in a while a possible explanation for this). The goal of this paper is to continue this line of study. For the remainder of the introductory section, we describe our results in slightly more detail.

The theme of our work is whether one can formulate analogues of Greenberg’s question for other different settings. The original work of Lei
[
19
]
can be thought as providing evidences towards Greenberg’s guess. Our approach towards this theme of study is to, a certain extent, the reversal of this development. Namely, we first seek to establish analogous results of Lei, and then propose forms of Greenberg’s question suggested by the shape of our results.

A key principle towards our investigation is to understand the variation of the fine Mordell-Weil groups in the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of interest. In
[
19
]
, Lei established a precise link between this said variation in the intermediate subextensions of the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}/F
and the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\rrbracket
-module structure of
\mathcal{M}(E/F_{\infty})
under the assumption that the full Mordell-Weil group
E(F_{\infty})
over the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}
. However, this latter finite generation is not expected to always hold. For instance, if
E
is an elliptic curve with complex multiplication given by the ring of integers of
F
and has good supersingular reduction at
p
, then the Mordell-Weil group
E(F^{\mathrm{ac}})
is not finitely generated, where
F^{\mathrm{ac}}
is the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of an imaginary quadratic field
F
(see
[
1
,
6
,
22
]
). In particular, the techniques developed in
[
19
]
cannot apply in this context. In our paper, we circumvent this by instead establishing a relation between the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil groups in the intermediate subextensions of the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}/F
and the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\rrbracket
-module structure of an appropriate Iwasawa module
Y_{f}(E/F_{\infty})
(see Definition
3.4
and Proposition
3.6
) under the assumption that

is cotorsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\rrbracket
. This latter torsionness is conjectured to always hold (see
[
25
,
26
,
31
]
), and has been established in many cases of interest (see Remark
3.3
and the references therein). We then show that our result recovers Lei’s result (see Proposition
3.7
) under his stronger assumption.

Since the module

is a quotient of

by definition, understanding this said module will provide information on the fine Selmer group. However, in view of the discussion in the preceding paragraph, the structure of

will only become transparent after we are able to understand the explicit variation of the fine Mordell-Weil groups over the intermediate subextensions of the

-extension in question.
The remainder of the paper is thus an effort towards realizing this latter aim. More precisely, we specialize to various

In this supersingular reduction situation, we also have a result for the anticyclotomic

Algebraic preliminaries

In this section, we recall certain algebraic preliminaries. We begin by introducing certain basic conventions. For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module

, we denote by

the submodule of

consisting of elements
of

which are annihilated by

. We then write
N[p^{\infty}]=\cup_{n\geq 1}N[p^{n}]
and

. In the event that

for the power of

, i.e.,

2.1
The functor
\mathfrak{G}

Throughout the paper, we shall write
\Lambda
for the classical Iwasawa algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
, where
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Recall that a homomorphism
M\longrightarrow N
of
\Lambda
-modules is said to be a
pseudo-isomorphism if its kernel and cokernel are finite. We shall write
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}N
. Note that the notion of being pseudo-isomorphic is not a reflexive relation (see
[
30
, P 300, Exercise 1]
). However, in the event that
M
and
N
are torsion
\Lambda
-modules, then one has
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}N
if and only if
N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M
(see
[
30
, Remarks after Proposition 5.1.7]
). In this case, we shall sometimes write
M\sim N
.

The unique subgroup of
\Gamma
of index

is in turn denoted by
\Gamma_{n}
. For a given
\Lambda
-module

, Lee defined a corresponding
\Lambda
-module

\mathfrak{G}(M):=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}\Big(M_{\Gamma_{n}}[p^{\infty}]\Big).

For more details on this module, the interested readers are referred to the paper of Lee
[
18
]
.
For our purposes, we require the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. Then
\mathfrak{G}(M)=0
if and only if there is a pseudo-isomorphism

M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Lambda^{r}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\Lambda/\Phi_{m_{j}}\Big),

where
\Phi_{m_{j}}
is a certain

-th cyclotomic polynomial.

Proof.

See
[
18
, Lemma 4.1.3]
.
∎

2.2
Some technical results

We now develop an axiomatic setting which will be useful for our eventual arithmetic application. As before, we let
\Lambda
denote the classical Iwasawa algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
, where
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
\{M_{n}\}
be a sequence of
\Lambda
-modules with transition maps
M_{n+1}\longrightarrow M_{n}
such that the action of
\Lambda
on
M_{n}
factors through
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}]
. We shall always assume that each
M_{n}
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Writing
M_{n,f}=M_{n}/M_{n}[p^{\infty}]
, one can check easily that every
M_{n}[p^{\infty}]
(resp.,
M_{n,f}
) is a
\Lambda
-submodule (resp.,
\Lambda
-quotient) of
M_{n}
and the transition map
M_{n+1}\longrightarrow M_{n}
induces a transition map
M_{n+1}[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow M_{n}[p^{\infty}]
(resp.,
M_{n+1,f}\longrightarrow M_{n,f}
).
Set
N=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}(M_{n}[p^{\infty}])
,
M=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}M_{n}
and
M_{f}=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}M_{n,f}
.
For each
n
, the natural map
M\longrightarrow M_{n}
factors through
M_{\Gamma_{n}}
to induce a map
M_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow M_{n}
. Similarly, we have maps
N_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow M[p^{\infty}]
and
(M_{f})_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow M_{n,f}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We warn the readers that
\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}(M_{n}[p^{\infty}])
and
(\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}M_{n})[p^{\infty}]
need not be equal and so
M_{f}
needs not be the same as
M/M[p^{\infty}]
. We shall illustrate this with a class of examples. Set
M=\Lambda/g(T)
, where
g(T)
is any irreducible polynomial coprime to every
p
-power cyclotomic polynomials. Then set
M_{n}=M_{\Gamma_{n}}
. Plainly, one has
M=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}M_{n}
,
M_{n}=M_{n}[p^{\infty}]
and
M_{n,f}=0
. Thus, by definition, we have
M_{f}=0
. On other hand,
M=\Lambda/g(T)
has no
p
-torsion, and so one has
M[p^{\infty}]=0
and
M/M[p^{\infty}]=M\neq 0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Retain settings as above. Suppose further that the map
M_{\Gamma_{n}}\longrightarrow M_{n}
has finite kernel and cokernel for every
n
. Then one has
\mathfrak{G}(M_{f})=0
. In other words, one has

M_{f}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Lambda^{\oplus r}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus s_{n}}\Big),

where
r
is a nonnegative integer and
\{s_{n}\}
is a sequence of nonnegative integers with
s_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
.

Proof.

Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{N_{\Gamma_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{\Gamma_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M_{f})_{\Gamma_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{n}}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n}[p^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n,f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

with exact rows. In view of our standing assumption of
M_{n}
being finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the module

is therefore finite. It then follows from this and the hypothesis of the proposition that

is finite for every

. Since

is

-torsionfree by definition, this in turn implies that

Taking inverse limit, we obtain

The following corollary will be a crucial component for our subsequent discussion in the paper.

Corollary 2.4
.

Retain the settings and assumptions in Proposition
2.3
. Suppose further that the following conditions are valid.

(a)

The module
M
is torsion over
\Lambda
. In particular, one has a pseudo-isomorphism

M_{f}\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus s_{n}}

of
\Lambda
-modules, where
\{s_{n}\}
is a sequence of nonnegative integers with

for

.

(b)

\{M^{\prime}_{n}\}
is a sequence of
\Lambda
-modules with transition maps

such that the action of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}]
and that each

Then for every

, one has

where the integer
n
appearing on both sides of the isomorphism are the same.

Proof.

Since
M^{\prime}_{n}\subseteq M_{n}[p^{\infty}]
, we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\prime}_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n}/M^{\prime}_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n}[p^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{n,f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

with exact rows. By the snake lemma, one has an exact sequence

3
Fine Selmer groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension

We begin with a remark. Although the paper is concerned with elliptic curves, the discussion in this section also holds for abelian varieties, and whence our presentation in this section will take this general approach. Now, let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
F
. Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
F
containing the primes above
p
, the bad reduction primes of
A
and the infinite primes. Denote by
F_{S}
the maximal algebraic extension of
F
which is unramified outside
S
. For every extension
\mathcal{L}
of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, write
G_{S}(\mathcal{L})=\Gal(F_{S}/\mathcal{L})
, and denote by
S(\mathcal{L})
the set of primes of
\mathcal{L}
above
S
.
We shall also write
S_{p}(\mathcal{L})
for the set of primes of
\mathcal{L}
above
p
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
. Following Coates, Sujatha and Wuthrich
[
8
,
34
]
, the fine Selmer group
R(A/L)
of
A
over
L
is defined by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R(A/L)\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{S}(L),A[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{v},A[p^{\infty}]).

At first viewing, it would seem that the fine Selmer group depends on the set
S
. But we shall see that this is not so. In fact, recall that the classical (
p
-primary) Selmer group
\Sel(A/L)
is defined by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Sel(A/L)\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{S}(L),A[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{v},A)[p^{\infty}]

and it is well-known that this definition is independent of the set
S
as long as the set
S
contains all the primes above
p
and the bad reduction primes of
A
(see
[
29

Proposition 3.8

Let

3.8

As a start, we note that all intermediate objects

(A(F_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\Gamma_{n}}
and their Pontryagin dual will be viewed as
\Lambda

By a theorem of Lee
[

, there is an injective

with a finite cokernel,
where
\{t_{j}\}
is a sequence of nonnegative integers with

for

.
An application of the
-_{\Gamma_{n}}
-functor yields a

\Big((A(F_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\Gamma_{n}}\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus(r+t_{j})}

with finite kernel and cokernel. On the other hand, the restriction map

with finite cokernel. Now, a combination of (

\big(A(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus c^{(n)}_{j}}

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

T_{p}\big(E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong F_{n,\mathfrak{p}}\cong F_{\mathfrak{p}}[\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}]

of
F_{\mathfrak{p}}[\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}]
-modules. From which, we have

\Big(T_{p}\big(E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big)[\Phi_{n}]\cong F_{\mathfrak{p}}[X]/\Phi_{n}.

Since
p
is inert in
F/\mathbb{Q}
, the quadratic extension
F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified. Thus, the cyclotomic polynomial
\Phi_{n}
remains irreducible over
F_{\mathfrak{p}}[X]
which in turn implies that
F_{\mathfrak{p}}[X]/\Phi_{n}
is an irreducible
\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}
-module. Therefore, it suffices to show that the restriction of the map
f_{F_{n}}
on the
\Phi_{n}
-component is nonzero. By hypothesis, there is a nontorsion point
P\in E(F_{n})-E(F_{n-1})
. Since the map
f_{F_{n}}
respects the
\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}
-action, it sends the point
P
to a nontorsion point in
E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})-E(F_{n-1,\mathfrak{p}})
which yields the required conclusion. ∎

We are now ready to prove the first main theorem of the section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that the prime
p
is inert in
F/\mathbb{Q}
. Let
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
be the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
. Assume that
\mbox{Ж}(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for every
n
, where
F_{n}
is the subextension of
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Then we have

\mathcal{M}(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus\max\{0,f_{n}-1\}},

(4.2)

where

f_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}E(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}E(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1$;}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}E(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It is well-known that
E(F^{\mathrm{cyc}})
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
14
,
32
]
). Consequently, we have
f_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
and so the right hand side of
(\ref{mainCMcyceqn})
is actually a finite sum. Furthermore, the finite generation allows us to apply an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-analog of Proposition
3.7
to obtain

\mathcal{M}(E/F^{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{n}}

with the property that

T_{p}(\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n}))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}

for every
n
. It therefore remains to show that
s_{n}=\max\{0,f_{n}-1\}
for every
n
. Now consider the following sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n})\longrightarrow E(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

To relate the terms in this exact sequence to the map (
4.1
), we first verify the following isomorphisms

E(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong E(F_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}};

E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Indeed, one can check easily that in the following diagram

\textstyle{E(F_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{E(F_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E(F_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E(F_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

that the leftmost vertical map is injective and the middle vertical map is bijective. Hence this yields the first claimed isomorphism. The second isomorphism can be established via a similar argument. In view of these identifications, upon applying the Tate module functor and tensoring with
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we obtain an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{p}\big(\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n})\big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow T_{p}\big(E(F_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{F_{n}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{p}\big(E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}},

where
f_{F_{n}}
is defined as in (
4.1
).
Taking the kernel of
\Phi_{n}
and applying
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-analogs of Propositions
3.7
,
3.8
and
3.9
, we obtain an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\big(F_{\mathfrak{p}}[X]/\Phi_{n}\big)^{\oplus s_{n}}\longrightarrow\big(F_{\mathfrak{p}}[X]/\Phi_{n}\big)^{\oplus f_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}[X]/\Phi_{n},

where the map
g_{n}
is induced by the map
f_{F_{n}}
.
If
f_{n}>0
, then the map
g_{n}
is surjective by Lemma
4.1
and so the above exact sequence is actually short exact. Therefore, we have
s_{n}=f_{n}-1
, which therefore concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5
CM elliptic curve: good ordinary reduction case

We continue to let
E
denote an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication given by
\mathcal{O}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is the ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic field
F
. Note that
F
necessarily has class number
1
. In this section,
we shall assume that
E
has good ordinary reduction at
p
, and so our prime
p
splits in
F/\mathbb{Q}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
the primes of
F
above
p
. Since
F
has class number 1, we have
\mathfrak{p}=\pi\mathcal{O}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}=\overline{\pi}\mathcal{O}
. We shall frequently identify
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
with
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
without any further mention.
We then define

E[\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}]=\cup_{n\geq 1}E[\pi^{n}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptE[\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}]=\cup_{n\geq 1}E[\overline{\pi}^{n}].

Note that
E[\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}]
and
E[\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}]
are cofree
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules of corank
1
, and there is a natural decomposition

E[p^{\infty}]=E[\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}]\oplus E[\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}]

of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F)
-modules.
For a given
\mathcal{O}
-module
M
, we shall write
T_{\mathfrak{p}}(M):=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}M[\pi^{n}]
and
T_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(M):=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}M[\overline{\pi}^{n}]
. One can check easily that
T_{p}M=T_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)\oplus T_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(M)
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
and let
L
be a finite extension of
F
. Following Coates
[
7
, P. 110, (6)]
, we define the
\mathfrak{q}
-primary fine Selmer group
R_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)
to be

R_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)=\ker\Big(H^{1}(G_{S}(L),E[\mathfrak{q}^{\infty}]\Big)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{w},E[\mathfrak{q}^{\infty}])\Big).

Remark 5.1
.

It is interesting to note that this
\mathfrak{q}
-primary fine Selmer group has appeared in an 1983 article of Coates
[
7
, P. 110, (6)]
in a different guise (also see the next remark). This was slightly more than 20 years before the paper of Coates-Sujatha
[
8
]
which christened the fine Selmer group as we know today.

We can also define the
\mathfrak{q}
-primary Selmer group
\Sel_{\mathfrak{q}}(A/L)
by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Sel_{\mathfrak{q}}(A/L)\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{S}(L),E[\mathfrak{q}^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{w},E)[\mathfrak{q}^{\infty}].

Analogously to the usual Selmer group, the
\mathfrak{q}
-primary Selmer group sits in the middle of the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow E(L)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}\longrightarrow\Sel_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\longrightarrow\sha(E/L)[\mathfrak{q}^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0.

Via a similar argument to that in
[
27
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have

0\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\longrightarrow\Sel_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S_{\mathfrak{q}}(L)}E(L_{w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}},

where
S_{\mathfrak{q}}(L)
is the set of primes of
L
above
\mathfrak{q}
.

Remark 5.2
.

In
[
7
]
, Coates has used the above exact sequence to define his
\mathfrak{q}
-primary fine Selmer group (also compare with
[
35
]
).

We then define the fine
\mathfrak{q}
-Mordell-Weil group by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)=\ker\Big(E(L)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in S_{\mathfrak{q}}(L)}E(L_{w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}}F_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}\Big)

and the fine
\mathfrak{q}
-Tate-Shafarevich group by

\mbox{Ж}_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)=\mathrm{coker}\,\Big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\Big).

By an entirely similar discussion to that in Section 3, one can show that the
\mathfrak{q}
-primary fine Selmer group sits in the middle of the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\longrightarrow\mbox{Ж}_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)\longrightarrow 0

with
\mbox{Ж}_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/L)
injecting into
\sha(E/L)[\mathfrak{q}^{\infty}]
.

Suppose that now
L
is a Galois extension of
F
at which
\mathfrak{q}
is totally ramified in

.
Then the natural inclusion

E(L)\hookrightarrow E(L_{\mathfrak{q}})
induces a

-map

f_{L}:T_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(E(L)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}\big)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\longrightarrow T_{\mathfrak{q}}\big(E(L_{\mathfrak{q}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}\big)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}.

(5.1)

Note that here we have made use of the identification
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
F_{\mathfrak{q}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

.

Let

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
. Denote by

the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of

. Assume that
\mbox{Ж}_{\mathfrak{q}}(E/F_{n})

In this section,
E
will always be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
which has good reduction at

. In other words,
E
either has good ordinary reduction or good supersingular reduction at
p

F
be an imaginary quadratic field at which the prime

splits completely and does not divide the class number of

satisfies the generalized Heegner hypothesis which we now recall.
The conductor

of the elliptic curve

will have a factorization

such that every prime dividing

splits in
F/\mathbb{Q}
, whereas every prime dividing
N^{-}
is inert in
F/\mathbb{Q}
. We further assume that
N^{-}
is a squarefree product of an even number of primes. Note that in this context, the group
E(F^{\mathrm{ac}})
is not finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
6
,
13
]
). In the event that
E
has good ordinary reduction, we assume further that
E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
has no

-torsion. (Note that the latter is automatically true had the reduction type of
E
at
p
is supersingular; see
[
15
, Proposition 8.7]
.)
Finally, we always assume that the natural group homomorphism
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(T_{p}E)
induced by the Galois action on
E
is surjective. All the assumptions in this paragraph will be in force throughout this section.

The goal of this section is to attempt to study the variation of the fine Mordell-Weil group in the intermediate subextensions of
F^{\mathrm{ac}}/F
, and hence the structure of
Y_{f}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
, under the above setting. Throughout this section, we shall let
F_{n}
denote the subextension of
F^{\mathrm{ac}}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. We also define

e_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
be the primes of
F
above
p
. Since the prime
p
is assumed to not divide the class number of
F
, both
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
are totally ramified in
F^{\mathrm{ac}}/F
. In this situation, we have two localization maps

\psi_{n,\mathfrak{p}}:E(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},

\psi_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}:E(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow E(F_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
\sha(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for every
n
, where
F_{n}
is the subextension of
F^{\mathrm{ac}}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Then the maps
\psi_{n,\mathfrak{p}}
and
\psi_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
are surjective for every
n
.

Proof.

When
E
has good supersingular reduction at
p
, this is established in
[
22
, Lemma 5.3]
. We shall therefore consider the case when
E
has good ordinary reduction at
p
. By the argument in Lemma
4.1
, it suffices to show that
e_{n}>0
for every
n
. But the latter follows from an application of
[
18
, Remark 2.1.6(2)]
.
∎

We now proceed to prove an analog of our results for the BDP-Selmer group in the sense of
[
13
]
using the techniques develop in this paper. We keep the standing assumptions as in the first paragraph of this section.
We recall two variants of Selmer groups which were introduced in
[
13
]
, although we shall adhere to the notation used in
[
22
]
. The first of which is defined as follow

\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})=\ker\Bigg(H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{n}),E[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\\
H^{1}(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}},E)[p^{\infty}]\oplus H^{1}(F_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},E[p^{\infty}])\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{w\in S(F_{n}),w\nmid p}H^{1}(F_{n,w},E[p^{\infty}])\Big)\Bigg).

The next is defined by

\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})=\ker\Big(H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{n}),E[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},E[p^{\infty}])\oplus\big(\bigoplus_{w\in S(F_{n}),w\nmid p}H^{1}(F_{n,w},E[p^{\infty}])\Big).

Set
\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{n}\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})
and
\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{n}\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})
. Then write
X^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
for the Pontryagin dual of
\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
and

X_{f}^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}X^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})/X^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}].

The modules
X^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
and
X_{f}^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
are defined similarly.
The connection between these Selmer groups is summarized in the next lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

For each
n
, we have the following exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\ker\psi_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\longrightarrow\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})\longrightarrow\sha(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0,

0\longrightarrow\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})\longrightarrow\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h_{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}},E)[p^{\infty}],

where
\mathrm{im}~h_{n}
is finite.

Proof.

When the elliptic curve has good supersingular reduction, the first exact sequence is
[
22
, Proposition 5.5]
, and the second exact sequence and the finiteness of
\mathrm{im}~h_{n}
can be found in the proof of
[
22
, Proposition 5.6]
. An examination of the proof reveals that the global duality argument there carries over to the good ordinary case.
∎

We record certain useful consequences of the preceding lemma in the form of the following proposition.

Proposition 6.3
.

One has
X_{f}^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})=X_{f}^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
.
Furthermore, the restriction map

\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})\longrightarrow\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\Gamma_{n}}

is injective with a finite cokernel.

Proof.

Taking Pontryagin dual of the second exact sequence in Lemma
6.2
, we obtain

0\longrightarrow C_{n}\longrightarrow X^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})\longrightarrow X^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})\longrightarrow 0,

for some finite
C_{n}
. Since
C_{n}
is finite, we have the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow C_{n}\longrightarrow X^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow X^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0

by
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
. Combining these two exact sequences, we obtain an identification

X^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})\big/X^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]\cong X^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})\big/X^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}].

Taking inverse limit yields the first equality of the proposition.

For the final assertion of the proposition, consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{im}~h_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\Gamma_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\Gamma_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\big(\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{n}\mathrm{im}~h_{n}\big)^{\Gamma_{n}}}

with exact rows. By
[
22
, Theorem 4.1]
, the middle map is injective with a finite cokernel. Since
\mathrm{im}\,~h_{n}
is also finite by Proposition
6.2
, an application of the snake lemma then shows that the leftmost vertical map is injective with finite cokernel. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

In general, it would seem that the cokernels in the preceding proposition need not be bounded independently of
n
(see
[
22
, Proposition 5.6 and Corollary 6.12]
). Nevertheless, the finiteness suffices for our purposes (see hypothesis (b) of our Corollary
2.4
).

Remark 6.5
.

Although we do not require this fact, we like to mention that in the event that
E
has good supersingular reduction at
p
, one even has

\Sel^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})=\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
. Indeed, this assertion is a consequence of
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{n}H^{1}(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}},E)[p^{\infty}]=0
and the second exact sequence in Proposition
6.2
.

We now come to the following theorem.

Theorem 6.6
.

Assume that
\sha(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for every
n
, where
F_{n}
is the subextension of
F^{\mathrm{ac}}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
.
Then we have

X_{f}^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus(e_{n}-1)}.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Corollary 3.5]
and
[
22
, Theorem 4.4]
,
\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
. In view of Lemma
6.2
, Proposition
6.3
and the finiteness of
\sha(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]
, we may apply Corollary
2.4
to conclude that

X_{f}^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})=X_{f}^{\mathrm{Gr},0}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus t_{n}}

with the property that at each level
n
, one has

T_{p}(\ker\psi_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus t_{j}}.

It remains to show that
t_{n}=e_{n}-1
for every
n
. By virtue of Lemma
6.1
, there is the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\ker\psi_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\longrightarrow E(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow 0.

Now, comparing
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-coranks of the terms in this said exact sequence, we obtain

\sum_{j=0}^{n}e_{j}\deg(\Phi_{j})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}t_{j}\deg(\Phi_{j})+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\deg(\Phi_{j})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(t_{j}+1)\deg(\Phi_{j})

for every
n
. The desired equality follows from this and an inductive argument.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

Recall that there is a main conjecture relating the BDP-Selmer group and the so-called BDP
p
-adic
L
-function
[
3
]
(also see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
16
,
24
]
for recent progress in this main conjecture, where this list is far from being exhaustive). Therefore, the preceding theorem gives a link between the precise growth of Mordell-Weil groups and the BDP
p
-adic
L
-function. (See discussion in subsection below)

We now come to our result on the fine Selmer groups.

Theorem 6.8
.

Assume that
\sha(E/F_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for every
n
, where
F_{n}
is the subextension of
F^{\mathrm{ac}}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
.
Then we have

Y_{f}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus s_{n}}

such that

T_{p}(\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n}))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}

and that

\max\{0,e_{n}-2\}\leq s_{n}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\}

for every
n
.

Proof.

As seen in the proof of Theorem
6.6
,
\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
. Since
R(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
is contained in
\Sel^{\mathrm{BDP}}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})
, it is also cotorsion over
\Lambda
. Therefore, we may apply Proposition
3.7
to conclude that

Y_{f}(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus s_{n}}

such that for each
n
, one has

T_{p}(\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n}))\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}.

On the other hand, it follows from definition that there is the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n})\longrightarrow\ker\psi_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\longrightarrow E(F_{n,\mathfrak{p}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Upon taking Tate-module functor, followed by tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and considering the
\Phi_{n}
-component, we obtain

0\longrightarrow\big(\mathbb{Q}_{p}[X]/\Phi_{n}\big)^{\oplus s_{n}}\longrightarrow\big(\mathbb{Q}_{p}[X]/\Phi_{n}\big)^{\oplus(e_{n}-1)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}[X]/\Phi_{n}.

(Note that for the middle term here, we have made use of the fact that

T_{p}(\ker\psi_{n,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus(e_{j}-1)}

which was derived in the proof of Theorem
6.6
.) From which, we may conclude that

\max\{0,e_{n}-2\}\leq s_{n}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\}

as required.
∎

Inspired by the above theorem, we pose the following question.

Question 6.
Can
\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\big(Y(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})\big)
be entirely described by cyclotomic polynomial? More precisely, is it possible that the following formula

\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\Big(Y(E/F^{\mathrm{ac}})\Big)=\prod_{n\geq 0}\Phi_{n}^{s_{n}}

holds for some
s_{n}
satisfying

\max\{0,e_{n}-2\}\leq s_{n}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\}?

Appendix: More on Remark

The goal of this short subsection is to say a bit on Remark

. We continue to retain the settings of the section. Now, let
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathrm{BDP}}

Corollary 6.10

Retain the assumptions in Theorem

\mathrm{ord}_{\Phi_{n}}\Big(\mathfrak{L}_{\mathrm{BDP}}\Big)\geq\frac{e_{n}-1}{2}.

Indeed, it follows from Theorem

∎

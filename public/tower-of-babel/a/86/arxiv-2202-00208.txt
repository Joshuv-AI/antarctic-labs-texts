A Structure Theorem for Bad 3-Orbifolds
R. Lehman, Yo’av Rieck

Abstract
. We explicitly construct a collection of bad 3-orbifolds, 𝒳 \mathcal{X} , satisfying the following properties: (1) The underlying topological space of any X ∈ 𝒳 X\in\mathcal{X} is homeomorphic to S 2 × I S^{2}\times I or ( S 2 × S 1 ) \ B 3 (S^{2}\times S^{1})\backslash B^{3} . (2) The boundary of any X ∈ 𝒳 X\in\mathcal{X} consists of one or two spherical 2-orbifolds. (3) Any bad 3-orbifold is obtained from a good 3-orbifold by repeating, finitely many times, the following operation: remove one or two orbifold-balls, and glue in some X ∈ 𝒳 X\in\mathcal{X} . Conversely, any bad 3-orbifold 𝒪 \mathcal{O} contains some X ∈ 𝒳 X\in\mathcal{X} as a sub-orbifold; we call removing X X and capping the resulting boundary cut-and-cap. Then by cutting-and-capping finitely many times we obtain a good orbifold.

Sometime around 1980 WP Thurston stated and sketched the proof of his celebrated

with non-empty singular set is good — covered by a manifold — then

admits a decomposition into geometric pieces (for definitions see Section

,

]

The case of an empty singular set — manifolds — is Thurston’s Geometrization Conjecture, proved by Perelman
[
perelman2
]

[
perelman1
]

[
perelman3
]
(a proof of the Orbifold Theorem along these lines can be found in
[
MR3244330
]
). These theorems have been immensely influential and allowed the study of various aspects of 3-dimensional topology by considering well-understood geometric pieces and combining them. Discussion about 3-orbifolds can also be found in
[
ConwayBurgielGoodmanStrauss

[

In
[

By its very definition a bad orbifold cannot be constructed from geometric pieces, and so the assumption that the orbifold is good cannot be removed. Our goal in this paper is to present an explicit structure theorem for bad orbifolds that allows their study by means of induction.

We now provide some more details about our strategy and results. Let
\mathcal{O}
be a closed, orientable (possibly bad) 3-orbifold. Thurston’s Orbifold Theorem and Perelman’s Geometrization Theorem state that after performing prime decomposition (decomposing
\mathcal{O}
along spherical 2-orbifolds — 2-orbifolds covered by
S^{2}
)
and then JSJ decomposition (decomposing along certain toroidal 2-orbifolds — 2-orbifolds covered by the 2-torus)
each of the pieces obtained, say
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, satisfies exactly one of the following:

(1)

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is geometric (the geometric quotient of one of Thurston’s eight geometries), or

(2)

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
contains an embedded bad 2-orbifold.

Consequently
\mathcal{O}
itself is bad if and only if it contains an embedded bad 2-orbifold, which we can take to be either a teardrop or a bad-football (see Proposition
2.2
).
Using this we show that if
\mathcal{O}
is bad, then it contains a compact 3-suborbifold,
X
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

X
is a member of one of
twelve explicitly constructed families,

(2)

X
contains an embedded teardrop or bad-football,

(3)

\partial X
consists of one or two spherical 2-orbifolds, and

(4)

X
has underlying topological space
S^{2}\times I
or
(S^{2}\times S^{1})\backslash B^{3}
.

Let the process of removing
X
from
\mathcal{O}
and capping the resulting boundary components by attaching a cone be called cutting and capping
X
.

The main result of this paper is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a closed, orientable 3-orbifold. Then there exists orbifolds
\mathcal{O}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{n+m}
(for some

\mathcal{O}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathcal{O}_{i-1}
by cutting and capping

(1)

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{0}
,

(2)

(1\leq i\leq n)

is listed in Theorem

(3)

(n+1\leq i\leq n+m)

is listed in Theorem

(4)

In particular, the boundary of each

or

Reversing the process of Theorem

gives us the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2

Any closed, orientable 3-orbifold is obtained from some good 3-orbifold by a repeating the following process finitely many times:

(1)

Removing 1 or 2 orbifold 3-balls, and —

(2)

Attaching a bad 3-orbifold listed in Theorem
5.1
or
16

Remark 1.3

(1)

Most of the notation we use is standard, with

We use

2
-orbifolds.

Any 2-orbifold that we consider is assumed to be closed and orientable (except in the proof of Proposition
2.2
where we are forced to consider non-orientable 2-orbifolds).
Any point
p
in such a 2-orbifold has a standard neighborhood that is either a smooth disk or a disk with a single singular point, corresponding to
p

a>1
(by which we mean that it is the quotient of a disk in

under the group generated by a rotation by angle

is an integer). A teardrop,
S^{2}(a)

|S^{2}(a)|\cong S^{2}
(the smooth 2-sphere) and exactly one singular point of weight

. A bad-football,

2
-orbifold with
|S^{2}(a,b)|\cong S^{2}
and exactly two singular points of unequal weights
a
and
b
, where we assume
b>a>1
. It is well-known that teardrops and bad-footballs are closed, orientable, bad 2-orbifolds.

A spherical 2-orbifold is a 2-orbifold that is covered by
S^{2}
(not to be confused with a 2-orbifold whose underlying topological space is
S^{2}
).
A closed spherical orbifold of the from
S^{2}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
must satisfy:

\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{1}{a_{3}}>1

This inequality allows us to list all the spherical
2
-orbifolds:

•

S^{2}

•

S^{2}(a,a)

•

S^{2}(2,2,a)
for any

•

S^{2}(2,3,3)
,

, and
S^{2}(2,3,5)

A toroidal 2-orbifold is a 2-orbifold that is covered by the 2-torus (for example,
S^{2}(3,3,3)
is toroidal and is not spherical).

3
-orbifolds.

By an orbifold we mean a compact, orientable
3
-orbifold.
Any point
p
in the interior of an orbifold has a standard neighborhood which is a cone on a closed spherical 2-orbifold; hence its underlying topological space is a closed 3-ball and (by the classification of finite group actions on

•

Empty.

•

A single unknotted arc, containing

•

Exactly three arcs, each connecting the boundary to

The weights of the arcs (say
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, and

\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{1}{a_{3}}>1

We observe that each component of the singular set of a closed orbifold has one of the following forms:

•

A smooth simple closed curve (here “smooth” means “contains no vertex”).

•

A trivalent graph, and in particular, the graph has no leaves (valence one vertices).

Lemma 2.3

If

∎

Consequently:

3.
A Bound on the Number of Teardrops

By Proposition
2.2
we have that an orbifold
\mathcal{O}
is bad if and only if it contains an embedded teardrop or bad-football. As our goal is to remove all bad components from
\mathcal{O}
we must first know that there exists a bound on their number, starting with teardrops:

Theorem 3.1

(finiteness of teardrops)
.

Given a closed, orientable 3-orbifold, there exists a bound on the number of disjointly embedded, pairwise non-parallel teardrops.

For a teardrop
T
, we consider
T\cap N(\Sigma)
and
T\cap E(\Sigma)
(recall that
E(\Sigma)
is the exterior of the singular set). We first prepare two lemmas:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T\subset\mathcal{O}
be a teardrop. Then
T\cap E(\Sigma)
is an essential disk in
E(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Obviously,
T\cap N(\Sigma)
is a disk that intersects
\Sigma
in a single point.
Since
|N(\Sigma)|
consists of handlebodies for which
|\Sigma|
are cores, we have that

T\cap\partial E(\Sigma)
is essential in
\partial E(\Sigma)
; the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be teardrops disjointly embedded in
\mathcal{O}
.
If
T_{1}\cap E(\Sigma)
and
T_{2}\cap E(\Sigma)
are parallel in
E(\Sigma)
, then
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are parallel in
\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
T_{1}\cap E(\Sigma)
and
T_{2}\cap E(\Sigma)
are parallel in
E(\Sigma)
. Then, in particular,
\partial(T_{1}\cap E(\Sigma))
and
\partial(T_{2}\cap E(\Sigma))
co-bound an annulus
A\subset\partial N(\Sigma)
. Then
\left(T_{1}\cap N(\Sigma)\right)\cup A\cup\left(T_{2}\cap N(\Sigma)\right)
is a football in

and bounds a product region by Lemma

which, together with the product region between
T_{1}\cap E(\Sigma)
and
T_{2}\cap E(\Sigma)
in

, shows that

and
T_{2}
are parallel.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
the intersection of a family of disjoint, pairwise non-parallel teardrops with

∎

\par\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@$T$ is parallel to $T^{\prime}$, for some $T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{i}$: Let $e$ be the edge of $\Sigma$ that meets $T$.
By considering all the forms of $X_{i}$ in Theorem \ref{localteardrop} we know that $e\subset X_{i}$. As $T^{\prime}$ is parallel to $T$, the orbifold point of $T^{\prime}$ must also lie on $e$. Therefore $T^{\prime}$ cannot be isotoped out of $X_{i}$, contradicting our construction. Therefore, case (2) cannot happen.
\end{enumerate}This establishes Lemma~\ref{lemma:FewerTeardrops}.
\qed\end@proof
\par\par\par\par\par\par\par This is the set-up for Lemma~\ref{lemma:FewerBadFootballs}: let $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ be a closed, orientable orbifold admitting an embedded bad-football but no embedded teardrop (the index is chosen to coincide with indices below).
Let $F\subset\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ be a member of a maximal family of disjointly embedded, non-parallel bad-footballs (a maximal family exists by Theorem~\ref{thm:Finiteness_of_bad_footballs}). Let $X_{n+i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ be the sub-orbifold containing $F$ guaranteed to exist by Theorem~\ref{localfootball}. Finally, let $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ be the orbifold obtained from $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ by cutting-and-capping $X_{n+i}$.
\par\par\par\par\par\par\begin{lemm}The maximal number of disjointly embedded, non-parallel bad-footballs in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ is strictly less than the maximal number of disjointly embedded, non-parallel bad-footballs in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$. Furthermore, there does not exist a teardrop in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$.
\end{lemm}\par\@proof Let $f_{\text{max}}$ be the maximal number of disjointly embedded, non-parallel footballs in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$. Suppose towards contradiction that $\mathcal{F}_{n+i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ is a collection of $f_{\text{max}}$ disjointly embedded, non-parallel footballs. By Lemma \ref{lemma:IsotopingIntoXi} we may assume that $\mathcal{F}_{n+i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}\backslash X_{i}$. As $F\subset X_{n+i}$ we have that
$$\mathcal{F}_{n+i}\cap F=\emptyset.$$Therefore, $\mathcal{T}_{n+i}\cup F$ form $f_{\text{max}}+1$ disjointly embedded bad-footballs in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ and by definition of $f_{\text{max}}$ one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}\enumerate@item@There exists $F^{{}^{\prime}},F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{n+i}$ that are parallel in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$: let $P\subset\mathcal{O}_{i-1}$ be a product region that $F^{{}^{\prime}}$ and $F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}$ co-bound. Since $F^{{}^{\prime}}\cap X_{n+i},F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap X_{n+i}=\emptyset$, one of the following two cases holds:
\begin{enumerate}\enumerate@item@$X_{n+i}\subset P$: By considering all of the forms of $X_{n+i}$ from
Theorem~\ref{localfootball},
we see that $\Sigma\cap X_{n+i}$ contains either a smooth simple closed curve (Smooth Form) or a vertex (all other forms). Therefore $X_{n+i}\not\subset P$, as these do not exist in a product region.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@$X_{n+i}\cap P=\emptyset$: Then $P\subset\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ showing that $F^{{}^{\prime}}$ and $F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}$ are parallel in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$, contrary to our assumption.
\end{enumerate}Therefore, case (1) cannot happen.
\par\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@$F$ is parallel to $F^{\prime}$, for some $F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{n+i}$: Let $e$ and $f$ be the edges of $\Sigma$ that meet $F$. By considering all the forms of $X_{n+i}$ in Theorem~\ref{localfootball} we see that $e\subset X_{n+i}$ or $f\subset X_{n+i}$. As $F^{\prime}$ is parallel to $F$, at least one orbifold point of $F^{\prime}$ must lie on $e$ or $f$.
Therefore $F^{\prime}$ cannot be isotoped out of $X_{i}$, contradicting our construction.
Therefore, case (2) cannot happen.
\end{enumerate}If $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ admitted a teardrop $T$ then, by Lemma~\ref{lemma:IsotopingIntoXi} we could isotope $T$ into $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$, contradicting our assumptions.
\par This establishes Lemma~\ref{lemma:FewerBadFootballs}.
\qed\end@proof
\par\@proof[Proof Theorem~\ref{mainthm}]
\par We construct $n$ and $\mathcal{O}_{i}$ $(i=0,\dots,n)$ as follows: set $\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{O}$ and assume we have constructed $\mathcal{O}_{i-1}$.
\begin{enumerate}\enumerate@item@If $\mathcal{O}_{i-1}$ does not contain a teardrop, set $n=i-1$.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@If $\mathcal{O}_{i-1}$ does contain a teardrop, let $T$ be a member of a maximal family of disjointly embedded, non-parallel teardrops, and let $X_{i}$ be a suborbifold containing $T$ guaranteed to exist by Theorem \ref{localteardrop}. Construct $\mathcal{O}_{i}$ by cutting-and-capping $X_{i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{i-1}$.
\end{enumerate}By Theorem \ref{badbounded} the number of disjointly embedded, pairwise non-parallel teardrops in $\mathcal{O}_{i}$ is finite, and by Lemma~\ref{lemma:FewerTeardrops} this number is smaller than the corresponding number in $\mathcal{O}_{i-1}$. Therefore the process terminates, yielding $n$ with $\mathcal{O}_{n}$ not containing a teardrop.
\par\par\par\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0pt\par\par\noindent We next construct $m$ and $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ $(i=1,\dots,m)$ as follows: starting with $\mathcal{O}_{n}$ above, assume we have constructed $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ not containing a teardrop.
\begin{enumerate}\enumerate@item@If $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ does not contain a bad-football, set $m=i-1$.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\enumerate@item@If $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$ does contain a teardrop, let $F$ be a member of a maximal family of disjointly embedded, mutually non-parallel bad-footballs, and let $X_{n+i}$ be a suborbifold containing $F$ guaranteed to exist by Theorem~\ref{localfootball}. Construct $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ by cutting-and-capping $X_{n+i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$.
\end{enumerate}By Theorem \ref{thm:Finiteness_of_bad_footballs} the number of disjointly embedded, pairwise non-parallel footballs in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ is finite, and by Lemma~\ref{lemma:FewerBadFootballs} this number is smaller than the corresponding number in $\mathcal{O}_{n+i-1}$. Lemma~\ref{lemma:FewerBadFootballs} also tells us that $\mathcal{O}_{n+i}$ does not admit a teardrop, and hence we may continue this process, eventually yielding $m$ with $\mathcal{O}_{n+m}$ not containing a bad-football or a teardrop.
\qed\end@proof

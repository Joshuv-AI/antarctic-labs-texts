Fractional Floquet theory [ Chaos, Solitons & Fractals 168 (2023) 113196 ]
Alexander Iomin

Abstract
A fractional generalization of the Floquet theorem is suggested for fractional Schrödinger equations (FTSE)s with the time-dependent periodic Hamiltonians. The obtained result, called the fractional Floquet theorem (fFT), is formulated in the form of the Mittag-Leffler function, which is considered as the eigenfunction of the Caputo fractional derivative. The suggested formula makes it possible to reduce the FTSE to the standard quantum mechanics with the time-dependent Hamiltonian, where the standard Floquet theorem is valid. Two examples related to quantum resonances are considered as well to support the obtained result.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider a Floquet theory for fractional time Schrödinger equations.
Separately, these two issues of both the Floquet theorem and fractional quantum mechanics
are well defined and well reviewed.
The Floquet theory is introduced to treat linear differential equations with time-periodic coefficients

[
1
]
and it is widely used in description of driven quantum systems such as
the interaction of radiation with matter, quantum nonlinear resonances, quantum chaos and so on
[
2
,
3
,
4

5

\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}(t+T)
is periodic in time with the period
T
, then the wave function, as the solution to the corresponding Schrödinger equation, has the form

\psi(t)=e^{-i\epsilon t}u(t)
. Here
u(t+T)=u(t)
is the periodic eigenfunction of a so called
Floquet operator
\hat{F}
,

\hat{F}u(t)\equiv[\hat{H}(t)-i\hbar\partial_{t}]u(t)=\epsilon u(t),

(1.1)

where
\hbar
is an effective dimensionless Planck constant and
\epsilon
is the quasienergy spectrum

[
6
,
7
]
(see also Ref.
[
3
]
). It is worth mentioning that in many cases,
Eq. (
1.1

[
4
]
. Moreover, the spectrum can have fractal band structures, such as the Hofstadter butterfly
[
8
,
9

, see also an extended review
[
5

. This situation is also reflected in the exploration of the Floquet engineering of quantum materials
[
10
,
11
]
.

Fractional time Schödinger equations belong to the field of fractional quantum mechanics.
The latter has been introduced in Refs.

An alternative replacement of the time derivative,

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\Psi(t)=\hat{H}(t)\Psi(t).

2
Fractional Floquet theorem

We formulate the fractional Floquet theorem in the form of the following
proposition.

Proposition 1

Let us consider the FTSE (
1.3
)

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\Psi(t)=\hat{H}(t)\Psi(t)

with the time-dependent periodic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t+T)=\hat{H}(t)
.
The solution to the FTSE (
1.3
) can be presented as follows

\Psi(t)=\sum_{n}E_{\alpha}\left[i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)t^{\alpha}\right]C_{n},

(2.1)

where
E_{\alpha.1}\equiv E_{\alpha}(z)
is the one-parameter Mittag -Leffler function
[
31
]
,

\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}
, and
C_{n}
are the coefficients of the Fourier series of
u(t)
, defined for the standard Floquet theorem in Eq. (
1.1
).

Proof 1

Important part of the consideration is the eigenvalue problem for the Floquet operator in Eq. (
1.1
),
which after the Fourier expansion, can be presented as follows

\sum_{n}\left[\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)C_{n}+\hbar(\omega n-\epsilon)C_{n}\right]e^{i\omega nt}=0,

(2.2)

see Eq. (
4.10
) and Comments
4.2
.
Due to the completeness of the Fourier basis
e^{i\omega nt}
,
one obtains the eigenvalue equation

\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)C_{n}=\hbar(\epsilon-\omega n)C_{n},~\forall n.

Then we take into account that the Mittag-Leffler function is the eigenfunction of the
Caputo fractional derivative,

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(iat^{\alpha})=iaE_{\alpha}(iat^{\alpha}),

where
a=\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)
, see Comment
3
-3.
Thus, the straightforward substitution of the fFT (
4.17
) in the FTSE (
1.3
)
yields

\sum_{n}\left[\hbar(-\omega n-\epsilon)C_{n}+\hat{H}(t)C_{n}\right]E_{\alpha}\left[i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)t^{\alpha}\right]=0,

(2.3)

see Comment
4.2
-2.
The expression in square brackets is just the expression in
Eq. (
2.2
) that reduces the FTSE to the standard Floquet theory in Eq. (
1.1
).

This proves the Proposition 1 for the fFT.

3
Subordination to the Floquet theorem

Equation (
2.1
) can be presented in a subordination form,
where the Floquet theorem subordinates the fFT.
Taking into account the Laplace image of the Mittag-Leffler function, we have the chain of transformations as follows
[
16
]

E_{\alpha}(iat^{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\frac{s^{\alpha-1}e^{st}ds}{s^{\alpha}-ia}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{s^{\alpha-1}e^{st}ds}{s^{\alpha}-ia}\right]\\
=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{s^{\alpha-1}e^{st}ds}{s^{\alpha}-iz}\delta(z-a)dz\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-ia\xi}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{iz\xi}E_{\alpha}(izt^{\alpha})\right]d\xi\\
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(\xi,t)\exp\left[i\xi(\omega n-\epsilon)\right]d\xi,

(3.1)

where the Fourier transformation of the Mittag-Leffler function,

\mathcal{K}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-iz\xi}E_{\alpha}(-izt^{\alpha})dz

(3.2)

can be reduced to the superposition of the odd and even functions of
z
and
\xi
.
Thus, we obtain

E_{\alpha}(-izt^{\alpha})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z^{2}t^{2\alpha})^{n}}{\Gamma(n2\alpha+1)}-izt^{\alpha}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z^{2}t^{2\alpha})^{n}}{\Gamma(n2\alpha+\alpha+1)}\\
=E_{2\alpha}(-z^{2}t^{2\alpha})-izt^{\alpha}E_{2\alpha,1+\alpha}(-z^{2}t^{2\alpha}).

(3.3)

The first term here is the even function of
z
, which stands for
the cosine-Fourier transformation and correspondingly leads to the
even function of
\xi
. The second term is the odd function of
z
and
stands for the sine-Fourier transform that leads to the odd function
of
\xi
. Therefore, integration with respect to
\xi\in(-\infty,\infty)
is reduced to the integration
with respect to
\xi\in(0,\infty)
. Correspondingly,
\xi
is
treated as a time parameter. Eventually, taking an analogy with a continuous
time random walk (CTRW) theory
[
32
]
, we suggest that
\mathcal{K}(\xi,t)

is the subordinator
[
33
,
34
]
, which subordinates the fractional evolution on the time scale
t

(physical time) to the unitary evolution on the time scale
\xi
(operational time).
In other words, the fFT is subordinated to the Floquet theorem:

\Psi(t)=\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(\xi,t)e^{-i\epsilon\xi}e^{i\omega n\xi}C_{n}d\xi=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(\xi,t)e^{-i\epsilon\xi}u(\xi).

(3.4)

4
Discussion: Floquet theorem in Fourier-Laplace space

We start the discussion with the case of
\alpha=1
, when

E_{\alpha}\left[i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)t^{\alpha}\right]=e^{i(\omega n-\epsilon)t}
.
Then Eq. (
2.1
) reduces to
the standard Floquet theorem,
which states that the solution to the Schrödinger equation

i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi(t)=\hat{H}\psi(t),\quad\quad\hat{H}(t+T)=\hat{H}(t)

(4.1)

is

\psi(t)=\sum_{n}e^{i(\omega n-\epsilon)t}C_{n}=e^{-i\epsilon t}u(t),

(4.2)

where
u(t)
is the periodic function, which together with the Hamiltonian can be presented in
the form of the the Fourier expansion

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}C_{n}e^{in\omega t},\quad\quad\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T},

(4.3a)

\displaystyle\hat{H}(t)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{h}(m)e^{im\omega t}.

(4.3b)

The Laplace transform of Eqs. (
4.2
) and (
4.3a
) yields

\mathcal{L}\left[\psi(t)\right]=\tilde{\psi}(s)=\sum_{n}C_{n}\left(s+i\epsilon-i\omega n\right)^{-1},

(4.4)

and the Laplace transform of the Schrödinger Eq. (
4.1
) yields

i\hbar s\sum_{n}\frac{C_{n}}{s+i\epsilon-i\omega n}=\sum_{n}\left(\sum_{m}\frac{\hat{h}(m)C_{n-m}}{s+i\epsilon-i\omega n}+i\hbar C_{n}\right),

(4.5)

where we take into account that the initial condition is
\Psi_{0}=\psi_{0}=\psi(t=0)=\sum_{n}C_{n}
, according to
the Floquet theorem (
4.2
) and Eq. (
4.3a
).

Considering
C_{n}
as a “lattice amplitude”, we have

C_{n}=\sum_{q}U_{q}e^{iqn},

(4.6)

and substituting the series of Eq. (
4.6
) in Eq. (
4.5
), we obtain,

\sum_{q}\sum_{n}\left[\frac{1}{s+i\epsilon-i\omega n}\left(i\hbar s-\sum_{m}\hat{h}(m)e^{-iqm}\right)-i\hbar\right]U_{q}e^{iqn}=0.

(4.7)

According to Eq. (
4.3b
), summation with respect to
m
yields

\sum_{m}\hat{h}(m)e^{-iqm}=\hat{H}(-q/\omega)
. Then taking into account
Eq. (
4.6
) and the fact that

\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)e^{iqn}=\hat{H}\left(-\frac{q}{\omega}\right)e^{iqn},

(4.8)

we obtain Eq. (
4.7
) as follows

\sum_{n}\frac{1}{s+i\epsilon-i\omega n}\left[\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)C_{n}-(\hbar\epsilon-\hbar\omega n)C_{n}\right]=0.

(4.9)

Therefore for any values of
\epsilon
and
n
, the coefficients
C_{n}
are eigenfunctions of the Hermitian
operator
\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)
, namely

\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)C_{n}=(\hbar\epsilon-\hbar\omega n)C_{n}.

(4.10)

Note that the Laplace argument
s
is not restricted as well, since
performing the Laplace inversion, the denominator in Eq. (
4.9
) becomes just the exponential
function,
e^{-it(\epsilon-\omega n)}
.

4.1
Floquet theorem for the FTSE

Let us return to the FTSE (
1.3
), and consider its solution in the form

\Psi(t)=\sum_{n}\Lambda_{n}(t)C_{n}e^{i\omega nt},

(4.11)

where
\Lambda_{n}(t)
is an unknown function of time.
The Laplace transformations of both the FTSE (
1.3
) and the solution (
4.11
) are

\displaystyle i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}\tilde{\Psi}(s)=\sum_{m}\hat{h}(m)\sum_{n}C_{n}\mathcal{L}\left[\Lambda_{n}(t)e^{it\omega(n+m)}\right]+i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\sum_{n}\hat{h}(m)C_{n}\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n-i\omega m)+i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}\Psi_{0},

(4.12a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}(s)=\sum_{n}\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)C_{n},\quad\quad\Psi_{0}=\Lambda(0)\sum_{n}C_{n}.

(4.12b)

Performing the shift of indexes
\sum_{n}\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n-i\omega m)C_{n}=\sum_{n}\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)C_{n-m}
, we obtain the equation for
\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)

from Eqs. (
4.12a
) and (
4.12b
) as follows

\sum_{n}\left[\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)\left(i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}C_{n}-\sum_{m}\hat{h}(m)C_{n-m}\right)-i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}\Lambda(0)C_{n}\right]=0.

(4.13)

One should bear in mind that the coefficients
C_{n}
and the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)

are the same as in the standard Floquet theory. Therefore, performing the expansion
(
4.6
) for the lattice amplitudes
C_{n}
and then performing summation
with respect to
m
, we obtain
\sum_{m}\hat{h}(m)e^{-iqm}=\hat{H}(-q/\omega)
.
Then taking into account
Eq. (
4.8
) and the eigenvalue Eq. (
4.10
), we obtain Eq.
(
4.13
) as follows

\sum_{n}\sum_{q}\left\{\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)\left[i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}-\hat{H}(-q/\omega)\right]-i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}\Lambda(0)\right\}U_{q}e^{iqn}\\
=\sum_{n}\left\{\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)\left[i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}-\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)\right]-i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}\Lambda(0)\right\}C_{n}\\
=\sum_{n}\left\{\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)\left[i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}-\hbar(\epsilon-\omega n)\right]-i\hbar^{\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}\Lambda(0)\right\}C_{n}=0.

(4.14)

Since the coefficients
C_{n}
are the functions in question in the Floquet theory, it is reasonable to
suppose that the Laplace image is

\tilde{\Lambda}(s-i\omega n)=\frac{s^{\alpha-1}\Lambda(0)}{s^{\alpha}+i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\epsilon-\omega n)},

(4.15)

which is the Laplace image of the Mittag-Leffler function

[
31
]
. Performing the Laplace inversion and setting
\Lambda(0)=1
, we obtain
the solution in the form of the one parameter Mittag-Leffler function,

\Lambda_{n}(t)e^{i\omega nt}=E_{\alpha}\left[i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)t^{\alpha}\right].

(4.16)

Taking into account the definition (
4.11
), we arrived at the wave function as follows

\Psi(t)=\sum_{n}\Lambda_{n}(t)e^{i\omega nt}C_{n}=\sum_{n}E_{\alpha}\left[i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)t^{\alpha}\right]C_{n}.

(4.17)

We stress that it has been proven in Sec.
2
that the wave function in the form of (
4.17
) is the fFT.

4.2
Comments

1

Evolution operator

The evolution of the wave function of the FTSE (
1.3
) is according to the evolution
operator
\hat{U}(t)
(do not confuse it with the band amplitude
U_{q}
), such that

\Psi(t)=\hat{U}(t)\Psi_{0}
. However, the explicit operator form of the evolution operator is unknown,
since the fractional Caputo derivative destroys the periodicity of the wave function, see
A
.
Therefore, the Fourier expansion in Eqs. (
4.3a
) and (
4.11
) is formal, such that
the coefficient
C_{n}
can be both an operator valued function and a function of coordinate space,
depending on the explicit operator form of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)
. The former case is considered
as an example in Sec.
5
, while the latter case is an example of Sec.
6
.

2

Lattice amplitude

Important part of the analysis is relates to the consideration of the amplitude
C_{n}
as a “lattice amplitude”
in the form of the expansion (
4.6
), namely

C_{n}=\sum_{q}U_{q}e^{iqn}
. Then taking into account the expansion (
4.3b
) and performing summation
with respect to
m
, one obtains that the time dependence in the Hamiltonian can be replaced as follows

\sum_{m}\hat{h}(m)e^{-iqm}=\hat{H}(-q/\omega)
. The latter leads to expression (
4.8
), which is

\hat{H}\left(\frac{i}{\omega}\frac{d}{dn}\right)e^{iqn}=\hat{H}\left(-\frac{q}{\omega}\right)e^{iqn}.

Therefore, taking into account this chain of transformations (that always can be done),
one can made the formal replacement

\hat{H}(t)C_{n}\equiv\hat{H}(\omega t)C_{n}\rightarrow\hat{H}\left(i\frac{d}{dn}\right)C_{n}.

3

Mittag-Leffler function

Note, that the Mittag-Leffler function is the eigenfunction of the Caputo fractional derivative.
Indeed, taking into account Eq. (
A.7
), we obtain

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(iat^{\alpha})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(ia)^{k}}{\Gamma(k\alpha+1)}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}t^{k\alpha}\\
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(ia)^{k}}{\Gamma(k\alpha+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(k\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(k\alpha-\alpha+1)}t^{(k-1)\alpha}\\
=ia\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(ia)^{k}t^{k\alpha}}{\Gamma(k\alpha+1)}=iaE_{\alpha}(iat^{\alpha}),

where
a=\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)
.

5
Example 1: Toy model

In this section, we consider a toy Hamiltonian presented in the form

\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}_{0}\cos(\omega t),

(5.1)

where
\hat{H}_{0}
can be an arbitrary time-independent Hamiltonian, which can
describe a variety of conservative systems. The standard Schrödinger equation for the toy model (
5.1
)
can be easily solved, and the evolution of the initial wave function
\psi_{0}
is

\psi(t)=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{H}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right)\psi_{0}=\exp\left[-i\frac{\hat{H}_{0}}{\hbar\omega}\sin(\omega t)\right]\psi_{0}.

(5.2)

The initial wave function
\psi_{0}
can be
e.g.
, the eigenfunction of
\hat{H_{0}}
, namely
\hat{H}_{0}\psi_{0}=e\psi_{0}
.
Then
\psi(t)=\exp[-i(e/\hbar\omega)\sin(\omega t)]
. Since for
t=kT
(
k
is integer),
\psi(kT)=\psi_{0}
,
correspondingly the quasienergy is zero,
\epsilon=0
and
\psi(t)=u(t)
.
The Fourier transformation yields

\psi(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{-n}\left(\frac{\hat{H}_{0}}{\hbar\omega}\right)e^{i\omega nt},

(5.3)

where
C_{n}=(-1)^{n}J_{n}(z)\equiv J_{-n}\left(\frac{\hat{H}_{0}}{\hbar\omega}\right)
is the Bessel function of the first kind, which also satisfies the eigenvalue Eq. (
4.10
). The latter reads

z\cos\left(\frac{d}{dn}\right)J_{-n}(z)=-nJ_{-n}(z),

(5.4)

which is just the recurrence relation of the Bessel functions
[
35
]
:

zJ_{n+1}(z)+zJ_{n-1}(z)=2nJ_{n}(z).

7
Conclusion

In the paper we concern with the fractional Schrödinger equations (FTSE) for systems, which are described by the time-dependent periodic Hamiltonians. An analog of the well known Floquet theorem is suggested to solve this class of the FTSEs, and this new result is called the fractional Floquet theorem (fFT).
The main result can be formulated in the form of the following generalization of the Floquet theorem for the wave function. Namely, considering the standard Floquet theorem as follows

\psi(t)=e^{-i\epsilon t}u(t)=\sum_{n}C_{n}e^{-i\epsilon t+i\omega nt},

(7.1)

we generalize the exponential by the one-parameter Mittag-Leffler function

E_{\alpha}\left[i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)t^{\alpha}\right]
, which yields
the fFT (
2.1
) as follows

\Psi(t)=\sum_{n}C_{n}E_{\alpha}\left[i\hbar^{1-\alpha}(\omega n-\epsilon)t^{\alpha}\right],

(7.2)

which for
\alpha=1
reduces to the Floquet theorem (
7.1
).
The fFT has been verified by the straightforward substitution of Eq. (
2.1
) in the FTSE that
yields the identity.
Two examples supporting the obtained result have been considered as well.

In conclusion, it should be admitted that by means of the Mittag-Leffler function the fFT reduces the FTSE (
1.3
) with the time dependent Hamiltonian to the standard Floquet theorem consideration. In this case, the quasienergy spectrum of the FTSE exists and is determined by the standard Floquet theorem.

Appendix A
Fractional differentiation of periodic functions

In this notes, we consider fractional calculus as an example of fractional differentiation of periodic functions. Recently, this issue has attracted
some attention in the form of non-existence of periodic solutions in fractional-order dynamical systems
[
39
]
.
It has been shown in the framework of the Mellin transform consideration that fractional differentiation of periodic functions destroys their periodicity. The result was obtained for the Caputo, Riemann-Liouville and
Grünwald-Letnikov definitions of fractional-order derivatives.
We suggest an alternative approach, following the fundamental work of Ref.

[
26
]
, and the Fourier and the Laplace transformations are the main machinery of the analysis.

Any periodic function
g(t)
of the period
T
, such that
g(t+T)=g(t)
,
can be presented in the form of its Fourier series

g(t)=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}g_{l}e^{i\bar{l}t}\,,\quad g_{l}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}g(t)e^{-i\bar{l}t}dt\,,\quad\bar{l}=2\pi l/T\,.

(A.1)

Therefore,
g(t)
is determined by its Fourier image
g_{l}
if the Fourier series (
A.1
) converges. In particular, the
n
-th derivative is a periodic function, determined by
\bar{l}^{n}g_{l}
as the convergence of

g^{(n)}(t)=\tfrac{d^{n}g(t)}{dt^{n}}
in Eq. (
A.1
).
Therefore, to define periodicity or non-periodicity of the fractional
integro-differentiation of
g(t)
one considers the exponential

\exp[2\pi ilt/T]
only.

A.1
Riemann-Liouville fractional integral

Note that the Caputo and Riemann-Liouville
fractional derivatives are regularization of the fractional derivative

\mathchoice{{\hskip 10.47656pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{D}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.55695pt\hskip 8.55695pt\mu}_{\scriptstyle\hskip-10.47656pt\hskip-8.55695pta+\hskip 8.55695ptt}}}{{\hskip 10.47656pt\hbox{$\textstyle{D}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-8.55695pt\hskip 8.55695pt\mu}_{\scriptstyle\hskip-10.47656pt\hskip-8.55695pta+\hskip 8.55695ptt}}}{{\hskip 9.01112pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{D}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.75377pt\hskip 6.75377pt\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-9.01112pt\hskip-6.75377pta+\hskip 6.75377ptt}}}{{\hskip 9.01112pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{D}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.72299pt\hskip 5.72299pt\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-9.01112pt\hskip-5.72299pta+\hskip 5.72299ptt}}}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{a}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\mu-1}f(\tau)\,d\tau,

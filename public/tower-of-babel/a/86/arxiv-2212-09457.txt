Spin-half bosons with mass dimension three half: Evading the spin-statistics theorem
Dharam Vir Ahluwalia, Cheng-Yang Lee

Abstract
By exploiting the freedom in defining the dual of spinors, we report an unexpected theoretical discovery of a quantum field theory of spin-half bosons. It fulfils Dirac’s 1969-70 observation that “there must be boson variables connected with electrons.” The theory is local, Lorentz-invariant, and has a positive-definite Hamiltonian. We formulate the unitarity-preserving scattering theory to accommodate the new dual and the associated adjoint. A model of Yukawa interaction with spin-half bosons and fermions of equal masses is studied to explicitly show unitarity.

U(\tau,\tau_{0})=e^{iH_{0}\tau}e^{-iH^{{\dagger}}(\tau-\tau_{0})}e^{-iH_{0}\tau_{0}}\eta,

(47)

we find

\displaystyle U^{\#}(\tau,\tau_{0})U(\tau,\tau_{0})

\displaystyle=e^{iH_{0}\tau_{0}}\left[e^{iH(\tau-\tau_{0})}\eta e^{-iH^{{\dagger}}(\tau-\tau_{0})}\right]e^{-iH_{0}\tau_{0}}\eta.

(48)

Taking the inverse of (
37
), we get

e^{iH(\tau-\tau_{0})}\eta^{-1}e^{-iH^{{\dagger}}(\tau-\tau_{0})}=\eta^{-1}.

(49)

Since
\eta=\eta^{-1}
, we obtain (
46
) as required.

From (
38
) and (
39
), we obtain

-i\frac{d}{d\tau}U^{\#}(\tau,\tau_{0})=U^{\#}(\tau,\tau_{0})V^{{\dagger}}(\tau).

(50)

Following the same procedure in deriving the Dyson series for
S_{\beta\alpha}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S^{\#}_{\gamma\beta}=

\displaystyle\eta_{\gamma\beta}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{i^{n}}{n!}\int d^{4}x_{1}\cdots d^{4}x_{n}

\displaystyle\times\langle\gamma_{0}|\eta\mathfrak{T}\left[\mathcal{H}^{{\dagger}}(x_{n})\cdots\mathcal{H}^{{\dagger}}(x_{1})\right]|\beta_{0}\rangle.

Normalizing the
S
-matrix as

\displaystyle S_{\beta\alpha}\equiv\eta_{\beta\alpha}-2\pi iM_{\beta\alpha}\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}),

(51)

\displaystyle S^{\#}_{\gamma\beta}\equiv\eta_{\gamma\beta}+2\pi iM^{\#}_{\gamma\beta}\delta^{4}(p_{\gamma}-p_{\beta}),

(52)

equation (
42
) becomes

\displaystyle i\int d\beta\left[\eta_{\gamma\beta}M_{\beta\alpha}\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha})-M^{\#}_{\gamma\beta}\eta_{\beta\alpha}\delta^{4}(p_{\gamma}-p_{\beta})\right]

\displaystyle=2\pi\int d\beta\left[\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\gamma})\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha})M^{\#}_{\gamma\beta}M_{\beta\alpha}\right].

(53)

Setting
\gamma=\alpha
, the term
\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha})
cancels from both side of (
53
) so we obtain

\displaystyle i\int d\beta\left(\eta_{\alpha\beta}M_{\beta\alpha}-M^{\#}_{\alpha\beta}\eta_{\beta\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\int d\beta\left[\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha})M^{\#}_{\alpha\beta}M_{\beta\alpha}\right].

(54)

Equations (
53
) and (
54
) can be seen as a generalization to the optical theorem. In the subsequent section, we will demonstrate that (
54
) is satisfied for a model of Yukawa interaction involving spin-half bosons.

To compute physical observables, we first need to define the transition probability for the process
\alpha\rightarrow\beta
. Since the unitarity of the
S
-matrix is defined using the generalized hermitian adjoint
\#
, the transition probability should be appropriately modified. In a finite volume
V
with duration
T
, we define the transition probability to be
[
7
]

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,1,1}P(\alpha\rightarrow\beta)\equiv}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,1,1}\left[\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}\right]^{N_{\alpha}+N_{\beta}}\frac{VT}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\wp_{\beta\alpha}M^{\#}_{\alpha\beta}M_{\beta\alpha}\right)}

\displaystyle{{\color[rgb]{0,1,1}\times\left[\delta^{3}_{V}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha})\delta_{T}(E_{\beta}-E_{\alpha})\right].}}

(55)

Because
M^{\#}_{\alpha\beta}M_{\beta\alpha}
is not guaranteed to be positive-definite, we multiply it by a constant phase
\wp_{\beta\alpha}\in\Re
to ensure that
P(\alpha\rightarrow\beta)\geq 0
. From the definition of
\eta
and the Dyson series of
S_{\beta\alpha}
and
S^{\#}_{\alpha\beta}
, we find that the
M^{\#}_{\alpha\beta}=e^{i\pi(n_{\beta}-n_{\alpha})}M^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha\beta}
where
n_{\alpha}
and
n_{\beta}
are the number of spin-half anti-bosons present in states
|\alpha_{0}\rangle
and
|\beta_{0}\rangle
respectively. Therefore, by choosing
\wp_{\alpha\beta}=e^{-i\pi(n_{\beta}-n_{\alpha})}
, equation (
55
) reduces to the standard transition probability and the physical observables defined using hermitian conjugation can be applied without difficulty.

3.1
Phenomenologies

We now consider two spin-half bosonic and fermionic fields of equal mass
coupled to a real scalar field
\phi(x)

\displaystyle V(t)=g\int d^{3}x\left[\overline{\psi}(x)\psi(x)+\mbox{$\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}}}$}(x)\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}$}(x)\right]\phi(x)

(56)

where
\psi(x)
is a fermionic field whose expansion coefficients are given by (
1
) and (
2
).

By the construction of
\eta
, it commutes with the fermionic and scalar fields

\left[\eta,\psi(x)\right]=\left[\eta,\phi(x)\right]=O.

(57)

Therefore, the Yukawa interaction (
56
) is a generalized hermitian operator
\eta^{-1}V^{{\dagger}}(t)\eta=V(t)
.

We now demonstrate that the Yukawa interaction satisfies (
42
), the generalized unitarity relation. Taking the initial state to be a single scalar boson,
the matrix element of
\eta
is
\eta_{\phi\beta}=\eta_{\beta\phi}=\delta(\beta-\phi)
so (
54
) becomes

i(M_{\phi\phi}-M^{\#}_{\phi\phi})=2\pi\int d\beta\left[\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\phi})M^{\#}_{\phi\beta}M_{\beta\phi}\right].

(58)

We will evaluate both sides of (
58
) independently. Starting with the left-hand side, the matrix elements
M_{\phi\phi}
and
M^{\#}_{\phi\phi}
are obtained by evaluating the amplitude for
\phi\rightarrow\phi^{\prime}
and subsequently setting
\phi^{\prime}=\phi
.

Using the solution of
\eta
, we obtain
\eta|\phi\rangle=|\phi\rangle
where
|\phi\rangle
denotes a single scalar boson state. Setting
\phi=\phi^{\prime}
, we find
M^{{\dagger}}_{\phi\phi}=M^{\#}_{\phi\phi}
. Therefore, we may rewrite (
58
) as

\text{Im}(M_{\phi\phi})=-\pi\int d\beta\left[\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\phi})M^{\#}_{\phi\beta}M_{\beta\phi}\right].

(59)

Here,
\mbox{Im}(M_{\phi\phi})
is proportional to the imaginary part of the loop corrections to the scalar propagator
[
20
]
. Due to the difference in statistics and the fact that the spin-half bosons and fermions have equal mass, the bosonic and fermionic loop corrections identically cancel to all orders in perturbation so
\text{Im}(M_{\phi\phi})=0
. Next, we evaluate the right-hand side of (
58
). It expands to

\displaystyle\int d\beta\left[\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha})M^{\#}_{\phi\beta}M_{\beta\phi}\right]

\displaystyle=\int d\beta_{\psi}\sum_{\text{spins}}M^{\#}_{\phi(\overline{\psi}\psi)}M_{(\overline{\psi}\psi)\phi}\delta^{4}(p_{\,\overline{\psi}}+p_{\psi}-p_{\phi})

\displaystyle+\int d\beta_{\mathfrak{a}}\sum_{\text{spins}}M^{\#}_{\phi(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathfrak{a})}M_{(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathfrak{a})\phi}\delta^{4}(p_{\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}}+p_{\mathfrak{a}}-p_{\phi})

(60)

where
d\beta_{\psi}=d^{3}p_{\psi}d^{3}p_{\,\overline{\psi}}
and
d\beta_{\mathfrak{a}}=d^{3}p_{\mathfrak{a}}d^{3}p_{\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}}
. The first and second term of (
60
) comes from the fermionic and bosonic interactions respectively.

Acting
\eta
on the relevant states in (
60
), we obtain

\displaystyle\eta|\phi\rangle=|\phi\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\eta|\overline{\psi}\psi\rangle=|\overline{\psi}\psi\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\eta|\mbox{$\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}}}$}\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}$}\rangle=-|\mbox{$\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}}}$}\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}$}\rangle.

where
|\overline{\psi}\psi\rangle
and
|\mbox{$\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}}}$}\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}$}\rangle
denote the pair of spin-half fermion-anti-fermion and boson-anti-boson states respectively. The actions of
\eta
on
|\overline{\psi}\psi\rangle
and
|\mbox{$\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}}}$}\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}$}\rangle
have opposite signs which induces a relative phase in (
60
). In effect, we find

M^{\#}_{\phi(\overline{\psi}\psi)}=M^{{\dagger}}_{\phi(\overline{\psi}\psi)},\hskip 9.24994ptM^{\#}_{\phi(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathfrak{a})}=-M^{{\dagger}}_{\phi(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathfrak{a})}.

(61)

Using the decay rate formula
[
7
]

\displaystyle\Gamma(1\rightarrow 1^{\prime}2^{\prime})=2\pi\int

\displaystyle d\beta_{1^{\prime},2^{\prime}}\sum_{\text{spins}}|M_{(1^{\prime}2^{\prime})1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\times\delta^{4}(p_{1^{\prime}}+p_{2^{\prime}}-p_{1}),

(62)

where
d\beta_{1^{\prime},2^{\prime}}=d^{3}p^{\prime}_{1}d^{3}p^{\prime}_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int d\beta\left[\delta^{4}(p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha})M^{\#}_{\phi\beta}M_{\beta\phi}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\Gamma(\phi\rightarrow\overline{\psi}\psi)-\Gamma(\phi\rightarrow\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathfrak{a})\right]

(63)

so the unitarity condition becomes

\text{Im}(M_{\phi\phi})=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Gamma(\phi\rightarrow\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathfrak{a})-\Gamma(\phi\rightarrow\overline{\psi}\psi)\right].

(64)

Since the bosons and fermions have the same mass and their quantum fields are expanded the same spinors, we have
\Gamma(\phi\rightarrow\overline{\psi}\psi)=\Gamma(\phi\rightarrow\overset{\>{}^{{}^{\boldsymbol{\neg}}}}{\smash[t]{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathfrak{a})
. Therefore, the right-hand side of (
64
) identically vanishes to all orders in perturbation. This is in agreement with
\text{Im}(M_{\phi\phi})=0
so the theory is unitary.

4
Conclusion

Spin-statistics theorem has a very venerated position in the theory of quantum fields, as does Dirac equation. The works in the past have shown that the Dirac formalism with mass dimension three half, needs to be updated with a new formalism of mass dimension one fermions. Here we have argued that the spin-statistics theorem can also be evaded by exploiting the freedom in defining new dual for spinors and the ensuing adjoint. This results in a new quantum theory of spin half bosons. We then formulated a generalisation of the S-matrix to calculate some elementary processes.

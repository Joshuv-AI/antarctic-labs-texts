Birational geometry of Deligne-Mumford stacks
Andrew Kresch, Yuri Tschinkel

Abstract
. We investigate the birational geometry of Deligne-Mumford stacks and define new birational invariants in this context.



1.
Introduction

Birational geometry of algebraic varieties is a classical subject in algebraic geometry.
Closely related to this is
G
-equivariant birational geometry for a finite group
G
, also a well-studied area.
There are meaningful parallels
between these theories: the study of birationality over an algebraically nonclosed field
k
is analogous, in many aspects, to equivariant birationality over an algebraically closed field, where the role of
G
is played by the absolute Galois group of
k
.
This point of view has been brought into focus by Manin
[
37
]
.

In this paper, we explore the birational geometry of Deligne-Mumford stacks over fields of characteristic zero.
In the setting of a projective
G
-variety
X
the associated Deligne-Mumford stack
[X/G]
carries features such as stabilizer groups, but does not carry the full information of
X
with
G
-action.
The passage

X\righttoleftarrow G\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad[X/G]\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad k(X)^{G}=k(X/G)

from equivariant geometry to geometry of Deligne-Mumford stacks and subsequently to classical birational geometry
involves a loss information at each step.
There is extensive literature on
rationality of fields of invariants and relevant obstructions, such as unramified
cohomology
(see, e.g.,
[
45
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
14
]
),
as well as a growing literature on equivariant birationality (see, e.g.,
[
41
]
and references therein,
[
31
]
,
[
25
]
).

The Burnside ring, introduced in
[
27
]
, encodes classical birational types:

\Burn=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\Burn_{n};

the grading is by dimension.
Refinements to Deligne-Mumford stacks and
G
-varieties, respectively, appear in
[
29
]
and
[
31
]
,
with corresponding Burnside groups and compatibility homomorphisms:

\Burn_{n}(G)\quad\longrightarrow\quad{\overline{\Burn}}_{n}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\Burn_{n}.

Important to the birational geometry of Deligne-Mumford stacks is the appropriate notion of birational equivalence.
In

[
29
, Thm. 4.1]
,
the class in
{\overline{\Burn}}_{n}
of an
n
-dimensional Deligne-Mumford stack (when this is defined) is shown to satisfy
[{\mathcal{X}}]=[{\mathcal{Y}}]
in
{\overline{\Burn}}_{n}
whenever
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
are
birationally equivalent
, in the sense of fitting into a diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Y}}

where the morphisms are representable, proper, and birational.

A subtle phenomenon is the existence of
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
, not birationally equivalent, with
[{\mathcal{X}}]=[{\mathcal{Y}}]
in
{\overline{\Burn}}_{n}
.
For instance, over an algebraically closed field we consider the unique (nontrivial) action of
G={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
on elliptic curves.
If
E
and
F
are non-isomorphic elliptic curves, then the quotient stacks
{\mathcal{X}}=[E/G]
and
{\mathcal{Y}}=[F/G]
constitute such a pair (
n=1
).
By way of contrast, the class in
\Burn_{n}
, respectively in
\Burn_{n}(G)
, fully captures the birational type of a projective variety, respectively the equivariant birational type of a projective
G
-variety of dimension
n
.

In this paper, we introduce a refinement of
{\overline{\Burn}}_{n}
which fully captures the relevant birational type.
This is an abelian group

\cBurn=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\cBurn_{n}

defined by explicit generators and relations.
This is a refinement, in that a quotient by further explicit relations

{\overline{\cBurn}}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{\cBurn}}_{n},

can be identified with the group from
[
29
]
:

{\overline{\cBurn}}_{n}\cong{\overline{\Burn}}_{n}.

Our principal constructions and results are:

•

The groups
\cBurn
and
{\overline{\cBurn}}
, in Section
7
;

•

Associated birational invariants of Deligne-Mumford stacks, in Section
8
;

•

Specialization homomorphisms, in Section
9
;

•

Comparisons among various Burnside and Grothendieck groups, in Section
10
.

A substantial part of the paper, Sections
2
to
6
, is devoted to foundations of birational geometry of stacks. Throughout, we provide examples illustrating various aspects of the theory.

2.
Orbifolds and stabilizer groups

We work over a field
k
of characteristic zero.
We are interested in the birational geometry of
orbifolds
over
k
.

Algebraic orbifolds are smooth separated irreducible Deligne-Mumford (DM) stacks of finite type over
k
, with trivial generic stabilizer.
All algebraic stacks in this section are of finite type over
k
, unless indicated otherwise.
All morphisms are morphisms over
k
.

Example 2.1
.

A standard example of a DM stack is

\mathcal{X}=[X/G],

where
X
is an algebraic variety over
k
with regular action of a finite group
G
.
This is the category, where an object is a
G
-torsor

E\to T
(over an arbitrary
k
-scheme
T
) with a
G
-equivariant morphism
E\to X
, and a morphism is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism
of torsors (over a morphisms of
k
-schemes), compatible with the morphisms to
X
.
Informally, the stack
\mathcal{X}
encodes the
G
-equivariant geometry of
X
, e.g., when
k
is algebraically closed,
the category
\mathcal{X}(k)
of
k
-points of
\mathcal{X}
has isomorphism classes of objects in bijective correspondence with the
G
-orbits of
X(k)
.
The case
X=\Spec(k)
gives

BG,

the category of
G
-torsors (over
k
-schemes) and
G
-equivariant morphisms
(over morphisms of
k
-schemes).

A separated DM stack, or more generally an algebraic stack satisfying the weaker hypothesis of having finite inertia, has a coarse moduli space.
For instance,
\mathcal{X}=[X/G]
has coarse moduli space
X/G
, the quotient variety when this exists (e.g., when
X
is quasi-projective), generally the algebraic space quotient.
When
X
is nonsingular and the
G
-action is faithful,
\mathcal{X}=[X/G]
is an example of an orbifold.

For an orbifold,
when the coarse moduli space is a projective scheme, we speak of a
projective orbifold
,
and when quasi-projective, a

quasi-projective orbifold
.
The same definitions apply to smooth separated DM stacks; cf.
[
28
]
.
If
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-projective, then

\mathcal{X}\cong[X/G]
for some linear algebraic group
G
acting on a smooth quasi-projective scheme
X
;
in fact, we may take
G
to be
GL_{N}
for some
N
.
But it is a subtle question,
whether this can be achieved with a
finite
group
G
. Even in dimension one, there exist orbifolds not of the form
[X/G]
, for any variety
X
and finite group
G
.
E.g., for any positive integers
a
and
b
, not both equal to
1
, the stack

{\mathbb{P}}(a,b)=[{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}],

with action
t\cdot(x,y)=(t^{a}x,t^{b}y)
,
cannot be expressed as stack quotient of a scheme by a finite group (cf.
[
8
]
).
This is an orbifold when
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

Algebraic stacks form a
2
-category, where besides morphisms (over
k
) there are
2
-morphisms given by natural transformations of functors (over identity morphisms in the base category of
k
-schemes).
The
2
-morphisms are all invertible.

We often perform local constructions, such as the projective fibration
\Proj\Sym{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
associated with a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{E}}
on a given algebraic stack
{\mathcal{X}}
.
This, along with a more general
projective
morphism to
{\mathcal{X}}
, which by definition is one that admits a factorization up to
2
-isomorphism as a closed immersion to a projective fibration, provides an example of
representable
morphism.
A morphism
{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
is representable if, for any scheme
T
with morphism to
{\mathcal{X}}
, the fiber product
T\times_{{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{Y}}
is an algebraic space.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
be a projective morphism of separated DM stacks.
Then the induced morphism of coarse moduli spaces
e:Y\to X
is projective.

Proof.

The morphism
e
is proper, since the composite
{\mathcal{Y}}\to X
is proper and
{\mathcal{Y}}\to Y
is proper surjective.
We will exhibit a line bundle
M
on
Y
, whose pullback to a finite scheme cover of
Y
is ample, relative to the composite morphism to
X
.
When
X
and
Y
are schemes,
[
20
, (2.6.2)]
tells us that

is projective, with relatively ample line bundle
M
.
The proof given in
[
20
, (2.6.3)]
uses Chevalley’s theorem.
This is available for algebraic spaces, and we reach the same conclusion, supposing just
X
to be a scheme.
The general case reduces to this one, by base change to an étale atlas of
X
, since the condition of obtaining a closed immersion
Y\to\Proj\Sym e_{*}(M^{n})
, for
n\gg 0
, may be checked étale locally.

By
[
35

, there exists a scheme

with a finite surjective morphism
Z\to{\mathcal{X}}
.
We let

and

The composite morphisms

As well, they are surjective.

Factoring

By
[

So

3.
Normal crossing divisor and root construction

We continue to work with stacks of finite type over a field
k
of characteristic
0
.

A
normal crossing divisor
on a smooth separated DM stack
{\mathcal{X}}
is a divisor
{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
, whose pre-image in one or equivalently every étale altas
U
of
{\mathcal{X}}
is a normal crossing divisor on
U
.
If, furthermore, we can write

{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{1}\cup\dots\cup{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
, where each
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
is smooth, then we call
{\mathcal{D}}
a
simple normal crossing (snc) divisor
.
Equivalently, an snc divisor on
{\mathcal{X}}
is a normal crossing divisor whose irreducible components are smooth.

Remark 3.1
.

Desingularization of a reduced Noetherian quasi-excellent algebraic stack in characteristic

This is applicable to reduced stacks, of finite type over a field of characteristic zero. The case of reduced stacks of finite type over a DVR with residue field of characteristic zero, needed in Section

4.
Birationality

Interest in birational geometry of stacks was stimulated by progress in the
Minimal Model Program
for moduli stacks, see e.g.
[

]
,
from which we have taken the notions in Definition

, below. All stacks in this section are DM stacks, separated and of finite type over

(In several places, clearly indicated, there are DM stacks which are separated and of finite type over a different field.)

Definition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
be stacks.

(i)

A morphism
f\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
is
birational
if
f
restricts to an isomorphism
{\mathcal{U}}\to{\mathcal{V}}
of dense open substacks of
{\mathcal{X}}
, respectively
{\mathcal{Y}}
.

(ii)

A
rational map

{\mathcal{X}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is an object of the category

\MAP({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}})=\colim_{{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}}\HOM({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{Y}}),

where the colimit is taken over dense open substacks
{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
.
So, a rational map is represented by

f\colon{\mathcal{U}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
for some such
{\mathcal{U}}
, with
({\mathcal{U}},f)\cong({\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},f^{\prime})

if and only if
f
and
f^{\prime}
become
2
-isomorphic after restriction to common dense open.

(iii)

A rational map
{\mathcal{X}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is
dominant
if, for some or equivalently every representative
({\mathcal{U}},f)
,
f({\mathcal{U}})
is dense in
{\mathcal{Y}}
.

(iv)

A rational map
{\mathcal{X}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is
birational
if, for some representative
({\mathcal{U}},f)
,

f
maps
{\mathcal{U}}
isomorphically to a dense open substack of
{\mathcal{Y}}
.

(v)

A rational map
{\mathcal{X}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is
proper
if there exist a representative
({\mathcal{U}},f)
, a
proper birational morphism

{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}
, and a proper morphism
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
such that
f
factors up to
2
-isomorphism as

{\mathcal{U}}\cong{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\times_{{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{U}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}.

(4.1)

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f\colon{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}
be a representable proper birational morphism of stacks, with
{\mathcal{X}}
normal and
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
reduced.
Then the maximal open substack
{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
, over which
f
restricts to an isomorphism

f^{-1}({\mathcal{U}})\to{\mathcal{U}}
,
has complement everywhere of codimension
\geq 2
.

Proof.

For any finite-type morphism of locally Noetherian schemes, the set of points of the source, where the morphism is flat, is open by
[
21
, (11.1.1)]
.
By the local nature of flatness, we have the same for any morphism of stacks.
Letting
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
denote the locus where
f
is open, we claim that
{\mathcal{U}}
is the complement of
f({\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\setminus{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})
.
A representable proper birational morphism, if additionally flat, must be an isomorphism.
So

{\mathcal{X}}\setminus f({\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\setminus{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})\subset{\mathcal{U}},

and we have equality, since

{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
contains
f^{-1}({\mathcal{U}})
.

This observation shows that the formation of
{\mathcal{U}}
from
f\colon{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}
commutes with étale base change.
Thus we are reduced to proving the statement when
{\mathcal{X}}
is a scheme and
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
an algebraic space.
Then we obtain the statement by a straightforward application of the valuative criterion for properness.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
be stacks, with
{\mathcal{X}}
reduced, and

\mathrm{(i)}

The closure

\mathrm{(ii)}

Suppose

is a representative of the rational map
{\mathcal{X}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
.
Then there exists a reduced stack
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
with birational morphism to
{\mathcal{X}}
and morphism to
{\mathcal{Y}}
, such that

factors up to
2
-isomorphism as (
4.1
) and the induced morphism

Proof.

The independence of choice of representative follows from the fact that
{\mathcal{X}}
is reduced.
Suppose
{\mathcal{X}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
is proper, i.e., there exist
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
as in Definition
4.1
(v);
we may suppose
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
reduced.
The corresponding morphism

{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}

Since

Since, also,

is surjective, properness of

The morphism

The latter is obtained by base change from the diagonal of

So we have a projective morphism to the open substack

[

to realize the coherent sheaf on the open substack in the definition of projective morphism

This yields a stack

Now if

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
be a proper birational map of stacks, and let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{Y}}
be dense open substacks.

), the pre-images of
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}

{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}

Proof.

First we show that equality of pre-images in a factorization (

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
denote the common pre-image.
Then the proper birational morphisms
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{T}}
exhibit
{\mathcal{S}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}
as proper.

It remains to deduce equality of pre-images from the properness of
{\mathcal{S}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}
.
For this we may suppose without loss of generality that
{\mathcal{X}}
is reduced.
Then we have

, and

where

and

If

is proper, then by Proposition

So in each composition the first map, a dense open immersion, is proper, hence an isomorphism.
Thus

{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
for any factorization (

).
∎

For schemes, the canonical representative on the maximal domain of definition of a rational map requires the source to be reduced.
For stacks, even more is required: with “ghost automorphisms” (see, e.g.,

The role of the residue field at a point of a scheme is, for a stack, played by the

Remark 4.22
.

By Propositions
4.10
and
4.18
(i), birational equivalence in Definition
4.21
is an equivalence relation.
In the setting of (
4.2
),
we have already observed that without loss of generality
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
may be taken to be smooth (by resolution of singularities).
By Chow’s lemma,
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
may further be taken to be quasi-projective.
We also record the observation, that when
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
is a smooth quasi-projective stack and
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a stack, the following conditions on a morphism

are equivalent:

•

g
is representable and proper;

•

g
is projective.

Generally, projective morphisms are representable and proper.
Now suppose that
g
is representable and proper.
Letting

denote the coarse moduli space of
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
, we factor

as

{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\times{\mathcal{Z}}\to X^{\prime}\times{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}.

The first morphism, the graph of

, is representable and finite.
The second morphism
is quasi-finite and proper.
Their composite is quasi-finite and proper, yet is representable (since

is representable), hence is a representable finite morphism.
The last morphism is quasi-projective.
So
g
is as well, and we have what we want, since a proper quasi-projective morphism is projective.
In conclusion, Definition

admits an equivalent reformulation, where

is a smooth quasi-projective stack and

and

are birational projective morphisms.

If

In this section, we

We define

abelian groups receiving birational invariants of

, respectively

The following definition is in parallel with the definition of

Definition 7.1
.

The abelian groups
\cBurn_{n}

and
{\overline{\cBurn}}_{n}
are generated by
pairs

where

•

\mathcal{K}
is a projective linearizable DM stack of dimension
d\leq n
, and

•

\alpha=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-d})

n-d
of nonzero characters of the generic stabilizer
H
of
\mathcal{K}
, generating
H^{\vee}

The group
\cBurn_{n}
is defined by the following relations.

(O)
(re)ordering: For all permutations
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-d}

(\mathcal{K},\alpha)=(\mathcal{K},\alpha^{\prime}),\quad\alpha^{\prime}:=(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(n-d)}).

(I)

(V)
vanishing: For all

and

(B)
blowup: suppose

(\mathcal{K},\alpha)=\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2},

where

\Theta_{1}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $a=0$},\\
(\mathcal{K},\alpha_{1})+(\mathcal{K},\alpha_{2}),&\text{if $a\neq 0$},\end{cases}

(\mathcal{Y},(b_{2},\dots,b_{n-d})),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\mathcal{Y}
obtained by construction

from

\mathcal{K}
and the character

; the generic stabilizer of

is naturally a subgroup of the generic stabilizer of

,
and the character

is obtained by restricting

, for

,
\dots
,
n-d
.

The group

is defined by the same relations, except that

is replaced by

(
\overline{\mathbf{I}}
)

({\mathcal{K}},\alpha)=({\mathcal{K}}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})

if there is a birational map
{\mathcal{K}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
transforming
\alpha
to
\alpha^{\prime}
.

.

Connection with Grothendieck groups

The Grothendieck ring

{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var)

of algebraic varieties over
k
plays a special role in the theory of motivic integration: its quotient by the ideal generated the class of the affine line serves is the universal object in the category of motivic measures. Incidentally, this quotient is also isomorphic to the free abelian group generated by stable birational equivalence classes of algebraic varieties over
k

[
34
]
.

Several authors considered versions of these constructions in the setting of equivariant geometry and in the framework of stacks, see
[
11
]
and the references therein. There is a Grothendieck
group of
G
-varieties

{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var^{G}),

defined via
G
-scissors relations

[
11
, Defn. 3.1]
.
There is also a Grothendieck ring of equivariant varieties
[
11
, Defn. 3.7]

{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var^{\rm eq}):=\bigoplus_{G}\,{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var^{G}),

with an additional
induction relation
.
The sum is over all isomorphism classes of finite groups.

Analogous definitions can be made for DM stacks.
The abelian group

{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\mathrm{DM})

is defined via scissors relations. This is (with the natural ring structure) the Grothendieck ring of DM stacks
[
10
, §1.1]
.
There is a well-defined ring homomorphism

{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var^{\rm eq})\to{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\mathrm{DM}),

cf.
[
11
, (3.15)]
, combined with
[
10
, Thm. 1.1]
.
It takes the class of a smooth quasiprojective
G
-variety
X
to the class of the quotient stack
[X/G]
.

From the initial definition of the Burnside group in
[
27
]
, it was understood that it was a refinement of
{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var)
, in the following sense: there is a surjective homomorphism

\Burn\to\mathrm{gr}({\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var)).

Here we explain the connection of equivariant and stacky versions of Grothendieck groups with our formalism of Burnside groups. The key difference is that, in our approach, we take into account the actions in normal bundles of strata, in the stabilizer stratification.

There is a naive map

\Burn(G)\to\mathrm{gr}({\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\Var^{G})),

that annihilates all triples with nonempty sequence of characters.
The term
(1,G\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(X),())
is sent to the class of the
G
-variety
X
.
There is an analogous map

\cBurn\to\mathrm{gr}({\mathrm{K}}_{0}(\mathrm{DM})),

annihilating pairs with nonempty sequence of characters and sending
(k({\mathcal{X}}),())
to the class of the orbifold
{\mathcal{X}}
.

A new reduced order model of linear parabolic PDEs
Noel Walkington, Franziska Weber, Yangwen Zhang

Abstract
How to build an accurate reduced order model (ROM) for multidimensional time dependent partial differential equations (PDEs) is quite open. In this paper, we propose a new ROM for linear parabolic PDEs. We prove that our new method can be orders of magnitude faster than standard solvers, and is also much less memory intensive. Under some assumptions on the problem data, we prove that the convergence rates of the new method is the same with standard solvers. Numerical experiments are presented to confirm our theoretical result.

The new algorithm and its implementation

Throughout the paper, we assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

V

is dense in

(\varphi,\psi)_{V}=a(\varphi,\psi)\quad\text{ for all }\varphi,\psi\in V

with an associated norm given by
\|\cdot\|_{V}=\sqrt{a(\cdot,\cdot)}
. Since
V
is continuously embedded into
H
, there exists a constant
C_{V}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|\leq C_{V}\|\varphi\|_{V}\quad\text{ for all }\varphi\in V.

(2.1)

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}
denotes the duality pairing between
V
and its dual
V^{\prime}
. By identifying
H
and its dual
H^{\prime}
it follows that

V\hookrightarrow H=H^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V^{\prime},

each embedding being continuous and dense.

For given
f\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
and

we consider the linear parabolic problem:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}(u(t),v)+a(u(t),v)&=(f,v)\qquad\;\forall v\in V,\\
(u(0),v)&=(u_{0},v)\qquad\forall v\in V.\end{split}

(2.2)

2.1

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(u_{h}(t),v_{h}\right)+a(u_{h}(t),v_{h})&=(f,v_{h}),\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},\\

2.2
Reduced basis generation

Recall that the source term
f
does not depend on time. Let
\ell

\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{0}=f
. For

\displaystyle\begin{split}a\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{i},v_{h}\right)=\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{i-1},v_{h}\right)\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.\end{split}

(2.5)

To find an optimal reduced basis, we then consider the following minimization problem:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\min_{{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{1},\ldots,{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{r}\in V_{h}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\|\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{j},{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{i}\right)_{V}{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{i}\right\|_{V}^{2}\quad\text{ s.t. }\left({\widetilde{\varphi}}_{i},{\widetilde{\varphi}}_{j}\right)_{V}=\delta_{ij},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq r.\end{split}

(P1)

Lemma 1

The solution to problem (

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{j},\varphi\right)_{V}\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{j}\quad\textup{ for }\varphi\in V_{h}.

Furthermore, let

be the first

\displaystyle\textup{argmin\eqref{P1}}=\sum_{i=r+1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}({\mathcal{R}}).

Lemma

Lemma 2

Let

\displaystyle y_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}\mathcal{U}x_{k}

(2.6)

are orthonormal eigenfunctions of

Lemma

Let us define the linear and bounded operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{i},\qquad\textup{where}\;\alpha=[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}.

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{U}^{*}v_{h},\alpha\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}=\left(v_{h},\mathcal{U}\alpha\right)_{V}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{i},v_{h})_{V}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{*}v_{h}=\left[(\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{1},v_{h})_{V},\ldots,(\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{\ell},v_{h})_{V}\right]^{\top}.

Then we have

2.3
Discussion of matrix

The main computational cost in

Recall that

\displaystyle U_{\ell}=[\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{b}\mid\widehat{A}^{-2}\widehat{b}\mid\ldots\mid\widehat{A}^{-\ell}\widehat{b}].

The matrix

is nothing but the so-called Krylov matrix. Let

be the rank of

, then

\displaystyle\textup{span}\{\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{b},\widehat{A}^{-2}\widehat{b},\ldots,\widehat{A}^{-\ell}\widehat{b}\}=\textup{span}\{\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{b},\widehat{A}^{-2}\widehat{b},\ldots,\widehat{A}^{-r}\widehat{b}\}.

In other words, there is no need to compute
\widehat{A}^{-(r+1)}\widehat{b}
,
\ldots

\widehat{A}^{-\ell}\widehat{b}

is positive semi-definite. Hence, we only need to compute the minimal eigenvalue of the matrices

In practice, we terminate the process if the minimal eigenvalue is small.

Next, we show that the matrix
K_{r}
is Hankel type matrix, i.e., each ascending skew-diagonal from left to right is constant.

Lemma 4
.

For any
1\leq i\leq\ell
,
1\leq j\leq\ell

\displaystyle K_{1,i+j-1}=K_{2,i+j-2}=\ldots=K_{i,j}=K_{i+1,j-1}=\ldots=K_{i+j-1,1}.

Hence,
K
is Hankel type matrix.

Proof.

First, we define
\mathcal{A}_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}

\displaystyle a(w_{h},v_{h})=(\mathcal{A}_{h}w_{h},v_{h})\quad\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(2.17)

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{A}_{h}^{-1}
exists and
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is self-adjoint since the bilinear form

is symmetric. By (
2.5

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{i}=\mathcal{A}_{h}^{-1}\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{i-1}=\mathcal{A}_{h}^{-2}\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{i-2}=\ldots=\mathcal{A}_{h}^{-i}\mathfrak{u}_{h}^{0}.

By the definition
K_{\ell}
in (

Incremental SVD was proposed by Brand in

Conclusion

In the paper, we proposed a new reduced order model (ROM) of linear parabolic PDEs. We proved that the singular values of the Krylov sequence are exponential decaying (
Theorem

1
). Furthermore, under some assumptions, we proved that the solutions of the ROM and the FEM have the same convergence rates (
Theorem

2
). There are many interesting directions for the future research. First, we will investigate the non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and the corresponding Dirichlet boundary control problems, such as
[
13
,
12
,
16
,
7
,
11
]
.
Second, we will consider the ROM for hyperbolic PDEs and Maxwell’s equations
[
22
,
21
]
. Third, we will apply our result for realistic problems, such as inverse problems, shape optimization problems and data assimilation problems. Our long term goal is to build accurate ROMs for nonlinear PDEs.

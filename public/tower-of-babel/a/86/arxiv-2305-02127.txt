In the main article (Sec. 5), we introduced the ratio of the shear stress at the plates
\tau_{w}
to the horizontal velocity at kinetic boundary layer height
U_{V}
as a proxy for the strength of Ekman vortices. In both,
\tau_{w}
and
U_{V}
, we utilized the RMS of the horizontal velocity, since there is no horizontal mean velocity in rotating RBC. The horizontal RMS velocity is accurately computed from the two lateral velocity components by
u_{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{RMS}}=\sqrt{\left<u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}\right>_{\mathcal{H}}}
. The average dimensionless wall shear stress at both plates is then computed as:

\tau_{w}=\left<\left|\partial_{z}u_{\mathcal{H}}\right|\right>_{\mathcal{H},z=\{0,1\}}\approx\left<\left|\partial_{z}u_{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{RMS}}\right|\right>_{z=\{0,1\}}=\left<\left|\partial_{z}\sqrt{\left<u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}\right>_{\mathcal{H}}}\right|\right>_{z=\{0,1\}}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{.}

(S1)

Under the assumption that horizontal motion is predominantly vortical in rotating RBC, the horizontal RMS velocity would indicate the average tangential velocity at the outer radius of the vortices. Consider that a vortex with constant azimuthal velocity
u_{\vartheta}
will cause spatial fluctuations in
u_{x,y}=\pm u_{\vartheta}
, which are reflected in
u_{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{RMS}}
. Therefore,
u_{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{RMS}}
can serve as measure for the average rotation speed of the vortices
U_{V}
:

U_{V}=\left<u_{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{RMS}}\right>_{z=\{\lambda_{u},1-\lambda_{u}\}}=\left<\max\limits_{z}\left(u_{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{RMS}}\right)\right>_{z\lessgtr 0.5}=\left<\max\limits_{z}\left(\sqrt{\left<u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}\right>_{\mathcal{H}}}\right)\right>_{z\lessgtr 0.5}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{.}

(S2)

We suppose that the faster a vortex is spinning the more difficult it is to perturb, why
U_{V}
can be seen as a proxy for the strength of the vortices. Fig.
S1
shows the individual behavior of
\tau_{w}
and
U_{V}
, i.e., the components of the wall shear stress to vortex strength ratio (inverse
slope
boundary layer
\lambda_{\tau_{w}}^{-1}=\tau_{w}/U_{V}
) in the
(\mathrm{Ra},\mathrm{Ro}^{-1})
parameter space. The main trend of
\lambda_{\tau_{w}}
arises from the wall shear stress, which principally increases with increasing
\mathrm{Ra}
and
\mathrm{Ro}^{-1}
(Fig.
S1
(a,b)). Further,
\tau_{w}
already correlates to some degree with locations of the heat transport maxima at large
\mathrm{Ra}
. Still the normalization by
U_{V}
improves the correlation as
\lambda_{\tau_{w}}
shows (see main article, Sec. 5, Fig. 9(c,d)). Finally, we note that
U_{V}
is largest towards the weakly or non-rotating cases, but also intensifies around the heat transport maxima in the small-
\mathrm{Ra}
subregime, where vertically-coherent vortices support strong enhancement. This supports the conceptual idea of
U_{V}
and
\lambda_{\tau_{w}}^{-1}
as measure for the strength of Ekman vortices.

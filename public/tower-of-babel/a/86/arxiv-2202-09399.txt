States of finite effect algebras
G. Bińczak 1, J. Kaleta 2, A.Zembrzuski 3

Abstract
. In this paper we characterize finite effect algebras which have a state. We construct two matrices A A and B B assigned to a finite effect algebra E E and show that if E E has a state then rank A = A= rank B B .

Introduction

Effect algebras have been introduced by Foulis and Bennet in 1994 (see
[
5
]
) for the study of foundations of quantum mechanics (see
[
4
]
). Independtly, Chovanec and Kôpka introduced an essentially equivalent structure called
D
-
poset

(see
[
9
]
). Another equivalent structure was introduced by Giuntini and Greuling in
[
6
]
).

The most important example of an effect algebra is
(E(H),0,I,\oplus)
, where
H
is a Hilbert space and
E(H)
consists of all self-adjoint operators
A
on
H
such that
0\leq A\leq I
. For
A,B\in E(H)
,
A\oplus B
is defined if and only if
A+B\leq I
and then
A\oplus B=A+B
. Elements of
E(H)
are called
effects
and they play an important role in the theory of quantum measurements (
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
).

A quantum effect may be treated as two-valued (it means
0
or
1
) quantum measurement that may be unsharp (fuzzy). If there exist some pairs of effects
a,b
which posses an orthosum
a\oplus b
then this orthosum correspond to a parallel measurement of two effects.

In this paper we assign to each finite effect algebra a matrix
A
such that
E
has a state if and only if rank
A
=rank
(A|1)
and matrix equation

AX=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{array}\right]
has a solution in the form

X=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{array}\right]
such that
x_{i}\geq 0
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let us start with the following definition of an effect algebra.

Definition 1.1
.

In
[
5
]
an effect algebra is defined to be an algebraic system
(E,0,1,\oplus)
consisting of a set
E
, two special elements
0,1\in E
called the zero and the unit, and a partially defined binary operation
\oplus
on
E
that satisfies the following conditions for all
p,q,r\in E
:

(1)

[Commutative Law] If
p\oplus q
is defined, then
q\oplus p
is defined and
p\oplus q=q\oplus p
.

(2)

[Associative Law] If
q\oplus r
is defined and
p\oplus(q\oplus r)
is defined, then
p\oplus q
is defined,
(p\oplus q)\oplus r
is defined, and
p\oplus(q\oplus r)=(p\oplus q)\oplus r
.

(3)

[Orthosupplementation Law] For every
p\in E
there exists a unique
q\in E
such that
p\oplus q
is defined and
p\oplus q=1
.

(4)

[Zero-unit Law] If
1\oplus p
is defined, then
p=0
.

For simplicity , we often refer to
E
, rather than to
(E,0,1,\oplus)
, as being an effect algebra.

Definition 1.2
.

For effect algebras
E_{1},E_{2}
a mapping
\phi\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
is said to be an isomorphism if
\phi
is a bijection,

a\perp b\iff\phi(a)\perp\phi(b)
,
\phi(1)=1
and
\phi(a\oplus b)=\phi(a)\oplus\phi(b)
.

If
p,q\in E
, we say that
p
and
q
are orthogonal and write
p\perp q
iff
p\oplus q
is defined in
E
. If
p,q\in E
and
p\oplus q=1
, we call
q
the
orthosupplement
of
p
and write
p^{\prime}=q
.

It is shown in
[
5
]
that the relation
\leq
defined for
p,q\in E
by
p\leq q
iff
\exists r\in E
with
p\oplus r=q
is a partial order on
E
and
0\leq p\leq 1
holds for all
p\in E
. It is also shown that the mapping
p\mapsto p^{\prime}
is an order-reversing involution and that
q\perp p
iff
q\leq p^{\prime}
. Furtheremore,
E
satisfies the following
cancellation law
: If
p\oplus q\leq r\oplus q
, then
p\leq r
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x\in E
let
nx=x\oplus x\oplus\ldots\oplus x
(
n
-times). We write
{\rm ord}(x)=n\in{\mathbb{N}}
if
n
is the greatest integer such that
nx
exists in
E
.

An
atom
of an effect algebra
E
is a minimal element of
E\setminus\{0\}
. An effect algebra
E
is
atomic
if for every non-zero element
x\in E
there exists an atom
a\in E
such that
a\leq x
.

Definition 1.3
.

A state on an effect algebra
E
is a map
s\colon E\to[0,1]\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
such that
s(1)=1
and if
a\oplus b
is defined, then
s(a)+s(b)\leq 1
and
s(a\oplus b)=s(a)+s(b)
.

Proposition 1.4
.

[
10
, Proposition 3.1]

Let
E
be an orthocomplete atomic effect algebra. Then for every
x\in R
, there is a set
\{a_{i}|i\in I\}
of mutually different atoms in
E
and a set
\{k_{i}|i\in I\}
of positive integers such that
x=\bigoplus\{k_{i}a_{i}|i\in I\}
.

Let
E
be a finite effect algebra. Then
A
is orthocomplete and atomic. If
E
has atoms
a_{1},\ldots a_{m}
then by
1.4
for every
x\in E
there exist non-negative integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}
such that
x=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}a_{i}
. By
M_{n\times n}({\mathbb{N}})
we denote the set of all
n\times m
matrices which quantities are natural numbers (including
0
).

Definition 1.5
.

Let

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
y_{n1}&\ldots&y_{nm}\end{array}\right]\in M_{n\times m}({\mathbb{N}})

then denote

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}&1\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
y_{n1}&\ldots&y_{nm}&1\end{array}\right]

by
(A|1)
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
E
be a finite effect algebra with atoms
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
. Then define
Seq(E)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}\colon\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}x_{t}a_{t}=1\}
. Let
n=card(Seq(E))
and

\begin{array}[]{l}M(E)=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
y_{n1}&\ldots&y_{nm}\end{array}\right]\right.\in M_{n\times m}({\mathbb{N}})\colon\mathop{\forall}\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\quad\mathop{\exists}\limits_{1\leq t\leq m}y_{it}\not=y_{jt},\\
\{(y_{i1},\ldots,y_{im})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}\colon 1\leq i\leq n\}=Seq(E)\Bigg\}.\end{array}

It turns out that rows in
A\in M(E)
are all elements of
Seq(E)
. Moreover if
A,B\in M(E)
then
B
can be obtained from
A
by permuting its rows.

In order to prove the main Theorem, we need use the following two lemmas:

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
E
be a finite effect algebra with atoms
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
and

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
y_{n1}&\ldots&y_{nm}\end{array}\right]\in M(E).

If
x=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}\in E
then there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
k_{t}\leq y_{it}
for
1\leq t\leq m
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a finite effect algebra with atoms
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
and

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
y_{n1}&\ldots&y_{nm}\end{array}\right]\in M(E).

Let
x=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}\in E
and
x^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}a_{t}
then
\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}(k_{t}+l_{t})a_{t}=x\oplus x^{\prime}=1
so

(k_{1}+l_{1},\ldots,k_{m}+l_{m})\in Seq(E)
and there exists
1\leq i\leq k
such that
(k_{1}+l_{1},\ldots,k_{m}+l_{m})=(y_{i1},\ldots,y_{im})
. Hence

k_{t}\leq k_{t}+l_{t}=y_{it}
for
1\leq t\leq m
.
∎

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
E
be a finite effect algebra with atoms
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
and

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
y_{n1}&\ldots&y_{nm}\end{array}\right]\in M(E).

If
x=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}a_{t}\in E
then there exists
1\leq p,q\leq k
such that
k_{t}\leq y_{pt}
,
l_{t}\leq y_{qt}
and
y_{pt}-k_{t}=y_{qt}-l_{t}
for
1\leq t\leq m
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a finite effect algebra with atoms
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
and

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
y_{n1}&\ldots&y_{nm}\end{array}\right]\in M(E).

Let
x=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}a_{t}\in E
. Let
z=x^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}r_{t}a_{t}
then
\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}(k_{t}+r_{t})a_{t}=x\oplus x^{\prime}=1

and
\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}(l_{t}+r_{t})a_{t}=x\oplus x^{\prime}=1
so
(k_{1}+r_{1},\ldots,k_{m}+r_{m})\in Seq(E)
and
(l_{1}+r_{1},\ldots,l_{m}+r_{m})\in Seq(E)
and there exist
1\leq p,q\leq n
such that

(k_{1}+r_{1},\ldots,k_{m}+r_{m})=(y_{p1},\ldots,y_{pm})
and
(l_{1}+r_{1},\ldots,l_{m}+r_{m})=(y_{q1},\ldots,y_{qm})
. Hence

k_{t}\leq k_{t}+r_{t}=y_{pt}
,
l_{t}\leq l_{t}+r_{t}=y_{qt}
and
y_{pt}-k_{t}=r_{t}=y_{qt}-l_{t}
for
1\leq t\leq m
.
∎

The following theorem (the main theorem) gives the necessary and sufficient conditions enabling algebra to have a state.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
E
be a finite effect algebra with atoms
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
. Let
A\in M(E)
and
B=(A|1)
. Then
E
has a state if and only if rank
A=
rank
B

and there exist

s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\in[0,+\infty)
such that

A\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\\
\vdots\\
s_{m}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{array}\right].

Proof.

Let

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{11}&\ldots&y_{1m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
y_{k1}&\ldots&y_{km}\end{array}\right]\in M(E).

\Leftarrow

Suppose that there exist
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\in[0,+\infty)
such that

A\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\\
\vdots\\
s_{m}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{array}\right].

Then
\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{it}s_{t}=1
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

Let
h\colon E\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a map such that
h(\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}x_{t}a_{t})=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}x_{t}s_{t}
. By
1.4

h
is defined for all
x\in E
.

First we show that
h
is well-defined.

Let
x\in E
and
x=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}a_{t}
by
1.8
there exist
1\leq p,q\leq k
such that
k_{t}\leq y_{pt}
,
l_{t}\leq y_{qt}
and
y_{pt}-k_{t}=y_{qt}-l_{t}
for
1\leq t\leq m
. Hence
\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}s_{t}-\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}s_{t}=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}(k_{t}-l_{t})s_{t}=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}(y_{pt}-y_{qt})s_{t}=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{pt}s_{t}-\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{qt}s_{t}=1-1=0
. Thus
\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}s_{t}=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}s_{t}
so
h(\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t})=h(\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}a_{t})
.

Now we show that
h\colon E\to[0,1]
. If
x=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}
then there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
k_{t}\leq y_{it}
for
1\leq t\leq m
by
1.7
. Hence

0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}s_{t}=h(\bigoplus_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t})\leq\sum_{t=1}^{m}y_{it}s_{t}=1,

that is why
0\leq h(x)\leq 1
.

Since
1=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{1t}a_{t}
so
h(1)=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{1t}s_{t}=1
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in E
,
x_{1}=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}
and
x_{2}=\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}a_{t}
. Suppose
x_{1}\oplus x_{2}
is defined then

x_{1}\oplus x_{2}=\bigoplus_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}a_{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}a_{t}=\bigoplus_{t=1}^{m}(k_{t}+l_{t})a_{t}

so by
1.7
there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
k_{t}+l_{t}\leq y_{it}
for
1\leq t\leq m
. Thus
h(x_{1})+h(x_{2})=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}s_{t}+\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}s_{t}=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}(k_{t}+l_{t})s_{t}\leq\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{it}s_{t}=1
.

Moreover
h(x_{1}\oplus x_{2})=h(\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}(k_{t}+l_{t})a_{t})=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}(k_{t}+l_{t})s_{t}=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}s_{t}+\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}l_{t}s_{t}=h(x_{1})+h(x_{2})
. Hence

h
is a state.

\Rightarrow

Suppose that
E
has a state
h\colon E\to[0,1]
. Let
s_{t}=h(a_{t})\geq 0
for
1\leq t\leq m
. We know that
\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{it}a_{t}=1
for
1\leq i\leq n
since
A\in M(E)
.
Hence
1=h(1)=h(\bigoplus\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{it}a_{t})=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{it}h(a_{t})=\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}y_{it}s_{t}
. Thus

A\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\\
\vdots\\
s_{m}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{array}\right].

By Rouché-Capelli Theorem rank
A=
rank
B
, where
B=(A|1)

∎

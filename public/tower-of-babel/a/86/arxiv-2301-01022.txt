Naoki Tsuge

Abstract
. In this paper, we are concerned with the Cauchy problem for isentropic gas dynamics. Through the contribution of many researchers such as Lax, P. D., Glimm, J., DiPerna, R. J. and Liu, T. P., the decay of solutions was established. They treated with initial data with the small total variation. On the other hand, the decay for large initial data has been open for half a century. Our goal is to provide a new method to analyze this problem. We prove the existence of a global attractor, which yields a decay of solutions for large data. To construct approximate solutions, we introduce a modified Godunov scheme.

1.
Introduction

The present paper is concerned with isentropic gas dynamics

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\rho_{t}+m_{x}=0,}\\
\displaystyle{m_{t}+\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p(\rho)\right)_{x}=0,}\end{cases}x\in{\bf R},\quad t\in{\bf R}_{+},

(1.1)

where
\rho
,
m
and
p
are the density, the momentum and the
pressure of the gas, respectively. If
\rho>0
,

v=m/\rho
represents the velocity of the gas. For a barotropic gas,

p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}/\gamma
, where
\gamma\in(1,5/3]
is the
adiabatic exponent for usual gases.

We consider the initial value problem (
1.1
)
with the initial data

\displaystyle(\rho,m)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0}(x),m_{0}(x)),

(1.2)

where

\displaystyle(\rho_{0}(x),m_{0}(x))=(\bar{\rho},0)\text{ outside a finite interval}

(1.3)

and

\bar{\rho}
is a positive constant.
The above problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) can be written in the following form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=0,\quad{x}\in{\bf R},\quad t\in{\bf R}_{+},\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

by using
u={}^{t}(\rho,m)
,
\displaystyle f(u)={}^{t}\!\left(m,\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p(\rho)\right)
.

We recollect the known results of the above problem. DiPerna
[
5
]

proved the global existence of solutions to
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
) by the vanishing viscosity method and a compensated compactness argument.
DiPerna first applied the method to (
1.1
) for
the special case where
\gamma=1+2/n
and
n
is an odd
integer. We notice that this result can treat with the arbitrary
L^{\infty}
data. Subsequently, Ding, Chen and Luo
[
6
]

and Chen
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
extended his analysis to any
\gamma
in
(1,5/3]
.

On the other hand, the existence of solutions to conservation laws including (
1.1
) was established by Glimm
[
8
]
. Glimm treatd the Cauchy problem
with initial data having small total variation.
The theory of decay for genuinely nonlinear 2
\times
2 systems of conservation laws was constructed by Glimm-Lax
[
9
]
. Glimm-Lax showed that if initial data are constant outside a finite interval and have locally bounded total variation and small oscillation, then the tonal
variation of the solution of
[
8
]
decays to zero at the rate
t^{-1/2}
.
The Glimm-Lax theory had been further developed by DiPerna and Liu:
general conservation laws with a convex entropy function
[
4
]
,
general conservation laws with small initial data in total variation
[
10
]
. However, the decay for large initial data has been open for half a century. Our goal in the present paper is to provide a new method to analyze this problem and investigate the decay structure of (
1.1
). We first introduce a modified Godunov scheme
to construct approximate solutions. We next prove the existence of a global attractor, which
yields decay estimates of solutions for large initial data.

To state our main theorem, we define the Riemann invariants
w,z
, which play important roles
in this paper, as

Definition 1.1
.

\displaystyle w(u):=\frac{m}{\rho}+\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}=v+\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta},\quad{z}(u):=\frac{m}{\rho}-\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}=v-\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\quad\left(\theta=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\right).

These Riemann invariants satisfy the following.

Remark 1.1
.

\displaystyle|w|\geq|z|,\;w\geq 0,\;\mbox{\rm when}\;v\geq 0.\quad|w|\leq|z|,\;z\leq 0,\;\mbox{\rm when}\;v\leq 0.

\displaystyle v=\frac{w+z}{2},\;\rho=\left(\frac{\theta(w-z)}{2}\right)^{1/\theta},\;m=\rho v.

(1.6)

From the above, the lower bound of
z
and the upper bound of
w
yield the bound of
\rho
and
|v|
.

We next introduce the mechanical energy as
\displaystyle\eta_{\ast}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\gamma(\gamma-1)}\rho^{\gamma}
and set

\displaystyle J(u)=\eta_{\ast}(u)-\frac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}\rho+\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\gamma}}{\gamma}.

(1.7)

Remark 1.2
.

From the convexity of
\eta_{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}J(u)=\eta_{\ast}(u)-\eta_{\ast}(\bar{\rho},0)-\dfrac{\partial\eta_{\ast}}{\partial\rho}(\bar{\rho},0)(\rho-\bar{\rho})-\dfrac{\partial\eta_{\ast}}{\partial m}(\bar{\rho},0)m\geq 0.\end{split}

(1.8)

From the conservation of mass and the energy inequality,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq\int^{x}_{-\infty}J(u(y,t))dy\leq\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}J(u(x,t))dx\leq\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}J(u_{0}(x))dx.\end{split}

(1.9)

Moreover, we define the entropy weak solution.

Definition 1.2
.

A measurable function
u(x,t)
is called a global entropy weak solution of the
Cauchy problems (
1
) if

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\int^{\infty}_{0}u\phi_{t}+f(u)\phi_{x}dxdt+\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}u_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)dx=0

holds for any test function
\phi\in C^{1}_{0}({\bf R}\times{\bf R}_{+})
and

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\int^{\infty}_{0}\hskip-4.30554pt

\displaystyle\eta(u)\psi_{t}+q(u)\psi_{x}+\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\hskip-4.30554pt\eta(u_{0}(x))\psi(x,0)dx\geq 0

holds for any non-negative test function
\psi\in C^{1}_{0}({\bf R}\times{\bf R}_{+})
, where

(\eta,q)
is a pair of convex entropy–entropy flux of (
1.1
).

Finally, we define
\tilde{z},\tilde{w}
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{z}(x,t)=z(x,t)-\int^{x}_{-\infty}J(u(y,t))dy,\;\;\tilde{w}(x,t)=w(x,t)-\int^{x}_{-\infty}J(u(y,t))dy.\end{split}

(1.10)

Then, our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

We assume that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{0}(x)\geq 0\quad{a.e.\ }x\in{\bf R},\quad\rho_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\bf R}),\quad\dfrac{m_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\in L^{\infty}({\bf R}).\end{split}

(1.11)

Then, there exists a global entropy weak solution of the
Cauchy problems (
1
). Moreover, for any positive constant
\varepsilon

there exist positive constants

such that the solution satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}-E_{0}-\varepsilon\leq\tilde{z}(x,t),\quad\tilde{w}(x,t)\leq\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}+\varepsilon,\quad\rho(x,t)\geq 0,\\
&{a.e.\ }(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[t_{0},\infty),\end{split}

(1.12)

where

\displaystyle E_{0}=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}J(u_{0}(x))dx.

(1.13)

Remark 1.3
.

We remark some points for the above theorem.

In view of (
1.3
), we find that

-\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}-E_{0}\geq{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(\tilde{z}(x,0)\right),\;{\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(\tilde{w}(x,0)\right)\geq\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}
. Therefore, (

) means the decay estimate of
{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(\tilde{z}(x,t)\right)
and

{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(\tilde{w}(x,t)\right)

We similarly obtain

-\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}\geq{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(z(x,0)\right),\;{\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(w(x,0)\right)\geq\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}
. If
-\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}-E_{0}-\varepsilon>{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(z(x,0)\right)
and

{\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(w(x,0)\right)>\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}+E_{0}+\varepsilon
,
(
1.12
) yields the decay estimate of

{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(z(x,t)\right)
and
{\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(w(x,t)\right)
. In fact, we find that

\displaystyle{\rm ess}\inf_{x}

\displaystyle\left(z(x,t_{0})\right)-{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(z(x,0)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(\tilde{z}(x,t_{0})+\int^{x}_{-\infty}J(u(y,t))dy\right)-{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(z(x,0)\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}-E_{0}-\varepsilon-{\rm ess}\inf_{x}\left(z(x,0)\right)>0,

\displaystyle{\rm ess}\sup_{x}

\displaystyle\left(w(x,t_{0})\right)-{\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(w(x,0)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(\tilde{w}(x,t_{0})+\int^{x}_{-\infty}J(u(y,t))dy\right)-{\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(w(x,0)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{\theta}}{\theta}+\varepsilon+E_{0}-{\rm ess}\sup_{x}\left(w(x,0)\right)<0.

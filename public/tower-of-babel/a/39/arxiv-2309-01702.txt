On Gevrey regularity of solutions for inhomogeneous nonlinear moment partial differential equations
Pascal Remy, Maria Suwińska

Abstract
. In this article we investigate Gevrey regularity of formal power series solutions for a certain class of nonlinear moment partial differential equations, the inhomogeneity of which is σ \sigma -Gevrey with respect to the time variable t t for a fixed σ ≥ 0 \sigma\geq 0 . The results are achieved by analyzing the geometric structure of the Newton polygon associated with the equation and are a generalization of similar results obtained for standard nonlinear partial differential equations as well as linear moment differential equations.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(e,E)

\mathbb{C}

(1)

The function

(a)

(b)

(c)

the function

(d)

\varepsilon>0

such that

(2)

The function

satisfies the following points:

(a)

is entire on
\mathbb{C}
with a global exponential growth of order at most

(b)

the function

is integrable at zero in

(3)

The functions

(a)

the function

(2)

m(\lambda)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\lambda-1}e(t)dt\quad\text{for all Re}(\lambda)\geq 0;

(b)

the function
E
has the power series expansion

(3)

E(t)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dfrac{t^{j}}{m(j)}\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{C}.

Example 2.8
.

For any fixed

is a regular moment function of order

Indeed, if we consider Stirling’s Formula

\sqrt{2\pi}t^{t-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-t}\leq\Gamma(t)\leq\sqrt{2\pi}e^{\frac{1}{12t}}t^{t-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-t}<\sqrt{2\pi}t^{t-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-t+1}\textrm{ for every }t\geq 1,

2.2.
Moment differentiation

The notion of moment differential operators or moment derivatives was first introduced by W. Balser and M. Yoshino in
[
3
]
.

Definition 2.9

(Moment derivation)
.

Let
m_{0}
be a moment function of order
s_{0}>0
and
\widetilde{u}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]
a formal power series written in the form

\widetilde{u}(t,x)=\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j,*}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{m_{0}(j)}.

Then, the
moment derivative
\partial_{m_{0};t}\widetilde{u}
of
\widetilde{u}(t,x)
with respect to
t
is the formal power series in
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]
defined by

\partial_{m_{0};t}\widetilde{u}(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j+1,\ast}(x)\dfrac{t^{j}}{m_{0}(j)}.

Observe that, for
m_{0}(\lambda)=\Gamma(1+\lambda)
, the operator
\partial_{m_{0};t}
coincides with the standard derivation operator
\partial_{t}
with respect to
t
.

Observe also that Definition
2.9
can be naturally extended to analytic functions at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
by means of their representation in the form of an infinite series. In particular, we can define in the same way the moment derivation
\partial_{m_{j};x_{j}}
with respect to
x_{j}
for any moment function
m_{j}
of order
s_{j}>0
and any
j\in\{1,...,N\}
. Thereby, for any formal power series
\widetilde{u}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]
written in the form

\widetilde{u}(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}u_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}}\dfrac{t^{j_{0}}}{m_{0}(j_{0})}\dfrac{x_{1}^{j_{1}}}{m_{1}(j_{1})}...\dfrac{x_{N}^{j_{N}}}{m_{N}(j_{N})},

the following identity holds for any
i_{0},i_{1},...,i_{N}\geq 0
:

\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{0}}\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{i_{1}}...\partial_{m_{N};x_{N}}^{i_{N}}\widetilde{u}(t,x)\\
=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{0},...,j_{n}\geq 0}u_{j_{0}+i_{0},j_{1}+i_{1},...,j_{N}+i_{N}}\dfrac{t^{j_{0}}}{m_{0}(j_{0})}\dfrac{x_{1}^{j_{1}}}{m_{1}(j_{1})}...\dfrac{x_{N}^{j_{N}}}{m_{N}(j_{N})}.

Observe that the operator
\partial_{m_{0};t}
commutes with any operator
\partial_{m_{j};x_{j}}
, and that the operator
\partial_{m_{j};x_{j}}
commutes with any operator
\partial_{m_{\ell};x_{\ell}}
as soon as
j\neq\ell
.

Observe also that the previous definition can be also naturally extended to analytic functions at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
by means of their representation in the form of an infinite series. Doing so, and using inequality (
4
), one can easily check that, if
a(t,x)
is an analytic function at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
, say on a polydisc
D_{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}}
, then the formal power series
\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{0}}\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{i_{1}}...\partial_{m_{N};x_{N}}^{i_{N}}a(t,x)
defines an analytic function on a polydisc
D_{\rho_{0}^{\prime},\rho_{1}^{\prime},...,\rho_{N}^{\prime}}
with convenient radii
0<\rho_{j}^{\prime}\leq\rho_{j}
for all
j=0,...,N
. In particular, this function may be analytic on a polydisc smaller than the initial polydisc of analyticity of
a(t,x)
.

However, as the following result shows, this does not occur in the case where the moments
m_{j}
are all regular.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
a(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
be an analytic function on
D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}}
, and let
m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{n}
be
n+1
regular moment functions of respective orders
s_{0},s_{1},...,s_{n}>0
. Then, for any
i_{0},i_{1},...,i_{N}\geq 0
, the formal power series
\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{0}}\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{i_{1}}...\partial_{m_{N};x_{N}}^{i_{n}}a(t,x)
also define analytic functions on
D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}}
.

Proof.

Let us write
a(t,x)
in the form

a(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}a_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}}\dfrac{t^{j_{0}}}{m_{0}(j_{0})}\dfrac{x_{1}^{j_{1}}}{m_{1}(j_{1})}...\dfrac{x_{N}^{j_{N}}}{m_{N}(j_{N})}

so that

\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{0}}\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{i_{1}}...\partial_{m_{N};x_{N}}^{i_{N}}a(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{0},...,j_{N}\geq 0}v_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}}t^{j_{0}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}

with

v_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}}=\dfrac{a_{j_{0}+i_{0},j_{1}+i_{1},...,j_{N}+i_{N}}}{m_{0}(j_{0})m_{1}(j_{1})...m_{N}(j_{N})}.

For any
d\in\{0,...,N\}
, let us choose two radii
r_{d},r^{\prime}_{d}>0
such that
r_{d}<r^{\prime}_{d}<\rho_{d}
. By assumption, there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\left|\dfrac{a_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}}}{m_{0}(j_{0})m_{1}(j_{1})...m_{N}(j_{N})}\right|\leq C\left(\dfrac{1}{r_{0}^{\prime}}\right)^{j_{0}}\left(\dfrac{1}{r_{1}^{\prime}}\right)^{j_{1}}...\left(\dfrac{1}{r_{N}^{\prime}}\right)^{j_{N}}

for all
j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0
. Then, for all
\left|t\right|\leq r_{0}
and all
\left|x_{d}\right|\leq r_{d}
,
d=1,...,N
, we get

\left|v_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}}t^{j_{0}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}\right|\leq\dfrac{C}{r_{0}^{\prime i_{0}}r_{1}^{\prime i_{1}}...r_{N}^{\prime i_{N}}}\left(\displaystyle\prod_{d=0}^{N}\left(\dfrac{r_{d}}{r^{\prime}_{d}}\right)^{j_{d}}\right)\left(\displaystyle\prod_{d=0}^{N}\dfrac{m_{d}(j_{d}+i_{d})}{m_{d}(j_{d})}\right)

for all
j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}
. Since
m_{d}
is a regular moment function of order
s_{d}
, there exist two positive constants
c_{d},C_{d}>0
such that

c_{d}(j+1)^{s_{d}}\leq\dfrac{m_{d}(j+1)}{m_{d}(j)}\leq C_{d}(j+1)^{s_{d}}

for all
j\geq 0
. Then,

\dfrac{m_{d}(j_{d}+i_{d})}{m_{d}(j_{d})}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i_{d}=0\\
\displaystyle\prod_{k=j_{d}}^{j_{d}+i_{d}-1}\dfrac{m_{d}(k+1)}{m_{d}(k)}&\text{if }i_{d}\geq 1\end{cases}\leq C_{d}^{i_{d}}(j_{d}+1)^{s_{d}}...(j_{d}+i_{d})^{s_{d}}

and the previous estimates become

\left|v_{j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{n}}t^{j_{0}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{n}^{j_{n}}\right|\leq\dfrac{C}{r_{0}^{\prime i_{0}}r_{1}^{\prime i_{1}}...r_{n}^{\prime i_{n}}}\left(\displaystyle\prod_{d=0}^{N}C_{d}^{i_{d}}(j_{d}+1)^{s_{d}}...(j_{d}+i_{d})^{s_{d}}\left(\dfrac{r_{d}}{r^{\prime}_{d}}\right)^{j_{d}}\right)

for all
j_{0},j_{1},...,j_{N}
, all
\left|t\right|\leq r_{0}
and all
\left|x_{d}\right|\leq r_{d}
.

Since
r_{d}<r^{\prime}_{d}
, these inequalities prove in particular that the formal power series
\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{0}}\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{i_{1}}...\partial_{m_{n};x_{n}}^{i_{n}}a(t,x)
is normally convergent on the closed polydisc
\overline{D}_{r_{0}}\times\overline{D}_{r_{1}}\times...\times\overline{D}_{r_{n}}
; hence, on all the compact sets of
D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}}
. Consequently, it defines an analytic function on
D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

3.
Gevrey formal power series

Let us now recall the definition and basic properties of formal power series of a given Gevrey order
\sigma
.

Definition 3.1

(Gevrey order)
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
. Then, a formal power series

\widetilde{u}(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j,\ast}(x)t^{j}\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]

is said to be
Gevrey of order
\sigma
(or, for short,
\sigma
-Gevrey
) if there exist a radius
0<r<\min\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{N}\}
and two positive constants
C,K>0
such that the inequalities

\left|u_{j,\ast}(x)\right|\leq CK^{j}\Gamma(1+\sigma j)

hold for all
x\in D_{r,...,r}
and all
j\geq 0
.

In other words, Definition
3.1
means that
\widetilde{u}(t,x)
is
\sigma
-Gevrey in
t
, uniformly in
x
on a neighborhood of
x=(0,...,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

Notation 3.2
.

We denote by
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}
the set of all the formal series in
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]
which are
\sigma
-Gevrey.

Observe that any formal power series in
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{0}
defines an analytic function at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
.

Observe also that the sets
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}
are filtered as follows:

(5)

\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{0}\subset\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}\\
\subset\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]

for all
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
satisfying
0<\sigma<\sigma^{\prime}<+\infty
.

The proposition below specifies the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}
.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
. Then, the set
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}
endowed with the usual algebraic operations and the usual derivations
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{x_{d}}
with
d=1,...,N
is a
\mathbb{C}
-differential algebra.

With respect to moment derivations
\partial_{m_{0};t}
and
\partial_{m_{d};x_{d}}
, we can also prove the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{N}
be
N+1
moment functions and
\widetilde{u}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]
a
\sigma
-Gevrey formal power series with
\sigma\geq 0
. Then,

(1)

the formal power series
\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{0}}\widetilde{u}(t,x)
is still
\sigma
-Gevrey for any
i_{0}\geq 0
.

(2)

the formal power series
\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{i_{1}}...\partial_{m_{N};x_{N}}^{i_{N}}\widetilde{u}(t,x)
is still
\sigma
-Gevrey for any
i_{1},....,i_{N}\geq 0
.

Proof.

The proof of the first point is similar to the one of Proposition
2.10
and is left to the reader. As for the proof of the second point, it is much more complicated and is essentially based on the integral representation of moment derivatives of analytic functions at the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
(
[
11
, Prop. 3]
). We refer to
[
16
]
for more details.
∎

Observe that Proposition
3.4
does not say that the set
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}
is stable under the moment derivatives
\partial_{m_{d};x_{d}}
, since we have a priori no control on the domain of analyticity of the function
\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{i_{1}}...\partial_{m_{N};x_{N}}^{i_{N}}u_{j,\ast}(x)
. However, when we consider only regular moment functions (see Proposition
2.10
), we can state the following.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{N}
be
N+1
regular moment functions and
\sigma\geq 0
. Then, the set
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}
is stable under the moment derivatives
\partial_{m_{0};t}
and
\partial_{m_{d};x_{d}}
for all
d=1,...,N
.

4.
Modified Nagumo norms

In this section we introduce the concept of modified Nagumo norms, aiming to create a tool with properties similar to standard Nagumo norms (see
[
14
,
4
]
) that can be used in the framework of moment differential operators. Below we expand on the idea introduced in
[
24
]
.

Notation 4.1
.

For any
\alpha\geq 0
and
s>0
, we consider the formal power series

\Theta_{\alpha,s}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha+j-1}{j}^{s}x^{j}

with

\dbinom{\alpha+j-1}{j}=\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha+j)}{\Gamma(1+j)\Gamma(\alpha)}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j=0\\
\dfrac{\alpha(\alpha+1)...(\alpha+j-1)}{j!}&\text{if }j\geq 1\end{cases}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\Theta_{0,s}(x)=1\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\Theta_{\alpha,1}(x)=1+\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\dfrac{\alpha(\alpha+1)...(\alpha+j-1)}{j!}x^{j}=\dfrac{1}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}\ \textrm{ for }|x|<1.

The definition of the modified Nagumo norms is based on the classical notion of
majorant series
. Recall that a formal power series

\widetilde{V}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}V_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}[[x]]

is called
a majorant series
of

\widetilde{v}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}v_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}\in\mathbb{C}[[x]]

if
\left|v_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}\right|\leq V_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}
for all
j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0
. In this case, we denote
\widetilde{v}(x)\ll\widetilde{V}(x)
.

Definition 4.2

(Modified Nagumo norms)
.

Let
f(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
be an analytic function on a polydisc
D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}}
. Moreover, let
s=(s_{1},...,s_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})^{N}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{N})\in[1,+\infty[^{N}\cup\{0\}
and suppose that
0<r<\min(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N})
. Then, the modified Nagumo norm
\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}
of
f
with indices
(\alpha,r,s)
is defined by:

\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}\left|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}\right|r^{j_{1}+...+j_{N}}&\text{if }\alpha=0\\
\inf\left(A\geq 0:f(x)\ll A\displaystyle\prod_{d=1}^{N}\dfrac{1}{r^{\alpha_{d}}}\Theta_{\alpha_{d},s_{d}}\left(\dfrac{x_{d}}{r}\right)\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Remark 4.3
.

The modified Nagumo norms are well defined for
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
. To prove that this is indeed the case, firstly let us notice that every
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
has a majorant series of the form
\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}|x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}
. From this it follows that

f(x)\ll r^{\lambda(\alpha)}\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}|\dfrac{x_{1}^{j_{1}}}{r^{\alpha_{1}}}...\dfrac{x_{N}^{j_{N}}}{r^{\alpha_{N}}}\\
\ll r^{\lambda(\alpha)}\sum_{j\geq 0}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0\\
j_{1}+...+j_{N}=j\end{subarray}}|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}|\dfrac{x_{1}^{j_{1}}}{r^{\alpha_{1}}}...\dfrac{x_{N}^{j_{N}}}{r^{\alpha_{N}}}

Furthermore, let us notice that

(6)

\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j-1}{j}^{s_{d}}\geq 1\ \textrm{for any}\ d=1,\ldots,N.

Moreover, seeing as
f(x)
is an analytic function, every coefficient

|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}|
can be bounded from above by
Mr^{-(j_{1}+\ldots+j_{N})}
with
M=\sup_{|\xi|\leq r}|f(\xi)|
. The conclusion follows directly from these facts.

Furthermore, let us observe that for
\alpha\in]0,1[^{N}
, inequality from (
6
) fails. Indeed, the coefficient
\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j-1}{j}
decreases when
n
tends to infinity. Hence, for any fixed
\alpha_{d}\in]0,1[
we have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j-1}{j}=0

from the Stirling’s Formula. Consequently, the modified Nagumo norms cannot be defined this way when
\alpha_{d}\in]0,1[
.

Proposition 4.4
.

For fixed (
\alpha,r,s)
, the function
\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}:\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
defines a norm on
\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
.

Proof.

Let us fix
\alpha
,
r
and
s
.
For any function
f
obviously
\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}\geq 0
and equality holds only for
f\equiv 0
. Moreover, for any constant
C
equality
\left\|Cf\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}=|C|\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}
holds following from the definition of the majorant series. As such it remains to show that the triangle inequality holds for any two functions
f,g\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
. To this end, notice that for functions
f(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}
and
g(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}g_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}...x_{N}^{j_{N}}
we have
|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}+g_{j_{1},...,h_{N}}|\leq|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}|+|g_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}|
for every
j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0
. The conclusion follows directly from the definition of the majorant series.
∎

For the remainder of this section, we shall show that the modified Nagumo norms given in Definition
4.2
have properties similar to the classical Nagumo norms. To this end, results from
[
24
]
will be adapted to the slightly more general case considered in this paper.

Let us start with two elementary technical lemmas.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. Then, the following identity holds for all integer
j\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j}\dbinom{\alpha+k-1}{k}\dbinom{\beta+j-k-1}{n-k}=\dbinom{\alpha+\beta+j-1}{j}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to observe that the identity

\dfrac{1}{(1-x)^{\alpha+\beta}}=\dfrac{1}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}\times\dfrac{1}{(1-x)^{\beta}}

implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha+\beta+j-1}{j}x^{j}

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha+j-1}{j}x^{j}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\beta+j-1}{j}x^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j}\dbinom{\alpha+k-1}{k}\dbinom{\beta+j-k-1}{j-k}\right)x^{j}.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
0\leq a\leq b
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Then,
\dbinom{a+\alpha}{a}\leq\dbinom{b+\alpha}{b}
.

Proof.

The inequality is clear for
\alpha=0
and for
a=b=0
. Let us now fix
\alpha,b>0
and let us consider the function
f_{b}:a\in[0,b]\longmapsto\dbinom{a+\alpha}{a}
. Its derivative is given by

f^{\prime}_{b}(a)=\dbinom{a+\alpha}{a}(\Psi(1+a+\alpha)-\Psi(1+a))

with is positive since
\Psi
is an increasing function on
]0,+\infty[
(the function
\ln\Gamma
is convex on
]0,+\infty[
). Lemma
4.6
follows.
∎

In the first two results below, we are interested in the modified Nagumo norms of a product.

Proposition 4.7

(Adaptation of
[
24
]
, Lemma 2)
.

Let
f(x),g(x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
.

Let
s\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
.
Let
\alpha,\beta\in[1,+\infty[^{N}\cup\{0\}
and
0<r<\min(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N})
.

Then,
\left\|fg\right\|_{\alpha+\beta,r,s}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}\left\|g\right\|_{\beta,r,s}
.

Proof.

First let us consider
(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
. Then

f(x)\ll\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{r,s,\alpha}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)}}\prod_{d=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\alpha_{d}+j-1}{j}^{s_{d}}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}}

and

g(x)\ll\frac{\left\|g\right\|_{r,s,\beta}}{r^{\lambda(\beta)}}\prod_{d=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\beta_{d}+j-1}{j}^{s_{d}}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}}.

From this and the definition of the majorant series, it follows that

f(x)g(x)\ll\dfrac{\left\|f\right\|_{r,s,\alpha}\left\|g\right\|_{r,s,\beta}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)+\lambda(\beta)}}\prod_{d=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{\alpha_{d}+k-1}{k}^{s_{d}}\binom{\beta_{d}+j-k-1}{j-k}^{s_{d}}\right)\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}}.

Let us now notice that
a^{s}+b^{s}\leq(a+b)^{s}
for any
a,b>0
and
s\geq 1
. Hence, for any
j\geq 0
and
d=1,\ldots N
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{\alpha_{d}+k-1}{k}^{s_{d}}\binom{\beta_{d}+j-k-1}{j-k}^{s_{d}}\leq\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{\alpha_{d}+k-1}{k}\binom{\beta_{d}+j-k-1}{j-k}\right)^{s_{d}}.

Using this fact and Lemma
4.6
, we conclude that

f(x)g(x)\ll\dfrac{\left\|f\right\|_{r,s,\alpha}\left\|g\right\|_{r,s,\beta}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha+\beta)}}\prod_{d=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\alpha_{d}+\beta_{d}+j-1}{j}^{s_{d}}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}};

hence,
\left\|fg\right\|_{\alpha+\beta,r,s}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}\left\|g\right\|_{\beta,r,s}
thanks to Definition
4.2
.

Now, let us suppose that
\alpha=0
and
\beta\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
. Then

f(x)g(x)\ll\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},...,j_{N}\geq 0}\left|f_{j_{1},...,j_{N}}\right|r^{j_{1}+...+j_{N}}\prod_{d=1}^{N}\frac{x^{i_{d}}}{r^{i_{d}}}\right)\times\\
\left(\dfrac{\left\|g\right\|_{r,s,\beta}}{r^{\lambda(\beta)}}\prod_{d=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\beta_{d}+j-1}{j}^{s_{d}}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}}\right)

and the conclusion follows from Lemma
4.6
. Of course, the same holds when
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
and
\beta=0
.

For
\alpha=\beta=0
, the inequality is obviously true.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Note that the assumption “
s_{d}\geq 1
for all
d=1,\ldots,N
” is necessary for the proposition above to hold true. Otherwise, it is not possible to use inequalities of the form
a^{s}+b^{s}\leq(a+b)^{s}
.

Considering in particular the case
g(x)=1
, we can easily derive from Proposition
4.7
the following.

Corollary 4.9
.

For all
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\beta\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha+\beta,r,s}\leq r^{\lambda(\beta)}\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}
.

The following two results show the action of the moment derivatives on the modified Nagumo norms.

Proposition 4.10

(
[
24
]
, Lemma 4)
.

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
.

Let
s\in(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})^{N}
,
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
and
0<r<\min(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N})
.

Let
e_{d}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{N}
be the multi-index with a
1
in the
d
-th coordinate and zeros everywhere else.

Let
m_{d}
be a regular moment function of order
s_{d}
. Then, there exists a positive constant
A>0
such that

\left\|\partial_{m_{d};x_{d}}f\right\|_{\alpha+e_{d},r,s}\leq C\alpha_{d}^{s_{d}}\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}

Proof.

Since
m_{d}
is a regular moment function, there exist positve constants
a,A>0
such that

a(j+1)^{s}\leq\frac{m_{d}(j+1)}{m_{d}(j)}\leq A(j+1)^{s}\quad\textrm{for every}\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, let us notice that

\displaystyle\partial_{m_{d};x_{d}}f(x)

\displaystyle\ll\dfrac{\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j}{j+1}^{s_{d}}\frac{m_{d}(j+1)}{m_{d}(j)}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j+1}}\displaystyle\prod_{i\neq d}\dbinom{\alpha_{i}+j-1}{j}^{s_{i}}\frac{x_{i}^{j}}{r^{j}}}

\displaystyle\ll\dfrac{A\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)+1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j}{j+1}^{s_{d}}(j+1)^{s_{d}}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}}\displaystyle\prod_{i\neq d}\dbinom{\alpha_{i}+j-1}{j}^{s_{i}}\frac{x_{i}^{j}}{r^{j}}}.

Since

\displaystyle\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j}{j+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{d}+j+1)}{\Gamma(2+j)\Gamma(\alpha_{d})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{d}\Gamma(\alpha_{d}+j+1)}{(j+1)\Gamma(1+j)\Gamma(\alpha_{d}+1)}=\frac{\alpha_{d}}{j+1}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+1+j-1}{j},

we receive

\partial_{m_{d};x_{d}}f(x)\ll\dfrac{A\alpha_{d}^{s_{d}}\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)+1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+1+j-1}{j}^{s_{d}}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}}\displaystyle\prod_{i\neq d}\dbinom{\alpha_{i}+j-1}{j}^{s_{i}}\frac{x_{i}^{j}}{r^{j}}}.

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Assume that
m_{1},...,m_{N}
are all regular moment functions. Then, for all
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
and all
q\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\left\|\partial_{m;x}^{q}f\right\|_{\alpha+q,r,s}\leq C^{\lambda(q)}\left(\displaystyle\prod_{d=1}^{N}q_{d}!^{s_{d}}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+q_{d}-1}{q_{d}}^{s_{d}}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}.

Note that if
q_{d}=0
, then the corresponding term in the product is
1
(see Notation
4.1
). In particular, this inequality remains valid when
q=0
.

The last two properties will enable us to link the modified Nagumo norms with the concept of Gevrey order of a formal power series.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let

\widetilde{u}(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j,\ast}(x)t^{j}\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}

a
\sigma
-Gevrey formal power series for a certain

\sigma\geq 0
. Let
0<r<\min\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{N}\}
as in Definiton
3.1
. Then, for all
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}\cup\{0\}
and all
s\in(\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})^{N}
, there exist two positive constants
A,B>0
such that the following inequality holds for all
j\geq 0
:

\left\|u_{j,\ast}\right\|_{j\alpha,r,s}\leq AB^{j}\Gamma(1+\sigma j).

Proof.

The proof is identical to the one presented in
[
24
]
and follows directly from Definition
3.1
and the Cauchy formula.
∎

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
0<\rho<r<\min(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{N})
. Then, there exists a positive constant
A>0
such that, for all
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
and all
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
, the following inequality holds for all
x\in D_{\rho,...,\rho}
:

\left|f(x)\right|\leq A^{\lambda(\alpha)}\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}.

Proof.

For
\alpha=0
, the inequality is obviously true. To show the same for any
\alpha\in[1,+\infty[^{N}
, let us first notice that for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
p\in[1,+\infty[
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
inequality

\dbinom{j+p-1}{j}a^{j}b^{p-1}\leq(a+b)^{j+p-1}

holds. Then, if we take
a+b=1
with
a^{-1}=1+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
, we receive

\dbinom{j+p-1}{j}(1+\varepsilon)^{-j}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\leq 1,

and then

(7)

\dbinom{j+p-1}{j}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{j}\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}.

Let us then fix
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small that
\rho(1+\varepsilon)^{\lambda(s)-N}<r
holds true. We can use inequality (
7
) to find a majorant series for
f(x)
. More precisely, we get

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)}}\displaystyle\prod_{d=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j-1}{j}^{s_{d}}\frac{x_{d}^{j}}{r^{j}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)}}\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\sum_{d=1}^{N}\alpha_{d}s_{d}}\displaystyle\prod_{d=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geq 0}\dbinom{\alpha_{d}+j-1}{j}\left(\frac{x_{d}(1+\varepsilon)^{s_{d}-1}}{r}\right)^{j}

Considering our previous restriction on

, we can notice that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in D_{\rho,...,\rho}}\left|f(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)}}\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\sum_{d=1}^{N}\alpha_{d}s_{d}}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{\rho(1+\varepsilon)^{\lambda(s)-N}}{r}\right)^{\lambda(\alpha)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,r,s}}{r^{\lambda(\alpha)}}\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\hat{s}\lambda(\alpha)}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{\rho(1+\varepsilon)^{\lambda(s)-N}}{r}\right)^{\lambda(\alpha)}}

for
\hat{s}=\max(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We are now able to turn to our initial problem.

5.
Main results

Let us consider
N+1
regular moment functions
m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{N}
of respective orders
s_{0}>0
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}\geq 1
. In this section, we focus on the inhomogeneous nonlinear moment partial differential equations of the form

(8)

\begin{cases}\partial_{m_{0};t}^{\kappa}u-P(t,x,(\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i}\partial_{m;x}^{q}u)_{(i,q)\in\Lambda})=\widetilde{f}(t,x)\\
\partial_{m_{0};t}^{j}u(t,x)_{|t=0}=\varphi_{j}(x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})\textrm{ for }0\leq j<\kappa,\end{cases}

where the following conditions are met:

•

\kappa\geq 1
is a positive integer;

•

\Lambda
is a non-empty finite subset of
\{0,...,\kappa-1\}\times\mathbb{N}^{N}
;

•

\partial_{m;x}^{q}
stands for the moment derivation
\partial_{m_{1};x_{1}}^{q_{1}}...\partial_{m_{N};x_{N}}^{q_{N}}
while
q=(q_{1},...,q_{N})
;

•

P
is a polynomial with analytic coefficients on the polydisc
D_{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}}
;

•

\widetilde{f}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]
.

More precisely, we shall always assume that the polynomial
P
reads in the form

(9)

P(t,x,(\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i}\partial_{m;x}^{q}u)_{(i,q)\in\Lambda})=\\
\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{I}}\displaystyle\sum_{(\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r})\in\Lambda_{n}}t^{v_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r}}}a_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r}}(t,x)\left(\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{1}}\partial_{m;x}^{q_{1}}u\right)^{r_{1}}...\left(\partial_{m_{0};t}^{i_{n}}\partial_{m;x}^{q_{n}}u\right)^{r_{n}},

where:

•

\mathcal{I}
is a non-empty finite subset of
\mathbb{N}^{*}
;

•

for any
n\in\mathcal{I}
, the set
\Lambda_{n}
is a non-empty finite subset of
n
-tuples

(\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r})=((i_{1},q_{1},r_{1}),...,(i_{n},q_{n},r_{n}))

composed of elements of
\{0,...,\kappa-1\}\times\mathbb{N}^{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
, whose the pairs
(i_{k},q_{k})
are all two by two distincts;

•

v_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r}}
is a nonnegative integer for every
(\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r})\in\Lambda_{n}
;

•

a_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r}}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})
and
a_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r}}(0,x)\not\equiv 0
for every
(\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r})\in\Lambda_{n}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Eq. (
8
) is formally well-posed.

Proof.

Let us take the coefficients
a_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r}}(t,x)
in the form

a_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r}}(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}a_{\underline{i},\underline{q},\underline{r};j,*}(x)\dfrac{t^{j}}{m_{0}(j)}

and the inhomogeneity
\widetilde{f}(t,x)
in the form

\widetilde{f}(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{j,\ast}(x)\dfrac{t^{j}}{m_{0}(j)}.

The coefficients
u_{j,\ast}(x)
of the formal solution
\widetilde{u}(t,x)
of Eq. (
8

(10)

The proof of Theorem

). As for the second point, it stems both from the first one and from Proposition
6.6
that gives an explicit example for which

is

\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma_{c}
while
\widetilde{f}(t,x)

(see Section

6.1.
Proof of the first point of Theorem
5.7

According to Propositiion
3.3
and Corollary
3.5
, it is clear that

\widetilde{u}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}\Rightarrow\widetilde{f}(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}(D_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N}})[[t]]_{\sigma}.

Reciprocally, let us fix
\sigma\geq\sigma_{c}
, and let us assume that

\widetilde{f}(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}f_{j,\ast}(x)\dfrac{t^{j}}{m_{0}(j)}

is
\sigma
-Gevrey. By assumption (see Definition
3.1
), there exist a radius
0<r<\min(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{N})
and two positive constants
C,K>0
such that
\left|f_{j,\ast}(x)\right|\leq CK^{j}m_{0}(j)\Gamma(1+\sigma j)
for all
x\in D_{r,...,r}
and all
j\geq 0
.

We must prove that the coefficients
u_{j,\ast}(x)
of the formal solution
\widetilde{u}(t,x)
satisfy similar inequalities. The approach we present below is analogous to the ones already developed in
[
2
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
in the framework of linear partial and integro-differential equations, in
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
in the case of nonlinear partial differential equations, and in
[
24
]
for some linear moment partial differential equations. It is based on the modified Nagumo norms introduced in Section
4
and on a technique of majorant series.

6.1.1.
First step: some preliminary inequalities

From relations (
10
) and (
11

\dfrac{u_{j+\kappa,\ast}(x)}{m_{0}(j+\kappa)\Gamma(1+\sigma(j+\kappa))}=\dfrac{f_{j,\ast}(x)}{m_{0}(j+\kappa)\Gamma(1+\sigma(j+\kappa))}\\

6.3.
Remark on the Cauchy-Kovalevskaya Theorem and directions for further research

When the moment functions

However, in the Kovalevskaya case, it is important to note here that our result is weaker than the Cauchy-Kovalevskaya Theorem. Let us consider for instance the partial differential equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{3}u+\partial_{t}\partial_{x}u+(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{3}=0\\

, whereas our Theorem
5.7
tells us that
\widetilde{u}(t,x)
is
1
-Gevrey. This is not contradictory, of course, but our result is clearly weaker.

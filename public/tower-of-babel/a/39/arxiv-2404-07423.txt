Fermi sea and sky in the Bogoliubov–de Gennes equation
Xian-Peng Zhang, Yugui Yao

Abstract
We develop a comprehensive logical framework for effectively handling the overcomplete basis set in the Bogoliubov–de Gennes equation that contains two orthonormal basis sets conjugate with each other, such as particle and hole orthonormal basis sets. We highlight the significant implications of our logical framework from theoretical concepts and experimental predictions. Firstly, we rigorously derive all many-body eigenfunctions of arbitrary nonuniform superconductors and uncover that the many-body eigenstates are full of superconducting spin clouds—the electron configuration within the Cooper-like pair of an arbitrary nonuniform superconductor. Secondly, we demonstrate a conjugate loop formed by the effective vacuum states of two orthonormal basis sets conjugate with each other, such as the Fermi sea and sky—the effective vacuum states of positive and negative orthonormal basis sets, respectively. We emphasize the conceptual importance of the Fermi sky, which serves as the starting point for the filling of all negative quasiparticles within the Fermi sea. Next, we emphasize the significance of our logical framework from the experimental prediction such as superconducting tunneling spectroscopy. The existing theory of the superconducting tunneling spectroscopy, based on an excitation orthonormal basis set, necessitates the breakup and formation of the Cooper pairs that nerve was directly observed in experiments. Our conjugate loop provides insights into how to populate negative quasiparticles and achieves the one-body quasiparticle representation of the many-body superconducting state. This discovery provides a solid foundation for treating a many-body superconducting state as a one-body problem and thus proves valuable for addressing superconducting tunneling spectroscopy as simply as the standard nonsuperconducting spectroscopy theory. Thirdly, we present a gate-, field-, and phase-tunable tunnel spectroscopy asymmetry arising from the imbalanced particle-hole distribution of the subgap quasiparticles in a quantum-dot Josephson junction. These findings underscore the power of our logical framework and its implications for advancing our understanding and utilization of solid-state devices based on superconductivity.

Conjugate loop

The conceptual significance of the conjugate loop is emphasized. Note that there exists a conceptual difficulty of apparent disregard of half quasiparticles in superconducting scattering and tunneling theory

IV.3
Tunnel spectroscopy asymmetry

The existing theory of the tunneling spectroscopy of uniform superconductors, based on an excitation OBS, requires the breakup and formation of the Cooper pairs that nerve was directly demonstrated in experiments. The tunneling Hamiltonian between superconductor and normal metal can be written as the sum of the processes

V
Conclusion and outlook

In summary, we have developed a general framework for dealing with the overcomplete basis set in the BdG formalism, that incorporates both positive and negative energy quasiparticle states and maintains the symmetry between spin-up and -down quasiparticles in scattering and tunneling theory. Here, we highlight the significant implications of our logical framework from both the theoretical concepts and the experimental predictions. Firstly, we rigorously derive alla many-body eigenfunction of arbitrary nonuniform superconductors and uncover that superconducting eigenstates are full of superconducting spin clouds—the electron configuration within the Cooper-like pair of a nonuniform superconductor. We show how Bogoliubov excitations reconfigure these superconducting spin clouds. Secondly, we demonstrate a conjugate loop formed by the effective vacuum states of two orthonormal basis sets conjugate with each other, such as the Fermi sea and sky – the effective vacuum states of positive and negative orthonormal basis sets, respectively. We emphasize the conceptual importance of the Fermi sky, which serves as the starting point for the filling of all negative quasiparticles within the Fermi sea. Importantly, our conjugate loop provides insights into how to populate negative quasiparticles and achieves the one-body quasiparticle representation of the many-body superconducting state. This discovery provides a solid foundation for treating a many-body superconducting state as a one-body problem and thus proves valuable for addressing superconducting tunneling spectroscopy as simply as the standard nonsuperconducting spectroscopy theory. Thirdly, we demonstrate a gate-, field-, and phase-tunable tunnel spectroscopy asymmetry due to the particle-hole distribution imbalance of the subgap quasiparticles in the quantum-dot Josephson junction.

Notably, we also derive the various transport properties of arbitrary superposition states from the wave-function perspective and demonstrate a novel supercurrent arising from the interference of distinct eigenstates that distinguishes with the supercurrent in the superconducting quantum interference device and exhibits oscillatory behaviour akin to the alternating current Josephson effect
[
50
]
. Notably, the novel probability amplitude and oscillation frequency dependence of our interference supercurrrent suggests a groundbreaking quantum nondestructive measurement technique—the quantum noncollapsing measurement of the arbitrary superposition states of Andreev qubits
[
51
]
. By following our logical framework, we have achieved a singlet-doublet quantum phase transition arising from the intricate competition between superconducting and spin-split proximity effects of superconducting leads rather than the Coulomb interaction of quantum dot
[
33
]
. Lastly, we have demonstrated how the singlet-doublet quantum phase transition induces additional asymmetry between the forward and reverse critical supercurrents, controlling the superconducting diode effect
[
32
]
.
These significant findings underscore the exceptional power and implications of our logical framework, providing invaluable insights into the understanding and utilization of solid-state devices based on superconductivity. Moreover, our conjugate loop might lighten infinity issues of the Dirac sea in high-energy physics and build a connection between the worlds constructed by the particles and antiparticles
[
52
]
.

By numerically diagonalizing the BdG matrix (
B
), we can construct the Bogoliubov quasiparticle operators
\gamma_{l\sigma\eta}(q,\phi_{L},\phi_{R})\rightarrow\gamma_{l\sigma\eta}
, which depend on the Cooper pair momentum
q
as well as the phase differences
\phi_{L}
and
\phi_{R}
. These operators can be represented as unit vectors in the overcomplete basis set (
49
) and are given by the expression

\gamma_{l\sigma\eta}=\sum^{8N+4}_{m=1}u_{l,m}\Psi_{m},

(62)

i.e.,

\gamma_{l\sigma\eta}=\sum^{N}_{n=1}\sum_{s=\uparrow,\downarrow}\left[(u_{\eta})^{\sigma s}_{ln}c_{ns}+(v_{\eta})^{\sigma s}_{ln}(-sc^{\dagger}_{n-s})\right].

(63)

Here,
\Psi_{m}
denotes the
m
-th component of
\Psi
in Eq. (
49
), and
[u_{l,1},u_{l,2},\cdots,u_{l,8N+4}]
represents the
l
-th eigenvector that diagonalizes the BdG Hamiltonian (
B
). The coefficients
u_{l,m}
satisfy the normalization condition

\displaystyle\sum^{8N+4}_{m=1}|u_{l,m}|^{2}=1.

(64)

By recasting the total Hamiltonian (
50
) in the form of Eq. (
1
) in the main text, we have:

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{2N+1}_{l=1}\sum_{\sigma=\Uparrow/\Downarrow}\sum_{\eta=\pm}E_{l\sigma\eta}\gamma_{l\sigma\eta}^{\dagger}\gamma_{l\sigma\eta}+\mathcal{E}.

(65)

In the above equation, the Bogoliubov quasiparticle energies
E_{l\sigma\eta}
depend solely on the phase difference
\phi=\phi_{R}-\phi_{L}
. Additionally, these eigenenergies satisfy the relationship
E_{l\sigma\eta}=-E_{l-\sigma-\eta}
due to particle-hole symmetry.

The quasiparticle operators
(\gamma_{l\sigma\eta})
for all
l
,
\sigma
, and
\eta
form an overcomplete basis set that consists of two mutually conjugate OBSs. The quasiparticle operators described by Eq. (
63
) satisfy the conjugate relation

\gamma_{l\sigma\eta}^{\dagger}=\gamma_{l-\sigma-\eta}.

(66)

As a consequence of this conjugate relation, the electron (
u_{\eta}
) and hole (
v_{\eta}
) distributions satisfy the following relation

(u_{\eta}^{*})^{\sigma s}_{ln}=s(v_{-\eta})^{-\sigma-s}_{ln}.

(67)

The conjugate relation (
66
) offers an intuitive and comprehensive illustration of the artificial redundancy in the BdG formalism from a quasiparticle operator point of view. It is important to note that the ubiquitous conjugation relation is different from the demanding requirement of the Majorana fermion, i.e.,
\gamma_{ls\eta}^{\dagger}=\gamma_{ls\eta}
.
By inverting Eq. (
63
), we can express the electron operator
c_{ns}
in terms of the
\gamma_{ls\eta}
operators as follows

\displaystyle c_{ns}=\sum_{l\sigma}(u^{*}_{+})^{\sigma s}_{ln}\gamma_{l\sigma+}+s(v_{+})^{-\sigma-s}_{ln}\gamma_{l\sigma-}.

(68)

Appendix C
Logic

We present our logic for the usage of the overcomplete basis set including two OBSs conjugate with each other

•

STEP I): divide the overcomplete basis set [
(\gamma_{l\sigma\eta})
for all
l
,
\sigma
, and
\eta
] into two OBSs conjugate with each other;

•

STEP II): derive the EVSs of these two OBSs;

•

STEP III): ground state is a filled sea of all negative basis states in the chosen OBS from the corresponding EVS.

Then we attain the following three properties

•

PROPERTY I): the EVSs of any two OBSs conjugate with each other form a conjugate loop;

•

PROPERTY II): there are
2^{N}
possible divisions of the two OBSs conjugate with each other. When a pair of basis states is exchanged between the two OBSs, the fermionic parity of the EVSs switches.

•

PROPERTY III): The even-parity eigenstates are full of Bogoliubov-like singlets – a superposition of vacuum state and Cooper-like pair, while the odd-parity eigenstates contain not only plenty of Bogoliubov-like singlets but also a pseudospin cloud.

To gain an intuitive understanding of this logic, we provide examples starting with a simple quantum dot case (Sec.
C.1
), followed by normal metal (Sec.
C.2
) and superconductor (Sec.
C.3
) cases.

C.1
Quantum dot case

It is illustrative to start with the quantum dot without the superconducting leads. The BdG Hamiltonian can be expressed in terms of the overcomplete basis set
(\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow+},\gamma_{\uparrow-},\gamma_{\downarrow-})
. The Hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle H_{D}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\eta}E_{s\eta}\gamma^{\dagger}_{s\eta}\gamma_{s\eta}+\mathcal{E}^{D},

(69)

with
\mathcal{E}^{D}=\epsilon_{D}
. Without loss of generality, let’s consider the case where
\epsilon_{D}
and
h_{D}
are both positive. In this case, we can identify
\gamma_{s+}
as the electron operator
d_{s}
(
\gamma_{s+}=d_{s}
) and
\gamma_{s-}
as the hole operator
d_{s}^{\dagger}
(
\gamma_{s-}=d_{-s}^{\dagger}
). The single-particle energy spectrum
E_{s\eta}
is given by
E_{s\eta}=\eta|\epsilon_{D}|+sh_{D}
, and it satisfies the relation
E_{-s-\eta}=-E_{s\eta}
. The field operators
\gamma_{s\eta}^{\dagger}
and
\gamma_{-s-\eta}
are conjugates of each other according to

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{s\eta}=\gamma_{-s-\eta}.

(70)

Each spin-
s
electron has both particle (
d_{s}=\gamma_{s+}
) and hole (
d_{s}^{\dagger}=\gamma_{-s-}
) representations. Therefore, there are four OBSs that can be constructed: two spin OBSs
(\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow+})
and
(\gamma_{\uparrow-},\gamma_{\downarrow-})
, as well as two Nambu OBSs
(\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\uparrow-})
and
(\gamma_{\downarrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow-})
. However, it is important to note that
(\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow-})
and
(\gamma_{\uparrow-},\gamma_{\downarrow+})
are not valid OBSs because the operators within each pair do not satisfy the anticommutation relation (
\{\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow-}\}\neq 0
and
\{\gamma_{\uparrow-},\gamma_{\downarrow+}\}\neq 0
). It is important to emphasize that the choice of which OBS to use does not affect the physical properties of the quantum dot. The quantum dot itself is unaware of the specific OBS chosen, and all four OBSs will yield the same ground and excitation states. This highlights the arbitrariness in the choice of OBS and the consistency of physical results regardless of the chosen representation.

C.1.1
Spin OBSs

STEP I): Let’s begin by considering two spin OBSs
(\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow+})
and
(\gamma_{\uparrow-},\gamma_{\downarrow-})

\displaystyle H_{D}=\sum_{s}E_{s+}\gamma^{\dagger}_{s+}\gamma_{s+}+\mathcal{E}^{D}_{+}=\sum_{s}E_{s-}\gamma^{\dagger}_{s-}\gamma_{s-}+\mathcal{E}^{D}_{-}.

In this case, we have
\mathcal{E}^{D}_{+}=0

\mathcal{E}^{D}_{-}=2\epsilon_{D}

(\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow+})
and
(\gamma_{\uparrow-},\gamma_{\downarrow-})

STEP II): Next, we determine the EVSs of the two spin OBSs
(\gamma_{\uparrow+},\gamma_{\downarrow+})
and

\displaystyle\gamma_{s+}|V\rangle_{+}=0\text{ for all }s=\uparrow/\downarrow,

\displaystyle\gamma_{s-}|V\rangle_{-}=0\text{ for all }s=\uparrow/\downarrow.

(73)

By solving these equations, we can find the corresponding EVSs

\displaystyle|V\rangle_{+}=|0\rangle,

(74)

\displaystyle|V\rangle_{-}=d^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}d^{\dagger}_{\downarrow}|0\rangle.

(75)

C.1.2

STEP I): By exchanging a pair of basis states in the spin OBSs, we arrive at the Nambu OBSs

\displaystyle H_{D}=\sum_{\eta}E_{\uparrow\eta}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\uparrow\eta}\gamma_{\uparrow\eta}+\mathcal{E}^{D}_{\uparrow}=\sum_{\eta}E_{\downarrow\eta}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\downarrow\eta}\gamma_{\downarrow\eta}+\mathcal{E}^{D}_{\downarrow}.

\displaystyle\gamma_{\downarrow\eta}|V\rangle_{\downarrow}=0\text{ for all }\eta=+/-.

\displaystyle|V\rangle_{\downarrow}=d^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}|0\rangle.

\mathcal{E}^{D}_{\uparrow}

and

C.2
Normal metal case

In this subsection, we use our logic in normal metal. The normal metal behaves as many quantum dots, which is described by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{L}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}s}(\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+sh_{L})c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}c_{\boldsymbol{k}s}.

(83)

Next, we work in Nambu space, which doubles the Hilbert space, and we obtain the BdG Hamiltonian in overcomplete basis set

\displaystyle H_{L}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sum_{s=\uparrow/\downarrow}\sum_{\eta=\pm}E_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}+\mathcal{E},

(84)

with
\mathcal{E}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}
. Additional index
\eta=+/-
labels the high/low energy levels of each spin species. The quasiparticle energy spectrum is given by
E_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}=sh_{L}+\eta|\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}|
and the corresponding operators read

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s+}=u_{\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}+sv_{\boldsymbol{k}}c_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s},

(85)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s-}=-sv^{*}_{\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}+u_{\boldsymbol{k}}c_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s},

(86)

with

\displaystyle u_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\left\{\begin{matrix}1,&\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}>0;\\
0,&\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}\leq 0.\end{matrix}\right.,

(87)

\displaystyle v_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\left\{\begin{matrix}0,&\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}>0;\\
1,&\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}\leq 0.\end{matrix}\right..

(88)

The quasiparticle operators satisfy a conjugate relation

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s-\eta}.

(89)

Each spinful quasiparticle always has its conjugate alternative with opposite spin and energy. Thus, we can have
4^{N}
combinations of complete and orthogonal quaiparticle bases where
N
describes the size of the normal metal (or number of the kinetic momentum). In principle, we can choose any OBS, for instance spin-momentum OBSs
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow\eta},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow\eta})
for all
\boldsymbol{k}
as well as Nambu-momentum OBSs
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s-})
for all
\boldsymbol{k}
.
We will see details in the following subsections.

C.2.1
Spin-momentum OBSs

STEP I): Let us first study the spin-momentum OBSs
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow\eta},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow\eta})
for all
\boldsymbol{k}
, which satisfy the anticommutation relation. The corresponding Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H_{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}s}E_{\boldsymbol{k}s+}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s+}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s+}+\mathcal{E}^{L}_{+}

C.3
Superconductor case

Let us generalize our new logic to the superconductor case. The BdG Hamiltonian, expressed in terms of an overcomplete basis set
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow-},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow-})
for all
\boldsymbol{k}
, is given by

\displaystyle H_{L}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sum_{s=\uparrow/\downarrow}\sum_{\eta=\pm}E_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}+\mathcal{E},

(101)

with
\mathcal{E}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}
. Electrons (
c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}
) interact with holes (
-sc_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s}
) to generate Bogoliubov quasiparticles with energy levels
E_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}=sh_{L}+\eta\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\epsilon^{2}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}
, as well as field operators

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s+}=u_{\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}+sv_{\boldsymbol{k}}c_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s},

(102)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s-}=-sv^{*}_{\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s}+u_{\boldsymbol{k}}c_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s},

(103)

where the Bogoliubov coefficients are given by
u_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}/\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\epsilon^{2}_{\boldsymbol{k}}})^{1/2}
and
v_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1-\epsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}/\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\epsilon^{2}_{\boldsymbol{k}}})^{1/2}
. We here also show the Bogoliubov transformation

\displaystyle c_{\boldsymbol{k}s}=u_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s+}-sv^{*}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}s-},

(104)

\displaystyle c_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s}=u_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s-}+sv^{*}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s+}.

(105)

It is important to note that the Bogoliubov quasiparticle operators are not independent and satisfy a conjugate relation

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}s\eta}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}-s-\eta}.

(106)

Each spinful Bogoliubov quasiparticle always has its conjugate alternative with opposite spin and energy. Therefore, we can have
4^{N}
combinations of complete and orthogonal quasiparticle bases, where
N
describes the size of the normal metal or the number of kinetic momenta. In principle, we can choose any OBSs, such as spin-momentum OBSs
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow+})
and
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow-},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow-})
, or Nambu-momentum OBSs
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow-})
and
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow-})
for
\boldsymbol{k}
in the entire Brillouin zone.
Furthermore, by choosing the OBS
(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\uparrow+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow-},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow+},\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\downarrow-})
for
\boldsymbol{k}
in the half Brillouin zone, the semiconductor analogy of the superconductor produces dramatic features in various spectroscopies of the superconducting condensate
[
11
]
.

where
t_{d}
is the tunnel coupling strength between dot and tip. We begin with the Liouville-von Neumann equation in the interaction representation of
H_{0}=H_{e}+H
,

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\check{\varrho}(t)=\dfrac{i}{\hbar}[\check{\varrho}(t),\check{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}(t)],

(187)

where

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{O}}(t)=e^{+i\check{H}_{0}t/\hbar}O(t)e^{-i\check{H}_{0}t/\hbar}.

(188)

This equation can be solved by time-convolutionless (TCL) projection operator method, and we reach TCL equation, in
Born-Markov approximation
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{\varrho}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{\varrho}(t),\check{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathrm{ep}}(t)]-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\hbar^{2}}[[\hat{\varrho}(t),\check{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathrm{ep}}(\tau)],\check{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathrm{ep}}(t)].

(189)

The transition rate between
|E_{ls\eta}\rangle
and
|E_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\rangle
is given by
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\boldsymbol{p}s,ls\eta}=t_{d}D_{ls\eta}
, where
D_{ls+}=(u^{*}_{+})^{ss}_{l1}
and
D_{ls-}=s(v_{+})^{-s-s}_{l1}
. Then, the electron tunneling Hamiltonian (
186
) becomes

\displaystyle V_{ep}=\sum_{l\boldsymbol{p}s\eta}\left(\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\boldsymbol{p}s,ls\eta}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\gamma_{ls\eta}+\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{ls\eta,\boldsymbol{p}s}\gamma^{\dagger}_{ls\eta}c_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\right).

(190)

The second term, i.e., collision term, reads

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{J}}(\hat{\varrho}|t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}_{i}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\tau\left\{\left[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}(t),\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}(\tau)\hat{\varrho}(t)\right]\right.

(191)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\left[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}(t),\hat{\varrho}(t)\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}(\tau)\right]\right\}.

Converting into Schrodinger representation, we reach

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{\rho}(t)-\hat{B}_{0}(t)\hat{\rho}(t)-\hat{B}_{1}(t)\hat{\rho}(t)=\hat{J}(\hat{\rho}|t),

(192)

with

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{0}(t)\hat{\rho}(t)=\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\hat{\rho}(t),\hat{H}_{0}\right],

(193)

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{1}(t)\hat{\rho}(t)=\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{\rho}(t),\check{V}_{\mathrm{ep}}],

(194)

\displaystyle\hat{J}(\hat{\rho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{q}_{i}}\int_{0}^{t-t_{0}}d\tau\left\{\left[\hat{V}_{eq},\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{eq}(-\tau)\right]\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\left[\hat{V}_{eq},\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{eq}(-\tau)\hat{\rho}(t)\right]\right\}.

(195)

The collision term
\hat{J}(\hat{\rho})
describes the scattering induced by dot-tip tunneling coupling.

We here define the tunneling current between tip and quantum-dot Josephson junction as the rate of change of the electron number in the tip

\displaystyle I_{T}=e\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\left\langle\hat{c}^{+}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\right\rangle(t),

(196)

with

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{c}^{+}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\right\rangle(t)=\text{Tr}\{\hat{c}^{+}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\hat{\rho}(t)\}.

(197)

Then, we define one-body density matrix from the many-body density matrix as follows

\varrho_{\kappa_{2}\kappa_{1}}(t)=\langle\kappa_{2}|\hat{\varrho}(t)|\kappa_{1}\rangle\equiv\mathrm{Tr}\{\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{1}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{2}}\hat{\rho}(t)\}.

(198)

For tip electrons, we have
\kappa_{i}=\alpha_{i}=(\boldsymbol{p}_{i},s_{i})
, corresponding to
\hat{f}_{\kappa_{i}}=f_{\alpha_{i}}=c_{\boldsymbol{p}_{i},s_{i}}
. For the quantum-dot Josephson junction, we have
\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}=(l_{i},s_{i},\eta_{i})
, corresponding to
\hat{f}_{\kappa_{i}}=f_{\lambda_{i}}=\gamma_{l_{i},s_{i},\eta_{i}}
. As shown in Eq. (
196
), we are only interested in the time evolution of the diagonal and tip components of the one-body density matrix, i.e.,
\varrho_{\alpha\alpha}(t)=\varrho_{\alpha}(t)
, given by

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\varrho_{\alpha}(t)-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\varrho(t),\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}]_{\alpha\alpha}=\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho}).

(199)

The collision integral in the right hand side of Eq. (
199
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t-t_{0}}d\tau\text{Tr}\left\{\left[\hat{V}_{eq},\hat{f}^{+}_{\alpha}\hat{f}_{\alpha}\right]\left[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{eq}(-\tau),\hat{\rho}(t)\right]\right\}.

(200)

It is required to calculate commutator

\displaystyle[V_{eq},\hat{f}^{+}_{\alpha}\hat{f}_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{1}}\left[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{1}\alpha}\hat{f}^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}\hat{f}_{\alpha}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{1}}\hat{f}^{+}_{\alpha}\hat{f}_{\lambda_{1}}\right].

(201)

Substituting Eq. (
201
) into (
200
), we reach

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{1}}\int_{0}^{t-t_{0}}d\tau\text{Tr}\left\{\left[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{1}\alpha}\hat{f}^{+}_{\lambda_{1}}\hat{f}_{\alpha}\right.\right.

(202)

\displaystyle-\left.\left.\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{1}}\hat{f}^{+}_{\alpha}\hat{f}_{\lambda_{1}}\right]\left[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{eq}(-\tau),\hat{\rho}(t)\right]\right\}.

By substitution of

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{eq}(-\tau)=\sum_{\kappa_{5},\kappa_{6}}e^{+i\omega^{\kappa_{6}}_{\kappa_{5}}\tau}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5},\kappa_{6}}f^{\dagger}_{\kappa_{5}}f_{\kappa_{6}}.

(203)

Eq. (
202
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{i}\kappa_{i}}\text{Tr}\left\{\left[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{3}\alpha}\hat{f}^{+}_{\lambda_{3}}\hat{f}_{\alpha}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{3}}\hat{f}^{+}_{\alpha}\hat{f}_{\lambda_{3}}\right]\right.

(204)

\displaystyle\times\left.\left[\delta^{+}(\omega^{\kappa_{6}}_{\kappa_{5}})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{5}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{6}},\hat{\rho}(t)\right]\right\},

with

\displaystyle\delta^{+}(\omega)=\int^{+\infty}_{0}d\tau e^{+i\omega\tau-\eta\tau},

(205)

where
t_{0}\rightarrow-\infty
, and
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}
was introduced to remove the divergence of the infinity integral.
Eq. (
204
) can be rewritten into

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa_{i}\lambda_{i}}\delta^{+}(\omega^{\kappa_{6}}_{\kappa_{5}})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\left\{\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{3}}[R^{\kappa_{5}\alpha}_{\kappa_{6}\lambda_{3}}(t)-R^{\alpha\kappa_{5}}_{\lambda_{3}\kappa_{6}}(t)]\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{3}\alpha}[R^{\lambda_{3}\kappa_{5}}_{\alpha\kappa_{6}}(t)-R^{\kappa_{5}\lambda_{3}}_{\kappa_{6}\alpha}(t)]\right\}.

(206)

The many-electron correlation functions, are defined as

\displaystyle R^{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3}}_{\kappa_{2}\kappa_{4}}(t)=\text{Tr}\left\{\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{1}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{2}}\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{3}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{4}}\hat{\rho}(t)\right\},

(207)

To calculate Eq. (
206
), it is required to calculate many-electron correlation function (
207
). To reach a normal order inside the trace
Tr
, we reach

\displaystyle R^{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3}}_{\kappa_{2}\kappa_{4}}(t)=\delta_{\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}\varrho_{\kappa_{4}\kappa_{1}}-\text{Tr}_{e}\left\{\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{1}}\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{3}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{2}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{4}}\hat{\rho}(t)\right\}.

(208)

The second term in normal order, can be approximated into

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{e}\left\{\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{1}}\hat{f}^{+}_{\kappa_{3}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{2}}\hat{f}_{\kappa_{4}}\check{\rho}_{e}(t)\right\}\simeq\varrho_{\kappa_{4}\kappa_{1}}\varrho_{\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}}-\varrho_{\kappa_{4}\kappa_{3}}\varrho_{\kappa_{2}\kappa_{1}}.

(209)

With the help from Eqs. (
208
) and (
209
), Eq. (
206
) can be divided into two terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}^{e}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})+\mathcal{J}^{a}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

(210)

with

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{e}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{3}\kappa_{i}}\delta^{+}(\omega^{\kappa_{6}}_{\kappa_{5}})[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{3}}\varrho_{\lambda_{3}\kappa_{5}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\bar{\varrho}_{\kappa_{6}\alpha}-\varrho_{\alpha\kappa_{5}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\bar{\varrho}_{\kappa_{6}\lambda_{3}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{3}\alpha}]

(211)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha_{i}}\delta^{+}(\omega^{\kappa_{6}}_{\kappa_{5}})[\bar{\varrho}_{\alpha\kappa_{5}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\varrho_{\kappa_{6}\lambda_{3}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{3}\alpha}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{3}}\bar{\varrho}_{\lambda_{3}\kappa_{5}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\varrho_{\kappa_{6}\alpha}],

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{a}_{\alpha}(\hat{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{3}\kappa_{i}}\delta^{+}(\omega^{\kappa_{6}}_{\kappa_{5}})[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{3}}\varrho_{\lambda_{3}\alpha}\varrho_{\kappa_{6}\kappa_{5}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}-\varrho_{\alpha\lambda_{3}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{3}\alpha}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\varrho_{\kappa_{6}\kappa_{5}}]

(212)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha_{i}}\delta^{+}(\omega^{\kappa_{6}}_{\kappa_{5}})[\varrho_{\alpha\lambda_{3}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda_{3}\alpha}\varrho_{\kappa_{6}\kappa_{5}}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha\lambda_{3}}\varrho_{\lambda_{3}\alpha}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\kappa_{5}\kappa_{6}}\varrho_{\kappa_{6}\kappa_{5}}],

with

\displaystyle\bar{\varrho}_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}=\delta_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}-\varrho_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}.

(213)

By means of Eq. (
199
), the tunneling current is given by

\displaystyle I_{T}=e\sum_{\alpha}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\check{\varrho}),

(214)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\check{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\left\{[\delta^{+}(\omega^{\alpha}_{\lambda})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha,\lambda}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda,\alpha}\varrho_{\lambda}\bar{\varrho}_{\alpha}-\delta^{+}(\omega^{\lambda}_{\alpha})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha,\lambda}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda,\alpha}\varrho_{\alpha}\bar{\varrho}_{\lambda}]\right.

\displaystyle+\left.[\delta^{+}(\omega^{\lambda}_{\alpha})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha,\lambda}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda,\alpha}\varrho_{\lambda}\bar{\varrho}_{\alpha}-\delta^{+}(\omega^{\alpha}_{\lambda})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\alpha,\lambda}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\lambda,\alpha}\varrho_{\alpha}\bar{\varrho}_{\lambda}]\right\},

(215)

where we have used the diagonal approximation

\displaystyle\varrho_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}\simeq\delta_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}\varrho_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{1}}=\delta_{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}\varrho_{\kappa_{1}},

(216)

which also results in

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}[\varrho(t),\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}]_{\alpha\alpha}=0.

(217)

Noting that

\displaystyle\delta^{+}(\omega_{\lambda}^{\alpha})+\delta^{+}(\omega^{\lambda}_{\alpha})=\delta(\omega_{\lambda}^{\alpha}),

(218)

and hence Eq. (
215
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{s}_{\boldsymbol{p}}(\check{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\eta}\left\{[\delta(\omega^{\boldsymbol{p}s}_{ls\eta})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\boldsymbol{p}s,ls\eta}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{ls\eta,\boldsymbol{p}s}\varrho_{ls\eta}\bar{\varrho}_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\right.

(219)

\displaystyle-\left.\delta(\omega^{ls\eta}_{\boldsymbol{p}s})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\boldsymbol{p}s,ls\eta}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{ls\eta,\boldsymbol{p}s}\varrho_{\boldsymbol{p}s}\bar{\varrho}_{ls\eta}]\right\}.

In the presence of the applied voltage,
V
, we assume

\displaystyle\varrho_{\boldsymbol{p}s}=f(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-V),\varrho_{ls\eta}=f(E_{ls\eta}),

(220)

where
f(E)=1/(1+e^{E/k_{B}T})
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution for temperature
\beta=1/k_{B}T
.
Then, Eq. (
219
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{s}_{\boldsymbol{p}}(\check{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{h}\sum_{l\eta}f(E_{ls\eta})[1-f(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-V)]|T^{\boldsymbol{p}s}_{ls\eta}|^{2}\delta(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-E_{ls\eta})

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi}{h}\sum_{l\eta}f(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-V)[1-f(E_{ls\eta})]|T^{\boldsymbol{p}s}_{ls\eta}|^{2}\delta(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-E_{ls\eta}).

(221)

with
|T^{\boldsymbol{p}s}_{ls\eta}|^{2}=\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{\boldsymbol{p}s+,ls\eta}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{ep}^{ls\eta,\boldsymbol{p}s+}
.
Finally, we obtain the tunneling current as follows

\displaystyle I_{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi e}{h}\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}ls\eta}f(E_{ls\eta})[1-f(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-V)]|T^{\boldsymbol{p}s}_{ls\eta}|^{2}\delta(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-E_{ls\eta})

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi e}{h}\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}ls\eta}f(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-V)[1-f(E_{ls\eta})]|T^{\boldsymbol{p}s}_{ls\eta}|^{2}\delta(E_{\boldsymbol{p}}-E_{ls\eta}).

(222)

This is the tunneling current between tip and quantum-dot Josephson junction from a microscopic perspective, given by Eq. (
21
) in the main text.

Abstract
. We provide a general preservation theorem for preserving selective independent families along countable support iterations. The theorem gives a general framework for a number of results in the literature concerning models in which the independence number 𝔦 \mathfrak{i} is strictly below 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} , including iterations of Sacks forcing, Miller partition forcing, h h -perfect tree forcings, coding with perfect trees. Moreover, applying the theorem, we show that 𝔦 = ℵ 1 \mathfrak{i}=\aleph_{1} in the Miller Lite model. An important aspect of the preservation theorem is the notion of “Cohen preservation”, which we discuss in detail.

be a countable support iteration of proper forcing notions so that for every

\Vdash_{\alpha}``\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}\hbox{ is Cohen preserving}".

If for every

\Vdash_{\alpha}``\dot{\mathbb{Q}}\hbox{ preserves the dense maximality of }\mathcal{I}"

then
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
preserves the selectivity of
\mathcal{I}
.

The proof of Theorem

is by induction on
\delta
. Before we can begin with it, we need to establish several lemmas. For the rest of this section fix an ordinal
\delta
, a selective independent family
\mathcal{I}
and let
\langle\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}\;|\;\alpha<\delta\rangle
be a countable support iteration of proper forcing notions so that for every
\alpha<\delta
we have that
\Vdash_{\alpha}
“
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
is Cohen preserving”. In particular, by Theorem

,
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
is Cohen preserving

Lemma 3.9
.

If
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
is generic over
V
then in

,
\mathrm{id}(\mathcal{I})
is generated by
\mathrm{id}(\mathcal{I})\cap V
.

Dually the lemma states that in
V[G]
the filter
\mathrm{fil}(\mathcal{I})
is generated by
\mathrm{fil}(\mathcal{I})\cap V
.

Proof.

It does not matter what
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
is here, just that it is proper and Cohen preserving. Let
p\in\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
and let
\dot{X}
be a
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
-name for an infinite subset of
\omega
. Suppose that
p\Vdash\dot{X}\in{\rm id}(\mathcal{I})
. We need to find a ground model
Y\in[\omega]^{\omega}\cap{\rm id}(\mathcal{I})
and an
r\leq p
, so that
r\Vdash\dot{X}\subseteq\check{Y}
. Towards this, use the properness of
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
to find a
q\leq p
and a countable
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
, so that
q\Vdash\dot{X}\in{\rm id}(\mathcal{J})
. This is possible since every element of the ideal is witnessed by a countable subfamily
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
and by the countable covering property of proper forcing notions, such a
\mathcal{J}
can be found in
V
. Since
\mathcal{J}
is countable, we can associate
\mathrm{FF}(\mathcal{J})
with
2^{<\omega}
. Let
\dot{D}
be the
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
-name for the dense open subset of
2^{<\omega}
defined by

q\Vdash\check{g}\in\dot{D}\hbox{ if and only if }\mathcal{J}^{g}\cap\dot{X}=\emptyset.

Since
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
is Cohen preserving, there is an
r\leq q
and a dense

so that
r\Vdash\check{E}\subseteq\dot{D}
. Let

. Observe that
Y\in{\rm id}(\mathcal{J})
and hence
Y\in{\rm id}(\mathcal{I})
. To see this, let

be arbitrary and let

be in
E
. We have that
Y\subseteq\omega\setminus\mathcal{J}^{g}
and hence
Y\cap\mathcal{J}^{g}=\emptyset
which by definition means that
Y
is in the ideal. Moreover

Claim 3.10
.

r\Vdash\dot{X}\subseteq\check{Y}
.

Proof.

Let
r\in G
be
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
-generic over
V
. Note that by the way
\dot{D}^{G}
is defined we have that
\dot{X}=\bigcap_{g\in\dot{D}^{G}}(\omega\setminus\mathcal{J}^{g})
and since
E\subseteq\dot{D}^{G}
we’re done.
∎

which completes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

The importance of the above can be summed up by the following:

Lemma 3.11
.

\Vdash_{\delta}
“
{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})
is Ramsey”

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
is Cohen preserving, it is
\omega^{\omega}
-bounding and hence
{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})
remains a Q-set in any generic extension of
V
by
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
. Thus, it remains to see that it is a P-set in any such extension as well. Let
\{\dot{X}_{n}\;|\;n<\omega\}
be a countable family of names for elements of
{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})
. By Lemma
3.9
, we know that each
\dot{X}_{n}
has a ground model almost subset
Y_{n}\in{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})
. By properness, there is a superset
\{Z_{n}\;|\;n<\omega\}
in the ground model and, since
{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})
is a P-set in the ground model, there is a pseudo-intersection
Z\in V\cap{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})
. But then
Z
is a pseudo-intersection of the
\dot{X}_{n}
’s and so we are done.
∎

It follows from Lemma
3.11
that all we need to do, in order to prove Theorem
3.8
, is to show that dense maximality is preserved. This we will do momentarily though first we need to recall a preservation result due to Shelah (see
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
):

Theorem 3.12
.

Assume
CH
. Let
\delta
be a limit ordinal and let
\langle\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\delta\rangle
be a countable support iteration of
{{}^{\omega}}\omega
-bounding proper posets. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq P(\omega)
be a Ramsey set and let
\mathcal{H}
be a subset of
P(\omega)\backslash\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle_{\mathrm{up}}
. If
V^{\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}}\vDash P(\omega)=\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle_{\mathrm{up}}\cup\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle_{\mathrm{dn}}
for all
\alpha<\delta
then
V^{\mathbb{P}_{\delta}}\vDash P(\omega)=\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle_{\mathrm{up}}\cup\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle_{\mathrm{dn}}
as well.

Now we can complete the proof of Theorem
3.8
:

Proof of
3.8
.

The proof is by induction on
\delta
. The successor step is by assumption and so we focus on the limit step. Inductively we have that if
\beta<\delta
and
G_{\beta}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
is generic over
V
then

V[G_{\beta}]\models P(\omega)=\langle{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})\cap V\rangle_{\rm up}\cup\langle\omega\setminus\mathcal{I}^{g}\;|\;g\in\mathsf{FF}(\mathcal{I})\rangle_{\rm dn}.

But then by Theorem
3.12
plus the fact that
{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})
is a Ramsey filter in
V^{\mathbb{P}_{\delta}}
we get

\Vdash_{\delta}P(\omega)=\langle{\rm fil}(\mathcal{I})\cap V\rangle_{\rm up}\cup\langle\omega\setminus\mathcal{I}^{g}\;|\;g\in\mathsf{FF}(\mathcal{I})\rangle_{\rm dn}.

By Lemma
3.3
, this is exactly what we needed to show.
∎

4.
An Application: Miller Lite Forcing

In this section we give an application of Theorem
3.8
. Recall from
[
9
]
that Miller Lite forcing, denoted
\mathbb{ML}
, consists of finitely branching trees
T\subseteq\omega^{<\omega}
so that for every
s\in T
and
n<\omega
there is a
t\in T
with
t\supseteq s
which has at least
n
many immediate successors. The order is inclusion. We will show the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
\mathsf{CH}
holds then
\mathbb{ML}
preserves the dense maximality of any selective independent family.

The forcing notion
\mathbb{ML}
is proper, in fact Axiom A (
[
9
, Lemma 6.3]
), has the Sacks property (
[
15
, Lemma 5.1]
) and hence
by Lemma
2.3
is Cohen preserving. As a consequence of our preservation theorem we obtain:

Corollary 4.2
.

In the Miller Lite model
\mathfrak{i}=\aleph_{1}
.

Here by “the Miller Lite model” we mean any model obtained by iteratively forcing with
\mathbb{ML}
with countable support over a ground model satisfying
\mathsf{GCH}
. The model was first studied by Geschke (see
[
9
]
) in his investigation of continuous colorings of
2^{\omega}
. Amongst other things, it is shown in
[
9
]
that in the Miller Lite model
\mathfrak{hm}<\mathfrak{l}_{n,\omega}
holds for all
n<\omega
3
3

3

Since we will not use these cardinals here, and their definitions are a little involved, we omit them and refer the reader to
[
9
]
.
. As a result we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

It is consistent that
\mathfrak{i}=\mathfrak{hm}<\mathfrak{l}_{n,\omega}
for all
n<\omega
.

The rest of this section is devoted to proving Theorem
4.1
. We begin with some terminology concerning Miller lite trees. Fix a
p\in\mathbb{ML}
. If
t\in p
let
p_{t}=\{s\in p\;|\;s\subseteq t\;{\rm or}\;t\subseteq s\}
and let
{\rm succ}_{p}(t)
denote the set of immediate successors of
t
in
p
.

Definition 4.4
.

For
n<\omega
let

p^{n}=\{t\in p\;|\;t\in{\rm succ}_{p}(s)\hbox{ for some }s\in p\hbox{ that is minimal with }|{\rm succ}_{p}(s)|>n\}.

For
p,q\in\mathbb{ML}
write
p\leq_{n}q
if
p\leq q
and
p^{n}=q^{n}
.

With this terminology, fusion works as usual. Namely if
\{p_{n}\}_{n<\omega}
is a sequence of conditions with
p_{n+1}\leq_{n}p_{n}
for all
n<\omega
then
p_{\omega}:=\bigcap_{n<\omega}p_{n}
is a condition again and
p_{\omega}\leq_{n}p_{n}
for all
n<\omega
. In particular, the sequence of partial orders
\{\leq_{n}\}_{n<\omega}
witnesses the aformentioned fact that
\mathbb{ML}
satisfies Axiom A. Given an
\mathbb{ML}
-name
\dot{X}
for an infinite set of natural numbers we say that a condition
p
is
preprocessed for

if for each
n<\omega
and each

we have that
p_{t}
decides
\dot{X}\cap\check{n}
. A straightforward fusion argument gives the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\dot{X}
be a
\mathbb{ML}
-name for an element of
[\omega]^{\omega}
. Then the set of conditions preprocessed for
\dot{X}
is dense in

.

For a convenience, for a condition

let us denote by

. Note that this set is finite for any

.

4.1
.

Assume

-name for an infinite subset of

Claim 4.6

For all

Otherwise there are

5.
Conclusion and Open Questions

The foregoing leaves a number of interesting questions open:

Question 1
.

Is Cohen preservation needed in Theorem
3.8
? In particular, is it possible that preserving selective independence implies Cohen preservation?

Question 2
.

If
\mathcal{I}
is densely maximal and not necessarily selective is it indestructible by some reasonable class of forcing notions?

Question 3
.

Is it consistent there are no selective independent families? Is it possible their (non)existence follows from some cardinal characteristic (in)equality? Are there selective independent families in the Silver model?

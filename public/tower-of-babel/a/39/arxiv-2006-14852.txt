Abstract
. Boolean valued models for a signature ℒ \mathcal{L} are generalizations of ℒ \mathcal{L} -structures in which we allow the ℒ \mathcal{L} -relation symbols to be interpreted by boolean truth values. For example, for elements a , b ∈ ℳ a,b\in\mathcal{M} with ℳ \mathcal{M} a 𝖡 \mathsf{B} -valued ℒ \mathcal{L} -structure for some boolean algebra 𝖡 \mathsf{B} , ( a = b ) (a=b) may be neither true nor false, but get an intermediate truth value in 𝖡 \mathsf{B} . In this paper we expand and relate the work of Mansfield and others on the semantics of boolean valued models, and of Monro and others on the adjunctions between 𝖡 \mathsf{B} -valued models and 𝖡 + \mathsf{B}^{+} -presheaves for a boolean algebra 𝖡 \mathsf{B} . First of all we introduce, for presheaves on the poset of positive elements 𝖡 + \mathsf{B}^{+} of a complete boolean algebra 𝖡 \mathsf{B} , a topological presentation via étalé bundles of the sheafification process with respect to most of the subcanonical Grothendieck topologies on 𝖡 + \mathsf{B}^{+} , including the dense Grothendieck topology. Next we link these topological/category theoretic results to the theory of boolean valued models. We start giving a different proof of a result by Monro identifying presheaves on (complete) boolean algebras with boolean valued models, and sheaves (according to the dense Grothendieck topology) with boolean valued models having the mixing property . We also give an exact topological characterization (the so called fullness property ) of which boolean valued models satisfy Łoś Theorem (i.e. the general form of the Forcing Theorem which Cohen —Scott, Solovay, Vopenka— established for the special case given by the forcing method in set theory). Next we separate the fullness property from the mixing property, by showing that the latter is strictly stronger. Finally we give an exact categorical characterization of which presheaves correspond to full boolean valued models in terms of the structure of global sections of their associated étalé space.

1.3.
Maximal filters of closed or open sets and Hausdorff compactifications

We now want to relate the points of Hausdorff compact spaces to (maximal) filters of topological sets. This is standard in the literature. However, we prefer to discuss in detail the delicate topological points we want the reader to focus on.
The guiding example of filter on a partial order

4.1.

Definition 4.9

\mathsf{B}

Theorem 4.11

(Łoś Theorem for boolean valued models)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a well behaved
\mathsf{B}
-valued model for the signature
\mathcal{L}
.
The following are equivalent:

•

\mathcal{M}
is full.

•

for all
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
-formulae
\phi(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})

and all
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
there exist
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\bigvee_{\tau\in\mathcal{M}}\left\llbracket\phi(\tau,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket=\bigvee_{i=1}^{m}\left\llbracket\phi(\sigma_{i},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket.

Proof.

We sketch the proof for the case of existential formulae.

First, assume
\mathcal{M}
to be full:

\mathcal{M}/_{G}\models\exists x\psi(x,[\tau_{1}]_{G},\dots,[\tau_{n}]_{G})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\left\llbracket\exists x\psi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}\in G.

Thus for every
G
such that
\left\llbracket\exists x\psi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}\in G
there exists
\sigma_{G}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{M}/_{G}\models\psi([\sigma_{G}]_{G},[\tau_{1}]_{G},\dots,[\tau_{n}]_{G}).

Again using the hypothesis, we obtain that for every
G\in N_{\left\llbracket\exists x\psi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket}
there exists
\sigma_{G}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
G\in N_{\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma_{G},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket}
, that is:

N_{\left\llbracket\exists x\psi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket}\subseteq\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{M}}N_{\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket}.

By compactness,
N_{\left\llbracket\exists x\psi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}N_{\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma_{i},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket}
, which is our thesis.

Conversely, assume that for every formula
\phi
,
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
there exist
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\bigvee_{\tau\in\mathcal{M}}\left\llbracket\phi(\tau,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket=\bigvee_{i=1}^{m}\left\llbracket\phi(\sigma_{i},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket.

By induction on the complexity of the formulae we prove that
\mathcal{M}
is full. Let us consider only the non-trivial case:
\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\exists x\phi(x,x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}/G\models\exists x\phi(x,[\tau_{1}]_{G},\dots,[\tau_{n}]_{G})\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}/G\models\phi([\sigma]_{G},[\tau_{1}]_{G},\dots,[\tau_{n}]_{G})

\displaystyle\textit{ for some }\sigma\in\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\phi(\sigma,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket\in G\textit{ for some }\sigma\in\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\exists x\phi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket\in G.

Conversely, if
\left\llbracket\exists x\phi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket\in G
, since
\left\llbracket\exists x\phi(x,\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket=\bigvee_{i=1}^{m}\left\llbracket\phi(\sigma_{i},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket
, there exists
i\in\left\{1,\dots,m\right\}
such that
\left\llbracket\phi(\sigma_{i},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\right\rrbracket\in G
. By inductive hypothesis

\mathcal{M}/G\models\phi([\sigma_{i}]_{G},[\tau_{1}]_{G},\dots,[\tau_{n}]_{G})

and so
\mathcal{M}/G\models\exists x\phi(x,[\tau_{1}]_{G},\dots,[\tau_{n}]_{G})
.

, let

\displaystyle\left\llbracket f=g\right\rrbracket:=

\displaystyle\bigl[\left\{r\in\mathbb{R}:f(r)=g(r)\right\}\bigr]_{\mathrm{Null}}

\displaystyle\left\llbracket f<g\right\rrbracket:=

\displaystyle\bigl[\left\{r\in\mathbb{R}:f(r)<g(r)\right\}\bigr]_{\mathrm{Null}}

\displaystyle\left\llbracket C(f)\right\rrbracket:=

\displaystyle\bigvee\left\{[U]_{\mathrm{Null}}\in\mathsf{MALG}:f\restriction U\textit{ is constant}\right\}.

A key property of analytic functions in one variable is that they are constant on a non discrete set
if and only if they are everywhere constant.

The reader can check that

(C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}),\left\llbracket-=-\right\rrbracket,\left\llbracket-<-\right\rrbracket,\left\llbracket C(-)\right\rrbracket)
is a
\mathsf{MALG}
-valued model.

Fix
f\in C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
and consider the sentence
\phi:=\exists y(f<y\wedge C(y))
. Let
c_{r}
denote the map
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

which is constantly

and let
a_{n}:=\sup(f\restriction{(n-1,n)})+1
. Then

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\exists y(f<y\wedge C(y))\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{g\in C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})}\left\llbracket f<g\wedge C(g)\right\rrbracket\geq\bigvee_{r\in\mathbb{R}}\left\llbracket f<c_{r}\right\rrbracket\wedge\left\llbracket C(c_{r})\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{r\in\mathbb{R}}\left\llbracket f<c_{r}\right\rrbracket\geq\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\llbracket f<c_{a_{n}}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\geq\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}[(n-1,n)]_{\mathrm{Null}}=[\mathbb{R}]_{\mathrm{Null}}.

In particular, taking

, we have

\left\llbracket\exists y(\textrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}}<y\wedge C(y)\right\rrbracket^{C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})}=1_{\mathsf{MALG}}.

However, let

be an ultrafilter in
\mathsf{MALG}
which extends the family
\left\{[(n,+\infty)]_{\mathrm{Null}}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
. Then, for every

,

\left\llbracket\neg(\textrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}}<c_{r})\right\rrbracket=\left[(r,+\infty)\right]_{\mathrm{Null}}\in G,

therefore
C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})/G\models\neg\exists y([\textrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}}]_{G}<y\wedge C(y))
.

4.2.
The mixing property and fullness

The mixing property gives us a sufficient condition for having the fullness property which is, usually, easier to check; as we will see later the mixing property characterizes those boolean valued models which are sheaves.

Definition 4.13
.

Let
\kappa
be a cardinal,

\mathcal{L}
be a first order language,
\mathsf{B}
a

\kappa
-complete boolean algebra,

\mathcal{M}
a
\mathsf{B}
-valued model for
\mathcal{L}
.

•

\mathcal{M}
satisfies the
\kappa
-mixing property
if for every antichain
A\subset\mathsf{B}

of size at most
\kappa
, and for every subset
\{\tau_{a}:a\in A\}\subseteq M
, there exists

\tau\in M
such that
a\leq\llbracket\tau=\tau_{a}\rrbracket
for every
a\in A
.

•

\mathcal{M}
satisfies the
<\kappa
-mixing property

if it satisfies the
\lambda
-mixing property for all
cardinals
\lambda<\kappa
.

•

\mathcal{M}
satisfies the
mixing property

if it satisfies the
|\mathsf{B}|
-mixing property.

In
[
2
]
models with the
<\omega
-mixing property are called models which
admit gluing
.
Whether a
\mathsf{B}
-valued model
\mathcal{M}
has the mixing property depends only on the interpretation of the equality symbol by
\left\llbracket-=-\right\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be a complete boolean algebra and let
\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathsf{B}
-valued model for
\mathcal{L}
. Assume that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the
\kappa
-mixing property for some
\kappa\geq\min\left\{|\mathsf{B}|,|M|\right\}
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is full.

Proof.

Fix a formula
\phi(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
in
\mathcal{L}
and
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in M
.
Fix moreover an enumeration
\left(\tau_{i}:i\in I\right)
of
M
.
Since
\left\llbracket\exists x\varphi(x,\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket=\bigvee_{i\in I}\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{i},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket,

we can refine the family
\left\{\left\llbracket(\varphi(\tau_{i},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket:i\in I\right\}
to an antichain
\left\{a_{j}:j\in J\right\}

as follows: let

J:=I\setminus\left\{i\in I:\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{i},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket\setminus\bigvee_{j<i}\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{j},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket=0\right\}.

In particular,
J
is well-ordered with the order induced by
I
and we have that
\min J=\min I
.
Define

a_{\min J}:=\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{\min I},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket

and, for
J\ni i>\min J
,

a_{i}:=\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{i},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket\setminus\bigvee_{J\ni j<i}\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{j},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket.

If
A:=\left\{a_{j}:j\in J\right\}
, it is clear that
\bigvee A=\left\llbracket\exists x\varphi(x,\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket
and

|A|\leq|M|,|\mathsf{B}|\leq\kappa
. Since
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the
\kappa
-mixing property, there exists
\tau\in M
such that

a_{i}\leq\left\llbracket\tau=\tau_{i}\right\rrbracket

for every
i\in J
. In particular, since
a_{i}\leq\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{i},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket
, we have that

a_{i}=a_{i}\wedge\left\llbracket\tau=\tau_{i}\right\rrbracket\leq\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau_{i},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket\wedge\left\llbracket\tau=\tau_{i}\right\rrbracket\leq\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau,\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket

for every
i\in J
.
Hence
\left\llbracket\varphi(\tau,\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket\geq\bigvee_{i\in J}a_{i}=\bigvee A=\left\llbracket\exists x\varphi(x,\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket
and so
\mathcal{M}
is full.
∎

\mathcal{B}^{\varphi}:=\left\{\left\{\left(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n},G\right):\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket\in G\in N_{c}\right\}:\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in M,N_{c}\subseteq N_{b_{\varphi}}\right\}.

It is easy to check that
E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
equipped with this topology renders continuous the map
p_{\varphi}
.
Moreover,
p_{\varphi}:E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}\to N_{b_{\varphi}}
is the bundle (e.g induced by
\Lambda^{0}
and according to
[
8
, II]
) associated to the étalé space of the presheaf
\mathcal{G}^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}:D_{\varphi}^{\textrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Set}
defined as follows:

\mathcal{G}^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}(c):=\left\{\left([\sigma_{1}]_{F_{c}},\dots,[\sigma_{n}]_{F_{c}}\right):\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in M\textit{ and }c\leq\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket\right\}.

Notice by the way that
each
\left([\sigma_{1}]_{F_{c}},\dots,[\sigma_{n}]_{F_{c}}\right)\in\mathcal{G}^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}(N_{c})
defines a continuous open section

(\dot{\sigma}_{1}\times\dots\times\dot{\sigma}_{n}):G\mapsto\left([\sigma_{1}]_{G},\dots,[\sigma_{n}]_{G},G\right)

of
p_{\varphi}
over
N_{c}
.

We can now give a local characterization (i.e. formula by formula) of the fullness property.

Definition 6.1
.

Given a
\mathsf{B}
-valued model
\mathcal{M}
for
\mathcal{L}
, an

\mathcal{L}
-formula
\varphi
is
\mathcal{M}
-full
if
A_{\varphi}=N_{b_{\varphi}}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be a boolean algebra and let
\mathcal{M}
be a well behaved
\mathsf{B}
-valued model for the language
\mathcal{L}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{M}
is full;

(2)

\varphi
is
\mathcal{M}
-full
for every
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
-formula
\varphi
;

(3)

A_{\varphi}
is closed for every
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
-formula
\varphi
;

(4)

for every
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
-formula
\varphi
such that
E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
is non-empty,
E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
has at least one
global section.

Moreover if
\mathcal{M}
has the
<\omega
-mixing property the above are also equivalent to

(5)

for every
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
-formula
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
such that
E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
is non-empty,
E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
has at least one
global section of the form
\dot{\sigma}_{1}\times\dots\times\dot{\sigma}_{n}
.

Proof.

1
implies
2
. Indeed, If
\mathcal{M}
is full, for every
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
-formula
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
there exist
\sigma_{1}^{(1)},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{(1)},\dots,\sigma_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{(m)}\in M
such that

\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1}^{(i)},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{(i)})\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\exists x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\,\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\right\rrbracket,\quad\text{ i. e. }\quad N_{b_{\varphi}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\,N_{\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1}^{(i)},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{(i)})\right\rrbracket}\subseteq A_{\varphi}.

Conversely, assume
2
. This means that

A_{\varphi}=\bigcup_{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in\mathcal{M}}N_{\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket}=N_{b_{\varphi}}.

Thus
\{N_{\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})\right\rrbracket}:\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in\mathcal{M}\}
is an open cover of the compact space
N_{b_{\varphi}}
. By compactness,
1
holds.

The equivalence between
2
and
3
is immediate. Now we prove that
1
implies
4
. We have that

N_{b_{\varphi}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\,N_{\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1}^{(i)},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{(i)})\right\rrbracket}.

Consider

C_{1}:=N_{\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1}^{(1)},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{(1)})\right\rrbracket},\qquad C_{i}:=N_{\left\llbracket\varphi(\sigma_{1}^{(i)},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{(i)})\right\rrbracket}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}C_{j},\quad i=2,\dots,m.

The sets just defined are clopen, pairwise disjoint and they cover
N_{b_{\varphi}}
. Define
s:N_{b_{\varphi}}\to E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
by

s\restriction C_{i}:=\dot{\sigma}^{(i)}_{1}\times\dots\times\dot{\sigma}^{(i)}_{n}.

Thus
s
is continuous and it is a global section of
p_{\varphi}
.

Finally, assume
s
is a section as in
4
, which means that
p_{\varphi}\circ s=\textrm{Id}_{N_{b_{\varphi}}}
. In particular,
p_{\varphi}
has to be surjective, and so
2
holds.

The missing details are left to the reader.
∎

The mixing property is strictly stronger than the fullness property. Here is a reformulation of Proposition
4.14
in the language of bundles and sheaves:

Corollary 6.3
.

Assume
\mathsf{B}
is a complete boolean algebra and
\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathsf{B}
-valued model with the mixing property.
Then, for every
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
-formula
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
\mathcal{G}^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a sheaf.
In particular, if
\mathcal{M}
has the mixing property, every local section of
E^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{M}}
can be extended to a global one. Moreover, each global section is induced by an
n
-tuple of elements of
\mathcal{M}
.

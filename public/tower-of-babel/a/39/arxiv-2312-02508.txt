The Diagonals of a Ferrers Diagram
Giuseppe Cotardo, Anina Gruica, Alberto Ravagnani

Abstract
We propose and develop a theory of Ferrers diagrams and their q q -rook polynomials solely based on their diagonals. We show that the cardinalities of the diagonals of a Ferrers diagram are equivalent information to their rook numbers, q q -rook polynomials, and the rank distribution of matrices supported on the diagram. Our approach is based on the concept of canonical form of a Ferrers diagrams, and on two simple diagram operations as the main proof tools. In the second part of the paper we develop the same theory for symmetric Ferrers diagrams, considering symmetric and alternating matrices supported on them. As an application of our results, we establish some combinatorial identities linking symmetric and alternating matrices, which do not appear to have an obvious bijective proof, and which generalize some curious results in enumerative combinatorics.



Introduction

Rook theory studies
the ways in which a certain number of non-attacking rooks can be placed on a given subset of the chess board; see e.g.
[
13
]
for a general reference. The number of such placements depends on the number of rooks one wishes to place and on the shape of the board’s subset. Ferrers diagrams are special subsets of the board
that have a “staircase” shape. Their rook theory has been extensively studied, also in connection with the number of matrices over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
whose support is contained in the diagram; see
[
6
,
9
,
8
,
4
,
3
]
. The study of this connection is often called
“
q
-rook theory” and it is the combinatorial
q
-analogue of (classical) rook theory. Ferrers diagrams appear to behave particularly well from a
q
-rook theory perspective. For instance, the number of matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of any given rank supported on a Ferrers diagram is a polynomial in
q
, a property that is false for general diagrams; see for instance
[
14
, Section 8]
. Skew Ferrers diagrams behave somewhat similarly to Ferrers diagrams, although their theory is substantially more complex
[
10
]
.

This paper shows that the
q
-rook theory of Ferrers diagrams
is linked to the combinatorics of their diagonals in a very natural way.
In fact, it follows from our results that the
q
-rook polynomials of a Ferrers diagrams are equivalent information to the cardinalities of their diagonals. As a corollary, we obtain that to check whether or not two Ferrers diagrams have the same (
q
-)rook numbers it suffices to check if their diagonals have the same cardinalities.
Clearly, the cardinalities of the diagonals of a Ferrers diagram do not fully specify the diagram itself: our paper shows that they
nonetheless retain the relevant combinatorial information.

Deriving the
q
-rook theory of Ferrers diagrams via their diagonals comes with some other interesting advantages, which we briefly survey in the remainder of this introduction, offering an overview of the contributions made by this paper.

Focusing on the diagonals of Ferrers diagrams allows us to
introduce a notion of equivalence between diagrams. We call Ferrers diagrams
diagonally equivalent
if their diagonals have the same cardinalities.
We then identify two operations
that take a diagram as input and return a smaller diagram. We call these operations
puncturing
and
reduction
, and study them in Section
2
.
Puncturing and reduction preserve diagonal equivalence of Ferrers diagrams. In particular, they can be efficiently used in recursive arguments that establish the properties of equivalent diagrams. That’s precisely the approach we take
in Section
3
. Our main result in this context is Theorem
3.1

and its consequences, stated in Corollary
3.2
.

In the second part of the paper we study the theory of Ferrers diagrams and their diagonals in connection with alternating and symmetric matrices supported on them. This naturally leads to the notion of
symmetric
Ferrers diagram; see Section
4
.
We introduce notions of
q
-rook polynomials for symmetric Ferrers diagrams that capture the combinatorics of alternating and symmetric matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This revises and partially extends the approach of
[
9
]
.

In the last section of the paper, we apply the theory of
q
-rook polynomials to derive curious identities involving symmetric and alternating matrices at the same time. It is well known that these two classes of matrices exhibit similarities that are hard to explain via bijective proofs; see e.g.
[
10
, Section 3]
.
q
-Rook polynomials turn out to be powerful tools to establish these identities. Our formulas generalize a result of
[
9
]
from unrestricted matrices to matrices supported on a Ferrers diagram.

Following Haglund’s work

Definition 1.2
.

The

\operatorname{supp}(M)=\{(i,j):M_{ij}\neq 0\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}

\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}

\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}]

\textnormal{supp}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.

Clearly,
\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}]
has dimension
|\mathcal{F}|
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We also recall the following definitions from rook theory.

Definition 1.3
.

A
placement
of
non-attacking rooks
is a subset
P\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
with the property that no two elements of
P
have the same row or column index. The elements of
P
are called
rooks
. For a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
and for
r\geq 0
, we denote by
\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)
the set of non-attacking rook placements
P
with
P\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and
|P|=r
.

Definition 1.4
.

The
degree
of a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}

is
\partial(\mathcal{F})=\max\{r:\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)\neq\emptyset\}
.

Note that
\partial(\mathcal{F})
is the degree of the classical rook polynomial associated with
\mathcal{F}
, which explains our choice for the name.
It turns out that
\partial(\mathcal{F})
coincides with the maximum rank a matrix supported on
\mathcal{F}
can have, independently on the field size
q
. This fact is well known, but we include a short proof for completeness.

Proposition 1.5
.

We have
\partial(\mathcal{F})=\max\{\textnormal{rk}(M):M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}]\}
for any Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)
. Construct a matrix
M
by setting

M_{ij}=1
if
(i,j)\in P
and
M_{ij}=0
otherwise. Then
M
is supported on
\mathcal{F}
and has rank
r
. Vice versa,
by the König-Egeváry Theorem (see
[
13
, Chapter 7]
)
\partial(\mathcal{F})
is the minimum number of lines (i.e., rows or columns) that are needed to cover all the elements of
\mathcal{F}
. Therefore the rank of any matrix
M
supported on
\mathcal{F}
cannot exceed
\partial(\mathcal{F})
.
∎

The
q
-rook polynomials of a Ferrers diagram are defined as follows.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Ferrers diagram and let
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
r
-th

-rook polynomial
of
\mathcal{F}
is

R_{r}(\mathcal{F};q)=\sum_{P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)}q^{\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P)}\in\mathbb{Z}[q].

We set
R_{r}(\mathcal{F};q)=0
if
r<0
or
\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)=\emptyset
, and
R_{0}(\mathcal{F};q)=q^{|\mathcal{F}|}
.

The polynomials of Definition
1.7

are the
q
-analogues of the classical rook polynomials; see e.g.
[
13
,
7
]
. A remarkable fact (see
[
2
, page 257]
) is that Ferrers diagrams have the same classical rook polynomial if and only if they have the same
q
-rook polynomials.

In this paper, we will often need to count the number of matrices of given rank in an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear space. We introduce the following notation to make the statements more compact.

Notation 1.8
.

For an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear space of matrices
\mathcal{C}\leq\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}
and an integer
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we let

W_{r}(\mathcal{C})=|\{M\in\mathcal{C}:\textnormal{rk}(M)=r\}|
.

We can finally state Haglund’s theorem connecting rook theory and the distribution of matrices supported on a Ferrers diagram.

Theorem 1.9

(
[
6
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Ferrers diagram and let
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We have

\displaystyle W_{r}(\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}])=(q-1)^{r}q^{|\mathcal{F}|-r}R_{r}(\mathcal{F};q^{-1}).

2
Operations on Ferrers Diagrams

The classical theory of Ferrers diagrams is mostly centered around the properties of their rows and columns. In this paper, we
show that the theory of Ferrers diagrams
can be reconstructed entirely
by looking only at their
diagonals
.
This gives rise to an interesting notion of equivalence of Ferrers diagrams:
we declare two diagrams equivalent if they have the same diagonal cardinalities. We then show that equivalent diagrams have the same
q
-rook polynomials, which has surprising consequences for the behaviour of matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
supported on a Ferrers diagram.

The main results are stated and proved in Section
3
. This section contains a series of technical results that we will need in the proofs. Our approach is based on two operations on diagrams (
puncturing
and
reduction
), which
preserve equivalence.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the
i
-th diagonal
of the board
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
is the set
\Delta_{i}=\{(r,i-r+1):r\in\mathbb{N}\}
. We say that Ferrers diagrams
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}

are
diagonally equivalent
if
|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|=|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., if the cardinalities of their diagonals are the same.

The following is a visual representation of
the five diagonals of the board
\{1,\ldots,5\}\times\{1,\ldots,5\}
, each of which has a different color:

We will see throughout the paper that the cardinalities of the intersections between a Ferrers diagram and the board’s diagonals retain a wealth of information about
the diagram. For instance, its degree can be easily computed as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

We have
\partial(\mathcal{F})=\max\{|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
for any Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

Proof.

Proposition
1.5
and
[
4
, Claim A]

give
\partial(\mathcal{F})\in\{|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
, which implies
\partial(\mathcal{F})\leq\max\{|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
. On the other hand, let
r=\max\{|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
and define the matrix
M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}
by
M_{ij}=1
if
(i,j)\in\Delta_{r}\cap\mathcal{F}
and
M_{ij}=0
otherwise. Then
M
is
supported on
\mathcal{F}
and
\textnormal{rk}(M)=r
. It follows that
\partial(\mathcal{F})\geq r
by Proposition
1.5
.
∎

Remark 2.4

The diagonals of a Ferrers diagram partition its entries

Therefore, and more formally,

Notation 2.5
.

For any subset

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

\Pi(\mathcal{F},i,j)

\Pi(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},i,j)

i\in\mathbb{N}
and

Claim
A

Let
(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}
be the greatest element of
\mathcal{F}

(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}=\max_{\textup{lex}}(\mathcal{F})
, and suppose that
(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}=(a,b)
, for some
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\Pi(\mathcal{F},i,j)
is the sub-board of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
obtained by deleting the cells in the set
\{(1,1),\ldots,(1,b),\ldots,(a,b)\}
and aligning the remaining cells to the top and then to the left.

Proof of the claim.

Recall that, by Definition
2.7
and Notation
2.5
, we have that
(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}
is the Ferrers sub-diagram of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
obtained by deleting the set
\textup{ATK}(\{(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}\})
, that is
\{(1,b),\ldots,(a,b),(a,b-1),\ldots,(a,1)\}
, since
(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}=\max_{\textup{lex}}(\mathcal{F})
. One can check that
\textup{ATK}(\{(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}\})
and
\{(1,1),\ldots,(1,b),\ldots,(a,b)\}
contain the same number of cells in the rows and columns of
\mathcal{F}
and the statement easily follows.
∎

We also note the following.

(i)

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be obtained by deleting the set
\{(\Delta_{s}\cap\mathcal{F})_{t}:s\in\mathbb{N},t\in\{1,\ldots,|\Delta_{s}\cap\mathcal{F}|\},(i,j)<_{\textup{lex}}(s,t)\}
from
\mathcal{F}
. Then
(\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j}=\max_{\textup{lex}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.

(ii)

For any
s\in\{1,\ldots,i\}
, the set
\{(1,1),\ldots,(1,b),\ldots,(a,b)\}
intersects
\Delta_{s}(\mathcal{F})
in exactly one cell.

(iii)

The Ferrers diagram
\Pi(\mathcal{F},i,j)
has
i-1
diagonals.

As a consequence,
|\Delta_{s}\cap\Pi(\mathcal{F},i,j)|=|\Delta_{s+1}\cap\mathcal{F}|-1
for any
s\in\{1,\ldots,i-1\}
, and
|\Delta_{s}\cap\Pi(\mathcal{F},i,j)|=0
for
s\geq i
. The statement now follows from the fact that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are diagonally equivalent.
∎

3
Equivalence of Ferrers Diagrams and Applications

This section contains the main results of the paper, showing that
the
q
-rook polynomials of a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}
are fully determined by the cardinalities of its diagonals. Note that these cardinalities are far from being enough to determine the diagram
\mathcal{F}
itself.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
be diagonally equivalent Ferrers diagrams in
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
. For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle|\{P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r):\textnormal{inv}(P)=\ell\}|=|\{P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},r):\textnormal{inv}(P)=\ell\}|.

In particular,
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
have the same
r
-th
q
-rook polynomial for all
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

We will show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between placements
P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)
with
\textnormal{inv}(P)=\ell
on the one hand, and placements
P^{\prime}\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},r)
with
\textnormal{inv}(P^{\prime})=\ell
on the other hand, under the assumption that
\Pi(\mathcal{F},P)
and
\Pi(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
are diagonally equivalent.
We proceed by induction on
r
. For
r=1
we have

\displaystyle|\{P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},1):\textnormal{inv}(P)=\ell\}|=|\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}|=|\{P^{\prime}\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},1):\textnormal{inv}(P^{\prime})=\ell\}|,

where we used that
|\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}|=|\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|
.
More in detail, the map
(\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F})_{s}\mapsto(\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime})_{s}
for
s\in\{1,\dots,|\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}|\}
is a bijection between the elements of
\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}
and the elements of
\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and by Lemma
2.12
,
\Pi(\mathcal{F},\ell,s)
and
\Pi(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\ell,s)
are diagonally equivalent.

Now assume that the statement of the theorem holds true for all the placements of
r-1
rooks. Let
P=\{(\Delta_{i_{1}}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j_{1}},\ldots,(\Delta_{i_{r}}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j_{r}}\}\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)
with
\textnormal{inv}(P)=\ell
and where
(i_{1},j_{1})\leq_{\textup{lex}}\dots\leq_{\textup{lex}}(i_{r},j_{r})
. Consider
\overline{P}=\{(\Delta_{i_{2}}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j_{2}},\ldots,(\Delta_{i_{r}}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j_{r}}\}
. Then
|\overline{P}|=r-1
and by assumption there exists a rook placement
\overline{P}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with
|\overline{P}^{\prime}|=r-1
,
\textnormal{inv}(P^{\prime})=\textnormal{inv}(\overline{P}^{\prime})
, and such that
\Pi(\mathcal{F},\overline{P})
and
\Pi(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\overline{P}^{\prime})
are again diagonally equivalent. Since no rook in
\overline{P}
attacks
(\Delta_{i_{1}}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j_{1}}
, there is a cell in
\Pi(\mathcal{F},\overline{P})
corresponding to
(\Delta_{i_{1}}\cap\mathcal{F})_{j_{1}}
(according to the reduction) which we denote by
(\Delta_{a}\cap\mathcal{F})_{b}\in\Pi(\mathcal{F},\overline{P})
. We let
\overline{P}=\overline{P}^{\prime}\cup(\Delta_{a}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime})_{b}
, and it is easy to see that
\overline{P}
satisfies all the needed properties, concluding the proof.
∎

As an easy consequence, we obtain the following result. It implies that
the number of matrices of rank
r
supported on
\mathcal{F}
only depends on the cardinalities of the diagonals of
\mathcal{F}
.
We are not able to provide an intuitive or easy explanation for this fact. The result also shows that two Ferrers diagrams have the same (classical) rook numbers
if and only if their diagonals have the same cardinalities.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,m\}
be Ferrers diagrams. The following are equivalent.

1.

\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are diagonally equivalent.

2.

R_{1}(\mathcal{F};q)=R_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime};q)
.

3.

R_{r}(\mathcal{F};q)=R_{r}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime};q)
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

4.

|W_{1}(\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}])|=|W_{1}(\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}^{\prime}])|
.

5.

|W_{r}(\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}])|=|W_{r}(\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}^{\prime}])|
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

6.

\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
have the same (classical) rook numbers.

Proof.

Define
d_{i}=|\triangle_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. By Remark
2.4
we have

\displaystyle R_{1}(\mathcal{F};q)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{F}}q^{\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P)}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d_{i}q^{|\mathcal{F}|-i},

where
\ell=\max\{i:d_{i}\neq 0\}
. This shows that 2. implies 1.
The fact that 6. is equivalent to 3. was shown in
[
2
, page 257]
.
We omit the proofs for the other implications, as they can be checked easily or obtained from Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Fix
r,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then there exists a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}
with

|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|=d_{i}
for all

if and only if

or there exists an integer

We write

The previous remark motivates the following concepts.

In the next theorem we give a recursive formula for the
q
-rook polynomials of a Ferrers diagram only taking into account its diagonals. The result is the diagonal-analogue
of
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
be a Ferrers sequence. We have

R_{r}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell};q)=R_{r}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1};q)q^{d_{\ell}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\ell}}R_{r-1}(d_{2}-1,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-1,j-1;q)q^{d_{\ell}-j}

if
1\leq r\leq\partial(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell})
, and

R_{r}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell};q)=\begin{cases}q^{|\mathcal{F}|}&\textup{ if }r=0,\\
0&\textup{ if }r>\partial(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell})\textup{ or }r<0.\end{cases}

Proof.

The cases
r=0
,
r>\partial(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell})
and
r<0
follow immediately from the definition of
q
-rook polynomial. We assume
1\leq r\leq\partial(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell})
in the remainder of the proof. By Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to prove the statement for a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}
with
|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|=d_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and
|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|=0
otherwise. By the definition of puncturing and reduction (see Definition
2.7
) and Lemma
2.9
, we have

\displaystyle R_{r}(\mathcal{F};q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F})}q^{\textnormal{inv}(P)}

\displaystyle=R_{r}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}};q)q^{d_{\ell}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\ell}}\Pi(\mathcal{F},\ell,j)q^{|\{(\Delta_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{F})_{s}:s\in\{j+1,\ldots,d_{\ell}\}\}|}

\displaystyle=R_{r}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}};q)q^{d_{\ell}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\ell}}\Pi(\mathcal{F},\ell,j)q^{d_{\ell}-j}.

Moreover, the following hold.

(i)

By definition of puncturing,
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is such that
\Delta_{i}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{F}}=\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}=d_{i}
if
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
and
|\Delta_{i}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{F}}|=0
otherwise.

(ii)

By the argument used in Lemma
2.12
, the diagram
\Pi(\mathcal{F},\ell,j)
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|\Delta_{i}\cap\Pi(\mathcal{F},\ell,j)|=|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|-1=d_{i}-1\quad&\textnormal{if $i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell-1\}$,}\\
|\Delta_{i}\cap\Pi(\mathcal{F},\ell,j)|=j-1\quad&\textnormal{if $i=\ell$,}\\
|\Delta_{i}\cap\Pi(\mathcal{F},\ell,j)|=0\quad&\textnormal{if $i>\ell$.}\end{cases}

This concludes the proof.
∎

.

In

4
Symmetric Ferrers Diagrams

In this section
we develop the theory of Ferrers diagrams, with a focus on their diagonals,
for alternating and symmetric matrices.
To this end,
we introduce new definitions of
q
-rook polynomials of
symmetric
Ferrers diagrams. These will allow us to establish the analogues of the results of the previous sections for the alternating and symmetric case.

Recall that a matrix
M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times n}
is
symmetric
if
M_{ij}=M_{ji}
for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and

alternating
if
M_{ii}=0
and
M_{ij}=-M_{ji}
for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
We denote the space of symmetric and alternating matrices by
\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}
and

\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}
, respectively.
We denote by
\Xi=\{(i,i)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\}
the
principal diagonal
of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
.

We already observed that a matrix of rank
r
can be associated to a placement of non-attacking rooks in
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
; see also
[
5
]
. If we restrict to symmetric and alternating matrices, such a placement can only be of a certain form. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
symmetric
if
(i,j)\in\mathcal{F}
implies
(j,i)\in\mathcal{F}
.
For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we let

\textup{NAR}^{\textup{alt}}(\mathcal{F},r)=\{P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r):(i,j)\in P\mbox{ implies }i\neq j\mbox{ and }(j,i)\in P\}.

Clearly, if
r
is odd then
\textup{NAR}^{\textup{alt}}(\mathcal{F},r)=\emptyset
. For any
t,s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we let

\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,s)\\
=\{P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},2t+s):P=T\cup S\textup{ with }T\in\textup{NAR}^{\textup{alt}}(\mathcal{F},2t),\,S\subseteq\Xi,\textup{ and }|S|=s\}.

We call placements in
\textup{NAR}^{\textup{alt}}(\mathcal{F},r)

alternating
and placements in
{\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,s)}

symmetric
.

Ferrers diagrams in connection with alternating matrices have been considered before by Haglund and Remmel in

We start by relating the degree of a Ferrers diagram with the ranks of symmetric and alternating matrices supported on it.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
be a symmetric Ferrers diagram. The following hold.

1.

\partial(\mathcal{F})=\max\{\textnormal{rk}(M):M\in\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]\}
.

2.

If
\partial(\mathcal{F})
is even, then
\partial(\mathcal{F})=\max\{\textnormal{rk}(M):M\in\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]\}
.

Proof.

Recall that by Proposition
1.5
, Proposition
2.2
, and
[
4
, Claim A]
, we have

\partial(\mathcal{F})=\max\{\textnormal{rk}(M):M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}]\}=\max\{|\Delta_{i}\cap\mathcal{F}|:i\in\mathbb{N}\}=|\Delta_{\partial(\mathcal{F})}\cap\mathcal{F}|.

Observe that
\partial(\mathcal{F})\geq\max\{\textnormal{rk}(M):M\in\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]\}
since
\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]\subseteq\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}[\mathcal{F}]
. On the other hand, define
M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{q}^{n\times m}
by
M_{ij}=1
if
(i,j)\in\Delta_{\partial(\mathcal{F})}\cap\mathcal{F}
and
M_{ij}=0
otherwise. Then
M\in\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]
and
\textnormal{rk}(M)=\partial(\mathcal{F})
, which implies the first part of the statement. The second part can be shown similarly.
∎

Definition 4.6

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}

symmetric
(t,s)
-th

\mathcal{F}
is

R_{t,s}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F};q)=\sum_{P\in\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,s)}q^{\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P)},

where we set
R_{t,s}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F};q)=0
if
t<0
,
s<0
or
\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,s)=\emptyset
, and
R_{0,0}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F};q)=q^{|\mathcal{F}|}
.

It is not hard to see that, for any
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,0)=\textup{NAR}^{\textup{alt}}(\mathcal{F},2t)
and therefore
R_{t,0}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F};q)=q^{|\Xi\cap\mathcal{F}|-t}R_{2t}^{\textup{alt}}(\mathcal{F};q)
.
Furthermore,
there exists a symmetric placement of non-attacking rooks
of size
2t+s
on a symmetric Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}
if and only if there exists a matrix of rank
2t+s
in
\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]
.

The next result is the analogue of
[
6
, Theorem 18]
for symmetric matrices. It gives an enumeration formula for the number of symmetric matrices supported on a Ferrers diagram using their associated
q
-rook polynomials.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}
be a symmetric Ferrers diagram and let
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\displaystyle W_{r}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}])=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\geq 0\\
2t+s=r\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{t+s}q^{(|\mathcal{F}|-t-s)/2}R_{t,s}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F};q^{-1/2}).

(4.1)

Proof.

The result is trivial for
r<0
and we assume
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the remainder of the proof. Let
M\in\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]
be a symmetric matrix of rank
r
. We denote by
M_{i}
and
M^{i}
the
i
-th row and the
i
-th column of
M
respectively, where
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We perform a modified Gaussian elimination on
M
which preserves the symmetry of
M
. This procedure consists of the following steps:

1.

Find the lowest nonzero entry
(i,j)
of
M
in the right-most column.

2.

Delete entries above
(i,j)
by adding suitable multiple of
M_{i}
to the corresponding row. Formally, we perform the elementary row operation
M_{s}\rightarrow M_{ij}M_{s}-M_{sj}M_{i}
for all
s\in\{1,\ldots,i-1\}
.

3.

Delete entries to the left of
(i,j)
by adding suitable multiple of
M^{j}
to the corresponding columns. Formally, we perform the elementary column operation
M^{s}\rightarrow M_{ij}M^{s}-M_{is}M^{j}
for all
s\in\{1,\ldots,j-1\}
.

4.

If
i\neq j
, then perform the same procedure to entry
(j,i)
as well.

It is not difficult to check that the resulting matrix is symmetric. We iterate this procedure until there are only
r
nonzero entries left. These entries form a placement of
r
non-attacking rooks on
\mathcal{F}
. Note that any matrix of rank
r
in
\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}]
generates a unique symmetric placement of non-attacking rooks
P\in\textup{NAR}(\mathcal{F},r)
with
|\{(i,j)\in P:i\neq j\}|=2t
and
|\{(i,j)\in P:i=j\}|=s
for some
t,s\geq 0
with
2t+s=r
. In particular, for such a rook placement
P
, we have
(q-1)
choices for each rook in
\{(i,j)\in P:i<j\}
and
(q-1)
choices for each rook in
\{(i,j)\in P:i=j\}
, which gives a total of
(q-1)^{t+s}
choices for the cells containing a rook. The number of the cells that are deleted by rooks
when computing the
inv
statistics is
(|\mathcal{F}|-\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P))/2+(t+s)/2-t-s
, taking into account that the matrix is symmetric and that we do not count the cells containing a rook themselves. This gives a total of
(q-1)^{t+s}q^{(|\mathcal{F}|-t-s-\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P))/2}
matrices with
P
as underlying rook placement. The statement follows by summing over all possible symmetric placements of non-attacking rooks.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Even if it is not immediate to see from the formula in Theorem
4.7
, the number of symmetric matrices of a given rank supported on a symmetric Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}
is a polynomial
q
(where
q
is the underlying field size). To see this, for
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we can rewrite (
4.1
) as

\displaystyle W_{r}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[\mathcal{F}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\geq 0\\
2t+s=r\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{t+s}q^{(|\mathcal{F}|-t-s)/2}\sum_{P\in\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,s)}q^{-\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P)/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\geq 0\\
2t+s=r\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{t+s}\sum_{P\in\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,s)}q^{(|\mathcal{F}|-\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P))/2+(t+s)/2-t-s}.

In order for the above expression to be a polynomial in
q
, it is enough to show that for any
t,s\geq 0
with
2t+s=r
and any
P\in\textup{NAR}^{\textup{sym}}(\mathcal{F},t,s)
, the number

(|\mathcal{F}|-\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P))/2+(t+s)/2-t-s

is a non-negative integer. This follows from the fact that
(|\mathcal{F}|-\textnormal{inv}(\mathcal{F},P))/2+(t+s)/2=\{(i,j)\in\textup{ATK}(P):i\leq j\}
, which is not hard to see.

Note that also the number of alternating matrices of a certain rank supported on a Ferrers diagram is a polynomial in
q
. This can be explained in a similar manner, using the enumeration formula of Theorem
4.5
.

Symmetric and Alternating Matrices

The goal of this section is to
provide connections between enumerative results about alternating and symmetric matrices. While these two classes of matrices are different, there are curious
connections between the number of such matrices with prescribed properties that are hard to explain with bijective proof; see e.g.
[
10
]
.
In this paper, we obtain these via rook theory.

Notation 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a symmetric Ferrers diagram associated with the symmetric Ferrers sequence
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}\cup\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
. Note that the intersection
\Xi\cap\mathcal{F}
only depends on the sequence
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})
. This is because
\mathcal{F}
is symmetric and therefore

\Xi\cap\mathcal{F}=\{(i,i)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\,:\,d_{i}\textnormal{ is odd}\}.

(5.1)

In the following, we sometimes write
\Xi\cap(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})
instead of
\Xi\cap\mathcal{F}
in this case.

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}\cup\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
be symmetric Ferrers sequence. If
r=\partial(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})
is even, then

q^{|\Xi\cap(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})|}W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}])=W_{r}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}]).

In order to prove this, we require some preliminaries results. The following proposition can be seen as a generalization of
[
9
, Proposition 3.8]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
r\geq 2
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}\cup\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
be a symmetric Ferrers sequence. If
r
is odd or
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})=(0,\ldots,0)
, then
W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}])=0
. Otherwise we have

\displaystyle W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}])

\displaystyle=W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1}])

\displaystyle+(q-1)q^{\ell-2}\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{d_{\ell}}{2}\right\rfloor}W_{r-2}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2]).

Proof.

The result is trivial for
r
odd or
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})=(0,\ldots,0)
. We assume
r
even and
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\neq(0,\ldots,0)
in the remainder of the proof. For every
1\leq i\leq\ell
, define

a_{i}=\begin{cases}0&\textup{ if }d_{i}\textup{ even},\\
1&\textup{ if }d_{i}\textup{ odd},\end{cases}\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad b_{\ell}=\left\lfloor\frac{d_{\ell}}{2}\right\rfloor.

Observe that if
b_{\ell}=0
then
\sum_{j=1}^{b_{\ell}}W_{r-2}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2])=0
by definition, since the sum runs over the empty set.
By applying Theorem
4.11
to Theorem
4.5
we get

W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}])\\
=(q-1)^{r/2}q^{(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-r)/2}\Big(R_{r}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1};q^{-1/2})q^{-(d_{\ell}-a_{\ell})/2}\\
+\sum_{j=1}^{b_{\ell}}R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2;q^{-1/2})q^{-(d_{\ell}-2j-a_{\ell})/2}\Big),

where we used the facts that
|\mathcal{F}|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d_{i}
and
|\Xi\cap\mathcal{F}|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}
; see (
5.1
). It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle(q-1)^{r/2}q^{(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-r)/2}R_{r}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1};q^{-1/2})q^{-(d_{\ell}-a_{\ell})/2}

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{r/2}q^{(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}d_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-r)/2}R_{r}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1};q^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1}]).

On the other hand, for any
1\leq j\leq b_{\ell}
, we have

(q-1)^{r/2}q^{(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-r)/2}\sum_{j=1}^{b_{\ell}}R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2;q^{-1/2})q^{-(d_{\ell}-2j-a_{\ell})/2}\\
=\sum_{j=1}^{b_{\ell}}(q-1)^{r/2}q^{(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}d_{i}+2j-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-r)/2}R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2;q^{-1/2}).

(5.2)

Note that if
d_{3}\leq 1
then this sum is
0
, and we can assume
d_{3}\in\{2,3\}
in the remainder of the proof. Observe that if
d_{3}=2
then the only possible symmetric Ferrers sequence is
(1,2,2,\ldots,2)

and we have

R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2-2;q^{-1/2})=R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(0,\ldots,0;q^{-1/2})

which is

Observe also that

and

.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}d_{i}+2j-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-r

\displaystyle=d_{1}+d_{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}(d_{i}-2)+\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}2+2j-2+2-a_{1}-a_{2}-\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-r

\displaystyle=3+\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}(d_{i}-2)+2(\ell-3)+(2j-2)-1-\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-(r-2)

\displaystyle(q-1)^{r/2}q^{(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-r)/2}R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2;q^{-1/2})q^{-(d_{\ell}-2j-a_{\ell})/2}

\displaystyle=q^{\ell-2}(q-1)^{r/2}q^{(\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}(d_{i}-2)+(2j-2)-\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-(r-2))/2}R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2;q^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=(q-1)q^{\ell-2}(q-1)^{(r-2)/2}q^{(\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}(d_{i}-2)+(2j-2)-\sum_{i=3}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-(r-2))/2}\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\quad R_{r-2}^{\textup{alt}}(d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2;q^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=(q-1)q^{\ell-2}W_{r-2}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2]),

which applied to (

) concludes the proof.
∎

The proof of
Theorem
5.2

relies on two more preparatory results.
We establish these
using an induction argument on posets,
sometimes called

.
We refer the reader to

Definition 5.6
.

A poset
(S,\leq)

is
well-founded
if every descending chain of elements is finite, that is, there are no infinite descending chains in
S
with respect to
\leq
.

The well-founded induction principle
for a well-founded poset
(S,\leq)

and a property
P
defined on the elements of
S
:

((P(y)\;\forall\;y\in S\mbox{ with }y\leq x)\Longrightarrow P(x)\;\forall\;x\in S)\Longrightarrow P(x)\;\forall\;x\in S.

In words, if for all
x\in S
, whenever
P(y)
holds for all
y\in S
that
precede

x
implies that
P(x)
holds, then
P(x)
holds for all
x\in S
.

One can check that, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the set of
symmetric Ferrers sequences
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}^{\textup{sym}}

with
the product order
\leq_{\textup{sym}}

is a well-founded poset.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Recall that
|\Xi\cap(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})|=|\{1\leq i\leq\ell\,:\,d_{i}\textup{ odd}\}|
; see (
5.1
). The result is trivial for

W_{r}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[0])=0=W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[0])

We assume

and
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\neq(0,\ldots,0)

5.3
and

then it holds for the symmetric Ferrers sequence
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell},0)
. Therefore it suffices to prove the result for
d_{\ell}\neq 0
. The case where
d_{\ell}
is even is given by Lemma
5.8
. For
d_{\ell}
odd

\ell

5.3
and
5.4

5.9
, and the first part of the proof, we have

\displaystyle W_{r}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}])

\displaystyle=W_{r}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1}])

\displaystyle\qquad+(q-1)q^{\ell-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{d_{\ell}}{2}\right\rfloor}W_{r-2}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{3}-2,\ldots,d_{\ell-1}-2,2j-2])

\displaystyle\qquad+(q-1)q^{(\ell-1)/2}W_{r-1}(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{2}-1,\ldots,d_{\ell}-1])

\displaystyle=q^{|\Xi\cap(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell-1})|}W_{r}(\operatorname{Alt}_{q}^{n\times n}[d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}-1])

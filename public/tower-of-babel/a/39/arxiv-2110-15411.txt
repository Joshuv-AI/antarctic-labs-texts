Remarks on convergence of Morley sequences
Karim Khanaki

Abstract
We refine results of Gannon [ G21 , Theorem 4.7] and Simon [ S15a , Lemma 2.8] on convergence of Morley sequences. We then introduce the notion of eventual N ​ I ​ P NIP , as a property of a model, and prove a variant of [ KP18 , Corollary 2.2] . Finally, we give new characterizations of generically stable types (for countable theories) and reinforce the main result of Pillay [ P18 ] on the model-theoretic meaning of Grothendieck’s double limit theorem.

Eventually stable models

The story started from Grothendieck’s
double limit characterization of weak relative compactness, Theorem 6 in
[
G52
]
. In
[
Ben14
]
Ben Yaacov showed that the “Fundamental Theorem of Stability” is in fact a consequence of Grothendieck’s theorem.
Shortly afterwards, Pillay
[
P18
]
pointed out that the model-theoretic meaning of the Grothendieck theorem is that the formula
\phi(x,y)
does not have the order property in
M
if and only if every complete
\phi
-type
p(x)\in S_{\phi}(M)
has an extension to a complete type
p^{\prime}\in S_{\phi}({\cal U})

which is finitely satisfiable in, and definable over
M
. There, he called such types ‘generically stable’ and said: “We will investigate later to what extent we can deduce the stronger notions of generic stability from not the order property in
M
”. Here, using the previous results/observations, we can prove a result similar to
[
P18
]
for the stronger notions of generic stability. Maybe the following result is the end of this story, and of course the beginning of another story.

(i) In stable theories, every model is eventually stable.

(ii) In

and
\ell_{p}
are symmetric in a strong sense. Finally, the types of
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}
are finitely satisfied in any Banach space, by Krivine’s theorem.

(4) In

]
, we showed that every
\aleph_{0}
-categotical Banach space contains
c_{0}
or
\ell_{p}
. What is the translation of this observation into “classical logic” (if such a translation is essentially possible)? Similar questions can be asked about the Krivine-Maurey theorem (and the claim in (3) whenever a proof of it is given).

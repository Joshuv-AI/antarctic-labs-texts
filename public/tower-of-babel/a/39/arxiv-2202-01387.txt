Data-Driven Stochastic Optimal Control using Linear Transfer Operators
Umesh Vaidya, Duvan Tellez-Castro

Abstract
We provide a data-driven framework for optimal control of a continuous-time stochastic dynamical system. The proposed framework relies on the linear operator theory involving linear Perron-Frobenius (P-F) and Koopman operators. Our first results involving the P-F operator provide a convex formulation to the optimal control problem in the dual space of densities. This convex formulation of the stochastic optimal control problem leads to an infinite-dimensional convex program. The finite-dimensional approximation of the convex program is obtained using a data-driven approximation of the P-F operator. Our second results demonstrate the use of the Koopman operator, which is dual to the P-F operator, for the stochastic optimal control design. We show that the Hamilton Jacobi Bellman (HJB) equation can be expressed using the Koopman operator. We provide an iterative procedure along the lines of a popular policy iteration algorithm based on the data-driven approximation of the Koopman operator for solving the HJB equation. The two formulations, namely the convex formulation involving P-F operator and Koopman based formulation using HJB equation, can be viewed as dual to each other where the duality follows due to the dual nature of P-F and Koopman operators. Finally, we present several numerical examples to demonstrate the efficacy of the developed framework.

The stochastic optimal control problem (SOCP) is a cornerstone of systems and control theory

10

In this paper, we propose an alternate approach for solving SOCP based on the convex formulation of the problem in the dual space of densities. This paper provides a data-driven solution to the SOCP over an infinite time horizon with continuous-time system dynamics. The dual approach leads to a convex infinite-dimensional optimization problem to be solved for the SOCP. Unlike iterative algorithms for solving HJB equation in the primal domain, the convex formulation in the dual space lends itself to a single-shot approach for solving SOCP. We use a linear operator theoretic framework involving P-F and Koopman operators
[
11
]
to provide a novel perspective to the SOCP problem and the computation of its solution using data. We show that the traditional primal formulation of SOCP involving the HJB equation is closely tied to the Koopman operator. Furthermore, the dual convex formulation of the SOCP can be understood naturally through the lenses of
duality between the Koopman and P-F operator.

Contributions
: We provide a convex formulation to the SOCP using the linear operator theoretic framework involving P-F and Koopman operators. The linear P-F and Koopman operators are dual to each other and provide for a linear lifting of nonlinear system dynamics in the space of density and function (observables), respectively. The results are inspired by the dynamical system theory, as the duality in SOCP is discovered through duality between the P-F and Koopman operators.
The SOCP problem is formulated in the dual space of density using P-F operator-based lifting of control system dynamics. This dual approach leads to the infinite-dimensional convex optimization-based formulation of the SOCP. We provide a computational framework based on the data-driven approximation of the P-F operator for the data-driven stochastic optimal control design.
The convex formulation of SOCP is made possible by exploiting the P-F operator’s linearity, positivity, and Markov properties. Furthermore, we show that the hard constraints on the control input and the state can also be written convexly in the dual formulation.
On the other hand, we establish a connection between the Koopman operator and the HJB equation. This connection allows us to develop a numerical algorithm for the data-driven solution of the HJB equation based on Koopman theory. In particular, we provide an iterative algorithm based on a data-driven approximation of the Koopman operator for solving the SOCP problem in the primal domain. This new algorithm is reminiscent of the generalized policy iteration (GPI) algorithm in RL and we call it as Koopman policy iteration (KPI). Moreover, the interpretation of GPI using the Koopman theory opens up the possibility of exploiting the rich spectral theory of the Koopman operator for data-driven control.
It is important to emphasize that the existing iterative algorithm for solving the HJB equation, including our proposed Koopman-based approach, requires an initial control policy to be stabilizing. However, designing stabilizing controller for a stochastic nonlinear system is far from a trivial problem. Our proposed dual approach to SOCP does not suffer from this drawback. The convex optimization problem in our dual framework can be solved as a single shot problem, where almost everywhere stochastic stabilizability arises as constraints of this optimization problem. So the data-driven stochastic stabilization will emerge as the particular case of the main result on SOCP. The results in this paper are an extended version of results from
[
33
]
. In particular, the data-driven computation framework is new to this paper. Results involving convex formulation to SOCP with state and input constraints are also new.
Furthermore, we provide precise characterization for the existence of optimal controller along with the proof of some of the key results which were missing from
[
33
]
.
The results involving the Koopman-based formulation of the HJB equation and the associated iterative computation scheme based on the data-driven approximation of the Koopman operator are also new to this paper.

IV-D

Local Optimal Controller

The density function
\rho
for the solution of optimization problem satisfy following integral formula

\rho({\mathbf{x}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mathbb{P}^{c}_{t}h_{0}]({\mathbf{x}})dt

where
\mathbb{P}_{t}^{c}
is the P-F operator for the closed-loop system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+{\bf g}({\mathbf{x}}){k}({\mathbf{x}})+{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
and hence
\rho
serves as an occupancy measure i.e.,
\int_{A}\rho({\mathbf{x}})d{\mathbf{x}}=\left<\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mathbb{U}_{t}\chi_{A}]dt,h\right>
signifies the amount of time closed-loop system trajectories spend in the set
A
with initial condition supported w.r.t. measure
\mu_{0}
. Because of this,
\rho({\mathbf{x}})
has singularity at the equilibrium point stabilized by the closed-loop system as all the trajectories are funnel through the neighborhood of the origin to the origin. Due to this singularity at the origin, we need to exclude the small neighborhood around the origin for the proper parameterization of the density function
\rho
in the computation of optimal control. In particular, the optimization problem (
24
) is solved excluding the small neighborhood around the origin. The local optimal control is obtained based on the linearization of nonlinear dynamics at the origin. From this local control we determine a local Lyapunov function
{\mathbf{P}}
and define a local density function as

\displaystyle\rho_{L}({\mathbf{x}})=\text{max}\{({\mathbf{x}}^{T}{\mathbf{P}}{\mathbf{x}})^{-3}-\gamma,0\},

(37)

which is used for applying general control strategy. We combine the local and global controllers through the blending procedure as follows:

\bar{u}({\mathbf{x}})=\frac{\rho_{L}({\mathbf{x}})}{\rho_{L}({\mathbf{x}})+\rho({\mathbf{x}})}u_{\ell}({\mathbf{x}})+\frac{\rho({\mathbf{x}})}{\rho_{L}({\mathbf{x}})+\rho({\mathbf{x}})}u({\mathbf{x}}).

(38)

Notice that with blending control, we can get a smooth control on the whole workspace.

IV-E

Stochastic OCP Using Koopman Operator

In this section, we show how the Koopman operator can be used to develop optimal control results. The results involving the Koopman operator can be viewed as a dual to P-F-based approach for SOCP discussed in the previous section. We show that the HJB equation can be viewed from the perspective of Koopman operator theory. The results from this section will be used to develop data-driven optimal control based on the approximation of the Koopman operator. Consider the following optimal control problem

V_{\ell}
, is paramaterized and approximated from time-series data

{\cal A}_{K}^{{\bf f}+{\mathbf{g}}k_{k}}

The spectrum of the Koopman operator carries essential information about the system dynamics. Our proposed Koopman-based perspective to the HJB equation will enable the discovery of a spectral-based method for optimal control.

V
Data-driven Approximation of Optimal Control

This section will discuss a linear operator-based numerical scheme for the data-driven approximation of optimal control. The numerical scheme for data-driven optimal control will rely on the P-F-based convex formulation to the OCP as discussed in (
24
) and Koopman-based value iteration as given in Eqs. (
47
)-(
48
). We start with the finite-dimensional approximation of the P-F and Koopman operators for stochastic control systems and then utilize these approximations to design optimal control and stabilizing feedback control.

V-A

Approximation of P-F Operator

For the finite dimensional approximation of the P-F based convex formulation of the OCP problem in Eq. (
24
), we need to approximate the P-F generator corresponding to dynamical systems

\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
and
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})
using the time series data generated from controlled stochastic system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})u+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
.

For the approximation of P-F generator for
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
we use control input
u=0
.
Let
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{y}}_{i}^{\ell},u_{i}=0\}
be the two consecutive snapshots obtained by simulating the system (
1
) using Euler-Maruyama discretization
[
50
]
. In particular, we have

\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\ell}={\mathbf{x}}_{i}+\Delta t{\bf f}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})\xi^{\ell}

(49)

where
i=1,\ldots,N
are the number of initial conditions assume to be uniformly distributed in the state space and
\ell=1,\ldots,R
are the number of realization. For the finite dimensional approximation, let
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}:{\mathbf{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{K}
be the vector valued basis functions. We make following assumption on the choice of basis functions.

Assumption 7
.

We assume that the basis functions,
\psi_{k}({\mathbf{x}})\in{\cal L}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
k=1,\ldots,K
are non-negative and linearly independent. We denote the set of basis functions as

{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}})=[\psi_{1}({\mathbf{x}}),\ldots,\psi_{K}({\mathbf{x}})]^{\top}.

Our objective is to construct the projection of the infinite-dimensional P-F operator on the finite-dimensional subspace spanned by
\{\psi_{k}({\mathbf{x}})\}_{k=1}^{K}
. The projection should preserve the positivity and Markov properties of the P-F operator.
Writing

\phi({\mathbf{x}})={\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}{\bf a},\;\;\;\hat{\phi}({\mathbf{x}})={\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}\hat{\bf a}

The action of the Koopman operator (
5
) on the finite dimensional basis function can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})={\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})\hat{\bf a}=[{\mathbb{U}}_{t}\phi]({\mathbf{x}}_{i})+r

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{x}}[\phi({\mathbf{X}}_{\Delta t}^{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}})]+r\approx\frac{1}{R}\sum_{\ell=1}^{R}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}({\mathbf{y}}_{i}^{\ell})^{\top}{\bf a}+r

(50)

where
r
is the residual term and the objective is to minimize the residual term. We have approximated the expectation above using multiple,
R
, realization of the system trajectories.
For the approximation of the P-F operator preserving the positivity and Markov property, Naturally Structured Dynamic Mode Decomposition (NSDMD) algorithm is proposed in
[
51
]
. The NSDMD algorithm can be viewed as the generaization of the popular Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD) algorithm to incorporate the positivity and Markov constraints. Pre-multiplying (
50
) with
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})
and summing over the initial conditions and the different realization to construct following matrices

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}:=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}){\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}:=\frac{1}{MR}\sum_{i,\ell}^{N,R}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}){\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}({\mathbf{y}}_{i}^{\ell}).

(51)

The
{\mathbf{G}}
and
{\mathbf{A}}
matrices are used to formulate a least square problem for the minimization of residual term
r
as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{K}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}}\parallel{\mathbf{G}}{\mathbf{K}}_{0}-{\mathbf{A}}\parallel_{F}

(52)

s.t.

\displaystyle[{\bf{\Lambda}{\bf K}_{0}\bf{\Lambda}^{-1}}]_{ij}\geq 0,\;\;\;\bf{\Lambda}{\bf K}_{0}\bf{\Lambda}^{-1}\mathds{1}=\mathds{1}

(53)

where
\mathds{1}
is a vector of all ones and
{\bf\Lambda}=\int{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}}){\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}({\mathbf{x}})d{\mathbf{x}}
Note that the
\bf\Lambda
matrix can be computed analytically as integral can be computed explicitly for Gaussian basis functions. The constraints are imposed to ensure the positivity and Markov property of the linear operator. Using the duality relation between the P-F and Koopman operators (
13
), the P-F operator can be obtained as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{\Delta t}\approx{\mathbf{P}}_{0}=\Lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{K}}_{0}^{\top}\Lambda

(54)

In practice, we implement following numerical efficient optimization problem which is equivalent to (
52
)-(
53
) to compute the P-F operator.

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\hat{\bf P}_{0}}\parallel\hat{\bf G}{\hat{\bf P}_{0}}-\hat{\bf A}\parallel_{F}

(55)

s.t.

\displaystyle[\hat{\bf P}_{0}]_{ij}\geq 0,\;\;\;\hat{\bf P}_{0}\mathds{1}=\mathds{1},

where,

\displaystyle\hat{\bf G}={\bf G}{\bf\Lambda}^{-1},\;\;\hat{\bf A}={\bf A}{\bf\Lambda}^{-1},

(56)

where
\bf G
and
\bf A
are as defined in (
51
). The P-F operator is obtained from
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}_{0}=\hat{\mathbf{P}}_{0}^{\top}.

(57)

Similarly, the infinitesimal generator for the P-F operator is then approximated as

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{{\bf f}+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}}_{PF}\approx\frac{{\mathbf{P}}_{0}-I}{\Delta t}=:{\mathbf{M}}_{0}.

(58)

Remark 8
.

The EDMD algorithm will arise as a special case of the NSDMD algorithm (
52
)-(
53
) where the constraints (
53
) in the optimization problem are removed. Similarly, dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) will arise as the special case of the EDMD when the basis function
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}})
are chosen to be identity basis functions i.e.,
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{x}}
.

The problem of convergence of EDMD algorithm for deterministic and stochastic systems is studied in
[
52
,
53
]
. The convergence results are asymptotic and guarantee the convergence of the infinite-dimensional Koopman operator in the limit as the data samples, and the number of basis go to infinity. These results will apply to the NSDMD algorithm as well as these operators are naturally positive and preserve Markov property.

For the approximation of the P-F generator corresponding to control vector field i.e.,
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})
we use control data set. Let
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{y}}_{i}^{\ell},{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\}
be the control data set obtained by simulating the control system (
17
) from different initial condition for
i=1,\ldots,N
and
\ell=1,\ldots,R
and
{\mathbf{u}}_{i}
is assumed to be the step input. In particular, we have

\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\ell}={\mathbf{x}}_{i}+\Delta t{\bf f}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})\xi^{\ell}+\Delta t{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})

(59)

With step input this data essentially correspond to be generated by stochastic system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi+{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})
.
Using exactly the same procedure outlined in previous section we construct the approximation of P-F generator corresponding to the above dynamical system. Following the notation convention from (
58
), let

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{PF}^{{\bf f}+\sigma{{\mathbf{n}}}+{\mathbf{g}}}\approx{\mathbf{M}}_{1}.

(60)

We now use linearity property of the P-F generator to extract the P-F generator corresponding to the dynamical system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{PF}^{{\mathbf{g}}}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{g}}\rho)

(61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[-\nabla\cdot(({\bf f}+{\mathbf{g}})\rho)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}[({\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{n}}^{\top})_{ij}\rho]}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}\right]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left[-\nabla\cdot({\bf f}\rho)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}[({\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{n}}^{\top})_{ij}\rho]}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[{\cal A}_{PF}^{{\bf f}+\sigma{{\mathbf{n}}}+{\mathbf{g}}}-{\cal A}_{PF}^{{\bf f}+\sigma{{\mathbf{n}}}}\right]\rho.

Hence, the P-F generator for
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})
is approximated as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{PF}^{{\mathbf{g}}}\approx{\mathbf{M}}_{1}-{\mathbf{M}}_{0}.

(62)

The finite-dimensional approximation of the generator corresponding to the control vector field and uncontrolled vector field, namely
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
and
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}
, will be used to approximate the optimization problem. We discuss this in subsection
V-C
.

V-B

Approximation of the Koopman operator

For the finite dimensional approximation of the Koopman-based policy interation algorithm in Eqs. (
47
)-(
48
), we need to approximate the Koopman generator corresponding to the control vector field
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})
and uncontrolled vector field
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
using the time-series data generated by
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})u+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
. The procedure for this approximation follows exactly along the lines of approximation procedure for the P-F operator as discussed in the previous subsection. The only difference is that we used EDMD algorithm for the approximation (refer to Remark
8
). Following (
58
) and (
60
), let
{\mathbf{K}}_{0},{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
and
{\mathbf{K}}_{1},{\mathbf{L}}_{1}
be the approximation of the Koopman operators and generators corresponding to the uncontrolled,
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
vector field and control vector field with unit step input,
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})\sigma+{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
respectively i.e.,

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{K}^{{\bf f}+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}}\approx\frac{{\mathbf{K}}_{0}-I}{\Delta t}=:{\mathbf{L}}_{0},\;\;{\cal A}_{K}^{{\bf f}+{\mathbf{g}}+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}}\approx\frac{{\mathbf{K}}_{1}-I}{\Delta t}=:{\mathbf{L}}_{1}

(63)

Again using the linearity property of the Koopman generator w.r.t. the vector field, we obtained following approximation for the Koopman generator corresponding the control vector field

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{K}^{\mathbf{g}}\approx{\mathbf{L}}_{1}-{\mathbf{L}}_{0}.

(64)

Similarly, time series data from the feedback control system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})k_{k}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
is used for the approximation of the Koopman operator and generator at the
k^{th}
step of the policy iteration. We denote by
{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}
and
{\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{k}
the Koopman operator and generator for the feedback control system

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{K}^{{\bf f}+{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})k_{k}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})}\approx\frac{{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}-I}{\Delta t}=:{\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{k}

(65)

Remark 9
.

In the above construction, we identified the P-F and Koopman generators for the drift vector field and the control vector field using zero and step input. However, it is possible to used arbitrary control inputs for the identification of the two generators. The identification process will correspond to the bilinear lifting of control dynamical system.

V-C

Data-driven Optimal Control: P-F-based Convex Approach

Let
\rho({\mathbf{x}})
,
\bar{\rho}({\mathbf{x}})
, and
h_{0}
be expressed in terms of the Gaussian RBF as

\displaystyle\rho({\mathbf{x}})\approx{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf v},\;\bar{\rho}({\mathbf{x}})\approx{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf w},\;h({\mathbf{x}})={\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf m}

(66)

With the above representation we have following approximation

\nabla\cdot({\bf f}\rho)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}[({\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{n}}^{\top})_{ij}\rho]}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}\approx-\Psi^{\top}({\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{M}}_{0}{\bf v}

\nabla\cdot({\mathbf{g}}\bar{\boldsymbol{\rho}})\approx-\Psi^{\top}({\mathbf{x}})({\mathbf{M}}_{1}-{\mathbf{M}}_{0}){\bf w}

We now proceed with the approximation of the cost function.

\int_{{\mathbf{S}}}q({\mathbf{x}})\rho({\mathbf{x}})d{\mathbf{x}}\approx\int_{\mathbf{X}}q({\mathbf{x}}){\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}d{\mathbf{x}}{\bf v}={\bf d}^{\top}{\bf v}

where the vector
{\bf d}:=\int_{X}q({\mathbf{x}}){\boldsymbol{\Psi}}d{\mathbf{x}}
can be pre-computed. For the approximation of the control cost we make following assumption.
The infinite dimensional optimization problem in terms of the basis function is then written as

\displaystyle\min_{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf v}\geq 0,{\bf w},\kappa>0}{\bf d}^{\top}{\bf v}+r\frac{{\bf w}^{\top}{\bf D}{\bf w}}{{\kappa}}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}\;-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{0}{\bf v}+({\mathbf{M}}_{1}-{\mathbf{M}}_{0}){\bf w}\right)={\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}{\bf m}

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}({\mathbf{x}}){\mathbf{v}}\leq\kappa

where
{\bf D}:=\int_{\mathbf{S}}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}d{\mathbf{x}}
. Since the basis function
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
is known, the matrix
\bf D
can be evaluated explicitly or computed from data as
{\bf D}\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}){\boldsymbol{\Psi}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k})^{\top}
Now using the fact that the basis functions are non-negative and are linearly independent with
s=\|\psi_{k}\|_{\infty}
for
k=1,\ldots,K
, we write the finite dimensional approximation of the optimization problem as

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf v}\geq 0,{\bf w},\kappa>0}{\bf d}^{\top}{\bf v}+\frac{{\bf w}^{\top}{\bf D}{\bf w}}{\kappa}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}\;-\left({\mathbf{M}}_{0}{\bf v}+{\mathbf{M}}_{1}{\bf w}\right)={\bf m},\;\;s\mathds{1}^{\top}{\mathbf{v}}\leq\kappa

(67)

where
\mathds{1}
is a vector of all ones.
The optimal control is then approximated as
u=\frac{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf w}}{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf v}}
.

V-D

Data-driven Optimal Control: Koopman-based Policy Iteration

For the data-driven Koopman-based policy iteration algorithm we proceed as follows. Let

{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}})=[\phi_{1}({\mathbf{x}}),\ldots,\phi_{K}({\mathbf{x}})]^{\top}

be the basis function assumed to be linearly independent. Unlike P-F-based computation, we do not require
\phi_{k}
to be non-negative. For the Koopman-based policy iteration we use polynomial basis functions.
Let,
q({\mathbf{x}}),k_{k}({\mathbf{x}})
, and
V_{k}({\mathbf{x}})
be the state cost function, the feedback control input, and value function at the
k^{th}
step of the value iteration and are approximated as follows:

\displaystyle(q({\mathbf{x}})+rk_{k}^{2}({\mathbf{x}}))\approx{\mathbf{b}}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}}),\;\;\;V_{k}({\mathbf{x}})\approx{\mathbf{W}}_{k}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}}).

(68)

The approximation of
q+rk_{k}^{2}
is obtained by solving a least square problem. Let
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\}_{t=0}^{N}
be the fixed data set uniformly distributed over the state space. Let

\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=[{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}),\ldots,{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}}_{N})]^{\top},\;\;\bar{\mathbf{q}}=[q({\mathbf{x}}_{0}),q({\mathbf{x}}_{1}),\ldots,q({\mathbf{x}}_{N})]^{\top}

and
\bar{\mathbf{k}}=[k_{k}^{2}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}),\ldots,k_{k}^{2}({\mathbf{x}}_{N})]^{\top}
. Following least square problem is solve to determine
{\mathbf{b}}
,

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{b}}}\|\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathbf{b}}-(\bar{\mathbf{q}}+r\bar{\mathbf{k}})\|

(69)

which admits analytical solution as follows.

{\mathbf{b}}=\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{\dagger}(\bar{\mathbf{q}}+r\bar{\mathbf{k}})

where
\dagger
denotes pseudo-inverse.
Using (
65
) and (
68
), we write the finite dimensional approximation of (
47
) as

{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}{\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{k}{\mathbf{W}}_{k}=-{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}{\mathbf{b}}.

(70)

Since the
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
are basis functions assumed to be independent, (
47
) can be written as

{\mathbf{L}}_{c}^{k}{\mathbf{W}}_{k}=-{\mathbf{b}}.

Using (
65
) the above equation can be written in terms of the Koopman operator as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{I-{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}}{\Delta t}\right){\mathbf{W}}_{k}=-{\mathbf{b}}.

(71)

There are several ways to solve for the coefficient vector
{\mathbf{W}}_{k}
. In particular,
{\mathbf{W}}_{k}
can be obtained as

{\mathbf{W}}_{k}=\Delta t(I-{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k})^{-1}{\mathbf{b}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Delta t\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}\right]^{\ell}{\mathbf{b}}

where
\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}\right]^{\ell}
stands for the
\ell^{th}
power of Koopman operator at the
{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}
obtained at the
k^{th}
step of the policy iteration. Alternatively,
{\mathbf{W}}_{k}
can also be obtained from the least square solution. For the above formula to work we require that spectrum of the Koopman operator is strictly less than one. This is equivalent to stability condition for the feedback control system expressed in terms of the linear Koopman operator
[
47
]
. However, from numerical standpoint it is desirable to compute
{\mathbf{W}}_{k}
in terms of the finite power series of
{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}
i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}_{k}=\Delta t\left(I+{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}+\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}\right]^{2}+\ldots+\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{c}^{k}\right]^{M}\right){\mathbf{b}}

(72)

for some finite
M
which forms an another tuning parameter and could be problem specific. In this paper, we choose
M=15n
where
n
is the dimension of the state space.
Using (
64
) the approximation to the policy update Eq. (
48
) for can be written as

k_{k+1}({\mathbf{x}})=-\frac{r^{-1}}{2}{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}({\mathbf{x}})^{\top}({\mathbf{L}}_{1}-{\mathbf{L}}_{0}){\mathbf{W}}_{k}.

(73)

Remark 10
.

There could be different variants of the above described basic algorithm for the realization of Koopman-based policy iteration algorithm. For example in the above described algorithm we are identifying the Koopman generator for the closed loop system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})k_{\ell}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
at the
\ell^{th}
step of the policy iteration. Instead one could use the linearity of the generator w.r.t. vector field to identify the generator corresponding to only part of vector field that is changing with the iteration i.e.,
{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}})k_{\ell}({\mathbf{x}})
.

V-E

Data-driven Design of Local Stabilizing Control

For the design of local optimal control we employ DMD algorithm for the identification of local system dynamics in the form of
{\mathbf{A}}
and
\bf b
matrices. In particular, zero and step input data are used for the simultaneous identification of the
{\mathbf{A}}
and
{\bf b}
matrices as the solution of following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}}\parallel{\mathbf{Y}}-{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{X}}-{\bf b}{\bf\mathds{1}}\parallel_{F}

(74)

where,
{\mathbf{X}}=[{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{t},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{N}]
and
{\mathbf{Y}}=[{\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{N+1}]
are the collection of time series data generated with step input
u=1
with
\mathds{1}=[1,\ldots,1]
. These
{\mathbf{A}}
and
{\mathbf{B}}
matrices are employed in the design of local optimal controller in discrete-time setting using MATLAB command
lqrd
. The cost function is assumed to be of the form
{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}{\bf Q}{\mathbf{x}}+ru^{2}
, where
{\bf Q}=\frac{\partial^{2}q(0)}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}^{2}}
(assuming the origin to be the equilibrium point). The local optimal controller is active only within the small neighborhood of the equilibrium point.

V-F

Model-based Approximation of Stochastic Optimal Control

The model-based approximation of optimal control will be simpler form of the proposed data-driven approximation. In particular, for the model-based approximation we assume that we have access to the system dynamics. Hence, time-series data from the system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf f}({\mathbf{x}})+\sigma{\mathbf{n}}({\mathbf{x}})\xi
and control vector field
\dot{\mathbf{x}}={\bf g}({\mathbf{x}})
can be used to obtained the approximation of the generators directly instead of using (
62
) and (
64
) to derive the generators for control vector field.

V-G

Stochastic Stabilization

The data-driven stochastic stabilization will arise as the special case of the stochastic optimal control problem. In particular, following
[
41
]
, the condition for feedback stabilization can be written as

\nabla\cdot({\bf f}\rho+{\bf g}\bar{\boldsymbol{\rho}}))-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}[({\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{n}}^{\top})_{ij}\rho]}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\partial{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}>0

(75)

We have introduced new variable
\bar{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\mathbf{k}}\rho
. Above inequality is linear in terms of variables
(\rho,\bar{\boldsymbol{\rho}})
and is solved for these variables. The control input
{\mathbf{k}}({\mathbf{x}})
is then obtained as
{\mathbf{k}}({\mathbf{x}})=\frac{\bar{\boldsymbol{\rho}}({\mathbf{x}})}{\rho({\mathbf{x}})}
. Following discussion from the previous section, we notice that the finite-dimensional approximation of the stabilization constraints can be written as following feasibility problem

\displaystyle-\left({\mathbf{M}}_{0}{\bf v}+{\mathbf{M}}_{1}{\bf w}\right)>0,\;\;\;{\bf v}\geq 0.

(76)

where the positivity is component-wise.
The stabilizing controller is then recovered as
u({\mathbf{x}})=\frac{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf w}}{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\top}{\bf v}}
.

VII
Conclusion

The duality in the SOCP is discovered through the lenses of linear transfer operator theory involving Koopman and P-F operators. Our first main result provides a convex formulation of the SOCP using the P-F-based lifting of nonlinear system dynamics. Our second main result establishes a connection between the Koopman operator and the HJB equation. This connection allows us to develop a new algorithm for the data-driven approximation of stochastic optimal control, called Koopman-based policy iteration (KPI).
A computation framework based on the finite-dimensional approximation of the P-F and Koopman operators is developed to compute stochastic optimal control. Simulation results are presented to demonstrate the application of the developed theoretical and computational framework.

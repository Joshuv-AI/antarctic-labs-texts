Four-loop QCD MOM beta functions from the three-loop vertices at the symmetric point
Alexander Bednyakov, Andrey Pikelner

Abstract
For the first time, we compute three-loop contributions to all triple vertices in QCD at the symmetric point. The analytic results are obtained in massless QCD with an arbitrary color group in the Landau gauge. All new loop integrals are expressed in terms of harmonic polylogarithms at the sixth root of unity. These corrections allow us to derive expressions for the four-loop QCD beta function in a set of momentum-subtraction schemes.



I
Introduction

The strong coupling constant
\alpha_{s}\equiv g_{s}^{2}/(4\pi)
is a fundamental
parameter of QCD. It enters the predictions of many Standard Model (SM)
observables, e.g., the Higgs production cross-section
Anastasiou et al. 2015
at the LHC,
and
the uncertainty of

\alpha_{s}
significantly influences the comparison between theory and experiment.
In itself,
\alpha_{s}
is not a physical observable,
its value depends on the scale
Q^{2}
and the utilized renormalization prescription (or scheme).
The freedom in choosing a normalization point
manifests itself in “running” governed by the renormalization group (RG) equations

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha_{s}(Q^{2})}{d\ln Q^{2}}=\beta(\alpha_{s}(Q^{2})).

(1)

Given the renormalization scheme (RS), one can calculate the
\beta
-function
order by order in perturbation theory (PT).
To parametrize the strength of strong interactions,
one usually uses the
{\rm\overline{MS}}
scheme and quotes the value of
\alpha_{s}^{(5)}(M_{Z})=0.1179(10)

Tanabashi et al. 2018
defined
in effective QCD with
n_{f}=5
massless flavors and evaluated at the
Z
-boson mass.
This definition is of great convenience when studying inclusive
observables dominated by short-distance effects.
For almost 20 years the four-loop

\beta^{\rm\overline{MS}}

van Ritbergen et al. 1997
;
Czakon 2005
in the
{\rm\overline{MS}}
scheme has been utilized in state-of-the-art QCD calculations, and only recently the five-loop result
Baikov et al. 2017
;
Luthe et al. 2017
;
Herzog et al. 2017a
;
Chetyrkin et al. 2017
became available in the literature. The latter
allows one to significantly improve the agreement between
\alpha_{s}
determinations (see, e.g.,
Ref.
Tanabashi et al. 2018
for details) in precision measurements carried out at different energy scales.

At lower energies other
\alpha_{s}
definitions can be more convenient.
For example, in lattice QCD one can introduce the strong coupling as a particular RG-invariant combination
(so-called
invariant
charge) of the
vertex and two-point Green functions evaluated at fixed external momenta in a fixed (usually Landau) gauge (see, e.g., Refs.
Boucaud et al. 2014
;
Zafeiropoulos et al. 2019
).
Since
lattice results
Aoki et al. 2019

give rise to
one of the most precise determinations of
\alpha_{s}^{(5)}(M_{Z})
, it is important to study the strong coupling and its running in the momentum-subtraction (MOM) schemes. In these RSs the vertex functions and propagators are normalized in such a way that at a certain kinematic point
there are no corrections beyond
the tree level.
As a consequence,
the renormalized couplings coincide with the corresponding invariant charges and can be directly compared to the nonperturbative lattice results.

Figure 1:
For symmetric point integrals
p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=q^{2}
and for auxiliary
integrals we use
p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2},q^{2}=x\,p_{1}^{2}

Contrary to the
{\rm\overline{MS}}
scheme, in the MOM scheme
one needs to know the Green
functions beyond the divergent terms.
Some choices of kinematics can make the calculation quite challenging
, especially of vertices.
In this paper, we consider three-point functions that depend on momenta
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
, and
q=p_{1}+p_{2}
(see Fig.
1
) and utilize the symmetric normalization point (SMOM) in the Euclidean region
p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=q^{2}=-Q^{2}
.
One-loop QCD renormalization in the SMOM scheme in a general linear gauge has
been known for quite a long time, following the pioneering work in Ref.
Celmaster and Gonsalves 1979
.
At the moment, only two-loop expressions for the QCD vertices in this kinematics
are available in the literature in numerical
Chetyrkin and Seidensticker 2000
and
analytical
Gracey 2011a
forms, allowing one to find three-loop

Chetyrkin and Seidensticker 2000
;
Gracey 2011b
strong-coupling beta functions in SMOM RS.
In our work, we exploit modern Feynman-integral evaluation methods
to improve these results by one more order of PT.
To simplify our calculation, we routinely use the Landau gauge, in which the gluon propagator is transversal and there is no need to consider the gauge-parameter renormalization.

In the context of perturbative QCD (pQCD) one can relate renormalized couplings
a_{\rm ren}\equiv\alpha^{\rm ren}_{s}/(4\pi)
defined in different RSs.
In what follows, we consider
a_{\rm ren}=\{a_{\rm ggg},a_{\rm ccg},a_{\rm qqg}\}
, which satisfy

\displaystyle\mu^{-2\varepsilon}a_{\rm bare}

\displaystyle=Z_{a_{\rm ren}}a_{\rm ren}=\left[\frac{Z_{\rm ggg}^{2}}{Z_{\rm gg}^{3}}\right]a_{\rm ggg}=\left[\frac{Z_{\rm ccg}^{2}}{Z_{\rm cc}^{2}Z_{\rm gg}}\right]a_{\rm ccg}=\left[\frac{Z_{\rm qqg}^{2}}{Z_{\rm qq}^{2}Z_{\rm gg}}\right]a_{\rm qqg},

(2)

with
a_{\rm bare}
being the bare coupling defined in the dimensionally regularized theory with
d=4-2\varepsilon
. The bare coupling is divergent and is related to the renormalized ones
a_{\rm ren}
via divergent factors
Z_{a_{\rm ren}}
. The latter are combinations of the renormalization constants of the
three-gluon (
Z_{\rm ggg}
), ghost-gluon (
Z_{\rm ccg}
), and quark-gluon (
Z_{\rm qqg}
) vertices together
with those of the gluon (
Z_{\rm gg}
), ghost (
Z_{\rm cc}
) and quark (
Z_{\rm qq}
) fields.

The vertex renormalization constants denoted collectively by
Z_{V}
are obtained order by order in perturbation theory from the bare
\Gamma^{V}_{\rm bare}
vertices by making sure that the renormalized counterpart
\Gamma^{V}_{\rm ren}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{V}_{\rm ren}(q_{i},a_{\rm ren})=Z_{V}(a_{\rm ren})\Gamma^{V}_{\rm bare}(q_{i},a_{\rm bare}),\quad\mu^{-2\varepsilon}a_{\rm bare}=Z_{a_{\rm ren}}a_{\rm ren}

(3)

is finite for all external momenta
q_{i}
and satisfies certain normalization conditions.

In the
{\rm\overline{MS}}
scheme only divergent terms are subtracted, and one can prove that
a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}=a_{\rm ggg}^{\rm\overline{MS}}=a_{\rm ccg}^{\rm\overline{MS}}=a_{\rm qqg}^{\rm\overline{MS}}
.
On the contrary, in the SMOM schemes one also subtracts finite terms and requires that there are no
\mathcal{O}(a_{\rm ren})
corrections to a particular
\Gamma^{V}_{\rm ren}
at the symmetric point,
characterized by momentum
Q^{2}
.
To avoid the appearance of
\log(Q^{2}/\mu^{2})
in the renormalization constants, it is convenient to choose
Q^{2}=\mu^{2}
.

The vertex functions of our interest have color and space-time indices and one can decompose them in terms of basis tensors with Lorentz-invariant coefficients (form-factors).
The choice of the basis is not unique and we make use of a
decomposition
Gracey 2011a
taht is valid for the symmetric point.
To save space, we present here only terms
Chetyrkin and Seidensticker 2000
that are relevant for the definition of the required vertex renormalization constants (
3
):

\displaystyle\Gamma^{abc}_{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=-ig_{s}f^{abc}\left(p^{\nu}_{1}g_{\nu\mu}\Gamma^{\rm ccg}(-\mu^{2})+\ldots\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{abc}_{\mu\nu\rho}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=ig_{s}f^{abc}\left(T_{\mu\nu\rho}\Gamma^{\rm ggg}(-\mu^{2})+\ldots\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{a}_{\mu,ij}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=g_{s}T^{a}_{ij}\left(\gamma_{\mu}\Gamma^{\rm qqg}(-\mu^{2})+\ldots\right).

(6)

Here all momenta are assumed to be outgoing,
p_{1}
in Eq.(
4
) corresponds to the antighost, and
T_{\mu\nu\rho}=g_{\mu\nu}(p_{1}-p_{2})_{\rho}+\ldots
represents the tensor that enters into the tree-level three-gluon vertex.
The SU(N) generators
T^{a}_{ij}
in the quark-gluon vertex (
6
) satisfy
[T^{a},T^{b}]_{ij}=if^{abc}T^{c}_{ij}
with structure constants
f^{abc}
.

The expressions for the bare form factors
\Gamma^{V}_{\rm bare}
with
V=\{{\rm ggg,~ccg,~qqg}\}
are extracted from the tensor vertices by means of the projectors given in Ref.
Gracey 2011a
.
To define
a_{\rm ggg}
in the respective SMOM scheme (MOMggg) via Eq. (
2
), we require that at the symmetric point
\Gamma^{\rm ggg}_{\rm ren}(-\mu^{2})=1
, i.e.
Z_{\rm ggg}^{-1}=\Gamma^{\rm ggg}_{\rm bare}(-\mu^{2})
.
In the same way one can relate the bare coupling
a_{\rm bare}
to
a_{\rm ccg}
or
a_{\rm qqg}
by requiring that either
\Gamma^{\rm ccg}_{\rm ren}(-\mu^{2})=1
(MOMh), or
\Gamma^{\rm qqg}_{\rm ren}(-\mu^{2})=1
(MOMq).
Since field renormalization constants also enter into Eq.(
2
), we have to impose conditions on
Z_{\rm gg}
,
Z_{\rm cc}
, and
Z_{\rm qq}
in the SMOM scheme: there should be no corrections to the corresponding tree-level propagator for the external momentum
q^{2}=-\mu^{2}
.

From Eq. (
2
) one can deduce that two renormalization prescriptions for
a_{\rm ren}
, say
a_{R}
and
a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}
, are related via finite correction factors
X_{R}
:

\displaystyle a_{R}

\displaystyle=\left(Z_{a_{R}}/Z_{a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}}\right)a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}\equiv a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}X_{R}=a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}\left[1+\sum_{l}X^{(l)}_{R}a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}^{l}\right].

(7)

Given
X_{R}
at
L
loops, one can determine the
(L+1)
MOM-scheme beta
functions from
\beta_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}
via a relation that is valid in the Landau gauge,

\displaystyle\beta_{R}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{da_{R}}{d\ln\mu^{2}}=\frac{\partial a_{R}(a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}})}{\partial a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}}\cdot\beta_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}(a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}),\qquad a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}=a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}(a_{R}),

(8)

where in the final step we invert Eq. (
7
) to express
\beta_{R}
in terms of
a_{R}
.
The main aim of this paper is to calculate the three-loop corrections

X^{(3)}_{R}
to the relations (
7
) between
a_{\rm ggg}
,
a_{\rm ccg}
,
a_{\rm qqg}
and
a_{{\rm\overline{MS}}}
.
As one of the applications of our result, we use the four-loop beta function in
the
{\rm\overline{MS}}
scheme
van Ritbergen et al. 1997
;
Czakon 2005

to find the corresponding beta functions
\beta_{\rm ggg}
,
\beta_{\rm ccg}
, and
\beta_{\rm qqg}
in the
considered SMOM schemes.

II
Details of the calculation

We generate Feynman diagrams
with
DIANA

Tentyukov and Fleischer 2000
, and obtain
8, 106, and 2382 graphs for the

\Gamma^{\rm ggg}_{\rm ren}

three-gluon vertex at one, two, and three loops, respectively.
Both
ghost-gluon
and
quark-gluon
vertices give rise to 2, 33, and 688 diagrams at the same loop levels.
After the application of projectors
Gracey 2011a
and taking fermion and
color traces
van Ritbergen et al. 1999
, we are left with scalar Feynman integrals,
which we reduce to a set of two one-loop, eight two-loop, and 51 three-loop master integrals by means of

Reduze 2
von Manteuffel and Studerus 2012
and
FIRE6
Smirnov and Chuharev 2019
.

The main challenge is calculating of the full set of three-loop three-point integrals in SMOM kinematics, which was not available in the literature.
To compute master integrals, we rely on the
linear reducibility
of
massless vertex-type integrals with arbitrary off-shell momenta, which is
proven to take place up to three-loop order
Chavez and Duhr 2012
;
Panzer 2014
.
Due to the latter property, for our integrals in
more restricted kinematics
we choose two
strategies of evaluation.
First of all, we
derive
a new basis of finite master integrals

von Manteuffel et al. 2015

and try to compute them directly by means of the
HyperInt
package
Panzer 2015
in terms of generalized polylogarithms (GPLs).
Unfortunately, we are unable to calculate the most complicated integrals and
instead use another
strategy based on the solution of the system of differential equations (DEs).
The method of DEs cannot be applied directly to the calculation of single scale integrals
we are interested in, and we construct a set of auxiliary integrals with arbitrary
external
q^{2}=x\,p_{1}^{2}
(Fig.
1
). After switching to a new variable
z
,

x=2-z-1/z
,
we reduce the original DE system
to the so called

\varepsilon
-form
Henn 2013
.
Due to the presence of singularities at complex points, we make use of the
epsilon

package
Prausa 2017
, which is capable of dealing with the latter.
The obtained system in the
\varepsilon
-form
is
easily solved
order by order in the
\varepsilon
expansion.
The solution is given by
linear combinations of
GPLs
G(a_{1},\dots,a_{n};z)
, where
a_{i}
correspond to different sixth roots of
unity,
with a number of unknown constants to be fixed from boundary conditions.
The latter can be
obtained
by matching the expansion in the limit
q^{2}\to 0
, corresponding to

z\to 1
, with the explicit result of
large-momentum asymptotic expansion.
Since
z=1
is a singular point of the DEs, naive Taylor expansion is not sufficient
to fix all of the constants
and we exploit
the
EXP
package
Harlander et al. 1998
;
Seidensticker 1999
to generate the series in terms of massless propagators. We compute the latter by the

MINCER
package
Gorishnii et al. 1989
;
Larin et al. 1991
keeping exact dependence
1
1

1

Available at

https://www.nikhef.nl/~form/maindir/packages/mincer/mincerex.tgz
.

on the space-time dimension variable.

In this way, we obtain the analytic results for auxiliary integrals depending on
z
.
Taking the limit
z\to e^{i\pi/3}
, which is regular and corresponds to SMOM kinematics,
we
compute the required single-scale master integrals.
The correctness of the analytic calculation is verified numerically using
pySecDec
Borowka et al. 2018
.
Substituting the integrals in the expressions for the three-loop
\Gamma^{V}_{\rm bare}
and expanding in
\varepsilon
up to the necessary order, we see that the maximal transcendental weight
in the final result is, as expected,
2L=6
with
L
being the loop order.
Using the basis and the reduction rules from Ref.
Henn et al. 2017
,
we are able to simplify the expressions for
\Gamma^{V}_{\rm bare}
significantly.
As a consequence, we obtain the SMOM renormalization constants, conversion
factors (
7
), and the beta functions (
8
) in a
rather compact form.

The obtained formulas can be used in a number of ways.
For example, one can improve the precision of matching between the lattice and pQCD results.
In addition, the possibility to switch from one RS to another in truncated PT
series for observables, or more generally RG-invariant quantities (see, e.g. Refs.
Gracey 2014
;
Herzog et al. 2017b
), provides us with an additional handle on
theoretical uncertainties beyond simple scale variation.
Moreover, the computed
integrals can also be used in SMOM calculations of more complicated three-point
Green functions with operator insertions, e.g., for studies of the light-quark masses as in Refs.
Almeida and Sturm 2010
;
Gracey 2011c
.

KP integrability of triple Hodge integrals. I. From Givental group to hierarchy symmetries
Alexander Alexandrov

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a relation between the Givental group of rank one and the Heisenberg-Virasoro symmetry group of the KP hierarchy. We prove, that only a two-parameter family of the Givental operators can be identified with elements of the Heisenberg-Virasoro symmetry group. This family describes triple Hodge integrals satisfying the Calabi-Yau condition. Using the identification of the elements of two groups we prove that the generating function of triple Hodge integrals satisfying the Calabi-Yau condition and its Θ \Theta -version are tau-functions of the KP hierarchy. This generalizes the result of Kazarian on KP integrability in the case of linear Hodge integrals.

Introduction

Since the works of ’t Hooft
[
35
]
and Brézin, Itzykson, Parisi, and Zuber
[
12
]
, it is known that the matrix models provide an efficient tool for the investigation of enumerative geometry problems. Recent remarkable progress in enumerative geometry/matrix model theory, in particular, is related to the Chekhov-Eynard-Orantin topological recursion
[
15
,
25
]
and Givental decomposition
[
31
,
32
]
. Both describe a non-trivial extension of the matrix model theory, which ranges far beyond the scope of the classical matrix integrals. Both topological recursion and Givental decomposition are naturally described in terms of summation over graphs, thus formally can be represented by infinite-dimensional Gaussian matrix integrals with integrands made of the Kontsevich-Witten tau-function and its generalizations.

While a relationship between these two methods is known
[
21
,
16
]
, their relation to other classical ingredients of the matrix model theory is not always clear. Usually, matrix models satisfy two different families of differential (or differential-difference) equations. One family, the
linear
equations, is given by the Virasoro or, more generally, W-constraints. For known examples, recursive solution of these equations naturally leads to topological recursion and Givental-type decomposition, see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
22
]
. Another family of equations is given by the
bilinear
Hirota equations of the KP/Toda type integrable hierarchies. While the Virasoro constraints constitute an inherent part of the Givental construction, the situation with integrability is much less clear. Many examples of the enumerative geometry generating functions, which can be described by topological recursion/Givental decomposition (TR/GD), are certain tau-functions of integrable hierarchies, however, a general relation between TR/GD and integrability is not known (see, however,
[
30
,
29
,
28
,
24
]
).

The main goal of this paper is to clarify the role of KP/Toda type integrability in the general scheme of TR/GD. It is important, in particular, because some elements of the integrable systems can be naturally identified with the main components of the TR/GD. For example, the quantum spectral curve, playing a central role in TR/GD construction, can be identified with a specific Kac-Schwarz operator, while the wave function, annihilated by this operator, is given by the Baker-Akhiezer function
[
6
,
10
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
]
.

In this paper, we address a question of the relation between the Givental group and the symmetry groups of the integrable hierarchies. These are different groups, acting on different spaces. However, sometimes it is possible to identify the elements of two groups up to a linear change of variables. An example of such identification was constructed in the work of Liu and Wang
[
46
]
. They showed that the Givental operator associated with the generating function of linear Hodge integrals is equal to an element of the Heisenberg-Virasoro subgroup of
GL(\infty)
up to a linear change of variables.

Therefore, a question arises: are there any other elements of the Givental group, which can be identified with the elements of the symmetry group of the integrable hierarchy? In this paper, we give a complete answer to this question for the rank one case of the Givental construction on one side and the KP integrable hierarchy on another. By
rank
here we mean the rank of the Frobenius manifold = the number of the KdV tau-functions in the Givental formula. The first important result of this paper, Theorem
1
, states that the infinite-dimensional Givental group contains only a two-dimensional family of such operators.

On the enumerative geometry side, this two-dimensional family is known to describe a very interesting generating function, namely, a generating function of triple Hodge integrals satisfying the Calabi-Yau condition. Hence, from the identification of the Givental and Heisenberg-Virasoro group elements it follows, that after a certain linear change of variables the generating function of triple Hodge integrals satisfying the Calabi-Yau condition becomes a tau-function of the KP hierarchy. Theorem
2
, which describes this KP tau-function, is the second important result of this paper. It generalizes the result of Kazarian for linear Hodge integrals
[
36
]
. Theorem
3
extends this KP integrability to the case with included Norbery’s
\Theta
-classes.

We restrict ourselfes to the simplest rank one case. However, we expect that the results of this paper can be immediately generalized to the higher rank Givental groups. This should lead to new interesting examples of integrable generating functions of the cohomological field theory. In the companion paper
[
11
]
we investigate a family of the tau-functions of the KP hierarchy, that generalizes the two-parametric family constructed in this paper and conjecturally describes interesting enumerative geometry invariants in the
r
-spin case. This family can be described by a deformation of the generalized Kontsevich model. The current project, in particular, was motivated by
[
42
]
, where a question about KP integrability of non-linear Hodge integrals and their description in terms of generalized Kontsevich model was raised.

It is known that enumerative geometry generating functions constitute only a small class of tau-functions of integrable hierarchies. We hope that the identification between the elements of the Givental group and symmetry groups of integrable hierarchies will help us to classify all such tau-functions.

The present paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we consider rank one Givental operators, which after a linear change of variables can be identified with the elements of the Heisenberg-Virasoro symmetry group of the KP hierarchy.
In Section
3
we identify this family with the generating functions of triple Hodge integrals, which proves the KP integrability of the latter.

2
Two groups of symmetries

In this section we consider two different infinite-dimensional groups acting on the bosonic Fock spaces. First of them, the Givental group of the quantized symplectic transformations is a group of symmetries of semi-simple cohomological field theory. Second is a Heisenberg-Virasoro subgroup of the
GL(\infty)
symmetry group of the KP hierarchy. We restrict the action of both groups to the space of functions of odd times. We prove, that on this space only a two-parametric family of the Givental operators can be identified, up to a linear change of variables, with the elements of the Heisenberg-Virasoro group.

2.1
Givental group

Let us consider the Givental quantization scheme for the rank one case. We basically follow the presentation of
[
32
,
40
,
19
]
. However, we consider a different normalization of the variables
T_{k}
, with an additional factor of
\hbar
. This change of normalization corresponds to the transition from the genus expansion of the generating function

\begin{array}[]{c}\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hbar^{2g-2}{\mathcal{F}}_{g,n}\right),\end{array}

(2.1)

where
g
is the genus,
n
is the number of the marked points, to the topological expansion

\begin{array}[]{c}\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hbar^{2g-2+n}{\mathcal{F}}_{g,n}\right),\end{array}

(2.2)

because the latter is more convenient for the KP hierarchy description.

Let
{H}={\hbox{{\alef C}}}[\![z,z^{-1}]\!]
be the space of formal Laurent series in an indeterminate
z
. We introduce the symplectic form on this space

\begin{array}[]{c}\Omega(f,g)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint f(-z)g(z)dz.\end{array}

(2.3)

For the natural decomposition

\begin{array}[]{c}{H}={H}_{+}\oplus{H}_{-},\end{array}

(2.4)

where
{H}_{+}={\rm span}\,_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb C}$}}\{1,z,z^{2},\dots\}
and
{H}_{-}={\rm span}\,_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb C}$}}\{z^{-1},z^{-2},z^{-3},\dots\}
, we introduce the Darboux coordinate system
\{p_{k},q_{m}\}
with

\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathcal{J}}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(q_{k}z^{k}+(-z)^{-k-1}p_{k}\right).\end{array}

(2.5)

After Givental, for any infinitesimal symplectic transformation
A
on
{H}
(that is, a transformation such that
\Omega(Af,g)+\Omega(f,Ag)=0
) we consider

\begin{array}[]{c}H_{A}:=\frac{1}{2}\Omega(A{\mathcal{J}},{\mathcal{J}}).\end{array}

(2.6)

This defines a Lie algebra isomorphism:

\begin{array}[]{c}H_{\left[A,B\right]}=\left\{H_{A},H_{B}\right\},\end{array}

(2.7)

where the Poisson bracket is given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\left\{H_{A},H_{B}\right\}:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\partial H_{A}}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial H_{B}}{\partial q_{i}}-\frac{\partial H_{B}}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial H_{A}}{\partial q_{i}}\right).\end{array}

(2.8)

Using the standard Weyl quantization we quantize these operators to order
\leq 2
linear differential operators

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{A}=\widehat{H}_{A}:=\frac{1}{2}{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}\Omega(A\widehat{\mathcal{J}},\widehat{\mathcal{J}}){\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}},\end{array}

(2.9)

where

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\tilde{T}_{k}z^{k}+(-z)^{-k-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}\right),\end{array}

(2.10)

and
{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}\dots{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}
denotes the standard bosonic normal ordering, which puts all
\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}
to the right of all
T_{k}
. The so-called
dilaton shift
is given by
\tilde{T}_{k}=T_{k}-\hbar^{-1}\delta_{k,1}
.
This gives us a central extension of the original algebra, with the commutator

\begin{array}[]{c}\left[\widehat{A},\widehat{B}\right]=\widehat{\left[A,B\right]}+\mathcal{C}(H_{A},H_{B}),\end{array}

(2.11)

where the so-called 2-cocycle term satisfies

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{C}\left(p_{i}p_{j},q_{k}q_{m}\right)=-\mathcal{C}\left(q_{k}q_{m},p_{i}p_{j}\right)=\delta_{i,k}\delta_{j,m}+\delta_{i,m}\delta_{j,k}\end{array}

(2.12)

and vanishes for all other elements
H_{A}
. For the finite symplectic transformations we define

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{e^{A}}:=e^{\widehat{A}}.\end{array}

(2.13)

Below we consider only the symplectic transformations of the form
R(z)=1+R_{1}z+R_{2}z^{2}+\dots\in 1+z{\hbox{{\alef C}}}[\![z]\!]
. They satisfy the symplectic condition

\begin{array}[]{c}R(z)R(-z)=1\end{array}

(2.14)

if and only if
\log R(z)
is odd function of
z
. Let us denote

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{W}_{k}:=\widehat{z^{2k-1}},\,\,\,\,k\in{\hbox{{\alef Z}}},\end{array}

(2.15)

then from (
2.9
) we have
[
32
,
40
]

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{W}_{k}=-\sum_{m}\tilde{T}_{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{m+2k-1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{-2k}(-1)^{l+1}\tilde{T}_{m}\tilde{T}_{-2k-m}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{2k-2}(-1)^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial T_{m}\partial T_{2k-m-2}}.\end{array}

(2.16)

These operators satisfy the commutation relations

\begin{array}[]{c}\left[\widehat{W}_{k},\widehat{W}_{m}\right]=-\frac{2k-1}{2}\delta_{k+m,1}.\end{array}

(2.17)

After quantization, using (
2.16
) we obtain

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{R}:=\exp\left(\widehat{\log R(z)}\right).\end{array}

(2.18)

Below we call the group of such operators the
Givental group
.

The Givental group acts of the space of cohomological field theories (CohFT) with flat unit. If we relax the flat unit condition, we can include translations of the
times, see an example in Section
3.2
. However, translations are natural symmetries of the KP hierarchy, so this enrichment is trivial from the point of view of the identification of operators of two groups.

Operators
\widehat{R}
can be factorized, namely, one can factor out the part, corresponding to the linear change of variables and the translation of variables. This factorization, except for simple extraction of the translation operator, is given by Proposition 7.3 in
[
32
]
. Here we briefly remind the reader this relation. Let us introduce a formal series in two variables

\begin{array}[]{c}V^{R}(z,w):=\frac{1-R(-w)R(-z)}{w+z}.\end{array}

(2.19)

The matrix of its coefficients

\begin{array}[]{c}V^{R}(z,w)=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}V^{R}_{kl}w^{k}z^{l}\end{array}

(2.20)

is symmetric,
V_{kl}^{R}=V_{lk}^{R}
, and its entries are polynomials in
R_{k}
. Let the linear change of the shifted variables from
{\bf T}
to
{\bf T^{R}}
be given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{T}^{R}_{k}z^{k}:=R(-z)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{T}_{k}z^{k}.\end{array}

(2.21)

This transformation generates the change of the dilaton shift

\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\delta_{k}z^{k}:=z(1-R(-z)).\end{array}

(2.22)

Then, for any element of the Givental group (
2.18
)
and any series
Z({\bf T})
we have
[
32
]

Lemma 2.1

(Givental)
.

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{R}\cdot Z({\bf T})=\,e^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}{V_{ij}^{R}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial T_{i}\partial T_{j}}}\,\left.e^{\hbar^{-1}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\delta_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}}\cdot Z({\bf T})\right|_{{T}_{k}\mapsto{T}^{R}_{k}}.\end{array}

(2.23)

In this expression one acts by the operator on the function
Z({\bf T})
, and then in the result substitutes
{T}_{k}
with
{T}^{R}_{k}
.

2.2
Heisenberg-Virasoro subgroup of
GL(\infty)

In this section we briefly describe the Heisenberg-Virasoro subgroup of
\GL(\infty)
symmetry group of KP hierarchy, for more details see the companion paper
[
11
]
and references therein. Let

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{J}(z):=\sum_{k\in\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}}\frac{\widehat{J}_{k}}{z^{k+1}},\end{array}

(2.24)

where

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{J}_{k}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{for}\quad k>0},\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle{0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{for}\quad k=0,\\[2.0pt]

or

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{L}_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a+b=-m}abt_{a}t_{b}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kt_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k+m}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a+b=m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{a}\partial t_{b}},\end{array}

2.10
) and (
2.9

{\mathcal{V}}
is generated by the operators
\widehat{J}_{k}
,
\widehat{L}_{k}
and a unit. Let us consider a Virasoro subgroup of
{\mathcal{V}}
given by the operators of the form

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{V}=\exp\left({\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}\widehat{L}_{k}}\right)\in{\mathcal{V}}.\end{array}

(2.28)

With any such operator one can associate the operator
e^{{\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}
,
where

\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathtt{l}}_{m}=-z^{m}\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{m+1}{2}\right)\end{array}

(2.29)

are the generators of the Witt subalgebra of the algebra of of diffeomorphisms on the circle.

For any set
a_{k}
,
k\in{\hbox{{\alef Z}}}_{>0}
, consider the series

\begin{array}[]{c}f(z):=e^{{\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}\,z\,e^{-{\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}\in z+z{\hbox{{\alef C}}}[\![z]\!].\end{array}

(2.30)

Then

\begin{array}[]{c}h(z):=e^{-{\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}\,z\,e^{{\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}\in z+z{\hbox{{\alef C}}}[\![z]\!]\end{array}

(2.31)

is an inverse series,
f(h(z))=h(f(z))=z
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between the space of series (
2.30
) and the subgroup (
2.28
) of the Virasoro group. From the commutation relations of the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra,

\displaystyle\left[\widehat{J}_{k},\widehat{J}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=k\delta_{k,-m},

\displaystyle\left[\widehat{L}_{k},\widehat{J}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=-m\widehat{J}_{k+m},

(2.32)

\displaystyle\left[\widehat{L}_{k},\widehat{L}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle=(k-m)\widehat{L}_{k+m}+\frac{1}{12}\delta_{k,-m}(k^{3}-k)

it follows that

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{V}\,\widehat{{J}}(z)\,\widehat{V}^{-1}=h^{\prime}(z)\widehat{{J}}(h(z)).\end{array}

(2.33)

Let
\widehat{V}_{0}
be a part of the element of the upper-triangular subgroup (
2.28
) that describes the linear change of variables,

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{V}_{0}:=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}mt_{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k+m}}\right).\end{array}

(2.34)

With the help of the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula operator
\widehat{V}
can be factorized

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{V}=\widehat{V}_{0}\,\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,m=1}^{\infty}v_{km}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{k}\partial t_{m}}\right)\end{array}

(2.35)

for some
v_{ij}\in{\hbox{{\alef C}}}[\![a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\dots]\!]
. Below we will describe the space of all

which can be obtained in this way.

Using the commutation relations of the Virasoro algebra we can always factor out a term
\exp(a_{1}\widehat{L}_{1})
,

\begin{array}[]{c}\exp\left({\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}\widehat{L}_{k}}\right)=\exp(a_{1}\widehat{L}_{1})\,\exp\left({\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>1}}\tilde{a}_{k}\widehat{L}_{k}}\right)\end{array}

for some new

is a first order differential operator, so

For the series (

\begin{array}[]{c}f(z)\mapsto f(z/(1+a_{1}z)).\end{array}

(2.37)

2.3
Quadratic part of the Virasoro group operators

To find the matrix
v_{km}
in (
2.35
) it is enough to act by both sides of (
2.35
) on the function

\begin{array}[]{c}E:=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kt_{k}q_{k}\right),\end{array}

(2.38)

where
{\bf q}
are auxiliary variables, independent on
{\bf t}
. Let us show that the coefficients
v_{nm}
in (
2.35
) are the so-called Grunsky coefficients of function
h(z)
.
Consider

\begin{array}[]{c}v(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=\sum_{k,m=1}^{\infty}v_{km}\eta_{1}^{k}\eta_{2}^{m}.\end{array}

(2.39)

Lemma 2.2
.

\begin{array}[]{c}v(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\log\left(\frac{h(\eta_{1})-h(\eta_{2})}{\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}}\right).\end{array}

(2.40)

Remark 2.1
.

This formula was known to the experts a long time ago, and can be proven via the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula. Here we provide its proof for completeness.

Proof.

On one hand, the action of the right hand side of (
2.35
) on
E
yields

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{V}\cdot E=\widehat{V}_{0}\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kt_{k}q_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,m=1}^{\infty}km\,v_{km}q_{k}q_{m}\right).\end{array}

(2.41)

On the other hand

\begin{split}\widehat{V}\cdot E&=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\left(\widehat{V}t_{k}\widehat{V}^{-1}\right)q_{k}\right)\cdot 1\\
&=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(kt_{k}\tilde{q}_{k}+\tilde{q}_{-k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\right)\right)\cdot 1,\end{split}

(2.42)

where from (
2.33
) we have

\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{q}_{k}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_{m}}{f(z)^{m}}\right)\frac{dz}{z^{k+1}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,k\in{\hbox{{\alef Z}}}.\end{array}

(2.43)

Hence

\begin{split}\widehat{V}\cdot E&=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(kt_{k}\tilde{q}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}k\tilde{q}_{k}\tilde{q}_{-k}\right)\right)\\
&=\widehat{V}_{0}\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(kt_{k}q_{k}+\frac{1}{2}k\tilde{q}_{k}\tilde{q}_{-k}\right)\right).\end{split}

(2.44)

Comparing this expression with (
2.41
) we conclude

\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{k,m=1}^{\infty}km\,\,v_{km}q_{k}q_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\tilde{q}_{k}\tilde{q}_{-k}.\end{array}

(2.45)

Therefore,

\begin{split}v_{km}&=\frac{1}{km}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{j}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{dz}{z^{j+1}f(z)^{k}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{w^{j-1}dw}{f(w)^{m}}\\
&=-\frac{1}{km}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{dz^{-j}}{f(z)^{k}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{dw^{j}}{f(w)^{m}}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{dz}{z^{k+1}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{dw}{w^{m+1}}\log\left(1-\frac{h(w)}{h(z)}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{dz}{z^{k+1}}\oint_{\infty}\frac{dw}{w^{m+1}}\log\left(\frac{h(z)-h(w)}{z-w}\right).\end{split}

(2.46)

Here in the transition from the second to the third line we use the change of variables from
z
and
w
to
f(z)
and
f(w)
. To derive the last line we use the positivity of
k
and
m
, and the function there is chosen in such way that it possess formal Taylor series expansion

\begin{array}[]{c}\log\left(\frac{h(z)-h(w)}{z-w}\right)\in{\hbox{{\alef C}}}[\![z,w]\!].\end{array}

(2.47)

This completes the proof.

2.4
Equivalence of Givental and Virasoro group elements

Let us compare the subgroup (
2.28
) of the Virasoro group and Givental group, given by Lemma

\begin{array}[]{c}T_{k}\equiv(2k+1)!!t_{2k+1}.\end{array}

Remark 2.3

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{L}_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kt_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k+m}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a+b=m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{a}\partial t_{b}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,m\geq 0,\end{array}

(2.110)

the linear change of variables

\begin{array}[]{c}T_{0}^{q,p}({\bf t})=t_{1},\\
T_{k}^{q,p}({\bf t})=\left(q\widehat{L}_{0}+\frac{2q+p}{\sqrt{p+q}}\widehat{L}_{-1}+\left(\widehat{L}_{-2}-\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{2}\right)\right)\cdot T_{k-1}^{q,p},\end{array}

(2.111)

and the coefficients
a_{k}
are given implicitly through the formal series

\begin{array}[]{c}f(z):=e^{{\sum_{k\in\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}\,z\,e^{-{\sum_{k\in\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}\in z+z{\hbox{{\alef C}}}[\![z]\!],\end{array}

(2.112)

which is defined by

\begin{array}[]{c}f(z)df(z)=\frac{zdz}{(1+\sqrt{p+q}z)(1+qz/\sqrt{p+q})},\end{array}

(2.113)

and a formal series

\begin{array}[]{c}R_{q,p}(z)=\exp\left({-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)}\left(p^{2k-1}+q^{2k-1}-\left(\frac{pq}{p+q}\right)^{2k-1}\right)z^{2k-1}}\right),\end{array}

(2.114)

which defines an element of the Givental group. Then

Theorem 1
.

Rank one Givental operator coincides up to a linear change of variables with the element of the Heisenberg-Virasoro group of symmetries of KP hierarchy if and only if
R(z)
belongs to a family (
2.114
). For this family

\begin{array}[]{c}\left.\widehat{R}_{q,p}\cdot Z({\bf t}^{o})\right|_{{\bf T}={\bf T}^{q,p}({\bf t})}=e^{\hbar^{-1}\sum_{k=4}v_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}}e^{\sum_{k\in\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}\widehat{L}_{k}}\cdot Z({\bf t}^{o})\end{array}

(2.115)

for any function
Z({\bf t}^{o})
.

Proof.

We have already identified the quadratic parts of two operators and linear changes of variables in Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
.
It remains to identify the translations of the variables. Let us show that for any
Z({\bf t}^{o})

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{V}_{0}^{-1}\left(\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}v_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\right)\widehat{V}_{0}\cdot Z({\bf t}^{o})=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\delta_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}Z({\bf t}^{o}),\end{array}

(2.116)

where
\delta_{k}
’s are given by (
2.22
). By definition,

\begin{split}e^{-{\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}\,\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}v_{k}z^{k}\,e^{{\sum_{\raise-1.0pt\hbox{$\mbox{\Bbbb Z}$}_{>0}}a_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}}}&=\int_{0}^{h(z)}(f(\eta)-y(\eta))dx(\eta)\\
&=\int_{0}^{z}(\eta-y(h(\eta)))\eta d\eta.\end{split}

(2.117)

Using integration by parts we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\int_{\gamma}d\eta(\eta-y(h(\eta)))\eta e^{-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2z}}&=z-\frac{z}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\int_{\gamma}dy(\eta)e^{-\frac{x(\eta)}{z}}\\
&=z(1-R(-z)).\end{split}

(2.118)

This completes the proof.
∎

Let us stress that this theorem essentially describes the equality of two operators, therefore the formula is true for any function
Z({\bf t}^{o})
for which both sides make sense, not only for the solutions of the KdV hierarchy.

Remark 2.4
.

The identification of the Givental and Heisenberg-Virasoro operators (
2.115
) for
p=0
was established in
[
46
]
.

It is natural to compare the higher rank Givental group with the symmetry group of the multicomponent KP hierarchy. However, for higher rank Givental groups there are other promising possibilities, and one can try to identify the Givental operators with the operators from the symmetry groups of other solitonic integrable hierarchies. In particular, for rank two, one can consider 2D Toda lattice and extended Toda lattice. It would be interesting to find a relation between our analysis and the vertex operator analysis of the bilinear Hirota equations considered in
[
29
,
30
]
for the ADE type singularities.

Remark 2.5
.

For any semisimple cohomological field theory one can derive the Virasoro constraints by conjugation of the constraints for the KdV tau-functions with the Givental group element. However, for the cases, when after a linear change of variables the generating function coincides with a tau-function of integrable hierarchy, these constraints gain additional properties. Namely, after a linear change of variables, they belong to the symmetry algebra of the hierarchy. Virasoro type constrains and their conjugation by the Givental operators can provide alternative way for analysis of the integrability and other interesting properties.

3
Hodge integrals

3.1
Triple Hodge integrals

Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
the moduli space of all compact Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
n
distinct marked points. Deligne and Mumford
[
18
]
defined a natural compactification
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
via stable curves with possible nodal singularities. The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a non-singular complex orbifold of dimension
3g-3+n
. It is empty unless the stability condition

\displaystyle 2g-2+n>0

(3.1)

is satisfied. We refer the reader to
[
18
,
34
]
for the basic theory.

In his seminal paper
[
49
]
, Witten initiated new directions in the study of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. For each marking index
i
consider the cotangent line bundle
{\hbox{{\alef L}}}_{i}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, whose fiber over a point
[\Sigma,z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is the complex cotangent space
T_{z_{i}}^{*}\Sigma
of
\Sigma
at
z_{i}
. Let
\psi_{i}\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\hbox{{\alef Q}})
denote the first Chern class of
{\hbox{{\alef L}}}_{i}
. We consider the intersection numbers

\displaystyle\left<\tau_{a_{1}}\tau_{a_{2}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}\right>_{g}:=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\psi_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}.

(3.2)

The integral on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) vanishes unless the stability condition (
3.1
) is satisfied, all
a_{i}
are non-negative integers, and the dimension constraint
3g-3+n=\sum a_{i}
holds true. Let
T_{i}
,
i\geq 0
, be formal variables and let

\begin{array}[]{c}\tau_{KW}:=\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2g-2+n}F_{g,n}\right),\end{array}

(3.3)

where

\begin{array}[]{c}F_{g,n}:=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\geq 0}\left<\tau_{a_{1}}\tau_{a_{2}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}\right>_{g}\frac{\prod T_{a_{i}}}{n!}.\end{array}

(3.4)

Witten’s conjecture
[
49
]
, proved by Kontsevich
[
38
]
, states that the partition function
\tau_{KW}
becomes a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy after the change of variables
T_{n}=(2n+1)!!t_{2n+1}
. Integrability immediately follows
[
37
]
from Kontsevich’s matrix integral representation, for more details see the companion paper
[
11
]
.

There are different interesting deformations of this tau-function. Let us consider the Hodge bundle
𝔼
, a rank
g
vector bundle over
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Let
{\rm ch}_{k}\in H^{2k}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
be the components of the Chern character of
𝔼
. According to Mumford, the even components of the Chern character of
𝔼
identically vanish. Then we consider

\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g,n}=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\geq 0}\frac{\prod T_{a_{i}}}{n!}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}{\rm ch}_{2k-1}}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\psi_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}.\end{array}

(3.5)

We introduce a generating function

\begin{array}[]{c}Z({\bf T};{\bf s})=\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2g-2+n}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g,n}\right).\end{array}

(3.6)

In general, this deformation does not preserve integrability, because it is easy to check that
Z({\bf T};{\bf s})
is not a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy in the variables
t_{k}
anymore. Investigation of the integrable properties of
Z({\bf t};{\bf s})
is one of the main goals of this paper.

From Mumford’s theorem
[
47
]
it follows that

\begin{array}[]{c}Z({\bf T};{\bf s})=\widehat{R}({\bf s})\cdot\tau_{KW}({\bf T}),\end{array}

(3.7)

where

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{R}({\bf s}):=\exp\left({\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!}s_{k}\widehat{W}_{k}}\right)\end{array}

(3.8)

and operators
\widehat{W}_{k}
are given by (
2.16
).

For the Hodge bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
let
\lambda_{i}=c_{i}({\hbox{{\alef E}}})
. Then

\begin{array}[]{c}\Lambda_{g}(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{g}u^{i}\lambda_{i}=e^{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(2m-2)!{\rm ch}_{2m-1}u^{2m-1}}\end{array}

(3.9)

satisfies
\Lambda_{g}(u)\Lambda_{g}(-u)=1
. Particular specifications of the parameters
{\bf s}
correspond to various families of Hodge integrals. The generating function of the
m
-linear Hodge integrals

\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Lambda_{g}(u_{1})\Lambda_{g}(u_{2})\dots\Lambda_{g}(u_{m})\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\psi_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}\end{array}

(3.10)

corresponds to the Miwa parametrization of
{\bf s}
variables

\begin{array}[]{c}s_{k}=(2k-2)!\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}^{2k-1}.\end{array}

(3.11)

In particular, the generating function of linear Hodge integrals corresponds to the case
s_{k}=(2k-2)!u^{2k-1}
. Form Givental’s theory it follows that a product of the
Hodge classes
\Lambda_{g}(u)
defines a CohFT with flat unit.

Remark 3.1
.

We see that it is natural to consider a Miwa-like parametrization of the
{\bf s}
variables. It might be an indication of the existence of the integrable structure in these variables, at least for certain values of
{\bf T}
variables.

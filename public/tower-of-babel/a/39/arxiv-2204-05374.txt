Time periodic motion of temperature driven compressible fluids
Eduard Feireisl, Piotr Gwiazda, Agnieszka Świerczewska-Gwiazda

Abstract
We consider the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system describing the motion of a compressible viscous fluid in a container with impermeable boundary subject to time periodic heating and under the action of a time periodic potential force. We show the existence of a time periodic weak solution for arbitrarily large physically admissible data.

Introduction

There are numerous examples of turbulent fluid motion excited by changes of the boundary temperature, among which the well studied problem of Rayleigh–Bénard convection, see see e.g. Davidson
[
8
]
.
Motivated by similar problems in astrophysics of gaseous stars, we consider a general compressible viscous possibly rotating fluid, occupying a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{d}
,
d=2,3
, driven
by periodic changes of boundary temperature. The relevant system of field equations for the standard variables: the mass density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
, the velocity
{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
, and the
(absolute) temperature
\vartheta=\vartheta(t,x)
reads:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\varrho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}+\varrho\nabla_{x}G,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})+\nabla_{x}{\bf q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u},

(1.3)

where
\mathbb{S}
is the viscous stress given by Newton’s rheological law

\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=\mu(\vartheta)\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\eta(\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},

(1.4)

and
{\bf q}
is the heat flux given by
Fourier’s law

{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)=-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta.

(1.5)

The momentum equation is augmented by the Coriolis force with the rotation constant vector
\boldsymbol{\omega}
, the associated centrifugal force as well as the gravitation and other possible inertial time-periodic forces are regrouped
in the potential
G
. The fluid occupies a bounded smooth domain

\Omega\subset R^{d}
,
d=2,3
endowed with the Dirichlet boundary conditions

\displaystyle{\bf u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

\displaystyle\vartheta|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}

(1.6)

The functions
\vartheta_{B}=\vartheta_{B}(t,x)
and
G=G(t,x)
are smooth and
T-
periodic in the time variable,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{B}(t+T,x)=\vartheta_{B}(t,x),

\displaystyle G(t+T,x)=G(t,x).

(1.7)

Hereafter, the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.6
) is referred to as
Navier–Stokes–Fourier system
.

Our goal is to show the existence of a time–periodic solution to problem (
1.1
)–(
1.7
). There is a substantial number of references, where such a result is proven under some smallness and smoothness
assumption on the data. Valli and Zajaczkowski
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
observe that the distance of two smooth global in time solutions decays in time for the system close to a stable equilibrium, and, as a by product, they deduce the existence of a time periodic solution. Similar ideas have been followed by many authors, see Březina and Kagei
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
, Jin and Yang
[
17
]
, Kagei and Oomachi
[
18
]
,
Kagei and Tsuda
[
19
]
, Tsuda
[
22
]
to name only a few. Turbulent fluid flows given by large forces out of equilibrium are mostly considered in the framework of
weak
solutions.
Based on the mathematical theory of compressible fluids developed by Lions
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
, the existence of large time periodic solutions for the simplified isentropic system was proved
in
[
9
]
for the isentropic pressure–density equation of state
p(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma}
,
\gamma\geq\frac{9}{5}
. The later development of the theory in
[
12
]
enabled to extend the result
to the case
\gamma>\frac{5}{3}
, see Cai and Tan
[
6
]
.

The situation is more delicate for the complete fluid systems including thermal effects. As a direct consequence of the Second law of thermodynamics, the existence of (forced) time periodic solutions
is ruled out for problems with purely conservative boundary conditions, see
[
13
]
. In
[
10
]
, the heat flux was controlled by means of a Robin type boundary condition

{\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}=d(\vartheta-\Theta_{0})\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,

(1.8)

with a given “mean” temperature
\Theta_{0}
. Accordingly, the internal energy is transferred out of the fluid domain in the high temperature regime and the time periodic motion is possible, see

[
10
, Theorem 1]
. Our goal is to show a similar result for the Dirichlet boundary conditions (
1.6
). Note that the problem is much more delicate than in
[
10
]
as the heat flux through the boundary is
a priori
not controlled.

Our approach is based on several rather new ideas that appeared only recently in the mathematical theory of open fluid systems.

•

The concept of
weak solution
for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system based on a combination of the entropy inequality and the ballistic energy balance developed in
[
7
]
.

•

Uniform bounds
and large time asymptotics of the weak solutions in the spirit of
[
14
]
.

•

An
approximation scheme
based on a penalization of the Dirichlet boundary conditions via (
1.8
).

The constitutive restrictions imposed on the equations of state as well as the transport coefficients are the same as in the existence theory
[
7
]
. In particular, the general equation of state
of real monoatomic gases proposed in
[

[

]
, and later revisited by

is needed. The price to pay is the potential form of the driving force

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2
Main result

Before stating the main result, we recall the form of the constitutive equations proposed in
[
11
, Chapters 1,2]
. To comply with the Second law of thermodynamics, we postulate the existence of
entropy

s
, related
to the internal energy
e
and the pressure
p
through Gibbs’ equation

\vartheta Ds(\varrho,\vartheta)=De(\varrho,\vartheta)+p(\varrho,\vartheta)D\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right).

(2.1)

2.1
Constitutive theory

Similarly to
[
11
, Chapters 1,2]
we consider the pressure equation of state in the form

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=p_{\rm m}(\varrho,\vartheta)+p_{\rm rad}(\vartheta),

where
p_{\rm m}
is the pressure of a general
monoatomic
gas related to the internal energy through

p_{\rm m}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{2}{3}\varrho e_{\rm m}(\varrho,\vartheta),

(2.2)

augmented by the radiation pressure

p_{\rm rad}(\vartheta)=\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4},\ a>0.

Similarly, the internal energy reads

e(\varrho,\vartheta)=e_{\rm m}(\varrho,\vartheta)+e_{\rm rad}(\varrho,\vartheta),\ e_{\rm rad}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{a}{\varrho}\vartheta^{4}.

Now, Gibbs’ equation (
2.1
) gives rise to a specific form of
p_{m}
,

p_{m}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

for a certain
P\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
.
Consequently,

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4},\ e(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\varrho}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\frac{a}{\varrho}\vartheta^{4},\ a>0.

(2.3)

In addition, we suppose

P(0)=0,\ P^{\prime}(Z)>0\ \mbox{for}\ Z\geq 0,\ 0<\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}\leq c\ \mbox{for}\ Z>0.

(2.4)

that may be seen as a direct consequence of
hypothesis of thermodynamic stability
, see
[
11
, Chapter 1]
, and Bechtel, Rooney, and Forrest
[
1
]
.
It follows that the function
Z\mapsto P(Z)/Z^{\frac{5}{3}}
is decreasing, and we suppose

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\frac{P(Z)}{Z^{\frac{5}{3}}}=p_{\infty}>0.

(2.5)

The associated entropy
takes the form

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\frac{4a}{3}\frac{\vartheta^{3}}{\varrho},

(2.6)

where

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Z)=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z^{2}}<0.

(2.7)

Finally, we impose the Third law of thermodynamics, see e.g. Belgiorno
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, requiring the total entropy to vanish
as soon as the absolute temperature approaches zero,

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\mathcal{S}(Z)=0.

(2.8)

It is easy to check that (
2.4
) – (
2.8
) imply

0\leq\varrho\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\leq c\left(1+\varrho\log^{+}(\varrho)+\varrho\log^{+}(\vartheta)\right).

(2.9)

As for the transport coefficients, we suppose that they are continuously differentiable functions of the absolute temperature satisfying

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\mu}(1+\vartheta)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\vartheta),\ |\mu^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\leq\overline{\mu},

\displaystyle 0\leq\eta(\vartheta)

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\eta}(1+\vartheta),

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\kappa}(1+\vartheta^{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa}(1+\vartheta^{\beta}),

(2.10)

where, in accordance with the existence theory developed in
[
7
]
, we require

\beta>6.

(2.11)

2.2
Weak solutions

It is convenient to identify the time periodic functions (distributions)
with objects defined on a periodic “flat sphere”

S_{T}=[0,T]|_{\{0,T\}}

We are ready to introduce the concept of
time periodic solution
to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system
(
1.1
)–(
1.7
).

Definition 2.1

(
weak solution).

We say that a trio
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
is a weak time–periodic solution to the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.7
)
if the following holds:

•

Regularity class
:

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle\in C_{\rm weak}(S_{T};L^{\gamma}(\Omega))\ \mbox{for}\ \gamma=\frac{5}{3},

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(S_{T};W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})),\ \varrho{\bf u}\in C_{\rm weak}(S_{T},L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;R^{d})),

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\beta/2},\ \log(\vartheta)

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(S_{T};W^{1,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle(\vartheta-\vartheta_{B})

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(S_{T};W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)).

(2.12)

•

Equation of continuity:

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=0,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left[b(\varrho)\partial_{t}\varphi+b(\varrho){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\Big(b(\varrho)-b^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=0

(2.14)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}(S_{T}\times\overline{\Omega})
, and any
b\in C^{1}(R)
,
b^{\prime}\in C_{c}(R)
.

•

Momentum equation:

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\varrho(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\bf u})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathbb{S}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\varrho\nabla_{x}G\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t,

(2.15)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}(S_{T}\times\Omega;R^{d})
.

•

Entropy inequality:

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi}{\vartheta}\left[\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

(2.16)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(S_{T}\times\Omega)
,
\varphi\geq 0
;

•

Ballistic energy balance:

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\partial_{t}\psi\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t+\int_{S_{T}}\psi\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left[\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S_{T}}\psi\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}G-\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}-\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

(2.17)

for any
\psi\in C^{1}(S_{T})
,
\psi\geq 0
, and any
\tilde{\vartheta}\in C^{1}(S_{T}\times\overline{\Omega})
,

\tilde{\vartheta}>0,\ \tilde{\vartheta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}.

(2.18)

The weak time–periodic solutions are therefore the weak solutions in the sense of
[
7
]
that are

T-
periodic in the time variable. The instantaneous values the

conservative variables

\varrho(\tau,\cdot)
,
(\varrho{\bf u})(\tau,\cdot)
are well defined as well as the
right and left hand limits of the total entropy
S=\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)
,

\displaystyle\left<S(\tau-,\cdot);\phi\right>

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau-\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(t,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\ \left<S(\tau+,\cdot);\phi\right>\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(t,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Main result

) and will be used in the forthcoming part to deduce the necessary bounds on the family of approximate solutions.

4
Uniform bounds

In order to perform the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
in the family of approximate solutions obtained in Proposition
3.1
, we need uniform bounds independent of
\varepsilon
.

4.1
Mass conservation

Obviously, as the total mass of the fluid is conserved, we get

M_{0}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for all}\ t\in S_{T}\ \Rightarrow\ \sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1.

(4.1)

4.2
Energy estimates

As both
\partial\Omega
and the boundary data
\vartheta_{B}
are smooth, we may suppose that

\Delta_{x}\vartheta_{B}(t,\cdot)=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\ \mbox{for any}\ t\in S_{T}.

(4.2)

Choosing
\psi=1
,
\tilde{\vartheta}=\vartheta_{B}
in the ballistic energy inequality (
3.12
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{k+2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}G-\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon}){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}-\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t.

(4.3)

By virtue of hypothesis (
2.10
) and Korn’s inequality, we obtain

\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Moreover, again by virtue of (
2.10
),

\int_{\Omega}\left[|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

By Poincarè inequality (see e.g. Theorem 4.4.6 in
[
26
]
) we obtain that

\int_{\Omega}|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}+\int_{\Omega}\left|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\right|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

as well as

\int_{\Omega}|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Collecting the last three inequalities, hypothesis (
3.11
) and estimating the boundary terms

|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|^{2}+|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{k+2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}

gives

\left\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{k+2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right).

Note that as
\varepsilon
is small, thus the coefficient at the boundary term may appear without violating the estimates, but aiming to match the formulation of approximate ballistic energy balance.
Gathering the previous observations, we may infer that

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\left[\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}+\left\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{\beta+1}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\left|\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}G-\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon}){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}-\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right|\right).

(4.4)

Now, as
\varrho_{\varepsilon}
,
{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}
solve the equation of continuity (
2.13
),

\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}G\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t=-\int_{S_{T}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}G\ \,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0},G).

In addition, denoting

\mathcal{K}(\vartheta)=\int_{1}^{\vartheta}\frac{\kappa(z)}{z}\ {\rm d}z,

we obtain, by virtue of (
4.2
),

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{K}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{K}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\cdot{\bf n}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}.

Consequently, as
\kappa
satisfies hypothesis (
2.10
), we conclude

\left|\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right|=\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{K}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\cdot{\bf n}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{\beta+1}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right).

Thus inequality (
4.4
) reduces to

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\left[\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}+\left\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{\beta+1}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon}){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}|+|\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right).

(4.5)

In accordance with hypothesis (
2.6
), we decompose the entropy as

\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})=\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+\frac{4a}{3}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{3}.

Consequently, the radiation component may be handled as

\int_{\Omega}|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{3}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\delta\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}+c(\delta,\vartheta_{B})\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{6}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

for any
\delta>0
. Consequently, as
\beta>6
, this term can be absorbed by the left–hand side of
(
4.5
) yielding

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\left[\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}+\left\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{\beta+1}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\right|+\left|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}\right|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right).

(4.6)

Finally, following the arguments of
[
14
, Section 4.4]
, we make use of the Third law of thermodynamics
enforced through hypothesis (
2.8
). Specifically, if

\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}<r\ \mbox{meaning}\ \varrho<r\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}},

we get, by virtue of (
2.9
),

0\leq\varrho\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+r\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}\left[\log^{+}(r\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}})+\log^{+}(\vartheta)\right]\right).

(4.7)

Consequently, we deduce from (
4.6
),

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\left[\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}+\left\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{\beta+1}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(\Lambda(r)+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq r\right\}}\left|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right){\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\right|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq r\right\}}\left|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}\right|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right),

(4.8)

where
\Lambda(r)\to\infty
as
r\to\infty
.

Now, again by hypothesis (
2.8
),

0\leq\mathds{1}_{\left\{\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq r\right\}}\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\leq\mathcal{S}(r)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ r\to\infty.

In an analogue way we treat the term
\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho_{\varepsilon}s\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t
.
Going back to (
4.8
) we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\left[\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}+\left\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{\beta+1}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(\Lambda(r)+\mathcal{S}(r)\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\right|+\varrho_{\varepsilon}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right),

\displaystyle\Lambda(r)\to\infty,\ \mathcal{S}(r)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ r\to\infty.

(4.9)

4.3
Pressure estimates

To close the estimates we have to control the density in terms of the integrals on the right–hand side of
(
4.9
). To this end, we use the nowadays standard pressure estimates obtained via Bogovskii operator.
Specifically, we use the quantity

\varphi(t,x)=\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right],\omega>0,

as a test function in the momentum equation (
3.5
). Here
\mathcal{B}
denotes the operator enjoying
the following properties:

•

\mathcal{B}:L^{q}_{0}(\Omega)\equiv\left\{v\in L^{q}(\Omega)\ \Big|\ \int_{\Omega}v\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\right\}\to W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d}),\ 1<q<\infty;

•

{\rm div}_{x}\mathcal{B}[v]=v;

•

if
v={\rm div}_{x}{\bf g}
, with
{\bf g}\in L^{q}(\Omega;R^{d})
,
{\rm div}_{x}{\bf g}\in L^{r}(\Omega)
,

{\bf g}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, then

\|\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}{\bf g}]\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;R^{d})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|{\bf g}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;R^{d})},

see Galdi
[
15
, Chapter 3]
or Geißert, Heck, and Hieber
[
16
]
. After a straightforward
manipulation (see e.g.
[
9
]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{S_{T}}\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}G\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+(\omega-1)\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t.

(4.10)

Since the total mass
M_{0}
is constant, the smoothing properties of
\mathcal{B}
yield

\left\|\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\right\|_{L^{\infty}(S_{T}\times\Omega;R^{d})}\leq c(M_{0})\ \mbox{as soon as}\ \omega<\frac{1}{d}.

Moreover, in accordance with hypotheses (
2.3
)–(
2.5
),

\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}+\vartheta^{4}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}p(\varrho,\vartheta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}+\vartheta^{4}+1.

In view of these facts, inequality (
4.10
) gives rise to

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0})\Big(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+(\omega-1)\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\Big).

(4.11)

The following steps will be performed for
d=3
. Obviously even better estimates can be obtained if
d=2
.
First,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{6}(\Omega;R^{3})}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho^{\omega}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}t,

where

q=\frac{3\gamma}{2\gamma-3}>1\ \mbox{provided}\ \gamma>\frac{3}{2}.

Fixing

\gamma=\frac{5}{3},\ \omega=\frac{3\gamma}{2\gamma-3}=\frac{1}{15}

(4.12)

and using the fact that the total mass
M_{0}
is conserved, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(M_{0})\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t.

Seeing that the integral containing the Coriolis force can be controlled in a similar way we may rewrite (
4.11
) in the form

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0})\Big(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+(\omega-1)\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\Big).

(4.13)

In a similar way, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega;R^{3})}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t,

where

\frac{1}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

In addition,

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;R^{3})}\leq\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega;R^{3})}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\mbox{where}\ \frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{p};

whence

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho^{\alpha}{\bf u})]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right|\leq c(M)\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\|\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\,{\rm d}t

as soon as (
4.12
) holds.

Finally,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega;R^{3})}\left\|\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t,

where

\frac{1}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

Here,

\left\|\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;R^{3})},\ q=\frac{3r}{3-r},

and

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;R^{3})}\leq\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\ \mbox{with}\ \frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{r}.

Thus using (
4.12
) we may infer that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(M_{0})\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t.

Going back to (
4.13
) and summarizing the previous estimates we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0})\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right.

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\left.\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right),\ \mbox{where}\ \omega=\frac{1}{15}.

(4.14)

The last step is estimating

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad\leq(1+\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)})\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\left\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega;R^{3})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(M)(1+\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)})\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}.

We therefore conclude the pressure estimates:

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0})\left[1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left(1+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t\right],\ \omega=\frac{1}{15}.

(4.15)

4.4
Uniform bounds for
\varepsilon\to 0

As
\beta>6
, we deduce from the inequalities (
4.9
), (
4.15
) that

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\ \,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right)

provided we fix
r=1
in (
4.9
). Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}TM_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{5}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c(M_{0})\left(1+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t\right)

Consequently, inequality (
4.15
) reduces to

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0})\left[1+\left(1+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t\right],\ \omega=\frac{1}{15}.

(4.16)

Next, going back to (
4.9
) we get

\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(\mathcal{S}(r)\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|+\varrho_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t+\Lambda(r)\right)

where, by means of the standard Sobolev embedding theorem,

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega;R^{3})}\leq c(M_{0})\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}.

Consequently,

\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(\mathcal{S}(r)\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\ \,{\rm d}t+\Lambda(r)\right)

(4.17)

Now, introducing the total energy of the system,

E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)

we first observe that

\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right).

(4.18)

The estimate (
4.18
) follows from the mean value theorem and the ballistic energy inequality (
3.12
).
Indeed, in view of the uniform bounds established in Section
4.2
, we first deduce (
4.18
)
for the ballistic energy

E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})-\vartheta_{B}\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon}),

and then use (
2.9
) to observe that the entropy part
\vartheta_{B}\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})
is a lower order perturbation.

Now, we estimate the kinetic energy using (
4.17
),

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{6}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\Lambda(r)\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{S}(r)\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\ \,{\rm d}t.

In addition, by interpolation,

\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\leq\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{5}{6}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{6}}.

(4.19)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}c(M_{0})\Lambda(r)\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{5}{6}}+\mathcal{S}(r)\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{5}{6}}\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{5}{6}}\ \,{\rm d}t.

(4.20)

Combining (
4.16
), (
4.17
), (
4.19
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{S_{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0})\left[1+\left(1+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\int_{S_{T}}\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \,{\rm d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq c(M_{0})\left[1+\left(1+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\left(\mathcal{S}(r)\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\ \,{\rm d}t+\Lambda(r)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq c(M_{0})\left[1+\left(1+\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\left(\mathcal{S}(r)\int_{S_{T}}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{5}{6}}\ \,{\rm d}t+\Lambda(r)\right)\right]

(4.21)

Interpolating
L^{1}
and
L^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}
and using boundedness of the total mass we have

\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\leq c(M_{0})\left(\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{10}{11}}\ \mbox{provided}\ \omega=\frac{1}{15}.

(4.22)

Thus summing up (
4.18
)–(
4.22
) we may infer that

\displaystyle\mbox{\eqref{U18}}\ \Rightarrow\

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left[1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{d})}^{2}+\left\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\log(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{\eqref{U9}}\ \Rightarrow

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left[1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{\eqref{U20}}\ \Rightarrow

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left[1+\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\Lambda(r)\left(\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{S}(r)\left(\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{\eqref{U22}}\ \Rightarrow

\displaystyle\leq c(M_{0})\left[1+\left(\int_{S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{5}{3}+\omega}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{10}{11}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\Lambda(r)\left(\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{S}(r)\left(\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{\eqref{U21}}\ \Rightarrow

\displaystyle\leq c(M_{0})\left[\Lambda(r)+\mathcal{S}^{\frac{10}{11}}(r)\left(\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\Lambda(r)\left(\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{S}(r)\left(\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\right].

(4.23)

As
\mathcal{S}(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
, we fix
r>0
large enough to deduce from (
4.23
) the desired energy bound

\sup_{t\in S_{T}}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq c(M_{0}).

(4.24)

5
Convergence

Our ultimate goal is to perform the limit in the sequence of approximate solutions
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
to obtain the existence of the time–periodic solution claimed in Theorem
2.2
. With the energy estimate (
4.24
) at hand, this is a routine matter nowadays well understood. Indeed the test functions used in the entropy inequality (
2.16
) are compactly supported thus unaffected
by the boundary integral in its approximate counterpart (
3.6
). Similarly, the approximate ballistic energy (
3.12
) is in fact stronger than (
2.17
) due to the penalization

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{T}}\psi\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{B}|^{k+2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\ \,{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\ \psi\geq 0.

(5.1)

In particular, for
\psi=1
, the above inequality together with the (
3.12
) yield

\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\to\vartheta\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{d}))

with the limit trace
\vartheta|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}

as required in Theorem
2.2
.

Consequently, the proof of convergence is exactly the same as in the existence theory elaborated in
[
7
]
with the exception of the strong convergence of the density, the “initial” value of which is
unspecified in the periodic setting. Fortunately, the compactness arguments based on Lions’ identity and boundedness of the oscillation defect measure can be modified to accommodate the time periodic
setting exactly as in
[
10
, Section 9.3]
. Thus the proof of Theorem
2.2
can be completed.

6
Concluding remarks

In comparison with
[
10
]
, the available
a priori
bounds do not allow to handle a general driving force
\varrho{\bf g}
in the momentum equation. Although the potential case

{\bf g}=\nabla_{x}G
is physically relevant, more general (non–potential) forces occur when the fluid is stirred up by the motion of the container. A detailed inspection of the arguments
in Section
4.3
reveals they could be considerably improved in the case
d=2
due to the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2}\subset L^{q}
for any finite
q
. Similar improvement may also be expected
in the case the total mass
M_{0}
is small, cf. Wang and Wang
[
25
]
. We therefore strongly conjecture that the present result can be extended to a general driving force
{\bf g}
provided

•

either
d=2
,

•

or

M_{0}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho\ \,{\rm d}{x}

is small enough with respect to the amplitude of
{\bf g}
.

As potentiality of
{\bf g}
was also used in the estimate (
3.8
) crucial for boundedness of the approximate sequence, the proof of the above conjecture would require a different kind of approximation scheme.

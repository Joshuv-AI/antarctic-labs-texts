Electrostatic potential and electric field in the z z axis of a non centered circular charged ring
F. Escalante

Abstract
In introductory level electromagnetism courses the calculation of electrostatic potential and electric field in an arbitrary point is a very common exercise. One of the most viewed cases is the calculation of electrostatic potential and electric field in the symmetry axis of a centered ring and it has been widely studied the potential off the axis of a charged ring centered in the origin coordinate. In this work, we calculated the electrostatic potential and electric field in the z z axis of a non centered charged ring using elliptic integrals as an pedagogical example of the application of special functions in electromagnetism.

Electrostatic potential on the axis

The electrostatic potential in terms of a charge density is given by
[
9
]

\Phi(\vec{r})=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\int\frac{\rho(\vec{r}^{\,{}^{\prime}})}{\mid\vec{r}-\vec{r}^{\,{}^{\prime}}\mid}d^{3}\vec{r}^{\,{}^{\prime}}.

(1)

We consider a uniformly charged ring of radius
R
and uniform charge density
\lambda=Q/2\pi R
located in the
xy
plane which is non centered respect to the origin coordinate
O
as shown in figure (

)

so we have

\Phi(\vec{r})=\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\int\frac{dl}{\mid\vec{r}-\vec{r}^{\;{}^{\prime}}\mid}.

(2)

Using the parametric equation of a non centered ring given by
x^{\prime}=a+R\cos\theta
and
y^{\prime}=b+R\sin\theta
, where (
a
,
b
) corresponds to the coordinates of the center of the ring and
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
. Therefore
\vec{r}=z\hat{k}
and
\vec{r}^{\,{}^{\prime}}=(a+R\cos\theta)\hat{\imath}+(b+R\sin\theta)\hat{\jmath}
, so we can obtain the infinitesimal length element by means of

dl=\mid d\vec{r}^{\,{}^{\prime}}\mid=\sqrt{(dx^{\prime})^{2}+(dy^{\prime})^{2}}=Rd\theta.

(3)

Then, the electrostatic potential in the
z
axis is given by

\Phi(z)=\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{R\,d\theta}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+R^{2}+z^{2}+2aR\cos\theta+2bR\sin\theta}}

(4)

defining
\alpha=a/R
,
\beta=b/R
,
\zeta=z/R
and
\Lambda=\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\zeta^{2}+1
we have

\Phi(\zeta)=\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{\Lambda+2\alpha\cos\theta+2\beta\sin\theta}},

(5)

The integral in the equation (
5
) can be written as
[
10
]

I=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{\Lambda+2\alpha\cos\theta+2\beta\sin\theta}}=\int_{-\varphi}^{2\pi-\varphi}\frac{du}{\sqrt{\Lambda+\varepsilon\cos u}}.

(6)

The parameters of equation (
6
) are defined by
\varphi=\arctan(\beta/\alpha)
and
\varepsilon=2\beta/\sin\varphi=2\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}
and the variable
u=\theta-\varphi
. Using the transformation
u=2\phi
we can re write the denominator of integrand (
6
) as

(\Lambda+\varepsilon\cos u)^{1/2}=(\Lambda+\varepsilon)^{1/2}\left(1-m\sin^{2}\phi\right)^{1/2},

(7)

where
m=2\varepsilon/(\Lambda+\varepsilon)
. So the equation (
6
) can be written as

I=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\Lambda+\varepsilon}}\int_{-\varphi/2}^{\pi-\varphi/2}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{1-m\sin^{2}\phi}}.

(8)

The solution of the integral (
8
) is given by the incomplete elliptic integral of the first kind
F(\varphi,m)

[
11
]

F(\varphi,m)=\int_{0}^{\varphi}(1-m\sin^{2}\phi)^{-1/2}d\phi,

(9)

then, the electrostatic potential in
z
axis is

\Phi(\zeta)=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_{0}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+\gamma)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}}\left[F\left(\frac{\varphi}{2},\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right)-F\left(-\pi+\frac{\varphi}{2},\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right)\right]

(10)

where
\gamma=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}
.

Since
F(0,m)=0
and
F(-\pi,m)=-2K(m)
, where
K(m)
is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind in terms of the parameter
m
, we can make
\beta=0
and
\alpha=0
then
m=0
. Noticing that
K(0)=\pi/2
, we have

\Phi(z)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{R^{2}+z^{2}}},

(11)

which is the electrostatic potential in the
z
axis of a centered ring.

\Phi(\varrho,\zeta)=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_{0}}\frac{2}{\sqrt{(\varrho+1)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}}K\left[\frac{4\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right],

3
Electric field

As is known, the electric field can be obtained from the potential gradient. In cylindrical coordinates, considering cylindrical symmetry, the electric field is given by

\vec{E}=-\vec{\nabla}\Phi(\rho,z)=-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\hat{\rho}+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\hat{k}\right)\Phi(\rho,z),

(14)

however, in order to obtain an expression for the electric field in terms of the coordinates
(\varrho,\zeta)
, we must consider the change of variable
\varrho=\rho/R
and
\zeta=z/R
to preserve the dimensions of the electric field, so we have

\vec{E}=-\frac{1}{R}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\hat{\varrho}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\hat{k}\right)\Phi(\varrho,\zeta).

(15)

As we seen before, the expressions (
10
) and (
12
) are analogous, so we can obtain the
(\varrho,\zeta)
components of the electric field by means of the gradient of equation (
12
), using the expression (
15
).

Considering that

\frac{dK(k)}{dk}=\frac{E(k)}{k(1-k^{2})}-\frac{K(k)}{k}

(16)

and

\frac{dE(k)}{dk}=\frac{E(k)-K(k)}{k},

(17)

where
E(k)
is the incomplete elliptic integral of the second kind. After some manipulations, the radial component of the electric field is given by

\displaystyle E_{\varrho}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_{0}R}\frac{1}{\varrho\sqrt{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}((\varrho-1)^{2}+\zeta^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\left\{(\varrho^{2}-1-\zeta^{2})E\left[\frac{4\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right]+((1-\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2})K\left[\frac{4\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right]\right\},

and the vertical component of the electric field is

E_{\zeta}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_{0}R}\frac{2\zeta}{\sqrt{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}((\varrho-1)^{2}+\zeta^{2})}E\left[\frac{4\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right].

(19)

From the equations (
3
) and (
19
) we can check the cases when
\varrho\to 0
. In this cases we must recover the expression for a centered uniformly charged ring for the vertical component and zero for the radial component of the electric field.

As we can see, the expression (
3
) can not be evaluated directly in
\varrho=0
as it presents a
0/0
behaviour. So we must calculate the limit as
\varrho\to 0
.

Considering (
16
) and (
17
), and after some manipulation we have

\displaystyle E_{\varrho}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_{0}R}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}(\varrho^{3}+4\varrho^{4}+5\varrho^{2}(\zeta^{2}-1)-\varrho(1+\zeta^{2})+(1+\zeta^{2})^{2})}

(20)

\displaystyle\times\left\{((1+\varrho)^{3}+(1+3\varrho)\zeta^{2})E\left[\frac{4\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(\varrho-1)(1+\varrho(4+3\varrho)+\zeta^{2})K\left[\frac{4\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^{2}+\zeta^{2}}\right]\right\}.

Evaluating the limit
\varrho\to 0
in the expression (
20
) and noticing that
E(0)=\pi/2
and
K(0)=\pi/2
, we ended up with

E_{\varrho}=0.

On the other hand, we can calculate directly the electric field in the vertical component for
\varrho=0
in the expression (
19
)

E_{\zeta}=\frac{\lambda}{2\epsilon_{0}R}\frac{\zeta}{(1+\zeta^{2})^{3/2}},

(21)

as
\zeta=z/R
and
\lambda=Q/2\pi R
we obtain the well known expression for the electric field of an centered uniformly charged ring along the symmetry axis

E_{z}=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\frac{z}{(R^{2}+z^{2})^{3/2}}.

(22)

4
Conclusions

It has been presented a general method to obtain the electrostatic potential using an adequate parametrization of a loop through
the use of elliptic integrals. From this result, it could recover the expressions of the electrostatic potential and electric field on the symmetry axis of a centered ring. Finally, the obtained results agree with the calculations of the electrostatic potential off the symmetry axis of a centered ring and are useful as a pedagogical exercise for to students can explore special functions in an electromagnetism problem.

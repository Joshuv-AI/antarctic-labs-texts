Iwasawa theory for quadratic Hilbert modular forms
David Loeffler, Sarah Livia Zerbes

Abstract
. We study the Iwasawa main conjecture for quadratic Hilbert modular forms over the p p -cyclotomic tower. Using an Euler system in the cohomology of Siegel modular varieties, we prove the “Kato divisibility” of the Iwasawa main conjecture under certain technical hypotheses. By comparing this result with the opposite divisibility due to Wan, we obtain the full Main Conjecture over the cyclotomic 𝐙 p \mathbf{Z}_{p} -extension. As a consequence, we prove new cases of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for quadratic Hilbert modular forms, and of the equivariant Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in analytic rank 0 0 for elliptic curves over real quadratic fields twisted by Dirichlet characters. As a “by-product” of the theory developed here, we also present new results on Iwasawa theory for Rankin–Selberg convolutions of modular forms, relaxing hypotheses of p p -distinction or p p -regularity assumed in previous works. This gives new cases of the equivariant BSD conjecture for elliptic curves over 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} twisted by 2-dimensional odd Artin representations, giving finiteness of the p p -part of the Tate–Shafarevich group for all but finitely many ordinary primes.

1.3.
Outline of the paper

The overall strategy we will follow is to construct an Euler system for the 4-dimensional Galois representation
\Ind_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}(\rho_{\pi,v}^{*})
. For
\pi
with sufficiently regular weight, we construct this Euler system as an application of the results of
[
LZ24
]
, using the “twisted Yoshida lift” – an instance of Langlands functoriality, transferring automorphic representations from
\GL_{2}/K
to
\GSp_{4}/\mathbf{Q}
. The main technical obstacle to be overcome is that the most interesting Hilbert modular forms – those of parallel weight – transfer to
non-cohomological
automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}
, so the results of
op.cit.
do not immediately apply. We approach these non-cohomological points using deformation along eigenvarieties, which requires an extremely delicate study of the “leading terms” of families of Euler systems at a given specialisation.

(This “leading term argument” also gives new results in other settings. As an example, in Appendix
A
we apply it to the Euler system of Beilinson–Flach elements, in order to bound Selmer groups associated to Artin twists of elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
. If the eigenvalues of Frobenius at
p
on the Artin representation are distinct, this case was treated in
[
KLZ17
]
; but this
p
-regularity assumption fails for a positive-density set of primes. The new methods allow us to handle these
p
-regular cases, and to show that the Tate–Shafarevich group is finite for a density 1 set of primes.)

Before embarking on the proofs of the main theorems of this paper, we will need to recall a great deal of machinery. For the convenience of the reader, we have attempted to split this into several independent blocks, so that sections
2
-
3
(dealing with Hilbert modular forms) are almost entirely independent of sections
4
–
5
(dealing with Siegel modular forms).

We shall begin to draw the threads together in
Section

6
, in which we construct an Euler system for automorphic representations
\pi
whose weights are “very regular”. In the following section
8
, we remove this very regular condition using variation along an eigenvariety, and at the same time prove an explicit reciprocity law relating the Euler system to
p
-adic
L
-functions. The analysis of leading terms is carried out in sections
9
and
10
, leading up to the construction of the Euler system in full generality in
Section

11
, and the proof of Theorem A in
Section

12
.

In §
13
, we recall a selection of results from
[
Wan15
]
and compare these with Theorem A, leading to the proof of Theorem B.

Let us say that

(for “big image”) is satisfied for

•

•

(\rho_{\pi,v}\times\rho_{\pi^{\sigma},v})(G_{K})

Proposition 2.5.2
.

Suppose

satisfies condition (BI). Then:

(a)

The residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{\pi,v}
is irreducible, and remains irreducible restricted to
\Gal(\overline{K}/K^{\mathrm{ab}})
.

(b)

The tensor product
\bar{\rho}_{\pi,v}\otimes\bar{\rho}_{\pi^{\sigma},v}
is irreducible as a representation of
\Gal(\overline{K}/K^{\mathrm{ab}})
.

(c)

The induced representation
\Ind_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}(\bar{\rho}_{\pi,v})
is irreducible as a representation of
\Gal\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})\right)
.

(d)

There exists

such that

is free of rank 1 over
\mathcal{O}_{v}
, for some (and hence any)
\mathcal{O}_{v}
-lattice

in
\rho_{\pi,v}
.

(e)

There exists

which acts as
-1

Proof.

Parts (a) and (b) are obvious, since

has no nontrivial abelian quotients, and hence the image of

still contains
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})\times\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
. Similarly, this shows that

is a direct sum of two non-isomorphic irreducibles as a representation of

For (d), we take

to be any element whose image under

\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}x&\\
&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

for some

which gives the element

.
∎

Remark 2.5.3

Parts (c) and (d) of the proposition show that if condition (BI) holds, then Rubin’s

holds for every Dirichlet character

We shall need part (b) since it is required as a hypothesis for some results of Dimitrov

]
which we shall need to quote later. This is also the reason why we assumed

We now show that if we fix

Definition 2.5.4
.

Let

A well-known theorem proved by Ribet for elliptic modular forms

below

.

.

An

of

such that

.

Note that

Proposition 2.5.6
.

(1)

If

is a field embedding, then

is a twist of
\pi
if and only if

, and
\tilde{\gamma}(\pi)
is a twist of

if and only if

.

(2)

If

is an exceptional automorphism of

is a twist of a base-change from

The first theorem is a consequence of the strong multiplicity one theorem for

, which shows that the ratios

for all but finitely many

]
.
∎

Proposition 2.5.7
.

If
\pi
does not have an exceptional automorphism, then for all but finitely many primes

of

, we have

If

has an exceptional automorphism

satisfying the condition that

. For all but finitely many primes with

, the image of

See

. In particular, this shows that if

.

We choose a prime

of the coefficient field

above

(for some choice of extension of

to

).

Definition 5.1.1
.

(i)

We have
\nu_{v}(\alpha)\geqslant 0
. If equality holds, we say

is

, and

is a
Siegel-ordinary refinement
.

(ii)

We have

.

.

Remark 5.1.2
.

In (ii), all the other pairwise products

always have valuation strictly larger than

Let

7.
Yoshida lifts in families

The elements of
Definition

7.1.
Families of twisted Yoshida lifts

Let
\underline{\pi}

Definition 7.1.1

The following theorem is an instance of much more general results on functoriality of eigenvarieties. (We are grateful to Chris Williams for his explanations in relation to this issue.)

Theorem 7.1.3

We can find a closed immersion of rigid-analytic spaces

Proof.

This follows from the same argument as the non-critical case of

\GL_{2}/K

\mathfrak{n}

\mathcal{E}_{K}(\mathfrak{n},\psi)
be the component corresponding to

\tilde{\Omega}
lies in

Using the general results on functoriality of eigenvarieties developed in

∎

Note 7.1.4
.

Note that if

If

\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi})^{\vee}\left(\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}\left({}_{c}z^{[\underline{\pi},r]}_{m}\right)\right)={}_{c}C_{n}^{[r]}\cdot\left(\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\epsilon}(\underline{\pi},\Delta_{n})(\mathbf{j})\otimes\mathcal{L}_{p}^{-\epsilon}(\underline{\pi})(\mathbf{t}_{2}+r)\right)

where
\epsilon=(-1)^{t_{2}+1+r}
.

Proof.

Let
v_{\mathrm{syn}}
be an element of
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\mathrm{syn},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi})
whose base-extension to
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{Z})
is a basis, and similarly
v_{\natural}=v_{+}\otimes v_{-}
for the Betti cohomology.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
denote the set of points in
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{W}
at which the reciprocity law applies, i.e. the set of points
(x,j)
with
x=(\underline{k}^{\prime},\underline{t}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}\cap\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}
, and
j\in\mathbf{Z}
with
t_{2}^{\prime}-1\geqslant j\geqslant t_{1}^{\prime}
. This set
\mathcal{Y}
is manifestly Zariski-dense in
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{W}
.

We first consider
r=0
. We have a “motivic”
p
-adic
L
-function

{}_{c}\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mot}}(\underline{\pi})\coloneqq\left\langle\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}\left({}_{c}z^{[\underline{\pi},0]}_{1}\right),v_{\mathrm{syn}}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{W}^{\epsilon}),

where
\epsilon=(-1)^{1+t_{2}}
. Similarly, choosing a basis
v_{+}\otimes v_{-}
of
\mathcal{M}^{\natural}_{B}(\underline{\pi})
, we also have the “analytic”
p
-adic
L
-function

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\underline{\pi})\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\epsilon}(\underline{\pi})(\mathbf{j}),v_{+}\rangle\cdot\langle\mathcal{L}_{p}^{-\epsilon}(\underline{\pi})(\mathbf{t}_{2}),v_{+}\rangle\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{W}^{\epsilon}).

The analytic
p
-adic
L
-function is not a zero-divisor, by
Proposition

3.2.2
. By the reciprocity law, the motivic
p
-adic
L
-function is also non-vanishing at all points in
\mathcal{Y}
of parity
\epsilon=(-1)^{1+t_{2}}
that do not lie in the vanishing locus of either
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\underline{\pi})
or
v_{\mathrm{syn}}
. So it is not a zero-divisor either, and we can form the ratio

R=\frac{{}_{c}\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mot}}(\underline{\pi})}{{}_{c}C_{1}^{[0]}\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\underline{\pi})}\in\operatorname{Frac}\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{W}^{\epsilon})^{\times},

i.e.
R
is a generically non-zero
p
-adic meromorphic function on
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{W}^{\epsilon}
.

Since
\mathcal{Y}
is Zariski-dense, so is the set of
(x,j)\in\mathcal{Y}
at which the numerator and denominator of
R
are both non-zero. At any such point, the value
R(x,j)
is equal to the (finite and non-zero) ratio of the periods
v_{\mathrm{syn}}(x)
and
v_{\natural}(x)
, compared via
\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi}(x))
. In particular, it is independent of
j
. So we can conclude that
R
itself is independent of the
\mathcal{W}^{\epsilon}
variable; that is, it lies in
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{Z})^{\times}
.

So, scaling
v_{\natural}
and
v_{\mathrm{syn}}
appropriately, we can arrange that this ratio is in fact exactly 1. That is, the isomorphism
\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi})
sending
v_{\natural}
to
v_{\mathrm{syn}}
interpolates the
\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi}(x))
for all but finitely many specialisations
x
.

This proves part (a), and also part (b) for
r=0
and
n=1
. The general case of (b) now follows readily from the Zariski-density of
\mathcal{Y}
.
∎

Remark 8.2.2
.

By a closer inspection of the argument, one sees that for a given weight
x=(\underline{k}^{\prime},\underline{t}^{\prime})\in\Omega_{\mathrm{cl}}^{1}
, either the “family” comparison isomorphism
\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi})
specialises at
x
to the known isomorphism
\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi}(x))
, or both sides of the explicit reciprocity law for
\underline{\pi}(x)
are 0 for every permissible value of
j
,
r
and
n
. The latter possibility seems exceedingly improbable, but we are not able to rule it out a priori.

8.3.
Behaviour at
(\underline{k},\underline{t})

We now consider the local behaviour of the above constructions at our chosen point
x_{0}=(\underline{k},\underline{t})
. Note that the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z},x_{0}}
is a DVR, and it has a canonical uniformiser
X
given by the image of
(k_{1}+4m,\dots)\mapsto m
.

Definition 8.3.1
.

We denote by
V(\pi)
the fibre at
x_{0}
of the sheaf
\mathcal{V}(\underline{\pi})
.

This is an
L
-vector space depending only on
\pi
(and our choice of ordering of the two embeddings
K\hookrightarrow\mathbf{R}
).

Since
\aleph(\underline{\pi})
is an isomorphism after inverting

, there is a uniquely-determined

such that if we let

be a local basis of the Betti-cohomology sheaf around

(8.1)

\aleph^{\prime}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\pi):M_{B}^{\natural}(\pi,L)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}\left(K_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}},\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}V(\pi)\right),

given by

v_{\natural}\pmod{X}\mapsto X^{q}\cdot\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\underline{\pi})(v_{\natural})\pmod{X}.

Remark 8.3.2
.

Note that even if

, so that
\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\pi)
is defined, it does not follow formally that
\aleph^{\prime}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\pi)
necessarily agrees with
\aleph_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\pi)
.

Just as we have defined the comparision isomorphism
\aleph^{\prime}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\pi)
for

as the “leading term” of a meromorphic object in the family
\underline{\pi}
, we shall define an Euler system for

using a similar leading-term construction. This construction is somewhat delicate, and may be of independent interest in other settings, so we have given a more general treatment in the next section; the discussion of the twisted Yoshida case will resume in
Section

11
.

In this section we shall recall some results from

9.1.
Setup

In this section,
p
is prime,
L
is a finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
,
\varpi
is a uniformiser of
\mathcal{O}
, and

is a finitely-generated free
\mathcal{O}
-module with a continuous action of

, unramified outside a finite set of primes

. We let

; and we set
\mathbf{T}=T\otimes\Lambda(-\mathbf{q})
, where

and
\mathbf{q}
its universal character.

Remark 9.1.1
.

Note that in
[
MR04
]
the base ring is assumed to be local;
\Lambda
is not a local ring, but it is a direct product of finitely many copies of the local ring
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}((1+2p\mathbf{Z}_{p})^{\times})
, so the theory of
[
MR04
]
extends immediately to this case.

We suppose that the hypotheses (H.0)–(H.4) of
[
MR04
, §3.5]
are satisfied by
T
, and also by the twisted representation
T(\eta)
for every
m\in\mathcal{R}
and every character
\Delta_{m}\to\bar{L}^{\times}
(taking
R
to be the ring of integers of the extension
L(\eta)
generated by the values of
\eta
). Then these are also satisfied for
\mathbf{T}
(with

) and its twists. Finally, we suppose that
\rk T^{(c_{\infty}=1)}=\rk T^{(c_{\infty}=-1)}=d
for some

, where

is complex conjugation.

Remark 9.1.2
.

In fact the assumption that
\rk T^{(c_{\infty}=1)}=\rk T^{(c_{\infty}=-1)}
is not really needed – it would suffice to assume that
T
have rank
\geqslant 2
, and define

, as long as we replace
\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}
with its maximal totally-real subfield

throughout. This generalisation is not needed for in the present paper, but may be useful in future applications.

9.2.
Selmer structures and core ranks

Let
T^{+}\subseteq T
be an
\mathcal{O}
-direct summand stable under
G_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
. As in
Section

3.3
above, this defines a (derived) Selmer structure
\square^{+}
on
\mathbf{T}
, in which the local conditions at
\ell\neq p
are the unramified ones, and the local condition at
p
is given by the morphism of complexes

C^{\bullet}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathbf{T}^{+})\longrightarrow C^{\bullet}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathbf{T}).

If
\mathfrak{Q}
is a height 1 prime of
\Lambda
, we write
T_{\mathfrak{Q}}
for
\mathbf{T}\otimes_{\Lambda}S_{\mathfrak{Q}}
, where
S_{\mathfrak{Q}}
is the integral closure of
\Lambda/\mathfrak{Q}
. Thus if
\mathfrak{Q}\neq(p)
, the ring
S_{\mathfrak{Q}}
is the ring of integers of a finite extension of
L
, and
T_{\mathfrak{Q}}
is the twist of
T
by an
S_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\times}
-valued character; whereas if
\mathfrak{Q}=(p)
,
S_{\mathfrak{Q}}
is isomorphic to
(\mathcal{O}/\varpi\mathcal{O})[[X]]
.

We use
T^{+}
to define a
simple
(i.e. non-derived) Selmer structure
\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}
on
T_{\mathfrak{Q}}
for each height 1 prime ideal
\mathfrak{Q}
of
\Lambda
, as in
[
KLZ17
, §12.3]
; this generalises the canonical Selmer structure of
[
MR04
]
(and reduces to it when
T^{+}=T
). If
\mathfrak{Q}\neq p\Lambda
, then the Selmer structure
\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}
satisfies the Cartesian condition (H.6) of
[
MR04
, §3.5]
.

Proposition 9.2.1
.

If
\mathfrak{Q}
is a height 1 prime ideal not containing
p
, then the “core rank”
\chi(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}})
, in the sense of
[
MR04
, Definition 4.1.11]
, is given by

\chi(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}})=\operatorname{max}\Big(0,d-\rank_{\mathcal{O}}(T/T^{+})+\rank_{S_{\mathfrak{Q}}}H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},(T^{+}_{\mathfrak{Q}})^{*}(1))\Big).

Proof.

This is a generalisation of
[
MR04
, Theorem 5.2.15]
, and is proved in exactly the same way.
∎

Notation
.

For all but finitely many
\mathfrak{Q}
, the term
H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},(T^{+}_{\mathfrak{Q}})^{*}(1))
is zero and hence the core rank is simply
\operatorname{max}\left(0,d-\rank_{\mathcal{O}}(T/T^{+})\right)
. We shall write
\chi(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda})
for this common value.

9.3.
Kolyvagin systems

We consider the module

\overline{\KS}\left(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda}\right)\coloneqq\varprojlim_{k}\varinjlim_{j}\KS\left(\mathbf{T}/\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}^{k},\square^{+}_{\Lambda},\mathcal{P}_{j}\right)

of Kolyvagin systems for
\mathbf{T}
. Here,
\mathcal{P}_{j}
is a set of primes as defined in
[
MR04
, Def. 3.6.1]
, and
\Delta^{+}_{\Lambda}
denotes the simple Selmer structure on
\mathbf{T}/I\mathbf{T}
, for any ideal
I
of
\Lambda
, in which the local condition at
p
is given by the image of
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathbf{T}^{+})
in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathbf{T}/I\mathbf{T})
. (Note that this subspace is generally smaller than the image of
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathbf{T}^{+}/I\mathbf{T}^{+})
.)

There is an analogous module
\overline{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right)
for
T_{\mathfrak{Q}}
, for any height 1 prime
\mathfrak{Q}
, and we have a diagram of maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\KS}\left(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},T_{\mathfrak{Q}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\boldsymbol{\kappa}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\boldsymbol{\kappa}\mapsto\kappa_{1}}

Theorem 9.3.1

(Mazur–Rubin)
.

If
\chi(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}})=0
, then we have
\overline{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right)=0
.

Proof.

By Theorem 4.2.2 of
[
MR04
]
, if
\chi(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}})=0
, then we have
\KS(T_{\mathfrak{Q}}/\varpi^{k},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}},\mathcal{P}_{j})=0
for all
j\geqslant k
. Hence the inverse limit is clearly also zero.
∎

Corollary 9.3.2
.

If
\chi(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda})=0
, then every Kolyvagin system
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\overline{\KS}\left(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda}\right)
satisfies
\kappa_{1}=0
.

Proof.

This follows from the same argument as
[
MR04
, Corollary 5.3.19]
: if
\kappa_{1}\neq 0
, then for almost all
\mathfrak{Q}
the image of
\kappa_{1}
in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},T_{\mathfrak{Q}})
is non-zero. So
a fortiori
the image of
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
in
\overline{\KS}(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\Lambda^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}})
is nonzero. However, this module is identically 0, as we have just seen.
∎

Remark 9.3.3
.

It seems likely that if
\chi(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda})=0
then
\overline{\KS}\left(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda}\right)=\{0\}
, but we have not proved this.

9.4.
Euler systems

Now let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes that are
1\bmod p
and not in
S
,
\mathcal{R}
the set of square-free products of primes in
\mathcal{P}
, and
\mathcal{K}
be the composite of
\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}
and the fields
\mathbf{Q}[m]
for
m\in\mathcal{R}
. We define
\mathrm{ES}(T,T^{+},\mathcal{K})
as in
[
KLZ17
, Definition 12.2.1]
, with
\mathcal{P}
taken to be the set of primes in
\mathcal{R}
; thus an element
\mathbf{c}\in\mathrm{ES}(T,T^{+},\mathcal{K})
is given by a collection of classes

\mathbf{c}=(c_{m})_{m\in\mathcal{R}},\qquad c_{m}\in\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T;\square^{+}).

satisfying the Euler-system norm-compatibility relation. By Proposition 12.2.3 of
op.cit.
, there is a canonical homomorphism

\mathrm{ES}(T,T^{+},\mathcal{K})\to\overline{\KS}(\mathbf{T},\square_{\Lambda}^{+}),

commuting with the maps from both sides to
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T)
.

Remark 9.4.1
.

Strictly speaking this is only proved in
op.cit.
under the additional assumption that
\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}^{p^{k}}-1
is injective on
T
for every
\ell\in\mathcal{P}
and every
k\geqslant 0
. This condition tends to be automatically satisfied in applications, but in any case we can always reduce to this situation by replacing
T
with
T\otimes\chi
for a suitable character
\chi:\Gamma\to L^{\times}
, and using the fact that there are canonical bijections between Euler or Kolyvagin systems for
T
and for
T(\chi)
. Each
\ell\in\mathcal{P}
only rules out countably many characters
\chi
, and since there are uncountably many characters
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to L^{\times}
, we can always find a twist such that this condition holds. Cf.
[
Rub00
, §VI.1]
.

Proposition 9.4.2
.

Suppose
\chi(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda})=0
. Then the module of Euler systems
\mathrm{ES}(T,T^{+},\mathcal{K})
is zero.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{c}=(c_{m})_{m\in\mathcal{R}}
be a non-zero element of
\mathrm{ES}(T,T^{+},\mathcal{K})
. Then
c_{m}
is non-zero for some
m\in\mathcal{R}
. Since
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T)
is torsion-free,
c_{m}
is non-zero as an element of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],V)
, where
V=T[1/p]
. Extending
L
if necessary, we can write this group as the direct sum
\bigoplus_{\eta}H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V(\eta))
, where
\eta
varies over characters of
\Delta_{m}
. So
c_{m}
must have non-zero image in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V(\eta))
for some
\eta
.

Replacing
T
by
T(\eta)
(and discarding the primes dividing
m
from
\mathcal{P}
), we can assume without loss of generality that
m=1
, so we have an Euler system
\mathbf{c}\in\mathrm{ES}(T,T^{+},\mathcal{K})
with
c_{1}\neq 0
. However, if
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\overline{\KS}(\mathbf{T},\square_{\Lambda}^{+})
is the image of
\mathbf{c}
, then we must have
\kappa_{1}=0
by
Corollary

9.3.2
. This is a contradiction, since by construction
\kappa_{1}=c_{1}
as elements of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T)
.
∎

9.5.
Vanishing modulo ideals

We preserve the notation of the previous section.

Assumption 9.5.1
.

Assume that for all
m\in\mathcal{R}
the following groups are finite (or, equivalently, zero):

•

H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],T^{+})

•

H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],(T^{+})^{*})

•

H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],(T/T^{+}))

This implies, in particular, that the core ranks
\chi(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}})
are equal to
\chi(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda})
for
all
characteristic 0 prime ideals
\mathfrak{Q}
of height 1.

Proposition 9.5.2
.

Suppose the above assumption holds, and the hypotheses of
Proposition

9.4.2
are satisfied. Let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a height 1 prime ideal of
\Lambda
not containing
p
, and suppose that
\mathbf{c}\in\operatorname{ES}(T,\mathcal{K})
is such that for every
m
, the image of
c_{m}
in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}[m],V_{\mathfrak{Q}}/V_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})
is zero, where
V_{\mathfrak{Q}}=T_{\mathfrak{Q}}[1/p]
.

Then we have
\mathbf{c}\in\mathfrak{Q}\cdot\operatorname{ES}(T,\mathcal{K})
.

Proof.

We shall show that
c_{m}
has zero image in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}})
, for all
m
. We shall in fact show that
c_{m}
maps to 0 in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}[m],V_{\mathfrak{Q}})
; this is equivalent, since
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}})
is
p
-torsion-free.

Suppose this is not the case. As above, after inverting
p
and extending
L
, it follows that there is a character
\eta
of
\Delta_{m}
(for some
m
) such that the twisted Euler system
\mathbf{c}^{\eta}
has
c_{1}^{\eta}
non-zero, and not
p
-torsion, modulo
\mathfrak{Q}
. As in the previous proof, without loss of generality we may assume
\eta=1
.

We claim that the Kolyvagin system
\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}
derived from
\kappa
satisfies
p^{a}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}\in{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right)
for some
a
. This can be proved using the “universal Euler system” as in
[
KLZ17
, Proposition 12.2.3]
. In
op.cit.
we used the existence of classes over the whole cyclotomic tower all satisfying the local condition, which does not apply here. However, since
H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],T^{+})=0
we certainly have
H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})=0
, and this allows us to apply the modified argument sketched in
[
Rub00
, §IX.1, ‘Construction of the derivative classes’]
(with
T^{+}
and
\mathbb{T}^{+}=\operatorname{Maps}(G_{\mathbf{Q}},T^{+})
in place of
W_{M}^{+}
and
\mathbb{W}_{M}^{+}
). The factor
p^{a}
arises since
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})\to H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}})
can have a finite kernel, and
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}}/T_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})
can have a finite torsion subgroup, and we need to kill both of these in order to obtain a map from the universal Euler system into
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})
.

By
Theorem

9.3.1
, the Kolyvagin system
p^{a}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}
is actually 0; and in particular
p^{a}\kappa_{1}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}=p^{a}c_{1}\bmod\mathfrak{Q}
is zero, a contradiction.

Consequently, we deduce that
c_{m}
lies in the kernel of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T)\to H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}[m],T_{\mathfrak{Q}})
for every
m
. Fixing a generator
X_{\mathfrak{Q}}
of
\mathfrak{Q}
, and considering the long exact sequence associated to multiplication by
X_{\mathfrak{Q}}
on
\mathbf{T}
, we conclude that
c_{m}\in X_{\mathfrak{Q}}\cdot H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T)
for all
m
. Since
\mathbf{T}/X_{\mathfrak{Q}}\mathbf{T}
has vanishing
H^{0}
, it follows that multiplication by
X_{\mathfrak{Q}}
is injective on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T)
; so the elements
c_{m}^{\prime}=X_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{-1}c_{m}
are well-defined and satisfy the Euler system norm-compatibility relations. Thus
\mathbf{c}=X_{\mathfrak{Q}}\cdot\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
for an Euler system
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
.
∎

The above method does not work for the height 1 prime ideals above
p
(one for each component of
\mathcal{W}
), since
c_{m}\neq 0\bmod\varpi
does not imply that there is an
\eta
such that
c_{1}^{\eta}\neq 0\bmod\varpi
. So in order to control powers of
p
we shall use a slightly different argument.

Proposition 9.5.3
.

Assume that
H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},(T^{+})^{*}\otimes k_{L})=0
. Suppose that
\mathbf{c}\in\operatorname{ES}(T,\mathcal{K})
satisfies

\loc_{p}(c_{m})\in\ker\Big[H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],T)\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],(T/T^{+})/\varpi^{k})\Big],

for some
k\geqslant 1
and all
m\in\mathcal{R}
. Then there exists an element
\mathcal{S}\in\Lambda
such that
\Lambda/\mathcal{S}\Lambda
is
p
-torsion-free, and for every height 1 prime
not
containing
p
, the Kolyvagin system

\mathcal{S}\cdot\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}\bmod\varpi^{k}\in\overline{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}}/\varpi^{k},\square_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right)

obtained from
\mathcal{S}\cdot\mathbf{c}
is zero.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\mathcal{S}\cdot\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}\bmod\varpi^{k}
is in the image of the map

\overline{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}}/\varpi^{k},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right)\to\overline{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}}/\varpi^{k},\square_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right),

since we have shown above that the left-hand space is 0.

The classes
\kappa_{m}
forming the Kolyvagin system
\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}
are defined as
S_{\mathfrak{Q}}[\Delta_{m}]
-linear combinations of the “derivative” classes
\kappa_{[\mathbf{Q},m,k]}
defined in
[
Rub00
, Chapter IV]
(with
\mathbf{c}
replaced by its twist
\mathbf{c}^{(\mathfrak{Q})}
), so it suffices to prove that these satisfy the local condition at
p
defining
\overline{\KS}\left(T_{\mathfrak{Q}}/\varpi^{k},\square^{+}_{\mathrm{can},\mathfrak{Q}}\right)
: that is, we must show that
\kappa_{[\mathbf{Q},m,k]}
is in the image of the saturation of
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},T_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})
in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},T_{\mathfrak{Q}})
. We shall, in fact, prove the stronger statement that it is in the image of
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},T_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})
.

As in
[
KLZ17
, Proposition 12.2.3]
, we follow the argument of
[
Rub00
, §IV.6]
(in its original form, not modified as in the previous proof). Let
m\in\mathcal{R}
, and write
W_{k}=T_{\mathfrak{Q}}/\varpi^{k}
and
\mathbb{W}_{k}=\operatorname{Maps}(G_{\mathbf{Q}},W_{k})
, and similarly
W_{k}^{+}
and
\mathbb{W}_{k}^{+}
. The localisations at
p
of the Euler system classes
c^{(\mathfrak{Q})}_{n}
for
n\mid m
determine a compatible family of homomorphisms

\mathbf{c}_{F[m],p}:\mathbf{X}_{F[m]}\to H^{1}(F[m]_{p},W_{k})

for each finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}
inside
\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}
, where
\mathbf{X}_{F[m]}
is Rubin’s universal Euler system. As in
op.cit.
, we can lift these to homomorphisms

\mathbf{d}_{F[m],p}:\mathbf{X}_{F[m]}\to(\mathbb{W}_{k}/\Ind_{\mathcal{D}}W_{k})^{G_{F[m]}},

whose image under the boundary map to
H^{1}(F[m]_{p},W_{k})
are the
\mathbf{c}_{F[m],p}
. Each
\mathbf{d}_{F[m],p}
is unique up to an element of
\Hom_{G_{\mathbf{Q}}}(\mathbf{X}_{F[m]},\mathbb{W}_{k})
. Note that this construction relies on the vanishing of
H^{0}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],W_{k})
.

Since
H^{0}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},W_{k}/W_{k}^{+})
is also zero, the homomorphisms
\mathbf{c}_{F[m],p}
have compatible liftings to homomorphisms
\mathbf{c}^{+}_{F[m],p}:\mathbf{X}_{F[m]}\to H^{1}(F[m]_{p},W_{k}^{+})
. Repeating the argument above, we obtain a second system of homomorphisms

\mathbf{d}^{+}_{F[m],p}:\mathbf{X}_{F[m]}\to(\mathbb{W}^{+}_{k}/\Ind_{\mathcal{D}}W^{+}_{k})^{G_{F[m]}},

whose image in
\mathbb{W}_{k}/\Ind_{\mathcal{D}}W_{k}
differs from
\mathbf{d}_{F[m],p}
by an element of
\Hom_{G_{\mathbf{Q}}}(\mathbf{X}_{F[m]},\mathbb{W}_{k})
. As in the proof of
[
Rub00
, Theorem IV.5.1]
, this implies that the localisations at
p
of the Kolyvagin classes
\kappa_{[F,m,k]}
lie in the images of the maps
\mathbf{d}^{+}_{F[m],p}
. In particular, this holds for
F=\mathbf{Q}
; so we have

\loc_{p}\left(\kappa_{[\mathbf{Q},m,k]}\right)\in\operatorname{image}\left(H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},W_{k}^{+})\to H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},W_{k})\right).

In general,
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},W_{k}^{+})
may be larger than the image of
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},T^{+}_{\mathfrak{Q}})
modulo
\varpi^{k}
. However, the obstruction is exactly
H^{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},T^{+}_{\mathfrak{Q}})[\varpi^{k}]
, and by local Tate duality,
H^{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},T^{+}_{\mathfrak{Q}})
is isomorphic to the Pontryagin dual of
H^{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},(T^{+}_{\mathfrak{Q}})^{*}(1)\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})
, where
\wedge
denotes Pontryagin duality. This is a submodule of

H^{0}\left(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},(T^{+})^{*}(1)\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}S_{\mathfrak{Q}}

and since the
\Lambda
-module
H^{0}\left(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},(T^{+})^{*}(1)\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}\right)
is a finite group, we can choose the
\mathcal{S}
above to annihilate it. Thus
\loc_{p}\left(\mathcal{S}\cdot\kappa_{[\mathbf{Q},m,k]}\right)
lies in the image of
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},T_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{+})
in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},W_{k})
, as required.
∎

9.6.
Nested local submodules

We now consider the following situation: we are given two submodules
T^{++}\subset T^{+}\subseteq T
with
\rk_{\mathcal{O}}(T^{+})=d+1
and
\rk_{\mathcal{O}}T^{++}=d
. Then we can apply the preceding theory to either
T^{+}
or
T^{++}
; and we have

\chi(\mathbf{T},\square^{+}_{\Lambda})=1,\qquad\chi(\mathbf{T},\square^{++}_{\Lambda})=0.

We shall denote by
T^{\sharp}
the rank 1 quotient
T^{+}/T^{++}
. We assume that both
T^{+}
and
T^{++}
satisfy
9.5.1
.

Theorem 9.6.1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{c}\in\mathrm{ES}(T,T^{+},\mathcal{K})
, and let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a height 1 prime ideal of
\Lambda
and
k\geqslant 0
. Assume that for all
m\in\mathcal{R}
, the image of
c_{m}
under the composition

\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T;\square^{+})\xrightarrow{\loc_{p}}H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],T^{+})\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],T^{\sharp})

is divisible by
\mathfrak{Q}^{k}
. Then we have the Selmer group bound

\mathfrak{Q}^{k}\cdot\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T;\square^{+})\ \Big|\ \operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}\left(\frac{\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T;\square^{+})}{\Lambda\cdot c_{1}}\right).

Proof.

First let us suppose
p\notin\mathfrak{Q}
. If
k=0
, then
\mathfrak{Q}
and
T^{++}
play no role, and the claim reduces to Corollary 12.3.5 of
[
KLZ17
]
. If
k>0
, then
9.5.2
shows that the entire Euler system is a multiple of
\mathfrak{Q}
, so we may use induction on
k
to reduce to the case
k=0
. Now suppose
\mathfrak{Q}
is of residue characteristic
p
. Since we already know the result after localisation at any prime other than
\mathfrak{Q}
, it suffices to prove the divisibility after localising at
\mathfrak{Q}
. Proposition

shows that after replacing
\mathbf{c}
by
\mathcal{S}\cdot\mathbf{c}
, for some
\mathcal{S}
which is a unit in the localisation at

, the bounds on the Selmer groups of the characteristic 0 specialisations

obtained from
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
are all non-optimal by a factor of

. Chasing this additional information through the proof we obtain the above sharpening of the final result.
∎

Corollary 9.6.2
.

Under the same hypotheses as above, suppose
\lambda
is a homomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules

whose kernel and cokernel are pseudo-null. Then we have

\mathfrak{Q}^{k}\cdot\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T;\square^{++})\ \big|\ \lambda(c_{1}).

Proof.

This follows from

by a standard argument using the exact triangle of Selmer complexes

\widetilde{\operatorname{R\Gamma}}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T;\square^{++})\to\widetilde{\operatorname{R\Gamma}}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},T;\square^{+})\to\operatorname{R\Gamma}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},T^{\sharp})\to[+1],

see e.g.
[

, Theorem 11.6.4]
.
∎

10.
An axiomatic “leading term” argument

We now consider the following general setting:

•

L
is a
p
-adic field.

•

\Omega
is a one-dimensional affinoid disc, and
X
is a uniformiser at a point of
\Omega
, so
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\cong L\langle X\rangle
.

•

\mathcal{M}
is a free module of finite rank over
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
with a continuous action of
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
, unramified outside some set
S\ni p
.

•

\mathcal{R}
denotes the set of square-free products of primes
\ell\notin S
such that
\ell=1\bmod p
.

10.1.
Leading terms of Euler systems

Suppose we are given some compatible collection

c_{m}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}):m\in\mathcal{R},

satisfying the Euler-system norm compatibility relation.

Lemma 10.1.1
.

For each
m\in\mathcal{R}
, we have
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M})[X]=0
.

Proof.

Since
H^{0}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}/X\mathcal{M})=0
the result follows by standard exact sequences.
∎

Definition 10.1.2
.

For
m\in\mathcal{R}
, let
h(m)\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\cup\{\infty\}
be the largest integer
n
such that
c_{m}\in X^{n}\cdot H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M})
; and let
h=\inf_{m\in\mathcal{R}}h(m)
.

We shall assume that
c_{1}\neq 0
, so that
h(1)
and hence
h
are finite. Since
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M})[X]=0
, there is a uniquely determined element
X^{-h}c_{m}\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M})
for every
m\in\mathcal{R}
, and these satisfy the Euler system norm relation (since their images after multiplication by
X^{h}
do, and multiplication by
X^{h}
is an injection).

Definition 10.1.3
.

Let
V=\mathcal{M}/X\mathcal{M}
, and let us write
c^{(h)}_{m}
for the image of
X^{-h}c_{m}
under the reduction map

H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M})/X\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],V).

Note that the injectivity of this reduction map follows from
H^{0}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}/X\mathcal{M})=0
. By the definition of
h
, there exists
m\in\mathcal{R}
such that
c^{(h)}_{m}\neq 0
. Evidently, the classes
c^{(h)}_{m}
satisfy the Euler-system norm relations for
\mathcal{M}/X\mathcal{M}
.

Proposition 10.1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{+}(\Omega)\cong\mathcal{O}\langle X\rangle
denote the subring of power-bounded elements of
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
, and suppose that there is a finitely-generated
\mathcal{O}^{+}(\Omega)
-submodule
\mathcal{M}^{+}\subset\mathcal{M}
(independent of
m
) which is stable under
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
and such that we have

c_{m}\in\operatorname{image}\left(H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}^{+})\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M})\right)\,\forall m.

Then we can find a
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
T\subset V
such that

c_{m}^{(h)}\in\operatorname{image}\left(H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T)\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],V)\right)\,\forall m.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathcal{M}^{+}/X\mathcal{M}^{+}
is
p
-torsion-free (by replacing
\mathcal{M}^{+}
with its reflexive hull, which is also finitely generated and
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
-stable, and using the fact that a reflexive
\mathcal{O}^{+}(X)
-module is free).

Let us temporarily write
\mathcal{H}^{+}=H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}^{+})
and similarly
\mathcal{H}=H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}(\mu_{m}),\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{H}[1/p]
. The long exact sequence associated to multiplication by
X^{h}
on
\mathcal{M}^{+}
gives an injection

\mathcal{H}^{+}/X^{h}\mathcal{H}^{+}\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}^{+}/X^{h}\mathcal{M}^{+}),

and the latter module is
p
-torsion-free, since
\mathcal{M}^{+}/X^{h}\mathcal{M}^{+}
is a finitely-generated free
\mathcal{O}
-module. So
\mathcal{H}^{+}/X^{h}\mathcal{H}^{+}
is also
p
-torsion-free.

Since
c_{m}\in X^{h}\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{H}^{+}
, it follows that the class of
x
in
\mathcal{H}^{+}/X^{h}\mathcal{H}^{+}
is
p
-torsion. From the above, we can conclude that in fact
c_{m}\in X^{h}\mathcal{H}^{+}
. Thus
X^{-h}c_{m}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}^{+})
, and we may take
T
to be the image of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
in
\mathcal{M}/X\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Since
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}[m],T)
is
p
-torsion-free, and the
c_{m}^{(h)}
satisfy the Euler system norm relations after inverting
p
, it follows that
c_{m}^{(h)}
are an Euler system for
(\mathcal{R},T)
. By construction, this is not the zero Euler system (that is,
c_{m}^{(h)}\neq 0
for some
m
, although we cannot at this point rule out the possibility that
c_{1}^{(h)}=0
).

10.2.
Local conditions at p (I)

Since the classes
c_{m}^{(h)}
lie in Iwasawa cohomology, they automatically satisfy the unramified local conditions at all primes away from
p
. However, local conditions at
p
are a more delicate question.

We suppose that the following conditions hold:

•

\mathcal{M}
has even rank
2d
, and the complex-conjugation eigenspaces
\mathcal{M}^{(c_{\infty}=+1)}
and
\mathcal{M}^{(c_{\infty}=-1)}
both have rank
d
.

•

There exist saturated
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
-submodules
\mathcal{M}^{++}\subset\mathcal{M}^{+}\subset\mathcal{M}
, all stable under
G_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
, such that
\mathcal{M}^{++}
has rank
d
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}
has rank
d+1
.

•

For each
m
we have

\loc_{p}(c_{m})\in\operatorname{image}\Big(H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}^{+})\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],\mathcal{M})\Big).

Notation
.

We let
V^{+}
and
V^{++}
denote the images of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
and
\mathcal{M}^{++}
in
V
.

Proposition 10.2.1
.

We have
\loc_{p}\left(c_{m}^{(h)}\right)\in\operatorname{image}\left(H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],V^{+})\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],V)\right)
.

Proof.

Let us write
d_{m}=X^{-h}c_{m}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}(\mu_{mp^{\infty}}),\mathcal{M})
. Then the image of
\loc_{p}(d_{m})
in the local Iwasawa cohomology of
\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{+}
is annihilated by
X^{h}
. However, we have

H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\left(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{+}\right)[X^{h}]=0,

since it is a quotient of
H^{0}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\left(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{+}\right)=0
. So
\loc_{p}(d_{m})
takes values in
\mathcal{M}^{+}
, and hence its reduction modulo
X
lands in
V^{+}
.
∎

So we have constructed an Euler system for
T
with the Greenberg-type Selmer structure
\square_{\Lambda}^{+}
determined by
\mathbf{T}^{+}=\Lambda\otimes T^{+}
; compare
[
KLZ17
, §12]
.

Proposition 10.2.2
.

Suppose that conditions (H.0)–(H.4) of
[
MR04
]
are satisfied by
T
and also by
T(\eta)
, for every
m\in\mathcal{R}
and every
\eta:\Delta_{m}\to\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\times}
. Then there exists an
m
such that the image of
c_{m}^{(h)}
in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],V^{+}/V^{++})
is non-zero.

Proof.

By construction, the
c_{m}^{(h)}
are not all zero. So if
c_{m}^{(h)}
lands locally in
V^{++}
for every
m
, then
(c_{m}^{(h)})_{m\in\mathcal{R}}
is a non-zero element of the module
\operatorname{ES}(T,T^{++},\mathcal{K})
in the notation of
Proposition

9.4.2
. Since
T^{++}
has rank
d
, the proposition shows that
\operatorname{ES}(T,T^{++},\mathcal{K})=\{0\}
, a contradiction.
∎

10.3.
Regulators at p

We now focus our attention on the subquotient
\mathcal{M}^{\sharp}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}^{+}/\mathcal{M}^{++}
. In the examples which interest us, there is some character
\tau:\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega)^{\times}
such that
\mathcal{M}^{\sharp}(\tau)
is an unramified character. Hence we can make sense of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{M}^{\sharp})
, as a free module of rank 1 over
\Omega
. Then we have the
\Delta_{m}
-equivariant
Perrin-Riou regulator
map associated to
\mathcal{M}^{\sharp}
,

\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\mathcal{M}^{\sharp},\Delta_{m}}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],\mathcal{M}^{\sharp})\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{M}^{\sharp})\mathop{\hat{\otimes}}\Lambda\otimes L[\Delta_{m}].

This is compatible under reduction modulo
X
with the regulator map
\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\Delta_{m}}
for the 1-dimensional
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-linear representation
V^{\sharp}=V^{+}/V^{++}
(cf.
Definition

5.4.1
). We note that the mod
X
map

\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{V^{\sharp},\Delta_{m}}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty}[m],V^{\sharp})\to\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V^{\sharp})\mathop{\hat{\otimes}}\Lambda\otimes L[\Delta_{m}]

has kernel equal to the torsion subgroup of

. We shall suppose that

, so that
\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{V^{\sharp},\Delta_{m}}
is injective and its cokernel is pseudo-null.

Remark 10.3.1
.

In general the map
\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\mathcal{M}^{\sharp},\Delta_{m}}
and
\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{V^{\sharp},\Delta_{m}}
can have poles at certain characters of
\Gamma
; but these poles only appear if
\operatorname{Frob}_{p}=1
on the unramified twist of
\mathcal{M}^{\sharp}
(resp.

) and this is also ruled out by the above assumption.

Assumption 10.3.2
.

There exists a collection of elements

\mathcal{L}_{m}\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\mathop{\hat{\otimes}}\Lambda\otimes L[\Delta_{m}]\quad\text{for all $m\in\mathcal{R}$},

and a section

\xi\in\operatorname{Frac}\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\Omega)}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{M}^{\sharp})^{\vee},

such that

•

\mathcal{L}_{m}\bmod X
is non-zero as an element of
\Lambda\otimes L[\Delta_{m}]
, for some
m
.

•

For every
m
, we have

\langle\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\mathcal{M},\Delta_{m}}\left(\loc_{p}c_{m}\right),\xi\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{m}.

Theorem 10.3.3
.

Under the above hypotheses, we necessarily have

\mathrm{ord}_{X}\left(\xi\right)=-h,

where
h
is as defined above; and if
\bar{\xi}=X^{h}\xi\bmod X
, then
\bar{\xi}
is a basis of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V^{\sharp})^{\vee}
, and we have

\left\langle\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{V,\Delta_{m}}\left(\loc_{p}c_{m}^{(h)}\right),\bar{\xi}\right\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{m}\bmod X.

Proof.

Replacing every
c_{m}
with
X^{-h}c_{m}
, and
\xi
with
X^{h}\xi
, we may assume that
h=0
. Thus
c_{m}^{(h)}=c_{m}\bmod X
.

Hence, if
\Xi
is a local basis of
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{M}^{\sharp})^{\vee}
around
X
, then
\mathcal{L}_{m}^{\flat}=\langle\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\mathcal{M}^{\sharp},\Delta_{m}}(c_{m}),\Xi\rangle
is regular at
X=0
, and its value modulo
X
is
\left\langle\mathscr{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{V^{\sharp},,\Delta_{m}}(c_{m}\bmod X),\Xi\bmod X\right\rangle
, which is non-zero for some
m
, by
Proposition

10.2.2
and the injectivity of the regulator map.

Scaling
\Xi
by a unit in the localisation
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)_{(X)}
we can arrange that
\xi=X^{t}\Xi
for some
t\in\mathbf{Z}
, so that
\mathcal{L}_{m}=X^{t}\mathcal{L}_{m}^{\flat}
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is regular at
X=0
for all
m
, we must have

. On the other hand, if
t>0
then
\mathcal{L}_{m}\bmod X
is zero for all
m
, which contradicts the assumptions.
∎

We now explain the outcome of the above process in our twisted-Yoshida setting. Recall that by the end of

Theorem 11.1.1

For each sign

Remark 11.1.2
.

The formulation adopted above is heavily influenced by the work of Kato for

[

, Theorem 12.5]
. Note that the curious “switch” of signs – that the

Proof.

We now indicate how to extract the above results from the general theory of
Section

10
.

We begin by noting that if a collection

satisfying the conditions exists, then it is unique, since otherwise we would obtain a non-zero Euler system for a Selmer structure of Euler characteristic 0, and (as we have seen in the previous section) this is not possible.

Let

be any integer, and
c>1
coprime to
6pN
. Then for
\epsilon=(-1)^{t_{2}+r+1}
we have a family of classes

{}_{c}z_{m}^{[\underline{\pi},r]}\in\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty},\mathcal{V}(\underline{\pi})^{*};\square^{(\mathfrak{p}_{1})})^{\epsilon}.

Given bases
v_{+},v_{-}
of

, we choose the

and

of the previous section as follows. We set
\mathcal{L}_{m}={}_{c}C_{m}^{[r]}\cdot\langle\mathcal{L}^{\epsilon}_{p}(\underline{\pi}),v_{\epsilon}\rangle
. We may assume that there is some

is non-zero (otherwise the conclusion of the theorem is satisfied with

taken as the zero map).

We define

This is independent of

and depends linearly on

is satisfied; so we can “run the machine” and obtain a family of classes

, and a non-zero vector

By construction, as bases of

modulo

where

We define

12.3.
The Bloch–Kato conjecture

Theorem 12.3.1
.

Let

be a Deligne-critical integer and
\chi
a finite-order character of
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Then we have

, then the Bloch–Kato Selmer group

This follows from

12.2.2
using the base-change properties of Selmer complexes, since the Bloch–Kato local condition for twists in the critical range coincides with the Greenberg local condition
\square^{(p)}
.
∎

Note that the special case when

,

, and

]

13.3.3.
Selmer groups

We refer to §2.5 of
op.cit.
for the definition of a module
X_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{+}}^{\Sigma}(T(\pi))
over
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(\Gamma^{1})
, which is the Pontryagin dual of a Selmer group for
T(\pi)(1)\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}
over
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{+}=\mathcal{K}K_{\infty}^{1}
. We want to compare this with the Selmer complexes used above.

Notation
.

In this section when we use Selmer complexes
\widetilde{\operatorname{R\Gamma}}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(-)
, the local condition will always be
\square^{(p)}
, and we omit it from the notation.

Proposition 13.3.3
.

The dual Selmer group
X_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{+}}^{\Sigma}(T(\pi))
has the same characteristic ideal as the direct sum

e_{0}\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty},T(\pi)^{*})\oplus e_{0}\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty},T(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})^{*}).

Proof.

Since
\Ind_{\mathcal{K}}^{K}(T(\pi)^{*}|_{G_{\mathcal{K}}})=T(\pi)^{*}\oplus T(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})^{*}
, we have

\widetilde{\operatorname{R\Gamma}}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{+},T(\pi)^{*})=e_{0}\widetilde{\operatorname{R\Gamma}}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty},T(\pi)^{*})\oplus\widetilde{\operatorname{R\Gamma}}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty},T(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})^{*}).

However, a standard argument using Poitou–Tate duality for Selmer complexes shows that there is an exact sequence

0\to X_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{+}}^{\Sigma}(T(\pi))\to\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{+},T(\pi)^{*})\to(\text{local $H^{2}$ terms})

where the local
H^{2}
terms are finite groups, and hence pseudo-null.
∎

13.3.4.
Wan’s theorem

Theorem 13.3.4

(Wan)
.

Assume the hypotheses of §
12
and §
13.1
hold. Then we have

e_{0}\cdot L_{p}(\pi)\cdot L_{p}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})\ \Big|\ \Char_{e_{0}\Lambda}\left(X_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{+}}^{\Sigma}(T(\pi))\right)

as ideals of
\Lambda[1/p]
. If in addition

, then this divisibility holds integrally, i.e. as ideals of
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]

Proof.

As in
[

, Corollary 3.28]
, it suffices to prove the statement after replacing

(replacing the

Remark 13.3.5
.

Note that if

we cannot rule out the possibility that
e_{0}L_{p}(\pi)L_{p}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})
might vanish identically (although we do not expect this to happen). If this occurs, then the theorem shows that

has positive rank.

13.4.
The Main Conjecture

Theorem 13.4.1
.

Assume the hypotheses of §
12
and §
13.1
hold. If
k=2
, suppose also that
w_{\pi}=+1
. Then we have

\Char\left(e_{0}\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty},T(\pi)^{*})\right)=\Big(e_{0}L_{p}(\pi)\Big)

as ideals of
e_{0}\Lambda_{L}(\Gamma)
. If in addition
\pi
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
13.2.2
, then this holds as an equality of ideals of
e_{0}\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let us write
L_{p}^{\alg}(\pi)
for a characteristic element of the
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{Iw}}
(well-defined up to units). Applying Theorem
12.2.2
to both
\pi
and
\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}}
, we have

e_{0}L_{p}^{\alg}(\pi)\mid e_{0}L_{p}(\pi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{0}L_{p}^{\alg}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})\mid e_{0}L_{p}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}}).

However, we also have

e_{0}L_{p}(\pi)L_{p}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})\mid e_{0}L_{p}^{\alg}(\pi)L_{p}^{\alg}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})

by Wan’s theorem. (Here these are to be understood as divisibilities in the Iwasawa algebra over
\mathcal{O}
if the hypotheses of Theorem
13.2.2
hold, and otherwise after inverting

.)

Taken together, these formulae imply that one of the following must hold:

•

e_{0}L_{p}^{\alg}(\pi)=e_{0}L_{p}(\pi)
up to units in
e_{0}\Lambda
;

•

e_{0}L_{p}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})=0
.

The latter cannot occur if

by Proposition
3.2.2
. If
k=2
and the global root number of
L(\pi,s)
is +1, then by Waldspurger’s theorem
[
Wal91
, Theorem 4]
we may suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is chosen such that
L(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}},1)\neq 0
, which also implies
e_{0}L_{p}(\pi\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}})\neq 0
.
∎

14.
Applications to non-vanishing

We note here a by-product of our method, which is the following “horizontal” non-vanishing theorem for central
L
-values. Let
K
,
\pi
and

be as in §

.

14.1.
Non-vanishing in characteristic 0

Proposition 14.1.1
.

Suppose
w
is even, and let
\chi
be a finite-order character of
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Suppose equality holds in Theorem
12.3.1
for
\chi
and
j=\tfrac{w-2}{2}
, i.e. for this

we have

\mathrm{ord}_{j+\chi}L_{p}(\pi)=\dim H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},V(\pi)^{*}(-j-\chi)).

There exists an

and

such that
L(\pi\otimes\eta^{-1}\chi^{-1},\tfrac{w}{2})\neq 0
.

Proof.

If
L(\pi\otimes\eta^{-1}\chi^{-1},1+j)=0
for all

, then we can apply

9.6.1
with

the ideal corresponding to

, and some

. This gives a strict inequality in Theorem

, which contradicts the hypothesis.
∎

In particular, if the stronger hypotheses of §

are satisfied, and

is not identically 0 (which is automatic if

), then the hypothesis of the theorem follows from

, so the non-vanishing result follows for every

.

Remark 14.1.2
.

(1)

The assumption that

is somewhat inconvenient: we are, essentially, assuming one kind of non-vanishing statement in order to deduce another. It may be possible to remove this assumption by considering leading terms along a Hida family as in
Section

. We hope to return to this in a future paper.

(2)

It is already known, by results of Rohrlich
[

]
, that one can always find a twist of

by a Hecke character of

such that the central

\mathbf{Q}
.

15.
A numerical example

We illustrate our results with the first example of an elliptic curve over a real quadratic field in the LMFDB database
[
lmf
]
: the elliptic curve
A
over
K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{5})
given by

y^{2}+xy+\phi y=x^{3}+\left(\phi+1\right)x^{2}+\phi x
, where
\phi=\tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
. The LMFDB entry for this curve, at
https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/2.2.5.1/31.1/a/1
, shows that its conductor is the prime
\mathfrak{q}=(5\phi-2)
of norm 31. So for any prime
p\neq 31
congruent to
\pm 1\bmod 5
,
p
will split in
K
and
A
will have good reduction at the primes above
p
. Moreover,
A
has rank 0, and

L(A,1)\sim 0.3599289594\dots.

The database also confirms that
A
is not a
\mathbf{Q}
-curve, and that
\rho_{A,p}
is surjective onto
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
for all
p\neq 2
. Since the only nontrivial proper normal subgroup of
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
for
p>3
is
\{\pm 1\}
, if
p>3
then we must either have

(\rho_{A,p}\times\rho_{A^{\sigma},p})(G_{K})=\{(x,y)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}):\det(x)=\det(y)\},

or

(\rho_{A,p}\times\rho_{A^{\sigma},p})(G_{K})\subseteq\{(x,y)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}):x=\pm y\bmod p\}.

If
p\neq 59
and
\gamma
is the Frobenius at the prime
\mathfrak{q}=7\phi-2
of norm 59, then we have
\operatorname{tr}\rho_{A,p}(\gamma)=a_{\mathfrak{q}}(A)=12
, but
\operatorname{tr}\rho_{A^{\sigma},p}(\gamma)=a_{\sigma(\mathfrak{q})}(A)=-4
. Since the only prime dividing
12\pm(-4)
is 2, we conclude that
\rho_{A,p}\times\rho_{A^{\sigma},p}
is surjective for all primes
p>2
(except possibly
p=59
, but we can eliminate this possibility by performing a similar computation with the primes above 61). So
\rho_{A,p}\times\rho_{A^{\sigma},p}
has full image for all primes
p>2
.

To verify the additional “minimality” hypothesis of Theorem
13.2.2
, we need to check that
\bar{\rho}_{A,p}
is ramified at the prime
\mathfrak{q}
. The primes where this fails are those which divide the valuation of the
j
-invariant at
\mathfrak{q}
; since the
j
-invariant has valuation
-1
, there are no such primes, and hence this condition is automatically satisfied for all
p
.

Hence the Iwasawa main conjecture for
A
over the cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
holds for all primes
p\geqslant 7
that split in
K
and such that
A
has good ordinary reduction above
p
. This includes all primes
p<200
with
p=\pm 1\bmod 5
, except 71, 79 and 191.

Appendix A
Beilinson–Flach elements revisited

A.1.
Setting

In this brief appendix we record a consequence of the “leading term argument” of
Sections

9
and
10
above, which is a refinement of our earlier results with Kings
[
KLZ17
]
on the arithmetic of Rankin–Selberg convolutions. For simplicity we state the results for Artin twists of elliptic curves, although the arguments apply more generally to Artin twists of any modular form of weight
\geqslant 2
.

Let
A
be a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
, and
\rho
a 2-dimensional odd irreducible Artin representation, with the conductors
N_{A}
and
N_{\rho}
coprime. Bertolini, Darmon and Rotger have shown in
[
BDR15
]
that the equivariant BSD conjecture holds for
E
and
\rho
in analytic rank 0: that is, we have the implication

L(A,\rho^{*},1)\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Hom_{\Gal(F/\mathbf{Q})}\left(\rho,A(F)\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}}\right)=0,

where
F
is the fixed field of the kernel of
\rho
. Our aim here is to give bounds for the
\rho
-part of the Tate–Shafarevich group.

A.2.
The result

Let
A
and
\rho
be as above, and assume that
L(A,\rho^{*},1)\neq 0
.

We let
F
be the coefficient field of
\rho
; and we choose a rational prime
p
and a prime
v\mid p
of
F
, and let
L
be the completion of
F
at
v
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
. We suppose
v
is chosen such that:

•

p\geqslant 5
;

•

p\nmid N_{A}N_{\rho}
;

•

A
is ordinary at
p
;

•

the
\mathcal{O}
-linear Galois representation
T=T_{p}(A)\otimes\rho
satisfies the big-image hypothesis 11.2.1 of
[
KLZ17
]
.

These are the same hypotheses as in
[
KLZ17
, Theorem 11.7.4]
with one crucial difference: we are
not
supposing here that the eigenvalues of
\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
be distinct mod
v
. So our present hypotheses apply for all but finitely many ordinary primes, while the the hypotheses of
op.cit.
rule out a positive-density set of primes (those which split completely in the subfield of
F
cut out by the projective representation
\operatorname{Ad}(\rho)
) for which
\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is scalar.

We define

\Sha_{v^{\infty}}(A,\rho)=\Hom_{\Gal(F/\mathbf{Q})}\left(\rho,\Sha_{p^{\infty}}(A/F)\otimes\mathcal{O}\right).

Theorem A.2.1
.

The group
\Sha_{v^{\infty}}(A,\rho)
is finite. If
p
does not divide
[F:\mathbf{Q}]
, its order is bounded by

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Sha_{v^{\infty}}(A,\rho)\leqslant\operatorname{ord}_{v}\left(\frac{L(E\times\rho^{*},1)}{\Omega^{+}_{A}\Omega^{-}_{A}G(\det\rho^{*})}\right).

In particular,
\Sha_{v^{\infty}}(A,\rho)
is trivial for all but finitely many ordinary primes
v
.

Proof.

Since we know that the
\rho
-part of the Mordell–Weil group is trivial, there is a natural map

H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},A[p^{\infty}]\otimes\rho^{*})=\Hom_{\Gal(F/\mathbf{Q})}(\rho,\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F)\otimes\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow\Sha_{v^{\infty}}(A,\rho).

The cokernel of this map injects into
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Gal(F/\mathbf{Q})}\left(\rho,A(F)\otimes L/\mathcal{O}\right)
; so it is finite, and trivial if
p
does not divide
[F:\mathbf{Q}]
. So it suffices to bound
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},A[p^{\infty}]\otimes\rho^{*})
.

Let
g
be the weight 1 newform corresponding to
\rho^{*}
. This must have at least one
p
-stabilisation
g_{\alpha}
(extending
L
if necessary). By results of Hida and Wiles recalled in
[
KLZ17
, §7.6]
,
g_{\alpha}
defines a point on the
\GL_{2}
ordinary eigencurve
\mathcal{C}(N_{\rho})
. As in §7.5 of op.cit., the space
\mathcal{C}(N_{\rho})
has finitely many irreducible components (“branches”)
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}
, corresponding to the minimal primes
\mathbf{a}
of the ordinary Hecke algebra. In general there can be several components passing through
g_{\alpha}
, and the irreducible components need not be smooth there; but we shall choose a branch
\mathbf{a}
passing through
g_{\alpha}
, and a point
x\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{a}}(L)
lifting
g_{\alpha}
(after replacing
L
by a finite extension if necessary). The analytic generic fibre of
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbf{a}}
is a smooth rigid-analytic curve, so we can choose a neighbourhood
\Omega\ni x
which is an affinoid disc.

Hida theory gives us a rank 2
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
-module
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{a})
with a
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
-action, interpolating the Galois representations of the specialisations of the Hida family
\mathfrak{a}
. Exactly as in §
8
, we can factor out the torsion in order to obtain a family of free modules, and by comparing traces, the fibre of this family at
x
is isomorphic to
\rho
. We let
\mathcal{M}
be the 4-dimensional family given by tensoring
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{a})^{*}
with
V_{p}(A)=T_{p}(A)[1/p]
. This gives a family of representations specialising to
V_{p}(A)\otimes\rho^{*}
, and it has subrepresentations

\mathcal{M}^{++}=\mathcal{F}^{+}V_{p}(A)\otimes\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{a})^{*},\qquad\mathcal{M}^{+}=\mathcal{M}^{++}+V_{p}(A)\otimes\mathcal{F}^{+}\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{a})^{*}

of rank 2 and rank 3 respectively, where
\mathcal{F}^{+}\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{a})^{*}
and
\mathcal{F}^{+}V_{p}(A)
are rank 1
G_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
-stable submodules with unramified quotient.

We now apply the theory of
Section

10
to
\mathcal{M}
, taking the Euler system
(c_{m})
to be the Beilinson–Flach elements associated to
f_{A}
and the family through
g_{\alpha}
as in
[
KLZ17
]
. By construction, these classes land in
\mathcal{M}^{+}
locally at
p
, and the reciprocity law for Beilinson–Flach elements (Theorem B of
op.cit.
) shows that their images in the quotient
\mathcal{M}^{+}/\mathcal{M}^{++}
are governed by the
L
-values of Dirichlet-character twists of
A\times\rho
. Exactly as in Theorem
12.2.2
above, we deduce that the dual Selmer group for
T_{p}(A)\otimes\rho^{*}
over the cyclotomic extension is bounded above by the
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}(A\times\rho^{*})
, where we can form our
p
-adic
L
-functions using any period which makes the equivariant

-adic

L_{p}(A\times\rho^{*},\Delta_{m})
be

. It follows readily from Hida’s construction of
p
-adic Rankin–Selberg

\Omega^{+}_{A}\Omega^{-}_{A}G(\det\rho^{*})
has this property. By a standard descent computation, we deduce the above bound for the Selmer group over
\mathbf{Q}
.
∎

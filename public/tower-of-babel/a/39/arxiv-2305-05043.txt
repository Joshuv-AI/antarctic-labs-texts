Hyperbolic Anderson model with time-independent rough noise: Gaussian fluctuations
Raluca M. Balan, Wangjun Yuan

Abstract
In this article, we study the hyperbolic Anderson model in dimension 1, driven by a time-independent rough noise, i.e. the noise associated with the fractional Brownian motion of Hurst index H ∈ ( 1 / 4 , 1 / 2 ) H\in(1/4,1/2) . We prove that, with appropriate normalization and centering, the spatial integral of the solution converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution, and we estimate the speed of this convergence in the total variation distance. We also prove the corresponding functional limit result. Our method is based on a version of the second-order Gaussian Poincaré inequality developed recently in [ 27 ] , and relies on delicate moment estimates for the increments of the first and second Malliavin derivatives of the solution. These estimates are obtained using a connection with the wave equation with delta initial velocity, a method which is different than the one used in [ 27 ] for the parabolic Anderson model.

Introduction

The study of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) using the random field approach originates in Walsh’ lecture notes
[
32
]
, which introduced the general framework, focusing mostly on equations driven by space-time Gaussian white noise in dimension 1.
In the seminal article
[
13
]
, Dalang extended the martingale measure method of Walsh to equations driven by spatially homogeneous Gaussian noise (white in time), and introduced powerful techniques for analyzing these equations.
Since then, this area has been growing at an accelerated pace. One of the tools that has been used extensively is Malliavin calculus. This tool is especially useful when the noise is colored in time (or time-independent), and Itô calculus techniques cannot be used, due to a lack of martingale structures. We refer the reader to

[
7
,
10
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
16
,
19
,
20
,
17
,
18
,
24
,
31
]

for a small sample of relevant contributions related to SPDEs with various types of Gaussian noise.

In
[
21
]
, a new line of investigations has been opened up in this area, focusing on the asymptotic behaviour of the spatial integral of the solution of the stochastic heat equation with space-time Gaussian white noise, as the size of the integration region becomes large. The main result of
[
21
]
states that, with suitable normalization and centering, this integral converges to the standard normal distribution, and gives the speed of this convergence in the total variation distance. This result, called the “Quantitative Central Limit Theorem” (QCLT) is obtained by combining Malliavin calculus with Stein’s method for normal approximations. Similar results and extensions have been obtained in the subsequent papers
[
22
,
28
,
27
,
6
]
for the solution of the stochastic heat equation with colored noise in space/time (or with time-independent noise), respectively in
[
12
,
15
,
4
,
5
]
for the solution of the stochastic wave equation. In both cases, the noise enters the equation multiplied by a Lipschitz function
\sigma(u)
of the solution.
The most difficult case is when the noise is
rough in space
, i.e. it behaves in space like the fractional Brownian motion (fBm) with Hurst index
H<1/2
. This case has been studied in
[
27
]
for the
parabolic Anderson model
(pAm), the stochastic heat equation with a linear term
\sigma(u)=u
multiplying the noise, using technical arguments that rely heavily on properties of the heat kernel.

The goal of the present article is to present the first study of this problem for the wave equation with rough noise in space. We will assume that the noise is time-independent, and we postpone the treatment of the time-dependent noise for future work. More precisely, in this article we consider the
hyperbolic Anderson model
(hAm) in dimension 1:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)+\sqrt{\theta}u(t,x)\dot{W}(x),\ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=1,\ \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0.\end{cases}

(1)

Often, we are interested in the case
\theta=1
. The reason we introduce a parameter
\theta>0
is the following. Our main result, the QCLT for the spatial average of the solution
u_{\theta}
of (
1
), is obtained by applying a version of the second-order Gaussian Poincaré inequality (Proposition 2.4 of
[
27
]
). For this, we need to estimate the
fourth
moment of the increments of the Malliavin derivative
Du_{\theta}
and of the rectangular increments of the second Malliavin derivative
D^{2}u_{\theta}
. The fact that we include a parameter
\theta>0
in equation (
1
) allows us to compare the
p
-th moment (for
p>2
) of the solution
u_{\theta}
, of its Malliavian derivatives, or of their increments, with the
second
moment of the similar quantities corresponding to the solution
u_{(p-1)\theta}
, which are then treated using their chaos expansions. This comparison between moments plays a crucial role in the present article, and is derived using a hypercontractivity property for general SPDEs, which is of independent interest and is included in Appendix
A
.

The noise
W
is time-independent and fractional in space with Hurst index
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, being given by a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\}
, defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, with covariance

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=c_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\xi)}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi=:\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}.

Here

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}\varphi(x)dx
is the Fourier transform of
\varphi
, and

c_{H}=\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)\sin(\pi H)}{2\pi}.

By approximation, the noise can be extended to an isonormal Gaussian process
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{0}\}
, as defined in Malliavin calculus, where
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is the Hilbert space defined as completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}
. In particular,
1_{[0,x]}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and the process
\{W(x)=W(1_{[0,x]});x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a fBm of index
H
.

We say that a process
u_{\theta}=\{u_{\theta}(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a (mild Skorohod)
solution
to equation (
1
) if it satisfies the following integral equation:

u_{\theta}(t,x)=1+\sqrt{\theta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y)u_{\theta}(s,y)W(\delta y)ds

where the
W(\delta y)
corresponds to the divergence operator
\delta
(or Skorohod integral) defined in Section
2.2
below, and
G_{t}
is the fundamental solution of the wave equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle G_{t}(x):=\dfrac{1}{2}1_{\{|x|<t\}},\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

We let
G_{t}(x)=0
when
t\leq 0
.
Note that the Fourier transform of
G_{t}
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}G_{t}(x)dx=\dfrac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|},\quad t\geq 0.

(3)

We are interested on the asymptotic behaviour as
R\to\infty
of the spatial average:

F_{R,\theta}(t)=\int_{-R}^{R}\big(u_{\theta}(t,x)-1\big)dx.

More precisely, letting
\sigma_{R,\theta}^{2}(t)={\rm Var}\big(F_{R,\theta}(t)\big)
, we would like to show that

\frac{F_{R,\theta}(t)}{\sigma_{R,\theta}(t)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Z\sim N(0,1)\quad\mbox{as $R\to\infty$},

and to give an estimate for the speed of this convergence in the total variation distance:

d_{\rm TV}(X,Y)=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})}|\mathbb{P}(X\in B)-\mathbb{P}(Y\in B)|.

The following theorems are the main results of this article.

Theorem 1.1

(Limiting Covariance)
.

For any
\theta>0
,
t>0
and
s>0

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[F_{R,\theta}(t)F_{R,\theta}(s)]}{R}=K_{\theta}(t,s),

(4)

where
K_{\theta}(t,s)
is finite and is given by (
65
) below. Moreover, for any
\theta>0
and
t>0
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\sigma_{R,\theta}^{2}(t)}{R}=K_{\theta}(t,t)>0.

(5)

Theorem 1.2

(QCLT)
.

For any
\theta>0
and
t>0
,

d_{\rm TV}\left(\frac{F_{R,\theta}(t)}{\sigma_{R,\theta}(t)},Z\right)\leq C_{t,H,\theta}R^{-1/2},

where
C_{t,H,\theta}>0
is a constant depending on
(t,H,\theta)
and
Z\sim N(0,1)
.

Theorem 1.3

(FCLT)
.

For any
\theta>0
, the process
\{R^{-1/2}F_{R,\theta}(t);t\geq 0\}
has a continuous modification which converges in distribution in
C([0,\infty))
as
R\to\infty
, to a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{\mathcal{G}_{\theta}(t);t\geq 0\}
with covariance

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}_{\theta}(t)\mathcal{G}_{\theta}(s)]=K_{\theta}(t,s),

where
C([0,\infty))
is equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
we use the Wiener chaos decomposition of
F_{R,\theta}
, by observing that the projection on the first chaos space does not contribute to the limit, while the projections on the other chaos spaces give rise to a convergent series. A similar phenomena has been observed in
[
26
]
for (pAm) with rough noise in space, that is colored in time. This is in contrast with the QCLT for equations with “regular” noise in space (studied in
[
28
,
27
,
5
]
) for which only the projection on the first chaos space contributes to the limit. In both cases, the QCLT is
non-chaotic
, in the sense described on page 3 of
[
15
]
, which means that not all the projections on the chaos spaces contribute to the limit.

Theorem
1.2
follows by applying a version of the second-order Gaussian Poincaré inequality
(Proposition 2.4 of
[
27
]
) for the time-independent noise. This leads to a study of the increments of
Du_{\theta}
, as well as the rectangular increments of
D^{2}u_{\theta}
. Analyzing these increments requires significant effort. For this task, we use a different method than in
[
27
]
for (pAm). In addition to the moment comparison technique mentioned above, we develop a connection with the solution
v_{\theta}
of (hAm) with delta initial velocity. This introduces a new problem, which leads us to examine the increments of
u_{\theta}
and
v_{\theta}
. A key role in this analysis is played by some translation-invariance properties of these processes. In addition, we exploit the fact that the increments of
v_{\theta}
are closely related to those of the solution
V_{\theta}
of (hAm) driven by a Gaussian noise
\mathfrak{X}
, which is
white in time
and fractional in space with the same index
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
. The covariance of
\mathfrak{X}
is given by (
33
) below.

For the proof of Theorem
1.3
, as usually, we need to show two things : (i) tightness; and (ii) finite-dimensional convergence. (i) follows by Kolmogorov-Centsov theorem, while (ii) follows using the same method as in
[
5
]
, based on a variance estimation that is proved using the same arguments as for the QCLT.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we include the background and some preliminary results from Malliavin calculus, and we prove the existence of the solution
u_{\theta}
. In Section
3
, we examine some properties of
v_{\theta}
and
V_{\theta}
.
In Section
4
, we derive some estimates for the increments of
Du_{\theta}
and the rectangular increments of
D^{2}u_{\theta}
. The increments of the processes
u_{\theta}
and
v_{\theta}
are studied in Section
5
. Finally, in Section
6
, we present the proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
.
The moment comparison result for a general SPDE is included
in Appendix
A
. In Appendix
B
, we study two stochastic Volterra equations driven by
\mathfrak{X}
, which are of the same form as the equations satisfied by the increments of
V_{\theta}
, and allow us to derive some properties of these increments. Appendix
C
contains some elementary inequalities which are used in the sequel.

2.1
Multiple Wiener integrals

We start by introduce some basic ingredients of Malliavin calculus, following
[
25
]
.

We denote by
J_{n}
the projection from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
\mathcal{P}_{0,n}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{0,n}
is the
n
-th Wiener chaos space, defined at the closed linear span in
L^{2}(\Omega)
of
\{H_{n}(W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{0})\}
, with
H_{n}(x)
being the
n
-th Hermite polynomial.
Let
I_{n}:\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}\to\mathcal{P}_{0,n}
be
the multiple Wiener integral of order
n
with respect to
W
,
where
\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}
is the
n
-th tensor product of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Since
I_{n}
is surjective, for any
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
J_{n}(F)=I_{n}(f_{n})

for some
f_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}
.

The Wiener chaos spaces
\mathcal{P}_{0,n}
are orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. More precisely, for any
f\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}
and
g\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes m}
,

E[I_{n}(f)I_{m}(g)]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n!\,\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}&\mbox{if $n=m$}\\
0&\mbox{if $n\not=m$}\end{array}\right.

where
\widetilde{f}
is the symmetrization of
f
in all
n

variables:

\widetilde{f}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}f(x_{\rho(1)},\ldots,x_{\rho(n)}),

and
S_{n}
is the set of all permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Note that
I_{n}(\widetilde{f})=I_{n}(f)
and

\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}.

(6)

Every random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)

which is measurable with respect to
W
has the
Wiener chaos expansion
:

F=E(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}J_{n}(F)=E(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}),

(7)

where the series converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Moreover,

E|F|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}E|I_{n}(f_{n})|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}n!\,\|\widetilde{f}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2},

where
I_{0}
is the identity map on
\mathbb{R}
and
f_{0}=E(F)
. Occasionally, we denote
J_{0}(F)=\mathbb{E}(F)
.

In general,
I_{p+q}(f\otimes g)\not=I_{p}(f)I_{q}(g)
. The exact expression of
I_{p+q}(f\otimes g)
is given by
the product formula
, which will not be used in the present paper. Instead, we use the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
.

(a)

For
f\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes p}
,
g\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes q}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|I_{p+q}(f\otimes g)\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\dfrac{(p+q)!}{p!q!}\left\|I_{p}(f)\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|I_{q}(g)\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(b)

For
f_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes p_{1}}
,
f_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes p_{2}}
and
f_{3}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes p_{3}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}}(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\otimes f_{3})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\dfrac{(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3})!}{p_{1}!p_{2}!p_{3}!}\|I_{p_{1}}(f_{1})\|_{2}^{2}\|I_{p_{2}}(f_{2})\|_{2}^{2}\|I_{p_{3}}(f_{3})\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

(a) Since
f\otimes g
and
\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g}
have the same symmetrization,

I_{p+q}(\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g})=I_{p+q}(f\otimes g)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|I_{p+q}(f\otimes g)\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|I_{p+q}(\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g})\right\|_{2}^{2}=(p+q)!\left\|\widetilde{\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g}}\right\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(p+q)}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(p+q)!\left\|\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(p+q)}}^{2}=(p+q)!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p+q}}\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g}\right)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p+q})\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{p+q}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p+q)!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p+q}}\left|\mathcal{F}\tilde{f}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p})\right|^{2}\left|\mathcal{F}\tilde{g}(\xi_{p+1},\ldots,\xi_{p+q})\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{p+q}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{(p+q)!}{p!q!}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes p}}^{2}\|\tilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes q}}^{2}=\dfrac{(p+q)!}{p!q!}\left\|I_{p}(f)\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|I_{q}(g)\right\|_{2}^{2}

(b) We use (a) twice, the first time for
p=p_{1}
,
q=p_{2}+p_{3}
,
f=f_{1}
,
g=f_{2}\otimes f_{3}
, and the second time for
p=p_{2}
,
q=p_{3}
,
f=f_{2}
,
g=f_{3}
.
∎

Next, we extend Lemma
2.1
to processes indexed by elements in a measure space.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(E,\cal E,\mu)
be a measure space. For each
\theta>0
and
r\in E
, let
F_{\theta}(r),G_{\theta}(r)
be random variables in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with the Wiener chaos expansions

F_{\theta}(r)=\sum_{p\geq 0}\theta^{p/2}I_{p}(f_{p}(\cdot,r))
and

G_{\theta}(r)=\sum_{q\geq 0}\theta^{q/2}I_{q}(g_{q}(\cdot,r))
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}\sum_{q\geq 0}\theta^{p+q}\left\|I_{p+q}\left(\int_{E}f_{p}(\cdot,r)\otimes g_{q}(\cdot,r)\mu(dr)\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{E}\left\|F_{2\theta}(r)\right\|_{2}\left\|G_{2\theta}(r)\right\|_{2}\mu(dr)\right)^{2}.

Moreover, if for each
\theta>0
and
r\in E
,
H_{\theta}(r)
is a random variable in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with the Wiener chaos expansion

H_{\theta}(r)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\theta^{k/2}I_{k}(h_{k}(\cdot,r))
,
then

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}\sum_{q\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\theta^{p+q+k}\left\|I_{p+q+k}\left(\int_{E}f_{p}(\cdot,r)\otimes g_{q}(\cdot,r)\otimes h_{k}(\cdot,r)\mu(dr)\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\left(\int_{E}\left\|F_{3\theta}(r)\right\|_{2}\left\|G_{3\theta}(r)\right\|_{2}\left\|H_{3\theta}(r)\right\|_{2}\mu(dr)\right)^{2}.

Proof.

We use the same idea as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
.(a). More precisely,

\displaystyle\left\|I_{p+q}\left(\int_{E}f_{p}(\cdot,r)\otimes g_{q}(\cdot,r)\mu(dr)\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|I_{p+q}\left(\int_{E}\tilde{f}_{p}(\cdot,r)\otimes\tilde{g}_{q}(\cdot,r)\mu(dr)\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq(p+q)!\left\|\int_{E}\tilde{f}_{p}(\cdot,r)\otimes\tilde{g}_{q}(\cdot,r)\mu(dr)\right\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(p+q)}}

\displaystyle\quad=(p+q)!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p+q}}\left|\int_{E}\mathcal{F}\big(\tilde{f}_{p}(\cdot,r)\otimes\tilde{g}_{q}(\cdot,r)\big)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p+q})dr\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{p+q}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\quad=(p+q)!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p+q}}\int_{E^{2}}\mathcal{F}\tilde{f}_{p}(\cdot,r_{1})(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p})\mathcal{F}\tilde{g}_{q}(\cdot,r_{1})(\xi_{p+1},\ldots,\xi_{p+q})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\overline{\mathcal{F}\tilde{f}_{p}(\cdot,r_{2})(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p})}\overline{\mathcal{F}\tilde{g}_{q}(\cdot,r_{2})(\xi_{p+1},\ldots,\xi_{p+q})}\mu(dr_{1})\mu(dr_{2})\prod_{j=1}^{p+q}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\quad=(p+q)!\int_{E^{2}}\left\langle\tilde{f}_{p}(\cdot,r_{1}),\tilde{f}_{p}(\cdot,r_{2})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes p}}\left\langle\tilde{g}_{q}(\cdot,r_{1}),\tilde{g}_{q}(\cdot,r_{2})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes q}}\mu(dr_{1})\mu(dr_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=\dfrac{(p+q)!}{p!q!}\int_{E^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{p}\left(f_{p}(\cdot,r_{1})\right)I_{p}\left(f_{p}(\cdot,r_{2})\right)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[I_{q}\left(g_{q}(\cdot,r_{1})\right)I_{q}\left(g_{q}(\cdot,r_{2})\right)\right]\mu(dr_{1})\mu(dr_{2}).

Noting that
\frac{(p+q)!}{p!q!}\leq 2^{p+q}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 1}\sum_{q\geq 1}\theta^{p+q}\left\|I_{p+q}\left(\int_{E}f_{p}(\cdot,r)\otimes g_{q}(\cdot,r)\mu(dr)\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E^{2}}\sum_{p\geq 1}(2\theta)^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{p}\left(f_{p}(\cdot,r_{1})\right)I_{p}\left(f_{p}(\cdot,r_{2})\right)\right]\sum_{q\geq 1}(2\theta)^{q}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{q}\left(g_{q}(\cdot,r_{1})\right)I_{q}\left(g_{q}(\cdot,r_{2})\right)\right]dr_{1}dr_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{E^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{2\theta}(r_{1})F_{2\theta}(r_{2})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G_{2\theta}(r_{1})G_{2\theta}(r_{2})\right]\mu(dr_{1})\mu(dr_{2})\leq\left(\int_{E}\left\|F_{2\theta}(r)\right\|_{2}\left\|G_{2\theta}(r)\right\|_{2}\mu(dr)\right)^{2},

where we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the last inequality.

The second estimate is proved in a similar way, using the inequality
\frac{(p+q+k)!}{p!q!k!}\leq 3^{p+q+k}
.
∎

2.2
The operators
D
and
\delta

In this section, we recall briefly the definitions of the Malliavin derivative
D
and the divergence operator
\delta
. We refer the reader to
[
25
]
for more details.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the class of “smooth” random variables, i.e variables of the form

F=f(W(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,W(\varphi_{n})),

(8)

where
f\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
,
n\geq 1
, and

C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the class of bounded
C^{\infty}
-functions
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whose partial derivatives of all orders are bounded.
The

Malliavin derivative
of a random variable
F
of the form (
8
) is the

\mathcal{P}_{0}
-valued random variable given by:

DF:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(W(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,W(\varphi_{n}))\varphi_{i}.

We endow
\mathcal{S}
with the norm

\|F\|_{\mathbb{D}^{1,2}}:=(E|F|^{2})^{1/2}+(E\|DF\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}^{2})^{1/2}
. The
operator
D
can be extended to the space
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
, the
completion of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{D}^{1,2}}
.

For any integer
k\geq 2
, we let
D^{k}
be the Malliavin derivative of order
k
, whose domain is denoted by
\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
.

If
F
is a random variable in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with the Wiener chaos expansion
F=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}(f_{n})=\sum_{n\geq 0}J_{n}(F)
for some
symmetric
functions
f_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}
, then

D_{x}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n-1}(f_{n}(\cdot,x)).

(9)

In particular,
D_{x}J_{n}(F)=nI_{n-1}(f_{n}(\cdot,x))=J_{n-1}(D_{x}F)
for any
n\geq 1
, and
D_{x}J_{0}(F)=0
. In general,

D^{k}J_{n}(F)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}J_{n-k}(D^{k}F)&\mbox{if $n\geq k$}\\
0&\mbox{if $n<k$}\end{array}\right.

(10)

The
divergence operator

\delta
is the adjoint of
the operator
D
. The domain of
\delta
, denoted by
\mbox{Dom}\ \delta
, is the set of
u\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{P}_{0})
such that

|E\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq c(E|F|^{2})^{1/2},\quad\forall F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2},

where
c
is a constant depending on
u
. If
u\in{\rm Dom}\ \delta
, then
\delta(u)
is the element of
L^{2}(\Omega)

characterized by the following duality relation:

E(F\delta(u))=E\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}},\quad\forall F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}.

(11)

In particular,
E(\delta(u))=0
. If
u\in\mbox{Dom}\ \delta
, we
use the notation

\delta(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)W(\delta x),

and we say that
\delta(u)
is the
Skorohod integral
of
u
with respect to
W
.

2.3
OU semigroup and hypercontractivity

In this section, we review the definition and some properties of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup.

The
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) semigroup
is the family
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of contraction operators on
L^{2}(\Omega)
defined by:

\displaystyle T_{t}(F)=\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-nt}J_{n}(F).

Using (
10
), we derive that

\displaystyle D^{k}\left(T_{t}(F)\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-nt}D^{k}(J_{n}(F))=\sum_{n\geq k}e^{-nt}J_{n-k}(D^{k}F)=e^{-kt}T_{t}(D^{k}F).

(12)

The OU semigroup has a
hypercontractivity property
: for any
t>0
and
F\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,

\|T_{t}F\|_{q(t)}\leq\|F\|_{p},\quad\mbox{where $q(t)=e^{2t}(p-1)+1>p>1$}.

(13)

An important consequence of this property is that for any
1<p<q<\infty
, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and
\|\cdot\|_{q}
are equivalent on the same chaos space
\mathcal{P}_{0,n}
. More precisely,

\|F\|_{q}\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{p-1}\right)^{n/2}\|F\|_{p},\quad\mbox{for any $F\in\mathcal{P}_{0,n}$ and $1<p<q$}.

(14)

The generator of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup is given by:

LF=\sum_{n\geq 1}nJ_{n}(F).

(15)

Its domain
{\rm Dom}\ L
consists of random variables
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for which the series in (
15
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Note that

F\in{\rm Dom}\ L
if and only if
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
DF\in{\rm Dom}\ \delta
, and in this case,
LF=-\delta(DF)
.

The
pseudo-inverse
of
L
is the operator
L^{-1}
defined by

L^{-1}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}J_{n}(F).

For any
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
with
\mathbb{E}(F)=0
, the process
u=-DL^{-1}F
belongs to
{\rm Dom}\ \delta

and

F=\delta(-DL^{-1}F).

(16)

2.4
Existence of solution

In this section, we show that equation (
1
) has a unique solution.

It is known that, if it exists, the solution to equation (
1
) has the Wiener chaos expansion:

u_{\theta}(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\theta^{n/2}I_{n}(f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)),

(17)

with kernel
f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)
given by:

\displaystyle f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x;t)=

\displaystyle\int_{T_{n}(t)}f_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)d\boldsymbol{t},

(18)

where
T_{n}(t)=\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}
and

f_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)=G_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})1_{\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}}.

(19)

is the kernel which appears in the chaos decomposition of the solution of (hAm) with time dependent noise (studied in
[
4
]
). In this case,

\mathbb{E}|u_{\theta}(t,x)|^{2}=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\theta^{n}\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}.

(20)

In fact, the solution exists if and only if the series (
17
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, i.e. the series (
20
) converges. In this case, the solution is unique.

The next result gives the existence of the solution and provides an upper bound for its moments. For this result, we will use the fact that for any
a>0
, there exist some constants
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
depending on
a
such that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}}{(n!)^{a}}\leq c_{1}\exp(c_{2}x^{1/a})\quad\mbox{for any $x>0$}.

(21)

In addition, we will need the value of the following integral: for any
t>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{\alpha}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{\alpha}d\xi=c_{\alpha}t^{1-\alpha}.

(22)

The integral converges if and only if
\alpha\in(-1,1)
; see for instance, Lemma 3.1 of
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, equation (
1
) has a unique (Skorohod) solution. Moreover, for any
\theta>0
,
p\geq 2
,
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|u_{\theta}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq c_{1}\exp\left(c_{2}\theta^{\frac{1}{2H+1}}p^{\frac{1}{2H+1}}t^{\frac{2H+2}{2H+1}}\right),

(23)

where
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
are constants that only depend on
H
. Consequently,
C_{\theta,p,T,u}:=\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\|u_{\theta}(t,x)\|_{p}<\infty
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{F}f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)(\boldsymbol{\xi})=e^{-i(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})x}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})
, where
t_{n+1}=t
and

\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
.
By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})d\boldsymbol{t}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{t},

where
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
. By Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{F}f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{n}\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=c_{H}^{n}\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\eta_{j})\right|^{2}|\eta_{1}|^{1-2H}\prod_{j=2}^{n}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H}d{\boldsymbol{\eta}}d\boldsymbol{t},

where for the last line, we used the change of variables
\eta_{j}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
, and we denoted
\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n})
.
We now use the inequality
|a+b|^{1-2H}\leq|a|^{1-2H}+|b|^{1-2H}
, followed by the identity:

x_{1}\prod_{j=2}^{n}(x_{j}+x_{j-1})=\sum_{a\in A_{n}}x_{j}^{a_{j}},

where
A_{n}
is a set of multi-indices
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
with
{\rm card}(A_{n})=2^{n-1}
such that
a_{1}\in\{1,2\}
,
a_{n}\in\{0,1\}
,
a_{2},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\{0,1,2\}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}=n
.
The exact description of the set
A_{n}
is given in Lemma 2.2 of
[
2
]
. We obtain that:

\displaystyle|\eta_{1}|^{1-2H}\prod_{j=2}^{n}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H}\leq\sum_{a\in A_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\eta_{j}|^{(1-2H)a_{j}}=\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}},

(24)

where
D_{n}
is the set of multi-indices
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
with
\alpha_{j}=(1-2H)a_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
and
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in A_{n}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{n}\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\eta_{j})\right|^{2}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\eta_{j}\right)d\boldsymbol{t}.

The inner integral above is calculated using (
22
). In our case,
\alpha_{j}=2(1-2H)<1
, since
H>1/4
. Using the rough bound (
6
), we infer that:

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\|f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq C^{n}\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}d\boldsymbol{t}\leq C^{n}\frac{t^{(2H+2)n}}{(n!)^{2H+2}},

(25)

where
C>0
is a constant that depends on
H
which is different in each of its appearances. Using Minkowski inequality, hypercontractivity property (
14
), and (
25
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\|u_{\theta}(t,x)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n/2}(p-1)^{n/2}\|I_{n}(f_{n}(\cdot,x;t))\|_{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n/2}(p-1)^{n/2}(n!)^{1/2}\|\widetilde{f_{n}}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n/2}(p-1)^{n/2}C^{n/2}\frac{t^{(H+1)n}}{(n!)^{H+1/2}}.

Relation (
23
) now follows using (
21
).
∎

3
Wave equation with delta initial condition

In this section, we study the (hAm) on
[r,\infty)
with zero initial condition initial velocity given by the measure
\delta_{z}
. The relation between the solution of this model and the Malliavin derivative
Du_{\theta}
plays an important role in the present article.

For any
\theta>0
,
r>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
fixed, we consider the following equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)+\sqrt{\theta}v(t,x)\dot{W}(x),\ t>r,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\\
v(r,\cdot)=0,\ \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}(r,\cdot)=\delta_{z}.\end{cases}

(26)

We say that
v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}
is a (mild Skorohod)
solution
of (
26
) if it satisfies the following equation:

v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)=G_{t-r}(x-z)+\sqrt{\theta}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y)v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(s,y)W(\delta y)ds,\quad t\geq r.

(27)

It can be proved that if a solution
v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}
exists, then it is unique and it has the chaos expansion:

\displaystyle v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)=G_{t-r}(x-z)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\theta^{n/2}I_{n}\left(g_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)\right),

(28)

where

\displaystyle g_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},z,x;r,t)=

\displaystyle\int_{r<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t}g_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},r,z,t,x)dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n},

(29)

and

g_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},r,z,t,x)=G_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots G_{t_{1}-r}(x_{1}-z)

We denote
g_{0}(z,x;r,t)=g_{0}(r,z,t,x)=G_{t-r}(x-z)
. The necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of the solution
v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}
is that the series in (
28
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\theta^{n}n!\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty,

(30)

where
\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)
is the symmetrization of
g_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)
.

Due to the special form (
2
) of
G
,
v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)
satisfies the following identity:

\displaystyle v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)=2G_{t-r}(x-z)v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x).

(31)

The existence of the solution
v_{\theta}
and some of its properties follow from a connection with the (hAm) model with another Gaussian noise (called
\mathfrak{X}
), which is
white in time
and fractional in space (with the same Hurst index
H
as the noise
W
):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)+\sqrt{\theta}V(t,x)\dot{\mathfrak{X}}(t,x),\ t>r,\ x\in\mathbb{R}\\
V(r,\cdot)=0,\ \dfrac{\partial V}{\partial t}(r,\cdot)=\delta_{z}.\end{cases}

(32)

More precisely,
\{\mathfrak{X}(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})\}
is a zero-mean Gaussian process with covariance

\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{X}(\varphi)\mathfrak{X}(\psi)]=c_{H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\cdot)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(t,\cdot)(\xi)}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi dt=:\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}},

(33)

which can be extended to an isonormal Gaussian process
\mathfrak{X}=\{\mathfrak{X}(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}
.

We say that
V_{\theta}^{(r,z)}
is a
(mild Skorohod) solution
of (
32
) if it satisfies:

\displaystyle V^{(r,z)}_{\theta}(t,x)=G_{t-r}(x-z)+\sqrt{\theta}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y)V^{(r,z)}_{\theta}(s,y)\mathfrak{X}(ds,dy),\quad t\geq r.

(34)

Using a similar method to the proof of Theorem 3.8 of
[
3
]
, we show in Appendix
B
that equation (
32
) has a unique solution
V_{\theta}^{(r,z)}
, and its moments are uniformly bounded:

K_{\theta,T,p}:=\sup_{0\leq r<t\leq T}\sup_{x,z\in\mathbb{R}}\|V_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)\|_{p}<\infty.

(35)

See Example (
B.2
).
Moreover, the solution
V^{(r,z)}_{\theta}
has the following chaos expansion

\displaystyle V^{(r,z)}_{\theta}(t,x)=G_{t-r}(x-z)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\theta^{n/2}I_{n}^{\mathfrak{X}}(g_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)),

(36)

where
I_{n}^{\mathfrak{X}}
is the
n
th multiple integral with respect to
\mathfrak{X}
. Since the solution
V^{(r,z)}_{\theta}

36
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. i.e.

\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\theta^{n}\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty,

(37)

where
\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)
is the symmetrization of
g_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)
.

We now establish the existence of the solution
v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}
and we show that its moments are also uniformly bounded.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
r>0
,

Proof.

We denote
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1}\ldots d\xi_{n})
. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{r<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t}\mathcal{F}g_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,r,z,t,x)(\boldsymbol{\xi})d\boldsymbol{t}\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

We obtain that:

\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\|g_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq(t-r)^{n}\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2},

The converges of the series (
30

. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n}n!\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,z,x;r,t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\theta^{n}n!(t-r)^{n}\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}|V_{\theta(t-r)}^{(r,z)}(t,x)|^{2}.

(39)

To treat the higher moments, we use Lê’s hypercontractivity principle. Note that this principle was originally developed in
[
23
]
for the heat equation, but it is in fact valid for a larger class of SPDEs (see Theorem B.1 of
[
1
]

\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\|v_{(p-1)\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)\|_{2}\leq\|V_{(p-1)\theta(t-r)}^{(r,z)}(t,x)\|_{2}\leq\|V_{(p-1)\theta T}^{(r,z)}(t,x)\|_{2}.

35

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\theta>0
,

,

Estimates for the Mallivian derivatives

In this section, we give some estimates for

If we denote by

See (
9
). For any

\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,z,x;t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t),

where

\displaystyle f_{j}^{(n)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},z,x;t)=f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},z,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n-1},x;t)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{j-1}<r<t_{j}<\ldots<t_{n-1}<t\}}G_{t-t_{n-1}}(x-x_{n-1})\ldots G_{t_{j}-r}(x_{j}-z)G_{r-t_{j-1}}(z-x_{j-1})\ldots

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n-1}dr.

If the series (
40
) converges in

(53)

Relation (

5
Increments of
u_{\theta}
and
v_{\theta}

In this section, study some integrals involving the increments of the solutions
u_{\theta}
and
v_{\theta}
, which will be used in the proof of the QCLT. A key role is played by some stationarity properties of these processes.

The first result examines the increments of
u_{\theta}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\theta>0
,
p\geq 2
and
T>0
,

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{u,p,H,T,\theta}:=\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|u_{\theta}(t,x)-u_{\theta}(t,x+h)\|_{p}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh<\infty.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
, since
H<1/2
, we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{|h|>1}\|u_{\theta}(t,x)-u_{\theta}(t,x+h)\|_{p}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh\leq 2C_{\theta,p,T,u}\int_{|h|>1}|h|^{2H-2}dh<\infty.

It remains to treat the integral over the set
|h|\leq 1
.

We use the chaos expansion
u_{\theta}(t,x)-u_{\theta}(t,x+h)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\theta^{n/2}I_{n}(f_{n,h}(\cdot,x;t))
where
f_{n,h}(\cdot,x;t)=f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)-f_{n}(\cdot,x+h;t)
. By hypercontractivity property (
14
),

\displaystyle\|u(t,x)-u(t,x+h)\|_{p}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\theta^{n/2}\|I_{n}(f_{n,h}(\cdot,x;t))\|_{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\theta^{n/2}[J_{n,h}(t)]^{1/2}

(54)

where
J_{n,h}(t)=n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n,h}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
. Note that

f_{n,h}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x;t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}f_{n,h}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)d{\bf t},

where
f_{n,h}(\cdot,t,x)=f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)-f_{n}(\cdot,t,x+h)
and
f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)
is given by (
19
).
Using the rough bound (
6
), followed by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle J_{n,h}(t)\leq n!\|f_{n,h}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{H}^{n}n!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{T_{n}(t)}\mathcal{F}f_{n,h}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})d\boldsymbol{t}\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{H}^{n}t^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{F}f_{n,h}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\right|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{H}^{n}t^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|1-e^{-i(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})h}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{H}^{n}t^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|1-e^{-i\eta_{n}h}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\eta_{j})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\eta}d\boldsymbol{t},

where we change the variable
\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}=\eta_{j}
with the convention
t_{n+1}=t
and
\eta_{0}=0
.

The product above is estimated as usually, using inequality (
24
). Then, using identity (
22
), we obtain:

\displaystyle J_{n,h}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{n}t^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|1-e^{-i\eta_{n}h}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\eta_{j})|\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\boldsymbol{\eta}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle\leq c^{n}t^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|1-e^{-i\eta_{n}h}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-t_{n}}(\eta_{n})|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}\eta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\left(\int_{T_{n-1}(t_{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n-1}\right)dt_{n}

\displaystyle\leq c_{t,H}\frac{c^{n}t^{n}}{(n!)^{2H+1}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|1-e^{-i\eta_{n}h}|^{2}(1+|\eta_{n}|^{1-2H})\frac{\sin^{2}((t-t_{n})|\eta_{n}|)}{|\eta_{n}|^{2}}t_{n}^{(2H+1)n-2}d\eta_{n}dt_{n},

where
c_{t,H}=t^{1-2H}+1
and
c>0
is a constant depending on
H

(which may be different from line to line).
Using the bound

\sin^{2}((t-t_{n})|\eta_{n}|)\leq 1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle J_{n,h}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c_{t,H}\frac{c^{n}t^{n}}{(n!)^{2H+1}}t^{(2H+1)n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|1-e^{-i\eta h}|^{2}(1+|\eta|^{1-2H})\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}d\eta

\displaystyle\leq c_{t,H}\frac{c^{n}}{(n!)^{2H+1}}t^{(2H+2)n-1}(|h|+|h|^{2H}).

The estimate above is now inserted in (
54
). We obtain:

\displaystyle\|u(t,x)-u(t,x+h)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq c_{t,H}^{1/2}\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\theta^{n/2}\frac{c^{n/2}}{(n!)^{H+1/2}}t^{(H+1)n-1/2}(|h|^{1/2}+|h|^{H})

\displaystyle=:C_{t,p,\theta,H}(|h|^{1/2}+|h|^{H}),

where
C_{t,p,\theta,H}>0
is a constant which depends in
t,p,\theta,H
and is increasing in
t
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{|h|\leq 1}\|u(t,x)-u(t,x+h)\|_{p}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh\leq C_{t,p,\theta,H}^{2}\int_{|h|\leq 1}(|h|+|h|^{2H})|h|^{2H-2}dh.

The last integral is finite due to the condition
H>1/4
.
∎

The following lemma gives some translation invariance properties of
u_{\theta}
and
v_{\theta}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\theta>0
,
0\leq r\leq t
,
x,z,h\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:

\displaystyle u_{\theta}(t,x)-u_{\theta}(t,x+h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}u_{\theta}(t,0)-u_{\theta}(t,h)

\displaystyle v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}v_{\theta}^{(r,0)}(t,x-z)-v_{\theta}^{(r,h)}(t,x-z)

\displaystyle v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}v_{\theta}^{(r,0)}(t,x-z)-v_{\theta}^{(r,0)}(t,x+h-z)

Moreover, for the rectangular difference, we have:

\displaystyle v^{(r,z)}_{\theta}(t,x)-v^{(r,z+h)}_{\theta}(t,x)-v^{(r,z)}_{\theta}(t,x+k)+v^{(r,z+h)}_{\theta}(t,x+k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\quad v^{(r,0)}_{\theta}(t,x-z)-v^{(r,h)}_{\theta}(t,x-z)-v^{(r,0)}_{\theta}(t,x+k-z)+v^{(r,h)}_{\theta}(t,x+k-z).

(55)

Proof.

The lemma follows using the chaos expansions of these differences and the fact that the noise
W
is spatially homogeneous, i.e.

W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}W^{(x)}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where

W^{(x)}(\varphi)=W(\varphi(\cdot-x))
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

For any
r\in[0,t],h\in\mathbb{R}
, the following integrals do not depend on

:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x^{\prime})-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x^{\prime})\right\|_{p}^{q}dx^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z^{\prime})}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z^{\prime}+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}^{q}dz^{\prime}=I_{r,t,h}^{(\theta,p,q)},

(56)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x^{\prime})-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x^{\prime}+h)\right\|_{p}^{q}dx^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z^{\prime})}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z^{\prime})}(t,x+h)\right\|_{p}^{q}dz^{\prime}=J_{r,t,h}^{(\theta,p,q)},

where

\displaystyle I_{r,t,h}^{(\theta,p,q)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,0)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}^{q}dx,\quad J_{r,t,h}^{(\theta,p,q)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,0)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,0)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{p}^{q}dx.

To see this, we apply Lemma
5.2
, followed by the changes of variables
x^{\prime\prime}=x^{\prime}-z
(for the integrals on the left), respectively
z^{\prime\prime}=x-z^{\prime}
(for the integrals on the right).
Moreover, the same argument shows that the two integrals below also do not depend on
x,z\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v^{(r,z)}_{\theta}(t,x^{\prime})-v^{(r,z+h)}_{\theta}(t,x^{\prime})-v^{(r,z)}_{\theta}(t,x^{\prime}+k)+v^{(r,z+h)}_{\theta}(t,x^{\prime}+k)\right\|_{p}^{q}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v^{(r,z^{\prime})}_{\theta}(t,x)-v^{(r,z^{\prime}+h)}_{\theta}(t,x)-v^{(r,z^{\prime})}_{\theta}(t,x+k)+v^{(r,z^{\prime}+h)}_{\theta}(t,x+k)\right\|_{p}^{q}dz^{\prime}.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle a)

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dxdh\leq C_{v,p,H,t,\theta},

\displaystyle c)

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{p}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dxdh\leq C_{v,p,H,t,\theta},

where

(p,H,t,\theta)

Proof.

Due to Remark
5.3
and (
5.2
), we only have to prove a), c) and e).

a) We use the following fact: for any

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}^{2}\leq\left\|V_{(p-1)\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-V_{(p-1)\theta t}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(57)

3.1
: we first prove it for
p=2
(replacing

v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)

[

Then, the inequality in part a) follows from relation (
98

B.5

C_{v,p,H,t,\theta}=C_{V,2,H,t,(p-1)\theta t}
, where

c) For any
x,z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v_{(p-1)\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{(p-1)\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V_{(p-1)\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-V_{(p-1)\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{2}^{2},

where the first inequality is due to Lemma
A.1
and the second inequality is proved similarly to (
57

Therefore, it suffices to show that the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{2}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dxdh

is uniformly bounded, for all
r\in[0,t]
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
To treat this integral, we cannot use the method of Example
B.5
, since
V_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-V_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)
does not satisfy an integral equation similar to the one given in Theorem
B.4
. So we need to proceed differently. The idea is to move the increment from the
x
variable to the
z
variable, and then use the bound given by (
98
) for the resulting integral.
From the chaos expansion, we have:

\displaystyle\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(x+h-z)\big|^{2}+

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}(\theta t)^{n}n!\,\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)-\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x+h)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}.

By direct calculation, it can be shown that

\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)-\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x+h)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=\|\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,0,x,t-r,z)-\widetilde{g}_{n}(\cdot,0,x+h,t-r,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2},

and hence

\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(0,x)}(t-r,z)-V_{\theta t}^{(0,x+h)}(t-r,z)\right\|_{2}^{2}.

We conclude that for any
z,x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x^{\prime})-V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x^{\prime}+h)\right\|_{2}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dx^{\prime}dh

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(0,x^{\prime})}(t-r,z)-V_{\theta t}^{(0,x^{\prime}+h)}(t-r,z)\right\|_{2}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dx^{\prime}dh

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(0,x)}(t-r,z^{\prime})-V_{\theta t}^{(0,x+h)}(t-r,z^{\prime})\right\|_{2}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dz^{\prime}dh,

where the last equality is proved exactly as (
56
), using some translation invariance properties of
V_{\theta}
(similar to those given in Lemma
5.2
for
v_{\theta}
). Finally, due to relation (

), the last integral above is bounded by the constant
C_{V,2,H,t,\theta t}
.

e) For fixed
r>0
,

and

which reduces the problem to the case

Next, we use the fact that for any
x,z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+k)+v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k)\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\left\|V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-V_{\theta t}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)-V_{\theta t}^{(r,z)}(t,x+k)+V_{\theta t}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k)\right\|_{2}^{2},

which is proved similarly to (
57
).
The conclusion follows from relation (
99
).
∎

The next result is derived using Lemma

Lemma 5.5

For any

\displaystyle a)

\displaystyle c)

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{p}dx\right)^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh\leq C_{v,p,H,t,\theta}^{\prime},

\displaystyle d)

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+h)\right\|_{p}dz\right)^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh\leq C_{v,p,H,t,\theta}^{\prime},

where
C_{v,p,H,t,\theta}^{\prime}>0
is a constant that depends on
(p,H,t,\theta)
and is increasing in
t
.

Proof.

By Remark
5.3
, it suffices to prove a), c) and e).

a) We denote by
I
the integral appearing in the this inequality. Then
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
, where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are the integrals on
|h|>1
, respectively
|h|\leq 1

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}dx\leq 4tC_{\theta,p,t,v},

It remains to treat
I_{2}
. For this, we insert artificially the
G
function, using identity (
31

Then, by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2G_{t-r}(x-z)\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle+2\big|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(x-z-h)\big|\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}.

Hence
I_{2}\leq 8\big(I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}\big)
, where

\displaystyle I_{2,1}

\displaystyle=\int_{|h|\leq 1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-z)\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}dx\right)^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|h|\leq 1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(x-z-h)\big|\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}dx\right)^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh.

We treat
I_{2,1}
. The the presence of
G
allows us to apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to bring the square inside the integral. Using the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}^{2}(x-z)dx=(t-r)/2
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-z)\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}dx\right)^{2}\leq t\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}^{2}dx.

The desired bound for
I_{2,1}
follows now from Lemma
5.4
.a).

For
I_{2,2}
, we use Theorem
3.1
, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(x-z-h)\big|dx\leq|h|
. Hence,

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta,p,t,v}^{2}\int_{|h|\leq 1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(x-z-h)\big|dx\right)^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dh

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta,p,t,v}^{2}\int_{|h|\leq 1}|h|^{2H}dh<\infty.

c) We use the same argument as in part a), using Lemma
5.4
.c).

e) We denote by
I^{\prime}
the integral appearing in this inequality. Inserting artificially the term
G_{t-r}(x-z)
(using identity (
31
)), we obtain:

\displaystyle v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+k)+v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k)=

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad 2G_{t-r}(x-z)v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-2G_{t-r}(x-z-h)v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)-

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad 2G_{t-r}(x+k-z)v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+k)+2G_{t-r}(x+k-z-h)v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k).

We now use the following identity:

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2}-a_{3}b_{3}+a_{4}b_{4}

\displaystyle\quad=a_{1}(b_{1}-b_{2})+(a_{1}-a_{2})b_{2}-a_{3}(b_{3}-b_{4})-(a_{3}-a_{4})b_{4}

\displaystyle\quad=a_{1}(b_{1}-b_{2}-b_{3}+b_{4})+(a_{1}-a_{3})(b_{3}-b_{4})+(a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3}+a_{4})b_{2}+(a_{3}-a_{4})(b_{2}-b_{4}).

Using this identity together with triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+k)+v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2G_{t-r}(x-z)\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+k)+v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\quad+|2G_{t-r}(x-z)-2G_{t-r}(x+k-z)|\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z)}(t,x+k)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\quad+|2G_{t-r}(x-z)-2G_{t-r}(x-z-h)-2G_{t-r}(x+k-z)+2G_{t-r}(x+k-z-h)|\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\quad+|2G_{t-r}(x+k-z)-2G_{t-r}(x+k-z-h)|\left\|v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x)-v_{\theta}^{(r,z+h)}(t,x+k)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{4}F_{j}(x,h,k).

Using the inequality
(\sum_{j=1}^{4}e_{j})^{2}\leq 4\sum_{j=1}^{4}e_{j}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle I^{\prime}\leq 4\sum_{j=1}^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{j}(x,h,k)dx\right)^{2}|h|^{2H-2}|k|^{2H-2}dhdk=:4\sum_{j=1}^{4}I_{j}^{\prime}.

Since
F_{j}
is a product of two terms (which involve
G
, respectively
v_{\theta}
), we bound
\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{j}(x,h,k)dx\right)^{2}
using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. For
I_{1}^{\prime}
, we use Lemma
5.4
.e) and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}^{2}(x-r)dx=(t-r)/2
.
For
I_{2}^{\prime}
, we use Lemma
5.4
.a), and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(x+k-z)|^{2}|k|^{2H-2}dxdk=c_{H}(t-r)^{2H},

(58)

where
c_{H}>0
is a constant depending on
H
. For
I_{3}^{\prime}
, we use Lemma
3.2
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(x-z-h)-G_{t-r}(x+k-z)+G_{t-r}(x+k-z-h)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad|h|^{2H-2}|k|^{2H-2}dhdkdx=c_{H}^{\prime}(t-r)^{4H-1},

(59)

where
c_{H}^{\prime}>0
is a constant that depends on
H
.
For
I_{4}^{\prime}
, we use Lemma
5.4
.c) and (
58
).
∎

6.1
Limiting covariance

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let

\rho_{t,s,\theta}(x-y):=\mathbb{E}\left[\big(u_{\theta}(t,x)-1\big)\big(u_{\theta}(s,y)-1\big)\right]=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}\theta^{n}\alpha_{n}(x-y;t,s),

(60)

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}(x-y;t,s)=(n!)^{2}\langle\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t),\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,y;s)\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}=(n!)^{2}\langle f_{n}(\cdot,x;t),\tilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,y;s)\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n!\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\overline{\mathcal{F}f_{n}(\cdot,y;s)(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\sigma(n)})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n!\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}(x-y)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\xi_{1}\ldots d\xi_{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}d\boldsymbol{t}\int_{T_{n}(s)}d\boldsymbol{s}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{s_{j+1}-s_{j}}(\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}).

(61)

Here we use the convention
t_{n+1}=t
and
s_{n+1}=s
. This shows that
\alpha_{n}(x-y;t,s)
and
\rho_{t,s}(x-y)
depend on
x
and
y
only through the difference
x-y
. In particular,
\{u(t,x);x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a (wide-sense) stationary process with covariance function
\rho_{t,s,\theta}
.

By Fubini theorem,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\mathbb{E}[F_{R,\theta}(t)F_{R,\theta}(s)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\rho_{t,s,\theta}(x-y)dxdy=\sum_{n\geq 1}\theta^{n}\frac{1}{Rn!}\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\alpha_{n}(x-y;t,s)dxdy

\displaystyle=:\sum_{n\geq 1}\theta^{n}Q_{n,R}(t,s),

(62)

The application of Fubini theorem is justified by (
25
), using that fact that:

\alpha_{n}(x-y;t,s)\leq(n!)^{2}\|f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}\|\tilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,y;s)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}\leq c^{n}\frac{t^{(2H+2)n}}{(n!)^{2H}}.

We now show that the first term in series (
62
) does not contribute to the limit.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
t,s>0
,

Q_{1,R}(t,s)=\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\alpha_{1}(x-y;t,s)dxdy\to 0\quad\mbox{as $R\to\infty$}.

Proof.

By Fubini theorem,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(x-y;t,s)

\displaystyle=c_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\xi x}\frac{\sin((t-t_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}dt_{1}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{i\xi y}\frac{\sin((s-s_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}ds_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi(x-y)}\frac{\sin((t-t_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\frac{\sin((s-s_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}d\xi ds_{1}dt_{1}.

The application of this theorem is justified using the inequality:

\frac{|\sin((t-t_{1})|\xi|)|}{|\xi|}\frac{|\sin((s-s_{1})|\xi|)|}{|\xi|}\leq(t-t_{1})(s-s_{1})1_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}+\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}1_{\{|\xi|>1\}}.

(63)

Another application of Fubini theorem, justified also by (
63
), shows that:

\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\alpha_{1}(x-y;t,s)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad=c_{H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{4\sin^{2}(R|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\frac{\sin((t-t_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\frac{\sin((s-s_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds_{1}dt_{1},

where we used the fact that:

\left(\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-i\xi(x-y)}dxdy\right)=\left|\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-i\xi x}dx\right|^{2}=\frac{4\sin^{2}(R|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}:=4\pi R\ell_{R}(\xi),

We denote

I_{1}(t_{1},s_{1}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin^{2}(R|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\frac{\sin((t-t_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\frac{\sin((s-s_{1})|\xi|)}{|\xi|}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi=I_{1}(t_{1},s_{1})+I_{2}(t_{1},s_{1}),

where
I_{1}(t_{1},s_{1})
and
I_{2}(t_{1},s_{1})
correspond to the integration regions
\{|\xi|\leq\varepsilon\}
, respectively
\{|\xi|>\varepsilon\}
. To estimate
I_{2}(t_{1},s_{1})
, we bound all three
\sin
functions by
1
, obtaining

|I_{2}(t_{1},s_{1})|\leq\int_{|\xi|>\varepsilon}|\xi|^{-3-2H}d\xi=:C_{\varepsilon,H}.

To bound
I_{1}(t_{1},s_{1})
, we choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
(t+s)\varepsilon<\frac{\pi}{2}
, and hence
\sin((t-t_{1})|\xi|)\geq 0
and

\sin((s-s_{1})|\xi|)\geq 0
for any
t_{1}\in[0,t],s_{1}\in[0,s]
and
\xi
with
|\xi|\leq\varepsilon
. Using the inequality
|\sin(x)|\leq|x|
for the last two
\sin
functions, followed by the inequality
|\sin x|\leq|x|^{\theta}
with
\theta\in[0,1]
for the last
\sin
function, we obtain that:

\displaystyle I_{1}(t_{1},s_{1})

\displaystyle\leq(t-t_{1})(s-s_{1})\int_{|\xi|\leq\varepsilon}\frac{\sin^{2}(R|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq(t-t_{1})(s-s_{1})R^{2\theta}\int_{|\xi|\leq\varepsilon}|\xi|^{2\theta-2H-1}d\xi=:(t-t_{1})(s-s_{1})R^{2\theta}C_{\varepsilon,\theta,H},

provided that
\theta>H
. Summarizing, for any
t_{1}\in[0,t]
,
s_{1}\in[0,s]
and
\theta\in(H,1]
,

|I(t_{1},s_{1})|\leq(t-t_{1})(s-s_{1})R^{2\theta}C_{\varepsilon,\theta,H}+C_{\varepsilon,H}.

Therefore,

\left|\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\alpha_{1}(x-y;t,s)dxdy\right|\leq 4c_{H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}|I(t_{1},s_{1})|ds_{1}dt_{1}\leq C(t^{2}s^{2}R^{2\theta}+ts).

Then
|Q_{1,R}(t,s)|\leq C(t^{2}s^{2}R^{2\theta-1}+tsR^{-1})\to 0
as
R\to\infty
, if we choose
\theta\in(H,\frac{1}{2})
.
∎

Next, we study the terms corresponding to
n\geq 2
. We use the fact that the function

\ell_{R}(\xi)=\frac{\sin^{2}(R|\xi|)}{\pi R|\xi|^{2}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}

is an approximation of the identity, when
R\to\infty
(see Lemma 2.1 of
[
28
]
).

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
t>0
and
s>0
,

K_{\theta}(t,s):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\sum_{n\geq 2}\theta^{n}Q_{n,R}(t,s)\quad\mbox{exists and is finite}.

Proof.

We use expression (
61
) for
\alpha_{n}(x-y;t,s)
, and we integrate
dxdy
on
[-R,R]^{2}
. Using Fubini’s theorem, we obtain the following expression for
Q_{n,R}(t,s)
:

\displaystyle Q_{n,R}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{-R}^{R}\alpha_{n}(x-y;t,s)dxdy

\displaystyle=4\pi c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{T_{n}(s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ell_{R}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}

\displaystyle\times\dfrac{\sin((t-t_{n})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n}|}\dfrac{\sin((s-s_{n})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n}|}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{\sin((t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{\sin((s_{j+1}-s_{j})|\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}|)}{|\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}|}d\boldsymbol{\xi}d\boldsymbol{s}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle:=4\pi c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{T_{n}(s)}Q_{n,R}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s},\sigma)d\boldsymbol{s}d\boldsymbol{t}

(64)

To evaluate
Q_{n,R}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s},\sigma)
,
we use the change the variables
\xi_{n}\to\eta_{n}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n}
. For any fixed permutation
\sigma
, we denote
j_{0}=\sigma^{-1}(n)
, so that
\sigma(j_{0})=n
. Then for all
j<j_{0}
, the sum
\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}
does not involve the variable
\xi_{n}
, and remains the same when we perform the change of variables. For
j\geq j_{0}
, the sum

\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}
contains
\xi_{n}
and will be replaced after the change of variables by

\sum_{k=1}^{j_{0}-1}\xi_{\sigma(k)}+\big(\eta_{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_{j}\big)+\sum_{k=j_{0}+1}^{j}\xi_{\sigma(k)}=\eta_{n}-\xi_{\sigma(j+1)}-\ldots-\xi_{\sigma(n)},

where
\sum_{\emptyset}=0
.
Using the convention
\prod_{\emptyset}=1
, we obtain the expression:

\displaystyle Q_{n,R}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s},\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ell_{R}(\eta_{n})\dfrac{\sin((t-t_{n})|\eta_{n}|)}{|\eta_{n}|}\dfrac{\sin((s-s_{n})|\eta_{n}|)}{|\eta_{n}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{\sin((t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|}\prod_{j<\sigma^{-1}(n)}\dfrac{\sin((s_{j+1}-s_{j})|\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}|)}{|\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{j\geq\sigma^{-1}(n)}\dfrac{\sin((s_{j+1}-s_{j})|\eta_{n}-\xi_{\sigma(j+1)}-\ldots-\xi_{\sigma(n)}|)}{|\eta_{n}-\xi_{\sigma(j+1)}-\ldots-\xi_{\sigma(n)}|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}|\eta_{n}-\xi_{1}-\ldots-\xi_{n-1}|^{1-2H}d\xi_{1}\ldots d\xi_{n-1}d\eta_{n}:=\left(\ell_{R}*g_{{\bf t},{\bf s},\sigma}^{(n)}\right)(0),

where the function
g_{{\boldsymbol{t}},\boldsymbol{s},\sigma}^{(n)}
is given by:

\displaystyle g_{\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s},\sigma}^{(n)}(x)

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{\sin((t-t_{n})|x|)}{|x|}\dfrac{\sin((s-s_{n})|x|)}{|x|}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{\sin((t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|}\prod_{j<\sigma^{-1}(n)}\dfrac{\sin((s_{j+1}-s_{j})|\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}|)}{|\xi_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+\xi_{\sigma(j)}|}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j\geq\sigma^{-1}(n)}\dfrac{\sin((s_{j+1}-s_{j})|x-\xi_{\sigma(j+1)}-\ldots-\xi_{\sigma(n)}|)}{|x-\xi_{\sigma(j+1)}-\ldots-\xi_{\sigma(n)}|}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}|x-\xi_{1}-\ldots-\xi_{n-1}|^{1-2H}d\xi_{1}\ldots d\xi_{n-1}.

To define
g_{{\bf t},{\bf s},\sigma}(0)
, we use the convention
\frac{\sin x}{x}\big|_{x=0}=1
.

Since
\ell_{R}
is an approximation of the identity as
R\to\infty
, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\theta^{n}Q_{n,R}(t,s)

\displaystyle=4\pi\sum_{n\geq 2}\theta^{n}c_{H}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{T_{n}(s)}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\left(\ell_{R}*g_{{\bf t},{\bf s},\sigma}\right)(0)d{\bf s}d{\bf t}

\displaystyle\to 4\pi\sum_{n\geq 2}\theta^{n}c_{H}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{T_{n}(s)}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}g_{{\bf t},{\bf s},\sigma}(0)d{\bf s}d{\bf t}=:K_{\theta}(t,s),\quad\mbox{as $R\to\infty$}.

(65)

It remains to justify the application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem. For this, we will prove that there exists a function
H_{n}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s})
such that

\left|\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\left(\ell_{R}*g_{{\bf t},{\bf s},\sigma}\right)(0)\right|\leq H_{n}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s})

for any
n\geq 2
,
R\geq 1
,
\boldsymbol{t}\in T_{n}(t)
,
\boldsymbol{s}\in T_{n}(s)
, and

\sum_{n\geq 2}\theta^{n}c_{H}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{T_{n}(s)}H_{n}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s})d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{s}<\infty.

(66)

In particular, this will imply that the limit
K_{\theta}(t,s)
is finite.

We will use the following inequality: for any functions
h_{1},h_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for any symmetric measure
\mu_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{1}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})h_{2}(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\sigma(n)})\mu_{n}(d\xi_{1},\ldots,d\xi_{n})\right|\leq

\displaystyle\quad\frac{n!}{2}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h_{1}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})|^{2}\mu_{n}(d\xi_{1},\ldots,d\xi_{n})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h_{2}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})|^{2}\mu_{n}(d\xi_{1},\ldots,d\xi_{n})\right\},

which can be proved applying the inequality
ab\leq\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})
. It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\left(\ell_{R}*g_{{\bf t},{\bf s},\sigma}\right)(0)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{2}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ell_{R}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sin^{2}((t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ell_{R}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sin^{2}((s_{j+1}-s_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}

\displaystyle=:\frac{n!}{2}\Big(\mathcal{I}_{n,R}({\boldsymbol{t}})+\mathcal{I}_{n,R}({\boldsymbol{s}})\Big).

We only evaluate the first integral, the second one being similar. We use the change of variables
\eta_{j}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
(with
\eta_{0}=0
), followed by inequality (
24
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n,R}({\boldsymbol{t}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin^{2}((t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|)}{|\eta_{j}|^{2}}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\eta_{j}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin^{2}((t-t_{n})|\eta_{n}|)}{|\eta_{n}|^{2}}\ell_{R}(\eta_{n})|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}d\eta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c^{n-1}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}(t-t_{n})^{2}\frac{1}{\pi R}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin^{2}(R|\eta_{n}|)}{|\eta_{n}|^{2}}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}d\eta_{n}

\displaystyle=c^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}(t-t_{n})^{2}R^{-\alpha_{n}}\leq c^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}t^{1+\alpha_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}

Therefore, assuming that
s\leq t
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\left(\ell_{R}*g_{{\bf t},{\bf s},\sigma}\right)(0)\right|

\displaystyle\leq n!c^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}t^{1+\alpha_{n}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}+\prod_{j=1}^{n}(s_{j+1}-s_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}\Big)=:H_{n}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s}).

To see that (
66
) holds, we note that

\displaystyle\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{T_{n}(s)}H_{n}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{s})d\boldsymbol{s}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}t^{1+\alpha_{n}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in D_{n}}t^{1+\alpha_{n}}\frac{t^{(2H+1)n}}{\Gamma((2H+1)n+1)}.

∎

Finally, we prove that when
s=t
, the limit

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{l}a^{(j)}D^{k}_{z_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,z_{k}^{(j)}}U^{(j)}_{\frac{p-1}{q-1}\theta}(t,x_{j})\right\|_{q}\leq\left(\dfrac{p-1}{q-1}\right)^{k/2}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{l}a^{(j)}D^{k}_{z_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,z_{k}^{(j)}}U^{(j)}_{\theta}(t,x_{j})\right\|_{p}.

In this section, we study two models involving parametric families of stochastic Volterra equations driven by the Gaussian noise

. This study will allow us to develop some properties of the solution
V_{\theta}
of the (hAm) model (
32
) with noise
\mathfrak{X}
and delta initial velocity,
which are needed in the sequel.

The stochastic heat and wave equations with noise
\mathfrak{X}
and affine function
\sigma(u)=au+b
multiplying the noise
were studied in
[
3
]
. Several facts from
[
3

will be needed here. For instance, from Theorem 2.9 of
[
3
]
and Minkowski’s inequality, we have: for any

,

\left\|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}S(t,x)\mathfrak{X}(dt,dx)\right\|_{p}^{2}\leq C_{p,H}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|S(t,x)-S(t,y)\|_{p}^{2}|x-y|^{2H-2}dxdydt,

where
C_{p,H}>0
is a constant which depends on

and

Let
T>0
be arbitrary. For each
t\in[0,T]
, let
\Gamma_{t}
be a deterministic non-negative function on
\mathbb{R}
. Consider the following functions:

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\Gamma_{t}(x)-\Gamma_{t}(x+h)|^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dxdh

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}J_{3}(s)J_{4}(t-s)ds

\displaystyle J_{3}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\big(\Gamma_{t}(x+h)-\Gamma_{t}(x)\big)-\big(\Gamma_{t}(x+h+k)-\Gamma_{t}(x+k)\big)|^{2}|h|^{2H-2}|k|^{2H-2}dhdkdx

\displaystyle J_{4}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{t}^{2}(x)dx.

We impose the following assumption:

Assumption A.

J_{i}(t)<\infty
for any
t\in[0,T]
and
i=1,2,3,4
, and

Q_{i}(T):=\int_{0}^{T}J_{i}(t)dt<\infty\quad\mbox{for $i=1,2$}.

Assumption A holds when
\Gamma_{t}(x)=G_{t}(x)
is the fundamental solution of the wave equation (given by (
2
)), or when
\Gamma_{t}(x)=g_{t}(x):=(2\pi t)^{-1/2}\exp(-\frac{x^{2}}{2t})

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}\Gamma_{t}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi=\begin{cases}Ct^{2H}\quad\mbox{for wave equation}\\
Ct^{H-1}\quad\mbox{for heat equation}\end{cases}

\displaystyle J_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}\Gamma_{t}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2(1-2H)}d\xi=\begin{cases}Ct^{4H-1}\quad\mbox{for wave equation}\\
Ct^{2H-3/2}\quad\mbox{for heat equation}\end{cases}

J_{4}(t)=\begin{cases}Ct\quad\mbox{for wave equation}\\
Ct^{-1/2}\quad\mbox{for heat equation}\end{cases}\quad\mbox{and}\quad J_{2}(t)=\begin{cases}Ct^{4H+1}\quad\mbox{for wave equation}\\
Ct^{2H-1}\quad\mbox{for heat equation}\end{cases}

Here
C
is a constant that depends on
H
and may be different in each of its appearances.

Let
p\geq 2
be arbitrary. For any

, let
\mathcal{X}_{r}

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r,1}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(t,x)\in[r,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\|X(t,x)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r,2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(t,x)\in[r,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma_{t-s}^{2}(x-y)\|X(s,y)-X(s,y+h)\|_{p}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dhdyds\right)^{1/2}.

Theorem B.1
.

Suppose Assumption A holds.
Let
\Lambda
be an arbitrary set.

a) For any
r\in[0,T]
and
\alpha\in\Lambda
, the family of stochastic Volterra equations:

X(t,x)=X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}(t,x)+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{t-s}(x-y)X(s,y)\mathfrak{X}(ds,dy),

(87)

with
t\in[r,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, has a unique solution
X^{(r,\alpha)}
in
\mathcal{X}_{r}
, provided that
X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\in\mathcal{X}_{r}
.

b) If in addition,

\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\sup_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\|X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}<\infty,

(88)

then

\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\sup_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\|X^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}<\infty.

(89)

Proof.

a) For any
n\geq 0
, we define the Picard iterations:

X_{n+1}(t,x)=X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}(t,x)+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{t-s}(x-y)X_{n}(s,y)\mathfrak{X}(ds,dy),

(90)

for all
t\in[r,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that
X_{n}=X_{n}^{(r,\alpha)}
depends on
(r,\alpha)
. To simplify the writing, we omit writing the upper index
(r,\alpha)
in the first part of the argument.

The recurrence relation (
90
) also holds for
n=-1
,
letting
X_{-1}(t,x)=0
. For any
n\geq 0
and
t\in[r,T]
, we define

\displaystyle V_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\|X_{n}(t,x)-X_{n-1}(t,x)\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle W_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma_{t-s}^{2}(x-y)

\displaystyle\|\big(X_{n}(s,y)-X_{n-1}(s,y)\big)-\big(X_{n}(s,y+h)-X_{n-1}(s,y+h)\big)\|_{p}^{2}|h|^{2H-2}dhdyds.

As in the proof of Theorem 3.8 of
[
3
]
, for any
t\in[r,T]
and
n\geq 0
, we have:

\displaystyle V_{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{p,H}\left(\int_{r}^{t}V_{n}(s)J_{1}(t-s)ds+W_{n}(t)\right)

\displaystyle W_{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{p,H}\left(\int_{r}^{t}V_{n}(s)J_{2}(t-s)ds+\int_{r}^{t}W_{n}(s)J_{1}(t-s)ds\right),

where
C_{p,H}
is the constant from (
86
). Letting
f_{n}(t)=V_{n}(t)+W_{n}(t)
, we have: for
n\geq 1
,

f_{n+1}(t)\leq 2C_{p,H}(C_{p,H}+1)\int_{r}^{t}\big(f_{n}(s)+f_{n-1}(s)\big)ds,\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[r,T].

Note that this is precisely the recurrence relation appearing in Lemma 3.10 of
[
3
]
. To apply this lemma, we need to show that
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are uniformly bounded. For
n=0
,

\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sqrt{f_{0}(t)}\leq\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sqrt{V_{0}(t)}+\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sqrt{W_{0}(t)}=\|X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}=:M_{0}

For
n=1
, using the recurrence relations above, we have:

\displaystyle V_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{p,H}\left(\int_{r}^{t}V_{0}(s)J_{1}(t-s)ds+W_{0}(t)\right)\leq 2C_{p,H}\|X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}^{2}\big(Q_{1}(t-r)+1\big)

\displaystyle W_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{p,H}\left(\int_{r}^{t}V_{0}(s)J_{2}(t-s)ds+\int_{r}^{t}W_{0}(s)J_{1}(t-s)ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{p,H}\|X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}^{2}\big(Q_{1}(t-r)+Q_{2}(t-r)\big),

and hence

\sup_{t\in[r,T]}f_{1}(t)\leq 2C_{p,H}\|X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}^{2}\big(2Q_{1}(T-r)+Q_{2}(T-r)+1\big)=:M_{1}.

Let
M=M_{0}+M_{1}
. Applying now Lemma 3.10 of
[
3
]
, we infer that there exists a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of positive numbers with the property
\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}^{1/p}<\infty
for any
p>1
, such that

\sup_{t\in[r,T]}f_{n}(t)\leq Ma_{n}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad n\geq 0.

It follows that

\|X_{n}-X_{n-1}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}=\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sqrt{V_{n}(t)}+\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sqrt{W_{n}(t)}\leq 2\sqrt{Ma_{n}}
.
Hence
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{X}_{r}
. Its limit
X
is the unique solution of (
87
) in
\mathcal{X}_{r}
.

b) We now prove the last statement regarding the uniform bound in
(r,\alpha)
. We include the upper indices
(r,\alpha)
in this part. We have:

\|X_{n}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}\leq\|X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|X_{k}^{(r,\alpha)}-X_{k-1}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}\leq\|X_{0}^{(r,\alpha)}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{r}}+2(M^{(r,\alpha)})^{1/2}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{1/2}.

Relation (
89
) follows letting
n\to\infty
, using the fact that
M^{(r,\alpha)}
is uniformly bounded for all
r\in[0,T]
and
\alpha\in\Lambda
, due to condition (
88
).
∎

Theorem B.4

Suppose Assumption B holds.

a) For any

To illustrate the main ideas, assume first that a solution

Appendix C
Integral inequalities

In this section, we give some basic inequalities related to the function
\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)=\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}
.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. For
\beta\in(0,2)
and
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{|\sin(t|x|)\sin(s|x|)|}{|x|^{2}}|\varphi(x)||x|^{\beta}dx\leq(1+|ts|)\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

We denote by
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
the integrals over the regions
|x|\leq 1
, respectively
|x|>1
. In these regions, we use the inequalities

|\sin(x)|\leq|x|
, respectively
|\sin(x)|\leq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|ts|\int_{|x|\leq 1}|\varphi(x)||x|^{\beta}dx\leq|ts|\int_{|x|\leq 1}|\varphi(x)|dx

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|>1}|\varphi(x)||x|^{\beta-2}dx\leq\int_{|x|>1}|\varphi(x)|dx.

∎

The next result is obtained by a similar method.

Lemma C.2
.

For
\beta\in(0,1)
and
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{|\sin(t|x|)\sin(s|x|)|}{|x|^{2}}|x|^{\beta}dx\leq\dfrac{2}{1-\beta}+\dfrac{2|ts|}{1+\beta}.

Isotopies of complete minimal surfaces
of finite total curvature

Abstract




Abstract

Let
M
be a Riemann surface biholomorphic to an affine algebraic curve.
We show that the inclusion of the space

\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})
of real parts of nonflat proper algebraic null immersions

M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, into the space
\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
complete nonflat conformal minimal immersions

M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite total curvature is a weak homotopy equivalence.
We also show that the
(1,0)
-differential
\partial
,
mapping
\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
or
\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})

to the space
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
of algebraic
1
-forms on
M

with values in the punctured null quadric
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
Analogous results are obtained for proper algebraic immersions
M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

n\geq 2
, directed by a flexible or algebraically elliptic punctured
cone in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Keywords

Riemann surface, minimal surface, algebraic immersion,
directed immersion, algebraically elliptic manifold, flexible manifold,
Oka manifold

1. Introduction

In this paper, we initiate the study of the homotopy theory of minimal surfaces
of finite total curvature in Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
,
and of algebraic directed immersions from affine algebraic
curves to complex Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
.

We begin by recalling the basic facts on minimal surfaces,
referring to
[
31
]
and
[
5
]

for more information. Let
M
be an open Riemann surface. An immersion

u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}):M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is conformal if and only if the
\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued

(1,0)
-form
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n})
, whose
i
-th component

\phi_{i}=\partial u_{i}
is the
(1,0)
-differential of the function
u_{i}
,
satisfies the nullity condition

(1.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}^{2}=0.

A conformal immersion
u:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
parametrises a minimal surface
if and only if
u
is harmonic if and only if

\phi=\partial u
is a holomorphic 1-form. Conversely,
a nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-form
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n})

on
M
satisfying the nullity condition (
1.1
) and the period
vanishing condition
\Re\int_{C}\phi=0
for all closed curves
C
in
M

integrates to a conformal minimal immersion
u=\Re\int\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
\int_{C}\phi=0
for all closed curves
C
in
M
,
then
\phi
integrates to a holomorphic null curve
h=\int\phi:M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}

whose real and imaginary parts parametrise conjugate minimal surfaces.
Define the null quadric

(1.2)

\mathbf{A}=\bigl\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}:z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n}^{2}=0\bigr\}.

Given a Riemann surface
M
, the null quadric
defines a holomorphic subbundle
\mathcal{A}
of the vector bundle

(T^{*}M)^{\oplus n}
, with fibre isomorphic to
\mathbf{A}
, whose sections
are
n
-tuples
(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n})

of
(1,0)
-forms on
M
without common zeros such that the ratio

[\phi_{1}:\cdots:\phi_{n}]:M\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
takes values in the projective quadric
defined by the same equation
z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n}^{2}=0
.
Hence, a smooth map
u:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a conformal minimal immersion if and
only if
\phi=\partial u
is a holomorphic section of the bundle
\mathcal{A}\to M
.
Note that the fibre multiplication by a nonzero complex
number is a well-defined operation on
\mathcal{A}
.

The flux homomorphism
\mathrm{Flux}_{u}:H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

of a conformal minimal immersion
u:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

(1.3)

\mathrm{Flux}_{u}(C)=\int_{C}d^{c}u\quad\text{for every}\ \ [C]\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}).

We may view
\mathrm{Flux}_{u}
as an element of
the cohomology group
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The
1
-form
d^{c}u=\Im(2\partial u)=\mathfrak{i}(\overline{\partial}u-\partial u)

is the differential of any local harmonic conjugate of
u
, so

u
is the real part of a holomorphic null curve

M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
if and only if
\mathrm{Flux}_{u}=0
.

Complete minimal surfaces of finite total Gaussian curvature
are among the most intensively studied
minimal surfaces, and they play an important role in the
classical global theory of minimal surfaces;
see
[
31
]
and
[
5
, Chapter 4]
,
among many other sources. If
u:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
a conformally immersed minimal surface of finite total curvature,
then the Riemann surface
M
is the complement in a compact
Riemann surface
\overline{M}
of a nonempty finite set

E=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
whose points are called the ends of
M
.
Such a surface
M
admits a biholomorphism
onto a closed embedded algebraic curve in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
hence will be called an
affine Riemann surface
.
The bundle
\mathcal{A}\to M
with fibre
\mathbf{A}
(
1.2
) is algebraic
and
\partial u
is a meromorphic
1
-form on

\overline{M}
without zeros or poles on
M
, that is, an algebraic (regular)
section of
\mathcal{A}
over
M
. Completeness of
u
is reflected in

\partial u
having an effective pole at every point of
E=\overline{M}\setminus M
.
The surface
u(M)
is then properly immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and has a fairly simple and
well-understood asymptotic behaviour at every end
of
M
, described by the Jorge–Meeks theorem
[
22
]
.
Although this family of minimal surfaces has been a focus of interest
since the seminal work of Osserman in the 1960s
[
31
]
,
the theories of approximation and interpolation for complete minimal surfaces
of finite total curvature in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, including Runge and
Mittag–Leffler type theorems, have been developed only recently

[
28
,
2
,
9
]
.
The present paper provides the first contributions
to the homotopy theory of this
most important class of minimal surfaces.

We denote by
\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of complete nonflat conformal
minimal immersions
M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite total curvature, and by

\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})
the spaces of complete
nonflat holomorphic null immersions
M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
of finite total curvature
(that is, proper and algebraic) and their real parts, respectively.
We denote by
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
the space of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued meromorphic

1
-forms
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n})
on
\overline{M}
having no zeros or
poles in
M
and satisfying the nullity condition (
1.1
),
that is, algebraic sections of the bundle
\mathcal{A}\to M
, and by

\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,\mathbf{A})
the subspace of nonflat 1-forms.
Nonflatness means that the map
[\phi_{1}:\cdots:\phi_{n}]:M\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}

is nonconstant. All these spaces are endowed with the compact-open
topology.

(1.4)

-differential. The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
M
is an affine Riemann surface, then the maps in (

)
are weak homotopy equivalences.

Recall that a continuous map
\alpha:X\to Y
between topological spaces
is said to be a weak homotopy equivalence if it induces
a bijection of path components of the two spaces and an isomorphism

\pi_{k}(\alpha):\pi_{k}(X)\to\pi_{k}(Y)
of their homotopy groups for
k=1,2,\ldots

and arbitrary base points.
Thus, Theorem
1.1
says that the three mapping spaces in
diagram (
1.4
) have the same rough topological shape.

Note that the images of the maps
\partial
in (
1.4
) are contained
in the subspace
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\infty}(M,\mathbf{A})
of
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,\mathbf{A})

consisting of nonflat
1
-forms that have poles at all ends of
M
.
It turns out that
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\infty}(M,\mathbf{A})
and
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,\mathbf{A})

are open everywhere dense subsets of
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
, and the inclusions

(1.5)

\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\infty}(M,\mathbf{A})\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,\mathbf{A})\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})

are weak homotopy equivalences (see Propositions

5.2
and
5.3
).
Hence, Theorem
1.1
also
holds if the operator
\partial
is considered as a map to any of these two smaller
spaces of
1
-forms on
M
. See the more detailed diagram
(
6.1
) and Corollary
6.5
.

Recall that the tangent bundle of an affine Riemann surface
M
is
holomorphically trivial (this holds for every open Riemann
surface by a theorem of Oka
[
30
]
,
see also
[
15
, Theorem 5.3.1 (c)]
and
the more precise result of Gunning and Narasimhan

[
20
]
), but is not
algebraically trivial in general. If the genus of
M
is 0 or 1,
that is,
\overline{M}
is the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{P}
or a torus,
then
TM
is algebraically trivial.
More generally,
TM
is algebraically trivial if
M
is
embeddable as a closed algebraic curve in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Note that
TM
is algebraically trivial if and only if the cotangent bundle

T^{*}M
is such, and this holds if and only if there is an
algebraic
1
-form
\theta
on
M
without zeros or poles.
Such a 1-form is the restriction to
M
of a meromorphic
1-form on
\overline{M}
. Every
\phi\in\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})

is then of the form
\phi=f\theta
where
f:M\to\mathbf{A}
is an algebraic
map. In this case we can replace the space
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})

in (
1.4
) by the space
\mathscr{A}(M,\mathbf{A})
of
algebraic maps
M\to\mathbf{A}
and the differential
u\mapsto\partial u

by the map
u\mapsto\partial u/\theta
.

For the spaces of nonflat holomorphic null curves and
nonflat conformal minimal immersions
(not necessarily complete or of finite total curvature)
from an arbitrary open Riemann surface
M
, and
with
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
replaced by the bigger space

\mathscr{O}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
of holomorphic 1-forms on
M

with values in
\mathbf{A}
, the homotopy principle for the maps in
(
1.4
) was obtained by Forstnerič and Lárusson

[
16
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
,
following the work of Alarcón and Forstnerič in

[
3
,
4
]
.
In this case, the maps in (
1.4
) were shown to be
genuine homotopy equivalences when the homology group

H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is finitely generated, which holds
if
M
is an affine Riemann surface.
We do not know whether the same is true in the
context of Theorem
1.1
. To show that a weak homotopy equivalence
is a genuine homotopy equivalence using Whitehead’s theorem, one needs to
know that the spaces in question have the homotopy type of CW complexes.
In
[
16
]
, this was established by showing that the spaces are absolute neighbourhood retracts (such results were first proved in
[
25
]
). We do not see any way to prove this in the present setting and have no reason to believe it is true.

The aforementioned results in
[
16
]

are used in an important way in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
given in Section
5
.
Another major ingredient is the algebraic homotopy approximation
theorem for sections of algebraically elliptic submersions over an affine
manifold, due to Forstnerič
[
14
]
. We shall use
improved versions given by Theorems
2.1

and
2.6
and Corollary
2.8
.

\alpha

For the inclusion
\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
Theorem
1.1
says in particular that every complete nonflat conformal
minimal immersion
M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of finite total curvature can be deformed
through maps of the same type to the real part of a proper algebraic null curve

M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The following result shows that, in addition, one can control the
flux along an isotopy in
\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be an affine Riemann surface and
u_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be a complete nonflat conformal minimal immersion of finite total curvature. Then there is a smooth isotopy
u_{t}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in[0,1]
, of complete nonflat conformal minimal immersions of finite total curvature such that
u_{1}
is the real part of a proper algebraic null curve
h:M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
More generally, for any homotopy
\mathcal{F}_{t}\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
t\in[0,1]
,
with
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathrm{Flux}_{u_{0}}
there is a smooth isotopy
u_{t}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

t\in[0,1]
, of complete nonflat conformal minimal immersions of finite total
curvature such that
\mathrm{Flux}_{u_{t}}=\mathcal{F}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

This is an analogue of
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
,
which gives a similar statement for nonflat minimal surfaces
without the finite total curvature condition;
see also
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
,
which permits prescribing the flux of all the surfaces in the isotopy
by using the same tools. If the immersion
u_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
Theorem
1.2
is not complete (which means that it extends
harmonically to an end of
M
), the theorem still holds with the weaker
conclusion that the nonflat conformal minimal immersions
of finite total curvature
u_{t}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in[0,1]
, are complete
for
c\leq t\leq 1
for any given
c\in(0,1)
.
This shows that the inclusion of
\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
into the
space
\mathrm{CMI}_{0}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of all nonflat conformal minimal immersions
of finite total curvature
M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
(including the incomplete ones)
induces a surjection of path components.
We show that this inclusion is in fact a weak homotopy equivalence
(see Corollary
6.5
). We also prove a parametric version of Theorem
1.2
(see Theorem
6.2
).

Holomorphic null curves are a special type
of directed holomorphic immersions of open Riemann surfaces to
complex Euclidean spaces. A connected compact complex submanifold

Y
of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
,
n\geq 2
, determines the punctured complex cone

(1.7)

A=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}:[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{n}]\in Y\}.

Note that
A
is smooth and connected, and its closure

\overline{A}=A\cup\{0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is algebraic by Chow’s theorem.
The map
\pi:A\to Y
given by
\pi(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{n}]
is an algebraic
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-bundle, and by adding the zero section we obtain the restriction to
Y
of the hyperplane bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.
A holomorphic immersion
h:M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
from an open Riemann surface
is said to be
directed by

A
, or an
A
-immersion
,
if its complex derivative with respect to any local holomorphic coordinate
on
M
takes its values in
A
. Equivalently, the differential
dh=\partial h

is a section of the subbundle
\mathcal{A}
with fibre
A
of the vector bundle

(T^{*}M)^{\oplus n}
. When
A
is the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
,
an
A
-immersion
M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the same thing as a
holomorphic null immersion.

Holomorphic directed immersions were studied by Alarcón and Forstnerič

[
3
]
. Under the assumption
that
A
is an Oka manifold not contained in any hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
they proved an Oka principle with Runge and Mergelyan approximation
for holomorphic
A
-immersions

[
3
, Theorems 2.6 and 7.2]
.
(By
[
15
, Theorem 5.6.5]
, the cone
A

in (
1.7
) is an Oka manifold if and only if
Y
is.)
They also showed that every holomorphic
A
-immersion can be
approximated by holomorphic
A
-embeddings when
n\geq 3
,
and by proper holomorphic
A
-embeddings under some natural extra assumptions on the cone
A

[
3
, Theorem 8.1]
.
In the subsequent paper
[
1
]
by Alarcón and Castro-Infantes, interpolation was added to the picture.
A parametric Oka principle for holomorphic immersions
directed by an Oka cone was proved as

[
16
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Recently, algebraic
A
-immersions from
affine Riemann surfaces into
\mathbb{C}^{n}
were studied in
[
8
]
under the assumption
that the cone
A
is algebraically elliptic in the sense of Gromov

[
19
]
(see also
[
15
, Definition 5.6.13]
);
we recall this notion in Section
2
. Several important
cones arising in geometric applications,
in particular the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
, are algebraically elliptic.
The optimal known sufficient condition for the cone

A
(
1.7
) on a projective manifold
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}

to be algebraically elliptic is given by a recent theorem of
Arzhantsev, Kaliman, and Zaidenberg

[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
.
They showed that
A
is algebraically elliptic if
Y

(with
\dim Y\geq 1
) is uniformly rational,
meaning that
Y
is covered by Zariski-open sets, each isomorphic
to a Zariski-open subset of affine space.

We obtain further results on directed algebraic immersions.
The following Runge approximation
and jet interpolation theorem is proved in Section
3
.
It strengthens the main implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
of
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
, upgrading
plain interpolation to jet interpolation. This requires a new idea.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a connected smooth punctured
complex cone (
1.7
) which is algebraically elliptic and not contained in a
hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
M
be an affine Riemann
surface,
\mathcal{A}
be the subbundle of
(T^{*}M)^{\oplus n}
defined by

A
,
K
be a compact holomorphically convex subset of
M
,

U
be an open neighbourhood of
K
, and

h:U\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a holomorphic
A
-immersion such that
the homotopy class of continuous sections of
\mathcal{A}|_{U}\to U

that contains
dh
also contains the restriction to
U
of an algebraic section

\vartheta:M\to\mathcal{A}
. Then
h
can be approximated
uniformly on a neighbourhood of
K
by proper algebraic

A
-immersions
\tilde{h}:M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
agreeing with
h
to a given
finite order on any given finite set in
K

such that
d\tilde{h}
is homotopic to
\vartheta
through algebraic
sections of
\mathcal{A}\to M
.

For the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
(
1.2
), Theorem
1.3
was first
proved by Alarcón and López
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]

using fairly technical results from the function theory of Riemann surfaces
and the special geometry of the cone.
In this case, the hypothesis that
dh
be homotopic
to the restriction of an algebraic section of
\mathcal{A}
is always fulfilled

[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
.
Their result also gives interpolation of poles at some ends of

and includes Mergelyan approximation on admissible sets, as opposed
to mere Runge approximation as in Theorem
1.3
.
The latter is well understood and we do not repeat it here.

We recall the following notion from

[
3
, Definition 2.2]
.
Let
A\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a smooth punctured complex cone.
We consider the tangent space
T_{x}A\subset T_{x}\mathbb{C}^{n}
at a point
x\in A

as a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
M
be an open Riemann surface
and
\theta
be a nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-form on
M
.
A holomorphic map
f:M\to A
is
nondegenerate
if the linear span of the
tangent spaces
T_{f(x)}A
,
x\in M
, equals
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
A holomorphic
A
-immersion
h:M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is nondegenerate
if the holomorphic map
f=dh/\theta:M\to A
is nondegenerate.
A holomorphic 1-form
\phi
on
M
with values in
A
is
nondegenerate if the holomorphic map
\phi/\theta:M\to A

is nondegenerate. Recall that a holomorphic null immersion is nondegenerate
if and only if it is nonflat; see
[
5
, Lemma 2.5.3]

and the references therein.

Given an open Riemann surface
M
, we denote by
\mathscr{I}_{\rm nd}(M,A)

the space of nondegenerate holomorphic
A
-immersions

M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
\theta
be as above.
Assuming that the cone

It is natural to ask to what extent this holds in the algebraic category,

a connected smooth algebraic cone not contained in any hyperplane.
If

The next question is whether this map is injective on path components.
A partial answer is given by Theorem

Under the stronger assumptions that the cone

for nondegenerate algebraic

-immersions,
analogous to Theorem

. Recall (see Arzhantsev et al.
[

)
that an algebraic manifold is said to be

spanning the tangent bundle at every point.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
be an affine Riemann surface, and let

,

, be a flexible smooth connected cone
not contained in any hyperplane. Then the map

(1.8)

\mathscr{I}_{*}(M,A)\to\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,A),\quad h\mapsto dh,

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

The only way in which flexibility is used in this paper is that it implies
algebraic ellipticity with a trivial spray bundle; see

and the proof of Corollary

.
The null quadric
\mathbf{A}
is flexible
(see

pertaining to the

,
is a special case of Theorem

. The proof of Theorem

is obtained by exactly the same argument as in the

, so we omit the details.
The large cone

Further examples are the cones

defined by a flag manifold

).

.

The paper is organised as follows.

In Section

,

2. Algebraic homotopy approximation theorems

In this section we prove results on approximating families of holomorphic
sections of an algebraically subelliptic or flexible submersion over an affine
variety by families of algebraic sections;
see Theorems
2.1
,
2.6
and
Corollary
2.8
.
These results are applications and extensions
of a theorem of Forstnerič
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]

(see also
[
15
, Theorem 6.15.3]
).
They provide some of the main tools used in the paper.

Let
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X
be an algebraic submersion from an algebraic
variety
Z
onto an affine algebraic variety
X
.
We recall the notions of algebraic ellipticity and subellipticity
introduced by Gromov
[
19
, 1.1.B]
;
see also
[
15
, Definition 6.1.1]
.

An algebraic fibre spray on
Z
is a triple
(E,\pi,s)
where

\pi:E\to Z
is an algebraic vector bundle and
s:E\to Z

is an algebraic map (the spray map) such that

s(0_{z})=z\ \ {\rm and}\ \ s(E_{z})\subset Z_{\mathfrak{h}(z)}=\mathfrak{h}^{-1}(\mathfrak{h}(z))\ \ \text{for every $z\in Z$}.

Here,
E_{z}=\pi^{-1}(z)
is the fibre of
E
over
z
and
0_{z}\in E_{z}

denotes the origin. A fibre spray
(E,\pi,s)
is said to be

dominating at a point

z\in Z
if the differential

ds_{0_{z}}:T_{0_{z}}E\to T_{z}Z
maps the subspace

E_{z}\subset T_{0_{z}}E
surjectively onto the vertical tangent space

\ker d\mathfrak{h}_{z}
. The fibre spray is said to be

dominating
if it is dominating at every point
z\in Z
.
A family of fibre sprays
(E_{j},\pi_{j},s_{j})

(j=1,\ldots,m)
on
Z

is said to be dominating at the point
z\in Z
if

(2.1)

(ds_{1})_{0_{z}}(E_{1,z})+(ds_{2})_{0_{z}}(E_{2,z})+\cdots+(ds_{m})_{0_{z}}(E_{m,z})=\ker(d\mathfrak{h}_{z}),

and to be
dominating
if this holds at every point
z\in Z
.
The algebraic submersion
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X

is
algebraically elliptic
if it admits a dominating algebraic fibre spray,
and is
algebraically subelliptic
if it admits a dominating family
of such sprays. An algebraic manifold
Y
is algebraically
(sub-)elliptic if the submersion from
Y
to a point is.
Recently, Kaliman and Zaidenberg
[
24
]

showed that algebraic ellipticity and algebraic subellipticity are equivalent.
However, the proofs of the results in this section do not
depend on this fact, and it is usually easier to find dominating families
of sprays than a dominating spray.

We begin with the following approximation result for sections
of algebraically subelliptic submersions.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X
be an algebraically subelliptic submersion from an algebraic
manifold
Z
onto an affine algebraic manifold
X
. Fix a distance
function
\mathrm{dist}
on
Z
inducing its standard Hausdorff topology.
Let
K\subset X
be a compact
\mathscr{O}(X)
-convex subset,

B\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a compact convex set containing the origin,

U\supset K
and
V\supset B
be open neighbourhoods,
and
f_{\zeta,t}:U\to Z|_{U}

(\zeta\in V,\ t\in[0,1]^{n},\ n\geq 1)

be a continuous family of holomorphic sections of
\mathfrak{h}:Z|_{U}\to U

depending holomorphically on
\zeta\in V

such that
f_{0,0}
is homotopic through sections of
Z|_{U}\to U

to the restriction of an algebraic section
X\to Z
.
Given
\epsilon>0
, there is an algebraic map

F:X\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to Z
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\mathfrak{h}(F(x,\zeta,t))=x
for all
x\in X
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and
t\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(b)

\mathrm{dist}(F(x,\zeta,t),f_{\zeta,t}(x))<\epsilon
for every

x\in K
,
\zeta\in B
, and
t\in[0,1]^{n}
.

(c)

If
f_{0,0}=f|_{U}
for an algebraic section
f:X\to Z
, then
F

can be chosen such that
F(\cdotp,0,0)=f
.

(d)

Assume that
(c)
holds.
If
X_{0}
is a closed algebraic subvariety of
X
and

f_{0,t}
is independent of
t
on
X_{0}\cap U
,
then
F
can be chosen such that in addition
F(\cdotp,0,t)

agrees with
f
on
K\cap X_{0}
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

If we wish to ignore
B
, that is, holomorphic dependence on a parameter in
B
is not required, then we take
N=0
and
\mathbb{C}^{N}=B=V
to be a point.

Proof.

The basic case when
n=1
,
N=0
(so
\mathbb{C}^{N}=B=V
is a point),

f_{0}
extends to an algebraic section
f:X\to Z
, and without condition
(d) is given by
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]

(see also
[
15
, Theorem 6.15.3]
).
An inspection of the proof also gives part (d);
cf.
[
13
, Theorem 6.4]
.
See also Remark
2.4
below.
The multiparameter case
t\in[0,1]^{n}
with
n>1
and
f_{0}=f|_{U}
for an
algebraic section
f:X\to Z
is obtained by induction on the number
n

of parameters
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
; see

[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
.

If
f_{0}
is merely homotopic to the restriction
f|_{U}
of an algebraic
section
f:X\to Z
(the assumption in parts (a) and (b) of
the theorem), we proceed as follows, still assuming that

B
is a point. By shrinking
U
around
K

we may assume that
U
is a Stein domain.
By the Oka principle, the homotopy from
f_{0}
to
f|_{U}

can be chosen to consist of holomorphic sections

[
15
, Theorem 5.4.4]
.
We parametrise this homotopy by the segment

T=\{(s,\ldots,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:-1\leq s\leq 0\}
, so that
f|_{U}
corresponds to the
point
(-1,\ldots,-1)\in T
and
f_{0}
to the origin
0=(0,\ldots,0)
.
Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the convex hull
of
T\cup[0,1]^{n}
. There is an affine diffeomorphism

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\psi([0,1]^{n})=C
,

\psi(0,\ldots,0)=(-1,\ldots,-1)
, and
\psi
fixes the
remaining vertices of
[0,1]^{n}
. Note that
\psi
complexifies
to a complex affine isomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Clearly, there is a strong deformation retraction

\tau:C\to T\cup[0,1]^{n}
. In particular,
\tau
restricts to the identity
map on
T\cup[0,1]^{n}
. Let
L=\psi^{-1}([0,1]^{n})\subset[0,1]^{n}
.
The composition
\phi=\tau\circ\psi:[0,1]^{n}\to C
is a surjective map with

\tau\circ\psi(0,\ldots,0)=(-1,\ldots,-1)
, which maps
L
onto

[0,1]^{n}
by
\psi
(since
\tau
is the identity on
[0,1]^{n}
).
We now apply the already proved case of the theorem
to the homotopy
\tilde{f}_{t}=f_{\phi(t)}:U\to Z|_{U}
with
t\in[0,1]^{n}

to approximate it by the restriction of an algebraic map

\widetilde{F}:M\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to Z
. Then the algebraic map
F:M\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to Z

given by
F(x,t)=\widetilde{F}(x,\psi^{-1}(t))
satisfies conditions (a) and (b)
in the theorem, while conditions (c) and (d) are vacuous.

Assume now that
N>0
. We may take
V\supset B
to be an open convex
domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Note that a family of holomorphic sections

f_{\zeta,t}:U\to Z|_{U}

(t\in[0,1]^{n})
of
\mathfrak{h}:Z|_{U}\to U
,
depending holomorphically on
\zeta\in V
and continuously on
t\in[0,1]^{n}
,
is the same thing as a homotopy of holomorphic sections

\tilde{f}_{t}

(t\in[0,1]^{n})
of the algebraic submersion

(2.2)

\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}:\widetilde{Z}=Z\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\widetilde{X}=X\times\mathbb{C}^{N}

over the domain
U\times V\subset\widetilde{X}
, where

\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}(z,\zeta)=(\mathfrak{h}(z),\zeta)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tilde{f}_{t}(x,\zeta)=(f_{\zeta,t}(x),\zeta)\ \ \text{for $x\in U$ and $\zeta\in V$}.

Note that the set
K\times B
is holomorphically convex in
M\times\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
By trivially extending the algebraic fibre sprays
(E_{j},\pi_{j},s_{j})
in the
hypothesis of the theorem (see (
2.1
)) to algebraic
fibre sprays on
\widetilde{Z}
we see that the extended submersion

\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}:\widetilde{Z}\to\widetilde{X}
is also algebraically subelliptic.
We introduce an extra parameter
s\in[0,1]
and consider the
continuous family of holomorphic sections

f^{\prime}_{s,t}:U\times V\to\widetilde{Z}
of
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
defined by

f^{\prime}_{s,t}(x,\zeta)=(f_{s\zeta,t}(x),\zeta)\in\widetilde{Z}\ \ \text{for}\ \ x\in U,\ \zeta\in V,\ s\in\mathbb{R},\ t\in[0,1]^{n}.

Note that
f^{\prime}_{1,t}=\tilde{f}_{t}
,
f^{\prime}_{0,t}(x,\zeta)=(f_{0,t}(x),\zeta)
, and

f^{\prime}_{0,0}(x,\zeta)=(f_{0,0}(x),\zeta)
extends to the algebraic section

(x,\zeta)\mapsto(f(x),\zeta)
of the submersion (
2.2
).
The already established case of the theorem with
approximation on
K\times B\times[0,1]^{n+1}
yields an algebraic
map
\widetilde{F}:X\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to\widetilde{Z}
satisfying
the conclusion of the theorem with respect to the homotopy
f^{\prime}_{s,t}

(with
(s,t)\in[0,1]^{n+1}
) of holomorphic sections of the submersion
(
2.2
). Setting
s=1
and postcomposing
\widetilde{F}
by the trivial
projection
\widetilde{Z}=Z\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\to Z
yields an algebraic map

F:X\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to Z
satisfying the
theorem with respect to the submersion
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Every algebraic fibre bundle
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X
with
algebraically elliptic fibre is algebraically subelliptic.
Hence, Theorem
2.1
applies in this case.

Proof.

Note that
X
is covered by finitely many Zariski open domains
U_{i}

such that the restriction
\mathfrak{h}:Z|_{U_{i}}\to U_{i}
is a trivial fibre bundle
with algebraically elliptic fibre
A
for every
i
, so it admits a
dominating algebraic fibre spray obtained by a trivial extension
of a dominating algebraic spray on
A
.
A straightforward generalisation of
[
15
, Proposition 6.4.2]

gives the localisation of algebraic subellipticity from sprays to fibre sprays,
and hence we obtain finitely many algebraic fibre sprays on
Z
which satisfy
the hypotheses of the theorem. The argument is the same
as in the proof of
[
15
, Proposition 6.4.2]
.
The only difference is that, in the case of fibre sprays, the spray maps
into the total space of the fibre bundle must respect the fibres.
∎

Since the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
(
1.2
) is flexible
and hence algebraically elliptic,
we have the following immediate corollary.
The last statement is
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
;
it can also be seen as an immediate corollary of the results in
[
28
,
9
]
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
M
be an affine Riemann surface and
\mathcal{A}\to M
be
the algebraic subbundle of
(T^{*}M)^{\oplus n}
whose fibre is the null quadric

\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. Then the conclusion of
Theorem
2.1
holds for sections
M\to\mathcal{A}
.
Furthermore, every section of
\mathcal{A}
over
M
is homotopic to an
algebraic section.

Remark 2.4
.

As noted already, the basic case of Theorem
2.1

with the parameter
t\in[0,1]
and without
\zeta
-parameters coincides
with
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]

(see also
[
15
, Theorem 6.15.3]
).
In the last part of the proof of this result in
[
14
, p. 251]

the author referred to a small extension of the Oka–Weil theorem
without being explicit. The point is to approximate the given homotopy

\{\eta^{m}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
(using the notation in the cited paper)
of holomorphic sections of a trivial vector bundle
over
X
, depending continuously on
t\in[0,1]
, by a homotopy which is
holomorphic in
t
in a neighbourhood of
[0,1]\subset\mathbb{C}
, and
applying the Oka–Weil theorem to approximate by a polynomial
map in all variables. We spell out this argument in greater generality
since we shall need it. In the next result, the relevant parameter
is called
p
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
Z
and
X
be complex manifolds,
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X
be a holomorphic submersion onto
X
,
K
be a compact
subset of
X
with a basis of Stein neighbourhoods, and

U\subset X
be an open set containing
K
.
Assume that
f:U\times[0,1]\to Z
is a continuous map such that

\mathfrak{h}(f(x,p))=x
for all
(x,p)\in U\times[0,1]

and the section
f_{p}=f(\cdotp,p):U\to Z|_{U}
of
\mathfrak{h}

is holomorphic for every
p\in[0,1]
. Then
we can approximate
f
uniformly on
K\times[0,1]
by holomorphic maps

F:U^{\prime}\times V^{\prime}\to Z
, defined on open neighbourhoods

U^{\prime}\times V^{\prime}\subset X\times\mathbb{C}
of
K\times[0,1]
, such that

\mathfrak{h}(F(x,p))=x
for all
(x,p)\in U^{\prime}\times V^{\prime}
and

F(\cdotp,p)=f(\cdotp,p)
for
p=0,1
.
Furthermore, a homotopy
f^{t}:U\times[0,1]\to Z
of maps
as above, depending continuously on
t\in[0,1]
, can be
approximated by a homotopy of holomorphic maps
F^{t}:U^{\prime}\times V^{\prime}\to Z
having the stated properties for every
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let us first consider the special case when
\mathfrak{h}:Z=X\times\mathbb{C}\to X

is the trivial submersion with fibre
\mathbb{C}
. In this case, a section
of
\mathfrak{h}
can be identified with a map to
\mathbb{C}
.
Thus, let
f:U\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous function such that

f(\cdotp,p)
is holomorphic on
U
for every
p\in[0,1]
.
Choose a partition
0=p_{0}<p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{k}=1
of
[0,1]

and set
p_{-1}=-1
and
p_{k+1}=2
. Let
1=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\chi_{i}

be a partition of unity on
[0,1]
, where
\chi_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
is a
smooth function supported in
(p_{i-1},p_{i+1})
for each
i=0,1,\ldots,k
.
Note that
\chi_{i}(p_{i})=1
for
i=0,1,\ldots,k
. By a theorem of
Weierstrass we can approximate
\chi_{i}
as closely as desired
uniformly on
[-1,2]
by a holomorphic polynomial
g_{i}
on
\mathbb{C}

with
g_{i}(p_{i})=1
. Assuming that the partition
\{p_{i}\}
is fine enough
and the approximation of
\chi_{i}
by
g_{i}
is close enough, the
holomorphic function

F(x,p)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}f(x,p_{i})g_{i}(p),\quad x\in U,\ p\in\mathbb{C},

approximates
f
as closely as desired on
U\times[0,1]
.

We now consider the general case.
The assumption on the compact set
K\subset X
implies that it
has a basis of open Stein neighbourhoods in
X
. Hence, we may
assume that the domain
U\subset X
is Stein and
K
is
\mathscr{O}(U)
-convex.
Consider the graph of
f
over
K\times[0,1]
:

\Sigma=\bigl\{(f(x,p),p):x\in K,\ p\in[0,1]\bigr\}\subset Z\times\mathbb{C}.

By
[
18
, Corollary 2.2]

(see also
[
15
, Corollary 3.6.6]
),

\Sigma
admits an open Stein neighbourhood
\Theta
in
Z\times\mathbb{C}

such that
\Sigma
is
\mathscr{O}(\Theta)
-convex.
By the standard method, embedding
\Theta
in a Euclidean
space and using a holomorphic retraction from a neighbourhood of

\Sigma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N}
onto
\Sigma
, we reduce the proof
to the already established case of approximating functions.
(The details of this argument can be found for example in

[
15
, proof of Theorem 3.8.1, p. 90]
.)
The same proof applies to homotopies
f^{t}

(t\in[0,1])
by
using a partition of unity in the parameter.
∎

We now modify Theorem
2.1
under the additional
assumption that the algebraic submersion
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X
admits
a fibre dominating algebraic spray
(E,\pi,s)
defined on a trivial vector bundle

\pi:E=Z\times\mathbb{C}^{r}\to Z
. Under this assumption, we have the following
approximation result in which the data and the approximants depend continuously on a parameter in a compact Hausdorff space.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}:Z\to X
be an algebraic submersion from an algebraic
variety
Z
onto an affine algebraic variety
X
.
Assume that there is a fibre dominating algebraic spray
(E,\pi,s)

on

defined on a trivial vector bundle
\pi:E=Z\times\mathbb{C}^{r}\to Z
.
Fix a distance function
\mathrm{dist}
on
Z
inducing its standard Hausdorff topology.
Let
K\subset X
be a compact
\mathscr{O}(X)
-convex
subset,
B\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a compact convex set
containing the origin,
U\supset K
and
V\supset B

be open neighbourhoods,
Q
be a closed subspace of a compact Hausdorff space

, and
f_{\zeta,p,t}:U\to Z|_{U}

(\zeta\in V,\ p\in P,\ t\in[0,1])

be a continuous family of holomorphic sections of
\mathfrak{h}:Z|_{U}\to U

depending holomorphically on
\zeta\in V
such that

is an algebraic section for every

holds for every

.

Given
\epsilon>0
, there is a continuous map

F:X\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\times P\times\mathbb{C}\to Z
such that the map

F(\cdotp,\cdotp,p,t):X\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\to Z

is algebraic for every
(p,t)\in P\times[0,1]

and the following conditions hold:

(a)

\mathfrak{h}(F(x,\zeta,p,t))=x
for all
x\in X
,

, and

\vartheta

on

By

to the given finite order

Choose a compact set

, where

.

Choose a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
1
-form
\theta

on

\displaystyle f_{\zeta}\ \ \text{for all $\zeta\in B_{1}$},

\displaystyle j^{k}_{Q}(f_{0,0})\ \ \text{for all $\tau\in B_{2}$, and}

of finite total curvature such that
\tilde{u}_{0}=u_{0}
,

\mathrm{Flux}_{\tilde{u}_{t}}=\mathrm{Flux}_{u_{t}}=\mathcal{F}_{t}
for all

proper algebraic null curve

.
∎

In order to justify the discussion below Theorem

concerning the case when the immersion

given in the theorem is not complete, we fix

and modify the above proof by choosing the functions

\xi_{j}:[0,1]\to[1,\infty)
such that (

) holds for all

.
Following the proof without any further modifications, we conclude that the conformal minimal immersion

in (

5. The h-principle for complete nonflat minimal surfaces

of finite total curvature

Assume that
M
is an affine Riemann surface,

\mathscr{A}_{*}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
is the space of nonflat algebraic
1-forms on
M
with values in the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
(
1.2
),
and
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
is the subset of
\mathscr{A}_{*}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})

consisting of
1
-forms with an effective pole at every end of
M
.
We denote by
\mathcal{A}\to M
the algebraic fibre bundle
whose sections are 1-forms on
M
with values in
\mathbf{A}
.

The main aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
.
To this end, we first prove the following h-principle for the
(1,0)
-differential

\partial
, applied on either of the spaces

\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})\subset\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})

and taking values in
\mathscr{A}_{*}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
or
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
M
is an affine Riemann surface.
Let
Q
be a closed subspace of a compact Hausdorff space
P
,

u:M\times Q\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be a continuous map
such that
u_{p}=u(\cdotp,p)\in\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in Q
, and

\phi:M\times P\to\mathcal{A}
be a continuous map such that

(a)

\phi_{p}=\phi(\cdotp,p)\in\mathscr{A}_{*}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
for every
p\in P
, and

(b)

\partial u_{p}=\phi_{p}
for every
p\in Q
.

Then there is a homotopy
\phi^{t}:M\times P\to\mathcal{A}
,
t\in[0,1]
,
such that
\phi^{0}=\phi
and the following conditions hold.

(i)

\phi^{t}_{p}=\phi^{t}(\cdotp,p)\in\mathscr{A}_{*}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
for every

p\in P
and
t\in[0,1]
.

(ii)

\phi^{t}_{p}=\phi_{p}
for every
p\in Q
and
t\in[0,1]
.

(iii)

\phi^{1}_{p}\in\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
for every
p\in P
.

(iv)

\Re\int_{C}\phi^{1}_{p}=0
for every
p\in P
and
[C]\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

If
(a)
is replaced by the stronger assumption

(a’)

\phi_{p}=\phi(\cdotp,p)\in\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
for every
p\in P
,

then conditions
(i)
and
(iii)
can be replaced by

(i’)

\phi^{t}_{p}\in\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})

for every
p\in P
and
t\in[0,1]
.

If
u_{p}\in\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})
for all
p\in Q
, then
(iv)
can be replaced by

(iv’)

\int_{C}\phi^{1}_{p}=0
for every
p\in P
and
[C]\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

It follows from (iii) and (iv) that the maps
u^{1}_{p}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

p\in P
, defined by

u^{1}_{p}(x)=c_{p}+\Re\int_{x_{0}}^{x}2\phi^{1}_{p}\quad\text{for}\ x\in M,

for a fixed
x_{0}\in M
and with suitably chosen constants

c_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
p\in P
, form a continuous family

P\to\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
p\mapsto u^{1}_{p}
,
of complete nonflat conformal minimal immersions of finite total
curvature such that
u^{1}_{p}=u_{p}
for all
p\in Q
.
(The map
c:P\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be taken to be any continuous extension
of the map
Q\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
p\mapsto u_{p}(x_{0})
.)
In case (iv’), the maps
h^{1}_{p}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

h^{1}_{p}(x)=c_{p}+\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\phi^{1}_{p}\quad\text{for}\ x\in M,

with suitable constants
c_{p}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

,
are proper nonflat algebraic null curves.

Proof.

We shall use the notation established in the previous two sections.
Pick a point
q\in M
and a family
\mathscr{C}=\{C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{s}\}

of closed oriented curves in
M
based at

which form a basis of

H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
and such that

is a connected
compact Runge set in
M
.
Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the associated period map (
3.1
).
Denote by
E=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}=\overline{M}\setminus M

the set of ends of
M
. Let
L\subset M
be a compact set such that

|\mathscr{C}|\subset L
and
M\setminus L=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}D_{j}\setminus\{x_{j}\}
,
where
D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}
are discs in
\overline{M}
with pairwise disjoint
closures and
x_{j}\in D_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Choose a nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-form
\theta
on
M

and write
\phi_{p}=f_{p}\theta
, where
f_{p}:M\to\mathbf{A}
is a
holomorphic map depending continuously on
p\in P
.
By
[
16
, Theorem 5.3]
,
there is a homotopy
f^{t}:M\times P\to\mathbf{A}

(t\in[0,1])
such that

f^{t}_{p}:=f^{t}(\cdotp,p):M\to\mathbf{A}
is a nonflat holomorphic map
for every
(p,t)\in P\times[0,1]
satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

f^{t}_{p}=f_{p}
for every

(p,t)\in(P\times\{0\})\cup(Q\times[0,1])
, and

(2)

\Re\mathcal{P}(f^{1}_{p})=0
for every
p\in P
.

If
u_{p}\in\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})
for all
p\in P
then
condition (2) can be replaced by

(2’)

\mathcal{P}(f^{1}_{p})=0
for every
p\in P
.

Although
[
16
, Theorem 5.3]

is stated for the case when

are Euclidean compacts,
the proof holds for any pair of compacts, just as

[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Pick an open Runge neighbourhood
V\Subset M
of
L
.
By
[
5
, Lemma 3.2.1]
,
there is a
\mathcal{P}
-period dominating spray of holomorphic maps

f^{t}_{\zeta,p}:V\to\mathbf{A}
, depending continuously on
(p,t)\in P\times[0,1]

and holomorphically on
\zeta\in B\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
where

is a ball around the origin in some
\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
such that
f^{t}_{0,p}=f^{t}_{p}|_{V}
for all
(p,t)\in P\times[0,1]
.

We now adapt to this situation the device,
used in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, to ensure effective poles
of the approximating algebraic 1-forms at all ends of

.
Pick a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
1
-form

\theta_{0}
on a neighbourhood of the compact set

D=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\overline{D}_{j}
in
\overline{M}
.
Also, choose a closed ball
B^{\prime}\subset B
containing the origin
in its interior. For every
j=1,\ldots,m
, we choose a point

x^{\prime}_{j}\in D_{j}\setminus\{x_{j}\}
sufficiently close to
x_{j}
such that

x^{\prime}_{j}\notin\overline{V}
and for every
p\in Q
we have

(5.1)

|f_{p}(x^{\prime}_{j})|\,\cdotp|(\theta/\theta_{0})(x^{\prime}_{j})|>\sup\{|f^{1}_{\zeta,p}(x)|\,\cdotp|(\theta/\theta_{0})(x)|:x\in bD_{j},\ \zeta\in B^{\prime}\}.

(Note that
\theta/\theta_{0}
is a holomorphic function on

Since the algebraic 1-form
\phi_{p}=f_{p}\theta
on
M

has a pole at

for every

has no zeros on

) holds

sufficiently close to

.
We can find for every

(1+r_{j}(p))|f_{p}(x^{\prime}_{j})|\,\cdotp|(\theta/\theta_{0})(x^{\prime}_{j})|>\sup\{|f^{1}_{\zeta,p}(x)|\cdotp|(\theta/\theta_{0})(x)|:x\in bD_{j},\ \zeta\in B^{\prime}\}

holds for every

) this holds for

and it then holds for all

is chosen sufficiently

to a small disc neighbourhood

f^{t}_{\zeta,p}(x)=(1+tr_{j}(p))f_{p}(x)\ \ \text{for all $x\in W_{j}$, $\zeta\in B$, $p\in P$, and $t\in[0,1]$}.

Set

Since the homology basis

Recall that

. By Corollary

, we can approximate
the homotopy of holomorphic sections

If the approximation is close enough,

|(\tilde{\phi}^{1}_{\zeta,p}/\theta_{0})(x^{\prime}_{j})|>\sup\big\{|(\tilde{\phi}^{1}_{\zeta,p}/\theta_{0})(x)|:x\in bD_{j},\ \zeta\in B^{\prime}\big\}

|f_{p}(x^{\prime}_{j})|\,\cdotp|(\theta/\theta_{0})(x^{\prime}_{j})|>\sup\{|f^{t}_{\zeta,p}(x)|\,\cdotp|(\theta/\theta_{0})(x)|:x\in bD_{j},\ \zeta\in B^{\prime},\ t\in[0,1]\}.

Hence, (

) holds with the functions

and
with

on the right hand side replaced by

for any

. Defining the extension of

to

as in (

for

as in (

Assuming that the approximation of

by

If (2’) holds, we can obtain

∎

The proof of Theorem

also establishes the following result.
We state it for more general cones

,

.
We denote by
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,A)
the space of nondegenerate

-forms with values in

, and by
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\infty}(M,A)

the space of those

-forms in

that have a pole at

.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M
be an affine Riemann surface
and

be a smooth connected flexible
cone (

) not contained in any hyperplane.
Then
\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\infty}(M,A)
is an open dense

\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,A)
and the inclusion

\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\infty}(M,A)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,A)

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.8
, the proof of Theorem
5.1

applies to any cone

as in the proposition. Given a compact Hausdorff
space
P
, a closed subspace
Q\subset P
, and a continuous
map
\phi:P\to\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,A)
such that
\phi(p)\in\mathscr{A}^{1}_{\infty}(M,A)

for

, we have seen that

can be approximated
by continuous maps

for all

.

∎

We also need the following result, which
holds in particular for the null quadric.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be an affine Riemann surface and

A\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
n\geq 2
, be a smooth connected
flexible cone not contained in any hyperplane. Then

\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,A)
is a dense open subset of
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,A)

and the inclusion

\mathscr{A}^{1}_{*}(M,A)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,A)

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}\to M
be the algebraic subbundle of
(T^{*}M)^{\oplus n}

determined by the cone
A
. If
TM
is algebraically trivial, the conclusion
follows from the algebraic analogue of the
general position theorem
[
16
, Theorem 5.4]
.
The flexibility of
A
allows us to replace the complete holomorphic vector
fields, used in
[
16
, proof of Theorem 5.4]
,
by complete algebraic vector fields on
A
with algebraic flows (that is, LNDs).
If
TM
is not algebraically trivial, choose a Zariski chart
U\subset M

(the complement of finitely many points in
M
) such that
TU

is algebraically trivial. The previous argument holds over
U

by using LNDs tangent to the fibre
A
of the trivial bundle

\mathcal{A}|_{U}\to U
. Each of them can be extended to
\mathcal{A}
by
multiplication with an algebraic function on
M
that vanishes to
a sufficiently high order on the finite set

. This gives
LNDs on
\mathcal{A}
tangent to the fibres of the projection

\mathcal{A}\to M
and vanishing over
M\setminus U
.
By using their flows, the proof can be completed as in

[
16
, proof of Theorem 5.4]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By a standard argument,
Theorem
5.1
implies that the
(1,0)
-differential
\partial
is a weak
homotopy equivalence as a map from
\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
or

\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})
to
\mathscr{A}_{*}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
or its subspace

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
. Namely, to obtain an
epimorphism at the level of
\pi_{0}
, we take
P
to be a point and
Q
to
be empty, and to obtain a monomorphism at the level of
\pi_{k-1}
and,
at the same time, an epimorphism at the level of
\pi_{k}
for each
k\geq 1
,
we take
P
to be the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
Q
to be its boundary,
the unit
(k-1)
-sphere. Since the inclusion

\mathscr{A}_{*}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
is a weak homotopy equivalence
by Proposition
5.3
, the same conclusion
holds for the maps
\partial:\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
and

\partial:\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,\mathbf{A})
.

If two of the maps in a commutative diagram of three maps are weak
homotopy equivalences, then so is the third map. It follows that the inclusion

\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak homotopy equivalence as well.
∎

The inclusion
\Re\mathrm{NC}_{*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{CMI}_{*}(M,\mathbb{R}^{n})

also satisfies an h-principle, analogous to the corresponding h-principle
in the holomorphic category
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
.
The basic h-principle for this inclusion is given by Theorem
1.2
.
For the parametric case and more general flexible cones, see Theorem

6.2
.

We remark that Theorem
5.1
can be upgraded with approximation,
(jet) interpolation, and flux conditions. We will not deal
with the first two generalisations, which are well understood from
earlier works (see Chapters 3 and 4 in
[
5
]
),
but will discuss the third in the following section.

.

7. Algebraic homotopies and algebraically elliptic cones

As mentioned in the introduction, the recent

[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
provides a good
sufficient condition for the punctured cone
A\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, on a
connected submanifold
Y
of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
with
\dim Y\geq 1
to be
algebraically elliptic, namely that
Y
is uniformly rational. By contrast, the
collection of known flexible cones is very small and consists of a few classes of
examples (luckily, the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
is among them).
This is no surprise: it is more difficult to construct algebraic flows than
dominating families of algebraic sprays.
The main classes of examples consist of the cones
A

that are defined by a flag manifold or a toric manifold

Y

[
12
]
.
Other examples are given in
[
32
,
29
,
33
]
.

In view of this, it is of interest to explore to what extent the flexibility assumption
on the cone
A
in Theorem
1.4
can be relaxed to
A
being
algebraically elliptic.
We are unable to prove Theorem
1.4
under this weaker assumption,
but partial results can be obtained. First, Theorem
1.3

(or
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
) shows that for every
algebraically elliptic connected smooth cone
A\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
which is not contained
in any hyperplane, the map
\mathscr{I}_{*}(M,A)\to\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,A)
,

h\mapsto dh
, in (
1.8
)
induces a surjection of path components of the two spaces.
The next important question is whether this map is also injective
on path components. We provide a partial answer
by introducing the following notion of algebraic homotopy.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to M
be an algebraic map onto an affine algebraic
variety
M
. Algebraic sections
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}:M\to\mathcal{A}
of
\pi

are
algebraically homotopic
, abbreviated
a-homotopic
,
if there is an algebraic map
\phi:M\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{A}

such that
\phi(\cdotp,t):M\to\mathcal{A}
is a section of
\pi

for every
t\in\mathbb{C}
and
\phi(\cdot,t)=\phi_{t}
for
t=0,1
.

Using this new notion, we have the following result,
asserting that a pair of proper nondegenerate
A
-immersions
with a-homotopic differentials lie in the same connected
component of the space
\mathscr{I}_{*}(M,A)
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
M
be an affine Riemann surface,
A\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
be a smooth connected algebraically elliptic punctured cone
not contained in any hyperplane,
\mathcal{A}\subset(T^{*}M)^{\oplus n}
be the algebraic fibre bundle over
M

with fibre
A
, and
h_{0},h_{1}:M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be proper nondegenerate
algebraic
A
-immersions. If the differentials
dh_{0}
and
dh_{1}
are
a-homotopic sections of
\mathcal{A}
,
then there is a homotopy of proper nondegenerate algebraic
A
-immersions

h_{t}:M\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
t\in[0,1]
, joining
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
.

Proof.

The assumptions imply that there is an algebraic map

\phi:M\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{A}
such that

\phi_{p}=\phi(\cdotp,p):M\to\mathcal{A}
is a section for every
p\in\mathbb{C}

and
\phi_{p}=dh_{p}
for
p=0,1
. Since
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
are nondegenerate,

\phi_{p}
is nondegenerate for all but finitely many values of

p\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence, there is an embedded
real analytic arc
P\subset\mathbb{C}
connecting
0
and
1
such that

\phi_{p}
is nondegenerate for every
p\in P
.
Let
Q=\{0,1\}\subset P
and let
\theta
be a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
1-form on
M
. By
[
16
, Theorem 5.3]
,
there is a homotopy
\phi_{p}^{t}=f_{p}^{t}\theta

(p\in P,\ t\in[0,1])

of nondegenerate holomorphic sections of the bundle
\mathcal{A}\to M

(that is,
f_{p}^{t}:M\to A
is a nondegenerate holomorphic map
for each
(p,t)
) such that

(i)

\phi_{p}^{t}=\phi_{p}
for every
(p,t)\in(P\times\{0\})\cup(Q\times[0,1])
,
and

(ii)

\phi_{p}^{1}
has vanishing periods on all closed curves in
M

for every
p\in P
.

Choose a basis
\mathscr{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}\}
of

and a compact set

as in the proof of

1.2
, so that

of pairwise disjoint punctured discs around the ends of

.
By
[

, Lemma 3.2.1]
,
we can find a
\mathscr{C}
-period dominating spray of nondegenerate

\phi_{\zeta,p}^{t}:V\to\mathcal{A}|_{V}
over
a neighbourhood

of

, depending holomorphically
on

, where

is a ball around the origin in

,
and continuously on the parameters

,
such that
\phi_{0,p}^{t}=\phi_{p}^{t}
for all

.

Choose a smaller ball

around the origin.
By Proposition

, applied to the extended bundle

,
we can approximate

uniformly on

by a homotopy

\widetilde{\phi}_{\zeta,p}^{t}
of nondegenerate holomorphic sections of

\mathcal{A}\to M
which depends holomorphically on

.
Thus, we may view
\widetilde{\phi}_{0,\cdotp}^{t}

over the domain

After slightly shrinking the ball

,

(see in particular part (d))
allows us to approximate the family

such that the period dominating spray of algebraic sections

agrees with

-immersions

connecting

and

If in addition

8. Local contractibility of algebraic mapping spaces

A topological space
X
is said to be locally contractible if for every point
x\in X
and every neighbourhood
U
of
x
, there is a neighbourhood
V\subset U
of
x
such that the inclusion
V\hookrightarrow U
is homotopic to a constant map. CW complexes and absolute neighbourhood retracts are locally contractible, as are, obviously, locally convex topological vector spaces.
Corollary
2.8
would allow us to determine the weak homotopy type of many algebraic mapping spaces if we knew that they were locally contractible in the compact-open topology.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X
be an affine algebraic variety and
Y
be a flexible algebraic manifold. Suppose that the space
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
of algebraic maps
X\to Y
is locally contractible with respect to the compact-open topology. Then the inclusion
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
induces an injection of path components and an isomorphism of homotopy groups in every degree.

Remark 8.2
.

(a) If
Y
is the prototypical flexible algebraic manifold
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, then, as a topological vector space,
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
is locally convex and hence locally contractible. We do not at present know how to prove or disprove local contractibility for more general manifolds.

(b) If
X
is an affine algebraic manifold and
Y
is a flexible algebraic manifold, the inclusion
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
may or may not induce a surjection of path components. For example, let
\Sigma^{n}
denote the complex
n
-sphere
\{(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}:z_{0}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n}^{2}=1\}
. As a smooth affine algebraic variety,
\Sigma^{n}
is flexible for all
n\geq 2
(see
[
5
, Proposition 1.15.3]
). Loday
[
27
]

showed that for
p
and
q
odd, every algebraic map
\Sigma^{p}\times\Sigma^{q}\to\Sigma^{p+q}
is null-homotopic, whereas there are non-null-homotopic continuous maps
\Sigma^{p}\times\Sigma^{q}\to\Sigma^{p+q}
(see also
[
15
, Example 6.15.7]
). On the other hand, it follows from the results in
[
8
]
that
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
does induce a surjection of path components if
X
is an affine Riemann surface and
Y
belongs to a class of punctured cones that includes the large cone
\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and the null quadric
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
.

(c) Under the assumptions of the theorem,
\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
and
\mathscr{C}(X,Y)
are absolute neighbourhood retracts and hence locally contractible
[
25
, Proposition 7 and Theorem 9]
. Also,

X
is Stein and
Y
is algebraically elliptic and hence Oka, so the inclusion
\mathscr{O}(X,Y)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}(X,Y)
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

We shall prove, at the same time, that the inclusion

\mathscr{A}(X,Y)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
induces a
monomorphism at the level of
\pi_{k-1}
and an epimorphism
at the level of
\pi_{k}
for all
k\geq 2
.

Let
B
be the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with boundary sphere
S
.
Choose a base point
b\in S
. Let
f:B\to\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
be continuous with

f(S)\subset\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
. What is required is to deform
f
, keeping
f(b)

fixed and keeping
f(S)
in
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
, until all of
f(B)
lies in

\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
. We will in fact prove a bit more.

View
B
as
S\times[0,1]
with
S\times\{1\}
identified to the origin
o
and view
f
as a continuous map
g:S\times[0,1]\to\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
, constant on
S\times\{1\}
and taking
S\times\{0\}
into
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
. By the algebraic homotopy approximation theorem (the original version
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
suffices),
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
is dense in any path component of
\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
that it intersects. Also,
\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
, being an absolute neighbourhood retract, is locally path connected. Hence,
f(o)\in\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
can be deformed to an algebraic map (in fact by an arbitrarily small deformation), so we may assume that
f(o)\in\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
.

By Corollary
2.8
,
applied with the parameter spaces
P=S
and
Q=\varnothing
,

g
can be approximated on
S\times[0,1]
by continuous maps
G:S\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
with
G=g
on
S\times\{0\}
. Since
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
is locally contractible by assumption, if the approximation is close enough,
G|_{S\times\{1\}}
is homotopic to the constant map with value
f(o)
through maps with values in an arbitrarily small neighbourhood of
f(o)
in
\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
.

This shows that
f
may be approximated arbitrarily closely by maps

F:B\to\mathscr{A}(X,Y)
that equal
f
on
S
. Since, again,
\mathscr{O}(X,Y)

is an absolute neighbourhood retract, if the approximation is close enough,
then
f
and
F
are homotopic as maps
B\to\mathscr{O}(X,Y)
by a
homotopy that is constant on
S

[
21
, Theorem IV.1.2]
.
∎

Now let
M
be an affine Riemann surface and
A\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}

itself or the null quadric
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{C}_{*}^{n}

Theorem 8.3
.

With assumptions as above, suppose that the space
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,A)
is locally contractible. Then the inclusion
\mathscr{A}^{1}(M,A)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{O}^{1}(M,A)
induces an injection of path components and an isomorphism of homotopy groups in every degree. If

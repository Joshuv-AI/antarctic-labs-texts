Essential normality of quotient modules vs. Hilbert-Schmidtness of submodules in H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 2 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
Penghui Wang, Chong Zhao, Zeyou Zhu

Abstract
In the present paper, the essential normality of non-algebraic quotient modules over the polydisc is considered. We prove that any quotient module in an integer-weighted Bergman module over the bidisk, associated to a finitely generated submodule containing a certain distinguished polynomial, is essentially normal. As an application, we prove that each finitely generated submodule containing a nonzero polynomial is Hilbert-Schmidt, which partially solves the conjecture of Yang [ 45 ] .

Introduction

In the present paper, we consider the essential normality of non-algebraic quotient modules in the weighted Bergman modules over the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{d}
for
d>1

\mathbb{D}^{d}
is defined by

{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})=\left\{f\in\text{Hol}(\mathbb{D}^{d}):\|f\|_{\alpha}^{2}=c_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d}}|f(z)|^{2}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{\alpha}\text{dV}_{d}(z)<\infty\right\},

where
\text{dV}_{d}
is the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{D}^{d}
and

is the constant that normalizes the measure
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{\alpha}\text{dV}_{d}
. It can be verified that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
is a Hilbert space with reproducing kernel

K_{\lambda,\alpha}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-\bar{\lambda}_{i}z_{i})^{-\alpha-2},\quad\lambda,z\in\mathbb{D}^{d}.

(1.1)

The weighted Bergman space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
can be viewed as a Hilbert module
[
5

15

\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]

\mathcal{M}
of

that is invariant under the module action is called a submodule, and
\mathcal{N}={\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}
is called the associated quotient module. The module actions on submodules and quotient modules are given by

p\cdot h=R_{p,\mathcal{M}}h,~p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}],~h\in\mathcal{M}

and

p\cdot g=S_{p,\mathcal{N}}g,~p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}],~g\in\mathcal{N}

R_{p,\mathcal{M}}=M_{p}|_{\mathcal{M}}
and
S_{p,\mathcal{N}}=P_{\mathcal{N}}M_{p}|_{\mathcal{N}}
, and

is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{N}
. For brevity, we write

R_{p}=R_{p,\mathcal{M}}\text{ and }S_{p}=S_{p,\mathcal{N}}

if there is no confusion on the modules being referred to. A quotient module
\mathcal{N}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
is said to be essentially normal if all the commutators
[S_{p}^{*},S_{p}]

are compact. Let

be the ideal of compact operators on

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})

{\mathcal{A}}_{-1}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
to refer to

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})

To precisely characterize the essential normality of quotient modules over

-essentially unitary, and
P_{[I]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[I]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}.

Proof.

By
[
42
, Proposition 3.1]
we have
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z(I)\leq 1
. In the case
I
is primary and homogeneous,
Z(I)\cap\mathbb{D}^{d}
is a disc, hence
[
42
, Theorem 2.17]
shows that
P_{[I]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[I]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}
.

In the case
I
is homogeneous, let
I=\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}I_{k}
be an irredundant primary decomposition of
I
. Then
P_{[I_{k}]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[I_{k}]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}
for
1\leq k\leq n
.
According to the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 3.2]
, there is a positive constant
c
such that
P_{[I]^{\perp}}\leq c\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{[I_{k}]^{\perp}}
, hence there is a bounded linear operator
A
such that
P_{[I]^{\perp}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{[I_{k}]^{\perp}}A
. Consequently

P_{[I]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[I]^{\perp}}=P_{[I]}M_{z_{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{[I_{k}]^{\perp}}A=P_{[I]}\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{[I_{k}]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[I_{k}]^{\perp}}A\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}.

In the general case, the linear mapping
\Psi_{\mathbf{K}}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
defined by
z^{\alpha}\mapsto z^{(\alpha_{1}K_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}K_{d})}

is an isometry with
\Psi_{\mathbf{K}}M_{z_{i}}=M_{z_{i}}^{K_{i}}\Psi_{\mathbf{K}},~1\leq i\leq d
. Clearly the ideal
\tilde{I}
generated by
\Psi_{\mathbf{K}}(I)
is homogeneous and distinguished. The argument in the preceding paragraph shows that
P_{[\tilde{I}]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[\tilde{I}]^{\perp}}
lies in
\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}
, and recursively we can prove that

P_{[\tilde{I}]}M_{z_{i}}^{n}P_{[\tilde{I}]^{\perp}}=P_{[\tilde{I}]}M_{z_{i}}^{n-1}P_{[\tilde{I}]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[\tilde{I}]^{\perp}}+P_{[\tilde{I}]}M_{z_{i}}^{n-1}P_{[\tilde{I}]^{\perp}}M_{z_{i}}P_{[\tilde{I}]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}

for
n=2,3,\ldots
. In particular
P_{[\tilde{I}]}M_{z_{i}}^{K_{i}}P_{[\tilde{I}]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}
, which gives
P_{\Psi_{\mathbf{K}}[I]}M_{z_{i}}^{K_{i}}P_{\Psi_{\mathbf{K}}[I]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}
i.e.
P_{[I]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[I]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)}.

From the following facts

\mathrm{Id}_{[I]^{\perp}}-S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}^{*}S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}=P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{[I]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[I]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}

we see that
S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially isometry, and then the essential normality of
S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}

[
44
, Theorem 3.1]
ensures its Fredholmness. Let
S_{z_{i}}=W|S_{z_{i}}|
be the polar decomposition of
S_{z_{i}}
, where
W
is the partial isometry mapping
(\ker S_{z_{i}})^{\perp}
onto
\mathrm{ran~}S_{z_{i}}
. Then the fact

\mathrm{Id}_{[I]^{\perp}}-S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}^{*}=W(\mathrm{Id}_{[I]^{\perp}}-S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}^{*}S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}})W^{*}+P_{\ker S_{z_{i}}^{*}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}

implies that
S_{z_{i},[I]^{\perp}}
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially co-isometry, completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

The following result gives the essential normality of a large class of inhomogeneous quotient Hardy modules.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
be a Clark-type ideal, then the quotient module
[I]^{\perp}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially normal. Moreover,
S_{z_{i}}
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially unitary for
i=1,\ldots,d
.

Proof.

Let
J,B_{1},\ldots,B_{d}
be as in Definition
2.1
. Since each
B_{i}
is a nonconstant finite Blaschke product, the multiplication operator
M_{B_{i}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is an isometry with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ker M_{B_{i}}^{*}<\infty
. Write
H_{0}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}\ker M_{B_{i}}^{*}
which is finite dimensional, then by the Wold decomposition for isometries we have

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}M_{B^{\alpha}}H_{0}.

For
p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
and
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
we write
A_{p}f=p(B)f
, then
A
defines a
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
-module structure on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
. Define

\displaystyle U:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})\otimes H_{0}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}z^{\alpha}\otimes f_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}B^{\alpha}f_{\alpha},

then
U
is unitary and
U(M_{p}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{H_{0}})=A_{p}U
. For
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})\otimes H_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle Uf\in[I]^{\perp}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle M_{p}^{*}Uf=0,~\forall p\in I

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle M_{q(B)}^{*}Uf=0,~\forall q\in J

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle A_{q}^{*}Uf=0,~\forall q\in J

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle U(M_{q}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{H_{0}})^{*}f=0,~\forall q\in J

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(M_{q}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{H_{0}})^{*}f=0,~\forall q\in J

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle f\in[J]^{\perp}\otimes H_{0},

and therefore
[I]^{\perp}=U([J]^{\perp}\otimes H_{0})
. In
[
42
,
44
]
it is proven that
[J]^{\perp}
is essentially normal, and Proposition
2.3
gives

P_{[J]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[J]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)},

and

P_{[J]^{\perp}}-P_{[J]^{\perp}}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{[J]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}.

Write
B_{i}(z_{i})=\varphi_{a}(z_{i})\tilde{B}_{i}(z_{i})
where
\varphi_{a}
is a Möbius transform and
\tilde{B}_{i}
is a Blaschke product. It follows that

P_{[I]}M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}}=U\big((P_{[J]}M_{z_{i}}P_{[J]^{\perp}})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{H_{0}}\big)U^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{(2,\infty)},

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}-S_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}S_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}-P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}P_{\ker M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}P_{\ker M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}^{*}}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}-P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U\big((P_{[J]^{\perp}}-P_{[J]^{\perp}}M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{i}}^{*}P_{[J]^{\perp}})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{H_{0}}\big)U^{*}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}.

Similarly, from (
2.2
) and

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]}M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}-P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{B_{i}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}-P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}}+P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}(P_{[I]^{\perp}}-M_{\tilde{B}_{1}(z)}^{*}P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\tilde{B}_{1}(z)})M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}-P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}},

we obtain

\mathrm{Id}_{[I]^{\perp}}-S_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}S_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}=P_{[I]^{\perp}}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}^{*}P_{[I]}M_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}P_{[I]^{\perp}}\mid_{[I]^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}.

(2.4)

Combining (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we conclude that
S_{\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially unitary. Then it follows from Lemma
2.2
that
S_{z_{i}}=S_{\varphi_{a}\circ\varphi_{a}(z_{i})}
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially unitary.
∎

In the proof of Theorem
2.4
, if one of the inner functions
B_{1},\ldots,B_{d}
, for instance
B_{1}
, is not a finite Blaschke product, then

Let

Using techniques similar to that in

Let

Proof.

The case

2.4
, so in the sequel we assume

. According to
[
25
, Proposition 2.5]
, it suffices to prove that both
S_{z,[I]^{\perp}}
and
S_{w,[I]^{\perp}}
are
(1,\infty)
-essential co-isometries.

By Definition
2.1
, there is a distinguished quasi-homogeneous ideal
J\in\mathbb{C}[z,w]

is generated by the numerators of functions in

2.4

The following theorem omits the requirement to the algebraic structure of Clark-type ideal in Corollary
2.6
, and relates the essential normality of quotient modules to the varieties.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
I\subset\mathbb{C}[z,w]
is a distinguished ideal with Clark-type variety, then for every integer
\alpha\geq-1
, the quotient weighted Bergman module
[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially normal. Moreover,
S_{z,[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}
and
S_{w,[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}
are
(1,\infty)
-essentially unitary.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that any algebraic branches of
Z(I)
intersects with
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.
According to
[
25
, Proposition 2.5]
, it suffices to prove that both
S_{z,[q]^{\perp}}
and
S_{w,[q]^{\perp}}
are
(1,\infty)
-essentially co-isometry.

By Definition
2.1
, there exists a radical distinguished quasi-homogeneous ideal
J=p\mathbb{C}[z,w]
and finite Blaschke products
B_{1},B_{2}
such that

Z(I)\cap\mathbb{D}^{2}=Z(J_{B})\cap\mathbb{D}^{2}=Z(p_{B})\cap\mathbb{D}^{2}.

Write
p_{B}=q/r
where
q
and
r
are coprime polynomials, then
q
is distinguished.

The Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz ensures the existence of an integer
N
such that
q^{N}\in I
. Clearly

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathbb{C}[z,w]:f=gp_{B}^{N},~g\in\mathbb{C}[z,w]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathbb{C}[z,w]:f=gq^{N}/r^{N},~g\in\mathbb{C}[z,w]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q^{N}),

Consequently
(q^{N})
a Clark-type ideal. Corollary
2.6
shows that
S_{z,[q^{N}]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}
is
(1,\infty)
-essentially unitary, and therefore

\mathrm{Id}_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}-S_{z,[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}S_{z,[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}^{*}=P_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{[q^{N}]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}-S_{z,[q^{N}]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}S_{z,[q^{N}]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}^{*}\right)\mid_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)},

By
[

, if

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{C}[z,w]
be an ideal which is the intersection of ideals

with Clark-type varieties, such that

Remark 2.9
.

For a distinguished ideal
I
of
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
, since each component of
Z(I)
intersects with
\mathbb{D}^{d}
, in each irredundant primary decomposition

, either
Z(I_{k})
is a single point in
\mathbb{D}^{d}
or
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z(I_{k})\geq 1
. In the former case

is finite codimensional in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
, and in the latter case

is a distinguished ideal. By the theory of primary decomposition, the primary components

with

are uniquely determined by

is not impacted by the primary components with finite codimension, for convenience we only refer to their intersection, and write the primary decomposition as

.

3
K-homology for Clark-type quotient modules

In this section, we consider the
K
-homology defined by Clark-type quotient modules. By the BDF-theory
[
4
]
, when a quotient module
[I]^{\perp}
is essentially normal, we have the following short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to{\mathcal{K}}\to C^{*}([I]^{\perp})+{\mathcal{K}}\to C(\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\cdots,S_{z_{d}}))\to 0,

(3.1)

where
C^{*}([I]^{\perp})
is the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
\{\mathrm{Id}_{[I]^{\perp}},S_{z_{1}},\cdots,S_{z_{d}}\}
, and
\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\cdots,S_{z_{d}})
is the Taylor joint essential spectrum
[
8
,
37
]
of the tuple
(S_{z_{1}},\cdots,S_{z_{d}})
. We will show that the short exact sequence (
3.1
) is not splitting, i.e.
C^{*}([I]^{\perp})+{\mathcal{K}}
is not the direct product of
\mathcal{K}
and
C(\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\cdots,S_{z_{d}}))
, then (
3.1
) gives a nontrivial element
e_{I}
in the K-homology group
K\big(\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\cdots,S_{z_{d}})\big)
. To this aim, we first show that

\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\cdots,S_{z_{d}})=Z(I)\cap\partial\mathbb{D}^{d}

for Clark-type quotient modules
[I]^{\perp}
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and a subset
S
of
\{1,\ldots,d\}
, we write
z_{S}=\prod_{i\in S}z_{i}
. For a Hilbert
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
-module
\mathcal{H}
, we set

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{S\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}}(-1)^{\mathrm{card~}S}M_{z_{S}}M_{z_{S}}^{*},

(3.2)

then for a submodule
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
,
C_{\mathcal{M}}
is just the core operator defined by Guo and Yang
[
26
]
. It is routine to verify that
C_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})}=1\otimes 1
is of rank
1
. For the weighted Bergman module
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
where
\alpha\in(-1,\infty)
, an orthonormal basis is

\left\{\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\Gamma(\beta_{i}+\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(\beta_{i}+1)\Gamma(\alpha+2)}}z^{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\right\}.

Direct computations verify that

C_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})}z^{\beta}=\frac{(\alpha+1)^{d}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\beta_{i}+\alpha+1)}z^{\beta},~\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d},

which ensures the compactness of
C_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
be an ideal such that

\partial\big(Z(I)\cap\mathbb{D}^{d}\big)=Z(I)\cap\partial\mathbb{D}^{d},

(3.3)

and
\alpha\geq-1
be a real number, such that the quotient module
[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
is essentially normal, then the Taylor essential spectrum
[
37
,
38
]

\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\ldots,S_{z_{d}})=Z(I)\cap\partial\mathbb{D}^{d}.

Proof.

Since
p(S_{z_{1}},\ldots,S_{z_{d}})=0
for all polynomials
p\in I
, the Spectral Mapping Theorem
[
38
]
gives

\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\ldots,S_{z_{d}})\subset\sigma(S_{z_{1}},\ldots,S_{z_{d}})\subset Z(I).

From

C_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}=P_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}C_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{d})}\mid_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}

(3.4)

we obtain the compactness of
C_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}
. Then since
C^{*}([I]_{\alpha}^{\perp})
is essentially commutative, it follows from the definition of
C_{[I]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}
that
\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\ldots,S_{z_{d}})\subset\partial\mathbb{D}^{d}
. Therefore we have proved

\sigma_{e}(S_{z_{1}},\ldots,S_{z_{d}})\subset Z(I)\cap\partial\mathbb{D}^{d}.

For the inverse inclusion, let
\lambda\in Z(I)\cap\partial\mathbb{D}^{d}
. By
[
8
, Corollary 3.9]
, it is sufficient to show that
T=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}(\lambda_{i}-S_{z_{i}})(\lambda_{i}-S_{z_{i}})^{*}
is not Fredholm. If
T
is Fredholm, then by the positivity of
T
, we can find an invertible positive operator
B
and a compact
K
such that
T=B+K
. Take a sequence
\{\mu^{(n)}\}
in

that converges to
\lambda
. Let

be the normalized reproducing kernel, then

converges to

weakly. Hence there is a positive number

such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle Tk_{\mu^{(n)}},k_{\mu^{(n)}}\rangle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle(B+K)k_{\mu^{(n)}},k_{\mu^{(n)}}\rangle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle Bk_{\mu^{(n)}},k_{\mu^{(n)}}\rangle\geq c.

However, since

, it holds that

, and consequently

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\langle Tk_{\mu^{(n)}},k_{\mu^{(n)}}\rangle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}|\lambda-\mu^{(n)}|^{2}=0,

(3.5)

contradicting to the previous inequality. Therefore

is not Fredholm and

For a quasi-homogeneous distinguished ideal

[

proves the essential normality of

For the Clark-type variety

To prove the non-triviality of the

Lemma 3.5

-quasi-homogeneous ideal, then

Proof.

Proposition

is a finite subset of

. Moreover, for every

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ker S_{z_{1},[J]^{\perp}}^{\,m}

\displaystyle\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{d}]/J_{0}:=s.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

In this section, we establish the connection between Hilbert-Schmidt submodules and essentially normal quotient modules in
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{2})

Let
p\in\mathbb{C}[z,w]
be a polynomial such that

Z(p)\cap Z(z)\cap\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}=\emptyset,

does not vanish in the unit circle, and
\alpha\geq-1
be an integer. Suppose that

\langle S_{q,[p]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}f,g\rangle=\langle P_{[p]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}M_{q}f,g\rangle=\langle qf,g\rangle=0.

For

\displaystyle T_{q,Q}=S_{q,[p]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}\mid_{Q}=P_{Q}M_{q}\mid_{Q}.

(4.1)

The following lemma is inspired by
[

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the above assumptions, the subspace
\ker T_{z,Q}^{*}
is of finite dimension.

Proof.

Please note that

\displaystyle\ker T_{z,Q}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q\ominus\overline{S_{z,[p]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in Q:\langle f,P_{[p]_{\alpha}^{\perp}}(zg)\rangle=0,~\forall g\in Q\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in Q:\langle f,zg\rangle=0,~\forall g\in Q\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in Q:\langle f,zg\rangle=0,~\forall g\in\mathcal{M}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in Q:\langle f,h\rangle=0,~\forall h\in z\mathcal{M}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\cap[p]_{\alpha}^{\perp}\cap\left(z\mathcal{M}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\ominus[z\mathcal{M}+p\mathcal{M}]_{\alpha}.

Next, we prove that
\mathcal{M}\ominus[z\mathcal{M}+p\mathcal{M}]_{\alpha}
is of finite dimension. By the assumption

Z(p)\cap Z(z)\cap\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}=\emptyset,

we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[z,w]/(z,p)<\infty,

where
(z,p)
is the ideal generated by
z
and
p
. Select a basis
\{e_{i}+(z,p):1\leq i\leq k\}
of
\mathbb{C}[z,w]/(z,p)

\mathcal{M}\ominus[z\mathcal{M}+p\mathcal{M}]_{\alpha}=\mathrm{span}\left\{P(e_{i}f_{j}):i=1,\cdots,k,~j=1,\cdots,n+1\right\},

where for convenience, we denote
p
by

and

is the projection onto
\mathcal{M}\ominus[z\mathcal{M}+p\mathcal{M}]_{\alpha}
. To prove this, suppose

satisfies

Now, for any

and

Furthermore,

are in the closure of

.
Therefore

\displaystyle\langle g,hf_{j}\rangle=\langle g,h_{1}f_{j}\rangle+\langle g,h_{2}f_{j}\rangle=\langle g,P(h_{1}f_{j})\rangle+\langle g,h_{2}f_{j}\rangle=0.

It follows from the fact

and let

\mathbb{U}_{r}=I\otimes U_{r}.

Set
R_{z}=M_{z}|_{\mathcal{M}_{N}},\,R_{w}=M_{w}|_{\mathcal{M}_{N}}
temporarily. Denote by

V_{r,w}=\varphi_{r}(R_{w})=(rI-R_{w})(I-rR_{w})^{-1}.

It can be seen that
V_{r,w}
is an isometry.
By direct calculations,

\mathbb{U}_{r}^{*}\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\varphi_{r}}}\mathbb{U}_{r}=I-R_{z}R_{z}^{*}-V_{r,w}V_{r,w}^{*}+R_{z}V_{r,w}R_{z}^{*}V_{r,w}^{*}.

Write
P=I-R_{z}R_{z}^{*}
, which is the orthogonal projection onto

\displaystyle W_{N}=\mathcal{M}_{N}\ominus z\mathcal{M}_{N}

(7.2)

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}_{r}^{*}\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\varphi_{r}}}\mathbb{U}_{r}=P-V_{r,w}PV_{r,w}^{*}.

(7.3)

Furthermore,
V_{r,w}PV_{r,w}^{*}
is the orthogonal projection onto
V_{r,w}W_{N}
. Now, set

T_{N}=PR_{w}|_{W_{N}}.

Obviously,
R_{w}^{*}W_{N}\subset W_{N}
. For
n>0
,
T_{N}^{n}=PR_{w}^{n}|_{W_{N}}
. Moreover,

PV_{r,w}|_{W_{N}}=P\varphi_{r}(R_{w})|_{W_{N}}=\varphi_{r}(T_{N}).

For convenience, let
\mathbb{N}_{0}
be the nonnegative integers. The following weighted-shift model is contained in
[
30
]
and we include the argument for completeness.

Proposition 7.4
.

There is an orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of
W_{N}
such that
T_{N}
can be represented as the following weighted shift:

T_{N}e_{k}=\alpha_{N,k}e_{k+1},

where the weight

\alpha_{N,k}=\frac{\sqrt{(k+1)(k+2N+1)}}{k+N+1}.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, consider the
N+k
-order homogeneous components of
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and
z{\mathcal{M}_{N}}
separately

\mathcal{M}_{N,N+k}=\big\{(z-w)^{N}z^{\alpha_{1}}w^{\alpha_{2}};\,(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ and }\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=k\big\},

and

\left(z\mathcal{M}_{N}\right)_{N+k}=z\mathcal{M}_{N,N+k-1}=\big\{z(z-w)^{N}z^{\alpha_{1}}w^{\alpha_{2}};\,(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ and }\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=k-1\big\}.

It follows that
\left(z\mathcal{M}_{N}\right)_{N+k}
is a 1-codimensional subspace of
\mathcal{M}_{N,N+k}
, i.e.

W_{N,N+k}:=\mathcal{M}_{N,N+k}\ominus\left(z\mathcal{M}_{N}\right)_{N+k}=\mathbb{C}\xi_{N,k},

and hence
T_{N}e_{k}=\alpha_{N,k}e_{k+1}
with the weights
\{\alpha_{N,k}\}
.

Next, we will use the standard argument from the theory of orthogonal polynomial on the unit circle
[
34
]
to determine the weight
\{\alpha_{N,k}\}
. Now, let

\mathcal{P}_{k}=\big\{f\in\mathbb{C}[\zeta];\deg(f)\leq k\big\},

where
\mathbb{C}[\zeta]
is the polynomial ring on
\zeta
. To identify its canonical generator, for
(z,w)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
write
\zeta=z/w
. Then the map

\Psi:w^{N+k}(\zeta-1)^{N}q(\zeta)\mapsto q(\zeta)

identifies
\mathcal{M}_{N,N+k}
with
\mathcal{P}_{k}
, where circular-Jacobi inner product on
\mathcal{P}_{k}
is given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{N}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\zeta)\overline{g(\zeta)}|1-\zeta|^{2N}\,dm(\zeta).

Under this identification,
(z\mathcal{M}_{N})_{N+k}
corresponds to

\zeta\mathcal{P}_{k-1}
. Let
\Phi_{N,k}
be the monic orthogonal polynomial of degree
k
for the weight
|1-\zeta|^{2N}
, and its reversed polynomial is

q_{N,k}(\zeta)=\Phi_{N,k}^{*}(\zeta)=\zeta^{k}\overline{\Phi_{N,k}(\zeta)},\,|\zeta|=1.

By using standard OPUC theory, we have

q_{N,k}\perp\zeta^{j},\,j=1,\cdots,k.

Therefore

\mathcal{M}_{N,N+k}=\text{span}\left\{\xi_{N,k}(z,w)=(z-w)^{N}w^{k}q_{N,k}(z/w);\,q_{N,k}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}\right\}.

By
[
34
, Chapter 1]
, for the circular-Jacobi weight
|1-\zeta|^{2N}
, the Verblunsky coefficients are

\alpha_{j}=-\frac{N}{N+j+1},\,j\geq 0.

By the direct computation,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|1-\zeta|^{2N}\,dm(\zeta)=\binom{2N}{N}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|q_{N,k}\|_{N}^{2}=\binom{2N}{N}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{N^{2}}{(N+j+1)^{2}}\right)=\frac{(2N+k)!\,k!}{(N+k)!^{2}}=h_{N,k}.

Since the constant terms of
q_{N,k}
and
q_{N,k+1}
are both
1
, so

q_{N,k+1}-q_{N,k}\in\zeta\mathcal{P}_{k}
. It follows from
q_{N,k+1}\perp\zeta\mathcal{P}_{k}
that

\langle q_{N,k+1},q_{N,k+1}-q_{N,k}\rangle=0,

and hence

\langle q_{N,k},q_{N,k+1}\rangle_{N}=\langle q_{N,k}-q_{N,k+1},q_{N,k+1}\rangle+\langle q_{N,k+1},q_{N,k+1}\rangle=\|q_{N,k+1}\|_{N}^{2}=h_{N,k+1}.

Set

e_{k}=\xi_{N,k}/\sqrt{h_{N,k}}
, and we have

\langle T_{N}e_{k},e_{k+1}\rangle=\frac{h_{N,k+1}}{\sqrt{h_{N,k}h_{N,k+1}}}=\sqrt{\frac{h_{N,k+1}}{h_{N,k}}}.

It follows that

\alpha_{N,k}=\sqrt{h_{N,k+1}/h_{N,k}}=\frac{\sqrt{(k+1)(k+2N+1)}}{k+N+1}.

∎

The following lemma is a direct specialization of Halmos two-projections theorem
[
27
]
, which is also a special case of the general formula of Omladič
[
32
]
. We list the proof here for reader’s convenience.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
P_{W}
be the orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace
\mathcal{W}
of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
V
be an isometry. Let

Q=VPV^{*},\,A=P_{\mathcal{W}}V|_{\mathcal{W}},\,D=I_{\mathcal{W}}-AA^{*}.

Assume that
A
is injective,
\dim\ker A^{*}=1
, and
D
is compact. Assume that

\sigma_{p}(D)\setminus\{0\}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\{1,\lambda_{k},k=1,\cdots\}}.

Rearranged the nonzero eigenvalue of
D
, counting multiplicities, as

\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots<1.

Then

\displaystyle\sigma(P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q)\setminus\{0\}=\{1\}\cup\{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}:j\geq 1\}.

(7.4)

Consequently, for every
0<p<\infty
,

\|P_{{\mathcal{W}}}-Q\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}=2\operatorname{Tr}(D^{p/2})-1,

where sides may be
+\infty
,

Proof.

According to the orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
, we have a block matrix

V|_{\mathcal{W}}=\binom{A}{B}.

Because
V|_{\mathcal{W}}
is an isometry, i.e.
V^{*}_{\mathcal{W}}V_{\mathcal{W}}=I
, we have

A^{*}A+B^{*}B=I_{\mathcal{W}},\text{ and }Q=VP_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}AA^{*}&AB^{*}\\
BA^{*}&BB^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

Since both
P_{\mathcal{W}}
and
Q
are orthogonal projections,

(P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q)^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}I-AA^{*}&0\\
0&BB^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}D&0\\
0&BB^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

Please noting that
B^{*}B=I-A^{*}A
,

\sigma(BB^{*})\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(B^{*}B)\setminus\{0\},

counting multiplicities. Similarly,

\sigma(I-AA^{*})\cap(0,1)=\sigma(I-A^{*}A)\cap(0,1),

counting multiplicities. Since
A
is injective and
\dim\ker A^{*}=1
, the eigenvalue
1
occurs once in
\sigma(D)
and
1\notin\sigma_{p}(B^{*}B)
. Thus
1\in\sigma_{p}\big((P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q)^{2}\big)
has multiplicity 1 and
\lambda_{j}\in\sigma_{p}\big((P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q)^{2}\big)\cap(0,1)
has multiplicities 2.
It is easy to verify

(P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q)(P_{\mathcal{W}}+Q-I)=-(P_{\mathcal{W}}+Q-I)(P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q),\text{ and }(P_{\mathcal{W}}+Q-I)^{2}=I-(P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q)^{2}.

Let
E_{j}
be the eigenspace of
P-Q
associated to the eigenvaluse
\lambda_{j}\in(0,1)
, the operator
(P+Q-I)|_{E_{j}}
is invertible and commutes with the projections onto the eignspaces of
P-Q
associated to the eigenvalues
+\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}
and
-\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}
. Hence these eigenvalues have equal multiplicities. If
x\in\ker A^{*}
, then
x\in\mathcal{W}
,
Qx=0
, and
(P-Q)x=x
. Hence
1
is the eigenvalue of
P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q
, with the multiplicity 1. The spectral formula (
7.4
) follows, and therefore

\|P_{\mathcal{W}}-Q\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}=1+2\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{p/2}=2\left(1+\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{p/2}\right)-1=2\operatorname{Tr}(D^{p/2})-1.

∎

Since
\|T_{N}\|=1
and
0\leq r<1
, the operator
I-rT_{N}
is invertible. For simplicity, write

A_{N,r}=\varphi_{r}(T_{N})=(rI-T_{N})(I-rT_{N})^{-1},\text{ and }D_{N,r}=I-A_{N,r}A_{N,r}^{*}.

Moreover, since
T_{N}
has no eigenvalues, we have
rI-T_{N}
is injective and so is
A_{N,r}
.

Next, we claim that
\dim\ker A_{N,r}^{*}=1
. In fact,

\ker A_{N,r}^{*}=\ker(rI-T_{N}^{*}).

Writing
x=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}e_{n}
, the equation
T_{N}^{*}x=rx
is equivalent to

\alpha_{N,n}x_{n+1}=rx_{n},\,n\geq 0.

After some direct calculations,
T_{N}^{*}K_{r}=rKr
, where

K_{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{\alpha_{N,0}\alpha_{N,1}\cdots\alpha_{N,n-1}}e_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}\sqrt{\frac{h_{N,0}}{h_{N,n}}}\,e_{n}.

By Stirling’s formula,
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}h_{N,n}=\frac{(2N+n)!\,n!}{(N+n)!^{2}}}\to 1
, and hence
K_{r}\in\ell^{2}
for
r<1
. Therefore

\dim\ker A_{N,r}^{*}=1.

To continue, we need the following easy lemma.

Lemma 7.6
.

For
0\leq r<1
,

\displaystyle D_{N,r}=(1-r^{2})(I-rT_{N})^{-1}D_{N,0}(I-rT_{N}^{*})^{-1}.

(7.5)

Consequently, for every
q>0
,

D_{N,r}\in\mathcal{S}_{q}\Leftrightarrow D_{N,0}\in\mathcal{S}_{q}\Leftrightarrow q>\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

It suffices to show (
7.5
).
Since
T_{N}
commutes with
(I-rT_{N})^{-1}
,

A_{N,r}=(I-rT_{N})^{-1}(rI-T_{N}),\,A_{N,r}^{*}=(rI-T_{N}^{*})((I-rT_{N})^{-1})^{*}.

Therefore

\displaystyle I-A_{N,r}A_{N,r}^{*}

\displaystyle=(I-rT_{N})^{-1}\big[\big(I-rT_{N})(I-rT_{N}^{*})-(rI-T_{N})(rI-T_{N}^{*})]((I-rT_{N})^{-1})^{*}

\displaystyle=(1-r^{2})(I-rT_{N})^{-1}(I-T_{N}T_{N}^{*})((I-rT_{N})^{-1})^{*}

\displaystyle=(1-r^{2})(I-rT_{N})^{-1}D_{N,0}(I-rT_{N}^{*})^{-1}.

Recall that

A_{N,0}=-T_{N}\text{ and }D_{N,0}=I-T_{N}T_{N}^{*}.

By Proposition
7.4
,
D_{N,0}e_{0}=e_{0}
and for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

D_{N,0}e_{k}=\frac{N^{2}}{(N+k)^{2}}e_{k}.

It follows that
D_{N,0}\in\mathcal{S}_{q}
if and only if
q>{1\over 2}
.
∎

Recall by (
7.3
),

\mathbb{U}_{r}^{*}\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\varphi_{r}}}\mathbb{U}_{r}=P-V_{r,w}PV_{r,w}^{*},

where
P
is the projection onto
W_{N}
and
A_{N,r}=PV_{r,w}|_{W_{N}}
. Since
A_{N,r}
is injective and
\dim\ker A_{N,r}^{*}=1
, by Lemma
7.6
,
D_{N,r}
is positive and compact. Applying Lemma
7.5
, we have

\|C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}=2\operatorname{Tr}(D_{N,r}^{p/2})-1.

Moreover,

C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}\Leftrightarrow D_{N,r}\in\mathcal{S}_{p/2}\Leftrightarrow D_{N,0}\in\mathcal{S}_{p/2}\Leftrightarrow p>1.

We summarize this argument as the following result.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\theta,\psi
be nonconstant inner functions, let
N\geq 1
, and set

r=\rho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\theta(0),\psi(0)\big).

Let
T_{N}
,
A_{N,r}
, and
D_{N,r}
be defined as above. Then

1)

the nonzero spectrum and nonzero singular values of
C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}
depend only on
N
and
r
;

2)

for every
0<p<\infty
,

C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad p>1;

moreover, for
p>1
,

\|C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}=2\operatorname{Tr}(D_{N,r}^{p/2})-1.

Corollary 7.8
.

Let
\theta
and
\psi
be two nonconstant inner functions,
\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}=[(\theta-\psi)^{N}]
, then for
p>1
we have the following estimation

{1-r\over 1+r}\big(1+2N^{p}\zeta(p,N+1)\big)^{1\over p}\leq\|C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\leq{1+r\over 1-r}\big(1+2N^{p}\zeta(p,N+1)\big)^{1\over p},

where
r=\rho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\theta(0),\psi(0)\big)
.

Proof.

Combining with Proposition
7.3
and
Lemma
7.1
,
the corollary comes from the classical norm formula; see e.g.
[
31
]
,

\|\mathcal{C}_{\varphi_{r}}\|=\|\mathcal{C}_{\varphi_{r}}^{-1}\|=\sqrt{1+r\over 1-r}.

∎

Finally, we will give the explicit formula of
\|C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}
. For convenience, setting

\varepsilon_{N,0}=1,\text{ and }\varepsilon_{N,n}=\frac{N}{N+n}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

one has that
D_{N,0}e_{n}=\varepsilon_{N,n}^{2}e_{n}
. By the standard argument of Neumann series,

(I-rT_{N})^{-1}e_{\ell}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}r^{m}\sqrt{\frac{h_{N,\ell+m}}{h_{N,\ell}}}\,e_{\ell+m}.

Substituting this into Lemma
7.6
, we have, for
i,j\geq 0
,

\langle D_{N,r}e_{j},e_{i}\rangle=(1-r^{2})\sqrt{h_{N,i}h_{N,j}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{i,j\}}\frac{\varepsilon_{N,\ell}^{2}r^{i+j-2\ell}}{h_{N,\ell}}.

Since
D_{N,r}\geq 0
, by the definition

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}D_{N,r}=(1-r^{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{N,n}^{2}\left\|(I-rT_{N})^{-1}e_{n}\right\|^{2}=(1-r^{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{N,n}^{2}r^{2m}\frac{h_{N,n+m}}{h_{N,n}}.

Therefore

\|C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{2}=2(1-r^{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{N,n}^{2}r^{2m}\frac{h_{N,n+m}}{h_{N,n}}-1.

At last, since

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}r^{2m}\frac{h_{N,n+m}}{h_{N,n}}={}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1,n+1,n+2N+1\\
n+N+1,n+N+1\end{matrix};r^{2}\right),

where
{}_{3}F_{2}
is the generalized hypergeometric function. This yields the explicit hypergeometric representation of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm:

\|C_{\mathcal{M}_{N;\theta,\psi}}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{2}=2(1-r^{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{N,n}^{2}{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}1,n+1,n+2N+1\\
n+N+1,n+N+1\end{matrix};r^{2}\right)-1.

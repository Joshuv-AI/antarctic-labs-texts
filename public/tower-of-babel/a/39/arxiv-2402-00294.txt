Explicit arithmetic Eisenstein cocycles: the toric case
Peter Xu

Abstract
. We use combinatorially-defined complexes parameterizing linear-algebraic data to construct explicit cocycles for GL n \text{GL}_{n} valued in the motivic cohomology of open subsets of 𝔾 m n \mathbb{G}_{m}^{n} over a DVR, by using these complexes to parameterize special classes in motivic complexes. The key step in our construction is to exhibit the needed relations as explicit cubical boundaries in the Bloch cycle complex. The resulting cocycles, valued in products of a kind of “cyclotomic theta function” whose specializations yield cyclotomic units, directly generalize the work of Sharifi and Venkatesh for n = 2 n=2 [ 18 ] , and refine the more complex-analytic construction of Lim and Park [ 13 ] , both by working algebro-geometrically and quotienting out by fewer relations in the target. We also compute the Hecke action on the resulting cocycle.

1.2.
Future work

The toric cocycle of the present article is essentially the “degeneration to the cusp” of the an analogous elliptic cocycle, built from theta functions and Siegel units rather than copies of
1-z
and cyclotomic units. The recent article
[

A version of the construction in the present article will be used in forthcoming joint work with Marti Roset Julia, relating it to a generalization of the

[

There are many other directions to explore in this general setting: for example, many natural generalizations or arithmetic applications of the elliptic version of the construction suggest themselves; we omit their discussion since they will be discussed in that article. To mention a direction tied more to the toric setting, the relation between the present article’s motivic cocycle and topologically-defined Eisenstein cocycles is somewhat subtle and worth examining, reflecting in some sense the functional equation of a Kronecker-Eisenstein series as well as a duality between cotangent and Bernoulli-type sums in the sense of

2.2.
Simplicial complexes from linear algebra

With the motivic background out of the way, we turn now to the other side of the picture, constructing combinatorial “symbol complexes” we will use to parameterize motivic data. These complexes, indexed by a dimension
n
corresponding to the power
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
, will have simple homology and carry natural
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
-actions.

We shall construct a
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
-chain complex
\widetilde{\text{Chains}}(n)
, and show that it computes the reduced homology of the sphere
S^{n-1}
and is thus almost exact. In a later section,
\widetilde{\text{Chains}}(n)
will be endowed with a realization map to the Gersten complex.

The construction of this section directly generalizes
[
18
, §5]
, who consider the case
n=2
, though we use slightly different conventions for the group action: we are taking
left
actions on spaces with a resulting pushforward left action on cohomology groups, while they consider right actions on spaces.

To construct
\widetilde{\text{Chains}}(n)
, consider the poset
\mathcal{P}
of finite geodesic triangulations
T
of
S^{n-1}
with vertices contained in

S^{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})\subset S^{n-1},

ordered by
refinement
: that is to say, we say
T_{1}\geq T
if every simplex of
T
is a union of simplices of
T_{1}
.

Here, by
geodesic triangulation
, we mean a triangulation where every face of every simplex is a regions of a
k
-dimensional great circle; thus, every
k
-simplex is the intersection of a
S^{n-1}
with dimension-
(k+1)
simplicial polyhedral cones in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
emanating from the origin (given by connecting all points of the simplex with the origin). The nomenclature is explained by the fact that these simplices are geodesic simplices taken with respect to the Euclidean metric under the natural inclusion

S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Notice that in particular, the great circle containing any given simplex can be recovered as the intersection of
S^{n-1}
with the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
spanned by the rays emanating to the vertices of the simplex. As such, each of these corresponding subspaces are themselves rationally defined, since the vertices are in
S^{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})
.

It is all well and good to define such objects, but we need to establish that enough of them exist for our purposes. This is accomplished by the following two propositions, which are both certainly well-known to the point of being folklore, but which we state for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2.9
.

Given any geodesic
k
-simplex
\Delta
, with
k\leq n-1
, with endpoints in
S^{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})
, there exists a triangulation
T\in\mathcal{P}
such that
\Delta
is one of the triangles in

.

Proof.

For each face of
\Delta
, take the corresponding great circle on
S^{n-1}
; these divide the sphere into polyhedral regions. In each region besides
\Delta
itself, choose a rational point, Pick a rational point in interior of each polyhedral cell and subdivide it into simplices by joining the point to each
(n-2)
-face of the cell with a
(n-1)
-simplex; we obtain a triangulation with all vertices in
S^{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})
such that
\Delta
is one of the triangles.
∎

.

Given any two triangulations

Superimposing the triangulations
T_{1}

(n-2)

(n-1)

S^{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})

\Delta\in\Delta(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})
and a rational point

(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})

(2.8)

[\Delta(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}[\Delta(m_{1},\ldots,\hat{m_{i}},m,m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{k})].

Proof.

From the discussion preceding the proposition, it remains only to prove that the stellar subdvision relations (
2.8
) generate all acyclic subdivision relations.

First, we say that a subdivision of a simplex
\Delta
is a
sequentially stellar subdivision
if it can be obtained by a sequence of stellar subdivisions of
\Delta
(and the resulting subsimplices at each sequential step). The relation corresponding to a sequentially stellar subdivision of an acyclic simplex is certainly generated by those of the implicated stellar subdivisions by repeated substitution.

We observe, therefore, that it suffices to show that any acyclic subdivision

\Delta=\Delta_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\Delta_{t}

can be refined to a sequentially stellar subdivision of
\Delta
, which is also a sequentially stellar subdivision of each
\Delta_{i}
when restricted to it.

The modern way of phrasing this problem in combinatorial topology is that a subdivision of a simplex gives a piecewise linear (PL) homeomorphism between the simplex and its subdivision, and any two PL-homeomorphic simplicial complexes are related by a sequence of “stellar moves”. This result is classical within that field, and was first proven in
[
14

; for a more modern treatment, see

12
]
.

We also write

Proof.

We show how to construct such an

2.3.3.
The Steinberg quotient

We end this section by considering the following three questions:

(1)

How can we modify our construction of
\theta_{com}(n)^{E}
into a parabolic cocycle?

(2)

What is the precise relation between

and “Shintani cocycles,” as defined, e.g. in

?
6
6

6

We here mention the work of Lim and Park since it is in a motivic setting close to ours, but of course the notion of such cocycles is older, going back to the work of the namesake Shintani

(3)

Can we obtain a more canonical version of

not relying on the arbitrary choice of extension

All three questions are answered by the same modification of our construction, which simultaneously elides the need to lift over the Euler class. We briefly describe it now: let

be the classical

[
15
]
defined as follows: it is the graded-commutative algebra generated in degree
1
by symbols
[\ell]
for
\ell\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})
, with relations generated multiplicatively by the dependence relations

\partial([\ell_{1}]\wedge\ldots\wedge[\ell_{k}])=0

for any lines
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k}
spanning a space of rank strictly less than
k
. Here, the differential-graded structure is defined via
\partial[\ell]=1
and extended by the graded Leibniz rule. The relations generated as above are closed under the differential, and the resulting differential-graded algebra is exact. Then we have a
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
-equivariant map of complexes

\text{Chains}(n)_{i}\twoheadrightarrow\text{OS}(n)_{i}

sending

\Delta(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})\mapsto[\mathbb{Q}r_{1}]\wedge\ldots\wedge[\mathbb{Q}r_{k}].

This map (
2.17
) is well-defined, since the Orlik-Solomon complex obeys alternation in the vertices, and stellar subdivision relations simply become dependence relations in the image.

In top degree, the map (
2.17
) corresponds to the
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
-equivariant quotient of the top spherical chains

(2.18)

R_{St}:\text{Chains}(n)_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\text{St}(n):=\text{OS}(n)_{n}

where

is the

. From the definition of the Orlik-Solomon algebra, we see that it can be described as generated by symbols

7

7

We need to consider realizations to fully answer question (2), i.e. construct an equivariant map from

to a motivic group of interest. In the next section, in particular subsection

We conclude this section with the following description of the kernel of (

Lemma 2.21
.

The kernel of the map (
2.18
) is generated by “wedge” classes of the form

\Delta(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})-\Delta(-r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})

for independent tuples
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
.

Proof.

The top-dimensional spherical chains are generated by acyclic simplices together with stellar subdivision relations, while the Steinberg module is generated by independent tuples modulo dependence relations. If one imposes all the identification of spherical simplices

\Delta(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\sim\Delta(\pm r_{1},\ldots,\pm r_{n}),

for any combination of signs, then the relations resulting from the stellar subdivision relations are precisely the dependence relations. These identifications can be deduced, by (anti)symmetry, from the identifications

\Delta(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\sim\Delta(-r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})

since we can then change one sign at a time and bootstrap to the general case.
∎

3.

3.1.

We now turn to constructing the symbol map for the Gersten complex. This section will be devoted to proving the following theorem:

3.2.2.
Better integrality?

As we noted in the preliminary section
2.1
, the technical details of descent for motivic complexes force us to work over DVRs in order to prove the relations needed for our cocycles: over a Dedekind domain, for example, we cannot take compute the hypercohomology of the Bloch cycle complex on global sections. In this section, we have seen that our methods allow us to obtain classes on hyperplane complements in
\mathbb{G}_{m}
defined over this DVR base. However, the actual cocycles we get are built from functions on
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
defined even over

, raising the question of whether everything can in fact be done over this base. We end this section by devoting a few words to this question.

In general, the situation is this: if

is a scheme over

, then we have a residue long exact sequence

\ldots\to H^{n}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\to H^{n}(X_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{Z}(n))\to\bigoplus_{p}H^{n-1}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},\mathbb{Z}(n-1))\to\ldots

coming from a distinguished triangle of Bloch complexes, see e.g. the motivic background section of the author’s thesis
[
24
]
. For
n=2
, the ambiguity term does vanish since

, so in that case, covered in
[
18
]
, one can obtain integral cocycles. However, for

there is no reason to expect splitting or degeneration; hence, one cannot easily lift the cocycles
\Theta_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
and its cousins to be defined over
\mathbb{Z}
.

In fact, we do expect that one can show that the trace-fixed part of the ambiguity term

\left(\bigoplus_{p}H^{n-2}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},\mathbb{Z}(n-1))\right)^{(0)}

vanishes for a hyperplane complement

via an inductive argument; this is an idea of the first author of
[
18
]
. This would yield integral cocycles for larger

. However, we do not pursue this idea in the present article.

is the map given by translating by the vector of roots unity
\exp(2\pi ia)
.

Proof.

There are two things to check: that the distribution relation hold, and that the map is equivariant.

Since we have specified the distribution via open sets of the form

a+q\mathbb{Z}^{n}

for scalars
q\in\mathbb{Q}
, it suffices to verify the distribution relations of the form

\mu(a+\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\sum_{a^{\prime}}\mu(a^{\prime}+k\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\sum\sum_{a^{\prime}}\mu\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{k}+\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)

for any
\mu
in the image of the map and any integer
k
, where
a^{\prime}
runs over equivalence classes in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}/k\mathbb{Z}^{n}
having image
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Here, the second equality follows from scalar invariance.

If
\mu
is the image of
s\in K_{n}^{M}(k(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}))^{(0)}
, this relation follows from the equalities

t_{a}^{*}s=t_{a}^{*}[k]_{*}s=\sum_{a^{\prime}}t_{a^{\prime}/k}^{*}s

where the first equality is from trace invariance of
s
, and the second is an equality of correspondences.

To show
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
-equivariance, we see that for any
\gamma
in the group,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\gamma s

\displaystyle\mapsto(1(a+\mathbb{Z}^{n})\mapsto t_{a}^{*}(\gamma_{*}s))

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\left(1(a+\mathbb{Z}^{n})\mapsto\gamma_{*}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\gamma^{-1}a}t_{a^{\prime}}^{*}s\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle=\left((1(a+\mathbb{Z}^{n})\circ\gamma)\mapsto\gamma_{*}t_{a}^{*}s\right)

which is what we want.
∎

3.4.
Hecke action

Let
S
be a set of primes, and take
\Gamma\subset\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
an arithmetic group. We wish to compute the action of the Hecke algebra of
\Gamma
on
\Theta_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(n)
(and its variants) at primes away from
S
.

To define a Hecke action on group cohomology with coefficients in a
\Gamma
-module
M
, one needs an action of not just
\Gamma
, but all of

\Delta:=M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\cap\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}).

We already have been acting on Milnor
K
-theory and motivic cohomology with groups even larger than this, via pushforward. However, if we use the action via pushforwards we have been using until now to define the Hecke action, we do not obtain actions compatible with
[
18
]
and more classical formulations; to make the actions line up, we must take
pullback
actions.

Since have been acting by
\gamma_{*}
, which on
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
coincides with
(\gamma^{-1})^{*}
, to extend the action to a pullback-type action for
\gamma\in\Delta
we need to define

(3.19)

\gamma\cdot x=(|\det\gamma|\gamma^{-1})^{*}x

for any element
x
in any of the various cohomology groups associated to
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
or subvarieties thereof.

For any prime
p
, we now follow
[
16
]
in defining the Hecke operator
T_{p}^{(i)}
to be the double coset operator

\Gamma\text{diag}(p,\ldots,p,1,\ldots,1)\Gamma=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{j}\Gamma\alpha

with
i
copies of
p
. For a
\Delta
-module
M
, it acts on
[c]\in H^{d}(\Gamma,M)
via

(3.20)

(T_{p}^{(i)}c)(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}):=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1}c(\alpha_{i}).

That this is well-defined and independent of chosen representatives is shown in loc. cit. Further, associated to a short exact sequence of
\Delta
-modules

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0

we have the standard connecting homomorphisms

H^{d}(\Gamma,C)\to H^{d+1}(\Gamma,A),

whose description in Lemma
2.12
makes plain that they intertwine with the Hecke action (
3.20
).

Consider the embedding of exact complexes

\text{Chains}(n)\hookrightarrow\text{Chains}(n)\otimes\mathbb{Z}\{Z_{0}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}[p^{\infty}])\}

sending
x\mapsto x\otimes[1]
, where
[1]
is the class of the identity section, and
Z_{0}
denotes the relative zero-dimensional cycles over
R
. By the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, there is a realization map extending
f_{\bullet}
of the form

\tilde{f}_{\bullet}:\text{Chains}(n)\otimes\mathbb{Z}\{Z_{0}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}[p^{\infty}])\}\to\left[Z_{Ger(K(n))}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n})^{n-\bullet}\right]^{(0)}

which sends

(3.21)

[\Delta(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})]\otimes[x]\mapsto t_{x}\circ\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}&\ldots&m_{k}\end{pmatrix}_{*}\{1-z_{1},\ldots,1-z_{k}\}\in K_{k}^{M}(k(L))^{(0)}

where the action of
[x]\in\mathbb{Z}\{Z_{0}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}[p^{\infty}])
written on the left-hand side is the action of the correspondence

t_{x}\subset\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\times_{R}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}

given by preimage of
x
under the division morphism

\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n},(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto z_{1}/z_{2}.

In particular, if
x
consists of a single torsion section,
t_{x}
acts by translation by that torsion section.
9
9

9

The reason for this artifice, instead of simply considering translates by torsion sections, is that one may not be able to separate the torsion over more general bases.
The map
\tilde{f}_{\bullet}
clearly restricts to
f_{\bullet}
on the identity component, and is visibly
\Delta
-equivariant for the pullback action (
3.19
) on both sides.

From this, we find the following theorem:

Theorem 3.8
.

We have

T_{p}^{(i)}\Theta_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}=\left(\binom{n-1}{i-1}_{p}[p]^{*}+\binom{n}{i}_{p}-\binom{n-1}{i-1}_{p}\right)\Theta_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}.

where the
p
-binomial coefficients are given by

\binom{a}{b}_{p}=\frac{a!_{p}}{b!_{p}(a-b)!_{p}}

and the
p
-factorial by

a!_{p}:=\prod_{k=1}^{a}(p^{k}-1).

Proof.

By the preceding discussion, it suffices to show the equality of the operators
T_{p}^{(i)}
and

\binom{n-1}{i-1}_{p}[p]^{*}+\binom{n}{i}_{p}-\binom{n-1}{i-1}_{p}

acting on the identity
[1]\in\mathbb{Z}\{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}[p^{\infty}]\}
. Since algebraic cycles satisfy fppf descent, this equality can be checked upon base change to a base over which all the
p
-torsion of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
is defined.

Under this assumption, this becomes a linear algebra problem: how many times does a given
p
-torsion point appear in
T_{p}^{(i)}[1]
? For any non-identity point, the answer is the number of
(n-i)
-planes in the
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space containing both that point, a Schubert cell whose point count over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is
\binom{n-1}{i-1}_{p}
. And for the identity, the answer is simply the total number of such
(n-i)
-planes, which is simply the Grassmannian of
i
-planes and whose point count is
\binom{n}{i}_{p}
. From this, the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

This nice formula does not extend to the further-refined cocycles
\Theta_{com}(n)^{\phi}
, as the additional term which is a multiple of the orientation class certainly does not transform in the same way under the Hecke operators.

Remark 3.10
.

The approach of
[
18
]
is to take the pullback action
\gamma^{*}
from the outset, making the treatment of Hecke operators slightly more natural. The pullback they obtain is a left action (like our pushforward), as they take a
right
group action on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. This approach, however, prevents them from considering
S
-arithmetic actions as we did in this article. If one examines the equation (
3.19
), this difference in conventions has the effect of interchanging the Hecke operators
T_{p}^{(i)}
and
T_{p}^{(n-i)}
. By the symmetry relation

\binom{a}{b}_{p}=\binom{a}{a-b}_{p},

this actually has no effect on the resulting Hecke action.

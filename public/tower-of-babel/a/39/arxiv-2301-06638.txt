Abstract
In this paper, we study a two stream species Lotka-Volterra competition patch model with the patches aligned along a line. The two species are supposed to be identical except for the diffusion rates. For each species, the diffusion rates between patches are the same, while the drift rates vary. Our results show that the convexity of the drift rates has a significant impact on the competition outcomes: if the drift rates are convex, then the species with larger diffusion rate wins the competition; if the drift rates are concave, then the species with smaller diffusion rate wins the competition. Keywords : Lotka-Volterra competition model, patch, evolution of dispersal. MSC 2020 : 92D25, 92D40, 34C12, 34D23, 37C65.

Main results

In this section, we state our main results about the global dynamics of model (
1.2
). Let
q_{0}=0
throughout the paper. We will consider (
1.2
) under two different scenarios for the flow rate
\bm{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\geq\bm{0}

(\textbf{H1})

q_{i+1}-q_{i}\leq q_{i}-q_{i-1}
for
i=1,\cdots,n-1
, with at least one strict inequality.

(\textbf{H2})

q_{i+1}-q_{i}\geq q_{i}-q_{i-1}
for
i=1,\cdots,n-1
, with at least one strict inequality.

Fig.

(\textbf{H1})
and
(\textbf{H2})
. Assumption
(\textbf{H1})
describes a stream whose flow rate is convex, while
(\textbf{H2})
depicts the situation that the flow rate is concave. We will show that the competition outcomes of the species are dramatically different under
(\textbf{H1})
or
(\textbf{H2})
. We remark that our assumption
(\textbf{H2})
implicitly implies that the components of

are strictly increasing as

\bm{q}\geq\bm{0}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that

,
\bm{q}\geq\bm{0}
, and

{\rm(i)}

If

and

{\rm(i_{1})}

(\bm{0},\bm{v}^{*})

{\rm(i_{2})}

if

, then

{\rm(ii)}

If
\displaystyle\min_{1\leq i\leq n}q_{i}>r

{\rm(ii_{1})}

{\rm(ii_{2})}

3
Eigenvalue problems

For a real vector
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})^{T}
, we write
\bm{u}\geq\bm{0}

(\bm{u}\gg\bm{0})
if
u_{i}\geq 0
(
u_{i}>0
) for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and
\bm{u}>\bm{0}
if
\bm{u}\geq\bm{0}
and
\bm{u}\neq\bm{0}
. Suppose that
A
is an
n\times n
real matrix. The spectral bound
s(A)
of
A
is defined to be

s(A)=\max\left\{{\rm Re}\lambda:\;\lambda\;\text{is}\;\text{an}\;\text{eigenvalue}\;\text{of}\;A\right\}.

The matrix
A
is
essentially nonnegative
if all the off-diagonal elements are nonnegative.
By the Perron-Frobenius theorem, if
A
is an irreducible essentially nonnegative matrix, then
s(A)
is the unique eigenvalue (called the principal eigenvalue) of
A
corresponding to a nonnegative eigenvector.

Let
{\bm{m}}=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{n})^{T}
be a real vector,
d>0
and
\bm{q}\geq\bm{0}
. Consider the following eigenvalue problem:

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(dD_{ij}+q_{j}Q_{ij})\phi_{j}+m_{i}\phi_{i}=\lambda\phi_{i},\;\;i=1,\cdots,n.

(3.1)

Since
dD+Q\text{diag}(q_{i})+\text{diag}(m_{i})
is irreducible and essentially nonnegative,

\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m}):=s(dD+Q\text{diag}(q_{i})+\text{diag}(m_{i}))

(3.2)

is the principal eigenvalue of
dD+Q\text{diag}(q_{i})+\text{diag}(m_{i})
. The eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})
plays an essential role in our analysis. In the rest of this section, we will present several results on the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})
, which will be used later.

We start by considering the limits of
\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})
as
d\to 0
or
\infty
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
d>0
and
\bm{m}
is a real vector. Let
\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})
be defined as above. Then the following equations hold:

\lim_{d\to 0}\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{m_{i}-q_{i}\}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lim_{d\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})=\frac{\sum^{n}_{i=1}m_{i}-q_{n}}{n}.

Proof.

Clearly,

\lim_{d\to 0}\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})=\lambda_{1}(0,\bm{q},\bm{m})=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{m_{i}-q_{i}\}.

It remains to consider the limit of
\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})
as
d\to\infty
. Let
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n})^{T}\gg\bm{0}
be the eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})
with
\sum^{n}_{i=1}\phi_{i}=1
. By (
3.1
), we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(dD_{ij}+q_{j}Q_{ij})\phi_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\phi_{i}=\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m}).

(3.3)

Since

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{ij}\phi_{j}=0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}Q_{ij}\phi_{j}=-q_{n}\phi_{n},

we see from (
3.3
) that

-q_{n}\phi_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\phi_{i}=\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m}).

(3.4)

This yields

\min_{1\leq i\leq n}m_{i}-q_{n}\leq\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}m_{i}.

So, up to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume

\lim_{d\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{m})=a\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lim_{d\to\infty}\bm{\phi}=\hat{\bm{\phi}},

for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\hat{\bm{\phi}}=\left(\hat{\phi}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\phi}_{n}\right)^{T}\geq\bm{0}
with
\sum^{n}_{i=1}\hat{\phi}_{i}=1
. Dividing both sides of (
3.1
) by
d
and taking
d\to\infty
, we obtain
D\bm{\hat{}}{\phi}=0
, which implies that

\hat{\bm{\phi}}=\left(\hat{\phi}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\phi}_{n}\right)^{T}=\left(\frac{1}{n},\cdots,\frac{1}{n}\right)^{T}.

Taking
d\to\infty
in (
3.4
), we have
a=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}-q_{n})/{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Then we explore some further properties of
\lambda_{1}(d^{*},\bm{q},\bm{r})
, which will be important in the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
\bm{r}=(r,\cdots,r)^{T}
with
r>0
. If
\lambda_{1}(d^{*},\bm{q},\bm{r})=0
for some
d^{*}>0
, then the following statements hold:

{\rm(i)}

If
(\rm{\bf{H1}})
holds, then

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial d}\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{r})\right|_{d=d^{*}}>0;

{\rm(ii)}

If
(\rm{\bf{H2}})
holds, then

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial d}\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{r})\right|_{d=d^{*}}<0.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n})^{T}\gg\bm{0}
be the eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{r})
with
\sum^{n}_{i=1}\phi_{i}=1
. Differentiating (
3.1
) with respect to
d
, we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(dD_{ij}+q_{j}Q_{ij})\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial d}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{ij}\phi_{j}+r\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial d}=\lambda\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial d}+\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial d}\phi_{i},\;\;i=1,\cdots,n.

(3.5)

Multiplying (
3.5
) by
\phi_{i}
and (
3.1
) by
\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial d}
and taking the difference of them, we have

\sum_{j\neq i}(dD_{ij}+q_{j}Q_{ij})\left(\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial d}\phi_{i}-\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial d}\phi_{j}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{ij}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}=\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial d}\phi_{i}^{2},\;\;i=1,\cdots,n.

(3.6)

Let

\rho_{i}=\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{d}{d+q_{k}},\;\;\;\;i=1,\cdots,n.

Multiplying (
3.6
) by
\rho_{i}
and summing up over
i
, we get

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\rho_{i}(dD_{ij}+q_{j}Q_{ij})\left(\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial d}\phi_{i}-\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial d}\phi_{j}\right)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\rho_{i}D_{ij}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}.

(3.7)

It is not difficult to check that
\left(\rho_{i}(dD_{ij}+q_{j}Q_{ij})\right)
is symmetric. Therefore, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\neq i}\rho_{i}(dD_{ij}+q_{j}Q_{ij})\left(\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial d}\phi_{i}-\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial d}\phi_{j}\right)=0.

It follows that

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\rho_{i}D_{ij}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}.

(3.8)

Let
\bm{\phi}^{*}=(\phi_{1}^{*},\dots,\phi_{n}^{*})^{T}
be the positive eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(d^{*},\bm{q},\bm{r})=0
with
\sum^{n}_{i=1}\phi^{*}_{i}=1
and

\rho^{*}_{i}=\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}\frac{d^{*}}{d^{*}+q_{k}},\ \ \ i=1,\dots,n.

Evaluating (
3.7
) at
d=d^{*}
leads to

\begin{split}\left.\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial d}\right|_{d=d^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho^{*}_{i}(\phi_{i}^{*})^{2}=&\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\rho^{*}_{i}D_{ij}\phi^{*}_{i}\phi^{*}_{j}\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\rho^{*}_{i}\phi^{*}_{i}(\phi^{*}_{i+1}-\phi^{*}_{i})+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\rho^{*}_{i}\phi^{*}_{i}(\phi^{*}_{i-1}-\phi^{*}_{i})\\
=&\frac{1}{d^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\rho^{*}_{i+1}(\phi^{*}_{i+1}-\phi^{*}_{i})\left[(d^{*}+q_{i})\phi^{*}_{i}-d^{*}\phi^{*}_{i+1}\right].\end{split}

(3.9)

By (
3.1
), if
n\geq 3
then
\bm{\phi}^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle d^{*}(\phi^{*}_{2}-\phi^{*}_{1})=-\phi^{*}_{1}(r-q_{1}),

(3.10a)

\displaystyle d^{*}(\phi^{*}_{i+1}-\phi^{*}_{i})-(d^{*}+q_{i-1})(\phi^{*}_{i}-\phi^{*}_{i-1})=-\phi^{*}_{i}(r+q_{i-1}-q_{i}),\;i=2,\cdots,n-1,

(3.10b)

\displaystyle-(d^{*}+q_{n-1})(\phi^{*}_{n}-\phi^{*}_{n-1})=-\phi^{*}_{n}(r+q_{n-1}-q_{n}).

(3.10c)

If
n=2
, then
\bm{\phi}^{*}
satisfies only (
3.10a
) and (
3.10c
).

Now we consider case (i) and suppose that assumption
(\textbf{H1})
holds. We claim
\phi^{*}_{2}>\phi^{*}_{1}
. To see it, suppose to the contrary that
\phi^{*}_{2}\leq\phi^{*}_{1}
. By (
3.10a
), we have
r\geq q_{1}

We only prove (
5.5

) can be proved similarly. For any

d_{2}T_{k}-(d_{2}+q_{k-1})T_{k-1}=-u_{k}(r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k})+(d_{2}-d_{1})(T_{k}-T_{k-1}),

(5.8)

and

d_{2}S_{k}-(d_{2}+q_{k-1})S_{k-1}=-v_{k}(r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k}).

(5.9)

Multiplying (
5.8
) by
\rho^{(2)}_{k}v_{k}
and summing up from

to
k=j
, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=i}^{j}\rho^{(2)}_{k}\left[d_{2}T_{k}-(d_{2}+q_{k-1})T_{k-1}\right]v_{k}\\
=&(d_{2}-d_{1})\sum_{k=i}^{j}\rho^{(2)}_{k}\left(T_{k}-T_{k-1}\right)v_{k}-\sum_{k=i}^{j}\rho^{(2)}_{k}u_{k}v_{k}(r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k}).\\
\end{split}

(5.10)

A direct computation yields

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=i}^{j}\rho^{(2)}_{k}\left[d_{2}T_{k}-(d_{2}+q_{k-1})T_{k-1}\right]v_{k}\\
=&-\rho^{(2)}_{i}(d_{2}+q_{i-1})v_{i}T_{i-1}-d_{2}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\rho^{(2)}_{k}T_{k}S_{k}+d_{2}\rho^{(2)}_{j}v_{j}T_{j},\\
\end{split}

(5.11)

and

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=i}^{j}\rho^{(2)}_{k}\left(T_{k}-T_{k-1}\right)v_{k}\\
=&-\rho^{(2)}_{i}v_{i}T_{i-1}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{2}}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\rho^{(2)}_{k+1}T_{k}g_{k}+\rho^{(2)}_{j}v_{j}T_{j}.\end{split}

(5.12)

5.11

\begin{split}&-(d_{1}+q_{i-1})\rho^{(2)}_{i}v_{i}T_{i-1}-d_{2}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\rho^{(2)}_{k}T_{k}S_{k}+d_{1}\rho^{(2)}_{j}v_{j}T_{j}\\
=&-\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}-d_{1}}{d_{2}}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\rho^{(2)}_{k+1}T_{k}g_{k}-\sum_{k=i}^{j}\rho^{(2)}_{k}u_{k}v_{k}(r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k}).\end{split}

(5.13)

Similarly, multiplying (
5.9

and summing up from

=&-\sum_{k=i}^{j}\rho^{(2)}_{k}u_{k}v_{k}(r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k}).\end{split}

(5.14)

Taking the difference of (
5.13
) and (

5.5

In the following, we say that a sequence changes sign if it has both negative and positive terms.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\{T_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
and
\{S_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
be defined in (
5.4
) and suppose that

. Then the following statements hold:

{\rm(i)}

If
(\rm{\bf{H1}})
holds, then

{\rm(i_{1})}

T_{1},S_{1},T_{n-1},S_{n-1}>0
;

{\rm(i_{2})}

If

, then

,
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
must change sign;

{\rm(ii)}

If
(\rm{\bf{H2}})
holds, then

{\rm(ii_{1})}

T_{1},S_{1},T_{n-1},S_{n-1}<0
;

{\rm(ii_{2})}

If
n\geq 4
, then
\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
,
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}

Proof.

We rewrite (
5.8
)-(
5.9
) as follows:

\displaystyle d_{1}T_{k}-(d_{1}+q_{k-1})T_{k-1}=-u_{k}(r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k}),\;\;\;\;k=1,\cdots,n,

(5.15a)

\displaystyle d_{2}S_{k}-(d_{2}+q_{k-1})S_{k-1}=-v_{k}(r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k}),\;\;\;\;k=1,\cdots,n.

(5.15b)

We first consider (i). Suppose to the contrary that
T_{1}\leq 0
. Since
T_{0}=0
, we see from (
5.15a
) that

r+q_{0}-q_{1}-u_{1}-v_{1}\geq 0,

which yields
S_{1}\leq 0
by (
5.15b
). By
S_{1},T_{1}\leq 0
, we have
u_{2}\leq u_{1}
and
v_{2}\leq v_{1}
. This, combined with assumption
(\textbf{H1})
, yields

r+q_{1}-q_{2}-u_{2}-v_{2}\geq 0.

Then by (

) and induction, we can show that

T_{k}\leq 0,\;\;S_{k}\leq 0,\;\;\text{and}\;\;r+q_{k}-q_{k+1}-u_{k+1}-v_{k+1}\geq 0\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=1,\cdots,n-1\;.

Moreover, by the assumption that there exists at least one strict inequality in
(\textbf{H1})
, we have
r+q_{n-1}-q_{n}-u_{n}-v_{n}>0
.
Noticing
T_{n}=0
, we see from (

) that

0\leq-(d_{1}+q_{n-1})T_{n-1}=-u_{n}(r+q_{n-1}-q_{n}-u_{k}-v_{k})<0,

which is a contradiction. Therefore we have
T_{1}>0
. It follows from (
5.15
) with
k=n
that
S_{1}>0
. Using a similar argument, we can show that
T_{n-1},S_{n-1}>0
.

Suppose to the contrary that
\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
does not change sign.
Since

, we must have
T_{k}\geq 0
for
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, which yields

u_{1}<u_{2}\leq\cdots\leq u_{n-1}<u_{n}.

(5.16)

Noticing
T_{0}=S_{0}=T_{n}=S_{n}=0
and substituting
i=1
and
j=n
into (
5.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}-d_{1}}{d_{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho^{(2)}_{k+1}T_{k}g_{k}=0,

(5.17)

which implies that
\{g_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
must change sign. It follows from Lemma
5.1
that
\bar{i}=\min\{i:g_{i}>0\}
is well defined with
1<\bar{i}<n-1
. Moreover,
g_{k}\leq 0
for
1\leq k\leq\bar{i}-1
and

. This, combined with (
5.1b
) and (
5.3

(5.18)

This, together with (
5.16
), implies that

. Then by (
5.3
) and induction, we can prove that

for

,
which contradicts Lemma
5.1
. Therefore,
\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
must change sign. Similarly, we can prove that
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
also changes sign.

Now we consider (ii). The proof of
{\rm(ii_{1})}
is similar to
{\rm(i_{1})}
, so we omit it here.
To see
{\rm(ii_{2})}
, suppose to the contrary that
\{T_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
does not change sign. This, together with
{\rm(ii_{1})}
, implies that

for

, which yields
f_{k}<0
for

.

and

into (

\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho^{(1)}_{k+1}S_{k}f_{k}=0,

which means that

Hence by

.

(A_{1})
, we have

S_{i^{s}}>0,\;\;S_{k}\leq 0\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=i^{s}+1,\cdots,n-1,\;\;\text{and}\;\;T_{k}\leq 0\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=i^{s},\cdots,n-1,

which yields that
f_{k}<0
for
k=i^{s},\cdots,n-1
.
Then substituting
i=i^{s}+1
and
j=n
into (
5.6
), we have

\begin{split}0\geq&\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{1}}\sum_{k=i^{s}+1}^{n-1}\rho^{(1)}_{k+1}S_{k}f_{k}=-\rho^{(1)}_{i^{s}+1}\left[(d_{1}+q_{i^{s}})v_{i^{s}+1}T_{i^{s}}-(d_{2}+q_{i^{s}})u_{i^{s}+1}S_{i^{s}}\right]>0,\end{split}

which is a contradiction.

For case
(C_{2})
, we have
S_{i^{s}}>0
,
S_{i^{s}+1}\leq 0
,
T_{i_{2}}<0
and
T_{i_{2}+1}\geq 0
. If
i^{s}=i_{2}
, then by (
5.15
), we have

0<\frac{1}{u_{i^{s}+1}}[d_{1}T_{i^{s}+1}-(d_{1}+q_{i^{s}})T_{i^{s}}]=\frac{1}{v_{i^{s}+1}}[d_{2}S_{i^{s}+1}-(d_{2}+q_{i^{s}})S_{i^{s}}]<0,

(5.32)

which is a contradiction.

If
i^{s}>i_{2}
, then
T_{k}\geq 0
for
k=i_{2}+1,\cdots,i^{s}
, and consequently,

u_{i_{2}+1}\leq\cdots\leq u_{i^{s}}\leq u_{i^{s}+1}.

(5.33)

By (
5.15
) again, and noticing that
S_{i^{s}}>0
and
S_{i^{s}+1}\leq 0
, we have

r+q_{i^{s}}-q_{i^{s}+1}-u_{i^{s}+1}-v_{i^{s}+1}>0.

This, combined with
S_{i^{s}}>0
, assumption
(\textbf{H2})
and (
5.33
), implies that

r+q_{i^{s}-1}-q_{i^{s}}-u_{i^{s}}-v_{i^{s}}>0.

(5.34)

Then by (
5.15b
) and induction, we can show that

r+q_{k-1}-q_{k}-u_{k}-v_{k}>0\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=i_{2}+1,\cdots,i^{s},

and

S_{k}>0\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=i_{2},\cdots,i^{s}.

By (
5.15a
) with
k=i_{2}+1
, we have that

0<d_{1}T_{i_{2}+1}-(d_{1}+q_{i_{2}})T_{i_{2}}=-u_{i_{2}+1}(r+q_{i_{2}}-q_{i_{2}+1}-u_{i_{2}+1}-v_{i_{2}+1})<0,

which is a contradiction.

For case
(C_{3})
, we have
S_{i^{s}}>0
,
S_{k}\leq 0
for
k\geq i^{s}+1
,
T_{i_{2}+1}\geq 0
, and
T_{k}\leq 0
for
k=i^{s},\cdots,i_{2}
, which implies that
f_{k}<0
for
k=i^{s},\cdots,i_{2}
. Then substituting
i=i^{s}+1
and
j=i_{2}+1
into (
5.6
), we see that

\begin{split}0\geq&\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{1}}\sum_{k=i^{s}+1}^{i_{2}}\rho^{(1)}_{k+1}S_{k}f_{k}\\
=&\rho^{(1)}_{i_{2}+1}\left(d_{1}v_{i_{2}+1}T_{i_{2}+1}-d_{2}u_{i_{2}+1}S_{i_{2}+1}\right)\\
&-\rho^{(1)}_{i^{s}+1}\left[(d_{1}+q_{i^{s}})v_{i^{s}+1}T_{i^{s}}-(d_{2}+q_{i^{s}})u_{i^{s}+1}S_{i^{s}}\right]>0,\end{split}

which is a contradiction.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
5.5
, we can inductively prove the desired result for any
p>1
.
∎

6
Local stability of semi-trivial equilibria

In this section, we consider the stability of the two semi-trivial equilibria
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
and
(\bm{0},\bm{v}^{*})
if they exist. It suffices to study only
the stability of
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
since the stability of
(\bm{0},\bm{v}^{*})
can be investigated similarly. It is easy to see that the stability of
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
, if exists, is determined by the sign of
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})
: if
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})<0
, then
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
is locally asymptotically stable; if
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})>0
, then
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
is unstable. Similarly, the stability of
(\bm{0},\bm{v}^{*})
, if exists, is determined by the sign of
\lambda_{1}(d_{1},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{v}^{*})
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2},r>0
and
\bm{q}\geq\bm{0}
. Suppose that the semi-trivial equilibrium
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
of model (
1.2
) exists. Then
the following statements hold:

\rm{(i)}

If
(\rm{\bf{H1}})
holds, then
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
is locally asymptotically stable when
d_{1}>d_{2}
and
unstable when
d_{1}<d_{2}
;

\rm{(ii)}

If
(\rm{\bf{H2}})
holds, then
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
is locally asymptotically stable when
d_{1}<d_{2}
and unstable when

d_{1}>d_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n})^{T}\gg 0
be an eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})
, where
\bm{r}=(r,\dots,r)
.

(i) It follows from Lemma
4.4
that

u^{*}_{i}<u^{*}_{i+1}\;\;\text{for}\;\;i=1,\cdots,n-1.

(6.1)

This, combined with Lemma
3.3
, implies that

d_{2}\phi_{i+1}-(d_{2}+q_{i})\phi_{i}<0\;\;\text{for}\;\;i=1,\cdots,n-1.

(6.2)

Since
\bm{u}^{*}
is an eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(d_{1},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})=0
, by Lemma
3.4
, we have

\begin{split}&-\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})\sum_{k=1}^{n}\rho^{(2)}_{k}u^{*}_{k}\phi_{k}\\
=&\frac{(d_{2}-d_{1})}{d_{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho^{(2)}_{k+1}\left(u^{*}_{k+1}-u^{*}_{k}\right)\left[d_{2}\phi_{k+1}-(d_{2}+q_{k})\phi_{k}\right],\end{split}

(6.3)

where
\rho^{(2)}_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
, are defined by (
3.20
).
Hence by (
6.1
)-(
6.2
),
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})<0
when
d_{1}>d_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})>0
when
d_{1}<d_{2}
. Therefore,
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
is locally asymptotically stable when
d_{1}>d_{2}
and unstable when
d_{1}<d_{2}
.

(ii) We first claim that

\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})\neq 0\;\;\text{if}\;\;d_{2}\neq d_{1}.

(6.4)

Without loss of generality, we may assume
d_{1}<d_{2}
.
Suppose to the contrary that
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})=0
. Let
\bm{\psi}\gg 0
be a corresponding eigenvector of
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})
. Recall that
\bm{u}^{*}
is an eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(d_{1},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})\;(=0)
. We define two sequences
\{\widetilde{f}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
and
\{\widetilde{g}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
as follows:

\begin{split}&\widetilde{f}_{0}=\widetilde{f}_{n}=0,\;\;\widetilde{f}_{k}=d_{1}u^{*}_{k+1}-(d_{1}+q_{k})u^{*}_{k}\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=1,\cdots,n-1,\\
&\widetilde{g}_{0}=\widetilde{g}_{n}=0,\;\;\widetilde{g}_{k}=d_{2}\psi_{k+1}-(d_{2}+q_{k})\psi_{k}\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=1,\cdots,n-1,\end{split}

(6.5)

and another two sequences
\{\widetilde{T}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
and
\{\widetilde{S}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
as follows:

\begin{split}&\widetilde{T}_{0}=\widetilde{T}_{n}=0,\;\;\widetilde{T}_{k}=u^{*}_{k+1}-u^{*}_{k}\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=1,\cdots,n-1,\\
&\widetilde{S}_{0}=\widetilde{S}_{n}=0,\;\;\widetilde{S}_{k}=\psi_{k+1}-\psi_{k}\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=1,\cdots,n-1.\end{split}

(6.6)

Using similar arguments as the proof of Lemma
5.2
, we can show that

\begin{split}\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{1}}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\rho^{(1)}_{k+1}\widetilde{S}_{k}\widetilde{f}_{k}=&\rho^{(1)}_{j}\left(d_{1}\psi_{j}\widetilde{T}_{j}-d_{2}u^{*}_{j}\widetilde{S}_{j}\right)\\
&-\rho^{(1)}_{i}\left[(d_{1}+q_{i-1})\psi_{i}\widetilde{T}_{i-1}-(d_{2}+q_{i-1})u^{*}_{i}\widetilde{S}_{i-1}\right],\end{split}

(6.7)

for any
1\leq i<j\leq n
, where
\rho^{(1)}_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
, are defined by (
5.7
).

By Lemma
4.4
, we have

\widetilde{T}_{k}<0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\widetilde{f}_{k}<0\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=1,\cdots,n-1.

(6.8)

It follows that

\widetilde{S}_{n-1}=\frac{\psi_{n}}{d_{2}+q_{n-1}}(r+q_{n-1}-q_{n}-u^{*}_{n})=\frac{\psi_{n}}{d_{2}+q_{n-1}}\frac{(d_{1}+q_{n-1})\widetilde{T}_{n-1}}{u^{*}_{n}}<0.

(6.9)

Substituting
i=1
and
j=n
into (
6.7
), we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho^{(1)}_{k+1}\widetilde{S}_{k}\widetilde{f}_{k}=0.

(6.10)

Then it follows from (
6.9
)-(
6.8
) that
i^{*}=\max\{i:\widetilde{S}_{i}>0\}
is well defined with
1\leq i^{*}<n-1
. By the definition of
i^{*}
, we have

\widetilde{S}_{i^{*}}>0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\widetilde{S}_{k}\leq 0\;\;\text{for}\;\;k=i^{*}+1,\cdots,n-1.

Substituting
i=i^{*}+1
and
j=n
into (
6.7
), we have

\begin{split}0\geq&\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{d_{1}}\sum_{k=i^{*}+1}^{n-1}\rho^{(1)}_{k+1}\widetilde{S}_{k}\widetilde{f}_{k}\\
=&-\rho^{(1)}_{i}\left[(d_{1}+q_{i^{*}})\psi_{i^{*}+1}\widetilde{T}_{i^{*}}-(d_{2}+q_{i^{*}})u^{*}_{i^{*}+1}\widetilde{S}_{i^{*}}\right]>0,\end{split}

which is a contradiction.
This proves that
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})\neq 0
if
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
.

Finally, it follows from (
3.9
) that

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial d}\lambda_{1}(d,\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})\right|_{d=d_{1}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\rho^{(1)}_{i+1}(u^{*}_{i+1}-u^{*}_{i})\left[(d_{1}+q_{i})u^{*}_{i}-d_{1}u^{*}_{i+1}\right]}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{1}\rho^{(1)}_{i}(u^{*}_{i})^{2}}<0.

This implies that
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})<0
when
0<d_{2}-d_{1}\ll 1
and
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})>0
when
0<d_{1}-d_{2}\ll 1
. Hence by (
6.4
), we know that
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})<0
when
d_{1}<d_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}(d_{2},\bm{q},\bm{r}-\bm{u}^{*})>0
when
d_{1}>d_{2}
. Therefore,
(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{0})
is locally asymptotically stable when
d_{1}<d_{2}
and unstable when
d_{1}>d_{2}
.
∎

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative Noetherian local ring with residue field k k . Let 𝒳 \operatorname{\mathcal{X}} be a resolving subcategory of finitely generated R R -modules. This paper mainly studies when 𝒳 \operatorname{\mathcal{X}} contains k k or consists of totally reflexive modules. It is proved that 𝒳 \operatorname{\mathcal{X}} does so if 𝒳 \operatorname{\mathcal{X}} is closed under cosyzygies. A conjecture of Dao and Takahashi is also shown to hold in several cases.



1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper,
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring and
\md R
denotes the category
of all finitely generated
R
-modules.
Whenever
R
is assumed to be local, its unique maximal ideal is denoted by
\mathfrak{m}
.
All subcategories are full.
For a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
, we denote by
\cm(R)
the
subcategory of
\md R
consisting of maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules.
The radius of a subcategory was defined by Dao and Takahashi
[
7
]
.
This notion is linked to both the
representation theory and the singularity of
R
. For example, in
[
7
, Proposition 2.5]
, over a Gorenstein complete local ring
R
, it is shown that the category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules has radius zero if and only if
R
has finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type, in other words,
R
is a simple hypersurface singularity (when the residue field of
R
is algebraically closed field of characteristic zero).
In
[
7
, Theorem II]
, for a Cohen–Macaulay complete local ring
R
with perfect coefficient field, it is shown that
\cm(R)
has finite radius. Dao and Takahashi
propose the following conjecture, and prove that it holds if R is a complete intersection.

Conjecture 1.1

(Dao–Takahashi)
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md R
with finite radius. Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in
\cm(R)
.

In the first part of this paper, we study the resolving subcategory with finite radius. We obtain the following result which supports Conjecture
1.1
in several cases (see Theorem
3.5
, Proposition
3.10
and Corollary
3.13
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring, and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md R
with finite radius. Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in
\cm(R)
(hence Conjecture
1.1
holds true) if one of the following holds.

(i)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under
R
-duals (i.e.
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
).

(ii)

R
has minimal multiplicity.

(iii)

R
is Gorenstein with
\cod R\leq 4
or
\e(R)\leq 11
and each module in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
has bounded Betti numbers.

Here
\cod R
denotes the codimension of
R
and
\e(R)
stands for the multiplicity of
R
with respect to the maximal ideal. A typical example of resolving subcategories closed under
R
-duals is
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
, the subcategory of totally reflexive modules.

It is quite natural to ask when a given resolving subcategory contains (some syzygy of) the residue field.
This is closely related to the vanishing problems of Tor and Ext modules.
Let
M
be an
R
-module, and suppose that its resolving closure contains some syzygy of
k
.
Then
M
is a so-called
test
module; an
R
-module
N
has finite projective dimension whenever
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{\gg 0}^{R}(M,N)=0
.
This question is also related to the radius of a resolving subcategory. For a resolving subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, over a local ring of positive dimension, if
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
contains the residue field, then it has infinite radius
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
(see also Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
).
In the second part of this paper, we study resolving subcategories which are closed under cosyzygies. A typical example of such subcategories is again
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.
It is observed that, over a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
of positive dimension,
\cm(R)
is closed under cosyzygies if and only if
R
is Gorenstein (i.e.
\cm(R)=\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
).
We obtain the following result, as a special case of Theorem
4.8
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
R
be a local ring with residue field
k
, and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md R
closed under cosyzygies.
Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
either contains
k
or is contained in
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
. In particular, if
\dim R>0
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
has finite radius, then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
and Conjecture
1.1
holds true.

We also prove that every resolving subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
with
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
(see Theorem
4.3
). Here,
\Omega
stands for the syzygy functor. As a consequence of Theorem
1.3
, for a non-trivial resolving subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
of finite radius, over a Golod local ring
R
of positive dimension, it is shown that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under cosyzygies if and only if
R
is a hypersurface.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In section 2, we collect preliminary notions,
definitions and some known results which will be used in this paper. In section 3, we study the resolving subcategory with finite radius. We prove our first main result, Theorem
1.2
, in this section. In section 4, we study resolving subcategories which are closed under cosyzygies. We prove Theorem
1.3
in this section.

We recall the definition of an approximation with respect to a subcategory.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}
be an additive category, and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}
.

(i)

A morphism

(resp.

)

(resp.
f^{\prime}:M\to X^{\prime}
) with

factors through

, that is,
f^{\prime}=fg
(resp.
f^{\prime}=gf
) for some morphism
g:X^{\prime}\to X
(resp.
g:X\to X^{\prime}
).

(ii)

A right (resp. left)
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
-approximation
f:X\to M
(resp.
f:M\to X
) is called
minimal
if every endomorphism
g:X\to X
with
f=fg
(resp.
f=gf
) is an automorphism.

Remark 2.2
.

For
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}=\md(R)
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\add R
one has the following.

(i)

A right
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
-approximation (resp. a minimal right
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
-approximation) is nothing but a surjective homomorphism from a projective
R
-module (resp. a projective cover).

(ii)

Let
M

R
-module.
Denote by
\lambda:M\to M^{**}
the natural homomorphism.
Take a surjection
\pi:P\to M^{*}
with

Then the composition
\pi^{*}\circ\lambda:M\to P^{*}
is a left
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
-approximation.

In the following we recall the definitions of the syzygy, transpose and cosyzygy of a given module, which will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module.

(i)

Assume that

\add(R)
-approximation of

is called the

of
M

\Omega^{1}M

\Omega_{R}^{1}M

\Omega^{n}M:=\Omega^{1}(\Omega^{n-1}M)

(ii)

Let

M

R
-dual map

(Auslander) transpose
of

\mathsf{Tr}M
(or
\mathsf{Tr}_{R}M
). Therefore we have the following exact sequence:

0\rightarrow M^{*}\rightarrow P_{0}^{*}\rightarrow P_{1}^{*}\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}M\rightarrow 0.

The transpose of a module is unique up to projective equivalence.

(iii)

Let
M\overset{d_{-1}}{\rightarrow}P_{-1}

be a left
\add(R)
-approximation of
M
. Then we call the cokernel of
d_{-1}
the
first cosyzygy
of
M

\Omega^{-1}M
(or
\Omega^{-1}_{R}M
). Inductively, we define the
n
-th cosyzygy

M

\Omega^{-n}M:=\Omega^{-1}(\Omega^{-(n-1)}M)
for all
n>0
.

Whenever
R
is local, we define the syzygy, cosyzygy and transpose by using a minimal right
\add(R)
-approximation, a minimal left
\add(R)
-approximation and a minimal projective presentation, respectively so that they are uniquely determined up to isomorphism.

Proposition 2.5
.

For an
R

The notion of Gorenstein dimension was introduced by Auslander
[
1
]
, and deeply developed by Auslander and Bridger in
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

An
R
-module
M
is called of Gorenstein dimension at most zero (or totally reflexive) if
M\cong M^{\ast\ast}
(or equivalently, the canonical map
M\rightarrow M^{**}
is bijective) and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M^{*},R)=0
for all
i>0
.
We denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
the subcategory of
\md R
consisting of totally reflexive modules.

The
Gorenstein dimension
of
M
, denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(M)
, is defined to be the infimum of all
nonnegative integers
n
such that there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow G_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

where each
G_{i}
are totally reflexive. Note by definition that
\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(0)=-\infty
.

In the following, we summarize some basic facts about Gorenstein dimension (see
[
2
]
).

Proposition 2.7
.

For an
R
-module
M
, the following statements hold true.

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(M)\leq 0
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}M)\leq 0
;

(ii)

(Auslander-Bridger formula) If
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension, then

\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R-\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R}(M).

(iii)

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(M)
is finite, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(M)=\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0\}.

(iv)

R
is Gorenstein if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R}(M)<\infty
for all finite
R
-module
M
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Recall that an
R
-module
M
is called
n
-torsion free
if

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}M,R)=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
An
R
-module

is said to be an
n
-th syzygy
if there exists an exact sequence

0\to M\to P_{0}\to\cdots\to P_{n-1}

with the
P_{0},\cdots,P_{n-1}
are projective. By convention, every module is a
0
-th syzygy. We denote by
\Omega^{n}(\md(R))

the full subcategory of
\md(R)
consisting of

n
-th syzygy modules. For an integer

, we define a set
\mathrm{X}^{m}(R)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R\mid\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq m\}

Lemma 2.8
.

[
13

Let
R
be a ring and let

be an

The notion of a resolving subcategory has been introduced by Auslander and Bridger

]

Definition 2.9
.

A subcategory

of
\md R

(i)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
contains the projective

(ii)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}

R
-module in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and

is an
R
-module that is a direct summand of

, then

is also in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}

(iii)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under extensions (i.e. for an exact sequence

of
R
-modules, if
L
,
N
are in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, then so is
M
).

(iv)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under kernels of epimorphisms (i.e. for an exact sequence
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
of
R
-modules, if
M
,
N
are in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, then so is
L
).

A subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
of
\md R
is resolving if and only if
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
contains
R
and is
closed under direct summands, extensions and syzygies
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
. There are many examples of resolving subcategories in
\md(R)
.
For example,
\cm(R)
is a resolving subcategory of
\md R
if

is Cohen–Macaulay. The subcategory of
\md R
consisting of totally reflexive

-modules is resolving by
[

, 3.11]
(see also
[

, Example 1.6]
for more examples).

A subcategory
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
of

is called

if

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under direct summands and that for each exact sequence

of totally reflexive

-modules, if two of

,

,

are in

, then so is the

.

]

.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}

be subcategories of

(i)

The subcategory

When

(iii)

The

is defined as follows:

[\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}]_{n}=\begin{cases}[\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}]&\text{for $n=1$}\,,\\

(iv)

The

3.
Resolving subcategories with finite radius

In this section, we study resolving subcategories with finite radius. We prove our first main result, Theorem

For an ideal
I

R

R
-module

\ann_{R}(\hh^{i}_{I}(M))

The following result is a generalization of

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
I
be an ideal of
R
. Assume that
M
and
C
are
R
-modules and that
n
is a positive integer.
If
M\in[C]_{n}
, then

\left(\underset{i=0}{\overset{t}{\prod}}\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{i}(R)\,\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{i}(C)\right)^{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{t}(M)\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, then
M
is isomorphic to a direct summand of a finite direct sum of copies of
R\oplus(\underset{j=0}{\overset{m}{\bigoplus}}\Omega^{j}C)
.
Therefore
\hh^{t}_{I}(M)
is isomorphic to a direct summand of a finite direct sum of copies of
\hh^{t}_{I}(R)\oplus(\underset{j=0}{\overset{m}{\bigoplus}}\hh^{t}_{I}(\Omega^{j}C))

(
3.1
.1)

\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{t}(R)\cap\left(\underset{j=0}{\overset{m}{\bigcap}}\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{t}(\Omega^{j}C)\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{t}(M).

For each
j>0
there is an exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Omega^{j}C\rightarrow P_{j-1}\rightarrow\Omega^{j-1}C\rightarrow 0
, where
P_{j-1}
is a projective
R
-module. This induces an exact sequence

\hh^{i-1}_{I}(\Omega^{j-1}C)\rightarrow\hh^{i}_{I}(\Omega^{j}C)\rightarrow\hh^{i}_{I}(P_{j-1})
, which gives the following inclusions

Therefore the assertion is clear for the case

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}

Lemma 3.3

Let

The following lemma plays a crucial role in the proof of our main results.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
. Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)\neq 0
for some
i>0
. Then there exists an
R
-module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\md_{0}(R)
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0
for some
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
.
If
\NF(M)=\V(\mathfrak{m})
, then we have nothing to prove. So assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\NF(M)
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
and that
x
is an element in
\mathfrak{m}
which is not in
\mathfrak{p}
. By
[
16
, Proposition 4.2]
, there is a commutative diagram:

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\Omega M@>{}>{}>R^{n}@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{}V{x}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\parallel}V\\
0@>{}>{}>\Omega M@>{}>{}>M_{1}@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>0\\
\end{CD}

where
M_{1}
is an
R
-module such that
\V(\mathfrak{m})\subseteq\NF(M_{1})\subseteq\NF(M)
and
\mathsf{D}(x)\cap\NF(M_{1})=\emptyset
. Hence
\V(\mathfrak{m})\subseteq\NF(M_{1})\subsetneq\NF(M)
.
It follows from the above diagram that
M_{1}\in\res(M)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. The above diagram induces the following commutative diagram:

\begin{CD}\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i-1}_{R}(\Omega M,R)@>{}>{}>\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)@>{}>{}>\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M_{1},R)\\
@V{}V{x}V@V{}V{\parallel}V@V{}V{}V\\
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i-1}_{R}(\Omega M,R)@>{}>{}>\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)@>{}>{}>0\\
\end{CD}

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M_{1},R)=0
, then it follows from the above diagram and the Nakayama’s Lemma that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0
which is a contradiction. Therefore
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M_{1},R)
is a nonzero
R
-module.
If
\V(\mathfrak{m})
coincides with
\NF(M_{1})
, then we are done. So we assume that
\V(\mathfrak{m})
is strictly contained in
\NF(M_{1})
. Then,
a similar argument to the above shows that there exists an
R
-module
M_{2}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
which satisfies

\V(\mathfrak{m})\subseteq\NF(M_{2})\subsetneq\NF(M_{1})\subsetneq\NF(M),

and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M_{2},R)\neq 0
. By
[
16
, Corollary 2.11(1)]
, all nonfree loci are closed subsets of
\Spec R
.
Since
\Spec R
is a Noetherian space, every descending chain of closed subsets stabilizes.
This means that the above procedure to construct modules
M_{i}
cannot be iterate infinitely many times. Hence there exists an
R
-module
N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
such that
\NF(N)=\V(\mathfrak{m})
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(N,R)\neq 0
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory
of
\md(R)
. Assume that
\mathsf{radius}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}})<\infty
and that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}^{*}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If
\dim R\geq 3
, then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.

(ii)

If
R
is Cohen–Macaulay, then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\cm(R)
.

Proof.

(i). First assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)=0
and that
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. As
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is resolving and closed under dual,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M^{*},R)
for all
i>0
. The exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Omega M\rightarrow F\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
induces the following commutative diagram with exact rows:

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\Omega M@>{}>{}>F_{0}@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{}V{f}V@V{}V{\|\wr}V@V{}V{g}V\\
0@>{}>{}>(\Omega M)^{**}@>{}>{}>F_{0}^{**}@>{}>{}>M^{**}@>{}>{}>0\\
\end{CD}

It follows from the above diagram and the snake Lemma that
f
is a monomorphism and
g
is an epimorphism. Similarly, the exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Omega^{2}M\rightarrow F\rightarrow\Omega M\rightarrow 0
induces the following commutative diagram with exact rows:

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\Omega^{2}M@>{}>{}>F_{1}@>{}>{}>\Omega M@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\|\wr}V@V{}V{f}V\\
0@>{}>{}>(\Omega^{2}M)^{**}@>{}>{}>F_{1}^{**}@>{}>{}>(\Omega M)^{**}@>{}>{}>0\\
\end{CD}

It follows from the above diagram and the snake Lemma that
f
is is an epimorphism. Hence
f
is an isomorphism and so is
g
. In other words,
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
. Now assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)\neq 0
. By Lemma
3.4
there exists an
R
-module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\md_{0}(R)
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0
.
Let
\sigma\in\mathsf{soc}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,R))
be a non-zero element. Hence we have the following non-split sequence:
0\rightarrow R\rightarrow N\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
which induces the following exact sequence:

(
3.5
.1)

0\rightarrow M^{*}\rightarrow N^{*}\rightarrow R\rightarrow k\rightarrow 0.

As
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under dual,
M^{*}
and
N^{*}
are belongs to
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. It follows from the exact sequence (
3.5
.1) and
[
15
, Lemma 4.3]
that

\Omega^{2}k\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
which is a contradiction by Proposition
3.2
.

(ii) Assume contrarily that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\nsubseteq\cm(R)
. Hence
\Omega^{n}k\notin\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
for all
n\geq 0
, by Lemma
3.3
. By Auslander-Bridger formula
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\nsubseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
. Hence, by the similar argument in the proof of first part, one can show that
\Omega^{2}k\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
which is a contradiction.
∎

t>1
. Hence we have the following isomorphisms:

(3.12.1)

\Omega^{-2}\Omega^{m}M\cong\Omega^{-2}(\Omega^{m-n}(\Omega^{n}M))\cong\Omega^{-2}\Omega^{m-n}(\Omega^{n+t}M)\\
\cong\Omega^{-2}\Omega^{m-n}\Omega^{t}(\Omega^{n}M)\cong\Omega^{t-2}\Omega^{m-n}(\Omega^{n}M)\cong\Omega^{t-2}(\Omega^{m}M)\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}.

Now the assertion follows from (
3.12
.1) and
[
7
, Theorem 4.10]
.
∎

The following is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.12
,

[
3
, Theorem 1.6]
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
. Assume that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
contains a module with bounded Betti numbers which is not maximal Cohen–Macaulay. Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
has infinite radius if either
\cod R\leq 4
or
\e(R)\leq 11
.

4.
Closedness under cosyzygies

In this section, we study resolving subcategories which are closed under cosyzygies. We prove our second main result, Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\Omega^{-1}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\Omega(\md(R))
.

(ii)

\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
As
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is resolving,
\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. On the other hand, for all
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, there exists an exact sequence
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow P\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}M\rightarrow 0
, where
P
is projective. As
\Omega^{-1}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
,
M\cong\Omega(\Omega^{-1}M)\in\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}.
Hence
\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). For all
R
-module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, there exists an
R
-module
N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
such that
M\cong\Omega N
.
By
[
2
, Proposition 2.21]
, there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow Q\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}\Omega N\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
,
where
Q
is projective. As
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is resolving, it follows from the above exact sequence that
\Omega^{-1}M\cong\Omega^{-1}\Omega N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Hence

\Omega^{-1}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a subcategory of
\md(R)
such that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\Omega(\md(R))

(ii)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under
\Omega^{-1}
and kernel of epimorphisms.

(iii)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{PD}}(R)=\add(R)
.

Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.

Proof.

By (i), (ii), for all
R
-module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, we obtain the following long exact sequence

(
4.2
.1)

0\longrightarrow M\overset{d_{0}}{\longrightarrow}Q_{0}\overset{d_{1}}{\longrightarrow}Q_{1}\longrightarrow\cdots

where
\coker(d_{i})=\Omega^{-i-1}M
for all
i\geq 0
and
Q_{i}
is projective for all
i
. As
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{-i}M,R)=0
for all
i>0
, the exact sequence (
4.2
.1) induces the following exact sequence:

(
4.2
.2)

\cdots\longrightarrow(Q_{1})^{*}\longrightarrow(Q_{0})^{*}\longrightarrow M^{*}\longrightarrow 0.

Dualizing the exact sequence (
4.2
.2), we get the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>Q_{0}@>{}>{}>Q_{1}@>{}>{}>\cdots\\
&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\|\wr}V@V{}V{\|\wr}V\\
0@>{}>{}>M^{**}@>{}>{}>(Q_{0})^{**}@>{}>{}>(Q_{1})^{**}@>{}>{}>\cdots\\
\end{CD}

It follows from the above diagram that
M\cong M^{**}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M^{*},R)=0
for all
i>0
. In other words,
M
is
n
-torsion free for all
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and
n>0
.
Now it is enough to show that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0
for all
i>0
. First we prove the following claim.

Claim
. For every
R
-module
N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n}_{R}(N,R)=0
for some
n>0
, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(N,R)=0
.

Set
Y=\Omega^{n-1}N
. Note that
\Omega^{2}Y\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and so it is
2
-torsion-free. Hence, by
[
2
, Proposition 2.21]
, there exists an exact sequence

(
4.2
.3)

\sigma:0\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow\Omega^{-2}\Omega^{2}Y\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow 0,

where
\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{R}(Z)
is finite. By (i) and (ii),
Z\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Hence by (iii),
Z
is projective. As
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}(Y,R)=0
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(Y,Z)=0
. In other words,
\sigma
is a split exact sequence and so

Y\approx\Omega^{-2}\Omega^{2}Y
. Note that
\Omega^{-1}\Omega^{2}Y\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and so it is a first syzygy module by (i). Hence we have the following exact sequence:
0\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}\Omega^{2}Y\rightarrow P\rightarrow\Omega^{-2}\Omega^{2}Y\rightarrow 0
, where
P
is a projective
R
-module. Therefore we get the following isomorphisms:

(
4.2
.4)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(N,R)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(Y,R)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\Omega^{-2}\Omega^{2}Y,R)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{-1}\Omega^{2}Y,R)=0,

which proves our claim. Now let
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. By (i),
M
is a first syzygy module and we have the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow Q\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}M\rightarrow 0
, where
Q
is a projective
R
-module. By (ii),
\Omega^{-1}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. As
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{-1}M,R)=0
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i+1}_{R}(\Omega^{-1}M,R)=0
for all
i>0
by the claim. Hence
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
with
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.

Proof.

By our assumption, for every
R
-module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and every positive integer number
n
there exists an
R
-module
N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
such that
M\cong\Omega^{n}N
. Therefore
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{PD}}(R)=\add(R)
and so the assertion is clear by Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
. ∎

Let
R
be a Golod local ring which is not a hypersurface (e.g. a Cohen-Macaulay non-Gorenstein local ring with minimal multiplicity). In
[
4
, Example 3.5]
, it is shown that
\add(R)=\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.
The following can be viewed as a generalization of this result.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
R
be a Golod local ring and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a non-trivial resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
(i.e.
\add(R)\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subsetneq\md(R)
). Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
if and only if
R
is a hypersurface and
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, then by Theorem
4.3

\add(R)\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
. Hence, by
[
4
, Example 3.5]
,
R
is a hypersurface.
On the other hand, if
R
is a hypersurface and
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
then
\Omega^{-1}M\cong\Omega^{2}\Omega^{-1}M\cong\Omega M
for all
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
by
[
8
, Theorem 6.1]
. Now the assertion follows from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Here is an example of a subcategory which is closed under cosyzygies.

Example 4.5
.

Let
S
be any subset of
\Spec R
.
Consider the subcategory

\NF^{-1}(S)=\{M\in\md R\mid\NF(M)\subseteq S\}

of
\md R
.
Then it is easy to see that
\NF^{-1}(S)
is a resolving subcategory closed under
\mathsf{Tr}
.
Hence it is also closed under
\Omega^{-1}
.
In particular,
\md_{0}R=\NF^{-1}(\{\mathfrak{m}\})
is a resolving subcategory of
\md R
closed under
\Omega^{-1}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
R
be a local ring and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
such that
\Omega^{-1}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\Omega(\md(R))
. Then either of the following holds.

(i)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.

(ii)

R
is a Gorenstein ring of dimension
1
.

Proof.

Assume that
R
is not a Gorenstein ring of dimension
1
. We want to show that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
. By Theorem (
4.3
), it is enough to show that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\Omega\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Let
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and consider the following exact sequences

0\rightarrow\Omega M\rightarrow F\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
and

0\rightarrow\Omega M\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}\Omega M\rightarrow 0
. We make the following pushout diagram

\begin{CD}&&&&&&0&&0\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\ \ &&&&0@>{}>{}>\Omega M@>{}>{}>F@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>0&\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\parallel}V\\
\ \ &&&&0@>{}>{}>F_{1}@>{}>{}>T@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>0&\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\ \ &&&&&&\\
&&&&&&\Omega^{-1}\Omega M&=&\Omega^{-1}\Omega M\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&&&&&0&&0\end{CD}

Note that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{-1}\Omega M,R)=0
.
Hence the second column in the above diagram splits, and we get an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow F_{1}\longrightarrow F\oplus\Omega^{-1}\Omega^{1}M\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0.

As
\Omega^{-1}\Omega M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, it is a first syzygy module by our assumption and so we have the following exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}\Omega M\rightarrow F^{\prime}\rightarrow\Omega^{-2}\Omega M\rightarrow 0
, which induces the following exact sequence:

0\rightarrow F\oplus\Omega^{-1}\Omega M\rightarrow F\oplus F^{\prime}\rightarrow\Omega^{-2}\Omega M\rightarrow 0
.
Thus the following pushout diagram is obtained:

\begin{CD}&&&&&&&&0&&0\\
&&&&&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\ \ &&&&0@>{}>{}>F_{1}@>{}>{}>F\oplus\Omega^{-1}\Omega M@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>0&\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{\parallel}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\ \ &&&&0@>{}>{}>F_{1}@>{}>{}>F\oplus F^{\prime}@>{}>{}>X@>{}>{}>0&\\
&&&&&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&&&&&&&\Omega^{-2}\Omega M&=&\Omega^{-2}\Omega M\\
&&&&&&&&@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
&&&&&&&&0&&0\end{CD}

It follows from the above diagram that
X\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{R}(X)\leq 1
. Hence it is enough to show that
X
is free. To do this, we prove that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{PD}}(R)\subseteq\add(R)
.
If
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R=0
, then the assertion follows from Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, so we may assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R>0
.
Assume contrarily that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{PD}}(R)\nsubseteq\add(R)
. Hence there exists an
R
-module
N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{R}(N)=1
.
By
[
7
, Lemma 4.6]
, we may assume that
N
is locally free.
Therefore
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(N,R)
is a nonzero
R
-module of finite length. Let
\sigma\in\mathsf{soc}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(N,R))
be a non-zero element. Hence we have the following non-split exact sequence:

(4.6.1)

\sigma:0\rightarrow R\rightarrow L\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0,

which induces the following exact sequence:
0\rightarrow k\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(N,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(L,R)\rightarrow 0
. Therefore
\ell(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(L,R))<\ell(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(N,R))
. Replacing
N
by

L
and repeating this process if
\ell(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(L,R))>0
, we can assume that

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(L,R)=0
. Therefore
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(N,R)\cong k
. As
\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{R}(N)=1
,
\mathsf{Tr}N\cong k
. Hence
\mathsf{Tr}k\cong N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. By our assumption,
\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{i}k\cong\Omega^{-i}\mathsf{Tr}k\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\Omega(\md(R))
for all
i>0
. Therefore
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(k,R)=0
for all
i>1
which is a contradiction because
R
is not a Gorenstein ring of dimension one.
∎

In the following example, it is shown that over a one dimensional Gorenstein local ring
R
there exists a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
which satisfies in the assumption of Proposition
4.6
, but not contained in
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.

Example 4.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
1
. Set

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\md_{0}(R)
. Then the following hold.

(i)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is resolving.

(ii)

\Omega^{-1}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\Omega(\md(R))
.

(iii)

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is not contained in
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R).

Proof.

Part (i) is stated in Example
4.5
. For all
R
-modules
M
,

\Omega\mathsf{Tr}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
, because
R
is Gorenstein of dimension
1
. Hence, by Lemma
2.4
and Proposition
2.7
(i),
\Omega^{-1}M=\mathsf{Tr}\Omega\mathsf{Tr}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
and so (ii) is clear.
Also
k
is an element of
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
which is not contained in
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.
∎

Now we can prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring and let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
. If
\Omega^{-n}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
for some
1\leq n\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R+1
, then either
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
or
\Omega^{n-1}k\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
holds.
In particular, if
\Omega^{-1}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, then either
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
or
k\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
holds.

Proof.

We begin with the case
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R=0
; then
n=1
.
First we consider the case
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)=0
. Then

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{>0}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)=0
. Let
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
and
i\geq 0
. By Lemma
2.4
and Proposition
2.5
(ii), there is an exact sequence

(
4.8
.1)

0\rightarrow E\rightarrow\Omega^{-i}M\rightarrow\Omega\Omega^{-(i+1)}M\rightarrow 0,

where
E:=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i+1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}M,R)
. As
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under cosyzygies, it follows from the exact sequence (
4.8
.1) that
E\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{>0}_{R}(E,R)=0
. By
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
, there is an exact sequence

(
4.8
.2)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{i+1}^{R}(\mathsf{Tr}M,R)\rightarrow E^{*}\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\Omega^{-(i+1)}M,R).

As
\Omega^{-(i+1)}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\Omega^{-(i+1)}M,R)=0
. It follows from the exact sequence (
4.8
.2) that
E^{*}=0
.
Hence
E
has infinite grade, and
E=0
. Thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{>0}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)=0
, and
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in
\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.

Next, let us consider the case
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)\neq 0
. We claim that

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)\neq 0
. Indeed, suppose
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)=0
. Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)\neq 0
, we find a module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0
. By lemma
3.4
, we may assume
M
is locally free on the punctured spectrum. Setting
M_{0}=M
and

M_{i+1}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}M_{i},R)
for
i\geq 0
, we have an exact sequence

(
4.8
.3)

0\rightarrow M_{i+1}\rightarrow M_{i}\rightarrow\Omega\Omega^{-1}M_{i}\rightarrow 0.

Inductively, the second and third modules are in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, and so is the
first. We get a descending chain

\cdots\subseteq M_{3}\subseteq M_{2}\subseteq M_{1}

of modules in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Since all of these are of finite length, for some
n>0
we have
M_{n}=M_{n+1}
. This means that
\Omega\Omega^{-1}M_{n}=0
, and

M_{n}=R^{\oplus e}
for some
e\geq 0
since
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}(R)=0
. The exact sequence (
4.8
.3) induces the following exact sequence:

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\Omega^{-1}M_{i},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M_{i},R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M_{i+1},R),

and the first Ext module vanishes since
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)=0
. Since

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M_{0},R)\neq 0
, we inductively see that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M_{n},R)\neq 0
which is a contradiction, because
M_{n}
is a free
R
-module.

Thus we find a module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0
. Again, by Lemma
3.4
, we may assume

M
is locally free on the punctured spectrum. Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(M,R)
has
finite length, and it contains a nonzero socle element, which is
regarded as an exact sequence

0\rightarrow R\rightarrow Y\rightarrow\Omega M\rightarrow 0.

Since
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
,
Y
is also in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Dualizing this, we get an exact sequence

(
4.8
.4)

0\rightarrow k\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}\Omega M\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}Y\rightarrow 0.

By rotating the exact sequence (
4.8
.4), we get the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Omega\mathsf{Tr}\Omega M\rightarrow\Omega\mathsf{Tr}Y\rightarrow k\rightarrow 0.

Dualizing the above exact sequence, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow k^{\oplus r}\rightarrow(\Omega\mathsf{Tr}Y)^{*}\xrightarrow{a}(\Omega\mathsf{Tr}\Omega X)^{*}\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(k,R)\xrightarrow{b}\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}Y,R),

where
r=r(R)
, the type of
R
. The exact sequence (
4.8
.4) induces an exact sequence

0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}\Omega M,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(k,R)\xrightarrow{b}\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}Y,R),

whence the map
b
is injective. Therefore the map
a
is surjective, and
we obtain an exact sequence

0\rightarrow k^{\oplus r}\rightarrow(\Omega\mathsf{Tr}Y)^{*}\xrightarrow{a}(\Omega\mathsf{Tr}\Omega M)^{*}\rightarrow 0.

Since the second and third modules are in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, so is the first, and so is
k
.
Consequently, the proof of the theorem is completed in the case where
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R=0
.

Now we assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R>0
.
First we claim the following:

Claim I
. If
\Omega^{n-1}k\notin\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)=0
.

Proof of Claim. Assume contrarily that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}},R)\neq 0
.
Then, by Lemma
3.4
, there exists an
R
-module
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\cap\md_{0}(R)
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(M,R)\neq 0
.
Hence
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(M,R)
has finite length, and it contains a nonzero socle element, which is
regarded as an exact sequence

0\rightarrow R\rightarrow N\rightarrow\Omega^{n}M\rightarrow 0.

Since
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
,
N
is also in
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Dualizing this, we get an exact sequence

(
4.8
.5)

0\rightarrow k\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{n}M\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}N\rightarrow 0.

By rotating the exact sequence (
4.8
.5), we get the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Omega\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{n}M\rightarrow\Omega\mathsf{Tr}N\rightarrow k\rightarrow 0.

The above exact sequence induces the following exact sequence:

(
4.8
.6)

0\rightarrow\Omega^{n}\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{n}M\rightarrow\Omega^{n}\mathsf{Tr}N\rightarrow\Omega^{n-1}k\rightarrow 0.

The exact sequence (
4.8
.6) induces the following exact sequence.

(
4.8
.7)

(\Omega^{n}\mathsf{Tr}N)^{*}\xrightarrow{a}(\Omega^{n}\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{n}M)^{*}\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{n-1}k\xrightarrow{b}\Omega^{-n}N\rightarrow\Omega^{-n}\Omega^{n}M\rightarrow 0.

Dualizing the exact sequence (
4.8
.6), we have an exact sequence

(
4.8
.8)

(\Omega^{n}\mathsf{Tr}N)^{*}\xrightarrow{a}(\Omega^{n}\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{n}X)^{*}\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n}_{R}(k,R)\xrightarrow{c}\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}N,R).

The exact sequence (
4.8
.5) induces an exact sequence

(
4.8
.9)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{n}M,R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n}_{R}(k,R)\xrightarrow{c}\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{n+1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}N,R).

As
M
is locally free on the punctured spectrum and

\widetilde{S}_{n}
if
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\min\{n,\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R
. Here is an example of a resolving subcategory which is closed under
\Omega^{-n}
for some but not all
n>0
.

Example 4.17
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d>1
. For an integer
n
,
0<n<d
, set

\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\{M\in\md(R)\mid M\text{ satisfies }\widetilde{S}_{n}\}.
Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
and the following statements hold.

(i)

\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
.

(ii)

\Omega^{-m}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\nsubseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
for
1\leq m\leq n
.

(iii)

\Omega^{-i}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
for all
i\geq d
.

(iv)

\mathsf{radius}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}})=\infty
.

Proof.

First note that by
[
13
, Theorem 42]
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}=\Omega^{n}(\md(R))
.
It is easy to see that
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is a resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
. Note that
\Omega^{n}k\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
and so (i) is clear. For the proof of (ii), assume contrarily that

\Omega^{-m}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
for some
m
,
1\leq m\leq n
.
Then by Corollary
4.16
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
which is a contradiction. Hence,
\Omega^{-m}\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\nsubseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
for
1\leq m\leq n
.
As for (iii), as
R
is Gorenstein,
\Omega^{i}\mathsf{Tr}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
for all
M\in\md(R)
and
i\geq d
. Therefore, by Proposition
2.7
and part (i),
\Omega^{-i}M\cong\mathsf{Tr}\Omega^{i}\mathsf{Tr}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
.
Part (iv) follows from Corollary
4.14
and part (iii).
∎

4.8

should be compared with Corollary
4.4

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
R
be a Golod local ring of positive dimension and
let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be a non-trivial resolving subcategory of
\md(R)
of finite radius. Then
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under cosyzygies if and only if
R
is a hypersurface.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
be closed under cosyzygies. As
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is non-trivial, by Theorem
4.8
and
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
,
\add(R)\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
.
It follows from
[
4
, Example 3.5]
that
R
is hypersurface.

Now assume that
R
is a hypersurface. As
\mathsf{radius}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathcal{X}})<\infty
,
by
[
7
, Theorem I]
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathcal{G}}(R)
. Hence, by
[
8
, Theorem 6.1]
,
\Omega^{-1}M\cong\Omega^{2}\Omega^{-1}M\cong\Omega M
for all
M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
. Thus,
\operatorname{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under cosyzygies.
∎

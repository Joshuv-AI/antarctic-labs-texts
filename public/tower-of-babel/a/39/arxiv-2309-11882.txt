Almost splitting and quantitative stratification for super Ricci flow
Keita Kunikawa, Yohei Sakurai

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to study almost rigidity properties of super Ricci flow whose Müller quantity is non-negative. We conclude almost splitting and quantitative stratification theorems that have been established by Bamler [ 5 ] for Ricci flow. As a byproduct, we obtain an almost constancy for a certain integral quantity concerning scalar curvature at an almost selfsimilar point, which is new even for Ricci flow.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a compact manifold equipped with a time-dependent Riemannian metric.
Such a time-dependent manifold is called
Ricci flow
when

\partial_{t}g=-2\operatorname{Ric},

which has been introduced by Hamilton
[
21
]
.
It is well-known that
Perelman
[
35
]
has vastly developed the Ricci flow theory in three dimensional case,
and utilized it for the resolution of the Poincaré and geometrization conjectures.
A supersolution to the Ricci flow is called
super Ricci flow
;
namely,

(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
is called super Ricci flow when

\partial_{t}g\geq-2\operatorname{Ric},

which has been introduced by McCann-Topping
[
32
]
in view of the relation between Ricci flow and mass transport.
Super Ricci flow can be viewed as an interpolation between Ricci flow and (static) manifolds of non-negative Ricci curvature.
Actually,
it has been examined in the literature of the geometry of metric measure spaces with a lower Ricci curvature bound developed by Sturm
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
, Lott-Villani
[
31
]
, Ambrosio-Gigli-Savaré
[
1
]
, Gigli
[
19
]
and so on (see the work of Sturm
[
39
]
, and also subsequent works
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
).

One of the central problems in Ricci flow theory is to classify singularity models at the first singular time,
and it has been already resolved in low dimensional cases.
In two dimensional case,
the classification can be derived from classical works by Chow
[
14
]
and Hamilton
[
22
]
.
In three dimensional case,
it has been completed by Perelman
[
35
]
and Brendle
[
7
]
.
To deal with high dimensional singularity models,
which may be non-smooth (see
[
2
]
),
Bamler
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
has established a convergence theory for super Ricci flow within the framework of
metric flows
and their
\mathbb{F}
-convergence
.
Based on fundamental results in
[
3
]
,
he has proved a precompactness theorem for super Ricci flow in
[
4
]
.
Furthermore,
he has concluded regularity properties of non-collapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limits of Ricci flows in
[
5
]
,
where the non-collapsed condition has been described in terms of the Nash entropy.
The key ingredient was to study almost rigidity phenomena concerning the monotonicity of the Nash entropy,
which is similar to the structure theory of Gromov-Hausdorff limits of manifolds with a lower Ricci curvature bound due to Cheeger-Colding
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
, Colding-Naber
[
16
]
and so on.

1.2.
Aim and setting

We are now concerned with regularity properties of
\mathbb{F}
-limits of super Ricci flows (not necessarily Ricci flow).
In this article,
we aim to generalize almost splitting and quantitative stratification theorems obtained in
[
5
, Part 2]
for super Ricci flow whose Müller quantity is non-negative.
We recall that
for a vector field
V
,
the
Müller quantity
is defined as follows (see
[
33
, Definition 1.3]
):

\mathcal{D}(V):=\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}-\Delta\mathcal{H}-2|h|^{2}+4\operatorname{div}h(V)-2\langle\nabla\mathcal{H},V\rangle+2\operatorname{Ric}(V,V)-2h(V,V),

where

h:=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}g,\quad\mathcal{H}:=\operatorname{tr}h.

Notice that
this quantity vanishes for Ricci flow by the evolution formula for scalar curvature and the contracted second Bianchi identity.
The non-negativity of the Müller quantity brings various benefits such as a lower bound of the (generalized) scalar curvature, Harnack estimate and the monotonicity of entropies (see Lemmas
2.1
,
4.1
and Theorem
2.5
below).
There are several examples of super Ricci flow whose Müller quantity is non-negative (see
[
33
, Section 2]
,
[
17
, Section 7]
):

(1)

Ricci flow;

(2)

Ricci flow coupled with heat equation, called
List flow
(
[
30
]
);

(3)

Ricci flow coupled with harmonic map heat flow, called
Müller flow
(
[
34
]
);

(4)

mean curvature flow for spacelike hypersurfaces in Lorentzian manifold of non-negative sectional curvature;

(5)

(scaled) twisted Kähler-Ricci flow.

In
[
29
]
,
the authors have extended some results by Bamler-Zhang
[
6
]
concerning geometric analysis for Ricci flow under a scalar curvature bound to this object.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section,
we recall some basic notions.
In what follows,
let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be an
n
-dimensional compact manifold with a time-dependent metric.

2.1.
Convention and notation

For positive constants
a,b,c,d,\dots
,
we denote by
C_{a,b,c,d,\dots}
positive constants depending only on
a,b,c,d,\dots
and the dimension
n
without distinction,
where we stress that
the dependence on the dimension
n
are omitted.
When we specify constants,
we put numbers such as
C_{1,a,b,c,d,\dots},C_{2,a,b,c,d,\dots}
.
When we assert that “there exists
\overline{C}_{a,b,c,d,\dots}
such that if
C\leq\overline{C}_{a,b,c,d,\dots}
”,
we abbreviate “if
C\leq\overline{C}_{a,b,c,d,\dots}
” for short.
In the same manner,
for a lower bound,
we say “if
C\geq\underline{C}_{a,b,c,d,\dots}
” instead of “if
C\leq\overline{C}_{a,b,c,d,\dots}
”.

Let
d_{t}
and
m_{t}
be the Riemannian distance and Riemannian volume measure with respect to
g_{t}
,
respectively.
For
x\in M,t\in I
and
r>0
,
let
B(x,t,r)
denote the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
with respect to
g_{t}
.
Note that
\partial_{t}(dm_{t})=-\mathcal{H}\,dm_{t}
.
The non-negativity of the Müller quantity (i.e.,
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
) is expressed by
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.

At the begging of the proof of each statement,
we carry out the parallel translation for time and the parabolic rescaling.
Recall that
for
r>0
,
the parabolic rescaling (at time
0
) is given by
\bar{g}_{s}:=r^{-2}\,g_{r^{2}s}=r^{-2}g_{t}
.
Note that
the super Ricci flow and the non-negativity of the Müller quantity are preserved under this rescaling (cf.
[
29
, Remark 2.2]
).

We notice the following lower bound for
\mathcal{H}
,
which is a direct consequence of the maximum principle (see
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
):

Lemma 2.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
If
\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t_{0})\geq-A
for
A>0
, then for all
t\in[t_{0},\infty)\cap I
,

\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\frac{A}{(n/2)+A(t-t_{0})}.

Remark 2.2
.

The following also holds (see
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
):
Assume
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
If
t_{0}\in I
,
then for all
t\in[t_{0},\infty)\cap I
, we have

\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t)\geq-\frac{n}{2(t-t_{0})},\quad\min_{M}\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t)\geq\min_{M}\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t_{0}).

2.2.
Heat kernels

The
heat operator
and the
conjugate heat operator
are defined by

\square:=\partial_{t}-\Delta,\quad\square^{*}:=-\partial_{t}-\Delta+\mathcal{H}.

We recall the following Duhamel principle (see e.g.,
[
15
]
):
For any
u,v\in C^{\infty}(M\times I)
,

(2.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\,uv\,dm_{t}=\int_{M}\,(\square u)v\,dm_{t}-\int_{M}\,u(\square^{*}v)\,dm_{t}.

For
x,y\in M
and
s,t\in I
with
s<t
,
we denote by
G(x,t;y,s)
the
heat kernel
(see e.g.,
[
15
]
,
[
19
]
);
namely,
for a fixed
(y,s)\in M\times I
,
it solves

(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x})G(\cdot,\cdot;y,s)=0,\quad\lim_{t\searrow s}G(\cdot,t;y,s)=\delta_{y}.

Notice that

G(x,t;\cdot,\cdot)
is the fundamental solution to the conjugate heat equation;
namely,
for any
(x,t)\in M\times I
,

(-\partial_{s}-\Delta_{y}+\mathcal{H})G(x,t;\cdot,\cdot)=0,\quad\lim_{s\nearrow t}G(x,t;\cdot,s)=\delta_{x}.

The reproduction formula (or semigroup property)

(2.2)

G(x,t;y,s)=\int_{M}\,G(x,t;\cdot,\sigma)G(\cdot,\sigma;y,s)\,dm_{\sigma}

holds for any
\sigma\in(s,t)
.

For
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
,
the
conjugate heat kernel measure

\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0})}=(\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t})_{t\in(-\infty,t_{0}]\cap I}
is defined by

\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}:=G(x_{0},t_{0};\cdot,t)\,m_{t},\quad\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}}:=\delta_{x_{0}}

for
t<t_{0}
,
which are probability measures.

Remark 2.3
.

We frequently set
\nu:=\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0})}
or
\nu^{0}:=\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0})}
.
In that case,
without mentioning,
we use the notation
\nu_{t}:=\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}
or
\nu^{0}_{t}:=\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}
,
respectively.

We define
\tau:=t_{0}-t
, which is called the
parameter
.
The
density

f\in C^{\infty}(M\times((-\infty,t_{0})\cap I))
is determined by

G(x_{0},t_{0};\cdot,t)=(4\pi\tau)^{-n/2}e^{-f(\cdot,t)},

which can be written as

(2.3)

f(x,t)=-\log G(x_{0},t_{0};x,t)-\frac{n}{2}\log\tau-\frac{n}{2}\log 4\pi.

The density enjoys

(2.4)

-\partial_{t}f=\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+\mathcal{H}-\frac{n}{2\tau}.

The following can be derived from (
2.1
) and integration by parts (cf.
[
5
, Lemma 4.4]
):

(2.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\,u\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}=\int_{M}\,\square u\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t},\quad\int_{M}\,\operatorname{div}(V)\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}=\int_{M}\,\langle V,\nabla f\rangle\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}

for all
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times I)
and vector fields
V
.

Remark 2.4
.

We do not always introduce the notations of the parameter and the density when it is clear from the context.

We define

(2.6)

\Psi:=h+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{2\tau}g,\quad w:=\tau(2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+\mathcal{H})+f-n.

We possess the following Perelman type Harnack estimate (see
[
35
]
,
[
8
, Theorems 1.1, 1.2]
):

Theorem 2.5

(
[
35
]
,
[
8
]
)
.

We set
u:=G(x_{0},t_{0};\cdot,\cdot)
.
Then we have

\square^{*}(wu)=-2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}u-\tau\,u\,\mathcal{D}(\nabla f).

Moreover,
if
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
,
then
w\leq 0
.

2.3.
Wasserstein distance

Let
(X,d)
be a complete separable metric space,
and let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
be two Borel probability measures.
We denote by
W_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
the
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance
between them.
The canonical formulation of the Wasserstein distance is based on a coupling method.
We would rather make use of the following characterization called the
Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality
(see e.g.,
[
40
, Theorem 5.10]
):

(2.7)

W_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\sup_{\phi}\bigg(\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu_{2}-\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu_{1}\bigg),

where the supremum is taken over all bounded
1
-Lipschitz functions
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
.
We also denote by
\operatorname{Var}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
the
variance
defined by

\operatorname{Var}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\int_{X}\int_{X}d^{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\,d\mu_{1}(x_{1})d\mu_{2}(x_{2}).

We recall the following (see e.g.,
[
4
, Lemma 2.8]
):

Lemma 2.6

(
[
4
]
)
.

For any two Borel probability measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
on
(X,d)
,
we have

W_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}.

3.
Super Ricci flow

In this section,
we summarize several facts on super Ricci flow.

3.1.
Wasserstein monotonicity and concentration bounds

We first recall the following monotonicity property for the Wasserstein distance (see
[
3
, Lemma 2.7]
):

Proposition 3.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0}),(x_{1},t_{1})\in M\times I
.
Then
W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t},\nu_{(x_{1},t_{1});t})
is non-decreasing in
t\in(-\infty,\min\{t_{0},t_{1}\}]\cap I
.

For
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
,
a point
(z,t)\in M\times((-\infty,t_{0}]\cap I)
is said to be a
(
H_{n}
-
)
center
if

H_{n}:=\frac{(n-1)\pi^{2}}{2}+4,\quad\operatorname{Var}(\delta_{z},\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t})\leq H_{n}(t_{0}-t).

For the existence of centers,
we have the following (see
[
3
, Proposition 3.12]
):

Proposition 3.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
Then for every
t\in(-\infty,t_{0}]\cap I
,
there is
z\in M
such that
(z,t)
is a center of
(x_{0},t_{0})
.

We possess the following concentration bounds (see
[
3
, Proposition 3.13, Theorem 3.14]
):

Lemma 3.3

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
,
and let
(z,t)
be a center of
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Then for every
R>1
we have

\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}\left(B(z,t,\sqrt{RH_{n}(t_{0}-t)})\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{R}.

Remark 3.4
.

We also use Lemma
3.3
in the following form:
For all
r>0
we have

(3.1)

\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}\left(M\setminus B(z,t,r)\right)\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t},\delta_{z})}{r^{2}}\leq\frac{H_{n}(t_{0}-t)}{r^{2}}.

Proposition 3.5

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
,
and let
(z,t)
be a center of
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Then for all
r>0
we have

\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}\left(M\setminus B(z,t,r)\right)\leq 2\exp\left({-\frac{\left(r-\sqrt{2H_{n}(t_{0}-t)}\right)_{+}^{2}}{8(t_{0}-t)}}\right).

3.2.
Analytic bounds

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
be a function defined by

(3.2)

\Phi(x):=\int^{x}_{-\infty}\,(4\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-y^{2}/4}\,dy.

For
t>0
,
let
\Phi_{t}:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
stand for a function defined by
\Phi_{t}(x):=\Phi(t^{-1/2}x)
,
which is the solution to the one dimensional heat equation such that its initial condition is the Heaviside function.
We have the following sharp gradient estimate (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
):

Theorem 3.6

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow.
For
[t_{0},t_{1}]\subset I
,
let
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times[t_{0},t_{1}])
be a solution to the heat equation.
Assume that

u
only takes values in
(0,1)
,
and
|\nabla(\Phi_{T}^{-1}(u(\cdot,t_{0})))|\leq 1
for
T>0
.
Then
|\nabla(\Phi^{-1}_{t-t_{0}+T}(u(\cdot,t)))|\leq 1
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
.

Remark 3.7
.

Due to Theorem
3.6
,
every super Ricci flow can be regarded as a metric flow in the sense of
[
4
, Definition 3.1]
.
Note that
Proposition
3.1
holds in the framework of metric flows (see
[

).
Furthermore,
every super Ricci flow satisfies a certain concentration property (see
[

In particular,
it can be regarded as an

-concentrated metric flow in the sense of

,
which is a crucial object in the compactness theory of metric flows (see
[

, Theorem 7.4, Corollary 7.5]

Proposition

immediately follows from the

-concentration property (see
[

, Proposition 3.25]

We next present the following heat kernel estimates (see
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
):

Proposition 3.8

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow.
Let

and

.
Then for every

we have

(3.3)

(t-s)^{p/2}\int_{M}\left(\frac{|\nabla_{x}G(x,t;\cdot,s)|}{G(x,t;\cdot,s)}\right)^{p}d\nu_{(x,t);s}\leq C_{p};

moreover,
for every Borel subset

we have

(3.4)

(t-s)^{p/2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\nabla_{x}G(x,t;\cdot,s)|}{G(x,t;\cdot,s)}\right)^{p}d\nu_{(x,t);s}\leq C_{p}\,\nu_{(x,t);s}(\Omega)(-\log(\nu_{(x,t);s}(\Omega)/2))^{p/2}.

Also,
for every
v\in T_{x}M
with
|v|_{t}=1
,

(3.5)

(t-s)\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial_{v}G(x,t;\cdot,s)}{G(x,t;\cdot,s)}\right)^{2}d\nu_{(x,t);s}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Remark 3.9
.

In (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), one can choose
C_{2}=n/2
.

We have the following Poincaré inequality (see
[

, Theorem 1.5]
,
[

, Theorem 11.1]
, and also

, Theorem 1.10]
):

Proposition 3.10

(
[

]
,
[
3
]
)
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
Let
\tau>0
satisfy
[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]\subset I
.
Then for any
u\in C^{1}(M)
with
\int_{M}u\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}=0
and

,

\int_{M}|u|^{p}d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}\leq C_{p}\,\tau^{p/2}\,\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}.

Proof.

Bamler
[
3
]
has stated this assertion only for Ricci flow,
but the argument also works for super Ricci flow without any changes.
For
p=2
,
the desired inequality has been obtained by Haslhofer-Naber
[
23
]
(see
[
23
, Theorem 1.5]
).
For
p=1
,
by noticing the following point,
one can prove the desired one along the lines of the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 11.1]
:
If
\square u=0
,
then the Bochner formula and the Kato inequality imply

\square|\nabla u|^{2}=2h(\nabla u,\nabla u)-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\leq-\frac{|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}}{2|\nabla u|^{2}};

in particular,

\square|\nabla u|\leq 0
.
For general
p
,
the desired one follows from the assertion for
p=1
together with the same argument of the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 11.1]
.

4.
Nash entropy

In this section,
we examine basic properties of the Nash entropy.

4.1.
Entropy monotonicity

Fix a base point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
\tau>0
with
[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]\subset I
,
the
pointed Nash-entropy
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau):=\int_{M}\,f\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}-\frac{n}{2}.

We set
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(0):=0
such that
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)
is continuous in
\tau
(cf.
[
3
, Proposition 5.2]
,
[
20
]
).
The
pointed
\mathcal{W}
-entropy
is defined by

\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau):=\int_{M}\left(\tau(|\nabla f|^{2}+\mathcal{H})+f-n\right)d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}.

We recall the following monotonicity properties (cf.
[
3
, Proposition 5.2]
,
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
20
, Theorem 5.2]
,
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
):

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
If
\tau>0
with
[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]\subset I
, then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\tau\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\right)=\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\leq 0,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\left(\tau\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\right)=-\tau\int_{M}\left(2\left|\Psi\right|^{2}+\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)\right)d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}\leq 0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\leq 0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\leq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau),

where
\Psi
is defined as (
2.6
).

Proof.

The formulas (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) are well-known (see e.g.,
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
20
, Theorem 5.2]
,
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
).
Furthermore,
(
4.3
) and (
4.4
) can be derived from the same calculation as in the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 5.2]
together with (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).

\Box

We also see the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}>0
satisfy
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
and
[t_{0}-\tau_{2},t_{0}]\subset I
.
For
A>0
,
we assume
\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau_{2})\geq-A
.
Then we have

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{1})-\left((\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})A+\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}\right)\right)\leq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{2}).

Proof.

From (
4.1
) we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\tau\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\right)=\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tau\left(|\nabla f|^{2}+\mathcal{H}\right)d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}+\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)-\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\tau A+\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)-\frac{n}{2},

and hence

\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\geq-\left(A+\frac{n}{2\tau}\right).

This implies

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{2})-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{1})\geq-\int^{\tau_{2}}_{\tau_{1}}\,\left(A+\frac{n}{2\tau}\right)\,d\tau=-\left((\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})A+\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}\right)\right).

We complete the proof.

\Box

Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
3.10
yield the following (cf.
[
3
, Proposition 5.13]
):

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
A>0
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\tau(|\nabla f|^{2}+\mathcal{H})d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}\leq\frac{n}{2},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(f-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}\leq n+2A\tau.

Proof.

By the same calculation as in the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 5.13]
,
and by (
4.1
), (
4.3
),

\displaystyle\int_{M}\tau(|\nabla f|^{2}+\mathcal{H})d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}+\tau\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)\leq\frac{n}{2},

which proves (
4.5
).
Proposition
3.10
with
p=2
together with

\int_{M}\left(f-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)-\frac{n}{2}\right)d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}=0

and

2\tau\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}\leq n-2\tau\int_{M}\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-\tau}\leq n+2A\tau

leads us to (
4.6

We arrive at the desired estimates.

4.2.
Derivative estimates

For a fixed
s\in I
,
we define

\mathcal{N}_{s}(x,t):=\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(t-s)

on
M\times((s,\infty)\cap I)
.
We have the following derivative estimates (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 5.9]
):

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
s\in I
.
For
A>0
,
we assume
\mathcal{H}(\cdot,s)\geq-A
.
Then on
M\times((s,\infty)\cap I)
we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathcal{N}_{s}|\leq\left(\frac{n}{2(t-s)}+A\right)^{1/2},

(4.8)

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{2(t-s)}\leq\square\mathcal{N}_{s}\leq 0.

Proof.

We may assume
s=0
.
Fix a base point
(x,t)\in M\times((0,\infty)\cap I)
.
For
v\in T_{x}M
with
|v|_{t}=1
,
the same calculation as in the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 5.9]
tells us that

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\partial_{v}\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial_{v}G(x,t;y,0)}{G(x,t;y,0)}\right)\left(f(y,0)-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,t)-\frac{n}{2}\right)d\nu_{(x,t);0}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial_{v}G(x,t;y,0)}{G(x,t;y,0)}\right)^{2}d\nu_{(x,t);0}(y)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{M}\left(f-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,t)-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}d\nu_{(x,t);0}(y)\right)^{1/2};

in particular,
(
3.5
) and (
4.6
) imply

|\nabla\mathcal{N}_{0}|^{2}(x,t)\leq\frac{1}{2t}(n+2At),

which is the desired gradient estimate (
4.7
) (see also Remark
2.2
).
Similarly,
we possess

\displaystyle\square\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,t)=\int_{M}\left(\frac{|\nabla_{x}G(x,t;y,0)|}{G(x,t;y,0)}\right)^{2}d\nu_{(x,t);0}(y)-\frac{n}{2t},

and (
3.3
) with
p=2
leads us to (
4.8
) (see also Remark
3.9
).

We conclude the following (cf.
[
3
, Corollary 5.11]
):

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
For a fixed
s\in I
,
let
(x_{1},t_{1}),(x_{2},t_{2})\in M\times((s,\infty)\cap I)
.
For
A>0
,
we assume
\mathcal{H}(\cdot,s)\geq-A
.
Then for every
t\in(s,\min\{t_{1},t_{2}\}]
we have

\mathcal{N}_{s}(x_{1},t_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{s}(x_{2},t_{2})\leq\left(\frac{n}{2(t-s)}+A\right)^{1/2}W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{1},t_{1});t},\nu_{(x_{2},t_{2});t})+\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{t_{2}-s}{t-s}\right).

We may assume
s=0
.
For

, we set
\nu^{i}:=\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i})}
.
In virtue of (
4.8
),
we see

(4.9)

\mathcal{N}_{0}(x_{i},t_{i})\leq\int_{M}\mathcal{N}_{0}(\cdot,t)d\nu^{i}_{t}\leq\mathcal{N}_{0}(x_{i},t_{i})+\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{t_{i}}{t}\right).

On the other hand,
due to (

) and (

),

(4.10)

\left|\int_{M}\mathcal{N}_{0}(\cdot,t)d\nu^{2}_{t}-\int_{M}\mathcal{N}_{0}(\cdot,t)d\nu^{1}_{t}\right|\leq\left(\frac{n}{2t}+A\right)^{1/2}W_{1}(\nu^{1}_{t},\nu^{2}_{t}).

The desired estimate follows from combining (
4.9
) and (
4.10
).

Lemma 4.6

,

in the following form:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

.

,

5.
Volume and heat kernel estimates

In this section,
we derive several volume and heat kernel estimates.

5.1.
Lower volume estimates

We begin with the following (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 6.2]
):

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
r>0
,
we assume
[t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
.
For
A>0
,
we assume
\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t_{0}-r^{2})\geq-A\,r^{-2}
.
Let
(z,t_{0}-r^{2})\in M\times I
be a center of
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Then we have

m_{t_{0}-r^{2}}\left(B(z,t_{0}-r^{2},\sqrt{2H_{n}}r)\right)\geq C_{A}\,\exp(\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2}))r^{n}.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=1
and
r=1
.
Let
\nu:=\nu_{(x_{0},1)}
.
By virtue of Lemma
3.3
,

(5.1)

\nu_{0}(B)\geq\frac{1}{2},

where
B:=B(z,0,\sqrt{2H_{n}})
.
Lemma
4.3
tells us that

(5.2)

\int_{M}\left|f-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},1)}(1)-\frac{n}{2}\right|d\nu_{0}\leq\left(\int_{M}\left(f-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},1)}(1)-\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}d\nu_{0}\right)^{1/2}\leq(n+2A)^{1/2}.

Combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
),
we conclude

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{0}(B)}\int_{B}f\,d\nu_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},1)}(1)+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{\nu_{0}(B)}\int_{B}\left|f-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},1)}(1)-\frac{n}{2}\right|d\nu_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},1)}(1)+\frac{n}{2}-2(n+2A)^{1/2}.

We set
u:=(4\pi)^{n/2}e^{-f}/\nu_{0}(B)
.
Since
\int_{B}u\,dm_{0}=1
,
(
5.1
) tells us that

\displaystyle\int_{B}u\log u\,dm_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\nu_{0}(B)}\int_{B}f\,d\nu_{0}-\log\nu_{0}(B)+\frac{n}{2}\log 4\pi

\displaystyle\leq-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},1)}(1)-\frac{n}{2}+2(n+2A)^{1/2}+\log 2+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi).

By the Jensen inequality,

\log\left(\frac{1}{m_{0}(B)}\int_{B}u\,dm_{0}\right)\frac{1}{m_{0}(B)}\int_{B}u\,dm_{0}\leq\frac{1}{m_{0}(B)}\int_{B}u\log u\,dm_{0},

and thus

-\log m_{0}(B)\leq\int_{B}u\log u\,dm_{0}\leq-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},1)}(1)+C+2(n+2A)^{1/2}.

We complete the proof.

\Box

5.2.
Heat kernel estimates

We next show the following (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 7.1]
):

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
,
and let
[t,t_{0}]\subset I
.
For
A>0
,
we assume
\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t)\geq-A(t_{0}-t)^{-1}
.
Then on
M
,
we have

G(x_{0},t_{0};\cdot,t)\leq\frac{C_{A}}{(t_{0}-t)^{n/2}}\exp(-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(t_{0}-t)).

Proof.

We may assume
t=0
and
t_{0}=1
.
For a fixed
y\in M
,
we set
u:=G(\cdot,\cdot;y,0)
.
By the same argument as in the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 7.1]
,
it suffices to show the following:
If
L\geq\underline{L}_{A}
,
and if for all
(x,s)\in M\times(0,1]
we have

(5.3)

u(x,s)\leq\frac{L}{s^{n/2}}\exp(-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)),

then for all
x\in M
we have

(5.4)

u(x,1)\leq\frac{L}{2}\exp(-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,1)).

First,
assuming (
5.3
),
we derive a gradient bound

(5.5)

|\nabla u|(x,s)\leq C_{A}L\exp\left({-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)}\right)

for all
(x,s)\in M\times[3/4,1]
.
We set

v:=\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}.

Due to the Bochner formula,

\square v=-|\nabla u|^{2}-2\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-2\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\operatorname{Ric}-h)(\nabla u,\nabla u)\leq 0

for every
s\in[1/2,1]
.
Using (
5.3
),
we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)|\nabla u|^{2}(x,s)\leq v(x,s)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}v(\cdot,1/2)d\nu_{(x,s);1/2}=\int_{M}u^{2}(\cdot,1/2)d\nu_{(x,s);1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}L^{2}\int_{M}\exp\left({-2\mathcal{N}_{0}(\cdot,1/2)}\right)d\nu_{(x,s);1/2}

for every
(x,s)\in M\times(1/2,1]
.
Let
(z,1/2)
be a center of
(x,s)
(see Proposition
3.2
).
In view of Lemma
4.6
,
we have

(5.7)

-\mathcal{N}_{0}(\cdot,1/2)\leq-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)+C_{A}\,(d_{1/2}(z,\cdot)+1)

on
M
.
From (
5.7
), the co-area formula and Proposition
3.5
,
we derive

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\int_{M}\exp\left({-2\mathcal{N}_{0}(\cdot,1/2)}\right)d\nu_{(x,s);1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\exp({-2\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)})\int_{M}\exp(C_{A}d_{1/2}(z,\cdot))d\nu_{(x,s);1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\exp({-2\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)})\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{C_{A}r}\,\nu_{(x,s);1/2}(M\setminus B(z,1/2,r))\,dr+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\exp({-2\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)})\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{C_{A}r}\exp\left({-\frac{\left(r-\sqrt{2H_{n}(t-1/2)}\right)_{+}^{2}}{8(t-1/2)}}\right)dr+1\right)

\displaystyle=C_{A}\exp({-2\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)})\left(\sqrt{t-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{C_{A}\sqrt{t-1/2}\,r}\exp\left({-\frac{\left(r-\sqrt{2H_{n}}\right)_{+}^{2}}{8}}\right)dr+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\exp({-2\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,s)}).

Combining this with (
5.6
) implies (
5.5
).

Let
(x,1)\in M\times I
,
and set
\nu:=\nu_{(x,1)}
.
For
\xi\in(0,1/2]
,
we put
t_{1}:=1-\xi^{2}
,
and let
(z_{1},t_{1})
be a center of
(x,1)
.
We set
B:=B(z_{1},t_{1},\sqrt{100H_{n}\xi^{2}})
.
By Lemma
4.6
,
if
\xi\leq\overline{\xi}_{A}
, then

(5.8)

-\mathcal{N}_{0}(\cdot,t_{1})\leq-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,1)+C_{A}d_{t_{1}}(z_{1},\cdot)+C_{A}\sqrt{H_{n}\xi^{2}}\leq-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,1)+C_{A}+\log 2

on
B
;
in particular,
(
5.5
) tells us that
on
B
,

(5.9)

|\nabla u|(\cdot,t_{1})\leq C_{A}L\exp(-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,1)).

Now,
we write

(5.10)

u(x,1)=\int_{B}u\,d\nu_{t_{1}}+\int_{M\setminus B}u\,d\nu_{t_{1}}

with the help of (
2.2
).
We first estimate the first term in (
5.10
).
Since

\frac{d}{ds}\int_{M}u\,dm_{s}=-\int_{M}\mathcal{H}u\,dm_{s}\leq A\int_{M}u\,dm_{s}

for all
s\in(0,1]
, we possess

(5.11)

\int_{M}u\,dm_{t_{1}}\leq e^{A}.

By Proposition
5.1
and (
4.3
) we have

(5.12)

m_{t_{1}}(B)\geq C_{A}\exp(\mathcal{N}_{(x,1)}(\xi^{2}))\xi^{n}\geq C_{A}\exp(\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,1))\xi^{n}.

In virtue of (
5.9
),
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in B
we have

(5.13)

u(x_{1},t_{1})\leq u(x_{2},t_{1})+C_{A}L\exp(-\mathcal{N}_{0}(x,1))\xi.

Integrating (
5.13
) over
B
with respect to
x_{2}
,
and using (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) imply

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})\geq-\kappa
.
Then we have

\int_{t_{0}-\alpha^{-1}r^{2}}^{t_{0}-\alpha r^{2}}\int_{M}\left(\tau(|h|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4})+\tau^{-1}f^{2}\right)e^{2\theta f}d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}dt\leq C_{\kappa,\alpha,A}.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=0
and
r=1
.
Set
\nu:=\nu_{(x_{0},0)}
and
u:=G(x_{0},0;\cdot,\cdot)
.
We define
\Psi
and
w
as (
2.6
).
In virtue of (
2.1
), (
2.4
) and Theorem
2.5
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}we^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}=\int_{M}\left\{(\square e^{\theta f})wu-e^{\theta f}\square^{*}(wu)\right\}dm_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}+\tau\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)-\theta\left\{\tau^{-1}\left(w-f+\frac{n}{2}\right)+\theta|\nabla f|^{2}\right\}w\right)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\left(2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}-\theta\tau^{-1}\left(w-f+\frac{n}{2}\right)w\right)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-\theta\tau^{-1}\int_{M}\left|w-f+\frac{n}{2}\right||w|e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\tau^{-1}\int_{M}(w^{2}+f^{2}+1)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\tau^{-1}\int_{M}(\tau^{2}((\Delta f)^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4}+H^{2})+f^{2}+1)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\tau^{-1}\int_{M}(\tau^{2}(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4}+|h|^{2})+f^{2}+1)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}.

Also, if
\theta\in[0,1]
,
then (
2.5
) and (
2.4
) yield

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}\,e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}=\int_{M}\square(\tau\mathcal{H}e^{\theta f})\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(2\tau|h|^{2}+\tau\mathcal{D}(0)-\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-\theta\int_{M}\mathcal{H}\left(w-f+\frac{n}{2}\right)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\theta(\theta-2)\tau\int_{M}\mathcal{H}|\nabla f|^{2}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}+2\theta\tau\int_{M}\mathcal{H}\Delta fe^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}(2\tau|h|^{2}-\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\int_{M}\left\{\tau\mathcal{H}^{2}+\tau^{-1}\left(w^{2}+f^{2}+1\right)\right\}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-C|\theta-2|\theta\tau\int_{M}(H^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\tau\int_{M}(\mathcal{H}^{2}+(\Delta f)^{2})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}(2\tau|h|^{2}-\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\int_{M}\left\{\tau\mathcal{H}^{2}+\tau^{-1}\left(w^{2}+f^{2}+1\right)\right\}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-C|\theta-2|\theta\tau\int_{M}(\mathcal{H}^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\tau\int_{M}(\mathcal{H}^{2}+(\Delta f)^{2})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}(2\tau|h|^{2}-\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\tau^{-1}\int_{M}(\tau^{2}((\Delta f)^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4}+H^{2})+f^{2}+1)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}(2\tau|h|^{2}-\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C\theta\tau^{-1}\int_{M}(\tau^{2}(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4}+|h|^{2})+f^{2}+1)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}.

Therefore,
if
\theta\in[0,1]
,
then

(6.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}(w+\tau\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\left(2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}+2\tau|h|^{2}-\mathcal{H}\right)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-C\theta\tau^{-1}\int_{M}(\tau^{2}(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4}+|h|^{2})+f^{2}+1)e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}=|\Psi-h|^{2}\leq 4|\Psi|^{2}+2|h|^{2},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|\leq\sqrt{n}|h|\leq\frac{\tau}{2}|h|^{2}+\frac{n}{2\tau},

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}\leq 2\left|\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}+\frac{n}{2\tau^{2}};

in particular,

(6.10)

\displaystyle 2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}+2\tau|h|^{2}-\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\geq 2\tau\left(\frac{1}{4}\left|\nabla^{2}f-\frac{n}{2\tau}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|h|^{2}\right)+2\tau|h|^{2}-\left(\frac{\tau}{2}|h|^{2}+\frac{n}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\tau\left(\frac{1}{8}|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|h|^{2}-\frac{n}{16\tau^{2}}\right)+2\tau|h|^{2}-\left(\frac{\tau}{2}|h|^{2}+\frac{n}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{4}|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}|h|^{2}-\frac{5n}{8\tau}.

Combining (
6.9
), (
6.10
) and Lemmas
6.2
,
6.4
yields that for
\theta\in[0,1/4]
,

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}(w+\tau\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\left\{\tau\left(\frac{1}{4}-C\theta\right)|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+\tau\left(\frac{1}{2}-C\theta\right)|h|^{2}-\tau^{-1}\left(\frac{5n}{8}+C_{\kappa,\alpha,A}\theta\right)\right\}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}.

In particular,
Lemma
6.3
implies that for
\theta\in[0,\overline{\theta}]
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}(w+\tau\mathcal{H})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau}{8}\int_{M}(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|h|^{2})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C_{\kappa,\alpha,A}\tau^{-1}\int_{M}e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau}{8}\int_{M}(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|h|^{2})e^{\theta f}d\nu_{t}-C_{\kappa,\alpha,A}\tau^{-1}e^{(n+\tau\,A)\theta}.

Once we obtain this estimate,
we can prove the desired one by the same cutoff argument on time as in the proof of
[
5

together with Lemmas
6.2
and
6.4
.

7.
Almost selfsimilar points

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1),r>0
,
we say that
a point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
is
(\varepsilon,r)
-selfsimilar
if we have:

(1)

[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
;

(2)

we have

(7.1)

\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2}}^{t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}}\int_{M}\tau\left(\left|h+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)\right)d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}dt\leq\varepsilon;

(3)

for all
t\in[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}]
, we have

(7.2)

\int_{M}\left|\tau(2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+\mathcal{H})+f-n-\mathrm{N}\right|d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}\leq\varepsilon

for
\mathrm{N}:=\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})
;

(4)

on
M\times[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}]
, we have

(7.3)

\mathcal{H}\geq-\varepsilon r^{-2}.

In this section,
we investigate various properties of almost selfsimilar points.

Remark 7.1
.

We discuss the validity of the formulation of the almost selfsimilarity by taking Ricci flow coupled with harmonic map heat flow as an example.
Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I},(\varphi_{t})_{t\in I})
be a compact manifold equipped with time-dependent Riemannian metric and map from
M
to another Riemannian manifold.
Such a time-dependent manifold is called
Ricci flow coupled with harmonic map heat flow
or
Müller flow
when

(7.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g=-2\operatorname{Ric}+4d\varphi\otimes d\varphi,\\
\partial_{t}\varphi=\mathsf{t}(\varphi),\end{cases}

which has been introduced by Müller
[
34
]
.
Here
\mathsf{t}(\varphi)
is the tension field of
\varphi
.
In the special case where
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in I}
is function,
(
7.4
) is called
Ricci flow coupled with heat equation
or
List flow
(
[
30
]
).
For this flow,
we have the following (see e.g.,
[
33
, Section 2]
):

(7.5)

h=\operatorname{Ric}-2d\varphi\otimes d\varphi,\quad\mathcal{D}(V)=4\left|\mathsf{t}(\varphi)-d\varphi(V)\right|^{2}.

Also,
the soliton identity on
(M,g,\varphi)
, which characterizes selfsimilar solutions of (
7.4
), is given as follows (see
[
34
, Lemma 2.2]
):

(7.6)

\begin{cases}(\operatorname{Ric}-2d\varphi\otimes d\varphi)+\nabla^{2}f=Kg,\\
\mathsf{t}(\varphi)-d\varphi(\nabla f)=0\end{cases}

for some
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
.
The first equation in (
7.6
) corresponds to the selfsimilarity of metric
(g_{t})_{t\in I}
,
and the second one does that of map
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in I}
.
In view of (
7.5
) and (
7.6
),
for the flow (
7.4
),
the first term of the integrand in (
7.1
) expresses the almost selfsimilarity of metric,
and the second term does that of map (see also
[
34
, (2.5)–(2.9)]
,
[
5
, Subsection 4.7]
for (
7.2
)).

7.1.
Characterization

In this subsection,
we prove that
the almost selfsimilarity can be characterized by the almost constancy of the Nash entropy.
We first prepare the following lemma (cf.
[
5
, Lemma 7.10]
):

Lemma 7.2
.

For
\kappa>0,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
if
\zeta\leq\overline{\zeta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}
, then the following holds:
Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
r>0
,
we assume
[t_{0}-\zeta^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
.
We assume
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})\geq-\kappa
.
If

(7.7)

|\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau)-\mathrm{N}|\leq\zeta

for all
\tau\in[\zeta r^{2},\zeta^{-1}r^{2}]
,
then

\int_{M}\left|w-\mathrm{N}\right|d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}\leq\varepsilon

for all
t\in[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2}]
, where
w
is defined as (
2.6
) and
\mathrm{N}:=\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})
.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=0
and
r=1
.
We set
\nu:=\nu_{(x_{0},0)}
and
u:=G(x_{0},0;\cdot,\cdot)
.
Let
v\in C^{\infty}(M\times[-\zeta^{-1},0])
be a solution to the heat equation with
|v(\cdot,-\zeta^{-1})|\leq 1
.
In view of the maximum principle,
we see
|v|\leq 1
on
M\times[-\zeta^{-1},0]
.
Due to (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.5
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})u\,dm_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}v\square^{*}(wu)dm_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\square^{*}(wu)dm_{t}=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}wu\,dm_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(|t|).

From (
7.7
),
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})\,d\nu_{-\tau_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})\,d\nu_{-\tau_{2}}-\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau_{2})+\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})d\nu_{-\tau_{2}}+2\zeta

for every
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in[\zeta,\zeta^{-1}]
,
and hence

(7.8)

\left|\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})\,d\nu_{-\tau_{1}}-\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})\,d\nu_{-\tau_{2}}\right|\leq 2\zeta.

From Lemma
6.2
and Proposition
6.5
,
we deduce

\int_{-2\zeta}^{-\zeta}\int_{M}\left(\tau^{2}(|h|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4})+f^{2}\right)d\nu_{t}dt\leq C_{\kappa}\zeta;

in particular,
there exists
\tau_{0}\in[\zeta,2\zeta]
such that

\int_{M}\left(\tau_{0}^{2}(|h|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4})+f^{2}\right)d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\leq C_{\kappa}.

It follows that

(7.9)

\int_{M}(w-\mathrm{N})^{2}d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\leq C_{\kappa}+\int_{M}\left(\tau_{0}^{2}(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4}+\mathcal{H}^{2})+f^{2}\right)d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\leq C_{\kappa}.

Thanks to Proposition
3.10
with
p=2
and Theorem
3.6
,
we also possess

(7.10)

\int_{M}|v-a|^{2}\,d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\leq 2\tau_{0}\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\leq C\tau_{0}\leq C\zeta,

where
a\in[-1,1]
is defined as
a:=\int_{M}v\,d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}
.
Therefore, (
7.10
) and (
7.9
) lead us to

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|a\int_{M}(w-\mathrm{N})d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\right|+\int_{M}|v-a||w-\mathrm{N}|d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{M}w\,d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}-\mathrm{N}\right|+\left(\int_{M}|v-a|^{2}d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{M}|w-\mathrm{N}|^{2}d\nu_{-\tau_{0}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau_{0})-\mathrm{N}|+C_{\kappa}\,\zeta^{1/2}\leq\zeta+C_{\kappa}\zeta^{1/2}.

Combining this with (
7.8
) tells us that
if
\zeta\leq\overline{\zeta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}
,
then for all
\tau\in[\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1}]
we have

\left|\int_{M}v(w-\mathrm{N})\,d\nu_{-\tau}\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Since
v
is arbitrarily,
we complete the proof.

\Box

We provide the following characterization (cf.
[
5
, Proposition 7.1]
):

Proposition 7.3
.

For
\kappa>0,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
if
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}
, then the following holds:
Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
r>0
, we assume
[t_{0}-\delta^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
and
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})\geq-\kappa
.
If

(7.11)

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\delta^{-1}r^{2})\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\delta r^{2})-\delta,

then
(x_{0},t_{0})
is
(\varepsilon,r)
-selfsimilar.
Vice versa, if
(x_{0},t_{0})
is
(\delta,r)
-selfsimilar, then for all
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in[\varepsilon r^{2},\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2}]
we have

(7.12)

|\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(\tau_{2})|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=0
and
r=1
.
Set
\mathrm{N}:=\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(1)
.
We first assume (
7.11
).
By (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
7.11
),

(7.13)

|\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)-\mathrm{N}|\leq\delta,\quad\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)\leq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)\leq\mathrm{N}+\delta

for all
\tau\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]
.
Let
\zeta\in(\delta,1)
.
By (
4.2
), (
4.1
) and (
7.13
),
if
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,\zeta}
,
then

(7.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\delta^{-1}-\tau}\int^{\delta^{-1}}_{\tau}\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\sigma)d\sigma=\frac{1}{\delta^{-1}-\tau}\left(\delta^{-1}\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\delta^{-1})-\tau\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta^{-1}}{\delta^{-1}-\tau}\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\delta^{-1})\geq\frac{\delta^{-1}}{\delta^{-1}-\zeta^{-1}}(\mathrm{N}-\delta)\geq\mathrm{N}-\zeta

for all
\tau\in[\zeta,\zeta^{-1}]
;
in particular,
|\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)-\mathrm{N}|\leq\zeta
for all
\tau\in[\zeta,\zeta^{-1}]
.
By Lemma
7.2
,
if
\zeta\leq\overline{\zeta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}
,

7.2
).
Secondly,
(
4.2
) yields

\int_{-\varepsilon^{-1}}^{-\varepsilon}\int_{M}\left(2\tau\left|\Psi\right|^{2}+\tau\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)\right)d\nu_{(x_{0},0);t}dt=\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\varepsilon)-\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\varepsilon^{-1})\leq\varepsilon,

\Psi
is defined as (
2.6
).

7.1
).
Finally,
(
7.3
) is a consequence of Lemma
2.1
.

We next show (
7.12
).

(x_{0},0)
is
(\delta,1)
-selfsimilar.
Integrating (
7.2
),
we have

(7.15)

|\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)-\mathrm{N}|\leq\delta

for all
\tau\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]
.

4.4
) and (
7.15
), we see

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)\geq\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)\geq\mathrm{N}-\delta

for all
\tau\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]
.
Furthermore,
(
4.2
), (
4.1
) and (

),

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}+\delta\geq\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\delta)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\tau-\delta}\int_{\delta}^{\tau}\mathcal{W}_{(x_{0},0)}(\sigma)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau-\delta}\left(\tau\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)-\delta\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\delta)\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon-\delta}\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\tau)

for all

,

for all

7.3.
Almost constancy

In this subsection,
we prove an almost constancy property for an integral quantity.
To do so,
let us show the following formula:

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
Then for all
t\in I\cap(-\infty,t_{0})
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}

\displaystyle=2\int_{M}\tau\left\langle\Psi,h+\nabla^{2}f-df\otimes df\right\rangle d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{M}\tau(\operatorname{tr}\Psi)(|\nabla f|^{2}-\Delta f)d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}+\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t},

where
\Psi
is defined as (
2.6
).

Proof.

We set
\nu:=\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0})}
.
From (
2.5
) we deduce

(7.16)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\square(\tau\mathcal{H})\,d\nu_{t}=\int_{M}\tau\square\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(2\tau|h|^{2}+\tau\mathcal{D}(0)-\mathcal{H})d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(2\tau\left\langle\Psi,h\right\rangle-2\tau\langle h,\nabla^{2}f\rangle+\tau\mathcal{D}(0)\right)d\nu_{t}.

By direct calculations,
we see

(7.17)

\operatorname{div}(h(\nabla f))=(\operatorname{div}h)(\nabla f)+\langle h,\nabla^{2}f\rangle,\quad\operatorname{div}(\Psi(\nabla f))=(\operatorname{div}\Psi)(\nabla f)+\langle\Psi,\nabla^{2}f\rangle.

The first one in (
7.17
) and (
2.5
) yield

(7.18)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle h,\nabla^{2}f\rangle\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(h(\nabla f,\nabla f)-(\operatorname{div}h)(\nabla f)\right)d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(h(\nabla f,\nabla f)-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla\mathcal{H},\nabla f\rangle\right)d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{Ric}-h)(\nabla f,\nabla f)-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}(0)\right)d\nu_{t}.

The second one in (
7.17
), (
2.5
) and the Bochner formula (see e.g.,
[
36
, Lemma 2.1]
) imply

(7.19)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\Psi,\nabla^{2}f\rangle\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\Psi(\nabla f,\nabla f)-(\operatorname{div}\Psi)(\nabla f)\right)d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\langle\Psi,df\otimes df\rangle-(\operatorname{div}h)(\nabla f)-(\operatorname{div}\nabla^{2}f)(\nabla f)\right)d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\langle\Psi,df\otimes df\rangle d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla\mathcal{H},\nabla f\rangle+\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{Ric}-h)(\nabla f,\nabla f)-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}(0)\right)d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}\left(-\langle\nabla\Delta f,\nabla f\rangle-\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)\right)d\nu_{t}.

Combining (
7.18
) and (
7.19
) tells us that

(7.20)

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\int_{M}\left(\langle h,\nabla^{2}f\rangle+\langle\Psi,\nabla^{2}f\rangle\right)\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\langle\Psi,df\otimes df\rangle-\langle\nabla(\operatorname{tr}\Psi),\nabla f\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(0)\right)d\nu_{t}.

By substituting (
7.20
) into (
7.16
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=2\tau\int_{M}\left\langle\Psi,h+\nabla^{2}f-df\otimes df\right\rangle d\nu_{t}+2\tau\int_{M}\langle\nabla(\operatorname{tr}\Psi),\nabla f\rangle d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\tau\int_{M}\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)d\nu_{t}.

From integration by parts (
2.5
),
we conclude the desired equation.

\Box

We are now in a position to conclude the following result,
which is new even for Ricci flow.

Proposition 7.6
.

For
\kappa>0,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
if
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}
, then the following holds:
Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
r>0
,
we assume that
(x_{0},t_{0})
is
(\delta,r)
-selfsimilar, and
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})\geq-\kappa
.
Then for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}]
, we have

\left|\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{1}}-\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{2}}\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=0
and
r=1
.
We set
\nu:=\nu_{(x_{0},0)}
.
We may also assume
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
.
From Lemma
7.5
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}d\nu_{t_{1}}-\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}d\nu_{t_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\tau\left\langle\Psi,h+\nabla^{2}f-df\otimes df\right\rangle d\nu_{t}dt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+2\left|\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\,\int_{M}\tau(\operatorname{tr}\Psi)(|\nabla f|^{2}-\Delta f)\,d\nu_{t}dt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau\,\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)\,d\nu_{t}dt.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
we have

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\left|\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\tau\left\langle\Psi,h+\nabla^{2}f-df\otimes df\right\rangle d\nu_{t}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau|\Psi|^{2}\,d\nu_{t}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(C\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau(|h|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{4})\,d\nu_{t}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\,\delta^{1/2}.

In the same manner,
we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\left|\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\,\int_{M}\tau(\operatorname{tr}\Psi)(|\nabla f|^{2}-\Delta f)\,d\nu_{t}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau(\operatorname{tr}\Psi)^{2}\,d\nu_{t}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau(|\nabla f|^{2}-\Delta f)^{2}\,d\nu_{t}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau|\Psi|^{2}\,d\nu_{t}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(C\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau(|\nabla f|^{4}+|\nabla^{2}f|^{2})\,d\nu_{t}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\,\delta^{1/2}.

Furthermore,
by the almost selfsimilarity (
7.1
),

\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{M}\,\tau\,\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)\,d\nu_{t}dt\leq\int^{-\delta}_{-\delta^{-1}}\int_{M}\,\tau\,\mathcal{D}(\nabla f)\,d\nu_{t}dt\leq 2\delta.

Hence,
we arrive at

\left|\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}d\nu_{t_{1}}-\int_{M}\tau\mathcal{H}d\nu_{t_{2}}\right|\leq C_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\,\delta^{1/2}+2\delta.

This completes the proof.

\Box

8.
Almost static points

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1),r>0
,
a point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
is called
(\varepsilon,r)
-static
if the following holds:

(1)

[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
;

(2)

we have

r^{2}\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2}}^{t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}}\int_{M}|h|^{2}d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}dt\leq\varepsilon;

(3)

for all
t\in[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}]
, we have

r^{2}\int_{M}\mathcal{H}\,d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}\leq\varepsilon;

\mathcal{H}\geq-\varepsilon r^{-2}
on
M\times[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}]
.

Our first main result is the following almost static cone splitting theorem,
which has been formulated by Bamler
[
5
]
for Ricci flow (cf.
[
5
, Proposition 10.1]
):

Theorem 8.1
.

\kappa,D>0,\alpha,\varepsilon\in(0,1)

(x_{0},t_{0}),(x_{1},t_{1})\in M\times I
.

(x_{0},t_{0}),(x_{1},t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha,\zeta,\xi}\,\int_{t_{0}-2\xi}^{t_{0}-\xi}\int_{M}\left|{-\tau_{1}(\mathcal{H}+|\nabla f_{1}|^{2})+f_{1}-\mathrm{N}_{1}}\right|e^{\theta f_{1}}d\nu^{1}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha,\zeta,\xi}\,\int_{t_{1}-\eta^{-1}}^{t_{1}-\eta}\int_{M}\left|{-\tau_{1}(\mathcal{H}+|\nabla f_{1}|^{2})+f_{1}-\mathrm{N}_{1}}\right|e^{\theta f_{1}}d\nu^{1}_{t}dt\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha,\zeta,\xi}\,\eta\leq\xi;

in particular,

(8.4)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-2\xi}^{t_{0}-\xi}\int_{M}\tau_{1}\mathcal{H}\,d\nu^{0}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq\xi-\int_{t_{0}-2\xi}^{t_{0}-\xi}\int_{M}(\tau_{1}|\nabla f_{1}|^{2}-f_{1}+\mathrm{N}_{1})\,d\nu^{0}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha}\,\xi+\int_{t_{0}-2\xi}^{t_{0}-\xi}\int_{M}f_{1}\,d\nu^{0}_{t}dt.

Fix
s\in[t_{0}-2\xi,t_{0}-\xi]
.
By (
8.1
) and (
8.2
),
if
\eta\leq\overline{\eta}_{\kappa,D,\alpha,\zeta,\xi}
, then

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\int^{s}_{t_{0}-1}\int_{M}\left|\square(\tau_{1}f_{1})+\frac{n}{2}+\mathrm{N}_{1}\right|d\nu^{0}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha,\zeta,\xi}\,\int^{s}_{t_{0}-1}\int_{M}\left|\square(\tau_{1}f_{1})+\frac{n}{2}+\mathrm{N}_{1}\right|e^{\theta f_{1}}d\nu^{1}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha,\zeta,\xi}\,\int_{t_{1}-\eta^{-1}}^{t_{1}-\eta}\int_{M}\left|\square(\tau_{1}f_{1})+\frac{n}{2}+\mathrm{N}_{1}\right|e^{\theta f_{1}}d\nu^{1}_{t}dt\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha,\zeta,\xi}\,\eta\leq\xi.

Hence,

8.1
) and (
8.5

\displaystyle\int_{M}\tau_{1}f_{1}d\nu^{0}_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\tau_{1}f_{1}d\nu^{0}_{t_{0}-1}+\int^{s}_{t_{0}-1}\int_{M}\square(\tau_{1}f_{1})d\nu^{0}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq(1-t_{0})\left(C_{\kappa,D,\alpha}+(1-\theta)^{-1}\int_{M}fd\nu^{0}_{t_{0}-1}\right)+\int^{s}_{t_{0}-1}\int_{M}\square(\tau_{1}f_{1})d\nu^{0}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha^{-1})\left(C_{\kappa,D,\alpha}+(1-\theta)^{-1}\frac{n}{2}\right)+\xi-\int^{s}_{t_{0}-1}\int_{M}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\mathrm{N}_{1}\right)d\nu^{0}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha^{-1})\left(C_{\kappa,D,\alpha}+(1-\theta)^{-1}\frac{n}{2}\right)+\xi+C_{\kappa,D,\alpha}(s-t_{0}+1)\leq C_{\kappa,D,\alpha},

which leads us to

in particular,
there exists

such that

Note that
\tau\leq 2\xi
and
\tau_{1}\geq\alpha
at

.

\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon,\xi}

for all

.

and

leads us to

If

9.
Almost splitting

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1),r>0
,
a
(k,\varepsilon,r)
-splitting map at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
is a map
\vec{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}):M\times[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}]\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
with the following properties for all
i,j=1,\ldots,k
:

(1)

[t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
;

(2)

we have

r^{-1}\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2}}^{t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}}\int_{M}|\square y_{i}|d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}dt\leq\varepsilon;

(3)

we have

r^{-2}\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}r^{2}}^{t_{0}-\varepsilon r^{2}}\int_{M}\left|\langle\nabla y_{i},\nabla y_{j}\rangle-\delta_{ij}\right|d\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t}dt\leq\varepsilon.

We say that
(x_{0},t_{0})
is
(k,\varepsilon,r)
-split
if
there is a
(k,\varepsilon,r)
-splitting map.
The aim of this section is prove the following almost splitting theorem,
which has been obtained by Bamler
[
5
]
for Ricci flow (cf.
[
5
, Proposition 10.8]
):

Theorem 9.1
.

For
\kappa,D>0,\varepsilon,\xi\in(0,1)
,
if
\beta\leq\overline{\beta},\mathfrak{D}\geq\underline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\kappa,D},\mathfrak{N}\geq\underline{\mathfrak{N}}_{\kappa,D},\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,D,\varepsilon,\xi}
,
then the following holds:
Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}^{N-1}_{i=0}\subset M\times I
with
t_{0}\leq\cdots\leq t_{N-1}
and
N\geq\mathfrak{N}\,\xi^{-k}
.
For
r>0
,
we assume
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}^{N-1}_{i=0}
are
(\delta,r)
-selfsimilar,
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})\geq-\kappa
and
0\leq t_{i}-t_{0}\leq\beta\,\xi^{2}r^{2}
for all
i
.
Assume the following:

(1)

W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},t_{0});t_{0}-r^{2}},\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});t_{0}-r^{2}})\leq Dr
for all
i
;

(2)

W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});t_{0}-2\xi^{2}r^{2}},\nu_{(x_{j},t_{j});t_{0}-2\xi^{2}r^{2}})\geq\mathfrak{D}\xi r
for all
i\neq j
.

Then
(x_{0},t_{0})
is
(k+1,\varepsilon,r)
-split.

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho,R}(W_{1}(\delta_{z_{0}},\nu_{(x_{0},R^{2});0})+1)\leq C_{\rho,R}.

From (
10.2
), (
10.3
) and (
10.4
),
we conclude the desired estimate.

\Box

Based on Lemma

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
r,\rho,R>0
,
we assume
[t_{0}-2(R^{2}+\rho)r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
. Let

\xi\in(0,\sqrt{\rho}]

Then there exists
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}^{N}_{i=1}\subset S

(10.5)

S\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}P(x_{i},t_{i};\xi r),\quad N\leq C_{\rho,R}\,\xi^{-n-2}.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=0
and
r=1
.
Let
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}^{N}_{i=1}\subset S
be a maximal collection of points such that
\{P(x_{i},t_{i};\xi/3)\}^{N}_{i=1}
are pairwise disjoint.
Let
(x,t)\in S
.
By the maximality we see
P(x,t;\xi/3)\cap P(x_{i},t_{i};\xi/3)\neq\emptyset
for some
i
.
From Lemma
10.1
, we deduce
(x,t)\in P(x_{i},t_{i};\xi)
,
which proves the inclusion in (
10.5
).

Let us derive an upper bound on
N
in (
10.5
).
We fix
\zeta\in(0,1/2]
.
If
\zeta\leq\overline{\zeta}_{\rho,R}
,
then there exist
\mathcal{I}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
s\in[-R^{2}-2\zeta\xi^{2},R^{2}-\zeta\xi^{2}]
such that

(10.6)

|\mathcal{I}|\geq\lfloor C_{\zeta,R}\,\xi^{2}\,N\rfloor,\quad s\in[t_{i}-2\zeta\xi^{2},t_{i}-\zeta\xi^{2}]

for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
.
For each
i\in\mathcal{I}
,
let
(z_{i},s)
be a center of
(x_{i},t_{i})
(see Proposition
3.2
).
By Lemma
2.6
and Proposition
3.1
,
if
\zeta\leq\overline{\zeta}
,
then for all
i,j\in\mathcal{I}
with
i\neq j
, it holds that

\displaystyle d_{s}(z_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle\geq W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});s},\nu_{(x_{j},t_{j});s})-W_{1}(\delta_{z_{i}},\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});s})-W_{1}(\delta_{z_{j}},\nu_{(x_{j},t_{j});s})

\displaystyle\geq W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});t_{i}-(\xi/3)^{2}},\nu_{(x_{j},t_{j});t_{i}-(\xi/3)^{2}})-2\sqrt{2H_{n}\zeta}\xi\geq\frac{\xi}{3}-2\sqrt{2H_{n}\zeta}\xi\geq\frac{\xi}{4};

in particular,

\{B(z_{i},s,\xi/8)\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
are pairwise disjoint.
For each
i\in\mathcal{I}
,
Lemma
2.6
and Proposition
3.1
lead us to

\displaystyle\quad\,\,W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},0);-R^{2}-2\zeta\xi^{2}},\nu_{(z_{i},s);-R^{2}-2\zeta\xi^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},0);-R^{2}-2\zeta\xi^{2}},\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});-R^{2}-2\zeta\xi^{2}})+W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});-R^{2}-2\zeta\xi^{2}},\nu_{(z_{i},s);-R^{2}-2\zeta\xi^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},0);-R^{2}},\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});-R^{2}})+W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});s},\delta_{z_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq R+\sqrt{H_{n}(t_{i}-s)}\leq R+\sqrt{2H_{n}\zeta}\xi<R+\frac{\xi}{8}

if
\zeta\leq\overline{\zeta}
,
and hence
(z_{i},s)\in P(x_{0},0;R+\xi/8)\cap(M\times\{s\})
.
For every
y\in B(z_{i},s,\xi/8)
,

\displaystyle\quad\,\,W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},0);-(R+\xi/8)^{2}},\nu_{(z_{i},s);-(R+\xi/8)^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},0);-(R+\xi/8)^{2}},\nu_{(y,s);-(R+\xi/8)^{2}})+d_{s}(y,z)\leq R+\frac{\xi}{4},

and thus

B(z_{i},s,\xi/8)\times\{s\}
is contained in
P(x_{0},0;R+\xi/4)\cap(M\times\{s\})
.
Summarizing the above observations,
from Lemma
10.2
and (
4.3
),
we derive

(10.7)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}|\,\inf_{i\in\mathcal{I}}m_{s}(B(z_{i},s,\xi/8))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}m_{s}(B(z_{i},s,\xi/8))

\displaystyle\leq m_{s}(P(x_{0},0;R+\xi/4)\cap(M\times\{s\})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho,R}\exp(\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\rho/2)).

On the other hand,
by Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
5.1
, if
\zeta\leq\overline{\zeta}
,
then

(10.8)

\displaystyle m_{s}(B(z_{i},s,\xi/8))

\displaystyle\geq m_{s}(B(z_{i},s,2\sqrt{H_{n}\zeta}\xi))\geq m_{s}(B(z_{i},s,\sqrt{2H_{n}(t_{i}-s)}))

\displaystyle\geq C_{\rho,R}\exp(\mathcal{N}_{(x_{i},t_{i})}(t_{i}-s))(t_{i}-s)^{n/2}\geq C_{\rho,R}\exp(\mathcal{N}_{(x_{i},t_{i})}(\rho/2))\xi^{n}.

Moreover, (
4.3
), Lemmas
4.5
and
4.2
yield

(10.9)

\mathcal{N}_{(x_{i},t_{i})}(\rho/2)\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{i},t_{i})}(t_{i}+R^{2}+\rho/2)\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(R^{2}+\rho/2)-C_{\rho,R}\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(\rho/2)-C_{\rho,R}.

Here,

\mathcal{H}(\cdot,-R^{2}-\rho/2)\geq-n/\rho
by Lemma
2.1
(see Remark

).
Combining (

), (
10.8
), (
10.9
) implies
|\mathcal{I}|\leq C_{\rho,R}\xi^{-n}
.
This together with (

,

Proposition 10.4
.

For
\kappa>0,\varepsilon,\xi\in(0,1)
,
if
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon,\xi}
,
then the following holds:
Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
r>0
,
we assume
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})\geq-\kappa
.
For a subset
S\subset M\times I
,
we assume that every point in
S
is
(\delta,r)
-selfsimilar, and none of the following two properties hold:

(1)

(k+1,\varepsilon,r)
-split;

(2)

(k-1,\varepsilon,r)
-split and
(\varepsilon,r)
-static.

Then there exists
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}^{N}_{i=1}\subset S\cap P(x_{0},t_{0};r)
such that

(10.10)

S\cap P(x_{0},t_{0};r)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}P(x_{i},t_{i};\xi r),\quad N\leq C_{\kappa}\,\xi^{-k}.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=0
and
r=1
.
We first notice that
by (
4.3
), Lemmas
4.5
and
4.2
,
for every
(x,t)\in P(x_{0},0;1)

\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(1)\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(t+2)\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(2)-C\geq\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},0)}(1)-C\geq-C_{\kappa}.

Let
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}^{N}_{i=1}\subset S\cap P(x_{0},0;1)
be a maximal collection of points such that
\{P(x_{i},t_{i};\xi/10)\}^{N}_{i=1}
are pairwise disjoint.
By the maximality and Lemma
10.1

).

We derive an upper bound on

We may assume
t_{1}\leq\ldots\leq t_{N}

We verify the following:
If
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon,\xi},N\geq\mathfrak{N}(100\mathfrak{D})^{l}\xi^{-l}

(x_{1},t_{1})

(l+1,\varepsilon,1)
-split,

9.1
.
Indeed,
for every
i
,
Proposition
3.1
yields

\displaystyle\quad\,\,W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{1},t_{1});t_{1}-1},\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});t_{1}-1})

\displaystyle\leq W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{1},t_{1});-1},\nu_{(x_{0},0);-1})+W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{0},0);-1},\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});-1})\leq 2.

i\neq j

3.1

\displaystyle\quad\,\,W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});t_{1}-2(\xi/100\mathfrak{D})^{2}},\nu_{(x_{j},t_{j});t_{1}-2(\xi/100\mathfrak{D})^{2}})

\displaystyle\geq W_{1}(\nu_{(x_{i},t_{i});t_{i}-(\xi/10)^{2}},\nu_{(x_{j},t_{j});t_{i}-(\xi/10)^{2}})\geq\frac{\xi}{10}\geq\mathfrak{D}\left(\frac{\xi}{100\mathfrak{D}}\right)

Theorem 10.5
.

Let
(M,(g_{t})_{t\in I})
be a super Ricci flow with
\mathcal{D}\geq 0
.
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
.
For
r,\kappa>0
we assume
[t_{0}-2r^{2},t_{0}]\subset I
and
\mathcal{N}_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(r^{2})\geq-\kappa
.
Then for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\sigma\in(0,\varepsilon)
,
there exists
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}^{N}_{i=1}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon,k}_{\sigma r,\varepsilon r}\cap P(x_{0},t_{0};r)
such that

\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon,k}_{\sigma r,\varepsilon r}\cap P(x_{0},t_{0};r)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}P(x_{i},t_{i};\sigma r),\quad N\leq C_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\,\sigma^{-k-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We may assume
t_{0}=0
and
r=1
.
For
\xi\in(0,1)
,
let
\lambda
stand for an integer determined by
\sigma\in[\xi^{\lambda},\xi^{\lambda-1}]
.
Let
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}_{\kappa,\varepsilon,\xi}
denote a constant obtained in Proposition
10.4
.
For each
(x,t)\in P(x_{0},0;1)
,
let
\mathcal{J}_{(x,t)}
be the set of all integers
j
such that
(x,t)
is not
(\delta,\xi^{j})
-selfsimilar.
In virtue of Proposition
7.3
,
if
\eta\leq\overline{\eta}_{\kappa,\delta}
,
then

C_{\kappa}\geq-\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}(1)\geq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{(x,t)}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}\left(\eta\,\xi^{2j}\right)-\mathcal{N}_{(x,t)}\left(\eta^{-1}\,\xi^{2j}\right)\right)\geq\eta\,|\mathcal{J}_{(x,t)}|.

Hence,

|\mathcal{J}_{(x,t)}|
is bounded by
C_{2}=C_{2,\kappa,\eta}
(cf.
[
16
, Section 3]
).
For
\{o_{j}\}^{\lambda-1}_{j=0}\subset\{0,1\}
, we set

S\left(\{o_{j}\}^{\lambda-1}_{j=0}\right):=\left\{(x,t)\in P(x_{0},0;1)~|~\text{$o_{j}=1$ if and only if $(x,t)$ is not $(\delta,\xi^{j})$-selfsimilar}\right\}.

Then
P(x_{0},0;1)
can be written as the union of at most
\lambda^{C_{2}}
many non-empty subsets of the form
S\left(\{o_{j}\}^{\lambda-1}_{j=0}\right)
since
S\left(\{o_{j}\}^{\lambda-1}_{j=0}\right)=\emptyset
if
\sum_{j}o_{j}>C_{2}
.

Fix
l=0,\dots,\lambda-1
and
(y,s)\in P(x_{0},0;1)
,
where let
(y,s):=(x_{0},0)
if
l=0
.
Due to Propositions
10.3
and
10.4
,
there is
\{(y_{i},s_{i})\}^{N}_{i=1}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon,k}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}\cap S\left(\{o_{j}\}^{\lambda-1}_{j=0}\right)\cap P(y,s;\xi^{l})
such that

\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon,k}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}\cap S\left(\{o_{j}\}^{\lambda-1}_{j=0}\right)\cap P(y,s;\xi^{l})\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}P(y_{i},s_{i};\xi^{l+1}),\quad N\leq\begin{cases}C_{3,\xi}&\text{if $o_{l}=1$ or $l=0$,}\\
C_{1,\kappa}\,\xi^{-k}&\text{if $o_{l}=0$ and $l\geq 1$,}\end{cases}

where the assertion for
l=0
or
o_{l}=1
, and that for
l\geq 1
and
o_{l}=0
are consequences of Propositions
10.3
and
10.4
,
respectively.
By induction,

\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon,k}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}\cap S\left(\{o_{j}\}^{\lambda-1}_{j=0}\right)\cap P(x_{0},0;1)
can be covered by at most

C_{3,\xi}^{1+\sum_{j}o_{j}}(C_{1,\kappa}\,\xi^{-k})^{\lambda-1-\sum_{j}o_{j}}

many parabolic balls of the form
P(y,s;\sigma)
.
It follows that

\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon,k}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}\cap P(x_{0},0;1)
can be covered by at most

\lambda^{C_{2}}\,C_{3,\xi}^{1+C_{2}}(C_{1,\kappa}\xi^{-k})^{\lambda}\leq C_{\kappa,\xi,\delta}\,\lambda^{C_{2}}C_{1,\kappa}^{\lambda}\,\xi^{-\lambda\,k}

many such parabolic balls.
If
\xi\leq\overline{\xi}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}
such that
\xi^{-\varepsilon/2}\geq C_{1,\kappa}
,
then

C_{\kappa,\xi,\delta}\,\lambda^{C_{2}}\,\xi^{-\lambda k-m\varepsilon/2}\leq C_{\kappa,\xi,\delta}\,\xi^{-\lambda k-m\varepsilon}\leq C_{\kappa,\xi,\delta}\,\sigma^{-k-\varepsilon}.

Thus,
we complete the proof.

Eigenvalues of matrix products
Richard Kenyon, Nicholas Ovenhouse

Abstract
We study pairs of matrices A , B ∈ GL n ​ ( ℂ ) A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}) such that the eigenvalues of A A , of B B and of the product A ​ B AB are specified in advance. We show that the space of such pairs ( A , B ) (A,B) under simultaneous conjugation has dimension ( n − 1 ) ​ ( n − 2 ) (n-1)(n-2) , and give an explicit parameterization. More generally let Σ \Sigma be a surface of genus g g with k k punctures. We find a parameterization of the space Ω g , k , n \Omega_{g,k,n} of flat GL n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}) -structures on Σ \Sigma whose holonomies around the punctures have prescribed eigenvalues. We show furthermore that, for 3 ≤ k ≤ 2 ​ g + 6 3\leq k\leq 2g+6 (or 3 ≤ k ≤ 9 3\leq k\leq 9 if g = 1 g=1 , or 3 ≤ k 3\leq k if g = 0 g=0 ), the space Ω g , k , n \Omega_{g,k,n} has an explicit symplectic structure and an associated Liouville integrable system, equivalent to a leaf of a Goncharov-Kenyon dimer integrable system.



1
Introduction

The
Deligne-Simpson problem
(DSP), see
[
Kos04
]
, is to find matrices
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}

whose product is the identity
and which have prescribed conjugacy classes in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
.
A more general setting is as follows.
Given an oriented surface
\Sigma
of genus
g
with
k
boundary components,
describe the space

\Omega^{\text{DS}}_{g,k,n}(C)
of representations
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})

with prescribed conjugacy classes
\{C_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,k}

for the peripheral curves around each boundary component.
This problem arises from the study of systems of singular linear ODEs on surfaces.

The simplest case is when
\Sigma
is a
3
-holed sphere (the
k=3
case of the DSP).
In this case,
this problem can be restated as finding matrices
A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, for which the conjugacy classes of
A,B
and
AB
are prescribed.

We deal here with the simpler version of prescribing the eigenvalues only.
Let
\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)
be the space of representations
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, up to conjugacy, where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
are the sets of eigenvalues for the monodromies of the
k
peripheral curves.
That is,
\lambda_{i}:=\{\lambda_{i,1},\lambda_{i,2},\dots,\lambda_{i,n}\}
are the eigenvalues of the monodromy around boundary component
i
.
Note that if each
\lambda_{i}
consists of distinct values, this is just the DSP,
that is,

\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)=\Omega^{\text{DS}}_{g,k,n}(\lambda)
when each
\lambda_{i}
consists of distinct eigenvalues. When some individual eigenvalues are not distinct,

\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)
is a union of subspaces of various dimensions consisting of
\Omega^{\text{DS}}_{g,k,n}(C)
for the various conjugacy classes
C
having the same eigenvalues as
\lambda
.

Our main result is a Laurent parameterization of the space
\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)
,

\Psi:({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{N}\to\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda),

where
N=2(g-1)n^{2}+(n^{2}-n)k+2
is the dimension of
\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)
. That is, we give explicit matrices representing a connection in
\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)

whose entries are Laurent polynomials in
N
parameters. When
(g,k)=(0,3)
and
n
is odd, and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
consist in positive reals,
there is a positive Laurent parameterization (with coefficients
1
)
\Psi:({\mathbb{R}}_{+})^{N}\to\Omega_{0,3,n}^{+}(\lambda)
onto a certain “positive” subvariety

\Omega_{0,3,n}^{+}(\lambda)\subset\Omega_{0,3,n}(\lambda)
, see Section
4.2
.

For
(g,k)=(0,3)
a similar problem, Horn’s problem, asks to prescribe the
singular values
of three matrices
A,B
and
AB
;
this was solved by Knutson and Tao
[
KT99
]
, based on earlier work of Klyachko
[
Kly98
]
,
by finding a surprising connection with “hives” or tropical degree-
n
curves.
Our method uses a similar (but pre-tropical) setup.
It arises from studying
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-local systems on certain graphs on surfaces
(see Figure
1
for the
(g,k)=(0,3)
case), and is inspired by the work of Fock and Goncharov
[
FG06
]
.

Figure 1:
The graph
{\mathcal{G}}
on the torus (the torus is obtained by identifying opposite sides of the rhombus) has two vertices and three edges.
The parallel transports along the three edges from black to white are
A,B,I
as shown.

The basic idea behind the construction is that there is an isomorphism between
\mathrm{GL}_{n}

structures on a bipartite surface graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, and
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-structures on a related graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
.
This idea
originated in
[
FG06
]
who coordinatized the space of (decorated)
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
structures
with explicit scalar quantities on the related graphs
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
. See also
[
KO23
]
.

If
3\leq k\leq 2g+6
, or
g=1
and
3\leq k\leq 9
, or
g=0
and
k\geq 3
,
we show in Section
6
that
the space
\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)
can be identified
with a symplectic leaf of a dimer integrable system as defined in
[
GK13
]
. That is, there is a certain nondegenerate Poisson structure on
\Omega_{g,k,n}(\lambda)
and a set of
explicit commuting Hamiltonian
functions on it forming a Liouville integrable system.
When
k
is outside the above bounds we don’t know if there is such a correspondence.

Conjugation

We replace
A
with
A^{-1}
and parameterize pairs
(A,B)
with prescribed eigenvalues for
A,B
and
BA^{-1}
. See Theorem
1
.

Given three multisets (sets allowing repeated elements)
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, each of size
n
:

\alpha=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\},~~\beta=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}\},~~\gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\},

let
\Omega=\Omega_{0,3,n}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be the space of pairs of matrices
A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, up to simultaneous conjugation,
with eigenvalues
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively,
and with product
BA^{-1}
having eigenvalues
\gamma
.
Note that simultaneous conjugation of
A,B
does not change any of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
.

Let
f_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq f_{n}
be a complete flag invariant under
A
: this means for each
i
,

f_{i}
is subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
of dimension
i
and
Af_{i}\subseteq f_{i}
. Likewise let

g_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq g_{n}
be an invariant flag for
B
.
Further suppose that the two flags are
transverse
; that is,
\dim(f_{i}\cap g_{j})=\max(0,i+j-n)
; this will be true for generic
A,B
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
M
be a matrix whose
k
th column spans the 1-dimensional space
f_{n+1-k}\cap g_{k}
.
Then
M^{-1}AM
is lower triangular and
M^{-1}BM
is upper triangular.

Proof.

Let
M_{k}
be the
k
th column of the matrix
M
(which spans
f_{n+1-k}\cap g_{k}
).
Since
M_{k}\in g_{k}
, and the
M_{k}
’s are linearly independent, the flag defined by the columns (in left-to-right order)

\left<M_{1}\right>\subset\left<M_{1},M_{2}\right>\subset\left<M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}\right>\subset\cdots

is the same as the flag
g
. Since
M^{-1}BM
is the coordinate expression of
B
in the
M_{k}
basis, and since
g
is invariant under
B
,
we see that
M^{-1}BM
is upper-triangular. Similarly, the flag
f
(invariant under
A
) is the same as the flag generated by the right-to-left ordering of the columns of
M
:

\left<M_{n}\right>\subset\left<M_{n},M_{n-1}\right>\subset\left<M_{n},M_{n-1},M_{n-2}\right>\subset\cdots,

and therefore
M^{-1}AM
is lower-triangular.
∎

Since for generic
A,B
the flags

f,g
are transverse, in the nongeneric case we can, by taking limits,
still assume
A,B
are lower and upper triangular.
This shows that without loss of generality we may assume that

A,B
are lower and upper triangular, respectively.

We may further conjugate by diagonal matrices (without loss of generality of determinant
1
), since these preserve upper and lower triangularity.
The resulting space of matrix pairs with eigenvalues
\alpha,\beta

up to conjugacy is then of dimension
n(n-1)-(n-1)=(n-1)^{2}
. Imposing
n-1
eigenvalue conditions on the product (one condition is
determined, since
\det BA^{-1}=\det B/\det A
)
reduces the dimension of the space of pairs in
\Omega
to
(n-1)(n-2)
.

Note that by choosing different flags for
A
and/or for
B
we can reorder the diagonal elements of
M^{-1}AM
and of

M^{-1}BM
independently. Our parameterization will depend on this choice, which is, equivalently, a choice of order for the eigenvalues of
A
and of
B
.
It will also depend on a choice of order for
\gamma
, the eigenvalues of
BA^{-1}
.

3.4
Zig-zag paths

Given some orientation of the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
(either by (E), (NW), or (SW)), a
zig-zag path
is a directed
path which alternately turns left, then right, then left, then right, etc.
Let us also use the notation
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
for the concatenation of the networks used to define
A
and
B
, as in Figure
5
.
These are both unfoldings of the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
.
The following result specifies how these paths correspond to the eigenvalues of the matrices
A
,
B
, and
BA^{-1}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
and
B
be defined as above.

(a)

The eigenvalues of
A
are the weights of the north-east zig-zag paths in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

(b)

The eigenvalues of
B
are the weights of the south-east zig-zag paths in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

(c)

The eigenvalues of
BA^{-1}
are
(-1)^{n+1}
times the weights of the south zig-zag paths in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

(a)
Recall that
A
is a lower-triangular matrix, and so its eigenvalues are simply the diagonal entries. It is easy to see that with
the (E) orientation, there is a unique path from
i
on the left to
i
on the right in
H_{A}
, given by the corresponding zig-zag path (see Figure
6
).
Thus the diagonal entry
A_{i,i}
(and hence the eigenvalue) is the weight of this single path.

(b)
Similarly, the diagonal entries of
B
(which is upper-triangular) are the weights of unique zig-zag paths in
H_{B}
from
i
to
i
.

(c)
Recall that
BA^{-1}
is conjugate to
DD^{\prime}
, and so it has the same eigenvalues. By Lemma
2
, the entries of
DD^{\prime}

are weighted sums of (SW) paths through
H_{A}
from the top edge to the bottom edge. Essentially the same argument from
(a)
and
(b)

works here, but with the following subtlety.

Although
D
and
D^{\prime}
are themselves not upper or lower triangular, the product
DD^{\prime}
is lower triangular, as we will now explain.
Because of the labels of the boundary vertices in the triangles
T
and
T^{\prime}
, one can see that
D
is lower anti-triangular (i.e.
D_{ij}=0
for
i+j<n+1
)
and
D^{\prime}
is upper anti-triangular (i.e.
D^{\prime}_{ij}=0
for
i+j>n+1
). If
P
is the permutation matrix for the permutation
k\mapsto n+1-k
,
then
DP
and

are both lower triangular, and hence
(DP)(PD^{\prime})
is also lower-triangular. But since

is an involution,

, and so
(DP)(PD^{\prime})=DD^{\prime}
.

Finally, since

is lower-triangular, we have (as in parts
(a)
and

) that its eigenvalues are the diagonal entries,
which are (up to sign) the weights of the unique south zig-zag paths in the network between corresponding boundary vertices.
However the signs in the product

combine coherently to give total sign

to all entries in

.

Let

Fix

The surface

5.2

-connections

For each puncture of
\Sigma

n

{\mathcal{G}}_{n}

n
prescribed eigenvalues. Pick an arbitrary bijection between these zig-zag paths and the eigenvalues.
We wish to define our
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-connection on
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
so that the monodromies of the zig-zag paths equal the
corresponding eigenvalues.

As previously, the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-connection is defined by the set of face monodromies
\{X_{f}\}
of
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}

(including around punctures), with one condition that their product is
1

2g^{\prime}

\{X_{x_{i}},X_{y_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{g^{\prime}}

for a basis for the homology of the

\bar{S}

the space of connections is parameterized by
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{f}:f\in F\}\cup\{X_{x_{i}},X_{y_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{g^{\prime}}

with the single relation above.

The monodromy conditions for the zig-zag paths
give a set of
kn-1

\mathcal{X}

for each eigenvalue/zig-zag path, but the last will be determined by the condition that the product of the eigenvalues is
1

The cycle space of
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
is of dimension
F+2g^{\prime}-1

F
is the number of its faces. We have

It remains to show that the

Consider the conjugate surface
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}_{n}}
of

{\mathcal{G}}_{n}

In conclusion the space of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-connections on

For certain

These are cases arising from a convex integer polygon

Given a convex polygon

{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
,
[
GK13
]
constructed
a “minimal” bipartite graph

on the torus with Newton polygon

[
GK13
]
, and by Newton polygon

the torus homology classes (in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
) of zig-zag paths in
{\mathcal{G}}

They showed that without loss of generality

In this case the dual graph of
{\mathcal{G}}
is not necessarily a triangulation, but has faces which are triangles and bigons. The graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
is constructed as before in each triangle but for a

n

The graph

is also

In

Moreover they showed that these monodromies freely generate

The common level sets of the zig-zag path monodromies therefore has a symplectic structure. An explicit Hamiltonian
Liouville integrable system was constructed in
[
GK13
]
.

It remains to be seen for which pairs

there exists such a polygon

This is provided by a theorem of Scott:

(

)

Proof.

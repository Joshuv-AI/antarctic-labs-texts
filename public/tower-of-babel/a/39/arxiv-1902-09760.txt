Magnetic, electrochemical and thermoelectric properties of P2 - Na x ​ ( Co 7/8 ​ Sb 1/8 ​ ) O 2 \text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{(}\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7/8}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1/8}}}\text{)}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
M. H. N. Assadi, S. Li, R. K. Zheng, S. P. Ringer, A. B. Yu

Abstract
We theoretically investigated the electronic, electrochemical and magnetic properties of Sb doped Na x ​ CoO 2 \text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ( x = 1 , 0.75 x=1,0.75 and 0.50). Sb Co \text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{Co}}} dopants adopt +5 oxidation state in Na x ​ CoO 2 \text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} host lattice for all Na concentrations (x). Due to high oxidation states, Sb 5+ \text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}} strongly repels Na ions and therefore it decreases the electrochemical potential (vs. Na/ Na + \text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} ). The electrons introduced by Sb 5+ \text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}} localize on nearby Co ions creating Co 2+ \text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}} species which are absent in undoped Na x ​ CoO 2 \text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . Co 2+ \text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}} ions reduce the spin entropy flow decreasing the Seebeck coefficient in the Sb doped compounds. The results can be generalized to other dopants with high oxidation state.



I
Introduction

Sodium cobaltate (
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
) is an interesting and promising compound for high efficiency thermoelectric
Fergus 2012
and sodium ion battery applications
Xiang et al. 2015
. This compound also exhibits a rich magnetic and structural phase diagrams
Mendels et al. 2005
;
Berthelot et al. 2011
. As demonstrated in FIG.
1
, the primitive cell of P2-
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
lattice is made of two alternating Na layers and edge-sharing
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
octahedra. Due to its frustrated triangular nature, the
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
creates a large spin entropy flow
Wang et al. 2003
and which results in a large Seebeck coefficient
Terasaki et al. 1997
. From materials engineering viewpoint, controlling Na concentration (
x
) has been the primary technique to tailor the properties of
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
for the desired applications. However, given the volatile nature of Na ions
Cushing and Wiley 1999
, establishing the precise property-structure relationship experimentally is somehow challenging. This experimental difficulty has motivated ongoing theoretical investigations into the structural and electronic properties of pristine
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Zhang et al. 2005
;
Meng et al. 2008
;
Wang and Ni 2007
;
Wang et al. 2008
.

Doping
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with other elements is another plausible method to improve the sodium cobaltate’s thermoelectric
Nagira et al. 2004
;
Seetawan et al. 2006
and electrochemical properties
Dollé et al. 2005
;
Bo et al. 2016
. Nonetheless, further progress in realizing high performance functional applications of the doped
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
compounds requires an accurate understanding of the electronic structure of the doped P2-
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. However, due to complex crystal structure of and strong electronic correlation in Na deficient sodium cobaltate, the theoretical aspects of doped
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
have not been fully explored. Accordingly, in this work, we study how Co’s substitution with Sb influences the electronic behavior of
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Sb doping was particularly chosen as it has recently shown to improve the electrochemical performance of other layered cathode materials
Twu et al. 2015
;
Twu et al. 2017
. Some of the conclusions drawn here can be applied to predict the magnetic, electrochemical and thermoelectric properties of
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
compounds in which dopants with high oxidation state partially replace Co.

III
Results and Discussion

It has been established that Sb ions are always stabilized by substituting a Co ions for a wide range of x in the
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
host lattice
Assadi and Katayama-Yoshida 2015b
. Now, we examine how Sb behave electronically in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
host lattice. Sb ions generally adopt two stable oxidation states;
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
and
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
.
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
ion is isovalent to
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
therefore
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
substitution with
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
does not alter the carrier density in the host system. This means that in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, all Co ions are in +3 oxidation state (
t_{2g}^{6}e_{g}^{0}
). In
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, two
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
convert to
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}
(
t_{2g}^{5}e_{g}^{0}
) to bear the holes generated by Na extraction. Similarly, In
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.50}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, four Co ions are in +4 oxidation state.
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
, on the other hand, introduces two electrons that would be borne on Co ions in an opposite manner to any holes created by Na extraction. Consequently, in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, two
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
(
t_{2g}^{6}e_{g}^{0}
) ions convert to
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
(
t_{2g}^{6}e_{g}^{1}
). In
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, however, there are two possibilities. First, the two extra electrons generated by
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
substitution compensate for the two holes created by Na removal leaving all Co ions in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at +3 oxidation state (
t_{2g}^{6}e_{g}^{0}
). The other possibility is that the hole created by Na extraction from the top Na layer localizes on the neighboring Co from the top
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer converting a
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
to
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}
while simultaneously, the hole created by Na extraction from the bottom
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer converts a
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
to back a
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
. In this scenario, there is one
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}
(
t_{2g}^{5}e_{g}^{0}
) in the top
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer and one
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
(
t_{2g}^{6}e_{g}^{1}
) in the bottom
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer. In
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.5}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
, the two electrons from
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
convert two nearby
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}
ions from the same
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer that contains the Sb dopant back to
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
.

Now let’s examine how dopants affect the thermoelectric performance of the sodium cobaltate. The Seebeck effect in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is driven by the spin entropy flow between
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
/
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}
according to the modified Heike’s formula for
S

Terasaki 2011
:

S=-\frac{k_{B}}{e}\ln\left[\frac{g\left({}\mathrm{Co}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}^{\mathrm{3+}}\right)}{g\left({}\mathrm{Co}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}^{\mathrm{4+}}\right)}\frac{t}{1-t}\right].

Here
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant,
e
is the electron charge,
t
is the concentration of
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
ions and
g
is electronic degeneracy of the Co ions. The electronic degeneracy (
g
) of an ion equals to the different possible ways in which electrons can be arranged. Consequently,
g
is the product of spin degeneracy (

g\hphantom{{}^{\mathrm{}}_{\mathrm{orbital}}}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}}_{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{}

g=g\hphantom{{}^{\mathrm{}}_{\mathrm{spin}}}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}}_{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{}\cdot g\hphantom{{}^{\mathrm{}}_{\mathrm{orbital}}}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}}_{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{}
.
g\hphantom{{}^{\mathrm{}}_{\mathrm{spin}}}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}}_{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{spin}}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{}
equals to
2\zeta+1

is the total spin number and
g\hphantom{{}^{\mathrm{}}_{\mathrm{orbital}}}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{}$}}}_{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\mathrm{2}}\smash[t]{\mathrm{orbital}}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{}

IV
Conclusions

We demonstrated that Sb dopant in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
adopt the highest possible oxidation state, in this regard, is acts similar to
\text{Eu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
,
\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}

Assadi et al. 2013
,
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}

Assadi and Katayama-Yoshida 2015c
and
\text{Ru}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}

Assadi and Katayama-Yoshida 2017
dopants. The electrons generated by
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
high oxidation state, localize on the neighboring Co ions instead of being delocalized over the valence band. This phenomenon results in the creation of
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
ions which do not occur in
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Magnetically,
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
ions behave similarly to
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}
as they couple ferromagnetically within the same
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layer and couple antiferromagnetically across
\text{CoO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
layers with
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}
ions. The higher electrostatic repulsion between
\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
and the
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions facilitates easier
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
extraction that eventually results in a slight decrease in the electrochemical potential. We therefore anticipate
\text{W}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{6+}}
,
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{5+}}
,
\text{Sn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4+}}

Assadi and Katayama-Yoshida 2015c
dopants having similar effect in reducing the Na extraction potential.

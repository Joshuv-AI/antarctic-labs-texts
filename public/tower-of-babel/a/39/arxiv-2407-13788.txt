New Borwein-type conjectures
Alexander Berkovich, Aritram Dhar

Abstract
. Motivated by recent research of Wang and Krattenthaler, we use Maple to propose five new “Borwein-type” conjectures modulo 3 3 and two new “Borwein-type” conjectures modulo 5 5 .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
05A16, 05A17, 05A30

1.
Introduction

In
1990
, Peter Borwein unearthed several mysteries involving sign patterns of coefficients of certain polynomials. He then personally communicated his observations to George Andrews who stated them as the following in his paper
[
1
]
:

Conjecture 1.1

(First Borwein Conjecture)
.

For the polynomials
A_{n}(q)
,
B_{n}(q)
, and
C_{n}(q)
defined by

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(1-q^{3j-2})(1-q^{3j-1})=A_{n}(q^{3})-qB_{n}(q^{3})-q^{2}C_{n}(q^{3}),

each has non-negative coefficients.

Conjecture 1.2

(Second Borwein Conjecture)
.

For the polynomials
\alpha_{n}(q)
,
\beta_{n}(q)
, and
\gamma_{n}(q)
defined by

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(1-q^{3j-2})^{2}(1-q^{3j-1})^{2}=\alpha_{n}(q^{3})-q\beta_{n}(q^{3})-q^{2}\gamma_{n}(q^{3}),

each has non-negative coefficients.

Conjecture 1.3

(Third Borwein Conjecture)
.

For the polynomials
\nu_{n}(q)
,
\phi_{n}(q)
,
\chi_{n}(q)
,
\psi_{n}(q)
, and
\omega_{n}(q)
defined by

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(1-q^{5j-4})(1-q^{5j-3})(1-q^{5j-2})(1-q^{5j-1})

\displaystyle=\nu_{n}(q^{5})-q\phi_{n}(q^{5})-q^{2}\chi_{n}(q^{5})-q^{3}\psi(q^{5})-q^{4}\omega(q^{5}),

each has non-negative coefficients.

Although Andrews did not prove any of the above conjectures, he derived explicit sum representations
[
1
, Theorem
3.1
, eq.
(3.4)
–
(3.6)
]
of the polynomials
A_{n}(q)
,
B_{n}(q)
, and
C_{n}(q)
respectively. Conjecture
1.1
was recently proved analytically by Chen Wang using the saddle point method
[
2
]
. Apart from other tools, his proof relied on a theorem of Andrews
[
1
, Theorem
4.1
]
and the following three recursive relations also given by Andrews
[
1
, eq.
(3.1)
–
(3.3)
]
:

\displaystyle A_{n}(q)

\displaystyle=(1+q^{2n-1})A_{n-1}(q)+q^{n}B_{n-1}(q)+q^{n}C_{n-1}(q),

\displaystyle B_{n}(q)

\displaystyle=(1+q^{2n-1})B_{n-1}(q)+q^{n-1}A_{n-1}(q)-q^{n}C_{n-1}(q),

\displaystyle C_{n}(q)

\displaystyle=(1+q^{2n-1})C_{n-1}(q)+q^{n-1}A_{n-1}(q)-q^{n-1}B_{n-1}(q).

Recently, Chen Wang and Christian Krattenthaler
[
3
]
proved Conjecture
1.1
and Conjecture
1.2
. Unlike Wang
[
2
]
who considered Andrews’ sum representations for his proof, Wang and Krattenthaler
[
3
]
applied the saddle point method directly to the “First Borwein polynomial”
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(1-q^{3j-2})(1-q^{3j-1})
and its higher powers. They also gave
[
3
, Conjecture
1.3
]
a cubic conjecture which was missed by both Borwein and Andrews and can be stated as follows:

Conjecture 1.4

(Cubic Borwein Conjecture)
.

For the polynomials
\kappa_{n}(q)
,
\delta_{n}(q)
, and
\theta_{n}(q)
defined by

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(1-q^{3j-2})^{3}(1-q^{3j-1})^{3}=\kappa_{n}(q^{3})-q\delta_{n}(q^{3})-q^{2}\theta_{n}(q^{3}),

each has non-negative coefficients.

They proved “two thirds” of Conjecture
1.4
above by showing that all coefficients of
\kappa_{n}(q)
are non-negative, “one half” of the coefficients of
\delta_{n}(q)
are non-negative, and “one half” of the coefficients of
\theta_{n}(q)
are non-negative. They also provided ideas
[
3
, Conjecture
11.1
]
along similar lines for (at least) “three fifths” of a proof of Conjecture
1.3
when the modulus
3
is replaced by
5
.

From here on, let us consider the standard notation for
q
-Pochammer symbols,

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k}),\,\,\text{for $n\geq 1$},

\displaystyle(a;q)_{0}

\displaystyle=1.

For positive integers
n
and
k
, we consider polynomials of the form

\displaystyle P_{n,k}(q):=\dfrac{(q;q)_{kn}}{(q^{k};q^{k})_{n}}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}(q^{j};q^{k})_{n}.

For
k=7
above, Wang and Krattenthaler
[
3
, Conjecture
11.5
]
stated an interesting modulus
7
Borwein Conjecture which is as follows:

Conjecture 1.5

(Modulus
7
Borwein Conjecture)
.

For positive integers
n
, consider the expansion of the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{n,7}(q):=\dfrac{(q;q)_{7n}}{(q^{7};q^{7})_{n}}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{21n^{2}}d_{m}(n)q^{m}.

Then

\displaystyle d_{7m}(n)\geq 0\quad\,\text{and}\quad\,d_{7m+1}(n),d_{7m+3}(n),d_{7m+4}(n),d_{7m+6}(n)\leq 0,\quad\,\text{for all $m$ and $n$},

while

d_{7m+5}(n)\begin{cases}\geq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m\leq 3\alpha(n)n^{2},\\
\leq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m>3\alpha(n)n^{2},\\
\end{cases}

where
\alpha(n)
seems to stabilise around
0.302
.

Motivated by Conjecture
1.5
, in Section
2
, we state new “Borwein-type” sign-pattern conjectures for the polynomial
P_{n,k}^{\,i}(q)
where
(k,i)\in\{(3,4),(3,5),(3,6),(3,7),(3,8),(5,2),(5,3)\}
. We conclude with some possible future directions in Section
3
.

2.
New Conjectures

In this section, we state the new “Borwein-type” conjectures.

Conjecture 2.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
i\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}
. Consider the expansion of the polynomial
P_{n,3}^{\,i}(q)
defined by

\displaystyle P_{n,3}^{\,i}(q):=\dfrac{(q;q)_{3n}^{i}}{(q^{3};q^{3})_{n}^{i}}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{3in^{2}}c_{m}^{(i)}(n)q^{m}.

Then

\displaystyle c_{3m}^{(i)}(n)\geq 0,\quad\,\text{for all $m$ and $n$},

while

c_{3m+2}^{(i)}(n)\begin{cases}\geq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m\leq\alpha^{(i)}(n)n^{2},\\
\leq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m>\alpha^{(i)}(n)n^{2},\\
\end{cases}

where
\alpha^{(i)}(n)
tends to a limiting value as
n
tends to
\infty
according to the following table

value of
i

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha^{(i)}(n)

4

5

1.270\cdots

6

We verified Conjecture
2.1
using
q
-series package in Maple. For instance, we got

\displaystyle\alpha^{(4)}(65)\cdot{65^{2}}=3170.

Remark 1
.

Since the polynomial
P_{n,3}^{\,i}(q)
is palindromic for any positive integer
i
, we can also predict the signs of the coefficients
c_{3m+1}^{(i)}(n)
in Conjecture
2.1
. This is true because of the following duality result.

Consider the polynomial
P_{n,3}^{\,i}(q)
as defined in Conjecture
2.1
. If we write

(2.1)

\displaystyle P_{n,3}^{\,i}(q)=X_{n,i}(q^{3})+qY_{n,i}(q^{3})+q^{2}Z_{n,i}(q^{3}),

then it is easy to show that the following relations hold

(2.2)

\displaystyle X_{n,i}(q)=q^{in^{2}}X_{n,i}(1/q)

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle Z_{n,i}(q)=q^{in^{2}-1}Y_{n,i}(1/q).

Conjecture 2.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
i\in\{2,3\}
. Consider the expansion of the polynomial
P_{n,5}^{\,i}(q)
defined by

\displaystyle P_{n,5}^{\,i}(q):=\dfrac{(q;q)_{5n}^{i}}{(q^{5};q^{5})_{n}^{i}}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{10in^{2}}d_{m}^{(i)}(n)q^{m}.

Then

\displaystyle d_{5m}^{(i)}(n)\geq 0,\quad\,\text{for all $m$ and $n$},

while

d_{5m+3}^{(i)}(n)\begin{cases}\geq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m\leq\tilde{\alpha}^{(i)}(n)n^{2},\\
\leq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m>\tilde{\alpha}^{(i)}(n)n^{2},\\
\end{cases}

and

d_{5m+4}^{(i)}(n)\begin{cases}\geq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m\leq\tilde{\beta}^{(i)}(n)n^{2},\\
\leq 0,\quad\text{for}\,\,\,m>\tilde{\beta}^{(i)}(n)n^{2},\\
\end{cases}

where
\tilde{\alpha}^{(i)}(n)
and
\tilde{\beta}^{(i)}(n)
tend to limiting values as
n
tends to
\infty
according to the following table

value of
i

Remark 2
.

Since the polynomial
P_{n,5}^{\,i}(q)
is palindromic for any positive integer
i
, we can also predict the signs of the coefficients
d_{5m+1}^{(i)}(n)
and
d_{5m+2}^{(i)}(n)
in Conjecture
2.2
. This is true because of the following duality result.

Consider the polynomial
P_{n,5}^{\,i}(q)
as defined in Conjecture
2.2
. If we write

(2.4)

\displaystyle P_{n,5}^{\,i}(q)=A_{n,i}(q^{5})+qB_{n,i}(q^{5})+q^{2}C_{n,i}(q^{5})+q^{3}D_{n,i}(q^{5})+q^{4}E_{n,i}(q^{5}),

then it is easy to show that the following relations hold

(2.5)

\displaystyle A_{n,i}(q)=q^{2in^{2}}A_{n,i}(1/q),

(2.6)

\displaystyle D_{n,i}(q)=q^{2in^{2}-1}C_{n,i}(1/q),

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle E_{n,i}(q)=q^{2in^{2}-1}B_{n,i}(1/q).

Remark 3
.

It must be noted that Wang and Krattenthaler
[
3
, Remark
11.6.(2)
]
mention that for
\delta\in\{2,3\}
, the coefficient of
q^{5m}
in
(q;q)_{5n}^{\delta}/(q^{5};q^{5})_{n}^{\delta}
is non-negative for all
m
, however, they do not state the limiting values of
\tilde{\alpha}^{(\delta)}(n)
and
\tilde{\beta}^{(\delta)}(n)
.

3.
Concluding Remarks

(1)

It would be very interesting to investigate whether the asymptotic approach of Wang and Krattenthaler in
[
3
]
applies to Conjecture
2.1
and Conjecture
2.2
.

(2)

The sign-pattern of coefficients of
P_{n,3}^{\,i}(q)
is much more complicated when
i\geq 9
. For instance, we have

\displaystyle X_{11,9}(q)

\displaystyle=1-3q+8q^{3}-9q^{4}+17q^{6}-27q^{7}+46q^{9}-57q^{10}+260q^{12}

\displaystyle+1899q^{13}+\cdots+1899q^{1076}+260q^{1077}-57q^{1079}+46q^{1080}-27q^{1082}

\displaystyle+17q^{1083}-9q^{1085}+8q^{1086}-3q^{1088}+q^{1089},

where
X_{11,9}(q)
is as defined in (
2.1
) with
n=11
and
i=9
. Definitely the sign-pattern above is something worth exploring further.

(3)

Similarly, the sign-pattern of coefficients of
P_{n,5}^{\,i}(q)
is complicated when

. For instance, we have

\displaystyle\qquad\,B_{2,4}(q)

\displaystyle-8418q^{9}-10544q^{10}-11369q^{11}-12772q^{12}-11134q^{13}-10072q^{14}

\displaystyle-7762q^{15}-3696q^{16}-2348q^{17}+1420q^{18}+2281q^{19}+2924q^{20}+3422q^{21}

\displaystyle+2300q^{22}+1983q^{23}+1044q^{24}+522q^{25}+168q^{26}-15q^{27}-48q^{28}-42q^{29}

is as defined in (

) with

. Definitely the sign pattern above is again worth exploring more.

(4)

It is a challenge to find partition theoretic explanation of why

in (
2.1
) with
1\leq i\leq 8
has non-negative coefficients for all
n
. Such interpretation, if any, would lead to a great insight into positivity problems in the theory of partitions and
q
-series.

Taub-NUT solutions in conformal electrodynamics
Alvaro Ballon Bordo, David Kubizňák, Tales Rick Perche

Abstract
We construct a novel charged Taub-NUT spacetime, providing a first non-trivial example of a self-gravitating solution to the recently proposed ModMax theory [ 3 , 20 ] , the most general (1-parametric) theory of non-linear electrodynamics that is characterized by both the conformal symmetry and the S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) SO(2) duality-rotation invariance. The spacetime features non-trivial magnetic fields, in their presence the non-linearity of the field becomes apparent and the solution is distinguished from that of the Maxwell theory. Thermodynamics of the new solution, the charged black hole limit of vanishing NUT parameter, and the self-dual solutions are also briefly discussed.



1
Introduction

Maxwell’s electromagnetism is a relativistic theory of linear electrodynamics which enjoys many fundamental symmetries, the two distinguished being the conformal invariance and the invariance under the
SO(2)
duality-rotation transformations. Over the years,
various non-linear extensions of this theory that modify the behavior of the field in a strong field regime have been considered. Perhaps the best known example of a theory of
non-linear electrodynamics
is the
Born–Infeld theory
[
8
]
. Although invariant under duality transformations
[
17
]
, the Born–Infeld theory contains a dimensionful parameter, and is not conformally invariant.

Remarkably, Maxwell’s theory is not the only (four-dimensional) theory that enjoys conformal and duality-rotation symmetries.
As shown recently
[
3
,
20
]
, there is a 1-parametric generalization of the Maxwell theory with these properties.
To stress its conformal invariance, in this paper we call this theory
conformal
(non-linear)
electrodynamics
. The corresponding Lagrangian density reads
[
3
,
20
]

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\left({\cal S}\cosh\gamma-\sqrt{{\cal S}^{2}+{\cal P}^{2}}\sinh\gamma\right)\,,

(1)

where
\gamma
is a free dimensionless parameter, and
{\cal S}
and
{\cal P}
are the two invariants of the electromagnetic field,

{\cal S}=\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,,\quad{\cal P}=\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu\nu}(*F)^{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

with the field strength
F_{\mu\nu}
given by

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
, in terms of the vector potential

A_{\mu}
. For
\gamma=0
, the theory reduces to the linear Maxwell theory. As discussed in
[
3
]
when
\gamma\neq 0
a birefringe phenomenon occurs; apart from the light-like polarization mode there is another mode which is subluminal for
\gamma>0
and superluminal for
\gamma<0
, hinting on a physical restriction
\gamma\geq 0
.

It is the aim of this paper to construct a first non-trivial self-gravitating solution of the conformal electrodynamics. As can be seen from the Lagrangian (
1
), the non-linear character of the theory only appears for solutions with magnetic fields for which the invariant
{\cal P}
is non-trivial. In what follows, we construct ‘the simplest possible’ such solution — the charged Taub-NUT(-AdS) spacetime. Even in the absence of magnetic charges, this spacetime features charged Misner strings and associated with them magnetic fields. In their presence, the invariant
{\cal P}
becomes non-trivial, the non-linearity of the field becomes apparent, and the solution is distinguished from that of the Maxwell theory.

The Taub–NUT spacetime
[
27
,
26
]
is a remarkable solution of the Einstein equations that has been a subject of controversy for many years, e.g.
[
24
,
25
]
.
Although predominantly studied in the Euclidean setting (see e.g. recent
[
9
]
and references therein),
it also provides an example of a cosmological model and a black hole spacetime. In what follows we concentrate on the (Lorentzian) black hole case, maintaining the
Misner string singularities on the axes
[

,
23
,
22
,
11
,

,
13
,
18
,
7
,
6
,

,

,

,

]
. Such a spacetime is geodesically complete and (despite the presence of regions with closed timelike curves) it is free of causal pathologies for geodesic observers
[

,
11
,

]
. Moreover, as shown recently, the corresponding black hole thermodynamics can be understood by standard
thermodynamic techniques
[

,

,

,

]
and yields a consistent first law, even in the presence of rotation and charges
[

2
Conformal electrodynamics

As shown in
[
3
,
20
]

the theory (
1
) is the most
general four-dimensional electrodynamics that possesses both the conformal symmetry and the
SO(2)
duality-rotation invariance.
Introducing the following ‘material’ field strength tensor:

E_{\mu\nu}=\partialderivative{\mathcal{L}}{F^{\mu\nu}}=2\Bigl({\cal L_{S}}F_{\mu\nu}+{\cal L_{P}}*\!F_{\mu\nu}\Bigr)\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle{\cal L_{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\mathcal{L}}{\mathcal{S}}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{\cal S}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}}}\sinh\gamma

(4)

\displaystyle-\cosh\gamma\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle{\cal L_{P}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\mathcal{L}}{\mathcal{P}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\cal P}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}^{2}+\mathcal{P}^{2}}}\sinh\gamma\,,

(5)

the field equations are simply written as

d*E=0\,,\quad dF=0\,,

(6)

and are invariant under the
SO(2)
duality rotations

\begin{pmatrix}E^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\\
*F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}E_{\mu\nu}\\
*F_{\mu\nu}\end{pmatrix}.

(7)

Similarly, under a conformal transformation of the metric,
g\to\Omega^{2}g
, we find

F\to F\,,\quad*F\to*F\,,

(8)

and since
{\cal S}\to\Omega^{-4}{\cal S},{\cal P}\to\Omega^{-4}{\cal P}
, and

{\cal L_{S}}\to{\cal L_{S}},{\cal L_{P}}\to{\cal L_{P}}
, we also have

E\to E\,,\quad*E\to*E\,,

(9)

and the field equations (
6
) are invariant
under conformal transformations.

The electromagnetic stress-energy tensor reads

T^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigl(2F^{\mu\sigma}F^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}{\cal L_{S}}+{\cal P}{\cal L_{P}}g^{\mu\nu}-{\cal L}g^{\mu\nu}\Bigr)\,,

(10)

and, using the fact that

{\cal L_{S}S}+{\cal L_{P}P}={\cal L}\,,

(11)

it is obviously traceless,

T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=0\,.

Employing (
11
), the energy momentum tensor can be further recast as

T^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigl({\cal S}g^{\mu\nu}-2F^{\mu\sigma}F^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}\Bigr){\cal L_{S}}\,.

(12)

In what follows we shall calculate the
electric and magnetic charges inside a closed spacelike two-surface
\mathcal{S}
. These are simply given by

q_{e}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{S}}*E\,,\quad q_{m}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{S}}F\,.

(13)

These definitions respect the duality rotations of Eq. (
7
), in the sense that magnetic charges rotate to electric charges and vice-versa.

3
Charged Taub-NUT solution

Let us now couple the conformal electrodynamics
to gravity and construct the corresponding (spherical topology) charged Taub-NUT(-AdS) solution. We thus consider the following bulk action for the coupled theory with gravity:

I=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(R-2\Lambda\right)-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\differential^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}\,.

(14)

Here,
\Lambda
stands for the negative cosmological constant
\Lambda=-3/l^{2}
.

We seek the Taub-NUT solution characterized by a single metric function
f=f(r)
,

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-f\big(dt+2n\cos\theta d\phi\big)^{2}}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f}

(15)

\displaystyle\quad+(r^{2}+n^{2})\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta d\phi^{2}\right)\,,\

and the following gauge potential:

A=a(dt+2n\cos\theta d\phi)\,,

(16)

where the gauge function
a=a(r)
. In the above, we have denoted the NUT parameter by
n
, and chosen a symmetric distribution for Misner strings, which are now located on both the north-pole and south-pole axes.

With this ansatz we find the following expressions for

F
and
*F
:

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a^{\prime}dt\wedge dr+2na^{\prime}\cos\theta dr\wedge d\phi

(17)

\displaystyle-2na\sin\theta d\theta\wedge d\phi\,,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-}\frac{2na}{n^{2}+r^{2}}dt\wedge dr{+}\frac{4n^{2}a}{n^{2}+r^{2}}\cos\theta dr\wedge d\phi

(18)

\displaystyle{+}(n^{2}+r^{2})a^{\prime}\sin\theta d\theta\wedge d\phi\,.

These are independent of the metric function
f
on behalf of the fact that the determinant of the metric (
15
) simply reads
\sqrt{-g}=r^{2}+n^{2}
.
The two electromagnetic invariants take a rather elegant shape

{\cal S}=-{a^{\prime 2}}+\frac{4n^{2}a^{2}}{(n^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}\,,\quad{\cal P}={-}\frac{4naa^{\prime}}{n^{2}+r^{2}}\,,

(19)

and so do the material tensors

\displaystyle E\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!{a^{\prime}e^{\gamma}}dt\wedge dr{-}2na^{\prime}e^{\gamma}\cos\theta dr\wedge d\phi

(20)

\displaystyle\!\!\quad{+}2nae^{-\gamma}\sin\theta d\theta\wedge d\phi\,,

\displaystyle E\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!{\frac{2ane^{-\gamma}}{n^{2}+r^{2}}dt\wedge dr-\frac{4n^{2}ae^{-\gamma}}{n^{2}+r^{2}}\cos\theta dr\wedge d\phi}

(21)

\displaystyle\!\!\quad{-(n^{2}+r^{2})a^{\prime}e^{\gamma}\sin\theta}d\theta\wedge d\phi\,.

The only non-zero component of the field equation (
6
) then yields

\displaystyle-e^{\gamma}\Bigl[\left(n^{2}+r^{2}\right)a^{\prime\prime}+2ra^{\prime}\Bigr]-\frac{4e^{-\gamma}n^{2}a}{n^{2}+r^{2}}=0\,,

(22)

which when integrated gives

a=c_{1}\sin\left(2e^{-\gamma}\arctan\frac{r}{n}\right)+c_{2}\cos\left(2e^{-\gamma}\arctan\frac{r}{n}\right),

(23)

where the integration constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are related to the electric and magnetic charges of the black hole, as described below.

Having obtained the gauge potential, let us now turn towards the Einstein equations with the electromagnetic tensor given by (
12
). They yield the following equation for the metric function
f
:

\displaystyle\frac{3}{l^{2}}+\frac{f(n^{2}-r^{2})+(n^{2}+r^{2})(rf^{\prime}-1)}{(n^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}-e^{\gamma}a^{\prime 2}

\displaystyle-\frac{4e^{-\gamma}n^{2}a^{2}}{(n^{2}+r^{2})^{2}}=0\,,

(24)

giving the following solution:

f=\frac{4e^{-\gamma}n^{2}(c_{1}^{2}\!+\!c_{2}^{2})\!-\!2mr+r^{2}-n^{2}}{r^{2}+n^{2}}-\frac{3n^{4}-6n^{2}r^{2}-r^{4}}{l^{2}(r^{2}+n^{2})}\,,

(25)

where the integration constant
m
represents the asymptotic mass of the solution (as can be seen by a large
r
expansion).

The final step is to determine the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
in terms of the asymptotic electric and magnetic charges. To this purpose we employ formulae (
13
), choosing
S
to be a sphere of radius
r
. Similar to the Maxwell case, the enclosed charges are radius dependent:

q_{e}=e^{\gamma}(n^{2}+r^{2})a^{\prime}\,,\quad q_{m}=2na\,,

(26)

reflecting the fact that the Misner strings carry electromagnetic charges. Evaluating these asymptotically, it is usual to define the asymptotic value of the electric and magnetic charges as the electric and magnetic parameters
e
and
g
,

\lim_{r\to\infty}q_{e}=e\,,\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}q_{m}=-2ng\,.

(27)

In terms of these, the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
above are determined as follows:

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=-g\cos\left(2e^{-\gamma}\pi\right)-\frac{e\cos(2 e^{-\gamma}\pi)}{2n}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=-g\sin\left(2e^{-\gamma}\pi\right)+\frac{e\sin(2 e^{-\gamma}\pi)}{2n}\,.

(29)

To summarize, the charged Taub-NUT(-AdS) solution constructed in this section takes the form (
15
), (
16
), where the metric and gauge functions are given by

f=\frac{e^{-\gamma}(e^{2}\!+\!4n^{2}g^{2})\!-\!2mr\!+\!r^{2}\!-\!n^{2}}{r^{2}+n^{2}}-\frac{3n^{4}\!-\!6n^{2}r^{2}\!-\!r^{4}}{l^{2}(r^{2}+n^{2})}\,,

(30)

and

\displaystyle a=

\displaystyle-g\cos\Bigl[e^{-\gamma}\left(\pi-2\arctan\frac{r}{n}\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{2n}\sin\Bigl[e^{-\gamma}\left(\pi-2\arctan\frac{r}{n}\right)\Bigr]\,.

(31)

To compare with the charged Taub-NUT solution in the Maxwell theory, we see that at the level of the metric function
f
, the effect of considering the conformal electrodynamics rather than the Maxwell theory corresponds to rescaling the electric and magnetic parameters by
e^{-\gamma/2}
.
To compare the gauge functions, we expand
a
as a power series in
\gamma

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\frac{r^{2}-n^{2}}{r^{2}+n^{2}}+\frac{er}{r^{2}+n^{2}}

(32)

\displaystyle+\gamma\left(\frac{e(r^{2}-n^{2})}{2n(n^{2}+r^{2})}-\frac{2gnr^{2}}{(n^{2}+r^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\times\Bigl(\pi-2\arctan\frac{r}{n}\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2})\,.

The leading term corresponds to the solution in the Maxwell theory, while the next term represents a non-linear effect that arises from the theory (
1
) to first-order in
\gamma
.

4.1
Basic thermodynamic quantities

To start our discussion we first turn to the black hole horizon. This is located at the largest root
r_{+}
of
f(r_{+})=0
. It is a Killing horizon generated by the Killing vector

k=\partial_{t}\,.

(33)

In what follows we identify the temperature of the spacetime with the black hole horizon temperature:

T=\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{+})}{4\pi}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{+}}\left(1+\frac{3(r^{2}_{+}+n^{2})}{l^{2}}-e^{-\gamma}\frac{e^{2}+4ng^{2}}{r_{+}^{2}+n^{2}}\right)\,,

(34)

and the entropy of the spacetime with the area of the black hole horizon:

S=\frac{\mbox{Area}}{4}=\pi(r_{+}^{2}+n^{2})\,.

(35)

In addition to the black hole horizon, there are two additional Killing horizons in the spacetime that are associated with the Misner strings. They are generated by the following Killing vectors:

k_{\pm}=\partial_{t}\mp\frac{1}{2n}\partial_{\phi}\,.

(36)

Contrary to the vector
k
, these Killing vectors are not properly normalized at infinity. In what follows we identify the associated surface gravity
\kappa_{\pm}
with the ‘Misner potential’
\psi

\psi=\frac{\kappa_{\pm}}{4\pi}=\frac{1}{8\pi n}\,.

(37)

Alternatively
[

10

\psi
can be attributed a meaning of the angular velocity of the string, c.f. (
36

\psi
the Misner charge and denote it by
N

Similar to the case of Maxwell electrodynamics, the
thermodynamic mass
M
is simply given by the parameter
m

M=m\,,

(38)

and the asymptotic angular momentum of the spacetime vanishes.

Since the Misner strings are charged, the electric and magnetic charges depend on the radius of the sphere and are given by (
26

One can easily check that they are related,
q_{e}\leftrightarrow q_{m}
, by the electromagnetic duality:

e\leftrightarrow-2ng\,,\quad 2ng\leftrightarrow e\,.

(39)

In particular, we have

Q=e\,,\quad Q_{m}=-2ng\,

(40)

for the asymptotic charges, and

\displaystyle Q^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{e}(r_{+})=e^{\gamma}(n^{2}+r_{+}^{2})a^{\prime}(r_{+})\,,

\displaystyle Q_{m}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{m}(r_{+})=2na(r_{+})\,

(41)

for the horizon charges.
The electrostatic potential
\phi
can be calculated by evaluating

in the horizon and subtracting its value at infinity,

\phi=-(\xi_{\mu}A^{\mu}\big|_{r=r_{+}}-\xi_{\mu}A^{\mu}\big|_{r=\infty})\,,

(42)

yielding

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a(r_{+})-g

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left(\cos\left(e^{-\gamma}\left(\pi-2\arctan\frac{r_{+}}{n}\right)\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{e}{2n}\sin\left(e^{-\gamma}\left(\pi-2\arctan\frac{r_{+}}{n}\right)\right)\,.

Upon using the electromagnetic duality (
39

\displaystyle\phi_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{2n}\left(\cos\left(e^{-\gamma}\left(\pi-2\arctan\frac{r_{+}}{n}\right)\right)-1\right)

(44)

\displaystyle-g\sin\left(e^{-\gamma}\left(\pi-2\arctan\frac{r_{+}}{n}\right)\right)\,.

[

we identify the cosmological constant with a dynamical pressure

(46)

Finally, we calculate the Gibbs free energy by evaluating the Euclidean action

\displaystyle{\cal I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{I}+I_{\mbox{\tiny GH}}+I_{\mbox{\tiny C}}

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}\differential^{4}x\sqrt{g}\left(R-2\Lambda\right)

4.2
Unconstrained thermodynamics

Being in the grandcanonical ensemble, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal G}(T,\psi,\phi,Q_{m}^{+},P)

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M-TS-\phi Q-\psi N\,,

and the Gibbs free energy satisfies

\delta G=-S\delta T-N\delta\psi-Q\delta\phi+\phi_{m}\delta Q_{m}^{+}+V\delta P\,.

(51)

These relations can be used to verify the above expressions for

and
\phi_{m}
, as well as to find the remaining missing thermodynamic quantities

V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r_{+}^{3}\left(1+\frac{3n^{2}}{r_{+}^{2}}\right)\,,

(52)

and is identical to the Maxwell case. The Misner charge
N
can be calculated either from (

N=-\frac{\partial G}{\partial\psi}\Bigl|_{T,\phi,Q_{m}^{+},P}\,.

(53)

In either case the full expression for

By construction the obtained thermodynamic quantities obey the full cohomogeneity first law:

\delta M=T\delta S+\psi\delta N+\phi\delta Q+\phi_{m}\delta Q_{m}^{+}+V\delta P\,,

(54)

as well as the corresponding Smarr relation

M=2TS+\phi Q+\phi_{m}Q_{m}^{+}+2\psi N-2PV\,.

(55)

\displaystyle{\cal G}(T,\psi,\phi,P)

Limit of vanishing NUT charge

5.1
Spherical solution

Following
[
7
]
let us now take the limit of a vanishing NUT charge. That is, we want to take the limit

n\to 0\,,

(63)

Q=e=\mbox{const.}\,,\quad Q_{m}=-2ng\equiv\hat{g}=\mbox{const.}

(64)

Obviously, in order to keep the latter charge constant as

g\to\infty

The limit of the metric simply reads

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-fdt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta d\phi^{2})\,,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2m}{r}+\frac{e^{-\gamma}(e^{2}+\hat{g}^{2})}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\,.

(65)

In order to take the limit of the gauge potential
A
, one has to first ‘renormalize’ the gauge potential by adding a pure gauge,
A\to A+gdt
, upon which it is straightforward to take the limit and obtain

A=-e^{-\gamma}\frac{e}{r}dt+\hat{g}\cos\theta d\phi\,.

(66)

The solution given by (
5.1
) and (
66
) represents the generalization of the (dyonic) charged AdS black hole to the case of the conformal electrodynamics. The thermodynamics of this solution follows from the thermodynamics of the Taub-NUT(-AdS) case discussed in the previous section.
In particular, from (
4.1
) we obtain the following Gibbs free energy

\mathcal{G}=\frac{m}{2}-e^{-\gamma}\frac{e^{2}-\hat{g}^{2}}{2r_{+}}-\frac{r_{+}^{3}}{2l^{2}}\,.

(67)

Since the Misner strings are no longer present, the horizon charges coincide with the asymptotic charges and the thermodynamics significantly simplifies.

6
Conclusion

We have obtained an exact axially symmetric black hole solution for Einstein gravity coupled to a maximally symmetric non-linear electromagnetic theory (
1
).
While the black hole metric calculated above is identical to the Einstein–Maxwell case, except for the rescaling of the charges, a salient feature of the solution is that the electric and magnetic fields in this spacetime do not correspond to simple rescalings. As a consequence, it is in principle possible to detect any deviations from linear electrodynamics by placing charged test particles in a curved spacetime. Whether this observed behaviour for the solutions is exclusive to Taub-NUT class or is a general feature for other spacetimes in this theory remains to be explored.

Moreover, we have extended the recent work on the thermodynamics of Lorentzian NUTs to this novel theory of electromagnetism, hence adding to the program of rehabilitating these spacetimes. Future work in these lines could aim towards studying the impact of non-linearity of the electromagnetic field on phase trasitions of the Taub-NUT solutions, as well as exploring the thermodynamics of other exact black hole solutions in this theory.
When calculating the Euclidean action, we have observed a very interesting feature: the Euclidean Wick-rotated solution no longer solves the equations of motion of the original non-linear electrodynamics Lagrangian (
1
) but rather the modified Lagrangian (
48
), obtained from (
1
) by replacing the invariant
P^{2}
with
-P^{2}
. We expect this to be a general feature of any non-linear theory of electrodynamics.

Finally, we have discussed the Euclidean self-dual solution (
5.2
), (
70
), emphasizing that the proper duality condition to impose is (
69
) instead of (
71
), as customary in linear Maxwell theory.

Abstract
. A subset S S of a transitive permutation group G ≤ Sym ⁡ ( n ) G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(n) is said to be an intersecting set if, for every g 1 , g 2 ∈ S g_{1},g_{2}\in S , there is an i ∈ [ n ] i\in[n] such that g 1 ​ ( i ) = g 2 ​ ( i ) g_{1}(i)=g_{2}(i) . The stabilizer of a point in [ n ] [n] and its cosets are intersecting sets of size | G | / n |G|/n . Such families are referred to as canonical intersecting sets. A result by Meagher, Spiga, and Tiep states that if G G is a 2 2 -transitive group, then | G | / n |G|/n is the size of an intersecting set of maximum size in G G . In some 2 2 -transitive groups (for instance Sym ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sym}(n) , Alt ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Alt}(n) ), every intersecting set of maximum possible size is canonical. A permutation group, in which every intersecting family of maximum possible size is canonical, is said to satisfy the strict-EKR property. In this article, we investigate the structure of intersecting sets in 3 3 -transitive groups. A conjecture by Meagher and Spiga states that all 3 3 -transitive groups satisfy the strict-EKR property. Meagher and Spiga showed that this is true for the 3 3 -transitive group PGL ⁡ ( 2 , q ) \mathrm{PGL}(2,q) . Using the classification of 3 3 -transitive groups and some results in literature, the conjecture reduces to showing that the 3 3 -transitive group AGL ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AGL}(n,2) satisfies the strict-EKR property. We show that AGL ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AGL}(n,2) satisfies the strict-EKR property and as a consequence, we prove Meagher and Spiga’s conjecture. We also prove a stronger result for AGL ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AGL}(n,2) by showing that “large” intersecting sets in AGL ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AGL}(n,2) must be a subset of a canonical intersecting set. This phenomenon is called stability.

1.
Introduction

Let
n
and
k
be positive integers such that
n<2k
. A family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-subsets in
[n]
is said to be intersecting if the intersection of any two elements of
\mathcal{F}
is non-empty. Given
i\in[n]
, the family

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{S\subset[n]:\ |S|=k\ \&\ i\in S\},

is a canonical example of an intersecting family of size
\binom{n-1}{k-1}
. The celebrated Erdős–Ko–Rado(EKR) Theorem (
[
10
]
) states that if
\mathcal{F}
is an intersecting family, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n-1}{k-1}
; moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{i}
, for some
i\in[n]
.

In this article, we consider EKR-type problems for permutation groups.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
be a finite permutation group. Two permutations
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
are said to be intersecting if and only if
g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}
fixes a point in
\Omega
. A subset
S\subseteq G
is said to be an
intersecting set
if any pair of permutations in
S
are intersecting. Given
\alpha,\ \beta\in\Omega
, the set
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\to\beta}=\{g\in G\ :\ g(\alpha)=\beta\}
is a canonically occurring intersecting set of size
|G|/|\Omega|
. Such intersecting sets are referred to as
canonical intersecting sets
. By a
maximum intersecting set
, we mean an intersecting set of maximum possible size. As in the case of the classical EKR Theorem, the following two questions are of interest:

(I) What is the size of a maximum intersecting set in
G
?

(II) What is the structure of maximum intersecting sets in
G
?

Extensive research has been done to answer these questions for various infinite families of permutation groups. A permutation group is said to satisfy the
EKR
property, if its canonical intersecting sets are also maximum intersecting sets. A permutation group is said to satisfy the
strict-EKR
property, if every maximum intersecting set is canonical.

The groups
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
(see
[
5
]
and
[
11
]
),
\mathrm{Alt}(n)
(see
[
1
]
),
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)
(see
[
18
]
), and
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)
(see
[
16
]
) satisfy the strict-EKR property. In
[
17
]
and
[
19
]
the EKR properties of
2
-transitive groups were investigated. They showed that all
2
-transitive groups satisfy the EKR property and another property known as the EKR-module property. Given a transitive permutation group
G
of degree
n
, let
U_{G}
be the
\mathbb{C}
-linear span of the indicator functions of the canonical intersecting sets in
G
. The permutation group
G
is said to satisfy the EKR-module property if
\mathbf{1}_{S}\in U_{G}
(here
\mathbf{1}_{S}
is the indicator function of
S
in
G
) for every maximum intersecting set
S
. There are many
2
-transitive groups that do not satisfy the strict-EKR property. For instance, when

, it was show in
[
23
]
, that, a maximum intersecting set in
\mathrm{PGL}(n,q)
, is either a coset of the stabilizer of a point or a coset of the stabilizer of a hyperplane.

Although not all
2
-transitive groups satisfy the strict-EKR property, in
[
18
]
, the authors conjecture that every
3
-transitive groups satisfy the strict-EKR property. As mentioned above, the

-transitive groups
\mathrm{Alt}(n)

satisfy the strict-EKR property. In this paper, we consider another

-transitive permutation group,
\mathrm{AGL}(n,2)
, with its natural action on the

points of the

-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{F}^{n}_{2}
.

\mathrm{AGL}(n,2)

2.
Notation and Background.

In this section, we establish some notation and collect some useful results from character theory and algebraic graph theory. We first start by describing the group
\mathrm{AGL}(n,q)
.

2.1.
The group
\mathrm{AGL}(n,q)

Let
q
be a power of a prime, and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the field with
q
elements. Let
V
denote the
n
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q}
of
n
-columns with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By
(\mathbf{e}_{1},\ \ldots,\ \mathbf{e}_{n})
, we denote the standard ordered basis of
V
.
By the group
\mathrm{AGL}(n,2)
, we mean the semidirect product
\mathrm{GL}(n,q)\ltimes V
. Elements of
\mathrm{AGL}(n,2)
are ordered pairs of the form
(M,\ v)
with
M\in\mathrm{GL}(n,q)
and

. Given
(M_{1},\ v_{1})
and
(M_{2},\ v_{2})
in
\mathrm{AGL}(n,q)
, we have
(M_{1},v_{1})\cdot(M_{2},\ v_{2})=(M_{1}M_{2},v_{1}+M_{1}v_{2})
. The natural action of

on

(M,\ v)\cdot w=v+Mw,

for all
(M,\ v)\in\mathrm{AGL}(n,q)
and

. This action is
2
-transitive for all

and

-transitive when

.

2.2.
The Module Method.

We now describe the Module Method: a sufficient condition for a
2
-transitive group to satisfy the strict-EKR property. This was discovered by Ahmedi and Meagher in
[
2
]
. In the same paper, they use this result to prove strict-EKR property of some
2
-transitive groups. This module method was also used in
[
18
]
and
[
16
]
.
Let
G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(n)
be a
2
-transitive permutation group. By a

in
G
, we mean a fixed-point-free permutation in
G
. Let
\mathfrak{Der}(G)
be the set of derangements in

. By

, we denote the set of ordered pairs of distinct elements in
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. We define the

of

to be the matrix indexed by
\mathfrak{Der}(G)\times[n]^{(2)}
with

M(G)_{d,\ (a,b))}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $d(a)=b$ and}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The following result–known as the Module Method–is essentially Theorem 4.5 of
[
2
]
. The main result of
[
17
]
and
[
19
]
show that conditions (a) and (b) of Theorem 4.5 of
[

]
are true for all
2
-transitive groups. Taking these results into account, we take the liberty of restating the Module Method as follows:

Theorem 6
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(n)
be a
2
-transitive permutation group and let
M(G)
be its derangement matrix. If
\mathrm{rank}(M(G))=(n-1)(n-2)
, then
G
satisfies the strict-EKR property.

Given a group
G
, by
\mathbb{C}[G]
, we denote its group algebra. Let
\Omega
be a
G
-set. Let
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
be the
|\Omega|
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space generated by
\{\Upsilon_{\omega}:\ \omega\in\Omega\}
as a basis. The action of
G
on
\Omega
leads to a realization of
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
as a
G
-module. Given
S\subseteq\Omega
, we use
\Upsilon_{S}
to denote
\sum\limits_{\omega\in S}\Upsilon_{\omega}
. The group
G
acts on both

and on
\mathfrak{Der}(G)
(via conjugation). The matrix

can be considered as a matrix representation of some

is an irreducible submodule of

or
\mathfrak{M}(U)\cong\{0\}
. So, characterizing irreducible submodules which are “annihilated” by

will give us the rank of

. We prove Theorem

using the module method.

2.3.
Delsarte–Hoffman ratio bound and stability.

The investigation of intersecting sets in a permutation group can be transferred to that of independent sets in a certain Cayley graph associated with the permutation group, called the derangement graph. Given a permutation group
G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(n)
with
\mathfrak{Der}(G)
as the set of derangements in
G
, the derangement graph
\Gamma_{G}
is the Cayley graph on
G
with
\mathfrak{Der}(G)
as its “connection” set.
It is elementary to observe that a set
\mathcal{S}\subset G
is intersecting if and only if
S
is an independent set in
\Gamma_{G}
. This identification of intersecting sets with independent sets empowers us to use spectral graph theoretic results. This methodology has proved useful in characterization of intersecting sets in many groups, for instance, see
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
18
]
.

We now describe a well-known spectral bound on independence number of regular graphs. We first establish some notation. Given a graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
, by
\mathbb{C}^{V}
, we denote the set of
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on the vertex set

. The adjacency matrix of
\Gamma

\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{V}\right)

\Gamma
, we mean an eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix. Given an eigenvalue
\zeta
of
\Gamma
, by
V_{\zeta}
, we denote the
\zeta
-eigenspace in
\mathbb{C}^{V}
. Given a subset

, by
\mathbf{1}_{S}
, we denote the indicator function of
S
as a subset of

. The following result–known as the Delsarte–Hoffman ratio bound–gives an upper bound on the independence number of a regular graph. The statement we provide is
[

, Theorem 2.4.2]
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a
k
regular graph on
v

\lambda
is the least eigenvalue of
\Gamma
, then for any independent set

, we have

|S|\leq\dfrac{v}{1-\dfrac{k}{\lambda}},

and if equality holds, then

\mathbf{1}_{S}\in V_{k}+V_{\lambda}.

The following is a useful corollary of the ratio bound.

Corollary 8

(Corollary 2.4.3 of
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a
k
regular graph on
v
vertices with
\lambda
as its least eigenvalue. If
S
is an independent set in
X
such that
|S|=\dfrac{v}{1-\dfrac{k}{\lambda}}
, then each vertex not in
S
has exactly
-\lambda

S
.

The vector space
\mathbb{C}^{V}
comes with a natural inner product whose norm
||\cdot||
satisfies

||f||^{2}=\frac{\sum\limits_{x\in V}f(x)\overline{f(x)}}{|V|},

for all
f\in\mathbb{C}^{V}
. Ellis (see
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
) provided the following generalization of the ratio bound.

Theorem 9

(Ellis)
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a
k
regular graph on
v

be its eigenvalues and let

. Let
\mu=\mathrm{min}\{\lambda_{i}\ :\ \lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{k}\}
. If

is an independent set in
\Gamma
with

, then

||\mathbf{1}_{S}-\operatorname{Proj}_{U}(\mathbf{1}_{S})||^{2}\leq\frac{c|\lambda_{v}|-c^{2}(k-\lambda_{v})}{|\lambda_{v}|-|\mu|}.

Roughly speaking, the above result shows that if

is a “large” independent set in

, then

is “close” (in Euclidean sense) to the space

. We use this to prove Theorem
5
. We finish this section, by mentioning a result that helps us compute the eigenvalues of derangement graphs.

We now recall some results on eigenvalues and eigenspaces of

Further Work.

We end with an open problem for future research. Theorem
2
gives a complete characterization of intersecting sets in the class of
3
-transitive groups. Theorem
4

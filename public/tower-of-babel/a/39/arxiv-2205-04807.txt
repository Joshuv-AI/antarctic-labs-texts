Abstract

In this paper we collect results concerning the
operator-norm
convergent

Trotter
product formula for two semigroups
\{{\rm e\,}^{-tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
,

\{{\rm e\,}^{-tB}\}_{t\geq 0}
, with densely defined generators
A
and
B
in a
Banach
space.
Although the
strong
convergence in Banach space for contraction semigroups is known
since the seminal paper by Trotter (1959),
which after more than three decades was extended to convergence in the
operator-norm

topology in
Hilbert
spaces by Rogava (1993),
the operator-norm convergence in a
Banach
space was established only in (2001).

For the first time this result was established under hypothesis that one of the involved
into the product formula contraction semigroups, e.g.
\{{\rm e\,}^{-tA}\}_{t\geq 0}\,
, is

holomorphic
together with certain conditions of
smallness
on generators
B
and

B^{*}
with respect to generators
A
and
A^{*}
.
Note that in spite of a quite strong assumptions on operators
A
and
B
the proof of the
operator-norm convergent Trotter product formula on a Banach space is (unexpectedly) involved
and technical.

To elucidate the question of how far these conditions are from optimal ones we
show an Example of the
operator-norm
convergent Trotter
product formula for two semigroups
\{{\rm e\,}^{-tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{{\rm e\,}^{-tB}\}_{t\geq 0}
on a
Banach space, where hypothesis on operator
A
is relaxed to condition that
A
is generator of
a
contraction
semigroup.

1
Preliminaries

1.1
Bounded semigroups on
\mathfrak{X}

For what follows the properties of holomorphic (contraction) semigroups on a Banach space

\mathfrak{X}
are essential. Therefore, we start by a suitable for our aim recall of details
concerning the bounded,
holomorphic semigroups, and fractional powers of their generators. We begin with definitions and
properties to introduce certain notations adapted in this section for semigroups on
\mathfrak{X}
.

Definition 1.1

We would remind that a family
\{U(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of bounded linear operators on a Banach
space
\mathfrak{X}
is called a one-parameter strongly continuous semigroup if it satisfies the
conditions:

(i)

U(0)={1}
,

(ii)

U(s+t)=U(s)U(t)
for all
s,t\geq 0
,

(iii)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+0}U(t)x=x
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{X}}
.

We recall some immediate consequences of this definition:

•

There are constants
C_{A}\geq 1
and
\gamma_{A}\in{{\mathbb{R}}}
, depending on the generator of the
semigroup,
such that

\|U(t)\|\leq C_{A}{\rm e\,}^{\gamma_{A}t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

•

t\mapsto U(t)
is a strongly continuous function from
[0,+\infty)
onto the algebra

\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{X})
of bounded linear operators on
\mathfrak{X}
.

•

There exists a closed densely defined linear operator
A
on
\mathfrak{X}
with domain

{\rm{dom}}(A)
, called the generator
of the semigroup, such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+0}(U(t)x-x)/t=-Ax
for any
x\in{\rm{dom}}(A)
,
that is, by convention
U(t):={\rm e\,}^{-tA}
.

•

The resolvent of the generator satisfies the estimate

\|R_{A}(-\lambda)\|

=

\|(A+\lambda\mathds{1})^{-1}\|

\leq

C_{A}/(\mbox{Re}(\lambda)-\gamma_{A})
for
all
\lambda
with

\mbox{Re}(\lambda)>\gamma_{A}
, thus the open half plane with
\mbox{Re}(z)<-\gamma_{A}
is
contained into the resolvent set of
A
, which is
defined as
\rho(A)=\{z\in{{\mathbb{C}}}:\|R_{A}(z)\|<+\infty\}
.

•

If
\gamma_{A}\leq 0
,
U(t)
is called a bounded semigroup (otherwise,
U(t)

is called a quasi-bounded semigroup of type
\gamma_{A}>0
). For any strongly continuous
semigroup, we can construct a bounded semigroup by adding some constant

\eta\geq\gamma_{A}
to its generator. Let
\tilde{A}=A+\eta\mathds{1}
, then for the semigroup

\tilde{Q}(t)
generated by
\tilde{A}
, one has
\|\tilde{Q}(t)\|\leq C_{A}
,
t\geq 0
,
and the open half-plane
\mbox{Re}(\lambda)<\eta-\gamma_{A}
is included into the
resolvent set
\rho(\tilde{A})
of
\tilde{A}
. So it is not restrictive to suppose
that the considered semigroup
U(t)
is bounded and that the set
\{z\in{{\mathbb{C}}}:\mbox{ Re}(z)\leq 0\}\subseteq\rho(A)
.

•

If
\|U(t)\|\leq 1
,
t\geq 0
, the semigroup is called a contraction semigroup.
We comment that the method of the preceding remark does not permit to construct a
contraction semigroup from a bounded semigroup in general, since it can not change the value of the
constant
C_{A}
.

Below we need a characterization of generators of these contraction semigroups. First we
recall that the space of linear bounded functionals

{\mathfrak{X}}^{*}=\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{X},\mathbb{C})
is a
dual
of the
Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
and that
{\mathfrak{X}}^{*}
is itself a Banach space.
Recall that a linear operator
A
in
\mathfrak{X}
is accretive if for all pairs

(u,\phi)\in{\rm{dom}}(A)\times{\mathfrak{X}}^{*}
with
\|u\|_{\mathfrak{X}}=1,\ \|\phi\|_{\mathfrak{X}^{*}}=1,\ (u,\phi)=1
, one has
\mbox{Re}\,(Au,\phi)\geq 0
.
We also add that
a densely defined in
\mathfrak{X}
accretive operator
A
is generator of contraction semigroup
if the range of
\lambda\mathds{1}+A
is
\mathfrak{X}
for some
\lambda>0
,

Now we prove a series of estimates indispensable throughout this paper.

Lemma 1.2

Let
U(t)
be a bounded semigroup with boundedly invertible generator
A
, then for all
t\geq 0
,
and for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have:

\left(U(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\frac{(-tA)^{k}}{k!}}\right)A^{-n-1}=-\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,\left(U(\tau)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\frac{(-\tau A)^{k}}{k!}}\right)A^{-n}\,,

(1.1)

\left\|\,\left(U(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\frac{(-tA)^{k}}{k!}}\right)A^{-n-1}\right\|\ \leq\ C_{A}{\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}}.

(1.2)

Proof

We proceed by induction, and we first prove that

(U(t)-\mathds{1})x=-\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,U(\tau)\ A\,x\ ,\ \ x\in{\rm{dom}}(A).

(1.3)

Note that for any
\epsilon>0
the semigroup properties yields the representation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\,U(s)\,{U(\epsilon)-\frac{\mathds{1}}{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\,{U(s+\epsilon)-\frac{U(s)}{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+\epsilon}ds\,{\frac{U(s)}{\epsilon}}-\int_{0}^{\epsilon}ds\,{\frac{U(s)}{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(U(t)-\mathds{1})\,{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon}ds\,U(s)\,.

Moreover, one also gets:

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon}ds\ U(s)\ x=x\ ,\ \ \ x\in{\mathfrak{X}}\ ,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{U(\epsilon)-\mathds{1}}{\epsilon}\,x=-\,A\,x\ ,\ \ \ x\in{\rm{dom}}(A)\,.

This proves (
1.3
), and since
A
is boundedly invertible, we obtain (
1.1
)
for
n=0
.
Furthermore,
since
U(t)
is bounded by
C_{A}
, we obtain the estimate (
1.2
) for
n=0
.

Suppose that (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are true for some
n
, then a
straightforward calculation
leads to
(
1.1
) for
n+1
.
Hence, using the representation (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for
n
to estimate the
integrand, we obtain (
1.2
) for
n+1
, which completes the proof by induction.

\square

Similarly, we obtain a representation for a restricted development of
(\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}
.

Lemma 1.3

Let
A
be as in Lemma
1.2
. Then for any
n\geq 0
:

(\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}A^{-n-1}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-A)^{k}\right)A^{-n-1}+(-1)^{n+1}(\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}.

(1.4)

Proof

For
n=0
, the representation (
1.4
) follows from the resolvent formula:

(\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}-A^{-1}=-(\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}A^{-1}.

(1.5)

Suppose that (
1.4
) holds for an integer
n>1
, then:

(\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}A^{-n-2}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-A)^{k}\right)A^{-n-2}+(-1)^{n+1}(\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}A^{-1}.

(1.6)

Applying (
1.5
) to the last term of (
1.6
) we get the representation
(
1.4
) for

n+1
,
and thus for any
n
by induction.

\square

Lemma 1.4

If
U(t)
is a bounded semigroup with boundedly invertible generator
A
then :

\left\|{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}\left((\mathds{1}+t\,A)^{-1}-U(t)\right)A^{-2}\right\|\ \leq\ 3C_{A}/2\ ,\ \quad t>0.

(1.7)

Proof

By Lemma
1.2
one gets

\|(U(t)-\mathds{1}+t\,A)\ \frac{1}{t^{2}}\,A^{-2}\|\ \leq\ {\frac{C_{A}}{2}}\ .

(1.8)

On the other hand by Lemma
1.3
, we have

\left\|\left((\mathds{1}+t\,A)^{-1}-\mathds{1}+t\,A\right)\ \frac{1}{t^{2}}\,A^{-2}\right\|\ =\ \|(\mathds{1}+t\,A)^{-1}\|\ \leq\ C_{A}\ .

(1.9)

Here the last estimate follows from
(\mathds{1}+t\,A)^{-1}=(1/t)\,R_{A}(-1/t)
and

\|R_{A}(-\lambda)\|\leq C_{A}/(\lambda+\delta)
,

\delta\geq 0
, which is valid for bounded semigroups with boundedly invertible generators.
Hence (
1.7
) follows from
(
1.8
) and (
1.9
).

\square

1.2
Holomorphic contraction semigroups on
\mathfrak{X}\ \ \

Now let
U:z\mapsto U(z)
be a family of operators with
z
taking their values in the sector
of the complex plane:

S_{\theta}=\left\{z\in{{\mathbb{C}}}:\ z\neq 0\mbox{ and }|\arg(z)|<\theta\right\}

(1.10)

where
0<\theta\leq\pi/2
.

Definition 1.5

Recall that the family of operators
\{U(z)\}_{z\in S_{\theta}}
is a bounded holomorphic
semigroup of semi-angle
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
on a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
if it satisfies
the following conditions:

(i)

If
0<\epsilon<\theta
, then
\|U(z)\|\leq M_{\epsilon}
for all
z\in S_{\theta-\epsilon}
and
some
M_{\epsilon}<\infty
.

(ii)

U(z_{1})U(z_{2})=U(z_{1}+z_{2})
for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in S_{\theta}
.

(iii)

U:z\mapsto U(z)
is analytic function of
z\in S_{\theta}
.

(iv)

If
x\in{\mathfrak{X}}
and
0<\epsilon<\theta
, then
\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}U(z)\,x=x

provided
z\in S_{\theta-\epsilon}
.

Let
\sigma(A)={{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\rho(A)
denote the spectrum of
A
. It can be used for the following
characterisation of the holomorphic semigroup generators,
Kato 95
, Chapter IX:

Proposition 1.6

Operator
A
in a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
is the generator of a bounded holomorphic semigroup
of semi-angle
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
if and only if
A
is a closed operator with a dense domain

{\rm{dom}}(A)
such that:

\sigma(A)\subseteq\left\{z\in{{\mathbb{C}}},\ |\arg(z)|\leq{\pi\over 2}-\theta\right\}\ ,\quad 0<\theta\leq{\frac{\pi}{2}}\ ,

and

\|(z\,\mathds{1}+A)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{N_{\epsilon}}{|z|}\ \quad{\rm{for}}\ \quad N_{\epsilon}>0\ ,\ \ z\in S_{\theta+\pi/2-\epsilon}\ \ .

where
0<\epsilon<\theta
.

For applications it is also useful the following property, which is an alternative characterisation
of these kind of semigroups.

Proposition 1.7

If
U(z)
is a bounded holomorphic semigroup of semi-angle
\theta
with generator
A
, then for
all
z\in S_{\theta}

and
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
one has
U(z){\mathfrak{X}}\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(A^{n})
. Moreover, there are positive
constants
C_{A}^{\prime}
,
C_{A}^{(n)}
such that for
t>0

:

\left\|\frac{dU(t)}{dt}\right\|=\|AU(t)\|\leq\frac{C_{A}^{\prime}}{t}\ \ \ {\rm{and}}\ \ \ \left\|\frac{d^{n}U(t)}{dt^{n}}\right\|=\left\|A^{n}U(t)\right\|\leq\frac{C_{A}^{(n)}}{t^{n}}\ .

(1.11)

Let
0<\theta<\pi/2
. Then estimates
(
1.11
)
are valid for complex argument

z\in S_{\theta}
with constants depending on
\theta
.

Remark 1.8

Similarly to strongly continuous semigroups, a family
U(z)
,
z\in S_{\theta}
is called a

quasi-bounded
holomorphic semigroup of semi-angle
\theta
if there exists a constant

\beta>0
such that
restriction of
\{{\rm e\,}^{-\beta\,z}\,U(z)\}_{z\in S_{\theta}}
to

{{\mathbb{R}}}^{+}_{0}
is a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup.

The class of semigroups that we consider here is restricted to
holomorphic

contraction
semigroups.

To this aim we recall definition of this notion below,
Kato 95
, Chapter IX.

Definition 1.9

We say that
\{U_{A}(z)\}_{z\in S_{\theta}}
, is a
holomorphic contraction
semigroup
with generator
A\in\mathscr{H}_{c}(\theta,0)
,
if its restriction

\{U_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
to
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{+}_{0}
is a contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup, that is

\mathscr{H}_{c}(\theta,0):=\mathscr{H}(\theta,0)\cap\mathscr{G}(1,0)
.

Note that this class of semigroups is
not
empty and corresponding generators have the
following properties:

(i) Let
\{U_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, be a
contraction
semigroup with generator

A\in\mathscr{G}(1,0)
in a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
, such that

U_{A}(t){\mathfrak{X}}\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(A)
for
t>0
. If
\|AU(t)\|\leq M_{1}\,t^{-1}
for some

M_{1}>0
and all
t>0
, then there exists
\theta=\arcsin\,(eM_{1})^{-1}{(<\pi/2)}

such that
U_{A}(t)
may be analytically continued to contraction holomorphic semigroup
of semi-angle
\theta
.

(ii) Let
A
be a sectorial operator in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
, i.e. its numerical range

W=\{(Au,u):u\in{\rm{dom}}(A)\mbox{ and }\|u\|=1\}\subset S_{\pi/2-\theta}
for

0<\theta\leq\pi/2
.
If the operator
A
is closed, then it is generator of the holomorphic contraction semigroup of
semi-angle
\theta
.

(iii) Let
A
be a generator of holomorphic semigroup on a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
. If
A
is
accretive, then
A
generates a holomorphic contraction semigroup.

(iv)
If
A
is the generator of a strongly continuous
group

\{U_{A}(t)\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

of contractions
\|U_{A}(t)\|\leq 1
, then
\pm A\in\mathscr{G}(1,0)
, and

A^{2}\in\mathscr{H}_{c}(\pi/2,0)

EN 00

1.3
Fractional powers of generators

We scrutinise in this section some properties of fractional powers of the generators for
bounded semigroups in a Banach space, see, e.g.,
Yos 80
, Chapter IX.

Recall that fractional power
A^{\alpha}
,
0<\alpha<1
, of generator of a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup

U(t)
(
\|U(t)\|\leq C_{A}
) can be expressed by the integral (
when it is well-defined
):

A^{\alpha}x={1\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\lambda\,\lambda^{-\alpha-1}(U(\lambda)-\mathds{1})\ x\ ,\quad x\in{\rm dom\,}(A),

(1.12)

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma-function and
\lambda^{\alpha}
is chosen to be positive for

\lambda>0
. Since for any
x\in{\rm{dom}}(A)
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
the integral (
1.12
)
is convergent,
{\rm{dom}}(A)\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha})
. We set
A^{0}:=\mathds{1}
and define

A^{\alpha}=A^{\alpha-[\alpha]}A^{[\alpha]}
for any
\alpha>0\
, where

[\alpha]
denotes the
integer
part of
\alpha
,

Proposition 1.10

For each
\alpha\in[0,1]
, there exists a constant
C_{A,\alpha}
, depending only on
C_{A}
and

\alpha
, such that for all
\mu>0
,

\left\|A^{\alpha}(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1}\right\|\leq{C_{A,\alpha}\over\mu^{1-\alpha}}.

(1.13)

Proof

For
\alpha=0
or
\alpha=1
, the result follows directly from the estimate of the resolvent.
Let
0<\alpha<1
and

x\in{\mathfrak{X}}
. Note that
\mbox{ran}(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1}={\rm{dom}}(A)\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha})
. Then

A^{\alpha}(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1}\ x={1\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \lambda^{-\alpha-1}(U(\lambda)-\mathds{1}))(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1}\,x\ .

(1.14)

We divide the integral (
1.14
) into two parts:
0<\lambda\leq\mu^{-1}
and

\lambda>\mu^{-1}
, and we use the
representation (
1.3
):

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\mu^{-1}}d\lambda\ \lambda^{-\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\lambda}dt\ (-U(t))(\mathds{1}-\mu(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1})x

\displaystyle+\ {1\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{\mu^{-1}}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \lambda^{-\alpha-1}(U(\lambda)-\mathds{1})(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1}\ x\ .

Now by the estimate of the resolvent
\|(A+\mu)^{-1}\|\leq C_{A}/\mu
for all
\mu>0
one obtains:

\displaystyle\|A^{\alpha}(A+\mu\mathds{1})^{-1}x\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{C_{A}(1+C_{A})\ \|x\|\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\left(\int_{0}^{\mu^{-1}}d\lambda\ \lambda^{-\alpha}+{1\over\mu}\int_{\mu^{-1}}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \lambda^{-\alpha-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{A}(1+C_{A})\mu^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\Gamma(-\alpha)}\ \|x\|.

Setting
C_{A,\alpha}:=C_{A}(1+C_{A})/(\alpha(1-\alpha)\Gamma(-\alpha))
we obtain the estimate
(
1.13
).

\square

A
:

Lemma 1.11

{\rm{dom}}((A+\delta\mathds{1})^{\alpha})={\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha})
for all
\delta>0
and
0<\alpha<1
.

Theorem 1.12

Let
U_{A}(t)
be a bounded holomorphic semigroup with generator
A
, then for any real
\alpha>0
,
we have

\sup_{t>0}\left\|t^{\alpha}A^{\alpha}U_{A}(t)\right\|=M_{\alpha}<\infty.

(1.15)

Proof

Let
0<\alpha<1
. By
{\rm{dom}}(A)\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha})
one gets

{\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha}U_{A}(t))={\mathfrak{X}}
.
Hence by (
1.12
) we have

A^{\alpha}U_{A}(t)={1\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\lambda\ \lambda^{-\alpha-1}(U_{A}(t+\lambda)-U_{A}(t))\ .

(1.16)

Now we split the integral (
1.16
) in two parts:
0<\lambda\leq t
and
\lambda>t
,
and we use the estimate of the derivative of the holomorphic semigroup
(see Proposition
1.7
) to obtain

\|U_{A}(t+\lambda)-U_{A}(t)\|\leq\lambda\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq t+\lambda}\|\,\partial_{\tau}U_{A}(\tau)\|\leq\lambda\ {C_{A}^{\prime}\over t}.

(1.17)

This leads to the estimate

\displaystyle\|A^{\alpha}U_{A}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{1\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}d\lambda\ \lambda^{-\alpha}\,{C_{A}^{\prime}\over t}+\int_{t}^{\infty}d\lambda\ 2\,C_{A}\,\lambda^{-\alpha-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{t^{-\alpha}\over\Gamma(-\alpha)}\left({C_{A}^{\prime}\over 1-\alpha}+{2\,C_{A}\over\alpha}\right).

Therefore one obtains (
1.15
) for
0<\alpha<1
by setting
M_{\alpha}:=\Gamma(-\alpha)^{-1}({C_{A}^{\prime}/(1-\alpha)}

+

{2\,C_{A}/\alpha})
.

For integer powers
\alpha
, (
1.15
) follows directly from Proposition
1.7
.
Notice that by Proposition
1.7

\mbox{ran}(U_{A}(t))\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(A^{n})
for
t>0
.
Then result
(
1.15
) follows for any non integer
\alpha>1
, from:
the observation that
{\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha}=A^{\alpha-[\alpha]}A^{[\alpha]})\supset{\rm{dom}}(A^{[\alpha]+1})
, the representation (
1.16
), and the estimate (
1.11
)
of derivatives of order

.

\square

2
Operator-norm Trotter product formula

2.1
Perturbation of holomorphic contraction semigroups

In this section we prove the operator-norm convergence of the Trotter product formula in Banach space

\mathfrak{X}
. Note that convergence of the abstract version of this formula in the strong
operator topology is known since
Trot 59
.
The proof goes via estimate of the rate of convergence in the case of
small

perturbations of the holomorphic contraction semigroups, cf.
CZ 01
.

We require that operator
A
generates a holomorphic contraction semigroup, and that
perturbation
B
satisfies the following hypothesis:

(H1)
B
is generator of a contraction semigroup on
\mathfrak{X}
.

(H2) There is a real
\alpha\in[\,0,1)
such that
{\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha})\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(B)

and that

{\rm{dom}}(A^{*})\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(B^{*})
adjoint operator in the dual space
\mathfrak{X}^{*}
.

Notice that we can suppose the operator
A
boundedly invertible. If it is not the case, one considers

A+\eta
for some
\eta>0
. Then
by Lemma
1.11
we have
{\rm{dom}}((A+\eta)^{\alpha})={\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha})\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(B)
.

Remark 2.1

We note that assumption (H2) implies that
B
is relatively bounded with respect to
A

with the relative bound equals to
zero
. Indeed, for
\eta>0
by

{\rm{dom}}(A+\eta\mathds{1})\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(A^{\alpha})\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(B)
and by
Proposition
1.10
, (
1.13
), one gets (here operator
A
is supposed to be
boundedly invertible):

\|B\,(A+\eta\mathds{1})^{-1}\|\leq\|BA^{-\alpha}\|\,\|A^{\alpha}(A+\eta\mathds{1})^{-1}\|\leq{C_{\alpha}\over\eta^{1-\alpha}}\,\|BA^{-\alpha}\|.

(2.1)

Since the operators
A^{\alpha}
and
B
are closed, the inclusions in (H2) are equivalent to

A^{\alpha}
-boundedness of

B
and the
A^{*}
-boundedness of
B^{*}
. In particular,
\|BA^{-\alpha}\|\leq d
and

\|B^{*}{A^{*}}^{-1}\|\leq d^{\prime}
for some
d,d^{\prime}>0
.
Therefore, for any
x\in{\rm{dom}}(A)\subseteq{\rm{dom}}(B)

3.1
Evolution semigroups and Trotter product formula

To proceed further we need some key notions from the
evolution semigroups
theory and
in particular the notion of
solution operator
.

A strongly continuous mapping
U(\cdot,\cdot):{\Delta}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})
,
where domain
{\Delta}:=\{(t,s):0<s\leq t\leq T\}
and
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})
is the set of bounded
operators on
separable
Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
, is called a

solution operator
if the conditions

\displaystyle{\rm{(i)}}\ \sup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\|U(t,s)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty\ ,

\displaystyle{\rm{(ii)}}\ U(t,s)=U(t,r)U(r,s)\,,\ \ 0<s\leq r\leq t\leq T\,,

are satisfied. Let us consider the Banach space
L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
for
{\mathcal{I}}:=[0,T]
and

p\in[1,\infty)
. The operator
{\mathcal{K}}
is an
evolution generator
of the evolution semigroup

\{{\mathcal{U}}({\tau}):={\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}\}_{\tau\geq 0}
if there is a solution operator such that the

Howland-Evans-Neidhardt
representation, see
How 74
,
Ev 76
,
Nei 79

and
Nei 81
:

({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}f)(t)=U(t,t-{\tau})\chi_{{\mathcal{I}}}(t-{\tau})f(t-{\tau}),\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})\ ,

(3.1)

holds for a.a.
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\tau}\geq 0
. Seeing that on account of (
3.1
) the
semigroup
\{{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}\}_{\tau\geq 0}
is
nilpotent
:
{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}f=0
for

{\tau}\geq T
, the evolution generator
{\mathcal{K}}
can never be generator of a holomorphic semigroup.

A simple example of an evolution generator is the differentiation operator, cf.
NSZ 20
:

\begin{split}(D_{0}f)(t)&:=\partial_{t}f(t),\\
f\in{\rm dom\,}(D_{0})&:=\{f\in W^{1,p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}):f(0)=0\}\,,\end{split}

(3.2)

where
W^{1,p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
is the
Sobolev
space of order
(1,p)
of
Bochner

p
-integrable functions.
Then by (
3.2
) one obviously gets the contraction shift semigroup:

({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}D_{0}}f)(t)=\chi_{{\mathcal{I}}}(t-{\tau})f(t-{\tau}),\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}),

(3.3)

for a.a.
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\tau}\geq 0
. Hence, (
3.1
) implies that the corresponding
solution operator of the
non-holomorphic
evolution semigroup
\{{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}D_{0}}\}_{\tau\geq 0}

is given by
U_{D_{0}}(t,s)={\mathds{1}}
, for all
(t,s)\in{\Delta}
.

Below we consider the operator
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}:=\overline{D_{0}+{\mathcal{A}}}\,
,
where
{\mathcal{A}}
is the
multiplication
operator induced by generator
A
of a

holomorphic contraction
semigroup on
\mathfrak{X}
. More precisely

\begin{split}({\mathcal{A}}f)(t)&:=Af(t),\ {\rm{and}}\ ({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{A}}}f)(t)={\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}A}f(t)\ ,\\
f\in{\rm dom\,}({\mathcal{A}})&:=\{f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}):Af\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})\}\ .\end{split}

Then the perturbation of the shift semigroup (
3.3
) by
{\mathcal{A}}
corresponds to the semigroup
with generator
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
. One easily checks that
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
is an evolution generator of a contraction
semigroup on
L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
, that is never holomorphic
NSZ 20
. Indeed, since the
generators
D_{0}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
commute, the representation (
3.1
) for evolution semigroup

\{{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}\}_{\tau\geq 0}
takes the form:

({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}\mathcal{K}_{0}}f)(t)={\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}A}\chi_{{\mathcal{I}}}(t-{\tau})f(t-{\tau}),\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})\ ,

for a.a.
t\in{\mathcal{I}}=[0,T]
and
{\tau}\geq 0
. Then by (
3.1
) the
solution operator
U_{0}(t,s)={\rm e\,}^{-(t-s)A}\,
.
Therefore,
{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}f=0
for
{\tau}\geq T
, that is, semigroup

\{{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}\}_{\tau\geq 0}
is
nilpotent
.

Furthermore, if
\{B(t)\}_{t\in{\mathcal{I}}}
is a
strongly measurable
family of generators of
contraction semigroups on
\mathfrak{X}
, that is,
B(\cdot):{\mathcal{I}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}(1,0)
, then the
induced
multiplication
operator
{\mathcal{B}}
:

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}f)(t)

\displaystyle:=B(t)f(t)\ ,

(3.4)

\displaystyle f\in{\rm dom\,}({\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}):\!\!\!\!\begin{matrix}\ \ &\ \ f(t)\in{\rm dom\,}(B(t))\ \ \ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t\in{\mathcal{I}}\\
&B(t)f(t)\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})\end{matrix}\right\}\,,

is a generator of a contraction semigroup on
L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
.

In the next Subsection
3.2
we consider perturbation of generator
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}

by multiplication operator
{\mathcal{B}}
(
3.4
). Thereupon we construct by means of the

Trotter
product formula approach a corresponding perturbed semigroup.

3.2
Trotter product formula

We reminisce (cf. Subsection

) that semigroup
\{{\mathcal{U}}({\tau})\}_{{\tau}\geq 0}
, on the
Banach space
L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
is called the
evolution semigroup
if there is a

solution operator
:
\{U(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}
, such that representation (
3.1
)
holds.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
be the generator of an evolution semigroup
\{{\mathcal{U}}_{0}({\tau})\}_{{\tau}\geq 0}
and let
{\mathcal{B}}

be a multiplication operator induced by a measurable family
\{B(t)\}_{t\in{\mathcal{I}}}
of generators of
contraction semigroups. Note that in this case the multiplication operator
{\mathcal{B}}
(
3.4
) is a
generator of a contraction semigroup
({\rm e\,}^{-\tau\,{\mathcal{B}}}f)(t)={\rm e\,}^{-\tau\,B(t)}f(t)
,
on the Banach space
L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
. Since
\{{\mathcal{U}}_{0}({\tau})\}_{{\tau}\geq 0}
is an evolution
semigroup, then by definition (
3.1
) there is a propagator
\{U_{0}(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}

such that the representation

({\mathcal{U}}_{0}({\tau})f)(t)=U_{0}(t,t-{\tau})\ \chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t-{\tau})f(t-{\tau}),\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}),

is valid for a.a.
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\tau}\geq 0
. Then we define
\tau_{n}:=(t-s)/n
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
and

G_{j}(t,s;n):=U_{0}(s+j\,\tau_{n}\,,\ s+(j-1)\,\tau_{n})\ {\rm e\,}^{-\ \tau_{n}\,B\,\big(s\,+\,(j-1)\ \tau_{n}\big)}\,,\quad(t,s)\in{\Delta}\,,

where
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(t,s)\in{\Delta}
, and we set

V_{n}(t,s):=\prod^{1}_{j=n}G_{j}(t,s;n),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad(t,s)\in{\Delta}\,.

That is, the product is increasingly ordered in
j
from the right to the left.
Then a straightforward computation shows that the representation

\left(\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}/n}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}f\right)(t)=V_{n}(t,t-{\tau})\ \chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t-{\tau})f(t-{\tau})\ ,

(3.5)

f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
, holds for each
{\tau}\geq 0
and a.a.
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

Theorem 3.2

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
be generators of evolution semigroups on the Banach space

L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
for some

p\in[1,\infty)
. Further, let
\{B(t)\in{\mathcal{G}}(1,0)\}_{t\in{\mathcal{I}}}
be a strongly measurable family of
generators of contraction semigroups on
\mathfrak{X}
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\left\|{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}/n}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}))}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\|U(t,s)-V_{n}(t,s)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Proof

Let
\{L({\tau})\}_{{\tau}\geq 0}
be the left-shift semigroup on the Banach space
L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X})
:

(L({\tau})f)(t)=\chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t+{\tau})f(t+{\tau}),\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}).

Using that we get

\displaystyle\left(L({\tau})\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}/n{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right)f\right)(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{U(t+{\tau},t)-V_{n}(t+{\tau},t)\right\}\,\chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t+{\tau})f(t)\ ,

for
{\tau}\geq 0
and a.a.
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

It turns out that for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the operator

L({\tau})\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}/n{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right)
is a
multiplication operator
induced by
\{(U(t+{\tau},t)-V_{n}(t+{\tau},t))\ \chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t+{\tau})\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\left\|L({\tau})\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}/n}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}))}

\displaystyle=\esssup_{t\in{\mathcal{I}}}\|U(t+{\tau},t)-V_{n}(t+{\tau},t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})}\ \chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t+{\tau})\ ,

for each
{\tau}\geq 0
. Note that one has

\displaystyle\sup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\left\|L({\tau})\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}/n}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}))}

\displaystyle=\esssup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\left\|L({\tau})\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}/n}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}))}.

This is based on the fact that if
F(\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}({\mathfrak{X}})
is strongly
continuous, then

\sup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\|F({\tau})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathfrak{X}})}=\esssup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\|F({\tau})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathfrak{X}})}
.
Hence, we find

\displaystyle\sup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\left\|L({\tau})\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}/n}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}({\mathcal{I}},\mathfrak{X}))}

\displaystyle=\esssup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\esssup_{t\in{\mathcal{I}}}\|U(t+{\tau},t)-V_{n}(t+{\tau},t))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})}\ \chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t+{\tau}).

Further, if
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathcal{I}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})
is a strongly
measurable function, then

\esssup_{({\tau},t)\,\in\,{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times\,{\mathcal{I}}}\|\Phi({\tau},t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})}=\esssup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\,\esssup_{t\in{\mathcal{I}}}\|\Phi({\tau},t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})}.

Then, taking into account two last equalities, one obtains

\displaystyle\sup_{{\tau}\,\geq\,0}\left\|L({\tau})\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}}-\left({\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{K}}_{0}/n}{\rm e\,}^{-{\tau}{\mathcal{B}}/n}\right)^{n}\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathfrak{X}})}
=

\displaystyle=\esssup_{({\tau},t)\,\in\,{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times\,{\mathcal{I}}}\|U(t+{\tau},t)-V_{n}(t+{\tau},t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})}\ \chi_{\mathcal{I}}(t+{\tau})=

\displaystyle=\esssup_{(t,s)\,\in\,{\Delta}}\|U(t,s)-V_{n}(t,s)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X})}\ ,

that proves (
3.6
).

\square

We study bounded perturbations of the evolution generator

(

).

\mathcal{I}=[0,1]
,
\mathfrak{X}=\mathbb{C}
and we denote by

For

induces a

on the Banach space

.

Let

&{\Theta}\left(\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\Big|\int^{t}_{s}\,dy\,q(y)-\tau_{n}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q\,(s+k\,\tau_{n})\Big|\right)\ ,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\,,\end{split}

\displaystyle=\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\left|\,{\rm e\,}^{-\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)}-{\rm e\,}^{-\tau_{n}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q(s+\,k\,\tau_{n})}\right|\ .

Rate of convergence

First we consider the case of a real Hölder-continuous function
q\in C^{0,{\beta}}({\mathcal{I}})
.

Theorem 3.4

If
q\in C^{0,{\beta}}({\mathcal{I}})
is non-negative, then

\sup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\left\|e^{-{\tau}\,(D_{0}+Q)}-\left(e^{-{\tau}\,D_{0}/n}\,e^{-{\tau}\,Q/n}\right)^{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{I}))}=O({1}/{n^{\beta}})\ ,

as
n\to\infty
.

Proof

One gets for
\tau_{n}=(t-s)/n
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\begin{split}\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)&-\tau_{n}\,\sum^{n-1}_{k}q\,(s+{k}\,\tau_{n})\\
=&\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\tau_{n}}_{{k}\,\tau_{n}}dy\,\left(q(s+y)-q(s+{k}\,\tau_{n})\right)\ ,\end{split}

which yields the estimate

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)&-\tau_{n}\,\sum^{n-1}_{k}q\,(s+{k}\tau_{n})\Big|\\
\leq&\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\tau_{n}}_{{k}\tau_{n}}dy\,\left|\,q(s+y)-q(s+{k}\,\tau_{n})\right|\ .\end{split}

Since
q\in C^{0,{\beta}}({\mathcal{I}})
, there is a constant
L_{\beta}>0
such that for
y\in[k\tau_{n},(k+1)\tau_{n}]
one has

\left|q(s+y)-q(s+{k}\tau_{n})\right|\leq L_{\beta}\ |y-{k}\tau_{n}|^{\beta}\leq L_{\beta}\ \frac{(t-s)^{\beta}}{n^{\beta}}\ .

Hence, we find

\Big|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-\tau_{n}\,\sum^{n-1}_{k}q(s+{k}\tau_{n})\Big|\leq L_{\beta}\ \frac{(t-s)^{1+{\beta}}}{n^{\beta}}\leq L_{\beta}\ \frac{1}{n^{\beta}}\ ,

which proves (cf. Remark
3.1
)

\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\Big|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-\tau_{n}\sum^{n-1}_{k}q(s+{k}\,\tau_{n})\Big|=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}\right)\,.

Applying now Theorem
3.3
one completes the proof.

\square

The next natural question is: what happens, when function
q
is only
continuous
?

Theorem 3.5

If
q:\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is continuous and non-negative, then

\left\|e^{-{\tau}(D_{0}+Q)}-\left(e^{-{\tau}D_{0}/n}e^{-{\tau}Q/n}\right)^{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{I}))}=o(1)\ ,

(3.11)

as
n\to\infty
.

Proof

Seeing that
q
is continuous, for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
{\delta}>0
such that for

y,x\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
|y-x|<{\delta}
we have
|q(y)-q(x)|<\varepsilon
.
Therefore, if
1/n<{\delta}
, then for
y\in({k}\tau_{n},(k+1)\tau_{n})
we have

|q(s+y)-q(s+{k}\,\tau_{n})|<\varepsilon,\quad(t,s)\in{\Delta}\ .

Hence,

\Big|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-\tau_{n}\sum^{n-1}_{k}q(s+{k}\,\tau_{n})\Big|\leq\varepsilon(t-s)\leq\varepsilon\ ,

which yields (cf. Remark
3.1
)

\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\Big|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-\tau_{n}\,\sum^{n-1}_{k}q(s+{k}\,\tau_{n})\Big|=o(1)\ .

Now it remains only to apply Theorem
3.3
.

\square

Here it is worth to note that for
general
continuous function
q
one can say

nothing
about the convergence rate. Indeed, it can be shown that in (
3.11
) the
convergence to zero can be
arbitrary slow
(
3.12
) for a bounded perturbation
Q
.
This is drastically different to the case, when dominating generator corresponds to a holomorphic
semigroup and perturbation operator is bounded, cf. (
2.17
), (
2.19
) for

\alpha=0\,
, or to the case of unbounded perturbation, when
0<\alpha<1
, see
(
2.16
), (
2.18
).

Theorem 3.6

Let
{\delta}_{n}>0
be a sequence with
{\delta}_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then there exists a continuous
function
q:{\mathcal{I}}=[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, such that

\sup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\left\|e^{-{\tau}(D_{0}+Q)}-\left(e^{-{\tau}D_{0}/n}e^{-{\tau}Q/n}\right)^{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{I}))}={\omega}({\delta}_{n})\,,

(3.12)

as
n\to\infty
, where
\omega
is the Landau symbol defined in Remark
3.1
,
see Subsection
3.1
.

Proof

Taking into account the
Walsh-Sewell
theorem
(
WaSe 37
, Theorem 6),
we find that for any sequence
\{{\delta}_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
{\delta}_{n}>0
satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\delta}_{n}=0
there exists a continuous function

f:[0,2\pi]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, such that

\left|\int^{2\pi}_{0}dx\,f(x)-\frac{2\pi}{n}\sum^{n}_{k=1}f(2k\pi/n)\right|={\omega}({\delta}_{n})\ ,

as
n\to\infty
. Setting
q(y):=f(2\pi(1-y))
for
y\in[0,1]
, we get a continuous function

q:[0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, such that

\left|\int^{1}_{0}dy\,q(y)-\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}q(k/n)\right|={\omega}({\delta}_{n})\ .

Given that function
q
is continuous, we find

\begin{split}\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\Big|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)&-\tau_{n}\,\sum^{n-1}_{n=0}q(s+k\,\tau_{n})\Big|\\
&\geq\Big|\int^{1}_{0}dy\,q(y)-\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}q(k/n)\Big|\ ,\end{split}

which yields

\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\Big|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-\tau_{n}\sum^{n-1}_{n=0}q(s+k\,\tau_{n})\Big|={\omega}({\delta}_{n})\ .

Applying now Theorem
3.3
we prove (
3.12
).

\square

Our final comment concerns the case, when function
q:[0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is only

measurable
. Then it can happen that the Trotter product formula for that pair

\{D_{0},Q\}

does not
converge in the
operator-norm
topology.

Theorem 3.7

There is a non-negative function
q\in L^{\infty}([0,1])
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\;\sup_{{\tau}\geq 0}\left\|e^{-{\tau}(D_{0}+Q)}-\left(e^{-{\tau}D_{0}/n}e^{-{\tau}Q/n}\right)^{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(\mathcal{I}))}>0\ .

(3.13)

Proof

Let us introduce open intervals:

\begin{split}{\Delta}_{0,n}&:=(0,\,\tfrac{1}{2^{2n+2}}),\\
{\Delta}_{k,n}&:=(t_{k,n}-\tfrac{1}{2^{2n+2}}\,,\ t_{k,n}+\tfrac{1}{2^{2n+2}}),\quad k=1,2,\ldots,2^{n}-1,\\
{\Delta}_{2^{n},n}&:=(1-\tfrac{1}{2^{2n+2}},\,1),\end{split}

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where

t_{k,n}=\frac{k}{2^{n}},\quad k=0,\ldots,n,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Notice that
t_{0,n}=0
and
t_{2^{n},n}=1
. One easily checks that the intervals

{\Delta}_{k,n}
,
k=0,\ldots,2^{n}
, are mutually disjoint. We introduce the open sets

{\mathcal{O}}_{n}=\bigcup^{2^{n}}_{k=0}{\Delta}_{k,n}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

and

{\mathcal{O}}=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{O}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}.

Then it is clear that

|{\mathcal{O}}_{n}|=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad|{\mathcal{O}}|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, the
Lebesgue
measure of the closed set
{\mathcal{C}}:={\mathcal{I}}\setminus{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}

can be estimated by

|{\mathcal{C}}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\ .

Using the characteristic function
\chi_{{\mathcal{C}}}(\cdot)
of the set
{\mathcal{C}}
we define

q(t):=\chi_{{\mathcal{C}}}(t),\quad t\in{\mathcal{I}}\ .

The function
q
is measurable and it satisfies
0\leq q(t)\leq 1
,
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We choose
s\in(0,\varepsilon)
and
t\in(1-\varepsilon,1)
and
we set

\xi_{k,n}(t,s):=s+k\ \frac{t-s}{2^{n}},\quad k=0,\ldots,2^{n}-1,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad(t,s)\in{\Delta}.

Note that
\xi_{k,n}(t,s)\in(0,1)
,
k=0,\ldots,2^{n}-1
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, we have

t_{k,n}-\xi_{k,n}(t,s)=k\ \frac{1}{2^{n}}-s-k\ \frac{t-s}{2^{n}}=k\ \frac{1-t+s}{2^{n}}-s\ ,

which leads to the estimate

|t_{k,n}-\xi_{k,n}(t,s)|\leq\varepsilon\ \,(1+{k}/{2^{n-1}}),\quad k=0,\ldots,2^{n}-1,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}\ .

Hence

|t_{k,n}-\xi_{k,n}(t,s)|\leq 3\varepsilon,\quad k=0,\ldots,2^{n}-1,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Let
\varepsilon_{n}:={1}/{(3\cdot 2^{2n+2})}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then we get that

\xi_{k,n}(t,s)\in{\Delta}_{k,n}

for
k=0,\ldots,2^{n}-1
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
s\in(0,\varepsilon_{n})
and for
t\in(1-\varepsilon_{n},1)
.

Now let

S_{n}(t,s;q):=\tau_{n}\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}q\,(s+k\,\tau_{n})\,,\quad(t,s)\in{\Delta}\,,\quad\tau_{n}=(t-s)/n\,,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,.

We consider

S_{2^{n}}(t,s;q)=\frac{t-s}{2^{n}}\ \sum^{2^{n}-1}_{k=0}q\,(s+k\,\tfrac{t-s}{2^{n}})=\frac{t-s}{2^{n}}\ \sum^{2^{n}-1}_{k=0}q\,(\xi_{k,n}(t,s))\,,

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(t,s)\in{\Delta}
. If
s\in(0,\varepsilon_{n})
and
t\in(1-\varepsilon_{n},1)
,
then
S_{2^{n}}(t,s;q)=0
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\left|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-S_{2^{n}}(t,s;q)\right|=\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)\ ,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,,

for
s\in(0,\varepsilon_{n})
and
t\in(1-\varepsilon_{n},1)
. In particular, this yields

\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\left|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-S_{2^{n}}(t,s;q)\right|\geq\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)\geq\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}dy\,\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ .

Hence, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\;\esssup_{(t,s)\in{\Delta}}\left|\int^{t}_{s}dy\,q(y)-S_{2^{n}}(t,s;q)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2},

and applying Theorem
3.3
we finish the prove of (
3.13
).

Remark 3.8

We note that Theorem
3.7
does not exclude the convergence of the Trotter product
formula for the pair
\{D_{0},Q\}
in the
strong
operator topology.
We would remind that the same kind of

is known for the Trotter product formula

By virtue of (

) and (

Again, there is a drastic difference between the origin of these conclusions in a Hilbert space
(

, Theorem B) for

perturbation of the
holomorphic
semigroup
and in a Banach space (Theorem
3.7
) for
bounded
perturbation of a
(non-holomorphic)
contractive
semigroup.

Level crossings of fractional Brownian motion
Purba Das, Rafał Łochowski, Toyomu Matsuda, Nicolas Perkowski

Abstract
Since the classical work of Lévy, it is known that the local time of Brownian motion can be characterized through the limit of level crossings. While subsequent extensions of this characterization have primarily focused on Markovian or martingale settings, this work presents a highly anticipated extension to fractional Brownian motion — a prominent non-Markovian and non-martingale process. Our result is viewed as a fractional analogue of Chacon et al. [ Cha+81 ] . Consequently, it provides a global path-by-path construction of fractional Brownian local time. Due to the absence of conventional probabilistic tools in the fractional setting, our approach utilizes completely different argument with a flavor of the subadditive ergodic theorem, combined with the shifted stochastic sewing lemma recently obtained in [ MP22 ] . Furthermore, we prove an almost-sure convergence of the ( 1 / H 1/H )-th variation of fractional Brownian motion with the Hurst parameter H H , along random partitions defined by level crossings, called Lebesgue partitions. This result raises an interesting conjecture on the limit, which seems to capture non-Markovian nature of fractional Brownian motion. Keywords and phrases. Fractional Brownian motion, level crossings, local time, ( 1 / H 1/H )-th variation, Lebesgue partition, stochastic sewing lemma. MSC 2020. 60G22, 60J55.

Introduction

Level crossings of stochastic processes have been studied since the classical works of Kac
[
Kac43
]
and Rice
[
Ric45
]
.
Depending on whether the process is smooth or rough, the study of its level crossings
rely on different methods.
As for the smooth case, which is not the scope of this article, the reader can
refer to the survey article
[
Kra06
]
, the textbook
[
AW09
]
and the reference therein.

By far the most prominent example of a rough stochastic process is a Brownian motion.
The first work on level crossings of Brownian motion
is attributed to Lévy
[
Lév48
]
, who characterized its local time as a limit of normalised numbers of level crossings.
More precisely, for a given process
w
, we set

U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)\vcentcolon=\#\big\{(u,v):s\leq u<v\leq t,\,\,w_{u}=0,w_{v}=\varepsilon,\forall r\in(u,v)\>w_{r}\in(0,\varepsilon)\big\},

as illustrated in Figure
1
.
The quantity
U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
counts the number of upcrossings from
0
to
\varepsilon

in the interval
[s,t]
.
For Brownian motion
W
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,W-a)=\frac{1}{2}L_{t}^{W}(a)

almost surely, where
L_{t}^{W}(a)
is the local time of
W
at time
t
and at level
a
.
The local time is defined as the density of the occupation measure, see Definition
1.2
below.
This result can be found in standard textbooks such as
[
IM74
,
RY99
,
MP10
]
,
and it can be generalized for semimartingales
[
El 78
]
and for Markov processes
[
FT83
]
.

Figure 1
:
Graphical illustration of
U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
. In the picture,
U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)=2
.

On the other hand, there exist rough stochastic processes that do not fall under the category of semimartingales or Markovian processes. One such example is the
fractional Brownian motion

B^{H}
, which is a Gaussian process parameterized by Hurst index
H\in(0,1)
. Precisely,
B^{H}
is neither a semimartingale nor Markovian for
H\neq 1/2
, and it becomes Brownian motion when
H=1/2
.

Fractional Brownian motion possesses a local time (defined as in Definition
1.2
). Given Lévy’s result on the local time of Brownian motion, a natural question arises regarding whether a similar result holds for the local time of fractional Brownian motion. However, a complete answer to this question has not been obtained thus far. This is surprising considering the age of Lévy’s result and that of fractional Brownian motion.

Some works have explored the characterization of the fractional Brownian local time in relation to level crossings. For instance, the works
[
Aza90
,
AW96
]
demonstrate that the number of zeros for certain smoothed fractional Brownian motions converges, in a suitable sense, to the local time. Furthermore, level crossings of stochastic processes have received attention in the context of pathwise stochastic calculus
[
PP15
,
DOS18
,
CP19
,
Łoc+21
,
Kim22
,
ACX20
]
, as well as in some applied literatures
[
FHW94
,
Kru98
]
.

Constructing the local time via level crossings
is not only a natural problem, but also it can lead to a significant implication on
the path properties of the process.
This was first observed by Chacon et al.
[
Cha+81
]
built on
[
Per81
]
.
Therein proven is the existence of a measurable set
\Omega_{W}
such that
\mathbb{P}(W\in\Omega_{W})=1
(recall that
W
is Brownian motion) and
for every
w\in\Omega_{W}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0
, the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,w-a)

(1)

exists and is equal to one-half times the local time of
w
at the level
a
. Hence, the existence of the limit of level crossings is a path property,
and this provides a path-by-path construction of Brownian local time.
Furthermore, the construction is global in that the convergence in (
1
) holds for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
off a single null set.
This result explains why such construction of the local time receives attention in the pathwise stochastic calculus.
It is worth noting that the master thesis
[
Lem83
]
of Lemieux extended the result for a large class of semimartingales.

The result of Chacon et al. has a remarkable consequence on
pathwise quadratic variation
, calculated as a limit of sums of square increments, where the increments are taken along partitions of a fixed interval with vanishing mesh.
The precise definition of the pathwise quadratic variation is as follows: given a sequence
\bm{\pi}
of partitions
\pi_{n}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) with vanishing mesh, the pathwise quadratic variation
[w]_{\bm{\pi}}
of
a process
w
is defined by

\displaystyle[w]_{\bm{\pi}}\vcentcolon=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi_{n}}\lvert w_{t}-w_{s}\rvert^{2}

whenever the limit exists.
A deterministic partition is a partition that does not depend on the path/process.
The classical works
[
Lév40
,
Lév48
]
of Lévy show that for any refining deterministic partition sequence
\bm{\pi}
of
[0,t]
with vanishing mesh we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}([W]_{\bm{\pi}}=t)=1.

(2)

Dudley
[
Dud73
]
proved that if the deterministic sequence
\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{n})
satisfies

\displaystyle\lvert\pi_{n}\rvert\vcentcolon=\max_{[s,t]\in\pi_{n}}\lvert t-s\rvert=o(1/\log n)

(3)

then (
2
) holds,
with the optimality of the condition (
3
) being shown as well.
In general, the pathwise quadratic variation (even when it exists) may depend on the choice of a sequence of partitions
[
Fre83
, page 47]
,
[
DOS18
]
.
Furthermore, the null set in Lévy’s and Dudley’s works depends on the sequence
\bm{\pi}
of partitions.

Hence, an obvious question is if, given a stochastic process
X
, there is any uncountable class
\mathrm{P}
of partition sequences such that
almost surely for any
\bm{\pi},\bm{\pi}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}
we have
[X]_{\bm{\pi}}=[X]_{\bm{\pi}^{\prime}}
.
Here comes the result of Chacon et al.,
which proves
that Brownian motion has a measure zero set outside which any quadratic variation along any sequence of the
Lebesgue partitions

(defined at the beginning of Section
1.1
) of

[0,t]
with vanishing mesh is equal to
t
.
We remark that, unlike in Dudley’s result, there is no condition on the decay of meshes of partitions and the null set is uniform over all sequences of the Lebesgue partitions.

In this paper,
we extend Levy’s construction of the local time and the result of Chacon et al. for fractional Brownian motions with the Hurst parameter
H<1/2
.

1.1
Main results

We write
B^{H}
for a fractional Brownian motion with
B_{0}^{H}=0
and
with the Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)

B^{H}
is a one-dimensional centered Gaussian process
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(B^{H}_{t}-B^{H}_{s})^{2}]=a_{H}(t-s)^{2H}.

We choose the constant

in a manner that the identity (

The specific value of

is not important for our study.

To introduce the Lebesgue partitions,
let
\mathrm{P}
be a partition of the space
\mathbb{R}
. That is, we have a strictly increasing sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

\lim_{n\to-\infty}y_{n}=-\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}y_{n}=+\infty,

\mathrm{P}=\{[y_{n-1},y_{n}]:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Let
\Lambda(\mathrm{P})
be the set of all endpoints of intervals from

, or
\Lambda(\mathrm{P})=\{y_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

Given a path

w:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}

T_{0}(\mathrm{P},w)\vcentcolon=0

\displaystyle T_{n}(\mathrm{P},w)\vcentcolon=\inf\{t>T_{n-1}(\mathrm{P},w):w_{t}\in\Lambda(\mathrm{P})\setminus\{w_{T_{n-1}(\mathrm{P},w)}\}\}

(4)

with
\inf\varnothing=+\infty
.
(If

not assume

.
The partition given by

\{[T_{n-1}(\mathrm{P},w),T_{n}(\mathrm{P},w)]:n\in\mathbb{N},T_{n}(\mathrm{P},w)\leq t\}

is called a

.

Lebesgue partition is also called partition along

T_{n}(\varepsilon,w)\vcentcolon=T_{n}(\mathrm{P}^{\varepsilon},w)
.
We denote by
K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
the number of
\varepsilon
-level crossings in the interval

[s,t]
, defined precisely as
1

Our convention is
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}

In particular

\displaystyle K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)\vcentcolon=\#\{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}:T_{n}(\varepsilon,w_{s+\cdot})\leq t\}+\mathbf{1}_{\{w_{s}\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{1}(\varepsilon,w_{s+\cdot})\leq t\}},

where

We observe

\sum_{n:T_{n}(\varepsilon,w)\leq t}\lvert w_{T_{n}(\varepsilon,w)}-w_{T_{n-1}(\varepsilon,w)}\rvert^{\frac{1}{H}}=\varepsilon^{1/H}K_{0,t}(\varepsilon,w)+\mathbf{1}_{\{w_{0}\notin\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\}}\lvert w_{T_{1}(\varepsilon,w)}-w_{0}\rvert^{\frac{1}{H}}.

For

Theorem 1.1

(Convergence of (

)-th variation along uniform Lebesgue partitions)
.

For all

, there exists a positive finite constant
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
with the following property.
Let

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}^{1/H}K_{0,t}(\varepsilon_{n},B^{H}+\rho)=\mathfrak{c}_{H}t\quad\text{almost surely.}

(7)

For

We can also consider level crossings at a specific level, leading to Lévy’s classical work on local time

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and

\displaystyle U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)\vcentcolon=\#\big\{(u,v)\in[s,t]^{2}:u<v,w_{u}=0,w_{v}=\varepsilon,\forall r\in(u,v)\>w_{r}\in(0,\varepsilon)\big\}.

Definition 1.2

(Local time)
.

We denote by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f(B^{H}_{r})\mathrm{d}r=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(a)L_{t}^{H}(a)\mathrm{d}a,\quad\forall t\geq 0,\forall f\in C(\mathbb{R}).

As for the existence of

The following is an extension of Lévy’s result to fractional Brownian motion.

Theorem 1.3

(Local time via level crossings)
.

Let
H<1/2
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\in(0,\infty)
and

(\varepsilon_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

We then have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}(\varepsilon_{n},B^{H}-a)=\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}^{H}(a)\quad\text{almost surely,}

In principle, the null set of (

However, an intriguing result by Chacon et al.

Before stating the theorem, we introduce some notation.
Analogously to

\displaystyle V_{s,t}(\mathrm{P},w)\vcentcolon=\sum_{[a,b]\in\mathrm{P}}(b-a)^{\frac{1}{H}}\left(U_{s,t}(b-a,w-a)+D_{s,t}(b-a,w-a)\right).

The quantity

For the uniform partition

(Fractional analogue of Chacon et al.)

\Omega_{H}\subseteq C([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})

•

\mathbb{P}(B^{H}\in\Omega_{H})=1

•

1.2
Conjecture

There is an interesting aspect on the constant
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
.
For Brownian motion, the quadratic variation along any deterministic partition almost surely matches with the quadratic variation along any Lebesgue partitions.
That is,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbb{E}[(B^{1/2}_{1})^{2}].

(14)

It is tempting to guess that such relation holds for
H\neq 1/2
as well.
Indeed, such conjecture is stated in
[
CP19
, after Lemma 3.5]
.
However, the identity (
14
) is due to
the strong Markov property of Brownian motion.
Therefore, for
H\neq 1/2
, there is no reason why

\mathfrak{c}_{H}
and
\mathbb{E}[\lvert B^{H}_{1}\rvert^{\frac{1}{H}}]
should be equal.
Motivated by the simulation shown in Figures
4
,
we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.6

(The constant
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
)
.

For fractional Brownian motion with
H\neq 1/2
,
we conjecture that the (
1/H
)-th variation of
fractional Brownian motion along deterministic partitions differs
from the (
1/H
)-th variation of fractional Brownian motion along uniform Lebesgue partitions.
To be more precise, we conjecture

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{c}_{H}>\mathbb{E}[\lvert B_{1}^{H}\rvert^{1/H}]&\text{if }H<1/2,\\
\mathfrak{c}_{H}<\mathbb{E}[\lvert B_{1}^{H}\rvert^{1/H}]&\text{if }H>1/2.\end{cases}

If this is indeed the case, the constant
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
captures
non-Markovian nature of fractional Brownian motion.

1.3
Strategy of the proofs

Let us outline our strategy to prove Theorem
1.1
.
The first observation is that it is easier to deal with an averaged version of
K
. Namely, we define

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)\vcentcolon=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{-\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon/2}K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w+\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho.

The quantity
\bar{K}
is related to
truncated variation
, see Remark
2.5
below.
By scaling (Lemma
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H})=\bar{K}_{0,\varepsilon^{-1/H}}(1,B^{H})\quad\text{in law.}

Therefore, at least in law, the limiting behavior of
\bar{K}_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
is the same as that of
\bar{K}_{0,\varepsilon^{-1/H}}(1,B^{H})
.
Rewriting
T=\varepsilon^{-1/H}
, we thus hope to see the limit of
T^{-1}\bar{K}_{0,T}(1,B^{H})
.

Regarding this, we are actually in the setting of the superadditive ergodic theorem.
Indeed, it is not difficult to see that the family
(\bar{K}_{s,t}(1,B^{H}))_{s<t}
is stationary and superadditive (Lemma
2.6
).
Therefore, the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\bar{K}_{0,T}(\varepsilon,B^{H})=\sup_{T>0}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,T}(\varepsilon,B^{H})]

exists almost surely and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
. It turns out that the limit is the constant
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
in Theorem
1.1
.

To take advantage of this finding on
\bar{K}
, we can naively guess that

\displaystyle\lvert K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H}+\rho)-K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H}+\rho^{\prime})\rvert

(15)

is small provided that
\lvert\rho-\rho^{\prime}\rvert
is small. It is therefore expected to have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}\bar{K}_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H})=\mathfrak{c}_{H}.

(16)

However, proving (
16
) requires a non-trivial argument. In fact, the map

\rho\mapsto K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,w+\rho)

can be highly discontinuous, and it is impossible to estimate (
15
) pathwisely.

This inherent difficulty motivates us to employ a
probabilistic
argument in order to prove Theorem
1.1
. In addition to superadditivity, a crucial ingredient is the stochastic sewing lemma introduced by Lê
[
Lê20
]
, which provides effective estimates for stochastic Riemann sums. For our specific problem, we require an extension of this lemma, called the shifted stochastic sewing lemma, recently obtained by the third and fourth authors
[
MP22
]
. This extension is particularly suitable for capturing asymptotic decorrelation in stochastic Riemann sums.
It is worth mentioning that there are already some studies that leverage the stochastic sewing lemma for analyzing local times
[
HP21
,
HL22
,
BLM23
]
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
proceeds as follows. Not only is the family
(K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B^{H}))_{s<t}
superadditive, but it is also almost subadditive:

\displaystyle K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B^{H})\leq K_{s,u}(\varepsilon,B^{H})+K_{u,t}(\varepsilon,B^{H})+1,\quad s<u<t.

This leads to an approximation:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{1/H}K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H})\approx\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{1/H}K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B^{H}),

where
\pi_{\varepsilon}
is a uniform partition of the interval
[0,1]
with
\lvert\pi_{\varepsilon}\rvert\approx\varepsilon^{1/H}
. A similar approximation holds for
\bar{K}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{1/H}K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H})-\varepsilon^{1/H}\bar{K}_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B^{H})\approx\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{1/H}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B^{H})-\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B^{H})].

The Riemann sum on the right-hand side can then be estimated using the shifted stochastic sewing lemma, which ultimately yields the desired convergence result.

To establish the convergence to the local time (Theorem
1.3
), we follow a similar line of argument. However, the technical difficulty increases substantially due to the lack of a counterpart to
\bar{K}
. We postpone an overview of this technical argument to Section
3.1
.

One notable strength of the stochastic sewing lemma is its ability to provide a quantitative bound. Thanks to this property, we can obtain a quantitative version of Theorem
1.3
, as stated in Theorem
3.1
. This quantitative result enables us to employ the pathwise argument of Chacon et al.
[
Cha+81
]
, which elevates the convergence result from Theorem
1.3
to the more refined version of Theorem
1.4
.

2.1
Elementary results

Let us first recall the Mandelbrot–Van Ness representation of fractional Brownian motion

Definition 2.1

(Fractional Brownian motion)
.

We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(t,s)\vcentcolon=(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-(-s)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}},\quad s<t.

(17)

Let
W=(W_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a two-sided Brownian motion in one dimension.
Throughout the paper, we suppose that fractional Brownian motion
B=B^{H}
has the
Mandelbrot–Van Ness representation

\displaystyle B_{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t,s)\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

(18)

Note that we have
\mathbb{E}[(B_{t}-B_{s})^{2}]=a_{H}(t-s)^{2H}

for some constant
a_{H}
, whose actual value is irrelevant for us.

Lemma 2.2

(Scaling of
K
)
.

For
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, we have

(K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho))_{s<t,\;\varepsilon>0,\rho\in\mathbb{R}}\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}(K_{\lambda^{1/H}s,\lambda^{1/H}t}(\lambda\varepsilon,B+\lambda\rho))_{s<t,\;\varepsilon>0,\rho\in\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

We set
B^{(\lambda)}_{t}\vcentcolon=\lambda B_{\lambda^{-1/H}t}
. Note
that
B^{(\lambda)}\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}B
and observe that

K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B_{t}+\rho)=K_{s,t}(\lambda\varepsilon,\lambda(B+\rho))\text{}=K_{\lambda^{1/H}s,\lambda^{1/H}t}(\lambda\varepsilon,B^{(\lambda)}+\lambda\rho).\qed

Lemma 2.3

(Superadditivity of
K
)
.

Let
r<s<t
and
w
be a path. Then,

K_{r,s}(\varepsilon,w)+K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)\leq K_{r,t}(\varepsilon,w)\leq K_{r,s}(\varepsilon,w)+K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)+1.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we set
r=0
.
Recalling the definition of
T_{n}
from (
4
), we set

N\vcentcolon=\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}:T_{n}(\varepsilon,w)\leq s\}.

Figure 6
:
Level crossings around the middle time
s
. In case (b), the level crossing around
s
is not counted in
K_{r,s}(\varepsilon,w)+K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
.

As shown in Figure
6
, we have the following cases.

(a)

If
w_{T_{N}(\varepsilon,w)}=w_{T_{1}(\varepsilon,s+w_{s+\cdot})}
or if
w_{T_{N}(\varepsilon,w)}=w_{s}
, then
K_{r,t}(\varepsilon,w)=K_{r,s}(\varepsilon,w)+K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
.

(b)

Otherwise, we have
K_{r,t}(\varepsilon,w)=K_{r,s}(\varepsilon,w)+K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)+1
. ∎

For our arguments, the following variants of
K
will appear.

Notation 2.4

(Averaged
K
)
.

We set

\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)\vcentcolon=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{-\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon/2}K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w+\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho.

Remark 2.5
.

The quantity
\bar{K}
is related to the so-called
truncated variation

[
Łoc08
]
defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{TV}^{\varepsilon}(w,[s,t])\vcentcolon=\sup_{\pi:\text{partition of }[s,t]}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}\max\{\lvert w_{v}-w_{u}\rvert-\varepsilon,0\}.

Indeed, as shown in
[
Łoc17
, Theorem 1]
, we have the identity

\displaystyle\operatorname{TV}^{\varepsilon}(w,[s,t])=\varepsilon^{-1}\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w).

The advantage of
\bar{K}
is that in addition to the superadditivity, it is stationary.

Lemma 2.6

(Scaling, superadditivity and stationarity of
\bar{K}
)
.

Let
r<s<t
and let
w
be a process.

(i)

For
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle(\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B))_{s<t,\varepsilon>0}\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}(\bar{K}_{\lambda^{1/H}s,\lambda^{1/H}t}(\lambda\varepsilon,B))_{s<t,\varepsilon>0}.

(ii)

We have

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{r,s}(\varepsilon,w)+\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)\leq\bar{K}_{r,t}(\varepsilon,w)\leq\bar{K}_{r,s}(\varepsilon,w)+\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)+1.

(iii)

We have
\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)=\bar{K}_{0,t-s}(\varepsilon,w_{s+\cdot}-w_{s})
. In particular,

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}\bar{K}_{0,t-s}(\varepsilon,B).

Proof.

The claim (a) follows from Lemma

2.2
and
the claim (b) follows from Lemma
2.3
. For the
claim (c), we observe that for every
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)=\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w+\rho)=\bar{K}_{0,t-s}(\varepsilon,w_{s+\cdot}+\rho)

In particular, we choose
\rho\vcentcolon=-w_{s}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7

(Moments of
K
)
.

For every
p,t,\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
we have

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{\rho\in\mathbb{R}}K_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)^{p}]<\infty
.

Proof.

For
\alpha\in(0,H)
, we set

\llbracket B\rrbracket_{C^{\alpha}([0,t])}\vcentcolon=\sup_{0\leq r<s\leq t}\frac{|B_{s}-B_{r}|}{(s-r)^{\alpha}}.

By the Kolmogorov continuity theorem, we have

\mathbb{E}[\llbracket B\rrbracket_{C^{\alpha}([0,t])}^{p}]<\infty.

(19)

We set

\delta\vcentcolon=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(1+\llbracket B\rrbracket_{C^{\alpha}([0,t])})^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

To lead to a contradiction, suppose that there exist integers
k
and
n
such that

k\delta\leq T_{n}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)<T_{n+1}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)\leq(k+1)\delta\quad\text{with }T_{n+1}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)\leq t.

Then,

\displaystyle\varepsilon=|B_{T_{n+1}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)}-B_{T_{n}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)}|

\displaystyle\leq\llbracket B\rrbracket_{C^{\alpha}([0,t])}\delta^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\llbracket B\rrbracket_{C^{\alpha}([0,t])}(1+\llbracket B\rrbracket_{C^{\alpha}([0,t])})^{-1}<\varepsilon,

which is a contradiction. Thus, we must have

\#\{n:k\delta\leq T_{n}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)\leq(k+1)\delta\}\leq 1\quad\text{for each }k

and

\sup_{\rho\in\mathbb{R}}K_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)\leq t\delta^{-1}=t\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}(1+\llbracket B\rrbracket_{C^{\alpha}([0,t])})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

(20)

which is
L^{p}(\mathbb{P})
-integrable by (
19
).
∎

In view of Lemma
2.6
, the family
(\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,t}(1,B)])_{t\geq 0}

satisfies

\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,s+t}(1,B)]\geq\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,s}(1,B)]+\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,t}(1,B)].

Therefore, by Fekete’s Lemma, the following limit exists in
[0,\infty]
:

\mathfrak{c}_{H}\vcentcolon=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,t}(1,B)]=\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,t}(1,B)].

(21)

The constant
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
coincides with the one from Theorem
1.1
.
The following lemma shows that
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
is non-trivial.

Lemma 2.8

(Non-triviality of
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
)
.

We have
\mathfrak{c}_{H}\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

To see
\mathfrak{c}_{H}>0
, we observe

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{H}\geq\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,1}(1,B)]\geq\mathbb{P}(B_{1}\geq 2)>0.

To see
\mathfrak{c}_{H}<\infty
, we note by Lemma
2.6

that
(\bar{K}_{s,t}+1)
,
s<t
is subadditive. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{H}\leq\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,1}(1,B)]+1\leq\mathbb{E}[\sup_{\rho}K_{0,1}(1,B-\rho)]+1,

which is finite by Lemma
2.7
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

By the subadditivity, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,t}(1,B)]}{t}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{H}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,t}(1,B)]+1}{t}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\mathfrak{c}_{H}-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,t}(1,B)]}{t}\Big\rvert\leq t^{-1}.

(22)

2.2
The shifted stochastic sewing lemma

A key ingredient to the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10

(Shifted stochastic sewing lemma,
[
MP22
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a filtration and
let
(A_{s,t})_{0\leq s<t\leq T}

be a two-parameter stochastic process such that
A_{s,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.
Suppose that for some
p\in[2,\infty)
we have
A_{s,t}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{P})

for every
s<t
.
Moreover, suppose that for
v<s<u<t
and
M\in(0,\infty)
we have the estimates

\displaystyle\lVert A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{1}(t-s)^{\beta_{1}},

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{2}(s-v)^{-\alpha}(t-s)^{\beta_{2}},

(23)

where
t-s\leq M^{-1}(s-v)
is assumed in (
23
), with
\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
satisfying

\displaystyle\min\{2\beta_{1},2(\beta_{2}-\alpha),\beta_{2}\}>1.

Then there exists a unique
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted stochastic process
(\mathcal{A}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

with
\mathcal{A}_{0}=0

such that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{A}_{s,t}-A_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},M}\Gamma_{1}(t-s)^{\beta_{1}}+\Gamma_{2}(t-s)^{\beta_{2}-\alpha},

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{A}_{s,t}-A_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},M}\Gamma_{2}(s-v)^{-\alpha}(t-s)^{\beta_{2}}

for every
v<s<t
, where
t-s\leq M^{-1}(s-v)
is assumed in the second estimate.
Furthermore, we can find a
\delta>0
,
depending only on
\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
, such that

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\mathcal{A}_{T}-\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}A_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},T}(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})\lvert\pi\rvert^{\delta}

for every partition
\pi
of
[0,T]
.

Remark 2.11
.

The stochastic sewing lemma was first obtained in the seminal work
[
Lê20
]
,
and the first shifted version, where
\alpha=0
, was obtained by
[
Ger22
]
,
which was extended by
[
MP22
]
to handle the case
\alpha>0
.

Remark 2.12
.

For our problems, the family
(A_{s,t})_{0\leq s<t\leq T}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert A_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\Gamma_{1}(t-s)^{\beta_{1}},\quad\lVert\mathbb{E}[A_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\Gamma_{2}(s-v)^{-\alpha}(t-s)^{\beta_{2}}.

In this case, by uniqueness of the limit
\mathcal{A}
, we must have
\mathcal{A}\equiv 0
.
In particular, with some
\delta=\delta(\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
we have

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}A_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{T,p,\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2})\lvert\pi\rvert^{\delta}.

Let us explain the strength of Lemma
2.10
.
The key is the estimate (
23
),
which allows us to bring a
weak
estimate into a strong estimate.
To illustrate an example, recall the Mandelbrot–Van Ness representation (
18
), and we write
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

for the filtration generated by the Brownian motion
W
in (
18
).
Suppose that we want to estimate
\mathbb{E}[F(B)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]
.
We then have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(B)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=\mathbb{E}[F(y+\tilde{B})]|_{y=\mathbb{E}[B|\mathcal{F}_{v}]},\quad\tilde{B}\vcentcolon=\int_{v}^{\cdot}\mathcal{K}(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Then the problem reduces to the estimate of a Gaussian expectation (weak estimate), which allows us to leverage regularity of the Gaussian density.

Obviously, weak estimates provide better bounds than strong estimates do. As this point of view is the key to our arguments,
let us elaborate on one simple example.
Let
X
be a one-dimensional standard Gaussian random variable, and let
F\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
If we want to estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\lvert F(X)-F(X+a)\rvert]

for a small
a\in\mathbb{R}
, then we need to assume some regularity of
F
.
On the other hand, if we want to estimate

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{E}[F(X)]-\mathbb{E}[F(X+a)]\rvert,

then by the Gaussian change of variable we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(X+a)]=e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}}\mathbb{E}[e^{aX}F(X)].

Hence, only assuming
F
is bounded, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

we get

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{E}[F(X)]-\mathbb{E}[F(X+a)]\rvert\lesssim\lVert F\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lvert a\rvert,\quad\forall a\text{ with }\lvert a\rvert\leq 1.

Very roughly speaking, we will go through such lines of reasoning to prove Theorem
1.1
. The Gaussian change of variable will be replaced by
Girsanov’s theorem in the spirit of Picard
[
Pic08
]
, see Lemma
2.16
.

As already suggested, to prove Theorem

Let

v<s<t
.

\varepsilon\vcentcolon=(\frac{t-s}{\zeta})^{H}

Lemma 2.14

(Asymptotic independence,
[

Let

Let

and

be measurable with respect to

and

\sigma(B_{t^{\prime}}-B_{s^{\prime}}:s\leq s^{\prime}<t^{\prime}\leq t)

(24)

respectively, and suppose that

with

. If

(t-s)(s-v)^{-1}
is sufficiently small, then we have

|\mathbb{E}[FG]-\mathbb{E}[F]\mathbb{E}[G]|\lesssim_{p}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{1-H}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\|G\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}.

Remark 2.15
.

Consequently, we have the following estimate.
Let
p\in[2,\infty)
, and we set
p^{\prime}\vcentcolon=p/(p-1)
.
If

is measurable with respect to the

-algebra (

), then

-measurable

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{E}[(G-\mathbb{E}[G])F]\rvert\lesssim_{p}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{1-H}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{P})}\lVert G\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{P})},

provided that
\frac{t-s}{s-v}
is sufficiently small.
Since
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{P})
is the dual of
L^{p}(\mathbb{P})
and

, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[G|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[G]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{1-H}\lVert G\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})},

(25)

where we do not need to assume that

is small.

Proof of Lemma
2.13
.

By Lemma
2.6
-(iii) (or by Remark

),

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{1-H}\lVert\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}.

By the stationarity and the scaling (Lemma

),

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)

and the moment

.
The claim now follows.
∎

We recall the Mandelbrot–Van Ness representation (Definition

).
The next lemma is a consequence of Girsanov’s theorem.

Lemma 2.16

(Weak estimate on

)
.

Let

,

,

\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in[-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2]
and

be a deterministic continuous path. We set

\tilde{B}^{v}_{r}\vcentcolon=\int_{v}^{r}(r-u)^{H-1/2}\mathrm{d}W_{u},\quad v\leq r\leq t

(26)

and

b_{H}\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{4(1-H)}\Big(\frac{1}{\Gamma(H+1/2)\Gamma(3/2-H)}\Big)^{2},

where
\Gamma
is the usual Gamma function

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}\mathrm{d}t.

(27)

We then have the bound

|\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho)]-\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho^{\prime})]|\\
\lesssim e^{b_{H}|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{2}(s-v)^{-2}(t-v)^{2-2H}}\times\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho)^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|(s-v)^{-1}(t-v)^{1-H}.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
[
Pic08
, Theorem A.1]
. Let
\delta\vcentcolon=\rho^{\prime}-\rho
and

h_{r}\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}(s-v)^{-1}(r-v)\delta&\text{if }v\leq r\leq s,\\
\delta&\text{if }s\leq r.\end{cases}

Note that the functions
r\mapsto\tilde{B}^{v}_{r}+y_{r}+\rho^{\prime}
and
r\mapsto\tilde{B}^{v}_{r}+y_{r}+h_{r}+\rho

are equal on the interval
[s,t]
.
Thus,

K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho^{\prime})=K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+h+\rho).

We claim

h_{r}=\int_{v}^{r}(r-u)^{H-1/2}\mathrm{d}g_{u},

where for
r>v
,

g_{r}\vcentcolon=\frac{\delta\{(r-v)^{3/2-H}-(r-r\wedge s)^{3/2-H}\}}{\Gamma(H+1/2)\Gamma(3/2-H)(3/2-H)(s-v)}.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\dot{g}_{r}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\frac{\mathrm{d}g_{r}}{\mathrm{d}r}

\displaystyle\phantom{\vcentcolon}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(H+1/2)\Gamma(3/2-H)}\frac{\delta}{s-v}\{(r-v)^{1/2-H}-(r-s)^{1/2-H}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\}}\}

and

\displaystyle\int_{v}^{r}(r-u)^{H-1/2}(u-v)^{1/2-H}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r-v}(r-v-u)^{H-1/2}u^{1/2-H}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=(r-v)\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{H-1/2}u^{1/2-H}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\Gamma(H+1/2)\Gamma(3/2-H)(r-v),

where in the last line the relation between the Beta function and the Gamma function is used.
Therefore,

\int_{v}^{r}(r-u)^{H-1/2}\mathrm{d}g_{u}=\frac{\delta}{s-v}\{(r-v)-(r-s)\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\}}\}=h_{r}.

If we set

F(w)\vcentcolon=K_{s,t}\Big(\varepsilon,\int_{v}^{\cdot}(\cdot-u)^{H-1/2}\mathrm{d}w_{u}+y+\rho\Big),

then
K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho^{\prime})=F(W+g)
and by Girsanov’s theorem
(or the Cameron-Martin theorem)

\mathbb{E}[F(W+g)]=\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\int_{v}^{t}\dot{g_{r}}\mathrm{d}W_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{v}^{t}|\dot{g}_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}F(W)\Big].

Thus,

\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho^{\prime})]-\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho)]\\
=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\{e^{\int_{v}^{t}\dot{g_{r}}\mathrm{d}W_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{v}^{t}|\dot{g}_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}-1\Big\}K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho)\Big].

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, it is bounded by

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(e^{\int_{v}^{t}\dot{g_{r}}\mathrm{d}W_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{v}^{t}|\dot{g}_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}-1\Big)^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B}^{v}+y+\rho)^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\int_{v}^{t}\dot{g}_{r}\mathrm{d}W_{r}
is centered Gaussian with variance

\displaystyle\int_{v}^{t}\lvert\dot{g}_{r}\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}r\leq 2b_{H}\delta^{2}(s-v)^{-2}(t-v)^{2-2H},

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(e^{\int_{v}^{t}\dot{g_{r}}\mathrm{d}W_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{v}^{t}|\dot{g}_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}-1\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=e^{\int_{v}^{t}|\dot{g}_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}-1

\displaystyle\leq\int_{v}^{t}|\dot{g}_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\times e^{\int_{v}^{t}|\dot{g}_{r}|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{2b_{H}|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{2}(s-v)^{-2}(t-v)^{2-2H}}|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{2}(s-v)^{-2}(t-v)^{2-2H},

(in the second line we used
e^{a}-1\leq a\times e^{a}
for
a\geq 0
) which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In view of the scaling, we may suppose that
T=1
.
The proof resembles that of the subadditive ergodic theorem
[
Dur19
, Theorem 6.4.1]
.

Step 1, lower bound.

We fix a parameter
\zeta\geq 1
, which will go to infinity at the end.
(The parameter
\zeta
corresponds to the parameter
m
in
[
Dur19
, Theorem 6.4.1]
.)
Let
\pi_{\varepsilon,\zeta}
be
the partition of
[0,1]
with identical mesh size
\zeta\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}
.
By the superadditivity (Lemma
2.3
), using the relation
t-s=\zeta\varepsilon^{1/H}
for
[s,t]\in\pi_{\varepsilon,\zeta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)\geq\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi_{\varepsilon,\zeta}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)=\zeta^{-1}\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi_{\varepsilon,\zeta}}A_{s,t}^{1},

where
A^{1}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=K_{s,t}((\frac{t-s}{\zeta})^{H},B+\rho)(t-s)
.
Furthermore, we set

\displaystyle A^{2}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\bar{K}_{s,t}\Big(\Big(\frac{t-s}{\zeta}\Big)^{H},B\Big)(t-s),\quad A^{3}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)](t-s).

We see that
A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=A^{1}_{s,t}-A^{3}_{s,t}
satisfies the condition
of Lemma
2.10
. Indeed, by scaling we have

\displaystyle\lVert K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}+\lVert\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}1

and hence

\displaystyle\lVert A_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\lVert A^{1}_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}+\lVert A^{3}_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}(t-s).

To estimate the conditional expectation, let
(t-s)/(s-v)
be so small that the claim of Lemma
2.13
holds.
Since

\displaystyle K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)-\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{-\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon/2}\{K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)-K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho+\rho^{\prime})\}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime},

by Lemma
2.16
, using
|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|\leq\varepsilon
,
(s-v)^{-1}(t-v)^{1-H}\leq\sqrt{2}(s-v)^{-H}
(this holds for
(t-s)/(s-v)
sufficiently small) and
\varepsilon(s-v)^{-H}=((t-s)/(s-v))^{H}/\zeta^{H}
we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)-\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\varepsilon}\int_{-\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon/2}e^{2b_{H}\varepsilon^{2}(s-v)^{-2H}}\varepsilon(s-v)^{-H}\mathrm{d}\rho^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\zeta}\mathbb{E}[K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{H},

which readily yields

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[A^{1}_{s,t}-A^{2}_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{H}(t-s).

By Lemma
2.13
,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[A^{2}_{s,t}-A^{3}_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{1-H}(t-s).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[A_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{\min\{H,1-H\}}(t-s),

and we indeed see that
(A_{s,t})_{s<t}
satisfies the conditions of
Lemma
2.10
.

Consequently, recalling Remark
2.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B)\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]}{\zeta}-R_{\varepsilon,\zeta},

(28)

where

\displaystyle\lVert R_{\varepsilon,\zeta}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}\varepsilon^{\delta}

for some
\delta
depending only on
H
. By the Borel–Cantelli lemma,
if
\varepsilon_{n}=O(n^{-\eta})
for some
\eta>0
, then

R_{\varepsilon_{n},\zeta}\to 0
a.s. This implies

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{1}{H}}K_{0,1}(\varepsilon_{n},B+\rho)\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]}{\zeta}\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
\zeta
is an arbitrary real no smaller than
1
, the lower bound is obtained.

Step 2, upper bound.
Since
(K_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B+\rho)+1)_{s<t}

is subadditive,
we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}K_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]}{\zeta}+\frac{1}{\zeta}+R_{\varepsilon,\zeta},

(29)

and we similarly obtain the upper bound.
∎

In this section, we are interested in level crossings at a specific level.
Our goal of this section is to prove Theorem

, fractional analogue of Chacon et al.
[
Cha+81
]
.
The key is to obtain a more quantitative version of Theorem
1.3
, as stated just below.
Recall the definition of
U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
from (
8
),
which counts the total number of upcrossings from
0
to
\varepsilon
in the interval
[s,t]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Quantitative bound on number of upcrossing
U
)
.

Let
H\in(0,1/2)
,
T\in(0,\infty)
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
.
The constant
\mathfrak{c}_{H}
is defined by (
21
).
Almost surely, we have the following quantitative bound:

\Big|\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,T}(\varepsilon,B-a)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{T}(a)\Big|\leq\zeta^{-1}L_{T}(a)+\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,\zeta,T,a},

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
and
\zeta\in(1,\infty)
,
where there exists a positive
\kappa
such that for every

we have

\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,\zeta,T,a}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\leq C_{p,\zeta}T^{1-H}\varepsilon^{\kappa}

with
C_{p,\zeta}
independent of
\varepsilon
,
T
and
a
.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is somewhat
similar to that of Theorem
1.1
, especially the bounds (
28
) and (
29
).
Indeed, it is based on
the super(sub)-additivity, Girsanov’s theorem and shifted stochastic sewing lemma. However, a major difficulty here is that we cannot find a counterpart to
\bar{K}
. This leads to more involved technical arguments.
Therefore, instead of directly going to the proof, in the next section we heuristically explain our strategy.

3.1
Heuristics

Herein we explain our heuristic strategy to prove Theorem
3.1
.
Let
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be the filtration generated by
W
in the Mandelbrot–Van Ness representation (
18
).
We set

\displaystyle A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=U_{s,t}((t-s)^{H},B-a)(t-s)^{1-H}.

In view of Lemma
2.10
, our goal is to show

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[A_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\approx\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}\mathbb{E}[L_{s,t}(a)|\mathcal{F}_{v}].

(30)

Indeed, once the estimate (
30
) is proven, the rest of the argument is similar
to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We thus explain heuristically how to prove (
30
).
For simplicity, we set
a=0
, and we write
\varepsilon\vcentcolon=(t-s)^{H}
.
(Strictly speaking, we actually introduce another parameter
\zeta
going to infinity
and set
\varepsilon\vcentcolon=(\frac{t-s}{\zeta})^{H}
,
but for simplicity here we set
\zeta=1
.)
Let us introduce another parameter
u\in(v,s)

(in mind
t-s\ll s-u\ll u-v
), and, recalling the Mandelbrot–Van Ness representation from Definition
2.1
, for
r\in[s,t]
we decompose

\displaystyle B_{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{v}\mathcal{K}(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}+\int_{v}^{u}\mathcal{K}(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}+\int_{u}^{r}\mathcal{K}(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\vcentcolon X_{r}+Y_{r}+Z_{r}.

In the interval
[s,t]
the smooth
processes
X
and
Y
do not change much compared to
Z
. Therefore, we
can freeze time of
X
and
Y
(Lemma
3.9
):

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\approx\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,X_{s}+Y_{s}+Z)|\mathcal{F}_{v}].

But we see

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,X_{s}+Y_{s}+Z)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x+Y_{s}+Z)]|_{x=X_{s}},

and the Gaussian change of variable to
Y
yields

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x+Y_{s}+Z)]=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{x}{\sigma_{Y}})^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{xY_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\Big],

where
\sigma_{Y}
is the variance of
Y_{s}
(Lemma
3.11
).

For
U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)

to be positive,
Y_{s}
must be
around
0
with high probability. (In other words, if
Y_{s}
is far away from
0
, the process
Z

must move quite a lot, which is costly.) Therefore (Lemma
3.12
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{xY_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\Big]\approx\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)]\approx\mathbb{E}\Big[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y+Z)\Big].

As
v\ll u\ll s
, we have
\sigma_{Y}\approx\sigma_{Y+Z}
, with
\sigma_{Y+Z}
being the variance of
Y+Z
(Lemma
3.15
).
In the end, we have (Lemma
3.7
)

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\approx\mathbb{E}\Big[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y+Z)\Big]e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y+Z}})^{2}}.

It is well-known that the local time is heuristically represented as integral of Dirac’s delta function along

(see Lemma
3.19

We then observe (Lemma
3.19
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{t}\delta_{0}(B_{r})\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{v}\Big]

\displaystyle\approx\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{0}(B_{s})|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y+Z}}e^{-\frac{X_{s}^{2}}{2\sigma_{Y+Z}^{2}}}(t-s).

It is not obvious, but in Lemma

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y+Z}(t-s)^{-H}\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y+Z)]\approx\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}.

Now we see (
30

With this heuristic argument in mind, we move to a rigorous proof in the next section.

\|\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)|\mathcal{F}_{u}]-\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,X_{s}+Y_{s}+Z)|\mathcal{F}_{u}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\\
\lesssim_{p,\kappa,\zeta}\lVert U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{P})}^{1-\kappa}e^{-ca^{2}}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
Pic08
, Lemma A.1]
.
We have

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,X_{s}+Y_{s}+Z)|\mathcal{F}_{u}]=\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x_{s}+y_{s}+Z)]|_{x=X,y=Y}

and

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x_{s}+y_{s}+Z)]=\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x+y+w+Z)]

where

w_{r}\vcentcolon=-(x_{r}+y_{r}-x_{s}-y_{s}),\quad r\in[s,t].

Since
X
and
Y
are smooth on
[s,t]
, we may suppose that the realizations
x
and
y
are smooth as well.
As
w_{s}=0
,

w=I_{1}\dot{w}=I_{H-\frac{1}{2}}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}

and

w_{r}+\int_{s}^{r}\mathcal{K}(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}=\int_{s}^{r}\mathcal{K}(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}\Big(W_{\theta}+c_{1}\big(I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\big)_{\theta}\Big)

for some constant
c_{1}
depending only on
H
.

By Girsanov’s theorem,

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x+y+w+Z)]=\mathbb{E}\Big[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x+y+Z)\\
\times\exp\Big(c_{1}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\,\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}-\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)\Big].

Therefore, if
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
, by Hölder’s inequality,

|\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x+y+Z)]-\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x_{s}+y_{s}+Z)]|\\
\lesssim\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\exp\Big(c_{1}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}-\frac{c^{2}_{1}}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)-1\Big|^{q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
\times\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,x+y+Z)^{p}]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since the random variable

\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}(\theta)\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}

is Gaussian, by Lemma
A.1
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\exp\Big(c_{1}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\mathrm{d}W_{\theta}-\frac{c^{2}_{1}}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)-1\Big|^{q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
\lesssim_{q}\Big(\int_{s}^{t}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\Big(C_{q}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}\dot{w}\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\Big).

Hence, by setting

\displaystyle S_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\int_{s}^{t}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}I_{\frac{3}{2}-H}(\dot{X}+\dot{Y})\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta,

we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)|\mathcal{F}_{u}]-\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,X_{s}+Y_{s}+Z)|\mathcal{F}_{u}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)^{p}|\mathcal{F}_{u}]S_{s,t}^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{pC_{q}S_{s,t}}\right)\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}\Big\|S_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{C_{q}S_{s,t}}\Big\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{P})},

where
p_{1}^{-1}+q_{1}^{-1}=p^{-1}
.
Choose
p_{2}
so that
p_{1}^{-1}=(1-\kappa)+\kappa p_{2}^{-1}
(since
\kappa>1-p^{-1}
, this is possible by choosing
p_{1}
close to
p
). By the log-convexity of
L^{p}
norms,

\displaystyle\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{P})}^{1-\kappa}\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{P})}^{\kappa}.

We also have (by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality)

\displaystyle\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(\lVert B\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\geq a)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\rVert_{L^{2p_{2}}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-c_{2}a^{2}}\lVert U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\rVert_{L^{2p_{2}}(\mathbb{P})}.

The scaling property (Lemma
3.3
) gives

\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)\|_{L^{2p_{2}}(\mathbb{P})}=\|U_{s/(t-s),t/(t-s)}(\zeta^{-H},B-(t-s)^{-H}a)\|_{L^{2p_{2}}(\mathbb{P})}.

By Lemma
3.6
,

\displaystyle\|U_{s/(t-s),t/(t-s)}(\zeta^{-H},B-(t-s)^{-H}a)\|_{L^{2p_{2}}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p_{2},\zeta}1.

It remains to see

\displaystyle\Big\|S_{s,t}^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{C_{q}S_{s,t}}\Big\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H},\quad\text{if }\frac{t-s}{s-u}\text{ is sufficiently small.}

This was essentially proven in
[
Pic08
, Lemma A.1]
(our
S_{s,t}

corresponds to
L
therein).

∎

Lemma 3.11

(Gaussian change of variable)
.

Recall
\sigma_{Y}
from (
34
). We have the estimate

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,X_{s}+Y_{s}+Z)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}})^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big.e^{\frac{X_{s}Y_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{v}\Big].

Proof.

We set
F(\eta)\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,\eta+Z)]
for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
X,Y
and
Z
are independent and
X
is
\mathcal{F}_{v}
-measurable,

\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,X_{s}+Y_{s}+Z)|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=\mathbb{E}[F(x_{s}+Y_{s})]|_{x=X}.

Since
Y_{s}
is Gaussian with the variance
\sigma_{Y}^{2}
and the mean
0
, we observe

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(x_{s}+Y_{s})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(x_{s}+\sigma_{Y}\eta)e^{-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\sigma_{Y}\eta)e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\eta-\sigma_{Y}^{-1}x_{s})^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{x_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}})^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{Y_{s}x_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}F(Y_{s})\Big].

The claim thus follows.
∎

Lemma 3.12

(
Y_{s}
must be near
0
)
.

For every
p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
, if
\frac{t-s}{s-u}
is
sufficiently small, then

\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{x_{s}Y_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\Big]-\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)]\Big|\\
\lesssim_{H,p_{1}}\frac{|x_{s}|(t-u)^{H}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}e^{c(\frac{|x_{s}|(t-u)^{H}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}})^{2}}\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y+Z)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}

with
c
depending only on
H
and
p_{1}
.

Proof.

For
U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)
to be non-zero, we must have
\inf_{r\in[s,t]}|Y_{s}+Z_{r}|=0
. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{x_{s}Y_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\Big]-\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)]\\
=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(e^{\frac{x_{s}Y_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}-1\Big)U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\mathbf{1}_{\{\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}\geq|Y_{s}|\}}\Big].

Using the inequality

|e^{\lambda}-1|\leq e^{|\lambda|}|\lambda|,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

we estimate

\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(e^{\frac{x_{s}Y_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}-1\Big)U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\mathbf{1}_{\{\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}\geq|Y_{s}|\}}\Big]\Big|\\
\leq\frac{|x_{s}|}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{|x_{s}|\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)\Big],

and, by Hölder’s inequality, the expectation on the right hand side is bounded by

\left[\mathbb{E}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)^{p_{1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\left[\mathbb{E}\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}^{p_{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\left[\mathbb{E}e^{\frac{p_{3}|x_{s}|\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}},

where
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{3}}=1.

By Remark
3.10
, if
\frac{t-s}{s-u}
is sufficiently small, we have

\left[\mathbb{E}U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y_{s}+Z)^{p_{1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\lesssim_{p_{1}}\left[\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y+Z)^{p_{4}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{4}}},\quad p_{4}\vcentcolon=p_{1}^{2}.

Recalling
\tilde{B}
from Definition
3.2
, the scaling
property yields

\mathbb{E}\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}^{p_{2}}\leq\mathbb{E}\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([u,t])}^{p_{2}}=(t-u)^{p_{2}H}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}^{p_{2}}

and similarly

\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{p_{3}|x_{s}|\|Z\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t])}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}}\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{p_{3}|x_{s}|(t-u)^{H}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}}\Big].

Since
\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}
has a Gaussian tail by
Fernique’s theorem, there exists a constant
c
depending only on
H
such
that

\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\frac{p_{3}|x_{s}|(t-u)^{H}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}}\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}}\Big]\lesssim e^{c(\frac{p_{3}|x_{s}|(t-u)^{H}}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}})^{2}}.

Now the claim is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.13

(Sharp bound on
U
)
.

For every
p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
we have

\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y+Z)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{\zeta,p_{1},p_{2}}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{\frac{H}{p_{1}p_{2}}}.

Proof.

By the scaling,

\|U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,Y+Z)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}=\|U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}=\|U_{\frac{s-v}{t-s},\frac{t-v}{t-s}}(\zeta^{-H},\tilde{B})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}.

We set
k_{1}\vcentcolon=\frac{s-v}{t-s}
and
k_{2}\vcentcolon=\frac{t-v}{t-s}
. We
observe

\displaystyle\|U_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\zeta^{-H},\tilde{B})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{P})}^{p_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{E}\Big[U_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\zeta^{-H},\tilde{B})^{p_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}\in(a-1,a]\}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{E}\Big[U_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\zeta^{-H},\tilde{B})^{p_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}\in(a-1,a]\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\max_{r\in[k_{1},k_{2}]}|\tilde{B}_{r}-\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}|\geq|a|-1\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{E}[U_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\zeta^{-H},\tilde{B})^{p_{1}q_{2}}]^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\mathbb{P}(\max_{r\in[k_{1},k_{2}]}|\tilde{B}_{r}-\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}|\geq|a|-1)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\times\mathbb{P}(\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}\in(a-1,a])^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}},

where
p_{2}^{-1}+(2q_{2})^{-1}=1
.
By Lemma
3.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\zeta^{-H},\tilde{B})^{p_{1}q_{2}}]^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\lesssim_{p_{1},p_{2},\zeta}1.

Since

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{B}_{r_{1}}-\tilde{B}_{r_{2}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}^{2}\leq\lVert B_{r_{1}}-B_{r_{2}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}^{2}\lesssim\lvert r_{1}-r_{2}\rvert^{2H},

by Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem and Fernique’s theorem (note that
k_{2}-k_{1}=1
)
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\max_{r\in[k_{1},k_{2}]}|\tilde{B}_{r}-\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}|\geq|a|-1)\lesssim e^{-ca^{2}}.

Finally, considering the density of
\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}
we see

\mathbb{P}(\tilde{B}_{k_{1}}\in(a-1,a])\lesssim k_{1}^{-H}
,
and the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 3.15

(

3.6
,

\begin{gathered}\lVert R^{1}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H},\\
\lvert R^{2}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\rvert\lesssim_{\zeta,\kappa}\Big(\frac{s-u}{t-s}\Big)^{H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(2-\kappa)H}+\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(1-\kappa)H}.\end{gathered}

Since
\frac{t-s}{s-v}\leq 1
, these bounds lead to (
41
).
∎

As a final ingredient in the proof of Lemma
3.7
, we estimate
\mathbb{E}[U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})]
.

Lemma 3.17

(Asymptotics on constants)
.

For every
\kappa\in(0,1)
, if
\frac{t-s}{s-u}
and
\frac{s-u}{u-v}
are sufficiently small, we have the estimate

\Big\lvert\sqrt{2\pi}\varepsilon^{-1}\sigma_{Y+Z}\mathbb{E}[U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]\Big\rvert\\
\lesssim_{\kappa,\zeta}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{-H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(1-\kappa)H}+(t-s)^{-\kappa H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}.

Proof.

By integrating (
38
) over
\mathbb{R}
with
respect to
a
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a=\mathbb{E}[U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})]\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y+Z}})^{2}}\mathrm{d}a\Big]\\
+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}R^{1}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\mathrm{d}a\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}R^{2}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\mathrm{d}a\Big].

(42)

We will estimate each term of (
42
).

By the scaling (Lemma
3.3
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{\frac{s}{t-s}\zeta,\frac{t}{t-s}\zeta}(1,B-\zeta^{H}(t-s)^{-H}a)]\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\zeta^{-H}(t-s)^{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{\frac{s}{t-s}\zeta,\frac{t}{t-s}\zeta}(1,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{\frac{s}{t-s}\zeta,\frac{t}{t-s}\zeta}(1,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a.

We recall the downcrossing
D_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
from (
11
). By definition, we have

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\{U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w-a)+D_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w-a)\}\mathrm{d}a

Since

D_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)=U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,-w-\varepsilon)

and
B\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}-B
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{\frac{s}{t-s}\zeta,\frac{t}{t-s}\zeta}(1,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[D_{\frac{s}{t-s}\zeta,\frac{t}{t-s}\zeta}(1,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{\frac{s}{t-s}\zeta,\frac{t}{t-s}\zeta}(1,B)].

By the stationarity of
\bar{K}
(Lemma
2.6
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{\frac{s}{t-s}\zeta,\frac{t}{t-s}\zeta}(1,B)]=\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]}{2}\varepsilon.

(43)

Recalling how
X
depends on
a
from (
33
), for any
\sigma>0

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma})^{2}}\mathrm{d}a=\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma,

in particular, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y+Z}})^{2}}\mathrm{d}a=\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y+Z},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y}})^{2}}\mathrm{d}a=\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y}.

(44)

Combining (
42
), (
43
) and (
44
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\sqrt{2\pi}\varepsilon^{-1}\sigma_{Y+Z}\mathbb{E}[U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})]-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]}{2}\Big\rvert\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\sum_{i=1,2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lVert R^{i}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{P})}\mathrm{d}a.

It remains to estimate the right-hand side.

By (
39
),

\displaystyle\lVert R^{1}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{\zeta,\kappa}\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)]^{1-\kappa}e^{-ca^{2}}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}.

By Jensen’s inequality and (
43
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)]^{1-\kappa}e^{-ca^{2}}\mathrm{d}a\lesssim_{\kappa}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)]e^{-ca^{2}}\mathrm{d}a\Big)^{1-\kappa}\lesssim_{\zeta,\kappa}\varepsilon^{1-\kappa}.

This gives an estimate for
R^{1}
.
To estimate
R^{2}
we use (
40
) and (
44
), and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left|R^{2}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\right|\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|R^{2}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\right|\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\zeta,\kappa}(t-u)^{H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(1-\kappa)H}+\sigma_{Y}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(1-\kappa)H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}.

Recalling (
34
), we have
\sigma_{Y}\lesssim(s-v)^{H}
. Using also
t-u\leq 2(s-u)
and
\varepsilon=(\frac{t-s}{\zeta})^{H}
,
we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lVert R^{2}_{v,u,s,t}(a)\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{P})}\mathrm{d}a\lesssim_{\zeta,\kappa}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{-H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(1-\kappa)H}+\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{-\kappa H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}.

Noting that
(\frac{t-s}{s-v})^{-\kappa H}\leq(t-s)^{-\kappa H}
(recall that we assume that
0\leq v<s<t\leq 1
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

By the bound (
41
) in Lemma
3.16
, we have

\Big\lVert U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y+Z}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y+Z}})^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\\
\lesssim_{p,\zeta,\kappa}\Big\lVert\Big(\mathbb{E}[U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})]-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y+Z}}\varepsilon\Big)e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y+Z}})^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\\
+\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}+\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{-H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(2-\kappa)H}

if
\frac{t-s}{s-u}
and
\frac{s-u}{u-v}
are sufficiently small.
By the bound
\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma_{Y+Z}}\lesssim_{\zeta}(\frac{t-s}{s-v})^{H}\leq 1
and Lemma
3.17
we have

\Big\lvert\mathbb{E}[U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})]-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y+Z}}\varepsilon\Big\rvert\leq\Big\lvert{\sqrt{2\pi}\varepsilon^{-1}\sigma_{Y+Z}}\mathbb{E}[U_{s-v,t-v}(\varepsilon,\tilde{B})]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]\Big\rvert\\
\lesssim_{\zeta,\kappa}(t-s)^{-\kappa H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}+\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{-H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(2-\kappa)H}.

Therefore,

\Big\lVert U_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{Y+Z}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{X_{s}}{\sigma_{Y+Z}})^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\\
\lesssim_{p,\zeta,\kappa}(t-s)^{-\kappa H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{1-H}+\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-u}\Big)^{-H}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(2-\kappa)H}

if
\frac{t-s}{s-u}
and
\frac{s-u}{u-v}
are sufficiently small.
To optimize, we choose
u
so that

\displaystyle\frac{t-s}{s-u}=\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(2-\kappa)H}.

Note that, as
H<1/2
, the exponent
(2-\kappa)H
is less than
1
.
Therefore, if
\frac{t-s}{s-v}
is sufficiently small, then

\frac{t-s}{s-u}
and
\frac{s-u}{u-v}
are sufficiently small as well.
This gives the claimed bound.
∎

.

Recall
\bar{U}
from Notation
3.4
.
Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[\bar{U}_{s,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)]\mathrm{d}a=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]+1}{2}\varepsilon,

3.2.2
Estimates on the local time

The following is the last technical ingredient for Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.19

(Local time approximation)
.

Let
H\in(0,1/2)
. We set

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{s,t}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[\delta_{0}(B_{s}-a)|\mathcal{F}_{s-(t-s)}](t-s)

(45)

\displaystyle\phantom{\vcentcolon}=\sqrt{\frac{H}{\pi}}e^{-\frac{H}{(t-s)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{s}-a|\mathcal{F}_{s-(t-s)}]^{2}}(t-s)^{1-H}.

Then, there exists a
\delta>0
such that for any
p<\infty
and
for any partition
\pi
of
[0,1]
,

\Big\|L_{1}(a)-\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}\tilde{A}_{s,t}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p}|\pi|^{\delta}.

Proof.

We write
L_{s,t}(a)\vcentcolon=L_{t}(a)-L_{s}(a)
.
We use the shifted stochastic sewing (Lemma
2.10
). To this end, it suffices to check

\|L_{s,t}(a)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p}(t-s)^{1-H},\quad\|\tilde{A}_{s,t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p}(t-s)^{1-H}

(46)

and

\|\mathbb{E}[L_{s,t}(a)-\tilde{A}_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p}(s-v)^{-1-H}(t-s)^{2},\quad t-s\leq s-v.

(47)

The estimate for
L
in (
46
) is well known and can be shown for example by (non-shifted) stochastic sewing with
\Xi_{u,v}=\mathbb{E}[\int_{u}^{v}\delta(B_{r}-a)\mathrm{d}r|\mathcal{F}_{u}]
, and the estimate for
\tilde{A}
in (
46
) is not difficult to show. Hence, we focus on the
estimate (
47
). In
[
MP22
, Lemma 4.7]
, an
estimate similar to (
47
) is obtained, but the exponents therein
depend on
p
. We slightly improve the argument.

We have

\mathbb{E}[L_{s,t}(a)-\tilde{A}_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=\sqrt{\frac{H}{\pi}}\int_{s}^{t}\Big\{e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}-a|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(r-v)^{-H}\\
-e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{s}-a|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(s-v)^{-H}\Big\}\mathrm{d}r.

For simplification, we replace
B-a
by
B
. We decompose the integrand as

R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}
, where

R_{1}\vcentcolon=e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(r-v)^{-H}-e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(s-v)^{-H},

R_{2}\vcentcolon=e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(s-v)^{-H}-e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(s-v)^{-H},

R_{3}\vcentcolon=e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(s-v)^{-H}-e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(s-v)^{-H}.

To obtain (
47
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle\lVert R_{1}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}+\lVert R_{2}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}+\lVert R_{3}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p}(s-v)^{-1-H}(t-s).

Since

0\leq(s-v)^{-H}-(r-v)^{-H}\lesssim(s-v)^{-H-1}(r-s),

we have

|R_{1}|\lesssim(s-v)^{-H-1}(t-s).

We observe

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}-e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(1-e^{-H((s-v)^{-2H}-(r-v)^{-2H})\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}})

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}((s-v)^{-2H}-(r-v)^{-2H})

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{H}{(r-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}(s-v)^{-2H-1}(r-s)

\displaystyle\lesssim(r-v)^{2H}(s-v)^{-2H-1}(r-s)

\displaystyle\lesssim(s-v)^{-1}(t-s),

where in the last step we used that
(r-v)\leq(t-s)+(s-v)\leq 2(s-v)
. Hence,

|R_{2}|\lesssim(s-v)^{-1-H}(t-s).

Finally, we estimate
R_{3}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}
. Then,

\Big|e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}-e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}\Big|\\
\leq e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}(\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}-\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}).

Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}-\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}=2\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}](\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}])+(\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}])^{2}

and

e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}(s-v)^{-H}|\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]|\lesssim 1,

we obtain

\Big|e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}-e^{-\frac{H}{(s-v)^{2H}}\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}\Big|\\
\lesssim(s-v)^{-H}|\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]|+(s-v)^{-2H}|\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]|^{2}.

A similar estimate holds if
\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}
. Therefore, it remains to note

\|\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]-\mathbb{E}[B_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p}(s-v)^{H-1}(t-s).\qed

Lemma 3.20

(Lower bound on
U
)
.

Let
H\in(0,1/2)
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\zeta\in[1,\infty)
.
Then, we have

\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,1}(\varepsilon,B-a)\geq\frac{1}{2\zeta}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]L_{1}(a)-R_{\varepsilon},

where for some
\delta
depending only on

we have

\|R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}\varepsilon^{\delta}.

Proof.

\tilde{A}
by (

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=U_{s,t}(\zeta^{-H}(t-s)^{H},B-a)\Big(\frac{t-s}{\zeta}\Big)^{1-H}.

By Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\hat{A}_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim(t-s)^{1-H}.

By Lemma

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{A}_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\zeta}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]\sqrt{\frac{H}{\pi(s-v)^{2H}}}e^{-\frac{H\mathbb{E}[B_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]^{2}}{(s-v)^{2H}}}(t-s)+R_{v,s,t}

where

\displaystyle\lVert R_{v,s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta,\kappa}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(2-\kappa)H(1-H)}(t-s)^{1-(1+\kappa)H}.

for any
\kappa\in(0,1)
.
Since

sufficiently small, we can suppose that

\displaystyle 1-(1+\kappa)H>\frac{1}{2},\quad 1-(1+\kappa)H+(2-\kappa)H(1-H)>1.

Hence, by Lemma

, with some

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\sum_{[s,t]\in\pi}\Big(\hat{A}_{s,t}-\frac{1}{2\zeta}\mathbb{E}[\bar{K}_{0,\zeta}(1,B)]\tilde{A}_{s,t}\Big)\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}\lvert\pi\rvert^{\delta}.

In particular, considering a partition of size
\zeta\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}}
,
the claim follows in view of (
48
) and Lemma
3.19
.

3.3
Uniform convergence

A naive application of Theorem
3.1
yields that, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and for
any
\bm{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
with polynomial decay, there exists
a measurable set
\Omega_{a,\bm{\varepsilon}}
such that

\mathbb{P}(B\in\Omega_{a,\bm{\varepsilon}})=1
and for every
w\in\Omega_{a,\bm{\varepsilon}}

the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}(\varepsilon_{n},w-a)

exists for every
t\geq 0
.
However, as observed by Chacon et al.
[
Cha+81
]
, the quantitative estimate in
Theorem
3.1
implies more strongly that we can take
\Omega_{a,\bm{\varepsilon}}

uniformly over
a
and
\bm{\varepsilon}
.
Furthermore, we can remove the polynomial decaying condition.

The arguments below are essentially given in
[
Cha+81
]
and
[
Lem83
]
, but we repeat them for the reader’s convenience.
We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.22

(Uniform convergence over grids)
.

Let
H\in(0,1/2)
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.
We define the grid

\displaystyle G_{k}:=\{ik^{-7}:i\in\mathbb{Z},|i|\leq k^{8}\},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

We then have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\max_{x\in G_{k}}\big\lvert k^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}U_{0,t}(k^{-6},B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert=0\quad\text{almost surely.}

Proof.

In the notation of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{x\in G_{k}}\big\lvert k^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}U_{0,t}(k^{-6},B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert\leq\zeta^{-1}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}L_{t}(x)+\max_{x\in G_{k}}R_{k,\zeta,x}.

Since
x\mapsto L_{t}(x)
is continuous and
L_{t}(\cdot)
is supported on

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\lvert x\rvert\leq\lVert B\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,t])}\},

we see that
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}L_{t}(x)<\infty
a.s.
By Theorem
3.1
,

\displaystyle\lVert\max_{x\in G_{k}}R_{k,\zeta,x}\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{x\in G_{k}}\lVert R_{k,\zeta,x}\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{p,\zeta}k^{-p\delta+8},

where
\delta
is independent of
p
. Since
p
can be arbitrarily large,
the Borel–Cantelli lemma implies that almost surely we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\max_{x\in G_{k}}R_{k,\zeta,x}=0

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\max_{x\in G_{k}}\big\lvert k^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}U_{0,t}(k^{-6},B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert\leq\zeta^{-1}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}L_{t}(x).

Since
\zeta
is arbitrary, we complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.23

(Uniform convergence to local time,
[
Lem83
, Theorem II.2.4]
)
.

Let
H\in(0,1/2)
and
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Almost surely, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\leq T}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(a)\big\rvert=0.

Proof.

Firstly, by an elementary argument using monotonicity of
U
and continuity of
L

[
Lem83
, Note after Theorem II.2.4]
,
it suffices to prove that for each
t\in(0,\infty)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(a)\big\rvert=0\quad\text{a.s.}

By Lemma
3.22
, we can find an

\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1})=1
such that
for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
there exists an
N=N(\delta,\omega)

with the following inequalities:

\displaystyle(k-1)^{-6}-(k-1)^{-7}>k^{-6}\quad\forall k\geq N,

(50)

\displaystyle\lVert B(\omega)\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,t])}<N-1,

(51)

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geq N}\max_{x\in G_{k}}\big\lvert k^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}U_{0,t}(k^{-6},B(\omega)-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)(\omega)\big\rvert<\delta.

(52)

The argument below holds on the event
\Omega_{1}
.
For
\varepsilon\leq(N+1)^{-6}
, there exists a unique
m=m_{\varepsilon}\geq N+1
such that

\displaystyle(m+1)^{-6}<\varepsilon\leq m^{-6}.

If
|x|\geq N-1
, then by (
51
) we have
L^{H}_{t}(x)=0
.
On the other hand, if
|x|<N-1
,
then we define

\displaystyle x_{k}\vcentcolon=\max_{y\in G_{k}}\{y\leq x\}

for all
k\geq N
. Since
x<x_{m-1}+(m-1)^{-7}
, we have

•

x_{m-1}\leq x<x+\varepsilon<x_{m-1}+(m-1)^{-7}+m^{-6}\leq x_{m-1}+(m-1)^{-6}
and

•

x<x_{m+2}+(m+2)^{-7}<x_{m+2}+(m+2)^{-7}+(m+2)^{-6}<x+\varepsilon
,

where (
50
) are applied in both items.
Hence, defining the two sets
I_{m-1}
and
\bar{I}_{m+2}
as

I_{m-1}:=\left[x_{m-1},x_{m-1}+(m-1)^{-6}\right],\quad\bar{I}_{m+2}:=\left[\bar{x}_{m+2},\bar{x}_{m+2}+(m+2)^{-6}\right],

where
\bar{x}_{m+2}\vcentcolon=x_{m+2}+(m+2)^{-7}
,
we have the inclusions

\displaystyle\bar{I}_{m+2}\subseteq[x,x+\varepsilon]\subseteq I_{m-1}.

(53)

Now we move to the bound on
U
. We first observe the monotonicity of
U
:

\displaystyle U_{0,t}(\varepsilon_{1},B-x_{1})\leq U_{0,t}(\varepsilon_{2},B-x_{2})

provided that
[x_{2},x_{2}+\varepsilon_{2}]\subseteq[x_{1},x_{1}+\varepsilon_{1}]
.
The relation (
53
) thus yields

\displaystyle U_{0,t}((m-1)^{-6},B-x_{m-1})\leq U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-x)\leq U_{0,t}((m+2)^{-6},B-\bar{x}_{m+2}).

Hence,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert\leq A_{\varepsilon}+\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}\sup_{x,y:\lvert x-y\rvert\leq 2\varepsilon}\lvert L_{t}(x)-L_{t}(y)\rvert,

(54)

where

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\sup_{x\in G_{m_{\varepsilon}-1}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}((m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6},B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert,

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\sup_{x\in G_{m_{\varepsilon}+2}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}((m_{\varepsilon}+2)^{-6},B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert.

Due to the uniform continuity of
L_{t}(\cdot)
, the last term of (
54
) converges to
0
.
To estimate

, we observe the bound

A_{\varepsilon}\leq\sup_{x\in G_{m_{\varepsilon}-1}}\big\lvert(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}U_{0,t}((m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6},B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert\\
+\sup_{x\in G_{m_{\varepsilon}-1}}\big\lvert\{(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}\}U_{0,t}((m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6},B-x)\big\rvert.

By (

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{x\in G_{m_{\varepsilon}-1}}\big\lvert(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}U_{0,t}((m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6},B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert\leq\delta.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\lvert(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert\varepsilon-(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6}\rvert^{\frac{1}{H}-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-7(\frac{1}{H}-1)}.

As (

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\sup_{x\in G_{m_{\varepsilon}-1}}(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}U_{0,t}((m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6},B-x)<\infty,

we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{x\in G_{m_{\varepsilon}-1}}\big\lvert\{(m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6(\frac{1}{H}-1)}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}\}U_{0,t}((m_{\varepsilon}-1)^{-6},B-x)\big\rvert=0.

Hence, we get
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}A_{\varepsilon}\leq\delta
, and we get a similar estimate for
\bar{A}_{\varepsilon}

Recalling (

), this implies

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-x)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(x)\big\rvert\leq 2\delta.

Since
\delta
is arbitrary, we conclude the proof.
∎

Recall the total number
D_{s,t}(\varepsilon,w)
of downcrossings from (

and the variation
V_{s,t}(\mathrm{P},w)
along Lebesgue partition

from (

Since the total number of upcrossings and that of downcrossings can differ by at most

,
almost surely we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\leq T}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}D_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)-\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{H}}{2}L_{t}(a)\big\rvert=0\quad\forall T\geq 0,

or

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\leq T}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\big\lvert\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{H}-1}(U_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-a)+D_{0,t}(\varepsilon,B-a))-\mathfrak{c}_{H}L_{t}(a)\big\rvert=0\quad\forall T\geq 0.

Theorem 3.24

(Uniform convergence of variation,
[
Lem83
, Proposition III.2.1]
)
.

Let
H\in(0,1/2)
and
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Almost surely, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\leq T}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{P}:\text{ \emph{partition of} $\mathbb{R}$},\\
\lvert\mathrm{P}\rvert\leq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\lvert V_{0,t}(\mathrm{P},B)-\mathfrak{c}_{H}t\rvert=0.

Proof.

We have the identity

\displaystyle V_{0,t}(\mathrm{P},B)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{[a,b]\in\mathrm{P}}(b-a)^{\frac{1}{H}-1}\{U_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)+D_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)\}\mathbf{1}_{(a,b]}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

Setting
I\vcentcolon=[-\lVert B\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T])},\lVert B\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}]
,
the occupation density formula yields

\lvert V_{0,t}(\mathrm{P},B)-\mathfrak{c}_{H}t\rvert\\
\leq\int_{I}\sum_{[a,b]\in\mathrm{P}}(b-a)^{\frac{1}{H}-1}\lvert U_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)+D_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)-\mathfrak{c}_{H}L_{t}(x)\rvert\mathbf{1}_{(a,b]}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

For
x\in[a,b]
, we have the bound

\lvert U_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)+D_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)-\mathfrak{c}_{H}L_{t}(x)\rvert\\
\leq\lvert U_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)+D_{0,t}(b-a,B-a)-\mathfrak{c}_{H}L_{t}(a)\rvert\\
+\mathfrak{c}_{H}\sup_{a_{1},a_{2}:\lvert a_{1}-a_{2}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathrm{P}\rvert}\lvert L_{t}(a_{1})-L_{t}(a_{2})\rvert.

Therefore,

\lvert V_{0,t}(\mathrm{P},B)-\mathfrak{c}_{H}t\rvert\leq\lvert I\rvert\times\Big\{\sup_{\delta\leq\lvert\mathrm{P}\rvert}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\lvert U_{0,t}(\delta,B-a)+D_{0,t}(\delta,B-a)-\mathfrak{c}_{H}L_{t}(a)\rvert\\
+\mathfrak{c}_{H}\sup_{\lvert a_{1}-a_{2}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathrm{P}\rvert}\lvert L_{t}(a_{1})-L_{t}(a_{2})\rvert\Big\}.

In view of (
55
) and the uniform continuity of
L
, the claim follows.
∎

3.4
Horizontally rough function

In a recent work
[
CD23
]
, a concept of
quadratic roughness
has been introduced. This pathwise quadratic roughness property ensures an invariant notion of quadratic variation,
i.e., given two (appropriate) partition sequences
\bm{\pi}
and
\bm{\sigma}
, quadratic roughness of function
x
implies
[x]_{\bm{\pi}}=[x]_{\bm{\sigma}}
.
As expected, Brownian motion satisfies this quadratic roughness property.
In fact, for any
deterministic
partition sequence
\bm{\pi}=(\pi^{n})
with
|\pi^{n}|\log n\to 0
, we have
[B^{1/2}]_{\bm{\pi}}(t)=t
almost surely.
That is there exists
\Omega_{\bm{\pi}}\subset\Omega
of full
\mathbb{P}
-measure
such that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{\bm{\pi}},\;[\omega]_{\bm{\pi}}(t)=t
.
On the other hand, by
[
Dud73
]
there exists for each
\omega\in\Omega
a partition
\pi=\pi(\omega)
such that
[\omega]_{\pi(\omega)}(t)=0
, and therefore
\cap_{\bm{\pi}}\Omega_{\bm{\pi}}=\varnothing
.
So even for Brownian motion quadratic roughness does not ensure an almost sure invariance of quadratic variation across all deterministic partitions (partitions purely on time variable).
So an obvious question is: are there any notion of roughness which ensures almost sure invariance of quadratic variation across a large (uncountable) class of partition sequence? To answer this question we define the notion of
horizontal roughness
(the word ‘horizontal’ represents path dependent Lebesgue type partitions constructed from level crossings).

Definition 3.25

(Horizontally rough: an invariance notion for
p
-th variation)
.

A function
x\in C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
is called
horizontally rough
if for any

t\in[0,T]
,
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon=\{\varepsilon_{n}\}
with
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{K_{0,t}(\varepsilon_{n},x+\rho)}{K_{0,t}(\varepsilon_{n},x)}=1.

This notion of horizontal roughness is completely pathwise and scale invariant. If a continuous function
x
has the horizontally rough property and
x
also has
p
-th variation along a uniform Lebesgue partition, then
x
has
p
-th variation along all uniform Lebesgue partition and the
p
-th variation is the same across different uniform Lebesgue partitions. Unlike the notion of quadratic roughness defined in
[
CD23
]
, this notion of horizontally rough ensures an invariant notion of
p
-th variation across a large class of Lebesgue type partitions almost surely i.e. there is a common measure zero set outside which the
p
-th variation along any uniform Lebesgue type partition is the same.

Example 3.26
.

The following are examples of horizontally rough functions.

•

From the definition any linear function is horizontally rough.

•

Using results from
[
Cha+81
,
Lem83
]
, we can show that Brownian motion and more generally continuous semimartingales are horizontally rough almost surely.

•

Theorem
1.4
shows that fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
H<1/2
is horizontally rough almost surely.

It is interesting to construct horizontally rough functions sampled from a non-Gaussian, non-semimartingale process. We leave this and further properties of such functions as a future work.

Appendix A
An estimate on log-normal distribution

Lemma A.1
.

Let
Z
be a standard normal distribution, and let
q\in[2,\infty)
. We then have

\displaystyle\lVert e^{\lambda Z-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}}-1\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\sqrt{2\log 2}\sqrt{q-1}\lambda e^{(q-1)\lambda^{2}},\quad\forall\lambda\geq 0.

Proof.

By
[
Nua06
, Equation (1.1)]
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\lambda Z-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}}-1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}H_{n}(Z),

where
H_{n}
is
n
th Hermite polynomial. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\lVert e^{\lambda Z-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}}-1\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\lVert H_{n}(Z)\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{P})}.

The hypercontractivity
[
Nua06
, Theorem 1.4.1]
implies that

\displaystyle\lVert H_{n}(Z)\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{P})}\leq(q-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}\lVert H_{n}(Z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}.

Furthermore, by
[
Nua06
, Lemma 1.1.1]
,

\displaystyle\lVert H_{n}(Z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert e^{\lambda Z-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}}-1\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{q-1}\lambda\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}(q-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\sqrt{n!}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{q-1}\lambda\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-n}}{n+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}\lambda^{2n}(q-1)^{n}}{n!}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{q-1}\lambda\sqrt{2\log 2}e^{(q-1)\lambda^{2}}.\qed

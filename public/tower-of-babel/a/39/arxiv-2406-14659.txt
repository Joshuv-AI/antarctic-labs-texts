Algebraic proof of modular form inequalities for optimal sphere packings
Seewoo Lee

Abstract
. We give algebraic proofs of Viazovska and Cohn-Kumar-Miller-Radchenko-Viazovska’s modular form inequalities for 8 and 24-dimensional optimal sphere packings.

Quasimodular forms

For any function

\displaystyle=1-24\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{1}(n)q^{n},

\displaystyle=1-504\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{5}(n)q^{n},

\displaystyle(E_{2}|_{2}S)(z)=z^{-2}E_{2}\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right)

(12)

\displaystyle(E_{6}|_{6}S)(z)=z^{-6}E_{6}\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right)

For a (positive) integer

For

2.4.
Linear programming bounds for optimal sphere packings and modular form inequalities

Cohn–Elkies’ linear programming bound for sphere packings reads as follows.

Theorem 2.1

(Cohn–Elkies
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible function satisfying the following three conditions for some
r>0
:

(1)

f(\mathbf{0})=\widehat{f}(\mathbf{0})>0
;

(2)

f(\mathbf{x})\leq 0
for
\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq r
;

(3)

\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Then the optimal density
\Delta_{d}
of sphere packings in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded above by

\Delta_{d}\leq\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{d}\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{(d/2)!}.

Hence one can prove optimality of certain sphere packings by finding
magic functions
with the corresponding radius
r
.
Based on their numerical experiments, Cohn and Elkies conjectured the existence of magic functions in dimension
2
,
8
, and
24
giving the optimal bounds corresponding to the best known packings.
After a few years, Viazovska
[
16
]
found an elegant construction of a magic function in dimension
8
that proves optimality of the
E_{8}
lattice sphere packing, and it only took one more week for her and other colleagues to find a similar magic function in dimension
24
(for Leech lattice)
[
3
]
.
Existence of a magic function in dimension
2
is still wide open.

Viazovska and Cohn et al.’s constructions are based on ingenious use of (quasi)modular forms.
They first decompose it as a sum of
(\pm 1)
-Fourier eigenfunctions
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
(hence
\widehat{f}_{+}=f_{+}
and
\widehat{f}_{-}=-f_{-}
), and assume that they have a following form:

f_{\pm}(\mathbf{x})=\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\pm}(it)e^{-\pi\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}t}\mathrm{d}t

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\varphi_{\pm}
is a function defined on
\mathbb{H}
.
Here we can assume that
f
is radial (by averaging over spheres centered at the origin), and the
\sin^{2}
factor is added to enforce roots at the desired radii, i.e. the lengths of the vectors in
E_{8}
or Leech lattices
(if
f
is a magic function, then both

and
\widehat{f}
should have zeros at the nonzero lattice points, which follows from the proof of Theorem

Surprisingly, under this assumption, they proved that

is a (
\pm 1
)-Fourier eigenfunction if and only if

behaves as “modular forms”.
For example, when

, we have
\varphi_{\pm}(t)=t^{2}\psi_{\pm}(i/t)
with
2
2

2

. This normalization will be also used in Section

\displaystyle\psi_{+}

\displaystyle=-\phi_{0}=-\frac{(E_{2}E_{4}-E_{6})^{2}}{\Delta},

\displaystyle\psi_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{36}{\pi^{2}}\psi_{S}=-\frac{18}{\pi^{2}}\frac{\Theta_{2}^{12}(2\Theta_{2}^{8}+5\Theta_{2}^{4}\Theta_{4}^{4}+5\Theta_{4}^{8})}{\Delta}.

Under this construction, the corresponding inequalities for

3.
Positive quasimodular forms

In this section, we study properties of quasimodular forms that are positive on the imaginary axis or have nonnegative Fourier coefficients.
We say that a quasimodular form
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
is
positive
if it takes positive real values on the (positive) imaginary axis (
F(it)>0
for all
t>0
).
We denote as
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}\subset\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
for the subset of positive quasimodular forms.
We define
completely positive
quasimodular forms as those with nonnegative (real) Fourier coefficients, i.e.
F(z)=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}a_{n}q^{n}
with
a_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
We denote the set of such forms of weight
w
and depth
s
as
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,++}
.
Both
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}
and
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,++}
are
convex cones
in
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
: they are closed under nonnegative linear combinations.
Clearly we have
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,++}\subset\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}
, and the inclusion is strict in general (e.g.
\Delta(z)=q\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{24}\in\mathcal{QM}_{12}^{0,+}
but
\Delta(z)=q-24q^{2}+252q^{3}-1472q^{4}+\cdots\not\in\mathcal{QM}_{12}^{0,++}
).

3.1.
Derivative and positivity

Differentiation preserves complete positivity of quasimodular cusp forms, since it changes the
n
-th Fourier coefficient from
a_{n}
to
na_{n}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
and
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1}
.
Assume
F
is a cusp form.
Then
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,++}
if and only if
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,++}
.

Positivity (not necessarily complete) is not preserved under derivatives in general.
For example, the discriminant form
\Delta
is positive due to its product expansion, but its derivative
\Delta^{\prime}=E_{2}\Delta
has a unique simple zero on the imaginary axis (
\lim_{t\to\infty}E_{2}(it)=1
and
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}E_{2}(it)=-\infty
).
However, the positivity is preserved under
antiderivatives.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
be a cusp form.
If
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,+}
, then
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}
.

Proof.

Let
f(t):=F(it)
for
t>0
.
If
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,+}
, then
\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}t}=-2\pi F^{\prime}(it)<0
and
f(t)
is monotone decreasing for all
t
.
Hence
f(t)>\lim_{u\to\infty}f(u)=0
.
∎

Complete positivity can be characterized as positivity of (higher) derivatives.
To prove this, we need the following version of Bernstein’s theorem.
3
3

3

This theorem was the motivation for the naming of
complete
positivity.

Theorem 3.3

(Bernstein)
.

Let
g:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

g(t)
is a
completely monotone function
, i.e.
(-1)^{n}g^{(n)}(t)\geq 0
for all
n\geq 0
and
t>0
.

(2)

There exists a unique nonnegative measure
\mu
on
(0,\infty)
such that

g(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tu}\mathrm{d}\mu(u)

where the integral converges absolutely.

A proof can be found in
[
13

.
Note that the original version of the Bernstein’s theorem consider functions on
[0,\infty)
and finite measures, and this is a slightly generalized version of it.

Proposition 3.4
.

A cusp form
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
is completely positive if and only if its derivatives are all positive.

Proof.

This is a direct corollary of Theorem
3.3
.
Observe that

Although the measure

∎

3.2.
Serre derivative and positivity

We can also prove that
anti-
Serre derivative preserves positivity, which is a simple but surprisingly powerful theorem.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
be a quasimodular form.
Assume that there exists
k
and
t_{0}>0
such that
(\partial_{k}F)(it)>0
for all
0<t<t_{0}
and
F(it_{0})>0
.
Then
F(it)>0
for all
0<t\leq t_{0}
.

Proof.

By
\Delta^{\prime}=E_{2}\Delta
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{F(it)}{\Delta(it)^{\frac{k}{12}}}\right)=(-2\pi)\frac{F^{\prime}(it)\Delta(it)^{\frac{k}{12}}-F(it)\frac{k}{12}E_{2}(it)\Delta(it)^{\frac{k}{12}}}{\Delta(it)^{\frac{k}{6}}}=(-2\pi)\frac{(\partial_{k}F)(it)}{\Delta(it)^{\frac{k}{12}}}<0,

hence
t\mapsto F(it)/\Delta(it)^{\frac{k}{12}}
is monotone decreasing and the result follows.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
be a quasimodular form.
If
\partial_{k}F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,+}
and
F(it)>0
for
sufficiently large
t>0
, then
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}
.
In particular, the assumption holds if all the Fourier coefficients of
F
are real and the first nonzero Fourier coefficient of
F
is positive.

Proof.

First assertion directly follows from Proposition
3.5
.
For the last assertion, when
F
has a Fourier expansion
F(z)=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}a_{n}q^{n}
with
a_{n_{0}}>0
, then

e^{2\pi n_{0}t}F(it)=a_{n_{0}}+e^{-2\pi t}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}+1}a_{n}e^{-2\pi(n-n_{0}-1)t}

and
\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{2\pi n_{0}t}F(it)=a_{n_{0}}>0
, so
F(it)>0
for sufficiently large
t
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

It is also possible to

the differential equation
\partial_{k}F=G
and express

in

Proposition 3.8

Let

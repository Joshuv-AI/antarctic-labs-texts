Inverse norm estimation of perturbed Laplace operators and corresponding eigenvalue problems
Kouta Sekine

Abstract




﻿

Abstract
.
In numerical existence proofs for solutions of the semi-linear elliptic system,
evaluating the norm of the inverse of a perturbed Laplace operator plays an important role.
We reveal an eigenvalue problem to design a method for verifying the invertibility of the operator and evaluating the norm of its inverse based on Liu’s method and the Temple-Lehman-Goerisch method.
We apply the inverse-norm’s estimation to the Dirichlet boundary value problem of the Lotka-Volterra system with diffusion terms and confirm the efficacy of our method.

Introduction

Numerical existence proofs for solutions of differential equations have been developed in recent decades and applied to various problems for which purely analytical methods have failed (see, for example,
[
9
]
and the references therein).
Our objective is the boundary value problem of the system of elliptic differential equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u_{1}=f_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})&{\rm in~~}\Omega,\\
-\Delta u_{2}=f_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})&{\rm in~~}\Omega,\\
~~~\cdots&\\
-\Delta u_{m}=f_{m}(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})&{\rm in~~}\Omega,\\
u_{1}=u_{2}=\cdots=u_{m}=0&{\rm on~~}\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}~(n=1,2,3)
is a bounded domain with a Lipschitz boundary,
and
f_{i}:(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{m}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
(
i=1,2,\cdots,m
) are given operators.
Here, for
H^{1}(\Omega)
denoting the first order real
L^{2}
-Sobolev space,
we define

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):u=0~\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega\}
with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}_{0}}:=(\nabla\cdot,\nabla\cdot)_{L^{2}}
.
For two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
,
{\mathcal{B}}(X,Y)
stands for the space of bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
defined over the whole of
X
with the norm
\|T\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,Y)}:=\sup\{\|Tu\|_{Y}/\|u\|_{X}:{u\in X\setminus\{0\}}\}
.
We simply write
{\mathcal{B}}(X)={\mathcal{B}}(X,X)
.
The expression “
T:D\subset X\rightarrow Y
” is equivalent to “an operator
T
from
X
to
Y
is defined on
D\subset X
”.
The domain of
T
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}(T)
.
When
\mathcal{D}(T)=X
, we simply write
T:X\rightarrow Y
.

To write (
1
) as an operator equation, we prepare required notation.
Let
X:=(L^{2}(\Omega))^{m}
and
V:=(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{m}
be the Hilbert spaces with the inner products
(\cdot,\cdot)_{X}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}_{0}}
, respectively.
We denote the topological dual of
V
by
V^{*}
with the duality product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the pair
\{V,V^{*}\}
.
We define
{\mathcal{A}}:V\rightarrow V^{*}
by

\langle{\mathcal{A}}u,v\rangle:=(u,v)_{V}
for all
u,v\in V

and
the densely-defined closed operator
A:{\mathcal{D}}(A)\subset X\rightarrow X
by

Au={\mathcal{A}}u,~u\in{\mathcal{D}}(A):=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):{\mathcal{A}}u\in X\}
.
The operator
A
is a positive self-adjoint operator with the compact inverse
A^{-1}:X\rightarrow X
.
Then, (
1
) is characterized as the operator equation

\displaystyle Au=f(u),

(6)

where
f(u)=(f_{1}(u),\cdots,f_{m}(u))^{T},~u\in V
assumed to be Fréchet differentiable.
In numerical existence proofs for (
6
), the following linear operators are considered:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{A}}-Q

\displaystyle:~~V\rightarrow V^{*},

\displaystyle L=A-Q

\displaystyle:~~{\mathcal{D}}({A})\subset X\rightarrow X,

where
Q\in{\mathcal{B}}(V,X)
.
Choosing
Q
as
f^{\prime}[\hat{u}]:V\rightarrow X
, the Fréchet derivative of
f
at an approximate solution
\hat{u}\in V
of (
6
), is often useful in existing methods.
To ensure the existence of a solution of (
6
) and obtain an explicit enclosure of the solution numerically,
it is important to confirm the invertibility of
L
(or
{\mathcal{L}}
) and estimate an upper bound of the inverse norm
\|L^{-1}\|
(or
\|{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\|
)

Methods in Category 1 estimate the desired norms
\|L^{-1}\|

by solving the linear problems

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~\phi\in V~~\mbox{s.t.}~~{\mathcal{L}}\phi=g\in V^{*}

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~\phi\in{\mathcal{D}}(A)~~\mbox{s.t.}~~L\phi=g\in X,

The third author of this paper has previously proposed a method in Category 1 in a general Banach space setting
[
12
]
.
Although this method would give a rough estimation of the inverse norm, it is widely applicable to both integral equations and differential equations. Subsequently, several works

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~(\lambda,\phi)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times V~~\mbox{s.t.}~~{\mathcal{L}}\phi=\lambda{\mathcal{A}}\phi

\|{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\|=|\lambda_{1}|^{-1}

Subsequently, Liu and the third author of this paper proposed an a priori error estimation method for evaluating the eigenvalues of the weak Laplacian

By applying this method to (

The eigenvalue evaluation method in

Therefore, if more accuracy is required, we can implement a refinement step.
This is a remarkable advantage of eigenvalue based methods,
whereas comparing Category 1 and 2 in a general level is difficult

Despite the methods in Category 2 have been applied to
\|{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\|

One alternative way is to use the bound

However, this may lead to overestimation.
Therefore, researchers have desired to solve the following questions:

I.

Does the eigenvalue problem corresponding to
\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}
exist?

II.

If so, can we solve it?

The purpose of this paper is to answer these question.
We reveal the eigenvalue problem corresponding to the inverse norm
\|L^{-1}\|

Then, using Liu’s evaluation method

2
Eigenvalue problem corresponding to
\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}

This section reveals an eigenvalue problem corresponding to the operator norm
\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}
.
We assume
Q\in\mathcal{B}(X)
and denote its dual operator by
Q^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(X)
.
In our method, the operators
A^{-\frac{1}{2}}:X\rightarrow X
and
A^{\frac{1}{2}}:{\mathcal{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})\subset X\rightarrow X
play an important role. They are defined by the spectral decomposition

\displaystyle A^{-\frac{1}{2}}u:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u,\psi_{i})_{X}\psi_{i}~~~\mbox{and}~~~A^{\frac{1}{2}}:=(A^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{-1},

where
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is the set of eigenfunctions of
A
completely orthonormalized in
X
, and
\mu_{i}
stands for the eigenvalue corresponding to
\psi_{i}
.
Note that the definition of
A^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is consistent with that given by the Dunford integral
[
24
, Remark 2.7]
.
The followings properties of
A^{\frac{1}{2}}
are known
[
24
]
:

-

{\mathcal{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})=V
;

-

A^{\frac{1}{2}}
is positive and self-adjpoint;

-

A^{\frac{1}{2}}
has a compact inverse
A^{-\frac{1}{2}}:X\rightarrow X
;

-

(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}v)_{X}=(u,v)_{V},~u,v\in V
;

-

(A^{-\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{-\frac{1}{2}}v)_{V}=(u,v)_{X},~~u,v\in X
.

We define the densely-defined closed linear operator
T:V\subset X\rightarrow X
by

\displaystyle T:=A^{\frac{1}{2}}-A^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q^{*},

obtaining the relation

(Tu,Tv)_{X}=(u,v)_{V}-((Q+Q^{*})u,v)_{X}+(A^{-1}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}
.
The following eigenvalue problem corresponds to the desired norm
\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}
(this is proved in Theorem
2.1
below):

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~(u,\lambda^{\prime})\in V\times{[0,\infty)}~~~\mbox{s.t.}~~~(Tu,Tv)_{X}=\lambda^{\prime}(u,v)_{X}~~\mbox{for~all}~~v\in V.

(8)

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}\geq 0
be the minimal eigenvalue of (
8
).
If
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}>0
, then
L
is bijective and

\displaystyle\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}}}.

(9)

Proof.

We consider the minimal positive constant
K_{T}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{X}\leq K_{T}\|T\phi\|_{X},~\phi\in V.

A variational method ensures that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{T}^{2}}=\inf_{\phi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|T\phi\|_{X}^{2}}{\|\phi\|_{X}^{2}}=\inf_{\phi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(T\phi,T\phi)_{X}}{(\phi,\phi)_{X}}=\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}.

Therefore, when
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}>0
, we have

K_{T}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}}}~(<\infty).

This ensures that
T
is injective because

\displaystyle T\phi=0\Leftrightarrow\|T\phi\|_{X}=0\Rightarrow\|\phi\|_{X}=0\Leftrightarrow\phi=0.

Thus, we have
\mbox{Null}(T):=\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}(T)~:~Tu=0\}=\{0\}
.

Because
\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{X}=\|u\|_{V}
for all
u\in V={\mathcal{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
,
we define an operator
\tilde{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\in{\mathcal{B}}(V,X)
with the relation
A^{\frac{1}{2}}u=\tilde{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}u
for all
u\in V
.
We denote
\tilde{T}:=\tilde{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}-A^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q^{*}|_{V}\in{\mathcal{B}}(V,X)
, where
\cdot|_{V}
stands for the restriction of the domain.
Because
Tu=\tilde{T}u
for all
u\in V={\mathcal{D}}(T)
, we have
\mbox{Null}(T)=\mbox{Null}(\tilde{T})
and
{\mathcal{R}}(T)={\mathcal{R}}(\tilde{T})
, where
{\mathcal{R}}(T)
stands for the range of
T
.
Because
\tilde{T}
is injective and
\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is bijective,
\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{T}
is injective.
Also,
\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q^{*}|_{V}\in{\mathcal{B}}(V)
is compact because
A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
is compact, and
\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)
and
Q^{*}|_{V}\in{\mathcal{B}}(V,X)
are bounded.
We can express
\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{T}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{T}=I-\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q^{*}|_{V}\in{\mathcal{B}}(V).

Therefore, the Riesz-Schauder theorem ensures that
\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{T}
is bijective
and so is
\tilde{T}
because of the bijectivity of
\tilde{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
As a result, we ensure that
{\mathcal{R}}(T)={\mathcal{R}}(\tilde{T})=X
and
T
is bijective.
Thus,
T
has a bounded inverse, that is,
{\mathcal{D}}(T^{-1})=X
and
T^{-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
.

Since
T
is bijective, its adjoint operator
T^{*}=A^{\frac{1}{2}}-QA^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is also bijective.
Because we have

\displaystyle T^{*}u=(A^{\frac{1}{2}}-QA^{-\frac{1}{2}})u=LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}u,~~u\in V,

(10)

L
is expressed as

\displaystyle Lu=T^{*}A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,~~u\in{\mathcal{D}}(A).

Therefore, the bijectivity of
A^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
T^{*}
ensures that of
L
.
The norm of
L^{-1}
is evaluated as

\displaystyle\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|L^{-1}\psi\|_{V}}{\|\psi\|_{X}}=\sup_{\psi\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{-1}\psi\|_{X}}{\|\psi\|_{X}}=\sup_{\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}(A)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|_{X}}{\|L\psi\|_{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\psi\|_{X}}{\|LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|_{X}}=\sup_{\psi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\psi\|_{X}}{\|T^{*}\psi\|_{X}}=\sup_{\psi\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|(T^{*})^{-1}\psi\|_{X}}{\|\psi\|_{X}}=\|(T^{*})^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}.

Because both
T^{-1}
and
(T^{*})^{-1}
are bounded linear operators on
X
, we have
(T^{*})^{-1}=(T^{-1})^{*}
and
\|(T^{-1})^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}=\|T^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}=

\displaystyle\|(T^{*})^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}=\|T^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}=\sup_{\psi\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|T^{-1}\psi\|_{X}}{\|\psi\|_{X}}=\sup_{\phi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\phi\|_{X}}{\|T\phi\|_{X}}=K_{T}.

∎

Remark 2.2
.

An alternative problem for (
8
) corresponding to
\|L^{-1}\|
is

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~~(u,\lambda^{\prime})\in V\times[0,\infty)~~~\mbox{s.t.}~~~(T^{*}u,T^{*}v)_{X}=\lambda^{\prime}(u,v)_{X}~~\mbox{for~all}~~v\in V,

(11)

where
(T^{*}u,T^{*}v)_{V}=(u,v)_{V}-(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,QA^{-\frac{1}{2}}v)_{X}-(QA^{-\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}v)_{X}+(QA^{-\frac{1}{2}}u,QA^{-\frac{1}{2}}v)_{X}
.
If the minimal eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}
of (
11
) is positive, we have
\|L^{-1}\|=1/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}}

(see Theorem
A.1
).
However, it is problematic to compute the terms associated with
A^{\frac{1}{2}}
or
A^{-\frac{1}{2}}
directly.
Another alternative problem equivalent to (
11
) is

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~~(u,\lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{D}}(A)\times[0,\infty)~~~\mbox{s.t.}~~~(Lu,Lv)_{X}=\lambda^{\prime}(u,v)_{V}~~\mbox{for~all}~~v\in{\mathcal{D}}(A).

(12)

We obtain this problem by applying the replacements
u^{\prime}=A^{-\frac{1}{2}}u
and
v^{\prime}=A^{-\frac{1}{2}}v
to (
11
),
eliminating the terms including
A^{\frac{1}{2}}
or
A^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Problem (
12
) includes the term
(Au,Av)_{X}
and requires higher-order approximations to estimate the desired eigenvalue.
We can avoid such problematic calculations by considering (
8
).

3
Lower bound of minimal eigenvalue

In this section, we estimate a lower bound for the minimal eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}
of (
8
) using Liu’s method
[
5
]
.
For this purpose, we introduce an infinite-dimensional eigenvalue problem as follows.
Let
R_{h}:V\rightarrow V_{h}
be the Ritz projection defined by

\displaystyle((I-R_{h})u,v_{h})_{V}=0,~u\in V,~v_{h}\in V_{h},

(13)

where
V_{h}\subset V
is a finite-dimensional subspace spanned by the bases
\{\phi_{1},~\phi_{2},\cdots,~\phi_{N}\}
.
Replacing the term
(A^{-1}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}
in (
8
) to
(R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}
,
we obtain the eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~(u,\lambda)\in V\times{\mathbb{R}}~~~\mbox{s.t.}~~~(u,v)_{V}-((Q+Q^{*})u,v)_{X}+(R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}=\lambda(u,v)_{X},~v\in V.

(14)

All the eigenvalues of (
14
) are real.
This is confirmed by the following consideration.
Let
P_{h}:X\rightarrow V_{h}
be the orthogonal projection defined by
((I-P_{h})u,v_{h})_{X}=0,u\in X,v_{h}\in V_{h}
.
We define
A_{h}:V_{h}\rightarrow V_{h}
by
(A_{h}u_{h},v_{h})_{X}=(u_{h},v_{h})_{V},~u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}
.
Because
R_{h}A^{-1}=A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}
( see
[
1
, (1.24)]
), we have

\displaystyle(R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}=(A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}=(QP_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u,v)_{X},~u,v\in V.

The reality follows from the relationship

\displaystyle(QP_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u,v)_{X}=(u,QP_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}v)_{X},~u,v\in V.

For the modified problem (
14
), we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

The minimal eigenvalues
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}
and
\lambda_{\min}
respectively of (
8
) and (
14
) satisfy the inequality

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}\leq\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}.

Proof.

For any
u\in V
, we have

\displaystyle(A^{-1}u,u)_{X}=(R_{h}A^{-1}u,u)_{X}+((I-R_{h})A^{-1}u,(I-R_{h})A^{-1}u)_{V}\geq(R_{h}A^{-1}u,u)_{X}.

Therefore, for the eigenfunction
\psi^{\prime}\in V
corresponding to
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})_{V}-((Q+Q^{*})\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})_{X}+(A^{-1}Q^{*}\psi^{\prime},Q^{*}\psi^{\prime})_{X}}{(\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})_{X}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\psi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(\psi,\psi)_{V}-((Q+Q^{*})\psi,\psi)_{X}+(R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}\psi,Q^{*}\psi)_{X}}{(\psi,\psi)_{X}}=\lambda_{\min}.

∎

Lemma
3.1
allows us to use
\lambda_{\min}
as a lower bound for
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}
.
To estimate
\lambda_{\min}
explicitly, we discretize (
14
), obtaining the following problem

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~(u_{h},\lambda_{h})\in V_{h}\times{\mathbb{R}}~~~\mbox{s.t.}~~~

\displaystyle~~(u_{h},v_{h})_{V}-((Q+Q^{*})u_{h},v_{h})_{X}+(R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}u_{h},Q^{*}v_{h})_{X}=\lambda_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})_{X},~v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(15)

This is equivalent to the matrix eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},\lambda_{h})^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}~\mbox{s.t.}~\left(\mbox{\boldmath$D$}-\left(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}+\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}\right)+\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$D$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\lambda_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}\mbox{\boldmath$x$},

(16)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$D$},~\mbox{\boldmath$L$},~\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
are real matrices respectively defined by

\mbox{\boldmath$D$}_{ij}:=(\phi_{j},\phi_{i})_{V},~\mbox{\boldmath$L$}_{ij}:=(\phi_{j},\phi_{i})_{X},~\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{ij}:=(Q\phi_{j},\phi_{i})_{X},

and
\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}
denotes the transposed matrix of
Q
.

By applying Liu’s method
[
5
]
to (
14
) and (
16
), we estimate eigenvalues
\lambda
of (
14
) in the following theorem.
Hereafter,
C_{p}
denotes the Poincaré constant that satisfies
\|u\|_{X}\leq C_{p}\|u\|_{V}
for all
u\in V
.
A positive number
\sigma
is chosen to satisfy
\sigma>\|Q+Q^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,X)}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\lambda^{(i)}
and
\lambda_{h}^{(i)}~(i=1,2,\cdots)
be the
i
th eigenvalues of (
14
) and (
16
), respectively.
Suppose that there exists a positive constant
C_{h}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\|(I-R_{h})u\|_{X}\leq C_{h}\|(I-R_{h})u\|_{V}

for all
u\in V
.
Let us define
C_{M_{\sigma}}
as

\displaystyle C_{M_{\sigma}}:=C_{h}\left(1+\frac{\|\sigma-\left(Q+Q^{*}\right)\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}+C_{p}^{2}\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}^{2}}{\lambda_{h}^{(1)}+\sigma}\right).

(17)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{h}^{(i)}+\sigma}{1+C_{M_{\sigma}}^{2}\left(\lambda_{h}^{(i)}+\sigma\right)}-\sigma\leq\lambda^{(i)}\leq\lambda_{h}^{(i)}.

(18)

Remark 3.3
.

Although Theorem
3.2
gives a rough lower bound, particularly when
\sigma
is large,
the lower bound approaches
\lambda^{(i)}
as
C_{h}\downarrow 0
.
If the lower bound in (
18
) for
i=1
is positive, Lemma
3.1
guarantees
\lambda^{\prime}_{\min}>0
and thus
L
is nonsingular,
the norm of which is evaluated as

\displaystyle\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}\leq\left(\frac{\lambda_{h}^{(1)}+\sigma}{1+C_{M_{\sigma}}^{2}\left(\lambda_{h}^{(1)}+\sigma\right)}-\sigma\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

When the positivity of
\lambda^{\prime}_{\min}
is not confirmed via Theorem
3.2
or more accuracy is required, we try to improve the accuracy using the Temple-Lehman-Goerisch method
[
2
]
.

3.1
Proof of Theorem
3.2
, Step1 — Applying Liu’s theorem to (
14
)

Because the right-hand side inequality in (
18
) immediately follows from the Rayleigh-Ritz bound, we prove the left-hand side inequality.
Liu’s theorem
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
requires the positive definiteness of the bilinear form in the left-hand side of (
14
).
Therefore, we add a perturbation so that the minimal eigenvalue of (
14
) becomes positive.
More precisely, we add
\sigma(u,v)_{X}
to both sides, and rewrite the problem as

\displaystyle\mbox{Find}~(u,\mu_{\sigma})^{T}\in V\times{\mathbb{R}}~~~\mbox{s.t.}~~~(u,v)_{M_{\sigma}}=\mu_{\sigma}(u,v)_{X},~~v\in V,

(19)

where
\mu_{\sigma}=\lambda+\sigma
and
(u,v)_{M_{\sigma}}
is the bilinear form defined by

\displaystyle(u,v)_{M_{\sigma}}:=(u,v)_{V}-((Q+Q^{*})u,v)_{X}+(R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}v)_{X}+\sigma(u,v)_{X}.

(20)

We then show that
(\cdot,\cdot)_{M_{\sigma}}
is an inner product of
V
to use
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Because
R_{h}A^{-1}=A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle(R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}u)_{X}=(A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u,Q^{*}u)_{X}=(QP_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u,u)_{X}=\|A_{h}^{-\frac{1}{2}}P_{h}Q^{*}u\|_{X}^{2},~u\in V.

(21)

This ensures that the third term of
(u,v)_{M_{\sigma}}
in (
20
) is nonnegative and symmetric with respect to
u,v\in V
.
Moreover, the inequality
\sigma>\|Q+Q^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,X)}
leads to

\displaystyle\left(\sigma u,u\right)_{X}-\left((Q+Q^{*})u,u\right)_{X}\geq\left(\sigma-\|Q+Q^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,X)}\right)\|u\|_{X}^{2}>0.

(22)

Consequently, we confirm that

\displaystyle(u,u)_{M_{\sigma}}>0,~u\in V\setminus\{0\}~~~\mbox{and}~~~(u,v)_{M_{\sigma}}=(v,u)_{M_{\sigma}},~u,v\in V,

that is, the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{M_{\sigma}}
is an inner product, and therefore we can use
\|u\|_{M_{\sigma}}:=\sqrt{(u,u)_{M_{\sigma}}},~u\in V
as a norm for
V
.

Let us define anther orthogonal projection
R_{M_{\sigma}}:V\rightarrow V_{h}
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{M_{\sigma}}
by

\displaystyle((I-R_{M_{\sigma}})u,v_{h})_{M_{\sigma}}=0,~v_{h}\in V_{h}.

By applying
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
to (
19
),
we confirm inequality (
18
) for a
positive number
C_{M_{\sigma}}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\|(I-R_{M_{\sigma}})u\|_{X}\leq C_{M_{\sigma}}\|(I-R_{M_{\sigma}})u\|_{M_{\sigma}},~u\in V.

(23)

3.2
Proof of Theorem
3.2
, Step2 — Calculation of
C_{M_{\sigma}}

To complete the proof,
we confirm that
C_{M_{\sigma}}
defined by (
17
) satisfies (
23
).
In the following proof is inspired by the approach provided in
[
7
, Theorem 5]
.
Let us define
B:X\rightarrow X
by

\displaystyle B:=\sigma I-(Q+Q^{*})+Q^{*}R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}.

Because (
21
) and (
22
) ensures
(Bu,u)_{X}>0,~u\in V
,
we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{V}^{2}=(u,u)_{V}\leq(u,u)_{V}+\left(Bu,u\right)_{X}=\|u\|_{M_{\sigma}}^{2},~\forall u\in V.

Moreover, for any
u\in V
,

\displaystyle\|(I-R_{M_{\sigma}})u\|_{X}\leq

\displaystyle\|(I-R_{h})u\|_{X}+\|(R_{h}-R_{M_{\sigma}})u\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{h}\|(I-R_{h})u\|_{V}+\|R_{M_{\sigma}}(I-R_{h})u\|_{X}.

(24)

We set
e:=(I-R_{h})u
.
For any
v_{h}\in V_{h}
,

\displaystyle(R_{M_{\sigma}}e,v_{h})_{M_{\sigma}}=

\displaystyle(e,v_{h})_{M_{\sigma}}=(e,v_{h})_{V}+(Be,v_{h})_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((I-R_{h})u,v_{h})_{V}+(Be,v_{h})_{X}=(Be,v_{h})_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P_{h}Be,v_{h})_{X}=(P_{h}\psi,v_{h})_{X},

where
\psi:=Be
.
Let
\mbox{\boldmath$G$}:=\mbox{\boldmath$D$}+\sigma\mbox{\boldmath$L$}-\left(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}+\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}\right)+\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$D$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}=\mbox{\boldmath$L$}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$},

where
e
and
\psi
are coefficient vectors of
R_{M_{\sigma}}e
and
P_{h}\psi
, respectively.
We denote the Euclidean norm and its matrix norm by
\|\cdot\|_{E}
.
Since
L
is a symmetric positive-definite matrix, it can be decomposed to
\mbox{\boldmath$L$}=\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{T}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, where
\cdot^{T}
indicates transposition, and
\cdot^{\frac{T}{2}}=(\cdot^{\frac{1}{2}})^{T}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|R_{M_{\sigma}}(I-R_{h})u\|_{X}=

\displaystyle\|R_{M_{\sigma}}e\|_{X}=\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}\|_{E}=\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{T}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{T}{2}}\|_{E}\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}\|_{E}=\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{T}{2}}\|_{E}\|P_{h}\psi\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{T}{2}}\|_{E}\|Be\|_{X}.

(25)

Because
(\cdot,\cdot)_{M_{\sigma}}
is an inner product,
G
is a symmetric positive-definite matrix.
Hence,

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{T}{2}}\|_{E}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{h}^{(1)}+\sigma}.

We obtain the following estimation

\displaystyle\|B\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|\left(\sigma-\left(Q+Q^{*}\right)+QP_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}\right)u\|_{X}}{\|u\|_{X}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sigma-\left(Q+Q^{*}\right)\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}+\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|P_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u\|_{X}}{\|u\|_{X}}.

The second term is further evaluated as

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|P_{h}A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u\|_{X}}{\|u\|_{X}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u\|_{X}}{\|u\|_{X}}\leq C_{p}\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}Q^{*}u\|_{V}}{\|u\|_{X}}=C_{p}\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|R_{h}A^{-1}Q^{*}u\|_{V}}{\|u\|_{X}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|A^{-1}Q^{*}u\|_{V}}{\|u\|_{X}}\leq C_{p}^{2}\sup_{u\in X}\frac{\|Q^{*}u\|_{X}}{\|u\|_{X}}=C_{p}^{2}\|Q^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}.

It follows from the relation
\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}=\|Q^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}
that

\displaystyle\|B\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}\leq\|\sigma-\left(Q+Q^{*}\right)\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}+C_{p}^{2}\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}^{2}.

From (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|R_{M_{\sigma}}(I-R_{h})u\|_{X}\leq

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{\frac{T}{2}}\|_{E}\|Be\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\sigma-\left(Q+Q^{*}\right)\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}+C_{p}^{2}\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}^{2}}{\lambda_{h}^{(1)}+\sigma}\|e\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{h}\frac{\|\sigma-\left(Q+Q^{*}\right)\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}+C_{p}^{2}\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X)}^{2}}{\lambda_{h}^{(1)}+\sigma}\|(I-R_{h})u\|_{V}.

As a result, it follows from (
24
) that

\displaystyle\|(I-R_{M_{\sigma}})u\|_{X}\leq

\displaystyle C_{h}\|(I-R_{h})u\|_{V}+\|R_{M_{\sigma}}(I-R_{h})u\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{M_{\sigma}}\|(I-R_{h})u\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{M_{\sigma}}\|(I-R_{M_{\sigma}})u\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{M_{\sigma}}\|(I-R_{M_{\sigma}})u\|_{M_{\sigma}}

Thus, we complete the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Conclusion

We provided the answers to the following questions:

I.

Does the eigenvalue problem corresponding to
\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}
exist?

II.

If so, can we solve it?

Theorems
2.1
and
A.1
answered to Question I,
revealing the eigenvalue problem corresponding to
\|L^{-1}\|
.
The fractional operator
A^{\frac{1}{2}}
played an important role in these theorems.
Using Liu’s theorem
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
, we proved Theorem
3.2
that gives a rough lower bound for the eigenvalues of the revealed problem.
We applied our method to the Dirichlet boundary value problem of the Lotka-Volterra system with diffusion terms.
We saw that the Temple-Lehman-Goerisch method has a significant influence on the accuracy of
\|L^{-1}\|
.

Appendix A
Simple eigenvalue problem corresponding to
\|L^{-1}\|

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}
be the minimal eigenvalue of the problem (
11
).
If
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}>0
, then
L
is bijective and

\displaystyle\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

Consider the minimal positive constant
K
that satisfies

\|\phi\|_{X}\leq K\|LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi\|_{X},~\phi\in V.

The relationship between
K
and
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K^{2}}=\inf_{\phi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi\|_{X}^{2}}{\|\phi\|_{X}^{2}}=\inf_{\phi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi,LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi)_{X}}{(\phi,\phi)_{X}}=\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}~(>0).

Therefore, when
\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}>0
, we have

K=1/{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}^{\prime}}}~(<\infty)
,
which ensures that
LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is injective.
As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, linear operators
LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
L
are bijective.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|L^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(X,V)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|L^{-1}\phi\|_{V}}{\|\phi\|_{X}}=\sup_{\phi\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{-1}\phi\|_{X}}{\|\phi\|_{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|(LA^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{-1}\phi\|_{X}}{\|\phi\|_{X}}=\inf_{\phi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|LA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi\|_{X}}{\|\phi\|_{X}}=K.

∎

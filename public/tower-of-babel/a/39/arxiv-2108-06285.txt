A geometric approach to spectrum interlacing
Ricardo S. Leite, Carlos Tomei

Abstract
We provide a detailed description of the maps associated with spectral interlacing in two scenarios, for rank one perturbations and bordering of symmetric and Hermitian matrices. The arguments rely on standard techniques of nonlinear analysis.



Keywords:
Spectral interlacing, degree theory.

MSC-class:
15A29, 15A42, 15B57

Introduction

We recall two standard results, presented in more detail in
[
6
]
. Endow
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with the Euclidean inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
which is anti-linear in the second coordinate. For

, the linear rank one map
v\otimes v=vv^{\ast}
is
u\mapsto\langle u,v\rangle\ v
. Let

be an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix, with ordered eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Cor. 4.3.9, Th. 4.3.21
[
6
]
)

For
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, let the eigenvalues of
T=T(v)=S+v\otimes v
be
\mu_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{n}
. Then the eigenvalues of
S
and
T
interlace,

\lambda_{1}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\mu_{n}\ .

Conversely, for a sequence
\{\mu_{j}\}
interlacing
\{\lambda_{k}\}
as above, there is
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
for which the eigenvalues of
T=S+v\otimes v
are
\{\mu_{j}\}
.

The second result is Cauchy’s interlacing theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Th. 4.3.17
[
6
]
)

Let
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{n+1}
be the eigenvalues of the bordered matrix

v&c\end{pmatrix}.

Then the eigenvalues of
S
and
T
interlace,

\mu_{1}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\mu_{n+1}\ .

For a sequence
\{\mu_{j}\}
interlacing
\{\lambda_{k}\}
, there exist
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
c>0
for which the eigenvalues of
T=T(v,c)
are
\{\mu_{j}\}
.

\displaystyle\sigma_{o}(S+v\otimes v)\hskip 51.21504pt(v,c)\ \mapsto\sigma_{o}(T(v,c))

Given a function

\operatorname{abs}:{\mathbb{K}}^{n}\to{\cal{O}}_{Q},\quad v=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\ q_{j}\ \mapsto\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}|c_{j}|\ q_{j}\ .

[

considered analogous inverse problems and pointed out the relevance of discrete information provided by strings of signs. We believe our presentation sheds some light on the issue.

Theorems

and

are a strengthened version of a

special case of the celebrated Horn’s conjecture
[
5
]
, whose resolution, after work by several authors (
[

]
,
[

]

[
12
]

[

]

. The conjecture answers a question by Weyl

,

with

is indeed

.

Part of the statements in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4

may be deduced from the sophisticated machinery of
symplectic geometry. To give an idea of a more familiar context, the Schur-Horn theorem for Hermitian
matrices
[
4
]
is a consequence of the powerful theorems about the convexity of the
image of moment maps of torus actions by Atiyah (
[
1
]
) and

[

]
). The result for symmetric matrices then follows by an

[
2
]
). Similarly, the surjectivity of the maps

and

also follow from convexity arguments, once the appropriate symplectic setting is identified. Here, we take what
Thompson (
[
17

) calls a low road in linear algebra, but gain some
information which does not follow directly from rote application of these more
general results.

The proof of Theorem

relies on a combination of well known facts of real analysis, condensed in Lemma

and

see how faces of the domain are ‘creased’ by either

or

the faces of the image parallelotope.
Theorem

is a simple consequence of Theorem

A real analysis lemma

The outline of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is the same for the functions
F:{\mathcal{D}}_{F}\to{\cal{P}}_{F}
and
G:{\mathcal{D}}_{G}\to{\cal{P}}_{g}
.
In a nutshell, we must check the hypotheses of the lemma below, which combines familiar arguments from real analysis. We state it so as it applies directly to
F
. Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be
{\cal{O}}_{I}
, the closed positive orthant of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and
{\cal{P}}
be
{\cal{P}}_{F}
.
Denote by
\operatorname{int}X
the interior of a set
X
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\widetilde{H}:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a function satisfying the following properties.

(H1)

\widetilde{H}
is a continuous, proper map, i.e.,
\lim_{\|v\|\to\infty}\|\widetilde{H}(v)\|=\infty
.

(H2)

The restriction of
\widetilde{H}
to
\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
is a
C^{1}
map with invertible Jacobians.

(H3)

Some point of
\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
is taken by
\widetilde{H}
to
\operatorname{int}{\cal{P}}
.

(H4)

The restriction
\hat{H}:\partial{\mathcal{D}}\to\partial{\cal{P}}
is a homeomorphism.

(H5)

No point of
\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
is taken by
\widetilde{H}
to
\partial{\cal{P}}
.

Then the image of
\widetilde{H}
is
{\cal{P}}
and the function
\widetilde{H}:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\cal{P}}
obtained from
\widetilde{H}
by restricting its counterdomain is a homeomorphism which restricts to a diffeomorphism between
\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
and
\operatorname{int}{\cal{P}}
.

In order to apply the lemma for the function
G
, consider
\widetilde{H}:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, where
{\mathcal{D}}={\cal{O}}_{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and set
{\cal{P}}={\cal{P}}_{G}
. The proof follows verbatim.

Proof.

We first show that points in the connected components of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\widetilde{H}(\partial{\mathcal{D}})={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\partial{\cal{P}}
have the same number of preimages. Take
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\partial{\cal{P}}
. By connectivity,
it suffices to show that, for a small open neighborhood
U
of
\mu
, points
in
U
have the same number of preimages. If
\mu
has infinite
preimages, by properness (hypothesis (H1)) they have to accumulate at some preimage
v_{\ast}
.
Preimages in
\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
are isolated, by the inverse function
theorem (use hypothesis (H2)), thus
v_{\ast}\in\partial{\mathcal{D}}
, contradicting
\widetilde{H}(v_{\ast})=\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\partial{\cal{P}}
.

Thus
\mu
has a finite number of isolated preimages, say
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
. From the inverse function theorem, for every sufficiently small open ball
B
centered around
\mu
,
there are open disjoint sets
V_{i},i=1,\ldots,k
, each containing
v_{i}
,
for which
\widetilde{H}
takes
V_{i}
to
B
diffeomorphically. Thus, points in
B

have at least
k
preimages.

If, for balls
B_{n}
of radius
1/n
there are
points
\tilde{\mu}_{n}
with (at least)
k+1
preimages, one preimage
w_{n}\in{\mathcal{D}}
is outside
\cup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
. By properness, they
accumulate at
w_{\ast}\notin\cup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
. But then
\widetilde{H}(w_{\ast})=\mu
, contradicting the fact that
\mu
has exactly
k
preimages, all
in
\cup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
.

By (H3), there is
v\in\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\widetilde{H}(v)\in\operatorname{int}{\cal{P}}
. From the argument above and
(H4)
, the set
{\cal{P}}
lies in the image of
\widetilde{H}
. If, for some
w\in\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
we have
\widetilde{H}(w)\notin{\cal{P}}
, then the segment joining
v
in
w
, which lies in
\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
, must contain a point whose image lies in
\partial{\cal{P}}
, contradicting (H5). Thus, the image of
\widetilde{H}
is
{\cal{P}}
and the associated function
H:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\cal{P}}
is well defined.

From (H1),
\widetilde{H}:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is proper and has a well defined topological degree
\deg(\widetilde{H},\mu)
(an excellent reference for degree theory is
[
15
]
) for any regular value
\mu\in\operatorname{int}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\partial{\cal{P}}
, i.e., a point whose preimages are regular points (regular values are dense, by Sard’s theorem). From (H4), for
\mu\in\operatorname{int}{\cal{P}}
,
\deg(\widetilde{H},\mu)=\pm 1
. Moreover,

\deg(\widetilde{H},\mu)\ =\ \sum_{v\in\widetilde{H}^{-1}(\mu)}\operatorname{sgn}\det D\widetilde{H}(v)

and all preimages are counted with the same sign, by (H5). Indeed, the determinant of the Jacobian
DH(v)
is never zero for
v\in\operatorname{int}{\mathcal{D}}
and is continuous, by (H1). Thus every point of
{\cal{P}}
has a unique preimage.

\blacksquare

\blacksquare

We are left with proving the hypotheses of the lemma for the counterparts

and
\widetilde{G}:{\mathcal{D}}_{G}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
of the functions
F
and

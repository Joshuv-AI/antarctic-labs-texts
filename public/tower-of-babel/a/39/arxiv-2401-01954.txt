Word-Representability of Graphs with respect to Split Recomposition
Tithi Dwary, K. V. Krishna

Abstract
In this work, we show that the class of word-representable graphs is closed under split recomposition and determine the representation number of the graph obtained by recomposing two word-representable graphs. Accordingly, we show that the class of parity graphs is word-representable. Further, we obtain a characteristic property by which the recomposition of comparability graphs is a comparability graph. Consequently, we also establish the permutation-representation number ( prn ) of the resulting comparability graph. We also introduce a subclass of comparability graphs, called prn -irreducible graphs. We provide a criterion such that the split recomposition of two prn -irreducible graphs is a comparability graph and determine the prn of the resultant graph.

Introduction

A simple graph is called a word-representable graph if there is a word over its vertices such that any two vertices are adjacent in the graph if and only if they alternate in the word. The class of word-representable graphs is an interesting class not only from graph theoretical point of view but also from algebraic and computational view points. Sergey Kitaev and Steven Seif first studied this class of graphs in the context of Perkin semigroups
[
26
]

[

The class of graphs which admit transitive orientations, known as comparability graphs, is an important subclass of word-representable graphs. This class has a special feature that they can be represented by a concatenation of permutations on their vertices, called permutationally representable graphs. In fact, the class of comparability graphs is precisely the class of permutationally representable graphs
[
26

3
Word-Representable Graphs

As stated in the following remark, while word-representability is preserved by split decomposition, in this section, we show that split recomposition also preserves the word-representability of graphs. Consequently, we observe that the class of parity graphs is word-representable. We also relate the representation number of the graph obtained by the recomposition with its components.

Remark 2

If a graph

If

We now summarize the characterization of word-representability of a graph with respect to its split decomposition in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2

Let
H_{i}

(1\leq i\leq s)
be the components of a split decomposition of a graph

. Then
G
is word-representable if and only if
H_{i}
is word-representable for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Further,
\mathcal{R}(G)=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq s}\mathcal{R}(H_{i})
.

In view of the fact that the class of perfect graphs is closed under split recomposition
[
2
]
, we have the following corollary of Theorem
3.2
. Although a characterization for word-representable perfect graphs is an open problem, this corollary may be useful in this regard.

Corollary 2

A perfect graph is not word-representable if and only if at least one of its prime components is a non-word-representable perfect graph.

Thus, the class of prime perfect graphs that are not word-representable completely determines the class of perfect graphs that are not word-representable.

A graph is called a parity graph if the lengths of any two induced paths between a pair of vertices are of the same parity. The class of parity graphs is a subclass of perfect graphs

. Recall from

that a graph

. Thus, we establish a class of word-representable graphs within perfect graphs which includes bipartite graphs as well as distance-hereditary graphs.

Corollary 3

The class of parity graphs is word-representable.

G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime}
permutationally, where

\displaystyle v_{i}

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}_{i}p_{i},\quad(1\leq i\leq k-1);

\displaystyle v_{j}

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}_{j}p_{k-1},\quad(k\leq j\leq k^{\prime}-1);

\displaystyle v_{k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=p_{{k-1}_{V\setminus N_{G}(m)}}p^{\prime}_{{k^{\prime}-1}_{N_{G^{\prime}}(m^{\prime})}}p_{{k-1}_{N_{G}(m)}}p^{\prime}_{{k^{\prime}-1}_{V^{\prime}\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}(m^{\prime})}}.

In any case, since
v
is a concatenation of
\max\{k,k^{\prime}\}
number of permutations, we have
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})\leq\max\{k,k^{\prime}\}
. Also, since
G
and
G^{\prime}
are isomorphic to certain induced subgraphs of

(cf. Remark
1
), we have
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})\geq\max\{k,k^{\prime}\}
. Hence,

\mathcal{R}^{p}(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})=\max\{k,k^{\prime}\}
. ∎

Theorem 5.2

For
k,k^{\prime}\geq 3
, if
G
and
G^{\prime}
be
k
-prn-irreducible and
k^{\prime}
-prn-irreducible comparability graphs, respectively, then
G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime}
is not prn-irreducible.

Proof

Suppose
G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime}
is a comparability graph. By Proposition
3
,
m
and
m^{\prime}
are not all-adjacent vertices. Also, by Theorem
5.1
, we have
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})=\max\{k,k^{\prime}\}=k
, say, without loss of generality.

Since
m^{\prime}
is not an all-adjacent vertex of
G^{\prime}
, there exists
c^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
such that
c^{\prime}\notin N_{G^{\prime}}(m^{\prime})
. Consider the induced subgraph
L=(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})[V\cup(V^{\prime}\setminus\{c^{\prime}\})]
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{R}^{p}(L)\leq k
. We show that
\mathcal{R}^{p}(L)=k
so that
G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime}
is not
prn
-irreducible.

For
b^{\prime}\in N_{G^{\prime}}(m^{\prime})
, note that the induced subgraph
(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})[V\cup\{b^{\prime}\}]
is isomorphic to
G
and it is also an induced subgraph of
L
. Thus,
\mathcal{R}^{p}(L)\geq k
and hence,
\mathcal{R}^{p}(L)=k
. ∎

6
Conclusion

This work paves a way, in general, to study the word-representability of graphs using split decomposition and recomposition. As the problem of deciding whether a given graph is word-representable is NP-complete, using split decomposition and recomposition, the problem can be reduced to its prime components. For example, the parity graphs are established as word-representable, because their prime components are word-representable (see Corollary
3
). In view of Corollary
2
, the open problem of characterizing word-representable perfect graphs can be approached through the following problem: What are the prime perfect graphs that are not word-representable? Similar to bipartite graphs, it is also interesting to find the subclasses of comparability graphs which are closed under recomposition. If one of the marked vertices is an all-adjacent vertex in a general case, or in the case
G
and
G^{\prime}
are
prn
-irreducible graphs, we proved that
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})=\kappa
, where
\kappa=\max\{\mathcal{R}^{p}(G),\mathcal{R}^{p}(G^{\prime})\}
. However, there are two categories of
prn
for the recomposed comparability graph, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G\!\;_{m}{\ostar}_{m^{\prime}}\!\;G^{\prime})=\kappa
or
1+\kappa
. Accordingly, one may target to classify the graphs with respect to the
prn
of their recomposition amongst the two categories.

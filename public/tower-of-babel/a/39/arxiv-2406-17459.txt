Orbits of a symmetric subgroup in the Bruhat-Tits building
Paul Broussous

Abstract
Let G G be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over a non-archimedean locally compact field F F of odd residue characteristic. Let θ \theta be an F F -rational involution of G G and H H be the reductive F F -group G θ G^{\theta} . We describe the orbits of the symmetric subgroup H F H_{F} in the set of chambers of the reduced building of G G over F F .



1
Introduction

Let
F
be a non-archimean locally compact field of residue caracteristic not
2
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group defined over
F
equipped with an
F
-rational involution
\theta
. We consider the reductive symmetric space
H_{F}\backslash G_{F}
, where
G_{F}
is the group of
F
-rational points in
G
and
H_{F}
is the subgroup of
\theta
-fixed elements.

Let
K
be a
good
maximal compact subgroup of
G_{F}
(the fixator of a
special vertex
in the Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{F}
). Some versions of the Cartan decomposition (or polar decomposition) have been obtained for the symmetric space
H_{F}\backslash G_{F}
(
[
7
]
,
[
2
]
), where by a Cartan decomposition one means a description of the
(H_{F},K)
-double cosets. Of course having such a decomposition is motivated by questions regarding harmonic analysis on the coset space
H_{\backslash}G_{F}
.

This paper is motivated by the study of the Iwahori-spherical representations of
G_{F}
contributing to harmonic analysis on
H_{F}\backslash G_{F}
(the so called
distinguished
representations). Recall that these representations
have been constructed by A. Borel (
[
1
]
) using the geometry of the Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{F}
. If
I
denotes the connected Iwahori subgroup of
G_{F}
, and
(\pi,V)
is an irreducible
Iwahori-spherical representation of
G_{F}
, so that the fixed vector space
V^{I}
is non-trivial, Borel gave a model of
\pi
as a space of functions defined on the set of chambers in Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{F}
with values in
V^{I}
.

The case of the Steinberg representation was studied by the author and F. Courtès (
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
) and other Iwahori-Spherical representations where discussed in
[
3
]
.

So the aim of this paper is to describe the double coset set
H_{F}\backslash G_{F}/I
. In fact we prefer taking a more geometric point of view by
instead considering the quotient
H_{F}\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}
, where
\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}
denotes the
G_{F}
-set of chambers in the affine building of
G_{F}
(note that we have a natural identification

H_{F}\backslash G_{F}/I\simeq H_{F}\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}
, when
G
is semisimple and simply connected).
This quotient turns out to be much easier to understand than
H_{F}\backslash G_{F}/K
.
Its description is quite similar to that of
H_{F}\backslash G_{F}/P_{\rm min}
where
P_{\rm min}
is a minimal parabolic subgroup of
G_{F}
(e.g. see
[
10
]
Proposition 6.10), and involves the set
H_{F}\backslash\mathscr{T}
of
H_{F}
-conjugacy classes of maximal
\theta
-stable
F
-tori in
G
that split over
F
.

We prove the following result (see theorem
3.6
below) :

Theorem 1.1

We have the disjoint union
decomposition :

H_{F}\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}=\coprod_{T\in H_{F}\backslash{\mathscr{T}}}(N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F})\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}\ .

where
\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}
denotes the set of chambers in the apartment of a
torus
T
) and
N(T)_{F}
the group of
F
-points of the normalizer of a torus
T
.

We make this decomposition entirely explicit when
G
is semisimple, simply connected and split over
F
and when

\theta
is a Galois involution attached to a quadratic unramified extension (Proposition
4.1
).
For this we use a description of
H_{F}\backslash{\mathscr{T}}
due to Paul Gérardin (
[
8
]
).

The first ingredient of our proof is due to Delorme and Sécherre (
[
7
]
Proposition 2.1) and was a key argument in loc. cit.: any chamber
C
in
\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}
, is contained in
a
\theta
-stable apartment
\mathscr{A}

of the affine building of
G
. Our second ingredient
(Theorem
3.2
)
makes Delorme and Sécherre’s result more precise: the apartment
\mathscr{A}

is unique up to the action of an element of
P_{C,0+}\cap H_{F}
, where
P_{C,0+}
is the pro-unipotent radical of the Iwahori subgroup
P_{C}
fixing
C
. Note that this an analogue of the result stating that any minimal parabolic subgroup
P
of
G_{F}
contains a
\theta
-stable maximal split torus, which is unique up to the action of
{\rm Rad}(P)\cap H_{F}
, where
{\rm Rad}(P)
is the unipotent radical of
P
(
[
10
]
Lemma 2.4).

2
Notation for algebraic groups and buildings

We let
F
be a non-archimedean, non discrete, locally compact field of
odd
residue characteristic.
If
M
is an algebraic group defined over
F
, we denote by
M_{F}
its group of
F
-rational points.

We fix once for all:

– a reductive algebraic group
G
that we assume connected and defined over
F
,

– an
F
-rational involution of
G
, that is a non trivial
F
-rational group automorphism
\theta
of
G
such that
\theta^{2}={\rm id}
.

We set
H=G^{\theta}
. Thanks to the hypothesis on the residue characteristic of
F
, the group
H
is a (non-necessarily connected) reductive group defined over
F
.

We denote by
X={\rm BT}(G,F)
the non-enlarged Bruhat-Tits building of
G
over
F
. Recall that this is a metric space endowed with an action of
G_{F}
by isometries. Moreover it has the structure of a polysimplicial complex on which
G_{F}
acts by simplicial automorphism. Its maximal (open) polysimplices are called chambers and we denote by
\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}
the corresponding
G_{F}
-set.

The automorphism
\theta
induced a polysimplicial automorphism of
X
that we shall denote by the same symbol
\theta
. This bijection permutes the chambers of
X
and satisfies
\theta(g.x)=\theta(g).\theta(x)
, for all
g\in G
,
x\in X
.

Attached to any maximal
F
-split torus
T
of
G
defined over
F
, we have the following objects:

– Its (relative) root system
\Phi(G,T)
, whose affine Weyl group will be denoted by
W_{T}^{\rm Aff}
.

– Its centralizer
Z=Z(T)
in
G
. Recall that it is a connected reductive group defined over
F
such that
Z/T
is anisotropic. In particular
Z_{F}
contains a unique connected parahoric subgroup
that we denote by
Z_{F}^{0}
.

– Its normalizer
N=N(T)

– Its apartment
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}(T)\subset X
. It is acted upon by
N_{F}
and
W^{\rm Aff}_{T}
. The parahoric subgroup
Z_{F}^{0}
fixes
\mathscr{A}
pointwise and the global stabilizer of
\mathscr{A}
in
G_{F}
is
N_{F}
.

–
\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}
the
N_{F}
-set of chambers contained in
\mathscr{A}
. Recall that
N_{F}
acts transitively on
\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}
.

Let
\Omega
a subset of
X
contained in some apartement
\mathscr{A}(T)
. We shall use the compact open subgroups
P_{\Omega}
and
U_{\Omega}
defined in
[
5
]
, §7, p. 157 (these subgroups are respectively denoted by
{\mathscr{P}}_{\Omega}
and
(G_{F})^{\sharp}_{\Omega}
in
[
11
]
, Definition 7.4.1, p. 255). The group
P_{\Omega}
is the connected
parahoric subgroup
attached to
\Omega
. It fixes
\Omega
pointwise, but the pointwise fixator of
\Omega
is
G_{F}
is in general bigger than
P_{\Omega}
. Recall that we have the decomposition
P_{\Omega}=Z_{F}^{0}\,U_{\Omega}
, and that the subgroup
U_{\Omega}
acts transitively on the set of apartments of
X
containing
\Omega
(
[
11
]
, Proposition 7.6.4, p. 265).

Let
x
be a point of
X
. We denote by
(P_{x,r})_{r\in\mathscr{R}_{\geqslant}}
the
Moy-Prasad filtration
of the parahoric subgroup
P_{x}=P_{\{x\}}
(
[
11
]
, Definition 13.2.1, p. 468, where
P_{x,r}
is denoted by
{\mathscr{P}}_{x,r}
). Following Moy and Prasad, we set
P_{x,0+}:=\bigcup_{r>0}P_{x,r}
. According to
[
11
]
, Proposition 13.5.2,
P_{x,0+}
is a pro-
p
-subgroup of
G_{F}
(a projective limit of
p
-subgroups). It is generally called the
pro-unipotent radical
of
P_{x}
.

If
\mathscr{F}
is a facet of
X
,
P_{x,0}
and
P_{x,0+}
do not depend on
x\in\mathscr{F}
; we denote these subgroups by
P_{{\mathscr{F}},0}
and
P_{{\mathscr{F}},0+}
respectively.

When
x
lies in a chamber, that is when
P_{x}
is an
Iwahori subgroup
of
G_{F}
,
P_{x,0+}
is a maximal pro-
p
-subgroup of
G_{F}^{0}
(
[
11
]
Proposition 13.5.2, p. 476, cf.
[
11
]
Definition 2.6.23 for the definition of the reductive subgroup
G^{0}\subset G
). In particular, the pro-unipotent radical of
Z_{F}^{0}
, that we shall denote by
Z_{F}^{0+}
, is a maximal pro-
p
-subgroup of
Z_{F}^{0}
.

3
Some conjugacy resuls

We denote by
\mathscr{T}
the
H_{F}
-set of maximal split
F
-tori of
G
that are stable by
\theta
.
If
T\in\mathscr{T}
, then
Z(T)
,
N(T)
,
\mathscr{A}(T)
and
\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}
are stable under the action of
\theta
.

The following key result is due to Delorme and Sécherre (but was proved independently by Courtès (
[
6
]
Proposition 4.1) for the case of a Galois involution, when
G
is split over
F
).

Theorem 3.1

[
7
]
Let
C
be a chamber of
X
. Then there exists a
\theta
-stable maximal
F
-split torus
T\in\mathscr{T}
such that
C
is contained in
\mathscr{A}(T)
.

Note that such a torus
T
is not unique. However the following result implies that
T
is uniquely determined modulo the conjugacy action of
H_{F}
on
\mathscr{T}
.

Theorem 3.2

Let
C
be a chamber in
X
and
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
be two
\theta
-stable maximal
F
-split tori of
G
containing
C
. Then there exists an element
g\in P_{C,0+}\cap H_{F}

g.\mathscr{A}(T_{1})=\mathscr{A}(T_{2})
.

Remark
. This theorem is due to Courtès when
G
is split and
\theta
is a Galois involution (
[
6
]
Proposition 4.8). Our proof is inspired from his proof.

Proof
. Let
\Omega
be the
enclos
(cf.
[
5
]
, (7.1.2), p. 157) of
C\cup\theta(C)
. This is a
\theta
-stable convex subset of
\mathscr{A}(T_{1})\cap\mathscr{A}(T_{2})
. Hence
U_{\Omega}
is
\theta
-stable. Since
U_{\Omega}
acts transitively on the set of apartments containing
\Omega
, there exists
g\in U_{\Omega}
such that
g.\mathscr{A}(T_{1})=\mathscr{A}(T_{2})
. Applying
\theta
to this equality, we obtain
\theta(g).\mathscr{A}(T_{1})=\mathscr{A}(T_{2})
, whence
g^{-1}\theta(g).\mathscr{A}(T_{1})=\mathscr{A}(T_{1})
and
g^{-1}\theta(g)\in U_{\Omega}\cap N(T_{1})_{F}
. Write
\Theta
for the two element group
\{1,\theta\}
. Then
\sigma\mapsto g^{-1}\sigma(g)
is a
1
-cocycle of
\Theta
in
U_{\Omega}\cap N(T_{1})_{F}
.

Lemma 3.3

Let
T
be a maximal
F
-split torus of
G
and
\Omega
be a subset of
\mathscr{A}(T)
containing a chamber
C
. Then
U_{\Omega}\cap N(T)_{F}\subset Z_{F}^{0}
.

Proof of the lemma
. Let
x
be any point of
C
. Since
C\subset\Omega
, we have
U_{\Omega}\subset U_{C}=U_{x}
. By
[
11
]
Definition 7.4.1(3) and Corollary 7.4.9, we have
U_{\Omega}\cap N(T)_{F}\subset U_{x}\cap N(T)_{F}=U_{x}\cap Z(T)_{F}
. Moreover, by
[
11
]
Lemma 7.4.3, we have that
U_{x}\cap Z(T)_{F}\subset Z(T)_{F}^{0}
, and we are done.

Since
\Omega
contains a chamber, the subgroup
U_{\Omega}
is a pro-
p
-subgroup. It follows from the lemma that
U_{\Omega}\cap N(T_{1})
is a pro-
p
-subgroup of
Z_{F}^{0}
, whence is contained in the pro-unipotent radical
Z_{F}^{0+}
of
Z_{F}^{0}
.

We need another result, due to Hakim and Murnaghan.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
9
]
Proposition 2.12.) Let
M
be a connected reductive
F
-group acted upon by a
F
-rational involution
\theta
.
Let
K
be a
\theta
-stable open subgroup of
M_{F}
contained in the pro-unipotent radical
M_{x,0+}
, form some point
x
of the Bruhat-Tits building of
M
. Then the non-abelian cohomology set
H^{1}(\Theta,K)
is trivial, where
\Theta=\{1,\theta\}
.

By applying the lemma to
M=Z
and
K=U_{\Omega}\cap N(T_{1})_{F}
, we have that the cocycle
\sigma\mapsto g^{-1}\sigma(g)
is split. So there exists
z\in Z_{F}^{0+}
such that
g^{-1}\theta(g)=z^{-1}\theta(z)
, that is
gz^{-1}\in H_{F}
. So replacing
g
by
gz^{-1}\in U_{\Omega}Z_{F}^{0+}
, we can arrange
g\in H_{F}
,
g.C=C
and
g.\mathscr{A}(T_{1})=\mathscr{A}(T_{2})
. Since
\Omega\subset C
, we hace
U_{\Omega}\subset U_{C}
, so we may choose
g\in U_{C}Z^{0+}_{F}\cap H
. Finally since
U_{\Omega}Z_{F}^{0+}
is a pro-
p
-subgroup of
P_{C}
, it is contained in
P_{C,0+}
; the theorem follows.

Corollary 3.5

Let
T
be a
\theta
-stable maximal
F
-split torus of
G
and
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
chambers in
\mathscr{A}(T)
. Then if
C_{2}\in H_{F}.C_{1}
, there exists
h\in N(T)_{F}\cap H
such that
C_{2}=h.C_{1}
.

Proof
. Since there exists
h\in H_{F}
such that
C_{2}=h.C_{1}
, we have
C_{1}\in\mathscr{A}(T)\cap h^{-1}.\mathscr{A}(T)
. By the previous theorem, there exists
k\in H_{F}\cap P_{C}
such that
h^{-1}.\mathscr{A}(T)=k.\mathscr{A}(T)
. It follows that
hk\in N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F}
and we are done by replacing
h
by
hk
.

For
T\in{\mathscr{T}}
, write
H_{F}\backslash{\mathscr{T}}
for a set of representatives for the action of
H_{F}
on
\mathscr{T}
. For each
T\in{\mathscr{T}}/H_{F}
, let
(N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F})\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}

be a set of representatives for the action of
N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F}
on
\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}
. We have proved the following result.

Theorem 3.6

A set of representatives for the action of
H_{F}
on
\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}
is given by the disjoint union

H_{F}\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}=\coprod_{T\in H_{F}\backslash{\mathscr{T}}}(N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F})\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}\ .

4
The case of a Galois symmetric space

Let us assume that

G
is semisimple and simply connected. Then for each
T\in\mathscr{T}
,
N(T)_{F}
acts on
\mathscr{A}(T)
through the quotient
N(T)_{F}/Z(F)_{F}^{0}
. This quotient is isomorphic to the affine Weyl group
W_{T}^{\rm Aff}
of the affine root system
\Phi^{\rm Aff}(G,T)
of
G
relative to
T
. The group
N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F}
acts on
\mathscr{A}(T)

through the quotient
(N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F})/(Z_{F}^{0}\cap H_{F})
.
Since
\mathscr{A}(T)
is a principal homogeneous space under the action of
W_{T}^{\rm Aff}
, the decomposition of Theorem
3.6
gives a bijection :

H_{F}\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}\simeq\coprod_{T\in H_{F}\backslash{\mathscr{T}}}\left[(N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F})/(Z_{F}^{0}\cap H_{F})\right]\backslash W_{T}^{\rm Aff}\ .

Now assume that the algebraic group
H
is a semisimple, simply connected, defined and split over
F
. Let
E/F
be an unramified quadratic field extension and set
G={\rm Res}_{E/F}H
(restriction of scalars). Finally let
\theta
be the action on
G
of the non trivial element of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
.

Then if
T\in{\mathscr{T}}
, we have
Z=T
and it is classical, since
Z_{F}^{0}
is a connected parahoric subgroup of
Z_{F}
, that
H^{1}({\rm Gal}(E/F),Z_{F}^{0})
is trivial (cf.
[
11
]
Lemma 8.1.4). Hence the natural map
(N(T)_{F}\cap H_{F})/(Z_{F}^{0}\cap H_{F})\longrightarrow\left(W_{T}^{\rm Aff}\right)^{{\rm Gal}(E/F)}
is a group isomorphism.
Let
\theta_{T}
be the involutive affine transformation of
\mathscr{A}(T)
induced by
\theta
. Then
\left(W_{T}^{\rm Aff}\right)^{{\rm Gal}(E/F)}
coincide with the centralizer
W_{T}^{{\rm Aff},\theta_{T}}
of
\theta_{T}
in
W_{T}^{\rm Aff}
.

We have proved the following.

Proposition 4.1

With the assumptions and notation as above, we have the decomposition :

H_{F}\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}=\coprod_{T\in H_{F}\backslash{\mathscr{T}}}W_{T}^{{\rm Aff},\theta_{T}}\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{T}\ .

In order to make this last decomposition more explicit, we now use the classification of the elements of
\mathscr{T}
due to Paul Gérardin (
[
8
]
§III, Tores maximaux non ramifiés).

Fix an element
T_{o}\in{\mathscr{T}}
that is split over
F
. Such tori exist and are conjugate under
H_{F}
. We abreviate
N_{0,F}=N(T_{0})_{F}
. According to
loc. cit
Théorème 2, page 109,
H_{F}\backslash{\mathscr{T}}
is in natural bijection with the set of conjugacy classes of elements of order dividing
2
in
W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}
. Let us explain how to associate to an element
w\in W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}
of order dividing
2
a torus
T_{w}\in{\mathscr{T}}
.

Let
\tilde{w}\in N_{0,F}
be an element lifting
w
. Since
H
is semisimple and simply connected, the Galois cohomology set
H^{1}({\rm Gal}(E/F),H(E))
is trivial and there exists
g\in H(E)=G_{F}
such that
g^{-1}\theta(g)={\tilde{w}}
. Then we set
T_{w}=gT_{o}g^{-1}
. The
H_{F}
-conjugacy class of
T_{w}
depends only on that of
w
in
W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}
. Of course if
w=1
, we have
T_{w}=T_{o}
.

The group isomorphism
N_{o,F}\longrightarrow N(T_{w})_{F}
,
n\mapsto gng^{-1}
, induces a group isomorphism
W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}\longrightarrow W_{T_{w}}^{\rm Aff}
. This isomorphism is compatible with the affine bijection:
\mathscr{A}_{T_{o}}\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{T_{w}}
,
x\mapsto g.x
. Through these identifications, the action of
\theta_{T_{w}}
on
\mathscr{A}_{T_{w}}
corresponds to the action of
w
on
\mathscr{A}_{T_{0}}
. Hence denoting by
W_{T_{o}}^{{\rm Aff},w}
the centralizer of
w
in
W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}
, we obtain an identification
W_{T_{o}}^{{\rm Aff},w}\simeq W_{T_{w}}^{{\rm Aff},\theta_{T_{w}}}
. As a consequence the decomposition of Proposition
4.1
may be rewritten:

H\backslash\mathscr{C}\!h_{G}\simeq\coprod_{w}W_{T_{o}}^{{\rm Aff},w}\backslash\mathscr{A}_{T_{o}}\simeq\coprod_{w}W_{T_{o}}^{{\rm Aff},w}\backslash W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}\ ,

where
w
runs over the
W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}
-conjugacy classes of elements of
W_{T_{o}}^{\rm Aff}
of order dividing
2
.

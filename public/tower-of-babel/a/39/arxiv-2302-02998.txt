Moment generating function bound from detailed fluctuation theorem
Domingos S. P. Salazar

Abstract
A famous consequence of the detailed fluctuation theorem (FT), p ⁡ ( Σ ) / p ⁡ ( − Σ ) = exp ⁡ ( Σ ) p(\Sigma)/p(-\Sigma)=\exp{(\Sigma)} , is the integral FT ⟨ exp ⁡ ( − Σ ) ⟩ = 1 \langle\exp(-\Sigma)\rangle=1 for a random variable Σ \Sigma and a distribution p ⁡ ( Σ ) p(\Sigma) . When Σ \Sigma represents the entropy production in thermodynamics, the main outcome of the integral FT is the second law, ⟨ Σ ⟩ ≥ 0 \langle\Sigma\rangle\geq 0 . However, a full description of the fluctuations of Σ \Sigma might require knowledge of the moment generating function (MGF), G ⁡ ( α ) := ⟨ exp ⁡ ( α ​ Σ ) ⟩ G(\alpha):=\langle\exp(\alpha\Sigma)\rangle . In the context of the detailed FT, we show the MGF is lower bounded in the form G ⁡ ( α ) ≥ B ⁡ ( α , ⟨ Σ ⟩ ) G(\alpha)\geq B(\alpha,\langle\Sigma\rangle) for a given mean ⟨ Σ ⟩ \langle\Sigma\rangle . As applications, we verify that the bound is satisfied for the entropy produced in the heat exchange problem between two reservoirs mediated by a weakly coupled bosonic mode and a qubit swap engine.



Introduction -

The second law of thermodynamics states that the entropy production is nonnegative. Although the random nature of
\Sigma
is practically negligible in large systems, it dominates the physics of the entropy production at small scale due to thermal and quantum fluctuations
Landi and Paternostro 2021
;
Seifert 2012
;
Campisi et al. 2011
;
Esposito et al. 2009
;
Jarzynski 2008
;
Jarzynski 1997
;
Ciliberto et al. 2013
;
Crooks 1998
;
Gallavotti and Cohen 1995
;
Evans et al. 1993
;
Hänggi and Talkner 2015
;
Batalhão et al. 2014
;
Pal et al. 2019
. In this context, the second law is stated as an average,
\langle\Sigma\rangle\geq 0
.

The detailed fluctuation theorem (DFT) is a stronger statement, which defines a random variable (typically the entropy production) in terms of the ratio of probabilities,

\Sigma(\Gamma):=\ln P(\Gamma)/P(\Gamma^{\dagger}),

(1)

where
\Gamma
is some process and
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is a conjugate (involution), such that
\Gamma^{\dagger\dagger}=\Gamma
. As a consequence of (
1
), for instance, we have the integral FT,
\langle\exp(-\Gamma)\rangle=1
and the second law,
\langle\Sigma\rangle\geq 0
, from Jensen’s inequality. The definition (
1
) is used to define the trajectory level entropy production
Seifert 2005
in stochastic thermodynamics, and a form of (
1
) is also called the strong detailed fluctuation theorem
Seifert 2012
, Evan-Searles fluctuation theorem
Evans and Searles 2002
, Gallavotti-Cohen relation
Gallavotti and Cohen 1995
and it appears in the heat exchange problem
Jarzynski and Wójcik 2004
. The structure of (
1
) might also be used to define generalizations of entropy production that contains information terms
Potts and Samuelsson 2019
and in quantum systems beyond the two point measurement scheme
Micadei et al. 2020
. For that reason, we treat (
1
) as a definition of a general random variable.

Beyond the second law, the DFT (
1
) has known consequences for the statistics of
\Sigma
. For instance, a form of Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation (TUR) can also be derived from it
Merhav and Kafri 2010
;
Timpanaro et al. 2019
;
Hasegawa and Van
Vu 2019
;
Salazar 2022a
, which can be understood as a lower bound for the variance in terms of the mean of
\Sigma
. Another example is a lower bound for apparent violations of the second law
Salazar 2021
, defined as the cases for which
\Sigma<0
.

The randomness of
\Sigma
is encoded in the moment generating function (MGF),

G(\alpha):=\langle e^{\alpha\Sigma}\rangle=\sum_{\Gamma}e^{\alpha\Sigma(\Gamma)}P(\Gamma),

(2)

where the sum above might be replaced by an integral and
\alpha
is a real number. Derivatives of (
2
) at
\alpha=0
have information of statistical moments. For instance, the first derivative is related to the second law,
G^{\prime}(0)=\langle\Sigma\rangle\geq 0
. In general, we have
G^{(n)}(0)=\langle\Sigma^{n}\rangle
for higher order moments. The MGF (
2
) is nonnegative,
G(\alpha)\geq 0
, but a stronger bound comes from Jensen’s inequality, where
G(\alpha)\geq\exp(\alpha\overline{\Sigma})\geq 0
.

In this letter, we propose the following question: what is the impact of the DFT (
1
) in the MGF (
2
)? The first immediate known consequence of (
1
) is the known Evan-Searles or Gallavotti-Cohen symmetry property for (
2
),

G(\alpha)=G(-1-\alpha),

(3)

as a direct application of the definition (
1
). Moreover, in terms of the MGF (
2
), the IFT for
\Sigma(\Gamma)
is obtained for
\alpha=-1
using (
3
),

\langle e^{-\Sigma}\rangle=G(-1)=G(0)=1,

(4)

and the second law follows from it. Our main result is to show that the DFT (
1
) implies a tight lower bound for the MGF (
2
) for a given mean
\overline{\Sigma}
,

G(\alpha)\geq\frac{\cosh[(\alpha+1/2)g(\overline{\Sigma})]}{\cosh[(1/2)g(\overline{\Sigma})]},

(5)

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
where
g(x)
is the inverse function of
h(x):=x\tanh(x/2)
, defined for
x\geq 0
and
\overline{\Sigma}:=\langle\Sigma\rangle=\sum_{\Gamma}\Sigma(\Gamma)P(\Gamma)
. We show that the distribution that saturates (
5
) is the distribution that saturates the TUR
Timpanaro et al. 2019
. This bound explores the DFT (
1
) directly to improve on the bound
\exp(\alpha\overline{\Sigma})
.

The letter is organized as follows. First, we present a general formalism for (
1
) in terms of involutions to derive (
5
). Then, we discuss how the result can be framed using information theory in terms of Rényi relative entropy. As applications, we show that the bound is satisfied in three different cases: a Gaussian distribution, and the heat exchanged between two reservoirs mediated by a bosonic mode in weak coupling and a qubit swap engine.

Formalism -

Let
\Gamma\in S
be an element of a set
S
and
m:S\rightarrow S
is any involution
m(m(\Sigma))=\Sigma
, where we define the notation

\Gamma^{\dagger}:=m(\Sigma)
. Let
P:\Gamma\rightarrow[0,1]
be a probability function such that
P(\Gamma)=0\rightarrow P(\Gamma^{\dagger})=0
(absolute continuity). For instance, one could choose
S=\mathbb{R}
and
m(x)=-x
. More generally, in
stochastic thermodynamics,
\Gamma=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a sequence and
\Gamma^{\dagger}=(x_{n},...,x_{1})
is the inverse.

Define the function
\Sigma:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as
\Sigma(\Gamma):=\ln(P(\Gamma)/P(\Gamma^{\dagger}))
for
P(\Gamma)>0
as in (
1
) and
\Sigma(\Gamma):=0
, for
P(\Gamma)=P(\Gamma^{\dagger})=0
. Usually, this expression for
\Sigma(\Gamma)
appears in nonequilibium thermodynamics as the stochastic entropy production or some generalization. The moment generating function of
\Sigma(\Gamma)
is given by (
2
)

G(\alpha)=\sum_{\Gamma}e^{\alpha\Sigma(\Gamma)}P(\Gamma)=\sum_{\Gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\alpha\sigma}\delta(\Sigma(\Gamma)-\sigma)P(\Gamma)d\sigma,

(6)

where we used
f(x)=\int f(y)\delta(y-x)dy
and
\delta(x)
is the Dirac’s delta function. Now define the pdf

p(\sigma):=\sum_{\Gamma}\delta(\Sigma(\Gamma)-\sigma)P(\Gamma),

(7)

such that the mgf (
8
) is given as

G(\alpha)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\alpha\sigma}p(\sigma)d\sigma=\langle e^{\alpha\sigma}\rangle,

(8)

where
\langle\rangle
are now understood as averages in
p(\sigma)
. Similarly, we have
\langle\sigma\rangle=\langle\Sigma\rangle
. From the definition of
\Sigma(\Gamma)
, we have the property for
p(\sigma)

p(\sigma)=\sum_{\Gamma}\delta(\Sigma(\Gamma)-\sigma)e^{\Sigma(\Gamma)}P(\Gamma^{\dagger})=e^{\sigma}\sum_{\Gamma}\delta(\Sigma(\Gamma)-\sigma)P(\Gamma^{\dagger}),

(9)

and using
\Sigma(\Gamma)=-\Sigma(\Gamma^{\dagger})
, definition (
7
) and the sum over the involution,
\sum_{\Gamma}f(\Gamma^{\dagger})=\sum_{\Gamma}f(\Gamma)
, it results in

p(\sigma)=e^{\sigma}p(-\sigma).

(10)

From (
10
), we have the useful property
Salazar 2022b
for odd functions
u(-\sigma)=-u(\sigma
),

\langle u(\sigma)\rangle=\langle u(\sigma)\tanh(\sigma/2)\rangle.

(11)

Now we decompose the mgf (
8
) in a sum of odd and even functions,
G(\alpha)=\langle\sinh(\alpha\sigma)\rangle+\langle\cosh(\alpha\sigma)\rangle
and we use property (
11
) for
u(\sigma)=\sinh(\alpha\sigma)
, which results in

\displaystyle G(\alpha)=\langle\sinh(\alpha\sigma)\tanh(\sigma/2)+\cosh(\alpha\sigma)\rangle.

(12)

Now we use the identity
\cosh(x+y)=\cosh(x)\cosh(y)+\sinh(x)\sinh(y)
to obtain the compact form from (
12
),

G(\alpha)=\big\langle\frac{\cosh[(\alpha+1/2)\sigma]}{\cosh(\sigma/2)}\big\rangle.

(13)

Finally, we define the functions
h(\sigma)=\sigma\tanh(\sigma/2)
and the inverse
g(h(\sigma))=|\sigma|
. Inserting
|\sigma|=g(h(\sigma))
in (
13
) leads to

G(\alpha)=\Big\langle\frac{\cosh[(\alpha+1/2)g(h(\sigma))]}{\cosh[(1/2)g(h(\sigma))]}\Big\rangle.

(14)

The final step is to use Jensen’s inequality in (
14
) in a strategy analogous to previous results
Salazar 2022b
as follows

\langle f[g(h(\sigma))]\rangle\geq\langle f[g(\langle h(\sigma)\rangle)]=f[g(\langle\sigma\rangle)],

(15)

which is true if
w^{\prime\prime}(h):=d^{2}w(h)/dh^{2}>0
, where
w(h):=f[g(h)]
and
f(x)=\cosh[(\alpha+1/2)x]/\cosh(x/2)
(see Appendix). Note that
\langle\sigma\rangle=\langle\sigma\tanh(\sigma/2)\rangle=\langle h(\sigma)\rangle
, from (
11
) because
u(\Sigma)=\sigma
is odd. Combining (
14
) and (
15
) and using
\langle\Sigma\rangle=\langle\sigma\rangle
, we obtain (
5
).

Discussion-

As in the case of previous results
Timpanaro et al. 2019
;
Salazar 2022a
, it can be checked directly that the bound is saturated by the minimal distribution,
p(\Sigma)=[\delta(\Sigma+a)\exp(-a/2)+\delta(\Sigma-a)\exp(a/2)]/(2\cosh(a/2))
, where
a=g(\overline{\Sigma})
.

An interesting aspect of (
1
) is that
\Gamma
could be replaced by the pair
\tilde{\Gamma}:=(d,\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma\in S
is the original set and
d\in\{F,B\}
denotes forward and backwards experiments, such that
m(\tilde{\Gamma})=m(d,\Gamma)=(d^{\dagger},\Gamma^{\dagger})
, where
F^{\dagger}=B
and
B^{\dagger}=F
,
P(\tilde{\Gamma}):=P_{d}(\Gamma)/2
. In this case, one has
\Sigma_{d}(\Gamma):=\Sigma(\tilde{\Gamma})=\ln P(d,\Gamma)/P(d^{\dagger},\Gamma^{\dagger})
, so that
\Sigma_{F}(\Gamma):=\ln P_{F}(\Gamma)/P_{B}(\Gamma)
, which is the usual definition of the detailed fluctuation theorem, as opposed to the strong DFT (
1
). Thus, in the general framework presented in this paper, we do not make distinction between the detailed FT and the strong DFT.

We also point out that our main result (
5
) could also be stated in terms of information theory as a bound for the Rényi relative entropy as follows. The definition of Rényi relative entropy for probabilities
P
and
Q
is

S_{\alpha}(P|Q):=\frac{-1}{1-\alpha}\ln\Big(\sum_{\Gamma}P(\Gamma)^{\alpha}Q(\Gamma)^{1-\alpha}\Big).

(16)

Now let (
S,P,m
) be defined as in the formalism, then
\overline{\Sigma}=D(P|P^{\prime})
is the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence,
D(P|Q):=\sum_{i}P_{i}\ln(P_{i}/Q_{i})
, where
P^{\prime}(\Gamma):=P(m(\Gamma))
. From (
2
) and (
16
), we have
G(\alpha)=\exp[\alpha S_{\alpha+1}(P|P^{\prime})]
. In terms of (
16
), the main result (
5
) reads

S_{\alpha}(P|P^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\Big(\frac{\cosh[(\alpha-1/2)g(D(P|P^{\prime}))]}{\cosh[(1/2)g(D(P|P^{\prime}))]}\Big),

(17)

for any set
S
, probability
P
and involution
m
. Actually, for
\alpha\rightarrow 1
, the bound saturates as both sides of (
17
) converge to
D(P|P^{\prime})
.
One might wonder how a result (
5
) that seemed dependent on the DFT is actually as general as (
17
). As it turns out, the involution property
m(m(\Gamma))=\Gamma
constrains the pair of probabilities
(P,P^{\prime})
in (
17
), creating the same effect of the DFT. For instance, if
m
is the identity, then
P=P^{\prime}
and, in thermodynamics, that would be equivalent to equilibrium (or detailed balance condition in the case of a Markov process). For a general involution
m
, probabilities
P
and
P^{\prime}
will differ (nonequilibrium), as represented by the KL divergence
D(P|P^{\prime})>0
and the relative entropy
S_{\alpha}(P|P^{\prime})>0
.

5

Chun and Noh 2019

G(\alpha)=\exp(\alpha(1+\alpha)\overline{\Sigma}),

(18)

as the DFT fixes the variance

3

18

Bosonic mode case-
For another comparison, we take a free bosonic mode with Hamiltonian
H=\hbar\omega(a^{\dagger}a+1/2)
weakly coupled to a thermal reservoir with a density matrix satisfying a Lindblad’s equation as developed in previous results
Santos et al. 2017
;
Salazar et al. 2019
;
Denzler and Lutz 2018
,

\partial_{t}\rho=\frac{-i}{\hbar}[H,\rho]+D_{i}(\rho),

(19)

for the dissipator given by

D_{i}(\rho)=\gamma(\overline{n}_{i}+1)[a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{a^{\dagger}a,\rho\}]+\gamma\overline{n}_{i}[a^{\dagger}\rho a-\frac{1}{2}\{aa^{\dagger},\rho\}],

(20)

where
\gamma
is a constant and
\overline{n}_{i}=[\exp(\hbar\omega/k_{B}T_{i})-1]^{-1}
is the bosonic thermal occupation number and
\beta_{i}=1/(k_{B}T_{i})
. We denote the solution of (
19
) as
\rho_{t}:=\Phi_{t}(\rho_{0})
. A two point measurement scheme is performed: first, the system is prepared in thermal equilibrium (temperature
T_{1}
) at time
t=0
, when the first energy measurement takes place yielding
E_{1}
. Then, the system is placed in thermal contact with a second reservoir (temperature
T_{2}
), when another energy measurement is performed (time
t>0
) yielding
E_{2}
. The dynamics of the system between the two measurements is given by (
19
) with temperature
T_{2}
. As explored in previous papers, repeating the experiment multiple times results in a distribution for
\Delta E=E_{2}-E_{1}
, where
\Sigma:=-(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})\Delta E

Campisi et al. 2015
;
Timpanaro et al. 2019
;
Sinitsyn 2011
is the entropy production. In this case, the distribution is given by
p(\Delta E=\hbar\omega m)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle n+m|\Phi_{t}\big(|n\rangle\langle n|\big)|n+m\rangle p_{n}
, where
p_{n}={e^{-\beta_{1}E_{n}}}/Z(\beta_{1})
and
Z(\beta_{1})=\text{tr}(e^{-\beta_{1}H})
, resulting in the closed form
Salazar et al. 2019
;
Denzler and Lutz 2018
for the entropy production

p(\Sigma)=\frac{1}{A(0,\lambda)}\exp(\frac{\Sigma}{2}-\lambda\frac{|\Sigma|}{2}),

(21)

with support
s=\{\pm\Delta\beta\hbar\omega m\}=\{\pm\varepsilon m\}
,
m=0,1,2,..
, and normalization constant
A(0,\lambda)
, where
A(\alpha,\lambda):=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}[\exp((\alpha+m/2)\epsilon)-\exp(-\alpha-m/2)\epsilon)]\exp(-\lambda\epsilon m/2))
, for
\lambda>|2\alpha+1|
. Upon inspection, note that (
21
) checks (
1
). The MGF (
2
) for (
21
) is given by

G(\alpha)=\frac{A(\alpha,\lambda)}{A(0,\lambda)},

(22)

for
\lambda>|2\alpha+1|
. In Fig.1, we plot the MGF (
22

\alpha

\varepsilon=1
and
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
such that
\langle\Sigma\rangle=1
, compared to the lower bound (
5
) for the same
\overline{\Sigma}=1
. This example shows a case where the domain of
\alpha
is limited,
\lambda^{*}>|2\alpha+1|
, but the bound is still satisfied.

Swap engine -
Consider a pair of qubits with energy gaps
\epsilon\in\{\epsilon_{A},\epsilon_{B}\}
. They are prepared in thermal equilibrium,
p(\pm)=\exp(\pm\beta\epsilon)/(\exp(-\beta\epsilon)+\exp(+\beta\epsilon))
, for
\beta\in\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}
, with reservoirs at temperatures
T_{1},T_{2}
. A TPM is performed before and after a swap operation
Campisi et al. 2015
,
|xy\rangle\rightarrow|yx\rangle
, for
x,y\in\{-,+\}
. The entropy production is given
Campisi et al. 2015
;
Timpanaro et al. 2019
by
\Sigma=\beta_{1}\Delta E_{A}+\beta_{2}\Delta E_{B}
,
where
\Delta E_{A}=E_{A}^{f}-E_{A}^{i}
,
\Delta E_{B}=E_{B}^{f}-E_{B}^{i}
are the variations of energy measurements before and after the swap. In this TPM, the outcomes are
\Sigma\in s=\{0,\pm 2a\}
for
2a=2(\beta_{2}\epsilon_{B}-\beta_{1}\epsilon_{A})
. The distribution of
\Sigma
is given by

p(\Sigma)=(1/Z_{0})[\delta(\Sigma)+\delta(\Sigma+2a)e^{-a}+\delta(\Sigma-2a)e^{a}],

(23)

for
Z_{0}=1+\exp(a)+\exp(-a)
, which satisfies the DFT (
1
). The MGF of (
23
) is given by

G(\alpha)=\frac{1+2\cosh(a(2\alpha+1))}{1+2\cosh(a)},

(24)

where
a
defines
\overline{\Sigma}
uniquely from
\overline{\Sigma}=2a(\exp(a)-\exp(-a))/(1+\exp(a)+\exp(-a))
. Comparison between (
24
) and (
5
) is also depicted in Fig.1 for
\overline{\Sigma}=1
.

Conclusions -

We explored the DFT (
1
) as a definition of a random variable
\Sigma
and proved the impact it has on the MGF (
2
). The result is that the MGF of
\Sigma
is lower bounded as a function of the mean
\overline{\Sigma}
and the parameter
\alpha
. This lower bound improves on the simple exponential bound
\exp(\alpha\overline{\Sigma})
because of the special definition of
\Sigma
in terms of the DFT. Although the structure (
1
) appears as a definition of entropy production in stochastic thermodynamics, it is also the case of general entropy related quantities (that might include boundary and information terms) in quantum thermodynamics even beyond two point measurement schemes
Micadei et al. 2020
. We also wrote the main result in terms of information theory, as a lower bound for the Rényi relative entropy between distributions
P
and
P^{\prime}
, where the pair is constrained by an involution,
P^{\prime}(\Gamma)=P(m(\Gamma))
.

Abstract
. Given a perfect field k k with algebraic closure k ¯ \overline{k} and a variety X X over k ¯ \overline{k} , the field of moduli of X X is the subfield of k ¯ \overline{k} of elements fixed by field automorphisms γ ∈ Gal ⁡ ( k ¯ / k ) \gamma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{k}/k) such that the twist X γ X_{\gamma} is isomorphic to X X . The field of moduli is contained in all subextensions k ⊂ k ′ ⊂ k ¯ k\subset k^{\prime}\subset\overline{k} such that X X descends to k ′ k^{\prime} . In this paper we extend the formalism, and define the field of moduli when k k is not perfect. Furthermore, Dèbes and Emsalem identified a condition that ensures that a smooth curve is defined over its field of moduli, and prove that a smooth curve with a marked point is always defined over its field of moduli. Our main theorem is a generalization of these results that applies to higher dimensional varieties, and to varieties with additional structures. In order to apply this, we study the problem of when a rational point of a variety with quotient singularities lifts to a resolution. As a consequence, we prove that a variety X X of dimension d d with a smooth marked point p p such that Aut ⁡ ( X , p ) \operatorname{Aut}(X,p) is finite, étale and of degree prime to d ! d! is defined over its field of moduli.



1.
Introduction

The concept of
field of moduli
was introduced by Matsusaka in
[
Mat58
]
, and considerably clarified by Shimura in
[
Shi59
]
. Suppose that
k
is a field with algebraic closure
\overline{k}
. Let us assume for simplicity that
k
is perfect. An algebraic variety
X
, perhaps with additional structure, like a polarization, or a marked point, will be defined over some intermediate field
k\to\ell\to\overline{k}
that is finite over
k
. If
\Gamma
is the Galois group of
\overline{k}
over
k
, call
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
the subgroup formed by elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that the twist
X_{\gamma}
is isomorphic to
X
as a
k
-scheme, possibly with its additional structure. Then
\Delta
is an open subgroup of
\Gamma
; the field of moduli of
X
is the fixed subfield
\overline{k}^{\Delta}
. It is contained in every field of definition of
X
.

If
X
has a finite group of automorphisms, and is one of a class of varieties with finite automorphism groups parametrized by a coarse moduli space
M\to\operatorname{Spec}k
(for example, smooth curves of genus at least
2
), then the field of moduli has a natural interpretation as the residue field of the image of the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\overline{k}\to M
corresponding to
X
.

One basic question is: when is
X
defined over its field of moduli? This problem has been the subject of a considerable amount of literature over the years.

An important early example is due to Goro Shimura
[
Shi72
]
. Let
A_{g}
be the moduli space of abelian varieties of genus
g
over
\mathbb{C}
, and call
K
its field of rational functions. Let
X
be the corresponding generic abelian variety defined over
\overline{K}
; its field of moduli is
K
. Then Shimura proved that
X
is defined over
K
if and only
g
is odd.

In the case that
X
is a smooth curve, an important advance is due to Pierre Dèbes and Michel Emsalem
[
DE99
]
.

Let
X
be as above; assume that the group
\operatorname{Aut}X
of automorphisms of
X
over
\overline{k}
is finite. Consider the group
\Delta
above; for each
\delta\in\Delta
we have an isomorphism
X_{\delta}\simeq X
, well defined up to an automorphism of
X
over
\overline{k}
. This descend to an canonical automorphism of
X/\operatorname{Aut}X
, defining an action of
\Delta
on
X
, compatible with it action on
\overline{k}
; by Galois descent this defines a scheme
X^{\mathrm{c}}
over
k
, a form of
X/\operatorname{Aut}X
, which we call the
compression
of
X
. If
X
is a smooth curve, so is
X^{\mathrm{c}}
.

Theorem

(Dèbes–Emsalem)
.

Assume that
X
is a smooth curve. If
X^{\mathrm{c}}(k)\neq\emptyset
, then
X
is defined over its field of moduli.

(See
[
Bre22b
]
for applications of this result.)

Let us describe the content of this paper.

The definition of field of moduli

The definition of field of moduli for an object defined over
\overline{k}
is present in the literature in two particular cases, as explained above: when
k
is perfect, and when
X
is in a class of object having a coarse moduli space. This last hypothesis is not a very natural one: for example, one could be interested in the field of moduli of non-polarized abelian varieties, or K3 surfaces. In §
3
we give a somewhat more general and flexible formalism for defining fields of moduli and residual gerbes under very weak hypotheses. In particular we adapt it to non-perfect fields and objects with non-reduced automorphism group schemes, using the fppf topology instead of the Galois group.

In §
4
we draw some consequences using results in the literature; this part is certainly well known to the experts, and essentially contained in
[
DE99
]
; but it seems useful to have a detailed explanation in our generality.

In the rest of the introduction we will assume
\operatorname{char}k=0
; this will simplify the statements considerably. We refer to the main body of the paper for precise statements in arbitrary characteristic.

The Dèbes–Emsalem theorem in arbitrary dimension

If
X
is as in the introduction, if
X
is singular, or higher-dimensional, it is not true that if
X^{\mathrm{c}}(k)\neq\emptyset
, then
X
is defined over its field of moduli. Our main result (Theorem
5.4
) is the following: let
\widetilde{X}
be a resolution of singularities of
X^{\mathrm{c}}
. If
\widetilde{X}(k)\neq\emptyset
, then
X
is defined over its field of moduli.

When
X
is a smooth curve, we have that the compression
X^{\mathrm{c}}
is smooth, so this recovers the result of Dèbes–Emsalem. Dèbes and Emsalem mention the fact that their methods can be generalized to curves with a structure such as pointed curves
[
DE99
, Remark 3.2.(b)]
, but they do not give details on how to do it. So in dimension
1
, we are essentially clarifying what curves with a structure are, and checking that the theorem of Dèbes–Emsalem holds for them. More importantly, we are able to generalize their result to arbitrary dimension.

Dèbes and Emsalem also observed that a suitable form of their theorem implies that every pointed curve of genus
\geq 2
is defined over its field of moduli
[
DE99
, Corollary 5.4]
. This fails, however, in higher dimension: smooth pointed varieties are not necessarily defined over their fields of moduli. An example is Shimura’s result on generic abelian varieties explained in the introduction. In order to apply the result above we need to ensure that a rational point of the compression
X^{\mathrm{c}}
lifts to a resolution of singularities.

More precisely, we need conditions that ensure that a resolution of singularities
\widetilde{X}\to X^{\mathrm{c}}
has a rational point. In the case of pointed varieties this translates into the following question. Suppose that
X
is a variety over
\overline{k}
and
G
is a finite group acting on
X
with a smooth fixed point
\overline{p}\in X(\overline{k})
. Let
X^{\mathrm{c}}
be a form of
X/G
defined over
k
, with a rational point
p\in X^{\mathrm{c}}(k)
lifting to
\overline{p}
. If
\widetilde{X}\to X^{\mathrm{c}}
is a resolution of singularities, under what conditions does it follow that there is a rational point of
\widetilde{X}
lying over
p
?

The arithmetic of quotient singularities

In §
6
we introduce two related concepts.

One is that of
R
-singularity
(Definition
6.13
). An
R
-singularity is a pair
(S,s)
, where
S
is a variety over a field
K
with quotient singularities and
s\in S(K)
, such that, in particular, if
k\subseteq K
is a subfield,
(S^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is a form of
(S,s)
defined over
k
, and
\widetilde{S}^{\prime}\to S
is a resolution of singularities, then
\widetilde{S}^{\prime}
has a
k
-rational point over
s^{\prime}
.

The other key definition is that of
R_{d}
-group (Definition
6.12
): if
d
is a positive integer, a finite group
G
is
R_{d}
if whenever it acts faithfully on a smooth variety
X
with a fixed rational point
x\in X(k)
, the pair
(X/G,[x])
, where
[x]
is the image of
x
, is an
R
-singularity. It follows from our main theorem that if
X
is a smooth variety over
\overline{k}
and
\overline{p}\in X(\overline{k})
is a
\overline{k}
-point, and
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\overline{p})
is an
R_{d}
group, then
(X,\overline{p})
is defined over its field of moduli.

Not all finite groups are
R_{d}
: for example, a cyclic group of order
2
is not
R_{d}
for any
d\geq 2
. Here we present two results, showing that this is not an empty definition.

The first (Theorem
6.18
) shows that there are infinitely many groups that are
R_{d}
for all
d
. The second (Theorem
6.19
) says that any finite group of order prime to
d!
is
R_{d}
. As a consequence (Theorem
6.21
), a
d
-dimensional variety
X
with a smooth marked point
p\in X
such that
\operatorname{Aut}(X,p)
is finite of degree prime to
d!
is defined over its field of moduli.

There is much more that one could say about
R_{d}
-groups. The forthcoming paper
[
Bre22a
]
will contain a complete classification of
R_{2}
-groups. The case
d>2
seems much harder; hopefully it will be the subject of further work.

Another result along these lines is Corollary
6.24
, stating that if
X
is an odd-dimensional variety over
\overline{k}
with a smooth marked point
\overline{p}\in X(\overline{k})
, and the automorphism group of
(X,\overline{p})
is cyclic of order
2
and has
\overline{p}
as an isolated fixed point, then
(X,\overline{p})
is defined over its field of moduli. This would be false without assuming that
\overline{p}
is an isolated fixed point, as the cyclic group
\mathrm{C}_{2}
is not
R_{d}
for any
d\geq 2
. This vastly generalizes Shimura’s result that odd dimensional generic principally polarized abelian varieties are defined over their field of moduli.

For the second and third part, the fundamental tool is the Lang–Nishimura theorem for tame stacks proved in
[
BV
]
(see Theorem
5.5
).

Notations and conventions

We will follow the conventions of
[
Knu71
]
and
[

]

We will follow the terminology of

: a

If

, whose objects are

-torsors.

Fields of moduli

Let
k
be a field,
(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
the category of affine
k
-schemes. All stacks will be fppf stacks over

. If
\mathcal{M}
is such a stack, and

-algebra, we set
\mathcal{M}(R)\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{Spec}R)

Suppose that

\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{K}\xi\colon(\mathrm{Aff}/K)^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow(\mathrm{Grp})\,,

where

If

If

is an algebraic space of finite type if and only if it is a group scheme of finite type.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
be a locally finitely presented fppf stack,

an extension of
k
,
\xi
and
\eta
two objects of

. Let

be an algebraic extension of

(1)

The functor
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}}\nolimits_{K\otimes_{k}K}(\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\xi,\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\eta)
is an algebraic space of finite type over

if and only if
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}}\nolimits_{K^{\prime}\otimes_{k}K^{\prime}}(\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\xi_{K^{\prime}},\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\eta_{K^{\prime}})
is an algebraic space of finite type over

.

(2)

The functor
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{K}\xi
is a group scheme of finite type if and only if
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{K^{\prime}}\xi^{\prime}
is a group scheme of finite type.

Both part are immediate consequences of the following, applied to the cases
R=K

R^{\prime}=K^{\prime}
, and

,

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring,
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a filtered inductive system of finitely presented

be an fppf sheaf that is locally of finite presentation, and call

the composite
(\mathrm{Aff}/R^{\prime})^{\mathrm{op}}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/R)^{\mathrm{op}}\to(\mathrm{Set})
, where the functor
(\mathrm{Aff}/R^{\prime})^{\mathrm{op}}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/R)^{\mathrm{op}}

is a finitely presented algebraic space over

Proof.

If

is a finitely presented algebraic space then

is also a finitely presented algebraic space. So, assume that

is a finitely presented algebraic space.

If

is finitely presented over

[

. So, denote by

the pullback of

to

; it is enough to show that

is a finitely presented algebraic space for some

. Since

is finitely presented, it will be obtained by pullback from a finitely presented algebraic space

over some

; we can replace

with

, and assume that

is defined over

. Then it is enough to show that for some

We claim that the isomorphism

comes from a morphism

for some

. For this, choose an affine scheme

with an étale surjective map

, and an affine scheme

with an étale surjective map

. Since

is an fppf sheaf, for any

is the equalizer of the two maps

; since

is locally of finite presentation, and filtered colimits commute with equalizers, we easily get
\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\prime}}(G_{R^{\prime}},F_{R^{\prime}})=\varinjlim_{i}\operatorname{Hom}_{R_{i}}(G_{R_{i}},F_{i})
, hence the thesis. So, by replacing

we may assume that there exists a morphism

that pulls back to the isomorphism

The residual gerbe

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}_{\xi}}

Proof.

We can assume that

Definition 3.11
.

The field

of

The field of moduli has the following interpretation. Denote by

.

The field of moduli

is the equalizer of the two arrows

\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\colon K\to\mathcal{O}(R)

Proof.

Since

In the “classical” case, where

.

Definition 3.14
.

3.2.
Residual gerbes and moduli spaces

In case

is an algebraic stack with finite inertia, there is another interesting interpretation of the field of moduli of a tame object.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}
is an algebraic stack with finite inertia, with moduli space
\mathcal{M}\to M
, and let

\xi
over the algebraic closure

Definition 3.15
.

Assume that

residual gerbe of

\mathcal{G}_{m}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}k(m)\times_{M}\mathcal{M}\bigr)_{\mathrm{r}ed}\,.

Proposition 3.16
.

The residual gerbe
\mathcal{G}_{m}
is a finite tame gerbe over
k(m)
.

Proof.

By
[

, Proposition 3.6]
the tame points of

form an open subspace

; we can replace

with

, and assume that
\mathcal{M}
is tame. Since formation of moduli spaces of tame stacks commutes with base change, we have that the moduli space of
\operatorname{Spec}k(m)\times_{M}\mathcal{M}
is

; and from this, that the moduli space of

is

. From

, Proposition 06RC]
it follows that

is a gerbe over

∎

Proposition 3.17
.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}
is an algebraic stack with finite inertia locally of finite type over
k
, with moduli space
\mathcal{M}\to M
. Let
\xi\colon\operatorname{Spec}K\to\mathcal{M}
be a tame object, and call

the image of the composite

. Then the residual gerbe of
\xi
is
\mathcal{G}_{m}
, and the field of moduli

is the residue field

.

Proof.

We may assume that
K
is finite over
k
. The morphism
\operatorname{Spec}K\to\mathcal{M}
factors through
\operatorname{Spec}k(m)\times_{M}\mathcal{M}
; since

is reduced we get a factorization
\operatorname{Spec}K\to\mathcal{G}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. Since

is a finite gerbe, it follows that

∎

It is easy to give counterexamples to the statement of Proposition

without the tameness hypothesis. The point is that the moduli space of

.

4.
Application of nonabelian cohomology

In the situation above, assume that
\xi\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{k})
is an algebraic object.

As an immediate corollary of the fact that every affine gerbe over a finite field is neutral
[
DPTZ20
, Theorem 8.1]
, we get the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
k
is finite and
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\overline{k}}\xi
is affine. Then
\xi
is defined over its field of moduli.

One can also apply standard results on the classification of gerbes, which usually go under the name of Grothendieck–Giraud nonabelian cohomology
[
Gir71
]
, to get conditions ensuring that
\xi
is defined over its field of moduli. This works very cleanly when
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\overline{k}}\xi
is finite and reduced.

Set
G\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\overline{k}}\xi
, and assume for the rest of the section that
G
is finite and reduced; according to our general conventions we think of
G
as an ordinary group. Denote by
\operatorname{Aut}G
the group of automorphisms of
G
, and by
\operatorname{Out}G
its group of outer automorphisms, that is, the cokernel of the homomorphism
G\to\operatorname{Aut}G
given by conjugation.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

the center of
G
is trivial, and

(2)

the projection
\operatorname{Aut}G\to\operatorname{Out}G
is split.

Then
\xi
is defined over its field of moduli.

This should be compared with
[
DE99
, Corollary 4.3(b)]
.

In similar spirit we get the following, which is a generalization of
[
DE99
, Corollary 4.3(a)]
, with the same proof.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that the absolute Galois group of
k
has cohomological dimension at most
1
. Then
\xi
is defined over its field of moduli.

These two propositions are immediate corollaries of the following standard application of nonabelian cohomology.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
k
a field. Let
\mathcal{G}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
be a gerbe such that
\mathcal{G}_{\overline{k}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}_{\overline{k}}G
. Assume that either

(1)

G
has trivial center, and
\operatorname{Aut}G\to\operatorname{Out}G
is split, or

(2)

k
has cohomological dimension
1
.

Then
\mathcal{G}
is neutral.

Proof.

Denote by
k^{s}
the separable closure of
k
and by
\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)
be the absolute Galois group of
k
, we have that
\mathcal{G}_{k^{s}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}_{k^{s}}G
by the following Lemma
4.5
. The tautological section
\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}\to\mathcal{B}_{k^{s}}G\to\mathcal{G}
induces a continuous homomorphism
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Out}G
. Under both conditions (1) and (2), we have a lifting
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aut}G
: for (1) this is obvious, while for (2) this follows from the fact that
G
is projective
[
Ser94
, Proposition 45]
. By descent theory, the homomorphism
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aut}G
induces a finite étale group scheme
\underline{G}
which is a twisted form of
G
.

Let
L
be the non-abelian band, in the sense of
[
Gir71
]
, of
\mathcal{G}
. By construction,
L
is represented by
\underline{G}
. By
[
Gir71
, Théorème 3.3.3]
, under both conditions (1) and (2), the gerbe
\mathcal{G}
is the only gerbe banded by
L
. Since the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}_{k}\underline{G}
is banded by
L
by construction, we get that
\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{B}_{k}\underline{G}
, i.e.
\mathcal{G}
is neutral.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

A finite gerbe with unramified diagonal over a separably closed field is neutral.

Proof.

Let
k
be a separably closed field with algebraic closure
\overline{k}
, and
\mathcal{G}
a finite gerbe over
k
with unramified diagonal. There exists a finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
with a section
s\in\mathcal{G}(k^{\prime})
. The scheme
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}}\nolimits(p_{1}^{*}s,p_{2}^{*}s)
is finite étale over the artinian local ring
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
and we have a lifting
\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}}\nolimits(p_{1}^{*}s,p_{2}^{*}s)
of the closed point
\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
, hence we get a section
\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\to\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}}\nolimits(p_{1}^{*}s,p_{2}^{*}s)
. By descent theory we obtain that
s
descends to
k
.
∎

These results do not apply in many cases of great interest, for example, when
G
is abelian and
k
is a number field.

Our main theorem, which is a generalization of
[
DE99
, Corollary 4.3(c)]
, gives a criterion for this to happen, when
\xi
is tame, for an interesting class of stacks, whose objects are algebraic spaces with additional structure.

The main theorem

5.1.
Categories of structured spaces

We will denote by
(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
the fibered category over
(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
whose objects over an affine scheme
S
over
k
are flat finitely presented morphisms
X\to S
, where
X
is an algebraic space.

Definition 5.1
.

A
category of structured spaces
over
k
is a locally finitely presented fppf stack
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
, with a faithful cartesian functor
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
.

Examples spring to mind.

Examples 5.2
.

(1)

The category
(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
itself is a category of structured spaces.

(2)

Any condition that we impose on objects
X\to S
in
(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
that is stable under base change and fppf local defines a full subcategory
\mathcal{M}\subseteq(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
that is a category of structured spaces. Thus, for example, the stacks
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of smooth, or stable, curves of genus
g
, the stack of smooth abelian varieties, of projective surfaces, and so on.

(3)

The stacks
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of
n
-pointed smooth, or stable, curves of genus
g
are all categories of structured spaces.

(4)

The category of smooth polarized projective schemes is a category of structured spaces.

An object
\xi
of a category of structured spaces
\mathcal{M}
will be denoted by
(X\to S,\xi)
, or simply
(X,\xi)
, where
X\to S
is the image of
\xi
in
(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
; we want to think of
(X,\xi)
as an algebraic space with an additional structure.

A category of structured spaces
\mathcal{M}
has a
universal family

\mathcal{X}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
; the objects of
\mathcal{X}
are triples
(X\to S,\xi,x)
, where
(X\to S,\xi)
is an object of
\mathcal{M}(S)
, and

is a section of the morphism
X\to S
. We have an obvious morphism
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{M}
which forgets the section. If
S\to\mathcal{M}
is a morphism, corresponding to an object
(X\to S,\xi)
of
\mathcal{M}(S)
, then the fibered product
S\times_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{X}
is equivalent to
X
; hence the morphism
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{M}
is representable, flat and finitely presented. The universal family is itself a category of structured spaces.

Now, let
(X,\xi)
be an algebraic object of
\mathcal{M}(\overline{k})
, and denote by
\mathcal{X}_{(X,\xi)}
the fibered product
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}\times_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{X}
. We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[X/\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\overline{k}}\xi]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{(X,\xi)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}\overline{k}}

in which the squares marked with

are cartesian.

Definition 5.3
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}_{(X,\xi)}
(and hence
\mathcal{X}_{(X,\xi)}
) has finite inertia. The

of
(X,\xi)
is the coarse moduli space of

, it is an algebraic space over the field of moduli

. We denote the compression using bold letters, for instance we write

In other words, while

5.2.
The main result

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
be a category of structured spaces,
(X,\xi)\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{k})
a tame object with

Assume that there exists a dominant rational map

-rational regular point. Then

is defined over its field of moduli

.

In this proof, and in the rest of the paper, a crucial role is played by the Lang–Nishimura theorem for tame stack that we prove in

. For the convenience of the reader we recall its statement.

Theorem 5.5

(
[

, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let

be a scheme and

a rational map of algebraic stacks over

, with

locally noetherian and integral and

S
. Let

be a field,

\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
; then it also lifts to a morphism

.

Proof of Theorem

.

There exists an
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\overline{k}}\xi
-invariant open subset

such that the action of
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\overline{k}}\xi
on
U
is free: by hypothesis the action of
\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits_{\overline{k}}\xi
on

is faithful, and for each non-trivial subgroup

. This in turn implies that there exists an open substack

which is an algebraic space; then the composite

is an open embedding.

Because of this, the hypothesis gives us a rational map

. We conclude by applying Theorem

be a category of structured spaces,

5.3.
The case of pointed spaces

One case in which we can ensure the existence of a rational point on
\mathbf{X}_{(X,\xi)}
is the case of pointed spaces. For the rest of the paper we will use the following definition.

Definition 5.7
.

A
pointed space

(X,p)
over an affine scheme
S
over
k
is a flat locally finitely presented morphism
X\to S
with a section
p\colon S\to X
landing in the smooth locus of
f
.

Pointed spaces form a fibered category
(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)\to(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
; there is an obvious representable cartesian functor
(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)\to(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
, which forgets the section.

Definition 5.8
.

A
category of pointed structured spaces
over
k
is a locally finitely presented fppf stack
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{Aff}/k)
, with a faithful cartesian functor
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)
.

If
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)
is a category of pointed structured spaces and
S
is a scheme, an element of
\mathcal{M}(S)
will be denoted by
(X,p,\xi)
, where
(X,\xi)
is the corresponding structured space given by the composition
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)\to(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
and
p\colon S\to X
is the given section.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a category of pointed structured spaces, it can be considered as a category of structured spaces by composing
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)
with the forgetful morphism
(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)\to(\mathrm{FAS}/k)
. One can think of categories of pointed structured spaces as a category of structured spaces, in which the structure includes a smooth marked point.

There are many natural examples of categories of pointed structured spaces: for example, the category of abelian varieties, or the category of
n
-pointed stable, or smooth, curves, for
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a category of pointed structured spaces,
(X,p,\xi)\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{k})
a tame object. The compression
\mathbf{X}_{(X,p,\xi)}
has a rational point
\mathbf{p}
over

such that
\mathbf{p}_{\overline{k}}
corresponds to
p
via the identification
\mathbf{X}_{(X,p,\xi),\overline{k}}=X/\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}}\nolimits(X,p,\xi)
.

Proof.

Consider the universal family
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{M}
, and its smooth locus
\mathcal{X}_{\rm sm}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
(this is the largest open substack of
\mathcal{X}
where the restriction of
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{M}
is smooth). The fibered product
\mathcal{M}\times_{(\mathrm{FAS}/k)}(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathcal{X}_{\rm sm}
; hence the cartesian functor
\mathcal{M}\to(\mathrm{PFAS}/k)
induces a section
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{X}_{\rm sm}
of the projection
\mathcal{X}_{\rm sm}\subseteq\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{M}
. By restricting to
\mathcal{G}_{(X,p,\xi)}
we obtain a section
\mathcal{G}_{(X,p,\xi)}\to\mathcal{X}_{(X,p,\xi)}
of the projection
\mathcal{X}_{(X,p,\xi)}\to\mathcal{G}_{(X,p,\xi)}
, and, passing to moduli spaces, a section
\operatorname{Spec}k(X,p,\xi)\to\mathbf{X}_{(X,p,\xi)}
of the projection
\mathbf{X}_{(X,p,\xi)}\to\operatorname{Spec}k(X,p,\xi)
, or, in other words, a

-rational point of
\mathbf{X}_{(X,p,\xi)}
.

This fails for stable curves that are not irreducible: one can give examples of

6.
The arithmetic of tame quotient singularities

Let
S
be an algebraic space of finite type over a field
k
. We say that
S

has tame quotient singularities
if there is an étale cover
\{S_{i}\to S\}
and, for each
i
, a smooth algebraic space
U_{i}
and a finite group
G_{i}
of order not divisible by
\operatorname{char}k
acting on
U_{i}
, such that
S_{i}
is isomorphic to
U_{i}/G
. In particular,
S
is normal.

More generally, one could consider spaces that are étale-locally quotients of smooth algebraic spaces by finite linearly reductive group schemes, as in
[
Sat12
]
; but the technology to adequately deal with these in our context still does not seem to be completely in place, which forces us to limit ourselves to considering tame Deligne–Mumford stacks, as opposed to general tame stacks.

6.1.
Minimal stacks

The following is known, see
[
Vis89
, Proposition 2.8]
. Our statement is slightly different from the one in the reference, though, so we give details.

Proposition 6.1
.

The moduli space of a smooth tame Deligne–Mumford stack with finite inertia over
k
has tame quotient singularities.

Conversely, if
S
is an algebraic space with finite quotient singularities, there exists a smooth tame Deligne–Mumford stack with finite inertia
\widehat{S}
with moduli space
S
, with the property that the morphism
\widehat{S}\to S
is an isomorphism over the smooth locus of
S
.

Furthermore, if
V
is a smooth integral Deligne–Mumford stack with a dominant morphism
V\to\widehat{S}
, there exists a factorization
V\to\widehat{S}\to S
, unique up to a unique isomorphism. In particular,
\widehat{S}
is unique, up to a unique isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth, tame Deligne–Mumford stack with finite inertia, and let
M
be its moduli space, we want to show that
M
has tame quotient singularities. By
[
AV02
, Lemma 2.2.3]
, we may assume that
\mathcal{X}=[U/G]
, where
G
is a finite group, so that
M=X/G
. If
u_{0}\colon\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to U
is a geometric point of
U
, and
G_{u_{0}}
is the stabilizer of
u_{0}
, then the natural morphism
U/G_{u_{0}}\to U/G
is étale in neighborhood of
u_{0}
; but
G_{u_{0}}
has order prime to
\operatorname{char}k
, because
[U/G]
is tame, so the result follows.

If
S
has tame quotient singularities, then the second half of the proof of
[
Vis89
, Proposition 2.8]
shows the existence of a stack
\widehat{S}\to S
as in the statement (the reference’s assumption
\operatorname{char}k=0
is not used in the relevant part of the proof).

Now let
V\to\widehat{S}
be as in the statement. Since
S
is normal,
\widehat{S}\to S
is an isomorphism in codimension
1
. Let
U
be the normalization of
V\times_{S}\widehat{S}
, since everything is of finite type over
k
and
S
is the moduli space of
\widehat{S}
, then
U\to V
is proper and birational. By purity of branch locus, it is étale too, hence
U\simeq V
and we obtain the desired morphism
V\to\widehat{S}
. The uniqueness follows from the fact that
\widehat{S}
is separated.
∎

We call the stack
\widehat{S}
above the
minimal stack
of
S
(also called the
canonical stack
in the literature). Clearly, if
S
has tame quotient singularities, and
k^{\prime}
is an extension of
k
, the space
S_{k^{\prime}}
obtained by base change also has tame quotient singularities, and the minimal stack of
S_{k^{\prime}}
is
\widehat{S}_{k^{\prime}}
. In other words, formation of the minimal stack commutes with extensions of the base field. Furthermore, if
S^{\prime}\to S
is an étale morphism and
S
has tame quotient singularities, then so does
S^{\prime}
, and
\widehat{S^{\prime}}=S^{\prime}\times_{S}\widehat{S}
.

It is known that algebraic spaces with tame quotient singularities have a resolution of singularities, that is, there is a proper birational morphism
\widetilde{S}\to S
where
\widetilde{S}
is a smooth algebraic space over
k
. If
k
is perfect, this is
[
BR19
, Theorem E]
; in the general case it is obtained by applying
[
BR19
, Theorem B]
to the minimal stack
\widehat{S}\to S
.

6.2.
Singularities and fundamental gerbes

Let
k
be a field; a
tame quotient singularity
over
k
is a pair
(S,s)
, where
S
is a an integral scheme of finite type over
k
with tame quotient singularities and

is a
k
-rational point. No other kinds of singularities will appear in this paper, so from now on a tame quotient singularity will be called simply
a singularity
.

Two singularities
(S,s)
and
(S^{\prime},s^{\prime})
are
equivalent
if there exists a singularity
(S^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime\prime})
, together with étale maps
S^{\prime\prime}\to S
and
S^{\prime\prime}\to S^{\prime}
sending

into
s
and
s^{\prime}
respectively. This is true if and only if the complete local
k
-algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,s}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{S^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
are isomorphic
[
Art69
, Corollary 2.6]
.

Definition 6.2
.

Given a singularity
(S,s)
, the
fundamental gerbe

\mathcal{G}_{(S,s)}
of
(S,s)
is the residual gerbe, as in Definition
3.15
, of
\widehat{S}\to S
at
s
. The
fundamental group

G_{(S,s)}
of
(S,s)
is the automorphism group of any geometric point of
\mathcal{G}_{(S,s)}
.

Thus, by definition, the fundamental group of
(S,s)
is a finite group, or order prime to
\operatorname{char}k
. It is well defined up to a non-canonical isomorphism.

One can prove that
\mathcal{G}_{(S,s)}
is the local fundamental gerbe of
S
, in the following sense. Let
S^{\prime}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{S,s}^{\rm h}
, where
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{S,s}^{\rm h}
is the henselization of
\mathcal{O}_{S,s}
, and let
U\subseteq S^{\prime}
be the smooth locus of
S
. Set
\widehat{S^{\prime}}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}S^{\prime}\times_{S}\widehat{S}
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{(S,s)}
is the fundamental gerbe, in the sense of
[
BV15
]
of

and also of
\widehat{S^{\prime}}
. We do not prove this here, as it is not needed in what follows.

Since formation of the minimal stack commutes with étale morphisms, equivalent singularities have isomorphic fundamental gerbes.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume that
k
is separably closed. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth, tame Deligne–Mumford stack which is generically a scheme, with moduli space
\mathcal{X}\to S
. Let
\xi
be an object in
\mathcal{X}(k)
and
s\in S(k)
its image of
\xi
.

There exists a faithful representation
\operatorname{Aut}\xi\subset\mathrm{GL}_{d}(k)
such that
(S,s)
is equivalent to
(\mathbb{A}^{d}/\operatorname{Aut}\xi,[0])
, and the quotient of
\operatorname{Aut}\xi
by the subgroup generated by pseudoreflexions is isomorphic to the fundamental group of
(S,s)
.

Proof.

After passing to an étale neighborhood of
s\in S
, we may assume
\mathcal{X}\simeq[U/H]
with
U
smooth and
H
finite of order prime to
\operatorname{char}k
. Since
k
is separably closed, the rational point
\xi\in\mathcal{X}(k)
lifts to a rational point

. Let
H_{u}\subset H
be the stabilizer of

, then
U/H_{u}\to U/H\simeq S
is étale in
[u]
, hence we may replace
\mathcal{X},H
with
[U/H_{u}],H_{u}=\operatorname{Aut}\xi
and assume that
H=\operatorname{Aut}\xi
and that

is a fixed point.

Call
V
the tangent space of
U
at
u
; then
\operatorname{Aut}\xi
acts on

, and by fixing a basis we get a representation
\operatorname{Aut}\xi\to\mathrm{GL}_{d}(k)
. By Cartan’s lemma, after passing to an equivariant étale neighborhood of

in
U
we may assume that there exists an étale
\operatorname{Aut}\xi
-equivariant map

. This implies that the action of
\operatorname{Aut}\xi
on

is faithful, and that
(S,s)=(U/\operatorname{Aut}\xi,[u])
is equivalent to
(V/\operatorname{Aut}\xi,[0])
.

Denote by
P\subset\operatorname{Aut}\xi
the subgroup generated by pseudoreflexions, by the Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem

is smooth and
\operatorname{Aut}\xi/P
acts on it with with no fixed points in codimension

. It follows that
[(V/P)/(\operatorname{Aut}\xi/P)]
is the minimal stack of

, and hence
\operatorname{Aut}\xi/P
is the fundamental group of
(V/\operatorname{Aut}\xi,[0])\sim(S,s)
.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Assume that

is separably closed, and let
(S,s)
be a tame quotient singularity over

. There exists a faithful representation

with no pseudoreflexions such that
(S,s)\sim(\mathbb{A}^{d}_{k}/G_{(S,s)},[0])

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma

, we may apply Lemma

to

.
∎

6.3.
Liftable singularities

The following is immediate from Theorem
5.5
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
(S,s)
be a tame quotient singularity,

a resolution of singularities. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

has a

(2)

The fundamental gerbe
\mathcal{G}_{(S,s)}
of

(3)

\widehat{S}\to S

(4)

Definition 6.6
.

Let

(S,s)

liftable

Remarks 6.7
.

(1)

If two singularities are equivalent, then one is liftable if and only if the other is.

(2)

Since formation of

commutes with extension of the base field, we see that if

is liftable over

is an extension of

The following construction gives a criterion for a singularity to be liftable, which will be a fundamental tool in the rest of the paper.

6.4.
The blowup construction

Let
K/k
be a separable closure. Let
(S,s)
be a tame quotient singularity over
k
; by Corollary
6.4

(S_{K},s_{K})
is equivalent to
(\mathbb{A}^{d}/G,[0])
for some
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{d}(K)
, such that
G
contains no pseudoreflexions and has order prime to
\operatorname{char}k
. Denote by
\overline{G}
the image of
G
in
\mathrm{PGL}_{d}(K)
.

Denote by
\mathcal{G}
the fundamental gerbe
\mathcal{G}_{(S,s)}
, and by
\mathcal{N}
the normal bundle of
\mathcal{G}
in
\widehat{S}
; this is a vector bundle over
\mathcal{G}
of rank
d
. Also, denote by
\mathcal{B}
the blowup of
\widehat{S}
along
\mathcal{G}
; clearly the exceptional divisor
\mathcal{E}
equals
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N})
. Denote by
E
the moduli space of
\mathcal{E}
; the morphism
\mathcal{E}\to E
factors through the minimal stack
\widehat{E}
.

We have
\mathcal{E}_{K}=[\mathbb{P}^{d-1}_{K}/G]
,
\mathcal{N}_{K}=[\mathbb{A}^{d}_{K}/G]
, and
E_{K}=\mathbb{P}^{d-1}_{K}/\overline{G}
.

Definition 6.8
.

We say that
E
is the
associated variety
of the singularity.

Recall that
\mathcal{B}
is the blow up of
\mathcal{G}
in
\widehat{S}
, let

be its coarse moduli space. Since
\mathcal{B}
is a tame stack and formation of coarse moduli spaces commutes with base change for tame stacks, the reduced fiber of

over

is

.

Corollary 6.9
.

There exists a rational morphism
E\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

The morphism
\mathcal{B}\to B
is birational. Since
\mathcal{B}
is smooth, then
B
is normal, in particular it is regular at the generic point of

. It follows that the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k(E)\to B
lifts to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k(E)\to\mathcal{B}
by our version of the Lang–Nishimura theorem. Clearly, the composite
\operatorname{Spec}k(E)\to\mathcal{B}\to\widehat{S}
factors through
\mathcal{G}\to\widehat{S}
.
∎

Given an irreducible algebraic space
X
of finite type over a field
k
, we say that a field extension

splits

if there exists a dominant rational map

where

is an integral scheme of finite type over
k^{\prime}
with a smooth

-rational point. If

, then we say that

is split. If

has tame quotient singularities, using our version of the Lang–Nishimura theorem we see that

has a

-rational point.

Proposition 6.10
.

A field extension

splits

if and only if it splits

.

Proof.

If
k^{\prime}
splits
\mathcal{G}
, then there exists a morphism of

\operatorname{Spec}k^{\prime}\to\mathcal{G}
; since
\mathcal{E}
is the projectivization of a vector bundle on

maps to

If

6.5.
R
-singularities

If
k

Definition 6.11
.

An

From the definition, it is not clear that there are any non-trivial examples of

Let

Remarks 6.14

(1)

(2)

.

Let

The point of this definition is the following result. Let us put ourselves in the situation of subsection

We still have to give meaningful examples of

The following is straightforward.

Proposition 6.17
.

(1)

If

(2)

To give examples of finite groups that are

Theorem 6.18

Let

(1)

(2)

While this result is interesting, it does not give any new examples of applications of Theorem

Theorem 6.19

A group of order prime to

6.8.
Isolated

-singularities in odd dimension

The proof of Theorem
6.19
can be adapted to prove that singularities of type
\mathbb{A}^{d}/G
are
R
-singularities for many cases that are not covered in the statement of the theorem. Here we give just one example, that has an interesting application.

Theorem 6.23
.

Let
n
be a positive integer which is not divisible by
\operatorname{char}K
, and consider the standard action of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
on
\mathbb{A}^{d}_{K}
by multiplication. If
n
and
d
are relatively prime, then
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{d}/\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
is an
R
-singularity.

If
d
is odd,
\operatorname{char}K\neq 2
,
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{d}(K)
is a finite subgroup of order
2
, and
\mathbb{A}^{d}_{K}/G
has an isolated singularity, then
G=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}{\rm Id}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{d}(K)
. So from the theorem we get the following.

Corollary 6.24
.

An isolated, odd-dimensional
\mathrm{C}_{2}
-singularity is an
R
-singularity.

Plugging this into our main result we get the following.

Corollary 6.25
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a category of pointed structured spaces,
(X,p,\xi)
a tame object of
\mathcal{M}(\overline{k})
, such that
X
is
d
-dimensional and integral. Assume that the automorphism group of
(X,p,\xi)
is cyclic of order

, and that

is odd,
(X,p,\xi)
is defined over its field of moduli.

Thus, for example, we get that an odd dimensional abelian variety

with automorphism group as small as possible, that is, cyclic of order
2
, is defined over its field of moduli, recovering in particular Shimura’s result on odd-dimensional generic abelian varieties that has already been mentioned (see
[

]
).

Proof of Theorem

.

Under the hypotheses of the Theorem, the associated variety

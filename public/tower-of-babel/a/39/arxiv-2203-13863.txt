Complex manifolds of Sobolev mappings and a Hartogs-type theorem in loop spaces Thanks: mohammed.anakkar@univ-lille.fr
M. Anakkar

Abstract
. We recall the complex structure on the generalised loop spaces W k , 2 ​ ( S , X ) W^{k,2}(S,X) , where S S is a compact real manifold with boundary and X X is a complex manifold, and prove a Hartogs-type extension theorem for holomorphic maps from certain domains in generalized loop spaces.



Introduction

All manifolds in this paper are supposed to be Hausdorff and second countable.
In the first section we recall the notion of the Sobolev class
W^{k,2}(\Omega)
for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as well as its basic properties. We explain that a composition of a smooth map with a map of class
W^{k,2}
is still in the class
W^{k,2}
. Namely we prove the following theorem which goes back to
[
M
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k>\frac{n}{2}
, then for every
u\in W^{k,2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and every
f\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
the function
f\circ u
is in
W^{k,2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
.

This permits us to define correctly the space of Sobolev maps
W^{k,2}(S,X)
between real manifolds
S
and
X

and provide the natural structure of a Hilbert manifold on this space. In the second section following Lempert

[
L
]
we discuss the complex Hilbert structure of the Sobolev manifold
W^{k,2}(S,X)
, where
X
is now a complex manifold. S everywhere is a compact real manifold with boundary.

For positive integers
q\geqslant 1
,
n\geqslant 1

and real
r\in]0,1[
the
q
-concave Hartogs figure in
\mathbb{C}^{q+n}
is defined as

(1)

H_{q}^{n}(r):=\left(\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n}(r)\right)\cup\left(A^{q}_{1-r,1}\times\Delta^{n}\right).

where
A^{q}_{1-r,1}=\Delta^{q}\backslash\bar{\Delta}^{q}_{1-r}
. Here
\Delta^{q}_{r}
stands for the polydisk in
\mathbb{C}^{q}
centered at zero of radius
r
. The envelope of holomorphy of
H_{q}^{n}(r)
is
\Delta^{q+n}
.
We say that a complex manifold
X
is
q
-Hartogs if every
holomorphic mapping
f:H_{q}^{1}(r)\to X
extends to a holomorphic mapping
\tilde{f}:\Delta^{q+1}\to X
.
If the same holds for a complex Hilbert manifold
\mathcal{X}
we say that
\mathcal{X}
is
q
-Hilbert-Hartogs.
We proved in
[
A-Z
]
that if
\mathcal{X}
is
q
-Hilbert-Hartogs then every holomorphic mapping
f:H_{q}^{n}(r)\to\mathcal{X}
extends to a holomorphic mapping
\tilde{f}:\Delta^{q+n}\to\mathcal{X}
. For finite dimensional
X

this was proved in
[
I
]
.
In the last section of this paper we prove the following Hartogs-type extension theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
q
-Hilbert-Hartogs manifold. Then every holomorphic map

F:W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))\to\mathcal{X}

extends to a holomorphic map
\tilde{F}:W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})\to\mathcal{X}
.

This statement gives us an example of pairs of open sets
\mathcal{U}\subsetneqq\hat{\mathcal{U}}

in a complex Hilbert manifold such that holomorphic mappings with values in
q
-Hilbert-Hartogs manifolds
extend from
\mathcal{U}
to
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
. It shows that
\hat{\mathcal{U}}{:=}W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})
is,
in some sense, the “envelope of holomorphy” of
\mathcal{U}{:=}W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))
.
For compact

without boundary this theorem was proved in
[
A-I
]
.

1.
Sobolev maps between smooth manifolds

Our goal in this section is to define the space of Sobolev
W^{k,2}
-maps from a manifold
S
to a manifold
X

and explain that this space possesses a natural topology.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain.

Definition 1.1
.

The Sobolev class
W^{k,2}(\Omega)

is defined as follows.

W^{k,2}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\ |\ \forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\ |\alpha|\leqslant k,\ D^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\},

where
L^{2}(\Omega)
is the Hilbert space of equivalence classes of real valued square integrable functions with respect to the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The space
W^{k,2}(\Omega)
endowed with the scalar product

<u,v>_{W^{k,2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}{<D^{\alpha}u\ ,\ D^{\alpha}v>_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

is a Hilbert space. Recall that the Fourier transform of a function from the Schwarz space
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as

\hat{u}(\xi):=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{u(x)e^{-i<x,\xi>}d\mu(x)},

where
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By the density of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and using the Placherel identity we can extend the Fourier transform to the isometry of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
onto itself, i.e. for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
one has that
||\hat{f}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
One can give the characterisation of Sobolev maps by the Fourier transform.

u\in W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Longleftrightarrow\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\alpha|\leqslant k,\ \ D^{(\alpha)}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\Longleftrightarrow\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\alpha|\leqslant k,\ \ \xi^{\alpha}\hat{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\Longleftrightarrow\ (1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\hat{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Moreover the corresponding norms are equivalent. More precisely we have the following.

Proposition 1.1
.

For
u\in W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have the inequalities:

i)

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\left\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
;

ii)

there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
wich depends on
k
and
n
only such that

(2)

\left\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant a\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

First we compute the following:

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\left\|D^{(\alpha)}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}={\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\left\|\widehat{D^{(\alpha)}u}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}={\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\left\|\xi_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\xi_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\hat{u}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\xi_{1}^{2\alpha_{1}}\ldots\xi_{n}^{2\alpha_{n}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}={\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\xi^{2\alpha}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}.

So we can establish the following equality

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\xi^{2\alpha}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}.

For
(i)
we have
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{k}=(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\ldots+\xi_{n}^{2})^{k}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}a_{\alpha}\xi^{2\alpha}\geqslant\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\xi^{2\alpha}
since
a_{\alpha}\geqslant 1
. And therefore

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\xi^{2\alpha}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}\leqslant\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{k}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

As for
(ii)
, since
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{k}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}a_{\alpha}\xi^{2\alpha}\leqslant a^{2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\xi^{2\alpha}
with
a=\max_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\sqrt{a_{\alpha}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{k}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}\leqslant a\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant k}\xi^{2\alpha}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle a\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}.

Proposition is proved.
∎

Denote by
W^{k,2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
the space of distributions
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
which are locally in
W^{k,2}
, i.e. for every relatively compact
D\Subset\Omega
the restriction
u|_{D}\in W^{k,2}(D)
.
It is clear that for
u\in W^{k,2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and a mapping
f:B\to\Omega
from a domain
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
\Omega
of class
\mathcal{C}^{k}
the composition
u\circ f\in W^{k,2}_{\text{loc}}(B)
.

Recall the Sobolev imbedding theorem:
if
f\in W^{k,2}(\Omega)
and
k>m+\frac{n}{2}
, then there exists a
m
-times continuously differentiable function on
\Omega
that is equal to
f
almost everywhere
.
Therefore the condition
k>\frac{n}{2}
will be always assumed along this text in order to insure that all
u\in W^{k,2}
are at least continuous.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k>\frac{n}{2}
, then for every
u\in W^{k,2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(\mathbb{R})
the function
f\circ u
is in
W^{k,2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.
The proof will be achieved in three steps.

Step 1:
We shall state it in the form of a lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

For
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\geqslant 0
we have the following inequality:

(3)

(1+|\xi|^{2})^{t}\leqslant 4^{t}\big((1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{t}+(1+|\eta|^{2})^{t}\big).

Proof.

Since
|\xi-\eta+\eta|^{2}\leqslant\Big(|\xi-\eta|+|\eta|\Big)^{2}\leqslant 2\Big(|\xi-\eta|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle(1+|\xi|^{2})^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+|\xi-\eta+\eta|^{2})^{t}\leqslant(1+2|\xi-\eta|^{2}+2|\eta|^{2})^{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(4+2|\xi-\eta|^{2}+2|\eta|^{2})^{t}\leqslant 2^{t}(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2}+1+|\eta|^{2})^{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2^{t}\Big(2\max(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2},1+|\eta|^{2})\Big)^{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{t}\Big(\max(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2},1+|\eta|^{2})\Big)^{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{t}\Big(\max(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2},1+|\eta|^{2})\Big)^{t}+4^{t}\Big(\min(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2},1+|\eta|^{2})\Big)^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{t}(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{t}+4^{t}(1+|\eta|^{2})^{t}.

Lemma is proved
∎

Step 2: Let us state it again as a lemma.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
k>n/2
then the product of two Sobolev maps
u,v\in W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is in
W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the estimate

\left\|uv\right\|_{W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant C\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{uv}=\hat{u}*\hat{v}
we have

\left\|uv\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\leqslant\left\|\widehat{uv}(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(\hat{u}*\hat{v})(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{k}d\xi}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\int_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ d\eta\right|^{2}d\xi}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt{\int_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ d\eta\right)^{2}d\xi}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}4^{k}|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)|(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}4^{k}|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)|(1+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}

The second inequality here follows from lemma
1.1
.
In the second integral, we make the change of variable
s=\xi-\eta
and get

\left\|\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}4^{k}|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)|(1+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}=\hskip 227.62204pt

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\int_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(4^{k}\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)|(1+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ d\eta\right)^{2}d\xi}

\displaystyle=4^{k}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(s)\hat{v}(\xi-s)|(1+|\xi-s|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ ds\right)^{2}d\xi}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4^{k}\left\|\int_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(s)\hat{v}(\xi-s)|(1+|\xi-s|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ ds\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Let recall the Minkowski’s inequality for integrals, see
[
S
]
§A.1.
For
p\in]1,+\infty[
and
F(x,y)
a measurable function on the product measure space
X\times Y
one has

(4)

\left(\int_{Y}\left|\int_{X}F(x,y)dx\right|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\int_{X}\left(\int_{Y}|F(x,y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}dx.

In other words, we have the inequality :

\left\|\int_{X}F(x,y)dx\right\|_{L^{p}}\leqslant\int_{X}\left\|F(x,y)\right\|_{L^{p}}dx.

Therefore by Minkowski integral inequality (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|uv\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{k}\left\|\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)|(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}+4^{k}\left\|\int_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(s)\hat{v}(\xi-s)|(1+|\xi-s|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\ ds\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{k}\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)\hat{v}(\eta)(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}d\eta+4^{k}\int_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|\hat{u}(s)\hat{v}(\xi-s)(1+|\xi-s|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{k}\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{v}(\eta)|\left\|\hat{u}(\xi-\eta)(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}d\eta+4^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(s)|\left\|\hat{v}(\xi-s)(1+|\xi-s|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{k}\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{v}(\eta)|a\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}d\eta+4^{k}\int_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(s)|a\left\|v\right\|_{W^{k,2}}ds.

In the first integral we transform as follows.

(5)

\displaystyle 4^{k}\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{v}(\eta)|a\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}d\eta

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{k}a\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\int_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{v}(\eta)|(1+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}(1+|\eta|^{2})^{-\frac{k}{2}}d\eta\leqslant

(6)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{k}a^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\left\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

We make the same with the second integral and obtain

\displaystyle\left\|uv\right\|_{W^{k,2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4^{k}a^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\left\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+4^{k}a^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\left\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\Big(2^{2k+1}a^{2}\left\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\left\|u\right\|_{W^{k,2}}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{k,2}}.

Lemma is proved.

Step 3 : For
I
a closed interval one defines the norm on the space
\mathcal{C}^{k}(I)
by

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}(I)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\sup_{x\in I}|f^{(j)}(x)}|.

Remark that:

•

The norm
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(I)}=\sup_{x\in I}|f(x)|
correspond to the usual sup-norm.

•

The space
\mathcal{C}^{k}(I)
endowed with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}(I)}
is a Banach space.

Recall the approximation theorem of Weirstrass:

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(I)
. Then for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a polynomial
P
such that for all
x\in[a,b]
, we have
|f(x)-P(x)|<\epsilon
, i.e.
\left\|f-P\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(I)}<\epsilon
.

From that statement, one can deduce an approximation with respect to the norm
\mathcal{C}^{k}
:

Proposition 1.2
.

Any function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
of class
\mathcal{C}^{k}
defined on a closed interval

can be uniformly approximated by polynomials with respect to the norm
\mathcal{C}^{k}

\forall\epsilon>0,\ \ \exists\ P\in\mathbb{R}[X],\ \text{such that}\ \left\|f-P\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}(I)}<\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
I=[a,b]
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}

Since
f

\mathcal{C}^{k}

\forall\ l\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\in[\![1,k]\!],\ P_{k-l}(x)=\int_{a}^{x}P_{k-l+1}(t)dt+f^{(k-l)}(a).

We can prove by induction on

(7)

\forall\ l\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\in[\![0,k]\!],\ \forall x\in[a,b],\ \left|P_{k-l}(x)-f^{(k-l)}(x)\right|\leqslant\epsilon\frac{(x-a)^{l}}{l!}.

Indeed for

the assertion (
7

\displaystyle|P_{k-l}(x)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle f^{(k-l)}(x)|=\left|\int_{a}^{x}P_{k-l+1}(t)dt+f^{(k-l)}(a)-f^{(k-l)}(x)\right|=

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{a}^{x}\Big(P_{k-l+1}(t)-f^{(k-l+1)}(t)\Big)dt\right|\leqslant\int_{a}^{x}\left|P_{k-l+1}(t)-f^{(k-l+1)}(t)\right|dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{x}\epsilon\frac{(t-a)^{l-1}}{(l-1)!}dt\leqslant\epsilon\frac{(x-a)^{l}}{l!}.

Proposition is proved.

2.
Complex structure on the space of Sobolev maps

From now on let
S
be a compact connected n-dimensional real manifold with boundary. Let
X
be a finite dimensional complex manifold. Our goal in this section to equip the Sobolev space
W^{k,2}(S,X)
with a natural structure of a
complex Hilbert manifold.

For a set
U\subset S\times X
and
s\in S
we write

U^{s}:=\{x\in X\ |\ (s,x)\in U\}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\epsilon^{s}&:&U^{s}&\to&U\\
&&x&\mapsto&(s,x).\\
\end{array}

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
g\in W^{k,2}(S,X)
with
k>\frac{n}{2}
, there exists a
W^{k,2}
-diffeomorphism
G
between a neighborhood
U\subset S\times X
of the graph
\{(s,g(s))\ |s\in S\}
of
g
and a neighborhood of the zero section in
g^{*}TX
such that

i)

\{G(s,g(s))\ |\ s\in S\}
is the zero section of
g^{*}TX
;

ii)

G^{s}=G\circ\epsilon^{s}
maps
U^{s}
biholomorphically on a neighborhood of
0\in T_{g(s)}X
;

iii)

dG^{s}_{g(s)}
is the identity map.

Proof.

i)
We recall the argument from
[
L
]
pointing out the smoothness of
G
. Let
(\Omega_{j},\phi_{j})
be an atlas of the complex manifold
X
. Then the sets
S_{j}=g^{-1}(\Omega_{j})\subset S
form an open covering of
S
. Consider
U_{j}\subset S_{j}\times\Omega_{j}
a neighborhood of the graph of
g|_{S_{j}}
. We can construct locally the diffeomorphism
G_{j}
by

\begin{array}[]{cccc}G_{j}:&U_{j}&\longrightarrow&g^{*}TX\\
&(s,x)&\mapsto&\Big(s,(d\phi_{j}^{-1})_{\phi_{j}(g(s))}\big[\phi_{j}(x)-\phi_{j}(g(s))\big]\Big).\end{array}

Notice that
G_{j}
is of class
W^{k,2}
because such is
g
.
It remains to glue all the
G_{j}
. Take
\{\eta_{j}\}
a
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-partition of unity subordinated to the covering
\{S_{j}\}
and define

G(s,x)=\sum_{j}\eta_{j}(s)G_{j}(s,x)
. Choose then the restriction of
G
to a suitable
U\subset\bigcup_{j}U_{j}
. Remark that
G(s,g(s))=(s,0)
.

ii) We have that
G_{j}^{s}(x)=G_{j}\circ\epsilon^{s}(x)=(d\phi_{j}^{-1})_{\phi_{j}(g(s))}\big[\phi_{j}(x)-\phi_{j}(g(s))\big]
. The map
G_{j}^{s}:U_{g}^{s}\to g^{*}TX
is holomorphic for every
s\in S_{j}
since
x\mapsto(d\phi_{j}^{-1})_{\phi_{j}(g(s))}\big[\phi_{j}(x)-\phi_{j}(g(s))\big]
is holomorphic. Here
U_{g}^{s}=\{x\in X\ |\ (s,x)\in U_{g}\}
. Holomorpicity of
G^{s}(x)=\sum_{j}\eta_{j}(s)G^{s}_{j}(x)
follows.

iii) We compute the differential of
G^{s}
and obtain

dG^{s}_{g(s)}=(d\phi_{j}^{-1})_{\phi_{j}(g(s))}(d\phi_{j})_{g(s)}=\text{Id}

∎

For
g\in W^{k,2}(S,X)
choose
U_{g}
and
G
as in the previous lemma. Those
h\in W^{k,2}(S,X)
whose graph
\Gamma_{h}:=\{(s,h(s))\ |\ s\in S\}

is contained in
U_{g}
form a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{g}\subset W^{k,2}(S,X)
of
g
:

\mathcal{U}_{g}=\left\{h\in W^{k,2}(S,X)\ |\ \Gamma_{h}\subset U_{g}\right\}.

For
h\in\mathcal{U}_{g}
define the section
\psi_{g}(h)=G(\cdot,h(\cdot))\in W^{k,2}(S,g^{*}TX)
. Thus
\psi_{g}
is a homeomorphism between
\mathcal{U}_{g}
and an open neighborhood of zero section in
W^{k,2}(S,g^{*}TX)
. We can define the chart
(\mathcal{U}_{g},\psi_{g})
where local coordinates are in a complex Hilbert space
W^{k,2}(S,g^{*}TX)
.
Now we need to verify that transition maps are holomorphic.

Let
h\in W^{k,2}(S,X)
be such that
\Gamma_{h}\subset U_{g}\cap U_{g^{\prime}}
, i.e.
h\in\mathcal{U}_{g}\cap\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}
. For
s\in S
we have

\psi_{g^{\prime}}(h)(s)=\left(\psi_{g^{\prime}}\circ\psi_{g}^{-1}\right)[\psi_{g}(h)(s)]=\left[{G^{\prime}}^{s}\circ{G^{s}}^{-1}\right][\psi_{g}(h)(s)]=\left[G^{\prime}(s,\cdot)\circ G(s,\cdot)^{-1}\right][\psi_{g}(h)(s)]

Due to item
ii)
of the Lemma just proved we have that
{G^{\prime}}^{s}\circ{G^{s}}^{-1}
is a biholomorphism between an appropriate open subsets of
T_{g(s)}X
and
T_{g^{\prime}(s)}X
. Therefore the value
\psi_{g^{\prime}}(h)(s)
depends holomorphically on
\psi_{g}(h)(s)
.

In more details we have a
W^{k,2}
-regular maps
P=G^{\prime}\circ G^{-1}
between open sets
V\subset g^{*}TX
and
V^{\prime}\subset{g^{\prime}}^{*}TX
such that for every
s\in S

P(s,v)
holomorphically depends on
v\in V\cap T_{g(s)}X
where
P(s,v)=(s,P^{s}(v))
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the open set of sections of
g^{*}TX
which are contained in
V
. The same for
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. We obtain a mapping
\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
defined as
\mathcal{P}(h)(s)=P(s,P^{s}(h(s)))
, where
h
is a
W^{k,2}
-section of
g^{*}TX
contained in
V
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is holomorphic. This easily follows from Gâteaux differentiability and continuity of
\mathcal{P}
.
Thus
W^{k,2}(S,X)
has the structure of a complex Hilbert manifold.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
D
and
X
be finite dimensional complex manifolds and let
S
be
an
n
-dimensional compact real manifold with boundary. A mapping

\Rightarrow

Given

we construct

we define

.
By the assumtion about

for any

and the map

To prove the holomorphicity of

for a fixed take any

in a neighborhood of some

take any chart

which conains the graph of

. This

.
Therefore

.

Conversely given

and

as follows. For

we define

by

. By

∎

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
q
-Hilbert-Hartogs manifold. Then every holomorphic map

F:W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))\to\mathcal{X}

extends to a holomorphic map
\tilde{F}:W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})\to\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

The proof will be achieved in a number of steps. First we shall construct some “natural extension”
of
F
. After that we shall prove that the extension is continuous and finally that it is holomorphic.

Step 1. Natural extension.
Let
f=(f^{q},f^{n}):S\to\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n}

be an element of
W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})
.
Here
f^{q}
and
f^{n}
are componnents of
f
.
We want to extend
F
to
f
. This will be done
along an appropriate analytic disc which passes through
f
. Consider the mapping

\phi_{f}:\bar{\Delta}^{q}\times S\to\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n}
defined as

(18)

\phi_{f}:(z,s)\to\left(h_{f^{q}(s)}(z),f^{n}(s)\right).

Here
h_{a}
is the following automorphism of
\Delta^{q}
interchanging
a=(a_{1},...,a_{q})
and
0
:

h_{a}(z)=\left(\frac{a_{1}-z_{1}}{1-\bar{a}_{1}z_{1}},...,\frac{a_{q}-z_{q}}{1-\bar{a}_{q}z_{q}}\right).

Notice that due to the compactness of
f^{q}(S)\subset\Delta^{q}
authomorphisms

h_{f^{q}(s)}
are defined for
z
in a fixed (independant on
s
) neighborhood of
\bar{\Delta}^{q}
,
and therefore such is
\phi_{f}
. Denote by
\phi_{f*}:\bar{\Delta}^{q}\to W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q+n})
the
analytic
q
-disk in
W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})
represented by
\phi_{f}
,
i.e

\phi_{f*}(z)\in W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q+1})
acts as follows

(19)

\phi_{f*}(z):s\to\left(\frac{f_{1}^{q}(s)-z_{1}}{1-\bar{f}_{1}^{q}(s)z_{1}},...,\frac{f_{q}^{q}(s)-z_{q}}{1-\bar{f}_{q}^{q}(s)z_{q}},f^{n}(s)\right).

Here
f^{q}(s)=(f_{1}^{q}(s),...,f_{q}^{q}(s))
is the
q
-component of
f
. Denote by
\Phi_{f}=\phi_{f*}(\bar{\Delta}^{q})
the image of
\phi_{f*}
. As in lemma
3.1
we mark here and everywhere with
*
analytic disks

D\to W^{k,2}(S,X)
represented by maps
D\times S\to X
and by capital letters we mark the images
of these discs. Our
\phi_{f}
possesses the following properties:

i)

\phi_{f*}(0)=f
,
i.e.
is our loop
f
.

ii)

For
z\in\partial\Delta^{q}
one has that
\phi_{f*}(z)(S)\subset A^{q}_{1-r,1}\times\Delta^{n}
, therefore

\partial\Phi_{f}{:=}\phi_{f*}(\partial\Delta^{q})\subset W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r)).

Indeed, for
z
close to
{\partial}\Delta^{q}
some
z_{j}
is close to
{\partial}\Delta
and then the
j
-component of
h_{f^{q}(s)}(z)

also is close to
{\partial}\Delta
for all
s\in S
as required.

Remark that due to the second item above
our map
F
is defined and holomorphic near the boundary of the analytic
q
-disc
\Phi_{f}
. In order
to assign to
f
some “natural” value
\tilde{F}(f)
, which we shall call a “natural extension”
of
F
, we shall extend
F
holomorphically to the
q
-disc
\Phi_{f}\ni f
.

Consider the analytic
(q+1)
-disk
\psi_{*}
in
W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})

represented by

(20)

\psi^{t}(z,s){:=}\psi(z,t,s){:=}\left(h_{f^{q}(s)}(z),tf^{n}(s)\right),\quad z\in\bar{\Delta}^{q},\quad|t|\leq 1+\delta,\quad s\in S

for an appropriate
\delta>0
small enough. Here we use the compactness of
f^{n}(S)
.
Remark that

iii)

\psi^{0}(\cdot,\cdot)
takes its values in
\Delta^{q}\times\{0\}\subset H_{q}^{n}(r)
and therefore for
|t|
small
\psi^{t}(.,.)
takes its values in
H_{q}^{n}(r)
by continuity.

iv)

\psi^{1}=\phi_{f}
.

v)

For all
t\in\bar{\Delta}_{1+\delta}
one has that
{\partial}\Psi^{t}=\Psi_{*}^{t}(\partial\Delta^{q})\subset W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))
by the second item as before.

Here
\Psi
and
\Psi^{t}
stand for the images of
\psi_{*}
and
\psi^{t}_{*}
respectively, i.e. for every t we see
\Psi_{*}^{t}
as an analytic
q
-disk, while
\Psi_{*}
as an analytic
(q+1)
-disk.
Therefore for
\delta>0
small enough the Hartogs figure

(21)

H_{q}^{1}(\delta){:=}\{(z,t):||z||<1+\delta,|t|<\delta\text{ or }1-\delta<||z||<1+\delta,|t|<1+\delta\}

is mapped by
\psi_{*}
to
W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))
and consequently the composition
F\circ\psi_{*}:H_{q}^{1}(\delta)\to\mathcal{X}
is well defined and holomorphic. Due to the assumed
q
-Hartogsness of

\mathcal{X}
this composition
F\circ\psi_{*}
holomorphically extends to the associated polydisc

\Delta^{q+1}_{1+\delta}
. In particular it extends to
\Delta^{q}\times\{1\}
,
i.e.

F
holomorphically
extends onto the
q
-disc
\Phi_{f}\ni f
.

(22)

\text{\it Denote by }\quad\widetilde{F\circ\psi_{*}}\quad\text{\it this extension and set }\quad\tilde{F}(f){:=}(\widetilde{F\circ\psi_{*}})(0,1).

Remark that
\tilde{F}
is indeed an extension of
F
,
i.e.
that
\tilde{F}(f)=F(f)

for
f\in W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))
because
\tilde{F}
is a holomorphic extension of
F
from

\Psi\cap W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))
. We call
\tilde{F}
the “natural extension” of
F
.

Step 2.

The natural extension is continuous.
Let
f^{\prime}\in W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})
be close to
f
. Construct
q
-disc
\phi_{f^{\prime}*}
and
(q+1)
-disc

\psi^{\prime}_{*}
for
f^{\prime}
as we did for
f
. Denote by
\Gamma_{\Psi}
and

\Gamma_{\Psi^{\prime}}
the graphs of
\widetilde{F\circ\psi_{*}}
and
\widetilde{F\circ\psi^{\prime}_{*}}
in

\Delta^{q+1}_{1+\delta}\times\mathcal{X}
correspondingly. Due to Lemma
3.1
all we need to prove is that

\Gamma_{\Psi^{\prime}}
enters to a given neighborhood of
\Gamma_{\Psi}
provided
f^{\prime}
is sufficently close
to
f
.
Due to Theorem
3.2
one can choose a 1-complete neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
\Gamma_{\Psi}
. Moreover, for an appropriate
\delta>0
the graph
\Gamma_{\Psi^{\prime}}
over the Hartogs
figure
H_{q}^{1}(\delta)
as in (
21
) enters to the neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of the graph

\Gamma_{\Psi}
because
\psi^{\prime}
is close to
\psi
and takes it values in
W^{k,2}(S,H^{n}_{q}(r))
when restricted to
H_{q}^{1}(\delta)
. A priori there is no reason that all the graph enters in
\mathcal{V}
. But the maximum principle applied to the plurisubharmonic exhausting function
of
\mathcal{V}
all the graph
\Gamma_{\Psi^{\prime}}
enters in the neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
. The lemma
3.1
permits us to obtain the continuity of the map and the step is proved.

Step 3.

Holomorphicity.
All what is left to prove is that our
natural extension
\tilde{F}
is Gâteaux holomorphic. Fix
f\in W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{n})
,
g\in W^{k,2}(S,\mathbb{C}^{q+n})
and consider the complex affine line
L{:=}\{f+\lambda g:\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}\subset W^{k,2}(S,\mathbb{C}^{q+1+n})
. We need to prove that
\tilde{F}|_{L}
is holomorphic
in a neighborhood of zero. Fix an
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small and consider an analytic
(q+2)
-disc

\theta_{*}:\bar{\Delta}^{q}\times\bar{\Delta}_{\epsilon}^{2}\to W^{k,2}(S,\mathbb{C}^{q+n})

represented by the mapping :

(23)

\theta:(z,\lambda,\mu,s)\to\left(\frac{f_{1}^{q}(s)+\lambda g_{1}^{q}(s)-z_{1}}{1-(\bar{f}_{1}^{q}(s)+\mu\bar{g}_{1}^{q}(s))z_{1}},...,\frac{f_{q}^{q}(s)+\lambda g_{q}^{q}(s)-z_{q}}{1-(\bar{f}_{q}^{q}(s)+\mu\bar{g}_{q}^{q}(s))z_{q}},f^{n}(s)+\lambda g^{n}(s)\right).

Denote by
\Theta=\text{Im}\theta_{*}
its image.
Notice that for
\epsilon>0
small enough the
q
-component of (
23
) defines for every fixed

|\lambda|,|\mu|\leq\epsilon
and
s\in S
a holomorphic imbedding of
\bar{\Delta}^{q}
to a neighborhood of

\bar{\Delta}^{q}
which is uniformly close to the authomorphism

z\to\left(\frac{f_{1}^{q}(s)-z_{1}}{1-\bar{f}_{1}^{q}(s)z_{1}},...,\frac{f_{q}^{q}(s)-z_{q}}{1-\bar{f}_{q}^{q}(s)z_{q}}\right)

of
\Delta^{q}
. Therefore for every
|\lambda|,|\mu|\leq\epsilon
the disk
\theta_{*}
has values in
W^{k,2}(S,\Delta^{q}_{1+r}\times\Delta^{n})
and the arguments of Step 1 can be repeated to

\theta(\cdot,\lambda,\mu,\cdot)
on the place of
\phi_{f}
:

Consider an analytic
(q+3)
-disk
\psi_{*}
in
W^{k,2}(S,\mathbb{C}^{q+n})

represented by

(24)

\psi^{t}(z,\lambda,\mu,s){:=}\psi(z,\lambda,\mu,t,s){:=}\left(\theta^{q}(z,\lambda,\mu,s),t\theta^{n}(z,\lambda,\mu,s)\right),\quad z\in\bar{\Delta}^{q},\quad|t|\leq 1+\delta,\quad s\in S

for an appropriate
\delta>0
small enough. Here
\theta^{q}
and
\theta^{n}
are components of
\theta=(\theta^{q},\theta^{n})
.
Remark that

•

\psi^{0}
takes its values in
\Delta^{q}\times\{0\}\subset H_{q}^{n}(r)
and therefore for
|t|
small
\psi^{t}
takes its values in
H_{q}^{n}(r)
by continuity.

•

\psi^{1}=\theta
.

•

For all
t\in\bar{\Delta}_{1+\delta}
one has that
{\partial}\Psi^{t}\subset W^{k,2}(S,H_{q}^{n}(r))
.

This gives a holomorphic extension
of
F\circ\theta_{*}
from
{\partial}\Delta^{q}\times\Delta^{2}_{\epsilon}
to
\bar{\Delta}^{q}\times\Delta^{2}_{\epsilon}
.
We denote this extension as
\hat{F}
. Notice now the following properties of
\theta
and
\hat{F}
:

i)

\theta(\cdot,\lambda,\bar{\lambda},\cdot)=\phi_{f+\lambda g}(\cdot,\cdot)
;

ii)

\theta(0,\lambda,\mu,\cdot)=f(\cdot)+\lambda g(\cdot)
, in particular, this
doesn’t depend on
\mu
.

Properties
iv)
and
v)
imply that

\tilde{F}(f+\lambda g)=\left(\sf{ext}F|_{\phi_{f+\lambda g}}\right)|_{z=0}=\left(\sf{ext}F|_{\theta(\cdot,\lambda,\bar{\lambda},\cdot)}\right)|_{z=0}=\left(\sf{ext}F|_{\theta(\cdot,\lambda,\mu,\cdot)}\right)|_{\mu=0,z=0}=

=\hat{F}|_{\mu=0,z=0}=\hat{F}(0,\lambda,0).

Here by
\sf{ext}F|_{\phi_{f+\lambda g}}
we denote the extension of
F

along the
q
-disc
\phi_{f+\lambda g}
(and then taking the value of this extension
at
z=0
). This was the definition of the natural extension. Therefore
the first equality is justified. As for second it is justified by (
i)
.
The third equality folows from (
ii)
since at
z=0
nothing depends on
\mu
and

\mu=\bar{\lambda}
can be replaced by
\mu=0
. But this is the extension
\hat{F}

evaluated at
z=0,\lambda,\mu=0
and the latter holomorphically depends on
\lambda
.
Therefore such is the left hand side
\tilde{F}(f+\lambda g)
. The holomorphicity
of
\tilde{F}
is proved.

Stochastic maximum principle for weighted mean-field system
Yanyan Tang and Jie Xiong

Abstract




Abstract.

We study the optimal control problem for a weighted mean-field system. A new feature of the control problem is that the coefficients depend on the state process as well as its weighted measure and the control variable. By applying variational technique, we establish a stochastic maximum principle. As an application, we investigate the optimal premium policy of an insurance firm for asset–liability management problem.

Keyword.

McKean-Vlasov equation, stochastic maximum principle.

AMS subject classifications.

60H10, 93E20, 93E03, 60H30

Introduction

Optimal control problems have been studied extensively since the pioneering work of Pontryagin
[
19
]
, where a maximum principle is obtained by using spike variation. Kushner (
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
) investigated the applicability of the maximum principle to the design of controllers for stochastic system, i.e. stochastic maximum principle (SMP) for optimal control. He showed that for system with additive white noise a maximum principle of the form developed by Pontryagin is valid when diffusion coeffcient does not depend on the control variable. When the diffusion coefficient contains a control variable, Bensoussan (
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
) studied such a case. The maximum principle he obtained are local conditions, and his method depends heavily on the control domain being convex. Peng
[
18
]
broke through this difficulty in 1990. In that paper, first and second order variational inequalities are introduced, when the control domain need not to be convex, and the diffusion coefficient contains the control variable.

In recent years, stochastic optimal control problems for the mean-field stochastic differential equations (SDEs) have attracted an increasing attention. The mean-field SDEs can trace their roots to the McKean–Vlasov model, which was first introduced by Kac
[
13
]
and McKean
[
17
]
to study physical systems with a large number of interacting particles. Lasry and Lions
[
5
]
extended the applications of the mean-field models to economics and finance. It is not until Buckdahn et al
[
4
]
and Buckdahn et al
[
5
]
established the theory of the mean-field BSDEs that the SMP for the optimal control system of mean-field type has become a popular topic. For example, Li
[
15
]
studied SMP for mean-field controls, with the domain of the control convex; Buckdahn et al
[
6
]
obtained the related SMP for a class of general stochastic control problem with McKean-Vlasov dynamics, in which the coefficients depend nonlinearly on both the state process as well as its distribution; Acciaio et al.
[
1
]
studied mean-field stochastic control problems where the cost functional and the state dynamics depend on the joint distribution of the controlled state and the control process. Strongly inspired by Buckdahn et al
[
6
]
, Buckdahn et al
[
7
]
investigated a generalized mean-field SMP for the optimal control problem where the coefficients not only depend on the distribution of
(X,v)
but also with partial information. Interested readers may refer to Shen and Siu
[
20
]
, Djehiche et al
[
8
]
, Guo and Xiong
[
9
]
, Zhang et al
[
23
]
, Lakhdari et al
[
16
]
, Zhang
[
22
]
and Wang and Wu
[
21
]
for various versions of the SMPs for the mean-field models.

As we mentioned above, one of the motivations of the study of mean-field SDE is from mathematical finance. The state process is usually the valuation of a certain asset, such as the stock price, by a typical investor which is affected by this investor as well as the others who are interested in this asset, in a collective manner. So far, this collective interaction is represented by the average measure of the whole system. By a propagation of chaos limit, it is described by the distribution of the state process. However, in the real-world situations, the opinion of the investors about the asset should be weighted accord to their wealth levels. This motivates our study of optimal control with weighted mean-field interaction.

In this paper, we are intersted in the SMP for a stochastic control problem with McKean-Vlasov dynamics:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}dX_{t}&=&b(X_{t},\mu^{X,A}_{t},u_{t})dt+{\sigma}(X_{t},\mu^{X,A}_{t},u_{t})dW_{t}\\
dA_{t}&=&A_{t}\left({\alpha}(X_{t},\mu^{X,A}_{t},u_{t})dt+{\beta}(X_{t},\mu^{X,A}_{t},u_{t})dW_{t}\right)\ \ \ t\in[0,T]\\
X_{0}&=&x,\ A_{0}=a,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
W_{t}
is an
m
-dimension Browanion motion defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},{P})
. In the above,
(b,{\sigma}):\ {R}^{d}\times\mathcal{M}_{F}({R}^{d})\times U\rightarrow{R}^{d}\times{R}^{d\times m}
and

({\alpha},{\beta}):\ {R}^{d}\times\mathcal{M}_{F}({R}^{d})\times U\rightarrow{R}\times{R}^{1\times m}
are continous mappings,
u_{\cdot}\in L_{{F}}^{2}([0,T];U)
is a control process,
U\subset{R}^{k}
is a convex domain for the control, and
\mu^{X,A}_{t}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}({R}^{d})
is the weighted measure given by

\left<\mu^{X,A}_{t},f\right>={E}\left(A_{t}f(X_{t})\right),\qquad\forall f\in C_{b}({R}^{d}).

(1.2)

The equation of the form (
1.1
) without the control has been studied by Kurtz and Xiong
[
12
]
as the limit of a system of interacting weighted particles.
The main difficulty in obtaining the uniqueness of the solution to this equation as well as the interacting system related to it is the non-Lipschitz property of the coefficients such as
(a,x,\mu)\mapsto a{\alpha}(x,\mu,u)
because
a
is unbounded. A stopping time technique was used in
[
12
]
. However, for stochastic control problem, the adjoint equation will be a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE), and hence, the stopping argument will not be convenient. In this article, we overcome this difficulty by estimating
X_{t}
and
A_{t}
using suitably chosen different norms.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: In the next section, we formulate the problem and present the stochastic maximum principle for our stochastic control problem. In Section 3, as an application, we give an example about the optimal premium policy for an insurance firm for asset–liability management problem.
In Sections 4 and 5, we give detailed proofs about the existence and uniqueness for the solution of the weighted mean-field system and about the
stochastic maximum principle, respectively.

2
Stochastic Maximum Principle

We recall that we cosider the stochastic control problem with the state equation (
1.1
). Let
{{\mathcal{U}}}[0,T]
be the collection of all
U
-valued
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-adapted processes
u_{t}
satisfying

{E}\int_{0}^{T}|u_{t}|^{2}dt<\infty.

{{\mathcal{U}}}[0,T]
is called the set of admissible controls. In order not to over complicate the already notational heavy presentation
of this paper, in what follows we shall assume all processes are 1-dimensional (i.e.,
d=k=m=1
). We should note that the higher dimensional cases can be argued along the same lines without substantial difficulties, except for even heavier notations.

We will take the cost function
J(u)
as

J(u)={E}\left(\int^{T}_{0}f(X_{t},A_{t},\mu^{X,A}_{t},u_{t})dt+\Phi(X_{T},A_{T})\right),

(2.1)

where
f:{R}\times{R}\times\mathcal{M}_{F}({R})\times{U}\rightarrow{R}
,
\Phi:{R}\times{R}\rightarrow{R}.
Here we have dropped
the superscript
u
of
X,A
for simplicity of notation.

Our goal is to find a control to minimize the cost functional over
\mathcal{U}
, namely, an admissible control
u\in\mathcal{U}
is said to the optimal if

J(u)=\min_{v\in\mathcal{U}}J(v).

(2.2)

In this section, we consider the necessary condition for the optimal control of the problem (
2.2
), also know as stochastic maximum principle, with the convexity assumption on the control set
{U}
.

For weighted mean-field type SDEs and BSDEs, we have to introduce some notations. Let
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathscr{F}^{\prime},{P}^{\prime})
be
a copy of the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},{P})
and
{E}^{\prime}(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(\cdot)d{P}^{\prime}
,
\xi^{\prime}
is a random variable defined on
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathscr{F}^{\prime},{P}^{\prime})
. For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{F}({R}),
the Wasserstein metric is defined by

\rho(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\sup\{|\left<\mu_{1},f\right>-\left<\mu_{2},f\right>|:f\in{B}_{1}\},

where
{B}_{1}=\{f:|f(x)-f(y)|\leq|x-y|,|f(x)|\leq 1,\forall x,y\in{R}\}
,
\left<\mu,f\right>
stands for the integral of the function
f
with respect to the measure
\mu
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
f:\mathcal{M}_{F}({R})\to{R}
. We say that
f\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{F}({R}))
if there exists
h(\mu;\cdot)\in C_{b}({R})
such that

f(\mu+{\epsilon}\nu)-f(\mu)=\left<\nu,h(\mu;\cdot)\right>{\epsilon}+o({\epsilon}).

We denote
h(\mu;x)=f_{\mu}(\mu;x)
.

Next, we make the following standard assumptions.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

The conefficients
b,\sigma,{\alpha},{\beta},f,\Phi
are measurable in all variables. Furthermore

(1)

b,\sigma,f\in C^{1}({R}\times\mathcal{M}_{F}({R})\times U)
with bounded partial derivatives;

(2)

{\alpha},{\beta}\in C^{1}({R}\times\mathcal{M}_{F}({R})\times U)
are bounded and with bounded partial derivatives;

(3)

\varphi_{\mu}(x,\mu,u,x^{\prime})
is differentable in
x^{\prime}
with bounded derivative for
\varphi=b,\sigma,{\alpha},{\beta}
. We denote the partial derivative of
\varphi_{\mu}(x,\mu,u;x^{\prime})
with respect to
x^{\prime}
by
\varphi_{\mu,1}(x,\mu,u;x^{\prime})
;

(4)

\Phi(x,a)
has bounded partial derivatives in
x,a
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
u\in\mathcal{U}
being fixed, the weighted mean-field SDE (
1.1
) has a unique solution.

The main result of this paper is to prove the following SMP. More precisely, we define Hamiltonnian

\displaystyle H(X,A,\mu,u,P,Q,p,q)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A\left(p{\alpha}(X,\mu,u)+q{\beta}(X,\mu,u)\right)

\displaystyle+Pb(X,\mu,u)+Q{\sigma}(X,\mu,u)-f(X,A,\mu,u).

The adjoint processes
(p_{t},q_{t}),(P_{t},Q_{t})
are governed by the following equations

\left\{\begin{split}\begin{aligned} dp_{t}=&-\big\{p_{t}{\alpha}({\theta}_{t})+q_{t}{\beta}({\theta}_{t})-f_{a}({\kappa}_{t})\big\}dt&\\
&-\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big\{\left(P_{t}^{\prime}b_{\mu}+Q_{t}^{\prime}{\sigma}_{\mu}+A_{t}^{\prime}(p_{t}^{\prime}{\alpha}_{\mu}+q_{t}^{\prime}{\beta}_{\mu})\right)({\theta}_{t}^{\prime};X_{t})-f_{\mu}({\kappa}_{t}^{\prime};X_{t})\big\}dt+q_{t}dW_{t},&\\
p_{T}=&-\Phi_{a}(X_{T},A_{T}),&\end{aligned}\end{split}\right.

(2.3)

and

\left\{\begin{split}\begin{aligned} dP_{t}=&-\big\{P_{t}b_{x}({\theta}_{t})+Q_{t}{\sigma}_{x}({\theta}_{t})+A_{t}(p_{t}{\alpha}_{x}({\theta}_{t})+q_{t}{\beta}_{x}({\theta}_{t}))-f_{x}({\kappa}_{t})\big\}dt&\\
&-\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\big\{A_{t}\left(\big(P_{t}^{\prime}b_{\mu 1}+Q_{t}^{\prime}{\sigma}_{\mu 1}+A_{t}^{\prime}(p_{t}^{\prime}{\alpha}_{\mu 1}+q_{t}^{\prime}{\beta}_{\mu 1})\right)({\theta}_{t}^{\prime};X_{t})-f_{\mu 1}({\kappa}_{t}^{\prime};X_{t})\big)\big\}dt&\\
&+Q_{t}dW_{t},&\\
P_{T}=&-\Phi_{x}(X_{T},A_{T}),&\end{aligned}\end{split}\right.

(2.4)

where
{\kappa}_{t}=(X_{t},A_{t},\mu_{t},u_{t}),{\theta}_{t}=(X_{t},\mu_{t},u_{t})
,
\varphi_{x}
stands for the partial derivative of
\varphi
with respect to
x
.
The same convention also apply to variables
a
and
u
. Note that the subscript
t
, such as
X_{t}
, stands for the time parameter of a stochastic process.

We are now ready to state the main theorem of the paper.

Theorem 2.2
.

(Stochastic Maximum Principle) Suppose that Hypothesis
2.1
holds. Let
(X_{t},u_{t})
be an optional solution of control problem (
2.2
). Then there are two pairs of
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-adapted processes
(p_{t},q_{t})
and
(P_{t},Q_{t})
there satisfy the equations (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) such that

H_{u}(X_{t},A_{t},\mu_{t},u_{t},P_{t},Q_{t},p_{t},q_{t})=0.

3
An asset liability management problem

As an application of Theorem
2.2
, in this section, we consider the optimal premium policy of an insurance firm for asset
liability management problem.

Let
u
be the premium strategy of the firm;
X_{t}
be the cash flow;
l_{t}\equiv l_{t}^{X,A,u}
be the liability process. Suppose that
a_{t},b_{t},c_{t}
are deterministic and uniformly bounded on
[0,T]
. Suppose that the liability process
l_{t}
satisfies

dl_{t}=-\big(a_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})+b_{t}u_{t}\big)dt-c_{t}dW_{t},

where
c_{t}
denotes the volatility rate, and
A_{t}
is the weighted process satisfying

dA_{t}=A_{t}{\alpha}_{t}dt+A_{t}{\beta}_{t}dW_{t}

(3.1)

with initial value
a_{0}
and
{\alpha}_{t},{\beta}_{t}
are deterministic and bounded on
[0,T]
. Suppose that the firm has no liability at time 0, and only invests in a money account due to certain market regulations. Accordingly, the insurance firm only invests in a money account with compounded interest rate
\rho_{t}
, and hence its cash-balance process
X_{t}
is

X_{t}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{s}ds}\left(X_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{r}dr}dl_{t}\right),\ \ \ X_{0}=x_{0}

where
x_{0}\geq 0
represents the initial reserve. According to It
\hat{o}
’s formula, we see that the cash flow statisfies a mean-field SDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}dX_{t}&=&\big(\rho_{t}X_{t}+a_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})+b_{t}u_{t}\big)dt+c_{t}dW_{t},\\
X_{0}&=&x_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

Let
U\subset{R}
be convex. For any
u\in\mathcal{U}
, equation (
3.2
) admits a unique adapted cash flow
X_{t}
. The insurance firm hopes
to drive its cash-balance process to
c_{0}
in average at the terminal time
T
to meet some regulatory requirement, i.e.
{E}X_{T}=c_{0}
. In order to reconcile the contradiction between the liquidity and profitability of insurance, we introduce a performance functional of the firm

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}{E}\big[\int_{0}^{T}(LX_{t}^{2}+MA_{t}^{2}+Nu_{t}^{2})dt+R(X_{T}-{E}(X_{T}))^{2}\big],

(3.3)

with terminal constraint
{E}(X_{T})=c_{0}
, for some given
c_{0}\in{R}
, where
L,M,N,R
are positive constants.

Then the asset liability management problem is to find an admissible premium policy
u\in\mathcal{U}
such that
J(u)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{U}}J[v].
Applying Lagrange multiplier technique, we define a cost functional for any
\lambda\in{R}
,

J_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{2}{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(LX_{t}^{2}+MA_{t}^{2}+Nu_{t}^{2})dt+R(X_{T}-c_{0})^{2}+2\lambda(X_{T}-c_{0})\right].

(3.4)

For every
\lambda\in{R}
, we find
u^{\lambda}
to minimize
J_{\lambda}(u)
. In this situation, by Throrem
2.2
we write the Hamiltonian function

\displaystyle H(X,A,\mu,u,P,Q,p,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A({\alpha}_{t}p+{\beta}_{t}q)+P\left(\rho_{t}X+a_{t}\int x\mu(dx)+b_{t}u\right)+c_{t}Q

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(LX^{2}+MA^{2}+Nu^{2}\big),

and the adjoint equations

\left\{\begin{split}\begin{aligned} dp_{t}=&-\big(p_{t}{\alpha}_{t}+q_{t}{\beta}_{t}-MA_{t}+a_{t}X_{t}\mathbb{E}(P_{t})\big)dt+q_{t}dW_{t},&\\
p_{T}=&0,&\end{aligned}\end{split}\right.

(3.5)

and

\left\{\begin{split}\begin{aligned} dP_{t}=&-\big(\rho_{t}P_{t}-LX_{t}+a_{t}A_{t}\mathbb{E}(P_{t})\big)dt+Q_{t}dW_{t},&\\
P_{T}=&-R(X_{T}-c_{0})-\lambda.&\end{aligned}\end{split}\right.

(3.6)

From Theorem
2.2
, we have

Nu_{t}=b_{t}P_{t}.

(3.7)

Hence, the related feedback control system takes the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}dX_{t}&=&\big(\rho_{t}X_{t}+a_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})+N^{-1}b_{t}^{2}P_{t}\big)dt+c_{t}dW_{t}\\
dA_{t}&=&A_{t}\left({\alpha}_{t}dt+{\beta}_{t}dW_{t}\right)\\
dP_{t}&=&-\big(\rho_{t}P_{t}-LX_{t}+a_{t}A_{t}\mathbb{E}(P_{t})\big)dt+Q_{t}dW_{t},\\
X_{0}&=&x_{0},\ A_{0}=a,\ P_{T}=-R(X_{T}-c_{0})-\lambda,\ \ \ t\in[0,T].\\
\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

Here we have to deal with a fully coupled mean-field forward–backward SDE. In order to solve this equation we set

P_{t}=\varphi_{t}X_{t}+\varphi_{t}{E}X_{t}+\chi_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})+\psi_{t},

(3.9)

where
\varphi
is a deterministic differentiable function with terminal
-R
,
\varphi_{t},\chi_{t}
, and
\psi_{t}
are stochastic processes. We write

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}d\varphi_{t}&=&\Gamma_{t}dt+\Lambda_{t}dW_{t},\\
d\chi_{t}&=&\Pi_{t}dt+\Theta_{t}dW_{t}.\\
d\psi_{t}&=&\Upsilon_{t}dt+\Sigma_{t}dW_{t}.\\
\varphi_{T}&=&\chi_{T}=0,\ \ \ \psi_{T}=Rc_{0}-\lambda.\end{array}\right.

(3.10)

Differentiating on
P_{t}
in (
3.9
) and comparing with (
3.6
), we get

\left\{\begin{split}\begin{aligned} &-\rho_{t}P_{t}+LX_{t}-a_{t}A_{t}{E}(P_{t})\\
&=\varphi_{t}\rho_{t}X_{t}+\varphi_{t}a_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})+\varphi_{t}b_{t}u_{t}\\
&+\varphi_{t}\rho_{t}{E}(X_{t})+\varphi_{t}a_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})+\varphi_{t}b_{t}{E}(u_{t})\\
&+\chi_{t}(\rho_{t}+{\alpha}_{t}+a_{t}{E}(A_{t})){E}(A_{t}X_{t})+\chi_{t}b_{t}{E}(A_{t}u_{t})+{\beta}_{t}c_{t}\chi_{t}{E}(A_{t})\\
&+\Upsilon_{t}+X_{t}\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{t}+\Gamma_{t}{E}(X_{t})+\Pi_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})\\
&Q_{t}=\varphi_{t}c_{t}+\Sigma_{t}+\Lambda_{t}{E}(X_{t})+\Theta_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t}).\end{aligned}\end{split}\right.

(3.11)

From (
3.7
) and by comparing the coefficients of
X_{t},{E}[X_{t}],{E}[A_{t}X_{t}],
in the first equation of (
3.11
), we obtain

\left\{\begin{split}\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{t}+2\rho_{t}\varphi_{t}+N^{-1}b_{t}^{2}\varphi_{t}^{2}-L=0,\\
&\varphi_{T}=-R.\end{aligned}\end{split}\right.

(3.12)

and

\left\{\begin{split}\begin{aligned} d\varphi_{t}=-\big(&\big(2\rho_{t}+2N^{-1}b_{t}^{2}\varphi_{t}+N^{-1}b_{t}^{2}{E}(\varphi_{t})\big)\varphi_{t}+a_{t}A_{t}{E}(\varphi_{t})\\
&+N^{-1}b_{t}^{2}{E}(A_{t}\varphi_{t})\chi_{t}+a_{t}\varphi_{t}A_{t}\big)dt+\Lambda_{t}dW_{t},\\

&+({\beta}c_{t}{E}(A_{t})+N^{-1}b_{t}^{2}{E}(A_{t}\psi_{t}))\chi_{t}\big)dt+\Sigma_{t}dW_{t},\\
\varphi_{T}&=0\ \ \ \chi_{T}=0\ \ \ \psi_{T}=Rc_{0}-\lambda.\end{aligned}\end{split}\right.

(3.13)

Hence,

u_{t}=N^{-1}b_{t}\left(\varphi_{t}X_{t}+\varphi_{t}{E}X_{t}+\chi_{t}{E}(A_{t}X_{t})+\psi_{t}\right),

(3.14)

with
\varphi_{t},\varphi_{t},\chi_{t},\psi_{t}
, given by Riccati equation (
3.12
) and the linear BSDE (

).

Note that

implies that

are linear functions of

. We write

{E}(\psi_{t})=\lambda g_{t}^{1}+\widetilde{g}_{t}^{1}\ \ \ and\ \ \ {E}(A_{t}\psi_{t})=\lambda g_{t}^{2}+\widetilde{g}_{t}^{2},

where
g_{t}^{i}
,
\widetilde{g}_{t}^{i}\;(i=1,2)
are determined by equations (

) and (

) explicitly.

To obtain the optimal premium policy, we must identify the value of

. To his end, pluging (

(3.15)

Since

), (

) and (

,

with
\zeta^{1}_{t}=(\xi^{1}_{t},\eta^{1}_{t})^{*}
,

A^{*}
denotes the transpose of the matrix

. Finally we can identify

.

.

4
The proof of the existence and uniqueness

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
2.1
. To this end, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under (
2
) of Hypothesis

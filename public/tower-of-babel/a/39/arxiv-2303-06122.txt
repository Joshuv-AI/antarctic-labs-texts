Abstract




On the fiftieth anniversary of Chen’s Goldbach theorem

Abstract:
In this work and its sister paper [FI3] we give a new proof of the famous Linnik theorem bounding the least prime in an arithmetic progression. Using sieve machinery in both papers, we are able to dispense with the log-free zero density bounds and the repulsion property of exceptional zeros, two deep innovations begun by Linnik and relied on in earlier proofs.

1.
Introduction: A bit of history

The theorem of Yu.V. Linnik [L] asserts that, for integers
q\geqslant 2
,
(a,q)=1
, the least prime
p\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
satisfies

(1.1)

p_{\rm min}(q,a)\ll q^{L},

where
L
and the implied constant are absolute.

The Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
,
\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
yields

(1.2)

p_{\rm min}(q,a)\ll(q\log q)^{2}.

Linnik’s unconditional arguments make use of the Deuring-Heilbronn phenomenon which describes a repulsion effect of the exceptional zero on all of the other zeros. Even if the exceptional zero does not exist, the standard arguments, then and now, do not produce (
1.1
) with an absolute constant
L
. To this end, Linnik established the so-called log-free density bound

(1.3)

\sum_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}\#\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma;L(\rho,\chi)=0,\,\alpha\leqslant\beta<1,|\gamma|\leqslant q\}\ll q^{c(1-\alpha)},

where
c
is an absolute constant.
Moreover, in the presence of the exceptional zero, say
\beta_{1}
, with
\eta_{1}=(1-\beta_{1})\log q
being sufficiently small, Linnik was able to expand the classical zero-free region, with the result that all the other zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
with
|\gamma|\leqslant q
satisfy

(1.4)

(1-\beta)\log q\gg\log 1/\eta_{1}

with an absolute implied constant. This is a quantitative form of the Deuring-Heilbronn phenomenon. Later, Bombieri [B] combined these two crucial estimations of Linnik into a single statement, namely that the number of all zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
with
\alpha\leqslant\beta<1
,
|\gamma|\leqslant q
of all
L(s,\chi)
other than
\beta_{1}
satisfies

(1.5)

\sum_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}N^{*}(\alpha,\chi,q)\leqslant c_{1}(1-\beta_{1})(\log q)q^{c(1-\alpha)},

where the constants
c_{1}
,
c
are absolute. See Théorème 14 of [B] for somewhat stronger statements. Bombieri employs the Turan power sum method, essentially in place of the sieve.

Sieve ideas in conjunction with the mollification of
L
-functions constitute the driving force of Linnik’s work. His arguments have been refined substantially over time with new innovative elements (particularly those of Heath-Brown), giving concrete values of
L
. Here are some remarkable records:

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}L&=&10^{5}&({\rm Pan},1957)\\
L&=&80&({\rm Jutila},1977)\\
L&=&20&({\rm Graham},1981)\\
L&=&13.5&({\rm Chen\,and\,Liu},1989)\\
L&=&5.5&({\rm Heath-Brown},1992)\\
L&=&5.18&({\rm Xylouris},2009)\end{array}

It is interesting that, if permitted the assistance of the exceptional character
\chi_{1}(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
, one can accomplish a lot more for this problem than with the Riemann Hypothesis. For example, we obtained (
1.1
) with
L=2-1/55
under suitable conditions, see [FI1]. If one tries to count primes
p\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
directly using the Riemann Hypothesis, by means of the explicit formula

\displaystyle\psi_{f}(x;q,a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}f(n/x)\Lambda(n)

\displaystyle={\hat{f}}(1)\frac{x}{\varphi(q)}-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\bar{\chi}(a)\sum_{\rho}{\hat{f}}(\rho)x^{\rho}

\displaystyle={\hat{f}}(1)\frac{x}{\varphi(q)}+O(\sqrt{x}\log x)

with
f
smooth and compactly supported on
(0,\infty)
,
then one may get (
1.1
) with
L<2
provided that there is a considerable cancellation in the terms
x^{\rho}
as
\rho
runs over low-lying zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
.
One actually expects that (
1.1
) holds with any
L>1
. The distribution of primes
p\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
in the vicinity of
q
is tricky; cf. [FG].

In these notes we do not attempt to obtain a reasonably small Linnik constant
L
. In fact, at the end of the day we arrive at (
1.1
) with the huge value
L=75,744,000
. So, what motivates us to produce this long proof of Linnik’s bound? Our first reason is to demonstrate how the sieve machinery can succeed in this problem when fueled by neither the log-free density estimation nor the repulsion property of the exceptional zero. However, we do appeal to the classical zero-free region, specifically Lemma A1 with
\eta=1/657
.

Moreover, these notes provide more details to our arguments in Chapter 24 of [FI2]. To diversify a bit we treat the case of the exceptional zero by Selberg’s lower-bound sieve rather than by a combinatorial sieve. Of course, the results are essentially the same, but we get nicer expressions. We have put the treatment of that case into a separate note [FI3].

Our second incentive to produce these notes is the desire to fix a defect in the technical arrangements of the partial sums
\psi_{\mathfrak{X}}(x;q,a)
in [FI2], a correction of which we have no other opportunity to publish due to an AMS copyright issue. The required correction is embarrassing but not difficult. The subdivision there of
n=pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
should have been made over the segments

(1.6)

D^{\frac{1}{6}}<p_{1},p_{2}<D^{\frac{1}{5}},\quad x_{3}<p_{3}\leqslant 2x_{3},\quad x_{4}<p_{4}\leqslant 2x_{4}

rather than over
x_{j}<p_{j}\leqslant 2x_{j}
for all
j=1,2,3,4
. That is, we should not have put
p_{1},p_{2}
into dyadic segments as we did for
p_{3},p_{4}
. However, adjusting the existing arguments to the larger segments (
1.6
) is simple with obvious modifications. The key difference appears in the factor
h(p/x)
of (24.47) in [FI2] which needs to be changed to
h(\log p/\log x)
. In other words, the first primes
p_{1},p_{2}
should have been logarithmically scaled. See how we proceed in Subsection 2.1 of these notes.

2.
The linear beta-sieve

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{n})
be a finite sequence of real numbers
a_{n}\geqslant 0
, supported on
x<n\leqslant 2x
,
x
large. Our goal is to estimate

(2.1)

S(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{p}a_{p}.

It is well understood that, due to the parity barrier, one cannot obtain a positive lower bound for
S(\mathcal{A})
within the basic sieve methods. In this section we reduce the problem for primes to that for products of five primes, that is to the estimation of

(2.2)

S_{5}(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{p}\sum_{p_{1}}\sum_{p_{2}}\sum_{p_{3}}\sum_{p_{4}}a_{pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}},

where the
p_{j}
run over specific segments, short in the logarithmic scale. We do not lose the correct order of magnitude. Note that
n=pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}
, which we call a prime quintet, consists of integers with an odd number of prime factors.

Let
P(z)
denote the product of primes
p<z
in some set
\mathcal{P}
. Sieve methods are capable of producing upper and lower bounds for the sifting function

(2.3)

S(\mathcal{A},z)=\sum_{(n,P(z))=1}a_{n},

but only in a limited range. The basic assumption needed for sifting is that the congruence sums

(2.4)

A_{d}=\sum_{n\equiv 0(\mathop{\rm{mod}}d)}a_{n}

can, on average over
d
, be well-approximated by a simple model
g(d)X
, where
g(d)
is multiplicative with
0\leqslant g(p)<1
and
X
is fixed. This requirement means that the error terms in

(2.5)

A_{d}=g(d)X+r_{d}

are relatively small. In particular, we must choose
X\sim A_{1}
. The probability of seeing
a_{n}
with
(n,P(z))=1
is given by the product

(2.6)

V(z)=\prod_{p|P(z)}\bigl(1-g(p)\bigr)

and sieve methods produce bounds of the true order of magnitude

(2.7)

S(\mathcal{A},z)\asymp XV(z)

provided that we can control the error terms
r_{d}
. In practice, we can do so only for moduli up to some level
y\leqslant x
. We need, say,

(2.8)

R(y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d<y\\
d|P(z)\end{subarray}}|r_{d}|\leqslant XV(z)(\log y)^{-1}.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{d}
be the subsequence of
\mathcal{A}
of elements
a_{n}
with
n\equiv 0(\mathop{\rm{mod}}d)
. We start from the combinatorial identity

(2.9)

S(\mathcal{A},z)=S^{-}(\mathcal{A},z)+S_{2}(\mathcal{A},z)+S_{4}(\mathcal{A},z)+\ldots

which is derived by a process of inclusion-exclusion; see (12.10) and (12.11) for the linear beta-sieve in [FI2]. This holds for
z=\sqrt{y}
with

(2.10)

S^{-}(\mathcal{A},z)=\sum_{d|P(z)}\lambda^{-}_{d}A_{d}

where
\lambda^{-}_{d}
are the sieve weights. We have

(2.11)

S_{4}(\mathcal{A},z)=\mathop{\mathop{\sum\!\sum}\!\mathop{\sum\!\sum}}_{p_{4}<p_{3}<p_{2}<p_{1}}S(\mathcal{A}_{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}},p_{4})

where the prime variables of summation are restricted by two inequalities

(2.12)

p_{1}p_{2}^{3}<y,\quad p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}^{3}\geqslant y.

The other
S_{n}(\mathcal{A},z)
are defined similarly but we are going to drop them due to positivity. Actually, we also discard some parts of (
2.12
). Specifically we take

(2.13)

x^{\frac{1}{6}}<p_{j}<x^{\frac{1}{5}},\quad j=1,2,3,4

and
a_{n}
with
n=pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}
,
p
prime only. We assume
x^{\frac{4}{5}}<y<x
, so that the restrictions (
2.12
) hold automatically because
x\leqslant n\leqslant 2x
.
Observe that
p
is the largest prime in the prime quintet
n=pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}
; specifically we have

(2.14)

x^{\frac{1}{5}}<p<2x^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Let
Q(\mathcal{A})
be the following specific choice for (
2.2
)

(2.15)

Q(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{p}\sum_{p_{1}}\sum_{p_{2}}\sum_{p_{3}}\sum_{p_{4}}a_{pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}}

where all prime variables are distinct and
p_{j}
run freely over the segment (
2.13
) without ordering. The variable
p
is restricted only by the support of
\mathcal{A}=(a_{n})
because (
2.14
) is redundant. We obtain the following combinatorial inequality:

(2.16)

S(\mathcal{A},z)\geqslant S^{-}(\mathcal{A},z)+\frac{1}{24}Q(\mathcal{A}),\quad{\rm if}\,\,y^{1/4}\leqslant z\leqslant y^{1/2}.

The linear beta-sieve in Chapter 12 of [FI2] requires the density function
g(d)
to satisfy the upper bound

(2.17)

\prod_{w\leqslant p<z}\bigl(1-g(p)\bigr)^{-1}\leqslant\frac{\log z}{\log w}\bigl(1+\frac{\ell}{\log w}\bigr)

for every
2\leqslant w<z
with some constant
\ell
. In our case, we shall verify (
2.17
) with an absolute constant
\ell
. Then we have

(2.18)

S^{-}(\mathcal{A},z)\geqslant\bigl(f_{1}(s)+O\bigl((\log y)^{-1/6}\bigr)\bigr)XV(z)-R(y)

where
s=(\log y)/\log z
and
f_{1}(s)=2e^{\gamma}s^{-1}\log(s-1)
, if
2\leqslant s\leqslant 4
. We take
z={\sqrt{y}}
, so
s=2
,
f_{1}(2)=0
and (
2.18
) gives nothing positive. Therefore, we are left with the following lower bound

(2.19)

S(\mathcal{A},\sqrt{y})\geqslant\frac{1}{24}Q(\mathcal{A})+O\bigl(XV(\sqrt{y})(\log y)^{-1/6}\bigr)\bigr).

On the other hand,
S(\mathcal{A})=S(\mathcal{A},\sqrt{2x})
is close to
S(\mathcal{A},{\sqrt{y}})
if the admissible level of distribution
y
is close to
x
in the logarithmic scale. Put

(2.20)

y=x^{\theta},\quad\frac{4}{5}<\theta<1.

The difference

(2.21)

S(\mathcal{A},\sqrt{y})-S(\mathcal{A},{\sqrt{2}x})=\sum_{\sqrt{y}\leqslant p<\sqrt{2x}}S(\mathcal{A}_{p},p),

counting
a_{n}
over prime duos
pp^{\prime}
can be estimated successfully by any upper-bound sieve method. Our sieve conditions (
2.8
) and (
2.17
) allow us to apply (12.12) of [FI2] for every subsequence
\mathcal{A}_{p}
. We replace
X
in (
2.5
) by
g(p)X
,
r_{d}
by
r_{pd}
and choose the level of distribution for
\mathcal{A}_{p}
to be
y/p
. We obtain
S(\mathcal{A}_{p},p)\leqslant S(\mathcal{A}_{p},y/p)
and

S(\mathcal{A}_{p},y/p)\leqslant g(p)V(y/p)X\bigl\{F_{1}(1)+O\bigl((\log y)^{-1/6}\bigr)\bigr\}+\sum|r_{pd}|,

where
F_{1}(1)=2e^{\gamma}
and the sum of the error terms
r_{pd}
runs over
d|P(p)
,
d\leqslant y/p
. By (
2.17
) we have

V(y/p)\leqslant V(y)\frac{\log y}{\log(y/p)}\bigl(1+O\bigl(\frac{1}{\log y}\bigr)\bigl).

Next, we apply (5.47) of [FI2] as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{\sqrt{y}<p<\sqrt{2x}}g(p)(\log y/p)^{-1}

\displaystyle<\int_{\sqrt{y}}^{\sqrt{2x}}(\log y/w)^{-1}\frac{d\log w}{\log w}+O\bigl((\log y)^{-2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\log\frac{1}{2\theta-1}+O(1/\log y)\bigr)(\log y)^{-1}.

Moreover, we write by Mertens’ formula,

V(y)\log y=e^{-\gamma}H\bigl(1+O(1/\log y)\bigr)

where

(2.22)

H=H(y)=\prod_{p<y}\bigl(1-g(p)\bigr)\bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)^{-1}.

Inputting the above estimates into (
2.21
) and (
2.19
), we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.1:

Suppose (
2.17
) holds for every
2\leqslant w<z\leqslant\sqrt{2x}
with an absolute constant
\ell
and the remainder
R(y)
of level (
2.20
) satisfies (
2.8
). Then we have the following inequality.

(2.23)

S(\mathcal{A})\geqslant\frac{1}{24}Q(\mathcal{A})-\frac{2HX}{\log y}\log\frac{1}{2\theta-1}+O\bigl(X(\log y)^{-7/6}\bigr),

where
Q(\mathcal{A})
is given by (
2.15
) and the implied constant is absolute.

Remarks:
We shall succeed to show that
Q(\mathcal{A})\geqslant cHX(\log y)^{-1}
with an absolute constant
c>0
, but it will be a small constant because we took account of only a small portion of the combinatorial decomposition (
2.9
). Nevertheless, we still obtain a positive lower bound for
S(\mathcal{A})
because
\theta
will be very close to
1
.

From now on we are interested in an arithmetic progression. Let
q
be large,
(a,q)=1
and
x\geqslant q^{6}
. We take
\mathcal{A}=(a_{n})
with

(2.24)

a_{n}=f(n/x),\quad{\rm if}\,\,n\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)

and
a_{n}=0
otherwise.
Here,
f(u)\geqslant 0
is a smooth function supported on
1\leqslant u\leqslant 2
with

(2.25)

{\hat{f}}(0)=\int f(u)du>0.

Our sequence
\mathcal{A}=(a_{n})
satisfies the sieve conditions with

(2.26)

X={\hat{f}}(0)x/q

and density function

(2.27)

g(d)=d^{-1}\,\,{\rm if}\,\,(d,q)=1,\quad g(d)=0\,\,{\rm if}\,\,(d,q)\neq 1.

Hence, the product (
2.22
) is constant, namely
H=q/\varphi(q)
because
y\geqslant q
. Now,
S(\mathcal{A})
becomes

(2.28)

\pi_{f}(x;q,a)=\sum_{p\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}f(p/x)

and (
2.23
) yields

(2.29)

\pi_{f}(x;q,a)\geqslant\frac{1}{24}Q(\mathcal{A})-{\hat{f}}(0)\bigl(\log\frac{1}{2\theta-1}\bigr)\frac{2x}{\varphi(q)\log y}+O\bigl(\frac{x}{q}(\log x)^{-7/6}\bigr).

The error terms are absolutely bounded and
R(y)\ll y
so we choose

(2.30)

y=x/q(\log x)^{3}=x^{\theta}

and check that the remainder satisfies (
2.8
).

Now
Q(\mathcal{A})
in (
2.23
) for our sequence (
2.24
) reads as

(2.31)

Q(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{p}\mathop{\mathop{\sum\!\sum}\!\mathop{\sum\!\sum}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)\\
x^{1/6}<p_{j}<x^{1/5},\,\,j=1,2,3,4\end{subarray}}f(pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}/x).

At this point we can predict the limit of our output. Definitely, we cannot do better than the asymptotic formula for
\varphi(q)Q(\mathcal{A})
:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathop{\sum\!\sum}\!\mathop{\sum\!\sum}}\int f(vp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}/x)(\log v)^{-1}dv

\displaystyle\sim{\hat{f}}(0)x\mathop{\mathop{\sum\!\sum}\!\mathop{\sum\!\sum}}(p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4})^{-1}\bigl(\log(x/p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4})\bigr)^{-1}

\displaystyle\sim{\hat{f}}(0)\frac{x}{\log x}{\iiiint}(1-u_{1}-u_{2}-u_{3}-u_{4})^{-1}\frac{du_{1}}{u_{1}}\frac{du_{2}}{u_{2}}\frac{du_{3}}{u_{3}}\frac{du_{4}}{u_{4}}

\displaystyle<{\hat{f}}(0)\frac{5x}{\log x}\bigl(\log\frac{6}{5}\bigr)^{4},

where
{\frac{\theta}{6}<u_{j}<\frac{\theta}{5}}
,
Hence, if the lower bound in (
2.29
) is positive, then

\frac{5}{24}\bigl(\log\frac{6}{5}\bigr)^{4}>2\log\frac{1}{2\theta-1},\quad{\rm so}\,\,1-\theta<6\cdot 10^{-5}.

This shows that we cannot produce
p\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
smaller than
q^{10^{5}/6}
.

2.1
Technical preparations:

Before detecting the congruence
n=pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
by characters, we take advantage of positivity to separate the variables
p_{3}
,
p_{4}
from the crop function
f(n/x)
. To this end we subdivide
p_{3}
,
p_{4}
into short segments

(2.32)

C<p_{3}\leqslant\lambda C,\quad D<p_{4}\leqslant\lambda D

with

(2.33)

P\leqslant C,D\leqslant\lambda^{-1}P^{6/5},\quad P=x^{1/6}.

The constant
\lambda
is just slightly larger than
1
. The complete interval (
2.13
) is covered by the union of the segments (
2.32
) with

C=\lambda^{m}P,\,D=\lambda^{n}P,\,\,0\leqslant m,n\leqslant(\log P)/5\log\lambda.

We want to replace
f(pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}/x)
by
f(pp_{1}p_{2}CD/x)
. To this end we introduce the function
g(u)=uf^{\prime}(u)
and apply the following:

(2.34)

\displaystyle f(pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}/x)

\displaystyle=f(pp_{1}p_{2}CD/x)+\int_{1}^{p_{3}p_{4}/CD}g(pp_{1}p_{2}CDt/x)t^{-1}dt

\displaystyle\geqslant f(pp_{1}p_{2}CD/x)-\int_{1}^{\lambda^{2}}|g(pp_{1}p_{2}CDt/x)|t^{-1}dt

\displaystyle=f(pp_{1}p_{2}CD/x)-h(pp_{1}p_{2}CD/x),

where

(2.35)

h(u)=\int_{1}^{\lambda^{2}}|g(ut)|t^{-1}dt=u\int_{1}^{\lambda^{2}}|f^{\prime}(ut)|dt\ll\log\lambda.

Note that
h(u)
is supported on
\lambda^{-2}\leqslant u\leqslant 2
and is small if
\lambda
is very close to
1
.

We put

(2.36)

Q_{f}(C,D)=\sum_{p}\mathop{\mathop{\sum\!\sum}\!\mathop{\sum\!\sum}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}pp_{1}p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}\equiv a(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)\\
P<p_{1},p_{2}<P^{6/5}\\
C<p_{3}\leqslant\lambda C,\,D<p_{4}\leqslant\lambda D\end{subarray}}f(pp_{1}p_{2}CD/x).

Similarly, we define
Q_{h}(C,D)
as in (
2.36
) with
f(u)
replaced by
h(u)
. Summing over
C=\lambda^{m}P
,
D=\lambda^{n}P
, in the segment (
2.33
), we get

(2.37)

Q(\mathcal{A})\geqslant\sum_{C}\sum_{D}\bigl(Q_{f}(c,D)-Q_{h}(C,D)\bigr).

The case of
Q_{h}(C,D)
can be treated as
Q_{f}(C,D)
, but we can also estimate
Q_{h}(C,D)
directly by applying the Brun-Titchmarsh theorem in the variable
p
. Let
\tau
be a quantity
O(1/\log P)
, not always the same one. We find

\sum_{p\equiv\alpha(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}h(pp_{1}p_{2}CD/x)<{\hat{h}}(0)\frac{(2+\tau)x}{\varphi(q)}(\log x/qp_{1}p_{2}CD)^{-1}

where
qpp_{1}p_{2}CD<qP^{24/5}=qx^{4/5}\leqslant x^{29/30}
. Hence

(2.38)

Q_{h}(C,D)<{\hat{h}}(0)\frac{(2+\tau)}{\varphi(q)}(\log\tfrac{6}{5})^{2}\bigl(\frac{\log\lambda}{\log P}\bigr)^{2}\frac{30x}{\log x}

and the contribution of
Q_{h}(C,D)
to (
2.37
) is
\ll{\hat{h}}(0)x/\varphi(q)\log x
, which is insignificant because
{\hat{h}}(0)=\int h(u)du=(1-\lambda^{-2})\int u|f^{\prime}(u)|du
is small for
\lambda
close to

. Actually, this contribution will be discarded entirely after rounding up (or down) the constants in the other contributions.

3.
Dual sums: primes versus zeros

Let
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
run over complex numbers with
0\leqslant\beta\leqslant 1
and
|\gamma|\leqslant T
. For
T\geqslant 1
and
P\geqslant 3
we put

(3.1)

V=\max_{|\gamma|\leqslant T}\sum_{\rho}\bigl(1+(1-\beta)\log P\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(1+(\gamma-t)^{2}(\log P)^{2}\bigr)^{-1}.

Lemma 3.1:

For arbitrary complex numbers
a_{p}
supported on primes in the segment
P\leqslant p\leqslant P^{6/5}
we have

(3.2)

\sum_{\rho}P^{{\frac{5}{2}}(\beta-1)}\big|\sum_{p}a_{p}p^{-\rho}\big|^{2}\leqslant\bigl(1387V+O(NT(\log P)^{-4})\bigr)\sum_{p}|a_{p}|^{2}p^{-1}

where
N=N(T)
denotes the number of
\rho
’s counted with multiplicity and the implied constant is absolute.

Proof:
The assertion (
3.2
) is, by duality, equivalent to the statement that the inequality

(3.3)

\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant P^{6/5}}\big|\sum_{\rho}z_{\rho}p^{{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho}P^{{\frac{5}{4}}(\beta-1)}\bigr|^{2}\leqslant\bigl(1387V+O(NT(\log P)^{-4})\bigr)\sum_{\rho}|z_{\rho}|^{2}

holds true for all complex numbers
z_{\rho}
.

For the proof of the dual inequality (
3.3
) we majorize the summation over
p
by attaching the weights
k(\log n/\log P)\Lambda(n)/\log P
where
k(u)\geqslant 0
is a twice differentiable function supported on
\frac{1}{4}\leqslant u\leqslant\frac{5}{4}
with
k(u)\geqslant 1
, if
1\leqslant u\leqslant\frac{6}{5}
. Then, the left side of (
3.3
) is bounded by

(3.4)

\sum_{\rho}\sum_{\rho^{\prime}}|z_{\rho}z_{\rho^{\prime}}||K(s)|P^{{\frac{5}{4}}(\sigma-2)}

where
s=\bar{\rho}+\rho^{\prime}=\sigma+it
with
\sigma=\beta+\beta^{\prime}\leqslant 2
,
t=\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma
,

\min(A,B/C)\leqslant(A+B)(1+C)^{-1}

Now we choose the particular crop function

The minimum of these two bounds is less than

divided by

To estimate

we integrate by parts and proceed as follows:

\displaystyle K_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=-(\log P)^{-1}\int\Delta(P^{u})dk(u)P^{(1-s)u}

\displaystyle=-\int\bigl(k(u)(1-s)+k^{\prime}(u)/\log P\bigr)\Delta(P^{u})P^{(1-s)u}du

\displaystyle\ll T(\log P)^{-4}\int_{1/4}^{5/4}P^{(2-\sigma)u}du\ll T(\log P)^{-4}P^{\frac{5}{4}(2-\sigma)}.

Hence the contribution to (

14608e^{-80/6}<1/42

Remark.
The true expected value of

Primes in an arithmetic progression

Finally, we proceed to the estimation of the sum over primes (

If

and one of the conditions (

), (
6.13
), (
6.14
) holds, then

(7.2)

\varphi(q)\pi_{f}(x;q,a)\geqslant\hat{f}(0)x/210000\log x.

In addition to (

), we have the lower bound

\varphi(q)\pi_{f}(x;q,a)\geqslant L(1,\chi_{1})V(\chi_{1})x/168

if

Let

(a,q)=1

Proof:

Appendix

In this section we select some classical results about the zeros of Dirichlet
L
-functions and character sums over prime numbers.

Lemma A1:
For every
\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
, all zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,\chi)
satisfy

\beta\leqslant 1-\eta/\log q(1+|\gamma|),

except possibly for one real simple zero
\beta_{1}
of
L(s,\chi_{1})
with one real character
\chi_{1}(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
. Here,
\eta
is an absolute positive constant.

We need the above inequality with
\eta=1/657
.
We do not need the log-free zero density estimation nor the Deuring-Heilbronn repulsion property. In place of these, we quickly prove the following inequality.

Lemma A2:
For
s=\sigma+it
with
\sigma>1
we let

V(s,\chi)=\sum_{\rho}\bigl(1+\frac{1-\beta}{\sigma-1}\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(1+\bigl(\frac{\gamma-t}{\sigma-1}\bigr)^{2}\bigr)^{-1},

where
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
runs over the zeros of
L(s,\chi)
with
\beta>0
. If
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
we have

V(s,\chi)\leqslant 1+\frac{\sigma-1}{2}\log cq|s|

where
c>1
is an absolute constant.

Proof:
We can assume
\chi
is primitive. Then (see e.g. (5.24) of [IK])

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{q}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(\frac{s_{\chi}}{2})+B(\chi)+\sum_{\rho}\bigl(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\bigr)

where
s_{\chi}=s+(1+\chi(-1))/2
,
\Gamma^{\prime}(s)/\Gamma(s)=\log s+O(1)
and

{\rm Re}\,B(\chi)=-\sum_{\rho}{\rm Re}\,\frac{1}{\rho}.

Hence,

\sum_{\rho}{\rm Re}\,\frac{1}{s-\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\log q|s|+{\rm Re}\,\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)+O(1),

\bigl|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\bigr|\leqslant-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma-1}+O(1).

Now Lemma A2 follows easily from the inequality

{\rm Re}\,\frac{1}{s-\rho}=\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(\gamma-t)^{2}}\geqslant 1/(\sigma-1)\bigl(1+\frac{1-\beta}{\sigma-1}\bigr)\bigl(1+\bigl(\frac{\gamma-t}{\sigma-1}\bigr)^{2}\bigr).

Corollary A3:
For
q
sufficiently large and
|t|\leqslant\log q
we have

\sum_{\rho}\bigl(1+(1-\beta)\log q\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(1+(\gamma-t)^{2}(\log q)^{2}\bigr)^{-1}\leqslant\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2000}.

Lemma A4:
Let
\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
be a real character and
\beta
a real zero of
L(s,\chi)
. Then, for
x\geqslant q^{2}
,
q
sufficiently large, we have

\sum_{q^{2}\leqslant p\leqslant x}\bigl(1+\chi(p)\bigr)p^{-1}\leqslant 2(1-\beta)\log x.

Proof:
See the text between Proposition 24.1 and Corollary 24.2 of [FI2].

Lemma A5:
If
C\geqslant q^{2}
and
|c_{p}|\leqslant 1
for
C<p\leqslant\lambda C
, then

\sum_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}\bigl|\sum_{C<p\leqslant\lambda C}\chi(p)c_{p}\bigr|^{2}\leqslant(2+\tau)(\lambda-1)^{2}C^{2}(\log C)^{-1}(\log C/q)^{-1}

where
\lambda>1
is a fixed number and
\tau\ll(\log C)^{-1}
.

Proof:
The left side is estimated by the Brun-Titchmarsh theorem as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi(q)\mathop{\sum\!\sum}_{p^{\prime}\equiv p(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}c_{p^{\prime}}{\bar{c}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varphi(q)\sum_{C<p\leqslant\lambda C}\bigl(\pi(\lambda C;q,a)-\pi(C;q,a)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leqslant(2+\tau)(\lambda-1)C(\log C/q)^{-1}\bigl(\pi(\lambda C)-\pi(C)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leqslant(2+\tau)(\lambda-1)^{2}C^{2}(\log C/q)^{-1}(\log C)^{-1}.

Remark:
Our treatment of character sums over prime quintets resembles a multiplicative version of the circle method for the ternary Goldbach problem. The prime trios alone would perform this part, but the extra two prime factors are needed to avoid the log-free zero density bound (
1.3
) and the repulsion property of the possible exceptional zero. There is a possibility to arrange a combinatorial identity of sieve type by means of which we could work with two primes and one almost prime rather than with prime quintets.

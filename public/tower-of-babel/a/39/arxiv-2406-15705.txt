1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to searching for the minimal number of distinct closed geodesics on positively curved Finsler spheres. This problem is closely related to a conjecture of Katok and Anosov, which claims that every Finsler metric on a sphere
S^{2k}
or
S^{2k-1}
carries at least
2k
distinct closed geodesics. In fact, in 1973, Katok constructed certain irreversible Finsler metrics on spheres with flag curvature
K=1
, which have exactly
2k
distinct closed geodesics. And based on these examples, in 1974 Anosov proposed the above conjecture.

A closed curve in a Finsler manifold is a closed geodesic if it is locally the shortest path connecting any two nearby points on this
curve. A closed geodesic
c:S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to M
is
prime

if it is not a multiple covering (i.e., iteration) of any other
closed geodesics. Here the
m
-th iteration
c^{m}
of
c
is defined
by
c^{m}(t)=c(mt)
. The inverse curve
c^{-1}
of
c
is defined by

c^{-1}(t)=c(1-t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We call two prime closed geodesics

c
and
d

distinct
if there is no
{\theta}\in(0,1)
such that

c(t)=d(t+{\theta})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We shall omit the word
distinct
when we talk about more than one prime closed geodesic.
For a reversible Finsler (or Riemannian) metric, two closed geodesics

c
and
d
are called
geometrically distinct
if
c(S^{1})\neq d(S^{1})
.

For a closed geodesic
c
on
(M,\,F)
with
\dim M=n
, denote by
P_{c}

the linearized Poincaré map of
c
. Then
P_{c}\in{\rm Sp}(2n-2)
is a symplectic matrix.
We define the
elliptic height

e(P_{c})

of
P_{c}
to be the total algebraic multiplicity of all eigenvalues of

P_{c}
on the unit circle
\mathbb{U}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\;|z|=1\}
in the complex plane

\mathbb{C}
. Since
P_{c}
is symplectic,
e(P_{c})
is even and
0\leq e(P_{c})\leq 2n-2
.
Then
c
is called
hyperbolic
if
e(P_{c})=0
;
elliptic
if
e(P_{c})=2n-2
;

non-degenerate
if
1
is not an eigenvalue of
P_{c}
. A Finsler metric
F
on
M
is called
bumpy
if all the
closed geodesics (including iterations) on
(M,F)
are non-degenerate.

There is a famous conjecture in geometry which claims the existence of infinitely many
closed geodesics on a compact Riemannian manifold. This conjecture
has been proved for many cases, but sitll open for CROSS (compact rank one symmetric
spaces) except for
S^{2}
(cf.
[
Ban
]
,
[
DLW1
]
,
[
Fra
]
,
[
GM
]
,
[
Hin2
]
,
[
Hin3
]
and therein).
It was surprising when Katok in
[
Kat
]
found certain irreversible Finsler metrics on CROSS (including spheres) with only finitely
many closed geodesics and all of them are non-degenerate and elliptic (cf.
[
Zil
]
).

Based on the Katok’s examples, in 1974, Anosov in
[
Ano
]
proposed the following conjecture (also cf.
[
Lon3
]
and
[
BuM
]
), and we call the
Katok-Anosov conjecture
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)\geq 2\left[\frac{n+1}{2}\right]\quad\mbox{for any Finsler metric $F$ on}\ S^{n},

where, denote by
{\mathcal{N}}(M,F)
the number of distinct closed geodesics on
(M,F)
, and
[a]=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,k\leq a\}
. In 2010, Bangert and Long in
[
BaL
]
proved this conjecture for every Finsler
2
-dimensional sphere
(S^{2},F)
. Since then, the index iteration theory of closed geodesics (cf.
[
Bot
]
and
[
Lon2
]
) has been applied to study the closed geodesic problem on Finsler manifolds.

When
n\geq 3
, the Katok-Anosov conjecture is still open in full of generality. About the multiplicity and stability problem of closed geodesics, two classes of typical conditions, including the positively curved condition and the non-degenerate (or bumpy) condition, have been studied widely.

In
[
Rad4
]
, Rademacher has introduced the reversibility
\lambda=\lambda(M,F)
of a compact Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\lambda=\max\{F(-X)\ |\ X\in TM,\ F(X)=1\}\geq 1.

Then Rademacher in
[
Rad5
]
has obtained some results about the multiplicity and stability of closed geodesics. For example, let
F
be a Finsler metric on
S^{n}
with reversibility
{\lambda}
and flag curvature
K
satisfying
\left(\frac{{\lambda}}{1+{\lambda}}\right)^{2}<K\leq 1
, then there exist at least
n/2-1
closed geodesics with length
<2n\pi
. If
\left(\frac{2n-4}{n-1}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{{\lambda}}{1+{\lambda}}\right)^{2}<K\leq 1
with
{\lambda}<\frac{n-1}{n-3}
and
n\geq 4
, then there exist at least
n-2
closed geodesics. If
\frac{9{\lambda}^{2}}{4(1+{\lambda})^{2}}<K\leq 1
with
{\lambda}<2
, then there exists a closed geodesic of elliptic-parabolic, i.e., its linearized Poincaré map split into
2
-dimensional rotations and a part whose eigenvalues are
\pm 1
.

In 2012, Wang in
[
Wan2
]
proved the conjecture (
1.1
) for
(S^{n},F)
provided that
F
is bumpy and its flag curvature
K
satisfies
\left(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^{2}<K\leq 1
. Also in
[
Wan2
]
, Wang showed that for every bumpy Finsler metric
F
on
S^{n}
satisfying
\frac{9{\lambda}^{2}}{4(1+{\lambda})^{2}}<K\leq 1
, there exist two prime elliptic closed geodesics if
{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)<+\infty
.
As a further generalization, Duan, Long and Wang in
[
DLW2
]
obtained the optimal lower bound of the number of distinct closed geodesics on every CROSS
(M,F)
(including spheres) if
F
is bumpy and the flag curvature is non-negative.

Without the assumption of the non-degenerate (or bumpy) metric, the proof of conjecture (
1.1
) is extremely hard. For example, under the assumption of the positively curved condition
\left(\frac{{\lambda}}{1+{\lambda}}\right)^{2}<K\leq 1
, it can only be showed that
{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)\geq 3
(cf.
[
Dua1
]
) or (
[
Dua2
]
)) and
{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)\geq\left[\frac{n+1}{2}\right]
(cf.
[
Wan4
]
), where
n\geq 3
.

1.2.
Main results

Motivated by the conjecture (
1.1
) and the above results, in this paper, we further consider the positively curved Finsler
n
-dimensional sphere
(S^{n},F)
without the bumpy assumption, and obtain some multiplicity results of closed geodesics on
(S^{n},F)
. In particular, the following Theorem 1.1 solved the Katok-Anosov conjecture (
1.1
) for certain positively curved spheres
(S^{n},F)
when
n
is even, and this result is optimal due to the Katok’s examples.

Next we assume that the reversibility
\lambda
and flag curvature
K
of
(S^{n},F)
satisfies the following condition:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2n-3}{n-1}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\right)^{2}<K\leq 1,\quad\mbox{where}\ \lambda<\frac{n-1}{n-2}.

Theorem 1.1.

Every Finsler metric
F
on
S^{n}

(n\geq 4)
satisfying (
1.2
) carries at least
n
prime closed geodesics.

On the other hand, we also obtain some stability results of closed geodesics on spheres satisfying (
1.2
) as follows.

Theorem 1.2.

Every Finsler metric
F
on
S^{n}

(n\geq 4)
satisfying (
1.2
) carries at least
2\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]-1
non-hyperbolic prime closed geodesics if
{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)<+\infty
.

Theorem 1.3.

Every Finsler metric
F
on
S^{n}

(n\geq 4)
satisfying (
1.2
) carries at least
n-2
prime closed geodesics, whose linearized Poincaré map possesses at least an eigenvalue of the form
\exp(\pi i\mu)
with an irrational
\mu
, if
{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)<+\infty
.

When the curvature condition is weaker than (
1.2
), we can obtain the existence of at least
n-1
prime closed geodesics, i.e. we obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.4.

On a Finsler sphere
(S^{n},F)

(n\geq 4)
satisfying
\frac{9}{4}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\right)^{2}<K\leq 1
and
\lambda<2
, there exist at least
n-1
prime closed geodesics.

1.3.
Outline of the proof

The main ingredients of the proof of Theorem 1.1 include the Fadell-Rabinowitz index theory (see Lemma 2.5 and Lemma 2.6 below) from
[
Rad3
]
and
[
Wan3
]
, the Morse theory and the enhanced common index jump theorem (see Theorem 3.6 below) recently developed in
[
DLW2
]
, which originally grown from the groundbreaking work
[
LoZ
]
.

Let
(S^{n},F)
be a Finsler sphere satisfying (
1.2
) and assume that there are only finitely many prime closed geodesics on
(S^{n},F)
, which is denoted by
\{c_{j}\}_{j=1}^{p}
. The outline of the proof of Theorem 1.1 is as follows.

First, under the curvature condition (
1.2
), Theorem 1.1 in
[
Rad4
]
established the lower bound of the length of any prime closed geodesics on
(S^{n},F)
, which in turn gives the lower bound of
i(c_{j}^{m})
and the average index
\hat{i}(c_{j})
for each closed geodesic
c_{j}
(cf. Lemma 4.1 below).

Second, it follows from the enhanced common index jump theorem (cf. Theorem 3.6 below) that there exist an appropriately chosen
(p+1)
-tuple
(N,m_{1},\cdots,m_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
such that the indices
i(c_{j}^{2m_{j}\pm m})
of iterates around
2m_{j}
of all
c_{j}
’s satisfy some precise equalities and estimates (cf. (
4.6
)-(
4.9
)). Then, together with those index estimates from Lemma 4.1, we can get much more useful and crucial estimates (cf. (
4.15
)-(
4.20
) and Lemma 4.2) for later proofs.

Third, with these above preparations about index estimates, as the first step of the proof of Theorem 1.1, we established the existence of
n-1
prime closed geodesics. More precisely, for each
i\in G_{1}\equiv[N-(n-2),N-1]\cap\mathbb{N}
(and denoted by
\bar{G_{1}}\equiv[2N-(n-3),2N+(n-3)]\cap\mathbb{N}
), by the Fadell-Rabinowitz index theory (cf. Lemma 2.6), there exists some
j(i)\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
and
m(i)\in\mathbb{N}
such that the integer
2i+n-1
(belonging to
\bar{G_{1}}
) lies between
i(c_{j(i)}^{m(i)})
and
i(c_{j(i)}^{m(i)})+\nu(c_{j(i)}^{m(i)})
, and
c_{j(i)}^{m(i)}
has non-trivial critical module
\overline{C}_{2i+n-1}(E,c_{j(i)}^{m(i)})\neq 0
. Through the analysis of index estimates and Morse theory, it can be deduced that
m(i)=2m_{j(i)}
(cf. Claim 1 below). Then the assumption
{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)<+\infty
and Lemma 2.5 yield that the different integer
i\in G_{1}
corresponds to the distinct closed geodesic
c_{j(i)}
. Thus it shows that
p\geq\#[N-(n-2),N-1]=n-2
. In addition, by a result of
[
Dua1
]
or
[
Dua2
]
(cf. Lemma 4.3 below), we can find one more closed geodesic
c_{j_{0}}
satisfying
\overline{C}_{q}(E,c_{j_{0}}^{2m_{j_{0}}})\neq 0
only for
q=2N+n-1
. Notice that
2N+n-1\notin\bar{G_{1}}
, thus
c_{j_{0}}
is distinct from each of the
n-2
closed geodesics found above. This yields
p={\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)\geq n-1
.

Finally, as the second step of the proof of Theorem 1.1, we carry out the proof by contradiction, i.e., assuming that there are only
n-1
closed geodesics. In order to get a contradiction, here we consider another integer interval
G_{2}\equiv[N-(2n-3),N-(n-1)]\cap\mathbb{N}
with empty intersection with
G_{1}
(and denoted by
\bar{G_{2}}\equiv[2N-3(n-1)+2,2N-(n-1)]\cap\mathbb{N}
). Then, by index estimates and Morse theory, we can show that there holds
m(i)=2m_{j(i)}-1
for every integer
i\in G_{2}
and
\{c_{j(i)}\,|\,i\in G_{2}\}
is the one-to-one correspondence to the
n-1
distinct closed geodesics found in Step 1 (cf. Claim 2 and (
4.46
) below). Then it can be deduced that the Morse-type number
M_{2N-(n-1)}
only can be contributed by
c_{j(N-n+1)}^{2m_{j(N-n+1)}-1}
(cf. Claim 3 and (
4.54
) below). However, this is a contradiction
1=M_{2N-(n-1)}\geq b_{2N-(n-1)}=2
by the Morse inequality, where note that
N
can be chosen to be the multiple of
n-1
. Thus we obtain the existence of another distinct closed geodesic, i.e.
{\mathcal{N}}(S^{n},F)\geq n
.

2.
Critical point theory and Fadell-Rabinowitz index theory

2.1.
Critical point theory for closed geodesics

On a compact Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, we choose an auxiliary Riemannian
metric. This endows the space
\Lambda=\Lambda M
of
H^{1}
-maps

\gamma:S^{1}\rightarrow M
with a natural Riemannian Hilbert manifold structure
on which the group
S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
acts continuously
by isometries, cf.
[
Kli
]
. This action is
defined by translating the parameter, i.e.,

(s\cdot\gamma)(t)=\gamma(t+s)

for all
\gamma\in{\Lambda}
and
s,t\in S^{1}
.
The Finsler metric
F
defines an energy functional
E
and a length
functional
L
on
\Lambda
by

(2.1)

E(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{1}}F(\dot{\gamma}(t))^{2}dt,\quad L(\gamma)=\int_{S^{1}}F(\dot{\gamma}(t))dt.

Both functionals are invariant under the
S^{1}
-action.
The functional
E
is
C^{1,1}
on
{\Lambda}
and
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition (cf.
[
Mer
]
). Thus we can apply the
deformation theorems in
[
Cha
]
and
[
MaW
]
.
The critical points
of
E
of positive energies are precisely the closed geodesics
c:S^{1}\to M

of the Finsler structure. If
c\in\Lambda
is a closed geodesic then
c
is
a regular curve, i.e.,
\dot{c}(t)\not=0
for all
t\in S^{1}
, and this implies
that the second differential
E^{\prime\prime}(c)
of
E
at
c
exists.
As usual we define the index
i(c)
of
c
as the maximal dimension of
subspaces of
T_{c}\Lambda
on which
E^{\prime\prime}(c)
is negative definite, and the
nullity
\nu(c)
of
c
so that
\nu(c)+1
is the dimension of the null
space of
E^{\prime\prime}(c)
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
we denote the
m
-fold iteration map

\phi^{m}:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda
by

(2.2)

\phi^{m}({\gamma})(t)={\gamma}(mt)\qquad\forall\,{\gamma}\in{\Lambda},t\in S^{1}.

We also use the
notation
\phi^{m}(\gamma)=\gamma^{m}
. For a closed geodesic
c
, the
average index is defined by

(2.3)

\hat{i}(c)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{i(c^{m})}{m}.

If
\gamma\in\Lambda
is not constant then the multiplicity

m(\gamma)
of
\gamma
is the order of the isotropy group
\{s\in S^{1}\mid s\cdot\gamma=\gamma\}
. If
m(\gamma)=1
then
\gamma
is
called
prime
. Hence
m(\gamma)=m
if and only if there exists a
prime curve
\tilde{\gamma}\in\Lambda
such that

\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}^{m}
.

In this paper for
{\kappa}\in\mathbb{R}
and a closed geodesic
c
we denote by

(2.4)

{\Lambda}^{{\kappa}}=\{d\in{\Lambda}\,|\,E(d)\leq{\kappa}\},\qquad{\Lambda}(c)=\{{\gamma}\in{\Lambda}\mid E({\gamma})<E(c)\}.

We call a closed geodesic satisfying the isolation condition, if
the following holds:

(Iso)

For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
the orbit
S^{1}\cdot c^{m}
is an
isolated critical orbit of
E
.

Note that if the number of prime closed geodesics on a Finsler manifold
is finite, then all the closed geodesics satisfy (Iso).

Using singular homology with rational
coefficients we consider the following critical
\mathbb{Q}
-module of a closed geodesic

:

(2.5)

\overline{C}_{*}(E,c)=H_{*}\left(({\Lambda}(c)\cup S^{1}\cdot c)/S^{1},{\Lambda}(c)/S^{1}\right).

(cf. Satz 6.11 of
[

Consequently it yields

\displaystyle\{j(i)\mid i\in G_{2}\}=\{1,2,\cdots,n-1\}.

Claim 3.

There holds

(4.47)

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{2N-(n-1)}(E,c_{j}^{m})\neq 0,\;\text{only if}\;j=j(N-(n-1))\;\text{and}\;m=2m_{j(N-n+1)}-1.

)-(

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{2N-(n-1)}(E,c_{j}^{m})\neq 0,\quad\text{only if}\ m\in\{2m_{j},2m_{j}-1\}.

Firstly, assuming that for some

\overline{C}_{2N-(n-1)}(E,c_{j_{0}^{\prime}}^{2m_{j_{0}^{\prime}}})\\

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{q}(E,c_{j_{0}^{\prime}}^{2m_{j_{0}^{\prime}}})=0\quad\text{for}\;q\neq 2N-(n-1)

\displaystyle c_{j_{0}^{\prime}}\neq c_{j_{0}},\quad\text{i.e.}\quad j_{0}^{\prime}\neq n-1.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3

In this subsection, we carry out the proofs of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 under the assumption (F1).

Proof of Theorem 1.2.
Under the assumption (F1), for those closed geodesics
\{c_{j}\}_{j=1}^{p}

Here we mainly follow some ideas in the proof of Theorem 1.4 in

Claim 4.

(4.56)

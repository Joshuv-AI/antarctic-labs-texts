1.
Introduction

Consider a closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. When
M
has negative sectional curvature everywhere, the geodesic flow defined on the unit tangent bundle
SM
is a prime example of Anosov flow (cf.
[
1
]
,
[
24
, Section 17.6]
). There is correspondence between closed geodesics on
M
and periodic orbits under the geodesic flow, and they are important from both geometric and dynamical points of view. The asymptotic growth of the number of closed geodesics is given by the celebrated theorem of Margulis in his 1969 thesis
[
31
,
32
]
:

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\#P(t)/\frac{e^{ht}}{ht}=1

where
P(t)
is the set of free-homotopy classes containing a closed geodesic with length at most
t
, and
h
is the topological entropy of the geodesic flow.

The main ingredient in the proof of Margulis’s theorem is from ergodic theory. Margulis
[
32
]
constructed an invariant measure under the geodesic flow. This measure is mixing, and the conditional measures on stable/unstable manifolds contract/expand with a uniform rate under the flow. Using different methods, Bowen constructed a measure of maximal entropy (MME for short) in
[
4
]
, for Axiom A flows. Later in
[
5
]
, Bowen proved that MME is unique. It turns out that the measures constructed by Bowen and Margulis are eventually the same one, which is called
Bowen-Margulis measure
. Bowen-Margulis measure has the Bernoulli property
[
43
]
, an ultimate mixing property for measurable dynamical systems.

An alternative approach to (
1
) for Axiom A flows is given by Parry and Pollicott
[
37
]
, based on zeta functions and symbolic dynamics. See the survey by Sharp in
[
32
]
for further progress that has been made since Margulis’s thesis.

In 1984, A. Katok
[
7
]
conjectured that the geodesic flow on a closed rank one manifold of nonpositive curvature admits a unique MME. On such manifolds, the geodesic flow exhibits nonuniformly hyperbolic behavior. In 1998, Katok’s conjecture was finally settled by Knieper
[
26
]
. In his proof, Knieper used Patterson-Sullivan measures on the boundary at infinity of the universal cover of
M
to construct a MME (called
Knieper measure
), and showed that this measure is the unique MME. Recently, Burns, Fisher, Climenhaga and Thompson
[
6
]
proved the uniqueness of equilibrium states for certain potentials, which generalized Knieper’s result.

Knieper’s measure can be used to obtain the following asymptotic estimates
[
25
,
27
]
: there exists
C>0
such that for
t
large enough

\frac{1}{C}\frac{e^{ht}}{t}\leq\#P(t)\leq C\frac{e^{ht}}{t}.

Since then, efforts are made to improve the above to the Margulis-type asymptotic estimates (
1
). A preprint
[
23
]
, though not published, contains many inspiring ideas to this problem. A breakthrough was made recently by Ricks
[
44
]
: (
1
) is established for rank one locally CAT
(0)
spaces, which include rank one manifolds with nonpositve curvature.

Beyond nonpositive curvature, manifolds without focal/conjugate points are studied extensively. Following Knieper’s method, Liu, Wang and the author construct the Knieper measure for manifolds without focal points and showed it is
the unique MME
[
30
]
. Gelfert and Ruggiero
[
22
]
also proved the uniqueness of MME for surfaces without focal points
by different methods. Extending the approach in
[
6
]
, Chen, Kao and Park proved the uniqueness of equilibrium states for certain potentials in
[
9
,
10
]
. Manifolds without conjugate points in general are far from being well understood. Remarkable progress was made by Climenhaga, Knieper and War for a class of manifolds (including all surfaces) without conjugate points: the uniqueness of MME is proved in
[
12
]
and (
1
) is obtained in
[
13
]
. Weaver
[
50
]
also obtained (
1
) for surfaces having negative curvature outside of a collection of radially symmetric caps.

In this paper, we obtain Margulis-type asymptotic estimates (
1
) for manifolds without focal points, by combining ideas from
[
44
]
and
[
13
]
. Compared to rank one locally CAT
(0)
or manifolds of nonpositive curvature in
[
44
]
, the lack of convexity properties causes some difficulties, and some rigidity and comparison results do not hold any more (see Remarks
2.2
,
3.6
and
3.8
below). We will provide full account of details on the angle metric, weak
\pi
-convergence theorem, continuity of Busemann functions in the setting of no focal points. We also adopt the new ideas in
[
13
]
: for example, the slice instead of full box is used in the intersection
S\cap\phi^{-t}\gamma B
(see Section 2.4 for notations).

As we are dealing with manifolds, we will follow more closely the terminology in
[
13
]
than
[
44
]
. Nevertheless, our setting is quite different from that in
[
13
]
. For example, manifolds without focal points do not have uniform visibility property in general, and thus we have to develop weak
\pi
-convergence theorem to prove a type of closing lemma. Moreover in
[
13
]
, by Morse Lemma the action induced by a deck transformation on the boundary at infinity has a unique repelling/attracting fixed point (
[
13
, Remark 2.6]
). In our setting, the action has no such property and its behaviour will be analyzed using the properties of no focal points.

In the appendix, we show that the Knieper measure for manifolds without focal points has the Bernoulli property. In nonpositive curvature case, the Knieper measure is proved to be mixing in
[
2
]
and Bernoulli in
[
8
]
. For manifolds without focal points, Knieper measure is known to be mixing by
[
29
]
and have the Kolmogorov property by
[
10
]
. The method of Call and Thompson
[
8
]
, actually proves the Kolmogorov property of the unique equilibrium states for certain potentials
[
8
,
10
]
. For Knieper measure, to lift the Kolmogorov property to the Bernoulli property, we adapt the classical argument in
[
33
,
43
]
for Anosov flows. In fact, we follow closely
[
11
]
, which is for systems with nonuniformly hyperbolic structure.

1.1.
Statement of main results

Suppose that
(M,g)
is a
C^{\infty}
closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
where
g
is a Riemannian metric. Let
\pi:SM\to M
be the unit tangent bundle over
M
. For each
v\in S_{p}M
,
we always denote by
c_{v}:{\mathbb{R}}\to M
be the unique geodesic on
M
satisfying the initial conditions
c_{v}(0)=p
and
\dot{c}_{v}(0)=v
. The geodesic flow
\phi=(\phi^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
(generated by the Riemannian metric
g
) on
SM
is defined as:

\phi^{t}:SM\rightarrow SM,\qquad(p,v)\mapsto(c_{v}(t),\dot{c}_{v}(t)),\ \ \ \ \forall\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

A vector field

along a geodesic

is called a

if it satisfies the

:

J^{\prime\prime}+R(J,\dot{c})\dot{c}=0

where

is the Riemannian curvature tensor and
′
denotes the covariant derivative along

Definition 1.1
.

Let
c
be a geodesic on
(M,g)
.

(1)

A pair of distinct points

and
q=c(t_{2})
are called

if there is a Jacobi field

along
c
such that
J(t_{1})=0
,
J^{\prime}(t_{1})\neq 0
and
\frac{d}{dt}\|J(t)\|^{2}\mid_{t=t_{2}}=0
;

(2)

p=c(t_{1})
and
q=c(t_{2})
are called
conjugate
if there is a nontrivial Jacobi field
J
along
c
such that
J(t_{1})=0=J(t_{2})
.

A compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called a manifold
without focal points/without conjugate points
if there is no focal points/conjugate points on any geodesic in
(M,g)
.

By definition, if a manifold has no focal points then it has no conjugate points. All manifolds of nonpositive curvature always have no focal points.

A Jocobi field
J(t)
along a geodesic
c(t)
is called
parallel
if
J^{\prime}(t)=0
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The notion of
rank
is defined as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

For each
v\in SM
, we define
rank
(
v
) to be the dimension of the vector space of parallel Jacobi fields along the geodesic
c_{v}
, and
rank
(
M
):=
\min\{
rank
(v)\mid v\in SM\}
. For a geodesic
c
we define
rank
(
c
)=
rank
(
\dot{c}(t)
),
\forall\ t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In this paper, we always let
M
be a rank one closed Riemannian manifold without focal points. Then
SM
splits into two invariant subsets under the geodesic flow: the regular set
\text{Reg}:=\{v\in SM\mid\text{rank}(v)=1\}
, and the singular set
\text{Sing}:=SM\setminus\text{Reg}
.

Recall that
P(t)
is the set of free-homotopy classes containing a closed geodesic with length at most
t
. If
M
has negative curvature, each free-homotopy class contains exactly one closed geodesic, so
\#P(t)
is just the number of closed geodesics with length at most
t
. If
M
has nonpositive curvature or without focal points, a free-homotopy class may have infinitely many closed geodesics.

The main result of this paper is the following Margulis-type asymptotic estimates of
\#P(t)
.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a rank one closed Riemannian manifold without focal points, then

\#P(t)\sim\frac{e^{ht}}{ht}

where
h=h_{\text{top}}(g)
denotes the topological entropy of the geodesic flow on
SM
, and the notation
f_{1}\sim f_{2}
means that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f_{1}(t)}{f_{2}(t)}=1
.

Recall that a
\phi
-invariant probability measure
\mu
is called the
measure of maximal entropy
(MME for short) if
h_{\mu}(\phi)\geq h_{\nu}(\phi)
for any
\phi
-invariant probability measure
\nu
. By the variational principle,
h_{\mu}(\phi)=h
. It is shown in
[
29
]
that MME is unique and can be constructed via Patterson-Sullivan measures. The unique MME is called
Knieper measure
(see Section 2.3 for more details).

The following is a result of equidistribution of periodic orbits with respect to the Knieper measure.

Theorem B
.

Suppose that
M
is a rank one closed Riemannian manifold without focal points, and
\epsilon\in(0,{\text{inj}}(M)/2
is fixed where
{\text{inj}}(M)
is the injectivity radius of
M
.
For
t>0
, let
C(t)
be any maximal set of pairwise non-free-homotopic
closed geodesics with lengths in
(t-\epsilon,t]
, and define the measure

\nu_{t}:=\frac{1}{C(t)}\sum_{c\in C(t)}\frac{Leb_{c}}{t}

where
Leb_{c}
is the Lebesgue measure along the curve
\dot{c}
in the unit
tangent bundle
SM
.

Then the measures
\nu_{t}
converge in the
weak
∗
topology to the unique measure of maximal entropy as
t\to\infty
.

In Section
2
, we recall some geometric and ergodic background of manifolds without foal points, including Busemann functions, construction of the Knieper measure and local product flow boxes. All Sections
3-6
are devoted to proving Theorem
A
. Meanwhile, Theorem
B
is proved in Section
6
.

In the appendix, we prove the following ultimate mixing property for the Knieper measure. We also recall the necessary definitions of Kolmogorov/Bernoulli properties and known results from the literature.

Theorem C
.

Let
M
be a rank one closed Riemannian manifold without focal points, then
the unique measure of maximal entropy is Bernoulli.

Geometric and ergodic background

In this section, we present some geometric and ergodic results on rank one manifolds without focal points, which will be used in the following discussions.

Geometric properties and boundary at infinity

Let

\mathop{\hbox{{\rm pr}}}:X\to M

\gamma(c(t))=c(t+t_{0})

of

where

We still denote by

and

Denote by

both the distance functions on

Suppose that

and

are both geodesics in

and

are

such that

(2)

d(c_{1}(t),c_{2}(t))\leq C,~~\forall~t\geq 0.

We say

The relation of positive/negative asymptoticity is an equivalence relation between geodesics on

The following are the fundamental properties of the geometry of manifolds without focal points.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Cf.
[
35
]
)
Let
X
be a simply connected closed Riemannian manifold without focal points.

(1)

Let
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
be distinct geodesics with

Then for

(2)

Let

(3)

For any geodesic

and asymptotic to

(4)

(Flat Strip Lemma) If

and

up to parametrization.

Remark 2.2
.

If

has nonpositive curvature, both the functions

and

are convex in

for any two geodesics

We can define the visual topology on

[

(

2.3.
Patterson-Sullivan measure and Knieper measure

We recall the construction of Patterson-Sullivan measure and Knieper measure in this section. Let us start with the definition of Busemann density.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
X
be a simply connected manifold without focal points and

\Gamma
a discrete subgroup of
\text{Iso}(X)
, the group of isometries of
X
. For a given constant
r>0
, a family of finite Borel measures
\{\mu_{p}\}_{p\in X}

on
{\partial X}
is called an
r
-dimensional
Busemann density
if

(1)

For any
p,\ q\in X
and
\mu_{p}
-a.e.
\xi\in{\partial X}
,

\frac{d\mu_{q}}{d\mu_{p}}(\xi)=e^{-r\cdot b_{\xi}(q,p)}

where
b_{\xi}(q,p)
is the Busemann function.

(2)

\{\mu_{p}\}_{p\in X}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant, i.e., for all Borel sets
A\subset{\partial X}
and for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
we have

\mu_{\gamma p}(\gamma A)=\mu_{p}(A).

The construction of such a Busemann density is due to Patterson
[
38
]
in the case of Fuchsian groups, and generalized by Sullivan
[
47
]
for hyperbolic spaces.

Extending the techniques in
[
26
]
to manifolds without focal points, we constructed Busemann density via Patterson-Sullivan construction and showed the following in
[
30
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

(
[
30
, Theorem B]
)
Let
M=X/\Gamma
be a compact rank
1
manifold without focal points, then up to a multiplicative constant, the Busemann density is unique, i.e., the Patterson-Sullivan measure is the unique Busemann density.

Let
\{\mu_{p}\}_{p\in X}
be the Patterson-Sullivan measure, which is the unique Busemann density with dimension
h=h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(g)
.
Let
P:SX\to{\partial X}\times{\partial X}
be the projection given by
P(v)=(v^{-},v^{+})
. Denote by
\mathcal{I}^{P}:=P(SX)=\{P(v)\mid v\in SX\}
the subset of pairs in
{\partial X}\times{\partial X}

which can be connected by a geodesic. Note that the connecting geodesic may not be unique and moreover, not every pair
\xi\neq\eta
in
{\partial X}
can be connected by a geodesic.

Fix a point
p\in X
, we can define a
\Gamma
-invariant measure
\overline{\mu}

on
\mathcal{I}^{P}
by the following formula:

(3)

d\overline{\mu}(\xi,\eta):=e^{h\cdot\beta_{p}(\xi,\eta)}d\mu_{p}(\xi)d\mu_{p}(\eta),

where
\beta_{p}(\xi,\eta):=-\{b_{\xi}(q,p)+b_{\eta}(q,p)\}
is the Gromov product, and
q
is any point on a geodesic
c
connecting
\xi
and
\eta
. It’s easy to see that the function
\beta_{p}(\xi,\eta)
does not depend on the choice of
c
and
q
.
In geometric language, the Gromov product
\beta_{p}(\xi,\eta)
is the length of the part of a geodesic

c
between the horospheres
H_{\xi}(p)
and
H_{\eta}(p)
.

Then
\overline{\mu}
induces a
\phi
-invariant measure
m
on
SX
with

(4)

m(A):=\int_{\mathcal{I}^{P}}\text{Vol}\{\pi(P^{-1}(\xi,\eta)\cap A)\}d\overline{\mu}(\xi,\eta),

for all Borel sets
A\subset SX
. Here
\pi:SX\rightarrow X
is the standard projection map and
Vol is the induced volume form on
\pi(P^{-1}(\xi,\eta))
.
If there are more than one geodesics connecting
\xi
and
\eta
,
and one of them has rank
k\geq 1
, then by the flat strip theorem, all of these connecting geodesics have rank
k
.
Thus,
\pi(P^{-1}(\xi,\eta))
is exactly the
k
-flat submanifold connecting
\xi
and
\eta
,
which consists of all the rank
k
geodesics connecting
\xi
and
\eta
.

For any Borel set
A\subset SX
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\text{Vol}\{\pi(P^{-1}(\xi,\eta)\cap\phi^{t}A)\}=\text{Vol}\{\pi(P^{-1}(\xi,\eta)\cap A)\}
.
Therefore
m
is
\phi
-invariant. Moreover,
\Gamma
-invariance of
\overline{\mu}
leads to the
\Gamma
-invariance of
m
.
Thus
m
induced a
\phi
-invariant measure on
SM
by

m(A)=\int_{SM}\#(\text{pr}^{-1}({\underline{v}})\cap A)d{\underline{m}}({\underline{v}}).

It is proved in
[
30
]
that
{\underline{m}}
is unique MME, which is called Knieper measure.
Furthermore,
{\underline{m}}
is proved to be mixing in
[
29
]
and Kolmogorov in
[
10
]
. In the appendix, we prove that
{\underline{m}}
is Bernoulli.

2.4.
Local Product flow boxes

From now on we fix
v_{0}\in SX
such that
{\underline{v}}_{0}:=\mathop{\hbox{{\rm pr}}}v_{0}
is a periodic and regular vector in
SM
. Let
p:=\pi(v_{0})
, which will be the reference point in the following discussions. For simplicity, we will suppress
v_{0}
and
p
from the notation. We also fix a scale
\epsilon\in(0,\min\{\frac{1}{8},\frac{{\text{inj}}(M)}{4}\})
.

The
Hopf map

H:SX\to{\partial X}\times{\partial X}\times{\mathbb{R}}
for
p\in X
is defined as

H(v):=(v^{-},v^{+},s(v)),\text{\ where\ }s(v):=b_{v^{-}}(\pi v,p).

From definition, we see

s(\phi^{t}v)=s(v)+t

for any
v\in SX
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
s
is continuous by Corollary
2.7
.

Following
[
13
]
, we define for each
\theta>0
and
0<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}\epsilon
,

\displaystyle\textbf{P}=\textbf{P}_{\theta}:=\{w^{-}:w\in S_{p}X\text{\ and\ }\angle_{p}(w,v_{0})\leq\theta\},

\displaystyle\textbf{F}=\textbf{F}_{\theta}:=\{w^{+}:w\in S_{p}X\text{\ and\ }\angle_{p}(w,v_{0})\leq\theta\},

\displaystyle B=B_{\theta}^{\alpha}:=H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times[0,\alpha]),

\displaystyle S=S_{\theta}:=B_{\theta}^{\epsilon^{2}}=H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times[0,\epsilon^{2}]).

B=B_{\theta}^{\alpha}
is called a flow
box
with depth
\alpha
, and
S=S_{\theta}
is a
slice
with depth
\epsilon^{2}
. We will consider
\theta>0
small enough, which will be specified in the following.

The following lemma is a variation of
[
44
, Lemma 1]
, which was crucial in constructing Knieper measure.

Proposition 2.12
.

(
[
30
, Propositions 6 and 7]
)
Let
X
be a simply connected manifold without focal points and
v_{0}\in SX
is regular. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is an
\theta_{1}>0
such that, for any
\xi\in\textbf{P}_{\theta_{1}}
and
\eta\in\textbf{F}_{\theta_{1}}
,
there is a unique geodesic
c_{\xi,\eta}
connecting
\xi
and
\eta
, i.e.,

c_{\xi,\eta}(-\infty)=\xi
and
c_{\xi,\eta}(+\infty)=\eta
.

Moreover, the geodesic
c_{\xi,\eta}
is regular and
d(c_{v}(0),c_{\xi,\eta})<\epsilon/10
.

Based on Proposition
2.12
, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
v_{0},p,\epsilon
be as above and
\theta_{1}
be given in Proposition
2.12
. Then for any
0<\theta<\theta_{1}
,

(1)

\text{diam\ }\pi H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
;

(2)

H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})\subset SX
is compact;

(3)

\text{diam\ }\pi B_{\theta}^{\alpha}<4\epsilon
for any
0<\alpha\leq\frac{3\epsilon}{2}
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary of (1), that is, there exist
\theta_{n}\to 0
,
v_{n},w_{n}\in H^{-1}(\textbf{P}_{\theta_{n}}\times\textbf{F}_{\theta_{n}}\times\{0\})
such that
d(\pi v_{n},\pi w_{n})\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.

By Proposition
2.12
, for
n
large enough, we know that
d(p,c_{v_{n}})<\frac{\epsilon}{8}
and
d(p,c_{w_{n}})<\frac{\epsilon}{8}
. Let
v_{n}^{\prime}=\phi^{t_{n}}v_{n}
and
w_{n}^{\prime}=\phi^{s_{n}}w_{n}
be such that
\pi v^{\prime}_{n}
and
\pi w^{\prime}_{n}
are the projections of
p
onto
c_{v_{n}}
and
c_{w_{n}}
respectively. So we have

\displaystyle|t_{n}|=|b_{v_{n}^{-}}(\pi v_{n}^{\prime},p)|\leq d(\pi v_{n}^{\prime},p)<\frac{\epsilon}{10},

\displaystyle|s_{n}|=|b_{w_{n}^{-}}(\pi w_{n}^{\prime},p)|\leq d(\pi w_{n}^{\prime},p)<\frac{\epsilon}{10}.

By passing to a subsequence, we can assume that
v_{n}^{\prime}\to v,w_{n}^{\prime}\to w
. Notice that as
\theta_{n}\to 0
, we know that
v^{+}=w^{+}=(v_{0})^{+}
and
v^{-}=w^{-}=(v_{0})^{-}
. Thus by Proposition
2.12

v=\phi^{t}v_{0}
and
w=\phi^{s}v_{0}
for some
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
0\leq|s|,|t|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{10}
. So
d(\pi v,\pi w)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{5}
.

On the other hand, we know by the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle d(\pi v,\pi w)=\lim_{n\to\infty}d(\pi v^{\prime}_{n},\pi w^{\prime}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(d(\pi v_{n},\pi w_{n})-d(\pi v_{n},\pi v^{\prime}_{n})-d(\pi w_{n},\pi w^{\prime}_{n}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(\frac{\epsilon}{2}-|t_{n}|-|s_{n}|)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{10}-\frac{\epsilon}{10}>\frac{\epsilon}{5}.

We arrive at a contradiction. So
\text{diam\ }\pi H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.

By the continuity of
H
,
H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})\subset SX
is closed. As the diameter of its projection under
\pi
is no more than
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
, we know
H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})\subset SX
is bounded. Thus it is compact.

Finally, by the triangle inequality, for any
0<\alpha\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
, we have
\text{diam\ }\pi B_{\theta}^{\alpha}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+2\alpha<4\epsilon
.
∎

At last, we also assume that the choice of
\theta\in(0,\theta_{1})
satisfies the following properties. Since
\theta\mapsto\bar{\mu}(\textbf{P}_{\theta}\times\textbf{F}_{\theta})
is nondecreasing and hence has at most countably many discontinuities, we always choose
\theta
to be the continuity point of this function, i.e.,

\lim_{\rho\to\theta}\bar{\mu}(\textbf{P}_{\rho}\times\textbf{F}_{\rho})=\bar{\mu}(\textbf{P}_{\theta}\times\textbf{F}_{\theta}).

Furthermore,
\theta
is chosen so that
\bar{\mu}(\partial\textbf{P}_{\theta}\times\partial\textbf{F}_{\theta})=0.
By the product structure of
B
and
S
and the definition of
m
, we have for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{3\epsilon}{2})
,

(5)

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\theta}m(S_{\rho})=m(S_{\theta}),\quad\lim_{\rho\to\theta}m(B^{\alpha}_{\rho})=m(B^{\alpha}_{\theta}),\text{\ \ and\ \ }m(\partial B^{\alpha}_{\theta})=0.

The following is a direct corollary of Lemma
2.9
.

Corollary 2.14
.

Given
v_{0},p,\epsilon>0
as above, there exists
\theta_{2}>0
such that for any
0<\theta<\theta_{2}
, if
\xi,\eta\in\textbf{P}_{\theta}
and any
q
lying within
4\epsilon
of
\pi H^{-1}(\textbf{P}_{\theta}\times\textbf{F}_{\theta}\times[0,\infty))
, we have
|b_{\xi}(q,p)-b_{\eta}(q,p)|<\epsilon^{2}
. Similar result holds if the roles of
\textbf{P}_{\theta}
and
\textbf{F}_{\theta}
are reversed.

Let
\theta_{0}:=\min\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
, where
\theta_{1}
is given in Lemma
2.13
,
\theta_{2}
is given in Corollary
2.14
. In the following, we always suppose that
0<\theta<\theta_{0}
and (
5
) is satisfied.

3.
Closing lemma

The aim of this section is to establish a type of closing lemma
3.11
. We need study the properties of angle metric and prove a weak
\pi
-convergence theorem
3.9
.

3.1.
Angle metric

The
angle metric
on
\partial X
is defined as

\angle(\xi,\eta):=\sup_{x\in X}\angle_{x}(\xi,\eta),\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in{\partial X}.

Then the angle metric defines a path metric
d_{T}
on
{\partial X}
, called the
Tits metric
. Clearly,
d_{T}\geq\angle
.
The angle metric induces a topology on
\partial X
finer than the visual topology.

Since
\angle
is a supremum of a continuous function, it is lower semicontinuous:

Lemma 3.1
.

(Cf.
[
49
, Proposition 2.7]
)
The angle metric is lower semicontinuous, i.e., if
\xi_{n}\to\xi
and
\eta_{n}\to\eta
in the visual topology, then

\angle(\xi,\eta)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\angle(\xi_{n},\eta_{n}).

Using Lemma
2.1
and the first variation formula, we get the following fact, see
[
17
, Lemma 1.1]
for another proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

(Cf.
[
49
, Lemma 2.9]
and
[
17
, Lemma 1.1]
)
Let
v\in SX
point at
\xi\in{\partial X}
, and let
\eta\in{\partial X}
. Then

t\mapsto\angle_{c_{v}(t)}(\xi,\eta)

is a nondecreasing function. Equivalently,

\angle_{c_{v}(t_{1})}(c(\infty),\eta)+\angle_{c_{v}(t_{2})}(c(-\infty),\eta)\leq\pi

for any
t_{1}<t_{2}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
v\in SX
point at
\xi\in{\partial X}
, and let
\eta\in{\partial X}
. Then

\angle(\xi,\eta)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\angle_{c_{v}(t)}(\xi,\eta).

Proof.

The above limit exists by Lemma
3.2
. Let
q\in X
and
t_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Denote by
v_{n}:=g^{t_{n}}v,p_{n}:=\pi v_{n}
. Let
c_{n}
be the unique geodesic from
q
to
p_{n}
. Write
v_{n}^{\prime}=\dot{c}_{n}(d(q,p_{n}))
.

We claim that
\angle_{p_{n}}(v_{n},v_{n}^{\prime})\to 0
. Assuming the claim, then by Lemmas
3.2
and
2.4
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\angle_{p_{n}}(\eta,v_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\angle_{p_{n}}(\eta,v^{\prime}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\angle_{q}(\eta,\dot{c}_{n}(0))=\angle_{q}(\eta,\xi).

Since
q
is arbitrary, the proposition follows.

It remains to prove the claim. Assume the contrary for contradiction, i.e.,
\angle_{p_{n}}(v_{n},v_{n}^{\prime})\geq\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
by passing to a subsequence. Fix a fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}\subset X
containing
\pi v
.
Let
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
be such that
\gamma_{n}p_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is compact, by passing to a subsequence, we assume that
\gamma_{n}p_{n}\to p\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Define
\sigma_{n}
to be the geodesic from
\gamma_{n}p_{n}
to
\gamma_{n}\pi v
, and
\tau_{n}
to be the geodesic from
\gamma_{n}p_{n}
to
\gamma_{n}q
. Denote
s_{n}:=d(\gamma_{n}p_{n},\gamma_{n}q)
and recall that
t_{n}=d(\gamma_{n}p_{n},\gamma_{n}\pi v)
. By the triangle inequality,
|s_{n}-t_{n}|\leq d(\gamma_{n}\pi v,\gamma_{n}q)=d(\pi v,q)
. So
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}=\infty
. Without loss of generality, assume
s_{n}:=\min\{s_{n},t_{n}\}
. For any
0\leq t\leq s_{n}
, by Lemma
2.1
(1),

d(\sigma_{n}(t),\tau_{n}(t))\leq d(\sigma_{n}(s_{n}),\tau_{n}(s_{n}))\leq 2d(\pi v,q).

Now by passing to a subsequence, suppose that
\dot{\sigma}_{n}(0)\to\dot{\sigma}(0)
and
\dot{\tau}_{n}(0)\to\dot{\tau}(0)
. Then
\sigma
and
\tau
are two geodesics starting from
p
with
\angle_{p}(\dot{\sigma}(0),\dot{\tau}(0))\geq\alpha>0
and
d(\sigma(t),\tau(t))\leq 2d(\pi v,q)
for any
t>0
, which is a contradiction to Lemma
2.1
(1). This proves the claim and the proposition.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
X/\Gamma
is not compact, the above proposition holds if
v\in SX
is assumed to be recurrent (cf.
[
49
, Corollary 2.11]
). Our proof above uses the compactness of
X/\Gamma
and works for arbitrary
v\in SX
.

The following lemma is a rigidity counterpart of Lemma
3.2
. It is a fundamental difference from the nonpositive curvature case that the geodesic considered in the lemma is required to be recurrent. We do not know if the recurrence assumption can be removed.

Lemma 3.5
.

(
[
49
, Corollary 6.8]
)
Let
c
be a recurrent geodesic, and suppose there exists
\xi\in{\partial X}
such that

\angle_{c(t)}(\xi,c(\infty))=\theta
for all
t
and some
0<\theta<\pi
. Then
c
is the boundary of a flat half plane.

Remark 3.6
.

In nonpositive curvature case, there is a much easier proof of the above lemma (without the recurrence assumption) using the following rigidity fact: If
t_{1}<t_{2}
and

\angle_{c(t_{1})}(\xi,c(\infty))+\angle_{c(t_{2})}(\xi,c(-\infty))=\pi,

then the infinite triangle with vertices
c(t_{1}),c(t_{1}),x
is flat. This rigidity fact does not hold for general manifolds without foal points.

The following proposition on Tits metric will not be used in subsequent discussions, but may have independent interest.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
c
be a recurrent geodesic, not the boundary of a flat half plane. Then

d_{T}(c(-\infty),c(\infty))>\pi.

Proof.

At first, we have

d_{T}(c(-\infty),c(\infty))\geq\angle(c(-\infty),c(\infty))\geq\angle_{c(0)}(c(-\infty),c(\infty))=\pi.

Assume for contradiction that

d_{T}(c(-\infty),c(\infty))=\pi.
Recall that
({\partial X},d_{T})
is a length space. It follows that there exist points
\eta_{i}\in{\partial X}
such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}d_{T}(\eta_{i},c(-\infty))=\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}d_{T}(\eta_{i},c(\infty))=\frac{\pi}{2}
. The sequence
\eta_{i}
has a limit point
\eta\in{\partial X}
in the visual topology. By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\angle(\eta,c(-\infty))\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\angle(\eta,c(-\infty))\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
,
and hence by the triangle inequality,

\angle(\eta,c(-\infty))=\angle(\eta,c(-\infty))=\frac{\pi}{2}.

For any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\angle_{c(t)}(\eta,c(-\infty))+\angle_{c(t)}(\eta,c(\infty))\geq\angle_{c(t)}(c(\infty),c(-\infty))=\pi.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\angle_{c(t)}(\eta,c(-\infty))\leq\angle(\eta,c(-\infty))=\frac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle\angle_{c(t)}(\eta,c(\infty))\leq\angle(\eta,c(\infty))=\frac{\pi}{2}.

It follows that
\angle_{c(t)}(\eta,c(\infty))=\frac{\pi}{2}
for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Applying Lemma
3.5
, we know that
c
is the boundary of a half plane, a contradiction. This proves the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

In nonpositive curvature case, we have
d_{T}(\xi,\eta)=\angle(\xi,\eta)
if there is no geodesic connecting
\xi
and
\eta
, and thus

\angle(\xi,\eta)=\min\{d_{T}(\xi,\eta),\pi\}.

The proof uses comparison results due to the nonpositive curvature. We do not know how to prove the above fact for manifolds without focal points, since the comparison results were absent.

3.2.
Weak
\pi
-convergence theorem

The following is a weak form of
\pi
-convergence theorem for manifolds without focal points. It is not known if the
\pi
-convergence theorem
[
36
, Lemma 19]
for CAT
(0)
space can be extended to manifolds without focal points, since many properties of Tits metric (like the one discussed in Remark
3.8
) are absent.

Theorem 3.9

(Weak
\pi
-convergence theorem)
.

Let
X
be a simply connected manifold without focal points,
v_{0},p,\epsilon
be fixed as in Section
2.4
, and
\theta_{1}
be given in Proposition
2.12
. Fix any
0<\rho<\theta<\theta_{1}
.

Suppose that
x\in X
, and
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{i}(x)\to p\in\textbf{F}_{\rho}
and
\gamma^{-1}_{i}(x)\to n\in\textbf{P}_{\rho}
as
i\to\infty
. Then for any open set
U
with
U\supset\textbf{F}_{\rho}
,
\gamma_{i}(\textbf{F}_{\theta})\subset U
for all
i
sufficiently large.

To prove the theorem, we need the following lemma. Denote
[x,n):=c_{0}([0,\infty))
where
c_{0}
is a geodesic ray from
x
and pointing at
n
, and similarly
(p,x]:=c_{1}((-\infty,0])
where
c_{1}
is a geodesic ray from
x
and pointing at
p
.

Lemma 3.10
.

For any
\tau>0
, there exists a point
y\in[x,n)
such that
\angle_{y}(n,c)>\pi-\tau
for all
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary. Then there is a monotone sequence
\{y_{i}\}\subset[x,n)
and a sequence of points
\{c_{i}\}\subset\textbf{F}_{\theta}
such that
y_{i}\to n
with
\angle_{y_{i}}(n,c_{i})\leq\pi-\tau
. By Lemma
3.2
, we know
\angle_{y_{i}}(n,c_{k})\leq\pi-\tau
for all
k\geq i
. Since
\textbf{F}_{\theta}
is compact, by passing to a subsequence, we assume that
c_{k}\to c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
. For any fixed
i
, the sequence of geodesic rays
[y_{i},c_{k})
converge to
[y_{i},c)
as
n\to\infty
. Thus
\angle_{y_{i}}(n,c_{k})\to\angle_{y_{i}}(n,c)\leq\pi-\tau
. Since this is true for any
y_{i}\to n
, it follows from Proposition
3.3
that
\angle(n,c)\leq\pi-\tau
. On the other hand, by Proposition
2.12
,
c
and
n
are connected by a geodesic, and thus
\angle(n,c)\geq\pi
. A contradiction and the lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

Let
U\supset\textbf{F}_{\rho}
be open in
{\partial X}
. Fix
x\in X
. For any
\tau>0
, Lemma
3.10
tells that there exists
y\in[x,n)
such that
\angle_{y}(n,c)>\pi-\tau
for all
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
.

Note that the geodesic segments
[x,\gamma_{i}^{-1}x]
converge to the geodesic ray
[x,n)
. For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
let
y_{i}
be the projection of
y
on
[x,\gamma_{i}^{-1}x]
. Notice that
\gamma_{i}^{-1}x\to n
as
i\to\infty
. So we may assume that
d(y_{i},\gamma_{i}^{-1}(x))\geq 1
. Let
z_{i}\in[y_{i},\gamma_{i}^{-1}x]
such that
d(y_{i},z_{i})=1
. It is clear that
z_{i}\to z\in[y,n)
with
d(y,z)=1
(see
[
36
, p. 569]
) for the figure).

By Lemma
2.4
and uniform continuity, we have for any
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
,

\angle_{y_{i}}(z_{i},c)\to\angle_{y}(n,c)>\pi-\tau.

So for
i
sufficiently large,
\angle_{y_{i}}(z_{i},c)>\pi-\tau
for any
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
.

Now applying the action of
\gamma_{i}
to the above geodesic segments, we have
\gamma_{i}([x,\gamma_{i}^{-1}x])=[\gamma_{i}x,x]\to(p,x]
as
i\to\infty
. Moreover, for
i
sufficiently large,
\angle_{\gamma_{i}y_{i}}(\gamma_{i}z_{i},\gamma_{i}c)=\angle_{y_{i}}(z_{i},c)>\pi-\tau
for any
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
.

Let
u\in(p,x]
, and for each
i
let
u_{i}
be the projection of
u
to
[\gamma_{i}(x),x]
.
For
i
sufficiently large,
u_{i}\in[\gamma_{i}(x),x]
and
d(u_{i},\gamma_{i}z_{i})>1
. So there exists
v_{i}\in[\gamma_{i}z_{i},u_{i}]
such that
d(v_{i},u_{i})=1
. By Lemma
3.2
,

\angle_{\gamma_{i}y_{i}}(\gamma_{i}z_{i},\gamma_{i}c)+\angle_{u_{i}}(v_{i},\gamma_{i}c)\leq\pi

for all
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
and
i
sufficiently large. It follows that

\angle_{u_{i}}(v_{i},\gamma_{i}c)<\tau

for all
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
and
i
sufficiently large.

Note that
v_{i}\to v
for some
v\in(p,u]
with
d(v,u)=1
. By Lemma
2.4
, for
i
sufficiently large,

\angle_{u}(p,\gamma_{i}c)=\angle_{u}(v,\gamma_{i}c)<\tau.

That is, for any
\tau>0
and any
u\in(p,x]
, there is
i_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
i\geq i_{0}
and all
c\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
,
\angle_{u}(p,\gamma_{i}c)<\tau.

We claim that
\gamma_{i}\textbf{F}_{\theta}\subset U
for all
i
sufficiently large. Assume the contrary. Then by passing to a subsequence, for each
i
there exists
c_{i}\in\textbf{F}_{\theta}
such that
\gamma_{i}c_{i}\notin U
. Again by passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\gamma_{i}c_{i}\to\hat{c}\in{\partial X}\setminus U
. So
\angle(p,\hat{c})>0
. Pick any
0<\tau<\angle(p,\hat{c})
. By Lemma
3.3
, there exists
w\in(p,x]
such that
\angle_{w}(p,\hat{c})>\tau
. However, by the last paragraph,
\angle_{w}(p,\gamma_{i}c_{i})<\tau
for
i
large enough. By continuity in Lemma
2.4
, we arrive at a contradiction. This proves the claim and the theorem.
∎

3.3.
Closing lemma

In the next section, we will count the number of elements in certain subsets of
\Gamma
. Let us collect the definitions here for convenience.

\displaystyle\Gamma(t,\alpha)=\Gamma_{\theta}(t,\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:S_{\theta}\cap\phi^{-t}\gamma_{*}B_{\theta}^{\alpha}\neq\emptyset\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{*}=\Gamma^{*}_{\theta}

\displaystyle:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\gamma\textbf{F}_{\theta}\subset\textbf{F}_{\theta}\text{\ and\ }\gamma^{-1}\textbf{P}_{\theta}\subset\textbf{P}_{\theta}\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{*}(t,\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\Gamma^{*}\cap\Gamma(t,\alpha),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}(t,\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}(t,\alpha):\gamma\neq\beta^{n}\text{\ for any\ }\beta\in\Gamma,n\geq 2\}.

Lemma 3.11

(Closing lemma)
.

Let
X
be a simply connected manifold without focal points,
v_{0},p,\epsilon
be fixed as in Section
2.4
, and
\theta_{1}
be given in Proposition
2.12
. Then for every
0<\rho<\theta<\theta_{1}
, there exists some
t_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geq t_{0}
, we have
\Gamma_{\rho}(t,\alpha)\subset\Gamma_{\theta}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
U
be the interior of
\textbf{F}_{\theta}
. Then
\textbf{F}_{\rho}\subset U
. We claim that there exists some
t_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geq t_{0}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, if
S_{\rho}\cap\phi^{-t}\gamma B_{\rho}^{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
, then
\gamma(\textbf{F}_{\theta})\subset U
.

Let us prove the claim. Assume not. Then for each
i
, there exist
t_{i}\to\infty
and
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma
such that
v_{i}\in S_{\rho}\cap\phi^{-t_{i}}\gamma_{i}B_{\rho}^{\alpha}
, but
\gamma_{i}(\textbf{F}_{\theta})\nsubseteq U
. Clearly, for any
x\in X
,
\gamma_{i}x
goes to infinity. By passing to a subsequence, let us assume that
\gamma_{i}x\to\xi\in{\partial X}
.

Let
v_{i}\in S_{\rho}\cap\phi^{-t_{i}}\gamma_{i}B_{\rho}^{\alpha}
. By Lemma
2.13
,
S_{\rho}
and
B_{\rho}^{\alpha}
are both compact. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
v_{i}\to v\in S_{\rho}
and
\gamma_{i}^{-1}\phi^{t_{i}}v_{i}\to w\in B_{\rho}^{\alpha}
. Note that
\gamma_{i}\pi w\to\xi\in{\partial X}
. Since
d(\gamma_{i}w,\phi^{t_{i}}v_{i})\to 0
, we have
\xi=\lim_{i}\pi\phi^{t_{i}}v_{i}\in\textbf{F}_{\rho}
.

We may assume that
\gamma_{i}^{-1}\pi v\to\eta\in{\partial X}
. Let
w_{i}=\gamma_{i}^{-1}\phi^{t_{i}}v_{i}\in B_{\rho}^{\alpha}
. Then
d(\gamma_{i}^{-1}v,\phi^{-t_{i}}w_{i})=d(\gamma_{i}^{-1}v,\gamma_{i}^{-1}v_{i})\to 0
, and thus
d(\gamma_{i}^{-1}\pi v,\pi\phi^{-t_{i}}w_{i})\to 0
. We then see that
\eta=\lim_{i}\pi\phi^{-t_{i}}w_{i}\in\textbf{P}_{\rho}
.

Now we have
\gamma_{i}\pi v\to\xi\in\textbf{F}_{\rho}
and
\gamma_{i}^{-1}\pi v\to\eta\in\textbf{P}_{\rho}
. Applying Theorem
3.9
, we have
\gamma_{i}(\textbf{F}_{\theta})\subset U
for all
i
sufficiently large. A contradiction and the claim follows.

By the claim, there exists some
t_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geq t_{0}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\rho}(t,\alpha)
, we have
\gamma\textbf{F}_{\theta}\subset U\subset\textbf{F}_{\theta}
.

Analogously, by reversing the roles of
P
and
F
,
S_{\rho}
and
B_{\rho}^{\alpha}
, and the roles of
\gamma
and
\gamma^{-1}
, we can prove that
\gamma^{-1}\textbf{P}_{\theta}\subset\textbf{P}_{\theta}
. Thus
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\theta}^{*}
and the proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

4.2.
Scaling and mixing calculation

We use the following notations in the asymptotic estimates.

\displaystyle f(t)=e^{\pm C}g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow e^{-C}g(t)\leq f(t)\leq e^{C}g(t)\text{\ for all\ }t;

\displaystyle f(t)\lesssim g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\leq 1;

\displaystyle f(t)\gtrsim g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\geq 1;

\displaystyle f(t)\sim g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}=1;

\displaystyle f(t)\sim e^{\pm C}g(t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow e^{-C}g(t)\lesssim f(t)\lesssim e^{C}g(t).

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}
, then

m(S^{\gamma})=e^{\pm 2h\epsilon}e^{-h|\gamma|}m(S).

Proof.

The proof is completely parallel to that in
[
13
, Lemma 5.2]
. The main work is to estimate
\beta_{p}(\xi,\eta)
and
b_{\eta}(\gamma^{-1}p,p)
given
\xi\in\textbf{P},\eta\in\textbf{F}
.

Firstly, take
q
lying on the geodesic connecting
\xi
and
\eta
such that
b_{\xi}(q,p)=0
. Then

|\beta_{p}(\xi,\eta)|=|b_{\xi}(q,p)+b_{\eta}(q,p)|=|b_{\eta}(q,p)|\leq d(q,p)<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

where we used Lemma
2.13
in the last inequality.

Secondly, since
\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}
, we know
\gamma^{-1}
has a rank one axis
c
. By Lemma
4.2
,
b_{c(-\infty)}(\gamma^{-1}p,p)=|\gamma^{-1}|=|\gamma|
. Then by Corollary
2.14
, we have
|b_{\eta}(\gamma^{-1}p,p)-|\gamma||<\epsilon^{2}
for any
\eta\in\textbf{F}
.

Notice that since
\gamma\textbf{F}\subset\textbf{F}
, we have
\gamma\eta\in\textbf{F}
if
\eta\in\textbf{F}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{m(S^{\gamma})}{m(S)}=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{2}\bar{\mu}(\textbf{P}\times\gamma\textbf{F})}{\epsilon^{2}\bar{\mu}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F})}=\frac{e^{\pm h\epsilon/2}\mu_{p}(\textbf{P})\mu_{p}(\gamma\textbf{F})}{e^{\pm h\epsilon/2}\mu_{p}(\textbf{P})\mu_{p}(\textbf{F})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\pm h\epsilon}\frac{\mu_{\gamma^{-1}p}(\textbf{F})}{\mu_{p}(\textbf{F})}=e^{\pm h\epsilon}\frac{\int_{\textbf{F}}e^{-hb_{\eta}(\gamma^{-1}p,p)}d\mu_{p}(\eta)}{\mu_{p}(\textbf{F})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\pm h\epsilon}\epsilon^{\pm h\epsilon^{2}}e^{-h|\gamma|}=e^{\pm 2h\epsilon}e^{-h|\gamma|}.

∎

Combining Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.6
, we have

Corollary 4.7

(Scaling)
.

Given
\alpha\leq\frac{3}{2}\epsilon
and
\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}(t,\alpha)
, we have
|t-|\gamma||\leq 2\epsilon
, and thus

m(S^{\gamma})=e^{\pm 4h\epsilon}e^{-ht}m(S).

Remark 4.8
.

It is clear that the conclusions in Lemma
4.6
and Corollary
4.7
hold if
m,S,S^{\gamma}
are replaced by
{\underline{m}},{\underline{S}},{{\underline{S}}}^{\gamma}
respectively.

Finally, we combine scaling and mixing properties of Knieper measure to obtain the following asymptotic estimates. The proof is a repetition of
[
13
, Section 5.2]
. Since it is a key step, we provide a detailed proof here.

Proposition 4.9
.

We have

\displaystyle e^{-4h\epsilon}\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\#\Gamma^{*}_{\theta}(t,\alpha)}{e^{ht}m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\lesssim e^{4h\epsilon}(1+\frac{4\epsilon^{2}}{\alpha}),

\displaystyle e^{-4h\epsilon}\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\#\Gamma_{\theta}(t,\alpha)}{e^{ht}m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\lesssim e^{4h\epsilon}(1+\frac{4\epsilon^{2}}{\alpha}).

Proof.

Recall that
\alpha\in(0,\frac{3\epsilon}{2}]
. By Lemmas
3.11
and
4.5
, for any
0<\rho<\theta
and
t
large enough, we have

{\underline{S}}_{\rho}\cap\phi^{-t}{\underline{B}}_{\rho}^{\alpha}\subset\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}_{\theta}(t,\alpha)}{\underline{S}}_{\theta}^{\gamma}\subset{\underline{S}}_{\theta}\cap\phi^{-(t+2\epsilon^{2})}{\underline{B}}_{\theta}^{\alpha+4\epsilon^{2}}.

By Corollary
4.7
,
{\underline{m}}({\underline{S}}_{\theta}^{\gamma})=e^{\pm 4h\epsilon}e^{-ht}{\underline{m}}({\underline{S}}_{\theta}).
Thus we have

\displaystyle e^{-4h\epsilon}{\underline{m}}({\underline{S}}_{\rho}\cap\phi^{-t}{\underline{B}}_{\rho}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\#\Gamma^{*}_{\theta}(t,\alpha)e^{-ht}{\underline{m}}({\underline{S}}_{\theta})

\displaystyle\leq e^{4h\epsilon}{\underline{m}}({\underline{S}}_{\theta}\cap\phi^{-(t+2\epsilon^{2})}{\underline{B}}_{\theta}^{\alpha+4\epsilon^{2}}).

Dividing by
{\underline{m}}({\underline{S}}_{\theta}){\underline{m}}({\underline{B}}_{\theta}^{\alpha})
and using mixing of
{\underline{m}}
, we get

(6)

\displaystyle e^{-4h\epsilon}\frac{m(S_{\rho})m(B_{\rho}^{\alpha})}{m(S_{\theta})m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\lesssim\frac{\#\Gamma^{*}_{\theta}(t,\alpha)}{e^{ht}m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\lesssim e^{4h\epsilon}\frac{m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha+4\epsilon^{2}})}{m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}.

By (
5
), letting
\rho\nearrow\theta
, we obtain the first equation in the proposition.

To prove the second equation, we consider
\theta<\rho<\theta_{0}
. Then by Lemma
3.11
,
\Gamma^{*}_{\theta}(t,\alpha)\subset\Gamma_{\theta}(t,\alpha)\subset\Gamma^{*}_{\rho}(t,\alpha).
By (
6
),

\displaystyle e^{-4h\epsilon}\frac{m(S_{\rho})m(B_{\rho}^{\alpha})}{m(S_{\theta})m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\#\Gamma^{*}_{\theta}(t,\alpha)}{e^{ht}m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\lesssim\frac{\#\Gamma_{\theta}(t,\alpha)}{e^{ht}m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\#\Gamma^{*}_{\rho}(t,\alpha)}{e^{ht}m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}\lesssim e^{4h\epsilon}\frac{m(B_{\rho}^{\alpha+4\epsilon^{2}})}{m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}.

Letting
\rho\searrow\theta
and by (

), we get the second equation in the proposition.
∎

5.
Measuring along periodic orbits

Recall that
C(t)
is any maximal set of pairwise non-free-homotopic closed geodesics with length
(t-\epsilon,t]
in
M
. We obtain in this section an upper bound and a lower bound respectively for
\#C(t)
.

5.1.
Upper bound for
\#C(t)

By definition of
\nu_{t}
, we have

\#C(t)=\frac{\sum_{{\underline{c}}\in C(t)}Leb_{\underline{c}}({\underline{B}}_{\theta}^{\alpha})}{t\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}_{\theta}^{\alpha})}.

Define

\Pi(t):=\{\dot{{\underline{c}}}(s)\in\mathop{\hbox{{\rm pr}}}H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F})\times\{0\}:{\underline{c}}\in C(t),s\in{\mathbb{R}}\}.

Then we have

(7)

\displaystyle\#C(t)=\frac{\alpha\#\Pi(t)}{t\nu(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})}.

Now we define a map
\Theta:\Pi(t)\to\Gamma(t,\epsilon)
as follows. Given
{\underline{v}}\in\Pi(t)
, let
\ell=\ell({\underline{v}})\in(t-\epsilon,t]
be such that
\phi^{\ell}{\underline{v}}={\underline{v}}
. Let
v
be the unique lift of
{\underline{v}}
such that
v\in H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F})\times\{0\}\subset B_{\theta}^{\alpha}
.
Define
\Theta(v)
to be the unique axial isometry of
X
such that
\phi^{\ell}v=\Theta(v)v
. Then
|\Theta(v)|=\ell
. If
\gamma=\Theta(v)
, then
\phi^{t}v=\phi^{t-\ell}\gamma v\in\gamma B_{\theta}^{\epsilon}
. So
v\in S_{\theta}\cap\phi^{-t}\gamma B_{\theta}^{\epsilon}
, and we get

(8)

\displaystyle\Theta(\Pi(t))\subset\Gamma(t,\epsilon).

To estimate
\#\Pi(t)
, we first show that
\Theta
is injective.

Lemma 5.1
.

\Theta
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\underline{v}},{\underline{w}}\in\Pi(t)
are such that
\Theta({\underline{v}})=\Theta({\underline{w}}):=\gamma
.
Let
v,w\in B_{\theta}^{\alpha}
be the lifts of
{\underline{v}},{\underline{w}}
respectively. Then by definition, both
c_{v}
and
c_{w}
are axes of
\gamma
.

We claim that
v^{+}=w^{+}
and
v^{-}=w^{-}
. Indeed, first notice that
\gamma^{n}c_{v}(0)\to v^{+}
and
\gamma^{n}c_{w}(0)\to w^{+}
. On the other hand,

d(\gamma^{n}c_{v}(0),\gamma^{n}c_{w}(0))=d(c_{v}(0),c_{w}(0))

for all
n\geq 0
. So
\gamma^{n}c_{v}(0)
and
\gamma^{n}c_{w}(0)
converge to the same point at infinity by the property of no focal points. Thus we get
v^{+}=w^{+}
. Similarly,
v^{-}=w^{-}
.

It follows that
c_{v}
and
c_{w}
are bi-asymptotic. If
c_{v}
and
c_{w}
are geometrically distinct, then they bound a flat strip by Lemma
2.1
(4). So
v
and
w
are singular vectors, which is a contradiction by the definition of
B_{\theta}^{\alpha}
and Proposition
2.12
. So
v
and
w
lie on a common geodesic. As
v,w\in H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})
, we have
v=w
and hence
{\underline{v}}={\underline{w}}
. So
\Theta
is injective.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

We have

\#C(t)\leq\frac{\epsilon\#\Gamma(t,\epsilon)}{t\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}^{\epsilon})}.

Proof.

The proposition follows from (
7
), (
8
) and Lemma
5.1
.
∎

5.2.
Lower bound for
\#C(t)

First we deal with the multiplicity of
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Given
\gamma\in\Gamma
, let
d=d(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{N}}
be maximal such that
\gamma=\beta^{d}
for some
\beta\in\Gamma
.
\gamma\in\Gamma
is called
primitive
if
d(\gamma)=1
, i.e.,
\gamma\neq\beta^{d}
for any
\beta\in\Gamma
and any

.

Define
\Gamma_{2}(\textbf{P},\textbf{F},t)
to be the set of all
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

(1)

\gamma
has an axis
c
with
c(-\infty)\in\textbf{P},c(\infty)\in\textbf{F}
;

(2)

|\gamma|\in(t-\epsilon,t]
;

(3)

.

Lemma 5.3
.

There exists
K>0
such that for any
t>0
we have

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{2}(\textbf{P},\textbf{F},t)}d(\gamma)\leq Ke^{\frac{2}{3}ht}.

Proof.

Since
X
is a simply connected manifold with no focal points, we have by
[
21
]

h=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}\log\text{Vol}(B(q,r))

where
B(q,r)
is the ball of radius
r
around
q
in
X
. So the proof is almost analogous to that in
[
13
, Lemma 4.5]
with only minor modifications as follows.

If
\gamma=\beta(\gamma)^{d(\gamma)}
for some
\beta(\gamma)\in\Gamma
, we must argue that
\beta(\gamma)
has an axis with endpoints in
P
and
F
, so that we can choose
v\in H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})
tangent to such an axis with
\phi^{|\beta(\gamma)|}v=\beta(\gamma)v
. We cannot use
[
13
, Lemma 2.10]
in our setting. Nevertheless, since
\gamma
has a rank one axis with endpoints in
P
and
F
, we know from the proof of Lemma
5.1
that this is the only axis for
\gamma
. Every axis of
\beta(\gamma)
is an axis of
\gamma
, so
\beta(\gamma)
also has a unique axis, which is the axis of
\gamma
, as we want.

Repeat the remaining part of the proof of
[

, Lemma 4.5]
and we are done.
∎

Recall that
\Gamma^{\prime}(t,\alpha):=\{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}(t,\alpha):\gamma\neq\beta^{n}\text{\ for any\ }\beta\in\Gamma,n\geq 2\}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Consider
\alpha=\epsilon-4\epsilon^{2}
. Then
\Theta(\Pi(t))\supset\Gamma^{\prime}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)
and

\#C(t)\geq\frac{\alpha\#\Gamma^{\prime}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)}{t\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)
.
Then there exists
v\in H^{-1}(\textbf{P}\times\textbf{F}\times\{0\})
such that
\phi^{|\gamma|}v=\gamma v
.
By Lemma
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle|\gamma|

\displaystyle\geq(t-2\epsilon^{2})-\alpha-\epsilon^{2}=t-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}>t-\epsilon,

\displaystyle|\gamma|

\displaystyle\leq(t-2\epsilon^{2})+2\epsilon^{2}=t.

It follows that
\underline{c}_{{\underline{v}}}
is a closed geodesic with length
|\gamma|\in(t-\epsilon,t]
.
Note that if
\underline{c}
is another closed geodesic in the free-homotopic class of
\underline{c}_{{\underline{v}}}
, then we can lift
\underline{c}
to a geodesic
c
such that
c
and
c_{v}
are bi-asymptotic. So
c
and
c_{v}
bound a flat strip by Lemma
2.1
(4), which is a contradiction since
v
is rank one. It follows that
\underline{c}_{{\underline{v}}}
is the only geodesic in its free-homotopic class. Thus
\underline{c}_{{\underline{v}}}\in C(t)
.

As a consequence,
v\in\Pi(t)
and
\gamma=\Theta(v)
. So
\Theta(\Pi(t))\supset\Gamma^{\prime}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)
and thus by (
7
),

\#C(t)\geq\frac{\alpha\#\Gamma^{\prime}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)}{t\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})}.

∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Consider
\alpha=\epsilon-4\epsilon^{2}
. We have

\#C(t)\geq\frac{\alpha}{t\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})}\cdot(\#\Gamma^{*}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)-Ke^{\frac{2}{3}ht}).

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
5.4
, we also see that

if

. Thus

\Gamma^{*}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)\subset\Gamma_{2}(\textbf{P},\textbf{F},t).

Then by Lemma

,

\#\Gamma^{*}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)-\#\Gamma^{\prime}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)\leq Ke^{\frac{2}{3}ht}.

This together with Lemma
5.4
proves the proposition.

6.
Equidistribution and completion of the proof

The following result is standard in ergodic theory, which is a corollary of the classical proof of variational principle
[
48
, Theorem 9.10]

Lemma 6.1
.

[
20
, Proposition 4.3.12]

Let
Y
be a compact metric space and
\phi
a continuous flow on
Y
. Fix
\epsilon>0
and suppose that
E_{t}\subset Y
is a
(t,\epsilon)
-separated set for all sufficiently large
t
. Define the measures
\mu_{t}
by

\mu_{t}(A):=\frac{1}{\#E_{t}}\sum_{v\in E_{t}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\chi_{A}(\phi^{s}v)ds.

If
t_{k}\to\infty
and the weak
∗
limit
\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{t_{k}}

exists, then

h_{\mu}(\phi^{1})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{k}}\log\#E_{t_{k}}.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

We claim that the set
\{\underline{\dot{c}}(0):c\in C(t)\}
is
(t,\epsilon)
-separated for any
0<\epsilon<{\text{inj}}(M)/2
.

Indeed, if it were not, then
C(t)
would contain two closed geodesics
\underline{c}_{1},\underline{c}_{2}
in distinct free-homotopic classes
such that
d(\underline{c}_{1}(s),\underline{c}_{2}(s))\leq\epsilon<{\text{inj}}(M)/2
for all
s\in[0,t]
. Define
{\underline{v}}=\underline{\dot{c}}_{1}(0)
and
{\underline{w}}=\underline{\dot{c}}_{2}(0)
.
We can lift
{\underline{v}},{\underline{w}}
to
v,w\in SX
, and
\underline{c}_{1},\underline{c}_{2}
to
c_{1},c_{2}
respectively, such that
d(c_{1}(s),c_{2}(s))\leq\epsilon
for all
s\in[0,t]
.
Moreover, there exist
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
and
t_{1},t_{2}\in(t-\epsilon,t]
such that
\gamma_{1}v=g^{t_{1}}v
and
\gamma_{2}w=g^{t_{2}}w
. Then

\displaystyle d(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}c_{1}(0),c_{2}(0))=

\displaystyle d(\gamma_{2}^{-1}c_{1}(t_{1}),\gamma_{2}^{-1}c_{2}(t_{2}))=d(c_{1}(t_{1}),c_{2}(t_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(c_{1}(t_{1}),c_{2}(t_{1}))+|t_{2}-t_{1}|.

Hence
d(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}c_{1}(0),c_{2}(0))\leq 3\epsilon<2{\text{inj}}(M)
, which is possible only if
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
. Then
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are both axes for a common
\gamma:=\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
. By the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we see that
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
must be bi-asymptotic and consequently bound a flat strip. It follows that
\underline{c}_{1}
and
\underline{c}_{2}
are free-homotopic. A contradiction, so the claim holds.

Now by Propositions
5.5
and
4.9
, we know

\displaystyle\#C(t)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{t\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})}\cdot(\#\Gamma^{*}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)-Ke^{\frac{2}{3}ht})

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{t\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})}\cdot(e^{-4h\epsilon}e^{ht}m(B_{\theta}^{\alpha})-Ke^{\frac{2}{3}ht}).

So
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\#C(t)\geq h.
Applying Lemma
6.1
, we know any limit measure of
\nu_{t}
has entropy equal to
h
, and thus it must be

, the unique MME. This proves Theorem
B
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

We have

\#C(t)\sim e^{\pm Q\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{t}e^{ht}

where
Q>0
is a universal constant depending only on

.

Proof.

By Theorem
B
and (
5
), we have
\nu_{t}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})\to{\underline{m}}({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})=m(B^{\alpha})
for

and
\alpha=\epsilon-4\epsilon^{2}
.

Consider
\alpha=\epsilon
. By Propositions

and

, we have

\#C(t)\lesssim\frac{\epsilon\#\Gamma(t,\epsilon)}{tm({\underline{B}}^{\epsilon})}\lesssim e^{4h\epsilon}(1+4\epsilon)\frac{\epsilon}{t}e^{ht}.

Now consider
\alpha=\epsilon-4\epsilon^{2}
. By Propositions
5.5
and
4.9
,

\displaystyle\#C(t)\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{tm({\underline{B}}^{\alpha})}\cdot(\#\Gamma^{*}(t-2\epsilon^{2},\alpha)-Ke^{\frac{2}{3}ht})

\displaystyle(1-4\epsilon)e^{-4h\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{t}e^{-2h\epsilon^{2}}e^{ht}.

As

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

The last step of the proof of Theorem

7.
Appendix: Bernoulli property of Knieper measure

We prove Theorem
C
in this appendix. To start, we recall some necessary definitions.

Definition 7.1

(Kolmogorov property)
.

Let
T:X\to X
be an invertible measure-preserving transformation in a Lebesgue space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
. We say that
T
has the
Kolmogorov property
, or that
T
is
Kolmogorov
, if there is a sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{B}
which satisfies

(1)

\mathcal{K}\subset T\mathcal{K}
,

(2)

\bigvee_{i=0}^{\infty}T^{i}\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{B}
,

(3)

\bigcap_{i=0}^{\infty}T^{-i}\mathcal{K}=\{\emptyset,X\}
.

Consider
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a probability vector
p=(p_{0},\cdots,p_{k-1})
, i.e.,
p_{i}\geq 0,i=0,\cdots,k-1
and
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}p_{i}=1
. A
Bernoulli shift
is a transformation
\sigma
on the space
\Sigma_{k}:=\{0,\cdots,k-1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
given by

\sigma((x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}})=(x_{n+1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
which preserves the Bernoulli measure
p^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
on
\Sigma_{k}
.

Definition 7.2

(Bernoulli property)
.

Let
T:X\to X
be an invertible measure-preserving transformation in a Lebesgue space
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
. We say that
T
has the
Bernoulli property
or is called
Bernoulli
if it is measurably isomorphic to a Bernoulli shift.

Obviously, the Bernoulli property implies the Kolmogorov property.

Definition 7.3
.

A measure-preserving flow
(X,\phi,\mu)
where
\phi=(\phi^{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is said to have the
Kolmogorov/Bernoulli property
if for every
t\neq 0
, the invertible measure-preserving transformation
(X,\phi^{t},\mu)
has the Kolmogorov/Bernoulli property.

For the the geodesic flow on manifolds of nonpositive curvature, Call and Thompson
[
8
]
showed that the unique equilibrium state for certain potential has the Kolmogorov property, based on the earlier work of
[
28
]
. Recently, this result is extended to manifolds without focal points in
[
10
]
, via methods developed in
[
8
]
. In particular, we have

Proposition 7.4
.

[
10
, Theorem B]

Let
M
be a rank one closed Riemannian manifold without focal points, then
the Knieper measure
m
is Kolmogorov.

A classical argument in
[
33
]
showed that the Kolmogorov property implies Bernoulli property for smooth invariant measures of Anosov flows. The argument was also carried out by Chernov and Haskell
[
11
]
for smooth invariant measures of suspension flows over some nonuniformly hyperbolic maps with singularities. It also works for hyperbolic invariant measures with local product structure. See also
[
42
,
41
]
for an introduction to terminology and ideas of the Kolmogorov-Bernoulli equivalence.

Based on the argument in Chernov and Haskell
[
11
]
, Call and Thompson
[
8
, Section
7
]
proved that the Knieper measure
m
is Bernoulli when
M
has nonpositive curvature. Following
[
8
]
, we show that the Knieper measure
m
is Bernoulli for manifolds without focal points. The main step is to show the existence of
\epsilon
-regular coverings as follows.

Definition 7.5
.

A
rectangle
in
SM
is a measurable set
R\subset SM
, equipped with a distinguished point
z\in R
with the property that for all points
x,y\in R
the local weak stable manifold
W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{0s}(x)
and the local unstable
W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{u}(y)
intersect each other at a single point, denoted by
[x,y]
, which lies in
R
.

Notice that a rectangle
R
can be thought of as the Cartesian product of
W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{0s}(z)\cap R
and
W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{u}(z)\cap R
, where a point
y\in R
is given by
[W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{0s}(y)\cap W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{u}(z),W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{u}(y)\cap W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{0s}(z)]
.

Given a probability measure
\mu
on
SM
, there is a natural product measure

\mu^{p}_{R}:=\mu^{u}_{z}\times\tilde{\mu}^{0s}_{z},

where
\mu^{u}_{z}
is the conditional measure induced by
\mu
on
W_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}^{u}(z)\cap R
with respect to the measurable partition of
R
into local unstable manifolds, and
\tilde{\mu}^{0s}_{z}
is the corresponding factor measure on
W^{0s}_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm loc}}}(z)
.

Below is Chernov and Haskell’s definition of
\epsilon
-regular coverings.

Definition 7.6
.

Given any
\epsilon>0
, we define an
\epsilon
-regular covering
for a probability measure
\mu
of
SM
to be a finite collection of disjoint rectangles
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}
such that

(1)

\mu(\cup_{R\in\mathcal{R}}R)>1-\epsilon
;

(2)

Given any two points
x,y\in R\in\mathcal{R}
, which lie in the same local unstable or local weak stable manifold, there is a smooth curve on that manifold which connects
x

and
y
and has length less than
100\cdot\text{diam}R
;

(3)

For every
R\in\mathcal{R}
with a distinguished point

, the product measure
\mu^{p}_{R}:=\mu^{u}_{z}\times\tilde{\mu}^{0s}_{z}
satisfies

\left|\frac{\mu^{p}_{R}(R)}{\mu(R)}-1\right|<\epsilon.

Moreover,
R
contains a subset
G
with
\mu(G)>(1-\epsilon)\mu(R)
such that for all
x\in G

\left|\frac{d\mu^{p}_{R}}{d\mu}(x)-1\right|<\epsilon.

Lemma 7.7

For any

and

\epsilon
-regular covering of connected rectangles

for Knieper measure

Proof.

Because

is a hyperbolic measure. By

To verify the third condition in Definition

provided by (

Let

Proof of Theorem
C
.

As in
[
11
]
, if a smooth invariant measure or a hyperbolic measure
\mu
is Kolmogorov and there exists an
\epsilon
-regular covering
with non-atomic conditionals for

\epsilon>0
, then any finite partition
\xi

C>0
such that

for all
\delta>0
is
Very Weak Bernoulli
(see e.g.
[
41
,
42
]
for definition). A refining sequence of such

suffices to conclude the Bernoulli property for

\mu

.

As for Knieper measure

that
\epsilon
-regular coverings for

. Notice that the conditional measure of

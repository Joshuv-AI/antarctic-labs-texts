Description Logics Go Second-Order — Extending EL with Universally Quantified Concepts
Joshua Hirschbrunn, Yevgeny Kazakov

Abstract
The study of Description Logics have been historically mostly focused on features that can be translated to decidable fragments of first-order logic. In this paper, we leave this restriction behind and look for useful and decidable extensions outside first-order logic. We introduce universally quantified concepts, which take the form of variables that can be replaced with arbitrary concepts, and define two semantics of this extension. A schema semantics allows replacements of concept variables only by concepts from a particular language, giving us axiom schemata similar to modal logics. A second-order semantics allows replacement of concept variables with arbitrary subsets of the domain, which is similar to quantified predicates in second-order logic. To study the proposed semantics, we focus on the extension of the description logic ℰ ​ ℒ \mathcal{EL} . We show that for a useful fragment of the extension, the conclusions entailed by the different semantics coincide, allowing us to use classical ℰ ​ ℒ \mathcal{EL} reasoning algorithms even for the second-order semantics. For a slightly smaller, but still useful, fragment, we were also able to show polynomial decidability of the extension. This fragment, in particular, can express a generalized form of role chain axioms, positive self restrictions, and some forms of (local) role-value-maps from KL-ONE, without requiring any additional constructors.

Introduction and Motivation

After the famous undecidability result of the early knowledge representation language used in the KL-ONE system
Schmidt-Schauß 1989
, it has been commonly agreed that
DL
should be restricted to decidable fragments of
FOL
(
FOL
), preferably of lower complexity.
The subsequent search for suitable languages has resulted in a large spectrum of
DL
ranging from simple tractable languages, such as
\mathcal{EL}

Baader et al. 2005
and
DL-Lite

Calvanese et al. 2007
to very expressive languages, such as
\mathcal{SROIQ}

Horrocks et al. 2006
, used as the basis of the Web ontology language OWL 2
Motik et al. 2009
.
After these extensive studies, finding new interesting fragments of
FOL
, has become increasingly difficult.
That is why, in this paper, we look at some features from
SOL
(
SOL
), specifically at
universally quantified concepts
.
Syntactically, we introduce new

concept variables
, which can be used in place of classical concepts.
For example, an axiom
\exists{\textsf{owns}}.(X\sqcap\textsf{Pet})\sqsubseteq\exists{\textsf{feeds}}.X
expresses that everyone who owns a pet from a set of objects
X
must feed someone from this set.
Such concept variables
X
are assumed to be implicitly
universally quantified
, that is, the above property should hold for
every reasonable choice
of a set
X
.

One possible way of defining semantics of our language extension, is to regard axioms with concept variables as
axiom schemata
.
Under this reading, the reasonable choices of
X
in the above axiom, are sets defined by
concepts
of a specific form, for example, all (atomic) concepts from an ontology, or all concepts that can be built in a particular
DL
, such as
\mathcal{EL}
.
After replacing each variable with every possible concept, one obtains a (possibly infinite) set of ordinary
DL
axioms, for which the logical entailment is defined in the standard way.

Axiom schemata is certainly not a new concept.
In fact, most logic languages, including
PL
(
PL
),
FOL
, and
ML
were originally defined
axiomatically
.
For example,
PL
can be defined using 3 axiom schemata:
X\to X
,
X\to(Y\to X)
, and
(X\to(Y\to Z))\to((X\to Y)\to(X\to Z))
, in which every variable can be replaced with any propositional formula.
Many
ML
were defined by extending
PL
with new axiom schemata, for example, K4:
\Box X\to\Box\Box X
(see, e.g.,
Blackburn et al. 2001
).
In the context of
DL
, axiom schemata occur in different contexts: Nominal Schemas
Carral et al. 2023
;
Krötzsch et al. 2011
;
Carral et al. 2013
introduce (nominal) variables that can be replaced with individual names.

ODP

He et al. 2015
;
Skjæveland et al. 2018
;
Kindermann et al. 2018
;
Krieg-Brückner et al. 2019
;
Kindermann et al. 2019
;
Skjæveland et al. 2017
;
Borgida et al. 2012
,
use concept variables to define axiom templates, which can be used to generate ordinary axioms for specific applications.
Axiom schemata with existentially-quantified concepts can be also used to define operations, such as interpolants or most-common subsumer
Colucci et al. 2010
.
To obtain decidability results, most works restrict the set of values that can be used for replacement of variables so that the resulting set of (ordinary) axioms is
finite
.
However, even for unrestricted axiom schemata, such as those found in
PL
and
ML
, it can be often shown that it is sufficient to replace variables with only finitely-many formulas found in (or built using) the entailment to be proved.
For
ML
, this
sub-formula property
usually follows from
cut-free sequent-style
calculi (see, e.g.,
Fitting 2007
).

The main advantage of the schema semantics for concept variables, is that any
DL
reasoning procedure could, in principle, be used for checking entailment in the corresponding
DL
extension, by systematically generating instances of axiom schemata and checking entailment from the resulting increasing sets of ordinary axioms.
This immediately implies
semi-decidability
of the schema extension and, in the cases when a form of sub-formula property can be proved, also
decidability
and
complexity
results.
Schema semantics, however, also has disadvantages, one of which, is that the entailments depend on the choices of concepts used for variable replacements.
Consider, for example, axiom schema
\top\sqsubseteq\exists r.X
.
If we only allow
\mathcal{EL}
concepts
C
for variable replacements, the resulting set of axioms is satisfiable since there is a model (with one element) that interprets every
\mathcal{EL}
concept (including every
\exists r.C
) by the whole domain.
If, however, we additionally allow
X
to be replaced by
\bot
, i.e., by taking concepts from
\mathcal{EL}^{\bot}
, then, clearly, this schema becomes unsatisfiable.
As a consequence, the
\mathcal{EL}
axiom schema obtains new
\mathcal{EL}
logical conclusions (e.g.,
A\sqsubseteq B
) when viewing it in a context of a larger language (e.g.,
\mathcal{EL}^{\bot}
).
In the next section we give a similar example when an
\mathcal{EL}
schema obtains new
\mathcal{EL}
consequences with
\mathcal{ALC}
concept replacements, this time, without making axioms inconsistent.

To see why this could be a concern, imagine that the axiom schemata of
PL
would obtain new logical consequences in the language of
PL
when allowing variables to be replaced by modal formulas!

Motivated by the above consideration, we define another,
language-independent
semantics of concept variables.
Specifically, an axiom with concept variables is satisfied by an interpretation if for
every
assignment of concept variables to
subsets
of its domain, the axiom is satisfied in the extension of this interpretation in which the variables are interpreted by these subsets (as ordinary concept names).
For example, axiom
\top\sqsubseteq\exists r.X
is
not
satisfied in any interpretation
\mathcal{I}
since for the assignment of
X\mapsto\emptyset
, this axiom does not hold if we extend
\mathcal{I}
by
X^{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
.
On the other hand, axiom
X\sqsubseteq\exists r.X
is satisfied in exactly those interpretations
\mathcal{I}
such that
r^{\mathcal{I}}
is a reflexive relation.
Similarly, axiom
\exists{\textsf{owns}}.(X\sqcap\textsf{Pet})\sqsubseteq\exists{\textsf{feeds}}.X
is satisfied in exactly those interpretations
\mathcal{I}
such that
\langle x,y\rangle\in\textsf{owns}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
y\in\textsf{Pet}^{\mathcal{I}}
imply
{\langle x,y\rangle}\in\textsf{feeds}^{\mathcal{I}}
.
2
2

2

Note that this property can be expressed in
\mathcal{SROIQ}
using a fresh role
pet
:
\textsf{Pet}\sqsubseteq\exists\textsf{pet}.\textsf{Self}
and
\textsf{pet}\circ\textsf{owns}\sqsubseteq\textsf{feeds}

Essentially, under this semantics, concept variables correspond to universally-quantified second-order (unary) predicates.
Hence, we refer to this semantics as
second-order semantics
.

It is easy to see that the second-order semantics is
stronger
than the schema semantics.
Indeed, if an axiom is satisfied in an interpretation under the second-order semantics, then for every replacement of variables by concepts, the resulting axioms are also (classically) satisfied in this interpretation because variables can be assigned to interpretations of these concepts in the second-order semantics.
The converse is not true, as has been shown on the example of axiom
\top\sqsubseteq\exists r.X
for the
\mathcal{EL}
-schema semantics.
Furthermore, whereas schema entailment can be always reduced to first-order entailment (from a possibly infinite set of formulas), there are axioms whose models under second-order semantics cannot be expressed by first-order formulas.
For example, the well-known
ML

McKinsey axiom

\Box\Diamond X\rightarrow\Diamond\Box X
, which can be written in the
DL

\mathcal{ALC}
as
\forall r.\exists r.X\sqsubseteq\exists r.\forall r.X
,
cannot be translated into even an infinite set of first-order formulas that holds in exactly the same interpretations (of
r
)
Goldblatt 1975
.
Hence, even semi-decidability for the second-order extension of
\mathcal{ALC}
seems to be an open question.

To combine advantages of the two proposed semantics (semi-decidability and schema language independence), it makes sense to look for restricted use of concept variables in
DL
for which the entailment under two semantics
coincide
.
In this paper, we describe a useful extension of the
DL

\mathcal{EL}
with concept variables for which this is the case (
Section

3
), and a further restriction, for which these (equivalent) entailments are
polynomially decidable
(
Section

4
).
Even though the use of concept variables in this fragment is restricted, it can express several other
DL
features, such as role chain axioms, positive self restrictions, reflexive roles, some forms of (local) role-value-maps from KL-ONE, and their generalizations (see
Section

5
).

The main idea for proving these results, is to show that the standard
\mathcal{EL}
canonical model
Baader et al. 2005
for axioms obtained by replacing concept variables with certain
relevant

\mathcal{EL}
concepts, is also a model of the original axioms under the second-order semantics.
This property holds because for our restricted form of axioms, it is sufficient to replace concept variables with only
singleton subsets
of the domain to capture the second-order semantics.

3

3

This means, in particular, that our restricted axioms can be translated to
FOL
. Note that any fragment for which the second-order entailment coincides with the schema entailment can be translated to
FOL
using the standard
FOL
translation for the schema instances.

For example, if
X\sqsubseteq\exists r.X
holds for all replacements of
X
with singleton subsets of the domain (that is, when
r
is reflexive) then this axiom also holds for replacements of
X
with any larger set.
To obtain the decidability and complexity result, we define a further restriction for which it is sufficient to use only polynomially-many relevant concepts for checking the schema entailment.

Definition 3

(Second-Order Semantics)

Let

A

Finally, for an ontology
\mathcal{K}

\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\mathcal{K}
if
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\beta

, and we write

C^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=C^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an
interpretation,
D
a
\mathcal{ELX}
concept containing a concept variable
X
and
\eta
a valuation such that
\eta(X)=\emptyset
.
Then
D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

The proof is by structural induction over
D
.
Note that the cases
D=\top
,
D=A\in N_{C}
, and
D=Y\in N_{X}\setminus\{X\}
are not possible since
D
must contain
X
.

•

If
D=X
then
D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\eta(X)=\emptyset
.

•

If
D=\exists r.E
then
E
contains
X
. By induction hypothesis,
E^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\emptyset
.
Hence
D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\emptyset
.

•

If
D=E_{1}\sqcap E_{2}
then
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
contain
X
.
By induction hypothesis,
E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\emptyset
or
E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\emptyset
.
Hence
D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\cap E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\emptyset
.
∎

Now, take any
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}C\sqsubseteq D
.
Then
C^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\emptyset
for every valuation
\eta
.
Then
\theta(C)^{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
for every concept variable substitution
\theta
.
Then for every
\mathcal{EL}
concept
E
such that
\theta(C)\in\textsf{sub}(E)
we have
E^{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
as well.
Thus
\{C\sqsubseteq D\}\models^{2}E\sqsubseteq F
for every
F
.
If the schema semantics preserves all these entailments then, in particular, all such concepts
E
(containing instances of
C
) must be equivalent.
This can happen only in some trivial cases, e.g., when the
\mathcal{K}
contains axiom of the form
X\sqsubseteq Y
, which implies that all concepts are equivalent.
To ensure that the semantics coincide in non-trivial cases, it is, therefore, make sense to require that all variables that are present on the right side of a concept
inclusion axioms are also present on the left side.
Axioms that fulfill this requirement we called
range restricted
axioms.

Example

2
presents another situation when schema semantics gives fewer consequences than the second-order semantics.
As has been shown in this example, for an
\mathcal{ELX}
axiom
\alpha
(
1
) and an
\mathcal{EL}
ontology
\mathcal{K}
from (
4
), we have
\mathcal{K}\cup\{\alpha\}\not\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}A\sqsubseteq D
, however, since
\{\alpha\}\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{ALC}}\beta
(
2
) and
\mathcal{K}\cup\{\beta\}\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}A\sqsubseteq D
, we have
\mathcal{K}\cup\{\alpha\}\models^{2}A\sqsubseteq D
.

The problem with
\alpha
in this example is that the variable
X
occurs twice on the left side of the axiom, which makes this axiom equivalent to
\beta
under the second-order semantics, and, consequently, being able to express an axiom with a concept disjunction
\exists r.C\sqcap\exists s.D\sqsubseteq\exists t.(C\sqcup D)
, which otherwise could not be expressed by ordinary
\mathcal{EL}
axioms, i.e., under the schema semantics.
To prevent such a case for our fragment of
\mathcal{ELX}
, it therefore, makes sense to prohibit the occurrence of the same variable twice on the left side of an axiom.
Concepts in which each variable occurs at most once, we call
linear
.

To motivate our last restriction, consider the next example.

Example 4
.

Consider the
\mathcal{ELX}
ontology
\mathcal{K}=\{\exists r.X\sqsubseteq\exists s.(X\sqcap A)\}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\exists r.\top\sqsubseteq\exists r.A
.
Indeed, take any
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\mathcal{K}
and
d\in(\exists r.\top)^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Then there exists
d^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
such that
\langle d,d^{\prime}\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Take any valuation
\eta
with
\eta(X)=\{d^{\prime}\}
.
Since
\langle d,d^{\prime}\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}}
, we have
d\in(\exists r.X)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
Since
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}_{\eta}\mathcal{K}
, we have
d\in(\exists s.(X\sqcap A))^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
In particular,
\emptyset\neq(X\sqcap A)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\{d^{\prime}\}\cap A^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Hence
d^{\prime}\in A^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Consequently,
d\in(\exists r.A)^{\mathcal{I}}
.

On the other hand,
\mathcal{K}\not\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}\exists r.\top\sqsubseteq\exists r.A
, as evidenced by the counter-model
\mathcal{I}=(\Delta^{\mathcal{I}},\cdot^{\mathcal{I}})
with

\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}=\{a,b\}
,

A^{\mathcal{I}}=\{a\}
,

r^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\langle a,b\rangle\}
,

s^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\langle a,a\rangle\}
,
and
E^{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
,
h^{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
for any remaining
E\in N_{C}
and
h\in N_{R}
.
To show that
\mathcal{I}\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}\mathcal{K}
, we prove that
\mathcal{I}\models\exists r.C\sqsubseteq\exists s.(C\sqsubseteq A)
for every
\mathcal{EL}
concept
C
.
For this, take any
d\in(\exists r.C)^{\mathcal{I}}
.
By definition of
r^{\mathcal{I}}
,
d=a
and
b\in C^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Then
C
can be only a conjunction of concept
\top
.
Hence
a\in C
.
Hence
d=a\in(\exists s.C)^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Thus
\mathcal{I}\models\mathcal{K}
.
Since,
a\in(\exists r.\top)^{\mathcal{I}}
but
(\exists r.A)^{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset
, we proved that
\mathcal{K}\not\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}\exists r.\top\sqsubseteq\exists r.A
.

Note that under the second order semantics, the axiom
\exists r.X\sqsubseteq\exists s.(X\sqcap A)
in
\mathcal{K}
from
Example

4
implies two properties: (1) that
r
is a
subrole
of
s
(
r\sqsubseteq s
), which is equivalent to axiom
\exists r.X\sqsubseteq\exists s.X
, and (2) that
A
is a
range
of the role
r
(
ran(r)\sqsubseteq A
), which is due to the fact that for any element
d^{\prime}
such that
\langle d,d^{\prime}\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}}
this axiom holds for
X=\{d^{\prime}\}
.
As was shown in the example, the schema semantics cannot capture the second kind of properties.
In fact, an extension of
\mathcal{EL}
with both (complex) role inclusions and range restrictions becomes undecidable
Baader et al. 2008
), which could explain why the schema semantics cannot characterize consequences in this extension.
To prevent situations like in
Example

4
, we require that variables in the right side of axioms appear only directly under existential restrictions.
We generalize a related notion of
safe nominals

Kazakov et al. 2012
to define this restriction:

Definition 4

(Safe Concept)
.

A
\mathcal{ELX}
concept
C
is called
safe
(for concept variables), if variables only occur in the
form of
\exists r.X
, i.e. safe concepts are defined by the grammar:

C_{s}^{(i)}=A\mid\top\mid\exists r.X\mid\exists r.C_{s}\mid C^{1}_{s}\sqcap C^{2}_{s}

3
When Semantics Coincide

In this section we prove that the restrictions on the use of concept variables discussed in
Section

2
are sufficient to guarantee that the logical consequences under the schema semantics and second-order semantics coincide.
Towards this goal, we define a fragment
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
of
\mathcal{ELX}
that satisfies these restrictions:

Definition 5

(
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
)
.

A
\mathcal{ELX}
axiom
\beta=C\sqsubseteq D
is in the fragment
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
, if

•

\beta
is range restricted, i.e.
\textsf{vars}(D)\subseteq\textsf{vars}(C)
,

•

C
is linear, i.e.
C
it does not contain a variable twice, and

•

D
is safe (cf.
4
).

An interesting consequence of the first two restrictions in
5
, is the so-called
singleton property
for valuations.
Intuitively, for checking entailment over the second-order semantics, it is sufficient to consider only valuations that assign concept variables to singleton subsets of the domain.

Definition 6

(Singleton Valuation)
.

A valuation
\eta
is a
singleton
valuation
if

\forall X:|\!|\eta(X)|\!|=1
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an interpretation,
C
a linear
\mathcal{ELX}
concept,
\eta
a valuation, and
a\in C^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
Then there is a singleton valuation
\eta^{\prime}
such that
\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for every
X\in\textsf{vars}(C)
and
a\in C^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Proof by induction over the structure of
C
:

•

If
\textsf{vars}(C)=\emptyset
then every singleton valuation
\eta^{\prime}
works since
a\in C^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}=C^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.

•

If
C=X
then
a\in X^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\eta(X)
let

\eta^{\prime}(X)=\{a\}
then
|\!|\eta^{\prime}(X)|\!|=1
,
\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
and
a\in\eta^{\prime}(X)=X^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
.

•

If
C=\exists r.E
then
a\in(\exists r.E)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
and there exists
b\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
such that
\langle a,b\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}}
and
b\in E^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
By induction hypothesis, there exists a singleton
\eta^{\prime}
such that
\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for every
X\in\textsf{vars}(E)
such that
b\in E^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
.
Then
\textsf{vars}(C)=\textsf{vars}(E)
and
a\in(\exists r.E)^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
.

•

If
C=E_{1}\sqcap E_{2}
then
a\in(E_{1}\sqcap E_{2})^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\cap E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
By induction hypothesis for
a\in E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
, there exists a singleton
\eta_{1}^{\prime}
such that
\eta_{1}^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for
X\in\textsf{vars}(E_{1})
and
a\in E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta_{1}^{\prime}}
.
Likewise, for
a\in E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
, there exists a singleton
\eta_{2}^{\prime}
such that
\eta_{2}^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for
X\in\textsf{vars}(E_{2})
and
a\in E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta_{2}^{\prime}}
.
Define
\eta^{\prime}(X):=\eta_{1}^{\prime}(X)
for
X\in\textsf{vars}(E_{1})
and
\eta^{\prime}(X):=\eta_{2}^{\prime}(X)
for
X\notin\textsf{vars}(E_{1})
.
Obviously,
\eta^{\prime}
is singleton,
\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for
X\in\textsf{vars}(C)=\textsf{vars}(E_{1})\cup\textsf{vars}(E_{2})
, and
E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}=E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta_{1}^{\prime}}
.
Since
C
is linear,
\textsf{vars}(E_{1})\cap\textsf{vars}(E_{2})=\emptyset
, hence
\eta^{\prime}(X)=\eta_{2}^{\prime}(X)
for
X\in\textsf{vars}(E_{2})
.
Hence
E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}=E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta_{2}^{\prime}}
.
Finally, since
a\in E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta_{1}^{\prime}}=E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
and
a\in E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta_{2}^{\prime}}=E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
, we obtain
a\in C^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
as required.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an
interpretation,
D
an
\mathcal{ELX}
concept, and
\eta^{\prime}
,
\eta
valuations such that
\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for every
X\in\textsf{vars}(D)
.
Then
D^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.

Proof.

Proof by induction over the structure of
D
:

•

If
\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset
, then, trivially,
D^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}=D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.

•

If
D=X
then
X^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}=\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)=X^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.

•

If
D=\exists r.E
then
E^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq E^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
by the induction hypothesis.
Then for any
a\in(\exists r.E)^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
there exists
b\in E^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq E^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
such that
\langle a,b\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Hence
a\in(\exists r.E)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.

•

If
C=E_{1}\sqcap E_{2}
then
E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
and
E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
by the induction hypothesis.
Hence
(E_{1}\sqcap E_{2})^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}=E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\cap E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq E_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\cap E_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=(E_{1}\sqcap E_{2})^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
∎

By combining the above two lemmata, we obtain the required singleton property:

Theorem 1

(Singleton Property)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an interpretation,
\beta=C\sqsubseteq D
a range restricted
\mathcal{ELX}
axiom such that
C
is linear, and
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}_{\eta^{\prime}}\beta
for every singleton valuation
\eta^{\prime}
.
Then
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\beta
.

Proof.

Take any valuation
\eta
.
We need to prove that
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}_{\eta}\beta
, i.e.,
C^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
Take any
a\in C^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
Then, by
1
, there is a singleton
\eta^{\prime}
with
\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for every
X\in\textsf{vars}(C)
such that
a\in C^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
.
Then
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}_{\eta^{\prime}}\beta
by the condition of the theorem, so
a\in C^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}
.
Since
\beta
is range restricted, we have
\eta^{\prime}(X)\subseteq\eta(X)
for every
X\in\textsf{vars}(D)\subseteq\textsf{vars}(C)
.
Then
a\in D^{\mathcal{I},\eta^{\prime}}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
by
3
.
Since
a
was chosen arbitrarily, this proves that
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}_{\eta}\beta
.
∎

Our next goal is to prove that if
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
for some
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
ontology
\mathcal{K}
and a (ground)
\mathcal{EL}
axiom
\alpha
, then
\mathcal{K}\models^{\ast}_{H}\alpha
for some concept base
H
consisting of (ground)
\mathcal{EL}
concepts, i.e.,
\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H}\models\alpha
(cf.
2
).
Since
\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H}
is an ordinary
\mathcal{EL}
ontology, we can use the well known characterization of
\mathcal{EL}
entailment using so-called
\mathcal{EL}

canonical models
(also called
completion graphs

Baader et al. 2005
).
Usually canonical models are defined using consequences of the ontology derived by certain inference rules (see, e.g.,
Kazakov et al. 2014
), however the following simplified definition is sufficient for our purpose.
To avoid confusion with
\mathcal{ELX}
ontologies, we denote
\mathcal{EL}
ontologies by
\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 7

(Canonical Interpretation)
.

Let
H
be a nonempty
concept base
and
\mathcal{G}
an
\mathcal{EL}
ontology.
The
canonical interpretation
(w.r.t.
\mathcal{G}
and
H
) is an interpretation
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G},H)=(\Delta^{\mathcal{I}},\cdot^{\mathcal{I}})
defined by:

\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x_{C}\mid C\in H\}
,

A^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x_{C}\mid\mathcal{G}\models C\sqsubseteq A\}
for
A\in N_{C}
, and

r^{\mathcal{I}}=\{\langle x_{C},x_{D}\rangle\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models C\sqsubseteq\exists r.D\}
for
r\in N_{R}
.

If we include all
\mathcal{EL}
concepts into the concept base
H
, it can be shown that the canonical interpretation
\mathcal{I}
is a model of
\mathcal{G}
that satisfies exactly
\mathcal{EL}
axioms entailed by
\mathcal{G}
:
\mathcal{I}\models\alpha
iff
\mathcal{G}\models\alpha
.
However, to ensure the latter property for a
fixed

\alpha
, it is sufficient to use a smaller concept base
H
that contains only certain sub-concepts of
\mathcal{G}
and
\alpha
.
It turns out that if
H
satisfies certain conditions for
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H}
and
\alpha
then
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
implies
\mathcal{I}\models\alpha
, which implies
\mathcal{G}\models\alpha
.
To prove that
\mathcal{I}\models\alpha
, we show that
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\mathcal{K}
, for which we use the singleton valuation property from
1
.
There is a nice connection between singleton valuations in
\mathcal{I}
and substitutions of concept variables with concepts from
H
, which we are going to exploit.

Definition 8

(Canonical Substitution)
.

We say that a concept variable substitution
\theta
is
canonical
for a concept base
H
, if
\theta(X)\in H
for every
X\in N_{X}
.
Given a canonical interpretation
\mathcal{I}
(for
\mathcal{G}
and
H
) and a singleton valuation
\eta
for
\mathcal{I}
, the
canonical substitution determined
by
\eta
is the substitution
\theta=\theta_{\eta}
defined by
\theta(X)=C
iff
\eta(X)=\{x_{C}\}\subseteq\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
.

The connection to canonical substitutions in
8
helps us to characterize interpretations of (complex)
\mathcal{ELX}
concepts under canonical models and singleton valuations.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the canonical interpretation w.r.t. some
\mathcal{EL}
ontology
\mathcal{G}
and a concept base
H
,
F
an
\mathcal{ELX}
concept,
\eta
a singleton valuation, and
\theta=\theta_{\eta}
the corresponding canonical substitution.
Then:

F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F)\}.

(5)

In addition, if
F
is a safe concpet (see Definition
4
) and

\theta(D)\in H
for every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}(F)
then

F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\supseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F)\}.

(6)

Proof.

Proof by induction over the definition of (safe)
\mathcal{EL}
concept
F
:

•

F=\top
. Then
\theta(F)=\top
and
\top^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\top^{\mathcal{I}}=\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\top\}
.

•

F=A\in N_{C}
. Then
\theta(F)=A
and
A^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=A^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq A\}
by definition of
A^{\mathcal{I}}
.

•

F=X\in N_{X}
and
\eta(X)=\{x_{C}\}
. Then
\theta(F)=C
and
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\eta(X)=\{x_{C}\}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq C\}
.
Since
F=X
is not safe, the converse direction does not apply.

•

F=\exists r.X
and
\eta(X)=\{x_{C}\}
. Then
\theta(F)=\exists r.C
and
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\{x_{D}\mid\langle x_{D},x_{C}\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}}\}=\{x_{D}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\exists r.C\}
by definition of
r^{\mathcal{I}}
.

•

F=\exists s.G
. Then
\theta(F)=\exists s.\theta(G)
and
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\{x_{D}\mid\langle x_{D},x_{C}\rangle\in s^{\mathcal{I}}\>\&\>x_{C}\in G^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\}\subseteq\{x_{D}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\exists s.C\>\&\>\mathcal{G}\models C\sqsubseteq\theta(G)\}\subseteq\{x_{D}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\exists s.\theta(G)\}
by definition of
s^{\mathcal{I}}
, induction hypothesis for
G
, and basic entailment properties.

If
F
is safe and
\theta(D)\in H
for every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}(\theta(F))
then either
G=X
(the previous case) or
G
is safe, in which case, by induction hypothesis,

G^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\supseteq\{x_{C}\mid\mathcal{G}\models C\sqsubseteq\theta(G)\}
.
Since
\theta(G)\in H
and
\mathcal{G}\models\theta(G)\sqsubseteq\theta(G)
, this implies that
x_{\theta(G)}\in G^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
Then
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\{x_{D}\mid\langle x_{D},x_{C}\rangle\in s^{\mathcal{I}}\>\&\>x_{C}\in G^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\}\supseteq\{x_{D}\mid\langle x_{D},x_{\theta(G)}\rangle\in s^{\mathcal{I}}\}=\{x_{D}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\exists s.\theta(G)\}=\{x_{D}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F)\}
.

•

F=F_{1}\sqcap F_{2}
. Then
\theta(F)=\theta(F_{1})\sqcap\theta(F_{2})
and
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=F_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\cap F_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F_{1})\}\cap\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F_{2})\}=\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F_{1})\sqcap\theta(F_{2})\}

by induction hypothesis and basic entailment properties.

If
F
is safe and
\theta(D)\in H
for every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}(\theta(F))
then so are
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, thus
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=F_{1}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\cap F_{2}^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\supseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F_{1})\}\cap\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F_{2})\}=\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F_{1})\sqcap\theta(F_{2})\}

by induction hypothesis.
∎

The restriction to safe concepts in
4
is necessary, as the following example shows:

Example 5
.

Consider
H=\{A,B\}\subseteq N_{C}
and
\mathcal{G}=\{A\sqsubseteq B\}
.
Then the canonical interpretation
\mathcal{I}
for
\mathcal{G}
and
H
has domain
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x_{A},x_{B}\}
and assigns
A^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x_{A},x_{B}\}
,
B^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x_{B}\}
.
Now take
F=X\in N_{X}
and define a singleton
\eta(X)=\{x_{B}\}
.
Then the corresponding canonical substitution
\theta(X)=B
.
As can be seen,
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\eta(X)=\{x_{B}\}\subsetneq\{x_{A},x_{B}\}=\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq B\}
.

We are now ready to harvest the fruits of our characterization of canonical interpretations.
We first show that, the canonical interpretation cannot entail more axioms than the ontology
\mathcal{G}
for which it is constructed if the concept base
H
contains relevant concepts from these axioms.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the canonical interpretation w.r.t.
\mathcal{G}
and
H
,
and
\alpha=F\sqsubseteq G
an
\mathcal{EL}
axiom such that
F\in H
and
D\in H
for every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}(F)
.
Then
\mathcal{I}\models\alpha
implies
\mathcal{G}\models\alpha
.

Proof.

By
4
,

G^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq G\}
and

F^{\mathcal{I}}\supseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq F\}

Since
\mathcal{G}\models F\sqsubseteq F
and
F\in H
, we have
x_{F}\in F^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Since
\mathcal{I}\models F\sqsubseteq G
, we have
x_{F}\in G^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq G\}
.
Hence
\mathcal{G}\models F\sqsubseteq G
.
∎

Next, we determine what to put into
H
and
\mathcal{G}
so that the canonical interpretation
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}(G,H)
satisfies a given
\mathcal{ELX}
axiom
\beta
(to eventually ensure that
\mathcal{I}\models\mathcal{K}
).

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the canonical interpretation w.r.t.
\mathcal{G}
and
H
,
and
\beta=F\sqsubseteq G
an
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
axiom such that 1)
\beta_{\downarrow H}\in\mathcal{G}
and 2)
D_{\downarrow H}\subseteq H
for every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}(G)
.
Then
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\beta
.

Proof.

Since
\beta
is an
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
axiom,
\beta
is range restricted and
F
is linear.
Therefore, by
1
,
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\beta
iff
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\subseteq G^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
for every singleton valuation
\eta
.
Now, let
\eta
be any such singleton valuation and
\theta
be the corresponding canonical substitution.
Then by
4
,
F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F)\}
.
Since
\beta
is an
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
axiom,
G
is a safe concept.
Furthermore,
\theta(D)\in D_{\downarrow H}\subseteq H
for every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}(G)
by Condition 2).
Therefore, by
4
,
G^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\supseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(G)\}
.
Finally, by Condition 1)
\theta(\beta)=\theta(F)\sqsubseteq\theta(G)\in\mathcal{G}
, therefore

F^{\mathcal{I},\eta}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(F)\}\subseteq\{x_{D}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\mathcal{G}\models D\sqsubseteq\theta(G)\}\subseteq G^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
.
∎

By combining
1
and
2
, for the given
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
ontology
\mathcal{K}
and an
\mathcal{EL}
axiom
\alpha
, we can define the

concept base

such that the canonical interpretation
\mathcal{I}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H}
and

satisfies all axioms

under the second-order semantics and entails
\alpha
only if
\mathcal{G}\models\alpha
and thus only if
\mathcal{K}\models^{\ast}\alpha
.
Note that Condition 2) in

is recursive over
H
.
Therefore, the required

is defined as a fixed point limit for this condition.

Definition 9

(Expansion & Expansion Base)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a
\mathcal{ELX}
ontology and
\alpha=F\sqsubseteq E
an
\mathcal{EL}
axiom.
Let

and

We show that the expansion base

is indeed a fixed point of the required condition:

Lemma 5
.

Let
H^{\infty}
be the expansion base for
\mathcal{K}
w.r.t.
\alpha
.
Then
D_{\downarrow H^{\infty}}\subseteq H^{\infty}
for every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})

Proof.

Take any
D
with
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})

Then
D^{\prime}=D[X_{1}/C_{1},\dots,X_{n}/C_{n}]
for

Since

\{C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\}\subseteq H^{i}
for some

Then

.
∎

By combining
1
,

and

, we now prove the following result:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be an
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
ontology,

an
\mathcal{EL}
axiom, and
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}
the expansion of
\mathcal{K}
w.r.t.

.
Then
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
implies
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}\models\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
H^{\infty}
be the expansion base for
\mathcal{K}
and
\alpha
, and
\mathcal{I}
the canonical interpretation for
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}
and

.
By

are satisfied for

,

and every

.
This implies

.
Then
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
implies
\mathcal{I}\models\alpha
.
Furthermore, by

are satisfied for

,
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{K}^{\infty}

\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{K}^{\infty}\models\alpha

∎

An immediate consequence of

is that the schema semantics coincides with second-order semantics for
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
ontologies.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
ontology and
\alpha
an
\mathcal{EL}
axiom.
Then
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{K}\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}\alpha
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
:
By
2
,
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
implies
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}\models\alpha
. Then

\mathcal{K}\models^{\ast}_{H^{\infty}}\alpha
, and so,
\mathcal{K}\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}\alpha
.

(\Leftarrow)
:
By
2
,
\mathcal{K}\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}\alpha
means that

\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H}\models\alpha
where
H
is the set of all
\mathcal{EL}
concepts.
In order to prove that
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
, take any
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\mathcal{K}
.
We prove that
\mathcal{I}\models\alpha
.
We first show that
\mathcal{I}\models\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H}
.
Indeed, take any
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H}
.
Then
\beta^{\prime}=\theta(\beta)
for some substitution
\theta
of concept variables to concepts from

.
Define a valuation
\eta
by
\eta(X)=\theta(X)^{\mathcal{I}}
.
By induction on the structure of
\mathcal{EL}
concepts

it is easy to show that
D^{\mathcal{I},\eta}=\theta(D)^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Then
\mathcal{I}\models\beta^{\prime}
follows from

since
\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\mathcal{K}
.
This proves

Since

∎

4
Decidability

Because the schema semantics and the second-order semantics coincide for
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
, we immediately obtain semi-decidability of the entailment for the latter.
In general, entailment in
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
ist still
undecidable
because
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
can express (unrestricted) role-value-maps:

Definition 10

(Role-Value-Maps)
.

A
role-value-map

Baader 2003
is an axiom of the form

r_{1}\circ\dots\circ r_{m}\sqsubseteq s_{1}\circ\dots\circ s_{n}

with
m,n\geq 1
,
r_{i},s_{j}\in N_{R}
(
1\leq i\leq m
,
1\leq j\leq n
).
The
interpretation of role-value-maps
is defined by:

\mathcal{I}\models r_{1}\circ\dots\circ r_{m}\sqsubseteq s_{1}\circ\dots\circ s_{n}\text{ iff }r_{1}^{\mathcal{I}}\circ\dots\circ r_{m}^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq s_{1}^{\mathcal{I}}\circ\dots\circ s_{n}^{\mathcal{I}}
, where
\circ
is the usual composition of binary relations.

Lemma 6
.

For every interpretation
\mathcal{I}
it holds

\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\exists r_{1}.\exists r_{2}.\dots\exists r_{m}.X\sqsubseteq\exists s_{1}.\exists s_{2}.\dots\exists s_{n}.X

iff

r_{1}^{\mathcal{I}}\circ\dots\circ r_{m}^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq s_{1}^{\mathcal{I}}\circ\dots\circ s_{n}^{\mathcal{I}}
.

Proof.

\Rightarrow
:

Assume that

\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\exists r_{1}.\exists r_{2}.\dots\exists r_{m}.X\sqsubseteq\exists s_{1}.\exists s_{2}.\dots\exists s_{n}.X

Then we need to show that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathllap{\forall x,z\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:\ }(\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{m-1}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:&\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in r_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in r_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,\\
&\langle y_{m-1},z\rangle\in r_{m}^{\mathcal{I}})\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathllap{\Rightarrow\ }(\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:&\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in s_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in s_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,\\
&\langle y_{n-1},z\rangle\in s_{n}^{\mathcal{I}})\end{aligned}

Take any
x,z\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
such that

\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{m-1}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in r_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in r_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,\langle y_{m-1},z\rangle\in r_{m}^{\mathcal{I}}

Let
\eta
be a valuation such that
\eta(X)=\{z\}
. Then
x\in(\exists r_{1}.\exists r_{2}.\dots\exists r_{m}.X)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}

and it follows that
x\in(\exists s_{1}.\exists s_{2}.\dots\exists s_{n}.X)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
. Then

\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in s_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in s_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,\langle y_{n-1},z\rangle\in s_{n}^{\mathcal{I}}

\Leftarrow
:

Assume that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathllap{\forall x,z\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:\ }(\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{m-1}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:&\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in r_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in r_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,\\
&\langle y_{m-1},z\rangle\in r_{m}^{\mathcal{I}})\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathllap{\Rightarrow\ }(\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:&\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in s_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in s_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,\\
&\langle y_{n-1},z\rangle\in s_{n}^{\mathcal{I}})\end{aligned}

We need to show
that

\mathcal{I}\models^{2}\exists r_{1}.\exists r_{2}.\dots\exists r_{m}.X\sqsubseteq\exists s_{1}.\exists s_{2}.\dots\exists s_{n}.X

Take any
x
and
\eta
then if
x\in(\exists r_{1}.\exists r_{2}.\dots\exists r_{m}.X)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
then

\displaystyle\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{m-1},z\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:

\displaystyle\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in r_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in r_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,

\displaystyle\langle y_{m-1},z\rangle\in r_{m}^{\mathcal{I}},z\in\eta(X)

Then from the role-value-map axiom it follows that

\exists y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}:\langle x,y_{1}\rangle\in s_{1}^{\mathcal{I}},\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in s_{2}^{\mathcal{I}},\dots,\langle y_{n-1},z\rangle\in s_{n}^{\mathcal{I}}

Then
x\in(\exists s_{1}.\exists s_{2}.\dots\exists s_{n}.X)^{\mathcal{I},\eta}
. As
x
and
\eta
were chosen
arbitrarily, this proves the proposition.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Axiom entailment in
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
is undecidable.

Proof.

Follows directly from
6
and the fact that
axiom entailment is undecidable in
\mathcal{EL}
extended with role-value-maps
Baader 2003
.
∎

The reason for this undecidiblity, is deep nesting of concept variables on the right side of axioms under existential restrictions.
Such nested variables result in infinite expansion base
H^{\infty}
as the following example shows:

Example 6
.

Consider the
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
ontology
\mathcal{K}=\{X\sqsubseteq\exists r.\exists r.X\}
and
\alpha=A\sqsubseteq B
.
Then according to
9
, we have
H^{1}=\{A\}
,

\mathcal{K}^{1}=\{A\sqsubseteq\exists r.\exists r.A\}
,

H^{2}=\{A,\exists r.A\}
,

\mathcal{K}^{2}=\{A\sqsubseteq\exists r.\exists r.A,\exists r.A\sqsubseteq\exists r.\exists r.\exists r.A\}
,

H^{3}=\{A,\exists r.A,\exists r.\exists r.A\}
, etc.

If we restrict
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
so that variables on the right side do not appear under nested existential restrictions, we can show that the expansion
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}
of the ontology is, in fact, polynomial in the size of
\mathcal{K}
, which gives us polynomial decidability of the (schema and second-order) entailment.

Definition 11

(
\mathcal{ELX}_{F2}
)
.

An
\mathcal{ELX}_{F2}
axiom is an
\mathcal{ELX}_{F1}
axiom
\alpha
such that for
every
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\alpha)
, either
D=X\in N_{X}
or
\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be an
\mathcal{ELX}_{F2}
ontology,
\alpha
an
\mathcal{EL}
axiom and
H^{\infty}
the expansion base for
\mathcal{K}
and
\alpha
.
Then
H^{\infty}=H^{0}\cup\{D\mid\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})\>\&\>\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset\}
.

Proof.

By
9
,
H^{i+1}=H^{i}\cup\bigcup_{\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})}D_{\downarrow H^{i}}
for
i\geq 0
.
By
11
,
\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})
implies
D=X\in N_{X}
or
\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset
.
If
D=X
then
D_{\downarrow H^{i}}=X_{\downarrow H^{i}}=H^{i}
.
If
\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset
then
D_{\downarrow H^{i}}=\{D\}
.
Then
H^{i+1}=H^{i}\cup\{D\mid\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})\>\&\>\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset\}
.
Hence
H^{\infty}=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}H^{i}\subseteq H^{0}\cup\{D\mid\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})\>\&\>\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset\}
.
∎

Since the elements of
H^{\infty}
are subsets of (ground) concepts appearing in
\mathcal{K}
and
\alpha
, we obtain:

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a
\mathcal{ELX}_{F2}
ontology and
\alpha
an
\mathcal{EL}
axiom.
Then the entailment
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
is decidable in polynomial time in the size of
\mathcal{K}
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

By
7
,
H^{\infty}=H^{0}\cup\{D\mid\exists r.D\in\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})\>\&\>\textsf{vars}(D)=\emptyset\}\subseteq\textsf{sub}^{-}(\alpha)\cup\textsf{sub}^{+}(\mathcal{K})
.
Hence
|\!|H^{\infty}|\!|\leq|\!|\mathcal{K}|\!|+|\!|\alpha|\!|
.
Since
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}=\mathcal{K}_{\downarrow H^{\infty}}
,
|\!|\mathcal{K}^{\infty}|\!|\leq|\!|\mathcal{K}|\!|\cdot|\!|H^{\infty}|\!|^{V}
where
V
is the maximal number of concept variables in axioms of
\mathcal{K}
.
It can be shown that, w.l.o.g.,
V\leq 1
for
\mathcal{ELX}_{F2}
ontologies.
Hence,
|\!|\mathcal{K}^{\infty}|\!|\leq|\!|\mathcal{K}|\!|\cdot(|\!|\mathcal{K}|\!|+|\!|\alpha|\!|)
.

By
2
,
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
implies
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}\models\alpha
.
Conversely,
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}\models\alpha
implies
\mathcal{K}\models^{\ast}_{\mathcal{EL}}\alpha
, which implies
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
by
3
.
Hence, entailment
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
can be reduced to the classical
\mathcal{EL}
entailment in polynomial time.
Since the latter is polynomially decidable
Baader et al. 2005
, this implies that the entailment
\mathcal{K}\models^{2}\alpha
is also decidable in polynomial time.
∎

5
Conclusions and Outlook

In this paper, we left behind the usual restriction of
DL
to fragments of

FOL
and introduced concept variables directly into
DL
.
These concept variables can be understood as simple placeholders for concrete
concepts, giving us axiom schemas. This results in a semantics that replaces
variables by a specific set of concepts and in this way reduces reasoning to the classical
case. Or they can be understood more strongly, as universally quantified
concepts, similar to predicates in
SOL
. This gives us a semantics that
interprets variables as arbitrary subsets of the interpretation domain and
results in the DL being a fragment of
SOL
.

We applied this extension to
\mathcal{EL}
and analyzed the difference in
entailed conclusions by the two semantics. We defined a fragment for which the
conclusions coincide, given us semi-decidability also for second-order semantics.
We also showed that for a slight limitation of this fragment, second-order semantics (and
schema semantics) even become decidable.

In this decidable fragment
\mathcal{ELX}_{F2}
, we can express a range of features that
usually require special constructors in classical
\mathcal{EL}
: We can express role chain axioms that reduce a chain of roles to a
connection via one role. What would normally be expressed as
r_{1}\circ\dots\circ r_{n}\sqsubseteq s
(i.e. role-value-maps where the right
side is a single role, cf.
10
), we can express as

\exists r_{1}.\exists r_{2}.\dots\exists r_{n}.X\sqsubseteq\exists s.X
.
For example
\textsf{father}\circ\textsf{father}\sqsubseteq\textsf{grandfather}
is equivalent to
\exists\textsf{father}.\exists\textsf{father}.X\sqsubseteq\exists\textsf{grandfather}.X
. We can also express self restrictions on the right side of axioms,
i.e. classical axioms of the form
C\sqsubseteq\exists r.\mathrm{Self}
, meaning
\forall x:(x\in C^{\mathcal{I}})\Rightarrow(\langle x,x\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}})
(cf. e.g.
Wang and Hitzler 2012
). We express this as
C\sqcap X\sqsubseteq\exists r.X
. For example

\textsf{GreatApes}\sqsubseteq\exists\textsf{recognize}.\mathrm{Self}
is
equivalent to
\textsf{GreatApes}\sqcap X\sqsubseteq\exists\textsf{recognize}.X
.
We can express positive occurrences of (local)
role-value-map concepts
, i.e., concepts of the form
r\subseteq s
interpreted as
\{x\mid\forall y:\langle x,y\rangle\in r^{\mathcal{I}}\Rightarrow\langle x,y\rangle\in s^{\mathcal{I}}\}
(cf. e.g.
Donini 2003
).
E.g., we express
C\sqsubseteq(r\subseteq s)
as
C\sqcap\exists r.X\sqsubseteq\exists s.X
.
For example
\textsf{Male}\sqsubseteq(\textsf{isParentOf}\subseteq\textsf{isFatherOf})
is equivalent to
\textsf{Male}\sqcap\exists\textsf{isParentOf}.X\sqsubseteq\exists\textsf{isFatherOf}.X
.
Finally, we can express restrictions that generalize
all
of these constructs, for example, axioms of the form:

C_{0}\sqcap\exists r_{1}.(C_{1}\sqcap\exists r_{2}.(C_{2}\dots\sqcap\exists r_{n}.(C_{n}\sqcap X)\dots))\sqsubseteq\exists s.X,\quad(n\geq 0)

(7)

Note that (
7
) can be expressed with self-restrictions over fresh roles:
C_{i}\sqsubseteq\exists h_{i}.\text{Self}

(0\leq i\leq n)
and role chain axiom:

h_{0}\circ r_{1}\circ h_{2}\circ r_{2}\cdots r_{n}\sqsubseteq s
.
Thus, it is not clear whether our decidable fragment has more expressive power than known polynomial extensions of
\mathcal{EL}

Baader et al. 2008
;
Wang and Hitzler 2012
.

It is possible to generalize
3
and
5
to entailments of arbitrary
\mathcal{ELX}
axioms
\alpha
because such entailments can be always reduced to entailment of
\mathcal{EL}
axioms by simply replacing all concept variables in
\alpha
with new atomic concepts, not occurring in
\mathcal{K}

or
\alpha
.
Intuitively, to prove that every (second order or schema) model of the ontology satisfies
\alpha
under each valuation
\eta
or substitution
\theta
, we can extend this model by interpreting the new atomic concepts according to the value of
\eta(X)
or
\theta(X)
on the variables
X
which they replace.
This still remains a model of the ontology since it does not contain these new concepts.

Summarizing, the results in this paper show that an extension of
DL
to
being fragments of
SOL
, instead of
FOL
, is possible (while remaining
decidable for reasonable restrictions) and does allow expressing facts in a new
way without the use of special constructors. This enlightens the relationship
between
FOL
,
SOL
and
DL
and opens the way for further extensions
of
DL
outside
FOL
.

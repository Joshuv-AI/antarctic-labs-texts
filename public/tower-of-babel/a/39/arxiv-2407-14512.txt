1.
Introduction

Modular curves
X(\Gamma)
are a type of algebraic curves which can be constructed as quotients of the compactified upper half plane
\mathcal{H}^{*}
with
\Gamma
, a congruence subgroup of the modular group
\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Most common modular curves are
X(N)
,
X_{0}(N)
,
X_{1}(N)
which correspond to congruence subgroups

\displaystyle\Gamma(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):a,d\equiv 1\pmod{N},\ b,c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):a,d\equiv 1\pmod{N},\ c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\}.

For every group
\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, there exists a modular curve
X_{\Delta}(N)
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. It corresponds to the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{\Delta}(N)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):(a\textup{ mod }N)\in\Delta,\ c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\}.

Since
-I
acts trivially on the upper half plane
\mathcal{H}^{*}
(where
I\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is an identity matrix), the curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
and
X_{\pm\Delta}(N)
are isomorphic. Therefore, in this paper we will always assume that
-1\in\Delta
.

For every group
\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, there exists an intermediate modular curve
X_{\Delta}(N)
lying between the curves
X_{1}(N)
and
X_{0}(N)
. Notice that, when
\Delta=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, the curve
X_{\Delta}(N)
is actually the curve
X_{0}(N)
and that, when
\Delta=\{\pm 1\}
, the curve
X_{\Delta}(N)
is the curve
X_{\pm 1}(N)
which is isomorphic to the curve
X_{1}(N)
. Moreover, if

\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times},

then we have natural projections

X_{1}(N)\to X_{\Delta_{1}}(N)\to X_{\Delta_{2}}(N)\to X_{0}(N)

defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

It is also possible to define modular curves in another way. For every group
H\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, there exists a modular curve
X_{H}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Using the same argument as before with the curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
, we may assume that
-I\in H
without loss of generality. If
H
has full determinant (that is, if
\det H=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
), the curve
X_{H}
is ensured to be geometrically irreducible.

Suppose that
H\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
such that
-I\in H
and that
H
has full determinant. Then there is a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
such that the curves
X_{H}
and
X(\Gamma)
are isomorphic. It is defined as follows:

H_{0}:=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})\cap H,\ \Gamma:=\{A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):(A\textup{ mod }N)\in H_{0}\}.

It is not hard to check that
\Gamma(N)\subseteq\Gamma
, therefore
\Gamma
is indeed a congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Conversely, in the case of intermediate modular curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
, its isomorphic curve
X_{H}
is defined as

H:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}):a\in\Delta,\ c=0\right\}.

We can easily see that
-I\in H
and that
H
has full determinant.

Now we define the gonality of a curve. Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over a field
k
. The
k
-gonality of
C
, denoted by
\textup{gon}_{k}C
, is the least degree of a non-constant
k
-rational morphism
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

[
26

[

[

3.
Results

In this section we list the known results that will be used to determine the
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality of intermediate modular curves. We first mention two obvious lower bounds for a curve
C
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
:

\displaystyle\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(C)

\displaystyle\leq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C),

\displaystyle\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C)

\displaystyle\leq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C).

Here
p
is a prime of good reduction for
C
.

A very important result we will use throughout the paper is Poonen’s
[
23
, Proposition A.1.]
, stated below.

Proposition 3.1

(Poonen)
.

Let
X
be a curve of genus
g
over a field
k
.

(i)

If
L
is a field extension of
k
, then
\textup{gon}_{L}(X)\leq\textup{gon}_{k}(X)
.

(ii)

If
k
is algebraically closed and
L
is a field extension of
k
, then
\textup{gon}_{L}(X)=\textup{gon}_{k}(X)
.

(iii)

If
g\geq 2
, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq 2g-2
.

(iv)

If
g\geq 2
and
X(k)\neq\emptyset
, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq g
.

(v)

If
k
is algebraically closed, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq\frac{g+3}{2}
.

(vi)

If
\pi:X\to Y
is a dominant
k
-rational map, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\leq\deg\pi\cdot\textup{gon}_{k}(Y)
.

(vii)

If
\pi:X\to Y
is a dominant
k
-rational map, then
\textup{gon}_{k}(X)\geq\textup{gon}_{k}(Y)
.

Since all modular curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
have at least one rational cusp, this result implies that their
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality is bounded from above by their genus. Moreover, if we have

\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times},

then, due to the natural projections

X_{1}(N)\to X_{\Delta_{1}}(N)\to X_{\Delta_{2}}(N)\to X_{0}(N)

and
Proposition

3.1
(vii), we conclude that

(1)

\displaystyle\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X_{1}(N))\geq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X_{\Delta_{1}}(N))

\displaystyle\geq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X_{\Delta_{2}}(N))\geq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X_{0}(N)),

(2)

\displaystyle\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(X_{1}(N))\geq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(X_{\Delta_{1}}(N))

\displaystyle\geq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(X_{\Delta_{2}}(N))\geq\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(X_{0}(N)).

Therefore, when searching for tetragonal curves
X_{\Delta}(N)
, we may restrict ourselves to the levels

has

-gonality at most

3.2.

This is a very useful tool for producing a lower bound on the gonality (see
[

for a proof).

Proposition 3.10

(Castelnuovo-Severi inequality)
.

Let
k
be a perfect field, and let
X,\ Y,\ Z
be curves over
k
with genera
g(X),g(Y),g(Z)
. Let non-constant morphisms
\pi_{Y}:X\rightarrow Y
and
\pi_{Z}:X\rightarrow Z
over
k
be given, and let their degrees be
m
and
n
, respectively. Assume that there is no morphism

of degree
>1
through which both
\pi_{Y}
and
\pi_{Z}
factor. Then the following inequality holds:

(3)

g(X)\leq m\cdot g(Y)+n\cdot g(Z)+(m-1)(n-1).

Since
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{Q}
are both perfect fields, we can use Castelnuovo-Severi inequality to get lower bounds on both
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{Q}
-gonalities.

As we can see, the assumption of the Castelnuovo-Severi inequality is that there is no morphism

defined over
\overline{k}
through which both
\pi_{Y}
and
\pi_{Z}
factor. However, Khawaja and Siksek have recently shown
[

, Theorem 14]
that we can weaken this assumption. Namely, that there is no morphism
X\to X^{\prime}
defined over
k
through which both
\pi_{Y}
and
\pi_{Z}
factor.

Theorem 3.20

(The Tower Theorem)
.

Let
C
be a curve defined over a perfect field
k
such that
C(k)\neq 0
and let
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a non-constant morphism over
\overline{k}
of degree
d
. Then there exists a curve
C^{\prime}
defined over
k
and a non-constant morphism
C\to C^{\prime}
defined over
k
of degree
d^{\prime}
dividing
d
such that the genus of
C^{\prime}
is
\leq(\frac{d}{d^{\prime}}-1)^{2}
.

Corollary 3.21
.

[
19
, Corollary 4.6. (ii)]

Let
C
be a curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with
\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{C}}(C)=4
and

and such that
C(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset
. Then
\textup{gon}_{\mathbb{Q}}(C)=4
.

3.20
and

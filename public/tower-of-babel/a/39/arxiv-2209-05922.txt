Abstract
In this paper, we propose a discrete Hamilton–Jacobi theory for (discrete) Hamiltonian dynamics defined on a (discrete) contact manifold. To this end, we first provide a novel geometric Hamilton–Jacobi theory for continuous contact Hamiltonian dynamics. Then, rooting on the discrete contact Lagrangian formulation, we obtain the discrete equations for Hamiltonian dynamics by the discrete Legendre transformation. Based on the discrete contact Hamilton equation, we construct a discrete Hamilton–Jacobi equation for contact Hamiltonian dynamics. We show how the discrete Hamilton–Jacobi equation is related to the continuous Hamilton–Jacobi theory presented in this work. Then, we propose geometric foundations of the discrete Hamilton–Jacobi equations on contact manifolds in terms of discrete contact flows. At the end of the paper we provide a numerical example to test the theory. Keywords: Hamilton-Jacobi theory; Discrete dynamics; Contact manifolds; Discrete Hamilton–Jacobi. Introduction

This work lies in the intersection among the geometric Hamilton-Jacobi (abbreviated as HJ) theory, discrete dynamics and contact geometry. A HJ theory for discrete Hamiltonian dynamics on contact manifolds was missing in the literature, this is why our aim in this work is to fill this gap by introducing a discrete HJ equation for discrete contact Hamiltonian dynamics. Additionally, we shall examine the geometric foundations of the discrete HJ equation. Accordingly, we shall propose a geometric discrete HJ theorem in the contact framework.

2.1
Contact Manifolds

We call a contact manifold a pair
(M,\eta)
, where
M
is an odd-dimensional manifold, say
(2n+1)
-dimensional, with a contact form
\eta
, i.e., a one-form on
M
such that
\eta\wedge\mathrm{d}\eta^{n}\neq 0
is a volume form. This type of manifold has a distinguished vector field, the Reeb vector field
\mathcal{R}
, which is the unique vector field that satisfies the two following identities.

i_{\mathcal{R}}\mathrm{d}\eta=0,\qquad\eta(\mathcal{R})=1.

(2.1)

There exists a Darboux coordinate system
(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p},s)=(q^{i},p_{i},s)
on
M
(with
i
ranging from 1 to the dimension of
M
) such that the contact one-form reads

\eta=\mathrm{d}s-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}.

(2.2)

In these coordinates, we have
\mathcal{R}=\nabla_{s}=\partial/\partial s
. This local observation provides an example of a contact manifold as the extended cotangent bundle

\big(T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R},\eta=\mathrm{d}s-\theta_{Q}\big)

(2.3)

where
\theta_{Q}
is the pullback of the tautological one-form of
T^{*}Q
.

Musical Morphisms.
For a contact manifold
(M,\eta)
, there is a musical isomorphism

\flat:TM\longrightarrow T^{*}M,\qquad v\mapsto\iota_{v}\mathrm{d}\eta+\eta(v)\eta.

(2.4)

This mapping takes the Reeb field
\mathcal{R}
to the contact one-form
\eta
. We denote the inverse of this mapping by
\sharp
. Referring to this, we define a bivector field
\Lambda
on
M
as

\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=-\mathrm{d}\eta(\sharp\alpha,\sharp\beta).

(2.5)

Then referring to
\Lambda
we introduce the following musical mapping

\sharp_{\Lambda}:T^{*}M\longrightarrow TM,\qquad\alpha\mapsto\Lambda(\alpha,\bullet)=\sharp\alpha-\alpha(\mathcal{R})\mathcal{R}.

(2.6)

Evidently, the mapping
\sharp_{\Lambda}
fails to be an isomorphism. Notice that the kernel is spanned by the contact one-form
\eta
.

Legendrian Submanifolds.

Let
(M,\eta)
be a contact manifold. Recall the associated bivector field
\Lambda
defined in (
2.5
). Consider a linear subbundle
\Xi
of the tangent bundle
TM
(that is, a distribution on
M
). We define the contact complement of
\Xi
as

\Xi^{\perp}:=\sharp_{\Lambda}(\Xi^{o}),

(2.7)

where the sharp map on the right hand side is the one in (
2.6
) and
\Xi^{o}
is the annihilator of
\Xi
. We say that
N
is Legendrian if
TN^{\perp}=TN
.

Consider the contact manifold
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
in (
2.3
) and let
W
be a real valued function on the base manifold
Q
. Its first prolongation is

\rm{J}^{1}W:Q\longrightarrow T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathbf{q}\mapsto\big(\mathbf{q},W_{\mathbf{q}},W(\mathbf{q})\big).

(2.8)

The image space of the first prolongation
\rm{J}^{1}W
is a Legendrian submanifold of
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
. The converse of this assertion is also true, that is, if the image space of a section of
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto Q
is a Legendrian submanifold then it is the first prolongation of a function
W
.

Contact Diffeomorphisms.
Let
(M,\eta)
and
(\widehat{M},\widehat{\eta})
be two contact manifolds. A diffeomorphism
\varphi
from
M
to
\widehat{M}
is said to be a contact diffeomorphism (or contactomorphism) if it preserves the contact structures, that is,
T\varphi(\ker\eta)=\ker\widehat{\eta}
. In terms of contact forms, a contact diffeomorphism
\varphi
is the one satisfying

\varphi^{*}\widehat{\eta}=\mu\eta.

(2.9)

where
\mu
is a conformal factor. If we additionally impose that the conformal factor
\mu
in definition (
2.9
) has to be equal to one, we arrive at the conservation of the contact form.
We call such a mapping a strict contact diffeomorphism (or quantomorphism).

Consider two contact manifolds
(M,\eta)
and
(\widehat{M},\widehat{\eta})
. A contact product is the product manifold
\widehat{M}\times M\times\mathbb{R}
with a contact one-form
\tau\widehat{\eta}\ominus\eta
where
\tau
is a global coordinate on
\mathbb{R}

[
3
]
. It is possible to validate that the graph of a contact diffeomorphism
\varphi
is a Legendrian submanifold of the contact product
\widehat{M}\times M\times\mathbb{R}
.

Generating Functions For Legendrian Submanifolds.

In particular, consider two same dimensional extended cotangent bundles
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
and
T^{*}\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with Darboux’ coordinates
(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p},s)
and
(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\widehat{\mathbf{p}},\widehat{s})
, respectively. Then, we determine the contact product
\big(T^{*}\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\big)\times\big(T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\big)\times\mathbb{R}
of these two contact manifolds and consider it as a fiber bundle over the product manifold
\widehat{Q}\times Q\times\mathbb{R}
given by

\big(T^{*}\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\big)\times\big(T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\big)\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\widehat{Q}\times Q\times\mathbb{R},\qquad(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\widehat{\mathbf{p}},\widehat{s};\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p},s;\tau)\mapsto(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s}),

(2.10)

where
\tau
is the coordinate on the extended
\mathbb{R}
. In the contact product manifold, the product contact one-form is defined to be

\tau\widehat{\eta}-\eta=\tau(\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}})-(\mathrm{d}s-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}).

(2.11)

Determining the isomorphism

\big(T^{*}\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\big)\times\big(T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\big)\times\mathbb{R}\simeq T^{*}(\widehat{Q}\times Q\times\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R},

(2.12)

we provide Darboux coordinates for the contact product manifold as

(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}},\boldsymbol{\pi},\widehat{\pi};z)=(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s};\tau\widehat{\mathbf{p}},-\mathbf{p},-\tau;-s).

(2.13)

This enables us to recast the contact one-form (
2.11
) in the canonical form given in (
2.2
). So that the one-form (
2.11
) turns out to be

\tau\widehat{\eta}-\eta=\mathrm{d}z-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}-\boldsymbol{\pi}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}-\widehat{\pi}\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}.

(2.14)

On the base manifold
\widehat{Q}\times Q\times\mathbb{R}
, we define a smooth function in form

W(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s})=\widehat{s}+S(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s}).

(2.15)

for a function
S
. The theory manifests that the image space of the first jet
\rm{J}^{1}W
is a Legendrian submanifold of the contact product manifold (
2.10
). The contact diffeomorphism determined by the Legendrian submanifold
im(\rm{J}^{1}W)
is computed to be

T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow T^{*}\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}},\qquad(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p},s)\mapsto(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\widehat{\mathbf{p}},\widehat{s})

(2.16)

where one has the following identifications

\mathbf{p}=D_{2}S(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s}),\qquad s=\widehat{s}+S(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s}),\qquad\widehat{\mathbf{p}}=-\frac{D_{1}S(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s})}{1+D_{3}S(\widehat{\mathbf{q}},\mathbf{q},\widehat{s})},

(2.17)

where
D_{i}S
represents the partial derivative of
S
with respect to its
i
-th entry.

2.2
Contact Lagrangian Dynamics

Now we review the Lagrangian picture of contact systems. In
[
15
]
we give a more comprehensive description which also covers the case of singular Lagrangians. Let
Q
be a
n
-dimensional configuration manifold and consider the extended tangent bundle
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
. If
\mathbf{q}=(q^{i})
is a local coordinate system, then the induced coordinates on the
(2n+1)
- dimensional manifold
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
are
(\mathbf{q},\dot{\mathbf{q}},s)
.

Herglotz’s Action and Herglotz’s Equation.

Consider two points
\mathbf{q}_{0}
and
\mathbf{q}_{T}
on
Q
, a Lagrangian
L
on the extended tangent bundle
TQ\times\mathbb{R}
and the following initial value problem

\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t}=L\Big(\mathbf{q},\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}}{\mathrm{d}t},s\Big),\qquad s(0)=s_{0},

(2.18)

where
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}(t)
is the curve containing
\mathbf{q}_{0}
and
\mathbf{q}_{T}
as initial and final points, i.e.,
\mathbf{q}(0)=\mathbf{q}_{0}
and
\mathbf{q}(T)=\mathbf{q}_{T}
, with
0\leq t\leq T
. Evidently, the Cauchy problem (
2.18
) will be different for different curves
\mathbf{q}(t)
. So that a solution
s=s(t)
of the problem depends on the curve
\mathbf{q}(t)
substituted in the Lagrangian function.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(Q)
be the space of all smooth curves on
Q
joining
\mathbf{q}_{0}
and
\mathbf{q}_{T}
. This space depends on the end points but we omit this fact in the notation.
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(Q)
is an infinite dimensional manifold. According to the Cauchy problem in (
2.18
), for every initial value
s(0)=s_{0}
, we determine the Herglotz action as a map from the product space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(Q)\times\mathbb{R}
to the real numbers as follows

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(Q)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad(\mathbf{q},s_{0})\mapsto s(T)-s(0)=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t}dt=\int_{0}^{T}L\left(\mathbf{q},\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}}{\mathrm{d}t},s\right)dt.

(2.19)

The extreme values of the action are the curves
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}(t)
solving the Herglotz’s (generalized Euler-Lagrange) equations
[
32
,
33
,
29
]

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}L_{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}-L_{\mathbf{q}}=L_{s}L_{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}.

(2.20)

2.3
Contact Hamiltonian Dynamics

Consider a contact manifold
(M,\eta)
. For a Hamiltonian function
H
, the contact Hamiltonian vector field is defined in terms of the contact one-form
\eta
as

\iota_{X_{H}}\eta=-H,\qquad\iota_{X_{H}}\mathrm{d}\eta=\mathrm{d}H-\mathcal{R}(H)\eta,

(2.21)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the Reeb vector field. A direct computation determines that a Hamiltonian flow does not preserve the contact one-form

\mathcal{L}_{X_{H}}\eta=\mathrm{d}\iota_{X_{H}}\eta+\iota_{X_{H}}\mathrm{d}\eta=-\mathcal{R}(H)\eta.

(2.22)

Notice that, according to (
2.22
), the flow of a contact Hamiltonian system preserves the contact structure which is defined to be the kernel of the contact one-form.
X_{H}
does not preserve the Hamiltonian function, nor the volume form
\mathrm{d}\eta^{n}\wedge\eta
. Instead, we obtain

{\mathcal{L}}_{X_{H}}\,H=-\mathcal{R}(H)H,\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{X_{H}}\,(\mathrm{d}\eta^{n}\wedge\eta)=-(n+1)\mathcal{R}(H)\mathrm{d}\eta^{n}\wedge\eta.

(2.23)

As manifested previously, all contact manifolds locally resemble the extended cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
. In this local view
(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p},s)
, the Hamiltonian vector field turns out to be

X_{H}=H_{\mathbf{p}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}-\big(H_{\mathbf{q}}+\mathbf{p}H_{s}\big)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}+\big(\mathbf{p}\cdot H_{\mathbf{p}}-H\big)\nabla_{s}

(2.24)

Thus, an integral curve
(\mathbf{q}(t),\mathbf{p}(t),s(t))
of
X_{H}
satisfies the
contact Hamilton equations

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}}{\mathrm{d}t}=H_{\mathbf{p}},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{p}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\big(H_{\mathbf{q}}+\mathbf{p}H_{s}\big),\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathbf{p}\cdot H_{\mathbf{p}}-H.

(2.25)

The Legendre Transformation.
The fiber derivative
\mathbb{F}L
of a Lagrangian function
L
determines a mapping from the extended tangent bundle to the extended cotangent bundle as

\mathbb{F}L:TQ\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R},\qquad(\mathbf{q},\dot{\mathbf{q}},s)\mapsto(\mathbf{q},L_{\dot{\mathbf{q}}},s).

(2.26)

See that, for a regular Lagrangian,
\mathbb{F}L
is a contactomorphism from the contact manifold
TM\times\mathbb{R}
equipped with the contact one-form
\eta_{L}=(\mathbb{F}L)^{*}\eta
to the contact manifold
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
equipped with
\eta
in (
2.3
). In this situation, we can define the corresponding Hamiltonian function as

H:T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad H=\dot{\mathbf{q}}\cdot L_{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}-L.

(2.27)

A direct computation proves that the contact Hamilton equations are the generalized Euler-Lagrange equations, they coincide for regular Lagrangian functions.
For the Legendre transformation of nonregular cases, we cite
[
23
,
15
,
13
]
.

2.4
A Continuous Geometric Hamilton-Jacobi Theory on Contact Manifolds

We start with the fibration
Q\mapsto\mathbb{R}
whose first jet bundle is the extended cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
. In this case, the fibration is given by the target map

\rho:T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow Q,\qquad(\mathbf{z},s)\mapsto\pi_{Q}(\mathbf{z}),

(2.28)

where
\pi_{Q}
is the cotangent bundle projection.
The first prolongation of a smooth function
F
on
Q
is a section of the projection
\rho
. We write the first prolongation as

\gamma=\rm{J}^{1}F:Q\longrightarrow T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathbf{q}\mapsto(\mathbf{q},F_{\mathbf{q}},F(\mathbf{q})).

(2.29)

Notice that
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
is a contact manifold and the image space of the first prolongation
\gamma
is a Legendrian submanifold of this space.

Consider a section
\gamma
in form (
2.29
) and a contact Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
given as in (
2.24
). We define a vector field

X_{H}^{\gamma}:=T\rho\circ X_{H}\circ\gamma,

(2.30)

on the base manifold
Q
according to the commutativity of the following diagram

\textstyle{T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\scriptstyle{X_{H}}
\textstyle{T(T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{T{\rho}}
\textstyle{Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\scriptstyle{X^{\gamma}_{H}}
\textstyle{TQ\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{T\gamma}

(2.31)

where
T{\rho}
is the tangent mapping of the fibration
\rho
in (
2.28
). Next, we state a geometric Hamilton-Jacobi theorem for contact Hamiltonian dynamics.

Theorem 1
.

For the first prolongation
\gamma
of a function
F
on
Q
, the following two conditions are equivalent:

1.

The vector fields
X_{H}
and
X^{\gamma}_{H}
are
\gamma
-related, that is

T\gamma\circ X_{H}^{\gamma}=X_{H}\circ\gamma

(2.32)

where
T\gamma:TQ\mapsto T(T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R})
is the tangent mapping of the section
\gamma
.

2.

The equation

d(H\circ\gamma)=0

(2.33)

is fulfilled.

Proof.

We prove this theorem in local coordinates. The restriction of the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
to the image of
\gamma
is computed to be

\begin{split}X_{H}\circ\gamma(\mathbf{q})&=\Big(\mathbf{q},F_{\mathbf{q}},F(\mathbf{q});H_{\mathbf{p}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)},-H_{\mathbf{q}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)}-H_{s}F_{\mathbf{q}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)},F_{\mathbf{q}}\cdot H_{\mathbf{q}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)}-H\Big|_{im(\gamma)}\Big).\end{split}

(2.34)

Using
T\rho
, we map this vector field down to the tangent bundle of
Q
. This is the projected vector field (
2.30
) that is

X^{\gamma}_{H}(\mathbf{q})=H_{\mathbf{p}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}.

(2.35)

On the other hand, the tangent mapping of the section
\gamma
is computed to be

T\gamma(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{\dot{q}})=\big(\mathbf{q},F_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{q}),F(\mathbf{q});\mathbf{\dot{q}},F_{\mathbf{qq}}(\mathbf{q})\mathbf{\dot{q}},F_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{q})\cdot\mathbf{\dot{q}}\big),

(2.36)

where the notation
F_{\mathbf{qq}}(\mathbf{q})\mathbf{\dot{q}}
stands for the multiplication of the Hessian matrix
F_{\mathbf{qq}}
with the column vector
\mathbf{\dot{q}}
.
Accordingly, the left hand side of the equation (
2.32
) is computed to be

\begin{split}T\gamma\circ X^{\gamma}_{H}(\mathbf{q})&=\big(\mathbf{q},F_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{q}),F(\mathbf{q});H_{\mathbf{p}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)},F_{\mathbf{qq}}H_{\mathbf{p}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)},F_{\mathbf{q}}\cdot H_{\mathbf{q}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)}\big).\end{split}

(2.37)

To satisfy (
2.32
), the expressions (
2.34
) and (
2.37
) must be same. The first three entries of these local realizations are the same. The first of the fiber variables (that coincides with the fourth entries) are also the same. See that the fifth and the sixth entries of (
2.34
) and (
2.37
) are not equal. The fifth entries are equal if and only if

F_{\mathbf{qq}}H_{\mathbf{p}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)}+H_{\mathbf{q}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)}+H_{s}F_{\mathbf{q}}\Big|_{im(\gamma)}=0.

(2.38)

See that, the identity (
2.38
) can be compactly written as
d(H\circ\gamma)=0
. This gives that
H
turns out to be a constant if it is restricted to the image space of the first prolongation
\gamma
. On the other hand, the sixth entries are the same if and only if
H
is not only constant but further it vanishes on the image space of the first prolongation
\gamma
. This is the second condition (
2.33
). The inverse of the assertion is proved by reversing the arguments. This completes the proof.

]
for the case of symplectic manifolds. In accordance with this, we state a geometric Hamilton-Jacobi theory. This is a contact generalization of the one presented in
[
19
]
.

3.1
Discrete Contact Lagrangian Dynamics

In the framework of discrete dynamics the role of differentiable curves in a configuration manifold
Q
is played by the finite sequences of points in
Q
. Therefore, we are interested in the space of sequences consisting of
N+1
points, denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{N}(Q)
. It is interesting to note that
\mathcal{C}^{N}(Q)
is a manifold isomorphic to
N+1
number of copies of
Q
, denoted by
Q^{N+1}
. We denote such a sequence by a set
[\mathbf{q}]=\{\mathbf{q}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{q}_{N}\}
which is a point in
Q^{N+1}
.
Here,
\mathbf{q}_{0}
is the initial point of the sequence, whereas
\mathbf{q}_{N}
is the end point. We use the subindex
\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}
to denote the location of the point in the sequence from
\kappa=0
to
\kappa=N
. We call
[\mathbf{q}]
a discrete curve.

Herglotz’s Action and Herglotz’s Equation.
A discrete Lagrangian in this framework is a real valued function
L_{d}
defined on the product space
Q\times Q\times\mathbb{R}
. Comparing with the continuous case, we see that in this geometry the role of the tangent bundle
TQ
is played by the pair
Q\times Q
. More technically, the discrete Lagrangian
L_{d}
is an approximation of the exact contact discrete Lagrangian
L_{d}^{ex}
which is defined to be

L_{d}^{ex}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})=\int_{t_{\kappa}}^{t_{\kappa+1}}{L\left(\mathbf{q},\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}}{\mathrm{d}t},s\right)dt}

(3.1)

where
\mathbf{q}
is a solution of the continuous generalized Euler-Lagrange equation (
2.20
) with boundary conditions
\mathbf{q}(t_{\kappa})=\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}
and
\mathbf{q}(t_{\kappa+1})=\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}
.
To each discrete curve
[\mathbf{q}]
and for a fixed initial value point
s_{0}
, we introduce the discrete Cauchy problem as

s_{\kappa}-s_{\kappa-1}=L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa-1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa-1}),\qquad s_{0}=s(0),

(3.2)

where
\kappa
runs from
1
to
N
.
We determine the discrete Herglotz action as a map from the product space
\mathcal{C}^{N}(Q)\times\mathbb{R}
to the real numbers that is

\mathcal{C}^{N}(Q)\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad([\mathbf{q}],s_{0})\mapsto s_{N}-s_{0}=\sum_{\kappa=0}^{N-1}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}).

(3.3)

The extreme values of the discrete action (
3.3
) are curves solving the discrete Herglotz’s (generalized discrete Euler-Lagrange) equations

\begin{split}&D_{1}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})+\big(1+D_{3}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})\big)D_{2}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa-1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa-1})=0\\
&s_{\kappa}-s_{\kappa-1}=L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa-1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa-1}),\qquad s_{0}=s(0),\end{split}

(3.4)

provided that
1+D_{3}L_{d}\neq 0
. Here,
D_{i}L_{d}
refers to the partial derivative of the discrete Lagrangian with respect to its
i
-th entry.

The Discrete Legendre Transformation.
Assume a discrete Lagrangian function
L_{d}
satisfying the condition
1+D_{3}L_{d}\neq 0
. We define the following Legendre transformations from the extended discrete space
Q\times Q\times\mathbb{R}
to the extended cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
as

\begin{split}\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+}&:Q\times Q\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R},\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})\mapsto\big(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},D_{2}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}),s_{\kappa}+L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})\big)\\
\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-}&:Q\times Q\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R},\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})\mapsto\left(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},-\frac{D_{1}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})}{1+D_{3}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})},s_{\kappa}\right).\end{split}

(3.5)

So that we have two alternative definitions of the momenta given as

\mathbf{p}^{+}_{\kappa-1,\kappa,\kappa}=D_{2}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa-1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa}),\qquad\mathbf{p}^{-}_{\kappa,\kappa+1,\kappa}=-\frac{D_{1}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})}{1+D_{3}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})}.

(3.6)

In the light of the discrete Herglotz equations (
3.4
), one can directly establish the momentum matching equation

\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})=\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+2},s_{\kappa+1}).

(3.7)

So that, in this case, one can define the momenta

\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}:=\mathbf{p}^{+}_{\kappa-1,\kappa,\kappa}=\mathbf{p}^{-}_{\kappa,\kappa+1,\kappa+1}.

(3.8)

A discrete Lagrangian
L_{d}
is called regular if the Hessian matrix obtained by taking the partial derivatives of
L_{d}
is nondegenerate, that is

\det[D_{2}D_{2}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa-1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa})]\neq 0.

(3.9)

Equivalently, a discrete Lagrangian is a regular Lagrangian if the Legendre transformations in (
3.5
) become invertible.

Diffeomorphisms.
Consider a regular discrete Lagrangian function
L_{d}
, referring to the discrete Legendre mappings in (
3.5
), one can define a local diffeomorphism on the extended discrete space as

\begin{split}\Phi&:Q\times Q\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow Q\times Q\times\mathbb{R},\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})\mapsto(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+2},s_{\kappa+1}):=(\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-})^{-1}\circ\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}).\end{split}

(3.10)

Similarly, one can define a diffeomorphism on the extended cotangent bundle as follows

\tilde{\Phi}:=\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+}\circ(\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-})^{-1}.

(3.11)

A direct comparison shows that the diffeomorphism
\Phi
given in (
3.10
) and the diffeomorphism
\tilde{\Phi}
given in (
3.11
) are related by the Legendre transformations

\tilde{\Phi}=\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+}\circ\Phi\circ(\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+})^{-1},\qquad\tilde{\Phi}=\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-}\circ\Phi\circ(\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-})^{-1}.

(3.12)

In order to be more specific about the diffeomorphism we plot the following commutative diagram.

\textstyle{(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+}}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+2},s_{\kappa+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{F}L_{d}^{+}}
\textstyle{(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\Phi}}
\textstyle{(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\Phi}}
\textstyle{(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+2},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+2},s_{\kappa+2})}

(3.13)

See that in this diagram in the bottom row we have the flow on the extended discrete cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
,
whereas the top row corresponds with the flow on the extended discrete space
Q\times Q\times\mathbb{R}
. Here, the Legendre transformations establish the equivalency between the flows.

3.2
Discrete Contact Hamiltonian Dynamics

To derive Hamiltonian dynamics, we use that a discrete contact Lagrangian is essentially a generating function of type one
[
1
]
and that
we can apply the defined Legendre transformations to the discrete Lagrangian to find a discrete Hamiltonian
[
1
,
26
]
. We start with the definition of momentum
\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}
and the local inversion operation

\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}=D_{2}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}),\qquad\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}=\Psi(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}),

(3.14)

provided that the regularity condition holds, i.e., the operator
D_{2}D_{2}L_{d}
does not vanish.
Referring to these local identifications, we introduce the right discrete Hamiltonian function on the extended cotangent bundle as

H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})=\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}-L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}).

(3.15)

By taking the partial derivatives of the Hamiltonian function
H^{+}_{d}
with respect to its arguments (applying the chain rule referring to (
3.14
)), we arrive at the following expressions

\begin{split}D_{1}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})&=-D_{1}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})\\
D_{2}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})&=\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}\\
D_{3}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})&=-D_{3}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}).\end{split}

(3.16)

Notice that, these equations hold under the identifications (
3.14
).
The second equation in the list determines
\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}
. We substitute the first and third identities in (
3.16
) into the discrete Euler-Lagrange equation (
3.4
), and using (
3.14
), we get

\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}=\frac{D_{1}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})}{1-D_{3}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})}.

(3.17)

Considering the discrete Cauchy problem (
3.2
) as part of the set, we arrive at the following system of equations, which we call right discrete contact Hamilton equations,

\begin{split}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}&=D_{2}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}),\\
\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}&=\frac{D_{1}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})}{(1-D_{3}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}))},\\
s_{\kappa+1}&=s_{\kappa}+\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}\cdot D_{2}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa})-H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa}).\end{split}

(3.18)

Left Hamilton Equations.

The left discrete Hamiltonian function on the extended cotangent bundle is

H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})=-\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}-L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1}).

(3.19)

By taking the partial derivatives of the Hamiltonian function
H^{-}_{d}
, we arrive at the following expressions

\begin{split}D_{1}H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})&=-\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}\\
D_{2}H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})&=\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}\\
D_{3}H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})&=-D_{3}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1}).\end{split}

(3.20)

The second equation in the list determines
\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}
.

\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}=D_{2}H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})=-D_{2}L_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1}).

(3.21)

Considering the discrete Cauchy problem (
3.2
) as part of the set, we arrive at the following system of equations, which we call the left discrete contact Hamilton equations,

\begin{split}&\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}=-D_{1}H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})\\
&\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}=D_{2}H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})\\
&s_{\kappa}=s_{\kappa+1}-\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot D_{1}H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})+H^{-}_{d}(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},s_{\kappa+1})\end{split}

(3.22)

From now on, we shall focus on the right discrete dynamics, since everything can be reenacted in terms of left discrete approach straightforwardly.

3.3
Discrete Contact Hamilton–Jacobi theory

The discrete contact Hamilton–Jacobi equation can be derived in terms of a generating function of a coordinate transformation that trivializes the dynamics, as it is done in the classical continuous theory
[
26
]
. For this we need a generating function to introduce a contact diffeomorphism from the contact manifold
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
to the contact manifold
T^{*}\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
that is

T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow T^{*}\widehat{Q}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}},\qquad(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa})\rightarrow(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa}).

(3.23)

Recall from (
2.17
) that, in Section
2.1
, we have obtained a generating function defined on the product space
\widehat{Q}\times Q\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
realizing the transformation. The main result for the generating function is described in the next theorem, following the lines of
[
42
]
in the symplectic case.

Theorem 2
.

Consider the right discrete contact Hamilton equations (
3.18
) and a discrete phase space
(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},s_{\kappa})
. Consider also a transformation (
3.23
)
that satisfies:

1.

The old and new coordinates are related by a generating function
S^{\kappa}:\widehat{Q}\times Q\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

of the type

\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}=D_{2}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa}),\quad s_{\kappa}=\widehat{s}_{\kappa}+S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa}),\quad\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}=-\frac{D_{1}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})}{1+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})}.

(3.24)

2.

The dynamics in the new coordinates
(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})
is rendered trivial, i.e.,

(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})=(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1}).

(3.25)

Then, the set of functions
\{S^{\kappa}\}
satisfies the right discrete contact Hamilton–Jacobi equation:

\begin{split}S^{\kappa+1}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{0},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{0})&-S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{0},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{0})-D_{2}S^{\kappa+1}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{0},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{0})\cdot\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}\\
&+H^{+}_{d}(\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},D_{2}S^{\kappa+1}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{0},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{0}),\widehat{s}_{0}+S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{0},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{0}))=0.\end{split}

(3.26)

Proof.

We establish the proof in four steps.

Step 1.
Consider the right discrete contact Hamilton equations (
3.18
) in the new variables
(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})
, that is

\begin{split}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}&=D_{2}\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa}),\\
\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}&=D_{1}\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})/\big(1-D_{3}\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\big),\\
\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1}&=\widehat{s}_{\kappa}+\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot D_{2}\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})-\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa}).\end{split}

(3.27)

These equations can be recast in the form of a total derivative of the right discrete contact Hamiltonian in the following way

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})&=D_{1}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}+D_{2}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt+D_{3}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\widehat{s_{\kappa}}\\
&=\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}+\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}+D_{3}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}\end{split}

(3.28)

where we have employed (
3.27
) in the total derivative. More explicitly we have replaced
D_{1}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}
from the second equation in (
3.27
). Notice that we have used the following notation for the discrete contact form

\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}=\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa}-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}.

(3.29)

Step 2.
Start with the generating function
S=S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})
satisfying (
3.24
). The total derivative of the generating function as

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})&=D_{1}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}+D_{2}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot d\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa}\\
&=-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}+\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot d\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}\end{split}

(3.30)

in which we can introduce
D_{1}S^{k}
and
D_{2}S^{k}
from (
3.24
).
Recursively, we can write the expression for
\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa+1}
, as:

\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa+1}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1})=-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}+\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}\cdot d\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}+D_{3}S^{\kappa+1}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa+1}

(3.31)

where
\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa+1}=\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1}-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}
.

Coming back to the expression of the total derivative of the Hamiltonian (
3.28
), realize that the second term
\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}
can be written as
d(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1})-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}
. Then we substitute the expressions (
3.30
) and (
3.31
) into the total derivative of the Hamiltonian function. In the light of these, we can continue the calculation (
3.28
) as follows

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},&\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})=\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}+\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}+D_{3}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}\\
&=\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}-\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}+\mathrm{d}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1})+D_{3}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}\\
&=\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}-\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa}\\
&\qquad-\big(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}+D_{3}S^{\kappa+1}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa+1}-\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa+1}\big)\\
&\qquad+\mathrm{d}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1})+D_{3}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}.\end{split}

(3.32)

Step 3.
We take the exterior derivative of the second identity in (
3.24
).
See that

\begin{split}ds_{\kappa}&=\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa}+\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\\
&=\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa}+D_{1}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}+D_{2}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa}\\
&=\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa}-(1+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa}))\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa}+\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\cdot\mathrm{d}\widehat{s}_{\kappa}\\
&=\big(1+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\big)\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}+\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}.\end{split}

(3.33)

So that we arrive at two expressions. One is the relationship between the contact forms in terms of the generating function

\eta_{\kappa}=\big(1+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\big)\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}.

(3.34)

Additionally, we have the followings identities

\begin{split}&\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}=ds_{\kappa}-\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa},\\
&\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa+1}=\mathrm{d}s_{\kappa+1}-\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa+1}.\end{split}

(3.35)

We continue the calculation (
3.32
) by substituting these equations. Accordingly, we have that

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}{\widehat{H}^{+}_{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})&=\mathbf{p}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa}+D_{3}S^{\kappa}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa},\widehat{s}_{\kappa})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa}-\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa}\\
&\qquad-\big(\mathbf{p}_{\kappa+1}\cdot\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1}+D_{3}S^{\kappa+1}(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{\kappa+1},\mathbf{q}_{\kappa+1},\widehat{s}_{\kappa+1})\widehat{\eta}_{\kappa+1}-\mathrm{d}S^{\kappa+1}\big)\\

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have proposed a discrete Hamilton–Jacobi equation (stated in Theorem
2
) for contact discrete Hamiltonian dynamics. We have presented its geometric foundations in terms of contact discrete flows in Theorem
3
. We have exhibited the relationship between the continuous geometric HJ Theorem
3.4
for contact Hamiltonian dynamics and Theorem
3
for discrete contact Hamiltonian dynamics.

We wish to continue in the following directions:

•

Contact Hamiltonian dynamics does not preserve the Hamiltonian function. There exists an alternative characterization of Hamiltonian dynamics on contact manifolds that preserves the energy, and it is known as evolution dynamics
[
46
,
47
]
. We wish to examine the discretization of evolution dynamics and its HJ formulation.

•

For the extended cotangent bundle
T^{*}Q\times\mathbb{R}
, the continuous HJ theory for contact Hamiltonian dynamics was presented in
[
14
,
19
,
24
]
. The authors consider the base manifold as the extended configuration space
Q\times\mathbb{R}
. In this work, we consider the base manifold as
Q
. We wish to write a discrete HJ equation on contact manifolds with base manifold
Q\times\mathbb{R}
.

•

If a Lagrangian is degenerate, then one cannot arrive at explicit Euler-Lagrange equations. In this case, the Legendre transformation is not immediate. Tulczyjew’s triple is a geometric formulation that allows us to achieve this in singular cases as well
[
49
]
. In a discrete framework, Tulczyjew’s triple was constructed in
[
38
]
. This determines a proper geometry for implicit discrete Lagrangian and Hamiltonian dynamics
[
34
]
. For the continuous case, a geometric HJ theory has been recently given in
[
21
,
22
]
in the symplectic framework and in
[
24
]
in the contact framework. We wish to concentrate on generalizing the discrete HJ theories both for symplectic and contact geometry including the implicit case. On the other hand, the Tulczyjew triple for contact geometry has been recently constructed in
[
23
]
. In the future, we aim at constructing a discrete contact Tulczyjew’s triple.

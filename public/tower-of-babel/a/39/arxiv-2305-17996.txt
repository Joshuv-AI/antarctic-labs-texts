Laplace transformations and sine-Gordon type integrable PDE
I T Habibullin, K I Faizulina, A R Khakimova

Abstract
It is well known that the Laplace cascade method is an effective tool for constructing solutions to linear equations of hyperbolic type, as well as nonlinear equations of the Liouville type. The connection between the Laplace method and soliton equations of hyperbolic type remains less studied. The article shows that the Laplace cascade also has important applications in the theory of hyperbolic equations of the soliton type. Laplace’s method provides a simple way to construct such fundamental objects related to integrability theory as the recursion operator, the Lax pair and Dubrovin-type equations, allowing one to find algebro-geometric solutions. As an application of this approach, previously unknown recursion operators and Lax pairs are found for two nonlinear integrable equations of the sine-Gordon type.

Consider a linear second order hyperbolic type PDE

u_{xy}+a(x,y)u_{x}+b(x,y)u_{y}+c(x,y)u=0.

(1.1)

Introduce new variables
u_{[1]}
and
u_{[-1]}
due to the relations (see, for example,
[
7
]
)

\eqalign{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+a\right)u=u_{[1]},\qquad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b\right)u_{[1]}=h_{[0]}u,\cr\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b\right)u=u_{[-1]},\qquad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+a\right)u_{[-1]}=k_{[0]}u.}

(1.2)

Here functions
h_{[0]}
,
k_{[0]}
defined due to the rule

h_{[0]}=a_{x}+ab-c,\qquad k_{[0]}=b_{y}+ab-c

(1.3)

are called Laplace invariants.
If the Laplace invariants do not vanish then the newly introduced functions solve linear PDE’s as well

\eqalign{u_{[1]xy}+a_{[1]}u_{[1]x}+b_{[1]}u_{[1]y}+c_{[1]}u_{[1]}=0,\cr u_{[-1]xy}+a_{[-1]}u_{[-1]x}+b_{[-1]}u_{[-1]y}+c_{[-1]}u_{[-1]}=0}

(1.4)

with the coefficients having the form

a_{[1]}=a-(\ln h_{[0]})_{y},\qquad b_{[1]}=b,\qquad c_{[1]}=a_{[1]}b_{[1]}+b_{y}-h_{[0]}

and

a_{[-1]}=a,\qquad b_{[-1]}=b-(\ln k_{[0]})_{x},\qquad c_{[-1]}=a_{[-1]}b_{[-1]}+a_{x}-k_{[0]}.

Transformations converting equation (
1.1
) into equations (
1.4
) are called Laplace y- and respectively Laplace x-transformations.

Repeated applications of these transformations create a sequence of the hyperbolic type equations referred to as the Laplace sequence:

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}u_{[i]}+a_{[i]}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u_{[i]}+b_{[i]}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}u_{[i]}+c_{[i]}u_{[i]}=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots

(1.5)

and

\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}u_{[-i]}+a_{[-i]}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u_{[-i]}+b_{[-i]}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}u_{[-i]}+c_{[-i]}u_{[-i]}=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad i=1,2,3,\ldots.

(1.6)

It is assumed that
a_{[0]}=a
,
b_{[0]}=b
,
c_{[0]}=c
,
u_{[0]}=u
. The coefficients are given by

\displaystyle a_{[i]}=a_{[i-1]}-(\ln h_{[i-1]})_{y},\qquad

\displaystyle b_{[i]}=b_{[i-1]},

(1.7)

\displaystyle k_{[i]}=h_{[i-1]},

\displaystyle c_{[i]}=a_{[i]}b_{[i]}+(b_{[i]})_{y}-h_{[i-1]},

\displaystyle h_{[i]}=2h_{[i-1]}-k_{[i-1]}-(\ln h_{[i-1]})_{xy},\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{for}\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots

and by

\displaystyle a_{[-i-1]}=a_{[-i]},

\displaystyle b_{[-i-1]}=b_{[-i]}-(\ln k_{[-i]})_{x},

(1.8)

\displaystyle h_{[-i-1]}=k_{[-i]},

\displaystyle c_{[-i-1]}=a_{[-i-1]}b_{[-i-1]}+(a_{[-i]})_{x}-k_{[-i]},

\displaystyle k_{[-i-1]}=2k_{[-i]}-h_{[-i]}-(\ln k_{[-i]})_{xy},\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{for}\quad i=1,2,3,\ldots.

The sequence of the functions
\left\{h_{[i]}\right\}_{i=-\infty}^{i=\infty}
is called the sequence of the Laplace invariants.
Equations (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) can be represented as systems of the form

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+a_{[i]}\right)u_{[i]}=u_{[i+1]},\qquad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b\right)u_{[i+1]}-h_{[i]}u_{[i]}=0

(1.9)

and correspondingly

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+b_{[-i]}\right)u_{[-i]}=u_{[-i-1]},\qquad\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+a\right)u_{[-i-1]}-k_{[-i]}u_{[-i]}=0.

(1.10)

As it is obvious from the formulas above if
h_{[i_{0}]}=0
for
i_{0}\geqslant 0
, then equation (
1.5
) is not defined for

i\geqslant i_{0}+1
. Therefore in this case the Laplace sequence terminates on the right side. Similarly, if
k_{[-i_{1}]}=0
for
i_{1}\geqslant 0
then equation (
1.6
) isn’t defined for
i\geqslant i_{1}
, i.e. the sequence terminates on the left side.

A remarkable application of the construction is based on the following statement. If the sequence of the Laplace transformations for equation (
1.1
) terminates on both sides then the equation is explicitly solved without any
quadrature. Even in the case when the sequence terminates only on one side the Laplace cascade method allows one finding of a special solution depending on an arbitrary function.

Proposition 1.
Assume that the Laplace invariants

don’t vanish for any integer

1.15

) is compatible with the constraint (
1.29
). Then for arbitrary integer

can be represented as follows

v_{[j]}=\alpha^{j}_{[-k]}v_{[-k]}+\alpha^{j}_{[-k+1]}v_{[-k+1]}+\cdots+\alpha^{j}_{[m]}v_{[m]}.

In other words the constraint (
1.29
) defines a reduction of the whole Laplace sequence.

Sketch of the proof.
By applying the operator
\left(D_{y}+a_{[m+1]}\right)
to the first equation in (
1.29
) we get the representation

v_{[m+2]}=\beta^{1}_{[-k]}v_{[-k]}+\beta^{1}_{[-k+1]}v_{[-k+1]}+\cdots+\beta^{1}_{[m+1]}v_{[m+1]}.

Now the variable
v_{[m+1]}
is easily excluded by virtue of (
1.29
). Finally we obtain

v_{[m+2]}=\alpha^{1}_{[-k]}v_{[-k]}+\alpha^{1}_{[-k+1]}v_{[-k+1]}+\cdots+\alpha^{1}_{[m]}v_{[m]}.

Continuing this way we find the desired representation for any

v_{[-k-2]}
we apply to the second part of (
1.29

\left(D_{x}+b_{[-k-1]}\right)
.

\eqalign{\tilde{\beta}_{[-1]}=C_{1}u_{x}+C_{2},\qquad&\tilde{\alpha}_{[1]}=C_{1}u_{y}+C_{3},}

where

and

are some constants.
Consequently functions

and

Let us concentrate on the remaining four equations of system (

and

\eqalign{\beta_{[0]u_{y}}(u_{y}^{2}+1)^{3/2}-\sqrt{u_{x}}(C_{1}u_{x}+C_{2})=0,\cr\beta_{[1]u_{y}}(u_{y}^{2}+1)+\beta_{[1]}u_{y}=0,\cr 2\alpha_{[-1]u_{x}}u_{x}+\alpha_{[-1]}=0,\cr 4\alpha_{[0]u_{x}}u_{x}^{3/2}+\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}(C_{1}u_{y}+C_{3})=0.}

It is obvious that these equations are effectively solved and give the expressions

\eqalign{\beta_{[0]}=\frac{(C_{1}u_{x}+C_{2})\sqrt{u_{x}}u_{y}}{\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}+\tilde{\beta}_{[0]}(u_{x}),\cr\beta_{[1]}=\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{[1]}(u_{x})}{\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}},\cr\alpha_{[-1]}=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{[-1]}(u_{y})}{\sqrt{u_{x}}},\cr\alpha_{[0]}=\frac{(C_{1}u_{y}+C_{3})\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}{2\sqrt{u_{x}}}+\tilde{\alpha}_{[0]}(u_{y}),}

where
\tilde{\alpha}_{[i]}(u_{y})
and
\tilde{\beta}_{[j]}(u_{x})
are some functions to be determined.

Let us specify the third and fourth equations of system (
2.7
) by virtue of found expressions of the functions
\alpha_{[1]}
and

\eqalign{\frac{\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{u_{x}}}&\left(\tilde{\beta}_{[1]u_{x}}u_{x}+\tilde{\beta}_{[0]}\sqrt{u_{x}}-\tilde{\beta}_{[1]}\right)+\cr&+\left(C_{1}\tilde{\beta}_{[1]}+C_{1}u_{x}^{3/2}+C_{2}\sqrt{u_{x}}\right)u_{y}+C_{3}\tilde{\beta}_{[1]}=0,}

\eqalign{\frac{\sqrt{u_{x}}}{\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}&\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{[-1]u_{y}}(u_{y}^{2}+1)-2\tilde{\alpha}_{[-1]}u_{y}+\tilde{\alpha}_{[0]}\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}\right)+\cr&+C_{1}\tilde{\alpha}_{[-1]}u_{x}+C_{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{[-1]}+\frac{\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}{2}(C_{1}u_{y}+C_{3})=0.}

,

and

Therefore the sought functions

After all of the specifications equations (

) and (

Thus all of the functions are found, they are of the form (

) and (
3.16
) are very close to each other we explain the procedure of derivation of the Lax pair in a very short way.

We begin with the third order ordinary differential equation (
2.44
). At first we reduce its order and find

\qquad v_{yy}=\frac{u_{y}u_{yy}}{u_{y}^{2}+1}v_{y}+u_{y}\sqrt{\lambda v_{y}^{2}+(u_{y}^{2}+1)(\lambda^{2}v^{2}-\lambda v^{2}+C)}-(\lambda-1)(u_{y}^{2}+1)v.

(3.17)

Then we derive a nonlinear first order PDE

v_{x}=\frac{\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+1}}{\lambda\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}\sqrt{\lambda v_{y}^{2}+(u_{y}^{2}+1)(\lambda^{2}v^{2}-\lambda v^{2}+C)},

(3.18)

by applying operator
D_{x}
to (
3.17
) and simplifying due to equation (
1.35
) and its linearization

v_{xy}=\frac{u_{x}\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}{2\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+1}}v_{x}+\frac{u_{y}\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}v_{y}.

(3.19)

We take
C=0
and pass to new variables
\varphi
,
\psi
in the system of equations (
3.17
), (
3.18
), (
3.19
) by setting

\displaystyle v=2\varphi\psi,

(3.20)

\displaystyle v_{y}=\sqrt{\lambda-1}\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}(\varphi^{2}-\psi^{2}).

(3.21)

The compatibility condition of the equations (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) as well as the consistency of (
3.21
) and (
3.17
) imply the
y
-part of the Lax pair to equation (
1.35
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\varphi_{y}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}u_{y}}{2}\varphi-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda-1}\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}{2}\psi,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\psi_{y}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda-1}\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}}{2}\varphi-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}u_{y}}{2}\psi.\end{array}\right.

(3.22)

Let us derive the
x
-part of the Lax pair. To this end we apply operator
D_{x}
on (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) and then simplify by virtue of (
3.18
) and (
3.19
). As a result we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\varphi_{x}=\frac{u_{x}}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\varphi+\frac{\sqrt{\lambda-1}\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+1}}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\psi,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\psi_{x}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda-1}\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+1}}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\varphi-\frac{u_{x}}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\psi.\end{array}\right.

(3.23)

The obtained Lax pair (
3.22
), (

\xi

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\varphi_{y}=\frac{\xi+\xi^{-1}}{4}u_{y}\varphi-\frac{\xi-\xi^{-1}}{4}\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}\psi,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\psi_{y}=\frac{\xi-\xi^{-1}}{4}\sqrt{u_{y}^{2}+1}\varphi-\frac{\xi+\xi^{-1}}{4}u_{y}\psi,\end{array}\right.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\varphi_{x}=\frac{1}{\xi+\xi^{-1}}u_{x}\varphi+\frac{\xi-\xi^{-1}}{2(\xi+\xi^{-1})}\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+1}\psi,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\psi_{x}=\frac{\xi-\xi^{-1}}{2(\xi+\xi^{-1})}\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+1}\varphi-\frac{1}{\xi+\xi^{-1}}u_{x}\psi.\end{array}\right.

Conclusions

The class of nonlinear integrable equations of hyperbolic type of the form (
1.11
) has been actively studied over the past few centuries. With the discovery of the inverse scattering transform method, interest in equations of this class increased significantly (see, for example,
[
17
]
). Of particular interest is the most important problem of enumerating all integrable equations (
1.11
) of the soliton type, i.e., equations without non-trivial characteristic integrals. One of the main tools for classifying integrable equations is the symmetry approach. Note that the first classification result within this approach was obtained in
[
18
]
. The current state of the problem of classifying integrable partial differential equations, as well as references to publications on this topic, can be found in the book
[
19
]
. As noted in
[
19
]
, at present the problem of a complete description of integrable equations of the form (
1.11
) remains an open problem.

Liouville-type equations constitute an important subclass of integrable equations of the form (
1.11
). They are characterized by the fact that the sequence of Laplace invariants
h_{[i]}
ends with zero in both directions (
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
). This condition was used in
[
7
]
as a classification criterion for Liouville-type equations.

Note that for soliton equations of the form (
1.11
), i.e. for equations of the sine-Gordon type, all Laplace invariants are nonzero, and the Laplace sequence does not terminate on either side. Our conjecture is that this subclass of integrable equations is completely characterized by the fact that the Laplace sequence for them admits a finite reduction of the form (
1.29
). The conjecture is confirmed by several examples from work
[
9
]
and examples (
1.34
), (
1.35
) above. We assume that the condition for the presence of such a reduction of the Laplace sequence can be chosen as a classification criterion in the problem of enumerating integrable soliton equations of the form (
1.11
).

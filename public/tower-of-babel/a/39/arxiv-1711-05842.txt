Introduction

Given a prime power
q=p^{k}
, let
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field of
q
elements and
\widehat{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
be the group of multiplicative characters of
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. For a character
\chi
in
\widehat{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
, we extend
\chi
to a function on
\mathbb{F}
by setting
\chi(0)=0
. Following Greene
[
16
]
, we define the hypergeometric function over the finite field
\mathbb{F}
by

\displaystyle{}_{n+1}F_{n}\left(\begin{matrix}A_{0}&A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n}\\
&B_{1}&\ldots&B_{n}\end{matrix};x\right):=\frac{q}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}}}\binom{A_{0}\chi}{\chi}\binom{A_{1}\chi}{B_{1}\chi}\cdots\binom{A_{n}\chi}{B_{n}\chi}\chi(x),

where
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
and
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
are multiplicative characters on
\mathbb{F}
, and

\displaystyle\binom{A}{B}:=\frac{B(-1)}{q}J(A,\overline{B}).

Here
J(A,B)
is the Jacobi sum defined by

\displaystyle J(A,B)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}A(x)B(1-x).

Equivalently, the hypergeometric functions can be defined inductively by

\displaystyle{}_{n+1}F_{n}\left(\begin{matrix}A_{0}&A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n}\\
&B_{1}&\ldots&B_{n}\end{matrix};x\right):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}}A_{n}(y)B_{n}\overline{A}_{n}(1-y){}_{n}F_{n-1}\left(\begin{matrix}A_{0}&A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n-1}\\
&B_{1}&\ldots&B_{n-1}\end{matrix};xy\right)

with

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}A_{0}&A_{1}\\
&B_{1}\end{matrix};x\right):=\varepsilon(x)\frac{A_{1}B_{1}(-1)}{q}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}}A_{1}(y)B_{1}\overline{A}_{1}(1-y)\overline{A_{0}}(1-xy),

where
\varepsilon
is the trivial character on
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. Being finite fields analogue of the classical hypergeometric functions, the hypergeometric functions over finite fields have close connection to arithmetic geometry and can be used to compute zeta functions of algebraic varieties of hypergeometric type. The cases where the parameters
A_{i}
are all the quadratic character
\phi
and
B_{j}
are all the trivial character
\varepsilon
have particularly drawn a great deal of attention.

Assume that
q=p
is an odd prime. For convenience, we denote by
{}_{n+1}F_{n}(x)
the hypergeometric functions with
A_{i}=\phi
and
B_{j}=\varepsilon
for all
i
and
j
. The evaluations of
{}_{2}F_{1}(x)
,
{}_{3}F_{2}(x)
at certain rational numbers have been studied by numerous mathematicians such as Barman–Kalita
[
4
,
5
]
, Evans–Lam
[
9
]
, Greene–Stanton
[
17
]
, Koike
[
20
,
21
]
, and Ono
[
25
]
. They gave explicit relationship between values of these hypergeometric functions and arithmetic of
elliptic curves. In some cases, the evaluations also have connection with congruence properties of the Apéry numbers. In
[
1
,
2
]
, Ahlgren–Ono expressed the special value
{}_{4}F_{3}(1)
in terms of Fourier coefficients of the Hecke eigenform associated to a certain modular Calabi–Yau manifold. In
[
3
]
, Ahlgren, Ono, and Penniston studied the zeta functions of a certain family of
K3
-surfaces, which lead to information about the special values of
{}_{3}F_{2}(x)
at
x=1,8,1/8,-4,-1/4,64
, and
-1/64
in terms of the trace of Frobenius on suitable elliptic curves with complex multiplication over
\mathbb{Q}

[
14
]
. In other words, these values of the hypergeometric functions can also be expressed in terms of Hecke characters on imaginary quadratic number fields.
In
[
7
,
8
]
, Evans and Greene obtained evaluations of
{}_{3}F_{2}
-hypergeometric functions with more general characters at
x=1/4,-1/8,-1
, etc. Key ingredients in their approach are some transformation formulas of hypergeometric functions over finite fields, such as a finite field analogue
of Clausen’s theorem. In recent years, Frechette, Fuselier, Goodson, Lennon, Ono, Papanikolas, and Salerno
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
investigated
connections between hypergeometric functions over finite fields and elliptic curves, algebraic varieties, and Hecke eigenforms. Very recently, McCarthy–Papanikolas
[
24
]
linked the hypergeometric functions to Siegel modular forms.

The focus of this paper will be the evaluations of the hypergeometric function

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\psi&\psi\\
&\phi\end{matrix};x\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{F}_{p},\qquad x\neq 0,1,

(1.1)

for a character
\psi
of
\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{p}}
. We will see that if
\psi
has order
N
, then the values of this hypergeometric function are related to the number of rational points on a certain superelliptic curve of degree
N
or
2N
, depending on whether
N
is even or odd, over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. In the case of
N=3,4,6,8,12
, we can explicitly construct morphisms from the superelliptic curve to several elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication. Since the
L
-functions of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication are known to be equal to the
L
-functions of Hecke characters of the CM field, the values of the hypergeometric function can be expressed in terms of the Hecke characters.

2
Main statements

To state our main results, we first recall that if
N
is a positive integer and
p
is a prime such that
p\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ N
, then
p
splits completely in
\mathbb{Q}\big(e^{2\pi i/N}\big)
. In other words, if
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
\mathbb{Q}\big(e^{2\pi i/N}\big)
lying above
p
, then
\mathbb{Z}\big[e^{2\pi i/N}\big]/\mathfrak{p}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The integers
0,1,\ldots,p-1
form a complete set of coset representatives of
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathbb{Z}\big[e^{2\pi i/N}\big]
. Thus, for a given element
a
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we may canonically regard it as an element of
\mathbb{Z}\big[e^{2\pi i/N}\big]/\mathfrak{p}
. We now describe our main results.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime congruent to
1
modulo
4
, and
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
\mathbb{Z}[i]
lying above
p
. Let
\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the quartic multiplicative character of
(\mathbb{Z}[i]/\mathfrak{p})^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
satisfying

\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\equiv x^{(p-1)/4}

\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}
, for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
. Then, for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
a\neq 0,1
, if one of
a
and
1-a
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\overline{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}&{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}\\
&\phi\end{matrix};a\right)=0,

and if
a=b^{2}
for some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, then

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\overline{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}&{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}\\
&\phi\end{matrix};a\right)=-\frac{2}{p}\phi\left(1+b\right)\chi({\mathfrak{p}}),

where
\chi
is the Hecke character associated to the elliptic curve
E\colon y^{2}=x^{3}-x
satisfying
\chi(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathfrak{p}
for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Z}[i]
.

Remark 2.2
.

The Hecke character
\chi
in the theorem has the following description. Suppose that the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is generated by
a+bi
, for some integers
a
and
b
. There exists a unique fourth root
i^{k}
of unity such that
a+bi\equiv i^{k}\ \mathrm{mod}\ (1+i)^{3}
. Then

\displaystyle\chi(\mathfrak{p})=i^{k}(a+bi).

Alternatively, we can write
\chi
as

\displaystyle\chi(\mathfrak{p})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle(-1)^{b/2}\left(\frac{-1}{a}\right)(a+bi),&\text{if}\ a\ \text{is odd and}\ b\ \text{is even},\\
\displaystyle i(-1)^{a/2}\left(\frac{-1}{b}\right)(a+bi),&\text{if}\ a\ \text{is even and}\ b\ \text{is odd}.\end{cases}

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
p
be a prime congruent to
1
modulo
8
,
\mathfrak{P}
a prime of
\mathbb{Z}\big[\sqrt{-2}\big]
lying above
p
, and
\mathfrak{p}
a prime of
\mathbb{Z}\big[e^{2\pi i/8}\big]
lying above
\mathfrak{P}
. Let
\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the character of
\big(\mathbb{Z}\big[e^{2\pi i/8}\big]/\mathfrak{p}\big)^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
of order
8
such that
x^{(p-1)/8}\equiv\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{p}
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{8}]
. Let
\chi
be the Hecke character associated to the elliptic curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+4x^{2}+2x
with CM by
\mathbb{Z}\big[\sqrt{-2}\big]
satisfying
\chi(\mathfrak{P}^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{P}^{\prime}
for all primes
\mathfrak{P}^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{Z}\big[\sqrt{-2}\big]
. If
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, then

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}&\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}\\
&\phi\end{matrix};a\right)=0,

and if

for some

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}&\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}\\

Remark 2.4
.

The character
\chi
in the above theorem can be explicitly described as follows. If a prime
\mathfrak{P}^{\prime}
of

-1,&\text{if}\ d\ \text{is odd}.\end{cases}

3
Preliminary lemmas

For any characters
A,B,C\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}
and any element
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the
{}_{2}F_{1}
-hypergeometric function over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}A&B\\
&C\end{matrix};z\right)=\varepsilon(z)\frac{BC(-1)}{q}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}B(x)C\overline{B}(1-x)\overline{A}(1-zx)\\
&\hphantom{{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}A&B\\
&C\end{matrix};z\right)}{}=\varepsilon(z)\frac{BC(-1)}{q}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}A\overline{C}(x)C\overline{B}(x-1)\overline{A}(x-z).\end{split}

Thus, if
\eta\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}
and
z\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right)=\frac{\phi\eta(-1)}{q}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\eta(x)\phi\overline{\eta}(x-1)\phi\overline{\eta}(x-z),

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right)=\frac{q}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}}\binom{\phi\eta\chi}{\chi}\binom{\eta\chi}{\phi\chi}\chi(z)\overset{\chi\mapsto\phi\chi}{=}\frac{q}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}}\binom{\eta\chi}{\phi\chi}\binom{\phi\eta\chi}{\chi}\phi\chi(z)

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}_2F_1\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right)}{}=\phi(z){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right).

(3.2)

The value
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right)
hence equals to zero if
\phi(z)=-1
.

In order to evaluate
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right)
when
\phi(z)=1
, we need to consider the character sum

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(z)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\eta(x)\phi\overline{\eta}(x-1)\phi\overline{\eta}(x-z),

on the right-hand side of (
3.1
) so that

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right)=\frac{\phi\eta(-1)}{q}F_{\eta}(z).

(3.3)

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(1)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\eta(x)\phi\overline{\eta}(x-1)\phi\overline{\eta}(x-1)=J\big(\eta,\overline{\eta}^{2}\big),

and if
\eta^{2}=\varepsilon
then

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(z)=-1-\phi(z),\qquad z\neq 0,1.

By a simple change of variables, one can derive a relation between the character sums
F_{\eta}(z)
and
F_{\phi\overline{\eta}}(z)
as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of
q
elements and
\eta
a character of order
N
with
N\,|\,(q-1)
. For any
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
z\neq 1
, we have

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(z)=\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}F_{\phi\overline{\eta}}(z).

Proof.

For
z\neq 1
, replacing
x
by
(x-1)/(x-z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\eta(x)\phi\overline{\eta}\left((x-1)(x-z)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\eta\left(\frac{x-1}{x-z}\right)\phi\overline{\eta}\left(\left(\frac{x-1}{x-z}-1\right)\left(\frac{x-1}{x-z}-z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\eta(-1)\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\eta\left((x-z)(x-1)\right)\phi\overline{\eta}(x-(1+z))

\displaystyle=\phi\eta(-1)\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\eta\left((x+1)(x+z)\right)\phi\overline{\eta}(x)

\displaystyle=\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}}\eta\left((1-x)(z-x)\right)\phi\overline{\eta}(x).

This gives the identity
F_{\eta}(z)=\overline{\eta}^{2}(1-z)F_{\phi\overline{\eta}}(z)
.
∎

Assume that
\eta
is a character of order
N>2
and
z\neq 1
. When
N
is even,
we have
\phi=\eta^{N/2}
and hence

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(z)

\displaystyle=\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}F_{\phi\overline{\eta}}(z)=\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\phi\overline{\eta}(x)\eta(x-1)\eta(x-z)

\displaystyle=\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\eta\big(x^{N/2-1}(x-1)(x-z)\big).

When
N
is odd,
\phi\overline{\eta}
is a character of order
2N
and
\eta=(\phi\overline{\eta})^{N-1}
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(z)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\phi\overline{\eta}\big(x^{N-1}(x-1)(x-z)\big).

Let
\eta
be a character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
of order
N
. Set

\displaystyle N^{\prime}=\begin{cases}N,&\text{if}\ N\ \text{is even},\\
2N,&\text{if}\ N\ \text{is odd},\end{cases}\qquad\eta^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\eta,&\text{if}\ N\ \text{is even},\\
\phi\overline{\eta},&\text{if}\ N\ \text{is odd},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle S_{\eta^{\prime}}(z)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\eta^{\prime}(x^{N^{\prime}/2-1}(x-1)(x-z)).

(3.4)

We summarize the short discussion above in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\eta
be a character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
of order
N>2
, and
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
z\neq 0,1
. Then we have

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\phi\eta&\eta\\
&\phi\end{matrix};z\right)=\frac{\phi\eta(-1)}{q}\begin{cases}\overline{\eta}(1-z)^{2}S_{\eta^{\prime}}(z),&\text{if}\ N\ \text{is even},\\
S_{\eta^{\prime}}(z),&\text{if}\ N\ \text{is odd}.\end{cases}

From the lemma, we see that the evaluation of our hypergeometric functions reduces to that of
S_{\eta}(z)
for a character of even order
N
. It is natural to consider the following families of curves

\displaystyle C_{N,a,c}\colon\ \begin{cases}y^{N}=cx^{N/2-1}(x-1)(x-a),&\mbox{if $N$ is even},\\
y^{2N}=cx(x-1)^{N-1}(x-a)^{N-1},&\mbox{if $N$ is odd}\end{cases}

(3.5)

over
\mathbb{Q}
. We will see that our sums
S_{\eta}(a)
appear in the
L
-functions of
C_{N,a,c}
. By varying
c
, we are able to determine
S_{\eta}(a)
.

The decomposition of the Jacobian variety of
C_{N,a,c}
will give us information about
S_{\eta}(a)
with
\eta
of order
N
. For this purpose, we first consider the quotient curves of

of these four curves at

(4.1)

(4.2)

&\phi\end{matrix};a\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(-1)}{p}\overline{\psi}_{\mathfrak{p}}(1-a)^{2}S_{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}(a)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(-1)^{(p-1)/12}}{p}\overline{\psi}_{\mathfrak{p}}(1-a)^{2}\big(\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(1+b)^{2}+\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}(1-b)^{2}\big)\chi(\mathfrak{P})

\displaystyle=-\frac{(-1)^{(p-1)/12}}{p}\big(\overline{\psi}_{\mathfrak{p}}(1+b)^{2}+\overline{\psi}_{\mathfrak{p}}(1-b)^{2}\big)\chi(\mathfrak{P}).

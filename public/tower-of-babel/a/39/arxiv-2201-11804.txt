Gravitational radiation from a binary system in odd-dimensional spacetime
M. Khlopunov, D.V. Gal’tsov

Abstract
We explore possible manifestations of an odd number of extra dimensions in gravitational radiation, which are associated with violation of Huygens’ principle in flat odd-dimensional spacetime. Our setup can be regarded as the limit of an infinite compactification radius in ADD model and is not viable as realistic cosmology, but it still may be useful as a simple analytically solvable model catching certain features of more realistic scenarios. The model consists of two point masses moving inside a flat three-dimensional brane, embedded in a five-dimensional Minkowski space and interacting only through a massless scalar field localized on the same brane, while gravitational radiation is emitted into the bulk. This setup avoids the difficulties associated with taking into account the gravitational stresses binding the system, which require the cubic terms in the perturbative gravitational Lagrangian, and permits to limit ourselves to linearized theory. We calculate radiation in a linearized five-dimensional gravity generalizing the Rohrlich-Teitelboim approach to extract the emitted part of the retarded gravitational field. The source term consists of a local contribution from point particles and a non-local contribution from scalar field stresses, which is calculated using the DIRE approach to post-Newtonian expansions. In the nonrelativistic limit, we find an analog of the quadrupole formula containing an integral over the history of the particles’ motion preceding the retarded time. We also show that, for an observer on the brane, the radiation contains a third polarization: the breathing mode.

It implies that, while in even dimensions the signal from the instant flash of the source, having reached the observer in the interval of time required for it to propagate with the speed of light, ends instantly, in odd dimensions the endless tail signal decaying with time is observed after that. On the other hand, free massless fields propagate exactly with the speed of light in all dimensions. Therefore, while the total retarded gravitational field of localized source propagates with all velocities up to that of light, its gravitational radiation, being the free field far from it, should propagate exactly with the speed of light. As a result, this mismatch makes the extraction of the emitted part of retarded gravitational field in odd dimensions a non-trivial task. ;

;

;

However, being based on the calculations in the momentum space irrelevant to the dimensionality of the spacetime, this method does not provide us with any information about the structure of the retarded field in the wave zone and the role of the tail term in the formation of radiation. As was shown recently, this obstacle can be overcome in two ways: by Fourier transforming the retarded Green’s functions over the temporal coordinate

;
Galtsov:2021zpb

To implement these ideas for the two-body system one needs a simplified model which would be tractable within the linearized gravity in Minkowski space. However, gravitational radiation in pure gravity requires second order expansion of the Ricci tensor to describe gravitational stresses binding the system. To avoid this, we propose the combination of the scalar and gravitational interactions to have the binding forces of nongravitational nature. Then gravitational radiation can be computed within the linearized gravity in Minkowski space. Our model also assumes that motion is restricted to the four-dimensional subspace (brane), while gravity operates in the odd-dimensional bulk (for simplicity we assume five dimensions). In contrary, the scalar field is assumed to live on the brane too. Then, starting with initial particles’ velocities lying on the brane, we will ensure motion remains on the brane perpetually without imposing any confining mechanism. Of course, this model cannot serve a realistic model of spacetime such as ADD or RS models, and it has no purely four-dimensional interpretation. In fact, our model can be regarded as the limit of the ADD model with an infinite compactification length. This limiting theory clearly is not physically viable, but here we sacrifice viability in favor of mathematical consistency and simplicity. Our model still captures basic features of violation of Huygens’s principle in odd-dimensions and demonstrate the emergence of tail and an extra polarisation in gravitational radiation for an observer living on the brane.

For consistency, we, also, explicitly take into account the contribution from the stresses of the field, through which particles forming binary system interact, into the gravitational radiation of the system. The stresses contribution is dealt with by use of the DIRE approach to the post-Newtonian expansions in the four-dimensional GR

In contrast, in four-dimensional theory power of the gravitational radiation at some moment of time is completely determined by the state of the source at the corresponding retarded time. The obtained result is in agreement with the one found by use of the Fourier transformations of the retarded Green’s functions

II
The Setup

As is known, the gravitational radiation of point particles interacting gravitationally differs from the electromagnetic radiation of point charges in the nonlocality of the source in the respective d’Alembert equation, which must take into account the contribution of gravitational stresses. For this reason, we cannot restrict ourselves to the linearized approximation of general relativity, but must include, as part of the radiation source, the quadratic part of the Ricci tensor as representing these stresses
Weinberg:1972kfs
. To simplify this setup, we introduce a scalar field that provides the forces needed to create acceleration, assuming that this force is much greater than the force of their gravitational interaction, and the latter can be neglected. In our model, it is possible to consistently describe gravitational radiation using purely linear gravity, but still taking into account the nonlocality of the source in the d’Alembert equation for the gravitational field
h_{MN}
due to the contribution of interaction field stresses. The consistency of such a model will be ensured if the interacting particles do not leave the four-dimensional subspace (brane); this is guaranteed because the scalar field is restricted to live on the brane. We do not specify the physical mechanism of such localization and do not introduce brane as a physical object. Our goal is to reveal the features of gravitational radiation in odd-dimensional space-time due to violation of the Huygens principle, which induces the effect of nonlocality of a different nature.

II.1
Brane and bulk

The model assumes that the system of point particles

m_{a}
with initial velocities, bounded by the four-dimensional subspace of the five-dimensional spacetime, moves under mutual scalar forces, forever remaining in this subspace, which we loosely call the three-brane. We choose the five-dimensional coordinates
x^{M}=(x^{\mu},x^{4})
with
M=\overline{0,4}
and
\mu=\overline{0,3}
, so that the brane is orthogonal to
x^{4}
and is parametrized by the same bulk coordinates.

We start with the generally covariant action

S=S_{g}+S_{\varphi}+S_{m},

(II.1)

which is the sum of the five-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert term

S_{g}=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{5}}\int d^{5}x\sqrt{-g}\,R,

(II.2)

where
\kappa_{5}
is the five-dimensional gravitational constant, the four-dimensional scalar field term

S_{\varphi}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int d^{4}\sigma\sqrt{-\gamma}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi(\sigma)\partial_{\nu}\varphi(\sigma),

(II.3)

where the induced metric
\gamma_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}
coincides with the four-dimensional part of the bulk metric
g_{MN}
, chosen in the gauge
g_{4\mu}=0
, and the particle term describes their motion along affinely parameterized world lines

(omitting an index
a
in
\tau_{a}

S_{p}=-\sum_{a}\int\left(m_{a}+g_{a}\varphi(z_{a})\right)\left(-\dot{z}_{a}^{2}\right)^{1/2}d\tau,\qquad\dot{z}_{a}^{2}=\dot{z}_{a}^{\mu}\dot{z}_{a}^{\nu}\gamma_{\mu\nu},\qquad\dot{z}_{a}^{\mu}=dz_{a}^{\mu}/d\tau.

(II.4)

The scalar charges
g_{a}
with this definition are dimensionless.

The corresponding Einstein equations will read

R_{MN}-\frac{1}{2}g_{MN}R=2\kappa_{5}T_{MN},\qquad T_{MN}=\delta^{\mu}_{M}\delta^{\nu}_{N}\,T_{\mu\nu}\,\delta(x^{4}),

(II.5)

where

is the (restricted to the brane) matter energy-momentum tensor consisting of the particles and scalar field terms
T_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm P}+T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm F}

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm P}=\sum_{a}\int d\tau\left(m_{a}+g_{a}\varphi(z_{a})\right)\dot{z}_{a\mu}\dot{z}_{a\nu}\frac{\delta^{(4)}(x-z_{a})}{\sqrt{-\gamma}},

(II.6)

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm F}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mu\nu}(\partial\varphi)^{2}\right).

(II.7)

In accordance with the localisation of the scalar field on the three-brane, it contains the distributional singularity provided by the five-dimensional delta function under the four-dimensional integral over the brane.

We also find the equations of motion for the matter localised on the three-brane. So, scalar field equation of motion has the form

{{}^{(\gamma)}}\square\varphi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\gamma}}\partial_{\mu}\left({\sqrt{-\gamma}}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\right)=4\pi\sum_{a}g_{a}\int d\tau\frac{\delta^{(4)}(x-z_{a})}{\sqrt{-\gamma}},

(II.8)

Equations of motion for the point particles can be presented in the form

m_{a}(\tau)\frac{Dz^{\mu}_{a}}{d\tau}=g_{a}\left(\gamma^{\mu\nu}+z_{a}^{\mu}z_{a}^{\nu}\right)\partial_{\nu}\varphi,

(II.9)

where the variable mass is introduced

(II.10)

In fact, the Einstein equations (

g_{MN}=\eta_{MN}+h_{MN},\quad\left|h_{MN}\right|\ll 1.

(II.11)

with the Minkowski metric

Introducing the reduced field

(II.12)

and imposing the Lorentz gauge condition

we find the linearised Einstein’s equation as

(II.14)

where

is the d’Alembert operator on the five-dimensional Minkowski space. Note, for the Lorentz gauge condition (

(II.15)

II.2
Gravitational radiation: polarizations

We want to calculate the 5D gravitational radiation of our system. To do this, we introduce another division of coordinates:
x^{M}=(x^{0}=t,x^{i}),\,i=\overline{1,4}
.

The energy-momentum flux carried by the 5D transverse-traceless wave polarizations is given by an effective energy-momentum tensor analogous to that in
maggiore2008

t_{MN}=\frac{1}{4\kappa_{5}}\left\langle\partial_{M}h_{ij}^{\rm TT}\partial_{N}h_{ij}^{\rm TT}\right\rangle,

(II.17)

where we assume that the motion of the source is periodic and the brackets
\langle\ldots\rangle
denote the averaging over the period. In the transverse-traceless gauge we use here, the following conditions hold:

h_{0M}^{\rm TT}=0,\quad h_{ii}^{\rm TT}=0,\quad\partial^{j}h_{ij}^{\rm TT}=0,

(II.18)

which ensure that we deal with five polarisations. These can be obtained acting on the unconstrained potentials by the projector
maggiore2008

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{\rm TT}(x)=\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\mathbf{n})h_{kl}(x),\quad\mathbf{n}=\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}

(II.19)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\mathbf{n})\equiv P_{ik}P_{jl}-\frac{1}{3}P_{ij}P_{kl},\quad P_{ij}(\mathbf{n})\equiv\delta_{ij}-n_{i}n_{j},

(II.20)

which has the vanishing traces

\Lambda_{ii,kl}(\mathbf{n})=\Lambda_{ij,kk}(\mathbf{n})=0.

(II.21)

Therefore, the transverse-traseless potentials can be equivalently obtained from the trace-reversed metric deviations

h_{ij}^{\rm TT}(x)=\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\mathbf{n})\bar{h}_{kl}(x).

(II.22)

From five polarizations of the bulk modes, three are present in the brane subspace. Choosing the propagation vector
\mathbf{n}
to be aligned with
x^{3}
-direction, we will have

\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm TT}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}h_{+}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}h_{\displaystyle\circ}&h_{\times}&0&h_{14}\\
h_{\times}&-h_{+}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}h_{\displaystyle\circ}&0&h_{24}\\
0&0&0&0\\
h_{14}&h_{24}&0&h_{\displaystyle\circ}\end{pmatrix},

(II.23)

where the ”cross” and ”plus” polarizations are standard

h_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(h_{11}-h_{22}\right),\quad h_{\times}=h_{12},

(II.24)

and the
h_{\displaystyle\circ}
is the ”breathing” polarization

given as

h_{\displaystyle\circ}=\frac{2}{3}h_{44}-\frac{1}{3}\left(h_{11}+h_{22}\right).

(II.25)

So the brane-living observer will see only three polarizations – cross, plus, and the breathing mode
Andriot2017
. From the linearized geodesic deviation equation
maggiore2008
;
Andriot2017

II.3
Dynamics of the non-relativistic binary

In the linear approximation (
II.14
), the particles and the scalar field propagate on the flat Minkowski background
\gamma_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}

II.8
) and (
II.9

\displaystyle{{}^{(4)}}\square\varphi(x)=4\pi\sum_{a}g_{a}\int d\tau\,\delta^{(4)}\left(x-z_{a}\right),

(II.26)

\displaystyle\left(m_{a}+g_{a}\varphi(z_{a})\right)\ddot{z}_{a}^{\mu}=-g_{a}\partial^{\mu}\varphi(z_{a})-g_{a}\partial_{\nu}\varphi(z_{a})\dot{z}_{a}^{\nu}\dot{z}_{a}^{\mu},

(II.28)

in the Eq. (

is the scalar field produced by

-th particle is a Coulomb field

As a result, we obtain the non-relativistic particles equation of motion simply as

(II.31)

where

\displaystyle G_{\rm ret}^{4+1}(x)=\frac{\theta(t)}{2\pi^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta(-x^{2})}{(-x^{2})^{3/2}}-\frac{\delta(-x^{2})}{(-x^{2})^{1/2}}\right].

Note that the Green’s function (

Also, while each term in Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\left(y^{n}\theta(y)\right)=ny^{n-1}\theta(y)+y^{n}\delta(y).

It is clear that if

We consider the point particles and scalar field contributions into the gravitational waves separately, decomposing the retarded gravitational field (

ADD-model with the infinite compactification radius

Let us see how our model can be interpreted as the limiting case of the ADD-model with the infinite compactification radius of an extra dimension. We start with the five dimensional ADD-model with the compactification radius

II.6
Rohrlich-Teitelboim definition of radiation

In four dimensions, the retarded electromagnetic field of the point charge consists of two terms: one proportional to
1/r^{2}
and being the deformed Coulomb-like part of the field, and another, which depends on the acceleration of the charge and scales as
1/r
. The latter gives a non-zero flux of the field energy-momentum through the distant sphere, and therefore represents radiation. To argue that this is radiation indeed, Rohrlich
Rohrlich1961
;
rohrlich2007
and Teitelboim
Teitelboim1970
(see also
Kosyakov1992
;
Galtsov:2004uqu
;
Kosyakov:2007qc
;
Galtsov:2010tny
;
Kosyakov:2018wek
), computed the most long-range part of the on-shell energy-momentum tensor, demonstrating that it exhibits special properties, meaning that the corresponding part of the field energy-momentum propagates at the speed of light.

The Rohrlich-Teitelboim approach is based on the use of certain covariantly defined quantities, so we briefly recall their definitions. Let us consider a point particle moving along a worldline
z^{M}(\tau)
with the
D
-velocity
v^{M}=dz^{M}/d\tau
. The observation point coordinates are denoted as
x^{M}
. Consider the observation point as a top of the light cone in the past, and denote the intersection point of this light cone with the particle’s worldline as
\hat{z}^{M}={z}^{M}(\hat{\tau})
. The quantity
\hat{\tau}
is called the retarded proper time and is defined by the equation

\left(x^{M}-z^{M}(\hat{\tau})\right)^{2}=0,\quad x^{0}\geq z^{0}(\hat{\tau}).

(II.49)

If the signal propagating with the speed of light were emitted at the retarded moment, it would reach the observation point at the corresponding moment of time
x^{0}
. Further, all the hatted quantities correspond to the retarded proper time
\hat{\tau}
. We introduce three spacetime vectors: a lightlike vector
\hat{X}^{M}=x^{M}-\hat{z}^{M}
directed from
\hat{z}^{M}
to the observation point; a spacelike unit vector
\hat{u}^{M}
, orthogonal to the particle’s velocity
\hat{v}^{M}
; and a lightlike vector
\hat{c}^{M}=\hat{v}^{M}+\hat{u}^{M}
being the sum of the formers. These vectors have the following properties:

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{2}=-\hat{u}^{2}=-1;\quad\hat{c}^{2}=0;\quad(\hat{c}\hat{v})=-(\hat{c}\hat{u})=-1;\quad(\hat{v}\hat{u})=0,

(II.50)

\displaystyle\hat{X}^{M}=\hat{\rho}\hat{c}^{M};\quad\hat{\rho}=-(\hat{v}\hat{X}),\quad\hat{X}^{2}=0,

(II.51)

where
(AB)=A^{M}B_{M}
. Note that
\hat{\rho}
is a Lorentz-invariant distance equal to the spatial distance in the Lorentz frame comoving with the particle at the retarded moment
\hat{\tau}
. Note also that for a point charge, moving along the worldline
z^{M}(\tau)
for an infinite proper time
-\infty<\tau<\infty
, certain care is needed to correctly define the asymptotic conditions for acceleration, for details see
Teitelboim1970
. Here we will not discuss this subtlety, considering the simple case of periodic motion. Far from the region of the particle’s motion
\hat{\rho}\sim r
, so the Lorentz-invariant definition of the distance is equivalent to the naive definition. Nevertheless, in order to obtain Lorentz-covariant expansions of tensors, one has to use
1/\hat{\rho}
as an expansion parameter.

Based on the definitions above, in four-dimensional electrodynamics Teitelboim
Teitelboim1970
obtained the following decomposition of the the on-shell energy-momentum tensor:

T^{\mu\nu}=T_{\rm Coul}^{\mu\nu}+T_{\rm mix}^{\mu\nu}+T_{\rm rad}^{\mu\nu},

(II.52)

where the Coulomb part falls down as
\hat{\rho}^{-4}
, the mixed part – as
\hat{\rho}^{-3}
, and the last part – as
\hat{\rho}^{-2}
. Teitelboim found that the most long-range term of the on-shell energy-momentum tensor has the following properties:

•

It is separately conserved
\partial_{\nu}T_{\rm rad}^{\mu\nu}=0
.

•

It is proportional to the direct product of two null vectors
\hat{c}^{\mu}\hat{c}^{\nu}
and, therefore,
\hat{c}_{\mu}T_{\rm rad}^{\mu\nu}=0
.

•

It scales as
1/\hat{\rho}^{2}
and gives positive definite energy-momentum flux through the distant sphere.

It is clear that the corresponding part of the field’s energy-momentum propagates with the speed of light. Therefore, the radiation power can be computed as the flux of the energy, associated with
T_{\rm rad}^{\mu\nu}
. Note that, due to the electromagnetic field’s energy-momentum tensor being the bilinear functional of the field derivatives
T_{\mu\nu}\sim\partial A\,\partial A
, one can define the emitted part of the latter
\left(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}\right)^{\rm rad}
, which contributes into the radiated part of the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\rm rad}^{\mu\nu}
.

Similar decomposition and reasoning holds in any spacetime dimensions
Kosyakov1999
;
Kosyakov:2008wa
;
Spirin2009
and, also, for the scalar
Spirin2009
;
Galtsov:2020hhn
and gravitational fields. The only difference is that in
D
dimensions area of the distant sphere is proportional to
r^{D-2}
, thus, the relevant asymptotic behaviour of the field derivative in the wave zone is
1/r^{D/2-1}
.

Passing to our theory of the five-dimensional gravitational radiation, we calculate the long-range part (with respect to the Lorentz-invariant distance
\hat{\rho}
) of derivative of the retarded gravitational field (
II.34
) and substitute it into the bilinear functional (
II.17
). All the listed properties of the emitted part of the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\rm rad}^{MN}
hold for the obtained tensor. Note that the transformation into the transverse-traceless gauge can be performed after the extraction of the emitted part of the field derivative, because the derivative of the
\Lambda
-tensor is proportional to the inverse distance
\partial_{m}\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\mathbf{n})\sim 1/r
and, therefore, does not contribute into the long-range part of the field derivative

\left(\partial_{M}h_{ij}^{\rm TT}\right)^{\rm rad}=\Lambda_{ij,kl}(\mathbf{n})\left(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{kl}\right)^{\rm rad}.

(II.53)

However, in contrast with the four-dimensional theory, in five dimensions emitted part of the field derivative depends on the entire history of particle’s motion preceding the retarded moment of proper time
\hat{\tau}
, while in four dimensions it depends only on the particle’s kinematic characteristics at this moment. Nevertheless, due to the above properties of the on-shell energy-momentum tensor, the energy-momentum associated with the long-range component of the field derivative propagate at the speed of light.

The flux of the radiated energy-momentum passing per unit time through the
(2\nu-1)
-dimensional sphere of radius
r
is given by the integral

W_{2\nu+1}^{\mu}=\int\,T_{\rm rad}^{\mu i}\;n^{i}\,r^{2\nu-1}\,d\Omega_{2\nu-1},\quad i=\overline{1,2\nu},

(II.54)

where
d\Omega_{2\nu-1}
is an angular element, and
\bf{n}
is a unit spacelike vector in the direction of observation.

Using the Rohrlich-Teitelboim approach, the scalar synchrotron radiation from a circularly moving particle in
2+1
and
4+1
dimensions was calculated
Galtsov:2020hhn
. The correctness of calculations was verified by the computation of the total radiation power using Fourier spectral decomposition, which are indifferent to the dimensionality of spacetime and are free from the problems discussed above.

III
Point particles contribution

The point particles contribution into the gravitational waves produced by the binary system is written as

\bar{h}_{MN}^{\rm P}(x)=-2\kappa_{5}\int d^{5}x^{\prime}\,T_{MN}^{\rm P}(x^{\prime})\,G_{\rm ret}^{4+1}(x-x^{\prime}),

(III.1)

where the particles energy-momentum tensor is given by the Eq. (
II.32
) and the retarded Green’s function by the Eq. (
II.35
).

We are interested in the non-relativistic approximation of the emitted part of the gravitational field’s (
III.1
) derivative. To clarify the main steps of derivation, let us start with the problem of scalar radiation of non-relativistic point charge as a simple but instructive example of five-dimensional radiation of a non-relativistic source.

III.1
Scalar radiation of a non-relativistic charge

In this subsection, we deviate from our basic setup and consider the case of a scalar field living in the bulk. In this case, the bulk scalar radiation (in the lowest approximation) can be calculated within the framework of a linearized theory, since there is no need to take into account the stresses that bind the system. This calculation may be interesting in itself, but for us it also provides an easier way to explain the characteristics of radiation in an odd-dimensional flat space. In fact, this gives a simplified setup for gravitational radiation, neglecting the contribution of stresses.

By analogy with (
II.26
), equation of motion of a five-dimensional massless scalar field interacting with a point charge moving along an arbitrary worldline
z^{M}(\tau)
is written as

{{}^{(5)}}\square\varphi(x)=2\pi^{2}g\int d\tau\,\delta^{(5)}(x-z).

(III.2)

Retarded solution of Eq. (
III.2
) is constructed by the corresponding five-dimensional Green’s function (
II.35
) and is found as

\varphi(x)=g\int d\tau\,\theta(X^{0})\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{3/2}}-\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{1/2}}\right],

(III.3)

where we introduced the vector
X^{M}(\tau)=x^{M}-z^{M}(\tau)
and omitted its dependence on proper time for brevity. Computing its derivative we obtain

\partial_{M}\varphi(x)=g\int d\tau\,\theta(X^{0})\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{\theta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{5/2}}+2\frac{\delta^{\prime}(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{1/2}}-2\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{3/2}}\right]X_{M}.

(III.4)

We integrate by parts the term containing derivative of delta function by use of the relation

\frac{dX^{2}}{d\tau}=-2(vX),

(III.5)

arriving at

\partial_{M}\varphi(x)=g\int d\tau\,\theta(X^{0})\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{\theta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{5/2}}X_{M}-\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{3/2}}X_{M}+\right.\\
\left.+\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(vX)^{2}(-X^{2})^{1/2}}\left[(aX)+1\right]X_{M}-\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(vX)(-X^{2})^{1/2}}v_{M}\right],

(III.6)

where we introduced the acceleration vector
a^{M}=d^{2}z^{M}/d\tau^{2}
.

To extract the leading
\hat{\rho}
-asymptotic of the Eq. (
III.6
), we use relation for the delta function of complex argument transforming its product with the Heaviside function as

\theta(X^{0})\delta(-X^{2})=\frac{\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})}{2\hat{\rho}}.

(III.7)

Also, we rewrite the vector
X^{M}
in the following form

X^{M}=Z^{M}+\hat{\rho}\hat{c}^{M},\quad Z^{M}=\hat{z}^{M}-z^{M}.

(III.8)

Then, expanding Eq. (
III.6
) in the inverse powers of the Lorentz-invariant distance
\hat{\rho}
we find the emitted part of the scalar field derivative as

\left(\partial_{M}\varphi(x)\right)^{\rm rad}=\frac{g\hat{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\hat{\rho}^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{(-Z\hat{c})^{5/2}}-\frac{\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})}{(-Z\hat{c})^{3/2}}+\frac{2(a\hat{c})\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})}{(v\hat{c})^{2}(-Z\hat{c})^{1/2}}\right],

(III.9)

where we have taken into account that up to the leading order
X^{2}\sim 2\hat{\rho}(Z\hat{c})
and
(vX)\sim\hat{\rho}(v\hat{c})
.

Each integral in (
III.9
) diverges on the upper integration limit
\tau\to\hat{\tau}
, where
(Z\hat{c})\to(\hat{v}\hat{c})(\hat{\tau}-\tau)=-(\hat{\tau}-\tau)
. However, by use of the regularization procedure discussed in section II.D one can show that the last two terms in Eq. (
III.9
) do not contain physical information concerning the properties of the field in wave zone and just subtract the divergences from the first one. Indeed, we introduce the regularising parameter
\epsilon\to+0
into the argument of delta function, obtaining the divergent result

\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\,\frac{\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau}+\epsilon)}{(-Z\hat{c})^{3/2}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3/2}},

(III.10)

which is rewritten as

\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3/2}}=\frac{3}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}-\epsilon}\frac{d\tau}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{3/2}}.

(III.11)

The second term with delta function is transformed in an analogous manner providing us with the integral

\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\frac{(a\hat{c})\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})}{(v\hat{c})^{2}(-Z\hat{c})^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}-\epsilon}d\tau\frac{(\hat{a}\hat{c})}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{3/2}}.

(III.12)

Then, emitted part of the retarded scalar field derivative takes the form

\left(\partial_{M}\varphi(x)\right)^{\rm rad}=\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\frac{g\hat{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\hat{\rho}^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}-\epsilon}d\tau\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{(-Z\hat{c})^{5/2}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{5/2}}+\frac{(\hat{a}\hat{c})}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{3/2}}\right].

(III.13)

Further, we omit the regularising parameter in the upper integration limit for simplicity, taking in mind that to make the finiteness of the obtained expression explicit one needs to perform some transformation of the first term in the integrand: usually it is the integration by parts, which extract the divergences from the first term and subtract them with the last two integrals. The obtained structure of the emitted part of the field is the general feature of the odd-dimensional fields
Galtsov:2020hhn
– it consists of several separately divergent integrals, first of which contains all the information concerning the field in the wave zone, while the remaining ones are just the counter-terms eliminating the divergence from the first one. Note, also, that the emitted part of the retarded field depends on the entire history of the charge’s motion preceding the retarded proper time
\hat{\tau}
.

Now let us find the non-relativistic approximation of the emitted part of the scalar field derivative (
III.13
). We make the following assumptions about the charge’s motion:

•

it is non-relativistic
|\mathbf{v}|\ll 1,\,\forall\tau
;

•

it moves inside compact region of space
|\mathbf{z}|\leq d,\forall\tau
(
d
is the characteristic size of this region);

•

the observation point is far from this region
d\ll r
.

Then, the covariant retarded quantities are expanded up to the leading order in small parameters
|\mathbf{v}|
and
|\mathbf{z}|/r
as

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}\simeq\bar{t}+\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{z}},\quad\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{x}/r,

(III.14)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}\simeq r\left(1-\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}-\frac{1}{r}\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{z}}\right),

(III.15)

\displaystyle\hat{c}^{M}\simeq\left[1+\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}};\,\mathbf{n}\left(1+\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}+\frac{1}{r}\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{z}}\right)-\frac{1}{r}\bar{\mathbf{z}}\right],

(III.16)

where
\bar{t}=t-r
is the retarded time calculated up to the leading contribution, and all the barred quantities correspond to this moment. Using the Eqs. (
III.14
–
III.16
), we rewrite the first term of the integrand in Eq. (
III.13
) as

\frac{1}{(-Z\hat{c})^{5/2}}\simeq\frac{1}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}\left[1+\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{n}\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})\left(1+\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}+\frac{1}{r}\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{z}}\right)-\frac{1}{r}\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})\bar{\mathbf{z}}\right]^{-5/2},

(III.17)

where we introduced the spacelike vector
\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})
defined as

\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})=\frac{\bar{\mathbf{z}}-\mathbf{z}(t^{\prime})}{\bar{t}-t^{\prime}},

(III.18)

and replaced the proper time with the coordinate one
\tau=t^{\prime}
, given their equivalence in the non-relativistic limit. It is clear that at the retarded time
\bar{t}
vector
\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})
has a finite value
\displaystyle\lim_{t^{\prime}\to\bar{t}}\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})=\bar{\mathbf{v}}
. Rewriting the particle’s worldline in terms of its velocity

\mathbf{z}(t)=\int_{t_{\rm in}}^{t}\mathbf{v}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}+\mathbf{z}(t_{\rm in}),

(III.19)

where
t_{\rm in}
is the initial moment of time in the remote past and using the first mean value theorem, we find that the vector
\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})
is of order of particle’s velocity

|\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})|\sim|\mathbf{v}|,\,\forall t^{\prime}.

(III.20)

So, it should be used as another expansion parameter. Thus, we calculate the integrand in (
III.13
) up to the leading order in small parameters arriving at

(-Z\hat{c})^{-5/2}=\frac{1}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}\left(1+\frac{5}{2}\left(\mathbf{n}\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})-\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right),\quad\hat{c}_{M}=\bar{c}_{M}=[-1,\mathbf{n}],\quad\hat{a}\hat{c}=\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{a}}.

(III.21)

Substituting the obtained expansions into the Eq. (
III.13
), we find the non-relativistic approximation of the emitted part of scalar field derivative as

(\partial_{M}\varphi(x))^{\rm rad}=-\frac{g\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\left[\frac{15}{4}\frac{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}-\frac{15}{4}\frac{\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}+\frac{\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{a}}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{3/2}}\right].

(III.22)

To make the finitness of the emitted part of the scalar field explicit, we introduce the regularising parameter into the upper integration limit and integrate the first term in (
III.22
) by parts three times arriving at

\frac{15}{4}\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}-\epsilon}dt^{\prime}\frac{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}=\frac{5}{2}\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\frac{\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}}{\epsilon^{3/2}}-2\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\frac{\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{a}}}{\epsilon^{1/2}}+2\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\mathbf{n}\dot{\mathbf{a}}(t^{\prime})}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}},

(III.23)

where
\dot{\mathbf{a}}=d^{3}\mathbf{z}/dt^{\prime 3}
. It is clear that the first two divergent terms in Eq. (
III.23
) are cancelled out by the ”counter-terms” in (
III.22
) yielding the simple expression for the emitted part of the scalar field derivative in the non-relativistic limit

\left(\partial_{M}\varphi(x)\right)^{\rm rad}=-\frac{g\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{3/2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\mathbf{n}\dot{\mathbf{a}}(t^{\prime})}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}.

(III.24)

Note that in five-dimensions, up to the leading order in non-relativistic expansion, the rectilinear uniformly accelerated scalar charge does not radiate, in contrast with the four-dimensional theory. However, as will be shown later, this is not the case for the gravitational radiation.

Substituting the Eq. (
III.24
) into the energy-momentum tensor of the five-dimensional massless scalar field, analogous to that of Eq. (
II.33
),

T_{MN}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\partial_{M}\varphi\partial_{N}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{MN}\partial^{A}\varphi\partial_{A}\varphi\right),

(III.25)

we find the radiation energy-momentum tensor as

T_{MN}^{\rm rad}(x)=\frac{g^{2}\bar{c}_{M}\bar{c}_{N}}{16\pi^{2}r^{3}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\mathbf{n}\dot{\mathbf{a}}(t^{\prime})}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}\right]^{2},

(III.26)

which has the tensor structure corresponding to the propagation of the given part of energy-momentum exactly with the speed of light. One can show that in the case of charge’s uniform circular motion Eq. (
III.26
) yields the finite value of radiation power coinciding with the one obtained from the spectral decompositions of the radiation power indifferent to the dimensionality of spacetime found in
Galtsov:2020hhn
. Note that in the non-relativistic limit the radiation power (
III.26
) depends on the entire history of charge’s motion preceding to the retarded time, while in the ultra-relativistic limit studied in
Galtsov:2020hhn
this dependence effectively reduces to the small interval of proper time near the retarded moment.

III.2
Emitted part of the gravitational field

Let us return to the point particles contribution into the gravitational radiation (
III.1
). Due to the particles energy-momentum tensor (
II.32
) being just the sum of energy-momentum tensors of individual particles, it is convenient to calculate the gravitational field of one particle and then take the sum of two analogous terms.

Retarded gravitational field of a point particle with mass
m
moving along an arbitrary worldline
z^{M}(\tau)
, in accordance with Eq. (
III.1
), is given as

\bar{h}_{AB}^{\rm P}=\frac{m\kappa_{5}}{\pi^{2}}\int d\tau\,v_{A}v_{B}\,\theta(X^{0})\left[\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{1/2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{3/2}}\right].

(III.27)

By analogy with the scalar field, its derivative has the form

\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{AB}^{\rm P}=-\frac{2m\kappa_{5}}{\pi^{2}}\int d\tau\,v_{A}v_{B}\,\theta(X^{0})\left[\frac{3}{4}\frac{\theta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{5/2}}-\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{\delta^{\prime}(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{1/2}}\right]X_{M}.

(III.28)

We transform the term containing derivative of delta function by the integration by parts using the relation (
III.5
). We, also, use relation (
III.7
) to transform the products of delta and Heaviside functions. Thus, the gravitational field derivative takes the form

\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{AB}^{\rm P}=-\frac{m\kappa_{5}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\left[3\frac{v_{A}v_{B}X_{M}}{(-X^{2})^{5/2}}-\frac{v_{A}v_{B}X_{M}}{\hat{\rho}(-X^{2})^{3/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})-\right.\\
-\left.\frac{2a_{(A}v_{B)}X_{M}-v_{A}v_{B}v_{M}}{\hat{\rho}(vX)(-X^{2})^{1/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})+\frac{v_{A}v_{B}X_{M}[(aX)+1]}{\hat{\rho}(vX)^{2}(-X^{2})^{1/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})\right],

(III.29)

where
a^{M}=d^{2}z^{M}/d\tau^{2}
is the acceleration five-vector, and we defined symmetrisation over two indices as
A_{(M}B_{N)}=\frac{1}{2}(A_{M}B_{N}+A_{N}B_{M})
.

We extract the emitted part of the gravitational field derivative (
III.29
) by use of the relations (
III.8
), expanding the integrand in the inverse powers of the Lorentz-invariant distance
\hat{\rho}
. By analogy with the scalar field, it is found as

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{AB}^{\rm P})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{m\kappa_{5}\hat{c}_{M}}{2^{3/2}\pi^{2}\hat{\rho}^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\left[\frac{3}{4}\frac{v_{A}v_{B}}{(-Z\hat{c})^{5/2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{v_{A}v_{B}}{(-Z\hat{c})^{3/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})-\right.\\
-\left.\frac{2a_{(A}v_{B)}}{(v\hat{c})(-Z\hat{c})^{1/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})+\frac{v_{A}v_{B}(a\hat{c})}{(v\hat{c})^{2}(-Z\hat{c})^{1/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})\right].

(III.30)

Terms containing delta functions are transformed by analogy with Eqs. (
III.10
–
III.12
)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\frac{1}{2}\frac{v_{A}v_{B}}{(-Z\hat{c})^{3/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})=\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hat{v}_{A}\hat{v}_{B}}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{5/2}},

(III.31)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\frac{2a_{(A}v_{B)}}{(v\hat{c})(-Z\hat{c})^{1/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})=-\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\,\frac{\hat{a}_{(A}\hat{v}_{B)}}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{3/2}},

(III.32)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\frac{v_{A}v_{B}(a\hat{c})}{(v\hat{c})^{2}(-Z\hat{c})^{1/2}}\delta(\tau-\hat{\tau})=\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\frac{1}{2}\frac{\hat{v}_{A}\hat{v}_{B}(\hat{a}\hat{c})}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{3/2}}.

(III.33)

Therefore, the emitted part of the retarded gravitational field takes the form analogous to that of the scalar field (
III.13
)

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{AB}^{\rm P})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{m\kappa_{5}\hat{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\pi^{2}\hat{\rho}^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{v_{A}v_{B}}{(-Z\hat{c})^{5/2}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\hat{v}_{A}\hat{v}_{B}}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{5/2}}+\frac{2\hat{a}_{(A}\hat{v}_{B)}+\hat{v}_{A}\hat{v}_{B}(\hat{a}\hat{c})}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{3/2}}\right].

(III.34)

Note that, by analogy with the scalar field (
III.13
), all the information concerning the gravitational field in the wave zone is contained in the first term of Eq. (
III.34
), while the remaining terms just subtract the divergences present in the first one at the upper integration limit. Also, as in the case of scalar field, the emitted part of five-dimensional gravitational field depends on the history of the particle’s motion.

III.3
Gravitational radiation of non-relativistic particle

Now we turn to the computation of gravitational radiation from the non-relativistic particle. By analogy with the case of scalar field, we assume that: (i) particle is non-relativistic
|\mathbf{v}|\ll 1,\,\forall\tau
; (ii) it moves inside the compact region of space
|\mathbf{z}|\leq d,\forall\tau
; (iii) the observation point is far from this region
d\ll r
. We are interested only in the spatial components of metric perturbations

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm P})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{m\kappa_{5}\hat{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\pi^{2}\hat{\rho}^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\hat{\tau}}d\tau\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{v_{i}v_{j}}{(-Z\hat{c})^{5/2}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\hat{v}_{i}\hat{v}_{j}}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{5/2}}+\frac{2\hat{a}_{(i}\hat{v}_{j)}+\hat{v}_{i}\hat{v}_{j}(\hat{a}\hat{c})}{(\hat{\tau}-\tau)^{3/2}}\right],

(III.35)

because it is them, which contribute into the effective energy-momentum tensor of gravitational field (
II.17
).

By analogy with the scalar field, using expansions of the retarded covariant quantities Eqs. (
III.14
–
III.16
) and introducing the spacelike vector
\mathbf{s}(t^{\prime})
defined by Eq. (
III.18
) (here we replaced the proper time with coordinate one), we obtain the non-relativistic approximation of the emitted part of the gravitatonal field up to the leading order in small parameters
|\mathbf{z}|/r,|\mathbf{v}|,|\mathbf{s}|\ll 1
as

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm P})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{m\kappa_{5}\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\left[\frac{15}{4}v_{i}v_{j}\frac{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{v_{i}v_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\bar{v}_{i}\bar{v}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}+\right.\\
+\left.\frac{2\bar{a}_{(i}\bar{v}_{j)}+\bar{v}_{i}\bar{v}_{j}\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{a}}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{3/2}}\right].

(III.36)

Here, all the information concerning the gravitational field in the wave zone is contained in the first two terms of the integrand, while the remaining two terms just subtract the divergences present in them at the upper integration limit.

To make the convergence of integral (
III.36
) explicit, by analogy with the scalar field, one needs to integrate the first two terms of the integrand by parts reducing the powers of denominators to
1/2
. Detailed discussion of this integration is relegated to the Appendix B, while here we just present the result remaining after the mutual cancellation of divergences

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm P})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{m\kappa_{5}\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\left[2v_{i}v_{j}\frac{\mathbf{n}\dot{\mathbf{a}}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}+12a_{(i}v_{j)}\frac{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{a}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}+\right.\\
+\left.\left(\dot{a}_{i}v_{j}+2a_{i}a_{j}+v_{i}\dot{a}_{j}\right)\frac{6\mathbf{n}\mathbf{v}-5\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{v}}+2}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}-2\left(\ddot{a}_{i}v_{j}+3\dot{a}_{i}a_{j}+3a_{i}\dot{a}_{j}+v_{i}\ddot{a}_{j}\right)\frac{\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{z}}-\mathbf{n}\mathbf{z}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}\right].

(III.37)

However, the terms in the integrand of Eq. (
III.37
) does not contribute equivalently into the non-relativistic approximation of the emitted part of the gravitational field.

To perform power counting, let us consider the Fourier transform of the particle’s world line

z^{k}(t)=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,\tilde{z}^{k}(\omega)e^{-i\omega t},\quad\tilde{z}^{k}(\omega)=\int dt\,z^{k}(t)e^{i\omega t}.

(III.38)

When particle performs a periodic motion inside the compact region of space
|\mathbf{z}|\leq d
, the Fourier transform of its world line satisfies the inequality

\tilde{z}^{k}(\omega)\leq d\int dte^{i\omega t}=2\pi d\delta(\omega),

(III.39)

where the right-hand side corresponds to the particle at rest at the border of this region. Thus, the Fourier transform of periodically moving particle’s world line has to be of form

\tilde{z}^{k}(\omega)=2\pi df^{k}(\omega),

(III.40)

where
f^{k}(\omega)
are the functions localised around a characteristic frequency of periodic motion
\omega_{\rm ch}
. Then, the
n
-th derivative of the particle’s world line is found as

\frac{d^{n}z^{k}}{dt^{n}}=(-i)^{n}\int d\omega\,d\,\omega^{n}f^{k}(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}\sim-i\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{d}\frac{d^{n-1}z^{k}}{dt^{n-1}},

(III.41)

where, due to the localisation of integration interval around
\omega_{\rm ch}
, product
d\omega
has to be of order of particle’s velocity
|\mathbf{v}|
. The case of circular motion is the most transparent example

\displaystyle z^{k}(t)=R_{0}\cos(\omega_{0}t+\phi),

(III.42)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}z^{k}}{dt^{n}}=\omega^{n-1}_{0}|\mathbf{v}|\cos\left(\omega_{0}t+\phi+\frac{\pi n}{2}\right),

(III.43)

where
\omega_{0}R_{0}
is the particle’s velocity. Therefore, in the non-relativistic limit, all the particle’s kinematic characteristics are of order of its velocity
d^{n}z^{k}/dt^{n}\sim|\mathbf{v}|\ll 1
. Following the same reasoning, one can show that
z^{k}\,d^{n}z^{l}/dt^{n}\sim|\mathbf{v}|^{2},\,n\geq 2
.

Based on the power counting scheme above, we find that in the non-relativistic limit only one term in the integrand in Eq. (
III.37
) contributes into the emitted part of the gravitational field

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm P})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{m\kappa_{5}\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{3/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\dot{a}_{i}v_{j}+2a_{i}a_{j}+v_{i}\dot{a}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}.

(III.44)

Note that, by analogy with the scalar field (
III.24
), in the non-relativistic limit, emitted part of the gravitational field depends on the entire history of particle’s motion preceding the retarded time.

III.4
Gravitational radiation of binary system

Having found the non-relativistic approximation of the gravitational field of individual particle (
III.44
), we proceed to the case of non-relativistic binary system. Without loss of generality, all the previous calculations could be performed in the center-of-mass frame

\mathbf{x}_{\rm CM}=\frac{m_{1}\mathbf{z}_{1}+m_{2}\mathbf{z}_{2}}{M}=0,\forall t,

(III.45)

where
M=m_{1}+m_{2}
is the total mass of the system. Then, the particles’ coordinates are rewritten in terms of the relative coordinate
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}_{2}-\mathbf{z}_{1}
in a standard manner

\mathbf{z}_{1}=-\frac{m_{2}}{M}\mathbf{z},\quad\mathbf{z}_{2}=\frac{m_{1}}{M}\mathbf{z}.

(III.46)

Therefore, in the center-of-mass frame, superposition of gravitational field’s from individual particles forming the binary system is given by the integral (
III.44
) calculated on the relative coordinate

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm P})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{3/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\dot{a}_{i}v_{j}+2a_{i}a_{j}+v_{i}\dot{a}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}},

(III.47)

where
\mu=m_{1}m_{2}/M
is the reduced mass of the system. It is the Eq. (
III.47
) that gives the point particles contribution into the gravitational radiation of the binary system.

IV
Scalar field contribution

Contribution of the scalar field into the gravitational radiation of the binary system, by analogy with the one from the point particles (
III.1
), is written as

\bar{h}_{MN}^{\rm F}(x)=-2\kappa_{5}\int d^{5}x^{\prime}\,T_{MN}^{\rm F}(x^{\prime})\,G_{\rm ret}^{4+1}(x-x^{\prime}),

(IV.1)

where the scalar field energy-momentum tensor is given by Eq. (
II.33
) and the retarded Green’s function is defined by Eq. (
II.35
).

Recall that to determine the energy-momentum flux of gravitational radiation (
II.17
) one needs only the spatial components of metric perturbations in the transverse-traceless gauge. Computing the contractions (
II.22
), we take into account the vanishing traces of the projector (
II.21
) and that the second term in the scalar field energy-momentum tensor
T_{ij}^{\rm F}
is proportional to the Kronecker delta. Therefore, the scalar field energy-momentum tensor effectively reduces to

T_{ij}^{\rm F}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{j}\varphi\,\delta(x^{4}).

(IV.2)

Also, recall the condition for absence of the scalar radiation from the system. Based on it, we omit the self-energy type terms in the scalar field energy-momentum tensor

T_{ij}^{\rm F}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_{i}\varphi_{1}\partial_{j}\varphi_{2}\,\delta(x^{4})+(i\leftrightarrow j)

(IV.3)

leaving only the interaction type ones. Therefore, we obtain the effective scalar field contribution into the gravitational field as

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm F}(x)=J_{ij}(x)+(i\leftrightarrow j),

(IV.4)

\displaystyle J_{ij}(x)=-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{2\pi}\int d^{5}x^{\prime}\,\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})\,G_{\rm ret}^{5}(x-x^{\prime})\,\delta(x^{\prime 4}).

(IV.5)

We compute the
J_{ij}(x)
integral and symmetrise the obtained result over the indices to find the gravitational radiation produced by the scalar field.

(IV.7)

\displaystyle J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{5}}{4\pi^{3}}\int d^{5}x^{\prime}\,C_{ij}(x^{\prime})\,\theta(X^{0})\delta(-X^{2})\,\delta(x^{\prime 4}),

where we introduced vector
X^{M}=x^{M}-x^{\prime M}
. The tail integral (

) corresponds to the integration of tensor

C_{ij}(x^{\prime})=\frac{\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})}{(-X^{2})^{1/2}}

(IV.10)

over the cross-section of the light cone by the hypersurface

. This integration region is denoted as

IV.1
Spacetime splitting – near and radiation zones

In our computations we follow the DIRE approach to the post-Newtonian expansions in the four-dimensional GR
Pati:2000vt
(see, also,
Thorne:1980ru
;
Will:1996zj
for compendium of formulas), limiting ourselves to the leading order contributions. In this approach, splitting the spacetime into zones yielding different in orders of magnitude contributions into the gravitational field seems to be essential. So, let us briefly recall their definitions.

In what follows, we work in the center-of-mass frame (
III.45
) and denote the characteristic size of the binary system as
{\cal S}
. First, the
source zone

{\cal T}
is defined as the world tube covering the region of particles motion

{\cal T}=\left\{x^{M}|r<{\cal S};-\infty<x^{0}<+\infty\right\}.

(IV.11)

It is clear that outside the source zone
\cal T
particles energy-momentum tensor vanishes
T_{MN}^{\rm P}=0
. In the center-of-mass frame, the origin lies inside
\cal T
.

We assume that the non-relativistic particles move with characteristic velocity
v\ll 1
determining the characteristic wavelength
\lambdabar_{\rm GW}\sim{\cal S}/v\gg{\cal S}
of the gravitational waves generated by the system. Then, the
near zone

\cal D
is defined as the world tube of radius
{\cal R}={\cal S}/v

{\cal D}=\left\{x^{M}|r<{\cal R};-\infty<x^{0}<+\infty\right\},

(IV.12)

see Figs. (
1
). Inside
\cal D
, we consider the gravitational and scalar fields as the instantaneous functions of the particles world lines, i.e., we neglect the retardation of the fields inside the near zone. Finally, the
radiation zone
is defined as the region exterior to the near zone,
r>{\cal R}
. As we are interested in the gravitational radiation of the system, the observation point is assumed to lie in the radiation zone,
|\mathbf{x}|\gg{\cal R}
.

Based on the spacetime zones above, we split the integration regions of the tail and cone integrals. We denote the intersection of the near zone with the tail integral’s region as
{\cal K}={\cal C}_{\rm in}\cap{\cal D}
, see Fig. (
1
). Analogously, intersection of the near zone with the cone integral’s hypersurface is denoted as
{\cal N}={\cal C}\cap{\cal D}
, see Fig. (
1
). Then, the Eqs. (
IV.7
) and (
IV.8
) split into the integrals over the near and radiation zones

\displaystyle J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal K}}d^{5}x^{\prime}\,T_{ij}(x^{\prime})-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal C}_{\rm in}\setminus{\cal K}}d^{5}x^{\prime}\,T_{ij}(x^{\prime}),

(IV.13)

\displaystyle J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{5}}{4\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal N}}d^{5}x^{\prime}\,C_{ij}(x^{\prime})+\frac{\kappa_{5}}{4\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal C}\setminus{\cal N}}d^{5}x^{\prime}\,C_{ij}(x^{\prime}).

(IV.14)

However, as we assume particles to be non-relativistic and we are interested only in the leading contributions into the tail and cone integrals, we suppose the radius of the near zone
\cal R
to be large enough for the scalar field energy-momentum tensor
T_{ij}^{\rm F}
to be vanishingly small in the radiation zone. Thus, the tail and cone integrals effectively reduce to

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal K}}d^{5}x^{\prime}\,T_{ij}(x^{\prime}),\quad J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{5}}{4\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal N}}d^{5}x^{\prime}\,C_{ij}(x^{\prime}).

(IV.15)

Also, based on the reasoning above, we suppose that the boundary terms, which would arise from the integrations by parts of the spatial integrals in (
IV.15
), are defined by regions
|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|\sim{\cal R}
and, thus, negligibly small. By analogy, we neglect the contributions into resulting integrals proportional to
{\cal R}^{-n},n>0
.

IV.2
Tail integral

We start with computation of the tail integral (
IV.15
) and its contribution into the emitted part of gravitational field. Restoring its explicit form we arrive at

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal K}}d^{4}x^{\prime}\,\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})\,\theta(X^{0})\left.\frac{\theta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{3/2}}\right|_{x^{\prime 4}=0},

(IV.16)

where the subscript
\cal K
denotes that we limit integration region to the near zone, and we performed integration over the
x^{\prime 4}
-coordinate by use of the delta function.

Heaviside functions in Eq. (
IV.16
) determine the interval for the temporal integral

t^{\prime}\leq t-\sqrt{(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})^{2}+(x^{4})^{2}}=t_{\rm ret}(\vec{x}^{\prime}),

(IV.17)

where
t=x^{0}
, and we denote coordinates on the brane as
\vec{x}
. Separating the spatial and temporal integrals, we obtain the tail integral as

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal K}}d^{3}x^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{\rm ret}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})}{\left[(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})^{2}-(x^{4})^{2}\right]^{3/2}}.

(IV.18)

Note that for the observation point in the radiation zone
|\vec{x}^{\prime}|\leq{\cal R}\ll r
. Then, expanding the retarded time (
IV.17
) and the denominator in Eq. (
IV.18
) up to the leading order in small parameter
|\vec{x}^{\prime}|/r\ll 1
as

\displaystyle t_{\rm ret}(\vec{x}^{\prime})\simeq\bar{t}+\vec{x}\vec{x}^{\prime}/r,

(IV.19)

\displaystyle(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})^{2}-(x^{4})^{2}\simeq(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-r^{2}+2\vec{x}\vec{x}^{\prime},

(IV.20)

we come to the tail integral in form

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal K}}d^{3}x^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}+\vec{x}\vec{x}^{\prime}/r}dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})}{\left[(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-r^{2}+2\vec{x}\vec{x}^{\prime}\right]^{3/2}}.

(IV.21)

As we are interested only in the leading contribution into the tail and cone integrals, we expand the temporal integral in (
IV.21
) up to the leading order in
|\vec{x}^{\prime}|/r
, obtaining

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal K}}d^{3}x^{\prime}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})}{[(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-r^{2}]^{3/2}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{\vec{x}^{\prime}}{r}\right)\right].

(IV.22)

Therefore, in Eq. (
IV.22
) the spatial and temporal integrals decoupled and we can calculate them independently.

To transform the Eq. (
IV.22
) into the form analogous to that of the point particles contribution (
III.47
), we have to compute the spatial integral. We integrate it by parts

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal K}}d^{3}x^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})}{[(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-r^{2}]^{3/2}},

(IV.23)

omitting, as discussed above, the boundary terms. Given that inside
\cal K
retardation of the fields is negligible, the scalar fields are given by Eq. (
II.29
), and the obtained integral is easily computed using the methods developed in the DIRE approach. We relegate the detailed discussion of this calculation to the Appendix C, while here we just present the result

\int_{{\cal K}}d^{3}x^{\prime}\,\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})=-\pi\mu[a_{i}(t^{\prime})z_{j}(t^{\prime})+z_{i}(t^{\prime})a_{j}(t^{\prime})]

(IV.24)

in terms of the relative coordinate of the binary system
z_{i}(t^{\prime})
, where
\mu
is its reduced mass. Thus, the tail integral takes the form

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{a_{i}z_{j}+z_{i}a_{j}}{[(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-r^{2}]^{3/2}}.

(IV.25)

Also, we transform the denominator in the Eq. (
IV.25
), rewriting the observation time
t
in terms of the retarded time
t=\bar{t}+r
and arriving at

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{a_{i}z_{j}+z_{i}a_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{3/2}[\bar{t}-t^{\prime}+2r]^{3/2}}.

(IV.26)

It is clear that the leading contribution into this integral comes from the small region around the retarded time
\bar{t}-t^{\prime}\ll 2r
. Therefore, the leading contribution into the tail integral has the form

J_{ij}^{\rm T}(x)=-\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}}{2^{9/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\,\frac{a_{i}z_{j}+z_{i}a_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{3/2}}

(IV.27)

analogous to the contribution of the point particles into the gravitational field of the system (
III.47
). Note that the obtained integral diverges on the upper integration limit.

IV.3
Cone integral

Now we turn to the calculation of the cone integral (
IV.15
), which explicitly is written as

J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{5}}{4\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal N}}d^{4}x^{\prime}\,\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})\,\theta(X^{0})\left.\frac{\delta(-X^{2})}{(-X^{2})^{1/2}}\right|_{x^{\prime 4}=0},

(IV.28)

where we performed the integration over
x^{\prime 4}
-coordinate by use of the delta function.

We transform the product of delta and Heaviside functions by use of the formula for delta function of complex argument

\left.\theta(X^{0})\delta(-X^{2})\right|_{x^{\prime 4}=0}=\frac{\delta(t^{\prime}-t_{\rm ret})}{2\sqrt{(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})^{2}+(x^{4})^{2}}},

(IV.29)

where

is the retarded time defined by Eq. (

J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}}\int_{{\cal N}}d^{3}x^{\prime}\int dt^{\prime}\,\frac{\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})\,\delta(t^{\prime}-t_{\rm ret})}{\sqrt{(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})^{2}+(x^{4})^{2}}\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})^{2}-(x^{4})^{2}}}.

(IV.30)

By analogy with the tail integral, we expand the Eq. (
IV.30
) with respect to the small parameter
|\vec{x}^{\prime}|/r\ll 1
keeping only the leading contribution

J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{3}r}\int_{{\cal N}}d^{3}x^{\prime}\int dt^{\prime}\,\frac{\partial_{i}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{1}(x^{\prime})\,\partial_{j}^{\,\prime}\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime})\,\delta(t^{\prime}-\bar{t})}{\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-r^{2}}},

(IV.31)

where the spatial and temporal integrals decouple.

Spatial integral in (

J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}}{8\pi^{2}r}\int dt^{\prime}\,\frac{a_{i}z_{j}+z_{i}a_{j}}{\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-r^{2}}}\,\delta(t^{\prime}-\bar{t}),

where

is the relative coordinate of the binary system. Rewriting the coordinate time in terms of the retarded time

and taking into account that the leading contribution into the integral (

, due to the delta function, we transform denominator of the integrand by analogy with Eq. (

J_{ij}^{\rm C}(x)=\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}}{2^{7/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int dt^{\prime}\,\frac{a_{i}z_{j}+z_{i}a_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}\,\delta(t^{\prime}-\bar{t}).

(IV.33)

Transforming the Eq. (

(IV.34)

IV.4
Emitted part of the gravitational field

Combining Eqs. (
IV.27
) and (

In accordance with the Rohrlich-Teitelboim approach, one needs to find the emitted part of the gravitational field derivative. Note that all the observation point dependence of the Eq. (

In addition, differentiation of the upper limit of integration containing retarded time leads to the sum of two diverging terms that cancel each other out, and thus also do not contribute to gravitational radiation.
Therefore, the emitted part of the gravitational field produced by the scalar field stresses has the form

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm F})^{\rm rad}=\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}(\partial_{M}\bar{t})^{\rm rad}}{2^{7/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{a_{i}z_{j}+z_{i}a_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{z}_{i}\bar{a}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}+\right.\\
+\left.\frac{\bar{\dot{a}}_{i}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{a}_{i}\bar{v}_{j}+\bar{v}_{i}\bar{a}_{j}+\bar{z}_{i}\bar{\dot{a}}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{3/2}}\right].

(IV.36)

By analogy with the contribution of point particles, the first term in the Eq. (

into the upper integration limit

and integrate the first term by parts twice, arriving at

\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\frac{3}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}-\epsilon}dt^{\prime}\frac{a_{i}z_{j}+z_{i}a_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{5/2}}=\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\frac{\bar{a}_{i}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{z}_{i}\bar{a}_{j}}{\epsilon^{3/2}}-2\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\frac{\bar{\dot{a}}_{i}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{a}_{i}\bar{v}_{j}+\bar{v}_{i}\bar{a}_{j}+\bar{z}_{i}\bar{\dot{a}}_{j}}{\epsilon^{1/2}}+\\
+2\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\ddot{a}_{i}z_{j}+2\dot{a}_{i}v_{j}+2a_{i}a_{j}+2v_{i}\dot{a}_{j}+z_{i}\ddot{a}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}.

(IV.37)

The first two divergent terms in Eq. (

The derivative of the retarded time

is found as

(\partial_{M}\bar{t})^{\rm rad}=\partial_{M}\bar{t}=-\bar{c}_{M}.

(IV.38)

Therefore, the emitted part of the gravitational field due to the scalar field contribution takes the form

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij}^{\rm F})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\frac{\ddot{a}_{i}z_{j}+2\dot{a}_{i}v_{j}+2a_{i}a_{j}+2v_{i}\dot{a}_{j}+z_{i}\ddot{a}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}},

(IV.39)

analogous to the emitted part of the gravitational field of point particles (

V
Five-dimensional quadrupole formula

Having found the point particles and scalar field contributions into the emitted part of gravitational field, we turn to the calculation of total gravitational radiation power from the binary system.

V.1
Emitted part of total gravitational field

Combining the Eqs. (
III.47

(\partial_{M}\bar{h}_{ij})^{\rm rad}=-\frac{\mu\kappa_{5}\bar{c}_{M}}{2^{5/2}\pi^{2}r^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{t}}dt^{\prime}\,\frac{\ddot{a}_{i}z_{j}+4\dot{a}_{i}v_{j}+6a_{i}a_{j}+4v_{i}\dot{a}_{j}+z_{i}\ddot{a}_{j}}{(\bar{t}-t^{\prime})^{1/2}}.

Rewriting the denominator in Eq. (

(V.3)

As we consider the non-relativistic particles, we define, by analogy with the four-dimensional theory

where

(V.6)

Therefore, by analogy with the gravitational radiation in four dimensions

Moreover, recall that we are interested in the emitted part of gravitational field in the transverse-traceless gauge given by Eq. (
II.53

V.3

V.4
Evolution of the orbit

As we emphasized from the very beginning, this calculation should be considered as a mathematically consistent but physically unrealistic model, so we refrain from making any quantitative physical predictions. In particular, the evolution of the orbit, which we consider in this subsection as a logical extension of our model, cannot be considered as an attempt to extract experimentally available predictions and gives a result that clearly contradicts the observational data of binary pulsars. However, we present this calculation simply as an example of a toy model showing how different frequency dependences give different predictions for the lifetime of a binary system.

Consider the orbital evolution of the system due to gravitational radiation. As in the non-relativistic limit the gravitational-wave energy loss over the period of system’s motion is small compared to its total energy, we assume that the system moves on a quasi-circular orbit. Indeed, by analogy with the four-dimensional theory
maggiore2008
, we consider the orbital radius
R_{\rm s}(t)
and the frequency of orbital motion

as the slowly varying functions of time satisfying condition

-\dot{R}_{\rm s}\ll\omega_{\rm s}R_{\rm s},

(V.28)

corresponding to the slow shrinking of orbit. The orbital evolution is governed by the energy conservation law

\frac{dE_{\rm tot}}{dt}=-W_{4+1}^{\rm circ},

(V.29)

where

is the total mechanical energy of the non-relativistic binary system, and gravitational radiation power is given by the Eq. (
V.27

We rewrite the mechanical energy of the system and its radiation power in terms of the frequency of orbital motion by use of its relation to the orbital radius. Substituting into the Eq. (
II.31

V.14

Using the Eq. (

\displaystyle E_{\rm tot}=\frac{\mu\mathbf{v}^{2}}{2}-\frac{g_{1}g_{2}}{|\mathbf{z}|}=-\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{\mu}g_{1}g_{2})^{2/3}\omega_{\rm s}^{2/3},

(V.31)

\displaystyle W_{4+1}^{\rm circ}=\frac{5}{9\pi}\kappa_{5}(\sqrt{\mu}g_{1}g_{2})^{4/3}\omega_{\rm s}^{13/3}.

(V.32)

Therefore, substituting the Eqs. (
V.31
) and (
V.32
) into the energy conservation law (
V.29
), we come to the equation for the evolution of the orbital frequency

\displaystyle\dot{\omega}_{\rm s}=\frac{5\kappa_{5}}{3\pi}(\sqrt{\mu}g_{1}g_{2})^{2/3}\omega_{\rm s}^{14/3},

(V.33)

\displaystyle\dot{\omega}_{\rm s}\ll\omega_{\rm s}^{2},

(V.34)

where Eq. (
V.34

V.28

Solution to the Eq. (
V.33

\omega_{\rm s}(t)=\left(\frac{9\pi}{55\kappa_{5}}\right)^{3/11}(\sqrt{\mu}g_{1}g_{2})^{-2/11}s^{-3/11},

(V.35)

where

, and

is the moment of coalescence of the binary system. From the Eq. (

\dot{R}_{\rm s}=-\frac{2}{3}R_{\rm s}\frac{\dot{\omega}_{\rm s}}{\omega_{\rm s}},

(V.36)

and from the Eq. (

(V.37)

where

is the orbital radius at some moment of time

\omega_{\rm s,\,4D}(t)\sim(t_{\rm coal}-t)^{-3/8},\quad R_{\rm s,\,4D}(t)\sim(t_{\rm coal}-t)^{1/4},

(V.38)

as could be expected, given that in our work gravity is described by the five-dimensional GR with the infinite-volume extra dimension being phenomenologically non-viable.

VI
Conclusion

In this article, we have considered (partially inspired by the DGP model) a mixed environment with five-dimensional gravity and four-dimensional matter consisting of point particles and a scalar field. The mechanism of dynamic confinement was not imposed, and the localization of particle motion in the brane was assumed to be purely kinematic due to the appropriate choice of initial conditions. The physical viability of this setup as a cosmological model is ruled out, but mathematically it is a consistent model that opens the way for calculating bulk gravitational radiation in the framework of linearized gravity. This model reflects a typical feature of radiation in odd space-time dimensions, which is the appearance of tails due to violation of the Huygens principle.

We have introduced techniques that can be useful in other problems of radiation in odd-dimensional spacetime, namely, the extraction of the radiative part of the retarded potential using a modification of the Rohrlich-Teitelboim approach. It has also been shown that the contribution of field stresses through which two point masses interact to gravitational radiation can be explicitly taken into account using the DIRE approach to post-Newtonian expansions in four dimensions. As a result, it turned out that in odd dimensions the radiated part of the gravitational field depends on the entire history of the system’s motion preceding the retarded time, in contrast to even dimensions, where it depends only on the state of the system at the retarded moment of proper time. Another interesting feature of odd dimensions is that the retarded gravitational field is given by the sum of separately divergent integrals. However, these divergences cancel each other out, and the resulting retarded field and its radiated part are finite.

We considered the gravitational radiation from the binary system of two non-relativistic point particles deriving the five-dimensional analog of the quadrupole formula for gravitational radiation power (
V.10
). We found that in contrast with the ultrarelativistic synchrotron radiation in odd dimensions
Galtsov:2020hhn
, the dependence of the gravitational radiation of a nonrelativistic source on its motion history is not localized near the retarded time.

Another feature concerns polarization. In five dimensions free gravitational waves have five polarizations. If motion is restricted to four-dimensional branes, an observer living on the brane will see three of them – two standard ”cross” and ”plus” polarisations and an additional ”breathing” mode. This feature was also provided from other
Andriot2017
considerations and may be of interest for more realistic models

Now let’s briefly discuss the relationship of our results with other work. Our quadrupole formula agrees with the formula obtained by Chu
Chu:2021uea
using the Fourier transforms of the retarded Green’s functions. In addition, our results fit into the general schemes for the quadrupole formula and the radiation power of the system in a circular orbit obtained by Cardoso
et al.

Cardoso:2002pa
for the arbitrary even dimensions. We also expect that the resulting formula for the radiation power of the system at the circular orbit can be verified using the results obtained by Cardoso
et al.

Cardoso:2008gn
in effective field theory approach framework.

Unconditional uniqueness for the Benjamin-Ono equation
Razvan Mosincat, Didier Pilod

Abstract
. We study the unconditional uniqueness of solutions to the Benjamin-Ono equation with initial data in H s H^{s} , both on the real line and on the torus. We use the gauge transformation of Tao and two iterations of normal form reductions via integration by parts in time. By employing a refined Strichartz estimate we establish the result below the regularity threshold s = 1 / 6 s=1/6 . As a by-product of our proof, we also obtain a nonlinear smoothing property on the gauge variable at the same level of regularity.

We now briefly describe our approach to proving the above unconditional well-posedness result for BO.
We first renormalize the equation (

At this point

\partial_{t}w-i\partial_{x}^{2}w=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big]+\text{``negligible term''}\,.

Also, by following the idea used in

It then remains to establish reverse estimates with constants that can be taken arbitrarily small.
To this purpose,
the idea is to further renormalize the main nonlinearity in (

via the fairly elementary method of integration by parts in the temporal variable.
By considering the Van der Pole change of variables on the Fourier side, i.e.

\widetilde{u}(t,\xi):=\mathcal{F}(e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t))(\xi)\ ,\ \widetilde{w}(t,\xi):=\mathcal{F}(e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}w(t))(\xi)\,,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{it\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi-\xi_{1})}\,\xi\,\widetilde{u}(\xi_{1})\widetilde{u}(\xi-\xi_{1})d\xi_{1}\ ,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widetilde{w}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{it\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi-\xi_{1})}\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi-\xi_{1})\,\frac{\xi(\xi-\xi_{1})}{\xi_{1}}\,\widetilde{w}(\xi_{1})\widetilde{u}(\xi-\xi_{1})d\xi_{1}\ ,

Here,

gathers the symbols of the frequency projections in the main nonlinearity of (

Also, for the sake of exposition, we dropped the contribution of the negligible term of (

We then integrate by parts in the Duhamel formulation of (

\begin{split}\widetilde{w}(t)-\widetilde{w}(0)=&-2\Bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{e^{it^{\prime}\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi-\xi_{1})}}{i\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi-\xi_{1})}\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi-\xi_{1})\,\frac{\xi(\xi-\xi_{1})}{\xi_{1}}\,\widetilde{w}(\xi_{1})\widetilde{u}(\xi-\xi_{1})d\xi_{1}\Bigg]_{t^{\prime}=0}^{t^{\prime}=t}\\

-norm,
s\geq 0

\frac{2}{\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi-\xi_{1})}\frac{\xi(\xi-\xi_{1})}{\xi_{1}}=\frac{1}{\xi_{1}}\,.

After substituting

Remark 1.6

To justify rigorously the two integration by parts, we perform dyadic decompositions of each function involved in the nonlinear terms. We recall that the Hilbert transform on

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}f)(x)=\mathrm{p.v.}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}dy

\widehat{\mathcal{H}f}(\xi)=-i\sgn(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)

where
\sgn(0)=0

\sgn(\xi)=1
for
\xi>0

\sgn(\xi)=-1
for
\xi<0

The Riesz projection operators

are defined via

\widehat{P_{\pm}f}(\xi)=\mathbf{1}_{>0}(\pm\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,,

where
\mathbf{1}_{>0}
and
\mathbf{1}_{<0}
denote the indicator functions of the intervals
(0,\infty)

and
(-\infty,0)
, respectively.
More generally, we use
\mathbf{1}_{\text{``Expr''}}
as the indicator function for the set on which
“Expr”
holds true.
We know that
P_{\pm}
are bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
, only for
1<p<\infty
.
Note that we have
\mathcal{H}=-iP_{+}+iP_{-}\,
.

Let
\psi
be a smooth bump (real-valued) even function
that is equal to
1
on
[-1,1]
and vanishes outside
[-2,2]
.
For any
N\in 2^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
we use the Littlewood-Paley operators:

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{\leq N}f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\psi(N^{-1}\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,,

\displaystyle P_{N}

\displaystyle=P_{\leq N}-P_{\leq\frac{N}{2}}\,,

\displaystyle P_{>N}

\displaystyle=1-P_{\leq N}\,.

Also, we set

\displaystyle P_{\textup{lo}}:=P_{\leq 1}\ ,\ P_{\textup{hi}}:=1-P_{\textup{lo}}\ ,\ P_{\pm\text{hi}}:=P_{\pm}P_{\textup{hi}}\,,

\displaystyle P_{\textup{LO}}:=P_{\leq 2}\ ,\ P_{\textup{HI}}:=1-P_{\textup{LO}}\ ,\ P_{\pm\text{HI}}:=P_{\pm}P_{\textup{HI}}\,.

We know that
P_{\textup{lo}},P_{\textup{LO}},P_{\textup{hi}},P_{\textup{HI}},P_{\pm\textup{hi}},P_{\pm\textup{HI}}

are bounded on

, while

are bounded on

, for any

.
Note that we have

, etc.
Also,

\overline{P_{\mp}f}=P_{\pm}\overline{f}\,,\ \overline{P_{\mp\text{hi}}f}=P_{\pm\text{hi}}\overline{f}\,,\ \overline{P_{\mp\text{HI}}f}=P_{\pm\text{HI}}\overline{f}\,,\ \overline{P_{\textup{lo}}f}=P_{\textup{lo}}\overline{f}\,,\ \overline{P_{\textup{LO}}f}=P_{\textup{LO}}\overline{f}\,.

By

and

, etc.

2.2.
Basic estimates

We first recall the well-known Bernstein inequalities.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. We have

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq N}D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\sim N^{s}\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{p}}\,,

\displaystyle\|P_{\geq N}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-s}\|D^{s}P_{\geq N}f\|_{L^{p}}\,,

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{p}}\,,

\displaystyle\|D^{\pm s}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\sim N^{\pm s}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\,.

Due to the gauge transformation that we use (see (
2.10
) below),
the estimates provided by the following lemma
come in handy when estimating terms involving
e^{\pm iF}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
44
, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{q}
.
Suppose
F_{1},F_{2}
are two real-valued functions such that
u_{j}:=\partial_{x}F_{j}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
for
j=1,2
. Then

(2.1)

\big\|J^{\alpha}\big(e^{\pm iF_{1}}g)\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)\big\|J^{\alpha}g\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big\|J^{\alpha}\big((e^{\pm iF_{1}}-e^{\pm iF_{2}})g\big)\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\Big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}+\big\|e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)\Big)\big\|J^{\alpha}g\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}\,.\end{split}

2.3.
Gauge transformation

We use the idea of Tao
[
60
]
,
namely the adaptation to the Benjamin-Ono equation
of the Cole-Hopf transformation
u\mapsto e^{-iF}
, where
F
is a spatial antiderivative of
u
, that transforms
the quadratic derivative Schrödinger equation

\partial_{t}u-i\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2})

into the linear Schrödinger equation.
However, the dispersive linear part of the Benjamin-Ono equation (
1.1
) changes sign
between positive and negative frequencies.
Nonetheless,
the idea is to work with

(2.3)

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle:=P_{+\textup{hi}}(e^{-iF})\,,

at the price of dealing with an equation for
W
which is not in closed form,
and subsequently inverting (
2.3
) is more involved than simply multiplying with
e^{iF}
.

Since we are working at low regularity,
we employ here the construction of the gauge transformation of Burq and Planchon

[
9
]
that can be carried over for
u\in C_{T}L^{2}
.
It proceeds by constructing
F=F[u]
, a spatial antiderivative of
u
(i.e.
\partial_{x}F=u
),
which also satisfies

(2.4)

\partial_{t}F+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}F=u^{2}

in the sense of distributions.
Such an
F
is uniquely determined up to an additive constant More precisely, we take

(2.5)

F(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(y)\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}u(t,z)dz\bigg)dy+G(t)\,,

for some smooth, compactly supported
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(y)dy=1
, and
where we choose

(2.6)

G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big(-\mathcal{H}\psi^{\prime}(y)u(t^{\prime},y)+\psi(y)u(t^{\prime},y)^{2}\big)\,dy\,dt^{\prime}\,.

Note that
F
is real-valued.

Remark 2.3
.

We have that

e^{-iF}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, but clearly
e^{-iF}\notin L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Hence
e^{-iF}
is a tempered distribution on
{\mathbb{R}}
and its Fourier transform
\widehat{e^{-iF}}
is defined via pairing with Schwartz functions.
Provided that we stay away from the zero frequency, i.e.
|\xi|\gtrsim 1
,
we can make sense of

\widehat{e^{-iF}}(\xi)
almost everywhere.
Indeed, since
\partial_{x}(e^{-iF})\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, one easily verifies that

(2.7)

\widehat{e^{-iF}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{i\xi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-ix\xi}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF})dx\,,

for almost every
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Hence, by using the Littlewood-Paley projections,

P_{\textup{hi}}(e^{-iF})
,
P_{\textup{HI}}(e^{-iF})
,
P_{\pm\textup{hi}}(e^{-iF})
, are well-defined
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-functions.
However, due to the possible singularity at the zero frequency which is apparent in (
2.7
),

P_{\pm}(e^{-iF})

might not be well-defined (unless we impose additional assumptions on
u
itself).
We make sense of
P_{\textup{lo}}(e^{-iF})
,
P_{\textup{LO}}(e^{-iF})
not via Littlewood-Paley projections,
but by defining:

P_{\textup{lo}}(e^{-iF}):=e^{-iF}-P_{\textup{hi}}(e^{-iF})\ ,\ P_{\textup{LO}}(e^{-iF}):=e^{-iF}-P_{\textup{HI}}(e^{-iF})\,.

Still, we have
P_{\textup{HI}}P_{\textup{lo}}(e^{-iF})=P_{\textup{HI}}(e^{-iF})-P_{\textup{HI}}(e^{-iF})=0

and that
\partial_{x}P_{\textup{lo}}(e^{-iF})=P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF})
.

Similarly, for
F
itself we do not have information about its decay at spatial infinity,
we only know that
\partial_{x}F=u\in H^{s}_{x}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Thus,
P_{\textup{hi}}F,P_{\textup{HI}}F,P_{\pm\textup{hi}}F
are well-defined, whereas
P_{\pm}F
might not be.

Remark 2.4
.

If
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
, i.e.

u(t)=e^{-t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}\partial_{x}(u(t^{\prime})^{2})dt^{\prime}\,,

in the sense of spatial distributions, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
then
F=F[u]
constructed via (
2.5
) is a solution to

\partial_{t}F+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}F=(\partial_{x}F)^{2}
, i.e.

F(t)=e^{-t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}F_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}\big(\partial_{x}F(t^{\prime})\big)^{2}dt^{\prime}\,,

in the sense of spatial distributions, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
where
F_{0}(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(y)\int_{y}^{x}u_{0}(z)dzdy
.

The following is a variant of
[
44
, Lemma 4.1]
stated for two solutions with the same initial data.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
u_{1},u_{2}\in C_{T}L^{2}
are two solutions to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]

emanating from initial data
u_{1,0}
,
u_{2,0}
having the same low frequency part, i.e.
P_{lo}u_{1,0}=P_{lo}u_{2,0}
.
Let
F_{1},F_{2}
denote the corresponding spatial antiderivatives of
u_{1},u_{2}
satisfying (
2.4
)
(as per the construction above).
Then
\|F_{1}|_{t=0}-F_{2}|_{t=0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{1,0}-u_{2,0}\|_{L^{2}}
and

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{C_{T}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle T\rangle\left(\|u_{1}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+\|u_{2}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\right)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\,.

Straightforward computations give the following equation for
W
:

(2.9)

\partial_{t}W-i\partial_{x}^{2}W=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\big[\big(P_{+\textup{hi}}e^{-iF}\big)\big(P_{-}\partial_{x}^{2}F\big)\big]-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\big[\big(P_{\textup{lo}}e^{-iF}\big)\big(P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big)\big]\,.

Note that
\big(P_{-\textup{hi}}e^{-iF}\big)\big(P_{-}\partial_{x}^{2}F\big)
vanishes under
P_{+\textup{hi}}
.
Also, by Lemma
2.2
, if
u(t)\in H^{s}
then we have
W(t)\in H^{s+1}
,
for any
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

However, as in
[
42
]
,
we prefer to work at the
H^{s}
-level, namely we consider

(2.10)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle:=\partial_{x}W

and thus the Benjamin-Ono equation becomes

(2.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w-i\partial_{x}^{2}w

\displaystyle=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big]-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\big(P_{\textup{lo}}e^{-iF}\big)\big(P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big)\big]\,.

The difficult term on the right-hand side is the first term and note that
its first factor, i.e.
\partial_{x}^{-1}w
, necessarily has larger frequency than the second factor.
Recall that due to the restriction of
w
to positive high frequencies,

\partial_{x}^{-1}w
is defined as the Fourier multiplier with symbol
(i\xi)^{-1}
;
this is not the case for
u
itself, hence the need to carefully construct its antiderivative
F
.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
2.11
) is negligible in the sense that we are essentially dealing with a quadratic term involving two smooth factors. Indeed, the estimate for the difference of two such terms is straightforward and it is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6

(estimate for the negligible term in (
2.11
))
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
,
u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{2}
and denote

E(f,g):=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\big(P_{\textup{lo}}f\big)\big(P_{-}\partial_{x}g\big)\big]\,.

Then, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Big\|E\big(e^{-iF_{1}},u_{1}\big)-E\big(e^{-iF_{2}},u_{2}\big)\Big\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\,.

Proof.

We can insert two
P_{\textup{LO}}
operators, namely we have

E(f,g)=-2P_{\textup{LO}}P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\big(P_{\textup{lo}}f\big)\big(P_{\textup{LO}}P_{-}\partial_{x}g\big)\big]\,,

and thus

\displaystyle\Big\|E\big(e^{-iF_{1}},u_{1}\big)-E\big(e^{-iF_{2}},u_{2}\big)\Big\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\Big\|P_{\textup{lo}}\Big(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}}\Big)P_{\textup{LO}}P_{-}\partial_{x}u_{1}+P_{\textup{lo}}\Big(e^{-iF_{2}}\Big)P_{\textup{LO}}P_{-}\partial_{x}\big(u_{1}-u_{2}\big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\big\|P_{\textup{lo}}\big(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}}\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\textup{LO}}P_{-}\partial_{x}u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\big\|P_{\textup{lo}}\big(e^{-iF_{2}}\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\textup{LO}}P_{-}\partial_{x}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\,.

∎

Remark 2.7
.

Formally (i.e. for smooth solutions or for limits of smooth solutions), one can verify that (
2.9
)-(
2.11
) hold by straightforward computations. For a low-regularity
C_{T}L^{2}
-solution
u
to (
1.1
), one can proceed as in
[
34
, Section 2]
to justify that the gauge transformation
w
is a solution to (
2.11
) in the sense of distribution.
More precisely, for any dyadic number
N\geq 1
, we use the truncation
u_{\leq N}:=P_{\leq N}u
and define its spatial antiderivative
F_{\leq N}
by

F_{\leq N}=F(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(y)\bigg(\int_{y}^{x}u_{\leq N}(t,z)dz\bigg)dy+G_{\leq N}(t)\

where
G_{\leq N}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big(-\mathcal{H}\psi^{\prime}(y)u_{\leq N}(t^{\prime},y)+\psi(y)P_{\leq N}\big(u(t^{\prime},y)^{2}\big)\big)\,dy\,dt^{\prime}
. Then,
\partial_{x}F_{\leq N}=u_{\leq N}
and if we define
H_{\leq N}:=P_{\leq N}(u^{2})-u_{\leq N}^{2}
, the equation

\partial_{t}F_{\leq N}+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}F_{\leq N}=u_{\leq N}^{2}+H_{\leq N}

holds in the classical sense. It follows that
w_{\leq N}:=\partial_{x}P_{+\textup{hi}}(e^{-iF_{\leq N}})
satisfies

(2.13)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}w_{\leq N}-i\partial_{x}^{2}w_{\leq N}&=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w_{\leq N}\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}u_{\leq N}\big]-iP_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[e^{-iF_{\leq N}}H_{\leq N}\big]\\
&\quad+E(e^{-iF_{\leq N}},u_{\leq N})\end{split}

also in the classical sense.
Now observe from Lemma
2.5
that
(u_{\leq N},w_{\leq N},F_{\leq N})\to(u,w,F)
in
C_{T}(L^{2}\times L^{2}\times L^{\infty})
. Moreover,
H_{\leq N}\to 0
in
C_{T}H^{-1}
as
N\to+\infty
. Therefore, it follows from the estimate (see
[
34
, Section 2]
)

\big\|P_{+\textup{hi}}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big]\|_{H^{-1}}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}

and Lemma
2.6
that the right-hand side of (
2.13
) converges to the right-hand side of (
2.11
) in
C_{T}H^{-2}
, which justifies that
w
solves (
2.11
) in the distributional sense.

2.4.
Strichartz estimates

We recall here that for
u
a solution to

(2.14)

\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u=F\,,\ u|_{t=0}=u_{0}

we have the classical Strichartz estimates:

(2.15)

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|F\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\,,

for any Strichartz admissible pair, i.e.
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
with
4\leq p\leq\infty
,
2\leq q\leq+\infty
.
Next, we follow an argument of Koch-Tzvetkov in
[
38
]
and Kenig-Koenig in
[
30
]
for the Benjamin-Ono equation of decomposing the time interval
[0,T]
into small subintervals whose length depends on the size of the frequency of the solution. See also Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
7
]
for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation on compact manifolds, and Bahouri-Chemin
[
4
]
and Tataru
[
61
]
for the wave equation.

Lemma 2.8

(refined Strichartz estimates)
.

Let
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{4}
,
N\in 2^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
N\geq 2^{6}
, and
T>0
.
We assume that
(p,q)
is a Strichartz admissible pair and
we denote

(2.16)

\alpha(s,p):=\frac{\frac{3}{2}-s}{p}-s\,.

(i)
If
u
is a solution to (
2.14
) with

F=\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2}+u_{3}u_{4})
, then we have

(2.17)

\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\alpha(s,p)}\Big(\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+\|u_{3}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{4}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\Big)\,;

(ii)
If
w
is a solution to

\partial_{t}w-i\partial_{x}^{2}w=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w_{1}\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}w_{2}+\partial_{x}^{-1}w_{3}\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}w_{4}\big]+\phi\,,

where
{\text{supp}}(\widehat{w_{1}}),{\text{supp}}(\widehat{w_{3}})\subset(2^{-3},\infty)
and
{\text{supp}}(\widehat{\phi})\subset[-2^{4},2^{4}]
,
then we have

(2.18)

\|P_{N}w\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\alpha(s,p)}\Big(\|P_{N}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+\|w_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|w_{2}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+\|w_{3}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|w_{4}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\Big)\,.

Remark 2.9
.

Note that
\alpha(s,p)\searrow-s
as
p\nearrow\infty
, but for
p=\infty

we can use directly the trivial estimate

\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\sim N^{-s}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\,.

The advantage of using this refinement of the Strichartz estimate (i.e. (
2.17
))
is evident
when comparing it with the estimate

(2.19)

\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{2}{p}-s}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\,,

which follows directly from the third Bernstein inequality and Hölder inequality in time.

Remark 2.10
.

Since we would also like to apply these estimates for differences of two solutions,
we stated the estimates (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) with the term involving
u_{3},u_{4}
and
w_{3},w_{4}
, respectively.

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

With
\delta>0
to be chosen later,
let
I_{j}=:[a_{j},b_{j}]
be such that
\bigcup_{j}I_{j}=[0,T]
,
b_{j}-a_{j}\sim N^{-\delta}
, and
the number of such intervals is
\sim TN^{\delta}
.
For (i), by using (
2.15
) we get

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u\|^{p}_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{x}^{q}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim TN^{\delta}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}+\sum_{j}|I_{j}|^{p-1}\|P_{N}F\|_{L_{I_{j}}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}

which gives us

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{\delta}{p}}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+N^{-\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)\delta}\|P_{N}F\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{\delta}{p}}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{-\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)\delta}\|P_{N}F\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\,.

In particular, for

F=\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2}+u_{3}u_{4})\,,

we get

\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{\delta}{p}-s}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{1-\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)\delta}\Big(\|P_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\|P_{N}(u_{3}u_{4})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\Big)\,.

Together with

(2.20)

\|P_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq N^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2r}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2r}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{2}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}\,,

where
1\leq r\leq 2
is determined by
s=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2r}
(or equivalently,
r=\frac{1}{1-2s}
),
and with the same estimate for
P_{N}(u_{3}u_{4})
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim

\displaystyle T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{\delta}{p}-s}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}

\displaystyle\qquad+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{3}{2}-\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)\delta-2s}\Big(\|u_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+\|u_{3}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{4}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\Big)\,.

Note that the restriction on
r
restricts us to
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
We choose
\delta
such that

\frac{\delta}{p}-s=\frac{3}{2}-\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)\delta-2s\,,

or equivalently
\delta=\frac{3}{2}-s
,
and with

\alpha(s,p):=\frac{3}{2p}-\big(1+\frac{1}{p}\big)s

we obtain
(
2.17
).

To obtain (
2.18
) we argue similarly. Indeed, by using the classical Strichartz estimate
for the linear Schrödinger equation, we obtain as above

(2.21)

\|P_{N}w\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{\delta}{p}}\|P_{N}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{-\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)\delta}\|P_{N}G\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\,,

where we set

G=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w_{1}\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}w_{2}+\partial_{x}^{-1}w_{3}\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}w_{4}\big]+\phi\,.

Note that
P_{N}\phi=0
and thus with
g_{j}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\big|\mathcal{F}(w_{j})\big|\big)
,

j=1,2,3,4
, we have

\|P_{N}G\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim N\|P_{N}(g_{1}g_{2})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+N\|P_{N}(g_{3}g_{4})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\,.

Then, similarly to (
2.20
), we get

\|P_{N}(g_{j}g_{k})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}-2s}\|g_{j}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}\|g_{k}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}=N^{\frac{1}{2}-2s}\|w_{j}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}\|w_{k}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}\,,

for
(j,k)=(1,2)
or
(j,k)=(3,4)
.
From (
2.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{N}w\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{\delta}{p}-s}\|P_{N}w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}

\displaystyle\qquad+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{3}{2}-\big(1-\frac{1}{p}\big)\delta-2s}\big(\|w_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|w_{2}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+\|w_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|w_{2}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\big)

and thus (
2.18
) follows by choosing
\delta
as above.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
0<s\leq\frac{1}{4}
,
2\leq q\leq 4
such that
\left(\frac{3}{2}-s\right)\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2q}\right)-s<0
, and
N\in 2^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
N\geq 2^{6}
.
Suppose
u,u^{\dagger}
are two solutions of (
1.1
).

(i)
We have

(2.22)

\big\|P_{N}\partial_{t}\big(\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{u}^{\dagger}\big)\big\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim TN^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\big)^{3}\|u-u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\,,

where
\widetilde{u}(t)=e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t)
and
\widetilde{u}^{\dagger}(t)=e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}u^{\dagger}(t)
.

(ii)
If
w,w^{\dagger}
are the corresponding gauge transformations of
u,u^{\dagger}
,
we also have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big\|P_{N}\partial_{t}\big(\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{w}^{\dagger}\big)\big\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\\
&\qquad\lesssim TN^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\big)^{6}\big(\|w-w^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|u-u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\big)\,,\end{split}

where
\widetilde{w}(t)=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}w(t)
and
\widetilde{w}^{\dagger}(t)=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}w^{\dagger}(t)
.

Proof.

We first prove (
2.22
) for which we set
v:=u-u^{\dagger}

The equation on the Fourier side satisfied by
\widetilde{v}:=e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}v=\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{u}^{\dagger}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{\widetilde{v}}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\xi\int_{\xi_{12}=\xi}e^{it\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\big(\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t,\xi_{1})+\widehat{\widetilde{u}^{\dagger}}(t,\xi_{1})\big)\widehat{\widetilde{v}}(t,\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=i\xi e^{it|\xi|\xi}\int_{\xi_{12}=\xi}\big(\widehat{u}(t,\xi_{1})+\widehat{u^{\dagger}}(t,\xi_{1})\big)\widehat{v}(t,\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}\,.

Then, by using the Hölder and Bernstein inequalities, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\|P_{N}\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}\big\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}&\lesssim N\big\|P_{N}\big((u+u^{\dagger})v\big)\big\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim T^{1-\frac{2}{p}}N^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}\big\|P_{N}\big((u+u^{\dagger})v\big)\big\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{p}{2}}L_{x}^{\frac{q}{2}}}\\
&\lesssim T^{1-\frac{2}{p}}N^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}+\|u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\big)\|v\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\end{split}

where
(p,q)
is a Strichartz admissible pair, with
p
such that
\alpha(s,p)
is negative,
or equivalently
\left(\frac{3}{2}-s\right)\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2q}\right)-s<0
.
Note that the refined Strichartz estimates given in Lemma
2.8
(i)
apply to both
u
and
v
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\|P_{N}\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}\big\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}&\lesssim TN^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\big)^{2}\big(1+\|v\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\big)\|v\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\\
&\leq TN^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\big)^{3}\|u-u^{\dagger}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\,.\end{split}

For (
2.23
), we set
z=w-w^{\dagger}
,
\widetilde{z}=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}z=\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{w}^{\dagger}
and
\widetilde{E}=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}E
.
We have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{\widetilde{z}}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\xi\int_{\xi_{12}=\xi}e^{it\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\big(\widehat{\widetilde{z}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(\xi_{2})+\widehat{\widetilde{w}^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{\widetilde{v}}(\xi_{2})\big)d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\widehat{\widetilde{E}}\big(e^{-iF},u\big)-\widehat{\widetilde{E}}\big(e^{-iF^{\dagger}},u^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=i\xi e^{it|\xi|\xi}\int_{\xi_{12}=\xi}\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\big(\widehat{z}(\xi_{1})\widehat{u}(\xi_{2})+\widehat{w^{\dagger}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{v}(\xi_{2})\big)d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\widehat{\widetilde{E}}\big(e^{-iF},u\big)-\widehat{\widetilde{E}}\big(e^{-iF^{\dagger}},u^{\dagger}\big)\,,

where
F
and
F^{\dagger}
denote the corresponding spatial antiderivatives of
u
and
u^{\dagger}
, respectively, constructed as in the previous subsection, and
\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\chi_{+}(\xi)\widetilde{\chi}_{+}(\xi_{1})\mathbf{1}_{<0}(\xi_{2}).

Since
N
is large enough, the
E
-terms vanish under
P_{N}
.
By taking into account that
|\xi_{2}|<\xi_{1}
on the support of
\sigma

and then arguing similarly as in part (i),
we get that
\big\|P_{N}\partial_{t}\widetilde{z}\big\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}
is controlled by

\displaystyle N\big\|P_{N}P_{+hi}\big(P_{+hi}\partial_{x}^{-1}zP_{-}\partial_{x}u\big)\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+N\big\|P_{N}P_{+hi}\big(P_{+hi}\partial_{x}^{-1}w^{\dagger}P_{-}\partial_{x}v\big)\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}

We only deal with the first term as the second one is estimated similarly. By taking into account that
|\xi_{2}|<\xi_{1}
on the support of
\sigma
, performing dyadic decompositions and using Hölder’s inequality, we control the first term by

\displaystyle N\sum_{N_{1}}

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}}\|P_{N}P_{+\textup{hi}}\big(P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}^{-1}P_{N_{1}}zP_{-}\partial_{x}P_{N_{2}}u\big)\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}T^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\sum_{N_{1}}\sum_{N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}}\|P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}^{-1}P_{N_{1}}z\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{q}_{x}}\|P_{-}\partial_{x}P_{N_{2}}u\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{q}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}T^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\sum_{N_{1}}\sum_{N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}}\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}}\|P_{N_{1}}z\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{q}_{x}}\|P_{N_{2}}u\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{q}_{x}}\,.

We conclude the proof by using Lemma
2.8
as above
(the dyadic summations are finite since
\alpha(s,p)<0
) and Lemma
2.2
.
∎

2.5.
Estimates for solutions to the original BO in terms of gauge transformations

Here we follow the idea from
[
34
, Section 4]
to establish a control
for
\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}
in terms of
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}
, where

u_{1},u_{2}
are two solutions to (
1.1
) and
w_{1},w_{2}
are the corresponding gauge transformations.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
0\leq s<\frac{1}{2}
,
N\in 2^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
, and
T>0
.
Assume that
u_{1},u_{2}
are two solutions to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
with the same initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}

and let
w_{1},w_{2}
be the corresponding gauge transformations of
u_{1},u_{2}
, respectively.
Then, we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq N}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim TN^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\big)^{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\,,

(2.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|P_{>N}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}&\lesssim\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\big)^{4}\\
&\qquad\times\Big(\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}+\langle T\rangle\big(N^{s-\frac{1}{2}}+\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}w_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\big)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\Big)\,.\end{split}

Proof.

For the low-frequency part, we use
directly (
1.4
) and take the difference term by term, namely we use

\displaystyle u_{1}-u_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}\partial_{x}\big(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}\big)(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\,.

By using
the Bernstein and Hölder inequalities, we get

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq N}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\big\|P_{\leq N}\partial_{x}(u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2})\big\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim TN^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\big\|P_{\leq N}\big((u_{1}+u_{2})(u_{1}-u_{2})\big)\big\|_{C_{T}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim TN^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\big(\|u_{1}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}+\|u_{2}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\big)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\,.

For the high-frequency part, we recall that since
u_{1},u_{2}
are real-valued,

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\sim\|P_{>N}P_{+}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\,.

We write
u_{j}
in terms of
F_{j}
and
w_{j}
in the following way:

\displaystyle u_{j}

\displaystyle=e^{iF_{j}}e^{-iF_{j}}u_{j}=ie^{iF_{j}}\partial_{x}\big[P_{+\textup{hi}}(e^{-iF_{j}})+P_{\textup{lo}}(e^{-iF_{j}})+P_{-\textup{hi}}(e^{-iF_{j}})\big]

\displaystyle=ie^{iF_{j}}w_{j}+ie^{iF_{j}}P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{j}})+ie^{iF_{j}}P_{-\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{j}})\,.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}P_{+}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{H^{s}}

(2.26)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\big(e^{iF_{1}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\big)\big\|_{H^{s}}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\big((e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}})w_{2})\big)\big\|_{H^{s}}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\big(e^{iF_{1}}P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}})\big)\big\|_{H^{s}}

(2.29)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\big((e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}})P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{2}})\big)\big\|_{H^{s}}

(2.30)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\big(e^{iF_{1}}P_{-\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}})\big)\big\|_{H^{s}}

(2.31)

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\big((e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}})P_{-\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{2}})\big)\big\|_{H^{s}}\,.

Before estimating each term (
2.26
)-(
2.31
) one by one, notice that
by Lemma
2.2
,
for any
0\leq\sigma\leq s
we have

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{x}\big(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}}\big)\big\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|e^{-iF_{1}}(u_{1}-u_{2})\big\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\big\|\big(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}}\big)u_{2}\big\|_{H^{\sigma}}

(2.32)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\big)^{2}\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,.

By (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\eqref{u1-u2:t1}\lesssim\big\|J^{s}\big(e^{iF_{1}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{s}}.

For the second term, we split
w_{2}=P_{\leq\frac{N}{2}}w_{2}+P_{>\frac{N}{2}}w_{2}
and then we use
Berstein’s inequality, Plancherel’s identity,
(
2.2
) and (
2.32
) with
\sigma=0
:

(
2.27
)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}J^{s}\big(e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}}\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|P_{\leq\frac{N}{2}}w_{2}\big\|_{L^{2}}+\big\|\big(e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}}\big)P_{>\frac{N}{2}}w_{2}\big\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-(\frac{1}{2}-s)}\|\partial_{x}(e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}})\|_{L^{2}}\|w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\big)\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}w_{2}\big\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\big)\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\Big(N^{s-\frac{1}{2}}+\big\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}w_{2}\big\|_{H^{s}}\Big)\,.

For the next term we can insert for free
P_{>\frac{N}{2}}P_{+}
in the first factor, namely
we have

(
2.28
)

\displaystyle=\Big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\Big(P_{>\frac{N}{2}}P_{+}(e^{iF_{1}})\cdot P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}\big(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}}\big)\Big)\Big\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}P_{+}J^{s}(e^{iF_{1}})\big\|_{L^{2}}\big\|P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}})\big\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s-1}\big\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}P_{+}\partial_{x}(e^{iF_{1}})\big\|_{L^{2}}\big\|P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}})\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s-1}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\big(\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\big)\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,.

Similarly, we have

(
2.29
)

\displaystyle=\Big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\Big(P_{>\frac{N}{2}}P_{+}(e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}})\cdot P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{2}})\Big)\Big\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s-1}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\big(\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\big)\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)

and

(
2.30
)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|P_{>\frac{N}{2}}P_{+}J^{s}(e^{iF_{1}})\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|P_{-\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{1}}-e^{-iF_{2}})\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}+\big(\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\big)\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,,

where in the last step we have used (
2.32
) with
\sigma=s
.
Lastly, we argue similarly to estimating (
2.30
) by using (
2.32
) with
\sigma=0
and we obtain:

(
2.31
)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|P_{>N}P_{+}\Big(P_{>\frac{N}{2}}P_{+}J^{s}(e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}})\cdot P_{-\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}(e^{-iF_{2}})\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\partial_{x}(e^{iF_{1}}-e^{iF_{2}})\big\|_{L^{2}}\|e^{-iF_{2}}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\big)^{3}\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\big(\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\big)\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,.

Hence, (
2.25
) follows from the above estimates and Lemma
2.5
.
∎

3.
Normal form reductions

The goal of this section is to prove an estimate for the difference of two solutions

w_{1},w_{2}
to (
2.11
) in terms of the difference of the corresponding solutions
u_{1},u_{2}
to the original equation (
1.1
). We proceed by renormalizing the main nonlinear term of (
2.11
) which introduces new nonlinearities.
We prove multilinear estimates for these new terms in several lemmata below, which together imply the
following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
3-\sqrt{33/4}<s\leq\frac{1}{4}
,
T>0
, and
M>1
.
Assume that
u_{1},u_{2}
are two solutions to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
with the same initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}
.
Then, for the corresponding gauge transformations
w_{1},w_{2}
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\\
&\ \lesssim\big(TM^{\frac{3}{2}}+M^{-\frac{1}{16}}\big)\big(1+\|u_{1}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\big)^{10}\big(\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\big)\,.\end{split}

Recall that after the gauge transformation
u\mapsto w=\partial_{x}P_{+\text{hi}}(e^{-iF})
,
BO transforms into

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w-i\partial_{x}^{2}w

\displaystyle=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big]+E(e^{-iF},u)

(see (
2.11
)), where the second term, given by

E(e^{-iF},u)=-2P_{+\textup{hi}}\partial_{x}\big[(P_{\textup{lo}}e^{-iF})(P_{-}\partial_{x}u)\big]
,
is easy to handle via Lemma
2.6
.
For simplicity of writing we drop the functional arguments for this negligible term,
i.e. we set
E:=E(e^{-iF},u)
.
Here we use the following change of variables:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}(t)

\displaystyle:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}w(t)\,,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(t)

\displaystyle:=e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t)\,,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}E(t)\,.

Then
\widetilde{w}
satisfies pointwise in time the following equation in
H^{s}_{x}

(3.6)

\widetilde{w}(t)-\widetilde{w}(0)=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbf{N}^{(1)}}(\widetilde{w},\widetilde{u})(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\,,

where the nonlinearity is defined by

(3.7)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}\big(\mathbf{N}^{(1)}(\widetilde{w},\widetilde{u})\big)(t,\xi)&=-2i\int_{\xi_{12}=\xi}e^{it\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\frac{\xi\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\widehat{\widetilde{w}}(t,\xi_{1})\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t,\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}\\
&\qquad+\widehat{\widetilde{E}}(t,\xi)\,.\end{split}

In (
3.7
) above we have set

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle:=\omega(\xi)-\omega(\xi_{1})-\omega(\xi_{2})=\xi|\xi|-\xi_{1}|\xi_{1}|-\xi_{2}|\xi_{2}|\,,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle:=\chi_{+}(\xi)\widetilde{\chi}_{+}(\xi_{1})\mathbf{1}_{<0}(\xi_{2})\,,

where

(3.10)

\chi_{+}(\xi):=(1-\psi(\xi))\mathbf{1}_{>0}(\xi)

is the symbol of
P_{+\textup{hi}}
. Also, we inserted
\widetilde{\chi}_{+}(\xi_{1})
, where

\widetilde{\chi}_{+}
is a smooth function equal to
1
on the support of
\chi_{+}
and vanishing on a neighborhood of zero.
Since
\chi_{+}
and
\widetilde{\chi}_{+}
play the same role (they indicate positive frequencies away from zero) we make a slight abuse of notation and replace
\widetilde{\chi}_{+}
by
\chi_{+}

in every occurrence below.

Due to the sign restrictions on the frequencies
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
,
we have the following factorization on the convolution plane
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
:

(3.11)

\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=2\xi\xi_{2}\,.

We note that the phase (
3.11
) is signed, namely
\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})<0
.
Also, we have

(3.12)

\langle\xi\rangle\sim|\xi|=\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}<\xi_{1}=|\xi_{1}|\sim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle

and

(3.13)

|\xi_{2}|=-\xi_{2}=\xi_{1}-\xi<\xi_{1}\,.

We also rewrite here (
1.4
) on the Fourier side, namely we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t,\xi)-\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=i\xi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\xi_{12}=\xi}e^{it^{\prime}\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t^{\prime},\xi_{1})\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t^{\prime},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}dt^{\prime}\,,

with
\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
as in (
3.8
)
(there is no factorization since there is no additional information on the signs of the frequencies involved).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in C_{T}L_{x}^{2}
be a solution to (
1.1
), let
w
be the corresponding gauge transformation,
and let
\widetilde{u},\widetilde{w}
be given by (
3.4
), (
3.3
), respectively.
Then for fixed
\xi
, the functions

:t\mapsto\widehat{u}(t,\xi),\ \widehat{w}(t,\xi),\ \widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t,\xi),\ \widehat{\widetilde{w}}(t,\xi)

are continuously differentiable.

Proof.

We argue here for the last function, the others follow analogously.
We claim that

\displaystyle:t\mapsto\int_{\xi=\xi_{12}}e^{it\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\widehat{\widetilde{w}}(t,\xi_{1})\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}

is continuous, which combined to (
3.6
)-(
3.7
) proves that
:t\mapsto\widehat{\widetilde{w}}(t,\xi)
is continuously differentiable.
Indeed,
since
:t\mapsto w(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}
and
:t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}
are continuous,
the claim follows from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the estimate

(3.15)

\int_{\xi=\xi_{12}}\frac{|\xi_{2}|}{\xi_{1}}\sigma(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\widehat{\widetilde{w}}(t,\xi_{1})||\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t,\xi_{2})|d\xi_{1}\lesssim\|w(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\,.

∎

3.1.
First step

Let us consider the main term in (
3.6
). We denote by
{\mathcal{N}^{(1)}}
the bilinear operator given by:

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big({\mathcal{N}^{(1)}}(\widetilde{w},\widetilde{u})\big)(t,\xi)=-2i\int_{\xi=\xi_{12}}e^{it\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\frac{\xi\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\chi_{+}(\xi)\chi_{+}(\xi_{1})\mathbf{1}_{<0}(\xi_{2})\widehat{\widetilde{w}}(t,\xi_{1})\widehat{\widetilde{u}}(t,\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}\,.

Note that the difference between

-conservation law. For this reason, we perform another gauge transformation
:w\mapsto e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}m(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}w
, so that the equation for the gauge variable becomes

(5.3)

\partial_{t}w-i\partial_{x}^{2}w=-2P_{+}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big]\,,

and, by setting
E[f,g]:=-2P_{+}P_{\textup{lo}}\partial_{x}[f\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}g]
, we rewrite (
5.3
) precisely as (
3.2
).
It is easy to check that the estimate corresponding to (
2.12
) also holds in this case.

5.2.
The Strichartz estimates

Lemma 5.1

(refined Strichartz estimates on the torus)
.

Let
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{4}
,
N\in 2^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
,
T>0
, and
2\leq p\leq 4
. Let
u
be a solution to (
2.14
) with
F=\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2})
. Then, we have

(5.4)

\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\beta(s,p)}\Big(\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\Big)\,,

where

(5.5)

\beta(s,p):=\big(\frac{3}{2}-s\big)\big(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p}\big)-s\,.

Proof.

Following the proof of
[
48
, Lemma 2.1]
, we use the
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimate due to Zygmund
[
64
]
to deduce

\big\|e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}f\big\|_{L^{4}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{8}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\,.

After interpolating with the trivial estimate

\big\|e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}f\big\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\,,

this implies

(5.6)

\big\|e^{t\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}})}\lesssim T^{\frac{3}{2p}-\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\,,

for any
2\leq p\leq 4
.

The proof then follows similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.8
. With
\delta>0
to be chosen later,
let
I_{j}=:[a_{j},b_{j}]
be such that
\bigcup_{j}I_{j}=[0,T]
,
b_{j}-a_{j}\sim N^{-\delta}
, and
the number of such intervals is
\sim TN^{\delta}
.
We then deduce from (
5.6
)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{x}^{p}}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim TN^{\delta(1-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{p}{4})}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}+\sum_{j}|I_{j}|^{p-1}|I_{j}|^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{p}{4}}\|P_{N}F\|_{L_{I_{j}}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}^{p},

so that

\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{(-\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1}{4})\delta}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{-\big(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2p}\big)\delta}\|P_{N}F\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\,.

In particular, for

F=\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2})\,,

we get

\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{(-\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1}{4})\delta-s}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{1-\big(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2p}\big)\delta}\|P_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\,.

Together with

\|P_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq N^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2r}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2r}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{2}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}\,,

where
1\leq r\leq 2
is determined by
s=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2r}
, or equivalently
r=\frac{1}{1-2s}
,
we obtain

\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{p}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{(-\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1}{4})\delta-s}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}+T^{\frac{1}{p}}N^{\frac{3}{2}-\big(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2p}\big)\delta-2s}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}

(the restriction on
r
imposes
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{4}
).
We choose
\delta
such that

(-\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1}{4})\delta-s=\frac{3}{2}-\big(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2p}\big)\delta-2s,

or equivalently
\delta=\frac{3}{2}-s
,
and with

\beta(s,p)
as in (
5.5
),
we obtain
(
5.4
).
∎

5.3.
Nonlinear estimates and proofs of the main results

We note that the normal form transformations as well as the nonlinear estimates
from Section
3
carry over exactly as in the real-line case.
The proof of the estimates of Lemma
2.11
is similar,
but now using the refined Strichartz estimate (
5.4
) instead of (
2.17
).
For the convenience of the reader we check the numerology of Corollary
3.11
and verify that the same regularity condition is necessary.
Indeed, we must ensure

\displaystyle\beta(s,p)<0\text{ and }\delta+\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{2}<s

(to be compared with (
3.49
) and (
3.50
) in the real-line case), or equivalently

(\frac{3}{2}-s)(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p})-s<0\text{ and }\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p}>\frac{1}{8}-\frac{s}{4}

which hold true for some
p\in(2,4)
under the same condition (
3.51
).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is then carried out by reasoning on equation (
5.3
) and by arguing exactly as on the real line case.

Finally, we comment on the proof of Corollary
1.2
in the periodic case. With Theorem
1.1
in hand, one can assume that any solution at this level of regularity is constructed through a (conditional) well-posedness result. In particular, by invoking the
L^{2}
-conservation law, we can then rigorously justify that
m(t)=m_{0}:=P_{0}(u_{0}^{2})
, so that the equation for the gauge variable (
5.2
) becomes

(5.7)

\partial_{t}w-i\partial_{x}^{2}w-im_{0}w=-2P_{+}\partial_{x}\big[\partial_{x}^{-1}w\cdot P_{-}\partial_{x}u\big]\,.

Since the
L^{p}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}})
-norms are invariant under
the transformation
:w\mapsto e^{im_{0}t}w
,
one also has the same Strichartz estimate for
P_{N}w
as in Lemma
2.16
(ii). Therefore, we conclude the proof of Corollary
1.2
in the periodic case by arguing exactly as in the continuous case.

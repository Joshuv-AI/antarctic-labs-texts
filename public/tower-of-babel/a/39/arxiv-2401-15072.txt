Einstein submanifolds with parallel mean curvature into 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
Estela Garcia, Fernando Manfio

Abstract
We prove that Einstein submanifolds in 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} with flat normal bundle and parallel mean curvature are warped product of isometric immersions.



Key words:

Einstein submanifolds,
Parallel mean curvature, Flat normal bundle.

1
Introduction

One of the basic problems in submanifold theory is to provide
conditions that imply that an isometric immersion of a product
manifold must be a extrinsic product of isometric immersions.
The first contribution to this problem was addressed by Moore

[
12
]
for isometric immersions into Euclidean space and
extended by Molzan
[
11
]
for any space form as ambient
space. They showed that an isometric immersion of a
Riemannian product into a space form must be an extrinsic
product of isometric immersions of the factors whenever its
second fundamental form is adapted to the product structure
of the manifold.

In this paper we consider isometric immersions of Einstein
manifolds into the product space
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, with flat normal bundle
and parallel mean curvature vector field. Recall that a
Riemannian manifold
M
is an
Einstein manifold
if
the Ricci tensor satisfies

\mathrm{Ric}(X,Y)=\lambda\langle X,Y\rangle,

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and some constante
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Riemannian manifolds
M^{n}_{c}
with constant sectional
curvature
c
are the simplest examples of Einstein
Manifolds, where
\lambda=(n-1)c
.

An isometric immersion is said to be Einstein if the submanifold,
endowed with the induced metric, is an Einstein manifold. For
example, Ryan
[
16
]
gave a local classification of Einstein
hypersurfaces in any space form. In higher codimension,
Di Scala
[
4
]
proved that Einstein real Kahler submanifolds
of a Euclidean space are totally geodesic provided that they
are minimal, and same conclusion, by Nolker
[
14
]
, still
holds for minimal Einstein submanifolds in the Euclidean space,
with flat normal bundle.

In
[
15
]
, Onti classified Einstein submanifolds into space forms,
with flat normal bundle and parallel mean curvature vector field,
extending a previous result due to Dajczer-Tojeiro
[
2
]

for isometric immersions of Riemannian manifolds with constant
sectional curvature. More recently, Leandro-Pina-dos Santos

[
6
]
classified Einstein hypersurfaces into product space

\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
.

In the present paper, we classify Einstein submanifolds

f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
m\geq 3
, with flat normal bundle and parallel
mean curvature vector field. As a first step, we prove that such
submanifolds have the property that the tangent component of
the second factor of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvector of all shape operators
of
f
. They belong to a more general class, named
class

\mathcal{A}
, introduced by Tojeiro
[
17
]
for the case of hypersurfaces
and extended to higher codimension by Mendonça-Tojeiro

[
10
]
. Some important classes of submanifolds of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}

that are included in class
\mathcal{A}
are hypersurfaces with constant
sectional curvature
[
8
]
, rotational hypersurfaces

[
3
]
, constant angle hypersurfaces
[
17
]
and
biconservative submanifolds
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
,
m\geq 3
, be an isometric immersion of a
connected Einstein manifold, with Ricci curvature
\lambda
and
flat normal bundle. Then
f
belongs to class
\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem
1.1
extends Lemma 5 of
[
6
]
for
the case of hypersurfaces. Our main result, which extends the
aforementioned result of Onti
[
15
]
is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
m\geq 3
, be a proper isometric immersion
of a connected Einstein manifold with Ricci curvature
\lambda
,
flat normal bundle, and parallel mean curvature vector field
H
.
Then
f
is a warped product of isometric immersions.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we just recall
the notion of extrinsic warped product of isometric immersions. In
Section
3
we recall the basic equations of an
isometric immersion into
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
. In Section
4
we study
submanifolds with flat normal bundle. In particular, we obtain an
equivalent condition for a submanifold, with such properties, to
belong to class
\mathcal{A}
. In Section
5
we calculate the
Ricci tensor of a submanifold in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, and prove that every
Einstein submanifold with flat normal bundle belongs to class

\mathcal{A}
. Finally, in the last section we prove a sequence of
lemmas and Theorem
1.2
.

.

suggestions which improved some results in this paper.

2
Preliminaries

A metric
g
on a product manifold
M^{m}=M_{0}\times M_{1}\times\ldots\times M_{k}
is called a
warped product
if there exist
positive functions
\rho_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M_{0})
,
1\leq i\leq k
, such
that

g=\pi_{0}^{\ast}g_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\rho_{i}\circ\pi_{i})^{2}\pi_{i}^{\ast}g_{i},

where
\pi_{i}\colon M^{m}\to M_{i}
denotes the canonical projection. We
call
M^{m}
endowed with this metric the
warped product
of

M_{0},\ldots,M_{k}
with warping functions
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}

and denote it by

M^{m}=M_{0}\times_{\rho_{1}}M_{1}\times\ldots\times_{\rho_{k}}M_{k}.

Let
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
denote a complete and simply connected space form
of constant sectional curvature
\epsilon
. Fix a point
q\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
and
let
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n_{0}},\mathbb{Q}^{n_{1}}_{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{Q}^{n_{k}}_{\epsilon_{k}}
, with

n=n_{0}+n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}
, be submanifolds of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
through

q
such that the first one is totally geodesic and all the others
are totally umbilical with mean curvature vectors
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}

at
q
and
\langle z_{i},z_{j}\rangle=-\epsilon
, for
i\neq j
. The
warped
product representation

\psi\colon\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n_{0}}\times_{\sigma_{1}}\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times_{\sigma_{k}}\mathbb{Q}^{n_{k}}_{\epsilon_{k}}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}

(2.1)

of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
is the map

\psi(p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})=p_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}(p_{0})(p_{i}-q),

where the functions
\sigma_{i}\colon\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
are
defined by

\sigma_{i}(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}1+\langle p-q,a_{i}\rangle,&\mbox{if}&\epsilon=0\\
\langle p,a_{i}\rangle,&\mbox{if}&\epsilon\neq 0\end{array},\right.

and satisfy
\sigma_{i}(q)=1
, with
a_{i}=cq-z_{i}
.

It was shown by Nolker
[
13
]
that any isometry of a warped
product with
k+1
factors onto an open dense subset of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}

arises as the restriction of a warped product representation as
above. Given a warped product representation as in (
2.1
)
and isometric immersions
f_{i}\colon M_{i}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
, with

0\leq i\leq k
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon
, the map

f=\psi\circ(f_{0}\times f_{1}\times\ldots\times f_{k})

is an isometric immersion of the warped product manifold
M^{m}

with warping functions
\rho_{i}=\sigma_{i}\circ f_{0}
, called an

extrinsic warped product
of isometric immersions. If all
factors
f_{i}
of the extrinsic product are identity maps, the the
map
f
is called the
multi-rotational submanifold

determined by
\psi
with
f_{0}
as profile.

3
Basic facts on submanifolds in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}

From now on, let
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}
denote either
\mathbb{S}^{n}
or
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
according to whether
\epsilon=1
or
\epsilon=-1
, respectively. Given an
isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, let
\partial_{t}
denote a unit
vector field tangent to the second
factor. Then, a tangent vector field
T
on
M^{m}

and a normal vector field
\eta
along
f
are defined by

\partial_{t}=f_{\ast}T+\eta.

(3.1)

Using that
\partial_{t}
is a parallel vector field in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, we obtain by
differentiating equation (
3.1
) that

\nabla_{X}T=A_{\eta}X

(3.2)

and

\alpha_{f}(X,T)=-\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\eta,

(3.3)

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Here and in the sequel
A_{\eta}
stands for the shape
operator of
f
in the direction
\eta
, given by

\langle A_{\eta}X,Y\rangle=\langle\alpha_{f}(X,Y),\eta\rangle,

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
The Gauss, Codazzi and Ricci equations for
f
are, respectively,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} R(X,Y)Z&=A_{\alpha_{f}(Y,Z)}X-A_{\alpha_{f}(X,Z)}Y+\epsilon\big(X\wedge Y\\
&+\langle X,T\rangle Y\wedge T-\langle Y,T\rangle X\wedge T\big)Z,\end{aligned}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\alpha_{f}\right)(Y,Z)-\left(\nabla^{\perp}_{Y}\alpha_{f}\right)(X,Z)=\epsilon\langle(X\wedge Y)T,Z\rangle\eta

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi=\alpha_{f}(X,A_{\xi}Y)-\alpha_{f}(A_{\xi}X,Y),

(3.6)

for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
an for all
\xi\in\Gamma(TM^{\perp})
. Equation
(
3.5
) can also be written as

(\nabla_{X}A)(Y,\xi)-(\nabla_{Y}A)(X,\xi)=\epsilon\langle\eta,\xi\rangle(X\wedge Y)T,

(3.7)

where

(X\wedge Y)T=\langle Y,T\rangle X-\langle X,T\rangle Y,

whereas equation (
3.6
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\langle R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi,\zeta\rangle=\langle[A_{\xi},A_{\zeta}]X,Y\rangle.

(3.8)

Although this will not be used in this work, it is worth mentioning that
equations (
3.2
)–(
3.6
) completely determine an
isometric immersion of a Riemannian manifold
M^{m}
into
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
up
to isometries of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
(see
[
7
, Corollary 3]
).

Given an isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, consider
M^{m}

as a submanifold in the underlying flat space
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
, that is,
consider the isometric immersion
\tilde{f}=i\circ f
, where

i:\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
denotes de canonical inclusion. The second
fundamental forms and normal connections of
f
and
\tilde{f}
are
related as follows. Let
\bar{N}=\pi\circ i
the unit normal vector
field to the inclusion
i
, where
\pi\colon\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{E}^{n+1}

is the projection. We have

\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}\bar{N}=\pi_{\ast}i_{\ast}Z=i_{\ast}(Z-\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}),

for every
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R})
, where
\widetilde{\nabla}
is the canonical connection
on
\mathbb{E}^{n+2}
. Hence,

A_{\bar{N}}^{i}Z=-Z+\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}.

(3.9)

The normal spaces of
f
and
\tilde{f}
are related by

TM^{\perp}_{\tilde{f}}=i_{\ast}TM^{\perp}_{f}\oplus\text{span}\{N\},

where
N=\bar{N}\circ f=\pi\circ\tilde{f}
. Denoting by
\overline{\nabla}
the Levi-Civita
connection of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, and given
\xi\in TM^{\perp}_{f}
, it follows from
(
3.9
) that

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}i_{\ast}\xi=-\tilde{f}_{\ast}A^{f}_{\xi}X+i_{\ast}\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi+\epsilon\langle X,T\rangle\langle\xi,\eta\rangle N.

In particular, we have

\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}_{X}i_{\ast}\xi=i_{\ast}\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi+\epsilon\langle X,T\rangle\langle\xi,\eta\rangle N,

(3.10)

for every
\xi\in TM^{\perp}_{f}
, where
\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}
is the normal
connection of
\tilde{f}
. Moreover,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N=\tilde{f}_{\ast}(X-\langle X,T\rangle T)-\langle X,T\rangle i_{\ast}\eta

which implies, in particular, that

\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}_{X}N=-\langle X,T\rangle i_{\ast}\eta.

(3.11)

4
Submanifolds with flat normal bundle in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}

An important class of submanifolds in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
consists of those which
have flat normal bundle. As in the case of submanifolds in a space
with constant sectional curvature, flatness of the normal bundle of a
submanifold into
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
has the following useful characterization.

Proposition 4.1
.

An isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
has flat normal bundle at
a point
x\in M^{m}
if and only if the shape operators

\{A_{\xi}:\xi\in T_{x}M^{\perp}\}

are simultaneously diagonalizable. Equivalently, if and only if there exists
an orthonormal basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
of
T_{x}M
such that

\alpha_{f}(X_{i},X_{j})=0,\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq m.

Proof.

By the Ricci equation (
3.8
), the normal curvature tensor

R^{\perp}
vanishes at
x\in M^{m}
if and only if all shape operators

A_{\xi}
,
\xi\in T_{x}M^{\perp}
, commute, and the conclusion follows.
∎

It follows from Proposition
4.1
that, at each
x\in M

where
R^{\perp}(x)=0
, the tangent space
T_{x}M
decomposes
orthogonally as

T_{x}M=E_{1}(x)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{s(x)}(x).

(4.1)

This decomposition has the property that for each
\xi\in T_{x}M^{\perp}
,
there exist real numbers
\lambda_{i}(\xi)
,
1\leq i\leq s=s(x)
, such
that

A_{\xi}|_{E_{i}(x)}=\lambda_{i}(\xi)\text{Id}

and the maps
\xi\mapsto\lambda_{i}(\xi)
are pairwise distinct. Since
such maps are linear, there exist unique pairwise distinct vectors

\xi_{i}(x)\in T_{x}M^{\perp}
,
1\leq i\leq s
, called the
principal
normals
of
f
at
x
, such that

\lambda_{i}(\xi)=\langle\xi_{i}(x),\xi\rangle,\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

Therefore, denoting by
E_{i}(x)=E_{\xi_{i}(x)}
, we have

E_{\xi_{i}(x)}=\{X\in T_{x}M:\alpha_{f}(X,Y)=\langle X,Y\rangle\xi_{i}(x),\ \forall\ Y\in T_{x}M\},

and the second fundamental form of
f
has the simple representation

\alpha(X,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\langle X^{i},Y^{i}\rangle\xi_{i}(x),

(4.2)

where
X\mapsto X^{i}
is the orthogonal projection onto
E_{i}(x)
.
Equivalently,

A_{\xi}X=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\langle\xi,\xi_{i}(x)\rangle X^{i}

(4.3)

for all
X\in T_{x}M
and
\xi\in T_{x}M^{\perp}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Throughout this paper a submanifold with flat normal bundle will
always be considered
proper
, that is, it has a constant number

s=s(x)
of principal normals vector fields. In this case, the principal
normal vector fields
x\in M^{m}\mapsto\xi_{i}(x)
,
1\leq i\leq s
, are
smooth. Moreover, the distributions
x\in M^{m}\mapsto E_{i}(x)
,

1\leq i\leq s
, have constant dimension and are also smooth.

A direct calculation shows the following

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
be an isometric immersion with flat normal
bundle. Then, the Codazzi equation (
3.5
) can be written
as

\langle X_{j},Y_{j}\rangle(\nabla^{\perp}_{X_{i}}\xi_{j}+\epsilon\langle X_{i},T\rangle\eta)=\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}Y_{j},X_{i}\rangle(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}),

(4.4)

for
1\leq i\neq j\leq s
,
X_{i}\in\Gamma(E_{i})
, and

X_{j},Y_{j}\in\Gamma(E_{j})
, or

\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{i},X_{k}\rangle(\xi_{i}-\xi_{k})=\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{j},X_{k}\rangle(\xi_{j}-\xi_{k}),

(4.5)

for
1\leq i\neq j\neq k\leq s
,
X_{i}\in\Gamma(E_{i})
,

X_{j}\in\Gamma(E_{j})
, and
X_{k}\in\Gamma(E_{k})
.

Following the notations of
[
10
]
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of
isometric immersions
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
with the property that
T

is an eigenvector of all shape operators of
f
. Our next result shows
a relationship between isometric immersions into
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
which have
flat normal bundle and those that are in class
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
be an isometric immersion with flat normal
bundle. Then,
f
is in class
\mathcal{A}
if and only if the tangent vector
field
T
belongs to some
E_{i}
in the decomposition
(
4.1
).

Proof.

If
f
belongs to class
\mathcal{A}
, then
A_{\xi}T=\lambda T
for every
normal vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(TM^{\perp})
, where
\lambda=\lambda(\xi)

is a smooth function along
M
. On the other hand, it follows from
(
4.3
) that

A_{\xi}T=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\rangle T^{i}.

(4.6)

Taking the inner product with
T^{i}\neq 0
of both sides of (
4.6
)
yields

\lambda\|T^{i}\|^{2}=\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\rangle\|T^{i}\|^{2}.

(4.7)

Suppose that there exists another index
j\neq i
such that
T^{j}\neq 0
.
It follows from (
4.7
) that
\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\rangle=\langle\xi,\xi_{j}\rangle
. In
particular, taking
\xi=\xi_{i}
and then
\xi=\xi_{j}
, we obtain

\|\xi_{i}\|^{2}=\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\rangle=\|\xi_{j}\|^{2}
. This implies that
\xi_{i}=\xi_{j}
,
which is a contradiction. Conversely, suppose that
T\in E_{i}
, for
some
1\leq i\leq s
. Thus

\alpha(T,X)=\langle T,X\rangle\xi_{i},

for every
X\in TM
. Therefore, given
\xi\in\Gamma(TM^{\perp})
, we have

A_{\xi}T=\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\rangle T
, and this shows that
T
is an eigenvector
of
A_{\xi}
, for any
\xi\in\Gamma(TM^{\perp})
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
4.4
, we can
suppose, without loss of generality, that
T\in E_{1}
.

5
A basic result

Given an isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
, we recall that the

Ricci tensor
of
M^{m}
is defined by

\mathrm{Ric}(X,Y)=tr\{Z\mapsto R(Z,X)Y\}

for every
X,Y\in TM
. In the next result we compute the Ricci
tensor of
M^{m}
in terms of the second fundamental form of
f
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The Ricci tensor of an isometric imersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
is given
by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Ric}(X,Y)=&\ \epsilon(m-1-\|T\|^{2})\langle X,Y\rangle+\epsilon(2-m)\langle X,T\rangle\langle Y,T\rangle\\
&+m\langle H,\alpha(X,Y)\rangle-III(X,Y),\end{aligned}

(5.1)

where

III(X,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\alpha(X,X_{i}),\alpha(Y,X_{i})\rangle

denotes the third fundamental form of
f
in terms of an orthonormal
tangent frame
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
of
M^{m}
.

Proof.

Given an orthonormal tangent frame
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
of
M^{m}
,
the Gauss equation of
f
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle R(X_{i},X)Y,X_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big[\langle\epsilon(X_{i}\wedge X)Y-\epsilon\langle X,T\rangle(X_{i}\wedge T)Y+\epsilon\langle X_{i},T\rangle(X\wedge T)Y

\displaystyle+\langle\alpha(X_{i},X_{i}),\alpha(X,Y)\rangle-\langle\alpha(X,X_{i}),\alpha(Y,X_{i})\rangle\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(m-1)\langle X,Y\rangle-\epsilon\langle X,T\rangle\big(m\langle Y,T\rangle-\langle Y,T\rangle\big)+\epsilon\langle Y,T\rangle\langle X,T\rangle

\displaystyle-\epsilon\langle X,Y\rangle\|T\|^{2}+m\langle H,\alpha(X,Y)\rangle-III(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(m-1-\|T\|^{2}\big)\langle X,Y\rangle+\epsilon(2-m)\langle X,T\rangle\langle Y,T\rangle

\displaystyle+m\langle H,\alpha(X,Y)\rangle-III(X,Y),

and the proof of (
5.1
) is completed.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The formula for the Ricci tensor in (
5.1
) extends
the one obtained in
[
6
]
for the case of hypersurfaces.

The
Ricci curvature
in the direction of a unit vector
X\in TM

is defined as

\mathrm{Ric}(X)=\frac{1}{m-1}\mathrm{Ric}(X,X).

It follows from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Ric}(X)=&\ \epsilon-\frac{\epsilon}{m-1}\big(\|T\|^{2}-\langle T,X\rangle^{2}\big)-\epsilon\langle T,X\rangle^{2}\\
&+\frac{m}{m-1}\langle H,\alpha(X,X)\rangle-\frac{1}{m-1}III(X,X),\end{aligned}

(5.2)

for any unit vector
X\in TM
. Equation (
5.2
) yields
the following obstruction for the existence of a minimal submanifold
into any product space
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
be a minimal isometric immersion. Then for any
point
x\in M
, we have
\mathrm{Ric}(X)\leq\epsilon
for every unit vector
X\in T_{x}M
.
Moreover, the equality holds identically if and only if
f(M)
is an open
subset of a slice
N^{m}\times\{t_{0}\}
, where
N^{m}
is a submanifold of

\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since
f
is minimal, we have

\mathrm{Ric}(X)=\epsilon-\frac{\epsilon}{m-1}\big(\|T\|^{2}-\langle T,X\rangle^{2}\big)-\epsilon\langle T,X\rangle^{2}-\frac{1}{m-1}III(X,X),

and this proves the first statement, since
\|T\|^{2}-\langle X,T\rangle^{2}\geq 0
.
The equality holds identically if and only if
f
is totally geodesic and

T=0
, that is,
f(M)
is an open subset of a slice of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}_{c}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
be a minimal isometric immersion.
Then:

(a)

c\leq\epsilon
,

(b)

c=\epsilon
if and only if
f(M)
is an open subset of a slice
in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
.

The next result provides a characterization of Einstein submanifolds
with flat normal bundle, extending the one of
[
6
]
for the
case of hypersurfaces.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
be an Einstein submanifold,
m\geq 3
, with flat
normal bundle at a point
x\in M
. If
T\neq 0
at
x\in M
, then
T

is an eigenvector of all shape operators of
f
at
x
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, there exists an orthonormal basis

\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
of
T_{x}M
such that

\alpha_{f}(X_{i},X_{j})=0,\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq m.

Write
T=\sum_{k=1}^{m}t_{k}X_{k}
at the point
x
. On the other hand,
since
M
is an Einstein manifold one has

\mathrm{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=\rho\delta_{ij},

(5.3)

for some
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
. That is,

\mathrm{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\langle R(X_{k},X_{i})X_{j},X_{k}\rangle=\rho\delta_{ij}.

When applying the Ricci tensor to the referencial
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=&\ \big(\epsilon(m-1-\|T\|^{2}+m\lambda_{i}(H)\big)\delta_{ij}\\
&+\epsilon(2-m)t_{i}t_{j}-III(X_{i},X_{j}).\end{aligned}

(5.4)

It follows from (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) that

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon(m-1-\|T\|^{2}+m\lambda_{i}(H)-\rho\big)\delta_{ij}+\epsilon(2-m)t_{i}t_{j}-III(X_{i},X_{j})=0.

(5.5)

Note that the third fundamental form of
f
, expressed in terms of
the basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
, is always equal to zero, for
i\neq j
.
Thus, it follows from (
5.5
) that

t_{i}t_{j}=0,\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq m.

Since
T(x)\neq 0
, there is only one index
1\leq k\leq m
, with

t_{k}\neq 0
, and this implies that
T=t_{k}X_{k}
at
x\in M
.
∎

6
The proof of the main result

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
,
m\geq 3
, be an isometric immersion of a
connected Einstein manifold with Ricci curvature
\lambda
, flat normal
bundle, and parallel mean curvature vector field
H
. In order to prove
Theorem
1.2
, we will show a sequence of auxiliary results.

Lemma 6.1
.

If the tangent vector field
T
is nowhere vanishing, then

\left\|\xi_{k}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\|^{2}=\frac{m^{2}}{4}\left\|H\right\|^{2}-\lambda+(m-2)\epsilon+\|\eta\|^{2}\epsilon,

(6.1)

for any
2\leq k\leq s
, and for
k=1
if
\dim E_{1}>1
.

Proof.

Let
X_{1}=T/\|T\|
, and consider a unit vector field

X_{2}\in\Gamma(E_{1})\cap\{T\}^{\perp}
. Complete to an orthonormal frame

\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}\}
of
M
, where each
X_{i}
belongs to some

\Gamma(E_{i})
. It follows from Gauss equation (
3.4
), for
k=1
,
that

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(X_{2},X_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle R(X_{i},X_{2})X_{2},X_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\|\eta\|^{2}+\|\xi_{1}\|^{2}(\dim(E_{1})-1)+(m-2)\epsilon+\sum_{i=2}^{s}\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{2}\rangle\dim(E_{i}),

that is,

\|\xi_{1}\|^{2}\dim(E_{1})+\sum_{i=2}^{s}\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{1}\rangle\dim(E_{i})=\lambda-(m-2)\epsilon+\|\xi_{1}\|^{2}-\|\eta\|^{2}\epsilon.

On the other hand, we can write

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} m\langle\xi_{1},H\rangle&=|\xi_{1}\|^{2}\dim(E_{1})+\sum_{i=2}^{s}\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{1}\rangle\dim(E_{i})\\
&=\lambda-(m-2)\epsilon-\|\eta\|^{2}\epsilon+\|\xi_{1}\|^{2}.\end{aligned}

(6.2)

Therefore, using (
6.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\xi_{1}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi_{1}-\frac{m}{2}H,\xi_{1}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\xi_{1}\|^{2}-m\langle\xi_{1},H\rangle+\frac{m^{2}}{4}\|H\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}}{4}\|H\|^{2}-\lambda+(m-2)\epsilon+\|\eta\|^{2}\epsilon.

The case
k\geq 2
is analogous to the previous case. In fact, it
follows from Gauss equation (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{k}\rangle\dim(E_{1})+\sum_{i=2}^{s}\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{k}\rangle\dim(E_{i})=\lambda-(m-2)\epsilon-\|\eta\|^{2}\epsilon+\|\xi_{k}\|^{2}.

The equation (
6.1
) follows by writing

m\langle\xi_{k},H\rangle=\lambda-(m-2)\epsilon-\|\eta\|^{2}\epsilon+\|\xi_{k}\|^{2},

(6.3)

as in the previous case.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

It follows from Lemma
6.1
that the functions

h_{k}=\left\|\xi_{k}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\|^{2}

are constant along
\{T\}^{\perp}
, for any
2\leq k\leq s
, and for
k=1

if
\dim E_{1}>1
.

Lemma 6.3
.

The principal normals
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s}
of
f
are parallel along

\{T\}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Given
X,Y\in\Gamma(E_{i})
and
Z\in\Gamma(E_{k})\cap\{T\}^{\perp}
, with
2\leq i\leq s

and
1\leq k\neq i\leq s
, it follows from Codazzi equation (
4.4
)
that

\langle X,Y\rangle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{i}=\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle(\xi_{i}-\xi_{k}).

(6.4)

Since
H
is parallel, we have

\langle X,Y\rangle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\left(\xi_{i}-\frac{m}{2}H\right)=\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle(\xi_{i}-\xi_{k}).

This implies that

\langle X,Y\rangle Z\left(\left\|\xi_{i}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\|^{2}\right)=2\left\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\right\rangle\left\langle\xi_{i}-\xi_{k},\xi_{i}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\rangle

(6.5)

By Remark
6.2
, the left-hand side of (
6.5
)
is zero. Moreover, from (
6.1
), and using (
6.2
)
and (
6.3
), one has

\left\langle\xi_{i}-\xi_{k},\xi_{i}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\|\xi_{i}-\xi_{k}\|^{2}\neq 0.

This implies that
\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle=0
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(E_{i})
, with

2\leq i\leq s
. Thus, from (
6.4
), one has

\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{i}=0,

for all
Z\in\Gamma(E_{k})\cap\{T\}^{\perp}
, with
2\leq k\neq i\leq s
.
For
Z\in\Gamma(E_{i})
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}H=\sum_{i\neq k=1}^{s}\dim(E_{k})\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{k}+\dim(E_{i})\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim(E_{i})\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{i}.

This shows that
\xi_{i}
is parallel along
\{T\}^{\perp}
, for
2\leq i\leq s
.
For the case
i=1
, it follows from Codazzi equation (
4.4
)
that

\|T\|^{2}\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{1}=\langle\nabla_{T}T,Z\rangle(\xi_{1}-\xi_{k})=0,

for every
Z\in\Gamma(E_{k})
, with
2\leq k\leq s
, that is,

\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{1}=0
, for every
Z\in\Gamma(E_{k})
.
Finally, if there exists
X\in\Gamma(E_{1})\cap\{T\}^{\perp}
, then

\dim(E_{1})\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi_{1}=\nabla^{\perp}_{X}H-\sum_{k=2}^{s}\dim(E_{k})\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi_{k}=0,

by the previous case. This shows that
\xi_{i}
is parallel along
\{T\}^{\perp}
.
∎

Given an isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}
as it has been fixed,
consider the composition
\tilde{f}=i\circ f
. In this case, it follows from

[
10
, Corollary 1.3]
that
\tilde{f}
also has flat normal bundle.
In order to relate the flatness of the normal bundle of
f
and
\tilde{f}
,
let
\bar{N}=\pi\circ i
the unit normal vector field to the inclusion
i
,
and consider the decomposition (
4.1
) for

f
with principal normal vector fields
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s}
, where

E_{i}(x)=\{X\in T_{x}M:\alpha_{f}(X,Y)=\langle X,Y\rangle\xi_{i}(x),\ \forall\ Y\in T_{x}M\}.

Lemma 6.4
.

The following assertions hold:

(a)

If
\dim E_{1}=1
, then (
4.1
) is also a
decomposition for
\tilde{f}
, with principal normal vector fields
given by

i_{\ast}\xi_{1}-\|\eta\|^{2}\bar{N},i_{\ast}\xi_{2}-\bar{N},\dots,i_{\ast}\xi_{s}-\bar{N}.

(b)

If
\dim E_{1}\geq 2
, then

T_{x}M=\text{span}\{T(x)\}\oplus\big(E_{1}(x)\cap\text{span}\{T(x)\}^{\perp}\big)\oplus E_{2}(x)\oplus\ldots\oplus E_{s}(x),

is a decomposition for
\tilde{f}
, whose principal normals are given
by

i_{\ast}\xi_{1}-\|\eta\|^{2}\bar{N},i_{\ast}\xi_{1}-\bar{N},i_{\ast}\xi_{2}-\bar{N},\dots,i_{\ast}\xi_{s}-\bar{N}.

Proof.

Take any vector
X\in E_{k}(x)
, with
1\leq k\leq s
and
X\in\{T(x)\}^{\perp}
.
Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{\tilde{f}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{\ast}\alpha_{f}(X,Y)+\alpha_{i}(f_{\ast}X,f_{\ast}Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{\ast}\langle X,Y\rangle\xi_{k}-\langle X,Y\rangle\bar{N}+\langle X,T\rangle\langle Y,T\rangle\bar{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle X,Y\rangle(i_{\ast}\xi_{k}-\bar{N}),

for all
Y\in TM
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\tilde{f}}(T,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{\ast}\alpha_{f}(T,Y)+\alpha_{i}(f_{\ast}T,f_{\ast}Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{\ast}\langle T,Y\rangle\xi_{1}-\langle T,Y\rangle\bar{N}+\langle T,T\rangle\langle T,Y\rangle\bar{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle T,Y\rangle(i_{\ast}\xi_{1}-\|\eta\|^{2}\bar{N}),

for all
Y\in TM
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

From now on we write
E_{0}=\text{span}\{T\}
and, with abuse of notation,
we write
E_{1}=E_{1}\cap E_{0}^{\perp}
. Thus, at each point
x\in M
,
we have the decomposition for
\tilde{f}
given by

T_{x}M=E_{0}(x)\oplus E_{1}(x)\oplus\ldots\oplus E_{s}(x),

(6.6)

whose principal normals are given by

i_{\ast}\xi_{1}-\|\eta\|^{2}\bar{N},i_{\ast}\xi_{1}-\bar{N},\dots,i_{\ast}\xi_{s}-\bar{N},

respectively,
E_{1}=\{0\}
may occur.

Lemma 6.5
.

The distributions
\{E_{k}\}
of the decomposition (
6.6
)
are such that
E_{k}
is spherical and
E_{k}^{\perp}
is totally geodesic,
for all
1\leq k\leq s
.

Proof.

Given
X,Y\in\Gamma(E_{k})
,
1\leq k\leq s
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}Y,T\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle Y,\nabla_{X}T\rangle=-\langle T,A_{\eta}X\rangle=-\langle Y,A_{i_{\ast}X}^{\tilde{f}}X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\alpha_{\tilde{f}}(X,Y),i_{\ast}\eta\rangle=-\langle X,Y\rangle\langle i_{\ast}\xi_{k}-\bar{N},i_{\ast}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle X,Y\rangle\langle\xi_{k},\eta\rangle=-\langle X,Y\rangle\langle T,\langle\xi_{k},\eta\rangle T\rangle\frac{1}{\|T\|^{2}}

that is,

\langle\nabla_{X}Y,T\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle\langle T,\delta\rangle,

(6.7)

where

\delta=-\frac{1}{\|T\|^{2}}\langle\xi_{k},\eta\rangle T\in\Gamma(E_{k}^{\perp}).

(6.8)

Moreover, for
Z\in\Gamma(E_{i})
, with
1\leq i\neq k\leq s
,
Lemma
6.3
yields
\nabla^{\perp}_{Z}\xi_{k}=0
. Thus, it follows
from Codazzi equation (
4.4
) that

\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle=0=\langle X,Y\rangle\langle Z,\delta\rangle.

(6.9)

Equations (
6.7
) and (
6.9
) imply that

E_{k}
is an umbilical distribution with mean curvature vector field

\delta
given in (
6.8
). On the other hand, it follows
from Lemma
6.3
and the fact that
\|T\|
is contant along

\{T\}^{\perp}
, that

\langle\nabla_{X}\delta,Z\rangle=-\frac{1}{\|T\|^{2}}\langle T,Z\rangle\langle\eta,\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi_{k}\rangle=0,

for all
X\in\Gamma(E_{k})
and
Z\in\Gamma(E_{k}^{\perp})
, and this
shows that
E_{k}
is a spherical distribution. In order to show that

E_{k}^{\perp}
is totally geodesic, it suffices to prove that

\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle=0,

(6.10)

for all
X\in\Gamma(E_{i})
,
Y\in\Gamma(E_{j})
and
Z\in\Gamma(E_{k})
, with

1\leq k\leq s
,
0\leq i,j\leq s
,
k\neq\{i,j\}
. For
0\leq i=j\leq s
,
equation (
6.10
) follows from Lemma
6.3

and Codazzi equation. Now, let
0\leq i\neq j\leq s
, and let us
consider the following cases.

Case 1: If
j=0
and
Y=\lambda T
, for some smooth function
\lambda

along
M
, then

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\lambda T,Z\rangle=\langle X(\lambda)T+\lambda\nabla_{X}T,Z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\langle A_{\eta}X,Z\rangle=0.

Case 2: If
i=0
and
X=\lambda T
, for some smooth function
\lambda

along
M
, it follows from Codazzi equation (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle(\xi_{j}-\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\lambda T}Y,Z\rangle(\xi_{j}-\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{Y}\lambda T,Z\rangle(\xi_{i}-\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

as in the previous case.

Case 3: Suppose now
1\leq i\neq j\leq s
and let
X,Y\in\{T\}^{\perp}
.
It follows from Codazzi equation (
4.5
) that

\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle(\xi_{j}-\xi_{k})=\langle\nabla_{Y}X,Z\rangle(\xi_{i}-\xi_{k}).

(6.11)

We claim that the vector fields
\xi_{i}-\xi_{k}
and
\xi_{j}-\xi_{k}
are linearly
independent. Indeed, assume otherwise that there exists a smooth
nonzero function
u
along
M
such that

\xi_{i}-\xi_{k}=u(\xi_{j}-\xi_{k}).

It follows that

(u-1)\left(\xi_{k}-\frac{m}{2}H\right)=u\left(\xi_{j}-\frac{m}{2}H\right)-\left(\xi_{i}-\frac{m}{2}H\right).

By taking the norms, it follows from (
6.1
) that

\left\|\xi_{j}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\|^{2}=\left\langle\xi_{j}-\frac{m}{2}H,\xi_{i}-\frac{m}{2}H\right\rangle.

(6.12)

Now, using (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) in (
6.12
),
we obtain
\|\xi_{i}=\xi_{j}\|
, that is,
\xi_{i}=\xi_{j}
, which is a contradiction.
The conclusion now follows from (
6.11
).
∎

Finally, we can prove the main result of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
m\geq 3
, be a proper isometric immersion
of a connected Einstein manifold, with flat normal bundle and parallel
mean curvature vector field
H
. Moreover, suppose that the vector
field
T
given in (
3.1
) is nowhere vanishing. Let
\tilde{f}=i\circ f

be considered as a submanifold
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
, and consider the distributions

(E_{k})
,
0\leq k\leq s
, of
M^{m}

given in (
6.6
) which, by virtue of Lemma

6.5
, is such that
E_{k}
is spherical and

E_{k}^{\perp}
is totally geodesic, for
1\leq k\leq s
. It follows
from theorem of the Hiepko
[
5
]
that there exists locally (globally,
if
M^{m}
is simply connected and complete) a product
representation

\psi\colon M_{0}\times M_{1}\times\ldots\times M_{s}\to M^{m}

which is an isometry with respect to a warped product metric on the
product
M_{0}\times M_{1}\times\ldots\times M_{s}
, where
M_{0}
is
totally geodesic and
M_{k}
are intrinsic spheres, for
1\leq k\leq s
.
In this case, as
\dim(M_{0})=1
, we can suppose that
M_{0}
is an
open interval
I_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}
. Consider now the immersion

F=\tilde{f}\circ\psi\colon I_{0}\times_{\rho_{1}}M_{1}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times_{\rho_{s}}M_{s}^{n_{s}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+2}.

F
is an isometric immersion defined in a warped product manifold,
whose second fundamental form is adapted to the product net
(
6.6
) of
M^{m}
. It follows from Nolker’s theorem

[
13
]
that
F
is an extrinsic warped product of isometric
immersions. More precisely, there exist a warped product
representation

\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{s+1}\times_{\sigma_{1}}N_{1}^{n_{1}+k_{1}}\times\ldots\times_{\sigma_{s}}N_{s}^{n_{s}+k_{s}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+2},

isometric immersions
f_{j}\colon M_{j}^{n_{j}}\to N_{j}^{n_{j}+k_{j}}
,
1\leq j\leq s
,
and an smooth curve
f_{0}\colon I_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{s+1}
such that

\rho_{j}=\sigma_{j}\circ f_{0}
for
1\leq j\leq s
and

F=\phi\circ(f_{0}\times\ldots\times f_{s}),

where
N_{j}^{n_{j}+k_{j}}
are Euclidean spheres. Moreover, each
isometric immersion
f_{j}\colon M_{j}^{n_{j}}\to N_{j}^{n_{j}+k_{j}}
is umbilical,
with mean curvature vector fiel given by
H_{j}=i_{\ast}\xi_{j}-\bar{N}
.
Each of these normal vector fields is parallel along the normal
connection
{}^{f_{j}}\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}
of
f_{j}
, which is the restriction of

\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}
along
M_{j}^{n_{j}}
. In fact, using Lemma
6.3
,
and equations (
3.10
) and (
3.11
),
we obtain

{}^{f_{j}}\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}_{X}H_{j}

\displaystyle=

{}^{f_{j}}\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}_{X}i_{\ast}\xi_{j}-^{f_{j}}\widetilde{\nabla}^{\perp}_{X}\bar{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{\ast}\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi_{j}+\langle X,T\rangle\langle\xi_{j},\eta\rangle\bar{N}+\langle X,T\rangle i_{\ast}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M_{j}^{n_{j}})
. Therefore, each
f_{j}
reduce
codimension to one, and by rigidity, all of them must be the
canonical inclusion. We conclude that
f
is a multi-rotational
submanifold with profile
f_{0}
, where
f_{0}
is a smooth
curve determined by the vector field
T
.
∎

Locally coherent exact categories
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. A locally coherent exact category is a finitely accessible additive category endowed with an exact structure in which the admissible short exact sequences are the directed colimits of admissible short exact sequences of finitely presentable objects. We show that any exact structure on a small idempotent-complete additive category extends uniquely to a locally coherent exact structure on the category of ind-objects; in particular, any finitely accessible category has the unique maximal and the unique minimal locally coherent exact category structures. All locally coherent exact categories are of Grothendieck type in the sense of Št​’ovíček. We also discuss the canonical embedding of a small exact category into the abelian category of additive sheaves in connection with the locally coherent exact structure on the ind-objects, and deduce two periodicity theorems as applications.



Introduction

Noetherian rings and modules over them play an important role in
commutative algebra and representation theory.
Over a Noetherian ring
R
, the category of finitely generated
modules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{mod--}}}R
is abelian, and the abelian category of arbitrary
modules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
is locally Noetherian.
Coherent rings are a natural generalization of Noetherian ones:
over a coherent ring, the category of finitely presentable modules

{\operatorname{\mathsf{mod--}}}R
is abelian, and the abelian category of arbitrary
modules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
is locally coherent.
Still, there are many rings that are not coherent.

The aim of this paper is to suggest a point of view allowing to
consider the category of modules over an arbitrary ring as “locally
coherent” in some sense.
This comes at the cost of passing from abelian to exact categories
(in Quillen’s sense).

We explain that, for any ring
R
, the abelian category
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R

(as, more generally, any finitely accessible additive category) has
a complete lattice of
locally coherent exact structures
.
The
minimal
locally coherent exact structure is the
pure

exact structure.
The
maximal locally coherent exact structure
is the closest
locally coherent approximation to the abelian exact structure on

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
.

The maximal locally coherent exact structure keeps track of the short
exact sequences of finitely presentable
R
-modules, but disregards
the short exact sequences in which the middle term and the cokernel
are finitely presentable while the kernel is not.
All information about short exact sequences of the latter type is
destroyed by considering
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
as an exact category with
the maximal (or any other) locally coherent exact structure.
That is the price one has to pay for viewing the category of modules
over an arbitrary ring through coherent lens.

More generally, for any regular cardinal
\kappa
and any

\kappa
-accessible additive category
\mathsf{A}
, an exact structure
on
\mathsf{A}
is said to be
locally
\kappa
-coherent
if
the admissible short exact sequences in
\mathsf{A}
are precisely
the
\kappa
-directed colimits of admissible short exact sequences
of
\kappa
-presentable objects.
We show that locally
\kappa
-coherent exact structures on
\mathsf{A}

correspond bijectively to arbitrary exact structures on the full
subcategory
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
of
\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{A}
.
(Notice that an abelian category
\mathsf{A}
is locally coherent if and
only if it is locally finitely presentable and its full subcategory of
finitely presentable objects is abelian; in other words, this means
that
\mathsf{A}
is the category of ind-objects of a small abelian category.)

Beyond the abelian category
\mathsf{A}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
of arbitrary
R
-modules,
the additive category
\mathsf{A}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fl}--}}}R
of flat
R
-modules provides
an important example for our theory.
There is a phenomenon of
flat coherence
: for any regular
cardinal
\kappa
, the kernel of any surjective morphism of

\kappa
-presentable flat
R
-modules is again a
\kappa
-presentable
flat
R
-module
[
24
, Lemma 4.1]
,
[
25
, Corollary 10.12]
.
In the language of the present paper, this observation appears as
a corollary of the fact that the category
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fl}--}}}R
with its natural
exact structure is locally
\kappa
-coherent for any ring
R
.
The exact structure on
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fl}--}}}R
is pure, but it is not

\kappa
-pure, of course.

So, for any small exact category
\mathsf{E}
, the accessible category

\Ind\mathsf{E}
of ind-objects of
\mathsf{E}
acquires the induced locally
coherent exact category structure.
As a digression, we discuss the classical construction of
the canonical embedding of
\mathsf{E}
into the abelian category
\mathsf{K}

of left exact functors
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
in
Section
6
.
We observe that the category
\mathsf{K}
is locally finitely presentable
(and in fact, locally type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
), and the locally coherent
exact category structure on
\Ind\mathsf{E}
is inherited from the abelian
exact structure on
\mathsf{K}
via a natural fully faithful embedding

\Ind\mathsf{E}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
.

The main results of this paper are two
periodicity theorems
.
Theorem
7.1
generalizes
the flat/projective periodicity theorem of Benson–Goodearl
and Neeman
[
5
,
19
]
and the fp-projective periodicity theorem
of Šaroch–Št​’ovíček
[
32
]
, while
Theorem
7.6
generalizes the cotorsion
periodicity theorem of Bazzoni, Cortés-Izurdiaga,
and Estrada
[
3
]
.
The idea of these generalizations is to replace the split exact
category of finitely generated projective
R
-modules (or
the abelian category of finitely presentable modules over
a coherent ring
R
) by an arbitrary small exact category.

As a particular case of Theorem
7.1
,
we deduce in Theorem
8.3
a new version
of fp-projective periodicity theorem applicable to an arbitrary
(and not necessarily coherent) ring
R
.
Another such version of fp-projective periodicity was previously
obtained in the paper
[
4
]
.

Beyond periodicity theorems, what can one do with locally
coherent exact categories?
Let us explain the idea which motivated the present research.
The results of
[
23
, Theorem 9.39 and Corollary 9.42]
, and
particularly
[
27
, Theorem 8.19 and Corollary 8.20]
, describe
compact generators of the coderived categories of locally coherent
abelian DG-categories, such as the category of curved DG-modules
over a curved DG-ring with a coherent underlying graded ring.
The coherence condition involved is somewhat restrictive, as
mentioned in the beginning of this introduction.

The definition of an
exact DG-category
was given and studied
in the paper
[
23
]
; the
abelian
DG-categories

[
23
,
27
]
are a particular case.
The definition of a locally coherent abelian DG-category can be
found in
[
23
, Section 9.5]
and
[
27
, Section 8.3]
.
We hope that a suitable definition of a locally coherent exact
(rather than abelian) DG-category can be worked out so that
the results concerning compact generators of coderived categories
mentioned above would be generalizable to locally coherent exact
DG-categories, such as the maximal locally coherent exact DG-category
structure on the category of CDG-modules over an arbitrary CDG-ring.

-accessible.

A^{2}
, and

-presentable

Proof.

Part (a) is an easy particular case of
Proposition

Alternatively, here is a simple direct proof of the “if and only if”

-accessible, but only that

-directed colimits

The converse assertion follows from the fact that directed colimits

Remark 1.3
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a locally
\kappa
-coherent exact category.
Then it follows from

Proposition 1.4
.

In any locally
\kappa
-coherent exact structure on
a
\kappa
-accessible additive category
\mathsf{A}
, the full subcategory
of
\kappa
-presentable objects

Let

\mathsf{A}

Then there exists a

-directed poset

For every

As the kernel of a termwise admissible epimorphism of short exact

Now a direct summand of a

Applying the same argument to the short exact sequence

Finally, we can conclude that the object

belongs to

\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}

is the cokernel of the composition

2.
The Induced Exact Category Structure on the Ind-Objects

In this section, as in the previous one,
\kappa
denotes
a regular cardinal.
Let
\mathsf{S}
be a small additive category.
Consider the category
\mathsf{A}=\Ind_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{S}
of ind-objects
representable by
\kappa
-directed diagrams of objects of
\mathsf{S}
.
Then the additive category
\mathsf{A}
is
\kappa
-accessible, and
the full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{A}
is
naturally equivalent to the idempotent completion
\overline{\mathsf{S}}
of
the category
\mathsf{S}
.
Conversely, for any
\kappa
-accessible additive category
\mathsf{A}
,
and any full additive subcategory
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
such that
all the objects of
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
are direct summands of objects
from
\mathsf{S}
, one has a natural equivalence of categories
\mathsf{A}\simeq\Ind_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{S}
.
In the case of the countable cardinal
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, we will use
the notation
\Ind\mathsf{S}
instead of
\Ind_{(\aleph_{0})}\mathsf{S}
.

The aim of this section is to show that any exact structure on
\mathsf{S}

extends uniquely to a locally
\kappa
-coherent exact structure
on
\mathsf{A}
with the property that the full subcategory
\mathsf{S}
is closed
under extensions in
\mathsf{A}
.
By Proposition
1.4
, the latter
condition holds automatically when the category
\mathsf{S}
is
idempotent-complete.
In particular, for any
\kappa
-accessible additive category
\mathsf{A}
,
locally
\kappa
-coherent exact structures on
\mathsf{A}
correspond
bijectively to exact structures on the (essentially small) additive
category
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.

We proceed in two steps: first, extend a given exact category structure
on
\mathsf{S}
to an exact category structure on the idempotent completion

\overline{\mathsf{S}}=\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
of
\mathsf{S}
, and then extend it further to
a locally
\kappa
-coherent exact structure on

\mathsf{A}=\Ind_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{S}
.
But first of all we present a counterexample demonstrating a pitfall
involved with the question of uniqueness of extension of exact
structures from
\mathsf{S}
to
\overline{\mathsf{S}}
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
denote the abelian category of morphisms of
finite-dimensional vector spaces
f\colon V_{0}\longrightarrow V_{1}
over a field
k
.
Let
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{B}
be the full additive subcategory whose objects are
all the morphisms
f
satisfying the equation
\dim_{k}V_{0}-\dim_{k}V_{1}=\rk f
(where
\rk f=\dim_{k}\im f
is the rank of
f
).
Then
\mathsf{S}
is a weakly idempotent-complete additive category and all
objects of
\mathsf{B}
are direct summands of objects from
\mathsf{S}
.
Still all short exact sequences in
\mathsf{B}
with all the three terms
belonging to
\mathsf{S}
are split (as the equation
\rk g=\rk f+\rk h

implies splitness of a short exact sequence
0\longrightarrow f\longrightarrow g\longrightarrow h\longrightarrow 0
in
\mathsf{B}
).
So both the abelian exact structure and the split exact structure on

\mathsf{B}
restrict to the split exact structure on
\mathsf{S}
.
Notice that
\mathsf{S}
is
not
closed under extensions in
the abelian exact structure on
\mathsf{B}
, however.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category and
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
be the idempotent
completion of the additive category
\mathsf{E}
.
Then there is a unique exact category structure on
\overline{\mathsf{E}}

such that
\mathsf{E}
is closed under extensions in
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
and
the inherited exact category structure on
\mathsf{E}
is the originally
given exact category structure.
Specifically, the exact category structure on
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
is defined
by the condition that the short exact sequences in
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
are
the direct summands of short exact sequences in
\mathsf{E}
.

Proof.

Uniqueness: let
0\longrightarrow\overline{A}\longrightarrow\overline{B}\longrightarrow\overline{C}\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence in
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
.
Then there exist objects
\overline{A}^{\prime}
and
\overline{C}^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathsf{E}}
such that
A=\overline{A}\oplus\overline{A}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{E}

and
C=\overline{C}\oplus\overline{C}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{E}
.
Now we have a short exact sequence
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow\overline{A}^{\prime}\oplus\overline{B}\oplus\overline{C}^{\prime}\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0
in
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
.
Since the full subcategory
\mathsf{E}
is closed under extensions in

\overline{\mathsf{E}}
by assumption, it follows that all the three terms of
the new short exact sequence belong to
\mathsf{E}
.
So any short exact sequence in
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
is a direct summand of
a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{E}
.
Conversely, in any exact category, any direct summand of a short
exact sequence is a short exact sequence, as one can easily show using
the pullback and pushout axioms.

Existence: define the short exact sequences in
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
to be
the direct summands of short exact sequences in
\mathsf{E}
.
This endows
\overline{\mathsf{E}}
with an exact category structure satisfying
all the conditions
[
9
, Proposition 6.13]
.
In particular, the fact that
\mathsf{E}
is closed under extensions in

\overline{\mathsf{E}}
is provable using the pullback and pushout axioms
in the exact category
\mathsf{E}
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible additive category.
Suppose given an exact category structure on
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
Then the class of all
\kappa
-directed colimits in
\mathsf{A}
of short
exact sequences in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
is an exact category structure
on
\mathsf{A}
.
Any short exact sequence in this exact category structure on
\mathsf{A}

whose terms belong to
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
is a short exact sequence in
the original exact category structure on
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
Consequently, the resulting exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
is locally

\kappa
-coherent.

Proof.

To begin with, notice that the inclusion

\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
preserves all cokernels that happen to exist
in the category
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
Indeed, let
g\colon T\longrightarrow S
be a cokernel of a morphism

f\colon U\longrightarrow T
in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.
Consider an arbitrary object
B\in\mathsf{A}
.
Then there exists a
\kappa
-directed diagram

(B_{\upsilon})_{\upsilon\in\Upsilon}
in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
such that

B=\varinjlim B_{\upsilon}
.
For any object
V\in\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
, we have
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(V,B)\simeq\varinjlim_{\upsilon\in\Upsilon}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(V,B_{\upsilon})
.
By assumption, the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(g,B_{\upsilon})
is the kernel of
the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(f,B_{\upsilon})
in the category of abelian groups

\mathsf{Ab}
for every
\upsilon\in\Upsilon
.
Using the fact that directed colimits commute with kernels in
\mathsf{Ab}
,
one concludes that the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(g,B)
is the kernel of the map

\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(f,B)
in
\mathsf{Ab}
.
So the morphism
g
is a cokernel of the morphism
f
in
\mathsf{A}
.

Now let
(0\to U_{\xi}\to T_{\xi}\to S_{\xi}\to 0)_{\xi\in\Xi}

be a
\kappa
-directed diagram of short exact sequences in

A_{<\kappa}
, indexed by a
\kappa
-directed poset
\Xi
.
Let

be the related short

\kappa
-directed colimits in
\mathsf{A}
.
Denote the morphisms involved by

and

and

.
Then the morphism
g
is the cokernel of the morphism
f
in
the category
\mathsf{A}
, because the morphisms
g_{\xi}
are the cokernels
of the morphisms
f_{\xi}
in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
(hence also in
\mathsf{A}
),
and any existing colimit functors preserve any existing cokernels
in any additive category.

To show that the morphism
f\colon C\longrightarrow D
is the kernel of
the morphism
g\colon D\longrightarrow E
in the category
\mathsf{A}
, consider
an arbitrary object
B\in\mathsf{A}
.
Then there exists a (
\kappa
-directed) diagram

(B_{\upsilon})_{\upsilon\in\Upsilon}
in the category
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}

such that

For any object

, we have
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B,A)\simeq\varprojlim_{\upsilon\in\Upsilon}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B_{\upsilon},A)
.
On the other hand, the functor
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B_{\upsilon},{-})\colon\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
preserves
\kappa
-directed colimits for every

\upsilon\in\Upsilon
.
By assumption, the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B_{\upsilon},f_{\xi})
is the kernel
of the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B_{\upsilon},g_{\xi})
in
\mathsf{Ab}
for every

\upsilon\in\Upsilon
and
\xi\in\Xi
.
Since all limits and directed colimits commute with kernels in
\mathsf{Ab}
,
we can conclude that the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B,f)
is the kernel of the map

\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B,g)
in
\mathsf{Ab}
.
So the morphism
f
is the kernel of the morphism
g
in
\mathsf{A}
.

It is clear from
Lemma
2.3
that
the class of all admissible epimorphisms in the would-be exact
structure on
\mathsf{A}
is closed under compositions.
The pullback and pushout axioms for short exact sequences in

\mathsf{A}
are provable using

Here, in the case of the pullbacks, one also needs to use the fact
that the
\kappa
-directed colimits of objects from
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}

in
\mathsf{A}
take pullbacks in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
to pullbacks in
\mathsf{A}

(more generally, this holds for all

from

The second assertion of the proposition follows from

3.
Example: Maximal Locally Coherent Exact Structure

Notice that there is a natural partial order on the class of all exact
structures on any given additive category (given by inclusion of
classes of admissible short exact sequences).
Moreover, the intersection of any nonempty set/class of classes of
short exact sequences defining exact structures is again a class
of short exact sequences defining an exact structure.
Furthermore, any additive category has a unique maximal exact
category structure
[
31
,
11
,
30
]
.
Hence the set of all exact structures on a small additive category is
a complete lattice.

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal and
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible
additive category.
Then Theorem
2.7

provides an order-preserving bijection between the poset of locally

\kappa
-coherent exact structures on
\mathsf{A}
and the poset of all
exact structures on the essentially small additive category

\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
of all
\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{A}
.
Thus the poset of all locally
\kappa
-coherent exact structures on

\mathsf{A}
is also a complete lattice.
In this section, we are interested in the
maximal
locally

\kappa
-coherent exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
.

Recall that a category
\mathsf{A}
is said to be
locally

\kappa
-presentable

[
1
, Definition 1.17 and Theorem 1.20]

if
\mathsf{A}
is
\kappa
-accessible and all colimits exist in
\mathsf{A}
.
Locally
\aleph_{0}
-presentable categories are called
locally
finitely presentable

[
1
, Definition 1.9 and Theorem 1.11]
.

The following lemma describes the maximal exact structure on
the category of
\kappa
-presentable objects
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
of
a locally
\kappa
-presentable abelian category
\mathsf{A}
.
Given an additive category
\mathsf{E}
and two morphisms
f
,
g
in

\mathsf{E}
, we denote by
\ker_{\mathsf{E}}(g)
and
\coker_{\mathsf{E}}(f)
the kernel
and cokernel of
g
and
f
computed in the category
\mathsf{E}
.

The following lemma was essentially already explained in
the beginning paragraphs of the proof of
Proposition
2.5
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a locally
\kappa
-presentable abelian category
and
S\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}T\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}U
be a composable pair
of morphisms in the full subcategory
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\subset\mathsf{A}
.
In this context:

(a)
if
g=\coker_{\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}}(f)
, then one also has

g=\coker_{\mathsf{A}}(f)
;

(b)
if
f=\ker_{\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}}(g)
, then one also has

f=\ker_{\mathsf{A}}(g)
.

Consequently, the exact category structure on
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}

inherited from the abelian exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
is
the maximal exact structure on
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.

Proof.

Notice first of all that the full subcategory
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
is
closed under extensions in
\mathsf{A}
by
[
33
, Lemma A.4]

(the running assumption in
[
33
]
that the category is
Grothendieck is not needed for this lemma, as it suffices to know
that the
\kappa
-directed colimit functors are exact in any locally

\kappa
-presentable abelian category
[
1
, Proposition 1.59]
).
So
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
inherits an exact category structure from
the abelian exact structure of
\mathsf{A}
, and the last assertion of
the lemma makes sense.

Part (a): notice that the full subcategory
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
is closed
under all
\kappa
-small colimits, and in particular, under cokernels
in
\mathsf{A}

[
1
, Proposition 1.16]
.
Consequently, one has
\coker_{\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}}(f)=\coker_{\mathsf{A}}(f)

for any morphism
f
in
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.

Part (b): denote by
S^{\prime}=\ker_{\mathsf{A}}(g)
the kernel of the morphism
g

computed in the category
\mathsf{A}
.
Then the morphism
f
factorizes as
S\overset{h}{\longrightarrow}S^{\prime}\longrightarrow T
.
Now
S^{\prime}

5.
Locally Coherent Exact Categories are of Grothendieck Type

Starting from this section, we gradually specialize our discussion
in this paper from the case of an arbitrary regular cardinal
\kappa

to that of
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
.
In this context, we will call the locally
\aleph_{0}
-coherent
exact categories in the sense of the definition in
Section
1
simply
locally coherent
exact categories
.

The aim of this section is to show that all locally coherent exact
categories are
of Grothendieck type
in the sense of
Št​’ovíček
[
34
, Definitions 3.4 and 3.11]
.
Consequently, the small object argument can be used to prove
completeness of cotorsion pairs in such exact
categories
[
34
, Theorem 5.16]
;
and in particular, any such exact category has enough injective
objects
[
34
, Corollary 5.9]
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a category and
\alpha
be an ordinal.
An
\alpha
-indexed chain
(of objects and morphisms)

(A_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta<\alpha}
in
\mathsf{A}
is a diagram in
\mathsf{A}
indexed
by the ordered set
\alpha
.
So
A_{\beta}
is an object of
\mathsf{A}
for every
0\leq\beta<\alpha
,
and a commutative diagram of morphisms
A_{\beta}\longrightarrow A_{\gamma}

in
\mathsf{A}
is given, indexed by all pairs of ordinals
0\leq\beta<\gamma<\alpha
.
An
\alpha
-indexed chain
(A_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta<\alpha}
in
\mathsf{A}
is
said to be
smooth
if
A_{\gamma}=\varinjlim_{\beta<\gamma}A_{\beta}

in
\mathsf{A}
for every limit ordinal
\gamma<\alpha
.
If the colimit
A_{\alpha}=\varinjlim_{\beta<\alpha}A_{\beta}
of a smooth
chain
(A_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta<\alpha}
of objects and morphisms in
\mathsf{A}

exists, then the morphism
A_{0}\longrightarrow A_{\alpha}
is said to be
the
transfinite composition
of the morphisms
A_{\beta}\longrightarrow A_{\beta+1}
,
0\leq\beta<\beta+1<\alpha
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a class of morphisms in
\mathsf{A}
.
We will say that
transfinite compositions of morphisms from

\mathsf{M}
exist
in
\mathsf{A}
if, for every smooth chain

(A_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta<\alpha}
of objects and morphisms in
\mathsf{A}

such that the morphism
A_{\beta}\longrightarrow A_{\beta+1}
belongs to
\mathsf{M}

for all
0\leq\beta<\beta+1<\alpha
, the colimit
A_{\alpha}=\varinjlim_{\beta<\alpha}A_{\beta}
exists in
\mathsf{A}
.
If this is the case, and moreover, the transfinite composition

A_{0}\longrightarrow A_{\alpha}
then also belongs to
\mathsf{M}
, we will say that
transfinite compositions of morphisms from
\mathsf{M}
exists in
\mathsf{A}

and
are themselves morphisms from
\mathsf{M}
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a category and
\mathsf{M}
be a class of morphisms in
\mathsf{A}
.
Then an object
B\in\mathsf{A}
is said to be
small relative to
\mathsf{M}

if there exists a cardinal
\lambda
satisfying the following
condition.
For every smooth chain of objects and morphisms

(A_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta<\alpha}
in
\mathsf{A}
such that the morphisms

A_{\beta}\longrightarrow A_{\beta+1}
belong to
\mathsf{M}
for all

0\leq\beta<\beta+1<\alpha
and the cofinality of the ordinal
\alpha

is not smaller than
\lambda
, the natural map of
sets
\varinjlim_{\beta<\alpha}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B,A_{\beta})\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(B,A_{\alpha})
must be bijective.
It is presumed here that the colimit
A_{\alpha}=\varinjlim_{\beta<\alpha}A_{\beta}
exists in
\mathsf{A}
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be an exact category.
An
\alpha
-indexed filtration

(F_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}

in
\mathsf{A}
is a smooth
(\alpha\penalty+1)
-indexed chain of objects
and morphisms such that
F_{0}=0
and the morphism
F_{\beta}\longrightarrow F_{\beta+1}
is an admissible monomorphism for all
0\leq\beta<\alpha
.
Given an
\alpha
-indexed filtration
(F_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}
,
the object
F=F_{\alpha}
is said to be
filtered by
the cokernels

U_{\beta}=\coker(F_{\beta}\to F_{\beta+1})
of the morphisms

F_{\beta}\longrightarrow F_{\beta+1}
,
0\leq\beta<\alpha
.

Given a class of objects
\mathsf{U}\subset\mathsf{A}
, one says that an object

F\in\mathsf{A}
is
filtered by
(objects from)
\mathsf{U}
if there is
an ordinal
\alpha
and an
\alpha
-indexed filtration

(F_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}
on
F=F_{\alpha}
such that

\coker(F_{\beta}\to F_{\beta+1})\in\mathsf{U}
for all
0\leq\beta<\alpha
.
The class of all objects in
\mathsf{A}
filtered by objects from
\mathsf{U}

is denoted by
\Fil(\mathsf{U})\subset\mathsf{A}
.

A class of objects
\mathsf{F}\subset\mathsf{A}
is said to be
deconstructible

if there exists a
set
of objects
\mathsf{U}\subset\mathsf{F}
such that

\mathsf{F}=\Fil(\mathsf{U})
.
In particular, the exact category
\mathsf{A}
is said to be

deconstructible in itself
if there exists a set of objects

\mathsf{U}\subset\mathsf{A}
such that
\mathsf{A}=\Fil(\mathsf{U})
.

Given an exact category
\mathsf{A}
, we say that
\mathsf{A}

admits
a generator
if there is an object
G\in\mathsf{A}
such that for
every object
B\in\mathsf{A}
there exists a set
\Lambda
and an admissible
epimorphism
G^{(\Lambda)}\longrightarrow B
.
Here
G^{(\Lambda)}
denotes the coproduct of
\Lambda
copies of

G
in
\mathsf{A}
.

An exact category
\mathsf{A}
is called

efficient

[
34
, Definition 3.4]
if it satisfies
the following conditions:

(Ef0) or (GT0):

The additive category
\mathsf{A}
is weakly
idempotent-complete.

(Ef1) or (GT1):

All transfinite compositions of admissible
monomorphisms exist in
\mathsf{A}
, and are themselves admissible
monomorphisms.

(Ef2) or (GT2):

Every object of
\mathsf{A}
is small relative to
the class of all admissible monomorphisms.

(Ef3) or (GT3):

The exact category
\mathsf{A}
admits a generator.

An exact category
\mathsf{A}
is said to be
of Grothendieck
type

[
34
, Definition 3.11]
if
\mathsf{A}
is efficient
(i. e., it satisfies conditions (GT0)–(GT3)), and the following
additional condition holds:

(GT4):

The exact category
\mathsf{A}
is deconstructible in itself.

Given a finitely accessible category
\mathsf{A}
, we will use the notation

\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{fp}}=\mathsf{A}_{<\aleph_{0}}
for the full subcategory of finitely
presentable objects in
\mathsf{A}
.
The following well-known lemma is a generalization
of
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[

, Proposition 5.2]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a finitely accessible additive category and
\mathcal{S}
be
a small category equivalent to
\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{fp}}
, viewed as a ring with
many objects.
Then the additive category
\mathsf{A}
is naturally equivalent to
the additive category
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fl}--}}}\mathcal{S}
of flat right
\mathcal{S}
-modules.
The pure exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
corresponds to the exact structure
on
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fl}--}}}\mathcal{S}
inherited from the abelian category
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathcal{S}
of
all right
\mathcal{S}
-modules.

Proof.

This is
[
10
, Section 1.4]
or
[
35
, Proposition 4.2]
,
and a particular case of
[
1
, Theorem 2.26]
.
The point is that the category
\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{fp}}
is naturally equivalent to
the category of finitely generated projective right
\mathcal{S}
-modules
(since
\mathsf{A}_{\mathsf{fp}}
is additive and idempotent-complete), and the flat
right
\mathcal{S}
-modules are the directed colimits of the finitely

For the second assertion, cf. Example
4.5
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{A}
be an exact category.
We will say that
\mathsf{A}
has
exact functors of directed colimit

if all directed colimits exist in the additive category
\mathsf{A}
and
the directed colimits of admissible short exact sequences are
admissible short exact sequences.
A locally
\kappa
-coherent exact category
need not
have
exact functors of directed colimit, but it always has exact functors
of
\kappa
-directed colimit (in the obvious sense), by

1.2
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible additive category with directed
colimits, endowed with an exact structure with exact functors of
directed colimit.
Then
\mathsf{A}
is an efficient exact category.

Proof.

We check the axioms one by one.

(Ef0) or (GT0) Any additive category with
\kappa
-directed colimits
is idempotent-complete.

(Ef1) or (GT1) All transfinite compositions of chains of morphisms
exist in any category with directed colimits.
All transfinite compositions of admissible monomorphisms are
admissible monomorphisms in any exact category with exact functors
of directed colimit (since finite compositions of admissible
monomorphisms are admissible monomorphisms in any exact category,
and directed colimits of admissible monomorphisms are admissible
monomorphisms in an exact category with exact functors of directed
colimit).

(Ef2) or (GT2) All objects in any accessible category have
presentability ranks, so they are small with respect to the class
of all morphisms.
In fact, if
B=\varinjlim_{\xi\in\Xi}S_{\xi}
in a category
\mathsf{A}
with

\kappa
-directed colimits, where
\Xi
is a small
\kappa
-filtered
category and
S_{\xi}\in\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
for all
\xi\in\Xi
, and if

\lambda\geq\kappa
is a regular cardinal greater than the cardinality
of (the set of all objects and morphisms in)
\Xi
, then the object
B

is
\lambda
-presentable in
\mathsf{A}
(see
[
1
, Proposition 1.16]
).

(Ef3) or (GT3) The point is that any admissible epimorphism in
the
\kappa
-pure exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
is also an admissible
epimorphism in any exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
with exact functors of

\kappa
-directed colimit.
So it suffices to consider the case of the
\kappa
-pure exact
structure on
\mathsf{A}
.

Now the coproduct of all representatives of isomorphism
classes of
\kappa
-presentable objects is a generator in
the
\kappa
-pure exact structure on any
\kappa
-accessible
additive category
\mathsf{A}
.
In fact, if
B=\varinjlim_{\xi\in\Xi}S_{\xi}
in
\mathsf{A}
, where
\Xi
is
a small
\kappa
-filtered category and
S_{\xi}\in\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
for all

\xi\in\Xi
, then the natural morphism
\coprod_{\xi\in\Xi}S_{\xi}\longrightarrow\varinjlim_{\xi\in\Xi}S_{\xi}
is a
\kappa
-pure epimorphism in (i. e.,
an admissible epimorphism in the
\kappa
-pure exact structure
on)
\mathsf{A}
, as one can see from
Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a finitely accessible category endowed with an exact
structure with exact functors of directed colimit.
Then
\mathsf{A}
is an exact category of Grothendieck type.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
5.2
, it remains to check
the last axiom (GT4).
Indeed, for any ring with many objects
\mathcal{S}
, the exact category of
flat
\mathcal{S}
-modules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fl}--}}}\mathcal{S}
is deconstructible in itself,
essentially by
[

, Lemma 1 and Proposition 2]
.
In view of Lemma

, it follows
that any finitely accessible additive category
\mathsf{A}
endowed with
the pure exact structure is deconstructible in itself.
As, under the assumptions of the theorem, any filtration in the pure
exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
is also a filtration in the given
exact structure on
\mathsf{A}
, we can conclude that any exact category
\mathsf{A}

satisfying the assumptions of the theorem is deconstructible in itself.
∎

All locally coherent exact categories are of Grothendieck type.

and

.
Alternatively, one can refer to

(a,c)
and

,
and
[

, Theorem 3.16]
(for the first

∎

6.
Canonical Embedding into Abelian Category

The aim of this section is to represent an arbitrary locally
coherent exact category
\mathsf{C}
as a full subcategory in a locally
finitely presentable (in fact, locally type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty})
Grothendieck
abelian category
\mathsf{K}
, and show that
\mathsf{C}
enjoys nice closure
properties as a full subcategory in
\mathsf{K}
.
It is important that the locally coherent exact structure on
\mathsf{C}

turns out to be inherited from the abelian exact structure on
\mathsf{K}
.

For any small exact category
\mathsf{E}
, there is a classical construction
of the canonical embedding of
\mathsf{E}
into the abelian category
\mathsf{K}
of

additive sheaves
on
\mathsf{E}
.
Let us recall some details
[
15
, Proposition A.2]
,

[
36
, Section A.7]
,
[
9
, Appendix A]
.

The category
\mathsf{K}
is defined as the category of all left exact
contravariant functors
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
.
Here
\mathsf{Ab}
denotes the category of abelian groups; and a contravariant
functor
K\colon\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
is said to be
left exact
if,
for any admissible short exact sequence
0\longrightarrow E^{\prime}\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow E^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0
in
\mathsf{E}
, the sequence of abelian groups
0\longrightarrow K(E^{\prime\prime})\longrightarrow K(E)\longrightarrow K(E^{\prime})
is left exact.
The functor
\rho\colon\mathsf{E}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
assigns to every object
E\in\mathsf{E}

the representable functor
\rho(E)=\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}({-},E)
.
The following proposition summarizes the classical theory.

Proposition 6.1
.

(a)
The functor
\rho
is fully faithful.
The category
\mathsf{K}
is abelian.

(b)
A short sequence
0\longrightarrow E^{\prime}\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow E^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0
in
\mathsf{E}
is admissible exact if and only if its image

0\longrightarrow\rho(E^{\prime})\longrightarrow\rho(E)\longrightarrow\rho(E^{\prime\prime})\longrightarrow 0
is exact
in
\mathsf{K}
.

(c)
The essential image of the functor
\rho
is closed under
extensions in
\mathsf{K}
.

(d)
For any objects
K\in\mathsf{K}
and
E\in\mathsf{E}
, and any
epimorphism
K\longrightarrow\rho(E)
in
\mathsf{K}
, there exists an admissible
epimorphism
F\longrightarrow E
in
\mathsf{E}
and a morphism
\rho(F)\longrightarrow K

in
\mathsf{K}
making the triangular diagram
\rho(F)\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\rho(E)
commutative in
\mathsf{K}
.

(e)
If the category
\mathsf{E}
is weakly idempotent-complete,
then the essential image of the functor
\rho
is closed under
kernels of epimorphisms in
\mathsf{K}
.

Proof.

The first assertion of part (a) holds by Yoneda’s lemma.
The second assertion of part (a) is
[
36
, Proposition A.7.13]

or
[
9
, Lemma A.20]
.
Part (b) is
[
36
, Propositions A.7.14 and A.7.16(a)]

or
[
9
, Lemmas A.21 and A.23]
.
Part (c) is
[
36
, Lemma A.7.18]
or
[
9
, Lemma A.24]
.
Part (d) is
[
36
, second paragraph of Lemma A.7.15]

or
[
9
, Lemma A.22]
.
Part (e) is
[
36
, Proposition A.7.16(b)]
.
∎

Following
[
12
, Section 3]
and
[
6
, Section 3 of the published
version or Section 2 of the
arXiv
version]
, an object
S

in a Grothendieck category
\mathsf{K}
is said to be
of type

\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
if, for every
n\geq 0
, the functor
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{K}}(S,{-})\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
preserves directed colimits.
Obviously, objects of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
are finitely presentable.
A Grothendieck category
\mathsf{K}
is said to be
locally type

\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
if it has a set of generators consisting of objects
of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

The category
\mathsf{K}
is a locally finitely presentable Grothendieck
category, and in fact, it is locally type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
.
The objects in the image of the functor
\rho\colon\mathsf{E}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
are
of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
, and they form a set of generators of
\mathsf{K}
.

Proof.

By construction,
\mathsf{K}
is a full subcategory in the category

\Fun_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{Ab})
of all contravariant additive functors

\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
.
Specifically, the full subcategory
\mathsf{K}\subset\Fun_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{Ab})

consists of all the left exact functors.
Since directed colimits of left exact sequences of abelian groups
are left exact, the full subcategory
\mathsf{K}
is closed under directed
colimits in
\Fun_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{Ab})
.
The objects
\rho(E)
,
E\in\mathsf{E}
are finitely presentable (in fact,
finitely generated projective) in
\Fun_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{Ab})
, and it
follows that these objects are also finitely presentable in
\mathsf{K}
.

The objects
\rho(E)
form a set of generators of

\Fun_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathsf{Ab})
, hence they also form a set of generators
of
\mathsf{K}
.
By
[
1
, Theorem 1.11]
, the category
\mathsf{K}
is locally finitely
presentable.
The assertion that the category
\mathsf{K}
is Grothendieck can be found
in
[
36
, Proposition A.7.13]
; but in fact any locally finitely
presentable abelian category is
Grothendieck
[
1
, Proposition 1.59]
.

It remains to show that the objects
\rho(E)
are of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}

in
\mathsf{K}
.
Replacing the category
\mathsf{E}
with its (weak) idempotent completion
does not change the category
\mathsf{K}
, so without loss of generality we
can assume
\mathsf{E}
to be weakly idempotent-complete.
Then Proposition
6.1
(e) is
applicable, and it remains to refer to
[
22
, Lemma 3.5]
.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a locally coherent exact category and
\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{fp}}\subset\mathsf{C}
be its exact subcategory of finitely presentable objects.
Put
\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{fp}}
, and consider the canonical embedding
\rho\colon\mathsf{E}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
of the exact category
\mathsf{E}
into the abelian category

\mathsf{K}
of left exact functors
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
.
Then the full subcategory
\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})\subset\mathsf{K}
is
closed under coproducts, directed colimits, extensions, and kernels
of epimorphisms in the abelian category
\mathsf{K}
.
Endowed with the exact category structure inherited from
the abelian category
\mathsf{K}
, the category
\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})

is naturally equivalent to the exact category
\mathsf{C}
.

Proof.

To establish the closure properties of the full subcategory

\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})\subset\mathsf{K}
, it suffices to refer to
Proposition
6.1
(c,e),
Proposition
6.2
,
and
[
22
, Proposition 3.6]
.
By Proposition
A.1
, the category

\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})
is finitely accessible and
\rho(\mathsf{E})
is its
full subcategory of finitely presentable objects; so

\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})\simeq\Ind\rho(\mathsf{E}))
.
Similarly we have
\mathsf{C}\simeq\Ind(\mathsf{E})
, so the category equivalence

\rho\colon\mathsf{E}\simeq\rho(\mathsf{E})
induces a category equivalence

\mathsf{C}\simeq\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})
.
It remains to show that the inherited exact structure on

\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})\subset\mathsf{K}
agrees with the original locally
coherent exact structure on
\mathsf{C}
.

The (admissible) short exact sequences in
\mathsf{C}
are the directed
colimits of short exact sequences in
\mathsf{E}
.
Since the functor
\rho
is exact by
Proposition
6.1
(b) and the directed
colimits are exact functors in
\mathsf{K}
, it follows that any short
exact sequence in
\mathsf{C}
is also a short exact sequence in

\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})
.
To prove the converse implication, we will use the characterization
of admissible epimorphisms in
\mathsf{C}
provided by
Lemma
2.3
.

Let
0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence
in
\mathsf{K}
with the terms
L
,
M
,
N\in\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})
.
Given an object
E\in\rho(\mathsf{E})
and a morphism
E\longrightarrow N
in
\mathsf{K}
,
we need to construct a commutative square

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

with an object
F\in\rho(\mathsf{E})
and an admissible epimorphism

F\longrightarrow E
in
\rho(\mathsf{E})
.
Indeed, let
K
be the pullback of the pair of morphisms
M\longrightarrow N

and
E\longrightarrow N
in the abelian category
\mathsf{K}
.
Then the morphism
K\longrightarrow E
is an epimorphism in
\mathsf{K}
, since
the morphism
M\longrightarrow N
is.
It remains to refer to
Proposition
6.1
(d).

Now that we have shown that the morphism
M\longrightarrow N
comes from
an admissible epimorphism in
\mathsf{C}
, it follows that the whole
short exact sequence
0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
comes
from a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{C}
(since we already know that
the functor
\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\varinjlim\rho(\mathsf{E})\subset\mathsf{K}
is exact).
∎

7.
Two Periodicity Theorems

Let
\mathsf{C}
be an idempotent-complete additive category and

\mathsf{F}\subset\mathsf{C}
be a class of objects.
Following the notation of Section
2
, we denote
by
\overline{\mathsf{F}}\subset\mathsf{C}
the class of all direct summands of
objects from
\mathsf{F}
in
\mathsf{C}
.

The aim of this section is to formulate and prove two periodicity
theorems for a locally coherent exact category.
We state the first one right away, postponing the formulation of
the second one towards the end of the section.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a locally coherent exact category and

\mathsf{P}=\overline{\Fil(\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{fp}})}
be the class of all direct summands
of objects filtered by finitely presentable objects in
\mathsf{C}

(in the sense of the definition in
Section
5
).
Let
0\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0
be an admissible
short exact sequence in
\mathsf{C}
with
P\in\mathsf{P}
.
Then one has
C\in\mathsf{P}
.

Examples 7.2
.

(1) Let
R
be a ring and
\mathsf{C}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fl}--}}}R
be the category of flat
right
R
-modules.
Then
\mathsf{C}
is a finitely accessible category, and the finitely
presentable objects of
\mathsf{C}
are the finitely generated projective
right
R
-modules.
Endowed with the exact structure inherited from the ambient abelian
category of modules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
, the category
\mathsf{C}
becomes a locally
coherent exact category with the pure exact structure (see
Example
4.5
).

In this context, the class
\mathsf{P}=\overline{\Fil(\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{fp}})}\subset\mathsf{C}

is the class of all projective
R
-modules.
Theorem
7.1
claims that, for any short
exact sequence of
R
-modules
0\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0

with a flat
R
-module
C
and a projective
R
-module
P
,
the
R
-module
C
is also projective.
This result is due to Benson and Goodearl
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
,
see also Neeman
[
19
, Remark 2.15 and Theorem 8.6]
.

(2) Let
R
be a ring and
\mathsf{C}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
be the abelian category of
right
R
-modules.
Then
\mathsf{C}
is a locally finitely presentable category, and
the finitely presentable objects of
\mathsf{C}
are the cokernels of
morphisms of finitely generated free
R
-modules.
Endowed with the maximal locally coherent exact structure as per
Section
3
,
\mathsf{C}
becomes a locally
coherent exact category.
Assume additionally that the ring
R
is right coherent; then

\mathsf{C}
is a locally coherent abelian category, so the maximal
locally coherent exact structure on
\mathsf{C}
coincides with the abelian
exact structure by Corollary
3.4
.

In this context, the class
\mathsf{P}=\overline{\Fil(\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{fp}})}\subset\mathsf{C}

is known as the class of all
fp-projective

R
-modules.
Theorem
7.1
claims that, for any short
exact sequence of
R
-modules
0\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0

with an fp-projective
R
-module
P
, the
R
-module
C
is also
fp-projective.
This result is due to Šaroch and
Št​’ovíček
[
32
, Example 4.3]
,
see also
[
4
, Theorem 0.7 or Corollary 4.9]
.

For an extension of example (2) to arbitrary rings
R
, see
Example
8.4
below.

Let
\mathsf{K}
be an exact category, and let
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{K}
be two
classes of objects.
Then
\mathsf{A}^{\perp_{1}}\subset\mathsf{K}
denotes the class of all objects

X\in\mathsf{K}
such that
\Ext_{\mathsf{K}}^{1}(A,X)=0
for all
A\in\mathsf{A}
.
Similarly,
\mathsf{A}^{\perp_{\geq 1}}\subset\mathsf{K}
is the class of all objects

X\in\mathsf{K}
such that
\Ext_{\mathsf{K}}^{n}(A,X)=0
for all
A\in\mathsf{A}
and

Dually,

8.
Maximal Locally Coherent Exact Structure II

In Example
7.2
(2) we spelled out
what Theorem
7.1
says in the context
of the abelian category
\mathsf{C}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
of right modules over
a right coherent ring
R
(and the abelian exact structure on
\mathsf{C}
).
The aim of this section is to extend this discussion to arbitrary
rings
R
and the maximal locally coherent exact structure on

\mathsf{C}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
.
This will lead us to a new version of fp-projective periodicity
theorem for modules over an arbitary ring, and more generally,
for objects of an arbitrary locally finitely presentable abelian
category.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a locally
\kappa
-presentable Grothendieck category
(where
\kappa
is a regular cardinal).
Following
[
4
, Remark 4.11]
, we will say that an object

is
\kappa
-p-projective
if
P
is a direct summand
of an object filtered by
\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{C}
.
In the case of the cardinal
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, the term

fp-projective objects
is used
[
4
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a locally
\kappa
-presentable Grothendieck category
and

be a short exact sequence
in
\mathsf{C}
with a
\kappa
-p-projective object

.
Then

is an admissible short
exact sequence in the maximal locally
\kappa
-coherent exact
structure on
\mathsf{C}
.

Proof.

Let
Q\in\mathsf{C}
be an object that the object
P\oplus Q
is filtered
by
\kappa
-presentable ones.
Consider the short sequence
0\longrightarrow P\oplus Q\longrightarrow D\oplus Q\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0
constructed as the direct sum of the short
sequences
0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0
and
0\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow 0
.
If the sequence
0\longrightarrow P\oplus Q\longrightarrow D\oplus Q\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0

is admissible exact, then so is the sequence
0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0
(by the pushout axiom).
Hence we can assume without loss of generality that the object
P

is filtered by
\kappa
-presentable objects.

It suffices to show that the morphism
D\longrightarrow E
is an admissible
epimorphism in the maximal locally
\kappa
-coherent exact structure
on
\mathsf{C}
.
For this purpose, we will apply the criterion provided by
Corollary
3.2
.
Given a
\kappa
-presentable object
S
and a morphism

in
\mathsf{C}
, we need to construct a commutative square diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

with an epimorphism

such that both

and

\ker(T\twoheadrightarrow S)
are
\kappa
-presentable objects
in
\mathsf{C}
.

Denote by

the pullback of the pair of morphisms

and

in
\mathsf{C}
.
Then we have a short exact sequence

A given filtration of the object

-presentable
objects then can be extended to such a filtration of the object

Applying the Hill lemma

, the desired

can be now found as

∎

.

Let

be a locally

It follows from Lemma

∎

.

\mathsf{C}

Remark 8.5
.

The proof of Theorem
8.3
via the proof
of Theorem

, applied in the context
of Example
8.4

confusing, so let us provide some explanation.
Given the module category
\mathsf{C}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
with its maximal locally
coherent exact structure, we consider the full subcategory

\mathsf{E}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod_{fp}--}}}R\subset{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
of finitely presentable
R
-modules,
endowed with the maximal exact structure, which coincides with
the exact structure inherited from the abelian exact structure of

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
(as per Lemma
3.1
).
Then we consider the abelian category
\mathsf{K}
of all left exact

\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}

What category is that?

The category of contravariant functors

to take cokernels

\mathsf{Ab}

Appendix. Generalities on Accessible Categories

Throughout this appendix,
\kappa
is a regular cardinal.
We use the book
[
1
]
as the background reference source on
accessible categories.
In particular, we refer to
[
1
, Definition 1.4, Theorem 1.5,
Corollary 1.5, Definition 1.13(1), and Remark 1.21]
for
an important discussion of
\kappa
-directed posets

vs.
\kappa
-filtered small categories
, and accordingly,

\kappa
-directed vs.
\kappa
-filtered colimits (for a regular
cardinal
\kappa
).

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a category with
\kappa
-directed (equivalently,

\kappa
-filtered) colimits.
An object
S\in\mathsf{A}
is said to be
\kappa
-presentable

if the functor
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(S,{-})\colon\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
preserves

\kappa
-directed colimits.
We will use the notation
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\subset\mathsf{A}
for the full
subcategory of all
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{A}
.
Given a class of objects
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
, we denote by

\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
the class of all

\kappa
-directed colimits of objects from
\mathsf{S}
in
\mathsf{A}
.
In the case of the cardinal
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, we will use
the notation
\varinjlim\mathsf{S}
instead of
\varinjlim_{(\aleph_{0})}\mathsf{S}
.

The category
\mathsf{A}
is said to be

\kappa
-accessible

[
1
, Definition 2.1]
if
there is a set of
\kappa
-presentable objects
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}

such that every object of
\mathsf{A}
is a
\kappa
-directed colimit
of objects from
\mathsf{S}
, i. e.,
\mathsf{A}=\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{S}
.
If this is the case, then the
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{A}

are precisely all the retracts of the objects from
\mathsf{S}
.

In particular,
\aleph_{0}
-presentable objects are known as

finitely presentable

[
1
, Definition 1.1]
, and

\aleph_{0}
-accessible categories are called
finitely
accessible

[
1
, Remark 2.2(1)]
.

The following proposition is well-known.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category and

\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
be a set of
\kappa
-presentable objects.
Then the class of objects
\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{A}

is closed under
\kappa
-directed colimits in
\mathsf{A}
.
An object
B\in\mathsf{A}
belongs to
\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}
if and
only if, for every object
S\in\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
, any morphism
S\longrightarrow B

in
\mathsf{A}
factorizes through an object from
\mathsf{T}
.

The full subcategory
\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{A}
is

\kappa
-accessible, and its
\kappa
-presentable objects are
precisely those objects of
\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}
that are

\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{A}
.
The intersection
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}\cap\varinjlim\mathsf{T}
consists precisely
of all the retracts of objects from
\mathsf{T}
in
\mathsf{A}
.

Assume that the category
\mathsf{A}
is additive and the set of objects

\mathsf{T}
is closed under finite direct sums in
\mathsf{A}
.
Then so is the class of objects
\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}
.
When
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, the class of objects

\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}
is closed under all coproducts in
\mathsf{A}
.

Proof.

In the context of additive categories and
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, this
result, going back to
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]
, can be found
in
[
10
, Section 4.1]
and
[
16
, Proposition 5.11]
.
(The terminology “locally finitely presented categories” was used
in
[
10
,
16
]
for what we call finitely accessible categories.)

The nontrivial part is to prove the “if” implication in the second
assertion: if any morphism
S\longrightarrow B
factorizes through an object
from
\mathsf{T}
, then
B\in\varinjlim_{(\kappa)}\mathsf{T}
.
Here one has to use the assumption that
B
is a
\kappa
-directed
colimit of objects from
\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
in
\mathsf{A}
, and show that
the canonical diagram of morphisms into
B
from objects of
\mathsf{T}
is

\kappa
-filtered and has
B
as the colimit.
We refer to
[
25
, Proposition 1.2]
for further details.
∎

\mathsf{K}
,
\mathsf{L}
, and
\mathsf{M}

It is easy to see that if

\mathsf{K}
,

\mathsf{L}
, and

, and are preserved by the functor

-directed colimits also exist in
\mathsf{C}

\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
and
\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}
.

Proposition A.3
.

Assume that the categories
\mathsf{K}
,
\mathsf{L}
, and
\mathsf{M}

\kappa
-accessible, and the functors

and

\mathsf{C}

\kappa
-accessible, and

\kappa

\kappa

Proof.

This is

Notice that

∎

Consider the comma-category
\mathsf{C}=F\downarrow G
as above, and
denote by
\Pi_{\mathsf{K}}\colon\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
and
\Pi_{\mathsf{L}}\colon\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}

Assume further that we are given a category
\mathsf{N}
and two functors

P\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}
and
Q\colon\mathsf{L}\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}
.
Consider the two compositions of functors
P\Pi_{\mathsf{K}}\colon\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}

and

, and suppose given a pair of parallel

\phi

\psi\colon P\Pi_{\mathsf{K}}\rightrightarrows Q\Pi_{\mathsf{L}}
.

Denote by
\mathsf{E}\subset\mathsf{C}
the full subcategory consisting of all
the objects
C\in\mathsf{C}
such that
\phi_{C}=\psi_{C}
.
We will call the category
\mathsf{E}
the

equations
.

Clearly, if
\kappa

\mathsf{K}
,

,

, and
\mathsf{N}
, and are preserved by the two functors

, then the full subcategory
\mathsf{E}

\kappa
-directed colimits in
\mathsf{C}
.

Theorem A.4
.

Assume that the four categories
\mathsf{K}
,
\mathsf{L}
,
\mathsf{M}
, and
\mathsf{N}
are

\kappa
-accessible, and the four functors
F
,
G
,
P

Q
preserve

\kappa
-directed colimits.
Assume further that the functors
F
and
P
take

\kappa
-presentable objects to
\kappa
-presentable objects.
Then the category
\mathsf{E}
is also
\kappa
-accessible, and
the
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{E}
are precisely all
the triples
(U,V,\upsilon)\in\mathsf{E}\subset\mathsf{C}
where the object
U
is

\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{K}
and the object
V
is

\kappa
-presentable in
\mathsf{L}
.

Proof.

In view of Propositions
A.1

and
A.3
, it suffices to check that, for every
pair of objects
(K,L,\theta)\in\mathsf{E}
and
(S,T,\sigma)\in\mathsf{C}_{<\kappa}
, and any morphism
(f,g)\colon(S,T,\sigma)\longrightarrow(K,L,\theta)
in
\mathsf{C}
, there exists an object

(U,V,\upsilon)\in\mathsf{C}_{<\kappa}\cap\mathsf{E}
such that the morphism
(f,g)

factorizes through the object
(U,V,\upsilon)
in the category
\mathsf{C}
.

Indeed, let
L=\varinjlim_{\xi\in\Xi}V_{\xi}
be a representation of
the object
L\in\mathsf{L}
as a
\kappa
-directed colimit of

\kappa
-presentable objects
V_{\xi}
, indexed by some

\kappa
-directed poset

.
Then, since the object
T\in\mathsf{L}
is
\kappa
-presentable,
there exists an index
\xi_{0}\in\Xi
such that the morphism

factorizes through the morphism
V_{\xi}\longrightarrow L
.

Since
(K,L,\theta)\in\mathsf{E}
and
(f,g)
is a morphism in
\mathsf{C}
,
the two compositions

\textstyle{P(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{(S,T,\sigma)}}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{(S,T,\sigma)}}
\textstyle{Q(T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Q(V_{\xi_{0}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Q(L)}

\mathsf{N}
.
As

\mathsf{N}
and

\kappa
-presentable object in
\mathsf{N}

there exists an index

\textstyle{P(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

A.2.
Finitely presented rigid diagram categories

We start with restating a result going back to
[
7
, Exposé I]
.
Given a small category
\mathsf{D}
and a category
\mathsf{A}
, we denote by

\Fun(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
the category of functors
\mathsf{D}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
.

Proposition A.5
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category and
\mathsf{D}
be
a finite category with no nonidentity endomorphisms of objects.
Then the category
\Fun(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
is
\kappa
-accessible.
The
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\Fun(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
are
the functors
\mathsf{D}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.

Proof.

In the case of
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, this is the result
of
[
7
, Exposé I, Proposition 8.8.5]
or
[
20
, page 55]
.
For an arbitrary regular cardinal
\kappa
, the assertion of
the proposition is a particular case of
[
14
, Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

The aim of this section is to extend the result of
Proposition
A.5
from arbitrary nonadditive
to
k
-linear
functors, where
k
is a commutative ring.
The related assertion is stated as
Proposition
A.6
below.
Both Propositions
A.5

and
A.6
are provable by induction on
the number of objects in the category
\mathsf{D}
using
Theorem
A.4
for
the induction step.
We will spell out the proof of
Proposition
A.6
, as this is the result
we use in the main body of this paper.

A
k
-linear category

\mathsf{A}
is a category enriched in

k
-modules.
So, for any two objects
A
and
B\in\mathsf{A}
, the set
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(A,B)

has a
k
-module structure, and the composition maps are

k
-bilinear.
Let us describe the class of small
k
-linear categories
\mathsf{D}
to
which our result applies.

Suppose given a totally ordered, finite set of objects
\{a\}
.
For every pair of objects
a<b
, suppose given a
generating
set
of morphisms
\Gen(a,b)
.
For
a\geq b
, put
\Gen(a,b)=\varnothing
.
Then, just as in
[
25
, Section 6]
, one can construct
the
k
-linear category
\mathsf{B}
on the given set of objects

freely generated
by the given sets of morphisms.
For every pair of objects
a
,
b
, the free
k
-module

\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(a,b)
is spanned by the set of all formal compositions

g_{n}\dotsm g_{1}
,
n\geq 0
, where
g_{i}\in\Gen(c_{i},c_{i+1})
,

c_{1}=a
,
c_{n+1}=b
.
Notice that one has
\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(a,a)=k
for all objects
a
,
and
\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(a,b)=0
if
a>b
.

Furthermore, suppose given a
set of defining relations

\Rel(a,b)\subset\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(a,b)
for every pair of objects
a<b
.
Let
\mathsf{J}
be the two-sided ideal of morphisms in
\mathsf{B}
generated
by all the sets
\Rel(a,b)
.
Consider the
k
-linear quotient category
\mathsf{D}=\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
of
the
k
-linear category
\mathsf{A}
by the ideal of morphisms
\mathsf{J}
.

We will say that a
k
-linear category
\mathsf{D}
is a
finitely
presented rigid category
if it has the form
\mathsf{D}=\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
as per
the construction above, where the set of all objects
a
,
the set of all generators
\coprod_{a,b}\Gen(a,b)
, and the set
of all relations
\coprod_{a,b}\Rel(a,b)
are all finite.
The word “rigid” here refers to the assumption that all
the generating morphisms go in one direction with respect to
a given total order on the set of objects.

Given a small
k
-linear category
\mathsf{D}
and a
k
-linear
category
\mathsf{A}
, we denote by
\Fun_{k}(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
the
k
-linear
category of
k
-linear functors
\mathsf{D}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
.
The following proposition is a
k
-linear version of
Proposition
A.5
.

Proposition A.6
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-accessible
k
-linear category and

\mathsf{D}
be a finitely presented rigid
k
-linear category.
Then the category
\Fun_{k}(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
is
\kappa
-accessible.
The
\kappa
-presentable objects of
\Fun_{k}(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
are
the
k
-linear functors
\mathsf{D}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}_{<\kappa}
.

Proof.

The argument proceeds by induction on the number of objects in
\mathsf{D}
.
If the set of objects of
\mathsf{D}
is a singleton, then one has

\Fun_{k}(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})\simeq\mathsf{A}
, and there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise, we separate all the objects of
\mathsf{D}
into two nonempty
groups, the upper and the lower one, so that one has
a^{\prime}<a^{\prime\prime}
for
any object
a^{\prime}
from the lower group and any object
a^{\prime\prime}
from
the upper one.
Let
\mathsf{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathsf{D}
be the full subcategories on
the lower and the upper groups of objects in
\mathsf{D}
, respectively.
Then both
\mathsf{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}
are also finitely presented rigid
categories; indeed, the sets of generators and relations of
\mathsf{D}^{\prime}

are just the sets of all generators and relations of
\mathsf{D}
on objects
from
\mathsf{D}^{\prime}
, and similarly for
\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}
.

The claim is that the category
\mathsf{E}=\Fun(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
can be produced
from the categories
\mathsf{K}=\Fun(\mathsf{D}^{\prime},\mathsf{A})
and
\mathsf{L}=\Fun(\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime},\mathsf{A})

by the construction of the comma-category with equations from
Section
A.1
.
Specifically, let
\mathsf{M}
be the product of copies of the category
\mathsf{A}

indexed by the finite set
\coprod_{a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}}\Gen(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
.
Let
F\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{M}
be the functor assigning to a functor

A^{\prime}\colon\mathsf{D}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
the collection of objects whose component indexed
by a generator
g\in\Gen(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
is the object
A^{\prime}(a^{\prime})\in\mathsf{A}
.
Similarly, let
G\colon\mathsf{L}\longrightarrow\mathsf{M}
be the functor assigning to a functor

A^{\prime\prime}\colon\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
the collection of objects whose component indexed
by a generator
g\in\Gen(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
is the object
A^{\prime\prime}(a^{\prime\prime})\in\mathsf{A}
.
Then the comma-category
\mathsf{C}=F\downarrow G
is naturally equivalent
to the category of functors
\Fun_{k}(\widetilde{\mathsf{B}},\mathsf{A})
, where

\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}
is the finitely presented rigid category whose objects
are all the objects of
\mathsf{D}
, generating morphisms are all
the generating morphisms of
\mathsf{D}
, and the set of defining relations is

\coprod_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime}}\Rel(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\sqcup\coprod_{a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}}\Rel(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})
.

Finally, let
\mathsf{N}
be the product of copies of the category
\mathsf{A}

indexed by the finite set
\coprod_{a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}}\Rel(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
.
Let
P\colon\mathsf{K}\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}
be the functor assigning to a functor

A^{\prime}\colon\mathsf{D}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
the collection of objects whose component indexed
by a relation
r\in\Rel(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
is the object
A^{\prime}(a^{\prime})\in\mathsf{A}
.
Similarly, let
Q\colon\mathsf{L}\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}
be the functor assigning to a functor

A^{\prime\prime}\colon\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
the collection of objects whose component indexed
by a relation
r\in\Rel(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
is the object
A^{\prime\prime}(a^{\prime\prime})\in\mathsf{A}
.
Let
\phi\colon P\Pi_{\mathsf{K}}\longrightarrow Q\Pi_{\mathsf{L}}
be the natural transformation
assigning to a functor
A\in\mathsf{C}=\Fun_{k}(\widetilde{\mathsf{B}},\mathsf{A})
the morphism
in the category
\mathsf{N}
whose component indexed by a relation

r\in\Rel(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
is the morphism
A(r)\colon A(a^{\prime})\longrightarrow A(a^{\prime\prime})
for
all
a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime}
,
a^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\prime\prime}
.
Let
\psi\colon P\Pi_{\mathsf{K}}\longrightarrow Q\Pi_{\mathsf{L}}
be the zero natural
transformation.
Then the comma-category with relations
\mathsf{E}
is naturally equivalent
to the category of functors
\Fun_{k}(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
.

The induction assumption tells us that the categories
\mathsf{K}
and
\mathsf{L}

are
\kappa
-accessible, and provides descriptions of their full
subcategories of
\kappa
-presentable objects.
Lemma
A.2
tells us that the categories
\mathsf{M}
and

\mathsf{N}
are
\kappa
-accessible, and provides descriptions of their
full subcategories of
\kappa
-presentable objects.
So Theorem
A.4
is applicable,
proving that the category
\mathsf{E}=\Fun_{k}(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{A})
is
\kappa
-accessible,
and providing the desired description of its full subcategory of

\kappa
-presentable objects.
∎

Abstract
In this paper, we mainly study subtransversality and two types of strong CHIP (given via Fréchet and limiting normal cones) for a collection of finitely many closed sets. We first prove characterizations of Asplund spaces in terms of subtransversality and intersection formulae of Fréchet normal cones. Several necessary conditions for subtransversality of closed sets are obtained via Fréchet/limiting normal cones in Asplund spaces. Then, we consider subtransversality for some special closed sets in convex-composite optimization. In this frame we prove an equivalence result on subtransversality, strong Fréchet CHIP and property (G) so as to extend a duality characterization of subtransversality of finitely many closed convex sets via strong CHIP and property (G) to the possibly non-convex case. As applications, we use these results on subtransversality and strong CHIP to study error bounds of inequality systems and give several dual criteria for error bounds via Fréchet normal cones and subdifferentials.

1.1
Definitions, notations and preliminary material

Let us begin with some notations and definitions. Throughout the text, the notation employed is fairly standard. Let
\mathbb{X}
be a real Banach space and
\mathbb{X}^{\ast}
the dual space of continuous linear functionals on
\mathbb{X}
. We use the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to indicate the duality pairing between
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{X}^{\ast}
.
As usual,
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\|x\|\leq 1\}
stands for the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{X}
and for
x\in\mathbb{X},

r>0,

\mathbf{B}(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{X}:\|x-y\|<r\}
refers to the open ball centered at
x
with radius
r
, respectively. If
x\in\mathbb{X}
, we define the distance from
x
to a subset

C
of
\mathbb{X}
by

\mathbf{d}(x,C):=\inf_{y\in C}\|x-y\|.

By a cone we mean a nonempty set in
\mathbb{X}
which is closed under nonnegative scalar multiplication. Given
a nonempty set
S\subseteq\mathbb{X}
, the notation
\mathop{\rm cone\,}S
is used for the conical hull of
S
, i.e., the cone generated by
S
. The set
\mathop{\rm cone\,}S
is convex whenever
S
is convex.
Suppose
S
is a nonempty set in
\mathbb{X}
. Then the negative polar cone of
S,
written
S^{0}
is defined by
\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{X^{\ast}}:\langle x^{\ast},x\rangle\leq 0\quad\text{for every }x\in S\}
.
The symbol
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\longrightarrow}}
stands for the weak
∗
convergence and the symbol
y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\longrightarrow}}x
means that
y
tends to
x
while staying in a given subset
A
of
\mathbb{X}
.
Let
A
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{X}
and
x\in A
. For
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we denote by

\hat{N}_{\varepsilon}(A,x):=\left\{x^{\ast}\in X^{\ast}:\limsup\limits_{y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}x}\frac{\langle x^{\ast},y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\leq\varepsilon\right\}

the set of
\varepsilon
-
normals
to
A
at
x
. When
\varepsilon=0
, we will write
\hat{N}(A,x)
instead of
\hat{N}_{0}(A,x)
. The set
\hat{N}(A,x)
is a closed convex cone called the
Fréchet normal cone to
A
at
x
.

Given a multifunction
\Phi:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}^{\ast}
, the symbol

\begin{array}[]{r}\mathop{\rm Limsup}\limits_{y\rightarrow x}\Phi(x):=\Big\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast}:\exists\ {\rm sequences}\ x_{n}\rightarrow x\ {\rm and}\ x_{n}^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\longrightarrow}}x^{\ast}\ {\rm with}\\
x_{n}^{\ast}\in\Phi(x_{n})\ {\rm for\ all\ }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}\end{array}

signifies the sequential Painlevé-Kuratowski outer/upper limit of
\Phi(x)
as
y\rightarrow x
.
The
limiting (Mordukhovich) normal cone
of
A
at
x
is defined by

N(A,x):=\mathop{\rm Limsup}_{y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}x,\,\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\hat{N}_{\varepsilon}(A,y).

Thus
N(A,x)
is the set of those
x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast}
such that
there exists a sequence
\{(x_{n},\varepsilon_{n},x_{n}^{\ast})\}
in
A\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}^{\ast}
such that
(x_{n},\varepsilon_{n})\rightarrow(x,0)
,
x_{n}^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{\ast}
and
x^{\ast}_{n}\in\hat{N}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(A,x_{n})
for each
n
. It is known from
Mordukhovich
that

\hat{N}(A,x)\subseteq N(A,x)
with an equality when
A
is convex. In this case these cones coincide with the normal cone (in the sense of the classical convex analysis):

\hat{N}(A,a)=N(A,a)=\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast}:\;\langle x^{\ast},x-a\rangle\leq 0\;\;{\rm for\ all}\ x\in A\}.

Recall that a Banach space is Asplund if every continuous convex function on
\mathbb{X}
is Fréchet differentiable at each point of a dense subset of
\mathbb{X}
(see
Phelps
;
Yost93
for definitions and their equivalences). An important fact should be underlined:
any Banach space, with an equivalent norm which is Fréchet differentiable off zero, is Asplund. This property implies that every reflexive Banach space is Asplund since any such space admits an equivalent Fréchet differentiable norm off zero (see
(
thibault
, Definition 2.2.3)
).

Another important property of Asplund spaces is the sequential representation of the limiting normal cone established by Mordukhovich and Shao
MS
:

N(A,a)=\mathop{\rm Limsup}_{y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}x}\hat{N}(A,y).

Equivalently,
x^{\ast}\in N(A,x)
if and only if there exist sequences
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{x_{n}^{\ast}\}
such that
x_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}x
,
x^{\ast}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{\ast}
and
x_{n}^{\ast}\in\hat{N}(A,x_{n})
for all
n
.

Let
f:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous extended real-valued function. We denote as usual by

\mathop{\rm dom\,}f:=\{y\in\mathbb{X}:f(y)<+\infty\}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathop{\rm Epi\,}f:=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}:f(x)\leq\alpha\}

the
domain
and the
epigraph
of
f
, respectively. The
Fréchet
and
limiting
subdifferentials of
f
at
x\in\mathop{\rm dom\,}f
are the subsets of
\mathbb{X}^{\ast}
which are respectively defined by

\hat{\partial}f(x):=\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast}:(x^{\ast},-1)\in\hat{N}(\mathop{\rm Epi\,}f,(x,f(x)))\}

and

\partial f(x):=\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast}:(x^{\ast},-1)\in N(\mathop{\rm Epi\,}f,(x,f(x)))\}.

Equivalently,

\hat{\partial}f(x)=\left\{x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast}:\liminf_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{f(y)-f(x)-\langle x^{\ast},y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0\right\},

and when
\mathbb{X}
is Asplund, Mordukhovich and Shao

have proved that

\partial f(x)=\mathop{\rm Limsup}_{y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}x}\hat{\partial}f(y),

where
y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}x
means that
y\rightarrow x
and
f(y)\rightarrow f(x)
. Thus
x^{\ast}\in\partial f(x)
if and only if there exist
x_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}x
and
x^{\ast}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{\ast}
such that
x_{n}^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}f(x_{n})
for all
n
.

1.2
A brief survey on the notions of convex feasibility problem, strong conical hull intersection property
(strong CHIP) and property (G), subtransversality.

The concepts of
subtransversality, strong conical hull intersection property
(strong CHIP) and property (G)
for a collection of closed convex sets have played important roles in various branches of approximation theory and optimization. They are used in the convex feasibility problem, constrained approximation, Fenchel duality, systems of convex inequalities and error bounds (see
BDL2005
;
BBL
;
BBT
;
HW
;
LJ
;
LN
;
LN1
;
LN2
;
LNP
;
WH
and references therein).

Let us briefly survey these concepts.

Convex feasibility problem (CFP)

The
convex feasibility problem
is the problem of finding a common point in a collection of finitely many closed convex intersecting sets (when this intersection is nonempty). This general formulation is of practical interest in diverse areas of mathematics and physical sciences and is therefore central in various applications such as for instance,
solution of convex inequalities, minimization of convex non-smooth functions, medical imaging, computerized tomography and electron microscopy. CFP often solved iteratively by projection algorithms, has been the subject of extensive research to ensure a better norm convergence of the sequences produced by projection-based algorithms (cf.
BB
;
BB1
;
BBL97
;
KL1997
). We refer the reader to the survey of Bauschke & Borwein
BB1
or to the contribution of Combettes
Combettes96
and the references therein. In particular Combettes
Combettes96
gives a concrete application to the image recovery problem. CFP has also a connection with the theory of error bounds.

Conical Hull Intersection Property (CHIP)

Chui, Deutsch and Ward
CDW1
;
CDW2
introduced the concept of
conical hull intersection property
(CHIP) when studying the problem of constrained interpolation from a convex subset or cone. Given a finite family of closed convex sets
\{C_{1},\cdots,C_{m}\}
of
\mathbb{X}
such that
C:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}\neq\emptyset
, we say that the CHIP holds at
x\in C
if

T(C,x)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}T(C_{i},x),

where
T(C,x)
, the closure of
\mathop{\rm cone\,}(C-x)
, stands for the
tangent cone
to
C
at
x
. Note that
T(C,x)=N(C,x)^{\circ}:=\{h\in\mathbb{X}:\langle x^{*},h\rangle\leq 0,\ \forall x^{*}\in N(C,x)\}
.

Strong Conical Hull Intersection Property (Strong CHIP)

It is well-known that important methods to solve the CFP in Hilbert spaces are based on some projection-based algorithms (including serial methods, simultaneous methods, extrapolated relaxation method, and subgradient methods) and usually the sequences obtained from these algorithms have the weak-convergence property, not strong-convergence in general. For ensuring a better convergence property, Deutsch, Li and Ward
DeuLiWar99
introduced strong CHIP, a stronger version of CHIP, to study an unconstrained reformulation of the constrained best approximation. Given a finite family of closed convex sets
\{C_{1},\cdots,C_{m}\}
of
\mathbb{X}
such that
C:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}\neq\emptyset
, we say that the strong CHIP holds at
x\in C
if

N(C,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}N(C_{i},x).

It is known that the pair
(C,D)
of closed and convex subsets of a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
has the strong CHIP if and only if the sum rule formula applies for the indicator functions
i_{C}
and
i_{D}
of
C
and
D
, where
i_{C}(x)=0
if
x\in C
and
+\infty
otherwise.
According to a classical result due to Moreau
moreau
, the strong CHIP is also equivalent to saying that the infimal convolution of their support functionals is exact.
Later, it was proved by Deutsch
D
that strong CHIP is a geometric version of the basic constraint qualification for constrained optimization problems. Deutsch, Li and Ward
DeuLiWar99
further proved that strong CHIP is necessary and sufficient for some “perturbation property”. Deutsch, Li and Swetits
DLS
showed that strong CHIP is an essential property when establishing duality relations between certain convex optimization problems. Jeyakumar further
J1
proved that strong CHIP is equivalent to a complete Lagrange multiplier characterization of optimality for convex programming model problems. Ernst and Théra
ET2007
considered strong CHIP for the pair of closed and convex sets, and used the strong CHIP to prove that the validity of the converse of Moreau’s theorem is associated with the absence of half-strips within the boundary of the concerned convex sets.
Let us point out an important fact: if strong CHIP holds, then when minimizing a convex function
f
over the intersection of two convex sets
C
and
D
, the first order optimality condition becomes

\ 0\in\partial f(x)+N(C,x)+N(D,x).

Deutsch proved in
Deutsch98
that strong CHIP is the weakest constraint qualification under which a minimizer of a convex function
f:C\cap D\to\mathbb{R}
can be characterized using the subdifferential of
f
at
x
and the normal cones of
C
and
D
at
x
.

Property (G)

In the early 1970s, Jameson
J
introduced the so-called property (G). This property provides a sufficient condition for the sum of two closed convex cones
C
and
D
to be closed and allows to establish a duality theory for two closed convex cones. Given a finite family of closed convex cones
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{m}\}
of
\mathbb{X}
, we say that
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{m}\}
has property (G) if there exists
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}P_{i}\Big)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}\subseteq\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}(P_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}).

See
BZ2005
.

Subtransversality

Given a collection of finitely many closed sets
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
, recall that the collection
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is said to be
subtransversal
at
\bar{x}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
if there exists
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{d}\Big(x,\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\Big)\leq\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(x,A_{i})\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ x\ {\rm close\ to}\ \bar{x}.

(1.1)

Subtransversality
is a well-known notion in mathematical programming and approximation theory. It is known from
(
Ioffe2017
, 7.1.3 Comments)
that inequality (
1.1
) was in reality introduced by Dolecki in a very different context (see
Dolecki1982
). This property later appeared in
Ioffe1989
as a qualification condition in the calculus of normal cones and was called a “metric qualification condition” in
IP1996
; it appears also as “linear coherence”
in
(
Penot2013
, Theorem 4.7.5)
. For convex sets, the inequality was also introduced in
BB
under the name “linear regularity”. Subsequently, Bauschke and Borwein
BB1
used this notion to study the problem of finding the projection from a given point to the intersection of finitely many closed convex sets, and proved that subtransversality plays a key role in establishing a linear convergence rate of the iterates generated by the cyclic projection algorithm. We refer the reader to
KLT2017
;
KLT2018
;
BCK2020
for a survey of the terminology and a comparison with similar or related concepts available in the literature. Since it is closely linked to many optimization issues, subtransversality has been extensively studied by many authors (cf.
BDL2005
;
BB
;
BBT
;
LP
;
LNP
;
W
;
WZ
and references therein).

Metric Subregularity

Let
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
be a set-valued mapping between two Banach spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
. Recall that
F
is said to be
metrically subregular
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}\in F(\bar{x})
, if there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq\kappa\mathbf{d}(\bar{y},F(x))\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ x\ {\rm close\ to}\ \bar{x}.

Metric subregularity has been thoroughly studied in various contexts in variational analysis and has been the object of a very active area of research in both theory and applications (see
DonRoc09
;
IO2008
and
Kru15
;
Penot2013
for its relationship with the theory of error bounds).

Using the
\ell^{1}
-norm on
\mathbb{X}^{n}
, let us consider the set-valued mapping
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}^{n}
defined as follows:

F(x):=(A_{1}-x)\times(A_{2}-x)\times\cdots\times(A_{n}-x).

We easily observe that

\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}=F^{-1}(0)=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:0\in F(x)\},

\mathbf{d}\Big(x,\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\Big)=\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(0))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{d}(0,F(x))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{d}(x,A_{i}).

Then subtransversality which satisfies (
1.1
) is equivalent to the metric subregularity of
F
at
(0,\bar{x})
. It should be noted that this equivalence also holds even if the convexity assumption is dropped. We refer to
NT2004
for details and results. Such equivalence was also given in
(
KLT2017
, Proposition 1)
and
(
KLT2018
, Theorem 1)
.

For closed convex cones, subtransversality is equivalent to property (G) (see
(
BBT
, Fact 2.11)
).
In 1996, Bauschke and Borwein
BB1
considered subtransversality when solving convex feasibility problems. Afterwards Pang
P
and Lewis and Pang
LP
established dual conditions for subtransversality of finitely many closed convex sets. In 1999, using convex analysis techniques, Bauschke, Borwein, and Li
BBL
discussed the close relationship among subtransversality, strong CHIP and property (G) for a collection of finitely many closed convex sets in Euclidean spaces. In 2005, Bakan, Deutsch and Li
BDL2005
used various normal properties to study property (G), strong CHIP and subtransversality for a finite collection of convex sets in Hilbert spaces. The normal property, property (G), strong CHIP and subtransversality for an infinite collection of closed convex sets in a Banach space was further considered in
LN2
and
WH
. The following well-established theorem is a relevant result on subtransversality, strong CHIP and property (G) for a collection of closed convex sets (cf.
BDL2005
;
NY
;
ZN2004
;
LN2
;
WH
):

Theorem A.

Let
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
be a collection of closed convex sets in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
such that
A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
is nonempty. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in A
.

(ii)

There exist
\eta,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that for any
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
,
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong CHIP at
x
and

\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x_{i}^{\ast}\|:x_{i}^{\ast}\in N(A_{i},x)\ {\it and}\ x^{\ast}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{\ast}\right\}\leq\eta\|x^{\ast}\|

holds for all
x^{\ast}\in N\left(A,x\right)
.

(iii)

There exists
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that for any
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
,
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong CHIP at
x
and
\{N(A_{1},x),\cdots,N(A_{n},x)\}
has property (G) with constant
\tau
.

This important theorem shows the equivalence relation among subtransversality, strong CHIP and property (G). A natural question that arises is whether similar results still hold for general closed sets. More precisely, whether subtransversality could also be characterized by strong CHIP and property (G) in the absence of convexity assumption?
Unfortunately, the answer to this question is negative once the convexity assumption is dropped (see
Remark

3
(b) below). The first difficulty to deal with the question under the absence of convexity is to choose one suitable kind of normal cones and then consider the corresponding concepts of strong CHIP and property (G) in terms of such normal cones. Along this line, our goal is to deepen the relation between subtransversality, strong CHIP and property (G) for a collection of a finite number of closed sets in Asplund spaces.

1.3
Organization of the paper

This paper is organized as follows. Several preliminaries and known
results will be given in Section 2. In Section 3, we first prove that a Banach space is Asplund if and only if the intersection formulae of Fréchet normal cones for every nonempty closed sets is implied by subtransversality. Several necessary conditions for subtransversality of closed sets are given via Fréchet/limiting normal cones.
Then we study subtransversality for some special closed sets in convex-composite optimization and prove that subtransversality is equivalent to the strong Fréchet CHIP and property (G) when
\mathbb{X}
is Asplund (see
Theorem

3.4
). In Section 4 we apply the results obtained in Section 3 to the study of error bounds of inequality systems. Conclusions of this paper are presented in Section 5.

In what follows, given a collection of finitely many closed sets, we consider the notions of strong Fréchet and limiting CHIP, respectively.

Definition 1

Let
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
be a collection of finitely many closed sets in
\mathbb{X}
such that
A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
is nonempty, and let
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{m}\}
be a collection of finitely many closed cones in
\mathbb{X}^{\ast}
. Recall that

(i)

\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is said to have the strong Fréchet conical hull intersection property (
strong Fréchet CHIP)
at
x\in A
if

\hat{N}(A,x)\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x)

(2.1)

and
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is said to have the strong Fréchet CHIP if
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong Fréchet CHIP at all points of
A
;

(ii)

\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is said to have the
strong limiting CHIP
at
x\in A
if

N(A,x)\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N(A_{i},x)

and
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is said to have the strong limiting CHIP if
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong limiting CHIP at all points of
A
.

(iii)

\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{m}\}
is said to have the
property (
G
)
if there exists
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}P_{i}\Big)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subseteq\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}(P_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}).

Remark 1

(i)

Since
\hat{N}(A,x)
is always a convex cone and contains
\hat{N}(A_{i},x)
for each
i
, the requirement (
2.1
) for the strong Fréchet CHIP is equivalent to

\hat{N}(A,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x).

(ii)

We note that the strong Fréchet/limiting CHIP refers to calculus of Fréchet/limiting normal cones (cf.
(
Mordukhovich
, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.4)
). For the case when
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is a collection of finitely many closed convex sets, the notions of strong Fréchet CHIP and strong limiting CHIP coincide and reduce to the strong CHIP studied in the convex optimization.

We conclude this section with the following example to show that strong Fréchet CHIP and strong limiting CHIP for non-convex closed sets could be valid.

Example 1

(a)

Let
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\bar{x}:=(0,0)\in\mathbb{X}
,
A_{1}:=\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\mathbb{R}_{-}
and
A_{2}:={\rm cl}(\mathbb{X}\backslash A_{1})
. Then

A_{1}\cap A_{2}=(\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{-}).

Then
\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})=\hat{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\hat{N}(A_{2},\bar{x})=\{(0,0)\}
and thus
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
has the strong Fréchet CHIP at
\bar{x}
.

(b)

Let
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\bar{x}:=(0,0)\in\mathbb{X}
,
A_{1}:=(\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\cup\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:s+t=0\}
and

A_{2}:=\left\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:(s-1)^{2}+t^{2}\leq 1\ \ {\rm and}\ \ s^{2}+(t-1)^{2}\leq 1\right\}.

Then
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\{\bar{x}\}
and by computation, one has
\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})=N(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{X}
,

\hat{N}(A_{2},\bar{x})=N(A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\mathbb{R}_{-}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \hat{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})=\{(0,0)\},

which means that strong Fréchet CHIP of
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
at
\bar{x}
is violated. However,

N(A_{1},\bar{x})=([0,+\infty)\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times(-\infty,0])\cup\{(t,t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

This implies that
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
has the strong limiting CHIP at
\bar{x}
.

3
Subtransversality and Strong CHIP of Closed Sets in
an
Asplund Space

In this section, we mainly study subtransversality for a collection of finitely many closed sets in Asplund spaces. We aim to provide necessary and/or sufficient conditions for subtransversality via strong Fréchet/limiting CHIP and property (G). We begin with the definition of subtransversality.

Let
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
be a collection of finitely many closed sets in
\mathbb{X}
such that the intersection
A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
is nonempty. Recall that the collection
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is said to be subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in A
if there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,A)\leq\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(x,A_{i})\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r).

(3.1)

We first prove the following result on necessary conditions for subtransversality of finitely many closed sets in an Asplund space.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
be a collection of finitely many closed sets in an Asplund space
\mathbb{X}
such that
A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
is nonempty. Suppose that
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in A
. Then there exist
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
, one has

\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subset\bigcup\Big\{\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i})\cap(1+\varepsilon)\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}:x_{i}\in A_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}(x,\varepsilon),i=1,\cdots,m\Big\}+\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

(3.2)

holds for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap A
.

Proof.
Since
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
, then there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.1
) holds. Let
\delta:=\frac{r}{2}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Take any
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap A
and
x^{\ast}\in\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}
. Choose
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that
(m+4+\tau m)\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon
. Note that
x^{\ast}\in\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}=\hat{\partial}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,A)(x)
and thus there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that

\langle x^{\ast},u-x\rangle\leq\mathbf{d}(u,A)+\varepsilon_{1}\|u-x\|\ \ \forall u\in\mathbf{B}(x,\delta_{1}).

By (
3.1
), one has

\langle{x}^{\ast},u-x\rangle\leq\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(u,A_{i})+\varepsilon_{1}\|u-x\|\ \ \forall u\in\mathbf{B}(x,\delta_{1}).

This implies that

x^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\Big(\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,A_{i})+\varepsilon_{1}\|\cdot-x\|\Big)(x).

By virtue of
Lemmas

1
and
3
, there exist
u_{i}\in\mathbf{B}(x,\varepsilon_{1})
,
u_{i}^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,A_{i})(u_{i})
,
x_{i}\in A_{i}
and
x^{\ast}_{i}\in\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i})(1\leq i\leq m)
such that

\|x_{i}-u_{i}\|<\mathbf{d}(u_{i},A_{i})+\varepsilon_{1},\|x_{i}^{\ast}-u_{i}^{\ast}\|<\varepsilon_{1}

(3.3)

and

x^{\ast}\in\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}^{\ast}+\varepsilon_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}+m\varepsilon_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subset\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{\ast}+(m+1+\tau m)\varepsilon_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}.

(3.4)

For any
i
, one has
\|u_{i}^{\ast}\|\leq 1
and thus
\|x_{i}^{\ast}\|\leq 1+\varepsilon_{1}<1+\varepsilon
by (
3.3
). This and (
3.4
) imply that

x^{\ast}\in\big(\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i})\cap(1+\varepsilon)\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\big)+\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}.

(thanks to the choice of
\varepsilon_{1}
). Since
\|x_{i}-x\|\leq\|x_{i}-u_{i}\|+\|u_{i}-x\|<3\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon
, it follows that (
3.2
) holds. The proof is complete.
\Box

It is known from
(
NT2001
, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2)
that necessary or sufficient conditions via the Fréchet subdifferential of
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,A_{1})
are provided to ensure the subtransversality (under the name of
metric inequality
therein). Further, Ngai and Théra
(
NT2001
, Theorem 3.8)
gave characterizations of Asplund spaces in terms of conditions ensuring the subtransversality and the intersection formulae. In particular, the result is presented as follows:

Theorem B.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space;

(ii)

for every nonempty closed subsets
A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}
of
\mathbb{X}
, all but one of which are sequentially normally compact at
\bar{x}\in A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{m}
and satisfying the following condition:

x_{i}^{\ast}\in N(A_{i},\bar{x}),i=1,\cdots,m\ \ \&\ \ x_{1}^{\ast}+\cdots+x_{m}^{\ast}=0\Rightarrow x_{1}^{\ast}=\cdots=x_{m}^{\ast}=0,

then
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
;

(iii)

for every nonempty closed subsets
A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}
of
\mathbb{X}
being subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{m}
,
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong limiting CHIP at
\bar{x}
.

Similar to Theorem B, we prove the following theorem on characterizations of Asplund spaces given by the subtransversality and intersection formulae of Fréchet normal cones. This theorem also provides necessary conditions in terms of Fréchet normal cones for the subtransversality of finitely many closed sets in an Asplund space.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space;

(ii)

for every family
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
of nonempty closed subsets of
\mathbb{X}
which is subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{m}
, there exists
\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
, the inclusion

\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{m},x)\subset\bigcup\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i}):x_{i}\in A_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}(x,\varepsilon),i=1,\cdots,m\Big\}+\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}

(3.5)

holds for all
x\in A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{m}\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
;

(iii)

for every family
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
of nonempty closed subsets of
\mathbb{X}
which is subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{m}

and for any
\varepsilon>0
, one has that (
3.5
) holds for
x=\bar{x}
.

Proof.
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
be a collection of closed sets in
\mathbb{X}
and denote
A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
. Suppose that the collection
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
. Then there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.1
) holds. Let
\delta:=\frac{r}{2}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Take any
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
.
Let
x^{\ast}\in\hat{N}(A,x)\backslash\{0\}
. Choose
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that
\|x^{\ast}\|\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon
. By virtue of
Theorem

3.1
as well as its proof, one has

\frac{x^{\ast}}{\|x^{\ast}\|}\in\bigcup\Big\{\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i})\cap(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}:x_{i}\in A_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}(x,\varepsilon_{1}),i=1,\cdots,m\Big\}+\varepsilon_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}.

This and the choice of
\varepsilon_{1}
imply that

\displaystyle x^{*}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\bigcup\Big\{\tau\|x^{*}\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i})\cap(1+\varepsilon_{1})\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}:x_{i}\in A_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}(x,\varepsilon_{1}),i=1,\cdots,m\Big\}+\|x^{*}\|\varepsilon_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\bigcup\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i}):x_{i}\in A_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}(x,\varepsilon),i=1,\cdots,m\Big\}+\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

and consequently (
3.5
) holds.

Note that (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) follows immediately and it remains to prove (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i).

Suppose on the contrary that
\mathbb{X}
fails to be Asplund. Following
MW2000
;
FM1998
, we can represent
\mathbb{X}
in the form
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}
with the norm
\|(y,\alpha)\|:=\|y\|+|\alpha|
for any
x=(y,\alpha)\in\mathbb{X}
. Then
\mathbb{Y}
is not an Asplund space. By virtue of
(
DGZ1993
, Theorem 1.5.3)
(also
(
FM1998
, Theorem 2.1)
), there exists an equivalent norm
|||\cdot|||
on
\mathbb{Y}
and
\gamma>0
such that

\frac{1}{2}\|y\|\leq|||y|||\leq\|y\|\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{|||y+h|||+|||y-h|||-2|||y|||}{\|h\|}>\gamma,\ \forall y\in\mathbb{Y}.

(3.6)

Define
f:\mathbb{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as
f(y):=-|||y|||,\forall y\in\mathbb{Y}
and let

A_{1}:=\{0_{\mathbb{Y}}\}\times(-\infty,0],A_{2}:=\mathop{\rm Epi\,}f\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \bar{x}:=(0_{\mathbb{Y}},0).

We first prove that

\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1}\cap A_{2})\leq 2(\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1})+\mathbf{d}(x,A_{2})),\ \ \forall x=(y,\alpha)\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R},

(3.7)

which means that
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
is substransversal.

Indeed, for any
x=(y,\alpha)\in\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}
, one has

\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1}\cap A_{2})=\|y\|+|\alpha|\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathbf{d}(x,A_{1})=\left\{\begin{aligned} \|y\|,\ \ \ &\alpha<0,\\
\|y\|+\alpha,\ &\alpha\geq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.8)

If
\alpha\geq 0
, then (
3.7
) holds since
\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1}\cap A_{2})=\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1})
.

If
-|||y|||\leq\alpha<0
, one has

\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1}\cap A_{2})=\|y\|+|\alpha|\leq 2\|y\|=2\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1})

and thus (
3.7
) holds.

If
\alpha<-|||y|||
, then for any
(z,\mu)\in A_{2}
, one has
-|||z|||\leq\mu
and

\displaystyle-|||y|||-\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-|||y|||-\alpha+|||z|||+\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big||||y|||-|||z|||\big|+|\alpha-\mu|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|||y-z|||+|\alpha-\mu|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|y-z\|+|\alpha-\mu|,

the last inequality following by (
3.6
). This implies that

-|||y|||-\alpha\leq\mathbf{d}(x,A_{2})

and then (
3.6
) and (
3.8
) give

\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1}\cap A_{2})=\|y\|-\alpha=\|y\|+|||y|||-|||y|||-\alpha\leq 2\|y\|+\mathbf{d}(x,A_{2}).

Hence (
3.7
) holds.

Next, we show that for any
\epsilon>0
, one has

\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})\not\subseteq\bigcup\Big\{\hat{N}(A_{1},x_{1})+\hat{N}(A_{2},x_{2}):x_{i}\in A_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon),\ i=1,2\Big\}+\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}},

(3.9)

which contradicts (iii).

Note that
\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}
. Take any
\epsilon>0
. For any
x_{1}=(y_{1},\alpha_{1})\in A_{1}\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\epsilon)
, one has
y_{1}=0_{\mathbb{Y}}
and

\hat{N}(A_{1},x_{1})=\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbb{Y}^{\ast}\times\{0\},\ \ \ \ \ \ &\alpha_{1}<0,\\
\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}\times[0,+\infty),\ &\alpha_{1}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Claim
. Let
x_{2}=(y_{2},\alpha_{2})\in A_{2}\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\epsilon)
. Then

\hat{N}(A_{2},x_{2})=\{(0,0)\}.

(3.10)

Let
(y^{\ast},-\lambda)\in\hat{N}(A_{2},x_{2})
. Suppose on the contrary that
\lambda\not=0
. Applying
Lemma

2
, we have
\lambda>0
and
\frac{y^{\ast}}{\lambda}\in\hat{\partial}f(y_{2})
. Thus,

\liminf_{\|h\|\rightarrow 0}\frac{-|||y_{2}+h|||+|||y_{2}|||-\langle\frac{y^{\ast}}{\lambda},h\rangle}{\|h\|}\geq 0.

Hence

\limsup_{\|h\|\rightarrow 0}\frac{|||y_{2}+h|||+|||y_{2}-h|||-2|||y_{2}|||}{\|h\|}\leq 0,

which is a contradiction with (
3.6
). The claim is established.

By the definition, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\langle y^{\ast},y-y_{2}\rangle\leq\epsilon(\|y-y_{2}\|+|\alpha-\alpha_{2}|),\ \ \forall(y,\alpha)\in(y_{2},\alpha_{2})+\delta\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{R}}\cap A_{2}.

For each
y\in y_{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}}
, take
\alpha:=-|||y|||+|||y_{2}|||+\alpha_{2}
. Then one has

\|(y,\alpha)-(y_{2},\alpha_{2})\|=\|y-y_{2}\|+|\alpha-\alpha_{2}|\leq 2\|y-y_{2}\|

and from (
3.6
) we get that

\displaystyle\langle y^{\ast},y-y_{2}\rangle\leq\epsilon(\|y-y_{2}\|+|\alpha-\alpha_{2}|)\leq 2\epsilon\|y-y_{2}\|.

This implies that
y^{\ast}=0
and thus (
3.10
) holds.

Noticing that
\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subseteq\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]
, this yields

\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})\not\subset\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}\times[0,+\infty)+\{(0,0)\}+\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{X^{\ast}},

and therefore it follows that (
3.9
) holds.
The proof is complete.
\Box

Remark 2

It would be ideal if in (iii) of
Theorem

3.2
,
\epsilon
could be taken as
0
as this consequence would imply the strong Fréchet CHIP of
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
at
\bar{x}
. However, the following example shows that such ideal case may not hold even in an Euclidean space.

Example 2

Let
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
A_{1}:=\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}\cup\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{X}:x_{1}=x_{2}\}
,
A_{2}:=\{0\}\times(-\infty,0]
and
\bar{x}:=(0,0)
. Then
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\{\bar{x}\}
and one can verify that
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
. However, by the computation, one has

\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{2},\hat{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})=\{(0,0)\}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \hat{N}(A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,0],

which implies that

\hat{N}(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})\not\subseteq\hat{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})+\hat{N}(A_{2},\bar{x}).

Hence
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
does not have the strong Fréchet CHIP at
\bar{x}
.

In the remainder of this paper, unless otherwise stated, we suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is Asplund and

\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}

is a collection of finitely many closed sets in
\mathbb{X}
such that the intersection is nonempty
.

We are now in a position to consider circumstances when one can pass to the limit in (
3.2
) as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and thus obtain necessary conditions for subtransversality of
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
in terms of limiting normal cones. The following theorem shows that it can be done in the finite-dimensional setting.

Theorem 3.3

Let
\bar{x}\in A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is finite-dimensional and
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
. Then there exist
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that

N(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subseteq\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}N(A_{i},x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

(3.11)

holds for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap A
.

Proof.
Since
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
, then there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.1
) holds. Let
\delta:=\frac{r}{2}
and
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
. Take any
x^{\ast}\in N(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}
. Since
\mathbb{X}
is finite-dimensional, it follows that there exist
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}x
and
x^{\ast}_{k}\rightarrow x^{\ast}
such that
x^{\ast}_{k}\in\hat{N}(A,x_{k})
for all
k
. For any
k
, set
\widetilde{x}^{\ast}_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}^{\ast}}{\|x_{k}^{\ast}\|}
. Similarly to the proof of (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) in
Theorem

3.2
, one can get

\widetilde{x}_{k}^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\Big(\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,A_{i})+\frac{1}{k}\|\cdot-x_{k}\|\Big)(x_{k}).

By using
Lemmas

1
and
3
, there are
u_{i,k}\in\mathbf{B}(x_{k},\frac{1}{k})
,
u_{i,k}^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,A_{i})(u_{i,k})
,
x_{i,k}\in A_{i}
and
x_{i,k}^{\ast}\in\hat{N}(A_{i},x_{i,k}),i=1,\cdots,m,
such that

\|x_{i,k}-u_{i,k}\|<\mathbf{d}(u_{i,k},A_{i})+\frac{1}{k},\ \ \|x^{\ast}_{i,k}-u^{\ast}_{i,k}\|<\frac{1}{k}

(3.12)

and

\widetilde{x}^{\ast}_{k}\in\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i,k}^{\ast}+\frac{1}{k}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}+\frac{1}{k}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subset\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i,k}^{\ast}+\frac{\tau m+2}{k}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}.

(3.13)

For any
i
, since
\{\|x_{i,k}^{\ast}\|\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded and
\mathbb{X}
is finite-dimensional, we can assume that
x_{i,k}^{\ast}\rightarrow x_{i}^{\ast}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
(considering subsequence if necessary) and it is not hard to verify that
x_{i,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A_{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}x
as
k\rightarrow\infty
(thanks to (
3.12
) and
x_{k}\rightarrow x
). From this, it follows that
x_{i}^{\ast}\in N(A_{i},x)
and

\|x_{i}^{\ast}\|=\lim_{k}\|x^{\ast}_{i,k}\|\leq\limsup_{k}\|u^{\ast}_{i,k}\|\leq 1.

Taking limits as
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.13
), one has

\frac{x^{\ast}}{\|x^{\ast}\|}=\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}x^{\ast}_{i}.

This implies that

x^{\ast}=\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x^{\ast}\|x^{\ast}_{i}\subset\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}N(A_{i},x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

as
\|x^{\ast}\|\leq 1
and
\|x^{\ast}_{i}\|\leq 1
. Hence (
3.11
) holds. The proof is complete.
\Box

Remark 3

(a) It is noted that (
3.11
) may not imply property (G) of limiting normal cones
\{N(A_{1},x),\cdots,N(A_{m},x)\}
necessarily since
N(A,x)\supseteq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N(A_{i},x)
may not hold trivially. Further, for the case that
A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}
are closed convex sets, it is easy to verify that (
3.11
) holds if and only if
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong CHIP at
x
and
\{N(A_{1},x),\cdots,N(A_{m},x)\}
has property (G) with constant
\tau>0
. This and
Theorem A
imply that subtransversality is equivalent to (
3.11
) which holds for all
x\in A
close to
\bar{x}
.

(b) It is known from
Theorem

3.3
that the subtransversality of
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
implies the validity of (
3.11
) around
\bar{x}
. However, the inverse implication may not hold necessarily. Consider Example 2.1(b). Then
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\{\bar{x}\}
and by computation, one has
N(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{X}
,

N(A_{2},\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}_{-}\times\mathbb{R}_{-}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ N(A_{1},\bar{x})=([0,+\infty)\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times(-\infty,0])\cup\{(t,t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Thus, one can verify that there exists
\tau>0
such that (
3.11
) holds with
x=\bar{x}
.

On the other hand, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
x_{k}=\big(\frac{1}{k},(\frac{2}{k}-\frac{1}{k^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)
. By computation, one has

\mathbf{d}(x_{k},A_{1}\cap A_{2})=\big(\frac{2}{k}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{d}(x_{k},A_{1})=\frac{1}{k}\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\mathbf{d}(x_{k},A_{2})=0.

Thus

\frac{\mathbf{d}(x_{k},A_{1}\cap A_{2})}{\mathbf{d}(x_{k},A_{1})+\mathbf{d}(x_{k},A_{2})}\rightarrow\infty\ ({\rm as}\ k\rightarrow\infty),

which means that
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
is not subtransversal at
\bar{x}
.

Further, we consider the subtransversality for a collection of some special closed sets in convex-composite optimization. It is proved that the equivalence result on subtransversality, strong Fréchet CHIP and property (G) still holds for this case. Before doing this, we need the following lemma which is of independent interest.

Lemma 5

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a continuously differentiable mapping between Banach spaces,
C
be a closed convex set in
Y
and
\bar{x}\in A:=f^{-1}(C)
such that
\triangledown f(\bar{x})
is surjective. Then there exist
\ell,L,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(Y^{\ast})\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{X^{\ast}}\subseteq\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbf{B}_{Y^{\ast}})\subseteq L\mathbf{B}_{X^{\ast}},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)

(3.14)

and

\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{X^{\ast}}\subseteq\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{Y^{\ast}})\subseteq\hat{N}(A,x)\cap L\mathbf{B}_{X^{\ast}},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\cap A,

(3.15)

where
\triangledown f(x)^{*}
is the adjoint of
\triangledown f(x)
.

Proof.
We first show that there exist
\ell>0
and
r\in(0,r_{0})
such that

\|\triangledown f(x)-\triangledown f(\bar{x})\|<\ell\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ell\mathbf{B}_{Y}\subseteq\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X})\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r).

(3.16)

Applying the celebrated Open Mapping Theorem,
0_{Y}
is an interior point of
\triangledown f(\bar{x})(\mathbf{B}_{X})
and thus there exists
\ell>0
such that

3\ell\mathbf{B}_{Y}\subseteq\triangledown f(\bar{x})(\mathbf{B}_{X}).

(3.17)

Noting that
f
is continuously differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then there exists
r>0
such that
f
is Fréchet differentiable on
\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)
and

\|\triangledown f(x)-\triangledown f(\bar{x})\|<\ell\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r).

(3.18)

Then by (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), one has

3\ell\mathbf{B}_{Y}\subseteq(\triangledown f(x)+\triangledown f(\bar{x})-\triangledown f(x))(\mathbf{B}_{X})\subseteq\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X})+\ell\mathbf{B}_{Y}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r).

By the Rådström Cancellation Lemma (cf.
(
Ra
, Lemma 2.3)
), one has

2\ell\mathbf{B}_{Y}\subseteq{\rm cl}(\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X}))\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r).

(3.19)

Note that
\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X})
is convex and then
(
Jameson
, P.183, Theorem A.1)
implies that
\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X})
and
{\rm cl}(\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X}))
have the same interior and consequently

\ell\mathbf{B}_{Y}\subseteq\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X})\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r).

Hence (
3.16
) holds.

By the continuity of
\triangledown f
, without loss of generality we can assume that there is an
L>0
such that

\|\triangledown f(x)-\triangledown f(\bar{x})\|<\ell\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|\triangledown f(x)\|\leq L,\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)

(taking a smaller
r
if necessary).

We first prove (
3.14
). Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)
and
x^{\ast}\in\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbb{Y}^{\ast})\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{X^{\ast}}
. Then there exists
y^{\ast}\in\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}
such that
x^{\ast}=\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(y^{\ast})
. By virtue of (
3.16
), one has

\displaystyle\|y^{\ast}\|=\sup_{y\in\mathbf{B}_{Y}}\langle y^{\ast},y\rangle\leq\sup_{y\in\frac{1}{\ell}\triangledown f(x)(\mathbf{B}_{X})}\langle y^{\ast},y\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\sup_{u\in\mathbf{B}_{X}}\langle y^{\ast},\triangledown f(x)(u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\sup_{u\in\mathbf{B}_{X}}\langle x^{\ast},u\rangle=\frac{\|x^{\ast}\|}{\ell}\leq 1.

Hence
x^{\ast}\in\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})
. Note that
\|\triangledown f(x)\|\leq L
and thus
\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})\subseteq L\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}
. This means that (
3.14
) holds.

We next prove (
3.15
). Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\cap A
and
x^{\ast}\in\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}
. Note that
A=f^{-1}(C)
and it follows from
(
Mordukhovich
, Corollary 1.15)
that

\hat{N}(A,x)=\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x))).

(3.20)

Then there exists
y_{1}^{\ast}\in N(C,f(x))
such that
x^{\ast}=\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(y_{1}^{\ast})
. This and (
3.22
) imply that there exists
y_{2}^{\ast}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}}
such that
x^{\ast}=\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(y_{2}^{\ast})
. Since
\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}
is one-to-one (due to (
3.16
)), it follows that
y_{1}^{\ast}=y_{2}^{\ast}
and thus
x^{\ast}\in\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})
. Note that
\|\triangledown f(x)\|\leq L
and so (
3.15
) follows immediately from (
3.20
). The proof is complete.
\Box

The following theorem shows that the subtransversality is characterized by the strong Fréchet CHIP and property (G) for some special closed sets in convex-composite optimization in the Asplund space setting.

Theorem 3.4

Let
\mathbb{Y}
be an Asplund space,
f:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
be a continuously differentiable mapping and
\{C_{1},\cdots,C_{m}\}
be a collection of finitely many closed convex sets in
\mathbb{Y}
such that
C:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}
is nonempty. Let
A_{i}:=f^{-1}(C_{i})(i=1,\cdots,m)
and
\bar{x}\in A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
such that
\triangledown f(\bar{x})
is surjective. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
;

(ii)

there exist
\tau,\delta>0
such that for all
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
, one has that
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong Fréchet CHIP and
\{\hat{N}(A_{1},x),\cdots,\hat{N}(A_{m},x)\}
has property (G) with constant
\tau
;

(iii)

there exist
\tau,\delta>0
such that for all
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
, one has that
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong limiting CHIP and
\{N(A_{1},x),\cdots,N(A_{m},x)\}
has property (G) with constant
\tau
.

Proof.
Since
\triangledown f(\bar{x})
is surjective, then
(
Mordukhovich
, Theorem 1.57)
implies that
f
is metrically regular at
\bar{x}
and thus there exist
\kappa,r_{0}>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,f^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa\|y-f(x)\|\ \ \forall(x,y)\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r_{0})\times\mathbf{B}(f(\bar{x}),r_{0}).

(3.21)

Applying
Lemma

5
, there exist
\ell,L>0
and
r_{1}\in(0,r_{0})
such that

\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbb{Y}^{\ast})\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subseteq\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})\subseteq L\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r_{1}),

(3.22)

\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subseteq\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})\subseteq\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

(3.23)

and

\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subseteq\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C_{i},f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})\subseteq\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap L\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

(3.24)

hold for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r_{1})\cap A
and for each
i=1,\cdots,m
.

Now, we first prove the equivalence between (i) and (ii). By virtue of
(
ZW
, Corollary 4.2)
, one has that
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
if and only if
\{C_{1},\cdots,C_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
f(\bar{x})
. Based on
Theorem A
in the Introduction, it suffices to prove that (ii) holds if and only if there exist
\tau_{1},\delta_{1}>0
such that

N(C,y)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}}\subset\tau_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(N(C_{i},y)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})

(3.25)

holds for all
y\in C\cap\mathbf{B}(f(\bar{x}),\delta_{1})
.

Suppose that (ii) holds. Then one can verify that

\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subset\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

(3.26)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\delta\in(0,r_{1})
(taking a smaller
\delta
if necessary). Take
\delta_{1}\in(0,\delta)
such that
\kappa\delta_{1}<\delta
. Let
y\in C\cap\mathbf{B}(f(\bar{x}),\delta_{1})
. Then by (
3.21
), one has

\mathbf{d}(\bar{x},f^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa\|f(\bar{x})-y\|<\kappa\delta_{1}

and thus there is
x\in f^{-1}(y)
such that
\|x-\bar{x}\|<\kappa\delta_{1}
. This implies that
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
. By virtue of (
3.23
), (
3.24
) and (
3.26
), one has

\displaystyle\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,y)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})\subset\hat{N}(A,x)\cap L\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle L\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L\tau}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\frac{L\tau}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C_{i},f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}\left(\frac{L\tau}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(N(C_{i},f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})\right).

Since
\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}
is one-to-one, it follows that

N(C,y)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}}\subset\frac{L\tau}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(N(C_{i},y)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}}).

This means that (
3.25
) holds with
\tau_{1}:=\frac{L\tau}{l}
.

Conversely, suppose that there exist
\tau_{1},\delta_{1}>0
such that (
3.25
) holds. By the continuity of
f
, there exists
\delta\in(0,r_{1})
such that

\|f(x)-f(\bar{x})\|<\delta_{1},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

(3.27)

Then, for any
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap A
, by (
3.27
), one has
f(x)\in\mathbf{B}(f(\bar{x}),\delta_{1})
and it follows from (
3.23
), (
3.24
) and (
3.20
) that

\displaystyle\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})\subset\frac{1}{\ell}\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}\left(\tau_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}N(C_{i},f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{1}}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C_{i},f(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{1}}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap L\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L\tau_{1}}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}).

Hence (ii) holds with
\tau:=\frac{L\tau_{1}}{\ell}
and
\delta>0
.

We next prove the equivalence between (ii) and (iii). Suppose that (ii) holds. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\delta\in(0,r_{1})
(taking a smaller
\delta
if necessary). We claim that

N(A,x)=\hat{N}(A,x)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ N(A_{i},x)=\hat{N}(A_{i},x)\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap A\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \forall i=1,\cdots,m.

(3.28)

Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap A
and
x^{\ast}\in N(A,x)
. Then there exist
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}x
and
x_{k}^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{\ast}
such that
x_{k}^{\ast}\in\hat{N}(A,x_{k})
for all
k
. Applying the Banach-Steinhaus Theorem,
we can assume that
\|x_{k}^{\ast}\|\leq M
for each
k
and some
M>0
. By
(
Mordukhovich
, Corollary 1.15)
, for any
k
sufficiently large, there exists
y_{k}^{\ast}\in N(C,f(x_{k}))
such that

x^{\ast}_{k}=\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(y_{k}^{\ast}).

This and (
3.22
) imply that

\displaystyle\frac{\ell}{M}x_{k}^{\ast}\in\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbb{Y}^{\ast})\cap\ell\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}\subset\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}})

and thus there is
z_{k}^{\ast}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}}
such that

\frac{\ell}{M}x^{\ast}_{k}=\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(z_{k}^{\ast}).

Note that
\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}
is one-to-one and thus

y_{k}^{\ast}=\frac{M}{\ell}z_{k}^{\ast},

which implies that
\{\|y_{k}^{\ast}\|\}
is bounded. Since
Y
is an Asplund space, it follows that
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}}
is weak
∗
-sequentially compact and without loss of generality, we can assume that

y_{k}^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}y^{\ast}\in\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}

(taking subsequence if necessary). Then by the continuity of
f
and
\triangledown f
, it is not hard to verify that
y^{\ast}\in N(C,f(x))
and

\triangledown f(x_{k})^{\ast}(y_{k}^{\ast})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(y^{\ast})

(thanks to
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\rightarrow}}x
). Noting that
\triangledown f(x_{k})^{\ast}(y_{k}^{\ast})=x^{\ast}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{\ast}
, it follows that
x^{\ast}=\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(y^{\ast})
and consequently

x^{\ast}\in\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x)))=\hat{N}(A,x)

as
\triangledown f(x)^{\ast}(N(C,f(x)))=\hat{N}(A,x)
follows from
(
Mordukhovich
, Corollary 1.15)
. This means that
N(A,x)=\hat{N}(A,x)
and then (
3.28
) holds as
N(A_{i},x)=\hat{N}(A_{i},x)
follows from the similar proof. The proof is complete.
\Box

Remark 4

It should be noted that properties (ii) and (iii) in
Theorem

3.4
are essentially the same due to (
3.28
). Further, for the case where
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}

4
Applications to Error Bounds of Inequality Systems

In this section, we apply results from Section 3 to error bounds of inequality systems. Let
\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{m}
be continuous functions on
\mathbb{X}
. For each
i
, let

A_{i}:=\{x\in X:\varphi_{i}(x)\leq 0\}.

We consider the following inequality system:

\varphi_{1}(x)\leq 0,\cdots,\varphi_{m}(x)\leq 0.

(4.1)

Let
A_{i}:=\{x\in X:\varphi_{i}(x)\leq 0\}
and
A=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
the solution set of the inequality system (
4.1
). We assume that such system has at least one solution. For any
x\in A
, set

I(x):=\{i:\varphi_{i}(x)=0\}.

(4.2)

We first recall the definition of error bounds of inequality systems.

Definition 2

Let
\bar{x}\in A
. We say that

(i)

the inequality system (
4.1
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
, if there exists
\eta,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,A)\leq\eta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{\varphi_{i}(x),0\},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta);

(4.3)

(ii)

the inequality system (
4.1
) has a global error bound, if there exists
\eta\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,A)\leq\eta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{\varphi_{i}(x),0\},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{X}.

Remark 5

It is noted that there is no necessary relation between the local error bound of the inequality system (
4.1
) and the subtransversality of
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
. For example, if the inequality system (
4.1
) has only one inequality, say
\varphi_{1}(x)\leq 0
, then the subtransversality (of
\{A_{1}\}
) is valid automatically while the local error bound of
\varphi_{1}
may not hold necessarily.

Next, we consider error bounds for convex-composite inequality systems.

Throughout the remainder of this section, we suppose that
\mathbb{Y}
is an Asplund space,
f:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
is continuously differentiable and each
g_{i}:\mathbb{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous convex function on
\mathbb{Y}
for
i=1,\cdots,m
.

For each
i
, let
\varphi_{i}:=g_{i}\circ f
,
A_{i}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\varphi_{i}(x)\leq 0\}
and
C_{i}:=\{y\in\mathbb{Y}:g_{i}(y)\leq 0\}
. We consider the following convex-composite inequality system:

(g_{1}\circ f)(x)\leq 0,\cdots,(g_{m}\circ f)(x)\leq 0.

(4.4)

We still denote by
A:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
the solution set. It is easy to verify that each
C_{i}
is closed and convex,

A_{i}=f^{-1}(C_{i})\ \forall i\ \ {\rm and}\ \ A=f^{-1}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}\right).

(4.5)

The following theorem provides sufficient conditions for the local error bound of convex-composite inequality system (
4.4
).

Theorem 4.1

Let
\bar{x}\in A
be such that
\triangledown f(\bar{x})
is surjective, each convex inequality
g_{i}(y)\leq 0
has a local error bound at
f(\bar{x})
and
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
. Then the convex-composite inequality system (
4.4
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
.

Proof
Since each convex inequality
g_{i}(y)\leq 0
has a local error bound at
f(\bar{x})
, there exist
\eta_{1},r_{1}>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(y,C_{i})\leq\eta_{1}\max\{g_{i}(y),0\},\ \ \forall y\in\mathbf{B}(f(\bar{x}),r_{1}).

(4.6)

Since
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is subtransversal at
\bar{x}
and
f
is continuous, there exist
\tau_{1},\delta_{1}>0
such that

\|f(x)-f(\bar{x})\|<r_{1}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathbf{d}(x,A)\leq\tau_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(x,A_{i})\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta_{1}).

(4.7)

Since
A_{i}=f^{-1}(C_{i})
for each
i
and
\{C_{1},\cdots,C_{m}\}
is a collection of closed convex sets,
(
ZW
, Proposition 3.5)
implies that there exist
\tau_{2}>0
and
\delta_{2}\in(0,\delta_{1})
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,A_{i})\leq\tau_{2}\mathbf{d}(f(x),C_{i})\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta_{2})\ {\rm and}\ \forall i.

(4.8)

By virtue of (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), for any
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta_{2})
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(x,A)\leq\tau_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(x,A_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}(f(x),C_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{g_{i}(f(x)),0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\{\varphi_{i}(x),0\}.

This implies that the convex-composite inequality system (
4.4
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
with constant
\eta:=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
. The proof is complete.
\Box

Now, we are in a position to prove dual characterizations for error bounds of the convex-composite inequality system (
4.4
). Before doing this, we need the following lemma on subdifferential calculus for the convex-composite function.

Lemma 6

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
. Then

\hat{\partial}\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})=\partial\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})=\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))),\ \ \forall i=1,\cdots,m.

(4.9)

Proof.
Let
i
be given. We first show that

\hat{\partial}\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})=\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))).

(4.10)

Note that
g_{i}
is a continuous convex function and thus
g_{i}
is locally Lipschtizian around
f(\bar{x})
. Then there exist
\kappa,r>0
such that

|g_{i}(y_{1})-g_{i}(y_{2})|\leq\kappa\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|,\ \ \forall y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbf{B}(f(\bar{x}),r).

(4.11)

Let
\epsilon>0
. Since
f
is differentiable at
\bar{x}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|f(x)-f(\bar{x})\|\leq r\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|f(x)-f(\bar{x})-\triangledown f(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x})\|<\epsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|,\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

This and (
4.11
) imply that for any
x
close to
\bar{x}
, one has

\frac{|g_{i}(f(x))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x})+\triangledown f(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x}))|}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq\kappa\epsilon,\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ x\ {\rm close\ to}\ \bar{x}.

(4.12)

Let
y^{\ast}\in\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))
. Then (
4.12
) gives that

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\frac{\varphi_{i}(x)-\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})-\langle\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(y^{\ast}),x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\frac{g_{i}(f(x))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))-\langle y^{\ast},\triangledown f(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x}))\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\frac{g_{i}(f(x))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x})+\triangledown f(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x}))+g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\frac{g_{i}(f(x))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x})+\triangledown f(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x}))}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\kappa\epsilon.

Taking limits as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, one has

\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(y^{\ast})\in\hat{\partial}\varphi_{i}(\bar{x}).

On the other hand, suppose on the contrary that there exists
x^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})\backslash\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x})))
. Since
\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}
is weak
∗
-to-weak
∗
continuous and
\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))
is weak
∗
-compact, it follows that
\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x})))
is weak
∗
-compact and convex. By the Separation Theorem (cf.
(
Rudin
, Theorem 3.4)
), there exist
h\in\mathbb{X}
with
\|h\|=1
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\langle x^{\ast},h\rangle>\gamma>\sup\{\langle x^{\ast},h\rangle:x^{\ast}\in\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x})))\}=d^{+}g_{i}(f(\bar{x}),\triangledown f(\bar{x})h).

(4.13)

Note that
x^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})
and so there exists
\delta>0
such that

g_{i}(f(x))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))-\langle x^{\ast},x-\bar{x}\rangle\geq-\epsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

Then for any
t>0
sufficiently small, one has

\displaystyle\gamma-\epsilon<\langle x^{\ast},h\rangle-\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{g_{i}(f(\bar{x}+th))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{i}(f(\bar{x}+th))-g_{i}(f(\bar{x})+\triangledown f(\bar{x})h)}{t}+\frac{g_{i}(f(\bar{x})+\triangledown f(\bar{x})h)-g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))}{t}.

Letting
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
and then
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, it follows from (
4.12
) that

d^{+}g_{i}(f(\bar{x}),\triangledown f(\bar{x})h)\geq\gamma,

which contradicts (
4.13
). Hence (
4.10
) holds.

We next prove that

\partial\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})=\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))).

(4.14)

Granting this, it follows from (
4.10
) that (
4.9
) holds.

Note that
\partial\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})\supseteq\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x})))
by (
4.10
) and so it suffices to prove the inclusion. To this aim, let
x^{\ast}\in\partial\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})
. Then there exist
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}\bar{x}
and
x_{k}^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{\ast}
such that

x_{k}^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\varphi(x_{k})=\triangledown f(x_{k})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(x_{k})))\ \ \forall k.

Thus for each
k
, there is
y_{k}^{\ast}\in\partial g_{i}(f(x_{k}))
such that

x_{k}^{\ast}=\triangledown f(x_{k})^{\ast}(y_{k}^{\ast}).

(4.15)

Note that
g_{i}
is a continuous convex function and thus is locally Lipschitz around
f(\bar{x})
. This implies that
\{\|y_{k}^{\ast}\|\}
is bounded. Since
\mathbb{Y}
is an Asplund space, it follows that
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}
is weak
∗
sequentially compact and without loss of generality, we can assume that

y_{k}^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}y^{\ast}\in\mathbb{Y}^{\ast}

(taking a subsequence if necessary). Noting that
y_{k}^{\ast}\in\partial g_{i}(f(x_{k}))
,
x_{k}\rightarrow x
and
\triangledown f
is continuous at
x
, it follows that

y^{\ast}\in\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x}))\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \triangledown f(x_{k})^{\ast}(y_{k}^{\ast})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(y^{*})

and consequently (
4.15
) gives

x^{\ast}=\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(y^{\ast})\in\triangledown f(\bar{x})^{\ast}(\partial g_{i}(f(\bar{x})))=\hat{\partial}\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})

(thanks to
x_{k}^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{\ast}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{\ast}
). Hence (
4.14
) holds. The proof is complete.
\Box

Remark 6

In (
4.9
) of
Lemma

6
, the first equality means that each
\varphi_{i}
is lower regular at
\bar{x}
(see
(
Mordukhovich
, Definition 1.91)
) and the second equality is inspired by
(
Clarke1981
, Chain Rule 1)
that established the calculus of Clarke subdifferentials for convex-composite functions.

Now, we are in a position to present dual characterizations for error bounds of the convex-composite inequality system (
4.4
)

Theorem 4.2

Consider the convex-composite inequality system (
4.4
). Then (
4.4
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}\in A
if and only if there exist
\eta,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that for any
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and
x^{*}\in\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}
, there are multipliers
\lambda_{i}\in[0,\eta],i\in I(x)
satisfying

x^{*}\in\sum_{i\in I(x)}\lambda_{i}\hat{\partial}\varphi_{i}(x).

(4.16)

Proof.
Set
\varphi_{i}^{+}(x):=\max\{\varphi_{i}(x),0\}
for any
x
.

The necessity part
. By the local error bound, there exist
\eta,\delta>0
such that (
4.3
) holds. Let
x\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and
x^{*}\in\hat{N}(A,x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{\ast}}
. Then
x^{\ast}\in\hat{\partial}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,A)(x)
and thus for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta_{1}\in(0,\delta-\|x-\bar{x}\|)
such that

\langle x^{\ast},u-x\rangle\leq\mathbf{d}(u,A)-\mathbf{d}(x,A)+\eta\epsilon\|u-x\|,\ \ \forall u\in\mathbf{B}(x,\delta_{1})

and it follows from (
4.3
) that

\langle\frac{x^{\ast}}{\eta},u-x\rangle\leq\psi(u)+\epsilon\|u-x\|,\ \ \forall u\in\mathbf{B}(x,\delta_{1}),

where
\psi(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}^{+}(x)
. This together with
(
Mordukhovich
, Theorem 3.36)
and
(
Mordukhovich
, Theorem 3.46)
implies that

\frac{x^{\ast}}{\eta}\in\hat{\partial}\psi(x)\subseteq\partial\psi(x)\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial\varphi_{i}^{+}(x)=\sum_{i\in I(x)}\partial\varphi_{i}^{+}(x)\subseteq\sum_{i\in I(x)}[0,1]\partial\varphi_{i}(x)

(the equality holds by the continuity of
\varphi_{i}
). Then there are multipliers
\lambda_{i}\in[0,\eta],i\in I(x)
such that

x^{\ast}\in\sum_{i\in I(x)}\lambda_{i}\partial\varphi_{i}(x)

This and
Lemma

6
imply that (
4.16
) holds.

The sufficiency part
. Note that each
g_{i}
is locally Lipschitz around
f(\bar{x})
and then there exist
\kappa,r_{0}>0
such that

\|g_{i}(y_{1})-g_{i}(y_{2})\|\leq\kappa\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|,\ \ \forall y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbf{B}(f(\bar{x}),r_{0})\ {\rm and}\ \forall i.

(4.17)

Denote
L:=\|\triangledown f(\bar{x})\|+1
. Let
\epsilon>0
be such that
m\eta\kappa\epsilon<1
. Since
f
is continuously differentiable at
\bar{x}
, there exists
r_{1}\in(0,\delta)
with
2Lr_{1}<r_{0}
such that

\|\triangledown f(x)\|\leq L\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|f(x)-f(u)-\triangledown f(u)(x-u)\|<\epsilon\|x-u\|,\ \forall x,u\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r_{1}).

(4.18)

Take
\delta_{1}:=\frac{r_{1}}{2}
and let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta_{1})\backslash A
. Then
\mathbf{d}(x,A)\leq\|x-\bar{x}\|<\delta_{1}
. Choose
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

\beta>\max\{\frac{\mathbf{d}(x,A)}{\delta_{1}},m\eta\kappa\epsilon\}
. Applying
Lemma

4
, there exist
a\in A
and
a^{*}\in\hat{N}(A,a)
with
\|a^{*}\|=1
such that

\beta\|x-a\|\leq\min\{\mathbf{d}(x,A),\langle a^{*},x-a\rangle\}.

(4.19)

Then

\|a-\bar{x}\|\leq\|a-x\|+\|x-\bar{x}\|<\frac{\mathbf{d}(x,A)}{\beta}+\delta_{1}<2\delta_{1}=r_{1}

and it follows from (
4.16
) and
Lemma

6
that there exist
\lambda_{i}\in[0,\eta],y_{i}^{*}\in\partial g_{i}(f(a))(\forall i\in I(a))
such that

a^{*}=\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\triangledown f(a)^{*}(y_{i}^{*}).

This together with (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) implies that

\displaystyle\langle a^{*},x-a\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\langle\triangledown f(a)^{*}(y_{i}^{*}),x-a\rangle=\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\langle y_{i}^{*},\triangledown f(a)(x-a)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\big(g_{i}(f(a)+\triangledown f(a)(x-a))-g_{i}(f(a))\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\big(g_{i}(f(x))-g_{i}(f(a))\big)+\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\big(g_{i}(f(a)+\triangledown f(a)(x-a))-g_{i}(f(x))\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\varphi_{i}(x)+\sum_{i\in I(a)}\lambda_{i}\kappa\|f(x)-f(a)-\triangledown f(a)(x-a))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}^{+}(x)+m\eta\kappa\epsilon\|x-a\|

and it follows from (
4.19
) that

(\beta-m\eta\kappa\epsilon)\|x-a\|\leq\eta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}^{+}(x).

By taking limits as
\beta\uparrow 1
, one gets

\mathbf{d}(x,A)\leq\frac{\eta}{1-m\eta\kappa\epsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}^{+}(x).

This means that the convex-composite inequality system (
4.4
) has a local error bound with constant
\frac{\eta}{1-m\eta\kappa\epsilon}
. The proof is complete.
\Box

Theorem 4.3

Let

5
Conclusions

The study on subtransversality and strong CHIP refers to the so-called convex feasibility problem of finding a common point in the intersection of convex sets in a Hilbert space. Since the two concepts are proved to ensure a better norm convergence of the sequence produced by projection-based algorithms for solving the convex feasibility problem, subtransversality and strong CHIP, especially relationships with other qualifications in approximation theory and optimization, have been well-recognized and extensively studied. One of the most important results on these concepts is that the dual characterization of subtransversality for a collection of closed convex sets can be established via the strong CHIP and property (G). The work in this paper was to study such issue for a collection of special closed sets in Asplund spaces. Although the dual characterization result on subtransversality for general closed sets may not hold (if dropping the convexity assumption), several necessary conditions for subtransversality are still obtained in terms of Fréchet/limiting normal cones. Further, the equivalence result among subtransversality, strong Fréchet CHIP and property (G) for some special closed sets in convex-composite optimization is proved. Our work is an extension of a duality characterization of subtransversality via the strong CHIP and property (G) to the possible non-convex case.

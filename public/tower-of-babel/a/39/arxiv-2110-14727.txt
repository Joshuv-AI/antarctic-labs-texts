Abstract
. We compute the Picard groups with integral coefficients of the Hurwitz stacks parametrizing degree 4 4 and 5 5 covers of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} . As a consequence, we also determine the integral Picard groups of the Hurwitz stacks parametrizing simply branched covers. For simple branching, the Picard groups are finite, with order depending on the genus.

Let

2.
Hurwitz Stacks

We say a morphism
P\rightarrow S
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration if it is a flat, proper, finitely presented morphism of schemes whose geometric fibers are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We define the unparametrized Hurwitz stack
\mathscr{H}_{k,g}
of degree
k
, genus
g
covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to be the stack whose objects over a scheme
S
are of the form
(C\rightarrow P\rightarrow S)
where
P\rightarrow S
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration,
C\rightarrow P
is a finite, flat, finitely presented morphism of constant degree
k
, and the composition
C\rightarrow S
is smooth with geometrically connected fibers. We do not impose the condition that our covers
C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be simply branched. In the case
k=3
,
\mathscr{H}_{3,g}
is the stack
\mathcal{T}_{g}
from
[
2
]
.

The parametrized Hurwitz scheme
\mathscr{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}
is defined similarly, except
P\rightarrow S
is replaced by
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}
. Therefore, the unparametrized Hurwitz stack is the
\PGL_{2}
quotient of the parametrized Hurwitz scheme.
There is also a natural action of
\SL_{2}
on
\mathscr{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}
(via
\SL_{2}\subset\GL_{2}\rightarrow\PGL_{2}
).

We shall use script font
\mathscr{H}_{k,g}:=[\mathscr{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}/\PGL_{2}]
for the
\PGL_{2}
quotient,

and caligraphic font
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}:=[\mathscr{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}/\SL_{2}]
for the
\SL_{2}
quotient.

Explicitly, the
\SL_{2}
quotient
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
is the stack whose objects over a scheme
S
are families
(C\rightarrow P\rightarrow S)
where
P=\mathbb{P}V\rightarrow S
is the projectivization of a rank
2
vector bundle
V
with trivial determinant,
C\rightarrow P
is a finite flat finitely presented morphism of constant degree
k
, and the composition
C\rightarrow S
has smooth fibers of genus
g
.
The benefit of working with
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
is that the
\SL_{2}
quotient is equipped with a universal
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
that has a relative degree one line bundle

(a

-fibration does not).

Bolognesi–Vistoli
[
2
]
gave a presentation for
\mathscr{V}_{r,d}
as a quotient stack, which we briefly summarize here. Let
M_{r,d}
be the affine space that represents the functor which sends a scheme
S
to the set of matrices of size
(r+d)\times d
with entries in
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}}(1))
. We can identify such a matrix with the associated map

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}}(-1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}}^{r+d}.

Let
\Omega_{r,d}\subset M_{r,d}
denote the open subscheme parametrizing injective maps with locally free cokernel. The group
\GL_{d}
acts on
M_{r,d}
by multiplication on the left,
\GL_{r+d}
acts by multiplication on the right, and
\GL_{2}
acts by change of coordinates on
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}}(1))
. These actions commute with each other and leave
\Omega_{r,d}
invariant, and hence
\GL_{d}\times\GL_{r+d}\times\GL_{2}
acts on
\Omega_{r,d}
. There is a copy of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
inside of
\GL_{d}\times\GL_{r+d}\times\GL_{2}
embedded by
t\mapsto(t\Id_{d},\Id_{r+d},t^{-1}\Id_{2})
. The image
T
acts trivially on
M_{r,d}
and so we can define an action of the quotient

\Gamma_{r,d}:=\GL_{d}\times\GL_{r+d}\times\GL_{2}/T

on
\Omega_{r,d}
. There is an exact sequence

1\rightarrow\GL_{d}\times\GL_{r+d}\rightarrow\Gamma_{r,d}\rightarrow\PGL_{2}\rightarrow 1,

where the map
\Gamma_{r,d}\rightarrow\PGL_{2}
is induced by the projection of
\GL_{d}\times\GL_{r+d}\times\GL_{2}\rightarrow\GL_{2}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Bolognesi–Vistoli
[
2
]

.

There is an isomorphism of fibered categories

\mathscr{V}_{r,d}\cong[\Omega_{r,d}/\Gamma_{r,d}.]

A slight modification of the argument in Bolognesi–Vistoli gives a quotient structure for

Proposition 3.3

There is an isomorphism of fibered categories

\mathcal{V}_{r,d}\cong[\Omega_{r,d}/\GL_{d}\times\GL_{r+d}\times\SL_{2}].

Proof.

\begin{cases}t_{1},u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}&\text{if $d,e$ both even}\\
2t_{1},u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}&\text{if $d$ odd and $e$ even}\\
t_{1},u_{1},2v_{1},w_{1}&\text{if $d$ even and $e$ odd}\\
t_{1}-v_{1},2t_{1},u_{1},w_{1}&\text{if $d,e$ both odd,}\end{cases}

or equivalently by

\begin{cases}a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{1},b_{2}^{\prime}&\text{if $d,e$ both even}\\

a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},2b_{1},b_{2}^{\prime}&\text{if $d$ even and $e$ odd}\\
a_{1}-b_{1},2a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime}&\text{if $d,e$ both odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that
\Pic(\mathrm{B}G)
is naturally identified with the character group of

because it is identified with Mumford’s functorial Picard group. The exact sequence of groups

0\rightarrow\mu_{2}\rightarrow G_{r,d,s,e}\times\SL_{2}\rightarrow\Gamma_{r,d,s,e}\rightarrow 0

induces a left exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Pic(\mathrm{B}\Gamma_{r,d,s,e})\rightarrow\Pic(\mathrm{B}(G_{r,d,s,e}\times\SL_{2}))\rightarrow\Pic(\mathrm{B}\mu_{2}).

The Picard group
\Pic(\mathrm{B}\mu_{2})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Let

be a generator of
\Pic(\mathrm{B}\mu_{2})
.
Recall that the map

sends

.
The generator

corresponds to the determinant of the rank

to

. Similarly,

and

are sent to zero, and

to

).

The translation between (

) and (

. We explain the case

both odd, the other cases being similar but simpler. Since

and

2t_{1}\\
u_{1}\\
w_{1}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}e&\frac{d-e}{2}&-1&1\\[3.0pt]
0&d&-2&0\\[3.0pt]
0&\frac{d+1}{2}&-1&0\\[3.0pt]

2a_{1}\\
a_{2}^{\prime}\\

The determinant of the

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X^{\prime}\subset X
be an open substack. Given a smooth map
f:Y\to X
, let
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
be the preimage of
X^{\prime}
.
If
\Pic(X)\to\Pic(Y)
is injective, then
\Pic(X^{\prime})\to\Pic(Y^{\prime})
is injective.

Proof.

It suffices to treat the
case when

is the complement of an irreducible divisor

. (Removing any component of codimension two or more from

does not change
\Pic(X)
; if
X^{\prime}
is the complement of a reducible divisor, then we just apply the irreducible case to each component in turn.)

Because

is smooth,

is the complement of the irreducible divisor

, which has class
f^{*}[D]
.
Let
\langle[D]\rangle
denote the subgroup of
\Pic(X)
generated by the fundamental class of

and similarly for
\langle f^{*}[D]\rangle
inside
\Pic(Y)
.
We therefore have a diagram of exact sequences where the left vertical map is surjective.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle[D]\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pic(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pic(X^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle f^{*}[D]\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pic(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pic(Y^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

The result now follows from the snake lemma.
∎

One natural class on

5.1.
The open

Lemma 5.1

If

Proof.

Following the notation of

Remark 5.2
.

The association of
\alpha:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the triple
(E_{\alpha},F_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})
gives rise to a map
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{5,g}
, for the base stack
\mathscr{B}_{5,g}
defined in
3.1.3
. Just like in the degree
4
case, the map
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{5,g}
does not factor through a vector bundle over
\mathscr{B}_{5,g}
, but an open substack
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}^{\prime}
does. When

, we will show that the complement of
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}
has codimension at least

\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g})
. We will then deal with the

6.1.
The open substack
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}

First, define
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g}:=\mathscr{B}_{5,g}\smallsetminus R^{1}\pi_{*}(\mathcal{H}om(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes\det\mathscr{E},\wedge^{2}\mathscr{F}))
. Let
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}
be the base change of
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{5,g}
along the open embedding
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{5,g}
. Arguing exactly as in
[
4
, Lemma 5.3]
, the morphism
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g}
factors through the total space of a vector bundle over
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g}
:

\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{5,g}:=\pi_{*}(\mathcal{H}om(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes\det\mathscr{E},\wedge^{2}\mathscr{F}))|_{\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g}}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
g\neq 3
. Then, every component of the complement of
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}\subset\mathscr{H}_{5,g}
has codimension at least
2
. In particular,
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g})=\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g}^{\prime})

Proof.

Following the notation of
[
7
]
,
let
M(E,F)\subset\mathscr{H}_{5,g}
denote the locus of covers
\alpha
with
E_{\alpha}\cong E
and
F_{\alpha}\cong F
.
The complement of
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}\subset\mathscr{H}_{5,g}
is the union of
M(E,F)
such that

(6.1)

h^{1}(E\otimes\det E^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2}F^{\vee})>0.

If
E=\mathcal{O}(e_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{4})
with
e_{1}\leq\cdots\leq e_{4}
and
F=\mathcal{O}(f_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(f_{5})
with
f_{1}\leq\cdots\leq f_{5}
, then (
6.1
) is equivalent to
e_{1}+f_{1}+f_{2}-(g+4)\leq-2
.

Next, let

E_{\gen}
, respectively
F_{\gen}
, denote the balanced bundle of rank
4
and degree
g+4
, respectively rank
5
and degree
2g+8
.
First note that
h^{1}(E_{\gen}\otimes\det E_{\gen}^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2}F_{\gen}^{\vee})=0
, as in this case,
e_{1}+f_{1}+f_{2}-(g+4)\geq\lfloor\frac{g+4}{4}\rfloor+2\lfloor\frac{2(g+4)}{5}\rfloor-(g+4)\geq-1
(except when
g=3
, in which case
f_{2}=\lceil\frac{2(g+4)}{5}\rceil
, so we still have
e_{1}+f_{1}+f_{2}\geq-1
.)
This says that
M(E_{\gen},F_{\gen})\subseteq\mathscr{H}_{5,g}^{\prime}
. Hence, any divisorial component of
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}\smallsetminus\mathscr{H}_{5,g}^{\prime}
is contained in a divisorial component of
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}\smallsetminus M(E_{\gen},F_{\gen})
.

Again, let us define bundles that are “one-off” from balanced

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{O}(n-1)\oplus\oplus\mathcal{O}(n)^{\oplus 3}\mathcal{O}(n+1)

if
n=\frac{2g+8}{5}
is an integer

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{O}(m-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m)^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}(m+1)

if
m=\frac{g+4}{4}
is an integer.

In
[
7
, p. 25]
, Deopurkar–Patel enumerate the divisorial components of
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}\smallsetminus M(E_{\gen},F_{\gen})
and show that, for
g\neq 3
, they are always of the form

\displaystyle\overline{M(E_{\gen},F_{1})}

if
5\mid 2g+8

\displaystyle\overline{M(E_{1},F_{\gen})}

\displaystyle\text{if $4\mid g+4$}.

Note that we are using the irreducibility of
M
and
CE
in
[
7
, Propositions 5.1 and 5.2]
to write these divisors as the closures above.
One readily checks that

h^{1}(E_{\gen}\otimes\det E^{\vee}_{\gen}\otimes\wedge^{2}F_{1})=h^{1}(E_{1}\otimes\det E_{1}^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2}F_{\gen})=0

Hence, when they are defined,
\mathscr{H}_{5,g}^{\prime}
contains each of

M(E_{\gen},F_{\gen}),\quad M(E_{\gen},F_{1}),\quad M(E_{1},F_{\gen}),

and all other possible
M(E,F)
have codimension at least
2
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

When
g=3
, the complement of
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,3}\subset\mathscr{H}_{5,3}
is an irreducible divisor whose class lies in the subgroup generated by
2a_{1}
and
a_{2}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The divisorial component of the complement of
\mathscr{H}_{5,3}^{\prime}
inside
\mathscr{H}_{5,3}
is the locus

\overline{M(E_{1},F_{1})}=\overline{M(\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(2)^{\oplus 3},\mathcal{O}(2)^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}(3)^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}(4))}.

By
[
7
, Proposition 5.2]
, this locus is precisely the preimage of the hyperelliptic locus under the natural morphism
\mathscr{H}_{5,3}\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{3}
. By
[
9
]
, the hyperelliptic locus in
\mathscr{M}_{3}
has class
9\lambda
.
The class
\lambda=c_{1}(f_{*}\omega_{f})=c_{1}(\pi_{*}\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{\pi})\in\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})
is pulled back from
\Pic(\mathscr{B}_{5,3})
. Since
\Pic(\mathscr{B}_{5,3})
includes into
\Pic(\mathcal{B}_{5,3})
, we can determine this class via a calculation on the
\SL_{2}
quotient, as in Example
3.4
:

[\overline{M(E_{1},F_{1})}]=9\lambda=9c_{1}(f_{*}\omega_{f})=9c_{1}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(-2))=54a_{1}-9a_{2}^{\prime}=27(2a_{1})-9a_{2}^{\prime},

which is in the span of
2a_{1}
and
a_{2}^{\prime}
.
∎

6.2.
Excision

We proceed similarly to the
k=4
case. Let
\mathscr{D}_{5,g}\subset\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{5,g}\smallsetminus\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}
. There is a series of surjections

(6.2)

\Pic(\mathscr{B}_{5,g})\rightarrow\Pic(\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g})\cong\Pic(\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{5,g})\rightarrow\Pic(\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}).

The middle map is an isomorphism because
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{5,g}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g}
is a vector bundle.
We have defined a sequence of morphisms

(6.3)

\mathscr{D}_{5,g}\to\mathscr{X}_{5,g}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{5,g}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{5,g}\rightarrow\mathscr{V}_{4,g+4}\times_{\BPGL_{2}}\mathscr{V}_{5,2g+8}\rightarrow\BPGL_{2}.

Lemma 6.3
.

For
g\geq 2
, some combination of the components of
\mathscr{D}_{5,g}
has class

\frac{(10g+36)}{\epsilon}(\epsilon a_{1})-7a_{2}^{\prime}-b_{2}^{\prime}\in\Pic(\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{5,g})\cong\Pic(\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g}).

Proof.

Base changing (
6.3
) by
\BSL_{2}\to\BPGL_{2}
, we obtain the stacks and morphisms considered in
[
4
, Section 5.3]
(below
\Delta_{5,g}^{\prime}
is the complement of the open inclusion
\mathcal{H}_{5,g}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{5,g}^{\prime}
of
[
4
, Lemma 5.11]
):

\Delta_{5,g}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}_{5,g}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{5,g}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{5,g}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{4,g+4}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{5,2g+8}\rightarrow\BSL_{2}.

By
[
3
, Lemma 6.6 and Equation 6.19]
, the relation
\frac{(10g+36)}{\epsilon}(\epsilon a_{1})-7a_{2}^{\prime}-b_{2}^{\prime}=0
holds in
\Pic(\mathcal{H}_{5,g})
, so it must also hold on the open substack
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{5,g}\smallsetminus\Delta_{5,g}^{\prime}
, any relation among
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{5,g}
must come from a class supported on

. Therefore, some combination of components of
\Delta_{5,g}^{\prime}
has class
(10g+36)a_{1}-7a_{2}^{\prime}-b_{2}^{\prime}
. The corresponding combination of components on
\mathscr{D}_{5,g}

Proof of Theorem 1.1(3) when
g\geq 4
.

By Lemma
6.1
,
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g})\cong\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g}^{\prime})
for
g\geq 4
. We have that
\Pic(\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g})
is a quotient of
\Pic(\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{5,g})\cong\Pic(\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{5,g})
by classes supported on
\mathscr{D}_{5,g}
. By Lemma
6.3
, one such class is
(10g+36)a_{1}-7a_{2}^{\prime}-b_{2}^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\Pic(\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g})
is a quotient of

\frac{\mathbb{Z}(\epsilon a_{1})\oplus\mathbb{Z}a_{2}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{Z}b_{2}^{\prime}}{\langle\frac{10g+36}{\epsilon}(\epsilon a_{1})-7a_{2}^{\prime}-b_{2}^{\prime}\rangle}\cong\mathbb{Z}(\epsilon a_{1})\oplus\mathbb{Z}a_{2}^{\prime}.

By
[
7
, Proposition 2.15]
, the rank of
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is at least
2
, so we must have that
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g})\cong\mathbb{Z}(\epsilon a_{1})\oplus\mathbb{Z}a_{2}^{\prime}
.
∎

6.2.1.
Genus 3

As in the
k=4
case, when
g=3
, we require a different argument because Lemma
6.2
tells us that
\mathscr{H}_{5,3}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{H}_{5,3}
is the complement of a divisor.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(2) when
g=3
.

By Lemma
6.2
, the kernel of the restriction map

\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})\to\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3}^{\prime})

lies in the subgroup
\langle 2a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}\rangle
. We know that
\Pic(\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{5,g})
is generated by the classes
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime}
, so it follows that
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})
is also generated by these
3
classes.

Next, we claim that
b_{2}^{\prime}
is integrally expressible in terms of
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
6.3
, we know
b_{2}^{\prime}
is expressible in terms of
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}
in
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3}^{\prime})
.
But, the kernel of
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})\to\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3}^{\prime})
lies in
\langle a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}\rangle
, so
b_{2}^{\prime}
must also be expressible in terms of
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}
in
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})
.

It follows that
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})
is a quotient of
\mathbb{Z}a_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}a_{2}^{\prime}
.
However, by
[
7
]
, we know that
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
has rank

, so
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,3})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

6.2.2.
Genus 2

As in Section
5.2.2
, the argument of Deopurkar–Patel
[
7
, Proposition 2.15]
in genus
2
establishes that the rank of
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,2})
is at least

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(2) when
g=2
.

We have already established that
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}
are generators for
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,2})
. We compute directly
\lambda=5a_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime}
as in Section
5.2.2
, from which we see
\lambda
and

are generators for
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,2})
.
Arguing as in Section
5.2.2
, it suffices to show that the map
\Pic(\mathscr{M}_{2})\to\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,2})
is injective.

Every degree
5
line bundle on a genus
2
curve has a
4
-dimensional space of sections. The Hurwitz space
\mathscr{H}_{5,2}
then sits naturally as an open inside the Grassmann fibration
\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{P}^{5}
parametrizing
2
-dimensional subspaces of the space of global sections of a degree
5
line bundle.
The complement of
\mathscr{H}_{5,2}\subset\mathscr{G}
is the locus of pencils with a base point, which we again see is irreducible and not equivalent to the pullback of a divisor on
\mathscr{P}^{5}
. In particular, the map
\Pic(\mathscr{P}^{5})\to\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,2})
must be injective. Applying Lemma

, we conclude that
\Pic(\mathscr{M}_{2})\to\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,2})

∎

6.3.
Generating line bundles

Line bundles generating
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{5,g})
are given by

\mathscr{L}_{1}=\begin{cases}\pi_{*}\left(\det\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes(g+4)/2}\right)&\text{if $g$ even}\\
\\

6.4.
Simple branching

Let
T
and
D
be as in Figure

. In

, we wrote the classes of

and

Proof of Corollary
1.3
(3).

By excision

.
Again, row operations over

diagonalize the change of basis matrix for

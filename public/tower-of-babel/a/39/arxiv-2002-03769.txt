Abstract
. In this paper we discuss and prove some new strong convergence theorems for partial sums and Fejér means with respect to the Vilenkin system.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification.
42C10.

Key words and phrases:
Vilenkin system, Fejér means, martingale Hardy space, strong convergence.

Introduction

It is well-known (for details see e.g.
[

,

,

L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).
Moreover, there is a function in the martingale
Hardy space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right),
such that the partial sums of
f

are not bounded in
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
-norm. However (for details see e.g.
[
tep12.tep13
]
), for all
p>0
and
f\in H_{p}
, there exists an absolute constant
c_{p}
such that

(1)

\left\|S_{M_{k}}f\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}.

In Gát
[
6
]
(see also Simon
[
17
]
) the following strong convergence result was obtained for
all
f\in H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right):

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f-f\right\|_{1}}{k}=0,

It follow that

\frac{1}{\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}}{k}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}},\ \ n=2,3,...

In
[
36
]
was proved that for any
f\in H_{1}
there exists an absolute constant
c,
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}},\ \ n=2,3,...

Moreover for every nondecreasing
function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}_{+}\rightarrow[1,

\infty)
satisfying the condition

\overline{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{\log n}{\varphi_{n}}=+\infty.

there exists a function
f\in H_{1},
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n\varphi_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

For the Vilenkin system Simon
[
18
]

proved that there is an absolute constant
c_{p},
depending only on
p,

such that

\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}^{p},

for all
f\in H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
, where
0<p<1.
In
[
25
]
was proved that for any nondecreasing function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[1,

\infty)
, satisfying the condition
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Phi\left(n\right)=+\infty,
there exists a
martingale
f\in H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right),
such that

(2)

\text{ }\underset{k=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{L_{p,\infty}}^{p}\Phi\left(k\right)}{k^{2-p}}=\infty,\text{ for }0<p<1.

Strong convergence theorems of two-dimensional partial sums was investigate
by Weisz

, Goginava

Weisz

considered the norm convergence of Fejér means of
Walsh-Fourier series and proved the following:

Theorem W1 (Weisz).
Let
p>1/2
and
f\in H_{p}.
Then

\left\|\sigma_{k}f\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}.

Moreover, Weisz
[
41
]
(see also
[

]
) also proved that for all

and
f\in H_{p}
,
there exists an absolute constant

such that

(3)

\left\|\sigma_{M_{k}}f\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}.

Theorem W1 implies that

\frac{1}{n^{2p-1}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|\sigma_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-2p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}^{p},\text{ \ \ \ }\left(1/2<p<\infty\right).

If Theorem W1 should hold for
0<p\leq 1/2,
then we would have

(4)

\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|\sigma_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-2p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}^{p},\text{ \ \ \ }\left(0<p<1/2\right)

(5)

\frac{1}{\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|\sigma_{k}f\right\|_{1/2}^{1/2}}{k}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1/2}}^{1/2}\text{ \ \ \ }\ \ n=2,3,...

and

(6)

\frac{1}{n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}{\left\|\sigma_{k}f\right\|_{1/2}^{1/2}}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1/2}}^{1/2}.

However, in
[

(see also
[

,

]
and
[

,
29
,

,

) it was proved
that the assumption
p>1/2
in Theorem W1 is essential. In particular, there
exists a martingale
f\in H_{1/2}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{1/2}=+\infty.

In
[
5
]
(see also
[
26
]
) it was proved that (

) and (

) hold though Theorem W1 is not true for

Moreover, in

be any nondecreasing function satisfying

then there exists a martingale

such that

\underset{m=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|\sigma_{m}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}^{p}}{\Phi_{m}}=\infty.

On the other hand, for the Walsh system (

[

(7)

\underset{n\in\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}\frac{1}{n}\underset{m=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left\|\sigma_{m}f\right\|_{1/2}^{1/2}=\infty.

In this paper we prove more general result for bounded Vilenkin system. In special case we also obtain (
7

This paper is organized as follows: in order not to disturb our discussions
later on some definitions and notations are presented in Section 2. For the proofs of the main results we need some auxiliary Lemmas, some of them are new and of independent interest. These results are presented in Section 3. The main result with proof is given in Section 4.

2.
Definitions and Notations

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.

Let
m:=(m_{0,}m_{1},\dots)
denote a sequence of the positive integers not
less than 2.

Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,\dots,m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k}.

Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{m_{j}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{j}}

,
s.

The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k}\text{ \qquad}(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is the Haar measure on
G_{m_{\text{ }}}
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

If
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}m_{n}<\infty
, then we call
G_{m}
a bounded
Vilenkin group. If the generating sequence
m
is not bounded then
G_{m}

is said to be an unbounded Vilenkin group. In this paper we discuss
bounded Vilenkin groups only.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots)\qquad\left(\text{ }x_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighbourhood of
G_{m}
namely

I_{0}\left(x\right):=G_{m},

and

I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},\dots,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\}\text{ }(x\in G_{m},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N})

Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\overline{I_{n}}:=G_{m}

\backslash

I_{n}

.

Let

e_{n}:=\left(0,\dots,0,x_{n}=1,0,\dots\right)\in G_{m}\qquad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

If we define the so-called generalized number system based on
m
in the
following way:

M_{0}:=1,\text{ \qquad}M_{k+1}:=m_{k}M_{k\text{ }},\ \qquad(k\in\mathbb{N})

then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as
n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j}
, where
n_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}

~(j\in\mathbb{N})
and only a finite number of
n_{j}`
s differ from zero. Let
\left|n\right|:=\max

\{j\in\mathbb{N};

n_{j}\neq 0\}.

For the natural number
n=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j},
we define

\delta_{j}=signn_{j}=sign\left(\ominus n_{j}\right),\text{ \ \ \ \ }\delta_{j}^{\ast}=\left|\ominus n_{j}-1\right|\delta_{j},

where
\ominus
is the inverse operation for
a_{k}\oplus b_{k}=\left(a_{k}+b_{k}\right)
mod
m_{k}.

We define functions
v
and
v^{\ast}
by

v\left(n\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\delta_{j+1}-\delta_{j}\right|+\delta_{0},\text{ \ }v^{\ast}\left(n\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\delta_{j}^{\ast},

The
n
-th Lebesgue constant is defined in the following way

L_{n}=\left\|D_{n}\right\|_{1}.

The norm (or quasi norm) of the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
is defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{G_{m}}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}\qquad\left(0<p<\infty\right).

The space
weak-L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
consists of all measurable
functions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}(G_{m})}:=\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\lambda^{p}\mu\left\{f>\lambda\right\}<+\infty.

Next, we introduce on
G_{m}
an orthonormal system which is called the
Vilenkin system.

At first define the complex valued function
r_{k}\left(x\right):G_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
the generalized Rademacher functions, as

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\exp\left(2\pi\imath x_{k}/m_{k}\right)\text{
\qquad}\left(\imath^{2}=-1,\text{ }x\in G_{m},\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now define the Vilenkin system
\psi:=(\psi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G_{m}
as:

\psi_{n}\left(x\right):=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Specially, we call this system the Walsh-Paley one if
m\equiv 2.

The Vilenkin system is orthonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G_{m}\right)\,
(for details see e.g.
[
1
,
16
,
37
]
).

If
\ f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
then we can define Fourier coefficients,
partial sums of the Fourier series, Fejér means, Dirichlet and Fejér kernels
with respect to the Vilenkin system in the usual manner:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(k)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\psi}_{k}d\mu\text{\thinspace\qquad\ \ \ \ }\left(\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ }\right);

\displaystyle S_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\psi_{k}\ \text{\qquad\ \ }\left(\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\text{ }S_{0}f:=0\right);

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S_{k}f\text{ \qquad\ \ \ \ \ }\left(\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\text{ }\right);

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k\text{ }}\text{ \qquad\ \ \qquad}\left(\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\text{ }\right);

\displaystyle K_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}D_{k}\text{ \qquad\ \ \ \thinspace}\left(\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\text{ }\right).

Recall that (for details see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
11
]
)

(8)

\quad\hskip 0.0ptD_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\text{ }M_{n}\text{ \ \ \ }x\in I_{n}\\
\text{ }0\text{ \qquad}x\notin I_{n}\end{array}\right.

and

(9)

D_{s_{n}M_{n}}=D_{M_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{s_{n}-1}\psi_{kM_{n}}=D_{M_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{s_{n}-1}r_{n}^{k},\text{ \qquad}1\leq s_{n}\leq m_{n}-1.

The
\sigma
-algebra generated by the intervals
\left\{I_{n}\left(x\right):x\in G_{m}\right\}
will be denoted by
\digamma_{n}

\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).
Denote by
f=\left(f_{n},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
a martingale with respect to
\digamma_{n}

\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
(for details see e.g.
[
38
]
). The maximal function of a
martingale
f
is defined by

f^{*}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|f_{n}\right|.

In the case
f\in L_{1}(G_{m}),
the maximal functions are also be given by

f^{*}\left(x\right)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left|I_{n}\left(x\right)\right|}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)\mu\left(u\right)\right|.

For
0<p<\infty
the Hardy martingale spaces
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)

consist of all martingales for which

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}:=\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

If
f\in L_{1}(G_{m}),
then it is easy to show that the sequence
\left(S_{M_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a martingale. If
f=(f_{n},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N})
is martingale, then the Vilenkin-Fourier coefficients must be
defined in a slightly different manner:

\widehat{f}\left(i\right):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{G_{m}}f_{k}\left(x\right)\overline{\psi}_{i}\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right).

The Vilenkin-Fourier coefficients of
f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
are
the same as those of the martingale
\left(S_{M_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
obtained from
f
.

A bounded measurable function
a
is p-atom, if there exist an interval
I
,
such that

\int_{I}ad\mu=0,\text{ \ \ }\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq\mu\left(I\right)^{-1/p},\text{ \ \ supp}\left(a\right)\subset I.\qquad

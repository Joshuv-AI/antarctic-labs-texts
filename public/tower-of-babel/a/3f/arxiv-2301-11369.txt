On unramified automorphic forms over the projective line
Roberto Alvarenga, Valdir Pereira Júnior

Abstract
. Let q q be a prime power and 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} be the finite field with q q elements. In this article we investigate the space of unramified automorphic forms for PGL n \PGL_{n} over the rational function field defined over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} (i.e. for ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} defined over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} ). In particular, we prove that the space of unramified cusp form is trivial and (for n = 3 n=3 ) that the space of eigenforms is one dimensional. Moreover, we show that there are no nontrivial unramified toroidal forms for PGL 3 \PGL_{3} over ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} and conjecture that the space of all toroidal automorphic forms is trivial.



1.
Introduction

Automorphic forms are central objects in modern number theory, for instance, they play a key role in the celebrated Langlands program, see eg.
[
JL70
]
. The goal of this work is to probe the space of unramified automorphic forms for
\PGL_{n}
over the projective line.

Let
F
be either a local or a global field, such as the function field attached to a nonsingular projective curve. In the context of understanding the absolute Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F},F)
, which is a fundamental problem on number theory, automorphic forms play the following role. As the theory of representation of groups suggests, we may study
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F},F)
through its finite dimension representations.
Given a finite dimensional representation
\myrho:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F},F)\rightarrow\GL_{n}(k)
, where
k
equals to
{\mathbb{C}}
or
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
for
\ell
a prime number different from the characteristic of
F
, there exists
L(s,\myrho)
an analytic invariant attached to
\myrho
, its
L
-function. Hence we might investigate
\myrho
, and thus understand
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F},F)
, by means of its
L
-function. For
n=1
, this approach is fundamental for the development of abelian class field theory and the understanding of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F},F)^{ab}
.

On the other side, when
F
is a global field, we might consider
\mathbb{A}
its adelic ring and the so-called
automorphic representations (forms)

\mypi
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
. Here is where emerge the main object of this paper. Attached to each automorphic representation
\mypi
, we also have an analytic invariant
L(s,\mypi)
, its
L
-function. The celebrated Langlands conjectures predict the existence of a correspondence between the
n
-dimensional representations of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F},F)
and the automorphic representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
which preserves in some sense the respective
L
-functions. We refer
[
BCdS+03
, Chap. 10 and 12]
for a complete discussion about how automorphic forms (representations) plays a role in the Langlands program.

Cusp forms

When
F
is the function field of a nonsingular projective geometrically irreducible curve
X
, automorphic forms with trivial central character and fixed ramification can be identified with continuous functions on the double quotient

\GL_{n}(F)Z(\mathbb{A})\setminus\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})/K

that are of moderate growth (see Section
2
for precise definitions). Where
K
stands for a compact open subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
and
Z(\mathbb{A})
is the center of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
.
Hence the domain of a
cusp
automorphic form (see Definition
3.1
) with fixed ramification is discrete modulo the right-action of a compact open subgroup
K
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
, which allows for explicit descriptions, see Lemma
3.5
.
In this context, roughly speaking, the geometric Langlands correspondence(cf.
[
Dri80
]
and
[
Laf02
]
)
states the existence of a bijective map between

(1)

the set isomorphism classes of irreducible continuous representations

\myrho:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F},F)\rightarrow\GL_{n}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}})

which are unramified outside a finite number of places and whose determinant is of finite order and,

(2)

the space of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-valued automorphic representations which are
cuspidal
and whose central character is of finite order.

Moreover, above correspondence is the bijection given by global class field theory for
n=1
and “preserves” the respective
L
-functions for
n>1
. We prove in section
3
that the space of cusp forms over the projective line is trivial. See Definition
3.1
for the definition of cusp forms.

\mathcal{H}_{K}
-eigenforms

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}}:=\prod\mathcal{O}_{x}
where the product is taken over all places
x
of
F
and
\mathcal{O}_{x}
is the ring of integers of the completion of
F
with respect to
x
. When
K
above is given by
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}})
, the standard maximal compact open subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
, we have the so-called
unramified
automorphic forms. For global function fields, the behave of unramified automorphic forms are particularly nice: every
unramified automorphic representation contains a unique unramified automorphic form,
or spherical vector, up to scalar multiples. Therefore the unramified automorphic representations correspond to certain
1
-dimensional representations of the (commutative)
spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{K}
, see Definition
2.1
. In other words, unramified automorphic representations
correspond to eigenforms of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
and are therefore determined by their eigenvalues
under the action of Hecke operators. We use the explicit description of the
graphs of Hecke operators
introduced by Lorscheid in
[
Lor13a
]
to parametrize the spaces of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
-eigenforms when
F
is the rational function field.

Toroidal forms

Associated to a degree
n
separable extension of
F
there exists a maximal torus of
\GL_{n}
. The automorphic forms which vanish the integral along that torus, for every degree
n
separable extension of
F
, are called
toroidal
automorphic forms, cf. Definition
5.3
. When
F={\mathbb{Q}}
, these special class of automorphic forms are closed related to the classical Riemann hypothesis, cf.
[
Zag81
]
. When
F
is the rational function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we prove in section
5
that there does not exist nontrivial (unramified) toroidal automorphic forms. Moreover, we conjecture that this is the situation for the whole space of toroidal automorphic forms for every
n
.

(resp.

Let

We might assume

. We will explain in case of ambiguity, which of these embeddings we use.

Let

be the center of

,
G(F):=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
and

. Note that
G(\mathbb{A})
comes together with an adelic topology that turns

into a locally compact group. Hence

is endowed with a Haar measure. We fix the Haar measure on

for which
\mathrm{vol}(K)=1.
The topology of
G(\mathbb{A})
has a neighborhood basis
\mathcal{V}
of the identity matrix that is given by all subgroups

K^{\prime}=\prod_{x\in|X|}K_{x}^{\prime}<\prod_{x\in|X|}K_{x}=K

where

, such that for all

the subgroup

of

is open and consequently of finite index and such that

differs from

only for a finite number of places.

Hecke algebra

Consider the space
C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A}))
of continuous functions
f:G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}.
Such a function is called
smooth
if it is locally constant. The group
G(\mathbb{A})
acts on
C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A}))
through the right regular representation

\myrho:G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A}))),

which is defined by right translation of the argument:
(g.f)(h):=(\myrho(g)f)(h):=f(hg)
for
g,h\in G(\mathbb{A})
and
f\in C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A})).

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G(\mathbb{A})
. We say that
f\in C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A}))
is
left
or
right

H
-
invariant
if for all
h\in H
and
g\in G(\mathbb{A})
,

f(hg)=f(g)\text{ or }f(gh)=f(g)
, respectively.
If
f
is right and left
H
-invariant, it is called
bi
-
H
-
invariant
.

Definition 2.1
.

The complex vector space
\mathcal{H}
of all smooth compactly supported functions
\Phi:G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
together with the convolution product

\Phi_{1}\ast\Phi_{2}:g\longmapsto\int_{G(\mathbb{A})}\Phi_{1}(gh^{-1})\Phi_{2}(h)dh

for
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
is called the
Hecke algebra for
G(\mathbb{A})
. Its elements are called
Hecke operators
.

The Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
acts on
C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A}))
by

\Phi(f):g\longmapsto\int_{G(\mathbb{A})}\Phi(h)f(gh)dh.

We say that a function

is
\mathcal{H}
-
finite
if the space
\mathcal{H}\cdot f
is finite dimensional.

The zero element of
\mathcal{H}
is the zero function, but there is no multiplicative unit. For
K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V},
we define
\mathcal{H}_{K^{\prime}}
to be the subalgebra of all bi-
K^{\prime}
-invariant elements. These subalgebras have multiplicative units. Namely, the normalized characteristic function
\myepsilon_{K^{\prime}}:=(\mathrm{vol}K^{\prime})^{-1}\mathrm{char}_{K^{\prime}}
acts as the identity on
\mathcal{H}_{K^{\prime}}
by convolution.

Definition 2.2
.

When
K^{\prime}=K
above, we call
\mathcal{H}_{K}
the
spherical (unramified) part of

\mathcal{H}
and its elements are called
spherical (unramified) Hecke operators
.

Every

is bi-
K^{\prime}
-invariant for some

, see
[
Lor08

Furthermore,

&\rm{I}_{n-r}\end{matrix}\right)K

(resp.

Identifying

with

an isomorphism of

Proof.

See

Automorphic forms

A function

is called
K
-
finite
if the complex vector space that is generated by

is finite dimensional.

We embed

g\mapsto(g,\det(g)^{-1}).
We define a local height

(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n^{2}+1})\mapsto\mathrm{max}\{|v_{1}|_{x},\ldots,|v_{n^{2}+1}|_{x}\}

F_{x}^{n^{2}+1}

|g_{x}|_{x}\geqslant 1

|g_{x}|_{x}=1
if

We define the global height

if there exists constants

such that

|f(g)|_{{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant C|g|^{N}

for all
g\in G(\mathbb{A}).

Let
V\subset C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A}))
be a sub-representation of
G({\mathbb{A}})
on

. For
K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
, let

be the subspace of all

that are right
K^{\prime}
-invariant i.e. such that
\myrho(k^{\prime})f=f
for all
k^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
. We say that the representation
V
is
admissible
if
V^{K^{\prime}}
is finite dimensional for every
K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
. In the following we take
V=\myrho(G({\mathbb{A}}))f
for some

f\in C^{0}(G(\mathbb{A})).

The
space of automorphic forms

(with trivial central character) is the complex vector space of all functions

-invariant and such that the smooth representation

.

3.
Cusp forms

In this section we investigate the space of unramified cusp forms for
\PGL_{n}
over the projective line.

Definition 3.1
.

An automorphic form
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
is a
cusp form
(or cuspidal) if

\int_{U(F)\setminus U({\mathbb{A}})}f(ug)du=0

for all
g\in G({\mathbb{A}})
and all unipotent radicals subgroups
U
of all standard parabolic subgroups
P
of
G({\mathbb{A}})
. We denote the whole space of cusp forms by
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
. If
f\in{\mathcal{A}}^{K}
is a cusp form we call it an
unramified cusp form
and denote the whole space of unramified cusp forms by
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{K}
.

Geometric interpretation

Let
\Bun_{n}X
be the set of
isomorphism classes of rank
n
vector bundles on
X
. Let
{\mathbb{P}}\Bun_{n}X
stands for the set of isomorphism classes of rank
n
projective vector bundles. The vector bundles
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\in\Bun_{n}X
are in the same class in
{\mathbb{P}}\Bun_{n}X
if there exists a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\Pic X
such that
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{L}.
In this case we denote
\overline{\mathcal{E}}=\overline{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}\Bun_{n}X.

It is well known that the double quotient
\GL_{1}(F)\setminus\GL_{1}(\mathbb{A})/\GL_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{A}}})
is in bijection with the set of classes of divisors on
X
. Hence, it yields the following bijection

\GL_{1}(F)\setminus\GL_{1}(\mathbb{A})/\GL_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{A}}})\longleftrightarrow\Bun_{1}X

since the set of classes of divisors on
X
are in correspondence with the line bundles over
X
.
The following theorem, due to Weil, extends above bijection to higher rank vector bundles on
X
.

Theorem 3.2

(Weil)
.

For every
n\geqslant 1
, there exists a bijection

\displaystyle\GL_{n}(F)\setminus\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})/\GL_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{A}}})

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\Bun_{n}(X)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathcal{E}_{g}

such that
\mathcal{E}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{a}=\mathcal{E}_{ag}
for
a\in{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
and
\mathcal{L}_{a}
the correspondent line bundle. Moreover,
\deg\mathcal{E}_{g}=\deg(\det g).

Proof.

Its well know that vector bundles are completely determined by its transition maps. The bijection follows, essentially, by associate to a vector bundle the adelic matrix given by the stalks of its transition maps. See either
[
Lor08
, Lemma 5.1.6]
or
[
Fre04
, Lemma 3.1]
for a complete proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For every
n\geqslant 1
, there exists a bijection

\GL_{n}(F)Z(\mathbb{A})\setminus\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})/\GL_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{A}}})\longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\Bun_{n}(X)

where
Z(\mathbb{A})
is the center of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})

Remark 3.4
.

Therefore, due to Weil’s theorem, we can interpret unramified automorphic forms

as the space of complex valued functions on

The next lemma reinterprets the cuspidal condition in geometric terms. Despite it is already known and largely used in the literature,
see for example
[
Kap97
, Sec. 2]
and
[
Bum97
, pag. 296]

We first observe, following
[

, Lemma 3]
, that
f\in\mathcal{A}^{K}
is cuspidal if

\int_{U(F)\setminus U({\mathbb{A}})}f(ug)du=0

for all

and all unipotent radicals subgroups
U
of all

parabolic subgroups
P
of

.

We fix

a maximal parabolic subgroup of

. It is well known that there exists integers

such that
P=UM
where

is the unipotent of

cf.
[

. One says that

is a parabolic maximal subgroup of type
(r,s)
.
Observe furthermore that an element of

has the following form

\left(\begin{matrix}\rm{I}_{r\times r}&h_{s\times r}\\
0&\rm{I}_{s\times s}\end{matrix}\right)

where
\rm{I}_{r\times r}
(resp.
\rm{I}_{s\times s}
) stands for the
r\times r
(resp.
s\times s
) identity matrix and

is a matrix with

That is,
U\cong M_{r,s}

From Iwasawa decomposition,
G({\mathbb{A}})=P({\mathbb{A}})K
where

is the above fixed maximal parabolic subgroup. Given

, we write

where

Hence,

\int_{U(F)\setminus U({\mathbb{A}})}f(ug)du=\int_{U(F)\setminus U({\mathbb{A}})}f(uxmk)du=\int_{U(F)\setminus U({\mathbb{A}})}f(ym)dy=0.

for all

and where

We write

y=\left(\begin{matrix}\rm{I}_{r\times r}&h_{s\times r}\\
0&\rm{I}_{s\times s}\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad m=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{r}&0\\
0&h_{s}\end{matrix}\right)

where

,
h_{s}\in\GL_{s}({\mathbb{A}})

.
Let

(resp.

)

.
Applying once again Weil’s theorem,

corresponds to an extension of

, see
[

[
Sha13

.
Therefore, considering an unramified automorphic form as a complex valuated map from

Lemma 3.5
.

An unramified automorphic form
f\in{\mathcal{A}}^{K}
is a cusp form if for any integers

with

{\mathcal{F}}\in\Bun_{r}X
,
{\mathcal{G}}\in\Bun_{s}X

(1)

\sum_{\mathcal{E}\in\Ext({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})}f(\mathcal{E})=0,

where we abuse the notation and write
\mathcal{E}
meant the middle term of the correspondent exact sequence.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
F
be the field of rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
i.e. the function field of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
the projective line defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{K}
is trivial for every
n\geqslant 2
.

Proof.

We denote the structural sheaf of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
simply by
{\mathcal{O}}
, i.e.
{\mathcal{O}}:={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}
.
Hence the canonical sheaf
\myomega:=\myomega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}(-2).

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\in\Bun_{r}X
and
{\mathcal{G}}\in\Bun_{s}X
, for some integers
r,s>0
. The Serre duality (see
[
Har77
, Sec. III.7]
) yields

\Ext^{1}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})^{\vee}\cong\Hom({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(-2))=\mathrm{H}^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(-2)\otimes{\mathcal{G}}^{\vee}).

From the Grothendieck classification (actually this was already known by Dedekind and Weber, see
[
DW12
]
) of vector bundles on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, see
[
GW10
, Thm. 11.51]
, every rank
n
vector bundle on the projective line is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(d_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(d_{n})

for some integers
d_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant d_{n}.

Hence we might write
{\mathcal{F}}:=\mathcal{O}(k_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(k_{r})
and

{\mathcal{G}}:=\mathcal{O}(\ell_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(\ell_{s})
, for some integers

k_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant k_{r}
and
\ell_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\ell_{s}
. Thus

{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\myomega\otimes{\mathcal{G}}^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{i,j}{\mathcal{O}}(k_{i}-\ell_{j}-2).

Let
k:=\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\}
and
\ell:=\min\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
. If
d-\ell<2
, then

\Ext^{1}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})=\{0\}

since
\Ext^{1}
commutes with direct sum and
\Hom(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\{0\}
if
\deg(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})>\deg(\mathcal{L})
for
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
line bundles.

Let
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{O}(d_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(d_{n})
be any rank n vector bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with
d_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant d_{n}.
If we take above
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{O}}(d_{1})
and
{\mathcal{G}}=\mathcal{O}(d_{2})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(d_{n})
, thus
\Ext^{1}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})=\{0\}
, i.e.
\Ext^{1}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})
consists only by the extension given by
\mathcal{E}
.
Therefore, for
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{K}
a cusp form, follows from above discussion and the geometric interpretation of cuspidal condition, Lemma
3.5
, that

f(\overline{\mathcal{E}})=0,\quad\text{ for all }\mathcal{E}\in\Bun_{n}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

and hence
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}^{K}
is trivial.
∎

Definition 4.1

\GL_{3}
. Given
\mylambda_{1},\mylambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
a closed point of degree one, there exists a nontrivial Eisenstein series induced from an unramified character of
T
which is an eigenfunction for
\Phi_{x,r}
(
r=1,2
) with eigenvalues
\mylambda_{1},\mylambda_{2}
, see
[
PJ20
, Thm. 1.7.7]
.
It follows from
[
PJ20
, Thm. 1.7.9]
and above theorem that

(2)

A_{0}^{K}\cap\mathcal{A}^{K}(x,\mylambda_{1},\mylambda_{2})=\{0\}.

Furthermore,
\mathcal{A}_{0}
splits as a direct sum of irreducible representations. Therefore,
we can write every
f\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{K}
as a sum of eigenforms and thus
f=0
by above (
2
).

∎

5.
Toroidal autormophic forms

We define the space of toroidal automorphic forms for any global function field
F
and, using the results from previous sections, we derive some results when
F
is the function field of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let
F
be any global function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
E/F
be a separable field extension of degree
n
.
Choosing a basis for
E
over
F
gives an embedding of
E^{*}
in
G(F)
and a non-split maximal torus
T\subseteq G
with
T(F)=E^{*}
and
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})=\mathbb{A}_{E}^{*}
. In this case we say that
T
is associates to
E/F
. We refer
[
Lor08
, Def. 1.5.1]
for the definitions of non-split and maximal torus.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
T
be a maximal torus of
\GL_{n}
over
F
associated with a separable extension
E/F
of degree
n
. Let
\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{A}_{F}
. Endow
T(\mathbb{A})
and
T(F)Z(\mathbb{A})
with the Haar measures and
T(F)Z(\mathbb{A})\setminus T(\mathbb{A})
with the quotient measure. For
f\in\mathcal{A}
we define

f_{T}(g):=\int_{T(F)Z(\mathbb{A})\setminus T(\mathbb{A})}f(tg)dt

the
toroidal integral
of
f
along
T
.

Remark 5.2
.

The quotient
T(F)Z(\mathbb{A})\setminus T(\mathbb{A})
is compact, see
[
PJ20
, Pag. 42]
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
T
be a maximal torus of
\GL_{n}
over
F
associated with a separable extension
E/F
of degree
n
. We define

\mathcal{A}_{tor}(E):=\big\{f\in\mathcal{A}\;|\;f_{T}(g)=0,\forall g\in G(\mathbb{A})\big\}

the space of
E
-toroidal automorphic forms
, and

\mathcal{A}_{tor}=\bigcap_{E/F}\mathcal{A}_{tor}(E)

the space of
toroidal automorphic forms
, where
E/F
runs over the separable extensions of degree
n
.

Remark 5.4
.

The spaces
\mathcal{A}_{tor}(E)
do not depend on the choice of the basis for
E
over
F
, see
[
PJ20
, Rem. 2.1.3]
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
F
be the function field of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
E
be the constant field extension of
F
of degree
3
, i.e.
E=\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}F.
If
x
a degree one place of
F
and
\mylambda_{1},\mylambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, then

\mathcal{A}_{tor}(E)\cap\mathcal{A}^{K}(x,\mylambda_{1},\mylambda_{2})=\{0\}

there does not exist nontrivial
\Phi_{x,r}
-toroidal eigenforms for
n=3
and
r=1,2
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the
3
-dimensional torus associated to
E/F
, where
E=\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}F.
Thus
E
is the function field of
\mathbb{P}_{3}^{1}:=\mathbb{P}^{1}\otimes_{\mathrm{Spec}\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathrm{Spec}\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
, the extension of scalars of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The extension of scalars yields

p:\mathbb{P}_{3}^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

the projection map.
Moreover
p
induces the inverse image
p^{*}:\Bun_{3}X\rightarrow\Bun_{3}\mathbb{P}_{3}^{1}
and the direct image (or trace)
p_{*}:\Bun_{1}\mathbb{P}_{3}^{1}\rightarrow\Bun_{3}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

From
[
PJ20
, Thm. 2.8.1]

\int_{T(F)Z(\mathbb{A})\setminus T(\mathbb{A})}f(tg)\mymu(t)=c_{T}\cdot\sum_{[\mathcal{L}]\in\mathrm{Pic}\mathbb{P}_{3}^{1}/p^{*}\mathrm{Pic}\mathbb{P}^{1}}f(\overline{p_{*}\mathcal{L}})

where and

c_{T}=\frac{vol(T(F)Z({\mathbb{A}})\setminus T({\mathbb{A}}))}{\#(\Pic\mathbb{P}_{3}^{1}/p^{*}\Pic{\mathbb{P}}^{1})}

Hence
f\in\mathcal{A}(x,\mylambda_{1},\mylambda_{2})
is
E
-toroidal, thus

f(\myvarepsilon_{0})=0
.
Therefore Theorem
4.12
implies that if
f(\myvarepsilon_{0})=0
, then
f
is trivial.
∎

Since the zeta function of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
has no zeros, a possible connection with the space of toroidal automorphic forms lead us to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.6
.

For all
n\geqslant 0
,
\mathcal{A}_{tor}=\{0\}.

We end this article with the following partial answer for previous conjecture.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
F
be the function field of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
E
be the constant field extension of
F
of degree
3
, i.e.
E=\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}F.
Then,

\mathcal{A}_{tor}(E)\cap\mathcal{A}^{K}=\{0\}
and therefore

\mathcal{A}_{tor}\cap\mathcal{A}^{K}=\{0\}
for
n=3
.

Proof.

We suppose by contradiction that
\mathcal{A}_{tor}(E)\cap\mathcal{A}^{K}\neq\{0\}.
Hence, let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{tor}(E)\cap\mathcal{A}^{K}
such that
f\neq 0,
By admissibility condition,
V=\mathcal{H}_{K}\cdot f
is a finite dimensional vector space, Moreover,

V
is invariant by the action of
\Phi_{x,1},\Phi_{x,2}\in\mathcal{H_{K}}
for some
x\in|\mathbb{P}^{1}|
of degree one. Thus, there exists a
\Phi_{x,r}
-eigenform (for
r=1,2
) in
V
, which disagree with Theorem
5.5
.
∎

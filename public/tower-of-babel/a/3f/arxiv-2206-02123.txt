On the volume of the Minkowski sum of zonoids
Matthieu Fradelizi, Mokshay Madiman, Mathieu Meyer, Artem Zvavitch

Abstract
We explore some inequalities in convex geometry restricted to the class of zonoids. We show the equivalence, in the class of zonoids, between a local Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality, a local Loomis-Whitney inequality, the log-submodularity of volume, and the Dembo-Cover-Thomas conjecture on the monotonicity of the ratio of volume to the surface area. In addition to these equivalences, we confirm these conjectures in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} and we establish an improved inequality in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} . Along the way, we give a negative answer to a question of Adam Marcus regarding the roots of the Steiner polynomial of zonoids. We also investigate analogous questions in the L p L_{p} -Brunn-Minkowski theory, and in particular, we confirm all of the above conjectures in the case p = 2 p=2 , in any dimension.



1
Introduction

The deep parallels between inequalities in Information Theory and Convex Geometry have been explored intensively. The recognition of these parallels goes back to at least 1984, when Costa and Cover
[
14
]
observed the formal resemblance between the Brunn-Minkowski inequality in Convex Geometry and the entropy power inequality in Information Theory.
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality (see, e.g.,
[
26
,
44
]
)
states that for all compact sets
A,B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

|A+B|^{1/n}\geq|A|^{1/n}+|B|^{1/n}
, where we write
|A|
for the volume of
A
.
The entropy power inequality (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
) states that for any pair of independent random vectors
X,Y
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

N(X+Y)\geq N(X)+N(Y)
,
where

N(X)=e^{\frac{2h(X)}{n}}

denotes the entropy power of
X
, and

h(X)=-\int f(x)\log f(x)dx
is the entropy of
X
if
X
has density
f
(and
h(X)=-\infty
if not). Despite the apparent distinctness of the settings, a compelling case can be made (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
46
,
26
,
48
,
36
]
) that these inequalities are deeply connected – in particular, it is now well understood that the functional
A\mapsto|A|^{1/n}
in the geometry of compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and the functional
f_{X}\mapsto N(X)
in probability are analogous in many (but not all) ways.
In the last decade, several further developments have been made that link
Information Theory to the Brunn-Minkowski theory, including entropy analogues of Blaschke-Santaló’s inequality
[
33
]
,
reverse Brunn-Minkowski’s inequality
[
6
,
7
]
, Rogers-Shephard’s inequality
[
9
,
35
]
and Busemann’s inequality
[
4
]
.
Indeed, volume inequalities and entropy inequalities (and also certain small ball inequalities
[
37
]
) can be unified using
the framework of Rényi entropies; this framework and the relevant literature is surveyed in
[
36
]
.

The analogies between Information Theory and Convex Geometry are not so direct when one moves to second-order functionals. In particular, convex geometry analogues of Fisher information inequalities hold sometimes but not always
[
22
,
2
,
25
]
.
Motivated by these analogies, Dembo, Cover and Thomas
[
17
]
proposed the inequality, called concavity of the ratio of volume to the surface area,

\frac{|A+B|}{|\partial(A+B)|}\geq\frac{|A|}{|\partial A|}+\frac{|B|}{|\partial B|}

(1)

as a natural analogue dual to the Fisher information inequality. It was noticed already in
[
17
]
(see also
[
27
]
) that (
1
) is true when
A
(or
B
) is an Euclidean Ball, moreover, in
[
22
]
, it was proved that this last inequality holds for any convex bodies
A
and
B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. It is interesting to note that even a weaker conjecture, called monotonicity of the ratio of volume to the surface area,

\frac{|A+B|}{|\partial(A+B)|}\geq\frac{|A|}{|\partial A|}

(2)

is not trivial. The case of
B
being a segment already gives another natural conjecture

\frac{|\partial(P_{u^{\perp}}A)|}{|P_{u^{\perp}}A|}\leq\frac{|\partial A|}{|A|},

(3)

where
P_{u^{\perp}}A
denotes the orthogonal projection
A
onto the hyperplane with normal vector
u\in S^{n-1}
. We explain in details the relationships between the above conjectures as parts of Theorems
3.6
and
3.9
below.
It was proved in
[
27
]
(see also Lemma
5.3
for a simple proof) that (
3
) holds with with a multiplicative constant:

\frac{|\partial(P_{u^{\perp}}A)|}{|P_{u^{\perp}}A|}\leq\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{|\partial A|}{|A|},

(4)

Moreover, it was shown in
[
22
]
that the constant is sharp. Thus, there are counterexamples to (
3
) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for any
n\geq 3
and inequalities (
1
) and (
2
) are not true in general for the whole class of convex bodies in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
.
Conjecture (
1
) is connected with the following conjectured inequality for the volume of projections:

\frac{|A+B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}(A+B)|_{n-1}}\geq\frac{|A|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}}+\frac{|B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}B|_{n-1}}.

(5)

Bonnesen proved in
[
11
]
(see
[
44
]
equation (7.196)) that, for any convex bodies
A
,
B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

|A+B|\geq\left(|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}+|P_{u^{\bot}}B|_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{|A|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}}+\frac{|B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}B|_{n-1}}\right),

(6)

which is (
5
) for
n=2
. The fact that (
1
) is true in dimension
2
and in the case when one of the bodies is an Euclidean ball inspires the natural conjecture that (
1
) holds for zonoids. Recall that zonoids are Hausdorff limits of zonotopes and that zonotopes are Minkowski sums of segments. Our main goal, in this paper, is to study a weaker version of this conjecture: is it true that for two zonoids
A,B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we have

\frac{|A+B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}(A+B)|_{n-1}}\geq\frac{|A|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}}?

(7)

Note that (
7
) is again not true for general convex bodies and
n\geq 3
. We prove it for zonoids and
n=3
. We also present a number of equivalent and useful restatements of (
7
).

The second main contribution of this paper has to do with an analogue of the Plünnecke-Ruzsa
[
40
,
42
]
inequality for zonoids, or equivalently, the log-submodularity property of volume on the space of zonoids with respect to Minkowski summation. More precisely, we study the following conjecture from
[
24
]
:
given zonoids
A,B_{1},B_{2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one has

|A|\,|A+B_{1}+B_{2}|\,\leq\,|A+B_{1}|\,|A+B_{2}|.

(8)

This conjecture was inspired by Bobkov and the second-named author
[
7
]
who proved that for convex bodies
A,B_{1},B_{2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one has

|A|\,|A+B_{1}+B_{2}|\leq 3^{n}|A+B_{1}|\,|A+B_{2}|.

Recently, it was proved in
[
24
]
that the constant
3^{n}
in the preceding inequality may be replaced by
\varphi^{n}
, where
\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
is the golden ratio, that the best constant
c_{n}
is lower bounded by
(4/3+o(1))^{n}
and that
c_{2}=1
and
c_{3}=4/3
.
These observations for dimensions
n\geq 3
imply that, for general convex bodies, it is impossible to have (
8
).
In this paper, we prove that (
8
) holds in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
for zonoids.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect background material on mixed volumes. Numerous equivalent descriptions of log-submodularity, on a given class of convex bodies, are explored in Section
3
.
In Section
4
, we first present an improved version of
\log
-submodularity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by proving a strong version of two dimensional conjecture of T. Courtade, and we then prove inequality (
7
) in the special case of paralleletopes. Section
5

explores log-submodularity of volume on the class of zonoids– we first prove that this holds in

{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, and then discuss an array of inequalities that are equivalent to log-submodularity
for zonoids in arbitrary dimension (all of which therefore now hold in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
).
Along the way, we answer a question of Adam Marcus about Steiner polynomials of zonoids. Finally, in Section
6
, inspired by a work of Brazitikos and McIntyre
[
12
]
on vector-valued Maclaurin inequalities, we discuss possible extensions of our results to the more general class of
L^{p}
-zonoids, which appear in the more general
L_{p}
-Brunn-Minkowski theory.

2
Preliminaries on mixed volumes

In this section, we introduce basic notations and collect essential facts and definitions
from convex geometry that are used in the paper. As a general reference on the theory, we use
[
44
]
.
We write
\langle x,y\rangle
for the inner product of vectors
x
and
y
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and by
|x|
the Euclidean norm of a vector
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The closed Euclidean ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is denoted by
B_{2}^{n}
, and its boundary by
S^{n-1}
. We also denote by
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
the standard orthonormal basis in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Moreover, for any set in
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we denote its boundary by
\partial K
. A compact set
K
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called star-shaped if, for every
x\in K
, the segment
[0,x]
is a subset of
K
; its radial function
\rho_{K}
is then defined by
\rho_{K}(x)=\sup\{a;ax\in K\}
. When
0
belongs to the interior of
K
then
\|x\|_{K}=\rho_{K}^{-1}(x)

is the Minkowski functional of
K
. A convex body is a convex, compact set with non-empty interior. For a convex body
K
, we define its support function by
h_{K}(x)=\max_{y\in K}\langle x,y\rangle
.

We write
|K|_{m}
for the
m
-dimensional Lebesgue measure (volume) of a measurable set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
m=1,...,n
is the dimension
of the minimal affine space containing
K
, we often use the shorten notation
|K|
for
n
-dimensional volume.
From
[
44
, Theorem 5.1.6]
, for any compact convex sets
K_{1},\dots,K_{r}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and any non-negative numbers
t_{1},\dots,t_{r}
,
one has

\displaystyle\left|t_{1}K_{1}+\cdots+t_{r}K_{r}\right|=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}=1}^{r}t_{i_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{n}}V(K_{i_{1}},\dots,K_{i_{n}})

(9)

for some non-negative numbers
V(K_{i_{1}},\dots,K_{i_{n}})
, which are the mixed volumes of
K_{i_{1}},\dots,K_{i_{n}}
.
We also often use a two bodies version of (
9
):

\displaystyle\left|A+tB\right|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}t^{k}V(A[n-k],B[k]),

(10)

for any
t>0
and compact, convex sets
A,B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where for simplicity we use notation
A[m]
for a convex set
A
repeated
m
times.
Mixed volumes satisfy a number of extremely useful inequalities. The first one is the Brunn-Minkowski inequality

|K+L|^{1/n}\geq|K|^{1/n}+|L|^{1/n},

whenever
K,L
and
K+L
are measurable. A direct consequence are Minkowski’s first inequality

\displaystyle V(L,K[n-1])\geq|L|^{1/n}|K|^{(n-1)/n},

(11)

and Minkowski’s second inequality

\displaystyle V(L,K[n-1])^{2}\geq|K|V(L[2],K[n-2]),

(12)

for two convex, compact subsets
K
and
L
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We will use the classical integral representation for the mixed volume:

V(L,K[n-1])=\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}h_{L}(u)dS_{K}(u),

(13)

where
S_{K}
is the surface area measure of
K

[
44
]
.
Mixed volumes are also useful to study the volume of the orthogonal projections of convex bodies. Let
E
be an
m
-dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, for
1\leq m\leq n
and let
P_{E}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to E
be the orthogonal projection onto
E
. Then for any convex body
K
we have

\displaystyle|U|_{n-m}|P_{E}K|_{m}={{n}\choose{m}}V(K[m],U[n-m]),

(14)

where
U
is any convex body in the subspace
E^{\perp}
orthogonal to
E
. It follows from (
14
) that for any orthonormal system
u_{1},\dots,u_{r},

1\leq r\leq n
we get

\left|P_{[u_{1},\dots,u_{r}]^{\bot}}K\right|_{n-r}=\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}V(K[n-r],[0,u_{1}],\dots,[0,u_{r}]).

(15)

For example, denote by
u^{\perp}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle=0\}
the hyperplane orthogonal to a vector
u\in S^{n-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|P_{u^{\perp}}K|_{n-1}=nV(K[n-1],[0,u]).

(16)

Another useful formula is connected with the computation of surface area and mixed volumes:

\displaystyle|\partial K|=nV(K[n-1],B_{2}^{n}),

(17)

where by
|\partial K|
we denote the surface area of the convex body
K
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

A polytope which is the Minkowski sum of
finitely many line segments is called a zonotope. Limits of zonotopes in the Hausdorff metric are called zonoids, see
[
44
, Section 3.2]
for details. Consider zonotopes
Z_{j}=\sum_{i_{j}=1}^{N_{j}}[0,w_{i_{j}j}]
, where,
j=1,\dots,n
and
w_{i_{j}j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Using the linearity of mixed volumes, we get that

V(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n})=\sum V([0,w_{i_{1}1}],\dots[0,w_{i_{n}n}])=\frac{1}{n!}\sum|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i_{j}j}\}_{j=1}^{n})|,

(18)

where the sums runs over the integers
i_{j}\in\{1,\dots,N_{j}\}
, for
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Notice that, if
Z_{1}=[0,u]
, with
u\in S^{n-1}
, we may use the basic properties of determinants to show that

V([0,u],Z_{2},\dots,Z_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{P_{u^{\perp}}w_{i_{j}j}\}_{j=2}^{n})|,

where the sum is taken over all integer
i_{j}\in\{1,\dots,N_{j}\}
, for
j\in\{2,\dots,n\}
. We mainly use the following particular case: for
Z=[0,u_{1}]+\cdots+[0,u_{m}]
, then

\displaystyle|Z|=\!\!\!\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq m}

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(u_{i_{1}},\dots,u_{i_{n}})|

(19)

\displaystyle|P_{e^{\bot}}Z|=\!\!\!\sum_{1\leq i_{2}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq m}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(P_{e^{\bot}}u_{i_{2}},\dots,P_{e^{\bot}}u_{i_{n}})|.

3
Equivalent forms of the log-submodularity of volume

3.1
Submodularity

Let us recall the notion of submodular set function and remind some basic properties, see
[
34
,
47
,
21
,
24
]
for more information on this subject and the proofs of the theorems. We denote
{[n]}=\{1,\cdots,n\}
and let
2^{[n]}
be the family of subsets of
{[n]}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A set function
F:2^{[n]}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is submodular if

\displaystyle F(S\cup T)+F(S\cap T)\leq F(S)+F(T)\quad\mbox{for all subsets $S,T$ of $[n]$}.

(20)

Submodularity is closely related to a partial ordering on hypergraphs
[
18
,
10
,
3
]
.
Let
\mbox{${\cal M}$}(n,m)
be the following family of
multi-hypergraphs: each consists of non-empty subsets
S_{i}
of
[n]
,

\sum_{i}|S_{i}|=m
, with
S_{i}=S_{j}
allowed.
Consider a given multi-hypergraph

\mathcal{C}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{l}\}\in\mbox{${\cal M}$}(n,m)
.
Take any pair of non-nested sets
\{S_{i},S_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{C}

and let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}(i,j)
be obtained from
\mathcal{C}
by replacing
S_{i}
and
S_{j}

by
S_{i}\cap S_{j}
and
S_{i}\cup S_{j}
,
keeping only
S_{i}\cup S_{j}
if
S_{i}\cap S_{j}=\emptyset
.

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is called an elementary compression of
\mathcal{C}
. The result of a sequence of
elementary compressions is called a compression.
Define a partial order on
\mbox{${\cal M}$}(n,m)
by setting
\mathcal{A}>\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
if
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a
compression of
\mathcal{A}
.
Using transitivity of the partial order and reducing to elementary compression, we get the following theorem (see
[
3
]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
F
is a submodular function on
[n]
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be finite multi-hypergraph of subsets of
[n]
, with
\mathcal{A}>\mathcal{B}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{A}}F(S)\geq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{B}}F(T).

For every multi-hypergraph
\mathcal{A}\in\mbox{${\cal M}$}(n,m)
there is a unique minimal multi-hypergraph

\mathcal{A}^{\#}
dominated by
\mathcal{A}
consisting of the sets

S^{\#}_{j}=\{i\in[n]:i\text{ lies in at least }j\text{ of the sets }S\in\mathcal{A}\}
. One implication of Theorem
3.2
is that submodular functions
F
with
F(\varphi)=0
are “fractionally subadditive” (see, e.g.,
[
38
]
)– this property has also been investigated in connection with volumes of Minkowski sums
[
8
,
23
,
5
]
.

We also have a notion of submodularity on the positive octant of the Euclidean space.

Definition 3.3
.

A function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is submodular if, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,

\displaystyle f(x\vee y)+f(x\wedge y)\leq f(x)+f(y),

where
x\vee y
(resp.
x\wedge y
) denotes the componentwise maximum (resp. minimum) of
x
and
y
.

The next very simple lemma connects submodularity for functions defined on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}
to submodularity for set functions. For a set
S\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
, let
\hbox{\rm\large{1}}_{S}
be the vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the
i
-th coordinate of
\hbox{\rm\large{1}}_{S}
is 1, for
i\in S
and
0
, for
i\notin S
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is submodular, and we set
F(S):=f(\hbox{\rm\large{1}}_{S})
for each
S\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,
then
F
is a submodular set function.

The fact that submodular functions are closely related to functions
with decreasing differences is classical (see, e.g.,
[
34
]
or
[
47
]
, which describes
more general results involving arbitrary lattices). We denote by
\partial^{2}_{i,j}
the second derivative with respect to coordinates
i,j
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a
C^{2}
function. Then
f
is submodular if and only if

\partial_{i,j}^{2}f\leq 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}
, for every
i\neq j
.

3.2
Classes closed under sums and dilation

Let us now show the various forms that the notions of submodularity and log-submodularity can have applied to volume of convex compact sets.

Theorem 3.6
.

Consider a collection
\cal{K}
of compact convex sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
stable by sums and dilation. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

For every
m\geq 1
and every
A,B_{1},\dots,B_{m}
in
{\cal K}

\displaystyle|A|^{m-1}\left|A+\sum_{i=1}^{m}B_{i}\right|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}|A+B_{i}|.

(21)

2.

For every
m\geq 1
and
A,B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\cal{K}
, the function
\bar{w}:2^{[m]}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by

\displaystyle\bar{w}(S)=\log\bigg|A+\sum_{i\in S}B_{i}\bigg|

(22)

for each
S\in 2^{[m]}
, is a submodular.

3.

For every
m\geq 1
and any multi-hypergraphs
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
on
[m]
with
\mathcal{A}>\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{A}}\bar{w}(S)\geq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{B}}\bar{w}(T),

where
\bar{w}
is defined by (
22
).

4.

For every
m\geq 1
and every
A,B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\cal{K}
, the function
w:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined, for
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}
, by

\displaystyle w(x)=\log\bigg|A+\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}B_{i}\bigg|,

(23)

is submodular.

5.

For every
A,B_{1},B_{2}\in\cal{K}

\displaystyle|A|V(A[n-2],B_{1},B_{2})\leq\frac{n}{n-1}V(A[n-1],B_{1})V(A[n-1],B_{2}).

Proof.

2.
\implies
1. This follows directly from the definition.

1.
\implies
2.: We use (
21
) with
A+\sum_{i\in S\cap T}B_{i}
,
\sum_{i\in S\setminus T}B_{i}
and
\sum_{i\in T\setminus S}B_{i}
.

2.
\Longleftrightarrow
3.: This follows from Theorem
3.2
applied to
-\bar{w}
.

2.
\Longleftrightarrow
4.: This comes from Lemma
3.4
and the fact that
\cal{C}
is dilation invariant.

4.
\Longleftrightarrow
5.: We apply
Proposition
3.5
to
f=-w
. Let
K_{x}=A+\sum_{i}x_{i}B_{i}
and
v(x)=|K_{x}|
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{j}v(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{|K_{x}+\varepsilon B_{j}|-|K_{x}|}{\varepsilon}=nV(K_{x}[n-1],B_{j}),

and for
k\neq j

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{j,k}^{2}v(x)&=n\partial_{k}V(K_{x}[n-1],B_{j})\\
&=n\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{V((K_{x}+\varepsilon B_{k})[n-1],B_{j})-V(K_{x}[n-1],B_{j})}{\varepsilon}\\
&=n(n-1)V(K_{x}[n-2],B_{k},B_{j}).\end{split}

By Proposition
3.5
, the submodularity of
w
is equivalent to
\partial_{j,k}^{2}w(x)\leq 0
. But
w=\log(v)
so

\displaystyle\partial_{j,k}^{2}w(x)=\frac{v\partial_{jk}v-\partial_{j}v\partial_{k}v}{v^{2}},

which is negative if and only if

\displaystyle|K_{x}|V(K_{x}[n-2],B_{j},B_{k})\leq\frac{n}{n-1}V(K_{x}[n-1],B_{j})V(K_{x}[n-1],B_{k}).

Thus plugging
x=0
we get
4.\implies 5.
and using 4. with
K
instead of
A
gives
5.\implies 4.

\Box

We note that it is enough to check property 1. in Theorem
3.6
, just in the case

. Indeed the case of general

follows by an iteration argument.

We also note that, in dimension

holds for any convex bodies by the classical local version of Alexandrov’s inequality that was proved by W. Fenchel (see

for any convex compact sets

(24)

The constant

is sharp in any dimension (see

and
[

). This shows also that log-submodularity doesn’t hold in the set of compact convex sets in

, for

.
From (

, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 3.7
.

For every
A,B_{1},B_{2}
convex compact in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
it holds

|A|\,|A+B_{1}+B_{2}|\leq|A+B_{1}|\,|A+B_{2}|.

It is easy to see that 1. from Theorem

works well with direct sums, more precisely if

satisfies (

can be used to create different classes of compact convex sets stable by sum and dilations which satisfy the properties of Theorem

.

Remark 3.8
.

Minkowski’s second inequality gives that, for any compact convex sets
A
and

, one has

\displaystyle|A|V(A[n-2],B[2])\leq V(A[n-1],B)^{2}.

(25)

Theorem

.

Let us, also, note that the same proof shows that for a fixed compact convex set

\displaystyle|A|\,|A+B_{1}+B_{2}|\leq|A+B_{1}|\,|A+B_{2}|,

3.3
Classes closed under linear transformations

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
{\cal L}
be a class of a compact convex sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
stable under any linear transformations. The following are equivalent.

1.

|A|\,|\partial(A+[0,u])|\leq|\partial A|\,|A+[0,u]|
for any
A\in{\cal L}
and any

.

2.

|A|\,|\partial(P_{u^{\bot}}A)|\leq|\partial A|\,|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}
, for any

and any

.

3.

|A|\,|P_{[u,v]^{\bot}}A|_{n-2}\sqrt{1-\langle u,v\rangle^{2}}\leq|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}|P_{v^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}
, for any
A\in{\cal L}
and any

4.

|A+[0,u]+[0,v]|\,|A|\leq|A+[0,u]|\,|A+[0,v]|
for any
A\in{\cal L}
and any

.

5.

|A|V(A[n-2],Z_{1},Z_{2})\leq\frac{n}{n-1}V(A[n-1],Z_{1})V(A[n-1],Z_{2})
, for any
A\in{\cal L}
and any
Z_{1},Z_{2}
zonoids.

6.

For any
A\in{\cal L}
and any

, let us define

, for

. Then

is the restriction to
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
of a polynomial on
{\mathbb{R}}
, which has only real roots.

Proof.

1.\iff 2.
: This was observed in
[

]
. It is true even for fixed

and

and follows from the identities

2.\implies 3.
: Define

. From

applied to

\displaystyle|A|V(A[n-2],[0,u],[0,v])\leq\frac{n}{n-1}V(A[n-1],[0,v])V(A[n-1],[0,u]).

(27)

From (

|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}=nV(A[n-1],[0,u])

3.\iff 4.
: We may assume that

and

Taking limits, we conclude that (

4
Some Special Cases

4.1
An improved inequality in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

Inspired by an analogous result
[
15
, Theorem 3]
in Information Theory, T. Courtade asked if

|B|^{1/n}|C|^{1/n}+|A|^{1/n}|A+B+C|^{1/n}\leq|A+B|^{1/n}|A+C|^{1/n}

(30)

for
A=B_{2}^{n}
being the Euclidean ball, and any convex bodies
B,C
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Here we confirm Courtade’s conjecture in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
in a more general setting.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider convex bodies
A,B,C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, then

|A|^{1/2}|A+B+C|^{1/2}+|B|^{1/2}|C|^{1/2}\leq|A+B|^{1/2}|A+C|^{1/2}.

(31)

Proof.

The main tool to prove the above inequality is the following classical inequality of Fenchel that we have already used. We will need now to use the most general form of this inequality (see
[
44
, (7.69) pp. 401]
):

(|A|V(B,C)-V(A,B)V(A,C))^{2}\leq(V(A,B)^{2}-|A||B|)(V(A,C)^{2}-|A||C|).

Note that the above can be rewritten as

|C|V(A,B)^{2}+|B|V(A,C)^{2}+|A|V^{2}(B,C)-|A||B||C|-2V(A,B)V(A,C)V(B,C)\leq 0

(32)

Squaring both sides of (
31
) we get

2\big(|A||A+B+C||B||C|\big)^{1/2}+|A||A+B+C|+|B||C|\leq|A+B||A+C|

We use (
9
) to rewrite above as

\left(|A||B||C||A+B+C|\right)^{1/2}+|A|V(B,C)\leq 2V(A,B)V(A,C)+V(A,B)|C|+|B|V(A,C).

Using (
24
) we may rewrite the above inequality as

|A||B||C||A+B+C|\leq\left(2V(A,B)V(A,C)+V(A,B)|C|+|B|V(A,C)-|A|V(B,C)\right)^{2}.

Consider
rB
,
r\geq 0
, instead of
B
:

|A||B||C||A+rB+C|\leq\left(2V(A,B)V(A,C)+V(A,B)|C|+r|B|V(A,C)-|A|V(B,C)\right)^{2}.

The above represents a quadratic inequality

\alpha r^{2}+\beta r+\gamma\geq 0,

with

\alpha=|B|^{2}V^{2}(A,C)-|A||B|^{2}|C|,

\beta=2\big(2V(A,B)V(A,C)+V(A,B)|C|-|A|V(B,C)\big)|B|V(A,C)-2|A||B||C|V(B,A+C),

\gamma=\big(2V(A,B)V(A,C)+V(A,B)|C|-|A|V(B,C)\big)^{2}-|A||B||C||A+C|.

It follows from (
11
) that
\alpha\geq 0
. It turns out
\beta
may be negative and thus we need to show that

D=\beta^{2}-4\alpha\gamma\leq 0,

which after division by
|B|^{2}
becomes

\left[\Big(2V(A,B)V(A,C)+V(A,B)|C|-|A|V(B,C)\Big)V(A,C)-|A||C|V(B,A+C)\right]^{2}

-\big(V^{2}(A,C)-|A||C|\big)\Big[\Big(2V(A,B)V(A,C)+V(A,B)|C|-|A|V(B,C)\Big)^{2}-|A||B||C||A+C|\Big]\leq 0.

Simplifying the above inequality and dividing it by
|A||C|
we may rearrange the terms to get that our goal is to show that

\Big(|C|V(A,B)^{2}-2V(A,B)V(A,C)V(B,C)+|A|V^{2}(B,C)\Big)\Big(|A|+2V(A,C)+|C|\Big)

+\big(V^{2}(A,C)-|A||C|\big)|B|(|A+C|)\leq 0.

Factoring out
|A+C|
we get that our goal is to show that

\Big(|C|V(A,B)^{2}-2V(A,B)V(A,C)V(B,C)+|A|V^{2}(B,C)+|B|V^{2}(A,C)-|A||C||B|\Big)|A+C|\leq 0.

Finally, the above inequality follows from (
32
).

\Box

From the failure of log-submodularity on the space of convex bodies for
n\geq 3

(observed independently by Nayar and Tkocz
[
39
]
and a subset of the authors
[
24
]
), we know that inequality (
30
) cannot possibly hold
for
n\geq 3
if
A
is an arbitrary convex body. Of course, Courtade’s conjecture
could still be true since it only considers the case
A=B_{2}^{n}
. We note
that a weaker version of the conjecture, namely,

|B_{2}^{n}||B_{2}^{n}+B+C|\leq|B_{2}^{n}+B||B_{2}^{n}+C|

was proved in
[
24
, Theorem 4.12]
in the special case
when
B
is a zonoid and
C
is an arbitrary convex body.

4.2
Parallelotopes in general dimension

It is clear that 3. from Theorem
3.9
holds for ellipsoids. Indeed, after applying a linear transformation, it reduces to the following inequality:
|B_{2}^{n}||B_{2}^{n-2}|\leq|B_{2}^{n-1}|^{2}
, which follows from the log-convexity of the Gamma function.
In the next theorem, we prove that 3. from Theorem
3.9
holds for the class of parallelotopes.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A
be a parallelotope in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
u,v\in S^{n-1}
, then

|A|\,|P_{[u,v]^{\bot}}A|_{n-2}\sqrt{1-\langle u,v\rangle^{2}}\leq|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}\,|P_{v^{\bot}}A|_{n-1},

with equality if and only, when
A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},a_{i}+w_{i}],
for some
a_{i},w_{i}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we have that

u=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}w_{i}
and
v=\sum_{i\in I^{c}}\lambda_{i}w_{i}
for some
I\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proof.

We use the representation of the volume of projections using mixed volumes (
14
) to restate the above statement as

|A|V(A[n-2],[0,u],[0,v])\leq\frac{n}{n-1}V(A[n-1],[0,u])V(A[n-1],[0,v]).

Applying a linear transformation, we may assume
A=[0,1]^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,e_{i}]
. Thus

V\left(A[n-1],[0,u]\right)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{|I|=n-1}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(u,(e_{i})_{i\in I})|=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|u_{i}|,

V\left(A[n-2],[0,u],[0,v]\right)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{|I|=n-2}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(u,v,(e_{i})_{i\in I})|=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j}|u_{i}v_{j}-u_{j}v_{i}|.

Finally we need to show

\sum_{i<j}|u_{i}v_{j}-u_{j}v_{i}|\leq\sum|u_{i}|\sum|v_{j}|,

(33)

which follows from the triangle inequality. The equality in (
33
) is only possible if and only if
u_{i}v_{i}=0
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\},
which implies the desired equality case.

\Box

We note that every zonotope
A
can be seen as an orthogonal projection of a high dimensional cube. Unfortunately, Theorem
4.2
can not be generalized directly to the case of projection of higher co-dimensions (as it is done in 3. from Theorem
5.2
below), indeed such direct generalization requires Theorem
4.2
for all zonotopes in place of the cube. Thus we prove this property directly for the case of parallelotopes in the next theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A
be a parallelotope in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then

|A||P_{E\cap F}A|\leq|P_{E}A||P_{F}A|.

(34)

for any subspaces
E,F
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
E^{\perp}\subset F
.

Proof.

Notice that (
34
) is invariant under application of rotation
S\in O(n)
to the parallelotope
A
and subspaces
E
and
G.
Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume
A=T\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,e_{i}]\right),
for some
T\in GL(n)
and
E=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}^{\perp}
and
F=\{e_{m+1},\dots e_{m+j}\}^{\perp}
. Then

|P_{E}\left(T\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,e_{i}]\right)|_{n-m}=|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,P_{E}(Te_{i})]|_{n-m}=|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,P_{E}Te_{i}]+\sum_{k=1}^{m}[0,e_{k}]|_{n},

where the last equality follows from
\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,P_{E}(Te_{i})]|_{n-m}\subset E=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}^{\perp}
. Thus,

\displaystyle|P_{E}\left(T\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,e_{i}]\right)|_{n-m}=

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=n-m}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(e_{1},\dots,e_{m},\{P_{E}Te_{i}\}_{i\in I}|_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=n-m}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(e_{1},\dots,e_{m},\{Te_{i}\}_{i\in I}|_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\sum_{|I|=n-m}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T^{-1}e_{1},\dots,T^{-1}e_{m},\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}|_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\sum_{|J|=m}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{J}\}_{i=1}^{m})|,

(35)

where we denote by
w_{i}=T^{-1}e_{i},

i=1,\dots,n
and by
u^{J}
we denote the orthogonal projection of vector
u
onto the
span\{e_{i}\}_{i\in J}
. We apply (
4.2
) to get that

\displaystyle|P_{E\cap F}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,Te_{i}]|_{n-j-m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\sum_{|N|=m+j}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{N}\}_{i=1}^{m+j})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\!\!\!\sum_{|N|=m+j}\left|\sum_{|I|=m,I\subset N}\!\!\!\!\!\!\varepsilon(N,I){\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{I}\}_{i\leq m}){\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{N\cap I^{c}}\}_{i>m})\right|\!,

where in the last step we have used the Laplace formula. Finally,

\displaystyle|P_{E\cap F}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,Te_{i}]|_{n-j-m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\sum_{N=m+j}\sum_{|I|=m,I\subset N}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{I}\}_{i\leq m})||{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{N\cap I^{c}}\}_{i>m})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\sum_{|I|=m}\sum_{N=m+j,I\subset J}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{I}\}_{i\leq m})||{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{N\cap I^{c}}\}_{i>m})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\sum_{|I|=m}\sum_{I\cup J,|J|=j,I\cap J=\emptyset}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{I}\}_{i\leq m})||{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{J}\}_{i>m})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|\sum_{|I|=m}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{I}\}_{i\leq m})|\sum_{|J|=j,}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{w_{i}^{J}\}_{i>m})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\mathop{\rm det}}(T)|^{-1}|P_{E}\left(T\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,e_{i}]\right)|\,|P_{F}\left(T\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,e_{i}]\right)|,

where the last equality, again, follows from (
4.2
).

\Box

5
Inequalities for zonoids

5.1
Zonoids in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

Zonoids form a natural class of bodies which is stable under addition and linear transformations.
In this section, we confirm property (4) from Theorem
3.9
in the class of three dimensional zonoids. Thus, using Theorem
5.1
, we get that all properties described in Theorems
3.6
and
3.9
are true for this class.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
A
be a zonoid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
u,v\in S^{2}
. Then

|A|_{3}|P_{[u,v]^{\bot}}A|_{1}\sqrt{1-\langle u,v\rangle^{2}}\leq|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{2}|P_{v^{\bot}}A|_{2}.

(36)

Proof.

Using (
15
), inequality (
36
) is equivalent to

|A|_{3}|V(A,[0,u],[0,v])\leq\frac{3}{2}V(A[2],[0,u])V(A[2],[0,v]).

(37)

We assume that
u,v
are linearly independent (otherwise the inequality is trivial) and note that it is enough to prove (
37
) in the case of
u=e_{1}
,
v=e_{2}
and any zonoid
A
. Indeed, the more general case then follows by applying the inequality to
T^{-1}A,
where
T\in GL(3)
is such that
Te_{1}=u
and
Te_{2}=v
. Thus our goal is to prove that, for any zonoid
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,

|A|_{3}|P_{[e_{1},e_{2}]^{\bot}}A|_{1}\leq|P_{e_{1}^{\bot}}A|_{2}|P_{e_{2}^{\bot}}A|_{2}.

(38)

By approximation, it is enough to prove
(
38
) when
A
is a zonotope. Suppose that
A=\sum_{i=1}^{M}[0,u_{i}],
where
u_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Using (
19
), we get that (
38
) is equivalent to

{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Let
P(t)=|Z+t([0,u]+[0,v])|,
then
P^{1/2}(t)
is a concave function for
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

We may assume that the vectors
u,v
are linearly independent. Then,
applying a linear transformation
T
to
Z
and
u,v
we may assume that vectors
u,v
are orthogonal to each others and belong to
S^{2}.
Using that

Z+t([0,u]+[0,v])
is again a zonoid, it is enough for us to show that
(P^{1/2}(0))^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
, or
2P(0)P^{\prime\prime}(0)\leq P^{\prime}(0)^{2}
. Using that

P(0)=|Z|,\quad P^{\prime}(0)=|P_{u^{\perp}}Z|+|P_{v^{\perp}}Z|\quad\hbox{and}\quad P^{\prime\prime}(0)=2|P_{[u,v]^{\bot}}Z|.

and Theorem
5.1
, we get

2P(0)P^{\prime\prime}(0)=4|Z||P_{[u,v]^{\bot}}Z|\leq 4|P_{u^{\bot}}Z||P_{v^{\bot}}Z|\leq(|P_{u^{\perp}}K|+|P_{v^{\perp}}K|)^{2}=P^{\prime}(0)^{2}.

\Box

It follows from part 6. of Theorem
3.9
and Theorem
5.1
that if
P(t)=|Z+t([0,u]+[0,v])|
, then
P(t)
, as a polynomial on
{\mathbb{R}}
, has only real roots, for any zonoid
Z
and any
u,v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Thus another way to prove Proposition
5.4
is to notice the following simple property. Consider a quadratic polynomial
P
with positive coefficients, then
\sqrt{P(t)}
is concave for
t\geq 0
if and only if
P
has only real roots. Thus, to study concavity of
\sqrt{P_{C}(t)}=|Z+tC|^{1/2},
when
C
is two dimensional, one may study the roots of
P
. The subject of roots of Steiner-type polynomials has attracted a fair bit of attention in the literature, see, e.g.,
[
29
]
and references therein. Proposition
5.4
makes the following conjecture plausible:

Question 5.5
.

Let
Z
be a zonoid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then is it true that the polynomial
P_{C}(t)=|Z+tC|
has only real roots for every convex body
C
of dimension
2
?

This question is directly connected to a question of Adam Marcus that we learned from Guillaume Aubrun about the roots of Steiner polynomials of zonoids,
and, in fact, it was one of the starting point of this investigation.

Question 5.6
.

Let
Z
be a zonoid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then is it true that the Steiner polynomial
P_{Z}(t)=|Z+tB_{2}^{n}|
has only real roots?

Observe that, in the plane, even more is true: for any convex bodies
K,L
, the polynomial
P_{K,L}(t)=|K+tL|
has only real roots. Indeed,
\sqrt{P_{K,L}(t)}
is concave for
t\geq 0
by Brunn-Minkowski inequality. This can be also seen from the computing the discriminant and noticing that
V(K,L)^{2}-|K||L|\geq 0
.

Analogously, Question
5.5
is equivalent to the following question for mixed volumes: fix
n\geq 3
, and let

K
a zonoid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
L
be a two dimensional zonoid, is it true that

|K|V(K[n-2],L[2])\leq\frac{n}{2(n-1)}V(K[n-1],L)^{2}?

The above inequality is true for
L
being a parallelogram, as follows from Theorem
3.6
. But, it is not true for general zonoids, as we show in the following proposition inspired by a work by Victor Katsnelson
[
30
]
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Then there exists a zonoid
Z
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that the Steiner polynomial
P_{Z}(t)=|Z+tB_{2}^{n}|
and the polynomial
Q_{Z}(t)=|tZ+B_{2}^{n}|
has roots which are not real.

Proof.

Noticing that
Q_{Z}(t)=t^{n}P_{Z}(1/t),
it is enough to show that
P_{Z}(t)
has a non-real root.
Consider
Z=B_{2}^{2}\times\{0\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-2}
. Integrating on sections parallel to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\{0\}
and changing variable, we get that, for
t\geq 0
,

P_{Z}(t)=|Z+tB_{2}^{n}|=|B_{2}^{2}|\int_{tB_{2}^{n-2}}\left(1+\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}dx=t^{n-2}|B_{2}^{2}|\int_{B_{2}^{n-2}}\left(1+t\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}dx.

Since this last expression is the restriction of a polynomial to
t\geq 0
, the equality between
P_{Z}(t)
and the last term is valid on whole
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
be fixed. For almost all
x\in B_{2}^{n-2}
,
(1+t\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}})^{2}>0
, thus
P_{Z}(t)>0
, for
t\neq 0
, so the only real root of
P_{Z}
is
0

and it is of order
n-2
. Hence
P_{Z}
has exactly
2
non-real roots.

\Box

Proposition
5.7
shows that the answers to both Question
5.5

and Question
5.6
are negative.

Note that the example of the zonoid
Z
created in Proposition
5.7
is flat (i.e.,
has an affine hull of lower dimension than the ambient space), but one can
replace it by a non-flat zonoid by using a small perturbation and the continuity of the roots of polynomials.

6
Inequalities for
L_{p}
-zonoids

Firey
[
20
]
extended the concept of Minkowski sum and introduced, for each real
p\geq 1
, a
p
-linear combination
of convex bodies, the so-called
\ell_{p}
-sum

K\oplus_{p}L
of the convex bodies
K
and
L
containing the origin by:

h_{K\oplus_{p}L}(x)=\left(h_{K}(x)^{p}+h_{L}(x)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

For any linear transform
T
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one has
T(K\oplus_{p}L)=(TK)\oplus_{p}(TL)
. In a series of papers, Lutwak
[
31
,
32
]
showed that the Firey sums lead to a
Brunn-Minkowski theory for each
p\geq 1
, including
L_{p}
-Brunn-Minkowski inequality, definition and inequalities for
L_{p}
-mixed volumes,
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem, as well as many other applications. In this section, we show the connection of the discussion from previous sections to this theory.

An
L_{p}
-zonotope is the
\ell_{p}
-sum of centered segments and an
L_{p}
-zonoid is the Hausdorff limit of
L_{p}
-zonotopes. For
p=2
, an
L_{2}
-zonoid is always an ellipsoid, possibly living in a lower-dimensional subspace, thus it can be written as the sum of
m\leq n
orthogonal segments and is therefore an
L_{2}
-zonotope.
The following extension of Conjecture
5
is thus natural.

Question 6.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
and consider
L_{p}
-zonoids
A,B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is it true that

\left(\frac{|A\oplus_{p}B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}(A\oplus_{p}B)|_{n-1}}\right)^{p}\geq\left(\frac{|A|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{|B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}B|_{n-1}}\right)^{p}?

(46)

6.1
The case
p=2

The next theorem gives an affirmative answer to this question in the case
p=2
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
A,B
be a pair of
L_{2}
-zonoids in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
u
in
S^{n-1}
. Then

\left(\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}(A\oplus_{2}B)|_{n-1}}\right)^{2}\geq\left(\frac{|A|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{|B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}B|_{n-1}}\right)^{2},

with equality if and only if
A
and
B
have parallel tangent hyperplanes at
\rho_{A}(u)u
and
\rho_{B}(u)u
.

We first give two proofs of the inequality and then prove the equality case.

Proof 1:
For
m\geq n
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let
U
be the
n\times m
matrix whose columns are
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
. One has
[-u_{1},u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{m},u_{m}]=UB_{2}^{m}=\sqrt{UU^{*}}B_{2}^{n}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle h^{2}_{[-u_{1},u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{m},u_{m}]}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle u_{i},x\rangle^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle Ue_{i},x\rangle^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle e_{i},U^{*}x\rangle^{2}=|U^{*}x|^{2}=h^{2}_{UB_{2}^{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle U^{*}x,U^{*}x\rangle=\langle\sqrt{UU^{*}}x,\sqrt{UU^{*}}x\rangle=|\sqrt{UU^{*}}x|^{2}=h^{2}_{\sqrt{UU^{*}}B_{2}^{n}}(x).

Using this and the Cauchy-Binet formula, one has

|[-u_{1},u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{m},u_{m}]|^{2}=|UB_{2}^{m}|^{2}_{n}={\mathop{\rm det}}(UU^{*})|B_{2}^{n}|^{2}=|B_{2}^{n}|^{2}\sum_{|I|=n}({\mathop{\rm det}}(u_{i})_{i\in I})^{2}.

(47)

Next, using

P_{u^{\bot}}([-u_{1},u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{m},u_{m}])=[-P_{u^{\bot}}u_{1},P_{u^{\bot}}u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-P_{u^{\bot}}u_{m},P_{u^{\bot}}u_{m}],

we get that

|P_{u^{\bot}}([-u_{1},u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{m},u_{m}])|^{2}=|B_{2}^{n-1}|^{2}\sum_{|I|=n-1}({\mathop{\rm det}}(P_{u^{\bot}}u_{i})_{i\in I})^{2}.

Thus our goal is to prove that, for
N\geq N^{\prime}
and
A=[-u_{1},u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{N^{\prime}},u_{N^{\prime}}]
and
B=[-u_{N^{\prime}+1},u_{N^{\prime}+1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{N},u_{N}]
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N^{\prime}\},|M|=n}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{u_{m}\}_{m\in M}|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N^{\prime}\},|M|=n-1}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{P_{e_{1}^{\perp}}u_{m}\}_{m\in M})|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sum\limits_{L\subset\{N^{\prime}+1,\dots,N\},|L|=n}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{u_{l}\}_{l\in L}|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{L\subset\{N^{\prime}+1,\dots,N\},|L|=n-1}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{P_{e_{1}^{\perp}}u_{l}\}_{l\in L}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N\},|M|=n}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{u_{m}\}_{m\in M}|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N\},|M|=n-1}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{P_{e_{1}^{\perp}}(u_{m})\}_{m\in M}|^{2}}.

(48)

For
I\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
let
U_{I}
be the
n\times n
submatrix built from
U
by taking the columns with indices in
I
.
Denote by
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
the rows of the matrix
U
. Since
n\leq m
, the set
\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,z_{i}]
is a parallelotope leaving in a
n
-dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Thus,

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,z_{i}]\right|_{n}^{2}=\sum_{|I|=n}({\mathop{\rm det}}\ U_{I})^{2}.

Therefore, we get that, for
m\geq n
, an
n\times m
matrix
U
whose rows are
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and columns
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
:

|[-u_{1},u_{1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[-u_{m},u_{m}]|_{n}=|B_{2}^{n}|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,z_{i}]\right|_{n}.

Let us write
U
as a block matrix with two blocks
U=(U_{1}|0)+(0|U_{2})
, with
U_{1}
being an
n\times k
matrix and
U_{2}
an
n\times(m-k)
matrix and we denote
V=(U_{1}|0)
and
W=(0|U_{2})
. Moreover, denote by
U^{\prime},V^{\prime},W^{\prime}
the matrices obtained from
U,V,W
by erasing the
n^{th}
row.
Then we only need to prove that

\frac{\sum_{|I|=n}({\mathop{\rm det}}\ U_{I})^{2}}{\sum_{|I|=n-1}({\mathop{\rm det}}\ U^{\prime}_{I})^{2}}\geq\frac{\sum_{|I|=n}({\mathop{\rm det}}\ V_{I})^{2}}{\sum_{|I|=n-1}({\mathop{\rm det}}\ V^{\prime}_{I})^{2}}+\frac{\sum_{|I|=n}({\mathop{\rm det}}\ W_{I})^{2}}{\sum_{|I|=n-1}({\mathop{\rm det}}\ W^{\prime}_{I})^{2}}.

Recall that
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
are the rows of
U
. Thus the rows of
V
are
P_{E}z_{1},\dots,P_{E}z_{n}
, where
P_{E}
denotes the projection on the first
k
coordinates and the rows of
W
are
P_{E^{\bot}}z_{1},\dots,P_{E^{\bot}}z_{n}
, where
P_{E^{\bot}}
denotes the projection on the
n-k
last coordinates. Thus we only need to show the following relationship for low-dimensional parallelotopes

\left(\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,z_{i}]\right|}{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[0,z_{i}]\right|}\right)^{2}\geq\left(\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,P_{E}z_{i}]\right|}{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[0,P_{E}z_{i}]\right|}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,P_{E^{\bot}}z_{i}]\right|}{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[0,P_{E^{\bot}}z_{i}]\right|}\right)^{2}.

Using that the volume of a parallelotope is the product of the volume of one of its face and its height, we get that, if
H_{n}={\mathrm{span}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-1})
, then

\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,z_{i}]\right|}{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[0,z_{i}]\right|}=d(z_{n},H_{n}).

So we are reduced to prove that

d(z_{n},H_{n})^{2}\geq d(P_{E}z_{n},P_{E}H_{n})^{2}+d(P_{E^{\bot}}z_{n},P_{E^{\bot}}H_{n})^{2}.

Let
h_{n}\in H_{n}
such that
d(z_{n},H_{n})=|z_{n}-h_{n}|
. By Pythagoras’ theorem,

d(z_{n},H_{n})^{2}=|z_{n}-h_{n}|^{2}=\|P_{E}z_{n}-P_{E}h_{n}\|^{2}+\|P_{E^{\bot}}z_{n}-P_{E^{\bot}}h_{n}\|^{2}.

Since
P_{E}h_{n}\in P_{E}H_{n}
and
P_{E^{\bot}}z_{n}\in P_{E^{\bot}}H_{n}
, we conclude.
\Box

Second proof of the inequality:
We give another proof of Theorem
6.2
, using the comparison of the
\ell_{2}
sum and the radial
2
-sum.
For any symmetric convex bodies
K
and
L
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one has

K\oplus_{2}L\supset K\widetilde{+}_{2}L:=\cup_{u\in S^{n-1}}\left[0,\sqrt{\rho_{K}^{2}(u)+\rho_{L}^{2}(u)}u\right].

(49)

Indeed, using support functions, the fact that
K\supset\rho_{K}(u)[-u,u]
implies that
h_{K}(x)\geq\rho_{K}(u)|\langle x,u\rangle|
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and thus

h_{K\oplus_{2}L}(x)=\sqrt{h_{K}(x)^{2}+h_{L}(x)^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\rho_{K}(u)^{2}+\rho_{L}(u)^{2}}|\langle x,u\rangle|=\sqrt{\rho_{K}(u)^{2}+\rho_{L}(u)^{2}}h_{[-u,u]}(x).

The formula (
49
) can be also restated in the language of radial functions:

\rho_{K\oplus_{2}L}(u)\geq\sqrt{\rho_{K}^{2}(u)+\rho_{L}^{2}(u)},\mbox{ for all }u\in S^{n-1}.

(50)

Next, we notice nice a formula for the volume of the orthogonal hyperplane projection of an ellipsoid (see, for example,
[
13
,
41
]
), to which we give a very simple proof. Let
\mathcal{E}=TB_{2}^{n},
for some positive definite
T
then

\frac{|P_{u^{\bot}}\mathcal{E}|}{|\mathcal{E}|\,\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}}}=\frac{nV(TB_{2}^{n}[n-1],[0,u])}{|TB_{2}^{n}|\,|T^{-1}u|}=\frac{nV(B_{2}^{n}[n-1],[0,T^{-1}u])}{|B_{2}^{n}|\,|T^{-1}u|}=\frac{|B_{2}^{n-1}|}{|B_{2}^{n}|}.

(51)

Using the above we get

\frac{|\mathcal{E}|}{|P_{u}\mathcal{E}|}=\frac{|B_{2}^{n}|}{|B_{2}^{n-1}|}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}(u).

(52)

Thus, using this formula and (
49
), we deduce that for any ellipsoids
A,B

\displaystyle\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|^{2}}{|P_{u}(A\oplus_{2}B)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|B_{2}^{n}|^{2}}{|B_{2}^{n-1}|^{2}}\rho_{A\oplus_{2}B}(u)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|B_{2}^{n}|^{2}}{|B_{2}^{n-1}|^{2}}(\rho_{A}(u)^{2}+\rho_{B}(u)^{2})=\frac{|A|^{2}}{|P_{u}A|^{2}}+\frac{|B|^{2}}{|P_{u}B|^{2}}.

(53)

\Box

Proof of the equality case:

This second proof also helps us to treat the equality case.
From (
52
) there is equality if and only if

\rho_{A\oplus_{2}B}(u)^{2}=\rho_{A}(u)^{2}+\rho_{B}(u)^{2}.

The above is equivalent to the
(\rho_{A}(u)^{2}+\rho_{B}(u)^{2})^{1/2}u\in\partial(A\oplus_{2}B)
. From here we get that, if
n
is a normal vector to
\partial(A\oplus_{2}B)
at
(\rho_{A}(u)^{2}+\rho_{B}(u)^{2})^{1/2}u
then

h_{A\oplus_{2}B}(n)=(\rho_{A}(u)^{2}+\rho_{B}(u)^{2})^{1/2}\langle u,n\rangle.

The above is equivalent to

h_{A}(n)=\rho_{A}(u)\langle u,n\rangle\mbox{ and }h_{B}(n)=\rho_{B}(u)\langle u,n\rangle,

or simply to say that the normal vector to
\partial A
at
\rho_{A}(u)u
is the normal vector to
\partial B
at
\rho_{B}(u)u
.

\Box

Remark 6.3
.

If in the proof of equality case in the Theorem
6.2
we would represent
A=T_{1}B_{2}^{n}
and
B=T_{2}B_{2}^{n},
where
T_{1},T_{2}
are two positive symmetric matrices. Then
\|x\|^{2}_{A}=\langle T_{1}^{-2}x,x\rangle.
and the normal vector to
\partial A
at
\rho_{A}(u)u
is parallel to

T_{1}^{-2}u
, and the similar statement is true for
B
. Thus our condition on parallel tangent hyperplanes is equivalent to the fact that there is
\lambda>0
such that

(T_{1}^{-2}-\lambda T_{2}^{-2})u=0

or simply that
u
is an eigenvector for matrix
T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{-2}
.

Remark 6.4
.

Observe that in Theorem
6.2
there is an equality at least for
n
directions
u
(not counting
-u
) and there is equality for every
u
if and only if two ellipsoids are homothetic.

Let us now present some consequences or Theorem
6.2
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Let
A
and
B
be
L_{2}
-zonoids in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
u
in
S^{n-1}
. Then, the function
h
defined, for
t\geq 0
, by

h_{2}(t)=\frac{|A\oplus_{2}(\sqrt{t}B)|^{2}}{|P_{u^{\bot}}(A\oplus_{2}(\sqrt{t}B))|^{2}_{n-1}}

is concave on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Proof.

For any
\lambda\in[0,1]
, one has
A=(\sqrt{1-\lambda}A)\oplus_{2}(\sqrt{\lambda}A)
.
Thus one deduces that

A\oplus_{2}\left(\sqrt{(1-\lambda)s+\lambda t}B\right)=\left(\sqrt{1-\lambda}(A\oplus_{2}\sqrt{s}B)\right)\oplus_{2}\left(\sqrt{\lambda}(A\oplus_{2}\sqrt{t}B)\right).

Using Theorem
6.2
and the homogeneity of volume, we deduce that
h_{2}
is concave.

\Box

Next we show that Theorem
6.2
has the following additional applications:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
n
be an integer, then for any
1\leq k\leq n
and for every pair of
L_{2}
-zonoids
A
and
B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and any (
n-k
)-dimensional subspace
E
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
one has

\left(\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|}{|P_{E}(A\oplus_{2}B)|_{n-k}}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}\geq\left(\frac{|A|}{|P_{E}A|_{n-k}}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}+\left(\frac{|B|}{|P_{E}B|_{n-k}}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}.

(54)

Proof.

The proof goes by induction on
k
. Theorem
6.2
establishes the case
k=1
and any
n\geq 1
. We assume that the inequality holds for some
1\leq k\leq n-1
for all
L_{2}
-zonoids
A,B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and all
n-k
dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
E
be a
n-k-1
dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then one may write
E=F\cap u^{\bot}
, for some
n-k
dimensional subspace
F
and
u\in F^{\bot}
. Then, applying Theorem
6.2
to
P_{F}A
and
P_{F}B
and using that
P_{u^{\bot}}\circ P_{F}=P_{E}
we get

\left(\frac{|P_{F}(A\oplus_{2}B)|_{n-k}}{|P_{E}(A\oplus_{2}B)|_{n-k-1}}\right)^{2}\geq\left(\frac{|P_{F}A|_{n-k}}{|P_{E}A|_{n-k-1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{|P_{F}B|_{n-k}}{|P_{E}B|_{n-k-1}}\right)^{2}.

(55)

Applying (
54
) to
E=F
, raising the equation to power
k/2
and taking the product with (
55
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|}{|P_{E}(A\oplus_{2}B)|_{n-k-1}}\geq

(56)

\displaystyle\left(\left(\frac{|P_{F}A|_{n-k}}{|P_{E}A|_{n-k-1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{|P_{F}B|_{n-k}}{|P_{E}B|_{n-k-1}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left(\frac{|A|}{|P_{F}A|_{n-k}}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}+\left(\frac{|B|}{|P_{F}B|_{n-k}}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}.

From Hölder’s inequality, we conclude that

\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|}{|P_{E}(A\oplus_{2}B)|_{n-k-1}}\geq\left(\left(\frac{|A|}{|P_{E}A|_{n-k-1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{k+1}}+\left(\frac{|B|}{|P_{E}B|_{n-k-1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{k+1}{2}},

which is (
54
) for
k+1
.

\Box

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
k,n
be a integer with
1\leq k\leq n.
Then for every
L_{2}
-zonoids
A
and
B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and every zonoids

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|}{V((A\oplus_{2}B)[n-k],Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}\geq

(57)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|A|}{V(A[n-k],Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}+\left(\frac{|B|}{V(B[n-1],Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}},

and thus the function
f
defined, for
t\geq 0
, by

f(t)=\frac{|A\oplus_{2}\sqrt{t}B|^{\frac{2}{k}}}{|V((A\oplus_{2}\sqrt{t}B)[n-k],Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})|^{\frac{2}{k}}_{n-k}}

is concave on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Moreover

\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|^{2}}{|\partial(A\oplus_{2}B)|^{2}}\geq\frac{|A|^{2}}{|\partial A|^{2}}+\frac{|B|^{2}}{|\partial B|^{2}},

(58)

and thus the function
g
defined, for
t\geq 0
, by

g(t)=\frac{|A\oplus_{2}\sqrt{t}B|^{2}}{|\partial(A\oplus_{2}\sqrt{t}B)|^{2}_{n-1}}

is concave on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Proof.

First notice that Theorem
6.6
may be reformulated in the following way. Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
be an orthonormal system in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then using (
15
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|A\oplus_{2}B|}{V((A\oplus_{2}B)[n-k],[0,u_{1}],\dots,[0,u_{k}])}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}\geq

(59)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|A|}{V(A[n-k],[0,u_{1}],\dots,[0,u_{k}])}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{|B|}{V(B[n-k],[0,u_{1}],\dots,[0,u_{k}])}\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}.

Applying a linear transform, (
59
) holds for any linearly independent system
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
. Then, for
x,y\geq 0
, define

\varphi(x,y)=(x^{-\frac{2}{k}}+y^{-\frac{2}{k}})^{-\frac{k}{2}}=\|(x,y)\|_{-\frac{2}{k}}.

(60)

For a compact convex set
A
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let

\psi_{A}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=\frac{V(A[n-k],[0,u_{1}],\dots,,[0,u_{k}])}{|A|}.

(61)

From (
59
) we know that, if
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
are linerly independent, then

\psi_{A\oplus_{2}B}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\leq\varphi(\psi_{A}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}),\psi_{B}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})).

For
i=1,\dots,k
, let
Z_{i}=[0,u_{i,1}]\oplus_{2}\cdots\oplus_{2}[0,u_{i,m_{i}}]
be a
2
-zonotope. Assume that for any set of distinct
k
vectors from the set
\{u_{i,j}\}
is an independent sequence (this can be achieved by a small perturbation of vectors
u_{i,j}
). Using that
\varphi
, being a
-\frac{2}{k}
-norm, satisfies the reverse Minkowski inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{m_{i}}\psi_{A\oplus_{2}B}(u_{1,j_{1}},\dots,u_{k,j_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{m_{i}}\varphi(\psi_{A}(u_{1,j_{1}},\dots,u_{k,j_{k}}),\psi_{B}(u_{1,j_{1}},\dots,u_{k,j_{k}}))

\displaystyle\leq\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{m_{i}}\psi_{A}(u_{1,j_{1}},\dots,u_{k,j_{k}}),\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{m_{i}}\psi_{B}(u_{1,j_{1}},\dots,u_{k,j_{k}})\right).

Thus we get

\frac{V((A\oplus_{2}B)[n-k],Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})}{|A\oplus_{2}B|}\leq\varphi\left(\frac{V(A[n-k],Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})}{|A|},\frac{V(B[n-k],Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k})}{|B|}\right),

which is (
57
), for
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
being a zonotopes. The result for zonoids follows by approximation.
We apply (
57
) with
k=1
and
Z_{1}=B_{2}^{n}
to prove (
58
).
The concavity of
f
and
g
is proved with the method used for Corollary
6.5
.

6.2
The case
p\neq 2

Proposition 6.8
.

The answer to Question
6.1
is negative when
p>2
and
n\geq 2
: a counterexample is given by
A=B_{2}^{n}
and
B=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}[-v,v]
, for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Let
p>2
. We disprove the weaker statement

\frac{|A\oplus_{p}B|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}(A\oplus_{p}B)|_{n-1}}\geq\frac{|A|}{|P_{u^{\bot}}A|_{n-1}}.

(62)

We restate (
62
) with
B=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}[-v,v],

v\in S^{n-1}
and
\varepsilon\to 0.

For this we use the extension of the classical notion of the mixed volumes introduced by
Lutwak
[
31
,
32
]
, who proved that

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{|A\oplus_{p}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}\,B)|-|A|}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}h^{p}_{B}(u)h_{A}^{1-p}dS_{A}(u),

(63)

for
p>1
and all convex compact sets
A,B,
containing the origin and defined

V_{p}(A[n-1],B)=\frac{1}{n}\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}h^{p}_{B}(u)h_{A}^{1-p}dS_{A}(u).

(64)

Taking
B=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}[-v,v]
for some
v\in S^{n-1},
we get

\Big|A\oplus_{p}B\Big|=|A|+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}|\langle v,x\rangle|^{p}h_{A}^{1-p}dS_{A}(x)+o(\varepsilon)

Assuming
v
in
u^{\perp},
we get

\Big|P_{u^{\perp}}A\oplus_{p}P_{u^{\perp}}B\Big|=|P_{u^{\perp}}A|+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\int\limits_{S^{n-1}\cap u^{\perp}}|\langle v,x\rangle|^{p}h_{A}^{1-p}(x)dS_{P_{u^{\perp}}A}(x)+o(\varepsilon).

Assume, by way of contradiction, that (
62
) is true for all
L_{p}
-zonoids
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.
Then,

|A|\int\limits_{S^{n-1}\cap u^{\perp}}|\langle v,x\rangle|^{p}h_{A}^{1-p}(x)dS_{P_{u^{\perp}}A}(x)\leq|P_{u^{\perp}}A|\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}|\langle v,x\rangle|^{p}h_{A}^{1-p}(x)dS_{A}(x).

(65)

Now we take
A=B_{2}^{n}
,
u=e_{2}
and
v=e_{1}
. Notice that
B_{2}^{n}
is an
L_{p}
zonoid for all
p\geq 1
. Next (
65
) becomes

|B_{2}^{n}|\int_{S^{n-2}}|x_{1}|^{p}dS_{B_{2}^{n-1}}(x)\leq|B_{2}^{n-1}|\int_{S^{n-1}}|x_{1}|^{p}dS_{B_{2}^{n}}(x).

(66)

Using polar coordinates and Fubini’s theorem, we get

\int_{S^{n-1}}|x_{1}|^{p}dS_{B_{2}^{n}}(x)=(n+p)\int_{B_{2}^{n-1}}|z_{1}|^{p}dz=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+n}{2}\right)}.

Thus (
66
) becomes

\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p+n}{2}\right)\leq\Gamma\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p+n-1}{2}\right).

Using the strict
\log
-convexity of
\Gamma
function the above is only true if and only if
p\leq 2
.

\Box

Proposition 6.9
.

Fix
p>1
. Then (
46
) does not hold in the class of all convex symmetric bodies in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let us first construct an example in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We note that

\Big|P_{u^{\perp}}A\oplus_{p}P_{u^{\perp}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}[-v,v]\Big|=2h_{A}(v)+\frac{2\varepsilon}{p}h_{A}(v)^{1-p}+o(\varepsilon).

Thus (
62
), for
n=2
, would imply

|A|\leq h_{A}(v)^{p}\int\limits_{S^{1}}|\langle v,x\rangle|^{p}h_{A}^{1-p}(x)dS_{A}(x).

(67)

Consider
a\in(0,1),
let

A=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},|x_{i}|\leq 1,|x_{1}\pm x_{2}|\leq 2-a\}.

Then
|A|=4-2a^{2}
. We check (
67
) with
v=e_{1}
. We first note that
h_{A}(e_{1})=1
. Next we compute

f(x)=|\langle e_{1},x\rangle|^{p}h_{A}^{1-p}(x)S_{A}(x)

for different normal vectors
x
of
A
. We first note that
f(\pm e_{2})=0
,
f(\pm e_{1})=2(1-a)
and

f((\pm 1/\sqrt{2},\pm 1/\sqrt{2}))=a(2-a)^{1-p}.

Thus to contradict (
67
) we must select
a
such that

4-2a^{2}>4(1-a)+4a(2-a)^{1-p},

or
a<2-2^{1/p},
which is possible for every
p>1
.

To build a counterexample in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
, we use the fact that if
K
is a convex body in
{\rm span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n_{1}}\}
and
L
is a convex body in
{\rm span}\{e_{n_{1}+1},\dots,e_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\}
, then

|K\oplus_{p}L|=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n_{1}}{q}+1)\Gamma(\frac{n_{2}}{q}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{n_{1}+n_{1}}{q}+1)}|K||L|=c_{n_{1},n_{2},q}|K||L|,

(68)

where
1/p+1/q=1
. Let
A_{n}=B_{q}^{n-2}\oplus_{p}A_{2}
, where
A_{2}\subset{\rm span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}

is the counterexample created above, and
B^{n-2}_{q}\subset{\rm span}\{e_{3},\dots,e_{n}\}
.
Then (
68
) gives

\frac{|A_{n}\oplus_{p}[-v,v]|}{|P_{e_{1}^{\bot}}(A_{n}\oplus_{p}[-v,v])|_{n-1}}=\frac{|B_{q}^{n-2}\oplus_{p}(A_{2}\oplus_{p}[-v,v])|}{|B_{q}^{n-2}\oplus_{p}P_{e_{1}^{\bot}}(A_{2}\oplus_{p}[-v,v])|_{n-1}}<\frac{|A_{n}|}{|P_{e_{1}^{\bot}}A_{n}|_{n-1}}.

Let us note that the direct interpretation of the volume of
L_{p}
-zonotopes in terms of determinants is only possible in the case when
p=1
or
p=2

[
28
]
, thus it is natural to ask if the determinant inequality that we proved in the case
p=2

Question 6.10
.

Let
p\geq 1
, consider
N\geq N^{\prime}\geq n
and a sequence for vectors
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is it true that

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N^{\prime}\},|M|=n}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{u_{m}\}_{m\in M}|^{p}}{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N^{\prime}\},|M|=n-1}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{P_{e_{1}^{\perp}}u_{m}\}_{m\in M})|^{p}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sum\limits_{L\subset\{N^{\prime}+1,\dots,N\},|L|=n}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{u_{l}\}_{l\in L}|^{p}}{\sum\limits_{L\subset\{N^{\prime}+1,\dots,N\},|L|=n-1}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{P_{e_{1}^{\perp}}u_{l}\}_{l\in L}|^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N\},|M|=n}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{u_{m}\}_{m\in M}|^{p}}{\sum\limits_{M\subset\{1,\dots,N\},|M|=n-1}|{\mathop{\rm det}}(\{P_{e_{1}^{\perp}}(u_{m})\}_{m\in M}|^{p}}?

(69)

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. We obtain the boundedness of the discrete fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces. From this and a discrete version of Whitney decomposition theorem, we deduce the boundedness of the discrete Riesz potentials
I_{\alpha}
on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces. The boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
on discrete weighted Morrey spaces is further obtained. Moreover, the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
is also obtained which is new even for unweighted case.

Introduction

In 1938, the classical Morrey spaces were introduced by Morrey in
[
16
]
to investigate the local behavior of solutions to second order elliptic partial differential equations. In 1987, Chiarenza and Frasca
[

showed the boundedness of the fractional integral operator on the Morrey spaces. In 2009, Komori and Shirai
[
14
]
defined a weighted Morrey space and investigated the boundedness of the fractional integral operator on this space.

During the past few years there has been renewed interest in the area of discrete harmonic analysis and then it becomes an active field of research. See also
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
18
,
19
]
for related works on
discrete analogues in harmonic analysis.

Let
m=(m_{1},\,\cdots,\,m_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
S_{m,N}:=\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\|k-m\|_{\infty}\leq N\}

for every

. Then
\left|S_{m,N}\right|=(2N+1)^{d}
-
the cardinality of

.

Definition 1.1
.

Let

characteristic sequences.

In 2019, Gunawan and Schwanke
[

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
1<p<q<\frac{d}{\alpha}
. Set
s=\frac{dp}{d-\alpha q}
and
t=\frac{qs}{p}
. Then
I_{\alpha}x\in l{{}^{s}_{t}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
for every

and there exists a positive constant

such that

\|I_{\alpha}x\|_{{l{{}^{s}_{t}}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}\leq C\|x\|_{{l{{}^{p}_{q}}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}.

In 2023, we
[
11
]
introduce a discrete version of weighted Morrey spaces and showed the boundedness of the discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on the discrete weighted Morrey spaces. Thus, we generate the following natural question:

Is it possible to prove Theorem
1.2
for weighted sequences on the discrete weighted Morrey spaces when
d=1
?

Our aim is to give an affirmative answer to the question which promotes the development of the applications of discrete Muckenhoupt weights and discrete Morrey spaces on discrete harmonic analysis.

This article is organized as follows. In Sect.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we mainly recall some preliminaries on discrete weighted Morrey spaces and discrete Muckenhoupt classes. Let us begin with the definition of discrete weighted Morrey spaces.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
S_{m,N}:=\{m-N,\,\dots,\,m,\,\dots,\,m+N\}
. Then
\left|S_{m,N}\right|=2N+1
-
the cardinality of
S_{m,N}
. A discrete weight
\omega
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence
\omega=\{\omega(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of positive real numbers.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
.

\rm(i)

[
11
, Definition 2.1]

For two discrete weights
\omega
and
v
, we define the
discrete weighted Morrey space

l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}:=l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}({\mathbb{Z}})
to the space of all sequences
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
for which

\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}}:=\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}(v(S_{m,N}))^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Particularly, when
p=q
,

\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}}=\|x\|_{l^{p}_{\omega}}:=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|x(k)|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

\rm(ii)

[
7
, Page 3]

Let
\omega
be a discrete weight. The discrete weighted weak Lebesgue space
l^{p,weak}_{\omega}:=l^{p,weak}_{\omega}({\mathbb{Z}})
is the set of sequences
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\|x\|_{l^{p,weak}_{\omega}}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\left[\omega(\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:|x(k)|>\lambda\})\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

\rm(iii)

When
\omega=v\equiv 1
,
l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}=:l{{}^{p}_{q}}
,
l^{p}_{\omega}=:l^{p}
and
l^{p,weak}_{\omega}=:l^{p,weak}
.

Now, we recall the definitions of the following weights
[
4
, Page 3]
. By interval
J
, we mean a finite subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
consisting of consecutive integers, i.e.,
J=\{a,\,a+1,\,\dots,\,a+n\}
,
a,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\left|J\right|
stands for its cardinality.

Definition 2.2
.

A discrete weight
\omega
is said to belong to the
discrete Muckenhoupt class

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}:={\mathcal{A}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}})
if

\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}})}:=\sup\limits_{J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\left(\frac{1}{\inf\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)\right)<\infty.

For
1<p<\infty
, a discrete weight
\omega
is said to belong to the
discrete Muckenhoupt class

{\mathcal{A}}_{p}:={\mathcal{A}}_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})
if

\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})}:=\sup\limits_{J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{J}\omega\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{J}\omega^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where
\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})}
denotes the norm of weight
\omega
and
J
is any bounded interval in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Define
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}:=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{1\leq p<\infty}{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
.

Some basic properties of discrete
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
weights are given as follows.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
11
, Proposition 2.8]

Let
\omega
be a discrete weight on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}

(1<p<\infty)
;

(ii)

\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\left|x(k)\right|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\omega(J)}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
,
x\in l{{}^{p}_{\omega}}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
7
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
\omega
be a discrete weight on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
1\leq r\leq p<\infty
, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{r}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
.

By Propositions
2.3
\rm(ii)
and
2.4
, we immediately obtain the following Proposition
2.5
, the proof of which in continuous version can be found in
[
15
, Page 22]
. Same line of proof also works here.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that for any subset
S\subset\,\text{interval}\,J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\left|S\right|\leq\delta\left|J\right|
, it holds true that
\omega(S)\leq\epsilon\omega(J)
.

Referring to the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
on
{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
15
, Page 139]
), we can give the definition of discrete
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A discrete weight
\omega
is said to belong to
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
for
1<p,q<\infty
, if the inequality

\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{Z}})}:=\sup\limits_{J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{J}\omega^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{J}\omega^{-p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty,

where
\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{Z}})}
denotes the norm of weight
\omega
and
J
is any bounded interval in
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Similar to the definition of discrete weight
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{Z}})
(
1<p,q<\infty
), replacing
{\mathbb{Z}}
by
{\mathbb{N}}
, we can also give the definition of discrete weight
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{N}})
. Particularly, if
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{N}})
, then
\widetilde{\omega}(\cdot):=\omega(\left|\cdot\right|)\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{Z}})
and its proof is similar to that of
[
11
, Proposition 2.11]
.

By the definitions of
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
and
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
, and discrete Hölder’s inequality, we immediately obtain the following proposition. The details being omitted and its corresponding results on real line can be referred to
[
15
, Page 139]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

(Relation between discrete
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
and discrete
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
)
Suppose that
0<\alpha<1
,
1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
.

(i)

If
p>1
, then
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)\iff\omega^{q}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1+\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\iff\omega^{-p^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1+\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}
.

(ii)

If
p>1
, then
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)\iff\omega^{q}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{q}\quad and\quad\omega^{p}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
.

Remark 2.9
.

By
[
4
, Page 8]
and Remark
2.7
, we obtain the relationships among weights
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{N}})
and
{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{Z}})
:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mu\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{R}}_{+})\iff\{\mu(k)\}^{\infty}_{k=0}\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{N}})\Rightarrow\{\mu(\left|k\right|)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)({\mathbb{Z}}).\end{array}

3
The estimates for discrete fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators

3.1
Estimates for discrete fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete Morrey spaces

Definition 3.1
.

[
21
, Definition 2.1]

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
and
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence. We define the
discrete fractional maximal operator

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
by

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(m):=\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|^{1-\alpha}}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|,\quad m\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

The boundedness of fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete Morrey spaces is shown in the following Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a sequence.

(i)

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
1<p\leq q<\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Set
s=\frac{p}{1-\alpha q}
and
t=\frac{qs}{p}
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x\in l{{}^{s}_{t}}
for every
x\in l{{}^{p}_{q}}
and there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x\|_{{l{{}^{s}_{t}}}}\leq C\|x\|_{{l{{}^{p}_{q}}}}.

(ii)

If
x\in l^{1}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x\in l^{{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}},weak}
and for every
\lambda>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for every
x\in l^{1}
,

\left|\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>\lambda\}\right|\leq C\left(\frac{\|x\|_{l_{1}}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we need some lemmas.

The following Lemma
3.3
is the boundedness of the discrete Riesz potentials on discrete Morrey space of arbitrary dimension
d
. We only use the case of
d=1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
10
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
1<p<q<\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Set
s=\frac{p}{1-\alpha q}
and
t=\frac{qs}{p}
. Then
I_{\alpha}x\in l{{}^{s}_{t}}
for every
x\in l{{}^{p}_{q}}
and there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|I_{\alpha}x\|_{{l{{}^{s}_{t}}}}\leq C\|x\|_{{l{{}^{p}_{q}}}}.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
19
, Theorem 1]

Let
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a sequence.

(i)

If
x\in l^{1}
, then
\mathcal{M}x\in l^{1,weak}
and for every
\lambda>0
,

\left|\{k:\mathcal{M}x(k)>\lambda\}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|x\|_{l_{1}},

where C is a positive constant independent of
\alpha
and
b
.

(ii)

If
x\in l^{p}
,
1<p\leq\infty
, then
\mathcal{M}x\in l^{p}
and

\|\mathcal{M}x\|_{l^{p}}\leq C_{p}\|x\|_{l^{p}},

where
C_{p}
is a positive constant depending on p.

By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we can obtain the following Lemma
3.5
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
1\leq p\leq q<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a sequence.

(i)

If
x\in l^{p}_{q}
, then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\left|I_{\alpha}x(k)\right|\leq C(\mathcal{M}x(k))^{1-\alpha q}\|x\|{{}^{\alpha q}_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}}}.

(ii)

If
x\in l^{1}
, then
I_{\alpha}x\in l^{{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}},weak}
and for every
\lambda>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for every
x\in l^{1}
,

\left|\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\left|I_{\alpha}x(k)\right|>\lambda\}\right|\leq C\left(\frac{\|x\|_{l_{1}}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}.

Proof.

By
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
we know that
\rm(i)
holds true for
0<\alpha<1
and
1<p<q<\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Actually,
\rm(i)
is also true for
0<\alpha<1
and
1\leq p\leq q<\frac{1}{\alpha}
by checking the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
with standard modifications. From
\rm(i)
with
p=q=1
and Lemma
3.4
\rm(i)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\left|I_{\alpha}x(k)\right|>\lambda\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:C\|x\|_{l_{1}}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{M}x(k))^{1-\alpha}>\lambda\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathcal{M}x(k)>\left(\frac{\lambda}{C\|x\|_{l_{1}}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\|x\|{{}^{\alpha}_{l_{1}}}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\|x\|_{l_{1}}

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{\|x\|_{l_{1}}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}.

We finish the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

The following lemma illustrates that discrete fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
can be dominated by discrete Riesz potentials
I_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a sequence. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)\leq I_{\alpha}(\left|x\right|)(k)
for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

For fixed
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(\left|x\right|)(k)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{k\}}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}\geq\sum\limits_{i\in S_{k,N+1}}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}\geq\frac{1}{(N+1)^{1-\alpha}}\sum\limits_{i\in S_{k,N+1}}\left|x(i)\right|

(3.1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(2N+1)^{1-\alpha}}\sum\limits_{i\in S_{k,N+1}}\left|x(i)\right|.

Taking the supremum for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
on both sides of (
3.1
), we obtain

I_{\alpha}(\left|x\right|)(k)\geq\sup\limits_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{(2N+1)^{1-\alpha}}\sum\limits_{i\in S_{k,N}}\left|x(i)\right|=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k).

We finish the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

By Lemmas
3.6
,
3.3
and
3.5
\rm(ii)
, we immediately obtain Theorem
3.2
.
∎

3.2
Estimates for discrete fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces

To discuss the weighted estimates of the discrete Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}
, we need to consider the weighted estimate of the discrete fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
. If
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
and
x\in l^{p}_{\omega^{p}}
, then there exists a positive constant
C
such that
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x\in l^{q}_{\omega^{q}}
and
\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x\|_{l^{q}_{\omega^{q}}}\leq C\|x\|_{l^{p}_{\omega^{p}}}
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.7
, we need some lemmas for preparation.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
. If
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
and
x\in l^{p}_{\omega^{p}}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x\in l^{q,weak}_{\omega^{q}}
and for every
\lambda>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for every
x\in l^{p}_{\omega^{p}}
,

\left(\sum_{\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>\lambda\}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|x(k)\omega(k)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

For any
0<\epsilon<1
,
\lambda>0
,
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define

\displaystyle S_{k,N}={\mathbb{Z}}\cap Q_{k,N+\epsilon},\,\,where\,\,Q_{k,N+\epsilon}:=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}:\left|y-k\right|<N+\epsilon\};

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}:=\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>\lambda\};

\displaystyle E_{\lambda,M}:=E_{\lambda}\cap S_{0,M},\,\,where\,\,S_{0,M}:=\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\left|m-0\right|\leq M\}.

Thus, for every
k\in E_{\lambda,M}
, by the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
, there exists a
S_{k,N_{k}}
such that

\left|S_{k,N_{k}}\right|^{-1+\alpha}\sum_{m\in S_{k,N_{k}}}\left|x(m)\right|>\lambda.

(3.2)

Since
E_{\lambda,M}\subset\mathop{\cup}\limits_{k\in E_{\lambda,M}}S_{k,N_{k}}\subset\mathop{\cup}\limits_{k\in E_{\lambda,M}}Q_{k,{N_{k}}+\epsilon}
, by Besicovitch overlapping theorem (see
[
8
, Page 220]
), there exists
\{k_{j}\}\subset E_{\lambda,M}
such that

4
Discrete Riesz potentials

4.1
Definition and basic properties of discrete Riesz potentials

Definition 4.1
.

[
10
, Page 8]

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence. The
discrete Riesz potential

I_{\alpha}
is defined by

I_{\alpha}x(k):=\sum\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{k\}}\frac{x(i)}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
1\leq p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence.

(i)

If
x\in l^{p}
, then the series
\sum\limits_{i\neq k}\frac{x(i)}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}
absolutely and uniformly converges on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(ii)

If
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
,
q<2p
and
x\in l^{p}_{q}
, then the series
\sum\limits_{i\neq k}\frac{x(i)}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}
absolutely and uniformly converges on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

\rm(i)
For every
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
i\neq k
, by Hölder’s inequality and
\alpha-\frac{1}{p}<0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\neq k}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\sum\limits_{2^{j-1}\leq\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}\leq\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(2^{j-1})^{1-\alpha}}\sum\limits_{\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}\left|x(i)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(2^{j-1})^{1-\alpha}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}\left|x(i)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}1\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{l^{p}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}(2^{j-1})^{\alpha-1}\left(2^{j+1}-1\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\leq 4\|x\|_{l^{p}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}(2^{j+1})^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\|x\|_{l^{p}}}{1-2^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}}.

The proof of
\rm(ii)
is similar to that of
\rm(i)
, by Hölder’s inequality,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
and
q<2p
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\neq k}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\sum\limits_{2^{j-1}\leq\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|k-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}\leq\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(2^{j-1})^{1-\alpha}}\sum\limits_{\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}\left|x(i)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(2^{j-1})^{1-\alpha}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}\left|x(i)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|k-i\right|<2^{j}}1\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{l^{p}_{q}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}(2^{j-1})^{\alpha-1}\left(2^{j+1}-1\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\leq 4\|x\|_{l^{p}_{q}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}(2^{j+1})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\|x\|_{l^{p}_{q}}}{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}}.

We finish the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Proposition
4.3
shows that if
x\in l^{p}
is monotonic sequence, then
I_{\alpha}x
inherits the monotonicity of
x
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
1\leq p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a monotonic sequence and
x\in l^{p}
. Then
I_{\alpha}x
is also monotonic.

Proof.

If
x\in l^{p}
, then by Proposition
4.2
, we obtain that
I_{\alpha}x
is converges absolutely on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}x(k+1)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{k+1\}}\frac{x(i)}{|i-(k+1)|^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\cdots+\frac{x(k-3)}{|(k-3)-(k+1)|^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k-2)}{|(k-2)-(k+1)|^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k-1)}{|(k-1)-(k+1)|^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\frac{x(k)}{|k-(k+1)|^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k+2)}{|(k+2)-(k+1)|^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k+3)}{|(k+3)-(k+1)|^{1-\alpha}}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\cdots+\frac{x(k-3)}{4^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k-2)}{3^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k-1)}{2^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k)}{1^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k+2)}{1^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k+3)}{2^{1-\alpha}}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{x(k)+x(k+2)}{1^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k-1)+x(k+3)}{2^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k-2)+x(k+4)}{3^{1-\alpha}}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{x(k+1-j)+x(k+1+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}},

and repeat the above steps, we obtain

I_{\alpha}x(k)=\sum\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{k\}}\frac{x(i)}{|i-k|^{1-\alpha}}=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{x(k-j)+x(k+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}
. From this, it follows that

\displaystyle\Delta(I_{\alpha}x(k))

\displaystyle=I_{\alpha}x(k+1)-I_{\alpha}x(k)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x(k+1-j)+x(k+1+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x(k-j)+x(k+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x(k+1-j)+x(k+1+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}-\frac{x(k-j)+x(k+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x(k+1-j)-x(k-j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{x(k+1+j)-x(k+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x(k+1-j)-x(k-j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}\bigg)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x(k+1+j)-x(k+j)}{j^{1-\alpha}}\bigg).

If
x
is nonincreasing, then we have
\Delta(I_{\alpha}x(k))\leq 0
. If
x
is nondecreasing, then we have
\Delta(I_{\alpha}x(k))\geq 0
. Therefore,
x
and
I_{\alpha}x
have the same monotonicity. We finish the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

4.2

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha

(4.1)

Now, we use the methods of Muckenhoupt and Wheeden
[

]
to prove Theorem
4.4
, and we
additionally require the following Lemma

which can be seen as a discrete version of Whitney decomposition theorem for sets.

Lemma 4.5
.

(Whitney decomposition theorem)
Let

be a set of non-empty integers. Then there exists a list of disjoint symmetric integers intervals
\{S_{m_{j},N_{j}}\}_{j}

Proof.

Any set of non-empty integers

sets, countable

sets, one

set and one

set, where

set is a single integer set like

satisfying

E_{2}
set is the finitely continuous integers set like
\{n,\,n+1,\,\cdots,\,n+m\}
satisfying

,
E_{3}
is an infinitely set like
\{i_{0},\,i_{0}+1,\,i_{0}+2,\,\cdots\}
satisfying
i_{0}-1\notin E
and
E_{4}
is an infinitely set like
\{j_{0},\,j_{0}-1,\,j_{0}-2,\,\cdots\}
satisfying
j_{0}+1\notin E
,
n,m,i_{0},j_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then we only need to decompose
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
separately.

For
E_{1}
set, we obtain that
E_{1}=\{i_{0}\}=S_{i_{0},0}
and
4S_{i_{0},0}=\{i_{0}-3,\,i_{0}-2,\,i_{0}-1,\,i_{0},\,i_{0}+1,\,i_{0}+2,\,i_{0}+3\}
, then it’s obvious that

.

For

set, we consider the following two cases.

Case I: If
|E_{2}|=m+1
is odd, then the set itself is an interval of symmetric integers, denoted as

and obviously,

Case II: If

is even, then there exist two odd numbers

and

k_{1}+k_{2}=|E_{2}|
and
E_{2}

|E_{2,1}|=k_{1}

|E_{2,2}|=k_{2}
. Then repeat case I.

For

set, we can decompose this set into a union of disjoint symmetric intervals of cardinalities
2N_{j}+1
,

, i.e.,

E_{3}:=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}S_{m_{j},N_{j}}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\{m_{j}-N_{j},\,\cdots,\,m_{j},\,\cdots,\,m_{j}+N_{j}\},

where

\displaystyle m_{j}:=i_{0}+2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}2^{j-i}+(j-1)+2^{j}=i_{0}+3\cdot 2^{j}+j-5;

\displaystyle N_{j}:=2^{j};

\displaystyle d_{j}:=|m_{j}-i_{0}|=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}2^{j-i}+(j-1)+2^{j}=3\cdot 2^{j}+j-5;

\displaystyle 4N_{j}-d_{j}=2^{j}-j+5=2^{j}-(j-1)+4>0.

This implies that

E_{3}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j}S_{m_{j},N_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad 4S_{m_{j},N_{j}}\bigcap{E}^{c}\neq\varnothing,\quad j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

The decompose for
E_{4}
set is similar to that for
E_{3}
set, the details being omitted. We finish the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

and

I_{\alpha}x(k)=\sum\limits_{i\in S}\frac{x(i)}{|i-k|^{1-\alpha}}\leq\left|k-s\right|^{\alpha-1}\sum_{i\in S}x(i)\leq\frac{\left|J\right|^{1-\alpha}}{\left|k-s\right|^{1-\alpha}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)\leq 4^{1-\alpha}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k).

Define
c=\min\left(D,\frac{1}{4^{1-\alpha}}\right)
, then we have

\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:I_{\alpha}x(k)>a\}\cap(3S)^{c}\subset\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>ac\}.

(4.6)

From (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), it follows that

\displaystyle\omega(A_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\omega((B_{1})^{c})+\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\omega(A_{2})=\omega((B_{1})^{c})+\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\omega(A_{2}\cap 3S)+\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\omega(A_{2}\cap(3S)^{c})

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\omega((B_{1})^{c})+\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\omega(A_{2}\cap 3S)+\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\omega((B_{1})^{c})

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega((B_{1})^{c})+\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\omega(A_{2}\cap 3S).

Next multiply both sides of (
4.7
) by
a^{q-1}
and integrate
a
from 0 to some positive integer
N
. After a change of variable the left side becomes

b^{-q}\int^{bN}_{0}a^{q-1}\omega(\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:I_{\alpha}x(k)>a\})da.

(4.8)

Similarly, with a change of variables for the first integral on right, the right side becomes

2c^{-q}\int^{Nc}_{0}a^{q-1}\sum_{\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>a\}}\omega(k)da+\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\int^{N}_{0}a^{q-1}\sum_{\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:I_{\alpha}x(k)>a\}\cap 3S}\omega(k)da.

(4.9)

Since
\omega(\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:I_{\alpha}x(k)>a\}\cap 3S)\leq\omega(3S)<+\infty
, then the second term in (
4.9
) is finite and

\int^{N}_{0}a^{q-1}\omega(\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:I_{\alpha}x(k)>a\}\cap 3S)da\leq\int^{bN}_{0}a^{q-1}\omega(\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:I_{\alpha}x(k)>a\})da.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{2}b^{-q}\int^{bN}_{0}a^{q-1}\omega(\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:I_{\alpha}x(k)>a\})da\leq 2c^{-q}\int^{Nc}_{0}a^{q-1}\omega(\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)>a\})da.

(4.10)

Letting
N\rightarrow\infty
on both sides of (
4.10
) and using Lemma
3.9
, it implies that

\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|I_{\alpha}x(k)\right|^{q}\omega(k)\leq 2c^{-q}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)]^{q}\omega(k).

(4.11)

If
x
doesn’t have compact support, applying
|x|=\{|x(k)|\mathcal{X}_{\{|k|\leq M\}}(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
to all of the above processes,

\displaystyle\frac{b^{-q}}{2}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[I_{\alpha}(|x|\mathcal{X}_{\{|k|\leq M\}})(k)]^{q}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{-q}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(x\mathcal{X}_{\{|k|\leq M\}})(k)]^{q}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{-q}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}x(k)]^{q}\omega(k).

Taking
M\rightarrow\infty
on above inequality and using monotone convergence theorem, we finish the proof of Lemma
4.7
.
∎

\rm(i)

4.3
Estimates for discrete Riesz potentials on discrete weighted Morrey spaces

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}
,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
and
q<2p
. Set
s=\frac{qp}{2p-q}
and
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
. Then
I_{\alpha}\in{l{{}^{q}_{s}}}{(\omega^{q})}
for every
x\in l{{}^{p}_{q}}(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})
and there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|I_{\alpha}x\|_{l{{}^{q}_{s}}(\omega^{q})}\leq C\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}.

The next lemma plays an important role in our proof of Theorem
4.8
. We say that
\omega
satisfies the
reverse doubling condition
if
\omega
has the property (
4.12
) of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
.

(i)

[
11
, Proposition 2.9]

Then there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\omega(\lambda S_{m,N})\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}C\right)^{p}\lambda^{p}\omega(S_{m,N})\quad\text{and}\quad\omega(nLI)\leq Cn^{p}\omega(I),

where
I:=\{(j-1)2^{N}+1,\cdots,j2^{N}\}
,
nLI:=\{(j-n)2^{N}+1,\cdots,j2^{N}\}
,
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(ii)

There exists a positive constant
C_{1}>1
such that

\omega(2S_{m,N})\geq C_{1}\omega(S_{m,N}),\,m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,N\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(4.12)

Proof.

\rm(ii)
When
N\geq 3
, we fix a symmetric interval with center
m
and cardinality
2N+1
, i.e.,
S_{m,N}=\{k:\left|k-m\right|\leq N\}
. Then we can choose a symmetric interval
J\subset 2S_{m,N}
with side length
\lfloor\frac{N-1}{2}\rfloor
which is disjoint from the
S_{m,N}
and

\omega(S_{m,N})+\omega(J)\leq\omega(2S_{m,N}).

On the other hand, since
S_{m,N}\subset 10J
, by Lemma
4.9
(i), there exists a positive constant
C
such that
\omega(S_{m,N})\leq\omega(10J)\leq C(15)^{p}\omega(J)
, thus we have

\omega(S_{m,N})+\frac{\omega(S_{m,N})}{C(15)^{p}}\leq\omega(2S_{m,N}).

When
0\leq N\leq 2
, for fixed symmetric interval
S_{m,N}=\{k:\left|k-m\right|\leq N\}
, we can choose a interval
J=\{m+N+1\}\subset 2S_{m,N}
which is disjoint from the
S_{m,N}
and

\omega(S_{m,N})+\omega(J)\leq\omega(2S_{m,N}).

(4.13)

On the other hand, we perform left dilation of the
J
, i.e.,

6LJ:=\{m+N-4,\cdots,m+N+1\},

then we have
S_{m,N}\subset 6LJ
, which together with Lemma
4.9
(i) implies that

\omega(S_{m,N})\leq\omega(6LJ)\leq C6^{p}\omega(J),

where C is a positive constant.
From this and (
4.13
), it follows that

\omega(S_{m,N})+\frac{\omega(S_{m,N})}{C(6)^{p}}\leq\omega(2S_{m,N}).

Combining the above estimates, we finish the proof of Lemma
4.9
.
∎

Next we shall prove the Theorem
4.8
.

Proof of Theorem
4.8
.

We may assume that
x
is a nonnegative sequence or else replace
x
by
|x|=\{|x(k)|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Fix a symmetric interval
S_{m,N}
and decompose
x=x_{1}+x_{2}
with
x_{1}=x\mathcal{X}_{2S_{m,N}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|I_{\alpha}x(k)\right|^{q}\omega(k)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left(\left|I_{\alpha}x_{1}(k)\right|^{q}+\left|I_{\alpha}x_{2}(k)\right|^{q}\right)\omega(k)^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}|I_{\alpha}x_{1}(k)|^{q}\omega(k)^{q}+C\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}|I_{\alpha}x_{2}(k)|^{q}\omega(k)^{q}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle{\rm\displaystyle I}+\rm{II}

To estimate term
\rm I
, using the fact that
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
l^{p}_{\omega^{p}}
to
l^{q}_{\omega^{q}}
with
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
(see Theorem
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|I_{\alpha}x_{1}(k)\right|^{q}\omega(k)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|I_{\alpha}x_{1}(k)\right|^{q}\omega(k)^{q}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|x_{1}(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\|x\|^{q}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}-1}.

It implies that

\|I_{\alpha}x_{1}\|_{{l^{q}_{\frac{qp}{2p-q}}}_{(\omega^{q})}}\leq C\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}.

To estimate term
\rm II
, for every
k\in S_{m,N}
and
i\in(2S_{m,N})^{c}
, we have
|m-k|\leq N
,
|k-i|\geq N+1
and

|m-i|\leq|m-k|+|k-i|\leq 2|k-i|
. Thus we obtain

\left|I_{\alpha}x_{2}(k)\right|\leq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus{\{k\}}}\frac{\left|x_{2}(i)\right|}{\left|i-k\right|^{1-\alpha}}\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\sum_{\left|i-m\right|>2N}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|m-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}.

Then

{\rm II}\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{2-\frac{q}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|i-m\right|>2N}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|i-m\right|^{1-\alpha}}\right)^{q}.

From discrete Hölder’s inequality,
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}(p,q)
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\alpha
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|>2N}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|m-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\sum\limits_{2^{j}N<\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|m-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(2^{j}N)^{1-\alpha}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\left|x(i)\right|^{p}\omega(i)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\omega(i)^{-p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{1}{(2^{j}N)^{1-\alpha}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\left|x(i)\right|^{p}\omega(i)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\omega(i)^{q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left|S_{m,2^{j+1}N}\right|^{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}(2^{j}N)^{\alpha-1}\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\omega(i)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}(2\cdot 2^{j+1}N+1)^{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}(2^{j}N)^{\alpha-1}(3\cdot 2^{j+1}N)^{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\omega(i)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C6^{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}(2^{j}N)^{\alpha-1+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\omega(i)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|m-i\right|\leq 2^{j+1}N}\omega(i)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}.

By Lemma
4.9
and
q<2p
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{q}\right)^{2-\frac{q}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{\left|i-m\right|>2N}\frac{\left|x(i)\right|}{\left|m-i\right|^{1-\alpha}}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|x\|^{q}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}\left(\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{\omega^{q}(S_{m,N})^{\frac{2}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}{\omega^{q}(S_{m,2^{j+1}N})^{\frac{2}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}}\right)^{q}\leq C\|x\|^{q}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}\left(\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=1}({C_{1}}^{j+1})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}\right)^{q}\leq C\|x\|^{q}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega^{p},\omega^{q})}}.

Combining the above estimates, we finish the proof of Theorem
4.8
.
∎

Kronecker Product of Tensors and Hypergraphs: Structure and Dynamics Thanks: Submitted to the editors August 9, 2023.
Joshua Pickard, Can Chen, Cooper Stansbury, Amit Surana, Anthony Bloch, Indika Rajapakse

Abstract
Hypergraphs and tensors extend classic graph and matrix theory to account for multiway relationships, which are ubiquitous in engineering, biological, and social systems. While the Kronecker product is a potent tool for analyzing the coupling of systems in graph or matrix contexts, its utility in studying multiway interactions, such as those represented by tensors and hypergraphs, remains elusive. In this article, we present a comprehensive exploration of algebraic, structural, and spectral properties of the tensor Kronecker product. We express Tucker and tensor train decompositions and various tensor eigenvalues in terms of the tensor Kronecker product. Additionally, we utilize the tensor Kronecker product to form Kronecker hypergraphs, a tensor-based hypergraph product, and investigate the structure and stability of polynomial dynamics on Kronecker hypergraphs. Finally, we provide numerical examples to demonstrate the utility of the tensor Kronecker product in computing Z-eigenvalues, various tensor decompositions, and determining the stability of polynomial systems.



keywords

tensor Kronecker product, hypergraph products, tensor decomposition, tensor eigenvalues, multilinear system, block tensors

Tensor preliminaries

Tensors are multidimensional arrays generalized from vectors and matrices

Definition 1
(Tensor norms

]

Given a

\|\textsf{T}\|_{1}=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\dots\sum_{j_{k}=1}^{n_{k}}|\textsf{T}_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}|\text{ and }\|\textsf{T}\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\dots\sum_{j_{k}=1}^{n_{k}}\textsf{T}_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}^{2}},

respectively.

In order to represent the fibers and slices of a tensor (as defined below), we employ the MATLAB colon notation. For example,
\mathbf{A}_{i:}
represents the

\mathbf{A}

\mathbf{A}

Definition 2
(Fibers and slices
[
37
]

Fibers refer to vectors obtained from a tensor by keeping all indices fixed except for one. Slices, on the other hand, correspond to matrices obtained from a tensor by fixing all indices except for two.

Consider a third-order tensor
\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

\textsf{T}_{:jl}

\textsf{T}_{i:l}

\textsf{T}_{i::}

\textsf{T}_{:j:}

\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}\times n_{4}},
we denote
\textsf{T}_{4=1}

These structures play a fundamental role in the tensor decompositions of the tensor KP.

2.1.1
Tensor products

Multiplying a tensor
T
by a vector, matrix, or another tensor involves multiplying each fiber of
T
along a specified mode with the corresponding vector, rows/columns of the matrix, or fibers of the other tensor.

Definition 3
(Tensor inner product
[
37
]
).

Given two
k
th-order tensors
\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
and
\textsf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
of the same size, the tensor inner product is

\langle\textsf{T},\textsf{S}\rangle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\dots\sum_{j_{k}=1}^{n_{k}}\textsf{T}_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}\textsf{S}_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}.

The tensor Frobenius norm therefore can be expressed as
\|\textsf{T}\|^{2}_{F}=\langle\textsf{T},\textsf{T}\rangle
. Two tensors
T
and
S
are called orthogonal if
\langle\textsf{T},\textsf{S}\rangle=0
. The tensor outer product is a direct extension of the vector outer product.

Definition 4
(Tensor outer product
[
37
]
).

Given a
k_{1}
th-order tensor
\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k_{1}}}
and a
k_{2}
th-order tensor
\textsf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times\dots\times m_{k_{2}}}
, the tensor outer product is

(\textsf{T}\circ\textsf{S})_{j_{1}\dots j_{k_{1}}i_{1}\dots i_{k_{2}}}=\textsf{T}_{j_{1}\dots j_{k_{1}}}\textsf{S}_{i_{1}\dots i_{k_{2}}}.

Furthermore, tensors can be multiplied by vectors or matrices, similar to matrix vector or matrix matrix multiplications.

Definition 5
(Tensor matrix multiplication
[
37
]
).

Given a
k
th-order tensor
\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
, the tensor matrix multiplication
\textsf{T}\times_{p}\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{p-1}\times m\times n_{p+1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
along mode
p
for a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{p}}
is

(\textsf{T}\times_{p}\mathbf{A})_{j_{1}\dots j_{p-1}ij_{p+1}\dots j_{k}}=\sum_{j_{p}=1}^{n_{p}}\textsf{T}_{j_{1}\dots j_{p}\dots j_{k}}\mathbf{A}_{ij_{p}}.

The tensor vector multiplication follows directly from the tensor matrix multiplication by treating the vector as a matrix. Furthermore, the tensor vector multiplication can be extended to

\begin{split}\textsf{T}\times_{1}\textbf{v}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{k}\textbf{v}_{k}\in\mathbb{R},\end{split}

(1)

for
\textbf{v}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}}
. The above expression is also known as the homogeneous polynomial associated with
T
. If
\textbf{v}_{p}=\textbf{v}
for all
p
, we write (
1
) as
\textsf{T}\textbf{v}^{k}
for simplicity. Last but not least, the Einstein product is one particular form of tensor tensor multiplications.

Definition 6
(Einstein product
[
18
]
).

Given an even-order tensor

\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times m_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}\times m_{k}}
and a
k
th-order tensor
\textsf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times\dots\times m_{k}},
the Einstein product
\textsf{T}*\textsf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
is

(\textsf{T}*\textsf{X})_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{m_{1}}\dots\sum_{i_{k}=1}^{m_{k}}\textsf{T}_{j_{1}i_{1}\dots j_{k}i_{k}}\textsf{X}_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}.

The above tensor products are fundamental to tensor eigenvalue problems and tensor decompositions and will be used throughout the remainder of the article.

2.1.3
Tensor decompositions

A wide range of tensor decompositions have been developed
[
37
,
48
]
. These decompositions serve as powerful tools to represent and analyze complex data structures that exhibit multidimensional relationships.

Definition 11
(Tucker decomposition
[
70
]
).

The Tucker decomposition represents a
k
th-order tensor
\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
as a core tensor
S
multiplied by matrices
\mathbf{U}_{p}
along each mode
p
, i.e.,

\textsf{T}=\textsf{S}\times_{1}\mathbf{U}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k},

(4)

where
\textsf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times\dots\times m_{k}}
and
\mathbf{U}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}\times m_{p}}
.

The Canonical Polyadic decomposition (CPD), orthogonal decomposition, and higher-order SVD (HOSVD) are special cases of the Tucker decomposition. In the case of CPD,
S
becomes a diagonal tensor such that
T
can be equivalently represented as the sum of rank one tensors, and the number of nonzero diagonal entries in
S
is referred to as the CP-rank
[
36
]
. For orthogonal decomposition, a supersymmetric tensor is called orthogonally decomposable when
S
is diagonal and the matrices
\mathbf{U}_{j}
contain orthanormal columns
[
61
]
. The HOSVD is a multilinear extension of the matrix SVD that requires the matrices
\mathbf{U}_{j}
to contain orthonormal columns and the core tensor
S
to satisfy the following two conditions
[
20
]
:

•

All-orthogonality: the sub-tensors
\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\alpha}
and
\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\beta}
are orthogonal for all possible values
p,\alpha,\beta
subject to
\alpha\neq\beta.

•

Ordering:
\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}\geq\dots\geq\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=n_{p}}\|_{F}\geq 0
for all possible values of
p
.

The Frobenius norms
\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\alpha}\|_{F}=\gamma_{\alpha}^{(p)}
are the
p
-mode singular values of
\textsf{T},
and the number of nonvanishing
p
-mode singular values is called the
p
-rank of
T
. The multilinear rank of
T
is defined as the maximum of its
p
-ranks.

The Tensor Train decomposition (TTD) is an alternative decomposition to Tucker decompositions.

Definition 12
(TTD
[
49
,
48
]
).

Given a
k
th-order tensor
\textsf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
, the TTD represents
T
as a sum of the outer product of fibers taken from third-order tensors, i.e.,

\textsf{T}=\sum_{j_{0}=1}^{r_{0}}\dots\sum_{j_{k}=1}^{r_{k}}\textsf{T}^{(1)}_{j_{0}:j_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\textsf{T}^{(k)}_{j_{k-1}:j_{k}},

(5)

where
\{r_{0},\dots,r_{k}\}
is called the set of TT-ranks and
\textsf{T}^{(p)}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{p-1}\times n_{p}\times r_{p}}
are called the core tensors of
T
.

The computation of the TTD is numerically stable, exhibiting linear complexity in both time and storage relative to the dimensions of
T
. This efficiency makes it particularly suitable for addressing high-dimensional problems.

Block Definition of KP

Block tensors, developed using principles similar to block matrix operations, are intimately related to the tensor KP. Just like block matrices, a block tensor is a tensor in which the elements themselves are tensors. In the case of a

Similar to matrices, operations on block tensors can be defined and executed either elementwise or through vectorization, bypassing the use of blocks. This viewpoint was adopted by

Given two

(6)

In accordance with the dimensions of

2.3
Algebraic properties

The tensor KP exhibits various algebraic properties similar to those of the matrix KP. Just like the matrix KP, the tensor KP
\otimes
is bilinear, meaning that the function
f(\textsf{B},\textsf{C})=\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
is linear with respect to both
B
and
C
.

Property 14
(Kronecker product algebra

[
3
]
).

Given three
k
th-order tensors
B
,
C
, and
D
with a scalar
\alpha
, the following relations hold:

•

Distribution over tensor addition:

\textsf{B}\otimes(\textsf{C}+\textsf{D})=\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}+\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{D}\text{ and }(\textsf{B}+\textsf{C})\otimes\textsf{D}=\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{D}+\textsf{C}\otimes\textsf{D};

(8)

•

Commutation with scalar multiplication:

\alpha(\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C})=(\alpha\textsf{B})\otimes\textsf{C}=\textsf{B}\otimes(\alpha\textsf{C});

(9)

•

Kronecker product association:

(\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C})\otimes\textsf{D}=\textsf{B}\otimes(\textsf{C}\otimes\textsf{D}).

(10)

For the matrix KP, the mixed product property, i.e.,
(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})\otimes(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{D})=(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{C})(\mathbf{B}\otimes\mathbf{D})

[
26

, simplifies the expression of diverse matrix decompositions. Similarly, translating tensor decompositions into KPs necessitates mixed product properties, each corresponding to the various tensor products involved.

Property 15
(Mixed tensor matrix multiplication

[
59

Given two

and
\textsf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times\dots\times m_{k}}

(\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C})\times_{p}(\mathbf{X}\otimes\mathbf{Y})=(\textsf{B}\times_{p}\mathbf{X})\otimes(\textsf{C}\times_{p}\mathbf{Y})

(11)

for any two matrices
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n_{p}}
and
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times m_{p}}
.

Properties

Given two

th-order tensors

For brevity, the proof is omitted. Furthermore, the tensor KP preserves fiber, slide, and sub-tensor structures, proving crucial in computing the TTD or HOSVD of a tensor generated via this product.

2.4
Tensor Kronecker structure

Many tensor structures, such as diagonal, banded, block, stochastic
[
6
]
, are similar to their corresponding matrix structures; for instance, a tensor is diagonal if
\textsf{T}_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}=0
except possibly when
j_{1}=\dots=j_{k}
. These structures are preserved under the tensor KP.

Property 22
(Tensor Kronecker structure).

The tensor KP of
k
th-order

\begin{pmatrix}\text{diagonal}\\
\text{banded}\\
\text{supersymmetric}\\
\text{upper (lower) triangular}\\
\text{stochastic}\\
\text{Hankel \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{batselier2017constructive}{}{}]}}\\
\text{Toeplitz \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{batselier2017constructive}{}{}]}}\end{pmatrix}\text{ tensors is a }\begin{pmatrix}\text{diagonal}\\
\text{block banded}\\
\text{supersymmetric}\\
\text{upper (lower) triangular}\\
\text{stochastic}\\
\text{Hankel \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{batselier2017constructive}{}{}]}}\\
\text{Toeplitz \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{batselier2017constructive}{}{}]}}\end{pmatrix}\text{ tensor.}

Proof.

These properties are relatively straightforward to verify, and as an example, we illustrate the preservation of diagonal structure. Suppose
B
and
C
are diagonal tensor such that
\textsf{B}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\neq 0
and
\textsf{C}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}\neq 0
if and only if
i_{1}=\dots=i_{k}
and
j_{1}=\dots=j_{k}
. It follows that
[\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}]_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\neq 0
if and only if
i_{1}=\dots=i_{k}.
More over, within the block
[\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}]_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
, the
(j_{1},\dots,j_{k})
th element is nonzero if and only if
j_{1}=\dots=j_{k}.
Therefore, an element of
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
is nonzero if and only if the element is a diagonal element of a diagonal block of
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C},
which is equivalent to saying the element is along the main diagonal of the KP
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
. Preservation of the remaining structures may be verified similarly.

Additionally, the tensor KP preserves the properties of being general symmetric, persymmetric, and centrosymmetric
[
3
]
.

2.6
Tensor decompositions

Computing tensor decompositions, particularly for high-dimensional, high-mode tensors, can be facilitated by leveraging the KP or tensors generated with this structure. In this subsection, we explore the use of Kronecker factors in expressing different tensor decompositions. Our focus centers around the problem of efficiently computing the tensor decomposition of the tensor KP
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
where the decompositions of

and

Property 27
(Tucker decomposition).

Given two
k
th-order tensors
B
and
C
with Tucker decompositions that have core tensors
S
and
R
and factor matrices
\mathbf{U}_{p}
and
\mathbf{V}_{p}
, respectively, the Tucker decomposition of the tensor KP
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
has a core tensor
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
and factor matrices

for all

.

Proof.

According to
15
, taking the tensor KP of the Tucker decompositions of

and

\begin{split}(\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C})&=(\textsf{S}\times_{1}\mathbf{U}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k})\otimes(\textsf{R}\times_{1}\mathbf{V}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k})\\
&=(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})\times_{1}(\mathbf{U}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{1})\times_{2}\dots\times_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{k}).\end{split}

In this form,
(\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C})
is expressed as a core tensor
(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})
times factor matrices
(\mathbf{U}_{p}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{p})
for all

This constitutes a Tucker decomposition of

Since the CPD and orthogonal decomposition are special cases of the Tucker decomposition, the Properties

and

follow similarly, and we state them without proof for brevity.

Property 28
(CPD).

Given two
k
th-order tensors

and

\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
and factor matrices

.

The CPD requirement that the core tensor

is fulfilled by

, Chapter 4]
. The diagonal requirement on the core tensor is similarly applied to the orthognal decomposition, and the additional constraint that the factor matrices of orthogonal decomposition remain orthogonal is satisfied by the well known property of the matrix KP which preserves orthogonality.

Property 29
(Orthogonal decomposition).

Given two
k
th-order supersymmetric tensors
B
and

with orthogonal decompositions that have diagonal core tensors

and

and orthogonal factor matrices
\mathbf{U}
and

, which is supersymmetric, has a core tensor
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
and factor matrix

.

Whereas the CPD and orthogonal decompositions of

come directly from the decompositions of
B
and
\textsf{C},
when

is not sufficiently well conditioned, computing the HOSVD of

can require reindexing the separate HOSVDs of

and

.

Property 30
(HOSVD).

Given two
k
th-order tensors
B
and
C
with HOSVDs that have core tensors
S
and
R
and orthogonal factor matrices
\mathbf{U}_{p}
and
\mathbf{V}_{p}
, respectively, the HOSVD of the tensor KP
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
has a core tensor that is generated by permuting the indices of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
and factor matrices
\mathbf{U}_{p}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{p}
with similarly permuted indices for all
p
.

Proof.

Consider two
k
th-order tensors
B
and
C
with HOSVDs that have core tensors
\textsf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dots\times n_{k}}
and
\textsf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times\dots\times m_{k}}
and orthogonal factor matrices
\mathbf{U}_{p}
and
\mathbf{V}_{p}
, respectively. In order for
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
and factor matrices
\mathbf{U}_{p}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{p}
for all
p
to constitute a valid HOSVD, both the all-orthogonality and ordering properties must be satisfied by
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}:

•

All-orthogonality: From the HOSVD of
B
and
\textsf{C},
the all-orthogonality property stipulates
\langle\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{1}},\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\beta_{1}}\rangle=0
and
\langle\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{1}},\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\beta_{1}}\rangle=0
for all valid
p,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},
subject to
\alpha_{1}\neq\beta_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}\neq\beta_{2}.
The sub-tensor of
(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})_{j_{p}=\alpha_{3}}
can be expressed as the product of sub-tensors
\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{1}}\otimes\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{2}},
where
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are set according to
21
. From
16
, the inner product of any two sub-tensors of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
can be written

\begin{split}\langle(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})_{j_{p}=\alpha_{3}},(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})_{j_{p}=\beta_{3}}\rangle&=\langle\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{1}}\otimes\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{2}},\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\beta_{1}}\otimes\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\beta_{2}}\rangle\\
&=\langle\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{1}},\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\beta_{1}}\rangle\langle\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\alpha_{2}},\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\beta_{2}}\rangle=0.\end{split}

All mode
j_{p}
-mode sub-tensors of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
are orthogonal, so
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
obeys the all-orthogonality property.

•

Ordering: From the HOSVD of
B
and
\textsf{C},
the ordering principle requires
\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}\geq\dots\geq\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=n_{p}}\|_{F}
and
\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}\geq\dots\geq\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=n_{p}}\|_{F}
for all valid
p.
The
j_{p}
-mode sub-tensors of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
have the relation
\|(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})_{j_{p}=\epsilon}\|_{F}=\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=\alpha}\|_{F}\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=\gamma}\|_{F},
which allows us to apply the mixed product property
19
and rewrite the ordering constraint
\|(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}\geq\dots\geq\|(\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R})_{j_{p}=n_{p}m_{p}}\|_{F}
in terms of the
p
-mode singular values of
S
and
R
as

\begin{split}\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}&\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}\geq\dots\geq\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=m_{p}}\|_{F}\geq\dots\geq\\
&\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=n_{p}}\|_{F}\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}\geq\dots\geq\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=n_{p}}\|_{F}\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=m_{p}}\|_{F}.\end{split}

(16)

When
\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=i+1}\|_{F}\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=m_{p}}\|_{F}\geq\|\textsf{S}_{j_{p}=i}\|_{F}\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}
for all indices
i
,
eq.

16
is satisfied. If
C
is sufficiently well conditioned such that
\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=1}\|_{F}-\|\textsf{R}_{j_{p}=m_{p}}\|_{F}
is small, this criteria is satisfied, and
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
obeys the ordering property. Otherwise, when the
p
-mode singular values of
C
are not well conditioned, the
p
-mode indices of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
may be rearranged so that the ordering property is satisfied. This rearrangement must coincide with the rearrangement of the indices in
\mathbf{U}_{p}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{p}
. Since the all-orthogonality principal is satisfied, rearranging the indices in one mode of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
does not impact the singular values in any other mode nor does it cause the all-orthogonality principle to be violated. By performing this rearrangement of indices in all modes, the ordering principle can be satisfied, and the HOSVD of
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
can be generated from the HOSVDs of
B
and
\textsf{C}.

We conclude that the all-orthogonality property is always obeyed, and it is possible to permute the indices of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
to satisfy the ordering.

A similar instance of permuting indices used in the proof above is required when computing the SVD of the matrix
\mathbf{B}\otimes\mathbf{C}
from the SVDs of
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{C}
separately. The thesis of Ragnarsson investigated the all-orthogonal property of
\textsf{S}\otimes\textsf{R}
in
[
59
, Chapter 4]
in the context of the HOSVD. Regardless of if the singular values must be reordered, the
p
-mode singular values of the tensor KP
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
are equal to the product of the
p
-mode singular values of
B
and
C
. Furthermore, the
p
-mode rank of the tensor KP is the product of the
p
-mode ranks of
B
and
\textsf{C}.

The multilinear rank of the tensor KP
\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C}
is less than or equal to the product of the multilinear ranks of
B
and
C
; this is equal only when the
p
-mode ranks of
B
and
C
are maximized in the same mode for both tensors, which occurs in the case of supersymmetric tensors such as those associated with hypergraphs.

Property 31
(TTD).

Given two

th-order tensors

and

and

Suppose that

and
\textsf{C}^{(p)}

B
and

17

In this form,

\textsf{B}\otimes\textsf{C},
and from the summations,

The expression of the Tucker Decompositions and the TTD for

in terms of the respective decompositions of

and

is of interest when computing the factorizations. For large systems where the Kronecker factorization is known, as we will see in

3.1
Hypergraph preliminaries

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is a pair
\{\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\}
where
\mathcal{V}
is the node set and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{V})\setminus\{\emptyset\}
is the hyperedge set, where
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{V})
is the power set of
\mathcal{V}
. We exclusively consider
k
-uniform, unweighted hypergraphs, where all hyperedges contain exactly
k

3.1.1
Hypergraph structure

As the multiway analog of an adjacency matrix, an adjacency tensor is the principle representation of a hypergraph.

Definition 32
(Adjacency tensors
[
68
]
).

Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=\{\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\}
with
n
nodes, the adjacency tensor
\textsf{A}(\mathcal{H})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times\dots\times n}
of
\mathcal{H}
, which is a
k
th order
n
-dimensional supersymmetric tensor, is defined as

\textsf{A}(\mathcal{H})_{j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{k}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(k-1)!}&\text{if $\{v_{j_{1}},v_{j_{2}},\dots,v_{j_{k}}\}\in\mathcal{E}$}\\
\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

The degree
d(v)
of a node
v\in\mathcal{V}
is
d(v)=|\{e\in\mathcal{E}\text{ s.t. }v\in e\}|
. Similar to dyadic graphs, the degree of node
v_{j}
of a hypergraph can be computed as

d(v_{j})=\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{3}=1}^{n}\dots\sum_{j_{k}=1}^{n}\textsf{A}(\mathcal{H})_{jj_{2}j_{3}\dots j_{k}},

(17)

which is equivalent to the vectorized equation
\mathbf{d}=\textsf{A}(\mathcal{H})\mathbf{1}_{n}^{k-1},
where
\mathbf{1}_{n}
denotes the vector of all ones in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{d}
is the degree vector. If all nodes have the same degree
d
, then
\mathcal{H}
is
d
-regular. The degree vector, which encodes the degree distribution, is one mechanism through which the importance or centrality of nodes may be ranked.

Definition 33
(Eigenvector centrality
[
5
]

Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H},
an H- or Z-eigenvector centrality vector

is any positive real vector satisfying (
2
) or (
3
), respectively, for the hypergraph adjacency tensor

.

There are additional notions of hypergraph centrality, but we restrict ourselves to the multilinear definition of centrality for the purpose of investigating the tensor KP; see
[

3.2

We define Kronecker hypergraphs in terms of the KP of adjacency tensors and explore the structural and dynamic properties of this hypergraph product.

3.3
Kronecker hypergraph dynamics

The dynamics of a Kronecker hypergraph can be decoupled and studied through the separate dynamics of its factor hypergraphs. For instance, given satisfactory initial conditions, the trajectory of the discrete-time equations of a Kronecker hypergraph can be integrated based upon the trajectory of the factor hypergraphs.

Property 45

\mathcal{H}_{1}

Since the factor hypergraph dynamical systems are asymptotically stable at the origin, it follows that

and
\mu_{j}\beta_{j}^{k-2}<0

5
Conclusion

This article provides a comprehensive description of the tensor KP and its properties and proposes the concept of Kronecker hypergraphs as a tensor-based hypergraph product. In particular, we have demonstrated that:

•

the KP generalizes naturally to tensors and has many nice structural, algebraic, and spectral properties in addition to being a convenient way to represent and calculate various tensor decompositions.

•

hypergraph products may be expressed and understood in terms of the KP of adjacency tensors, and the stability of homogeneous polynomial dynamics defined according to Kronecker hypergraphs may be characterized using its factored hypergraphs.

•

Kronecker structure can aid large tensor decompositions and eigenvalue calculation.

In the future we plan to apply the tensor and hypergraph KP concepts to real-world applications.

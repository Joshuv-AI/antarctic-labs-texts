Some progress in the Dixmier Conjecture Thanks: Bowen Tan is the corresponding author.
Gang Han, Bowen Tan

Abstract
. Let p p and q q , where p ​ q − q ​ p = 1 pq-qp=1 , be the standard generators of the first Weyl algebra A 1 A_{1} over a field of characteristic zero. Then the spectrum of the inner derivation a ​ d ​ ( p ​ q ) ad(pq) on A 1 A_{1} are exactly the set of integers. The algebra A 1 A_{1} is a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded algebra with each i i -component being the i i -eigenspace of a ​ d ​ ( p ​ q ) ad(pq) , where i ∈ ℤ i\in\mathbb{Z} . The Dixmier Conjecture says that if some elements z z and w w of A 1 A_{1} satisfy z ​ w − w ​ z = 1 zw-wz=1 , then they generate A 1 A_{1} . We show that if either z z or w w possesses no component belonging to the negative spectrum of a ​ d ​ ( p ​ q ) ad(pq) , then the Dixmier Conjecture holds. We give some generalization of this result, and some other useful criterions for z z and w w to generate A 1 A_{1} . An important tool in our proof is the Newton polygon for elements in A 1 A_{1} .

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of Characteristic 0. Let
A_{1}
be the first Weyl algebra over
K
generated by
p,q
with

[p,q]=pq-qp=1.

In the influential paper
[
D1968
]
, Dixmier studied the structure of
A_{1}
in great detail, and in the end he posed six problems. Joseph gave some characterization for semisimple and nilpotent elements of
A_{1}
and solved two problems in
[
J1973
]
.

The first problem of
[
D1968
]
asks if
every endomorphism of the Weyl algebra
A_{1}
is an automorphism, i.e., given elements

z,w
of
A_{1}
such that
[z,w]=1
, do they generate the algebra
A_{1}
? A similar problem
for the
n
-th Weyl algebra is called the Dixmier Conjecture. It is open for all
n\geq 1
. In this paper, the Dixmier Conjecture for
A_{1}
will be abbreviated by DC.

People have made many explorations in DC.

In
[
MV2015
]
, the authors showed that DC holds for a particular type of endomorphism of
A_{1}
. By taking the same idea further, Moskowicz gave some equivalent formulation of DC
[
MO2015
]
.

The spectrum of the inner derivation
ad(pq)
on
A_{1}
are exactly the set of integers.
A_{1}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra with each
i
-component
D_{i}
being the
i
-eigenspace of
ad(pq)
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. An element
z\in D_{i}
is said to be homogeneous of degree
i
. One has

A_{1}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}D_{i},\ and\ D_{i}\cdot D_{j}\subseteq D_{i+j},\forall i,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

It follows that
D_{\geq 0}=\oplus_{i\geq 0}D_{i}
is a subalgebra of
A_{1}
.

In
[
B2011
]
, Bavula showed that the eigenvector algebra of
zw
, i.e., the subalgebra of
A_{1}
generated by all the eigenvectors of
ad(zw)
in
A_{1}
, is equal to the subalgebra
K\langle z,w\rangle
of
A_{1}
generated by
z
and
w
.

In
[
GGV2012
]
, the authors showed that if
z,w
of
A_{1}
satisfying
[z,w]=1
, then the centralizer
C(z)
of
z
in
A_{1}
equals
K[z]
.

If
z
and
w
do not generate
A_{1}
, the authors in
[
VGG2017
]
showed that the
greatest common divisor of the total degrees of
z
and
w
are
>15
, where
z
and
w
are written as polynomials in
p
and
q
.

In
[
BL2020
]
the authors prove that DC holds if
z
and
w
are sums of no more than two homogeneous elements of
A_{1}
.

In this paper we show that if
z
and
w
satisfy certain conditions then they generate
A_{1}
. In particular we prove the following main result in Theorem
3.7
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
z,w\in A_{1},

[z,w]=1
, and either
z
or
w
is in
D_{\geq 0}
. Then
z
and
w
generate
A_{1}
.

This result is generalized in Corollary
3.11
. Proposition
3.12
and Theorem
3.13
give some other criterions for
z
and
w
to generate
A_{1}
. As a corollary, the main result in
[
BL2020
]
is generalized in Theorem
3.15
.

Our proof are partially motivated by the idea in
[
J1973
]
of Joseph. The Newton polygon for any element in
A_{1}
is defined and plays important role in the proof. Our definition of Newton polygon is slightly different to that defined in
[
ML2021
]
. Some important observations of Newton polygons of elements in
A_{1}
can be found in
[
GZ2022
]
.

Here are some of the conventions of the paper.

X,Y
denote indeterminants;

i,j,k,l,m,n,r,s
denote integers;

For positive integers
m
and
n
,
gcd(m,n)
is the positive greatest common devisor of
m
and
n
;

K
is a field of characteristic 0;
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\lambda,\mu
denotes elements in
K
;
K^{*}=K\setminus\{0\}
;

For
z\in A_{1}
,
C(z)=\{w\in A_{1}|zw=wz\}
is the centralizer of
z
in
A_{1}
;
K[z]=\{f(z)|f\in K[X]\}
;

\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the Euclidean plane with the Cartesian coordinate system
Oxy
;

(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{2}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|x\geq 0,y\geq 0\}
;

V_{+}=\{(x,y)\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{2}|y\geq x\},V_{-}=\{(x,y)\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{2}|y\leq x\}
;

For a nonempty set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

Convex(S)
is the convex hull of
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

Cone(S)=\{t(x,y)|t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},(x,y)\in S\}.

2.
Some preliminaries

2.1.
Recall that
K
is a field of Characteristic 0. For
f,g\in K[X,Y]
, define their Poisson product

(2.1)

\{f,g\}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ll}{\partial f}/{\partial X}&{\partial f}/{\partial Y}\\
{\partial g}/{\partial X}&{\partial g}/{\partial Y}\end{array}\right|.

In particular, one has

(2.2)

\{X^{i}Y^{j},X^{k}Y^{l}\}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ll}i&j\\
k&l\end{array}\right|X^{i+k-1}Y^{j+l-1}.

For
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, if
f\in K[X,Y]
satisfies

\rho X\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}+\sigma Y\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}=\tau f,

then
f
is said to be
(\rho,\sigma)
-
homogeneous of degree

\tau
, and is denoted by
\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)=\tau
. Note that if
f
is
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
\tau
, then
f
is
(\iota\rho,\iota\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
\iota\tau
, for any
\iota\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. If
(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, then for
X^{i}Y^{j}\in K[X,Y]
,

\mathbf{v}_{r,s}(X^{i}Y^{j})=ri+sj\in\mathbb{Z}.

By this definition, 0 is
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
\tau
, for any
\tau
in
\mathbb{R}
; a nonzero constant polynomial is always
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree 0.

For
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\ and\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}
, let

V_{\rho,\sigma}(\tau)=\{f\in K[X,Y]|\rho X\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}+\sigma Y\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}=\tau f\}.

Then
V_{\rho,\sigma}(\tau)
is a linear subspace of
K[X,Y]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(Proposition 2.1 of
[
J1973
]
)
If
f\in V_{\rho,\sigma}(u),g\in V_{\rho,\sigma}(v)
with
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, then

fg\in V_{\rho,\sigma}(u+v),\ and\ \{f,g\}\in V_{\rho,\sigma}(u+v-\rho-\sigma).

For
f\in K[X,Y]\setminus\{0\}
, assume
f=\sum\alpha_{ij}X^{i}Y^{j}
. Define

E(f)=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}|\alpha_{ij}\neq 0\}.

Let
Convex(E(f))
be the convex hull of
E(f)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is usually a solid polygon.
For
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, we also set

\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)=sup\{\rho i+\sigma j|(i,j)\in E(f)\},

which is usually called the
(\rho,\sigma)
-degree of
f
. Note that if
f
is a nonzero
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous polynomial then
\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)
agrees with the previous definition. Let

E(f;\rho,\sigma)=\{(i,j)\in E(f)|\rho i+\sigma j=\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f).\}

Let

\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)=\sum_{(i,j)\in E(f;\rho,\sigma)}\alpha_{ij}X^{i}Y^{j}.

Assume that
f\in K[X,Y]\setminus\{0\}
, and
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, then there is a unique decomposition

(2.3)

f=\sum_{i=0}^{k}f_{i}

with
f_{i}\neq 0
being
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous for all
i
and
\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f_{i})>\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f_{i+1})
for
i=0,\cdots,k-1
. We will refer to (
2.3
) as the
(\rho,\sigma)
-
homogeneous decomposition
of
f
, and
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)=f_{0}
is the
(\rho,\sigma)
-
leading component
. Note that for
\tau>0
, one has
\mathbf{f}_{\tau\rho,\tau\sigma}(f)=\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)
.

The set
Convex(E(\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)))
is either a vertex or an edge of
Convex(E(f))
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f,g\in K[X,Y]\setminus\{0\}
,
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
,
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(f)=f_{0}
, and
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(g)=g_{0}
. Then

(1)
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(fg)=f_{0}g_{0}
;

(2) If
\{f_{0},g_{0}\}\neq 0
then
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(\{f,g\})=\{f_{0},g_{0}\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
f=\sum_{i=0}^{k}f_{i}
and
g=\sum_{j=0}^{l}g_{j}
are the respective
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous decomposition of
f
and
g
, where

\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f_{i})=\tau_{i}
, and
\tau_{i}>\tau_{i+1}
for
i=0,\cdots,k-1
;
\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(g_{j})=\nu_{j}
, and
\nu_{j}>\nu_{j+1}
for all
j=0,\cdots,l-1
.

(1) One has

fg=f_{0}g_{0}+\sum_{i+j\geq 1}f_{i}g_{j},

where
f_{i}g_{j}
are
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
\tau_{i}+\nu_{j}
, for all
i,j
.

As
f_{0}g_{0}\neq 0
, for any
i,j
with
i+j\geq 1
,

\tau_{0}+\nu_{0}=\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f_{0}g_{0})>\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(f_{i}g_{j})=\tau_{i}+\nu_{j},

one has
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(fg)=f_{0}g_{0}
.

(2) One has

\{f,g\}=\{f_{0},g_{0}\}+\sum_{i+j\geq 1}\{f_{i},g_{j}\},

where
\{f_{i},g_{j}\}
are
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
\tau_{i}+\nu_{j}-(\rho+\sigma)
, for all
i,j
.

By assumption,
\{f_{0},g_{0}\}\neq 0
. Since for any
i,j
with
i+j\geq 1
,

\tau_{0}+\nu_{0}-(\rho+\sigma)=\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(\{f_{0},g_{0}\})>\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(\{f_{i},g_{j}\})=\tau_{i}+\nu_{j}-(\rho+\sigma),

one has
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(\{f,g\})=\{f_{0},g_{0}\}
.

∎

2.2.
Assume that
f,g\in K[X,Y]
are
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
v,u
respectively, one has

(2.4)

\rho X\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}+\sigma Y\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}=vf

and

(2.5)

\rho X\frac{\partial g}{\partial X}+\sigma Y\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}=ug.

Subtract
\frac{\partial g}{\partial X}
times (
2.4
) from
\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}
times (
2.5
), one has

(2.6)

\sigma Y(\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}\frac{\partial g}{\partial X})=ug\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}-vf\frac{\partial g}{\partial X}.

Subtract
\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}
times (
2.4
) from
\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}
times (
2.5
), one has

(2.7)

-\rho X(\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}\frac{\partial g}{\partial X})=ug\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}-vf\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}.

If
v,u
are integers (which may be 0), then the above two equations become

(2.8)

\sigma Y\{f,g\}=f^{-u+1}g^{v+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial X}(g^{-v}f^{u});

(2.9)

-\rho X\{f,g\}=f^{-u+1}g^{v+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y}(g^{-v}f^{u}).

The above argument is due to Dixmier; see Lemma 1.4 of
[
D1968
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
f,g\in K[X,Y]\setminus K
, and
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. Assume that
f,g
are
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
v,u
respectively. Then
v,u
are integers and

\{f,g\}=0\ \text{if and only if}\ f^{u}=\lambda g^{v}\ \text{for some }\ \lambda\in K^{*}.

Proof.

Assume that
\{f,g\}=0
. Noticing that
f^{-u+1}g^{v+1}\neq 0
, by (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) one has

\frac{\partial}{\partial X}(g^{-v}f^{u})=0\ and\ \frac{\partial}{\partial Y}(g^{-v}f^{u})=0,

which implies
f^{u}=\lambda g^{v}
for some
\lambda\in K
. As
f\neq 0
,
\lambda\in K^{*}
.

Assume that
f^{u}=\lambda g^{v}
for some
\lambda\in K^{*}
. Then by (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) one has

\sigma Y\{f,g\}=\rho X\{f,g\}=0.

As at least one of
\sigma
and
\rho
is nonzero, one has
\{f,g\}=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
f,g\in K[X,Y]\setminus K
, and
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. Assume that
f,g
are
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
v,u
respectively, and
\{f,g\}=0
.

(1) If
\rho,\sigma
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
f,g
are monomials, and
E(f),E(g)
are on the same ray through O.

(2) If
\rho,\sigma
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then one can replace
(\rho,\sigma)
with some suitable multiple of it so that
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
and
v,u\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. One has that for some
\lambda\in K^{*}
,

(2.10)

f^{u}=\lambda g^{v},

and
f,g
are in one of the following cases:

(2.1)
v=u=0
. In this case
Convex(E(f))
and
Convex(E(g))
are on the ray through O orthogonal to
(\rho,\sigma)
;

(2.2)
v,u>0
. Assume that
d=gcd(v,u)
and
{v}_{0}=v/d,u_{0}=u/d
. Then there exists some polynomial
h\in K[X,Y]
such that
f=\gamma h^{{v}_{0}},g=\beta h^{u_{0}}
,
\gamma\beta\neq 0
. In this case
Convex(E(f))
and
Convex(E(g))
are homothetic with respect to O.

In all the 3 cases, one has that
Cone(Convex(E(f)))=Cone(Convex(E(g)))
.

Proof.

(1) Assume that
\rho,\sigma
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
f,g
are monomials. It follows from (
2.2
) that
E(f),E(g)
are on the same ray through O. It is clear that
Cone(Convex(E(f)))=Cone(Convex(E(g)))
.

(2) Assume that
\rho,\sigma
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{Q}
. After replacing
(\rho,\sigma)
with
(\tau\rho,\tau\sigma)
for some suitable
\tau\neq 0
one has
(\tau\rho,\tau\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
,
v,u\in\mathbb{Z}
and at least one of
v,u
is
\geq 0
. As
\{f,g\}=0
, (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) are just

\frac{\partial}{\partial X}(g^{-v}f^{u})=0,\ \ \frac{\partial}{\partial Y}(g^{-v}f^{u})=0,

then
g^{-v}f^{u}=\lambda\in K
. As
f,g
are both nonzero polynomials,
\lambda\neq 0
.

So

f^{u}=\lambda g^{v},\lambda\neq 0.

It is clear that if
u=0
then
v=0
, and vice versa. So there are 2 cases:

(2.1)
v=u=0
.

In this case
Convex(E(f))
and
Convex(E(g))
are both on the line through O orthogonal to
(\rho,\sigma)
. One has that
Cone(Convex(E(f)))=Cone(Convex(E(g)))
.

(2.2)
v\neq 0,u\neq 0
.

As
g^{-v}f^{u}
is a constant, if
u>0>v
then both
f
and
g
are constant polynomials, which contradicts to the assumption. Similarly the case
v>0>u
can not happen.

So one must have
v>0,u>0
. Let
d=gcd(v,u)
and
v_{0}=v/d,u_{0}=u/d
. As
K[X,Y]
is a unique factorization domain, one has

f=\gamma h_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots h_{k}^{s_{k}},g=\beta h_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots h_{k}^{t_{k}},s_{i}>0,t_{i}>0,i=1,\cdots,k;

where
h_{i}
’s are irreducible monic polynomials. By (
2.10
),

(\gamma h_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots h_{k}^{s_{k}})^{u_{0}d}=\lambda(\beta h_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots h_{k}^{t_{k}})^{v_{0}d}.

Then
h_{i}^{s_{i}u_{0}d}=h_{i}^{t_{i}v_{0}d}
and
s_{i}u_{0}=t_{i}v_{0},
for
i=1,\cdots,k
. As
gcd(v_{0},u_{0})=1
,
v_{0}
divides
s_{i}
. Let
s_{i}/v_{0}=m_{i},i=1,\cdots,k
. Then
t_{i}/u_{0}=m_{i},i=1,\cdots,k.
So

s_{i}=v_{0}m_{i},t_{i}=u_{0}m_{i};i=1,\cdots,k.

Let
h=h_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots h_{k}^{m_{k}}
.
Then
f=\gamma h^{v_{0}},g=\beta h^{u_{0}}
,
\gamma\beta\neq 0
. So
Convex(E(f))
and
Convex(E(g))
are homothetic with respect to O, thus
Cone(Convex(E(f)))=Cone(Convex(E(g)))
.

It is verified that in all the 3 cases
Cone(Convex(E(f)))=Cone(Convex(E(g)))
.
∎

For
f\in K[X,Y]
, let

C(f)=\{g\in K[X,Y]|\{f,g\}=0\}.

It is easy to verify that

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
f\in K[X,Y]\setminus K
, and that
f
is
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree

for some
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. Let

-homogeneous decomposition of

\mathbf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}(g_{i})=u_{i}

.

By Proposition

\{f,g_{i}\}\in V_{\rho,\sigma}(v+u_{i}-\rho-\sigma)

. One has
\{f,g\}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\{f,g_{i}\}
. Then
\{f,g\}=0
if and only if
\{f,g_{i}\}=0

.

For any
f\in K[X,Y]\setminus K
, there exists a unique decomposition

(2.12)

f=\lambda h^{m},

such that

(1)
\lambda\neq 0,h\in K[X,Y]

;

(2)

is not a proper power of some polynomial in

, i.e.

cannot be written as

with

.

We will refer to such decomposition (

of

Corollary 2.6
.

Assume that
f\in K[X,Y]\setminus K
, and that
f
is
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
v\neq 0
for some
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. Assume
f=\mu h^{m}
is the power decomposition of

. Then

(1)
h
is also
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous and
C(f)=K[h]
.

(2)
C(f)=K[f]
if and only if
m=1
.

Proof.

(1)
By
f=\mu h^{m}
(with
\mu\neq 0
),
h
must also be
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous.

Assume that
g\in C(f)\setminus K
is
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree

. Then as we have seen in Proposition

,

f^{u}=\lambda g^{v},for\ some\ \lambda\neq 0.

As

, one has

By the uniqueness of the power decomposition of

, one knows that

. Thus

, and

by Lemma

.

As it is clear that

, one has
C(f)=K[h]
.

(2) follows from (1).
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Assume that
f\in K[X,Y]\setminus K
, and that
f
is
(\rho,\sigma)
-homogeneous of degree
v\neq 0
for some
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. Assume that

Theorem 3.13
.

for some
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

and

Proof.

As

\rho,\sigma
are linearly dependent over

(\rho,\sigma)
by some positive multiple of itself so that

\rho,\sigma

\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}

\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbf{f}_{\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(z)=f
, and

(\rho,\sigma)

(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})

\rho+\sigma>0

\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(z)
is still
f
.

After the adjustment as above (if necessary), one can always assume that
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\rho+\sigma>0
,
\mathbf{f}_{\rho,\sigma}(z)=f
is not a nonzero multiple of some proper power of a nonconstant polynomial.

By assumption,
[z,w]=1
for some

. Set

and

Set

and

\{f,g\}=0

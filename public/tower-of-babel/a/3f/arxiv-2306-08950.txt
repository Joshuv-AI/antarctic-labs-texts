A generalization of Kac polynomials and tensor product of representations of GL n ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) \Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
Tommaso Scognamiglio

Abstract
Given a generic k k -tuple ( 𝒳 1 , … , 𝒳 k ) (\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k}) of split semisimple irreducible characters of GL n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) {\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) , Hausel, Letellier and Rodriguez-Villegas [ 12 , Theorem 1.4.1] constructed a star-shaped quiver Q = ( I , Ω ) Q=(I,\Omega) together with a dimension vector α ∈ ℕ I \alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I} and they proved that ⟨ 𝒳 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 𝒳 k , 1 ⟩ = a Q , α ( q ) \left\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\right\rangle=a_{Q,\alpha}(q) (0.0.1) where a Q , α ​ ( t ) ∈ ℤ ​ [ t ] a_{Q,\alpha}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t] is the so-called Kac polynomial , i.e. it is the counting polynomial for the number of isomorphism classes of absolutely indecomposable representations of Q Q of dimension vector α \alpha over finite fields. Moreover it was conjectured by Kac [ 16 ] and proved by Hausel-Letellier-Villegas [ 13 ] that a Q , α ​ ( t ) a_{Q,\alpha}(t) has non-negative integer coefficients. From the above formula together with Kac’s results [ 16 ] they deduced that ⟨ 𝒳 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 𝒳 k , 1 ⟩ ≠ 0 \left\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\right\rangle\neq 0 if and only if α \alpha is a root of Q Q ; moreover ⟨ 𝒳 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 𝒳 k , 1 ⟩ = 1 \left\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\right\rangle=1 exactly when α \alpha is a real root. In this paper we extend their result to any k k -tuple ( 𝒳 1 , … , 𝒳 k ) (\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k}) of split semisimple irreducible characters (which are not necessarily generic). To do that we introduce a stratification indexed by subsets V ⊂ ℕ I V\subset\mathbb{N}^{I} on the set of k k -tuples of split semisimple irreducible characters of GL n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) {\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) . The part corresponding to V = { α } V=\{\alpha\} consists of the subset of generic k k -tuples ( 𝒳 1 , … , 𝒳 k ) (\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k}) . A k k -tuple ( 𝒳 1 , … , 𝒳 k ) (\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k}) in the stratum corresponding to V ⊂ ℕ I V\subset\mathbb{N}^{I} is said to be of level V V . A representation ρ \rho of ( Q , α ) (Q,\alpha) is said to be of level at most V ⊂ ℕ I V\subset\mathbb{N}^{I} if the dimension vectors of the indecomposable components of ρ ⊗ 𝔽 q 𝔽 ¯ q \rho\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q} belong to V V . Given a k k -tuple ( 𝒳 1 , … , 𝒳 k ) (\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k}) of level V V , our main theorem is the following generalisation of Formula ( 0.0.1 ) ⟨ 𝒳 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 𝒳 k , 1 ⟩ = M Q , α , V ( q ) \left\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\right\rangle=M_{Q,\alpha,V}(q) where M Q , α , V ​ ( t ) ∈ ℤ ​ [ t ] M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t] is the counting polynomial for the number of isomorphism classes of representations of ( Q , α ) (Q,\alpha) over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} of level at most V V . Moreover we prove a formula expressing M Q , α , V ​ ( t ) M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t) in terms of Kac polynomials and so we get a formula expressing any multiplicity ⟨ 𝒳 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 𝒳 k , 1 ⟩ \left\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\right\rangle in terms of generic ones. As another consequence we prove that ⟨ 𝒳 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 𝒳 k , 1 ⟩ \left\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\right\rangle is a polynomial in q q with non-negative integer coefficients and we give a criterion for its non vanishing in terms of the root system of Q Q .



1
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to study the relationship between the computation of multiplicities for tensor product of representations of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and quiver representations.

1.1
Review on multiplicities

The character table of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is known since 1955 by the work of Green
[
10
]
, who gave a combinatorial description of it. His formulae for the values of the irreducible characters have an algorithmic nature.

Deligne and Lusztig
[
6
]
later introduced
\ell
-adic cohomological methods to the study of the representation theory of finite reductive groups. Using this approach, in
[
24
]
Lusztig found a geometric way to construct the irreducible characters of a finite reductive group. In the same book, he introduced the notion of a semisimple and unipotent irreducible character by analogy with the Jordan decomposition for the conjugacy classes.

For the finite general linear group
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, Lusztig’s costruction led to a geometric interpretation of the character table found by Green, see for example Lusztig and Srinivasan
[
27
]
.

Given
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{X}_{3}
irreducible characters of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the multiplicity
\left<\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{X}_{3}\right>
is given by the formula

\left<\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{X}_{3}\right>=\dfrac{1}{|\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|}\sum_{g\in\Gl_{n}(q)}\mathcal{X}_{1}(g)\mathcal{X}_{2}(g)\overline{\mathcal{X}_{3}(g)}

(1.1.1)

Altough the character table of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is known for a long time, it is not easy to extract general information from Formula (
1.1.1
) above, due to the inductive description of the values of the characters.

Example 1.1.1
.

Recall that the
unipotent
characters of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, which are the "building blocks" of the character table, are in bijection with the irreducible representations of
S_{n}
and so with the partitions of
n
.

For a partition
\mu
, we denote by
\chi_{\mu}
the associated character of
S_{n}
and by
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
the associated unipotent character of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
(in our paramatrization, we associate to the partition
(n)
the trivial character
1
).

From Formula (
1.1.1
), it is nearly impossible to obtain directly a combinatorial description of the set
\{(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\ |\ \langle\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{\mu},\mathcal{X}_{\nu}\rangle\neq 0\}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the set of the partitions of
n
.

Already for
S_{n}
, the problem of giving a combinatorial criterion for the non-vanishing of the
Kronecker coefficients

g_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\coloneqq\langle\chi_{\mu}\otimes\chi_{\lambda},\chi_{\nu}\rangle
, is still open and is a very active area of research.

Interestingly, the two problems were shown to be related by Letellier
[
22
]
: in particular, Letellier
[
22
, Proposition 1.2.4]
, showed that if
g_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\neq 0
then
\langle\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{\mu},\mathcal{X}_{\nu}\rangle\neq 0
too.

Recall that the multiplicity
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{X}_{3}\rangle
is equal to
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{3}^{*},1\rangle
where
\mathcal{X}_{3}^{*}
is the dual character of
\mathcal{X}_{3}
. One of the aims of this paper is to contribute to the study of the multiplicites
\left<\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\right>
for any
k
-tuple of irreducible characters
(\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k})
.

The understanding of these quantites is still an open problem in general but substantial progress were made recently.

The first cases studied in the literature concerned
k
-tuples
(\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k})
where each
\mathcal{X}_{i}
is an unipotent character. Hiss, Lübeck and Mattig
[
14
]
computed, for example, the multiplicities
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{3},1\rangle
for unipotent characters
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{X}_{3}
and
n\leq 8
using CHEVIE. They noticed that these quantities are polynomials in
q
, with positive coefficients. Lusztig studied multiplicities for unipotent character sheaves of
\PGl_{2}

[
26
]
.

The first general results were obtained in the papers
[
11
, Theorem 1.4.1]
,
[
12
, Theorem 3.2.7]
by Hausel, Letellier, Rodriguez-Villegas and were later generalised by Letellier
[
21
, Theorem 6.10.1,Theorem 7.4.1]
,
[
22
, Theorem 3.3.1, Proposition 3.4.1]
.

The authors
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
restricted themselves to a certain class of
k
-tuples
(\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k})
, called
generic
(see Definition
4.4.1
). Notice that a
k
-tuple of unipotent characters is never generic.

For generic
k
-tuples, the calculations of Formula (
1.1.1
) simplify and we can deduce a general combinatorial formula for the multiplicity
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
, involving symmetric functions (see
[
21
, Theorem 6.10.1]
).

The generic case is particularly interesting due to the surprising connection with quiver representations and Kac polynomials. In
[
12
, Corollary 3.4.2]
[
21
, Theorem 7.4.5]
, the authors found, for example, a criterion for the non-vanishing of the multiplicity
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
for generic
k
-tuples in terms of the root system of a certain star-shaped quiver.

In the same papers
[
11
, Theorem 6.2.1]
,
[
21
, Theorem 7.4.1]
, it is given also a geometric interpretation of the quantities
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
in terms of the cohomology of certain quiver varieties.

In this paper we are interested in the study of the multiplicities
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
by dropping the genericity constraint. We will mainly focus on
k
-tuples
(\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k})
where each
\mathcal{X}_{i}
is a semisimple (split) character (see
section

1.3
).

1.2
Generalities on quivers and Kac polynomials

Let
\Gamma=(J,\Omega)
be a (finite) quiver, where
J
is its set of vertices and
\Omega
its set of arrows. In
[
16
]
, for each dimension vector
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{J}
, Kac introduced a polynomial with integer coefficients
a_{\Gamma,\beta}(t)
, called
Kac polynomial
, defined by the fact that
a_{\Gamma,\beta}(q)
counts the number of isomorphism classes of
absolutely indecomposable
representations of
\Gamma
(see Definiton
2.2.4
) of dimension
\beta
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, for any
q
.

Kac showed that
a_{\Gamma,\beta}(t)
is non-zero if and only if
\beta
is a root of
Q
and conjectured that it has non-negative coefficients.

The latter conjecture was first proved by Crawley-Boevey and Van der Bergh
[
3
]
in the case of indivisible
\beta
(i.e
\gcd(\beta_{j})_{j\in J}=1
)
and later for any
\beta
by Hausel, Letellier, Rodriguez-Villegas in
[
13
]
. In both cases, the authors obtained the non-negativity property by giving a description of the coefficients in terms of the cohomology of certain quiver varieties.

For instance, if
\beta
is indivisible, in
[
3
, End of Proof 2.4]
, it is shown, that there is an equality

P_{c}(\mathcal{Q},t)=t^{d_{\mathcal{Q}}}a_{Q,\alpha}(t^{2}),

for a certain quiver variety
\mathcal{Q}
, where
P_{c}(\mathcal{Q},t)
is the compactly supported Poincaré polynomial of
\mathcal{Q}
and
d_{\mathcal{Q}}
is the dimension of
\mathcal{Q}
.

Kac polynomials enjoy other remarkable properties. For instance, they are related to the representation theory of Kac-Moody Lie algebras (see
[
16
]
) and they appear in the theory of Donaldson-Thomas invariants of the quiver
\Gamma
(see
[
30
]
,
[
18
]
).

1.3

be the Levi subgroup

In

Hausel, Letellier, Rodriguez-Villegas showed that if the

(1.3.1)

Notice that the Formula (

The aim of this paper is to generalize these results to any

The level of the partition associated to

A representation

\langle\Lambda\otimes R^{G}_{L_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes R^{G}_{L_{k}}(\gamma_{k}),1\rangle=M_{Q^{\prime},\alpha,V}(q)
where
Q^{\prime}
is the quiver obtained from
Q
by adding
g
loops at the central vertex
0
. Equation (
1.3.3
) is thus the
g=0
case of this result.

The presence of the character
\Lambda
seems not very relevant from the point of view of the representation theory of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. This generalization is however interesting from the point of view of quiver representations and for the relationship of the formula for the multiplicity
\langle\Lambda\otimes R^{G}_{L_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes R^{G}_{L_{k}}(\gamma_{k}),1\rangle
to those computing the E-polynomials of character varieties for a Riemann surface of genus
g
(see for example
[
11
, Theorem 5.2.1,Theorem 6.1.1]
)

1.4
Final questions

1.4.1
Geometric interpretation for representations of level
V

Consider a finite quiver
\Gamma=(J,\Omega)
and a dimension vector
\eta\in\mathbb{N}^{J}
. Notice that for
V=\{\eta\}
, there is an equality
M_{\Gamma,\eta,\{\eta\}}(t)=a_{\Gamma,\eta}(t)
. In this case
M_{\Gamma,\eta,V}(t)
has thus non-negative coefficients which have an interpretation in terms of the cohomology of quiver varieties.

From Formula (
1.3.2
) and the positivity of Kac polynomials, in Proposition
3.1.12
we manage to show that, for any
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I},\eta\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, the polynomial
M_{\Gamma,\eta,V}(t)
has nonnegative integer coefficients. It would be interesting to look for geometric interpretations of the coefficients of
M_{\Gamma,\eta,V}(t)
for any
V
.

It is likely that the arguments of
[
5
, Theorem C]
could be used to relate the polynomials
M_{\Gamma,\eta,V}(t)
to the DT-invariants of the Harder-Narasimhan part of the preprojective stack of
\Gamma
whose graded associated has semistable factors with dimension vector belonging to
V
.

However, it seems that there is no immediate way to relate the polynomial
M_{\Gamma,\eta,V}(t)
to the geometry of quiver varieties for a general
V
. For instance, the argument used by Crawley-Boevey and Van der Bergh
[
3
, Proposition 2.2.1]
to link absolutely indecomposable representations to the cohomology of quiver varieties cannot be extended to an arbitrary level
V
.

1.4.2
Multiplicity for a general
k
-tuple of characters

It remains an open question to obtain a full understanding of the multiplicity
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
for an arbitrary
k
-tuple
\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{k})
of not necessarily split semisimple irreducible characters.

However, we do not expect that it would be possible to obtain an immediate generalization of Formula (
1.3.3
) or a direct interpretation of the quantity
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
in terms of quiver representations.

In the generic case, Letellier
[
21
, Theorem 6.10.1]
found a formula
giving a combinatorial expression for the multiplicity
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
for any generic
k
-tuple
\mathcal{X}
. His formula has an interpretation in terms of the cohomology of a certain quiver variety (see
[
23
]
). However, Letellier’s result does not provide a straightforward way to relate the multiplicity
\langle\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{X}_{k},1\rangle
to the counting of absolutely indecomposable representations of star-shaped quivers.

Let
I

R
be the ring of power series in

-variables, with coefficients in

R\coloneqq{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[[y_{i}]]_{i\in I}

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}

the monomial
\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
and we will denote by

the ideal generated by the

The ring
R
is endowed with a natural
\lambda

\lambda

[
9

), given by the following Adam operations

\psi_{d}(f(t,y))\coloneqq f(t^{d},y^{d}).

We define the
plethystic exponential

\Plexp:R^{+}\to 1+R^{+}
by the following rule

\Plexp(f)\coloneqq\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\psi_{n}(f)}{n}\right).

(2.1.1)

Notice that for
A,B\in R^{+}
we have
\Plexp(A+B)=\Plexp(A)\Plexp(B).

Example 2.1.1
.

If
R={\mathbb{Q}}(t)[[T]]
(i.e
|I|=1
), we have:

\Plexp(T)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{T^{n}}{n}\right)=\exp\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{1-T}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{1-T}

(2.1.2)

The plethystic exponential admits an inverse operation
\Plelog:1+R^{+}\to R^{+}
known as
plethystic logarithm
. The plethystic logarithm can either be defined by the property
\Plelog\Plexp(f)=f
or by the following explicit rule. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
we put

\overline{\alpha}\coloneqq\gcd(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}

and we define
U_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
as the coefficients appearing in the expansion:

\log(f)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}\frac{U_{\alpha}}{\overline{\alpha}}y^{\alpha}.

(2.1.3)

Then put

\Plelog(f)\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}V_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}

(2.1.4)

where

V_{\alpha}\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{\overline{\alpha}}\sum_{d|\overline{\alpha}}\mu(d)U_{\frac{\alpha}{d}}(t^{d})

(2.1.5)

Notice that for
A,B\in 1+R^{+}
, the following equality holds
\Plelog(AB)=\Plelog(A)+\Plelog(B).

The plethystic exponential is well defined over the subring
R_{\mathbb{Z}}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}(t)[[y_{i}]]_{i\in I}
i.e:

Lemma 2.1.2
.

Given
f\in\mathbb{Z}(t)[[y_{i}]]^{+}_{i\in I}
we have
\Plexp(f)\in 1+\mathbb{Z}(t)[[y_{i}]]_{i\in I}^{+}

We recall also the useful lemma below (for a proof see
[
29
, Lemma 22]
). For
g\in R
let

g_{n}\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{s|n}\mu\left(\dfrac{n}{s}\right)\psi_{s}(g).

Lemma 2.1.3
.

Given
f_{1},f_{2}\in 1+R^{+}
such that

\log(f_{1})=\sum_{d\geq 1}g_{d}\log(\psi_{d}(f_{2}))

(2.1.6)

the following equality holds

\Plelog(f_{1})=g\Plelog(f_{2}).

(2.1.7)

2.2
Quiver representations

A quiver
Q
is an oriented graph
Q=(I,\Omega)
, where
I
is its set of vertices and
\Omega
is its set of arrows. We will always assume that
I,\Omega
are finite sets. For an arrow
a:i\to j
in
\Omega
we denote by
i=t(a)
its
tail
and by
j=h(a)
its
head
. Fix a field
K
. A representation
M
of
Q
over
K
is given by a (finite dimensional)
K
-vector space
V_{i}
for each vertex
i\in I
and by linear maps
M_{a}:M_{t(a)}\to M_{h(a)}
for each
a\in\Omega
.

Given two representations
M,M^{\prime}
of
Q
, a morphism
f:M\to M^{\prime}
is given by a collection of linear maps
f_{i}:M_{i}\to M^{\prime}_{i}
such that, for all
a\in\Omega
, the following equality holds:
f_{h(a)}M_{a}=M^{\prime}_{a}f_{t(a)}
.
The category of representations of
Q
over
K
is denoted by
\Rep_{K}(Q)
. For a representation
M
, the
dimension vector

\dim M\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
is the vector
\dim M\coloneqq(\dim M_{i})_{i\in I}
. It is an isomorphism invariant of the category
\Rep_{k}(Q)
.

For a representation
M
of dimension
\alpha
, up to fixing a basis of the vector spaces
M_{i}
for each
i\in I
, we can assume that
M_{i}=K^{\alpha_{i}}
. For
a\in\Omega
, the linear map
M_{a}:K^{t(a)}\to K^{h(a)}
can be therefore identified with a matrix in
\Mat(\alpha_{h(a)},\alpha_{t(a)},K)
.

Consider then the affine space

R(Q,\alpha)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{a\in\Omega}\Mat(\alpha_{h(a)},\alpha_{t(a)},K).

We can endow
R(Q,\alpha)
with the action of the group
\Gl_{\alpha}=\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}\Gl_{\alpha_{i}}
defined by

g\cdot(M_{a})_{a\in\Omega}=(g_{h(a)}M_{a}g_{t(a)}^{-1})_{a\in\Omega}.

The orbits of this action are exactly the isomorphism classes of representations of
Q
of
\dim=\alpha
.

We briefly recall also the definition of the
moment map
of
Q
. Denote by
\overline{Q}
the double quiver
\overline{Q}=(I,\overline{\Omega})
with the same vertices of
Q
and as set of arrows
\overline{\Omega}=\{a,a^{*}\ |\ a\in\Omega\}
where
a^{*}:j\to i
for
a:i\to j
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, the moment map
\mu_{\alpha}
is the morphism

\mu_{\alpha}:R(\overline{Q},\alpha)\to\End_{0}(\alpha)

(x_{a},x_{a^{*}})_{a\in\Omega}\rightarrow\sum_{a\in\Omega}[x_{a},x_{a^{*}}]

where

\End_{0}(\alpha)=\{(M_{i})\in\End(\alpha)\ |\ \sum_{i\in I}\tr(M_{i})=0\}.

Given
\lambda\in K^{I}
such that
\lambda\cdot\alpha=0
, the element
(\lambda_{i}I_{\alpha_{i}})_{i\in I}
(which we still denote by
\lambda
) is a central element of
\End_{0}(\alpha)
and the fiber
\mu_{\alpha}^{-1}(\lambda)
is
\Gl_{\alpha}
invariant. We denote by
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,\alpha}
the quiver variety associated to
\lambda
, which is defined as the GIT quotient

\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda,\alpha}\coloneqq\mu^{-1}_{\alpha}(\lambda)//\Gl_{\alpha}.

The quiver stack
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha}
is defined as the quotient stack

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha}\coloneqq[\mu^{-1}_{\alpha}(\lambda)/\Gl_{\alpha}].

2.2.1
Krull-Schmidt decomposition and endomorphism rings

The category
\Rep_{K}(Q)
is abelian and Krull-Schimdt, i.e i.e we have the following Theorem, see for example
[
17
, Theorem 1.11]
.

Theorem 2.2.1
.

Each object
M\in\Rep_{K}(Q)
admits a decomposition into a direct sum of indecomposable ones

M=\bigoplus_{j\in J}M_{j}^{n_{j}}

and such a decomposition is unique up to permuting the factors
M_{j}
.

Recall that a representation
M
is indecomposable if and only if
\End(M)
is a local algebra.

Its maximal ideal is denoted by
\Rad(M)\subseteq\End(M)
. It is the set of nilpotent endomorphisms of
M
. The quotient
\End(M)/\Rad(M)
is thus a division algebra, which is usually denoted by
\topp(M)
.

More generally, given two representations
M,N
it is possible to define a subset
\Rad(M,N)
of
\Hom(M,N)
as

\Rad(M,N)=\{g\in\Hom(M,N)\ |\ 1+gf\in\Aut(N)\ ,\forall f\in\Hom(N,M)\}.

If
M=N
and
M
is indecomposable,
\Rad(M)=\Rad(M,M)
. In general,
\Rad(N,N)
is still an ideal of
\End(N)
and if
M,N
are non-isomorphic indecomposable representations,
\Rad(M,N)=\Hom(M,N)
. The radical is additive i.e
\Rad(M\oplus M^{\prime},N)=\Rad(M,N)\oplus\Rad(M^{\prime},N).
The following proposition holds (see
[
8
, Section 3.2]
):

Proposition 2.2.2
.

Given a representation
X
of
Q
and an endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(X)
,
\varphi
is invertible if and only if its class
\overline{\varphi}
in
\End(X)/\Rad(X)
is invertible.

Remark 2.2.3
.

Fix a representation
X
and its Krull-Schmidt decomposition
X=\bigoplus_{j\in J}X_{j}^{r_{j}}
. For an endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(X)
, we denote by
\varphi_{j}
the associated element in
\End(X_{j}^{r_{j}})
and by
\overline{\varphi}_{j}
the associated element in
\End(X^{r_{j}}_{j})/\Rad(X^{r_{j}}_{j})\cong\Mat(r_{j},\topp(X_{j}))
. As
\Rad(X_{i},X_{j})=\Hom(X_{i},X_{j})
for every
i\neq j
, the following isomorphism of
K
-algebras holds:

\End(X)/\Rad(X)\cong\bigoplus_{j\in J}\Mat(r_{j},\topp(X_{j})).

(2.2.1)

\varphi\longrightarrow(\overline{\varphi}_{j})_{j\in J}

(2.2.2)

Proposition
2.2.2
can therefore be rephrased as:
\varphi
is an isomorphism if and only if
\overline{\varphi_{j}}
is invertible for each
j\in J
.

2.2.2
Indecomposable over finite fields and Kac polynomials

In this paragraph, unless explicitly specified, we assume
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The Wedderburn’s theorem implies that every finite dimensional division algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field. For an indecomposable representation
M\in\Rep_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(Q)
, we have therefore
\topp(M)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
for some

.

Fix a representation
X
with Krull-Schimdt’s decomposition
X=\bigoplus_{j\in J}X_{j}^{r_{j}}
and integers
d_{j}
such that
\topp(X_{j})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{j}}}
for each
j\in J
. From Remark
2.2.3
, there is a morphism of finite groups

p_{X}:\Aut(X)\to\prod_{j\in J}\Gl_{r_{j}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{j}}})

(2.2.3)

\varphi\longrightarrow(\overline{\varphi})_{j\in J}.

(2.2.4)

and its kernel
\Ker(p_{X})
is the subset
U_{X}\coloneqq\{1+f\ |\ f\in\Rad(X)\}\subseteq\Aut(X)
. In particular, all the elements inside
\Ker(p_{X})
are unipotent.

The integers
d_{j}
admit the following description in terms of
absolutely indecomposable
representations (see Definition
2.2.4
below).

Definition 2.2.4
.

A representation
V
over a field
K
is absolutely indecomposable if
V\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
is indecomposable.

To relate absolutely indecomposable and indecomposable representations over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we introduce the action of the Frobenius. Denote by
(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})_{\Fr^{i}}
the set
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with the structrure of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
vector space given by

\lambda\cdot v=\Fr^{i}(\lambda)v.

Consider an
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-representation
N
. We define
\Fr^{i}(N)
to be the representation
N\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})_{\Fr^{i}}.

Over the parameter space
R(Q,\alpha)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, the Frobenius action corresponds to the usual (geometric) Frobenius, i.e for
x\in R(Q,\alpha)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
the representation
\Fr^{i}(x)
is given by the element
F^{i}(x)\in R(Q,\alpha)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, where
F:R(Q,\alpha)\to R(Q,\alpha)
raises each entry of each matrix to its
q
-th power.

Fix now an indecomposable representation
M
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\topp(M)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
. In
[
16
]
it is shown that there exists an absolutely indecomposable representation
W
of
Q
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
such that
\dim(W)=\frac{\alpha}{d}
and the following isomorphism holds :

M\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d-1}\Fr^{i}(W).

(2.2.5)

By a theorem of Kac (see
[
16
, Theorem A]
), there exists a polynomial with integer coefficients
a_{Q,\alpha}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
such that, for each
q
,
a_{Q,\alpha}(q)
is equal to the number of isomorphism classes of absolutely indecomposable representations of
Q
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of dimension vector
\alpha
.

Thanks to the isomorphism (
2.2.5
), in
[
16
, Section 1.14]
it is shown that the number of isomorphism classes of indecomposable representations
M
such that
\dim M=\alpha
and
\topp(M)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
is given by

\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\dfrac{d}{r}\right)a_{Q,\frac{\alpha}{d}}(q^{r})

(2.2.6)

Moreover, Kac
[
16
, Theorem A,Theorem B]
gave the following combinatorial characterization of the dimension vectors
\alpha
such that
a_{Q,\alpha}(t)
does not vanish. Let
\Phi(Q)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{I}
be the root system associated to
Q
as defined by Kac
[
16
, Section (a)]
and let
\Phi(Q)^{+}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}\cap\Phi(Q)
the subset of the positive roots of the former root system.

Theorem 2.2.5
.

The Kac polynomial
a_{Q,\alpha}(t)\neq 0
if and only if
\alpha\in\Phi(Q)^{+}.
Moreover,
a_{Q,\alpha}(t)\equiv 1
if and only if
\alpha
is a real root (see
[
16
, Section (a)]
for a definition). Otherwise

is monic of degree
\dfrac{2-\alpha^{t}C\alpha}{2}
.

Here
C=(C_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
is the Cartan matrix of the quiver given by

C_{ij}=\begin{cases}2-2(\text{the number of edges joining $i$ to
itself})\hskip 5.69046pt\text{if }i=j\\
-(\text{the number of edges joining $i$ to
$j$})\hskip 34.14322pt\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We end by recalling the Kac conjecture which was proved by Hausel, Letellier, Rodriguez-Villegas in
[

, Corollary 1.5]

Theorem 2.2.6
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, the Kac polynomial

3.1

Let

Example 3.1.1
.

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{I}
, we denote by
V_{\lambda}
the subset
V_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{I}\ |\ \beta\cdot\lambda=0\}
, where
\cdot
is the canonical orthogonal product on
\mathbb{N}^{I}

\lambda=0
we have
V_{\lambda}=\mathbb{N}^{I}
.

To a representation
X
of dimension
\alpha

\mathcal{H}_{X}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}_{\leq\alpha}
. Given the decomposition of
X\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
into indecomposable components

X\otimes_{K}\overline{K}=\bigoplus_{j\in J}Y_{j}^{r_{j}},

we define

\mathcal{H}_{X}\coloneqq\{0<\beta\leq\alpha\ |\ \exists j\text{ s.t }\beta=\dim Y_{j}\}.

(3.1.1)

For any
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}
, we give the following definition of the representations of the quiver
Q
of level
V
.

Definition 3.1.2
.

A representation
X
of dimension
\alpha

V
if we have
\mathcal{H}_{X}=V_{\leq\alpha}
. For
V=V_{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{I}
, we say that
X
is of level
\lambda
.

Example 3.1.3
.

Let
V=\{\alpha\}
for a vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
. A representation
X\in R(Q,\alpha)
is of level
\{\alpha\}
if and only if
X\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
is indecomposable, i.e if and only if

is absolutely indecomposable.

The notion of being of level
V
induces a partition (indexed by the subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{I}_{\leq\alpha}
) on the representations of
Q
of dimension
\alpha
. As on the set of subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{I}_{\leq\alpha}

V
.

Definition 3.1.4

For a subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}

if it is of level

for some
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
. For

.

Remark 3.1.5
.

Notice that a representation
X
of dimension

is of level at most
V
if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{X}\subseteq V_{\leq\alpha}
. In particular,
for

\mathbb{N}^{I}

If
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{I}
, the Definition

for representations of level at most

is equivalent to the following one:

Lemma 3.1.6
.

A representation
X
is of level at most

if given its Krull-Schimdt decomposition
\displaystyle X=\bigoplus_{j\in J}X_{j}^{r_{j}}
we have
\dim X_{j}\cdot\lambda=0

Proof.

Given the Krull-Schidmt decomposition
\displaystyle X=\bigoplus_{j\in J}X_{j}^{r_{j}}
, for each

there is an isomorphism
X_{j}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=Y_{j}\oplus F(Y_{j})\oplus\cdots\oplus F^{d_{j}-1}(Y_{j})
where
\topp(X_{j})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{j}}}
. The decomposition in indecomposable factors of

is thus

\displaystyle X\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\bigoplus_{j\in J}(Y_{j}\oplus F(Y_{j})\oplus\cdots\oplus F^{d_{j}-1}(Y_{j}))

with
\dim Y_{j}=\frac{\dim x_{j}}{d_{j}}
. We have then
\dim Y_{j}\cdot\lambda=0
if and only if
\dim X_{j}\cdot\lambda=0
, for each
j\in J
.
∎

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, the representations of
Q
of level at most

Proposition 3.1.7
.

There exists a constructible subset
R(Q,\alpha,V)\subseteq R(Q,\alpha)
such that ,for each extension

, the set of
L

is the subset of representations of level at most
V
of
Q
over
L
. If

for

, we denote

by
R(Q,\alpha,\lambda)
.

Proof.

In
[
16
, Section 1,8]
, Kac showed that, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, there exists a
constructible subset
A(Q,\alpha)\subseteq R(Q,\alpha)
, such that, for any field extension
K\subseteq L
, the set of
L
-points
A(Q,\alpha)(L)
is the subset of absolutely indecomposable representations over
L
.

Let
\Psi_{V,\alpha}
be the constructible subset defined by

\Psi_{V,\alpha}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in V\\
\text{s.t }\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}=\alpha\end{subarray}}A(Q,\alpha_{1})\times\cdots\times A(Q,\alpha_{r})\subseteq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in V\\
\text{s.t }\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}=\alpha\end{subarray}}R(Q,\alpha_{1})\times\cdots\times R(Q,\alpha_{r}).

Consider the morphism

\Phi_{V,\alpha}:\Gl_{\alpha}\times\Psi_{V,\alpha}\to R(Q,\alpha)

defined by

\Phi_{V,\alpha}(g,M_{1},\dots,M_{r})\coloneqq g\cdot(M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{r}).

for
g\in\Gl_{\alpha}
,
M_{1}\in A(Q,\alpha_{1}),\dots,M_{r}\in A(Q,\alpha_{r})
and
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}=\alpha
. We define
R(Q,\alpha,V)
to be the image of
\Phi_{V,\alpha}
, which is a constructible subset by Chevalley’s theorem.

∎

Example 3.1.8
.

Consider the case where
Q
is the Jordan quiver (the quiver with one vertex and one arrow), i.e
|I|=|\Omega|=1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a field

, the representation space

is given by the

\Mat(n,K)
. The isomorphism classes of

correspond to the conjugacy classes of
\Mat(n,K)
.

If
\overline{K}=K
, the indecomposable representations correspond to matrices conjugated to a single Jordan block and the decomposition into indecomposable components of a representation

corresponds to the writing of
M
into its Jordan form.

Consider now the subset
V=\{1\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. Notice that in this case, a representation is of level
\{1\}
if and only if is of level at most
\{1\}
.

A matrix
M\in\Mat(n,K)
is of level
\{1\}
if and only if its Jordan form over
\overline{K}
has only blocks of size

, i.e if and only if

is diagonalizable over
\overline{K}
.

The subset
R(Q,n,\{1\})\subseteq R(Q,n)=\Mat(n,K)
is therefore given by the semisimple matrices of size
n
.

Remark 3.1.9
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of
\ch=0
and let
\lambda
be an element of

. We denote by
\pi
the projection map

From the result of Crawley-Boevey

belongs to

if and only if, given its Krull-Schimdt decomposition

Altough the result of Crawley-Boevey is stated there only for algebraically closed field of

Using an argument similar to the one of Proof of Lemma

The following Lemma provides a way to compute the number of isomorphism classes of representations of level at most
V
of dimension
\alpha
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Lemma 3.1.10
.

For each
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
there exists a polynomial
M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
such that, for any
q
,
M_{Q,\alpha,V}(q)
is equal to the number of isomorphism classes of representations of level at most
V
of dimension vector
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, the following identity holds:

\Plexp\left(\sum_{\beta\in V}a_{Q,\beta}(t)y^{\beta}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)y^{\alpha}

(3.1.2)

Proof.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
denote by
a_{Q,\beta,V}(t)
the polynomial defined by:

a_{Q,\beta,V}(t)=\begin{cases}0\ \ \text{if }\beta\not\in V\\
a_{Q,\beta}(t)\ \ \text{if }\beta\in V\end{cases}.

The proof of the Formula (
2.2.6
) given by Kac
[
16
]
can be slightly modified to show that the number of isomorphism classes of indecomposable representations
M
of level at most
V
of dimension
\beta
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\topp(M)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
is equal to

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\dfrac{d}{r}\right)a_{Q_{V},\frac{\alpha}{d}}(q^{r}).

Let then
I_{Q,\beta,V}(t)
be the polynomial defined by:

I_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)=\sum_{d|\overline{\alpha}}\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\dfrac{d}{r}\right)a_{Q,\frac{\alpha}{d},V}(t^{r})

(3.1.3)

Notice that for any
q
,
I_{Q,\beta,V}(q)
is equal to the number of isomorphism classes of indecomposable representations of level at most
V
and of dimension
\beta
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For each
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, denote by
M_{Q,\gamma,V}(t)
the polynomials defined by the following identity:

\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}M_{Q,\gamma,V}(t)y^{\gamma}=\prod_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}\exp(-\log(1-y^{\beta})I_{Q,\beta,V}(t)).

(3.1.4)

As
\Rep_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(Q)
is a Krull-Schmidt category, we deduce that, for any
q
,
M_{Q,\gamma,V}(q)
is equal to the number of isomorphism classes of representations of level at most
V
of dimension
\gamma
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Specializing the identity (
3.1.4
) at
t=q
we can rewrite it as the following identity :

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}M_{Q,\alpha,V}(q)y^{\alpha}=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in Ind(Q,\alpha,V)/\cong\\
\topp(M)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{1-y^{\frac{\alpha}{d}(d)}}=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}\prod_{d|\overline{\alpha}}\psi_{d}\left(\dfrac{1}{1-y^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\frac{d}{r}\right)a_{Q,\frac{\alpha}{d},V}(q^{r})}

(3.1.5)

The right hand side of Equation (
3.1.5
) can be rewritten as

\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}\prod_{d|\overline{\alpha}}\psi_{d}\left(\dfrac{1}{1-y^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\frac{d}{r}\right)a_{Q_{V},\frac{\alpha}{d}}(q^{r})}=\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}\prod_{d\geq 1}\psi_{d}\left(\dfrac{1}{1-y^{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\frac{d}{r}\right)a_{Q,\gamma,V}(q^{r})}

(3.1.6)

As Equation (
3.1.6
) holds for any
q
, we deduce that the following identity holds

\log\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)y^{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{I}\\
d\geq 1\end{subarray}}\psi_{d}\left(\dfrac{1}{1-y^{\gamma}}\right)(a_{Q,\gamma,V}(t))_{d}

(3.1.7)

where
\displaystyle(a_{Q,\gamma,V}(t))_{d}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{r|d}\mu\left(\frac{d}{r}\right)a_{Q,\gamma,V}(t^{r})
. By Lemma
2.1.3
and Equation (
3.1.7
), we deduce finally:

\Plelog\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)y^{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}a_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)\Plelog\left(\dfrac{1}{1-y^{\alpha}}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in V}a_{Q,\alpha}(t)y^{\alpha}.

(3.1.8)

∎

Example 3.1.11
.

Consider the Jordan quiver

of Example
3.1.8
,

From Lemma
3.1.10
, we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1.12
.

(1)
For any subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, the polynomial
M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)
has nonnegative integers coefficients.

(2)
The polynomial
M_{Q,\alpha,V}(t)
is non-zero if and only there exist

•

\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}\in(\Phi^{+}(Q)\cap V)

•

h_{1},\dots,h_{r}\in\mathbb{N}

such that
h_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+h_{r}\beta_{r}=\alpha

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1.2
the polynomials

and Lemma

\frac{a_{Q,\beta_{1}}(t^{n_{1}})^{m_{1}}}{k_{1}}\frac{a_{Q,\beta_{2}}(t^{n_{2}})^{m_{2}}}{k_{2}}\cdots\frac{a_{Q,\beta_{l}}(t^{n_{l}})^{m_{l}}}{k_{l}}

(3.1.10)

with

) is different from

.

Let
K=\mathbb{C}
and fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{I}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
and consider the associated quiver stack
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha}
. For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha})\coloneqq H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha},\mathbb{C})
be the compactly supported cohomology of the quotient stack
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}.
We denote by
P_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha},t)
the compactly supported Poincaré series

\displaystyle P_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha},t)\coloneqq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha})t^{i}.

Davison
[
4
, Theorem B]
showed that the following equality holds:

P_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha},t)=t^{-2(\alpha,\alpha)}\Coeff_{\alpha}\left(\Plexp\left(\sum_{\beta\cdot\lambda=0}\dfrac{t^{2}}{t^{2}-1}a_{Q,\beta}(t^{2})y^{\beta}\right)\right)

(3.1.11)

where
(\alpha,\alpha)
is the Euler form of
Q
. From Identity (
3.1.11
) and Proposition
3.1.10
in the case where
V=V_{\lambda}
, we deduce the following Proposition.

Proposition 3.1.13
.

•

The quiver stack
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\alpha}
(and so the quiver variety
Q_{\lambda,\alpha}
) is non empty if and only
M_{Q,\alpha,\lambda}(t)\neq 0
.

The number of irreducible components of top dimension of the stack

.

3.2
Counting representations of level at most
V

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, we denote by
\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
the finite group
\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\coloneqq\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}\Gl_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In the paper
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
, Letellier linked the Kac polynomial
a_{Q,\alpha}(t)
to the representation theory of the finite group
\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In this paragraph, we will explain how to generalize his results to the case of representations of level at most
V
for certain subsets
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}
.

The finite group
\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts on the finite set
R(Q,\alpha)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. We denote the associated complex character of
\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
by
R_{\alpha}
. Let
\det_{I}
be the morphism
\det_{I}:\Gl_{\alpha}(q)\to(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{I}
which maps an element
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
to the element
(\det(g_{i}))_{i\in I}
.

Given
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i\in I}\in\Hom(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\mathbb{C}^{*})^{I}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, we denote by
\sigma^{\delta}
the element of
\Hom(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\mathbb{C}^{*})
defined by

\sigma^{\delta}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}^{\delta_{i}}.

Given
\sigma\in\Hom(\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q},\mathbb{C}^{*})^{I}
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
the subset of
\mathbb{N}^{I}
defined by
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}\coloneqq\{\delta\in\mathbb{N}^{I}\ |\ \sigma^{\delta}=1\}
and by
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,\alpha}
the intersection
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,\alpha}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}\cap\mathbb{N}^{I}_{\leq\alpha}
. Finally, we denote by
\rho
the character of
\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
defined by

\rho((g_{i})_{i\in I})\coloneqq\prod_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}(\det(g_{i})).

Remark 3.2.1
.

Fix a dimension vector
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, an integer
r\in\mathbb{N}
, an indecomposable representation
M\in R(Q,\beta)(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and denote by
N
the representation
N=M^{\oplus r}
. As seen at the beginning of paragraph
section

2.2.2
, there is an isomorphism
\Aut(N)/U_{N}\cong\Gl_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}})
where
\topp(M)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
. As
U_{N}
is a unipotent subgroup, the morphism
\det_{I}
passes to the quotient
\Aut(N)/U_{N}
and induces thus a morphism
\det_{I}:\Gl_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}})\to(\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q})^{I}
. Its value at a matrix
A\in\Gl_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}})
is given by

\det{}_{I}(A)=(N_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\det(A)^{\frac{\beta_{i}}{d}}))_{i\in I}

(3.2.1)

The main result of this paragraph is the following Theorem:

Theorem 3.2.2
.

For a subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{I}
and a dimension vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, if there exists an element
\sigma\in\Hom(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\mathbb{C}^{*})^{I}
such that
V_{\leq\alpha}=\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,\alpha}
, the following equality holds:

\langle R_{\alpha}\otimes\rho,1\rangle=M_{Q,\alpha,V}(q)

(3.2.2)

Proof.

Fix a representation
x
inside
R(Q,\alpha)
: we start by showing that
\rho|_{\Stab(x)}\cong 1
if and only if
x\in R(Q,\alpha,V)
. Consider the Krull-Schmidt decomposition

x=\bigoplus_{j\in J}x_{j}^{r_{j}}.

Let
\beta_{j}
be the dimension vector
\dim x_{j}
and
d_{j}
the integer such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{j}}}=\topp(x_{j})
for
j\in J
.

As explained at the beginning of paragraph
section

2.2.2
, quotienting by the subgroup
U_{x}\subseteq\Stab(x)
there is an isomorphism
\Stab(x)/U_{x}\cong\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}\Gl_{r_{j}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{j}}})
. The character
\rho
is trivial over
U_{x}
and induces therefore a character
\rho:\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}\Gl_{r_{j}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{j}}})\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
which by Remark
3.2.1
is given by

\rho((A_{j}))_{j\in J}=\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}\sigma^{\frac{\beta_{j}}{d_{j}}}(N_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{j}}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(A_{j})).

Therefore, we deduce that
\rho|_{\Stab(x)}\equiv 1
if and only if
\dfrac{\beta_{j}}{d_{j}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,\alpha}=V_{\leq\alpha}
for each
j\in J
. This is exactly the condition that must hold for
x
to be of level at most
V
.

From the discussion above, we deduce therefore that it holds:

\displaystyle\langle R_{\alpha}\otimes\rho,1\rangle=\dfrac{1}{|\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|}\sum_{x\in R(Q,\alpha)(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\sum_{g\in\Stab(x)}\rho(g)=\dfrac{1}{|\Gl_{\alpha}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|}\sum_{x\in R(Q,\alpha,V)(\mathbb{F}_{q})}|\Stab(x)|.

(3.2.3)

Applying the Burnside formula to the RHS of eq.(
3.2.3
) we obtain thus the equality

\langle R_{\alpha}\otimes\rho,1\rangle=M_{Q,\alpha,V}(q).

∎

4
Tensor product of characters of finite general linear group

In this section, we recall how to construct the semisimple split characters of
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
via Harisha-Chandra induction. We begin by fixing some notations and recalling some generalities about
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

4.1
Notations

Given two
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
varieties
X,Y
endowed with Frobenius morphisms
F_{X}:X\to X
,
F_{Y}:Y\to Y
respectively, we write
f:(X,F_{X})\to(Y,F_{Y})
for a morphism
f:X\to Y
commuting with the Frobenius maps.

For any
n
, we denote by
\Gl_{n}
the general linear group over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The group
\Gl_{n}
is endowed with the standard Frobenius morphism
F:\Gl_{n}\to\Gl_{n}
where
F((a_{i,j}))=(a_{i,j}^{q})
. The finite general linear group
\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is thus the finite subgroup
\Gl_{n}^{F}\subseteq\Gl_{n}
.

For a
1
-parameter subgroup
\eta:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\Gl_{n}
the
weight

|\eta|
is defined as the integer
|\eta|\in\mathbb{Z}
such that we have

\displaystyle\det(\eta):\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}

(4.1.1)

\displaystyle z\to z^{|\eta|}

(4.1.2)

For any integers
n,d\geq 1
, we denote by
F_{d}:\Gl_{n}^{d}\to\Gl_{n}^{d}
the (twisted) Frobenius

F_{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=(F(x_{d}),F(x_{1}),\dots,F(x_{d-1})).

Notice that

(\Gl_{n}^{d})^{F_{d}}=\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}).

We define more generally the
weight
for an homomorphism
\eta:(\mathbb{G}^{d}_{m},F_{d})\to(\Gl_{n},F)
as the integer given by

|\eta|\coloneqq\dfrac{|\eta(\Delta)|}{d}

(4.1.3)

where
\Delta:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{d}
is the diagonal embedding. The weight
|\eta|
is an integer as
\eta
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

4.2
F
-stable Levi subgroups

For any
F
-stable Levi subgroup
M\subseteq\Gl_{n}
, there exist integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{r},m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
such that
m_{1}d_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}d_{r}=n
and such that

(M,F)\cong((\Gl_{m_{1}})^{d_{1}}\times\dots\times(\Gl_{m_{r}})^{d_{r}},F_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times F_{d_{r}}).

(4.2.1)

We say that
M
is
split
if
d_{1}=\cdots=d_{r}=1
.

The isomorphism (
4.2.1
) implies that there is an isomorphism

M^{F}=M(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cong\displaystyle\prod_{i}\Gl_{m_{i}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}})

and an isomorphism

(Z_{M},F)\cong((\mathbb{G}_{m})^{d_{1}}\times\dots\times(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{d_{r}},F_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times F_{d_{r}}).

(4.2.2)

Notice that, for each
j=1,\dots,r
, the integer
m_{j}
is equal to the weight
|i_{j}|
of the embedding
i_{j}:(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d_{j}},F_{d_{j}})\hookrightarrow(\Gl_{n},F)
induced by the isomorphism of eq.(
4.2.2
).

4.3
Harisha-Chandra induction for split Levi subgroups

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
G=\Gl_{n}
. Let
L\subseteq G
be the split Levi subgroup
\Gl_{n_{0}}\times\cdots\times\Gl_{n_{s}}
embedded diagonally into
\Gl_{n}
i.e

L=\begin{pmatrix}\Gl_{n_{s}}&0&0&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&\Gl_{n_{s-1}}&0&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&\Gl_{n_{s-2}}&0&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\dots&\dots&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&0&\Gl_{n_{0}}\end{pmatrix}.

The finite group
L^{F}
is therefore isomorphic to

L^{F}=\Gl_{n_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\cdots\times\Gl_{n_{s}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Let
P
be the
F
-stable parabolic subgroup containing
L
and the upper triangular matrices.

Recall that the quotient
G/P
is identified with the variety of flags inside
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
:

G/P=\{F_{s}\subseteq F_{s-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{0}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\ :\ \dim(F_{i})=\sum_{j=i}^{s}n_{j}\}.

(4.3.1)

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points
(G/P)^{F}
are thus identied with flags of vector subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

(G/P)^{F}=G^{F}/P^{F}=\{F_{s}\subseteq F_{s-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\ :\ \dim(F_{i})=\sum_{j=i}^{s}n_{j}\}.

(4.3.2)

Denote by
\pi_{L}
the quotient map
\pi_{L}:P\to L
. Consider now a linear character
\gamma:L^{F}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
, given by

\displaystyle\gamma((M_{0},\dots,M_{s}))=\prod_{j=0}^{s}\gamma_{j}(\det(M_{j})),

where
M_{j}\in\Gl_{n_{j}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for
j=0,\dots,s
and the
\gamma_{j}
’s are homomorphisms
\gamma_{j}:\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
. We will usually denote by
\gamma
also the
s+1
-uple
\gamma=(\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{s})\in\Hom(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\mathbb{C}^{*})^{s+1}
. We define the associated character
R^{G}_{L}(\gamma)
of
G^{F}
by the following rule:

R^{G}_{L}(\gamma)(g)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in G^{F}/P^{F}\\
h^{-1}gh\in P^{F}\end{subarray}}\gamma(\pi_{L}(h^{-1}gh)).

(4.3.3)

The character
R^{G}_{L}(\gamma)
is the character of the induced representation
\Ind_{P^{F}}^{G^{F}}(\gamma(\pi_{L}))
. Notice that, for
h\in G^{F}/P^{F}
and

is equivalent to having that

on

. Via the identification (

-stable flags.

We will call the characters of

of the form

(not necessarily irreducible) for some

the Harisha-Chandra characters. If

is irreducible, we will call it a semisimple split irreducible character.

The character

is irreducible if and only if

for every

(see

4.4
Generic

-tuples of Harisha-Chandra characters

Hausel, Letellier, Rodriguez-Villegas

Definition 4.4.1

We say that the a

-stable Levi subgroup

and

]

to a general

-tuple of Harisha-Chandra characters.

Example 4.4.2
.

Consider a

-tuple of irreducible characters

has order

.

For each

There is a free action of

on

On the other side, the matrix

\gamma(h^{-1}gh)=\prod_{j=0}^{l}\gamma_{j}(\det(g^{\prime}_{j}))=

4.5.2
Non-vanishing of multiplicities

From Proposition
3.1.12
, we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5.11
.

(1) For a
k
-tuple of Harisha-Chandra characters
\mathcal{X}=(R^{G}_{L_{1}}(\gamma_{1}),\dots,R^{G}_{L_{k}}(\gamma_{k}))
, the multiplicity
\langle\Lambda\otimes R^{G}_{L_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes R^{G}_{L_{k}}(\gamma_{k}),1\rangle
is the evaluation at
q
of a polynomial with non-negative coefficients.

(2) Given
Q,\alpha
as above the multiplicity
\left<\Lambda\otimes R^{G}_{L_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes R^{G}_{L_{k}}(\gamma_{k}),1\right>
is non-zero if and only there exist

•

\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}\in(\Phi^{+}(Q)\cap V)

•

m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\in\mathbb{N}

such that
m_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}\beta_{r}=\alpha

Notice that this implies that if
\Phi^{+}(Q)\cap V=\varnothing
the multiplicity is
0
. Similarly, if
\alpha\not\in V
we have that the multiplicity is
0
. Indeed, as
V_{\leq\alpha}=\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\sigma_{\mathcal{X}},\alpha}
, if
\beta_{i}\in V
with
\beta_{i}\leq\alpha
and
m_{i}\beta_{i}\leq\alpha
too, we have
m_{i}\beta_{i}\in V
and
\alpha=m_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}\beta_{r}\in V
.

Remark 4.5.12
.

Identity (
4.5.7
) implies that the multiplicity
\displaystyle\left<\Lambda\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}R^{G}_{L_{i}}(\gamma_{i}),1\right>
does not depend on the characters
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
but only on the Levi subgroups
L_{1},\dots,L_{k}
and the subset
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\sigma_{\mathcal{X}},\alpha}
.

As
a_{Q,\beta}(t)\neq 0
if and only if
\beta\in\Phi^{+}(Q)
, we deduce more precisely that the multiplicity depends only on the intersection of
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\sigma_{\mathcal{X}},\alpha}
and
\Phi^{+}(Q)
.

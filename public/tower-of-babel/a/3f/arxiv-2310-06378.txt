A

[

]
, quantum secret sharing
[

, and holographic quantum

[

]
.
There exists a trivial upper bound on the parameter
k

k
-uniform states in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}

k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor

[

]
.
Specially, a

-uniform state is also called an absolutely maximally

]

-,

-,

,

II
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the concepts of
k
-uniform states and enumerators, and some related results.

II-A

k
-uniform states

For any positive integer
d\geq 2
, we denote
\mathbb{Z}_{d}:=\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}:=\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}
. An
N
-partite pure quantum state
|\psi\rangle
with local dimension
d
refers to a normalized vector in the Hilbert space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}:=\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
, which can be written as
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{{\bf u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}}a_{{\bf u}}|{\bf u}\rangle
, where
a_{{\bf u}}\in\mathbb{C}
. The density matrix of
|\psi\rangle
is defined by
\rho:=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|=\sum_{{\bf u},{\bf u}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}}a_{{\bf u}}\overline{a_{{\bf u}^{\prime}}}|{\bf u}\rangle\langle{\bf u}^{\prime}|
.
For any subset
A
of
[N]:=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, let
A^{c}
be the complement of
A
. For a vector
{\bf u}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
, let
{\bf u}_{A}
be the projection of
{\bf u}
on
A
. The reduced density matrix of
|\psi\rangle
to
A
is defined by
\rho_{A}:=\sum_{{\bf u},{\bf u}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}}a_{{\bf u}}\overline{a_{{\bf u}^{\prime}}}\langle{\bf u}_{A^{c}}|{\bf u}^{\prime}_{A^{c}}\rangle|{\bf u}_{A}\rangle\!\langle{\bf u}^{\prime}_{A}|
, where
\langle{\bf u}_{A^{c}}|{\bf u}^{\prime}_{A^{c}}\rangle=1
if
{\bf u}_{A^{c}}={\bf u}^{\prime}_{A^{c}}
and
0
otherwise.

Definition 1

A pure state
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{{\bf u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}}a_{{\bf u}}|{\bf u}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
is called a
k
-uniform state
if the reduction to any
k
-parties is maximally mixed, i.e. for any
k
-subset
A
of
[N]
,
\rho_{A}=\frac{1}{d^{k}}\sum_{{\bf u}_{A}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{k}}|{\bf u}_{A}\rangle\!\langle{\bf u}_{A}|
. Specially, a
\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
-uniform state in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
is called an
absolutely maximally entangled (AME) state
.

For example, the Bell state

|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}

is a
1
-uniform state (AME state). This is because
\rho_{\{1\}}=\rho_{\{2\}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}}|i\rangle\!\langle i|
.
A
k
-uniform state in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
corresponds to a pure
(N,1,k+1)_{d}
QECC
[
8
]
. According to the Schmidt decomposition of bipartite pure states
[
31
]
or quantum Singletion bound for QECCs
[
32
]
, there exists a trivial upper bound on the parameter
k
for the existence of
k
-uniform states in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
, i.e.

k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor.

When
d=2
, Rains
[
20
]
improved this trivial upper bound.

Theorem 2
(Rains’ bound
[
20
]
)

For a
k
-uniform state in
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes(6m+\ell)}
, where
m\geq 0
and
0\leq\ell\leq 5
, it satisfies

k\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}2m+1,&&\text{if $\ell<5$};\\
2m+2,&&\text{if $\ell=5$}.\end{array}\right.

For general
d
, Scott
[
8
]
improved the trivial upper bound as below.

Theorem 3
(Scott’s bound
[
8
]
)

For a
k
-uniform state in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
, if

N>\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}2(d^{2}-1),&&\text{if $N$ is even};\\
2d(d+1)-1,&&\text{if $N$ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

(1)

then
k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-1
.

Scott’s bound means that AME states do not exist in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
when Eq. (
1
) holds.
For
d=3,4,5
, Huber
et al.
gave the non-existence of more AME states
[
24
]
, i.e.
k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-1
, when
d=3
and
N=8,12,13,14,16,17,19,21,23
;
d=4
and
N=12,16,20,24,25,26,28,29,30,33,37,39
;
d=5
and
N=28,32,36,40,44,48
.
Note that when
d=2
, Scott’s bound is weaker than Rains’ bound. According to Scott’s bound, a
k
-uniform state in
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}
must satisfy
k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-1
for
N\geq 8
and
N\neq 9,11
, while
k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-1
for
N=4
and
N\geq 8
by Rains’ bound.
Moreover, Rains’ bound not only shows the non-existence of some AME states, but also shows the non-existence of some
k
-uniform states for
k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor-1
. For example, Rains’ bound shows that
4
-uniform states in
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes 10}
do not exist.

II-B

Enumerators

Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d^{2}}}

(e_{0}=I)
be an orthonormal basis acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, such that
\mathop{\rm Tr}(e_{i}^{{\dagger}}e_{j})=\delta_{ij}d
. If
j\neq 0
,
\mathop{\rm Tr}(e_{j})=\mathop{\rm Tr}(e_{0}^{{\dagger}}e_{j})=\delta_{0j}d=0
. This basis is also called a
nice error basis

[
33
]
. When
d=2
,
\{e_{j}\}_{j=0}^{3}
can be built from the Pauli operators; when
d\geq 3
,
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d^{2}}}
can be built from the generalized Pauli operators
[
34
]
. For the Hilbert space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
, a local error basis
\mathcal{E}=\{E_{\alpha}\}
consists of

E_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes e_{\alpha_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{\alpha_{N}},

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{d^{2}}^{N}
, each
e_{\alpha_{i}}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, and
\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{{\dagger}}E_{\beta})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}d^{n}
. The support of a local error operator
E_{\alpha}
is defined as

\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha}):=\{i\mid\alpha_{i}\neq 0,\ 1\leq i\leq N\}
, and the weight of
E_{\alpha}
is define as
\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha}):=|\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})|
. For a subset
A\subset[N]
, we denote
E_{\alpha}^{A}=\otimes_{i\in A}e_{\alpha_{i}}
. Specially, let
E_{\alpha}^{\mathop{\rm supp}}:=E_{\alpha}^{\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})}=\otimes_{i\in\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})}e_{\alpha_{i}}
.

For a pure state
|\psi\rangle
in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
, let
\rho=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
be the density matrix of
|\psi\rangle
. The Shor-Laflamme enumerator
[
35
]
is

a_{j}=\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})=j,E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}}\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}\rho)\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho).

The corresponding enumerator polynomial is

A(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}x^{N-j}y^{j}.

(2)

Moreover, the quantum MacWilliams identity
[
35
,
36
,
24
]
is

A(x,y)=A\left(\frac{x+(d^{2}-1)y}{d},\frac{x-y}{d}\right).

(3)

Note that

a_{j}=\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})=j,E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}}\langle\psi|E_{\alpha}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|E_{\alpha}^{\dagger}|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})=j,E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}}|\langle\psi|E_{\alpha}|\psi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0,

where
0\leq j\leq N
. Specially,
a_{0}=|\langle\psi|\psi\rangle|^{2}=1
.

Next, we introduce the shadow enumerator.
The shadow inequality
[
37
]
is as follows. For any fixed subset
T\subset[N]
,

\sum_{S\subset[N]}(-1)^{|S\cap T|}\mathop{\rm Tr}(\rho_{S}^{2})\geq 0.

The shadow enumerator
[
20
,
24
]
is

s_{j}=\sum_{|T|=j}\sum_{S\subset[N]}(-1)^{|S\cap T^{c}|}\mathop{\rm Tr}(\rho_{S}^{2}),

(4)

and the corresponding enumerator polynomial is

S(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}s_{j}x^{N-j}y^{j},

(5)

where
s_{j}\geq 0
for
0\leq j\leq N
.
The shadow enumerator polynomial and the Shor–Laflamme enumerator polynomial have the following relation
[
20
,
24
]
,

S(x,y)=A\left(\frac{(d-1)x+(d+1)y}{d},\frac{y-x}{d}\right).

Moreover,

\displaystyle S(x,y)

\displaystyle=A\left(\frac{(d-1)x+(d+1)y}{d},\frac{y-x}{d}\right)

\displaystyle=A\left(\frac{\frac{(d-1)x+(d+1)y}{d}+(d^{2}-1)\frac{y-x}{d}}{d},\frac{\frac{(d-1)x+(d+1)y}{d}-\frac{y-x}{d}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle=A\left(\frac{-(d-1)x+(d+1)y}{d},\frac{y+x}{d}\right)

\displaystyle=S(-x,y),

where the second equality is from Eq. (
3
). Then
S(x,y)=S(-x,y)
implies that
s_{N-j}=0
when
j
is odd.

Above all, for a pure state
|\psi\rangle
in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
, the following
constraints must hold
[
35
,
36
,
20
,
37
,
24
]
:

\displaystyle A(x,y)=A\left(\frac{x+(d^{2}-1)y}{d},\frac{x-y}{d}\right);

(6)

\displaystyle a_{0}=1;

(7)

\displaystyle a_{j}\geq 0,\quad\text{for all $1\leq j\leq N$};

(8)

\displaystyle S(x,y)=A\left(\frac{(d-1)x+(d+1)y}{d},\frac{y-x}{d}\right);

(9)

\displaystyle s_{j}\geq 0,\quad\text{for all $0\leq j\leq N$};

(10)

\displaystyle s_{N-j}=0,\quad\text{when $j$ is odd, for all $0\leq j\leq N$.}

(11)

Specially, if
|\psi\rangle
is a
k
-uniform state in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
,
then there is another constraint
[
35
,
36
,
24
]
:

a_{j}=0,\quad\text{for all $1\leq j\leq k$.}

(12)

This is because
a_{j}=\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})=j,E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}}\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}\rho)\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho)=\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})=j,E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}}|\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\mathop{\rm supp}}\rho_{\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})})|^{2}=\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})=j,E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}}

|\frac{1}{d^{j}}\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\mathop{\rm supp}})|^{2}=0
for

IV
non-existence of AME states in heterogeneous systems

In this section, we generalize Scott’s bound to heterogeneous systems whose local dimensions are not all equal, and give some non-existence results of AME states in heterogeneous systems. We also compare the results obtained by the generalized Scott’s bound and shadow inequality.

Note that there are two definitions of AME states in heterogeneous systems
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
. The first one requires the reduction to any
\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor
parties is maximally mixed
[
25
]
, while the second one requires every subsystem whose dimension is not larger than
that of its complement must be maximally mixed
[
24
]
. We only focus on the first one in this paper.
According to the Schmidt decomposition of bipartite pure states
[
31
]
, an AME state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
must satisfy:
for any
\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor
-subset
A
of
[N]
,
\prod_{i\in A}d_{i}\leq\prod_{i\in A^{c}}d_{i}
.
Specially, if
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{N}
are not all equal, then
N
must be odd.
Next, we generalize Scott’s bound to heterogeneous systems.

Theorem 10
(Generalized Scott’s bound)

AME states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
do not exist if there exists a
(\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2)
-subset
A
of
[N]
, such that

\frac{\prod_{i\in A}d_{i}^{2}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}\left(1-\sum_{i\in A}\frac{1}{d_{i}^{2}}\right)+\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1<0.

(19)

Proof:

The proof is motivated by the new proof of Scott’s bound in the Supplemental material of
[
21
]
. For completeness, we present every detail here. Firstly, we choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d_{i}^{2}}}
(
e_{0}=\mathbb{I}
) of Hermitian operators (e.g. the generalized Gell-Mann matrix basis
[
34
]
) acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq N
, which satisfies
\mathop{\rm Tr}(e_{j}e_{j^{\prime}})=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}d_{i}
, and
\mathop{\rm Tr}(e_{j})=0
for
j\neq 0
.
For the Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
, a local error basis
{\cal E}=\{E_{\alpha}\}
consists of
E_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes e_{\alpha_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{\alpha_{N}}
, where

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{d_{1}^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{2}^{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{N}^{2}}
,
each
e_{\alpha_{i}}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{i}}
,
\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}E_{\beta})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{n}
, and
\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha})=0
for
1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq N
.
For an AME state
|\psi\rangle
in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
, let
\rho=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
, then
\rho
has the Bloch representation
[
40
]
,

\rho=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}\sum_{E_{\alpha}\in{\cal E}}r_{\alpha}E_{\alpha},

where
r_{\alpha}=\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}\rho)\in\mathbb{R}
. When
1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor
,
\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}\rho)=\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\mathop{\rm supp}}\rho_{\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})})=\frac{1}{\prod_{i\in\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})}d_{i}}\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{\mathop{\rm supp}})=0
since the reduction to any
\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor
parties is maximally mixed. Then

\rho=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}\left(\mathbb{I}+\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\geq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1}r_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}\right).

Let
A=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
be a
k
-subset of
[N]
with
k\geq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1
, then

\rho_{A}=\frac{1}{d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}}}\left(\mathbb{I}+\sum_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq k,\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})\subset A}r_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}^{A}\right).

(20)

Note that Eq. (
20
) is true even if we only require the reduction to any
\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor
-subset of
A
is maximally mixed.
Since
\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{\alpha}^{A}E_{\beta}^{A})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}d_{i_{1}}d_{d_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}}
for any
\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha}),\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\beta})\subset A
, we obtain

\mathop{\rm Tr}(\rho_{A}^{2})=\frac{1}{d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}}}\left(1+\sum_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq k,\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})\subset A}r_{\alpha}^{2}\right).

Moreover,
\rho_{A}
also has the property
[
21
]
:

\rho_{A}^{2}=\frac{d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}\rho_{A},

(21)

which is obtained by the property that
\rho_{A^{c}}
is maximally mixed.
By Eq. (
21
),

\mathop{\rm Tr}(\rho_{A}^{2})=\frac{d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}
. Then we have

\mathop{\rm Tr}(\rho_{A}^{2})=\frac{1}{d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}}}\left(1+\sum_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq k,\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})\subset A}r_{\alpha}^{2}\right)=\frac{d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}.

Hence,

\sum_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq k,\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})\subset A}r_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{(d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{k}})^{2}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}-1,

(22)

for any
k
-subset
A=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
of
[N]
with
k\geq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1
.

Now let
k=\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2
(i.e.
A=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2}\}
) and
B_{j}=A\setminus\{i_{j}\}
for
1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})=A}r_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2,\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})\subset A}r_{\alpha}^{2}-\sum_{\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})=\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1,\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})\subset A}r_{\alpha}^{2}

(23)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq\mathop{\rm wt}(E_{\alpha})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2,\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})\subset A}r_{\alpha}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2}\ \sum_{\mathop{\rm supp}(E_{\alpha})=B_{j}}r_{\alpha}^{2}\geq 0.

By Eq. (
22
) and Eq. (
23
),
we obtain

\left(\frac{\left(d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2}}\right)^{2}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}-1\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2}\left(\frac{\left(d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}}\cdots d_{i_{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+2}}\right)^{2}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}d_{j}^{2}}-1\right)\geq 0,

(24)

i.e.

\frac{\prod_{i\in A}d_{i}^{2}}{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}}\left(1-\sum_{i\in A}\frac{1}{d_{i}^{2}}\right)+\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor+1\geq 0.

(25)

This completes the proof.

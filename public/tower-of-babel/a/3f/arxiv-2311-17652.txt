Potentials and fields of a charge set suddenly from rest into uniform motion
V Hnizdo, G Vaman

Abstract
The fact that electromagnetic effects propagate at the speed of light suggests how the Lorenz-gauge scalar and vector potentials of a uniformly moving point charge must be modified when the charge was initially at rest and then set suddenly into uniform motion. The modified potentials are shown to satisfy the requisite inhomogeneous wave equations. The gauge function of the transformation of these potentials to the Coulomb gauge is calculated in closed form. It is validated by confirming that the Coulomb-gauge vector potential that is calculated using it yields together with the Coulomb-gauge scalar potential the same electric and magnetic fields as those calculated with the Lorenz-gauge potentials.



and

\displaystyle=A_{{\rm L}x}({\bi r},t)+\frac{c}{v}\,[\Phi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)-\Phi_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)],

(3)

\displaystyle A_{{\rm C}y}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{c}{v}\,\frac{y(x-vt)}{y^{2}+z^{2}}[\Phi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)-\Phi_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)],

\displaystyle A_{{\rm C}z}({\bi r},t)

(5)

2
Lorenz-gauge potentials

The charge and current densities of a point charge
q
that had been at rest at the origin
{\bi r}=0
until time
t=0
and then it started suddenly to move with a constant velocity
v
along the
x
-axis are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}({\bi r},t)=q\,\delta[x-vt\Theta(t)]\,\delta(y)\,\delta(z),\quad\tilde{\bi J}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=vq\,\delta(x-vt)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(t)\hat{\bi x}.

(7)

Here,
\Theta(\cdot)
is the Heaviside step function, which we shall treat, on a par with the Dirac delta
function, as a generalized function; thus
\Theta(0)
is undefined and
\rmd\Theta(x)/\rmd x=\delta(x)
.
Despite the unphysical jump of the charge’s velocity from zero to a nonzero value, the continuity equation is satisfied. Writing the charge density of equation (
7
) as

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}({\bi r},t)=q\delta(x-vt)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(t)+q\delta(x)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(-t)

(8)

we have, indeed, using the identity
\delta(x-vt)\delta(t)=\delta(x)\delta(t)
and the expression of equation (
7
) for
\tilde{\bi J}
,

\displaystyle\partial\tilde{\rho}({\bi r},t)/\partial t

\displaystyle=-v\delta^{\prime}(x-vt)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(t)+\delta(x-vt)\delta(y)\delta(z)\delta(t)-\delta(x)\delta(y)\delta(z)\delta(t)

\displaystyle=-v\delta^{\prime}(x-vt)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(t)

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bi J}({\bi r},t).

(9)

The Lorenz-gauge potentials, as the electromagnetic field itself, are governed by wave equations according to which electromagnetic effects propagate at the speed of light. Following Purcell
[
4
]
, we use this fact to modify the Lorenz-gauge potentials (
2
) of a uniformly moving charge to those generated by the charge and current densities (
7
), as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)=\Phi_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)\,\Theta(ct-r)+(q/r)\,\Theta(r-ct),\;\;\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)={\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)\,\Theta(ct-r).

(10)

It can be shown that the potentials (
10
) satisfy the Lorenz-gauge condition

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bf A}_{\rm L}+\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}/c\partial t=0.

(11)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bf A}_{\rm L}

\displaystyle=\beta\frac{\partial\Phi_{\rm L}}{\partial x}\,\Theta(ct-r)-\beta\frac{x}{r}\Phi_{\rm L}\,\delta(ct-r),

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}}{c\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\beta\frac{\partial\Phi_{\rm L}}{\partial x}\,\Theta(ct-r)+(\Phi_{\rm L}-q/r)\,\delta(r-ct),

(13)

where here and henceforth
\beta=v/c
, and so

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bf A}_{\rm L}+\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}/c\partial t

\displaystyle=\left[(1-\beta x/r)\Phi_{\rm L}-q/r\right]\delta(ct-r)

\displaystyle=\left[(1-\beta x/r)\Phi_{\rm L}|_{t=r/c}-q/r\right]\delta(ct-r)=0.

(14)

The last line here obtains because

\displaystyle(1-\beta x/r)\Phi_{\rm L}|_{t=r/c}-q/r

\displaystyle=q(1-\beta x/r)/\sqrt{(x-\beta r)^{2}+(r^{2}-x^{2})(1-\beta^{2})}-q/r

\displaystyle=q(1-\beta x/r)/|r-\beta x|-q/r=0.

(15)

If the potentials (
10
) satisfy the requisite inhomogeneous equations,

\displaystyle\Box\,\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)=-4\pi\tilde{\rho}({\bi r},t),\quad\Box\,\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)=-(4\pi/c)\tilde{\bi J}({\bi r},t),

(16)

where
\Box=\nabla^{2}-\partial^{2}/c^{2}\partial t^{2}
is the d’Alembertian operator, then these potentials
are indeed the Lorenz-gauge potentials of a point charge set suddenly from rest into uniform motion.
The calculation of
\Box\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}
is facilitated by the use of the identity

\displaystyle\Box[f({\bi r},t)g({\bi r},t)]=(\Box f)g+f\Box g+2[\boldsymbol{\nabla}f\boldsymbol{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\nabla}g-(\partial f/c\partial t)(\partial g/c\partial t)].

(17)

Thus

\displaystyle\Box\,\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)=

\displaystyle(\Box\Phi_{\rm L})\Theta(ct{-}r)+\Phi_{\rm L}\Box\Theta(ct{-}r)

\displaystyle+2[\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Phi_{\rm L}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Theta(ct{-}r)-(\partial\Phi_{\rm L}/c\partial t)(\partial\Theta(ct{-}r)/c\partial t)]

\displaystyle+[\Box(q/r)]\Theta(r{-}ct)+(q/r)\Box\Theta(r{-}ct)+2\boldsymbol{\nabla}(q/r)\boldsymbol{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Theta(r{-}ct).

(18)

To evaluate (
18
), we need these results

\displaystyle\Box\Phi_{\rm L}

\displaystyle=-4\pi q\delta(x-vt)\delta(y)\delta(z),

(19)

\displaystyle\Box\Theta(ct-r)

\displaystyle=-(2/r)\delta(ct-r),

(20)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Phi_{\rm L}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Theta(ct-r)

\displaystyle=\frac{q\gamma\delta(ct-r)[\gamma^{2}x(x-vt)+y^{2}+z^{2}]}{r[\gamma^{2}(x-vt)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}]^{3/2}},

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{\rm L}}{c\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{q\beta\gamma^{3}(x-vt)}{[\gamma^{2}(x-vt)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}]^{3/2}},

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Theta(ct{-}r)}{c\partial t}

\displaystyle=\delta(ct-r),

(23)

\displaystyle\Box(q/r)

\displaystyle=-4\pi q\delta(x)\delta(y)\delta(z),

(24)

\displaystyle\Box\Theta(r{-}ct)

\displaystyle=(2/r)\delta(r-ct),

(25)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}(q/r)\boldsymbol{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Theta(r{-}ct)

\displaystyle=-(q/r^{2})\delta(r-ct).

(26)

Here, the result (
19
) expresses the fact that the charge density that produces the Lorenz-gauge scalar potential of equation (
2
) is that of a uniformly moving charge. With these results, we obtain

\displaystyle\Box\,\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)=

\displaystyle-4\pi q\big[\delta(x{-}vt)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(ct{-}r)+\delta(x)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(r{-}ct)\big]

\displaystyle+q\frac{2\beta\gamma^{3}(x{-}vt)(ct{-}r)\delta(ct{-}r)}{r[\gamma^{2}(x-vt)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}]^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi q\big[\delta(x-vt)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(ct-|vt|)+\delta(x)\delta(y)\delta(z)\Theta(-ct)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\tilde{\rho}({\bi r},t),

(27)

where the delta-function identities
f({\bi r})\delta({\bi r}-{\bi a})=f({\bi a})\delta({\bi r}-{\bi a})
and

x\delta(x)=0
are used to obtain the third line. The scalar potential
\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}
is thus shown to
satisfy the requisite inhomogeneous wave equation. It can be shown similarly that also the vector potential

\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}
satisfies the requisite inhomogeneous wave equation,
\Box\,\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}=-(4\pi/c)\tilde{\bi J}_{\rm L}
.

3
Fields

Using the Lorenz-gauge potentials (
10
), we can now calculate the electric field of a point charge set suddenly from rest into uniform motion. This field is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\bi E}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)-\partial\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)/c\partial t

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Phi_{\rm L})\Theta(ct-r)-\Phi_{\rm L}\,\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Theta(ct-r)+(q\hat{\bi r}/r^{2})\,\Theta(r-ct)-(q/r)\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Theta(r-ct)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,-\beta(\partial\Phi_{\rm L}/c\partial t)\Theta\,(ct-r)\hat{\bi x}-\beta\Phi_{\rm L}\,\delta(ct-r)\hat{\bi x}

\displaystyle=-(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Phi_{\rm L})\Theta(ct-r)+\Phi_{\rm L}\,\hat{\bi r}\,\delta(r-ct)+(q\hat{\bi r}/r^{2})\,\Theta(r-ct)-(q\hat{\bi r}/r)\delta(r-ct)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,+\beta^{2}(\partial\Phi_{\rm L}/\partial x)\Theta(ct-r)\hat{\bi x}-\beta\Phi_{\rm L}\,\delta(ct-r)\hat{\bi x}.

(28)

First, we collect and examine the delta-function terms,

\displaystyle[(\hat{\bi r}-\beta\hat{\bi x})\,\Phi_{\rm L}|_{t=r/c}-q\hat{\bi r}/r]\delta(ct-r)

\displaystyle=q\left(\frac{\hat{\bi r}-\beta\hat{\bi x}}{r-\beta x}-\frac{\hat{\bi r}}{r}\right)\delta(r-ct)

\displaystyle=q\frac{\beta(x\hat{\bi r}-r\hat{\bi x})}{r(r-\beta x)}\,\delta(r-ct).

(29)

This non-vanishing delta-function term can contribute to the electric field only on the spherical surface

r=ct
. The full electric field (
28
) is thus

\displaystyle\tilde{\bi E}({\bi r},t)=

\displaystyle-\left[(1-\beta^{2})\frac{\partial\Phi_{\rm L}}{\partial x}\,\hat{\bi x}+\frac{\partial\Phi_{\rm L}}{\partial y}\,\hat{\bi y}+\frac{\partial\Phi_{\rm L}}{\partial z}\,\hat{\bi z}\right]\Theta(ct{-}r)

\displaystyle+\frac{q\hat{\bi r}}{r^{2}}\,\Theta(r{-}ct)+q\frac{\beta(x\hat{\bi r}-r\hat{\bi x})}{r(r-\beta x)}\,\delta(r{-}ct).

(30)

In a final form, this reads

\displaystyle\tilde{\bi E}({\bi r},t)={\bi E}({\bi r},t)\,\Theta(ct-r)+\frac{q\hat{\bi r}}{r^{2}}\Theta(r-ct)+q\frac{\beta(x\hat{\bi r}-r\hat{\bi x})}{r(r-\beta x)}\,\delta(r-ct),

(31)

where
{\bi E}
is the Heaviside electric field of equation (
1
).
For times
t>0
, within a sphere of radius
r=ct
, the electric field (
31
) reduces to the Heaviside field, outside this sphere, it is the static Coulomb field
q\hat{\bi r}/r^{2}
of a point charge located on the origin
r=0
; on the infinitesimally thin spherical surface
r=ct
itself, the field is, strictly speaking, not defined. For times
t<0
, the field equals
q\hat{\bi r}/r^{2}
at all values of
r
. These results confirm the informal findings in the classic text of Purcell
[
4
]
for the field of a ‘charge that starts’.

The Lorenz-gauge vector potential of equation (
10
) produces the magnetic field corresponding to the electric field (
31
):

\displaystyle\tilde{\bi B}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\times\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\beta}\times{\bi E}({\bi r},t)\,\Theta(ct-r)+q\frac{\boldsymbol{\beta}\times\hat{\bi r}}{r-\beta x}\,\delta(r-ct),

(32)

where
{\bi E}
is again the Heaviside electric field; the delta-function term arises through the factor
\Theta(ct-r)
in the vector potential. Within the sphere of radius
r=ct
, this magnetic field equals the Heaviside magnetic field of a uniformly moving charge, but outside this sphere, the magnetic field vanishes.

The electromagnetic field
(\tilde{\bi E},\tilde{\bi B})
of equations (
31
) and (
32
) was obtained using the Lorenz-gauge potentials (
10
)
that satisfy the requisite inhomogeneous wave equations,

\Box\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}=-4\pi\tilde{\rho}
and
\Box\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}=-(4\pi/c)\tilde{\bi J}
, where
the charge density
\tilde{\rho}
and the current density
\tilde{\bi J}
are those of a point charge set instantaneously from rest into uniform motion but still satisfying the continuity equation. In view of these facts, the electromagnetic field
(\tilde{\bi E},\tilde{\bi B})
complies with all the four Maxwell equations, the ‘unphysicality’ of its discontinuity and lack of regular definition on the spherical surface
r=ct
notwithstanding.
The delta-function terms in the fields (
31
) and (
32
), which are novel features of our approach, are a mathematical consequence of the instantaneous jump in the charge’s velocity from zero to a nonzero value at the space-time point
(r{=}0,\,t{=}0)
.

4
Coulomb-gauge potentials

The Lorenz-gauge potentials

\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}
and
\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}
of equation (
10
) can be transformed to Coulomb-gauge potentials

\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm C}
and
\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm C}
using a gauge function
\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)
that is defined by the relations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)}{c\partial t}=\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)-\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm C}({\bi r},t),\quad\boldsymbol{\nabla}\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)=\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)-\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t).

(33)

The conditions of the Lorenz and Coulomb gauges are

\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}/c\partial t+\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}=0
and
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm C}=0
, respectively, and so the divergence of the 2nd equality of equation (
33
) yields

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\chi_{\rm C}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm C}-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}=\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)/c\partial t.

(34)

The gauge function
\chi_{\rm C}
thus satisfies Poisson’s equation

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)=\frac{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)}{c\partial t},

(35)

the free-space Green’s function for which gives the gauge function by

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\frac{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)}{c\partial t},

(36)

where, using the first equality of equation (
10
),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)}{c\partial t}=-\beta\frac{\partial\Phi_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)}{\partial x}\,\Theta(ct-r)+\Phi_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)\,\delta(ct-r)-\frac{q}{r}\,\delta(r-ct).

(37)

Here, the factors

\Theta(ct-r)
and
\delta(ct-r)

make
\partial\tilde{\Phi}({\bi r},t)/c\partial t
a well-localized function of
\bi r
at any finite time
t
, ensuring the convergence of the integral representation (
36
) of the gauge function
\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)
.

It can be checked directly that the first relation of equation (
33
) is satisfied by the integral representation (
36
) of
\chi_{\rm C}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)}{c\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)}{c^{2}\partial t^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}(\nabla^{\prime 2}-\Box^{\prime})\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\,[\nabla^{\prime 2}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)+4\pi\tilde{\rho}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\,\nabla^{\prime 2}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)-\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}\,\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)\,\nabla^{\prime 2}\frac{1}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}-\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)-\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm C}({\bi r},t).

(38)

Here, the integral in the 4th line is transformed by applying integration by parts twice and, in the last line, the identity
\nabla^{\prime 2}|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|^{-1}=-4\pi\delta({\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime})
is used.

We can show also directly that the second relation of equation (
33
) is satisfied by the integral representation (
36
) of
\chi_{\rm C}
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\chi_{\rm C}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}\left[\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\prime}\frac{1}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\right]\frac{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)}{c\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}\frac{1}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\prime}[\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\cdot}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}\frac{1}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\,\{\nabla^{\prime 2}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)+\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\prime}\times[\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\prime}\times\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)]\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)\nabla^{\prime 2}\frac{1}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\times\int\frac{\rmd^{3}r^{\prime}}{|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|}\,\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\prime}\times\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r}^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}({\bi r},t)+\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm C}({\bi r},t).

(39)

Here, in the 2nd line, the integral is transformed by integrating by parts and using the Lorenz-gauge condition;
in the 4th line, integration by parts is applied twice on the 1st term of the integrand; and, in last line, the identity

\nabla^{\prime 2}|{\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime}|^{-1}=-4\pi\delta({\bi r}-{\bi r}^{\prime})
is used and the 2nd term in 4th line is recognized as the transverse part of the Lorenz-gauge vector potential
\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm L}
(integrating by parts, the curl operators can be moved outside the integral) and as such as the Coulomb-gauge vector potential

\tilde{\bi A}_{\rm C}

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{{\rm C}x}({\bi r},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\chi_{\rm C}}{\partial x}+\tilde{A}_{{\rm L}x}

\displaystyle=q\left[\frac{r-\beta x}{r\sqrt{(x{-}\beta r)^{2}+s^{2}/\gamma^{2}}}-\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{\sqrt{(x{-}vt)^{2}+s^{2}/\gamma^{2}}}\right]\frac{\Theta(ct{-}r)}{\beta}

5
Concluding remarks

We found the Lorenz-gauge and Coulomb-gauge potentials of a point charge that is set suddenly into uniform motion. The Coulomb-gauge vector potential was obtained using the pertinent gauge function of the transformation between those two gauges, calculated in closed form. The electric field of the charge calculated using the potentials agrees with that found by Purcell
[
4
]
by just employing imaginatively the fact that electromagnetic effects propagate at the speed of light.

The electromagnetic field of the charge complies with all the four Maxwell equations, despite the ‘unphysical’ instantaneous jump of the charge’s velocity from zero to a nonzero value. In any case, it can be assumed that the charge attains its constant velocity in an initial time interval of a finite, but arbitrarily short duration
\tau
by application of an external force of sufficiently high magnitude. In the limit
\tau\rightarrow 0
, the regular electromagnetic field that would connect the field in the regions interior and exterior to the sphere of radius
r=ct
is replaced by the delta-function terms of the fields (
31
) and (
32
).

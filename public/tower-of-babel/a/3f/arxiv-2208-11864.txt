Boundedness of the Gaussian Riesz potentials on Gaussian variable Lebesgue spaces
Eduard Navas, Ebner Pineda, Wilfredo O. Urbina

Abstract
. In this paper we prove the boundedness of the Gaussian Riesz potentials I β I_{\beta} , for β ≥ 1 \beta\geq 1 on L p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ​ ( γ d ) L^{p(\cdot)}(\gamma_{d}) , the Gaussian variable Lebesgue spaces under a certain additional condition of regularity on p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) p(\cdot) following [ 5 ] . 1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

In the classical case, the Riesz potential of order
\beta>0
is defined as negative fractional powers of the negative Laplacian
-\Delta=-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
,

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{-\beta/2},

which means, using Fourier transform, that

(1.2)

((-\Delta)^{-\beta/2}f)\hat{}\;(\xi)=(2\pi|\xi|)^{-\beta}\hat{f}(\xi).

for more details; see
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
.

Analogously, the
Gaussian fractional integrals
or
Gaussian Riesz potentials
can be also defined as negative fractional powers of the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator

(1.3)

(-L)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\langle x,\nabla_{x}\rangle=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big].

However, since the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator has eigenvalue
0,
the negative powers are not defined on all of
L^{2}(\gamma_{d}),
and therefore we need to be more careful with the definition. Let us consider

\Pi_{0}f=f-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)\gamma_{d}(dy),

for
f\in L^{2}(\gamma_{d})
, the orthogonal projection on the orthogonal complement of the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
0
.

Definition 1.1
.

The Gaussian Fractional Integral or Gaussian Riesz potential of order
\beta>0
,
I_{\beta}
, is defined spectrally as,

(1.4)

I_{\beta}=(-L)^{-\beta/2}\Pi_{0},

which means that for any multi-index
\nu,\;|\nu|>0
its action on the Hermite polynomial
\vec{H}_{\nu}
is given by

(1.5)

I_{\beta}\vec{H}_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{\left|\nu\right|^{\beta/2}}\vec{H}_{\nu}(x),

and for
\nu=0=(0,...,0),\,I_{\beta}(\vec{H}_{0})=0.

By linearity, using the fact that the Hermite polynomials are an algebraic basis of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

I_{\beta}
can be defined for any polynomial function
f(x)=\sum_{\nu}\widehat{f}_{\gamma_{d}}(\nu)\vec{H}_{\nu}(x),
where
\widehat{f}_{\gamma_{d}}(\nu)=\frac{1}{\|\vec{H}_{\nu}\|_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)\vec{H}_{\nu}(t)dt,
as

(1.6)

I_{\beta}f(x)=\sum_{\nu}\frac{\widehat{f}_{\gamma_{d}}(\nu)}{\left|\nu\right|^{\beta/2}}\vec{H}_{\nu}(x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k^{\beta/2}}{\bf J}_{k}f(x),

and similarly for
f\in L^{2}(\gamma_{d}),
as the Hermite polynomials are an orthogonal basis of
L^{2}(\gamma_{d}).

It can be proved that the Gaussian Riesz potential
I_{\beta},

\beta>0,
has the following integral representations, for
f
a polynomial function or
f\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.7)

I_{\beta}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\beta/2-1}T_{t}(I-{\bf J}_{0})f(x)\,dt,

with respect to the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
\{T_{t}\}
, and

(1.8)

I_{\beta}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\beta-1}P_{t}(I-{\bf J}_{0})f(x)\,dt,

with respect to the Poisson-Hermite semigroup,
\{P_{t}\}
.

Therefore, from (
1.7
) we have an explicit integral representation of
I_{\beta}
as

(1.9)

I_{\beta}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}N_{\beta/2}(x,y)f(y)dy\

where the kernel
N_{\beta/2}
is defined as

(1.10)

N_{\beta/2}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{\beta}{2})}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{|y-e^{-t}x|^{2}}{1-e^{-2t}}}}{(1-e^{-2t})^{\frac{d}{2}}}-e^{-|y|^{2}}\right)dt

For more details and background we refer to
[
14
]
.

From (
1.6

are the simplest Meyer’s multipliers (see for instance Theorem 6.2 of

-boundedness follows immediately.

In this paper we prove that Gaussian Riesz potentials

Given

\mu_{-}

We use the abbreviations

Definition 1.2
.

Let

and

\displaystyle|\alpha(x)-\alpha_{\infty}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{log(e+|x-x_{0}|)}

for all

. We say that

is log-Hölder continuous, and denote this by

Definition 1.5

The variable exponent Lebesgue space on

P(x,y)=|x+y|^{d}e^{-\alpha_{\infty}|x+y||x-y|},\;Q(x,y)=|x+y|^{d+1}e^{-\alpha_{\infty}|x+y|}

and

\alpha_{\infty}=\left(\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}-\left|\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\right|\right).

It is easy to see that
\alpha_{\infty}>0
if
\epsilon<1/p^{\prime}_{\infty}
.

Therefore, in order to make sense of all the estimates above we need to take

0<\epsilon<\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{\infty}},\frac{2}{d}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}-r\right)\right\}.

Observe that
P(x,y)
is the same kernel considered in the proof of Theorem 3.5, page 416 of
[
5
]
, so we can conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{h}(x)\cap E_{x}}P(x,y)|f(y)|e^{-|y|^{2}/p(y)}dy\right)^{p(x)}dx\leq C.

On the other hand, it can be proved that
Q(x,y)
is integrable on each variable and the value of each integral is independent of
x
and
y
.
Now, we use an analogous argument as in
[
5
]
for
Q(x,y)
. Taking,

J=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)|f(y)|e^{-|y|^{2}/p(y)}dy,

and using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

J\lesssim\|Q(x,\cdot)\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\|fe^{-|\cdot|^{2}/p(\cdot)}\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq\|Q(x,\cdot)\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}.

and,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)^{p^{\prime}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x+y|^{(d+1)p^{\prime}(y)}e^{-\alpha_{\infty}|x+y|p^{\prime}(y)}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|x+y|<1}|x+y|^{d+1}e^{-\alpha_{\infty}|x+y|}dy+\int_{|x+y|\geq 1}|x+y|^{(d+1)p^{\prime}_{+}}e^{-\alpha_{\infty}|x+y|}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(|z|^{d+1}+|z|^{(d+1)p^{\prime}_{+}}\right)e^{-\alpha_{\infty}|z|}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{p,d}.

Thus,
J\lesssim\|Q(x,\cdot)\|_{p^{{}^{\prime}}(\cdot)}\leq C_{p,d}
, and therefore

\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)|f(y)|e^{-|y|^{2}/p(y)}dy\leq 1.

We set
g(y)=|f(y)|e^{-|y|^{2}/p(y)}=g_{1}(y)+g_{2}(y)
, where
g_{1}=g\chi_{\{g\geq 1\}}
and
g_{2}=g\chi_{\{g<1\}}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)|f(y)|e^{-|y|^{2}/p(y)}dy\right)^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g(y)dy\right)^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{1}(y)dy\right)^{p(x)}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{2}(y)dy\right)^{p(x)}dx.

By Hölder’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{1}(y)dy\right)^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{1}(y)dy\right)^{p_{-}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{-}}}(x,y)Q^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}}(x,y)g_{1}(y)dy\right)^{p_{-}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)dy\right)^{p_{-}/p^{\prime}_{-}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g^{p_{-}}_{1}(y)dy\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g^{p_{-}}_{1}(y)dy\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{p_{-}}_{1}(y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)dx\right)dy=C_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{p_{-}}_{1}(y)dy.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{1}(y)dy\right)^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{p(y)}_{1}(y)dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p(y)}e^{-|y|^{2}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{p(\cdot),\gamma_{d}}(f)\leq 1.

On the other hand, applying the inequality (
2.1
) in Lemma
2.1
, since

G(x):=\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{2}(y)dy\leq 1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{C_{p,d}}Q(x,y)g_{2}(y)dy\right)^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(G(x))^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(G(x))^{p_{\infty}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{dx}{(e+|x|)^{dp_{-}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{2}(y)dy\right)^{p_{\infty}}+C_{d,p}.

Finally, to estimate the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{2}(y)dy\right)^{p_{\infty}}dx,

we proceed in an analogous way, applying Hölder’s inequality to the exponent
p_{\infty}
, Fubbini’s theorem and inequality (
2.2
) in Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,y)g_{2}(y)dy\right)^{p_{\infty}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{2}^{p_{\infty}}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{2}^{p(y)}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{dy}{(e+|y|)^{dp_{-}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p(y)}e^{-|y|^{2}}dy+C_{d,p}\lesssim 1+C_{d,p}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int\limits_{B_{h}^{c}(x)\cap E_{x}}\left|N_{\beta/2}(x,y)\right||f(y)|dy\right)^{p(x)}\gamma_{d}(dx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{d,p}.

With this, we obtain that
\|I_{\beta}(f\chi_{B_{h}^{c}(\cdot)\cap E_{(\cdot)}})\|_{p(\cdot),\gamma_{d}}\leq C_{d,p}
.

We conclude that

\left\|I_{\beta,G}(f)\right\|_{p(\cdot),\gamma_{d}}=\left\|I_{\beta}\left(f\chi_{B^{c}_{h}(\cdot)}\right)\right\|_{p(\cdot),\gamma_{d}}\leq C,

and by homogeneity of the norm,

\left\|I_{\beta,G}(f)\right\|_{p(\cdot),\gamma_{d}}\leq C\|f\|_{p(\cdot),\gamma_{d}}
, for all function
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma_{d})
.

Therefore, the global part
I_{\beta,G}
is bounded in
L^{p(\cdot)}(\gamma_{d})
and the proof is complete.

On the polynomiality conjecture of cluster realization of quantum groups
Ivan Chi-Ho Ip, Jeff York Ye

Abstract
In this paper, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for a regular element of a quantum cluster algebra 𝒪 q ​ ( 𝒳 ) \mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X}) to be universally polynomial. This resolves several conjectures by the first author on the polynomiality of the cluster realization of quantum group generators in different families of positive representations.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra. The quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
was originally defined by Drinfeld
[
Dri90
]
and Jimbo
[
Jim85
]
, which is a Hopf algebra deforming the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
. The Drinfeld’s double
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g})
corresponds to the double of the Borel part
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
. To study the structure of quantum groups, one can embed them into simpler algebras such as a quantum torus algebra
\mathbb{C}_{q}[\mathbb{T}]
or its skew field of rational functions
\mathbb{C}_{q}(\mathbb{T})
. This was explored by Gerasimov–Kharchev–Lebedev–Oblezin in
[
GKL05
]
and
[
GKLO05
]
by generalizing the Gelfand–Tsetlin representations originally defined for classical Lie groups, and they obtained an embedding of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
into
\mathbb{C}_{q}(\mathbb{T})
. The Feigin’s homomorphism also provides an embedding of the positive Borel part
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}_{+})
to the quantum torus algebra
[
Ber96
]
. However, the embedding of the whole quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
into
\mathbb{C}_{q}[\mathbb{T}]
is more difficult. For type
A_{n}
, this was solved by Kashaev–Volkov
[
KV98
]
.

Meanwhile, these embeddings are related to quantum Liouville theory in physics, where the Chevalley generators of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
act as positive self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
case was studied by Schmüdgen
[
Sch96
]
, Ponsot–Teschner
[
PT99
]
[
PT01
]
.

To study this problem for general Lie types, Frenkel and the first author introduced the notion of positive representations for split real quantum groups in
[
FI14
]
. Split real quantum groups were developed by Faddeev in
[
Fad95
]
and extended to its modular double in
[
Fad99
]
, motivated from string theory and conformal field theory. Based on the idea by Schrader–Shapiro in the case of type
A_{n}

[
SS19
]
, by forgetting the real structure the positive representations lead to an embedding of the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
of general Lie type into a quotient of a certain quantum torus algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{{D}}(\textnormal{{i}}_{0})})
associated to a quiver
\textnormal{{D}}(\textnormal{{i}}_{0})

[
Ip18
]
. The quiver
\textnormal{{D}}(\textnormal{{i}}_{0})
is mutation equivalent to the quiver for the moduli space of framed
G
-local systems
\mathscr{P}_{G,\odot}
, where
\odot
denotes the once punctured disk with two special points on the boundary
[
GS22
]
. In later works, this embedding was modified in
[
Ip20
]
and
[
IM22
]
to give positive representations which are related to the quantization of the classical parabolic induction, and certain degeneration of the generalized Casimir operators.

It was conjectured in
[
Ip18
, Section 1.1]
and
[
Ip20
, Conjecture 7.1]
that the images of Chevalley generators of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
are polynomials in the cluster variables for any cluster in the mutation class of the quantum torus algebra. It is also suspected that the same statement is true for the homomorphisms introduced in
[
IM22
]
. The main goal of this paper is to resolve these conjectures.

Main Theorem

(Theorem
4.9
)
.

For the homomorphisms

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle:\mathfrak{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle\overline{\kappa}

\displaystyle:\mathfrak{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{O}_{q}(\overline{\mathcal{X}})

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}

\displaystyle:\mathfrak{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X}^{0})

described in Section
3.3
, the image of the Chevalley generators
\textnormal{{E}}_{i},\textnormal{{F}}_{i},\textnormal{{K}}_{i},\textnormal{{K}}_{i}^{\prime}
are all polynomials in the quantum cluster variables with coefficients in
\mathbb{N}[q^{\pm 1}]
for any cluster in the mutation class.

The idea of the proof is to use
c
and
g
-vectors, which are known to be sign coherent for skew-symmetric seed data
[
GHKK14
]
. This allows us to control the degree of the cluster
\mathcal{X}
-variables after each mutation, and leads directly to the proof.

It will be important to understand the geometric meaning of the polynomiality of the image of quantum groups in the quantum cluster algebra. Just as the ring of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}[X_{k},X_{k}^{-1}]
describes regular functions on the
torus
, the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[X_{k}]
is usually associated to regular functions on the
affine space
. Therefore we can expect an interpretation of the quantum groups to be generalized from elements of the quantum torus algebra to elements associated to
quantum plane
, motivated by
[
Ip13
]
, in which
C^{*}
-and von Neumann algebraic techniques can be utilized to study the multiplier Hopf structures of their images, thus building a new connection between Drinfeld–Jimbo type quantum groups and non-commutative geometry.

Furthermore, it will be interesting to understand the combinatorial description of the polynomial images, where the coefficients are expected to count certain canonical objects associated to a given cluster seed. In
[
SS17
]
, this is answered explicitly for type
A_{n}
quantum groups in the case of the standard embedding, in which the coefficients of the polynomial images of the Borel part
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
gives a weighted count of the homology cycles around the dual planar bipolar graph of the associated basic quivers. The Lusztig’s braid group action
T_{\textnormal{{i}}_{0}}
described in
[
GS22
]
can be interpreted as a sequence of mutations which provides an
S_{3}
action to the quantum group embedding for each triangle on the surface, and the results of this paper shows that these embeddings remain a polynomial. For example, in type
E_{8}
and starting with the Coxeter words as initial seed, by a heavy brute force check, the
\textnormal{{f}}_{0}
generator is mutated up to a polynomial of 825887337 terms before reducing to the final configuration, with very curious coefficients in
\mathbb{N}[q^{\pm 1}]
represented in terms of ratios of
q
-numbers
[
Ip20
]
. It will be important to give a more combinatorial description of these polynomials and their coefficients.

Finally, in
[
IM22
]
, we proposed a program to classify the
regular positive representations
as described in Section
5
. One of the defining conditions is that we have a homomorphism of the quantum group into universally Laurent polynomials of some quantum cluster variety. The results of this paper shows that all the classes considered so far have the image of quantum group lies in the
universally polynomial
part of the quantum cluster variety, and it will be essential to understand whether this is characterization of such class of representations, or there are examples in between that is yet to be discovered.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the definition of cluster varieties and quantum cluster algebra in general. In Section
3
, we give the definition of quantum groups. Then we provide a summary of the works by the first author and Man
[
Ip18
]
,
[
Ip20
]
,
[
IM22
]
, as well as the works by Goncharov and Shen
[
GS22
]
,
[

]
on embeddings of

into cluster algebras. In Section

, we use the sign coherence of

-vectors to give a sufficient condition for a regular element of a quantum cluster algebra

to be universally polynomial. Using this, we prove the polynomiality conjectures by the first author. Another more abstract, but sufficient and necessary condition is also formulated using
G
-matrices. For the polynomiality of zero Casimir representations, we define cluster folding via a group action and relate the quantum cluster algebras
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X}^{G})
. Finally, we provide some generalizations of the main theorem, including the amalgamation of multiple copies of the quiver and corresponds to the coproduct in
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. In Section

[

, in view of the new results obtained in Section

2.4
Amalgamation

Another important construction of cluster algebras is amalgamation, which corresponds to the gluing of two quivers along frozen variables. This construction introduced in
[
FG06
]
appears naturally in the study of cluster algebras arising from higher Teichmüller theory, since we glue quivers associated to triangles along their boundaries to get quivers for a general surface.

Definition 2.21
.

Let
\{(\Gamma_{a},\textnormal{{s}}_{a})\}_{a\in A}
be a finite collection of seed datum. For any index set
I
with surjection

\rho:\bigsqcup_{a\in A}I_{a}\to I

such that:

•

The restriction
\rho_{a}:=\rho|_{I_{a}}
is injective for all
a\in A
,

•

If
\rho(j)=\rho(k)
for
j\in I_{a}
and
k\in I_{a^{\prime}}
, then
j,k
are frozen and
d_{j}=d_{k}
.

The

is a new seed data
(\Gamma,\textnormal{{s}})
where

•

N
is the sublattice of
\bigoplus_{a\in A}N_{a}
spanned by
\textnormal{{s}}=\{\textnormal{{e}}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, where

\textnormal{{e}}_{i}=\sum_{\rho(j)=i}\textnormal{{e}}_{j},

•

The bilinear form
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is induced from the direct sum
\bigoplus_{a\in A}N_{a}
.

•

For each
i\in I
,

for any

It is easy to see that the cluster algebra

is a subalgebra of the tensor product

, and we have natural injections

-side as well as their quantizations.

4
Polynomial conditions

4.1
Main results

To prepare for the main theorem, we first discuss the sufficient conditions for monomials or polynomials in the cluster
\mathcal{X}
-variables for one seed to be universally monomial or universally polynomial. Consider a fixed data
\Gamma
with a fixed initial seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{0}=(\textnormal{{e}}_{i})_{i\in I}
as in Definition
2.1
–
2.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
z^{v}\in\Bbbk_{q}[N]
be such that
\{\textnormal{{e}}_{i},v\}=0
for each
i\in I_{\textnormal{uf}}
. Then
z^{v}
is always a monomial in the quantum cluster variables for any seed in the mutation class of
\textnormal{{s}}_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
z^{v}
and
z^{-v}
both satisfy the assumptions in Theorem
2.20
, they are Laurent polynomials in the cluster variables for any seed in the mutation class, hence they must be monomials.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For any seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{t}
,
t\in\mathfrak{T}
, let

v=\sum_{i\in I-I_{\textnormal{uf}}}a_{i}\textnormal{{e}}_{i;t},

for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and suppose
\{\textnormal{{e}}_{j;t},v\}\geq 0
for each
j\in I_{\textnormal{uf}}
. Then
z^{v}\in\Bbbk_{q}[N]
remains a polynomial in the quantum cluster variables for any seed in the mutation class.

Proof.

It suffices to check at the initial seed. By Theorem
2.20
,
\mu_{t}^{*}(z^{v})
is a Laurent polynomial.

Meanwhile, consider the tropicalization (
2.16
). For each
l\in I
, using Lemma
2.17
we get

\textnormal{{e}}_{l}^{*}(\mu_{t}^{*}(z^{v}))=\sum_{i\in I-I_{\textnormal{uf}}}a_{i}x_{li;t}=\sum_{i\in I-I_{\textnormal{uf}}}a_{i}c_{li;t}.

Since the frozen vertices
i\in I-I_{\textnormal{uf}}
are never mutated,
c_{ii;t}=1
by definition. By sign coherence of
c
-vectors,
c_{li;t}\geq 0
for all
l\in I
. This shows that
\textnormal{{e}}_{l}^{*}(\mu_{t}^{*}(z^{v}))\geq 0
for all
l\in I
, so the power of each
X_{l}
in
\mu_{t}^{*}(z^{v})
is non-negative and
\mu_{t}^{*}(z^{v})
is a polynomial.
∎

The condition in Proposition
4.2
is sufficient but not necessary. For example, for the quantum cluster algebra associated to the Markov quiver (without frozen vertices), the monomial
X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}
is invariant under mutation, hence universally polynomial.

It is interesting to ask exactly which standard monomials are universally polynomial, or more generally, what exactly is the subring of universal polynomials in
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X})
or
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
. A necessary condition is given as follows.

Fix an initial seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{0}
with the corresponding cluster
\mathcal{X}
-variables
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
be a polynomial in
X_{i}
, such that each monomial appearing in
f
determines a vector
\vec{v}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Let
\widetilde{G}_{t}
be the matrix for
g_{ji;t}^{\dagger}
as described in Lemma
2.18
.

Proposition 4.3
.

f
is universally polynomial if and only if
\widetilde{G}_{t}\vec{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
for all
t\in\mathfrak{T}
and all
\vec{v}
.

Proof.

The forward direction is proved in the same as Proposition
4.2
. Conversely, suppose
\widetilde{G}_{t}\vec{v}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
for some
t\in\mathfrak{T}
and
\vec{v}
. By
[
NZ12
, Proposition 4.2]
and Theorem
2.13
,
\widetilde{G}_{t}
is invertible, so
\widetilde{G}_{t}\vec{v}
is distinct for different
\vec{v}
. Recall from Lemma
2.18
and the definition of
y_{ij;t}
that
\widetilde{G}_{t}\vec{v}
corresponds to the lowest degree term with respect to
\textnormal{{s}}_{t}
. This shows that its negative powers do not cancel and
f
has a term with negative powers in the seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{t}
.
∎

While Proposition
4.3
characterizes all universal polynomials, we do not know a good way to compute the exact condition, as there are in general infinitely many
\widetilde{G}_{t}
when
t
ranges through
\mathfrak{T}
. However, using the language of
[
GHKK14
]
,
g
-vectors are generators of the chambers in the cluster complex
\Delta^{+}(\mathbb{Z})
, which is the collection of cluster chambers, each of which is a cone in the tropicalization
\mathcal{A}^{\vee}(\mathbb{Z}^{\textnormal{trop}})
. Thus we can reinterpret the condition as follows.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
. Then
f
is universally polynomial if and only if for each monomial
z^{n}
appearing in
f
in some seed
s
,
\langle n,m\rangle\geq 0
for each
m\in\Delta^{+}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Example 4.5
.

If
\mathcal{A}
has a large cluster complex,
\Delta^{+}(\mathbb{Z})
does not lie in a half space, so there are no non-trivial universal polynomials in
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
. This happens if for example
\mathcal{A}
has an acyclic seed, or has a seed with a maximal green sequence
[
GHKK14
, Corollary 8.30]
.

This shows that in many cases, including the moduli space of local systems
\mathcal{P}_{G,\mathbb{S}}
in
[
GS22
, Theorem 2.13]
, frozen variables are necessary for
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
to have non-trivial universal polynomials.

to apply folding of root systems to the moduli spaces of

Definition 4.11

For any fixed data
\Gamma

\Sigma
acting on

preserving

\displaystyle\{\textnormal{{e}}_{\sigma(i)},\cdot\}=\{\textnormal{{e}}_{i},\cdot\}

(4.8)

for all

and
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Clearly
\Sigma
induces an automorphism of
N
.
Then we define the

of
\Gamma
to be a fixed data
\Gamma^{\Sigma}
consisting of:

•

The index set
\overline{I}:=I/\Sigma
,
\overline{I}_{\textnormal{uf}}:=I_{\textnormal{uf}}/\Sigma
,
d_{\overline{i}}:=d_{i}/|O(i)|
, where
O(i)
is the orbit of
i\in I
under
\Sigma
.

•

N^{\Sigma}
is the invariant sublattice generated by
\overline{\textnormal{{s}}}=(\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}})_{\overline{i}\in\overline{I}}

\displaystyle\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}}:=\sum_{j\in O(i)}\textnormal{{e}}_{j},

(4.9)

with

generated by
(\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}})_{\overline{i}\in\overline{I}_{\textnormal{uf}}}

(N^{\Sigma})^{\circ}
generated by
(d_{\overline{i}}\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}})_{\overline{i}\in\overline{I}}
.

•

The skew-symmetric bilinear form on
N^{\Sigma}
is inherited from that on
N
, so

\displaystyle\{\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}},\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{j}}\}:=|O(i)||O(j)|\{\textnormal{{e}}_{i},\textnormal{{e}}_{j}\}.

(4.10)

•

Lattices
M^{\Sigma}:=\textnormal{Hom}(N^{\Sigma},\mathbb{Z})
,
(M^{\Sigma})^{\circ}:=\textnormal{Hom}((N^{\Sigma})^{\circ},\mathbb{Z})
.

From the definition, it is obvious that the requirements
\{(N^{\Sigma})^{\circ},N_{\textnormal{uf}}^{\Sigma}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
\{N_{\textnormal{uf}}^{\Sigma}\cap(N^{\Sigma})^{\circ},N^{\Sigma}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
are always satisfied. The numbers
d_{\overline{i}}
might not be integers, but this does not affect any of our calculations and we can always fix this by rescaling if necessary.

In the remainder of this section, we will always assume that
\Gamma
has a folding
\overline{\Gamma}
defined using the initial seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{0}
.

Define analogously
\textnormal{{f}}_{\overline{i}}:=d_{\overline{i}}^{-1}\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}}^{*}
,
\varepsilon_{\overline{i}\overline{j}}:=d_{\overline{i}}\{\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}},\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{j}}\}
and the cluster varieties
\mathcal{X}^{\Sigma}:=\mathcal{X}_{\Gamma^{\Sigma}}
,
\mathcal{A}^{\Sigma}:=\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma^{\Sigma}}
and similarly
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X}^{\Sigma})
,
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{A}^{\Sigma})
respectively.

Let
\overline{\mathfrak{T}}
be the infinite oriented rooted tree for
\Gamma^{\Sigma}
. We define an injection
\iota:\overline{\mathfrak{T}}\to\mathfrak{T}
as follows:

1.

The root
\overline{t}_{0}
of
\overline{\mathfrak{T}}
is mapped to the root
t_{0}
of
\mathfrak{T}
.

2.

Inductively, suppose
\iota(t)
is already defined and
t\xrightarrow{\overline{k}}t^{\prime}
in
\overline{\mathfrak{T}}
. We choose an arbitrary order
k_{1},\cdots,k_{|O(k)|}
of the orbit
O(k)
of
k
and move from
\iota(t)
along the edges labeled by
k_{1},\cdots,k_{|O(k)|}
in such order giving the vertex
\iota(t^{\prime})
.

The following lemma shows that the folding
\Gamma^{\Sigma}
is independent of the seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{t}
for
t\in\iota(\overline{\mathfrak{T}})
.

Lemma 4.12
.

For any
\overline{t}\in\overline{\mathfrak{T}}
, the seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{\iota(\overline{t})}
also defines a folding. Then
N^{\Sigma}_{\overline{t}}=N^{\Sigma}
, and the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{\Sigma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{\Sigma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{\overline{t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{\iota(\overline{t})}}

where the horizontal maps are inclusions.

Proof.

We reduce to the case
\overline{t}_{0}\xrightarrow{\overline{k}}\overline{t}
by induction.

By the assumption in Definition
4.11
and the mutation formulas in Section
2.1
, if
i
and
j
have the same orbit, the mutations
\mu_{i}
and
\mu_{j}
are identical up to swapping
i
and
j
. In this case,
\varepsilon_{ij}=0
, and it is also well known that the mutations
\mu_{i}
and
\mu_{j}
commute. Thus we still have

\{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}(\textnormal{{e}}_{i}),\cdot\}=\{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}(\textnormal{{e}}_{j}),\cdot\}.

This shows that the assumption in Definition
4.11
is still satisfied and inductively
\textnormal{{s}}_{\iota(\overline{t})}
also defines a folding.

The condition
N^{\Sigma}_{\overline{t}}=N^{\Sigma}
is immediate since mutation sends
N^{\Sigma}
into itself. Let
\mu_{O(k)}
be the composition of the commuting mutations
\mu_{l}
over all
l\in O(k)
. Then

\mu_{O(k)}(\textnormal{{e}}_{i})=\begin{cases}\textnormal{{e}}_{i}+[\varepsilon_{ki}]_{+}\textnormal{{e}}_{k}&i\not\in O(k)\\
-\textnormal{{e}}_{i}&i\in O(k)\end{cases},

so we have

\mu_{O(k)}\circ(\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}})=\begin{cases}\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}}+|O_{i}|[\varepsilon_{ki}]_{+}\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{k}}&i\not\in O(k)\\
-\textnormal{{e}}_{\overline{i}}&i\in O(k)\end{cases}.

Since

|O(i)|\varepsilon_{ki}=|O(i)|d_{k}\{\textnormal{{e}}_{k},\textnormal{{e}}_{i}\}=\varepsilon_{\overline{k}\overline{i}},

we get
\mu_{\overline{t}}=\mu_{\iota(\overline{t})}
on
N^{G}
.
∎

The inclusion
N^{\Sigma}\to N
also induces an injection
\iota:\Bbbk_{q}[N^{\Sigma}]\to\Bbbk_{q}[N]
. Unlike for

above, the mutations for
\mathcal{X}
does not commute with
\iota
, so we cannot glue them to an injection
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X}^{\Sigma})\to\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
globally.

However, we note that the image of
\iota
lies the the invariant subring
\Bbbk_{q}[N]^{\Sigma}
. It is well known that
\Bbbk_{q}[N]^{\Sigma}
is generated over
\Bbbk_{q}
by
\mathcal{E}_{i,a}
for

,

, where

is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree

in the variables

for

and
X_{j}^{-1}
for

, over all
j\in O(i)
. We have
\iota(X_{\overline{i}})=\mathcal{E}_{i,|O(i)|}
.

Then by extending the map
\iota
and the mutation maps to the completion of
\Bbbk_{q}[N^{G}]
and
\Bbbk_{q}[N]^{\Sigma}
with respect to the ideal generated by the cluster variables

, we can still salvage the following fact:

Proposition 4.13
.

The injection
\iota:\textnormal{Frac}(\widehat{\Bbbk_{q}[N^{\Sigma}]})\to\textnormal{Frac}(\widehat{\Bbbk_{q}[N]^{\Sigma}})
has a
\Bbbk_{q}
-linear inverse

such that the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{Frac}(\widehat{\Bbbk_{q}[N]^{\Sigma}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{Frac}(\widehat{\Bbbk_{q}[N^{\Sigma}]})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{Frac}(\widehat{\Bbbk_{q}[N]^{\Sigma}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{Frac}(\widehat{\Bbbk_{q}[N^{\Sigma}]})}

For any

and

with

This shows that for

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{*}(X_{i_{1}}^{\prime}\cdots X_{i_{a}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=X_{i_{1}}\prod_{r=1}^{|\varepsilon_{ki}|}(1+q_{k}^{2r-1}X_{k})\cdots X_{i_{a}}\prod_{r=1}^{|\varepsilon_{ik}|}(1+q_{k}^{2r-1}X_{k})

\displaystyle=X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{a}}\prod_{r=1}^{|\varepsilon_{ki}|}\prod_{s=0}^{a-1}(1+q_{k}^{2r+2s|\varepsilon_{ik}|-1}X_{k})

\displaystyle=X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{a}}\prod_{r=1}^{a|\varepsilon_{ki}|}(1+q_{k}^{2r-1}X_{k}).

Then the mutation

4.3
Extension to general
G
-local systems

Our result can be extended to embeddings of
\mathfrak{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
into
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{G,\mathbb{S}})
for general
G
-local systems. The main example is
\odot_{n}
, the disk with
n
-punctures and two boundary special points. The relation between quantum groups and multiple punctured disk is first investigated in
[
Kas01
]
for
n=2
,
[
SS19
]
for general
n
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}
, and in
[
GS22
]
for general
\mathfrak{g}
.

Geometrically,
\odot_{n}
is obtained by gluing
n
copies of
\odot
along boundary intervals, thus by amalgamation, the cluster algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{G,\odot_{n}})
is a subalgebra of the tensor product
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{G,\odot})^{\otimes n}
. For example, one checks that for
n=2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{s,i,\odot_{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{s,i,\odot}\otimes 1+\mathcal{K}_{s,i,\odot}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{s,i,\odot}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{s,i,\odot_{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{s,i,\odot}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{s,i,\odot}

(4.14)

and the formula for general
n
can be derived inductively. Combining with the embedding
\kappa
, we obtain the formula for the coproduct in
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Corollary 4.15
.

\mathcal{W}_{s,i,\odot_{n}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{s,i,\odot_{n}}
remain polynomials in the quantum cluster variables with coefficients in
\mathbb{N}[q^{\pm 1}]
for any cluster in the mutation class.

Proof.

For each
i
, the cluster
\mathcal{X}
-variables appearing in
\mathcal{W}_{s,i,\odot_{n}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{s,i,\odot_{n}}
are still in the configuration of a type
A_{n}
quiver. Therefore, the polynomiality result follows from Lemma
4.8
. The statement about the coefficients again follows from the main theorem of
[
DM21
]
.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Note that the proof for the parabolic case in
4.9
also relies on mutations into polynomials only involving frozen variables, and folding can be applied to each
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{G,\odot})
separately. Therefore, the same result still holds if for some copies of
\odot
, the quiver for
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{G,\odot})
is replaced by the parabolic or zero Casimir variant.

For general marked surfaces, we no longer have the symmetry in
\odot
or
\odot_{n}
, so for any boundary special point
s
, the embedding is restricted to only

\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})\to\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{G,\mathbb{S}}),

where the choice of Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
is determined by the Borel subgroup from the framing at
s

[
GS22
, Theorem 3.21]
. Lemma
4.8
again shows that the image of
\textnormal{{E}}_{i}
and
\textnormal{{K}}_{i}
are also universally polynomial in this case.

As mentioned in
[
GS22
, Remark 15.3]
, the above result can be generalized to arbitrary Kac-Moody
\mathfrak{b}
, where the map is no longer injective and the RHS is defined directly using a quiver without using
G
-local systems. Our polynomiality result extends naturally as well. Furthermore, our result also applies to the rare example
[
Ip20
, Proposition 4.10]
which maps the whole quantum affine algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{n+1})
to certain quantum cluster algebra via the evaluation module.

5
Regular positive representations

After proving Theorem
4.9
, we would like to discuss another conjecture proposed in
[
IM22
, Section 6]
. The first author and Man introduced the notion of
regular positive representations
, which are positive representations
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
that are induced from a homomorphism
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
to the regular functions of some cluster variety
\mathcal{X}
, with a suitable polarization of a quantum torus algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{{s}}})
for some seed
s
.

It was shown in
[
Ip18
]
,
[
Ip20
]
and
[
IM22
]
that the following list of irreducible regular positive representations is known:

(1)

The standard representation
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
,

(2)

The parabolic representation
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{J}
for
W_{J}\subset W
,

(3)

The degenerate representation
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{0,J}
for
W_{J}\subset W
of type
A_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times A_{k_{m}}
,

(4)

The modular double
\mathcal{P}_{\widetilde{\lambda}}^{0,J}
of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{0,J}
,

(5)

A mixture of type (2)-(4) for disconnected subsets of Dynkin indices.

By construction, all these representations stem from the standard representation. It is conjectured in
[
IM22
, Section 6]
that the above list is complete.

To give some intuition, recall the isomorphism

\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{L}_{G,\odot})

for simply laced
\mathfrak{g}
and conjectured for general
\mathfrak{g}
. Based on this isomorphism, it is natural to guess that all irreducible regular positive representations must come from the standard one. However, it is hard to determine if there are other modifications we can do to the standard representation, and generate more irreducible regular positive representations.

Combining Theorem
4.9
, we conjecture that for any irreducible regular positive representation, the homomorphisms
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathcal{X})
must send the Chevalley generators to universal polynomials. Even if there are other representations missed by the list, it is interesting to ask whether universal polynomiality always hold.

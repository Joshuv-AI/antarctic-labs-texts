Adam Martin, and Michael Trott

Abstract
We develop Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT) predictions of σ ⁡ ( 𝒢 ​ 𝒢 → h ) \sigma(\mathcal{G}\,\mathcal{G}\rightarrow h) , Γ ⁡ ( h → 𝒢 ​ 𝒢 ) \Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{G}\,\mathcal{G}) , Γ ⁡ ( h → 𝒜 ​ 𝒜 ) \Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}) to incorporate full two loop Standard Model results at the amplitude level, in conjunction with dimension eight SMEFT corrections. We simultaneously report consistent Γ ⁡ ( h → Ψ ¯ ​ Ψ ) \Gamma(h\rightarrow\bar{\Psi}{\Psi}) results including leading QCD corrections and dimension eight SMEFT corrections. This extends the predictions of the former processes Γ , σ \Gamma,\sigma to a full set of corrections at 𝒪 ⁡ ( v ¯ T 2 / Λ 2 ​ ( 16 ​ π 2 ) 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\bar{v}_{T}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}(16\pi^{2})^{2}) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( v ¯ T 4 / Λ 4 ) \mathcal{O}(\bar{v}_{T}^{4}/\Lambda^{4}) , where v ¯ T \bar{v}_{T} is the electroweak scale vacuum expectation value and Λ \Lambda is the cut off scale of the SMEFT. Throughout, cross consistency between the operator and loop expansions is maintained by the use of the geometric SMEFT formalism. For Γ ⁡ ( h → Ψ ¯ ​ Ψ ) \Gamma(h\rightarrow\bar{\Psi}{\Psi}) , we include results at 𝒪 ⁡ ( v ¯ T 2 / Λ 2 ​ ( 16 ​ π 2 ) ) \mathcal{O}(\bar{v}_{T}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}(16\pi^{2})) in the limit where subleading m Ψ → 0 m_{\Psi}\rightarrow 0 corrections are neglected. We clarify how gauge invariant SMEFT renormalization counterterms combine with the Standard Model counter terms in higher order SMEFT calculations when the Background Field Method is used. We also update the prediction of the total Higgs width in the SMEFT to consistently include some of these higher order perturbative effects.

The operator and loop expansions are not formally independent in SMEFT calculations
– due to scheme dependence introduced by defining conventions in leading order results.
The geoSMEFT and the BFM are, in this sense, fundamentally linked
when theoretical self consistency is demanded to subleading order(s).
The scheme dependence is unsurprising in principle, as higher order perturbative corrections always carry
a significant scheme dependence. However in the case of the SMEFT, scheme dependence is not simply numerical.
Formulated at the Lagrangian level, the SMEFT is based on the
freedom to redefine the theoretical description with operators being removed or introduced by
field redefinitions (or appropriate use of the Equations of Motion).
As such, scheme dependence in the SMEFT is also associated with operator basis dependence,
and the specific parameter dependence present in a calculation. This scheme dependence
is also present in the geoSMEFT, despite its background field independence,
when the field space connections, metrics, etc are expanded out to a

2
Framework of the calculation

Consider the perturbation due to a SMEFT
operator to a dimensionless SM amplitude in an on shell process (such as

\sigma(\mathcal{G}\,\mathcal{G}\rightarrow h)
or
\Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A})
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\langle\cdots\rangle_{SM}+\sum_{i}C^{(6)}_{i}\,\langle\cdots\rangle_{\bar{v}_{T}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}+\cdots

(1)

For three-particle on shell processes, such as
1\rightarrow 2
decays or
2\rightarrow 1
production, derivative terms
\mathcal{O}(\partial^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
are trivial within the SMEFT expansion so all corrections scale with
\bar{v}_{T}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}
. Thus, each amplitude has a series of SMEFT corrections

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}\langle\cdots\rangle_{\bar{v}_{T}^{2n}/\Lambda^{2n}}

(2)

associated with operators in
\mathcal{L}^{(4+2n)}
.

2.1
Terms retained in the calculation(s)

Due to a proliferation of superscripts and subscripts indicating the various
expansions present in these calculations, we introduce a more schematic notation.
Amplitudes are expanded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{A}=\sum_{i,j}\langle\cdots|\cdots\rangle^{i}_{(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})^{j}}.

(3)

We generally use
\Delta
to indicate a loop correction
while a power of
\delta
is used to indicate a SMEFT perturbation
\propto 1/\Lambda^{2}

for more condensed notation, and to track the scaling of cross terms in the expansions.
In this work, we focus on improving the treatment of
\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{0,1,2}
,
and
\langle h|\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}\rangle^{0,1,2}
,
where
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{G},\mathcal{A}\}

compared to Ref.
Corbett:2021cil
.
Each of the terms for the amplitudes in this work scale as

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{SM}

\displaystyle\sim\Delta,

\displaystyle\quad\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{2}_{SM}

\displaystyle\sim\Delta^{2},

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{0}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}

\displaystyle\sim\delta,

\displaystyle\quad\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{0}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{4}/\Lambda^{4})}

\displaystyle\sim\delta^{2},

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}

\displaystyle\sim\delta\,\Delta

\displaystyle\quad\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{2}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}

\displaystyle\sim\Delta^{2}\,\delta,

and so on. Cross terms when the amplitude is squared scale as

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{SM}\,\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}\propto\Delta^{2}\delta.

(4)

In this work, we include the corrections
\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{2}_{SM}

and
\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{2}_{SM}\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{0}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}\propto\Delta^{2}\delta,

(5)

which should be retained for consistency at the amplitude squared level
when
\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{SM}

\times\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}

terms are retained, as in Ref.
Corbett:2020ymv
. We retain the terms that scale as
\delta
,
\Delta
,
\delta^{2}
,
\Delta^{2}
,
\delta\,\Delta

in the amplitude expansion in this work. Note that
\Delta,\Delta^{2}
terms are pure SM terms. We retain the SM cross terms of order

\Delta^{2}
,
\Delta^{3}
in the amplitude squared. As well as terms of order
\delta\,\Delta
,
\delta\,\Delta^{2}
,
\delta^{2}
,
\delta^{2}\,\Delta

for the SMEFT corrections in the amplitude squared. All other higher order terms are consistently dropped.

Note that
when constructing the interference term, one could choose to numerically retain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{2}_{SM}\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}\propto\Delta^{3}\delta.

(6)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{1}_{\mathcal{O}(v^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}\langle\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}|h\rangle^{2}_{SM}\propto\Delta^{3}\delta.

Djouadi:1997yw
;
Djouadi:2018xqq
, we modify our finite terms as follows.
As verified in Ref.
Corbett:2021cil
, the finite terms of the charge and wavefunction renormalization
are related by the preserved
\rm QED
Ward identity to be

\displaystyle\Delta Z_{e}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta Z_{\hat{\mathcal{A}}},

\displaystyle\Delta R_{e}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta R_{\hat{\mathcal{A}}}.

(80)

We extend
\Delta R_{e}
and

\Delta R_{\hat{\mathcal{A}}}
by finite terms to cancel the effect of the
running through the hadronic resonance region. Explicitly,
\Delta R_{e}

is defined at one loop to be
Sirlin:1980nh
;
Dekens:2019ept
;
Corbett:2021cil

\displaystyle\Delta R_{e}=\frac{\bar{g}_{1}^{2}\bar{g}_{2}^{2}}{(\bar{g}_{1}^{2}+\bar{g}_{2}^{2})}\,\left[\frac{7}{32\pi^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m_{W}^{2}}\right)-\frac{N^{f}_{c}Q^{2}_{f}}{24\pi^{2}}\,\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}_{f}^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{48\pi^{2}}\right],

(81)

and the charge renormalization is related to the Thompson limit measured value by

\displaystyle-i\,\left[\frac{4\,\pi\,\hat{\alpha}(q^{2})}{q^{2}}\right]_{q^{2}\rightarrow 0}\equiv\frac{-i\,(e_{0}+\Delta R_{e})^{2}}{q^{2}}\left[1+{\rm Re}\frac{\Sigma^{AA}(m_{Z}^{2})}{m_{Z}^{2}}+\nabla\alpha\right].

(82)

Here
\nabla\alpha
includes corrections form QCD bound states (see Table.
1
)

Wells:2005vk
;
Agashe:2014kda
;
Baikov:2012zm
;
PhysRevD.22.971
and
\Sigma^{AA}(\bar{m}_{Z}^{2})
is given explicitly in
Ref.
Corbett:2021cil
. Now, redefining

\displaystyle\Delta R_{e}\rightarrow\Delta R_{e}+e_{0}\left({\rm Re}\frac{\Sigma^{AA}(m_{Z}^{2})}{m_{Z}^{2}}+\nabla\alpha\right)+\cdots

(83)

numerically absorbs the effect of running through the Hadronic resonance region into the finite renormalization
of the electric charge. So long as the Ward identity derived relation for finite terms

\Delta R_{e}=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta R_{\hat{\mathcal{A}}}
is imposed, this leads to the cancelation of the
numerical effects of running through the hadronic resonance region in
\Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A})

in the (so-defined)
\left\{\alpha(0),\hat{M}_{W},\hat{G}_{F}\right\}
input scheme. For further discussion see Refs.
Denner:2019vbn
;
Dittmaier:2021loa
.

The SM predictions from
Hdecay
are produced in the effective
\left\{\alpha(0),\hat{M}_{W},\hat{G}_{F}\right\}

scheme. While the
\left\{\hat{M}_{Z},\hat{M}_{W},\hat{G}_{F}\right\}
scheme is used in
in global studies

;
Almeida:2021asy
;
Ethier:2021bye

This leads to an important numerical shift in the central value of the SM prediction compared to a

\left\{\alpha(\hat{M}_{Z}),\hat{M}_{W},\hat{G}_{F}\right\}

This numerical difference should be noted given that, at leading order,

\Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A})\propto\alpha_{ew}^{2}

\displaystyle(\alpha_{ew}^{\alpha(0)})^{2}

\displaystyle=5.33\times 10^{-5},\quad

\displaystyle(\alpha_{ew}^{\alpha(\hat{m}_{Z})})^{2}

\displaystyle=6.01\times 10^{-5},\quad

\displaystyle(\alpha_{ew}^{\hat{m}_{W}})^{2}

\displaystyle=5.72\times 10^{-5}.

(84)

As the perturbations (or lack of perturbations) in
\Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A})
numerically
is quite dominant in global SMEFT fits, numerical consistency on this issue is critical for precise constraints.
In what follows we present results in the
\left\{\alpha(0),\hat{M}_{W},\hat{G}_{F}\right\}

\left\{\alpha(\hat{M}_{Z}),\hat{M}_{W},\hat{G}_{F}\right\}

and
\left\{\hat{M}_{Z},\hat{M}_{W},\hat{G}_{F}\right\}

\Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A})

Scheme dependence is minimal in the observables
\sigma(\mathcal{G}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow h)

Conclusions

In this paper we have advanced the results in the geoSMEFT formulation of the SMEFT for
\sigma(\mathcal{G}\,\mathcal{G}\rightarrow h)
,

\Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{G}\,\mathcal{G})
,
\Gamma(h\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A})
,
\Gamma(h\rightarrow\bar{\psi}\,\psi)
, and the total Higgs width. Previous literature
Alonso:2013hga
;
Brivio:2017vri
;
Brivio:2017btx
;
Helset:2020yio
;
Brivio:2020onw
;
Corbett:2021cil
;
Brivio:2019myy
has provided terms in the SMEFT
\times
loop expansion of orders
\mathcal{O}(\bar{v}^{2}_{T}/\Lambda^{2}),\mathcal{O}(\bar{v}^{2}_{T}/\Lambda^{2}(16\pi^{2}))
and
\mathcal{O}(\bar{v}^{4}_{T}/\Lambda^{4})
. This work extends the expansion to
\mathcal{O}(\bar{v}^{2}_{T}/\Lambda^{2}(16\pi^{2})^{2})
by consistently including the interference of two-loop (NLO in QCD) SM amplitudes with
\mathcal{O}(\bar{v}^{2}_{T}/\Lambda^{2})
SMEFT terms. Additionally, we have incorporated a set of QCD loop corrections determined previously in
Braaten:1980yq
;
Gauld:2016kuu

into the characterization of
\Gamma(h\rightarrow\bar{\psi}\,\psi)
. Combining these updated results, we determine the leading loop correction to the Higgs total width.
We have also characterized a more consistent numerical treatment of input parameter choices and effects,
updating past numerical results.

A.2

The manner in which the ultraviolet (UV) divergences of the SMEFT combine with those of the SM is subtle.
The counterterm induced modifications in results depend on the renormalization scheme used.

that can rise to level of the deviations being searched for and interpreted.
We specify our scheme for combining SMEFT and SM counterterms in some detail here, along with modifications of SM results.

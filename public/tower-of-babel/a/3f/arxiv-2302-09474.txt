Orbifold theory for vertex algebras and Galois correspondence
Chongying Dong, Li Ren, Chao Yang

Abstract
Let V V be a simple vertex algebra of countable dimension, G G be a finite automorphism group of V V and σ \sigma be a central element of G G . Assume that 𝒮 \cal S is a finite set of inequivalent irreducible σ \sigma -twisted V V -modules such that 𝒮 \cal S is invariant under the action of G G . Hence, for any

\displaystyle Y_{M}(u_{n}v^{1},z_{2})w^{1}+\cdots+Y_{M}(u_{n}v^{s},z_{2})w^{s}=0.

(3.1)

Let

.
Define a linear mapping

by

and
f(v_{i})=0
for any

Since

is a simple
\mathcal{U}(V)
-module of countable dimension,
by Corollary
3.2
and Corollary

, there exist some

and

such that

(u^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+u^{p}_{i_{p}})v^{1}=v^{1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ (u^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+u^{p}_{i_{p}})v^{i}=0,\ \ \text{for}\ \ i\neq 1.

By (
3.1
), we obtain
Y_{M}(v^{1},z_{2})w^{1}=0
.
Similarly, using weak associativity again, we see that

.
Since

The proof is complete.
∎

In this section, we introduce dual pairs in the vertex algebra setting.

Definition 4.1

-module.

The definition of

-module isomorphism and simple

Remark 4.2
.

In the following sections, we will let

be a group algebra or it’s generalization and let

be a fixed point vertex algebra

.

Note that for an

-module

-module

-module
by defining

and

for

and

.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional semisimple associative algebra.
Let

be the set of all irreducible characters of

.
For
\lambda\in\Lambda

.

Assume that

-module.
Let

be the sum of all

-submodules of

isomorphic to

.
Since the actions of

-submodule of

.
Hence,

Let

be the multiplicity space of

in

.
Note that

has a

\displaystyle(v_{n}f)(w)=v_{n}(f(w))

(4.1)

for

and

in the following way: Let

Define a linear mapping

.
Now, we have the following Lemma.

.

The mapping

-module isomorphism.

Proof.

It is well known that

is a linear isomorphism (see also

, Proposition 4.1.15]
).
It is easy to verify that

-module homomorphism. The proof is complete.
∎

By the discussion above,

, as an

-module, has the following decomposition:

Definition 4.4
.

Let

be a finite dimensional semisimple associative algebra and let

be a vertex algebra.
If

-module such that

(2)

Remark 4.5
.

Let

be an

-module.
Denote by

to

Remark 4.6
.

Assume that

forms a dual pair on

Since

is finite dimensional for any

The following result is the key for the further discussions.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional semisimple associative algebra and let
V
be a vertex algebra.
Let
M
be an
A\otimes V
-module. Assume that for any finite dimensional
A
-submodule
X
of
M
and any
f\in\text{Hom}_{A}(X,M)
,
there exist
v^{1},\cdots,v^{n}\in V
and
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

f=v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}}.

Then

(1)

M_{\lambda}
is an irreducible
V
-module for any
\lambda\in\text{Irr}_{M}(A),

(2)

M_{\lambda}\cong M_{\mu}
if and only if
\lambda=\mu.

Proof.

(1) Let
\lambda\in\text{Irr}_{M}(A)
and
x,y\in M_{\lambda}
be nonzero.
We need to find
v^{1},\cdots,v^{n}\in V
and
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
y=(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})x
. Consequently,
M_{\lambda}
is an irreducible
V
-module.

Let
X=W_{\lambda}\otimes x
. Then
X
is a finite dimensional
A
-submodule of
M
.
Define a mapping
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(X,M)
by
f(w\otimes x)=w\otimes y
for
w\in W_{\lambda}
.
It is claer that
f\in\text{Hom}_{A}(X,M)
.
Then, there exist
v^{1},\cdots,v^{n}\in V
and
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

f=v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}}.

Thus we have

w\otimes y=f(w\otimes x)=(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})(w\otimes x)=w\otimes(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})x,

for any

. This implies that
(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})x=y
, as required.

(2) Let
\lambda,\mu\in\text{Irr}_{M}(A)
and
\lambda\neq\mu
. Assume that

\phi:M_{\lambda}\to M_{\mu}
be a
V
-module isomorphism. Let

Then
0\neq y=\phi(x)\in M_{\mu}.
Set

which is an
A
-submodule of

Define

such that
f(w_{1}\otimes x+w_{2}\otimes y)=w_{2}\otimes y
for
w_{1}\in W_{\lambda}
and

w_{2}\in W_{\mu}.
Then there exist
v^{1},\cdots,v^{n}\in V
and
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

f=v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}}.

Then
(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})x=0
and
(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})y=y.

So

0=\phi((v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})x)=(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})\phi(x)=(v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}})y=y,

a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

Let

Let

Assume that (1)

of

Note that

. Let

\displaystyle=\text{Hom}_{A}(\oplus_{\lambda\in\text{Irr}_{M}(A)}W_{\lambda}\otimes N_{\lambda},\oplus_{\lambda\in\text{Irr}_{M}(A)}W_{\lambda}\otimes M_{\lambda})

Let

Then

Let

. Define a linear mapping

\varphi_{n}(v^{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{1}\otimes v^{0})=Y(v^{n},z_{n})\cdots Y(v^{1},z_{1})v^{0}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The mapping
\varphi_{n}
is an injective
G
-homomorphism.

Proof.

Since

, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(g(v^{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{1}\otimes v^{0}))

\displaystyle=Y(gv^{n},z_{n})\cdots Y(gv^{1},z_{1})gv^{0}

\displaystyle=g(Y(v^{n},z_{n})\cdots Y(v^{1},z_{1})v^{0})

\displaystyle=g\varphi_{n}(v^{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{1}\otimes v^{0})

for

That is,
\varphi_{n}
is a
G
-homomorphism.

Now we prove that
\varphi_{n}
is injective by induction on

(see also
[
Li1
]
).
Assume that
v^{1}\otimes\alpha_{1}+\cdots+v^{s}\otimes\alpha_{s}\in\text{Ker}(\varphi_{n})
,
where

are linearly independent and

Then

By Lemma

, we obtain
\varphi_{n-1}(\alpha_{1})=\cdots=\varphi_{n-1}(\alpha_{s})=0.

By induction, we have
\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{s}=0
. Hence
\text{Ker}(\varphi_{n})=\{0\}
, as required.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Every irreducible
G
-module appears in

.

Proof.

Let

be any irreducible

-module.
By Corollary

,

for some

.
If

as

D({\cal O}_{M})\otimes V^{G}
-module.
By the discussion in section 4,

\text{Hom}_{D({\cal O}_{M})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}M)

is a
V^{G}
-module.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
W
be a simple
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]
-module, then

\text{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]}(W,M)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \text{Hom}_{{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,{\cal M})

are isomorphic
V^{G}
-modules.

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps.

Claim 1:

\text{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]}(W,M)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \text{Hom}_{D({\cal O}_{M})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}M)

are isomorphic
V^{G}
-modules.

Define a mapping

\theta:\text{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]}(W,M)\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{D({\cal O}_{M})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}M)

by
\theta(f)=\text{Id}\otimes f
for
f\in\text{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]}(W,M)
.
Since the functor
D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}(-)
is an equivalence of categories by Theorem
7.1
,
\theta
is a linear isomorphic.
Moreover, for any
v\in V^{G},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\otimes w\in D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W
, we have

\displaystyle(v_{n}\theta(f))(x\otimes w)=(v_{n}(\text{Id}\otimes f))(x\otimes w))

\displaystyle=v_{n}((\text{Id}\otimes f)(x\otimes w))=v_{n}(x\otimes f(w))=x\otimes v_{n}f(w);

and

\theta(v_{n}f)(x\otimes w)=(\text{Id}\otimes v_{n}f)(x\otimes w)=x\otimes(v_{n}f)(w)=x\otimes v_{n}f(w).

Hence
\theta
is a
V^{G}
-module isomorphism, as required.

We can also see the claim directly from Theorem
6.7
with
G
replaced by
G_{M}.

We can assume that
W=W_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{M}.
Then
\text{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]}(W,M)=M_{\lambda}
is an irreducible
V^{G_{M}}
-module and
M=\oplus_{\mu\in\Lambda_{M}}W_{\mu}\otimes M_{\mu}.
So

D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}M=\oplus_{\mu\in\Lambda_{M}}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W_{\mu})\otimes M_{\mu}.

From Theorem
7.1
we know that
D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W_{\mu}
are inequivalent simple
D({\cal O}_{M})
-modules, the claim follows.

Claim 2:
D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}M
and
\bigoplus_{X\in{\cal O}_{M}}X
are isomorphic
V^{G}
-modules .

Define a mapping

\rho:D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}M\rightarrow\bigoplus_{X\in{\cal O}_{M}}X

by

\rho((g\otimes e(M))\otimes x)=\phi_{M}(g)(x)

for
g\in G,x\in M
.
By the proof of Theorem
7.1
(2),
\rho
is an
{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})
-module isomorphism (see also
[
DY
, Proposition 6.2]
).
Since
v_{n}\phi_{M}(g)=\phi_{M}(g)v_{n}
for any
g\in G,v\in V^{G}
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we see that
\rho
is a
V^{G}
-module isomorphism, as required.

Claim 3:

\text{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]}(W,M)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \text{Hom}_{{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,{\cal M})

are isomorphic
V^{G}
-modules.

Note that if
N\notin{\cal O}_{M}
, by Theorem
7.1
(3) and Claim 2, we have

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,\bigoplus_{X\in{\cal O}_{N}}X)

\displaystyle\cong\text{Hom}_{{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,D(N)\otimes_{S(N)}N)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence, as
V^{G}
-modules, we have

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]}(W,M)

\displaystyle\cong\text{Hom}_{D({\cal O}_{M})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}M)

\displaystyle\cong\text{Hom}_{D({\cal O}_{M})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,\bigoplus_{X\in{\cal O}_{M}}X)

\displaystyle\cong\text{Hom}_{{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})}(D(M)\otimes_{S(M)}W,{\cal M}),

as expected.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
X
be a finite dimensional
{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})
-submodule of
{\cal M}
and let
f
be any element of

\text{Hom}_{{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})}(X,{\cal M})
.
Then there exist
v^{1},\cdots,v^{n}\in V^{G}
and
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

f=v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}}.

Proof.

Let
X_{M}=(1\otimes e(M))X
for
M\in{\cal S}.
Then
X_{M}\subseteq X\cap M
and
X=\bigoplus_{M\in{\cal S}}X_{M}.
For any
x\in X
we see that
f((1\otimes e(M))x)=(1\otimes e(M))f(x)\in M.
That is,
f(X_{M})\subseteq M
for any
M\in{\cal S}
.
Consequently,

f\in\bigoplus_{M\in{\cal S}}\text{Hom}(X_{M},M).

Note that
{\cal M}
is a direct sum of inequivalent irreducible
\mathcal{U}(V[\sigma])
-modules of countable dimensions.
By Corollary
3.3
and Corollary
2.5
, there exist some
u^{1},\cdots,u^{n}\in V
and
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}\in\frac{1}{T}{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

f=u^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+u^{n}_{i_{n}}.

Since
f\in\text{Hom}_{{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})}(X,{\cal M})
,
for any
g\in G
and any
M\in{\cal S}
, we have
(g\otimes e(M\cdot g))f=f(g\otimes e(M\cdot g))
.
Consequently,
\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)f=f\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)
for any
g\in G
and
M\in{\cal S}
.
For any
g\in G
and
M\in{\cal S}
, restricting
f
to
M
, by (
7.1
), we see that

\displaystyle f=\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)f\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)^{-1}=\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)u^{1}_{i_{1}}\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)^{-1}+\cdots+\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)u^{n}_{i_{n}}\phi_{M\cdot g}(g)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(gu^{1})_{i_{1}}+\cdots+(gu^{n})_{i_{n}}.

For
i=1,\cdots,n
, set
v^{i}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}(gu^{i})\in V^{G}
. Now, we have

f=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}((gu^{1})_{i_{1}}+\cdots+(gu^{n})_{i_{n}})=v^{1}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v^{n}_{i_{n}}.

If
i_{k}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
k
, since
v^{k}\in V^{G}
, we have
v^{k}_{i_{k}}=0
.
Hence, we can assume that
i_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
k
, as required.
∎

Recall that
{\cal S}=\cup_{j\in J}{\cal O}_{j}
and
M^{j}
is a representation of
{\cal O}_{j}.

For convenience, we let
G_{j}=G_{M^{j}},

\Lambda_{j}
be the set of irreducible characters
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M^{j}}}[G_{M^{j}}],

W_{j,\lambda}
be the irreducible
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M^{j}}}[G_{M^{j}}]
-module with character
\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}
,
M_{\lambda}^{j}
be the multiplicity space of
W_{j,\lambda}
in
M

and

W^{j}_{\lambda}=\mbox{Ind}^{D(M^{j})}_{S(M^{j})}W_{j,\lambda}.
Then

M^{j}

has the following decomposition:

M^{j}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}W_{j,\lambda}\otimes M^{j}_{\lambda}

as
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M^{j}}}[G_{j}]\otimes V^{G_{j}}
-module and each
M^{j}_{\lambda}
is an irreducible
V^{G_{j}}
-module by Theorem
6.7
. By the discussion above and Lemma
7.2
,
\cal M

has the following decomposition

{\cal M}=\bigoplus_{j\in J,\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}W^{j}_{\lambda}\otimes M^{j}_{\lambda}

as
{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})\otimes V^{G}
-module.

Finally, we have

Theorem 7.4
.

The
{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})\otimes V^{G}
-module decomposition

{\cal M}=\bigoplus_{j\in J,\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}W^{j}_{\lambda}\otimes M^{j}_{\lambda}

gives a dual pair
({\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S}),V^{G})
on
{\cal M}:

(1)

M^{j}_{\lambda}
is nonzero for any
j\in J
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}
.

(2)

Each
M^{j}_{\lambda}
is an irreducible
V^{G}
-module.

(3)

M^{j_{1}}_{\lambda_{1}}
and
M^{j_{2}}_{\lambda_{2}}
are isomorphic

V^{G}
-modules if and only if
j_{1}=j_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
.

In particular, all irreducible
\sigma
-twisted
V
module is a direct sum of finitely many
irreducible
V^{G}
-modules.

Proof.

(1) follows from Theorem
6.7
(2) immediately. (2) and (3) follow form Lemma
7.3
and Theorem
4.7
.
∎

.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
M
be an irreducible
\sigma
-twisted
V
-module.
Then
M
is an irreducible
V^{G}
-module if and only if
G_{M}=\{1\}.

Proof.

Let
{\cal S}=\{M\cdot g\ |\ g\in G\}.
Note that
{\cal A}_{\alpha}(G,{\cal S})=D({\cal O}_{M})
. It follows from Theorem
7.4
that
M
is irreducible
V^{G}
-module if and only if
{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha_{M}}[G_{M}]\cong{\mathbb{C}}
which is clearly equivalent to that
G_{M}=\{1\}.

∎

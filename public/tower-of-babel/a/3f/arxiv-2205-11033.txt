Augmented Newton Method for Optimization: Global Linear Rate and Momentum Interpretation
Md Sarowar Morshed

Abstract
We propose two variants of Newton method for solving unconstrained minimization problem. Our method leverages optimization techniques such as penalty and augmented Lagrangian method to generate novel variants of the Newton method namely the Penalty Newton method and the Augmented Newton method. In doing so, we recover several well-known existing Newton method variants such as Damped Newton , Levenberg , and Levenberg-Marquardt methods as special cases. Moreover, the proposed Augmented Newton method can be interpreted as Newton method with adaptive heavy ball momentum. We provide global convergence results for the proposed methods under mild assumptions that hold for a wide variety of problems. The proposed methods can be sought as the penalty and augmented extensions of the results obtained by Karimireddy et. al [ 24 ] .

Introduction

In this work, we consider solving the following unconstrained minimization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x),

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

Although, Newton’s method is originally proposed in the context of finding roots of polynomial equations, from the last century it has been recognised as one of the fundamental second order methods for solving optimization problems. In the following, we discuss the historical developments of the Newton method as well as the modern developments of Newton method variants.

Historical developments

The first occurrence of Newton type method can be traced back to the work of Persian astronomer and mathematician Al-Kashi (1380–1429). In his seminal work
’The Key to Arithmetic’
published in 1427, he discussed a variant of Newton method that was based on the earlier works by the famous polymath Al-Biruni (973–1048) and mathematician Al-Tusi (1135-1213). The work of Al-Kashi remained hidden to the western scientific community until the work of Francois Vieta (1540-1603). In 1600, Vieta rediscovered a similar technique like Al-Kashi’s in the context of solving scalar polynomial equations of degree six
[
45
]
. Vieta’s method gained considerable attention by the work of Sir Isaac Newton (1643–1727) who improved Vieta’s method immensely and developed a closely related method
[

’De analysi per aequationes numero terminorum infinitas’
(1669, published in 1711) and
’De metodis fluxionum et serierum infinitarum’
(1671, published in 1736). Based on the work of Newton, Joseph Raphson (1648-1715) in 1690 proposed a closely related method for finding roots of polynomial equations. However, the connection between Newton method and function derivative was unknown until the later work of Thomas Simpson (1710-1761). In 1740, Simpson proposed a closely related variant of Newton method which was the earliest method that resembles the modern Newton method most

During early 1900s, the works of Bennet

1.1
Newton Method

The full Newton method for solving problem (
1
) can be expressed as the following update formula:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\left(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})\right)^{\dagger}\nabla f(x_{k}).

(2)

A more general form of the update is the so-called
Damped Newton
(DN) method that can expressed as follows:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-t\left(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})\right)^{\dagger}\nabla f(x_{k}).

(3)

An standard way of choosing step size
t
is by backtracking line search which goes as follows: start with
t=1
and
0<\alpha\leq 1/2
,
0<\beta<1
and check the following:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})>f(x_{k})-\alpha t\nabla f(x_{k})^{\top}\left(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})\right)^{\dagger}\nabla f(x_{k}).

The quantity
\left(\nabla f(x_{k})^{\top}\left(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})\right)^{\dagger}\nabla f(x_{k})\right)^{1/2}
is known as the
Newton Decrement
. It is well-known that the above method with backtracking line search achieve quadratic convergence rate under some nice assumptions on function
f(x)
.

Contributions

In this paper, we make the following fundamental contributions:

•

New variants of Newton method.
We propose two variants of Newton method namely the
Penalty Newton Method
(PNM) and
Augmented Newton Method
(ANM) by incorporating the Lagrangian penalty function based techniques on the Newton system (
16

•

Special cases.
From our proposed algorithms we recover well-known algorithms such as

and
Levenberg-Marquardt

•

Momentum interpretation.
Moreover, the proposed ANM algorithm can be interpreted as Newton method with adaptive heavy ball momentum.

Our second approach uses the idea of the augmented Lagrangian method for optimization. The method was proposed by Hestenes in 1969

\displaystyle x_{k+1}\in

\displaystyle\argmin_{x}\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}(x_{k})}+\frac{\rho}{2}\Big\|\mathbf{H}(x_{k})(x-x_{k})+\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})\Big\|^{2}_{G^{-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\textbf{subject to}\qquad\mathbf{H}(x_{k})(x-x_{k})+\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})=0.

(21)

The Lagrangian of the above problem is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,z,\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}(x_{k})}

\displaystyle+z^{\top}\left[\mathbf{H}(x_{k})(x-x_{k})+\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho}{2}\Big\|\mathbf{H}(x_{k})(x-x_{k})+\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})\Big\|^{2}_{G^{-1}}.

(22)

Then, we set the ANM update formulas as follows:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}\in\argmin_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,z_{k},\rho),\quad z_{k+1}=z_{k}+\rho\ G^{-1}\left[\mathbf{H}(x_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})\right].

(23)

Algorithm 2

x_{K+1}=\textbf{ANM}(G,c,f(x),K)

Choose initial points
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \rho_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

while

k\leq K

do

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right],\quad\rho_{k+1}=c\rho_{k};

k\leftarrow k+1
;

end

while

Lemma 8
.

Denote,
u(x_{k},z_{k})=\frac{\rho}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})+Gz_{k}
. Then, the following update formulas solve the Lagrangian system of (
23
):

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\ u(x_{k},z_{k})=x_{k}-z_{k+1},\quad z_{k+1}=\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\ u(x_{k},z_{k}).

(24)

Moreover, the following update solves the Lagrangian system of (
23
):

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\left(\frac{1}{\rho}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\rho}G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right].

(25)

Proof.

Setting
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}(x,z_{k},\rho)}{\partial x}=0
, we get

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}(x,z_{k},\rho)}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{H}(x_{k})+\rho\mathbf{H}(x_{k})G^{-1}\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right](x-x_{k})+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\left(\frac{\rho}{L}G^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k})+z_{k}\right).

(26)

Using the value of
x_{k+1}
from (
24
) in place of
x
, we get the following:

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{H}(x_{k})+\rho\mathbf{H}(x_{k})G^{-1}\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right]

\displaystyle(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\overset{\eqref{als:an:25}}{=}-\frac{1}{\rho}\left[\mathbf{H}(x_{k})+\rho\mathbf{H}(x_{k})G^{-1}\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right]\mathbf{K}(x_{k})u(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:15}}{=}-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\mathbf{K}(x_{k})u(x_{k})-\mathbf{H}(x_{k})G^{-1}\left(I-\frac{1}{\rho}G\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\right)u(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\left(\frac{\rho}{L}G^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k})+z_{k}\right).

(27)

Substituting the expression in (
26
), we get that
x_{k+1}
satisfies the equation (
26
). Now, from the definition of
z_{k+1}
, we have

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:24}}{=}z_{k}+\rho G^{-1}\left[\mathbf{H}(x_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:25}}{=}z_{k}+\rho G^{-1}\left[-\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\mathbf{K}(x_{k})u(x_{k},z_{k})+\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:15}}{=}z_{k}+\frac{\rho}{L}G^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k})-G^{-1}\left(I-\frac{1}{\rho}G\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\right)u(x_{k},z_{k})=\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\ u(x_{k},z_{k}).

That is precisely the expression provided in (
24
). This proves the first part of the Lemma. To prove the second part, we will simplify the update formula further by cancelling the
z
variables from the update formulas. Since,
z_{k+1}=x_{k}-x_{k+1}
and
z_{k}=x_{k-1}-x_{k}
, we can combine the update formulas as follows:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}\overset{\eqref{als:an:24}}{=}

\displaystyle x_{k}-\left(\frac{1}{\rho}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})+\frac{1}{\rho}Gz_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\ x_{k}-\left(\frac{1}{\rho}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\rho}G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right].

(28)

This proves the second part.

3.3
Special Cases

In this subsection, we discuss some special cases that can be obtained from the proposed PNM and ANM algorithms by varying parameters
G
and
\rho
.

Newton Method

By taking
\rho\rightarrow\infty
in both the PNM and ANM algorithms, we get the Newton method. In the following Lemma, we provide a detailed discussion about the transformation.

Lemma 9
.

In the limiting case, both PNM and ANM resolves into the Newton method, i.e.,
\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\text{PNM}\equiv\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\text{ANM}\equiv\text{NM}
.

Proof.

From the update formula of the PNM method, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:19}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Assumption}\ \ref{als:an:a:2}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k}).

(29)

In the last line, we used Assumption
2
. Denote,
Y_{k}=\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:30}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+Y_{k}^{\top}Y_{k}\right)^{-1}Y^{\top}_{k}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:310}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}Y^{\dagger}_{k}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k})\overset{\eqref{als:an:310}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}(Y^{\top}_{k}Y_{k})^{\dagger}Y^{\top}_{k}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}G^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(G^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}(x_{k})G^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\dagger}G^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Assumption}\ \ref{als:an:a:2}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k}).

(30)

Here, we used Assumption
2
along with the following identities of matrix pseudo-inverse:

Y^{\dagger}_{k}=(Y_{k}^{\top}Y_{k})^{\dagger}Y_{k}^{\top}=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+Y^{\top}_{k}Y_{k}\right)^{-1}Y_{k}^{\top}.

(31)

The update formula of (
3.3
) is precisely the update formula of the Newton method. Moreover, from the update formula of the ANM method, we get,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:29}}{=}x_{k}-\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\left[\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\rho}G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right]=x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{K}(x_{k})\nabla f(x_{k}).

(32)

Here we used the limit
\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\left[\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})-\frac{1}{\rho}G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right]=\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k})
. This means the
x
update sequences of the two methods are same in the limiting case. This proves the Lemma.

Levenberg
\&
Augmented Levenberg Algorithms

With the choice
G=I
, the PNM algorithm resolves into the original
Levenberg
algorithm, i.e.,

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:19}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k}).

(33)

Similarly, substituting
G=I
in the ANM algorithm, we get the following update:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:29}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k})+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}).

(34)

We refer to the above method as the
Augmented Levenberg
algorithm.

Levenberg-Marquardt
\&
Augmented Levenberg-Marquardt Algorithms

With the choice
G=\textbf{diag}(\mathbf{H}(x_{k}))
in the PNM algorithm we get the following update:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:19}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\textbf{diag}(\mathbf{H}(x_{k}))+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k}).

(35)

This is precisely the
Levenberg-Marquardt
variant of the Newton method. Similarly, substituting
G=\textbf{diag}(\mathbf{H}(x_{k}))
in the ANM algorithm, we get the following update:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:29}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{D}_{k}+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k})+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbf{D}_{k}+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}).

(36)

here, we denote
\mathbf{D}_{k}=\textbf{diag}(\mathbf{H}(x_{k}))
. We refer to the above method as the
Augmented Levenberg-Marquardt
algorithm. Using the above parameter values in Theorems
15
and
16
, we get the respective convergence results of the above-mentioned methods.

Newton Method With Momentum

Next, we discuss the ANM algorithm from a heavy ball/Polyak momentum perspective. Note that, from the simplified update formula of the ANM algorithm provided in (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:29}}{=}x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k})+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k})+\Theta(x_{k})(x_{k}-x_{k-1}).

(37)

The above update can be interpreted as Newton method with adaptive heavy ball/Polyak momentum with
\Theta(x_{k})=\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}G+\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}G
. To the best of our knowledge this is the first variant of Newton method that incorporates the heavy ball/Polyak momentum update to the Newton method.

Penalty & Augmented Newton Method for Root Finding

Let’s take the equation
f(x)=0,\ f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Denote
f^{{}^{\prime}}(x)
as the function derivative of
f
, then the proposed methods take the following form:

\displaystyle\textbf{Penalty Newton: }\quad x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\frac{\rho\ f(x_{k})}{1+\rho\ f^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{k})}

\displaystyle\textbf{Augmented Newton: }\quad x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\frac{\rho\ f(x_{k})}{1+\rho\ f^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{k})}+\frac{x_{k}-x_{k-1}}{1+\rho\ f^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{k})}

Remark 10
.

Note that in Algorithms
1
and
2
, we provided adaptive versions of the above methods. In that regard, we gradually increase the penalty parameter
\rho
in a way such that
\rho_{k}
grows larger as the iteration progresses. We used the simplest update possible to achieve this, i.e.,
\rho_{k+1}=c\rho_{k}
and replace
\rho
with
\rho_{k}
is the PNM and ANM methods.

4
Main Results

We now present the convergence results for the proposed variants of Newton method. In brief, for the PNM algorithm we show that
f(x_{k})\rightarrow f^{*}
holds and for the ANM method we show that both
f(x_{k})\rightarrow f^{*}
and
\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{G}\rightarrow 0
hold. To achieve this we define the following sequence:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k}(\rho)=f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})+\frac{L}{2\rho}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{G},

(38)

for any
k\geq 1
, and
\rho>0
. We will show that for the ANM algorithm,
\mathcal{V}_{k}(\rho)
is a Lyapunov function. Before we delved into the convergence results, let us provide some technical Lemmas that are crucial to the convergence analysis. First, define the following constant:

\displaystyle\xi(x)=\min_{v\in\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x))}\frac{\langle\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)v,v\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}},\quad\ \xi=\min_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{\rho}}\xi(x).

(39)

Constant
\xi(x)
can be interpreted as the condition number of matrix
\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)
.

Lemma 11
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, matrices
\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)
and
G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}
have the same nonzero eigenvalues, i.e.,
\lambda_{i}^{+}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\right)=\lambda_{i}^{+}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
.

Proof.

Let,
Y=\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Assume
Y
has the following singular value decomposition, i.e.,
Y=U\Sigma V^{\top}
. Then we have the following simplifications:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:4}}{=}Y\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+Y^{\top}Y\right)^{-1}Y^{\top}=U\Sigma\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma\right)^{-1}\Sigma^{\top}U^{\top},

(40)

\displaystyle G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:4}}{=}Y^{\top}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+YY^{\top}\right)^{-1}Y=V\Sigma^{\top}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}I+\Sigma\Sigma^{\top}\right)^{-1}\Sigma V^{\top}.

(41)

Since,
U
and
V
are orthogonal matrices from the above expressions we get the following:

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{+}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\right)\overset{\eqref{als:an:40}}{=}\frac{\rho\lambda^{+}_{i}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{1+\rho\lambda^{+}_{i}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\overset{\eqref{als:an:41}}{=}\lambda_{i}^{+}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

(42)

These resolve from the fact that both
U
and
V
are orthogonal matrices. Also, both
\Sigma(1/\rho\ I+\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma)^{-1}\Sigma^{\top}
and
\Sigma^{\top}(1/\rho\ I+\Sigma\Sigma^{\top})^{-1}\Sigma
are diagonal matrices with
\frac{\rho\lambda^{+}_{i}\left(Y^{\top}Y\right)}{1+\rho\lambda^{+}_{i}\left(Y^{\top}Y\right)}
be the nonzero diagonal entries in
i^{th}
position. This proves the Lemma.

Lemma 12
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x))=\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)),

that implies the following:

\displaystyle\xi(x)=\lambda_{\min}^{+}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\right)=\min_{i:\sigma_{i}(x)>0}\frac{\rho\sigma^{2}_{i}(x)}{1+\rho\sigma^{2}_{i}(x)}=\frac{\rho\lambda^{+}_{\min}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{1+\rho\lambda^{+}_{\min}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)},

(43)

where,
\sigma_{i}(x)=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)G^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\right)}=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}(x)G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)}
.

Proof.

Since,
\mathbf{K}(x)\succ 0
, we have the following relation:

\displaystyle\textbf{Null}(\mathbf{K}(x))\subset\textbf{Null}(\mathbf{H}(x))=\textbf{Null}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)).

(44)

Now, applying Lemma
6
with
N(x)=\mathbf{K}(x)
and
M(x)=\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)
, we get the following:

\displaystyle\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x))=\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x))=\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)).

(45)

Then, from the definition of
\xi(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\xi(x)=

\displaystyle\min_{v\in\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x))}\frac{\langle\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)v,v\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:45}}{=}\min_{v\in\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x))}\frac{\langle\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)v,v\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}}=\lambda_{\min}^{+}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\right).

(46)

Considering the singular value decomposition provided in the previous Lemma, we get the complete result.

Lemma 13
.

Assume, assumption
2
holds. Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbf{K}(x)}\geq\ \xi(x)\ \|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}(x)}\geq\ \xi\ \|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}(x)}.

(47)

Proof.

Since,
\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x))=\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x))
using the relation
\nabla f(x)\in\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x))
(assumption
2
), we have the following:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x)\nabla f(x)=\nabla f(x),

(48)

here, we used the notation
\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x)
to denote the quantity
\left(\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then using the above relation, we have

\displaystyle\nabla f(x)^{\top}\mathbf{K}(x)\nabla f(x)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:48}}{=}\nabla f(x)^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x)\nabla f(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x)^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{K}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x)\nabla f(x)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{als:an:43}}{\geq}\xi(x)\ \|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}(x)}\overset{\eqref{als:an:39}}{\geq}\xi\ \|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{\dagger}(x)},

here, we used the definition of
\xi(x),\ \xi
along with the relation
\mathbf{H}^{\dagger/2}(x)\nabla f(x)\in\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x))
.

Assumption 3
.

Let,
x_{k}
be the random iterate generated by the ANM algorithm and for all
k\geq 1
, assume one of the following holds:

\displaystyle\text{Either}\quad\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\succ 0\quad\text{or}\quad G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\in\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x_{k})).

(49)

Lemma 14
.

Assume assumption
3
holds. Then we have the following:

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}(x_{k})}\geq\lambda_{\min}^{+}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{G}=\xi(x_{k})\ \|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{G}.

(50)

Proof.

First, assume
\mathbf{H}(x_{k})\succ 0
. This implies
G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\succ 0
. Since,
G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\succ 0
we have
\lambda_{\min}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\lambda_{\min}^{+}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\xi(x_{k})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}(x_{k})}

\displaystyle=(x_{k}-x_{k-1})^{\top}G^{\frac{1}{2}}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}G^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\min}\left(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{G}=\xi(x_{k})\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{G}.

(51)

Considering the second condition of assumption
3
, we have

\displaystyle G(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\in\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}(x_{k}))=\textbf{Range}(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})).

(52)

As
\textbf{Null}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\rho}+\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})G^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}\right)\subset\textbf{Null}(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k}))
, applying Lemma
6
with
N(x_{k})=\left(\frac{1}{\rho}+\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})G^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})\right)^{-1}
and
M(x_{k})=\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we get the following:

\displaystyle\textbf{Range}(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k}))=\textbf{Range}(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})N(x_{k})\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}})=\textbf{Range}(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

(53)

This implies

\displaystyle G^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\overset{\eqref{als:an:52}}{\in}\textbf{Range}(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k}))\overset{\eqref{als:an:53}}{=}\textbf{Range}(G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}(x_{k})G^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

(54)

Using the Courant-Fisher Theorem along with the above relation, we get the following identity:

5
Conclusion

In this work, we proposed two variants of the Newton method for solving the unconstrained minimization problem. Our proposed approach incorporates the penalty method and augmented Lagrangian techniques to the Newton system. We provided convergence results of the proposed method under mild conditions imposed on the function
f(x)
. The proposed penalty Newton method generalizes the so-called
Levenberg
and
Levenberg-Marquardt
algorithm into one framework. The proposed algorithms outperform the existing Newton method on various test instances. Building on our work one can design penalty and augmented variants of the so-called
Randomized Subspace Newton
(RSN) method proposed in
[
15
]
.

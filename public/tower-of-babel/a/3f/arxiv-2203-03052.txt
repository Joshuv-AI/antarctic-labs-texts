Cone Vertex Algebras, Mock theta functions, and Umbral Moonshine modules
Miranda C. N. Cheng, Gabriele Sgroi

Abstract
We describe a family of indefinite theta functions of signature ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) that can be expressed in terms of trace functions of vertex algebras built from cones in lattices. The family of indefinite theta functions considered has interesting connections with mock theta functions and Appell-Lerch sums. We use these relations to write the McKay-Thompson series of umbral moonshine at lambency ℓ = 8 , 12 , 16 \ell=8,12,16 in terms of trace functions of vertex algebras modules, and thereby provide the modules for these instances of umbral moonshine.

2.1
Lattice Vertex Algebras

In this subsection we will briefly summarize the construction of vertex algebras associated to lattices, closely following the exposition in
[
20
]
. The main goal is to introduce the notation and conventions that will be used in the rest of the paper. More details can be found in, e.g,
[
18
]
,
[
30
]
, and
[
31
]
.

Consider a lattice
L
.
Let’s define
\mathfrak{h}:=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
with the symmetric
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear form
\braket{\cdot,\cdot}
naturally inherited from the bilinear form on
L
. Given a formal variable
t
, define
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}:=\mathfrak{h}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}
with the Lie algebra structure given by
[u\otimes t^{m},v\otimes t^{n}]=m\braket{u,v}\delta_{m+n,0}{\bf c}
with
{\bf c}
a central element. The algebra
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
has a natural decomposition as
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{-}\oplus\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{0}\oplus\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{+}
with
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{\pm}:=\mathfrak{h}[t^{\pm 1}]t^{\pm}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{0}:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}
. Given an ordered integral basis
\{\epsilon_{j}\}
for the lattice
L
, define

b(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }i\leq j\\
1&\text{ if }i>j\end{cases}

(2.1)

extended linearly over
L
, and set
\beta(\lambda,\mu):=(-1)^{b(\lambda,\mu)}
. We then consider the ring
\mathbb{C}_{\beta}[L]
generated by
{\bf v}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in L
, satisfying
{\bf v}_{\lambda}{\bf v}_{\mu}=\beta(\lambda,\mu){\bf v}_{\lambda+\mu}
. Give
\mathbb{C}_{\beta}[L]
a
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{0}\oplus\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{+}
-module structure by setting, for
h\in\mathfrak{h}
and
\lambda\in L
,
\mathbb{c}\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
and
u(m)\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}=\delta_{m,0}\braket{u,\lambda}\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}
, where we have used the standard notation
u(m)=u\otimes t^{m}
. Finally, we consider the module

V_{L}:=U(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})\otimes_{U(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{0}\otimes{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{+})}}\mathbb{C}_{\beta}[L].

(2.2)

We can equip this module with a (unique) vertex algebra structure with vacuum vector
1\otimes\mathbb{v}_{0}
, vertex operator map
Y:V_{L}\to({\rm End}V_{L})[[z,z^{-1}]]
given by, for
u\in\mathfrak{h}
and
\lambda\in L

\begin{split}&Y(u(-1)\otimes\mathbb{v}_{0},z)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}u(n)z^{-n-1}\\
&Y(\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{v}_{\lambda},z)=\text{exp}\left(-\sum\limits_{n<0}\frac{\lambda(n)}{n}z^{-n}\right)\text{exp}\left(-\sum\limits_{n>0}\frac{\lambda(n)}{n}z^{-n}\right)\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}z^{\lambda(0)}\end{split}

(2.3)

where
\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}
in the right hand side denotes the operator
p\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\mu}\to\beta(\lambda,\mu)p\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\lambda+\mu}
, and
z^{\lambda(0)}(p\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\mu}):=(p\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\mu})z^{\braket{\lambda,\mu}}
. Furthermore, given the basis
\{\epsilon_{j}\}
for
L
and the dual basis
\{\epsilon^{\prime}_{j}\}
,
\epsilon^{\prime}_{j}\in L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
satisfying
\braket{\epsilon^{\prime}_{i},\epsilon_{j}}=\delta_{ij}
, we can define the conformal element

\omega:=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i}\epsilon^{\prime}_{i}(-1)\epsilon_{i}(-1)\otimes\mathbb{v}_{0}.

(2.4)

Writing
Y(\omega,z)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{-n-2}
, we have
[L(0),v(n)]=-nv(n)
and
L(0)1\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}=\frac{\braket{\lambda,\lambda}}{2}1\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
.
In particular, when the bilinear form on
L
is positive definite, this give
V_{L}
the structure of a vertex operator algebra. In the more general case, vector of zero length give infinite dimensional eigenspaces for
L(0)
. We can define a finite order automorphism of
V_{L}
by choosing
h\in L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}}
acting as
h(0)p\otimes\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}=\braket{h,\lambda}p\otimes\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}
with
p\in S(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{-})
and defining

g_{h}:=e^{2\pi ih(0)}.

(2.5)

In order to build twisted modules for the lattice vertex algebra , let’s consider
\mathbb{C}_{\beta}[L+h]
generated by
\mathbb{v}_{\mu+h}
, with
\mu\in L
and
h\in L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
, equipped with the
\mathbb{C}_{\beta}[L]
-module structure given by
\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}\mathbb{v}_{\mu+h}=\beta(\lambda,\mu)\mathbb{v}_{\lambda+\mu+h}
and the
U(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{0}\otimes{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{+})}
-module structure
\mathbb{c}\mathbb{v}_{\mu+h}=\mathbb{v}_{\mu+h}
,
u(m)\mathbb{v}_{\mu+h}=\delta_{m,0}\braket{u,\mu+h}\mathbb{v}_{\mu+h}
for
u\in\mathfrak{h}
,
\mu,\lambda\in L
. We can then define
g_{h}
-twisted modules for the lattice vertex algebra
V_{L}
by setting
V_{L+h}:=U(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})\otimes_{U(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{0}\otimes{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}^{+})}}\mathbb{C}_{\beta}[L+h]
and defining
Y_{h}:=V_{L}\to(\text{End}V_{L+h})[[z,z^{-1}]]
similarly as before but with
\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}
acting as
\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}(p\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\mu+h})=\beta(\lambda,\mu)p\otimes\mathbb{v}_{\lambda+\mu+h}
. When

belongs to the dual lattice
L^{*}=\{\lambda\in L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{Q}|\braket{\lambda,L}\in\mathbb{Z}\}

g_{h}
-twisted modules of

are given by

for some

congruent to

L\otimes_{Z}\mathbb{Q}
on
V_{L}
specified by
g_{h}
extends to
g_{h^{{}^{\prime}}}
-twisted modules through

g_{h}(p\otimes\mathbb{v}_{\lambda+h^{{}^{\prime}}}):=e^{2\pi i\braket{h,\lambda}}p\otimes\mathbb{v}_{\lambda+h^{{}^{\prime}}}.

(2.6)

In order to include vertex algebras associated to cones, as opposed to the full lattice, we will describe a family of sub-vertex algebras of
V_{L}
.
For a
K\subset L
that is closed under addition that contains 0, the submodule
V_{K}
of

generated by
\mathbb{v}_{\lambda}

\gamma\in L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}}

Indefinite Theta Functions

(r-1,1)

We will start by introducing some notation. For the rest of the paper we will set

A

B(\mathbb{v},\mathbb{w}):=\mathbb{v}^{T}A\mathbb{w}

. It is a well known fact that, when

is positive definite, given
\mathbb{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
we have that

\Theta_{Q,\mathbb{x}_{0}}(\tau;z):=\sum\limits_{\mathbb{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}q^{Q(\mathbb{n})}y^{B(\mathbb{n},\mathbb{x}_{0})}

(2.7)

is a Jacobi form of weight
\frac{r}{2}
and index

[
32
]
. This result does not hold when
Q
is not positive definite. When the quadratic form is of signature
(r-1,1)
, i.e. when the largest linear subspace on which

is negative definite has dimension

2.7
) were studied by Zwegers
[
4
]
. For such quadratic forms, the set
\{\mathbb{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:Q(\mathbb{c})<0\}
splits into two connected components, we fix one of these and denote it
C_{Q}
. Explicitly we choose a
\mathbb{c}_{0}
such that
Q(\mathbb{c}_{0})<0
and define

C_{Q}:=\{\mathbb{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:Q(\mathbb{c})<0,B(\mathbb{c},\mathbb{c}_{0})<0\}.

(2.8)

We also define

S_{Q}:=\{\mathbb{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}:\text{gcd}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})=1,Q(\mathbb{c})=0,B(\mathbb{c},\mathbb{c}_{0})<0\}

(2.9)

and consider the compactification of
C_{Q}

\overline{C}_{Q}:=C_{Q}\cup S_{Q}
. We furthermore define,
\forall\mathbb{c}\in\overline{C}_{Q}
,

R(\mathbb{c}):=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{r}&\text{if }\mathbb{c}\in C_{Q},\\
\{\mathbb{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:B(\mathbb{a},\mathbb{c})\not\in\mathbb{Z}\}&\text{if }\mathbb{c}\in S_{Q}.\end{cases}

(2.10)

With the above notation, given a symmetric matrix
A

\mathbb{c}_{1},\mathbb{c}_{2}\in\overline{C}_{Q}

Lemma 3.1

Let

Proof.

Explicitly, (

\begin{split}T^{(N)}_{\mathbb{a},{\bf b}}(\tau)&=\frac{1}{\eta(\tau)^{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{\mu\in P^{(N)}+\rho^{+}_{{\bf a}}}e^{2\pi iB({\bf n},{\bf b})}q^{\frac{\braket{\mu,\mu}}{2}}-\sum\limits_{\mu\in P^{(N)}+\rho^{-}_{{\bf a}}}e^{-2\pi iB({\bf n}+\mathbf{1},{\bf b})}q^{\frac{\braket{\mu,\mu}}{2}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\eta(\tau)^{2}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\

n_{1},n_{2}<0\end{subarray}}\right)e^{2\pi iB({\bf n},{\bf b})}q^{Q(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{a})}\end{split}

(3.10)

where we have written

where we have also used

6
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper we showed how certain trace functions of cone vertex algebras are related to a certain family of indefinite theta functions of signature
(1,1)
. This family possesses interesting number theoretic properties and it is related to Appell-Lerch sums and Ramanujan’s mock theta functions. For three instances of umbral moonshine, those with lambency
\ell=8,12,16
, this allowed us to construct modules for the relevant finite groups
in terms of cone vertex algebras and other known super vertex operator algebras modules. We end the paper with a collection of open questions and possible future directions.

•

We expect that the family of indefinite theta functions expressible as trace functions of cone vertex algebras can be extended by studying vertex algebras associated to cones with a more general shape than what used in (
3.4
). The condition in (
3.1
) on the choice of
\mathbf{c}
is chosen to restrain the sum over the lattice vectors on the first and third quadrant of the plane. More general choices for the vectors
\mathbf{c}
will lead to a sum on different cones.

•

Another natural generalization is to investigate more general cone vertex algebras that can reproduce, through trace functions, indefinite theta functions of general signature
(r-n,n)
.
In particular, it is worth investigating whether cone vertex algebras could be useful to gain a better understanding of the umbral moonshine phenomenon more generally, including the potential moonshine phenomenon involving all the optimal Jacobi theta functions classified in
[
37
]
.
As remarked in previous sections, all mock theta functions appearing in the McKay-Thompson series of umbral moonshine can be written in terms of the traces of cone vertex algebras discussed in this paper.
The remaining challenge is thus to find expressions of the McKay-Thompson series that are compatible with the umbral group actions. While here we have limited our analysis to three instances of umbral moonshine with small umbral groups that turn out to act trivially on the cone vertex algerba structure, more involved group actions can certainly appear in other examples, akin to what happens in
[
20
]
. Furthermore, we note that the trace functions of the cone vertex algebras seem to connect the McKay-Thompson series to the meromorphic Jacobi forms associated to various instances of umbral moonshine, as a consequence of Corollary
3.4
.

•

Finally, it would be interesting to investigate the physical significance of the cone vertex algebras. Vertex operator algebras provide a mathematical axiomatization of the chiral algebra of conformal field theories in two dimension and it would be interesting to understand what kind of conformal fields theories cone vertex algebras are related to.
For instance,
it is known that the specialized Appell-Lerch sum (
1.6
) captures the non-modular part of the elliptic genus of non-compact supersymmetric coset models
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
.
This could shed light on the still mysterious relation between umbral moonshine and string theories compactified on
K3
surfaces
[
29
]
(see also
[
38
]
for more complete references).

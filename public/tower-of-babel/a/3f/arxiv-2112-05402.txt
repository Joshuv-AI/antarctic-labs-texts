Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following fractional Laplacian eigenvalue problem

(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u={\mu}u+am(x)|u|^{p}u,\quad u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1.1)

where
s\in(0,1)
,
{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
and
a>0
are parameters,
N\geq 2
,
p=\frac{4s}{N}
is the so-called mass critical exponent,
V(x)
and
m(x)
are in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
can be defined as follows

(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=C_{N,s}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy=C_{N,s}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\epsilon}(0)}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

for any function
f(x)
in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}
.
It is known that
(-\Delta)^{s}
gives the standard Laplace operator
-\Delta
if
s=1
, see, e.g.,
[
17
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Problem (
1.1
) arises in studying the standing wave solutions for the following fractional Schrödinger equation

-i\phi_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}\phi+V(x)\phi=am(x)|\phi|^{p}\phi,\ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.2)

the background for this equation we refer the reader to the papers
[
13
,
14
]
, etc.

It is easy to see that to seek a standing wave solution for (
1.2
), that is, a solution of the form
\phi(t,x)=u(x)e^{-i\mu t}
, we need only to get a solution
u(x)
of (
1.1
) in a suitable function space. When
s=1
and
N=2
, the solutions with prescribed
L^{2}
-norm (or, saying
L^{2}
-normalized solutions) for (
1.1
) have attracted much attention in recent years, since in this case the problem is essentially the Gross-Pitaevskii (GP) equation (with mass critical power
p=\frac{4s}{N}=2
), which is a very important model in the study of Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) theory. For examples, in this case, Bao-Cai
[
2
]
and Guo-Seiringer
[
8
]
studied (
1.1
) under
m(x)\equiv 1
and
V(x)
satisfies

0\leq V(x)\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \text{and}\ \lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}V(x)=+\infty,

(1.3)

i.e.,
V(x)
is a coercive potential which leads to very useful compactness in a weighted Sobolev space. By using constrained variational method and energy estimates, they proved that there is a threshold value
a^{*}
, which can be given by the unique positive solution
Q(x)
(up to translations) of the following equation

-\Delta u+u=u^{3},\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

i.e.,
a^{*}=\|Q\|^{2}_{2}
, such that (
1.1
) in the above mentioned case has always a least energy solution
u_{a}
for any
a\in[0,a^{*})
, see also
[
19
]
for
V(x)=|x|^{2}
. In
[
8
]
, the authors also made some detailed analysis on the asymptotic behavior of the least energy solution
u_{a}
as
a\nearrow a^{*}
, by assuming the potential
V(x)
being a polynomial type function with finite global minimum. The results of
[
8
]
have been extended to more general potentials, such as,
V(x)
is allowed to have infinite many global minimum in
[
9
,
10
]
, and
V(x)
can be a periodic function in
[
18
]
, etc. Moreover, under the condition (
1.3
) some results were also obtained for (
1.1
) in
[
3
]
with
s=1
and
m(x)\not\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Very recently, He-Long
[
11
]
investigated the existence and concentrating behavior of the least energy solutions with given
L^{2}
-norm for (
1.1
) under
s\in(0,1)
,
m(x)\equiv 1
and
V(x)
being bounded. Luo-Zhang
[
12
]
studied the existence and nonexistence of normalized solutions for (
1.1
) with
V(x)\equiv 0
and combined nonlinearities, i.e.,
\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u
,
2<q<p<2^{*}_{s}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
. Motivated by the mentioned works, it seems natural to ask how about the least energy solutions with prescribed
L^{2}
- norm for (
1.1
) in more general case:
s\in(0,1)
,
V(x)
being a bounded function and
m(x)\not\equiv 1
? The aim of this paper is to give a detailed answer to this question. However, different from the above mentioned papers
[
8
,
11
,
18
]
, in our case we should be confront with a series difficulties simultaneously, such as the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is nonlocal which makes the energy estimates more difficult, the potential
m(x)\not\equiv 1
is involved in the nonlinear term which makes the concentration analysis more complicated, and the potential
V(x)
is bounded (not coercive) which leads to the lack of compactness for Sobolev embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, etc.

To start our discussions on the equation (
1.1
), we first introduce the following fractional Sobolev space,
s\in(0,1)
,

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\Big\},

with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then,
(-\Delta)^{s}
is well defined in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, by
[
17
, Proposition 3.6]
, we have

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|^{2}_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy.

As in the above mentioned papers, we are also concerned with the least energy solutions of (
1.1
) with given
L^{2}
-norm. So, the Euler-Lagrange principle implies that we may get this kind of solutions for (
1.1
) by searching the minimizers of the following constrained minimization problem with
\lambda>0
:

I_{\lambda}(a)=\inf\big\{J_{a}(u):\ u\in S_{\lambda}\big\}\text{ and }I_{1}(a)=I_{\lambda}(a)|_{\lambda=1}

(1.4)

where

S_{\lambda}:=\big\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=\lambda\big\}\text{ and }S_{1}=S_{\lambda}|_{\lambda=1}

J_{a}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2}\big)dx-\frac{aN}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx,

and
J_{a}(u)
is well defined in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
since
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuous and locally compact for

2\leq r<2^{*}_{s}
with
2^{*}_{s}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
if
N>2s
, and
2^{*}_{s}=+\infty
if
N\leq 2s
, see
[
17
, Theorems 6.7, 6.9, Corollary 7.2]
. Moreover,
J_{a}(u)\in C^{1}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, and for any
\varphi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}_{a}(u),\varphi\rangle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big[(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi+V(x)u\big]dx-2a\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}}u\varphi dx.

Therefore, any minimizer of (
1.4
) is a least energy solution with prescribed
L^{2}
-norm of (
1.1
). So, in what follows, we focus on the existence and asymptotic behaviors for the minimizers of the minimization problem (
1.4
).

To state our main results, we first recall some results on the following equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+u=|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}}u,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.5)

For
N\geq 1
, Frank-Lenzmann-Silvester
[
6
]
proved that (
1.5
) has a unique and non-degeneracy radial (up to translations) ground state solutions
U(x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, the ground state solution is algebraic decay

\frac{C_{1}}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\leq U(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1+|x|^{N+2s}},

(1.6)

where
C_{1}\geq C_{2}>0
are constants depending only on
s
and
N
. These are the generalizations on the results obtained in
[
1
,
5
]
for
N=1
. The same as Lemma 8.2 of
[
6
]
, we know also that the ground state
U(x)
of (

) satisfies the following Pohozaev identity:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}U|^{2}dx=\frac{N}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|U|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx.

(1.7)

Also, it is known (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
11
]
) that
U(x)
is the unique (up to translations) extremal of the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality

\|u\|_{\frac{4s}{N}+2}^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}\leq\frac{N+2s}{Na_{s}^{*}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{4s}{N}},\ \forall u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.8)

where
a_{s}^{*}>0
is given by

a_{s}^{*}=\|U(x)\|_{2}^{\frac{4s}{N}}.

(1.9)

Moreover, applying (
1.8
) for

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|U|^{2}dx=\frac{2s}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|U|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx.

(1.10)

Throughout this paper, we assume that

and

satisfy the following conditions.

(V_{1})

V(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the set

and for some

2
Existence of minimizers

This section is devoted to the existence of minimizers for
I_{1}(a)
. For this purpose, we need some preliminary lemmas.
Similar to Lemma 2.1 of
[
18
]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

Suppose
V(x)
satisfies
(V_{1})
, and
m(x)
satisfies
(M_{1})
, then for
0<a<a_{s}^{*}
, the map
\lambda\mapsto I_{\lambda}(a)
is a continuous function on
\big(0,(\frac{a_{s}^{*}}{a})^{\frac{N}{2s}}\big)
.

Proof.
Choose
\lambda\in\big(0,(\frac{a_{s}^{*}}{a})^{\frac{N}{2s}}\big)
and

\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\big(0,(\frac{a_{s}^{*}}{a})^{\frac{N}{2s}}\big)\ \text{ such that }\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=\lambda.

(2.1)

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
u\in S_{\lambda}
such that
J_{a}(u)\leq I_{\lambda}(a)+\epsilon
. Let
w_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda}}u
, then
w_{n}\in S_{\lambda_{n}}
and

\displaystyle J_{a}(w_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle-a\frac{N}{N+2s}\big(\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda}\big)^{\frac{2s}{N}+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda}J_{a}(u)+a\frac{N}{N+2s}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda}\Big(1-\big(\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda}\big)^{\frac{2s}{N}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx.

This gives that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{\lambda_{n}}(a)\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J_{a}(w_{n})=J_{a}(u)\leq I_{\lambda}(a)+\epsilon.

By the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{\lambda_{n}}(a)\leq I_{\lambda}(a).

(2.2)

On the other hand, since
V(x)\geq 0
, it is easy to know from (
1.8
) that
I_{\lambda_{n}}(a)\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Therefore, there exists
\{v_{n}\}\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\lambda_{n}
and

I_{\lambda_{n}}(a)\leq J_{a}(v_{n})\leq I_{\lambda_{n}}(a)+\frac{1}{n}.

(2.3)

Applying (
1.8
) again, we have

\displaystyle J_{a}(v_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\big(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}^{*}}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2s}{N}}\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v_{n}|^{2}dx\geq 0.

(2.4)

By (
2.1
), we see that
\{v_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Let
h_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{n}}}v_{n}
, then,

J_{a}(h_{n})=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{n}}J_{a}(v_{n})+a\frac{N}{N+2s}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{n}}\Big(1-\big(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{n}}\big)^{\frac{2s}{N}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|v_{n}|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx.

Consequently, there exist
M_{1},M_{2}>0
such that

\lim\limits_{a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}}I_{1}(a)=I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})
.

Hence, it remains to show that
I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})
has no minimizers. Otherwise, if

is a minimizer for
I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})=0
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{2}dx=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)=0,

(2.19)

and by (

),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx=\frac{a_{s}^{*}N}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{s}^{*}N}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx.

Therefore, using
I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})=0
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx=\frac{a_{s}^{*}N}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx.

Combining the property of (
1.8
), this implies that
u
has to be equal to
U
, up to a translation and dilation, which contradicts (
2.19
). Thus,
I_{1}(a^{*})
cannot be attained.

3
Asymptotic behavior under general bounded trapping potential

In this section, we aim to prove Theorem
1.2
, which is to give a description on the asymptotic behavior of the minimizers for
I_{1}(a)
as
a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}
under general bounded trapping potentials.

Let
u_{a}
be a minimizer of
I_{1}(a)
, then
u_{a}
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{a}(x)+V(x)u_{a}(x)=\frac{\lambda_{a}}{2}u_{a}+am(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{\frac{4s}{N}}u_{a}(x),

(3.1)

where
\lambda_{a}\in\mathbb{R}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier. We give first the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, let
u_{a}\in S_{1}
be a minimizer of
I_{1}(a)
with
a\in(0,a_{s}^{*})
. Define

\epsilon^{-2s}_{a}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{a}|^{2}dx.

(3.2)

Then

(i)

\epsilon_{a}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}.

(3.3)

(ii)

There exists a sequence
\{\bar{z}_{a}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\bar{w}_{a}(x):=\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}_{a}u_{a}(\epsilon_{a}x+\bar{z}_{a})\in S_{1}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\bar{w}_{a}|^{2}dx=1,\quad\frac{a_{s}^{*}N}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(\epsilon_{a}x+\bar{z}_{a})|\bar{w}_{a}|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx\overset{a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}}{\longrightarrow}1.

(3.5)

Furthermore, there exist
R,\eta>0
such that

\lim_{a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\bar{w}_{a}|^{2}dx\geq\eta>0.

(3.6)

(iii)

Up to a subsequence, there holds

\lim_{a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}}\bar{z}_{a}=x_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}.

(3.7)

Proof.

(i)
If (
3.3
) is false, then there exists a sequence
\{a_{k}\}
with
a_{k}\nearrow a_{s}^{*}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
such that
\{u_{a_{k}}\}
is bounded in

and

\displaystyle J_{a_{s}^{*}}(u_{a_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{a_{k}}(u_{a_{k}})+(a_{k}-a_{s}^{*})\frac{N}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u_{a_{k}}|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}(a_{k})+O(a_{s}^{*}-a_{k})\rightarrow I_{1}(a_{s}^{*}).

This means that
\{u_{a_{k}}\}
is a minimizing sequence of
I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i), by applying
Lemma
2.4
, we know that there exists a subsequence of
\{a_{k}\}
(still denoted by
\{a_{k}\}
) and
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

Thus,

0=I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})\leq J_{a_{s}^{*}}(u_{0})\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}J_{a_{k}}(u_{a_{k}})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}I_{1}(a_{k})=0.

This means that
u_{0}
is a minimizer of
I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})
, which contradicts Theorem
1.1
(iii). So, part (i) is proved.

(ii).
By Theorem
1.1
,
I_{1}(a)\rightarrow I_{1}(a_{s}^{*})=0
, as
a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}
. Then,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{a}|^{2}dx-\frac{Na}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u_{a}|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-2s}_{a}-\frac{Na}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(x)|u_{a}|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx

\displaystyle I_{1}(a)\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}.

Set

w_{a}(x):=\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}_{a}u_{a}(\epsilon_{a}x).

(3.9)

Then, by (
3.3
) and (
3.8
) we see that

\frac{aN}{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m(\epsilon_{a}x)|w_{a}|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx\rightarrow 1,\ \text{as}\ a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}.

(3.10)

Now, we claim that there exist
\{z_{a}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
R>0
such that

\lim_{a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}}\int_{B_{R}(z_{a})}|{w}_{a}|^{2}dx\geq\eta>0.

(3.11)

Indeed, if (

) is false, then by the vanishing lemma

, which contradicts (

Set

) and (

) hold.

(iii).
By (

) and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{2}\leq I_{1}(a)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}.

(3.12)

It then follows from (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(\epsilon_{a}x+\bar{z}_{a})|\bar{w}_{a}(x)|^{2}dx\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ a\nearrow a_{s}^{*}.

(3.13)

We first
claim
that
\{\bar{z}_{a}\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
By contradiction, if, passing to a subsequence,
\bar{z}_{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\infty
, it follows from

.

and

\displaystyle 2(-\Delta)^{s}\bar{w}_{k}(x)+2\epsilon^{2s}_{k}V(\epsilon_{k}x+\bar{z}_{a})\bar{w}_{k}(x)

(3.22)

where

is a certain point in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since

\bar{w}_{k}\overset{k}{\rightarrow}\bar{w}_{a_{s}^{*}}\ \text{strongly in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(3.23)

1.2

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{w}_{k}|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx=\frac{2s}{N}\|U\|_{2}^{-\frac{4s}{N}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big|U\big|^{\frac{4s}{N}+2}dx=\frac{N+2s}{a_{s}^{*}N}.

(4.15)

denotes a quantity which goes to 0 as

.

We

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\big|\frac{\bar{z}_{k}-x_{0}}{\epsilon_{k}}\big|<\infty,

(4.16)

i.e.,
\big\{\frac{\bar{z}_{k}-x_{0}}{\epsilon_{k}}\big\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\big|\frac{\bar{z}_{k}-x_{0}}{\epsilon_{k}}\big|=\infty

By Fatou’s lemma, (

As a consequence of (

Deformation of a magnetic liquid drop and unsteady flow inside and outside it in a non-stationary magnetic field
Alexander N. Tyatyushkin

Abstract
Small steady-state deformational oscillations of a drop of a viscous magnetic liquid in a non-stationary uniform magnetic field are theoretically investigated for low Reynolds numbers. The drop is suspended in another viscous magnetic liquid immiscible with the former. The non-stationary magnetic field causes flow inside and outside the drop. The inertia is taken into account by regarding the flow as essentially unsteady. The variation of the magnetic field is so slow that the approximation of quasi-stationary magnetic field may be used.



Introduction.

A non-stationary magnetic field applied to a drop of a magnetic liquid suspended in an ordinary liquid or to a drop of an ordinary liquid suspended in a magnetic liquid causes a motion of the liquids inside and outside the drop. Investigation of this phenomenon is interesting from the point of view of application to the actuation of motion of liquids in various devices including microfluidic ones. Besides, the study of this phenomenon is interesting from the point of view of basic science.

In the present work the problem about the shape of the drop and flow inside and outside it is also solved with asymptotic methods without the use of any assumptions about them. Within this approach, the shape of the drop is determined by equations obtained with the help of asymptotic methods from the system of equations and boundary conditions that determine the magnetic field and the flow. In order to provide the applicability of the asymptotic methods, the scope of the present theoretical investigation is restricted by the case of weakly deformed drops. The unsteady flow inside and outside a drop of a viscous magnetic liquid suspended in another viscous magnetic liquid in non-stationary magnetic field is investigated with taking into account the influence of form of the drop on the field and flow. The Reynolds number in the problem about deformational oscillations of a drop under action of non-stationary fields tends to zero as the amplitude of the oscillation tends to zero. Indeed, the velocity in that case has the order of magnitude
\omega\Delta{a}
, where
\omega
is the angular frequency of the oscillations of the applied field,
\Delta{a}
is the amplitude of the deformational oscillations of the drop (maximal variation of the distance from the center of the drop to its surface). Then the Reynolds number tends to zero as the amplitude of the oscillations tends to zero. Thus, in the case of small oscillations of the drop, the influence of the convection term in the momentum equation can be neglected, and the influence of the inertia can be described by the unsteady therm. This is directly confirmed by the fact that, in the problem about deformational oscillations of a drop in the case of when the influence of the viscosity is completely neglected (see
FD21
), the influence of the convection term in the correction of the first order over the parameter the smallness of which provides the smallness of the deformations is absent, although it is taken into account in the input system of equations. It is also followed from this fact that the solution of the problem about small oscillations of the drop with taking into account both the viscosity and inertia should tend to the solution in
FD21
as the maximal viscosity of the liquids tends to zero with exception of the case when the frequency of the applied field is close to the resonance frequency.

I
Setting of the problem.

I.1
Object of investigation.

Consider a drop of incompressible magnetic liquid in an applied non-stationary uniform magnetic field with intensity
\vec{H}_{\mathrm{a}}=\vec{H}_{\mathrm{a}}(t)
, where
t
is the time. The density, viscosity, and magnetic permeability of the liquid inside the drop are
\rho_{\mathrm{i}}
,
\eta_{\mathrm{i}}
, and
\mu_{\mathrm{i}}
. The radius of the drop in the undeformed state is
a
. The drop is suspended in an incompressible magnetic liquid with the density
\rho_{\mathrm{e}}
, viscosity
\eta_{\mathrm{e}}
, and magnetic permeability
\mu_{\mathrm{e}}
. The surface tension of the interface between the liquids is
\sigma_{\mathrm{s}}
. The liquids are regarded as sufficiently viscous so that the low Reynolds number approximation is valid. The influence of the inertia of the liquids is taken into account by means of regarding the flow as essentially unsteady. The variation of the magnetic field is so slow and the conductivities of the liquids are so small that the ferrohydrodynamics approximation is valid (see
Rosensweig
).

I.2
System of equations.

In order to find the magnetic field intensity,
\vec{H}
, velocity,
\vec{v}

\vec{r}

\nabla\cdot\vec{v}=0,

(1)

the motion equation in the low Reynolds number approximation for arbitrary Strouhal numbers

(2)

\nabla\cdot\vec{B}=0,\qquad\nabla\times\vec{H}=0,

(3)

and the constitutive relation

(4)

Here, the formulas are written down for Gaussian system of units,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\vec{B}\vec{H}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\vec{B}\cdot\vec{H}\hat{I},

(5)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{\rm v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta\left(\nabla\vec{v}\right)^{\mathrm{S}},

(6)

II
Solution.

II.1
Deformation of the drop.

Let the surface of the drop be given by the following equation

r=\left|\vec{r}\right|=a+h\left(\dfrac{\vec{r}}{{r}},t\right).

(19)

In order the deformations of the drop to be small, the following condition should be fulfilled

h=h\left(\dfrac{\vec{r}}{{r}},t\right)\ll a.

(20)

The function
h\left(\vec{r}/{r},t\right)
can be represented in the form

h=h\left(\dfrac{\vec{r}}{{r}},t\right)=\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\hat{h}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdot}}\dfrac{\vec{r}\,^{n}}{{r}^{n}}.

(21)

Here,
\hat{h}_{n}=\hat{h}_{n}(t)
(
n>1
) are some tensors each of which is an arbitrary irreducible tensor of
n
th order, i.e., a tensor of
n
th order symmetric with respect to any pair of indices and such that its contraction with the identity tensor over any pair of indices is equal to zero (see
LL2
). Here and in what follows,
\vec{b}\,^{n}
denotes
n
th dyadic degree of the vector
\vec{b}
(i.e., the
(n-1)
-multiple dyadic product of the vector
\vec{b}
by itself),
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdot}}
denotes
n
-multiple contraction of tensors over the adjacent indices.

The impenetrability condition (
8
) yields the following equation for the function
h\left(\vec{r}/{r},t\right)

\vec{n}\cdot\dfrac{\vec{r}}{{r}}\dfrac{\partial{h}}{\partial{t}}={v}_{\mathrm{s}n}.

(22)

II.2
Magnetic field.

The intensity of the magnetic field is sought for in the form

\vec{H}=\nabla\psi,

(23)

where

\psi=\begin{cases}\vec{H}_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\vec{r}-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{M}_{\mathrm{e}n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdot}}\dfrac{\vec{r}\,^{n}}{r^{2n+1}},\ r>a+h,\\
\vec{H}_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\vec{r}-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{M}_{\mathrm{i}n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdot}}\dfrac{\vec{r}\,^{n}}{a^{2n+1}},\ r\leqslant a+h.\end{cases}

(24)

Here,
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{e}n}=\hat{M}_{\mathrm{e}n}(t)
and
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{i}n}=\hat{M}_{\mathrm{i}n}(t)
are tensors which are either some vectors for
n=1
or, for
n>1
, some irreducible tensors of
n
th order. For a field given in this form, Maxwell’s equations Eq. (
3
), the constitutive relation Eq. (
4
), the condition at infinity Eq. (
18
), and the condition of boundedness of
\vec{H}
are automatically satisfied.

Flow.

(40)

Here,

III
Conclusion

The behavior of a drop of a viscous magnetic liquid suspended in another viscous magnetic liquid immiscible with the former in an applied non-stationary uniform magnetic field is theoretically investigated within the approximation of low Reynolds numbers with taking into account that the flow of the liquids is unsteady. The general solution of the Navier–Stokes equation in the low Reynolds number approximation is found for unsteady flows of an incompressible liquid. With the use of this general solution in the first order approximation over the small parameter smallness of which provides the smallness of the deformation of the drop, the equations are obtained that describe the variation of the form of the drop in an arbitrarily varying uniform magnetic field. The solutions of these equations are found for forced steady-state oscillations of the drop in harmonically oscillating and rotating applied magnetic fields.

As the solution of the given problem shows, rather cumbersome expressions even for the corrections of the first order appear in taking into account the unsteady term. So more careful study of the general solution with the goal to find more simple asymptotic expressions for the case when the viscosity tends to zero acquires actuality. Those asymptotic expressions should allow describing the process of the oscillation decay and finiteness of the oscillation amplitude for the resonance.

1.
Introduction

In
[
7
]
, Lindestrauss and Weiss introduced the notion of metric mean dimension for any continuous map
\phi:X\rightarrow X
, where
X
is a compact metric space with metric
d
. We will denote by
\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi)
and
\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi)
, respectively, the lower and upper metric mean dimension of
\phi:X\rightarrow X
. We have

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi)\leq\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi).

(1.1)

Denote by
h_{\text{top}}(\phi)
the topological entropy of
\phi:X\rightarrow X
. We have if
\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi)>0
, then
h_{\text{top}}(\phi)=\infty
.
Example
4.3
proves there exist continuous maps
\phi:X\rightarrow X
with infinite topological entropy and metric mean dimension equal to zero.

Let
N
be a compact Riemannian manifold with topological dimension
\dim(N)=n
. In
[
2
]
, the authors proved if
n\geq 2
, then the set consisting of homeomorphisms on
N
with upper metric mean dimension equal to
n
is residual in
\text{Hom}(N)
. This fact is proved in
[
1
]
for
C^{0}(N)
instead of
\text{Hom}(N)
. On the other hand,
any Lipschitz continuous map defined on a finite dimensional compact metric space has finite entropy (see
[
5
]
, Theorem 3.2.9), therefore, it has metric mean dimension equal to zero.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we denote by
H^{\alpha}([0,1])
the set consisting of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous maps on the interval
[0,1]
. Hazard in
[
4
]
proves there exist continuous maps on the interval with infinite entropy which are
\alpha
-Hölder for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
. However, the example showed by Hazard has zero metric mean dimension (see Example
4.3
). In this work, we will show, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists a
\phi\in H^{\alpha}([0,1])
with

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=1-\alpha.

In the next section, we will present the definition of metric mean dimension. In Section
3
, we will show some results about the metric mean dimension for continuous maps with horseshoes and several examples. Although the inequality given in (
1.1
) is clear, we do not know any reference in which is showed an explicit example of a continuous map on the interval such that the inequality is strict. We will construct this kind of examples in Section
3
.
Furthermore, we prove for
a,b\in[0,1]
, with
a<b
, the set consisting of continuous maps
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
such that

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=a\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=b

is dense in

C^{0}([0,1]).
In Section
4
we show the existence of Hölder continuous maps with positive metric mean dimension. Finally, in the last section we will leave some conjectures that arise from this research.

2.
Metric mean dimension for continuous maps

Throughout this work,
X
will be a compact metric space endowed with a metric
d
and
\phi:X\rightarrow X
a continuous map. For any

n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the metric
d_{n}:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
by

d_{n}(x,y)=\max\{d(x,y),d(\phi(x),\phi(y)),\dots,d(\phi^{n-1}(x),\phi^{n-1}(y))\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.

•

We say that
A\subset X
is an
(n,\phi,\varepsilon)
-separated set
if
d_{n}(x,y)>\varepsilon
, for any two distinct points
x,y\in A
. We denote by
\emph{sep}(n,\phi,\varepsilon)
the maximal cardinality of any
(n,\phi,\varepsilon)
-separated
subset of
X
.

•

We say that
E\subset X
is an
(n,\phi,\varepsilon)
-spanning set for
X
if
for any
x\in X
there exists
y\in E
such that
d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
. Let
\emph{span}(n,\phi,\varepsilon)
be the minimum cardinality
of any
(n,\phi,\varepsilon)
-spanning subset of
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
topological entropy
of
(X,\phi,d)
is defined by

h_{\emph{top}}(\phi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\emph{sep}(\phi,\varepsilon)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\emph{span}(\phi,\varepsilon),

where

\emph{sep}(\phi,\varepsilon)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\emph{sep}(n,\phi,\varepsilon)\quad\text{and}\quad\emph{span}(\phi,\varepsilon)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\emph{span}(n,\phi,\varepsilon).

Definition 2.3
.

We define the
lower metric mean dimension
and the
upper metric mean dimension
of
(X,d,\phi)
by

\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(X,d,\phi)=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\emph{sep}(\phi,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(X,d,\phi)=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\emph{sep}(\phi,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|},

respectively.

We also have that

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi)=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{span}(\phi,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi)=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{span}(\phi,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

Remark 2.4
.

Throughout the paper, we will omit the underline and the overline on the notations of the metric mean dimension when the result be valid for both cases.

Remark 2.5
.

Topological entropy does not depend on the metric
d
. However, the metric mean dimension depends on the metric (see
[
7
]
), therefore, it is not an invariant under topological conjugacy.
If
X=[0,1]
, we consider the metric
d(x,y)=|x-y|
, for every
x,y\in[0,1]
. We will denote this metric by
|\cdot|
.

Remark 2.6
.

For any continuous map
\phi:X\rightarrow X
, it is proved in
[
9
]
that

0\leq\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(X,d,\phi)\leq\overline{\emph{dim}}_{\emph{B}}(X,d)\quad\text{ and }\quad 0\leq\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(X,d,\phi)\leq\underline{\emph{dim}}_{\emph{B}}(X,d),

(2.1)

where
\underline{\emph{dim}}_{\emph{B}}(X,d)
and
\overline{\emph{dim}}_{\emph{B}}(X,d)
are respectively the lower and upper box dimension of
X
with respect to
d
.

From the above remark we have if
N
is an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with Riemannian metric
d
we have

0\leq{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(N,d,\phi)\leq n,

for any continuous map
\phi:N\rightarrow N
. In particular, if
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is a continuous map, we have

0\leq{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)\leq 1.

(2.2)

Proposition 3.3
.

If
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
satisfies the conditions in Theorem

{\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)\geq\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\frac{\frac{\log s_{k-1}}{\log s_{k}}}{1-\frac{\log|I_{k}|}{\log s_{k}}}.

(3.2)

If
\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\frac{\frac{\log s_{k-1}}{\log s_{k}}}{1-\frac{\log|I_{k}|}{\log s_{k}}}=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\frac{1}{\left|1-\frac{\log|I_{k}|}{\log s_{k}}\right|},

{\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\frac{1}{\left|1-\frac{\log|I_{k}|}{\log{s_{k}}}\right|}.

Proof.

Take any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. For any
k\geq 1
set
\varepsilon_{k}=\frac{|I_{k}|}{s_{k}}.
There exists some
k\geq 1
such that
\varepsilon\in[\varepsilon_{k},\varepsilon_{k-1}]
. We have

\text{sep}(n,\phi,\varepsilon)\geq\text{sep}(n,\phi,\varepsilon_{k-1})\geq\text{sep}(n,\phi|_{I_{k-1}},\varepsilon_{k-1}),\quad\text{for any }n\geq 1,

and thus
\text{sep}(\phi,\varepsilon)\geq\text{sep}(\phi|_{I_{k-1}},\varepsilon_{k-1}).

Hence, from Lemma
3.1
, it follows that

\text{sep}(\phi,\varepsilon)\geq\log\left(\frac{s_{k-1}}{2}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\text{sep}(\phi,\varepsilon)}{|\log{\varepsilon}|}\geq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log{s_{k-1}}}{|\log\varepsilon_{k}|}

\displaystyle=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\frac{\log s_{k-1}}{\log s_{k}-\log|I_{k}|}=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\frac{\frac{\log s_{k-1}}{\log s_{k}}}{1-\frac{\log|I_{k}|}{\log s_{k}}}.

The second part of the proposition follows from the above fact and Theorem
3.2

4.
Hölder continuous maps with positive metric mean dimension

We say that
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is an
\alpha
-
Hölder continuous map
, for
\alpha\in(0,1]
, if there exists
K>0
such that

\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\leq K\quad\text{for all }x,y\in[0,1],\text{ with }x\neq y.

If
\phi
satisfies the above condition for
\alpha=1
, then
\phi
is called a
Lipschitz continuous map
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1),

H^{\alpha}([0,1])
will denote the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous maps on
[0,1]
.
C^{1}([0,1])
and
H^{1}([0,1])
will denote respectively the space of
C^{1}
-maps and the space of Lipschitz continuous maps on
[0,1]
. We have

C^{1}([0,1])\subset H^{1}([0,1])\subset H^{\beta}([0,1])\subset H^{\alpha}([0,1])\subset C^{0}([0,1]),\quad\text{ where }0<\alpha<\beta<1.

Next, suppose that
N
is a compact Riemannian manifold with topological dimension equal to

and Riemannian metric

[
1
]
, Theorem 4.5, is proved the set consisting of continuous maps on

with

and

metric mean dimension equal to a fixed

is dense in

. Furthermore, in Theorem 4.6, is showed the set consisting of continuous maps on

with

is residual in

. If

, in
[
2

Hence, it remains to show the existence of Hölder continuous maps on finite dimensional compact manifolds. In Theorem

we will prove there exist Hölder continuous maps on the interval with positive metric mean dimension, with certain conditions of the Hölder exponent. In Conjectures A, B and C the authors leave three problems that can be objects of future studies.

The next lemma, whose proof is straightforward and
left to the reader, will be useful in order to prove that some functions are Hölder continuous.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|\leq\frac{3^{(1-m)r}-3^{-nr}}{1-3^{-r}}=\frac{3^{(n-m+1)r}-1}{3^{nr}(1-3^{-r})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1}{|x-y|}\leq\frac{(1-3^{-r})3^{nr}}{3^{(n-m)r}-1},

hence

\frac{|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{3^{nr\alpha}(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}[3^{(n-m+1)r}-1]}{3^{nr}[3^{(n-m)r}-1]^{\alpha}}={\frac{3^{nr(\alpha-1)}(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}[3^{(k+1)r}-1]}{(3^{kr}-1)^{\alpha}}}.

(4.2)

Note that, if the sequence
\{k\}
is bounded, then (
4.2
) is convergent to
0
as
n\to\infty
, since
\alpha-1<0
. Hence (
4.2
) is bounded if
\{k\}
is bounded. Therefore, we can assume that the sequence
\{k\}
is unbounded. From (
4.2
), we write

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{nr(\alpha-1)}(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}\ \frac{\frac{3^{(k+1)r}-1}{3^{kr\alpha}}}{\frac{(3^{kr}-1)^{\alpha}}{3^{kr\alpha}}}=3^{nr(\alpha-1)}(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}\frac{(3^{kr+r-kr\alpha}-3^{-kr\alpha})}{\biggl(1-\frac{1}{3^{kr}}\biggr)^{\alpha}}.

Since

\underset{k\to\infty}{\lim}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{3^{kr}}\biggr)=1
and
\underset{k\to\infty}{\lim}3^{-kr\alpha}=0,

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}3^{nr(\alpha-1)}(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}\ \frac{(3^{kr+r-kr\alpha}-3^{-kr\alpha})}{\biggl(1-\frac{1}{3^{kr}}\biggr)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}\lim_{k\to\infty}3^{nr\alpha-nr+kr+r-kr\alpha}

\displaystyle=(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}\ 3^{r}\ \lim_{k\to\infty}3^{(n-k)r\alpha-(n-k)r}

\displaystyle=(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}\ 3^{r}\ \lim_{k\to\infty}3^{(n-k)(\alpha-1)r}

\displaystyle=(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}\ 3^{r}\ \lim_{k\to\infty}3^{m(\alpha-1)r}

\displaystyle=(1-3^{-r})^{\alpha-1}3^{m(\alpha-1)r+r}.

Thus, in any case,
\frac{|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}
is bounded with
x\in I_{n}
,
y\in I_{m}
,
n>m+1
.

Case

n=m+1
: we have
I_{m}=[a_{m-1},a_{m}],

I_{m+1}=[a_{m},a_{m+1}],

I_{m}=I^{1}_{m}\cup\cdots\cup I^{3^{m}}_{m};\ \ I_{m+1}=I^{1}_{m+1}\cup\cdots\cup I^{3^{m+1}}_{m+1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ I_{m}\cap I_{m+1}=\{a_{m}\},

where
|I_{m}^{j}|=\frac{|I_{m}|}{3^{m}}
and
|I_{m+1}^{j}|=\frac{|I_{m+1}|}{3^{m+1}}
.
Suppose that
x\in I^{1}_{m+1}
and
y\in I^{3^{m}}_{m}
.
Hence

|x-y|\leq a_{m}+\frac{|I_{m+1}|}{3^{m+1}}-\biggl(a_{m}-\frac{|I_{m}|}{3^{m}}\biggr)=\frac{|I_{m+1}|}{3^{m+1}}+\frac{|I_{m}|}{3^{m}}=\frac{C(1+3^{r+1})}{3^{m(r+1)+1}}.

(4.3)

It is easy to check that

\phi_{a}(y)=3^{m}(y-a_{m})+a_{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{a}(x)=3^{m+1}(x-a_{m})+a_{m}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|

\displaystyle=|3^{m+1}(x-a_{m})-3^{m}(y-a_{m})|=3^{m+1}x-3^{m}y-(3^{m+1}-3^{m})a_{m}

\displaystyle(y\leq a_{m})\quad

\displaystyle\leq 3^{m+1}x-3^{m}y-(3^{m+1}-3^{m})y=3^{m+1}(x-y).

Therefore, from (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{m+1}|x-y|^{1-\alpha}\leq 3^{m+1}\biggl(\frac{C(1+3^{r+1})}{3^{m(r+1)+1}}\biggr)^{1-\alpha}=\frac{3^{m+1}(C(1+3^{r+1}))^{1-\alpha}}{3^{(m(r+1)+1)(1-\alpha)}}

\displaystyle=3^{m+1+(m(r+1)+1)(\alpha-1)}(C(1+3^{r+1}))^{1-\alpha}=3^{m(\alpha(r+1)-r)}3^{\alpha}(C(1+3^{r+1}))^{1-\alpha},

which is bounded if and only if

\alpha(r+1)-r\leq 0,
that is, if
\alpha\leq\frac{r}{1+r}.

If
x\in I^{j}_{m+1}
and
y\in I^{i}_{m}
for some
j
and
i
, then there are
{x}^{\prime}\in I^{1}_{m+1}
and
{y}^{\prime}\in I^{3^{m}}_{m}
such that
\phi_{a}(x)=\phi_{a}({x}^{\prime})
and
\phi(y)=\phi({y}^{\prime}).
Moreover,
|x-y|\geq|{x}^{\prime}-{y}^{\prime}|
, which provides

\displaystyle|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi(y)|

\displaystyle=|\phi_{a}({x}^{\prime})-\phi_{a}({y}^{\prime})|\leq 3^{m(\alpha(r+1)-r)}3^{\alpha}(C(1+3^{r+1}))^{1-\alpha}|{x}^{\prime}-{y}^{\prime}|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{m(\alpha(r+1)-r)}3^{\alpha}(C(1+3^{r+1}))^{1-\alpha}|{x}-{y}|^{\alpha}.

Case

n=m
: we have
\phi(x)=3^{n}(x-a_{n})+a_{n}
for any
x\in I^{3n}_{n}
. Hence, if
x,y\in I^{3n}_{n}
, then

|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|=3^{n}|x-y|
and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi_{a}(x)-\phi_{a}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=3^{n}|x-y|^{1-\alpha}\leq 3^{n}\ |I^{3n}_{n}|^{1-\alpha}=3^{n}\biggl(\frac{|I_{n}|}{3^{n}}\biggr)^{1-\alpha}=3^{n}\biggl(\frac{C}{3^{(n-1)r}3^{n}}\biggr)^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle=C^{1-\alpha}3^{n-(1-\alpha)(nr+n)}=C^{1-\alpha}3^{r-\alpha r}3^{n(\alpha-r+\alpha r)},

which is bounded if and only if

\alpha-r+\alpha r\leq 0
, that is, if and only if
\alpha\leq\frac{r}{1+r}.

We can conclude that
\phi_{a}
is an
(1-a)
-Hölder continuous map. ∎

Let
X
be a compact metric space with metric
d
. If
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\in C^{0}(X)
and

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi_{k})=\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X,d,\phi_{k})=a_{k},\quad\text{for any }k=1,\dots,n,

we have

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X\times\cdots\times X,d^{n},\phi_{1}\times\cdots\times\phi_{n})=\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(X\times\cdots\times X,d^{n},\phi_{1}\times\cdots\times\phi_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k},

where

d^{n}((x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}))=d(x_{1},y_{1})+\cdots+d(x_{n},y_{n}),\quad\text{ for }x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in X.

(see
[
1
]
, Theorem 3.13, v). Hence, it follows from Theorem
4.5
that:

Corollary 4.6
.

Fix
b\in[0,n]
and
\alpha={1-\frac{b}{n}}
. There exists
\psi_{b}\in H^{\alpha}([0,1]^{n})
such that

\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}([0,1]^{n},d^{n},\psi_{b})=\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}([0,1]^{n},d^{n},\psi_{b})=b.

Proof.

Take
\phi_{a}:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
defined in the proof of Theorem
4.5
, where
a=\frac{b}{n}
, and set
\psi_{b}=\phi_{a}\times\cdots\times\phi_{a}:[0,1]^{n}\rightarrow[0,1]^{n}
. We have

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1]^{n},d^{n},\psi_{b})=\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1]^{n},d^{n},\psi_{b})=na=b.

Given that each
\phi_{a}
is an
(1-\frac{b}{n})
-Hölder continuous map, we have
\psi_{b}
is an
(1-\frac{b}{n})
-Hölder continuous map.
∎

5.
Some conjectures about this research

Note in Theorem
4.5
there exists a relationship between the Hölder exponent
\alpha
and the metric mean dimension of a continuous map on the interval. We will present the next conjectures about this relationship which can be the subject of future research.

Conjecture A
.

If
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous map, then

\emph{mdim}_{\emph{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)\leq 1-\alpha.

Note in Theorem
4.5
we prove there exists an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous map
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
with
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=1-\alpha
, where
\alpha\in(0,1)
. From (
2.1
) we have does not exist any continuous map on the interval with metric mean dimension biggest to 1.

Conjecture B
.

There is no any
\alpha
-Hölder continuous map
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
, with
\alpha>0
and

\emph{mdim}_{\emph{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=1.

Note if Conjecture A is true, then Conjecture B is a consequence of Conjecture A.

Conjecture C
.

If
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous map for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then

\emph{mdim}_{\emph{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi)=0.

Note if Conjecture A is true, then Conjecture C is a consequence of Conjecture A.

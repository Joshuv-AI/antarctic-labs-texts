Winding number and Zak phase in multi-band SSH models
Chen-Shen Lee, Iao-Fai Io, Hsien-chung Kao

Abstract
We use multi-band SSH models to demonstrate a prescription for calculating the correct Zak phase and winding number of multi-band systems. We verify our prescription by comparing the resultant winding number of a four-band SSH model with the edge states of a semi-infinite chain which we find by solving the equation of motion. We then also carry out an extensive comparison with the numerical results obtained by solving the matrix eigenvalue problem of finite chains with various number of sites. As a double check of our prescription, we also confirm the bulk edge correspondence in a six-band SSH model. Similar to the usual SSH model, the winding numbers associated to the left and right boundaries in a finite chain may be different if space inversion symmetry is violated in the system. We believe the prescription we propose here may also be applied to other 1D multi-band systems.

I. Introduction

Bulk-edge correspondence is one of the most notable signature of topological materials, which have been intensively studied by physicists since they were discovered
Review
. The SSH model describes a one-dimension (1D) chain of polyacetylene, on which electrons hop with staggered hopping amplitudes
SSH
. The system has the 1D winding number
\nu
as its corresponding topological invariant, and is arguably the simplest system with a topological phase. Hence, it has often been used as a toy model to illustrate the properties of this kind of systems. In particular, the number of edge states on a boundary of the system has a one-to-one correspondence to the winding number associated to the boundary see Ref.
Asboth
and references therein.

There are two sites in each unit cell of the SSH model, and thus it is a two-band model. However, it was pointed out that polyacetylene may also form a stable trimerized chain
Trimer
. To describe such a system, it is natural to consider generalizing the SSH model to a three-band one, which has been studied in Ref.
Three-band
. There is no chiral symmetry in a generic three band SSH model, and the winding number contributed by a single band is thus generally not an integer. For such a system to have a well-defined topological invariant, it is believed that one has to focus on the case that there is a space inversion symmetry in the system. Moreover, ladder-like structures of polyacetylene have also been investigated in the literature as well due to its excellent optoelectronic properties
PA-ladder
. This would of course necessitate more study of multi-band topological systems, which has been relatively scare so far.

It is known that 1D winding number in the SSH model is closely related to the Zak phase
Zak
, which is basically similar to the Berry phase
Berry
. It has been measured directly by using a 1D optical lattice to simulate the SSH model
Atala
. By adding an on-site energy term in the SSH model, one may extend it to the Rice-Mele model
Rice-Mele
. It has been shown that the Zak phase in this model is no longer quantized, since the chiral and space inversion symmetries are both violated
Atala
;
Rhim
. One may then relate the SSH model to a Chern insulator by considering a charge-pumping process in such a system
Thouless
;
polarization
. In fact, a relation between the 1D winding number and 2D Chern number may be established, if the 2D system has certain space inversion symmetry
Ext-SSH
. Making use of such a relation, one may achieve some understanding of a 2D topological system by looking into the associated 1D system, which is much simpler in general.

It has been shown in the literature that there is an ambiguity in defining the Zak phase,
\gamma
, due to its dependence on the
real-space origin and unit cell
Kudin
;
Rhim
. It is proposed in Ref.
Kudin
that one may decompose the Zak phase into the
intracellular
and
intercellular
ones. The ambiguity only affect the intracellular Zak phase. In contrast, the intercellular Zak phase is well-defined and the bulk edge correspondence is given by
n_{s}=\gamma^{\rm inter}/\pi\;({\rm mod}\;2)
, with
n_{s}
the number of edge states
Rhim
. Although this seems to resolve the ambiguity issue in a two band system, such a difficulty remains for generic multi-band systems, since the contribution to
\gamma^{\rm inter}
from a band may be as large as
2\pi
. As there are usually more than one occupied bands in this kind of topological systems, holonomies or equivalently a Wilson loop must be used to describe their topological structures
Holonomy
;
W-loop
. This is effectively the non-Abelian generalization of the Berry phase
Zak
;
Berry
. It certainly has a much richer structure than its Abelian counterpart and has a wide application in physics
H-application
. Therefore, it is desirable to gain a deeper understanding about the non-Abelian Berry phase using a simple system such as a multi-band SSH model.

Because of the simplicity of the SSH model, we think it is worthwhile to study multi-band SSH models so that we may come up with a “recipe” which leads to the correct Zak phase and winding number. We believe that the lessons we learn from such a study may serve as a stepping stone for other 1D and 2D multi-band topological systems in the future. The rest of the paper is organized in the following way. In Sec. II, we focus on the four-band SSH model. We first carry out a detailed analysis of the edge states of a semi-infinite chain. We then propose a “recipe” to resolve the gauge ambiguity. We consider the half-filling case throughout the paper and calculate the winding number of the system analytically by summing up the contribution from the lower half of bands. By making use of the secular equation ingeniously, we are able to carry out the calculation without having to solve the secular equation explicitly. We confirm the correctness of our proposal by comparing the winding number with the number of edge states in a semi-infinite chain. Similar to the usual SSH model, there are both the left and right boundaries in a finite chain. For the bulk-edge correspondence to work properly, we must choose the four sites by the left and right edges to form the unit cells accordingly. In Sec. III, we carry out numerical calculations by solving the matrix eigenvalue problem of a finite chain. To confirm the validity of our prescription, we check the bulk-edge correspondence in the system by comparing the two winding numbers associated to the left and right boundaries to the number of edge states seen in the numerical studies. By varying the total number of sites, we go through a comprehensive comparison of the analytical and numerical results. This provides a very strict check on the recipe that we propose. As a double check, we also use the same method to carry out similar analysis for the six-band SSH model in Appendix B. Finally, we make conclusion and discuss possible topics for future studies in Sec. IV.

II. Edge states and winding numbers of the four-band SSH models

Let’s begin with a right semi-infinite chain of four-band SSH models starting from the site
A_{1}
:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptH_{\rm SSH-4}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\{t_{0}{\bf A}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf B}_{j}+t_{1}{\bf B}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf C}_{j}+t_{2}{\bf C}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf D}_{j}+t_{3}{\bf D}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf A}_{j+1}\right\}+\mbox{ h. c.}.

(1)

Here,
j
denotes the unit cell.
t_{0},t_{1},t_{2}
are the intra-cell hopping amplitudes and
t_{3}
the inter-cell hopping amplitude, respectively. This is a generalization from the dimer and trimer models for polyacetylene chains and it is a four-band model. From our experience with the SSH model, it is expected that the 1D winding number may now range from
0
to
3
.

The corresponding energy eigenstates would satisfy the following equation of motion(EOM):

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega A_{j}-\left(t_{0}B_{j}+t_{3}D_{j-1}\right)=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega B_{j}-\left(t_{0}A_{j}+t_{1}C_{j}\right)=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega C_{j}-\left(t_{1}B_{j}+t_{2}D_{j}\right)=0,

(4)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega D_{j}-\left(t_{2}C_{j}+t_{3}A_{j+1}\right)=0,

(5)

where
\Omega
denotes the energy of the system. The EOM is a linear recurrence relation with constant coefficients. As the
A_{1}
site is the leftmost one, it is impossible for electrons to hop further to the left on this site. Hence, the open boundary condition (OBC) is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega A_{1}-t_{0}B_{1}=0,

which may be simplified to

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptD_{0}=0.

(6)

To find the solutions to the EOM, we let

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptA_{j}=\alpha s^{j},B_{j}=\beta s^{j},C_{j}=\gamma s^{j},D_{j}=\delta s^{j}.

(7)

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\alpha-\left(t_{0}\beta+t_{3}s^{-1}\delta\right)=0,

(8)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\beta-\left(t_{0}\alpha+t_{1}\gamma\right)=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\gamma-\left(t_{1}\beta+t_{2}\delta\right)=0,

(10)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\delta-\left(t_{2}\gamma+t_{3}s\alpha\right)=0.

(11)

We may have non-trivial solutions for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and
\delta
only if the determinant formed by the coefficients of them is vanishing, i.e.

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega^{4}-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\Omega^{2}+\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}u\right)=0,

(12)

with
u=\left(s+s^{-1}\right)/2.
We will refer to this as the secular equation in this article. Note that the equation is quartic in
\Omega
, so there are four roots of
\Omega
to the equation. Since the above equation is quadratic in
s
and is symmetric with respect to space inversion,
s\to s^{-1}
, the most general forms of
A_{j},B_{j},C_{j}
and
D_{j}
are given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptA_{j}=\alpha_{+}s^{j}+\alpha_{-}s^{-j},B_{j}=\beta_{+}s^{j}+\beta_{-}s^{-j},C_{j}=\gamma_{+}s^{j}+\gamma_{-}s^{-j},D_{j}=\delta_{+}s^{j}+\delta_{-}s^{-j}.

(13)

If we assume
|s|<1
and focus on the edge states, then physical solutions exist only if
\alpha_{-}=\beta_{-}=\gamma_{-}=\delta_{-}=0
. Combining this with the boundary condition (BC)
D_{0}=0
, we see in turn that
\delta_{+}=0
. As a result, eq. (
8
) reduces to

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\alpha_{+}-t_{0}\beta_{+}=0,

(14)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\beta_{+}-\left(t_{0}\alpha_{+}+t_{1}\gamma_{+}\right)=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\gamma_{+}-t_{1}\beta_{+}=0,

(16)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptt_{2}\gamma_{+}+t_{3}s\alpha_{+}=0.

(17)

There are four equations with only three independent variables
\alpha_{+},\beta_{+}
and
\gamma_{+}
. By choosing arbitrary three equations among the four, we may obtain four
3\times 3
determinants formed by these coefficients. To have non-trivial solutions, all the four determinants must be vanishing. By counting the degrees of freedom, one see that only two among the four conditions are independent. For simplicity, let’s choose the first three equations to achieve the determinant for the first condition, and choose the first, the second , and the last equations to achieve that for the second condition. They take the following forms:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\left(\Omega^{2}-t_{0}^{2}-t_{1}^{2}\right)=0,

(18)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptt_{0}\left(t_{0}t_{2}-t_{1}t_{3}s\right)-t_{2}\Omega^{2}=0.

(19)

The solutions to the above equations are given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pts=\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}},\;\Omega=0,

(20)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pts=-\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{t_{0}t_{3}},\;\Omega=\pm\sqrt{t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}}.

(21)

From the above results, we see there are indeed four phases:

1)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|<1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{t_{0}t_{3}}\right|<1
, three edge states, one of which is a zero energy state.

2)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|>1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{t_{0}t_{3}}\right|<1
, two edge states.

3)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|<1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{t_{0}t_{3}}\right|>1
, one zero energy edge state.

4)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|>1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{t_{0}t_{3}}\right|>1
, no edge state.

From the bulk edge correspondence, we expect the winding numbers
\nu
of the system to be
3,2,1
and
0
, respectively. To calculate
\nu
or equivalently the Zak phase
Zak
, which may be used to classify the system, we have to impose the periodic boundary condition. Consequently, we obtain the following Bloch Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm SSH-4}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&t_{0}&0&t_{3}{\rm e}^{-ip}\cr t_{0}&0&t_{1}&0\cr 0&t_{1}&0&t_{2}\cr t_{3}{\rm e}^{ip}&0&t_{2}&0\cr\end{matrix}\right),

(22)

where
p
is the lattice momentum. The chiral operator that anti-commutes with
\mathcal{H}_{\rm SSH-4}
is given by

\displaystyle\Pi=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0&0\cr 0&-1&0&0\cr 0&0&1&0\cr 0&0&0&-1\cr\end{matrix}\right).

(23)

Its existence grantees that eigenstates of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm SSH-4}
with non-zero energy would always appear in pairs with eigenvalues
(\Omega,-\Omega)
and the corresponding eigenstates are related by
\left|-\Omega\right\rangle=\Pi\left|\Omega\right\rangle
. In contrast, zero energy eigenstates can always be chosen to be chiral eigenstates and the left-handed and right-handed states are decoupled from each other. Thus, this kind of systems belong to the BDI class
Periodic-table
.

In this case, eqs. (
12
) becomes

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega^{4}(p)-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\Omega^{2}(p)+\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos p\right)=0

(24)

The four solutions are given by
\Omega_{1}(p)=-\omega_{2}(p),\Omega_{2}(p)=-\omega_{1}(p),\Omega_{3}(p)=\omega_{1}(p)
, and
\Omega_{4}(p)=\omega_{2}(p)
, with

\displaystyle\omega_{1,2}(p)=\Biggl\{\frac{\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\mp\Bigl[\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)^{2}-4\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos p\right)\Bigr]^{1/2}}{2}\Biggr\}^{1/2}.

(25)

Since we focus on the half-filled case, we must sum over the lower two bands to achieve the topological invariant
\nu
here in contrast to the case of two-band models. The analytical expression for the 1D winding number
\nu
is given by

\displaystyle\nu=\sum_{a=1,2}\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dp\,\Bigl\{\left\langle\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle\Bigr\},

(26)

where
\left|\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle\equiv\partial_{p}\left|\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle
, with
a
the band index
Zak
;
Berry
;
TKNN
.

To proceed, we must find the corresponding wave functions:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left|\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle={\cal N}_{a}(p)\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p),\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p),\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)\right\}^{T}.

(27)

\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p),\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p),
and
\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)
satisfy eq. (
52
) and
a=1,\ldots,4
. It is known in the literature that there exist ambiguity in determining the Zak phase,
\gamma
, in 1D topological systems
Atala
;
Kudin
;
Rhim
. It is proposed that such an ambiguity may be resolved by separating it into the intracellular and intercellular Zak phases. It has been shown that the intercellular Zak phase is origin-independent. Hence, it is suggested that the bulk edge correspondence is given by
n_{s}=\gamma^{\rm inter}/\pi\;({\rm mod}\;2)
, with
n_{s}
the number of edge states. This, however, cannot be applied to a generic multi-band system, since the contribution to
\gamma^{\rm inter}
from a band may be as large as
2\pi
.

Let’s now spell out the details of our “recipe” to achieve the correct winding number. From eq. (
52
), we see that
\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p),\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p),
and
\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)
may be expressed as

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p)=\Bigl\{t_{3}\left(\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)-t_{1}^{2}\right){\rm e}^{-ip}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}\Bigr\},

(28)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)=\Omega_{a}(p)\Bigl\{-t_{1}t_{2}-t_{0}t_{3}{\rm e}^{-ip}\Bigr\},

(29)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)=\Bigl\{t_{2}\left(\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)-t_{0}^{2}\right)+t_{0}t_{1}t_{3}{\rm e}^{-ip}\Bigr\},

(30)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)=\Omega_{a}(p)\Bigl\{t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}-\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)\Bigr\}.

(31)

Here,
{\cal N}_{a}(p)
is a proper normalization constant that enforces the condition
\left\langle\Omega_{a}(p)\,\right|\left.\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle=1.
In other words,

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt{\cal N}_{a}(p)=\left\{\Bigl|\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p)\Bigr|^{2}+\left|\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)\right|^{2}+\Bigl|\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)\Bigr|^{2}+\left|\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)\right|^{2}\right\}^{-1/2}.

(32)

Here, we have deliberately chosen
\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)
to be real, which is equivalent to choosing a gauge. The reason for such a choice is that we have in mind to express
\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)
in terms of
\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)
, which is in turn dictated by the BC on the left edge:
D_{0}=0
. To confirm that this is indeed the correct gauge choice, we will compare the resultant winding number to the number of edge states both analytically and numerically later on. We would like to emphasize here that although the bulk edge correspondence is expected to hold in a topological system, it is by no means trivial to come up with a viable prescription for the correct form of the winding number so that such a correspondence is enforced explicitly in a multi-band system.

We relegate the details of the calculation to Appendix A and would just give the final expression for the winding number here.

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\nu=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dp\,\Biggl\{\frac{t_{0}t_{3}{\rm e}^{ip}}{t_{1}t_{2}+t_{0}t_{3}{\rm e}^{ip}}+\frac{t_{1}t_{3}{\rm e}^{ip}}{2\Bigl(t_{0}t_{2}+t_{1}t_{3}{\rm e}^{ip}\Bigr)}\Biggr\},

(33)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt=2\theta\left(|t_{0}t_{3}|-|t_{1}t_{2}|\right)+\theta\left(|t_{1}t_{3}|-|t_{0}t_{2}|\right),

(34)

where
\theta(x)
is the step function. One can easily check that the above result is identical to that obtained by analyzing the edge states.

To verify the above results numerically, we must consider a finite chain of four-band SSH model. Let’s start with a system consisting of
N
unit-cells, i. e.
4N
sites:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptH_{\rm SSH}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left\{t_{0}{\bf A}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf B}_{j}+t_{1}{\bf B}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf C}_{j}+t_{2}{\bf C}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf D}_{j}+t_{3}{\bf D}_{j}^{\dagger}{\bf A}_{j+1}\right\}+t_{0}{\bf A}_{N}^{\dagger}{\bf B}_{N}+t_{1}{\bf B}_{N}^{\dagger}{\bf C}_{N}+t_{2}{\bf C}_{N}^{\dagger}{\bf D}_{N}+\mbox{h. c.}.

(35)

It is straightforward to see that the energy eigenstates would satisfy the following EOM:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega A_{j}-\left(t_{0}B_{j}+t_{3}D_{j-1}\right)=0,\quad\mbox{\rm for }j=2,\ldots,N;

(36)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega B_{j}-\left(t_{0}A_{j}+t_{1}C_{j}\right)=0,\quad\quad\mbox{\rm for }j=2,\ldots,N;

(37)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega C_{j}-\left(t_{1}B_{j}+t_{2}D_{j}\right)=0,\quad\quad\mbox{\rm for }j=2,\ldots,N;

(38)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega D_{j}-\left(t_{2}C_{j}+t_{3}A_{j+1}\right)=0,\quad\mbox{\rm for }j=1,\ldots,N-1.

(39)

Meanwhile, the BC’s are

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptD_{0}=0;

(40)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptA_{N+1}=0.

(41)

By using the most general forms of
A_{j},B_{j},C_{j}
and
D_{j}
given in eq. (
13
), the above BC’s become

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\delta_{+}+\delta_{-}=0;

(42)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\alpha_{+}+\alpha_{-}=0.

(43)

From eq. (
52
), we may express
\alpha_{I}
in terms of
\delta_{I}
:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\alpha_{I}=\frac{\left(t_{3}\Omega^{2}-t_{1}^{2}t_{3}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}s_{I}\right)\delta_{I}}{s_{I}\Omega\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}-\Omega^{2}\right)},

(44)

where
I=+,-
and
s_{+}=s,s_{-}=s^{-1}.
Substitute the above results to the BC’s and they may then be simplified to

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\delta_{+}+\delta_{-}=0;

(45)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left(t_{3}\Omega^{2}-t_{1}^{2}t_{3}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}s\right)s^{N}\delta_{+}+\left(t_{3}\Omega^{2}-t_{1}^{2}t_{3}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}s^{-1}\right)s^{-N}\delta_{-}=0.

(46)

Non-trivial solutions for
\delta_{+}
and
\delta_{-}
exist only if the determinant formed by them is vanishing. This leads to the characteristic equation of
s
:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptt_{3}\left(\Omega^{2}-t_{1}^{2}\right)U_{N-1}(u)+t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}U_{N}(u)=0,

(47)

where
U_{N}(u)
is the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind of order
N
.
Combining this with the secular equation in (
12
), we may find
u
and the corresponding energy. Of course, this can only be done numerically. We may compare the energy spectrum and wave functions obtained in this way with those obtained by numerical diagonalization of the Hamiltonian matrix. Hence, it provides a double check on the numerical results.

Since there is also a right boundary in a finite chain, there would be right edge states as well. To find these associated edge states and their energies, we must consider a left semi-infinite chain of four-band SSH models with OBC ending at the site
D_{N}
. Upon space inversion, we now rename
D_{N},C_{N},B_{N},A_{N}
to be
\tilde{A}_{1},\tilde{B}_{1},\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{D}_{1}
. As a result, we may achieve the right edge states from the results of the right semi-infinite chain easily by identifying
\tilde{t}_{0}=t_{2},\tilde{t}_{1}=t_{1},\tilde{t}_{2}=t_{0}
and
\tilde{t}_{3}=t_{3}
. Here,
\tilde{t}_{0},\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2},\tilde{t}_{3}
are the hopping amplitudes between
(\tilde{A},\tilde{B}),(\tilde{B},\tilde{C}),(\tilde{C},\tilde{D})
and
(\tilde{D},\tilde{A})
sites, respectively. Consequently, we have for the right boundary

1)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|<1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{0}}{t_{2}t_{3}}\right|<1
, three edge states, one of which is a zero energy state.

2)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|>1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{0}}{t_{2}t_{3}}\right|<1
, two edge states.

3)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|<1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{0}}{t_{2}t_{3}}\right|>1
, one zero energy edge state.

4)

\left|\frac{t_{0}t_{2}}{t_{1}t_{3}}\right|>1,\left|\frac{t_{1}t_{0}}{t_{2}t_{3}}\right|>1
, no edge state.

If
t_{0}=t_{2}
then the system is inversion symmetric with respect to the center of a unit cell. When this is the case, the numbers of edge states on the left and right boundaries would be the same. Moreover, the Zak phase or equivalently the winding number contributed by each band would be quantized, as predicted in Ref.
Space-inversion
. In contrast, if
t_{0}\neq t_{2}
such a symmetry would be spoiled and the numbers of edge states on the left and right boundaries would generally be different. Thus, it is possible that the two boundaries of the system are in different phases. Furthermore, the Zak phase contributed by each band would not be quantized in general. This is very different from the two-band SSH model, and may only appear in a system with four or more bands. The conclusion that we achieve here may be used to understand some of the results obtained in Ref.
Trimer
. Applying the analysis for the edge states that we carried out previously to the three band SSH model, it may be shown that
\nu_{\rm L}=2\theta\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)
and
\nu_{\rm R}=2\theta\left(t_{0}-t_{1}\right)
for the case that
N_{\rm tot}=3N
. As we identify the parameters
(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2})
with
(u,v,w)
in the article, we see immediately why there are three phases when we vary
w
while setting
u=1
and
v=2
.

We would like to point out this kind of phenomenon can only appear multi-band SSH models. The reason is that the usual SSH has both chiral and space inversion symmetries, and it is impossible to break one of them without violating the other in such a system. On the contrary, we may separate the effect of the two symmetries in a multi-band SSH model. In particular, a three band SSH model may only have inversion symmetry, while a four-band SSH model may only have chiral symmetry. Hence, multi-band SSH models are examples of study topological crystalline insulators
TC-insulators
.

We may also consider finite chains with other total numbers of sites,
N_{\rm tot}=4N+1,4N+2,4N-1
. In these cases, even if
t_{0}=t_{2}
so that a complete unit cell itself is inversion symmetric, such a symmetry may still be violated generally by the BC on the right boundary. We will just summarize the results here.

1)

N_{\rm tot}=4N+1
, the right BC:
B_{N+1}=0
.
\tilde{t}_{0}=t_{3},\tilde{t}_{1}=t_{2},\tilde{t}_{2}=t_{1},
and
\tilde{t}_{3}=t_{0}
. The corresponding characteristic equation is

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptt_{0}t_{3}U_{N-1}(u)+t_{1}t_{2}U_{N}(u)=0,

(48)

2)

N_{\rm tot}=4N+2
, the right BC:
C_{N+1}=0
.
\tilde{t}_{0}=t_{0},\tilde{t}_{1}=t_{3},\tilde{t}_{2}=t_{2},
and
\tilde{t}_{3}=t_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptt_{0}t_{1}t_{3}U_{N-1}(u)+t_{2}\left(\Omega^{2}-t_{0}^{2}\right)U_{N}(u)=0.

(49)

3)

\displaystyle\omega_{1,2}=\Biggl\{\frac{\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\mp\Bigl[\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)^{2}-4\left\{t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos(\pi k/N)\right\}\Bigr]^{1/2}}{2}\Biggr\}^{1/2}.

Some comments are in order. First, we notice that some of the edge states in the four-band SSH model are mid-gap states with non-vanishing energy unlike those in the usual SSH model. In fact, the energies of some of the edge states may lie very close to a band edge. It is therefore not clear whether this kind of edge states are robust under perturbation. Secondly, the above results seem to suggest that

\displaystyle N_{\rm tot}\equiv\nu_{\rm L}+\nu_{\rm R}\left(\mbox{mod 4}\right).

Finally, as a double check of the prescription for calculating the right winding number that we put forward in this paper, we also study the six-band SSH model by following similar procedures taken previously. Since the reasoning has been laid out explicitly in the previous section, we will only summarize the main results and leave them in Appendix B. Suffice to say that the results confirm that our winding number prescription is indeed correct.

.4
IV. Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we propose a recipe to calculate the correct Zak phase and winding number for a multi-band topological system. We use the four-band SSH model to explicitly demonstrate how this may be done. We first compare the winding numbers obtained in this way with the analysis of edge states of a semi-infinite chain of the model. To further check the validity of our prescription, we then consider a finite chain with various number of sites so that we may carry out an extensive comparison between the analytic formula and the numerical computation. We verify all the results predicted by our prescription, including the number of edge states on the left and right boundaries respectively and their corresponding energies. Thus, we are quite confident our prescription for calculating the winding numbers of multi-band models are correct. In contrast to the usual SSH model, even if
N_{\rm tot}=4N,6N
in these systems,
\nu_{\rm L}
and
\nu_{\rm R}
are generally different if they do not have an inversion symmetry. Thus, we need two bulk Hamiltonians to completely describe the topological phase of a finite chain for such systems. Since little assumption has been made in our analysis of the edge states in a semi-infinite chain, the method may also be applied to investigate other 1D multi-band systems of interest.

Finally, we would like to discuss some possible directions for further study. First, as we mentioned previously, the left and right winding numbers in a finite chain of a SSH model with four or more bands are generally different if the system does not have a inversion symmetry. It would be interesting to see if this phenomenon also exists in 2D or 3D topological systems. Moreover unlike the edge states in the two-band SSH model, some of the edge states in the multi-band SSH models have non-vanishing energies. In fact, some of these edge states may lie very close to the band edge, see Figs. 5b and 5c in Appendix B for example. Hence, it is not certain whether they are still robust under perturbation. In particular, if we analyze the edge state of a right semi-infinite chain of a three-band SSH model, we find that there would be two edge states with energy
\Omega=\pm t_{0}
when
t_{2}>t_{1}
and there would be no edge state when
t_{2}<t_{1}
. From our experience with the two-band SSH model, we would expect
t_{2}=t_{1}
to be a transition point and band crossing would occur in the energy spectrum of such a system. However, it may be easily seen that this is not the case by choosing
(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2})=(1,2,2)
. As a result, we cannot be sure whether the system would indeed change from a trivial phase to a topological phase when we tune the value of
t_{2}
from the
t_{2}<t_{1}
region to the
t_{2}>t_{1}
one. One possible explanation is that a three-band SSH model does not have chiral symmetry and it can have topological phase only if the system has inversion symmetry. At any rate, it is worthwhile to carry out a more detailed study to clarify this issue in the future.

Appendix A: Details of the calculation of the winding number in the four band SSH models

To calculate the winding number, we must first find the wave functions of energy eigenstates:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left|\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle={\cal N}_{a}(p)\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p),\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p),\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)\right\}^{T},

(52)

with
a=1,\ldots,4
. From eq. (
52
), we see that they are given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p)=\Bigl\{t_{3}\left(\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)-t_{1}^{2}\right){\rm e}^{-ip}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}\Bigr\},

(53)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)=\Omega_{a}(p)\Bigl\{-t_{1}t_{2}-t_{0}t_{3}{\rm e}^{-ip}\Bigr\},

(54)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)=\Bigl\{t_{2}\left(\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)-t_{0}^{2}\right)+t_{0}t_{1}t_{3}{\rm e}^{-ip}\Bigr\},

(55)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)=\Omega_{a}(p)\Bigl\{t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}-\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)\Bigr\}.

(56)

Here,
{\cal N}_{a}(p)
is a proper normalization constant that enforces the normalization condition
\left\langle\Omega_{a}(p)\,\right|\left.\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle=1.
In other words,

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt{\cal N}_{a}(p)=\left\{\Bigl|\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p)\Bigr|^{2}+\left|\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)\right|^{2}+\Bigl|\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)\Bigr|^{2}+\left|\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)\right|^{2}\right\}^{-1/2}.

(57)

Moreover, we must choose
\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)
to be real, which is equivalent to choosing a gauge. The reason for such a choice is that we should express
\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)
in terms of
\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)
, which in turn is coming from the BC on the left edge:
D_{0}=0
. We will confirm that this is indeed the correct choice by checking the bulk edge correspondence both analytically and numerically later on. By direct calculation, we see

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left|\partial_{p}\Omega(p)\right\rangle={\cal N}^{\prime}_{a}(p)\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p),\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p),\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)\right\}^{T}+{\cal N}_{a}(p)\left\{\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{a}(p),\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}_{a}(p),\tilde{\delta}^{\prime}_{a}(p)\right\}^{T},

(58)

and hence

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left\langle\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle=\frac{{\cal N}^{\prime}_{a}(p)}{{\cal N}_{a}(p)}+{\cal N}_{a}(p)^{2}\left\{\tilde{\alpha}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\beta}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\gamma}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\delta}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\delta}^{\prime}_{a}(p)\right\},

(59)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left\langle\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle=\frac{{\cal N}^{\prime}_{a}(p)}{{\cal N}_{a}(p)}+{\cal N}_{a}(p)^{2}\left\{\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\beta}^{\prime*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\delta}^{\prime*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)\right\}.

(60)

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left\langle\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle=\frac{2i{\rm Im}\left\{\tilde{\alpha}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\beta}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\gamma}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}_{a}(p)+\tilde{\delta}^{*}_{a}(p)\tilde{\delta}^{\prime}_{a}(p)\right\}}{\Bigl|\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p)\Bigr|^{2}+\left|\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)\right|^{2}+\Bigl|\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)\Bigr|^{2}+\left|\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)\right|^{2}}.

(61)

From eq. (
24
), we may achieve the following identity:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt2\Omega_{a}(p)\Omega^{\prime}_{a}(p)\left\{2\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\right\}+2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\sin p=0.

(62)

Making use of all these relations, we will arrive at

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\frac{i}{2}\Bigl\{\left\langle\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{p}\Omega_{a}(p)\right|\left.\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle\Bigr\}=\frac{H(p,a)}{G(p,a)}.

(63)

Here,

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptG(p,a)=G_{6}(p)\Omega_{a}^{6}(p)+G_{4}(p)\Omega_{a}^{4}(p)+G_{2}(p)\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)+G_{0}(p),

(64)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptH(p,a)=H_{6}(p)\Omega_{a}^{6}(p)+H_{4}(p)\Omega_{a}^{4}(p)+H_{2}(p)\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)+H_{0}(p),

with

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptG_{6}(p)=1,\quad G_{4}(p)=-2t_{0}^{2}-2t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2},

(65)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptG_{2}(p)=-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}-2t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}-2t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+6t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos p,

(66)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptG_{0}(p)=\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}\right)\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{1}t_{3}\cos p\right),

(67)

and

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptH_{6}(p)=-1,\quad H_{4}(p)=2t_{0}^{2}+2t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2},

(68)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptH_{2}(p)=-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}+2t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}-3t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos p,

Note that the dependence on the band index

only shows up through
\Omega_{a}(p)
. It turns out that a direct calculation would be too tedious to carry out even using mathematica. Thus, it is important that we exploit this property to simplify the calculation of the winding number.

Since
\Omega_{a}(p)
satisfies eq. (
24
), we may use the identity to simplify
G(p,a)
and
H(p,a)
. The best way to utilize it is to consider the polynomial division of
G(p,a)
and

\displaystyle K(p,a)=\Omega_{a}^{4}(p)-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)+\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos p\right),

which is the polynomial of degree four in
\Omega_{a}(p)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{G}_{2}(p)=-2\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}-t_{2}^{4}-t_{0}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{2}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-t_{3}^{4}-4t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos p\right),

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{G}_{0}(p)=2\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}-t_{3}^{2}\right)\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-2t_{1}t_{3}\cos p\right).

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\tilde{H}_{2}(p)=2t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}-2t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}-2t_{2}^{4}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-3t_{2}^{2}t_{3}^{2}-t_{3}^{4}-5t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\cos p,

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\sum_{a=1,2}\frac{\tilde{H}_{2}(p)\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)+\tilde{H}_{0}(p)}{\tilde{G}_{2}(p)\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)+\tilde{G}_{0}(p)}

\displaystyle\hskip-45.52458pt=\frac{2\tilde{G}_{2}(p)\tilde{H}_{2}(p)\Bigl[\Omega_{1}^{2}(p)\Omega_{2}^{2}(p)\Bigr]+\Bigl[\tilde{G}_{2}(p)\tilde{H}_{0}(p)+\tilde{G}_{0}(p)\tilde{H}_{2}(p)\Bigr]\Bigl[\Omega_{1}^{2}(p)+\Omega_{2}^{2}(p)\Bigr]+2\tilde{G}_{0}(p)\tilde{H}_{0}(p)}{\tilde{G}_{2}^{2}(p)\Bigl[\Omega_{1}^{2}(p)\Omega_{2}^{2}(p)\Bigr]+\tilde{G}_{2}(p)\tilde{G}_{0}(p)\Bigl[\Omega_{1}^{2}(p)+\Omega_{2}^{2}(p)\Bigr]+\tilde{G}_{0}^{2}(p)}.

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega D_{j}-\left(t_{4}F_{j}+t_{0}A_{j+1}\right)=0,

(85)

which is again a linear recurrence relation with constant coefficients. After simplification, the OBC is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptF_{0}=0.

(86)

To find the solutions, we let

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptA_{j}=\alpha s^{j},B_{j}=\beta s^{j},C_{j}=\gamma s^{j},D_{j}=\delta s^{j},E_{j}=\epsilon s^{j},F_{j}=\eta s^{j}.

(87)

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\alpha-\left(t_{0}\beta+t_{5}s^{-1}\eta\right)=0,

(88)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\beta-\left(t_{0}\alpha+t_{1}\gamma\right)=0,

(89)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\gamma-\left(t_{1}\beta+t_{2}\delta\right)=0,

(90)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\delta-\left(t_{2}\gamma+t_{3}\epsilon\right)=0

(91)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\epsilon-\left(t_{3}\delta+t_{4}\eta\right)=0,

(92)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\Omega\eta-\left(t_{4}\epsilon+t_{5}s\alpha\right)=0.

(93)

We may obtain from the above equations the following secular equation:

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\Omega^{6}-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}+t_{4}^{2}+t_{5}^{2}\right)\Omega^{4}+\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{5}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}t_{5}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}t_{5}^{2}\right)\Omega^{2}

(94)

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt-\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}t_{5}^{2}+2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}t_{4}t_{5}u\right)=0.

(95)

Note that the equation is sextic in
\Omega
, so there are six roots. Similar to the four-band case, the most general forms of
A_{j},B_{j},C_{j},D_{j},E_{j}
and
F_{j}
are given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptA_{j}=\alpha_{+}s^{j}+\alpha_{-}s^{-j},B_{j}=\beta_{+}s^{j}+\beta_{-}s^{-j},C_{j}=\gamma_{+}s^{j}+\gamma_{-}s^{-j},

(96)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptD_{j}=\delta_{+}s^{j}+\delta_{-}s^{-j},E_{j}=\epsilon_{+}s^{j}+\epsilon_{-}s^{-j},F_{j}=\eta_{+}s^{j}+\eta_{-}s^{-j}.

(97)

If we focus on the edge states and assume
|s|<1
, then physical solutions exist only if
\alpha_{-}=\beta_{-}=\gamma_{-}=\delta_{-}=\epsilon_{-}=\eta_{-}=0
. After some algebra, we find the solutions are given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pts=s_{0},\;\Omega=0,

(98)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pts=s^{*}_{+},\;\Omega=\pm\left\{\frac{t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}-\sqrt{(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2})^{2}-4(t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{3}^{2})}}{2}\right\}^{1/2},

(99)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pts=s^{*}_{-},\;\Omega=\pm\left\{\frac{t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}+\sqrt{(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2})^{2}-4(t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{3}^{2})}}{2}\right\}^{1/2}.

(100)

Here,

\displaystyle s_{0}=-\frac{t_{0}t_{2}t_{4}}{t_{1}t_{3}t_{5}},\;s^{*}_{\pm}=\frac{t_{3}t_{4}\left\{t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}-t_{3}^{2}\pm\sqrt{(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2})^{2}-4(t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{3}^{2})}\right\}}{2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{5}}.

From the above results, we see there are indeed six phases as expected.

1)

\left|s_{0}\right|<1,|s^{*}_{+}|<1,|s^{*}_{-}|<1
: five edge states, one of which has zero energy.

2)

\left|s_{0}\right|>1,|s^{*}_{+}|<1,|s^{*}_{-}|<1
: four edge states.

3)

\left|s_{0}\right|<1
, either
|s^{*}_{+}|>1,|s^{*}_{-}|<1
or
|s^{*}_{+}|<1,|s^{*}_{-}|>1
: three edge states, one of which has zero energy.

4)

\left|s_{0}\right|>1
, either
|s^{*}_{+}|>1,|s^{*}_{-}|<1
or
|s^{*}_{+}|<1,|s^{*}_{-}|>1
: two edge states.

5)

\left|s_{0}\right|<1,|s^{*}_{+}|>1,|s^{*}_{-}|>1
: one zero energy edge state.

6)

\left|s_{0}\right|>1,|s^{*}_{+}|>1,|s^{*}_{-}|>1
: no edge states.

The Bloch Hamiltonian here is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm SSH-6}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&t_{0}&0&0&0&t_{5}{\rm e}^{-ip}\cr t_{0}&0&t_{1}&0&0&0\cr 0&t_{1}&0&t_{2}&0&0\cr 0&0&t_{2}&0&t_{3}&0\cr 0&0&0&t_{3}&0&t_{4}\cr t_{5}{\rm e}^{ip}&0&0&0&t_{4}&0\cr\end{matrix}\right).

(101)

From eqs. (
94
), we see the secular equation becomes

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\Omega^{6}-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}+t_{4}^{2}+t_{5}^{2}\right)\Omega^{4}+\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{5}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}t_{5}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}t_{5}^{2}\right)\Omega^{2}

(102)

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt-\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{2}^{2}t_{4}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}t_{5}^{2}+2t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}t_{4}t_{5}\cos p\right)=0.

(103)

Here, the chiral operator is given by

\displaystyle\Pi=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0&0&0&0\cr 0&-1&0&0&0&0\cr 0&0&1&0&0&0\cr 0&0&0&-1&0&0\cr 0&0&0&0&1&0\cr 0&0&0&0&0&-1\cr\end{matrix}\right).

(104)

Hence, the system also belongs to the BDI class and non-zero energy states always appear in pairs. The six solutions to eq. (
102
) are given by
\Omega_{1}(p)=-\omega_{3}(p),\Omega_{2}(p)=-\omega_{2}(p),\Omega_{3}(p)=-\omega_{1}(p),\Omega_{4}(p)=\omega_{1}(p),\Omega_{5}(p)=\omega_{2}(p)
, and
\Omega_{6}(p)=\omega_{3}(p)
.

The corresponding wave functions are given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\left|\Omega_{a}(p)\right\rangle={\cal N}_{a}(p)\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p),\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p),\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p),\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p),\tilde{\epsilon}_{a}(p),\tilde{\eta}_{a}(p)\right\}^{T},

(105)

with
a=1,\ldots,6
. Here,

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\alpha}_{a}(p)={\rm e}^{-ip}\Bigl\{t_{1}t_{3}\left(t_{0}t_{2}t_{4}{\rm e}^{ip}+t_{1}t_{3}t_{5}\right)-t_{5}\left(t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\Omega^{2}_{a}(p)+t_{5}\Omega^{4}_{a}(p)\Bigr\},

(106)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\beta}_{a}(p)=-\Omega_{a}(p){\rm e}^{-ip}\Bigl\{t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}t_{4}{\rm e}^{ip}-t_{0}t_{5}\left(t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)+t_{0}t_{5}\Omega^{2}_{a}(p)\Bigr\},

(107)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(p)=\Bigl\{t_{0}t_{3}\left(-t_{0}t_{2}t_{4}-t_{1}t_{3}t_{5}{\rm e}^{-ip}\right)+\left(t_{2}t_{3}t_{4}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{5}{\rm e}^{-ip}\right)\Omega^{2}_{a}(p)\Bigr\},

(108)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\delta}_{a}(p)=-\Omega_{a}(p)\Bigl\{-t_{0}^{2}t_{3}t_{4}-t_{1}^{2}t_{3}t_{4}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}t_{5}{\rm e}^{-ip}+t_{3}t_{4}\Omega^{2}_{a}(p)\Bigr\},

(109)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\epsilon}_{a}(p)=\Bigl\{t_{0}t_{2}\left(t_{0}t_{2}t_{4}+t_{1}t_{3}t_{5}{\rm e}^{-ip}\right)-t_{4}\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}\right)\Omega^{2}_{a}(p)+t_{4}\Omega^{4}_{a}(p)\Bigr\},

(110)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\tilde{\eta}_{a}(p)=-\Omega_{a}(p)\Bigl\{t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}+t_{0}^{2}\left(t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)-\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}\right)\Omega^{2}_{a}(p)+\Omega^{4}_{a}(p)\Bigr\}.

(111)

Again, we must choose
\tilde{\eta}_{a}(p)
to be real.

The rest of the procedures are more or less the same as those in the four-band case, except that the calculation is much more tedious. Make use of eq. (
102
) to simplify the expression and the integrand of the winding number may be expressed as:

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\sum_{a=1,2,3}\frac{\tilde{H}_{4}(p)\Omega_{a}^{4}(p)+\tilde{H}_{2}(p)\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)+\tilde{H}_{0}(p)}{\tilde{G}_{4}(p)\Omega_{a}^{4}(p)+\tilde{G}_{2}(p)\Omega_{a}^{2}(p)+\tilde{G}_{0}(p)}.

(112)

Eventually, the winding number is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\nu=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dp\,\Biggl\{4+\gamma_{1}+\bar{\gamma}_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\bar{\gamma}_{2}\Biggr\}.

(113)

Here,
\gamma_{1}=\frac{t_{1}t_{3}t_{5}}{t_{1}t_{3}t_{5}+t_{0}t_{2}t_{4}{\rm e}^{ip}}
,
\gamma_{2}=\frac{2t_{1}t_{2}\left(t_{0}^{2}t_{5}^{2}{\rm e}^{ip}+t_{3}^{2}t_{4}^{2}{\rm e}^{-ip}\right)}{t_{1}t_{2}t_{0}^{2}t_{5}^{2}{\rm e}^{ip}-t_{0}t_{3}t_{4}t_{5}\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}-t_{3}^{2}\right)-t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}^{2}t_{4}^{2}{\rm e}^{-ip}}
and
\bar{\gamma}_{1},\bar{\gamma}_{2}
are their complex conjugates, respectively. From the expressions for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, we may find the following analytic form for the winding number:

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\nu=2+\theta\left(\left|t_{1}t_{3}t_{5}\right|-\left|t_{0}t_{2}t_{4}\right|\right)-\left[2\theta\left(t_{3}^{2}t_{4}^{2}-t_{0}^{2}t_{5}^{2}\right)-1\right]\theta\left[\left|t_{1}t_{2}\left(t_{3}^{2}t_{4}^{2}-t_{0}^{2}t_{5}^{2}\right)\right|-\left|t_{0}t_{3}t_{4}t_{5}\left(t_{0}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}-t_{3}^{2}\right)\right|\right].

(114)

One can easily check that the above result is equivalent to that obtained by analyzing the edge states.

Provable Risk-Sensitive Distributional Reinforcement Learning with General Function Approximation
Yu Chen, Xiangcheng Zhang, Siwei Wang, Longbo Huang

Abstract
In the realm of reinforcement learning (RL), accounting for risk is crucial for making decisions under uncertainty, particularly in applications where safety and reliability are paramount. In this paper, we introduce a general framework on Risk-Sensitive Distributional Reinforcement Learning (RS-DisRL), with static Lipschitz Risk Measures (LRM) and general function approximation. Our framework covers a broad class of risk-sensitive RL, and facilitates analysis of the impact of estimation functions on the effectiveness of RSRL strategies and evaluation of their sample complexity. We design two innovative meta-algorithms: RS-DisRL-M , a model-based strategy for model-based function approximation, and RS-DisRL-V , a model-free approach for general value function approximation. With our novel estimation techniques via Least Squares Regression (LSR) and Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) in distributional RL with augmented Markov Decision Process (MDP), we derive the first 𝒪 ~ ​ ( K ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{K}) dependency of the regret upper bound for RSRL with static LRM, marking a pioneering contribution towards statistically efficient algorithms in this domain.

Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL)
[
43
]
has emerged as a powerful framework for sequential decision-making in dynamic and uncertain environments. While traditional RL methods, predominantly focused on maximizing the expected return, have seen significant advancements through approaches such as Q-learning
[
37
,
25
]
and policy gradients
[
28
,
10
]
, they often fall short in real-world scenarios demanding strict risk control, such as financial investment
[
9
]
, medical treatment
[
16
]
, and automous driving
[
11
]
.

The significance of comprehending risk management in RL has led to the emergence of Risk-Sensitive RL (RSRL). Unlike risk-neutral RL, which primarily focuses on maximizing expected returns, RSRL seeks to optimize risk metrics, such as entropy risk measures (ERM)
[
17
,
18
]
or conditional value-at-risk (CVaR)
[
46
]
, of the possible
cumulative reward
which emphasizes its distributional characteristics. However, traditional RL framework based on Q-learning which typically considers the mean of reward-to-go and corresponding Bellman equation, cannot efficiently capture the characteristics of the cumulative reward’s distribution. Therefore, there has been an upsurge of interest in Distributional RL (DisRL) due to its capacity to understand the intrinsic distributional attributes of cumulative rewards, which has already achieved significant empirical success in risk-sensitive tasks
[
8
,
14
,
30
,
45
,
34
]
.

However, there remains a dearth of comprehensive theoretical insights into the sample complexity of Risk-Sensitive Distributional RL (RS-DisRL), particularly in scenarios encompassing general risk measures and function approximation. Previous theory works of RS-DisRL have primarily been constrained to tabular MDPs
[
6
,
32
]
which fail in extending to infinite-state settings, or have been confined to specific risk measures such as CVaR or ERM
[
41
]
.

In this paper, we delve into the RS-DisRL with
staic Lipschitz risk measures
(LRM), a general risk measure class that includes various well-known risk measures such as coherent risk, convex risk, CVaR, and ERM.
In order to address the challenges posed by extremely large or infinite state spaces, we consider two distinct general function approximation scenarios: model-based function approximation and value function approximation.
The model-based function approximation, as extensively used in prior works such as
[
42
,
35
,
36
]
, typically assumes that the agent has access to a model class that contains the true transition model. On the other hand, the general value function approximation, as explored in
[
48
,
26
,
2
,
49
,
47
]
, offers the agent a distributionally Bellman-complete value function class with the true value distribution.

Under these settings, we introduce general model-based and model-free meta-algorithms, respectively, and employ estimate techniques including Least Squares Regression (LSR) and Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), achieving a sublinear regret upper bound with respect to the number of episodes. Importantly, our work establishes the first statistically efficient framework for RS-DisRL with static Lipschitz risk measures.

Challenges
Significant gaps persist in our quest to achieve statistically efficient RS-DisRL with static LRM.
(i) The utilization of static LRM in RSRL presents unique complexities. Unlike standard RL, where the focus is on maximizing the expected cumulative reward, RSRL with static LRM involves optimizing the entire distribution of cumulative rewards. This distinct characteristic makes the traditional Q-learning methods, which typically consider the mean of reward-to-go and the corresponding Bellman equation for mean value functions, inadequate for characterizing policy performance and the reward’s distribution.
(ii)
In RSRL with static LRM, the optimal policy is non-Markovian, dependent not only on the current state but also on the rewards received thus far. Thus, it is hard to extend previous works for learning a Markovian policy within polynomial sample complexity.

Technical Contribution
To surmount these obstacles, our approach involves several novel technical components. (i) We integrate rigorous distribution analysis techniques from DisRL into the RSRL framework and design novel distributional learning in augmented MDPs, which help us better understand the distributional characteristics of the problem objective.
(ii) We pioneer the application of LSR in the exploration process of distributional RL with augmented MDP, incorporating our innovative regression technique tailored for cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) (see Sections
6.1
and
7.1
).
(iii) Furthermore, we extend traditional MLE methods to DisRL within the augmented MDP framework, supported by a novel connection technique: the augmented simulation lemma (Lemma
D.2
). In these manners, we present the first statistically efficient algorithms for RSRL with static LRM in this paper.

Lipschitz Risk Measures

Lipschitz Risk Measures
(LRM) are quantified by a function
\rho:{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
mapping random variables
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
to real numbers, distinguished by two critical properties.

C1. Law invariance:
If
Z,W\in{\mathcal{Z}}
have the same distribution functions,
F_{Z}=F_{W}
, then
\rho(Z)=\rho(W)
.

C2. Lipschitz continuity:
Consider the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
over the set of all distribution functions of the random variable class
{\mathcal{Z}}

L_{\infty}(\rho)
such that
\left|\rho(Z)-\rho(W)\right|\leq L_{\infty}(\rho)\|F_{Z}-F_{W}\|_{\infty}
holds for any
Z,W\in{\mathcal{Z}}

The law invariance condition, foundational in risk measure studies
[

20
,
33

[

]

[

[

]
, highlighting the versatility of LRM. Popular metrics like Entropy Risk Measures (ERM) and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) also align with these conditions, with Lipschitz constants
L_{\infty}(\operatorname{ERM})=\frac{\exp(|\gamma|H)-1}{|\gamma|}
and
L_{\infty}(\operatorname{CVaR})=\frac{H}{a}

Augmented MDPs and Distributional Bellman Equation

The key of our analysis revolves around the distributional Bellman equation applied to augmented MDPs.
Recognizing that learning an optimal history-dependent policy
\pi^{*}
can be prohibitively sample-intensive, previous works
[
7
,
6
]
leverage the concept of augmented MDP in risk-sensitive conditions,
where any history-dependent policy
\tilde{\bm{\pi}}
corresponds to a
Markov
policy
{\bm{\pi}}^{\dagger}
in the augmented MDP.
This equivalence allows for facilitating effective risk-sensitive policy learning without sacrificing computational tractability.

Augmented MDPs

We embrace the notion of augmented MDPs, originally introduced by
[
7
]
and widely used in RSRL with static risk measures
[
6
,
46
,
52
]
. In the context of augmented MDPs, the state space is expanded to
{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}=\{s^{\dagger}_{h}=(s_{h},y_{h})\}
, where
y_{h}=\sum_{h=1}^{H}r_{h}
represents the cumulative reward accumulated up to time step
h
.We denote the augmented MDP as
{\mathcal{M}}^{\dagger}=({\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger},{\mathcal{A}},K,H,\{{\mathbb{P}}_{h}\}_{h=1}^{H},\{\mathbb{R}_{h}\}_{h=1}^{H})

\mathbb{T}_{h}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h}):=\mathbb{P}_{h}(s_{h+1}|s_{h},a_{h})\mathbb{R}_{h}(y_{h+1}-y_{h}|s_{h},a_{h})

Let
\Pi^{\dagger}
denote the set of the Markov policies within the augmented MDP
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}

[
6

\tilde{\pi}
in original MDP, there exists a Markov policy in the augmented MDP
{\bm{\pi}}\in\Pi^{\dagger}

. This result underscores the equivalence between the distribution of cumulative rewards under a history-dependent policy in original MDP and a corresponding Markov policy in the augmented MDP.

Theoretical Guarantees

The theoretical guarantees for the
RS-DisRL-M

M-Est

Condition 6.2

(Concentration)
.

For
\delta\in(0,1]
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\in\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}_{k}
holds for every
k\in[K]

The concentration condition, common in theoretical RL analysis
[
1

25
,
5
]

Condition 6.3

(General elliptical potential)
.

For
\delta\in(0,1]
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, the supremum distance between the CDF of the chosen estimated model

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|F_{Z_{\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}}-F_{Z_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\xi(K,H,{\bm{\Theta}},\beta,\delta)

is the complexity bound determined by the estimation function

6.1
Estimation by Model-Based LSR Approach

Least Squares Regression (LSR), a well-established estimation technique, has been effectively utilized in linear and linear mixture MDPs
[
27
,
54
]
. Its common application involves regression on combinations of the transition model with bounded functions. In risk-neutral scenarios, it’s often paired with mean value functions
[
27
]
, while in risk-sensitive settings, utility functions are preferred
[
12
,
51
]
. This section explores a novel approach in distributional RL by combining the transition model with mixed distribution functions based on the distributional Bellman equation for CDFs. The newly proposed
M-Est-LSR
algorithm represents a statistically efficient LSR method tailored for this context.

The intuition of LSR is to approximate the Bellman operator with the empirical sample. Different from previous stuides
[
27
,
18
,
12
]
, we have to analyze the augmented distributional Bellman equation for transition model
{\bm{\theta}}
. Denote
F_{h}^{{\bm{\pi}},{\bm{\theta}}}(x|s_{h}^{\dagger}):=\int_{a_{h}}\pi_{h}(a_{h}|s_{h}^{\dagger})F_{h}^{{\bm{\pi}},{\bm{\theta}}}(x|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})
, we have the following Bellman equation

\displaystyle F_{h}^{{\bm{\pi}},{\bm{\theta}}}(x|s_{h}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\int_{(a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})}\pi_{h}(a_{h}|s_{h}^{\dagger})\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})F_{h+1}^{{\bm{\pi}},{{\bm{\theta}}}}\left(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{h})\middle|s_{h+1}^{\dagger}\right)\,.

Notice that the transition
\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}
for augmented MDP compresses the real transition
{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{h}}
in original MDP and the reward distribution
\mathbb{R}_{h}
. However, the only empirical observation available to the agent is the transfer sample
(s_{k,h},a_{k,h})\to s_{k,h+1}
and the reward sample
r_{k,h}\sim\mathbb{R}_{h}(s_{k,h},a_{k,h})
. Therefore, we have to decompose the augmented Bellman operator for estimating the transition models of original MDP. To do so, we design a mixed distribution function

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{k\!,h\!+\!1}(s)\!:=\!\!\int_{y_{k\!,h}}^{1}\!\!\!{\mathbb{R}}_{h}(r|s_{k\!,h},\!a_{k\!,h})F^{{{\bm{\pi}}}^{k}\!,\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h\!+\!1}(x_{k\!,h}\!\!-\!r|s,\!y_{k\!,h}\!\!+\!r)dr\,,

where
{\bm{\pi}}^{k}
and
\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}
are given by the optimistic planning (Line
4
in Algorithm
1
) based on the information before episode
k
. Here
x_{k,h}
is defined as Eq.(
C.1
), which maximizes the diameter of
\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}
with mixed CDFs, intuitively contributing to the exploration direction by maximizing the uncertainty. For simplicity, we denote the combination form
[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{h}}\widehat{F}_{k,h+1}](s_{h},a_{h}):=\int_{s_{h+1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}|s_{h},a_{h})\widehat{F}_{k,h+1}(s_{h+1})
, and the combination set
\mathcal{W}_{{\bm{\Theta}}}:=\{[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{h}}F]:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to[0,1]:{\bm{\theta}}\in{\bm{\Theta}},F:{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,1]\}
.

We are now ready to present the procedure of the estimation function
M-Est-LSR
.
Due to space limitations, we defer the formal pseudocode to the appendix (see Algorithm
4
).
First,
M-Est-LSR
estimate a model
\theta^{\mathrm{LSR}}_{k,h}
for step
h
at episode
k
by LSR based on the information before episode
k-1
, which serves as the center of the confidence set:

\displaystyle\theta^{\mathrm{LSR}}_{k,h}\!\leftarrow\!\argmin_{\theta_{h}\in\Theta_{h}}\!\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\!\left(\widehat{F}_{i\!,h\!+\!1}(s_{i\!,h\!+\!1})\!-\![{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta_{h}}\widehat{F}_{i\!,h\!+\!1}](s_{i\!,h},a_{i\!,h})\right)^{2}\,.

Then, the confidence set for each step
h\in[H]
is constructed by previous history

\displaystyle\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}:=\left\{\theta_{h}\in\Theta_{h}:\mathrm{Dist}_{\mathcal{H}_{k-1},h}^{\mathrm{LSR}}(\theta_{h}||{\theta}^{\mathrm{LSR}}_{k,h})\leq\beta^{\mathrm{LSR}}\right\}\,.

where the Euclidean-type distance function is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dist}_{\mathcal{H}_{k},h}^{\mathrm{LSR}}(\theta^{1}||\theta^{2}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left([{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{1}}\widehat{F}_{i,h\!+\!1}](s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\!-\![{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{2}}\widehat{F}_{i,h\!+\!1}](s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\right)^{2}

Finally, the function returns the confidence set
\widehat{{\bm{\Theta}}}_{k}=\left\{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\bm{\Theta}}:\theta_{h}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h},h\in[H]\right\}
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let the confidence radius for LSR
\beta^{\mathrm{LSR}}\!:=8\log(2H^{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{C}({\bm{\Theta}},1/K,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1})/\delta)+4\sqrt{\log(4HK^{2}/\delta)}

and

In this section,
we design a novel LSR approach

for random-variable estimation through CDFs in the augmented MDP.
To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to present the statistically efficient LSR estimation for DisRL with general value function approximation.

Denote
F_{h}(\cdot|s^{\dagger},a)
as the CDF of

V-Est-LSR
focuses on estimating the Bellman operator with real transition probability. For a given target CDF

and
{\bm{\pi}}

in episode

gives us an empirical sample of

Conclusion

We give a comprehensive discussion of RS-DisRL with static LRM and general function approximation. We propose the model-based meta-algorithm

(Algorithm

Additionally, we establish a computationally tractable and statistically efficient algorithm in the specific setting with static CVaR risk measure and linear function approximation. In this case, we provide numerical experiments to validate the theoretical results (see Appendix

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta^{*}_{1},{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}F_{2}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}(x|s_{k,1}^{\dagger})-{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta^{*}_{1},{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}F_{2}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(x|s_{k,1}^{\dagger})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\Bigg|\int_{s_{2}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{\widehat{\theta}_{k,1}}(s_{2}^{\dagger}|s_{k,1}^{\dagger},a_{k,1})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{2}(x-(y_{2}-y_{k,1})|s_{2}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle{\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\int_{s_{2}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}_{1}^{*}}(s_{2}^{\dagger}|s_{k,1}^{\dagger},a_{k,1})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{2}(x-(y_{2}-y_{k,1})|s_{2}^{\dagger})\Bigg|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|F_{2}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}(x|s_{k,2}^{\dagger})-F_{2}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(x|s_{k,2}^{\dagger})\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Delta_{k,1}^{(1)}+\Delta_{k,1}^{(2)}+\Delta_{k,1}^{(3)}+\Delta_{k,1}^{(4)}\,,

where the sequence
\Delta_{k,h}^{(i)}
for
i=1,2,3,4
is defined as follows.

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,h}^{(1)}=\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{\widehat{\theta}_{k,h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})-{\mathcal{T}}_{\widehat{\theta}_{k,h},{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}F_{h+1}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}(x|s_{k,h}^{\dagger})\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{\widehat{\theta}_{k,h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle-\int_{a_{h}}\pi^{k}_{h}(a_{h}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger})\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{\widehat{\theta}_{k,h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger},y_{h+1})\Bigg|\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,h}^{(2)}=\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}_{h}^{*}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})-{\mathcal{T}}_{{\theta}_{h}^{*},{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}F_{h+1}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}(x|s_{k,h}^{\dagger})\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}_{h}^{*}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle-\int_{a_{h}}\pi^{k}_{h}(a_{h}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger})\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}_{h}^{*}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})\Bigg|\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,h}^{(3)}=\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta^{*}_{h},{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}F_{h+1}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}(x|s_{k,h}^{\dagger})-F_{h+1}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}(x-r_{k,h}|s_{k,h+1}^{\dagger})\right|\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,h}^{(4)}=\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta^{*}_{h},{\bm{\pi}}^{k}}F_{h+1}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},{{\bm{\theta}}}^{*}}(x|s_{k,h}^{\dagger})-F_{h+1}^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},{{\bm{\theta}}}^{*}}(x-r_{k,h}|s_{k,h+1}^{\dagger})\right|\,.

Thus we have
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k,h}^{(i)}|\sigma_{k,h}]=0
for any
k\in[K],h\in[H]
and
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. Thus we have
\{\Delta_{k,h}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{k}
is a martingale difference sequence. Repeat the above method for
h
steps,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|F_{Z^{{{\bm{\pi}}}^{k}}_{\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}}-F_{Z^{{{\bm{\pi}}}^{k}}_{{{\bm{\theta}}}^{*}}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\Bigg|\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{\widehat{\theta}_{k,h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle\underbrace{\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}_{h}^{*}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})\Bigg|}_{I}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\Delta_{k,h}^{(1)}+\Delta_{k,h}^{(2)}+\Delta_{k,h}^{(3)}+\Delta_{k,h}^{(4)}}_{J}\,.

Applying the standard Azuma-Hoeffding inequality to the martingale difference sequence, we have

\displaystyle J\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(H\sqrt{K})

The main challenge falls in bounding the term
I
. By Lemma
C.1
, we have with probability at least
1-\delta
,
\theta_{h}^{*}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}
holds for all
k\in[K]
and
h\in[H]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle I\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\sup_{\theta^{1}_{h}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}}\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}^{1}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle{\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\inf_{\theta^{2}_{h}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}}\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}^{2}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sup_{\theta^{1}_{h}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}}\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}^{1}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x_{k,h}-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle{\quad\quad\quad-\inf_{\theta^{2}_{h}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}}\int_{s_{h+1}^{\dagger}}\mathbb{T}_{{\theta}^{2}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{k,h}^{\dagger},a_{k,h})F^{{\bm{\pi}}^{k},\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}_{h+1}(x_{k,h}-(y_{h+1}-y_{k,h})|s_{h+1}^{\dagger})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sup_{\theta^{1}_{h}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{1}_{h}}\widehat{F}_{k,h}](s_{k,h},a_{k,h})-\inf_{\theta^{2}_{h}\in\widehat{\Theta}_{k,h}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{2}_{h}}\widehat{F}_{k,h}](s_{k,h},a_{k,h})\,,

where the first inequality holds by
\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}\in\widehat{{\bm{\Theta}}}_{k}
and
{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\in\widehat{{\bm{\Theta}}}_{k}
with high probability, the first equality is due to the definition of
x_{k,h}
, and the last equality holds by the definition of
\widehat{F}_{k,h}(s)
. By Lemma
C.2
and Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\displaystyle I\leq\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}\left(H\sqrt{K\beta^{\mathrm{LSR}}\dim_{E}({\mathcal{W}}_{\bm{\Theta}},1/\sqrt{K})}\right)\,.

Overall, we can conclude the result

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|F_{Z^{{{\bm{\pi}}}^{k}}_{\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}}}-F_{Z^{{{\bm{\pi}}}^{k}}_{{{\bm{\theta}}}^{*}}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(H\sqrt{K\beta^{\mathrm{LSR}}\dim_{E}({\mathcal{W}}_{\bm{\Theta}},1/\sqrt{K})}\right)\,.

∎

Model-Based Estimation by MLE Approach

In this section we propose our algorithm and analysis for model-based risk-sensitive RL via the MLE estimation approach.

D.1
Algorithm
M-Est-MLE

Algorithm 5

M-Est-MLE
({\bm{\Theta}},{\mathcal{H}}_{k-1},\beta)

Input:
History information
{\mathcal{H}}_{k-1}
, Model class
{\bm{\Theta}}
, and confidence radius
\beta^{\mathrm{MLE}}
.

Estimate the transition model for every
h\in[H]

\displaystyle\theta^{\mathrm{MLE}}_{k,h}=\argmax_{\theta_{h}\in{\bm{\Theta}}_{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{h}}(s_{i,h+1}|s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\right]\,.

Construct Confidence set:

\displaystyle\widehat{{\bm{\Theta}}}_{k}=\left\{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\bm{\Theta}}:\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{i,h+1}|s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\mathrm{MLE}}_{k,h}}(s_{i,h+1}|s_{i,h},a_{i,h})-\beta^{\mathrm{MLE}}\right\}\,.

Return

\widehat{{\bm{\Theta}}}_{k}^{\mathrm{MLE}}
.

Here we present our construction of confidence set via MLE in Algorithm
5
, which is inspired by the OMLE algorithm of
[
36
]
.
In this algorithm, we first calculate the maximal likelihood estimator
\theta_{k,h}^{\mathrm{MLE}}
for each step
h
based on the history
{\mathcal{H}}_{k-1}
before episode
k
by the following equation.

\displaystyle\theta^{\mathrm{MLE}}_{k,h}=\argmax_{\theta_{h}\in\Theta_{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{i,h+1}|s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\,.

Then we can construct the confidence set centered at the maximal likelihood estimator with radius
\beta
:

\displaystyle\widehat{{\bm{\Theta}}}_{k}=\left\{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\bm{\Theta}}:\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{i,h+1}|s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\mathrm{MLE}}_{k,h}}(s_{i,h+1}|s_{i,h},a_{i,h})-\beta^{\mathrm{MLE}}\right\}\,.

Where
\beta^{\mathrm{MLE}}=H\log\left(eHK\mathcal{N}_{[\cdot]}(\epsilon,\Theta,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1})/\delta\right)
.

D.2
Simulation Lemma in Augmented MDP

Next, we build the relationship between the supremum norm of the cumulative distribution function and the
\ell_{1}
-norm of the trajectory probability kernel
\mu_{\bm{\theta}}^{\bm{\pi}}
. Throughout this section, we define
R(\bm{\tau})
as the cumulative reward of the trajectory
\bm{\tau}
.

Lemma D.1

(Distribution difference)
.

For any fixed model
{\bm{\theta}}\in{\bm{\Theta}}
,
Z_{\bm{\theta}}^{\bm{\pi}}
is the random variable of the cumulative reward collected by policy
{\bm{\pi}}
in the MDP modeled by
{\bm{\theta}}
.Thus we have the following holds

\displaystyle\left\|F_{Z_{\bm{\theta}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}-F_{Z_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{1}\,.

Proof.

By the definition of the CDF, we have
F_{Z^{\bm{\pi}}_{\bm{\theta}}}(x)=\int_{\bm{\tau}}\mu_{\bm{\theta}}^{\bm{\pi}}(\bm{\tau}){\mathbb{I}}(R(\bm{\tau})\leq x)
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\left\|F_{Z_{\bm{\theta}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}-F_{Z_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|F_{Z_{\bm{\theta}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}(x)-F_{Z_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|\int_{\bm{\tau}}\left(\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(\bm{\tau})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(\bm{\tau})\right){\mathbb{I}}(R({\bm{\tau}})\leq x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{\tau}}\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\bm{\pi}}(\bm{\tau})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(\bm{\tau})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{1}\,,

where the first inequality holds by the triangle inequality, and the last equality is due to the definition of the
\ell_{1}
-norm.
∎

We then establish the simulation lemma for augmented MDP, which connect the
\ell_{1}
-norm difference of the probability measure
\mu_{\bm{\theta}}^{\bm{\pi}}
on the augmented MDP with the
\ell_{1}
-norm difference of transition probabilities. This is one of the key lemmas that bridge the gap between the analysis in augmented MDP and origin MDP.

Lemma D.2

(Augmented simulation lemma)
.

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{1}\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{E}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim\nu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})-\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})\right\|_{1}\leq 2H\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{1}\,.

Proof.

First, we decompose
\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{1}
. Following standard simulation lemma analysis techniques, we have for any trajectory
\bm{\tau}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(\bm{\tau})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(\bm{\tau})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\pi_{h}(a_{h}|s_{h}^{\dagger})-\prod_{i=1}^{H}\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\pi_{h}(a_{h}|s_{h}^{\dagger})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{h=1}^{H}\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{i}^{*}}(s_{i+1}^{\dagger}|s_{i}^{\dagger},a_{i})\pi_{i}(a_{i}|s_{i}^{\dagger})\left(\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}^{*}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right)\pi_{h}(a_{h}|s_{h}^{\dagger})\prod_{i=h+1}^{H}\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{i}}(s_{i+1}^{\dagger}|s_{i}^{\dagger},a_{i})\pi_{i}(a_{i}|s_{i}^{\dagger})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{H}\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{i}}(s_{i+1}^{\dagger}|s_{i}^{\dagger},a_{i})\pi_{i}(a_{i}|s_{i}^{\dagger})\left|\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right|\pi_{h}(a_{h}|s_{h}^{\dagger})\,.

Integral the above inequality over the entire space
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}({\bm{\tau}})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}({\bm{\tau}})\right|d{\bm{\tau}}=\int_{\bm{\tau}}\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}({\bm{\tau}})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}({\bm{\tau}})\right|=\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{H}\int_{\Omega}\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{i}}(s_{i+1}^{\dagger}|s_{i}^{\dagger},a_{i})\pi_{i}(a_{i}|s_{i}^{\dagger})\pi_{h}(a_{h}|s_{h}^{\dagger})\left|\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right|d{\bm{\tau}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{H}\int_{(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}}\left|\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right|ds_{h+1}^{\dagger}d\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{E}_{(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\sim\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right\|_{1}\,,

where
\mu^{\bm{\pi}}_{{\bm{\theta}},h}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})
represent the probability of arriving the augmented state-action pair
(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})
at step
h
with policy
{\bm{\pi}}
in the MDP modeled by
{\bm{\theta}}
. Moreover, we define
\mu^{\bm{\pi}}_{{\bm{\theta}},h}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})
as the probability of visit
(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})
at step
h
and
h+1
, i.e.,
\mu^{\bm{\pi}}_{{\bm{\theta}},h}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})=\mu^{\bm{\pi}}_{{\bm{\theta}},h}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\mathbb{T}_{h}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})
. With this fact, we can rewrite the summation of the
\ell_{1}
-norm difference of the transition probabilities of the augmented MDP.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\sim\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{A}}}d\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}}\left|\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right|ds_{h+1}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}}\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})\right|+\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}_{h}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right|\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}^{\dagger}|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})ds_{h}^{\dagger}da_{h}ds_{h+1}^{\dagger}\,.

By the fact that for any state action pair
(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{A}}}\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}_{h}}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right|ds_{h}^{\dagger}da_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h}}\int_{\{{\bm{\tau}}:\tau_{h}=(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\}}\left|\mu_{\bm{\theta}}^{\bm{\pi}}({\bm{\tau}})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{\bm{\pi}}({\bm{\tau}})\right|d{\bm{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{\tau}}\left|\mu_{\bm{\theta}}^{\bm{\pi}}({\bm{\tau}})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{\bm{\pi}}({\bm{\tau}})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{\pi}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\pi}\right\|_{1}\,.

We can apply a similar method to give

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}}\left|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h},s_{h+1}^{\dagger})\right|ds_{h}^{\dagger}da_{h}ds_{h+1}^{\dagger}\leq\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{\pi}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\pi}\right\|_{1}\,.

Thus we can get

\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{E}_{(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\sim\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right\|_{1}\leq 2H\left\|\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}^{\pi}-\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\pi}\right\|_{1}\,.

At last, we need to prove the equivalence between augmented transition difference and original transition difference.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\sim\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{\pi}}\left[\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathbb{T}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right\|_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\dagger}\times{\mathcal{A}}}d\mu_{\theta^{*},h}^{\pi}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\int_{[y_{h},1]}\left|\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h+1}|s_{h},a_{h})-\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h+1}|s_{h},a_{h})\right|\mathbb{R}_{h}(y_{h+1}-y_{h}|s_{h},a_{h})dy_{h+1}ds_{h+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})-\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})\right\|_{1}d\mu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}((s_{h},y_{h}),a_{h})\int_{y_{h}}^{1}\mathbb{R}_{h}(y_{h+1}-y_{h}|s_{h},a_{h})dy_{h+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})-\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})\right\|_{1}d\nu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}(s_{h},a_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})-\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\prime}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})\right\|_{1}d\nu_{\theta^{*},h}^{\pi}(s_{h},a_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim\nu_{{\bm{\theta}}^{*},h}^{{\bm{\pi}}}}\left[\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})-\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\prime}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})\right\|_{1}\right]\,.

where the first equality holds by definition, the second equality is due to the Fubini Theorem, and the third equality holds by the decomposition of the probability measures of augmented MDP.
∎

D.3
Concentration Condition of MLE Approach

In this section, we prove the concentration condition of the MLE approach. The key idea is utilizing the property of MLE which is also studied by
[
3
,
35
,
36
]
.

We define the
\ell_{1}
-norm difference on the model set
{\bm{\Theta}}
. For any
{\bm{\theta}}^{1},{\bm{\theta}}^{2}\in{\bm{\Theta}}
, we define
\|{\bm{\theta}}^{1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{2}\|_{1}
as:

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\theta}}^{1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{2}\|_{1}:=\sup_{h\in[H],(s_{h},a_{h})\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}\|{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{1}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})-{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{2}_{h}}(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in[H],(s_{h},a_{h})\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left|{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{1}_{h}}(s_{h+1}|s_{h},a_{h})-{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta^{2}_{h}}(s_{h+1}|s_{h},a_{h})\right|ds_{h+1}\,.

Then we denote
{\bm{\Theta}}^{\downarrow}
as the lower bracket function set of
{\bm{\Theta}}
such that for any
{\bm{\theta}}\in{\bm{\Theta}}
, we have
{\bm{\theta}}^{\downarrow}\in{\bm{\Theta}}^{\downarrow}
satisfying
\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\downarrow}\|_{1}\leq\epsilon

We remark, however, that when specified to specific risk measures such as OCE

In this section, we discuss the policy covering given a policy class

.
For any policy

\epsilon

.

Instances

Consider a softmax policy set
\Pi(U,\tau)
with temperature
\tau
and utility function

\pi(a|s^{\dagger})=\frac{e^{\tau u(s^{\dagger},a)}}{\int_{\mathcal{A}}e^{\tau u(s^{\dagger},a^{\prime})}}

. Its normalized version is

. We can verify that:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dist}_{k,h,\underline{{\bm{\pi}}},\underline{{\widetilde{F}}}}^{\mathrm{LSR}}\left({\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}{\widetilde{F}}_{h+1}||\widehat{F}_{k,h,\underline{{\bm{\pi}}},\underline{{\widetilde{F}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{\mathrm{LSR}}+K\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{\mathrm{LSR}})\,.

From the definition of the confidence set, we have for any

and

where the equality is because the

Proof of Condition

We proof the Lemma via induction. If

The next lemma decomposes the supremum distance between the CDFs of the cumulative return via the bellman error, which is the distributional analogue of the performance difference lemma.

Lemma F.2

(Performance difference)
.

For any random variable
Z
representing the estimated cumulative return, with CDF function
\bm{F}=F_{1}\times F_{2}\times\cdots F_{H}
, we can decompose the
\ell_{\infty}
distance between the estimated return CDF
F_{Z}
and the real return CDF
F_{Z^{\bm{\pi}}}
for policy
{\bm{\pi}}\in\Pi^{\dagger}
by the bellman error as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|F_{Z}-F_{Z^{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{{\bm{\pi}}}}\left\|F_{h}(\cdot|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{h+1}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right\|_{\infty}\,.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left\|F_{Z}-F_{Z^{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|\int_{a_{1}}\pi_{1}(a_{1}|s_{1}^{\dagger})\left(F_{1}(x|s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-F_{1}^{\bm{\pi}}(x|s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1}\sim\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{1}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-F_{1}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1}\sim\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{1}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{1}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right\|_{\infty}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{1}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right\|_{\infty}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{2}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})-F_{2}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})\right\|_{\infty}\,,

where the first and second inequalities holds by triangle inequality, the third inequality is because:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1}}\mu^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\sup_{x\in[0,H]}\left|\int_{s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{2}}\mathbb{T}(s_{2}^{\dagger}|s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\pi(a_{2}|s_{2}^{\dagger})\left(F_{2}(x|s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})-F_{2}^{\bm{\pi}}(x|s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1}}\mu^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\int_{s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{2}}\mathbb{T}(s_{2}^{\dagger}|s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\pi(a_{2}|s_{2}^{\dagger})\left\|F_{2}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})-F_{2}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{2}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})-F_{2}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})\right\|_{\infty}\,.

Repeat this analysis for every step
h\in[H]
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|F_{Z}-F_{Z^{\bm{\pi}}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{1}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{2}(s_{1}^{\dagger},a_{1})\right\|_{\infty}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\bm{\pi}}}\left\|F_{2}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})-F_{2}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{2}^{\dagger},a_{2})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\|F_{h}(\cdot|s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})-\mathcal{T}_{h,{\bm{\pi}}}^{\dagger}F_{h+1}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})\right\|_{\infty}

∎

Similarly to

,
26
]
, the general algorithms provided for general version space are information theoretic, which means that they cannot be implemented efficiently in general. This is because we consider the general risk measure LRM and the general function approximation settings. However, when specified to the CVaR risk measure under the discretized linear MDP, a distributional extension of natural linear MDP

Definition I.1

(Discretized Linear MDP)
.

An augmented MDP
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}
is a discretized linear MDP with feature map
\phi:(s,a)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an uniform grid of
M
points
\left\{z_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M}
, if for any
h\in[H]
,
(r_{h},y_{h})\in\left\{z_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M}
, and there exists unknown measures
\mu_{h}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\theta_{h}:\left\{z_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{h}(s_{h+1}|s_{h},a_{h})=\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}\mu_{h}(s_{h+1})

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{h}(z_{i}|s_{h},a_{h})=\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}\theta_{h}(z_{i})~,

for all
s_{h},a_{h},s_{h+1},z_{i}
.

This discretized linear MDP is the natural extension of the linear MDP assumption in
[
27
]
, where we consider the discretized distributional reward instead of determined reward. and we generalize the linear expected reward to its distributional counterpart. Another important ingredient in our definition is the discretized reward space, which is commonly used in practice. C51 and Rainbow
[
8
,
22
]
both set
M=51
and achieved empirical success in Atari games. We need the discretized reward space mainly to bound the covering number of the value distribution, similar to
[
47
]
.

In a discretized linear MDP, we have that the distribution function have a quadratic structure:
f_{h}^{\pi}(z_{i}|(s_{h},y_{h}),a_{h})=\phi(s_{h},a_{h})W_{h}^{\pi}(z_{i},y_{h})\phi(s_{h},a_{h})
. Thus, we can use linear regression in estimating statistical functionals of
Z_{h}^{\bm{\pi}}(s_{h}^{\dagger},a_{h})
. We present our regret bound as follows:

Theorem I.2
.

If MDP
\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}
is a discretized linear MDP satisfying Definition
I.1
, and the risk measure
\rho(Z^{\bm{\pi}})=CVaR_{\tau}(Z^{\pi})=\max_{b}\left\{b-\tau^{-1}\mathbb{E}[(b-Z^{\pi})^{+}]\right\}
, we can bound the regret as:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Regret}(K)\leq\mathcal{\tilde{O}}(\tau^{-1}d^{3}H^{2}\sqrt{MK})

We highlight that this is the first efficient model free algorithm for linear MDPs, and the
\sqrt{M}
dependency is due to the covering number of the value distribution class which also appears in other model free distributional RL algorithms, such as
[
47
]
.

Define
\operatorname{CVaR}_{\tau}(Z^{\pi})=\argmax_{b}\left\{b-\tau^{-1}\mathbb{E}[(b-Z^{\pi})^{+}]\right\}
. We can define the statistical functionals
Q
and
V
as:
Q^{\pi}_{h}(s_{h},y_{h},a_{h})=\sum_{z_{i}}f_{h}^{\pi}(z_{i}|s_{h},y_{h},a_{h})[(-z_{i}-y_{h})^{+}]
, and
V^{\pi}_{h}(s_{h},y_{h})=\sum_{z_{i}}f_{h}^{\pi}(z_{i}|s_{h},y_{h},\pi(s_{h},y_{h}))[(-z_{i}-y_{h})^{+}]
. Then we can write the CVaR objective as
\operatorname{CVaR}_{\tau}(Z^{\pi})=\argmax_{b\in[0,H]}\left\{b-\tau^{-1}V_{1}^{\pi}(s_{1},-b)\right\}
.

Define recursively
\pi^{*}_{h}(s_{h},y_{h})=\argmin_{a}Q_{h}^{\pi^{*}}(s_{h},y_{h},a_{h})
, then by
[
46
]
we have for any
b\in[0,H]
,

\displaystyle V_{1}^{*}(s_{1},-b)=V_{1}^{\pi^{*}}(s_{1},-b)=\argmin_{\pi\in\Pi^{\dagger}}V_{1}^{\pi}(s_{1},-b)

We denote
\operatorname{CVaR}_{\tau}^{*}=\max_{\pi\in\Pi^{\dagger}}\operatorname{CVaR}_{\tau}^{\pi}=b^{*}-\tau^{-1}V_{1}^{*}(s_{1},-b^{*})

Because the density function of the rewards satisfy the distributional bellman equation:

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{T}_{h}V_{h+1}\right)(s_{h},y_{h},a_{h})

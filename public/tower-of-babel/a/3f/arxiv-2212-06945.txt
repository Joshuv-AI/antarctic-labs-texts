Variational Convexity of Functions in Banach Spaces
Pham Duy Khanh, Vu Vinh Huy Khoa, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Vo Thanh Phat

Abstract




Abstract
. This paper addresses the study and characterizations of variational convexity of extended-real-valued functions on Banach spaces. This notion has been recently introduced by Rockafellar, and its importance has been already realized and applied to continuous optimization problems in finite-dimensional spaces. Variational convexity in infinite-dimensional spaces, which is studied here for the first time, is significantly more involved and requires the usage of powerful tools of geometric functional analysis together with variational analysis and generalized differentiation in Banach spaces.

Key words
. Functional analysis and continuous optimization, variational analysis and generalized differentiation, extended-real-valued functions, variational convexity, monotone operators, Moreau envelopes

49J52, 49J53, 46B20, 46B10, 46A55

Introduction

The notion of
variational convexity
for extended-real-valued functions has been recently introduced and studied by Rockafellar
[
22
]
in finite-dimensional spaces. This notion is different from the standard local convexity of functions while offering instead a new insight on local behavior of the function in question via a certain local maximal monotonicity of its subdifferential. Such a novel viewpoint occurs to be useful in applications to various issues in continuous optimization including the design and justification of numerical algorithms; see
[
23
,
24
]
. Useful subdifferential characterization of variational convexity are given in
[
22
]
. More recently, it has been revealed in

[
13
]
that the variational convexity of extended-real-valued function in finite-dimensional spaces is equivalent to the standard local convexity of their Moreau envelopes. Furthermore, paper
[
13
]
contains applications of the obtained characterization of variational convexity via Moreau envelopes to
variational sufficiency
in constrained nonsmooth optimization.

We are not familiar with any publications on variational convexity of functions defined on infinite-dimensional spaces, which is the main topic of this paper. The main goals here are to establish such characterizations in general frameworks of Banach spaces and extend in this way finite-dimensional characterization from both papers
[
22
]
and
[
13
]
. As seen below, accomplishing these goals is a highly challenging task, which requires the usage and developments of delicate advanced techniques of Banach space geometry and variational analysis in infinite dimensions with considering a variety of appropriate settings in Banach spaces.

The subsequent material is organized as follows. Section
2
collects the basic definitions, preliminaries, and discussions from functional and variational analysis, which are broadly used in the paper. In Section
3
, we define and discuss the basic notion of
variational convexity
of extended-real-valued functions on Banach spaces and establish its
graphical subdifferential characterization
in the case of reflexive spaces with the necessity part holding in general Banach spaces. Section
4
provides characterizations of variational convexity via
local maximal monotonicity
of subdifferential and
local convexity
of
Moreau envelopes
of functions defined on
uniformly convex spaces
, where some implications hold in more general Banach space settings.

Throughout of the paper, we use the standard notation and terminology of variational analysis and Banach space theory; see, e.g.,
[
6
,
16
,
20
,
25
]
.

2
Preliminaries and Initial Discussions

Unless otherwise stated, the space
X
in question is
Banach
with the norm
\|\cdot\|
and its topological dual
X^{*}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
indicates the canonical pairing between
X
and
X^{*}
. The symbol
\rightarrow
refers to the strong/norm convergence, while
\overset{w}{\rightarrow}
and
\overset{w^{*}}{\rightarrow}
signify the weak and weak
∗
convergence, respectively. Given a set-valued
mapping/multifunction
F:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
from a Banach space
X
to it dual space
X^{*}
, the notation

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\exists\text{ seqs. }x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},\;x_{k}^{*}\overset{w^{*}}{\rightarrow}x^{*}\text{ with }x_{k}^{*}\in F(x_{k}),\;k\in{\rm I\!N}\big\}

(2.1)

signifies the (
sequential
)
Painlevé-Kuratowski outer limit
of
F(x)
as
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
. Consider further the
duality mapping

J:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
between
X
and
X^{*}
defined by

J(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\langle x^{*},x\rangle=\|x\|^{2}=\|x^{*}\|^{2}\big\},\quad x\in X,

(2.2)

which plays a significant role in what follows. The notation
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
and
B_{r}(x)
, stands, respectively, for the closed ball and open ball centered at
x
with radius
r>0
.

Given an extended-real-valued function
\varphi\colon X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
, we always assume that
\varphi
is
proper
, i.e.,
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi:=\{x\in X\;|\;\varphi(x)<\infty\}\neq\emptyset
. For any
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is defined by

\widehat{\partial}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x}):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\Big|\;\liminf\limits_{x\to\overline{x}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq-\varepsilon\Big\}.

(2.3)

When
\varepsilon=0
in (
2.3
), this construction is called the (Fréchet)
regular subdifferential

of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
and is denoted by
\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x})
. The (Mordukhovich)
limiting/basic subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is defined via the sequential outer limit (
2.1
) by

\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\overset{\varphi}{\to}\bar{x}\\
\varepsilon\downarrow 0\end{subarray}}\;\widehat{\partial}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x),

(2.4)

where
x\overset{\varphi}{\to}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
with
\varphi(x)\rightarrow\varphi(\bar{x})
. In the case where
\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x})=\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, the function
\varphi
is called
lower regular
at
\bar{x}
; see
[
16
]
. It is well known that both (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) reduce to the classical gradient
\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})
for continuously differentiable (in fact strictly differentiable) functions. If
\varphi
is convex, then both of these subdifferentials reduce to the standard subdifferential of convex analysis defined as

\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})\;\text{ for all }\;x\in X\big\}.

Thus the aforementioned two classes of functions exhibit lower/subdifferential regularity, while the latter collection of extended-real-valued functions is much broader; see, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
25
]
.

It follows from
[
16
, Theorem 2.34]
that we can equivalently put
\varepsilon=0
in (
2.4
) if
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) around
\bar{x}
and the space
X
is
Asplund
, i.e., a Banach space where each separable subspace has a separable dual. This class of spaces is fairly large including, e.g., every reflexive Banach space and every Banach space which dual is separable; see
[
5
,
10
,
16
,
20
]
with more details and the references therein. It has been well recognized in variational analysis that the limiting subdifferential (
2.4
) and the associated constructions for sets and set-valued mappings enjoy
full calculi
in Asplund spaces with a variety of applications presented in the two-volume book
[
16
]
, while finite-dimensional specifications can be found in
[
17
,
25
]
. Some useful results for (
2.4
) hold in general Banach spaces; see, e.g.,
[
16
, Chapter 1]
.

The next material is mostly taken from
[
25
]
in the case of finite dimensions and from
[
3
,
4
]
where extensions of these notions are studied in infinite-dimensional spaces. An l.s.c. function
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on a Banach space
X
is
prox-bounded
if it majorizes a quadratic function, i.e.,

\varphi(x)\geq\alpha\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\beta\;\mbox{ for some }\;\alpha,\beta\in{\rm I\!R},\;\mbox{ and }\;\bar{x}\in X.

(2.5)

An l.s.c. function
\varphi
is
prox-regular
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if there exist numbers
\varepsilon>0
and
r\geq 0
such that we have the estimate

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x-u\|^{2}

(2.6)

for all
x\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
and
(u,u^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\times\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}^{*}))
with
\varphi(u)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
. If (
2.6
) holds for all
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, then
\varphi
is said to be
prox-regular at

\bar{x}
.

Further, we say that
\varphi
is
subdifferentially continuous
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|<\varepsilon
whenever
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\bar{x})\times\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\bar{x}^{*}))
. When this holds for all
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, the function
\varphi
is said to be
subdifferentially continuous at

\bar{x}
. It is easy to see that if
\varphi
is subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
, then the inequality “
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
” in the definition of prox-regularity can be dropped. Extended-real-valued functions that are both prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous are called
continuously prox-regular
. This is a major class of extended-real-valued functions in second-order variational analysis that is a common roof for particular collections of functions important in applications as, e.g., the so-called amenable functions, etc.; see
[
25
, Chapter 13]
and
[
3
,
4
]
.

The
\varphi
-attentive
\varepsilon
-localization
of the subgradient mapping
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
is the set-valued mapping
T^{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
defined by

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T^{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}:=\big\{(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\;\big|\;\|x-\bar{x}\|<\varepsilon,\;|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|<\varepsilon\;\text{ and }\;\|x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*}\|<\varepsilon\big\}.

(2.7)

If
\varphi
is an l.s.c. function, a localization can be taken with just
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
.

Now we start exploring the notion of
epi-convergence
of extended-real-valued functions on a Banach space
X
. More details and references can be found in
[
25
]
in finite dimensions and in
[
1
,
5
]
in Banach spaces. Let
{\rm I\!N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
, and let

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{N\subset{\rm I\!N}\;\big|\;{\rm I\!N}\setminus N\;\text{ finite}\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\infty}^{\#}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big\{N\subset{\rm I\!N}\;\big|\;N\;\text{ infinite}\big\}.

For a sequence
\{C^{k}\}_{k\in{\rm I\!N}}
of subsets of
X
, the
outer limit
is the set

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}C^{k}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;\exists N\in\mathcal{N}^{\#}_{\infty},\;\exists x^{k}\in C^{k}(k\in N)\;\text{ with }\;x^{k}\xrightarrow{N}x\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;\forall V\in\mathcal{N}(x),\ \exists N\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}^{\#},\ \forall k\in N:C^{k}\cap V\neq\varnothing\big\},

while the
inner limit
of
\{C^{k}\}_{k\in{\rm I\!N}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}C^{k}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;\exists N\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty},\ \exists x^{k}\in C^{k}(k\in N)\;\text{ with }\;x^{k}\xrightarrow{N}x\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;\forall V\in\mathcal{N}(x),\ \exists N\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty},\ \forall k\in N:C^{k}\cap V\neq\emptyset\big\},

where
\mathcal{N}(x)
denotes the collection of neighborhoods of
x
.
The
limit
of the sequence
\{C^{k}\}
exists if the outer and inner limit sets are equal, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}C^{k}:=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}C^{k}=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}C^{k}.

We need the following simple observation. For a sequence of sets
E^{k}\subset X\times{\rm I\!R}
that are epigraphs of some extended-real-valued function, both the outer limit set
\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}E^{k}
and inner limit set
\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}E^{k}
are also epigraphs of some functions. Indeed, if either set contains
(x,\alpha)
, then it also contains
(x,\alpha^{\prime})
for all
\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha,\infty)
. On the other hand, since both limit sets are closed, they intersect
\{x\}\times{\rm I\!R}
in a set which, unless empty, is a closed interval. Thus the criteria for being an epigraph of some function is satisfied. For any sequence
\left\{\varphi^{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of extended-real-valued functions on
X
, the
lower epi-limit
, e-
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}
, is the function having as its epigraph the outer limit of the sequence of sets
\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}
, i.e.,

\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\text{e-}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}).

The
upper epi-limit
, e-
\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}
, is the function having as its epigraph the inner limit of the sets
\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}
, which is defined by

\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\text{e-}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}).

Thus
\text{e-}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}\leq\text{e-}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}
in general. When these two functions coincide, it is said that the full limit
epi-limit
function e-
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}
exists, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\text{e-}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}:=\text{e-}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}=\text{e-}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{k}.

On this case, the functions
\varphi^{k}

epi-converge
to
\varphi
, which is denoted by
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
. It is clear that
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}\rightarrow\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi
as
k\to\infty
.
The next characterization of epi-convergence taken from
[
1
, Proposition 1.14]
is instrumental for deriving the main result of Section
3
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
\varphi^{k}\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
as
k\in{\rm I\!N}
and

\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be given functions. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
as
k\to\infty
.

(ii)

At each point
x\in X
, we have the relationship

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varphi^{k}(x^{k})\geq\varphi(x)\ \text{ for every sequence }\;x^{k}\rightarrow x,\\
\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\varphi^{k}(x^{k})\leq\varphi(x)\ \text{ for some sequence }\;x^{k}\rightarrow x.\end{cases}

To proceed further, along with standard definitions of lower semicontinuity of functions in the strong/norm topology of
X
and its
sequential
version, we recall their weak counterparts. A function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is
weakly sequentially lower semicontinuous
(weakly sequentially l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
if for any sequence
\{x_{k}\}
which weakly converges to
\bar{x}
, it holds that
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(x_{k})\geq\varphi(\bar{x})
. We say that
\varphi
is
weakly lower semicontinuous
(weakly l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
in the weak topology of
X
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})-\varepsilon\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in U.

It is easily to see that the weak lower semicontinuity yields the weak sequential lower semicontinuity, while the reverse implication is not true in general; see, for instance, a counterexample in
[
14
, Example 2.1]
. On the other hand, if
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. at
\bar{x}
, then it is automatically l.s.c. at this point. The function
\varphi
is called lower semicontinuous (resp. weakly sequentially lower semicontinuous, weakly lower semicontinuous)
around

\bar{x}
if it possesses this property for all points in some neighborhood of
\bar{x}
.

Next we recall some notions related to monotonicity of set-valued mappings that are often called operators in this framework. The classical monotonicity notion is formulated as follows. An operator
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is (globally)
monotone
on
X
if

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ whenever }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T.

It is called to be (globally)
strongly monotone
on
X
with modulus
\kappa>0
if

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\kappa\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}\;\text{ whenever }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T.

A monotone (resp. strongly monotone) operator
T
is
maximal monotone
(resp.
strongly maximal monotone
) if
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
for any monotone operator
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
. We refer the reader to the monographs
[
2
,
5
,
20
,
25
]
for various properties and applications of monotone and maximal monotone operators in finite and infinite dimensions. Note, in particular, that the graph of any maximal monotone mapping is nonempty and closed.

The
local monotonicity
of set-valued mappings is naturally defined as follows; see, e.g.,
[
25
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. We say that:

(i)

T
is
locally monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if there are neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{x}^{*}
with

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ for all }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

(ii)

T
is
locally monotone
with respect to
W\subset X\times X^{*}
if

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ for all }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

(iii)

T
is
locally strongly monotone
with modulus
\kappa>0
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if there are neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{y}
satisfying the estimate

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\kappa\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}\;\text{ for all }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

It is easy to observe the following
robustness property
: if
T
is locally monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with respect to a neighborhood
U\times V
, then we have the same property around any
(x,x^{*})\in U\times V
. In this case, it is said that
T
is locally monotone relative to
U\times V
. We now follow
[
19
]
to define the
local maximal monotonicity
of set-valued mappings.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. Then we say that:

(i)

T
is
locally maximal monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if there exist a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a maximal monotone operator
\overline{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\overline{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

(ii)

T
is
locally maximal monotone
with respect to
W\subset X\times X^{*}
if there exists a maximal monotone operator
\overline{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\overline{T}\cap W=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

(iii)

T
is
locally strongly maximal monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if there is a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a strongly maximal monotone operator
\overline{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\overline{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

Observe that if
T
is locally maximal monotone with respect to
W\subset X\times X^{*}
, then it is also locally maximal monotone with respect to any subset
Q\subset W
.

Finally in this section, we recall some geometric properties of Banach spaces; see, e.g.,
[
9
,
10
,
18
]
for more details. A norm
\|\cdot\|
on a space
X
is called (Gâteaux)
smooth
if it is Gâteaux differentiable on
X\setminus\{0\}
. Equivalently, a norm on
X
is smooth if and only if the corresponding duality mapping
J
defined in (
2.2
) is single-valued on the whole
X
. Note that any separable Banach space admits an equivalent norm that is Gâteaux differentiable off the origin.

The
moduli of convexity and smoothness
of
X
are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\xi_{X}(t):=\inf\bigg\{1-\dfrac{\|x+y\|}{2}\;\bigg|\;x,y\in S_{X},\;\|x-y\|=t\bigg\}\ \text{ for all }\;t\in[0,2],\quad{\rm and}

\displaystyle\rho_{X}(s):=\sup\bigg\{\dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\|x+y\|-\|x-y\|\Big)-1\;\bigg|\;x\in S_{X},\;\|y\|=s\bigg\}\ \text{ for }\;s>0,

where
S_{X}:=\{x\in X\;|\;\|x\|=1\}
.
A Banach space
X
is said to be
uniformly convex
if
\xi_{X}(t)>0
. It is called
2
-uniformly convex
(resp.
2
-uniformly smooth
) if there exists a constant
b>0
such that
\xi_{X}(t)\geq bt^{2}
(resp.
\rho_{X}(s)\leq bs^{2}
). It is shown in
[
8
, Theorem 7 and Theorem 8, respectively]
that any
2
-uniformly convex space (resp.
2
-uniformly smooth space) can be characterized via a lower (resp. upper)
weak parallelogram law
:

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}+c\|x-y\|^{2}\leq 2\big(\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}\big)\ \text{ for all }\;x,y\in X,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}+c\|x-y\|^{2}\geq 2\big(\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}\big)\ \text{ for all }\;x,y\in X.

(2.9)

In particular, a Banach space
X
is
2
-uniformly convex (resp.
2
-uniformly smooth) with constant
b>0
if and only if it satisfies condition (
2.8
) (resp. condition (
2.9
)) for some
c_{b}>0
. Therefore, any Hilbert space is simultaneously a
2
-uniformly convex and
2
-uniformly smooth Banach space. Moreover, it is obvious that any
2
-uniformly convex space (resp.
2
-uniformly smooth space) is uniformly convex (resp. uniformly smooth), and hence it is reflexive by the Milman-Pettis theorem. Some well-known non-Hilbert
2
-uniformly convex (resp.
2
-uniformly smooth) spaces are
l^{p}(\mu)
and
L^{p}(\mu)
with
1<p<2
(resp. with
2<p<\infty
). Recall
[
7
]
that in any reflexive Banach space, there exists an equivalent norm such that the corresponding duality mapping
J
from (
2.2
) is bijective and continuous with its inverse
J^{-1}
also being continuous. If in addition
X
is
2
-uniformly convex, then it follows from
[
26
]
that the duality mapping is strongly monotone.

3
Subdifferential Characterization of Variational Convexity

In this section, we formulate the underlying notion of
variational convexity
for extended-real-valued functions on Banach spaces and establish its characterization in terms of the limiting subdifferential (
2.4
). The following definition is taken from Rockafellar
[
22
]
, where it is formulated for functions on finite-dimensional spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

An l.s.c. function
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is called
variationally convex
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if for some convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
there exists a convex l.s.c. function
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
and
\varepsilon>0
such that we have the relationships

\left(U_{\varepsilon}\times V\right)\cap\operatorname{gph}\partial\varphi=(U\times V)\cap\operatorname{gph}\partial\widehat{\varphi}\;\text{ and }\;\varphi(x)=\widehat{\varphi}(x)\;\text{ at the common elements }\;(x,x^{*}),

where
U_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in U\;|\;\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\}
.

As demonstrated and discussed in
[
22
]
and also in
[
13
]
, this notion is different from the usual local convexity of extended-real-valued functions.

Now we present two lemmas on epi-convergence in minimization used in what follows. The first one is taken from
[
1
, Proposition 2.9]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
\varphi^{k}\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
for
k\in{\rm I\!N}
and
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be l.s.c. functions such that
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
as
k\to\infty
. Then we have

\inf\varphi\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}(\inf\varphi^{k}).

(3.1)

The second rather simple lemma reveals behavior of argminimum sets under epi-convergence of extended-real-valued functions defined on Banach spaces.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
\varphi^{k}\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
for
k\in{\rm I\!N}
and
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be l.s.c. functions. Suppose that
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
as
k\to\infty
and that
\argmin\varphi^{k}\neq\emptyset
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
as well as
\argmin\varphi\neq\emptyset
. Then we have the inclusion

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}(\argmin\varphi^{k})\subset\argmin\varphi.

Therefore, for any choice of
x^{k}\in\argmin\varphi^{k}
,
k\in{\rm I\!N}
, the sequence
\{x^{k}\}
has all its cluster points belonging to
\argmin\varphi
. If
\argmin\varphi
consists of a unique point
\bar{x}
, then
x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
.

[
Proof.]
Pick
x\in\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}\argmin\varphi^{k}
and find subsequences
\{k_{n}\}
of

{\rm I\!N}
and
x^{k_{n}}\in\argmin\varphi^{k_{n}}
such that
x^{k_{n}}\rightarrow x
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Since
\varphi^{k}\xrightarrow{e}\varphi
, we have
\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k})=\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi
. By

\big(x,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})\big)=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}(x^{k_{n_{l}}},\varphi^{k_{n_{l}}}(x^{k_{n_{l}}}))\;\text{ for some subsequence }\;\{k_{n_{l}}\}\;\text{ of }\;\{k_{n}\}

and
(x^{k_{n_{l}}},\varphi^{k_{n_{l}}}(x^{k_{n_{l}}}))\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\varphi^{k_{n_{l}}})
for all
l\in{\rm I\!N}
, it follows that

\big(x,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})\big)\in\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{k\to\infty}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi^{k}).

Hence
\big(x,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})\big)\in\mbox{\rm epi}\,\varphi
, i.e.,

\varphi(x)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}}).

This readily implies that

\varphi(x)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{k_{n}}(x^{k_{n}})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}(\inf\varphi^{k_{n}})\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}(\inf\varphi^{k})\leq\inf\varphi,

where the last inequality is due to estimate (
3.1
) in Lemma
3.2
. This gives us
x\in\argmin\varphi
and therefore completes the proof of this lemma.

The next result provides a major characterization of variational convexity of extended-real-valued functions defined on Banach spaces. It is a far-going generalization of the finite-dimensional characterization given in
[
22
, Theorem 1, (c)
\Longleftrightarrow
(b)]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function defined on a Banach space
X
, and let
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
be a basic subgradient from (
2.4
). Consider the following assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
.

(ii)

There exists a convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
along with
\varepsilon>0
such that we have

\big[(u,u^{*})\in\left(U_{\varepsilon}\times V\right)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\big]\Longrightarrow\big[\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle\big]\;\text{ for all }\ x\in U,

(3.2)

where
U_{\varepsilon}
is taken from Definition
3.1
.

Then implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
holds in general Banach spaces. The reverse implication is satisfied if
X
is a reflexive space and if
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
.

[
Proof.]
First we verify implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) in any Banach space
X
. Assuming the variational convexity of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{x}^{*}
, find a convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a convex l.s.c. function
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
such that

\left(U_{\varepsilon}\times V\right)\cap\operatorname{gph}\partial\varphi=(U\times V)\cap\operatorname{gph}\partial\widehat{\varphi}\text{ and }\varphi(x)=\widehat{\varphi}(x)\;\text{ at the common elements }\;(x,x^{*})

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
\widehat{\varphi}
is convex on
X
. For any
(u,u^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
we get
(u,u^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
, and therefore

\widehat{\varphi}(x)\geq\widehat{\varphi}(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle\;\text{ whenever }\;x\in U.

Since
\widehat{\varphi}(x)\leq\varphi(x)
and
\widehat{\varphi}(u)=\varphi(u)
, it follows that
\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle
for all
x\in U
, which justifies implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii).

To verify next the reverse implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i), observe first that having
{\bf(ii)}
allows us to shrink
U
and
V
if necessary so that
U\subset B_{r_{1}}(\bar{x})
and
V\subset B_{r_{2}}(\bar{x}^{*})
with
r_{1}<\varepsilon/(\|\bar{x}^{*}\|+r_{2})
. Define the function
\widehat{\varphi}:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
by

\widehat{\varphi}(x):=\sup\big\{\varphi(u)+\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle\;\big|\;(u,u^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\big\},\quad x\in X.

(3.3)

Since
\widehat{\varphi}
is the supremum of a collection of affine functions, it is l.s.c. and convex on the whole space
X
. We clearly have the estimate

\widehat{\varphi}(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle>-\infty\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\in X.

Moreover, it follows from (
3.2
) and the construction of
\widehat{\varphi}
that
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
. We split the remaining proof of (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) into the
six steps/claims
as follows.

Claim 1:

In any Banach space
X
, the inclusion
(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
is equivalent to having
(x,x^{*})\in(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
together with
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
for any l.s.c. function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
.

To justify this claim, consider
(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
and check first that
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
. Indeed,
\varphi(x)
can be equivalently written as

\varphi(x)=\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},x-x\rangle\;\text{ with }\;(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi,

which implies that
\widehat{\varphi}(x)\geq\varphi(x)
. Combining the latter with the inequality
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
, we arrive at
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
. To check further that
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
, deduce from
(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
that for any
x^{\prime}\in X
it follows that

\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\varphi(x)\leq\widehat{\varphi}(x^{\prime}),

where the last inequality is a consequence of (
3.3
). This tells us that
\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle\leq\widehat{\varphi}(x^{\prime})-\widehat{\varphi}(x)
for all
x^{\prime}\in X
, i.e.,
x^{*}\in\partial\widehat{\varphi}(x)
.

To verify the reverse implication in Claim 1, pick
(x,x^{*})\in(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
such that
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
. We aim to show that
(x,x^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
. By
x^{*}\in\partial\widehat{\varphi}(x)
, we have

\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\widehat{\varphi}(x)\leq\widehat{\varphi}(x^{\prime})\;\text{ for all }\;x^{\prime}\in X.

It follows from
\widehat{\varphi}\leq\varphi
on
U
that

\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(x^{\prime})\ \text{ whenever }\ x^{\prime}\in U,

(3.4)

and therefore we have the limiting condition

\liminf_{x^{\prime}\to x}\frac{\varphi(x^{\prime})-\varphi(x)-\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle}{\|x^{\prime}-x\|}\geq 0,

which means that
x^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x)
and hence
x^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x)
. It remains to check that
x\in U_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.,
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
. Indeed, it follows from (
3.4
) and the choice of
U
and
V
above that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-x\rangle\leq\varphi(\bar{x})+\|x^{*}\|\cdot\|\bar{x}-x\|\\
\leq\varphi(\bar{x})+(\|x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*}\|+\|\bar{x}^{*}\|)\cdot\|\bar{x}-x\|\leq\varphi(\bar{x})+(r_{2}+\|\bar{x}^{*}\|)r_{1}<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,\end{array}

which completes the proof of Claim 1.

To proceed further, select a bounded, closed, and convex set
\Omega\subset U
containing
\bar{x}
as an interior point and such that
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. at any point in
\Omega
. For
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
, define the auxiliary l.s.c. function
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
by

\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y):=\varphi(y)-\widehat{\varphi}(x)-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}+\delta_{\Omega}(y),\quad y\in X,

(3.5)

via the
indicator function

\delta_{\Omega}(x)
of
\Omega
equal to
0
if
x\in\Omega
and
\infty
otherwise.

Claim 2:

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
be taken from (
3.5
). Then for any
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
and any
y\in X
, we have
\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y)\geq 0
. Furthermore, it follows that

{\rm argmin}\,\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=\{\bar{x}\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\min\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=0.

Indeed, picking
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
ensures that
\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle+\widehat{\varphi}(x)\leq\widehat{\varphi}(y)\leq\varphi(y)
whenever
y\in U
. Since
\Omega\subset U
, it follows that
\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y)\geq 0
for all
y\in X
, and that
\psi
vanishes if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y=x,\\
y\in\Omega,\\
\varphi(y)=\widehat{\varphi}(x).\end{cases}

Applying this to
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
gives us
\argmin\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=\{\bar{x}\}
and
\min\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}=0
as stated in Claim 2.

Claim 3:

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space, and let
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
. Then each function
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
achieves its global minimum on
X
.

Since
\Omega
is closed and convex, this set is weakly closed by the classical Mazur theorem, and hence it is weakly sequentially closed. On the other hand, we know that the function
\langle x^{*},\cdot-x\rangle
is weakly continuous, while
\|\cdot-x\|^{2}
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
. Also, the indicator function
\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. at any point in
\Omega
. Combining this with the imposed weakly sequentially l.s.c. property of
\varphi
tells us that the function
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. on the bounded and weakly sequentially closed subset
\Omega
of the reflexive Banach space
X
. Thus the generalized Weierstrass theorem in this setting (see, for instance,
[
15
, Theorem 7.3.4]
) ensures that each
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
attains its minimum on
\Omega
, which is actually a global minimum on
X
due to the construction of
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
in (
3.5
). We are done with the proof of Claim 3.

Claim 4:

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be l.s.c. on
X
. Then the function
\psi_{x,x^{*}}
depends
epicontinuously
on
(x,x^{*})
with respect to
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
, in the sense that the convergence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\xrightarrow{\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}}(x,x^{*})
implies that
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\xrightarrow{e}\psi_{x,x^{*}}
as
k\to\infty
.

To justify this claim, take a sequence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\xrightarrow{\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}}(x,x^{*})
as
k\to\infty
and verify the epi-convergence
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\xrightarrow{e}\psi_{x,x^{*}}
by using its characterization given in Lemma
2.1
. To this end, pick any
y\in X
and any sequence
y_{k}\rightarrow y
. Since
\widehat{\varphi}

is l.s.c. and convex on
X
, it is subdifferentially continuous at
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\widehat{\varphi}
; see, e.g.,
[
25
, Example 13.30]
which proof works in arbitrary Banach spaces. Combining this with
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\xrightarrow{\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}}(x,x^{*})
gives us the convergence
\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\rightarrow\widehat{\varphi}(x)
as
k\to\infty
. Therefore, we readily get the relationships

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(y_{k})\geq\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varphi(y_{k})+\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(y_{k})-\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\\
+\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\Big[\dfrac{1}{2}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\langle x^{*}_{k},y_{k}-x_{k}\rangle\Big]\geq\varphi(y)+\delta_{\Omega}(y)-\widehat{\varphi}(x)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle\\
=\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y).\end{array}

On the other hand, choosing the constant sequence
z_{k}:=y
tells us that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(y)=\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\varphi(y)+\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(y)-\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})\\
+\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\Big[\dfrac{1}{2}\|y-x_{k}\|^{2}-\langle x^{*}_{k},y-x_{k}\rangle\Big]=\varphi(y)+\delta_{\Omega}(y)-\widehat{\varphi}(x)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle\\
=\psi_{x,x^{*}}(y),\end{array}

which ensures that
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\xrightarrow{e}\psi_{x,x^{*}}
as
k\to\infty
and thus verifies the claim.

Claim 5:

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space. Suppose that the sequence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\in(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
converges to some

(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
as
k\to\infty
and that the function
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
. Then for each
k\in{\rm I\!N}
sufficiently large, there exists a pair
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
such that
\widehat{\varphi}(\widetilde{x}_{k})=\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})
and
x_{k}^{*}-\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k}\in J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
.

Indeed, it follows from Claim 4 that
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\xrightarrow{e}\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}
as
k\to\infty
. Taking into account that
\argmin\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}\neq\emptyset
by Claim 3 and employing Lemma
3.3
allow us to construct a sequence
\{\widetilde{x}_{k}\}
such that
\widetilde{x}_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
and that

\displaystyle\widetilde{x}_{k}\in\argmin\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}\cap\mathrm{int}\,\Omega\;\text{ for all large }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

Lemma
3.2
gives us the relationships

0=\min\psi_{\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}}\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}(\inf\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}})=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\big(\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\big),

which ensure that
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\searrow 0
due to
\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\geq 0
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
. Thus for sufficiently large
k\in{\rm I\!N}
, we have the conditions

\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})-\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})=\psi_{x_{k},x_{k}^{*}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})+\langle x^{*}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\rangle-\dfrac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}<\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}.

The latter clearly implies the estimate

\displaystyle\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(x_{k})+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\quad\text{(since $\widehat{\varphi}$ is subdifferentially continuous at $\bar{x}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,

which tells us that
\widetilde{x}_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon}
for sufficiently large
k
. Remembering that
\widetilde{x}_{k}
is a minimizer of
\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}
on
X
, we get by the subdifferential Fermat rule (see, e.g.,
[
16
, Proposition 1.114]
) that
0\in\partial\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})
. Since
\varphi
is l.s.c. while all other functions in (
3.5
) are locally Lipschitzian around
\bar{x}
due to the construction of
\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}
, and since any reflexive Banach space is Asplund, we apply to
\partial\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})
the semi-Lipschitzian subdifferential sum rule from
[
16
, Theorem 2.33]
to obtain

\displaystyle 0\in\partial\psi_{x_{k},x^{*}_{k}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})\subset\partial\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})-x^{*}_{k}+J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\;\mbox{ for all large }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

Observe that
\partial(\|\cdot-x_{k}\|^{2})(\widetilde{x}_{k})=J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
via the duality mapping (
2.2
) and that
\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\widetilde{x}_{k})=\{0\}
due to the choice of
\bar{x}
and the convergence
\widetilde{x}_{k}\to\bar{x}
. Thus there exist
\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k}\in\partial\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})
and
j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\in J(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
such that
\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k}=x^{*}_{k}-j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})
. Since
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and
\widetilde{x}_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
, we also have
\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
. By the fact that
\|j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\|=\|\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k}\|
, this gives us
j(\widetilde{x}_{k}-x_{k})\rightarrow 0
, and so
\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*}\rightarrow\bar{x}^{*}
as
k\to\infty
. In summary, for all
k
sufficiently large, there are
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
such that
\widetilde{x}_{k}\in U_{\varepsilon}
and
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
as
k\to\infty
. Therefore,
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*})\in(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
. Consequently,
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}_{k}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
and
\widehat{\varphi}(\widetilde{x}_{k})=\varphi(\widetilde{x}_{k})
for large
k
due to Claim 1. This verifies the formulated assertions.

Claim 6:

In the setting of Claim
5
, we have
\widetilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}
for all large
k\in{\rm I\!N}
.

Indeed, the subgradient mapping
\partial\widehat{\varphi}
is monotone due to the properness and convexity of
\widehat{\varphi}
. Since both pairs
(x_{k},x^{*}_{k})
and
(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k})
belong to
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
due to Claim 5, we get that

0\leq\langle x^{*}_{k}-\widetilde{x}^{*}_{k},x_{k}-\widetilde{x}_{k}\rangle=-\|x_{k}-\widetilde{x}_{k}\|^{2},

and thus
\widetilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}
, which verifies this claim.

Unifying the above claims with shrinking the neighborhoods
U
and
V
if necessary tell us that for all
(x,x^{*})\in(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\widehat{\varphi}
we have
\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(x)
. This completes the proof of implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) and hence of the whole theorem.

4
Characterizations of Variational Convexity via Local Monotonicity and Moreau Envelopes

This section provides new characterizations of variational convexity of extended-real-valued l.s.c. functions on Banach spaces. The first type of characterizations involves
local monotonicity
and
local maximal monotonicity
of
limiting subgradient mappings
, while the second type characterizes variational convexity of functions via the standard
local convexity
of their
Moreau envelopes
.

Given an l.s.c. function
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on a Banach space
X
together with a parameter
\lambda>0
, the
Moreau envelope

e_{\lambda}\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
and the
proximal mapping

P_{\lambda}\varphi:X\rightrightarrows X
associated with
\varphi
and
\lambda
are defined, respectively, by

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\inf_{w\in X}\left\{\varphi(w)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\right\},\quad x\in X,

(4.1)

P_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\text{\rm argmin}_{w\in X}\left\{\varphi(w)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\right\},\quad x\in X.

(4.2)

Prior to establishing the main characterizations of this section, we present several lemmas, which are of their own interest while being instrumental for the proof of the major results below. The first lemma provides a description of the
prox-boundedness
(
2.5
) of extended-real-valued functions via their Moreau envelopes (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function defined on a Banach space
X
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is prox-bounded.

(ii)

There exist
\lambda>0
and
x\in X
such that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
.

(iii)

There exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
for any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
and for any
x\in X
.

[
Proof.]
We begin with verifying implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). The prox-boundedness of
\varphi
gives us
\alpha
,
\beta\in{\rm I\!R}
and
x\in X
such that

\varphi(y)\geq\alpha\|y-x\|^{2}+\beta\;\text{ for all }\;y\in X.

Then for any
\lambda>0
satisfying
\alpha+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\geq 0
, we get

\varphi(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}\geq\Big(\alpha+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\Big)\|y-x\|^{2}+\beta\;\text{ whenever }\;y\in X.

Taking the infimum of both sides above with respect to
y\in X
brings us to
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\geq\beta>-\infty
, which therefore verifies assertion (ii).

Suppose next that (ii) holds. Then denote

\lambda_{0}:=\sup\big\{\lambda>0\;\big|\;\exists x\in X\;\text{ and }\;e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty\big\}

and deduce from (ii) that
\lambda_{0}>0
. Consider any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
and any
x\in X
. Since
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
, there exists
\bar{\lambda}>\lambda
with
\bar{x}\in X
satisfying
e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})>-\infty
. For
y\in X
, we clearly have that

\displaystyle\varphi(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\bar{\lambda}}\|y-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2\bar{\lambda}}\|y-\bar{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2\bar{\lambda}}(\|y-x\|+\|x-\bar{x}\|)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})+\left(\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}-\dfrac{1}{2\bar{\lambda}}\right)\cdot\|y-x\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{\bar{\lambda}}\|y-x\|\cdot\|x-\bar{x}\|-\dfrac{1}{2\bar{\lambda}}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}.

Since
e_{\bar{\lambda}}\varphi(\bar{x})>-\infty
and
\bar{\lambda}>\lambda
, the expression
\varphi(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}
is bounded below when
y
varies in
X
. This gives us the conditions

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)=\inf_{y\in X}\left\{\varphi(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}\right\}>-\infty,

which verify (iii). The remaining implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) is trivial, and thus we are done.

Given
x^{*}\in X^{*}
and
\lambda>0
, define the
x^{*}
-Moreau envelope
for
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
by

e^{x^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\inf_{w\in X}\left\{\varphi(w)-\langle x^{*},w\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\right\},\quad x\in X,

(4.3)

and the
x^{*}
-proximal mapping
associated with
\varphi
by

P_{\lambda}^{x^{*}}\varphi(x):=\text{\rm argmin}_{w\in X}\left\{\varphi(w)-\langle x^{*},w\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\right\},\quad x\in X.

(4.4)

It is easy to see that the
x^{*}
-Moreau envelope for
\varphi
at
x\in X
is the standard Moreau envelope for the
tilted function

\varphi(\cdot)-\langle x^{*},\cdot\rangle
at the same point. Therefore, we have the following relationships between
x^{*}
-Moreau envelopes (resp.
x^{*}
-proximal mappings) and standard Moreau envelopes (resp. proximal mappings) in the setting of Hilbert spaces:

e_{\lambda}^{\bar{x}^{*}}\varphi(\bar{x})=e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{x}^{*})-\langle\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x}\rangle-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\bar{x}^{*}\|^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad P_{\lambda}^{\bar{x}^{*}}\varphi(\bar{x})=P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{x}^{*}).

(4.5)

This result can be found in
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
which proof works for functions on Hilbert spaces.

The next lemma provides characterizations of
fixed points
of the
x^{*}
-proximal mappings defined in (
4.4
). Recall from
[
25
, Definition 8.45]
that
\bar{x}^{*}\in X^{*}
is a
proximal subgradient
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
if there exist numbers

and
\varepsilon>0
such that for all

we have

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\dfrac{r}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}.

(4.6)

Lemma 4.2
.

Consider an l.s.c. function
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
, and
\bar{x}^{*}\in X^{*}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is prox-bounded, and
\bar{x}^{*}
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
.

(ii)

P^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
for some
\lambda>0
.

(iii)

P^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small.

[
Proof.]
Assume that (i) holds. To verify (ii), take
\bar{x}^{*}
as a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
and then find
r,\varepsilon>0
such that (
4.6
) is satisfied. We intend to show that (

) holds for all
x\in X
with a certain modification of

. Since
\varphi
is prox-bounded, Lemma

implies that there exists
\lambda>0
with

. On the other hand, choosing
\bar{\lambda}>0
to be sufficiently small guarantees that the estimates

\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\frac{1}{2\bar{\lambda}}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq\varphi(x)

(4.7)

hold for all

and deduce from (

The latter is equivalent to saying that
P_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\bar{x}^{*}}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
, where
\bar{\lambda}:=1/\overline{r}
, and hence (ii) is verified.

Implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) is trivial. Finally, suppose that (iii) holds and deduce from

that

, which yields the prox-boundedness of

by Lemma

. The assertion that
\bar{x}^{*}
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is implied by the fact that
\bar{x}
is a global minimizer of
\varphi(\cdot)-\langle\bar{x}^{*},\cdot\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|\cdot-\bar{x}\|^{2}

The next result reveals important properties of the duality mapping (

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be a uniformly convex Banach space which norm is Gâteaux differentiable at nonzero points of
X
. Then the corresponding duality mapping
J
is single-valued and norm-to-norm continuous on the whole space
X
.

[
Proof.]
Fix an arbitrary point
x\in X
and take a sequence
x_{k}\to x
as
k\to\infty
. By the Milman-Pettis theorem, the space
X
is reflexive. Due to the reflexivity of
X
and the assumed smoothness of the norm
\|\cdot\|
, it follows from
[
9
, Corollary 1.4]
that the dual space
X^{*}
is
strictly convex
, i.e., its closed unit ball is a strictly convex set. Moreover,
[
9
, Corollary 1.5]
tells us that

is single-valued and norm-to-weak
∗
continuous. This implies that
J(x_{k})\xrightarrow{w^{*}}J(x)

. Since

is reflexive, we actually have
J(x_{k})\xrightarrow{w}J(x)
. Furthermore, the convergence

and definition (
2.2
) of the duality mapping

ensure that
\|J(x_{k})\|\to\|J(x)\|
. Finally, we get by
[
6

as

, which thus completes the proof.

x=P^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi\big(x+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)\;\mbox{ and }\;\nabla e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi\big(x+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)=x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*},

which yield in turn to the relationships

\displaystyle\varphi(x^{\prime})-\langle\bar{x}^{*},x^{\prime}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\big\|x^{\prime}-\big(x^{\prime}+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi\big(x^{\prime}+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi\big(x+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)+\big\langle\nabla e^{\bar{x}^{*}}_{\lambda}\varphi\big(x+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big),x^{\prime}-x\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\varphi(x)-\langle\bar{x}^{*},x\rangle+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\big\|x-\big(x+\lambda J^{-1}(x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*})\big)\big\|^{2}\right]+\langle x^{*}-\bar{x}^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle.

After the simplification of the above, we arrive at the estimate

\varphi(x^{\prime})\geq\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle,

which verifies (
4.16
) and thus completes the proof of the theorem.

If
\bar{x}
is a
stationary point
of
\varphi
, i.e.,
\bar{x}^{*}=0
,
we get the immediate consequence of Theorem
4.6
:

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
X
be a
2
-uniformly convex Banach space which norm is Gâteaux differentiable at nonzero points, and let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. and prox-bounded function with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Consider the following assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
0
.

(ii)

\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0
, and the Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}\varphi
is locally convex around
\bar{x}
for all small
\lambda>0
.

Then we have implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
under the general assumptions made, while the reverse implication
(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i)
holds provided that
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around

.

The last result of this section establishes a characterization of variational convexity for functions defined on
Hilbert spaces
, where we can replace the
\bar{x}^{*}
-Moreau envelope (
4.3
) around
\bar{x}
by its standard counterpart (
4.1
) around
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
, where
\bar{x}^{*}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\subset X^{*}
is replaced by
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})\subset X
due to
X=X^{*}
. The corollary below is a Hilbert space extension of the finite-dimensional characterization obtained in
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space, and let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. and prox-bounded function with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Consider the following assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
.

(ii)

\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
, and the Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}\varphi
is locally convex around
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
for all small
\lambda>0
.

Then implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
is satisfied under the general assumptions imposed, while the reverse implication
(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i)
holds if in addition
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
.

[
Proof.]
To deduce this corollary from Theorem
4.6
, it suffices to show that the local convexity of the
\bar{v}
-Moreau envelope
e^{\bar{v}}_{\lambda}\varphi
around
\bar{x}
is equivalent to the local convexity of the standard one
e_{\lambda}\varphi
around
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
. Indeed, we get from (
4.5
) that

e_{\lambda}^{\bar{v}}\varphi(x)=e_{\lambda}\varphi(x+\lambda\bar{v})-\langle\bar{v},x\rangle-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\bar{v}\|^{2}\;\text{ for all }\;x\in X.

(4.17)

Defining
\psi_{\bar{v}}(x):=x+\lambda\bar{v}
for
x\in X
, the equality in (
4.17
) can be rewritten as

e_{\lambda}^{\bar{v}}\varphi(x)=(e_{\lambda}\varphi\circ\psi_{\bar{v}})(x)-\langle\bar{v},x\rangle-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\bar{v}\|^{2},\quad x\in X.

Since
\psi_{\bar{v}}
is an affine mapping while
\langle\bar{v},\cdot\rangle
is a convex function, the local convexity of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
around
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
yields the same property of
e^{\bar{v}}_{\lambda}\varphi
around
\bar{x}
. On the other hand, (
4.17
) is represented as

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)=e^{\bar{v}}_{\lambda}\varphi(x-\lambda\bar{v})+\langle\bar{v},x\rangle-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\bar{v}\|^{2}=(e^{\bar{v}}_{\lambda}\varphi\circ\theta_{\bar{v}})(x)+\langle\bar{v},x\rangle-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\bar{v}\|^{2},

where
\theta_{\bar{v}}(x):=x-\lambda\bar{v}
,
x\in X
, is an affine mapping. Thus the local convexity of
e^{\bar{v}}_{\lambda}\varphi
around
\bar{x}
reduces to that of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
around
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
. Combining finally Theorem
4.6
with the above equivalence, we arrive at the claimed result.

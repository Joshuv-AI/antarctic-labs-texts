Spontaneous scalarization of charged stars
Masato Minamitsuji, Shinji Tsujikawa

Abstract
We study static and spherically symmetric charged stars with a nontrivial profile of the scalar field ϕ \phi in Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theories. The scalar field is coupled to a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge field A μ A_{\mu} with the form − α ( ϕ ) F μ ​ ν F μ ​ ν / 4 -\alpha(\phi)F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4 , where F μ ​ ν = ∂ μ A ν − ∂ ν A μ F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu} is the field strength tensor. Analogous to the case of charged black holes, we show that this type of interaction can induce spontaneous scalarization of charged stars under the conditions ( d ​ α / d ​ ϕ ) ​ ( 0 ) = 0 ({\rm d}\alpha/{\rm d}\phi)(0)=0 and ( d 2 ​ α / d ​ ϕ 2 ) ​ ( 0 ) > 0 ({\rm d}^{2}\alpha/{\rm d}\phi^{2})(0)>0 . For the coupling α ( ϕ ) = exp ( − β ϕ 2 / M pl 2 ) \alpha(\phi)=\exp(-\beta\phi^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2}) , where β ( < 0 ) \beta~(<0) is a coupling constant and M pl M_{\rm pl} is a reduced Planck mass, there is a branch of charged star solutions with a nontrivial profile of ϕ \phi approaching 0 0 toward spatial infinity, besides a branch of general relativistic solutions with a vanishing scalar field, i.e., solutions in the Einstein-Maxwell model. As the ratio ρ c / ρ m \rho_{c}/\rho_{m} between charge density ρ c \rho_{c} and matter density ρ m \rho_{m} increases toward its maximum value, the mass M M of charged stars in general relativity tends to be enhanced due to the increase of repulsive Coulomb force against gravity. In this regime, the appearance of nontrivial branches induced by negative β \beta of order − 1 -1 effectively reduces the Coulomb force for a wide range of central matter densities, leading to charged stars with smaller masses and radii in comparison to those in the general relativistic branch. Our analysis indicates that spontaneous scalarization of stars can be induced not only by the coupling to curvature invariants but also by the scalar-gauge coupling in Einstein gravity.

The occurrence of spontaneous scalarization does not necessarily

\phi

of the form
-\alpha(\phi)F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4

In string theory, for example, there is a dilaton field
\phi

the form
\alpha(\phi)=e^{-\phi}

On the other hand, if we consider a coupling
\alpha(\phi)

satisfying the conditions

({\rm d}\alpha/{\rm d}\phi)(0)=0
and

({\rm d}^{2}\alpha/{\rm d}\phi^{2})(0)>0
,
e.g.,
\alpha(\phi)=\exp(-\beta\phi^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2})
,
there exist charge-induced scalarized black hole solutions
besides the GR branch with a vanishing scalar field.
In this case, the Reissner-Nordström black holes can
evolve into perturbatively stable hairy solutions.
Moreover, the occurrence of spontaneous scalarization is
not necessarily restricted to strong gravity backgrounds.
Even in the absence of gravity, the coupling

-\alpha(\phi)F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4

is capable of inducing scalarization of a charged object like
a conducting sphere

;
Herdeiro:2020htm

Essentially, the notion of spontaneous scalarization
is not exclusive for black holes/neutron stars
and for the presence of gravity in underlying theories,
and the external strong gravitational or electric field
just acts as the trigger of tachyonic instabilities
in the scalar-field sector.

The past works about charge-induced spontaneous scalarization
have been restricted to black holes or conducting charged spheres
in Minkowski spacetime.
In this paper, we will study whether spontaneous scalarization
can take place for electrically charged stars in Einstein-Maxwell-scalar
theories.
For the matter sector inside the star, we consider a perfect fluid with a
matter density
\rho_{m}
endowed with a conserved charged current

{J_{c}}^{\mu}
coupled to the
gauge field
A_{\mu}
. In GR, it is known that the extra
Coulomb force induced by a large charge density
\rho_{c}

leads to an imbalance between gravity and
fluid pressures
Ray:2003gt
(see also
Refs.
Bekenstein:1971ej
;
deFelice:1999qp
;
Anninos:2001yb
).
Introducing the ratio
\mu:=M_{\rm pl}\left(\rho_{c}/\rho_{m}\right)
,
charged stars in GR tend to be unstable as
\mu

approaches its maximally allowed value around 0.7.

Our goal in this paper is to elucidate whether the scalar-gauge coupling

-\alpha(\phi)F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4
gives rise to scalarized
solutions which can be the endpoint of tachyonic instabilities of
the GR branch
\phi=0
.
For the coupling
\alpha(\phi)=\exp(-\beta\phi^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2})
,
we will show that, above some threshold values of
\mu
,
the scalarized stars whose masses and radii are
smaller than those in the GR branch arise for
\beta=-{\cal O}(1)

in the wide range of matter densities.
Thus, charge-induced spontaneous scalarization can
occur not only for black holes but also for gravitationally
bounded stars.

Throughout the paper, we will work in natural units with
the reduced Planck mass
M_{\rm pl}
.
If necessary, one can switch to Gaussian-cgs units
with the gravitational constant
G

and the speed of light
c
,
by the replacements of

M_{\rm pl}\to c^{2}/\sqrt{8\pi G}
,

\rho_{m}\to\rho_{m}c^{2}
,
and
\rho_{c}\to\sqrt{8\pi}\rho_{c}
,
with
\mu\to(1/\sqrt{G})\left(\rho_{c}/\rho_{m}\right)
.

II
Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theories with a charged perfect fluid

We begin with Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theories given by the action

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\left[\frac{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}{2}\sqrt{-g}R+L_{\phi F}+L_{m}+L_{c}\right]\,,

(1)

where

\displaystyle L_{\phi F}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\phi+\frac{\alpha(\phi)}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right]\,,

(2)

\displaystyle L_{m}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\left[\sqrt{-g}\,\rho_{m}(n)+{J_{m}}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\ell_{m}\right]\,,

(3)

\displaystyle L_{c}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-{J_{c}}^{\mu}\left(\partial_{\mu}\ell_{c}-A_{\mu}\right)=-J_{c\mu}\left(\partial_{\nu}\ell_{c}-A_{\nu}\right)g^{\mu\nu}\,.

(4)

Here,
g
is the determinant of metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
,

R
is the Ricci scalar,
\alpha(\phi)
is a function of
the scalar field
\phi
, and

F_{\mu\nu}:=\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu}\,(=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu})

is the antisymmetric field strength tensor of a gauge field
A_{\mu}
,
with
\nabla_{\mu}
the covariant derivative operator,
respectively.

The term
L_{m}
given by Eq. (
3
) corresponds to the
Schutz-Sorkin Lagrangian
Sorkin
;
Brown
;
DGS
;
Kase:2020hst

describing a perfect fluid with the matter
density
\rho_{m}
and current vector
{J_{m}}^{\mu}
.
The matter density
\rho_{m}
is a function of
the fluid number density
n
.
The scalar quantity
\ell_{m}
is a Lagrange multiplier,
where
\partial_{\mu}\ell_{m}
is the partial derivative of

\ell_{m}
with respect to the spacetime coordinate
x^{\mu}
.
The existence of the term
\partial_{\mu}\ell_{m}

in Eq. (
1
) ensures the current conservation

\partial_{\mu}{J_{m}}^{\mu}=0\,.

(5)

Besides the derivative
\partial_{\mu}\ell_{m}
, we generally have the term

{\cal A}_{i}\partial_{\mu}{\cal B}^{i}
arising from spatial vectors

{\cal A}_{i}
and
{\cal B}^{i}
.
Since
{\cal A}_{i}
vanishes on a static and spherically symmetric
background
Kase:2020qvz
, we will not consider this term throughout the paper.
The fluid four velocity
u^{\mu}
is related to
{J_{m}}^{\mu}
, as

{J_{m}}^{\mu}=n\sqrt{-g}\,u^{\mu}\,.

(6)

Due to the property
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
, the number density
can be expressed in the form

n=\sqrt{\frac{{J_{m}}^{\mu}{J_{m\mu}}}{g}}\,.

(7)

In the Lagrangian
L_{c}
given by Eq. (
4
),

{J_{c}}^{\mu}
corresponds to a charged current.
The presence of a Lagrange multiplier
\partial_{\mu}\ell_{c}
ensures
the charge current conservation

\partial_{\mu}{J_{c}}^{\mu}=0\,.

(8)

Analogous to Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
),
there are the following relations

{J_{c}}^{\mu}=\rho_{c}\sqrt{-g}\,u^{\mu}\,,\qquad\rho_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{{J_{c}}^{\mu}{J_{c\mu}}}{g}}\,,

(9)

where
\rho_{c}
is a charge density.
Varying the action (
1
) with respect to

{J_{c}}^{\mu}
,
it follows that

\partial_{\mu}\ell_{c}=A_{\mu}\,.

(10)

Variations of the action (
1
) with respect to
A_{\mu}

and
\phi
lead, respcetively, to

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left[\alpha(\phi)F^{\mu\nu}\right]=-\frac{{J_{c}}^{\nu}}{\sqrt{-g}}=-\rho_{c}u^{\nu}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi-\frac{\alpha_{,\phi}}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(12)

where
\alpha_{,\phi}:={\rm d}\alpha/{\rm d}\phi
.

Varying the Lagrangian
L_{\phi F}
given by Eq. (
2
)
with respect to
g^{\mu\nu}
and using the
property
\delta\sqrt{-g}=-\sqrt{-g}g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}/2

tensor arising from

T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi F)}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta L_{\phi F}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi\nabla^{\alpha}\phi+\alpha(\phi)g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\sigma}-\frac{\alpha(\phi)}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\,.

We also vary the Schtuz-Sorkin Lagrangian
L_{m}
given by Eq. (
3
)
with respect to
g^{\mu\nu}
and exploit the property

\delta n=(n/2)\left(g_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\right)\delta g^{\mu\nu}
.
We also make use of the relation

\partial_{\mu}\ell_{m}=\rho_{m,n}u_{\mu}
, which follows from
the variation of Eq. (
1
) with respect to

{J_{m}}^{\mu}

;
Tsujikawa:2020die

Then, we obtain the standard form of the

T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta L_{m}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=\left(\rho_{m}+P_{m}\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+P_{m}g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(14)

where
P_{m}
is the matter pressure defined by

P_{m}=n\rho_{m,n}-\rho_{m}\,.

(15)

For the last term of Eq. (
1
), we vary
the Lagrangian
L_{c}
given by Eq. (
4
) with respect to
g^{\mu\nu}
.
On using Eq. (
10
), the energy-momentum tensor associated

is

T_{\mu\nu}^{(c)}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta L_{c}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=0\,.

(16)

Then, the gravitational field equations arising from the action
(
1
) with Eqs. (
2
)-(
4
) are

M_{\rm pl}^{2}G_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi F)}+T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}\,,

(17)

where
G_{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein tensor obeying the
Bianchi identity
\nabla^{\nu}G_{\mu\nu}=0
.
On using Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) as well as
the Maxwell equations

\nabla_{\mu}F_{\nu\sigma}+\nabla_{\nu}F_{\sigma\mu}+\nabla_{\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}=0
, it follows that

\nabla^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi F)}=-\rho_{c}F_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}\,.

(18)

Due to the conservation of the total energy-momentum tensor

\nabla^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi F)}+\nabla^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}=0
,
we have

\nabla^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}=\rho_{c}F_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}\,,

(19)

where the right hand side corresponds to the Coulomb force.
Since the anti-symmetric tensor
F_{\mu\nu}
satisfies the relation

F_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=0
, multiplying Eq. (
19
)
with
u^{\mu}
gives

u^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}=0\,.

(20)

From Eq. (
6
), the matter current
conservation (
5
) translates to

u_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\rho_{m}+(\rho_{m}+P_{m})\nabla^{\mu}u_{\mu}=0\,.

(21)

On using (
21
), Eq. (
19
) can be expressed
in the form

\left(g_{\mu\nu}+u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\right)\nabla^{\nu}P_{m}+\left(\rho_{m}+P_{m}\right)u_{\nu}\nabla^{\nu}u_{\mu}=\rho_{c}F_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}\,.

(22)

Multiplying the left hand side of Eq. (
22
) with

u^{\mu}
and exploiting the property
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
,
we can confirm that the relation (
20
) indeed holds.
We also introduce a unit vector
n^{\nu}
orthogonal to

u_{\nu}
, such that

n^{\nu}u_{\nu}=0
and
n^{\nu}n_{\nu}=1
.
Multiplying Eq. (

) with

, we obtain

(23)

This shows the balance between the three forces, i.e.,

n^{\mu}

III
Static and spherically symmetric background

Let us consider a static and spherically symmetric background
given by the line element

{\rm d}s^{2}=-f(r){\rm d}t^{2}+h^{-1}(r){\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}\left({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,{\rm d}\varphi^{2}\right)\,,

(24)

where
f(r)
and
h(r)
are functions of the radial
coordinate
r
.
The configurations of
\phi
,
n
,
A_{\mu}
, and
u^{\mu}

compatible with this background are given by

\phi=\phi(r)\,,\qquad n=n(r)\,,\qquad A_{\mu}=\left(A_{t}(r),0,0,0\right)\,,\qquad u^{\mu}=\left(f(r)^{-1/2},0,0,0\right)\,.

(25)

Then, Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) reduce,
respectively, to

\displaystyle A_{t}^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{2}{r}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}+\frac{h^{\prime}}{2h}+\frac{\alpha_{,\phi}}{\alpha}\phi^{\prime}\right)A_{t}^{\prime}-\frac{\sqrt{f}\rho_{c}}{h\alpha}=0\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}+\frac{h^{\prime}}{2h}\right)\phi^{\prime}+\frac{A_{t}^{\prime 2}}{2f}\alpha_{,\phi}=0\,,

(27)

where a ‘prime’ represents the derivative with respect to
r
.
The
tt
and
rr
components of Einstein Eqs. (
17
) give

\displaystyle h^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{h-1}{r}-\frac{hr\phi^{\prime 2}}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha rhA_{t}^{\prime 2}}{2fM_{\rm pl}^{2}}-\frac{r}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\rho_{m}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{f(h-1)}{hr}+\frac{fr\phi^{\prime 2}}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha rA_{t}^{\prime 2}}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}}+\frac{fr}{hM_{\rm pl}^{2}}P_{m}\,.

(29)

Taking the unit vector orthogonal to
u_{\mu}
as

n^{\mu}=(0,h^{1/2}(r),0,0)
, Eq. (
23
) reduces to

P_{m}^{\prime}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\left(\rho_{m}+P_{m}\right)=\frac{\rho_{c}A_{t}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{f}}\,.

(30)

The star radius
r_{s}
is identified by the condition

P_{m}(r_{s})=0\,.

(31)

We assume that
\rho_{m}
,
P_{m}
, and
\rho_{c}
vanish
outside the star. Inside the star, we define the
dimensionless ratio

\mu:=M_{\rm pl}\frac{\rho_{c}}{\rho_{m}}\,,

(32)

which generally depends on
r
.
For the numerical analysis performed later in Sec.
IV
,
we will consider the case in which
\mu
is constant.

We define the mass function
{\cal M}(r)
, according to

h(r)=1-\frac{{\cal M}(r)}{4\pi M_{\rm pl}^{2}r}\,.

(33)

The Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) mass corresponds to
the asymptotic value of
{\cal M}(r)
, such that

M=\lim_{r\to\infty}{\cal M}(r)=\lim_{r\to\infty}4\pi M_{\rm pl}^{2}r\left[1-h(r)\right]\,.

(34)

As we see in Eq. (
28
), the ADM mass not only contains the contribution
from
\rho_{m}
but also those from
A_{t}^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime}
.
Since both
A_{t}^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime}
do not vanish outside the star, the mass

{\cal M}(r_{s})
computed at the surface of star is not generally
identical to
M
.
To extract the mass from the matter density
\rho_{m}
, we define the
proper mass of star, as

M_{p}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}^{3}x\,\rho_{m}\sqrt{{}^{(3)}g}=\int_{0}^{r_{s}}\frac{4\pi\rho_{m}\,r^{2}}{\sqrt{h}}{\rm d}r\,,

(35)

where
{}^{(3)}g
is the determinant of
three-dimensional spatial metric.
We define the gravitational binding energy, according to
the difference between
M_{p}
and
M
, as

\Delta:=M_{p}-M\,.

(36)

The star is gravitationally bound for
\Delta>0
,
which can be regarded as a necessary condition for
its perturbative stability,
while if
\Delta<0
the star is gravitationally unbounded.

For the equation of state (EOS) of relativistic stars,
we consider a polytropic type of
the form
Damour:1993hw

\rho_{m}=\bar{\rho}_{m}\left(\chi+\frac{K}{\Gamma-1}\chi^{\Gamma}\right)\,,\qquad P_{m}=K\bar{\rho}_{m}\chi^{\Gamma}\,,

(37)

where
\bar{\rho}_{m}
,
K
, and
\Gamma
are constants.
We define the constant matter density

as

\bar{\rho}_{m}=\bar{n}_{m}m_{b}=1.6749\times 10^{14}
g cm
-3
,
where
\bar{n}_{m}=0.1\,{\rm(fm)^{-3}}
is
the typical nuclear number density and
m_{b}
is
the mean rest mass of baryons.
The dimensionless variable
\chi
is given by
\chi=n_{b}/\bar{n}_{m}
,
where
n_{b}
is the baryon number density.
The EOS parameter is defined by

w_{m}:=\frac{P_{m}}{\rho_{m}}=\frac{K\chi^{\Gamma-1}}{1+K\chi^{\Gamma-1}/(\Gamma-1)}\,.

(38)

In the low-density regime characterized by
K\chi^{\Gamma-1}\ll 1

the EOS parameter reduces to
w_{m}\simeq K\chi^{\Gamma-1}
, whereas,
in the high-density regime (
K\chi^{\Gamma-1}\gg 1
),
w_{m}\simeq\Gamma-1
.

To solve the background Eqs. (
26
)-(
30
) numerically,
we introduce the following dimensionless quantities

s=\ln\frac{r}{\bar{r}}\,,\qquad y=\frac{\rho_{m}}{\bar{\rho}_{m}}\,,\qquad z=\frac{P_{m}}{\bar{\rho}_{m}}\,,\qquad\tilde{A}_{t}=\frac{A_{t}}{M_{\rm pl}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\phi}=\frac{\phi}{M_{\rm pl}}\,,\qquad m(r)=\frac{3{\cal M}(r)}{4\pi\bar{\rho}_{m}\bar{r}^{3}}\,,

(39)

where

\bar{r}=\frac{M_{\rm pl}}{\sqrt{\bar{\rho}_{m}}}=17.885~{\rm km}\,.

(40)

Then, Eq. (
30
) can be expressed in the form

\frac{{\rm d}z}{{\rm d}s}+\frac{1}{2f}\frac{{\rm d}f}{{\rm d}s}\left(y+z\right)=\frac{\mu y}{\sqrt{f}}\frac{{\rm d}\tilde{A}_{t}}{{\rm d}s}\,,

(41)

where, for the polytropic EOS, we have

y=\left(\frac{z}{K}\right)^{1/\Gamma}+\frac{z}{\Gamma-1}\,,\qquad z=K\chi^{\Gamma}\,.

(42)

In terms of the solar mass
M_{\odot}=1.9884\times 10^{33}
g,
the mass function
{\cal M}(r)
is expressed as

{\cal M}(r)=2.0186\,m(r)M_{\odot}\,,

(43)

where, from Eq. (
28
),
m(r)
obeys

\frac{{\rm d}m}{{\rm d}s}=3ye^{3s}+\frac{3x-m}{2f}\left[f\left(\frac{{\rm d}\tilde{\phi}}{{\rm d}s}\right)^{2}+\alpha\left(\frac{{\rm d}\tilde{A}_{t}}{{\rm d}s}\right)^{2}\right]\,.

(44)

For a given value of
\chi
around
r=0
,
we integrate Eqs. (
41
) and (
44
) outwards
together with the normalized versions of
Eqs. (
26
), (
27
), and
(
29
) to solve for
z
,
m
,
\tilde{A}_{t}
,
\tilde{\phi}
,
and
f
. The ADM mass
M
is known from Eq. (
43
)
after the integration to a distance much larger than
r_{s}
.
We also define
{\cal M}_{p}(r)=4\pi\bar{\rho}_{m}\bar{r}^{3}m_{p}(r)/3=\int_{0}^{r}4\pi\rho_{m}\tilde{r}^{2}/\sqrt{h}\,{\rm d}\tilde{r}

and solve the differential equation

\frac{{\rm d}m_{p}}{{\rm d}s}=\frac{3ye^{3s}}{\sqrt{h}}\,,

(45)

up to
r=r_{s}
. Then, we obtain the proper mass of star (
35
),
according to the relation
M_{p}=2.0186m_{p}(r_{s})M_{\odot}
.

IV
Spontaneous scalarization of charged stars

In this section we first derive a necessary condition for spontaneous scalarization
to occur and then study the existence of scalarized solutions for a concrete
scalar-gauge coupling. Varying the action (
1
)
with respect to
\phi
on a general background,
the scalar field obeys

\square\phi+\alpha_{,\phi}(\phi)F=0\,,\qquad{\rm where}\qquad F=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,.

(46)

First, we require that, in the absence of the scalar field,
\phi=0
,
the standard normalization for the Maxwell term

-F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4

is recovered in the gravitational action (
1
), i.e.,
\alpha(0)=1
.
The existence of the GR branch
\phi=0
requires
that
\alpha_{,\phi}(0)=0
.
In this paper, by “GR solutions”
we mean the solutions in Einstein-Maxwell theory
minimally coupled to the matter sector.

The perturbation
\delta\phi
around the solution
\phi=0
obeys

\left(\square-m_{\rm eff}^{2}\right)\delta\phi=0\,,\qquad{\rm where}\qquad m_{\rm eff}^{2}=-F\alpha_{,\phi\phi}(0)\,.

(47)

There is a tachyonic instability for
m_{\rm eff}^{2}<0
,
i.e.,
F\alpha_{,\phi\phi}(0)>0
.
On the background (
24
),
we have
F=hA_{t}^{\prime 2}/(2f)>0
,
so the solution
\phi=0
is unstable for
\alpha_{,\phi\phi}(0)>0
.
In summary, the necessary conditions for spontaneous scalarization
to occur from the GR branch to the other nontrivial
branch with
\phi\neq 0
are given by

\alpha_{,\phi}(0)=0\,,\qquad\alpha_{,\phi\phi}(0)>0\,.

(48)

To satisfy these conditions, the coupling
\alpha(\phi)
needs to
take the form

\displaystyle\alpha(\phi)=1-\beta\frac{\phi^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\beta_{n}\left(\frac{\phi^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\right)^{n},

(49)

where
\beta
and
\beta_{n}
(
n=2,3,4,\cdots
) are constants.
One of the examples is

\alpha(\phi)=\exp\left(-\beta\frac{\phi^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\right)\,.

(50)

In this case, the above necessary
conditions are satisfied for

\beta<0\,.

(51)

In the following, we will focus on the coupling (
50
)
to study the existence of scalarized solutions.

IV.1
Expansions in the vicinity of center
r=0
and
spatial infinity
r\to\infty

Around the origin of star (
r=0
), we need to impose the regular
boundary conditions

\phi^{\prime}(0)=A_{t}^{\prime}(0)=h^{\prime}(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=\rho_{m}^{\prime}(0)=P_{m}^{\prime}(0)=\rho_{c}^{\prime}(0)=0
.
The solutions compatible with these conditions
are expressed in the forms of

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\phi_{0}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\phi_{i}r^{i}\,,\qquad A_{t}(r)=A_{t0}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}A_{ti}r^{i}\,,\qquad h(r)=1+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}h_{i}r^{i}\,,\qquad f(r)=f_{0}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}f_{i}r^{i}\,,

\displaystyle\rho_{m}(r)=\rho_{m0}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\rho_{mi}r^{i}\,,\qquad P_{m}(r)=P_{m0}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}P_{mi}r^{i}\,,\qquad\rho_{c}(r)=\rho_{c0}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\rho_{ci}r^{i}\,,

(52)

where
\phi_{0},\phi_{i},A_{t0},A_{ti},h_{i},f_{0},f_{i},\rho_{m0},\rho_{mi},P_{m0},P_{mi},\rho_{c0}
, and
\rho_{ci}
are constants.
Substituting Eq. (
52
) into Eqs. (
26
)-(
30
),
the iterative solutions, up to next-to-leading order, are given by

\displaystyle\phi(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{0}+\frac{\beta\phi_{0}e^{\beta\phi_{0}^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\rho_{c0}^{2}}{180M_{\rm pl}^{2}}r^{4}+{\cal O}(r^{6})\,,

(53)

\displaystyle A_{t}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{t0}+\frac{\sqrt{f_{0}}\rho_{c0}e^{\beta\phi_{0}^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2}}}{6}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{4})\,,

(54)

\displaystyle h(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\rho_{m0}}{3M_{\rm pl}^{2}}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{4})\,,

(55)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}+\frac{f_{0}(\rho_{m0}+3P_{m0})}{6M_{\rm pl}^{2}}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{4})\,,

(56)

\displaystyle P_{m}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{m0}-\frac{(\rho_{m0}+3P_{m0})(\rho_{m0}+P_{m0})-2e^{\beta\phi_{0}^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\rho_{c0}^{2}M_{\rm pl}^{2}}{12M_{\rm pl}^{2}}r^{2}+{\cal O}(r^{4})\,.

(57)

The constant
f_{0}
represents the freedom of
rescaling of the time coordinate,
whose choice does not affect physical
quantities such as the mass and radius of star.

When
\beta=0
or
\phi_{0}=0
,
the scalar field
\phi(r)
vanishes,
so that it does not affect the profiles of
A_{t}(r)
,
h(r)
,
f(r)
,
and
P_{m}(r)
.
The existence of charge density
\rho_{c0}
gives rise to
a nonvanishing electric field
A_{t}^{\prime}(r)=\sqrt{f_{0}}\rho_{c0}r/3+{\cal O}(r^{3})

around the origin.
For
\beta=0
or
\phi_{0}=0
, the last term

\rho_{c0}^{2}r^{2}/6
in Eq. (
57
)
works as a Coulomb force against gravity.
In the absence of this term, the pressure decreases for increasing
r
.
In order to ensure the existence of charged stars
against the destabilization by the Coulomb force,
we require that
P_{m}^{\prime}(r)<0
.
We use the relation
\rho_{c0}=\rho_{m0}\mu/M_{\rm pl}

obtained from Eq. (
32
) and assume that

\mu
is constant. Then, from Eq. (
57
), the condition

P_{m}^{\prime}(r)<0
translates to

\mu<\sqrt{\frac{(1+3w_{m0})(1+w_{m0})}{2}}\,,

(58)

where
w_{m0}=P_{m0}/\rho_{m0}
is the EOS parameter at
r=0
.
To satisfy this condition in the nonrelativistic regime with
w_{m0}\ll 1
,
the parameter
\mu
should be in the range

\mu<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

(59)

Thus, the charge density relative to the matter density is
bounded from above to realize a gravitationally
bounded star.

When
\beta<0
and
\phi_{0}\neq 0
,
the second term on the right hand side of
Eq. (
53
), which is proportional to
r^{4}
,
leads to the variation of
\phi(r)
.
Around
r=0
, the metric components
h(r)
and
f(r)

do not possess the
\phi_{0}
dependence up to the order of
r^{2}
,
but the scalar field affects
P_{m}(r)
through the
Coulomb force
e^{\beta\phi_{0}^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\rho_{c0}^{2}r^{2}/6
.
For the negative
\beta
we have
e^{\beta\phi_{0}^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2}}<1
,
so the Coulomb force is suppressed in comparison to
the case of
\beta=0
.
This suggests that the scalar-gauge coupling with
\beta<0

may stabilize the charged star even for the values of
\mu

close to the upper bound (
58
).
The iterative solutions (
53
)-(
57
) lose
their validity around the surface of star, so we will numerically
integrate Eqs. (
26
)-(
30
) around from
r=0
up to
a sufficiently large
r
to confirm the
existence of hairy solutions with
\phi(r)\neq 0
.

Outside the star (
r>r_{s}
), we have
\rho_{c}=0
,
\rho_{m}=0
,
and
P_{m}=0
in Eqs. (
26
)-(
30
).
Then, the integrated solution to Eq. (
26
) is
expressed in the form

A_{t}^{\prime}(r)=Q\frac{\sqrt{f(r)}\,e^{\beta\phi^{2}(r)/M_{\rm pl}^{2}}}{r^{2}\sqrt{h(r)}}\,,

(60)

where
Q
is a constant.
We will consider the case in which the boundary conditions
at spatial infinity are given by

\phi(\infty)=0\,,\qquad h(\infty)=1\,,\qquad f(\infty)=1\,.

(61)

Then, for
r\gg r_{s}
, Eq. (
60
) has the radial dependence

A_{t}^{\prime}(r)\simeq Q/r^{2}
.
Neglecting the term
A_{t}^{\prime 2}\alpha_{,\phi}/(2f)
in Eq. (
27
)
and integrating this equation, we obtain

\phi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{c_{1}}{r^{2}\sqrt{f(r)h(r)}}\,,

(62)

where
c_{1}
is a constant.
At the distance
r\gg r_{s}
the term
A_{t}^{\prime 2}\alpha_{,\phi}/(2f)

decreases faster than
1/r^{4}
, so we can ignore its contribution
to Eq. (
27
).
Substituting the solutions (
60
) and (
62
) into
Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
), it follows that
the contributions of
A_{t}^{\prime}(r)
and
\phi^{\prime}(r)
to
f(r)
and
h(r)

work as corrections (proportional to
1/r^{2}
) to the leading-order
solutions
f(r)=h(r)=1-M/(4\pi M_{\rm pl}^{2}r)
.

The two asymptotic solutions derived in the regimes
r\ll r_{s}
and

r\gg r_{s}
should be matched around the surface of star.
Since we are considering
U(1)
gauge-invariant
theory, the contributions arising from the vector field
to Eqs. (
26
)-(
30
) are the derivatives of
A_{t}
alone.
Hence,
we do not need to specify the constant
A_{t0}
in Eq. (
54
).
On the other hand, the field value
\phi
at
r=0
, i.e.,
\phi_{0}
,
should be determined to satisfy the boundary condition
\phi(\infty)=0

at spatial infinity.
We will iteratively identify the value of
\phi_{0}
by solving the
background equations of motion numerically.

IV.2
No-hair solutions with
\phi=0

Let us first revisit compact star solutions
with the trivial scalar field
\phi=0
.
In this case the term
A_{t}^{\prime 2}\alpha_{,\phi}/(2f)
vanishes even inside
the star for the coupling (
50
), so Eq. (
27
)
is trivially satisfied for any value of
\beta
.
From Eq. (
60
), the solution to
A_{t}^{\prime}(r)
outside the star
(
r>r_{s}
) is given by

A_{t}^{\prime}(r)=Q\frac{\sqrt{f(r)}}{r^{2}\sqrt{h(r)}}\,.

(63)

Substituting Eq. (
63
),
\phi(r)=\phi^{\prime}(r)=0
,
and
\rho_{m}=0=P_{m}

into Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
),
the metric components outside the star consistent with
boundary conditions (
61
) are

f=h=1-\frac{M}{4\pi M_{\rm pl}^{2}r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}r^{2}}\,,

(64)

where
M
corresponds to the ADM mass.
This is known as the Reissner-Nordström metric
in the Einstein-Maxwell model.
Thus, the GR solution exists for any value of
\beta
.

The iterative solutions around the center of star are given by
Eqs. (
54
)-(
57
) with
\phi_{0}=0
.
Since
\phi(r)=0
everywhere, we
integrate Eqs. (
26
) and (
28
)-(
30
)
from
s=\ln r/\bar{r}=-10
to the distance
s>40
(i.e.,
r>10^{17}\bar{r}
)
by using Eqs. (
54
)-(
57
) as the boundary conditions
around
r=0
. For concreteness we consider the polytropic
EOS (
42
) with
\Gamma=5/3
and
K=0.018
, but
the qualitative behavior of solutions should be similar for other
choices of
\Gamma
and
K
.
In Fig.

, we plot the normalized ADM mass

(left)
and

(right)
for several different values of
\mu
.
For increasing
\mu

,

gets larger.
This is mostly attributed to the fact that, around the center of star,
the larger charge density
\rho_{c0}
leads to the slower
decrease of
P_{m}(r)
, according to Eq. (

).
For larger

Scalarized solutions for
\beta<0

IV.3.1
Existence of scalarized solutions

For the negative scalar-gauge coupling (
\beta<0
), we explore the presence
of scalarized solutions with
\phi(r)\neq 0
besides the GR branch
with
\phi(r)=0
. Note that the GR branch corresponds to that
discussed in Sec.
IV.2
.
We consider a positive value of
\phi_{0}
and adopt the polytropic EOS
same as used in Sec.
IV.2
.

\chi_{0}

IV.4
Implications for spontanenous scalarization of charged stars

The results explained above show that, for
\beta=-{\cal O}(1)
and

0.45\lesssim\mu\lesssim 0.7
, the GR branch can undergo
spontaneous scalarization toward the nonvanishing
and nontrivial scalar field solution.
The masses and radii of the scalarized branch are smaller than those of
the GR branch as a result of the suppressed Coulomb force.

Without performing detailed numerical simulations about the process
of spontaneous scalarization, we can speculate to some extent
how the transition from
the GR solution to the scalarized branch proceeds.
For
\mu=0.65
and
\mu=0.68
shown in Fig.
3
,
the 0-node branch is smoothly connected to the GR branch
in low density regimes, showing that the scalarized solution arises
as the consequence of continuous phase transition from the GR solution.
Fig.
3
also indicates that, for
\beta=-5
,
the bifurcation point of the 0-node branch from the GR branch
is located in the vicinity of the maximal star mass in GR.
Hence the bifurcation to the 0-node solution occurs around
the maximum mass of gravitationally bounded charged stars
in GR.

For a fixed value of
\mu
, the increase of
|\beta|
within

5\lesssim|\beta|\lesssim 10
shifts bifurcation points
of the 0-node branch toward the regime of smaller
densities (larger radii) of the star.
In particular, for
\mu=0.68
, the bifurcation point is shifted
to the region
\rho_{m0}\lesssim{\bar{\rho}}_{m}
.
Thus, spontaneous scalarization to 0-node solutions
can occur for a wider range of
\rho_{m0}
.
In the presence of a scalar-gauge coupling,
tachyonic instabilities excite the scalar field
inside the charged GR star and
transfer the energies of matter and electric field to the scalar field.
A part of the energy of this system would be radiated away
via scalar radiation, which reduces the star mass and
makes the 0-node scalarized star perturbatively more stable.

V
Conclusions

In Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theories, we have studied the possibility for the
occurrence of spontaneous scalarization of charged stars.
Such theories can be represented by the explicit action (
1
)
with Eqs. (
2
)-(
4
) containing
the scalar-gauge coupling
-\alpha(\phi)F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4

and the matter and charge currents as a
generalization of the Schtuz-Sorkin Lagrangian.
In Sec.
II
, we have derived all the field equations of
motion in the covariant form and shown that the perfect-fluid energy
momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}
obeys the continuity Eq. (
19
)
with the appearance of a Coulomb force
on its right hand side.

On the static and spherically symmetric spacetime given
by the line element (
24
),
the background equations of motion are of
the forms (
26
)-(
30
).
The field-dependent coupling
\alpha(\phi)

in Eq. (
27
) allows the existence of
a nonvanishing and nontrivial
scalar field profile both inside and outside the star.
This affects the gauge-field derivative
A_{t}^{\prime}
through
Eq. (
26
).
Then, the nontrivial
A_{t}^{\prime}
gives rise to a modified Coulomb force
which changes the force balance inside the charged star, so that
its mass
M
and radius
r_{s}

are generally subject to modifications.

In Sec.
IV
, we showed that the necessary conditions
for spontaneous scalarization to occur are characterized
by Eq. (
48
), which leads to the generic form of the coupling
function (
49
).
One of the examples for this realization is the coupling

\alpha(\phi)=\exp(-\beta\phi^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2})
with
\beta<0
.
For negative
\beta
, besides the GR branch with
\phi(r)=0
,
there exists a scalarized branch where the scalar field profile is given by
Eq. (
53
) for
r\ll r_{s}
and Eq. (
62
) for
r\gg r_{s}
.
As the ratio
\mu=M_{\rm pl}\rho_{c}/\rho_{m}
increases
toward the upper limit
1/\sqrt{2}
, there are significant
enhancement of
M
and
r_{s}
in the GR branch
induced by a large Coulomb force.
For
\beta=-{\cal O}(1)
and
\rho_{m0}\leq 100\bar{\rho}_{m}
,
we numerically confirmed the existence of 0-node
scalarized solutions where
\phi(r)
monotonically decreases from a
positive value
\phi_{0}
(at
r=0
) toward the asymptotic
value 0 at spatial infinity.
The scalar-gauge coupling with
\beta<0
effectively reduces the
Coulomb force, which results in faster decrease of
P_{m}(r)
.
As we observe in Fig.
3
, the masses and radii of stars for
the scaralized branch are smaller than those of the GR branch.
For this scaralized solution, the modifications to
M
and
r_{s}
induced
by a small change of
\mu
around
\mu=0.7
are much less significant
in comparison to those of the GR branch.

The appearance of scalarized solutions depends on the central matter
density
\rho_{m0}
. As
\rho_{m0}
increases, the scalar-field
profiles higher than the 0-node start to appear, see the right
panel of Fig.
2
.
Since these higher-node solutions correspond to excited states of
the charged star, we picked up the 0-node solution as a most stable ground
state of the scalarized branch for the polytropic EOS with
\rho_{m0}\leq 100\bar{\rho}_{m}
.
We found that, for
\beta=-{\cal O}(1)
and
0.45\lesssim\mu\lesssim 0.7
,
the scalarized (0-node) solutions can exist for a wide range of
\rho_{m0}
.
In this regime of
\mu
, the charged GR star with a large Coulomb force
should undergo spontaneous scalarization toward
the scalarized branch with smaller values of
M
and
r_{s}
.
This property should also hold for other EOSs because
the Coulomb force
e^{\beta\phi_{0}^{2}/M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\rho_{c0}^{2}r^{2}/6

appearing in the iterative solution (
57
) of
P_{m}(r)

is generally suppressed for the scalarized branch with negative
\beta
.

It will be of interest to study the stability of our new scalarized solutions
against perturbations in the odd- and even-parity sectors.
The analysis can be done in the similar way to that performed
for relativistic stars in nonminimally coupled
scalar-tensor theories
Kase:2020qvz
.
We leave this issue for a future work.

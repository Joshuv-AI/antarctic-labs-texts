Improved K e ​ 3 K_{e3} radiative corrections sharpen the K μ ​ 2 K_{\mu 2} – K l ​ 3 K_{l3} discrepancy
Chien-Yeah Seng 1, Daniel Galviz 1, Mikhail Gorchtein 2,3,4, Ulf-G. Meißner 1,5,6

Abstract
The measurements of V u ​ s V_{us} in leptonic ( K μ ​ 2 ) (K_{\mu 2}) and semileptonic ( K l ​ 3 ) (K_{l3}) kaon decays exhibit a 3 ​ σ 3\sigma disagreement, which could originate either from physics beyond the Standard Model or some large unidentified Standard Model systematic effects. Clarifying this issue requires a careful examination of all existing Standard Model inputs. Making use of a newly-proposed computational framework and the most recent lattice QCD results, we perform a comprehensive re-analysis of the electroweak radiative corrections to the K e ​ 3 K_{e3} decay rates that achieves an unprecedented level of precision of 10 − 4 10^{-4} , which improves the current best results by almost an order of magnitude. No large systematic effects are found, which suggests that the electroweak radiative corrections should be removed from the “list of culprits” responsible for the K μ ​ 2 K_{\mu 2} – K l ​ 3 K_{l3} discrepancy.

After going through all the virtual corrections, we switch to the contribution from the
n=1

process, which is simply known as the “bremsstrahlung contribution”. According to the discussions in
Appendix
A
, the bremsstrahlung process contributes to the differential decay width

d\Gamma_{K_{e3}}/dydz
not only in the
\mathcal{D}_{3}
region but also in the
\mathcal{D}_{4-3}
region,
the latter has no correspondence in the
n=0
process. Therefore, it is eventually up to the
experimentalists to decide in which region of
\{y,z\}
will the data be taken, and whether or not a
veto will be applied to exclude decay events with hard photons. Of course, the simplest choice
is to not apply any veto, and to collect data from all available regions of
\{y,z\}
. This
corresponds to a fully-inclusive prescription of the real photon emission process, or in other
words, we should calculate the sum of the full
n=0
and
n=1
decay width. This
prescription was adopted in Ref.
Cirigliano:2008wn
and will be followed in this work.

The bremsstrahlung amplitude, depicted by the two diagrams in Fig.
2
, reads:

\displaystyle M_{K\rightarrow\pi e^{+}\nu\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{F}e}{\sqrt{2}}\bar{u}_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\left\{\frac{p_{e}\cdot\varepsilon^{*}(k)}{p_{e}\cdot k}+\frac{\not{k}\not{\varepsilon}^{*}(k)}{2p_{e}\cdot k}\right\}v_{e}F_{\mu}^{K\pi}(p^{\prime},p)

(58)

\displaystyle+\frac{iG_{F}e}{\sqrt{2}}\bar{u}_{\nu}\gamma^{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})v_{e}\varepsilon^{\mu*}(k)T_{\mu\nu}^{K\pi}(k;p^{\prime},p)\penalty\ .

We observe that the generalized Compton tensor
T_{\mu\nu}^{K\pi}
appears again, only that now one
deals with a real photon. Unlike in the loop diagrams, here we only need to know
T_{\mu\nu}^{K\pi}

for small (due to the phase-space constraint) and on-shell photon momentum
k
, so instead of
exhausting the contributions from all intermediate states, it is possible to adopt a
low-energy effective expression
T_{\mu\nu}^{K\pi}
. It should, however, satisfy three basic criteria:

•

It must contain the full convection term contribution to ensure an exact cancellation
of the IR-divergence from the virtual corrections.

•

It should include the seagull term, as the effect of the latter is not particularly suppressed
in the decay rate, unlike in the loop diagrams.

•

It should satisfy exact electromagnetic gauge invariance, so that one could perform the usual
replacement
\sum_{s}\varepsilon^{\mu}_{s}(k)\varepsilon^{\nu*}_{s}(k)\rightarrow-g^{\mu\nu}
in the sum of
the outgoing photon polarizations.

The simplest effective expression that satisfies all these criteria is:

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{K^{0}\pi^{-}}(k;p^{\prime},p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i(2p^{\prime}+k)^{\mu}F^{\nu}_{K^{0}\pi^{-}}(p^{\prime},p)}{(p^{\prime}+k)^{2}-M_{\pi}^{2}}+\left\{iV_{us}^{*}\left[\frac{(2p^{\prime}+k)^{\mu}k^{\nu}}{(p^{\prime}+k)^{2}-M_{\pi}^{2}}-g^{\mu\nu}\right]+\mathcal{O}(p^{4})\right\}

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{K^{+}\pi^{0}}(k;p^{\prime},p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i(2p-k)^{\mu}F^{\nu}_{K^{+}\pi^{0}}(p^{\prime},p)}{(p-k)^{2}-M_{K}^{2}}+\left\{\frac{iV_{us}^{*}}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\frac{(2p-k)^{\mu}k^{\nu}}{(p-k)^{2}-M_{K}^{2}}+g^{\mu\nu}\right]+\mathcal{O}(p^{4})\right\}.

(59)

The first term on the right-hand side in the expressions above is just the convection term, whereas
the remainders are the seagull term and the extra pieces from the Born contribution needed to
recover gauge invariance. Notice that the convection term is exact, and only the terms in
the curly bracket undergo a chiral expansion. In fact, if we expand the convection term to

\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
, the LO ChPT expression in Eq.(
25
) is recovered. In fact,
the existing ChPT calculation uses exactly Eq.(

VIII
Comparing with the ChPT result

We have now finished calculating all components of the
\mathcal{O}(G_{F}^{2}\alpha)
electroweak RC to
the
K_{e3}
decay rate. The total result is simply given by:

\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{tot}}=\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{conv}}^{\mathrm{fin}}+\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{Born-conv}}+\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{3}^{\mathrm{fin}}+\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\gamma W}^{b,A}+\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{I,II,III+brem(\mathcal{D}_{3})}}+\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{brem(\mathcal{D}_{4-3})}}\penalty\ ,

(67)

where the numerical values of different components can be found in Tables

–

. On the other hand, in the existing standard
ChPT treatment the full electroweak RC is broken down into
“short-distance” and “long-distance” pieces, and are allocated to several different quantities,
some of which are somewhat implicitly hidden. This section serves to perform a rigorous matching between
our result and the values quoted in the existing ChPT literature, with special attention paid to
the so-called “long-distance electromagnetic corrections”
\delta_{\mathrm{EM}}^{Ke}
.

In the standard ChPT framework, the photon-inclusive
K_{e3}
decay rate is parameterized
as
Zyla:2020zbs
:

\Gamma_{K_{e3}}=\frac{G_{F}^{2}|V_{us}|^{2}M_{K}^{5}C_{K}^{2}}{192\pi^{3}}S_{\mathrm{EW}}|f_{+}^{K^{0}\pi^{-}}(0)|^{2}I_{Ke}^{(0)}(\lambda_{i})\left(1+\delta_{\mathrm{EM}}^{Ke}+\delta_{\mathrm{SU(2)}}^{K\pi}\right)\penalty\ ,

(68)

where
C_{K}
is a simple isospin factor. Apart from the quantity
|f_{+}^{K^{0}\pi^{-}}(0)|
that requires a
lattice input, all the small QCD and electroweak corrections to
\Gamma_{K_{e3}}
are distributed
into the following four quantities:
S_{\mathrm{EW}}
,
I_{Ke}^{(0)}(\lambda_{i})
,
\delta_{\mathrm{SU(2)}}^{K\pi}

and
\delta_{\mathrm{EM}}^{Ke}
. We shall take a serious look at each of these quantities, and study
their relations to the different components of electroweak RC we calculated in this work.

VIII.1
S_{\mathrm{EW}}

The quantity
S_{\mathrm{EW}}
was first introduced by Marciano and Sirlin in Ref.
Marciano:1993sh

as a process-independent factor that accounts for the large electroweak logarithm in the electroweak
RC
Sirlin:1977sv
;
Sirlin:1981ie
including the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
pQCD corrections on top
of it, as well as the resummation of the QED logs (i.e.
\delta_{\mathrm{HO}}^{\mathrm{QED}}
in our notation).
It was often quoted schematically in the literature as
Cirigliano:2008wn
;
Cirigliano:2011ny
:

S_{\mathrm{EW}}=1+\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)\ln\frac{M_{Z}}{M_{\rho}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\alpha\alpha_{s}}{\pi^{2}}\right)\penalty\ ,

(69)

where the
\rho
-mass appears as a low-energy scale. It is not straightforward
to infer its exact value from the expression above because some of the important components
(e.g.
\delta_{\mathrm{HO}}^{\mathrm{QED}}
) are not explicitly shown, and it is also not clear what scale one
should choose for
\alpha_{s}
. Fortunately, as a common consensus, the value

S_{\mathrm{EW}}=1.0232(3)_{\mathrm{HO}}
was always used for all practical purposes in the recent
years (see, e.g. Refs.
Cirigliano:2001mk
;
Cirigliano:2004pv
and the FLAVIAnet global analysis,
Ref.
Antonelli:2010yf
), where the central value comes from Ref.
Marciano:1993sh
and
the estimated uncertainty of the QED log resummation comes from Ref.
Erler:2002mv
. Notice
that although Ref.
Cirigliano:2011ny
quoted a slightly different value of

S_{\mathrm{EW}}=1.0223(5)
, but that number was never used in any subsequent analysis.

Now, the process-independent physics included in our
\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{tot}}
are not
only those described by
S_{\mathrm{EW}}
but even more. For example, the most important pQCD correction
contained in
\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\gamma W}^{b,A}
is calculated to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{4})

instead of just
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
in
S_{\mathrm{EW}}
. Therefore, it is not the most natural
choice to remove
S_{\mathrm{EW}}-1

analytically
from
\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{tot}}

in order to compare our result with the ChPT result. Instead, it is more convenient to take the
above-mentioned numerical value of
S_{\mathrm{EW}}
simply as its
definition
, i.e.,

S_{\mathrm{EW}}-1\equiv 0.0232(3)_{\mathrm{HO}}\penalty\ ,

(70)

and remove this value
numerically
from
\left(\delta_{K_{e3}}\right)_{\mathrm{tot}}
for
the comparison. This prescription keeps us on the same track with all the recent
literature mentioned above.

VIII.2
I_{Ke}^{(0)}(\lambda_{i})

The quantity
I_{Ke}^{(0)}(\lambda_{i})
is formally defined as the
“phase space integral depending on slope and curvature of the form factors
f_{\pm}^{K\pi}(t)
” according to Ref.
Cirigliano:2011ny

Refs.
Cirigliano:2001mk
;
Cirigliano:2004pv

t

at

\pi^{\pm}

(e.g.
\pi^{0}

by short-distance electromagnetic effects, or more specifically, the
\mathcal{O}(e^{2})
term in the
chiral Lagrangian
Knecht:1999ag

\mathcal{L}_{e^{2}}=Ze^{2}F_{0}^{4}\left\langle Q_{\mathrm{em}}UQ_{\mathrm{em}}U^{\dagger}\right\rangle\penalty\ ,

(71)

so the observation above implies that a part of the short-distance electromagnetic effect
proportional to
Z
is actually assigned implicitly to
I_{Ke}^{(0)}(\lambda_{i})
through
H_{PQ}(t)

within the ChPT framework. In our notation, this residual effect is represented exactly by the

\bar{h}_{PQ}(t)

\left(\delta f_{+,3}^{K\pi}(t)\right)_{e^{2}p^{2}}^{\mathrm{fin}}

\bar{h}_{PQ}(t)

to

\mathcal{O}(Z)

1.

Our calculation permits a much better control of the

are the main source of uncertainty in the ChPT treatment. With the new theory framework introduced

the non-Born/non-convection term contributions are in general an order of magnitude smaller

and
5

\mathcal{O}(e^{2}p^{4})

treatment one must multiply the full result by

to obtain the
\mathcal{O}(e^{2}p^{4})

uncertainty, so it is much larger.

2.

We used latest lattice QCD results to pin down
\delta M_{\gamma W}^{b,A}
, which corresponds to the
LECs
X_{1}
and
\bar{X}_{6}^{\mathrm{phys}}
in ChPT. In the existing literature, these LECs were calculated within
resonance models and were assigned a 100% uncertainty. On the other hand, the highly-precise lattice
results of
\Box_{\gamma W}^{VA}
would correspond exactly to
\delta M_{\gamma W}^{b,A}
if
K
and
\pi
were
degenerate. We investigated the region of integration in
\delta M_{\gamma W}^{b,A}
where this
non-degeneracy starts to take effect, and assigned a reasonable NF-uncertainty to the contribution
from this region on top of the lattice results. In the ChPT language, our treatment above simultaneously
take into account the uncertainties of the LECs themselves as well as the
\mathcal{O}(e^{2}p^{4})

uncertainties on top of the LEC contributions.

IX
Final discussions

The
3\sigma
discrepancy in the extraction of
V_{us}
from
K_{\mu 2}
and
K_{l3}
decays has triggered
renewed interest within the particle physics community about its possible implications on the
existence of BSM physics. However, the current level of significance is not sufficient to claim a
discovery so one needs further reduction of not just the experimental errors but also the SM theory
uncertainties. Our re-analysis of the SM electroweak RC in
K_{e3}
therefore, serves as a crucial
step along this direction. We successfully overcome the natural limitations in traditional ChPT
by adopting a new computational framework that allows for a resummation of the numerical largest
components in the RC, and also utilizing the most recent lattice QCD outcomes to reduce the
uncertainties from the non-perturbative QCD at the chiral symmetry breaking scale. Our work
reduces the existing uncertainties in the
K_{e3}
RC by almost an order of magnitude, and finds
no large shift in the central values. This suggests that we should remove the electroweak RC
from the “list of culprits” responsible for the
K_{\mu 2}
–
K_{l3}
discrepancy.

Is it evident now that the
V_{us}
anomaly cannot be explained by SM effects? We would say that
it is still too early to decide at this stage. Further investigations must also be made on other
SM inputs, just to mention a few:

•

Based on the analysis of a newly-constructed ratio
R_{V}=\Gamma_{K_{e3}}/\Gamma_{\pi_{e3}}
,

would
reconcile the
K_{\mu 2}
and
K_{l3}
results, and encouraged the lattice community to examine
this possibility. Lattice calculations of

Appendix A
Three- and four-body phase space in
K_{e3}

In this Appendix we derive the phase space formula for the
K\rightarrow\pi e^{+}\nu(\gamma)
process. We start from the following master formula: suppose
A(x,y,z)
is an arbitrary Lorentz-invariant function of the three dimensionless variables
\{x,y,z\}
defined in Eq.(
4
), then its integration with respect to
\vec{p}^{\prime}
and
\vec{p}_{e}
can be expressed as

\frac{1}{2M_{K}}\int\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E^{\prime}}\frac{d^{3}p_{e}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{e}}A(x,y,z)=\frac{M_{K}^{3}}{512\pi^{4}}\int_{2\sqrt{r_{\pi}}}^{\infty}dz\int_{2\sqrt{r_{e}}}^{\infty}dy\int_{\alpha_{-}(y,z)}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dxA(x,y,z)\penalty\ ,

(80)

where

\alpha_{\pm}(y,z)\equiv 1-y-z+r_{\pi}+r_{e}+\frac{yz}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{y^{2}-4r_{e}}\sqrt{z^{2}-4r_{\pi}}\penalty\ .

(81)

We can apply the master formula above to derive the expressions for the
K\rightarrow\pi e^{+}\nu(\gamma)
phase space. First, for
K(p)\rightarrow\pi(p^{\prime})e^{+}(p_{e})\nu(p_{\nu})
, we can identify:

\displaystyle A(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}p_{\nu}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\nu}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(P-p_{\nu})|M|^{2}_{K\rightarrow\pi e^{+}\nu}

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{M_{K}^{2}}\delta(x)|M|^{2}_{K\rightarrow\pi e^{+}\nu}\penalty\ .

When plugging the expression above into Eq.(
80
), the
x
-integral is non-zero only when
\alpha_{-}(y,z)<0<\alpha_{+}(y,z)
, which imposes constraints on the integration region of
\{y,z\}
. Solving these inequalities gives the well-known formula:

(83)

where the integration region
\mathcal{D}_{3}
can be represented in two equivalent ways, namely:

\displaystyle c(z)-d(z)<y<c(z)+d(z)\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{r_{\pi}}<z<1+r_{\pi}-r_{e}

\displaystyle c(z)=\frac{(2-z)(1+r_{e}+r_{\pi}-z)}{2(1+r_{\pi}-z)}\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle d(z)=\frac{\sqrt{z^{2}-4r_{\pi}}(1+r_{\pi}-r_{e}-z)}{2(1+r_{\pi}-z)}\penalty\ ,

(84)

or

\displaystyle a(y)-b(y)<z<a(y)+b(y)\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{r_{e}}<y<1+r_{e}-r_{\pi}

and the different step functions in front of each term impose different constraints on the integration region of

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{r_{e}}<y<c(z)-d(z)\penalty\ ,

Appendix B
Resonances at low energy

In this Appendix, we briefly review the basics of the resonance chiral theory that includes the
1^{--}
and
1^{++}
resonances as dynamical DOFs in the chiral Lagrangian
Ecker:1988te
;
Ecker:1989yg
;
Cirigliano:2006hb
. Based on this formalism we calculate the contribution of these resonance to
\delta M_{2}+\delta M_{\gamma W}^{a}
and
\delta M_{\gamma W}^{b,V}
.

In most of the literature on resonance chiral theory, the massive spin-1 particles are described by a totally-antisymmetric tensor field instead of a vector field
Gasser:1983yg
, so we start by introducing the formalism. First, the free Lagrangian of a (real) massive spin-1 particle is written as:

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{\lambda}R_{\lambda\mu}\right)\left(\partial_{\nu}R^{\nu\mu}\right)+\frac{1}{4}M_{R}^{2}R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}\penalty\ ,

(93)

where
R_{\mu\nu}
is the antisymmetric tensor field. It satisfies the following classical equation of motion:

\partial^{\mu}\left(\partial_{\lambda}R^{\lambda\nu}\right)-\partial^{\nu}\left(\partial_{\lambda}R^{\lambda\mu}\right)+M^{2}_{R}R^{\mu\nu}=0\penalty\ .

(94)

The quantized field takes the form:

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{R}(\vec{k})}\frac{i}{M_{R}}\left\{\left(k_{\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu}^{s}(\vec{k})-k_{\mu}\varepsilon_{\nu}^{s}(\vec{k})\right)\hat{a}_{s}(\vec{k})e^{-ik\cdot x}\right.

(95)

\displaystyle\left.-\left(k_{\nu}\varepsilon_{\mu}^{s*}(\vec{k})-k_{\mu}\varepsilon_{\nu}^{s*}(\vec{k})\right)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(\vec{k})e^{ik\cdot x}\right\}\penalty\ ,

where
\varepsilon_{s}(\vec{k})
is the polarization vector of the spin-1 particle that satisfies the following relations:

k\cdot\varepsilon_{s}(\vec{k})=0\penalty\ ,\>\>\>\sum_{s}\varepsilon_{\mu}^{s}(\vec{k})\varepsilon_{\nu}^{s*}(\vec{k})=-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{k_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{M^{2}_{R}}\penalty\ ,

(96)

and
\hat{a}^{+}_{s}(\vec{k}),\hat{a}_{s}(\vec{k})
are the creation and annihilation operators. Finally, by inverting the free Lagrangian one obtains the covariant propagator of the antisymmetric tensor field:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{R}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{p^{2}-M^{2}_{R}+i\varepsilon}\frac{1}{M^{2}_{R}}\left(g_{\mu\alpha}p_{\nu}p_{\beta}-g_{\mu\beta}p_{\nu}p_{\alpha}-g_{\nu\alpha}p_{\mu}p_{\beta}+g_{\nu\beta}p_{\mu}p_{\alpha}\right)

(97)

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{M^{2}_{R}}\left(g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}-g_{\nu\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}\right)\penalty\ .

We can now construct the chiral Lagrangian with dynamical vector and axial resonances. The
1^{++}
octet resonances are represented by
V_{\mu\nu}
which is a traceless, Hermitian matrix in the flavor space. Its chiral covariant derivative is given by:

\nabla_{\lambda}V_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\lambda}V_{\mu\nu}+[\Gamma_{\lambda},V_{\mu\nu}]\penalty\ ,

(98)

where

\Gamma_{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left\{u^{\dagger}\left[\partial_{\mu}-i(v_{\mu}+a_{\mu})\right]u+u\left[\partial_{\mu}-i(v_{\mu}-a_{\mu})\right]u^{\dagger}\right\}

(99)

is the standard connection vector, with
v_{\mu},a_{\mu}
the vector and axial external sources. Similarly, the
1^{--}
resonances are represented by the matrix
A_{\mu\nu}
. Other elementary building blocks of the ordinary ChPT. include the “vielbein”:

u_{\mu}\equiv i\left\{u^{\dagger}\left[\partial_{\mu}-i(v_{\mu}+a_{\mu})\right]u-u\left[\partial_{\mu}-i(v_{\mu}-a_{\mu})\right]u^{\dagger}\right\}\penalty\ ,

(100)

and the anti-symmetric tensors
f_{R,L}^{\mu\mu}
built from the vector and axial external sources:

f_{R,L}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\partial^{\mu}(v^{\mu}\pm a^{\nu})-\partial^{\nu}(v^{\mu}\pm a^{\mu})-i\left[v^{\mu}\pm a^{\mu},v^{\nu}\pm a^{\nu}\right]\penalty\ ,

(101)

and finally,
f_{\pm}^{\mu\nu}\equiv uf_{L}^{\mu\nu}u^{\dagger}\pm u^{\dagger}f_{R}^{\mu\nu}u
. With the above we can now write down the chiral Lagrangian with
1^{++}
and
1^{--}
resonances. The LO Lagrangian scales as
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{R}}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle(\nabla^{\lambda}V_{\lambda\mu})(\nabla_{\nu}V^{\nu\mu})-\frac{1}{2}M_{V}^{2}V_{\mu\nu}V^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle(\nabla^{\lambda}A_{\lambda\mu})(\nabla_{\nu}A^{\nu\mu})-\frac{1}{2}M_{A}^{2}A_{\mu\nu}A^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle

(102)

\displaystyle+\frac{F_{V}}{2\sqrt{2}}\left\langle V_{\mu\nu}f_{+}^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle+\frac{iG_{V}}{\sqrt{2}}\left\langle V_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\right\rangle+\frac{F_{A}}{2\sqrt{2}}\left\langle A_{\mu\nu}f_{-}^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle\penalty\ ,

where
\left\langle...\right\rangle
represents the trace over the flavor space,
M_{V}
and
M_{A}
are the vector and axial resonance masses in the chiral limit, while
F_{V}
,
F_{A}
and
G_{V}
are real coupling constants.

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{v}}{m_{1}m_{2}(1-x_{v}^{2})}\left\{\ln x_{v}\left[-\ln\left(\frac{M_{\gamma}^{2}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\ln x_{v}+2\ln(1-x_{v}^{2})\right]-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right.

(111)

\displaystyle\left.+\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x_{v}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\right)+\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(1-x_{v}\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\right)+\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(1-x_{v}\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{x_{v}}{m_{1}m_{2}(1-x_{v}^{2})}\ln x_{v}\ln\left(\frac{M_{\gamma}^{2}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\right)+C_{0}^{\mathrm{fin}}\penalty\ ,

with

x_{v}\equiv-\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{1}m_{2}}{v-(m_{1}-m_{2})^{2}}}}{1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{1}m_{2}}{v-(m_{1}-m_{2})^{2}}}}\penalty\ .

(112)

Appendix D
Dimensional regularization of the IR-divergent integral in the bremsstrahlung contribution

The only IR-divergent integral in bremsstrahlung process
K(p)\rightarrow\pi(p^{\prime})e^{+}(p_{e})\nu(p_{\nu})\gamma(k)
reads:

\displaystyle I_{i}(y,z)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dx\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{k}}\frac{d^{3}p_{\nu}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\nu}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(P-k-p_{\nu})\left(\frac{p_{e}}{p_{e}\cdot k}-\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}\cdot k}\right)^{2}

(113)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dx\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{k}}\delta(M_{K}^{2}x-2k\cdot P)\left(\frac{p_{e}}{p_{e}\cdot k}-\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}\cdot k}\right)^{2},

where
i=K
or
\pi
(obviously,
p_{K}=p
and
p_{\pi}=p^{\prime}
). Here, we can use the single delta function in the second line to integrate out
E_{k}
, so the IR-divergence comes from the final integration with respect to
x
, where the integrand behaves as
x^{-1}
at small
x
. A common prescription to regularize this IR-divergence is to introduce a non-zero photon mass such that
k^{2}=M_{\gamma}^{2}
. This sets a lower bound of
M_{\gamma}^{2}/M_{K}^{2}
for the
x
-integral that regularizes the IR-divergence, but also introduces a complicated
M_{\gamma}
-dependence in the integrand that needs to be carefully taken into account in order to correctly reproduce all the IR-finite terms in the
M_{\gamma}\rightarrow 0
limit.

A more elegant way to deal with the IR-divergence is to use dimensional-regularization
Gastmans:1973uv
;
Marciano:1974tv
. With this prescription, we first generalize the three-dimensional
k
-integral to
d-1
dimension:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{k}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mu^{4-d}\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}2E_{k}}

(114)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{4-d}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}E_{k}^{d-2}\sin^{d-3}\theta_{1}\sin^{d-4}\theta_{2}...\sin\theta_{d-3}dE_{k}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}...d\theta_{d-3}d\theta_{d-2}\penalty\ ,

where
0\leq\theta_{1},\theta_{2},...,\theta_{d-3}\leq\pi
and
0\leq\theta_{d-2}\leq 2\pi
.
The spatial components of
k
are parameterized as:

\displaystyle k_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{k}\cos\theta_{1}

\displaystyle k_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{k}\sin\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}

\displaystyle k_{d-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{k}\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}...\sin\theta_{d-3}\cos\theta_{d-2}

\displaystyle k_{d-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{k}\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}...\sin\theta_{d-3}\sin\theta_{d-2}\penalty\ .

(115)

With the prescription above, the IR-divergent integral over
x
can now be simply performed:

\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dxx^{d-5}=\frac{\left(\alpha_{+}(y,z)\right)^{d-4}}{d-4}\penalty\ ,

(116)

assuming
d>4
. Meanwhile, the angles can be integrated using the formula:

\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{m}\theta d\theta=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(m+1)\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(m+2)\right)}\penalty\ .

(117)

And finally, one expands the result to
\mathcal{O}\left((d-4)^{0}\right)
. It is also customary to switch the result back to the expression with the
M_{\gamma}
-regularization. For that purpose one simply performs the following matching:

\frac{2}{4-d}-\gamma_{E}+\ln 4\pi\rightarrow\ln\frac{M_{\gamma}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\penalty\ .

(118)

Next, we discuss some useful tricks in the evaluation of
I_{i}(y,z)
with dimensional regularization. First, the full integral can be split into three terms, with the integrand proportional to:

\frac{1}{(p_{e}\cdot k)^{2}}\penalty\ ,\>\>\frac{1}{(p_{i}\cdot k)^{2}}\penalty\ ,\>\>\frac{1}{(p_{e}\cdot k)(p_{i}\cdot k)}

(119)

respectively. The integration with respect to the first term is most easily done in the
\vec{p}_{e}
-rest frame, while the next two terms should be done in the
\vec{p}_{i}
-rest frame. The following identity is also useful in performing the integration of the third term:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dxx^{d-5}f(d,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dxx^{d-5}f(d,0)+\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dxx^{d-5}\left(f(d,x)-f(d,0)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\alpha_{+}(y,z)\right)^{d-4}}{d-4}+\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dx\frac{1}{x}\left(f(4,x)-f(4,0)\right)+\mathcal{O}(d-4)\penalty\ .

We are now ready to write down the full result of the integral:

I_{i}(y,z)=I_{i}^{\mathrm{IR}}(y,z)+I_{i}^{\mathrm{fin}}(y,z)\penalty\ ,

(121)

where

I_{i}^{\mathrm{IR}}(y,z)=\frac{1}{2\pi M_{K}^{2}}\left\{\left(1-\frac{1}{\beta_{i}(0)}\tanh^{-1}\beta_{i}(0)\right)\ln\left[\frac{M_{K}^{2}}{M_{\gamma}^{2}}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[\frac{M_{K}^{2}}{m_{e}^{2}}\right]\right\}

(122)

is the IR-divergent piece after switching back to the
M_{\gamma}
-prescription using Eq.(
118
), and

\displaystyle I_{i}^{\mathrm{fin}}(y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi M_{K}^{2}}\left\{\left(1-\frac{2}{\beta_{i}(0)}\tanh^{-1}\beta_{i}(0)\right)\ln\left[\frac{M_{K}^{2}\alpha_{+}^{2}}{4P_{0}^{2}(0)}\right]+\ln\left[\frac{\alpha_{+}^{2}(y,z)}{(1-z+r_{\pi}-r_{e})^{2}}\right]\right.

(123)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta_{i}(0)}\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left[\frac{2\beta_{i}(0)}{1+\beta_{i}(0)}\right]+\frac{1}{\beta_{i}(0)}\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left[-\frac{2\beta_{i}(0)}{1-\beta_{i}(0)}\right]

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2}{\beta_{i}(0)}\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left[\frac{\beta_{i}(0)}{1+\beta_{i}(0)}\left(\frac{P_{1}(0)}{P_{0}(0)}+1\right)\right]-\frac{2}{\beta_{i}(0)}\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left[\frac{\beta_{i}(0)}{1-\beta_{i}(0)}\left(\frac{P_{1}(0)}{P_{0}(0)}-1\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi M_{K}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\alpha_{+}(y,z)}dx\frac{1}{x}\left\{\frac{1}{\beta_{i}(x)}\ln\left[\frac{1+\beta_{i}(x)}{1-\beta_{i}(x)}\right]-\frac{1}{\beta_{i}(0)}\ln\left[\frac{1+\beta_{i}(0)}{1-\beta_{i}(0)}\right]\right\}

is the IR-finite piece, with

\beta_{i}(x)\equiv\sqrt{1-\frac{M_{i}^{2}m_{e}^{2}}{(p_{i}\cdot p_{e})^{2}}}\penalty\ ,\>\>P_{0}(x)\equiv\frac{p_{i}\cdot P}{M_{i}}\penalty\ ,\>\>P_{1}(x)\equiv\frac{1}{\beta_{i}(x)}\left(P_{0}(x)-\frac{p_{e}\cdot P}{p_{i}\cdot p_{e}}M_{i}\right)\penalty\ .

(124)

Of course all the quantities in Eq.(
124
) are functions of
\{y,z\}
as well. Their physical meanings are apparent:
\beta_{i}(x)
is the speed of the positron,
P_{0}(x)
is the zeroth component of
P^{\mu}
, and
P_{1}(x)
is the spatial component of
P^{\mu}
along the direction of
\vec{p}_{e}
, all in the
\vec{p}_{i}
-rest frame. Notice that the residual, IR-finite integral in the last line of Eq.(
123
) vanishes for
i=K
, because
\beta_{K}(x)=\beta_{K}(0)
.

The correct analytic expression for
I_{K}(y,z)
and
I_{\pi}(y,z)
first appeared in Ref.
Ginsberg:1969jh
and Ref.
Cirigliano:2004pv
respectively (notice that Ref.
Ginsberg:1968pz
also attempted to calculate
I_{\pi}(y,z)
, but the result there is wrong even with the Errata). It is easy to check the numerical equivalence between Eq.(
121
) and those expressions, after accounting for the difference in the overall normalization.

In this Appendix, we outline the general strategy to evaluate the IR-finite numerical integrations from the bremsstrahlung process, in both the
\mathcal{D}_{3}
and
\mathcal{D}_{4-3}
region. We start by providing the expressions of the relevant integrands. In
K_{e3}^{0}
we have:

\displaystyle|M_{A}|_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathfrak{Re}\left\{M_{A}M_{B}^{*}\right\}

\displaystyle-2e^{2}G_{F}^{2}V_{us}F_{\mu}\left(\frac{p_{e}}{p_{e}\cdot k}-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}\cdot k}\right)_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\times\mathfrak{Re}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\not{P}-\not{k})\gamma^{\mu}(\not{p}_{e}-m_{e})\left\{\frac{p^{\prime\alpha}}{p^{\prime}\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma^{\alpha}\right\}(1-\gamma_{5})\right]

\displaystyle-e^{2}G_{F}^{2}V_{us}F_{\mu}\frac{1}{p_{e}\cdot k}\mathfrak{Re}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\not{P}-\not{k})\gamma^{\mu}\not{k}\gamma^{\alpha}(\not{p}_{e}-m_{e})\left\{\frac{p^{\prime}_{\alpha}}{p^{\prime}\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma_{\alpha}\right\}(1-\gamma_{5})\right]

\displaystyle|M_{B}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{2}G_{F}^{2}|V_{us}|^{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\not{P}-\not{k})\left\{\frac{p^{\prime\mu}}{p^{\prime}\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma^{\mu}\right\}(\not{p}_{e}-m_{e})\left\{\frac{p^{\prime}_{\mu}}{p^{\prime}\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma_{\mu}\right\}(1-\gamma_{5})\right]\penalty\ ,

and similarly for
K_{e3}^{+}
,

\displaystyle|M_{A}|_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{2}\left(\frac{p_{e}}{p_{e}\cdot k}-\frac{p}{p\cdot k}\right)^{2}\left\{|M_{0}|^{2}(x,y,z)-|M_{0}|^{2}(0,y,z)\right\}

\displaystyle+e^{2}G_{F}^{2}F_{\mu}F_{\nu}^{*}\left(\frac{p_{e}}{p_{e}\cdot k}-\frac{p}{p\cdot k}\right)^{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\not{k}\gamma^{\mu}(\not{p}_{e}-m_{e})\gamma^{\nu}(1-\gamma_{5})\right]

\displaystyle 2\mathfrak{Re}\left\{M_{A}M_{B}^{*}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}e^{2}G_{F}^{2}V_{us}F_{\mu}\left(\frac{p_{e}}{p_{e}\cdot k}-\frac{p}{p\cdot k}\right)_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\times\mathfrak{Re}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\not{P}-\not{k})\gamma^{\mu}(\not{p}_{e}-m_{e})\left\{\frac{p^{\alpha}}{p\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma^{\alpha}\right\}(1-\gamma_{5})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{2}G_{F}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}F_{\mu}\frac{1}{p_{e}\cdot k}\mathfrak{Re}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\not{P}-\not{k})\gamma^{\mu}\not{k}\gamma^{\alpha}(\not{p}_{e}-m_{e})\left\{\frac{p_{\alpha}}{p\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma_{\alpha}\right\}(1-\gamma_{5})\right]

\displaystyle|M_{B}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{2}G_{F}^{2}}{2}|V_{us}|^{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\not{P}-\not{k})\left\{\frac{p^{\mu}}{p\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma^{\mu}\right\}(\not{p}_{e}-m_{e})\left\{\frac{p_{\mu}}{p\cdot k}\not{k}-\gamma_{\mu}\right\}(1-\gamma_{5})\right]\penalty\ .

In the above, we have used
F_{\mu}
as a shorthand of
F_{\mu}^{K\pi}(p^{\prime},p)
. We do not display the explicit results after taking the spinor trace, as the latter can be done with, e.g., various packages in
Mathematica
such as
Tracer
or
Package-X
. After taking the trace, all the expressions above are functions of
\{x,y,z\}
as well as two of the three following dot products involving
k
:
\{k\cdot p,k\cdot p^{\prime},k\cdot p_{e}\}
using the identity
2k\cdot(p-p^{\prime}-p_{e})=M_{K}^{2}x
.

The integration can be performed with the following strategy. Take
|M_{A}|^{2}_{\mathrm{res}}
in
K_{e3}^{0}
as an example: we first express the squared amplitude as a finite sum:

|M_{A}|_{\mathrm{res}}^{2}=\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}(x,y,z)\frac{1}{(k\cdot p^{\prime})^{m}(k\cdot p_{e})^{n}}\penalty\ ,

(127)

where
-2\leq m,n\leq 2
and
c_{m,n}(x,y,z)
are known scalar coefficients. The
p_{\nu}
and
k
-integrations return the following functions:

I_{m,n}(p_{1},p_{2})\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{E_{k}}\frac{d^{3}p_{\nu}}{E_{\nu}}\frac{\delta^{(4)}(P-k-p_{\nu})}{(p_{1}\cdot k)^{m}(p_{2}\cdot k)^{n}}\penalty\ ,

(128)

of which analytic expressions are given in the Appendix of Ref.
Ginsberg:1969jh
(we have checked their correctness). With this, we obtain:

\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{k}}\frac{d^{3}p_{\nu}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\nu}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(P-k-p_{\nu})|M_{A}|^{2}_{\mathrm{res}}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}(x,y,z)I_{m,n}(p^{\prime},p_{e})\penalty\ ,

(129)

where the right-hand side is now a function of
\{x,y,z\}
, so the remaining three-fold integration with respect to these variables are completely regular and can be performed numerically. The same strategy applies to the IR-finite integrals in
K_{e3}^{+}
, except that one should choose
1/\left\{(k\cdot p)^{m}(k\cdot p_{e})^{n}\right\}
as the basis.

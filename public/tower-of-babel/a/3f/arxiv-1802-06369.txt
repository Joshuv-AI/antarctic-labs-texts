Linear-Time Algorithm for Long LCF with k k Mismatches
Panagiotis Charalampopoulos, Maxime Crochemore, Costas S. Iliopoulos, Tomasz Kociumaka, Solon P. Pissis, Jakub Radoszewski, Wojciech Rytter, Tomasz Waleń

Abstract
In the Longest Common Factor with k k Mismatches ( LCF k ) problem, we are given two strings X X and Y Y of total length n n , and we are asked to find a pair of maximal-length factors, one of X X and the other of Y Y , such that their Hamming distance is at most k k . Thankachan et al. [ 26 ] show that this problem can be solved in 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ​ log k ​ n ) \mathcal{O}(n\log^{k}n) time and 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{O}(n) space for constant k k . We consider the LCF k ( ℓ \ell ) problem in which we assume that the sought factors have length at least ℓ \ell , and the LCF k ( ℓ \ell ) problem for ℓ = Ω ⁡ ( log 2 ​ k + 2 ⁡ n ) \ell=\Omega(\log^{2k+2}n) , which we call the Long LCF k problem. We use difference covers to reduce the Long LCF k problem to a task involving m = 𝒪 ⁡ ( n / log k + 1 ⁡ n ) m=\mathcal{O}(n/\log^{k+1}n) synchronized factors. The latter can be solved in 𝒪 ⁡ ( m ​ log k + 1 ​ m ) \mathcal{O}(m\log^{k+1}m) time, which results in a linear-time algorithm for Long LCF k . In general, our solution to LCF k ( ℓ \ell ) for arbitrary ℓ \ell takes 𝒪 ⁡ ( n + n ​ log k + 1 ​ n / ℓ ) \mathcal{O}(n+n\log^{k+1}n/\sqrt{\ell}) time.

Introduction

The longest common factor (LCF) problem is a classical and well-studied problem in theoretical computer science.
It consists in finding a maximal-length factor of a string
X
occurring in another string
Y
.
When
X
and
Y
are over a linearly-sortable alphabet, the LCF problem can be solved in the optimal
\mathcal{O}(n)
time and space
[
16
,
14
]
, where
n
is the total length of
X
and
Y
.
Considerable efforts have thus been made on improving the
additional
working space; namely, the space required for computations, not taking into account the space providing read-only access to
X
and
Y
.
We refer the interested reader to
[
24
,
20
]
.

In many bioinformatics applications and elsewhere, it is relevant to consider potential alterations within the pair of input strings (e.g. DNA sequences). It is thus natural to define the LCF problem under a distance metric model.
The problem then consists in finding a pair of maximal-length factors of
X
and
Y
whose distance is at most
k
. In fact, this problem has received much attention recently, in particular due to its applications in alignment-free sequence comparison
[
28
,
21
]
.

Under the Hamming distance model, the problem is known as the
Longest Common Factor with at most
k
Mismatches
(
LCF
k
) problem.
The restricted case of
k=1
was first considered in
[
4
]
, where an
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
-time and
\mathcal{O}(n)
-space solution was given. It was later improved by Flouri et al.
[
11
]
, who built heavily on a technique by Crochemore et al.
[
10
]
to obtain
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
time and
\mathcal{O}(n)
space.

For a general value of
k
, the problem can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time and space by a dynamic programming algorithm, but more efficient solutions have been devised.
Leimeister and Morgenstern
[
21
]
first suggested a greedy heuristic algorithm. Flouri et al.
[
11
]
proposed an
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
-time algorithm that uses
\mathcal{O}(1)
additional space.
Grabowski
[
12
]
presented two algorithms with running times
\mathcal{O}(n((k+1)(\ell_{0}+1))^{k})
and
\mathcal{O}(n^{2}k/\ell_{k})
, where
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{k}
are, respectively, the length of an LCF of
X
and
Y
and the length of an
LCF
k
of
X
and
Y
.
Thankachan et al.
[
26
]
proposed an
\mathcal{O}(n\log^{k}n)
-time and
\mathcal{O}(n)
-space algorithm (for any constant
k
).

Abboud et al.
[
1
]
employed the polynomial method to obtain a
k^{1.5}n^{2}/2^{\Omega(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{k}})}
-time randomized algorithm.
Kociumaka et al.
[
19
]
showed that a strongly subquadratic-time algorithm for the
LCF
k
problem, for binary strings and
k=\Omega(\log n)
, refutes the Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis
[
18
,
17
]
. Thus, subquadratic-time solutions for approximate variants of the problem have been developed
[
19
,
23
]
. The average-case complexity of this problem has also been considered
[
27
,
2
,
3
]
.

1.1
Our Contribution

We consider the following variant of the
Longest Common Factor with at most
k
Mismatches
problem in which the result is constrained to have at least a given length. Let
LCF
k
(X,Y)
denote the length of the longest common factor of
X
and
Y
with at most
k
mismatches.

LCF of Length at Least
\ell

k

(
LCF
k
(X,Y,\ell)
)

Input:
Two strings
X
and

of total length
n

k\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1

Output:

LCF
k
(X,Y)
if it is at least
\ell

We focus on a special case of this problem with

which we call
Long

LCF

Apart from its theoretical interest, solutions to the
LCF
k
(X,Y,\ell)
problem, and
Long

LCF

LCF
k
length has been used as a measure of sequence similarity
[
28
,
21
]
. It is thus assumed that similar sequences share relatively long factors with

mismatches.

We show an
\mathcal{O}(n)
-time algorithm for the
Long

LCF
k
problem.
Moreover, we prove that
LCF

(X,Y,\ell)
can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(n+n\log^{k+1}n/\sqrt{\ell})
time for arbitrary
\ell

and constant

.
In the final section we discuss the complexity for
k=\mathcal{O}(\log n)
.
This unveils that the
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
notation hides a multiplicative factor that is actually subconstant in
k
.

For simplicity, we only describe how to compute the length
\mathsf{LCF}_{k}(X,Y)
.
It is straightforward to amend our solution so that it extracts the corresponding factors of
X
and
Y
.

Toolbox.

We use the following algorithmic tools:

•

Difference covers (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
7
]
) let us reduce the
LCF
k
(X,Y,\ell)
problem to searching for longest common prefixes and suffixes with at most
k
errors (
\mathsf{LCP}_{k}
,
\mathsf{LCS}_{k}
) at positions belonging to sets
A
in
X
and
B
in
Y
such that
|A|,|B|=\mathcal{O}(n/\sqrt{\ell})
.

•

We use a technique of recursive heavy-path decompositions by Cole et al.
[
8
]
, already used in the context of the
LCF
k
problem by Thankachan et al.
[
26
]
, to reduce computing
\mathsf{LCP}_{k}
,
\mathsf{LCS}_{k}
to computing
\mathsf{LCP}
,
\mathsf{LCS}
in sets of modified prefixes and suffixes starting at positions in
A
and
B

k

\mathcal{O}(\log^{k}n)
. We adjust the original technique of Cole et al.
[
8
]
so that all modified strings are stored in one compacted trie. Details are given in the appendix.

•

Finally we apply to the compacted trie a solution to a problem on colored trees that is the cornerstone of the previous
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
-time solution for
LCF
1
problem by Flouri et al.
[
11
]
(and originates from efficient merging of AVL trees
[
6
]
).

In total we arrive at
\mathcal{O}(n\log^{k+1}n/\sqrt{\ell}+n)
complexity.

In our solution we actually use the following problem related to families of strings represented on a compacted trie. It reduces to the
Colored Trees Problem
.

Two String Families LCP Problem

Input:

A compacted trie
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})
of a family of strings
\mathcal{F}

and two sets
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{2}

Output:
The value
\mathrm{maxPairLCP}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})
, defined as

\mathrm{maxPairLCP}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\!=\!\max\{\mathsf{LCP}(P_{1},Q_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}(P_{2},Q_{2}):(P_{1},P_{2})\in\mathcal{P}\text{ and }(Q_{1},Q_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}\}

Lemma 1
.

The
Two String Families LCP Problem
can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{F}|+N\log N)
time, where
N=|\mathcal{P}|+|\mathcal{Q}|
.

Proof.

First, we create two copies
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
of the tree
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})
,
removing the edge labels but preserving the node weights
w(v)
equal to the sum of lengths of edges on the path to the root.

Next, for each
(P_{1},P_{2})\in\mathcal{P}
we attach a blue leaf to the terminal node of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
representing
P_{1}
and to the terminal of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
representing
P_{2}
.
We label these two blue leaves with a unique label, denoted here
L_{\mathcal{P}}(P_{1},P_{2})
.
Similarly, for each
(Q_{1},Q_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}
, we attach red leaves to the terminal node of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
representing
Q_{1}

and the terminal node of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
representing
Q_{2}
.
We label these two red leaves with a unique label
L_{\mathcal{Q}}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
.
Finally, in both
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
we remove all nodes which do not contain any colored leaf in their subtrees
and dissolve all nodes with exactly one child
(except for the roots).
This way, each tree
\mathcal{T}_{i}
contains
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{P}|+|\mathcal{Q}|)
nodes, including
|\mathcal{P}|+|\mathcal{Q}|
leaves, each with a distinct label.

Observe that for
(P_{1},P_{2})\in\mathcal{P}
,
(Q_{1},Q_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}
, and
j\in\{1,2\}
,
the value
\mathsf{LCP}(P_{j},Q_{j})
is the weight of the lowest common ancestor in
\mathcal{T}_{j}

of the two leaves with labels
L_{\mathcal{P}}(P_{1},P_{2})
and
L_{\mathcal{Q}}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
.
Consequently, our task can be formulated as follows:
Find a pair of internal nodes
v_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
v_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}

of maximal total weight
w(v_{1})+w(v_{2})
so that the subtrees rooted
at
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
contain blue leaves with the same label and red leaves with the same label.
This is exactly the
Colored Trees Problem
that can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(m\log m)
time, where
m=|\mathcal{T}_{1}|+|\mathcal{T}_{2}|=\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{P}|+|\mathcal{Q}|)
(Fact
2
).
∎

4
The case of

In this section, as a warmup, we show how the
Two String Families LCP Problem
can be used to solve two special cases of
LCF
k
(X,Y,\ell)
.
Then in

In order to solve
LCF
k
(
X,Y,\ell
) for
k=0
,
we observe that, by Lemma
2
, if

\mathsf{LCF}_{0}(X,Y)\geq\ell
,
then
\mathsf{LCF}_{0}(X,Y)=\mathrm{maxPairLCP}(\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X),\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y))
.
Thus, we simply build the joint suffix tree
\mathcal{T}
of
X
,
Y
,
X^{R}
, and
Y^{R}
,
and we solve the appropriate instance of
Two String Families LCP Problem
.

The preprocessing time is clearly
\mathcal{O}(n)
,
while solving the
Two String Families LCP Problem
takes
\mathcal{O}(n+n\log n/\sqrt{\ell})
time, which is
\mathcal{O}(n)

provided that
\ell=\Omega(\log^{2}n)
.

For
k\geq 1
, we would ideally like to extend the family
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(S)
to
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}^{(k)}(S)

replacing the suffixes and reversed prefixes of
S
with their approximate copies
so that

\mathsf{LCF}_{k}(X,Y)=\mathrm{maxPairLCP}(\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}^{(k)}(X),\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}^{(k)}(Y)).

A very naive solution would be to extend the alphabet
\Sigma
to
\Sigma_{\$}
adding a symbol
\$\notin\Sigma
,
and for each
(S_{1},S_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(S)
to replace an arbitrary subset of
k
symbols with
\$
’s.
However, this results in
\binom{n}{k}
copies of each
(S_{1},S_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(S)
, which is by far too much.

Our approach is therefore based on the technique of Cole et al.
[
8
]
,
which has already been used in the context of the
Longest Common Factor with at most
k
Mismatches
problem by Thankachan et al.
[
26
]
.
It allows us to reduce the number of approximate copies of each
(S_{1},S_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(S)
to
\mathcal{O}(\log^{k}n)
.
However, the sets
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}^{(k)}(X)
and
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}^{(k)}(Y)
cannot be constructed independently,
and we actually have to build several pairs of such sets rather just one.

Below, we explain the main points for

Let

X
,
X^{R}
,
Y
, and
Y^{R}
, appearing
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)
or
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y)
.
We apply the heavy-light decomposition on the compacted trie
\mathcal{T}(F)
; this technique can be summarized as follows:

Fact 3

(Tarjan
[
25
]
)
.

If
T
is a rooted tree, then in linear time we can mark some edges in
T
as
light
so that:

•

each node has at most one outgoing edge which is
not
light,

•

each root-to-leaf path contains
\mathcal{O}(\log|T|)
light edges.

Next, for each string
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we construct a set
N(F)
consisting of
F
and any
string which can be obtained from
F
by flipping the first symbol of a single light edge
on the path representing
F
in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})
. By
3
, we have
|N(F)|=\mathcal{O}(\log|\mathcal{F}|)=\mathcal{O}(\log n)
.

Let us denote
N_{0}(F)=\{F\}
and
N_{1}(F)=N(F)
.
These sets have been constructed so that they enjoy the following crucial property:

Lemma 3
.

If
F,G\in\mathcal{F}
, then

\mathsf{LCP}_{1}(F,G)=\max_{d_{1}+d_{2}=1}\{\mathsf{LCP}(F^{\prime},G^{\prime}):F^{\prime}\in N_{d_{1}}(F),G^{\prime}\in N_{d_{2}}(G)\}.

Proof.

First, let us bound
\mathsf{LCP}_{1}(F,G)
from above.
Let
p=\mathsf{LCP}(F^{\prime},G^{\prime})
be the maximum on the right-hand side,
We have

d_{H}(F[\mathinner{.\,.}p],G[\mathinner{.\,.}p])=d_{H}(F[\mathinner{.\,.}p],F^{\prime}[\mathinner{.\,.}p])+d_{H}(G^{\prime}[\mathinner{.\,.}p],G[\mathinner{.\,.}p])\leq\\
\leq d_{H}(F,F^{\prime})+d_{H}(G^{\prime},G)\leq d_{1}+d_{2}=1.

Consequently,
\mathsf{LCP}_{1}(F,G)\geq p
as claimed.

To bound
\mathsf{LCP}_{1}(F,G)
from above, let us consider
terminal nodes
v_{F}
and
v_{G}
in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})
representing
F
and
G
, respectively,
and their lowest common ancestor
v
.
If
v=v_{F}
or
v=v_{G}
, then
\mathsf{LCP}_{1}(F,G)=\mathsf{LCP}(F,G)
and the claimed bound holds due to
F\in N_{0}(F)
and
G\in N_{1}(G)
(and vice versa).
Otherwise, the edge from
v
towards
v_{F}
or the edge from
v
towards
v_{G}
has to be light (according to
3
).
If the former edge is light, then
N_{1}(F)
contains a string
F^{\prime}
obtained from
F

by flipping the first character on that edge. Such a string
F^{\prime}
satisfies

\mathsf{LCP}_{1}(F,G)=\mathsf{LCP}(F^{\prime},G)
, so the claimed bound holds due to
G\in N_{0}(G)
.
Symmetrically, if the edge towards
v_{G}
is light, then

\mathsf{LCP}_{1}(F,G)=\mathsf{LCP}(F,G^{\prime})
for some
G^{\prime}\in N_{1}(G)
.
∎

For
S\in\{X,Y\}
and
d\in\{0,1\}
, let us define

\mathsf{Pairs}^{(d)}_{\ell}(S)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(U_{1},U_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)\\
d_{1}+d_{2}=1\end{subarray}}\{(U^{\prime}_{1},U^{\prime}_{2}):U^{\prime}_{1}\in N_{d_{1}}(U_{1}),U^{\prime}_{2}\in N_{d_{2}}(U_{2})\}.

Observe that
\mathsf{Pairs}^{(0)}_{\ell}(S)=\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(S)
, whereas the set
\mathsf{Pairs}^{(1)}_{\ell}(S)
satisfies
|\mathsf{Pairs}^{(1)}_{\ell}(S)|=\mathcal{O}(|\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(S)|\log|\mathcal{F}|)=\mathcal{O}(n\log n/\sqrt{\ell})
.

Lemmas

2
and
3
yield the following

Corollary 1
.

If
\mathsf{LCF}_{1}(X,Y)\geq\ell
, then

\mathsf{LCF}_{1}(X,Y)=\max_{k_{1}+k_{2}=1}\mathrm{maxPairLCP}(\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k_{1})}_{\ell}(X),\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k_{2})}_{\ell}(Y)).

Proof.

By
Lemma

2
, we have
\mathsf{LCF}_{1}(X,Y)=\mathsf{LCP}_{p}(U_{1},V_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}_{q}(U_{2},V_{2})
for some
(U_{1},U_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)
,

(V_{1},V_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y)
, and
p+q=1
.

Lemma

3
yields that
\mathsf{LCP}_{p}(U_{1},V_{1})=\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{1})
for some
U^{\prime}_{1}\in N_{p_{1}}(U_{1})
and
V^{\prime}_{1}\in N_{p_{2}}(V_{1})

such that
p=p_{1}+p_{2}
. Similarly,
\mathsf{LCP}_{q}(U_{2},V_{2})=\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{2})
for some
U^{\prime}_{2}\in N_{q_{1}}(U_{2})
and
V^{\prime}_{2}\in N_{q_{2}}(V_{2})
.
Observe that
(U^{\prime}_{1},U^{\prime}_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}^{(p_{1}+q_{1})}_{\ell}(X)
and
(V^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}^{(p_{2}+q_{2})}_{\ell}(Y)
,
so

\mathsf{LCF}_{1}(X,Y)\leq\mathrm{maxPairLCP}(\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k_{1})}_{\ell}(X),\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k_{2})}_{\ell}(Y))

for
k_{i}=p_{i}+q_{1}

(which satisfy
k_{1}+k_{2}=p+q=1
, as claimed).

Next, suppose that
(U^{\prime}_{1},U^{\prime}_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k_{1})}_{\ell}(X)
and
(V^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k_{2})}_{\ell}(Y)
.
We shall prove that
\mathsf{LCF}_{1}(X,Y)\geq\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{2})
.
Note that
U^{\prime}_{1}\in N_{p_{1}}
and
U^{\prime}_{2}\in N_{q_{1}}(U_{2})
for some
p_{1}+q_{1}=k_{1}
and
(U_{1},U_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)
;
symmetrically,
V^{\prime}_{1}\in N_{p_{2}}
and
V^{\prime}_{2}\in N_{q_{2}}(V_{2})
for some
p_{2}+q_{2}=k_{2}
and
(V_{1},V_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y)
.
By
Lemma

3
,
\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{1})\leq\mathsf{LCP}_{p_{1}+p_{2}}(U_{1},V_{1})
and
\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{2})\leq\mathsf{LCP}_{q_{1}+q_{2}}(U_{2},V_{2})
.
Hence, the claimed bound holds due to
Lemma

2
:

\mathsf{LCF}_{1}(X,Y)\geq\mathsf{LCP}_{p_{1}+p_{2}}(U_{1},V_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}_{q_{1}+q_{2}}(U_{2},V_{2})\geq\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{2}).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Consequently, it suffices to solve two instances of
Two String Families LCP Problem
,
with
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})
equal to
(\mathsf{Pairs}^{(0)}_{\ell}(X),\mathsf{Pairs}^{(1)}_{\ell}(Y))
and
(\mathsf{Pairs}^{(1)}_{\ell}(X),\mathsf{Pairs}^{(0)}_{\ell}(Y))
, respectively.

Proposition 2
.

The problem
LCF
k
(X,Y,\ell)
for
k=1
and binary alphabet can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(n+n\log^{2}n/\sqrt{\ell})

time. If
\ell=\Omega(\log^{4}n)
, this running time is
\mathcal{O}(n)
.

Proof.

First, we build the sets
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)
and
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y)
.
Next, we construct the joint suffix tree of strings
X
,
Y
,
X^{\prime}
,
Y^{\prime}
(along with a component for constant-time LCA queries)
and we extract the compacted trie
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})
of the family
\mathcal{F}
.
Then, we process light edges on
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F})
(determined by
3
).
For each light edge
e
, we traverse the corresponding subtree and for each terminal node (representing
F\in\mathcal{F}
),
we insert to
N(F)
a string
F^{\prime}
obtained from
F
by flipping the first character represented by
e
.
Technically, in
N(F)
we just store the set of positions for which
F
should be flipped to obtain
F^{\prime}
.

To compute the compacted trie
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
of a family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}N(F)
,
we sort the strings in
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
using a comparison-based algorithm.
Next, we extend the representation of
N(F)
so that each
F^{\prime}\in N(F)
stores a pointer to the corresponding terminal node in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.
This way, we can generate sets
\mathsf{Pairs}^{d}_{\ell}(S)
for
d\in\{0,1\}
and
S\in\{X,Y\}

with strings represented as pointers to terminal nodes of
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.
Finally, we solve two instances of
Two String Families LCP Problem
according to
Corollary

1
.

We conclude with the running-time analyis.
In the preprocessing, we spend
\mathcal{O}(n)
time construct the joint suffix tree.
Then, applying
3
to build the sets
N(F)
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
takes
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{F}|\log|\mathcal{F}|)=\mathcal{O}(n\log n/\sqrt{\ell})

time. We spend further
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\log|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|)=\mathcal{O}(n\log^{2}n/\sqrt{\ell})
time to construct
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.
Since
|\mathsf{Pairs}^{(d)}_{\ell}(S)|=\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{F}|\log|\mathcal{F}|)
for
d\in\{0,1\}
and
S\in\{X,Y\}
,
the time to solve both instances of the
Two String Families LCP Problem
is also
\mathcal{O}(n\log^{2}n/\sqrt{\ell})
(see Lemma
1
).
Hence, the overall time complexity is
\mathcal{O}(n+n\log^{2}n/\sqrt{\ell})
.
∎

5
Arbitary
k
and
\sigma

In this section, we describe the core concepts of our solution for arbitrary number of mismatches
k

and alphabet size
\sigma
. They depend heavily on the ideas behind the
\mathcal{O}(n\log^{k}n)
-time solution to
LCF
k

[
26
]
, which originate in approximate indexing
[
8
]
.

Definition 1
.

Consider strings
U,V\in\Sigma^{*}
and an integer
d\geq 0
.
We say that strings
U^{\prime},V^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\$}^{*}
form a
(U,V)_{d}
-pair if

•

|U^{\prime}|=|U|
and
|V^{\prime}|=|V|
;

•

if
i>\mathsf{LCP}_{d}(U,V)
or
U[i]=V[i]
, then
U^{\prime}[i]=U[i]
and
V^{\prime}[i]=V[i]
;

•

otherwise,
U^{\prime}[i]=V^{\prime}[i]\in\{U[i],V[i],\$\}
.

Definition 2
.

Consider a finite family of strings
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
.
We say that sets
N(F)\subseteq\Sigma_{\$}^{*}
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
form a
k
-complete family

if for every
U,V\in\mathcal{F}
and
0\leq d\leq k
, there exists a
(U,V)_{d}
-pair
U^{\prime},V^{\prime}
with
U^{\prime}\in N(U)
and
V^{\prime}\in N(V)
.

Remark 3
.

A simple (yet inefficient) way to construct a
k
-complete family is to include
in
N(F)
all strings which can be obtained from
F
by replacing up to
k
characters with
\$
’s. An example of a more efficient family is shown in Table
1
in the appendix.

The following lemma states a property of
k
-complete families that we will use in the algorithm.
For
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
0\leq d\leq k
, let us define
N_{d}(F)=\{F^{\prime}\in N(F):d_{H}(F,F^{\prime})\leq d\}
.
Moreover, for a half-integer
d^{\prime}
,
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
, let

N_{d,d^{\prime}}(F)=\{F^{\prime}\in N_{d}(F):d_{H}(F,F^{\prime})-\tfrac{1}{2}\#_{\$}(F^{\prime})\leq d^{\prime}\}.

Lemma 4
.

Let
N(F)
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
be a
k
-complete family.
If
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
and
0\leq d\leq k
, then

\mathsf{LCP}_{d}(F_{1},F_{2})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}+d_{2}=d\\
F^{\prime}_{i}\in N_{d,d_{i}}(F_{i})\end{subarray}}\mathsf{LCP}(F^{\prime}_{1},F^{\prime}_{2})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lfloor d_{1}+d_{2}\rfloor\leq d\\
F^{\prime}_{i}\in N_{k,d_{i}}(F_{i})\end{subarray}}\mathsf{LCP}(F^{\prime}_{1},F^{\prime}_{2}).

Proof.

We shall prove that

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}+d_{2}=d\\
F^{\prime}_{i}\in N_{d,d_{i}}(F_{i})\end{subarray}}\mathsf{LCP}(F^{\prime}_{1},F^{\prime}_{2})\geq\mathsf{LCP}_{d}(F_{1},F_{2})\geq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lfloor d_{1}+d_{2}\rfloor\leq d\\
F^{\prime}_{i}\in N_{k,d_{i}}(F_{i})\end{subarray}}(F^{\prime}_{1},F^{\prime}_{2}).

This is sufficient due to the fact that
N_{d,d^{\prime}}(F)
is monotone with respect to both
d
and
d^{\prime}
.

For the first inequality, observe that (by definition of a
k
-complete family)
the sets
N(F_{1})
and
N(F_{2})
contain an
(F_{1},F_{2})_{d}
-pair
(F^{\prime}_{1},F^{\prime}_{2})
.
Let
P
be the longest common prefix of
F^{\prime}_{1}
and
F^{\prime}_{2}
(
|P|=\mathsf{LCP}_{k}(F_{1},F_{2})
)
and recall that by definition
F^{\prime}_{i}=PF_{i}[|P|+1\mathinner{.\,.}]
.
Moreover, let
d_{i}=d_{H}(F_{i},F^{\prime}_{i})-\frac{1}{2}\#_{\$}(F^{\prime}_{i})
so that
F^{\prime}_{i}\in N_{d,d_{i}}(F_{i})
.
Consequently,

d\geq d_{H}(F_{1}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|],F_{2}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|])=d_{H}(F_{1},F^{\prime}_{1})+d_{H}(F_{2},F^{\prime}_{2})-\#_{\$}(P)=d_{1}+d_{2}.

If
d>d_{1}+d_{2}
, we may increase
d_{1}
or
d_{2}
.

For the second inequality, suppose that
F^{\prime}_{i}\in N_{k,d_{i}}(F_{i})
for
\lfloor d_{1}+d_{2}\rfloor\leq d
.
Let
P
be the longest common prefix of
F^{\prime}_{1}
and
F^{\prime}_{2}
.
Then

d_{H}(F_{1}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|],F_{2}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|])\,\leq\,d_{H}(F_{1}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|],P)+d_{H}(F_{2}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|],P])-\#_{\$}(P)\leq\\
\leq\,d_{H}(F_{1},F_{1}^{\prime})-\#_{\$}(F_{1})+d_{H}(F_{2},F_{2}^{\prime})-\#_{\$}(F_{2})+\#_{\$}(P)\leq d_{1}+d_{2}.

Consequently,
d_{H}(F_{1}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|],F_{2}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|])=\lfloor d_{H}(F_{1}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|],F_{2}[\mathinner{.\,.}|P|])\rfloor\leq\lfloor d_{1}+d_{2}\rfloor\leq d
,
as claimed.
∎

In the algorithms, we represent a
k
-complete family using the compacted trie
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
of the union
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}N(F)
.
Its terminal nodes
F^{\prime}
are marked with a subset of strings
F\in\mathcal{F}
for which
F^{\prime}\in N(F)
;
for convenience we also store

and

.
Each edge is labeled by a factor of
F\in\mathcal{F}
, perhaps prepended by
\$
.

Our construction of a
k
-complete family is based on the results of
[
8
,
26
]
,
but we provide a self-contained proof in the appendix.

Proposition 3

(see also
[
8
,
26
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
be a finite family of strings
and let
k\geq 0
be an integer.
There exists a
k
-complete family
N

such that
|N_{d}(F)|\leq 2^{d}\binom{\log|\mathcal{F}|+d}{d}
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
0\leq d\leq k
.
Moreover, the compacted trie
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
can be constructed in
\mathcal{O}(2^{k}|\mathcal{F}|\binom{\log|\mathcal{F}|+k+1}{k+1})
time
provided constant-time
\mathsf{LCP}

.

Remark 4
.

The 1-complete family from Table

.

6
Main Result

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of suffixes and reverse prefixes of
X
and
Y
occurring in
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)
or
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y)
,

.
For a half-integer

,

Combining

2

Corollary 4
.

If
\mathsf{LCF}_{k}(X,Y)\geq\ell
,

\mathsf{LCF}_{k}(X,Y)=\max_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}\mathrm{maxPairLCP}(\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k,k_{1})}_{\ell}(X),\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k,k_{2})}_{\ell}(Y)).

Proof.

By
Lemma

2
, there exist
(U_{1},U_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)
,
(V_{1},V_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y)

4

\mathsf{LCF}_{k}(X,Y)=\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{2})

,

,

We set

and

which concludes the proof of the upper bound on

.

For the lower bound, we shall prove
that
\mathsf{LCF}_{k}(X,Y)\geq\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{2})

for all
(U^{\prime}_{1},U^{\prime}_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k,k^{\prime}_{1})}_{\ell}(X)
and
(V^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k,k^{\prime}_{2})}_{\ell}(Y)

such that

.
By definition of
\mathsf{Pairs}^{(k,k^{\prime}_{1})}_{\ell}
, there exist
(U_{1},U_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(X)

such that
U^{\prime}_{1}\in N_{k,p^{\prime}_{1}}(U_{1})
and
U^{\prime}_{2}\in N_{k,q^{\prime}_{1}}(U_{2})
for half-integers

.
Similarly, there exist
(V_{1},V_{2})\in\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}(Y)

such that

and

for half-integers

.
We set

and

as well as

due to
Lemma

4
.
Now,

2
yields

\mathsf{LCF}_{k}(X,Y)\geq\mathsf{LCP}_{p}(U_{1},V_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}_{q}(U_{2},V_{2})\geq\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{1})+\mathsf{LCP}(U^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{2})

Theorem 5
.

For
k=\mathcal{O}(\log n)
,
the
LCF
k
(X,Y,\ell)
problem can be solved in time
\mathcal{O}(n+\tfrac{2^{\mathcal{O}(k)}n\log^{k+1}{n}}{k^{k}\sqrt{\ell}})
.
For

, this running time becomes
\mathcal{O}(n+\tfrac{n\log^{k+1}n}{\sqrt{\ell}})
.

Proof.

First, we build the joint suffix tree of
X
,
X^{R}
,
Y
, and
Y^{R}
,
as well as the family
\mathcal{F}
. A component for the LCA queries on the suffix tree
lets us compare any suffixes of
F\in\mathcal{F}
in constant time.
This allows us to build the
k
-complete family
N(F):F\in\mathcal{F}
,
represented as a compacted trie of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\bigcup\{N(F):F\in\mathcal{F}\}
using
3
.
Next, we construct the sets
\mathsf{Pairs}_{\ell}^{(k,k^{\prime})}(X)\subseteq(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})^{2}

In this section we show an efficient construction of a

-complete family.

We apply a recursive procedure that builds the subtree rooted at the node representing

The input

can be seen as a “budget” of remaining symbol changes in the string that prevents exceeding the number

In the first call we have

and

In the pseudocode below we state this procedure in an abstract way; afterwards we explain how to implement it efficiently.

//

\mathcal{F}_{P\$}:=\mathcal{F}_{P\$}\cup\{\,(S[2\mathinner{.\,.}],F,b-1)\,\}

In the implementation of the procedure we use finger search trees
[
13
]
,
which maintain subsets of a linearly-ordered universe supporting constant-time queries.
Among many applications (see
[
5
]
for a survey),
they support the following two operations
[
15
,
5
]
:

•

insert an element into a set
A
, which takes
\mathcal{O}(\log|A|)
time,

•

for a given key
t
, split the set
A
into
A_{\leq t}=\{a\in A:a\leq t\}
and
A_{>t}=\{a\in A:a>t\}
,
which takes
\mathcal{O}(\log\min(|A_{\leq t}|,|A_{>t}|))
time.

Claim
.

The
k
-complete family
N
represented as a trie

can be constructed
in
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{F}|2^{k}\binom{\log|\mathcal{F}|+k+1}{k+1})

provided constant-time

queries for suffixes of strings
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

To a tuple
(S,F,b)\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
we assign a number of tokens:

\mathit{Tokens}_{P}(S,F,b)=C(2^{b+1}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|+b+1}{b+1}

where
C
is a sufficiently large constant.
We shall inductively prove that

Generate
(
P,\mathcal{F}_{P}
) can be implemented in time

\sum_{(S,F,b)\in\mathcal{F}_{P}}\mathit{Tokens}_{P}(S,F,b).

Before that, let us specify how the arguments to the procedure are specified.
The string
P
is represented by the corresponding node of the constructed trie
T_{N}
;
we also explicitly store
|P|
and
\#_{\$}(P)
.
The set
\mathcal{F}_{P}
is stored in a finger search tree with tuples
(S,F,b)

ordered by
S
. However,
S
is not stored itself as it is uniquely specified as a suffix of
F
of length
|F|-|P|
. Thus each element in the tree is stored in
\mathcal{O}(1)
space.

First, we process tuples
(S,F,b)
with
S=\varepsilon
. They are conveniently located at the beginning of
\mathcal{F}_{P}
.
We remove these tuples from
\mathcal{F}_{P}
and store
F
at the current node of
T_{N}
.
This simulates inserting
P
to
N(F)
; we also store auxiliary values
d_{H}(P,F)=k-b
and
\#_{\$}(P)
.

Next, we compute the length of longest common prefix
P^{\prime}
of non-empty strings
S
with
(S,F,b)\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
.
For this, we make an
\mathsf{LCP}
query for the smallest and the largest of these suffixes.
If the longest common prefix
P^{\prime}
is non-empty, we observe that
\mathcal{F}_{PP^{\prime}}=\mathcal{F}_{P}

(with the stored representation unchanged) and Algorithm
1
does not explore any other branch.
Hence, we immediately call
Generate
(
PP^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{PP^{\prime}}
) which corresponds to creating a complete compacted edge of the resulting trie. This step takes
\mathcal{O}(1)
time,
but it guarantees that
Generate
(
PP^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{PP^{\prime}}
) outputs or branches. Hence, this time gets amortized.

If
P^{\prime}=\varepsilon
, we partition
\mathcal{F}_{P}
into at most
\sigma

finger search trees
\mathcal{F}_{P,c}
each storing tuples sharing the character
S[1]=c
,
and we identify the heavy character
h
by choosing the largest
\mathcal{F}_{P,c}
.
For this, we iteratively split out the tree with the smallest unprocessed
S[1]
,
which takes time proportional to
\sum_{c\neq h}\log|\mathcal{F}_{P,c}|
.

The sets
\mathcal{F}_{Pc}
for
c\neq h
already represented by
\mathcal{F}_{P,c}
(note that the order does not change, and the tuples need not be altered since the “budget”
b
remains the same and
S
is stored implicitly).
Similarly, we can build
\mathcal{F}_{Ph}
by inserting new tuples into
\mathcal{F}_{P,h}
.

Thus, we define

\mathcal{L}_{P}:=\{(S,F,b)\in\mathcal{F}_{P}:S\neq\varepsilon\text{ and }S[1]\neq h\}

and insert to
\mathcal{F}_{Ph}
and
\mathcal{F}_{P\$}
tuples
(S[2\mathinner{.\,.}],F,b-1)
for
(S,F,b)\in\mathcal{L}_{P}
with
b>0
,
which takes
\mathcal{O}(\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|)
time per element.

In total, the processing time is
\mathcal{O}(1)
for each element of
\mathcal{L}_{P}
with
b=0
,
and
\mathcal{O}(\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|)
when
b>0
. Additionally, we may spend
\mathcal{O}(1)
time for a tuple with
S=\varepsilon
. Let us check that the difference in the number of tokens is sufficient to cover the running time of these operations.

The tuples with
S=\varepsilon
do not appear in future computations. Hence, we spend all their tokens on the computations related to them. It is indeed sufficient:

\mathit{Tokens}_{P}(\varepsilon,F,b)=C(2^{b+1}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|+b+1}{b+1}\geq C\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|+1}{1}=C(\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|+1)\geq C.

We don’t spend any time on tuples with
S[1]=h
, and number of tokens for such a tuple does not increase:

\displaystyle\mathit{Tokens}_{P}(S,F,b)-\mathit{Tokens}_{Ph}(S,F,b)=

\displaystyle C(2^{b+1}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|+b+1}{b+1}-C(2^{b+1}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{Ph}|+b+1}{b+1}\geq 0.

Finally, for a tuple with
S[1]\neq h
(i.e., in
\mathcal{L}_{p}
) the difference in the number of tokens is

\displaystyle\mathit{Tokens}_{P}(S,F,b)-\mathit{Tokens}_{Pc}(S^{\prime},F,b)-\mathit{Tokens}_{Ph}(S^{\prime},F,b-1)

\displaystyle-\mathit{Tokens}_{P\$}(S^{\prime},F,b-1)=

\displaystyle C(2^{b+1}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|+b+1}{b+1}-C(2^{b+1}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{Pc}|+b+1}{b+1}-C(2^{b}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{Ph}|+b}{b}

\displaystyle-C(2^{b}-1)\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{P\$}|+b}{b}

\displaystyle\geq C\tbinom{\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|+b}{b}

where
c=S[1]
and
S^{\prime}=S[2\mathinner{.\,.}]
.
It is sufficient since we spend constant time for
b=0
and
\mathcal{O}(\log|\mathcal{F}_{P}|)
time for
b\geq 1
.

The claimed bound on the overall running time follows.
∎

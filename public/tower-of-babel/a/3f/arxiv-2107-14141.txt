Joins and meets in effect algebras
G. Bińczak 1, J. Kaleta 2, A. Zembrzuski 3

Abstract
. We know that each effect algebra E E is isomorphic to π ⁡ ( X ) \pi(X) for some E E -test spaces ( X , 𝐓 ) (X,{\bf T}) .We describe when π ⁡ ( x ) ∨ π ⁡ ( y ) \pi(x)\lor\pi(y) and π ⁡ ( x ) ∧ π ⁡ ( y ) \pi(x)\land\pi(y) exists for x , y ∈ 𝐄 ⁡ ( X , 𝐓 ) x,y\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T}) . Moreover we give the formula for π ⁡ ( x ) ∨ π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x)\lor\pi(x) and π ⁡ ( x ) ∧ π ⁡ ( y ) \pi(x)\land\pi(y) using only x , y x,y and tests which are elements of 𝐓 {\bf T} . We obtain an example of finite, not homogeneous effect algebra E E such that sharp elements of E E form a lattice, whereas E E is not a lattice.

1.
Introduction

Effect algebras have been introduced by Foulis and Bennet in 1994 (see
[
4
]
) for the study of foundations of quantum mechanics (see
[
3
]
). Independently, Chovanec and Kôpka introduced an essentially equivalent structure called
D
-
poset

(see
[
9
]
). Another equivalent structure was introduced by Giuntini and Greuling in
[
5
]
).

The most important example of an effect algebra is
(E(H),0,I,\oplus)
, where
H
is a Hilbert space and
E(H)
consists of all self-adjoint operators
A
on
H
such that
0\leq A\leq I
. For
A,B\in E(H)
,
A\oplus B
is defined if and only if
A+B\leq I
and then
A\oplus B=A+B
. Elements of
E(H)
are called
effects
and they play an important role in the theory of quantum measurements (
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
).

A quantum effect may be treated as two-valued (it means
0
or
1
) quantum measurement that may be unsharp (fuzzy). If there exist some pairs of effects
a,b
which posses an orthosum
a\oplus b
then this orthosum correspond to a parallel measurement of two effects.

In this paper we describe the join and meet in effect algebras. In order to do this we use algebraic
E
-test spaces in the following way: we know that for every effect algebra
E
there exists an algebraic
E
-test space
(X,{\bf T})
such that
E
is isomorphic to
\Pi(X)
(please see
[
6
]
) so it is enough to describe joins and meets in
\Pi(X)
.

Definition 1.1
.

In
[
4
]
an effect algebra is defined to be an algebraic system
(E,0,1,\oplus)
consisting of a set
E
, two special elements
0,1\in E
called the zero and the unit, and a partially defined binary operation
\oplus
on
E
that satisfies the following conditions for all
p,q,r\in E
:

(1)

[Commutative Law] If
p\oplus q
is defined, then
q\oplus p
is defined and
p\oplus q=q\oplus p
.

(2)

[Associative Law] If
q\oplus r
is defined and
p\oplus(q\oplus r)
is defined, then
p\oplus q
is defined,
(p\oplus q)\oplus r
is defined, and
p\oplus(q\oplus r)=(p\oplus q)\oplus r
.

(3)

[Orthosupplementation Law] For every
p\in E
there exists a unique
q\in E
such that
p\oplus q
is defined and
p\oplus q=1
.

(4)

[Zero-unit Law] If
1\oplus p
is defined, then
p=0
.

For simplicity, we often refer to
E
, rather than to
(E,0,1,\oplus)
, as being an effect algebra.

Definition 1.2
.

If
p,q\in E
, we say that
p
and
q
are orthogonal and write
p\perp q
iff
p\oplus q
is defined in
E
. If
p,q\in E
and
p\oplus q=1
, we call
q
the orthosupplement of
p
and write
p^{\prime}=q
.
For effect algebras
E_{1},E_{2}
a mapping
\phi\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
is said to be an isomorphism if
\phi
is a bijection,

a\perp b\iff\phi(a)\perp\phi(b)
,
\phi(1)=1
and
\phi(a\oplus b)=\phi(a)\oplus\phi(b)
.

It is shown in
[
4
]
that the relation
\leq
defined for
p,q\in E
by
p\leq q
iff
\exists r\in E
with
p\oplus r=q
is a partial order on
E
and
0\leq p\leq 1
holds for all
p\in E
. It is also shown that the mapping
p\mapsto p^{\prime}
is an order-reversing involution and that
q\perp p
iff
q\leq p^{\prime}
. Furthermore,
E
satisfies the following
cancellation law
: If
p\oplus q\leq r\oplus q
, then
p\leq r
.

An element
a\in E
is
sharp
if the greatest lower bound of the set
\{a,a^{\prime}\}
equals
0
(i.e.
a\land a^{\prime}=0
). We denote the set of sharp elements of
E
by
E_{S}
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x\in E
let
nx=x\oplus x\oplus\ldots\oplus x
(
n
-times). We say that ı
(x)=\max\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon nx
exists
\}
is
the isotropic index of

x
.

An
atom
of an effect algebra
E
is a minimal element of
E\setminus\{0\}
. An effect algebra
E
is
atomic
if for every non-zero element
x\in E
there exists an atom
a\in E
such that
a\leq x
.

In
[
6
]
Gudder introduced algebraic
E
-test spaces:

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a nonempty set and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
. For
f,g\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}
, we write
f\leq g
if
f(x)\leq g(x)
for all
x\in X
and define

f+g\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}
by
(f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x)
. If
f\leq g
, we define
g-f\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}
by
(g-f)(x)=g(x)-f(x)
. Denote by
f_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}
the function
f_{0}(x)=0
for all
x\in X
.

A pair
(X,\bf T)
is an
E
-test spaces if and only if
{\bf T}\subseteq N_{0}^{X}
and the following conditions hold:

(1)

For any
x\in X
there exists a
t\in\bf T
such that
t(x)\not=0
.

(2)

If
s,t\in\bf T
with
s\leq t
, then
s=t
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})=\{f\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}\colon f\leq t
for some
t\in\bf T\}
. Let
f,g,h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then

(1)

f\perp g
if
f+g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,

(2)

f{\rm\;loc\;}g
if
f+g\in{\bf T}
,

(3)

f\approx_{h}g
if
f+h\in{\bf T}
and
g+h\in{\bf T}
,

(4)

f\approx g
if there exists
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
such that
f\approx_{h}g
.

We say that
(X,{\bf T})
is algebraic if for
f,g,h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,
f\approx_{h}g
and
h\perp f
imply that
h\perp g
.

Definition 1.5
.

(see
[
8
]
)
Let
E
be a finite effect algebra. The atomic effect test space of
E
is the pair
({\rm At}(E),{\bf T})
, where
{\rm At}(E)
is the set of all atoms of
E
and

{\bf T}=\{t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{E\setminus\{0\}}\colon{\rm supp}(t)\subseteq{\rm At}(E)\land\bigoplus_{a\in E}t(a)a=1\}

The elements of
{\bf T}
are called atomic tests of
E
. We say that a mapping
f\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{E\setminus\{0\}}
is an atomic event of
E
iff there is a
t\in{\bf T}
such that
t\geq f
. For an atomic event
f
of
E
, we write shortly
\bigoplus f
instead of
\bigoplus_{a\in{\rm At}(E)}f(a)a
.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space. If
f\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
, we define
\pi(f)=\{g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})\colon g\approx f\}
and

\Pi=\Pi(X)=\{\pi(f)\colon f\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})\}

We define
0,1\in\Pi
by
0=\pi(f_{0})=\{f_{0}\}
and
1=\pi(t)
for any
t\in\bf T
. We define
\pi(f)^{\prime}=\pi(g)
if
g{\rm\;loc\;}f
(such
g
exists since there exists
t\in\bf T
such that
f\leq t
and then
t-f\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
(t-f){\rm\;loc\;}f
). We define
\pi(f)\oplus\pi(g)=\pi(f+g)
when
f\perp g
.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.2]

If
(X,\bf T)
is an algebraic
E
-test space, then
\Pi(X)
can be organized into an effect algebra.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
6

If

Thanks to the above theorems the study of effect algebras can be reduced to the study of effect algebras of the form

2.
Main Theorems

Theorems
2.7
and
2.11
are the main theorems which one need to prove. In order to do this we need the following three technical lemmas:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space. Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then
\pi(f)=\pi(g)\iff\exists{t_{1},t_{2}\in{\bf T}}f\leq t_{1}\land g\leq t_{2}\land t_{1}-f=t_{2}-g
.

Proof.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space and
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
.

Assume that there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\bf T}
such that
f\leq t_{1}
,
g\leq t_{2}
and
t_{1}-f=t_{2}-g
. Let
h=t_{1}-f=t_{2}-g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then
f+h=t_{1}\in{\bf T}
and
g+h=t_{2}\in{\bf T}
so
f\approx_{h}g
and
\pi(f)=\pi(g)
.

Assume that
\pi(f)=\pi(g)
then there exists
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
such that
f\approx_{h}g
. It follows that
t_{1}=f+h\in{\bf T}
and
t_{2}=g+h\in{\bf T}
. Then
f\leq t_{1}
,
g\leq t_{2}
and
t_{1}-f=h=t_{2}-g
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space. Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then
\pi(f)\leq\pi(g)\iff\exists{h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})}

\pi(h)=\pi(g)\land f\leq h
.

Proof.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space. Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
.

Let
\pi(f)\leq\pi(g)
than there exists
i\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
such that
f\perp i
and
\pi(f)\oplus\pi(i)=\pi(g)
so
\pi(f+i)=\pi(g)
. Let
h=f+i\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
. Then

f\leq h
and
\pi(h)=\pi(f+i)=\pi(g)
.

Assume that there exists
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
such that
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
and
f\leq h
. Let
i=h-f\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
. Then
h=f+i
and
\pi(f)\oplus\pi(i)=\pi(f+i)=\pi(h)
so
\pi(f)\leq\pi(h)=\pi(g)
.
∎

The above Lemma implies that if
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
f\leq g
(i.e.
f(x)\leq g(x)
for all
x\in X
) then
\pi(f)\leq\pi(g)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space. Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then
\pi(f)\leq\pi(g)\iff\exists_{t_{1},t_{2}\in{\bf T}}\;g\leq t_{2}\land f\leq(t_{1}+g)-t_{2}

Proof.

Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
\pi(f)\leq\pi(g)
. By Lemma
2.2
there exists
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
such that
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
and
f\leq h
. Then there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\bf T}
such that
h\leq t_{1}
,
g\leq t_{2}
and
t_{1}-h=t_{2}-g
by Lemma
2.1
. Hence
f\leq h=(t_{1}+g)-t_{2}
.

Now let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and assume that there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\bf T}
such that
g\leq t_{2}
and
f\leq(t_{1}+g)-t_{2}
. Let
h=(t_{1}+g)-t_{2}
. Then

h=(t_{1}+g)-t_{2}\leq t_{1}+t_{2}-t_{2}=t_{1}
so
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
. Moreover
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
by Lemma
2.1
since
h\leq t_{1}
,
g\leq t_{2}
and

t_{1}-h=t_{1}-((t_{1}+g)-t_{2})=t_{2}-g
. Hence
\pi(f)\leq\pi(g)
by Lemma
2.2
since
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
and
f\leq(t_{1}+g)-t_{2}=h
which ends the proof.

∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space. Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then

ub(f,g)=\{(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}\colon f\leq f_{1},g\leq f_{3},f-f_{1}+f_{2}\leq f_{4},g-f_{3}+f_{4}\leq f_{2}\}

The set
ub(f,g)
is non-empty since there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\bf T}
such that
f\leq t_{1}
and
g\leq t_{2}
and then
(t_{1},t_{1},t_{2},t_{1})\in ub(f,g)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
be an algebraic
E
-test space and
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
. For every
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in ub(f,g)
we have

(1)

i=\max(f-f_{1}+f_{2},g-f_{3}+f_{4},f_{0})\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})

(2)

\pi(f)\leq\pi(i)
and
\pi(g)\leq\pi(i)

On the other hand if
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,
\pi(f)\leq\pi(h)
and
\pi(g)\leq\pi(h)
then
there exists
(f_{1}.f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in ub(f,g)
such that
i=\max(f-f_{1}+f_{2},g-f_{3}+f_{4},f_{0})\leq h
.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in ub(f,g)
.
Let
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}

for every

. Then

[formula]

f-f_{1}+f_{2}\leq i

and

[formula]

g-f_{3}+f_{4}\leq i

i\leq f_{2}
and

g-f_{3}+f_{4}\leq f_{3}-f_{3}+f_{4}=f_{4}

.

\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)
.

h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,
\pi(f)\leq\pi(h)
and
\pi(g)\leq\pi(h)
. By Lemma
2.3
there exist
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\in{\bf T}
such that

[formula]

h\leq f_{2},f\leq(f_{1}+h)-f_{2},h\leq f_{4},g\leq(f_{3}+h)-f_{4}.

.

.
Moreover
f-f_{1}+f_{2}\leq h
and
g-f_{3}+f_{4}\leq h

.
∎

. On the other hand one can assign
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in ub(f,g)
to every
upper bound of
\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
be an algebraic
E
-test space and
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then
\pi(f)\lor\pi(g)

if and only if there exist

such that

(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}
.

f,g
satisfy J-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in ub(f,g)
. then

\pi(f)\lor\pi(g)=\pi(h),

\Rightarrow

and
\pi(g)\leq\pi(h)

f,g

(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}

Let

\pi(g)\leq\pi(i_{2})

Therefore
\pi(i_{2})
is an upper bound of
\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)

\pi(h)

\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)

\pi(i_{1})\leq\pi(i_{2})
i.e.

\pi(\max(f-f_{1}+f_{2},g-f_{3}+f_{4},f_{0}))\leq\pi(\max(f-g_{1}+g_{2},g-g_{3}+g_{4},f_{0}))

so
f,g
satisfy
J
-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}
.

\Leftarrow

Assume that
f,g
satisfy J-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}
. Let

i_{1}=\max(f-f_{1}+f_{2},g-f_{3}+f_{4},f_{0}).

We show that
i_{1}\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
\pi(f)\lor\pi(g)=\pi(i_{1})
.

By (J1) we have

f\leq f_{1},g\leq f_{3},f-f_{1}+f_{2}\leq f_{4},g-f_{3}+f_{4}\leq f_{2}

so
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in ub(f,g)
and by Lemma
2.5
we have

(a)

i_{1}\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,

(b)

\pi(f)\leq\pi(i_{1})
and
\pi(g)\leq\pi(i_{1})
.

Therefore
\pi(i_{1})
is an upper bound of
\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)
.

Assume that
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,
\pi(f)\leq\pi(h)
and
\pi(g)\leq\pi(h)
. By lemma
2.5
there exist
(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\in ub(f,g)
such that

i_{2}=\max(f-g_{1}+g_{2},g-g_{3}+g_{4},f_{0})\leq h.

Hence
\pi(i_{2})\leq\pi(h)
by Lemma
2.2
.

By (J2) we have

\pi(\max(f-f_{1}+f_{2},g-f_{3}+f_{4},f_{0}))\leq\pi(\max(f-g_{1}+g_{2},g-g_{3}+g_{4},f_{0}))

so
\pi(i_{1})\leq\pi(i_{2})
.

Hence
\pi(i_{1})\leq\pi(i_{2})\leq\pi(h)
so
\pi(i_{1})\leq\pi(h)
. Therefore

\pi(i_{1})=\pi(\max(f-f_{1}+f_{2},g-f_{3}+f_{4},f_{0}))=\pi(f)\lor\pi(g).

∎

Now we deal with the meets in effect algebras.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(X,\bf T)
be an algebraic
E
-test space. Let

then

The set

t_{1},t_{2}\in{\bf T}
such that
f\leq t_{1}
and

and then
(t_{1},t_{1},t_{2},t_{2})\in lb(f,g)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
be an algebraic
E
-test space and
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
. For every
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
we have

(1)

i=\min(f-f_{2}+f_{1},g-f_{4}+f_{3})\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})

(2)

\pi(f)\geq\pi(i)
and
\pi(g)\geq\pi(i)

On the other hand if
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,
\pi(f)\geq\pi(h)
and
\pi(g)\geq\pi(h)
then
there exists
(f_{1}.f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
such that
i=\min(f-f_{2}+f_{1},g-f_{4}+f_{3},)\geq h
.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
.
Let
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}
be a function such that

i(x)=\min(f(x)-f_{2}(x)+f_{1}(x),g(x)-f_{4}(x)+f_{3}(x))

for every
x\in X
. Then
i\leq f-f_{2}+f_{1}\leq f_{1}\in{\bf T}
since
f\leq f_{2}
.
Moreover
f-f_{2}+f_{1}\geq f_{0}
,
g-f_{4}+f_{3}\geq f_{0}
since
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
so
i\geq f_{0}
and
i\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
.

By the definition of
i
we have
i\leq f-f_{2}+f_{1}
and
i\leq g-f_{4}+f_{3}
.
Moreover
f\leq f_{2}
and
g\leq f_{4}
since
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
. Thus
\pi(i)\leq\pi(f)
and
\pi(i)\leq\pi(g)
by Lemma
2.3
.

Now assume that
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,
\pi(f)\geq\pi(h)
and
\pi(g)\geq\pi(h)
. By Lemma
2.3
there exist
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\in{\bf T}
such that

f\leq f_{2},h\leq(f_{1}+f)-f_{2},g\leq f_{4},h\leq(f_{3}+g)-f_{4}

so

f\leq f_{2},g\leq f_{4},f-f_{2}+f_{1}\geq f_{0},g-f_{4}+f_{3}\geq f_{0}

and
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
.

We know that
h\leq(f_{1}+f)-f_{2}
and
h\leq(f_{3}+g)-f_{4}
so
h\leq i=\min(f-f_{2}+f_{1},g-f_{4}+f_{3})
.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
be an algebraic
E
-test space and
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
then
\pi(f)\land\pi(g)
exists in
\Pi(X)
if and only if there exist
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\in{\bf T}
such that
f,g
satisfy M-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}
.
Moreover if
f,g
satisfy M-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
then

\pi(f)\land\pi(g)=\pi(h),

where
h(x)=\min(f(x)-f_{2}(x)+f_{1}(x),g(x)-f_{4}(x)+f_{3}(x))
for every
x\in X
.

Proof.

\Rightarrow

Let
f,g\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
\pi(f)\land\pi(g)=\pi(h)
for some
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
. Then
\pi(f)\geq\pi(h)
and
\pi(g)\geq\pi(h)
so by Lemma
2.9

there exists
(f_{1}.f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
such that

i_{1}=\min(f-f_{2}+f_{1},g-f_{4}+f_{3},)\geq h.

We show that
f,g
satisfy M-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}
.

We know that
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
so

f\leq f_{2},g\leq f_{4},f-f_{2}+f_{1}\geq f_{0},g-f_{4}+f_{3}\geq f_{0}

and (M1) is satisfied.

Now we show that (M2) is also satisfied.

Assume that there exist
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4}\in{\bf T}
such that

f\leq g_{2},f_{0}\leq f+g_{1}-g_{2},g\leq g_{4},f_{0}\leq g+g_{3}-g_{4}.

so
(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\in lb(f,g)
.

Let
i_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{X}
be a function such that

i_{2}(x)=\min(f(x)+g_{1}(x)-g_{2}(x),g(x)+g_{3}(x)-g_{4}(x))

for every
x\in X
. Then

(a)

i_{2}=\min(f-g_{2}+g_{1},g-g_{4}+g_{3})\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})

(b)

\pi(f)\geq\pi(i_{2})
and
\pi(g)\geq\pi(i_{2})

by Lemma
2.9
.

Therefore
\pi(i_{2})
is a lower bound of
\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)
. We know that
\pi(h)
is a meet of
\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)
so
\pi(i_{2})\leq\pi(h)
. By Lemma
2.2
we know that

\pi(i_{1})\geq\pi(h)
since
i_{1}\geq h
. Thus
\pi(i_{2})\leq\pi(i_{1})
i.e.

\pi(\min(f-f_{2}+f_{1},g-f_{4}+f_{3}))\geq\pi(\min(f-g_{1}+g_{2},g-g_{3}+g_{4})).

so
f,g
satisfies M-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}
.

\Leftarrow

Assume that
f,g
satisfy M-condition for
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in{\bf T}^{4}
. Let

i_{1}=\min(f+f_{1}-f_{2},g+f_{3}-f_{4}).

We show that
i_{1}\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
and
\pi(f)\land\pi(g)=\pi(i_{1})
.

By (M1) we have

f\leq f_{2},\quad g\leq f_{4},\quad f-f_{2}+f_{1}\geq f_{0},\quad g-f_{4}+f_{3}\geq f_{0}

so
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\in lb(f,g)
and by Lemma
2.9
we have

(a)

i_{1}=\min(f-f_{2}+f_{1},g-f_{4}+f_{3})\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})

(b)

\pi(f)\geq\pi(i_{1})
and
\pi(g)\geq\pi(i_{1})

Therefore
\pi(i_{1})
is an lower bound of
\pi(f)
and
\pi(g)
.

Assume that
h\in{\bf E}(X,{\bf T})
,
\pi(h)\leq\pi(f)
and
\pi(h)\leq\pi(g)
. By lemma
2.9
there exists
(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\in lb(f,g)
such that

i_{2}=\min(f-g_{1}+g_{2},g-g_{3}+g_{4})\geq h.

\pi(i_{2})\geq\pi(h)
by Lemma
2.2
.

By (M2) we have

\pi(\min(f-f_{2}+f_{1},g-f_{4}+f_{3}))\geq\pi(\min(f-g_{1}+g_{2},g-g_{3}+g_{4})).

\pi(i_{1})\geq\pi(h)

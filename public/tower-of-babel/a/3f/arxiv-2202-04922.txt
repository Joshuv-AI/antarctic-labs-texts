Abstract
. For K / F K/F a finite Galois extension of number fields, the relative Pólya group Po ⁡ ( K / F ) \Po(K/F) is the subgroup of the ideal class group of K K generated by all the strongly ambiguous ideal classes in K / F K/F . The notion of Ostrowski quotient Ost ⁡ ( K / F ) \Ost(K/F) , as the cokernel of the capitulation map into Po ⁡ ( K / F ) \Po(K/F) , has been recently introduced in [ 19 ] . In this paper, using some results of González-Avilés [ 8 ] , we find a new approach to define Po ⁡ ( K / F ) \Po(K/F) and Ost ⁡ ( K / F ) \Ost(K/F) which is the main motivation for us to investigate analogous notions in the elliptic curve setting. For E E an elliptic curve defined over F F , we define the Ostrowski quotient Ost ⁡ ( E , K / F ) \Ost(E,K/F) and the coarse Ostrowski quotient Ost c ⁡ ( E , K / F ) \Ost_{c}(E,K/F) of E E relative to K / F K/F , for which in the latter group we do not take into account primes of bad reduction. Our main result is a non-trivial structure theorem for the group Ost c ⁡ ( E , K / F ) \Ost_{c}(E,K/F) and we analyze this theorem, in some details, for the class of curves E E over quadratic extensions K / F K/F .

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be an algebraic number field. For a prime power
q
, the
Ostrowski ideal

\Pi_{q}(K)
of
K
is defined as follows
[
15
]
:

(1.1)

\Pi_{q}(K):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{K}\\
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})=q\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{p}.

By convention, if
K
has no ideal with norm
q
, we set
\Pi_{q}(K)=\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The number field
K
is called a
Pólya field
if all the Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{q}(K)
for arbitrary prime powers
q
are principal
[
23
]
. The notion of
Pólya-Ostrowski group
or
Pólya group
was introduced in
[
4
, Chapter II,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
.

Definition 1.1
.

The Pólya group of a number field
K
is the subgroup
\Po(K)
of
the class group
\Cl(K)
generated by the classes of all the Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{q}(K)
.

Hence
K
is a Pólya field if and only if
\Po(K)
is trivial. Obviously every number field with class number one is a Pólya field, but not conversely. For instance, Zantema proved that every cyclotomic field is a Pólya field
[
23
, Proposition 2.6]
. The reader is referred to
[
4
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
23
]
for some results on Pólya fields and Pólya groups. Recently the notion of Pólya group has been relativized independently in
[
11
,
5
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
K/F
a finite extension of number fields, the
relative Pólya group
of
K
over
F
, is the subgroup of
\Cl(K)
generated by the classes of the
relative Ostrowski ideals

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}(K/F):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{P}\in\mathbb{P}_{K}\\
N_{K/F}(\mathfrak{P})=\mathfrak{p}^{f}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{P},

where
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
F
,
f
is a positive integer and by convention, if
K
has no ideal with relative norm
\mathfrak{p}^{f}
(over
F
), then
\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}(K/F)=\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
We denote the relative Pólya group of
K
over
F
by
\Po(K/F)
. In particular,
\Po(K/\mathbb{Q})=\Po(K)
and
\Po(K/K)=\Cl(K)
.

If
K/F
is a Galois extension with Galois group
G
, the ideals
\Pi_{\mathfrak{P}^{f}}(K/F)
freely generate the ambiguous ideals
I(K)^{G}

[
3
, Proof of Proposition 2.2]
. Hence in this case,
\Po(K/F)
coincides with the group of strongly ambiguous ideal classes of
K/F
. Moreover, using some results of Brumer and Rosen
[
3
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
, one may find the following exact sequence which is also obtained by Zantema in the absolute case
[
23
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

[
5
,
13
]

For
K/F
a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, the following sequence is exact:

(BRZ)

0\rightarrow\Ker({\epsilon}_{K/F})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,U_{K})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{\mathfrak{p}(K/F)}\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\frac{\Po(K/F)}{{\epsilon}_{K/F}(\Cl(F))}\rightarrow 0.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that “BRZ” stands for Brumer-Rosen
[
3
]
and Zantema
[
23
]
whose cohomological results are the main tools to prove the above proposition, see
[
19
, Remark 1.4]
.

As a modification of the relative Pólya group, the notion of “Ostrowski quotient” has been recently introduced in
[
19
]
.

Definition 1.5
.

For
K/F
a finite extension of number fields, the
Ostrowski quotient

\Ost(K/F)
is defined as

(1.3)

\Ost(K/F):=\frac{\Po(K/F)}{\Po(K/F)\cap\epsilon_{K/F}(Cl(F))}.

In particular,
\Ost(K/\mathbb{Q})=\Po(K/\mathbb{Q})=\Po(K)
and
\Ost(K/K)=0
. The extension
K/F
is called
Ostrowski
(or
K
is called
F
-Ostrowski) whenever
\Ost(K/F)
is trivial.

Remark 1.6
.

If
K/F
is Galois, then
\epsilon_{K/F}(\Cl(F))\subseteq\Po(K/F)

[
13
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
. Hence

(1.4)

\Ost(K/F)=\frac{\Po(K/F)}{\epsilon_{K/F}(\Cl(F))}.

Some applications of (

), especially generalizations of some results for Pólya fields, have been found in
[
13
,
\lx@sectionsign 2
]
. Moreover, using (

) one may find simple proofs for some classical well known results in the literature for the capitulation problem
[

,
\lx@sectionsign
2.2.1]
. For more applications of (

[

,

,

]
.

2.
Localization and transgression maps

From now on, we fix the following notations:

•

K/F
: a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
;

•

G_{F}:=Gal(\overline{F}/F)
, where
\overline{F}
is the algebraic closure of
F
;

•

M_{F}
: a complete set of places of
F
;

•

M_{F}^{\infty}
: the set of all infinite places of
F
;

•

M_{F}^{0}
: the set of all finite places of
F
;

•

F_{v}
: the completion of
F
at a place
v\in M_{F}
;

•

w
: a fixed place of
K
lying above
v\in M_{F}
;

•

U_{K_{w}}
: the group of local units in
K_{w}
;

•

G_{w}:=Gal(K_{w}/F_{v})
for a place
v\in M_{F}
;

•

G_{F_{v}}:=Gal(\overline{F_{v}}/F_{v})
, where
\overline{F_{v}}
is the algebraic closure of
F_{v}
.

Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
F
with
M_{F}^{\infty}\subseteq S
.
In
[
8
]
, González-Avilés found some interesting results on the kernel and cokernel of the
S
-capitulation map
. Assuming
S=M_{F}^{\infty}
, we summarize a few results of González-Avilés which we need for the rest of the paper.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
8
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]

For
K/F
a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, the following sequences are exact:

(2.1)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow P(K)^{G}\rightarrow I(K)^{G}\rightarrow\Cl(K)_{\trans}^{G}\rightarrow 0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\Cl(K)_{\trans}^{G}\rightarrow\Cl(K)^{G}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,P(K))\rightarrow 0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow H^{1}(G,P(K))\rightarrow H^{2}(G,U_{K})\rightarrow H^{2}(G,K^{\times}),

(2.4)

0\rightarrow\Ker({\epsilon}_{K/F})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,U_{K})\xrightarrow{\lambda}\bigoplus_{v\in M_{F}^{0}}H^{1}(G_{w},U_{K_{w}})\rightarrow\frac{\Cl(K)^{G}_{\trans}}{\epsilon_{K/F}(\Cl(F))}\rightarrow 0.

Remark 2.2
.

The composite
\Cl(K)^{G}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,P(K))\hookrightarrow H^{2}(G,U_{K})
is called the
transgression map
which we denote it by
\trans_{K/F}
. Also

\Cl(K)^{G}_{\trans}
, the kernel of
\trans_{K/F}
, is called
the group of transgressive ambiguous classes
, see
[
8
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
. It must be noted that the transgression map is defined in a more general case as
\Trans_{K/F}:H^{1}(G_{K},U_{\overline{K}})^{G}\rightarrow H^{2}(G,U_{K})
, see
[
9
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
or
[
22
,
\lx@sectionsign
4]
for the definition (We call both as transgression map and use different notations to avoid confusion).

Using Proposition
2.1
, we find the following description of relative Pólya group and Ostrowski quotient.

Corollary 2.3
.

For
K/F
a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, we have

\Po(K/F)=\Cl(K)^{G}_{\trans}\quad\text{and}\quad\Ost(K/F)\simeq\Coker(\lambda),

where

\lambda:H^{1}(G,U_{K})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in M_{F}^{0}\end{subarray}}H^{1}(G_{w},U_{K_{w}})

denotes the localization map as in the exact sequence (
2.4
).

Proof.

Since
I(K)^{G}
is generated by all the relative Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}(K/F)
, see
[
3
, proof of Proposition 2.2]
,
I(K)^{G}/P(K)^{G}
coincides with the relative Pólya group
\Po(K/F)
. Using (
BRZ
) and Proposition
2.1
, we conclude that
\Ost(K/F)
is nothing else than the cokernel of the localization map
\lambda
.
∎

3.
On Tate-Shafarevich groups

Keep the same notations introduced in the previous section.
Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve defined over
F
. By the
Mordell-Weil Theorem
the group of
F
-rational points of
E
, denoted by
E(F)
, is finitely generated. An important tool to study
E(F)
, is the
Tate-Shafarevich group

\Sha(E/F)
, which measures the failure of the
Hasse local-global principle
for
curves that are
principal homogeneous spaces
for
E/F
. More formally,

\Sha(E/F)
is defined to be the group of everywhere locally trivial elements of
H^{1}(G_{F},E(\overline{F}))

[
20
, Chapter X]
:

(3.1)

\Sha(E/F):=\Ker\left(H^{1}(G_{F},E(\overline{F}))\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in M_{F}}H^{1}(G_{F_{v}},E(\overline{F_{v}})\right).

The famous conjecture of Tate and Shafarevich states that
\Sha(E/K)
is finite
[
20
, Conjecture 4.13]
. Though it has been shown that if the analytic rank of
E
is smaller that
2
, then
\Sha(E/K)
is finite
[
17
, Theorem 5.12]
, but the general case has not yet been proved.
Using the cohomological analogy between the Mordell-Weil group
E(F)
and the unit group
U_{F}
, Tate-Shafarevich group of
F
is defined similarly.

Definition 3.1
.

[
18
,
\lx@sectionsign
1]

The “Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sha(F)
” of
F
is defined as

(3.2)

\Sha(F):=\Ker\left(H^{1}(G_{F},U_{\overline{F}})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in M_{F}}H^{1}(G_{\overline{F}_{v}},U_{\overline{F}_{v}})\right),

where for non-archimedean
v
(resp. archimedean
v
),
U_{\overline{F}_{v}}
denotes the valuation ring in
\overline{F}_{v}
(resp.
U_{\overline{F}_{v}}=\overline{F}_{v}^{\times}
).
Note that by Hilbert’s Theorem 90,
\Sha(F)
can be defined just in term of the non-archimedean places of
F
.

Despite Tate-Shafarevich conjecture for elliptic curves is still open, the situation for number fields is more understandable.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
18
, Proposition 1]

\Sha(F)
is canonically isomorphic to the ideal class group of
F
. In particualr,
\Sha(F)
is finite.

Replacing
\overline{F}
with a finite Galois extension of
F
, one obtains the following notion.

Definition 3.3
.

[
18
,
\lx@sectionsign
1]

Let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
. The
locally trivial cohomology group

H^{1}_{lt}(G,U_{K})
is defined as

(3.3)

H^{1}_{lt}(G,U_{K}):=\Ker\left(H^{1}(G,U_{K})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in M_{F}}H^{1}(G_{w},U_{K_{w}})\right),

where for each
v\in M_{F}
,
w
is a fixed place of
K
lying above
v
.

Proposition 3.4
.

[
18
, Corollary of Proposition 1]

Let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
. Then

(3.4)

\Ker(\epsilon_{K/F})\simeq H^{1}_{lt}(G,U_{K}),

where
\epsilon_{K/F}:\Cl(F)\rightarrow\Cl(K)^{G}
denotes the capitulation map.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that isomorphism (
3.4
) is also obtained from González-Avilés exact sequence (
2.4
).

With the notations of this section, one has

Let

5.
Main Results

Convention.
We keep the same notations introduced in Section

We recall that the main goal of this paper is to find elliptic curve analogue of Ostrowski quotient. To achieve this, we use two analogies between number fields and elliptic curves. The first one is the cohomological analogy between unit groups of number fields and Mordell-Weil groups of elliptic curves. The other is the fact that the multiplicative group

, i.e., the trivial one-dimensional algebraic torus over

, and elliptic curves (two very different

over

over
\mathcal{O}_{F}

Remark 5.1

Note that

5.2.
Ostrowski quotient of
\mathbb{G}_{m,F}

As mentioned before, the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{m,F}
and the elliptic curve

have Néron models over

F
. First consider
\mathbb{G}_{m,F}
and
denote its Néron model over
\mathcal{O}_{F}

\mathcal{G}_{m}

[
2

\mathcal{O}_{F}
-group schemes

0\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathcal{O}_{F}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{m}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}(i_{\mathfrak{p}})_{*}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0,

where, for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}

is induced by the canonical projection
\mathcal{O}_{F}\rightarrow k(\mathfrak{p})=\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}

\mathcal{G}_{m}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{F})=\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathcal{O}_{F}}(\mathcal{O}_{F})=U_{F}

\mathcal{O}_{F}
-cohomology sequence induced by (

The
0
at the right comes from the fact that
H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{F},\mathcal{G}_{m})
injects into
H^{1}(F,\mathbb{G}_{m})

0
by Hilbert’s Theorem 90. Since

\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}\mathbb{Z}\simeq I(F)\quad\text{and}\quad H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{F},\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq\text{Pic}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\simeq\Cl(F),

the last sequence is simply the well-known exact sequence

Working over
K

-modules

0\rightarrow U_{K}\rightarrow K^{\times}\rightarrow P(K)\rightarrow 0.

Taking

using Corollary

Remark 5.15
.

Studying the structure of
\Ost(E,K/F)
is (much) more difficult than studying that of
\Ost(\mathbb{G}_{m,F},K/F)
. In the case of
\Ost(E,K/F)
one has to take into account the primes of bad reduction for
E
, whereas in the case of
\Ost(\mathbb{G}_{m,F},K/F)

there are no primes of bad reduction
. Indeed, if
T
is an algebraic torus over
F
with Néron model
\mathcal{T}
over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, one says that
T
has
good reduction
at a prime
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
if the connected algebraic
k(\mathfrak{p})
-group
\mathcal{T}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{0}
is a
k(\mathfrak{p})
-torus. When
T=\mathbb{G}_{m,F}
, we have

\mathcal{T}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{0}=(\mathcal{G}_{m}^{0})_{k(\mathfrak{p})}=(\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathcal{O}_{F}})_{k(\mathfrak{p})}=\mathbb{G}_{m,k(\mathfrak{p})},

which is certainly a
k(\mathfrak{p})
-torus for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
. So
\mathbb{G}_{m,F}
has
good reduction everywhere over
F
. Consequently, the complications that arise in the study of
\Ost(\mathbb{G}_{m,F},K/F)
come
mainly from the primes of
F
that ramify in
K
. Further,
T=\mathbb{G}_{m,F}
is just
one
(very simple)
1
-dimensional algebraic group with very simple reductions everywhere, whereas the class of all elliptic curves over
F
is an infinite family of
1
-dimensional algebraic groups whose members degenerate (i.e., have bad reduction) at some primes of
F
. The above comments clearly show that the gulf that separates the classical case
\Ost(\mathbb{G}_{m,F},K/F)
from the elliptic case
\Ost(E,K/F)
is
truly vast
.

Although
\Ost(E,K/F)
is the “correct” analog of
\Ost(\mathbb{G}_{m,F},K/F)
, one should also investigate the cokernel of the right-hand vertical map in diagram (
5.29
), i.e., the map
\lambda_{\mathcal{E},K/F}
which is the same localization map
\mathcal{F}
appeared in diagram (
5.2
).

\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)

coincide
. For example, if
j(E)\in\mathbb{Z}
(e.g.,
E
has complex multiplication
[
20
, VII, Exersice 7.10]
), then there exists an extension
K/F
as above such that
E/K
has good reduction at all primes of
K

[
20
, VII, Proposition 5.5]
, whence
E(K)=E_{0}(K)
and (therefore)
\Ost(E,K/F)=\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)
.

The main result of this paper is the following non-trivial structure theorem for the group
\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)
.

Theorem 5.18
.

There exists a canonical exact sequence of abelian groups

(5.32)

0\rightarrow\frac{\Ker(\trans^{E}_{K/F})}{\Image\left(\widetilde{\Res}_{K/F}\right)}\rightarrow\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}\widehat{E(F)}^{*},

where
\widetilde{\Res}_{K/F}^{E}:\Sha(E/F)\rightarrow\Sha(E/K)^{G}
and
\trans^{E}_{K/F}:\Sha(E/K)^{G}\rightarrow H^{2}(G,E(K))
denote the restriction map (
5.11
) and
the transgression map (
5.12
), respectively. Also

\mathcal{I}:\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)\rightarrow\Coker\left(H^{1}(G_{F},E)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\bigoplus_{v\in M_{F}}H^{1}(G_{F_{v}},E)\right)

is the map (
5.5
), and
\widehat{E(F)}^{*}=\Hom_{\cts}(\widehat{E(F)},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
as introduced in Theorem (
5.2
).

Proof.

Immediately follows from Corollary
5.8
, since
\Image(\mathcal{I})\subseteq\widehat{E(F)}^{*}
by Theorem
5.2
.
∎

5.4.
Investigating
\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)
for quadratic extensions

In this section, for the class of curves
E
relative to quadratic extensions
K/F
, we use some results of Qiu
[
16
]
and Yu
[
22
]
, to find some information about the left-hand group and the image of the map
\mathcal{I}
in exact sequence (
5.32
). Then as application of Theorem
5.18
, we give the structure of
\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)
for some explicit examples of
(E,K/F)
in this family.

Theorem 5.19
.

For a number field
F
, let
E/F
be an elliptic curve and
K=F(\sqrt{D})
be a quadratic extension of
F
with
Gal(K/F)=<\sigma>
. Denote by
E_{D}
the quadratic
D
-twist of
E
. If the Tate-Shafarevich groups
\Sha(E/F)
,
\Sha(E_{D}/F)
and
\Sha(E/K)
are finite and (
5.15
) is exact, then

(5.33)

\#\Ker(\trans^{E}_{K/F})=\frac{\#\Sha(E/K).\left(E(F):N^{E}_{K/F}(E(K))\right)}{\#\Sha(E_{D}/F)},

where
N^{E}_{K/F}\left(E(K)\right)=\{P+\sigma(P):P\in E(K)\}
.

Proof.

From (
5.15
) we have

(5.34)

\#\Ker(\trans^{E}_{K/F})=\frac{\#\Sha(E/F).\prod_{v\in M_{F}}H^{1}\left(G_{w},E(K_{w})\right)}{\#H^{1}\left(G,E(K)\right)}.

On the other hand, under finiteness assumption of Tate-Shafarevich groups, Yu
[
22
, Main Theorem]
proved

(5.35)

\frac{\#\Sha(E/F).\#\Sha(E_{D}/F)}{\#\Sha(E/K)}=\frac{\#\widehat{H}^{0}(G,E(K)).\#H^{1}(G,E(K))}{\prod_{v\in M_{F}}\#H^{1}\left(G_{w},E(K_{w})\right)}.

Since
\#\widehat{H}^{0}(G,E(K))=\left(E(F):N^{E}_{K/F}(E(K))\right)
, using equations (
5.34
) and (
5.35
) we obtain the desired equality.
∎

The example below shows that the exactness of (
5.15
), equivalently
\Image(\mathcal{I})=0
by Theorem
5.6
, is a necessary condition in Theorem
5.19
.

Example 5.20
.

For the elliptic curve

E/\mathbb{Q}:y^{2}=x^{3}-x+1/4

and the imaginary quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
with

\displaystyle D\in\{

\displaystyle-7,-11,-47,-71,-83,-84,-127,-159,-164,-219,-231,

\displaystyle-263,-271,-287,-292,-303,-308,-359,-371,-404,-443,-447,-471\},

we have
\Sha(E/K)=\Sha(E_{D}/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\left(E(\mathbb{Q}):N^{E}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(E(K))\right)=2
, see
[
16
, part (2) of Theorem 4.1]
. In particular,
\Ker(\trans^{E}_{K/\mathbb{Q}})=0
and equality (
5.33
) doesn’t hold. Hence by Theorem
5.19
, we conclude that for the given elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and the imaginary quadratic fields
K
, (
5.15
) can not be exact. Theorem
5.6
shows that the map

\mathcal{I}:\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)\rightarrow\Coker(\mathcal{G})

is nonzero. Also since
\Ker(\trans^{E}_{K/\mathbb{Q}})=0
, by Corollary
5.8
the map
\mathcal{I}
is injective. Now
by
[
22
, Lemma 5]
, one has

\#\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)=\#\Image(\mathcal{I})\leq\#\widehat{H}^{0}(G,E(K))=\left(E(\mathbb{Q}):N^{E}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(E(K))\right)=2.

Consequently,

\Ost_{c}(E,K/F)\simeq\Image(\mathcal{I})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\Coker(\mathcal{G})\simeq\widehat{E(\mathbb{Q})}^{*}=\Hom_{\cts}(\widehat{E(\mathbb{Q})},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

where
\widehat{E(\mathbb{Q})}
is defined as in Theorem
5.2
. Note that
E(\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, see
[
16
, proof of Theorem 4.1]
.

Proposition 5.21
.

[
16
,

1–2]

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve defined over
F
, and
K=F(\sqrt{D})
be a quadratic extension of

with
G=Gal(K/F)=<\sigma>
. Denote by
E_{D}
the quadratic
D
-twist of
E
. Also denote by
r_{F}
and
r_{D,F}
the
\mathbb{Z}
-rank of
E(F)

The following assertions hold.

(i)

\#H^{1}\left(G,E(K)\right)=2^{r_{D,F}-r_{F}}.\left(E(F):N^{E}_{K/F}(E(K))\right)

(ii)

Assume that
\Sha(E/F)

\Sha(E_{D}/F)

(ii-1)

(5.36)

\frac{\#\Sha(E/F).\#\Sha(E_{D}/F)}{\#\Sha(E/K)}=2^{r_{D,F}-r_{F}-\delta(E,F,K)}.\left(E(F):N(E(K))\right)^{2};

.

Keep the same assumptions and notations as in Proposition

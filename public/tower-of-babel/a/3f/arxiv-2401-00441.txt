Quantitative unique continuation for real-valued solutions to second order elliptic equations in the plane
Kévin Le Balc’h, Diego A. Souza

Abstract
In this article, we study a quantitative form of the Landis conjecture on exponential decay for real-valued solutions to second order elliptic equations with variable coefficients in the plane. In particular, we prove the following qualitative form of Landis conjecture, for W 1 , W 2 ∈ L ∞ ​ ( ℝ 2 , ℝ 2 ) W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}) , V ∈ L ∞ ​ ( ℝ 2 , ℝ ) V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}) and u ∈ H loc 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) a real-valued weak solution to − Δ ​ u − ∇ ⋅ ( W 1 ​ u ) + W 2 ⋅ ∇ u + V ​ u = 0 -\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(W_{1}u)+W_{2}\cdot\nabla u+Vu=0 in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , satisfying for δ > 0 \delta>0 , | u ⁡ ( x ) | ⩽ exp ⁡ ( − | x | 1 + δ ) |u(x)|\leqslant\exp(-|x|^{1+\delta}) , x ∈ ℝ 2 x\in\mathbb{R}^{2} , then u ≡ 0 u\equiv 0 . Our methodology of proof is inspired by the one recently developed by Logunov, Malinnikova, Nadirashvili, and Nazarov that have treated the equation − Δ ​ u + V ​ u = 0 -\Delta u+Vu=0 in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . Nevertheless, several differences and additional difficulties appear. New weak quantitative maximum principles are established for the construction of a positive multiplier in a suitable perforated domain, depending on the nodal set of u u . The resulted divergence elliptic equation is then transformed into a non-homogeneous ∂ z ¯ \partial_{\overline{z}} equation thanks to a generalization of Stoilow factorization theorem obtained by the theory of quasiconformal mappings, an approximate type Poincaré lemma and the use of the Cauchy transform. Finally, a suitable Carleman estimate applied to the operator ∂ z ¯ \partial_{\overline{z}} is the last ingredient of our proof.



1
Introduction

1.1
Qualitative and quantitative unique continuation at infinity

In the late 1960’s, see
[
KL88
]
, Landis conjectured the following: for
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\delta>0
,

(-\Delta u+Vu=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\ \text{and}\ |u(x)|\leqslant\exp(-|x|^{1+\delta})\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\Rightarrow\quad u\equiv 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.1)

One can see (
1.1
) as a
qualitative unique continuation
property at infinity. The decay rate
\exp(-|x|^{1+\delta})
seems to be a natural barrier, by considering the function
u(x)=\exp(-C\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}})
for a suitable constant
C>0
. Moreover, (
1.1
) holds when
N=1
by an ordinary differential argument, see for instance
[
Ros21
]
or
[
LB21
, Introduction]
.

Landis conjecture was first disproved by Meshkov in 1991 in the case of complex-valued potentials
V
. In fact, the work
[
Mes91
]
exhibits in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
a counterexample to (
1.1
):

\exists V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C})\ \text{and}\ u\not\equiv 0,\ -\Delta u+Vu=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\ \text{and}\ |u(x)|\leqslant\exp(-|x|^{4/3})\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.2)

[
Mes91
]
also shows that this is the right scale, proving the qualitative unique continuation property at infinity: for
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\delta>0
, we have

(-\Delta u+Vu=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\ \text{and}\ |u(x)|\leqslant\exp(-|x|^{4/3+\delta})\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\Rightarrow\quad u\equiv 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.3)

In their work on Anderson localization
[
BK05
]
, Bourgain and Kenig establish a
quantitative version
of Meshkov’s result, that is assuming that
-\Delta u+Vu=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
\|V\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and
\|u\|_{\infty}=|u(0)|=1
, then for
C,C^{\prime}>0
sufficiently large

\sup_{B(x_{0},1)}|u(x)|\geqslant\exp(-CR^{4/3}\log(R))\qquad\forall R\geqslant C^{\prime},\ \forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ \text{with}\ |x_{0}|=R.

(1.4)

The case of real-valued potentials has been addressed in
[
BK05
]
and is more tricky. We may first ask if the qualitative Landis conjecture (
1.1
) holds for real-valued bounded potentials
V
. Then, we may wonder if the quantitative Landis conjecture holds for real-valued potentials, i.e. if (
1.4
) holds replacing
4/3
by
1
. The difficulty for tackling such a question comes from the fact that Carleman estimates do not seem to distinguish between real-valued and complex-valued solutions to elliptic equations.

A first breakthrough was achieved in
[
KSW15
]
, regarding the quantitative unique continuation at infinity in the plane. Assuming that
-\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(Wu)+Vu=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
-\Delta u+W\cdot\nabla u+Vu=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with
W\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
,
\|W\|\leqslant 1
,
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
,
0\leqslant V\leqslant 1
and
\|u\|_{\infty}=|u(0)|=1
, then for
C,C^{\prime}>0
sufficiently large

\sup_{B(x_{0},1)}|u(x)|\geqslant\exp(-CR\log(R))\qquad\forall R\geqslant C^{\prime},\ \forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ \text{with}\ |x_{0}|=R.

(1.5)

Then, subsequent papers established analogous results in the settings of variable coefficients and singular lower-order terms,
[
KW15
,
Dav20
,
DW20
]
, always assuming a sign condition on the zero order term
V
.

A second breakthrough was achieved very recently in the
2
-d case in the work
[
LMNN20
]
by withdrawing the sign condition on the potential
V
, proving in particular (
1.1
) in the real-valued case. More precisely, the authors prove that for
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
, there exists
C>0
sufficiently large such that

(-\Delta u+Vu=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\ \text{and}\ |u(x)|\leqslant\exp(-C|x|\log^{1/2}(1+|x|))\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\Rightarrow\quad u\equiv 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.6)

Actually, the authors prove the following quantitative unique continuation at infinity. Assuming that
-\Delta u+Vu=0\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with
-1\leqslant V\leqslant 1
and
\|u\|_{\infty}=|u(0)|=1
, then for
C,C^{\prime}>0
sufficiently large

\sup_{B(x_{0},1)}|u(x)|\geqslant\exp(-CR\log^{3/2}(R))\qquad\forall R\geqslant C^{\prime},\ \forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ \text{with}\ |x_{0}|=R.

(1.7)

Based on the new idea coming from
[
LMNN20
]
, the goal of this article is to give a positive answer to the Landis conjecture (
1.1
) for real-valued solutions to elliptic equations
-\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(W_{1}u)+W_{2}\cdot\nabla u+Vu=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
and prove a quantitative version of the Landis conjecture in the spirit of (
1.4
), (
1.5
), (
1.7
).

1.2
Main results

The first main result of this paper is the following positive answer to the qualitative Landis conjecture in the plane for real-valued solutions to second order elliptic equations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a real-valued weak solution to

-\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(W_{1}u)+W_{2}\cdot\nabla u+Vu=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.8)

with

W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}).

(1.9)

Assume that there exists
\delta>0
such that

|u(x)|\leqslant\exp(-|x|^{1+\delta})\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.10)

Then,
u\equiv 0
.

Our second main result is the following quantitative unique continuation property at infinity.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a real-valued weak solution to

-\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(W_{1}u)+W_{2}\cdot\nabla u+Vu=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.11)

with

W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}),\ \left\lVert W_{1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 1,\ \left\lVert W_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 1,\ \left\lVert V\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 1.

(1.12)

Assume that

\|u\|_{\infty}=|u(0)|=1.

(1.13)

Then, for every
\delta>0
, there exist a positive constant
C=C(\delta)\geqslant 1
, such that

\sup_{B(x_{0},1)}|u(x)|\geqslant\exp(-CR^{1+\delta})\qquad\forall R\geqslant 2,\ \forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ \text{with}\ |x_{0}|=R.

(1.14)

Theorem

1.1
and

Local quantitative unique continuation property

The following result relates on the vanishing order of real-valued solutions to second order elliptic equations.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(B_{2})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{2})
be a real-valued weak solution to

-\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(W_{1}u)+W_{2}\cdot\nabla u+Vu=0\ \text{in}\ B_{2},

(1.18)

to cut-off version of

Differences of Step 1 in comparison to
[
LMNN20
, Act 1]
.
The main difference is the presence of the drift terms

.

We first prove a weak quantitative maximum principle for
\Phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying
-\Delta\Phi+W\cdot\nabla\Phi=f
with

, where
\Omega
is a bounded open set with small Poincaré constant, see

2.1
below. This is a generalization of the weak quantitative maximum principle
[
LMNN20
, Lemma 6.10]
for the Laplace equation
-\Delta\Phi=f
. In
[
LMNN20
, Section 6.3]
, the authors use De Giorgi method conjugated with the fact that

is the minimizer of the functional
F(\Phi)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}f\Phi
to establish
[
LMNN20
, Lemma 6.10]
. Here, because the operator

is not symmetric, we have to proceed in another way. We instead implement De Giorgi method in the associated variational formulation of the elliptic equation
-\Delta\Phi+W\cdot\nabla\Phi=f
.

We then prove a weak quantitative maximum principle
\Phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying
-\Delta\Phi+W\cdot\nabla\Phi=\nabla\cdot g
with
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is a bounded open set with small Poincaré constant, see
Lemma

2.4
below. This part is new in comparison to
[
LMNN20
]
. For establishing such a result, we first derive precise Sobolev’s inequalities, quantified in function of the Poincaré constant, see
Lemma

2.5

-bound. It is worth mentioning that the

-bound coming from the maximum principle depends on the measure of the open set, that is not the case of the previous maximum principle.

On the one hand, the maximum principles with both

Differences of Step 2 in comparison to
[
LMNN20
, Act 2]
.

The philosophy of this step is the same as
[
LMNN20
, Act 2]
, we try to reduce the divergence elliptic equation
-\nabla\cdot(\varphi^{2}(\nabla v+\hat{W}v))=0
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
to a very simple elliptic equation by using a quasi-conformal change of variable. But here the strategy is rather different.

The problem is that one cannot reduce it to a simple harmonic equation because of the presence of the drift term
\hat{W}
. By working as in
[
LMNN20
, Act 2]
, one can define a local stream
\tilde{v}
function, in an arbitrary ball
B
contained in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
, by using Poincaré lemma. Then, the function
w=v+i\tilde{v}
satisfies a Beltrami equation

\partial_{\overline{z}}w=\mu\partial_{z}w-\overset{\diamond}{W}w\ \text{in}\ B\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},

with

|\mu|\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2/(2+\delta)}\left\lVert W_{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}+C\varepsilon^{2}\|V\|_{\infty}\ \text{and}\ \|\overset{\diamond}{W}\|_{\infty}\leqslant C\|W_{1}\|_{\infty}+C\|W_{2}\|_{\infty}.

One can then introduce a quasiconformal mapping
L:B_{2}\to B_{2}
, satisfying
\partial_{\overline{z}}L=\mu\partial_{z}L
. An adaptation of the Stoilow factorization theorem then leads to the equation (
1.29
) satisfied by
h=v\circ L^{-1}
, see
Lemma

3.2
below.

One needs to reduce a bit more because of the presence of the new drift term
\tilde{W}
. In order to do this, we first establish that
L^{-1}\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(B_{2})
for every
1\leqslant p<+\infty
so that for
c_{0}>0
,
\|\tilde{W}\|_{L^{p}(B_{2-c_{0}})}\leqslant C\|\overset{\diamond}{W}\|_{\infty}
, see
Lemma

3.3
below. We then introduce a cut-off
\chi
near the images of the disks of the perforated domain, and near the boundary of
B_{2}
to recast (
1.29
) into a divergence elliptic equation, with a non-homogeneous source term

-\nabla\cdot(\chi(\nabla h+\tilde{W}h))=-\nabla\chi\cdot(\nabla h+\tilde{W}h)\ \text{in}\ B_{2},

(1.37)

see
Lemma

3.4
below. The advantage of such a procedure is that we are now working in the simply connected domain
B_{2}
and one can then deduce an approximate type Poincaré lemma to define
\tilde{h}
where

\chi(\nabla h+\tilde{W}h)=\mathrm{curl}(\tilde{h})+\text{error term}\ \text{in}\ B_{2},

(1.38)

see
Lemma

3.5
below. One can compare this procedure to the one employed in
[
KSW15
, Section 5]
for proving the Landis conjecture in an exterior domain. Now, one can observe that

satisfies the following Beltrami equation

\partial_{\overline{z}}\gamma=\alpha\gamma+\text{error term}\ \text{in}\ B_{2},\ \left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{2})}\leqslant C\|\tilde{W}\|_{L^{p}(B_{2-c})},

(1.39)

see
Lemma

3.6

with
\partial_{\overline{z}}\beta=\alpha

\partial_{\overline{z}}\zeta=\text{error term}\ \text{in}\ B_{2},

(1.40)

see
Lemma

3.9
below. It is worth mentioning that during each step the error term is changing but at the end of this step, it has the following form

\text{error term}=\exp(-\beta)\left[\text{local term}+\text{non local term}\right],

(1.41)

where both local term and non-local term involve the values of

near the images of the disk of the perforation. The particularity of the non-local term is contained in the fact that it is local in the angular variable while it is non-local in the radial variable. This is due to the fact that the approximate stream function has been introduced with respect to polar coordinates in prevision of the next step. We also formulate well-known properties for the Cauchy transform, i.e.
L^{\infty}
bound on
\beta
and Hölder’s estimate on
\beta
in function of the
L^{p}
bound on
\alpha

3.8
below.

Differences of Step 3 in comparison to
[
LMNN20
, Act 3]
.
Here, we do not follow
[
LMNN20
, Act 3]
because we have not reduced our equation to a harmonic equation. Our strategy takes its inspiration in

We apply a Carleman estimate to a cut-off version of

\partial B_{2}
and near the observation set

Step 1: Construction of a positive multiplier in a suitable perforated domain

The main goal of this step is to construct a positive multiplier of the following equation

2.1
Weak quantitative maximum principles

The goal of this first part is to prove maximum principles for elliptic operators in an open bounded set

-source terms, see

The goal of this part is to obtain a Poincaré lemma for the divergence free vector field

We introduce a smooth cut-off function

and value

(3.43)

We have the following lemma, coming directly from (
3.16

Lemma

3.3

Lemma 3.4
.

The function
\chi h
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
belongs to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and satisfies in the weak sense

-\nabla\cdot(\chi(\nabla h+\tilde{W}h))=-\nabla\chi\cdot(\nabla h+\tilde{W}h)\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

(3.44)

For the next, we need to recast the divergence elliptic equation in polar coordinates. For that purpose, we recall well-known useful relations between Cartesian coordinates and polar coordinates in the following paragraph.

From Cartesian to polar coordinates.
By taking
e_{1}=(1,0)
and
e_{2}=(0,1)
the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for
(\rho,\theta)\in[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi)
, we set
e_{\rho}=\cos(\theta)e_{1}+\sin(\theta)e_{2},\ e_{\theta}=-\sin(\theta)e_{1}+\cos(\theta)e_{2},
therefore we have the relation
e_{1}=\cos(\theta)e_{\rho}-\sin(\theta)e_{\theta},\ e_{2}=\sin(\theta)e_{\rho}+\cos(\theta)e_{\theta}
. We then have that
(e_{\rho},e_{\theta})
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Given a function
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we then define

U(\rho,\theta)=u(\rho\cos(\theta),\rho\sin(\theta)),\qquad(\rho,\theta)\in[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi).

(3.45)

By the chain rule, we have the following relation for
\nabla u=\partial_{x_{1}}ue_{1}+\partial_{x_{2}}ue_{2}

\nabla u=\partial_{\rho}Ue_{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\theta}Ue_{\theta},

(3.46)

and for
\text{curl}(u)=\partial_{x_{2}}ue_{1}-\partial_{x_{1}}ue_{2}
,

\text{curl}(u)=\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\theta}Ue_{\rho}-\partial_{\rho}Ue_{\theta}.

(3.47)

Given now a vector valued-function
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, setting

g(\rho\cos(\theta),\rho\sin(\theta))=G_{\rho}(\rho,\theta)e_{\rho}+G_{\theta}(\rho,\theta)e_{\theta},

we then have by the chain rule applied to
\nabla\cdot g=\partial_{x_{1}}g_{1}+\partial_{x_{2}}g_{2}
that

\nabla\cdot g=\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\left(\rho G_{\rho}\right)+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\theta}\left(G_{\theta}\right).

(3.48)

In the following, for the sake of simplicity, we will make an abuse of notation by identifying
u
with
U

We have the following result.

Lemma 3.5

-\partial_{\rho}(\rho F_{n,\rho})\\
-\partial_{\theta}F_{n,\theta}\\

5.2
A Carleman estimate in a bounded open set with small Poincaré constant

The goal of this part is to present a specific two-dimensional Carleman estimate in a bounded open set with small Poincaré constant. This type of estimate seems to be new but unfortunately we have not found applications of it. We hope that it can be useful for the reader in another context.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,
C^{\prime}\geqslant 1
, there exists
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that for every bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
C_{P}(\Omega)^{2}\leqslant(C^{\prime})^{2}\varepsilon^{2}
, for every
s\geqslant 1
, for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
such that
-\Delta\varphi\geqslant s\geqslant 1
, for every
u\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, we have

\varepsilon^{-4}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi}|u|^{2}+s^{2}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi}|u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi}|\nabla u|^{2}+s\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi}|\nabla u|^{2}\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi}|\Delta u|^{2}.

(5.7)

The idea behind this was to exploit this Carleman estimate in the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, defined in (
2.56
) that has a small Poincaré constant. But even the solution
u
to
-\Delta u+Vu=0
belongs to
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, we do not have
u\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
that prevents the application of such an inequality.

Proof.

We can assume that
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
by density.

By
Lemma

2.1
, one can construct
\Phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
-\Delta\Phi=\varepsilon^{-2}\text{ in }\Omega
and
\Phi
satisfies
\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant C
. Then we directly apply
[
DF90
, Proposition 2.2]
with the weight
e^{-\varphi+\Phi}
to get

\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi+\Phi}|u|^{2}+s\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi+\Phi}|u|^{2}\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi+\Phi}|\overline{\partial}u|^{2}.

(5.8)

Now, because
u
is real-valued,
|\overline{\partial}u|=|\partial u|=|\nabla u|
, so one can apply
[
DF90
, Proposition 2.2]
to
\partial{u}
,

\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi+\Phi}|\partial u|^{2}+s\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi+\Phi}|\partial u|^{2}\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi+\Phi}|\overline{\partial}\partial u|^{2}=C\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varphi+\Phi}|\Delta u|^{2}

(5.9)

The combination of the last two estimates and the fact that
\Phi
is uniformly bounded give the result.

estimate for second order elliptic equations with divergence drift term.

Lemma A.1
.

For every
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists
C>0
such that for every
W\in L^{p}(B_{2})
, for every
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(B_{2})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{2})
satisfying

-\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(Wu)=0\ \text{in}\ B_{2},

(A.1)

then,

\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leqslant C\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{2})}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}.

(A.2)

Proof.

This is a direct application of
[
Mey63
, Theorem 2]
, stating that if
u
is a weak solution to

-\Delta u=\nabla\cdot g\ \text{in}\ B_{2},

then we have the local estimate

\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leqslant C\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{2})}+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{2})}.

(A.3)

This concludes the proof by taking
g=Wu
.
∎

We have the following local
W^{1,p}
estimate for second order elliptic equations with divergence drift term at scale
\varepsilon
.

Lemma A.2
.

For every
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
c>0
,
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that for every
W\in L^{p}(B_{2\varepsilon})
, satisfying

\varepsilon+\varepsilon\|W\|_{L^{p}(B_{2\varepsilon})}\leqslant c,

(A.4)

and
for every
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(B_{2})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{2})
satisfying

-\Delta u-\nabla\cdot(Wu)=0\ \text{in}\ B_{2\varepsilon},

(A.5)

then we have

\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon})}\leqslant C\varepsilon^{-1}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\varepsilon})}.

(A.6)

Proof.

By
[
Mey63
, Theorem 2]
and a scaling argument, if

-\Delta u=\nabla\cdot g\ \text{in}\ B_{2\varepsilon},

then we have the local estimate

\varepsilon\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon})}\leqslant C\varepsilon\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{2\varepsilon})}+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{2\varepsilon})}.

(A.7)

Taking
g=Wu
leads to the conclusion.
∎

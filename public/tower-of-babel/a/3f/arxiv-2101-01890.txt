Equivariant Spectral Flow and Equivariant η \eta -invariants on Manifolds with Boundary


Abstract
. In this article, we study several closely related invariants associated to Dirac operators on odd-dimensional manifolds with boundary with an action of the compact group H H of isometries. In particular, the equality between equivariant winding numbers, equivariant spectral flow, and equivariant Maslov indices is established. We also study equivariant η \eta -invariants which play a fundamental role in the equivariant analog of Getzler’s spectral flow formula. As a consequence, we establish a relation between equivariant η \eta -invariants and equivariant Maslov triple indices in the splitting of manifolds.

1.
Introduction

Back in the 1970s, in a series of their seminal papers
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, Atiyah, Patodi, and Singer (APS) established a well-known index theorem for Dirac operators on even-dimensional compact manifolds with boundary, for which, if compared with the case of closed manifolds (cf.
[
4
]
), a
boundary correction term
arises that comprises the
\eta
-invariant and the kernel of some tangential operator. Not long after, Donnelly
[
13
]
extended the APS index theorem to the case of compact group actions. About three decades later, Dai and Zhang (DZ) in
[
11
]
provided a candidate for the counterpart of the APS index theorem where they considered a certain variant of Toeplitz operators on odd-dimensional manifolds with even-dimensional boundary, and proved an index formula taking the form
\mathrm{Ind}(T_{g})=\displaystyle\int\alpha-\bar{\eta}_{DZ}+\tau^{\mu}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot),
where
\alpha
is some local index forms,
\bar{\eta}_{DZ}
is an even-type
\eta
-invariant specifically defined for an even-dimensional boundary, and
\tau^{\mu}
is some Maslov triple index (cf.
[
17
]
). It is then natural to ask about an equivariant analog of DZ, or in other words, an odd analog of Donnelly’s equivariant index theorem. In
[
18
]
, we carried out the study and established an
equivariant Toeplitz index theorem
, extending DZ’s work to the case of compact group actions. In particular, it is necessary to define new notions, including equivariant DZ
\eta
-invariants, equivariant spectral flows, equivariant Maslov triple indices, etc.
This leads to the main theme of this article: to establish the equivariant extension of some classical invariants, which will serve as a prequel to
[
18
]
.
We emphasize that the main reference for the non-equivariant objects of study here is the work by Kirk and Lesch in
[
17
]
. They elegantly showed the interlinked connections between
\eta
-invariants, spectral flows, Maslov (double and triple) indices,
\zeta
-determinants, and others.

In this article, firstly, we study an equivariant version of the classical
spectral flow
first introduced by APS in
[
3
]
. For our purposes, we adopt a useful machinery by Phillips
[
23
]
for an equivalent description of spectral flows in terms of grid partitions.
The upshot is that the complication of counting the crossings is simplified by merely considering the intersection of a spectral path with the vertical “wall” of each partitioned grid.
This is then generalised to the equivariant case (Definition
3.2
) where it takes value in trace when evaluated over a grid partition.
Under this setting, the equivariant spectral flow is well-defined (Proposition
3.4
) and is an isomorphism (Theorem
3.5
) between the
H
-equivariant fundamental group of the non-trivial component of the algebra of self-adjoint equivariant Fredholm operators and the representation ring
R(H).

On the other hand, Getzler
[
15
]
shows that the spectral flow of a path can be expressed in terms of APS’s
\eta
-invariants
[
1
,
2
]
, which comprises the evaluation at the endpoints and the variation of
\eta
-invariants along the path. Kirk and Lesch
[
17
]
provide another proof of this (in terms of reduced
\eta
-invariants) via the path-lifting method to the universal cover. In this paper, we generalise this well-known formula to the equivariant case in Theorem
9.5
. Moreover, we also establish the gluing formula (Theorem
9.10
) of equivariant
\eta
-invariants along the boundary splitting via a closed hypersurface. Readers can refer to
[
8
,
21
]
for expositions on gluing formulae for spectral flow and
\eta
-invariants.

Another spectral invariants closely related to
\eta
-invariants are the
\zeta
-function
\zeta(D^{2},s)
of the square of Dirac operators and its variation
\dot{\zeta}(D^{2},s)
evaluated at
s=0,
with
\dot{\zeta}(D^{2},0)
being the most sensitive one in the sense that its value changes upon a perturbation by a smoothing operator, cf.
[
29
]
. We discuss an equivariant generalisation of both of these invariants explicitly in Section
7
, as well as defining the equivariant
\zeta
-determinant of
D
(Definition
7.11
). Inspired by
[
17
]
, we extend our discussion of
\zeta
-determinants to the equivariant Scott-Wojciechowski theorem (Conjecture
8.3
) – we merely state this as a conjecture, instead of a theorem, as it is not our intention to re-prove
[
25
]
in full details in the equivariant settings. Despite this, we reasonably justify why our settings are compatible and extend to theirs.

In addition, we also show an identification (Theorem
9.7
) between the equivariant spectral flow of a path of Dirac operators and the Maslov index of a pair of possibly infinite-dimensional Lagrangian
H
-spaces associated to the Calderón projection and boundary conditions. As an intermediate step, we give a description of the winding number (Definition
5.6
) of equivariant unitary paths associated to a Lagrangian pair, which corresponds to a pair of boundary conditions. These can be viewed as slight generalisations of
[
17
]
, which is itself an infinite version of the classical Maslov index, e.g. in
[
22
,
9
]
.

As a summary, this paper is dedicated in discussing the following correspondences and providing a unified overview of “spectral intersection counting” via different perspectives:

\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize equivariant}\\
\text{\normalsize spectral flow}\end{subarray}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize equivariant}\\
\text{\normalsize Maslov index}\end{subarray}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize equivariant}\\
\text{ \normalsize winding number}\end{subarray}

where the corresponding ingredients are respectively

\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize an equivariant path $D_{P}(t)$}\\
\text{\normalsize connecting $P(t)$ and ${\mathcal{P}}_{M}(t)$}\end{subarray}\;\leftrightarrow\;\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize a pair of $H$-Lagrangians}\\
\text{\normalsize$({\mathcal{L}}_{P}(t),{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}(t))$}\end{subarray}\;\leftrightarrow\;\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize an equivariant path of}\\
\text{ \normalsize unitary operators $T^{*}(t)K(t).$}\end{subarray}

The organisation of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we lay out the basic setups, assumptions, and notations. In Section
3
, we study the equivariant spectral flow of self-adjoint equivariant Fredholm operators via grid partitions. In Section
4
, we study the equivariant Maslov index of pairs of
h
-Lagrangians. In Section
5
, we discuss the equivariant winding number of some associated unitary operators and its relation with equivariant Maslov indices. Combining the notions in Sections
4
and
5
, we study an equivariant analog of Maslov triple indices which can be expressed as an algebraic combination of equivariant Maslov indices associated to three pairs of
H
-Lagrangians. Sections
7
and
8
are dedicated to the study of equivariant
\zeta
-determinants of self-adjoint elliptic equivariant Fredholm operators and its fundamental role in the equivariant analog of Scott-Wojciechowski’s theorem. Lastly, in Section
9
we discuss the equivariant
\eta
-invariants of Dirac operators on manifolds with boundary and its relation with the equivariant Maslov triple index associated to a triple of boundary projections.

Preliminaries

Let
M
be a smooth compact odd-dimensional
{\text{spin}^{c}}
manifold with boundary
\partial M.
Assume that an identification between a neighborhood of
\partial M
and
(-\epsilon,0]\times\partial M
is fixed. Let
E
be a complex vector bundle over
M,
equipped with a Hermitian metric and a unitary connection
\nabla^{E}
. For
S^{c}(TM)
the spin
c
spinor bundle over
M
, consider the tensor product bundle
S^{c}(TM)\otimes E
equipped with a compatible connection. Assume that the spin
c
structure is fixed on
M.
For simplicity, we denote the space of
L^{2}
-sections
L^{2}(S^{c}\otimes E)
by
L^{2}(E)
with the underlying bundle
S^{c}
implicitly implied. Let
D^{E}:L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
be the twisted Dirac operator on
M
which takes the form

(2.1)

D^{E}=\gamma\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+A\Big)

near the cylinder
(-\epsilon,0]\times\partial M
equipped with the product metric. Here,
\gamma:E_{|_{\partial M}}\to E_{|_{\partial M}}
is a bundle isomorphism such that

(2.2)

\gamma^{2}=-{\mathrm{Id}},\;\;\;\gamma^{*}=-\gamma

and
A:L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})\to L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})
is the twisted Dirac operator on
\partial M.
In particular, since
\partial M
is closed,
A
is essentially self-adjoint and has point spectrum.
In view of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),
A
anti-commutes with
\gamma,

(2.3)

\gamma A=-A\gamma.

Since
M
is odd-dimensional,
\gamma
may be assumed to take the form
{\mathrm{Diag}}(i,-i),
see
[
7
, §9]
.
As a consequence, the space
L^{2}(E|_{\partial M})
splits into the direct sum of

{\mathcal{E}}_{i}:=L^{2}(E_{i})=\ker(\gamma-i)\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}:=L^{2}(E_{-i})=\ker(\gamma+i)

whose grading is given by
\gamma.

Let
\{\lambda,\psi_{\lambda}\}
be the spectral decomposition of
A.
Let
P^{+}:L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})\to L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})
be the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) spectral projection defined by

P^{+}(\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})=\sum_{\lambda>0}a_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}.

Let
G
be a compact group of isometries acting on
M
preserving the fixed
{\text{spin}^{c}}
structure. For
h\in G,
we consider
H=\overline{\langle h\rangle}
to be the closed subgroup of
G
generated by
h.

Assumption 1
.

Let
h\in H.
Assume that
h
commutes with the Dirac operator
D.

In view of (
2.1
), it follows that
h\in H
commutes with the self-adjoint operator
A
and the Clifford multiplication
\gamma.
In particular, there is an inner-product-preserving induced action of
h\in H
on the bundle
L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}}).

Let
P:L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})\to L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})
be a projection. Then,
P
is said to be
h
-equivariant
if
P
commutes with
h\in H.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
h\in H.
The
h
-equivariant self-adjoint Fredholm Grassmannian of
A,
denoted by
{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A),
is the set of all
h
-equivariant projections
P:L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})\to L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})
such that

(1)

P
is a zeroth order pseudodifferential projection;

(2)

P^{2}=P
and
P^{*}=P,
i.e.
P
is an orthogonal projection;

(3)

\gamma P\gamma^{*}=1-P,
i.e.
P
is Lagrangian;

(4)

(P,P^{+})
is a Fredholm pair, i.e.
PP^{+}:\text{im}(P^{+})\to\text{im}(P)
is a Fredholm operator.

This is an immediate equivariant adaptation of
[
17
, Definition 2.1]
. Note that the classical APS projection
P^{+}\notin{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A)
if and only if
A
is not invertible. In fact,
A
is in general not invertible in (
2.1
). Now, we modify
P^{+}
by a finite rank projection
P_{{\mathcal{L}}},
i.e.

(2.4)

P^{\partial}({\mathcal{L}}):=P^{+}+P_{{\mathcal{L}}}

where
{\mathcal{L}}\subset\ker(A)
is a Lagrangian subspace such that
\gamma({\mathcal{L}})={\mathcal{L}}^{\perp}\cap\ker(A).
Since
h
commutes with
P_{{\mathcal{L}}}
, its corresponding Lagrangian
{\mathcal{L}}
is an
h
-space for
h\in H.
Then, one can verify that
P^{\partial}({\mathcal{L}})\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A),
which shows that the set
{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A)
is indeed non-empty. The projection
P^{\partial}({\mathcal{L}})
is
a priori
dependent on the choice of a Lagrangian subspace
{\mathcal{L}}.
Henceforth, we shall assume a choice of
{\mathcal{L}}
is fixed and write
P^{\partial}=P^{\partial}({\mathcal{L}}).

Consider the subset

(2.5)

{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\infty}_{h}(A)=\{P\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A)\;|\;P-P^{+}\in\Psi^{-\infty}\}

where
\Psi^{-\infty}
stands for the algebra of smoothing operators. Recall that there is a canonical projection in
{\mathrm{Gr}}(A)
that depends only on
M
and
D,
which is commonly known as the Calderón projection
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
on
M.
It is defined as the orthogonal projection onto the Cauchy-data space

(2.6)

{\mathcal{L}}_{M}:=r\{\ker D:H_{1/2}(E)\to H_{-1/2}(E)\}\subset L^{2}(E|_{\partial M})

where
r
denotes the restriction to the boundary. See
[
7
]
. By assumption,
h
commutes with
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}.
This
h
-equivariant Calderón projection is an element of
{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A).
In fact, an equivariant refinement obtained from
[
24
]
implies that
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}\in{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\infty}_{h}(A).

Let
P\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A)
be a self-adjoint spectral projection with respect to the boundary (tangential) operator. Then, the operator
D
equipped with
P
whose domain restricts to

\text{Dom}(D_{P})=\{s\in L^{2}(E)\;|\;s\in H^{1}(E),P(s_{|_{\partial M}})=0\},

is self-adjoint and Fredholm.

As explained in
[
17
, §2]
, every orthogonal projection
P\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A)
takes the explicit form

(2.7)

P=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{Id}}&T^{*}\\
T&{\mathrm{Id}}\end{pmatrix}

for some odd unitary operator
T:{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}.
On the other hand, given such an odd unitary operator

the matrix in
T
on the right side of (
2.7
) satisfies all four conditions in Definition
2.1
. We shall use the notation
T
and
\Phi(P)
interchangeably. The following lemma is a characterisation of

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
h\in H.
Let
P\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A).
By Assumption
1
, the isometry
h
can be characterized by the form

(2.8)

h=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&\Phi(P)a\Phi(P)^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

As
h
acts as an isometry on
M,
which lifts to act on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded
Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(E)=L^{2}_{+}(E)\oplus L^{2}_{-}(E)
and
L^{2}(E_{|_{\partial M}})=L^{2}_{+}(E_{|_{\partial M}})\oplus L^{2}_{-}(E_{|_{\partial M}})={\mathcal{E}}_{i}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{-i},
whose gradings are given by
\gamma,
we write
h
in the form

h=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

Let
T=\Phi(P).
Note that

hP=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{Id}}&T^{*}\\
T&{\mathrm{Id}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a+bT&b+aT^{*}\\
c+dT&d+cT^{*}\end{pmatrix}

and

Ph=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{Id}}&T^{*}\\
T&{\mathrm{Id}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a+T^{*}c&b+T^{*}d\\
c+Ta&d+Tb\end{pmatrix}

By the commutative relation
Ph=hP,
we obtain

h=\begin{pmatrix}a&T^{*}cT^{*}\\
TbT&TaT^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

On the other hand, by Assumption 1,
h
must also commute with
\gamma={\mathrm{Diag}}(i,-i)
which satisfies (
2.2
). Then, we can verify by a direct computation that
h
commutes with
\gamma
if and only if

TbT=0
and
T^{*}cT^{*}=0.
Thus, the isometry
h
is a diagonal matrix

h=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&TaT^{*}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}}_{i})\oplus{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}}_{-i}).

Remark 2.3
.

In general, the explicit form of the isometry

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
h={\mathrm{Diag}}(a,WaW^{*})\in H.
Let
P,Q\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}(A)
be given by (
2.7
), i.e.

P=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{Id}}&T^{*}\\
T&{\mathrm{Id}}\end{pmatrix},\quad Q=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{Id}}&S^{*}\\
S&{\mathrm{Id}}\end{pmatrix}

for some odd unitary operators
T,S,W:{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}.
Then,

(1)

(P,Q)
is a Fredholm pair if and only if
-1\notin{\text{spec}}_{ess}((a-{\mathrm{Id}})+T^{*}S)),

(2)

(P,Q)
is an invertible pair (i.e.
PQ:\operatorname{im}(Q)\to\operatorname{im}(P)
is invertible) if and only if
-1\notin{\text{spec}}((a-{\mathrm{Id}})+aT^{*}S),

(3)

\ker(P)\cap\operatorname{im}(Q)\cong\ker(a({\mathrm{Id}}+T^{*}S)),

(4)

h(P-Q)\in\Psi^{-\infty}
if and only if
({\mathrm{Id}}-T^{*}S)a\in\Psi^{-\infty}.

Proof.

We follow an argument of
[
17
]
. Let
L^{2}(E|_{\partial M})={\mathcal{E}}_{i}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}.
Then, any element in
L^{2}(E|_{\partial M})
can be written as
(x,Sy)^{T}
for
x,y\in{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
and for some
S:{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}.
One verifies that
\operatorname{im}(Q)
is the set
\{(x,Sx)^{T}\;|\;x\in{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}.
Then, the composition
h(PQ)
takes the form

(2.9)

hPQ=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}a({\mathrm{Id}}+T^{*}S)\\
Ta({\mathrm{Id}}+T^{*}S)\end{pmatrix}

where we use the fact that both

and its adjoint

commute with

Hence,
(P,Q)
is a Fredholm pair if and only if

is Fredholm, which is exactly when

Similarly,
(P,Q)
is an invertible pair if and only if the condition
-1\notin{\text{spec}}((a-{\mathrm{Id}})+aT^{*}S)
holds. For (3), in view of (
2.9
) the intersection

coincides with
\ker(a({\mathrm{Id}}+T^{*}S)).
For the last part, one verifies that

(2.10)

h(P-Q)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&a(T^{*}-S^{*})\\
(T-S)a&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since

and

(T-S)a
is smoothing if and only if

is smoothing.

3.
Equivariant spectral flow

Let
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
be the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
of all self-adjoint equivariant Fredholm operators. By
[
5
]
, the space
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
consists of three connected components:

(3.1)

\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})=\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{+}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})\oplus\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{-}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})\oplus\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{*}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}).

Here,
\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{+}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})

f(i)=f^{\prime}(i)
and

be two paths in

such that

Then,

\tau_{h}(f(t),g(t))=\tau_{h}(f^{\prime}(t),g^{\prime}(t)).

(3)

(Path additivity) Consider

f_{2}(2t-1),\;\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Let

and

(4)

Let

Let

and

be two constant paths in

Equivariant Maslov triple index

Inspired by

Definition 6.1
.

Let

Let

such that every pair

-Fredholm and at least one of the differences

and

be the associated unitary operators of

and

. Moreover, equivariant Maslov triple indices can be expressed as some mixed terms of equivariant Maslov double indices:

(6.2)

\displaystyle\tau^{h}_{\mu}

\displaystyle(P_{1},Q_{1},N_{1})-\tau^{h}_{\mu}(P_{0},Q_{0},N_{0})

Proposition 6.2
.

For

,

satisfies the following properties.

(6.3)

\displaystyle\tau^{h}_{\mu}(Q_{t},P_{t},N_{t})

\displaystyle=-\tau^{h}_{\mu}(P_{t},Q_{t},N_{t})+\tau_{h}(T^{*}_{1}S_{1},(T^{*}_{1}S_{1})^{-1})

(6.4)

\displaystyle\tau^{h}_{\mu}(P_{t},N_{t},Q_{t})

\displaystyle=-\tau^{h}_{\mu}(P_{t},Q_{t},N_{t})+\tau_{h}(S^{*}_{1}R_{1},(S^{*}_{1}R_{1})^{-1})

(6.5)

\displaystyle\tau^{h}_{\mu}(N_{t},Q_{t},P_{t})

\displaystyle=-\tau^{h}_{\mu}(P_{t},Q_{t},N_{t})+\tau_{h}(T^{*}_{1}S_{1},(T^{*}_{1}S_{1})^{-1})

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+\tau_{h}(S^{*}_{1}R_{1},(S^{*}_{1}R_{1})^{-1})-\tau_{h}(T^{*}_{1}R_{1},(T^{*}_{1}R_{1})^{-1}).

Proof.

For (

\displaystyle\tau^{h}_{\mu}(P_{t},Q_{t},N_{t})+\tau^{h}_{\mu}(Q_{t},P_{t},N_{t})

\displaystyle=\tau_{h}(T^{*}_{t}S_{t},S^{*}_{t}R_{t})+\tau_{h}(S^{*}_{t}T_{t},T^{*}_{t}R_{t})

\displaystyle=w_{h}(T^{*}_{t}S_{t})+w_{h}(S^{*}_{t}R_{t})-w_{h}(T^{*}_{t}R_{t})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+w_{h}(S^{*}_{t}T_{t})+w_{h}(T^{*}_{t}R_{t})-w_{h}(S^{*}_{t}R_{t})

\displaystyle=\tau_{h}(T^{*}_{1}S_{1},(T^{*}_{1}S_{1})^{-1}).

Equalities (

Remark 7.3
.

It was shown in
[

]
and

For

By an argument of partition of unity, the trace

(7.5)

{\mathrm{Tr}}(he^{-tD})\sim t^{-n/d}(a_{0}(D)+a_{1}(D)t^{1/d}+\cdots+a_{N}(D)t^{N/d}+O(t^{(N+1)/d}))

h
-equivariant elliptic differential operator

and each

Lemma 7.4
.

For
h\in H,
the
h
-equivariant
\zeta
-function of
D
is the Mellin transform of
{\mathrm{Tr}}(he^{-tD}).

Proof.

For
\lambda_{p_{i}}\leq\lambda_{p_{i+1}}\leq\cdots<0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots,
the trace
{\mathrm{Tr}}(he^{-tD})
is given by the sum
\sum^{\infty}_{k=p_{i}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}.
Set
\tau=\lambda t.
Then, we compute

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{s-1}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})e^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\tau^{s-1}}{\lambda^{s-1}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})e^{-\tau}\lambda^{-1}d\tau=\frac{{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})}{\lambda^{s}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\tau^{s-1}e^{-\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})\lambda^{-s}\Gamma(s).

Thus,

\displaystyle\zeta_{h}(D,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})\lambda^{-s}_{k}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{k}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{s-1}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{s-1}\sum_{k}\Big({\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}\Big)dt=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{s-1}{\mathrm{Tr}}(he^{-tD})dt.

∎

In contrast to
\eta
-functions, the
\zeta
-function is often defined for unbounded operators such as the square of Dirac operators whose discrete spectrum is positive. In this case, the appropriate heat trace is the
renormalised heat trace

{\mathrm{Tr}}(he^{-tD|_{P^{+}}})=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=p_{i}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}-\sum^{0}_{k=p_{i}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}.

Corollary 7.5
.

For
h\in H,
the
h
-equivariant
\zeta
-function of the operator
D|_{P^{+}}
is given by

(7.6)

\zeta_{h}(D,s)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})\lambda_{k}^{-s}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{s-1}{\mathrm{Tr}}(he^{-tD|_{P^{+}}})dt.

Remark 7.6
.

In this case, the renormalised heat trace also has an asymptotic expansion as
t\to 0
which is in general different than that of (
7.5
). In particular, the corresponding coefficient
\tilde{a}_{i}(D^{2})

takes the form

\tilde{a}_{i}(D)=a_{i}(D)-\frac{1}{r!}\sum^{0}_{k=n_{p}}(-{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})\lambda_{k})^{r}

when

The existence and an explicit description of the expansion were shown by Donnelly
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
. Moreover, by a slight generalisation of Gilkey’s result
[
16
]
, the residue of

at

is

(7.7)

\text{Res}_{s=(n-i)/d}(\Gamma(s)\zeta_{h}(D,s))=\tilde{a}_{i}(D).

At
s=0,
the gamma function is known to have a Laurent expansion

(7.8)

\Gamma(s)=\frac{1}{s}+\gamma+p(s)

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant and
p(s)
is the part of the expansion with positive powers of
s.
As it has no pole at

the function
\zeta_{h}(D,s)
is regular at

The constant term is

(7.9)

\zeta_{h}(D):=\zeta_{h}(D,0)=\text{Res}_{s=0}(\Gamma(s)\zeta_{h}(D,s))=\tilde{a}_{n}(D)

for

We shall call
\zeta_{h}(D)
the

-equivariant
\zeta
-invariant of

This is a slight generalisation of

8.
Equivariant Scott-Wojciechowski Theorem

Next, we conjecture an equivariant adaptation of the Scott-Wojciechowski Theorem which, roughly speaking, allows us to express the ratio of the
h
-equivariant
\zeta
-determinants of the Dirac operators (associated with two boundary projecitons) as the Fredholm determinant of the respective unitaries associated to the projections. The original version was first introduced by Scott and Wojciechowski
[
25
]
. Using this, Kirk and Lesch proved a remarkable relation between the difference of the
\eta
-invariants of Dirac operators (associated with two different boundary projections) and the Fredholm determinant of the corresponding unitary operators, see
[
17
, §4]
.

To begin with, we propose a suitable modification to the settings in
[
25
]
. The main reason for such a modification is due to the convention that we have used in our definitions above such as the equivariant winding number
w_{h}
and the equivariant double index
\tau_{h}
. For instance, in our case the unitary operator under the
h
action is taken to be
aT^{*}S
acting from
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
to
{\mathcal{E}}_{i};
whilst in the case of
[
25
]
the unitary operator is acting from
{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}
to
{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}.
If this was the case, then it would imply the unitary operators are acted on by
WaW^{*},
which creates unwanted complication. In the following, we
do not intent
to prove the equivariant version of the Scott-Wojciechowski Theorem in details, but only to lay out some suggestions of our modification to their settings. Therefore, we will leave out most of the original details out for the interested readers.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}=L^{2}(H,{\mathcal{H}})
be our model Hilbert space as defined by (
2.11
). Let
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
be the space of equivariant Fredholm operators on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
be the connected component of index zero. Fix
B\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}).
Set

{\mathcal{F}}_{B}={\mathcal{F}}_{B}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})=\{S\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})\;|\;S-B\text{ is trace-class}\}.

Fix a trace-class operator
\mathscr{B}
such that
S=B+\mathscr{B}
is invertible. Let
h\in H,
define the determinant line bundle of
B
by

\text{Det}(B)={\mathcal{F}}_{B}\times_{\sim_{h}}{\mathbb{C}}

where the equivalence relation
\sim_{h}
incorporated with the
h
-action is given by

Lemma 8.1
.

V(P)

by a smoothing operator, i.e.

Proof.

The proof here is modified from that of

by using our new setting. The operator

acts by its inverse from

to
\text{Ran}(P)=\text{graph}(T^{-1})

\begin{pmatrix}x\\
Kx\end{pmatrix}\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}T^{-1}K&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&{\mathrm{Id}}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{-i}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
Kx\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}T^{-1}(Kx)\\

By a direct computation, the composition

{\mathcal{S}}(P)=P{\mathcal{P}}_{M}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{Id}}+T^{-1}K&T^{-1}+K^{-1}\\

Then,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(P)\begin{pmatrix}x\\

As a consequence,

\displaystyle=[B,1]_{1_{H}}=[V(P)(V^{-1}(P)B),1]_{1_{H}}=[V(P),1\cdot{\mathrm{det}}_{F}(V^{-1}(P)B)]_{1_{H}}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(V^{-1}(P)B)\cdot[V(P),1]_{1_{H}}={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(V^{-1}(P)B)\cdot{\mathrm{det}}(V(P)).

Definition 8.2
.

For
h\in H
, define the
h
-canonical determinant of

by

{\mathrm{det}}^{h}_{\mathcal{C}}(D_{P}):={\mathrm{det}}^{h}_{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{S}}(P):={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(hV^{-1}(P){\mathcal{S}}(P)).

It is straightforward to verify that

The determinant on the right side of (

Hence,

{\mathrm{det}}^{h}_{\mathcal{C}}(D_{P})={\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{a({\mathrm{Id}}+T^{-1}K)}{2}\Big).

(Compare with

\displaystyle\frac{a({\mathrm{Id}}+T^{-1}K)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+aT^{-1}K-({\mathrm{Id}}-a)}{2}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Id}}+\frac{[aT^{-1}K-({\mathrm{Id}}-a)]-{\mathrm{Id}}}{2}.

Since

An equivariant version of the Scott-Wojciechowski Theorem reads as follows.

Conjecture 8.3
.

For
h\in H,
the following equality holds over

(8.2)

{\mathrm{det}}^{h}_{\zeta}(D_{P})={\mathrm{det}}^{h}_{\zeta}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})\cdot{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{a({\mathrm{Id}}+T^{-1}K)}{2}\Big).

We state this as a conjecture instead of a theorem. The basis of believing that the conjecture is true are (i) the equivariant modification above is compatible with that of Scott and Wojciechowski, so the proof is only expected to subject to minor changes; (ii) the formula of equivariant
\zeta
-determinants given in Corollary

turns out to have the same form as the one they are working with.

By using our settings above, we give a subcase of an equivariant version of
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
as follows.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
h\in H.
Let
P,Q\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{h}^{\infty}(A)
and
T=T(P),S=S(Q)
be the respective associated unitary operators such that
(P,{\mathcal{P}}_{M})
and
({\mathcal{P}}_{M},Q)
are invertible pairs. Then,

(8.3)

\exp(2\pi i(\tilde{\eta}_{h}(D_{P})-\tilde{\eta}_{h}(D_{Q})))={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(aT^{*}S),

where
\tilde{\eta}_{h}(D_{P})
and
\tilde{\eta}_{h}(D_{Q})
are the reduced equivariant
\eta
-invariants of
D_{P}
and
D_{Q}
respectively (see
\lx@sectionsign 9
).

Proof.

We adopt an elegant method introduced by Kirk and Lesch
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
. First, consider the
h
-equivariant invertible pair
({\mathcal{P}}_{M},Q),
for which the corresponding boundary problems
D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}
and
D_{Q}
are both invertible.
Let
K=K({\mathcal{P}}_{M})
be the unitary operator associated to
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
. By Lemma
7.8
, we know that the equivariant
\zeta
-invariant is independent of the projections, i.e.
\zeta_{h}(D^{2}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})=\zeta_{h}(D^{2}_{Q}).
By Conjecture
8.3
and Corollary
7.12
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{a({\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S)}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathrm{det}}^{h}_{\zeta}(D_{Q})}{{\mathrm{det}}^{h}_{\zeta}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})}=\frac{\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\zeta_{h}(D^{2}_{Q})-\eta_{h}(D_{Q}))-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\zeta}_{h}(D^{2}_{Q})\Big)}{\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\zeta_{h}(D^{2}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})-\eta_{h}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}))-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\zeta}_{h}(D^{2}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})\Big)}

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\eta_{h}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})-\eta_{h}(D_{Q}))+\frac{1}{2}(\dot{\zeta}_{h}(D^{2}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})-\dot{\zeta}_{h}(D^{2}_{Q}))\Big).

By taking the modulus on both sides, we see that

\Big|{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{a({\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S)}{2}\Big)\Big|=\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2}(\dot{\zeta}_{h}(D^{2}_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})-\dot{\zeta}_{h}(D^{2}_{Q}))\Big)

and thus

(8.4)

{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{a({\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S)}{2}\Big)\Big/\Big|{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{a({\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S)}{2}\Big)\Big|=\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\eta_{h}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})-\eta_{h}(D_{Q})\Big).

On the other hand, by Lemma
5.5
, we have

{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(a\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S}{2}\Big)\Big)={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(a){\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S}{2}\Big).

The right side is well-defined because
a={\mathrm{Id}}+(a-{\mathrm{Id}})
for
a\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}),
so
a-{\mathrm{Id}}
is smoothing and is of trace class. Hence,
a
is of determinant class. Similarly,

\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S}{2}={\mathrm{Id}}+\frac{K^{*}S-{\mathrm{Id}}}{2}.

Since
K^{*}S-{\mathrm{Id}}
is smoothing and is of trace class,
\frac{1}{2}(K^{*}S-{\mathrm{Id}})
is of determinant class.
Then,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(a\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S}{2}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(a){\mathrm{det}}_{F}(K^{*}S){\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+S^{*}K}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(aK^{*}S){\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+S^{*}K}{2}\Big).

Note that
\frac{1+S^{*}K}{2}
is in general not unitary and hence its Fredholm determinant is a complex number whose modulus is a non-zero real number. By taking modulo, we obtain

(8.5)

{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(a\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S}{2}\Big)\Big)\Big/\Big|{\mathrm{det}}_{F}\Big(a\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Id}}+K^{*}S}{2}\Big)\Big)\Big|={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(aK^{*}S).

From (
8.4
) and (
8.5
), we have

\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\eta_{h}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})-\eta_{h}(D_{Q})\Big)={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(aK^{*}S).

Then,

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\eta_{h}(D_{P})-\eta_{h}(D_{Q})\Big)

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\eta_{h}(D_{P})-\eta_{h}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})\Big)\exp\Big(\frac{i\pi}{2}(\eta_{h}(D_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})-\eta_{h}(D_{Q})\Big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(aT^{*}K){\mathrm{det}}_{F}(aK^{*}S)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{det}}_{F}(aT^{*}S)

where the third line follows from the multiplicative property of equivariant Fredholm determinants. The invertibility of
D_{P}
and
D_{Q}
imply that there are no contributions by the
h
-traces and thus proving (
8.3
).
∎

Definition 9.1
.

(
[
13
]
)
For
h\in H,
the
h
-equivariant
\eta
-function of a self-adjoint elliptic equivariant differential operator

is defined by

(9.1)

\eta_{h}(D,s):=\sum_{\lambda\neq 0,\lambda\in{\text{spec}}(D)\backslash\{0\}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})\frac{\text{sgn}(\lambda)}{|\lambda|^{s}}

where
h^{*}
is the induced linear map on the
\lambda
-eigenspace. The equivariant
\eta
-invariant
\eta_{h}(D)
is defined to be
\eta_{h}(D,0).

Lemma 9.2
.

For
h\in H,

\eta_{h}(D,s)
is the Mellin transform of
{\mathrm{Tr}}(hDe^{-tD^{2}}).

Proof.

Recall that the
\Gamma
-function with the variable
\frac{1}{2}(s+1)
is given by

(9.2)

\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+1}{2}\Big)=\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\frac{s-1}{2}}e^{-t}dt.

Set
\tau=\lambda^{2}t.
Then,

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\frac{s-1}{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})\lambda e^{-\lambda^{2}t}dt

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\Big(\frac{\tau}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}}e^{-\tau}d\tau=\lambda^{-1}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{|\lambda|}{|\lambda|^{s}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})\tau^{\frac{s-1}{2}}e^{-\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=\Big(\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{|\lambda|}{|\lambda|^{s}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})\tau^{\frac{s-1}{2}}e^{-\tau}d\tau\Big)\frac{|\lambda|}{\lambda}|\lambda|^{-s}

\displaystyle=\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+1}{2}\Big){\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda})\text{sgn}(\lambda)|\lambda|^{-s}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\eta_{h}(D,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\frac{s+1}{2}\big)}\sum_{k}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\frac{s-1}{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})\lambda e^{-\lambda^{2}_{k}t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\frac{s+1}{2}\big)}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\frac{s-1}{2}}\sum_{k}\Big({\mathrm{Tr}}(h^{*}_{\lambda_{k}})\lambda e^{-\lambda^{2}_{k}t}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\frac{s+1}{2}\big)}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\frac{s-1}{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(hDe^{-tD^{2}})dt.

∎

Definition 9.3
.

Let
h\in H.
Let

\eta
-function of

by

\eta_{h,\geq\epsilon}(D,s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\big(\frac{s+1}{2}\big)}\int^{\infty}_{\epsilon}t^{\frac{s-1}{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}}(hDe^{-tD^{2}})dt.

.

Let
h\in H.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
h\in H.
For
t\in[0,1],
let

be a smooth path in
{\mathrm{Gr}}(A_{t})
and let

Proof.

Note that our definition of equivariant spectral flow are based on the approach of Phillips
[
23
]
, which is another approach to that of
[
3
]

[

extends to Getzler’s setting. In the following we only highlight the relevant changes to Getzler’s setting without reciting all details. For
h\in H,
consider the associated equivariant one-form

for some self-adjoint bounded linear operator

on

on

is invariant under isotopies of the smooth paths

In particular, there is an isotopy from

to a path transversal to

is transversal to

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\eta_{h,\epsilon}(D_{P}(1))-\eta_{h,\epsilon}(D_{P}(0))-\int^{1}_{0}\frac{d}{dt}\eta_{h,\epsilon}(D_{P}(t))dt\Big)

The next lemma is, strictly speaking, a special case of

See

For any

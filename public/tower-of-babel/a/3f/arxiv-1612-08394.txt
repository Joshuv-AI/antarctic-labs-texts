Wen Huang, Zeng Lian, Kening Lu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the complicated dynamics of Anosov systems driven by an external force in the context of geometric theory (an abundance of random periodic points and random horseshoes) and smooth ergodic theory (random periodic measures and random Livšic Theorem).

Primary: 37D45, 37C40

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the complicated dynamics of Anosov systems driven by an external force in the context of geometric theory and smooth ergodic theory.

1.1.
Description of Main Results.

Let
(\Omega,d_{\Omega})
be a compact metric space and
\theta:\Omega\to\Omega
be a homeomorphism. Let
M
be a connected compact smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. A dynamical system driven by an external force
\theta
or a cocycle is a family of continuous maps

F(n,\cdot,\cdot):\Omega\times M\to M,\quad(\omega,x)\mapsto F(n,\omega,x),\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{Z}

such that the map
F(n,\omega):=F(n,\omega,\cdot)
forms a
cocycle over
\theta
:

F(0,\omega)=Id,\quad\hbox{ for all }\;\omega\in\Omega,

F(n+m,\omega)=F(n,\theta^{m}\omega)F(m,\omega),\quad\hbox{ for all }\;m,n\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\omega\in\Omega.

When space
\Omega
is endowed an invariant probability measure such as the Haar measure for
\theta
,
F
is also called a random dynamical system, see Arnold
[
2
]
.

Let
f(\omega)
be the time-one map of the system, i.e.,
f(\omega)x=F(1,\omega,x)
, which is assumed to be a diffeomorphism from
M
to
M
. Conversely, such a family of diffeomorphisms also generates a cocycle (or a random dynamical system):

F(n,\omega,x)=\begin{cases}f(\theta^{n-1}\omega)\circ\cdots\circ f(\omega)x,&n>0,\\
x,&n=0,\\
f^{-1}(\theta^{n}\omega)\circ\cdots\circ f^{-1}(\theta^{-1}\omega)x,&n<0.\end{cases}

Equivalently, putting
f
and
\theta
together forms a skew product system
\phi:M\times\Omega\to M\times\Omega
given by

\phi(x,\omega)=(f(\omega)x,\theta\omega)=(f_{\omega}x,\theta\omega),\ \forall x\in M,\omega\in\Omega

where we rewrite
f(\omega)
as
f_{\omega}
for the sake of convenience.

We consider a system
\phi(x,\omega)=(f_{\omega}x,\theta\omega)
which we call
Anosov on fibers
, i.e., the following hold:
for every
(x,\omega)\in M\times\Omega
there is a splitting of the tangential fiber of
M_{\omega}:=M\times\{\omega\}
at
x

T_{x}M_{\omega}=E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}\oplus E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}

which depends continuously on
(x,\omega)\in M\times\Omega
with
\dim E_{(x,\omega)}^{u},\dim E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}>0
and satisfies that

Df_{\omega}(x)E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}=E_{\phi(x,\omega)}^{u},\ \ \ Df_{\omega}(x)E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}=E_{\phi(x,\omega)}^{s},

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|Df_{\omega}(x)\xi|\geq e^{\lambda_{0}}|\xi|,&\forall\ \xi\in E_{(x,\omega)}^{u},\\
|Df_{\omega}(x)\eta|\leq e^{-\lambda_{0}}|\eta|,&\forall\ \eta\in E_{(x,\omega)}^{s},\end{array}\right.

where
\lambda_{0}>0
is a constant.

We note that if
\Omega
is a differentiable manifold and
\theta
is a diffeomorphims with expansion weaker than
e^{\lambda_{0}}
and contraction weaker than
e^{-\lambda_{0}}
, then the system is a partially hyperbolic system.

We assume that
\phi:M\times\Omega\to M\times\Omega
is
topological mixing on fibers
, that is, that for any nonempty open sets
U,V\subset M
, there exists
N>0
such that for any
n\geq N
and
\omega\in\Omega

\phi^{n}(U\times\{\omega\})\bigcap V\times\{\theta^{n}\omega\}\neq\emptyset.

In the following, we first introduce two ingredients of dynamical complexity:
random periodic orbit
and
random horseshoe.

Let
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
be the space of Borel measurable maps from
\Omega
to
M
endowed with the metric
d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
:

d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}(g_{1},g_{2})=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M).

The set
\{graph(g_{i})|\ g_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is called a
random periodic orbit
of
\phi
with period
n
if
for all
1\leq i\leq n

\phi(\text{graph}(g_{i}))=\text{graph}(g_{i+1\mod n}),\ \forall 1\leq i\leq n.

Equivalently,

g_{i}\text{ is a periodic point of }\tilde{\phi}\text{ i.e., }\tilde{\phi}(g_{i})=g_{i+1\mod n}\ \forall 1\leq i\leq n,

where
\tilde{\phi}
is the induced map of
\phi
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
given by for each
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)

\tilde{\phi}(g)(\omega)=f_{\theta^{-1}\omega}g(\theta^{-1}\omega).

Each
graph(g_{i})
is called a random periodic point of
\phi
. Without causing any confusion and for the sake of convenience, we call
g_{i}
a random periodic point for short.
Moreover, if
g_{i}
is a continuous map then we call
g_{i}
a continuous random periodic point.

Next, we introduce the concept of random horseshoes on different levels, which are distinguished by the following separation functions on
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
measuring the separations of elements of
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
on different levels.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}(g_{1},g_{2})&=\sup_{\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega\text{ is open and nonempty}}\inf_{\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M);\\
\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}(g_{1},g_{2})&=\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M).\end{split}

Let
\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{1,\ldots,k\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the space of two sides sequences of
k(\geq 2))
symbols endowed with the standard metric, and
\sigma
be the (left-)shift map.

Using the separation funcation
\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
, we define a
full random horseshoe
with
k
-symbols of
\phi
as a continuous embedding
\Psi:\mathcal{S}_{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
satisfying

i)

There exists
\Delta>0
such that for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\text{ with }\hat{a}_{1}(l)\neq\hat{a}_{2}(l)

\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}_{1}}),\Psi({\hat{a}_{2}})\right)>\Delta,

where for
l\geq 0

\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}(g_{1},g_{2}):=\max_{0\leq i\leq l}\left\{\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{1}),\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{2})\right)\right\},\ g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)

and for
l<0
we let
\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}=\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}^{-1}}_{|l|};

ii)

For all
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}

\tilde{\phi}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}})\right)=\Psi({\sigma\hat{a}}).

Then, using the stronger separation function
\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
, we define a
strong full random horseshoe
as a continuous embedding
\Psi:\mathcal{S}_{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
satisfying

i)

There exists
\Delta>0
such that for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\text{ with }\hat{a}_{1}(l)\neq\hat{a}_{2}(l)

\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}_{1}}),\Psi({\hat{a}_{2}})\right)>\Delta,

where for
l\geq 0

\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}(g_{1},g_{2}):=\max_{0\leq i\leq l}\left\{\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{1}),\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{2})\right)\right\},\ g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)

and for
l<0
we let
\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}=\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}^{-1}}_{|l|};

ii)

For all
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}

\tilde{\phi}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}})\right)=\Psi({\sigma\hat{a}}).

A full random horseshoe we have here is an embedding of a two-sides symbolic dynamical system into
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
, which is an extension of the standard topological horseshoe.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\text{diff}^{2}(M)
be the space of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms on
M
with
C^{2}
-topology. Throughout this paper, we assume
f:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}
is a continuous map.

Our first result states that there is an abundance of random periodic points.

Theorem A
(
Density of Random Periodic Points
).
Assume that
\phi
is Anosov and topological mixing on fibers. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
n\geq N
, there exists a random periodic point
\tilde{g}
with period
n
such that

d_{L^{\infty}}(g,\tilde{g})\leq\epsilon.

Remark:
The study of hyperbolic dynamics goes back to Poincáre
[
37
]
on
3
-body problem and Hadamard
[
14
]
on geodesic flow. The modern theory of uniformly hyperbolic dynamical systems was initiated by Anosov
[
1
]
and Smale
[
40
]
where Anosov and Axiom A diffeomeophism/flows were introduced respectively. The core ingredient in these systems is the uniform hyperbolicity which is an invariant geometric structure describing the exponential divergence of nearby orbits. This exponential divergence together with the compactness of phase space produces rich and complicated dynamical structures. The dynamics of these systems has been understood very well. One of results is that for a transitive Anosov diffeomorphism, the set of its periodic points (the number of the periodic orbits with same period is finite) is countable and dense in
M
. However, Theorem A tells that for the systems we study here, the set of random periodic points is uncountable because of the non-separability of
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
, where we exclude the case that
\Omega
has trivial topology. In fact, Theorem A indicates that the cardinality of random periodic orbits with sufficient large period is infinite (actually uncountable), thus the increasing rate of numbers of random periodic orbits with same period as periods tends to infinity is always infinite. Therefore such an increasing rate fails in representing the topological entropy of the system, which is one of the main tasks that periodic orbits perform for Anosov diffeomorphisms in deterministic scenario. Nevertheless, there are other candidates which are able to carry out the task of representing complexity of the system such as the random horseshoes, which will be introduced in the next.

Another interesting observation based on an example given in Section
8
is that the function corresponding to random periodic points are
NOT
necessarily continuous. Actually, we show that under certain conditions, the graph corresponding to
each
random periodic point is
discontinuous
.

Our next result is on the existence of random horseshoes with entropy closed to the entropy of the system.

Theorem B
(
Entropy and Random Horseshoe
).
Assume that
\phi
is Anosov and topological mixing on fibers. Then the following statements hold

(a)

Strong Random Horseshoe: For any
\gamma>0
, there exist
N,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following hold

i)

\frac{1}{N}\ln k\geq\underline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)-\gamma
, where
\underline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)>0
and

\underline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega):=\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}});

ii)

\phi^{N}
has a
k
-symbol strong full random horseshoe.

(b)

Random Horseshoe: For any
\gamma>0
, there exist
N,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following hold

i)

\frac{1}{N}\ln k\geq\overline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)-\gamma
, where
\overline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)>0
and

\overline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega):=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}});

ii)

\phi^{N}
has a
k
-symbol full random horseshoe.

Remark:
The measure-theoretic entropy was introduced in 1950’s by Kolmogorov
[
24
]
and Sinai
[
38
]
to measure the rate of increase in dynamical complexity as the system evolves with time. Sinai
[
39
]
studied an ergodic measure preserving automorphism
f
of a Lebesgue space
(X,\mu)
and proved that if the measure-theoretic entropy of
f
is positive, then
f
contains factor automorphisms which are isomorphic to Bernoulli shifts. The topological entropy was first introduced in 1965 by Adler, Konheim and McAndrew for studying dynamical systems in topological spaces. In his remarkable paper
[
18
]
, A. Katok proved that for a nonuniformly hyperbolic
C^{2}
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold, the positive topological entropy implies the existence of a Smale horseshoe. Recently, Lian and Young
[
27
]
extended Katok’s results to
C^{2}
differentiable maps with a nonuniformly hyperbolic compact invariant set in a separable Hilbert space and to a
C^{2}
semiflow in a Hilbert space which has a nonuniformly hyperbolic compact invariant set. Without assuming any hyperbolicity, Huang and Lu
[
16
]
proved if a continuous random map in a Polish space has a positive topological entropy on a random compact invariant set , then it contains a weak horseshoe (see also
[
15
]
for topological dynamical systems in compact metric spaces and
[
20
]
for
C^{*}
-dynamics). The horseshoe we have here is for hyperbolic systems driven by an external force, which cannot be obtained by using Katok’s result or Lian and Young’s result for flow. When space
\Omega
is endowed an invariant probability measure such as the Haar measure for
\theta
, it follows from
[
13
]
that
\phi
has a so called random SRB measure. In particular, for the Anosov systems driven by a quasiperiodic force which appears in Section
8.1.2
, the systems have a unique random SRB measure since the driven system is uniquely ergodic.

Next, we consider random periodic
\phi
-invariant measures. We fix a
\theta
-invariant probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\Omega
. For
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
, we define a Borel probability measure
\mu_{g}
in
\mathcal{M}(M\times\Omega)
(the set of Borel probability measures) as follows: for any Borel set
A\subset M\times\Omega

\mu_{g}(A)=\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega\big|\ (g(\omega),\omega)\in A\cap M\times\{\omega\}\right\}\right).

Let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{P}}(M\times\Omega)
be the collection of
\phi
-invariant measures whose marginal is
\mathbb{P}
.
An invariant measure
\mu\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{P}}(M\times\Omega)
is called a random periodic measure if there is a random periodic orbit
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1,\cdots,n}
such that

\mu=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{g_{i}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{1})}.

Then, we have the following theorem which states that any
\phi
-invariant measure with marginal
\mathbb{P}
can be approximated by random periodic measures.

Theorem C
(
Density of Random Periodic Invariant Measures
).
Assume that
\phi
is Anosov and topological mixing on fibers. Then the set of all periodic
\phi
-invariant measures is dense in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{P}}(M\times\Omega)
in the narrow topology.

Unlike the standard periodic measure supported by one periodic orbit, the random periodic measure supported by one random periodic orbit is
not
necessarily ergodic.

Finally, we present a weak version of the classic Livšic Theorem for random dynamical systems. For a
\theta
-invariant probability measure
\mathbb{P}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, denote
L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))
the collection of real functions
\Phi
on
M\times\Omega
, which is Hölder on
M
and measurable on
\Omega
, and satisfies the following condition

\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))}:=\int_{\Omega}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P}<\infty.

For each
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
and
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))
, we define a functional
\tilde{\Phi}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
as follows

\tilde{\Phi}(g)=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(g(\omega),\omega)d\mathbb{P}.

Theorem D
(
Random Livšic Theorem
).
Assume that
\phi
is Anosov and topological mixing on fibers.
Then for any
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))
satisfying

(1.1)

\int_{M\times\Omega}\Phi d\mu=0\;\text{ for any random periodic }\phi\text{-invariant measure $\mu$ with marginal }\mathbb{P},

there exists a functional
\tilde{\Psi}:L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\to\mathbb{R}
which is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous and satisfies the following cohomological equation

(1.2)

\tilde{\Phi}(g)=\tilde{\Psi}(\tilde{\phi}(g))-\tilde{\Psi}(g),\ \forall g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M).

Moreover,
\tilde{\Psi}
is uniquely determined up to a constant functional.

Theorem D is a weak version of Livšic theorem for random dynamical systems,
in the proof of which a significant difference from the deterministic case shows up: Because of the absence of the separability the phase space

Remark on Horseshoe:

System S1) is an Anosov system driven by a quasi-periodic forcing and is also a partially hyperbolic system. In fact, with each invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
, system S1) has zero Lyapunov exponent of multiplicity
d
(the dimension of
\Omega=T^{d}
). Thus, the Katok’s result (
[
18
]
) does not apply to this system. Lian and Young’s results (
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
) for semiflows or flows, which allow only one-dimensional center associated to the flow direction, are also not applicable to this case because of high dimensional center. Futhermore, the quasi-periodic forced systems has no periodic points, and so has no standard horseshoe. However, because of the uniform hyperbolicity of the map between fibers (
f(\omega):M_{\omega}\to M_{\theta\omega}
) and the rigidity of the evolution of quasiperiodic forcing, one expects the co-existence of certain kinds of periodicity and chaotic behavior over some random invariant structures.

The main difficulty appearing in these systems is that the pseudo orbits derived from Poincaré Recurrence Theorem only provides returns in
M\times\Omega
, thus does not have the shadowing property since the hyperbolicity only happens on fibers which are identified by
M
. To overcome this difficulty, instead of looking at one single pseudo orbit, we construct a group of globally returned pseudo orbits for all
\omega\in\Omega
at the same time.

For non-uniformly hyperbolic systems, one needs to look at the return map on Pesin blocks (as being done in
[
18
]
). If there is a globally returning time, the projection of such Pesin blocks to
\Omega
should be a
\mathbb{P}
-full-measure-set. This is not satisfied even in the setting of
[
13
]
in which hyperbolicity depends on the sample “
\omega
”. Such observation reveals that for non-uniform hyperbolic system, accurate information about “shapes” of the Pesin blocks is required, which makes the situation much more complicated than autonomous case. Second, even dealing with systems which is Anosov on fibers, constructing a global return is not trivial, since one needs infinite many (usually uncountable many) orbits return at a same time. Our immediate feeling is that for non-uniformly hyperbolic systems on fibers, one may need a new approach.

Finally, we mention some existing results which are closely relevant to the results derived in this paper, which may shed a light on further research directions, for example, chaotic behavior of Anosov flows driven by quasi-periodic noises. Of course, this is far from being a complete picture. In

and

, Lu and Wang studied the chaos behavior of a type of differential equations driven by a nonautononous force deduced from stochastic processes which are the truncated and classical Brownian motions in
[

]
and in
[
33

respectively. The authors extended the concept of topological horseshoe and showed that such structure (thus chaotic behavior) exists almost surely. Moreover, they further applied the results to randomly forced Duffing equations and pendulum equations. There are also many works on the existence of random periodic solutions for stochastic differential equations, see

2.
Setting, Definitions, and Statement of the Main Results

In this section, we set up the systems, introduce notions, and give the statements of the main results precisely. Although some of the definitions have been given in Section
1
, we restate them formally for the sake of completeness and convenience.

Let
M
be a connected compact smooth Riemannian manifold with no boundary,
d_{M}
be the induced Riemannian metric of
M
,
(\Omega,d_{\Omega})
be a compact metric space, and
\theta:\Omega\to\Omega
be a homeomorphism.
Let
\mathcal{H}=\text{diff}^{2}(M)
be the space of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms on
M
with
C^{2}
-topology, and
f:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}
be a continuous map. Then the skew product of
f
and
\theta
induces a map
\phi:M\times\Omega\to M\times\Omega
in the following way:

\phi(x,\omega)=(f(\omega)x,\theta\omega)=(f_{\omega}x,\theta\omega),\ \forall x\in M,\omega\in\Omega

where we rewrite
f(\omega)
as
f_{\omega}
for the sake of convenience. Clearly,
\phi
induces a cocycle over
(\Omega,\theta)
, which can be inductively defined by

\begin{cases}\phi^{0}(x,\omega)&=(x,\omega)\\
\phi^{n}(x,\omega)&=(f_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}\phi^{n-1}(x,\omega),\theta^{n}\omega),\ n\geq 1\\
\phi^{n}(x,\omega)&=(f^{-1}_{\theta^{n+1}\omega}\phi^{n+1}(x,\omega),\theta^{n}\omega),\ n\leq-1\end{cases}.

Note that
M\times\Omega
is a compact metric space when we introduce a metric
d
by defining that

d((x_{1},\omega_{2}),(x_{2},\omega_{2}))=d_{M}(x_{1},x_{2})+d_{\Omega}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\forall(x_{1},\omega_{1}),(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in M\times\Omega.

Then
\phi
and
D_{M}\phi(\text{where }D_{M}\phi(x,\omega):=Df_{\omega}(x))
are continuous on
M\times\Omega
.

Definition 2.1
.

The system generated by
\phi
(or simply
\phi
) is called
Anosov on fibers
if the following holds:
for every
(x,\omega)\in M\times\Omega
there is a splitting of the tangential fiber of
M_{\omega}:=M\times\{\omega\}
at
x

T_{x}M_{\omega}=E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}\oplus E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}

which depends continuously on
(x,\omega)\in M\times\Omega
with
\dim E_{(x,\omega)}^{u},\dim E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}>0
and satisfies that

Df_{\omega}(x)E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}=E_{\phi(x,\omega)}^{u},\ \ \ Df_{\omega}(x)E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}=E_{\phi(x,\omega)}^{s},

and

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|Df_{\omega}(x)\xi|\geq e^{\lambda_{0}}|\xi|,&\forall\ \xi\in E_{(x,\omega)}^{u},\\
|Df_{\omega}(x)\eta|\leq e^{-\lambda_{0}}|\eta|,&\forall\ \eta\in E_{(x,\omega)}^{s},\end{array}\right.

where
\lambda_{0}>0
is a constant.

Remark 2.1
.

It is clear that the splitting
T_{x}M_{\omega}=E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}\oplus E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}
is uniformly continuous on
M\times\Omega
. Actually, one has that the above splitting is Hölder continuous on
x
, which follows from the standard arguments. Moreover, the Hölder constant and exponent can be chosen independent on
(x,\omega)
. For more details, we refer the readers to
[
5
]
, Section 5.3 of
[
4
]
, and Section 5.4 of
[
25
]
.

Denote
\pi^{u}_{(x,\omega)},\pi^{s}_{(x,\omega)}
the projections associated to the splitting
T_{x}M_{\omega}=E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}\oplus E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}
, which map
T_{x}M_{\omega}
onto
E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}
and
E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}
respectively. Since the splitting
T_{x}M_{\omega}=E_{(x,\omega)}^{u}\oplus E_{(x,\omega)}^{s}
is uniformly continuous on
M\times\Omega
, there exists a real number
\mathcal{P}>1
such that

(2.2)

\max\left\{\|\pi_{(x,\omega)}^{\tau}\|\big|\ (x,\omega)\in M\times\Omega,\tau=u,s\right\}<\mathcal{P}.

The general systems we consider is topological mixing on fibers which is defined as follows:

Definition 2.2
.

\phi:M\times\Omega\to M\times\Omega
is called
topological mixing on fibers
if for any nonempty open sets
U,V\subset M
, there exists
N>0
such that for any
n\geq N
and
\omega\in\Omega

\phi^{n}(U\times\{\omega\})\bigcap V\times\{\theta^{n}\omega\}\neq\emptyset.

In the following, we first introduce two ingredients of dynamical complexity:
random periodic orbit
and
random horseshoe.

Let
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
be the space of Borel measurable maps from
\Omega
to
M
endowed with the metric
d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
:

d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}(g_{1},g_{2})=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M).

For later use, we also define two functions on
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
to measure the separations of elements of
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
on different levels:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}(g_{1},g_{2})&=\sup_{\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega\text{ is open and nonempty}}\inf_{\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M).\\
\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}(g_{1},g_{2})&=\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M).\end{split}

Since
\phi
and
\phi^{-1}
are both continuous,
\phi
induces a map
\tilde{\phi}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
given by

\tilde{\phi}(g)(\omega)=f_{\theta^{-1}\omega}g(\theta^{-1}\omega).

Definition 2.3
.

\{graph(g_{i})|\ g_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is called a random periodic orbit of
\phi
with period
n
if
for all
1\leq i\leq n

\phi(\text{graph}(g_{i}))=\text{graph}(g_{i+1\mod n})\ \forall 1\leq i\leq n,

equivalently,

(2.4)

g_{i}\text{ is a periodic point of }\tilde{\phi}\text{ i.e. }\tilde{\phi}(g_{i})=g_{i+1\mod n}\ \forall 1\leq i\leq n.

Each
graph(g_{i})
is called a random periodic point of
\phi
. Without causing any confusion and for the sake of convenience, we call
g_{i}
a random periodic point for short.
Moreover, if
g_{i}
is a continuous map then we call
g_{i}
a continuous random periodic point.

Remark 2.2
.

The system generated by
\phi
is usually called a skew-product system rather than a random dynamical system, which makes the name ” random periodic orbit” sound a little bit more in name than reality. Nevertheless, once a
\theta
-invariant measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\Omega
is introduced, the resulting system induced by
\phi
becomes a random dynamical systems over the metric dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathbb{P},\theta)
(see
[
2
]
for more details). On the other hand, since the name ”random periodic orbit” has been introduced in exiting literature (see
[
22
,
42
]
) and has the essentially equivalent meaning, we prefer not to introduce different name or notions.

Next, we introduce the concept of random horseshoes in different levels, which are distinguished by the separation functions given by (
2.3
).

Let
\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{1,\ldots,k\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the space of two sides sequences of
k(\geq 2))
symbols endowed with the standard metric, and
\sigma
be the (left-)shift map. The following is the standard definition of Smale horseshoe for the deterministic map
\tilde{\phi}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We call a continuous embedding
\Psi:\mathcal{S}_{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
a horseshoe with
k
-symbols of
\tilde{\phi}
if
the following hold:

i)

There exists
\Delta>0
such that for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\text{ with }\hat{a}_{1}(l)\neq\hat{a}_{2}(l)
,

(2.5)

d^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}_{1}}),\Psi({\hat{a}_{2}})\right)>\Delta,

where for
l\geq 0

d^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}(g_{1},g_{2}):=\max_{0\leq i\leq l}\left\{d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{1}),\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{2})\right)\right\},\ g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)

is the Bowen metric induced by
\tilde{\phi}
and for
l<0
we let
d^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}=d^{\tilde{\phi}^{-1}}_{|l|};

ii)

For all
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}

\tilde{\phi}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}})\right)=\Psi({\sigma\hat{a}}).

The next definition we introduce here is the random horseshoe for a skew-product system, which has a slightly finer structure by employing the separation function
\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
in (
2.3
) to replace
d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
.

Definition 2.5
.

We call a continuous embedding
\Psi:\mathcal{S}_{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
a full random horseshoe with
k
-symbols of
\phi
if
the following hold:

i)

There exists
\Delta>0
such that for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\text{ with }\hat{a}_{1}(l)\neq\hat{a}_{2}(l)

(2.6)

\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}_{1}}),\Psi({\hat{a}_{2}})\right)>\Delta,

where for
l\geq 0

\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}(g_{1},g_{2}):=\max_{0\leq i\leq l}\left\{\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{1}),\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{2})\right)\right\},\ g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)

and for
l<0
we let
\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}=\overline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}^{-1}}_{|l|};

ii)

For all
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}

\tilde{\phi}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}})\right)=\Psi({\sigma\hat{a}}).

Finally, by using the stronger separation function
\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
as in (
2.3
), we define a stronger random horseshoe as follows:

Definition 2.6
.

We call a continuous embedding
\Psi:\mathcal{S}_{k}\to L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
a strong full random horseshoe with
k
-symbols of
\phi
if
the following hold:

i)

There exists
\Delta>0
such that for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\text{ with }\hat{a}_{1}(l)\neq\hat{a}_{2}(l)

(2.7)

\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}_{1}}),\Psi({\hat{a}_{2}})\right)>\Delta,

where for
l\geq 0

\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}(g_{1},g_{2}):=\max_{0\leq i\leq l}\left\{\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{1}),\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{2})\right)\right\},\ g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)

and for
l<0
we let
\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}}_{l}=\underline{d}^{\tilde{\phi}^{-1}}_{|l|};

ii)

For all
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}

\tilde{\phi}\left(\Psi({\hat{a}})\right)=\Psi({\sigma\hat{a}}).

Remark 2.3
.

Clearly, we have that

d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}\geq\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}\geq\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}.
Therefore, we have that a strong full random horseshoe is automatically a full random horseshoe, and a full random horseshoe of
\phi
automatically induces a horseshoe of
\tilde{\phi}
.

Before stating the main results, we introduce several notations. Let

\displaystyle N(\omega,\epsilon,n):=

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\max\left\{card(E)\big|\ E\subset M_{\omega},E\text{ is an }\epsilon\text{ separated set with respect to }d^{\phi}_{M\times\Omega,n}\right\},

where
d^{\phi}_{M\times\Omega,n}
is the Bowen metric. Let

h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}})=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N(\omega,\epsilon,n),

\underline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega):=\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}}),

\overline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega):=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}}).

Here
h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}})
is called the topological fiber entropy on the fiber
M_{\omega}
,
\underline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)
and
\overline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)
are called lower and upper topological fiber entropy respectively.

The systems we consider here satisfy the following two hypotheses:

H1)

\phi
is Anosov on fibers.

H2)

\phi
is topological mixing on fibers.

Theorem 2.1

(Density of Random Periodic Points)
.

Let
\phi
satisfy condition H1) and H2). Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
n\geq N
, there exists a random periodic point
\tilde{g}
with period
n
such that

d_{L^{\infty}}(g,\tilde{g})\leq\epsilon.

Theorem 2.2

(Entropy and Random Horseshoe)
.

Let
\phi
satisfy condition H1)and H2). Then the following assertions hold:

A.

For any
\gamma>0
, there exist
N,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following hold

i)

\frac{1}{N}\ln k\geq\underline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)-\gamma
, where
\underline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)>0
;

ii)

\phi^{N}
has a
k
-symbol strong full random horseshoe.

B.

For any
\gamma>0
, there exist
N,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following hold

i)

\frac{1}{N}\ln k\geq\overline{h}(M\times\Omega|\Omega)-\gamma
;

ii)

\phi^{N}
has a
k
-symbol full random horseshoe.

Next, we fix a
\theta
-invariant probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\Omega
, and consider the random dynamical systems over
\theta
.

For
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define a Borel probability measure,
\mu_{g}
, in
\mathcal{M}(M\times\Omega)
as follows: for any Borel set
A\subset M\times\Omega

(2.8)

\mu_{g}(A)=\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega\big|\ (g(\omega),\omega)\in A\cap M\times\{\omega\}\right\}\right).

Let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{P}}(M\times\Omega)
be the collection of
\phi
-invariant measures whose marginal are
\mathbb{P}
. Now, we define the periodic measures.

Definition 2.7
.

\mu\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{P}}(M\times\Omega)
is called a random periodic measure if and only if there is a random periodic orbit
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1,\cdots,n}
such that

\mu=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{g_{i}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g_{1})}.

The following theorem states that any
\phi
-invariant measure with marginal
\mathbb{P}
can be approximated by random periodic measures.

Theorem 2.3

(Density of Random Periodic Invariant Measures)
.

Let
\phi
satisfy H1) and H2). Then, the set of all periodic measures is dense in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{P}}(M\times\Omega)
in the narrow topology.

Remark 2.4
.

We remark here that, unlike the standard periodic measure supported by one periodic orbit, the random periodic measure supported by one random periodic orbit is
not
necessarily ergodic.

Finally, we present a weak version of the classic Livšic Theorem for random dynamical systems. For the
\theta
-invariant probability measure
\mathbb{P}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, denote
L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))
the collection of functions
\Phi
on
M\times\Omega
, which are Hölder on
M
and measurable on
\Omega
, and satisfy the following condition

(2.9)

\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))}:=\int_{\Omega}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P}<\infty,

where

\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}:=\sup_{x\in M_{\omega}}|\Phi(x,\omega)|+\sup_{x,y\in M_{\omega},\ x\neq y}\frac{|\Phi(x,\omega)-\Phi(y,\omega)|}{\left(d_{M}(x,y)\right)^{\alpha}}.

For any
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
and
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))
, define a functional
\tilde{\Phi}
on
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
as follows

(2.10)

\tilde{\Phi}(g)=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(g(\omega),\omega)d\mathbb{P}.

Remark 2.5
.

First, one needs to show that the above integration is well defined and is finite. Since
\Phi(x,\omega)
is continuous on
x
and Borel measurable on
\omega
, we have that
\Phi
is Borel measurable on
M\times\Omega
. Then, for any real number
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

U:=\Phi^{-1}\left((-\infty,a)\right)
is a Borel subset of
M\times\Omega
.
Note that
graph(g)
is also a Borel subset of
M\times\Omega
since
g:\Omega\to M
is Borel. Therefore,
U\cap graph(g)
is Borel in
M\times\Omega
. By applying Theorem
B.1
, we have that

\left\{\omega|\ \Phi(g(\omega),\omega)<a\right\}=\pi_{\Omega}\left(U\cap graph(g)\right)\text{ is Borel in }\Omega.

Hence
\Phi(g(\cdot),\cdot):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is Borel measurable. As
\Phi
satisfying (
2.9
), we have that (
2.10
) is well defined and is finite.

We call the following theorem a weak version of random Livšic Theorem. In the case that there is no confusion caused, we simply use ”periodic measures with marginal
\mathbb{P}
” short for ”periodic
\phi
-invariant Borel probability measure with marginal
\mathbb{P}
” for the sake of convenience.

Theorem 2.4

(Random Livšic Theorem)
.

Let
\phi
satisfy condition H1) and H2). Then for any
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))
satisfying

(2.11)

\int_{M\times\Omega}\Phi d\mu=0\text{ when }\mu\text{ is periodic with marginal }\mathbb{P},

there exists a functional
\tilde{\Psi}:L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\to\mathbb{R}
which is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous and satisfies the following cohomological equation

(2.12)

\tilde{\Phi}(g)=\tilde{\Psi}(\tilde{\phi}(g))-\tilde{\Psi}(g),\ \forall g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M).

Moreover,
\tilde{\Psi}
is uniquely determined up to a constant functional.

3.1.
Invariant Manifolds

We define the local stable manifolds and unstable manifolds as follows:

\displaystyle W^{s}_{\epsilon}(x,\omega)=\{(y,\omega)\in M_{\omega}|\ d_{M}(\pi_{M}\phi^{n}(y,\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{n}(x,\omega))\leq\epsilon\text{ for all }n\geq 0\}

\omega\in\Omega

which yields

\mathbb{P}\left(\pi_{\Omega}\left(A_{N_{2},\frac{1}{5}\epsilon}^{h}\right)\right)>1-\frac{1}{4M}\epsilon\text{ and }\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}<\frac{1}{10M}\epsilon,

.

Applying Theorem

Let

Next, we show that the periodic measure induced by
graph(\tilde{g}_{0})

\mu_{\epsilon}^{h}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mu_{\tilde{g}_{i}},\text{ provided }graph(\tilde{g}_{i})=\phi^{i}(graph(\tilde{g}_{0})),\ 0\leq i\leq N-1,

satisfies (

).

Note that
\mathbb{P}
can be decomposed into the following form

\mathbb{P}=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\nu_{i},

where

,

,

, and

\theta^{N}
-ergodic.

By Birkhoff ergodic theorem, there exists an
\phi
-invariant Borel function

\nu_{i}

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{h}(\tilde{g}_{0}(\omega),\omega)-\int hd\mu\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{nN}\sum_{i=0}^{nN-1}h\left(\phi^{i}(\tilde{g}_{0}(\omega),\omega)\right)-\int hd\mu\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-1,\theta^{kN}\notin\pi_{\Omega}(K)}\left|\sum_{i=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}h\left(\phi^{i}(\tilde{g}_{0}(\omega),\omega)\right)-N\int hd\mu\right|\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\epsilon+2M(1-\nu_{i}(\pi_{\Omega}(K))).

7.
Livšic Theorem

Throughout this section , we let
\mathbb{P}
be a fixed
\theta
-invariant probability measure on
\Omega
.
Before proving the main result, we introduce several notions and derive two lemmas, namely Random specification and Bowen property.

7.1.
Random specification

The specification property plays an important role in the study of equilibrium state of uniformly hyperbolic systems such as Anosov diffeomorphisms or Axiom A systems. Under the setting of this paper, specification can be introduced for random systems in a natural way. For the sake of simplicity, we first introduce some notions.

For
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define that

\displaystyle(g,n)=\left\{(g_{0},g_{1},\cdots,g_{n-1})\in\left(L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\right)^{n}\big|\ g_{i}=\tilde{\phi}^{i}\left(g\right),\ 0\leq i\leq n-1\right\},

\displaystyle B_{n}(g,\epsilon)=\left\{h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\big|\ d_{n}^{\tilde{\phi}}\left(g,h\right)<\epsilon\right\},

where
d_{n}^{\tilde{\phi}}
is the Bowen metric induced by
\tilde{\phi}
and
d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)}
. We call
(g,n)
a random orbit segment of
g
with length
n
(or
n
-segment of
g
for short), and
B_{n}(g,\epsilon)
the Bowen ball of
g
with length
n
and radius
\epsilon
(or
(n,\epsilon)
-Bowen ball of
g
for short).

Definition 7.1
.

A collection of random orbit segments
\mathcal{G}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\times\mathbb{N}
has specification at scale
\epsilon
if there exists
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
\{(g_{i},n_{i})|\ 1\leq i\leq k\}\subset\mathcal{G}
, there exists a
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
satisfying that

h_{i}\in B_{n_{i}}(g_{i},\epsilon),\ 1\leq i\leq k

where
h_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
is given by

\displaystyle h_{i}=\tilde{\phi}^{\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}n_{j}+(i-1)\tau}h.

Lemma 7.1
.

For the systems satisfying Conditions H1) and H2),
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\times\mathbb{N}
has specification at any scale.

Proof.

This result simply follows from Lemma
4.1
and the shadowing property (Lemma
3.7
). We omit the detailed proof here.

We remark that the connecting time ”
\tau
” in the definition of specification above only depends on the scale ”
\epsilon
” and the given system. More precisely, the connecting time is determined by the accuracy of shadowing and the speed of mixing.
∎

7.2.
Bowen property

For a potential
\psi\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(M))
and a collection of random orbit segments
\mathcal{G}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\times\mathbb{N}
, we define the variation
V(\mathcal{G},\psi,\mathbb{P},\epsilon)
and the average variation
\bar{V}(\mathcal{G},\psi,\mathbb{P},\epsilon)
of
\psi
on
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
at scale
\epsilon
by the following

(7.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V(\mathcal{G},\psi,\mathbb{P},\epsilon):=\sup_{(g,n)\in\mathcal{G},\ h\in B_{n}(g,\epsilon)}\left\{\sup_{\mathbb{P}}\left|S_{n}\psi\left(g(\omega),\omega\right)-S_{n}\psi\left(h(\omega),\omega\right)\right|\right\},\end{split}

(7.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bar{V}(\mathcal{G},\psi,\mathbb{P},\epsilon):=\sup_{(g,n)\in\mathcal{G},\ h\in B_{n}(g,\epsilon)}\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left|S_{n}\psi\left(g(\omega),\omega\right)-S_{n}\psi\left(h(\omega),\omega\right)\right|d\mathbb{P}\right\},\end{split}

where we set
S_{n}(\psi(x,\omega))=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\psi(\phi^{i}(x,\omega))
for
(x,\omega)\in M\times\Omega
.

Definition 7.2
.

Given a collection of random orbit segments
\mathcal{G}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\times\mathbb{N}
, a potential
\psi
has the
Bowen property
(or
the average Bowen property
) on
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
at scale
\epsilon
if the following holds

V(\mathcal{G},\psi,\mathbb{P},\epsilon)<\infty\ (\text{ or }\bar{V}(\mathcal{G},\psi,\mathbb{P},\epsilon)<\infty).

Let
L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(M))\subset L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(M))
be the collection of functions
\psi
with the following holds

\sup_{\mathbb{P}}\|\psi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}<\infty.

We have the following results.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\phi
satisfy Condition H1) and H2), then for
\mathcal{G}=L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\times\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
each potential
\psi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(M))
(or
\psi\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(M))
has the
Bowen property
( or
average Bowen property
) on
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
at scale
\epsilon
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
3.2
, and P2) in the proof of Lemma
3.5
, for a fixed positive number
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
, there exist
\epsilon>0
,
\delta\in(0,\epsilon)
,
L>1
such that for any
x,y\in M
with
d_{M}(x,y)<\delta
, the following hold

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\omega}=:W^{s}_{\epsilon}(x,\omega)\cap W^{u}_{\epsilon}(y,\omega),\text{ which is continuous on }x,y\text{ and }\omega,

\displaystyle\max\left\{d_{M}(x,[x,y]_{\omega}),d_{M}(y,[x,y]_{\omega})\right\}\leq Ld_{M}(x,y),

\displaystyle d_{M}(\pi_{M}\phi^{n}(x,\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{n}(y,\omega))\leq e^{-n\lambda}d_{M}(x,y)\text{ for }(y,\omega)\in W^{s}_{\epsilon}(x,\omega),\ n\geq 0,

\displaystyle d_{M}(\pi_{M}\phi^{-n}(x,\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{-n}(y,\omega))\leq e^{-n\lambda}d_{M}(x,y)\text{ for }(y,\omega)\in W^{u}_{\epsilon}(x,\omega),\ n\geq 0.

For
(g,n)\in\mathcal{G}
and
h\in B_{n}(g,\epsilon)
, we have that for any
\omega\in\Omega
and
0\leq i\leq n-1

(7.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&d_{M}(\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(g(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(h(\omega),\omega)))\\
\leq&d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(g(\omega),\omega),\left[\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(g(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(h(\omega),\omega))\right]_{\theta^{i}\omega}\right)\\
&+d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(h(\omega),\omega),\left[\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(g(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(h(\omega),\omega))\right]_{\theta^{i}\omega}\right)\\
\leq&e^{-i\lambda}d_{M}\left(g(\omega),\left[g(\omega),h(\omega)\right]_{\omega}\right)\\
&+e^{-(n-1-i)\lambda}d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{n-1}(h(\omega),\omega),\left[\pi_{M}\phi^{n-1}(g(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{n-1}(h(\omega),\omega))\right]_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}\right)\\
\leq&2Le^{-i\lambda}d_{M}(g(\omega),h(\omega))+2Le^{-(n-1-i)\lambda}d_{M}(\pi_{M}\phi^{n-1}(g(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{n-1}(h(\omega),\omega))\\
\leq&4Le^{-\min\{i,n-1-i\}\lambda}\epsilon.\end{split}

Then

(7.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{\mathbb{P}}|S_{n}(\psi(g(\omega),\omega))-S_{n}(\psi(h(\omega),\omega))|\\
\leq&\sup_{\mathbb{P}}\|\psi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}(4L\epsilon)^{\alpha}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e^{-\min\{i,n-1-i\}\alpha\lambda}\\
<&\frac{2\sup_{\mathbb{P}}\|\psi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}(4L\epsilon)^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha\lambda}},\end{split}

which is independent on
n
,
g
and
h
.

For the average Bowen property, we need only to replace the
\sup_{\mathbb{P}}
in (
7.4
) by integration on
\Omega
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
and obtain the following

(7.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|S_{n}(\psi(g(\omega),\omega))-S_{n}(\psi(h(\omega),\omega))|d\mathbb{P}\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left|\psi\left(\phi^{i}(g(\omega),\omega)\right)-\psi\left(\phi^{i}(h(\omega),\omega)\right)\right|\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}(4L\epsilon)^{\alpha}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\|\psi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\theta^{i}\omega})}e^{-\min\{i,n-1-i\}\alpha\lambda}d\mathbb{P}\\
<&\frac{2(4L\epsilon)^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha\lambda}}\int_{\Omega}\|\psi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P},\end{split}

which is independent on
n
,
g
and
h
.
This completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 7.5

There exists

such that for any given
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0,\alpha}(M))
satisfying (
2.11
) and the associated
\hat{\Psi}
as in Lemma

the following holds

(7.12)

\left|\hat{\Psi}(h_{1})-\hat{\Psi}(h_{2})\right|\leq L^{\prime}\overline{\Phi}\left(3\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h_{1},h_{2},\rho}\right)\right)+L^{\prime}\rho^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P},\ \forall h_{1},h_{2}\in C(\Omega,M),

where

\Omega_{h_{1},h_{2},\rho}=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\big|\ d_{M}(h_{1}(\omega),h_{2}(\omega))<\rho\right\}

and

\overline{\Phi}(s):=\sup_{A\subset\Omega:\mathbb{P}(A)\leq s}\int_{A}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P},\ s\in(0,1).

Proof.

Since
\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}

\rho

\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h_{1},h_{2},\rho}\right)

Let

be the point that the closure of whose orbit contains

, and
\alpha_{0}
be as in the second part of Lemma
3.7
, and
\alpha_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{6}\alpha_{0})
. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists an
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(7.13)

\forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M),\ \exists g_{3}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)\text{ s.t. }g_{3}\in B(g_{1},\alpha_{1})\text{ and }\tilde{\phi}^{N_{1}}(g_{3})\in B(g_{2},\alpha_{1}),

where

is the
r
-ball centered at
h
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
.

Given
\rho\in(0,\alpha_{1})
and
h_{1},h_{2}\in C(\Omega,M)
, we denote
\Omega_{1}=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h_{1},h_{2},\rho}
. For
\alpha_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}\rho)
, let

be such that
\tilde{\phi}^{n}(g)\in B(h_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
\tilde{\phi}^{n+k}(g)\in B(h_{2},\alpha_{2})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that

. By the choice of
\alpha_{1}
and
N_{1}

h_{3}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
satisfying the following

h_{3}\in B(\tilde{\phi}^{n+k-N_{1}}(g),\alpha_{1})\text{ and }\tilde{\phi}^{N_{1}}(h_{3})\in B(h_{1},\alpha_{1}).

Define a periodic sequence
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
with period
k
by the following:

(7.14)

g_{i}=\begin{cases}\tilde{\phi}^{i+n}(g),&\text{when }0\leq i\leq k-N_{1}-1\\
\tilde{\phi}^{i+N_{1}}(g)\chi_{\Omega\setminus\theta^{-N_{1}}\Omega_{1}}+h_{3}\chi_{\theta^{-N_{1}}\Omega_{1}},&\text{when }i=k-N_{1}\\
\tilde{\phi}^{i-k+N_{1}}g_{k-N_{1}},&\text{when }k-N_{1}+1\leq i\leq k-1\\
g_{i\mod k}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

where
\chi_{O}
is the characteristic function of

.
It is clear that

\tilde{\phi}(g_{i})\in B(g_{i+1},\rho+2\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}),\ \forall i\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus, using
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\rho\in(0,\frac{1}{6}\alpha_{0})
and Lemma
3.7
, we obtain that there exists a random periodic point
g^{\prime}
satisfying that

(7.15)

\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g^{\prime})\in B(g_{i},C(\rho+2\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2})),\ i\in\mathbb{Z},

where

.

Note that for each

where

g_{i}(\theta^{i}\omega)=\begin{cases}\tilde{\phi}^{n+i-k}(g)(\theta^{i}\omega)&k\leq i\leq 2k-1\\
\tilde{\phi}^{n+i}(g)(\theta^{i}\omega)&0\leq i\leq k-1\\
\tilde{\phi}^{n+k+i}(g)(\theta^{i}\omega)&-k\leq i\leq-1\\
\end{cases}.

Thus, we have a
(\rho+\alpha_{1},\omega)
-pseudo orbit

\left(g_{i}(\theta^{i}\omega),\theta^{i}\omega\right)&-k\leq i\leq 2k-1\\
\phi^{i+k}\left(g_{-k}(\theta^{-k}\omega),\theta^{-k}\omega\right)&i\leq-k-1\\
\end{cases}.

Here

(\rho+2\alpha_{2},\omega)

(\rho+2\alpha_{2},\omega)
-pseudo orbit. By Lemma
3.5
and the choice of
\alpha_{2}
and
\rho
, we have that there exists a true orbit
\{\phi^{i}(y^{\prime},\omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
which is
(C(\rho+2\alpha_{2}),\omega)
-shadowing
\{(y_{i},\theta^{i}\omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Recall that
\{\phi^{i}(g^{\prime}(\omega),\omega)\}_{-k\leq i\leq 2k}
is
(C(\rho+2\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}),\omega)
-shadowing
\{\left(g_{i}(\theta^{i}\omega),\theta^{i}\omega\right)\}_{-k\leq i\leq 2k-1}
, which means that

\max_{-k\leq i\leq 2k-1}d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(g^{\prime}(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(y^{\prime},\omega)\right)\leq C(2\alpha_{1}+4\alpha_{2}+2\rho),\ \forall\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}.

Note that
k
can be chosen arbitrarily large. Hence Lemma
3.6
implies that one can make
d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{k-1}(g^{\prime}(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{k-1}(y^{\prime},\omega)\right)
and

arbitrarily small by taking

large enough. It is worth to point out that the largeness of

is depending on
C(2\alpha_{1}+4\alpha_{2}+2\rho)
only while it is nothing to do with the choice on specific
\omega
as long as
\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
. Now we choose
k
large enough so that the following holds

\max\left\{d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{k}(g^{\prime}(\omega),\omega),\pi_{M}\phi^{k}(y^{\prime},\omega)\right),d_{M}\left(g^{\prime}(\omega),y^{\prime}\right)\right\}<C\rho,\ \forall\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}.

Thus

(7.16)

\max\left\{d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{k}(g^{\prime}(\omega),\omega),\tilde{\phi}^{n+k}(g)(\theta^{k}\omega)\right),d_{M}\left(g^{\prime}(\omega),\tilde{\phi}^{n}(g)(\omega)\right)\right\}<4C\rho,\ \forall\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime},

here we used the fact that
C(2\rho+2\alpha_{2})\leq 4C\rho
.

Therefore, by using the same argument as in (
7.3
), we obtain that

(7.17)

d_{M}\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g^{\prime})(\theta^{i}\omega),\tilde{\phi}^{n+i}(g)(\theta^{i}\omega)\right)\leq 16e^{-\min\{i,k-i\}\lambda}C\rho,\ \forall\ \omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime},0\leq i\leq k.

Now we are ready to estimate
\left|\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}(h_{2})-\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}(h_{1})\right|
:

\displaystyle\left|\hat{\Psi}(h_{2})-\hat{\Psi}(h_{1})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\hat{\Psi}(h_{2})-\sum_{i=0}^{n+k}\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g))\right|+\left|\hat{\Psi}(h_{1})-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g))\right|+\left|\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+k}\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g))\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\alpha}+\left|\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+k}\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g))\right|\ \left(C^{\prime}\text{ is the H\"{o}lder constant of functional }\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\alpha}+\left|\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+k}\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g))-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g^{\prime}))\right|\ \left(g^{\prime}\text{ is a periodic point of }\tilde{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i+n}(g))-\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g^{\prime}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\theta^{i}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime})}\left|\Phi\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i+n}(g)(\omega),\omega\right)-\Phi\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g^{\prime})(\omega),\omega\right)\right|d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k-N_{1}}\int_{\theta^{i}(\Omega^{\prime})}\left|\Phi\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i+n}(g)(\omega),\omega\right)-\Phi\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g^{\prime})(\omega),\omega\right)\right|d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=k-N_{1}+1}^{k}\int_{\theta^{i}(\Omega^{\prime})}\left|\Phi\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i+n}(g)(\omega),\omega\right)-\Phi\left(\tilde{\phi}^{i}(g^{\prime})(\omega),\omega\right)\right|d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\theta^{i}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime})}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}\left(16LC\rho\right)^{\alpha}e^{-\min\{i,k-i\}\alpha\lambda}d\mathbb{P}\ \left(\text{by }(\ref{E:EstOnOrbSeg})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k-N_{1}}\int_{\theta^{i}(\Omega^{\prime})}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}\left(20LC\alpha_{1}\right)^{\alpha}e^{-\min\{i,k-N_{1}-i\}\alpha\lambda}d\mathbb{P}\ \left(\text{by }(\ref{E:PeriodicShadowg'})\text{ and }(\ref{E:EstOnOrbit})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=k-N_{1}+1}^{k}\int_{\theta^{i}(\Omega^{\prime})}2\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\alpha}+\frac{2\left(16LC\rho\right)^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha\lambda}}\int_{\Omega}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P}+\left(\frac{2\left(20LC\alpha_{1}\right)^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha\lambda}}+2N_{1}\right)\overline{\Phi}(\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})),

where the fact that

is used.

Note that, in the last line of above estimate,
\alpha_{1}
and
N_{1}
are fixed while

can be chosen arbitrarily small which consequentially forces

and

tend to

\displaystyle\left|\hat{\Psi}(h_{2})-\hat{\Psi}(h_{1})\right|\leq

\displaystyle L^{\prime}\overline{\Phi}(\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime}))+L^{\prime}\rho^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L^{\prime}\overline{\Phi}(3\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1}))+L^{\prime}\rho^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P},

where
L^{\prime}=\max\left\{\frac{2\left(16C\right)^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha\lambda}},\frac{2\left(20C\alpha_{1}\right)^{\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha\lambda}}+2N_{1}\right\}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Now we are ready to define the functional
\tilde{\Psi}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
based on
\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}
and Lemma
7.5
. For any
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
and
\epsilon>0
, by Lusin’s Theorem, there exists

such that
\mathbb{P}(\{\omega\in\Omega|\ h(\omega)\neq h_{\epsilon}(\omega)\})<\epsilon
. Let
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive numbers, then by taking
\rho
arbitrarily small while applying Lemma
7.5
, we have that

\left|\hat{\Psi}(h_{\epsilon_{n}})-\hat{\Psi}(h_{\epsilon_{k}})\right|\leq L^{\prime}\overline{\Phi}\left(3(\epsilon_{n}+\epsilon_{k})\right),\ \forall n,k\geq 1.

Since
\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\cdot})}\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)
, the absolute continuity of
\int\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P}
implies that

\{\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}(h_{\epsilon_{i}})\}_{i\geq 1}

is a Cauchy sequence as long as
\lim_{i\to\infty}\epsilon_{i}=0
. Thus

\lim_{i\to\infty}\hat{\Psi}(h_{\epsilon_{i}})
exists when
\lim_{i\to\infty}\epsilon_{i}=0
, and moreover such limit is independent on the choice of
\{h_{\epsilon_{i}}\}_{i\geq 1}
.
Thereafter, define

(7.18)

\tilde{\Psi}(h)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\hat{\Psi}(h_{\epsilon}).

It is clear that
\tilde{\Psi}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous by (
7.12
), and the absolute continuity of

\int\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(M_{\omega})}d\mathbb{P}

together with (
7.6
) implies that

\tilde{\Phi}(h)=\tilde{\Psi}(\tilde{\phi}(h))-\tilde{\Psi}(h),\ \forall h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

It remains to show that such
\tilde{\Psi}
is uniquely defined up to a constant functional. Suppose that both
\tilde{\Psi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\Psi}_{2}
satisfy (
7.6
). Then for any

, we have that
\tilde{\Psi}_{1}-\tilde{\Psi}_{2}
is constantly valued on

. Since the closure of

contains

for some

, and by the Hölder continuity of

, we have that
\tilde{\Psi}_{1}-\tilde{\Psi}_{2}
is constantly valued on
C(\Omega,M)
, which means that
\hat{\Psi}_{1}-\hat{\Psi}_{2}
is a constant functional on

. Thus,
\tilde{\Psi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\Psi}_{2}
defined by (

) varies at a constant functional. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.4
.

8.
Examples

In this section, we give concrete examples which can be adapted into the theoretic framework in previous sections. The systems we consider mainly contains two types: a) Transitive fiber Anosov system driven by a quasi-periodic driven force; b) Fiber volume preserving Anosov system with a continuous driven force, of which the precise formulations will be given later. At the end of this section, we will make some further discussions on the random horseshoe with respect to a given marginal
\mathbb{P}
, and give some relevant results.

8.1.
Systems driven by a quasi-periodic force

In this section, we consider a type of Anosov systems which is driven by a quasi-periodic force. This section contains two main parts: in the first part, we state and prove the general result in a general setting; in the second part, we will investigate a concrete example which is generated by rotation on torus and affine Anosov maps on 2-d torus, and will show that such a system satisfies the Conditions 1A.-1C. below and illustrate some interesting phenomenas (see Remark
8.2
).

8.1.1.
Setting and results

We assume that the systems satisfy the following conditions:

1A.

(\theta,\Omega)
is a minimal irrational rotation on a compact torus;

1B.

\phi
is Anosov on fibers (Condition H1));

1C.

\phi
is topological transitive on
M\times\Omega
.

For the sake of convenience, we call the systems satisfying Conditions 1A.-1C. the S1) systems.
The main result we will prove is the following Theorem:

Theorem 8.1
.

All S1) systems satisfy Condition H2) that is topological mixing on fibers.

We first prove a weaker version of Theorem
2.1
for S1) systems, which is the following proposition:

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\phi
be an S1) system. Then for any
g\in C(\Omega,M)
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a random periodic point
\tilde{g}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)

(8.1)

d_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(g,\tilde{g})\leq\epsilon.

Proof.

Actually, this proposition still holds when
\theta
is assumed to be equicontinuous only. We say
\theta
is

if there exist

and
\delta>0
such that for any
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega
with

\frac{1}{c}d_{\Omega}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\leq d_{\Omega}(\theta^{n}\omega_{1},\theta^{n}\omega_{2})\leq cd_{\Omega}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

When
\theta
is equicontinuous, if we set

\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{\Omega}(\theta^{n}\omega_{1},\theta^{n}\omega_{2}),\,\forall\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega,

then
\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}
is a metric on
\Omega

•

\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}
and

are equivalent;

•

\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}(\theta^{n}\omega_{1},\theta^{n}\omega_{2})=\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
for any
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega

and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Therefore, without loss of generality, for an equicontinuous
\theta

(8.2)

d_{\Omega}(\theta^{n}\omega_{1},\theta^{n}\omega_{2})=d_{\Omega}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\ \forall\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

\omega

\delta

\bigcup_{\xi_{i}\in\xi}B_{g}(\xi_{i},\delta)

Lemma 8.1

For any

Lemma 8.6

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k,l^{\prime},n^{\prime}}e^{2\pi ik\omega}=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k,(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})A}e^{2\pi ik(\omega-\alpha)}\right)e^{-2\pi i(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})h(\omega-\alpha)}

in
L^{2}(m_{\mathbb{T}})
.
Thus

\int\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k,l^{\prime},n^{\prime}}e^{2\pi ik\omega}\right|^{2}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\omega)=\int\left|\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k,(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})A}e^{2\pi ik(\omega-\alpha)}\right)e^{-2\pi i(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})h(\omega-\alpha)}\right|^{2}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\omega).

Hence

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k,l^{\prime},n^{\prime}}|^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k,(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})A}|^{2}.

When
(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
,
(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})A^{m}\neq(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})A^{k}
for
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\neq k
.
Hence if
(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k,l^{\prime},n^{\prime}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k,(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})A^{i}}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(k,l,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|c_{k,l,n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Let
N\nearrow+\infty
, we get
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k,l^{\prime},n^{\prime}}|^{2}=0
for
(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
.
When
(l^{\prime},n^{\prime})=(0,0)
, by (
8.17
) we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k,0,0}e^{2\pi ik\omega}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k,0,0}e^{-2\pi ik\alpha}e^{2\pi ik\omega}

in
L^{2}(m_{\mathbb{T}})
.
Thus

c_{k,0,0}=c_{k,0,0}e^{-2\pi ik\alpha}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. When
k\neq 0
, we have
c_{k,0,0}=0
as
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
.
Summing over we get
f=c_{0,0,0}
. This shows that
(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}},\phi,\mu)
is ergodic. The proof of this lemma is complete.

∎

For a continuous function
p
from
\mathbb{T}
to
\mathbb{T}
, we denote the degree of
p
by
deg(p)
.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
deg(h_{i})=n_{i}
for
i=1,2
. If
n_{2}
is odd number, then there are no
g\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{T}^{2})
and positive integer
n
such that

\phi^{n}(graph(g))=graph(g)
, that is,
(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T},\phi)
has no random periodic point whose graph is continuous.

Proof.

If this is not true, then there are
g(\omega)=\binom{g_{1}(\omega)}{g_{2}(\omega)}\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{T}^{2})
and positive integer
n
such that

\phi^{n}(graph(g))=graph(g)
, i.e.

\phi^{n}(g(\omega),\omega)=(g(\omega+n\alpha),\omega+n\alpha)

for
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
.
Let
deg(g_{i})=k_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Note that

\phi^{n}(g(\omega),\omega)=\left(A^{n}\binom{g_{1}(\omega)}{g_{2}(\omega)}+A^{n-1}\binom{h_{1}(\omega)}{h_{2}(\omega)}+\cdots+\binom{h_{1}(\omega+(n-1)\alpha)}{h_{2}(\omega+(n-1)\alpha)},\omega+n\alpha\right)

Hence

\displaystyle\binom{g_{1}(\omega+n\alpha)}{g_{2}(\omega+n\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{n}\binom{g_{1}(\omega)}{g_{2}(\omega)}+A^{n-1}\binom{h_{1}(\omega)}{h_{2}(\omega)}+A^{n-2}\binom{h_{1}(\omega+\alpha)}{h_{2}(\omega+\alpha)}+\cdots+\binom{h_{1}(\omega+(n-1)\alpha)}{h_{2}(\omega+(n-1)\alpha)}.

Compare the degree of continuous functions from
\mathbb{T}
to
\mathbb{T}
appearing in the above equation, we have

\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}}=A^{n}\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}}+A^{n-1}\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}}+A^{n-2}\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}}+\cdots+\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}}.

That is

(I-A^{n})\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}}=(I+A+\cdots+A^{n-1})\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}}.

Since
\det(I-A^{n})\neq 0
and
(I-A)(I+A+\cdots+A^{n-1})=I-A^{n}
, we have

(I-A)\binom{k_{1}}{k_{2}}=\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}}.

This implies
k_{1}=-\frac{n_{2}}{2}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, a contradiction. The proof is complete.

8.2.
Systems preserving a volume on fibers driven by a force

In this section, we consider a type of Anosov systems which are volume-preserving on fiber. We first state and prove the mixing property in a relatively general setting. Then, we investigate a concrete example which is generated by the random composition of matrices on 2-d torus, whose entries are positive integers, and show such a system satisfies the Conditions 2A.-2C. below.

8.2.1.
Setting and results

We assume that the systems satisfy the following conditions:

2A.

(\theta,\Omega)
is a heomomorphism on a compact metric space;

2B.

\phi
is Anosov on fibers (Condition H1));

2C.

There exists an
f
-invariant Borel probability measure
\nu
with full support (i.e.
supp\nu=M
).

Here by a Borel probability measure
\nu
on
M
being
f
-invariant
we mean that for any Borel measurable set
A\subset M
and any
\omega\in\Omega
,
\nu(f_{\omega}^{-1}(A))=\nu(A)
.

For the sake of convenience, we call the systems satisfying Conditions 2A.-2C. the S2) systems.
The main result we will prove is the following Theorem:

Theorem 8.2
.

S2) systems satisfity Condition H2) that is topological mixing on fibers.

Proof.

Unlike the case of S1) systems, the proof for S2) is based on the measure preserving property of
f_{\omega}
, which leads to a much more direct approach.

For a given
\omega\in\Omega
, an open subset
V\subset M
, and a real number
r>0
, we inductively define a sequence of subsets
V^{r}_{n}(\omega)\subset M
,
n=0,1,2,\cdots
as follows:

(8.18)

V^{r}_{n}(\omega)=\begin{cases}V,&n=0\\
\cup_{x\in f_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}(V^{r}_{n-1}(\omega))}B_{M}(x,r),&n>1\end{cases},

where
B_{M}(x,r)
is the ball in
M
with radius
r
centered at
x
. It is not hard to see that each
V^{r}_{n}(\omega)
is open and
\{\nu(V^{r}_{n}(\omega))\}_{n=0,1,\cdots}
is non-decreasing on both
n
and
r
since
\nu
is
f
-invariant and
f
is invertible. Thus
\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(V^{r}_{n}(\omega))
exists.

Next, we show that

(8.19)

\forall\ r>0,\exists\ N(\omega)>0\text{ such that }\forall\ n\geq N(\omega),V^{r}_{n}(\omega)=M.

Since
supp\ \nu=M
, we know that any open subset of
M
is not a
\nu
-null set. Additionally, because
M
is compact, for any
R>0
,
M
can be covered by finitely many balls with radius
\frac{1}{3}R
, each of which has a positive
\nu
-measure. Thus,

(8.20)

\forall\ R>0,\exists\ \epsilon>0,\text{ such that }\forall\ x\in M,\ \nu(B_{M}(x,R))>\epsilon.

For a given
n\geq 1
, let
t^{r}_{n}(\omega)=\min\{\sup\{d_{H}(x,f_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}(V^{r}_{n-1}(\omega))|\ x\in M\},r\}
, where for
x\in M
and
A\subset M

d_{H}(x,A):=\inf\{d_{M}(x,y)|\ y\in A\}

is the Hausdoff distance. Since
d_{H}(x,V^{r}_{n}(\omega))
is continuous on
x
and
M
is connected, we have that there exists
x^{\prime}\in M
such that

\frac{1}{2}t^{r}_{n}(\omega)<d_{H}(x^{\prime},f_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}(V^{r}_{n-1}(\omega)))<r.

Therefore,
B\left(x^{\prime},\frac{1}{2}t^{r}_{n}(\omega)\right)\subset V^{r}_{n}(\omega)\setminus f_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}(V^{r}_{n-1}(\omega))
which implies that

\nu\left(B_{M}\left(x^{\prime},\frac{1}{2}t^{r}_{n}(\omega)\right)\right)\leq\nu(V^{r}_{n}(\omega))-\nu(f_{\theta^{n-1}\omega}(V^{r}_{n-1}(\omega)))=\nu(V^{r}_{n}(\omega))-\nu(V^{r}_{n-1}(\omega)(\omega)).

Because of the existence of
\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(V^{r}_{n}(\omega))
and (
8.20
), we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}^{r}=0.

By the definition of
V_{n}^{r}(\omega)
as in (
8.18
), once
t_{n}^{r}<r
,
V_{n}^{r}(\omega)=M
, thus (
8.19
) holds.

Now, we are ready to show that S2)-type systems are topological mixing on fibers.

Let
U_{1},U_{2}\subset M
be two nonempty open sets. Choose two open balls
B_{M}(x_{i},3R)\subset U_{i}
,
i=1,2
, with
R>0
. Applying Lemma
3.4
by setting
\beta=R
, we have an
\alpha>0
, such that any
(\omega,\alpha)
-pseudo orbit can be
(\omega,\beta)
-shadowed by a true orbit.

For a given
\omega\in\Omega
, take
V=B_{M}(x_{1},R)
and
r=\alpha
, and define
V^{r}_{n}(\omega)
as in (
8.18
). By (
8.19
), there exists an
N_{1}(\omega)
such that for any
n\geq N_{1}(\omega)
,
V_{n}^{r}(\omega)=M
. By continuity of
\phi
, we have that for any
\omega\in\Omega
, there exists
\delta(\omega)>0
such that the following holds:

\forall\ \omega^{\prime}\in B_{\Omega}(\omega,\delta(\omega))\text{ and }n\geq N_{1}(\omega)+1,\ V^{r}_{n}(\omega^{\prime})=M,

where
B_{\Omega}(\omega,\delta(\omega))
is the ball in
\Omega
with radius
\delta(\omega)
centered at
\omega
.

Since
\Omega
is compact, and
\{B_{\Omega}(\omega,\delta(\omega))\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
forms an open cover on
M
, there exists a finite sub-cover,
\{B_{\Omega}(\omega_{j},\delta(\omega_{j})\}_{1\leq j\leq q},\ q\in\mathbb{N}
. Take
N=\max\{N_{1}(\omega_{1}),\cdots,N_{1}(\omega_{q})\}+1
, then the following holds

(8.21)

\forall\ \omega\in\Omega,\text{ if }n\geq N,\text{ then }V^{r}_{n}(\omega)=M.

Next, we show that

(8.22)

\forall\ \omega\in\Omega\text{ and }n\geq N,\ \phi(n,U_{1}\times\{\omega\})\cap U_{2}\times\{\theta^{n}\omega\}\neq\emptyset.

This can be done by constructing an
(\omega,r)
-pseudo orbit,
\{(y^{\prime}_{n,i},\theta^{i}\omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, connecting two balls
B_{M}(x_{1},R)\times\{\omega\}
and
B_{M}(x_{2},R)\times\{\theta^{n}\omega\}
, which is
(\omega,R)
-shadowed by a true orbit

\{(y_{n,i},\theta^{i}\omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Thus this true orbit,
\{(y_{n,i},\theta^{i}\omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, will hit both
B_{M}(x_{1},3R)\times\{\omega\}
and
B_{M}(x_{2},3R)\times\{\theta^{n}\omega\}
. Hence (
8.22
) holds. Actually, the existence of such an
(\omega,r)
-pseudo orbits (
\forall\ \omega\in\Omega
),
\{(y^{\prime}_{n,i},\theta^{i}\omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, follows from (
8.21
), which can be constructed in the following:

For any
n\geq N
and
\omega\in\Omega
, since
V_{N}^{r}(\omega)=M
, we have that there exists
z_{N-1}(\omega)\in V_{N-1}^{r}(\omega)
such that

\pi_{M}\phi^{-(n-N)}(x_{2},\theta^{n}\omega)\in B_{M}(f_{\theta^{N-1}\omega}z_{N-1}(\omega),r).

Inductively, we have that for any
i\in\{0,\cdots,N-2\}
, there exists
z_{i}(\omega)\in V_{i}^{r}(\omega)
such that

z_{i+1}(\omega)\in B_{M}(f_{\theta^{i}\omega}z_{i}(\omega),r).

Also note that
V_{0}^{r}(\omega)=B_{M}(x_{1},R)
, then we have that
\{z_{i}(\omega)\}_{1\leq i\leq N-1}
satisfies the following properties:

(8.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&d_{M}(z_{0}(\omega),x_{1})<r;\\
&d_{M}(z_{i+1}(\omega),f_{\theta^{i}\omega}z_{i}(\omega))<r,\ 0\leq i\leq N-2;\\
&d_{M}\left(\pi_{M}\phi^{-(n-N)}(x_{2},\theta^{n}\omega),f_{\theta^{N-1}\omega}z_{N-1}(\omega)\right)<r.\end{split}

We define

y^{\prime}_{n,i}=\begin{cases}\pi_{M}\phi^{i}(x_{1},\omega),&i\leq-1\\
z_{i}(\omega),&0\leq i\leq N-1\\
\pi_{M}\phi^{i-n}(x_{2},\theta^{n}\omega),&i\geq N\end{cases}.

By (
8.23
), it is not hard to see that the above
y^{\prime}_{n,i}
’s fit all the requirements. The proof is complete.

∎

8.2.2.
Random composition of
2\times 2
area-preserving positive matrices

The main purpose of this section is to provide an example of S2)-type systems, which is generated by the random composition of a class of area-preserving positive
2\times 2
matrices. Then, we apply the results for S2)-type systems to investigate the dynamical behavior of the given example.

Let

\left\{A_{i}=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{matrix}\right)\right\}_{1\leq i\leq p}

be
2\times 2
matrices with positive integer entries and

|\det A_{i}|=1,\ \forall i\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{p}:=\{1,\cdots,p\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
with the left shift operator
\sigma
be the symbolic dynamical system with
p
symbols.

Define a map
f:\mathcal{S}_{k}\to\{A_{1}\cdots,A_{p}\}
by letting

f(\hat{a})=A_{\hat{a}(0)},\ \forall\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}.

Define that, for
x\in[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
and
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
,

\phi^{n}_{\hat{a}}=\begin{cases}I_{2}&\text{ when }n=0\\
f(\sigma^{n-1}\hat{a})\circ\cdots\circ f(\hat{a})&\text{ when }n>0\\
f^{-1}(\sigma^{n}\hat{a})\circ\cdots\circ f^{-1}(\sigma^{-1}\hat{a})&\text{ when }n<0\\
\end{cases},

where
I_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)
.
Note that
\phi
induces a random dynamical system on 2 dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, which is denoted by
\hat{\phi}
. Let

(8.24)

\kappa:=\min_{1\leq i\leq p}\min\left\{\sqrt{a_{i}^{2}+c_{i}^{2}},\sqrt{b_{i}^{2}+d_{i}^{2}}\right\},

which is obviously greater than or equal to
\sqrt{2}
.

Proposition 8.2
.

\hat{\phi}:\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathcal{S}_{k}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathcal{S}_{k}
is an S2)-type system by letting
M=\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
(\Omega,\theta)=(\mathcal{S}_{p},\sigma)
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathbb{R}^{2+}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\Big|\ x\geq 0,y\geq 0\right\}
. For any
v_{i}=\left(\begin{matrix}x_{i}\\
y_{i}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2+}
,
i=1,2
, by a straightforward computation, we have that

\sin\angle(v_{1},v_{2})=\left|\frac{y_{2}x_{1}-y_{1}x_{2}}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}}\right|,

where
\angle(v_{1},v_{2})
is the angle between
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.

By a straightforward computation, we have that, for any
j\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
,
i=1,2
,

(8.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|A_{j}v_{i}|=\sqrt{(a_{j}^{2}+c_{j}^{2})x_{i}^{2}+2(a_{j}b_{j}+c_{j}d_{j})x_{i}y_{i}+(b_{j}^{2}+d_{j}^{2})y_{i}^{2}}\geq\kappa|v_{i}|\\
&\sin\angle(A_{j}v_{1},A_{j}v_{2})=\left|\frac{(a_{j}d_{j}-b_{j}c_{j})(y_{2}x_{1}-y_{1}x_{2})}{|A_{j}v_{1}||A_{j}v_{2}|}\right|\leq\kappa^{-2}\sin\angle(v_{1},v_{2}).\end{split}

Let
Proj^{+}:=\left\{\mathrm{span}\{v\}|\ v\in\mathbb{R}^{2+}\right\}
which is a compact subset of the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. It is not hard to see that the second inequality of (
8.25
) yields that for any
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
the map
\phi(\cdot,\hat{a})
induces a contraction map on
Proj^{+}
with a uniform contracting rate. By the contracting mapping theorem, we have that there is a unique
1
-dimensional space
E_{\hat{a}}
such that

E_{\hat{a}}\in\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\bar{\phi}^{n}(Proj^{+},\sigma^{-n}\hat{a}),

where we denote
\bar{\phi}(\cdot,\hat{a})
the induced map on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by
\phi(\cdot,\hat{a})
. By the uniqueness, we have that

\phi(E_{\hat{a}},\hat{a})=E_{\sigma\hat{a}}.

The first inequality of (
8.25
) yields that
\phi(|_{E_{\hat{a}}},\hat{a})
is expanding with rate at least
\kappa
. The continuity of
E_{\hat{a}}
on
\hat{a}
simply follows from the second inequality of (
8.25
).

By applying exactly the same argument, we can obtain that for any
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
there exists a unique
1
-dimensional space
F_{\hat{a}}\notin Proj^{+}
such that
F_{\hat{a}}
varies continuously on
\hat{a}
and the following holds

\phi(F_{\hat{a}},\hat{a})=F_{\sigma\hat{a}}\text{ and }\phi^{-1}(|_{F_{\hat{a}}},\hat{a})\text{is expanding with rate at least }\kappa.

Note that
\phi
itself is linear on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, therefore the linearization of
\hat{\phi}^{n}
,
D_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\hat{\phi}^{n}
, can be identified by
\phi
. Hence,
\hat{\phi}
is an S2)-type system with continuous co-invariant splitting
\mathbb{R}^{2}=E_{\hat{a}}\oplus F_{\hat{a}}
for
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
.

8.3.
Further discussions on Random Horseshoes with marginal
\mathbb{P}

In this section, we further discuss the random horseshoes for systems with a given marginal
\mathbb{P}
which is an
\theta
-invariant probability measure on
\Omega
. The standard fiber topological entropy of
\phi
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
is defined by the following

(8.26)

h_{top}(M\times\Omega|\ \mathbb{P})=\int_{\Omega}h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}})d\mathbb{P}(\omega).

Since
h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}})=h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\theta\omega}})
for
\omega\in\Omega
by the definition, we have that when
\mathbb{P}
is ergodic the following holds

h_{top}(M\times\Omega|\mathbb{P})\equiv h_{top}(\phi|_{M_{\omega}})\text{ for }\mathbb{P}-a.e.\ \omega.

For the random dynamical system
\phi
over a metric dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathbb{P},\theta)
mentioned in Remark
2.2
, it is natural to modify the definitions of random horseshoes correspondingly.

Let
L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)
be the space of Borel measurable maps from
\Omega
to
M
endowed with the metric
d_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}
:

d_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})=\sup_{\mathbb{P}}\left\{d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\omega\in\Omega\right\},\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,M),

where
\sup_{\mathbb{P}}
is the essential supreme with respect to
\mathbb{P}
. Similarly,
\phi:M\times\Omega
induces a homeomorphism on
L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)
, which is denoted by
\tilde{\phi}_{\mathbb{P}}
.
Analogically, we define two functions on
L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)
to measure separations of elements of
L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)
on different levels:

(8.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})&=\sup_{\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega,\ \mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})>0}\inf_{\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega),\\
\underline{d}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})&=\inf_{\mathbb{P}}\left\{d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \omega\in\Omega\right\},\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega),\end{split}

where
\inf_{\mathbb{P}}
is the essential infimum with respect to
\mathbb{P}
. First, we claim that

\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}=d_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}.

For the sake of completeness, we give the proof here. Recall that

d_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})=\inf_{\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega,\ \mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega)),\ \forall g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega).

For a given
\epsilon>0
, let

\Omega^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(g_{1},g_{2})=\left\{\omega\in\Omega|\ d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega))>d_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})-\epsilon\right\}.

By the definition of
d_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(g_{1},g_{2}))>0
. Thus

\overline{d}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})\geq\inf_{\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(g_{1},g_{2})}d_{M}(g_{1}(\omega),g_{2}(\omega))>d_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega)}(g_{1},g_{2})-\epsilon.

Let

By the definition of

By the arbitrariness of

and

\omega_{1}\in\Omega

Let

Definition A.1

A map

A map

Lemma A.1

F
is topological mixing on fibers (see Section

Let

It is not hard to see that each

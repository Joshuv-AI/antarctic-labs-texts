Deterministic three-photon down-conversion by a passive ultrastrong cavity-QED system
Kazuki Koshino, Tomohiro Shitara, Ziqiao Ao, Kouichi Semba

Abstract
In ultra- and deep-strong cavity quantum electrodynamics (QED) systems, many intriguing phenomena that do not conserve the excitation number are expected to occur. In this study, we theoretically analyze the optical response of an ultrastrong cavity-QED system in which an atom is coupled to the fundamental and third harmonic modes of a cavity, and report the possibility of deterministic three-photon down-conversion of itinerant photons upon reflection at the cavity. In the conventional parametric down-conversion, a strong input field is needed because of the smallness of the transition matrix elements of the higher order processes. However, if we use an atom-cavity system in an unprecedentedly strong-coupling region, even a weak field in the linear-response regime is sufficient to cause this rare event involving the fourth order transitions.

IV

In the following part of this paper,
we analyze the response of the qubit-cavity system to a waveguide field.
For this purpose, the input-output formalism is useful.
However, although highly useful in the weak- and (usual)
strong-coupling regimes of cavity QED,
the conventional formalism based on the white reservoir approximation
has several difficulties in treating the ultrastrong cavity QED

The input-output formalism applicable to the ultrastrong cavity QED
has been discussed first in linear systems

inout_NL2

However, assuming weak dissipation,

V.1
Initial state vector

In this study, instead of a single photon pulse,
we apply a weak classical monochromatic field
close to the resonance of the third cavity mode
[
{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)=E_{\mathrm{in}}e^{-i\omega_{\mathrm{in}}t}
with
\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\sim\omega_{3}
]
to the cavity (Fig.
1
).
Assuming that, at the initial moment (
t=0
),
the overall system is in the vacuum state
except the applied field, the initial state vector is written as

\displaystyle|\psi_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sqrt{2\pi}E_{\mathrm{in}}\hat{d}_{\omega_{\mathrm{in}}}^{{\dagger}}-\sqrt{2\pi}E_{\mathrm{in}}^{*}\hat{d}_{\omega_{\mathrm{in}}}\right)|vac\rangle,

(20)

where
|vac\rangle
is the overall vacuum state.
Note that this is an eigenstate of the noise operators:

\hat{\Gamma}^{(3e)}(t)|\psi_{i}\rangle=\sqrt{2\pi}\xi_{\omega_{\mathrm{in}}}^{(3e)}{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)|\psi_{i}\rangle

and
\hat{\Gamma}^{(j)}(t)|\psi_{i}\rangle=0
for
j=1e,1i,3i
, and
q
.

V.2
Density matrix elements

The Heisenberg equation for a system operator is given
by Eq. (
10
) with the dissipators and the noise operators
corresponding to the five decay channels (
1e
,
1i
,
3e
,
3i
and
q
).
The equation of motion for
s_{ij}(t)=\langle\psi_{i}|\hat{s}_{ij}(t)|\psi_{i}\rangle
,
which is identical to the density matrix element

\rho_{ji}(t)=\langle j|\hat{\rho}(t)|i\rangle
in the Schrödinger picture,
is then given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}s_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}\eta^{(1)}_{ijmn}s_{mn}+{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}^{*}(t)\sum_{m,n}\eta^{(2)}_{ijmn}s_{mn}+{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)\sum_{m,n}\eta^{(3)}_{ijmn}s_{mn},

(21)

where the coefficients
\eta^{(1,2,3)}_{ijmn}
are given by

\displaystyle\eta^{(1)}_{ijmn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})\delta_{im}\delta_{jn}+x_{mi}x_{jn}(h_{nj}^{(1)}+h_{mi}^{(1)*})-\delta_{im}\left(\sum_{l}x_{jl}x_{ln}h_{nl}^{(1)}\right)-\delta_{jn}\left(\sum_{l}x_{il}x_{lm}h_{ml}^{(1)*}\right)

(22)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle y_{mi}y_{jn}(h_{nj}^{(3)}+h_{mi}^{(3)*})-\delta_{im}\left(\sum_{l}y_{jl}y_{ln}h_{nl}^{(3)}\right)-\delta_{jn}\left(\sum_{l}y_{il}y_{lm}h_{ml}^{(3)*}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle z_{mi}z_{jn}(h_{nj}^{(q)}+h_{mi}^{(q)*})-\delta_{im}\left(\sum_{l}z_{jl}z_{ln}h_{nl}^{(q)}\right)-\delta_{jn}\left(\sum_{l}z_{il}z_{lm}h_{ml}^{(q)*}\right),

\displaystyle\eta^{(2)}_{ijmn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sqrt{2\pi}\xi_{\omega_{d}}^{(3e)}(y_{mi}\delta_{jn}-y_{jn}\delta_{im}),

(23)

\displaystyle\eta^{(3)}_{ijmn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta^{(2)}_{ijmn})^{*},

(24)

where
h_{ij}^{(1)}=h_{ij}^{(1e)}+h_{ij}^{(1i)}
and

h_{ij}^{(3)}=h_{ij}^{(3e)}+h_{ij}^{(3i)}
.
In this study, we apply a continuous field and
observe the stationary response of the system.
Therefore, we numerically determine
the stationary solution of these simultaneous equations
by perturbation with respect to
{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)
.
Further details on analysis are presented in Appendix
B
.

V.3
Photon flux

Since we treat a stationary input/output field,
we quantify the amount of photons by the photon flux, namely,
the rate of incoming/outgoing photons per unit time.
The input photon flux is evaluated by

F_{\mathrm{in}}=\langle\psi_{i}|\hat{d}_{\mathrm{in}}^{{\dagger}}(t)\hat{d}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)|\psi_{i}\rangle
.
From Eq. (
20
), this quantity reduces to

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)|^{2}=|E_{\mathrm{in}}|^{2}.

(25)

In the output port,
the fluxes of down-converted and unconverted photons
are respectively evaluated by

F_{\mathrm{out}}^{1}=\langle\psi_{i}|\hat{c}_{\mathrm{out}}^{{\dagger}}(t)\hat{c}_{\mathrm{out}}(t)|\psi_{i}\rangle
and

F_{\mathrm{out}}^{3}=\langle\psi_{i}|\hat{d}_{\mathrm{out}}^{{\dagger}}(t)\hat{d}_{\mathrm{out}}(t)|\psi_{i}\rangle
.
From Eq. (
19
) and its counterpart for
\hat{d}_{\mathrm{out}}
,
these quantities are given by

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{out}}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\sum_{i,j}\left(\sum_{m}x_{mi}x_{mj}\xi^{(1e)}_{\varepsilon_{im}}\xi^{(1e)}_{\varepsilon_{jm}}\right)s_{ij}(t),

(26)

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{out}}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)|^{2}+2\pi\sum_{i,j}\left(\sum_{m}y_{mi}y_{mj}\xi^{(3e)}_{\varepsilon_{im}}\xi^{(3e)}_{\varepsilon_{jm}}\right)s_{ij}(t)+i\sqrt{2\pi}\sum_{i,j}\xi_{\varepsilon_{ji}}^{(3e)}y_{ij}\left[s_{ji}(t){\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)-\mathrm{c.c.}\right].

(27)

VI.4
Dependence on input photon rate

In the previous subsections, we discussed
the down-conversion efficiency assuming a low input photon rate,
in other words, the linear-response limit.
Here, we observe the conversion efficiency for a higher input photon rate.
In Fig.
7
, we plot the dependence of the conversion efficiency
on the input photon rate for various detuning of the input field.
We observe that the efficiency decreases gradually for higher input photon rate.
This is due to saturation of the atom-cavity system,
which originates from the nonlinearity of the qubit.
The star symbols in Fig.
7
represent the onset of saturation,
which is given by

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{(\kappa_{e}/2)^{2}+(\Delta\omega)^{2}}{10\kappa_{e}},

(30)

where
\Delta\omega
is the detuning of the input field frequency
from its optimal value. This is derived as follows.
When one applies a monochromatic field
{\cal E}(t)=E_{\mathrm{in}}e^{-i\omega_{\mathrm{in}}t}

to an empty one-sided cavity with an external decay rate
\kappa_{e}
,
the mean intracavity photon number
\overline{n}

is proportional to the drive photon rate
F_{\mathrm{in}}=|E_{\mathrm{in}}|^{2}

and is given by
\overline{n}=\kappa_{e}F_{\mathrm{in}}/|\kappa_{e}/2+i\Delta\omega|^{2}
.
In the present system, the saturation effect due to nonlinearity
would appear when the cavity is populated substantially.
If we set this criterion at
\overline{n}\sim 0.1
for example,
the onset of saturation is estimated by Eq. (
30
).
This explains the fact that
the onset of saturation occurs at a higher input photon rate
for a larger detuning.

VII
Summary

We theoretically proved the possibility of the deterministic
three-photon down-conversion of itinerant photons
using a passive ultrastrong cavity QED system,
in which an atom is coupled to the fundamental and third-harmonic cavity modes.
For this purpose, we developed an input-output formalism
applicable to highly dissipative cavity QED systems.
The conditions for the deterministic conversion are as follows:
(i) the frequencies of the qubit and the cavity modes are adequately chosen
so that the two relevant levels (
|g30\rangle
and
|g01\rangle
) are coupled effectively,
and (ii) the cavity loss rates are adequately chosen
so that they are comparable to the effective coupling.
Such down-conversion is characteristic to
the ultrastrong coupling regime of cavity QED,
considering the upper limit of the intrinsic loss rates of the cavity.

Appendix B
Stationary solution of Eq. (
21
)

In this Appendix, we present the method to determine
the stationary solution of Eq. (
21
) perturbatively.
As the stationary solution, we employ the following form,

\displaystyle\langle s_{ij}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q=0}^{\infty}\overline{s}_{ij}^{(p,q)}[{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}^{*}(t)]^{p}[{\cal E}_{\mathrm{in}}(t)]^{q},

(31)

where
\overline{s}_{ij}^{(p,q)}
is time independent.
Substituting Eq. (
31
) into Eq. (
21
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}\left(\eta^{(1)}_{ijmn}-i(p-q)\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\delta_{im}\delta_{jn}\right)\overline{s}_{mn}^{(p,q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m,n}\left(\eta^{(2)}_{ijmn}\overline{s}_{mn}^{(p-1,q)}+\eta^{(3)}_{ijmn}\overline{s}_{mn}^{(p,q-1)}\right),

(32)

with the understanding that
\overline{s}_{ij}^{(p,q)}=0

if
p
or
q
is negative.
This is a matrix equation
which we determines
\overline{s}_{ij}^{(p,q)}

from the lower-order quantities,

\overline{s}_{ij}^{(p-1,q)}
and
\overline{s}_{ij}^{(p,q-1)}
.
Note that this matrix equation is indeterminate for
p=q
.
Then, we add the normalization condition of the density matrix,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\overline{s}_{jj}^{(p,p)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{p,0}.

(33)

Appendix C
Impedance matching condition

Figure 9:

Schematic of the coupled oscillators-waveguides system.

We consider a linear system composed of
two harmonic oscillators (oscillator 1 and 2) and
two waveguides (waveguide 1 and 2), as depicted in Fig.
9
.
The two oscillators, which model the levels

|g01\rangle
and
|g30\rangle
of the main text,
have the same resonance frequency
\omega_{c}

and are coupled with a coupling constant
g_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
Oscillator
j
(
j=1,2
) is coupled to waveguide
j

with an external decay rate
\kappa_{j}
.
We denote the annihilation operator of oscillator
j
by
\hat{a}_{j}
,
and the input and output field operators of waveguide
j

by
\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},j}
and
\hat{b}_{\mathrm{out},j}
, respectively.
The Heisenberg equations for the two oscillators
and the input-output relations are given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{a}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i\omega_{c}-\kappa_{1}/2)\hat{a}_{1}-ig_{\mathrm{eff}}\hat{a}_{2}-i\sqrt{\kappa_{1}}\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},1},

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{a}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i\omega_{c}-\kappa_{2}/2)\hat{a}_{2}-ig_{\mathrm{eff}}\hat{a}_{1}-i\sqrt{\kappa_{2}}\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},2},

(35)

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{out},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},1}-i\sqrt{\kappa_{1}}\hat{a}_{1},

(36)

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{out},2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},2}-i\sqrt{\kappa_{2}}\hat{a}_{2}.

(37)

We apply a classical monochromatic field
at frequency
\omega_{\mathrm{in}}
and amplitude
E_{\mathrm{in}}

through waveguide 1, and apply no field through waveguide 2.
Namely,
\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},1}\rangle=E_{\mathrm{in}}e^{-i\omega_{\mathrm{in}}t}
and

\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},2}\rangle=0
.
Then, the equations of motion for the cavity and waveguide amplitudes are given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{a}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i\omega_{c}-\kappa_{1}/2)\langle\hat{a}_{1}\rangle-ig_{\mathrm{eff}}\langle\hat{a}_{2}\rangle-i\sqrt{\kappa_{1}}\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},1}\rangle,

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{a}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i\omega_{c}-\kappa_{2}/2)\langle\hat{a}_{2}\rangle-ig_{\mathrm{eff}}\langle\hat{a}_{1}\rangle,

(39)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{out},1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},1}\rangle-i\sqrt{\kappa_{1}}\langle\hat{a}_{1}\rangle,

(40)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{out},2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\sqrt{\kappa_{2}}\langle\hat{a}_{2}\rangle.

(41)

The stationary solution is readily obtained
by the replacement of
d/dt\to-i\omega_{\mathrm{in}}

in Eqs. (
38
) and (
39
).
The transmission coefficient,

T=\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{out},2}\rangle/\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},1}\rangle
, is then given by

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{out},2}\rangle}{\langle\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in},1}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\sqrt{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}g_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}/4+g_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}.

(42)

The impedance-matching condition,
|T|=1
,
reduces to
\sqrt{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}=2g_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
This is in agreement with the optimal condition
of the external cavity decay rate,
\kappa_{e}^{\mathrm{opt}}\sim g_{\mathrm{eff}}
,
derived in Sec.
VI.1
.

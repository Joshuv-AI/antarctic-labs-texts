Abstract
Given a graph G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G , we define 𝐛𝐜𝐠 ⁡ ( G ) \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf bcg}(G) as the minimum k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}k for which G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G can be contracted to the uniformly triangulated grid Γ k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k} . A graph class 𝒢 \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G} has the SQG C property if every graph G ∈ 𝒢 \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G\in{\cal G} has treewidth 𝒪 ⁡ ( 𝐛𝐜𝐠 ​ ( G ) c ) \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mathcal{O}({\bf bcg}(G)^{c}) for some 1 ≤ c < 2 \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}1\leq c<2 . The SQG C property is important for algorithm design as it defines the applicability horizon of a series of meta-algorithmic results, in the framework of bidimensionality theory, related to fast parameterized algorithms, kernelization, and approximation schemes. These results apply to a wide family of problems, namely problems that are contraction-bidimensional . Our main combinatorial result reveals a wide family of graph classes that satisfy the SQG C property. This family includes, in particular, bounded-degree string graphs. This considerably extends the applicability of bidimensionality theory for contraction bidimensional problems.

Treewidth, Bidimensionality, Parameterized Algorithms

Treewidth

Gavril is the first to introduce the concept in

.
Before we present some key combinatorial properties of treewidth, we need some definitions.

Graph contractions and minors.

Our first aim is to define two parameterized versions of the contraction relation on graphs.

Definition 1

(Contractions)
.

Given a non-negative integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
,
two graphs
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, and
a surjection
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\sigma:V(G)\rightarrow V(H)
we write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq_{\sigma}^{c}G

if

•

for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x\in V(H)
, the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G[\sigma^{-1}(x)]
is a non-empty graph (i.e., a graph with at least one vertex) of
diameter
at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
and

•

for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x,y\in V(H),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\{x,y\}\in E(H)\iff G[\sigma^{-1}(x)\cup\sigma^{-1}(y)]
is connected.

We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
-diameter contraction
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
if there exists a surjection
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\sigma:V(G)\rightarrow V(H)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq_{\sigma}^{c}G
and we write this
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq^{c}G
.
Moreover, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\sigma
is such that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x\in V(H)
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}|\sigma^{-1}(x)|\leq c^{\prime}
,
then we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c^{\prime}
-size contraction
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, and we write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq^{(c^{\prime})}G
. Given two graphs
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
, if there exists an integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq^{c}G
, then we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is a
contraction
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, and we write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq G
.
Moreover, if there exists a subgraph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G^{\prime}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq G^{\prime}
, we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is a
minor
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
and we write this
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\preceq G
. Given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
, we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf excl}(H)
the class of graphs that exclude
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
as a minor.

Optimization parameters and bidimensionality.

A
graph parameter
is a function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
mapping graphs to non-negative integers.
We say that
p
is a
minimization graph parameter
if

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}(G)=\min\{k\mid\exists S\subseteq V(G):|S|\leq k\mbox{ and }\phi(G,S)={\tt true}\},
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\phi
is a some predicate on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}S
. Similarly, we say that
p
is a
maximization graph parameter
if in the above definition we we replace
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\min
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\leq
by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\max
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\geq
respectivelly.
Minimization or maximization parameters are briefly called
optimization parameters
.

Definition 3

(Bidimensionality)
.

Given two real functions
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}f
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}g
, we use the term
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}f\gtrsim g
to denote that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}f(x)\geq g(x)-o(g(x))
.
A graph parameter
p
is
minor-closed
(resp.
contraction-closed
) when
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\preceq G\Rightarrow{\bf p}(H)\leq{\bf p}(G)
(resp.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq G\Rightarrow{\bf p}(H)\leq{\bf p}(G)
).
We can now give the two following definitions:

p
is
minor-bidimensional
if

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
is minor-closed,
and

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}(\boxplus_{k})\gtrsim\delta\cdot k^{2}.

p
is
contraction-bidimensional
if

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
is contraction-closed,
and

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}(\Gamma_{k})\gtrsim\delta\cdot k^{2}.

for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\delta>0
. In the above definitions, we use
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\boxplus_{k}
for the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(k\times k)
-grid
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k}
for the uniformly triangulated
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(k\times k)
-grid (see Figure
1
).
If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
is a minimization (resp. maximization) graph parameter, we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Pi_{\bf p}
the problem
that, given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
and a non-negative integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}k
, asks whether
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}(G)\leq k
(resp.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}(G)\geq k
). We say that a problem is
minor/contraction-bidimensional
if it is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Pi_{\bf p}

for some bidimensional optimization parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
.

Subquadratic grid minor/contraction property.

In order to present the meta-algorithmic potential of bidimensionality theory we need to define some
property on graph classes
that defines the horizon of its applicability.

Definition 4

(SQG
C
and SQG
C
)
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
be a graph class. We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}

has the
subquadratic grid minor property
(SQG
M
property for short) if there exist a constant
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}1\leq c<2
such that every graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G\in{\cal G}
which excludes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\boxplus_{t}
as a minor, for some integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}t
, has treewidth
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mathcal{O}(t^{c})
. In other words, this property holds for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
if
Subsection 1.1

can be proven for a sub-quadratic
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}f
on the graphs of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
.

Similarly, we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}

has the
subquadratic grid contraction property
(SQG
C
property for short) if there exist a constant
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}1\leq c<2
such that every graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G\in{\cal G}
which excludes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{t}
as a contraction, for some integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}t
, has treewidth
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mathcal{O}(t^{c})
.
For brevity we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}\in\mbox{\rm SQGM}(c)
(resp.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}\in\mbox{\rm SQGC}(c)
) if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}

has the SQG
M
(resp SQG
C
) property for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
.
Notice that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGC}(c)\subseteq\mbox{\rm SQGM}(c)
for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}1\leq c<2
.

1.2
Algorithmic implications

The meta-algorithmic consequences of bidimensionality theory are summarised as follows.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}\in\mbox{\rm SQGM}(c)
, for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}1\leq c<2
, and let
p
be a minor-bidimensional-optimization parameter.

[A]

As it was observed in
[
10
]
, the problem
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Pi_{\bf p}
can be solved in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}2^{o({k})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
steps on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
,
given that the computation of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
can be done in

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}2^{\mathcal{O}({\bf tw}(G))}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
steps (here
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf tw}(G)
is the treewidth of the input graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
). This last condition can
be implied by a purely meta-algorithmic condition that is based on some
variant of
Modal Logic

[
37
]
.
There is a wealth of results that yield the last condition for various
optimization problems either in classes satisfying the SQG
M
propety
[
42
,
19
,
18
,
18
,
41
]
or to general graphs
[
7
,
3
,
24
]
.

[B]

As it was shown in
[
26
]
(see also
[
27
]
), when the predicate
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\phi

can be expressed in Counting Monadic Second Order Logic (CMSOL) and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
satisfies some additional combinatorial property called
separability
, then the problem
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Pi_{\bf p}

admits a
linear kernel
, that is a polynomial-time algorithm that transforms
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(G,k)

to an equivalent instance
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Pi_{\bf p}
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G^{\prime}
has size
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mathcal{O}(k)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}k^{\prime}\leq k
.

[C]

It was proved in
[
22
]
(see also
[
25
]
and
[
28
]
), that the problem of computing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}(G)
for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G\in{\cal G}

admits a
Efficient Polynomial Approximation Scheme
(EPTAS) — that is an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\epsilon
-approximation
algorithm running in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}f(\frac{1}{\epsilon})\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
steps — given that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
is hereditary and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\bf p}
satisfies the separability property and some reducibility property (related to CMSOL expresibility).

As an example we mention the well known
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d
-
Domination Set
problem (for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d\geq 1

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}S

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}k

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}S

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d

that satisfies the additional meta-algorithmic conditions in

,
[B]
, and

that it can be solved in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}2^{O(\sqrt{k})}\cdot n
time, it admits a linear kernel, and its optimization version admits an EPTAS on every graph class that has the SQG
C
property.

The emerging direction of research is to detect the most general classes in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGM}(c)

and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGC}(c)
.
Concerning the SQG
M
property, the following result was proven in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 5
.

For every graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf excl}(H)\in\mbox{\rm SQGM}(1)
.

A graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is an
apex graph
if it contains a vertex whose removal from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
results to a planar graph. For the SQG
C
property, the following counterpart of
Subsection 1.2
was proven in
[
21
]
.

Proposition 6
.

For every apex graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf excl}(H)\in\mbox{\rm SQGC}(1)
.

Notice that both above results concern graph classes that are defined by excluding some graph as a minor. For such graphs,
Subsection 1.2
is indeed optimal. To see this, consider
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}K_{h}
-minor free graphs where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}h\geq 6
(these graphs are not apex graphs). Such classes do not satisfy the SQG
C
property:
take
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k}
, add a new vertex, and make it adjacent, with all its vertices. The resulting
graph excludes
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k}
as a contraction and has treewidth
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}>k
.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal U}_{d}\in\mbox{\rm SQGM}(1)

Definition 9
.

(String graphs)
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal L}=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}\}

agreeing that the upper-left corner (i.e., the unique vertex of degree 3) is the vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(0,0)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\hat{\Gamma}_{k}
has two vertices of degree
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}3
, the top left and the bottom right of the grid part. We call
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k}
the
uniformly triangulated grid
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\hat{\Gamma}_{k}

Definition 18

(Treewidth)
.

A
tree-decomposition
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, is a pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(T,{\cal X})
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T
is a tree and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal X}=\{X_{t}:\ t\in V(T)\}
is a family of subsets of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V(G)
, called
bags
, such that the following three properties are satisfied:

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\bullet

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\bigcup_{t\in V(T)}X_{t}=V(G)
,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\bullet
for every edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e\in E(G)
there exists
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}t\in V(T)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e\subseteq X_{t}
, and

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\bullet

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\forall v\in V(G)
, the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T_{v}=\{t\in V(T)\mid v\in X_{t}\}
is a connected vertex set of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T
.

The
width
of a tree-decomposition is the cardinality of the maximum size bag minus 1 and the
treewidth
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
is the minimum width over all the tree-decompositions of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
. We denote the treewidth of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf tw}(G)
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
be a graph and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
be a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
-size contraction of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
.
Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf tw}(G)\leq(c+1)\cdot({\sf tw}(H)+1)-1
.

Proof.

By definition, since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
-size contraction of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, there is a mapping between each vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
and a connected set of at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c
edges in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, so that by contracting these edge sets we obtain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
. The endpoints of these edges form disjoint connected sets in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, implying a partition of the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
into connected sets
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\{V_{x}\mid x\in V(H)\}
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}|V_{x}|\leq c+1
for any vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x\in V(H)
.

Consider now a tree decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(T,{\cal X})
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
. We claim that the pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(T,{\cal X^{\prime}})
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}X^{\prime}_{t}:=\bigcup_{x\in X_{t}}V_{x}
for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}t\in T
is a tree decomposition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
. Clearly all vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
are included in some bag, since all vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
did. Every edge of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
with both endpoints in the same part of the partition is in a bag, as each of these vertex sets is placed as a whole in the same bag. If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e
is an edge of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
with endpoints in different parts of the partition, say
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{x}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{y}
, then this implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\{x,y\}\in E(H)
. Thus, there is a node
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}t
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T
for which
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x,y\in X_{t}
and therefore
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e\subseteq X_{t}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the continuity property remains unaffected, since for any vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x\in V(H)
each vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{x}
induces the same subtree in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T
that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x
did.
∎

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e_{1}=\{v,x\}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e_{2}=\{v,y\}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}v

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e_{1}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\{x,y\}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H

of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G

Lemma 20
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}Q
be a graph where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\delta(Q)\geq 3
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H,G
be
graphs where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is a dissolution of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
. If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}Q\leq G
, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}Q\leq H
.

Proof.

As
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}Q\leq G
then there exist
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\sigma:V(G)\to V(Q)
such that for
all
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x\in V(Q)
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G[\sigma^{-1}(x)]
is a non-empty graph and for
every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x,y\in V(Q),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\{x,y\}\in E(Q)\iff G[\sigma^{-1}(x)\cup\sigma^{-1}(y)]
is connected.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}v
be a vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
incident to exactly two edges

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e_{1}=\{v,v^{\prime}\}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e_{2}=\{v,v^{\prime\prime}\}
, and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G^{\prime}
be the graph
obtained from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
after the dissolution of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}v
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\sigma^{\prime}:V(G^{\prime})\to V(Q)
such that for all
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}z\in V(G^{\prime})
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\sigma^{\prime}(z)=\sigma(z)
.
As the dissolution maintains connectivity, we have that for every

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x,y\in V(Q),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\{x,y\}\in E(Q)\iff G[\sigma^{\prime-1}(x)\cup\sigma^{\prime-1}(y)]
is connected. Moreover, as
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\delta(Q)\geq 3
, we
know that for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}x\in V(Q)
, there exists
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}z\in\sigma^{-1}(x)

such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}z
has edge degree at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}3
. In particular we know
that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}z
is different from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}v
. So we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G[\sigma^{\prime-1}(x)]

is a non-empty graph. Thus
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}Q\leq G^{\prime}
. The lemma follows by
iterating this argument. ∎

Lemma 25
.

If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
is a graph class that is topological minor closed, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf inter}_{d}({\cal G})\subseteq{\cal G}^{(d+1,d-1)}
.

Proof.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
be a graph on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}h
vertices in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf inter}_{d}({\cal G}),
for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d\in\mathbb{N}
.
This means that there is a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
such that
we can see the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
as a set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{h}\}
of connected subsets of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
and each multi-edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e=\{C_{i},C_{j}\}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
corresponds
to two mutually intersecting subsets of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal C}
and the multiplicity
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}|C_{i}\cap C_{j}|
.
For every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\{i,j\}\in{h\choose 2}
, we set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{i,j}=C_{i}\cap C_{j}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}m_{i,j}=|C_{i}\cap C_{j}|
, and we assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e_{i,j}=\{C_{i},C_{j}\}
is a multi-edge of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H

of multiplicity
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}m_{i,j}
(if this edge does not exists in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
, then the multiplicity of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}e_{i,j}
is 0).

We define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{i}=\bigcup_{j\in[h]}V_{i,j}
,
for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}i\in[h]
and also set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{V}=\bigcup_{i\in[h]}V_{i}
.
Notice that, for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}i\in[h]
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}|V_{i}|
is upper bounded by the edge-degree, in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
, of the vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}C_{i}
, therefore,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}|V_{i}|\leq d
for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}i\in[h]
. Also, a vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{V}
cannot belong in more
that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d+1
distinct
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}C_{i}
’s as, otherwise
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
would contain a clique with at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d+2
vertices.
As
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\in{\sf inter}_{d}({\cal G})
, this is not possible.

Recall that, for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}i\in[h]

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{i}
is a subset of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}C_{i}
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T_{i}
be a minimum-size tree of

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G[C_{i}]
containing the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{i}
.
We partition the set of vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T_{i}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{i},\overline{V}_{i},D_{i}

where among the vertices in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V(T_{i})\setminus V_{i}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}D_{i}
are the vertices of degree 2 and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\overline{V}_{i}
are the rest. By minimality, the leaves of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T_{i}
belong in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{i}
. Moreover, there is no vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\overline{V}_{i}
that belongs to some other
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\overline{V}_{i\textquoteright}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}i\in[h]\setminus\{i\}
.
We denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\hat{T}_{i}
the tree obtained from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T_{i}
if we dissolve in

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}T_{i}
all vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}D_{i}
. That way we can still partition the vertices of each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\hat{T}_{i}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}i\in[h]

into
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}V_{i}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\overline{V}_{i}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\hat{T}_{i}

4.2
Triangulated grids inside triangulated grids

The next lemma is the main combinatorial engine of our results.
We assume that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H\leq^{c}G
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k}\leq G
. Here
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
should be seen as the result of a “shrink” of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
in the sense
that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
can be contracted to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
so that each vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is created after a bounded number of contractions. The lemma states that if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
can be contracted to a uniformly triangulated grid, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
, as a “shrunk version” of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
, can also be contracted to a uniformly triangulated grid that is no less than linearly smaller.

The proof strategy views the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
as being contracted into a uniformly triangulated grid
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k}
(see
Figure 7
), we choose a scattered set of
“capitals” in it (the black vertices in
Figure 7
). Then
we set up a “conquest” procedure where each capital is trying to expand to a country. This procedure has three phases.
The first phase is the
expansion face
where each country tries to incorporate unconquested territories around it (the limits of this expansion is depicted by the red cycles in
Figure 7
).
The second phase is the
clash face
, where different countries are fighting for disputed territories. Finally, the third phase is the
annex phase
, where each country naturally incorporates remaining enclaves.
The end of this war creates a set of countries occupying the whole
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G
that, when contracted, give rise to a uniformly triangulated grid
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\Gamma_{k^{\prime}}
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}k^{\prime}=\Omega(k)
.

5
Conclusions, extensions, and open problems

The main combinatorial result of this paper is that, for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}d
and every apex-minor-free graph class
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal G}
, the intersection class

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf inter}_{d}({\cal G})

has the SQG
C
property for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}c=1
.
Certainly, the main general question is to detect even wider graph classes with the SQG
M
/SQG
C
property.
In this direction, some insisting open issues are the following:

•

Is the bound on the (multi-)degree necessary? Are there classes of intersection graphs
with unbounded or “almost bounded” maximum degree that have the SQG
M
/SQG
C
property?

•

All so far known results classify graph classes in
SQG
M
(1) or
SQG
C
(1). Are there (interesting) graph classes in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGM}(c)
or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGC}(c)
for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}1<c<2
that do not belong in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGM}(1)
or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGC}(1)
respectively? An easy (but trivial) example of such a class
is the class
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal Q}_{d}
of the q-dimensional grids, i.e., the cartesian products of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}q\geq 2
equal length paths.
It is easy to see that the maximum
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}k
for which an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}n
-vertex graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}G\in{\cal Q}_{q}
contains a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}(k\times k)
-grid as a minor is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}k=\Theta(n^{\frac{1}{2}})
. On the other size, it can also be proven that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\sf tw}(G)=\Theta(n^{\frac{q-1}{q}})
. These two facts together
imply that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal Q}_{q}\in\mbox{\rm SQGM}(2-\frac{2}{q})
while
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}{\cal Q}_{q}\not\in\mbox{\rm SQGM}(2-\frac{2}{q}-\epsilon)
for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\epsilon>0
.

•

Usually the graph classes in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGC}(1)
are characterised by some “flatness” property. For instance, see the results in
[
31
,
34
,
34
]
for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
-minor free graphs, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}H
is an apex graph. Can

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.41}\mbox{\rm SQGC}(1)
be useful as an intuitive definition of the “flatness” concept? Does this have some geometric interpretation?

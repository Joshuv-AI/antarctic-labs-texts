Ramanujan-inspired series for 1 / π 1/\pi involving harmonic numbers Thanks: This work is supported by Natural Science Foundation of Shanghai (22ZR1424100).
Qinghu Hou, Haihong He, Xiaoxia Wang*

Abstract
. By applying the derivative operator to the known identities from hypergeometric series or WZ pairs, we obtain seven series associated with harmonic numbers. Specifically, six of them are Ramanujan-like formulas for 1 / π 1/\pi and the remaining one contains harmonic numbers of order 2. As conclusions, Sun’s five conjectural series are proved.

1.
Introduction

In 1914, Ramanujan
[
13
]
first systematically investigated some series for
1/\pi
, including the following beautiful ones

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}^{3}}(4k+1)=\frac{2}{\pi},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{k}}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}^{3}}(6k+1)=\frac{4}{\pi},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}(\frac{1}{4})_{k}(\frac{3}{4})_{k}}{(-4)^{k}(1)_{k}^{3}}(20k+3)=\frac{8}{\pi},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{64^{k}}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}^{3}}(42k+5)=\frac{16}{\pi}.

(1.4)

Here and in what follows, the Pochhammer symbol is defined as

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\Gamma(x+n)/\Gamma(x),

where
\Gamma(x)
is Euler’s gamma function and
n
is a nonnegative integer.

In 2013, Guillera
[
12
, (32)]
proved an elegant Ramanujan-type formula

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}^{3}}\big\{3(4k+1)H_{k}-2\big\}=-\frac{12\log 2}{\pi},

where
H_{n}
denotes the ordinary harmonic numbers

H_{0}=0\quad\text{and}\quad H_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}.

For a complex number
x
and a positive integer
l
, the generalized harmonic numbers of order
l
are defined as

H_{0}^{(l)}(x)=0\quad\text{and}\quad H_{n}^{(l)}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(x+k)^{l}}.

In
[
14
]
, Sun conjectured hundreds of series and congruences with summands containing harmonic numbers, such as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}(\frac{1}{4})_{k}(\frac{3}{4})_{k}}{16^{k}(1)_{k}^{5}}\big\{(120k^{2}+34k+3)(H_{2k}-2H_{k})+68k+9\big\}=\frac{128\log 2}{\pi^{2}},

which was recently confirmed by Wei
[
16
]
through the derivative operator method. It is an interesting topic to evaluate summations involving harmonic numbers. Apart from
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
, more mathematical literature concerning harmonic sums can be found in
[
6
,
15
,
5
,
4
,
11
,
3
,
17
]
.

Motivated by the work forementioned and the formulas (
1.1
)–(
1.4

involving harmonic numbers as follows. We see that Sun’s four conjectural series
[

(1.11)

As a prerequisite, we review some basic concepts.
Given a differentiable function
f(x)
, the derivative operator
\mathcal{D}_{x}
is defined as

\mathcal{D}_{x}f(x)=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}f(x).

Besides,
the digamma function is given by

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\mathcal{D}_{x}\big(\log\Gamma(x)\big)=-\gamma+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{k+x}\bigg\},

where
\gamma
stands for the Euler’s constant. By acting the operator
\mathcal{D}_{x}

on the above equation, we obtain

\mathcal{D}_{x}\psi(x):=\psi^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+x)^{2}}.

Additionally, there is a recurrence relation about the digamma function

\displaystyle\psi(x+1)=\psi(x)+\frac{1}{x}

and some concrete values which will be utilized in the proof

\displaystyle\psi(1)=-\gamma,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\psi(\tfrac{1}{2})=-\gamma-2\log 2,

\displaystyle\psi(\tfrac{1}{4})=-\gamma-3\log 2-\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad\quad\psi(\tfrac{3}{4})=-\gamma-3\log 2+\frac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(1)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\psi^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}.

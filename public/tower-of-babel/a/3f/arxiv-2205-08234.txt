Delaytron: Efficient Learning of Multiclass Classifiers with Delayed Bandit Feedbacks
Naresh Manwani, Mudit Agarwal

Abstract
In this paper, we present online algorithm called Delaytron for learning multi class classifiers using delayed bandit feedbacks. The sequence of feedback delays { d t } t = 1 T \{d_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T} is unknown to the algorithm. At the t t -th round, the algorithm observes an example 𝐱 t \mathbf{x}_{t} and predicts a label y ~ t \tilde{y}_{t} and receives the bandit feedback 𝕀 [ y ~ t = y t ] \mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}] only d t d_{t} rounds later. When t + d t > T t+d_{t}>T , we consider that the feedback for the t t -th round is missing. We show that the proposed algorithm achieves regret of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 2 ​ K γ ​ [ T 2 + ( 2 + L 2 R 2 ​ ‖ 𝐖 ‖ F 2 ) ​ ∑ t = 1 T d t ] ) \mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+\left(2+\frac{L^{2}}{R^{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}}\right)\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\right]}\right) when the loss for each missing sample is upper bounded by L L . In the case when the loss for missing samples is not upper bounded, the regret achieved by Delaytron is 𝒪 ⁡ ( 2 ​ K γ ​ [ T 2 + 2 ​ ∑ t = 1 T d t + | ℳ | ​ T ] ) \mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}\right) where ℳ \mathcal{M} is the set of missing samples in T T rounds. These bounds were achieved with a constant step size which requires the knowledge of T T and ∑ t = 1 T d t \sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t} . For the case when T T and ∑ t = 1 T d t \sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t} are unknown, we use a doubling trick for online learning and proposed Adaptive Delaytron. We show that Adaptive Delaytron achieves a regret bound of 𝒪 ⁡ ( T + ∑ t = 1 T d t ) \mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{T+\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}\right) . We show the effectiveness of our approach by experimenting on various datasets and comparing with state-of-the-art approaches.

Introduction

Learning classifiers using bandit feedback is a well-addressed problem in the machine learning community. The learner can only get feedback on whether the predicted label is correct in the bandit feedback setting. In
[
12
]
, the Banditron algorithm is proposed to learn with bandit feedback. When the data is linearly
separable, Banditron achieves a mistake bound of
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})
in the expected sense. In the general
case, Banditron makes
\mathcal{O}(T^{2/3})
mistakes. On the other hand, Newtron
[
9
]
, which uses the online Newton method on a strongly convex objective function (adding regularization term with the loss function), achieves
\mathcal{O}(\log T)
regret bound in the best case and
\mathcal{O}(T^{2/3})
in the worst case. An exact passive-aggressive approach is proposed to learn classifiers in the bandit setting in
[
2
]
. A second-order algorithm is discussed in
[
4
]
which achieves
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret. Banditron algorithm has been extended in the noisy bandit feedback setting in
[
1
]
and shown to achieve
\mathcal{O}(T^{2/3})
mistake bound in the worst case. A dilute bandit feedback setting is proposed in
[
3
]
in which the algorithm predicts a subset of labels and gets the feedback on whether the actual label lies in that set. It is shown that even with this weaker bandit feedback, the algorithm can learn good classifiers
[
3
]
.

The above approaches assume that the feedback for trial
t
is received in the same round—however, it is not the case in many practical situations.
Consider the example of sponsored advertising in which user’s queries are answered as a list of product ads to maximize the clicks (by considering the query as well as the user’s history and buying patterns)
[
10
]
. Users’ clicks on a particular ad might indicate that the user is interested in that product. However, the user may take their own sweet time to click on an ad, and sometimes it may not. This causes a delay in the feedback on whether the user liked some ad or not. Meanwhile, the system still serves ads to other users and does not wait to know previous users’ click outcomes. This situation also arises in the healthcare system where the feedback of a specific treatment on a patient can be received only after some time or delay
[
16
]
. This delay is natural as it takes time to observe the effects of the treatment. However, the healthcare professional still needs to treat other patients for the same problem before watching the treatment outcome of the previous patients. In finance, online learning algorithms which are used to manage portfolios always suffer from information and transaction delays from the market
[
14
]
. Financial firms invest a massive amount of effort in minimizing these delays.

Adversarial multi arm bandits were first studied in
[
6
]

under the assumption of
fixed delay
d
. They gave a lower bound of
\Omega(\max(\sqrt{kT},\sqrt{dT\log k}))
for
d\leq\frac{T}{\log k}
and a matching upper bound of
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{dT\log k}+\sqrt{kT\log k}\right)
. Bistritz et al.
[
5
]
proposed a variant of exp3 algorithm that can handle non-uniform delays and achieves
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{kT\log(k)}+\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\log(k)})
regret bound under the assumption that
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
are known in advance. Bistritz et al.
[
5
]
provide a doubling scheme that achieves an
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{k^{2}T\log(k)}+\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\log(k)})
regret bound when
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
are unknown. Stochastic gradient descent on convex objective function with delayed feedback is explored in
[
17
]
. In
[
17
]
, it is assumed that the convex function corresponding to time
t
is revealed after a delay of
d_{t}
only and proposed an approach which achieves a regret bound of
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}})
.

There has been no work that deals with learning multiclass classifiers using delayed bandit feedback. This paper proposes an approach that efficiently learns classifiers using delayed bandit feedback, and this is the first work in this direction. There has been no work that deals with learning multiclass classifiers using delayed bandit feedback. This paper proposes an approach that efficiently learns classifiers using delayed bandit feedback. This is the first work in this direction. Following are the key features of our proposed approach.

1.

The proposed approach achieves a regret bound of
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+\left(2+\frac{L^{2}}{R^{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}}\right)\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\right]}\right)
for constant step size. Here,
\mathcal{M}
is the set of samples for which the feedback is not received till
T
, and
L
is the upper bound on the loss for samples in
\mathcal{M}
.

2.

When there is not boundedness assumption on the loss for samples in the set
\mathcal{M}
, the algorithm achieves a regret bound of
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}\right)
for constant step size.

3.

Constant step size requires the algorithm to know
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
beforehand. In the case when
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
are not known, using doubling trick we proposed
Adaptive Delaytron
approach which achieves a regret bound of
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T+\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}})
.

4.

We show experimentally that both the proposed algorithms (Delaytron and Adaptive Delaytron) can learn efficient classifiers using delayed bandit feedbacks for various datasets.

II
Proposed Approach: Delaytron

Here, the goal is learn a function
g:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[K]
which given an example
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, assigns a label in the set
[K]=\{1,\ldots,K\}
. A linear multi class classifier is modeled using a weight matrix
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
as
g(\mathbf{x})=\arg\max_{j\in[K]}\;(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x})_{j}
. To learn the weight matrix
W
, we use the training set
\{\mathbf{x}_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(\mathbf{x}_{T},y_{T})\}
where
(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})\in\mathcal{X}\times[K],\;\forall i\in[T]
. Performance of classifier
g
is measured using 0-1 loss (
l_{0-1}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i}))=\mathbb{I}[g(\mathbf{x}_{i})\neq y_{i}]
). In practice, convex surrogates of
l_{0-1}
are used to learn the classifier. One such surrogate is multiclass hinge loss
[
7
]
described as

\displaystyle l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i}))=\max(0,1-(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}_{i})_{y_{i}}+\max_{j\neq y_{t}}\;(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}_{i})_{j}).

The loss is 0 when
(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}_{i})_{y_{i}}-\max_{j\neq y_{t}}\;(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}_{i})_{j}\geq 1
and
1-(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}_{i})_{y_{i}}+\max_{j\neq y_{t}}\;(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}_{i})_{j}
otherwise.

At round
t
, the algorithm receives an example
\mathbf{x}_{t}
. It then finds
\hat{y}_{t}
as
\hat{y}_{t}={\arg\max}_{j\in[K]}\;(\mathbf{W}_{t}\mathbf{x}_{t})_{j}
. Then, it defines a distribution over the classes as
P_{t}(r)=(1-\gamma)\mathbb{I}[r=\hat{y}_{t}]+\frac{\gamma}{K},\;r\in[K]
. It samples
\tilde{y}_{t}
using distribution
P^{t}
and predicts
\tilde{y}_{t}
. The algorithm gets to know the bandit feedback (
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
) of predicting
\tilde{y}_{t}
for round
t
at the end of the
t+d_{t}-1
round. In other words, the algorithms observes a delay of
d_{t}
(
\geq 1
) to observe the feedback
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
. So, the feedback is available at the beginning of round
t+d_{t}
. Note that
y_{t}
is the true label of the example
\mathbf{x}_{t}
. The goal of the algorithm is to minimize the number of mistakes
\hat{M}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}\neq y_{t}]
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{t}
denotes the set of feedbacks received at the beginning of round
t
. So,
r\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
means that the feedback of predicting
\tilde{y}_{r}
at round
r<t
is received at round
t
. Since, the algorithm runs for
T
rounds, all feedbacks for which
t+d_{t}>T
are never received. These feedbacks are called missing samples and
\mathcal{M}
denotes the set of missing samples. At round
t
, the algorithm considers all the feedbacks received at time
t
and updates the parameters as
\mathbf{W}^{t+1}=\mathbf{W}^{t}+\eta\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{s}
, where
\eta>0
is the step size. Also, for all
r\in[K],j\in[d]
,
\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{t}_{r,j}
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{t}_{r,j}=\mathbf{x}_{t}(j)\left[\frac{\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=r]}{P_{t}(r)}-\mathbb{I}[\hat{y}_{t}=r]\right].

Note that
\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{y}_{t}\sim P_{t}}[\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{t}_{r,j}]=\mathbf{U}^{t}_{r,j}

[
12
]
, where matrix
\mathbf{U}^{t}
is negative of the gradient of the loss
l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))
evaluated at
\mathbf{W}^{t}
. The approach is described in details in Algorithm 1.

III
Analysis

We split sum of gradients in a single round and apply them one by one. For each
s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, let
\mathcal{S}_{t,s}=\{q\in\mathcal{S}_{t}\;:\;q<s\}
, which is the set of bandit feedback samples that the algorithm uses before the bandit feedback from round
s
is used. Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\left(\{\tilde{y}_{s}\;|\;s+d_{s}\leq t\}\right)
,
which is generated from all the actions for which the feedback was received upto round
t
.
The performance of the algorithm, is measured using the regret as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}_{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{*},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]

(1)

where
\mathbf{W}^{*}={\arg\min}_{\mathbf{W}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t})
.

We first show the regret bound for the examples other than missing samples. We use the following lemma to analyze the contribution of the ”delay term” to the expected regret.

Lemma 1
.

[
5
]

Let
d_{t}
be the delay in the bandit feedback corresponding to round
t
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{t}
be the set of feedbacks received at time
t
and define
\mathcal{S}_{t,s}=\{r\in\mathcal{S}_{t}\;|\;r<s\}
which is the set of feedback samples that the algorithm uses before the feedback from round
s
is used. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of missing samples, then

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\left[|\mathcal{S}_{t,s}|+\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}|\mathcal{S}_{r}|\right]\leq 2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}
.

Proof of this lemma can be found in
[
5
]
. We now bound the regret
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}\left\{l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(x_{t},y_{t}))-l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{t},y_{t}))\right\}\right]
with the help of following Theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be the sequence of examples observed by Algorithm 1. Let
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
be the bandit feedback corresponding to round
t
which is observed only after a delay of
d_{t}
. Let
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2}\leq R,\;\forall t\in[T]
. Then for any
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, the regret achieved by Delaytron on examples for which the bandit feedback is received before
T
is as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{W}^{t,s}=\mathbf{W}^{t}+\eta\sum_{q\in\mathcal{S}_{t,s}}\tilde{U}^{q}
. Suppose
\mathcal{S}_{t}
is nonempty and let
s^{\prime}=\max\mathcal{S}_{t}
be the last index in the set
\mathcal{S}_{t}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{W}^{t+1}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s^{\prime}}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+2\eta\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s^{\prime}}-\mathbf{W},\tilde{U}^{s^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta^{2}\|\tilde{U}^{s^{\prime}}\|_{F}^{2}

Repeatedly unrolling the first term on the RHS gives us the following

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{W}^{t+1}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\eta^{2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\eta\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W},\tilde{U}^{s}\rangle

Taking expectation on both sides, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t+1}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2\eta\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W},\tilde{U}^{s}\rangle]+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}]+2\eta\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W},U^{s}\rangle]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}]

Where we used the fact that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W},\tilde{U}^{s}\rangle]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W},{U}^{s}\rangle]
. Now,
using the convexity property of the loss function
l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x},y))
, we know that

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W},U^{s}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{W}^{s}-\mathbf{W},U^{s}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{s},y_{s}))-l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(x_{s},y_{s}))+\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle.

Using this, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(x_{s},y_{s}))\right]-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{s},y_{s}))]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\Bigg[\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}-\|\mathbf{W}^{t+1}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\eta^{2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}\Bigg]+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle\right]

Summing from
t=1
to
T
, we get the following.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(x_{s},y_{s}))\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{s},y_{s}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}-\|\mathbf{W}^{t+1}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}\right]\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle\right]

Using the fact that
\mathbf{W}^{1}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}-\|\mathbf{W}^{t+1}-\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}]=\|\mathbf{W}^{1}-\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-\|\mathbf{W}^{T+1}-\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{W}^{1}-\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}=\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}.

This gives,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(x_{s},y_{s}))\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{s},y_{s}))

\displaystyle\;\;\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{P_{s}}\left[\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle\right].

Using Lemma 5 in
[
12
]
, we know that
\mathbb{E}_{P_{s}}\left[\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}\right]\leq 2\|\mathbf{x}_{s}\|^{2}\left(\frac{K}{\gamma}\mathbb{I}[y_{s}\neq\hat{y}_{s}]+\gamma\mathbb{I}[y_{s}=\hat{y}_{s}\right)
. Which can be further upper bounded as
\mathbb{E}_{P_{s}}\left[\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}
, where we used the fact that
R^{2}=\max_{s\in[T]}\;\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}
. Using this, the bound becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(x_{s},y_{s}))\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{s},y_{s}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta KTR^{2}}{2\gamma}+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle\right].

(2)

Each summand
\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle
contributes loss proportional to the distance between the matrix
\mathbf{W}^{s}
when update
\tilde{U}^{s}
is generated and the matrix
\mathbf{W}^{t,s}
when
\tilde{U}^{s}
is applied. This distance is created by the other updates that are applied in between. Using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we first bound the delay terms as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\langle\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s},U^{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s}\|_{F}\|U^{s}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq R\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s}\|_{F}

(3)

Consider a single term
\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s}\|_{F}
for fixed
t\in[T]
and
s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
. The difference
\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s}
is roughly the sum of updates received between round
s
and when we apply the update from round
s
in round
t
. Using triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{t}\|_{F}+\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}^{s}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{t}\|_{F}+\|\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}(\mathbf{W}^{r+1}-\mathbf{W}^{r})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{t}\|_{F}+\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}\|\mathbf{W}^{r+1}-\mathbf{W}^{r}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\sum_{p\in\mathcal{S}_{t,s}}\|\tilde{U}^{p}\|_{F}+\eta\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{S}_{r}}\|\tilde{U}^{q}\|_{F}.

Taking expectation on both sides, we get following bound.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s}\|_{F}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{p\in\mathcal{S}_{t,s}}\|\tilde{U}^{p}\|_{F}+\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{S}_{r}}\|\tilde{U}^{q}\|_{F}\right]

Using the concavity property of square root function, we observe that
\mathbb{E}_{P_{s}}[\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{s}}[\sqrt{\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{P_{s}}[\|\tilde{U}^{s}\|_{F}^{2}]}\leq\frac{\sqrt{K}R}{\sqrt{\gamma}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t,s}-\mathbf{W}^{s}\|_{F}]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}}\left(|\mathcal{S}_{t,s}|+\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}|\mathcal{S}_{r}|\right)

(4)

Using eq.(
4
) and (
3
) in eq.(
2
), we get the following.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(x_{s},y_{s}))\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{s},y_{s}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{\eta T}{2}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\left\{|\mathcal{S}_{t,s}|+\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}|\mathcal{S}_{r}|\right\}\right]

(5)

Using Lemma
1
, we have
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\left[|\mathcal{S}_{t,s}|+\sum_{r=s}^{t-1}|\mathcal{S}_{r}|\right]\leq 2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}
. Using this in eq.(
5
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(\mathbf{x}_{s},y_{s}))\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{s},y_{s}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]

But,
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\left\{l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{s},(x_{s},y_{s}))-l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{s},y_{s})\right\}=\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}\left\{l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(x_{t},y_{t}))-l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{t},y_{t})\right\}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(x_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(x_{t},y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right].

(6)

∎

III-A

Case 1: No Delay

When there is no delay, set
\mathcal{M}
is empty and
d_{t}=1,\forall t\in[T]
. Thus,
\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}=T
. In that case the regret bound becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{5\eta KTR^{2}}{2\gamma}.

Using
\eta=\frac{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}}{\sqrt{\frac{KTR^{2}}{\gamma}}}
, the regret bound becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t=1}^{T}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq 6R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{KT}{\gamma}},\;\forall\;\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}.

We now derive the complete regret bound for two different cases. These cases are based on whether the losses for the missing samples are bounded.

III-B

Case 2: Bounded Loss for Missing Samples

Here, we assume that the loss
l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))
is upper bounded for all the missing samples. Which means,
l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\leq L,\;\forall t\in\mathcal{M}
. This seems a fair assumption as the bandit feedback for such examples is never revealed.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be the sequence of examples observed by Algorithm 1. Let
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
be the bandit feedback corresponding to round
t
which is observed only after a delay of
d_{t}
. Let
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2}\leq R,\;\forall t\in[T]
and
\mathcal{M}=\{t\in[T]\;|\;t+d_{t}>T\}
. Let
l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\leq L,\;\forall t\in\mathcal{M},\forall W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
. Then for any
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, the regret achieved by Delaytron is as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]}+L|\mathcal{M}|

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]\leq L|\mathcal{M}|

(7)

Using Theorem 1 and (
7
), we get, the regret as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]+L|\mathcal{M}|

Using
\eta=\frac{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}}{\sqrt{\frac{2KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]}}
, we get the following bound.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]}+L|\mathcal{M}|

∎∎

Our bound does not account for the delays of the missing samples, and it only depends on
\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}
. Moreover, counting delays that go beyond
T
is redundant.
It is worth noting that
m=|\mathcal{M}|
contribute at least
m(m+1)/2
to the sum of delays
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
. It happens in the following scenario. Feedback of round
T
is delayed by one, feedback of round
(T-1)
is delayed by two, and so on
m
times. We use this idea and get regret bound independent of
\mathcal{M}
in the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be the sequence of examples observed by Algorithm 1. Let
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
be the bandit feedback corresponding to round
t
which is observed only after a delay of
d_{t}
. Let
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2}\leq R,\;\forall t\in[T]
and
\mathcal{M}=\{t\in[T]\;|\;t+d_{t}>T\}
. Let
l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\leq L,\;\forall t\in\mathcal{M},\forall W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
. Then for any
W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, the regret of Delaytron is upper bounded as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+\left(2+\frac{L^{2}}{R^{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}}\right)\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\right]}

Proof.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]}+L|\mathcal{M}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}\right]}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{KL^{2}m(m+1)}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+\frac{L^{2}m(m+1)}{2R^{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+\left(2+\frac{L^{2}}{R^{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}}\right)\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\right]}

Where the second inequality follows from the concavity property of
f(x)=\sqrt{x}
.

∎

The regret bound showed above is independent of
\mathcal{M}
. However, it depends on
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
.

III-C

Without Boundedness Condition on the Loss for Missing Samples

Here, we do not assume that the loss for the missing samples is upper bounded by a fixed quantity. Now, let us find regret on missing samples.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be the sequence of examples observed by Algorithm 1. Let
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
be the bandit feedback corresponding to round
t
which is observed only after a delay of
d_{t}
. Let
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2}\leq R,\;\forall t\in[T]
and
\mathcal{M}=\{t\in[T]\;|\;t+d_{t}>T\}
. Then for any
W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, the regret achieved by Delaytron is as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\left[|\mathcal{M}|+\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}\right]

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}\left\{l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}\langle\nabla l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t})),\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}^{*}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\|_{F}.\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}^{*}\|_{F}\right]

But,
\|\nabla l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2}\leq R
. Also,
\|\mathbf{W}^{t}-\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{W}^{t}\|_{F}+\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}
. But,
\mathbf{W}^{t}=\eta\sum_{r=1}^{t-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{r}}\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{s}
. Thus,
\|\mathbf{W}^{t}\|_{F}\leq\eta\sum_{r=1}^{t-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{r}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{s}\|_{F}
. Taking expectation, we get,
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t}\|_{F}]\leq\eta\sum_{r=1}^{t-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{r}}\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{s}\|_{F}]\leq\eta R\sqrt{\frac{K}{\gamma}}\sum_{r=1}^{t-1}|\mathcal{S}_{r}|
.
\sum_{r=1}^{t-1}|\mathcal{S}_{r}|
is the number of feedbacks received till time
t-1
which is not more than
t-1
. Thus,
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{W}^{t}\|_{F}]\leq\frac{\eta KR}{\gamma}(t-1)\leq\frac{\eta KTR}{\gamma}
. So, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{M}|R(\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}+\frac{\eta KTR}{\gamma})

(8)

Using Theorem 1 and eq.(
8
), we get, the overall regret as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq|\mathcal{M}|R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]

Using
\eta=\frac{\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}}{\sqrt{\frac{2KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}}
, we get the following bound.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\left[|\mathcal{M}|+\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}\right]

∎

We get
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T}\right)
regret bound in case when we don’t have any boundedness assumption on the loss incurred on missing samples. Corollary below give further refined regret bound.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be the sequence of examples observed by Algorithm 1. Let
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
be the bandit feedback corresponding to round
t
which is observed only after a delay of
d_{t}
. Let
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2}\leq R,\;\forall t\in[T]
and
\mathcal{M}=\{t\in[T]\;|\;t+d_{t}>T\}
. Then for any
W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, the regret achieved by Delaytron is as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(R\|W\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}\right)

Proof.

We see that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}+|\mathcal{M}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{Km(m+1)}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+m(m+1)/2+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}.

Where the second inequality follows from the concavity property of
f(x)=\sqrt{x}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\left[\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}+|\mathcal{M}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq R\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}

∎

IV
Adaptive Delaytron for Unknown
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}

The fixed step size
\eta
used in Delaytron requires the knowledge of
T
and
\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}
. We can make it independent of
T
and
\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}
by using doubling trick. However, with delays, the standard doubling trick
[
15
]
does not work. We use the doubling trick proposed in
[
5
]
, where
T
and
\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}
are unknown. We define
m_{t}
as the number of samples for which bandit feedback is missing till round
t
. The idea is to start a new epoch every time
\sum_{s=1}^{t}m_{s}
(that tracks
\sum_{s=1}^{t}d_{s}
) doubles. We define the
e
-th epoch as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{e}=\{t\;|\;2^{e-1}\leq\sum_{s=1}^{t}m_{s}<2^{e}\}.

(9)

\mathcal{T}_{e}
is the set of consecutive rounds in which the sum of delays is within a given interval. During the
e
-th epoch, the algorithm uses the step size
\eta_{e}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2^{e}}}
. The resulting approach is called Adaptive Delaytron and is discussed in details in Algorithm
2
.

T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}

:
\gamma,\beta\in(0,1)
, Step size

W^{1}=0\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
. Set the epoch index
e=0
and
\eta_{0}=1
.

for

do

\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

4:

Set

5:

Randomly sample
\tilde{y}_{t}
according to
P_{t}
.

6:

Predict
\tilde{y}_{t}

7:

Obtain a set of delayed feedbacks

for all

, where
\tilde{y}_{s}
is the prediction made at round

and
y_{s}

8:

Update the number of missing samples so far

\displaystyle m_{t}=t-\sum_{s=1}^{t}|\mathcal{S}_{s}|

9:

10:

for

end

Update:
W^{t+1}

end

Theorem 4
.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be the sequence of examples observed by Algorithm 1. Let
\mathbb{I}[\tilde{y}_{t}=y_{t}]
be the bandit feedback corresponding to round
t
which is observed only after a delay of
d_{t}
. Let
\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2}\leq R,\;\forall t\in[T]
and
\mathcal{M}=\{t\in[T]\;|\;t+d_{t}>T\}
. Let
l_{H}(W,(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\leq L,\;\forall t\in\mathcal{M},\forall W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
. Then for any
W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, the regret achieved by Adaptive Delaytron is as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\leq 10\left[\frac{1}{2}\|W\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]\sqrt{T+\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}

Proof.

Define
\mathcal{M}_{e}
as the set of bandit feedbacks in epoch
e
that are not received withing epoch
e
. Denote by
T_{e}=\max\mathcal{T}_{e}
as the last round in
\mathcal{T}_{e}
. Note that
\mathcal{T}_{e}
is also set of consecutive rounds from
T_{e-1}+1
to
T_{e}
. Every round
t\notin\mathcal{T}_{e}
such that
t\in\mathcal{M}_{e}
contributes exactly
d_{t}
to
\sum_{s=T_{e-1}+1}^{T_{e}}m_{s}
, since the
t
-th feedback is missing for
d_{t}
rounds sometime between
T_{e-1}+1
and
T_{e}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{e},t\notin\mathcal{M}_{e}}d_{t}\leq\sum_{s=T_{e-1}+1}^{T_{e}}m_{s}\leq 2^{e-1}.

Where the last inequality uses the fact that if
\sum_{s=T_{e-1}+1}^{T_{e}}m_{s}>2^{e-1}\geq 2^{e-1}+2^{e-1}=2^{e}
, then epoch
e+1
should have been already started. We apply Theorem 1 separately on each epoch, which gives upper bound on the regret of epoch
e
as follows.

\displaystyle R_{e}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{e}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W}^{t},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))-\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{e}}l_{H}(\mathbf{W},(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\eta_{e}}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta_{e}KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T_{e}}{2}+2\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{e},t\notin\mathcal{M}_{e}}d_{t}\right]+L|\mathcal{M}_{e}|

Now we want to find the largest set
\mathcal{M}_{e}
such that
\sum_{s=T_{e-1}+1}^{T_{e}}m_{s}\leq 2^{e-1}
is still possible. The cheapest way to that is when the feedback from round
T_{e}
is delayed by 1 (contributing 1 to
\sum_{s=T_{e-1}+1}^{T_{e}}m_{s}
), feedback from round
T_{e}-1
is delayed by 2 (contributing 2 to
\sum_{s=T_{e-1}+1}^{T_{e}}m_{s}
) and so on. This gives
\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{M}_{e}|}i=\frac{|\mathcal{M}_{e}|(|\mathcal{M}_{e}|+1)}{2}\leq 2^{e-1}
. This can happen if
|\mathcal{M}_{e}|\leq 2^{\frac{e}{2}}
. By choosing
\eta_{e}=2^{-\frac{e}{2}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{e}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}2^{\frac{e}{2}}\|\mathbf{W}\|^{2}_{F}+\frac{2^{-\frac{e}{2}}KR^{2}}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T_{e}}{2}+2^{e}\right]+L2^{\frac{e}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{e}{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]+\frac{2KR^{2}T_{e}}{\gamma}2^{-\frac{e}{2}}

Let the last epoch be denoted as
E
, then we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}(T)]=\sum_{e=1}^{E}R_{e}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e=1}^{E}\left\{2^{\frac{e}{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]+\frac{2KR^{2}T_{e}}{\gamma}2^{-\frac{e}{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{E/2}-1}{\sqrt{2}-1}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]+\frac{2KR^{2}}{\gamma}\sum_{e=1}^{E}T_{e}2^{-\frac{e}{2}}

Final error rates of Delaytron as the function of exploration parameter
\gamma
for four different values of delay (
D=100,1000,2500
and

) for CIFAR-10, Digits, Ecoli, F-MNIST and USPS datasets

The maximum value of
\sum_{e=1}^{E}T_{e}2^{-\frac{e}{2}}
(subject to
\sum_{e=1}^{E}T_{e}=T
) is achieved when
E=\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil
and
e
-th epoch having length
2^{e}
. So,
\sum_{e=1}^{E}T_{e}2^{-\frac{e}{2}}\leq\sum_{e=1}^{\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil}2^{\frac{e}{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}\frac{2^{\frac{\lceil\log_{2}T\rceil}{2}}-1}{\sqrt{2}-1}\leq 5\sqrt{T}
. We also note that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\geq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\min\{d_{t},T-t-1\}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}m_{t}\geq\sum_{t=1}^{T_{E}}m_{t}\geq 2^{E}-1
. Thus,

2^{\frac{E}{2}}\leq\sqrt{1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}\leq\sqrt{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}
.
Also, using
\sqrt{2}-1\geq 0.4
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}(T)]\leq\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]+\frac{10\sqrt{T}KR^{2}}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq 10\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+10\sqrt{T}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]

\displaystyle\leq 10\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{KR^{2}}{\gamma}+L\right]\sqrt{T+\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}

∎

We see that even for unknown
T

\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{M}}d_{t}
, Adaptive Delaytron achieves a regret bound of
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{T+\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}\right)
.

VI
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we proposed Delaytron algorithm that can efficiently learn multiclass classifiers with delayed bandit feedback. We show that the regret bound of the proposed approach for fixed step size is
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+\left(2+\frac{L^{2}}{R^{2}\|\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}}\right)\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}\right]}\right)
. Here, we assumed that the loss for each missing sample is upper bounded by
L
. On the other hand, when the loss for missing samples is not upper bounded, the regret bound of Delaytron is
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\gamma}\left[\frac{T}{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}+|\mathcal{M}|T\right]}\right)
where
\mathcal{M}
is the set of missing samples in
T
rounds. Note that the constant step size used in these bounds require the knowledge of
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
. When
T
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}
are unknown, we propose Adaptive Delaytron using a doubling trick for online learning and show that it achieves a regret bound of
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{T+\sum_{t=1}^{T}d_{t}}\right)
. Experimental results show that Delaytron and Adaptive Delaytron with delayed bandit feedback performs comparable to the state of the art bandit feedback approaches which don’t accept delays.

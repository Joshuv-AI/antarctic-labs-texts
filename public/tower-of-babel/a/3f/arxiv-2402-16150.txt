Effective MSO-Definability for Tree-width Bounded Models of an Inductive Separation Logic of Relations
Marius Bozga, Lucas Bueri, Radu Iosif, Florian Zuleger

Abstract
A class of graph languages is definable in Monadic Second-Order logic ( MSO ) if and only if it consists of sets of models of MSO formulæ. If, moreover, there is a computable bound on the tree-widths of the graphs in each such set, the satisfiability and entailment problems are decidable, by Courcelle’s Theorem [ 15 ] . This motivates the comparison of other graph logics to MSO . In this paper, we consider the MSO definability of a Separation Logic of Relations ( SLR ) that describes simple hyper-graphs, in which each sequence of vertices is attached to at most one edge with a given label. Our logic SLR uses inductive predicates whose recursive definitions consist of existentially quantified separated conjunctions of relation and predicate atoms. The main contribution of this paper is an expressive fragment of SLR that describes bounded tree-width sets of graphs which can, moreover, be effectively translated into MSO .

Introduction

Formal languages study finite representations of infinite sets of
objects (e.g., words, trees, graphs). We distinguish between

descriptive
representations, that define sets by a logical
property of their members (e.g., even numbers, planar graphs, etc.)
and
constructive
representations, explaining how the members of
the set are built. From a system designer’s point of view, descriptive
representations are useful for specifying properties (e.g., a shape
invariant of a distributed network), whereas constructive
representations model low-level implementation details (e.g., how the
network components are interconnected). The verification problem
“does each implementation satisfy a given property?” amounts to
checking if the set of implementations built according to a
constructive representation is included in the descriptive
specification set.

Finite representations of sets of graphs are used in many areas of
computing, e.g., static analysis
[
31
]
,
databases
[
1
]
and concurrency

[
18
]
. A well-established
descriptive representation of graphs is Monadic Second Order Logic
(
MSO
)
[
17
]
. On the other hand, Hyperedge
Replacement (
HR
) grammars are standard constructive representations,
that define sets of graphs as least solutions of a finite set of
recursive equations that uses operations of substitution of a hyperedge
in a graph by another graph
[
17
]
.

The
tree-width
of a graph is a positive integer intuitively
measuring how far the graph is from a tree. For instance, trees have
treewidth one, series-parallel graphs (i.e., circuits with one input
and one output that can be either cascaded or overlaid) have treewidth
two, whereas
n\times n
square grids have treewidth
n
. This
parameter is important in obtaining efficient algorithms on
graphs. For instance, the
\mathsf{NP}
-complete problems of Hamiltonicity and
3-Coloring can be solved in polynomial time, when restricted to
tree-width bounded classes of graphs
[
25
, Chapter
11]
.

Bounding the tree-width draws the frontier between the decidability
and undecidability of the satisfiability problem for
MSO
. Courcelle

[
15
]
showed that
MSO
is decidable over bounded
tree-width graphs. Dually, Seese
[
37
]
showed that each class
of graphs with a decidable

theory necessarily has bounded
tree-width. These seminal results motivate finding effective
translations of graph representation languages into
MSO
. For
instance, Courcelle defined a class of
regular
HR
graph
grammars
that describe
MSO
-definable sets
[
16
, §5]

and Doumane developped a language of regular expressions that is
equivalent to the
MSO
-definable sets of tree-width at most
two
[
20
]
.

Substructural logics

The Separation Logic of Relations (

Substructural logics constitute powerful tools for reasoning about
mutations of graphs. They can describe actions

only with respect to the resources (e.g., memory cells, network nodes)
involved, while framing out the part that is irrelevant for that

powerful compositional proof techniques for pointer programs

[
10
]
.

Related Work

Our motivation for studying the symbolic heap-like fragment (i.e.,
formulæ written using inductive predicates whose definitions are
existentially quantified separated conjunctions of relation atoms and
predicates) of
SLR
arose from recent work on deductive verification
of self-adapting distributed systems, where
SLR
is used to write
Hoare-style correctness proofs for component-based and distributed
systems with structured reconfigurable networks

[
3
,
6
,
8
]
.
A key ingredient of automated proof generation in Hoare logic is the
availability of a decision procedure for the
entailment problem

\phi\models_{\Delta}\psi
asking if each model of a formula
\phi
is
also a model of another formula
\psi
, when the predicate symbols in

\phi
and
\psi
are interpreted by a set of inductive definitions

\Delta
. We note that, in general, the entailment problem is
undecidable for
SLR

[
5
, Theorem 3]
.

One of the first fragments of
SL
with a decidable entailment
problem relied on an ad-hoc translation into equivalent

MSO
formulæ, together with a syntactic guarantee of tree-width
boundedness
[
27
]
. More recently, this
fragment was the focus of an impressive body of
work
[
14
,
32
,
22
,
23
,
34
]
.
The entailment problem for
SLR
has been tackled by a reduction to
the same problem for
SL
, that encodes graphs as heaps of fixed
arity and thus works only for graphs of bounded
degree
[
5
]
. A goal of the present paper is
to lift the bounded degree restriction and achieve decidability of
entailments in a fragment of
SLR
, that describes set of graphs of

unbounded degree
.

Recently, we compared the expressivity of
SLR
with inductive
definitions with that of
MSO

[
28
]
. When
restricting the interpretation of the logics to tree-width bounded
graphs,
SLR
strictly subsumes
MSO
, i.e., for each
MSO
formula

\phi
and integer
k\geq 1
, there exists a formula
\psi
of

SLR
that defines the models of
\phi
of tree-width at most

k
. Moreover, the logics are incomparable for classes of graphs of
unbounded tree-width.

Contribution

This paper contributes to a dual expressivity result. We define a
fragment of
SLR
, that can specify rich shape properties of graphs of
unbounded size and degree (e.g., chains, rings, trees, stars, etc.),
whose formulæ

(1)

define bounded tree-width sets of graphs, with an effectively
computable bound, and

(2)

can be effectively translated into equivalent
MSO
formulæ.

Our fragment is defined by an easy-to-check condition on the syntax of
the inductive definitions of the predicates used in a formula.

To understand the argument behind the decidability of entailments, let

\overline{\phi}
be the equivalent
MSO
formula obtained from an

SLR
formula
\phi
by our translation. Each instance
\phi\models_{\Delta}\psi
of the entailment problem reduces to the
unsatisfiablity of the
MSO
formula
\overline{\phi}\wedge\neg\overline{\psi}
. The latter problem is decidable for graphs of
bounded tree-width, by Courcelle’s Theorem
[
15
, Corollary 4.8
(2)]
. In particular, the tree-width of the models of

\overline{\phi}\wedge\neg\overline{\psi}
is bounded by the property (1)
above and the equivalence of
\phi
and
\overline{\phi}
.

\mathtt{vert}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scalebox{.4}{$\mathsf{def}$}}}{{=}}\bigvee_{a\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathfrak{d}}}\exists y_{1}\ldots\exists y_{\#{a}}.~\mathsf{edg}_{a}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{\#{a}})
and

z_{1},\ldots,z_{\#{a}})\wedge y=z_{i}
. We show that

Rigidity

We give a syntactic condition that guarantees the bounded
fusionability of the set of canonical models of a given formula. This
provides us with everything needed to prove the main result (Theorem

4.1
). As before, let
\Delta
be an all-satisfiable,
equality-free regular SID and
\mathsf{A}
be a nullary predicate.

The
coloring
of an equality-free qpf-formula
\psi
on the
alphabet of edge labels
\mathbb{A}
is the mapping
\mathit{C}_{\psi}:\mathrm{fv}({\psi})\rightarrow\mathrm{pow}({\mathbb{A}})
, defined as
\mathit{C}_{\psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scalebox{.4}{$\mathsf{def}$}}}{{=}}\{{a\in\mathbb{A}\mid a(x,x,\ldots,x)\mbox{ occurs in }\psi}\}
, for all
x\in\mathrm{fv}({\psi})
. Intuitively, a fusion can only
join vertices that correspond to the store values of variables with
disjoint colors in a canonical model. For a productive rule
r
of

\Delta
and a
(\Delta,\mathsf{A})
-parse tree
T
denote by

\sharp_{r}(T)
the number of edges labeled by
r
in
T
.

A non-empty set
\Delta^{p}\subseteq\Delta
of productive rules is

pumping
for
\Delta
and
\mathsf{A}
if the set
\{{\min_{r\in\Delta^{p}}\sharp_{r}(T)~|~T\in\mathcal{T}({\Delta},{\mathsf{A}})}\}
is not finite. Intuitively, a pumping
set of rules can be unfolded any number of times to produce larger and
larger
\Delta
-models of
\mathsf{A}
.

Definition 14

A regular SID
\Delta
is
rigid
for
\mathsf{A}
if, for any subset

\Delta^{p}\subseteq\Delta
, that is pumping for
\Delta
and
\mathsf{A}
,
any rules
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\Delta^{p}
, such that
\psi_{i}
is the
largest qpf formula occuring in
\rho_{i}
and
\mathbf{y}_{i}
is the set
of existentially quantified variables in
\psi_{i}
, we
have
\mathcal{C}_{\psi_{1}}(y_{1})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\psi_{2}}(y_{2})\neq\emptyset
, for all
y_{i}\in\mathbf{y}_{i}
and
i=1,2
.

The above definition is effective as the pumping
sets of a given SID can be computed:

Proposition 3

The set
\{{\Delta^{p}\subseteq\Delta\mid\Delta^{p}\text{ is pumping
for }\Delta\text{ and }\mathsf{A}}\}
is effectively computable.

8
Conclusions

We tackle the problem of defining an expressive fragment of Separation
Logic of Relations that describes sets of graphs of bounded tree-width
and, moreover, has an effective equivalent
MSO
translation. This
fragment ensures the decidability of entailments. We leverage from
existing results on
MSO
-definable hyperedge replacement graph
grammars and
MSO
-definable transductions, applied to the sets of
models of a substructural logic with separating conjunction
interpreted by standard disjoint union of the relations defined by the
edge labels of a graph.

Appendix 0.A
Fission is not
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
-Definable

We denote by
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
the variant of
MSO
in which the atoms

\mathsf{edg}_{a}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\#{a}+1})
are replaced by

a(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\#{a}})
, with the following semantics:

G\Vdash^{\mathfrak{s}}a(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\#{a}})\iff\text{there exists }e\in E_{\scriptscriptstyle{G}}\text{, such that }\lambda_{\scriptscriptstyle{G}}(e)=a\text{ and }\upsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle{G}}(e)=\langle{\mathfrak{s}(x_{1}),\ldots,\mathfrak{s}(x_{\#{a}})}\rangle

It is easy to see that
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
is a
fragment of
MSO
because each atom
a(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\#{a}})

can be translated as
\exists y~.~\mathsf{edg}_{a}(y,x_{1},\ldots,x_{\#{a}})
, where
y
does not occur
anywhere else in the context of the atom.

Proposition 4

The transduction
(\mathtt{F}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\text{-}{1}}})^{*}
is not
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
-definable.

Proof

Consider the following
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
formula written using the
relation symbols
\mathsf{e}
binary and
\mathsf{c}
unary:

\displaystyle\mathit{Hamiltonian}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scalebox{.4}{$\mathsf{def}$}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\forall x\exists!y~.~\mathsf{e}(y,x)\wedge\mathsf{c}(y)~\wedge

\displaystyle\forall x~.~\mathsf{c}(x)\rightarrow\exists!y~.~[\mathsf{e}(x,y)\wedge\mathsf{c}(y)]~\wedge

\displaystyle\forall x~.~\neg\mathsf{c}(x)\rightarrow\neg\exists y~.~\mathsf{e}(x,y)~\wedge

\displaystyle\exists!x\forall y~.~\mathit{reach}_{\mathsf{e}}(y,x)

where
\exists!x.\psi(x)
stands for
\exists x~.~\psi(x)\wedge\forall y~.~\psi(y)\rightarrow x=y
and

\mathit{reach}_{\mathsf{e}}(x,y)
is the
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
formula stating that
y

is reachable from
x
via a path that uses only pairs from the
interpretation of
\mathsf{e}
. This formula describes any structure
consisting of a ring of
\mathsf{c}
-vertices, each attached to zero or
more vertices not in the interpretation of
\mathsf{c}
. Note that the
only possible pairs in a fusion consist of two
\neg\mathsf{c}
or a

\neg\mathsf{c}
and a
\mathsf{c}
vertex. Moreover, by joining a

\neg\mathsf{c}
with an
\mathsf{c}
vertex, we obtain an
\mathsf{c}
-vertex.
Let
\mathcal{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scalebox{.4}{$\mathsf{def}$}}}{{=}}\{{G\mid G\Vdash\mathit{Hamiltonian}}\}
be the set of models of

\mathit{Hamiltonian}
. Then, the set of structures in which every
vertex is labeled by
\mathsf{c}
which, moreover, has a Hamiltonian

\mathsf{e}
-cycle that visits every vertex exactly once, is the
intersection of
\mathtt{F}^{*}(\mathcal{H})
with the set

\mathcal{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scalebox{.4}{$\mathsf{def}$}}}{{=}}\{{G\mid G\Vdash\forall x~.~\mathsf{c}(x)}\}
, i.e., by fusion, every vertex becomes part of the
cycle of
\mathsf{c}
-vertices.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
(\mathtt{F}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\text{-}{1}}})^{*}
is an

\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
-definable transduction. By Thm.
6.1
, the set

\mathtt{F}^{*}(\mathcal{H})\cap\mathcal{C}
is

\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
-definable. However, this contradicts the fact that the set of
Hamiltonian structures is not
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
-definable. To see this,
consider the set
\mathcal{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scalebox{.4}{$\mathsf{def}$}}}{{=}}\{{K_{n,m}\mid n,m\geq 1}\}

of bipartite graphs with
n
vertices on the left and
m
on the
right. Clearly, this set is defined by the
\mathsf{MSO}_{1}
formula:

\displaystyle\mathit{Bipartite}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scalebox{.4}{$\mathsf{def}$}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\exists X\exists Y~.~\neg\exists x~.~X(x)\wedge Y(x)~\wedge

\displaystyle\forall x\forall y~.~X(x)\wedge\mathsf{e}(x,y)\rightarrow Y(y)~\wedge

\displaystyle\forall x\forall y~.~Y(x)\wedge\mathsf{e}(x,y)\rightarrow X(y)

Hence the set
\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{K}=\{{K_{n,n}\mid n\geq 1}\}
is
MSO
-definable, contradicting the fact that

MSO
cannot express equality of sets. ∎

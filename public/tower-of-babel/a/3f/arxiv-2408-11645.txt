Finite abelian groups acting on rationally connected threefolds I: Groups of product type
Konstantin Loginov

Abstract




Abstract.
We initiate the study of finite abelian groups that faithfully act on
3
-dimensional rationally connected varieties. We show that these groups can be naturally divided into three types: The groups of product type are finite abelian groups that are products of two groups that belong to the Cremona group of rank
1
and
2
, respectively; the groups of K3 type faithfully act on
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds
X
preserving a K3 surface
S\in|-K_{X}|
with at worst du Val singularities; the third type consists of groups that act on
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds with empty anti-canonical linear system. The classification of groups of product type follows from a result of J. Blanc. For the groups of K3 type, we establish a boundedness result. We also formulate a conjecture regarding the groups of the third type.

Keywords.
Automorphism group, Cremona group, dual complex, Fano varieties

1. Introduction

We work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

Finite abelian groups are among the most elementary objects studied in algebra. In algebraic geometry, rational varieties form a rather simple and well-behaved class of varieties. Consider the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Which finite abelian groups can faithfully and biregularly act on rational (or rationally connected) varieties of a given dimension?

Since we consider varieties endowed with the action of a finite group, there exists an equivariant resolution of singularities, so we can consider the class of finite abelian groups that act on rational (or rationally connected) smooth projective varieties. Also, the notions of biregular and birational group actions would give us the same list of groups in answer to Question
1.1
since we can resolve the indeterminacy of the group action.

In dimension
1
, the answer to Question
1.1
follows from the classification of finite subgroups of
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\PGL(2,\mathbb{C})
or, equivalently, from the classification of Platonic solids. The answer is: either a cyclic group or the Klein four-group. We formulate this elementary result for later use.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian subgroup of
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\PGL(2,\mathbb{C})
. Then
G
is isomorphic to one of the following groups:

(1)

\mathbb{Z}/n
for
n\geq 1
,

(2)

(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
.

Let us examine this proposition more closely.
We see that there are two types of finite abelian groups that can faithfully act on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
: The groups of the first type are subgroups of a torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
in
\PGL(2,\mathbb{C})
, and the group of the second type is rather special. First of all, there is only one such group, so there is a “boundedness property.” Then, the group of the second type induces a non-trivial automorphism of a divisor
D=\{0\}+\{\infty\}
(in suitable coordinates) on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
that is anti-canonical; that is,
D\sim-K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
, where
K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is the canonical class. We can consider
D
as a “Calabi–Yau variety” embedded into
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as its anti-canonical section.
The goal of this work is to generalize this observation to higher dimensions.

To put Question
1.1
into context, recall some definitions. In what follows, we denote by
\Bir(X)
the group of birational automorphisms of a variety
X
. The
Cremona group

\Cr_{n}(\mathbb{C})=\Bir(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is the group of birational automorphisms of the
n
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
over the field
\mathbb{C}
. For
n=1
, this group is isomorphic to
\PGL(2,\mathbb{C})
. In contrast, for
n\geq 2
, the Cremona group becomes a more complicated object to work with. One way to understand its structure is by means of its finite subgroups. The study of finite subgroups of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is classical and goes back to the 19th century, to the works of Kantor and Wiman
[
Kan95
,
Wim96
]
. However, the complete classification of finite subgroups of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
was obtained much later by Dolgachev and Iskovskikh; see
[
DI09
]
. Note that to answer Question
1.1
in the case of rational varieties of dimension
n
is the same as to classify finite abelian subgroups of
\Cr_{n}(\mathbb{C})
up to isomorphism. The answer to Question
1.1
in dimension
2
is as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(
cf.

[
Bla07
]
)
.

Let
X
be a rational surface, and let
G\subset\Bir(X)=\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be a
finite abelian group. Then
G
is isomorphic to one of the following groups:

(1)

\mathbb{Z}/n\times\mathbb{Z}/m
for
n\geq 1,m\geq 1
,

(2)

\mathbb{Z}/2n\times(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
for
n\geq 1
,

(3)

(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}/2
,

(4)

(\mathbb{Z}/3)^{3}
,

(5)

(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}
.

Again, we can divide these groups into two classes.
The groups (
1
), (
2
), and (
5
) are products of groups that act on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In particular, they are realized as subgroups in

\Cr_{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\Cr_{1}(\mathbb{C})\subset\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C}).

The groups (
3
) and (
4
) can be realized as subgroups of the automorphism group of a del Pezzo surface of degree
2
associated with a Fermat quartic curve, and of a Fermat cubic surface, respectively. In fact, the groups (
3
) and (
4
) are cyclic extensions of abelian groups acting on elliptic curves that are
G
-invariant anti-canonical sections of the corresponding del Pezzo surfaces. These curves admit complex multiplication (
cf.
Proposition
4.2
), so we can consider them as anti-canonically embedded
1
-dimensional Calabi–Yau varieties with “extra” symmetries (see Section
2.3
for our definition of Calabi–Yau varieties). In more detail, this is explained in Section
6
, where Theorem
1.3
is re-proved. Note that there are only two groups of type (
3
)
and (
4
), so again there is a “boundedness property.” It is also interesting to note that groups of type (
3
) and (
4
) cannot faithfully act on a conic bundle; see Proposition
5.1
.

We recall some results related to Question
1.1
. By a
p
-group
we mean a finite group of order
p^{k}
, where
p
is a prime number. The following result can be deduced from Theorem
1.3
; however, it was obtained independently.

Theorem 1.4

(
cf.

[
Beau07
]
)
.

Let
X
be a rational surface, and let
G\subset\Bir(X)=\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be a
finite abelian
p
-group. Then
G
can be generated by at most
r

elements, where

•

r=4
when
p=2
,

•

r=3
when
p=3
,

•

r=2
when
p\geq 5
.

Moreover, these bounds are sharp.

In dimension
3
, the geometry becomes much more complicated, and the complete classification of finite subgroups of
\Cr_{3}(\mathbb{C})
seems to be out of reach.
However, there exist classification results for some classes of finite groups; see
e.g.

[
Pro09
]
in the case of simple groups. There are also numerous boundedness results for finite subgroups of the Cremona group; see
[
PS16
]
and references therein. Starting from dimension
3
, from the point of view of the minimal model program, it is more natural to consider the groups of birational automorphisms of rationally connected varieties of a given dimension. In a series of works, a generalization of Theorem
1.4
to dimension
3
was obtained using explicit methods of the minimal model program.

Theorem 1.5

(
cf.

[
Pro11
,
Pro14
,
PS18
,
Kuz20
,
Xu20
,
Log22
]
)
.

Let
X
be a rationally connected variety of dimension
3
, and let

G\subset\Bir(X)
be a finite
p
-group. Then
G
can be
generated by at most
r
elements, where

•

r=6
when
p=2
,

•

r=4
when
p=3
,

•

r=3
when
p\geq 5
.

Moreover, these bounds are attained in
\Cr_{3}(\mathbb{C})
.

Recently, a generalization to higher dimensions was obtained using a different technique.

Theorem 1.6

(
cf.

[
KZ26
, Corollary 11]
)
.

Let
X
be a rationally connected variety of dimension
n
, and let
G\subset\Bir(X)
be a finite abelian
p
-group. Then
G
can be generated by
r
elements, where

r\leq\frac{pn}{p-1}\leq 2n.

Our goal is to generalize Theorem
1.3
to dimension
3
, that is, to obtain the classification of finite abelian groups that can faithfully act on
3
-dimensional rationally connected varieties. We introduce the following definition. A finite abelian group
G
is called
a group of product type
if
G\simeq G_{1}\times G_{2}
, where
G_{i}\subset\Cr_{i}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular,
G
is isomorphic to a subgroup in

\Cr_{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})\subset\Cr_{3}(\mathbb{C}).

Hence, the list of groups of product type can be easily deduced from Proposition
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
; see Table
1
in Section
3
.

In this paper, by a K3 surface we mean a normal projective surface
S
with at worst canonical (that is, du Val) singularities such that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})=0
and
K_{S}\sim 0
. We say that a finite abelian group
G
is of
K3 type
if
G
faithfully acts on a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X
preserving a K3 surface
S\in|-K_{X}|
with at worst du Val singularities. In particular, we have that
G
is an extension of a finite abelian group
H
that faithfully acts on a K3 surface by a cyclic group (cf. Corollary
2.9
):

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/m\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow 0.

Note that if a finite group faithfully acts on a singular K3 surface, then this action lifts to its minimal resolution. Our main result is the following theorem (see Section
2.4
for the definitions of a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety and a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Mori fiber space).

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a rationally connected variety of dimension
3
, and let
G\subset\Bir(X)
be a finite abelian group. Then

(1)

either
G
is of product type,

(2)

or
G
is of K3 type,

(3)

or
G
faithfully acts on a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X^{\prime}
with
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|=\varnothing
, and, moreover, any
G\mathbb{Q}
-Mori fiber space with rationally connected total space and a faithful action of
G
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold with empty anti-canonical system.

These three types are not mutually exclusive. Actually, we expect
that there is no need for the groups of the third type in
Theorem
1.7
. We formulate this as a conjecture.

Conjecture 1.8
.

In the notation of Theorem
1.7
, any group of the third type is either of product type or of K3 type.

On the other hand, there exist examples of groups of K3 type that are not of product type; see Example
11.5
. It follows that such groups do not admit a faithful action on a Mori fiber space whose total space is rationally connected and whose base is positive-dimensional; see Corollary
3.14
.

Our next result is a boundedness property for groups of K3 type. By the Weil index of a Fano variety
X
with at worst canonical singularities, we mean the number
C(X)=\max\{k\ |\,-K_{X}\sim kH\}
, where
H
is a Weil divisor on
X
.

Proposition 1.9
.

Assume that
G
is a group of K3 type. Then in the corresponding exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/m\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow 0,

where the group
H
faithfully acts on a K3 surface, we have
m\leq C
, where
C
is the maximal Weil index of a Fano threefold with at worst canonical singularities.

The existence of the constant
C
follows from the boundedness for Fano threefolds with canonical singularities; see
[
Bir21
]
. Recently, an effective estimate
C=66
was obtained in
[
JL25
]
. On the other hand, a complete classification of finite groups that can faithfully act on a smooth K3 surface is given in
[
BH23
]
. Since there exist only finitely many isomorphism classes of such groups, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.10
.

There are only finitely many isomorphism classes of groups of K3 type.

We will work with groups of K3 type in a forthcoming paper. We also establish the following result on the extensions of finite abelian groups that can act on rational curves and rational surfaces.

Proposition 1.11

(Proposition
3.11
)
.

Let
H\subset\Cr_{1}(\mathbb{C})
and
K\subset\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be finite abelian groups. Then an abelian extension
G
of
H
by
K

(
or
K
by
H)
is of product type.

Note that we do not claim that such an extension is necessarily split. We prove that under the assumptions of the proposition,
G
is isomorphic to a product of some finite abelian groups
H^{\prime}
and
K^{\prime}
that are subgroups of
\Cr_{1}(\mathbb{C})
and of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, respectively, and
H^{\prime}
,
K^{\prime}
may be different from
H
,
K
. This purely algebraic result on finite abelian groups is very useful for us. From this result it follows that if a finite abelian group
G
faithfully acts on a rationally connected
3
-dimensional Mori fiber space with a positive-dimensional base, then such a group can faithfully act on a Fano threefold that is a product of a rational curve and a rational surface; see Corollary
3.14
. This allows us to reduce our classification problem to the case of
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds. An analogous result holds in dimension
2
; see Proposition
3.10
. This leads to the following natural question.

Question 1.12
.

Is there a finite group
G
such that
G
faithfully acts on a Mori fiber space
f\colon X\to Z
with rationally connected total space
X
,
\dim X=n
, and
\dim Z>0
, but
G
does not admit a faithful action on a Fano variety with terminal singularities of dimension
n
?

Although the classification of finite subgroups of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is obtained in
[
DI09
]
, the answer to Question
1.12
is not known even in the case
n=2
; see
[
DI09
, Section 5.7]
. By Proposition
1.11
, the answer to Question
1.12
is negative for finite abelian groups in the case
n=3
.

We expect that Theorem
1.7
could be generalized to arbitrary dimension in the following way: Finite abelian groups that can faithfully act on a rationally connected variety
X
of dimension
n
either are products of groups that faithfully act on rationally connected varieties
X_{i}
of dimension
n_{i}<n
with
\sumop\displaylimits n_{i}=n
, or they are cyclic extensions of groups faithfully acting on Calabi–Yau varieties of dimension
n-1
anti-canonically embedded into some birational modification of
X
, or they act on a Fano variety with empty anti-canonical linear system.

Our approach

We present a sketch of a proof of the main results and explain the structure of the paper. The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.7
is as follows. Let
X
be a
3
-dimensional projective rationally connected variety, and let
G
be a finite abelian group such that
G\subset\Bir(X)
. After passing to a
G
-equivariant resolution, we may assume that
X
is smooth and that
G\subset\Aut(X)
. We run a
G
-equivariant minimal model program that terminates on a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Mori fiber space
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
.

In Section
3
, we study the extension properties of finite abelian groups. In particular, we prove Proposition
1.11
(= Proposition
3.11
). This excludes the case
\dim Z>0
and allows us to assume that
X
is a
3
-dimensional
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety; see Corollary
3.14
.

In Sections
4
and
5
, we do some preparations, considering the action of finite abelian groups on some classes of curves and surfaces. After that, it is easy to apply our methods to finite abelian subgroups of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and re-prove Theorem
1.3
. This is done in Section
6
. After that, we consider the action of finite abelian groups on a germ of terminal singularity of dimension
3
. In Section
7
, we prove that if there exists an (either smooth or singular)
G
-fixed point on
X
, then
G
is of product type. We also prove some more precise results that will be used later on.

The main idea is to consider the action of
G
on the anti-canonical linear system
|-K_{X}|
of a
3
-dimensional
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety. If it is empty, we are in the last case of Theorem
1.7
. So we may assume that
|-K_{X}|
is non-empty. Since the abelian group
G
acts on
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathscr{O}(-K_{X}))
, there exists a
G
-invariant element
S\in|-K_{X}|
. We analyze singularities of the pair
(X,S)
; see Section
2.2
for the relevant definitions. In Section
8
, we show that either the pair
(X,S)
is lc, or
G
is of product type. On the other hand, we show that if
(X,S)
is plt, then
G
is of K3 type, and we are in the second case of Theorem
1.7
.

So we assume that
(X,S)
is lc but not plt. We prove that
G
is of product type in this case, which is the most technical part of this paper. We consider three possibilities. If
S
is irreducible, in Section
8
we carefully apply the theory of elliptic surface singularities as well as threefold terminal singularities to show that
G
is of product type.
In Section
9
, we consider the case where
(X,S)
is dlt and
S
is reducible. Here we use the theory of the dual complex of Calabi–Yau pairs developed in
[
KX16
]
. We prove that the dual complex of
(X,S)
is homeomorphic either to a line segment or to a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
; see Corollary
9.4
. Using this result, we study combinatorics of the group action on the dual complex and conclude that
G
is of product type in this case.
In Section
10
, we consider the case where
(X,S)
is lc but not dlt, and
S
is reducible. Here we also need the theory of the dual complex, as well as a boundedness result for the number of components of
S
passing through a terminal point; see Proposition
10.3
. Again, this allows us to show that
G
is of product type in this case. This proves Theorem
1.7
.

Finally, in Section
11
, we establish a boundedness result for groups of K3 type. We do this in Proposition
11.3
, thus proving Proposition
1.9
. After that, we present some examples.

2. Preliminaries

All the varieties are projective and defined over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
, unless stated otherwise. We use the language of the minimal model program (the MMP for short); see
e.g.

[
KM98
]
.

2.1. Contractions

By a
contraction
we mean a projective
morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of normal varieties such that
f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}=\mathscr{O}_{Y}
. In particular,
f
is surjective and has connected fibers. A

fibration
is defined as a contraction
f\colon X\to Y
such
that
\dim Y<\dim X
.

Let
G
be a finite group. By a
G
-variety
we mean a variety

X
together with an action of a group
G
. If
f
is a

G
-equivariant contraction (resp. a
G
-equivariant fibration) of

G
-varieties, we call it a
G
-contraction
(resp. a

G
-fibration
).

2.2. Pairs and singularities

A
pair

(X,B)
consists of a normal variety
X
and a boundary Weil
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
with coefficients in
[0,1]
such that
K_{X}+B
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Let
\phi\colon W\to X
be a log resolution of
(X,B)
, and let

K_{W}+B_{W}=\phi^{*}(K_{X}+B)

be the log pullback of
(X,B)
. The
log discrepancy
of a prime divisor
D
on
W
with respect to
(X,B)
is
1-\mathrm{coeff}_{D}B_{W}
, and it is denoted by
a(D,X,B)
. We say
(X,B)
is lc (resp. klt,
\epsilon
-lc) if
a(D,X,B)\geq 0
(resp.
>0
,
\geq\epsilon
) for every
D
. Note that if
(X,B)
is
\epsilon
-lc, then automatically
\epsilon\leq 1
because
a(D,X,B)=1
for almost all
D
. We say that the pair
(X,B)
is
strictly lc
if it is lc but not klt. We say that the pair
(X,B)
is
plt
if
a(D,X,B)>0
holds for any
\phi
-exceptional divisor
D
and for any log resolution
\phi
. We say that the pair
(X,B)
is
dlt
if
a(D,X,B)>0
holds for any
\phi
-exceptional divisor
D
and for some log resolution
\phi
.

An
lc-place
of
(X,B)
is a prime divisor
D
over
X
, that
is, on a birational model of
X
, such that
a(D,X,B)=0
. An

lc-center
is the image on
X
of an lc-place.

2.3. Log Fano and log Calabi–Yau pairs

Let
(X,B)
be an lc pair. We say
(X,B)
is a
log Fano pair
if
-(K_{X}+B)
is ample. If
B=0
, then
X
is called a
Fano variety
. We say that an lc pair
(X,B)
is a
log Calabi–Yau pair
if
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. If
B=0
, then
X
is called a
Calabi–Yau variety
.

2.4. Mori fiber space

Let
G
be a finite group. Recall that a
G
-variety
X
is called
G\mathbb{Q}
-factorial if every
G
-invariant Weil divisor on
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. A
G\mathbb{Q}
-
Mori fiber space
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety
X
(we assume that the action of
G
on
X
is faithful) with at worst terminal singularities, together with a
G
-contraction
f\colon X\to Z
to a variety
Z
such that
\rho^{G}(X/Z)=1
and
-K_{X}
is ample over
Z
. If
X
is
G
-factorial (
e.g.
if
X
is smooth), we call it a
G
-
Mori fiber space
. If
Z
is a point, we say that
X
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety. If
\dim X=2
and
\dim Z=1
, then we say that
f\colon X\to Z
is a
G
-conic bundle
. If
\dim X=2
and
Z
is a point, then we say that
f\colon X\to Z
is a
G
-del Pezzo surface
.

2.5. Dual complex

Let
D=\sumop\displaylimits D_{i}
be a Cartier divisor on a smooth projective variety
X
. Recall that
D
has
simple normal crossings
(snc for short) if all the components
D_{i}
of
D
are smooth and any point in
D
has an open neighborhood in the analytic topology that is analytically equivalent to the union of coordinate hyperplanes.

The dual complex
, denoted by
\mathcal{D}(D)
, of a simple normal crossing divisor
D=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}D_{i}
on a smooth variety
X
is a CW-complex constructed as follows. The simplices
v_{Z}
of
\mathcal{D}(D)
are in bijection with the irreducible components
Z
(called
strata of

D
) of the intersection
\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i\in I}D_{i}
for any non-empty subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,r\}
, and the vertices of
v_{Z}
correspond to the components
D_{i}
with
i\in I
. In particular, the dimension of
v_{Z}
is equal to
\#I-1
. The gluing maps are constructed as follows. For any non-empty subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,r\}
, let
Z\subset\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i\in I}D_{i}
be an irreducible component, and for any
j\in I
, let
W
be the unique component of
\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i\in I\setminus\{j\}}D_{i}
containing
Z
. Then the gluing map is the inclusion of
v_{W}
into
v_{Z}
as a face of
v_{Z}
that does not contain the vertex
v_{i}
corresponding to
D_{i}
. Note that the dimension of
\mathcal{D}(D)
does not exceed
\dim X-1
. If
\mathcal{D}(D)
is empty, that is,
D=0
, we say that
\dim\mathcal{D}(D)=-1
.

In what follows, for a divisor
D
, we denote by
D^{=1}
the sum of the components of
D
with coefficient
1
. For an lc log CY pair
(X,D)
, we define
\mathcal{D}(X,D)
as
\mathcal{D}(D_{Y}^{=1})
, where
f\colon(Y,D_{Y})\to(X,D)
is a log resolution of

, so the formula

K_{Y}+D_{Y}=f^{*}(K_{X}+D)

is satisfied. It is known that the PL-homeomorphism class of
\mathcal{D}(D_{Y}^{=1})
does not depend on the choice of a log resolution; see
[
dFKX17
, Proposition 11]
. For more results on the topology of dual complexes of Calabi–Yau pairs, see
[
KX16
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that if a finite group
G\subset\Aut(X)
preserves a divisor

on a projective variety

such that the pair
(X,D)
is lc, then
G
acts on the topological space
\mathcal{D}(X,D)
by PL-homeomorphisms.

2.6. Group action on spheres

We recall some facts about the action of finite groups on spheres of lower dimensions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group that faithfully acts on a circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
by PL-homeomorphisms. Then
G=\mathbb{Z}/n
for some
n\geq 1
or
G=\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
.

Proof.

Observe that
G
can be realized as a subgroup of symmetries of a regular
n
-gon in a plane.

Hence,
G
is a subgroup of the dihedral group
D_{n}=\mathbb{Z}/n\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2
. Thus
G
fits into an exact sequence
0\to H\to G\to K\to 0
, where
K
is either trivial or
\mathbb{Z}/2
and
H
is cyclic contained in
\mathbb{Z}/n
. If
K
is trivial,
G
is cyclic. If
K=\mathbb{Z}/2
, then
H
is fixed by the action of
\mathbb{Z}/2
on
\mathbb{Z}/n
, but the action is
x\mapsto-x
, so
H
is either trivial or
\mathbb{Z}/2
. This proves that
G
is either cyclic or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(
cf.

[
Zim12
, Section 2]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group that faithfully acts on a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
by PL-homeomorphisms. Then
G
is conjugate to a subgroup of the orthogonal group
O(3)
. In particular, if
G
is abelian, then either
G\subset\mathbb{Z}/n\times\mathbb{Z}/2
for some
n\geq 1
, or
G=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}
. Consequently, either
G
has a fixed point on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, or
G
has an orbit of length
2
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

2.7. Group actions on algebraic varieties.

We collect some generalities on the actions of finite groups on algebraic varieties.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group. By
\mathfrak{r}(G)
, we denote the
rank
of
G
, that is, the minimal number of generators of
G
.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that if
G
is a finite abelian group and
H\subset G
is a subgroup, then
\mathfrak{r}(H)\leq\mathfrak{r}(G)
.

Lemma 2.6

(
cf.

[
Pop14
, Lemma 4]
)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety, and let
G
be a finite group such that
G\subset\Aut(X)
. Assume
P\in X
is a fixed point of
G
. Then the induced action of
G
on the tangent space
T_{P}X
is faithful.

Corollary 2.7
.

If a finite abelian group
G\subset\Aut(X)
has a fixed point
P\in X
, where
X
is an algebraic variety of dimension
n
and
P
is a smooth point, then
\mathfrak{r}(G)\leq n
.

In what follows, we will need the following results.

Lemma 2.8

(
cf.

[
Pro11
, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

Let
X
be a
3
-dimensional algebraic variety
X
with isolated singularities, and let
G
be a finite abelian group such that
G\subset\Aut(X)
.

(1)

If there is a curve
C\subset X
of
G
-fixed points, then
\mathfrak{r}(G)\leq 2
.

(2)

If there is a
(
possibly, reducible
)
divisor
S\subset X
of
G
-fixed points, then
\mathfrak{r}(G)\leq 1
. If moreover
S
is singular along a curve, then
G
is trivial.

Proof.

We prove the first claim. Let
P
be a general point in
C
. Since
X
has isolated singularities,
P
is smooth on
X
. In particular,
\dim T_{P}X=3
. By Lemma
2.6
, the induced action of
G
on
T_{P}X
is faithful. On the other hand, the action of
G
on the
1
-dimensional subspace
T_{P}C\subset T_{P}X
is trivial, since by assumption the action of
G
is trivial on
C
. Hence
G
faithfully acts on a
G
-invariant complement
T^{\prime}
to
T_{P}C
in
T_{P}X
. So we have
\dim T^{\prime}=2
. Since
G
is a finite abelian group, it follows that
\mathfrak{r}(G)\leq 2
in this case.

The first part of the second claim is proven analogously. We prove the second part. By assumption, the action of
G
on
S
it trivial. It follows that the induced action of
G
on
T_{P}S
is trivial for any point
P\in S
. For a general point
P\in\Sing(S)
, we have
T_{P}S=T_{P}X
since
X
is smooth at
P
and
P
is singular on
S
. By Lemma
2.6
, we conclude that
G
is trivial.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

If a finite abelian group
G
faithfully acts on a
3
-dimensional algebraic variety
X
with isolated singularities preserving a surface
S
, then there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/m\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow 0

for
m\geq 1
, where
H\subset\Aut(S)
.

Proof.

Denote the image of a natural map
\phi\colon G\to\Aut(S)
by
H
. By Lemma
2.8
(
2
), the kernel of
\phi
is cyclic. The claim follows.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety, and let
G
be a finite abelian group such that
G\subset\Aut(X)
. Let
S=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}S_{i}
be a divisor on
X
such that the induced action of
G
on
S
is faithful. Denote by
G_{S}
the image of
G
in the symmetric group on the set
\{S_{i}\}
. Assume that the action of
G_{S}
on the set of components
\{S_{i}\}
is transitive. Then in the exact sequence

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G_{S}\longrightarrow 0,

the group
H
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\Aut(S_{i})
.

Proof.

Fix a component
S_{1}
of
S
.
Since the action of
G_{S}
on the set
\{S_{i}\}
is transitive, for any component
S_{j}
, there exists an element
g^{\prime}_{j}\in G_{S}
such that
g^{\prime}_{j}(S_{1})=S_{j}
. Pick preimages
g_{j}\in G
of the elements
g^{\prime}_{j}\in G_{S}
.
For any
j
, we identify
S_{1}
and
S_{j}
via the isomorphism
S_{1}\simeq g_{j}(S_{1})=S_{j}
.
Since by assumption the action of
G
on
S
is faithful,
G
can be included in the exact sequence (
2.1
) with

H\subset\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\Aut(S_{i}).

Consider an arbitrary element

h\in H\subset\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\Aut(S_{i})\simeq\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\Aut(S_{1}),

where we used the isomorphisms chosen above.
Let
\mathrm{pr}_{j}\colon\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\Aut(S_{1})\to\Aut(S_{1})
be the projection to
j^{\mathrm{th}}
factor.
Since
G
is abelian, for any
j
, we have
g_{j}hg_{j}^{-1}=h
. It follows that
\mathrm{pr}_{i}(h)=\mathrm{pr}_{j}(h)
for any
i,j
.
Let

\Delta\colon\Aut(S_{1})\longrightarrow\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\Aut(S_{1})

be the diagonal embedding:
\Delta(g)=(g,\ldots,g)
. Then
H\simeq\Delta(\mathrm{pr}_{1}(H))
. Thus,
H\simeq\mathrm{pr}_{1}(H)\subset\Aut(S_{1})
, and the claim follows.
∎

3. Groups of product type

In this section, we fix some notation related to finite abelian groups and introduce the definition of abelian groups of product type.
We also study extension properties of finite abelian groups.
We start with the following lemma, which follows from the classification of finite abelian groups; see also
[
Ka69
, Theorem 5]
for a more general statement.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. Let
g\in G
be an element, and let
\langle g\rangle\subset G
be a subgroup generated by
g
. Assume that
\langle g\rangle
is a maximal cyclic subgroup in
G
, which means that if there is a cyclic subgroup
H\subset G
such that
\langle g\rangle\subset H
, then
H=\langle g\rangle
.
Then
G\simeq\langle g\rangle\times K
for some subgroup
K\subset G
. In particular,
\mathfrak{r}(G/\langle g\rangle)<\mathfrak{r}(G)
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group such that
G
fits in the exact sequence

(3.1)

0\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow G\overset{\phi}{\longrightarrow}K\longrightarrow 0.

Assume that
H
is cyclic. Then either the sequence (
3.1
) splits, or we have
G\simeq H^{+}\times K^{-}
, where
H^{+}
is a cyclic group that contains
H
as a subgroup and
K^{-}=K/\phi(H^{+})
.

Proof.

Assume that there is no cyclic subgroup
H^{+}
of
G
such that
H\subset H^{+}
and
H\neq H^{+}
. Then by Lemma
3.1
, we have
G\simeq H\times K
, and the exact sequence (
3.1
) splits. So we may assume that there is a cyclic subgroup
H^{+}
of
G
such that
H\subset H^{+}
and
H\neq H^{+}
. We may assume that
H^{+}
is maximal with this property.
Put
K^{-}=K/\phi(H^{+})
.
Then we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{+}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow K^{-}\longrightarrow 0.

Since the cyclic subgroup
H^{+}
is maximal in
G
, by Lemma
3.1
, we have
G\simeq H^{+}\times K^{-}
.
∎

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
In what follows, by
G_{p}
we denote the
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
, where
p
is a prime number, so we have

G=\mathop{\scalebox{1.2}{$\prodop\displaylimits$}}_{p\geq 2}G_{p}.

We occasionally say that
G_{p}
is the
p
-part
of
G
.

Remark 3.3
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{r}(G_{p})\leq k
for any prime number
p
. Then
\mathfrak{r}(G)\leq k
.

For example, by Theorem
1.5
, if
G
faithfully acts on a rationally connected threefold, then
\mathfrak{r}(G_{2})\leq 6
,
\mathfrak{r}(G_{3})\leq 4
, and
\mathfrak{r}(G_{p})\leq 3
for
p\geq 5
.

For a finite abelian group
G
, we put

G_{\neq p}=\mathop{\scalebox{1.2}{$\prodop\displaylimits$}}_{q\neq p}G_{q},\quad\text{so }G=G_{p}\times G_{\neq p}.

For an abelian
p
-group
G_{p}
, we say that
G_{p}
has type

\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}]\quad\text{for }\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}

if

G_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p^{\lambda_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}/p^{\lambda_{k}}.

Remark 3.4
.

A sequence of finite abelian groups

(3.2)

0\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow 0

is exact if and only if for any prime
p
the
p
-parts
H_{p}
,
G_{p}
,
K_{p}
of the groups
H
,
G
,
K
, respectively, form an exact sequence

(3.3)

0\longrightarrow H_{p}\longrightarrow G_{p}\longrightarrow K_{p}\longrightarrow 0.

We say that the exact sequence (
3.3
) is the
p
-part
of the exact sequence (
3.2
).

To any type
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}]
corresponds the Young diagram with
\lambda_{i}
squares in the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
row. For two Young diagrams
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}]
and
\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots\mu_{k}]
, one can define their product
\lambda\cdot\mu
as a formal linear combination of Young diagrams with non-negative coefficients; see
e.g.

[
Ful00
, Section 2]
. Then the
Littlewood–Richardson coefficient

c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}
is the coefficient at the Young diagram
\nu=[\nu_{1},\ldots\nu_{k}]
in the product of Young diagrams
\lambda\cdot\mu
.

Theorem 3.5

(
cf.

[
Ful00
, Section 2]
)
.

In the above notation, assume that
G_{p}
has type
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}]
,
H_{p}
has type
\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}]
, and
K_{p}
has type
\nu=[\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k}]
.
Then an extension of the form (
3.3
) exists if and only if for the Littlewood–Richardson coefficient, we have
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}>0
.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
G
,
H
, and
K
be finite abelian groups. Since
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}=c^{\lambda}_{\nu\mu}
(
cf.

[
Ful97
, Corollary I.5.1.3]
), from Remark
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
. it follows that an abelian extension of
K
by
H
isomorphic to
G
exists if and only if there exists an abelian extension of
H
by
K
isomorphic to
G
.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that a finite abelian group
G
is
a group of product type
if
G\simeq G_{1}\times G_{2}
, where
G_{i}\subset\Cr_{i}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular,
G
is isomorphic to a subgroup in

\Cr_{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})\subset\Cr_{3}(\mathbb{C}).

Proposition 3.8
.

For a finite abelian group
G
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

G\subset A_{1}\times A_{2}
, where the
A_{i}
are arbitrary groups;

(2)

G\simeq G_{1}\times G_{2}
, where
G_{i}\subset A_{i}
.

Thus, a finite abelian group
G
is of product type if and only if
G
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\Cr_{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
is clear. Assume that
G\subset A_{1}\times A_{2}
.
Let
P_{i}=\pi_{i}(G)\subset A_{i}
.
It is enough to argue separately for each
p
-primary component. Thus,

G_{p}\subset P_{1,p}\times P_{2,p}.

Write

G_{p}\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb Z/p^{\lambda_{j}}\mathbb Z,\qquad P_{1,p}\times P_{2,p}\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\mathbb Z/p^{\gamma_{j}}\mathbb Z,

where the exponents are arranged in non-increasing order. Clearly, one has
\lambda_{j}\leq\gamma_{j}
for all
j
.
Also, each cyclic factor
\mathbb Z/p^{\gamma_{j}}\mathbb Z
belongs either to

P_{1,p}
or to
P_{2,p}
. Assign the corresponding factor

\mathbb Z/p^{\lambda_{j}}\mathbb Z
to
G_{1,p}
or
G_{2,p}
accordingly.
Since
\lambda_{j}\leq\gamma_{j}
, we have

\mathbb Z/p^{\lambda_{j}}\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Z/p^{\gamma_{j}}\mathbb Z.

Hence

G_{p}\simeq G_{1,p}\times G_{2,p},\qquad G_{i,p}\subset P_{i,p}\subset A_{i}.

Taking the product over all primes
p
, define

G_{i}=\prodop\displaylimits_{p}G_{i,p}.

Then

G\simeq G_{1}\times G_{2}
,
G_{i}\subset A_{i}
.
This proves
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
.

∎

Note that Proposition
3.8
does not hold for arbitrary (finite) groups.

Using Proposition
1.2
and Theorem

, one checks that the groups of product type are exactly the

Note that the class of groups of product type is closed under taking subgroups and quotient groups. We will use the following observation repeatedly.

Remark 3.9
.

If
\mathfrak{r}(G)\leq 3
, then

is of product type.

Another simple observation is the following proposition, which can be proven by an easy case-by-case analysis.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let

be finite abelian groups. Let

be an abelian group that is an extension of

by

(
or

by

for some subgroups

is isomorphic to a subgroup of

has type (

), (

), or (

It is a kind of surprise that an analogous result holds in dimension

Proposition 3.11

Let

by

(

Proof.

According to Remark

, it is enough to consider the case of an extension of

by

.
We have an exact sequence

(3.4)

where

is a finite abelian group.

, there are two possibilities for

,

(\mathbb{Z}/3)^{3}
,

If the exact sequence (

Corollary 3.12
.

Let

be a

-dimensional algebraic variety

.

, then

(

Remark 6.1

Our proof of Theorem

Our goal is to generalize these observations to dimension

) and (

) as in Theorem

and certain elliptic curves with complex multiplication.

Corollary 6.2
.

) or (

) as in Theorem

on a rational surface is conjugate to an action of

on a smooth del Pezzo surface

such that each of its

-invariant anti-canonical elements is a smooth elliptic curve. Moreover, such an elliptic curve admits an automorphism of order

, and there are the following possibilities
(

:

(1)

,

,

,

;

(2)

Let

and

be distinct

. By our assumption on the group

and the proof of Theorem

, we know that

and

are smooth elliptic curves.
In the notation of Diagram (

), the elements of

(resp.

(resp.

7. Terminal singularities

We recall some facts on the classification of
3
-dimensional terminal singularities from
[
Mor85
,
Rei87
]
.
Let
P\in X
be a germ of a
3
-dimensional terminal singularity. Then the singularity is isolated:
\Sing(X)=\{P\}
. The
index
of
P\in X
is the minimal positive integer
r
such that
rK_{X}
is Cartier. If
r=1
, then
P\in X
is Gorenstein. In this case,
P\in X
is analytically isomorphic to a hypersurface singularity in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
of multiplicity
2
. Moreover, any Weil
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

on

is Cartier. Also, in this case,

is a compound du Val singularity, which means that the general hyperplane section

that contains

is a surface with a du Val singularity at
P
. We say that

has type
cA_{n}
,
cD_{n}
,
cE_{n}
, respectively, if
H
is a du Val singularity of type
A_{n}
,
D_{n}
,
E_{n}
, respectively.

If
r>1
, then there is a cyclic, étale outside
P
, covering

\pi\colon\widetilde{P}\in\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow X\ni P

of degree
r
such that
\widetilde{P}\in\widetilde{X}
is a Gorenstein terminal singularity (or a smooth point). The map
\pi
is called the
index
1
cover
of
P\in X
, and it is defined canonically.

Theorem 7.1

(
cf.

[
Mor85
,
Rei87
]
)
.

In the above notation,

\widetilde{P}\in\widetilde{X}
is
\mathbb{Z}/r
-equivariantly analytically isomorphic to a hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
with
\mathbb{Z}/r
-semi-invariant coordinates

, the action of
\mathbb{Z}/r
is given by

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\longmapsto(\xi^{a_{1}}x_{1},\xi^{a_{2}}x_{2},\xi^{a_{3}}x_{3},\xi^{a_{4}}x_{4})

for some primitive

root of unity
\xi
, and one of the following holds:

(1)

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{4})=(1,-1,a_{3},0)\bmod r
and
\gcd(a_{3},r)=1
,

(2)

r=4
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{4})=(1,-1,1,2)\bmod 4

Now, we consider the action of a finite abelian group on a
3
-dimensional terminal singularity germ. Recall the following result.

Proposition 7.2

(
cf.

[
Pro11
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[

, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let

be a germ of a threefold terminal singularity, and let

and
\mathfrak{r}(G_{p})\leq 3

.

The main goal of this section is to improve this result as follows.

Theorem 7.3

\mathfrak{r}(G)\leq 3

.

Before proving this theorem, we deduce from it the following useful result.

Assume that

The latter case in Theorem

Definition 7.8
.

We say that the action of the group

For a group

with

,

,

\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a change of generators of

, the action of

becomes standard on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with respect to the coordinates

.

.

be a

\phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\sumop\displaylimits\phi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=0

\mathbb{C}^{4}
with coordinates

.
Let
G
be a finite abelian group such that

.
Assume that

.
Then

•

\phi

for

; that is,
\phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\psi(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{2})
for some function

;

•

\rk(\phi_{2})\geq 2
, and
P\in X
is a singularity of type
cA
;

•

\mathfrak{r}(G_{p})\leq 2
for any

; in particular,

and hence

is of product type.

Since

, there exists a subgroup
H=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}\subset G
. We may assume that the action of

is standard with respect to

for

. Since the hypersurface

. Hence
\phi
depends on
x_{i}^{2}
for

; that is,
\phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\psi(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{2})
for some

. In particular,
\phi_{i}=0
for odd

. By the classification of terminal singularities,

, and since

, we have

Without loss of generality and after rescaling coordinates, we may assume that

contains the terms

and a power of

, and similarly for a power of

. Thus, up to a rescaling of coordinates, we have

)), we have

(8.9)

\phi=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2k}+x_{4}^{2l}+\cdots

for some

.

Let
\psi=0
be the equation of the divisor

in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
such that

. Since

, by Equation (

), we have
\dim T_{P}S=3
. Therefore, for the linear term
\psi_{1}
of

, we have
\psi_{1}\neq 0
. By the classification of simple elliptic singularities,
cf.
Equation (
5.4
), we have
\rk(\phi_{2}|_{\psi=0})=1
. Since
\rk(\phi_{2}|_{\psi=0})=1
, we see that
\psi_{1}
contains either
x_{1}
or
x_{2}
with a non-zero coefficient.

Thus we may assume that
\psi_{1}
contains
x_{1}
. Note that
\psi
(and so
\psi_{1}
) can be chosen

-semi-invariant, and also
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}

should be divisible by

. But since

is irreducible, this implies that, up to a rescaling of the coordinates, we have
\psi=x_{1}
.

By
(\phi|_{\psi=0})_{4}
we denote the homogeneous term of degree

of

By Equation (

), we have that

and

By Propositions
8.16
and
8.17

G_{N}=H_{N}
, where
G_{N}
is as in the sequence (

) and

is a subgroup of

.
Let

It follows that

∎

9. Reducible anti-canonical element: Dlt case

In this section, we treat the case of a
G
-invariant reducible element
S\in|-K_{X}|
such that the pair
(X,S)
is divisorially log terminal. We study the induced action of
G
on

. The key ingredient here is the notion of dual complex.
We recall some results on dual complexes of Calabi–Yau pairs; see Section
2.5
for the main definitions.

Lemma 9.1

(
cf.

[
KX16
, Claim 32.1(1)]
)
.

Let
(X,S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i})
be a dlt log CY pair.
Assume that
\dim\mathcal{D}(S)=\dim X-1
. Then all the strata of
S
are rationally connected. In particular, if
\dim X=3
, then all the strata of

are rational.

Lemma 9.2

(
cf.

[
KX16
, Lemma 25]
)
.

Let
(X,S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i})
be an snc pair. Then for any
i\geq 0

there is an inclusion

\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathcal{D}(S),\mathbb{C})\xhookrightarrow{\hphantom{0pt}}\mathrm{H}^{i}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S}).

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
(X,S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i})
be a dlt pair. Then for any
i\geq 0
there is an inclusion

\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathcal{D}(S),\mathbb{C})\xhookrightarrow{\hphantom{0pt}}\mathrm{H}^{i}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S}).

Proof.

Let
f\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{S})\to(X,S)
be a log resolution of the dlt pair
(X,S)
, so that

f^{*}(K_{X}+S)=K_{\widetilde{X}}+\widetilde{S}.

Since
(X,S)
is dlt, we can choose the log resolution

in such a way that the log discrepancies of all the
f
-exceptional divisors are positive. Put
S^{\prime}=\widetilde{S}^{=1}
.
The induced map
\pi=f|_{S^{\prime}}\colon S^{\prime}\to S
induces a bijection between the components of
S^{\prime}
and the components of
S
and is birational on each component of
S^{\prime}
.
The argument in
[
KX16
, proof of Theorem 59(1)]
gives isomorphisms

\mathrm{H}^{i}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{i}(S^{\prime},\mathscr{O}_{S^{\prime}})

for every
i
.
We obtain the following commutative diagram:

(9.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime}),\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i}(S^{\prime},\mathscr{O}_{S^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathcal{D}(S),\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{i}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})\hbox to0.0pt{,\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

where

is an isomorphism. Note that

is an isomorphism as well since

induces a homeomorphism between
\mathcal{D}(S)
and
\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})
.
By Lemma

, the map

is an inclusion. We conclude that the induced map

is an inclusion. The claim follows.
∎

Corollary 9.4
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with terminal singularities.
Assume that the pair
(X,S)
is lc, where
S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i}
and
S\sim-K_{X}
. Then the dual complex
\mathcal{D}(X,S)
is homeomorphic to one of the following topological spaces:

(1)

a point,

(2)

an interval
I
,

(3)

a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{S})\to(X,S)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
dlt modification of
(X,S)
, so we have

K_{\widetilde{X}}+\widetilde{S}=f^{*}(K_{X}+S)\sim 0.

Put
S^{\prime}=\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{S}^{=1}
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})
the dual complex of
S^{\prime}
, which is equal to the dual complex
\mathcal{D}(X,S)
by definition. Assume that
\dim\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})=0
. Since
S
is ample, its support is connected, and so is the support of
S^{\prime}
; hence
\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})
is a point.

Assume that
\dim\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})=1
. By the main result of
[
KX16
]
, we have that
\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})

\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})
is homeomorphic either to a circle or to an interval. Assume that
\mathcal{D}(S^{\prime})
is homeomorphic to a circle

.
Consider an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-S^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{S^{\prime}}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\widetilde{X}
is rationally connected, we have

for

.
Using Serre duality, we obtain

. This contradicts Corollary

. We conclude that

Finally, assume that

. Then

∎

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
Assume that

•

X
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold and the action of
G
on
X
is faithful,

•

S\in|-K_{X}|
is a
G
-invariant element,

•

the pair
(X,S)
is lc, and

•

S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i}
is reducible.

Then either
G
is of product type, or the action of
G
on the set
\{S_{i}\}
of irreducible components of
S
is transitive.

Proof.

Since
X
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety, we have
\rho^{G}(X)=1
. If the action of
G
on the set of irreducible components of
S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i}
is not transitive, then
S=S^{\prime}+S^{\prime\prime}
, where
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
are
G
-invariant Weil
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors. In particular,
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
are proportional in
\Pic(X)
. It follows that the pair
(X,S^{\prime})
is an lc log Fano pair.
The following argument is similar to the one in the proof of Proposition
8.1
.

Consider a minimal lc-center
Z
of
(X,S^{\prime})
. After perturbing the pair by a
G
-invariant divisor with small coefficients, we may assume that

is an isolated lc-center of an lc log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
. Since every translate

is also an isolated lc-center, Kollár–Shokurov connectedness implies that

is
G
-invariant. By subadjunction,

is either a point, a smooth rational curve, or a klt log del Pezzo surface (in particular, it is rational). In the first case, apply Corollary
7.4
to conclude that

is of product type. In the last two cases, apply Corollary
3.12
to conclude that
G
is of product type. The claim is proven.
∎

Remark 9.6
.

Let
(X,S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i})
be a dlt log CY pair with
\dim X=3
.
Assume that a finite abelian group
G
acts faithfully on
(X,S)
in such a way that the induced action on a dual complex

has a fixed point. Then there exists a
G
-invariant stratum of

. In particular, if

has terminal singularities and

, then by Lemma

, all the strata of

are rational, and hence

is of product type by Corollary
7.4
or

.

Proposition 9.7
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
Assume that

•

X
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold and the action of
G
on
X
is faithful,

•

S\in|-K_{X}|
is a reducible
G
-invariant element,

•

the pair
(X,S)
is dlt, and

•

the dual complex
\mathcal{D}(S)
is homeomorphic to an interval.

Then
G
is of product type.

Proof.

By assumption,
S=\sumop\displaylimits S_{i}
is reducible, and hence
S
is singular along a curve.
By Lemma
2.8
, the action of
G
on
S
is faithful.
By Lemma
9.5
, we may assume the action of
G
on the set of components of
S
is transitive.
Thus,
S
has only two components:
S=S_{1}+S_{2}
. We claim that both
S_{i}
are rational.

Indeed, the two components are isomorphic because they are
interchanged by
G
. Suppose that they are not rational. By the proof
of Lemma
5.2
, each
S_{i}
is then birationally
ruled over an elliptic curve, and therefore

h^{1}(S_{i},\mathscr{O}_{S_{i}})=1.

Put
C=S_{1}\cap S_{2}
. The curve
C
is connected, and adjunction gives

p_{a}(C)\leq 1
. As in the proof of
Proposition
8.2
, the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}(K_{X})\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{S}\longrightarrow 0

gives
H^{1}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})=0
. On the other hand, from

(9.2)

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{S_{1}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{S_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{C}\longrightarrow 0

we obtain an injection

which is impossible because the left-hand side has dimension
2
, whereas the
right-hand side has dimension at most
1
. Thus both
S_{i}
are rational.

By Lemma
2.10
, we have that
G
is an extension of a finite abelian subgroup of
\Cr_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by
\mathbb{Z}/2
. By Proposition
3.11
, we conclude that
G
is of product type.
∎

Proposition 9.8
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
Assume that

•

X
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold and the action of
G
on
X
is faithful,

•

S\in|-K_{X}|
is a
G
-invariant element,

•

the pair
(X,S)
is dlt, and

•

the dual complex
\mathcal{D}(S)
is homeomorphic to a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Then
G
is of product type.

Proof.

The action on
S
is faithful by Lemma
2.8
,
and by Lemma
9.5
we may assume that
G
acts
transitively on the set
\Omega
of components of
S
. Since

\dim\mathcal{D}(S)=2
, all components and strata are rational by
Lemma
9.1
.

If the induced action of
G
on

\mathcal{D}(S)\simeq\mathbb S^{2}
has a fixed point, we are done
by Remark
9.6
. Otherwise,
Lemma
2.3
gives a
G
-orbit
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
.
Let
\sigma_{i}
be the unique open cell containing
x_{i}
, and let
V_{i}

be the set of components of
S
corresponding to the vertices of

\overline{\sigma}_{i}
. Both

V_{1}\cap V_{2}
and
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
are

-invariant subsets of
\Omega
.
Transitivity implies that
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=\Omega
and that either

or
V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\varnothing
. In the first case

, since a cell of the two-dimensional dual complex has
at most three vertices. In the second case

|\Omega|=2|V_{1}|\in\{2,4,6\}.

Put

.
Since

is abelian and acts transitively on

, the stabilizers of all

. Since

is reducible,

.
Therefore

is cyclic or isomorphic to

, and hence

. By Lemma
2.10
,
the kernel of

embeds into the automorphism group of a rational

. Proposition
3.11

now shows that

is of product type.

∎

10. Reducible anti-canonical element: Lc case

In this section, we treat the case of a
G
-invariant reducible element
S\in|-K_{X}|
such that the pair
(X,S)
is lc but not dlt. We study the induced action of
G
on
S
. The key ingredient here is the boundedness of the number of components of
S
that pass through a terminal point on a threefold
X
. This is done in Proposition
10.3
.

Remark 10.1
.

Let
P\in X
be a
2
-dimensional canonical singularity germ.
Assume that
(X,S=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}S_{i})
is an lc pair such that
P\in S_{i}
for every
i
.
Then using the classification of canonical singularities on surfaces, one can check that
n\leq 2
.

Example 10.2
.

Let
P\in X
be the germ of an ordinary double point given by the equation

x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4}=0

where
P
is the origin.
Consider a divisor
S
on
X
given by the equation
x_{1}x_{2}=0
. Then
S=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4}
has four components passing through
P
. The pair
(X,S)
is toric; in particular, it is lc.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
P\in X
be a
3
-dimensional terminal singularity germ.
Let
(X,S=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}S_{i})
be an lc pair such that
P\in S_{i}
for every
i
.
Then
n\leq 4
.

Proof.

Let

\pi\colon\widetilde{P}\in\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow X\ni P

be the canonical cover as in Section
7
, and let
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{S})
be the log pullback of
(X,S)
.
We see that the number of components of
S
is bounded by the number of components of
\widetilde{S}
.
So we may assume that
P\in X
is Gorenstein.

Clearly, if
X
is smooth, then
n\leq 3
.
Hence we may assume that
X
is singular at
P
. Let us assume that
0=P\in X\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}
is given by the equation

\phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\sumop\displaylimits_{i\geq 2}\phi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=0,

where
\deg\phi_{i}=i
. Since the point
P\in X
is terminal, we have
1\leq\rk(\phi_{2})\leq 4
. Consider the blow-up
g\colon\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{4}}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}
of the closed point
P\in X\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}
with exceptional divisor
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
Let
Y
be the strict transform of
X
on
\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{4}}
.
By abuse of notation, we denote by
g\colon Y\to X
the induced blow-up of
X
.
Since
K_{X}+S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
K_{X}
is Cartier on
X
, we have that
S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and hence Cartier; see
[
Kaw88
, Lemma 5.1]
.
We denote by
D
a Cartier divisor on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
such that
D|_{X}=S
. Write

(10.1)

K_{\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{4}}}=g^{*}K_{\mathbb{C}^{4}}+3E,\quad Y=g^{*}X-2E,\quad D^{\prime}=g^{*}D-bE,

where
D^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
D
on
\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{4}}
. Put
E_{Y}=E|_{Y}
.
Since the pair
(X,S)
is lc, for the log discrepancy of
E_{Y}
, we have
a(E_{Y},X,S)=2-b\geq 0
, and so

b\leq 2
.

Note that
E|_{E}=-H_{E}
, where
H_{E}
is a hyperplane on
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
Write

0=g^{*}D|_{E}=D^{\prime}|_{E}+bE|_{E}=D^{\prime}|_{E}-bH_{E}.

We now show that if
b=1
, then
n\leq 2
.
Assume that
b=1
. Then
D
is smooth at the point
P
. We claim that
X\cap D=S
, considered as a Cartier divisor on
D
, is given by an equation with a non-zero quadratic term in the expansion near

. Indeed, for the discrepancy of
E_{Y}
, we have

1=2-b=a(E_{Y},X,S)=a(E_{Y},D,X|_{D})=3-\mult_{P}(X|_{D}),

and so
\mult_{P}(X|_{D})=2
.
Hence
P\in S=X|_{D}
, considered as a surface germ in a smooth
3
-dimensional space

, has at most two components meeting at
P
. This shows that
n\leq 2
in this case.

So we may assume that
b=2
and the log pullback via
g
of the pair
(\mathbb{C}^{4},X+D)
is
(\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{4}},Y+D^{\prime}+E)
.
By assumption, the pair
(X,S)
is lc. By inversion of adjunction, it follows that
(\mathbb{C}^{4},X+D)
is lc. Hence,
(\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{4}},Y+D^{\prime}+E)
is lc as well. Thus, the intersection

cannot contain a surface. Therefore,
D^{\prime}|_{Y}
is equal to the strict transform
S^{\prime}=\sumop\displaylimits S^{\prime}_{i}
of

on
Y
. Also, again by the lc condition,
Y
is normal along

, and hence
Y
is normal by the Serre criterion.
By adjunction, the pair
(Y,S^{\prime}+E_{Y})
is lc.

We can write

S^{\prime}|_{E_{Y}}=D^{\prime}|_{E_{Y}}\sim bH_{E}|_{E_{Y}}=2H_{E}|_{E_{Y}}.

Observe that the intersection
S^{\prime}_{i}\cap E_{Y}
is a curve for every
i
. Indeed, this follows from the fact that
E_{Y}
is a Cartier divisor and
S^{\prime}_{i}\cap E_{Y}
is non-empty for every

by assumption.

Since
S
is Cartier on
X
, we have that
S^{\prime}=g^{*}S-2E_{Y}
is Cartier on
Y
.
Recall that the pair
(Y,S^{\prime}+E_{Y})
is lc,
Y
is Gorenstein, and both
S^{\prime}
and
E_{Y}
are Cartier. It follows that
Y
is smooth at the generic point of
S^{\prime}\cap E_{Y}
. Therefore, two components of
S^{\prime}
cannot intersect along a curve that belongs to
E_{Y}
. Thus, it is enough to show that
S^{\prime}|_{E_{Y}}
has at most four components on
E_{Y}
.
We consider four cases.

Case

\rk(\phi_{2})=4
.
Then
P\in X
is an ordinary double point,
Y
is smooth, and
E_{Y}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Since
H_{E}|_{E_{Y}}\sim(1,1)
, we obtain

S^{\prime}|_{E_{Y}}\sim(2,2).

Since
S^{\prime}|_{E_{Y}}
can be decomposed into at most four components, it follows that
S^{\prime}
(and
S
) has at most four components, so

.

Case

\rk(\phi_{2})=3

\mathcal{D}(B)
corresponding to their strict transforms. A finite
group of homeomorphisms of an interval has order at most
2
.
Since
S
is reducible, it follows that
S=S_{1}+S_{2}
, and its two
rational components are interchanged. Lemma
2.10

and Proposition
3.11
finish the proof.

∎

11. Groups of K3 type

In this section, we work with finite abelian groups of K3 type. Recall that by a K3 surface we mean a normal projective surface

S
with at worst canonical (that is,
1
-lc) singularities such that

\mathrm{H}^{1}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})=0
and
K_{S}\sim 0
.

Definition 11.1
.

We say that a finite abelian subgroup
G
is of
K3 type
if
G
faithfully acts on a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X
preserving a K3 surface
S\in|-K_{X}|
with at worst du Val singularities.

Let
X
be a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold, where
G
is a finite abelian group and
G
acts on
X
faithfully. Assume that there exists a
G
-invariant element
S\in|-K_{X}|
that is a K3 surface with at worst canonical singularities. Consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow 0,

where
H\subset\Aut(S)
and
K
fixes
S
pointwise. Note that
K=\mathbb{Z}/m
for some
m\geq 1
(
cf.
Corollary
2.9
). In particular, the group
G
is of K3 type. Let
Y=X/K
be the quotient, and let
f\colon X\to Y
be the natural projection.

Lemma 11.2
.

Assume that
K
is non-trivial.
Then the action of
K
is free in codimension
1
outside
S
.
Hence, the divisorial part of the ramification of
f
is precisely
S
.

Proof.

Let
K^{\prime}
be a non-trivial subgroup of
K
.
Assume there exists a
K^{\prime}
-fixed divisor
D
such that
D\neq S
.
Then
K^{\prime}
fixes
S\cup D
pointwise.
Since
S
is ample, we have
S\cap D\neq\varnothing
. Since
S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, there exists a curve
C\subset S\cap D
. At a general point of
C
, the variety
X
is smooth, so the tangent space to
S\cup D
at some point
P\in C
is equal to the tangent space to
X
. However, this contradicts Lemma
2.6
applied to the group
K^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 11.3
.

The threefold
Y=X/K
is an
H\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Fano variety with at worst canonical singularities. Moreover,
-K_{Y}\sim mB
for some
B\in\Cl(Y)
.

Proof.

Recall that we have
K=\mathbb{Z}/m
for some
m\geq 1
. We may assume that
m>1
.
First we show that
Y
is
H\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Indeed, this follows from the fact that

\Cl(Y)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=(\Cl(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{K}

and hence

(\Cl(Y)\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{H}=(\Cl(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{G}=(\Pic(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{G}.

Put
D=f(S)
. Since
K
fixes
S
pointwise, we have
S\simeq D
. The Hurwitz formula yields

0\sim K_{X}+S=f^{*}(K_{Y}+D),

and hence
m(K_{Y}+D)\sim 0
. Note that
Y
is a Fano variety with klt singularities. By inversion of adjunction, the pair
(Y,D)
is plt. Observe that the divisor
D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.
Put
T=K_{Y}+D
. We show that
T\sim 0
. Assume otherwise. Since
mT\sim 0
, the divisor
T
is non-trivial torsion, and hence
\mathrm{H}^{0}(Y,\mathscr{O}_{Y}(T))=0
. By adjunction (
cf.

[
KM98
, Corollary 5.25]
), there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{Y}(K_{Y})\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{Y}(T)\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{D}(K_{D})\longrightarrow 0.

Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing and Serre duality give
\mathrm{H}^{1}(Y,\mathscr{O}_{Y}(K_{Y}))=0
. It follows that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(D,\mathscr{O}_{D}(K_{D}))=0
, which contradicts
K_{D}\sim 0
. Thus
K_{Y}+D\sim 0
.

We have shown that
-K_{Y}\sim D
. Since
K_{Y}+D
is Cartier and
(Y,D)
is plt, the log discrepancy
a(E,Y,D)
of every exceptional divisor
E
over
Y
is a positive integer. Therefore,

a(E,Y)=a(E,Y,D)+\operatorname{ord}_{E}(D)\geq 1,

so
Y
has canonical singularities.

We claim that there exists a Weil divisor
B
such that
mB\sim D
. Indeed, let
Y^{0}
be a smooth locus of
Y
, and let
X^{0}
be its preimage via the map
f
. We denote the corresponding map by
f^{0}\colon X^{0}\to Y^{0}
. Put
D^{0}=D\cap Y^{0}
. Then applying
[
Wav68
, Theorem 1.2]
, we see that there exists an element
B^{0}\in\Pic(Y^{0})
such that
mB^{0}\sim D^{0}
. Since
\Pic(Y^{0})=\Cl(Y^{0})=\Cl(Y)
, we see that
mB\sim D
, where
B
is the closure of
B^{0}
in
Y
.
∎

Remark 11.4
.

In the above notation, if
Y
is terminal (this holds for example if the action of
K
is free outside
S
), then by
[
Suz04
]
we have
m\leq 19
. In general, by
[
JL25
]
we have
m\leq 66
.

Example 11.5
.

We present some examples of actions on Fano varieties in weighted projective spaces of groups of K3 type that are not of product type:
In each case,
-K_{X}\sim\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)
, and

is a
G
-invariant K3 surface with at worst du Val singularities.

(1)

Let

be a hypersurface of degree
4
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}

Let

with the diagonal action of the group

(3)

Let

12. Proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
X
be a
3
-dimensional rationally connected variety, and let
G
be a finite abelian group such that
G\subset\Bir(X)
. After passing to a
G
-equivariant resolution of singularities, we may assume that
X
is smooth and projective, and that
G\subset\Aut(X)
. We run a
G
-equivariant minimal model program that terminates on a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Mori fiber space
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
. If
\dim Z>0
, then by Corollary
3.14
, we conclude that
G
is of product type. So we may assume from the start that
X=X^{\prime}
is a
G\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold and
G\subset\Aut(X)
.

Consider the linear system
|-K_{X}|
. If it is empty, we are in the last case of Theorem
1.7
. So we may assume that
|-K_{X}|
is non-empty. Since the group
G
acts on

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathscr{O}(-K_{X}))
and
G
is abelian, there exists a
G
-invariant element
S\in|-K_{X}|
. We analyze singularities of the pair
(X,S)
.
By Proposition
8.1
, if the pair
(X,S)
is not lc, then
G
is of product type. So we may assume that
(X,S)
is lc.
By Proposition
8.2
, if
(X,S)
is plt, then
G
is of K3 type, and we are in the second case of Theorem
1.7
. So we may assume that
(X,S)
is not plt.

Assume that
(X,S)
is lc but not plt and
S
is irreducible. By Proposition
8.4
, either
G
is of product type, or
S
is normal. Hence we may assume that
S
is normal. Similarly, by Proposition
8.5
, we may assume that
S
has two simple elliptic singularities
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
interchanged by
G
.
Let
H
be an index
2
subgroup of
G
that stabilizes
P_{i}
.
Propositions
8.8
and
8.9
imply that the exceptional elliptic curve
E_{i}
over
P_{i}\in S
has an
H
-orbit of length at most
7
.
We denote by
H_{E}
the image of
H
in the automorphism group of
E_{i}
.
Corollary
8.10
shows that either
\mathfrak{r}(H_{E})\leq 1
, or
H_{E}
belongs to a list of three groups.
If
\mathfrak{r}(H_{E})\leq 1
, then
G
is of product type by Corollary
8.13
.
Then, we show that
G
is of product type in the three exceptional cases. This is done in Propositions
8.15
,
8.16
and
8.17
and Corollary
8.18
.

Assume that
(X,S)
is dlt and
S
is reducible. Then by Corollary
9.4
, the dual complex of
S
is either an interval or a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(the case of a point is not possible since we assume that
S
is reducible). Applying Proposition
9.7
in the former case and Proposition
9.8
in the latter case, we conclude that
G
is of product type.

Finally, assume that
(X,S)
is lc but not dlt and
S
is reducible. Again, by Corollary
9.4
, the dual complex of
S
is either an interval or a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Applying Proposition
10.11
in the former case and Proposition
10.10
in the latter case, we conclude that
G
is of product type. Suppose that
G
is neither of product type nor of K3 type, and let
W\to T
be any
G\mathbb Q
-Mori fiber space with rationally connected total space and faithful
G
-action. Corollary
3.14
forces
T
to be a point; if
|-K_{W}|\neq\varnothing
, the preceding argument applied to

shows that

is of product or K3 type. Hence

, proving the final assertion of the theorem. The theorem is proven.
∎

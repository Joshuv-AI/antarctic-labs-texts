Inductive Freeness of Ziegler’s Canonical Multiderivations
Torsten Hoge and Gerhard Röhrle

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} be a free hyperplane arrangement. In 1989, Ziegler showed that the restriction 𝒜 ′′ {\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime} of 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} to any hyperplane endowed with the natural multiplicity κ \kappa is then a free multiarrangement ( 𝒜 ′′ , κ ) ({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa) . The aim of this paper is to prove an analogue of Ziegler’s theorem for the stronger notion of inductive freeness: if 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} is inductively free, then so is the multiarrangement ( 𝒜 ′′ , κ ) ({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa) . In a related result we derive that if a deletion 𝒜 ′ {\mathscr{A}}^{\prime} of 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} is free and the corresponding restriction 𝒜 ′′ {\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime} is inductively free, then so is ( 𝒜 ′′ , κ ) ({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa) – irrespective of the freeness of 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} . In addition, we show counterparts of the latter kind for additive and recursive freeness.

1.
Introduction

The class of free arrangements, respectively free multiarrangments,
plays a pivotal role in the theory of
hyperplane arrangements, respectively
multiarrangements.
In
[
Z89
]
, Ziegler
introduced the notion of multiarrangements and initiated the study of their
freeness.
We begin by recalling Ziegler’s
fundamental construction from
loc. cit
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement.
Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and consider the restriction

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
with respect to
H_{0}
.
Define the
canonical multiplicity

\kappa
on
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
as follows. For
Y\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
set

\kappa(Y):=|{\mathscr{A}}_{Y}|-1,

i.e.,
\kappa(Y)
is the number of hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}

lying above
Y
.
Ziegler showed that freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
implies
freeness of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
.
We also call
\kappa
the
Ziegler multiplicity
and

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
the
Ziegler restriction
of
{\mathscr{A}}
on
H_{0}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Z89
, Thm. 11]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a free arrangement with exponents

\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.
Let
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and consider the restriction

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
with respect to
H_{0}
.
Then the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is free with
exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)=\{e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

Because of the relevance of Ziegler’s multiplicity
in the theory of free arrangements,
it is natural to investigate stronger
freeness properties for
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
and
specifically to ask for an analogue of
Theorem
1.2
for
inductive freeness. This is the content of
our main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free, then so is any Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.

In
[
HR18
]
, we
initialized the study
of the implication of Theorem
1.3

and examined the multiarrangements
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
where
the underlying
class of arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}

consists of reflection arrangements of complex reflection groups.

Remark 1.4
.

While the multiset of exponents of a free simple arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is a combinatorial invariant of
{\mathscr{A}}
, i.e.,
\exp{\mathscr{A}}
only depends on the intersection lattice of
{\mathscr{A}}
,
thanks to Terao’s seminal factorization theorem, cf.
[
OT92
, Thm. 4.137]
,
this is no longer the case when we pass to multiarrangements, as Ziegler already observed in
[
Z89
, Prop. 10]
. Thus, unsurprisingly,
while inductive freeness is a combinatorial
property for simple arrangements, e.g. see
[
CH15
, Lem. 2.5]
, in contrast,
for multiarrangements it is not combinatorial, see
[
DW18
, Ex. 2.4]
.
Moreover,
despite the fact that the Ziegler multiplicity
\kappa
only depends on the intersection lattice of
{\mathscr{A}}
,
the freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is not combinatorial,
as demonstrated by the following example, which is based on the so called whirl matroid, cf.
[
DW18
]
.
Indeed, the example demonstrates
that the freeness of a Ziegler restriction is not combinatorial in a strong sense, for it neither depends on the intersection lattice of the underlying ambient arrangement nor on that of the restriction.
So the assertion of Theorem
1.3
is rather unexpected.

Example 1.5
.

We define two rational rank
4
arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
which have the
12
hyperplanes given by the following linear forms

x+5t,x+6t,x-5t,y+2t,y-4t,y-t,z+4t,z+3t,z-3t,x+z+7t,y+z-6t,t

in
{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y,z,t]
in common, and
{\mathscr{A}}
has additionally the hyperplane given by
x+y-2t
and
{\mathscr{B}}
the one given by
x-2y-2t
. So
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
only differ by one hyperplane.
The intersection lattices of
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are identical.

The Ziegler restrictions of
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
to
H_{0}=\ker(t)
are given by

Q({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)=x^{3}y^{3}z^{3}(x+y)(x+z)(y+z)

and

Q({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)=x^{3}y^{3}z^{3}(x-2y)(x+z)(y+z),

respectively. Consequently, thanks to

[
DW18
, Thm. 4.2]
,
we get that
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is free, but
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is not.
Moreover, the intersection lattices of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
and
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime}
are identical as well.
Finally, note that none of the simple arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}
,
{\mathscr{B}}
,
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
, and
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime}
is free, see
[
DW18
, Prop. 5.2]
.

Note that even if
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free, there might be
restrictions
{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
of
{\mathscr{A}}
which are themselves not even free, e.g. see
[
AMR18
, Rem. 3.6]
. Nevertheless, as asserted by Theorem
1.3
, the Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is again inductively free for
every

H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

There is also the weaker notion of
recursive freeness
, both for simple and also for multiarrangements, see
[
OT92
, Def. 4.60]
and Definition
2.17
(
[
HRS17
, Def. 2.21]
), respectively.
We show the following analogue of Theorem
1.3
in this context.

Theorem 1.6
.

If
{\mathscr{A}}
is recursively free, then so is any Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Even if
{\mathscr{A}}
itself is not inductively free, we derive the following result still guaranteeing
inductive freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
, provided the restriction
{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
is still inductively free; likewise for additive and recursive freeness. For the notion of
additive freeness
for simple and multiarrangements, see Definitions
2.3
and
3.3
, respectively.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Suppose that
{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
is free.
If
{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
is inductively free (resp. additively free, resp. recursively free), then so is
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
.

Note that one cannot drop the freeness condition on
{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
in
Theorem
1.7
; see
[
HR18
, Ex. 2.24]
for an example where

{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
is inductively free while both
{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
and

({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
are not even free.
While in
loc. cit.

both
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
are non-free,
Example
4.2
illustrates the failure of Theorem
1.7
when

{\mathscr{A}}
is still free and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
is not.
It turns out that Theorem
1.7
is a consequence of Theorem
1.3
(resp. Theorem
1.6
), see §
4
.

The
3
-arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
given by
Q({\mathscr{A}})=xyz(x+y+z)
is the smallest example for Theorem
1.7

when
{\mathscr{A}}
is non-free,
[
OT92
, Ex. 4.34]
.

The following is immediate from Theorems
1.7
and
2.1
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Suppose both
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
are free and
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}\subset\exp{\mathscr{A}}
. If
{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
is inductively free (resp. additively free, resp. recursively free),
then so is
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
.

Surprisingly, the analogue of Theorem
1.3
in the weaker setting of additive freeness turns out to be false. A particular subarrangement in a restriction of the Weyl arrangement of type
E_{7}
constructed in

[
HR21
]
which is additively free but not inductively free provides an example of this kind, see Example
4.1
.

Theorem 1.9
.

There are arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}
which are additively free such that for some
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
the Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is not additively free.

We briefly discuss some immediate consequences of the theorems above.

In
[
HR18
, Thm. 1.4]
, we gave a complete classification of
all complex reflection arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
, for
W
a complex reflection group,
so that
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is inductively free, for every
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
. In part this involved carrying out
various intricate induction tables or complicated arguments, depending on computer calculations.
With the aid of Theorem
1.3
the proof can be simplified and shorted considerably, as illustrated by the following consequence of Theorem
1.3
.
Let
W
be a Coxeter group and let
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
be the associated Coxeter arrangement.
Coxeter arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
are known to be inductively free, owing to
[
BC12
]
. Therefore,
thanks to Theorem
1.3
, we immediately recover the following
without any further case by case analysis and free of any computer computations.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
be a Coxeter arrangement. Then every Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free.

The proof of Corollary
1.10
in the case
when
W
is of type
E_{8}
in
[
HR18
]
is rather involved, entailing intricate computer calculations.

In
[
ATW08
, Thm. 5.10]
,
certain multiplicities defined on supersolvable arrangements were shown to be
inductively free.
Since supersolvable arrangements are inductively free
[
OT92
, Thm. 4.58]
, we immediately get the following consequence from Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.11
.

For
{\mathscr{A}}
supersolvable, any Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)

is inductively free.

The reflection arrangement
of the complex reflection group
G(r,p,\ell)

is supersolvable for all
r,\ell\geq 2
and
p\neq r
if
\ell\geq 3
, cf.
[
HR14
, Thm. 1.2]
. As part of the classification in
[
HR18
, Thm. 1.4]
, we showed the following, which now follows readily from Corollary
1.11
.

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(G(r,p,\ell))
for
r,\ell\geq 2
and
p\neq r
if
\ell\geq 3
. Then
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is inductively free, for every
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

The so called

arrangements of ideal type

{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
are certain natural subarrangements
of Weyl arrangements associated with
ideals in the set of positive roots of
a reduced root system.
They are known to be free due to work of Sommers and Tymoczko
[
ST06
]
and
Abe et al
[
ABC+16
]
. In particular, in
loc. cit
, the
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
were shown to be free
in a uniform fashion where the exponents are given in a combinatorial manner.
This in turn lead to the question whether all
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
do satisfy the stronger property of inductive freeness.
This was settled affirmatively in

[
R17
]
and
[
CRS19
]
.
Thus Theorem
1.3
immediately entails the following.

Corollary 1.13
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
be an ideal subarrangement of the Weyl
arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
.
Then every Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
of
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
is inductively free.

Note that in general, if
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is inductively free (resp. additively free, resp. recursively free), it need not follow that
{\mathscr{A}}
itself is inductively free (resp. additively free, resp. recursively free). That is, while there is an inductive (resp. additive, resp. recursive) chain of free subarrangements from the empty arrangement to
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
(cf. Remark
2.18
, Definition
3.3
), such a chain need not pass through the
simple arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
itself.
We illustrate this by our next example.

Example 1.14
.

Thanks to Corollary
1.13
,
for an ideal arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
, any Ziegler restriction

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{I}},\kappa)
of
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
is inductively free.
However,
there are certain ideal arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
in Weyl arrangements of type
D
which admit restrictions
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{I}}
that are not free, see
[
AMR18
, Rem. 3.6]
. It thus follows that in each of these instances the inductively free Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{I}},\kappa)
does not admit a chain of free subarrangements passing through the simple arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{I}}
, as the latter itself is not free.

Example 1.15
.

Thanks to work of Edelman and Reiner
[
ER96
, Thm. 3.2]
and Athanasiadis
[
Ath98
, Thm. 3.3]
, respectively, the cones of the extended
Catalan arrangements in type
A_{\ell}
and cones of the extended
Shi arrangements in type
A_{\ell}
are inductively free.
As a consequence of Theorem
1.3
, the multiarrangements
({\mathscr{A}}(A_{\ell}),\mu)
are inductively free, where
{\mathscr{A}}(A_{\ell})
is the reflection arrangement of type
A_{\ell}
and
\mu=c\cdot\mathbbm{1}
, for
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, is a constant multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}(A_{\ell})
. The latter fact was proved in
[
CR16
, Thm. 1.1]
in an elaborate induction argument.

We continue with some applications of Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
.

Example 1.16
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(G_{31})
be the reflection arrangement of the complex reflection group of type
G_{31}
.
Note that while
{\mathscr{A}}
is not inductively free, by
[
HR15
, Lem. 3.5]
,

{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
is inductively free for each
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
, thanks to
[
AHR14
, Lem. 4.1]
.
Since
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}\subset\exp{\mathscr{A}}
, owing to
[
OT92
, Table C.12]
, it follows from Corollary
1.8
that

({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is inductively free, for every
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
. This was first shown in
[
HR18
, Cor. 3.14]
by more complicated means.

This example is going to make another appearance in §
5.1
below in connection with the question of a converse to Theorem
1.3
.

Example 1.17
.

Orlik and Solomon defined so called intermediate arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
,
cf.
[
OT92
, §6.4]
, which
interpolate between the
reflection arrangements of the imprimitive complex reflection groups
G(r,r,\ell)
and
G(r,1,\ell)
.
They show up as restrictions of the reflection arrangement
of
G(r,r,n)
,
cf.
[
OT92
, Prop. 6.84]
.

For
r,\ell\geq 2
and
0\leq k\leq\ell
, the defining polynomial of

{\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
is given by

Q({\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r))=x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq\ell}(x_{i}^{r}-x_{j}^{r}),

so that

{\mathscr{A}}^{\ell}_{\ell}(r)={\mathscr{A}}(G(r,1,\ell))
and

{\mathscr{A}}^{0}_{\ell}(r)={\mathscr{A}}(G(r,r,\ell))
.
For
k\neq 0,\ell
, these are not reflection arrangements
themselves.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
for
1\leq k\leq\ell
.
First let
H_{0}
be a coordinate hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Then, owing to
[
OT92
, Prop. 6.82]
,
{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}\cong{\mathscr{A}}^{\ell-1}_{\ell-1}(r)={\mathscr{A}}(G(r,1,\ell-1))
. The latter is inductively free, by
[
HR15
, Thm. 1.1]
.
Since
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\cong{\mathscr{A}}^{k-1}_{\ell}(r)
is also free,
it follows from Theorem
1.7
that

({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is also inductively free.
Observe that
{\mathscr{A}}
itself is
not
inductively free, for
r,\ell\geq 3
and
0\leq k\leq\ell-3
, due to
[
AHR14
, Lem. 4.1]
.

Next suppose
\ell\geq 5
and let
H_{0}=\ker(x_{1}-x_{2})
. Then
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is no longer inductively free, according to
[
HRS17
, Prop. 4.2]
.
Nevertheless, by
[
AHR14
, Thm. 3.6(ii)]
,
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
is recursively free for
r,\ell\geq 3
and any
0\leq k\leq\ell
. It follows from Theorem
1.6
that

({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is still recursively free, for every
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

In view of Example
1.17
, with the aid of Theorems
1.3
and
1.7
, it is feasible to obtain a
classification of all instances when the Ziegler restriction is inductively free where the underlying simple arrangement is a
restriction of a complex reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
; this is carried out in

[
HRW25
]
.

2.
Recollections and Preliminaries

2.1.

Let
V={\mathbb{K}}^{\ell}

be an
\ell
-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space.
A
hyperplane arrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},V)
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in
V
.
Usually, we simply write
{\mathscr{A}}
in place of
({\mathscr{A}},V)
.
We write
|{\mathscr{A}}|
for the number of hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
The empty arrangement in
V
is denoted by
\Phi_{\ell}
.

2.2.

The
lattice

L({\mathscr{A}})
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is the set of subspaces of
V
of
the form
H_{1}\cap\dotsm\cap H_{i}
where
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{i}\}
is a subset
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
For
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
, we have two associated arrangements,
firstly

{\mathscr{A}}_{X}:=\{H\in{\mathscr{A}}\mid X\subseteq H\}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
,
the
localization of
{\mathscr{A}}
at
X
,
and secondly,
the
restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
X
,
({\mathscr{A}}^{X},X)
, where

{\mathscr{A}}^{X}:=\{X\cap H\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\setminus{\mathscr{A}}_{X}\}
.
Note that
V
belongs to
L({\mathscr{A}})

as the intersection of the empty
collection of hyperplanes and
{\mathscr{A}}^{V}={\mathscr{A}}
.
The lattice
L({\mathscr{A}})
is a partially ordered set by reverse inclusion:

X\leq Y
provided
Y\subseteq X
for
X,Y\in L({\mathscr{A}})
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\Phi_{\ell}
. Fix a member
H_{0}
in
{\mathscr{A}}
. Set
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
. Then
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
is frequently referred to as a
triple of arrangements
.

2.3.

Let
S=S(V^{*})
be the symmetric algebra of the dual space
V^{*}
of
V
.
If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
is a basis of
V^{*}
, then we identify
S
with
the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
.
Letting
S_{p}
denote the
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspace of
S

consisting of the homogeneous polynomials of degree
p
(along with
0
),

S
is naturally
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded:
S=\oplus_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}}S_{p}
, where

S_{p}=0
in case
p<0
.

Let
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
be the
S
-module of algebraic
{\mathbb{K}}
-derivations of
S
.
Using the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading on
S
,
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
becomes a graded
S
-module.
For
i=1,\ldots,\ell
,
let
D_{i}:=\partial/\partial x_{i}
be the usual derivation of
S
.
Then
D_{1},\ldots,D_{\ell}
is an
S
-basis of
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
.
We say that
\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
is

homogeneous of polynomial degree p

provided

\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}D_{i}
,
where
f_{i}
is either
0
or homogeneous of degree
p

for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
In this case we write
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta=p
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement in
V
.
Then for
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
we fix
\alpha_{H}\in V^{*}
with

H=\ker(\alpha_{H})
.
The
defining polynomial

Q({\mathscr{A}})
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is given by

Q({\mathscr{A}}):=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}\in S
.

The
module of
{\mathscr{A}}
-derivations

2.7.

A
multiarrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
consisting of a hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
and a

multiplicity
function

\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
associating
to each hyperplane

in
{\mathscr{A}}
a non-negative integer
\mu(H)
.
The

of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

is defined by

|\mu|:=|({\mathscr{A}},\mu)|=\sum_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\mu(H)
.
The
defining polynomial

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is given by

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu):=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{\mu(H)},

a polynomial of degree
|\mu|
in
S
, where
\alpha_{H}\in V^{*}
such that
H=\ker\alpha_{H}
.

For a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, the underlying
arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is sometimes called the associated

simple
arrangement, and so
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
itself is
simple if and only if
\mu(H)=1
for each
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
In the sequel, we denote by
\mathbbm{1}
the simple multiplicity on a given arrangement.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be a multiarrangement in
V
and let

X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
. The

localization of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
at
X
is defined to be
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},\mu_{X})
,
where
\mu_{X}=\mu|_{{\mathscr{A}}_{X}}
.

2.8.

Following Ziegler
[

]
,
we extend the notion of freeness to multiarrangements as follows.
Let

such that

for each

.
The
module of
{\mathscr{A}}
-derivations
of

is

We say that
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is

if

is a free
S
-module,

[

As in the case of simple arrangements,

Here the notation

indicates equality

2.10.

In our following rather general result, we consider an arbitrary chain of free subarrangements of a free arrangement. For a restriction to a hyperplane stemming from the smallest of these free subarrangements
we then investigate the resulting chain of multiarrangements where we endow each restriction with the canonical multiplicity.
It turns out that
the Euler multiplicity at each step for the associated restricted multiarrangement is just itself again another Ziegler multiplicity. This result turns out to be extremely useful in our sequel in determining inductively free Ziegler restrictions.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an
\ell
-arrangement and let
H_{1}
,…,
H_{n}
be distinct hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Define
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}:={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\}
, and
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}:={\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}\cup\{H_{i}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Suppose that
{\mathscr{A}}_{0},\ldots,{\mathscr{A}}_{n}={\mathscr{A}}
are all free. Then for a fixed
H\in{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
, we have the following:

(1)

({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H},\kappa_{i})
is free for
i=0,\ldots,n
, where
\kappa_{i}
is the Ziegler multiplicity of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H}
.

(2)

Consider the triple
({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H},\kappa_{i})
,
({\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}^{H},\kappa_{i-1})
, and
(({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H})^{H\cap H_{i}},\kappa_{i}^{*})
. Then the Euler multiplicity
\kappa_{i}^{*}
is just the
Ziegler multiplicity
\kappa
of the restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H_{i}}
to
H\cap H_{i}
.

Proof.

(1). This is immedidate from Theorem
1.2
.

(2). Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Note that
({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H})^{H\cap H_{i}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H_{i}})^{H\cap H_{i}}
.
Let
\kappa
be the Ziegler multiplicity of the restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H_{i}}
with respect to
H\cap H_{i}
.
We need to show that
\kappa_{i}^{\ast}=\kappa
.
Therefore, for a fixed
X\in({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H})^{H\cap H_{i}}
, there are
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\exp\left(({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H})_{X},(\kappa_{i})_{X}\right)=\{a,b,0,\ldots,0\}

and

\exp\left(({\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}^{H})_{X},(\kappa_{i-1})_{X}\right)=\{a,b-1,0,\ldots,0\}.

By Definition
2.6
, we have
\kappa_{i}^{\ast}(X)=a
. Since
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
is free, so is
({\mathscr{A}}_{i})_{X}
, and since
({\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H})_{X}=(({\mathscr{A}}_{i})_{X})^{H}
, it follows from Theorem
1.2
that

\exp(({\mathscr{A}}_{i})_{X})=\{1,a,b,0,\ldots,0\}

and with the same argument for
{\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}
it follows that
({\mathscr{A}}_{i-1})_{X}
is free with

\exp(({\mathscr{A}}_{i-1})_{X})=\{1,a,b-1,0,\ldots,0\}.

Hence, by Theorem
2.1
,
({\mathscr{A}}_{i})_{X}^{H_{i}}
is free with
\exp\left(({\mathscr{A}}_{i})_{X}^{H_{i}}\right)=\{1,a,0,\ldots,0\}
and so thanks to Theorem
1.2
, its multi-restriction to
H\cap H_{i}
is free with

\exp\left(\left(({\mathscr{A}}_{i})_{X}^{H_{i}}\right)^{H\cap H_{i}},\kappa_{X}\right)=\{a,0,\ldots,0\}
where
\kappa_{X}
is the Ziegler multiplicity. Thus,
\kappa_{X}(X)=a
, since
\left(({\mathscr{A}}_{i})_{X}^{H_{i}}\right)^{H\cap H_{i}}=\{X\}
. Consequently,
\kappa(X)=\kappa_{X}(X)=a
, as desired.
∎

Remarks 2.13
.

3.1.

Several of the results in this section are taken from
[

.
We begin with recalling a natural partial order on the
set of multiplicities for a simple arrangement.

Definition 3.1
.

For multiplicities
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
on a fixed arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
, define

\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}
provided
\mu_{1}(H)\leq\mu_{2}(H)
for every
H
in
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a free arrangement.
Suppose there is a free multiplicity
\mu>\mathbbm{1}
on
{\mathscr{A}}

such that
there is a sequence of free multiplicities
\mu_{i}
on
{\mathscr{A}}
satisfying

\mu_{i}<\mu_{i+1}
and
|\mu_{i+1}|=|\mu_{i}|+1
,
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, where
\mu_{1}:={\mathbbm{1}}
and
\mu_{n}:=\mu
.
Then we say that the sequence of multiarrangements

({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i})
is a
free filtration
of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
or
simply that the sequence

\mu_{i}
is a
filtration
of free multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}
.

It is natural to consider the
generalization of Definition
2.3
for
multiarrangements.

Definition 3.3
.

The multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is said to be

additively free
if there is a free filtration of multiplicities
\mu_{i}
on
{\mathscr{A}}
,

\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}<\cdots<\mu_{n}=\mu,

i.e., where each
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i})
is free with
|\mu_{i}|=i
.
Denote this class by
{\mathcal{AFM}}
. In particular,
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{0})=\Phi_{\ell}
.
For
\mu\equiv\mathbbm{1}
, this
specializes to Definition
2.3
.

Remarks 3.4
.

(i). It is obvious from the definitions that if
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is inductively free, it is
additively free. The converse is false already for simple arrangements, see
[
HR21
]
.

(ii). If
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is additively free, it need not be the case that
{\mathscr{A}}
itself is additively free, see Example
1.14
.

For an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
, we choose
\alpha_{H}\in S
so that
H=\ker\alpha_{H}
, for each
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
For a multiplicity
\mu
on
{\mathscr{A}}
, we associate the following canonical derivation
in
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
,

(3.5)

\theta_{\mu}:=\left(\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{\mu(H)-1}\right)\theta_{E},

where

\theta_{E}
is the Euler derivation in
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
,

[
OT92
, Def. 4.7]
.
We consider the case when
\theta_{\mu}

belongs to a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free and
\theta_{\mu}

is part of a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
. Then
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is free for every multiplicity
\nu
with
\mathbbm{1}\leq\nu\leq\mu
. Moreover, if
\exp({\mathscr{A}})=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
, then
\exp({\mathscr{A}},\nu)=\{1+|\nu|-|{\mathscr{A}}|,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
for each such
\nu
.

Proof.

Suppose
\{\theta_{\mu},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
is a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
Then the first statement is immediate by
Theorem
2.5
with
\theta_{\mu}
replaced by
\theta_{\nu}
. Moreover, if
\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
are homogeneous, the second statement follows.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Suppose
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free and that
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{\mu}=\min\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
. Then
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is free for every multiplicity
\nu
with
\mathbbm{1}\leq\nu\leq\mu
, and
\exp({\mathscr{A}},\nu)=\{1+|\nu|-|{\mathscr{A}}|,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
, where
\exp({\mathscr{A}})=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{\mu}=\min\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
,
\theta_{\mu}
can be chosen as part of a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
. The result thus follows from Lemma
3.6
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free and there is an
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
with
\mu(H_{0})=1
such that
the corresponding deletion
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is also free.
Suppose one of the following
holds:

(i)

\min\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=|\mu|-|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}|
.

(ii)

\theta_{\mu}
is part of a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
and
|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}|\not=|\mu^{\ast}|
.

Then
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\nu)
is free for every multiplicity
\nu
with
\mathbbm{1}\leq\nu\leq\mu^{\prime}
. In particular,
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
is free.

Proof.

According to Theorem
2.7
there exists a basis
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
such that

B:=\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{0}\theta_{k},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}

is a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
We claim that conditions (i) and (ii) ensure that we may pick
\theta_{\mu}
as one of the
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k-1},\theta_{k+1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
.
Assuming the claim,
\theta_{\mu}
is also part of a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
and thus the result follows from Lemma
3.6
applied to
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
.

If condition (i) is satisfied then
\theta_{\mu}
is a derivation of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
of minimal degree, by Corollary
3.7
.
Since
\theta_{\mu}
is not divisible by
\alpha_{0}
, it can be chosen as a part of the basis
B
.

In view of Theorems
2.7
and
2.11
, condition (ii) implies that

(3.9)

\operatorname{pdeg}(\theta_{\mu})=|\mu|-|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}|\not=|\mu|-|\mu^{\ast}|=\operatorname{pdeg}(\alpha_{0}\theta_{k}).

The derivations of a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
do not depend on the picked derivations of other degrees.
Hence
\theta_{\mu}
can be chosen as a part of the basis
B
.
The claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Equation (
3.9
) shows that the second part in condition (ii) of Lemma
3.8
is necessary, since else
\alpha_{0}\theta_{k}
might be an
S
-linear combination of
\theta_{\mu}
and a derivation of lower degree.

We illustrate Lemma
3.8
with two easy examples. In both cases, both conditions of the lemma fail, though the final statement in the lemma still holds in the first instance.

Example 3.11
.

Define
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
by

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=x^{2}y^{2}z(x+y)(y+z).

Then
\theta_{1}:=x^{2}D_{x}-y^{2}D_{y}+z^{2}D_{z}
,
\theta_{2}:=z(y+z)D_{z}
, and
\theta_{\mu}=xy\theta_{E}
form a basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
Let
H_{0}:=\ker(x+y)
. A basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
as in Theorem
2.7
is given by
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
, and
\theta_{3}:=(x+y)(y^{2}D_{y}-z^{2}D_{z})
.
Hence

, by Theorem
2.11
.
Also,
\min\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=2<|\mu|-|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}|=7-4=3
.
Thus neither condition in Lemma
3.8
is satisfied. Nevertheless, one can check that the statement of the conclusion of the lemma still holds:

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\nu)
is free for every multiplicity
\nu
with
\mathbbm{1}\leq\nu\leq\mu^{\prime}
.

In our second example the conclusion of Lemma
3.8
fails since

is not free.

Example 3.12
.

Define
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
by

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=x^{2}y^{2}z^{2}(x+y)(x-y)(x-z)(y+z).

Then

Let

of

is given by
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
, and
\theta_{3}:=(x+y)(x^{2}zD_{x}+y^{2}(y+z-x)D_{y}+xz^{2}D_{z})
.
Hence
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})=\{3,3,3\}
and so

.
Also,

.

Lemma 3.13

(
[
HR18
, Lem. 3.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an inductively free arrangement.
Suppose there is a free multiplicity
\mu>\mathbbm{1}
on
{\mathscr{A}}

along with a free filtration

\mathbbm{1}=:\mu_{1}<\ldots<\mu_{n}:=\mu

of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
If each restriction
along the free filtration is inductively free, then
so is
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.

In our next result we present a mild condition on a free multiplicity
\mu

of a free arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
which implies that every intermediate
multiplicity
\mathbbm{1}<\nu<\mu
is also free.

Lemma 3.14

(
[
HR18
, Lem. 3.4]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a free arrangement with exponents
1\leq e_{2}\leq\ldots\leq e_{\ell}
.
Assume that there is a free multiplicity
\mu>\mathbbm{1}
on
{\mathscr{A}}
with

\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=\{e,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
, where

e=1+|\mu|-|{\mathscr{A}}|
.
Suppose that
|\mu|-|{\mathscr{A}}|\geq e_{\ell}
.
Let
\nu
be a multiplicity satisfying
\mathbbm{1}<\nu<\mu
.
Then
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is free with

\exp({\mathscr{A}},\nu)=\{\tilde{e},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
, where

\tilde{e}=1+|\nu|-|{\mathscr{A}}|
.

We record an important consequence of Lemma
3.14

and Theorem
2.11
which shows that
the multisets of exponents of the restrictions
along a free filtration, as in Lemma
3.14
,
are constant and do not depend on
the Euler multiplicities
\mu^{*}_{i}
.

Corollary 3.15

(
[
HR18
, Cor. 3.8]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
and
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be as in Lemma
3.14
.
Let
\mathbbm{1}=:\mu_{1}<\ldots<\mu_{n}:=\mu
be
a free filtration of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
Then for each restriction along the chain, we have
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*}_{i})=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
,
where
\kappa
is Ziegler’s canonical multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
.
In particular, each such restriction along the filtration is free with

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*}_{i})=\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)=\{e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
,
where
\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

The next lemma is a crucial ingredient in our proofs in §
4
of Theorems

to
1.9
.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an
\ell
-arrangement. Suppose
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
are free.
Let
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
be the Ziegler restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
H_{0}
. Then
D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu)
is free for every multiplicity

\mathbbm{1}\leq\mu\leq\kappa
with
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu)=\{1+|\mu|-|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}|,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell-1}\}
, where
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell-1}\}
.

Moreover, let
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu_{i})
be a free filtration of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
, as in Definition
3.2
, and let
H_{i}
be the hyperplane of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
involved in the
i
-th addition step in this filtration.
Then the Euler restriction
\mu_{i}^{*}
is the Ziegler restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
to
H_{i}\cap H_{0}
.

Proof.

Owing to
[
Abe24
, Thm. 1.13]
,
the restriction map
\rho\colon D({\mathscr{A}})\rightarrow D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
from (
2.9
)
is surjective, since
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
is free.
Let
\alpha_{0}\in V^{*}
with
H_{0}=\ker\alpha_{0}
and let

D_{H_{0}}({\mathscr{A}}):=\{\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}})\mid\theta(\alpha_{0})=0\}

be the annihilator of
H_{0}
in
D({\mathscr{A}})
which is a
graded
S
-submodule of
D({\mathscr{A}})
.
Then, since

D({\mathscr{A}})=S\theta_{E}\oplus D_{H_{0}}({\mathscr{A}}),

as
S
-modules (cf.
[
OT92
, Prop. 4.27]
),

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is free, and
\rho
is surjective,
we may pick an
\overline{S}
-basis of homogeneous derivations
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell-1}
of
D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
such
that
\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell-1}\in\rho(D_{H_{0}}({\mathscr{A}}))
.
We observe that the restriction of
\rho
to
D_{H_{0}}({\mathscr{A}})
affords a restriction map

\rho|_{D_{H_{0}}({\mathscr{A}})}:D_{H_{0}}({\mathscr{A}})\to D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa),

[
Z89
]
; see also
[
Y14
, Thm. 1.34(i)]
.

Hence
\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell-1}\in D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
. Let
H=\ker\alpha_{H}
for
H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
and recall from (
3.5
) that
\theta_{\kappa}:=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{H}^{\kappa(H)-1}\cdot\theta_{E}\in D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
.

Since the chosen basis elements
\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell-1}
of
D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
are independent over
\overline{S}
, it follows that also

\theta_{\kappa},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell-1}\in D({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)

are independent over
\overline{S}
and thus

\det M(\theta_{\kappa},\ldots,\ldots\theta_{\ell-1})=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{H}^{\kappa(H)-1}\det M(\theta_{E},\ldots,\theta_{\ell-1})\ \dot{=}\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{H}^{\kappa(H)}

is a non-zero scalar multiple of
Q({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
.
Hence
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is free, thanks to Theorem
2.5
.

Likewise and more generally,
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu)
is free for every multiplicity

; explicitly,

(3.17)

\left\{\theta_{\mu},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{\ell-1}\right\}

is an
\overline{S}
-basis of

The fact that the corresponding Euler multiplicities

in a free filtration of

are Ziegler multiplicities on

follows from a concentrated multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
; see the proof of Corollary
3.15
in

[
HR18
, Cor. 3.8]
.

Finally, the statement about the exponents of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu)
follows readily from the proof above and (
3.17
).
∎

Remark 3.18
.

As already pointed out in Remark
2.13
(i), in general the Euler multiplicity in connection with the restriction of a multiarrangement is not combinatorial, cf.
[

, Ex. 4.2]
. In contrast, a canonical multiplicity in a restriction of a simple arrangement is combinatorial, by definition.
Again, as in the case of Lemma

(2),
likewise in the situations of Lemma

and Corollary

,
the Euler multiplicities

are always combinatorial.

Proofs of Theorems

—
1.9

4.1.

Armed with the results from the previous sections, we address the main theorems from the introduction starting with the first three of these.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We argue by induction on
\lvert{\mathscr{A}}\rvert
.
For
\lvert{\mathscr{A}}\rvert\leq 3
the assertion is true since all restrictions are of rank at most
2
and all multiplicities on such are inductively free,
[
Z89
]
.
Now suppose
\lvert{\mathscr{A}}\rvert>3
. Since
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free, there is an
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
such
that both
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}:={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H\}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}:={\mathscr{A}}^{H}
are inductively free. We consider two cases.

Suppose
H=H_{0}
. It then follows from Lemma
3.16
that there is a sequence of
free multiplicities from the inductively free (multi-)arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mathbbm{1})
to
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)

and each occurring Euler multiplicity in the sequence is a Ziegler multiplicity of a restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. Now each of the latter is inductively free by induction, since
\lvert{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\rvert<\lvert{\mathscr{A}}\rvert
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is inductively free. Finally,
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is inductively free, thanks to Lemma
3.13
.

Suppose that
H\neq H_{0}
. Now we use Lemma
2.12
and consider
the triple
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
,
\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime})^{H_{0}},\kappa^{\prime}\right)
, and
\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{*}\right)
. Then the Euler multiplicity
\kappa^{*}
is just the
Ziegler multiplicity, say
\kappa^{\prime\prime}
, of the restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
to
H\cap H_{0}
. Therefore, as

\lvert{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\rvert<\lvert{\mathscr{A}}\rvert
and
\lvert{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\rvert<\lvert{\mathscr{A}}\rvert
and both

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
are inductively free, it follows by induction that both
\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime})^{H_{0}},\kappa^{\prime}\right)
and
\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{*}\right)=\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{\prime\prime}\right)
are inductively free. It follows from
Theorem
2.7

that the exponents of
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
and
\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime})^{H_{0}},\kappa^{\prime}\right)

differ in precisely one entry by
1
.
So that, by Theorem
2.11
,

\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{*}\right)
is free and the exponents satisfy

\exp\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{*}\right)\subseteq\exp\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime})^{H_{0}},\kappa^{\prime}\right)
.
It thus follows from Definition
2.15
that also
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is inductively free.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}},V)
. We argue by induction on
\dim V
.
If
\dim V\leq 3
, then the assertion holds, since all restrictions are of rank at most
2
and all multiplicities on such are inductively free and so in particular, are recursively free.

Now suppose
\dim V>3
and that the statement holds for recursively free arrangements in smaller dimensions.

Since
{\mathscr{A}}
is recursively free, it admits a recursive chain, as in Remark
2.18
.
We argue further by induction on the length of such a recursive chain. Obviously, if the length of such a chain is at most three, then
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free, and then so is
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
, by Theorem
1.3
, and so
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is recursively free.

Now fix a recursive chain for
{\mathscr{A}}
and assume that its length is at least four and that the result holds for recursively free arrangements admitting a shorter recursive chain than the fixed one for
{\mathscr{A}}
. We consider two cases.

Suppose
H_{0}
is the last hyperplane in the fixed chain for
{\mathscr{A}}
. It follows from Lemma
3.16
that

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu)
is free for every multiplicity
\mu
satisfying
\mathbbm{1}\leq\mu\leq\kappa
and furthermore, every corresponding Euler multiplicity
\mu^{*}
is a Ziegler multiplicity
of a restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. Since
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mathbbm{1})
is recursively free and
\dim H_{0}<\dim V
, it follows by induction on dimension that these Ziegler restrictions are recursively free.
Fix a chain of free multiplicities
\mathbbm{1}\leq\mu_{i}\leq\kappa
, as in Definition
3.2
. Arguing by induction on
i
, we have that

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu_{i}^{\prime})
is recursively free. It follows from Theorems
2.7
and
2.10
that the exponents of the corresponding restriction form a subset of
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu_{i}^{\prime})
. Thus

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu_{i})
is recursively free, by Definition
2.17
.
By induction on
i
, we get that
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is recursively free, as desired.
(Note that the argument shows that indeed
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu)
is recursively free for every multiplicity
\mu
satisfying
\mathbbm{1}\leq\mu\leq\kappa
.)

Let
H\not=H_{0}
be the last hyperplane in our recursive chain for
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Let
\kappa_{1}
be the Ziegler multiplicity of
{\mathscr{B}}:={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H\}
restricted to
H_{0}
.
By Lemma
2.12
, both
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
and
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa_{1})
are free and the multiplicities
\kappa
and
\kappa_{1}

differ only in one hyperplane, namely
\kappa(H\cap H_{0})=\kappa_{1}(H\cap H_{0})+1
. By induction on the length of the recursive chain, one of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
or
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa_{1})

is recursively free (depending on whether
H
has been deleted or added in the last step in the chain, respectively). Hence we only have to check that the corresponding Euler restriction

\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{*}\right)
is recursively free. This in turn holds by Lemma
2.12
,
since this is the Ziegler restriction, say
\kappa^{\prime\prime}
on
({\mathscr{A}}^{H})^{H\cap H_{0}}
. Since

\dim H<\dim V
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{H}

is recursively free,
\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{*}\right)=\left(({\mathscr{A}}^{H})^{H\cap H_{0}},\kappa^{\prime\prime}\right)
is recursively free, by induction on dimension. ∎

We obtain Theorem
1.7
as a consequence of Theorem
1.3
(resp. Theorem
1.6
)
and Lemmas
3.13
and
3.16
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
. By Lemma
3.16
,
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu)
is free for each
\mathbbm{1}\leq\mu\leq\kappa
. Therefore,

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is additively free whenever
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is so.

For the other two properties we have to check that the corresponding
Euler restriction corresponding to the multiplicity
\mu^{*}
has also the same property.
First suppose
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is inductively free. It follows from Lemma
3.16
that there is a sequence of
free multiplicities from the inductively free (multi-)arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mathbbm{1})
to
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)

and each occurring Euler multiplicity in the sequence is a Ziegler multiplicity of a restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. It thus follows from Theorem
1.3
, applied to
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
,
that each of the latter is inductively free.
Finally,
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is inductively free, thanks to Lemma
3.13
.

The argument in the previous paragraph also applies in case when
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is recursively free where in place of Lemma

we require an analogue for recursive freeness and Theorem

is used in place of Theorem

.
∎

4.2.

Theorem
1.9
is a consequence of the following example of an additively free arrangement which admits a Ziegler restriction that is not additively free.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the rank

{\mathscr{D}}
over the reals whose defining polynomial is the product of the

\displaystyle Q({\mathscr{D}})=\

\displaystyle x_{2}(x_{1}+x_{3}-x_{5})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+2x_{3}+x_{4}-x_{5})

\displaystyle x_{5}(x_{1}+x_{3})(x_{2}+x_{5})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+2x_{3}+x_{4})(2x_{1}+x_{3}-x_{5})

\displaystyle(2x_{1}+2x_{2}+2x_{3}+x_{4})(x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4})(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4})

\displaystyle(x_{3}+x_{4})(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})x_{1}(x_{1}+x_{3}+x_{4})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}-x_{5})

\displaystyle(x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5})(x_{1}-x_{5})(x_{1}-x_{4}-x_{5})x_{4}.

This arrangement occurs naturally as a subarrangement of a particular rank
5
restriction of the Weyl arrangement of type
E_{7}
, see
[
HR21
, §3.3]
.
It has a remarkable rare property: it is additively free but not inductively free.
A free filtration of
{\mathscr{D}}

is given by the order of the hyperplanes as they appear above in
Q({\mathscr{D}})

ending in
\ker(x_{4})
, cf.
[
HR21
, Table 4]
.
We aim to show that the Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{D}}^{\ker(x_{4})},\kappa)
is not additively free, thus proving Theorem
1.9
.

Abbreviate
{\mathscr{B}}:={\mathscr{D}}^{\ker(x_{4})}
.
By
[
HR21
, §3.3]
,
{\mathscr{D}}
is additively free with
\exp({\mathscr{D}})=\{1,5,5,5,5\}
,
{\mathscr{B}}
is free with
\exp({\mathscr{B}})=\{1,5,5,5\}
, but
{\mathscr{B}}
is not additively free.
One easily checks from
Q({\mathscr{D}})
above that

{\mathscr{B}}
consists of
16
hyperplanes and that
({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)
is given by

\displaystyle Q({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)=

\displaystyle\ x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{5}(x_{1}+x_{3})^{2}(x_{1}-x_{5})^{2}(x_{2}+x_{3})(x_{2}+x_{5})(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})^{3}\cdot

\displaystyle\ (x_{1}+x_{3}-x_{5})(2x_{1}+x_{3}-x_{5})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+2x_{3})\cdot

\displaystyle\ (x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{5})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}-x_{5})(2x_{1}+x_{2}+2x_{3}-x_{5}).

Since
{\mathscr{D}}
is free with with
\exp({\mathscr{D}})=\{1,5,5,5,5\}
,

({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)
is free with
\exp({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)=\{5,5,5,5\}
, by Theorem
1.2
.
Recall the derivation

\theta_{\kappa}=\left(\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{B}}}\alpha_{H}^{\kappa(H)-1}\right)\theta_{E}
in
D({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)
from (
3.5
).
Since
\min\exp({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)=5=1+|\kappa|-|{\mathscr{B}}|=\operatorname{pdeg}(\theta_{\kappa})
,
\theta_{\kappa}

is a derivation in
D({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)
of minimal degree, and so it
can be chosen as an element of an
S
-basis of
D({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)
. Thus, thanks to Corollary
3.7
,

D({\mathscr{B}},\mu)
is free for every
\mu
with
\mathbbm{1}\leq\mu\leq\kappa
and
\exp({\mathscr{B}},\mu)=\{1+|\mu|-|{\mathscr{B}}|,5,5,5\}
.

Now fix such a multiplicity
\mu
on
{\mathscr{B}}

and some
H_{0}=\ker\alpha_{0}\in{\mathscr{B}}
with
\mu(H_{0})=1
.
We claim that the deletion
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is not free (i.e. where
H_{0}
is removed from
{\mathscr{B}}
in passing from
({\mathscr{B}},\mu)
to
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
).
By way of contradiction, suppose
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is free.

Since
1+|\mu|-|{\mathscr{B}}|\leq 1+|\kappa|-|{\mathscr{B}}|=5
, we have
\min\exp({\mathscr{B}},\mu)=1+|\mu|-|{\mathscr{B}}|=|\mu|-|{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime}|
.
It follows from Lemma
3.8
(i) that

{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime}
is also free with exponents
\{1,4,5,5\}
. This however is false. For, if
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime}
were free, so would be
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime}
. But we have already observed in
[
HR18
, §3.3]
that there is no restriction
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime}
of
{\mathscr{B}}
at all which is free with exponents
\{1,5,5\}
.
As a result,
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is not free after all, as claimed. Therefore,
\mathbbm{1}
is the smallest free multiplicity on

{\mathscr{B}}
that we can reach by a sequence of free deletions from
({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)
. And since
{\mathscr{B}}
itself is not additively free, thanks to
[
HR18
, §3.3]
, neither is
({\mathscr{B}},\kappa)
.

Next, we present an instance of the failure of Theorem
1.7
, where
the ambient arrangement is still free and only the deletion is not.

Example 4.2
.

Consider the triple
({\mathscr{B}},{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime})
for the
arrangement
{\mathscr{B}}
from Example
4.1
with respect to
H_{0}=\ker(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})
. One checks that
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime}
is not free while
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime}
is inductively free with exponents
\{1,3,3\}
. Since
{\mathscr{B}}
is free with exponents
\{1,5,5,5\}
, by Example
4.1
,
({\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is free with exponents
\{5,5,5\}
, by Theorem
1.2
. However, the latter is not inductively free, because for any hyperplane in
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime}
,
the order of the Euler multiplicity
|\kappa^{\ast}|
is bounded above by
8
, and so the condition on the exponents in
Definition
2.15
(ii) is not satisfied for any member of
{\mathscr{B}}^{\prime\prime}
, as then
|\kappa^{\ast}|
would have to be
10=5+5
, cf. Theorem
2.11
.

5.
Complements

In this section we present some complements to our main developments.

5.1.
A converse to Theorem
1.3

There is an important and celebrated converse to Ziegler’s Theorem
1.2
, due to Yoshinaga; see
[
Y04
]
and
[
Y14
, Thm. 1.38]
. It is rather natural to ponder whether there might be
a converse to Theorem
1.3
analogous of Yoshinaga’s theorem:
Suppose that
{\mathscr{A}}
is an
\ell
-arrangement for
\ell\geq 4
,
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is inductively free
for some
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}

and
{\mathscr{A}}
is locally inductively free along
H_{0}
, i.e.
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
is inductively free for each
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
with
\{0\}\neq X\subset H_{0}
. Then one might hope that
{\mathscr{A}}
itself is inductively free.

The rank
4
reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(G_{31})
of the complex reflection group of type
G_{31}
is an example which dashes this hope. Owing to

[
HR18
, Thm. 1.4]
,
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is inductively free for any
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
Moreover, inspecting
[
OT92
, Table C.12]
and observing
[
OT92
, Cor. 6.28(2)]
, it follows from
[
HR14
, Thm. 1.2]
that all proper localizations
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
are supersolvable. In particular,

{\mathscr{A}}
is locally inductively free along any
H_{0}
.
Nevertheless,
{\mathscr{A}}(G_{31})
itself is not inductively free, according to

[
HR15
, Thm. 1.1]
.

5.2.
Divisional freeness

It follows from Theorem
1.9
that additive freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
does not imply inductive freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
in general.
In this section we investigate whether the weaker freeness conditions
of divisional freeness
on
{\mathscr{A}}
still entails inductive freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
.
Divisional freeness is a canonical notion weaker than inductive freeness due to Abe,
[
Abe16
]
. It is defined as follows, where
\chi({\mathscr{A}},t)
denotes the characteristic polynomial of the arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
Abe16
, Def. 1.5]
)
.

An
\ell
-arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is

divisionally free
if either
\ell\leq 2
,
{\mathscr{A}}=\Phi_{\ell}
, or
else there is a flag of subspaces
X_{i}
of rank
i
in
L({\mathscr{A}})
,

X_{0}=V\supset X_{1}\supset X_{2}\supset\cdots\supset X_{\ell-2},

so that
\chi({\mathscr{A}}^{X_{i}},t)
divides
\chi({\mathscr{A}}^{X_{i-1}},t)
, for

i=1,\ldots,\ell-2
.
Denote this class by
{\mathcal{DF}}
.

Owing to
[
Abe16
, Thm. 1.1]
, each member of
{\mathcal{DF}}
is free.
Indeed, in
[
Abe16
, Thms. 1.3 and 1.6]
, Abe observed that
{\mathcal{IF}}\subsetneq{\mathcal{DF}}
. For, the reflection arrangement of the complex reflection group of type
G_{31}
is
divisionally free but not inductively free. The latter is not even additively free, see the proof of
[
HR15
, Lem. 3.5]
.

It turns out that divisonal freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
does not imply inductive freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
, as our next example demonstrates.

Example 5.2
.

Recall the intermediate arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
from Example
1.17
.
Owing to
[
Abe16
, Thm. 5.6]
,
{\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
is divisionally free for
k\geq 1
.
It follows from

[
HRS17
, Prop. 4.2]
that
for
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}^{k}_{\ell}(r)
with
r\geq 3
,
\ell\geq 5
, and
1\leq k\leq\ell-3
, the Ziegler restriction

({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}},\kappa)
is not inductively free for
H_{0}=\ker(x_{1}-x_{2})
in
{\mathscr{A}}
.

5.3.
Concentrated multiplicities

In our final section
we study
multiplicities which are
concentrated at a single hyperplane.
These were introduced by
Abe, Terao and Wakefield,

[
ATW08
, §5]
.
While in general, a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
need not be free
for a free hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
and
an arbitrary multiplicity
\mu
,
e.g. see
[
Z89
, Ex. 14]
,
for these concentrated multiplicities freeness is
also induced from the simple arrangement.
It turns out that they are closely related to Ziegler’s
canonical multiplicity,
see Proposition
5.5
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a simple arrangement.
Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
define the

multiplicity
\delta
concentrated at
H_{0}

by

\delta(H):=\delta_{H_{0},m_{0}}(H):=\begin{cases}m_{0}&\text{ if }H=H_{0},\\
1&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

It turns out that both

{\mathscr{A}}
and
({\mathscr{A}},\delta)

inherit freeness from one another:

Theorem 5.4

(
[
HR18
, Thm. 1.7]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement.
Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
,
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and let

\delta=\delta_{H_{0},m_{0}}
be
the multiplicity concentrated at
H_{0}
,
as in Definition
5.3
.
Then

{\mathscr{A}}
is free
with exponents

\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}

if and only if

({\mathscr{A}},\delta)
is free with exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}},\delta)=\{m_{0},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

The following combines

[
ATW08
, Prop. 5.2]
, parts of its proof
and Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 5.5

(
[
HR18
, Prop. 2.14]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a free arrangement with exponents

\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.
Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
,
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and let

\delta=\delta_{H_{0},m_{0}}
be
as in Definition
5.3
.
Let
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\delta^{*})
be the restriction with respect to
H_{0}
.
Then we have

(i)

({\mathscr{A}},\delta)
is free with exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}},\delta)=\{m_{0},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
;

(ii)

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\delta^{*})=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is free with exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)=\{e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

Here is
the analogue of Theorem
1.3
in this setting which answers
[
HR18
, Ques. 1.8]
.

Corollary 5.6
.

If
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free, then so is

({\mathscr{A}},\delta)
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free, so is
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
, by Theorem
1.3
.
The result now follows from induction on
m_{0}\geq 1
,
Proposition
5.5
(ii) and a repeated
application of the addition part of Theorem
2.11
.
∎

Retrieving non-linear features from noisy quantum states
Benchi Zhao, Mingrui Jing, Lei Zhang, Xuanqiang Zhao, Yu-Ao Chen, Kun Wang, Xin Wang

Abstract
Accurately estimating high-order moments of quantum states is an elementary precondition for many crucial tasks in quantum computing, such as entanglement spectroscopy, entropy estimation, spectrum estimation, and predicting non-linear features from quantum states. But in reality, inevitable quantum noise prevents us from accessing the desired value. In this paper, we address this issue by systematically analyzing the feasibility and efficiency of extracting high-order moments from noisy states. We first show that there exists a quantum protocol capable of accomplishing this task if and only if the underlying noise channel is invertible. We then establish a method for deriving protocols that attain optimal sample complexity using quantum operations and classical post-processing only. Our protocols, in contrast to conventional ones, incur lower overheads and avoid sampling different quantum operations due to a novel technique called observable shift, making the protocols strong candidates for practical usage on current quantum devices. The proposed method also indicates the power of entangled protocols in retrieving high-order information, whereas in the existing methods, entanglement does not help. We further construct the protocol for large quantum systems to retrieve the depolarizing channels, making the proposed method scalable. Our work contributes to a deeper understanding of how quantum noise could affect high-order information extraction and provides guidance on how to tackle it.

1.

Under what conditions can we retrieve the high-order moments from noisy quantum states?

2.

For such conditions, what is the quantum protocol that achieves the optimal sampling complexity?

These two questions address the existence and efficiency of quantum protocols for retrieving high-order moment information and essential properties from noisy states, which help us to access accurate non-linear feature estimations.

In the present study, we aim to address both of these questions. For the first question, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the retrieval of high-order moments from noisy states, which states that a quantum protocol can achieve this goal if and only if the noisy channel is invertible. Regarding the second question, we propose a quantum protocol that can attain optimal sampling complexity using quantum operations and classical post-processing only. In contrast to the conventional sampling techniques, our protocol only employs one quantum operation due to avoiding quasi-probability decomposition and developing a novel technique called

In the end, numerical experiments are performed to demonstrate the effectiveness of our protocol with depolarizing noise applied on the ground state of the Fermi-Hubbard model. Our sampling results illustrate a more accurate estimation on

II
Moment recoverability

In this section, we are going to address the first question proposed in the introduction. We discover a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a high-order moment extraction protocol as shown in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 1

(Necessary and sufficient condition for existence of protocol)

Given a noisy channel
{\cal N}
, there exists a quantum protocol to extract the
k
-th moment
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{k}]
for any state
\rho
if and only if the noisy channel
{\cal N}
is invertible.

Intuitively, we can understand Theorem
1
from the following aspects.
Estimating high-order moment demands complete information about quantum channels. If a noise channel
{\cal N}
is invertible, it means information stored in quantum states is deformed, which can be carefully re-deformed back to the original information with extra resources of noisy states and sampling techniques.
However, when the loss of information is unattainable, i.e., the noise is non-invertible. Part of the information stored in the quantum state is destroyed completely, leading to an infeasible estimation problem even with extra quantum resources. An illustration of the theorem is shown in Fig.
2
.

Figure 2:
Illustration of Theorem
1
. Suppose a state
\rho
is corrupted by an invertible channel
{\cal N}
, and
H
is the moment observable, such that
\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}]=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{k}]
. The state information is deformed but can be retrieved via applying
{\cal D}
and post-processing (Top). However, for non-invertible
{\cal N}
, the high-order moment is completely destroyed and cannot be retrieved (Bottom).

In the following, we will present a sketch of proof for our main theorem. Starting with the definition of a
quantum protocol
which is usually described as a sequence of realizable quantum operations and post-processing steps used to perform a specific task in the domain of quantum information processing.
Mathematically, we say there exists a quantum protocol to retrieve the
k
-th moment from copies of a noisy state
{\cal N}(\rho)
if there exists an operation
{\cal D}
such that

\operatorname{Tr}[H{\cal D}\circ{\cal N}^{\otimes k}(\rho^{\otimes k})]=\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}],

(1)

where
H
is what we call the
moment observable
, as the usage of it is the core of extracting the high-order moment from quantum states, i.e.,
\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}]=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{k}]
. For example, in estimating the purity of single-qubit states, the moment observable
H
is just a SWAP operator correlating two qubits. It is proved in the Supplementary Material that for any order
k
, there exists such a moment observable
H
to extract the
k
-th moment information. The inspiration for our proof comes from the QPD method used to simulate Hermitian-preserving maps on quantum devices, which has enjoyed great success in a variety of tasks, such as error mitigation
Temme et al. 2017
;
Endo et al. 2018a
;
Takagi 2021
, and entanglement detection
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 2009
. We extend our allowed operation
{\cal D}
to the field covering the Hermitian-preserving maps.

If the noisy channel
{\cal N}
is invertible, then there exists the inverse operation of the noisy channel
{\cal N}^{-1}
, which is generally a Hermitian-preserving map, and
({\cal N}^{-1})^{\otimes k}
stands for a feasible solution to the high-order moment retriever.
On the other hand, by assuming a Hermitian-preserving map
{\cal D}
satisfying Eq. (
1
) and non-invertible
{\cal N}
. In the view of the Heisenberg picture, the adjoint of the maps in Eq. (
1
) satisfies:

\operatorname{Tr}[({\cal N}^{\otimes k})^{\dagger}\circ{\cal D}^{\dagger}(H)\rho^{\otimes k}]=\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}].

(2)

It has been proven in
Zhao et al. 2023
that given an observable
O
, a Hermitian-preserving map
{\cal M}
satisfies
\operatorname{Tr}[{\cal M}(\rho)O]=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho O]
for any state
\rho
if and only if it holds that
{\cal M}^{\dagger}(O)=O
. Thus, we can derive that as long as we find a Hermitian-preserving operation
{\cal D}
such that the condition

({\cal N}^{\otimes k})^{\dagger}\circ{\cal D}^{\dagger}(H)=H

(3)

is satisfied, the problem is solved. Since the effective rank of
H
is full, whose definition and proof are shown in Supplementary Material, then from the fact that
\operatorname{Rank}(B)\leq\min\left(\operatorname{Rank}(A),\operatorname{Rank}(B)\right)
, we can deduce that
{\cal N}
is invertible, contradicting to our assumption. This means there exists no quantum protocol for extracting high-order moments when the noise is non-invertible. The detailed proof is given in the Supplementary Material.

III
Observable shift method

In the previous part, we mentioned that applying the inverse operation of a noisy channel
{\cal N}^{-1}
to noisy states simultaneously to mitigate the error is one feasible solution to retrieve high-order moments. However, this channel inverse method requires exponentially many resources with respect to
k
to retrieve the
k
-th moment. Also, the implementation of inverse operation
{\cal N}^{-1}
is not quantum device friendly because it has to sample and implement different quantum channels probabilistically.

In this section, we propose a new method called
observable shift
to retrieve high-order moment information from noisy states, which requires only one quantum operation with comparable sampling complexity.

Lemma 2

(Observable shift)

Given an invertible quantum channel
{\cal N}
and an observable
O
, there exists a quantum channel
{\cal C}
, called retriever, and coefficients
t,f
such that

{\cal N}^{\dagger}\circ{\cal C}^{\dagger}(O)=\frac{1}{f}\left(O+tI\right),

(4)

where
I
is identity.

We develop this observable shift technique since the expectation of
O+tI
regarding any quantum states can be computed as
\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho]+t
during the measurement procedures. Moreover, if one wants to maintain the retrievability of
\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho^{k}]
from the noise channel
{\cal N}
with respect to any possible quantum states, then the only change that could be made to the observable is to add constant identity since such a transformation could maintain the original information of
\rho
. Therefore, the trace value can be retrieved via measurements and post-processing. For instance, when we estimate
\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho]
, where
O=I+X+Z
, By skipping the identity, often called
shifting the observable
, the value of
\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho]
can still be re-derived by post-adding a value of one to the expectation value of the
shifted observable

O^{\prime}=X+Z
, i.e.,
\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho]=1+\operatorname{Tr}[O^{\prime}\rho]
.

Besides, instead of mitigating noise states individually, our method utilizes entanglement to retrieve the information with respect to the moment observable
H
. Compared with the channel inverse method, the proposed observable shift method requires fewer quantum resources, and its implementation is easier. The proposed observable shift method leads to Proposition
3
.

Proposition 3

Given error tolerance
\delta
, the
k
-th moment information can be retrieved by sampling one quantum channel with complexity
\mathcal{O}(f_{\min}^{2}({\cal N},k)/\delta^{2})
and post-processing. The quantity
f_{\min}({\cal N},k)
is the sampling overhead defined as

\displaystyle f_{\min}({\cal N},k)=\min\Big\{f\mid

\displaystyle({\cal N}^{\otimes k})^{\dagger}\circ{\cal C}^{\dagger}(H)=\frac{1}{f}\left(H+tI\right),

\displaystyle f\in\mathbb{R}^{+},t\in\mathbb{R},{\cal C}\in{\rm CPTP}\Big\},

(5)

where
{\cal N}
is the noisy channel,
{\cal C}
is quantum channel,
t
is the shifted distance,
H
is the moment observable.

In our cases, we aim to find a Hermitian-preserving map
{\cal D}
such that Eq. (
3
) holds together with the allowance of observable shifting (
4
), i.e.,

({\cal N}^{\otimes k})^{\dagger}\circ{\cal D}^{\dagger}(H)=H^{\prime}-tI,

(6)

where
H^{\prime}=H+tI
is the shifted observable, and
t
is a real coefficient. Note that the quantum channel
{\cal N}^{\otimes k}
is a completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP) map, and the adjoint of a CPTP map is completely positive unital preserving
Khatri and Wilde 2020
, which refers
({\cal N}^{\otimes k})^{\dagger}(I)=I
. Thus we have

({\cal N}^{\otimes k})^{\dagger}({\cal D}^{\dagger}(H)+tI)=H^{\prime},

(7)

where we can consider
{\cal D}^{\dagger}(H)+tI
as a whole and denote it as
\tilde{{\cal D}}(H)
.
With proper coefficient
t
, the map
\tilde{{\cal D}}
could reduce to a completely positive map
{\cal C}
. The detailed proof is given in the Supplementary Material.

If we apply the quantum channel
{\cal C}
to a noisy state and make measurements over moment observable
H
, the expectation value will be

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}[H{\cal C}\circ{\cal N}^{\otimes k}(\rho^{\otimes k})]=\operatorname{Tr}[({\cal N}^{\otimes k})^{\dagger}\circ{\cal C}^{\dagger}(H)\rho^{\otimes k}]

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f}\operatorname{Tr}[(H+tI)\rho^{\otimes k}]=\frac{1}{f}(\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}]+t).

(9)

Obviously, the desired high-order moment is given by
\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}]=f\zeta-t
. In order to obtain the target expectation value of
\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}]
within an error
\delta
with a probability no less than
1-p
, the number of total sampling times
T
is given by Hoeffding’s inequality

T\geq f^{2}\frac{2}{\delta^{2}}\log(\frac{2}{p}).

(10)

Usually, the success probability

is fixed. Thus we consider it as a constant in this paper, and the corresponding sample complexity is
{\cal O}(f^{2}/\delta^{2})
, which only depends on error tolerance
\delta
and the
sampling overhead

{\cal C}
and shift distance

to make the sampling overhead

The optimal sampling overhead

of our method can be calculated by SDP as follows:

\displaystyle f_{\min}({\cal N},k)

\displaystyle=\min\quad f

(11a)

subject to

\displaystyle\quad J_{\tilde{{\cal C}}}\geq 0

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{Tr}_{C}[J_{\tilde{{\cal C}}_{BC}}]=fI_{B}

(11c)

\displaystyle\quad J_{{\cal F}_{AC}}\equiv\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[(J_{{\cal N}_{AB}^{\otimes k}}^{T_{B}}\otimes I_{C})(I_{A}\otimes J_{\tilde{{\cal C}}_{BC}})]

(11d)

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{Tr}_{C}[(I_{A}\otimes H_{C}^{T})J_{{\cal F}_{AC}}^{T}]=H_{A}+tI_{A}.

The

are the Choi-Jamiołkowski matrices for the completely positive trace-scaling map

is the Choi matrix of the composed map

Beyond retrieving particular non-linear features, i.e.,

IV
Protocols for particular noise channels

We have introduced the observable shift method in the previous part, next will provide the analytical protocol for retrieving the second-order moment information
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
from noisy quantum states suffering from depolarizing channel and amplitude dimpling channel, respectively.

Depolarizing channels have been extensively studied due to its simplicity and ability to represent a wide range of physical processes that can affect quantum states
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
. A quantum state that undergoes a depolarizing channel would be randomly replaced by a maximally mixed state with a certain error rate.
The single-qubit depolarizing (DE) noise
{\cal N}_{\rm DE}^{\epsilon}
has an exact form,

{\cal N}_{\rm DE}^{\epsilon}(\rho)=(1-\epsilon)\rho+\epsilon\frac{I}{2},

(13)

where
\epsilon
is the noise level, and
I
refers to the identity operator.

Given many copies of such noisy quantum states, our method derives a protocol for retrieving the second-order moment
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
using only one quantum channel and post-processing. Specifically, we have Proposition
4
.

Proposition 4

Given two copies of noisy states,
{\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}
, and error tolerance
\delta
, the second order moment
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
can be estimated by
f\operatorname{Tr}\left[H{\cal C}\left({\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}\right)\right]-t
, with optimal sample complexity
\mathcal{O}(1/(\delta^{2}(1-\epsilon)^{4})
, where
f=\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}
,
t=\frac{1-(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{2(1-\epsilon)^{2}}
. The term
\operatorname{Tr}\left[H{\cal C}\left({\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}\right)\right]
can be estimated by implementing a quantum retriever
{\cal C}
on noisy states and making measurements over moment observable
H
. Moreover, there exists an ensemble of unitary operations
\{p_{j},U_{j}\}_{j}
such that the action of the retriever
{\cal C}
can be interpreted as,

{\cal C}(\cdot)=\sum_{j=1}p_{j}U_{j}(\cdot)U_{j}^{\dagger}

(14)

We derive an explicit form of a mixed-unitary ensemble containing twelve fixed unitary
U_{j}
’s given in the Supplementary Material.

As a result, the second order moment can be retrieved from depolarized states
{\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}
by applying the unitaries
U_{j}
randomly with equal probabilities and then performing measurements with respect to the moment observable
H
. After repeating these steps for
T
rounds, where
T
is given by Eq. (
10
), and averaging the measurement results, we can obtain the estimated expectation value
\zeta=\operatorname{Tr}[H{\cal C}({\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes 2})]
. Then, the desired second-order moment is given by

\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]=\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}\zeta-\frac{1-(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{2(1-\epsilon)^{2}}.

(15)

When estimating
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
from copies of the noisy state
{\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}
, QPD-based methods incurs a sampling overhead
\frac{(1+\epsilon/2)^{2}}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}
. On the other hand, our observable shift method offers a protocol with a lower sampling overhead
\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}
, which is much lower than that of the QPD-based method.

Besides, the quantum amplitude damping (AD) channel is another important model that we are interested in, which often appears in superconducting qubits or trapped ions. This type of noise is particularly relevant for the loss of energy or the dissipation of excited states
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
, whose action results in the transition of a qubit’s excited state to its ground state, offering a more realistic representation of energy relaxation processes in quantum systems. The AD channel
{\cal N}_{\rm AD}^{\varepsilon}
is characterized by a single parameter
\varepsilon
, representing the damping rate, which has two Kraus operators:
A_{0}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}|1\rangle\!\langle 1|
and
A_{1}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\sqrt{\varepsilon}|0\rangle\!\langle 1|
, where
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Similarly, given many copies of AD-produced quantum states, the second-order information
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
can be retrieved by applying only one quantum channel and post-processing with our protocol in Proposition
5
.

Proposition 5

Given two copies of noisy states,
{\cal N}^{\varepsilon}_{\rm AD}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}
, and error tolerance
\delta
, the second order moment
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
can be estimated by
f\operatorname{Tr}\left[H{\cal C}\left({\cal N}^{\varepsilon}_{\rm AD}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}\right)\right]-t
, with optimal sample complexity
\mathcal{O}(1/(\delta^{2}(1-\varepsilon)^{4})
, where
f=\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}
,
t=-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}
. The term
\operatorname{Tr}\left[H{\cal C}\left({\cal N}^{\varepsilon}_{\rm AD}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}\right)\right]
can be estimated by implementing a quantum retriever
{\cal C}
on noisy states and making measurements. Moreover, the Choi matrix of such the retriever
{\cal C}
is

\displaystyle J_{\cal C}

\displaystyle=|00\rangle\!\langle 00|\otimes\frac{1}{6}((1+2\varepsilon)II+(1-4\varepsilon)H)

\displaystyle\quad+|\Psi^{+}\rangle\!\langle\Psi^{+}|\otimes\frac{1}{6}((1+2\varepsilon)II+(1-4\varepsilon)H)

\displaystyle\quad+|\Psi^{-}\rangle\!\langle\Psi^{-}|\otimes\frac{1}{2}(II-H)

\displaystyle\quad+|11\rangle\!\langle 11|\otimes\frac{1}{6}(II+H),

(16)

where
|\Psi^{\pm}\rangle\!\langle\Psi^{\pm}|=\frac{1}{2}(|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle)(\langle 01|\pm\langle 10|)
are Bell states.

The above retriever
{\cal C}
can be implemented based on the following measurements and post-processing. Given amplitude damping noisy states
{\cal N}^{\varepsilon}_{\rm AD}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}
, we make measurements in the basis
{\cal B}=\{|00\rangle,|\Psi^{+}\rangle,|\Psi^{-}\rangle,|11\rangle\}
.
From the Choi matrix of the retriever
{\cal C}
, which is shown in Eq. (
5
), we know that based on the obtained measurement results, the quantum system collapses to the states

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3},\sigma_{4}
correspondingly, where
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=\frac{1}{6}((1+2\varepsilon)II+(1-4\varepsilon)H)
,
\sigma_{3}=\frac{1}{2}(II-H)
and
\sigma_{4}=\frac{1}{6}(II+H)
. Each state corresponds to a fixed expectation value
\operatorname{Tr}[H\sigma_{i}]
, which can be predetermined via direct matrix calculation with fixed
H
and known
\sigma_{i}
.
The next step is to run sufficient shots of basis-
{\cal B}
measurements to determine the probability of measuring each basis state, denoted as
p_{i}
, respectively. The term
\operatorname{Tr}\left[H{\cal C}\left({\cal N}^{\varepsilon}_{\rm AD}(\rho)^{\otimes 2}\right)\right]
is then given by the estimated value
\zeta=\sum_{i=1}^{4}p_{i}\operatorname{Tr}[H\sigma_{i}]
. The desired second-order moment is obtained by

\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]=\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}\zeta+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}.

(17)

More details can be found in the Supplementary Material. The sampling overhead for QPD-based methods is
\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}
, while the overhead incurred by our method is still as low as
\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}
, saying that our method requires fewer quantum resources.

V
Generalized observable shift method

The specific protocol of the observable shift method with minimal sampling overhead is given by SDP as shown in Eq. (
11
). However, when the size of the system increases, the computer memory for solving such SDP increases exponentially. In personal computers, a 5-qubit system is the largest problem size that SDP can solve. Achieving a specific error mitigation protocol on a large system is a crucial problem.

We present a scalable approach for creating an error mitigation protocol by exploiting the observable shift method recursively. More specifically, we demonstrate the capability to design a quantum protocol for mitigating depolarizing noises on arbitrary copies of noisy qudits, as evidenced in the subsequent Proposition
6
.

Proposition 6

Given arbitrary
k
copies of noisy states
{\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes k}
, the
k
-th order moment
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{k}]
can be estimated by
f_{k}\operatorname{Tr}\left[H{\cal C}_{k}\left({\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes k}\right)\right]-t_{k}
, where the term
\operatorname{Tr}\left[H{\cal C}_{k}\left({\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)^{\otimes k}\right)\right]
can be estimated by implementing a quantum retriever

VI
Comparison with existing protocols

To extract high-order moment information from noisy states, one straightforward method is to apply an inverse operation of noisy channel
{\cal N}^{-1}
on quantum states to mitigate error, and then perform measurements over moment observable
H
, which is
\operatorname{Tr}[H\rho^{\otimes k}]=\operatorname{Tr}[H\left({\cal N}^{-1}\right)^{\otimes k}\left({\cal N}(\rho)^{\otimes k}\right)]
. However, the map
{\cal N}^{-1}
might not be a physical quantum channel
Jiang et al. 2021
, thus we cannot implement it directly on a quantum system. Fortunately, we can simulate such channel by quasi-probability decomposition, which decomposes such non-physical map into a linear combination of physical quantum channels, i.e.,
{\cal N}^{-1}=\sum_{i}c_{i}{\cal C}_{i}
, where
c_{i}
are the real coefficients and
{\cal C}_{i}
are physical quantum channels. We need to note that
c_{i}
can be negative. From the aspect of physical implementation, in the
t
-th round of total
T
times of sampling, we first sample a quantum channel
{\cal C}^{(t)}
from
\{{\cal C}_{i}\}
with probability
\{|c_{i}|/g\}
, where
g=\sum_{i}|c_{i}|
, and apply it to noisy state
{\cal C}^{(t)}\circ{\cal N}(\rho)
. Then we take measurements and get results
o^{(t)}
. After
T
rounds of sampling, we attain an estimation for the expectation value
\zeta=\frac{g}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\text{\rm sgn}(c_{i}^{(t)})o^{(t)}=\operatorname{Tr}[O\rho]
. The total sampling times
T
is also given by Hoeffding’s inequality as shown in Eq. (
10
). The optimal sampling overhead
g_{\min}({\cal N})
is given by
Jiang et al. 2021
;
Zhao et al. 2023
;
Regula et al. 2021

\displaystyle g_{\min}({\cal N})=\min\Big\{\sum_{i}|c_{i}|\mid

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{-1}=\sum_{i}c_{i}{\cal C}_{i},

\displaystyle c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},{\cal C}_{i}\in\text{CPTP}\Big\},

(21)

which can be obtained by SDP as displayed in the Supplementary Material.

When we apply the channel inverse method to retrieve the
k
-th moment, we should apply the inverse operation simultaneously on

quantum systems, the corresponding optimal sampling overhead if given by

\displaystyle g_{\min}({\cal N},k)=\min\Big\{\sum_{i}|c_{i}|\mid

\displaystyle c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},{\cal C}_{i}\in\text{CPTP}\Big\},

VIII
Conclusion and discussion

In this study, we establish that when quantum states are distorted by noises, the original moment information can still be retrieved through post-processing if and only if the noise is invertible.
Furthermore, our proposed method, called observable shift, outperforms QPD-based techniques in two aspects: (1) The proposed method requires lower quantum sampling complexity than the existing one, which implies the superiority of entangled protocols over product protocols. This contrasts with the multiplicativity of overhead observed in QPD-based methods for quantum error mitigation. (2) The observable shift method is easier to implement than the QPD-based method as it only involves a single quantum operation, which makes our method more friendly to quantum devices. We also propose the construction of a protocol to retrieve the depolarizing channel of large-size quantum systems. Our findings have implications for the dependable estimation of non-linear information in quantum systems and can influence various applications, including entanglement spectroscopy and ground-state property estimation.

For further work, one important task is to improve the scalability of the observable shift method, which makes this approach more practical. Also, investigating the approximate version of retrieving non-linear features is also interesting. We expect the observable shift technique can be incorporated into more algorithms and protocols to boost efficiency. It will be also interesting to explore other error mitigation methods
Cai et al. 2023
;
Endo et al. 2018b
;
Cai et al. 2020
;
Koczor 2021
;
Cai 2021
;
Huggins et al. 2021
to extract non-linear features from noisy quantum states.

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{Tr}\left[S_{k}\rho^{\otimes k}\right]

Lastly, the statement

The depolarizing channel is one of the most common noises in quantum computers which maps part of the quantum state into a maximally mixed state. The
1
-qubit depolarizing channel can be written in the following form:

{\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)=(1-\epsilon)\rho+\epsilon\frac{I}{2},

(76)

where
\epsilon\in[0,1]
is the noisy level and
I
is identity matrix. The retrieving protocol and retrieving overhead for such noise channel was well studied in
Zhao et al. 2023

\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^{2}]
from two-copy of depolarizing noised state
{\cal N}(\rho\otimes\rho)={\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}\otimes{\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho\otimes\rho)

\displaystyle{\cal N}(\rho\otimes\rho)

\displaystyle={\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)\otimes{\cal N}^{\epsilon}_{\rm DE}(\rho)

(77)

\displaystyle=[(1-\epsilon)\rho+\epsilon\frac{I}{2}]\otimes[(1-\epsilon)\rho+\epsilon\frac{I}{2}]

(78)

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon)^{2}\rho\otimes\rho+\frac{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}{2}I\otimes\rho+\frac{(1-\epsilon)\epsilon}{2}\rho\otimes I+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}I\otimes I.

Depolarizing noise retriever for the

Appendix

demonstrates the efficacy of the observable shift method in extracting the second moment of an
n
-qubit quantum state
\rho
subjected to a depolarizing channel
D_{\varepsilon}
. This section extends the method’s applicability, showing it can also determine the
k
-th moment of a
\rho
given
k
copies of
D_{\varepsilon}(\rho)
for
100\geq k>2
.
Our extension is based on the fact that the
k
-th moment of
\rho
is intrinsically a linear combination of
\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho^{2}\right],\ldots,\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho^{k-1}\right]
and
\operatorname{Tr}\left[D_{\varepsilon}(\rho)^{k}\right]
with a constant, as evidenced by the following binomial expansion:

\operatorname{Tr}\left[D_{\varepsilon}(\rho)^{k}\right]=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left((1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon I/d\right)^{k}\right]=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{l}\varepsilon^{k-l}}{d^{k-l}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho^{l}\right],

where

. Then the recovery map can be recursively found by reducing
\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho^{l}\right]
to an expectation value of Hamiltonian
H_{k}
under the depolarizing noise, and finally the observable shift method can be applied. Before presenting the details, we need an extra proposition to guarantee the existence of such a reduction, and the proof of which is deferred to Appendix
K.1
.

Proposition 13

Suppose
100\geq k>2
. Denote
H_{l}=\frac{1}{2}(S_{l}+S_{l}^{\dagger})
for the cyclic permutation operator
S_{l}\in{{\mathbb{C}}}^{ld\times ld}
permuting
l
subsystems. Then there exists CP maps
{\cal T}_{k}
and
\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{k}
such that

{\cal T}_{k}(H_{k})=H_{k-1}\otimes I/d,\quad\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{k}(H_{k})=-H_{k-1}\otimes I/d.

(181)

To see how this proposition works, for any
k>l\geq 2
, one can construct a CP map

{\cal R}_{l}=\left({\cal T}_{l+1}\otimes{\rm id}_{k-l-1}\right)\circ\ldots\circ\left({\cal T}_{k-2}\otimes{\rm id}_{2}\right)\circ\left({\cal T}_{k-1}\otimes{\rm id}\right)\circ\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{k}.

(182)

By Proposition
13
,

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{l}(H_{k})

\displaystyle=\left({\cal T}_{l+1}\otimes{\rm id}_{k-l-1}\right)\circ\ldots\circ\left({\cal T}_{k-2}\otimes{\rm id}_{2}\right)\circ\left({\cal T}_{k-1}\otimes{\rm id}\right)\circ\widetilde{{\cal T}}_{k}(H_{k})

(183)

\displaystyle=-\left({\cal T}_{l+1}\otimes{\rm id}_{k-l-1}\right)\circ\ldots\circ\left({\cal T}_{k-2}\otimes{\rm id}_{2}\right)\left({\cal T}_{k-1}\left(H_{k-1}\right)\otimes I/d\right)

(184)

\displaystyle=-{\cal T}_{l+1}\left(H_{k-l+1}\right)\otimes I_{k-l-1}/d^{k-l-1}=-H_{l}\otimes I_{k-l}/d^{k-l},

(185)

where the negative sign here is to preserve completely-positiveness of
{\cal C}_{k}
in the later proposition. Then for any state
\sigma
, the computation of
\operatorname{Tr}\left[\sigma^{l}\right]
is understood as an expectation value of
H_{k}
:

\operatorname{Tr}\left[\sigma^{l}\right]=\operatorname{Tr}\left[H_{l}\sigma^{\otimes l}\right]\propto-\operatorname{Tr}\left[{\cal R}_{l}(H_{k})\sigma^{\otimes k}\right]=-\operatorname{Tr}\left[H_{k}\cdot{\cal R}_{l}^{\dagger}\left(\sigma^{\otimes k}\right)\right].

(186)

We are ready to present the main conjecture in this section.

1.
Introduction

A classical topic in complex analysis is to characterize interpolating sequences for some spaces of bounded analytic functions. Let
H^{\infty}
be the space of bounded analytic functions on the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. For
X\subseteq H^{\infty}
, a sequence
\{z_{n}\}
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be an interpolating sequence for
X
if for each
bounded sequence
\{\zeta_{n}\}
of complex values, there exists a function
f\in X
such that
f(z_{n})=\zeta_{n}

for every
n
. A notable result of L. Carleson
[
11
]
stated that

\{z_{n}\}
is an interpolating sequence for
H^{\infty}
if and only if
\{z_{n}\}
is uniformly separated, namely there exists a
\delta>0
with

\inf_{m}\prod_{n\neq m}\left|\frac{z_{m}-z_{n}}{1-\overline{z_{m}}z_{n}}\right|\geq\delta.

We refer to
[
12
,
15
]
for more results related to this topic.

This paper is devoted to consider interpolating sequences for certain spaces of bounded analytic functions related to analytic Besov type spaces. Next we recall some definitions and notations.

For an arc
I
of the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, the
Carleson box
S(I)
is

S(I)=\{r\zeta\in\mathbb{D}:1-|I|<r<1,\ \zeta\in I\},

where
|I|
is the normalized arclength of
I
. For
0<s<\infty
, a nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is said to be an
s
-Carleson measure if

\sup_{I\subseteq\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|^{s}}<\infty.

For
s=1
, we get the classical Carleson measure. Also, if
\mu
is a
1
-Carleson measure, we write that
\mu
is a Carleson measure.

Recall that for
a
and
z
in
\mathbb{D}
,
\rho(a,z)=|a-z|/|1-\overline{a}z|
is the pseudo-hyperbolic metric between
a
and
z
.
A sequence
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{D}
is called separated if

\inf_{n\not=k}\rho(z_{n},z_{k})>0.

It is well known (cf.
[
18
]
) that
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{D}
is an interpolating sequence for
H^{\infty}
if and only if
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is separated and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\delta_{z_{n}}
is a Carleson measure.

Denote by
H(\mathbb{D})
the space of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.
For
0<p<\infty
and
s>1-p
, let
B_{p}(s)

be the Besov type space consisting of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{B_{p}(s)}=|f(0)|+\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(z)\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
dA
is the normalized area measure in
\mathbb{D}
. It is easy to check that when
s\leq 1-p
, the integral above is finite only if
f
is constant. If
s>1
, then
B_{p}(s)
is the Bergman space
A^{p}_{s-2}
(see
[
37
]
).
Besov type spaces
B_{p}(s)
have been studied extensively.
N. Arcozzi, D. Blasi and J. Pau
[
2
]
characterized universal interpolating sequences for
B_{p}(s)
spaces. D. Blasi and J. Pau
[
10
]
studied Hankel-type operators associated with
B_{p}(s)
spaces. See
[
13
,
30
]
for more results of
B_{p}(s)
spaces.

Denote by
M(B_{p}(s))
the class of multipliers of the space
B_{p}(s)
. Namely

M(B_{p}(s))=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):fg\in B_{p}(s)\ \ \text{for all}\ \ g\in B_{p}(s)\}.

A nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is called a Carleson measure for
B_{p}(s)
if there is a positive constant
C
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}d\mu(z)\leq C\|f\|_{B_{p}(s)}^{p}

for all
f\in B_{p}(s)
.

Next we recall the Möbius invariant function space generated by
B_{p}(s)
. For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, let

\sigma_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Namely
\sigma_{a}
is a Möbius transformation of
\mathbb{D}
interchanging the points
0
and
a
. Recall that

\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})=\{e^{i\theta}\sigma_{a}:\ \ a\in\mathbb{D}\ \text{and}\ \theta\ \ \text{is real}\}

is the group of Möbius maps of
\mathbb{D}
. For
0<p<\infty
and
0<s<\infty
, the space
F(p,p-2,s)
consists of those functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{F(p,p-2,s)}^{p}=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}dA(z)<\infty.

Namely, for
f\in F(p,p-2,s)
if and only if
|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}
is an
s
-Carleson measure.
It is also known that
F(p,p-2,s)
contains only constant functions if
p+s\leq 1
. Hence, to study

F(p,p-2,s)
spaces, we let
p+s>1
.
F(p,p-2,s)
spaces and a general family of analytic function spaces were introduced by R. Zhao
[
35
]
and further investigated in
[
29
]
.
If
p=2
and
s=1
, then
F(p,p-2,s)
is
BMOA
, the space of analytic functions whose boundary values having bounded mean oscillation on the unit circle.
If
s>1
, all nontrivial
F(p,p-2,s)
spaces are the same and equal to the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
consisting of those functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

F(p,p-2,s)
spaces are Möbius invariant
in the sense that

\|f\circ\phi-f(\phi(0))\|_{F(p,p-2,s)}=\|f\|_{F(p,p-2,s)}

for every
f\in F(p,p-2,s)
and
\phi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. Indeed,
F(p,p-2,s)
is equal to the Möbius invariant subspace generated by
B_{p}(s)
; that is,
F(p,p-2,s)
is the space of those functions

f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\sup_{\phi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})}\left\|f\circ\phi-f(\phi(0))\right\|_{B^{p}_{s}}<\infty.

See
[
3
]
for the general theory of Möbius invariant function spaces. If
p=2
, then
F(p,p-2,s)
is the well-known Möbius invariant space
\mathcal{Q}_{s}
(see
[
5
,
31
,
32
]
).

As usual, let
\delta_{a}
be the unit point-mass measure at
a\in\mathbb{D}
. N. Arcozzi, D. Blasi and J. Pau
[
2
]
gave a series of interesting results on
B_{p}(s)
and
M(B_{p}(s))
. In particular, they obtained the following
theorem.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an interpolating sequence for
M(B_{p}(s))
;

(b)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a separated sequence and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is a Carleson measure for
B_{p}(s)
.

Ch. Yuan and C. Tong
[
34
]
characterized interpolating sequences for
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
which generalizes the case of
\mathcal{Q}_{s}\cap H^{\infty}
in
[
24
]
. We recall Ch. Yuan and C. Tong’s result as follows.

Theorem B
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an interpolating sequence for
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
;

(b)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a separated sequence and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure.

In light of Theorems
A
and
B
, it is quite natural to characterize interpolating sequences for
M(B_{p}(s))
and
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
when
0<s<1
and
1-s<p\leq 1
.
In this paper, we get the corresponding result for
0<s<1
and
\max\{s,1-s\}<p\leq 1
. In fact, we observe that
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}=M(B_{p}(s))
when
0<p\leq 1
and
1-p<s\leq 1
.
For
0<s<1
and certain
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{D}
,
s
-Carleson measures
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
play an important role in our interpolating theorem. This inspires us to study them further. In Section 3, we consider these
s
-Carleson measures via an
L^{p}
description. In Section 4, we apply these
s
-Carleson measures to investigate proper inclusion relation related to the closure of
F(p,p-2,s)
in the Bloch space.

Throughout this paper, we write
a\lesssim b
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that
a\leq Cb
.
The symbol
a\thickapprox b
means that
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
.

2.
Interpolating sequence for
M(B_{p}(s))
and
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
with small exponents

Suppose
0<s<1
and
1-s<p\leq 1
. The function
\log(1-z)\in F(p,p-2,s)\backslash H^{\infty}
. From
[
27
, Proposition A]
, there exists a function in
H^{\infty}\backslash F(p,p-2,s)
.
Hence
F(p,p-2,s)\nsubseteq H^{\infty}
and
H^{\infty}\nsubseteq F(p,p-2,s)
.

The main result in this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0<s<1
,
\max\{s,1-s\}<p\leq 1
and
t>0
. Suppose
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an interpolating sequence for
M(B_{p}(s))
;

(b)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an interpolating sequence for
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
;

(c)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a separated sequence and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure;

(d)

\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a separated sequence and

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{t}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{a}z_{n}|^{s+t}}<\infty.

A function
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
is called an inner function if it is an
H^{\infty}
-function with radial limits of modulus one almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.
A sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be a Blaschke sequence if

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-|a_{k}|)<\infty.

This condition ensures the convergence of the corresponding Blaschke product
B
which is

B(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{k}|}{a_{k}}\frac{a_{k}-z}{1-\overline{a_{k}}z}.

Every Blaschke product is an inner function. A Blaschke product is said to be an interpolating Blaschke product if its zero sequence is an interpolating sequence for
H^{\infty}
.

F. Pérez-González and J. Rättyä
[
27
]
described inner functions in
F(p,p-2,s)
spaces as follows.

Theorem C
.

Let
0<s<1
and
p>\max\{s,1-s\}
. Then an inner function belongs to
F(p,p-2,s)
if and only if it is a Blaschke product associated with a sequence
\{z_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
which satisfies that

\sum_{k}(1-|z_{k}|)^{s}\delta_{z_{k}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure; that is

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z_{k})|^{2}\right)^{s}<\infty.

We also recall the following well-known description of Carleson type measures (cf.
[
9
]
).

Lemma D
.

Suppose
s>0
,
t>0
and
\mu
is a nonnegative Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Then
\mu
is an
s
-Carleson measure if and only if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{s+t}}d\mu(w)<\infty.

The following (a) and (b) of Theorem
E
are from Theorem 3.3 and Theorem 4.2 in
[
30
]
, respectively.

Theorem E
.

The following statements are true:

(a)

let
0<p<\infty
,
0\leq s<\infty
and
p+s>1
. Then
g\in M(B_{p}(s))
if and only if
g\in H^{\infty}
and
|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
B_{p}(s)
;

(b)

let
0<p\leq 1
and
1-p<s\leq 1
. Then a nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is a Carleson measure for
B_{p}(s)
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{d\mu(w)}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2}}=O((1-|z|^{2})^{s-2}).

When
0<p<\infty
and
s>1
, recall that
F(p,p-2,s)
is the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
, and
B_{p}(s)
is the Bergman space
A^{p}_{s-2}
(cf.
[
37
]
). Hence, for
0<p<\infty
and
s>1
,

M(B_{p}(s))=F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}=H^{\infty}
.

From
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
, if
0<s\leq 1
, then
M(B_{2}(s))\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{s}
.
By
[
2
, Lemma 5.1]
, if
1<p\leq 2
and
0<s<1
, then
M(B_{p}(s))\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{s}\cap H^{\infty}
.

Suppose
1<p\leq 2
and
0<s<1
. Proposition
2.2
below gives
M(B_{p}(s))\subseteq F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
. It is known that (cf.
[
33
, Remark 2]
)
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{s}\cap H^{\infty}
.
Thus we also get that
M(B_{p}(s))\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{s}\cap H^{\infty}
when
1<p\leq 2
and
0<s<1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The following statements hold:

(a)

if
0<p\leq 1
and
1-p<s\leq 1
, then

M(B_{p}(s))=F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty};

(b)

if
p>1
and
0<s\leq 1
, then

M(B_{p}(s))\subseteq F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}.

Proof.

(a) From Theorem
E
,
g\in M(B_{p}(s))
if and only if
g\in H^{\infty}
and

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|g^{\prime}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(w)}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2}}=O((1-|z|^{2})^{s-2}).

(1)

Taking
t=2-s
and
d\mu(w)=|g^{\prime}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(w)
in Lemma
D
, we see that (
1
) holds if and only if

|g^{\prime}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(w)
is an
s
-Carleson measure. In other words, (
1
) holds if and only if
g\in F(p,p-2,s)
.
Hence
M(B_{p}(s))=F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
.

(b) Let
g\in M(B_{p}(s))
. For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, set

f_{a}(z)=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s/p}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{\frac{2s}{p}}},\ \ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that
p>1
and
0<s\leq 1
. We see that (cf.
[
10
, p. 407]
)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\|f_{a}\|_{B_{p}(s)}<\infty.

Bear in mind that
|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
B_{p}(s)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{a}(z)|^{p}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(z)\lesssim\|f_{a}\|_{B_{p}(s)}^{p}

for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
. This gives

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}dA(z)<\infty.

Thus
g\in F(p,p-2,s)
. Hence
M(B_{p}(s))\subseteq F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The equivalence of (a) and (b) is from Proposition
2.2
. By Lemma
D
, (c) and (d) are also equivalent.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c). Note that
0<s<1
and
\max\{s,1-s\}<p\leq 1
. By a simple observation (cf.
[
7
, p. 207]
), we see that
F(p,p-2,s)\subseteq F(2,0,s)
.
Since
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an interpolating sequence for
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
,
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is also an interpolating sequence for
F(2,0,s)\cap H^{\infty}
.
By Theorem
B
, we get that (c) holds.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(b). Suppose
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a separated sequence and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure. Then

\sum_{z_{n}\in S(I)}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\leq|I|^{1-s}\sum_{z_{n}\in S(I)}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\lesssim|I|

for all
I\subseteq\mathbb{T}
. Hence
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\delta_{z_{n}}
is a Carleson measure. Thus
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an interpolating sequence for
H^{\infty}
.
Then

\inf_{k}\prod_{j\neq k}\rho(z_{j},z_{k})=:\delta>0.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be any bounded sequence of complex numbers. From J. Earl’s constructive proof of L. Carleson’s interpolating theorem
for
H^{\infty}
(see
[
15
]
), there exists a complex constant
C
and a Blaschke product
B
with zeros
\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that

f(z)=:C\sup_{n}\{a_{n}\}B(z)

satisfies

f(z_{n})=a_{n},\ \ n=1,2,\cdots,

and

\rho(z_{n},\zeta_{n})\leq\frac{\delta}{3},\ n=1,2,\cdots.

(2)

It suffices to prove that
B\in F(p,p-2,s)
. Because of (2), we know from
[
37
, p. 69]
and
[
37
, Lemma 4.30]
that

1-|z_{n}|\thickapprox 1-|\zeta_{n}|\thickapprox|1-\overline{z_{n}}\zeta_{n}|

(3)

for all positive integers
n
, and

|1-\overline{z_{n}}w|\thickapprox|1-\overline{\zeta_{n}}w|

(4)

for all positive integers
n
and all
w\in\mathbb{D}
. Also, the equivalence of (c) and (d) yields

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{a}z_{n}|^{2s}}<\infty.

(5)

Joining (
3
), (

) and (

), we get

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}(1-|\zeta_{n}|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{a}\zeta_{n}|^{2s}}<\infty.

Combining this with Theorem

, we obtain

. This finishes the proof.

3.
An
L^{p}
description related to
s
-Carleson measure
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
with
0<s<1

Suppose
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. For
0<s<1
, the
s
-Carleson measure
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
plays a key role in our Theorem
2.1
, which characterizes interpolating sequence for
M(B_{p}(s))
and
F(p,p-2,s)\cap H^{\infty}
for certain range of parameters
p
and
s
. In this section, we consider further this kind of
s
-Carleson measures. In fact, these
s
-Carleson measures defined by sequences
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
were used to characterize inner functions in
\mathcal{Q}_{s}
and
F(p,p-2,s)
spaces (see
[
16
,
27
]
), and also used to study solutions of second order complex differential equation having certain pre-given zeros (cf.
[
21
,
33
]
). We refer to
[
24
,
28
]
for more results associated with these
s
-Carleson measures.

Now we recall the classical case of
s=1
. Namely we let
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\delta_{z_{n}}
is a Carleson measure. From
[
22
]
, this is equivalent to say that
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a union of finitely many uniformly separated sequences in
\mathbb{D}
. By an
L^{p}
description, C. Nolder
[
25
, Theorem D]
characterized the union of finitely many uniformly separated sequences. We refer to P. Duren and A. Schuster
[
14
]
and A. Nicolau
[
23
]
for more interesting results on these sequences.

Give an
s
-Carleson measure
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
, where
0<s\leq 1
and
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is certain sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. Then it is easy to see that
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is also a Blaschke sequence.
For
s=1
, C. Nolder
[
25
, Theorem D]
obtained an
L^{p}
characterization related to these
s
-Carleson measures as follows.

Theorem F
.

Suppose
0<p<2
and
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Blaschke sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. Let
B
be the Blaschke product whose zero sequence is
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\delta_{z_{n}}
is a Carleson measure;

(b)

\sup_{\phi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{|B(\phi(z))|^{p}}dA(z)<\infty.

In this section, motivated by C. Nolder’s result above, we obtain an
L^{p}
description associated with these
s
-Carleson measures with
0<s<1
. As shown by Theorem
3.1
below, the case of
0<s<1
seems to be more delicate.

Next we recall some well-known estimates (cf.
[
37
, Lemma 3.10]
).

Lemma G
.

Suppose
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
c
is real and
t>-1
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\bar{z}w|^{2+t+c}}dA(w)\thickapprox\begin{cases}1&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \ c<0,\\
\log\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \ c=0,\\
\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{c}}&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \ c>0,\end{cases}

as
|z|\rightarrow 1^{-}
.

Now we give our result in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
0<s<1
,
\max\{s,1-s\}<p\leq 1
and
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Blaschke sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. Let
B
be the Blaschke product whose zero sequence is
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure;

(b)

\sup_{\phi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{B^{\prime}(\phi(z))}{B(\phi(z))}\right|^{p}(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{s-2}dA(z)<\infty.

Proof.

By a change of variables, (b) is equivalent to

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{B^{\prime}(z)}{B(z)}\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}dA(z)<\infty.

(6)

For the sake of comparison with Theorem
F
, we use (b) in the expressions of this theorem.

First, let (b) hold. Then (
6
) is true. Combining this with the fact that
|B(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we see that

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|B^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}dA(z)<\infty,

which yields
B\in F(p,p-2,s)
. Note that
0<s<1
and
\max\{s,1-s\}<p\leq 1
. By Theorem
C
,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure.

On the other hand, let
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
be an
s
-Carleson measure. By Lemma
D
, we get

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{a}z_{n}|^{2s}}<\infty.

(7)

Note that

\frac{B^{\prime}(z)}{B(z)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|z_{n}|^{2}-1}{(z_{n}-z)(1-\overline{z_{n}}z)}

for
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Since
0<\max\{s,1-s\}<p\leq 1
, we have

\left|\frac{B^{\prime}(z)}{B(z)}\right|^{p}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{p}}{|z_{n}-z|^{p}|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{p}}.

Hence, for each
a\in\mathbb{D}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{B^{\prime}(z)}{B(z)}\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}dA(z)

(8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{p}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}}{|z_{n}-z|^{p}|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{p}}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|a|^{2})^{s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{2p}|\sigma_{z_{n}}(z)|^{p}}dA(z).

Making a change of variables
w=\sigma_{z_{n}}(z)
in the integral in (
8
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2+s}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{2p}|\sigma_{z_{n}}(z)|^{p}}dA(z)

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)|^{2})^{p+s}}{|w|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{2}|1-\overline{a}\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)|^{2s}|1-\overline{z_{n}}\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)|^{2p}}dA(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s-p}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{p+s-2}}{|w|^{p}|1-\overline{z_{n}}w|^{2s}|1-\overline{a}\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)|^{2s}}dA(w).

For
0<p\leq 1
,
dA(w)/|w|^{p}=r^{1-p}drd\theta
, where
w=re^{i\theta}
. Thus the integral in (
9
) is convergent. In fact, from
[
37
, Lemma 4.26]
, we know

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{p+s-2}}{|w|^{p}|1-\overline{z_{n}}w|^{2s}|1-\overline{a}\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)|^{2s}}dA(w)

(10)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{p+s-2}}{|1-\overline{z_{n}}w|^{2s}|1-\overline{a}\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)|^{2s}}dA(w)

for all
z_{n}
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
. We compute that

\displaystyle 1-\overline{a}\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\overline{a}z_{n}-w(\overline{z_{n}}-\overline{a})}{1-\overline{z_{n}}w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\overline{a}z_{n})\frac{1-w\overline{\sigma_{z_{n}}(a)}}{1-\overline{z_{n}}w}.

Combining this with Lemma
G
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{p+s-2}}{|1-\overline{z_{n}}w|^{2s}|1-\overline{a}\sigma_{z_{n}}(w)|^{2s}}dA(w)

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-\overline{a}z_{n}|^{2s}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{p+s-2}}{|1-w\overline{\sigma_{z_{n}}(a)}|^{2s}}dA(w)

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|1-\overline{a}z_{n}|^{2s}},

where we use
0<\max\{s,1-s\}<p
.

Consequently, formulas (
7
), (
8
), (
9
), (
10
) and (
11
) yield

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{B^{\prime}(z)}{B(z)}\right|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{a}z_{n}|^{2s}}<\infty.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.
An application of
s
-Carleson measures
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
to
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))

In 1974, J. Anderson, J. Clunie and Ch. Pommerenke
[
1
]
posed the following problem: what is the closure in the Bloch norm of
H^{\infty}
? This problem remains open.
For a space
X
of functions analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, let
C_{\mathcal{B}}(X)
be the closure of
X
in the Bloch norm. An important result in this field is P. Jones’ characterization of
C_{B}(BMOA)
(see
[
19
]
).
Later, R. Zhao
[
36
]
described
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
when
0<s\leq 1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. R. Zhao’s characterization of
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
is independent of the parameter
p
. In this section, we consider the proper inclusion relation related to
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
via interpolating Blaschke products associated with
s
-Carleson measures
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
.

For
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\epsilon>0
, let
\Omega_{\epsilon}(f)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|f^{\prime}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})\geq\epsilon\}
. We recall R. Zhao’s characterization of
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
as follows.

Theorem H
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
0\leq t<\infty
. Suppose
f\in\mathcal{B}
. Then
f\in C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}(f)}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{t-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}dA(z)<\infty.

Based on some arguments of
s
-Carleson measures
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
, we obtain the following proper inclusion relation, which is the main result in this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then

C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))\subsetneqq\bigcap_{s<t<\infty}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t)).

Before the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we need some preliminary results. The following useful inequality is well known (cf.
[
26
, Lemma 2.5]
).

Lemma I
.

Suppose
s>-1
,
r>0
,
t>0
, and
t<s+2<r
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{r}|1-\overline{w}\zeta|^{t}}dA(w)\leq C\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+s-r}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{t}}

for all
z
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
.

We also need the following result, which characterizes interpolating Blaschke products in
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
0<s<1
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
I
is an interpolating Blaschke product with zero set
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Then
I\in C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
if and only if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure.

Proof.

First, let
I\in C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
. We adapt an argument from
[
17
, p. 1220]
(see also
[
6
]
). Since
I
is an interpolating Blaschke product, there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\inf_{n}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})|I^{\prime}(z_{n})|\geq\gamma.

Take
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\gamma}{4})
. From
[
20
, p. 681]
), there exists a positive constant
\rho
such that

(1-|z|^{2})|I^{\prime}(z)|\geq\epsilon

on every
D_{h}(z_{n},\rho)
, where

D_{h}(z_{n},\rho)
is the hyperbolic disk with center
z_{n}
and radius
\rho
. Namely,

D_{h}(z_{n},\rho)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+|\sigma_{z_{n}}(z)|}{1-|\sigma_{z_{n}}(z)|}<\rho\}.

Then

\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D_{h}(z_{n},\rho)\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{D}:(1-|z|^{2})|I^{\prime}(z)|\geq\epsilon\}.

Since
\{a_{n}\}
is uniformly separated, we can select
\rho
such that these hyperbolic disks are pairwise disjoints. Combining these with
Theorem
H
, we see that

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\ (1-|z|^{2})|I^{\prime}(z)|\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{(1-|\sigma_{w}(z)|^{2})^{s}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{D_{h}(z_{n},\rho)}\frac{(1-|\sigma_{w}(z)|^{2})^{s}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z).

By
[
37
, Proposition 4.5]
and
[
37
, Lemma 4.30]
, we know that

1-|z|\thickapprox 1-|z_{n}|\thickapprox|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|,\ \ |1-\overline{w}z|\thickapprox|1-\overline{w}z_{n}|,

for all
z\in D_{h}(z_{n},\rho)
and all
w\in\mathbb{D}
.
Therefore,

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|\sigma_{w}(z_{n})|^{2})^{s}<\infty,

which gives that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is an
s
-Carleson measure.

On the other hand, let
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
be an
s
-Carleson measure. Bear in mind that

|I^{\prime}(z)|\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-|z_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{2}},\ \ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then for every
\epsilon>0
, one gets that

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}(I)}\frac{(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}(I)}(1-|z|^{2})|I^{\prime}(z)|\frac{(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}(I)}\frac{1-|z_{n}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{2}}\frac{(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}}{1-|z|^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})}{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s-1}}{|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{2}|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}}dA(z).

Note that
0<s<1
. By Lemma
I
, we see that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s-1}}{|1-\overline{z_{n}}z|^{2}|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}}dA(z)\lesssim\frac{(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s-1}}{|1-\overline{z_{n}}a|^{2s}}.

Thus,

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}(I)}\frac{(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)\lesssim\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z_{n})|^{2})^{s}<\infty.

By Theorem
H
, one gets
I\in C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Note that for
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s_{1}\leq s_{2}<\infty
,
F(p,p-2,s_{1})\subseteq F(p,p-2,s_{2})
. Hence
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s_{1}))\subseteq C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s_{2}))
. Thus for
0<s\leq 1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))\subseteq\bigcap_{s<t<\infty}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t)).

If
s=1
, then
C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))=C_{\mathcal{B}}(BMOA)
and

\bigcap_{s<t<\infty}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t))=\mathcal{B}.

It is known from
[
19
]
that
C_{\mathcal{B}}(BMOA)\subsetneqq\mathcal{B}
. Thus

C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,1))\subsetneqq\bigcap_{1<t<\infty}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t)).

Let
0<s<1
. From Lemma 2.1 in
[
8
]
, there exists a Blaschke sequence
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{s}\delta_{z_{n}}
is not an
s
-Carleson measure and

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-|\sigma_{w}(z_{n})|^{2}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{\ln\frac{2}{1-|\sigma_{w}(z_{n})|^{2}}}\right)^{2}<\infty.

(12)

Denote by
I
the Blaschke product whose zero sequence is
\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Formula (
12
) implies that

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-|\sigma_{w}(z_{n})|^{2}\right)<\infty;

that is,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\delta_{z_{n}}
is a Carleson measure. Hence
I
is an interpolating Blaschke product. By Lemma
4.2
,

I\not\in C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s)).

(13)

If
0<s<t<1
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-|\sigma_{w}(z_{n})|^{2}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-|\sigma_{w}(z_{n})|^{2}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{\ln\frac{2}{1-|\sigma_{w}(z_{n})|^{2}}}\right)^{2}<\infty,

which means that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{t}\delta_{z_{n}}
is a
t
-Carleson measure. From Lemma
4.2
again,
I\in C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t))
for any
t\in(s,1)
. Hence

I\in\bigcap_{s<t<1}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t)).

(14)

Clearly,

\bigcap_{s<t<1}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t))=\bigcap_{s<t<\infty}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t)).

(15)

From (
13
), (
14
) and (
15
), we get

C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,s))\subsetneqq\bigcap_{s<t<\infty}C_{\mathcal{B}}(F(p,p-2,t)).

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is finished.

\square

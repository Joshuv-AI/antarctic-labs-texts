On a weak maximum principle for a class of fractional diffusive equations
Cyrille Kenne, Gisèle Mophou

Abstract
. We consider two evolution equations involving space fractional Laplace operator of order 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 . We first establish some existence and uniqueness results for the considered evolution equations. Next, we give some comparison theorems and prove that, if the data of each equation are data bounded, then the solutions are also bounded.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we denote by
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\,N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
an open bounded domain with a Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
and
\omega
an open subset of
\Omega.
For
T>0
, we set
Q:=\Omega\times(0,T)
and
\Sigma:=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,T)
. We consider the space fractional diffusion model with non-homogeneous Robin boundary condition

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle\rho_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}\rho&=&f&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho+\rho&=&g&\mbox{in}&\Sigma,\\
\rho(.,0)&=&\rho_{0}&\mbox{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

and the space fractional diffusion model with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle\rho_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}\rho&=&f&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\rho&=&0&\mbox{in}&\Sigma,\\
\rho(.,0)&=&\rho_{0}&\mbox{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
T>0
, the functions
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
f\in L^{2}(Q)
and
g\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
.
(-\Delta)^{s}
(
0<s<1
) denotes the fractional Laplace operator given formally for a suitable function
\psi
by:

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}\psi=C_{N,s}\,\mbox{P.V.}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{\psi(x)-\psi(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;dy,

where P.V denotes the principal value and
C_{N,s}
is a normalization constant depending only on
N
and
s
. We refer to Section
2
for more details.
We aim to establish the weak maximum principle results for the systems (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Recently, the fractional calculus has become increasingly considered by researchers. It has been used for many application, for instance, modeling of chemical and physical process, in engineering and finance (see e.g
[
8
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
23
]
for instance and the references therein).
With this emergence of fractional calculus, the maximum principles results have gained much attention.
Theses techniques are used to obtain more information about a solution even if we do not have its explicit expression.
They are also of a good use to prove existence and uniqueness results for partial differential equations.
They can also be used in control theory to prove the uniqueness of an optimal control, specially for bilinear systems.

The maximum principles for fractional order has been widely concerned by authors. In
[
18
]
,
Luchko formulated and established a maximum principle result for a time-fractional diffusion equation.
It was done by using an extremum principle established earlier for the Caputo-Dzherbashyan fractional derivative.
This result was used later in
[
19
]
, to prove well posedness results of a initial-boundary-value problem
for the multi-dimensional time-fractional diffusion equation. Al-Refai
et al
.
[
1
]

established maximum principles for a fractional diffusion equation involving the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative.
They employed these results to show an uniqueness result for solutions to a initial-boundary-value problem for
the nonlinear fractional diffusion equation under some standard assumptions.

We point out that in the above papers, there is no consideration of the fractional Laplace operator. But, this latter is a non local operator and has found applications in many areas see
[
7
]
, for instance. For more topics on fractional Laplace and its applications, we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
25
,
27
]
and the references therein.
Concerning maximum principle involving fractional operator, Wang
et al.

[
28
]
established maximum principles results for Hadamard fractional differential equation with fractional Laplace operator. They applied this result to a linear and nonlinear Hadamard fractional equations in order to prove the uniqueness and continuous dependence of the solution to the initial-boundary-value problem.

More recently, Barrios
et al.

[
22
]
, studied comparison results for a class of elliptic and
parabolic problems involving the fractional Laplace operator. They proved a generalization of a Hopf’s lemma in a non-local form for elliptic and parabolic problems with mixed conditions. Trong
et al.

[
26
]
, proved a strong maximum principle result for the spectral Dirichlet Laplacian. The result was obtained by using the Poincaré’s inequality in a fractional version and by means of a certain truncated function.

In this article, we study two evolution equations involving space fractional Laplace operator. After some well posedness results of our systems, we establish weak maximum principles.
The rest of the work is organized as follows. We introduce some Sobolev spaces and recall some known results on Fractional Laplace operator in Section
2
. In Section
3
, the existence and uniqueness of solutions to systems (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is established. We derive maximum principles results in Section
4
. In Section
5
, a conclusion is given.

2.
Preliminaries

We start this section by recalling the definition of the fractional Laplace operator. Given
0<s<1
, we let the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\coloneqq\left\{u:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}\;\mbox{ measurable and}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\}.

Let
w\in\mathcal{L}_{s}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
\varepsilon>0
, we set

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{\varepsilon}^{s}w(x)\coloneqq C_{N,s}\int_{\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}:\;|x-y|>\varepsilon\}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}y,\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

where
C_{N,s}
, normalization constant is given by

\displaystyle C_{N,s}\coloneqq\frac{s2^{2s}\Gamma\left(\frac{2s+N}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)}.

(2.1)

We define the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}w
as the following singular integral:

(-\Delta)^{s}w(x)\coloneqq C_{N,s}\,\mbox{P.V.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}y=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}(-\Delta)_{\varepsilon}^{s}w(x),\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(2.2)

provided that the limit exists for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We refer to
[
10
]
and the references therein for the class of functions for which the limit in (
2.2
) exists for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(
N\geq 1
) be an arbitrary open set and
0<s<1
. We define the fractional order Sobolev space

H^{s}(\Omega):=\left\{\rho\in L^{2}(\Omega):\;\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(x)-\rho(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;dxdy<\infty\right\}

and we endow it with the norm given by

\|\rho\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\rho|^{2}\;dx+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(x)-\rho(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;dxdy\right)^{1/2}.

We introduce for any
\rho\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
the
nonlocal normal derivative
\mathcal{N}_{s}
defined by

\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho(x)\coloneqq C_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho(x)-\rho(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}y,~~~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},

(2.3)

where
C_{N,s}
is the normalization constant given in (
2.1
). We have that
\mathcal{N}_{s}
maps
\rho\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
continuously into
L^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
(see
[
9
, Lemma 2.5, page 1604]
).

We have the integration by parts formula which can be found in
[
11
,
30
]
for smooth functions. Let
\rho\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
be such that
(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
. Then for every
\psi\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, the following equality

\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\psi\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x,

(2.4)

holds.

Hence, if

\rho,\,\psi\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
with
(-\Delta)^{s}\rho,\,(-\Delta)^{s}\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho,\,\mathcal{N}_{s}\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
. Then the following identity holds,

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(-\Delta)^{s}\psi\;\mathrm{d}x+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\rho\mathcal{N}_{s}\psi\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&-&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\psi\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x.\end{array}

(2.5)

It is clear that if
\rho=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
or
\psi=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, then

\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}}\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

and the integration by part formula (
2
) becomes

\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}}\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\Omega}\psi(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall\rho,\psi\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})

(2.6)

We set

\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega):=\Big\{\rho\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\;\rho=0\;\hbox{ in }\;{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega\Big\}.

Then, endowed with the norm

\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}:=\left(\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/2},

(2.7)

\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space (see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 7]
). Let
\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega):=(\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega))^{\star}
be the dual space of
\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
with respect to the pivot space
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then we have the following continuous embedding (see e.g.
[
2
]
):

\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega).

(2.8)

From now on, for any
\rho,\psi\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
, we set

\mathcal{F}(\rho,\psi):=\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

(2.9)

Therefore, the norm on
\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
given by (
2.7
) becomes

\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}=\left(\mathcal{F}(\rho,\rho)\right)^{1/2}
.

Let
(-\Delta)_{D}^{s}
be the operator defined on
L^{2}(\Omega)
by

D((-\Delta)_{D}^{s}):=\{\rho\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega):\;(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\in L^{2}(\Omega)\},\;(-\Delta)_{D}^{s}\rho:=(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\;\hbox{ in }\Omega.

(2.10)

Then,
(-\Delta)_{D}^{s}
is the realization in
L^{2}(\Omega)
of
(-\Delta)^{s}
with the zero Dirichlet exterior condition.

The following result is well-known (see e.g.
[
9
,
13
]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(-\Delta)_{D}^{s}
be the operator defined in (
2.10
).
Then,
(-\Delta)_{D}^{s}
can be also viewed as a bounded operator from
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
into
H^{-s}(\Omega)
given by

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{D}^{s}\rho,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-s}(\Omega),H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}=\mathcal{F}(\rho,\varphi),\;\;\;\rho,\varphi\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega).

(2.11)

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if
N>2s,
then there is a positive constant
C_{0}=C(N,s)>0
such that for any
\rho\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
,

\|\rho\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)},

(2.12)

where
r=\displaystyle\frac{2N}{N-2s}
(see e.g.
[
24

). Therefore,

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)},

(2.13)

because
\Omega
is an open and bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r>2
. Moreover, the following holds true,

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C_{0}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega))}.

(2.14)

Next, let
0<s<1
, we consider the space

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}\coloneqq\left\{\rho:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\hbox{ measurable such that }\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}<\infty\right\},

(2.15)

where

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}\coloneqq\displaystyle\left(\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}}\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.16)

Then proceeding exactly as for the proof of
[
11

, we have that
\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}
endowed with the norm (
2.16

Remark 2.3
.

For any
\rho
and
\psi
in
\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}
, we set

\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\psi):=\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}}\frac{(\rho(x)-\rho(y))(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

(2.17)

Then the norm on
\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}
becomes

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}\coloneqq\displaystyle\left(\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\rho)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.18)

Note also that from (
2.16
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}&\leq&\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}},\\
\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq&\|\rho\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}.\end{array}

(2.19)

Let
(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star}
the dual of
\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}.
Then

\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star}.

(2.20)

We denote by
(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
, the operator defined as follows:

D((-\Delta)^{s}_{R})=\left\{\rho\in\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}\,:\,(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\right\};\quad(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}\rho=(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\hbox{ in }\Omega.

(2.21)

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}
be the operator defined in (
2.21
). Then
(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}
can be viewed as a bounded operator from
\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}
into
(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star}
given by

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}\rho,\phi\rangle_{(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star},\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}=\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\phi)-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\phi dx\quad\forall\rho,\phi\in\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}

Proof.

From (
2.21
) and the integration by parts formula (
2
) we have

\int_{\Omega}\phi\,(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi(-\Delta)^{s}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x=\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\phi)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\phi\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\phi,\rho\in\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}.

Then using the definition of the norm of
\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}
given by (
2.16

\left|\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\phi)-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\phi dx\right|\leq\left(\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\rho)+\|\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\right)^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}

Therefore for any
\phi\in\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega},

\left|\int_{\Omega}\phi\,(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}\rho\;\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\left(\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\rho)+\|\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\right)^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}

and we deduce that

\|(-\Delta)^{s}_{R}\rho\|_{(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star}}\leq\left(\mathcal{F}_{R}(\rho,\rho)+\|\mathcal{N}_{s}\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\right)^{1/2}.

∎

3.
Existence results

In this section, we are interested in the existence and uniqueness of solutions to system (
1.1
).
To this end, we start by studying the existence and uniqueness of and auxiliary model. So,
we consider the auxiliary system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle z_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}z+z&=&\zeta&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\mathcal{N}_{s}z+z&=&\eta&\mbox{in}&\Sigma,\\
z(\cdot,0)&=&\rho_{0}&\mbox{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where

\zeta=e^{-t}f\hbox{ and }\eta=e^{-t}g.

(3.2)

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\zeta\in L^{2}((0,T);(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star}),

\eta\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{R}(\cdot,\cdot)
be defined by (
2.17
). A function

z\in W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})
is said to be a weak solution to (
3.1
), if the following equality holds:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi_{t},z\rangle_{(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star},\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}\,dt+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{R}(z,\phi)dt+\int_{Q}\,z\,\phi dx\,dt+\int_{\Sigma}z\,\phi\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\zeta,\,\phi\rangle_{(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star},\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}dt+\int_{\Sigma}\eta\,\phi\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\,\phi(\cdot,0)\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\phi\in H(Q),\end{array}

(3.3)

where

H(Q):=\left\{\xi\in W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})\hbox{ and }\xi(\cdot,T)=0\hbox{ a.e.in }\Omega\right\}.

Remark 3.2
.

Note also that if
\varphi\in H(Q)
, then
\varphi\in W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})
. Therefore, we have from (
2.24
) that
\varphi(0)
and
\varphi(T)
exist and belong to
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\zeta\in L^{2}((0,T);(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star}),

\eta\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique weak solution
z\in W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})
to (
3.1
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
In addition, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|z(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\displaystyle\left(\|\zeta\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star})}+\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}\times(0,T))}+\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/2},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|z\|_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})}\leq\displaystyle\left(\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}((0,T);(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star})}+\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}+\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(3.5)

and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|z\|_{W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})}\leq C\displaystyle\left(\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}((0,T);(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star})}+\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}+\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(3.6)

Proof.

The proof is done in three steps.

Step 1.
We proceed as in
[
17
, Page 37]
to prove the existence of solutions. To this end, we recall that the norm on
L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}_{\Omega}^{s})
is given by

\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}_{\Omega}^{s})}=\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}dt=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\varphi(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\mathcal{F}_{R}(\varphi(\cdot,t),\varphi(\cdot,t))\right)dt

and we consider the norm on
H(Q)
given by

\|\rho\|^{2}_{H(Q)}:=\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})}+\|\rho(\cdot,0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\Omega)},\,\forall\rho\in H(Q).

It is clear that for any
\rho\in H(Q),

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}_{\Omega}^{s})}\leq\|\rho\|_{H(Q)}.

This shows that embedding
H(Q)\hookrightarrow L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}_{\Omega}^{s})
is continuous .

Now, let
\varphi\in H(Q)
and consider the bilinear form
\mathcal{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined on
L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}_{\Omega}^{s})\times H(Q)
by

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{E}(z,\varphi)&:=&\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\varphi_{t},z\rangle_{(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star},\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega}}\,dt+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{R}(z,\varphi)dt+\int_{\Sigma}z\varphi\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{Q}\,z\,\varphi dx\,dt,\end{array}

(3.7)

where
\mathcal{F}_{R}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined as in (
2.17
).
Using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, the continuity of the bilinear form
\mathcal{F}_{R}
and (
2.19
),
it follows that there exists a constant
C=C(\varphi,N,s)>0
such that

\begin{array}[]{rlll}\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(z,\varphi)|&\leq&\displaystyle\|z\|_{L^{2}((0,T);\mathbb{H}_{\Omega}^{s})}\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}((0,T);(\mathbb{H}^{s}_{\Omega})^{\star})}+\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{R}(z,z)dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{R}(\varphi,\varphi)dt\right)^{1/2}\\
&+&\|z\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}+\|z\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\\

-\mathcal{G}(\varphi,\varphi^{-})\geq\|\varphi^{-}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}\geq 0.

(4.1)

Proof.

We have

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\varphi,\varphi^{-})&=&\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\displaystyle\int\int_{\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}\right)}\frac{(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))(\varphi^{-}(x)-\varphi^{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=&-\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\displaystyle\int\int_{\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}\right)}\frac{(\varphi^{-}(x)-\varphi^{-}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&&-\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\displaystyle\int\int_{\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}\right)}\frac{\varphi^{-}(x)\rho^{+}(y)+\rho^{+}(x)\varphi^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=&\displaystyle-\|\varphi^{-}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}-\displaystyle\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}\right)}\frac{\varphi^{-}(x)\varphi^{+}(y)+\varphi^{+}(x)\varphi^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{array}

Using that
\varphi^{+}(x)\varphi^{-}(y)\geq 0\;\;\hbox{for a.e. }\;\;x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},
we obtain (
4.1
).
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\rho\in W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega))
be the solution of (
1.2
) corresponding to data
f\in L^{2}(Q)
and
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\tilde{\rho}\in W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega))
be the solution of (

\tilde{\rho}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)

\rho_{0}\leq\tilde{\rho}_{0}
almost everywhere in
\Omega
and

, then
\rho\leq\tilde{\rho}
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]
. In particular, if

, then
\rho\geq 0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]
.

Proof.

Let

. Then,

is solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle z_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}z&=&\tilde{f}-f&\mbox{in}&Q,\\

z(.,0)&=&\tilde{\rho}_{0}-\rho_{0}&\mbox{in}&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

Let
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]
, we write
z(x,t)=z^{+}(x,t)-z^{-}(x,t)
, where
z^{+}(x,t)=\max(z(x,t),0)
and
z^{-}(x,t)=\max(0,-z(x,t))
. It is sufficient to show that
z^{-}(x,t)=0
for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]
. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{llllll}z^{-}z^{+}&=&0\;\;\hbox{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T],\\

z^{-}(x,0)&=&\max(0,-(\tilde{\rho}_{0}(x)-\rho_{0}(x))=0\;\;\hbox{a.e. in }\Omega,\end{array}

and
z^{-}\in W(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega))
(see e.g.
[
29
]
).
If we take the duality map between (
4.2
) and
\psi\in\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
, and use the integration by parts (
2
), we have for any

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\langle z_{t}(t),\psi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega),\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{F}(z(t),\psi)=\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{f}(t)-f(t))\psi dx.\end{array}

Taking

in this latter identity yields

\displaystyle\langle z_{t}(t),z^{-}(t)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega),\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{F}(z(t),z^{-}(t))=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{f}(t)-f(t))z^{-}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(4.3)

where
\mathcal{F}
is defined as in (
2.9

Observing that

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\langle z_{t}(t),z^{-}(t)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega),\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)}&=&\displaystyle\langle(z^{+}-z^{-})_{t}(t),z^{-}(t)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega),\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)}\\
&=&-\displaystyle\langle(z^{-})_{t}(t),z^{-}(t)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega),\mathbb{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)}\\
&=&-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|z^{-}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\end{array}

and using this latter identity, (

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|z^{-}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\mathcal{F}(z(t),z^{-}(t))=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{f}(t)-f(t))z^{-}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{array}

Using (
4.1
) and the fact that

we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|z^{-}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 0

(4.4)

and it follows from the Gronwall’s Lemma that

because

a.e. in

.
Hence

and since

almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]

almost everywhere in

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

5.
Conclusion

In this article, we established existence results for a class of linear parabolic equations involving the fractional Laplace operator of order
s
(
0<s<1
). We proved the positiveness of the solutions under the assumption that the data are positive and established weak maximum principle for these equations. We also proved that the solutions of the equations considered in the paper are bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T])
if the initial condition is bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and the source term is bounded in

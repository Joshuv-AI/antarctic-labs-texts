Mapping spaces in homotopy coherent nerves
Fabian Hebestreit, Achim Krause

Abstract
. We give a direct proof that middle mapping spaces in coherent nerves of Kan enriched categories have the same homotopy type as the original mapping spaces.



1.
Introduction

For a quasi-category
\mathcal{C}
, there are three common definitions of morphism complexes, often termed left, right and middle. For morphisms from
x
to
y
they can be defined as the fibres of the canonical maps

\mathcal{C}_{/y}\rightarrow\mathcal{C},\quad\mathcal{C}_{x/}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}

over
x,y
and
(x,y)
, respectively, and we shall denote them by
\Hom^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathcal{C}}
,
\Hom^{\mathrm{R}}_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}
.

There are canonical inclusions

\Hom^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)\longleftarrow\Hom^{\mathrm{R}}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)

that are homotopy equivalences of Kan complexes (though not usually isomorphisms unless
\mathcal{C}
is an ordinary category) by non-trivial results of Joyal. Though all of these have their uses, arguably it is the middle one which is the most natural: For example, its definition is symmetric in source and target, it readily compares to morphism spaces of complete Segal spaces, has the most clearly defined composition law and features in the standard proof that fully faithful and essentially surjective functors are equivalences.

Now, one of the most prominent constructions of quasi-categories is the coherent nerve
\mathfrak{N}
of Cordier and Porter, which takes as input a category enriched in Kan complexes. There arises the problem of relating, for such a category
\mathrm{C}
the given morphism complexes
\mathrm{C}(x,y)
with those of
\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})
. This was done by Lurie by producing an explicit equivalence
\mathrm{C}(x,y)\rightarrow\Hom^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)
, which can be combined with Joyal’s result above to obtain an equivalence
\sigma\colon\mathrm{C}(x,y)\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)
. The main goal of the present note is to present a direct proof of this latter result avoiding the rather less elementary theorem of Joyal.

Theorem
.

The map

\sigma\colon\mathrm{C}(x,y)\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)

is a homotopy equivalence of Kan complexes for every Kan enriched category
\mathrm{C}
with objects
x
and
y
.

By formal considerations there is in fact a tautological isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)\cong\Sing_{\mathrm{W}}(\mathrm{C}(x,y))

for some particular
\mathrm{W}\colon{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Delta$}\put(4.33337,5.26167){\line{-13}{-26}{2.3917}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Delta$}\put(4.33337,5.26167){\line{-13}{-26}{2.3917}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Delta$}\put(3.41615,3.68318){\line{-13}{-26}{1.6742}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}\put(2.85284,2.61708){\line{-13}{-26}{1.18959}}\end{picture}}}\rightarrow\mathrm{sSet}
; here and in general we denote for a functor
T\colon\mathrm{A}\rightarrow\mathrm{B}
by

|\cdot|_{T}\colon\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{A})\longleftrightarrow\mathrm{B}\penalty\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\mskip 2.0mu\Sing_{T}

the adjunction arising by colimit extension of
T
(assuming of course that
A
is small and
B
cocomplete).

The map
\sigma
of the theorem is then induced by an explicit transformation
\sigma\colon\mathrm{W}\rightarrow\Delta
, where
\Delta
denotes the cosimplicial object of
\mathrm{sSet}
formed by the simplices (which satisfies
|\cdot|_{\Delta}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{sSet}}=\Sing_{\Delta}
). Our proof of the theorem boils down to the fact that the cosimplicial object
\mathrm{W}
is both Reedy cofibrant and termwise contractible. This is the exact same strategy employed in the analogous result for left mapping spaces, except that the relevant cosimplicial object is a lot less complicated in that case. We, in fact, verify both cases simultaneously with comparatively little explicit combinatorial manipulation.

We also provide a somewhat more detailed analysis of mapping spaces in coherent nerves, in particular producing (to us) surprising isomorphisms

\Hom^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)\cong\Hom^{\mathrm{R}}_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)^{\mathrm{op}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Hom_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)\cong\Hom_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)^{\mathrm{op}}.

Remark
.

Our hope is that the proof we present here can be used in a first course on quasi-categories, which in our experience is better served by considering middle mapping spaces, than either the left or right variants. It was precisely this context which originally provided the motivation for the present note.

Our proofs are therefore designed to work with minimal input (though we are not aware that they could be simplified much by investing results other than the comparison of left and middle mapping spaces). We will, however, either have to rely on the non-trivial fact that cofibrations of simplicial sets that are also weak homotopy equivalences are in fact anodyne extensions or on the consequence of Joyal’s lifting theorem that middle mapping spaces in quasi-categories are Kan; this dependence could be avoided by directly checking that
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{W}}
preserves anodyne extensions, but we refrain from doing so here, since we would expect at least one of these results to be available by the time the statements we prove here become relevant in any context.

Let us first state Reedy’s lemma, which we will need for both simplicial and bisimplicial sets (it generally holds over any Reedy category, which both

{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Delta$}\put(4.33337,5.26167){\line{-13}{-26}{2.3917}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Delta$}\put(4.33337,5.26167){\line{-13}{-26}{2.3917}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Delta$}\put(3.41615,3.68318){\line{-13}{-26}{1.6742}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}\put(2.85284,2.61708){\line{-13}{-26}{1.18959}}\end{picture}}}^{\times 2}
are examples of). To give a uniform statement, let us agree that by a simplex we shall mean one of the
\Delta^{i}
in the case of
\mathrm{sSet}
and one of the
\Delta^{i}\boxtimes\Delta^{j}
in the case of
\mathrm{ssSet}
. Similarly, by the boundary of a simplex we shall mean either
\partial\Delta^{i}
or

\partial(\Delta^{i}\boxtimes\Delta^{j})=(\partial(\Delta^{i-1})\boxtimes\Delta^{j})\cup(\Delta^{i}\boxtimes\partial\Delta^{j-1}),

as appropriate. A (bi)cosimplicial simplicial set
X
is called Reedy cofibrant if
|\cdot|_{X}
takes these boundary inclusions to cofibrations (i.e. degreewise injections). Here is the version of Reedy’s result we shall need:

4.1 Proposition

(Reedy’s lemma)
.

Let
X
be a Reedy cofibrant (bi)cosimplicial simplicial set, whose structure maps are weak homotopy equivalences. Then
|\cdot|_{X}
preserves degreewise injections and weak homotopy equivalences, and in the cosimplicial case
\Sing_{X}
preserves homotopy equivalences between Kan complexes.

If furthermore,
\eta\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a pointwise weak equivalence between two such (bi)cosimplicial simplicial sets, then

\eta_{*}\colon|S|_{X}\rightarrow|S|_{Y}

is a weak homotopy equivalence for every (bi)simplicial set
S
, and in the cosimplicial case for any Kan complex
T
the map

\eta^{*}\colon\Sing_{Y}(T)\longrightarrow\Sing_{X}(T)

is a homotopy equivalence of Kan complexes.

Proof sketch:

The statement about
\eta_{*}
is a simple skeletal induction using the glueing lemma, and that
|\cdot|_{X}
preserves cofibrations is similarly easy (and both statements only use Reedy cofibrancy of
X
and
Y
). The second part implies that
\Sing_{X}
preserves trivial fibrations. Next, one shows that
|\cdot|_{X}
preserves weak homotopy equivalences, for example by considering the composite

\operatorname{const}X_{0}\times\Delta\longrightarrow X\times\Delta\xrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}}X,

which gives the claim by an application of the first part, since

S\longmapsto|S|_{\operatorname{const}X_{0}\times\Delta}=X_{0}\times S

evidently preserves weak homotopy equivalences. Investing the fact that cofibrations that are also weak equivalences are in fact anodyne, then shows that
|\cdot|_{X}
also preserves anodynes and it follows that
\Sing_{X}
preserves Kan fibrations, so in particular
\Sing_{X}(T)
is Kan again. Using this knowledge one easily checks that the adjunction isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathrm{sSet}}(|S|_{X},T)\cong\Hom_{\mathrm{sSet}}(S,\Sing_{X}(T))

is compatible with the homotopy relation. This immediately implies that
\Sing_{X}
preserves homotopy equivalences between Kan complexes. Finally, the diagram

\textstyle{[S,\Sing_{Y}(T)]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta^{*}}
\textstyle{[S,\Sing_{X}(T)]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{[|S|_{Y},T]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta_{*}}
\textstyle{[|S|_{X},T]}

commutes for every
S\in\mathrm{sSet}
and by the previous parts has three maps bijective, hence also the fourth.
∎

4.2 Remark
.

(1)

The only step in this result requiring non-trivial input (namely that trivial cofibrations are anodyne) is that
\Sing_{X}(T)
and
\Sing_{Y}(T)
are indeed Kan complexes (which, as far as we know, is a necessary step for proving that they are even weakly equivalent). In the case we want to apply the second part of the lemma, i.e. for
X=\mathrm{W}
,
Y=\Delta
and
T=\mathrm{C}(x,y)
for some Kan enriched category
\mathrm{C}
, this can be avoided: It is trivial for
\Sing_{Y}(T)
and follows for
\Sing_{X}(T)
, since by design
\Sing_{\mathrm{W}}(\mathrm{C}(x,y))=\Hom_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)
and morphism complexes in quasi-categories are Kan as a straight forward consequence of Joyal’s lifting theorem
[
Jo02
]
.

(2)

Alternatively, by inspection of the proof above, one only needs to know that
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{W}}
preserves anodynes to conclude that
\Hom_{\mathfrak{N}(\mathrm{C})}(x,y)
is Kan without investing either result. A saturation argument reduces this to the case of horn inclusions, and here an explicit verification should be possible, but we shall refrain from attempting it here.

(3)

The statements about
\Sing_{X}
are restricted to the cosimplicial case (as opposed to the bicosimplicial one) to avoid a discussion of various possible choices of fibrations and cofibrations in
\mathrm{ssSet}
, as it is not required for our applications. By
[
GJ99
, Section IV.3.2]
the argument above works equally well for example upon replacing the words ‘anodyne’ and ‘Kan complex’ by ‘trivial Reedy cofibration’ and ‘Reedy fibrant bisimplicial set’, respectively.

We will now set out to show that
\mathrm{Q}
satisfies the assumptions of Reedy’s lemma. We already showed in
3.11
that
\mathrm{Q}
is termwise contractible, so it remains to check Reedy cofibrancy.

We will employ the following criterion:

4.3 Lemma
.

Let
X
by a (bi)cosimplicial simplicial set, such that for any pair of codimension
1
faces
K,K^{\prime}
of a simplex
L
the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|}K\cap K^{\prime}{|}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|}K{|}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|}K^{\prime}{|}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|}L{|}_{X}}

is cartesian. Then
X
is Reedy cofibrant.

The point of the lemma is that all four terms in the square are actually simplices of varying dimensions (before realisation) and therefore the diagram itself is given by evaluating
X
on a diagram, with no colimits occuring in the computation of the four corners.

Note also, that the case
K=K^{\prime}
in the condition above boils down to precisely the statement that the boundary maps of
X
are cofibrations (which is automatic as the degeneracies give left inverses).

Proof.

It is easy to give a hands-on argument; here is an abstract one:

The bisimplicial set
\partial L\subseteq L
is the levelwise image of the tautological map
F=\coprod_{K}K\to L
, where the index runs through all codimension
1
-faces of
L
. In the category of sets, images of an arbitrary morphism
X\to Y
can be computed by
X\to\Coeq(X\times_{Y}X\rightrightarrows X)\to Y
, in the sense that the composition is the original map, the first map surjective, and the second one injective. Consequentially, this also holds in any
\mathrm{Set}
-valued functor category, like
\mathrm{sSet}
. It follows that
\partial L\rightarrow L
agrees with the canonical map
\Coeq(F\times_{L}F\rightrightarrows F)\rightarrow L
. We can therefore compute

\displaystyle|\partial L|_{X}

\displaystyle=\Coeq(|F\times_{L}F|_{X}\rightrightarrows|F|_{X})

\displaystyle=\Coeq(\coprod_{K,K^{\prime}}|K\times_{L}K^{\prime}|_{X}\rightrightarrows|F|_{X})

\displaystyle=\Coeq(\coprod_{K,K^{\prime}}|K|_{X}\times_{|L|_{X}}|K^{\prime}|_{X}\rightrightarrows|F|_{X})

\displaystyle=\Coeq(|F|_{X}\times_{|L|_{X}}|F|_{X}\rightrightarrows|F|_{X}),

where we have used that pullbacks commute with colimits in the second and last steps, and the assumption in the third one. By the considerations above, this last expression agrees with the image of
|\partial L|_{X}
in
|L|_{X}
, which forces the induced map to be injective in each degree.
∎

4.4 Corollary
.

The bicosimplicial simplicial set
\mathrm{Q}
is Reedy cofibrant.

This statement is the only piece of combinatorics we did not manage to avoid; alas the work has to be somewhere.

Proof.

Recall then that

\mathrm{Q}_{i,j}=\mathrm{N}(\{S\subseteq[i]*[j]\mid[i]\cap S,[j]\cap S\neq\emptyset\})/\sim

where two chains of subsets
S_{0}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq S_{n}
and
S^{\prime}_{0}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq S^{\prime}_{n}
are identified if there exist
i_{0}\in[i]
and
j_{0}\in[j]
with
\{i_{0},j_{0}\}\subseteq S_{0},S_{0}^{\prime}
and
S_{k}\cap[i_{0},j_{0}]=S_{k}^{\prime}\cap[i_{0},j_{0}]
for all
k
.

We wish to apply the criterion from the previous lemma, which will require a case distinction based on whether the two faces are taken in the same simplicial direction or not. Consider the first case and, say, faces in the first direction, the arguments for the second being dual. Then we need to show that for any
0\leq l<k\leq i
the intersection of
d_{k}\mathrm{Q}_{i-1,j}
and
d_{l}\mathrm{Q}_{i-1,j}
is no larger than
d_{k}d_{l}\mathrm{Q}_{i-2,j}
.
Denote by

\pi\colon\mathrm{N}(\{S\in[i+1+j]\mid[i]\cap S,[i+1,i+1+j]\cap S\neq\emptyset\})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Q}_{i,j}

the projection. Then one easily checks that
\pi^{-1}(d_{k}\mathrm{Q}_{i-1,j})
consists of all those sequences
S_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq S_{n}
for which

S_{0}\cap[k+1,i]\neq\emptyset\quad\text{or}\quad k\notin S_{n}.

Similarly, one checks that both the preimage of the intersection and
\pi^{-1}(d_{k}d_{l}\mathrm{Q}_{i-2,j})
consist of those sequences with

(1)

S_{0}\cap[k+1,i]\neq\emptyset
,

(2)

S_{0}\cap[l+1,i]\neq\emptyset
, but
k\notin S_{n}
, or

(3)

k,l\notin S_{n}
.

For the mixed case, say regarding
d_{k}\mathrm{Q}_{i-1,j}
and
d_{l}\mathrm{Q}_{i,j-1}
both the preimage of the intersection and the preimage of
d_{k,l}\mathrm{Q}_{i-1,j-1}
consist of those chains with

(1)

S_{0}\cap[k+1,i]\neq\emptyset
and
S_{0}\cap[i+1,i+l]\neq\emptyset
,

(2)

S_{0}\cap[k+1,i]\neq\emptyset
and
i+1+l\notin S_{n}
,

(3)

k\notin S_{n}
and
S_{0}\cap[i+1,i+l]\neq\emptyset
, or finally

(4)

k,i+1+l\notin S_{n}
.

The claim follows.
∎

4.5 Lemma
.

The bisimplicial sets
\Cut^{n}
are weakly contractible.

Proof.

Let
J:{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Delta$}\put(4.33337,5.26167){\line{-13}{-26}{2.3917}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Delta$}\put(4.33337,5.26167){\line{-13}{-26}{2.3917}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Delta$}\put(3.41615,3.68318){\line{-13}{-26}{1.6742}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}\put(2.85284,2.61708){\line{-13}{-26}{1.18959}}\end{picture}}}^{\times 2}\rightarrow\mathrm{sSet}
be the functor given by
(i,j)\mapsto\Delta^{i}*\Delta^{j}
. From Reedy’s lemma we obtain weak homotopy equivalences

|\Cut^{n}|_{J}\longleftarrow|\Cut^{n}|_{J\times\Delta^{\boxtimes 2}}\longrightarrow|\Cut^{n}|_{\Delta^{\boxtimes 2}}=\mathrm{diag}(\Cut^{n}),

where
\Delta^{\times 2}
is the bicosimplicial simplicial set
(i,j)\mapsto\Delta^{i}\times\Delta^{j}
. By the same argument as in
3.8
we have
|\Cut^{n}|_{J}\cong\Delta^{n}\times\Delta^{1}
.
∎

Since

by construction of
\mathrm{W}
an application of Reedy’s lemma to the map

constructed at the beginning of this section will give the claim. We therefore need to check that

is Reedy cofibrant and degreewise weakly contractible. To this end, recall that

, or in other words
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{W}}=|\cdot|_{\mathrm{Q}}\circ|\cdot|_{\Cut}
. But both of these preserve degreewise injections,

by inspection, see

, and

by
4.4
. Furthermore, by

another application of Reedy’s lemma gives weak homotopy equivalences

\mathrm{W}_{n}=|\Cut^{n}|_{\mathrm{Q}}\longleftarrow|\Cut^{n}|_{\mathrm{Q}\times\Delta^{\boxtimes 2}}\longrightarrow|\Cut^{n}|_{\Delta^{\boxtimes 2}}=\mathrm{diag}(\Cut^{n})

the right hand term of which is contractible by the previous lemma.

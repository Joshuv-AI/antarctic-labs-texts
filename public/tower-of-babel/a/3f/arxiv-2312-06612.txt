Scalar fields in Bonnor-Melvin-Lambda universe with potential: A study of dynamics of spin-zero particles-antiparticles


Abstract
In this study, our primary focus is on exploring the relativistic quantum dynamics of spin-zero scalar particles in a magnetic space-time background. Our investigation revolves around solving the Klein-Gordon (KG) equation within the framework of an electrovacuum space-time, while incorporating an external scalar potential. Specifically, we consider a cylindrical symmetric Bonnor-Melvin magnetic universe featuring a cosmological constant, where the magnetic field aligns parallel to the symmetry axis. Our approach involves deriving the radial equation of the wave equation, initially considering a linear confining potential and subsequently incorporating a Cornell-type scalar potential. We successfully obtain an approximate analytical solution for the eigenvalues of the quantum system under examination. Worth noting is our observation that the energy spectrum and the corresponding radial wave function experience notable modifications due to the presence of various factors including the cosmological constant, the topological parameter characterizing the space-time geometry, and the potential parameters.

Scalar fields in Bonnor-Melvin-Lambda universe with potential: The Klein-Gordon Equation

In this part, our focus turns to solve the Klein-Gordon equation within the background of Bonnor-Melvin space-time-enriched with a cosmological constant. The metric under consideration stands as static solution characterized by cylindrical symmetry, emerging from Einstein’s field equations. This metric is intricately shaped by a homogeneous magnetic field, offering a compelling scenario for our investigation and the line-element describing this magnetic universe is given by

,

]

H(\rho)=\sigma\,\sqrt{\Lambda}\,\sin\left(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho\right),\quad\Lambda>0.

(3)

g_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-1&0&0&0\\

We introduce a static scalar potential into the Klein-Gordon equation (
1
) by modifying the mass term in this way:
M\to M+S(\rho)
, where

is a position-dependent scalar potential
[
58
]
. Numerous authors have been studied the relativistic wave equations in the presence of scalar potentials of various kinds through this technique (see, Ref.

\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_{\mu}\,\left(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\nu}\right)-(M+S)^{2}\right]\,\Psi=0.

(5)

Expressing this wave equation (
5
) in the space-time background (
2
) and using (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigg[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sin(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho)}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\Big(\sin(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\Big)+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}\,\sin^{2}(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho)}\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varphi^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-(M+S)^{2}\Bigg]\,\Psi=0,

(6)

where the determinant of the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
for the space-time (
2
) is given by

\sqrt{-g}=\sigma\,\sin(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho).

(7)

The differential equation (
6
) is independent of the time coordinate
t
, the angular coordinate
\varphi
, and the translation coordinate
z
. Therefore, we can choose one possible ansatz for the total wave function in the following form:

\Psi(t,\rho,\varphi,z)=e^{i\,(-E\,t+\ell\,\varphi+k\,z)}\,\psi(\rho),

(8)

where
E
is the particle’s energy,
\ell
is the angular momentum quantum number, and
k
is an arbitrary constant.

Using this wave function (
8
) into the differential equation (
6
), we can write the following form of the second order differential equation

\psi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}{\tan(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho)}\,\psi^{\prime}+\Bigg[\beta^{2}-2\,M\,S(\rho)-S^{2}(\rho)-\frac{\ell^{2}}{\sigma^{2}\,\sin^{2}(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho)}\Bigg]\,\psi=0,

(9)

where

\beta^{2}=E^{2}-M^{2}-k^{2}\,.

(10)

We solve this radial equation by considering two different types of potential. Many potential models have been proposed in quantum system both in the context of relativistic and non-relativistic limit. Among these potential models are, linear confining potential, Yukawa potential, Coulomb potential which is used for H-atom problem, Cornell potential and many more. These potentials have wide applications including atomic and molecular physics, nuclear and particle physics.

2.1
Linear potential

In this part, we consider a linear potential into the above discussed quantum system and see how the relativistic scalar particles is influenced by this potential. Therefore, the linear confining potential is given by

S=\eta\,\rho,

(11)

where
\eta
is the confining parameter. This potential has widely been used in the confinement of quark phenomenon
[
59
]
, and in the quantum system by numerous authors (see, Refs.
[
60
,
61
,
62
]
).

Thereby, substituting this confining potential into the radial equation (
9
) results the following equation:

\psi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}{\tan(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho)}\,\psi^{\prime}+\Bigg[\beta^{2}-2\,M\,\eta\,\rho-\eta^{2}\,\rho^{2}-\frac{\ell^{2}}{\sigma^{2}\,\sin^{2}(\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}\,\rho)}\Bigg]\,\psi=0,

(12)

The exact solution of the above differential equation is very difficult and complicated. Therefore, to obtain an eigenvalue solution, we assume that the cosmological constant
\Lambda
is very small, and therefore, taking approximation up to the first order, we arrive from (
12
) the following differential equation form:

\psi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{\rho}\,\psi^{\prime}+\Bigg[\beta^{2}-2\,M\,\eta\,\rho-\eta^{2}\,\rho^{2}-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\Bigg]\,\psi=0,

(13)

where
\tau=\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}
.

Now, we choose the following ansatz for the above differential equation:

\psi(\rho)=\rho^{\tau}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\,\rho^{2}\,\eta-M\,\rho}\,\chi(\rho)

(14)

which is regular everywhere including at
\rho\to 0
.

Thereby, substituting this wave function in Eq. (
13
), we obtain the following differential equation form:

\chi^{\prime\prime}+\left[\frac{(2\,\tau+1)}{\rho}-2\,\eta\,\rho-2\,M\right]\,\chi^{\prime}+\left[\beta^{2}+M^{2}-2\,(\tau+1)\,\eta-\frac{M\,(2\,\tau+1)}{\rho}\right]\chi=0.

(15)

Finally transforming to a new variable via
s=\sqrt{\eta}\,\rho
in Eq. (
15
) results the following form:

\displaystyle s\,\chi^{\prime\prime}(s)+\left(1+2\,\tau-\alpha^{\prime}\,s-2\,s^{2}\right)\,\chi^{\prime}(s)+\left(\mu\,s-\nu\right)\,\chi(s)=0,

(16)

where we set the parameters

\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{2\,M}{\sqrt{\eta}},\quad\mu=\frac{\beta^{2}+M^{2}}{\eta}-2\,(1+\tau),\quad\nu=\frac{M}{\sqrt{\eta}}\,(1+2\,\tau).

(17)

Equation (
16
) is the Biconfluent Heun second-order differential equation form
[
63
]
whose solution is well-known, and thus,
\chi(s)
is the biconfluent Heun function given by the following form:

\chi(s)=\mathrm{HeunB}\left(2\,\tau,\alpha^{\prime},\mu+2\,(1+\tau),0,s\right).

(18)

Now, we solve the differential equation (
16
) using a power series solution method. Therefore, we consider the following power series around the origin given by
[
64
]

\chi=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}_{j}\,s^{j}.

(19)

Therefore, we obtain

\chi^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\,j\,\mathcal{C}_{j}\,s^{j-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\,(j+1)\,\mathcal{C}_{j+1}\,s^{j}.

(20)

And its second-order derivative gives

\chi^{\prime\prime}(s)=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\,j\,\left(j-1\right)\mathcal{C}_{j}s^{j-2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\,(j+1)\,(j+2)\,\mathcal{C}_{j+2}\,s^{j}.

(21)

Thereby, substituting equations (
19
)–(
21
) in equation (
16
), we get the following three-terms recurrence relation given by

\mathcal{C}_{j+2}=\frac{\left(\alpha^{\prime}\left(j+1\right)+\nu\right)}{\left[\left(j+2\right)\left(2\left|\tau\right|+j+2\right)\right]}\mathcal{C}_{j+1}+\frac{\left(2j-\mu\right)}{\left[\left(j+2\right)\left(2\left|\tau\right|+j+2\right)\right]}\mathcal{C}_{j},

(22)

with the following coefficient

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\frac{\nu}{2\,\tau+1}\mathcal{C}_{0}.

(23)

To obtain bound-state solution of the quantum system under investigation, we must truncate the three-terms recurrence relation such that the power series function (
19
) becomes a finite degree polynomial. This truncation of a power series is obtained by setting the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{j+1}=0
and
\mu=2\,j
in the recurrence relation (
24
) results the higher power coefficients of
s
vanishes, that is,
\mathcal{C}_{j+2}=0
and so on. In that case, the power series becomes
\chi=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\,\mathcal{C}_{j}\,s^{j}
becomes a finite degree polynomial of degree
j
, and thus, the wave function (
14
) is finite and well-behaved everywhere. The truncating conditions are given by

\mathcal{C}_{j+1}=0,\quad\mu=2\,j.

(24)

Simplifying the second condition gives us the approximate (but not exact) energy eigenvalue expression associated with the mode
\{j,\ell\}
given by

E_{j,\ell}=\pm\,\sqrt{k^{2}+2\,\eta\,(j+1+\tau)}.

(25)

The radial wave function is given by

\psi_{j,\ell}=\mathcal{N}_{1}\rho^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\,\rho^{2}\,\eta-M\,\rho}\,\mathrm{HeunB}\,\Big(2\,\tau,\frac{2\,M}{\sqrt{\eta}},\frac{M^{2}+\beta^{2}}{\eta},0,\sqrt{\eta}\,\rho\Big).

(26)

One should remembered that the first condition given by
\mathcal{C}_{j+1}=0
must also analyze simultaneously to get a complete information of the quantum system under investigation. This analysis will give us the individual energy level expression and the corresponding radial function of the scalar particles. The lowest state of the quantum system is defined by
j=1
. In that case, the coefficient
\mathcal{C}_{2}=0
. Therefore, the ground-state approximate energy level from (
25
) will be

E_{1,\ell}=\pm\,\sqrt{k^{2}+2\,\eta\,\Big(2+\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}\Big)}.

(27)

From the recurrence relation (
24
), we obtain

\mathcal{C}_{2}=\frac{1}{2\,(1+2\,\tau)}\Bigg[(\alpha^{\prime}+\nu)\,\mathcal{C}_{1}-\mu\,\mathcal{C}_{0}\Bigg].

(28)

For the ground state we have
\mathcal{C}_{2}=0
and
\mu=2
which implies
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\frac{2}{(\alpha^{\prime}+\nu)}\,\mathcal{C}_{0}
. Therefore, equating this one with the equation (
23
) results the following condition

\eta_{1,\ell}=\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}\Big)\,M^{2}

(29)

a constraint on the confining potential parameter
\eta\to\eta_{1,\ell}
that permit us to construct a first degree polynomial of
\chi(\rho)
. We see that this parameter
\eta_{1,\ell}
depends on the quantum number
\ell
and the geometric parameters
(\sigma,\Lambda)
.

Therefore, from equation (
27

E_{1,\ell}=\pm\,\sqrt{k^{2}+2\,M^{2}\,\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}\Big)\Big(2+\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}\Big)},\quad\Lambda>0.

(30)

And the corresponding approximate ground state wave function will be

\psi_{1,\ell}=\rho^{\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\,\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}\Big)\,M^{2}\,\rho^{2}-M\,\rho}\,\Bigg[1+\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{|\ell|}{\sigma\,\sqrt{2\,\Lambda}}\Big)\,\rho\Bigg]\,\mathcal{C}_{0}.

(31)

Equation (
30

31

Following the similar procedure, we one find excited states energy levels and wave function of the quantum system defined by the mode
j\geq 2

\sigma

\Lambda

2.2
Cornell-type potential

In this part, we consider a linear plus Coulomb-type potential together called Cornell-type potential given by

S=\eta\,\rho+\frac{\xi}{\rho},

(32)

where
\xi>0
is potential parameter. The linear part is responsible for long range interactions whereas the Coulomb part for short range interactions. The Cornell potential consists of a linear potential plus a Coulomb potential and a harmonic term that is used in quark-antiquark interaction

50

Thereby, substituting this Cornell-type potential into the radial equation (
9

\psi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{\rho}\,\psi^{\prime}+\Bigg[\beta^{2}-2\,\eta\,\xi-2\,M\,\eta\,\rho-\eta^{2}\,\rho^{2}-\frac{2\,M\,\xi}{\rho}-\frac{\iota^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\Bigg]\,\psi=0,

(33)

\iota^{2}=\xi^{2}+\tau^{2}=\xi^{2}+\frac{\ell^{2}/\sigma^{2}}{2\,\Lambda}.

(34)

Analogue to the previous section, let us consider the following wave function ansatz:

\psi\left(\rho\right)=\rho^{\left|\iota\right|}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\eta-M\rho}\Omega(\rho).

Substituting this wave function into the equation (

\Omega^{\prime\prime}+\left[-2\,M-2\,\eta\,\rho+\frac{(2\,\iota+1)}{\rho}\right]\,\Omega^{\prime}+\left[M^{2}+\beta^{2}-2\,\xi\,\eta-2\,(1+\iota)\,\eta-\frac{2\,M}{\rho}\left(\xi+\iota+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\,\Omega=0.

Transforming to a new variable via the following transformation
\mathcal{P}=\sqrt{\eta}\rho

\Omega^{\prime\prime}+\left[\frac{2\,\iota+1}{\mathcal{P}}-\frac{2\,M}{\sqrt{\eta}}-2\,\mathcal{P}\right]\Omega^{\prime}+\left[\frac{M^{2}+\beta^{2}-2\,\xi\,\eta}{\eta}-2\,(\iota+1)-\frac{\left(\frac{M}{\sqrt{\eta}}\right)}{\mathcal{P}}\,(2\,\xi+2\,\iota+1)\right]\,\Omega=0.

(37)

Defining the following parameters

\delta=\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\eta}},\quad\gamma=\frac{M^{2}+\beta^{2}-2\,\xi\,\eta}{\eta}-2\,(\iota+1),\quad\varsigma=\frac{M}{\sqrt{\eta}}(2\,\xi+2\,\iota+1)

\mathcal{P}\,\Omega^{\prime\prime}+\left(1+2\,\iota-\delta\,\mathcal{P}-2\,\mathcal{P}^{2}\right)\,\Omega^{\prime}+\left(\gamma\,\mathcal{P}-\varsigma\right)\,\Omega=0.

This differential equation is the Biconfluent Heun equation form

whose solution is given by

\Omega\left(\mathcal{P}\right)=\mathrm{HeunB}\left(2\,\iota,\delta,\gamma+2\,(\iota+1),2\,\varsigma-4\,(\iota+1),\mathcal{P}\right).

Following the previous analysis, we choose a power series given by

\Omega\left(\mathcal{P}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{j}\mathcal{P}^{j}

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\frac{\varsigma}{\left[(2\,\iota+1)\right]}\,\mathcal{D}_{0}

(42)

\mathcal{D}_{j+2}=\frac{\left[\alpha^{\prime}\,(j+1)+\varsigma\right]}{\left[\left(j+2\right)\left(2\left|\iota\right|+j+2\right)\right]}\mathcal{D}_{j+1}+\frac{\left[2j-\gamma\right]}{\left[\left(j+2\right)\left(2\left|\iota\right|+j+2\right)\right]}\mathcal{D}_{j}.

Conclusions

In our investigation, we focused into the relativistic quantum dynamics of scalar particles embedded within the framework of a magnetic universe, taking into account the presence of a cosmological constant. Our focal point was the Bonnor-Melvin magnetic universe, where the strength of the magnetic field is determined by the topological parameter
\sigma
and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
. Additionally, we introduced a static non-electromagnetic scalar potential denoted as
S
, introducing a modification to the mass term
M\to(M+S)
within the Klein-Gordon equation. We derived the radial wave equation for this scalar potential
S
in the magnetic universe background, opting for a linear confining potential. The resulting radial equation took the form of a Biconfluent Heun second-order differential equation. Utilizing a power series solution method, we obtained approximate solution for eigenvalues, such as the ground state energy level, and the corresponding wave function for the scalar particles. Importantly, we illustrated that the eigenvalue solution is influenced by the topology of the geometry represented by
\sigma
and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
, leading to notable modifications.

Next, we selected a Cornell-type scalar potential, and following the established procedure, we presented approximate ground state energy level and the radial wave function of the quantum system. In this case, we observed that the eigenvalue solution is not only influenced by the parameters
(\sigma,\Lambda)
but also by the potential parameters
(\eta,\xi)
. This underscores the intricate interplay between the magnetic universe’s topology, the cosmological constant, and the scalar potential, showcasing the behaviour of scalar particles in this magnetic universe space-time background.

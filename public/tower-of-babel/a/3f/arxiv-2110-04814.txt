Finding Second-Order Stationary Points in Nonconvex-Strongly-Concave Minimax Optimization
Luo Luo, Yujun Li, Cheng Chen

Abstract
We study the smooth minimax optimization problem min 𝐱 ⁡ max 𝐲 ⁡ f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) \min_{\bf x}\max_{\bf y}f({\bf x},{\bf y}) , where f f is ℓ \ell -smooth, strongly-concave in 𝐲 {\bf y} but possibly nonconvex in 𝐱 {\bf x} . Most of existing works focus on finding the first-order stationary points of the function f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) f({\bf x},{\bf y}) or its primal function P ⁡ ( 𝐱 ) ≜ max 𝐲 ⁡ f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) P({\bf x})\triangleq\max_{\bf y}f({\bf x},{\bf y}) , but few of them focus on achieving second-order stationary points. In this paper, we propose a novel approach for minimax optimization, called Minimax Cubic Newton (MCN), which could find an ( ε , κ 1.5 ​ ρ ​ ε ) \big(\varepsilon,\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho\varepsilon}\,\big) -second-order stationary point of P ⁡ ( 𝐱 ) P({\bf x}) with calling 𝒪 ⁡ ( κ 1.5 ​ ρ ​ ε − 1.5 ) {\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho}\varepsilon^{-1.5}\big) times of second-order oracles and 𝒪 ~ ​ ( κ 2 ​ ρ ​ ε − 1.5 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{2}\sqrt{\rho}\varepsilon^{-1.5}\big) times of first-order oracles, where κ \kappa is the condition number and ρ \rho is the Lipschitz continuous constant for the Hessian of f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) f({\bf x},{\bf y}) . In addition, we propose an inexact variant of MCN for high-dimensional problems to avoid calling expensive second-order oracles. Instead, our method solves the cubic sub-problem inexactly via gradient descent and matrix Chebyshev expansion. This strategy still obtains the desired approximate second-order stationary point with high probability but only requires 𝒪 ~ ​ ( κ 1.5 ​ ℓ ​ ε − 2 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{1.5}\ell\varepsilon^{-2}\big) Hessian-vector oracle calls and 𝒪 ~ ​ ( κ 2 ​ ρ ​ ε − 1.5 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{2}\sqrt{\rho}\varepsilon^{-1.5}\big) first-order oracle calls. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that considers the non-asymptotic convergence behavior of finding second-order stationary points for minimax problems without the convex-concave assumptions.



1
Introduction

We consider minimax optimization of the form

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}}\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}),

(1)

where
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is
\ell
-smooth,
\mu
-strongly-concave in
{\bf{y}}
, but possibly nonconvex in
{\bf{x}}
.
Problem (
1
) can also be written as

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}}\left\{P({\bf{x}})\triangleq\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\right\}.

(2)

This framework covers a wide range of applications in machine learning such as regularized GAN
[
30
]
, reinforcement learning
[
29
]
, domain adaptation
[
9
]
and adversarial training
[
33
]
.

Most recent works focus on finding an
{\varepsilon}
-first-order stationary point (FSP) of
P({\bf{x}})
.

Lin et al. 2020a
showed that the vanilla gradient descent ascent (GDA) method could obtain an
{\varepsilon}
-FSP with
{\mathcal{O}}((\kappa^{2}\ell+\kappa\ell^{2}){\varepsilon}^{-2})
first-order oracle calls. This complexity can be reduced to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{\kappa}\ell{\varepsilon}^{-2}\right)
by proximal iteration algorithms
[
21
]
, which matches the gradient oracle lower bound for finding
{\varepsilon}
-FSP of
P({\bf{x}})

[
13
,
42
]
.
The theory of first-order optimization for problem (
1
) has also been studied in stochastic settings
[
20
,
22
,
40
,
12
,
15
,
39
]
. However, the approximate FSP obtained by these algorithms cannot guarantee the local optimality since the primal function
P({\bf{x}})
could be nonconvex.

In this paper, we focus on finding a second-order stationary point (SSP) of
P({\bf{x}})
to capture the local optimal properties
[
24
,
8
]
.
Inspired by the success of second-order optimization in nonconvex minimization
[
26
,
43
,
36
,
1
,
5
,
6
,
18
,
14
]
, we propose a novel method, called Minimax Cubic
Newton (MCN), which runs cubic Newton update on
{\bf{x}}
and maximizes the objective on
{\bf{y}}
alternatively.
This iteration scheme avoids getting stuck at an unexpected FSP.
Specifically, we show MCN will converge to an
\big({\varepsilon},\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho{\varepsilon}}\,\big)
-SSP of
P({\bf{x}})
with
{\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5}\big)
number of iterations, where
\kappa
is the condition number and
\rho
is the Lipschitz continuous constant of
\nabla^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
.
For high-dimensional problems,
we also propose an efficient algorithm, called Inexact Minimax Cubic Newton (IMCN), which avoids the expensive second-order oracle calls.
IMCN approximates the second-order information by matrix Chebyshev polynomial and solves the cubic regularized sub-problem inexactly. It only requires
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{1.5}\ell{\varepsilon}^{-2}\big)
Hessian-vector oracle calls and
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{2}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5}\big)
first-order oracle calls to find an
\big({\varepsilon},\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho{\varepsilon}}\,\big)
-SSP.
Under mild strict saddle condition
[
10
,
11
,
35
,
34
,
3
]
, the approximate SSP of
P({\bf{x}})
implies an approximate local minimax point of
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
defined by
Jin et al. 2020
, which successfully characterizes the local optimality for problem (
1
).
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that considers non-asymptotic convergence behavior of finding SSP for minimax problems without convex-concave assumptions.
We also conduct experiments on both synthetic function and the real application to validate our theoretical analysis. The empirical results show that the proposed algorithms significantly outperform the GDA method.

In a concurrent work,
Chen et al. 2021
also studied Problem (
1
) and proposed Cubic-GDA which is similar to our MCN algorithm. MCN has advantage on complexity of first-order oracles by a factor of
\sqrt{\kappa}
since Cubic-GDA adopts GD to update
{\bf{y}}
while MCN uses AGD instead.
Chen et al. 2021
mentioned that the cubic sub-problem can be efficiently solved by gradient-based algorithms, but they did not provide theoretical analysis for this inexact variant, which is more practical in high dimensional case. As a comparison, we provide the complexity of both Hessian-vector oracles and first-order oracles of our inexact algorithm IMCN.

Preliminaries

This section first presents the notations and assumptions for our settings. Then we introduce the background of local optimality for minimax optimization and some basic algorithms.

2.1
Notations and Assumptions

For a twice differentiable function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
, its partial gradients with respect to
{\bf{x}}
and
{\bf{y}}
are denoted as
\nabla_{x}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
and
\nabla_{y}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
respectively. Its Hessian matrix at point
({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
can be partitioned as

\nabla^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=[\nabla_{xx}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}),f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}});\nabla_{yx}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}),\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})]
,
where
\nabla_{xx}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}\times d_{x}}
,
\nabla_{xy}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}\times d_{y}}
,
\nabla_{yx}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}\times d_{x}}
and
\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}\times d_{y}}
. We also denote

{\bf H}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=\nabla_{xx}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})-\nabla_{xy}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})(\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}))^{-1}\nabla_{yx}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
if
\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is invertible.

Given a symmetric matrix
{\bf A}
, we denote
\lambda_{\min}({\bf A})
as the smallest eigenvalue of
{\bf A}
.
We use
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
to denote the spectral norm of matrices and Euclidean norm of vectors. We also denote the closed Euclidean ball with radius
r
and center
{\bf{x}}^{*}
as
{\mathcal{B}}({\bf{x}}^{*},r)=\{{\bf{x}}:\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}\leq r\}
. Additionally, we use notation
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
to hide logarithmic terms in the complexity.

We suppose the objective function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
of Problem (
1
) satisfies the following assumptions.

Assumption 1
.

The function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
has
\ell
-Lipschitz gradients, i.e., there exists a constant
\ell>0
such that
\left\|\nabla f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\!-\!\nabla f({\bf{x}}^{\prime},{\bf{y}}^{\prime})\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\ell^{2}\big(\!\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{\bf{y}}-{\bf{y}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}\!\big)

for any
{\bf{x}},{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
and
{\bf{y}},{\bf{y}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
.

Assumption 2
.

The function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
has
\rho
-Lipschitz Hessian, i.e., there exists a constant
\rho>0
such that
\left\|\nabla^{2}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})-\nabla^{2}f({\bf{x}}^{\prime},{\bf{y}}^{\prime})\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\rho^{2}\big(\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{\bf{y}}-{\bf{y}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}\big)

for any
{\bf{x}},{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
and
{\bf{y}},{\bf{y}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
.

Assumption 3
.

The function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is
\mu
-strongly-concave in
{\bf{y}}
, i.e., there exists a constant
\mu>0
such that
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\leq f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}^{\prime})+\nabla_{y}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})^{\top}({\bf{y}}-{\bf{y}}^{\prime})-\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|{\bf{y}}-{\bf{y}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}
for any
{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
and
{\bf{y}},{\bf{y}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
.

Assumption 4
.

The function
P({\bf{x}})\triangleq\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
satisfies
P^{*}\triangleq\inf_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}}P({\bf{x}})>-\infty
.

Definition 1
.

Under Assumption
1
and
3
, we define the condition number of
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
as
\kappa\triangleq\ell/\mu
.

The assumptions of Lipschitz continuous gradient and strongly-concavity on
f
indicate that the primal function
P({\bf{x}})\triangleq\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is well-defined and has Lipschitz continuous gradients as shown in Lemma
1
.

Lemma 1

(
[
20
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

Suppose the objective function
f
satisfies Assumptions
1
and
3
, then the primal function
P({\bf{x}})\triangleq\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
has

(\kappa+1)\ell
-Lipschitz continuous gradients. Additionally, the function
{\bf{y}}^{*}({\bf{x}})=\argmax_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is well-defined and
\kappa
-Lipschitz. We also have
\nabla P({\bf{x}})=\nabla_{\bf{x}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}^{*}({\bf{x}}))
.

Now we give the definitions of
{\varepsilon}
-FSP and
({\varepsilon},\delta)
-SSP as follows.

Definition 2
.

Suppose the function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
satisfies Assumption
1
and
3
,
then we call
{\bf{x}}
an
{\varepsilon}
-FSP of
P({\bf{x}})
if
\left\|\nabla{P({\bf{x}})}\right\|_{2}\leq{\varepsilon}
.

Definition 3
.

Suppose the function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
satisfies Assumption
1
,
2
and
3
, then we call
{\bf{x}}
is an
({\varepsilon},\delta)
-SSP of
P({\bf{x}})
if
\left\|\nabla P({\bf{x}})\right\|_{2}\leq{\varepsilon}
and
\nabla^{2}P({\bf{x}})\succeq-\delta{\bf I}
.

The following two lemmas provide the closed form of
\nabla^{2}P({\bf{x}})
and its Lipschitz continuity.

Lemma 2

(
[
32
]
)
.

Suppose the function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
satisfies Assumption
1
,
2
and
3
.
We use the definition of
{\bf{y}}^{*}({\bf{x}})
in Lemma
1
, then it holds that
\nabla^{2}P({\bf{x}})={\bf H}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}^{*}({\bf{x}}))
.

Lemma 3
.

Under assumptions of Lemma
2
, we have
\left\|\nabla^{2}P({\bf{x}})-\nabla^{2}P({\bf{x}}^{\prime})\right\|_{2}\leq 4\sqrt{2}\kappa^{3}\rho\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}

for any
{\bf{x}}
and
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
.

2.2
Local Optimality of Minimax Optimization

The Nash equilibrium is widely used in the study of convex-concave minimax optimization
[
38
,
41
]
, but it is intractable in general when the objective function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is nonconvex in
{\bf{x}}
or nonconcave in
{\bf{y}}
.
For the general minimax problem, we introduce the local minimax point
[
17
]
, which characterizes the optimality in two-player sequential games where players can only change their strategies locally.

Definition 4

(
[
17
]
)
.

Given a differentiable function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}):{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that is strongly-concave in
{\bf{y}}
, a point
({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
is called a local minimax point of
f
, if there exists
\delta_{0}>0
and a function
h
satisfying
h(\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
, such that for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
,
{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\bf{x}}^{*},h(\delta))
and
{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
, we have

\displaystyle f({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}})\leq f({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})\leq\max_{{\bf{y}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}^{\prime}).

(3)

Remark 1
.

The definition of local minimax point for general nonconvex-nonconcave
[
17
]
only requires (
3
) holds for any
{\bf{y}}
and
{\bf{y}}^{\prime}
in a neighbour of
{\bf{y}}^{*}
, while the constraint on
{\bf{y}}
and
{\bf{y}}^{\prime}
is unnecessary in our setting since we assume that
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is strongly-concave in
{\bf{y}}
.

The local minimax point enjoys the following property.

Lemma 4

(
[
17
, Proposition 19]
)
.

Suppose
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is twice differentiable, then any point
({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})
satisfying
\nabla f({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})={\bf{0}}
,
\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})\prec{\bf{0}}
and
{\bf H}({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})\succ 0

is a local minimax point of
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
.

Based on Lemma
4
,
we introduce the strict-minimax condition on
f
which is an extension of strict-saddle condition for nonconvex minimization
[
10
,
11
,
35
,
34
,
3
,
16
]
.

Definition 5
.

Under Assumption
1
,
2
and
3
, we say
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-strict-minimax for some
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
and
\gamma>0
if every
({{\hat{\bf{x}}}},{{\hat{\bf{y}}}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
satisfies at least one of the following three conditions:
(a)
\left\|\nabla{f({{\hat{\bf{x}}}},{{\hat{\bf{y}}}})}\right\|_{2}>\alpha
;
(b)
\lambda_{\min}({\bf H}({{\hat{\bf{x}}}},{{\hat{\bf{y}}}}))<-\beta
;
(c) There exists a local minimax point
({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})
such that
\left\|{{\hat{\bf{x}}}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{{\hat{\bf{y}}}}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\gamma^{2}
.

Note that if
f
is
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-strict-minimax and there exists a point
{{\hat{\bf{x}}}}
which is an
({\varepsilon},\delta)
-SSP of
P({\bf{x}})
with sufficient small
{\varepsilon}
and
\delta
, then we can find
{{\hat{\bf{y}}}}\approx\argmax_{\bf{y}}f({{\hat{\bf{x}}}},{\bf{y}})
via running a first-order algorithm to minimize
-f({{\hat{\bf{x}}}},\cdot)
and obtain
({{\hat{\bf{x}}}},{{\hat{\bf{y}}}})
which is in a neighborhood of
({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})
. In other words, under the strict-minimax condition, we can reduce the task of finding approximate local minimax point of
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
to finding an approximate SSP of
P({\bf{x}})
. We will provide the formal statement in Section
3.2
.

Accelerated Gradient Descent

Algorithm 1

{{\rm AGD}}(h,{\bf{y}}_{0},K,\eta,\theta)

1:

\tilde{\bf{y}}_{0}={\bf{y}}_{0}

2:

for

k=0,\dots,K-1

do

3:

{\bf{y}}_{k+1}={\tilde{\bf{y}}}_{k}-\eta\nabla h(\tilde{\bf{y}}_{k})

4:

{\tilde{\bf{y}}}_{k+1}={\bf{y}}_{t+1}+\theta({\bf{y}}_{k+1}-{\bf{y}}_{k})

5:

end for

6:

Output:

{\bf{y}}_{K}

Nesterov’s accelerated gradient descent (AGD) is the optimal first-order algorithm for convex optimization
[
27
,
25
]
, which is widely used in minimax optimization algorithms
[
21
,
37
]
. We describe the details of AGD for smooth and strongly-convex functions in Algorithm
1
, which has the following convergence rate.

Lemma 5

(
[
37
, Lemma 2]
)
.

Running Algorithm
1
on a
\ell_{h}
-smooth and
\mu_{h}
-strongly-convex objective function
h(\cdot)
with parameters
\eta=1/\ell_{h}
and
\theta=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{h}}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{h}}+1}
produces the output

satisfying

\left\|{\bf{y}}_{K}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq(\kappa_{h}+1)\big(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{h}}}\big)^{K}\left\|{\bf{y}}_{0}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}
,
where
{\bf{y}}^{*}=\argmin_{y}h({\bf{y}})
and
\kappa_{h}=\ell_{h}/\mu_{h}
.

2.4
Cubic Regularized Newton

Cubic regularized Newton (CRN) is a classic algorithm for nonconvex minimization
[
26
,
25
,
5
,
6
,
36
]
.
It solves the nonconvex minimization problem

\min_{\bf{x}}g({\bf{x}})
via the following update rules

{\bf{s}}_{t}=\argmin_{{\bf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla g({\bf{x}}_{t})^{\top}{\bf{s}}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf{s}}^{\top}\nabla^{2}g({\bf{x}}_{t}){\bf{s}}+\frac{\rho_{g}}{6}\left\|{\bf{s}}\right\|_{2}^{3},~~~~~~{\bf{x}}_{t+1}={\bf{x}}_{t}+{\bf{s}}_{t}.

The CRN method could find an
\big({\varepsilon},\sqrt{\rho_{g}{\varepsilon}}\,\big)
-SSP of
g({\bf{x}})
with
{\mathcal{O}}\big(\sqrt{\rho_{g}}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5}\big)
number of iterations, where
\rho_{g}
is the Lipschitz continuous constant of the Hessian of
g({\bf{x}})
.

3
Minimax Cubic Newton Algorithm

In this section, we propose our minimax cubic Newton algorithm and give its convergence results.

3.1
Minimax Cubic Newton Method

Algorithm 2

Minimax Cubic-Newton (MCN)

1:

Input:

{\bf{x}}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
,
{\bf{y}}_{-1}={\bf{0}}
,
T
,
\{K_{t}\}_{t=0}^{T}
,
{\varepsilon}
.

2:

for

t=0,\cdots T-1

do

3:

{\bf{y}}_{t}={{\rm AGD}}\left(-f({\bf{x}}_{t},\cdot),{\bf{y}}_{t-1},K_{t},\frac{1}{\ell},\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)

4:

{\bf{g}}_{t}=\nabla_{x}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})
,
{\bf H}_{t}={\bf H}({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})

5:

{\bf{s}}_{t}^{*}=\argmin\limits_{{\bf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}}\left({\bf{g}}_{t}^{\top}{\bf{s}}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf{s}}^{\top}{\bf H}_{t}{\bf{s}}+\frac{M}{6}\left\|{\bf{s}}\right\|_{2}^{3}\right)

6:

if

\left\|{\bf{s}}_{t}^{*}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{{\varepsilon}/M}

then

7:

8:

end for

9:

{{\hat{\bf{x}}}}={\bf{x}}_{t}+{\bf{s}}_{t}^{*}

We present the details of Minimax Cubic Newton (MCN) method in Algorithm
2
. In each round, the MCN algorithm performs following steps:

•

Run AGD as presented in Algorithm
1
to estimate
{\bf{y}}_{t}\approx{\bf{y}}^{*}({\bf{x}}_{t})=\argmax_{\bf{y}}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}})
.

•

Compute the inexact first-order and second-order information of
P
at
{\bf{x}}_{t}
as

\displaystyle\nabla P({\bf{x}}_{t})\approx{\bf{g}}_{t}=\nabla_{x}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla^{2}P({\bf{x}}_{t})\approx{\bf H}_{t}={\bf H}({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t}).

•

Solve the following cubic regularized problem

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt{\bf{s}}_{t}^{*}{=}\argmin_{{\bf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}}\left({\bf{g}}_{t}^{\top}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf{s}}^{\top}{\bf H}_{t}+\frac{M}{6}\left\|{\bf{s}}\right\|_{2}^{3}\right).

(4)

The expressions of
{\bf{g}}_{t}
and
{\bf H}_{t}
in the algorithm are inspired from Lemma
1
and
3
. The smoothness of
\nabla P({\bf{x}})
and
\nabla^{2}P({\bf{x}})
allow the total complexity of AGD iteration of the algorithm has the desired upper bound.
Our theoretical analysis show the termination condition in Line 7 of Algorithm
2
can be attained in no more than
{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5})
number of iterations. We also show that a small
\left\|{\bf{s}}_{t}^{*}\right\|_{2}
will lead to a desired approximate second-order stationary point of
P({\bf{x}})
. Hence, using AGD to optimize
f({\bf{x}}_{t}+{\bf{s}}_{t}^{*},{\bf{y}})
with respect to
{\bf{y}}
generates an approximate local minimax point of
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
.

3.2
Complexity Analysis for MCN

In this section, we let
M=4\sqrt{2}\kappa^{3}\rho
for MCN. Our analysis for MCN algorithm contains three parts:

1.

We follow
Tripuraneni et al. 2018
’s idea to show that our algorithm with sufficient accurate gradient and Hessian estimator of
P({\bf{x}})
requires no more than
T={\mathcal{O}}\left(\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5}\right)
rounds of iterations to achieve an
\left({\varepsilon},\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho{\varepsilon}}\,\right)
-SSP of
P({\bf{x}})
.

2.

We prove the AGD step in line 3 requires at most
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\kappa^{2}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5}\right)
gradient calls in total.

3.

The last part shows we can achieve an approximate local minimax point of
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})

6
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we study second-order optimization methods for nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax problems.
We have proposed a novel algorithm so-called minimax cubic Newton (MCN) which could find an
({\varepsilon},\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho{\varepsilon}}\,)
-SSP of the primal function with
{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{1.5}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5})
second-order oracle calls and
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{2}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5})
gradient oracle calls.
We also provide an efficient algorithm for high dimensional problem, which avoids accessing second-order oracle and contains
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{1.5}\ell{\varepsilon}^{-2})
Hessian-vector oracle calls and
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{2}\sqrt{\rho}{\varepsilon}^{-1.5})
gradient oracle calls.
To best of our knowledge, this paper first achieves non-asymptotic convergence result for finding SSP of minimax problem without convex-concave assumption.

There are several interesting problems for future work:
(a) The proposed algorithms and analysis require the strongly convexity assumption on
{\bf{y}}
. We would like to study how to find SSPs for general nonconvex-concave minimax problems.
(b) The upper complexity bounds of proposed algorithms look not optimal.
It is possible to apply the acceleration techniques to establish more efficient algorithms for our task.
(c) The implementations of proposed IMCN still require accessing the Hessian-vector oracle. It is interesting to investigate how to find SSPs of our minimax problem by pure first-order algorithms.
(d) This paper does not consider the specific structure of the objective function.
However, many machine learning models can be formulated as minimax problems where the objective functions have finite-sum or expectation form.
Designing efficient stochastic algorithms for such formulations is an interesting problem to the machine learning community.

Appendix E
Implementation of IMCN

In the implementation of IMCN (the cubic sub-problem solver), all of steps related to
{\bf H}_{t}
can be view as computing Hessian-vector product of the form

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{t}{\bf{u}}^{\prime}=\left(\nabla_{xx}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})+\nabla_{xy}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})\left(\frac{c_{0}}{4\ell}{\bf I}+\frac{1}{2\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}c_{k}{\bf T}_{k}({\bf Z}_{t})\right)\nabla_{yx}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})\right){\bf{u}}^{\prime},

(31)

where
{\bf{u}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
is given. Recall that Chebyshev polynomial satisfies

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{k}({\bf Z}_{t})=2{\bf Z}_{t}{\bf T}_{k-1}({\bf Z}_{t})-{\bf T}_{k-2}({\bf Z}_{t}),

which allows us computing (
31
) without constructing any Hessian matrix explicitly. Concretely, For fixed
t
, we define

\displaystyle{\bf{u}}=\nabla_{yx}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t}){\bf{u}}^{\prime},\quad{\bf B}_{k}=\frac{c_{0}}{4\ell}{\bf I}+\frac{1}{2\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}{\bf T}_{k}({\bf Z}_{t}),\quad{\bf{v}}_{k}={\bf T}_{k}({\bf Z}_{t}){\bf{u}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\bf{w}}_{k}={\bf B}_{k}{\bf{u}}.

Since we have
{\bf Z}_{t}=\frac{4\ell}{\ell-\mu}\left(-\frac{1}{2\ell}\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})-\frac{\ell+\mu}{4\ell}{\bf I}\right)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf{w}}_{k}=&\left({\bf B}_{k-1}+c_{k}{\bf T}_{k}({\bf Z}_{t})\right){\bf{u}}\\
=&{\bf{w}}_{k-1}+c_{k}\left(2{\bf Z}_{t}{\bf T}_{k-1}({\bf Z}_{t})-{\bf T}_{k-2}({\bf Z}_{t})\right){\bf{u}}\\
=&{\bf{w}}_{k-1}+c_{k}\left(\frac{8\ell}{\ell-\mu}\left(-\frac{1}{2\ell}\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})-\frac{\ell+\mu}{4\ell}{\bf I}\right){\bf{v}}_{k-1}-{\bf{v}}_{k-2}\right)\\
=&{\bf{w}}_{k-1}-\frac{4c_{k}}{\ell-\mu}\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t}){\bf{v}}_{k-1}-\frac{2c_{k}(\ell+\mu)}{\ell-\mu}{\bf{v}}_{k-1}-c_{k}{\bf{v}}_{k-2}\\
\end{split}

(32)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf{v}}_{k}=&{\bf T}_{k}({\bf Z}_{t}){\bf{u}}\\
=&2{\bf Z}_{t}{\bf T}_{k-1}({\bf Z}_{t}){\bf{u}}-{\bf T}_{k-2}({\bf Z}_{t}){\bf{u}}\\
=&\frac{8\ell}{\ell-\mu}\left(-\frac{1}{2\ell}\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t})-\frac{\ell+\mu}{4\ell}{\bf I}\right){\bf{v}}_{k-1}-{\bf{v}}_{k-2}\\
=&-\frac{4}{\ell-\mu}\nabla_{yy}^{2}f({\bf{x}}_{t},{\bf{y}}_{t}){\bf{v}}_{k-1}-\frac{2(\ell+\mu)}{\ell-\mu}{\bf{v}}_{k-1}-{\bf{v}}_{k-2}.\end{split}

(33)

Hence, based on update rules (
32
) and (
33
), we can obtain
{\bf H}_{t}{\bf{u}}^{\prime}
with
{\mathcal{O}}(K^{\prime})=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{\kappa})
Hessian-vector calls. Additionally, this strategy avoids
{\mathcal{O}}\left(d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}\right)
space to keep Hessian matrices.

Experimental Details

In this section, we present the details of both synthetic minimax problem and the setting of domain adaptation experiments.

G.1
Details of Synthetic Minimax Problem

Our synthetic experiment are based on the following nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax problem

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=w(x_{3})-\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{40}+x_{1}y_{1}-\frac{5y_{2}^{2}}{2}+x_{2}y_{2},

where
{\bf{x}}=[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]^{\top}
,
{\bf{y}}=[y_{1},y_{2}]^{\top}
and

\displaystyle w(x)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\epsilon}\left(x+(L+1)\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(x+(L+1)\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)^{3}-\frac{1}{3}(3L+1)\epsilon^{3/2},&x\leq-L\sqrt{\epsilon};\\
\epsilon x+\frac{\epsilon^{3/2}}{3},&-L\sqrt{\epsilon}<x\leq-\sqrt{\epsilon};\\
-\sqrt{\epsilon}x^{2}-\frac{x^{3}}{3},&-\sqrt{\epsilon}<x\leq 0;\\
-\sqrt{\epsilon}x^{2}+\frac{x^{3}}{3},&0<x\leq\sqrt{\epsilon};\\
-\epsilon x+\frac{\epsilon^{3/2}}{3},&\sqrt{\epsilon}<x\leq L\sqrt{\epsilon};\\
\sqrt{\epsilon}\left(x-(L+1)\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(x-(L+1)\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)^{3}-\frac{1}{3}(3L+1)\epsilon^{3/2},&L\sqrt{\epsilon}\leq x.\\
\end{cases}

The nonconvex function
w(x)
is designed by
Tripuraneni et al. 2018
and we set
\epsilon=0.01
and
L=5
in our experiment.
We can verify that

\displaystyle{\bf{y}}^{*}({\bf{x}})=\begin{bmatrix}20x_{1}\\[2.84544pt]
\dfrac{x_{2}}{5}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad P(x)=w(x_{3})+10x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{10}x_{2}^{2}.

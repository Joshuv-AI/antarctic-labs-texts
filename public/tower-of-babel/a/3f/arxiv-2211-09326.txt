Inadmissibility of the corrected Akaike information criterion
Takeru Matsuda

Abstract
For the multivariate linear regression model with unknown covariance, the corrected Akaike information criterion is the minimum variance unbiased estimator of the expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy. In this study, based on the loss estimation framework, we show its inadmissibility as an estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy itself, instead of the expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy. We provide improved estimators of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy that work well in reduced-rank situations and examine their performance numerically.

Introduction

We consider the multivariate linear regression model with
p
explanatory variables and
q
response variables:

\displaystyle y_{i}=B^{\top}x_{i}+\varepsilon_{i},\quad\varepsilon_{i}\sim{\rm N}_{q}(0,\Sigma),

(1)

for
i=1,\dots,n
, where
n\geq p
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
is an unknown regression coefficient matrix,
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
is an unknown covariance matrix (positive definite) and
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}
are independent.
In the following, the probability density function of
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times q}
is denoted by
p(Y\mid B,\Sigma)
and the expectation of
f(Y)
under
p(Y\mid B,\Sigma)
is written as
{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[f(Y)]
.

The maximum likelihood estimate for the model (
1
) is given by

\displaystyle\hat{B}=(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}Y,\quad\hat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{n}(Y-X\hat{B})^{\top}(Y-X\hat{B}).

The Akaike Information Criterion
(
Akaike 1973
, AIC;)
is an approximately unbiased estimator of the expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy:

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[{\rm AIC}]={\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))]+o(1)

as
n\to\infty
, where

\displaystyle d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))

\displaystyle=-2\int p(\widetilde{Y}\mid B,\Sigma)\log p(\widetilde{Y}\mid\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}){\rm d}\widetilde{Y}

(2)

\displaystyle=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det\hat{\Sigma}+n{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}^{-1}\Sigma)+{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}^{-1}(\hat{B}-B)^{\top}X^{\top}X(\hat{B}-B))

is called the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy from
p(\widetilde{Y}\mid B,\Sigma)
to
p(\widetilde{Y}\mid\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma})
.
The AIC is widely used for evaluation and selection of linear regression models
(
Burnham and Anderson 2002
;
Konishi and Kitagawa 2008
)
.

The bias of the AIC is non-negligible when the sample size
n
is not sufficiently large. Thus, a corrected AIC (AICc) has been derived
(
Sugiura 1978
;
Hurvich and Tsai 1989
;
Bedrick and Tsai 1994
)
,
which is exactly unbiased:

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[{\rm AICc}]={\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))].

Cavanaugh 2006
provided a unified derivation of AIC and AICc and
Davies et al. 2006
showed that AICc is the minimum variance unbiased estimator of the expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.

Both AIC and AICc were developed to unbiasedly estimate the expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.
This idea dates back to Stein’s unbiased risk estimate
(
Stein 1973
, SURE;)
, which unbiasedly estimates the quadratic risk of estimators of a normal mean
(
Lehmann and Casella 2006
;
Fourdrinier et al. 2018
)
.
In this context,
Johnstone 1988
considered estimation of the quadratic loss itself, instead of its average (quadratic risk).
Although SURE is still unbiased for this problem,
Johnstone 1988
showed that it can be improved in terms of the mean squared error.
In other words, SURE is inadmissible as an estimator of the quadratic loss.
See Section
2
for details.
This finding led to the development of a field called loss estimation
(
Fourdrinier and Wells 2012
)
.

In this study, we examine AIC and AICc from the loss estimation viewpoint and investigate their admissibility as estimators of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
, instead of its average
{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))]
(expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy).
The former estimand is random (depends on
Y
as well as
(B,\Sigma)
) whereas the latter one is non-random (depends only on
(B,\Sigma)
).
In this sense, it may be more correct to refer to the current problem as prediction rather than estimation
1
1

1

Similarly,
Lehmann and Casella 2006
states that it is common to speak of prediction, rather than estimation, of random effects (Example 3.5.5). See also
Sandved 1968
.
The current setting to estimate (or predict)
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
is considered to reflect the practical usage of AIC (and AICc) as a model evaluation criterion more faithfully as follows.
Given data at hand, we estimate
(B,\Sigma)
by
(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma})
and construct the plug-in predictive distribution
p(\widetilde{Y}\mid\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma})
for a future observation.
The disparity between this predictive distribution and the true data-generating distribution
p(\widetilde{Y}\mid{B},{\Sigma})
is given by the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
.
Whereas the usual argument on AIC (and AICc) considers estimation of the average of
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
over the possible realizations of
Y
(expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy), here we focus on estimation (or prediction) of
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
itself for the specific realization of
Y
at hand.
Thus, the current setting provides direct (conditional) assessment of the performance of the predictive distribution obtained from the data at hand.
Note that
Matsuda and Strawderman 2016
studied the Pitman closeness property of predictive distributions in a similar spirit.
We develop improved estimators of
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
and show that they attain better variable selection result than AIC and AICc in simulation.
It demonstrates a practical advantage of introducing the current setting.
See
Fourdrinier and Wells 2012
for further discussion on motivation for considering loss estimation.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we briefly review the loss estimation framework and existing results for normal mean vector and matrix.
We also derive an improved loss estimator for a normal mean matrix, which will be the basis of the main results of this paper.
In Section
3
, we introduce the general setting of loss estimation for a predictive distribution and study the properties of AIC and AICc as loss estimators in multivariate linear regression.
For the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with known covariance, AIC is shown to be inadmissible and an improved loss estimator is given.
For the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with unknown covariance, AIC is shown to be inadmissible and dominated by AICc.
Then, in Section
4
, we prove that AICc is still inadmissible and provide improved loss estimators that work well in reduced-rank situations.
In Section
5
, we present numerical results to examine the performance of the improved estimators.
The results demonstrate that the improved estimators often outperform the corrected AIC in variable selection.
Finally, we provide concluding remarks in Section
6
.
Technical lemmas are given with proofs in the Appendix.

2
Loss estimation framework

2.1
General setting

Here, we briefly introduce the loss estimation framework.
See
Fourdrinier and Wells 2012
;
Fourdrinier et al. 2018
for a comprehensive review of loss estimation.
Recently, the idea of loss estimation has been applied to high-dimensional inference
(
Bellec and Zhang 2021
)
.

Suppose that we have an observation
Y\sim p(y\mid\theta)
, where
\theta
is an unknown parameter.
In usual setting of point estimation
(
Lehmann and Casella 2006
)
, we consider estimation of
\theta
using an estimator
\hat{\theta}=\hat{\theta}(y)
.
The discrepancy of an estimate
\hat{\theta}
from the true value
\theta
is quantified by a loss function
L(\theta,\hat{\theta})
.
Then, estimators are compared by using the risk function
R(\theta,\hat{\theta})={\rm E}_{\theta}[L(\theta,\hat{\theta}(y))]
, which is the average of the loss. An estimator
\hat{\theta}_{1}
is said to dominate another estimator
\hat{\theta}_{2}
if
R(\theta,\hat{\theta}_{1})\leq R(\theta,\hat{\theta}_{2})
holds for every
\theta
, with strict inequality for at least one value of
\theta
.
An estimator
\hat{\theta}
is said to be admissible if no estimator dominates
\hat{\theta}
. An estimator
\hat{\theta}
is said to be inadmissible if it is not admissible (i.e. there exists an estimator that dominates
\hat{\theta}
).

In the setting above, loss estimation concerns estimation of the loss
L(\theta,\hat{\theta}(y))
, which depends not only on
\theta
but also on
y
.
The performance of a loss estimator
\lambda(y)
is evaluated by squared error
(\lambda(y)-L(\theta,\hat{\theta}(y)))^{2}
.
Thus, a loss estimator
\lambda_{1}(y)
is said to dominate another loss estimator
\lambda_{2}(y)
if

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{\theta}[(\lambda_{1}(y)-L(\theta,\hat{\theta}(y)))^{2}]\leq{\rm E}_{\theta}[(\lambda_{2}(y)-L(\theta,\hat{\theta}(y)))^{2}]

holds for every
\theta
, with strict inequality for at least one value of
\theta
.
A loss estimator
\lambda(y)
is said to be admissible if no loss estimator dominates
\lambda(y)
.
A loss estimator
\lambda(y)
is said to be inadmissible if it is not admissible (i.e. there exists a loss estimator that dominates
\lambda(y)
).

2.2
Loss estimation for a normal mean vector

Now, we focus on loss estimation for a normal mean vector
(
Johnstone 1988
)
.
Suppose that we estimate
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
from an observation
Y\sim{\rm N}_{p}(\theta,I_{p})
by an estimator
\hat{\theta}(y)=y+g(y)
under the quadratic loss

\displaystyle L(\theta,\hat{\theta})=\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}.

Stein 1973
showed that the quadratic risk
R(\theta,\hat{\theta})={\rm E}_{\theta}[L(\theta,\hat{\theta}(y))]
satisfies

\displaystyle R(\theta,\hat{\theta})={\rm E}_{\theta}[\lambda^{{\rm U}}(y)],

where

\displaystyle\lambda^{{\rm U}}(y)=p+2\nabla\cdot g(y)+\|g(y)\|^{2}

is called Stein’s unbiased risk estimate (SURE).
SURE plays a central role in the theory of shrinkage estimation
(
Fourdrinier et al. 2018
)
and also closely related to model selection criteria such as Mallows’
C_{p}
and AIC
(
Boisbunon et al. 2014
)
.
However,
Johnstone 1988
showed that SURE is inadmissible for the maximum likelihood estimator when
p\geq 5
as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
Johnstone 1988
)

In estimation of
\theta
from
Y\sim{\rm N}_{p}(\theta,I_{p})
under the quadratic loss, consider the maximum likelihood estimator
\hat{\theta}(y)=y
.
If
p\geq 5
, then SURE
\lambda^{{\rm U}}(y)=p
is inadmissible and dominated by the loss estimator

\displaystyle\lambda(y)=p-2(p-4)\|y\|^{-2}.

(3)

Figure
1
plots the percentage improvements in mean sqaured error of the loss estimator (
3
) over SURE defined by

\displaystyle 100\frac{{\rm E}_{\theta}[(\lambda^{{\rm U}}(y)-\|\hat{\theta}(y)-\theta\|^{2})^{2}]-{\rm E}_{\theta}[(\lambda(y)-\|\hat{\theta}(y)-\theta\|^{2})^{2}]}{{\rm E}_{\theta}[(\lambda^{{\rm U}}(y)-\|\hat{\theta}(y)-\theta\|^{2})^{2}]}.

The improvement is large when the true value of
\theta
is close to the origin, which is qualitatively similar to the risk behavior of the James–Stein estimator.
In addition to the maximum likelihood estimator,
Johnstone 1988
also proved the inadmissibility of SURE for the James–Stein estimator and provided improved loss estimators.
Based on these findings by
Johnstone 1988
, many studies have investigated loss estimation for a normal mean vector and single-response linear regression, such as
(
Boisbunon et al. 2014
;
Fourdrinier et al. 2003
;
Fourdrinier and Wells 2012
;
Lu and Berger 1989
;
Narayanan and Wells 2015
;
Wan and Zou 2004
)
.

2.3
Loss estimation for a normal mean matrix

Recently,
Matsuda and Strawderman 2019
generalized the results of
Johnstone 1988
to matrices and developed loss estimators that dominate SURE.
This is motivated from the Efron–Morris estimator, which is a matrix generalization of the James–Stein estimator that shrinks singular values towards zero
(
Efron and Morris 1972
)
.
Specifically, suppose that we estimate
M\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
from an observation
Y\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(M,I_{p},I_{q})
by an estimator
\hat{M}(Y)
under the Frobenius loss

\displaystyle L(M,\hat{M})=\|\hat{M}-M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}(\hat{M}_{ij}-M_{ij})^{2}.

We write the singular values of a matrix
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
with
p\geq q
by
\sigma_{1}(Z)\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{q}(Z)\geq 0
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
Matsuda and Strawderman 2019
)

In estimation of
M
from
Y\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(M,I_{p},I_{q})
under the Frobenius loss, consider the maximum likelihood estimator
\hat{M}(Y)=Y
.
If
p\geq 3q+2
and
q\geq 2
, then SURE
\lambda^{{\rm U}}(Y)=pq
is inadmissible and dominated by the loss estimator

\displaystyle\lambda(Y)=pq-\sum_{i=1}^{q}c_{i}\sigma_{i}(Y)^{-2},\quad c_{i}=\frac{4(p-q-2i-1)}{q}.

(4)

Whereas Proposition
2.2
shows the inadmissibility of SURE, it excludes
2

2

The condition
q\geq 2
in Proposition
2.2
was not explicitly stated in the original paper

)
.
the case

.
Here, we provide another loss estimator dominating SURE, which reduces to (

Theorem 2.1
.

In estimation of
M
from
Y\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(M,I_{p},I_{q})
under the Frobenius loss, consider the maximum likelihood estimator
\hat{M}(Y)=Y
.
If
p\geq 2q+3
, then SURE
\lambda^{{\rm U}}(Y)=pq
is inadmissible and dominated by the loss estimator

\displaystyle\lambda(Y)=pq-\frac{2(p-2q-2)}{q}{\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-1}).

(5)

Proof.

Let

with

so that
\lambda(Y)=\lambda^{\mathrm{U}}(Y)+h(Y)
.

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}[(\lambda(Y)-\|\hat{M}(Y)-M\|^{2})^{2}]-{\rm E}_{M}[(\lambda^{\mathrm{U}}(Y)-\|\hat{M}(Y)-M\|^{2})^{2}]={\rm E}_{M}[-2\Delta h(Y)+h(Y)^{2}],

where

\displaystyle\Delta h(Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial Y_{ij}^{2}}(Y).

From Lemma
A.2
and Lemma

\displaystyle\Delta h(Y)

\displaystyle=-c\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial Y_{ij}^{2}}{\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-1})

\displaystyle=2c\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{ij}}(Y(Y^{\top}Y)^{-2})_{ij}

\displaystyle=2c(p-q-2){\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-2})-2c({\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-1}))^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle-2\Delta h(Y)+h(Y)^{2}

\displaystyle=-4c(p-q-2){\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-2})+(c^{2}+4c)({\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-1}))^{2}.

From the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle({\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-1}))^{2}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_{i}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-1})\right)^{2}\leq q\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_{i}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-1})^{2}=q{\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-2}),

(6)

where
\lambda_{i}(A)
denotes the
i

Therefore, by substituting

\displaystyle-2\Delta h(Y)+h(Y)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c(-4(p-q-2)+q(c+4)){\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{4(p-2q-2)^{2}}{q}{\rm tr}((Y^{\top}Y)^{-2})

\displaystyle<0.

Hence,

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}[(\lambda(Y)-\|\hat{M}(Y)-M\|^{2})^{2}]<{\rm E}_{M}[(\lambda^{\mathrm{U}}(Y)-\|\hat{M}(Y)-M\|^{2})^{2}]

for every
M
.

3
Information criterion as loss estimator

3.1
Loss estimation for a predictive distribution

Suppose that we have an observation
Y\sim p(y\mid\theta)
, where
\theta
is an unknown parameter.
Then, we consider prediction of a future observation
\widetilde{Y}\sim p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta)
by using a predictive distribution
\hat{p}(\widetilde{y}\mid y)
.
The discrepancy of a predictive distribution
\hat{p}(\widetilde{y}\mid y)
from the true distribution
p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta)
is evaluated by the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy

\displaystyle d(p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta),\hat{p}(\widetilde{y}\mid y))

\displaystyle=-2\int p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta)\log{\hat{p}(\widetilde{y}\mid y)}{\rm d}\widetilde{y},

which is equivalent to twice the Kullback–Leibler divergence

\displaystyle D(p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta),\hat{p}(\widetilde{y}\mid y))

\displaystyle=\int p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta)\log\frac{p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta)}{\hat{p}(\widetilde{y}\mid y)}{\rm d}\widetilde{y}

up to an additive constant. The plug-in predictive distribution is defined by
p(\widetilde{y}\mid\hat{\theta}(y))
, where
\hat{\theta}(y)
is the maximum likelihood estimate of
\theta
from
y
.
AIC
(
Akaike 1973
)
is an approximately unbiased estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy for the plug-in predictive distribution:

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{\theta}[\mathrm{AIC}]\approx d(p(\widetilde{y}\mid\theta),{p}(\widetilde{y}\mid\hat{\theta}(y))).

See
Burnham and Anderson 2002
;
Konishi and Kitagawa 2008
for details.

Similarly to point estimation in Section
2
, we can formulate estimation of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy as a loss estimation problem.
Then, AIC can be viewed as a default loss estimator like SURE in estimation of a normal mean.
From this viewpoint, it is of interest to determine whether AIC is admissible or not.
In the following, we investigate this problem for the multivariate linear regression model (
1
).

3.2
Multivariate linear regression with known covariance

First, consider the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with known covariance
\Sigma\succ O
.
The maximum likelihood estimate is
\hat{B}=(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}Y
.
From (
2
), the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy for the plug-in predictive distribution is

\displaystyle d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\Sigma))=nq\log(2\pi e)+n\log\det{\Sigma}+{\rm tr}({\Sigma}^{-1}(\hat{B}-B)^{\top}X^{\top}X(\hat{B}-B)).

The AIC is

\displaystyle{\rm AIC}

\displaystyle=-2\log p(Y\mid\hat{B},\Sigma)+2pq

\displaystyle=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det{\Sigma}+{\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}(Y-X\hat{B})^{\top}(Y-X\hat{B}))+2pq.

Then, the inadmissibility of AIC is proved as follows, where MAIC is an abbreviation of “Modified AIC.”

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with known
\Sigma\succ O
.
If
p\geq 2q+3
, then AIC is inadmissible and dominated by

\displaystyle{\rm MAIC}={\rm AIC}-\frac{2(p-2q-2)}{q}{\rm tr}(\Sigma((X\hat{B})^{\top}(X\hat{B}))^{-1})

as an estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.

Proof.

Let
R=(Y-X\hat{B})^{\top}(Y-X\hat{B})
be the residual.
Then, from the standard theory of multivariate linear regression
(
Anderson 2003
)
,
\hat{B}
and
R
are independent and distributed as
\hat{B}\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(B,(X^{\top}X)^{-1},\Sigma)
and
R\sim W_{q}(n-p,\Sigma)
, respectively.
Thus,
Z=(X^{\top}X)^{1/2}\hat{B}\Sigma^{-1/2}
is independent from
R
and distributed as
Z\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(\bar{Z},I_{p},I_{q})
where
\bar{Z}=(X^{\top}X)^{1/2}{B}\Sigma^{-1/2}
.

Let
h=-c{\rm tr}(\Sigma((X\hat{B})^{\top}(X\hat{B}))^{-1})=-c{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})
with
c=2(p-2q-2)/q
so that
{\rm MAIC}={\rm AIC}+h(Y)
.
Then,

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B}[({\rm MAIC}-d)^{2}]-{\rm E}_{B}[({\rm AIC}-d)^{2}]

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{B}[h^{2}+2h({\rm AIC}-d)],

where we write
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\Sigma))
by
d
for simplicity.
Note that

\displaystyle{\rm AIC}-d

\displaystyle=(2p-n)q+{\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}(Y-X\hat{B})^{\top}(Y-X\hat{B}))-{\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}(\hat{B}-B)^{\top}X^{\top}X(\hat{B}-B))

\displaystyle=(2p-n)q+{\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}R)-{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})).

From
{\rm E}[R]=(n-p)\Sigma
and the independence of
Z
and
R
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}){\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}R)]=(n-p)q\cdot{\rm E}_{B}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})].

Also, from Lemma
B.8
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}){\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z}))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle pq{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]-2(p-q-2){\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]+2{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B}[({\rm MAIC}-d)^{2}]-{\rm E}_{B}[({\rm AIC}-d)^{2}]

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{B}[(c^{2}+4c)({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}-4c(p-q-2){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]

\displaystyle\leq qc\left(c-\frac{4(p-2q-2)}{q}\right){\rm E}_{B}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]

\displaystyle<0

for every
B
, where we used
{({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}}\leq q{{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})}
from (
6
) and
c=2(p-2q-2)/q
.
∎

For the Gaussian linear regression model with known variance (
q=1
),
Boisbunon et al. 2014
discussed the equivalence between AIC and SURE.
Such a correspondence holds in the current setting as well.
Specifically, consider estimation of
M
from
Y\sim{\rm N}_{n,q}(M,I_{n},\Sigma)
under the loss
L(M,\hat{M})={\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M))
.
Then, SURE for the estimator
\hat{M}=X\hat{B}
is

\displaystyle\lambda^{{\rm U}}(Y)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}(Y-X\hat{B})^{\top}(Y-X\hat{B}))+(2p-n)q

\displaystyle={\rm AIC}-nq\log(2\pi e)-n\log\det{\Sigma},

and the loss is related to the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy as

\displaystyle L(M,\hat{M})=d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\Sigma))-nq\log(2\pi e)-n\log\det{\Sigma}.

Thus, estimation of the loss
L(M,\hat{M})
for the estimator
\hat{M}=X\hat{B}
is equivalent to estimation of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy for the plug-in predictive distribution, and both SURE and AIC are exactly unbiased.
Under this correspondence, Proposition
3.1
is rewritten as follows.

Corollary 3.1
.

For the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with known
\Sigma\succ O
, consider the estimator
\hat{M}=X\hat{B}
of
M=XB
under the loss
L(M,\hat{M})={\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M))
.
If
p\geq 2q+3
, then SURE is inadmissible and dominated by the loss estimator

\displaystyle\lambda(Y)=\lambda^{{\rm U}}(Y)-\frac{2(p-2q-2)}{q}{\rm tr}(\Sigma((X\hat{B})^{\top}(X\hat{B}))^{-1}).

3.3
Multivariate linear regression with unknown covariance

Next, consider the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with unknown covariance
\Sigma\succ O
.
The maximum likelihood estimate is
\hat{B}=(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}Y
and
\hat{\Sigma}=(Y-X\hat{B})^{\top}(Y-X\hat{B})/n
.
From (
2
), the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy is

\displaystyle d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det\hat{\Sigma}+n{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}^{-1}\Sigma)+{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}^{-1}(\hat{B}-B)^{\top}X^{\top}X(\hat{B}-B)).

The AIC is

\displaystyle{\rm AIC}=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det\hat{\Sigma}+2\left(pq+\frac{q(q+1)}{2}\right).

The corrected AIC is

\displaystyle{\rm AICc}=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det\hat{\Sigma}+\frac{2n}{n-p-q-1}\left(pq+\frac{q(q+1)}{2}\right).

The corrected AIC is exactly unbiased while AIC is biased
(
Hurvich and Tsai 1989
;
Sugiura 1978
;
Bedrick and Tsai 1994
)
.
Then, we obtain the following.

Theorem 3.2
.

For the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with unknown
\Sigma\succ O
, AIC is inadmissible and dominated by AICc as an estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.

Proof.

For two random variables
S
and
T
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}[(S-T)^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}[(S-{\rm E}[S]+{\rm E}[S]-{\rm E}[T]+{\rm E}[T]-T)^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}[(S-{\rm E}[S])^{2}]+({\rm E}[S]-{\rm E}[T])^{2}+{\rm E}[(T-{\rm E}[T])^{2}]-2{\rm E}[(S-{\rm E}[S])(T-{\rm E}[T])]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Var}[S]+({\rm E}[S]-{\rm E}[T])^{2}+{\rm Var}[T]-2{\rm Cov}[S,T].

Hence, the mean squared error of AIC is given by

\displaystyle{\rm E}[({\rm AIC}-d)^{2}]={\rm Var}[{\rm AIC}]+({\rm E}[{\rm AIC}]-{\rm E}[d])^{2}+{\rm Var}[d]-2{\rm Cov}[{\rm AIC},d],

(7)

where we write
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
by
d
for simplicity.
Similarly, the mean squared error of AICc is

\displaystyle{\rm E}[({\rm AICc}-d)^{2}]={\rm Var}[{\rm AICc}]+{\rm Var}[d]-2{\rm Cov}[{\rm AICc},d],

(8)

where we used
{\rm E}[{\rm AICc}]={\rm E}[d]
.

On the other hand, since the difference between AIC and AICc is constant,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}[{\rm AIC}]={\rm Var}[{\rm AICc}],\quad{\rm Cov}[{\rm AIC},d]={\rm Cov}[{\rm AICc},d].

(9)

From (
7
), (
8
) and (
9
),

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm AICc}-d)^{2}]\leq{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm AIC}-d)^{2}]

for every
B
and
\Sigma
.
∎

In the next section, we show that the corrected AIC is still inadmissible and provide improved loss estimators.
Note that
Davies et al. 2006
showed that the corrected AIC is the minimum variance unbiased estimator of the expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.

4
Inadmissibility of the corrected AIC

For the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with unknown covariance, the corrected AIC is the minimum variance unbiased estimator of the expected Kullback–Leibler discrepancy from the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem
(
Davies et al. 2006
)
.
Also, Theorem
3.2
showed that the AIC is dominated by the corrected AIC as an estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.
However, the corrected AIC is still inadmissible as follows, where MAICc is an abbreviation of “Modified AICc.”

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider the multivariate linear regression model (
1
) with unknown
\Sigma\succ O
.
Let

\displaystyle\bar{c}=\frac{4n^{2}}{(n-p)(q(n-p)+2)}\left(p-2q-2-\frac{q^{2}+q-2}{n-p-q-1}\right).

If
n-p-q-1>0
and
\bar{c}>0
, then for any
c\in(0,\bar{c}]
, AICc is inadmissible and dominated by

\displaystyle{\rm MAICc}={\rm AICc}-c{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}((X\hat{B})^{\top}(X\hat{B}))^{-1})

(10)

as an estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.

Proof.

From the standard theory of multivariate linear regression
(
Anderson 2003
)
, the maximum likelihood estimates
\hat{B}=(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}Y

and
\hat{\Sigma}=(Y-X\hat{B})^{\top}(Y-X\hat{B})/n
for (
1
) are independently distributed as

\displaystyle\hat{B}\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(B,(X^{\top}X)^{-1},\Sigma),\quad\hat{\Sigma}\sim W_{q}\left(n-p,\frac{1}{n}\Sigma\right).

Thus,
Z=(X^{\top}X)^{1/2}\hat{B}\Sigma^{-1/2}
and
S=\Sigma^{-1/2}\hat{\Sigma}\Sigma^{-1/2}
are independetly distributed as

\displaystyle Z\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(\bar{Z},I_{p},I_{q}),\quad S\sim W_{q}\left(n-p,\frac{1}{n}I_{q}\right),

where
\bar{Z}=(X^{\top}X)^{1/2}{B}\Sigma^{-1/2}
.

Again, we write
d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
in (
2
) as
d
for simplicity.
Let
d=d((B,\Sigma),(\hat{B},\hat{\Sigma}))
and

\displaystyle h={\rm MAICc}-{\rm AICc}=-c{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}((X\hat{B})^{\top}(X\hat{B}))^{-1}).

Then,

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm MAICc}-d)^{2}]-{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm AICc}-d)^{2}]={\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[h^{2}+2h({\rm AICc}-d)].

(11)

We evaluate each term.
Note that
h=-c{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})
, since

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}((X\hat{B})^{\top}(X\hat{B}))^{-1})

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(S{\Sigma}^{1/2}((X\hat{B})^{\top}(X\hat{B}))^{-1}{\Sigma}^{1/2})

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(S({\Sigma}^{-1/2}\hat{B}^{\top}X^{\top}X\hat{B}{\Sigma}^{-1/2})^{-1})

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}).

In the following, we write
{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}
as
{\rm E}
for simplicity.

First, by using Lemma
B.2
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[h^{2}]

\displaystyle=c^{2}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=\frac{2(n-p)}{n^{2}}c^{2}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]+\frac{(n-p)^{2}}{n^{2}}c^{2}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

(12)

Next, from

\displaystyle{\rm AICc}

\displaystyle=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det\hat{\Sigma}+\frac{nq(n+p)}{n-p-q-1},

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det\hat{\Sigma}+n{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}^{-1}\Sigma)+{\rm tr}(X(\hat{B}-B)\hat{\Sigma}^{-1}(\hat{B}-B)^{\top}X^{\top})

\displaystyle=nq\log(2\pi)+n\log\det\hat{\Sigma}+n{\rm tr}(S^{-1})+{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})S^{-1}),

we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}[2h({\rm AICc}-d)]=

\displaystyle-\frac{2n(n+p)q}{n-p-q-1}c{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]+2nc{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle\quad+2c{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})].

(13)

Using the independence of
S
and
Z
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}({\rm E}[S]\cdot{\rm E}[(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})])

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\left(\frac{n-p}{n}I_{q}\cdot{\rm E}[(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-p}{n}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})].

(14)

Similarly, using the independence of
S
and
Z
and Lemma
B.6
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}({\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1})S]\cdot{\rm E}[(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}])

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\left(\frac{(n-p)q-2}{n-p-q-1}I_{q}\cdot{\rm E}[(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-p)q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})].

(15)

Also, from Lemma
B.9
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\frac{(n-p)q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]-2\frac{(n-p-1)(p-q-2)+2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]

\displaystyle\quad+2\frac{n-q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

(16)

Therefore, by substituting (
14
), (
15
) and (
16
) into (
13
),

\displaystyle{\rm E}[2h({\rm AICc}-d)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2(n-p)(n+p)q+2n((n-p)q-2)+2p((n-p)q-2)}{n-p-q-1}c{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle\ -4c\frac{(n-p-1)(p-q-2)+2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]+4c\frac{n-q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4c\frac{n+p}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle\ -4c\frac{(n-p-1)(p-q-2)+2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]+4c\frac{n-q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

(17)

Hence, by substituting (
12
) and (
17
) into (
11
),

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm MAICc}-d)^{2}]-{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm AICc}-d)^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4c\frac{n+p}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{2(n-p)}{n^{2}}c^{2}-4c\frac{(n-p-1)(p-q-2)+2}{n-p-q-1}\right){\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{(n-p)^{2}}{n^{2}}c^{2}+4c\frac{n-q-2}{n-p-q-1}\right){\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-4c\frac{n+p}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle\quad+c\left(\frac{(n-p)(q(n-p)+2)}{n^{2}}c-4\frac{(n-p-1)(p-q-2)-q(n-q-2)+2}{n-p-q-1}\right){\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4c\frac{n+p}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]+\frac{(n-p)(q(n-p)+2)}{n^{2}}c(c-\bar{c}){\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})],

where we used
({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}\leq q{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})
from (
6
) and
(n-p-1)(p-q-2)-q(n-q-2)=(n-p-q-1)(p-2q-2)-q^{2}-q
.
Therefore, if
0<c\leq\bar{c}
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm MAICc}-d)^{2}]\leq{\rm E}_{B,\Sigma}[({\rm AICc}-d)^{2}]

for every
B
and
\Sigma
.

∎

Note that, when
n
is sufficiently large, the condition
\bar{c}>0
in Theorem
4.1
is reduced to
p\geq 2q+3
, which is the same as in Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
3.1
.

In the case of a single response (
q=1
), Theorem
4.1
is reexpressed as follows.
Here, we employ the usual notation for the linear regression with a single response:

\displaystyle y\sim{\rm N}_{n}(X\beta,\sigma^{2}I_{n}),\quad\hat{\beta}=(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}y,\quad\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\|y-X\hat{\beta}\|^{2}/n.

(18)

Corollary 4.1
.

Consider the linear regression model
y\sim{\rm N}_{n}(X\beta,\sigma^{2}I_{n})
with unknown
\sigma^{2}
.
Let

\displaystyle\bar{c}=\frac{4n^{2}(p-4)}{(n-p)(n-p+2)}.

If
n-p-2>0
and
\bar{c}>0
, then for any
c\in(0,\bar{c}]
,
{\rm AICc}
is inadmissible and dominated by

\displaystyle{\rm MAICc}={\rm AICc}-c\hat{\sigma}^{2}\|X\hat{\beta}\|^{-2}

as an estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy.

Note that the condition
\bar{c}>0
in Corollary
4.1
is equivalent to
p\geq 5
, as in Proposition
2.1
.

Conclusion

In this study, we showed that the corrected AIC is inadmissible as an estimator of the Kullback–Leibler discrepancy and provided improved loss estimators.
To the best of our knowledge, such a loss estimation framework has not been employed in the study of information criteria, and there are several possible directions for future research.
For example, generalizations of the current results to out-of-sample prediction
(
Rosset and Tibshirani 2020
)
, high-dimensional settings
(
Bellec and Zhang 2021
;
Fujikoshi et al. 2014
;
Yanagihara et al. 2015
)
, and mis-specified cases
(
Fujikoshi and satoh 1997
;
Reschenhofer 1999
)
may be interesting.
Also, whereas we focused on the Gaussian linear regression model in this study, similar results may be obtained in general models for AIC and other information criteria such as TIC and GIC
(
Konishi and Kitagawa 2008
)
by asymptotic arguments.
Improvement of model averaging criteria such as Mallows criterion
(
Hansen 2007
;
Wan et al. 2010
)
is another future problem.
Finally, whereas we focused on the plug-in predictive distribution in this study, it would be interesting to study extension to the Bayesian predictive distribution, which minimizes the Bayes risk under the Kullback–Leibler loss
(
Aitchison 1975
)
.
For the linear regression model,
Kitagawa 1997
derived an information criterion for the Bayesian predictive distribution and
Kobayashi and Komaki 2008
studied the problem of Bayesian out-of-sample prediction.

Appendix A
Matrix derivative formulas

Lemma A.1
.

For
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kl}=-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kj}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{il}-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})_{ki}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{jl}.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{ab}
be the Kronecker delta:
\delta_{ab}=1
if
a=b
and
\delta_{ab}=0
if
a\neq b
.
From

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z^{\top}Z)_{ab}=\sum_{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z_{ca}Z_{cb})=\delta_{ja}Z_{ib}+\delta_{jb}Z_{ia}

and
{\rm d}(A^{-1})=-A^{-1}({\rm d}A)A^{-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kl}

\displaystyle=\left(-(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z^{\top}Z)\right)(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}\right)_{kl}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a,b}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ka}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z^{\top}Z)_{ab}\right)((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{bl}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a,b}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ka}(\delta_{ja}Z_{ib}+\delta_{jb}Z_{ia})((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{bl}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{b}(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kj}Z_{ib}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{bl}-\sum_{a}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ka}Z_{ia}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{jl}

\displaystyle=-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kj}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{il}-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})_{ki}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{jl}.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

For
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
and
S\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})=-2(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ij}.

Proof.

From Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}S_{kl}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}S_{kl}(-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kj}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{il}-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})_{ki}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{jl})

\displaystyle=-2(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ij}.

∎

Lemma A.3
.

For
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
and
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-q-2){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)-{\rm tr}(A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B).

Proof.

From Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}Z_{ik}\right)((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{kj}+\sum_{k,l}Z_{ik}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kl}\right)(A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{lj}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k,l}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A)_{ik}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kl}\right)B_{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\delta_{jk}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{kj}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k,l}Z_{ik}(-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kj}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{il}-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})_{ki}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{jl})(A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{lj}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k,l}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A)_{ik}(-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{kj}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{il}-((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})_{ki}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{jl})B_{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{jj}-(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ij}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{ij}

\displaystyle\quad-(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})_{ii}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{jj}-(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ij}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{ij}

\displaystyle\quad-(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})_{ii}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{jj}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)-{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top}\cdot Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)

\displaystyle\quad-{\rm tr}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top}){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)-{\rm tr}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}\cdot B^{\top}(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top})

\displaystyle\quad-{\rm tr}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}Z^{\top}){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)-{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)-q{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)

\displaystyle\quad-{\rm tr}(A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}\cdot B^{\top}(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})-{\rm tr}(A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-q-2){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B)-{\rm tr}(A(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}B).

∎

Appendix B
Expectation formulas

Lemma B.1
.

(
Stein 1973
)

If
Z\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(O,I_{p},I_{q})
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}\to\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
is absolutely continuous, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(Z^{\top}g(Z))]={\rm E}\left[\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z)\right].

Lemma B.2
.

(
Gupta and Nagar 2000
, Theorem 3.3.15 (iv))

If
d-q\geq 0
,
S\sim W_{q}(d,\Sigma)
and
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(AS){\rm tr}(BS)]=d{\rm tr}(A\Sigma B\Sigma)+d{\rm tr}(A^{\top}\Sigma B\Sigma)+d^{2}{\rm tr}(A\Sigma){\rm tr}(B\Sigma).

Lemma B.3
.

(
Gupta and Nagar 2000
, Theorem 3.3.16 (i))

If
d-q-1>0
and
S\sim W_{q}(d,\Sigma)
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[S^{-1}]=\frac{1}{d-q-1}\Sigma^{-1}.

Lemma B.4
.

(
Gupta and Nagar 2000
, Theorem 3.3.16 (iii))

If
d-q-3>0
,
S\sim W_{q}(d,\Sigma)
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
is positive semidefinite, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[S^{-1}AS^{-1}]=\frac{{\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1}A)}{(d-q)(d-q-1)(d-q-3)}\Sigma^{-1}+\frac{1}{(d-q)(d-q-3)}\Sigma^{-1}A\Sigma^{-1}.

In particular, when
\Sigma=A=I_{q}
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[S^{-2}]=\frac{d-1}{(d-q)(d-q-1)(d-q-3)}I_{q}.

Lemma B.5
.

(
Gupta and Nagar 2000
, Theorem 3.3.17 (ii))

If
d-q-3>0
and
S\sim W_{q}(d,\Sigma)
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1})S^{-1}]=\frac{d-q-2}{(d-q)(d-q-1)(d-q-3)}{\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1})\Sigma^{-1}+\frac{2}{(d-q)(d-q-1)(d-q-3)}\Sigma^{-2}.

In particular, when
\Sigma=I_{q}
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1})S^{-1}]=\frac{q(d-q-2)+2}{(d-q)(d-q-1)(d-q-3)}I_{q}.

Lemma B.6
.

(
Gupta and Nagar 2000
, Theorem 3.3.17 (iii))

If
d-q-1>0
and
S\sim W_{q}(d,\Sigma)
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1})S]=\frac{d}{d-q-1}{\rm tr}(\Sigma^{-1})\Sigma-\frac{2}{d-q-1}I_{q}.

In particular, when
\Sigma=I_{q}
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1})S]=\frac{dq-2}{d-q-1}I_{q}.

Lemma B.7
.

Styan 1989

If
d-q-1>0
,
S\sim W_{q}(d,\Sigma)
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[SAS^{-1}]=\frac{1}{d-q-1}(d\Sigma A\Sigma^{-1}-A^{\top}-{\rm tr}(A)I_{q}).

In particular, when
\Sigma=I_{q}
and
A^{\top}=A
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[SAS^{-1}]=\frac{1}{d-q-1}((d-1)A-{\rm tr}(A)I_{q}).

Lemma B.8
.

If
n-p-q-1>0
and
Z\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(\bar{Z},I_{p},I_{q})
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle pq{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]-2(p-q-2){\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]+2{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

(19)

Proof.

From Lemma
B.1
and Lemma
A.2
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}((Z-\bar{Z}))_{ij}{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\sum_{i,j}({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})-2((Z-\bar{Z}))_{ij}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ij})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle pq{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]-2{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})].

(20)

From Lemma
B.1
and Lemma
A.3
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle={\rm E}\left[\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle={\rm E}[(p-q-2){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})-({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

(21)

Substituting (
21
) into (
20
), we obtain (
19
).
∎

Lemma B.9
.

If
n-p-q-1>0
,
Z\sim{\rm N}_{p,q}(\bar{Z},I_{p},I_{q})
and
S\sim W_{q}(n-p,(1/n)I_{q})
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\frac{(n-p)q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]-2\frac{(n-p-1)(p-q-2)+2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]

\displaystyle\quad+2\frac{n-q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

(22)

Proof.

From Lemma
B.1
and Lemma
A.2
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}(Z-\bar{Z})S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}((Z-\bar{Z})S^{-1})_{ij}{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\sum_{i,j}((S^{-1})_{jj}{\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})-2((Z-\bar{Z})S^{-1})_{ij}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})_{ij})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]-2{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1}(Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})].

(23)

Then, from the linearity of expectation and Lemma
B.6
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1}){\rm tr}(S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}({\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1})S\cdot(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}])

\displaystyle={\rm tr}({\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1})S]\cdot{\rm E}[(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}])

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-p)q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})].

(24)

Also, from Lemma
B.1
and Lemma
A.3
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1}(Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{ij}}(Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S^{-1})_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}[(p-q-2){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S^{-1})-{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S^{-1})].

Now, from Lemma
B.7

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}\cdot{\rm E}[S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S^{-1}\mid Z])]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[{\rm tr}\left((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}\cdot\frac{1}{n-p-q-1}((n-p-1)(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}-{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})I_{q})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-p-1}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]-\frac{1}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

(26)

Also, by putting
\tilde{S}=((Z^{\top}Z)^{1/2}S((Z^{\top}Z)^{1/2}\sim W_{q}(n-p,Z^{\top}Z)
and using Lemma
B.6
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S){\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2}\tilde{S}){\rm tr}(\tilde{S}^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2}\cdot{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(\tilde{S}^{-1})\tilde{S}\mid Z])]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2}\left(\frac{n-p}{n-p-q-1}{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})(Z^{\top}Z)-\frac{2}{n-p-q-1}I_{q}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-p}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}]-\frac{2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})].

Thus, by substituting (

27

\displaystyle{\rm E}[{\rm tr}(S^{-1}(Z-\bar{Z})^{\top}Z(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}S(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-q-2)\left(\frac{n-p-1}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]-\frac{1}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}]\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{n-p}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}]-\frac{2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]\right)

\displaystyle\frac{(n-p-1)(p-q-2)+2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[{\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-2})]-\frac{n-q-2}{n-p-q-1}{\rm E}[({\rm tr}((Z^{\top}Z)^{-1}))^{2}].

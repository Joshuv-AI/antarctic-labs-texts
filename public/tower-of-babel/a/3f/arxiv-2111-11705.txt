Factorizing Wormholes
in a Partially Disorder-Averaged SYK Model

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a “partially disorder-averaged” SYK model. This model has a real parameter that smoothly interpolates between the ordinary totally disorder-averaged SYK model and the totally fixed-coupling model. For the large N N effective description, in addition to the usual bi-local collective fields, we also introduce a new additional set of local collective fields. These local fields can be understood as “half” of the bi-local collective fields, and in the totally fixed-coupling limit, they represent the “half-wormholes” which were found in recent studies. We find that the large N N saddles of these local fields vanish in the total-disorder-averaged limit, while they develop nontrivial profiles as we gradually fix the coupling constants. We argue that the bulk picture of these local collective fields represents a correlation between a spacetime brane and the asymptotic AdS boundary. This illuminates how the half-wormhole saddles emerge in the SYK model with fixed couplings.

Introduction

Recent studies suggested a holographic dual of a bulk gravitational theory has to involve an ensemble average of theories with random couplings.
The Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model

is a quantum mechanical many-body system with all-to-all interactions on fermionic
N

N\gg 1

Its coupling constant is random with a Gaussian probability distribution.
The model provides an interesting, highly nontrivial example of the AdS/CFT duality and a potential framework for quantum black holes,
with related out-of-time-order correlators exhibiting quantum chaos
and the maximal Lyapunov exponent characteristic of black holes
[

A central role is played by the emergent Schwarzian mode

,

, which dominates the dynamics of the model in the low-temperature limit.
The dual of the Schwarzian mode in the gravity theory is well understood as the boundary graviton of the Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity
[

,

]

in the context of nearly AdS
2
/CFT

[

The relationship between a boundary ensemble average and a bulk gravity theory
becomes more evident in the recent calculation of the JT gravity partition function at a non-perturbative level, defined by summing over all higher genus topologies. For instance, it was shown that the non-perturbative JT gravity partition coincides with a random Hermitian matrix integral
[
18
]
.
In this correspondence, the central role is played by Euclidean wormholes in the gravitational path integrals. For instance, in the presence of multiple conformal boundaries, the natural rule to define semi-classical gravitational path integral is to include all Euclidean manifolds consistent with the boundary condition. This includes Euclidean wormhole geometries where several conformal boundaries get connected, resulting in the random matrix integral, as mentioned above. It was also shown that even in a very simple topological model of gravity, once we include all possible topologically distinct manifolds respecting the boundary condition, its boundary naturally realizes an ensemble average
[
19
]
. Its generalization to supersymmetric cases can be found in
[
20

.

One manifestation of the emergence of ensemble averages in the presence of wormholes is the non-factorization of partition functions. Let us consider the simplest example, where we have two disjoint conformal boundaries. Then the gravitational path integral receives contributions from wormholes connecting the two boundaries. This is puzzling from the boundary field theory point of view, because on these two disjoint boundaries, its partition function gets factorized
[
21
]
. The resolution is to regard its dual as an ensemble of theories living on the boundaries.
The relation between ensemble averages and the physics of wormholes was extensively discussed a few decades ago
[

Given these developments, a natural question arises: How do we get a bulk gravity description of a fixed boundary field theory

We are particularly interested in the fate of Euclidean wormholes in such a setup.
There have been several attempts toward this goal.
For example, it was shown in

This question was studied in

There, in the calculation of a product of partition functions

One is the usual wormhole saddles which connect two boundaries

In addition to these, there is another type of saddles called “half-wormholes”.
From a bulk gravity point of view, these saddles can be interpreted as geometries starting from a conformal boundary and ending somewhere in the bulk.
Quite remarkably, the contributions of these half-wormholes are essential for

To further investigate these ideas, wormholes vs. half-wormholes and ensemble averaging vs. fixed-coupling (or theory),
in this paper, we introduce a partially disorder-averaged SYK model.
This model has a real parameter
\sigma
that smoothly interpolates
between the ordinary totally disorder-averaged SYK model (
\sigma=\infty
) and the totally fixed-coupling model (
\sigma=0
).
This is archived by modifying the probability distribution of the coupling constant.
In the
\sigma=\infty
limit, the probability distribution is reduced to the ordinary Gaussian form. In contrast, in the
\sigma=0
limit, the probability distribution becomes a product of delta functions that enforces each component of the coupling constant to a fixed external value.
This type of interpolation between the totally disorder-averaged and fixed-coupling cases is previously studied in the context of matrix integrals in
[
45

.

One convenient way to study the conventional SYK model in the large
N
limit is introducing bi-local collective fields
\{G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\}

depending on two boundary times.
This formalism has a further advantage for studies of two-point (or higher-point) functions of the partition functions.
When each time corresponds to each boundary theory, the bi-local fields have a natural bulk interpretation for the corresponding gravitational configurations,
including Euclidean wormholes in a suitable setup.
Below we will argue that in the large
N
analysis of our deformed SYK model,
we need to introduce additional local collective fields
\{G_{\sigma}(\tau),\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau)\}
.
These local fields are analogs of “half-wormholes” because they depend only on one boundary time.
We will study in detail the behavior of these local fields
\{G_{\sigma}(\tau),\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau)\}
as we change the deformation parameter
\sigma
,
from
\sigma=\infty
whose low energy part has a complete gravitational description in terms of JT gravity,
to the totally fixed coupling model
\sigma=0
whose gravitational description we would like to know better.

2
Partial Disorder Average

In this section, we define our partially disorder-averaged SYK model,
which smoothly interpolates between the ordinary totally disorder-averaged SYK model and the totally fixed coupling model by introducing a real parameter
\sigma
.
The original SYK model
[
1
,
2
]
is defined by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H\,=\,i^{\frac{q}{2}}\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\,\chi_{i_{1}}\cdots\chi_{i_{q}}\,,

(2.1)

where the one-dimensional Majorana fermions satisfy

\displaystyle\{\chi_{i},\chi_{j}\}\,=\,\delta_{ij}\,.

(2.2)

The coupling
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}
is random, and drawn from Gaussian ensemble with the vanishing mean value
\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\rangle=0
.
Its Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle L\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}\partial_{\tau}\chi_{i}\,-\,i^{\frac{q}{2}}\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\,\chi_{i_{1}}\cdots\chi_{i_{q}}\,.

(2.3)

Now we introduce a partial disorder averaging by deforming the probability distribution of the random coupling as

\displaystyle P(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}})\,=\,\exp\left[-\frac{N^{q-1}}{2(q-1)!}\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}\left(\frac{J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{2}}{J^{2}}\,+\,\frac{(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}-J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)})^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]\,,

(2.4)

with

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{O}\big\rangle_{J}\,\equiv\,\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}^{-1}\int\prod_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}dJ_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\,P(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}})\,\mathcal{O}\,.

(2.5)

Here
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
is the external fixed coupling, and the parameter
\sigma
controls how much we take disorder averaging.
This type of partial disorder averaging was studied in
[
45
]
for a matrix model.
As in
[
45
]
,
\sigma=\infty
corresponds to the total disorder-averaging coincides with the ordinary SYK model,
while
\sigma\to 0
corresponds to totally fixing the coupling constant
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}=J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
.
In the following, it is convenient to introduce a new set of effective couplings by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\widetilde{J}^{2}}\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{J^{2}}\,+\,\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\,,\qquad J_{\sigma}\,\equiv\,\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}J_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}\,,

(2.6)

where
J_{0}
is defined in (
3.4
) and we assume
\widetilde{J}\geq 0
.
In the extreme limits of the parameter
\sigma
, these two couplings behave as

\displaystyle\sigma\,\to\,\infty\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\widetilde{J}\,\to\,J\,,\qquad J_{\sigma}\,\to\,0\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sigma\,\to\,0\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\widetilde{J}\,\to\,\sigma\,,\qquad J_{\sigma}\,\to\,J_{0}\,.

(2.8)

The normalization factor
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}\,\equiv\,\int\prod_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}dJ_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\,P(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}})\,,

(2.9)

and this gives us

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}^{-1}\,=\,\left(\frac{N^{q-1}}{2(q-1)!\pi\widetilde{J}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\binom{N}{q}}\,\exp\left(\frac{N^{q-1}}{2(q-1)!(J^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}\Big(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\Big)^{2}\right)\,.

(2.10)

For the usual total-disorder-averaged SYK model, this factor does not play any crucial role,
but for our partially disorder-averaged SYK model, we have to keep at least nontrivial
\sigma
-dependence coming from this normalization factor.
For example, in the totally-fixed-coupling limit (
\sigma\to 0
), combined with this normalization factor, the probability distribution gives delta functions:

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\ \mathcal{N}_{\sigma}^{-1}\,P(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}})\,=\,\prod_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}\delta\left(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}-J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\right)\,.

(2.11)

This type of delta function projection of the coupling constant was also studied in
[
50
]
.

This definition of partial disorder averaging does not give a vanishing mean value of the coupling unlike the ordinary SYK model, but we find

\displaystyle\big\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\,J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\,,\qquad\big\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{2}\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\frac{(q-1)!\widetilde{J}^{2}}{N^{q-1}}\left(1+\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}N^{q-1}}{(q-1)!\sigma^{4}}\,\big(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\big)^{2}\right)\,.

(2.12)

In the total disorder-averaging limit, we recover the ordinary SYK results:

\big\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\big\rangle_{J}^{\sigma=\infty}=0
and
\big\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{2}\big\rangle_{J}^{\sigma=\infty}=(q-1)!J^{2}/N^{q-1}
,
while in the totally fixed-coupling limit, we find

\big\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\big\rangle_{J}^{\sigma=0}=J^{(0)}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}
and
\big\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{2}\big\rangle_{J}^{\sigma=0}=\big(J^{(0)}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\big)^{2}
.

3
Partially Disorder-Averaged Partition Function

Having specified the model of our interest, in this section we study the partially disorder-averaged partition function of this model in the large
N
limit,
by introducing collective fields. We see that to study the partially disordered model with finite
\sigma
, in addition to ordinary bi-local fields
\{G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\}
in the totally averaged theory at
\sigma=\infty
, it is convenient to introduce
an additional set of collective fields denoted by
\{G_{\sigma}(\tau),\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau)\}
.
We will explain a possible bulk picture of these local collective fields in section
3.4

and for the two-point function we study in section
4
, they eventually correspond to the degrees of freedom of “half-wormhole”.

The original partition function with fixed coupling
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}
is given by

\displaystyle Z\,

\displaystyle=\,\int D\chi_{i}\,e^{-S}

\displaystyle=\,\int D\chi_{i}\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}\partial_{\tau}\chi_{i}\,+\,i^{\frac{q}{2}}\int d\tau\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\,\chi_{i_{1}}\cdots\chi_{i_{q}}\right]\,.

(3.1)

Taking the partial disorder averaging of this partition function with respect to the probability distribution (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\int D\chi_{i}\,e^{-S_{\rm eff}[\chi]}\,,

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[\chi]\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}\partial_{\tau}\chi_{i}\,-\,\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{2qN^{q-1}}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\quad-\,\frac{i^{\frac{q}{2}}\widetilde{J}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\int d\tau\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\,\chi_{i_{1}}\cdots\chi_{i_{q}}\,.

(3.3)

The normalization factor
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}
is canceled out by the Gaussian integral of the coupling. We emphasize that even though we integrated
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}
to obtain
\langle Z\rangle_{J}
,
this still can be the totally fixed-coupling partition function in the
\sigma\to 0
limit.
Indeed, we can see that in the totally fixed-coupling limit (
\sigma\to 0
), the second term vanishes,
while the last term survives because
\widetilde{J}^{2}/\sigma^{2}\to 1
.
The result is nothing but the totally fixed-coupling action (
2.3
).
On the other hand, in the totally disorder-averaged limit (
\sigma\to\infty
) the last term vanishes
and the remaining first two terms are the usual disorder-averaged SYK effective action.

We also note that besides the last term, the original SYK effective action has the global
O(N)
gauge symmetry:
\chi_{i}\to\sum_{j}O_{ij}\chi_{j}
.
However, this global
O(N)
symmetry is now broken by the last term by fixing the coupling constant
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
.
In addition, this term contains a factor
i^{q/2}
, so in order to keep the action real, it looks like we need a complex
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
,
such that the entire last term is still real.
This type of complex coupling is similar to the one discussed in
[
31
,
46
]
, but the precise relation is unclear.

Fixed-couplings as an external source

Here we will explain how to introduce collective fields in our partially disorder-averaged SYK model.
For the first line in (
3.3
), we introduce a set of bi-local fields
(G_{LR},\Sigma_{LR})
as usual.
To introduce a new set of collective fields for the second line in (
3.3
), we will give the following interpretation of the external fixed couplings.

Let us now assume a specific form of the external coupling
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
.
We choose it to be proportional to the totally anti-symmetric tensor
\varepsilon_{{i_{1},\cdots i_{q}}}
for
i_{k}=1,\cdots,N
, with
\varepsilon_{1,2,\cdots,q}=1
,
\varepsilon_{2,3,\cdots,q+1}=1
, e.t.c.
From now on, we interpret the second term in (
3.3
) as the couplings between the fermions
\chi_{i}
in the SYK model and the external fermionic sources
\theta_{i}
by decomposing the anti-symmetric tensor
\varepsilon_{{i_{1},\cdots i_{q}}}
into the products of
\theta_{i}
,

\displaystyle\qquad J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\,=\,\frac{(q-1)!J_{0}}{i^{\frac{q}{2}}N^{q-1}}\,\theta_{i_{1}}\cdots\theta_{i_{q}}\,,\qquad\quad{\rm with}\qquad\{\theta_{i},\theta_{j}\}\,=\,\delta_{ij}\,,

(3.4)

where the coefficient was chosen for later convenience.

1
1

1

Here we introduced a non-vanishing square of the variables
\theta_{i}
in order to keep the second term of
\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{2}\rangle_{J}
in (
2.12
) non-zero
(or to keep non-vanishing
\langle J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{2}\rangle_{J}^{\sigma=0}
).
If we regard
\theta_{i}
as external Grassmann variables, it is natural to have
\{\theta_{i},\theta_{j}\}=0
,
but we can think that the external coupling originally had a slight time-dependence, and we smeared over that time-dependence, so we have
\theta_{i}^{2}=1/2
.
A similar argument was also given in footnote 9 of
[
24
]
.

This variable
\theta_{i}
can be interpreted as non-dynamical fermions living in another universe. We discuss this interpretation in Appendix
A
.
After using this decomposition, we have the effective action

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[\chi]\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}\partial_{\tau}\chi_{i}\,-\,\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{2qN^{q-1}}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\quad-\,\frac{J_{\sigma}}{qN^{q-1}}\int d\tau\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}(\tau)\right)^{q}\,,

(3.5)

with
J_{\sigma}
defined in (
2.6
). Now the term depending on the fixed couplings is rewritten as a local term similar to the usual bi-local term in the first line, which enables us to introduce the collective fields to this term. Now we are considering the SYK model coupled to the external fields
\theta_{i}
. We will analyze this model instead of the original partially disorder-averaged SYK model. After all computations, we replace all the dependence of
\theta_{i}
by the original fixed-couplings
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
, then we expect to recover the result in the original partially disorder-averaged SYK model.

Now it is useful to employ the following Hubbard–Stratonovich trick:

\displaystyle 1\,

\displaystyle=\,\int DG\int DG_{\sigma}\,\delta\left(G-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}\chi_{i}\right)\delta\left(G_{\sigma}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\,\int DGD\Sigma DG_{\sigma}D\Sigma_{\sigma}\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\,\Sigma\cdot\left(NG-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}\chi_{i}\right)-\textrm{Tr}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}\cdot\left(NG_{\sigma}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}\right)\right]\,,

We insert the above identity to the partially disorder-averaged partition function (
3.2
) with
S_{\rm eff}[\chi]
given by (
3.5
).
Rewriting all terms in
S_{\rm eff}[\chi]
(
3.5
) by the bi-local
G
and the local
G_{\sigma}
fields,
we can perform the fermion integrals (see Appendix
B
for detail) and obtain

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}^{-1}\int DGD\Sigma DG_{\sigma}D\Sigma_{\sigma}\,e^{-S_{\rm eff}[G,\Sigma,G_{\sigma},\Sigma_{\sigma}]}\,

(3.7)

with

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[G,\Sigma,G_{\sigma},\Sigma_{\sigma}]\,=\,-\frac{N}{2}\textrm{Tr}\log(\Sigma)-\frac{N}{2}\int d\tau\big[\partial_{\tau}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\big]_{\tau^{\prime}=\tau}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{N}{2}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{q}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q}\right)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\qquad+N\int d\tau\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau)G_{\sigma}(\tau)-\frac{J_{\sigma}}{q}G_{\sigma}(\tau)^{q}\right)\,-\,\frac{N}{4}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{1})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{2})\,.

Before studying this effective action in detail, let us first consider the meaning of the local field
G_{\sigma}
we introduced here in addition to the usual SYK bi-local fields.
If we consider the combination of
G_{\sigma}^{q}(\tau_{1})G_{\sigma}^{q}(\tau_{2})
, this is written as

\displaystyle\Big(G_{\sigma}(\tau_{1})G_{\sigma}(\tau_{2})\Big)^{q}\,=\,\frac{i^{q}}{\big((q-1)!\big)^{2}J_{0}^{2}N^{2}}\,

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{q}}^{N}J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\,\chi_{i_{1}}(\tau_{1})\cdots\chi_{i_{q}}(\tau_{1})

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{q}}^{N}J_{j_{1}\cdots j_{q}}^{(0)}\,\chi_{j_{1}}(\tau_{2})\cdots\chi_{j_{q}}(\tau_{2})\,,

(3.9)

where we used the decomposition of
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
(
3.4
).
If we take the total disorder averaging of
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
for this quantity
with using the same form of probability distribution as (
2.4
) for
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}
, we find

\displaystyle\Big\langle G_{\sigma}^{q}(\tau_{1})G_{\sigma}^{q}(\tau_{2})\Big\rangle_{J_{0}}\,=\,\frac{i^{q}\widetilde{J}^{2}}{(q-1)!J_{0}^{2}N}\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q}\,.

(3.10)

This relation implies that we can think of the local field
G_{\sigma}(\tau)
as the “half” of the bi-local field
G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
. Furthermore, one can regard the anti-symmetric variable
\theta_{i}
as fermionic degrees of freedom living in the surface at which the bulk spacetime ends. Since the new variable
G_{\sigma}(\tau)
is the propagator between the original SYK fermions
\chi_{i}
and the new ones
\theta_{i}
, this also suggests that
G_{\sigma}(\tau)
corresponds to the correlation between the boundary and the interior of the bulk. These interpretations will be useful in the following discussion.

Since all terms in the effective action (
3.8
) are proportional to
N
, we can use large
N
saddle-point evaluation. Variation of each field leads to the following saddle-point equations

\displaystyle\delta G\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\widetilde{J}^{2}\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q-1}\,+\,\partial_{\tau_{1}}\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{1})\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{2})\,,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma^{-1}(\tau_{2},\tau_{1})\,=\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\delta G_{\sigma}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau)\,=\,J_{\sigma}\,G_{\sigma}(\tau)^{q-1}\,,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma_{\sigma}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad G_{\sigma}(\tau)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau^{\prime}\,G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau^{\prime})\,,

(3.14)

where the inverse is defined by

\displaystyle\int d\tau_{3}\,\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})\Sigma^{-1}(\tau_{3},\tau_{2})\,=\,\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,.

(3.15)

From (

), in order to keep the time translation symmetry, the only possible solution for the local field

Such a constant term in (

3.1
Zero-dimensional SYK

Before studying this model in detail, let us first consider the zero-dimensional case discussed in
[

,
44
]
.
This model is not dynamical, but it might capture some important topological contributions.
The treatment of this case is slightly different from the one-dimensional case we discussed above.
This is because in the effective action (
3.3
), the second term identically vanishes in this case,
and of course, the kinetic term also does not exist:

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\int d\chi_{i}\,\exp\left[\frac{J_{\sigma}}{qN^{q-1}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}\bigg)^{q}\right]\,.

(3.16)

Therefore, in this case, we cannot introduce the bi-local fields, which are identically zero, so at most, we can introduce the local fields only:

\displaystyle 1\,=\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dG_{\sigma}\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\frac{d\Sigma_{\sigma}}{2\pi i/N}\,\exp\bigg[\Sigma_{\sigma}\Big(NG_{\sigma}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}\Big)\bigg]\,.

(3.17)

Hence, in this case, after integrating out the fermions (see Appendix
B
for detail) we have

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\frac{N}{2\pi i}\,\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\int dG_{\sigma}d\Sigma_{\sigma}\,e^{-S_{\rm eff}[G_{\sigma},\Sigma_{\sigma}]}\,,

(3.18)

with

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[G_{\sigma},\Sigma_{\sigma}]\,=\,-\,N\log(-\Sigma_{\sigma})\,-\,N\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}G_{\sigma}+\frac{J_{\sigma}}{q}G_{\sigma}^{q}\right)\,,

(3.19)

where the
\log(-\Sigma_{\sigma})
term comes from expanding the linear in the fermion term in the Hubbard–Stratonovich trick (
3.17
).

Now we can simply integrate out both fields as

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{N}{2\pi i}\,\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\int dG_{\sigma}d\Sigma_{\sigma}\,\left(-N^{-1}\partial_{G_{\sigma}}\right)^{N}e^{N\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}G_{\sigma}+\frac{J_{\sigma}}{q}G_{\sigma}^{q}\right)}

\displaystyle=\,\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\int dG_{\sigma}\,e^{\frac{NJ_{\sigma}}{q}G_{\sigma}^{q}}\left(-N^{-1}\partial_{G_{\sigma}}\right)^{N}\,\delta(G_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\,\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\left(\frac{J_{\sigma}N}{q}\right)^{\frac{N}{q}}\frac{N!}{N^{N}(N/q)!}\,,

(3.20)

where for the last line, we assume
N
is a multiple of
q
.
For the total-disorder-averaging limit (
\sigma\to\infty
), we have
J_{\sigma}\to 0
(
2.8
). Therefore, in this limit we have
\langle Z\rangle_{J}=0
.
On the other hand, for finite
\sigma>0
, we have a nontrivial partition function, which is proportional to the “hyperpfaffian” of the external coupling
[
44
]
:

\displaystyle{\rm PF}\Big(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\Big)\,

\displaystyle\equiv\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{A_{1}<\cdots<A_{p}}\textrm{sgn}(A)\,J_{A_{1}}^{(0)}\cdots J_{A_{p}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{N!}{p!(q!)^{p}}\,J_{1\,\cdots\,q}^{(0)}\cdots J_{N-q\,\cdots\,N}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{N!}{p!N^{N}}\left(\frac{NJ_{0}}{q}\right)^{\frac{N}{q}}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\,,

(3.21)

where
p=N/q
and
A

q
-subsets

For the third line, we used (

) and assumed

to eliminate the factor coming from the

Therefore, we find

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\bigg(\,\frac{\widetilde{J}}{\sigma}\,\bigg)^{\frac{2N}{q}}{\rm PF}\Big(J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{(0)}\Big)\,.

(3.22)

Going back to the expression of (
3.20
), for the numerical coefficient, besides the
J_{\sigma}
dependence, we find in the large
N
approximation that

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,\approx\,\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\big(J_{\sigma}\big)^{\frac{N}{q}}\sqrt{q}\,e^{-(1-\frac{1}{q})N}\,.

(3.23)

We can also evaluate this partition function in the large
N
saddle-point approximation.
From the effective action, we have the large
N
saddle-point equations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\,=\,-\,G_{\sigma}\,,\qquad\Sigma_{\sigma}\,=\,-\,J_{\sigma}\,G_{\sigma}^{q-1}\,,

(3.24)

and there are
q
solutions:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}^{(s)}\,=\,e^{\frac{2m\pi i}{q}}\,J_{\sigma}^{-\frac{1}{q}}\,,\qquad\Sigma_{\sigma}^{(s)}\,=\,-\,e^{-\frac{2m\pi i}{q}}\,J_{\sigma}^{\ \frac{1}{q}}\,,

(3.25)

where
m=1,2,\cdots,q

Evaluating the on-shell action for these large

saddle-point solutions, we find

\displaystyle S_{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}\,=\,-\,\frac{N}{q}\log J_{\sigma}\,+\,N\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\,+\,\frac{2m\pi iN}{q}\,,

(3.26)

and

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\big(J_{\sigma}\big)^{\frac{N}{q}}\,\sum_{m=1}^{q}e^{-(1-\frac{1}{q})N-\frac{2m\pi iN}{q}}\,.

(3.27)

We obtained the same
J_{\sigma}

The remaining sum can also be evaluated as

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{q}e^{-2mp\pi i}\,=\,\frac{1-e^{2pq\pi i}}{e^{2pq\pi i}(1-e^{2p\pi i})}\,=\,q\,,

(3.28)

where we put
p=N/q
and for the first equality we assumed
p\neq\mathbb{Z}
, but for the second equality we took
p\to\mathbb{Z}
and evaluated by using l’Hôpital’s rule.
The

difference from the exact result (

) is accounted by the one-loop contribution
[

]
.
To evaluate this contribution, we expand the fields around the saddle-point solutions:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}\,=\,G_{\sigma}^{(s)}\,+\,\frac{\delta G_{\sigma}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\qquad\Sigma_{\sigma}\,=\,\Sigma_{\sigma}^{(s)}\,+\,\frac{\delta\Sigma_{\sigma}}{\sqrt{N}}\,.

(3.29)

Then, the quadratic action is written as

\displaystyle S_{(2)}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}\Big[J_{\sigma}^{-\frac{2}{q}}e^{\frac{4m\pi i}{q}}\,\delta\Sigma_{\sigma}^{2}\,-\,2\delta\Sigma_{\sigma}\delta G_{\sigma}\,-\,(q-1)J_{\sigma}^{\ \frac{2}{q}}e^{-\frac{4m\pi i}{q}}\,\delta G_{\sigma}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}\Big[\delta\tilde{\Sigma}_{\sigma}^{2}\,+\,2i\delta\tilde{\Sigma}_{\sigma}\delta\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\,+\,(q-1)\delta\tilde{G}_{\sigma}^{2}\Big]\,,

(3.30)

where in the second line we change the variables by
\tilde{G}_{\sigma}=iJ_{\sigma}^{1/q}e^{-\frac{2m\pi i}{q}}\delta G_{\sigma}
and

\tilde{\Sigma}_{\sigma}=J_{\sigma}^{-1/q}e^{\frac{2m\pi i}{q}}\delta\Sigma_{\sigma}
to make the Gaussian integral convergent.
Therefore, the one-loop contribution is given by
\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}^{\scriptsize\mbox{one-loop}}=1/\sqrt{q}
. Finally, including this one-loop contribution, we find

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\big(J_{\sigma}\big)^{\frac{N}{q}}\sqrt{q}\,e^{-(1-\frac{1}{q})N}\,.

(3.31)

This precisely agrees with (
3.23
).

3.2
Total disorder-averaging

Now let us come back to the one-dimensional model (
3.8
).
In the totally disorder-averaging limit (
\sigma\to\infty
), we have
J_{\sigma}\to 0
.
This means that the variation of
G_{\sigma}
(
3.13
) sets
\Sigma_{\sigma}=0
. Therefore, the remaining dynamics is described by the bi-local fields
\{G,\Sigma\}
, which is nothing but the effective system of the ordinary SYK model.

Combining (
3.11
) and (

), the Schwinger-Dyson equation for
G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
is now obtained as

\displaystyle-\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,

\displaystyle=\,\int d\tau_{3}\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})\Sigma(\tau_{3},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle=\,\partial_{\tau_{1}}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,+\,J^{2}\int d\tau_{3}\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})G(\tau_{3},\tau_{2})^{q-1}\,.

(3.32)

The free solution, which is obtained by setting
J=0
, is found as

\displaystyle G_{\rm free}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{\textrm{sgn}(\tau_{12})}{2}\,,

(3.33)

where
\tau_{12}\equiv\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
.
On the other hand, the IR solution, which is obtained by eliminating the kinetic term, is given by

\displaystyle G^{\textrm{IR}}_{\beta=\infty}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{b^{\Delta}}{J^{2\Delta}}\,\frac{\textrm{sgn}(\tau_{12})}{|\tau_{12}|^{2\Delta}}\,,

(3.34)

for zero temperature case where
\Delta=1/q
and

\displaystyle b^{\Delta}\,=\,\left(\frac{1-2\Delta}{2}\right)\,\tan(\pi\Delta)\,.

(3.35)

This solution can be determined by using the scaling ansatz and the Fourier transform

\displaystyle\frac{\textrm{sgn}(\tau)}{|\tau|^{\alpha}}\,=\,c(\alpha)\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,e^{-i\omega\tau}\,|\omega|^{\alpha-1}\textrm{sgn}(\omega)\,,\qquad c(\alpha)\,=\,i2^{1-\alpha}\sqrt{\pi}\,\frac{\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2})}\,.

(3.36)

Now we realize that the IR effective action

\displaystyle S_{\rm IR}[G]\,=\,\frac{N}{2}\textrm{Tr}\log(G)-\frac{NJ^{2}}{2q}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q}\,,

(3.37)

is invariant under the conformal transformation
\tau\to f(\tau)
together with the bi-local field transformation

\displaystyle G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,\to\,G_{f}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,\equiv\,|f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})f^{\prime}(\tau_{2})|^{\Delta}\,G(f(\tau_{1}),f(\tau_{2}))\,.

(3.38)

For the IR solution (
3.34
), a general transformation gives

\displaystyle G^{\textrm{IR}}_{f}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{b^{\Delta}}{J^{2\Delta}}\,\frac{|f^{\prime}(\tau_{1})f^{\prime}(\tau_{2})|^{\Delta}}{|f(\tau_{1})-f(\tau_{2})|^{2\Delta}}\,\textrm{sgn}(\tau_{12})\,.

(3.39)

In particular, the finite temperature solution can be obtained by this transformation with
f(\tau)=(\pi/\beta)\tan(\pi\tau/\beta)
as

\displaystyle G^{\textrm{IR}}_{\beta}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\frac{b^{\Delta}}{J^{2\Delta}}\,\left|\frac{\pi}{\beta\sin\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}}\right|^{2\Delta}\,\textrm{sgn}(\tau_{12})\,.

(3.40)

Slightly fixed-coupling

In this subsection, we study the effect of slightly fixing the coupling constant.
For a slightly fixed coupling (which means large

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}^{2}\,

\displaystyle J_{0}\,\sim\,\sigma\,\gg\,T\,\gg\,J\,.

\displaystyle=\,\int\prod_{a,b}^{L,R}DG_{ab}\,\delta\left(G_{ab}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}^{a}\chi_{i}^{b}\right)\int\prod_{a}^{L,R}DG_{\sigma}^{a}\,\delta\left(G_{\sigma}^{a}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}^{a}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\int\prod_{a}^{L,R}DG_{\sigma}^{a}D\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}\exp\left[-\textrm{Tr}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}\cdot\left(NG_{\sigma}^{a}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}^{a}\right)\right]\,,

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{a,b}\textrm{Tr}\log(\Sigma_{ab})-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{a}\int d\tau\big[\partial_{\tau}G_{aa}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\big]_{\tau^{\prime}=\tau}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{N}{2}\sum_{a,b}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\Sigma_{ab}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})G_{ab}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{q}G_{ab}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+N\sum_{a}\int d\tau\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau)G_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau)-\frac{J_{\sigma}}{q}G_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau)^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\,\frac{N}{4}\sum_{a,b}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau_{1})G_{ab}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\Sigma_{\sigma}^{b}(\tau_{2})\,.

(4.4)

Similar to (

\displaystyle\delta G_{ab}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma_{ab}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\widetilde{J}^{2}\,G_{ab}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q-1}+\delta_{ab}\,\partial_{\tau_{1}}\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau_{1})\Sigma_{\sigma}^{b}(\tau_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma_{ab}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma_{ab}^{-1}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,G_{ab}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\delta G_{\sigma}^{a}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau)\,=\,J_{\sigma}\,G_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau)^{q-1}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad G_{\sigma}^{a}(\tau)\,=\,\frac{1}{4}\sum_{b}\int d\tau^{\prime}\,G_{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\Sigma_{\sigma}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\,.

(4.5)

4.1.1
Saddle-point solutions

The large
N
saddle-point equations are a little complicated to solve, so let us first consider the zero-dimensional case again.
In this case, we only have
\{G_{LR},\Sigma_{LR}\}
for the bi-local fields.
For the following discussion, it is more convenient to combine the two couplings into one effective coupling that appears in the interaction term between the local and bi-local sectors.
This can be implemented by rescaling the Hubbard–Stratonovich fields as
G_{LR}\to\widetilde{J}^{-2/q}G_{LR}
,
\Sigma_{LR}\to\widetilde{J}^{2/q}\Sigma_{LR}
,

G_{\sigma}\to J_{\sigma}^{-1/q}G_{\sigma}

\Sigma_{\sigma}\to J_{\sigma}^{1/q}\Sigma_{\sigma}

After all of these rescalings, the effective action of this case reads

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[G_{LR},\Sigma_{LR},G_{\sigma}^{a},\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}]\,

\displaystyle=\,-N\log(\Sigma_{LR})\,+\,N\left(\Sigma_{LR}G_{LR}-\frac{1}{q}\,G_{LR}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+N\sum_{a}\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}G_{\sigma}^{a}-\frac{1}{q}\big(G_{\sigma}^{a}\big)^{q}\right)\,-\,N\lambda_{\sigma}G_{LR}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\,,

(4.6)

where we introduced the coupling between the local and bi-local sectors by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\sigma}\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{J_{\sigma}}{\widetilde{J}}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\,.

(4.7)

This coupling behaves
\lambda_{\sigma}\sim\frac{1}{2}(J_{0}/\sigma)^{2/q}

\lambda_{\sigma}\sim\frac{1}{2}(JJ_{0}/\sigma^{2})^{2/q}

\sigma\rightarrow\infty

Here from the action, we excluded the constant contribution

coming from the rescaling of
\Sigma_{LR}
,
so that the two-point function of the partition functions is now written as

\displaystyle\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\widetilde{J}^{\,\frac{2N}{q}}\int dG_{LR}d\Sigma_{LR}\int\prod_{a}^{L,R}dG_{\sigma}^{a}d\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}\ e^{-S_{\rm eff}[G_{LR},\Sigma_{LR},G_{\sigma}^{a},\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}]}\,.

(4.8)

Now the saddle-point equations are written as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{LR}\,=\,\big(G_{LR}\big)^{q-1}+\,\lambda_{\sigma}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\Sigma_{LR}}\,=\,G_{LR}\,,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}\,=\,\,\,\big(G_{\sigma}^{a}\big)^{q-1}\,,\qquad G_{\sigma}^{L}\,=\,\lambda_{\sigma}\,G_{LR}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\,,\qquad G_{\sigma}^{R}\,=\,\lambda_{\sigma}\,G_{LR}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\,.

4.1.2
On-shell action

In this subsection, we study the
\sigma

Let us also study the value of the on-shell action directly (without taking derivative with respect to

For the wormhole solutions, this is given by

\displaystyle S^{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}_{\scriptsize\mbox{WH}}\,

\displaystyle=\,-N\log\Sigma_{LR}\big|_{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}\,+\,N\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\,G_{LR}^{q}\big|_{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}

\displaystyle=\,N\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\,+\,\frac{2m\pi iN}{q}\,.

For the half-wormhole solutions at small

\displaystyle\approx\,-\,N\log(\lambda_{\sigma})\,+\,2N\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\,+\,\frac{2n\pi iN}{q}\,.

We can see that the

In this section, we study the spectrum of the partially disorder-averaged SYK model.

6
Conclusions and Discussions

In this paper, we studied a partially disorder-averaged SYK model.
We introduced a partially disorder-averaged SYK model by modifying the probability distribution of the coupling constants with a real parameter
\sigma
.
The probability distribution becomes the usual Gaussian form in the
\sigma\to\infty
limit
while in the
\sigma\to 0
limit, it becomes a product of delta functions which enforces each component of the coupling constant to a fixed external value.
Given this partial disorder averaging, we studied the one- and two-point functions of the partition functions as well as spectral density and the spectral form factor.
For the large
N
effective description, in addition to the usual bi-local collective fields, we introduced a new additional set of local collective fields.
We explained that these local fields can be understood as the “half” of the bi-local collective fields.

Bulk dual to the half-wormhole saddles

For the study of the two-point function of the zero-dimensional model partition function,
we found that it contains the wormhole and half-wormholes as large
N
saddles, and their configurations change as we gradually vary
\sigma
.
A natural question is what is the gravity dual to the half-wormhole for each value of
\sigma
.
As we saw in section
4.1
, in the totally disorder-averaged limit
\sigma=\infty
, the half-wormhole and the wormhole saddles degenerate,
so in this sense, the dual geometry is just a smooth wormhole geometry drawn as the left picture in Figure
5
.
Such geometry can be found in the one-dimensional model
[
24
]
as well as in the dual JT gravity set-up for example in
[
18
]
.

Let us consider the half-wormhole saddles in the slightly fixed coupling region. As we explained section
4.1
, in this region, the half-wormhole saddle can be obtained by considering a perturbation around the wormhole saddles by adding the small effect of the half-bi-local fields. The interpretation of this perturbation in the gravity side is considered in section
3.4
. We found that adding the small effect of the half-bi-local fields
G_{\sigma}
corresponds to having a nontrivial classical configuration of the bulk field dual to
G_{\sigma}
on the original wormhole background. As we also pointed out in Appendix
A
, the
G_{\sigma}
can be interpreted as the correlation between the fermions
\chi_{i}
on the boundary and other fermioninc degrees of freedom corresponding to
\theta_{i}
, which represents the fixed couplings, living on “another universe”. We interpret the gravity dual of “another universe” as a bulk brane, whose structure strongly depends on the microscopic details of the bulk gravity theory. Such a “UV-sensitive brane” might appear in the low-energy effective gravity from the condensation of some stringy modes in string theory as pointed out in
[
51
]
. We leave it as an interesting research direction for the future.

Similar proposals are also mentioned in
[
45
,
46
]
. The intuitive picture can be drawn as the middle picture of Figure
5
. In this picture, the spacetime ends at the bulk brane placed in the middle of the background wormhole geometry.
G_{\sigma}
represents the correlation between the spacetime boundary and this brane. Since we have a nontrivial profile for the bi-local field
G_{LR}
in this parameter region, the left and right boundaries are still connected by the wormhole. We imagine that as we gradually lower the value of
\sigma
, the “gash” made by the brane is getting bigger, and in the totally fixed-coupling limit
\sigma=0
, the wormhole will be eventually torn into two pieces (right picture in Figure
5
). This is consistent with the fact that the half-wormhole saddle in this limit represents no correlation between the left and right boundaries, i.e.,
G_{LR}=0
while there is a nontrivial profile for the half-bi-local fields
G_{\sigma}
and
\Sigma_{\sigma}
. This is also consistent with the bulk picture of the half-wormhole in
[
33
]
.

Figure 5:
The intuitive bulk pictures dual to the half-wormholes for various values of
\sigma
in the partially fixed-coupling SYK model. The left picture corresponds to the totally disorder-averaged limit
\sigma=\infty
, where the half-wormhole and the wormhole saddles degenerate. The middle corresponds to the slightly fixed-coupling parameter region, where the half-wormhole saddles can be considered as a small perturbation of the wormhole saddle. The right picture represents the half-wormhole in the totally fixed-coupling limit, where the correlation between the left and the right boundaries totally vanishes.

Linked and unlinked half-wormholes

We analyzed the saddle point solutions for
\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle_{J}
for large and small values of
\sigma
. We found that there exist both wormhole and half-wormhole saddles except for the totally disorder-averaged limit
\sigma=0
, where these two types of the saddles degenerate. In the totally disorder-averaged limit, only the half-wormhole saddles contribute to
\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle^{\sigma=0}_{J}
, and we can write it schematically as

\displaystyle\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle^{\sigma=0}_{J}\approx e^{-S_{\rm on\mathchar 45shell}^{(L)}({\rm Half\mathchar 45wormhole})}e^{-S_{\rm on\mathchar 45shell}^{(R)}({\rm Half\mathchar 45wormhole})}\,.

(6.1)

On the other hand, in
[
33
]
they analyzed the saddle point solutions in the (totally) fixed-coupling SYK model and found that

\displaystyle Z_{L}Z_{R}\underset{\rm SSSY}{\approx}e^{-S_{\rm on\mathchar 45shell}^{(LR)}({\rm Wormhole})}+e^{-S_{\rm on\mathchar 45shell}^{(LR)}({\rm(Linked)\,Half\mathchar 45wormhole})}\,,

(6.2)

i.e., the wormhole saddles and the half-wormhole saddles contribute in the same order to
Z_{L}Z_{R}
. Here “linked half-wormhole” means the half-wormhole saddle in the collective field description where both wormhole and half-wormhole saddle exist. On the other hand, in
[
33
]
they introduced another collective field description without wormholes. In this description, the half-wormholes are called “(unlinked) half-wormholes” to distinguish them from the linked half-wormholes. The half-wormhole saddles expressed as the nontrivial profile of the half-bi-local fields, i.e.,
G_{\sigma},\Sigma_{\sigma}\neq 0
discussed in this paper, would correspond to their unlinked half-wormhole saddles. In this paper, when writing the effective action for the collective fields (
4.6
) in
0
d SYK, we rescaled them to have nontrivial profiles of the wormhole solutions even in the strictly totally fixed coupling limit. It fits more into the situation of
[
33
]
given the collective fields without rescaling (
4.4
). As you can see, since
J_{\sigma}\rightarrow J_{0}
and
\tilde{J}\rightarrow 0
in the totally fixed-coupling limit, we can simply integrate over the bi-local field
G_{LR}
, forcing us to
\Sigma_{LR}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}(\tau_{1})\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}(\tau_{2})
. As a result, the effective action can be written solely with half-bi-local fields (as well as bi-local fields of the type
G_{LL},G_{RR},\Sigma_{LL},\Sigma_{RR}
). In this collective field description, it is obvious that there are only half-wormholes described by the half-bi-local fields and no wormholes represented by the solutions
G_{LR}\neq 0,\Sigma_{LR}\neq 0
with
G_{\sigma}^{L,R}=\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L,R}=0
. This situation corresponds to the collective field description with no wormhole saddles discussed in
[
33
]
.

Future directions

In this paper, we studied a partially disorder-averaged Majorana SYK model. Many generalizations of the SYK model were studied by various authors.
Some examples include the complex SYK model
[
52
,
53
,
54
]
, supersymmetric SYK model
[
56
,
57
,
58
]
,
SYK model with global symmetries
[
59
,
60
]
,
and higher dimensional generalization of the SYK model
[
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
]
.
It would be interesting to study a partially disorder-averaged version of these generalizations of the SYK model.

The initial motivation to introduce disorder in the original Sachdev-Ye model
[
1
,
65
,
66
]

was to simulate zero-temperature quantum phase transition between the quantum disordered spin-liquid phase and the magnetically ordered spin-glass phase.
It would be interesting to study whether our partially disorder-averaged model brings any further insights into this aspect.

The original motivation to consider the half-wormholes was to resolve the tension between the factorization of the partition functions and the existence of the wormholes in the bulk in the AdS/CFT context. We found that the wormhole solutions persist even in the fixed coupling limit, but they have zero measure in the path integral.
Instead, the half-wormhole solutions emerge as the “dissolved”-wormholes, and they dominate the partition function. It would be interesting to study whether a similar thing could happen in the gravitational path integral.

Appendix A
External couplings as another universe fermions

In this appendix, we discuss the interpretation of the
\theta_{i}
variables introduced in (
3.4
) as non-dynamical fermions living in another universe.
To consider another universe, we start from the two-point function (
4.1
).
Taking the total disorder-averaging (
\sigma=\infty
) of this function, we find

\displaystyle\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\int D\chi^{L}_{i}D\chi^{R}_{i}\,

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=L,R}\int d\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi^{a}_{i}\partial_{\tau}\chi^{a}_{i}

\displaystyle\ \qquad+\,\frac{(q-1)!\widetilde{J}^{2}}{2N^{q-1}}\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}}^{N}\left(\int d\tau\sum_{a=L,R}\chi^{a}_{i_{1}}\cdots\chi^{a}_{i_{q}}\right)^{2}\Bigg]\,.

(A.1)

Now we freeze the dynamics of
\chi^{R}_{i}
and denote the non-dynamical fermions as
\theta_{i}\propto\chi^{R}_{i}
.
Therefore, now we can interpret the above two-point function as a one-point function of
Z_{L}
with external fermionic sources
\theta_{i}
:

\displaystyle\big\langle Z_{L}\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\int D\chi_{i}\,

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}\partial_{\tau}\chi_{i}\,+\,\frac{J_{\sigma}}{qN^{q-1}}\int d\tau\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}(\tau)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{2qN^{q-1}}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})\right)^{q}\Bigg]\,,

(A.2)

where we denoted
\chi_{i}\equiv\chi^{L}_{i}
and also adjusted the proportionality constant of
\theta_{i}\propto\chi^{R}_{i}
to give the precise second term.
This effective action precisely agrees with (
3.5
), and in this sense, we can interpret the external variable
\theta_{i}
as non-dynamical fermions living in another universe.

Appendix B
Integration over the fermions

In this appendix, we present some details for the integration over fermions.
This is different from the usual Gaussian integrals of Grassmann variables due to the external variables
\theta_{i}
(
3.4
) and its anti-commutation relation

\displaystyle\{\theta_{i},\theta_{j}\}\,=\,\delta_{ij}\,.

(B.1)

To implement this anti-commutation relation in the path-integral,
we can regard this
\theta_{i}
as
N
-dimensional Dirac matrices constructed from the Pauli matrix (for example see
[
67
]
).

With this representation of
\theta_{i}
,
we first consider the fermion integration in the one-point function (of the one-dimensional model) studied in section
3
:

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}D\chi_{i}\,\exp\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})\,+\int d\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}(\tau)\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau)\theta_{i}\Bigg]\,.

(B.2)

For the kinetic term, using the delta function
\delta(G-N^{-1}\sum_{i}\chi_{i}\chi_{i})
, we rewrite it in terms of the bi-local field
G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
,
so that it does not appear in the fermion integral.
As usual Majorana fermion integrals, we introduce complex
N/2
fermions
c_{i}
and also complex
N/2
external variables
\varphi_{i}
by

\displaystyle\chi_{2i}\,=\,\frac{c_{i}+\bar{c}_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\qquad\quad\chi_{2i-1}\,=\,\frac{i(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(B.3)

\displaystyle\theta_{2i}\,=\,\frac{\varphi_{i}+\bar{\varphi}_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\qquad\quad\theta_{2i-1}\,=\,\frac{i(\varphi_{i}-\bar{\varphi}_{i})}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

(B.4)

Then, the above integral is given by

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{N/2}Dc_{i}D\bar{c}_{i}\,\exp\Bigg[\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2}c_{i}(\tau_{1})\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\bar{c}_{i}(\tau_{2})\,+\int d\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N/2}\Sigma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{i}\bar{c}_{i}+\bar{\varphi}_{i}c_{i})\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\,\Big[\textrm{Pf}\left(\Sigma+\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\sigma}\Sigma_{\sigma}\right)\Big]^{N}\,.

(B.5)

Exponentiating this into the effective action and sifting
\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\to\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{1})\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{2})
, we find

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[G,\Sigma,G_{\sigma},\Sigma_{\sigma}]\,=\,-\frac{N}{2}\textrm{Tr}\log(\Sigma)-\frac{N}{2}\int d\tau\big[\partial_{\tau}G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\big]_{\tau^{\prime}=\tau}

(B.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{N}{2}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{q}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+N\int d\tau\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau)G_{\sigma}(\tau)-\frac{J_{\sigma}}{q}G_{\sigma}(\tau)^{q}\right)\,-\,\frac{N}{4}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{1})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\Sigma_{\sigma}(\tau_{2})\,.

For the zero-dimensional case in section
3.1
, we do not have the bi-local fields. Therefore, the fermion integral is simply

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}d\chi_{i}\,\exp\bigg[-\Sigma_{\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\chi_{i}\bigg]\,=\,\big(-\Sigma_{\sigma}\big)^{N}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\bigg)\,.

(B.7)

Finally, let us consider the fermion integration for the two-point function in the zero-dimensional SYK model studied in section
4.1
.
Since we have

\displaystyle\quad\int d\chi^{L}d\chi^{R}\,\exp\Big[\Sigma_{LR}\chi^{L}\chi^{R}\Big]\exp\Big[\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\theta\chi^{L}\Big]\exp\Big[\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\theta\chi^{R}\Big]

\displaystyle=\,\int d\chi^{L}d\chi^{R}\Big(1+\Sigma_{LR}\chi^{L}\chi^{R}+\theta^{2}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\chi^{L}\chi^{R}+\cdots\Big)

\displaystyle=\,\Sigma_{LR}+\frac{1}{2}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\,,

(B.8)

where we used
\theta^{2}=1/2
, the integrating out
\chi^{L}_{i}\chi^{R}_{i}
gives
-N\log(\Sigma_{LR}+\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R})
in the effective action.
Sifting
\Sigma_{LR}\to\Sigma_{LR}-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}
, we find

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[G_{LR},\Sigma_{LR},G_{\sigma}^{a},\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}]\,

\displaystyle=\,-N\log(\Sigma_{LR})\,+\,N\left(\Sigma_{LR}G_{LR}-\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{q}\,G_{LR}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+N\sum_{a}\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}G_{\sigma}^{a}-\frac{J_{\sigma}}{q}\big(G_{\sigma}^{a}\big)^{q}\right)\,-\,\frac{N}{2}G_{LR}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\,.

(B.9)

To be precise, we should regard
G_{\sigma}^{a}
as a matrix as

\displaystyle\big[G_{\sigma}^{L},G_{\sigma}^{R}\big]\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\big[\theta_{i}\chi_{i}^{L},\theta_{j}\chi_{j}^{R}\big]

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}(\theta_{i}\theta_{j}+\theta_{j}\theta_{i})\chi_{i}^{L}\chi_{j}^{R}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i}\chi_{i}^{L}\chi_{i}^{R}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{N}\,G_{LR}\,.

(B.10)

However, as long as we keep the large
N
limit, we can treat
G_{\sigma}^{a}
as a usual
c
-number.

Appendix C
Another formalism for slightly fixed-coupling

In this appendix, we will show that the same structure we saw in section
3.3
and
3.4

also appears in another formulation of the slightly fixed-coupling SYK model.
For simplicity, in this appendix, we focus on the
q=4
case.

For this purpose, instead of (
3
), we introduce another Hubbard–Stratonovich trick

\displaystyle 1\,

\displaystyle=\,\int DG\int DG^{\sigma}_{ij}\,\delta\left(G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})\right)\delta\Big(G^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)-\chi_{i}(\tau)\chi_{j}(\tau)\Big)

\displaystyle=\,\int DGD\Sigma\int DG^{\sigma}_{ij}D\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}\exp\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\,\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\left(NG(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 160.0pt-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int d\tau\,\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)\Big(G^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)-\chi_{i}(\tau)\chi_{j}(\tau)\Big)\Bigg]\,.

(C.1)

Using this trick for the partially disorder-averaged partition function and performing the Gaussian integral for
\chi_{i}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\langle Z\big\rangle_{J}\,=\,\int DGD\Sigma DG^{\sigma}D\Sigma^{\sigma}\,e^{-S_{\rm eff}[G,\Sigma,G^{\sigma},\Sigma^{\sigma}]}\,,

(C.2)

with

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[G,\Sigma,G^{\sigma},\Sigma^{\sigma}]\,

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\log(\Delta_{ij})+\frac{N}{2}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{4}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int d\tau\left(\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)G^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)+\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{12\sigma^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}J_{ijkl}^{(0)}G^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)G^{\sigma}_{kl}(\tau)\right)\,,

(C.3)

where the trace is now taken both for the indices

and the bi-local time
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
with

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,\equiv\,\delta_{ij}\Big(\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\partial_{\tau_{2}}-\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\Big)-\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,.

(C.4)

If this
\Delta_{ij}
is completely diagonal for the indices

in front of the trace term, but for a general

, we miss this factor of

The extra local fields
\{G^{\sigma},\Sigma^{\sigma}\}
represents a non-singlet sector of the global

In general, it is hard to understand the non-singlet sector of a large

theory
[

,

.
In the context of AdS

/CFT

, these fields are probably related to the open string degrees of freedom attached to the boundary (see section 6.2 of
[
4
]
).

Even though we miss the factor of
N
in front of the trace term and the second line of the above action, let us here pretend that we can use the large

Variation of each field leads to saddle-point equations

\displaystyle\delta G\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\widetilde{J}^{2}\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{3}\,,

(C.5)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\Delta^{-1}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\Big]_{ii}\,=\,G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,,

(C.6)

\displaystyle\delta G^{\sigma}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)\,=\,-\,\frac{\widetilde{J}^{2}}{6\sigma^{2}}\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\,J_{ijkl}^{(0)}G^{\sigma}_{kl}(\tau)\,,

(C.7)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma^{\sigma}\,:

\displaystyle\qquad\Big[\Delta_{ij}^{-1}\Big](\tau,\tau)\,=\,G^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)\,,

(C.8)

where we defined two kinds of inverse. The first one is inverse for the bi-local time:

\displaystyle\int d\tau_{3}\,\Delta_{ij}(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})\Big[\Delta^{-1}(\tau_{3},\tau_{2})\Big]_{ij}\,=\,\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,,\qquad\quad({\rm for\ fixed}\ i,\,j)\,,

(C.9)

and the second one is for the
O(N)
index:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\Delta_{ik}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\Big[\Delta^{-1}_{kj}\Big](\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,=\,\delta_{ij}\,,\qquad\quad({\rm for\ fixed}\ \tau_{1},\,\tau_{2})\,.

(C.10)

We first note that the saddle-point solution of the diagonal part of
G^{\sigma}
must be given by

\displaystyle G^{\sigma}_{ii}(\tau)\,=\,\big\langle\chi_{i}(\tau)\chi_{i}(\tau)\big\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,,

(C.11)

where for the second equality, we used the equal-time anti-commutation relation of the fermion (
2.2
).
Let us now consider the following external coupling
J_{ijkl}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\qquad J_{ijkl}^{(0)}\,=\,\frac{J_{0}}{6}\Big(\delta_{ij}\delta_{ik}\delta_{il}\,+\,A_{ijkl}^{(0)}\Big)\,,

(C.12)

where

is the totally anti-symmetric tensor without any diagonal piece. Namely, if any of the indices coincide,

.
Then, from (

) the index structure of
\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}
is decomposed into symmetric and antisymmetric parts as

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}(\tau)\,=\,\delta_{ij}\Sigma^{\sigma}_{\rm dig}(\tau)\,+\,\Sigma^{\rm ant}_{ij}(\tau)\,,

(C.13)

and the equation (

) is reduce to

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\sigma}_{\rm dig}(\tau)\,

\displaystyle=\,-\,\frac{J_{\sigma}}{N}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}\,G^{\sigma}_{kk}(\tau)\,=\,-\frac{J_{\sigma}}{2}\,,

(C.14)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\rm ant}_{ij}(\tau)\,

\displaystyle=\,-\,\frac{J_{\sigma}}{N}\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\,A_{ijkl}^{(0)}G^{\sigma}_{kl}(\tau)\,.

(C.15)

Using this expression of
\Sigma^{\sigma}_{ij}
into (
C.4
), we can see that the diagonal components of
\Delta_{ij}
are independent of the index

\displaystyle\Delta_{ii}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,

\displaystyle=\,\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\left(\partial_{\tau_{2}}+\frac{J_{\sigma}}{2}\right)\,-\,\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\,\Delta_{\textrm{Dig}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,.

(C.16)

Therefore, the summation in (

) is trivially taken and gives a factor of

, which cancels with the

coefficient.
Hence, we can simply invert
\Delta^{-1}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
and this equation is now written as

\displaystyle\delta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\,=\,\int d\tau_{3}\,\Delta_{\textrm{Dig}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})G(\tau_{3},\tau_{2})\,.

(C.17)

The modification from the ordinary SYK is the
J_{\sigma}
term in (
C.16
), which acts as a mass or chemical potential term.

Appendix D
Two-point function of
q=2
, 0d SYK

In this appendix, we study the
q=2
case of the
0d
SYK model discussed in section
4.1
.
For
q=2
, we have the saddle-point equations

\displaystyle\Sigma_{LR}\,=\,G_{LR}\,+\,\lambda_{\sigma}\,\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\Sigma_{LR}}\,=\,G_{LR}\,,

(D.1)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}\,=\,G_{\sigma}^{a}\,,\qquad G_{\sigma}^{L}\,=\,\lambda_{\sigma}G_{LR}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\,,\qquad G_{\sigma}^{R}\,=\,\lambda_{\sigma}G_{LR}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\,.

(D.2)

From (
D.2
), we find equations for
G_{\sigma}^{L}
as

\displaystyle\Big[\big(\lambda_{\sigma}G_{LR}\big)^{2}+1\Big]G_{\sigma}^{L}\,=\,0\,.

(D.3)

Therefore, the trivial solution is
\{G_{\sigma}^{L}\,,G_{\sigma}^{R}\}=\{0,0\}
, which can be understood as the “wormhole” saddle-point solution again.
The nontrivial solution can be found only if

\displaystyle G_{LR}\,=\,\pm\,\lambda_{\sigma}^{-1}\,.

(D.4)

In this case,
\{G_{\sigma}^{L}\,,G_{\sigma}^{R}\}
are not yet fixed by the equations (
D.2
).
Since in the totally fixed-coupling limit (
\sigma\to 0
), we have
\lambda_{\sigma}^{-1}\propto\sigma^{2/q}
, this solution behaves as
G_{LR}\to 0
in this limit.
Therefore, this nontrivial solution is understood as the “half-wormhole” saddle-point solution.

For the wormhole case, the solution of the bi-local sector is again given by (
4.12
) as
\{G_{LR},\Sigma_{LR}\}=\{\pm 1,\pm 1\}
.
For the half-wormhole case, solving (
D.1
), we find

\displaystyle\{G_{\sigma}^{L}\,,G_{\sigma}^{R}\}\,=\,\left\{\pm\sqrt{1-\lambda_{\sigma}^{-2}}\,,\,\sqrt{1-\lambda_{\sigma}^{-2}}\right\}\,.

(D.5)

The on-shell actions are obtained

\displaystyle S^{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}_{\scriptsize\mbox{WH}}\,=\,\frac{N}{2}\,+\,m\pi iN\,,

(D.6)

for the wormhole saddles with
m=1,2
, and

\displaystyle S^{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}_{\scriptsize\mbox{HW}}\,\approx\,-\,N\log(\lambda_{\sigma})\,+\,N\sqrt{1-\lambda_{\sigma}^{-2}}\,+\,n\pi iN\,.

(D.7)

for the half-wormhole solutions with
n=1,2
.

Appendix E
Direct computation of
\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle_{J}
at
\sigma=0

In this appendix, we directly solve the two-point function (
4.8
) at
\sigma=0
.
Because the prefactor
\widetilde{J}^{2N/q}\to\sigma^{2N/q}
in the
\sigma\to 0
limit, while the coupling in the effective action (
4.6
) is
\lambda_{\sigma}\to\sigma^{-2/q}
in this limit,
so that the action gives a divergence.
To get a finite non-zero result,
we have to expand the interaction term of (
4.6
) perturbatively and consider the
N
-th order term (which gives
\lambda_{\sigma}^{N}
).
Therefore, the two-point function at
\sigma=0
is given by

\displaystyle\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle_{J}^{\sigma=0}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{N^{N}J_{0}^{\frac{2N}{q}}}{2^{N}N!}\int dG_{LR}\,\frac{d\Sigma_{LR}}{2\pi i/N}\int\prod_{a}^{L,R}dG_{\sigma}^{a}\,\frac{d\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}}{2\pi i/N}\,\Big(G_{LR}\Sigma_{LR}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\Sigma_{\sigma}^{R}\Big)^{N}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left[-N\left(\Sigma_{LR}G_{LR}-\frac{1}{q}G_{LR}^{q}\right)-N\sum_{a}\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}^{a}G_{\sigma}^{a}-\frac{1}{q}(G_{\sigma}^{a})^{q}\right)\right]\,.

(E.1)

Now we can see that each set of
\{G,\Sigma\}
decoupled from each other.
Therefore, we can perform the integrals separately. For the bi-local sector, we have

\displaystyle\int dG_{LR}\,\frac{d\Sigma_{LR}}{2\pi i/N}\,\Big(G_{LR}\Sigma_{LR}\Big)^{N}\exp\left[-N\left(\Sigma_{LR}G_{LR}-\frac{1}{q}G_{LR}^{q}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int dG_{LR}\,e^{\frac{N}{q}G_{LR}^{q}}\big(G_{LR}\big)^{N}\big(-N^{-1}\partial_{G_{LR}}\big)^{N}\delta(G_{LR})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\,\frac{N!}{N^{N}}\,,

(E.2)

while for the local sector, we have

\displaystyle\int dG_{\sigma}^{L}\,\frac{d\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}}{2\pi i/N}\,\big(\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}\big)^{N}\exp\left[-N\left(\Sigma_{\sigma}^{L}G_{\sigma}^{L}-\frac{1}{q}(G_{\sigma}^{L})^{q}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int dG_{\sigma}^{L}\,e^{\frac{N}{q}(G_{\sigma}^{L})^{q}}\big(-N^{-1}\partial_{G_{\sigma}^{L}}\big)^{N}\delta(G_{\sigma}^{L})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\,\frac{N!}{N^{N}}\frac{(N/q)^{N/q}}{(N/q)!}\,.

(E.3)

Therefore, combining these integral results, we find that the two-point function at
\sigma=0
is given by

\displaystyle\big\langle Z_{L}Z_{R}\big\rangle_{J}^{\sigma=0}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{J_{0}^{\frac{2N}{q}}}{2^{N}}\left(\frac{N!}{N^{N}}\frac{(N/q)^{N/q}}{(N/q)!}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\,2^{-N}J_{0}^{\frac{2N}{q}}\,q\,e^{-2N\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\,,

(E.4)

where for the last line, we used the large
N
approximation besides the factor
2^{-N}J_{0}^{2N/q}
.
This result agrees with the saddle-point evaluation (
4.21
).

Abstract
. In this paper we are interested in the partial sums

When we consider the case of the squarefree integers (the case

By these results for a random multiplicative function we can naturally ask if there are examples of multiplicative functions

Our examples in Theorems

The original formulation of the Erdös discrepancy problem (EDP) is whether there exists an arithmetic function

such that its partial sums over any homegenous arithmetic progression are uniformly bounded by some constant

. As shown by Tao in
[
16

[
16
]

A consequence of the solution of the EDP is that a completely multiplicative function

has unbounded partial sums. It is important to mention that if we allow completely multiplicative functions vanish over a finite subset of primes, then we might get bounded partial sums, for instance, any real and non-principal Dirichlet character
\chi

Following Granville and Soundararajan
[
6
]
, define the “distance” up to

\mathbb{D}(f,g;x):=\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}\frac{1-Re(f(p)\overline{g(p)})}{p}\right)^{1/2},

where in the sum above

In the resolution of the EDP (see Remark 3.1 of
[

\frac{1}{\log x}\sumop\displaylimits_{\sqrt{x}\leq n\leq x}\frac{|f(n)|^{2}}{n}\gg 1
. Thus, if we seek for multiplicative functions supported on the
k
-free integers with small partial sums, then we must look firstly to those that are

\chi
. In our particular case, to construct examples for theorems

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}|1-g(p)\chi(p)|\ll\frac{x^{1/k}}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log x})}.

The condition (

We observe that if

2.1.
Partial sums of
\mu
and of
\mu\chi

As stated in the introduction, for some constant
c>0
, the partial sums of the Möbius function are (unconditionally)

(3)

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\mu(n)\ll\frac{x}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log x})}.

This can be obtained by adapting the proof of the Prime Number Theorem as in
[
13
]
, chapter 6. Indeed, this follows by combining an effective Perron’s formula (Corollary 5.3 of
[

]
) with the classical zero free region for the Riemann zeta function, namely those points

, and the estimate

, valid for

in the classical zero free region with
|t|\geq 1
.

If
\chi
is a real and non-principal Dirichlet character, then we also have that for some constant
c>0
, the function
\mu\chi
has (unconditionally) partial sums

(4)

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\mu(n)\chi(n)\ll\frac{x}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log x})}.

The proof of this result follows the same line of reasoning of the proof of (
3
) with some extra care. This extra care is related to exceptional zeros of

functions. Indeed, the Dirichlet series of
\mu\chi
is

, where

is the Dirichlet

function associated to

. The

has a zero free region of the same type of Riemann zeta function with at most one possible exception, a real zero

inside this region, see
[
13
]
Theorem 11.3. Further, by Theorem 11.4 of
[
13
]
, whether there exists an exceptional zero or not, we have that for

in this zero free region with sufficiently large

, and thus the proof of (
4
) is completed with an effective Perron’s formula.

Now if we assume the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), then for any
\epsilon>0
the partial sums

(5)

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\mu(n)\ll x^{1/2+\epsilon}.

A proof of this can be found at

Finally, under GRH, for a real and non-principal Dirichlet character

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\mu(n)\chi(n)\ll x^{1/2+\epsilon},

and the proof follows the lines of the proof of (

2.2.
The Dirichlet Hyperbola Method

This method allow us to evaluate the partial sums of the Dirichlet convolution between two arithmetic functions
h
and
g
. We recall that the Dirichlet convolution is defined as
h\ast g(n)=\sumop\displaylimits_{d|n}h(d)g(n/d)
, where the notation
d|n
means that
d
divides
n
. Denote
M_{f}(x):=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}f(n)
. The Dirichlet hyperbola method states: If
UV=x
with
U,V\geq 1
, then

(7)

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}(h\ast g)(n)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}h(n)M_{g}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)+\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}g(n)M_{h}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)-M_{g}(V)M_{h}(U).

Lemma 3.1

Let

Proof.

By partial summation, or by Kroenecker’s Lemma (see

14

\sumop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h(n)\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log n})}{n^{1/k}}.

Notation: By

\sqrt{\log n}=\sqrt{\sumop\displaylimits_{p^{r}\|n}\log p^{r}}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{p^{r}\|n}\sqrt{\log p^{r}}

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\frac{h(n)\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log n})}{n^{1/k}}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\frac{\tilde{h}(n)}{n^{1/k}},

where

is the multiplicative function such that
\tilde{h}(p^{r})=\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log p^{r}})h(p^{r})
, for all primes

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\frac{\tilde{h}(n)}{n^{1/k}}

[
17
]
), we only need to show that
\sumop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}(p^{r})}{p^{r/k}}
converges, where

Let

be small such that
\frac{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log p})}{p^{1/k}}<1
for all primes

. By the hypothesis
(i)

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}(p^{r})}{p^{r/k}}

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{h(p^{r})\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log{p^{r}}})}{p^{r/k}}\leq h(p)\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(\delta\sqrt{r+2}\sqrt{\log{p}})}{p^{\frac{r+2}{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq h(p)\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(\delta(r+2)\sqrt{\log{p}})}{p^{\frac{r+2}{k}}}=h(p)\frac{\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log{p}})}{p^{2/k}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(r\delta\sqrt{\log{p}})}{p^{r/k}}

\displaystyle=h(p)\frac{\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log p})}{p^{2/k}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log p})}{p^{1/k}}}\ll h(p)\frac{\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log{p}})}{p^{1/k}(p^{1/k}-\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log{p}}))}

\displaystyle\ll_{\delta}\frac{h(p)\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log p})}{p^{1/k}}.

Define

if

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}\frac{h(p)\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log p})}{p^{1/k}}

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{1}^{x}\frac{\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log t})}{t^{1/k}}dT(t)

\displaystyle\ll T(x)\frac{\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log x})}{x^{1/k}}+\intop\nolimits_{1}^{x}T(t)\frac{\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log t})}{t^{1+\frac{1}{k}}}dt

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\exp((c-2\delta)\sqrt{\log x})}+\intop\nolimits_{1}^{x}\frac{1}{t\exp((c-2\delta)\sqrt{\log t})}dt

\displaystyle\ll 1,

where in the penultimate inequality we used the hypothesis
(ii)
.

Thus,

\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}(p^{r})}{p^{r/k}}\ll_{\delta}\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}\frac{h(p)\exp(2\delta\sqrt{\log{p}})}{p^{1/k}}\ll 1.

∎

g=h\ast\chi
, we have that
\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}g(n)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}h(n)\sumop\displaylimits_{m\leq x/n}\chi(m)
, and as
\chi
has bounded partial sums, we have that
\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}g(n)\ll\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}|h(n)|
. ∎

Before we state our next Lemma, we will introduce some notation. Given an arithmetic function
f
, we denote its partial sums up to
x
by

M_{f}(x):=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}f(n).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g:\mathbb{N}\to\{-1,1\}
be multiplicative and let
\chi
be a real and non-principal Dirichlet character. Assume that for some
\delta>0

\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}|1-g(p)\chi(p)|\ll\frac{x^{1/k}}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log x})},

where
k\geq 2
. Then, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

M_{\mu g}(x)\ll\frac{x}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log x})}.

Proof.

Recall that
\chi=(\mu\chi)^{-1}
, where the exponent
-1
means that we are taking the inverse with respect to the Dirichlet convolution. Define
h:=\mu g\ast(\mu\chi)^{-1}
. Let
1/L(s,\chi)
,
Z(s)
,
H(s)
be the Dirichlet series associated to
\mu\chi
,
\mu g
and
h
, respectively. Recall the Euler product formula for
L(s,\chi)
:

L(s,\chi)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1+\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{\chi(p)^{2}}{p^{2s}}+\cdots\bigg).

For
\mu g
we have

Z(s)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)g(n)}{n^{s}}=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1-\frac{g(p)}{p^{s}}\bigg).

By the rules of Dirichlet convolution, we can write
H(s)=Z(s)L(s,\chi)
, and hence

\displaystyle H(s)

\displaystyle=Z(s)L(s,\chi)=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1+\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{\chi(p)^{2}}{p^{2s}}+\cdots\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{g(p)}{p^{s}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1+\frac{\chi(p)-g(p)}{p^{s}}+\cdots+\frac{\chi(p)^{n-1}(\chi(p)-g(p))}{p^{ns}}+\cdots\right).

Since
h
is multiplicative, we have that
h(p^{n})=\chi(p)^{n-1}(\chi(p)-g(p))
. Thus
|h(p)|\leq 2
and for all powers
r
,
|h(p^{r})|\leq|h(p)|
. Moreover, since
|\chi(p)-g(p)|\leq|1-\chi(p)g(p)|
, we have by hypothesis that

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}|h(p)|

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}|\chi(p)-g(p)|\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{p\leq x}|1-\chi(p)g(p)|\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\left(\delta\sqrt{\log x}\right)}.

Thus, by Lemma
3.1
, there exists a new
\delta>0
such that

|M_{h}(x)|\leq M_{|h|}(x)\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\left(\delta\sqrt{\log x}\right)}\ll x^{1/k}.

In particular, by partial summation, the series
\sumop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|h(n)|}{n}
converges.

Now we will use the Dirichlet Hyperbola method for
\mu g=h\ast\mu\chi
, see subsection
2.2
. For all
U\geq 1
and
V\geq 1
such that
UV=x
, we have that

M_{\mu g}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}h(n)M_{\mu\chi}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)+\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}\mu(n)\chi(n)M_{h}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)-M_{\mu\chi}(U)M_{h}(V):=A+B-C.

By (
4
), we have that for some constant
a>0
,
M_{\mu\chi}(x)\ll\frac{x}{\exp(a\sqrt{\log{x}})}
. Now choose
V=\exp(\epsilon\sqrt{\log{x}})
, where
0<\epsilon<a
. Further, let
c>0
be a parameter
c<\min\left(\epsilon\frac{k-1}{k},a\right)
.

Estimate for
A
.

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}|h(n)M_{\mu\chi}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)|\ll\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}|h(n)|\frac{x}{n\exp\left(a\sqrt{\log\frac{x}{n}}\right)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(a\sqrt{\log\frac{x}{V}}\right)}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}\frac{|h(n)|}{n}\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(a\sqrt{\log\frac{x}{V}}\right)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(a\sqrt{\log x-\epsilon\sqrt{\log x}}\right)}\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(a\sqrt{\log x}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{\log x}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(c\sqrt{\log x}\right)}.

Estimate for
B
.

\displaystyle\left|B\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}\left|\mu(n)\chi(n)M_{h}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)\right|\ll\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)^{1/k}\ll x^{1/k}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}\frac{1}{n^{1/k}}\ll x^{\frac{1}{k}}U^{1-\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(\epsilon\frac{k-1}{k}\sqrt{\log{x}}\right)}\ll\frac{x}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log{x}})}.

Estimate for
C
.

\displaystyle C=M_{\mu\chi}(U)M_{h}(V)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{UV^{1/k}}{\exp(a\sqrt{\log{U}})}\ll\frac{x}{V}\frac{V^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(a\sqrt{\log{x}-\log{V})}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{V^{1-\frac{1}{k}}\exp\left(a\sqrt{\log{x}-\epsilon\sqrt{\log{x}}}\right)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(a\sqrt{\log{x}-\epsilon\sqrt{\log{x}}}+\epsilon\frac{k-1}{k}\sqrt{\log{x}}\right)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\exp\left(\sqrt{\log{x}}\left(a\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\right)^{1/2}+\epsilon\frac{k-1}{k}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log{x}})}.

By combining these estimates for
A
,
B
, and
C
we conclude the proof. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
h:=f\ast g^{-1}
, where
g^{-1}
is the inverse of
g
with respect to the Dirichlet convolution. Let
F
,
G
and
H
be the Dirichlet series associated to
f
,
g
and
h
respectively. Since
g
is completely multiplicative, by the Euler product formula we have that

G(s)=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1-\frac{g(p)}{p^{s}}\bigg)^{-1}.

For
f=\mu_{k}^{2}g
, we have that

F(s)=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1+\frac{f(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{f(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\dots\bigg)=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1+\frac{g(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{g(p)^{2}}{p^{2s}}+\dots+\frac{g(p)^{k-1}}{p^{(k-1)s}}\bigg).

Since
h=f\ast g^{-1}
, we can write
H(s)=F(s)/G(s)
, and hence

\displaystyle H(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{F(s)}{G(s)}=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1+\frac{g(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{g(p)^{2}}{p^{2s}}+\dots+\frac{g(p)^{k-1}}{p^{(k-1)s}}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{g(p)}{p^{s}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\prodop\displaylimits_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(1-\frac{g(p)^{k}}{p^{ks}}\bigg).

Since
h
is multiplicative, we obtain that
h(p^{k})=-g(p)^{k}
and
h(p^{r})=0
, for all
r\geq 1
and
r\neq k
. Thus,
h(n)=0
unless
n=m^{k}
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Let
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n^{1/k})
be the indicator that
n^{1/k}
is an integer. Now observe that if
k
is odd, then
h(p^{k})=-g(p)^{k}=-g(p)
, since
g(p)=\pm 1
, and hence
h(n)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n^{1/k})\mu(n^{1/k})g(n^{1/k})
.

Thus, in the case that
k
is odd, by Lemma
3.3
, for some constant
c>0
we have that

M_{h}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}h(n)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n^{\frac{1}{k}})\mu(n^{\frac{1}{k}})g(n^{\frac{1}{k}})\\
=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x^{\frac{1}{k}}}\mu(n)g(n)\\
\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log{x}^{1/k}})}.

If
k
is even, then
h(p^{k})=-1
, and hence
h(n)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n^{1/k})\mu(n^{1/k})
. By (
3
), we have for some (new) constant
c>0
,
M_{\mu}(x)\ll\frac{x}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log{x}})}
. Therefore,

M_{h}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n^{\frac{1}{k}})\mu(n^{\frac{1}{k}})=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq x^{\frac{1}{k}}}\mu(n)\ll\frac{x^{1/k}}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log{x^{1/k}}})}.

Thus, for any integer
k\geq 1
, for some constant
c>0
,

M_{h}(x)\ll\frac{x^{1/k}}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log{x^{1/k}}})}.\\

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.2
, for some
\delta>0
, we have that

M_{g}(x)\ll\frac{x^{1/k}}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log x})}.

Now we use the Dirichlet hyperbola method (subsection
2.2
): For all
U\geq 1
and
V\geq 1
such that
UV=x
, we have that

(8)

M_{f}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}h(n)M_{g}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)+\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}g(n)M_{h}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)-M_{g}(V)M_{h}(U):=A+B-C.

We choose
V=\exp(\epsilon(\sqrt{\log x}))
, where
0<\epsilon<\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}
and
U=\frac{x}{V}
. Besides that,
\lambda>0
is a parameter

\lambda<\min(\delta\sqrt{\epsilon},\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{\epsilon(k-1)}{k})
.

Estimate for
A
.

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n^{\frac{1}{k}})M_{g}(x/n)|=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U^{\frac{1}{k}}}|M_{g}(x/n^{k})|

\displaystyle\ll\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U^{\frac{1}{k}}}\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{n}\frac{1}{\exp\bigg(\delta\sqrt{\log\frac{x}{n^{k}}}\bigg)}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\bigg(\delta\sqrt{\log\frac{x}{U}}\bigg)}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U^{\frac{1}{k}}}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}\log{U}}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log{V}})}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}(\log{x}-\log{V})}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\epsilon}(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{4}})}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}\exp(\log{\log{x}})}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\epsilon}(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{4}})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(\lambda(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{4}})},

since
\lambda<\delta\sqrt{\epsilon}
.

Estimate for
B
.

\displaystyle|B|

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}|g(n)M_{h}(x/n)|\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}|M_{h}(x/n)|\ll\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{1}{\exp\bigg(c\sqrt{\log({\frac{x}{n})^{\frac{1}{k}}}}\bigg)}

\displaystyle\ll x^{\frac{1}{k}}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{k}}}\frac{1}{\exp\bigg(\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\sqrt{\log{\frac{x}{n}}}\bigg)}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\bigg(\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\sqrt{\log{\frac{x}{V}}}\bigg)}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{k}}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}V^{\frac{k-1}{k}}}{\exp\bigg(\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\sqrt{\log{x}-\log{V}}\bigg)}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}\exp\bigg(\epsilon\frac{k-1}{k}\sqrt{\log{x}}\bigg)}{\exp\bigg(\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\sqrt{\log{x}-\epsilon(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\bigg(\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\sqrt{\log{x}-\epsilon(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\epsilon\frac{k-1}{k}\sqrt{\log{x}}\bigg)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\bigg((\log{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\bigg(1-\frac{\epsilon}{(\log{x})^{1/2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\epsilon(k-1)}{k}\bigg)\bigg)}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\bigg(\lambda(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp\big(\lambda(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{4}}\big)},

since
0<\lambda<\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{\epsilon(k-1)}{k}
.

Estimate for
C
.

\displaystyle C=M_{g}(V)M_{h}(U)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{V^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log{V}})}\frac{U^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(c\sqrt{\log{U^{1/k}}})}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log{V}})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\log{\exp(\epsilon\sqrt{\log{x}})}})}\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(\delta\sqrt{\epsilon}(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{4}})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\exp(\lambda(\log{x})^{\frac{1}{4}})}.

Combining these estimates for
A
,
B
and
C
we complete the proof. ∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof.

Let
\chi
be any real and non-principal Dirichlet character
\mbox{mod }q
and
g:\mathbb{N}\to\{-1,1\}
be the completely multiplicative function such that:

\displaystyle g(n)

\displaystyle=\chi(n),\mbox{ if }\gcd(n,q)=1,

\displaystyle g(p)

\displaystyle=1,\mbox{ for each prime }p|q.

Let
\omega(q)
be number of distinct primes that divide
q
. Then, by Lemma 2.4 of
[
3
]
:

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{|M_{g}(x)|}{(\log x)^{\omega(q)}}\leq\frac{\max_{y\geq 1}|M_{\chi}(y)|}{\omega(q)!}\prodop\displaylimits_{p|q}\frac{1}{\log p}.

In particular,
M_{g}(x)\ll x^{\alpha}
, for each
\alpha>0
.

Let
h=f\ast g^{-1}
, where
g^{-1}
is the Dirichlet inverse of
g
. As in the proof of theorems
1.1
and
1.3
above, if
k
is odd, we have that
h(n)=\mathbbm{1}{N}(n^{\frac{1}{k}})\mu(n^{\frac{1}{k}})g(n^{\frac{1}{k}})
; If
k
is even, then
h(n)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n^{\frac{1}{k}})\mu(n^{\frac{1}{k}})
.

In any case, by assuming GRH, we have (
5
) and (
6
), and hence, for any
k\geq 2
,
M_{h}(x)\ll x^{\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}
, for all
\epsilon>0
.

Now we use the Dirichlet Hyperbola method following the same notation of (
8
), and setting
U=x^{\frac{2k}{2k+1}}
and
V=x^{\frac{1}{2k+1}}
. It is noteworthy that these choices for
U
and
V
are optimal in our method.

Estimate for
A
.

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U}\bigg|h(n)M_{g}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)\bigg|=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U^{\frac{1}{k}}}\bigg|M_{g}\bigg(\frac{x}{n^{k}}\bigg)\bigg|\ll\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq U^{\frac{1}{k}}}\frac{x^{\alpha}}{n^{k\alpha}}\ll x^{\alpha}U^{\frac{1}{k}(1-k\alpha)}

\displaystyle\ll x^{\alpha}x^{\frac{2}{2k+1}(1-k\alpha)}\ll x^{\frac{2}{2k+1}+\alpha\left(1-\frac{2k}{2k+1}\right)}\ll x^{\frac{2}{2k+1}+\frac{\alpha}{2k+1}}.

Estimate for
B
.

\displaystyle|B|

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}\bigg|g(n)M_{h}\bigg(\frac{x}{n}\bigg)\bigg|\ll x^{\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\leq V}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}}\ll x^{\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}V^{1-\frac{1}{2k}-\epsilon}

\displaystyle\ll x^{\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}x^{\frac{1}{2k+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2k}-\epsilon\right)}\ll x^{\frac{2}{2k+1}+\epsilon\left(1-\frac{1}{2k+1}\right)}\ll x^{\frac{2}{2k+1}+\frac{\epsilon 2k}{2k+1}}.

Estimate for
C
.

\displaystyle C=M_{g}(V)M_{h}(U)\ll V^{\alpha}U^{\frac{1}{2k}+\epsilon}\ll x^{\frac{\alpha}{2k+1}}x^{\frac{1}{2k+1}+\frac{\epsilon 2k}{2k+1}}\ll x^{\frac{1}{2k+1}+\frac{\alpha}{2k+1}+\frac{\epsilon 2k}{2k+1}}.

Since we have freedom to choose
\alpha
and
\epsilon
arbitrarily small, the proof is complete. ∎

A simulation

In the figure below, we plot the partial sums of
\mu^{2}\chi_{3}
up to
x=10^{7}
, where
\chi_{3}
is the non-principal Dirichlet character with modulus
3
.

Figure 1.
The dashed curves are given by
x\mapsto\pm x^{1/4}
, and the continuous line is given by

As we observed in the introduction, for a quadratic character

,

is not

Constraining Dark Energy from Local Group dynamics
David Benisty, Anne-Christine Davis, N. Wyn Evans

Abstract
This Letter develops a method to constrain the Cosmological Constant Λ \Lambda from binary galaxies, focusing on the Milky Way and Andromeda. We provide an analytical solution to the two-body problem with Λ \Lambda and show that the ratio between the Keplerian period and T Λ = 2 ​ π / ( c ​ Λ ) ≈ 63.2 T_{\Lambda}=2\pi/(c\sqrt{\Lambda})\approx 63.2 Gyr controls the importance of effects from the Cosmological Constant. The Andromeda-Milky Way orbit has a period of ∼ 20 \sim 20 Gyr and so Dark Energy has to be taken into account. Using the current best mass estimates of the Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies, we find the Cosmological Constant value based only on the Local Group dynamics to be lower then 5.44 5.44 times the value obtained by Planck. With future astrometric measurements, the bound on the Cosmological Constant can be reduced to ( 1.67 ± 0.79 ) ​ Λ PL \left(1.67\pm 0.79\right)\Lambda_{\rm PL} . Our results offer the prospects of constraints on Λ \Lambda over very different scales than previously. The Local Group provides also a completely novel platform to test alternative theories of gravity. We illustrate this by deriving bounds on scalar-tensor theories of gravity over Megaparsec scales.



I
Introduction

The discovery that the cosmic expansion is accelerating puzzled scientists and led them to propose the existence of a mysterious “dark energy” that makes up 68% of the energy of the observable universe
(
Perlmutter et al. 1999
;
Riess et al. 2019
;
Aghanim et al. 2020a
)
. The Cosmological Constant is also important on the local Universe scale
(
Chernin et al. 2002
;
Kim et al. 2020
;
Karachentsev et al. 2003
;
Silbergleit & Chernin 2019
, e.g.,)
. In particular, the Cosmological Constant changes the predicted mass of the Local Group
(
Partridge et al. 2013
;
Peñarrubia et al. 2014
;
Hartl & Strigari 2022
;
Benisty 2021
;
Benisty et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. In this
Letter
, we give a bound on
\Lambda
from Local Group dynamics which is compatible with the cosmological measurements. The novelty here is that this provides an exploration of dark energy on very different scales. We extend this idea to other Dark Energy models and show that this method can also constrain modified gravity theories.

II
Analytical Solution of the Two Body Problem with
\Lambda

The dynamics of two bodies that include the Cosmological Constant contribution is governed by the action
(
Carrera & Giulini 2006
)
:

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}v^{2}-\frac{GM}{r}-\frac{1}{6}\Lambda c^{2}r^{2},

(1)

where
r
is their mutual separation,
v
is the total velocity,
\Lambda
is the Cosmological Constant,
c
is the speed of light and
G
is the Newtonian Constant. We use
\mu
as the reduced mass and
M
as the total mass of the system. The conservation of energy and angular momentum reads

\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}\dot{r}^{2}+\frac{l^{2}}{2r^{2}}-\frac{GM}{r}+\frac{1}{6}\Lambda c^{2}r^{2},\quad l=r^{2}\dot{\theta},

(2)

where
\epsilon
is the total energy per reduced mass,
l
is the total angular momentum per reduced mass, while a dot denotes the derivative with respect to time and
\theta
is the true anomaly. In order to find the relation between the energy and the angular momentum to the eccentricity and the semi-major axis, we use the boundary condition that the time derivative of the separation is zero at the extremal separations (pericentre and apocentre)
\dot{r}\left[a\left(1\pm e\right)\right]=0
. Inserting this condition into Eq. (
2
) yields the relations:

\epsilon=-\frac{GM}{2a}\left(1-\frac{2}{3}\lambda\left(1+e^{2}\right)\right)

(3)

l^{2}=GMa\left(1-e^{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{3}\left(1-e^{2}\right)\right)

(4)

where we have introduced the dimensionless parameter:

\lambda:=\frac{a^{3}c^{2}}{GM}\Lambda=\left(\frac{T_{\text{Kep}}}{T_{\Lambda}}\right)^{2},

(5)

that quantifies the impact of the Cosmological Constant. Here,
T_{\text{kep}}
is the Keplerian period of the system and
T_{\Lambda}
is a period that relates the cosmological constant:

T_{\text{Kep}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{a^{3}}{GM}},\quad T_{\Lambda}=\frac{2\pi}{c\sqrt{\Lambda}}=\left(63.22\pm 0.16\right){\rm Gyr}.

(6)

T_{\Lambda}
is calculated from the measured Planck values
(
Aghanim et al. 2020b
)
and is much larger than the age of the Universe
\sim 13.8\,
Gyr. In order to solve the motion, it is useful to parameterize the separation as

r/a=1-e\cos\eta,

(7)

where
e
is the eccentricity,
a
is the semi-major axis and
\eta
is the eccentric anomaly. Putting this parameterizations into (
2
) gives a differential equation (that relates
\dot{\eta}
into
\eta
). The limit
\lambda<1
is readily tackled with perturbation theory. Up to the first order correction, we obtain:

\frac{\sqrt{GM/a^{3}}}{1-ec}\frac{dt}{d\eta}\approx 1-\frac{\lambda}{12}\left(8-e(8c_{1}-c_{2}e+e)\right),

(8)

where
c_{1}=\cos\eta
and
c_{2}=\cos 2\eta
and etc. Integration gives the solution:

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{T}\left(t-t_{0}\right)=\eta-e_{t}\sin\eta-\lambda\left(\frac{5e^{2}}{24}s_{2}-\frac{e^{3}}{72}s_{3}\right).

(9)

where
s_{1}=\sin\eta
and
s_{2}=\sin 2\eta
etc. with the couplings:

\frac{e_{t}}{e}=1-\frac{16-7e^{2}}{24}\lambda,\quad\frac{1}{T}=\frac{1}{T_{\text{Kep}}}\left[1-\frac{8+3e^{2}}{12}\lambda\right].

(10)

In order to find the relationship between the true and the eccentric anomaly, we use the conserved angular momentum from Eq.
2
. Integrating the derivative of the true anomaly with respect to the eccentric anomaly gives:

2\pi\frac{\theta-\theta_{0}}{\Phi}=\tilde{\nu}_{e}-\frac{\lambda}{6}\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\left(3\eta-e\sin\eta\right),

(11)

where

\tan\frac{\tilde{\nu}_{e}}{2}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{1+e}{1-e}}\,\tan\frac{\eta}{2},\quad\Delta\theta=\frac{\Phi}{2\pi}-1=\frac{\lambda}{2}\sqrt{1-e^{2}},

(12)

where
\theta_{0}
is the initial true anomaly,
\Delta\Phi
is the contribution of the precession from
\Lambda
which coincides with earlier expressions
(
Kerr et al. 2003
;
Iorio 2012
)
. The two equations (
9
) and (
11
) describe a precessing elliptical orbit, which is the full motion of the binary system up to first order in
\lambda
.

Besides the orbital equations, the tangential and the radial velocity terms are useful, since these are related to the velocities we measure. Using
v_{r}=\dot{r}
and
v_{t}=\dot{\theta}r=l^{2}/r^{3}
relations, we get:

v_{r}^{2}=\frac{GM}{a}\left(\frac{es}{1-ec}\right)^{2}\left[1+\frac{\lambda}{6}\left(8+e^{2}c_{2}-e^{2}-8ec\right)\right],

(13)

v_{t}^{2}=\frac{GM}{a}\frac{1-e^{2}}{\left(1-ec\right)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1-e^{2}}{3}\lambda\right),

(14)

The Timing Argument (TA)
(
Kahn & Woltjer 1959
)
for the Local Group assumes that the Milky Way (MW) and Andromeda (M31), modelled as point masses, have been approaching each other despite cosmic expansion. Briefly after their formation (for the sake of simplicity the “Big Bang”), these two galaxies must have been in the same place with zero separation (
r=0
). Due to the Hubble expansion, these two galaxies moved apart. After a couple of billion years, they slowed down and then moved towards each other again as a consequence of the gravitational pull. Many authors
(
Peñarrubia et al. 2014
;
Chamberlain et al. 2023
;
Sawala et al. 2022
)
consider the dynamics as two body motion without the Cosmological Constant. Here, for the first time, we have modified these terms analytically in order to track the dynamics in the presence of
\Lambda
. Using values for the Local Group taken from
Benisty et al. 2022
, based on the proper motion measurements of
van der Marel et al. 2012
and
Salomon et al. 2021
, we find
\lambda=0.103\pm 0.002
. Since
\lambda<1
, the analytical approximation is good.

Given the simplicity of the TA, it is now customary to include corrections motivated from the galaxy formation simulations to get unbiased estimates of the true Local Group mass
(
Li & White 2008
, e.g.,)
. The matter has been re-investigated recently by
Hartl & Strigari 2022
, who used N-body and hydrodynamical simulations. The tendency of the TA is to overestimate the Local Group mass by a factor of
0.63\pm 0.02

(
Benisty et al. 2022
)
.

III
Bounds on dark energy and modified gravity

The Local Group mass depends on dark energy. By adding up the mass associated with the galaxies in the Local Group,
Benisty et al. 2022
estimated it is
\left(3.7\pm 0.5\right)\times 10^{12}M_{\odot}

(see also
Sawala et al. 2022
, for a very similar value)
. From this data-driven measurement, we can constrain the Cosmological Constant, as well as modifications of the Newtonian force on the scales of Mpcs.

M
at each
\Lambda
. The horizontal red curves show the latest observationally derived mass and its upper and lower bounds. Requiring consistency, we define the reduced
\chi^{2}
as the difference with respect to the data driven mass:

\Delta\chi^{2}=\left(\frac{M(r,v_{r},v_{t},\Lambda)-\bar{M}_{\rm LG}}{\Delta M_{\rm LG}}\right)^{2},

(15)

where
M(r,v_{r},v_{t},\Lambda)
is the predicted mass for different values of
\Lambda
and
\bar{M}_{\rm LG}\pm\Delta M_{\rm LG}
is the data driven one. The lower panel of Fig.

shows the
\Delta\chi^{2}
for different values of
\Lambda
. Using a broad prior of
\Lambda/\Lambda_{\rm PL}=[0,10]
, we obtain the posterior on
\Lambda
to be:

{\Lambda/\Lambda_{\rm PL}=3.074\pm 2.369.}

(16)

The upper panel of Fig.

compares the bound on
\Lambda
from the Local Group dynamics with the measured value from Planck. The value of Planck sits with
1\sigma
of the distribution, and the upper bound is
\preceq 5.44\,\Lambda_{\rm PL}
. Since the
\Delta\chi^{2}
is flattish, with the broad uniform prior we get weak constraints on the corresponding
\Lambda
value.

This value of
\Lambda
will greatly improve in the near future thanks to new data. In the lower panel, we now examine the effects of (i) improved proper motions, (ii) reduced cosmic bias uncertainty, (iii) better observational constraints on the mass of the Local Group. From the data summary provided in
Benisty et al. 2022
, the M31-MW separation has an uncertainty of
\delta r=5.2\%
, whilst the velocities have uncertainties
\delta v_{r}=4\%
,
\delta v_{t}=37.86\%
. However, improved proper motions for M31 are likely to come quickly thanks to the ability of
JWST
to obtain high precision astrometry in the Local Group, often exceeding the accuracy of
Gaia

(

)
. The main uncertainty in the mass of the Local Group is the contribution of its largest member, M31. This is inferred from the kinematics of its satellite galaxies and so will also benefit from
JWST

5\%
error and the other quantities to have a
2\%
error. This gives the value to be:

{\Lambda/\Lambda_{\rm PL}=1.670\pm 0.794}.

(17)

From the posterior, we predict that it will be possible not only to give strong upper bound on
\Lambda
which is in order of the Planck value, but will give a detection of
\Lambda
distribution with
\sim 40\%
error.

III.2
Modified gravity

Local Group dynamics in the context of modified gravity has already been discussed in the literature
(
McLeod & Lahav 2020
;
Benisty & Capozziello 2023
)
. We are also able to constrain modified gravity theories. We only consider theories that are able to simultaneously drive the acceleration of the cosmic expansion (dark energy) while remaining compatible with solar system tests of gravity
(
Brax et al. 2008
;
Benisty et al. 2023
)
. For scalar tensor theories of gravity with conformal coupling to matter (via
{g}_{\mu\nu}^{(J)}=\left(1+{2\beta}\frac{\phi}{m_{\rm Pl}}\right)g_{\mu\nu}^{(E)}
, where
{g}_{\mu\nu}^{\left(J\right)}
is the metric in Jordan frame and
g_{\mu\nu}^{\left(E\right)}
is the metric in the Einstein frame) gives an effective Newtonian constant:
G_{\text{eff}}/G_{\rm N}=1+\beta^{2}
where
\beta
is conformal strength
(
Brax et al. 2019
)
. By using the measured Planck value for
\Lambda
and the Local Group mass, we can constraint any other modified gravity parameter. Fig
3
shows the posterior distribution for
\Delta G_{\rm N}/G_{\rm N}
that reads:

{\Delta G_{\rm N}/G_{\rm N}=0.264\pm 0.454.}

(18)

Although the constraint on
G_{\rm N}
is not as strong as other bounds, it is nonetheless interesting as it is probing deviations from Newtonian gravity on scales of Mpc. However, we note that in theories with screening we would expect the constraint on
G_{\rm N}
to depend on the environment, being more screened in dense environments such as the solar system, see
(
Benisty et al. 2023
)
and references therein. The Cassini bound
\Delta G_{\rm N}/G_{\rm N}=\left(2.1\pm 2.5\right)\times 10^{-5}

(
Bertotti et al. 2003
)
is on Solar system scales. Recently,
Desmond et al. 2021
have claimed a five percent measurement of the Gravitational constant using Cepheid variable stars in detached eclipsing binaries in the Large Magellanic Cloud:
\Delta G_{\rm N}/G_{\rm N}=0.07^{+0.05}_{-0.04}
. This result is interesting as it provides a measurement of
G_{\rm N}
in another galaxy, but it is again probing scales of astronomical units rather than Megaparsecs.

IV
Discussion

The Cosmological Constant has a small but measurable effect on the motion of the Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies, which are the two largest members of the Local Group. This
Letter
shows that the ratio between the Keplerian period and
T_{\Lambda}=2\pi/(c\sqrt{\Lambda})\approx 63.2\,\,{\rm Gyr}
controls the importance of the Cosmological Constant in the two body problem. Since the period of the Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies is about
20\,{\rm Gyr}
the Cosmological Constant is a roughly
10\%
effect on the motion of the galaxies. In this limit, we provide an analytical solution for the two-body problem.

We show that a larger value of the Cosmological Constant gives a larger value for the mass of the Local Group. Based on the observationally derived measurements of the Local Group mass, we give a constraint on the Cosmological Constant only from dynamics. We find that upper limit on the Cosmological Constant is about
\sim 5.44
times larger then the Planck value. Anticipating future gains in accuracy from
JWST
astrometry, we find that the upper bound could reduce to a direct detection of the
\Lambda
with the value:
\left(1.67\pm 0.8\right)\Lambda_{PL}
in the near future. This detection will be on very different scales than that probed by the CMB.

The Local Group is also a new arena in which to test modified theories of gravity. We obtain new bounds on scalar-tensor theories. Although these are not as strong as Solar system constraints, they are still the first constraints on unprecedentedly large Megaparsec scales. Of-course the constraint could also be density dependent as in many popular models
(
Brax et al. 2008
)
for example. Based on the condition for the domination of the Cosmological Constant in a binary system, we suggest for future research bounds on the Cosmological Constant and alternative gravity theories derived from binary galaxies in the Local Universe.

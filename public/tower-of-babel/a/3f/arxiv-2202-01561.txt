Abstract
. S. Banach [ 1 ] proved that good differential properties of function do not guarantee the a.e. convergence of the Fourier series of this function with respect to general orthonormal systems (ONS). On the other hand it is very well known that a sufficient condition for the a.e. convergence of an orthonormal series is given by the Menshov-Rademacher Theorem. The paper deals with sequence of positive numbers ( d n ) (d_{n}) such that multiplying the Fourier coefficients ( C n ​ ( f ) ) (C_{n}(f)) of functions with bounded variation by these numbers one obtains a.e. convergent series of the form ∑ n = 1 ∞ d n ​ C n ​ ( f ) ​ φ n ​ ( x ) . \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}C_{n}(f)\varphi_{n}(x). It is established that the resulting conditions are best possible.

SOME NOTATIONS AND THEOREMS

Let
\left(\varphi_{n}\right)
be an orthonormal system (ONS) on
\left[0,1\right]
and

\displaystyle C_{n}(f)=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\varphi_{n}\left(x\right)\,dx,\quad n=1,2

(1)

be the Fourier coefficients of a function
f\in L_{2}\left(0,1\right).

We denote by
V\left(0,1\right)
the class of all functions of bounded variation and write
V\left(f\right)
for the total variation of a function
f
on
\left[0,1\right]
.

Let
A
be the class of all absolutely continuous functions
f
on
[0,1]
. This is a Banach space with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{A}=\int_{0}^{1}\left|f^{{}^{\prime}}\left(x\right)\right|dx+\left\|f\left(x\right)\right\|_{C},

where
C(0,1)
is the class of all continuous functions
f
on
[0,1];

\left\|f(x)\right\|_{C}
is the norm of
f
on
C(0,1)
.

Definition 1
.

A positive bounded sequence of numbers
\left(d_{n}\right)
is called a multiplier of convergence with respect to a function class
E
if

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}C_{k}\left(f\right)\varphi_{k}\left(x\right),\qquad\left(C_{k}\left(f\right)=\int_{0}^{1}f\left(x\right)\varphi_{k}\left(x\right)dx\right)

is convergence a.e. for all
f\in E.

Theorem 1
.

(see

]
ch.9.p332)(Menshov-Rademacher).
If
\left(\varphi_{n}\right)
is an ONS on
\left[0,1\right]

\left(c_{n}\right)
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{2}\log_{2}^{2}n<+\infty,

.

Lemma 1

(see
[
4
]
)
.

If
f\in L_{2}\left(0,1\right)
takes only finite values on
\left[0,1\right]
and

is an arbitrary function, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f\left(x\right)g\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)-f\left(\frac{i+1}{n}\right)\right)\int_{0}^{i/n}g\left(x\right)dx

(2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left(f\left(x\right)-f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right)g\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f\left(1\right)\int_{0}^{1}g\left(x\right)dx.

We have that
(\log n:=\log_{2}n)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}^{2}C_{k}^{2}\left(f\right)\log^{2}k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}^{2}C_{k}\left(f\right)C_{k}\left(f\right)\log^{2}k

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f\left(x\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}C_{k}\left(f\right)\log^{2}k\varphi_{k}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f\left(x\right)P_{n}\left(d,c,x\right)dx,

\displaystyle G_{n}\left(d,a\right)=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\int_{0}^{i/n}P_{n}\left(d,a,x\right)dx\right|

(4)

T_{n}\left(d,a\right)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}a_{k}^{2}\log^{2}k\right)^{1/2},

(5)

where
\left(a_{n}\right)\in l_{2}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(d_{n})
be a positive, bounded sequence of numbers. Then for every
i,\left(i=1,2,\dots,n\right)

\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left|P_{n}\left(d,a,x\right)\right|dx=O\left(1\right)T_{n}\left(d,a\right).

Proof.

If we use the Cauchy inequality and we mean that

D=\sup_{k}d_{k}

we get

\displaystyle\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left|P_{n}\left(d,a,x\right)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}P_{n}^{2}\left(d,a,x\right)dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}a_{k}\log^{2}k\varphi_{k}\left(x\right)\right)^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{4}a_{k}^{2}\log^{4}k\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}a_{k}^{2}\log^{2}k\right)^{1/2}=O\left(1\right)T_{n}\left(d,a\right).

Lemma
2
is proved.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\varphi_{n})
be ONS on
[0,1]
,
\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{n}(x)dx=0,

n=1,2,\dots,
such that uniformly for

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}C_{n}(f)\varphi_{n}(x)

According to the condition of Theorem

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{x}P_{n}(d,c,y)dy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}\left|C_{k}(f)\right|\log^{2}k\frac{1}{k}

\displaystyle O(1)T_{n}(c)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}\log^{2}k\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(1)T_{n}(c)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k^{2\gamma}}{k^{2}}\right)^{1/2}=O(1)T_{n}(c)

by the Cauchy inequality (see (

Theorem 6

Let

be an increasing sequence of numbers such that

Abstract
We introduce the family of axioms, denoted Slice κ \operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa} , that claim the existence of strictly increasing decompositions of the form 2 δ = ⋃ α < κ 2 δ ∩ M α , 2^{\delta}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}2^{\delta}\cap M_{\alpha}, where δ < κ \delta<\kappa , and { M α | α < κ } \{M_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\kappa\} is a ⊆ \subseteq -increasing sequence of transitive models of set theory. We study compatibility of these axioms with versions of Martin’s Axiom, and in particular show that Slice ω 1 \operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}} is compatible only with some very weak form of MA \operatorname{MA} .



Keywords:
Martin’s Axiom, Suslin forcing, transitive models

Preliminaries

All non-standard notions are introduced in the subsequent sections. By
reals
we mean elements of the sets
\omega^{\omega}
,
2^{\omega}
, or seldom
\mathbb{R}
. We take liberty to freely identify Borel functions with their Borel codes, so whenever we claim that

f\in M\models\operatorname{ZFC}

for some Borel function
f\subseteq 2^{\omega}\times 2^{\omega}
, it should be understood that it is the Borel code of
f
that belongs to
M
(so we don’t bother if, for instance,
\operatorname{dom}{f}\not\subseteq M
). For a cardinal
\kappa
,
\mathbb{C}_{\kappa}=\{p:\operatorname{dom}(p)\rightarrow 2|\;\operatorname{dom}(p)\in[\kappa]^{<\omega}\}
, and
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}_{\omega}
.

When we write
\mathbb{P}=\{\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\theta\}
for a finite support iteration of forcings, we sometimes denote by
\mathbb{P}
the final step of the iteration, that is
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
. When working with infinite iterations, we assume that
\mathbb{P}_{0}
is the trivial forcing. A function
i:\mathbb{P}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{1}
is a
complete embedding
if the following assertions hold:

1.

\forall\;{p,q\in\mathbb{P}_{0}}\quad p_{0}\leq p_{1}\implies i(p_{0})\leq i(p_{1})
,

2.

\forall\;{p,q\in\mathbb{P}_{0}}\quad p_{0}\bot p_{1}\implies i(p_{0})\bot i(p_{1})
,

3.

If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{0}
is a maximal antichain, then
i[\mathcal{A}]\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}
is a maximal antichain.

We write
\mathbb{P}_{0}\lessdot\mathbb{P}_{1}
if
\mathbb{P}_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}
and the inclusion is a complete embedding. The importance of complete embeddings comes from the closely related notion of
quotient forcing
. If
\mathbb{P}_{0}\lessdot\mathbb{P}_{1}
, and
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{0}
is a filter generic over
V
, then
V[G]
contains the quotient forcing
\mathbb{P}_{1}/G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}
, consisting of all conditions
p\in\mathbb{P}_{1}
that are compatible with every element of
G
. The crucial property of this notion is that forcing with
\mathbb{P}_{1}
is equivalent to forcing with a two-step iteration:
\mathbb{P}_{0}\ast\mathbb{P}_{1}/\dot{G}
(see
[
10
]
, p.244).

We will be frequently using the following two observations.

Proposition 7
.

If
V
is a countable transitive model of
\operatorname{ZFC}
,
\mathbb{P}_{0},\mathbb{P}_{1}\in V
, and
\mathbb{P}_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}
is an inclusion of partial orders, then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

\mathbb{P}_{0}\lessdot\mathbb{P}_{1}
,

2.

If a filter
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}
is
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-generic over
V
then

G\cap\mathbb{P}_{0}
is
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-generic over
V
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathbb{P}\lessdot\mathbb{S}
be any forcing notions, and fix
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Let
\dot{q}
,
\dot{r}
be
\mathbb{P}
-names, and finally let
\sigma(-,-)
be a formula with parameters in the ground model, which is also absolute between transitive models of set theory (for example a
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
formula in the language of arithmetic, or a bounded formula in the language of set theory). Then

p\Vdash_{\mathbb{S}}\sigma(\dot{q},\dot{r})\iff p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\sigma(\dot{q},\dot{r}).

Proof.

In the direction from left to right, if
p\in G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is generic over the ground model
V
, then we can extend
G
to a generic filter
G^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{S}
. Notice that
\dot{q}[G]=\dot{q}[G^{\prime}]
, and
\dot{r}[G]=\dot{r}[G^{\prime}]
. By the absoluteness of
\sigma(-,-)
we have

V[G]\models\sigma(\dot{q}[G],\dot{r}[G])\iff V[G^{\prime}]\models\sigma(\dot{q}[G^{\prime}],\dot{r}[G^{\prime}]).

In the other direction, we proceed in a similar way, using the fact that for any generic filter
G\subseteq\mathbb{S}
, the intersection
G\cap\mathbb{P}
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic.
∎

2
The Slicing Axioms

Definition 9
.

Let
\kappa
be any uncountable cardinal. We will say that
\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
holds if there exists a
\subseteq
-increasing sequence of transitive classes (not necessarily proper)
\{M_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\kappa\}
, such that the following conditions are satisfied

1.

\forall\;{\alpha<\kappa}\quad(M_{\alpha};\in)\models\operatorname{ZFC}

2.

\forall\;{\alpha<\kappa}\quad\omega_{1}^{M_{\alpha}}=\omega_{1}
,

3.

\forall\;{\delta\in[\omega,\kappa)}\quad 2^{\delta}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}2^{\delta}\cap M_{\alpha}}
,

4.

\forall\;{\alpha<\beta<\kappa}\;\forall\;{\delta\in[\omega,\kappa)}\quad 2^{\delta}\cap M_{\alpha}\subsetneq 2^{\delta}\cap M_{\beta}
.

Let us comment briefly on the choice of the definition. The reader uncomfortable with the idea of working with models of
\operatorname{ZFC}
can replace
\operatorname{ZFC}
by some sufficiently, large finite fragment of it. Such a formal weakening has no impact on the results of the paper. Moreover, it seems very natural to consider a sequence of models the length
\kappa>\omega_{1}
, which is "slicing" just
2^{\omega}
, instead
2^{\delta}
, for any
\delta<\kappa
, as in condition
3
. We opted for this definition mainly because, as we will see later, slicing
2^{\delta}
for uncountable
\delta
ensures the slicing of
2^{\omega}
in generic extensions. Moreover, in the main cases of our interest, the models
M_{\alpha}
will be intermediate forcing extensions, and then condition
3.
will hold. As for the condition
4.
it is equivalent to a formally weaker

4a.

\forall\;\alpha<\beta<\kappa\quad 2^{\omega}\cap M_{\alpha}\subsetneq 2^{\omega}\cap M_{\beta}.

This is because, having a real
x\in 2^{\omega}\in(M_{\beta}\setminus M_{\alpha})
, we can add a trail of zeros to get a sequence of any given length
1
1

1

of any given length belonging to
M_{\beta}
, to be precise. However, condition
3.
ensures that the models from the sequence know every ordinal below
\kappa
, from some point on.
to
M_{\beta}\setminus M_{\alpha}
. For the same reason, if
\kappa=\lambda^{+}
, we can just require

4b.

\forall\;\alpha<\beta<\kappa\quad 2^{\lambda}\cap M_{\alpha}\subsetneq 2^{\lambda}\cap M_{\beta}.

The axiom
\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
is outright false for any singular
\kappa
.

Proposition 10
.

If
\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
holds, then
\kappa
is regular, and
\kappa\leq 2^{\omega}
.

Proof.

If
\delta=\operatorname{cof}{\kappa}<\kappa
, then
\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
gives us a nontrivial decomposition of the form

2^{\delta}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}2^{\delta}\cap M_{\alpha},

and after passing to a cofinal sequence, we also have a decomposition

2^{\delta}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\delta}2^{\delta}\cap M_{\gamma_{\alpha}}.

For each
\alpha<\delta
we pick
s_{\alpha}\in 2^{\delta}\setminus M_{\gamma_{\alpha}}
. Using a definable bijection between
\delta
and
\delta\times\delta
, we see that the sequence
(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\delta}
must belong so some model
M_{\alpha}
. But this contradicts the choice of
s_{\alpha}
. The inequality
\kappa\leq 2^{\omega}
follows outright from the condition
4.
in the definition of
\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
, applied to
\delta=\omega
.
∎

The most important of the slicing axioms is perhaps
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
, since it claims that the real line can be decomposed into an increasing union of
\omega_{1}
many sets, which belong to bigger and bigger models. The fact that
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
is inconsistent with
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
shows, that the Martin’s Axiom on
\omega_{1}
imposes certain compactness on the real line.

Theorem 11

\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}\implies\lnot\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}(\text{$\sigma$-centred})

In the proof we will utilize a known result from

Theorem 12

(

\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}(\sigma\text{-centred})

\omega_{1}

Proof of Theorem
11
.

Assume that
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}(\text{$\sigma$-centred})
holds, and
(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
is a sequence of models witnessing
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
.
M_{0}\models``\text{$2^{\omega}$ is uncountable}"

X=\{x_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\omega_{1}\}\in M_{0}
(note that this sequence is

\operatorname{rg}(f)\subseteq M_{\eta}

For every natural number

, where

is an

\operatorname{rg}(f)=\{x\in 2^{\omega}|\;\exists\alpha<\omega_{1}\;\forall m<\omega\quad x_{\alpha}\in F_{m}\iff x(m)=1\}.\hfill\qed

It is compatible with any value of

Proposition 13
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be any finite support product of productively c.c.c. forcings adding reals, and of the length at least

\mathbb{P}\Vdash\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}

Proof.

The forcing

\mathbb{P}=\prod_{i\in I\times\kappa}\mathbb{P}_{i},

where each

adds some real number, and

\displaystyle{\prod_{i\in I\times\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{i}}

\mathbb{P}

is generic over some model

\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
is witnessed by the sequence

M_{\alpha}=V[G\cap\prod_{i\in I\times\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{i}].\qed

Corollary 14

For each uncountable, regular

Theorem 17
.

\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
implies that there is an
\omega_{1}
-dense rigid subset of the real line.

Proof.

Let
(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
be a sequence witnessing
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
. For each
\alpha
, we choose

x_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}\cap(M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}M_{\beta}).

We can easily arrange the construction, so that we hit each open interval
\omega_{1}
-many times. The set
X=\{x_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
is
\omega_{1}
-dense, and it remains to prove, that it is also rigid. Suppose that
f:X\rightarrow X
is an order isomorphism.
f
extends uniquely to a continuous function
f^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and each such function can be coded by a real number. Therefore there is some
\eta<\omega_{1}
, such that
f^{\prime}\in M_{\eta}
. Now, for any
\xi>\eta
, it is not possible that
f(x_{\eta})=x_{\xi}
, because it would mean
x_{\xi}\in M_{\eta}
, contrary to the choice of

. But, likewise, it is not possible that
f^{-1}(x_{\eta})=x_{\xi}
. The conclusion is that for all
\xi>\eta
,
f(x_{\xi})=x_{\xi}
. But this means that

is identity on a dense set, and therefore everywhere.
∎

Slicing the real line while preserving
\operatorname{MA}
(Suslin)

We are going to show that
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
is consistent with a version of Martin’s Axiom which takes into account only partial orders representable as analytic sets (see
[
2
]
, Ch. 3.6, or
[
9
]

Definition 18
.

A partial order
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
has a
Suslin definition
if
\mathbb{P}\in\Sigma^{1}_{1}(\omega^{\omega})
, and both ordering and incompatibility relations in
\mathbb{P}
are analytic relations on
\omega^{\omega}
.
\mathbb{P}
is
Suslin

The rest of the Section is devoted to the proof of the next theorem.

Theorem 19
.

If
\kappa
is a regular cardinal such that
\kappa^{\omega}=\kappa
, then the following theory is consistent

\operatorname{ZFC}+\operatorname{MA}(\text{Suslin})+\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}+``2^{\omega}=\kappa".

Let
\psi(-,-,-,-)
be a universal analytic formula, i.e. a
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
formula with the property that for each analytic set
P\subseteq\omega^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}
there exists

such that

P=\{x\in\omega^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}|\;\psi(x,r)\}.

We want to use
\psi
to add generic filters to all possible Suslin forcings. We will say that
\psi(-,-,-,\dot{r}_{\alpha})

defines

\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
if
\dot{r}_{\alpha}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-name for a real and
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
forces each of the following

\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}\text{ is a separative partial order with the greatest element }0,

\psi(x,1,1,\dot{r}_{\alpha})\iff x\in\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},

\psi(x,y,2,\dot{r}_{\alpha})\iff x\leq_{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}}y,

\psi(x,y,3,\dot{r}_{\alpha})\iff x\bot_{\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}}y.

We will write
\psi^{\in}(x,z)
for
\psi(x,1,1,z)
,
\psi^{\bot}(x,y,z)
for
\psi(x,y,3,z)

\psi^{\leq}(x,y,z)

\psi(x,y,2,z)

We are going to iterate all Suslin forcings, each of them cofinally many times. More precisely, we define by induction a finite support iteration
\{\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\kappa\}

•

\mathbb{P}_{0}=\{0\}
,

•

\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\Vdash``\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\omega^{\omega}|\;\psi(x,\dot{r}_{\alpha})\}\text{ if this formula defines a Suslin forcing;\; \\
else }\dot{\mathbb{Q}}=\{0\}"
,

The variable

ranges over all reals, and all possible names for reals, each of them cofinally many times. In order to iterate through all possible parameters using a suitable bookkeeping, we introduce the class of

[
2
]

Definition 20

By the induction on
\alpha

conditions in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
.

•

\alpha=0.

\mathbb{P}_{0}=\{0\}
, and we declare

•

4.1
Computation of cardinal invariants

For any given group
G
, one can study an associated cardinal invariant
\operatorname{c(G)}
that stands for the minimal cardinality
\kappa
, for which the group
G
can be represented as a union of a chain of the length
\kappa
, consisting of proper subgroups of
G
. A substantial amount of literature is devoted to study this cardinal invariant for symmetric groups of infinite sets (for example
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
8
]
). It is known that

\mathfrak{g}\leq\operatorname{c(Sym(\omega))}\leq\mathfrak{d},

where
\mathfrak{g}
is the
groupwise density number
. The lower bound was proved by Brendle and Losada
[
5
]
, and the upper bound is due to Sharp and Thomas
[
13
]
.

It is easy to observe that
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}\implies``\operatorname{c(Sym(\omega))}=\omega_{1}"
: if
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
is witnessed by a sequence
(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
, then the equality
\operatorname{c(Sym(\omega))}=\omega_{1}
is witnessed by the sequence of groups
(M_{\alpha}\cap\operatorname{Sym}(\omega))_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
. As a matter of fact, this observation shows that Theorem
19
generalizes Lemma 2.6 from
[
16
]
, which claims that the equality
\operatorname{c(Sym(\omega))}=\omega_{1}
is preserved under finite support iterations of Suslin forcings. Indeed, what the proof of Theorem

shows, is that any finite support iteration of Suslin forcings preserves
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
over a model of a stronger variant of
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
.

Together with some well-known results (see
[
4
]
), we have the following series of inequalities:

\operatorname{c(Sym(\omega))}\geq\mathfrak{g}\geq\mathfrak{h}\geq\mathfrak{t}\geq\mathfrak{m}\geq\omega_{1}.

It follows that in our model all these invariants are equal
\omega_{1}

decides all cardinal characteristics from the Cichoń’s diagram to be equal

\alpha=0.

\mathbb{P}_{0}=\{0\}
, and we declare
0
to be simple.

•

\alpha+1.

(p,\dot{q})\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1}
is simple if
p\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
is simple,
\dot{q}=\{(\gamma_{n},p_{n})|\;n<\omega\}
, and the conditions
p_{n}
are simple.

•

\lim{\alpha}.

p\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
is simple if for each
\beta<\alpha
,
p\restriction\beta\in\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
is simple.

Like in the previous section, it is easy to check that the set of simple conditions is always dense.

Lemma 29
.

For each
\alpha\leq\theta
, if
p\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
is simple then
p
is definable (in the language of set theory) with a parameter from
\theta^{\omega}
.

Proof.

•

\alpha=0.
Clear.

•

\alpha+1
. Let
r=(p,\dot{q})
be a simple condition. We can write
\dot{q}=\{(\gamma_{n},p_{n})|\;n<\omega\}
, where conditions
p_{n}
are simple. By the induction hypothesis each
p_{n}
is definable with a parameter from
\theta^{\omega}
, and so is
p
. Clearly
r
can be defined from them, and so
r
is definable with countably many parameters, which we can code as one.

•

\lim{\alpha}
. Fix
r\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
.
r
has finite support, so there exists
\beta<\alpha
containing the support of
r
. By the induction hypothesis
p\restriction\beta
is definable with a parameter from
\theta^{\omega}
, and so
p
is definable with the parameters
p\restriction\beta
,
\beta
, and
\alpha
.
∎

An immediate consequence is that each of the names
\dot{\leq}_{\alpha}
is definable with some parameter
\sigma_{\alpha}\in\theta^{\lambda}
. Like previously, we fix a list of such parameters
\{\sigma_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\theta\}\subseteq\theta^{\lambda}
. We define by induction the relativized forcings
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}^{N}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
, taking into account only names from some transitive class
N
.

•

\mathbb{P}^{N}_{0}=\{0\}
,

•

Assume
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}
is defined. We define a
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}
-name
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}_{\alpha}
as follows

–

\dot{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}_{\alpha}=\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
if
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}\in N
, and

\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}
-name,

–

\dot{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}_{\alpha}=\{0\}
otherwise.

•

\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha+1}=\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}_{\alpha}
.

In limit steps we take direct limits, so
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\kappa}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\kappa}
.

Lemma 30
.

Let
N
be a transitive model of
\operatorname{ZFC}
, containing
\theta
. Let
M\prec H((2^{\theta})^{+})
be an elementary submodel, such that
\lambda+1\subseteq M
, and
\{\sigma_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\theta\}\in M
(see the remark after Lemma
29
). We assume moreover that
\theta^{\lambda}\cap M\subseteq N
. Then for each
\alpha\leq\theta
,
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\cap M\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction.

•

\alpha=0
. Clear.

•

\lim{\alpha}.
Fix
r\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\cap M
. By the elementarity of
M
, there exists
\gamma\in\alpha\cap M
that contains the support of
r
. From the induction hypothesis it follows that
r\restriction\gamma\in\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}^{N}
. It is routine to verify by induction that for all
\gamma\leq\delta\leq\alpha
,
r\restriction\delta\in\mathbb{P}_{\delta}^{N}
.

•

\alpha+1.
Fix
r=(p,\dot{q})\in M\cap(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha})
. Clearly
p\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
by the induction hypothesis. The name
\dot{q}
is of the form

\dot{q}=\{(\gamma_{n},p_{n})|\;n<\omega\},

and for each
n<\omega
,
p_{n}\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\cap M\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
. This shows that
\dot{q}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}
-name. it remains to show that
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}\Vdash\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}^{N}=\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
, and this in turn reduces to showing that
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}
-name belonging to
N
. To see this, let us note that, since
(p,\dot{q})\in M
, also
\alpha\in M
, and so
\sigma_{\alpha}\in\theta^{\lambda}\cap M\subseteq N
. It follows that
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}\in N\cap M
. Recall, that
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-name for a partial ordering of the form

\dot{\leq}_{\alpha}=\{(\phi(\beta),p^{i}_{\beta})|\;\beta<\lambda,i<\omega\}.

Given that
\lambda+1\subseteq M
, we conclude that each of the conditions
p^{i}_{\beta}
belongs to
M
, and by the induction hypothesis, also to
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
. This shows that
\dot{\leq}_{\alpha}
, and in turn also
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
, are
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
-names, and concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 31
.

If
N
is a transitive class, then for all
\alpha\leq\theta

\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}\lessdot\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}.

Specifically:

1.

If
p_{0}\bot p_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
, then
p_{0}\bot p_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
.

2.

If
p_{0}\leq p_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
, then
p_{0}\leq p_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
.

3.

If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}
is a maximal antichain, then
\mathcal{A}
is maximal in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\alpha
.

Concerning the point 1.

•

\alpha=0.
Clear.

•

\alpha+1.
Assume
(p_{0},\dot{q}_{0})\bot(p_{1},\dot{q}_{1})
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha+1}
. If
p_{0}\bot p_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
, then by the induction hypothesis
p_{0}\bot p_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
and we are done. Suppose otherwise, and fix a condition
p\leq p_{0},p_{1}
from
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
. If
p
can be extended to a
p^{*}
such that
p^{*}\Vdash\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}^{N}=\{0\}
, then
p^{*}\leq(p_{0},\dot{q}_{0}),(p_{1},\dot{q}_{1})
, contradicting
(p_{0},\dot{q}_{0})\bot(p_{1},\dot{q}_{1})
. So
p
forces that
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}^{N}
is defined in the nontrivial way. Let
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
be any filter generic over
V
, containing
p
. Since
p_{0},p_{1}\in G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
, we see that

\dot{q}_{0}[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}]\bot\dot{q}_{1}[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}]

in the model
V[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}]
, and so in
V[G]
as well (see Proposition
8
). Since
p
and
G
were arbitrary, it follows that
(p_{0},\dot{q}_{0})\bot(p_{1},\dot{q}_{1})
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1}
.

•

\lim{\alpha}.
Follows from the induction hypothesis, since the supports are finite.

Concerning the point 2.

•

\alpha=0.
Clear.

•

\alpha+1.
Assume
(p_{0},\dot{q}_{0})\leq(p_{1},\dot{q}_{1})
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha+1}
. From the induction hypothesis we know, that
p_{0}\leq p_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
, and
p_{0}\Vdash\dot{q}_{0}\leq\dot{q}_{1}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{N}
. We must show that the assertion

p_{0}\Vdash\dot{q}_{0}\leq\dot{q}_{1}

holds also in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
. But it follows outright from Proposition
8
.

•

\lim{\alpha}.
Follows from the induction hypothesis, since the supports are finite.

Concerning the point 3.

•

\alpha=0.
Clear.

•

\alpha+1.
The proof is exactly the same, as in the successor case of Theorem
23
point 3, except this time the conclusion of Lemma
22
follows straightforward from the relation

\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}\Vdash\dot{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}_{\alpha}\lessdot\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}.

•

\lim{\alpha}
. Let
\{\overline{p}_{n}|\;n<\omega\}
be a maximal antichain in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\alpha}
, and fix
\overline{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
. There is some
\gamma<\alpha
such that
\overline{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
. The set
\{\overline{p}_{n}\restriction\gamma|\;n<\omega\}
might not be an antichain in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\gamma}
, however each condition in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\gamma}
is compatible with some
p_{n}\restriction\gamma
. We can refine
\{\overline{p}_{n}\restriction\gamma|\;n<\omega\}
to an antichain in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\gamma}
, and this antichain will remain maximal
in
\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
by the induction hypothesis. Therefore
\{\overline{p}_{n}\restriction\gamma|\;n<\omega\}
meets every condition from
\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
, and in particular some
\overline{p}_{n}\restriction\gamma
is compatible with
\overline{p}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
. But then
\overline{p}_{n}
is compatible with
\overline{p}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
26
.

We start with any model
V\models\operatorname{ZFC}+``2^{\omega}=\kappa"+\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
, for example an extension of a model of
\operatorname{CH}
by
\mathbb{C}_{\kappa}
(see Corollary
14
). Let
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
be the forcing defined in the beginning of the Section. Suppose that a sequence
\{M_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\kappa\}
witnesses
\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
in
V
, and
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is generic over
V
. We aim to shows that the sequence
V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}]
witnesses
\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}
in
V[G]
. For this we need to show two things

1.

If
F\in 2^{<\kappa}\cap V[G]
, then
F\in V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}]
for some
\alpha<\kappa
.

2.

None of the models
V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}]
contains all reals from
V[G]
.

Concerning
1.
assume that
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\Vdash\dot{F}\in 2^{\delta}
, for some ordinal
\delta<\kappa
. Without loss of generality
\delta=|\delta|\geq\lambda
. We can also assume that

\dot{F}=\{(\alpha,\alpha_{n},p_{n}^{\alpha})|\;\alpha<\delta,\;n<\omega\},

and all conditions
p_{n}^{\alpha}
are simple. We fix some elementary submodel
\overline{M}\prec\operatorname{H}((2^{\theta})^{+})
of size
\delta
, of which we assume that
\delta+1\subseteq\overline{M}
, and
\{\sigma_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\theta\}
,
\dot{F}\in\overline{M}
. Notice that
\delta+1\subseteq\overline{M}
guarentees that each of the conditions
p_{n}^{\alpha}
is in
\overline{M}
. We pick
\alpha<\kappa
big enough, so that
\overline{M}\cap\theta^{\lambda}\subseteq M_{\alpha}
. Now Lemma
31
shows that
p_{n}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}^{n}
, for all
\alpha<\delta
,
n<\omega
. This shows that
\dot{F}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{M_{\alpha}}
-name, and it follows that

\dot{F}[G]=\dot{F}[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}]\in V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}].

Concerning
2.
fix a real
r\in 2^{\omega}\setminus M_{\alpha}
. Let
\mathbb{C}_{r}
be any representation of
\mathbb{C}
, from which the real
r
is definable, and
\mathbb{C}_{r}
is of the form

\mathbb{C}_{r}=(\lambda,\leq_{r}).

This of course leaves plenty of space for what specifically
\mathbb{C}_{r}
might be, but for the sake of concreteness we can define
\leq_{r}
as the transitive closure of the union of the following three relations:

1.

\leq_{r}\restriction\omega\times\omega
isomorphic to the countable atomless Boolean algebra,

2.

\forall\;1\leq\alpha<\omega\quad\alpha\cdot\omega<_{r}\alpha\cdot\omega+1\iff r(\alpha)=1
,

3.

\forall\;1\leq\alpha<\omega\quad\alpha\cdot\omega>_{r}\alpha\cdot\omega+1\iff r(\alpha)=0
.

We pick
\gamma<\theta
for which

\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\Vdash\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma}=\mathbb{C}_{r}.

In this case, we also have

\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\gamma}\Vdash\dot{\mathbb{Q}}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\gamma}=\{0\},

since
r\notin M_{\alpha}
.

Let
c
be the real added by
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma}
over
V[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}]
. We claim that
c\notin V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\theta}]
. Suppose towards a contradiction that
c\in V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\theta}]
. Let
\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{M_{\alpha}}/(G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\gamma+1})
. If
c\in V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\theta}]
, then either
c
is already in the initial fragment
V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\gamma+1}]
, or is added generically over it by
\mathbb{R}
. The first option is ruled out, because

V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\gamma+1}]=V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\gamma}]\subseteq V[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}],

and
c
was added generically over that model. Therefore in
V[G\cap\mathbb{P}^{M_{\alpha}}_{\gamma+1}]
there is a
\mathbb{R}
-name
\dot{x}
such that two of the following hold:

•

\mathbb{R}\Vdash``\text{$\dot{x}$ is a new real}"
,

•

\dot{x}[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{M_{\alpha}}]=c.

Consider now the forcing
\mathbb{R}
in
V[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\gamma+1}]
. It is still true that
\mathbb{R}
forces
\dot{x}
to be a new real, since this is an absolute property of the forcing and the name (it says that for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
there is
n<\omega
, such that
r
does not decide
\dot{x}(n)
). But this is a contradiction, since
c=\dot{x}[G]\in V[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\gamma+1}]
.

A standard verification shows that
V[G]\models``2^{\omega}=\theta"
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
26
.
∎

Corollary 32
.

The following theories are consistent

\operatorname{ZFC}+\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}+\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{2}}+``2^{\omega}=\omega_{2}",

\operatorname{ZFC}+\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}+\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{2}}+``2^{\omega}=\omega_{3}",

\operatorname{ZFC}+\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{2}}+\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{3}}+``2^{\omega}=\omega_{29}".

6
Final comments

We proved that
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
and
\operatorname{Slice}_{\omega_{1}}
are not compatible. It looks reasonable to expect that for any regular cardinal
\kappa

\operatorname{MA}_{\kappa}\implies\lnot\operatorname{Slice}_{\kappa}.

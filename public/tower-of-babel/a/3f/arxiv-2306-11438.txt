F F -invariant in cluster algebras
Peigen Cao

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the F F -invariant in cluster algebras using tropicalization. This is an analog of the E E -invariant introduced by Derksen, Weyman and Zelevinsky in the additive categorification of cluster algebras and the 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} -invariant introduced by Kang, Kashiwara, Kim and Oh in the monoidal categorification of (quantum) cluster algebras. We prove that the product of two cluster monomials is still a cluster monomial if and only if their F F -invariant is zero. For cluster algebras with a compatible Poisson bracket, we prove that if two cluster variables are log-cannonical, then they are contained in the same cluster. Inspired by F F -invariant, we introduce the dominant sets for seeds of cluster algebras as an replacement of torsion classes for τ \tau -tilting pairs in τ \tau -tilting theory and as an replacement of inversion sets in the study of right weak order on Weyl groups. With the help of the dominant sets, we prove that the oriented exchange graphs of cluster algebras are acyclic. In particular, this implies that green mutations induce a partial order on the set of seeds (up to seed equivalence) of cluster algebras. We prove that the oriented exchange graphs of cluster algebras coincide with the Hasse quivers of the above posets of seeds.

1.
Introduction

Cluster algebras.
Cluster algebras were invented by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
18
]
as a combinatorial approach to the upper global bases of quantum groups and to the theory of total positivity in algebraic groups. Such algebras often arise as the coordinate rings of spaces, such as double Bruhat cells
[
6
]
, unipotent cells
[
23
]
, double Bott-Samelson cells
[
39
]
and arise as the Grothendieck rings of certain monoidal subcategories of the representations of quantum affine algebras
[
26
,
27
]
.

A cluster algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}
-subalgebra of a rational function field generated by a special set of
generators called
cluster variables
, which are grouped into overlapping subsets,
called
clusters
. A
seed
is a pair consisting of a cluster and a rectangular
integer matrix with skew-symmetrizable principal part. One can obtain new seeds from a given one by a procedure called
mutation
.
The sets of cluster variables and clusters of a cluster algebra are determined by an initial seed and the iterative mutations.
A
cluster momomial
is a monomial in variables from the same cluster. In the main text, we actually use a broader definition of cluster monomials, which allows the presence of the inverse of
frozen variables
.

A fundamental problem in cluster algebras is to characterize when two cluster variables are contained in the same cluster. A more general problem is to characterize when the product of two cluster monomials is still a cluster monomial. Many progresses have been made by introducing different type of compatibility degrees or invariants.

Compatibility degrees in cluster algebras.
In the study of Y-systems and generalized associahedra, Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
20
]
introduce a function

(-\mid\mid-):\Phi_{\geq-1}\times\Phi_{\geq-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

defined on the set
\Phi_{\geq-1}
of almost positive roots associated to a Cartan matrix
C
of finite type. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cluster algebra of finite type with the same Cartan-Killing type as the Cartan matrix
C
. Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
19
]
proved that there is a bijection
from
\Phi_{\geq-1}
to the set
\mathcal{X}
of cluster variables of
\mathcal{A}
and they proved that two cluster variables
x_{\alpha}
and
x_{\beta}
are contained in the same cluster if and only if their corresponding roots
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi_{\geq-1}
are
compatible
, i.e.,
(\alpha\mid\mid\beta)=0
.

Now let
\mathcal{A}
be any skew-symmetrizable cluster algebra (not necessarily finite type). In
[
10
]
, Li and the author of the present paper proved that there exists a well-defined function

(-\mid\mid-)_{d}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}

defined on the set of cluster variables
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{A}
, which is called the
d
-compatibility degree
. The values of the
d
-compatibility degree
(-\mid\mid-)_{d}
are given by the components of the
d
-vectors
[
21
]
in cluster algebras. It is proved that two cluster variables
x,z\in\mathcal{X}
are contained in the same cluster if and only if

(x\mid\mid z)_{d}=0\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;(x\mid\mid z)_{d}=-1,

moreover,
(x\mid\mid z)_{d}=-1
if and only if
x=z
. This confirms a conjecture on
d
-vectors proposed by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
21
, Conjecture 7.4]
.

Recently, Fu and Gyoda
[
22
]
introduced the
f
-compatibility degree in cluster algebras using the components of
f
-vectors, which is a function

(-\mid\mid-)_{f}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

defined on the set
\mathcal{X}
of cluster variables of
\mathcal{A}
.
Based on the results in
[
10
]
, Fu and Gyoda proved that two cluster variables
x,z\in\mathcal{X}
are contained in the same cluster if and only if
(x\mid\mid z)_{f}=0
. We remark that when restricting to cluster algebras of finite type, the
f
-compatibility degree coincide with Fomin-Zelevinsky’s compatibility degree.

E
-invariant.

The
E
-invariant is introduced by Derksen, Weyman and Zelevinsky
[
14
]
in the additive categorification of cluster algebras using decorated representations of quivers with potentials. We refer to
[
13
, Definition 10.1]
for the definition of decorated representations.
For any two decorated representations
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
of a quiver with potential
(Q,W)
,
Derksen, Weyman and Zelevinsky
[
14
, Section 7]
defined an integer
E^{\rm inj}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
, which turns out to take non-negative values (see
[
14
, Section 10]
). The
E
-invariant

E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
associated to
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
is defined to be the symmetrized sum

E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=E^{\rm inj}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})+E^{\rm inj}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})\geq 0.

Derksen, Weyman and Zelevinsky
[
14
, Theorem 7.1]
proved that the integer
E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
is mutation-invariant in the sense that

E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=E^{\rm sym}(\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M}),\mu_{k}(\mathcal{N})),

where
\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mu_{k}(\mathcal{N})
are decorated representations of the new quiver with potential
\mu_{k}(Q,W)
obtained by mutation in direction
k
. As a corollary, they obtained
E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})=0
for any negative-reachable decorated representations, i.e., those decorated representations corresponding to cluster monomials. This result plays a crucial role in their proofs of
[
14
, Conjectures 1.3–1.6]
.

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with potential. There is a dg algebra
\Gamma=\Gamma_{(Q,W)}
and a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}

associated to
(Q,W)
, which are called (complete) Ginzburg dg algebra and cluster category, cf.,
[
2
]
and
[
32
]
.
We remark that
\Gamma
can be viewed as an object in
\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}
satisfying
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}}(\Gamma,\Gamma[1])=0
.
Plamondon
[
34
, Section 4.1]
constructed a bijection
\Psi_{(Q,W)}
from the isomorphism classes of decorated representations of
(Q,W)
to the isomorphism classes of objects in a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}
.
For two decorated representations
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
of
(Q,W)
, we denote by
M=\Psi_{(Q,W)}(\mathcal{M})
and
N=\Psi_{(Q,W)}(\mathcal{N})
the corresponding objects in the cluster category
\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}
. In
[
34
, Proposition 4.15]
, Plamondon proved

E^{\rm inj}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\dim(\Gamma[1])(M,N[1]),

where
(\Gamma[1])(M,N[1])
denotes the subspace of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}}(M,N[1])
consisting of morphisms that factor through an object of
\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(\Gamma[1])
. Thus we have

E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\dim(\Gamma[1])(M,N[1])+\dim(\Gamma[1])(N,M[1]).

For the case of
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
, Plamondon also proved that the above equality becomes

E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})=\dim\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}}(M,M[1]).

One has that
E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})=0
if and only if the corresponding object
M
in the cluster category
\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}
is
rigid
, that is,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}}(M,M[1])=0
. Notice that if
\mathcal{M}
corresponds to a cluster monomial, then
M
is rigid in
\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}
. Let us mention another very special case. If
N
belongs to
\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(\Gamma)
, that is,
\mathcal{N}
corresponds to a cluster monomial in initial cluster variables,
then it is easy to see

(1.1)

\displaystyle E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\dim\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{(Q,W)}}(M,N[1]).

Inspired by Derksen-Weyman-Zelevinsky’s
E
-invariant for Jacobian algebras, the
E
-invariant for any finite dimensional algebra is introduced by Derksen and Fei
[
12
]
in the study of general presentations and by Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
1
]
in the study of
\tau
-tilting theory.

Recently, tropical
F
-polynomials were studied by Fei
[
15
,
16
]
from the viewpoint of representation theory. It turns out that
E^{\rm inj}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
is related to the evaluations of tropical
F
-polynomials, c.f.,
[
16
, Theorem 3.22]
.

\mathfrak{d}
-invariant.
The
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant is introduced by Kang, Kashiwara, Kim and Oh
[
29
]
in the monoidal categorification of (quantum) cluster algebras using representations of quiver Hecke algebras. Here we will mainly follow
[
29
,
31
]
.
Let

R\text{-gmod}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q^{+}}R(\beta)\text{-gmod}

be the category of finite-dimensional graded modules with
homomorphisms of degree
0
given in
[
31
, Page 2270]
, which is a monoidal category with the tensor product
\Huge{\circ}
given there. Denote by

q
the grading shift functor in
R\text{-gmod}
: for
M\in R\text{-gmod}
, we have
(qM)_{i}=M_{i-1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
by definition. For each pair
(M,N)
with
0\neq M\in R(\beta)\text{-gmod}
and
0\neq N\in R(\gamma)\text{-gmod}
, one has the (renormalized)
R
-matrix
{\bf r}_{M,N}
(see
[
29
, Definition 2.2.1]
), which by construction is a morphism from
M\Huge{\circ}N
to
q^{c}N\Huge{\circ}M
in
R\text{-gmod}
for some integer
c
.
We denote by

\Lambda(M,N)=-c.

The
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant

\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
associated to
(M,N)
is defined to be

\mathfrak{d}(M,N)=\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda(M,N)+\Lambda(N,M)).

By
[
29
, Lemma 3.2.1]
,
\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
is a non-negative integer.
A simple object
S
in
R\text{-gmod}
is said to be
real
, if the tensor product
S{\huge\circ}S
is still simple. Kang, Kashiwara, Kim and Oh proved that if
M
is a real simple object in
R\text{-gmod}
, then
\mathfrak{d}(M,M)=0
, cf.
[
29
, Lemma 3.2.3]
. Notice that in the monoidal categorification of cluster algebras, each cluster monomial corresponds to a real simple object. In particular, the
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant associated to a “cluster monomial” is zero. This is an analog of the corresponding result in the additive categorification case.

Recently, Kashiwara, Kim, Oh and Park
[
30
]
introduced the similar invariants
\Lambda(M,N)
and
\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
into the monoidal categorification of cluster algebras using representations of quantum affine algebras. In particular, in this setting, they proved that (i)
\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
takes non-negative values; (ii) if
M
is a real simple object, then
\mathfrak{d}(M,M)=0
, cf.
[
30
, Proposition 3.16, Corollary 3.17]
.

Tropical invariant and
F
-invariant.
In this paper, we introduce the
F
-invariant in cluster algebras using tropicalization. This is an analog of the
E
-invariant introduced by Derksen, Weyman and Zelevinsky in the additive categorification of cluster algebras and
the
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant introduced by Kang, Kashiwara, Kim and Oh in the monoidal categorification of (quantum) cluster algebras. The
F
-invariant is also a great improvement of Fu-Gyoda’s
f
-compatibility degree defined on the set of cluster variables.

Decorated representations of
(Q,W)
and finite-dimensional graded modules in
R\text{-gmod}
are replaced by “good elements” in upper cluster algebras. A
good element
is a compatibly pointed element in the sense of
[
37
]
satisfying the universally positive property, see Definition
3.6
for details. One notice is that cluster monomials belong to the class of good elements.

For any choice of seed
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
, we define an integer
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
for a pair of good elements
u
and
u^{\prime}

in Definition
4.7
.
Our first main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.8
)
.

The integer
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
only depends on
u
and
u^{\prime}
, not on the choice of seed
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
.

From the proof of Theorem
4.8
, we can see that the integer
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
can be interpreted as the evaluation of the positive function
u
(on a cluster
\mathscr{A}
-variety in the sense of
[
17
]
) at a tropical point (associated to the same cluster
\mathscr{A}
-variety) determined by
u^{\prime}
. Different choices of seeds provide different charts to calculate this evaluation.

By Theorem
1.1
, we get an invariant
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle:=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
for any pair
(u,u^{\prime})
of good elements. We call
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle
the
tropical invariant
between
u
and
u^{\prime}
.
We remark that the tropical invariant
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
is an analog of the invariant
\Lambda(M,N)
in the monoidal categorification of cluster algebras, because both
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle
and
\Lambda(M,N)
are related to the quantization matrices appearing in compatible pairs that are used to define quantum cluster algebras in the sense of
[
7
]
, cf. Remark
4.14
.

The
F
-invariant

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}
is defined to be the symmetrized sum of the tropical invariants.

(1.2)

\displaystyle(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}:=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle+\langle u^{\prime},u\rangle=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}+\langle u^{\prime},u\rangle_{w}.

We remark that
the
F
-invariant defined above is an analog of the
E
-invariant
E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
in the additive categorification and
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant
\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
in the monoidal categorification.
Strictly speaking, the
F
-invariant
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}
is analogous to
2\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
, because
in (
1.2
) we did not divide out
``2"
from the symmetrized sum
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle+\langle u^{\prime},u\rangle
.

We prove that the
F
-invariant admits the similar properties with the
E
-invarant and
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant.

Proposition 1.2

(Proposition
4.11
)
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

For any pair
(u,u^{\prime})
of good elements, we have
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(ii)

If
u
is a cluster monomial, then
(u\mid\mid u)_{F}=0
.

We remark that the class of good elements satisfying
(u\mid\mid u)_{F}=0
is analogous to the class of decorated representations satisfying
E^{\rm sym}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})=0
and to the class of finite-dimension graded modules satisfying
\mathfrak{d}(M,M)=0
.

As mentioned in
[
30
, Pages 1040–1041]
, the integer-valued invariants
\Lambda(M,N)
and
\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
provide important information in the representation theory of quiver Hecke algebra. However, in general, computing those values is quite difficult. We hope our tropical invariant
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle
and
F
-invariant
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}
can shed some light on the calculation of the integer-valued invariants
\Lambda(M,N)
and
\mathfrak{d}(M,N)
in the monoidal categorification of cluster algebras.

Since the
F
-invariant
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}
always takes non-negative values, it is natural to ask when
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
holds.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.22
)
.

Let
u
and
u^{\prime}
be two cluster monomials in an upper cluster algebra. Then
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
if and only if the product
uu^{\prime}
is still a cluster monomial.

For arbitrary two good elements
u
and
u^{\prime}
(not necessarily cluster monomials), we give a necessary condition for
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
in Theorem
4.27
. Before stating the result, we need some preparations.

We use
m
to denote the number of cluster variables (frozen and unfrozen) in a cluster and use
n
to denote the number of unfrozen cluster variables in a cluster. By Definition
3.6
, each good element
u
corresponds to a tropical point
[{\bf g}]=\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
(associated to a cluster
\mathscr{X}
-variety in the sense of
[
17
]
).
Two tropical points
[{\bf g}]=\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]=\{({\bf g}^{\prime})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
are said to be
sign-coherent
if for any vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
and any
k\in[1,n]
, we have
g_{k}^{t}\cdot(g_{k}^{\prime})^{t}\geq 0
, where
g_{k}^{t}
and
(g_{k}^{\prime})^{t}
are the
k
th components of
{\bf g}^{t}
and
({\bf g}^{\prime})^{t}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
4.27
)
.

Let
u
and
u^{\prime}
be two good elements in an upper cluster algebra
\mathcal{U}
. If
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
, then
the tropical points correspond to
u
and
u^{\prime}
are sign-coherent and the product
uu^{\prime}
is still a good element in
\mathcal{U}
.

We also have a discussion on the link between the compatible Poisson structure of (upper) cluster algebras and the
F
-invariant.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
4.29
)
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

If a good element
u
and an unfrozen cluster variable
x
are log-canonical with respect to the given compatible Poisson bracket, then
(x\mid\mid u)_{F}=0
.

(ii)

If two cluster variables are log-canonical, then they are contained in the same cluster.

Dominant sets and oriented exchange graphs.

Two seeds of a cluster algebra are said to be
equivalent
, if they are the same up to a relabeling. The
exchange graph

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{A})
of a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
is the graph whose vertices correspond to the seeds (up to seed equivalence) of
\mathcal{A}
and whose edges correspond to seed mutations.
Thanks to the sign-coherence of
C
-matrices
[
25
]
, each mutation has two states: green mutation or red mutation. Fix an initial seed
({\bf x}_{t_{0}},\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}})
, the
oriented exchange graph

\overrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{t_{0}}
of
\mathcal{A}
is the quiver whose underlying graph is the exchange graph
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{A}
and whose orientation is induced by the green mutations.

\tau
-tilting theory is introduced by Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
1
]
as a far reaching generalization of cluster-tilting theory (one of the first major developments in representation theory
discovered thanks to the relation with cluster algebras). Various of the fundamental concepts in cluster algebras, such as clusters/seeds, mutations,
C
-matrices,
G
-matrices, (oriented) exchange graphs,
etc., were generalized to
\tau
-tilting theory.

The seeds in cluster algebras correspond to (basic)
\tau
-tilting pairs in
\tau
-tilting theory, which are in bijection with the functorially finite torsion classes in the module categories. The inclusion of subcategories induces a natural partial order on the set of functorially finite torsion classes and thus a partial order on the set of
\tau
-tilting pairs (up to isomorphisms).

Thanks to the existence of the partial order, the oriented exchange graphs in
\tau
-tilting theory are acyclic. Actually, Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
1
, Theorem 0.6]
proved that the oriented exchange graphs in
\tau
-tilting theory coincide with the Hasse quivers of the posets of functorially finite torsion classes, equivalently, the posets of
\tau
-tilting pairs.

It is natural to ask whether the oriented exchange graphs of (skew-symmetrizable) cluster algebras are acyclic and whether these oriented exchange graphs coincide with
Hasse quivers of some posets.

Inspired by
F
-invariant, we introduce the dominant sets for seeds of cluster algebras as an replacement of torsion classes for
\tau
-tilting pairs in
\tau
-tilting theory and as an replacement of inversion sets in the study of right weak order on Weyl groups. With the help of the dominant sets, we prove the following results on oriented exchange graphs.

Theorem 1.6

(Theorems
5.7
,
5.12
, Corollary
5.13
)
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

The oriented exchange graphs of cluster algebras are acyclic. In particular, green mutations induce a partial order on the set of seeds (up to seed equivalence) of cluster algebras.

(ii)

The oriented exchange graphs of cluster algebras coincide with the Hasse quivers of the posets of seeds of cluster algebras.

Remark 1.7
.

When I was a PhD student (2014-2019), I was interested in the above problems. But at that time, I was not able to prove them, because I did not know what is the right replacements of torsion classes in cluster algebras.

Finally, we would like to point out that by ignoring initial cluster variables, the dominant sets in cluster algebras correspond to the sets of indecomposable
\tau
-rigid modules contained in the functorially finite torsion classes (see Proposition
5.21
). In this sense, we view the dominant sets as an replacement of torsion classes in
\tau
-titling theory and please see Section
5.4
for details. By ignoring initial cluster variables, the dominant sets also correspond to the inversion sets in the study of right weak order on Weyl groups (see Proposition
5.30
).

2.
Preliminaries on cluster algebras

Throughout this paper, we fix a pair
(n,m)
of integers with
m\geq n>0
. For any positive integer
r
, we denote
[1,r]:=\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
The matrices in this paper are always integer matrices.

2.1.
Mutation matrices and their mutations

Recall that an
n\times n
integer matrix
B
is said to be
skew-symmetrizable
, if there exists a diagonal integer matrix
S=diag(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
with
s_{i}>0
(
i\in[1,n]
) such that
SB
is skew-symmetric. Such a diagonal matrix
S
is called a
skew-symmetrizer
of
B
.

An
m\times n
integer matrix
\widetilde{B}=\begin{bmatrix}B\\
P\end{bmatrix}=(b_{ij})
is called a
mutation matrix
, if its top
n\times n
submatrix
B
is skew-symmetrizable. The submatrix
B
is called the
principal part
of
\widetilde{B}
.

Let
\widetilde{B}=\begin{bmatrix}B\\
P\end{bmatrix}=(b_{ij})
be an
m\times n
mutation matrix. The
mutation
of
\widetilde{B}

in direction
k\in[1,n]
is
defined to be the new integer matrix
\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B})=\widetilde{B}^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}B^{\prime}\\
P^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=(b_{ij}^{\prime})
given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle b_{ij}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-b_{ij},&\text{if}\;i=k\;\text{or}\;j=k;\\
b_{ij}+[b_{ik}]_{+}[b_{kj}]_{+}-[-b_{ik}]_{+}[-b_{kj}]_{+},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
[a]_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

The following statements are well-known, c.f.,
[
18
, Proposition 4.5]
,
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
.

•

\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B})=\widetilde{B}^{\prime}
is still a mutation matrix, namely, the submatrix
B^{\prime}
of
\widetilde{B}^{\prime}
is skew-symmetrizable;

•

B
and
B^{\prime}
share the same skew-symmetrizer;

•

\mu_{k}
is an involution, i.e.,
\mu_{k}^{2}=id
;

•

The rank of
\widetilde{B}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B})
is equal to the rank of
\widetilde{B}
.

As shown in
[
6
, (3.2)]
, the matrix
\widetilde{B}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B})
can be written as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B})=E_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}\widetilde{B}F_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}},

where
\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}
and

•

E_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}
is the
m\times m
matrix obtained by replacing the
k
th column of
I_{m}
by the column vector
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{m})^{T}
, where

e_{i}=\begin{cases}-1,&\text{if }i=k;\\
[-\varepsilon b_{ik}]_{+},&\text{if }i\neq k.\end{cases}

•

F_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}
is the
n\times n
matrix obtained by replacing the
k
th row of
I_{n}
by the row vector
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
, where

f_{i}=\begin{cases}-1,&\text{if }i=k;\\
[\varepsilon b_{ki}]_{+},&\text{if }i\neq k.\end{cases}

It turns out that
\widetilde{B}^{\prime}
is independent of the choice of a sign
\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}
in (
2.2
). The matrices
E_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}
and
F_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}
defined above coincide with the ones used in
[
7
, (3.2), (3.3)]
. It is easy to check

(2.3)

\displaystyle(E_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}})^{2}=I_{m}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;(F_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}})^{2}=I_{n}.

2.2.

Let
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})
be an

Definition 2.1

(Compatible pair,
[

]
)

Keep

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{kj}\lambda_{ki}=\delta_{i,j}s_{j}.

In other words,

, where

is the

Notice that the diagonal matrix

is uniquely determined by the compatible pair

. The matrix

in a compatible pair

is called a

.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
7
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
(\widetilde{B},\Lambda)
be a compatible pair of type
S
and
B
the top

submatrix of
\widetilde{B}
. Then the matrix
\widetilde{B}
has the full rank

. In particular,

is skew-symmetrizable.

Conversely, by
[

, Theorem 1.4]
, whenever

is a mutation matrix of full rank, we can find a skew-symmetric integer matrix

such that

is a compatible pair.

Let
(\widetilde{B},\Lambda)
be a compatible pair of type
S
and
k\in[1,n]
. We set
\widetilde{B}^{\prime}:=\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B})=E_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}\widetilde{B}F_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}
and

\Lambda^{\prime}:=(E_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}})^{T}\Lambda E_{k,\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{B}}.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
7
, Proposition 3.4]

(i) The matrix
\Lambda^{\prime}
is independent of the choice of
\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}
.

(ii) The pair
(\widetilde{B}^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
is still a compatible pair of type
S
.

Keep the above setting. We call
(\widetilde{B}^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
the
mutation of the compatible pair

(\widetilde{B},\Lambda)
in direction

and denote

. It is known from
[

, Proposition 3.6]
that

, i.e.,
\mu_{k}
is an involution.

The following proposition will be not used in this paper, but it is very useful to construct many examples of compatible pairs from a given skew-symmetrizable matrix.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
40
, Example 0.5]

Let

be an

skew-symmetrizable integer matrix and set
\widetilde{B}=\begin{bmatrix}B\\

. Then the skew-symmetric

in the sense of (

where

is an arbitrary skew-symmetric integer

matrix and

is an arbitrary skew-symmetrizer of

.

2.3.

-pattern and cluster pattern

Recall that we fixed a pair

of integers with

in

•

over

•

of

Let

be a

-seed of rank

in
\mathbb{F}
. The

of

(2.5)

\displaystyle y_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{k}^{-1},&\text{if}\;i=k;\\
y_{i}y_{k}^{[\hat{b}_{ki}]_{+}}(1+y_{k})^{-\hat{b}_{ki}},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{ij}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\hat{b}_{ij},&\text{if}\;i=k\;\text{or}\;j=k;\\
\hat{b}_{ij}+[\hat{b}_{ik}]_{+}[\hat{b}_{kj}]_{+}-[-\hat{b}_{ik}]_{+}[-\hat{b}_{kj}]_{+},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

One can check that the new pair

-seed of rank

and

.

A

\mathbb{Q}

The matrices

Let

be a seed of rank

\displaystyle x_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}x_{i},&\text{if}\;i\neq k;\\

3.1.

Recall that
(\mathbb{P},\cdot,\oplus)
is called a

(\mathbb{P},\cdot)
is an abelian multiplicative group endowed with a binary operation of auxiliary addition

\mathbb{Q}_{\rm sf}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})

.

From now on, we fix
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{Q}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})

\mathbb{F}_{>0}:=\mathbb{Q}_{\rm sf}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})

for any

.
The map
\pi:{\bf p}\mapsto\pi_{\bf p}
induces a bijection from
\mathbb{P}^{m}
to the set
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\rm sf}(\mathbb{F}_{>0},\mathbb{P})
of semifield homomorphisms from
\mathbb{F}_{>0}
to
\mathbb{P}
.

In this paper, we often take
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max}=(\mathbb{Z},+,\max)

are called

3.2.
Compatibly pointed elements and good elements

In this subsection, we fix a
full rank
upper cluster algebra
\mathcal{U}
, which means its initial mutation matrix
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}
has the full rank
n
, equivalently, any mutation matrix
\widetilde{B}_{t}
of
\mathcal{U}
has the full rank
n
.

Since
\mathcal{U}
is of full rank, each seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
of
\mathcal{U}
defines a partial order
\preceq_{t}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. For two vectors
{\bf g},{\bf g}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, we write
{\bf g}^{\prime}\preceq_{t}{\bf g}
if there exists some vector
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that

{\bf g}^{\prime}={\bf g}+\widetilde{B}_{t}{\bf v},

equivalently,
{\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}^{\prime}}={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf g}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{t}^{\bf v}
, where
\widehat{\bf y}_{t}^{\bf v}={\bf x}_{t}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}{\bf v}}
. We denote by
{\bf g}^{\prime}\prec_{t}{\bf g}
for the case
{\bf g}^{\prime}\preceq_{t}{\bf g}
and
{\bf g}^{\prime}\neq{\bf g}
.

The partial order
\preceq_{t}
above is known as
dominance order
associated to seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
in
[
35
]
. The dominance order plays an important role in study of the bases problem of (upper) cluster algebras.

For any seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
of
\mathcal{U}
, we denote by
R_{t}
the collection of the formal Laurent series

u=\sum_{{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}b_{\bf h}{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h},\;\;\;b_{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z},

such that the set
\{{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid b_{\bf h}\neq 0\}
has finitely many maximal elements with respect to the dominance order
\preceq_{t}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. One can see that the collection
R_{t}
has a ring structure.

Now we recall the definition of pointed elements introduced in
[
35
]
. Let

u=\sum_{{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}b_{\bf h}{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}\in R_{t}.

We say that
u
is
pointed
for the seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
if the following two conditions are satisfied.

•

The set
\{{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid b_{\bf h}\neq 0\}
has a unique maximal element
{\bf g}
under the dominance order
\preceq_{t}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. We denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{t}(u):={\bf g}
and call it the
degree
of
u
with respect to the seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
.

•

The coefficient corresponds to the degree term is
1
, i.e.,
b_{\bf g}=1
for
{\bf g}=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{t}(u)
.

By definition, each pointed element
u
for the seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
takes the following form.

u={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf g}+\sum_{{\bf h}\prec_{t}{\bf g}}b_{\bf h}{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf g}F(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),

where
b_{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
F\in\mathbb{Z}[[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]]
is a polynomial series with constant term
1
.

In cluster algebras, we are often interested in the pointed elements which can be controlled by tropical points associated to a
Y
-pattern. Such elements correspond to compatibly pointed elements introduced in
[
37
]
.

Definition 3.6

(Compatibly pointed elements and good elements)
.

Let
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y})
be a Langlands dual pair and
\mathcal{U}
the upper cluster algebra associated to the cluster pattern

. Assume that
\mathcal{U}

(i)

forms a tropical point in

. In this case, we also say that

is
[{\bf g}]
-pointed
.

(ii)

A
[{\bf g}]
-pointed element

[{\bf g}]
-good

of

can be written as

(3.3)

\displaystyle u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),

where
{\bf g}_{u}^{t}=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{t}(u)
is the value of
[{\bf g}]
at vertex
t
and

is a polynomial in

1
.

Let
u
be a
[{\bf g}]
-pointed element, then the Laurent expansion of
u
with respect to any seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
of
\mathcal{U}
has a
canonical expression:

(3.4)

\displaystyle u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),

where
{\bf g}_{u}^{t}=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{t}(u)
is the value of
[{\bf g}]
at vertex
t
and
F_{u}^{t}
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with constant term
1
.

•

The degree vector
{\bf g}_{u}^{t}=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{t}(u)
is often called the
extended
g
-vector
of
u
with respect to vertex
t
;

•

The polynomial
F_{u}^{t}
is called the
F
-polynomial
of
u
with respect to vertex
t
;

•

For
k\in[1,n]
, we denote by

the highest degree of

in
F_{u}^{t}
. The vector

{\bf f}_{u}^{t}:=(f_{1;u}^{t},\ldots,f_{n;u}^{t})^{T}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

is called
the
f
-vector
of

with respect to vertex

;

•

The
[{\bf g}]
-pointed element

[{\bf g}]
-bipointed

the monomial

[{\bf g}]

Recall that a

in
\mathcal{U}

Keep the setting as in Definition

be an edge in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. The following statements hold.

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}({\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}})={\bf g}^{w}
, where
{\bf e}_{k}
is the
k
th column of
I_{n}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
u\in\mathcal{U}
is a
[{\bf g}]
-pointed element. Let
u={\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf v}
be the Laurent expansion of
u
with respect to seed
({\bf x}_{s},\widetilde{B}_{s})
. Then
the coefficient of
\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}}
in
\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf v}
is
1
and for any integer
d
with
d>[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}
, the coefficient of
\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{d{\bf e}_{k}}
in
\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf v}
is zero.

Proof.

(i) Let
\widetilde{G}_{s}^{w}
and
C_{s}^{w}
be the extended
G
-matrix and
C
-matrix of vertex
s
with respect to
w
.
By Lemma
3.9
and Lemma
3.11
, we have

\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}({\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}})=\widetilde{G}_{s}^{w}{\bf g}^{s}+\widetilde{B}_{w}C_{s}^{w}\cdot([-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}).

Since the two vertices
w
and
s
are jointed in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
by an edge labeled by
k
,
we know that
\widetilde{G}_{s}^{w}=(g_{ij;s})
and
C_{s}^{w}=(c_{ij;s})
are given as follows:

\displaystyle g_{ij;s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-1,&i=j=k;\\
[-b_{ik;w}]_{+}=[b_{ik;s}]_{+},&j=k,\;i\neq k;\\
\delta_{i,j},&\text{else},\end{cases}

\displaystyle c_{ij;s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-1,&i=j=k;\\
[b_{kj;w}]_{+}=[-b_{kj;s}]_{+},&i=k,\;j\neq k;\\
\delta_{i,j},&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Because the
k
-th column of
C_{s}^{w}
equals
-{\bf e}_{k}
, we have

\widetilde{G}_{s}^{w}{\bf g}^{s}+\widetilde{B}_{w}C_{s}^{w}\cdot([-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k})=\widetilde{G}_{s}^{w}{\bf g}^{s}-\widetilde{B}_{w}\cdot([-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}).

Now we look at the
i
th component
g_{i}^{\prime}
of the vector

{\bf g}^{\prime}:=\widetilde{G}_{s}^{w}{\bf g}^{s}-\widetilde{B}_{w}\cdot([-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}).

We have

g_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}-g_{k}^{s}-0=-g_{k}^{s},&i=k;\\
(g_{i}^{s}+[b_{ik;s}]_{+}g_{k}^{s})-b_{ik;w}[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}=g_{i}^{s}+[b_{ik;s}]_{+}g_{k}^{s}+b_{ik;s}[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+},&i\neq k.\end{cases}

Since
\widehat{B}_{s}=-\widetilde{B}_{s}^{T}
, we can rewrite the above equality as follows:

g_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}-g_{k}^{s},&i=k;\\
g_{i}^{s}+[-\hat{b}_{ki;s}]_{+}g_{k}^{s}-\hat{b}_{ki;s}[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+},&i\neq k.\end{cases}

Using the equality
[b]_{+}g-[-b]_{+}g=bg=b[g]_{+}-b[-g]_{+}
for any
b,g\in\mathbb{R}
, one can see the above equality is the same as

g_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}-g_{k}^{s},&i=k;\\
g_{i}^{s}+[\hat{b}_{ki;s}]_{+}g_{k}^{s}-\hat{b}_{ki;s}[g_{k}^{s}]_{+},&i\neq k.\end{cases}

Then by (
3.1
), we get
{\bf g}^{w}={\bf g}^{\prime}=\widetilde{G}_{s}^{w}{\bf g}^{s}-\widetilde{B}_{w}\cdot([-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k})
. Hence,

\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}({\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}})={\bf g}^{w}
.

(ii) Since
u
is
[{\bf g}]
-pointed, we know that it is pointed for the seed
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
.
By Corollary
3.12
(ii), there exists a unique
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with

such that

\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}({\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf v})\preceq_{w}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}({\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf u})=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}(u)={\bf g}^{w}

for any
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
c_{\bf v}\neq 0
. By the result in (i), we know that
{\bf u}=[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}
.

Notice that
\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}({\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf v})\preceq_{w}\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{w}({\bf x}_{s}^{{\bf g}^{s}}\cdot\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf u})
holds if and only if the following relation holds.

C_{s}^{w}{\bf v}-C_{s}^{w}{\bf u}=C_{s}^{w}({\bf v}-{\bf u})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

Since the
k
th column of
C_{s}^{w}
is
-{\bf e}_{k}
, we obtain that
C_{s}^{w}(d{\bf e}_{k}-[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k})
belongs to
\mathbb{N}^{n}
if and only if

. Hence, for any integer
d
with
d>[-g_{k}^{s}]_{+}
, the coefficient of
\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{d{\bf e}_{k}}
in
\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}\widehat{\bf y}_{s}^{\bf v}
is zero.

∎

Corollary 3.14
.

Let

u
be a
[{\bf g}]
-pointed element in
\mathcal{U}
and let
{\bf f}_{u}^{t}=(f_{1;u}^{t},\ldots,f_{n;u}^{t})^{T}
be the
f
-vector of
u
with respect to vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
. The following statements hold.

(i)

If
f_{k;u}^{t}=0
for some
k\in[1,n]
, then the
k
th component
g_{k}^{t}
of
{\bf g}_{u}^{t}=\operatorname{\mathsf{deg}}^{t}(u)
is non-negative;

(ii)

If
f_{i;u}^{t}=0
for any
i\in[1,n]
, then
u
is a cluster monomial in
{\bf x}_{t}
;

(iii)

Let
k
be an integer in
[1,n]
and
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})=\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
. Suppose that

f_{k;u}^{t}\neq 0
but
f_{i;u}^{t}=0
for any
i\in[1,n]\backslash\{k\}
. Then
u
is a cluster monomial in
{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Since
u
is
[{\bf g}]
-pointed in
\mathcal{U}
, the Laurent expansion of
u
with respect to
{\bf x}_{t}
has a cannonical expression:

u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),

where
{\bf g}_{u}^{t}=(g_{1}^{t},\ldots,g_{m}^{t})^{T}
is the value of
[{\bf g}]
at vertex
t
and
F_{u}^{t}
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with constant term
1
.

(i) By Corollary
3.13
(ii), we know that
y_{k}^{[-g_{k}^{t}]_{+}}
must be a monomial appearing in
F_{u}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. Recall that
f_{k;u}^{t}
is the highest degree of
y_{k}
in
F_{u}^{t}
. So we have
f_{k;u}^{t}\geq[-g_{k}^{t}]_{+}\geq 0
. Hence, if
f_{k;u}^{t}=0
, then
[-g_{k}^{t}]_{+}=0
. So
g_{k}^{t}
is non-negative.

(ii) Since
f_{i;u}^{t}=0
for any
i\in[1,n]
, we get
F_{u}^{t}=1
and
u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}
. On the other hand, by (i), we have
g_{i}^{t}\geq 0
for any
i\in[1,n]
. Hence,
u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}
is a cluster monomial in
{\bf x}_{t}
.

(iii) Since
f_{i;u}^{t}=0
holds for any
i\in[1,n]\setminus\{k\}
, we get that
F_{u}^{t}
is a polynomial in the single variable
y_{k}
. In this case, Corollary
3.13
(ii) implies
f_{k;u}^{t}=[-g_{k}^{t}]_{+}
. Since
f_{k;u}^{t}\neq 0
, we have
g_{k}^{t}<0
and thus
f_{k;u}^{t}=-g_{k}^{t}
.

Now
u
has the following form:

u=x_{1;t}^{g_{1}^{t}}\cdots x_{m;t}^{g_{m}^{t}}(1+\sum_{0<d<-g_{k}^{t}}c_{d}\hat{y}_{k;t}^{d}+\hat{y}_{k;t}^{-g_{k}^{t}}),

where
c_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
. By the exchange relations, we have
x_{j;t^{\prime}}=x_{j;t}
for any
j\neq k
and
x_{k;t}\cdot x_{k;t^{\prime}}=M\cdot(1+\hat{y}_{k;t})
, where
M=\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{j;t}^{[-b_{jk;t}]_{+}}
.
By substituting these relations into the above equality and using the fact
u\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1;t^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{m;t^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}]
, one deduces that

(1+\sum_{0<d<-g_{k}^{t}}c_{d}\hat{y}_{k;t}^{d}+\hat{y}_{k;t}^{-g_{k}^{t}})=(1+\hat{y}_{k;t})^{-g_{k}^{t}}

and the Laurent expansion of
u
with respect to seed
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})
is a Laurent monomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1;t^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{m;t^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}]
. Thus the
F
-polynomial
F_{u}^{t^{\prime}}
of
u
with respect to vertex
t^{\prime}
is
1
. In particular, the
f
-vector
{\bf f}_{u}^{t^{\prime}}
of
u
with respect to vertex
t^{\prime}
is zero. By (ii), we know that
u
is a cluster monomial in
{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}
.

∎

4.1.
Maps between sets of tropical points

Let
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y})
be a Langlands dual pair. In this subsection, we give two maps. The first map is from the tropical points in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
to those in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
and the second one is from the tropical points in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
to those in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
.
The second map will play an important role in the proof Theorem
4.8
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y})
be a Langlands dual pair and
\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
a collection of vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
indexed by the vertices in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. Then the collection
\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a tropical point in

\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
if and only if for any edge
\textstyle{t}
\textstyle{t^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{k}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, one has
{\bf g}^{t^{\prime}}=E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}{\bf g}^{t}
,
where

\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})=\begin{cases}-,&\text{if }g_{k}^{t}\geq 0;\\
+,&\text{if }g_{k}^{t}<0,\end{cases}

and

E_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}
is as defined in (
2.2
).

Proof.

By definition, the collection
\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a tropical point in

\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
if and only if the relations in (
3.1
) hold. Since
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y})
is a Langlands dual pair, we have
\widehat{B}_{t}=-\widetilde{B}_{t}^{T}
. Thus
\hat{b}_{ij;t}=-b_{ji;t}
for any
i\in[1,n]
and
j\in[1,m]
. Then the relations in (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle g_{i}^{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-g_{k}^{t},&\text{if}\;i=k;\\
g_{i}^{t}+[-b_{ik;t}]_{+}g_{k}^{t}+b_{ik;t}[g_{k}^{t}]_{+},&\text{if}\;i\neq k.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-g_{k}^{t},&\text{if}\;i=k;\\
g_{i}^{t}+[b_{ik;t}]_{+}g_{k}^{t},&\text{if}\;i\neq k\;\;\text{and }\;g_{k}^{t}\geq 0;\\
g_{i}^{t}+[-b_{ik;t}]_{+}g_{k}^{t},&\text{if}\;i\neq k\;\;\text{and }\;g_{k}^{t}<0.\end{cases}

This is exactly the same with
{\bf g}^{t^{\prime}}=E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}{\bf g}^{t}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{X}
be a cluster pattern of rank
n
in
\mathbb{F}
and
\{{\bf a}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
a
collection of vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
indexed by the vertices of
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. Then the collection
\{{\bf a}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a
tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
if and only if for any edge
\textstyle{t}
\textstyle{t^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{k}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, one has
{\bf a}^{t^{\prime}}=(E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}
, where

\varepsilon_{0}=\begin{cases}-,&\text{if }\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}\geq 0;\\
+,&\text{if }\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}<0,\end{cases}

and

E_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}
is as defined in (
2.2
).

Proof.

By definition, the collection
\{{\bf a}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a
tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
if and only if the relations in (
3.2
) hold. The relations in (
3.2
) can be rewritten as follows:

a_{i}^{t^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}-a_{k}^{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}[b_{jk;t}]_{+}a_{j}^{t},&\text{if}\;i=k\;\;\text{and }\;\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}\geq 0;\\
-a_{k}^{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}[-b_{jk;t}]_{+}a_{j}^{t},&\text{if}\;i=k\;\;\text{and }\;\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}<0;\\
a_{i}^{t},&\text{if}\;i\neq k.\end{cases}

This is exactly the same with
{\bf a}^{t^{\prime}}=(E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}
.
∎

Let
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y})
be a Langlands dual pair. We extend
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}
to an
m\times m
skew-symmetrizable matrix
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}=(\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}\mid M)
, where
M
is an
m\times(m-n)
matrix. Denote by
\{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
the collection of matrices obtained from
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}
by any sequence of mutations in directions

. Let
\widetilde{S}=diag(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
be a skew-symmetrizer of the
transposed
matrix
(\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}})^{T}
. In particular,
\widetilde{S}(\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}})^{T}
is a skew-symmetric matrix.

Proposition 4.3
.

Keep the above setting. Then

[{\bf a}]:=\{{\bf a}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}\mapsto\{\widetilde{S}(\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}

defines a map from the tropical points in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
to those in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
.

Proof.

Denote by
{\bf g}^{t}:=\widetilde{S}(\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}=(g_{1}^{t},\ldots,g_{m}^{t})^{T}
for any vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
. We will use Lemma
4.1
to show the collection
\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
.

Let
\textstyle{t}
\textstyle{t^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{k}
be an edge in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
.
Since
[{\bf a}]=\{{\bf a}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
and by Lemma
4.2
, we have
{\bf a}^{t^{\prime}}=(E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}
, where

\varepsilon_{0}=\begin{cases}-,&\text{if }\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}\geq 0;\\
+,&\text{if }\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}<0.\end{cases}

By (

\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}=E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}}\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}F_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}}

Thus

\widetilde{S}

\widetilde{S}(\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}})^{T}=-\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}\widetilde{S}

.
In the following proofs, we will use the following obvious relations:

\widetilde{S}(F_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}})^{T}=(E_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}})\widetilde{S},\;\;\;E_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}}=E_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}},\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;(E_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{2}=I_{m}.

\displaystyle{\bf g}^{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}(\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t^{\prime}}=\widetilde{S}\left((F_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}})^{T}(\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}})^{T}(E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{sq}}}})^{T}\right)\cdot(E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}

\displaystyle(E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}){\bf g}^{t}.

Now we prove
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})
, where
\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})
is as given in Lemma
4.1
. By the definitions of
\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})
and
\varepsilon_{0}
, it suffices to show that

if and only if

.
This is clear, because

, we know the collection

.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y},\Lambda)
be a Langlands-Poisson triple. Then

[{\bf g}]=\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}\mapsto\{\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}

defines a map from the tropical points in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})

\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})

Denote by
{\bf a}^{t}:=\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}^{t}=(a_{1}^{t},\ldots,a_{m}^{t})^{T}
for any vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.
We will use Lemma
4.2
to show the collection
\{{\bf a}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
.

Let
\textstyle{t}
\textstyle{t^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{k}
be an edge in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
.
Since
[{\bf g}]=\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
and by Lemma
4.1
, we have
{\bf g}^{t^{\prime}}=E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}{\bf g}^{t}
,
where

\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})=\begin{cases}-,&\text{if }g_{k}^{t}\geq 0;\\

\displaystyle{\bf a}^{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\text{by def.}}}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}{\bf g}^{t^{\prime}}=\left((E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}\Lambda_{t}E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}\right)\cdot\left(E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}}{\bf g}^{t}\right)

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{(E_{k,\pm}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{2}=I_{m}}}

\displaystyle(E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}(\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}^{t})=(E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}.

Now we prove
\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})=\varepsilon_{0}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is as given in Lemma
4.2
. By the definitions of
\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})
and
\varepsilon_{0}
, it suffices to show that

if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}\geq 0
.
Since
(\widetilde{B}_{t},\Lambda_{t})
is a compatible pair, we know that

\widetilde{B}_{t}^{T}\Lambda_{t}
has the form

\widetilde{B}_{t}^{T}\Lambda_{t}=(S\mid{\bf 0}),

where
S=diag(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
is a diagonal matrix with
s_{i}>0
,
i\in[1,n]
and
{\bf 0}
is the
n\times(m-n)
zero matrix.
Thus we have
\widetilde{B}_{t}^{T}{\bf a}^{t}=\widetilde{B}_{t}^{T}\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}^{t}=(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}^{t}
. By comparing the
k
th component on both sides of the equality, we get

\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}=s_{k}g_{k}^{t}.

Since
s_{k}
is a positive integer, we get that
g_{k}^{t}\geq 0
if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jk;t}a_{j}^{t}\geq 0
. So we have
\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})=\varepsilon_{0}
.

Hence,
{\bf a}^{t^{\prime}}=(E_{k,\varepsilon(g_{k}^{t})}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}=(E_{k,\varepsilon_{0}}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}})^{T}{\bf a}^{t}
. By Lemma
4.2
, we know the collection

\{{\bf a}^{t}=\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}

is a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Tropical invariant and
F
-invariant

Given a non-zero polynomial

F=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}{\bf y}^{{\bf v}}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

and a vector
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we denote by

F[{\bf r}]:=\max\{{\bf v}^{T}{\bf r}\mid c_{{\bf v}}\neq 0\}\in\mathbb{Z}.

We call the map
F[-]:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
a
tropical polynomial
.

Example 4.5
.

Take
F=1+y_{1}+y_{1}y_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},y_{2}]
and
{\bf r}=\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix}
. Then

\displaystyle F[{\bf r}]=\max\{\begin{bmatrix}0,0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1,0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1,1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix}\}=\max\{0,-2,-1\}=0.

The following proposition can be easily checked.

Proposition 4.6
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

If
0\neq F\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
has constant term
1
, then
F[{\bf r}]\geq 0
for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

(ii)

If
0\neq F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
, then
(F_{1}F_{2})[{\bf r}]=F_{1}[{\bf r}]+F_{2}[{\bf r}]

for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Now we fix a Langlands-Poisson triple
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y},\Lambda)
, and let
\mathcal{U}
be the upper cluster algebra associated to the cluster pattern
\mathcal{S}_{X}
. Denote by
S=diag(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
the type of the compatible pair
(\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}},\Lambda_{t_{0}})
, namely,
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}^{T}\Lambda_{t_{0}}=(S\mid{\bf 0})
, where
{\bf 0}
is the
n\times(m-n)
zero matrix.
By Proposition
2.2
, the mutation matrices of
\mathcal{U}
always have full rank.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}^{[{\bf g}]}
the set of
[{\bf g}]
-good elements in
\mathcal{U}
and by

\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{good}}}:=\bigcup_{[{\bf g}]\in\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})}\mathcal{U}^{[\bf g]}

the set of all good elements in
\mathcal{U}
. Notice that the union
\bigcup_{[{\bf g}]\in\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})}\mathcal{U}^{[\bf g]}
is disjoint.

Definition 4.7

(Tropical pairing)
.

Let
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{good}}}
and consider their canonical expressions with respect to a vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
:

u={\bf x}_{w}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{w}}F_{u}^{w}(\hat{y}_{1;w},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;w}),\;\;\;u^{\prime}={\bf x}_{w}^{{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}}F_{u^{\prime}}^{w}(\hat{y}_{1;w},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;w}).

Define an integer
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
as follows:

\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}:=({\bf g}_{u}^{w})^{T}\Lambda_{w}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}+F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}].

The pairing
\langle-,-\rangle_{w}:\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{good}}}\times\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{good}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

is called the
tropical pairing
at vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

For any two good elements
u
and
u^{\prime}
, the integer
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
only depends on
u
and
u^{\prime}
, not on the choice of
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
. We call
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
the tropical invariant between
u
and
u^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For a vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, let
u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t})
and
u^{\prime}={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}}F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t})

be the canonical expressions of
u
and
u^{\prime}
with respect to
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
. We know that

[{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}]:=\{{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}

is a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
. By Proposition
4.4
, the collection

[{\bf a}]:=\{{\bf a}^{t}=\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}

is a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
. By Corollary
3.5
, the tropical point
[{\bf a}]\in\mathcal{S}_{X}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
corresponds to a unique semifield homomorphism

\beta_{u^{\prime}}:\mathbb{F}_{>0}:=\mathbb{Q}_{\rm sf}(x_{1;t_{0}},\ldots,x_{m;t_{0}})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max}

such that
\beta_{u^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{t})=({\bf a}^{t})^{T}
for any vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
. Notice that the simifield homomorphism
\beta_{u^{\prime}}
is uniquely determined by
u^{\prime}
.

By applying the semifield homomorphism
\beta_{u^{\prime}}
to
u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t})\in\mathbb{F}_{>0}
, we see that

\beta_{u^{\prime}}(u)=\beta_{u^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}})+\beta_{u^{\prime}}(F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t})).

Since
\beta_{u^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{t})=({\bf a}^{t})^{T}=(\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t})^{T}
, we have

\beta_{u^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}})=(\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t})^{T}{\bf g}_{u}^{t}=[(\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t})^{T}{\bf g}_{u}^{t}]^{T}=({\bf g}_{u}^{t})^{T}\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}.

We write
F_{u}^{t}=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}{\bf y}^{{\bf v}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
, then we know that

F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t})=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}{\bf x}_{t}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}{\bf v}}.

We see that

\displaystyle\beta_{u^{\prime}}(\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}{\bf x}_{t}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}{\bf v}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{({\bf a}^{t})^{T}\widetilde{B}_{t}{\bf v}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}=\max\{[({\bf a}^{t})^{T}\widetilde{B}_{t}{\bf v}]^{T}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{{\bf v}^{T}\widetilde{B}_{t}^{T}{\bf a}^{t}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}=\max\{{\bf v}^{T}\widetilde{B}_{t}^{T}\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{{\bf v}^{T}(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}=F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}].

Hence,
\beta_{u^{\prime}}(F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}))=\beta_{u^{\prime}}(\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}{\bf x}_{t}^{\widetilde{B}_{t}{\bf v}})=F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]
and

\beta_{u^{\prime}}(u)=\beta_{u^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}})+\beta_{u^{\prime}}(F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}))=({\bf g}_{u}^{t})^{T}\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}+F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{t}.

Since the semifield homomorphism
\beta_{u^{\prime}}
is uniquely determined by
u^{\prime}
, we know that the integer
\beta_{u^{\prime}}(u)
only depends on
u
and
u^{\prime}
, not on the choice of an initial seed. The equality
\beta_{u^{\prime}}(u)=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{t}
says that each choice of a vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
provides an explicit formula to calculate the integer
\beta_{u^{\prime}}(u)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

From the proof above, we see that the integer
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
can be interpreted as the evaluation of the positive function
u\in\mathbb{F}_{>0}
(on cluster
\mathscr{A}
-variety in the sense of
[
17
]
) at the tropical point
[{\bf a}]:=\{{\bf a}^{t}=\Lambda_{t}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
(associated to the same cluster
\mathscr{A}
-variety) determined by
u^{\prime}
.

Since the tropical invariant
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}
only depends on
u
and
u^{\prime}
, not on the choice of
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, we denote by
\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle:=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}

and call
\langle-,-\rangle:\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{good}}}\times\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{good}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
the
tropical pairing
of
\mathcal{U}
.

Definition 4.10

(
F
-invariant and
F
-compatibility)
.

Let
u
and
u^{\prime}
be two good elements in
\mathcal{U}
.

(i)

The
F
-invariant

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}
between
u
and
u^{\prime}
is defined to be the symmetrized sum of the tropical invariants:

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle+\langle u^{\prime},u\rangle=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}+\langle u^{\prime},u\rangle_{w},

where
w
is any vertex in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

(ii)

u
and
u^{\prime}
are said to be
F
-compatible
, if
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
u
and
u^{\prime}
be two good elements in
\mathcal{U}
. The following statements hold.

(i)

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}]+F_{u^{\prime}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]
, where
w
is any vertex in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

(ii)

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(iii)

If
u
is a cluster monomial, then
(u\mid\mid u)_{F}=0
.

Proof.

(i) Let
w
be a vertex in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. By the definition of
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}
, we see that

\displaystyle(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{w}+\langle u^{\prime},u\rangle_{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bf g}_{u}^{w})^{T}\Lambda_{w}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}+F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}]+({\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w})^{T}\Lambda_{w}{\bf g}_{u}^{w}+F_{u^{\prime}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}].

Since
\Lambda_{w}
is skew-symmetric, we know that
({\bf g}_{u}^{w})^{T}\Lambda_{w}{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}+({\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w})^{T}\Lambda_{w}{\bf g}_{u}^{w}=0
. Thus

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{w}]+F_{u^{\prime}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}].

(ii) Let
w
be a vertex in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. Since both
F_{u}^{w}
and
F_{u^{\prime}}^{w}
are polynomials with constant term
1
, we have

F_{u}^{w}[{\bf h}],\;F_{u^{\prime}}^{w}[{\bf h}]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

for any
{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Then by (i), we have
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(iii) Say
u
is a cluster monomial in seed
({{\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w}})
. Then
F_{u}^{w}=1
, where
F_{u}^{w}
is the
F
-polynomial of
u
with respect to vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
. Then by (i), we see that
(u\mid\mid u)_{F}=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
u
be a
[{\bf g}]
-bigood element in
\mathcal{U}
, and let
{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{j;t}^{h_{j}}
be a cluster monomial of
\mathcal{U}
, i.e.,
{\bf h}^{\circ}:=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. Then

(i)

({\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}\mid\mid u)_{F}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot(x_{j;t}\mid\mid u)_{F}
.

(ii)

F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{w}]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}]
for any vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

Proof.

(i) Consider the canonical expression of
u
with respect to the vertex
t
,

u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),

where
{\bf g}_{u}^{t}
is the value of
[{\bf g}]
at vertex
t
. Since
u
is a
[{\bf g}]
-bigood element,
F_{u}^{t}
has the form:

F_{u}^{t}=1+\text{middle terms }+y_{1}^{f_{1}^{t}}\cdots y_{n}^{f_{n}^{t}},

where
{\bf f}_{u}^{t}=(f_{1}^{t},\ldots,f_{n}^{t})^{T}
is the
f
-vector of
u
with respect to vertex
t
. Since
{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}
is a cluster momomial, we know that
F_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{t}=1
and
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. By using the chart
{\bf x}_{t}
to calculate
({\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}\mid\mid u)_{F}
, we have

\displaystyle({\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}\mid\mid u)_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf h}]=F_{u}^{t}[(s_{1}h_{1},\ldots,s_{n}h_{n})^{T}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{t}s_{1}h_{1}+\ldots+f_{n}^{t}s_{n}h_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot(x_{j;t}\mid\mid u)_{F}.

(ii) Let
w
be a vertex in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. We have

\displaystyle({\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}\mid\mid u)_{F}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\text{by (i)}}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot(x_{j;t}\mid\mid u)_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot\left(F_{x_{j;t}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]+F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot F_{x_{j;t}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}].

(The last equality comes from the facts that
F_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{w}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(F_{x_{j;t}}^{w})^{h_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(F_{x_{j;t}}^{w})^{h_{j}}
and that for any two polynomials
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
and any vector
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have

(F_{1}F_{2})[{\bf r}]=F_{1}[{\bf r}]+F_{2}[{\bf r}],

by the definition of tropical polynomials.) On the other hand, we have

({\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}\mid\mid u)_{F}=F_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]+F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{w}].

By comparing the two equalities, we obtain

F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{w}]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}]
.
∎

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
x
and
z
be two unfrozen cluster variables of
\mathcal{U}
. If
(x\mid\mid z)_{F}=0
, then they are contained in the same cluster.

Proof.

Since
(x\mid\mid z)_{F}=0
and by Remark
4.17
, the
f
-compatibility degree
(x\mid\mid z)_{f}
is zero. Then the result follows from
[
22
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Let us give another proof here. Consider the tropical point

[{\bf g}_{z}]:=\{{\bf g}_{z}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})

corresponding to the cluster variable
z
.
Since
(x\mid\mid z)_{F}=0
and by Corollary
4.18
(i), we know that the
k
th component
g_{k;z}^{t}
of
{\bf g}_{z}^{t}
satisfies
g_{k;z}^{t}\geq 0
whenever
x=x_{k;t}
.

On the other hand, by
[
10
, Theorem 8]
, we know that there exists a seed
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
containing
x
(say
x=x_{k;w}
) such that
g_{i;z}^{w}\geq 0
for any
i\in[1,n]
with
x_{i;t}\neq x
, i.e., for any
i\in[1,n]\setminus\{k\}
. Thus for the vertex
w
, we have
g_{j;z}^{w}\geq 0
for
j=1,\ldots,n
, i.e., the principal part
({\bf g}_{z}^{t})^{\circ}
of
{\bf g}_{z}^{t}
is in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. Thus
u:={\bf x}_{w}^{{\bf g}_{z}^{w}}
is a cluster monomial of
\mathcal{U}
. Hence, the two cluster monomials
u={\bf x}_{w}^{{\bf g}_{z}^{w}}
and
z
have the same extended
g
-vector
{\bf g}_{z}^{w}
with respect to vertex
w
. It is well-known that different cluster monomials have different extended
g
-vectors, c.f.,
[
25
]
. So we must have
z=u={\bf x}_{w}^{{\bf g}_{z}^{w}}
. This implies
z
is a cluster variable in
{\bf x}_{w}
. In particular,
z
and
x=x_{k;w}
are contained in the same cluster
{\bf x}_{w}
.
∎

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}\}
be a subset of unfrozen cluster variables of
\mathcal{U}
. If
(z_{i}\mid\mid z_{j})_{F}=0
for any
i,j\in[1,p]
, then
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}\}
is a subset of some cluster of
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.20
, we know that any two cluster variables in
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}\}
are contained in the same cluster. Then the result follows from
[
10
, Theorem 13]
.
∎

The following result says that
F
-invariant is a very concrete and easily computable numerical invariant to distinguish whether the product of two cluster monomials is still a cluster monomial.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
u
and
u^{\prime}
be two cluster monomials in
\mathcal{U}
. Then the product
uu^{\prime}
is still a cluster monomial in
\mathcal{U}
if and only if
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
, i.e.,

F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]+F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{t}]=0,

for some (equivalently, any) vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

Proof.

“
\Longrightarrow
”: Suppose that the product
uu^{\prime}
is a cluster monomial in
\mathcal{U}
. Say,
uu^{\prime}
is a cluster monomial in cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
. Then both
u
and
u^{\prime}
can be viewed as cluster monomials in
{\bf x}_{t}
. Thus
F_{u}^{t}=1=F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}
. So we have
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]+F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{t}]=0
.

“
\Longleftarrow
”: Suppose
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
. We need to show that
uu^{\prime}
is a cluster monomial in
\mathcal{U}
. Since
u
and
u^{\prime}
are cluster monomials in
\mathcal{U}
, we can assume
u={\bf x}_{s}^{\bf h}
and
u^{\prime}={\bf x}_{s^{\prime}}^{\bf r}
, where
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
and
{\bf h}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})^{T},{\bf r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with
h_{i},r_{i}\geq 0
for any
i\in[1,n]
.

By Proposition
3.7
and Proposition
4.19
, we have

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{i}r_{j}(x_{i;s}\mid\mid x_{j;s^{\prime}})_{F}.

Since all the numbers
h_{i},r_{j}
and
(x_{i;s}\mid\mid x_{j;s^{\prime}})_{F}
are non-negative for
i,j\in[1,n]
, the assumption
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
implies
h_{i}r_{j}(x_{i;s}\mid\mid x_{j;s^{\prime}})_{F}=0
for any
i,j\in[1,n]
.
Hence,
(x_{i;s}\mid\mid x_{j;s^{\prime}})_{F}=0
for any
i,j\in[1,n]
with
h_{i}r_{j}\neq 0
.

Let
V=\{x_{i;s}\mid i\in[1,n],h_{i}\neq 0\}
and
V^{\prime}=\{x_{j;s^{\prime}}\mid j\in[1,n],r_{j}\neq 0\}
. We have
(z\mid\mid z^{\prime})_{F}=0
for any
z\in V
and
z^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
. Since
V
and
V^{\prime}
are subsets of clusters of
\mathcal{U}
, we have

(z_{1}\mid\mid z_{2})_{F}=0\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;(z_{1}^{\prime}\mid\mid z_{2}^{\prime})_{F}=0

for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in V
and
z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
.

Hence,
(z\mid\mid z^{\prime})_{F}=0
holds for
any two cluster variables
z,z^{\prime}
in
V\cup V^{\prime}
. Then by Lemma
4.21
, we know that
V\cup V^{\prime}
is a subset of some cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
of
\mathcal{U}
. Hence, the product
uu^{\prime}
is a cluster monomial in
{\bf x}_{t}
.
∎

Example 4.23
.

Let continue with Example
3.8
. Take
\Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}=\widetilde{B}
. We know that
\widetilde{B}^{T}\Lambda=(S\mid{\bf 0})=I_{2}
and the canonical expressions of
x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}
with respect to the initial seed
t_{0}=({\bf x},\widetilde{B})
are given as follows:

x_{3}={x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}\cdot(1+\widehat{y}_{1}),}\;\;\;x_{4}={x_{1}^{-1}\cdot(1+\widehat{y}_{1}+\widehat{y}_{1}\widehat{y}_{2}),}\;\;\;x_{5}={x_{2}^{-1}\cdot(1+\widehat{y}_{2})}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle(x_{3}\mid\mid x_{4})_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{x_{3}}^{t_{0}}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{4}}^{t_{0}}]+F_{x_{4}}^{t_{0}}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{3}}^{t_{0}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+y_{1})\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
0\end{bmatrix}+(1+y_{1}+y_{1}y_{2})\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{0,\;-1\}+\max\{0,\;-1,\;0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

\displaystyle(x_{3}\mid\mid x_{5})_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+y_{1})\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-1\end{bmatrix}+(1+y_{2})\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{0,\;0\}+\max\{0,\;1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

Then by Theorem
4.22
, we know that
x_{3}x_{4}
is a cluster monomial, while
x_{3}x_{5}
is not.

Recall that the
Newton polytope
of a polynomial
F=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}{\bf y}^{{\bf v}}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
is
the convex hull of
\{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Corollary 4.24
.

Let
u
and
u^{\prime}
be two cluster monomials in
\mathcal{U}
and
t
a vertex of
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. If the two
F
-polynomials
F_{u}^{t}
and
F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}
have the same Newton polytope, then the product
uu^{\prime}
is a cluster monomial of
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

Since
u
and
u^{\prime}
are cluster monomials and by Proposition
4.11
(iii), we have

(u\mid\mid u)_{F}=2F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{t}]=0\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;(u^{\prime}\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=2F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]=0.

We get
F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{t}]=0=F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]
.
Since
F_{u}^{t}
and
F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}
have the same Newton polytope, we have
F_{u}^{t}[{\bf h}]=F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}[{\bf h}]
for any
{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Hence,
we have

\displaystyle(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]+F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{t}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}]+F_{u}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{t}]=0.

Then by Theorem
4.22
, we know that the product
uu^{\prime}
is a cluster monomial.
∎

Definition 4.25
.

Two tropical points
[{\bf g}]=\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]=\{({\bf g}^{\prime})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})

are said to be
sign-coherent
, if for any vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
and any
k\in[1,n]
, we have
g_{k}^{t}\cdot(g_{k}^{\prime})^{t}\geq 0
, where
g_{k}^{t}
and
(g_{k}^{\prime})^{t}
are the
k
th components of
{\bf g}^{t}
and
({\bf g}^{\prime})^{t}
.

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
[{\bf g}]=\{{\bf g}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]=\{({\bf g}^{\prime})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
be two tropical points in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
. If
[{\bf g}]
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]
are sign-coherent, then the collection
\{{\bf g}^{t}+({\bf g}^{\prime})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
is still a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
. We denote it by
[{\bf g}\uplus{\bf g}^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the collection
\{{\bf g}^{t}+({\bf g}^{\prime})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
satisfies the relations in (
3.1
). Thanks to the sign-coherence assumption, this is clear.
∎

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
u
be a
[{\bf g}]
-good element in
\mathcal{U}
and
u^{\prime}
a
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]
-good element in
\mathcal{U}
. Suppose that
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

The two tropical points
[{\bf g}]
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]
are sign-coherent. In particular, the tropical point
[{\bf g}\uplus{\bf g}^{\prime}]
can be defined.

(ii)

The product
uu^{\prime}
is a
[{\bf g}\uplus{\bf g}^{\prime}]
-good element in
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

(i) We write
[{\bf g}]=\{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}=(g_{1;u}^{t},\ldots,g_{m;u}^{t})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]=\{{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}=(g_{1;u^{\prime}}^{t},\ldots,g_{m;u^{\prime}}^{t})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
.
Assume by contradiction that the two tropical points
[{\bf g}]
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]
are not sign-coherent.
Then there exist some vertex
s\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
and some
k\in[1,n]
such that
g_{k;u}^{s}\cdot g_{k;u^{\prime}}^{s}<0
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
g_{k;u}^{s}<0
and
g_{k;u^{\prime}}^{s}>0
.

By Corollary
3.13
, we know that the monomial
{\bf y}^{[-g_{k;u}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k}}=y_{k}^{[-g_{k;u}^{s}]_{+}}
appears in the
F
-polynomial
F_{u}^{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with coefficient
1
, where
{\bf e}_{k}
is the
k
th column of
I_{n}
. Thus we have

F_{u}^{s}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{s}]\geq([-g_{k;u}^{s}]_{+}{\bf e}_{k})^{T}(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{s}=[-g_{k;u}^{s}]_{+}\cdot s_{k}g_{k;u^{\prime}}^{s}.

Then by
g_{k;u}^{s}<0
,
g_{k;u^{\prime}}^{s}>0
and
s_{k}>0
, we get
F_{u}^{s}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{s}]>0
.

Using the chart
{\bf x}_{s}
to calculate the
F
-invariant
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}
, we have

(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=F_{u}^{s}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{s}]+F_{u^{\prime}}^{s}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{s}]\geq F_{u}^{s}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{s}]>0,

which contradicts
(u\mid\mid u^{\prime})_{F}=0
. Hence, the two tropical points
[{\bf g}]
and
[{\bf g}^{\prime}]
must be sign-coherent.

(ii) For any vertex
t
, consider the Laurent expansions of
u
and
u^{\prime}
with respect to vertex
t
.

u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),\;\;\;u^{\prime}={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}}F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),

where
F_{u}^{t}
and
F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}
are polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with constant term
1
. Hence, we have

uu^{\prime}={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}+{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}}H_{uu^{\prime}}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t}),

where
H_{uu^{\prime}}^{t}:=F_{u}^{t}F_{u^{\prime}}^{t}
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with constant term
1
.

By (i) and Lemma
4.26
, we know that
[{\bf g}\uplus{\bf g}^{\prime}]:=\{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}+{\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\mid t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\}
forms a tropical point in
\mathcal{S}_{Y}(\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
. Then by the definition of good elements, we know that the product
uu^{\prime}
is a
[{\bf g}\uplus{\bf g}^{\prime}]
-good element in
\mathcal{U}
.
∎

4.3.
Log-canonicality and
F
-compatibility

In this subsection, we discuss the link between the compatible Poisson structure of (upper) cluster algebra and the
F
-compatibility.

As before, we fix a Langlands-Poisson triple
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y},\Lambda)
, and let
\mathcal{U}
be the upper cluster algebra associated to the cluster pattern
\mathcal{S}_{X}
. Denote by
S=diag(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
the type of the compatible pair
(\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}},\Lambda_{t_{0}})
, namely,
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}^{T}\Lambda_{t_{0}}=(S\mid{\bf 0})
, where
{\bf 0}
is the
n\times(m-n)
zero matrix.

Recall that we can define a Poisson bracket
\{-,-\}
on the ambient field
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}(x_{1;t_{0}},\ldots,x_{m;t_{0}})
using the
m\times m
skew-symmetric matrix
\Lambda_{t_{0}}=(\lambda_{ij;t_{0}})
at the rooted vertex
t_{0}
:

\{x_{i;t_{0}},x_{j;t_{0}}\}:=\lambda_{ij;t_{0}}\cdot x_{i;t_{0}}x_{j;t_{0}}.

This Poisson bracket is compatible with the cluster pattern
\mathcal{S}_{X}
, that is, for any cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
of
\mathcal{S}_{X}
, we have

\{x_{i;t},x_{j;t}\}=\lambda_{ij;t}\cdot x_{i;t}x_{j;t},

where
\lambda_{ij;t}
is the
(i,j)
-entry of
\Lambda_{t}
, cf.
[
24
]
,
[
7
, Remark 4.6]
.

Two elements
f_{1},f_{2}
in
\mathbb{F}
are said to be
log-canonical
with respect to the Poisson bracket
\{-,-\}
, if

\{f_{1},f_{2}\}=cf_{1}f_{2}

for some
c\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Clearly, any two cluster variables in the same cluster are log-canonical. In Theorem
4.29
(ii) we will give
the converse result.

Lemma 4.28
.

Let
w
be a vertex of
\mathbb{T}_{n}
and
u
an element in
\mathbb{F}
with the form

u={\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}F(\hat{y}_{1;w},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;w}),

where
F=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}{\bf y}^{{\bf v}}
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with constant term
1
.
If
u
and
x_{k;w}
are log-canonical for some
k\in[1,n]
, then
f_{k}=0
, where
f_{k}
is the highest degree of
y_{k}
in
F
.

Proof.

We know that
\Lambda_{w}=(\lambda_{ij;w})
is the coefficient matrix of
\{-,-\}
with respect to the cluster
{\bf x}_{w}
. Since
(\widetilde{B}_{w},\Lambda_{w})
is a compatible pair of type
S
, we have
\widetilde{B}_{w}^{T}\Lambda_{w}=(S\mid{\bf 0})
. For
i\in[1,n]
and
j\in[1,m]
, we denote by
{\bf e}_{i}
the
i
th column of
I_{n}
and
\widetilde{\bf e}_{j}
the
j
th column of
I_{m}
. We have

\displaystyle\{\hat{y}_{i;w},x_{j;w}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\bf x}_{w}^{\widetilde{B}_{w}{\bf e}_{i}},{\bf x}_{w}^{\widetilde{\bf e}_{j}}\}=\left((\widetilde{B}_{w}{\bf e}_{i})^{T}\Lambda_{w}\widetilde{\bf e}_{j}\right)\hat{y}_{i;w}x_{j;w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bf e}_{i}^{T}(\widetilde{B}_{w})^{T}\Lambda_{w}\widetilde{\bf e}_{j}\right)\hat{y}_{i;w}x_{j;w}=\left({\bf e}_{i}^{T}(S\mid{\bf 0})\widetilde{\bf e}_{j}\right)\hat{y}_{i;w}x_{j;w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}s_{j}\cdot\hat{y}_{j;w}x_{j;w},&i=j;\\
0,&i\neq j.\end{cases}

Now we consider the integer
k\in[1,n]
such that
u
and
x_{k;w}
are log-canonical. For
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have

\{\widehat{\bf y}_{w}^{\bf v},x_{k;w}\}=s_{k}(v_{k}\widehat{\bf y}_{w}^{\bf v})x_{k;w}.

For simplicity, we write
F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w})=F(\hat{y}_{1;w},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;w})
.
We have

\{F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w}),x_{k;w}\}=\{\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}\widehat{\bf y}_{w}^{{\bf v}},x_{k;w}\}=s_{k}(\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}v_{k}\widehat{\bf y}_{w}^{\bf v})x_{k;w}.

Now let us calculate
\{u,x_{k;w}\}=\{{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w}),x_{k;w}\}
.

\displaystyle\{{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w}),x_{k;w}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g},x_{k;w}\}F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w})+{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}\{F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w}),x_{k;w}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bf g}^{T}\Lambda_{w}\widetilde{\bf e}_{k})\cdot{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}\cdot x_{k;w}\cdot F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w})+{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}\cdot s_{k}(\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}v_{k}\widehat{\bf y}_{w}^{\bf v})x_{k;w}.

Since
u
and
x_{k;w}
are log-canonical, there exists some
c\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

\{u,x_{k;w}\}=c\cdot ux_{k;w}=c\cdot{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}\cdot F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w})\cdot x_{k;w}.

Thus we have the following equality in
\mathbb{F}
.

({\bf g}^{T}\Lambda_{w}\widetilde{\bf e}_{k})\cdot{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}\cdot x_{k;w}\cdot F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w})+{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}\cdot s_{k}(\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}v_{k}\widehat{\bf y}_{w}^{\bf v})x_{k;w}=c\cdot{\bf x}_{w}^{\bf g}\cdot F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w})\cdot x_{k;w}.

We get

(4.1)

\displaystyle s_{k}(\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{{\bf v}}v_{k}\widehat{\bf y}_{w}^{\bf v})=(c-{\bf g}^{T}\Lambda_{w}\widetilde{\bf e}_{k})\cdot F(\widehat{\bf y}_{w}).

5.
Dominant sets and oriented exchange graphs

In this section, we fix a Langlands-Poisson triple
(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{Y},\Lambda)
, and let

\mathcal{A}
be the cluster algebra associated to the cluster pattern
\mathcal{S}_{X}
. We fix a rooted vertex

of
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, that is, we fix an initial seed
({\bf x}_{t_{0}},\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}})
of
\mathcal{A}

\tau
-tilting pairs in

-tilting theory and as an replacement of inversion sets in the study of right weak order on Weyl groups. Then we prove the main results on oriented exchange graphs.

5.1.
Oriented exchange graphs are acyclic

Two seeds

and
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
of
\mathcal{A}
are said to be
equivalent
if they are the same up to a relabeling. We denote by
[{\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t}]
the equivalent class of
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
.

The
exchange graph

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
is the graph whose vertices correspond to the seeds (up to seed equivalence) of
\mathcal{A}
and whose edges correspond to seed mutations.

For a seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
of
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by
C_{t}=C_{t}^{t_{0}}
the
C
-matrix of the vertex
t
with respect to the rooted vertex
t_{0}
.

Definition 5.1

(Green mutation and exchange quiver)
.

(i) A seed mutation
\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
in
\mathcal{A}
is called a
green mutation
, if the
k
th column of the
C
-matrix
C_{t}=C_{t}^{t_{0}}
is a non-negative vector. Otherwise, it is called a
red mutation
.

(ii) The
exchange quiver
(or
oriented exchange graph
) of
\mathcal{A}
with initial seed
({\bf x}_{t_{0}},\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}})
is the quiver
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{t_{0}}=\overrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{t_{0}}(\mathcal{A})
whose vertices correspond to the seeds (up to seed equivalence) of
\mathcal{A}
and whose arrows correspond to green mutations.

We remark that if two seeds
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
and
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
are related by once mutation, then either the mutation from
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
to
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
is a green mutation or the mutation from
({\bf x}_{w},\widetilde{B}_{w})
to
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
is a green mutation.

For a vector
{\bf h}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, we denote by

{\bf h}^{\circ}:=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
the
principal part
of
{\bf h}
.

Denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
the set of cluster variables (frozen and unfrozen) of
\mathcal{A}
and by
\mathcal{X}
the set of
unfrozen
cluster variables of
\mathcal{A}
. Given a cluster monomial
u={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}
in
{\bf x}_{t}
, that is,
{\bf h}^{\circ}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we define two subsets of
\mathcal{X}
.

•

The subset
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(u)=\{x_{i;t}\mid i\in[1,n],\;h_{i}\neq 0\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is called the
support
of
u={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}
. We remark that this set only depends on
u
, not on the choice of
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
and
{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
such that
u={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}
.

•

For a vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
and a cluster variable
z
, recall that

(z\mid\mid u)_{F}=F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]+F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{z}^{w}].

The subset

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]=0\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is called the
dominant set
of
u={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}
with respect to vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

Definition 5.2

(Dominant set of a seed)
.

Let
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
be a seed of
\mathcal{A}
. The
dominant set

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]
of
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
with respect to a vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
is defined to be the dominant set of the multiplicity free cluster monomial
u_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}
supported on the unfrozen part of
{\bf x}_{t}
, i.e.,

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]:=\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u_{t})=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u_{t}}^{w}]=0\}.

Clearly, if
[{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}}]=[{\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t}]
, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t^{\prime}]=\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]
. Recall that for a cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
, we denote by
{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}=(x_{1;t},\ldots,x_{n;t})
the unfrozen part of
{\bf x}_{t}=(x_{1;t},\ldots,x_{m;t})
. If we want to view
{\bf x}_{t},{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}
as sets, we will write

[{\bf x}_{t}]:=\{x_{i;t}\mid i\in[1,m]\}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;[{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}]:=\{x_{j;t}\mid j\in[1,n]\}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
u={\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}
be a cluster monomial of
\mathcal{A}
. Then the following statements hold for any vertex
w\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

(i)

[{\bf x}_{w}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)
and in particular,
[{\bf x}_{w}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]
.

(ii)

[{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)
and in particular,
[{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]
.

(iii)

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)=\bigcap_{x\in\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(u)}\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(x)
and in particular,
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(x_{i;t})
.

Proof.

(i) Since
F_{x_{i;w}}^{w}=1
for
i\in[1,n]
, we have
F_{x_{i;w}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]=0
. Thus
x_{i;w}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)
for
i\in[1,n]
, that is,
[{\bf x}_{w}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)
. In particular, if we take
u=u_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}
, we can get

[{\bf x}_{w}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u_{t})=\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t].

(ii) Fix
i\in[1,n]
. By using the vertex
t
to calculate
(x_{i;t}\mid\mid u)_{F}=(x_{i;t}\mid\mid{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h})_{F}
, we see
(x_{i;t}\mid\mid u)_{F}=0
. On the other hand, we know that

(x_{i;t}\mid\mid u)_{F}=F_{x_{i;t}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]+F_{u}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{i;t}}^{w}]

and
each term in this sum is non-negative. Thus we obtain
F_{x_{i;t}}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]=0
, i.e.,
x_{i;t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)
. Since
i
is an arbitrary integer in
[1,n]
, we get
[{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)
. In particular, if we take
u=u_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}
, we can get
[{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u_{t})=\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]
.

(iii) By definition, an unfrozen cluster variable
z\in\mathcal{X}
belongs to
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)
if and only if

F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]=0.

By Proposition
4.19
(ii), we have

F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]=F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{{\bf x}_{t}^{\bf h}}^{w}]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}\cdot F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}].

Since
h_{j}\geq 0
and
F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}]\geq 0
for any
j\in[1,n]
, we know that
F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{w}]=0
if and only if

F_{z}^{w}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{j;t}}^{w}]=0
for any
j\in[1,n]
with
h_{j}\neq 0
, i.e., for any
x_{j;t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(u)
. This is equivalent to say that
z
belongs to
\bigcap_{x\in\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(u)}\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(x)
. In particular, if we take
u=u_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}
, we see that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]=\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u_{t})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(x_{i;t})
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let

and

be two cluster monomials of

such that

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u^{\prime})

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}[t]=\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{w}(u)

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
5.3
(iii).
∎

For a seed
({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
of
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by
G_{t}=G_{t}^{t_{0}}
the
G
-matrix of the vertex
t
with respect to the rooted vertex

and by

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t}:=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}{\bf g}_{1;t}+\ldots+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}{\bf g}_{n;t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

the cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
spanned by the columns of
G_{t}=({\bf g}_{1;t},\ldots,{\bf g}_{n;t})
. We remark that
{\bf g}_{i;t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the principal part of

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{>0}G_{t}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{>0}{\bf g}_{1;t}+\ldots+\mathbb{Q}_{>0}{\bf g}_{n;t}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{n},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{>0}G_{t}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{>0}{\bf g}_{1;t}+\ldots+\mathbb{Z}_{>0}{\bf g}_{n;t}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})=\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
be a green mutation in
\mathcal{A}
.
Then there exists
{\bf v},{\bf v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}
such that
G_{t}{\bf v}-G_{t^{\prime}}{\bf v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
k=1
. We write
G_{t}=({\bf g}_{1},{\bf g}_{2},\ldots,{\bf g}_{n})
, where
{\bf g}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Since
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})=\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})=\mu_{1}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
, the
G
-matrix
G_{t^{\prime}}
has the form
G_{t^{\prime}}=({\bf g}_{1}^{\prime},{\bf g}_{2},\ldots,{\bf g}_{n})
.

By Theorem
3.10
(i), we know that
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t^{\prime}}
are two cones of dimension
n
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and they have a common facet
\mathcal{C}
(cone of dimension
n-1
) given by

\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}{\bf g}_{2}+\ldots+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}{\bf g}_{n}
.

Let
S=diag(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be the type of the compatible pair
(\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}},\Lambda_{t_{0}})
, i.e.,
\widetilde{B}_{t_{0}}^{T}\Lambda_{t_{0}}=(S\mid{\bf 0})
. We know that
S
is a skew-symmetrizer of
B_{t_{0}}
. Let
{\bf c}_{1;t}
be the first column of the
C
-matrix
C_{t}=C_{t}^{t_{0}}
.

By Theorem
3.10
(iii), we have
SC_{t}S^{-1}(G_{t})^{\rm T}=I_{n}
, equivalently,
(G_{t})^{\rm T}(SC_{t}S^{-1})=I_{n}
. So the first column vector
s_{1}^{-1}(S{\bf c}_{1;t})
of
SC_{t}S^{-1}
is an inner normal vector of the facet
\mathcal{C}
of the cone
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t}
. So when we go along the direction
s_{1}^{-1}(S{\bf c}_{1;t})
, we can cross the common facet
\mathcal{C}
from the cone
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t^{\prime}}
to the cone
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t}
.

Now we take a vector
{\bf h}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}G_{t^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t^{\prime}}
, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
such that

{\bf h}:={\bf h}^{\prime}+\lambda(s_{1}^{-1}(S{\bf c}_{1;t}))\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}G_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}G_{t}.

We can take a positive integer
p
large enough such that
{\bf a}:=p{\bf h}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}G_{t}
and set
{\bf a}^{\prime}=p{\bf h}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}G_{t^{\prime}}
. Then we have
p\lambda s_{1}^{-1}(S{\bf c}_{1;t})={\bf a}-{\bf a}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Since
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})=\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
is a green mutation, we have
{\bf c}_{1;t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. So we have
{\bf a}-{\bf a}^{\prime}=p\lambda s_{1}^{-1}(S{\bf c}_{1;t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.

Since
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}G_{t}
and
{\bf a}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}G_{t^{\prime}}
, there exist
{\bf v},{\bf v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}
such that
{\bf a}=G_{t}{\bf v},{\bf a}^{\prime}=G_{t^{\prime}}{\bf v}^{\prime}
. Thus
G_{t}{\bf v}-G_{t^{\prime}}{\bf v}^{\prime}={\bf a}-{\bf a}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
F
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with constant term
1
. If
({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
for some
{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
F[{\bf r}]=0
, then
F[{\bf r}^{\prime}]=0
.

Proof.

Let
{\bf y}^{\bf u}
be a monomial appearing in
F
. We know that
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. Since
({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we have
{\bf u}^{T}({\bf r}^{\prime}-{\bf r})=-{\bf u}^{T}({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})\leq 0
. Since
F[{\bf r}]=0
, we have
{\bf u}^{T}{\bf r}\leq 0
. Thus

{\bf u}^{T}{\bf r}^{\prime}={\bf u}^{T}({\bf r}^{\prime}-{\bf r}+{\bf r})={\bf u}^{T}({\bf r}^{\prime}-{\bf r})+{\bf u}^{T}{\bf r}\leq 0.

Since
F
has constant term
1
and by the definition of
F[{\bf r}^{\prime}]
, we obtain
F[{\bf r}^{\prime}]=0
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

If
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})=\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
is a green mutation, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]

\overrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{t_{0}}=\overrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{t_{0}}(\mathcal{A})

\mathcal{A}

Proof.

Since
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},\widetilde{B}_{t^{\prime}})=\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},\widetilde{B}_{t})
is a green mutation and by Lemma
5.5
, there exists

{\bf v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})^{T},\;{\bf v}^{\prime}=(v_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{n}^{\prime})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}

such that
G_{t}{\bf v}-G_{t^{\prime}}{\bf v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. Set
u=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}^{v_{i}}
and
u^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t^{\prime}}^{v_{i}^{\prime}}
. Notice that we have

({\bf g}_{u}^{t_{0}})^{\circ}=G_{t}{\bf v},\;\;({\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t_{0}})^{\circ}=G_{t^{\prime}}{\bf v}^{\prime}.

Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(u)=[{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}],\;\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(u^{\prime})=[{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}^{\circ}]
and by Corollary
5.4
, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}(u)=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid F_{z}^{t_{0}}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u}^{t_{0}}]=0\}=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid F_{z}^{t_{0}}[SG_{t}{\bf v}]=0\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}(u^{\prime})=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid F_{z}^{t_{0}}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{u^{\prime}}^{t_{0}}]=0\}=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid F_{z}^{t_{0}}[SG_{t^{\prime}}{\bf v}^{\prime}]=0\}.

F_{z}^{t_{0}}[SG_{t}{\bf v}]=0

and by Lemma
5.6
, we get
F_{z}^{t_{0}}[SG_{t^{\prime}}{\bf v}^{\prime}]=0
. This implies that
z\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]
. So we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]
.

In rest part of the proof, we will show
x_{k;t^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]
but
x_{k;t^{\prime}}\notin\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]
. Thus the inclusion
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]
is strict.

By Proposition
5.3
(ii), we have
[{\bf x}_{t}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]
and
[{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}^{\circ}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]
. In particular,

x_{k;t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;x_{k;t^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}].

Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]
, we get
x_{k;t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]
. Then by Proposition
5.3
(iii), we have

x_{k;t}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t^{\prime}]\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}(x_{k;t^{\prime}})

By using the vertex
t_{0}
to calculate the
F
-invariant
(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}

(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}=F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{k;t}^{\prime}}^{t_{0}}]+F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}]=F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}].

On the other hand, if we use the vertex
t
to calculate

(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}=0+F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t}[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t}]=(1+y_{k})[(S\mid{\bf 0}){\bf e}_{k}]=s_{k},

We fix a finite dimensional basic algebra

5.4.
Dominant sets and inversion sets

The aim of this subsection is to point out there exists links between dominant sets and inversion sets so that the readers can have a better understanding on the dominant sets from the viewpoint of inversion sets. We will not go into depth and generality.

Let
C=(c_{ij})_{n\times n}
be a Cartan matrix of finite type and
\Phi
the corresponding root system. We fix a base
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}\subseteq
of
\Phi
.
Denote by
\Phi^{+}
the set of positive roots of
(\Phi,\Delta)
and
\Phi_{\geq-1}=\Phi^{+}\cup\{-\alpha_{1},\ldots,-\alpha_{n}\}

the set of almost positive roots. Let
W
be the Weyl group corresponding to
\Phi
and denote by
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
the simple reflections in
W
corresponding to the simple roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
. There is a natural action of
W
on the root lattice
Q:=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
induced by

s_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\alpha_{j}-c_{ij}\alpha_{i},

where
c_{ij}
is the
(i,j)
-entry of the Cartan matrix

. It is known that
w(\Phi)=\Phi
for any

.

We use
{\bf w}={\bf s}_{i_{1}}\ldots{\bf s}_{i_{r}}
to denote a word in formal symbols
{\bf s}_{1},\ldots,{\bf s}_{n}
and use
w=s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{r}}
to denote the corresponding element in the Weyl group

. The

\ell(w)
of

is the smallest number

such that

for some

. In this case,

of

.

Definition 5.23

(Inversion set and decorated inversion set)
.

Let
{\bf w}={\bf s}_{i_{1}}\ldots{\bf s}_{i_{r}}
be an reduced word of
w\in W
.

(i)

The
inversion set

\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}(w)
of

is defined by

\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}(w):=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r}\}\subseteq\Phi^{+},

where
\beta_{k}=s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{k})
with

.

(ii)

The

of

\widetilde{\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}}(w):=\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}(w)\cup\{-\alpha_{1},\ldots,-\alpha_{n}\}
, which is a subset of almost positive roots.

The inversion set
\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}(w)
is actually the set of positive roots which are sent to negative roots by

, i.e.,

\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}(w)=\{\beta\in\Phi^{+}\mid w^{-1}(\beta)\in-\Phi^{+}\}=\Phi^{+}\cap w(-\Phi^{+}).

Proposition 5.24
.

[
8
, Corollaries 1.4.4, 1.4.5 and 3.1.4]
All the reduced words of
w\in W
give the same inversion set. Different elements of

have different inversion sets. The size of the inversion set

of

.

on

equivalently,

has a reduced word

such that
{\bf v}
is a prefix of some reduced word
{\bf w}
of

. This partial order is known as

on

.

\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}({\bf w}):=\{{\bf s}_{i_{1}},\ldots,{\bf s}_{i_{r}}\},

Proposition 5.30
.

Keep the above setting. Let

be a seed of
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
and
(M_{t},P_{t})
the corresponding
\tau
-tilting pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A

c

W(Q)

\eta:z\mapsto d_{1;z}\alpha_{1}+\ldots+d_{n;z}\alpha_{n}

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]
to the decorated inversion set

\widetilde{\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}}(w_{t})=\operatorname{\mathsf{inv}}(w_{t})\cup\{-\alpha_{1},\ldots,-\alpha_{n}\}

d

Proof.

Under the categorification, the dominant set
\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}^{t_{0}}[t]
is identified with

\operatorname{\mathsf{dom}}[M_{t},P_{t}]=\{\text{indecomposable }\tau\text{-rigid modules in }\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M_{t}\}\cup\{(0,P_{1}),\ldots,(0,P_{n})\}.

z

and
M_{z}
the corresponding indecomposable
\tau
-rigid module in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M_{t}
with

5.29
(iii), we know that

Abstract
. We show that M d M_{d} -approximation-property, M d M_{d} -weak-amenability, and M d M_{d} -weak-Haagerup-property are stable under von Neumann equivalence (hence also Measure equivalence and W*-equivalence). We also show that these properties are inherited from lattices.

Primary 43A22; Secondary 16W22, 37A20, 20F65, 43A07, 46L07

1.
Introduction

Von Neumann equivalence (vNE) was introduced in
[
IPR19
]
on the class of discrete countable groups as a coarser version of Gromov’s Measure equivalence (ME) and W*-equivalence (W*E). In the same paper, the (non-)approximation type properties, amenability, Haagerup property (also known as a-T-menability), Kazhdan’s property (T), and proper proximality, were shown to be stable under vNE. It is followed by some extension works that weak amenability, weak Haagerup property, Haagerup-Kraus’s approximation property
[
Ish21
]
, and exactness
[
Bat23
]
are vNE invariants too. In this paper, we contribute by more invariants, namely
M_{d}
type approximation properties.

One of the motivations to study vNE is its similarity to ME. Many techniques used in measured group theory could be used in vNE, leading to potential applications in W*E. It is known that the groups
PSL_{n}(\Z)
,
n\geq 3
represent distinct ME-classes
[
Fur99
, Theorem A]
, while it is an open problem for W*E. It would be delightful if the ME-rigidity result of
[
Fur99
]
extends to vNE and W*E.

The algebras
M_{d}(G)
were introduced in
[
Pis05
]
by means of studying the similarity problem. Let
G
be a locally compact group and let
d
be an integer greater than 1. The space
M_{d}(G)
consists of the functions
\varphi\in C_{b}(G)
admitting Hilbert spaces
\h_{i}
and bounded maps
\xi_{i}:G\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
with
i=1,...,d
and
\h_{d}=\h_{0}=\C
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})=\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})(1)\quad\text{for all}\quad x_{1},...,x_{d}\in G.

With the norm
\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}=\inf\sup_{x_{1}\in G}\|\xi_{1}(x_{1})\|\cdots\sup_{x_{d}\in G}\|\xi_{d}(x_{d})\|
and pointwise product,
M_{d}(G)
becomes a commutative Banach algebra. When
d=2
, this is the algebra
B_{2}(G)
of Herz-Schur multipliers (also denoted by
M_{0}A(G)
and
M_{cb}(G)
in some literature). The similarity problem asks if all unitarizable groups are amenable (see
[
Pis01
]
for a survey). Unitarizable groups indeed reflect amenable groups. For example, in
[
Boż85
]
the amenability of
G
was characterized by the equality
M_{2}(G)=B(G)
, and in
[
Pis05
]
it is shown that if
G
is unitarizable, then
M_{d}(G)=B(G)
for some
d\geq 2
.

In
[
Ver23
]
, the algebra
M_{d}(G)
was used to define
M_{d}
-approximation-property (resp.
M_{d}
-weak-amenability), which is a refinement of Haagerup Kraus’ approximation property (resp. weak amenability). When
d=2
these properties coincide, and when
d
increases the property gets stronger. As an application of this new approximation type property, it is shown in
[
Ver23
, Theorem 1.2]
that a discrete group satisfying
M_{d}
-approximation-property for all
d\geq 2
has an affirmative answer for the similarity problem.

Weak amenability and approximation property are known to be stable under vNE
[
Ish21
]
. This fact inspired us to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d
be an integer greater than 1.
The following are stable under von Neumann equivalence.

(1)

M_{d}
-approximation-property.

(2)

M_{d}
-weak-amenability and
M_{d}
-Cowling-Haagerup constant
\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)
.

(3)

M_{d}
-weak-Haagerup-property and
M_{d}
-weak-Haagerup constant
\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)
.

We call these three properties
M_{d}
type approximation properties
. The precise definitions are given in Section
2.1
.
The key step of the proof lies in showing that if
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are discrete countable vNE groups, then the induction map
\Phi:\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
introduced in
[
Ish21
]
gives a norm decreasing map
\Phi:M_{d}(\Lambda)\rightarrow M_{d}(\Gamma)
. As we mentioned before, vNE is coarser than both ME and W*E, hence the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
d
be an integer greater than 1.

M_{d}
type approximation properties,
M_{d}
-Cowling-Haagerup constant, and
M_{d}
-weak-Haagerup constant are stable under ME and W*E.

In
[
Ver23
, Theorem 1.3]
, it was shown that countable groups acting properly on a finite-dimensional CAT(0) cube complex are
M_{d}
-weakly amenable. The proof was inspired by Valette’s work involving analytic family of representations
[
Val93
]
. We prove the following theorem using Bożejko-Picardello’s method
[
BP93
]
. Note that Theorem
1.3
is an immediate consequence of
[
Ver23
, Theorem 1.3]
, but the proof ideas differ.

Theorem 1.3
.

A discrete group acting properly on a tree is
M_{d}
-weakly-amenable with
M_{d}
-Cowling-Haagerup constant 1 for any
d\geq 2
.

Some group properties are extendable to von Neumann algebras in a way that a group has the property if and only if its group von Neumann algebra has the property. For example, a discrete group is amenable if and only if its group von Neumann algebra is injective
[
Con76
]
. This kind of characterization is usually observed for discrete groups. In this sense, working on discrete groups is advantageous. The following theorem allows one to work with discrete groups instead of its ambient group when investigating
M_{d}
type approximation properties.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d
be an integer greater than 1.
Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of a locally compact group
G
. If
\Gamma
has
M_{d}
-approximation-property, then so does
G
. Moreover, we have
\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)\leq\Lambda_{CH}(\Gamma,d)
and
\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)\leq\Lambda_{WH}(\Gamma,d)
.

The assertions for
d=2
were done in
[
HK94
, Theorem 2.4]
,
[
Haa16
, Theorem 2.6]
, and
[
Knu16
, Theorem A]
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and useful facts.

2.1.
M_{d}
type approximation properties

Throughout the paper,
d
is always an integer greater than 1,
G
is a locally compact group,
H
is a closed subgroup of
G
, and
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are discrete groups. We denote by
G_{\mathrm{d}}
the discrete realization of
G
. When
E
is a set, we denote by
\chi_{E}
the characteristic function of
E
.

The
Fourier-Stieltjes algebra

B(G)
consists of the coefficients of all unitary representations of
G
. For
\varphi\in B(G)
, its norm is given by
\|\varphi\|_{B}=\inf\|u\|\|v\|
where the infimum runs through all unitary representations
(\pi,\h)
and vectors
u,v\in\h
satisfying
\varphi(x)=\langle\pi(x)u,v\rangle
for all
x\in G
. With this norm and pointwise product,
B(G)
is a commutative Banach algebra.
The
Fourier algebra

A(G)
is a subalgebra of
B(G)
consisting of all coefficients of the left regular representation
\lambda_{G}:G\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(G))
endowed with the norm
\|\varphi\|_{A}=\inf\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}
where the infimum runs through all vectors
u,v\in L^{2}(G)
satisfying
\varphi(x)=\langle\lambda_{G}(x)u,v\rangle
for all
x\in G
. To mention some facts about the Fourier algebra:

(1)

The infimum
\|\varphi\|_{A}=\inf\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}
reaches its minimum.

(2)

A(G)
is a commutative Banach algebra.

(3)

The inclusion
A(G)\rightarrow B(G)
is isometric.

(4)

C_{c}(G)\cap B(G)
is dense in
A(G)
.

(5)

A(G)\subseteq C_{0}(G)
.

See
[
Eym64
]
,
[
KL18
]
, and
[
Run20
, Appendix F]
for more about these algebras.

Definition 2.1

(
[
Pis05
]
)
.

A continuous bounded function
\varphi\in C_{b}(G)
is called a
M_{d}
-multiplier

if there exist Hilbert spaces
\h_{i}
and bounded maps
\xi_{i}:G\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
for
i=1,...,d
such that
\h_{d}=\h_{0}=\C
and

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(g_{1}\cdots g_{d})=\xi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(g_{d})\quad\text{for all}\quad g_{1},...,g_{d}\in G.

Here
\B(\C)
is identified with
\C
.
We denote by
M_{d}(G)
the space of all
M_{d}
-multipliers of
G
endowed with the norm
\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}=\inf\sup_{g_{1}\in G}\|\xi_{1}(g_{1})\|\cdots\sup_{g_{d}\in G}\|\xi_{d}(g_{d})\|
where the infimum runs through all
\h_{i}
’s and
\xi_{i}
’s satisfying (
2.1
).

When
d=2
, it is the algebra of completely bounded Fourier multipliers
M_{cb}A(G)
.
It is obvious that the inclusion
M_{d+1}(G)\subseteq M_{d}(G)
is norm decreasing. The coefficients of a unitary representation are typical elements of
M_{d}(G)
. To see that, take any
\varphi\in B(G)
. There exist a unitary representation
(\pi,\h)
and vectors
u,v\in\h
satisfying
\varphi(x)=\langle\pi(x)u,v\rangle
for all
x\in G
. Then by choosing
\h_{1}=...=\h_{d-1}=\h
and
\xi_{d}(g)(1)=\pi(g)u
,
\xi_{i}(g)=\pi(g)
,
\xi_{1}(g)(w)=\langle\pi(g)w,v\rangle
for all
g\in G
,
w\in\h
, and
i=2,...,d-1
, we get
\varphi(g_{1}\cdots g_{d})=\langle\pi(g_{1}\cdots g_{d})u,v\rangle=\xi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(g_{d})
for all
g_{1},...,g_{d}\in G
and
\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|v\|\|w\|
. Thus we have the following norm decreasing inclusions

\displaystyle A(G)\subseteq B(G)\subseteq M_{d+1}(G)\subseteq M_{d}(G)\subseteq M_{cb}A(G)\subseteq C_{b}(G).

Note that the inclusion
M_{d}(G)\rightarrow M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})
is isometric. Indeed, we have
M_{d}(G)=M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})\cap C(G)
. It is also worth noting that if
H
is a closed subgroup of
G
, then the restriction map
M_{d}(G)\rightarrow M_{d}(H)
is norm decreasing.

Just like
B(G)
and
M_{cb}A(G)
, the space
M_{d}(G)
is in duality with
L^{1}(G)
via

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle=\int_{G}\psi\varphi dx\quad\text{for}\quad\varphi\in M_{d}(G),\psi\in L^{1}(G).

We denote by
X_{d}(G)
the completion of
L^{1}(G)
in the dual space
M_{d}(G)^{*}
. As explained in
[
Pis05
]
, if
G
is a discrete group, then for any
d\geq 2
the Banach space
M_{d}(G)
is isometrically isomorphic to the dual of
X_{d}(G)
. For
d=2
, the same result holds for general locally compact groups
[
DCH85
, Proposition 1.10]
. It seems that the case
d\geq 3
for locally compact group is unclear. Nevertheless, for our use we only need the duality between
M_{d}(G)
and
X_{d}(G)
extended from (
2.2
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and let
d\geq 2
be an integer.

(1)

We say that
G
has
M_{d}
-approximation-property
(
M_{d}
-AP in short) if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in the Fourier algebra
A(G)
such that
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-topology.

(2)

We say that
G
is
M_{d}
-weakly-amenable
(
M_{d}
-WA in short) if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in
A(G)
such that
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}<\infty
and
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-topology. The minimum possible value of
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}
is called
M_{d}
-Cowling-Haagerup
constant and denoted by
\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)
. If
G
is not
M_{d}
-WA, we simply write
\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)=\infty
.

(3)

We say that
G
has
M_{d}
-weak-Haagerup-property
(
M_{d}
-WH in short) if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in
M_{d}(G)\cap C_{0}(G)
such that
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}<\infty
and
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-topology. The minimum possible value of
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}
is called
M_{d}
-weak-Haagerup
constant and denoted by
\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)
. If
G
does not have
M_{d}
-WH, we simply write
\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)=\infty
.

Remark 2.3
.

For
M_{d}
-AP and
M_{d}
-WA, since
C_{c}(G)\cap A(G)
is dense in
A(G)
and the inclusion
A(G)\subseteq M_{d}(G)
is norm decreasing, we can assume that the functions
\varphi_{i}
are compactly supported. It is also worth noting that for a bounded net
(\varphi_{i})
in
M_{d}(G)
, the uniform convergence on compact subsets is stronger than the convergence in
\sigma(M_{d},X_{d})
-topology.

Example 2.4
.

Let us check the simplest example, the infinite cyclic group
G=\Z
. Put
F_{n}=\{1,...,n\}
and
\varphi_{n}=\langle\lambda(\cdot)\dfrac{\chi_{F_{n}}}{n^{1/2}},\dfrac{\chi_{F_{n}}}{n^{1/2}}\rangle\in A(\Z)
. Then

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|\varphi_{n}\|_{A}\leq\left\|\dfrac{\chi_{F_{n}}}{n^{1/2}}\right\|^{2}_{2}=1,

and for any
m\in\Z

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(m)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{n-|m|}{n}&\text{if}\quad|m|<n\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\longrightarrow 1

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,
\Z
is
M_{d}
-WA with
\Lambda_{CH}(\Z,d)=1
. A similar proof works for any locally compact amenable groups.

We have the implications

Amenability

\displaystyle\Rightarrow M_{d}\text{-WA}\Rightarrow M_{d}\text{-AP}\Rightarrow\text{AP},

\displaystyle M_{d}\text{-WA}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow M_{d}\text{-WH},\quad M_{d+1}\text{-AP}\Rightarrow M_{d}\text{-AP},

\displaystyle M_{d+1}\text{-WA}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow M_{d}\text{-WA},\quad M_{d+1}\text{-WH}\Rightarrow M_{d}\text{-WH}.

Since the non-abelian free groups are
M_{d}
-WA
[
Ver23
]
, the first implication is strict for any
d\geq 2
. The second implication is also strict for any
d\geq 2
because of the semidirect product
SL_{2}(\Z)\ltimes\Z^{2}

[
Ver23
, Corollary 4.4]
. The fourth implication is also strict. Indeed, the wreath product
\Gamma=\Z\wr F_{2}
satisfies Haagerup property
[
CSV12
, Theorem 1.1]
. In particular, it is
M_{d}
-WH with
\Lambda_{WH}(\Gamma,d)=1
for all
d\geq 2
. However, it is not weakly amenable
[
Oza12
, Corollary 4]
.
The strictness of the other implications seem unclear.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The proof idea is essentially from
[
BP93
]
.
Suppose that
\Gamma
acts properly on a tree
T
. We identify
T
with its vertex set. Let
d
be the usual distance on
T
.

Fix a base point
v_{0}\in T
and a geodesic ray
\gamma_{0}:\N_{0}\rightarrow T
beginning from
v_{0}
. Every point
v\in T
admits a geodesic ray
\gamma_{v}:\N_{0}\rightarrow T
that begins at
v
and eventually merges with
\gamma_{0}
. For
n\in\N_{0}
, define

\displaystyle E_{n}=\{x\in\Gamma:d(xv_{0},v_{0})=n\},\quad\chi_{n}=\chi_{E_{n}},\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\chi_{n-2i}.

Since the action is proper, these functions are finitely supported. Put
\h_{0}=\h_{d}=\C
and

\displaystyle\h_{1}=...=\h_{d-1}=\overline{\text{span}}\{\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{\gamma_{yv_{0}}(k)}:y\in\Gamma\}\subseteq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\ell^{2}(T).

Define

\displaystyle\xi_{d}(x)(1)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{\gamma_{xv_{0}}(k)}

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(x)\left(\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{\gamma_{yv_{0}}(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{\gamma_{xyv_{0}}(k)}

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(x)\left(\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{\gamma_{yv_{0}}(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\langle\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{\gamma_{xyv_{0}}(k)},\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{\gamma_{v_{0}}(n-k)}\rangle

for
x,y\in\Gamma
and
i=2,...,d-1
. It is easily checked that these are well defined bounded operators with
\|\xi_{d}(x)\|\leq(n+1)^{1/2}
,
\|\xi_{i}(x)\|=1
, and
\|\xi_{1}(x)\|\leq(n+1)^{1/2}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\langle\delta_{\gamma_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d}v_{0}}(k)},\delta_{\gamma_{v_{0}}(n-k)}\rangle=\varphi_{n}(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})

for all
x_{1},...,x_{d}\in\Gamma
. It follows that
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{M_{d}}\leq n+1
and
\|\chi_{n}\|_{M_{d}}=\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{n-2}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|\varphi_{n}\|_{M_{d}}+\|\varphi_{n-2}\|_{M_{d}}\leq 2n
.

On the other hand, the kernels
(u,v)\in T\times T\mapsto\exp(-td(u,v))
are positive definite for any
t>0

[
BP93
, Proposition 3.2]
. In particular, the functions
\rho_{t}:x\in\Gamma\mapsto\exp(-td(xv_{0},v_{0}))
are positive definite. Moreover,
\rho_{t}\rightarrow 1
as
t\rightarrow 0
and
\|\rho_{t}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|\rho_{t}\|_{B}=\rho_{t}(e)=1
. The only problem about the net
(\rho_{t})
is that we do not know if
\rho_{t}
is in the Fourier algebra
A(\Gamma)
. To regulate that, we approximate
\rho_{t}
by the finitely supported functions defined as

\displaystyle\varphi_{n,t}=\rho_{t}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\chi_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\chi_{k}e^{-tk}\quad\text{for}\quad n\in\N_{0}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{t}-\varphi_{n,t}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}2ke^{-tk}\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Von Neumann equivalence (vNE)

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
,
\Lambda
, and
\Gamma
be discrete groups. Let
(M,\Tr)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra endowed with a faithful normal semifinite trace. The group of trace preserving *-automorphisms of
M
is denoted by
\Aut(M,\Tr)
. A group homomorphism
\sigma:G\rightarrow\Aut(M,\Tr)
is called a
G
-action on
(M,\Tr)
. A
fundamental domain
for the action
\sigma
is a projection
p\in M
such that
\sum_{x\in G}\sigma_{x}(p)=1
, where the convergence is with respect to the strong operator topology.
We say that
\Gamma
is a
von Neumann equivalence subgroup (or
vNE
-subgroup)
of
\Lambda
if there exists an action

\sigma:\Gamma\times\Lambda\rightarrow\Aut(M,\Tr)
and fundamental domains
p
and
q
for each of
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
actions, respectively, such that the trace
\Tr(p)
is finite. Furthermore, if the trace
\Tr(q)
is finite, we say that
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
are von Neumann equivalent (vNE) and write
\Lambda\sim_{vNE}\Gamma
.

Example 2.6
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\Lambda
. Then
\Gamma
is a vNE-subgroup of
\Lambda
. To see that, define
M=\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)
and
\Tr(f)=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}f(s)
for
f\in M_{+}
. Then we have the trace preserving action
\sigma:\Gamma\times\Lambda\rightarrow\Aut(M,\Tr)
,
\sigma_{\gamma,s}(f)(x)=f(s^{-1}x\gamma)
for all
s,x\in\Lambda
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
, and
f\in M
. The projection
\delta_{e}\in M
is a finite trace fundamental domain for
\Lambda
-action. If
S\subseteq\Lambda
is a set of representatives of the left
\Gamma
-cosets, the projection
\chi_{S}\in M
is a fundamental domain for
\Gamma
-action.

The vNE is originated from Measure equivalence (ME) and W*-equivalence (W*E). The following two examples show their connection.

Example 2.7
.

Recall that
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are said ME if there is a standard measure space
(X,m)
and commuting, measure preserving, free actions of
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
on
(X,m)
with finite measure fundamental domains
\Omega_{\Gamma}
and
\Omega_{\Lambda}
, respectively. In this case,
\Gamma\times\Lambda
acts on
(M,\Tr)=(L^{\infty}(X,m),\int dm)
, and the characteristic functions
\chi_{\Omega_{\Gamma}},\chi_{\Omega_{\Lambda}}\in M
are finite trace fundamental domains for
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
actions, respectively. Thus
\Lambda\sim_{vNE}\Gamma
.

Example 2.8
.

Recall that
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are W*E if their group von Neumann algebras
L(\Gamma)
and
L(\Lambda)
are *-isomorphic. In this case, by the standard representation argument, the Hilbert spaces
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
,
L^{2}(L(\Gamma))
,
L^{2}(L(\Lambda))
, and
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
are isomorphic, and
\Gamma\times\Lambda
acts on
M=\B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))\cong\B(\ell^{2}(\Lambda))
by left and right regular representations. The projection onto the subspace
\C\delta_{e}
becomes a common fundamental domain with finite trace for both
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
actions, hence
\Lambda\sim_{vNE}\Gamma
.

We need the following fact from
[
IPR19
,
Ish21
]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose that we have a trace preserving action
\sigma:G\rightarrow\Aut(M,\Tr)
. If
p\in M
is a fundamental domain for
\sigma
, then the map
\theta_{p}:\ell^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow M
,
\theta_{p}(f)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\sigma_{x^{-1}}(p)
is a normal faithful *-representation.

3.
Main section

3.1.
Induction map

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.1
. The main tool is the induction map derived from vNE
[
Ish21
, Lemma 3.1]
.
We explain the construction now. Suppose that we have an action
\sigma:\Gamma\times\Lambda\rightarrow\Aut(M,\Tr)
that establishes
\Gamma
as a vNE-subgroup of
\Lambda
. Let
p
and
q
be fundamental domains for
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
actions, respectively. We assume that the trace is normalized so that
\Tr(p)=1
. The induction map is defined as

\displaystyle\Phi:\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)\mapsto\widehat{\varphi}\in\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma),\quad\widehat{\varphi}(\gamma)=\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\theta_{p}(\varphi))\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,

where
\theta_{p}
is as in Lemma
2.9
.
In
[
Ish21
, Lemma 3.1]
, it was shown that the restriction
\Phi:M_{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow M_{2}(\Gamma)
of the above mapping is well defined and norm decreasing. The following lemma extends the latter result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer.
If
\varphi\in M_{d}(\Lambda)
, then
\widehat{\varphi}\in M_{d}(\Gamma)
and
\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in M_{d}(\Lambda)
. There exist Hilbert spaces
\h_{1},...,\h_{d}
and bounded maps
\xi_{i}:\Lambda\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
for
i=1,...,d
such that
\h_{d}=\h_{0}=\C
and

\displaystyle\varphi(s_{1}\cdots s_{d})=\xi_{1}(s_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(s_{d})\quad\text{for all}\quad s_{1},...,s_{d}\in\Lambda.

We assume that
d
is even so that we can write
d=2k
for some integer
k
. The odd case is done similarly.
Define the Hilbert spaces

\displaystyle\hh_{0}

\displaystyle=\hh_{d}=\C,

\displaystyle\hh_{2i}

\displaystyle=(L^{2}(M,\Tr)p)\overline{\otimes}\h_{2i}\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,...,k-1,

\displaystyle\hh_{2i+1}

\displaystyle=(pL^{2}(M,\Tr))\overline{\otimes}\h_{2i+1}\quad\text{for}\quad i=0,...,k-1.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, define the operators
\widehat{\xi}_{j}(\gamma):\hh_{j}\rightarrow\hh_{j-1}
for
j=1,...,d
as

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi}_{d}(\gamma)(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}p\sigma_{\gamma,s}(p)\otimes\xi_{d}(s)(1),

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi}_{2i}(\gamma)(xp\otimes u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}p\sigma_{\gamma,s}(xp)\otimes\xi_{2i}(s)(u),

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi}_{2i+1}(\gamma)(px\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sigma_{\gamma,s}(px)p\otimes\xi_{2i+1}(s)(v),

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi}_{1}(\gamma)(px\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\Tr\left(\sigma_{\gamma,s}(px)p\right)\xi_{1}(s)(w)

for all
x\in L^{2}(M,\Tr)
,
u\in\h_{2i}
,
v\in\h_{2i+1}
, and
w\in\h_{1}
. We claim that these are well defined bounded operators. Let us check
\widehat{\xi}_{2i}(\gamma)
. Take any
x=xp\in L^{2}(M,\Tr)p
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since
p
is a finite trace fundamental domain for
\Lambda
-action, the vectors
\{p\sigma_{\gamma,s}(xp):s\in\Lambda\}
are pairwise orthogonal and summable in
pL^{2}(M,\Tr)
. The same thing happens for
\{p\sigma_{\gamma,s}(x)\otimes\xi_{2i}(s)(v):s\in\Lambda\}\subseteq\hh_{2i}
because the operators
\{\xi_{2i}(s):s\in\Lambda\}
are uniformly bounded. Now, take any finite sum

z=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}\otimes v_{j}\in\hh_{2i}
where
x_{j}=x_{j}p\in L^{2}(M,\Tr)p
are pairwise orthogonal. Then, noting that the elements
\{p\sigma_{\gamma,s}(x_{j})\otimes\xi_{2i}(s)(v_{j}):s\in\Lambda,j=1,...,N\}
are pairwise orthogonal, we get

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\xi}_{2i}(\gamma)(z)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\|p\sigma_{\gamma,s}(x_{j})\|^{2}\|\xi_{2i}(s)(v_{j})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{s\in\Lambda}\|\xi_{2i}(s)\|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\Tr(\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p)\sigma_{\gamma}(x_{j}x_{j}^{*}))\|v_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in\Lambda}\|\xi_{2i}(s)\|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}\|^{2}\|v_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in\Lambda}\|\xi_{2i}(s)\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}.

By density, it follows that
\widehat{\xi}_{2i}(\gamma)
is a well defined bounded operator with
\|\widehat{\xi}_{2i}(\gamma)\|\leq\sup_{s\in\Lambda}\|\xi_{2i}(s)\|
. A similar argument works for
\widehat{\xi}_{d}(\gamma)
and
\widehat{\xi}_{2i+1}(\gamma)
. The operator
\widehat{\xi}_{1}(\gamma)
can be seen as the composition

\displaystyle(pL^{2}(M,\Tr))\overline{\otimes}\h_{1}\rightarrow(L^{2}(M,\Tr)p)\overline{\otimes}\h_{0}\cong L^{2}(M,\Tr)p\xrightarrow{\Tr}\C,

where the first mapping defined by

\displaystyle px\otimes w\mapsto\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sigma_{\gamma,s}(px)p\otimes\xi_{1}(s)(w)\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in L^{2}(M,\Tr)\quad\text{and}\quad w\in\h_{1}

is bounded by the previous argument. The trace
\Tr
is contracting when restricted to
L^{2}(M,\Tr)p
because
\Tr(p)=1
. Thus we have
\|\widehat{\xi_{1}}(\gamma)\|\leq\sup_{s\in\Lambda}\|\xi_{1}(s)\|
.

Our next claim is that

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d})=\widehat{\xi}_{1}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\widehat{\xi}_{d}(\gamma_{d})\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{d}\in\Gamma.

Should this be true, we get
\widehat{\varphi}\in M_{d}(\Gamma)
and
\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}
.
Observe that

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi}_{1}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\widehat{\xi}_{d}(\gamma_{d})=\widehat{\xi}_{1}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\widehat{\xi}_{d-1}(\gamma_{d-1})\sum_{s_{d}\in\Lambda}p\sigma_{\gamma_{d},s_{d}}(p)\otimes\xi_{d}(s_{d})

\displaystyle=\widehat{\xi}_{1}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\widehat{\xi}_{d-2}(\gamma_{d-2})\sum_{s_{d}\in\Lambda}\sum_{s_{d-1}\in\Lambda}\sigma_{\gamma_{d-1},s_{d-1}}(p)\sigma_{\gamma_{d-1}\gamma_{d},s_{d-1}s_{d}}(p)p\otimes\xi_{d-1}(s_{d-1})\xi_{d}(s_{d})

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{d}\in\Lambda}...\sum_{s_{1}\in\Lambda}\Tr\left(\Pi\left(\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d},s_{1}\cdots s_{d}}(p),...,\sigma_{\gamma_{1},s_{1}}(p),p\right)\right)\xi_{1}(s_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(s_{d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{d}\in\Lambda}...\sum_{s_{1}\in\Lambda}\Tr\left(\Pi\left(\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d},s_{1}\cdots s_{d}}(p),...,\sigma_{\gamma_{1},s_{1}}(p),p\right)\right)\varphi(s_{1}\cdots s_{d})

for
\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{d}\in\Gamma
, where
\Pi:M^{d+1}\rightarrow M
is the multiplication map defined by

\displaystyle\Pi(a_{1},...,a_{d+1})=a_{d}a_{d-2}\cdots a_{2}a_{1}a_{3}\cdots a_{d+1}\quad\text{for all}\quad a_{1},...,a_{d+1}\in M.

By the change of variable
s_{1}\mapsto s_{1}s_{d}^{-1}\cdots s_{2}^{-1}

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi}_{1}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\widehat{\xi}_{d}(\gamma_{d})=

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{d}\in\Lambda}...\sum_{s_{1}\in\Lambda}\Tr\left(\Pi\left(\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d},s_{1}}(p),...,\sigma_{\gamma_{1},s_{1}s_{d}^{-1}\cdots s_{2}^{-1}}(p),p\right)\right)\varphi(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{d}\in\Lambda}...\sum_{s_{2}\in\Lambda}\Tr\left(\Pi\left(\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d}}(p),...,\sigma_{\gamma_{1},s_{d}^{-1}\cdots s_{2}^{-1}}(p),\left[\sum_{s_{1}\in\Lambda}\varphi(s_{1})\sigma_{s_{1}^{-1}}(p)\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{d}\in\Lambda}...\sum_{s_{2}\in\Lambda}\Tr\left(\Pi\left(\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d}}(p),...,\sigma_{\gamma_{1},s_{d}^{-1}\cdots s_{2}^{-1}}(p),\theta_{p}(\varphi)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d}}(p)\theta_{p}(\varphi)\right)=\widehat{\varphi}(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\varphi\in A(\Lambda)
, then
\widehat{\varphi}\in A(\Gamma)
with
\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{A(\Gamma)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{A(\Lambda)}
.

Proof.

This was explained in the proof of
[
Ish21
, Theorem 1.1]
using
[
IPR19
, Proposition 4.2]
. We reproduce the proof for the convenience of the readers.

Recall that the Koopman representation associated to the action
\sigma:\Gamma\rightarrow\Aut(M,\Tr)
is the unitary representation given by

\displaystyle\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(M,\Tr)),\quad\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}(\gamma)x=\sigma_{\gamma}(x)\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma\quad\text{and}\quad x\in\mathfrak{n_{\Tr}},

where
\mathfrak{n}_{\Tr}=\{x\in M:\Tr(x^{*}x)<\infty\}
is dense in
L^{2}(M,\Tr)
.

If
\varphi\in A(\Lambda)
, there exist vectors
\xi,\eta\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
such that
\varphi(s)=\langle\lambda_{\Lambda}(s)\xi,\eta\rangle
for all
s\in\Lambda
and
\|\varphi\|_{A(\Lambda)}=\|\xi\|_{2}\|\eta\|_{2}
. Define the vectors

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\xi(s)\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p)\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\eta}=\sum_{t\in\Lambda}\eta(t)\sigma_{t}^{-1}(p)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(M,\Tr).

The calculation

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\varphi(s)\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\Lambda}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\xi(s^{-1}t)\overline{\eta(t)}\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\Lambda}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\xi(s)\overline{\eta(t)}\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\sigma_{st^{-1}}(p))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\Lambda}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\xi(s)\overline{\eta(t)}\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma,s^{-1}}(p)\sigma_{t^{-1}}(p))

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}(\gamma)\hat{\xi},\hat{\eta}\rangle

shows that
\widehat{\varphi}\in B(\Gamma)
and
\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{B(\Gamma)}\leq\|\hat{\xi}\|\|\hat{\eta}\|=\|\xi\|_{2}\|\eta\|_{2}=\|\varphi\|_{A(\Lambda)}
. It remains to show that
\widehat{\varphi}\in A(\Gamma)
.

We claim that all coefficients of the Koopman representation
\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}
are in
A(\Gamma)
. Let
q\in M
be a fundamental domain for
\Gamma
-action on
M
. Denote by
M^{\Gamma}
the von Neumann subalgebra of
M
consisting of all
\Gamma
-fixed elements. The mapping
\tau:x\in M^{\Gamma}_{+}\mapsto\Tr(qx)
defines a faithful normal semifinite trace on
M^{\Gamma}
. The operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{q}:\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\overline{\otimes}L^{2}(M^{\Gamma},\tau)\rightarrow L^{2}(M,\Tr),\quad\mathcal{F}_{q}(\delta_{\gamma}\otimes x)=\sigma_{\gamma}(q)x

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tau}
defines a unitary operator. See
[
IPR19
, Proposition 4.2]
for detail of these facts. Observe that

\displaystyle\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}(\alpha)(\mathcal{F}_{q}(\delta_{\gamma}\otimes x))

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\alpha}(\sigma_{\gamma}(q)x)=\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}(q)x=\mathcal{F}_{q}(\delta_{\alpha\gamma}\otimes x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{q}((\lambda_{\Gamma}\otimes id)(\alpha)(\delta_{\gamma}\otimes x))

for all
\alpha,\gamma\in\Gamma
and
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tau}\subseteq M^{\Gamma}
. In other words, the unitary operator
\mathcal{F}_{q}
intertwines the amplification
\lambda_{\Gamma}\otimes id
of the left regular representation and the Koopman representation
\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}
. It follows that every coefficient of
\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}
is a coefficient of
(\lambda_{\Gamma}\otimes id,\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\overline{\otimes}L^{2}(M^{\Gamma},\tau))
. Let
\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\overline{\otimes}L^{2}(M^{\Gamma},\tau)
be any vectors, and consider the function
\psi(\gamma)=\langle(\lambda_{\Gamma}\otimes id)(\gamma)\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}\rangle
. Let
\{v_{i}:i\in I\}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(M^{\Gamma},\tau)
. Then we can write

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma,i\in I}a_{\alpha,i}\delta_{\alpha}\otimes v_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta^{\prime}=\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma,j\in I}b_{\beta,j}\delta_{\beta}\otimes v_{j}

for some numbers
a_{\alpha,i},b_{\beta,j}\in\C
such that

\displaystyle\|\xi^{\prime}\|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma,i\in I}|a_{\alpha,i}|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\eta^{\prime}\|=\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma,j\in I}|b_{\beta,j}|^{2}.

Developing
\psi(\gamma)=\langle(\lambda_{\Gamma}\otimes id)(\gamma)\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}\rangle
further, we get

\displaystyle\psi(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma,i\in I}\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma,j\in I}a_{\alpha,i}b_{\beta,j}\langle\lambda_{\Gamma}(\gamma)\delta_{\alpha},\delta_{\beta}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\lambda_{\Gamma}(\gamma)(\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}a_{\alpha,i}\delta_{\alpha}),(\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma}b_{\beta,i}\delta_{\beta})\rangle.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, one can see that the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\Big\|\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}a_{\alpha,i}\delta_{\alpha}\Big\|_{2}\Big\|\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma}b_{\beta,i}\delta_{\beta}\Big\|_{2}

is convergent and bounded by
\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{2}\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{2}
. Thus
\psi\in A(\Gamma)
. In particular,
\widehat{\varphi}\in A(\Gamma)
and

\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{A(\Gamma)}=\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{B(\Gamma)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{A(\Lambda)}.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The induction map
\Phi:M_{d}(\Lambda)\rightarrow M_{d}(\Gamma)
is
\sigma(M_{d},X_{d})
-
\sigma(M_{d},X_{d})
-continuous.

Proof.

The case
d=2
was done in
[
Ish21
, Proposition 3.2.(b)]
. The same argument works to see the general case.

On one hand, the induction map
\Phi:\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
is normal. Indeed, if
(\varphi_{i})
is a bounded net of positive functions in
\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)
with
\varphi_{\infty}=\sup_{i}\varphi_{i}\in\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)
, then for any positive
f\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
we have

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\varphi}_{i},f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}f(\gamma)\varphi_{i}(s)\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p))

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\varphi_{i}(s)\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p))

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi_{i},g\rangle\rightarrow\langle\varphi_{\infty},g\rangle=\langle\widehat{\varphi}_{\infty},f\rangle,

where we note that the function

g:s\in\Lambda\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p))
is in
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
since

\displaystyle\|g\|_{1}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}f(\gamma)\Tr(\sigma_{\gamma}(p)\sigma_{s}^{-1}(p))=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)=\|f\|_{1}.

Thus its dual map defines a contraction
\Phi^{*}|_{\ell^{1}(\Gamma)}:\ell^{1}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\ell^{1}(\Lambda)
.

On the other hand, we have a contraction
\Phi|_{M_{d}(\Lambda)}:M_{d}(\Lambda)\rightarrow M_{d}(\Gamma)
by Lemma
3.1
. It follows that the dual map
(\Phi|_{M_{d}(\Lambda)})^{*}:X_{d}(\Gamma)\rightarrow M_{d}(\Lambda)^{*}
is also a contraction and coincides with
\Phi^{*}
on
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
. Thus by continuity and density argument, we have
\Phi^{*}:X_{d}(\Gamma)\rightarrow X_{d}(\Lambda)
well defined and contracting. Now, if
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow\varphi_{\infty}
in
\sigma(M_{d}(\Lambda),X_{d}(\Lambda))
, then for any
f\in X_{d}(\Gamma)
we have

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\varphi}_{i},f\rangle=\langle\varphi_{i},\Phi^{*}(f)\rangle\rightarrow\langle\varphi_{\infty},\Phi^{*}(f)\rangle=\langle\widehat{\varphi}_{\infty},f\rangle,

showing that the map
\Phi:M_{d}(\Lambda)\rightarrow M_{d}(\Gamma)
is
\sigma(M_{d},X_{d})
-
\sigma(M_{d},X_{d})
-continuous.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(
[
Ish21
, Lemma 3.1.(b)]
)
.

If
\varphi\in c_{0}(\Lambda)
, then
\widehat{\varphi}\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
.

This lemma was presented in
[
Ish21
, Lemma 3.1.(b)]
under the condition that the
\Gamma
-action on
(M,\Tr)
is mixing, i.e. all coefficients of the Koopman representation
\sigma^{0}_{\Gamma}
vanish at infinity. This condition follows from the assumption that the
\Gamma
-action admits a fundamental domain. Indeed, as we have seen in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, the coefficients of the Koopman representation are in the Fourier algebra, hence vanish at infinity.
We are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{i})
be a net of functions in
M_{d}(\Lambda)
converging to 1 in
\sigma(M_{d}(\Lambda),X_{d}(\Lambda))
-topology. By Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
, the net
(\widehat{\varphi}_{i})
is in
M_{d}(\Gamma)
with
\sup_{i}\|\widehat{\varphi}_{i}\|_{M_{d}(\Gamma)}\leq\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}(\Lambda)}
and converges to 1 in
\sigma(M_{d}(\Gamma),X_{d}(\Gamma))
-topology. By Lemma
3.2
, if the net
(\varphi_{i})
is in
A(\Lambda)
, then the net
(\widehat{\varphi}_{i})
is in
A(\Gamma)
. It follows that if
\Lambda
has
M_{d}
-AP, then so does
\Gamma
, and that
\Lambda_{WA}(\Gamma,d)\leq\Lambda_{WA}(\Lambda,d)
. The converse statements are also true by symmetry. Similarly, Lemma
3.4
shows that
\Lambda_{WH}(\Gamma,d)=\Lambda_{WH}(\Lambda,d)
. This completes the proof.
∎

The above proof works to see that

3.2.
Inheritance from lattices

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. Although this fact implicitly follows from the argument used in the previous subsection and some known similar results on weak amenability, approximation property, and weak Haagerup property
[
Haa16
,
HK94
,
Knu16
]
, we provide a complete explanation for convenience.

Let us recall first how the case
d=2
was handled. A discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
is called a
lattice
if there is a finite measure subset
\Omega\subseteq G
such that
G=\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\Omega\gamma
. In this case, there exist measurable maps
\gamma:G\rightarrow\Gamma
and
\omega:G\rightarrow\Omega
such that every element
g\in G
can be uniquely written as
g=\omega(g)\gamma(g)
. The induction map used in
[
Haa16
, Section 2]
is defined as

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}:\varphi\in M_{2}(\Gamma)\mapsto\widetilde{\varphi}\in M_{2}(G),\quad\widetilde{\varphi}=\chi_{\Omega}*(\varphi\mu_{\Gamma})*\chi_{\Omega}^{*},

where
\mu_{\Gamma}
is the measure on
G
that counts
\Gamma
-elements, and
\chi_{\Omega}
is the characteristic functions of
\Omega
. In other terms, we can write

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}(g)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\gamma(gw))dw\quad\text{for all}\quad g\in G.

It is a crucial fact that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a continuous bounded function on
G
. Moreover, we have the following.

(1)

[
Haa16
, Lemma 2.1]
If
\varphi\in A(\Gamma)
, then
\widetilde{\varphi}\in A(G)
.

(2)

[
Knu16
, Lemma 5.13]
If
\varphi\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
, then
\widetilde{\varphi}\in C_{0}(G)
.

(3)

[
HK94
, Proof of Lemma 1.16]
The dual map
\Phi_{1}^{*}:M_{2}(G)^{*}\rightarrow M_{2}(\Gamma)^{*}
restricts to a well defined map
\Phi_{1}^{*}:L^{1}(G)\rightarrow\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
. In particular,
\Phi_{1}^{*}:X_{2}(G)\rightarrow X_{2}(\Gamma)
is well defined and norm decreasing, and
\Phi_{1}
is
\sigma(M_{2},X_{2})
-
\sigma(M_{2},X_{2})
-continuous.

On the other hand, putting
(M,\Tr)=(L^{\infty}(G),\int dx)
, we have the action
\sigma:G\times\Gamma\rightarrow\Aut(M,\Tr)
given by
\sigma_{(g,\alpha)}f(x)=f(g^{-1}x\alpha)
for
f\in M
,
g,x\in G
, and
\alpha\in\Gamma
. The projection
p=\chi_{\Omega}
is a finite trace fundamental domain for
\Gamma
-action. This data is enough to construct the induction map

\displaystyle\Phi:M_{d}(\Gamma)\rightarrow M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}}),\quad\widehat{\varphi}(g)=\Tr(\sigma_{g}(p)\theta_{p}(\varphi))\quad\text{for all}\quad g\in G,

where
G_{\mathrm{d}}
is the discrete realization of
G
.
It turns out that
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi
coincide. Indeed,

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(g)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\sigma_{g}(p)\theta_{p}(\varphi))=\int_{G}\chi_{\Omega}(g^{-1}x)\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\varphi(\alpha)\chi_{\Omega}(x\alpha^{-1})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\varphi(\alpha)\chi_{\Omega}(gx\alpha^{-1})dx=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\gamma(gw))dw=\widetilde{\varphi}(g)

for all
g\in G
.
It follows that the map
\Phi:M_{d}(\Gamma)\rightarrow M_{d}(G)
is well defined and norm decreasing. The same proof as (3) works to see that
\Phi
is
\sigma(M_{d},X_{d})
-
\sigma(M_{d},X_{d})
-continuous. Now, Theorem
1.4
is immediate.

3.3.
Some questions

We end the paper with naturally arising questions.

(1)

Is unitarizability invariant under vNE? Does unitarizability pass to vNE-subgroups?

(2)

Is it possible to define
M_{d}
type approximation properties for
C^{*}
-algebras, operator spaces, and von Neumann algebras in a compatible way with the current definition for discrete groups?

The first question follows from the similarity problem because amenability pass to vNE-subgroups. The second question is quite promising firstly because if two groups have isomorphic group von Neumann algebras, then they share the same value for
M_{d}
type approximation properties by Theorem
1.1
, and secondly because the case
d=2
is done in
[
Haa16
,
HK94
,
Knu16
]
.

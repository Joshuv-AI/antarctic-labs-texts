Tail Bounds for Tensor-valued Random Process
Shih Yu Chang

Abstract
To consider a high-dimensional random process, we propose a notion about stochastic tensor-valued random process (TRP). In this work, we first attempt to apply a generic chaining method to derive tail bounds for all p p -th moments of the supremum of TRPs. We first establish tail bounds for suprema of processes with an exponential tail, and further derive tail bounds for suprema of processes with arbitrary number of exponential tails. We apply these bounds to high-dimensional compressed sensing and empirical process characterizations.



Index terms—
tensor, random process, generic chaining, tail bounds, empirical processes, compressed sending.

1
Introduction

Generic chaining is a powerful probabilistics tool developed by Talagrand to determine the expected value of the supremum of a real-valued stochastic process. This approach stems from the traditional chaining argument invented by Kolmogorov and the majorizing measures method invented by Dudley, Fernique and Talagrand
[
1
]
. Generic chaining applies
\gamma
-functionals, a quantitative measure of the metric complexity of the index set of the process, to estimate the expectation of the supremum of a random process. Such estimations are known to be sharp at several special cases, e.g., the celebrated majorizing measure theorem provides the sharpness for Gaussian random processes
[
2
]
. The applications of generic chaining can be discovered at signal processing, statistics, and harmonic analysis
[
1
]
. Besides knowing the upper bound for the expected supremum of a random process, we wish to know how probable it is that the supremum of the random process exceeds the expectation of the upper bound. To answer this question, a generic chaining bound has to provide other tail bounds for the deviation of the supremum with respect to the expected supremum of a random process. Such concentration studies of random variables have generated many research results about tail bounds estimations and produced various applications in various fields, see
[
3
]
.

Tensors, high-dimensional data, have been applied to different domains in science and engineering, e.g., theoretical physics
[
4
]
, signal processing
[
5
]
, machine learning
[
6
]
, etc. Concentration of random matrices (order 2 tensors) has begun its study since 2010
[
7
]
, and the generalization of concentration theory to random tensors with arbitrary order can be found at following works: non-independent random tensors by expander mathod
[
8
]
, majorization approach
[
9
]
, T-product tensors
[
10
,
11
]
, and others
[
12
,
13
]
. In this work, we first attempt to apply a generic chaining method to derive tail bounds for all
p
-th moments of the supremum of a stochastic tensor-valued random process (TRP). Our work follows the same generic chaining approach given by
[
14
]
, however, we extend this work to random tensors settings, instead of random variables, and consider the mixed TRP with an arbitrary number of exponential tails with different exponents.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we will define tensor-valued random process (TRP) and review notions about tensors briefly. We then consider two types of tail bounds, exponential tail and mixed tail, based on generic chaining in Section
3
. The application of exponential tail bound to compressed sensing is discussed in Section
4
. The application of mixed tail bound to the empirical process is presented in Section
5
.

On the other hand, for tensors

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}\star_{N}\mathcal{B})_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},k_{1}\times\cdots\times k_{L}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}

(2)

Note that we will often abbreviate a tensor product

This tensor product will be reduced to the standard matrix multiplication as

). In analogy to matrix analysis, we define some basic tensors and elementary tensor operations as follows.

Definition 1

A tensor whose entries are all zero is called a

, denoted by

.

Definition 2

An

\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{I})_{i_{1}\times\cdots\times i_{N}\times j_{1}\times\cdots\times j_{N}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{i_{k},j_{k}},

In order to define

(or

Definition 3

Given a tensor
\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}(a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{H})_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N},i_{1},\cdots,i_{M}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\overline{a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}},

.

Definition 5

Given a

\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}(a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}})\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}

We also list other crucial tensor operations here. The

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{j}\leq I_{j},\hskip 1.42271ptj\in[N]}\mathcal{A}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},i_{1},\cdots,i_{M}}.

The

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}\right).

(8)

According to Eq. (

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}\rangle}.

In later sections, we use

Below, we will define tensor-valued processes and recall important notions about generic chaining. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a normed linear space made by the collection of tensors with the same dimensions and let

be a metric space. We assume that the cardinality

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{t}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}

(10)

\displaystyle(t,\omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{t}(\omega).

The diameter of
T
with respect to

is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta_{d}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{s,t\in T}d(s,t).

(11)

We define that an
\mathfrak{X}
-valued process
(\mathcal{X}_{t})_{t\in T}
is exponential tail with parameter
\beta
under the metric space
(T,d)
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{\alpha}\geq ud(s,t))\leq 2\exp(-u^{\beta}),

(12)

where
\beta\geq 0
.

A sequence of set
\mathfrak{T}=(T_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of subsets of
T
is called admissible if
|T_{0}|=1
and
|T_{n}|\leq 2^{2^{n}}
for all
n\geq 1
. For any
0<\beta<\infty
, the
\gamma_{\beta}
functional of
(T,d)
is defined by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\beta}(T,d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\mathfrak{T}}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n/\beta}d(t,T_{n}),

(13)

where the infimum is taken over all admissible sequences and
d(t,T_{n})
is obtained by
\inf\limits_{s\in T_{n}}d(t,s)
.

3
Suprema and Tail Estimates for TRP

In this section, we will consider two types of tail bound analysis based on generic chaining. The first is to establish tail bounds for suprema of processes with an exponential tail, see Section
3.1
. The second is to establish tail bounds for suprema of processes with mixed exponential tails, see Section

3.1
Tail Bounds for Suprema of Processes with an Exponential Tail

In order to consider
p
-th moments, we have to define the following truncated
\gamma_{\beta}
functional. For a given
1\leq p<\infty
, we use the symbol
n^{\prime}
to represent the integer obtained by
n^{\prime}=\lfloor\log_{2}(p)\rfloor
, where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
represents the integer part. We define the truncated
\gamma_{\beta}
functional with respect to the power
p
as

\displaystyle\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\mathfrak{T}}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\sum\limits_{n=n^{\prime}}^{\infty}2^{n/\beta}d(t,T_{n}),

(14)

where
n^{\prime}=\lfloor\log_{2}(p)\rfloor
. By comparing definitions given by Eq. (
14
) and Eq. (
13
), we have
\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d)\leq\gamma_{\beta}(T,d)
for all
1leqp<\infty
and
\gamma_{\beta,1}(T,d)=\gamma_{\beta}(T,d)
. The sequence
\mathfrak{T}
that achieves the infimum is named as
optimal sequence
.

We have to prepare the following three lemmas which will be used in every generic chaining argument later.

Lemma 1

Given
1\leq p<\infty
, set
n^{\prime}=\lfloor\log_{2}(p)\rfloor
and let
(\mathcal{X}_{t})_{t\in T}
be a collection of random tensors. If
|T|\leq 2^{2^{n^{\prime}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq 2\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(15)

Proof:

Because
|T|\leq 2^{2^{n^{\prime}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{t\in T}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\leq|T|\sup\limits_{t\in T}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\leq 2^{p}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}.

(16)

\hfill\Box

Lemma 2

Given
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<\beta\infty
,
u\geq 2^{1/\beta}
, and set
n^{\prime}=\lfloor\log_{2}(p)\rfloor
. For every
n>n^{\prime}
, let
(E_{j,n})_{j\in J_{n}}
be a collection of events satisfying

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(E_{j,n})\leq 2\exp(-2^{n}u^{\beta}),

(17)

where
j\in J_{n}
. If
|J_{n}|\leq 2^{2^{n+1}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\bigcup\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\bigcup\limits_{j\in J_{n}}E_{j,n}\right)\leq C_{1}\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4).

(18)

Proof:

From union bound and the fact that
u^{\beta}\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\bigcup\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\bigcup\limits_{j\in J_{n}}E_{j,n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}2^{2^{n+1}}2\exp(-2^{n}u^{\beta})

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\exp(2(\log 2)2^{n})\exp(-2^{n}u^{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\exp((\log 2-1)2^{n}u^{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(-2^{n^{\prime}}u^{\beta}/2)\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\exp((\log 2-1)2^{n}u^{\beta}+2^{n^{\prime}}u^{\beta}/2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp(-2^{n^{\prime}}u^{\beta}/2)\sum\limits_{n>0}\exp((\log 2-1)2^{n}u^{\beta}+2^{n}u^{\beta}/4).

Note that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n>0}\exp((\log 2-1)2^{n}u^{\beta}+2^{n}u^{\beta}/4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n>0}\exp(2^{n}u^{\beta}(\log 2-0.75))

(20)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n>0}\exp(2^{n+1}(\log 2-0.75))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n>0}\exp(2n(\log 2-0.75))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}.

where we apply
u\geq 2^{1/\beta}
at
\leq_{1}
and
(\log 2-0.75)<0
. Then, we have Eq. (
19
) since
2^{n^{\prime}}\geq p/2
.

\hfill\Box

Lemma 3

Given a positive random variable
x
, ratio
r\geq 0
, boundary constant
u_{b}>0
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta<\infty
, if we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(x>ru\right)\leq d\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4),

(21)

where
u\geq u_{b}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}x^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq r(c_{\beta}d+u_{b}),

(22)

where the constant
c_{\beta}
depends only on
\beta
.

Proof:

By integration by parts and a change of variable, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}x^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}px^{p-1}\mathrm{P}(x>u)du

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}pv^{p-1}\mathrm{P}(x>rv)dv

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle r^{p}\left(\int_{u_{b}}^{\infty}pv^{p-1}d\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4)dv+\int_{0}^{u_{b}}pv^{p-1}dv\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{p}\left(d\int_{u_{b}}^{\infty}pv^{p-1}\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4)dv+u_{b}^{p}\right),

where we apply Eq. (
21
) in
\leq_{1}
. We need to analyze the term
\int_{u_{b}}^{\infty}pv^{p-1}\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4)dv
further. Because we have

\displaystyle\int_{u_{b}}^{\infty}pv^{p-1}\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4)dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}pv^{p-1}\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4)dv

(24)

\displaystyle=_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{2p^{1-p/\beta}2^{p/\beta}}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{2p/\beta-1}e^{-w^{2}/2}dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{2\pi}p^{1-p/\beta}2^{p/\beta}}{2\beta}\mathbb{E}|y|^{2p/\beta-1}

where
=_{1}
is obtained by change of variable and
y
is a standard normal random variable. From the work
[
16
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|y|^{2p/\beta-1}=\frac{2^{p/\beta-1/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(p/2\beta).

(25)

The result follows by combining all of the above estimations.

\hfill\Box

We are ready to present the following theorem about tail bounds for suprema of tensor random processes with an exponential tail.

Theorem 1

Given
0<\beta<\infty
and
(\mathcal{X}_{t})_{t\in T}
satisfying Eq. (
12
), then there exist constants
C_{\beta},D_{\beta}>0
, which depends only on
\beta
, such that for any
t_{0}\in T
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\beta}\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d)+2\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(26)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}\geq e^{1/\beta}\left(C_{\beta}\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d)+uD_{\beta}\Delta_{d}(T)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp(-u^{\beta}/\beta).

(27)

Proof:

Let
\mathfrak{T}=(T_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be an optimal admissible sequence for
\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d)
and let

\varpi_{n}:T\rightarrow T_{n}
be a sequence of functions defined as
\varpi_{n}(t)=\arg\min\limits_{s\in T_{n}}d(s,t)
. By setting
n^{\prime}=\lfloor\log_{2}(p)\rfloor
, we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(28)

For the first term on R.H.S. of Eq. (
28
), we will apply telescoping sum representation for
\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}
. It is

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n-1}(t)}.

(29)

Due to that the tensor random process
(\mathcal{X}_{t})_{t\in T}
satisfies Eq. (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}\geq u2^{n/\beta}d(\varpi_{n}(t),\varpi_{n-1}(t))\right)\leq 2\exp(-u^{\beta}2^{n}),

(30)

where
n>n^{\prime}
. If
u\geq 2^{1/\beta}
, we use
E_{u,p}
to represent the following event:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}\leq u2^{n/\beta}d(\varpi_{n}(t),\varpi_{n-1}(t)),

(31)

where
\forall n>n^{\prime}
and
\forall t\in T
. Then, by Lemma
2
, we have the following probability bound for the complement of the event
E_{u,p}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(E^{c}_{u,p})\leq C_{1}\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4).

(32)

If the event
E_{u,p}
happens, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}

(33)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle u\sum\limits_{n>n^{\prime}}2^{n/\beta}d(\varpi_{n}(t),\varpi_{n-1}(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle u(1+2^{1/\beta})\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}u(1+2^{1/\beta})\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d)\right)\leq C_{1}\exp(-pu^{\beta}/4).

(34)

By Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{\beta}\gamma_{\beta,p}(T,d).

(35)

For the second term on R.H.S. of Eq. (
28
), we can estimate this by Lemma
1
. It is

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

(36)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Then, Eq. (
26
) follows by combining Eq. (
28
), Eq. (
35
) and Eq. (
36
).

Before proving Eq. (
27
), we have to state two facts about a random variable. Their proof can be found at Chapter 7 in

]

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(|X|\geq e^{1/\beta}(au+b)\right)\leq\exp(-u^{\beta}/\beta),

where

. The second fact is about how to bound the random variable moment from its tail bound. If a random variable

satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(|X|\geq e^{1/\beta}au)\leq be^{-u^{\beta}/\beta},

(39)

where

\displaystyle(\mathbb{E}|X|^{p})^{1/p}\leq e^{1/2e}ap^{1/\beta}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}}be^{\beta/12}\right)^{1/p}.

(40)

From the condition provided by Eq. (
12
) and the fact given by Eq. (
40
), we have

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}\right)^{1/p}\leq D_{\beta}\Delta_{d}(T)p^{1/\beta}.

(41)

Then Eq. (
27
) is obtained by applying Eq. (
41
) to Eq. (
26
) with Eq. (
38
).

\hfill\Box

We use the symbol
\preceq
in

\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{X}
is a positive definite tensor.

Since Theorem
1

, we can apply Theorem
1
to martingale context. If
\left(\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)_{i=0,1,2,\cdots,n}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
is a discrete-time Hermitian tensor-valued martingale and its

-th difference tensor is by
\mathcal{D}_{i}=\mathcal{X}_{i}-\mathcal{X}_{i-1}
, then, from Theorem 14 (Tensor Azuma Inequality) in
[

]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\mathcal{X}_{n}-\mathcal{X}_{0}\right)\geq u\right)\leq\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right),

(42)

where
\sigma^{2}=\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{2}\right\|_{\mbox{\tiny max spec}}
and

represents the maximum spectral norm. Combing this Tensor Azuma Inequality given by Eq. (

Corollary 1

Let
\mathcal{X}_{t}=\left(\mathcal{X}_{t,i}\right)_{i=0,1,\cdots,n},t\in T

with respect to the same filtration. We define the following metric:

\displaystyle d_{s,t}=\left(\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{2}\right\|_{\mbox{\tiny max spec}}\right)^{1/2},

(43)

where
\mathcal{D}_{i}=(\mathcal{X}_{t,i}-\mathcal{X}_{s,i})-(\mathcal{X}_{t,i-1}-\mathcal{X}_{s,i-1})
. For any

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in T}\lambda_{\max}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t,n}-\mathcal{X}_{t,0}\right)\geq e^{1/2}\left(C_{2}\gamma_{2}(T,d)+D_{2}\Delta_{d}(T)u\right)\right)\leq e^{-u^{2}/2}.

(44)

3.2
Tail Bounds for Suprema of Processes with Mixed Exponential Tails

The purpose of this section is to establish tail bouns for suprema of tensor processes with mixed exponential tails. We have to prepare following two lemmas which will be used in proving tail bouns for suprema of tensor processes.

Lemma 4

If
X
is a random variable satisfying

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}|X|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}a_{n}p^{1/n}+a_{n+1},

(45)

where
p\geq 1
and
0\leq a_{n}<\infty
; then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(|X|\leq e\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}a_{n}p^{1/n}+a_{n+1}\right)\right)\leq\exp(-u),

(46)

where
u\geq 1
.

Proof:

The proof is a straightforward consequence of Markov’s inequality.

\hfill\Box

Lemma 5

If a random variable
X
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(|X|\geq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}a_{n}p^{1/n}\right)\leq\exp(-u),

(47)

where
u\geq 0
and
0\leq a_{n}<\infty
; then,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}|X|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}ma_{n}f_{n}(p)p^{1/n},

(48)

where
f_{n}(p)
are positive real-valued functions related to the parameter
p
and
p\geq 1
.

Proof:

From Eq. (
48
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\frac{1}{m}|X|\geq u\right)\leq\begin{cases}e^{-u^{m}/a^{m}_{m}},&\mbox{if $0\leq u\leq a_{m}^{m}/a^{m-1}_{m-1}$};\\
\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \vdots\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ ,&\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \vdots\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ ;\\
e^{-u^{3}/a^{3}_{3}},&\mbox{if $a_{4}^{4}/a^{3}_{3}\leq u\leq a_{3}^{3}/a^{2}_{2}$};\\
e^{-u^{2}/a^{2}_{2}},&\mbox{if $a_{3}^{3}/a^{2}_{2}\leq u\leq a_{2}^{2}/a_{1}$};\\
e^{-u/a_{1}},&\mbox{if $u\geq a_{2}^{2}/a_{1}$}.\end{cases}

(49)

Using integration by parts and a change of variable, we have

\displaystyle m^{-p}\mathbb{E}|X|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{p-1}\mathrm{P}\left(\frac{1}{m}|X|\geq u\right)du

(50)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{a_{m}^{m}/a^{m-1}_{m-1}}u^{p-1}e^{-u^{m}/a^{m}_{m}}du+p\sum\limits_{n=2}^{m-1}\int_{a_{m-n+2}^{m-n+2}/a_{m-n+1}^{m-n+1}}^{a_{m-n+1}^{m-n+1}/a^{m-n}_{m-n}}u^{p-1}e^{-u^{m-n+1}/a^{m-n+1}_{m-n+1}}du

\displaystyle+p\int_{a_{2}^{2}/a_{1}}^{\infty}u^{p-1}e^{-u/a_{1}}du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}pa_{m}^{p}\Gamma(p/m)+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{m-1}\frac{1}{n}pa_{n}^{p}\Gamma(p/n)+pa_{1}^{p}\Gamma(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n}pa_{n}^{p}\Gamma(p/n)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}ma_{n}f_{n}(p)p^{1/n},

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function and
\leq_{1}
comes from estimation for Stirling’s formula. The Stirling’s formula of
\Gamma(p)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Gamma(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi}p^{p-1/2}e^{-p}e^{\mu(p)/12p},

(51)

where
0\leq\mu(p)\leq 1
. This Lemma is proved by finding
f_{n}(p)
to bound
\frac{1}{n}p\Gamma(p/n)
for each
n
. For example, if
m=2
, we have

\displaystyle f_{1}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi p}p^{p}e^{-p+1/12p},

\displaystyle f_{2}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}e^{1/6p}(2e)^{-p/2}e^{p/2e}p^{p/2}.

(52)

\hfill\Box

With these two lemmas, we are ready to present the following theorem about tail bouns for suprema of tensor processes with mixed exponential tails.

Theorem 2

If
\left(\mathcal{X}_{t}\right)_{t\in T}
has a mixed tail which satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{s}\right\|_{\alpha}\geq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}u^{1/n}d_{n}(s,t)\right)\leq 2e^{-u},

(53)

where
d_{n}(s,t)
are metrics defined on
T
and
u\geq 0
. Then, there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for any
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}\gamma_{n}(T,d_{n})+2\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|^{p}_{\alpha}\right)^{1/p}.

(54)

Accordingly, there are constants
C_{2},C_{3}
such that for any
u\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}\geq C_{2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}\gamma_{n}(T,d_{n})+C_{3}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}u^{1/n}\Delta_{d_{n}}(T)\right)\leq e^{-u}.

(55)

Proof:

We select
m
admissible sequences of paritions
\mathfrak{Q}^{(n)}=(\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
for
n=1,2,\cdots,m
, such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in T}\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}2^{i/n}\Delta_{d_{n}}(\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{i}(t))\leq 2\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n}),

(56)

where
\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n})
is defined as

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\mathfrak{Q}^{(n)}}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{i/n}\Delta_{d_{n}}(\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{i}(t)).

(57)

For
i\geq 0
, let
\mathfrak{A}=(A_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be the partition generated by
\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{i-j}
by

\displaystyle A_{i}=\left\{\bigcap\limits_{n=1}^{m}Q^{(n)}:Q^{(n)}\in\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{i-j}\right\}

(58)

where
j=\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil
and
\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{-j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{0}
so that
A_{0}=T
. Then,
(A_{i})_{i\geq 0}
is increasing and

\displaystyle|A_{i}|\leq\prod\limits_{n=1}^{m}|\bm{Q}^{(n)}_{i-j}|\leq 2^{2^{i-j}}\cdot 2^{2^{i-j}}\cdots 2^{2^{i-j}}=2^{2^{i}},

(59)

which shows that
\mathfrak{A}
is admissible. For every
i\geq 0
, we define
T_{i}
of
T
by selecting exactly one point from each
A_{i}
. We define
\varpi_{i}(t):T\rightarrow T_{i}
be the unique element of
T_{i}\bigcap A_{i}(t)
, then we have a sequence
(\varpi_{i})_{i\geq 0}
of maps.

By setting
i^{\prime}=\lfloor\log_{2}(p)\rfloor
, we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i^{\prime}}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i^{\prime}}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(60)

We will focus the estimation for the first term on R.H.S. of Eq. (
60
) since the second term term on R.H.S. of Eq. (
60
) can be derived similarly in the proof of Theorem
1
. For the frst term, we can apply the telescoping sum as

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i^{\prime}}(t)}=\sum\limits_{i>i^{\prime}}\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i-1}(t)}.

(61)

From condition of mixed tail given by Eq. (
53
), for
i>i^{\prime}
and
u\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}\geq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}u^{1/n}2^{i/n}d_{n}(\varpi_{i}(t),\varpi_{i-1}(t))\right)\leq 2e^{-u2^{i}}.

(62)

Let
E_{u,p}
represent the event

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}\geq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}u^{1/n}2^{i/n}d_{n}(\varpi_{i}(t),\varpi_{i-1}(t)),

(63)

where
i>i^{\prime}
and
t\in T
. If the event
E_{u,p}
happens, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i>i^{\prime}}\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i>i^{\prime}}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i}(t)}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{i-1}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}

(64)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}u^{1/n}\sum\limits_{i>i^{\prime}}2^{i/n}d_{n}(\varpi_{i}(t),\varpi_{i-1}(t)).

By our choice of
\mathfrak{Q}^{(n)}
, we can find constrants
h_{n}
for
n=1,2,\cdots,m
such that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i>i^{\prime}}2^{i/n}d_{n}(\varpi_{i}(t),\varpi_{i-1}(t))\leq h_{n}\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n}).

(65)

Due to the fact that
\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}\leq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}h_{n}\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}>C^{\prime}_{2}u\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n})\right)\leq c\exp(-pu/4),

(66)

where
C^{\prime}_{2}=\max\limits_{n=1,2,\cdots,m}h_{n}
. By Lemma
3
and the fact
\gamma_{n}(T,d_{n})\leq\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n})\leq g(n)\gamma_{n}(T,d_{n})
, where
g(n)
is a constant depending only on
n
; we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{\varpi_{n^{\prime}}(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq C^{\prime}_{2}u\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}\gamma^{\prime}_{n}(T,d_{n})\leq C_{2}u\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}\gamma_{n}(T,d_{n}).

(67)

Then, we have establish Eq. (
54
).

For the tail bound, Lemma
5
and the mixed tail condition provided by Eq. (
53
) give us

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{t}-\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{3}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}p^{1/n}\Delta_{d_{n}}(T).

(68)

Eq. (
55
) can be obtained by applying Eq. (
68
) to Eq. (
54
) with Lemma
4
.

4
Application: Compressed Sensing

In this section, we will apply Theorem
1
to high-dimensional compressed sensing. We begin with some basic notations for high-dimensional compressed sensing based on tensors. Note that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
is the standard Frobenius norm.

We say that a tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}}
is
\xi
-sparse if

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}|\{(j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{N}):x_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{N}}\neq 0\}|\leq\xi.

(69)

For a given
\xi\in\mathbb{N}
, the
\xi
-th restricted isometry constant

\tau_{\xi}
of an
I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}
tensor
\mathcal{A}
with the restrction
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}\leq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}
is the smallest constant
\tau
such that

\displaystyle(1-\tau)\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq\left\|\mathcal{A}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq(1+\tau)\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

(70)

for all
\xi
-sparse tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}}
. We also can define a set for
\xi
-sparse tensors
\mathcal{X}
as

\displaystyle S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}}:\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}=1,\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|_{0}\leq\xi\right\},

(71)

then, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}(\mathcal{A})=\sup\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}}\left|\left\|\mathcal{A}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-1\right|.

(72)

The restricted isometry constant
\tau_{\xi}(\mathcal{A})
is an important parameter in compressed sensing.

Let
[u_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{N},j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}]\in\mathcal{U}\in J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}
be a unitary tensor and we have some positive contant
\Upsilon\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n},j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}}\sqrt{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}}|u_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n},j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}}|\leq\Upsilon.

(73)

We consider a sequence
(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})_{1\leq j_{i}\leq J_{j}}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,N
of i.i.d. copies of the random selector, i.e., a Bernoulli random variable that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\theta=1\right)=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}}.

(74)

Let
\Pi=\{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}):\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}=1\}
be the random set of selected indices with
expected cardinality as
\mathbb{E}|\Pi|=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{\Pi}
be a sampled tensor obtained by the unitary tensor
\mathcal{U}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}}}\mathcal{S}_{\Pi}\star_{N}\mathcal{U},

(75)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\Pi}
is the operator which restricts a tensor to its entries in
\Pi
of
\mathcal{U}
.

We are ready to present following theorem about the restricted isometry constant
\tau_{\xi}
.

Theorem 3

If there are universal constants
C_{4},C_{5}>0
such that for any given
\xi\in\mathbb{N}
and any
0<\tau,\eta<1
satisfying

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}\geq\xi\Upsilon^{2}\tau^{2}\max\{C_{4}\log^{2}\xi\log\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}\right)\log\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}\right),C_{5}\log(\eta^{-1})\},

(76)

then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\tau_{\xi}(\mathcal{U}_{\Pi})\geq\tau\right)\leq\eta.

(77)

Proof:

Let
\mathcal{U}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}
be the
(j_{1},\cdots,j_{N})-
th projection tensor of
\mathcal{U}
. For every
tensor
\mathcal{X}\in S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}
, we define following function

\displaystyle f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})=\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}\sqrt{\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}}}\langle\mathcal{U}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}},\mathcal{X}\rangle.

(78)

Because
\mathcal{U}
is an unitary tensor, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\mathbb{E}f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}J_{j}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{i}}\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\mathbb{E}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\left|\langle\mathcal{U}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}},\mathcal{X}\rangle\right|^{2}

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\left|\langle\mathcal{U}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}},\mathcal{X}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{U}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}\right\|_{2}^{2}=1.

We can express
\tau_{\xi}(\mathcal{U}_{\Pi})
as

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}(\mathcal{U}_{\Pi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}}\left|\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\left(f^{2}_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})-\mathbb{E}f^{2}_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right)\right|.

(80)

Let
\epsilon_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}
be a Rademacher sequence, i.e., a sequence of independent
symmetric Bernoulli random variables, and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\tau^{p}_{\xi}(\mathcal{U}_{\Pi})\right)^{1/p}\leq 2\left(\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}\sup\limits_{\mathcal{X}\in S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}}\left|\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\epsilon_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(81)

By Hoeffding’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\epsilon_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}\left((f^{2}_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})-f^{2}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)\leq\exp(-u^{2}/2).

(82)

Because we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\left(f^{2}_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})-f^{2}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\left(f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})-f_{\mathcal{Y}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right)^{2}\left(f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})+f_{\mathcal{Y}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sup\limits_{\mathcal{Z}\in S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}}\left(\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}f_{\mathcal{Z}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right)^{1/2}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{i}\leq J_{i},\\
\mbox{\tiny for $1\leq i\leq N$}\end{subarray}}\left|f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})-f_{\mathcal{Y}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right|,

then, we can hve the following subgaussian process

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}\epsilon_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}f^{2}_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}}),

(84)

with respect to the metric

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sup\limits_{\mathcal{Z}\in S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}}\left(\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}f_{\mathcal{Z}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right)^{1/2}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{i}\leq J_{i},\\
\mbox{\tiny for $1\leq i\leq N$}\end{subarray}}\left|f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})-f_{\mathcal{Y}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sup\limits_{\mathcal{Z}\in S_{\xi;(J_{1},\cdots,J_{N})}}\left(\sum\limits_{j_{1}=1,\cdots,j_{N}=1}^{J_{1},\cdots,J_{N}}f_{\mathcal{Z}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right)^{1/2}d_{\theta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}),

where we represent the metric
d_{\theta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
as

\displaystyle d_{\theta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{i}\leq J_{i},\\
\mbox{\tiny for $1\leq i\leq N$}\end{subarray}}\left|f_{\mathcal{X}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})-f_{\mathcal{Y}}(\theta_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{N}})\right|.

(86)

From Theorem

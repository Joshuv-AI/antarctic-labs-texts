The σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} -based dimensionless accretion rate and its connection with the corona for AGN
Yu-Qin Chen, Yan-Sheng Liu, Wei-Hao Bian

Abstract
With respect to the H β \beta full width at half-maximum ( FWHM H ​ β \rm FWHM_{H\beta} ), the broad H β \beta line dispersion ( σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} ) was preferred as a velocity tracer to calculate the single-epoch supermassive black hole mass ( M BH M_{\rm BH} ) suggested by Yu et al. 2020b . For a compiled sample of 311 broad-line active galactic nuclei (AGN) with measured hard X-ray photon index ( z < 0.7 z<0.7 ), σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} and the optical Fe ii relative strength ( R Fe R_{\rm Fe} ) are measured from their optical spectra, which are used to calculate σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} -based virial M BH M_{\rm BH} and dimensionless accretion rate ( ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} ). With respect to FWHM H ​ β \rm FWHM_{H\beta} , it is found that the mean value of σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} -based M BH M_{\rm BH} is on average larger by 0.26 dex, and the mean value of σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} -based ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} is on average smaller by 0.51 dex. It is found that there exists a non-linear relationship between the Eddington ratio ( L Bol / L Edd L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd} ) and ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} , i.e., L Bol / L Edd ∝ ℳ ˙ 0.56 ± 0.01 L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{0.56\pm 0.01} . This non-linear relationship comes from the accretion efficiency η \eta , which is smaller for AGN with higher ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} . We find a strong bivariate correlation of the fraction of energy released in the corona F X F_{\rm X} with ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} and M BH M_{\rm BH} , F X ∝ ℳ ˙ − 0.57 ± 0.05 ​ M BH − 0.54 ± 0.06 F_{\rm X}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.57\pm 0.05}M_{\rm BH}^{-0.54\pm 0.06} . The flat slope of − 0.57 ± 0.05 -0.57\pm 0.05 favours the shear stress tensor of the accretion disk being proportional to the geometric mean of gas pressure and total pressure. We find a strong bivariate relation of Γ \Gamma with ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} and F X F_{\rm X} , Γ ∝ ℳ ˙ − 0.21 ± 0.02 ​ F X 0.02 ± 0.04 \Gamma\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.21\pm 0.02}F_{\rm X}^{0.02\pm 0.04} . The hard X-ray spectrum becomes softer with increasing of F X F_{\rm X} , although the scatter is large.



I
Introduction

Supermassive black holes (SMBHs) are believed to exist at the centers of nearly all galaxies
(
Kormendy & Ho 2013
, e.g.,)
. There are mainly two key parameters for SMBHs, i.e., mass (
M_{\rm BH}
) and spin. Understanding the properties of SMBHs will clarify the physics of active galactic nuclei (AGN)
(
Bian & Zhao 2002
;
Kormendy & Ho 2013
;
Netzer 2013
, e.g.,)
. Based on the accretion disk theory, the accretion rates can be calculated from the emitted spectra of AGN
(
Netzer 2013
, e.g.,)
.

A model with a hot corona surrounding a cold accretion disk in AGN describes the X-ray emission through Compton up-scattering of disk UV photons by the relativistic electrons in the hot corona
(
Liang et al. 1979
;
Haardt & Maraschi 1991
;
Netzer 2013
)
. There are various models of the coronal geometry, such as hot parallel planes covering the cold accretion disk, a hot sphere around the central SMBH, and an inner hot sphere plus an inner warm disk
(
Haardt & Maraschi 1991
;
Haardt et al. 1994
;
Netzer 2013
;
Kubota & Done 2018
, e.g.,)
.
For the standard accretion disk model,
Shakura & Sunyaev 1973
assumed that angular momentum transport was carried out by turbulence and that the stress tensor scaled with the disk pressure,
t_{r\phi}=-\alpha P
, with
\alpha
being the viscosity. Other expressions of the viscous stress
t_{r\phi}
have been discussed, with varying dependence on the gas pressure
P_{\rm gas}
, radiation pressure
P_{\rm rad}
or both
(
Wang et al. 2004
;
Yang et al. 2007
, e.g.,)
.
It is believed that the strong buoyancy and magnetic-field reconnection inevitably lead to the formation of a hot corona
(
Stella & Rosner 1984
)
. A fraction of total dissipated energy is transferred vertically outside the disk, and released in the hot, magnetically dominated corona
(
Haardt & Maraschi 1991
;
Svensson & Zdziarski 1994
, e.g.)
.
The fraction of the energy transported by magnetic buoyancy is
F_{\rm X}=P_{\rm mag}v_{p}/Q
, where
P_{\rm mag}=B^{2}/8\pi
is the magnetic pressure,
v_{p}
is the transport velocity, and the dissipated energy flux density in the disk
Q=-(3/2)c_{\rm s}t_{\rm r\phi}

(
Merloni & Fabian 2002
)
.
The viscous stress is assumed to be
t_{r\phi}=-k_{0}P_{\rm mag}
, and

F_{\rm X}=\frac{2v_{p}}{3k_{0}c_{s}}=\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}}b}{3k_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{P_{\rm mag}}{P_{\rm tot}}}=\frac{2^{\frac{3}{2}}b}{3k_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{(-t_{r\phi})}{P_{\rm tot}}}=C\sqrt{\frac{(-t_{r\phi})}{P_{\rm tot}}}

(1)

where
b=v_{p}/v_{A}
, the
Alfv\acute{e}n
velocity
v_{A}=B/\sqrt{4\pi\rho}
,
P_{\rm tot}=P_{\rm rad}+P_{\rm gas}
,
c_{s}^{2}=P_{\rm tot}/\rho
and
C=2^{3/2}b/(3k_{0}^{3/2})\sim 1
.
Therefore, if the shear stress is assumed, the fraction
F_{\rm X}
can be theoretically obtained for different accretion rates
(
Svensson & Zdziarski 1994
;
Merloni & Fabian 2002
;
Wang et al. 2004
;
Yang et al. 2007
, e.g.)
. Through X-ray and optical observations,
F_{\rm X}\equiv L_{\rm X}/L_{\rm Bol}
(
L_{\rm X}
and
L_{\rm Bol}
are the X-ray luminosity and the bolometric luminosity, respectively) can be estimated. The dependence from Equation 1 gives us a possible opportunity to determine the working magnetic stress from hard X-ray observations.

The relation between
F_{\rm X}
and the Eddington ratio (
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
, where
L_{\rm Edd}
is the Eddington luminosity) was investigated by some authors with different AGN samples, suggesting a relation between
F_{\rm X}
and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
,
F_{\rm X}\propto(L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd})^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is between
-0.64
and
-0.74

(
Merloni & Fabian 2002
;
Wang et al. 2004
;
Yang et al. 2007
;
Wang et al. 2019a
, e.g.)
. These results supported the magnetic stress tensor being of the form
t_{\rm r\phi}\propto P_{\rm gas}
.

\alpha_{\rm OX}

Another relation between the hard X-ray photon index

In these studies, the Eddington ratio
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
is usually used to represent the accretion strength. The dimensionless accretion rate
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is also used to represent the accretion strength instead of
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
, where
\dot{\mathscr{M}}\equiv\dot{M}/\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}
,
\dot{M}
is the mass accretion rate,
\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}=L_{\rm Edd}/c^{2}

;

;
Kubota & Done 2018
;
Liu et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
.
The relation between
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
depends on the conversion efficiency
\eta
(from the accretion mass to the radiation),
L_{\rm Bol}=\eta\dot{M}c^{2}

(
Bian & Zhao 2003b
;
Davis & Laor 2011
)
. For both parameters
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
,
M_{\rm BH}
is the key parameter that needs to be determined.

For type 1 AGN, broad-line region (BLR) clouds can be used as a probe of the gravitational potential of the SMBH. The virial mass can be derived as follows
(
Peterson et al. 2004
;
Netzer 2013
, e.g.,)
:

M_{\rm BH}=f\times\frac{R_{\rm BLR}~(\Delta V)^{2}}{G}.

(2)

where
\Delta V
is the velocity dispersion of the BLR clouds,
f
is a corresponding virial factor,
R_{\rm BLR}
is the distance from the SMBH to the BLR, and
G
is the gravitational constant.
R_{\rm BLR}
can be estimated from the reverberation mapping (RM) method
(
Blandford & McKee 1982
, e.g.,)
or from the empirical
R_{\rm BLR}-L_{5100}
relation (
L_{\rm 5100}
is the 5100 Å monochromatic luminosity)
(
Kaspi et al. 2000
;
Bentz et al. 2013
;
Du & Wang 2019
;
Yu et al. 2020a
, e.g.,)
.
\Delta V
is usually determined from the broad H
\beta
Full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) or the line dispersion (
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
) measured from a single-epoch spectrum or a mean/rms spectrum
(
Peterson et al. 2004
;
Wang et al. 2019b
;
Yu et al. 2020b
, e.g.,)
. The factor
f
is a key quantity, which is usually calibrated through the
M_{\rm BH}-\sigma_{\ast}
relation (
\sigma_{*}
is the bulge stellar velocity dispersion) or other independent methods to derive the SMBH masses
(
Onken et al. 2004
;
Yu et al. 2019
;
Yu et al. 2020b
, e.g.,)
. For a sample of RM AGN, with respect to
M_{\rm BH}
from
\sigma_{*}
or
\sigma_{\rm H\beta,rms}
, it was found that we can obtain
M_{\rm BH}
from
\sigma_{\rm H\beta,mean}
with smaller scatter than from
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
.
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
is preferred as a velocity tracer to calculate the single-epoch virial
M_{\rm BH}

(
Yu et al. 2020b
)
.

Dalla Bontà et al. 2020
also suggested that the use of
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
gives better results, while the use of
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
introduces a bias, stretching the mass scale such that high masses are overestimated and low masses are underestimated, although both velocity tracers are usable.

The accretion rate can be derived from the disk model of
Shakura & Sunyaev 1973
, which has been extensively applied to fit the spectra of AGN
(
Davis & Laor 2011
;
Mejía-Restrepo et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. Considering that the radial distribution of the effective disk temperature is given by
T_{\rm eff}\propto R^{-3/4}
,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is
(
Du et al. 2016a
, e.g.,)

\dot{\mathscr{M}}\equiv\dot{M}/\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}=20.1\left(\frac{l_{44}}{\cos\,\it{i}}\right)^{3/2}m_{7}^{-2}.

(3)

where
l_{44}=L_{5100}/10^{44}{\rm erg\ s}^{-1}
,
m_{7}=M_{\rm BH}/10^{7}M_{\odot}
. An average value of
{\rm cos}~i=0.75
is adopted.
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is inversely proportional to
M_{\rm BH}
to the second power.
Recently,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
was used to search for super-Eddington accreting massive black holes in a RM project
(
Wang et al. 2013
;
Du et al. 2016a
, SEAMBHs,)
. From the SEAMBH RM project, an extended empirical
R_{\rm BLR}-L_{5100}
relation including
R_{\rm Fe}
(the flux ratio of the broad H
\beta
to the optical Fe
ii
within
4435-4685
Å) was suggested
(
Du & Wang 2019
;
Yu et al. 2020a
;
Khadka et al. 2022
)
. This extended relation is possibly due to the dependence of the spectral energy distribution (SED) on the accretion rate
(
Kilerci Eser et al. 2015
;
Yu et al. 2020a
, e.g,)
.

We have used a sample of 208 broad-line AGN to investigate the relations between the coronal properties
and the accretion process
(
Wang et al. 2019a
)
.
That sample was selected from the Swift/BAT AGN Spectroscopic Survey (BASS) in the ultra-hard X-ray band (14–195 keV).

There are 13 narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies (NLS1s) with

)

In that paper,

)

In order to extend our sample to higher redshifts, a new sample of 113 quasars (

) drawing from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) DR14 quasar catalogue with available hard X-ray
\Gamma
(2-10 keV) are also used here

)
.
From these two samples (Swift BASS and SDSS) with measured hard X-ray photon index, in this paper, we compile a large sample of 311 broad-line AGN (

) to further investigate the coronal properties.
Through spectral decomposition, we measure

and

from their optical spectra.
For determination of

(

Koss et al. 2017
)
and the SDSS DR16 archives. There are 142 out of the 642 Swift/BASS AGN with spectra collected from SDSS
(
Koss et al. 2017
)
. The spectra for the remaining 64 AGN were collected by other telescopes, which were given on the Swift BASS website
3
3

3

http://www.bass-survey.com/data.html
. We do the spectral decomposition using the Python code PyQSOFit
(
Guo et al. 2018
;
Shen et al. 2019
)
. The fitting is performed in the rest frame of the AGN using the redshift, after correcting for Galactic reddening using the dust map in
Schlegel et al. 1998
and the extinction curve with
R_{V}=3.1
from
Cardelli et al. 1989
. The decomposition for the host and AGN contributions is applied, where the templates used are from
Yip et al. 2004a
;
Yip et al. 2004b
.
The optical Fe
ii
emission is fitted using empirical templates from
Boroson & Green 1992
. A pseudo-continuum including the optical Fe
ii
emission, a power law and a third-order polynomial is subtracted from the spectrum to form a line-only spectrum for which we measure emission-line properties.

Considering the possible broad wing shown in [O
iii
]
(
Hu et al. 2008
;
Wang et al. 2019b
, e.g.,)
, two sets of two Gaussians are used to fit the [O
iii
]
\lambda\lambda
4959, 5007 doublets, with constraints on their corresponding strengths, velocities and widths. The [O
iii
]
\lambda\lambda
4959, 5007 lines are forced to have the same FWHM and no relative wavelength shift, and their intensity ratio fixed to the theoretical value of 3.0. Three Gaussians are used to model the H
\beta
profile, two Gaussians as the broad component and a Gaussian as a narrow component. The narrow H
\beta
component is tied to the core component of [O
iii
]5007 (same FWHM and no relative wavelength shift), which is coming from the narrow-line region (NLR). We also use two Gaussians to fit He
ii

\lambda
4686, one is from the NLR and the other is from the BLR. An example of fitting a spectrum is shown in Figure
3
.
From the pseudo-continuum and the reconstructed profile from the two H
\beta
broad components, we measure the optical integral Fe
ii
flux (
F_{\rm Fe{~\sc II}}
) between 4434 Å and 4684 Å,
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
, FWHM, and integral flux (
F_{\rm H\beta}
). Then we calculate the optical Fe
ii
relative strength,
R_{\rm Fe}=F_{\rm Fe{~\sc II}}/F_{\rm H\beta}
. Based on the flux error, Monte Carlo simulation is performed for 20 trials to estimate the measurement uncertainties of the spectral properties. We correct the spectral resolution effect on the line width measurements following
Peterson et al. 2004
, adopting the spectral resolutions presented by
Koss et al. 2017
.
The results are presented in Tables 1 and 2.

and

by

.

2
and
3

\dot{\mathscr{M}}\propto L_{5100}^{1.5}M_{\rm BH}^{-2}

L_{5100}
is weaker than on
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. The slope of
0.5
is well consistent with our fitted slope of
0.56\pm 0.01
. The non-linear relation between
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
would have an impact on the relations using
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
instead of
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}

, where

is the accretion efficiency,

and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
implies that the conversion efficiency

is anti-correlated with
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
or
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
( including a relatively weaker dependence on

\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. The Spearman correlation test gives
r=-0.60
,
p_{\rm null}=3.0\times 10^{-32}
. The correlation is significant.
Using the uncertainties of
F_{\rm X}
,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
of
0.3
,
0.62
dex, respectively, the BCES(
\rm Y|X
) best-fitting relation for our total sample gives
\log F_{\rm X}=-(0.45\pm 0.05)\log\dot{\mathscr{M}}-(1.01\pm 0.06)
.
Comparing with the result by
Wang et al. 2019a
, our slope of
-0.45\pm 0.05
is flatter than theirs, i.e.,
-0.60\pm 0.1
. The negative correlation shows that, with increasing dimensionless accretion rate, the energy dissipated in the corona becomes smaller.

It was suggested that
F_{\rm X}
depends on
M_{\rm BH}

(
Yang et al. 2007
;
Wang et al. 2019a
)
.
The multivariate regression analysis technique is used to investigate the relation of
F_{\rm X}
with
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
and
M_{\rm BH}

(
Wang et al. 2019a
;
Yu et al. 2020a
;
Yu et al. 2020b
)
.
For the best linear fit in the form
y=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\beta_{1}
, the estimator of
\chi^{2}
is used to find the best values for these fitting coefficients:
\chi^{2}=\Sigma_{i}\frac{(y_{i}-\alpha_{1}x_{1i}-\alpha_{2}x_{2i}-\beta_{1})^{2}}{\sigma_{\rm int}^{2}+\sigma_{y_{i}}^{2}+(\alpha_{1}\sigma_{x_{1i}})^{2}+(\alpha_{2}\sigma_{x_{2i}})^{2}}
.
y_{i}
is the dependent variable.
x_{1i}
and
x_{2i}
are the independent variables.
\sigma_{y_{i}}
,
\sigma_{x_{1i}}
and
\sigma_{x_{2i}}
are the uncertainties of
y_{i}
,
x_{1i}
and
x_{2i}
, respectively.
\sigma_{\rm int}
is the intrinsic scatter.
Considering the uncertainties of
F_{\rm X}
,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
,
M_{\rm BH}
of
0.3
,
0.62
,
0.3
dex, respectively (see section 3.2), the fitting result is
\log F_{\rm X}=-(0.57\pm 0.05)\log\dot{\mathscr{M}}-(0.54\pm 0.06)\log M_{\rm BH}+(3.31\pm 0.44)
(see the right panel in Figure
8
).
The bootstrap method is used to estimate the error bars of the fitting coefficients. We re-sample the data 100 times and repeat the multiple linear regressions. Therefore, we can derive 100 values for each coefficient. For each coefficient, we sort these values, adopt the 16th and 84th values as end points of the 1
\sigma
confidence interval, and calculate its error
(
Yu et al. 2020a
)
.
Including
M_{\rm BH}
in the relation between
F_{\rm X}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
, the correlation becomes stronger, from
r=0.60,p_{\rm null}=3.0\times 10^{-32}
to
r=0.73,p_{\rm null}=1.0\times 10^{-308}
.
The scatter reduces from 0.440 to 0.398. The
\chi^{2}
decreases from 663.98 to 546.23.
We compare the
\chi^{2}_{N}
,
\chi^{2}_{M}
, respectively for the single variable regression and multivariate regression, and use the
F
-test
(
Lupton 1993
, chap. 12.1)
to calculate how significantly the multivariate regression model improves the fit.
f=(n-s)\frac{\chi^{2}_{N}-\chi^{2}_{M}}{\chi^{2}_{N}}
, n=311, s=2. We found that the probability P that the multivariate regression model cannot improve the fit is
2.55\times 10^{-12}
. Therefore, the significance of improving the fitting by the multivariate regression is greater than 3
\sigma
(
1-P>99.73\%
).
With increasing
M_{\rm BH}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
,
F_{\rm X}
becomes smaller. The trend is consistent with the result by
Wang et al. 2019a
, where
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
instead of
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
was used. However, our fitting slope of
-0.57\pm 0.05
is flatter than the value of
-0.74\pm 0.14
by
Wang et al. 2019a
.

For the SDSS subsample by
Huang et al. 2020
, the X-ray data don’t cover the band beyond
\rm 10~keV
. We also do the analysis only for the Swift BASS subsample by
Wang et al. 2019a
. For the relation of
F_{\rm X}
with
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
, the slope is
-0.30\pm 0.07
for the Swift BASS subsample.
Considering the uncertainties of
F_{\rm X}
,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
,
M_{\rm BH}
of
0.3
,
0.62
,
0.3
dex, respectively (see section 3.2), multivariate regression analysis shows that
F_{\rm X}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.55\pm 0.05}M_{\rm BH}^{-0.52\pm 0.05}
with
r=0.55,p_{\rm null}=2.3\times 10^{-17}
for the Swift BASS subsample, which is well consistent with the entire sample. Considering the uncertainty, the slope of
-0.55\pm 0.05
is very well consistent with
-0.57\pm 0.05
as shown in the right panel in Figure
8
. The slope can be used to constrain the shear stress tensor of the accretion disk of AGN.

The fraction of the energy transported by magnetic buoyancy is
F_{\rm X}=P_{\rm mag}v_{p}/Q
, where
P_{\rm mag}=B^{2}/8\pi
,
Q=-(3/2)c_{\rm s}t_{\rm r\phi}
(see Section 1). The viscous stress is
t_{r\phi}=-k_{0}P_{\rm mag}
,
F_{\rm X}=C\sqrt{\frac{(-t_{r\phi})}{P_{\rm tot}}}
.
If the magnetic stress
t_{r\phi}
is assumed, we can get
F_{\rm X}
at every radius and for different accretion rates. A global value of
<F_{\rm X}>
can then be obtained by integrating over all the disc area for different magnetic-stress tensors
(
Svensson & Zdziarski 1994
;
Merloni & Fabian 2002
;
Wang et al. 2004
;
Yang et al. 2007
)
.
Theoretical curves were presented for the relation between
F_{\rm X}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
for six distinct types of
t_{r\phi}
, which have relations with
P_{gas},P_{rad},andP_{total}

(
Wang et al. 2004
;
Yang et al. 2007
)
. The stress tensors of
t_{r\phi}
are
-\alpha P_{tot}
,
-\alpha P_{gas}
,
-\alpha P_{rad}
,
-\alpha\sqrt{P_{rad}P_{tot}}
,
-\alpha\sqrt{P_{gas}P_{tot}}
, and
-\alpha\sqrt{P_{gas}P_{rad}}
for models 1 to 6, respectively, where
\alpha
is the viscosity. Different models have different slopes for the
F_{\rm X}
-
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
relation. For model 1, the slope is zero. For models 3 and 4, the slope is positive. For model 6, the slope changes from a positive value to a negative value and has a peak at
\dot{\mathscr{M}}\sim 0.1
. For models 2 and 5, the slope is negative, but their slope values are different
(
Yang et al. 2007
, see Figure 5 in)
.
For their model 2 of magnetic stress tensor
t_{r\phi}\propto P_{gas}
,
F_{\rm X}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.77}
. For their model 5 of magnetic stress tensor
t_{r\phi}\propto\sqrt{P_{gas}P_{total}}
,
F_{\rm X}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.44}
.
Using
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
instead of
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
for the accretion strength, the flat slope of
-0.45\pm 0.05
or
-0.57\pm 0.05
favors model 5, which suggests the shear stress tensor being proportional to
\sqrt{P_{gas}P_{tot}}
.

Considering the relation of
\eta\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.5}L_{5100}^{0.25}
, the negative relation between
F_{\rm X}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
(i.e.,
F_{\rm X}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.57}
) implies that there is a positive correlation between
F_{\rm X}
and
\eta
, i.e.,
F_{\rm X}\propto\eta^{1.14}L_{5100}^{-0.285}
.

F_{\rm X}
increases more slowly than expected from the fitting solid line in Figure
8
for decreasing
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
, implying a limit on
\eta
for smaller
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
for the faint AGN with large
L_{5100}
.
The accretion efficiency
\eta
depends on the innermost radius of the accreting matter, which is related to the SMBH spin
(
Netzer 2013
, e.g.,)
. The fraction of energy dissipated in the hot corona
F_{\rm X}
has a correlation with the accretion efficiency
\eta
. It implies that AGN with SMBHs that spin faster (smaller
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
) would have larger
F_{\rm X}
.

IV.3
The relation between
\Gamma
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}

\Gamma
versus
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. The solid black line is our best fit. The purple line is the result by

. Right: Dependence of
\Gamma
on
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
and
F_{\rm X}
. The solid line is 1:1. The symbols are the same as in Figure

.

In the left panel in Figure
9
, we show the hard X-ray photon index
\Gamma
versus
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. The Spearman correlation test gives
r=0.52
,
p_{\rm null}=1.1\times 10^{-22}
. The correlation is significant. Considering errors in both coordinates, the BCES(
\rm Y|X
) best-fitting relation for our total sample shows
\Gamma=(0.22\pm 0.02)\log\dot{\mathscr{M}}+(2.19\pm 0.03)
.
For 113 SDSS AGN by
Huang et al. 2020
,
r=0.52,p_{\rm null}=3.1\times 10^{-9}
,
\Gamma\propto(\log\dot{\mathscr{M}})^{0.23\pm 0.04}
. For 198 Swift-BASS AGN by
Wang et al. 2019a
,
r=0.31,p_{\rm null}=1.1\times 10^{-5}
,
\Gamma\propto(\log\dot{\mathscr{M}})^{0.14\pm 0.03}
.
The slope (
0.23\pm 0.04
) for the SDSS subsample is slightly larger than the slope (
0.14\pm 0.03
) for the Swift-BASS subsample. It is possibly due to the high-luminosity for the SDSS subsample and/or lacking AGN with small
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
in the SDSS subsample.
For 32 RM AGN in our sample, the Spearman correlation test gives
r=0.4
,
p_{\rm null}=0.024
.
For 22 super-Eddington accreting AGN with
\dot{\mathscr{M}}\geq 3
, we find that they are following the linear fitting (solid line the left panel in Figure
9
), although with a large uncertainty in
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
(
\sim 0.62~
dex).

For our sample, the relation between
\Gamma
and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
is also investigated. The Spearman correlation test gives
r=0.56
,
p_{\rm null}=6.7\times 10^{-27}
. The correlation is also significant. Considering errors in both coordinates, the BCES(
\rm Y|X
) best-fitting relation for our total sample shows
\Gamma=(0.39\pm 0.04)\log L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}+(2.54\pm 0.06)
. Using
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
to calculate
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
, the slopes are
0.21
by
Wang et al. 2019a
and
0.23
by
Huang et al. 2020
, which are smaller than ours of
0.39
. Considering the relation of
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}\propto(\dot{\mathscr{M}})^{0.56}
,
\Gamma\propto(\log L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd})^{0.39}
is consistent with
\Gamma\propto(\log\dot{\mathscr{M}})^{0.22}
. Using
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
, the larger slope of
0.39\pm 0.04
will relieve the need for large viscosity in a model of the condensation of the corona by
Qiao & Liu 2018
. We also find that the relation between
\Gamma
and
M_{\rm BH}
is very weak with
r_{s}=-0.066
, and
p_{\rm null}=0.247
.

The correlation between the hard X-ray photon index
\Gamma
and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
is usually interpreted in the scenario of the disk–corona connection. For AGN with a larger
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
, the enhanced UV/optical emission from the accretion disk leads to more effective Compton cooling in the corona. This decreases the temperature and/or optical depth in the corona, which leads to the softening of the X-ray spectrum (a larger value of
\Gamma
)
(
Pounds et al. 1995
;
Fabian et al. 2015
;
Qiao & Liu 2018
;
Cheng et al. 2020
, e.g., )
.

Considering the uncertainties of
F_{\rm X}
,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
of
0.3
,
0.62
dex and the error of
\Gamma
, multivariate regression analysis
shows that
\Gamma=(0.21\pm 0.02)\log\dot{\mathscr{M}}+(0.02\pm 0.04)\log F_{\rm X}+(2.21\pm 0.44)
.
Including
F_{\rm X}
in the relation between
\Gamma
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
, the correlation becomes slightly stronger than the single variable regression, from
r=0.52,p_{\rm null}=1.1\times 10^{-22}
to
r=0.53,p_{\rm null}=4.7\times 10^{-24}
.
The scatter decreases slightly from 0.237 to 0.234. The
\chi^{2}
decreases from 7109 to 6756. Using the F-test, from the
\chi^{2}
difference, we found that the probability P that the multivariate regression model cannot improve the fit is
2.18\times 10^{-4}
. Therefore, the significance of improving the fitting by the multivariate regression is greater than 3
\sigma
(
1-P>99.73\%
).

Kubota & Done 2018
developed a spectral model for the AGN SED, including an outer standard disc to produce UV/optical emission, an inner warm Comptonizing region to produce the soft X-ray excess and a hot corona to produce hard X-rays. They suggested that the corona size scale has to decrease with increasing Eddington fraction, as well as the steeper spectral index with increasing
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. Considering the reprocessed emission produced by the hot corona illuminating the warm Comptonization and standard disc regions, they predicted a decreasing amount of optical variability with increasing
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. They also predicted that with increasing
F_{\rm X}
, the curve of
\Gamma-\dot{\mathscr{M}}
relation is lower (see their Figure 5). With increasing of
F_{\rm X}
, the curve of the
\Gamma-\dot{\mathscr{M}}
relation is lower and the X-ray spectrum becomes harder.
For our compiled sample, we find that the hard X-ray spectrum becomes softer with increasing of
F_{\rm X}
, although the scatter is large (
0.02\pm 0.04
). It is not consistent with the result for the spectral model suggested by
Kubota & Done 2018
. We caution that this contradiction is partly due to the large uncertainties in these parameters.

V
Conclusions

A sample of 311 broad-line AGN (
z<0.7
) is collected from Swift BASS and SDSS with hard X-ray photon indexes, and
L_{5100}
covering from
\sim 10^{41}
to
\sim 10^{46}
erg s
-1
. Through optical spectral decomposition,
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
and
R_{\rm Fe}
are measured, which are used to calculate the
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based virial
M_{\rm BH}
. Using the standard disk model, the dimensionless accretion rate
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
can be derived from
L_{5100}
and the
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
. The main conclusions can be summarized as follows:

•

For our entire sample, with respect to
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
, the mean value of
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
is on average larger by
0.26
dex, the mean value of
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is on average smaller by
0.51
dex.

•

There exists a non-linear relationship between the Eddington ratio (
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
) and the dimensionless accretion (
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
),
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{0.56}
. From the accretion efficiency
\eta
,
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}=\eta\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. The difference between
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
comes from
\eta
. The non-linear relationship implies smaller efficiency for AGN with higher dimensionless accretion rate, i.e.,
\eta\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.5}L_{5100}^{0.25}
.

•

The difference between
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
-based and
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
comes from the corresponding proportionality factor
f
. For a variable
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
-based
f
, we find a relation between
f
and
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
,
\log f=-(0.61\pm 0.05)\log\rm FWHM_{H\beta}+(2.48\pm 0.18)
. It has a similar trend to that suggested by
Yu et al. 2020b
.

•

We find a strong bivariate correlation of the fraction of energy released in the corona
F_{\rm X}
with
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
and
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
,
F_{\rm X}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.57\pm 0.05}M_{\rm BH}^{-0.54\pm 0.06}
. The flat slope of
-0.57\pm 0.05
favors the shear stress tensor being proportional to the geometric mean of gas pressure and total pressure. It implies AGN with SMBHs that spin faster (smaller
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
) would have larger
F_{\rm X}
.

•

It is found that the hard X-ray photon index
\Gamma
has a significant correlation with
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
, with a shallow slope with respect to that with
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
. The relation between
\Gamma
and
M_{\rm BH}
is very weak with
r_{s}=-0.066,p_{\rm null}=0.247
. Including
F_{\rm X}
, we find a slightly stronger bivariate relation of
\Gamma
with
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
and
F_{\rm X}
. We find that the hard X-ray spectrum becomes softer with increasing of
F_{\rm X}
, although the scatter is large (
0.02\pm 0.04
).

An isometric extensor of metrics
Yoshito Ishiki

Abstract
. In this paper, for a metrizable space Z Z , we consider the space of metrics that generate the same topology on Z Z , and that space of metrics is equipped with the supremum metrics. For a metrizable space X X and a closed subset A A of it, we construct a map E E from the space of metrics on A A into the space of metrics on X X such that E E is an extension of metrics and preserves the supremum metrics between metrics.

1.1.
Backgrounds

For a metrizable space

Z
,
we denote by

\mathrm{Met}(Z)

the
set of all
metrics on

Z

that generate the same topology of

Z
.
We write

\mathrm{BMet}(Z)

as the set of all bounded metrics in

\mathrm{Met}(Z)
.
We define the
supremum
metric

\metdis_{Z}

on

\mathrm{Met}(Z)
by

\metdis_{Z}(d,e)=\sup_{x,y\in Z}|d(x,y)-e(x,y)|.

Take a closed subset

A

of

Z
.
It was Felix Hausdorff

[
17
]

who first considered
the extension problem of
metrics on

A

to

Z
,
and proved that
for each

d\in\mathrm{Met}(Z)
,
there exists

D\in\mathrm{Met}(Z)

such that

D|_{A^{2}}=d
.
Independently,
in later years,
Bing

[
7
]

also proved the
same theorem
(see also

[
3
]
,

[
1
]
,
and

[
41
]
).
Based on Hausdorff’s result,
several mathematicians
considered
development of it.
For variants of Hausdorff’s extension theorem,
see, for example,

[
19
]
,

[
12
]
,

[
11
]
.
For extensions of ultrametrics
(non-Archimedean metrics),
see

[
42
]
,

[
39
]
,

[
20
]
,

[
21
]
,

[
23
]
,
and

[
22
]
.

Hausdorff’s extension theorem
only states that
we can find a extended metric

D\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

of each

d\in\mathrm{Met}(A)
.
Thus,
the next point of
iterrest is
whether these extensions of metrics
can be taken simultaneously.
In other words,
can we construct
an extensor

\mathrm{Met}(A)\to\mathrm{Met}(X)

that is continuous
with respect to
some topology on
spaces of metrics?
As long as the author knows,
in 1981,
Nguyen Van Khue and Nguyen To Nhu

[
27
]

first
constructed
simultaneous
extensions

\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\colon\mathrm{Met}(A)\to\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
which satisfy that

\Phi_{1}

is

20
-Lipschitz
with respect to
the supremum
metrics
on spaces of metrics,
and

\Phi_{2}

is continuous with
respect to the topologies of
point-wise convergence
and preserving orders.

An extensor

\Phi\colon\mathrm{BMet}(A)\to\mathrm{BMet}(Z)

on spaces of bounded metics
is

isometric

(with respect to the supremum
metrics)
if
we have

\metdis_{A}(d,e)=\metdis_{Z}(\Phi(d),\Phi(e))

for all

d,e\in\mathrm{Met}(A)
.
Such extensors on bounded metrics
have been constructed
by
Bessaga

[
5
]
,
Banakh

[
4
]
,
Pikhurko

[
35
]
,
and
Zarichnyi

[
44
]
.
In this paper,
we shall
construct
an isometric extensor

E\colon\mathrm{Met}(A)\to\mathrm{Met}(X)

of metrics.
Remark that, in contrast to the previous results,
our extensor

E

can be applied to
not only bounded metric but also
unbounded metrics.

1.2.
Main result

For a metrizable space

X
,
we denote by

\mathrm{Comp}(X)

the set of all complete metrics

d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
Of course,
we have

\mathrm{Comp}(X)\neq\emptyset

if and only if

X
is completely metrizable.
We also
denote by

\mathrm{CPM}(X)

the set of all
pseudometrics

d\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)

that are continuous as maps between

X\times X

and

[0,\infty)
.
We consider that

\mathrm{CPM}(X)

is equipped with
the supremum metric

\metdis_{X}
, for which
we use the same symbol
as the metric on
\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
We have

\mathrm{Met}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{CPM}(X)

and moreover,
the set

\mathrm{Met}(X)

is comeager in

\mathrm{CPM}(X)

(see
[
24
]
).

We prove the following
generalization of
Hausdorff’s metric extension
theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

X

be
a metrizable space,
and

A

be a closed subset of

X
.
Then there exists a map

E\colon\mathrm{Met}(A)\to\mathrm{Met}(X)

such that

(1)

for every

d\in\mathrm{Met}(A)

we have

E(d)|_{A^{2}}=d
;

(2)

the map

E

is
an isometric embedding,
i.e.,
for every pair

d,e\in\mathrm{Met}(A)
,
we have

\metdis_{A}(d,e)=\metdis_{X}(E(d),E(e));

(3)

we have

E(\mathrm{BMet}(A))\subseteq\mathrm{BMet}(X)
;

(4)

we have

E(\mathrm{Comp}(A))\subseteq\mathrm{Comp}(X)
;

Furthermore,
we also obtain an isometric extensor

F\colon\mathrm{CPM}(A)\to\mathrm{CPM}(X)

of pseudometrics
such that

F|_{\mathrm{Met}(A)}=E
.

This theorem is
an Archimedean
analogue of

[
22
, Theorem 4.7]
.
Our proof of
the main result is
based on
the idea of extending spaces and
extending homeomorphisms,
which was used in

[
44
]
,
and can go back to

[
17
]
,

[
18
]
,
and

[
29
]
.
Namely,
for a metric space

Z

and for a closed subset

A

of

Z
,
we will find an extension metrizable space

L

of

A

with
n topological embedding

I\colon A\to L

such that every metric

d\in\mathrm{Met}(A)

can be naturally
extended to a metric on

L

through

I
.
In this situation, we
will construct
an topological embedding

J\colon Z\to L

such that

J|_{A}=I
.

In

,

.

2.2.
Components of spaces of metrics

For a metrizable space

,
and for

\metdis_{Z}(d,e)<\infty
.
Then

We represent

\mathbf{M}_{<\infty}(Z)=\mathrm{Met}(Z)/\!\!\sim
.
For a member

\mathfrak{C}(d)

as

.
Remark that
each

\mathfrak{C}\in\mathbf{M}_{<\infty}(Z)

is
a
(path-)connected component of

\mathrm{Met}(Z)
, and

\mathrm{Met}(Z)

,

.

2.3.
Spaces of maps with finite supports

For
a set

T
,
we denote by

\mathbf{L}_{fin}^{\infty}(T)

the set of
all maps

f

from

T

into

\mathbb{R}

such that

Let

stand for the supremum metric on

Fix a metric space

\$\!\left(Z,S\right)=\$\!\left(Z\right)

\$\!\left[d\right]((x,s),(y,t))=d(x,y)+u(s,t)

Fix a point

\mathrm{I}^{\$}_{Z}(x)=(x,o)

.

Let

(S,u)

be a fixed metric space,

be

We also fix

.
Then

(1)

,
and
for every pair

,
we have

\$\!\left[d\right]\left(\mathrm{I}^{\$}_{Z}(x),\mathrm{I}^{\$}_{Z}(y)\right)=d(x,y).

(2)

The map

\$\colon\mathrm{Met}(Z)\to\mathrm{Met}(\$(Z,S))

by

d\mapsto\$\!\left[d\right]

is an isometric embedding,

for every pair

d,e\in\mathrm{Met}(Z)

\metdis_{Z}(d,e)=\metdis_{\$(Z)}(\$\!\left[d\right],\$\!\left[e\right]).

(3)

\$\!\left[d\right]

\$\!\left[e\right]

\$\!\left(Z\right)

2.4.2.
1
-Wasserstein spaces

Since we
will consider the
Wasserstein space
on a metrizable space that is not assumed to be
separable,
we follow the
construction
of Wasserstein spaces
discussed in

[
14
]

and

[
32
]

using Radon measures.

Let

Z

be
a metrizable space.
A Borel measure

\mu

on

Z

is said to be

Radon

if
for every Borel subset

A

of

Z
,
and for every

\epsilon\in(0,\infty)

there exists a compact subset

K

of

Z
such that

\mu(A\setminus K)<\epsilon
.
We denote by

\mathcal{RP}\!\left(Z\right)

the set of all
Radon
probability
measures on

Z
.
We define

\mathrm{I}^{\mathrm{W}_{1}}_{Z}\colon Z\to\mathcal{RP}\!\left(Z\right)

by

\mathrm{I}^{\mathrm{W}_{1}}_{Z}(x)=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x}
,
where

\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x}

is the Dirac measure on

x
.

For

\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{RP}\!\left(Z\right)
,
we
denote by

\Pi(\alpha,\beta)

the set of
all

\pi\in\mathcal{RP}\!\left(Z\times Z\right)

such that

\pi(A\times Z)=\alpha(A)

and
\pi(A\times Z)=\beta(A)
for
all Borel subsets
A

of

Z
.
Now we define
the
1
-Wasserstein distance

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right]

with respect to

d

by

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right](\alpha,\beta)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\alpha,\beta)}\int_{Z\times Z}d(s,t)\ d\pi(s,t),

where

\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{RP}\!\left(Z\right)
.
It should be noted that,
in general,

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right]

can take
the value

\infty
.
Thus,
we focus on the subset of

\mathcal{RP}\!\left(Z\right)

where

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right]

takes
only finite values.
Fix an equivalence class

\mathfrak{C}\in\mathbf{M}_{<\infty}(Z)
.
We denote by

\mathrm{W}_{1}^{\mathfrak{C}}\!\left(Z\right)

the set of

\alpha\in\mathcal{RP}\!\left(Z\right)

such that

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right](\alpha,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{p})<\infty

for some

p\in Z
.
This condition is also
equivalent to the inequality

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right](\alpha,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{q})<\infty

for every

q\in Z
.
By the definition,
the set

\mathrm{W}_{1}^{\mathfrak{C}}\!\left(Z\right)

is depending on
the class

\mathfrak{C}
;
namely,
for every pair

d,e\in\mathfrak{C}
,
we have

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right](\alpha,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{p})<\infty

if and only if

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[e\right](\alpha,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{p})<\infty
.
When
we fix a class

\mathfrak{C}
,
we simply write

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left(Z\right)=\mathrm{W}_{1}^{\mathfrak{C}}\!\left(Z\right)
.
We call

(\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left(Z\right),\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right])

the

1
-Wasserstein space on

(Z,d)
.

The

1
-Wasserstein
space is sometimes
called
the

Lipschitz-free space
with
emphasizing the aspect of the dual space of
the space of Lipschitz functions through
the
Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality.
This space is also sometimes referred
to
as
the

Arens–Eells space
,
named after
the
Arens–Eells
embedding theorem

[
2
]
.

From now on,
we review properties of
the Wasserstein spaces which
we will use in the present paper.

For a metric sapce

(X,d)
,
we denote by

\mathbf{Lip}_{1}\left(X,d\right)

the set of
all
real-valued

1
-Lipschitz functions.
To compute
values of Wasserstein distance
of specific measures,
we make use of
the Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality.

Theorem 2.2

(The Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality)
.

Let

(Z,d)

be a metric space.
Then for every pair

\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left(Z\right)
,
we have

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right](\alpha,\beta)=\sup\left\{\,\int_{Z}f(x)\ d(\alpha-\beta)(x)\ \middle|\ f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{1}\left(X,d\right)\,\right\}.

Proof.

Remark that we does not assume that

X

is Polish in the statement
of Theorem
2.2
.
For the proof,
we refer the readers to

[
14
, Theorem 4.1]

and

[
26
, Theorem 1]
,
or
we can prove this generalized duality using
the duality for Polish spaces
together with

[
32
, Lemma 2.5]
,
the McShane–Whitney extension of Lipschitz functions,
and the definition of Radon measures.

To show the main result,
we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

Z

be a metrizable space,
and
fix

\mathfrak{C}\in\mathbf{M}_{<\infty}(Z)

and

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Take
two
sequences

s_{1},\dots,s_{n}

and

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}

of non-negative reals
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}=1
,
and take
a finite sequence

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}

in

X
.
Then
for every point

b\in Z
,
we have

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right]\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x_{i}},\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x_{i}},\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|s_{i}-t_{i}|d(x_{i},b).

Proof.

Put

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x_{i}}
,
and

\beta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x_{i}}
.
Then
for every

1
-Lipschitz map

f\colon(Z,d)\to\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{Z}f(x)\ d(\alpha-\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}f(x_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}f(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}f(x_{i})-f(b)+f(b)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}f(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}f(x_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}f(b)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}f(b)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}f(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(f(x_{i})-f(b))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}(f(b)-f(x_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(s_{i}-t_{i})(f(x_{i})-f(b)).

Here,
for each

i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
we take
a
number

\mathbf{sgn}_{i}

in

\{1,-1\}

such that

\mathbf{sgn}_{i}\cdot|s_{i}-t_{i}|=(s_{i}-t_{i})
.
Then we can continue to compute.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(s_{i}-t_{i})(f(x_{i})-f(b))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|s_{i}-t_{i}|\cdot\mathbf{sgn}_{i}\cdot(f(x_{i})-f(b))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|s_{i}-t_{i}|\cdot|f(x_{i})-f(b)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|s_{i}-t_{i}|\cdot d(x_{i},b).

Thus,
for every
f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{1}\left(Z,d\right)
, we have

\int_{Z}f(x)\ d(\alpha-\beta)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|s_{i}-t_{i}|\cdot d(x_{i},b).

Therefore,
the Kantorovich–Rubinstein
duality
(Theorem
2.2
)
implies the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

Z

be a
metrizable space,
and fix

\mathfrak{C}\in\mathbf{M}_{<\infty}(Z)
.
Take a finite
sequence

c_{1},\dots,c_{n}

of
non-negative reals
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=1
,
and take
a
finite sequence

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}

in

X

Then
for every

p\in X
,
we have

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right]\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x_{i}},\boldsymbol{\delta}_{p}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}d(x_{i},p).

In particular,
we also have

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right]\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x_{i}},\boldsymbol{\delta}_{p}\right)\leq d(x_{i},p).

Proof.

Put

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x_{i}}
,
and

\beta=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{p}
.
Define

\mu\in\Pi(\alpha,\beta)
by

\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{(x_{i},p)}
.
Under this situation,
we have

\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right](\alpha,\beta)\leq\int_{Z\times Z}d(x,y)\ \mu(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}d(x_{i},p).

To obtain the opposite inequality,
consider

f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{1}\left(X,d\right)

define by

f(x)=d(x,p)
.
Then
we have

\displaystyle\int_{Z}f\ d(\alpha-\beta)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}f(x_{i})\right)-f(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}d(x_{i},p).

Thus
the Kantorovich–Rubinstein
duality
(Theorem
2.2
)
implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}d(x_{i},p)\leq\mathrm{W}_{1}\!\left[d\right](\alpha,\beta)
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

In this subsection,

\mathrm{F}

\mathfrak{C}\to\mathrm{Met}(\mathrm{F}^{\mathfrak{C}}\left(Z\right))

(O3)

3.
The Whitney–Dugundji decomposition

To show our main result,
we review the
classical
discussion called the
Whitney–Dugundji
decomposition,
which is
a special
partition of unity
on the complement of
a closed subset in consideration.
Such a decomposition in the Euclidean case
was
used by Whitney

[
43
]

to prove the famous Whitney extension theorem.
The general case of metric spaces
was found by
Dugundji

[
13
]
.
See also

[
1
]
.

For the sake of
convenience,
in this paper,
we
define such a decomposition
as a quadruplet
of open covering,
a partition of unity,
two family of
points.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space,
and

A

be a closed subset of

X
.
Fix

w\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
For

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

and
for a metric space

(X,w)
,
we say that
a
quadruplet

\mathcal{WD}=[\mathcal{O},\{\mathbf{y}_{O}\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}},\{\mathbf{a}_{O}\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}},\{\varphi_{O}\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}}]

is
a

(k,w)
-WD collection

if
the following conditions are
satisfied:

(WD1)

the family

\mathcal{O}

is a locally finite
open covering of

X\setminus A

consisting of open sets of

X\setminus A
,
each

\mathbf{y}_{O}

is a point in

X\setminus A
,
each

\mathbf{a}_{O}

is a point in

A
,
and
each

\varphi_{O}

is a continuous function
from

X

to

[0,1]
;

(WD2)

For every

O\in\mathcal{O}
,
we have

O\subset U\left(\mathbf{y}_{O},\frac{w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)}{4^{k+1}};w\right);

(WD3)

For every

O\in\mathcal{O}
,
we have

w(\mathbf{y}_{O},\mathbf{a}_{O})\leq\frac{4^{k+1}+1}{4^{k+1}}w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A);

(WD4)

For every

O\in\mathcal{O}
,
we have

\text{{\rm supp}}(\varphi_{O})\subseteq O,

where

\text{{\rm supp}}(\varphi_{O})

stands for the support of

\varphi_{O}

defined as

\{\,x\in X\mid\varphi_{O}(x)\neq 0\,\}
;

(WD5)

The family

\{\varphi_{O}\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}}

satisfies

\sum_{O\in\mathcal{O}}\varphi_{O}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $x\in A$;}\\
1&\text{if $x\in X\setminus A$}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
if the quadruplet satisfies the
following condition,
then
it is called a

strong
(k,w)
-WD collection
:

(WD6)

if

x\in X\setminus A

and

O\in\mathcal{O}

satisfy

\varphi_{O}(x)>0
,
then

\yodiam_{w}(O)\leq 16\cdot\frac{w(x,A)}{4^{k+1}}.

Before proving the
existence of
strong
WD collections,
we provides
a convenient estimation
related to
WD collections.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space,
and

A
be a closed subset of

X
.
Fix

w\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

and

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Assume that
a quadruplet

\mathcal{WD}=[\mathcal{O},\{\mathbf{y}_{O}\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}},\{\mathbf{a}_{O}\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}},\{\varphi_{O}\}_{O\in\mathcal{O}}]

is
a

(k,w)
-WD collection.
Then,
for every

a\in A

and
for every

x\in X
,
if

\varphi_{O}(x)>0
,
then we have

w(a,\mathbf{a}_{O})\leq 4w(a,x).

Proof.

Our proof is based on
that of

[
1
, Statement 2.3]
.
Since

\varphi_{O}(x)>0
,
the conditions

(WD4)

and

(WD2)

implies
that

x\in O

and

O\subset U\left(\mathbf{y}_{O},\frac{w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)}{4^{k+1}}\right).

Thus we have

w(x,\mathbf{y}_{O})\leq\frac{w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)}{4^{k+1}}.

Using this estimation
and
the condition

(WD3)
,
we also have

\displaystyle w(a,\mathbf{a}_{O})

\displaystyle\leq w(a,x)+w(x,\mathbf{y}_{O})+w(\mathbf{y}_{O},\mathbf{a}_{O})

\displaystyle\leq w(a,x)+\frac{1}{4^{k+1}}w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)+\frac{1+4^{k+1}}{4^{k+1}}w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)

\displaystyle=w(a,x)+\frac{4^{k+1}+2}{4^{k+1}}w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A).

Namely,

(1)

\displaystyle w(a,\mathbf{a}_{O})\leq w(a,x)+\frac{4^{k+1}+2}{4^{k+1}}w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A).

Moreover,

w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)\leq w(\mathbf{y}_{O},a)\leq w(\mathbf{y}_{O},x)+w(x,a)\leq\frac{1}{4^{k+1}}w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)+w(x,a),

\displaystyle w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)\leq\frac{4^{k+1}}{4^{k+1}-1}w(x,a).

Therefore,
combining these inequalities
(
1
)
and
(

),
we confirm the following computations.

\displaystyle w(a,\mathbf{a}_{O})\leq w(a,x)+\frac{4^{k+1}+2}{4^{k+1}}w(\mathbf{y}_{O},A)\leq w(a,x)+\frac{4^{k+1}+2}{4^{k+1}-1}w(a,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cdot 4^{k+1}+1}{4^{k+1}-1}w(a,x)=\frac{2+4^{-k-1}}{1-4^{-k-1}}w(x,a)\leq\frac{2+1}{3/4}\leq 4w(x,a).

This finishes the proof.

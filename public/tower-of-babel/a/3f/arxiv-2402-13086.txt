Profinite trees, through monads and the λ \lambda -calculus
Vincent Moreau

Abstract
In its simplest form, the theory of regular languages is the study of sets of finite words recognized by finite monoids. The finiteness condition on monoids gives rise to a topological space whose points, called profinite words, encode the limiting behavior of words with respect to finite monoids. Yet, some aspects of the theory of regular languages are not particular to monoids and can be described in a general setting. On the one hand, Bojańczyk has shown how to use monads to generalize the theory of regular languages and has given an abstract definition of the free profinite structure, defined by codensity, given a fixed monad and a notion of finite structure. On the other hand, Salvati has introduced the notion of language of lambda-terms, using denotational semantics, which generalizes the case of words and trees through the Church encoding. In recent work, the author and collaborators defined the notion of profinite lambda-term using semantics in finite sets and functions, which extend the Church encoding to profinite words. In this article, we prove that these two generalizations, based on monads and denotational semantics, coincide in the case of trees. To do so, we consider the monad of abstract clones which, when applied to a ranked alphabet, gives the associated clone of ranked trees. This induces a notion of free profinite clone, and hence of profinite trees. The main contribution is a categorical proof that the free profinite clone on a ranked alphabet is isomorphic, as a Stone-enriched clone, to the clone of profinite lambda-terms of Church type. Moreover, we also prove a parametricity theorem on families of semantic elements which provides another equivalent formulation of profinite trees in terms of Reynolds parametricity.

Introduction

Regular languages are fundamental objects of computer science. In the simplest form, they are sets of finite words over a given alphabet that can be recognized by finite deterministic automata, or equivalently, finite monoids. This finiteness condition, amounting to finite memory, can be studied from a topological point of view. Indeed, one can define a metric on
\Sigma^{*}
, for which words are far when they can be separated by a monoid of small cardinality
[
45
]
. The completion
\widehat{\Sigma^{*}}
of this metric space is the space of profinite words, a generalization of finite words that encodes their limiting behavior with respect to finite monoids. Moreover, this space is the Stone dual of the Boolean algebra of regular languages over the alphabet
\Sigma
, as described by Pippenger
[
46
]
and Almeida
[
4
, § 3.6]
.

Multiple aspects of the theory of regular languages of finite words, like the construction of profinite words, are fairly general. There have been multiple lines of research developing a more abstract theory of regular languages; we now describe two of them.

Monads

The first approach, developed by Bojańczyk in
[
13
]
and then with Klin and Salamanca in
[
15
]
, is the one using monads. Coming from category theory, monads provide an abstract way to deal with the notion of algebra by describing the free ones on given generators. There is a monad sending every set
\Sigma
on the set
\Sigma^{*}
of finite words whose letters belong to
\Sigma
; the algebras of this monad are exactly the monoids. The set
\Sigma^{*}
equipped with concatenation is then the free monoid, in the sense that any function
p:\Sigma\to M
from the set
\Sigma
to a monoid
M
can be uniquely extended to a monoid homomorphism
\bar{p}:\Sigma^{*}\to M
.

The notion of profinite word can also be reconstructed categorically using the notion of codensity monad, a specific kind of right Kan extension. Indeed, finite monoids induce a notion of profinite completion, sending any monoid
M
to its completion
\widehat{M}
which is a limit of finite monoids. In this way, the monoid of profinite words
\widehat{\Sigma^{*}}
can be recovered as the profinite completion of the free monoid
\Sigma^{*}
.

The
\lambda
-calculus

The second approach is based on the simply typed
\lambda
-calculus. For any finite set
\Sigma
, the Church encoding is a bijection between the set of simply typed
\lambda
-terms of type

\operatorname{Church}_{\Sigma}\quad:=\quad\underbrace{(\mathbbmss{o}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})}_{\text{first letter}}\ \Rightarrow\ \cdots\ \Rightarrow\ \underbrace{(\mathbbmss{o}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})}_{\text{last letter}}\ \Rightarrow\ \underbrace{\mathbbmss{o}}_{\text{input}}\ \Rightarrow\ \underbrace{\mathbbmss{o}}_{\text{output}}

and the set of words over the alphabet
\Sigma
. For example, if
\Sigma=\{a,b\}
, the word
abbab
is encoded as the following
\lambda
-term of type
\operatorname{Church}_{\{a,b\}}
, i.e.
(\mathbbmss{o}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})\Rightarrow(\mathbbmss{o}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}
:

\lambda(a:\mathbbmss{o}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}).\,\lambda(b:\mathbbmss{o}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}).\,\lambda(c:\mathbbmss{o}).\,b\ (a\ (b\ (b\ a\ c)))\ .

The Church encoding also relates how a word is read by an automaton with the semantic interpretation in finite sets of its encoding. This key observation led Salvati to introduce in
[
49
]
the notion of recognizable language of
\lambda
-terms using denotational semantics, which generalizes the notion of regular language to higher-order types like
\operatorname{Church}_{\Sigma}\Rightarrow\operatorname{Church}_{\Gamma}
. This generalization permits to consider languages of quantified boolean formulas, using abstraction to declare new atomic formulas, see
[
49
, § 3]
.

Inspired by the ideas of Salvati and the topological point of view on regular languages provided by profinite words, the author with collaborators defined in
[
28
]
the notion of
profinite
\lambda
-term
of any type, living in harmony with Stone duality and the principles of Reynolds parametricity. This generalizes profinite words, which are exactly the
profinite
\lambda
-terms
of type
\operatorname{Church}_{\Sigma}
:

Profinite trees

In this article, we want to unify the two general points of view given by monads and
\lambda
-calculus, and to show that the profinite trees coming from codensity monads are also
profinite
\lambda
-terms
. To do so, we consider
clones
which are multi-sorted algebras modelling the composition of contexts. These provide a suitable notion of algebra for finite ranked trees, close to the notion of preclone of
[
20
]
. We then work in the functor category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}=[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{N}}{}{},\mathbf{Set}]
of
signatures
, on which there is a monad
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}T}\,{}{}
whose algebras are exactly
clones
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
ranked alphabet

\{a_{1}:n_{1},\dots,a_{l}:n_{l}\}
. On the one hand,
\Sigma
can be seen as a
signature
from which we can build the free
clone

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma
, whose elements are trees with variables. As in the case of monoids, the notion of
locally finite clone
induces by codensity a profinite completion monad
\widehat{(-)}{}{}
on
clones
. Therefore, for every
ranked alphabet

\Sigma
, we get a
clone

\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}
whose elements we call
profinite trees
on the
signature

\Sigma
.

On the other hand, the
ranked alphabet

\Sigma
can be seen as a simple type and then induces a
clone

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{Church}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
of all the
\lambda
-terms which can be built out of
\Sigma
and variables. In the same way, we get a
clone

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{ProChurch}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
of all the
profinite
\lambda
-terms
which can be built out of
\Sigma
and variables.

These two approaches, via monads and
\lambda
-terms, are very different in spirit and rely on different structures. Yet, the Church encoding for finite trees, together with the preservation of the clone structure, states that the two clones
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma
and
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{Church}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
are isomorphic, cf.
26
.

In this paper, we introduce the notion of
profinite completion
of a clone and the notion of
profinite tree
resulting from it. The two main results of this paper are the following:

•

the
isomorphism theorem
of
Section

5.2
, stating that
\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}
and
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{ProChurch}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
are isomorphic as
\mathbf{Stone}
-enriched clones, as pictured in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{ProChurch}}}}(\Sigma){}{}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{Church}}}}(\Sigma){}{}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

•

the
parametricity theorem
of
Section

5.3
, from which we deduce that
parametric families
corresponds to the
profinite
\lambda
-terms
of
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{ProChurch}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
; it extends
[
28
, Theorem B]
in the case of types of the form
\operatorname{Church}_{\Sigma}
by removing the
definability
hypothesis.

Combined together, these two theorems show that three different definitions of
profinite trees
– i.e. via
codensity
,
profinite
\lambda
-terms
, and
parametricity
– actually coincide. In particular, this provides further evidence of the robustness of the notion of profinite
\lambda
-term recently introduced in
[
28
]
. These ideas take part in a more global convergence between automata theory and
\lambda
-calculus, through concepts and tools coming from category theory.

Our approach to proving the
isomorphism theorem
relies crucially on the notion of
bidefinability
, a strengthening of parametricity introduced in
Definition

39
. Then,
Lemma

41
relates
profinite trees
, defined in terms of
naturality
, to
bidefinability
, while
Lemma

31
relates
profinite
\lambda
-terms
to
bidefinable families
. Once both are translated in the same language, the isomorphism of
Section

5.2
follows in a natural way. Moreover, the
parametricity theorem
can also be understood as a
definability
result, and uses ideas coming from the literature relating first-order structures and
\lambda
-calculus
[
52
,
11
]
. Along the way, we show in
Section

2.3
how to encode monoid actions, which are crucial devices in automata theory, in the setting of
signatures
and
clones
.

1.1
Related work

Stone duality has tight links with automata theory, see
[
45
]
and
[
25
]
. Profinite words naturally appear in the Reiterman theorem for pseudovarieties, as proved in
[
47
]
and
[
9
]
, as sets of profinite equations determine pseudovarieties
[
4
]
. Profinite words have also been used to understand the limitedness problem in
[
50
]
and to show the decidability of weak MSO+U over infinite trees in
[
16
]
. They also appear in relationship with symbolic dynamics
[
5
]
. There is a celebrated line of research extending Stone duality to take into account monoid operations on the topological side and residuation operations on the algebraic side, see
[
26
,
27
]
, which inspired the introduction of
profinite
\lambda
-terms
in
[
28
]
.

There is a growing connection between automata theory and
\lambda
-calculus. Salvati has defined the notion of regular language of
\lambda
-terms in
[
49
]
, using semantic tools. In a more syntactic direction, Hillebrand and Kanellakis established in
[
30
]
a link between the regularity of a language and the
\lambda
-definability of its characteristic function. This idea is at the heart of the implicit automata program research by Nguyễn and Pradic started in
[
43
]
, which shows that Hillebrand and Kanellakis’ result can be adapted to get a correspondence between star-free languages and a substructural fragment of the simply typed
\lambda
-calculus, see
[
42
]
. These two directions, semantic and syntactic, yield two notions of languages of
\lambda
-terms of any type which have been shown to coincide in
[
41
]
.

A lot of different algebras can be considered for trees, see
[
14
]
for a survey. In
[
20
]
, Ésik and Weil have shown how preclones, i.e. non-symmetric operads, provide a suitable notion of algebras of trees. In
[
21
]
, they introduce their block product and use it to give a characterization of first-order definable tree languages. In this article, we use clones as we then obtain an
isomorphism theorem
, and not only a bijection between the sets of constants as in the case of operads.

The general approach to regular languages and profinite completions using monads began in
[
13
]
and was further studied in
[
15
]
. A profinite tree was already used in
[
12
]
, where it was defined as a Cauchy sequence of profinite trees. The link between metric completions and Stone duality is a fundamental aspect of
[
27
]
and was elaborated further in relation to Pervin spaces in
[
44
]
.

Codensity is the topic of abundant categorical literature, see e.g. in
[
36
,
8
,
18
]
where it provides a new point of view on already known monads. It has also been studied in relation to automata theory in a series of papers
[
17
,
51
,
3
]
.

Finally, this article uses tools coming from the tradition of abstract syntax, from the seminal article
[
24
]
by Fiore, Plotkin, and Turi to its most recent developments for second-order algebraic theories
[
22
,
23
]
and higher algebraic theories
[
7
]
.

1.2
Plan of the paper

In
Section

2
, we start by recalling the notion of
clone
before recalling how they arise as algebras for a monad on the category of
signatures
. In
Section

3
, we recall how the Church encoding yields the free
clone
over a
ranked alphabet
. In
Section

4
, we turn on to profiniteness and apply the notion of
codensity monad
to
clones
, from which one gets the notion of
profinite tree
. We then introduce the crucial notion of
bidefinability
and show, in
Lemma

41
, that
profinite trees
are
bidefinable
. In
Section

5
, we recall the notion of
profinite
\lambda
-terms
which are related to
bidefinability
by virtue of
Lemma

31
, before proving the
isomorphism theorem
and the
parametricity theorem
.

We write
\mathbf{Set}
for the category of sets and functions,

1
,
\times
and
\prod
for empty, binary and small products,

0

We write

Remark 4
.

We will use the notion of
\mathbf{Stone}
-enriched

are Stone spaces, and the functions

Definition 5
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a cartesian category and

be an object of
\mathbf{C}

{\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\operatorname{Endo}}}(c){}{}
for the

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(c){}{}_{n}\quad:=\quad\mathbf{C}(c^{n},c)\qquad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$,}\quad\text{where}\ c^{n}=\underbrace{c\times\dots\times c}_{\text{$n$ times}}

whose clone structure comes from the cartesian structure of

.

Remark 6
.

If
Q
is a finite set, the endofunction monoid

is the monoid whose composition represents the way automata run words. Transition functions of letters in a set
\Sigma
can be thought as a monoid morphism from
\Sigma^{*}
to

, which is sometimes called a semiautomaton.

More generally, a

can be understood as a way to combine abstract contexts with holes, generalizing the case of monoids whose elements can be thought of as
1

endomorphism clone

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(Q){}{}
is the clone whose composition corresponds to the one of tree automata. The transition functions associated with letters of a

from
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(Q){}{}

Definition 7

where the
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(-){}{}

2.2
Clones as algebras

In this section, we define the category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
and recall how
clones
can be seen at the same time as monoids in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
and as algebras for the monad
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}T}\,{}{}
. More details on these aspects can be found in
[
24
]
and
[
34
]
. One can find a presentation of monoidal categories in
[
40
, § 4]
.

Let
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{N}}{}{}
be the category whose objects are natural numbers and whose morphisms from
m
to
n
are functions
[m]\to[n]
. This category is a skeleton of
\mathbf{FinSet}
and is hence also the free strict cocartesian category on one object. We now introduce the category of
signatures
.

Definition 8
.

We write
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
for the functor category
[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{N}}{}{},\mathbf{Set}]
and refer to objects
X
of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
as
signatures
. For any natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n){}{}
for the
signature
given by

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{y}}(n){}{}_{m}\quad:=\quad\mathbf{FinSet}([n],[m])\ .

Remark 9
.

The category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
can be understood as the category of presheaves on
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{N}}{}{}}^{\operatorname{op}}
, which is the free strict cartesian category and admits a presentation in terms of (unityped) contexts and projections.

Remark 10
.

We follow in the footsteps of the tradition of abstract syntax, in particular, the seminal work
[
24
]
for variable binding. However, what we call a
signature
, i.e. the objects of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
, differs slightly from what the word denotes in this line of work. This is so because we think of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
as a category whose objects represent configurations of generators in our monadic approach to

. Yet, in the case of a
ranked alphabet

\Sigma
, the endofunctor
\Sigma\bullet(-)
of
Definition

12
below corresponds to the one in
[
24
, § 2]
by seeing
\Sigma
as a first-order signature.

Definition 11
.

For any
clone

C
, we write
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}U}\,{}{}C
for the
signature
defined as

({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}U}\,{}{}C)_{n}\ :=\ C_{n}\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}U}\,{}{}C)_{f}\ :=\ s_{m,n}(-,v_{n}\circ f)\quad\text{for $f:[m]\to[n]$.}

This defines a functor
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}U}\,{}{}:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
.

Following
[
34
, § 4]
, we remark that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
has the following monoidal product whose monoids can be identified with

.

Definition 12
.

If
X
and

are

, then we define their composition
X\bullet X^{\prime}
as

X\bullet X^{\prime}\quad:=\quad\int^{m\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{N}}{}{}}X_{m}\times{X^{\prime}}^{m}\qquad\text{where ${X^{\prime}}^{m}$ is the {\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}signature} $X^{\prime}\times\dots\times X^{\prime}$.}

This makes
({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{},\bullet,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(1){}{})
a monoidal category.

Remark 13
.

Intuitively, the composition
X\bullet X^{\prime}
can be understood as the

containing equivalence classes of formal substitutions, i.e. elements of the form

, for
x
in
X_{m}
and
x^{\prime}_{1}
, …,
x^{\prime}_{m}
in

.

More formally, the left Kan extension
\operatorname{Lan}:[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{N}}{}{},\mathbf{Set}]\to[\mathbf{Set},\mathbf{Set}]
along the inclusion
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{N}}{}{}\to\mathbf{Set}
is fully faithful and monoidal, i.e.
\operatorname{Lan}(X\bullet X^{\prime})
is the composition of functors
\operatorname{Lan}(X)\circ\operatorname{Lan}(X^{\prime})
, see
[
34
, § 4]
. Its essential image is the full subcategory of finitary functors.

In
[

, § 4]
, Kelly and Power state that, for any

such that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}U}\,{}{}C=X
is the same thing as a monoid structure on

in the monoidal category
({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{},\bullet,V)
, see also
[

, Proposition 3.4]
. We recall here their construction of the free

, seen as a monoid.

2.3
Encoding structures as signatures

We recall that if a functor is monoidal (strong) and has a right adjoint, then its right adjoint inherits a lax structure. This has originally been shown by Kelly in
[
32
]
, see also
[
40
, Proposition 13]
. This result dualizes to the case of a monoidal functor with a left adjoint.

The reformulation of
clones
as monoids in the monoidal category
({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{},\bullet,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(1){}{})
can be used to exhibit the following links with usual structures used in automata theory.

Any set

induces a

\coprod_{a\in A}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(1){}{}
, which amounts to see a set as a
signature
of
1
-hole contexts. This assignment is a monoidal functor from
\mathbf{Set}
to
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
, where
\mathbf{Set}
is endowed with its cartesian monoidal structure, whose monoids are the usual ones.

Any set
Q
also induces a
signature

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(1){}{}+\coprod_{q\in Q}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(0){}{}
, which represents a
1
-hole context together with all the elements of
Q
seen as constants, i.e.
0
-holes contexts. Again, this assignment is a monoidal functor from
\mathbf{Set}
to
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
, this time with
\mathbf{Set}
endowed with its cocartesian monoidal structure, for which each set has a unique monoid structure.

We now show how to factor these two encodings through a third one.

Definition 17
.

For
(Q,A)
and
(R,B)
two objects of
\mathbf{Set}
, we define the object

(Q,A)\ \immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\rtimes}{}{}\ (R,B)\quad:=\quad(Q+(A\times R)\,,\,A\times B)\ .

This extends to a bifunctor
\mathbf{Set}^{2}\times\mathbf{Set}^{2}\to\mathbf{Set}^{2}
which endows
\mathbf{Set}^{2}
with a monoidal structure whose unit is the object
(0,1)
.

Remark 18
.

This monoidal product on
\mathbf{Set}^{2}
is an instance of the square-zero extension of
[
38
, Definition 5.1]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, the link with monoid actions established in
Proposition

19
seems to be original.

Actions of finite monoids on finite sets appear in automata theory, generally as right actions under the name of transformation monoids. They are central to Krohn-Rhodes’ theorem, see
[
19
,
29
]
.

Proposition 19
.

The category of monoids in the monoidal category
(\mathbf{Set}^{2},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\rtimes}{}{},(0,1))
is isomorphic to the category of left monoid actions.

The proof is in
Appendix

C
. We now relate the monoidal categories
\mathbf{Set}^{2}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
through the following lax adjunction.

Proposition 20
.

Any pair of sets
(Q,A)
induces a
signature

\underline{Q,A}\quad:=\quad\coprod_{q\in Q}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(0){}{}\ +\ \coprod_{a\in A}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(1){}{}\ .

This induces a monoidal functor
\underline{(-)}:\mathbf{Set}^{2}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
, which has a right adjoint given by

\overline{X}\quad:=\quad(\,X_{0}\,,\,X_{0}+X_{1}\,)\ .

Together, they assemble into a lax adjunction
\underline{(-)}\dashv\overline{(-)}
.

The proof is in
Appendix

C
.

Remark 21
.

Using the equivalence described in
Remark

13
, the finitary endofunctor on
\mathbf{Set}
associated to
\underline{Q,A}
is the one sending any set
S
on the set
Q+(A\times S)
.

Observation 22
.

We remark that the two described ways to encode sets as
signatures
factor through
\mathbf{Set}^{2}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sig}}{}{}
, by sending

on the pair
(0,A)
and
Q
on the pair

. These two functors are monoidal and have retractions, which are right, resp. left adjoints.

The fact that a functor is lax means that it transport monoids of its domain into monoids of its codomain. In particular, a left action of

on

by
\underline{(-)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathbf{Set},\times,1)}}

3

\lambda
-terms

We consider the simply typed

.

Definition 23
.

We write
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
for the category whose objects are types
A
,
B
and whose morphisms from
A
to
B
are
\lambda
-terms of type

modulo
\beta\eta
-conversion.

Definition 24
.

A
ranked alphabet

\Sigma
is a finite sequence
[n_{1},\dots,n_{l}]
of natural numbers. Such a
ranked alphabet
induces both

•

a type, defined as
(\mathbbmss{o}^{n_{1}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})\times\dots\times(\mathbbmss{o}^{n_{l}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})

•

a
signature
, defined as
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n_{1}){}{}+\dots+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n_{l}){}{}
,

which we will also write
\Sigma
.

Definition 25
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
ranked alphabet
. We write
{\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\textsf{{Church}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
for the
endomorphism clone
of
\mathbbmss{o}
in the Kleisli category of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
associated to the reader monad
\Sigma\Rightarrow(-)
, i.e.

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{Church}}}}(\Sigma){}{}_{n}\quad\cong\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}){}{}\qquad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

As we now see, the free
clone
construction of
Definition

14
corresponds exactly to the
clone of endomorphisms
of the base type
\mathbbmss{o}
.

Claim 26

(The Church clone is the free clone)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
ranked alphabet
. The
clone

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{Church}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
together with the
signature
morphism

\Sigma\quad\longrightarrow\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}U}\,{}{}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{Church}}}}(\Sigma){}{}

is the free
clone
on the
ranked alphabet

\Sigma
seen as a
signature
.

Definition 27
.

We write
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{Tree}}{}{}
for the full subcategory of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
whose objects are
ranked alphabets
. Thus, morphisms in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Tree}}{}{}
from
\Sigma
to
\Gamma
are
\lambda
-terms of type
\Sigma\Rightarrow\Gamma
.

Remark 28
.

The category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Tree}}{}{}
is equivalent to the full subcategory of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
of types whose order is at most

. We prefer to work with
ranked alphabet
rather than types of order at most

in order to be able to apply
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}
to objects of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Tree}}{}{}
, see
Definition

24
.

In the category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Tree}}{}{}
, the type
\mathbbmss{o}
, induced by the
ranked alphabet

[0]
, is exponentiable. Indeed, the category that we write
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Tree}}{}{}
in this article corresponds to
\mathbf{M}
in
[
22
, § 4]
and to
\mathbb{L}_{2}(\{\mathbbmss{o}\})
in
[
7
, § 4.5]
, where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
corresponds to
\mathbb{L}_{\omega}(\{\mathbbmss{o}\})
.

For any finitary monad
T
on
\mathbf{Set}
, we have a distributive law
((-)+1)\circ T\to T\circ((-)+1)
. This gives a monad structure to
T\circ((-)+1)
, which has been shown to be the coproduct of

and
(-)+1
, see
[
31
, Corollary 3]
and also
[

,

]
. Through the equivalence described in

13
, this operation

on finitary monads corresponds exactly to the endofunctor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\delta}\,{}{}
on
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}
that we define below. We moreover show that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\delta}\,{}{}
has a left adjoint.

Proposition 29
.

For any clone
C
, we write
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\delta}\,{}{}C
for the clone defined by

({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\delta}\,{}{}C)_{n}\quad:=\quad C_{n+1}\qquad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Then, we have that

•

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\delta}\,{}{}C
is the coproduct of

with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(0){}{}

•

is a functor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}

Remark 37
.

By using the Yoneda lemma, we see that for any clone

As a consequence, functions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}(D,C)}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n){}{},C^{\prime})}}

Observation 38

(Key observation)
.

For

and any

has the type signature of a precomposition function

.

Definition 39
.

Let

, and

If

In these two cases, one can pick a representative in each isomorphism class and obtain a set to avoid size issues.

Proposition 40
.

For any

, if a family

For

5.1
Profinite

We now give a definition of

Definition 42
.

Let
A
be a type. A

\lambda
-term

\theta
of type

is a family
\theta_{Q}\in{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
such that for any two finite sets
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, there exists a
\lambda
-term
M\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
such that

\theta_{Q}\ =\ {\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta_{Q^{\prime}}\ =\ {\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q^{\prime}}\ .

We write
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\widehat{\Lambda}}(A){}{}
for the set of

\lambda
-terms
of type

.

Remark 43
.

In
[
28
, Definition 3.3]
,
profinite
\lambda
-terms
were introduced as elements of a limit of finite sets. There, it is remarked that this boils down to families of elements
\theta_{Q}\in{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
, where
Q
ranges over finite sets, such that the two following conditions hold:

•

for any finite set
Q
, there exists
M\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
such that
\theta_{Q}={\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}
,

•

for any finite sets
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
with
|Q^{\prime}|\geq|Q|
and
M\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
, if we have
\theta_{Q^{\prime}}={\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q^{\prime}}
, then
\theta_{Q}={\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}
.

It can easily be seen that these two conditions on
\theta
, when taken together, are equivalent to the one given in
Definition

42
.

We have seen at the end of
Section

4
in
Lemma

41
that
profinite trees
can be seen as
bidefinable families
over the class of all
locally finite clones
.
Lemma

31
makes a step in the opposite direction, as detailed in
33
, by showing that
profinite
\lambda
-terms
can be seen as
bidefinable families
on the class of all
endomorphism clones
of a finite set.

Definition 44
.

We write
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{ProLam}}{}{}
for the category whose objects are types

,

to

are

\lambda
-terms
of type

.

As proved in
[

, §5]
,

is a cartesian closed category. We now consider the following definition, analogous to
Definition

where
\lambda
-terms are replaced by

\lambda
-terms
.

Definition 45

Let

. We write
{\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\textsf{{ProChurch}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
for the

of

in the Kleisli category of

Let

(From trees to

.

Let

over

with

This defines a

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}r}{}{}

\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}

The proof is in
Appendix

E
. To obtain a

\lambda
-term
from a

, we only need to restrict from all

to those of the form
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(Q){}{}
for a finite set

. To go the other way around, we define the following function.

Definition 47
.

Let

be a
clone
and

. The application of a function

to the variables

induces a function

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\operatorname{appvar}}{}{}\quad:\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n){}{},{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(C_{n}){}{})\ \longrightarrow\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n){}{},C)

which, by definition of

, is a retraction of

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{cay}}}{}{}^{n}\circ(-)\quad:\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n){}{},C)\ \longrightarrow\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{y}}(n){}{},{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(C_{n}){}{})\ .

(From
\lambda
-terms to trees)
.

Let
\theta
be a
profinite
\lambda
-term
of type
\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}
. Then,
\theta
induces a

over
\Sigma
with

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma,C)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Clone}}{}{}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(C_{n}){}{})}}

E
. Each

(

)
.

For any

\Sigma
, the

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}r}{}{}\quad:\quad\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{ProChurch}}}}(\Sigma){}{}

The proof is in
Appendix

.

5.3
The parametricity theorem

Let
Q
be a finite set. The semantic interpretation of the
\lambda
-calculus in
\mathbf{FinSet}
associates to every type
A
a set
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
, and to every
\lambda
-term
M
of type
A
an element
{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}\in{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
. We now describe another semantic interpretation. Let
R\subseteq Q\times Q^{\prime}
be a relation between two finite sets. For any type
A
, we have a relation

{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{R}\quad\subseteq\quad{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}\times{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q^{\prime}}

whose definition is recalled in
Appendix

A
. The fundamental lemma of logical relations then states that, for every
\lambda
-term
M
of type
A
,

({\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q},{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q^{\prime}})\quad\in\quad{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{R}\ .

Definition 50
.

Let
A
be any type. A
parametric family
of type
A
is a family of elements
\rho_{Q}\in{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
, where
Q
ranges over all finite sets, such that for any relation
R\subseteq Q\times Q^{\prime}
, we have
(\rho_{Q}\,,\,\rho_{Q^{\prime}})\in{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{R}
.

It was shown in
[
28
, Theorem B]
that every
profinite
\lambda
-term
is a
parametric family
. Moreover, every
parametric family
whose components are all
definable
are automatically
bidefinable
, due to
[
28
, Proposition 3.2]
. Therefore, a
parametric family
whose components are
definable
is a
profinite
\lambda
-term
.

We first introduce a
\lambda
-term for each element of the
ranked alphabet

\Sigma
, which we call its
generators
. These terms have already appeared in the literature studying
\lambda
-definability, e.g. in
[
11
, Definition 5.2]
and as
\bar{C}_{i}
in
[
52
, p. 5]
.

Definition 51
.

Let
\Sigma=[n_{1},\dots,n_{l}]
be a ranked alphabet.
For each
1\leq i\leq n
, we define the
i^{\text{th}}

generator
of
\Sigma
, written
{\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}g}_{i}}{}{}
, as the term

\displaystyle{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{i}}{}{}\quad

\displaystyle:\quad(\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})^{n_{i}}\Rightarrow\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}

\displaystyle{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{i}}{}{}\quad

\displaystyle:=\quad\lambda(t:(\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})^{n_{i}}).\,\lambda(\sigma:\Sigma).\ \sigma.i\ (t.1\ \sigma)\ \dots\ (t.n_{i}\ \sigma)

We now state a partial converse to
[
28
, Theorem B]
, in the case of the type of morphisms of the category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Tree}}{}{}
, hence including the type
\operatorname{Church}_{\Sigma}\,=\,\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}
.

Theorem 52

(
Parametricity theorem
)
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
be two
ranked alphabets
. Every
parametric family

\rho
of semantic elements of type
\Sigma\Rightarrow\Gamma
is a
profinite
\lambda
-term
of that type.

The proof is in
Appendix

F
. It relies on proving an analog of the fixed point equation of
[
11
, Corollary 5.7]
, originally in the setting of System F, for
parametric families
. From this equation, we deduce that every component of the
parametric family
is
definable
.

6
Conclusion and future work

In this article, we started in
Section

2
by recalling some fundamental aspects of
clones
, like their monadicity over
signatures
. In
Section

4
, we then turn to the definitions of the
profinite completion
of
clones
and on
profinite trees
, before showing in
Section

5
the
isomorphism theorem
and the
parametricity theorem
, which prove together that
profinite trees
coincide with
profinite
\lambda
-terms
and
parametric families
.

We would like to describe the following ideas for future work:

•

We have only considered here a single base type
\mathbbmss{o}
. It would be interesting to extend the
isomorphism theorem
to the case of multiple base types, which corresponds to the case of typed holes in trees, and which should be the colors of the cartesian multicategories. This should make it possible to encode more structures using colored variants of
ranked alphabets
, as exemplified in
[
11
, § 1.4]
.

•

The clones
\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}
and
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{ProChurch}}}}(\Sigma){}{}
carry a profinite topology. Following
[
3
]
, one could want to recast the free profinite clone monad on the category
\mathbf{Pro}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{FinSig}}{}{})
from which one could get the topology afterward. It would be interesting to find a concrete description of
\mathbf{Pro}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{FinSig}}{}{})
, along the lines of
\mathbf{Pro}(\mathbf{FinSet})
as
\mathbf{Stone}
.

•

The monoid structure of the free profinite monoid
\widehat{\Sigma^{*}}
on a set
\Sigma
has been shown in
[
26
,
27
]
to be dual to the residuation operations of the Boolean algebra of regular languages. Given that
clones
generalize monoids, it might be asked if the
clone
structure can be understood as a topological operation dual to one of algebraic nature.

•

Finally, one could wonder if a similar story would hold in the case of a monad of
\lambda
-terms, e.g. as described in
[
53
]
, and if an associated codensity monad would coincide with the notion of
profinite
\lambda
-terms
introduced in
[
28
]
.

Appendix A
About the
\lambda
-calculus

In this article, we consider the simply typed
\lambda
-calculus on one base type
\mathbbmss{o}
. For the convenience of the reader, we now recall the definitions of the simply typed
\lambda
-calculus, and point to
[
6
, Chapter 4]
for more details.

Syntax of the
\lambda
-calculus

The syntax of types is given by the inductive definition

A,B\quad::=\quad\mathbbmss{o}\mid A\Rightarrow B\mid A\times B\mid 1

The syntax of preterms is given by the inductive definition

M,N\quad::=\quad x\mid\lambda(x:A).\,M\mid M\ N\mid\texttt{pair}(M,N)\mid M.1\mid M.2\mid\texttt{()}

Notice that
\lambda
-abstraction is annotated with a type. If
M
and
N
are preterms, we write
M[x:=N]
for the substitution of
N
for
x
in
M
, without capture of free variables, cf.
[
10
, 2.1.15]
. We call context any list of typed variables

\Gamma\quad=\quad x_{1}:A_{1}\ ,\ \dots\ ,\ x_{n}:A_{n}

Given a context
\Gamma
, a preterm
M
and a type
A
, we define an inductive judgment

\Gamma\vdash M:A

whose rules are the following:

\frac{(x:A)\ \in\ \Gamma}{\Gamma\vdash x:A}\qquad\frac{\Gamma,x:A\vdash M:B}{\Gamma\vdash\lambda(x:A).M:A\Rightarrow B}\qquad\frac{\Gamma\vdash M:B\Rightarrow A\quad\Gamma\vdash N:B}{\Gamma\vdash M\ N:A}

\frac{\Gamma\vdash M:A_{1}\quad\Gamma\vdash N:A_{2}}{\Gamma\vdash\texttt{pair}(M,N):A_{1}\times A_{2}}\qquad\frac{\Gamma\vdash M:A_{1}\times A_{2}}{\Gamma\vdash M.i:A_{i}}\ (\text{for $i=1,2$})\qquad\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash\texttt{()}:1}

The fact that the
\lambda
-abstractions are annotated by types makes it so that, for a given context
\Gamma
and preterm
M
, there exists at most one type
A
such that the judgment
\Gamma\vdash M:A
can be derived.

We now define the notion of
\beta\eta
-conversion under the form of an inductive judgment

\Gamma\vdash M=_{\beta\eta}N:A

whose rules are the following

\frac{\Gamma,x:A\vdash M:B\quad\Gamma\vdash N:A}{\Gamma\vdash(\lambda(x:A).M)N=_{\beta\eta}M[x:=N]:B}\qquad\frac{\Gamma\vdash M_{1}:A_{1}\quad\Gamma\vdash M_{2}:A_{2}}{\Gamma\vdash\texttt{pair}(M_{1},M_{2}).i=_{\beta\eta}M_{i}:A_{i}}\ (\text{for $i=1,2$})

\frac{\Gamma\vdash M:A\Rightarrow B}{\Gamma\vdash M=_{\beta\eta}\lambda(x:A).M\,x:A\Rightarrow B}\quad\frac{\Gamma\vdash M:A_{1}\times A_{2}}{\Gamma\vdash M=_{\beta\eta}\langle M.1,M.2\rangle:A_{1}\times A_{2}}\quad\frac{\Gamma\vdash M:1}{\Gamma\vdash M=_{\beta\eta}\texttt{()}:1}

together with congruence rules. For any context
\Gamma
and type
A
, this judgment induces an equivalence relation

\Gamma\vdash(-)=_{\beta\eta}(-):A

on preterms of type
A
in context
\Gamma
. For any type
A
, we then define

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\Lambda}(A){}{}\quad:=\quad\{\text{preterm $M$ s.t. $\varnothing\vdash M:A$}\}/(\varnothing\vdash(-)=_{\beta\eta}(-):A)

and say that a simply typed
\lambda
-term of type
A
is an element of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
, i.e. an equivalence class of
\beta\eta
-convertible closed preterms of type
A
.

Semantics of the
\lambda
-calculus

We now describe the semantics of the simply typed
\lambda
-calculus in the category
\mathbf{FinSet}
of finite sets and functions between them. Let
Q
be a finite set which we use to interpret the base type
\mathbbmss{o}
. For every type
A
, we define a finite set
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
by induction on
A
as follows:

{\llbracket\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}:=Q\qquad{\llbracket A\Rightarrow B\rrbracket}_{Q}:={\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}\Rightarrow{\llbracket B\rrbracket}_{Q}\qquad{\llbracket A\times B\rrbracket}_{Q}:={\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}\times{\llbracket B\rrbracket}_{Q}\qquad{\llbracket 1\rrbracket}_{Q}:=\{*\}

where, for
X
and
Y
two sets, we write
X\Rightarrow Y
for the set of functions from
X
to
Y
. We extend this assignment to any context
\Gamma=x_{1}:A_{1},\dots,x_{n}:A_{n}
by defining

{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}_{Q}\quad:=\quad{\llbracket A_{1}\rrbracket}_{Q}\times\dots\times{\llbracket A_{n}\rrbracket}_{Q}\ .

To any preterm
M
such that
\Gamma\vdash M:A
, we associate a function

{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}\quad:\quad{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}_{Q}\ \longrightarrow\ {\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}

defined by induction on
M
in the following way: for every
\bar{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\in{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}_{Q}
,

\displaystyle{\llbracket x_{i}\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\quad

\displaystyle:=\quad q_{i}

\displaystyle{\llbracket\lambda(x:A).M\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\quad

\displaystyle:=\quad q_{n+1}\mapsto{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q},q_{n+1})

\displaystyle{\llbracket M\ N\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\quad

\displaystyle:=\quad{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})({\llbracket N\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q}))

\displaystyle{\llbracket\texttt{pair}(M,N)\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\quad

\displaystyle:=\quad(\,{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\,,\,{\llbracket N\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\,)

\displaystyle{\llbracket M.i\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\quad

\displaystyle:=\quad\pi_{i}({\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q}))\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{for $i=1,2$}

\displaystyle{\llbracket\texttt{()}\rrbracket}_{Q}(\bar{q})\quad

\displaystyle:=\quad*

This interpretation is such that, for any two preterms
M
and
N
,

\text{if}\quad\Gamma\vdash M=_{\beta\eta}N:A\ ,\qquad\text{then}\quad{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}={\llbracket N\rrbracket}_{Q}\ .

Therefore, for every type
A
, the interpretation lifts to a function from
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
to
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
.

If
\Gamma
is a context and
A
a type, then a function

f\quad:\quad{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}_{Q}\ \longrightarrow\ {\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}

is said to be
\lambda
-definable if there exists
M
such that
\Gamma\vdash M:A
and
f={\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}
. Even though the semantic interpretation is taken into finite sets, the problem of knowing whether some function
f
is
\lambda
-definable is undecidable, as shown by Loader in
[
37
]
.

This semantic interpretation does not depend on the notion of finite set and can be understood more abstractly. A category
\mathbf{C}
is cartesian closed if it has finite cartesian products and every object is exponential, i.e. for every object
c
of
\mathbf{C}
, the functor
c\times(-):\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}
has a right adjoint
c\Rightarrow(-):\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}
. Cartesian closed categories provide a general way to define the semantic interpretation.

We write
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
for the category whose objects are types
A
,
B
and whose set of morphisms from
A
to
B
is
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A\Rightarrow B){}{}
. The category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
is the free cartesian closed category on one object, represented as the base type
\mathbbmss{o}
. This means that for any cartesian closed category
\mathbf{C}
and object
c
of
\mathbf{C}
, there exists a unique cartesian closed functor
{\llbracket-\rrbracket}_{c}
from
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Lam}}{}{}
to
\mathbf{C}
sending
\mathbbmss{o}
on

. This is represented by the diagram

Instead of
\mathbf{FinSet}
, we now consider the category
\mathbf{FinRel}
. Its objects are tuples
(X,Y,R)
such that
X
and
Y
are finite sets and
R\subseteq X\times Y
. Its morphisms from
(X,Y,R)
to
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},R^{\prime})
are pairs
(f,g)
of functions
f:X\to X^{\prime}
and
g:Y\to Y^{\prime}
such that for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
,

\text{if}\quad(x,y)\ \in\ R\ ,\quad\text{then}\quad(f(x),g(y))\ \in\ R^{\prime}\ .

This category
\mathbf{FinRel}
is a cartesian closed category, whose product is computed pointwise and whose exponential of
(X,Y,R)
and
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},R^{\prime})
is the relation

\{(f,g)\ \mid\ \forall(x,y)\in R,(f(x),g(y))\in R^{\prime}\}\quad\subseteq\quad(X\Rightarrow X^{\prime})\times(Y\Rightarrow Y^{\prime})

Therefore, we can use the abstract interpretation available for any cartesian closed category. For any relation
R\subseteq X\times Y
and every
\Gamma\vdash M:A
, we have two objects
{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}_{R}
and
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{R}
of
\mathbf{FinRel}
and a morphism

{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{R}\quad:\quad{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}_{R}\ \longrightarrow\ {\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{R}

To each object
(X,Y,R)
of
\mathbf{FinRel}
, we can associate the finite set
X
and the finite set
Y
, and these assignments respect the products and exponentials. More formally, we have two cartesian closed functors

\begin{matrix}\mathbf{FinRel}&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{FinSet}\\
(X,Y,R)&\longmapsto&X\end{matrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{matrix}\mathbf{FinRel}&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{FinSet}\\
(X,Y,R)&\longmapsto&Y\end{matrix}\ .

The existence of these cartesian closed functors shows that, for every type
A
, the tuple
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{R}
is actually of the form

({\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{X}\,,\,{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Y}\,,\,R^{A})\qquad\text{for some $R^{A}\ \subseteq\ {\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{X}\times{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Y}$.}

In
Section

5.3
, we write
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{R}
for the relation
R^{A}
. Then, for
M\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
, the same argument shows that

({\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{X}\,,\,{\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Y})\quad\in\quad R^{A}\ .

This property is called the fundamental lemma of logical relations.

Appendix B
About codensity monads

We recall here the notion of
codensity monad
, as described for example in
[
36
, § 2 and § 5]
.
Codensity monads
are closely related to Isbell duality, see
[
18
]

Definition 53
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
be categories and
G:\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{A}
be a functor. The
codensity monad
associated to
G
, if it exists, is the right Kan extension of
G
along itself:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

The monad structure comes from the universal property of the right Kan extension.

Example 54
.

•

If
F\dashv G
is an adjoint pair, then the
codensity monad
of
G
is
G\circ F
.

•

The codensity monad of the inclusion
\mathbf{FinSet}\to\mathbf{Set}
is the ultrafilter monad, see
[
35
]
.

•

The codensity monad of the inclusion of convex sets into measurable spaces is the Giry monad, sending a measurable space on the space of all probability measures on it
[
8
]
.

may not exist in general. However, there are some conditions on

, we now state such conditions, which will be verified in the setting of

U_{p,S}\quad:=\quad\left\{u\in\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}_{n}\mid u_{C}(p)\in S\right\}

The topology on
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\widehat{\Lambda}}(\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}){}{}
is the topology induced by the inclusion in the product

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\widehat{\Lambda}}(\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}){}{}\quad\subseteq\quad\prod_{Q}{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}

More concretely, it has a subbase whose open sets are of the form, for
Q
a finite set and
T
a subset of
{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}
,

V_{T}\quad:=\quad\left\{\theta\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\widehat{\Lambda}}(\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}){}{}\mid\theta_{Q}\in T\right\}\ .

We now verify that, for
u\in\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}_{n}
,
Q
any finite set and
T\subseteq{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}r}{}{}_{n}(u)\in V_{T}\quad

\displaystyle\iff\quad u_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(Q){}{}}\in V_{T}

\displaystyle\iff\quad\exists f\in T,u_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(Q){}{}}=f

\displaystyle\iff\quad\exists f\in T,\forall p\in{\llbracket\Sigma\rrbracket}_{Q},u_{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Endo}}}(Q){}{}}(p)=f(p)

\displaystyle\iff\quad\exists f\in T,\forall p\in{\llbracket\Sigma\rrbracket}_{Q},u\in U_{p,\{f(p)\}}

\displaystyle\iff\quad u\in\bigcup_{f\in T}\bigcap_{p\in{\llbracket\Sigma\rrbracket}_{Q}}U_{p,\{f(p)\}}\ .

Therefore,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}r}{}{}^{-1}(V_{T})
is a finite union of finite intersections of
U_{p,S}
, so it is an open set of
\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}_{n}
. This proves that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}r}{}{}_{n}\quad:\quad\widehat{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}F}\,{}{}\Sigma}{}{}_{n}\ \longrightarrow\ {\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\widehat{\Lambda}}(\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}^{n}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}){}{}

is continuous, so it is a homeomorphism. Therefore,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}r}{}{}
is an isomorphism of
\mathbf{Stone}
-enriched clones.

Appendix F
Proof of the
parametricity theorem

Let
\rho
be a
parametric family
of type
\Sigma\Rightarrow\Gamma
. The proof amounts to show the
bidefinability
, in the sense of
Definition

42
, of the family
\rho
. As
\rho
is parametric, using
[
28
, Theorem B]
it suffices to show that for each finite set
Q
,
\rho_{Q}
is
\lambda
-definable, i.e. there exists
M\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(\Sigma\Rightarrow\Gamma){}{}
such that
\rho_{Q}={\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}
. More generally, for any type
A
the subset of
\lambda
-definable elements of
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}
is written

{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}^{\bullet}\quad:=\quad\{q\in{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}\mid\exists M\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}\text{ s.t. }q={\llbracket M\rrbracket}_{Q}\}\quad\subseteq\quad{\llbracket A\rrbracket}_{Q}\ .

If
\Gamma
is of the form
(\mathbbmss{o}^{m_{1}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})\times\dots\times(\mathbbmss{o}^{m_{k}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})
, then it suffices to show that each
parametric family

\rho^{j}
of type
(\Sigma\times\mathbbmss{o}^{m_{j}})\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}
defined as

\rho^{j}_{Q}\quad:=\quad\lambda^{-1}_{Q^{m_{j}}}(\pi_{j}\circ\rho_{Q})\ \in\ {\llbracket(\Sigma\times\mathbbmss{o}^{m_{j}})\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}

is
\lambda
-definable. Therefore, without loss of generality, we consider the case where
\Gamma=\mathbbmss{o}
, i.e.
\rho
is a
parametric family
of type
\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}
.

Let us consider
\Sigma=(\mathbbmss{o}^{n_{1}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})\dots(\mathbbmss{o}^{n_{l}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o})
and
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{1}}{}{},\dots,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{l}}{}{}
the associated
generators
.

Let
Q
be a finite set. To establish that
\rho_{Q}
is
\lambda
-definable, we first show that

\immediate\immediate\rho_{Q}\quad=\quad\rho_{{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}}\left({\llbracket g_{1}\rrbracket}_{Q},\dots,{\llbracket g_{l}\rrbracket}_{Q}\right)

(G)

This is very close in spirit to the fixed point equation of
[
11
, Corollary 5.7]
.

To show that
Equation

G
holds, we show that the two functions are equal on all inputs. For this purpose, let
\bar{f}:=(f_{1},\dots,f_{l})\in{\llbracket\Sigma\rrbracket}_{Q}
be any such input.

R_{\bar{f}}\quad:=\quad\{(q,h)\mid h(\bar{f})=q\}\ \subseteq\ Q\times{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}

For any
1\leq i\leq l
, we have

(f_{i}\,,\,{\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{i}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q})\quad\in\quad{\llbracket\mathbbmss{o}^{n_{i}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{R_{\bar{f}}}

from which we deduce that

((f_{1},\dots,f_{l})\,,\,({\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{1}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q},\dots,{\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{l}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q}))\quad\in\quad{\llbracket\Sigma\rrbracket}_{R_{\bar{f}}}\ .

As
\rho
is a
parametric family
, we know that

(\rho_{Q}\,,\,\rho_{{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}})\quad\in\quad{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{R_{\bar{f}}}

and we thus obtain that

(\rho_{Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{l})\,,\,\rho_{{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}}({\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{1}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q},\dots,{\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{l}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q}))\quad\in\quad R_{\bar{f}}

which boils down to

\rho_{Q}(f_{1},\dots,f_{l})\quad=\quad\rho_{{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}}({\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{1}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q},\dots,{\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{l}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q})(f_{1},\dots,f_{l})\ .

As this holds for any
\bar{f}
, we therefore get that
Equation

G
holds.

Moreover,
parametric families
are parametric with respect to unary predicates on
Q
as they can be encoded as relations between
\{*\}
and
Q
. We consider the definability predicate

D\quad:=\quad{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}^{\bullet}\ \subseteq\ {\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}\ .

For any
1\leq i\leq l
, we have

{\llbracket g_{i}\rrbracket}_{Q}\quad\in\quad{\llbracket\mathbbmss{o}^{n_{i}}\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{D}

from which we deduce

({\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{1}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q},\dots,{\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{l}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q})\quad\in\quad{\llbracket\Sigma\rrbracket}_{D}\ .

As
\rho
is a
parametric family
, we have that

\rho_{{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}}\quad\in\quad{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{D}

from which we deduce that

\rho_{{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}}({\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{1}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q},\dots,{\llbracket{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}g}_{l}}{}{}\rrbracket}_{Q})\quad\in\quad D\ .

By
Equation

G
, we therefore obtain that

\rho_{Q}\quad\in\quad{\llbracket\Sigma\Rightarrow\mathbbmss{o}\rrbracket}_{Q}^{\bullet}\ .

This finishes the proof that
\rho
is a
profinite
\lambda
-term
.

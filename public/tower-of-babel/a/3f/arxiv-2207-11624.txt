On asymptotic packing of convex geometric and ordered graphs
Jiaxi Nie, Erlang Surya, Ji Zeng

Abstract
A convex geometric graph G G is said to be packable if there exist edge-disjoint copies of G G in the complete convex geometric graph K n K_{n} covering all but o ⁡ ( n 2 ) o(n^{2}) edges. We prove that every convex geometric graph with cyclic chromatic number at most 4 4 is packable. With a similar definition of packability for ordered graphs, we prove that every ordered graph with interval chromatic number at most 3 3 is packable. Arguments based on the average length of edges imply these results are best possible. We also identify a class of convex geometric graphs that are packable due to having many “long” edges.

Introduction

For two graphs

-packing of

. Clearly,

. When equality holds, we call the corresponding collection a

-packing
of

. Due to a well-known result of Kirkman
[
13
]
,

has a perfect combinatorial

-packing if and only if

or

. More generally, Wilson
[
21
]
proved that, for

has a perfect combinatorial

-packing if and only if

divides

and the greatest common divisor of the vertex degrees of

divides

. This result is also generalized to hypergraphs
[
10

]
.

Although a perfect combinatorial

-packing of the complete graph

doesn’t always exist, we can pack any

into complete graphs “almost perfectly”. Let us call a graph

combinatorially packable
if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{p(K_{n},G)|E(G)|}{|E(K_{n})|}=1.

In other words,

is combinatorially packable if there exist combinatorial

-packings of

covering all but
o(n^{2})
edges. Using a technique known as Rödl Nibble (see Theorem

), one can easily show that all fixed graphs are combinatorially packable. See
[
22
]
for a survey on combinatorial packing problems.

In this paper, we consider the packability of convex geometric graphs. A
convex geometric graph
(shortly

)

on its vertex set

. Equivalently, it can be represented as a graph drawn in the plane such that

consists of points in convex position,

consists of straight-line segments connecting the corresponding points, and

is the clockwise ordering of

. Two cgg’s

and
H
are

if there is a graph isomorphism

between them preserving the cyclic order, that is,

if and only if
f(u)\prec f(v)\prec f(w)
. Note that any two complete cgg’s of the same size are isomorphic, we use
K_{n}
to denote a complete cgg on
n
vertices. For cgg’s
G
and
H
, a
convex
G
-packing
(shortly
G
-packing
) of

covering all but
o(n^{2})

In contrast to combinatorial packability, there exist cgg’s that are not packable, for example, the plane cycle

of length

. To see why

isn’t packable, we can consider the average length of edges. Inside a cgg

is a subset that is contiguous with respect to

of an edge

is one less than the smallest cardinality of an interval containing

. One can check that the average length of all edges in

is

. On the other hand, the average length of the edges in a copy of

is at most

, so the average length of all edges covered by a

-packing of

is at most

. Hence, a

-packing can never cover all but

.

.

Let

Theorem

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

, then

Theorem

2
Fractional packing of weighted convex geometric graphs

In this section, we prove an auxiliary lemma which relates the packability of a given cgg to a fractional packing problem of an associated weighted cgg. We introduce some notions before we state and prove this lemma.

A
weighted cgg
is a cgg
G
together with a function
\omega_{G}:E(G)\to{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
. A weighted sub-cgg
G
of
H
is a weighted cgg whose underlying cgg structure is a sub-cgg of
H
. An unweighted cgg
H
can be seen as a weighted cgg with each edge having weight
1
. An isomorphism
f:G\to H
between two weighted cgg’s is a cgg-isomorphism such that
\omega_{G}(e)=\omega_{H}(f(e))
for all
e\in E(G)
. We use
\mathcal{I}(H,G)
to denote the set of all weighted sub-cgg’s of
H
that are isomorphic to
G
. A
perfect fractional
G
-packing
of
H
is a function
\phi:\mathcal{I}(H,G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{I}(H,G)}\phi(S)\omega_{S}(e)=\omega_{H}(e)\text{ for all $e\in E(H)$}.

(2.1)

Here, we take the convention that
\omega_{S}(e)=0
if
e\not\in E(S)
.

For an unweighted cgg
G
and an interval partition
\mathcal{P}=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{k}\}
of

with each edge having its endpoints in different parts, we described in Section
1
that

has an induced cyclic order. A
weighted representation
of

(constructed from

) is a complete weighted cgg

with vertex set
\mathcal{P}
and edge weight given by

\omega_{W}(\{V_{i},V_{j}\})=|E_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})|\text{ for every $i\neq j\in[k]$}.

Proof of Theorem

Let

Orthogonality relations for conical functions of imaginary order
Job Feldbrugge a and Nynke M.D. Niezink b

Abstract
Orthogonality relations for conical or Mehler functions of imaginary order are derived and expressed in terms of the Dirac delta function. This work extends recently derived orthogonality relations of associated Legendre functions.

Introduction

The associated Legendre (or Ferrers) functions of the first kind
P_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)
and second kind
Q_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)
, with the complex degree
\lambda
and complex order
\mu
, are generalizations of the Legendre polynomial
P_{l}(x)
, where the degree
\lambda=l
is an integer and the order vanishes, and the associated Legendre polynomial
P_{l}^{m}(x)
, where the degree
\lambda=l
is an integer and the order
\mu=m
is an integer with
|m|\leq l
. The Legendre polynomial and function are widely used in applied mathematics and theoretical physics. The polynomial, for example, appears in the study of the Newtonian potential
[
1
]
, spherical harmonics, and the energy eigenstates of the hydrogen model in quantum mechanics as in,
e.g.
, the textbook
[
2
]
. Its generalization to complex degrees and orders plays a pivotal role in many applications including the (generalized) Mehler-Fock transform
[
3
]
. The Legendre function
P_{\lambda}^{\mu}
with imaginary order
\mu
arises in the study of modified Pöschl-Teller potentials in quantum mechanics
[
4
]
, backscattering of radiation in plasmas
[
5
]
, topological black holes
[
6
]
, two-particle Hamiltonians
[
7
]
, and Yang-Mills matrix models
[
8
]
.

The associated Legendre polynomial satisfies the orthogonality condition

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{l}^{m}(x)P_{l}^{n}(x)}{1-x^{2}}dx=\frac{(l+m)!}{m(l-m)!}\delta_{m,n}\,,

(1)

for
m,n\neq 0
. Kalmykov and Shvets
[
5
]
found a similar relation for the associated Legendre function of the first kind with imaginary order,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{1}^{iq}(x)P_{1}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2\sinh(\pi q)}{q}\delta(q-q^{\prime})\,,

(2)

with real
q,q^{\prime}
. Hutasiot
et al.

[
6
]
generalized this relation to integer degree,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{l}^{iq}(x)P_{l}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2\sinh(\pi q)}{q}\delta(q-q^{\prime})\,,

(3)

for
l=0,1,2,\dots
. Finally, by an elegant algebraic derivation, Bielski
[
9
]
demonstrated that these identities are special cases of more general orthogonality relations. In particular, Bielski evaluated the integrals

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,\quad\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)Q_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,\quad\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{Q_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)Q_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,

(4)

for general complex degree
\lambda
, in terms of the Dirac delta functions
\delta(q-q^{\prime})
and
\delta(q+q^{\prime})
.

In this paper, we evaluate the related orthogonality relations for the conical functions,

\displaystyle I_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)^{*}}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,\qquad J_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(-x)^{*}}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,

(5)

\displaystyle I_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)Q_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)^{*}}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,\qquad J_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)Q_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(-x)^{*}}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,

(6)

\displaystyle I_{3}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{Q_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)Q_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)^{*}}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,\qquad J_{3}^{q,q^{\prime}}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{Q_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)Q_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(-x)^{*}}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,,

(7)

when
\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}+i\nu
for real
\nu
, in terms of the Dirac delta functions
\delta(q-q^{\prime})
and
\delta(q+q^{\prime})
, following the derivation described in Bielski
[
9
]
. We evaluate the
I_{i}^{q,q^{\prime}}
integrals from Bielski’s result (
4
), and use these to derive the
J_{i}^{q,q^{\prime}}
integrals.

The Legendre function with degree
\text{Re}[\lambda]=-\frac{1}{2}
is known as the conical or Mehler function, first analyzed in the study of conics in electrostatics
[
10
]
. The conical function plays a prominent role in the Mehler-Fock transformation and, with imaginary degree, forms the energy eigenstates of the modified Pöschl-Teller or Rosen-Morse barrier in quantum mechanics
[
11
]
. For a detailed study of the conical function with imaginary order, see
[
12
]
.

These orthogonality relations are of special interest when normalizing the continuum spectrum of the modified Pöschl-Teller model in quantum mechanics. The relations also apply to many other physical models (see,
e.g.
,
[
8
]
), as the inner product in quantum mechanics includes a complex conjugation,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1}|\psi_{2}\rangle=\int\psi_{1}(x)^{*}\psi_{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\,,

(8)

using Dirac’s bra–ket notation.

2
Relevant properties of the associated Legendre function

To aid the evaluation of the integrals (
5
)–(
7
), we briefly summarize several useful properties of associated Legendre functions. The associated Legendre functions
P_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)
and
Q_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)
are solutions of the general Legendre equation

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[(1-x^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}w(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}\right]+\left[\lambda(\lambda+1)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{1-x^{2}}\right]w(x)=0\,,

(9)

and are related by the equation

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2\sin(\pi\mu)}\left(P_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)\cos(\pi\mu)-\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+\mu+1)}{\Gamma(\lambda-\mu+1)}P_{\lambda}^{-\mu}(x)\right)\,,

(10)

where
\Gamma
denotes the gamma function
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
(
e.g.
, equation
(14.9.2)
in
[
17
]
). The Legendre function of the first kind is often expressed as

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\mu)}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^{\mu/2}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(-\lambda,\lambda+1;1-\mu,\frac{1-x}{2}\right)\,,\quad-1<x<1,

(11)

in terms of the hypergeometric function

\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c,x)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+n)\Gamma(b+n)}{\Gamma(c+n)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,,

(12)

(
e.g.
, equation
(14.3.1)
in
[
17
]
). Moreover, the Legendre function satisfies the reflection condition
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}^{\mu}(-x)

\displaystyle=P_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)\cos(\pi(\lambda+\mu))-\frac{2}{\pi}Q_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)\sin(\pi(\lambda+\mu))\,,

(13)

(
e.g.
, equation
(14.9.10)
in
[
17
]
).

For the conical functions, with
\lambda=-\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu
, conjugation of the degree leaves the Legendre functions invariant,

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)=P_{\lambda^{*}}^{\mu}(x)\,,\quad Q_{\lambda}^{\mu}(x)=Q_{\lambda^{*}}^{\mu}(x)\,,

(14)

as the Legendre equation is identical, for
\lambda(\lambda+1)=\lambda^{*}(\lambda^{*}+1)=-\tfrac{1}{4}-\nu^{2}
.

3
Orthogonality relations of conical functions

The Legendre function
P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)
and
P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)
satisfy the differential equations

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[(1-x^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}\right]+\left[\lambda(\lambda+1)+\frac{q^{2}}{1-x^{2}}\right]P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)=0\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[(1-x^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}\right]+\left[\lambda(\lambda+1)+\frac{q^{\prime 2}}{1-x^{2}}\right]P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)=0\,.

(16)

Multiplying the first equation by
P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)
and the second equation by
P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)
, subtracting the resulting two equations, and integrating the resulting identity from
a
to
b
where
-1<a<b<1
, we obtain upon integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\left[P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)(1-x^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}-P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)(1-x^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}\right]^{b}_{a}}{q^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}\,.

(17)

Letting
a\to-1
from above and
b\to 1
from below, we write the integral of interest as the difference between two limits

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{b\to 1^{-}}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(b)(1-b^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(b)}{\mathrm{d}b}-P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(b)(1-b^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq}(b)}{\mathrm{d}b}}{q^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}

\displaystyle-\lim_{a\to-1^{+}}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(a)(1-a^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(a)}{\mathrm{d}a}-P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(a)(1-a^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\lambda}^{iq}(a)}{\mathrm{d}a}}{q^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}\,.

(18)

Bielski
[
9
]
evaluates these limits and shows that

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)P_{\lambda}^{iq^{\prime}}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\Gamma(iq)\Gamma(-iq)\sin(\pi\lambda)}{\Gamma(1+\lambda-iq)\Gamma(-\lambda-iq)}\delta(q-q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{\pi}{\Gamma(1-iq)\Gamma(1+iq)}+\frac{\sin^{2}(\pi\lambda)\Gamma(iq)\Gamma(-iq)}{\pi}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{\pi\Gamma(iq)\Gamma(-iq)}{\Gamma(1+\lambda-iq)\Gamma(-\lambda-iq)\Gamma(1+\lambda+iq)\Gamma(-\lambda+iq)}\bigg]\,.

(19)

Using the fact that for conical functions

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}^{iq}(x)^{*}

\displaystyle=P_{\lambda^{*}}^{-iq}(x)=P_{\lambda}^{-iq}(x)\,,

(20)

we find

\displaystyle I_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh(2\pi q)+\cosh(2\pi\nu)}{q\sinh(\pi q)}\delta(q-q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{2\pi\cosh(\pi\nu)}{q\sinh(\pi q)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}\delta(q+q^{\prime})\,.

(21)

The integrals
I_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}
and
I_{3}^{q,q^{\prime}}
follow directly from relation (
10
),

\displaystyle I_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi}{2\sinh(\pi q^{\prime})}\left[\cosh(\pi q^{\prime})I_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}-\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu+iq^{\prime})}I_{1}^{q,-q^{\prime}}\right]

(22)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi(\sinh(2\pi q)+\sinh(2\pi\nu))}{2q\sinh(\pi q)}\delta(q-q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{i\pi^{2}\sinh(\pi\nu)}{q\sinh(\pi q)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}\delta(q+q^{\prime})\,,

(23)

and

\displaystyle I_{3}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi}{2\sinh(\pi q)}\left[\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu+iq)}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}I_{2}^{-q,q^{\prime}}-\cosh(\pi q)I_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right]

(24)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}(\cosh(2\pi q)+\cosh(2\pi\nu))}{4q\sinh(\pi q)}\delta(q-q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{\pi^{3}\cosh(\pi\nu)}{2q\sinh(\pi q)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}\delta(q+q^{\prime})\,.

(25)

Using the reflection equation (
13
), we obtain the orthogonality relations for
J_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}
,

\displaystyle J_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-i\sinh(\pi(\nu+q^{\prime}))I_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}+\frac{2}{\pi}\cosh(\pi(\nu+q^{\prime}))I_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi i}{q\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}\delta(q+q^{\prime})\,.

(27)

Again applying equation (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi}{2\sinh(\pi q^{\prime})}\left[\cosh(\pi q^{\prime})J_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}-\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu+iq^{\prime})}J_{1}^{q,-q^{\prime}}\right]

(28)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh(\pi(q-\nu))}{q\sinh(\pi q)}\delta(q-q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{q\tanh(\pi q)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}\delta(q+q^{\prime})\,,

(29)

and

\displaystyle J_{3}^{q,q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi}{2\sinh(\pi q)}\left[\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu+iq)}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}J_{2}^{-q,q^{\prime}}-\cosh(\pi q)J_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right]

(30)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i\pi^{3}}{2q\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-i\nu-iq)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\nu-iq)}\delta(q+q^{\prime})\,.

(31)

Note that Bielski’s formulas do not directly imply the
J_{i}^{q,q^{\prime}}
integrals.

These expressions satisfy the following conjugation conditions which we can obtain from their definitions,

\displaystyle\left(I_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=I_{1}^{q^{\prime},q}\,,

\displaystyle\left(I_{3}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=I_{3}^{q^{\prime},q}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\left(I_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=I_{2}^{-q,-q^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle\left(J_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=J_{2}^{-q,-q^{\prime}}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\left(J_{1}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=J_{1}^{q^{\prime},q}\,,

\displaystyle\left(J_{3}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=J_{3}^{q^{\prime},q}\,.

(34)

From the derived equations, we find that
\left(I_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=-I_{2}^{q^{\prime},q}
and
\left(J_{2}^{q,q^{\prime}}\right)^{*}=J_{2}^{q^{\prime},q}
.

Fabes-Stroock approach to higher integrability of Green’s functions and ABP estimates with L d L_{d} drift
Pilgyu Jung, Kwan Woo

Abstract
Résumé We explore the higher integrability of Green’s functions associated with the second-order elliptic equation a i ​ j ​ D i ​ j ​ u + b i ​ D i ​ u = f a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b^{i}D_{i}u=f in a bounded domain Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , and establish an enhanced version of Aleksandrov’s maximum principle. In particular, we consider the drift term b = ( b 1 , … , b d ) b=(b^{1},\ldots,b^{d}) in L d L_{d} and the source term f ∈ L p f\in L_{p} for some p < d p<d . This provides an alternative and analytic proof of a result by N. V. Krylov ( Ann. Probab. , 2021) concerning L d L_{d} drifts. The key step involves deriving a Gehring-type inequality for Green’s functions by using the Fabes-Stroock approach ( Duke Math. J. , 1984). 1
Introduction

Let
d\geq 2
be a positive integer and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional Euclidean space with points
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
.
Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
a=[a^{ij}(x)]
be a measurable
d\times d
symmetric matrix-valued function satisfying an ellipticity condition:

\delta|\xi|^{2}\leq a^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\delta^{-1}|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\xi,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.1)

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
b=(b^{1},\ldots,b^{d})
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued measurable function satisfying

\|b\|_{L_{d}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|b(x)|^{d}\,dx\right)^{1/d}\leq\|b\|

for some non-negative fixed constant
\|b\|
.
We define

\mathcal{L}u=\mathcal{L}_{a,b}u:=a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b^{i}D_{i}u

and let
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=D_{ij}(a^{ij}v)-D_{i}(b^{i}v)
be the formal adjoint of
\mathcal{L}
.

This article addresses the
higher integrability
of Green’s functions for
\mathcal{L}
together with the following
maximum principle
:

\sup_{\Omega}u_{+}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u_{+}+N\|(\mathcal{L}u)_{-}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}.

(1.2)

Here,
N
depends only on
d
,
p
,
\delta
,
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)
, and
\|b\|
.

The classical Aleksandrov-Bakel’man-Pucci (ABP) estimate, which corresponds to the case
p=d
in (
1.2
), is a foundational result in the regularity theory of non-divergence form elliptic and parabolic equations.
This estimate, due to the work of Aleksandrov
[
1
]
, Bakel’man
[
3
]
, and Pucci
[
24
]
, has been instrumental in various fields.
For instance, based on the ABP estimate, one can proceed with the Krylov-Safonov theorem, which is about the Harnack inequality and Hölder continuity of the solution to linear equation
a^{ij}D_{ij}u=f

[
22
,
25
]
.
For nonlinear equations, the regularity theory of viscosity solutions based on ABP estimates is well introduced in
[
8
]
.
Furthermore, in the context of stochastic analysis, the ABP-type estimate also plays a crucial role in the proof of weak uniqueness of stochastic differential equations, see
[
19
]
, for instance.

When there is no drift term,
i.e.
,
b=0
, Fabes and Stroock
[
12
]
extended the estimate (
1.2
) to the case
p>d-\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
More precisely, by analyzing the local integrability of Green’s functions and utilizing a Gehring-type Lemma, they raised the integrability beyond
d/(d-1)
.
This result is significant in
L_{p}
-theory for fully nonlinear equations, in particular, Escauriaza
[
11
]
(see Winter
[
27
]
for boundary estimates) used the Fabes-Stroock’s result to extend Caffarelli’s
W_{p}^{2}
(
p>d
) estimates
[
7
]
for solutions to a class of fully nonlinear equations to the case
p>d-\varepsilon
.

On the other hand, for a general drift term
b\in L_{d}(\Omega)
, the corresponding extension of the Fabes-Stroock result remained an open problem until it was resolved by Krylov
[
20
]
.
Building on results in
[
20
]
, the
L_{p}
-theory (
p>d-\varepsilon
) for fully nonlinear elliptic equations with
L_{d}
drifts was further developed in
[
21
]
, leading to (
1.2
) for
L_{p}
-viscosity solution in
[
17
]
.
In particular, the author in
[
20
]
obtained (among other various results on the stochastic process) the ABP estimate with
b\in L_{d}(\Omega)
when
\mathcal{L}u\in L_{p}
for some
p<d
through a probabilistic approach such as sophisticated stopping-time arguments.
However, such probabilistic techniques may be challenging for readers unfamiliar with probability theory.
To make this significant result more accessible, we provide self-contained analytic proof in line with Fabes-Stroock
[
12
]
.

1.1
Fabes-Stroock approach

We briefly introduce the approach used in
[
12
]
to establish the higher integrability of Green’s function.
The authors investigated the behavior of nonnegative solutions to adjoint equations
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{0}v=0
in
\Omega
(see (
4.16
) for the precise definition of adjoint problems), where
\mathcal{L}_{0}=a^{ij}D_{ij}
.
Their approach begins with the inequality

\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B}v(y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\right)^{(d-1)/d}\leq N(d,\delta)\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B}v(y)\,dy,

(1.3)

which holds for any balls
B
such that its concentric double
2B
lies within
\Omega
.
This result is obtained by solving the equation
\mathcal{L}_{0}u=f
for an arbitrary
f\in L_{d}(\Omega)
, and then estimating

\int_{B}v(y)f(y)\,dy=\int_{B}v(y)\mathcal{L}_{0}u(y)\,dy\leq N\int_{B}v(y)\,dy

using the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}v=0
and regularity properties of
u
.

On the other hand, classical theory provides lower bounds for the mean of Green’s function
G
associated with
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, as given by

\int_{B}{G}_{2B}(x,y)\,dy\geq\nu

(1.4)

for some
\nu=\nu(d,\delta)>0
.
Since
{G}_{\Omega}-{G}_{2B}
(
2B\subset\Omega
) is a nonnegative solution to
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}v=0
, combining (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) yields the reverse Hölder inequality for
{G}_{\Omega}
:

\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B}{G}_{\Omega}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\right)^{(d-1)/d}\leq N(d,\delta)\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B}{G}_{\Omega}(x,y)\,dy

(1.5)

for any
x\in\Omega
and any ball
B
such that its concentric quadruple
4B
is contained in
\Omega
.
Finally, by (
1.5
) and the
A_{\infty}
-weight theory
[
14
,
9
]
, one can deduce that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}{G}_{\Omega}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\leq N(d,\delta,\operatorname{diam}(\Omega))

for some
q>(d-1)/d
.

For the case
b\neq 0
, Fok
[
13
]
adapted the Fabes-Stroock approach to obtain an inequality similar to (
1.3
) for nonlinear problems.
However, in this case, even for the linear operators
\mathcal{L}u=a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b^{i}D_{i}u
, a slightly higher integrability of drift term is required (for example,
b\in L_{d+\varepsilon}
) due to the low integrability of
Du
.
We also refer to
[
4
]
, which presents a refined ABP estimate for bounded drift
b
, where the domain dependence is captured via geometric quantities finer than just the diameter.

In this paper, to handle the critical drift
b\in L_{d}(\Omega)
, we employ Aleksandrov solutions
z\in W_{\infty}^{1}
(see Lemma
3.2
) that satisfy
f\leq\mathcal{L}z
instead of solving the linear equation
\mathcal{L}u=f
.
Unlike
u
of
\mathcal{L}u=f
, it is difficult to expect regularity of
D^{2}z
in our situation.
However, the analysis needed to obtain (
1.3
) does not rely on the regularity of
D^{2}z
.
Instead, by leveraging the properties of the convex solution
z
, we obtain the boundedness of
|Dz|
(generally
|Du|
is not bounded), which can be used more effectively in the proof. See Proposition
4.1
.
We also prove a counterpart of (
1.4
) that takes into account
b\in L_{d}(\Omega)
; see Lemma
4.4
and Corollary
4.7
.
Another fundamental idea we employ to obtain the aforementioned key estimate is the fact that the
L_{d}
-norm of
b
is invariant under scaling
b(x)\mapsto rb(rx)
.
This property renders the effect of the drift
b
negligible compared to the diffusion
[a^{ij}]
as the size of the ball increases.
The advantage of this approach is that the required ball size can be determined solely by
d,\delta
and
\|b\|
using the arithmetic-geometric mean, making the
a priori
estimate (
1.2
) more practical.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce some notation and main results.
We state auxiliary estimates in section
3
.
The main theorems are proved in section
4
.

Notation

Throughout this paper, we use the following notation.

We denote by
|x|
the Euclidean norm of a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and by
|A|
the Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The same symbol
|\cdot|
is used for both the norm and the measure, and the intended meaning will be clear from the context.
For any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive measure, we define the average of a function
f
over
A
by

\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{A}f:=\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}f.

For real numbers

, and
c
, we define

a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\},\quad a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\},\quad c_{+}:=\max\{c,0\},\quad c_{-}:=\max\{-c,0\}.

We adopt the summation convention over repeated indices and denote partial derivatives by
D_{i}=\partial/\partial x_{i}
and
D_{ij}=D_{i}D_{j}
.
If a constant
N
depends on parameters
d,\nu,\ldots
, we indicate this dependence by writing
N=N(d,\nu,\ldots)
.

Given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write

for its boundary and
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)
for its diameter, defined by

\operatorname{diam}(\Omega):=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}|x-y|.

For
R>0
, we denote the open ball of radius
R
centered at
x
by

B_{R}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y-x|<R\},

and simply write
B_{R}:=B_{R}(0)
when centered at the origin.
Similarly, the open cube of side length
R
centered at
x
is denoted by

Q_{R}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x_{i}-y_{i}|<R/2\quad\text{for all }i=1,\ldots,d\},

with
Q_{R}:=Q_{R}(0)
when centered at the origin.

We now introduce the function spaces used throughout the paper.
Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We denote by
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
the space of infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\Omega
, and by
C(\overline{\Omega})
the space of continuous functions on the closure
\overline{\Omega}
.
The space
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
denotes the set of functions that are infinitely differentiable up to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we denote by
L_{p}(\Omega)
the usual Lebesgue space of
p
-integrable functions on
\Omega
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}
.
An element

means that
u\in L_{p}(K)
for every compact set
K\subset\Omega
.
We denote by
W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)
the Sobolev space consisting of functions
u\in L_{p}(\Omega)
whose weak derivatives

and

also belong to
L_{p}(\Omega)
.
The norm on
W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)
is given by

\|u\|_{W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\|Du\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\|D^{2}u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}.

We similarly define
W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)
as the space of functions that locally belong to
W_{p}^{2}(\Omega)
.

2
Preliminaries and main results

We call
G_{\Omega}
the
Green’s function of
\mathcal{L}
associated with
\Omega
if

u(x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)f(y)\,dy

belongs to
W_{d,\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
for
f\in L_{d}(\Omega)
and satisfies the equation

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}u\,(=a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b^{i}D_{i}u)=-f\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2.6)

Unless otherwise specified, solutions
u
to equation (
2.6
) are understood to lie in suitable function spaces where the equation holds almost everywhere in
\Omega
.
We emphasize that all the estimates derived in this paper are
independent of the regularity
of the coefficients
a^{ij}
,
b^{i}
, and the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
This fact, together with an approximation argument, allows us to assume the existence of Green’s functions in the proof of a main result, Theorem
2.2
, which presents a refined version of ABP estimate.

For completeness, in the following remark, we also include an argument for the existence and uniqueness of the Green’s function, based on
L_{p}
-theory.
While several approaches are available in the literature, we adopt this method for its relevance to the present framework.

Remark 2.1

(Unique existence of the Green’s function)
.

If we assume that
a^{ij}
are uniformly continuous and
b^{i}
are bounded, then the Green’s function associated with
\Omega
exists and is unique, provided that
\Omega
is bounded and
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
.
Indeed, by the classical
L_{p}
-theory (see, for instance,
[
16
, Chapter 9]
or
[
18
, Theorem 11.3.2]
), if
f\in L_{d}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
\mathcal{R}f
to equation (
2.6
) such that

\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{W_{d}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq N(d,p,a^{ij},b^{i},\Omega)\|f\|_{L_{d}(\Omega)},

where
a^{ij}
,
b^{i}
, and
\Omega
satisfy the aforementioned conditions.
Then, using Sobolev embedding, we have that
\mathcal{R}f\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and

\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq N_{0}\|f\|_{L_{d}(\Omega)},

where
N_{0}=N_{0}(d,p,a^{ij},b^{i},\Omega)
.
This implies that for each
x\in\Omega
, the resolvent mapping
f\in L_{d}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathcal{R}f(x)
defines a bounded linear functional whose norm is bounded by
N_{0}
.
By Riesz’s representation theorem, for
x\in\Omega
, there exists a unique function
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)\in L_{d/(d-1)}(\Omega)
such that

\mathcal{R}f(x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)f(y)\,dy

Therefore, the existence and uniqueness of
G_{\Omega}
are guaranteed.

On the other hand, even when the coefficients and the domain boundary are irregular, one can still define Green’s functions using probability theory.
For more details on probabilistic solutions, see
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain. There is a constant
d_{0}=d_{0}(d,\delta,\|b\|)\in(d/2,d)
such that the following holds:
If
u\in W_{p,\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
with
p\in[d_{0},\infty)
, then

\sup_{\Omega}u_{+}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u_{+}+N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/p}\|\left(\mathcal{L}u\right)_{-}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},

(2.7)

where
N
depends only on
d
,
\delta
, and
\|b\|
.

Remark 2.3

(Neumann problem on half balls)
.

By employing even/odd reflection and Green’s function representation, an estimate analogous to (
2.7
) can be obtained even in the case of the Neumann boundary value problem on a half ball. More precisely, for
R>0
, we have

\sup_{B_{R}\cap\{x_{1}>0\}}u_{+}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=R\\
x_{1}>0\end{subarray}}u_{+}+2R\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|<R\\
x_{1}=0\end{subarray}}\left(D_{1}u\right)_{-}+NR^{2-d/d_{0}}\|\left(\mathcal{L}u\right)_{-}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(B_{R}\cap\{x_{1}>0\})},

where
N
and
d_{0}
are the constants from Theorem
2.2
.
See Remark
4.2
for a discussion on the adjoint problems with Neumann boundary conditions.

From Theorem
2.2
, we derive a Fabes-Stroock-type refined version of the integrability estimate for Green’s functions.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain.
Suppose that the operator
\mathcal{L}
admits a Green’s function
G_{\Omega}
associated with
\Omega
.
Then there exist positive constants

and

depending only on

,

, and

such that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}\leq N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/p},

where

is the conjugate exponent of

, i.e.,
1/p+1/q=1
.

Note that the results in
[
23
]
depend on the profile of the function

, whereas the constant

in Theorem

depends only on the upper bound

of the norm

Hence, (

On the other hand, the author in
[

]
addressed a more general boundary value problem, specifically the oblique derivative problem.
We also mention the recent work
[

Note that applying the estimate in

.
We now define

z^{\varepsilon}(x)=r^{2}z_{r}^{\varepsilon}\left(x/r\right),\quad\textrm{where}\quad z_{r}^{\varepsilon}=z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}+N_{1}r^{-1}z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}.

We shall establish the
W_{\infty}^{1}
estimate (
3.9
), the comparison inequality (
3.10
), and the error estimate (
3.11
), based on the properties of convex functions
z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}
and
z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}
.

W_{\infty}^{1}
estimates.

First, note that
z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}
and
z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}
enjoy the Aleksandrov-type maximum principle (
cf
.
[
16
, Ch. 9]
)

|z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}|\leq N_{1}(r^{-1}+\varepsilon)\quad\textrm{and}\quad|z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|\leq N_{2}+N_{1}\varepsilon,

where
N_{1}=N_{1}(d)
and
N_{2}=N_{2}(d,\delta,\|b\|)
.
Here, we use the assumption that
\|f\|_{L_{d}(B_{2r})}\leq 1
in the first inequality.

To estimate
Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}
and
Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}
in
B_{2}
, we make use of the convexity and non-positivity of
z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}
and
z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}
, together with the fact that
z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}=z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial B_{3}
.
More precisely, to estimate
|Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}(x)|
for
x\in B_{2}
, suppose that
|Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}(x)|\neq 0
.
Let
e=Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}(x)/|Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}(x)|
and
t_{0}
be a positive number such that
x+t_{0}e\in\partial B_{3}
.
We define
g(t)=z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}(x+te)
.
Then, by convexity and the estimate on
|z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}|
, we obtain

|Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}(x)|=g^{\prime}(0)\leq\frac{g(t_{0})-g(0)}{t_{0}}=\frac{-g(0)}{t_{0}}\leq\frac{|z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}(x)|}{3-|x|}\leq N_{1}(r^{-1}+\varepsilon).

(3.13)

A similar argument shows that
|Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq N_{2}+N_{1}\varepsilon
for
x\in B_{2}
.
Then, together with the above estimates, it is straightforward to check that
z^{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
3.9
) for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.

Comparison and error estimate.

It remains to show (
3.10
) and (
3.11
).
Note that

\displaystyle a^{ij}_{r}D_{ij}z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}+b^{i}_{r}D_{i}z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq d\sqrt[d]{\det a_{r}}\sqrt[d]{\det D^{2}z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}}-|b_{r}||Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle=d\sqrt[d]{\det a_{r}}\cdot f_{r}^{\varepsilon}-|b_{r}||Dz_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\geq d\sqrt[d]{\det a_{r}}\cdot f_{r}^{\varepsilon}-|b_{r}|N_{1}(r^{-1}+\varepsilon)

in
B_{2}
, where the last inequality is due to (
3.13
) in
B_{2}
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle a^{ij}_{r}D_{ij}z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}+b_{r}^{i}D_{i}z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq d\sqrt[d]{\det a_{r}}\sqrt[d]{\det D^{2}z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}}-|b_{r}||Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\det a_{r}}{\det a_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/d}\cdot\left(1+(\varepsilon+|Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{1/2}\right)\cdot b_{r}^{\varepsilon}\,-\,|b_{r}||Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|.

Thus, from the definition
z_{r}^{\varepsilon}=z_{1,r}^{\varepsilon}+N_{1}r^{-1}z_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

a^{ij}_{r}D_{ij}z_{r}^{\varepsilon}+b^{i}_{r}D_{i}z_{r}^{\varepsilon}\geq d\sqrt[d]{\det a_{r}}\cdot f_{r}^{\varepsilon}-I_{r}^{\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{in}\,\,B_{2},

(3.14)

where

\displaystyle I_{r}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=|b_{r}|N_{1}(r^{-1}+\varepsilon)-N_{1}r^{-1}\left(\frac{\det a_{r}}{\det a_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/d}\cdot\left(1+(\varepsilon+|Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{1/2}\right)\cdot b_{r}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+N_{1}r^{-1}|b_{r}||Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle=N_{1}r^{-1}|b_{r}|\cdot(1+|Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|)-\left(\frac{\det a_{r}}{\det a_{r}^{\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/d}\cdot N_{1}r^{-1}b_{r}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(1+(\varepsilon+|Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle+N_{1}|b_{r}|\varepsilon.

Recall that
b_{r}^{\varepsilon}=(|b_{r}|1_{B_{2}})^{(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon
and then, by dominated convergence theorem with the help of the bound
|Dz_{2,r}^{\varepsilon}|\leq N_{2}+N_{1}\varepsilon
in
B_{2}
, we see that

\|I_{r}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{d}(B_{2})}\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

Finally, by using the relation
z^{\varepsilon}(x)=r^{2}z_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x/r)
,
a(x)=a_{r}(x/r)
,
b(x)=r^{-1}b_{r}(x/r)
, and
f^{\varepsilon}(x)=f_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x/r)
, we obtain (
3.10
) for
x\in B_{2r}
together with the error estimate (
3.11
) for
I^{\varepsilon}(x):=I_{r}^{\varepsilon}(x/r)
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

We end this section by presenting the classical ABP estimate which is read as the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain. Then for any
u\in W_{d,\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
,

\sup_{\Omega}u_{+}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u_{+}+N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)\|(\mathcal{L}u)_{-}\|_{L_{d}(\Omega)},

(3.15)

where
N=N(d,\delta,\|b\|)
.

Using (
3.15
), one verifies that the Green’s function
G_{\Omega}
is nonnegative,
e.g.
, the proof of Lemma
4.6
.
Moreover, by using duality, we obtain the corollary below.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
admits a Green’s function
G_{\Omega}
associated with
\Omega
.
Then we have

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\right)^{(d-1)/d}\leq N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega),

where
N=N(d,\delta,\|b\|)
.

4
Proofs of the main results

Our strategy for proving the main results, Theorems
2.2
and
2.4
, is as follows.
In Section
4.1
, we derive a key estimate (Proposition
4.8
) by combining Proposition
4.1
and Corollary
4.7
, in order to apply a Gehring-type lemma.
This estimate is then used in Section
4.2
to establish the higher integrability of the Green’s function (see (

Finally, the main results are deduced from (

4.1
Key estimate for Gehring-type lemma

We start with introducing a notion of solution to the adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.
We say
v\in L_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a nonnegative solution of an adjoint problem
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
if
v\geq 0
, and

\int_{\Omega}v\mathcal{L}u\,dx=0

(4.16)

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
u\geq 0
.

We establish the following estimate for nonnegative homogeneous solutions of the adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
v
be a nonnegative solution of
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
in
\Omega
such that
v^{d/(d-1)}\in L_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
There exist positive constants
N
and
K(\geq 1)
depending only on
d,\delta
, and
\|b\|
such that

\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}v(y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}(x_{0})}v(y)\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)}

+\frac{1}{8}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}(x_{0})}v(y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy,

where
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
R\in(0,\infty)
satisfy
B_{KR}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

By translation, we may assume that
x_{0}=0
.
Let
r
be a positive number such that
B_{4r}\subset\Omega
.
For a nonnegative function
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2r})
with
\|f\|_{L_{d}(B_{2r})}\leq 1
, we take
z
from Lemma
3.2
.
Let
\varphi
be a nonnegative smooth function such that
\varphi(x)=1
if
|x|\leq r
and
\varphi(x)=0
if
|x|>3r/2
with

|D\varphi|\leq\frac{N}{r}I_{B_{2r}\setminus B_{r}}\quad\text{and}\quad|D^{2}\varphi|\leq\frac{N}{r^{2}}I_{B_{2r}\setminus B_{r}}.

(4.17)

Then, by (
3.10
) of Lemma
3.2
, we see that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,r)
,

\int_{B_{r}}v(y)f^{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\leq N\int_{B_{r}}v(y)(\mathcal{L}z^{\varepsilon}(y)+I_{\varepsilon}(y))\,dy

\leq N\int_{B_{3r/2}}v(y)\left(\mathcal{L}z^{\varepsilon}(y)+I_{\varepsilon}(y)\right)\varphi(y)\,dy=N\int_{B_{3r/2}}v(y)\mathcal{L}(z^{\varepsilon}\varphi)(y)\,dy

+N\int_{B_{3r/2}}v(y)\left(\mathcal{L}z^{\varepsilon}(y)\varphi(y)-\mathcal{L}(z^{\varepsilon}\varphi)(y)\right)\,dy+N\int_{B_{3r/2}}v(y)I_{\varepsilon}(y)\varphi(y)\,dy

\leq N\int_{B_{2r}}v(y)|I_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy+Nr^{-1}\int_{B_{2r}}v(y)|Dz^{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy

+Nr^{-2}\int_{B_{2r}}v(y)|z^{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy+Nr^{-1}\int_{B_{2r}\setminus B_{r}}v(y)|b(y)||z^{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy,

where we use
(
4.17
)
and the fact that
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
in
\Omega
to obtain the last inequality.
By applying the pointwise estimates (
3.9
) to the above inequalities and Hölder’s inequality, we reach to

\int_{B_{r}}v(y)f^{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\leq N\int_{B_{2r}}v(y)|I_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy+Nr^{-1}\int_{B_{2r}}v(y)\,dy

+N\int_{B_{2r}\setminus B_{r}}v(y)|b(y)|\,dy

\leq Nr^{-1}\int_{B_{2r}}v(y)\,dy+N\left(\|b\|_{L_{d}(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})}+\|I_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{d}(B_{2r})}\right)\|v\|_{L_{d/(d-1)}(B_{2r})}.

By taking
\varepsilon\to 0
to the above estimates and utilizing (
3.11
), it holds that

\int_{B_{r}}v(y)f(y)\,dy=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}}v(y)f^{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy

\leq Nr^{-1}\int_{B_{2r}}v(y)\,dy+N\|b\|_{L_{d}(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})}\|v\|_{L_{d/(d-1)}(B_{2r})}

Since the above inequalities hold for any positive
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2r})
with
\|f\|_{L_{d}}\leq 1
, we have

\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{r}}v^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{2r}}v\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)}+N\|b\|_{L_{d}(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})}^{d/(d-1)}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{2r}}v^{d/(d-1)}\,dy.

(4.18)

For
n=0,1,2,\ldots
, take
r=R_{n}:=2^{n}R
and let
\Gamma_{n}=B_{R_{n+1}}\setminus B_{R_{n}}
,

A_{n}=\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R_{n}}}v^{d/(d-1)}\,dy,\quad B_{n}=\|b\|_{L_{d}(\Gamma_{n})}^{d/(d-1)},\quad C_{n}=\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R_{n+1}}}v\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)}.

Then we rewrite (
4.18
) as

A_{n}\leq{N}_{0}C_{n}+N_{0}B_{n}A_{n+1},\quad\forall n=0,1,\cdots,

where
{N}_{0}=N_{0}(d,\delta,\|b\|)
.
We apply the above inequality recursively to see that

\begin{split}A_{0}&\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}N_{0}^{n+1}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}B_{k}\right)C_{n}+N_{0}^{m}\left(\prod_{n=0}^{m-1}B_{n}\right)A_{m}\\
&\leq NC_{m-1}+N_{0}^{m}\left(\prod_{n=0}^{m-1}B_{n}\right)A_{m},\end{split}

where
N=N(d,\delta,\|b\|,m)
.
By the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, we see

N_{0}^{m}\left(\prod_{n=0}^{m-1}B_{n}\right)\leq\left(m^{-1}N_{0}^{d-1}\|b\|^{d}\right)^{m/(d-1)}.

Take sufficiently large
m=m(d,\delta,\|b\|)
, so that the last expression of the above inequality is less than
1/8
.
Thus, if
B_{2^{m}R}\subset\Omega
, we have that

\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}}v^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{2^{m}R}}v\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)}+\frac{1}{8}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{2^{m}R}}v^{d/(d-1)}\,dy,

where
N=N(d,\delta,\|b\|,m)
.
By taking
K=2^{m}
, the proposition is proved.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
can be applied to solutions to adjoint problems
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
on a half ball with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions on the flat boundary, that is, to
v
satisfying

\int_{B_{r}\cap\{x_{1}>0\}}v\mathcal{L}u=0

for any

.

with considering proper even/odd extensions of

, and

from

, we have a non-positive convex function
z^{\varepsilon}
defined on

satisfying (
3.9
)–(

).
Then by the uniqueness of solutions to Monge-Ampére equations (
3.12
) with an even symmetry property of determinant of Hessian (
i.e.
,
\det D^{2}f(x)=\left(\det D^{2}g\right)\left(-x_{1},x^{\prime}\right)
for
f(x)=g(-x_{1},x^{\prime})
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
), one immediately verify that
z^{\varepsilon}
is even in
x_{1}
, that is,
D_{1}z^{\varepsilon}=0
on
B_{r}\cap\{x_{1}=0\}
.
This enables us to follow the argument in the proof of Proposition
4.1
for
v
above.
We refer the reader to
[
23
, Section 4]
for discussions on adjoint solutions to oblique derivative problems.

4.3
,
4.4
are needed to obtain Corollary
4.7
, a kind of lower bound of Green’s function associated with
B_{R}
.

[
20

4.7
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exist positive constants
S_{0}=S_{0}(d,\delta)
and
\nu=\nu(d,\delta)
such that if
\|b\|\leq S_{0}
and
u\in W_{d}^{2}(B_{1})
satisfies
\mathcal{L}u=-1
in
B_{1}
and
u\geq 0
on
\partial B_{1}
, then
u(x)\geq\nu
in
B_{1/2}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{0}\leq 1
be a positive number to be specified later.
Take a nonnegative smooth
\zeta
such that
\zeta(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1/2
and
\zeta(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1
.
Then we have

\mathcal{L}\zeta=a^{ij}D_{ij}\zeta+b^{i}D_{i}\zeta\geq-N_{0}I_{\Gamma}-N_{0}|b|I_{\Gamma}\quad\textrm{in}\quad B_{1},

where
\Gamma={B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2}}
and
N_{0}
is a positive constant depending on
d,\delta
.
Set
v=\zeta/N_{0}
.
Then, we see that

\mathcal{L}v-\mathcal{L}u\geq-|b|I_{\Gamma}\quad\textrm{in}\quad B_{1},

which implies that

\left(\mathcal{L}v-\mathcal{L}u\right)_{-}\leq|b|I_{\Gamma}\quad\textrm{in}\quad B_{1}.

Using Theorem
3.3
with the fact that
u\in W_{d}^{2}(B_{1})\subset C(\overline{B}_{1})
and
(v-u)_{+}=0
on
\partial B_{1}
, we see that

\frac{1}{N_{0}}-u(x)=v(x)-u(x)\leq N_{1}\left(\int_{\Gamma}|b|^{d}\right)^{1/d},\quad\forall x\in B_{1/2},

that is,

\frac{1}{N_{0}}-N_{1}S_{0}\leq u(x),\quad\forall x\in B_{1/2},

where
N_{1}
is a positive constant depending only on
d,\delta
.
Hence, it suffices to take
S_{0}:=1\wedge(2N_{0}N_{1})^{-1}
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists positive constants
R_{0}=R_{0}(d,\delta,\|b\|)>2
and
\nu=\nu(d,\delta)
such that if
u\in W_{d}^{2}(B_{R_{0}})
satisfies
\mathcal{L}u=-1
in
B_{R_{0}}
and
u\geq 0
on
\partial B_{R_{0}}
, then
u\geq\nu
in
B_{1}
.

Proof.

Take
m=1+[(\|b\|/S_{0})^{d}]
(that is,
m
is the least positive integer greater than
(\|b\|/S_{0})^{d}
), where
S_{0}
is taken from Lemma
4.3
. Let
R_{0}=R_{0}(d,\delta,\|b\|):=2m+2
and for
i=1,\cdots,m
, set
\Gamma^{(i)}:=B_{2i+2}\setminus B_{2i}
.
Since

\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\left(\int_{\Gamma^{(i)}}|b|^{d}\,dx\right)^{1/d}\leq\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Gamma^{(i)}}|b|^{d}\,dx\right)^{1/d}\leq m^{-1/d}\|b\|<S_{0},

there is at least one
i_{0}\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
such that
\|b\|_{L_{d}(\Gamma^{(i_{0})})}\leq S_{0}
.

By applying Theorem
3.3
to
-u\in W_{d}^{2}(B_{R_{0}})\subset C(\overline{B_{R_{0}}})
, we have
u\geq 0
in
B_{R_{0}}
.
Also note that
B_{1}(y)\subset\Gamma^{(i_{0})}
and
\|b\|_{L_{d}(B_{1}(y))}\leq S_{0}
for any
y
satisfying
|y|=2i_{0}+1
.
Then it follows from Lemma
4.3
that there is a positive number
\nu=\nu(d,\delta)
such that
u\geq\nu
in
B_{1/2}(y)
for any
y\in\partial B_{2i_{0}+1}
.
Finally, by applying Theorem
3.3
to
\nu-u
, we obtain
u\geq\nu
in
B_{2i_{0}+1}
, which implies the desired result.
∎

The following lemma refers to the intrinsic properties of Green’s functions.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
be bounded domains such that
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\subset\Omega
.
Suppose that the operator
\mathcal{L}
admits Green’s functions
G_{\Omega}
and
G_{\Omega^{\prime}}
associated with
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
, respectively.
Then,

1.

for
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)-G_{\Omega^{\prime}}(x,\cdot)
is a nonnegative solution of
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
in
\Omega^{\prime}
.

2.

for
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
,
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)
is also a nonnegative solution of
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
in
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(1)
First, we show that for any
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
G_{\Omega}(x,y)-G_{\Omega^{\prime}}(x,y)\geq 0
for almost every
y\in\Omega^{\prime}
.
Let
f
be a nonnegative function in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})
.
Set

u(x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)I_{\Omega^{\prime}}f(y)\,dy,\quad w(x)=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}G_{\Omega^{\prime}}(x,y)I_{\Omega^{\prime}}f(y)\,dy.

Under the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
admits Green’s functions
G_{\Omega}
and
G_{\Omega^{\prime}}
, and by the definition of the Green’s function together with classical
L_{p}
-theory (refer to Remark
2.1
), we see that
u
and
w
are
W_{d}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
solutions to

\mathcal{L}u=-fI_{\Omega^{\prime}}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\quad u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega

and

\mathcal{L}w=-f\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega^{\prime},\quad w=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega^{\prime},

respectively.
By applying Theorem
3.3
to
w-u
, we obtain that for
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

w(x)-u(x)\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}(w-u)_{+}+N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega^{\prime})\|\left(\mathcal{L}\left(w-u\right)\right)_{-}\|_{L_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})}

=\sup_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}(w-u)_{+}=0.

Therefore, we see that for any nonnegative
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})
,

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}G_{\Omega^{\prime}}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\leq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}G_{\Omega}(x,y)f(y)\,dy.

This implies that
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)-G_{\Omega^{\prime}}(x,\cdot)
is nonnegative almost everywhere in
\Omega^{\prime}
.

To show that
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)-G_{\Omega^{\prime}}(x,\cdot)
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
in
\Omega^{\prime}
for
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
, we verify

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left(G_{\Omega}\left(x,y\right)-G_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left(x,y\right)\right)\mathcal{L}\varphi\left(y\right)\,dy=0

(4.19)

for any nonnegative
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})
.
Let

v_{1}(x)=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}G_{\Omega^{\prime}}(x,y)\mathcal{L}\varphi(y)\,dy.

Then
v_{1}
satisfies

\mathcal{L}v_{1}=-\mathcal{L}\varphi,\quad v_{1}=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega^{\prime}.

Since
\varphi
is also zero on
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
,
v_{1}=-\varphi
in
\Omega^{\prime}
by Theorem
3.3
.
Likewise, if we set
v_{2}
as follows,

v_{2}(x)=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\mathcal{L}\varphi(y)\,dy=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\mathcal{L}\varphi(y)\,dy,

then
v_{2}=-\varphi
in
\Omega
.
This implies that
v_{1}=v_{2}
in
\Omega^{\prime}
, so that the equation (
4.19
) holds true.

(2)
Moreover, by the observation
v_{2}=0
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
, we see that for any
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
,
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)
is also a solution of
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
in
\Omega^{\prime}
.
∎

The following corollary can be obtained by combining Corollary
4.5
with Lemma
4.6
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
R\in(0,\infty)
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
.
Suppose that
a^{ij}
are uniformly continuous and
b^{i}
are bounded, ensuring that the operator
\mathcal{L}
admits a Green’s function
G_{R}
associated with
B_{R}
.
Then for
x\in B_{\kappa R}
, we have

\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}}G_{R}(x,y)\,dy\geq\nu R^{2-d},

where
\nu
depends only on
d
,
\delta
,
\|b\|
, and
\kappa
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in B_{\kappa R}
and set
B=B_{(1-\kappa)R}(x_{0})\subset B_{R}
.
Let
G_{B}(x,y)
be the Green’s function of
\mathcal{L}
associated with
B
.
Then
v(x)=\int_{B}G_{B}(x,y)\,dy
is the
W_{d}^{2}(B)\cap C(\overline{B})
solution to the equation

\mathcal{L}v=-1\quad\text{in}\quad B,

with the boundary condition
v=0
on
\partial B
.
By Corollary
4.5
, we have

\int_{B}G_{B}(x_{0},y)\,dy=v(x_{0})\geq\nu(1-\kappa)^{2}R^{2}.

(4.20)

Employing Lemma
4.6
-(
1
) with (
4.20
), we reach that

\int_{B_{R}}G_{R}(x_{0},y)\,dy\geq\int_{B}G_{R}(x_{0},y)\,dy\geq\int_{B}G_{B}(x_{0},y)\,dy\geq\nu(1-\kappa)^{2}R^{2}.

By dividing both sides of the above inequality by
|B_{R}|
, we finish the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove a key estimate: a version of the reverse Hölder inequality for Green’s functions.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
.
Suppose that
a^{ij}
are uniformly continuous and
b^{i}
are bounded, ensuring that the operator
\mathcal{L}
admits a Green’s function
G_{\Omega}
associated with
\Omega
.
Then there is a positive constants
K(\geq 1)
and
N
which depend on
d
,
\delta
, and
\|b\|
such that the following holds:

For any
x\in\Omega
,
R>0
and
x_{0}\in\Omega
with
B_{4KR}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
,
we have

\begin{split}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy&\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{3KR}(x_{0})}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\,dy\right)^{(d-1)/d}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}(x_{0})}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy.\end{split}

Proof.

We may assume that
x_{0}=0
.
Let
K\geq 1
be the one in Proposition
4.1
.
If
x\notin B_{2KR}
, then the conclusion holds from Lemma
4.6
-(
2
) and Proposition
4.1
(and
3.4
for the assumption of
v
in Proposition
4.1
).
Let
x\in B_{2KR}
and
G_{3KR}
be the Green’s function of
\mathcal{L}
associated with
B_{3KR}
, whose existence is ensured by the assumptions on the coefficients.
By Lemma
4.6
-(
1
), we have
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)\geq G_{3KR}(x,\cdot)
in
B_{3KR}
, which implies

\begin{split}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy&\leq 2\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}}\left(G_{\Omega}(x,y)-G_{3KR}(x,y)\right)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\\
&\quad+2\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}}G_{3KR}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy.\end{split}

(4.21)

Note that from Lemma
4.6
-(
1
),
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)-G_{3KR}(x,\cdot)
is also a solution of
\mathcal{L}^{*}v=0
in
B_{3KR}
.
Then, by applying Proposition
4.1
to
G_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)-G_{3KR}(x,\cdot)
, we see that

\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}}\left(G_{\Omega}(x,y)-G_{3KR}(x,y)\right)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy

\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}}G_{\Omega}(x,y)-G_{3KR}(x,y)\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)}

+\frac{1}{8}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}}\left(G_{\Omega}(x,y)-G_{3KR}(x,y)\right)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy

\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)}+\frac{1}{8}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy,

(4.22)

where the last inequality is due to the nonnegativity of Green’s functions.
On the other hand, Corollary
3.4
and Corollary
4.7
imply that

\begin{split}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}}G_{3KR}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy&\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{3KR}}G_{3KR}(x,y)\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)}\\
&\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{3KR}}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\,dy\right)^{d/(d-1)},\end{split}

(4.23)

where we use
G_{\Omega}(x,y)\geq G_{3KR}(x,y)
to get the last inequality.
By combining (
4.21
), (
4.22
), and (
4.23
), we finish the proof.
∎

4.2
Proofs of Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.4

We now establish the
higher integrability
of Green’s functions under regularity assumptions on
a^{ij}
,
b^{i}
, and the domain
\Omega
.
This result, stated in Lemma
4.9
, forms the foundation for the proofs of our main theorems presented below.

Lemma 4.9

(Theorem
2.4
with smooth data)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary.
Assume that
a^{ij}
are uniformly continuous and
b^{i}
are bounded.
Then there exist positive constants
N
and
q>d/(d-1)
depending only on
d
,
\delta
, and
\|b\|
such that the following holds:

The Green’s function
G_{\Omega}
of
\mathcal{L}
associated with
\Omega
satisfies

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}\leq N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/p},

(4.24)

where
p
is the conjugate exponent of
q
, i.e.,
1/p+1/q=1
.

Proof.

By using a translation and scaling argument, we may assume that
\Omega\subset Q_{1/2}
.
We take a domain
\widetilde{\Omega}
such that
\partial\widetilde{\Omega}\in C^{2}
and

\Omega\subset Q_{1/2}\subset Q_{1}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}\subset Q_{2}.

Let
x\in Q_{1}
.
We rewrite
b
as
bI_{\Omega}
in
\widetilde{\Omega}
.
By Proposition
4.8
, for any
R>0
and
x_{0}\in Q_{1}
satisfying
B_{4KR}(x_{0})\subset Q_{1}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
, we have

\begin{split}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy&\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{3KR}(x_{0})}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)\,dy\right)^{(d-1)/d}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{KR}(x_{0})}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy,\end{split}

where
G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
is the Green’s function of the operator
\mathcal{L}
associated with
\widetilde{\Omega}
, and
N
,
K
are positive constants from Proposition
4.8
depending on
d
,
\delta
, and
\|b\|
.
Therefore, it follows that for any
x_{0}\in Q_{1}
, we have

\left(\mathcal{M}_{r_{0}d(x_{0})}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,\cdot)^{d/(d-1)}\right)\left(x_{0}\right)\\
\leq N\left[\left(\mathcal{M}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,\cdot)\right)\left(x_{0}\right)\right]^{d/(d-1)}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{M}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,\cdot)^{d/(d-1)}\right)\left(x_{0}\right),

where
r_{0}=1/(4K)
and
d(x_{0})=\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\partial Q_{1})
.
Using Proposition
3.1
, we see that there exists
q>d/(d-1)
such that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{Q_{1/2}}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{Q_{1}}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\right)^{(d-1)/d}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{d/(d-1)}\,dy\right)^{(d-1)/d}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}\right)^{(d-1)/d}\cdot\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{\Omega}),

where the last inequality is due to Corollary
3.4
.
On the other hand, from Lemma
4.6
-(
1
), we have
G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\Omega
.
Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}\leq\left(\int_{Q_{1/2}}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

(4.25)

\displaystyle=N|Q_{1/2}|^{1/q}\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{Q_{1/2}}G_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq N|Q_{1/2}|^{1/q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}\right)^{(d-1)/d}\cdot\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{\Omega})

\displaystyle\leq N|Q_{1/2}|^{1/q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}\right)^{(d-1)/d}\cdot\operatorname{diam}(Q_{2})

\displaystyle=N(d,\delta,\|b\|)

for any
x\in\Omega
, which proves the scaled version of the lemma.

For a general bounded domain
\Omega
, we make the following observation. Let
z_{0}\in\Omega
and
R=2\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)
. We define the rescaled domain and coefficients as follows:

\displaystyle\Omega_{R,z_{0}}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:z_{0}+Rx\in\Omega\}

\displaystyle a^{ij}_{R,z_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle=a^{ij}(z_{0}+Rx)

\displaystyle b^{i}_{R,z_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle=R\cdot b^{i}(z_{0}+Rx)

The Green’s function
G_{\Omega}(x,y)
of the operator
\mathcal{L}=a^{ij}D_{ij}+b^{i}D_{i}
on
\Omega
is related to the Green’s function
G_{R,z_{0}}
of the rescaled operator
\mathcal{L}_{R,z_{0}}:=a^{ij}_{R,z_{0}}D_{ij}+b^{i}_{R,z_{0}}D_{i}
on
\Omega_{R,z_{0}}
by the formula:

G_{\Omega}(x,y)=R^{2-d}\cdot G_{R,z_{0}}\left(\frac{x-z_{0}}{R},\frac{y-z_{0}}{R}\right).

Using this relation and a change of variables, for
x\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=R^{2-d}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{R,z_{0}}\left(\frac{x-z_{0}}{R},\frac{y-z_{0}}{R}\right)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=R^{2-d+d/q}\left(\int_{\Omega_{R,z_{0}}}G_{R,z_{0}}\left(\frac{x-z_{0}}{R},y\right)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=R^{2-d+d/q}\left(\int_{\Omega_{R,z_{0}}}G_{R,z_{0}}\left(\frac{x-z_{0}}{R},y\right)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq NR^{2-d+d/q},

where the last inequality follows from (
4.25
), since
(x-z_{0})/R\in\Omega_{R,z_{0}}\subset Q_{1/2}
and
\|b_{R,z_{0}}\|_{L_{d}(\Omega_{R,z_{0}})}=\|b\|_{L_{d}(\Omega)}\leq\|b\|.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Now, we apply Lemma
4.9
to derive the following Aleksandrov-type estimate

\sup_{\Omega}u_{+}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u_{+}+N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/p}\|\left(\mathcal{L}u\right)_{-}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

using a standard approximation argument that ensures the existence of Green’s functions; see Remark
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We begin by fixing a number

q\in(d/(d-1),d/(d-2))

(with the convention
d/(d-2):=\infty
when
d=2
), appeared in Lemma
4.9
.
We set
d_{0}=d_{0}(d,\delta,\|b\|)\in(d/2,d)
be the conjugate exponent of
q
.
Note that it is sufficient to prove (
2.7
) for the case
p=d_{0}
, that is,

\sup_{\Omega}u_{+}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u_{+}+N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/d_{0}}\|\left(\mathcal{L}u\right)_{-}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)},

(4.26)

as the case
p>d_{0}
reduces to the case
p=d_{0}
by Hölder’s inequality.
To prove (
4.26
), we proceed through a series of approximations, reducing the problem to a smooth setting.

Step 1: Reduction to a smooth domain.

For given
\Omega
, let
\{\Omega_{k}\}
be a sequence of bounded
C^{2}
domains such that
\Omega_{k}\nearrow\Omega
as
k\to\infty
.
We observe that the conclusion of the theorem for each
\Omega_{k}
implies the desired estimate on
\Omega
.
More precisely, if (
4.26
) holds for
\Omega_{k}
in place of
\Omega
, we have the following:
For any
x\in\Omega
and
u\in W_{d_{0},\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
, there is
k
such that
x\in\Omega_{k}
and

\displaystyle u_{+}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega_{k}}u_{+}+N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega_{k})^{2-d/d_{0}}\|\left(\mathcal{L}u\right)_{-}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega_{k}}u_{+}+N(d,\delta,\|b\|)\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/d_{0}}\|\left(\mathcal{L}u\right)_{-}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)},

where the last inequality is due to
d_{0}>d/2
and
\Omega_{k}\nearrow\Omega
.
Then by letting
k\to\infty
we obtain the desired inequality for general bounded
\Omega
.
This step allows us to assume
\Omega
is sufficiently smooth (
e.g.

\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
) and assume
u\in W_{d_{0}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
, rather than merely being locally in the appropriate function space.

Step 2: Reduction to smooth
u
.

We now restrict our attention to the case where
\Omega
has a
C^{2}
boundary and
u\in W_{d_{0}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
.
In this setting, there exists a family of smooth functions
\{u^{\varepsilon}\}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
approximating
u
in
W_{d_{0}}^{2}(\Omega)
, which is continuously embedded into
C(\overline{\Omega})
since
d_{0}>d/2
.
We observe that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}u-\mathcal{L}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\|D^{2}u-D^{2}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)}+\left\|b\left(Du-Du^{\varepsilon}\right)\right\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\|D^{2}u-D^{2}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)}+\|b\|_{L_{d}(\Omega)}\left\|Du-Du^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{\frac{dd_{0}}{d-d_{0}}}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left\|u-u^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W_{d_{0}}^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\,\,\varepsilon\to 0,

where the last inequality is due to Sobolev embedding and
N
is independent of
\varepsilon
.
Also, it is clear that

\sup_{\partial\Omega}u^{\varepsilon}_{+}\to\sup_{\partial\Omega}u_{+}\quad\textrm{as}\,\,\varepsilon\to 0.

Thus, if (
4.26
) holds for
u^{\varepsilon}
for each
\varepsilon>0
(with smooth
\Omega
), by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, the same conclusion holds for general
u\in W_{d_{0}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
.
This, together with Step 1, allows us to assume
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
.

Step 3: Reduction to regular
a^{ij}
and
b^{i}
.

Now we focus on the case where
\Omega
has a
C^{2}
boundary and
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
.
We approximate the coefficients
a^{ij}
and
b^{i}
by

a^{ij}_{\varepsilon}:=(a^{ij})^{(\varepsilon)}\quad\textrm{and}\quad b^{i}_{\varepsilon}:=(b^{i}I_{\Omega})^{(\varepsilon)},

where
f^{(\varepsilon)}
means the standard mollification of a locally integrable function
f
.
From the basic properties of mollification, we see that
a^{ij}_{\varepsilon}
are uniformly continuous and
b^{i}_{\varepsilon}
are bounded.
We also note that
a^{ij}_{\varepsilon}
satisfy the ellipticity condition with same
\delta
, and
\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{d}(\Omega)}\leq\|b\|
, where
b_{\varepsilon}=(b_{\varepsilon}^{1},\ldots,b_{\varepsilon}^{d})
.
Moreover, since
d_{0}<d
,

a^{ij}_{\varepsilon}\to a^{ij},\quad b^{i}_{\varepsilon}\to b^{i}\quad\textrm{in}\,\,L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and then, having in mind that
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, it follows that

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u:=a^{ij}_{\varepsilon}D_{ij}u+b^{i}_{\varepsilon}D_{i}u\to\mathcal{L}u\quad\textrm{in}\,\,L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Thus, if (
4.26
) holds for
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
for each
\varepsilon>0
(with smooth
\Omega
and
u
), by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, the same conclusion holds for general
\mathcal{L}
.

Step 4: The smooth case.

Due to the above reductions, it suffices to prove (
4.26
) with further assumptions that
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
,
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
,
a^{ij}
are uniformly continuous, and
b^{i}
are bounded.
From Remark
2.1
, the Green’s function
G_{\Omega}
exists and

w(x):=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\,(\mathcal{L}u(y))_{-}\,dy,\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$,}

solves
\mathcal{L}w=-(\mathcal{L}u)_{-}
in
\Omega
and
w=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
It follows from (
4.24
) that

\sup_{\Omega}w_{+}\leq N(d,\delta,\|b\|)\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/d_{0}}\|(\mathcal{L}u)_{-}\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, by Theorem
3.3
, we have

\sup_{\Omega}v_{+}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}v_{+}=\sup_{\partial\Omega}u_{+},

where
v=u-w
.
Combining the above inequalities yields the desired estimate (
4.26
).
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We now drop the regularity assumptions on
a^{ij}
,
b^{i}
, and
\partial\Omega
in the statement of Lemma
4.9
, and assume only the existence of the Green’s function. That is, we proceed to prove Theorem
2.4
.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
d_{0}=d_{0}(d,\delta,\|b\|)\in(d/2,d)
be the constant from Theorem
2.2
.
We use the duality argument.
Let
f
be a nonnegative function in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then, by the definition of the Green’s function, we see that

u(x):=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy,\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$,}

is in
W_{d,\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})\subset W_{d_{0},\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
and solves
\mathcal{L}u=-f
in
\Omega
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
Then, by applying the estimate (
2.7
) to
u
, we obtain that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\,f(y)\,dy\leq N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/d_{0}}\|f\|_{L_{d_{0}}(\Omega)}.

Combining the above with the fact that
G_{\Omega}\geq 0
, we reach to

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}\leq N\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)^{2-d/d_{0}},

where
q
is the conjugate exponent of
d_{0}
. Thus, the desired result has been established.
∎

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the inhomogeneous nonlinear
Schrödinger (INLS) equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{iu_{t}+\Delta u=\lambda\left|x\right|^{-b}\left|u\right|^{\sigma}u{\rm,}}\\
{u\left(0,\;x\right)=u_{0}\left(x\right),}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
u:{\rm\;}\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
,
u_{{\rm 0}}:{\rm\;\;}\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C},{\rm\;}b,{\rm\;}\sigma>0
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
\lambda<0
is the focusing case
and
\lambda>0
is the defocusing case.

The case
b=0
is the well-known classic nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation
which has been widely studied during the last three decades. One the other
hand, in the end of the last century, it was suggested that in some situations the
beam propagation can be modeled by the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger
equation in the following form:

iu_{t}+\Delta u+V\left(x\right)\left|u\right|^{\sigma}u=0.

(1.2)

We refer the reader to [3, 4, 16, 19] for the physical background and
applications of (
1.2
). E.q. (
1.2
) has attracted
a lot of interest in the last two decades. We also refer the reader to [2,
5-15, 18, 20-22, 24] for recent work on E.q. (
1.2
). The INLS
equation (
1.1
) is a particular case of (
1.2
)
and it has also been studied by many authors in recent years. But several
challenging technical difficulties arise in its study and therefore there are
many unsolved problems in its study. For instance, even the local
well-posedness for (
1.1
) with initial data in
L^{2}
in the
mass-critical case
\sigma=\frac{4-2b}{n}
is still an open problem (cf. [18]). We refer
the reader to [5-12, 14, 15, 18] for recent work on (
1.1
).

Before recalling the existence results for (
1.1
), let us give
some information for this equation. The INLS equation (
1.1
) has
the following equivalent form:

u\left(t\right)=S\left(t\right)u_{0}-i\lambda\int_{0}^{t}S\left(t-\tau\right)\left|x\right|^{-b}\left|u\left(\tau\right)\right|^{\sigma}u\left(\tau\right)d\tau,

(1.3)

where
S\left(t\right)=e^{it\Delta}
is Schrödinger semi-group. Recall that
the INLS equation (
1.1
) has the following conservation laws:

M\left(u\left(t\right)\right):=\left\|u\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{2}=M\left(u_{0}\right),

(1.4)

E\left(u\left(t\right)\right):=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2+\sigma}\left\|\left|x\right|^{-b}\left|u\right|^{\sigma+2}\right\|_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}=E\left(u_{0}\right).

(1.5)

It is well-known that under a standard scaling argument, the critical power in

H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
with
s<\frac{n}{2}
for (
1.1
) is
given by

\sigma_{s}=\frac{4-2b}{n-2s}.

(1.6)

When
s<\frac{n}{2}
, corresponding to the critical case,
\sigma<\frac{4-2b}{n-2s}
is said to be a subcritical power in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. If
s\geq\frac{n}{2}
,
\sigma<\infty
is said to be a subcritical
power in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. Especially if
\sigma=\frac{4-2b}{n}
,
the problem is known as
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)-
critical or mass-critical;
if
\sigma=\frac{4-2b}{n-2}
, it is called
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)-
critical or energy-critical.

We briefly recall some local and global well-posedness results for the INLS
equation (
1.1
). Cazenave [5] studied the well-posedness in

H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. Using an abstract theory, he proved that it is
appropriate to seek solution of (
1.1
) satisfying

u\in C\left(\left[0,{\rm\;}T\right),\;H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)\bigcap C^{1}\left(\left[0,{\rm\;}T\right),\;H^{-1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)

for some
T>0
. He also proved that any local solution of the defocusing INLS
equation (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

extends globally in time. Using the abstract theory developed by Cazenave [5],
Genoud–Stuart [14] also studied the local and global well-posedness in
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for the focusing INLS equation. They proved that the
focusing INLS equation (
1.1
) with
0<b<\min\{2,{\rm\;}n\}

is locally well-posed in
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
in the subcritical case,
i.e. in the case
0<\sigma<\sigma^{*}
where

\sigma^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\frac{4-2b}{n-2},{\rm\;}n\geq 3,}\\
{\infty,{\rm\;}n=1,{\rm\;}2.}\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

They also established the large data global well-posedness in the case

0<\sigma<\sigma_{0}
and the small data global well-posedness in the case

\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma<\sigma^{*}
, where
\sigma_{0}
is
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)-
critical power.
Later, Genoud [15] and Farah [10] also studied the global well-posedness in
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for the
focusing INLS equation (
1.1
) with
0<b<\min\left\{2,{\rm\;}n\right\}
in the case
\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma<\sigma^{*}
by using some
sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities. Recently, Guzmán [18] used contraction
mapping principle based on the Strichartz estimates for the first time to
establish the local and global well-posedness of the INLS equation
(
1.1
). He proved that the INLS equation (
1.1
)
with
0<b<\min\left\{2,{\rm\;}n\right\}
is locally well-posed in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
in the mass-subcritical case, i.e.
0<\sigma<\frac{4-2b}{n}
and that the local solution above extends globally in time by
using the mass-conservation law. He also studied the local and global
well-posedness in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
with
0<s\leq\min\left\{1,{\rm\;}\frac{n}{2}\right\}
. He proved that the INLS equation is
locally well-posed in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
if
0<b<\tilde{2}
,
0<s\leq\min\left\{1,{\rm\;}\frac{n}{2}\right\}
and
0<\sigma<\tilde{\sigma}
where

\tilde{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\frac{n}{3},{\rm\;}n=1,{\rm\;}2,{\rm\;}3,}\\
{2,{\rm\;}n\geq 4,}\end{array}\right.\tilde{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\sigma_{s},{\rm\;}s<\frac{n}{2},}\\
{\infty,{\rm\;}s=\frac{n}{2}.}\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

Moreover, he established the small data global well-posedness in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
in the case
0<s\leq\min\left\{1,{\rm\;}\frac{n}{2}\right\}
,
0<b<\tilde{2}
and
\sigma_{0}<\sigma<\tilde{\sigma}
. Later, Dihn [7] improved the local well-posedness result in
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
of [18] by extending the range of
b
. See also Theorem 1.2 of [8].

But the local and global well-posedness in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
with
0\leq s<\frac{n}{2}
for the
INLS equation (
1.1
) in the critical case, i.e.
\sigma=\frac{4-2b}{n-2s}
have remained open until now. See [9] or Remark 1.7 of [18]
for example.

In this paper, we mainly establish the local well-posedness and small data
global well-posedness in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for the mass-critical INLS
equation which have remained open until now. To arrive at this goal, we
first establish the Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger semi-group in
Lorentz spaces. We know that the Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger
semi-group in Soblev spaces or Besov spaces play an important role in the study of
classic nonlinear Schrödinger equation. But these estimates don’t give the
sufficient tools to study the mass-critical INLS equation. For this reason, we
establish the Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger semi-group in Lorentz
spaces and by using them, we prove the local well-posedness and small data
global well-posedness in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for the mass-critical INLS
equation (see Theorem 1.1). In addition, we give the alternative proof of the
local well-posedness in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for the mass-subcritical
INLS equation (see Theorem 1.3).

Before stating our main results, we define the important numbers which are used
throughout the paper:

2^{*}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\frac{2n}{n-2},{\rm\;}n\geq 3,}\\
{\infty,{\rm\;}n=1,{\rm\;}2,}\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

\frac{1}{\gamma(\cdot)}=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\cdot}\right).

(1.10)

The main results are the following local well-posedness and small data global
well-posedness in
L^{2}
for the mass-critical INLS equation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<b<\min\left\{2,{\rm\;}n\right\}
and
\sigma=\frac{4-2b}{n}
. If
u_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, then there exists
T^{*}=T^{*}\left(u_{0}\right)>0
such that
(
1.1
) has a unique solution

u\in L_{loc}^{\gamma\left(r\right)}\left(\left[0,{\rm\;}T^{*}\right),{\rm\;}L^{r,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right),

(1.11)

where

r=\frac{n\left(\sigma+2\right)}{n-b}.

(1.12)

If
T^{*}<\infty
, then

L^{\gamma\left(r\right)}\left(\left[0,{\rm\;}T^{*}\right),{\rm\;}L^{r,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)=\infty.

(1.13)

Moreover, for any
2\leq p<2^{*}
, we have

u\in L_{loc}^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(\left[0,{\rm\;}T^{*}\right),{\rm\;}L^{p,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right),

(1.14)

If
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{2}
is sufficiently small, then the above
solution is a global one, i.e.
T^{*}=\infty
and

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(\left[0,{\rm\;}\infty\right),{\rm\;}L^{p,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)}\lesssim\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{2},

(1.15)

for any
2\leq p<2^{*}
.

The similar statements are valid in the negative time direction and
we omit the details.

Remark 1.2
.

In theorem 1.1,
T^{*}
depends on not only

\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{2}
but also the choice of
u_{0}
in
L^{2}
,
which is easily seen from the scaling
u\left(t,{\rm\;}x\right)\to u_{\lambda}\left(t,{\rm\;}x\right):=\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}u\left(\lambda^{2}t,{\rm\;}\lambda x\right)
. Thus we can’t use the mass conservation law to extend
the local solution above to global one.

We also have the following local well-posedness in
L^{2}
for the

Strichartz estimates in Lorentz spaces

In this section, we establish the Strichartz estimates in Lorentz spaces

L^{p,2}

First we obtain the time decay estimates in Lorentz spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},S\left(t\right)=e^{it\Delta}
and
2\leq p<\infty
. Then

\left\|S\left(t\right)f\right\|_{L^{p,2}}\lesssim\left|t\right|^{-n\left({1\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{12}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}2}-{1\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{1p}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}p}\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},2}}.

(3.1)

Proof.

For any
2<p<r<\infty
, we can take
\theta\in\left(0,{\rm\;}1\right)
such that
\frac{1-\theta}{{\rm 2}}+\frac{\theta}{r}=\frac{1}{p}

It is well-known that

\left\|S\left(t\right)f\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left|t\right|^{-n\left({1\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{12}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}2}-{1\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{1p}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}p}\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},

(3.2)

for every
2\leq p\leq\infty
. Noticing

\left\|S\left(t\right)f\right\|_{L^{2,2}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2,2}},

(3.3)

\left\|S\left(t\right)f\right\|_{L^{r,r}}\lesssim\left|t\right|^{-n\left({1\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{12}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}2}-{1\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{1r}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}r}\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{r^{\prime},r^{\prime}}}.

(3.4)

Using (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and the real interpolation
between Lorentz spaces (see Theorem 5.3.1 of [1]):
\left(L^{2,2},{\rm\;}L^{r,r}\right)_{\theta,{\rm\;}2}=L^{p{\rm,2}}
, we have
(
3.1
) for
2<p<\infty
. (
3.1

(
3.3
) if

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S\left(t\right)=e^{it\Delta}

\left\|S\left(t\right)\phi\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(\mathbb{R},L^{p,2}\right)}\lesssim\left\|\phi\right\|_{2},

(3.5)

\left\|A_{S}f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(I,L^{p,2}\right)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(r\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,L^{r^{\prime},2}\right)},

(3.6)

We use the well-known dual estimate techniques (cf. [25]) and

Step 1.
Using time-decay estimate (
3.1
) and

\left\|A_{S}f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(I,L^{p,2}\right)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,L^{p^{\prime},2}\right)},

(3.7)

for
2\leq p<2^{*}

(3.8)

for any
2\leq p<2^{*}
and

Step 2.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|A_{S}f\right\|_{2}&=\left(\int_{0}^{t}S\left(t-\tau\right)f\left(\tau\right)d\tau,{\rm\;}\int_{0}^{t}S\left(t-\sigma\right)f\left(\sigma\right)d\sigma\right)\\

for any

Step 3.
We have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\left(A_{S}f,{\rm\;}\varphi\left(t\right)\right)dt&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\left(S\left(t-\tau\right)f\left(\tau\right),{\rm\;}\varphi\left(t\right)\right)d\tau dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\left(f\left(\tau\right),{\rm\;}S\left(\tau-t\right)\varphi\left(t\right)\right)dtd\tau\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\left(f\left(\tau\right),{\rm\;}\int_{\tau}^{T}S\left(\tau-t\right)\varphi\left(t\right)dt\right)d\tau.\end{split}

(3.13)

Thus using Hölder inequality, (
3.12
) and
(
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\left(A_{S}f,{\rm\;}\varphi\left(t\right)\right)dt\right|&\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{2}\right)}\left\|\int_{\tau}^{T}S\left(\tau-t\right)\varphi\left(t\right)dt\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{2}\right)}\\
&\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{2}\right)}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,L^{p^{\prime},2}\right)}.\end{split}

(3.14)

Using (
3.14
) and the duality (see Section 1.4.3 of [17]), we can obtain

\left\|A_{S}f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(I,L^{p,2}\right)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{2}\right)}.

(3.15)

Step 4.
We prove (
3.6
) by using (
3.7
),
(
3.11
) and (
3.15
). We divide study in two cases.

Case 1.

p\in\left[2,{\rm\;}r\right]
. We can take
\theta\in\left[0,{\rm\;}1\right]
such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{\theta}{r},{\rm\;}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(p\right)}=\frac{1-\theta}{\infty}+\frac{\theta}{\gamma\left(r\right)}.

(3.16)

It follows from (
3.7
), (
3.11
), Lemma 2.4 and
Hölder inequality that

\left\|A_{S}f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{p,2}\right)}\lesssim\left\|A_{S}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{2,2}\right)}^{1-\theta}\left\|A_{S}f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(r\right)}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{r,2}\right)}^{\theta}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(r\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,L^{r^{\prime},2}\right)}.

(3.17)

Case 2.

p>r\geq 2
. We can take
\theta\in\left(0,{\rm\;}1\right)
such
that

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{\theta}{p},{\rm\;}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(r\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}=\frac{1-\theta}{1}+\frac{\theta}{\gamma\left(p\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}.

(3.18)

Using Theorem 5.1.2 of [1] and the complex interpolation (cf. [23]):
\left[L^{2,2},{\rm\;}L^{p^{\prime},2}\right]_{\theta}=L^{r^{\prime},2}
, we have the following complex interpolation:

\left[L^{1}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{2}\right),{\rm\;}L^{\gamma\left(p\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,L^{p^{\prime},2}\right)\right]_{\theta}=L^{\gamma\left(r\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,L^{r^{\prime},2}\right).

(3.19)

Hence, (

A_{S}:{\rm\;}L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(I,{\rm\;}L^{p,2}\right)\to L^{\gamma\left(r\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,L^{r^{\prime},2}\right)

(3.20)

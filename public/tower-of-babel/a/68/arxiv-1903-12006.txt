Poisson Principal Bundles This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Noncommutative Manifolds and their Symmetries in honour of Giovanni Landi. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Landi.html
Shahn MAJID and Liam WILLIAMS

Abstract
We semiclassicalise the theory of quantum group principal bundles to the level of Poisson geometry. The total space X X is a Poisson manifold with Poisson-compatible contravariant connection, the fibre is a Poisson–Lie group in the sense of Drinfeld with bicovariant Poisson-compatible contravariant connection, and the base has an inherited Poisson structure and Poisson-compatible contravariant connection. The latter are known to be the semiclassical data for a quantum differential calculus. The theory is illustrated by the Poisson level of the q q -Hopf fibration on the standard q q -sphere. We also construct the Poisson level of the spin connection on a principal bundle.

Introduction

It is known since the work of Drinfeld in the 1980’s
[
13
,
14
]
that the infinitesimal notion of a quantum group is that of a Lie bialgebra. This is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a ‘Lie cobracket’ map

1

enveloping algebra deformations. The same applies to the bicrossproduct family of quantum groups associated to Lie algebra factorisations
[
25

which tend to coordinate algebras of inhomogeneous groups or dually to their enveloping algebras and have a role in quantum spacetime models.

Meanwhile, quantum groups also led in the 1990s to the emergence of a constructive approach to quantum differential geometry including but not limited to their quantum geometry. By now, there is a significant body of work and we refer to the text
[
4
]

[
10

[

[

,

and base algebra

Our results effectively extend Drinfeld’s work to a Poisson–Lie group

Meanwhile, one of the powerful lessons of quantum geometry model building is that very often no quantum differential calculus of the expected classical dimension exists. There are broadly two remedies to this ‘quantum anomaly for differentiation’. One is to allow an extra dimension in the cotangent bundle to absorb the anomaly

Because this work stands at a triple point of Poisson geometry, noncommutative differential geometry and deformation theory, there are necessarily significant preliminaries, collected in Section

The paper concludes with some directions for further work. Note that the seminal work
[
17

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Elements of noncommutative differential geometry

We outline a formalism of noncommutative differential geometry in a bimodule approach, including Hopf algebras with differential structure. The classical model is
A=C^{\infty}(M)
for a smooth real manifold
M
with exterior algebra
\Omega(M)
, while the general formulation works over any unital algebra with an algebraically defined exterior algebra
\Omega_{A}
.

Thus, a ‘differential calculus’ on a unital algebra
A
consists of a graded algebra
\Omega_{A}=\oplus\Omega^{n}_{A}
with
n
-forms
\Omega^{n}_{A}
for
n\geq 0
, an associative product
\wedge\colon\Omega^{n}_{A}\otimes\Omega^{m}_{A}\rightarrow\Omega^{n+m}_{A}
and an exterior derivative
{\rm d}\colon\Omega^{n}_{A}\rightarrow\Omega^{n+1}_{A}
satisfying the graded-Leibniz rule. Here
\Omega^{0}_{A}=A
and we assume that
\Omega_{A}
is generated by degree
0
,
1
and that
\Omega^{1}_{A}
is spanned by products of
A
and
{\rm d}A
. Both conditions are slightly stronger than a DGA in homological algebra.

Vector bundles are expressed as (typically projective)
A
-modules
E
(classically, this would be the space of sections). A left connection on this is
\nabla_{E}\colon E\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{A}\otimes_{A}E
obeying the left Leibniz rule
\nabla_{E}(ae)={\rm d}a\otimes e+a\nabla_{E}e
for all
a\in A
,
e\in E
. We have a
bimodule connection

[
16
,
31
]
if
E
is a bimodule and there is a bimodule map with

\sigma_{E}\colon\ E\otimes_{A}\Omega^{1}_{A}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{A}\otimes_{A}E,\qquad\nabla_{E}(ea)=(\nabla_{E}e)a+\sigma_{E}(e\otimes_{A}{\rm d}a).

If
\sigma_{E}
is well-defined then it is uniquely determined, so its existence is a property of a left connection on a bimodule. Bimodule connections extend to tensor products, giving us a monoidal category, where the tensor product connection on
E\otimes_{A}F
uses
\sigma_{E}
to reorder the output of
\nabla_{F}
. More details and results are in
[
4
]
.

The notion of a Hopf algebra is covered in several texts, e.g.,
[
25
]
. Briefly, this means a unital algebra
H
which is also a coalgebra with counit
\epsilon\colon H\to k
(for
k
the ground field) and coproduct
\Delta\colon H\to H\otimes H
, with these maps being algebra homomorphisms, and for which an antipode
S\colon H\to H
exists. The latter is required to obey
(Sh{}_{\scriptstyle(1)})h{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}=\epsilon(h)=h{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}Sh{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}
in the ‘Sweedler notation’
\Delta h=h{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}\otimes h{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}
(sum of such terms implicit). A left action of a classical group on a vector space
V
corresponds in our function algebra point of view to a right coaction
\Delta_{R}\colon V\to V\otimes H
obeying the arrow-reversal of the usual axioms for a right action. If
V=A
then we say that
A
is a
comodule algebra
if
\Delta_{R}
is an algebra homomorphism. If we have a suitable dually paired Hopf algebra (the algebraic dual typically being to big) then
A
becomes a
module algebra
for the left action
{\triangleright}
given by evaluation against the left factor of the output of
\Delta_{A}
, meaning
\phi{\triangleright}(ab)=(\phi{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}{\triangleright}a)(\phi{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}{\triangleright}b)
and
\phi{\triangleright}1=\epsilon(\phi)1
for
\phi
in the dually paired Hopf algebra. If
H
coacts from the left then we similarly have a right action
{\triangleleft}
by evaluation against the left factor of the output of
\Delta_{L}
.

In particular, a Hopf algebra coacts on itself as a comodule algebra from both the left and the right via
\Delta
. A calculus
\Omega^{1}_{H}
is left (resp. right) translation covariant if the coaction extends to
\Omega^{1}
by

\Delta_{L}(h{\rm d}g)=h{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}g{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}\otimes h{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}{\rm d}g{}_{\scriptstyle(2)},\qquad\Delta_{R}(h{\rm d}g)=h{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}{\rm d}g{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}\otimes h{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}g{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}

(making it a ‘Hopf module’) with the coaction commuting with
{\rm d}
. A calculus is called bicovariant if both coactions extend, and strongly bicovariant if
\Delta_{L}+\Delta_{R}
makes the exterior algebra
\Omega_{H}
into a super-Hopf algebra. We will not need higher forms, but this is true for the canonical
\Omega_{H}
in the bicovariant case in
[
36
]
.

Finally, and equally briefly, the data for a quantum principal bundle is
P
a right
H
-comodule algebra with ‘base’ the algebra
A=P^{H}=\{p\in P\,|\,\Delta_{R}p=p\otimes 1\}
of elements fixed under
\Delta_{R}p=p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\otimes p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}
in a compact notation. We assume that
\Omega^{1}_{P}
is
H
-covariant in that
\Delta_{R}
extends in a similar way to right-covariance of
\Omega^{1}_{H}
above. In this case
\Omega^{1}_{A}=A{\rm d}A
computed in
\Omega^{1}_{P}
is the inherited calculus. We also assume that
\Omega^{1}_{H}
is bicovariant and let
\Lambda^{1}_{H}
be the space of left-invariant 1-forms. Next, we assume that there is a well-defined map
{\rm ver}\colon\Omega^{1}_{P}\to P\otimes\Lambda^{1}_{H}
(the generator of vector fields for the infinitesimal action) given by

\text{ver}(p{\rm d}q)=pq{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\otimes\big(Sq{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}\big){\rm d}q_{\bar{(1)}((2)}=pq{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\otimes\varpi\pi_{\epsilon}\big(q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}\big),\qquad\forall\,p,q\in P,

(2.1)

where
\pi_{\epsilon}\colon H\to H^{+}
defined by
\pi_{\epsilon}(h)=h-1\epsilon(h)
projects onto the kernel of the counit, and the Maurer–Cartan form
\varpi\colon H^{+}\to\Omega^{1}
is defined by
\varpi(h)=Sh{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}{\rm d}h{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}
and affords an identification of
\Lambda^{1}_{H}
as a quotient of
H^{+}
. Finally, as a replacement for local triviality, we require a short exact sequence of left
P
-modules

0\longrightarrow P\Omega^{1}_{A}P\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{P}\xrightarrow{\text{ver}}P\otimes\Lambda^{1}_{H}\longrightarrow 0.

(2.2)

More details are in
[
7
,
28
]
and
[
4
, Chapter 5]
. We will at certain points need to discuss this algebraic theory, adapted to a putative formal deformation version, but only to the point of extracting Poisson level data which then make sense by themselves in the smooth setting.

2.2
Elements of deformation theory

In deformation theory, it is usual to adapt the algebraic context above by now working over the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[[\lambda]]
for a formal deformation parameter
\lambda
and with an associative
{\mathbb{C}}[[\lambda]]
-linear
*
-product of the form
a*b=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}C_{i}(a,b)\lambda^{i}
on
A=C^{\infty}(M)[[\lambda]]
, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
. In this context, it is well known that

a*b-b*a=\lambda\{a,b\}+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big).

defines a Poisson bracket
\{a,b\}=C_{1}(a,b)-C_{1}(b,a)
for
a,b\in C^{\infty}(M)
. We denote the associated Poisson bivector by
\pi
so that
\{a,b\}=\pi({\rm d}a,{\rm d}b)
. Similarly, if we have a vector bundle over
M
with space of sections
\Gamma
as a projective module over
C^{\infty}(M)
, and this is deformed to an
A
-bimodule
\Gamma[[\lambda]]
with left and right bullet products
a\bullet\eta=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}L_{i}(a,\eta)\lambda^{i}
and
\eta\bullet a=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}R_{i}(\eta,a)\lambda^{i}
then

a\bullet\eta-\eta\bullet a=\lambda\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}\eta+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)

for all
a\in C^{\infty}(M),\eta\in\Gamma
, where
\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}\eta:=L_{1}(a,\eta)-R_{1}(\eta,a)
is a
contravariant connection
or ‘Lie–Rinehart’ covariant derivative
[
9
,
17
,
21
]
characterised by

\hat{\nabla}_{a\zeta}=a\hat{\nabla}_{\zeta},\qquad\hat{\nabla}_{\zeta}(a\eta)=\pi(\zeta,{\rm d}a)\eta+a\hat{\nabla}_{\zeta}\eta,

(2.3)

for all
\zeta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. In the strictly associative case, this will be flat (as a special case of a more general statement in
[
9
]
). We will be interested in the case
\Gamma=\Omega^{1}(M)
, the classical space of 1-forms, and for
\Omega^{1}[[\lambda]]
to obey the Leibniz rule with an undeformed exterior derivative
{\rm d}
, which requires the further
Poisson-compatibility

[\eta,\tau]=\hat{\nabla}_{\eta}\tau-\hat{\nabla}_{\tau}\eta

(2.4)

with respect to a ‘Schouten bracket’
[\eta,\tau]
of 1-forms defined by
[{\rm d}a,{\rm d}b]={\rm d}\{a,b\}
along with
[a\eta,b\tau]=ab[\eta,\tau]+a\pi(\eta,{\rm d}b)\tau-b\pi(\tau,{\rm d}a)\eta
. More usually in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
), condition (
2.4
) is called ‘torsionless’ and Poisson-compatible refers to something stronger, whereas we reserve torsion for its usual meaning for linear connections. Equations (
2.3
)–(
2.4
) are the geometric version on general 1-forms of the conditions
[
1
,
3
,
20
]

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}(ab)}\eta=a\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}b}\eta+(\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}\eta)b,\qquad\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}(b\eta)=b\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}(\eta)+\{a,b\}\eta,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\{a,b\}=\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}{\rm d}b-\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}b}{\rm d}a

coming out of the order
\lambda
analysis. The point of view in
[
1
,
3
]
was to think if this equivalently as a partially defined connection or preconnection
\nabla
by
\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}=\nabla_{\hat{a}}
, where
\hat{a}=\{a,\;\}
is the Hamitonian vector field associated to
a\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\nabla
is a usual connection but only defined along such vector fields. Both points of view are useful. In the symplectic case, the data is equivalent to a linear connection which preserves the Poisson tensor up to torsion, and the contravariant
\hat{\nabla}
is its pull back along the map
\pi^{\#}\colon\Omega^{1}(M)\to\rm{Vect}(M)
given by
\pi^{\#}(\eta)=\pi(\eta,\ )
with
\pi^{\#}({\rm d}a)=\hat{a}
.

The strictly associative setting is, however, often not compatible with deformation theory in that for examples related to quantum groups, one may not have
\Omega^{1}
,
{\rm d}
with desired covariance properties and with expected classical dimension over the noncommutative algebra (i.e., even in the free module case where there is a dimension). This was noted at the Poisson level in
[
1
,
20
]
and leads into a somewhat different point of view
[
3
]
where we consider formal products of the form

\displaystyle a*b=ab+{\lambda\over 2}\{a,b\}+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big),\qquad a\bullet\eta=a\eta+{\lambda\over 2}\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}\eta+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big),

\displaystyle\eta\bullet a=a\eta-{\lambda\over 2}\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}\eta+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)

(2.5)

but demand associativity and the bimodule property only
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
. The minimal setting is to assume only that
\{\ ,\ \}
is a biderivation and
\hat{\nabla}
a possibly curved contravariant connection compatible with it. This leads into fully nonassociative geometry, while the most important special case appears to be the intermediate one where
*
is associative (deformation-quantising a Poisson manifold) but
\bullet
is not necessarily a bimodule structure
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
, so that
\{\ ,\ \}
is a Poisson bracket but
\hat{\nabla}
can be curved. This leads to a self-contained setting, which we adopt, of a Poisson manifold equipped with possibly curved Poisson-compatible contravariant connection.

2.3
Elements of Poisson–Lie theory

Following the work of Drinfeld
[
14
]
, the semiclassical object underlying a deformation Hopf algebra is a
Poisson–Lie group
(PLG). This means a Lie group
G
which is also a Poisson manifold such that the multiplication map
G\times G\rightarrow G
is a Poisson map, where
G\times G
is equipped with the product Poisson structure. In terms of the Poisson tensor
\pi
, this is

\pi(gh)=L_{g*}(\pi(h))+R_{h*}(\pi(g))

for all
g,h\in G
. Here
R_{g}\colon G\to G
and
L_{g}\colon G\to G
are right and left translation by
g
and have derivatives
R_{h*}\colon T_{g}G\to T_{gh}G
and
L_{g*}\colon T_{h}G\to T_{gh}G
. The corresponding data at the Lie algebra level is a
Lie bialgebra
, i.e.,
(\mathfrak{g},\delta)
where
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie coalgebra (so its dual is a Lie bracket map
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
), with
\delta
a
1
-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}
relative to the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
in the sense

\delta([\xi,\eta])=\rm{ad}_{\xi}(\delta(\eta))-\rm{ad}_{\eta}(\delta(\xi)),\qquad\forall\,\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}.

Next, the right and left actions of the group on itself define (respectively) left and right actions on the algebra of functions on the group
C^{\infty}(G)
. We define the
left-translation invariant formulation
as follows. There is a
right
action

(a{\triangleleft}h)(g)=a(hg),\qquad\forall\,g,h\in G,a\in C^{\infty}(G)

(usually made into a left action via the group inverse, but we refrain from this). Setting
g=e^{t\xi}
and differentiating at
t=0
gives a right action of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
,

(a{\triangleleft}\xi)(g)=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{0}a\big(e^{t\xi}g\big)=\grave{\xi}(a)(g),

defining an associated
right-invariant vector field

\grave{\xi}
. This can be defined also as
\grave{\xi}(g)=R_{g*}(\xi)
. The actions extends to 1-forms by

(\tau{\triangleleft}h)(g)=L_{h}^{*}(\tau(hg))

and
{\triangleleft}\xi={\mathcal{L}}_{\grave{\xi}}=i_{\grave{\xi}}{\rm d}+{\rm d}i_{\grave{\xi}}
on forms, where
i
denotes interior product and
{\mathcal{L}}
the Lie derivative. We similarly define the
left-invariant vector field

\tilde{\xi}
associated to
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
as
\tilde{\xi}(g)=L_{g*}\xi
for
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
equating to
\xi{\triangleright}
defined below by right translation. We also recall that there is a one to one correspondence between
1
-forms
\tau\in\Omega^{1}(G)
and
\tilde{\tau}\in C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})
via
\tilde{\tau}(g)=L^{*}_{g}(\tau(g))
or conversely
s\in C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})
defines a
1
-form which we will denote
\check{s}(g)=L^{*}_{g^{-1}}(s(g))
. In these terms, the action is
(s{\triangleleft}h)(g)=s(hg)
as for a function. In particular,
\check{v}(g)=L_{g^{-1}}^{*}v
is the left-translation invariant 1-form (invariant under
{\triangleleft}
) associated to
v\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and
{\langle}\tilde{\xi},\check{v}{\rangle}={\langle}\xi,v{\rangle}
is constant. We also have
\big\langle\xi,\big(\tilde{\rm d}a\big)(g)\big\rangle=\tilde{\xi}(a)(g)
. Finally, in the right translation-invariant formulation, we start with the left action

(h{\triangleright}a)(g)=a(gh),\qquad(\xi{\triangleright}a)(g)={{\rm d}\over{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}a\big(ge^{t\xi}\big)=\tilde{\xi}(a)(g)

is the left-invariant vector field
\tilde{\xi}
for the infinitesimal action of
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. The group action extends to differential forms
\tau\in\Omega^{1}(G)
by

(h{\triangleright}\tau)(g)=R^{*}_{h}(\tau(gh))

with Lie algebra action
{\triangleright}
given by Lie derivative along
\tilde{\xi}
. In this case, the relevant one to one correspondence between
1
-forms
\tau\in\Omega^{1}(G)
and, say,
\grave{\tau}\in C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is via
\grave{\tau}(g)=R^{*}_{g}(\tau(g))
with inverse
s\in C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})
defining a
1
-form
\breve{s}(g)=R^{*}_{g^{-1}}(s(g))
. In these terms, the action is
(h{\triangleright}s)(g)=s(gh)
as for a function. In particular,
\breve{v}(g)=R_{g^{-1}}^{*}v
is a right-translation invariant 1-form (invariant under
{\triangleright}
) associated to
v\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. We have
{\langle}\grave{\xi},\breve{v}{\rangle}={\langle}\xi,v{\rangle}
and
{\langle}\xi,(\grave{\rm d}a)(g){\rangle}=\grave{\xi}(a)(g)
where
\grave{\xi}(g)=R_{g*}\xi
is the right-invariant vector field associated to
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Using this second formulation, we can view any Poisson structure on a Lie group as the right translation of some map
D\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.,
\pi(g)=R_{g*}(D(g))
. We define

\delta\xi:={{\rm d}\over{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}D\big(e^{t\xi}\big)\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathfrak{g}.

Drinfeld showed that if
({\mathfrak{g}},\delta)
is a Lie bialgebra then the associated connected and simply connected Lie group
G
is a Poisson–Lie group by exponentiating
\delta
from a Lie algebra to a Lie group cocycle. Conversely, if
(G,D)
is a Poisson–Lie group, then its Lie algebra is a Lie bialgebra by differentiating
D
at the identity. Here
\delta
on a Lie bialgebra is a
1
-cocycle hence exponentiates to a 1-cocycle
D\in Z^{1}_{{\rm Ad}}(G,\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g})
. More details are in
[
25
]
.

Drinfeld
[
14
]
also introduced a deformation theory point of view where quantum groups
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
are deformations of
U({\mathfrak{g}})
as a Hopf algebra with
q=e^{\lambda\over 2}
. Suffice it to say that the coproduct homomorphism property applied to
\Delta(\xi\eta-\eta\xi)
leads at order
\lambda
to the cocycle axiom for
\delta:=\Delta-\tau\circ\Delta
, with
\tau
the transposition map. Meanwhile, if given
\delta
, we can suppose a specific form of deformation,

\Delta\xi=\xi\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi+\frac{\lambda}{2}\delta(\xi)+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)

(2.6)

for
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
as the analogue on the coalgebra side of the specific form in (
2.5
) on the algebra side. Here
\delta
can be viewed as a Lie bracket on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
which in turn is the Poisson bracket of linear functions on
G
near the identity. These quantum groups are dual to corresponding
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[G]
which again have formal deformation versions. A complication here is that
C^{\infty}(G)
is only a Hopf algebra with respect to a topological tensor product, but this can be handled in the compact connected case
[
5
]
. In practice, one can work with a dense algebraic model
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
, generated in the case of classical Lie type by the matrix entry coordinate functions
t^{i}{}_{j}
according to
\rho\colon G\subset M_{n}
. Here
\rho
extends to
U({\mathfrak{g}})
and provides a dual pairing
U({\mathfrak{g}})
by
{\langle}h,t^{i}{}_{j}{\rangle}=\rho(h)^{i}{}_{j}
of Hopf algebras. With such complications understood, we will similarly suppose for the sake of discussion some formal deformation
C^{\infty}(G)[[\lambda]]
for the specific leading form of product (
2.5
) and a dual
U({\mathfrak{g}})[[\lambda]]
with the leading form (
2.6
) of coproduct.

3
Poisson–Lie groups and their semiclassical calculi revisited

We start with a more geometric characterisation of the PLG condition.

Lemma 3.1

(cf.
[
12
,
23
,
35
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group with Poisson bracket
\{\ ,\ \}
vanishing at the identity and associated map
D\colon G\to{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
with differential at the identity
\delta(\xi)=\delta_{\xi}^{1}\otimes\delta_{\xi}^{2}

(
sum of such terms understood
)
. Then

\xi\triangleright\{a,b\}=\{\xi\triangleright a,b\}+\{a,\xi\triangleright b\}+\big(\delta_{\xi}^{1}\triangleright a\big)\big(\delta_{\xi}^{2}\triangleright b\big),

(3.1)

for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
,
a,b\in C^{\infty}(G)
if and only if
G
is a PLG via
\{\ ,\ \}
.

Proof.

The proof in one direction, where the PLG condition holds, is clear from
[
23
]
but we include it for completeness. This can also be found in
[
12
]
as well as easily derived from
[
35
, Proposition 10.7]
. We recall that
\{a,b\}(g)=\langle\pi(g),{\rm d}a\otimes{\rm d}b\rangle
, where evaluation here is the standard one between sections of the tangent bundle and cotangent bundle extended to tensor products. Then

\displaystyle\xi{\triangleright}\{a,b\}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\xi}(\{a,b\})={\langle}{\mathcal{L}}_{\tilde{\xi}}\pi,{\rm d}a\otimes{\rm d}b{\rangle}+\big\langle\pi,{\rm d}\tilde{\xi}(a)\otimes{\rm d}b+{\rm d}a\otimes{\rm d}\tilde{\xi}(b)\big\rangle

with the last two terms
\{\xi{\triangleright}a,b\}+\{a,\xi{\triangleright}b\}
. For the first term, if the PLG condition holds then

\displaystyle({\mathcal{L}}_{\tilde{\xi}}\pi)(g)

\displaystyle=(\xi{\triangleright}\pi)(g)={{\rm d}\over{\rm d}t}\Big|_{0}R_{e^{-t\xi}*}\pi\big(ge^{t\xi}\big)={{\rm d}\over{\rm d}t}\Big|_{0}\big(R_{e^{-t\xi}*}L_{g*}\pi\big(e^{t\xi}\big)+\pi(g)\big)

\displaystyle={{\rm d}\over{\rm d}t}\Big|_{0}L_{g*}D\big(e^{t\xi}\big)=L_{g*}\delta\xi,

which evaluates on
{\rm d}a\otimes{\rm d}b
to
\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\xi}(a)\tilde{\delta}^{2}_{\xi}(b)
, cf.
[
23
, p. 54]
. Conversely, if (
3.1
) holds then we deduce that the differential at
t=0
of
R_{e^{-t\xi}*}\pi\big(ge^{t\xi}\big)-R_{e^{-t\xi}*}L_{g*}\pi\big(e^{t\xi}\big)
vanishes, i.e., that
{\rm Ad}_{g*}\delta\xi={{\rm d}\over{\rm d}t}\big|_{0}D\big(ge^{t\xi}\big)=\tilde{\xi}(D)(g)
.
If we suppose that
D(e)=0
and that
G
is connected, then
D
is a 1-cocycle on the group (by the same argument as in the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 8.4.1]
). Hence
G
is a PLG.
∎

Next, we consider the semiclassical data for differential calculus, i.e., a Poisson-compatible contravariant connection
\hat{\nabla}
. For any contravariant connection, we define

\tilde{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}\colon\ C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\qquad\tilde{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}s:=\tilde{\ }\circ{\hat{\nabla}}_{{\rm d}a}\check{s},

where
\check{s}
is the 1-form corresponding to
s\in C^{\infty}(G,{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
and
\tilde{\ }
in this context turns a 1-form back into an element of
C^{\infty}(G,{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
, as explained in Section
2.3
.
The starting point in
[
1
]
is that
\tilde{\nabla}
necessarily has the form

(\tilde{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}s)(g)=\{a,s\}(g)+\tilde{\Xi}(g,\tilde{{\rm d}a}(g),s(g)),

(3.2)

for some map
\tilde{\Xi}\colon G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and is left translation covariant (in a manner corresponding to a calculus being left covariant in a Hopf algebra sense) if and only if
\tilde{\Xi}(g,\phi,\psi)
is independent of
g
for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, i.e., given by
\Xi\colon{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. In this case, the (contravariant) connection is Poisson-compatible if and only if

\Xi(\phi,\psi)-\Xi(\psi,\phi)=[\phi,\psi]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}},\qquad\forall\,\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

(3.3)

This uses the left-translation invariant formulation associated with right action
{\triangleleft}
. We equally well have a right-translation invariant formulation with left action
{\triangleright}
, and in fact we will focus proofs on this case. In this case,
\grave{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}\colon C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G,\mathfrak{g}^{*})
defined by
\grave{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}s:=\grave{\ }\circ{\hat{\nabla}}_{{\rm d}a}\breve{s}
has the form

(\grave{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}s)(g)=\{a,s\}(g)+\grave{\Xi}(g,\grave{{\rm d}a}(g),s(g))

and is right translation covariant if and only if
\grave{\Xi}
is again given by a constant
\Xi\colon{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, with Poisson-compatibility if and only if (
3.3
) holds. The latter and the contravariant connection being flat is equivalent to
\Xi
being a pre-Lie structure for
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}

[
30
]
(but we do not limit ourselves to this case.) Our next goal is a more geometric reformulation of these results as following from Lemma
3.1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a connected PLG and
\hat{\nabla}
a contravariant connection on
\Omega^{1}(G)
. Then
\hat{\nabla}
is right-translation covariant in the sense

\xi{\triangleright}\hat{\nabla}_{\eta}\tau=\hat{\nabla}_{\xi{\triangleright}\eta}\tau+\hat{\nabla}_{\eta}(\xi{\triangleright}\tau)+i_{\tilde{\delta_{\xi}^{1}}}(\eta)\big(\delta_{\xi}^{2}{\triangleright}\tau\big)

(3.4)

for all
\tau,\eta\in\Omega^{1}(G)
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
if and only if
\grave{\Xi}
is constant on
G
.

Proof.

In terms of
s\in C^{\infty}(G,{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
in the right-translation invariant formulation,

\displaystyle\big(\xi{\triangleright}\grave{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}s\big)(g)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\xi}(\{a,s\})+\tilde{\xi}(\grave{\Xi})\big(g,\grave{({\rm d}a)}(g),s(g)\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\grave{\Xi}\big(g,\tilde{\xi}(\grave{{\rm d}a})(g),s(g)\big)+\grave{\Xi}\big(g,\grave{({\rm d}a)}(g),\tilde{\xi}(s)(g)\big)

\displaystyle=\{\xi{\triangleright}a,s\}+\{a,\xi{\triangleright}s\}+\big(\delta^{1}_{\xi}{\triangleright}a\big)\big(\delta^{2}_{\xi}{\triangleright}s\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\tilde{\xi}(\grave{\Xi})\big(g,\grave{({\rm d}a)}(g),s(g)\big)+\grave{\Xi}\big(g,\tilde{\xi}(\grave{{\rm d}a})(g),s(g)\big)+\grave{\Xi}\big(g,\grave{({\rm d}a)}(g),\tilde{\xi}(s)(g)\big)

\displaystyle=\grave{\nabla}_{{\rm d}\xi{\triangleright}a}s+\grave{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}(\xi{\triangleright}s)+\big(\delta^{1}_{\xi}{\triangleright}a\big)\big(\delta^{2}_{\xi}{\triangleright}s\big)+\tilde{\xi}(\grave{\Xi})\big(g,\grave{({\rm d}a)}(g),s(g)\big)

using that
\xi{\triangleright}
is the action of
\tilde{\xi}
on functions and commutes with
{\rm d}
, being the Lie derivative along
\tilde{\xi}
on 1-forms. Note that
\tilde{\xi}(\grave{\Xi})
is
\tilde{\xi}
acting on
\grave{\Xi}
as a function on
G
(its first argument). We see that

\xi{\triangleright}\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}{\rm d}b=\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}(\xi{\triangleright}a)}{\rm d}b+\hat{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}{\rm d}(\xi{\triangleright}b)+\big(\delta_{\xi}^{1}{\triangleright}a\big){\rm d}\big(\delta_{\xi}^{2}{\triangleright}b\big)

(3.5)

holds for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
a,b\in C^{\infty}(G)
if and only if
\grave{\Xi}
is constant in its first argument. This extends to general 1-forms as (
3.4
) in a straightforward manner, the details of which are omitted. ∎

Theorem 3.5
.

A right-covariant contravariant connection given by
\tilde{\nabla}_{{\rm d}a}s=\{a,s\}+\Xi\big(\tilde{{\rm d}a},s\big)

where

\Xi^{*}_{[\eta,\xi]}=\big[\Xi^{*1}_{\eta},\xi\big]\otimes\Xi_{\eta}^{*2}+\Xi^{*1}_{\eta}\otimes\big[\Xi_{\eta}^{*2},\xi\big]+\delta^{1}_{\xi}\otimes\big[\eta,\delta^{2}_{\xi}\big]

\Xi^{*}
,
\delta\colon{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
are written explicitly
(
with a sum of such terms understood
)

Proof.

Under the first identification
\Omega^{1}(G)
with

as the action commutes with

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}(\{a,s\})

\displaystyle+{\rm ad}_{\xi}^{*}(\{a,s\})+\tilde{\xi}\big(\Xi(\tilde{{\rm d}a},s)\big)+{\rm ad}_{\xi}^{*}\Xi\big(\tilde{{\rm d}a},s\big)

\displaystyle=\{\tilde{\xi}(a),s\}+\Xi\big(\widetilde{{\rm d}}\big(\tilde{\xi}(a)\big),s\big)+\big\{a,\tilde{\xi}(s)+{\rm ad}_{\xi}^{*}(s)\big\}+\Xi\big(\tilde{{\rm d}a},\tilde{\xi}(s)+{\rm ad}_{\xi}^{*}(s)\big)

\displaystyle=[\xi{\triangleright}\tau,\eta]+[\tau,\xi{\triangleright}\eta]+\big(i_{\tilde{\delta}_{\xi}^{1}}\tau\big)\big(\delta^{2}_{\xi}{\triangleright}\eta\big)-\big(i_{\tilde{\delta}_{\xi}^{1}}\eta\big)\big(\delta^{2}_{\xi}{\triangleright}\tau\big).

\displaystyle=i_{v\wedge w}({\rm d}\tau)\eta-i_{v}(\eta)i_{w}({\rm d}\tau)+i_{v}({\rm d}\tau)i_{w}(\eta)-i_{v}({\rm d}\eta)i_{w}(\tau)

\displaystyle\quad{}+i_{v}(\tau)i_{w}({\rm d}\eta)-i_{v\wedge w}({\rm d}\eta)\tau-{\rm d}(i_{v}(\eta)i_{w}(\tau)-i_{v}(\tau)i_{w}(\eta))

5
Formulation of Poisson principal bundles

A principal bundle in a smooth setting is a smooth manifold
X
with a smooth, free and proper action of a Lie group
G
and a local triviality condition so that
M=X/G
is a smooth manifold and
X
a fibre bundle over it with fibre
G
. We have discussed actions and now we consider the further data we need for a principal bundle at the quantum and hence semiclassical level. From a practical perspective, the key expression of local triviality is transversality: the
C^{\infty}(X)
-module of horizontal forms
\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
(the pull back of
\Omega^{1}(M)
along the canonical projection
X\to M
) is precisely the joint kernel of the vector fields for the infinitesimal action. The invariant horizontal forms are then the forms on the base,
\Omega^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}(X)
. In the quantum case, the transversality is exactness of the sequence (
2.2
), the horizontal forms are
P\Omega^{1}_{A}P
with (under reasonable conditions) invariants under the coaction of
H
recovering the quantum calculus
\Omega^{1}_{A}\subseteq\Omega^{1}_{P}
. Therefore the main missing ingredient we need is the semiclassical version of this transversality condition. Motivated by the assumption of
*
-product quantisations, see Remark
5.5
, we are led to the following.

Definition 5.1
.

A
Poisson principal bundle
means a classical principal bundle
X\to X/G
with

1.

X
a Poisson manifold and
G
a Poisson–Lie group such that the action on
X
is a Poisson–Lie group action of
G
in the sense of Lemma
4.1
.

2.

A bicovariant Poisson-compatible connection on
G
given by
\Xi\colon{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
.

3.

A Poisson-compatible connection on
X
which is left
G
-covariant in the sense of Definition
4.2
.

4.

A
Poisson transversality condition
for all
\eta,\tau\in\Omega^{1}(X)
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

i_{\tilde{\xi}}\big(\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{\eta}\tau\big)=i_{\widetilde{\Xi^{*1}_{\xi}}}(\eta)i_{\widetilde{\Xi^{*2}_{\xi}}}(\tau)+\pi_{X}(\eta,{\rm d}i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\tau)).

In principle, we could need more conditions, but we will see that the above is sufficient for our purposes.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\hat{\nabla}
be a left-covariant contravariant connection
\Omega^{1}(X)
obeying the transversality condition
(4)
in Definition
5.1
. If this is Poisson-compatible then

i_{\tilde{\xi}}[\eta,\tau]=\pi_{X}(\eta,{\rm d}i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\tau))-\pi_{X}({\rm d}i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\eta),\tau)-\tfrac{1}{2}i_{\tilde{\delta\xi}}(\eta\wedge\tau)

for all
\eta,\tau\in\Omega^{1}(X)
.

Proof.

We compute using Poisson-compatibility,

\displaystyle i_{\tilde{\xi}}[\eta,\tau]

\displaystyle=i_{\tilde{\xi}}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{\eta}\tau-i_{\tilde{\xi}}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{\tau}\eta

\displaystyle=i_{\widetilde{\Xi^{*1}_{\xi}}}(\eta)i_{\widetilde{\Xi^{*2}_{\xi}}}(\tau)+\pi_{X}(\eta,{\rm d}i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\tau))-i_{\widetilde{\Xi^{*1}_{\xi}}}(\tau)i_{\widetilde{\Xi^{*2}_{\xi}}}(\eta)-\pi_{X}(\tau,{\rm d}i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\eta))

\displaystyle=\pi_{X}(\eta,{\rm d}i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\tau))-\pi_{X}({\rm d}i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\eta),\tau)+i_{(\tilde{\delta\xi)^{1}}}(\eta)i_{(\tilde{\delta\xi)^{2}}}(\tau).

Here, (
3.3
) in dual form allows us to recognise
\tilde{\delta\xi}
from the cocommutator. We also use antisymmetry of
\pi_{X}
. We then put the result as a bivector interior product as discussed before.
∎

On exact differentials, this reduces to the covariance condition on
\xi{\triangleright}\{p,q\}
for the Poisson bracket. We also want to know that our definition is fit for purpose and implies that the base is not only a manifold but a Poisson manifold with an induced Poisson-compatible contravariant connection.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
G
be a PLG with a free smooth proper right PLG action on a Poisson manifold
X
, equipped with Poisson-compatible contravariant connections
\hat{\nabla}^{G}
and
\hat{\nabla}^{X}
so as to form a Poisson-principal bundle as in Definition
5.1
. Then
M=X/G
becomes a Poisson manifold with Poisson-compatible contravariant connection
\hat{\nabla}^{M}
the restriction of
\hat{\nabla}^{X}
.

Proof.

Here
C^{\infty}(M)
can be identified with smooth functions on
X
which are killed by all vector fields for the infinitesimal action. By the invariance of the Poisson bracket, it follows that if
p
,
q
are killed by all
\tilde{\xi}
then so is
\{p,q\}
, so the Poisson bracket restricts. Now let
\hat{\nabla}^{M}
be the restriction of
\hat{\nabla}^{X}
to the differentials of such functions. By the covariance condition, it follows that the output of
\hat{\nabla}^{M}
is invariant under all
\xi{\triangleright}={\mathcal{L}}_{\tilde{\xi}}
. By the bundle condition, it follows that the output of
\hat{\nabla}^{M}
is killed by
i_{\tilde{\xi}}
. However, if
\sum p_{i}{\rm d}q_{i}
with
p_{i},q_{i}\in C^{\infty}(X)
has these properties then
\sum p_{i}\tilde{\xi}(q_{i})=0
and moreover, by transversality of the classical bundle, we know that the form is horizontal, so we can assume

. Then by the first property,
\sum\tilde{\xi}(p_{i}){\rm d}q_{i}=0
so that taking the

independent, the

are also in
C^{\infty}(M)
, i.e., our form is an element of
\Omega^{1}(M)
. Thus
\hat{\nabla}^{M}
is defined, and in this case inherits the connection properties. ∎

\displaystyle\qquad{}=p*q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\otimes\varpi^{\bullet}\pi_{\epsilon}\big(q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}\big)-q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}*p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\otimes S^{\bullet}p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}{}_{\scriptstyle(1)}\bullet\varpi^{\bullet}\pi_{\epsilon}\big(q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}\big)\bullet p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}{}_{\scriptstyle(2)}

where we dropped the bullets in the last line as there is already a

\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
. Hence at order

\displaystyle\text{ver}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}q

\displaystyle=\big\{p,q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\big\}\otimes\varpi\pi_{\epsilon}\big(q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}\big)+q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\otimes\hat{\nabla}^{G}_{\varpi\pi_{\epsilon}(p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}})}\varpi\pi_{\epsilon}\big(q{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}\big)

where we brought

Next, recall that
\hat{\nabla}^{G}

\displaystyle i_{\tilde{\xi}}\big(\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}q\big)

, and similarly for the other factor, gives us the condition

for all

,

6
Connections on Poisson principal bundles

In noncommutative differential geometry, once we have a quantum principal bundle
P
, the next order of business is to find a ‘spin connection’
\omega\colon\Lambda^{1}_{H}\to\Omega^{1}_{P}
which is equivariant (where
H
coacts on
\Lambda^{1}_{H}
by a right adjoint coaction that follows from the assumed
\Omega^{1}_{H}
bicovariance) and such that
{\rm ver}(\omega(v))=1\otimes v
for all
v\in\Lambda^{1}_{H}
. This provides a splitting of
\Omega^{1}_{P}
in the form of an associated projection
\Pi_{\omega}
defined by
\Pi_{\omega}({\rm d}p)=p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\omega\big(\varpi\pi_{\epsilon}p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}\big)
, which is equivariant for the right coaction of
H
, a left
P
-module map, idempotent and has
{\rm{ker}}\Pi_{\omega}=P\Omega^{1}_{A}P
, see
[
7
]
and
[
4
, Chapter 5]
. We also require
\omega
to be such that
({\rm id}-\Pi_{\omega}){\rm d}P\subseteq\Omega^{1}_{A}P
, in which case it defines a connection on
P
itself by

\nabla_{P}\colon\ P\to\Omega^{1}_{A}P=\Omega^{1}_{A}\otimes_{A}P,\qquad\nabla_{P}p={\rm d}p-p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\omega(\varpi\pi_{\epsilon}p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}).

The first equality is canonically afforded by viewing
\Omega^{1}_{A}\subseteq\Omega^{1}_{P}
and multiplying there.
It is known
[
4
, Proposition 5.50]
that this is a bimodule connection if and only if
\sum a_{i}\omega\big(\Lambda^{1}_{H}\big)b_{i}=0
for all
\sum a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in A\otimes A
such that
\sum a_{i}b_{i}=0
and
\sum({\rm d}a_{i})b_{i}=0
. In this case, the generalised braiding is

\sigma_{P}(p\otimes({\rm d}a)b)=p({\rm d}a)b-p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(0)}}\big[a,\omega\big(\varpi\pi_{\epsilon}p{}_{\scriptstyle\bar{(1)}}\big)\big]b.

Moreover,
\nabla_{P}
commutes with the right coaction of
H
under our assumptions. As a consequence, for any
H
-comodule
V
, we can define
E=(P\otimes V)^{H}
as a noncommutative analogue of the space of sections of the associated bundle (the superscript
H
indicates the invariant subspace under the tensor product coaction) and a connection
[
4
,
7
]

\nabla_{E}\colon\ (P\otimes V)^{H}=E\to\big(\Omega^{1}_{A}P\otimes V\big)^{H}=\Omega^{1}_{A}\otimes_{A}E,\qquad\nabla_{E}=\nabla_{P}\otimes{\rm id}.

We see that the key here for the theory of associated bundles and ‘spin connections’ is the construction of an equivariant
\nabla_{P}
. Its properties of being a bimodule connection when viewing
A\subseteq P
and working inside
\Omega^{1}_{P}
come down to

\displaystyle\nabla_{P}\colon\ P\to\Omega^{1}_{A}P,\qquad\sigma_{P}\colon\ P\Omega^{1}_{A}\to\Omega^{1}_{A}P,

\displaystyle\nabla_{P}(a.p)=({\rm d}a).p+a.\nabla_{P}(p),\qquad\nabla_{P}(p.a)=(\nabla_{P}p).a+\sigma_{P}(p.{\rm d}a),

(6.1)

\displaystyle a.\sigma_{P}(p.{\rm d}b)=\sigma_{P}(a.p.{\rm d}b),\qquad(\sigma_{P}(p.{\rm d}b)).a=\sigma_{P}(p.({\rm d}b).a)

(6.2)

for all
a,b\in A
,
p\in P
. We constructed
\nabla_{P}
from
\omega
above with conditions to ensure that
\sigma_{P}
is well-defined by (
6.1
), after which it automatically obeys the
A
-bimodule map conditions (
6.2
). We emphasise the product of
P
and
\Omega^{1}_{P}
for clarity. There are exactly similar inherited formulae for
\nabla_{E}
, now with
\sigma_{E}\colon\big(P\Omega^{1}_{A}\otimes V\big)^{H}\to\big(\Omega^{1}_{A}P\otimes V\big)^{H}
. The point is that by working ‘upstairs’ in
\Omega^{1}_{P}
, we avoid mention of
\otimes_{A}
.

What this comes down to for a classical principal bundle
P=C^{\infty}(X)
, Lie structure group
G
and Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with basis
\{e_{i}\}
, say, (with
\Lambda^{1}_{H}
replaced now by
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
) is that a spin connection is an equivariant Lie-algebra valued 1-form on
X
,

\omega=\omega^{i}\otimes e_{i},\qquad\xi{\triangleright}\omega^{i}+\omega^{i}\otimes[\xi,e_{i}]=0,\qquad i_{\tilde{\xi}}(\omega^{i})e_{i}=\xi

(6.3)

for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and sum over
i
understood. Then

\nabla_{P}\colon\ P\to\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor},\qquad\nabla_{P}p={\rm d}p-\omega^{i}\tilde{e_{i}}(p),\qquad\sigma_{P}(p{\rm d}a)=({\rm d}a)p

(6.4)

obeys the above Leibniz rules with everything commutative (so
\sigma_{P}={\rm id}
on
\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
). From this, restricting to
E=C^{\infty}_{G}(X,V)
(the sections of the vector bundle associated to a representation
V
of
G
) gives the induced connection
\nabla_{E}
on the associated bundle. Interior product with a vector field gives the covariant derivative along that vector field in the usual sense.

We now suppose that we have such a classical principal bundle with spin connection
\omega
and seek to deformation-quantise everything. In fact, we are only going to look at this up to and including first order in
\lambda
, what could be called the ‘semiclassical’ level. Formally, this can be done by working over the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[\lambda]/\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
in place of
{\mathbb{C}}[[\lambda]]
as explained in
[
3
]
, but for continuity with the general scheme, we will use the deformation point of view but ignore
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
as errors to be corrected in a 2nd order theory. We can assume that
C^{\infty}(X)[[\lambda]]
has an associative
*
-product but we only assume this
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
for the bimodule product
\bullet
of
\Omega^{1}(X)[[\lambda]]
, since we allow that
\hat{\nabla}^{X}
could have curvature. We do, however, fix the antisymmetric form of the leading products as per (
2.5
) applied now on
X
as a Poisson manifold. This means that our theory has a slightly different flavour from previous sections, being specific to the leading part of a preferred deformation scheme.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
X
,
G
be a Poisson principal bundle as in Definition
5.1
and suppose we are given a connection in the sense

\nabla_{P}\colon\ C^{\infty}(X)\to\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor},\qquad\nabla_{P}(ap)=({\rm d}a)p+a\nabla_{P}p

for all
p\in C^{\infty}(X),a\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Consider
\Omega^{1}(X)[[\lambda]]
quantised as in (
2.5
) and
\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}p
in here of the form

\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}p=\nabla_{P}p+\frac{\lambda}{2}\Gamma(p)+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big),\qquad\Gamma\colon\ C^{\infty}(X)\to\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}.

Then the first of (
6.1
) holds with
*
and
\bullet
products to
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
if and only if

\Gamma(ap)=a\Gamma(p)+\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\nabla_{P}p-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}a-\nabla_{P}\{a,p\}_{X},

(6.5)

while the second of (
6.1
) holding requires

\displaystyle\sigma^{\bullet}_{P}(p{\rm d}a)=p{\rm d}a+\lambda\varsigma(p{\rm d}a)+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big),\qquad\varsigma\colon\ \Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}\to\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor},

\displaystyle\varsigma(p{\rm d}a)=\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\nabla_{P}p-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}a-\nabla_{P}\{a,p\}_{X}.

If this is well-defined then (
6.2
) hold with
*
and
\bullet
products to
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
.

Proof.

This is simply a matter of putting the form of the
*,\bullet
products and
\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}
into (
6.1
)–(
6.2
) and discarding anything
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
. For example,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}(a*p)=\nabla_{P}(ap)+{\lambda\over 2}\nabla_{P}\{a,p\}_{X}+{\lambda\over 2}\Gamma(ap)+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big),

\displaystyle({\rm d}a)\bullet p+a\bullet\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}p=({\rm d}a)p-{\lambda\over 2}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}a+a\nabla_{P}p+{\lambda\over 2}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\nabla_{P}p+a{\lambda\over 2}\Gamma(p)+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)

gives the first result. For
\sigma_{P}^{\bullet}
we first establish that
\sigma_{P}^{\bullet}(p{\rm d}a)=p{\rm d}a+O(\lambda)
by computing the right hand side at classical order, then
\sigma(p\bullet{\rm d}a)=\sigma_{P}^{\bullet}\big(p{\rm d}a+{\lambda\over 2}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}a\big)=\sigma_{P}^{\bullet}(p{\rm d}a)+{\lambda\over 2}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}a
to
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
and we equate this to
\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}(p*a)-(\nabla^{\bullet}_{P})\bullet a
, from which
\Gamma
conveniently cancels given our result for
\Gamma(ap)
. That (
6.2
) holds to
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
is a tedious calculation. E.g.,
a\bullet\sigma(p{\rm d}b)=\sigma(a\bullet(p{\rm d}b))
, comes down to
\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}{\rm d}b-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}b}{\rm d}a={\rm d}\{a,b\}_{X}
on expanding both sides and using the stated formula for
\sigma_{P}^{\bullet}
. Details are omitted. Finally, note that
\eta\in\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
is characterised by
i_{\tilde{\xi}}\eta=0
for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and the transversality condition in Definition
5.1
then ensures that
\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{\tau}
preserves
\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
for all
\tau\in\Omega^{1}(X)
(because
i_{\tilde{\xi}}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{\tau}\eta=0
for all
\eta\in\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
). It is then clear that the image of the stated formula for
\varsigma
is in
\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
. ∎

This lemma clarifies what we mean by quantizing a spin connection in the
\nabla_{P}
form to semiclassical order as a bimodule connection at this level. The order
\lambda
data for the associated bundle connection is just
\Gamma
restricted to
f\in E=C^{\infty}_{G}(V)
with
\nabla_{E}^{\bullet}f=\nabla_{E}f+\frac{\lambda}{2}\Gamma(f)+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
.

Our approach to construct
\Gamma
and
\varsigma
is to start with a deformed ‘spin connection’ which, at order
\lambda
, can have an additional component

\omega_{\bullet}=\omega+{\lambda\over 2}\alpha^{i}\otimes e_{i}+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big),\qquad\xi{\triangleright}\alpha^{i}\otimes e_{i}+\alpha^{i}\otimes[\xi,e_{i}]=0,

(6.6)

where
\alpha^{i}\in\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
, and follow the lines of a deformed version of (
6.4
). We can take
\alpha=0
as a canonical choice here.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
,
G
be a Poisson principal bundle as in Definition
5.1
,
\omega
a classical ‘spin connection’ on
X
with corresponding
\nabla_{P}
in (
6.4
), and
\alpha
a horizontal equivariant
{\mathfrak{g}}
-valued
1
-form on
X
as in (
6.6
). Then

\Gamma(p)=\widetilde{\Xi_{e_{i}}^{*1}}\big(\widetilde{\Xi_{e_{i}}^{*2}}(p)\big)\omega^{i}-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}\tilde{e_{i}}(p)}\omega^{i}-\tilde{e_{i}}(p)\alpha^{i},\qquad\varsigma(\tau)=-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{e_{i}{\triangleright}\tau}\omega^{i}

(6.7)

for all
p\in C^{\infty}(X)
and
\tau\in\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
constructs
\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}=\nabla_{P}+{\lambda\over 2}\Gamma+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
and
\sigma^{\bullet}_{P}={\rm id}+\lambda\varsigma+\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
as a bimodule connection at semiclassical order by Lemma
6.1
. Moreover,
\Gamma
and hence
\nabla^{\bullet}_{P}
to
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
are equivariant.

Proof.

We look at
\Gamma
first. Using the bundle transversality condition, we have

i_{\tilde{\xi}}\nabla^{X}_{{\rm d}\tilde{e_{i}}(p)}\omega^{i}=\widetilde{\Xi_{\xi}^{*1}}(\tilde{e_{i}}(p))i_{\widetilde{\Xi_{\xi}^{*2}}}\big(\omega^{i}\big)+\big\{\tilde{e_{i}}(p),i_{\tilde{\xi}}\big(\omega^{i}\big)\big\}_{X}=\widetilde{\Xi_{\xi}^{*1}}\big({\widetilde{\Xi_{\xi}^{*2}}}(p)\big)=i_{\tilde{\xi}}\big(\widetilde{\Xi_{e_{i}}^{*1}}\big({\widetilde{\Xi_{e_{i}}^{*2}}}(p)\big)\omega^{i}\big),

where
i_{\tilde{\xi}}\big(\omega^{i}\big)=\xi^{i}
for a spin connection as in (
6.3
), these being constants as
\xi=\xi^{i}e_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence the first two terms of
\Gamma
are horizontal. The last term of
\Gamma
is horizontal as the
\alpha^{i}
are assumed so. In fact, to be horizontal, we just need
\tilde{e_{i}}(p)i_{\tilde{\xi}}\big(\alpha^{i}\big)=0
for all
p
. But since the classical action is free,
{\rm ver}
is surjective and it follows that we need
\alpha^{i}\in\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
if we want the image of
\Gamma
to be horizontal.

Next, for
a\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have
\tilde{\xi}(ap)=a\tilde{\xi}(p)
for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}
from which it is clear that

\Gamma(ap)-a\Gamma(p)=-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}(a\tilde{e_{i}}(p))}\omega^{i}+a\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}\tilde{e_{i}}(p)}\omega^{i}=-\tilde{e_{i}}(p)\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\omega^{i},

while

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\nabla_{P}p-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}a-\nabla_{P}\{a,p\}_{X}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}{\rm d}p-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}(\tilde{e_{i}}(p)\omega^{i})-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}p}{\rm d}a-{\rm d}\{a,p\}_{X}+e_{i}{\triangleright}\{a,p\}_{X}\omega^{i}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=-\tilde{e_{i}}(p)\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\omega^{i}-\{a,\tilde{e_{i}}(p)\}\omega^{i}+e_{i}{\triangleright}\{a,p\}_{X}\omega^{i}=-\tilde{e_{i}}(p)\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\omega^{i}

also, by Lemma
3.1
and the Poisson-compatibility of
\hat{\nabla}^{X}
. This also gives us

\varsigma(p{\rm d}a)=-\tilde{e_{i}}(p)\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}a}\omega^{i}=-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{\tilde{e_{i}}(p){\rm d}a}\omega^{i}=-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{e_{i}{\triangleright}(p{\rm d}a)}\omega^{i}

since
e_{i}{\triangleright}
is the Lie derivative along
\tilde{e_{i}}
and acts trivially on 1-forms on the base. Hence, this is well-defined on
\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
and as stated. Since it is also
\Gamma(ap)-a\Gamma(p)
, it follows that it lies in
\Omega^{1}_{\rm hor}
also, as one can also directly check using the bundle transversality condition.

So far, we have not used the assumed equivariance properties of
\omega
and
\alpha
. We now assume these. Then

\displaystyle\xi{\triangleright}\Gamma(p)

\displaystyle=\big(\tilde{\xi}\widetilde{\Xi^{*1}_{e_{i}}}\widetilde{\Xi^{*2}_{e_{i}}}(p)\big)\omega^{i}+\big(\widetilde{\Xi^{*1}_{e_{i}}}\widetilde{\Xi^{*2}_{e_{i}}}(p)\big)(\xi{\triangleright}\omega^{i})-(\xi e_{i}{\triangleright}p)\alpha^{i}-(e_{i}{\triangleright}p)\big(\xi{\triangleright}\alpha^{i}\big)-\xi{\triangleright}\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}e_{i}{\triangleright}p}\omega^{i}

\displaystyle=\big(\tilde{\xi}\widetilde{\Xi^{*1}_{e_{i}}}\widetilde{\Xi^{*2}_{e_{i}}}(p)\big)\omega^{i}-\big(\widetilde{\Xi^{*1}_{[\xi,e_{i}]}}\widetilde{\Xi^{*2}_{[\xi,e_{i}]}}(p)\big)\omega^{i}-(e_{i}\xi{\triangleright}p)\alpha^{i}

\displaystyle\quad{}-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}\xi e_{i}{\triangleright}p}\omega^{i}-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}e_{i}{\triangleright}p}\big(\xi{\triangleright}\omega^{i}\big)-\big(\delta^{1}_{\xi}e_{i}{\triangleright}p\big)\big(\delta^{2}_{\xi}{\triangleright}\omega^{i}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\xi\Xi^{*1}_{e_{i}}\Xi^{*2}_{e_{i}}{\triangleright}p\big)\omega^{i}-\big(\Xi^{*1}_{[\xi,e_{i}]}\Xi^{*2}_{[\xi,e_{i}]}{\triangleright}p\big)\omega^{i}-(e_{i}\xi{\triangleright}p)\alpha^{i}-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}e_{i}\xi{\triangleright}p}\omega^{i}+\big(\delta^{1}_{\xi}[\delta^{2}_{\xi},e_{i}]{\triangleright}p\big)\omega^{i}

\displaystyle=\big(\Xi^{*1}_{e_{i}}\Xi^{*2}_{e_{i}}\xi{\triangleright}p\big)\omega^{i}-(e_{i}\xi{\triangleright}p)\alpha^{i}-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}e_{i}\xi{\triangleright}p}\omega^{i}=\Gamma(\xi{\triangleright}p)

as required. We used equivariance of
\alpha
to transfer
\xi{\triangleright}\alpha^{i}
to
[\xi,e_{i}]
, then covariance of
\hat{\nabla}^{X}
in our Poisson sense of Definition

to expand
\xi{\triangleright}\hat{\nabla}^{X}
and equivariance of the classical

as in (

) to transfer any actions
\xi{\triangleright}\omega^{i}
and
\delta^{2}_{\xi}{\triangleright}\omega^{i}
to the relevant
e_{i}
. We finally used the bicovariance condition in terms of
\Xi
in Theorem
3.5
to recognise the answer. We already have
\nabla_{P}
equivariant, so
\nabla_{P}^{\bullet}
would be quantum group covariant to
\mathcal{O}\big(\lambda^{2}\big)
similarly to Remark
4.3
.
∎

Example 6.3
.

Our theme of the

[

,

\Gamma(p)=\tilde{H}(\tilde{H}(p))e^{0}-\hat{\nabla}^{X}_{{\rm d}\tilde{H}(p)}e^{0}-\tilde{H}(p)\alpha=-|p|p\alpha

7
Concluding remarks

We have shown that Drinfeld’s theory of Poisson–Lie groups
[
13
,
14
]
extends in a natural way to principal bundles
X
which are Poisson manifolds and have Poisson–Lie structure group
G
. The PLG condition itself is viewed as a certain covariance of the Poisson bracket
\{\ ,\ \}_{G}
and extends to the notion of a PLG action on
\{\ ,\ \}_{X}
. We extended these covariance notions to Poisson-level quantum differential structures in the sense of Poisson-compatible contravariant connections
\hat{\nabla}^{G}
and
\hat{\nabla}^{X}
respectively in the sense of
[
1
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
30
]
and characterised bicovariance of the former. Section
5
introduced a further transversality condition expressing that
X\to M=X/G
is a Poisson-level version of a quantum principal bundle. This is such that
M
is not only a Poisson manifold but inherits a Poisson-level quantum differential structure in the sense of
\hat{\nabla}^{M}
. We were then able to formulate ‘spin connections’ and connections on associated bundles at semiclassical level. Results were illustrated on the Poisson level of the
q
-Hopf fibration deforming
{\rm SU}_{2}\to S^{2}
with
S^{1}
fibre, the latter as a PLG with zero
\{\ ,\ \}_{G}
but nonzero
\hat{\nabla}^{G}
. It remains to compute more complicated examples.

A direction for further work would be a general Poisson version of the theory of quantum homogeneous bundles, of which the
q
-Hopf fibration above is the simplest example. This theory with semiclassical differential structures would be rather different from previous discussions of Poisson homogeneous spaces, for example in
[
15
]
. Whether our approach can extend to a geometric picture of the full 2-parameter Podleś spheres
[
32
]
is unclear since a direct quantum principal bundle approach would seem to require coalgebra bundles
[
8
]
, for which the notion of differential calculus on the fibre is not known. Another issue, even for ordinary quantum principal bundles, is that associative bicovariant calculi on
q
-deformation quantum groups are often not possible with classical dimension
[
1
]
. This was not a problem in our approach at the Poisson level as we were careful not to assume flatness of
\hat{\nabla}^{G}
, but quantisation would be a challenge. It should be possible, for example, to construct a nonassociative deformation of the next Hopf fibration
S^{7}\to S^{4}
with
{\rm SU}_{2}
fibre, combining the Hopf-quasigroup methods in
[
22
]
and the nonassociative bicovariant calculi by twisting in
[
1
,
2
]
.

A third interesting direction would be quantum group frame bundles as in
[
26
]
at the Poisson level whereby
\Omega^{1}(M)
is an associated bundle to a Poisson principal bundle. In this context, it could make sense to ask that
\hat{\nabla}
comes from a contravariant connection on the underlying classical principal bundle in the sense of
[
17
]
. The framed theory would, moreover, extend the above to a PLG-covariant version of Riemannian geometry on
M
as a somewhat different approach to
[
3
]
. Indeed, one expects the quantum metrics at semiclassical order to be different in the two approaches even for the Poisson level of the metric for the
q
-sphere. It could also be of interest to consider quantum fibrations more generally, and to relate to integrability of Lie algebroids
[
11
]
.

Abstract
Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges are crucial in Decentralized Finance, but Ethereum implementations suffer from high transaction costs and price synchronization challenges. To address these limitations, we compare the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange, a protocol-level implementation, against a Generic AMM-based DEX on Ethereum, akin to Uniswap’s V2 Automated Market Maker implementation, through agent-based simulations using real market data and multiple volatility scenarios generated via Geometric Brownian Motion. Results demonstrate that the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange achieves superior price synchronization, reduced slippage, and improved returns due to XRP Ledger’s lower fees and shorter block times, with benefits amplifying during market volatility. The integrated Continuous Auction Mechanism further mitigates impermanent loss by redistributing arbitrage value to Liquidity Providers. To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first comparative analysis between protocol-level and smart contract Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchange implementations and the first agent-based simulation validating theoretical auction mechanisms for Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges.

VI
Discussion

Our comparison of XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange (without Continuous Auction Mechanism) and Generic AMM-based DEX reveals that XRP Ledger ’s faster block times
[
72
]
lead to better price synchronization, higher transaction realization, reduced slippage, and lower price impact. These findings align with recent empirical and theoretical studies
[
23
,
42
]
, highlighting the importance of shorter block confirmation times for Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges.

Why does blockchain infrastructure
matter so much for Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges, and why should it be a consideration for their design? Unlike market makers’ active role responding to trading activity in Limited Order Books
[
44
]
, Automated Market Makers update prices only when trades occur against the liquidity pools of their Decentralized Exchange. Intuitively, a blockchain infrastructure that processes transactions faster allows Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges to react faster to market changes with an external market, keeping prices in sync and reducing slippage. This ripple effect even touches impermanent loss, given its relationship with price slippage
[
25
]
. Our results validate this intuition.

Even with faster infrastructure, Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges face another issue: Maximal Extractable Value attacks. For instance, Ethereum-based Decentralized Exchanges are particularly vulnerable because Ethereum’s transparent mempool and miner-controlled ordering
[
73
,
74
]
create a playground for attackers. Miners can cherry-pick transactions order, sparking a high-stakes race among arbitrageurs and attackers competing for prime positions in the next block
[
51
,
50
,
75
]
. By contrast, the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange leverages the XRP Ledger’s pseudo-random transaction ordering
8
8

8

https://github.com/XRPLF/XRPL-Standards/discussions/34
, significantly reducing the risk of front-running attacks
[
76
,
77
,
78
]
. While this does not make it immune – sandwich attacks, for example, remain a threat
[
78
]
– it is a substantial defensive boost that could reduce price slippage.

Beyond speed and security, the underlying infrastructure affects Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges in other crucial ways. Most Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges run on smart contracts competing for computational resources. These smart contracts can be resource-hungry, potentially consuming more gas fees than native transactions, depending on their complexity and data payloads. This resource competition could directly impact transaction costs and execution speed. Moreover, the blockchain’s fee structure is pivotal in market dynamics. XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange’s lower network fees boost arbitrageur profits and trading volume (
§V
). This increased activity helps keep prices aligned with external markets. In contrast, Generic AMM-based DEX’s higher fees lead to broader price impact spreads, indicating more significant trade-induced market disturbances. These fee differences highlight how infrastructure choices can significantly shape an Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchange’s market efficiency and liquidity.

The XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange’s Continuous Auction Mechanism further enhances these advantages. For Liquidity Providers, XRP Ledger-Continuous Auction Mechanism yields higher returns and lower divergence loss in their best-case scenarios, while arbitrageurs see more profits in their best-case scenario. In typical conditions, Liquidity Providers benefit from higher earnings and reduced divergence loss, especially in volatile markets. This aligns with theoretical proposals for auction mechanisms in Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges
[
38
,

,
40
]
. Interestingly, as volatility increases, the proportion of transactions executed by auction slot holders decreases from

A fascinating insight is that when we level the playing field by equalizing transaction fees and block times, the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange performs remarkably similar to Generic AMM-based DEX. This highlights how crucial the underlying infrastructure is in shaping Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges dynamics.

VII
Limitations and future work

Our agent-based simulations use simplifying assumptions such as fixed Ethereum fees, one Geometric Brownian Motion-generated price path, constant pool sizes, single users per auction slot, and no dynamic voting or pathfinding
[
29
]
. Despite these simplifications, our results align with recent research on slippage, impermanent loss, and auction mechanisms
[

,
42
,
38
,
40
]
, highlighting how faster block times, lower fees, and built-in auctions improve market efficiency in Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges. Additionally, we used block interarrival times as a proxy for infrastructure efficiency to level the playing field in benchmarking the Generic AMM-based DEX, akin to Uniswap V2 that runs in smart contracts, versus the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange implemented at the protocol-level. Future studies could integrate more intricate factors, such as dynamic fees, diverse pool sizes, and multiple concurrent auction participants. It is important to note that the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange is relatively new, launching in early 2024

, and real-world adoption could impact its price synchronization and liquidity differently.

VIII
Conclusion

Our findings, using simulated and real market price data, show that the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange leverages two key elements to reduce impermanent loss and price slippage: the XRP Ledger’s shorter block times for rapid price synchronization, and its Continuous Auction Mechanism feature to incentivize beneficial arbitrage during volatility. These elements benefit both arbitrageurs and Liquidity Providers, enhancing overall market efficiency. This inaugural study ventures into the unexplored domain of Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges in the XRP Ledger and provides, to the best of our knowledge, the first agent-based simulation of an auction mechanism for Automated Market Maker-based Decentralized Exchanges, experimentally validating implications from theoretical proposals
[

,
39
,
40
]
. However, as the XRP Ledger-Automated Market Maker-Decentralized Exchange is still in its early stages, at the time of this writing, its long-term success will depend on adoption rates, real-world market conditions, and its adaptability to the evolving Decentralized Finance landscape.

Support Varieties and cohomology of Verdier quotients of stable category of complete intersection rings
Tony J. Puthenpurakal

Abstract
. Let ( A , 𝔪 ) (A,\mathfrak{m}) be a complete intersection with k = A / 𝔪 k=A/\mathfrak{m} algebraically closed. Let CM ¯ ⁡ ( A ) \operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A) be the stable category of maximal Cohen-Macaulay A A -modules. For a large class of thick subcategories 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of CM ¯ ⁡ ( A ) \operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A) we show that there is a theory of support varieties for the Verdier quotient 𝒯 = CM ¯ ⁡ ( A ) / 𝒮 \mathcal{T}=\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)/\mathcal{S} . As an application we show that the analogous version of Auslander-Reiten conjecture, Murthys result, Avramov-Buchweitz result on symmetry of vanishing of cohomology holds for 𝒯 \mathcal{T}

Remark 1.2

It is easier to work with varieties in

Let

\mathcal{S}_{X}=\{M\mid M\ \text{is MCM and }\ \mathcal{V}^{*}(M)\subseteq X\}

It is easy to verify that

Applications:

(1) Auslander and Reiten conjectured that if
R
is an Artin ring,
\Lambda
is an Artin
R
-algebra and
M
is a finitely generated
\Gamma
-module then

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\Lambda}(M,M\oplus\Lambda)=0\quad\text{for all}\ i\geq 1\implies M\ \text{is projective}.

We note that this conjecture makes sense for any ring
\Lambda
. In
[
2
, 1.9]
it is shown that AR-conjecture holds for complete intersection rings. Also see

[
17
, Main Theorem]
and
[
1
, Corollary 4]
where it is shown that AR-conjecture holds for normal local Gorenstein rings.
The generalized Auslander Reiten (GAR) conjecture states that if

is any ring then

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\Lambda}(M,M\oplus\Lambda)=0\quad\text{for all}\ i\geq m\implies\operatorname{projdim}_{\Lambda}M<m.

GAR has been verified for complete intersections, see
[
5
, 4.2]
. In this case we just require
\operatorname{Ext}^{2i}_{R}(M,M)=0
for some

.
We note that if

is Gorenstein local and

are MCM
R
-modules then
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)=\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{R}(M,\Omega^{-i}(N))
.
We say a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
with shift operator
\Sigma
satisfies
generalized Auslander-Reiten property (GAR)
if for

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(U,\Sigma^{n}U)=0\ \text{for all}\ n\gg 0\implies U=0.

1.1
and notation as in

2.4

The stable category of a Gorenstein local ring:

Let

is zero in
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)
if and only if it is free considered as an
A
-module. Furthermore
M\cong N
in
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)
if and only
if there exists finitely generated free modules

with

A

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(E))=0\quad\text{for}\ j=m+1,\ldots,m+r-2.

As
\operatorname{ecx}_{A}E\leq\operatorname{cx}_{A}E=r-1
we get by induction hypotheses that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(E))=0
for all
j\geq m+1
.
Putting
j=m+r-2
in (
\dagger
) we get
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-m-r+1}(N))=0
.
Iterating we obtain
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(N))=0
for all
j\geq m
.
∎

We now state and prove a result which implies Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 6.5
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
and notation as in
1.3
.) Let
M,N
be objects in
\mathcal{T}_{X}
.
Let
r=\operatorname{ecx}N
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-n}(N))

\displaystyle=0\ \text{for }\ n=m,m+1,\cdots.m+r

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-n}(N))=0\ \text{for all }\ n\geq m.

Proof.

Set
a=\dim\mathcal{V}_{X}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{X}(N)
. We prove the result by induction on
a
.
We first consider the case when
a=-1
; i.e.,
\mathcal{V}_{X}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{X}(N)=\emptyset
.
In this case the result follows from Theorem
6.3
.

Let
a=t\geq 0
and assume the result is proved when
a<t
.
Next we consider the case when
r=\operatorname{cx}_{A}N\geq 2
and assume the results are proved for MCM modules
E
with
\operatorname{ecx}_{A}E<r
.
By
6.1
we have a short exact sequence,

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow\Omega^{n+2}(N)\rightarrow\Omega^{n}(N)\rightarrow 0;

for some
n\geq 0
with
\operatorname{cx}_{A}K=r-1
. So we have a triangle in
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)

K\rightarrow\Omega^{n+2}(N)\rightarrow\Omega^{n}(N)\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}(K)

Rotating it several times we get the following triangle in
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)

\Omega^{2}(N)\rightarrow N\rightarrow E\rightarrow\Omega(N)

where
\operatorname{cx}E=r-1
. Also note
\mathcal{V}^{*}(E)\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{*}(N)
. We takes its image in
\mathcal{T}_{X}

s\colon\Omega^{2}(N)\rightarrow N\rightarrow E\rightarrow\Omega(N).

Note
\mathcal{V}_{X}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{X}(E)\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{X}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{X}(E)
.
So
\dim\mathcal{V}_{X}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{X}(E)\leq t
.
By applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,-)
to
s
we obtain long exact sequence:

(
\dagger\dagger
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(N))

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(E))\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j+1}(N))\rightarrow

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j-1}(N))

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j-1}(E))\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(N))\rightarrow.

It follows that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(E))=0\quad\text{for}\ j=m+1,\ldots,r.

As
\operatorname{ecx}_{A}E\leq\operatorname{cx}_{A}E=r-1
we get by induction hypotheses that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(E))=0
for all
j\geq m+1
.
Putting
j=m+r
in (
\dagger\dagger
) we get
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-m-r-1}(N))=0
.
Iterating we obtain
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-j}(N))=0
for all
j\geq m
.
∎

Proof of Theorems
1.12
,
1.13
and
1.14

In this section we give proofs of Theorems
1.12
,
1.13
and
1.14
. We first prove:

Theorem 7.1
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
and notation as in
1.11
.)
Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,c-1\}
.
Let
M,N
be any two MCM
A
-modules.
Consider the following two statements:

(1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{i}}(M,\Omega^{-n}(N))=0\ \text{for all}\ n\gg 0
.

(2)

\dim(\mathcal{V}_{i}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{i}(N))\leq i-1
.

Then
(1)\implies(2)
.

Proof.

The proof of this result is completely analogous to proof of Theorem
4.1
. One simply replaces
W_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{X}
with
W_{j}\in\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{\leq i}(A)
.
∎

As a consequence we give

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

We get
\dim\operatorname{top}(M)=\dim\mathcal{V}_{i}(M)\leq i-1
. This forces
\dim\mathcal{V}^{*}(M)\leq i-1
. So
\operatorname{cx}_{A}M\leq i
. Therefore
M=0
in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
.
∎

As we observed in the proof of Theorem
1.5
it is convenient to prove a slightly more general result:

Theorem 7.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.1
and notation as in
1.11
.) Let
M,N
be objects in
\mathcal{T}_{X}
.
Let
r=\operatorname{cx}N
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-n}(N))

\displaystyle=0\ \text{for }\ n=m,m+1,\cdots.m+r-i

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(M,\Omega^{-n}(N))=0\ \text{for all }\ n\geq m.

Proof.

If
r\leq i
then
N=0
in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
and so we have nothing to prove.
Next we consider the case when
\operatorname{cx}_{A}N=i+1
. By
6.1

we have a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow\Omega^{n+2}(N)\rightarrow\Omega^{n}(N)\rightarrow 0;

for some
n\geq 0
and
\operatorname{cx}_{A}K=i
.
This yields an exact triangle in
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A)

K\rightarrow\Omega^{n+2}(N)\rightarrow\Omega^{n}(N)\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}(K).

Taking the image of the above exact triangle in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
and noting
K=0
in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
we get

\Omega^{n+2}(N)\cong\Omega^{n}(N)
in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
. It follows that
N
is two periodic in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
. The result trivially follows.

Rest of the proof is similar to proof of Theorem
6.5
.
∎

Finally we give:

Proof of Theorem
1.14
.

Suppose
U,V
are MCM
A
-modules such that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{c-1}}(U,V)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{c-1}}(U,\Omega^{-1}(V))=0.

Suppose if possible
U,V\neq 0
in
\mathcal{T}_{c-1}
.
Thus
\operatorname{cx}_{A}U=\operatorname{cx}_{A}V=c
.
We have
\mathcal{V}^{*}(U)=\mathcal{V}^{*}(V)=\mathbb{P}^{c-1}
.
Thus
\mathcal{V}_{c-1}(U)=\mathcal{V}_{c-1}(V)=\mathbb{P}^{c-1}
.

Note
V
is two periodic in
\mathcal{T}_{c-1}
. So we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{c-1}}(U,\Omega^{-j}(V))=0\quad\text{for all}\ j\geq 0.

By Theorem
7.1
we get
\dim(\mathcal{V}_{i-1}(U)\cap\mathcal{V}_{c-1}(V))\leq c-2
. We get
\dim\mathbb{P}^{c-1}\leq c-2
which is a contradiction.
∎

Abstract
. Aharoni and Ziv conjectured that if M M and N N are finitary matroids on E E , then a certain “Hall-like” condition is sufficient to guarantee the existence of an M M -independent spanning set of N N . We show that their condition ensures that every finite subset of E E is N N -spanned by an M M -independent set.

1.
Introduction

Hall’s classical theorem
[
8
]
states that a finite bipartite graph
G=(S,T,E)
has no matching that covers
T
iff there exists an

X\subseteq T
with
\left|N(X)\right|<\left|X\right|
. The literal generalization of this theorem to infinite graphs fails. Indeed, in the classical
counterexample (“playboy paradox”)

\left|S\right|=\left|T\right|=\aleph_{0}
, a
t\in T
is connected to each point in
S
and
E(G-t)
is a
perfect matching of
G-t
. On the one hand, no matching covers
T
because each
t^{\prime}\in T-t
has a unique neighbour and
N(T-t)=S
. On the other hand,
\left|N(X)\right|\geq\left|X\right|
for every
X\subseteq T
. The “right” generalization of Hall’s
theorem which is also valid for infinite graphs was discovered by Aharoni. The key idea is that the
sizes of
X
and
N(X)
should be
compared via injections along
graph edges:

Theorem 1.1

(infinite Hall’s theorem by Aharoni,
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

A bipartite graph
G=(S,T,E)
has a matching that covers
T
iff there is no
X\subseteq T
such that
N(X)

can be matched to
X

and no matching covers
X
.

Theorem
1.1
turned out to be an efficient tool, the infinite version of König’s Duality Theorem was derived from it

[
2
]
, i.e. every bipartite
graph (finite or infinite)
has a
matching
F
such that one can obtain a vertex-cover by choosing precisely one endpoint from each
e\in F
. Other types of criteria of
matchability was introduced by Nash-Williams
[
10
]
. For more on infinite matching theory (including the discussion of the
non-bipartite case) we
refer to
[
1
]
.

Bipartite matchings are special cases of common independent sets of two matroids. Let
G=(S,T,E)
be a bipartite graph. For
v\in S\cup T
, let
U_{\delta(v),1}
be the
1
-uniform matroid on the set

\delta(v)
of edges incident with
v
(i.e. the singletons and the empty set are the independent sets). Consider the direct sums

M:=\bigoplus_{s\in S}U_{\delta(s),1}\ \ N:=\bigoplus_{t\in T}U_{\delta(t),1}.

Then matchings of
G
are exactly the common independent sets of
M
and
N
. Moreover, if
G
has no isolated
vertices in
T
, then a matching
F
covers
T
iff it is a base of
N
. This led the authors of

[
3
]
to call the common independent sets of two matroids a “matroidal matching”. Let us call the
pair
(M,N)
of matroids matchable if there exists an
M
-independent base of
N
and finitely matchable if every finite set is
N
-spanned by an
M
-independent set. The following matroidal generalization of Hall’s theorem was obtained by Edmonds (implicit in

[
7
]
):

Theorem 1.2

(Edmonds)
.

Assume that
M
and
N
are matroids on the common finite edge set
E
. Then
(M,N)
is matchable iff there is no
X\subseteq E

with
r_{M}(X)<r_{N.X}(X)
.

A standard way to extend the concept of matroids to allow infinite ground set is provided by the following axiomatization (see for example

[
11
]
or

[
12
, 745]
):

Definition 1.3
.

A finitary matroid

1

Some authors call it “infinite matroid”, “independence space” or “independence structure”.
is a pair

with
{\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(E)}
such that

;

(II)

If all finite subsets of an infinite set

, then

.

Theorems

and

made Aharoni and Ziv to expect that the following statement

(
[

Assume that

are finitary matroids on the common edge set

-independent base

Technically they used a slightly stronger condition they call “unhindered” but our “if and only if” formulation of

They reduced in the same paper the Matroid Intersection Conjecture of Nash-Williams
[

to

. In all the partial

so far, structural or size restrictions are assumed about the matroids

. In this paper, we

The paper is structured as follows. In the following section, Section
2
, we introduce the notation and recall a
few previous results that we need later. Section

is devoted to the proof of the main result and it is subdivided into three
subsections. In Subsection

, the concept of negligible and stable sets are introduced and a couple of reductions are

Notation and preliminaries

We write
\bm{\bigcup\mathcal{F}}
for the union of the sets in
\mathcal{F}
. The symmetric difference
\bm{X\triangle Y}
is defined as
(X\setminus Y)\cup(Y\setminus X)
. A pair
{M=(E,\mathcal{I})}
is a

if
{\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(E)}
satisfies the axioms

(I)
-
(IV)
.
The sets in
\mathcal{I}
are called
independent
and the sets
\mathcal{P}(E)\setminus\mathcal{I}
are
dependent
.
The maximal independent sets are the

while the minimal dependent sets are the
circuits
of the matroid.
For an
{X\subseteq E}
,
{\bm{M\!\!\upharpoonright\!\!X}:=(X,\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{P}(X))}
is a matroid and it is called the
restriction
of
M
to
X
.
We write
{\bm{M-X}}
for
M\!\!\upharpoonright\!\!(E\setminus X)
. Let
B
be a base of
M\!\!\upharpoonright\!\!X
.
The
contraction
of
X
in

is the matroid

\bm{M/X}=(E\setminus X,\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
where
I\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
iff
B\cup I\in\mathcal{I}
. This is well-defined, i.e.
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
does not depend on the choice of
B
. Let
\bm{M.X}:=M/(E\setminus X)
.
If
I
is independent in
M
but
I+e
is dependent for some
e\in E\setminus I
, then there is a unique
circuit
\bm{C_{M}(e,I)}
of
M
through
e
contained in
I+e
. We say
{X\subseteq E}

spans

{e\in E}
in matroid
M
if either
{e\in X}
or there exists a circuit
{C\ni e}
with
{C-e\subseteq X}
.
By letting
\bm{\mathsf{span}_{M}(X)}
be the set of edges spanned by

in
M
, we obtain a closure
operation

\mathsf{span}_{M}:\mathcal{P}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(E)
.
An
{S\subseteq E}
is

if
{\mathsf{span}_{M}(S)=E}
.

Let an ordered pair
(M,N)
of finitary matroids defined on

be fixed. The
matroidal Hall’s condition

asserts that
for every
X\subseteq E
for which there is an

-independent base
of

M\!\!\upharpoonright\!\!X
-independent base of
N.X
.

Observation 2.1
.

\mathsf{Hall}(M,N)
implies that
\mathsf{Hall}(M-X,N/X)
holds for every
X\subseteq E
.

We say that
(M,N)
is
finitely

if for every finite

there is an
I\in\mathcal{I}_{M}
with

.

The classical “augmenting path technique” developed by Edmonds
[
7
]
to prove his Matroid Intersection Theorem
will play an
important role in our proofs. For
I\in\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
, let

\bm{D(I)}=D_{(M,N)}(I)
be a digraph on
E
with the following arcs. For
x\in E\setminus I
and
y\in I
,

xy\in D(I)
if
x\in\mathsf{span}_{M}(I)
with
y\in C_{M}(x,I)
and
yx\in D(I)
if
x\in\mathsf{span}_{N}(I)
with

y\in C_{N}(x,I)
. We call these the arcs of
first type
and arcs of
second type
respectively. For
X\subseteq E
, we write
\bm{E(X,I)}
for
the set of those elements of
E

that are reachable from
X
in
D(I)
by a directed path. The set of edges entering (leaving) vertex set
X
in digraph
D
is denoted by
\bm{\delta_{D}^{-}(X)}
(
\bm{\delta_{D}^{+}(X)}
). An

augmenting path
for
I
is a directed path in
D(I)
from
E\setminus\mathsf{span}_{N}(I)
to
E\setminus\mathsf{span}_{M}(I)
without “jumping arcs”. More precisely, it is a
set
P=\{x_{0},\dots,x_{2n}\}\subseteq E
such that

(1)

x_{0}\in E\setminus\mathsf{span}_{N}(I)
,

(2)

x_{2n}\in E\setminus\mathsf{span}_{M}(I)
,

(3)

x_{i}x_{i+1}\in D(I)
for
i<2n
,

(4)

x_{i}x_{j}\notin D(I)
if
i+1<j\leq 2n
.

An
xy
-augmenting path
is an augmenting path starting at
x
and terminating at
y
. An

x
-augmenting path
is an augmenting path starting at
x
. The following lemma is due to Edmonds (for a proof with our notation see

[
9
, Corollary
3.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

If
P=\{x_{0},\dots,x_{2n}\}
is an augmenting path for
I\in\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
, then
I\triangle P\in\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
with
\mathsf{span}_{M}(I\triangle P)=\mathsf{span}_{M}(I+x_{2n})
and

\mathsf{span}_{N}(I\triangle P)=\mathsf{span}_{N}(I+x_{0})
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

If
P
is an augmenting path for
I
and
x\in E
such that
P\cap(\{x\}\cup\delta^{+}_{D(I)}(x))=\emptyset
, then
\delta^{+}_{D(I)}(x)\subseteq\delta^{+}_{D(I\triangle P)}(x)
.

For
I\in\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
, let
\bm{\mathcal{A}(I)}
consists of those elements of
\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
that can be obtained from
I
by a finite iteration of applying augmenting paths, i.e.

\mathcal{A}(I)=\{I\triangle P_{0}\triangle\cdots\triangle P_{n-1}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}\wedge P_{i}\text{ is an augmenting path for }I\triangle P_{0}\triangle\cdots\triangle P_{i-1}\}.

Reductions via negligible and stable sets

Definition 3.2
.

A set

Observation 3.3
.

In order to check the negligibility of a set, it is enough
to consider

N
-loops and
Y
without

Lemma 3.4

If

Proof.

Let a finite

-independent

-spans

. The matroid

is finitary

3

Axiom

.
, thus there is a finite

spans

-independent

If

Definition 3.9
.

A set
I\in\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
is called
(M,N)

\mathcal{I}_{M/I}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{N/I}

In other words,

Remark 3.10

The name “stable” was motivated by the fact that by applying augmenting paths iteratively starting with a stable

The empty set is obviously stable.

Observation 3.11
.

The union of a

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a chain of stable sets with

Lemma 3.12

For every family

Fix an enumeration

is already defined for some

be an extension of

to a maximal

. Then

-independent and spans

-independent, moreover, it is also

Now we show that

-independent. But then

-independent.

3.2.
Equivalence classes of finite common independent sets

Let
[\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}]^{<\aleph_{0}}
be the set of the finite common independent sets of
M
and
N
. For
I,J\in[\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}]^{<\aleph_{0}}
, we let
I\trianglelefteq J
if
I\subseteq\mathsf{span}_{M}(J)\cap\mathsf{span}_{N}(J)
. Clearly,
\trianglelefteq
is a preorder. Let
I\sim J
if
I\trianglelefteq J
and
I\trianglerighteq J
. This is an equivalence relation on
[\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}]^{<\aleph_{0}}
and
\trianglelefteq
induces a partial order on
([\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}]^{<\aleph_{0}})/\sim
in the usual way. We abuse the notation and
denote this partial order also by
\trianglelefteq
. The following two lemmas provide a characterisation of the equivalence
classes and a way to transition between the elements of a fixed class.

Lemma 3.16
.

If
O\subseteq E
induces a chordless directed cycle in
D(I)
for some
I\in\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
, then
I\triangle O\in\mathcal{I}_{M}\cap\mathcal{I}_{N}
with
I\sim I\triangle O
.

Proof.

By symmetry, it is enough to show that
I\triangle O\in\mathcal{I}_{M}

Characterization of solutions of refined Fermat-type functional equations in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n}
Molla Basir Ahamed, Sanju Mandal

Abstract
. The main purpose of this article is concerned with the existence and the precise forms of the transcendental solutions of several refined versions of Fermat-type functional equations with polynomial coefficients in several complex variables by utilizing the Nevanlinna theory of meromorphic functions in several complex variables. In fact, we investigate the existence and forms of the transcendental solutions of non-linear quadratic trinomial equations in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . As a consequence of our result, we show that solutions of binomial equations in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} can be explored and this exploration broaden the scope of the study of functional equations in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . The results we obtained are improvements over certain recent findings, noted as remarks. In addition, some examples relevant to the content of the paper have been exhibited in support of the validation of each results. Further, we discuss situations when solutions of such equations does not exist.

2.
Description of the solutions of Fermat-type functional equations in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

Over the past few years, Nevanlinna theory has made significant strides in the study of several complex variables. Notable advancements include the second fundamental theory of Nevanlinna and the application of difference analogues to logarithmic derivative lemmas, which have contributed substantially to the literature on holomorphic functions and their associated functional equations. Furthermore, through the application of the Nevanlinna theory for several complex variables with a difference perspective, Xu and Cao

4.21
) that
h_{2}(z)-h_{2}(z+c)
must be a constant, Hence,
e^{h_{2}(z)-h_{2}(z+c)}
must be a constant, say
A_{12}
. Suppose that

(4.22)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\sqrt{a}Q_{2}(z+c)}{i\sqrt{b}P(z+c)\left(Q_{2}(z)\frac{\partial h_{2}}{\partial z_{i}}+\frac{\partial Q_{2}}{\partial z_{i}}\right)}=A_{12}.

Rewriting (
4.20
), we get

(4.23)

\displaystyle P(z+c)=\frac{\sqrt{a}Q_{1}(z+c)}{A_{11}i\sqrt{b}\left(Q_{1}(z)\frac{\partial h_{1}}{\partial z_{i}}+\frac{\partial Q_{1}}{\partial z_{i}}\right)}

which shows that
Q_{1}(z)
and
\frac{\partial h_{1}}{\partial z_{i}}
must be constant, say
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}
, respectively. Then
h_{1}(z)=\beta_{1}z_{i}+\phi(w)
, where
w=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i-1},z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{n})
. Thus, it follows from (
4.22
) that
P(z+c)=\frac{\sqrt{a}\alpha_{1}}{i\sqrt{b}A_{11}\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}=P(z)
. Moreover, from (
4.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle P(z+c)=-\frac{\sqrt{a}Q_{2}(z+c)}{i\sqrt{b}A_{12}\left(Q_{2}(z)\frac{\partial h_{2}}{\partial z_{i}}+\frac{\partial Q_{2}}{\partial z_{i}}\right)}

and from this, we conclude that
Q_{2}(z)
and
\frac{\partial h_{2}}{\partial z_{i}}
are non-zero constant, say
\alpha_{2}
and
\beta_{2}
, respectively. Thus
h_{2}(z)=\beta_{2}z_{i}+\psi(w)
and this shows that
P(z+c)=-\frac{\sqrt{a}\alpha_{2}}{i\sqrt{b}\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}A_{12}}=P(z).
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{a}\alpha_{1}}{i\sqrt{b}\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}A_{11}}=-\frac{\sqrt{a}\alpha_{2}}{i\sqrt{b}\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}A_{12}}

and this implies that
A_{11}\beta_{1}+A_{12}\beta_{2}=0
. Moreover,
e^{h_{1}(z)-h_{1}(z+c)}=A_{11}
implies that
h_{1}(z)-h_{1}(z+c)=\ln A_{11}+2k\pi i
;
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. That is

\displaystyle\phi(w+c)-\phi(w)=-(\beta_{1}c_{i}+\ln A_{11}+2k\pi i)=\mbox{constant}.

Hence,
\phi(w)=a_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+a_{i-1}z_{i-1}+a_{i+1}z_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{n}z_{n}+r_{1}
. Therefore,
h_{1}(z)=a_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+a_{i-1}z_{i-1}+\beta_{1}z_{i}+a_{i+1}z_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{n}z_{n}+r_{1}
. Similarly, we obtain that
h_{2}(z)=b_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+b_{i-1}z_{i-1}+\beta_{2}z_{i}+b_{i+1}z_{i+1}+\cdots+b_{n}z_{n}+r_{2}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\beta_{2}e^{(a_{1}-b_{1})c_{1}+\cdots+(a_{i-1}-b_{i-1})c_{i-1}+(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})c_{i}+(a_{i+1}-b_{i+1})c_{i+1}+\cdots+(a_{n}-b_{n})c_{n}}+\beta_{1}=0.

Case-II:
If
e^{h_{2}(z)-h_{1}(z)}
is a constant, then
h_{2}(z)-h_{1}(z)
must be a constant. Then by (
4.17
),
e^{h_{1}(z+c)-h_{1}(z)}
is also a constant, otherwise, we get contradiction. Hence, we deduce that
h_{1}(z)=L_{1}(z)+H(s)+r_{3}
and
h_{2}(z)=L_{1}(z)+H(s)+r_{4}
, where
L_{1}(z)=\xi_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}z_{n}
,
H(s)
is a polynomial in
s=d_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}z_{n}
such that
d_{1}c_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}c_{n}=0
with
H(z+c)=H(z)
and
r_{3},r_{4}\in\mathbb{C}
. Therefore, from (
4.15
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle g(z)=2L_{1}(z)+2H(s)+r_{5},\;\mbox{where}\;r_{3}+r_{4}=r_{5}.

Indeed, we have the form of the solution as

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{1}(z-c)e^{L_{1}(z-c)+H(s)+r_{3}}+Q_{2}(z-c)e^{L_{1}(z-c)+H(s)+r_{4}}}{2\sqrt{a}}

\displaystyle=\gamma(z-c)e^{L_{1}(z-c)+H(s)},

where
\gamma(z)
satisfies

\displaystyle a\gamma^{2}(z)+bP^{2}(z)e^{-2L_{1}(c)}\left(\frac{\partial\gamma(z-c)}{\partial z_{i}}+(\xi_{i}+d_{i})\gamma(z-c)\right)^{2}=e^{r_{5}}Q(z).

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle aF^{2}+2\omega FG+bG^{2}=1,

Further, we can transform (

\displaystyle(\sqrt{a}F-\omega_{1}\sqrt{b}G)(\sqrt{a}F-\omega_{2}\sqrt{b}G)=1.

Since

4.1

Using (

(4.27)

\displaystyle f(z)=\dfrac{\omega_{2}e^{p(z)}-\omega_{1}e^{-p(z)}}{\sqrt{a}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})}e^{\frac{g(z)}{2}},

(4.28)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}f(z+c)+\gamma_{2}f(z)=\dfrac{e^{p(z)}-e^{-p(z)}}{\sqrt{b}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})}e^{\frac{g(z)}{2}}.

For brevity, let

(4.29)

\displaystyle h_{1}(z)=\dfrac{g(z)}{2}+p(z)\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;h_{2}(z)=\dfrac{g(z)}{2}-p(z).

The equations (
4.27
) and (
4.28
) can be written as

(4.30)

\displaystyle f(z)=\dfrac{\omega_{2}e^{h_{1}(z)}-\omega_{1}e^{h_{2}(z)}}{\sqrt{a}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})}

(4.31)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}f(z+c)+\gamma_{2}f(z)=\dfrac{e^{h_{1}(z)}-e^{h_{2}(z)}}{\sqrt{b}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})}.

Combining (
4.30
) and (
4.31
), an easy computation shows that

(4.32)

\displaystyle R_{11}e^{h_{1}(z)-h_{1}(z+c)}+R_{12}e^{h_{2}(z)-h_{1}(z+c)}+R_{13}e^{h_{2}(z+c)-h_{1}(z+c)}\equiv 1,

where

\displaystyle R_{11}=-\dfrac{(\omega_{2}\gamma_{2}\sqrt{b}-\sqrt{a})}{\omega_{2}\gamma_{1}\sqrt{b}},\;\;R_{12}=\dfrac{(\omega_{1}\gamma_{2}\sqrt{b}-\sqrt{a})}{\omega_{2}\gamma_{1}\sqrt{b}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;R_{13}=\dfrac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{2}}.

Case A:
If

\displaystyle f(z)=B_{1}e^{\frac{g(z)}{2}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\gamma_{1}f(z+c)+\gamma_{2}f(z)=B_{2}e^{\frac{g(z)}{2}},

\displaystyle B_{1}=\dfrac{\omega_{2}\xi-\omega_{1}\xi^{-1}}{\sqrt{a}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;B_{2}=\dfrac{\xi-\xi^{-1}}{\sqrt{b}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})}.

It is easy see that

and

. From (
4.33

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}B_{1}}{(B_{2}-\gamma_{2}B_{1})}e^{\frac{g(z+c)-g(z)}{2}}=1.

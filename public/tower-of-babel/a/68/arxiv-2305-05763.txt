On the Number of t t -Lee-Error-Correcting Codes
Nadja Willenborg, Anna-Lena Horlemann, Violetta Weger

Abstract
We consider t t -Lee-error-correcting codes of length n n over the residue ring ℤ m := ℤ / m ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}_{m}:=\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} and determine upper and lower bounds on the number of t t -Lee-error-correcting codes. We use two different methods, namely estimating isolated nodes on bipartite graphs and the graph container method. The former gives density results for codes of fixed size and the latter for any size. This confirms some recent density results for linear Lee metric codes and provides new density results for nonlinear codes. To apply a variant of the graph container algorithm we also investigate some geometrical properties of the balls in the Lee metric.



1
Introduction

A classical discipline in coding theory is the study of the behaviour of random codes and computing the density of optimal codes with respect to some bounds.
For Hamming metric codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
we have several famous results, such as
[
8
,
31
]
, where the authors show that if we let the length
n
grow, random codes attain with high probability the Gilbert-Varshamov bound
[
19
,
39
]
. On the other hand, it is well known, that if we let
q
grow, random codes are with high probability Maximum Distance Separable (MDS) codes, i.e., they attain the Singleton bound. Similar results have been obtained in the rank metric, namely for
n
growing random rank-metric codes attain the rank-metric Gilbert-Varshamov bound
[
28
]
and we know that the density of Maximum Rank Distance (MRD) codes
depends on whether they are
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear or
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear.
For the former it was shown in
[
29
]
that MRD codes are dense, while for the latter case
it was shown that MRD codes are neither dense nor sparse
[
3
,
12
,
20
,
23
]
. In this paper, we investigate such questions in a new setting: modules over integer residue rings endowed with the Lee metric.
The Lee metric has been introduced to cope with phase modulation, error correction in constrained memories, or in cryptographic applications (see for example
[
32
]
).
Density questions for such Lee metric codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
have already been studied in
[
11
,
13
]
. In particular, it has been shown that for
n
growing, random codes attain the respective Gilbert-Varshamov bound with high probability and that optimal linear codes with respect to Lee metric Singleton bounds
[
35
,
2
]
are sparse, for
n,s
or
p
growing. Sparseness has also been shown for optimal linear codes with respect to the Lee metric Plotkin bound. In this paper we confirm these results and use techniques from graph theory to obtain new results for nonlinear codes.

We consider two different techniques: bipartite graphs and the container method.
The former technique is based on bounding the number of some non-isolated nodes in certain bipartite graphs whose right node set consists of codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
of cardinality
S
and whose left node set contains elements from
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
whose minimum weight is upper bounded. From this approach we first derive upper and lower bounds on the density of Lee metric codes. These bounds then give us asymptotic results in the nonlinear case, which was previously unknown.

The second technique uses a graph
G=(V,E)
with node set
V=\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and edge set
E=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:x\neq y,d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)\leq 2t\}
, where
d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)
is the Lee distance between
x
and
y
. Note that
G
is also the
2t
graph power of the Cartesian product
C_{m}\Box C_{m}\Box\cdots\Box C_{m}
, where
C_{m}
is the cycle of length
m
. The node set of this Cartesian product is
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and there is an edge between
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
if and only if their Lee distance is
1
from each other, see also
[
1
,
25
]
. We show that when fixing
m
, the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes of length
n
is at most
2^{(1+o(1))H_{m}(n,t)}
, for all
t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}
. This problem has already been considered in
[
18
]
and
[
7
]
for Hamming metric codes, and also in
[
7
]
for codes endowed with the Enomoto-Katona distance (also called transportation distance). The key tool for estimating
t
-error-correcting codes are so-called ‘container results’ which state that the independent sets of a graph lie only in a small number of subsets of the node set which are ‘almost independent sets’. The estimate of
t
-error-correcting codes with distance
2t+1
is equivalent to the estimate of independent sets in our graph. Although our graph does not have the properties desired by Sapozhenko, i.e., the graph is almost regular and an expander, we can transfer the saturation estimates from
[
18
]
to the Lee metric and then apply the container result from
[
7
]
. To do this, we first derive estimates of the volumes of balls in the Lee metric with different radii. We also characterize the intersection of two balls with equal Lee radii with distance
\ell
between their respective centers. In particular we show that the size of these intersections is monotonically decreasing in
\ell
. We then give an upper bound on these quantities, including only the Lee volume for which explicit formulae are known, dating back to 1982
[
6
]
. All these results are new to the best of our knowledge. The consideration of the intersection of balls is interesting for several fundamental aspects of coding theory. Our intersection results determine the packing and covering properties of Lee metric codes which are important for decoding and performance analysis of these codes. As recently addressed in
[
30
]
, finding upper and lower bounds on the size of the intersection of two balls that can
be computed fast would be helpful for
considering covering properties of codes. We believe that our techniques could therefore be of interest in this context.

The outline of the article is as follows. We start with the preliminaries in Section
2
, including the Lee weight, the Lee volume and some useful bounds or asymptotic relations. In the second part of the preliminaries we examine the ratios of certain Lee volumes and also study the intersections of Lee spheres. Section
3
begins with the supersaturation estimates, which we then use in the container algorithm. We also show how we can use this method to estimate
t
-error-correcting codes of a fixed cardinality. This issue has already been studied in
[
22
]
for codes in the Hamming, rank and sum-rank metric. There, the counting problem was modelled in a bipartite graph satisfying certain regularity conditions and the number of codes was estimated by the number of isolated nodes in the graph. We modify this method slightly for the Lee metric and present it again in Section
4
Finally, we conclude this paper in Section
5
.

\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers,
\mathbb{Z}_{m}:=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}

. By

. We omit “

.
For nonnegative quantities depending on

.
We write

n
.
Given a finite set

, we write

for the collection of
t

X
, and

for the family of subsets of
X

elements.
The following identities, respectively upper bound, involving the binomial coefficient are well known

.

,

2.

\binom{n-1}{k}=\frac{n-k}{n}\binom{n}{k}

2.1
Lee Metric Codes

We briefly recall the Lee metric and the volume of its balls.

Definition 2.2
.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
we define the
Lee weight
to be

\displaystyle w^{\textnormal{L}}:\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rightarrow\left\{0,\dots,\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor\right\},\quad x\mapsto\min\{x,m-x\}.

We denote the average Lee weight over
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
by
\theta_{m}
and extend the weight additively to
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, so that the Lee weight for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is

\displaystyle w^{\textnormal{L}}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{i}).

We define the
Lee distance
of
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
as

\displaystyle d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y):=w^{\textnormal{L}}(x-y).

A
(Lee metric) code
is a subset
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
. When
|\mathscr{C}|\geq 2
its
minimum Lee distance
is

d^{\textnormal{L}}(\mathscr{C}):=\min\{d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)\colon x,y\in\mathscr{C},\,x\neq y\}.

A code
\mathscr{C}
is
linear
if it is a finitely generated submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
. The smallest number of elements in
\mathscr{C}
which generate
\mathscr{C}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-module is called the
rank
of
\mathscr{C}
. Since we restrict to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
in the linear case all linear codes we consider are
free
, i.e., the size of any generating set of
\mathscr{C}
determines the rank of
\mathscr{C}
.

Note that for
m=2
and
m=3
the Hamming distance and the Lee distance are identical by definition.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Lee metric ball
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
of radius
0\leq r\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor
with center
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is

\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r,c):=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)\leq r\}.

Its volume is independent of its center and we denote it by
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)
.

Definition 2.4
.

For any vector
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
we define its
Lee composition

\ell(z)
as

\ell(z):=(\ell_{0}(z),\ell_{1}(z),\dots,\ell_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(z)),

where

\ell_{i}(z):=|\{j\in[n]:w^{\textnormal{L}}(z_{j})=i\}|.

Clearly,
\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\ell_{i}(z)=n
and
\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}i\ell_{i}(z)=w^{\textnormal{L}}(z).

We denote by
\mathscr{L}_{m}^{n}:=\{\ell_{\mathbf{0}},\dots,\ell_{\mathbf{\alpha}}\}=\{\ell(z):z\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\}
the set of all Lee compositions, where
\alpha+1
is the number of different Lee compositions in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and
\ell_{\mathbf{0}}=\ell(0)=(n,0,\dots,0).

We then define the
Lee association scheme
such that
R=(R_{\ell_{\mathbf{0}}},\dots,R_{\mathbf{\ell_{\alpha}}})
forms a partition on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
by

(x,y)\in R_{\mathbf{\ell_{i}}}\Leftrightarrow\ell(x-y)=\ell_{\mathbf{i}},

while satisfying the axioms
1.-4.
, see
[
36
, Section 2.1]
:

1.

R_{\ell_{\mathbf{0}}}=\{(x,x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\}
.

2.

R_{\ell_{\mathbf{i}}}^{-1}=\{(y,x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:(x,y)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{i}}}\}=R_{\ell_{\mathbf{j}}}
for some
\mathbf{j}\in\{\mathbf{0},\dots,\mathbf{\alpha}\}
.

3.

The size of
\{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:(x,z)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{i}}}\,\text{and}\,(z,y)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{j}}}\}
is described by a number
p_{i,j}^{k}
which depends on
k
, but not on
(x,y)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{k}}}.

4.

For all
i,j,k\in\{0,\dots,\alpha\}
we have
p_{i,j}^{k}=p_{j,i}^{k}.

Remark 1
.

If
(x,y)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{i}}}
, then the Lee distance between
x
and
y
is

d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)=\ell_{\mathbf{i},1}(x-y)+2\ell_{\mathbf{i},2}(x-y)+\dots+\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor\ell_{\mathbf{i},\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(x-y).

This directly implies that the Lee metric is constant on the classes of the scheme. Let the classes of pairs
(x,y)
with
d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)=j
be denoted by
R_{\ell_{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\dots,R_{\ell_{\mathbf{j_{s}(j)}}},
where
\mathbf{s(j)}
is the number of different compositions having weight
j
.
This number
\mathbf{s(j)}
corresponds to the number of weak compositions of
j
into
n
parts such that each part is at most
\lfloor m/2\rfloor.

This number can be computed as

\mathbf{s(j)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r,s)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\\
r+s\lfloor m/2\rfloor=j\end{subarray}}(-1)^{s}\binom{n}{s}\binom{n+r-1}{r},

see, e.g.,
[
37
, Chapter 1]
.
Thus,

\mid\mathscr{L}_{m}^{n}\mid=\alpha+1=\sum_{j=0}^{n\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\mathbf{s(j)}.

By axiom
3.
we obtain:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
such that
d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)=k
. Then the number of vectors
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
with
d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,z)\leq r
and
d^{\textnormal{L}}(y,z)\leq r
is equal to

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{\mathbf{s(i)}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\mathbf{s(i)}}p_{i_{j},i_{\ell}}^{k},

where
p_{i,j}^{k}:=|\{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:(x,z)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{i}}}\wedge(y,z)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{j}}}\}|
which depends on
k
, but not on the pair
(x,y)\in R_{\ell_{\mathbf{k}}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A
t
-Lee-error-correcting code
of length
n
is a code in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
with minimum Lee distance at least
2t+1
. Its maximum size is denoted by
A_{m}(n,2t+1)
.

A simple counting argument shows the following:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
A_{m}(n,2t+1)
denote the maximum size of a
t
-Lee-error-correcting code
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and

(2.1)

H_{m}(n,t):=\frac{m^{n}}{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)},

then

\displaystyle A_{m}(n,2t+1)\leq H_{m}(n,t).

This upper bound is known as the
sphere packing (or Hamming) bound
. Codes that achieve equality for this upper bound are called
perfect codes
.

Furthermore, many Singleton-like bounds are known for Lee metric codes (see
[
13
]
for an overview on these bounds).
However, the smallest known bound on the minimum Lee distance of free linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
can be achieved using a Plotkin-like argument, where one averages the weight over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

(Plotkin-like bound, see
[
13
, Theorem 26]
)
Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
be a free linear
t
-Lee-error-correcting code. For
p\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}|\leq p^{s(n-\frac{8t+4}{p^{s}+1}+1)}.

2.2
Intersections and Estimates of the Lee Metric Volume

The volumes of the Lee metric balls are known:

Lemma 2.9
.

[
5
, Chapter 6.3]

\displaystyle\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor 2r/m\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}{{\sum}}_{j=0}^{r-(m/2)i}2^{j}\binom{n}{j}\binom{r-(m/2)i}{j}&{if $m$ is even},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{n+1}{i}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor 2r/m\rfloor}(-2)^{j}\binom{n}{j}\binom{n-j}{r-j(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1)-i}&{if $m$ is odd}.\\
\end{cases}

This gives rise to the following bounds:

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq r\leq n\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor
. Then

1.

\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)\leq{{\sum}}_{i=0}^{n}2^{i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{r}{i},

2.

\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)\geq\begin{cases}\binom{n}{r}2^{r}&{if $r<n$,}\\
2^{n}&{if $r\geq n$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The upper bound is given in
[
33
, Section 10]
. The lower bound is known from
[
40
]
. Indeed, if
r<n
,
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)
contains all vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
with
r
entries in
\{1,m-1\}
and zero elsewhere. If
r\geq n
,
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)
contains all vectors in
\{1,m-1\}^{n}
.
∎

We obtain the following asymptotic behavior of the volumes:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},C>0
be a constant and
1\leq r\leq n\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor
. Then

1.

\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)\in\omega(1)
as
n\to+\infty.

2.

\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)\in O(1)
as
m\to+\infty.

3.

If
r\lesssim n^{a/b}
, where
a<b
, then
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)\in o\left(\frac{C^{n}}{n^{(b+1)/b}}\right)
as
n\to+\infty.

Proof.

The first two statements follow directly from Lemma
2.10
. For
r\leq n^{a/b}
we have

\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)\leq{{\sum}}_{i=0}^{n^{a/b}}2^{i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{n^{a/b}}{i}\sim(2e^{2}n^{a/b})^{n^{a/b}}

for
n\rightarrow+\infty
(from Lemmata
2.1
and
2.10
).
Moreover

\displaystyle\frac{n^{(b+1)/b}(2e^{2}n^{a/b})^{n^{a/b}}}{C^{n}}=\frac{\text{exp}\left(\frac{b+1}{b}\log(n)+n^{a/b}(\log(2)+2+\log(n^{a/b}))\right)}{\text{exp}(n\log(C))}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\rightarrow+\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}0,

since the exponent of the denominator grows linearly in
n
, while the exponent of the nominator only grows by
n^{a/b}.

∎

Before studying the intersection of two Lee balls we derive estimates of the volumes of balls of different radii. The proofs can be found in the appendix.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
1\leq t\leq n\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor
and
C_{m}
be a sufficiently large constant. Then for any
1\leq i

\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)\leq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{t+i}{2(n+i)}\right)^{i}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n+i,t+i)&{if $m$ is even,}\\
\left(\frac{C_{m}}{n+i}\right)^{i}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n+i,t+i)&{if $m$ is odd and $t=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil$,}\\
\left(\frac{t+i}{n+i}\right)^{i}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n+i,t+i)&{else,}\end{cases}

and for
1\leq i\leq n\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor-t

\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)\leq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{t+i}{2n}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{n-i+1}{n-i+1-t}\right)^{i}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t+i)&{if $m$ is even,}\\
\left(\frac{C_{m}}{n}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{n-i+1}{n-i+1-t}\right)^{i}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t+i)&{if $m$ is odd and $t=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil$,}\\
\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{n-i+1}{n-i+1-t}\right)^{i}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t+i)&{else.}\\
\end{cases}

Next we investigate the intersection of two balls with equal Lee radii.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
0\leq t\leq n\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
, then the size of
\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r,c_{2})
depends on
c_{1},c_{2}
only through
d^{\textnormal{L}}(c_{1},c_{2}).

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Hence for
0\leq t\leq n\lfloor m/2\rfloor
, we denote the cardinality of the intersection of two balls in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
of radius
t
with centers being Lee distance
\ell
apart as
W(m,n,t,\ell)
. When there is no ambiguity about the ambient space considered we simply denote it as
W(t,\ell)
.
Obviously
W(t,\ell)=0
when
\ell>2t
. Thus, we will only focus on
1\leq\ell\leq 2t\leq nm,
as
t\leq n\lfloor m/2\rfloor.

In the following we show that the numbers
W(t,\ell)
are decreasing for increasing
\ell
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq\text{min}\{2t,n\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1\},
then

W(t,\ell+1)\leq W(t,\ell).

Proof.

We define the notation

(1^{\ell}0^{n-\ell}):=(\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{\ell},\underbrace{0,0,\dots,0}_{n-\ell}).

By Lemma
2.13
it is sufficient to determine the intersection of the radius
t
Lee balls centered at
c_{1}=(0^{n})
and
c_{2}=(1^{\ell}0^{n-\ell})
for
W(t,\ell)
, respectively the balls centered at
c_{1}
and
c_{3}=(1^{\ell+1}0^{n-\ell-1})
for
W(t,\ell+1)
.
Define

X:=\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{3})\setminus\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{2})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad Y:=\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{2})\setminus\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{3}).

Let
x\in X
. Since
x\in\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{3})
we have
d^{\textnormal{L}}(c_{1},x)\leq t
and
d^{\textnormal{L}}(c_{3},x)\leq t
. As
x\notin\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{2})
we also have
d^{\textnormal{L}}(c_{2},x)>t
. We obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}-1,\dots,x_{\ell+1}-1,x_{\ell+2},\dots,x_{n})\leq t

(2.3)

\displaystyle w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}-1,\dots,x_{\ell}-1,x_{\ell+1},\dots,x_{n})>t.

As
(x_{1}-1,\dots,x_{\ell+1}-1,x_{\ell+2},\dots,x_{n})
and
(x_{1}-1,\dots,x_{\ell}-1,x_{\ell+1},\dots,x_{n})
differ only in the
(\ell+1)^{th}
coordinate by exactly one, we have from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

(2.4)

\displaystyle w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}-1,\dots,x_{\ell+1}-1,x_{\ell+2},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=t

(2.5)

\displaystyle w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}-1,\dots,x_{\ell}-1,x_{\ell+1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=t+1.

Hence,
d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,c_{2})=t+1
and
d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,c_{3})=t.
Moreover, subtracting (
2.4
) from (
2.5
) gives

w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{\ell+1})-w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{\ell+1}-1)=1
, from which we deduce that
x_{\ell+1}\in\{1,\dots,\lfloor m/2\rfloor\},
as otherwise
w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{\ell+1})-w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{\ell+1}-1)=-1
and in particular,
d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,c_{2})<d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,c_{3}),
which is not possible.

To show that
|X|\leq|Y|
we define an injective map
\phi:X\rightarrow Y
by

\phi(x)_{i}:=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{ if }\,i\in[n]\setminus\{\ell+1\},\\
m-x_{i}+1&\text{if }\,i=\ell+1.\end{cases}

In the following we show that this map is well-defined. For this we will show that for every
x\in X
we have

\displaystyle d^{\textnormal{L}}(\phi(x),c_{1})<d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,c_{1})\leq t,\quad d^{\textnormal{L}}(\phi(x),c_{3})=t+1,\quad d^{\textnormal{L}}(\phi(x),c_{2})=t.

The first observation follows from the fact that
x_{\ell+1}\in\{1,\dots,\lfloor m/2\rfloor\}
and hence

w^{\textnormal{L}}(\phi(x)_{\ell+1})=x_{\ell+1}-1<x_{\ell+1}=w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{\ell+1}).

For the second observation recall that
d^{\textnormal{L}}(c_{3},x)=t
. We can deduce that

\displaystyle w^{\textnormal{L}}(\phi(x)_{\ell+1}-1)=w^{\textnormal{L}}(m-x_{\ell+1})=x_{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=(x_{\ell+1}-1)+1=w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{\ell+1}-1)+1,

which gives
d^{\textnormal{L}}(\phi(x),c_{3})=t+1.
The last observation follows similarly
and hence
\phi(x)\in Y
. As no two distinct elements are mapped onto the same image we have
|X|\leq|Y|
and thus

|\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{3})|=|X\sqcup Z|\leq|Y\sqcup Z|=|\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{2})|

where
Z=\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{2})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{3})
.
∎

In order to obtain relations between the numbers
W(t,\ell)
(which will be important for our saturation estimates given in Section
3
) we start by giving an explicit formula for
W(t,1)
and an upper bound for
W(t,\ell)
for general
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\lfloor m/2\rfloor\}
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq\text{min}\{2t,n\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1\}.

1.

For
\ell=1
we have

W(t,1)=\begin{cases}2\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m/2}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-i)&\text{if $m$ is even},\\
2\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{(m-1)/2}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-i)+\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-\frac{m-1}{2})&\text{if $m$ is odd.}\end{cases}

2.

For
\ell\geq 1
we have
W(t,\ell)\leq m^{\ell}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-\lceil\ell/2\rceil,t-\lceil\ell/2\rceil).

Proof.

Let
\ell=1
, suppose that the two balls are centered at
c_{1}:=(0^{n})
and
c_{2}:=(10^{n-1})
and let
x\in\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{2})
. This gives
w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1})\leq t
and because of translation invariance the remaining
n-1
coordinates have Lee weight at most

t-\max\{w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}),w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}-1)\}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\max\{w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}),w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{1}-1)\}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\,x_{1}=0,\\
x_{1}&\text{if}\,1\leq x_{1}\leq\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\\
m-x_{1}&\text{if}\,x_{1}=\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\,\textnormal{and $m$ is odd},\\
m-x_{1}+1&\text{in all other cases}.\end{cases}

Hence, for a fixed

•

x_{1}=0
there are
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-1)
many choices for the remaining
n-1
coordinates of
x
,

•

x_{1}\in\{1,2,\dots,\lfloor m/2\rfloor\}
there are
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-x_{1})
many choices for the remaining
n-1
coordinates,

•

x_{1}\in\{\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor+1,\dots,m-1\}
there are
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-m+x_{1}-1)
many choices for the remaining
n-1
coordinates,

•

x_{1}=(m+1)/2
and
m
odd there are
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-(m-1)/2)
many choices for the remaining
n-1
coordinates.

For the second statement suppose that the two balls are centered at
c_{1}=(0^{n})
and
c_{2}=(1^{\ell}0^{n-\ell})
. For
x\in\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{1})\cap\mathbf{B}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t,c_{2})
we have that the last
n-\ell
coordinates have Lee weight at most
t-\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{i}),\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{i}-1)\}.

Moreover, we observe that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{i}),\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{i}-1)\right\}\geq\left\lceil\ell/2\right\rceil.

Let
s
be the support size of the first
\ell
coordinates of
x
. If
s\geq\lceil\ell/2\rceil
(
2.6
) can be immediately derived from the fact that the Lee weight of the first
\ell
coordinates is at least as large as the support size. If
s<\lceil\ell/2\rceil
at least
\lceil\ell/2\rceil
coordinates of
x
are zero and therefore
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w^{\textnormal{L}}(x_{i}-1)\geq\lceil\ell/2\rceil
.
Now the upper bound can be derived using the monotonicity of the Lee volume in both parameters.
∎

Using Lemma
2.15
we can see that the intersection of two balls with centers being distance
\ell
apart is significantly smaller than the intersection of two balls with centers being distance
1
apart. Moreover, by applying Lemma
2.12
we can give an upper bound on
W(t,1)
which then contains only the volume
\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
0\leq\ell<\min\{t,n\lfloor m/4\rfloor\},
and
C_{m}
be a sufficiently large constant, then

\displaystyle W(t,2\ell+1)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{mt}{2n}\right)^{\ell}W(t,1)\leq\left(\frac{mt}{2n}\right)^{\ell+1}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)&\text{if }m\text{ is even,}\\
\left(\frac{mC_{m}}{n}\right)^{\ell}W(t,1)\leq\left(\frac{mC_{m}}{n}\right)^{\ell+1}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)&\text{if }m\text{ is odd and }t=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil,\\
\left(\frac{mt}{n}\right)^{\ell}W(t,1)\leq\left(\frac{mt}{n}\right)^{\ell+1}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)&\text{in all other cases.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We only show the case where
m
is even, the other cases follow from similar arguments.

Combining both statements from Lemma
2.15
, together with Lemma
2.12
we can deduce the first statement, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{W(t,2\ell+1)}{W(t,1)}\leq\frac{m^{\ell}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-\ell-1,t-\ell-1)}{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-1)}\leq m^{\ell}\left(\frac{t}{2n}\right)^{\ell},

which proves the first inequality. To prove the second inequality, apply again Lemma
2.12
to yield

\displaystyle\frac{W(t,1)}{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)}\leq\frac{m\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n-1,t-1)}{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)}\leq m\left(\frac{t}{2n}\right).

∎

3
The Number of
t
-Lee-Error-Correcting Codes

In this section we give an upper bound on the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes, where we do not prescribe the cardinality of the codes. Throughout this section, unless otherwise stated, we allow
t
to be a function in
n
. More precisely, we let
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}t(n)/(10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n})=T,
where
T\in[0,1]
and
\theta_{m}
denotes the average Lee weight over
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. However, we will often use
t
as a shorthand for
t(n)
.

To obtain an upper bound on the number of
t
-error-correcting codes we use a similar container algorithm as in
[
7
]
. In order to apply this algorithm, we will first derive the necessary supersaturation estimates.

Throughout this section
G=(V,E)
is a simple, undirected graph without self-loops, nodes
V=\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and edges
E=\{(x,y)\in V^{2}:d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)\leq 2t\}.
Any set
I\subseteq V
that does not contain an edge is called an
independent set
and corresponds to a
t
-Lee-error-correcting code.
Moreover, we write
\Delta(G)
for the
maximum degree
of
G
and
i(G)
for the
number of independent sets
in
G
. By
N_{G}(u)
we denote the neighbourhood of a node
u
, i.e., the subset of nodes that are adjacent to
u
. Hence,

\Delta(G)=\max\{\mid N_{G}(u)\mid:u\in V\}.

The container algorithm is based on a dynamic graph
(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
(initialized as
G_{0}=G
), iteratively reducing its node set
V(G_{i})
by at least one node. More precisely, in each iteration of the algorithm the node with maximum degree (and possibly its neighbourhood) is removed from the graph.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
be a code and
G[\mathscr{C}]=(\mathscr{C},E[\mathscr{C}])
be the corresponding induced subgraph of
G
.
For each
1\leq r\leq 2t
, we define

E_{r}:=\{(x,y)\in E[\mathscr{C}]:d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)=r\}

and for
v\in\mathscr{C}
the number of edges in
E_{r}
incident to
v
by

\deg_{r}(v):=|\{u\in\mathscr{C}:d^{\textnormal{L}}(u,v)=r\}|.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
v\in\mathscr{C}
and
r\leq n/2
. Then

\deg_{r}(v)\leq 3^{r}\binom{n}{r}

and therefore

|E_{r}|\leq\frac{3^{r}}{2}\binom{n}{r}|\mathscr{C}|.

Proof.

With the binomial theorem we get
\deg_{r}(v)\leq\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}2^{i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{r}{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}2^{i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{r}{i}

\leq\binom{n}{r}\sum_{i=0}^{r}2^{i}\binom{r}{i}=3^{r}\binom{n}{r}
, which proves the first statement. The second follows from summing over all pairs of codewords in
\mathscr{C}
.
∎

We have the following estimates for the Lee metric.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
be a
t
-Lee-error-correcting code and
C_{m}
be a sufficiently large constant. If
|\mathscr{C}|\geq 2H_{m}(n,t)
,
then

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{2t}W(t,r)|E_{r}|\geq\frac{|\mathscr{C}|^{2}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)^{2}}{10m^{n}}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\quad|E[\mathscr{C}]|\geq\begin{cases}\frac{n|\mathscr{C}|^{2}}{5mtH_{m}(n,t)}&\,\textnormal{if $m$ is even},\\
\frac{n|\mathscr{C}|^{2}}{10mC_{m}H_{m}(n,t)}&\,\textnormal{if $m$ is odd and $t=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil$},\\
\frac{n|\mathscr{C}|^{2}}{10mtH_{m}(n,t)}&\,\textnormal{in all other cases}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Similar to
[
7
, Lemma 5.3]
, we use Lemma
2.14
and Lemma
2.16
to obtain the result. For completeness we include the proof in the appendix.
∎

The container method gives estimates for the number of independent sets of a graph by showing that the independent sets lie only in a small number of subsets of the node set which are ‘almost independent sets‘.
We will study the more detailed relationships after we have established the required saturation estimates. Our first supersaturation result gives a constraint on the size of the node set to ensure that the maximum degree of the induced graph is above a certain bound.

Proposition 3.3
.

(Supersaturation 1)
Let
t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}
,
C_{m}
be a sufficiently large constant and
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
be a
t
-Lee-error-correcting code. If
|\mathscr{C}|\geq n^{4}H_{m}(n,t)
, then

\Delta(G[\mathscr{C}])\gtrsim\begin{cases}\frac{n^{3}|\mathscr{C}|}{C_{m}H_{m}(n,t)}&\,\textnormal{if $m$ is odd and $t=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil$},\\
\frac{n^{3}|\mathscr{C}|}{t^{3}H_{m}(n,t)}&\,\textnormal{in all other cases},\\
\end{cases}

asymptotically with respect to
n
.

Proof.

We will show the statement for the case where
m
is even, the proof is similar if
m
is odd.
Since
|\mathscr{C}|\geq n^{4}H_{m}(n,t)
we have from Lemma
3.2

|E[\mathscr{C}]|\geq\frac{|\mathscr{C}|^{2}n}{5mtH_{m}(n,t)}\geq|\mathscr{C}|\frac{n^{5}}{5mt}.

Together with
|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|\lesssim n^{2}|\mathscr{C}|
and
|E_{3}|+|E_{4}|\lesssim n^{4}|\mathscr{C}|
(which follows from Lemma
3.1
) this gives
|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|\lesssim\frac{t|E[\mathscr{C}]|}{n^{3}}
and
|E_{3}|+|E_{4}|\lesssim\frac{t|E[\mathscr{C}]|}{n}.

Furthermore, by Lemma
2.16
we have

\displaystyle W(t,2)\leq W(t,1),

\displaystyle W(t,4)\leq W(t,3)\lesssim\frac{tW(t,1)}{n},

\displaystyle W(t,r)\leq W(t,5)\lesssim\frac{t^{2}W(t,1)}{n^{2}}\quad\forall r\geq 5,

which gives

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{2t}W(t,r)|E_{r}|\lesssim\left(\frac{t}{n^{3}}+\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}+\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}\right)|E[\mathscr{C}]|W(t,1)\lesssim\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}W(t,1)|E[\mathscr{C}]|.

By Lemma
3.2
we have

\frac{|\mathscr{C}|^{2}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)^{2}}{m^{n}}\lesssim\sum_{r=1}^{2t}W(t,r)|E_{r}|\lesssim\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}W(t,1)|E[\mathscr{C}]|.

Using
W(t,1)\lesssim(t/n)\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)
from Lemma
2.16
we obtain

|E[\mathscr{C}]|\gtrsim\frac{n^{2}|\mathscr{C}|^{2}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)^{2}}{t^{2}m^{n}W(t,1)}\gtrsim\frac{n^{3}|\mathscr{C}|^{2}}{t^{3}H_{m}(n,t)}.

Thus, the average degree in
G[\mathscr{C}]
is
\gtrsim\frac{n^{3}|\mathscr{C}|}{t^{3}H_{m}(n,t)}
, and hence
\Delta(G[\mathscr{C}])\gtrsim\frac{n^{3}|\mathscr{C}|}{t^{3}H_{m}(n,t)}.

∎

In the following we will give an estimate for the maximum degree that certifies that
i(G[\mathscr{C}])
is below
2^{(1+\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}
. To get our estimate we consider the nodes of
G[\mathscr{C}]
in terms of their number of neighbours such that their Lee distance is at most
20
from each other. By considering balls of radius
t
and showing that their overlap is negligible we can estimate the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes for
60\leq t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}
. Using a number less than
20
would not give us suitable estimates in Lemma
3.5
. Therefore, we choose this number. We define the sets

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{1}:=\left\{v\in\mathscr{C}:\deg_{r}(v)\leq\varepsilon n^{\lceil r/2\rceil/2}\quad\forall r\in\{1,\dots,20\}\right\},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{2}:=\left\{v\in\mathscr{C}:\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{20}\deg_{r}(v)\geq\frac{\log(n)}{\varepsilon}\right\}

and compute
i(G[\mathscr{C}_{i}]),
for
i=1,2
, assuming that the maximum degree of
G[\mathscr{C}_{i}]
is not too large. Note that for
v\in\mathscr{C}
we have
\sum_{r=1}^{20}\deg_{r}(v)\in o(n^{5})
if
n\to+\infty
and hence
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}_{1}\cup\mathscr{C}_{2}
for sufficiently large
n
. Since every independent set of
G[\mathscr{C}]
can potentially be a union of an independent set of
G[\mathscr{C}_{1}]
and
G[\mathscr{C}_{2}]
when
n
is sufficiently large, we get an overall estimate using
i(G[\mathscr{C}])\leq i(G[\mathscr{C}_{1}])i(G[\mathscr{C}_{2}])
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}
,
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
be a
t
-Lee-error-correcting code,
\Delta(G[\mathscr{C}])\leq n^{5}
and fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let

\mathscr{C}_{1}
be as defined in (
3.1
).

Then
|\mathscr{C}_{1}|\leq(1+O(\varepsilon))H_{m}(n,t)
, for sufficiently large
n
.

Proof.

The proof can be found in the appendix.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
60\leq t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}
,
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
be a
t
-Lee-error-correcting code and
\Delta(G[\mathscr{C}])\leq n^{5}
. Fix
0<\varepsilon
, let
n
be sufficiently large and
\mathscr{C}_{2}
be as defined in (
3.2
).
Then the number of independent subsets of
\mathscr{C}_{2}
satisfies

Proof.

The proof can be found in the appendix.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

(Supersaturation 2)
Let
0<\varepsilon
,
60\leq t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n},\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
be a
t
-Lee-error-correcting code and let
n
be sufficiently large. If

Defining
\mathscr{C}_{1}
and

) and (

∎

To proof the container lemma also for small
t
, i.e.,

.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and

pairs

Using Lemma
2.16
the proof is similar to the proof of

Assuming these saturation results, we can use a modification of the container lemma
[
18
, Lemma 2.3]
, which directly implies the main theorem. For completeness, we give the proof. However, we will first describe the container algorithm and its properties.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
1\leq t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n},0<\varepsilon
and
n
be sufficiently large. Then there is a collection
\mathscr{F}
of subsets of

with the following properties

(i)

|\mathscr{F}|\leq 2^{\varepsilon H_{m}(n,t)}.

(ii)

Every
t

.

(iii)

i(G[F])\leq 2^{(1+\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}
for every
F\in\mathscr{F}
.

Recall that
G=(V,E)
describes a graph with

and

.
For the proof of Lemma
3.8
we will apply the following version of the algorithm of Kleitman and Winston
[
26
,
27
]
.

Algorithm 1
.

Fix an arbitrary order
v_{1},\dots,v_{m^{n}}
of the elements of
V
. Let
I
be an independent set in
G
. Initialize
G_{0}:=G
and
P:=\emptyset
. If

is even set

, if

is odd and

sufficiently large such that Lemma

is satisfied and set

. In each step
i
of the algorithm add nodes to
P
through the following process.

Let
u
be the node of maximum degree in

In case there are two nodes having maximum degree, choose the first node in the ordering.

If

, define

and proceed to step

.

If

to
P
, define

and proceed to step

.

If

and

terminate and output

and

.

.

Lemma 3.10

Let

there is some subset
P\subseteq I
that satisfies the following three conditions:

i)

I\subseteq P\cup f(P)
,

ii)

|P|\leq\frac{CH_{m}(n,t)\log(n)}{n}
,

iii)

i(G[P\cup f(P)])\leq 2^{(1+\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}
.

Proof.

The first observation follows by structural induction. To prove the remaining two conditions, we distinguish two stages of the algorithm according to the size of
V(G_{i})
.

•

Let
P_{1}
denote the set of nodes
u\in P
added to
P
when
|V(G_{i-1})|\geq n^{4}H_{m}(n,t)
.

•

Let
P_{2}
denote the set of nodes
u\in P
added to
P
when
|V(G_{i-1})|<n^{4}H_{m}(n,t).

That is, there exists a
k>0
such that every node added to
P
up to and including step
k
is in
P_{1}
, and every node added to
P
after step
k
is in
P_{2}
. Let
\beta:=\frac{n^{3/2}}{H_{m}(n,t)}
.
As we add nodes to
P_{1}
, by Lemma
3.3
we remove at least a
\beta
fraction of nodes from
G_{i-1}
to get
G_{i}
.
So
(1-\beta)^{k}m^{n}\leq n^{4}H_{m}(n,t)
, respectively
(1-\beta)^{k}\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)\leq n^{4}
.
By Lemma
2.11
we have
\beta\rightarrow 0
as
n\to+\infty
, so that
\beta\leq\log\left(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\right)
for sufficiently large
n
and therefore

\displaystyle|P_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log\left(\frac{n^{4}H_{m}(n,t)}{m^{n}}\right)}{\log\left(1-\beta\right)}\leq\frac{\log\left(\frac{m^{n}}{n^{4}H_{m}(n,t)}\right)}{\log\left(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\right)}\lesssim\frac{\log(\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t))-4\log(n)}{\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{t\log(n)H_{m}(n,t)}{n^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\log(n)H_{m}(n,t)}{n},

where (*) follows from Lemmata
2.1
and
2.10
.
As we add nodes to
P_{2}
we have
i(G_{i-1})\geq 2^{(1+\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}
during the associated iteration. By Lemma
3.6
we remove at least
n^{5}
nodes in each iteration and hence

|P_{2}|\lesssim\frac{n^{4}H_{m}(n,t)}{n^{5}}\leq\frac{H_{m}(n,t)}{n}.

This gives

|P|=|P_{1}|+|P_{2}|\leq\frac{C\log(n)\cdot H_{m}(n,t)}{n},

and thus

i(G[P\cup f(P)])\leq 2^{|P|}\cdot i(f(P))\leq 2^{(1+2\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}.

∎

We can now proceed with the proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be the graph with
V=\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and
E=\{(x,y)\in V^{2}:d^{\textnormal{L}}(x,y)\leq 2t\}
.
Suppose that
I
ranges over all independent sets of
G
. If
1\leq t\leq 60
, let
\mathscr{F}
be the collection of all
P\cup f(P)
obtained by Algorithm
1
, otherwise let
\mathscr{F}
be the collection of all
P\cup f(P)
obtained by Algorithm
2
. Since
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes are exactly the independent sets in
G
, we get from Lemmata
3.9
and
3.10
that every
t
-Lee-error-correcting code is contained in some
F\in\mathscr{F}
. The third condition in Lemmata
3.9
and
3.10
gives
i(G[F])\leq 2^{(1+2\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}
, for each
F\in\mathscr{F}
. By Lemma
2.1

\log\binom{n}{m}\leq m\log(O(n)/m)
. This gives for
1\leq t<60

\displaystyle\log|\mathscr{F}|

\displaystyle\leq\log\binom{m^{n}}{\leq\frac{\varepsilon H_{m}(n,t)\log(n)}{n}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon H_{m}(n,t)\log(n)}{n}\log\left(O\left(\frac{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t)n}{\varepsilon\log(n)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon H_{m}(n,t)\log(n)}{n}\log\left(O\left(\frac{(2en)^{t}n}{t^{t}\varepsilon\log(n)}\right)\right)

and similarly, for
60\leq t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}
and sufficiently large
C
that

\displaystyle\log|\mathscr{F}|\leq\log\binom{m^{n}}{\leq\frac{C\log(n)}{n}H_{m}(n,t)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{C\log(n)}{n}H_{m}(n,t)\log\left(O\left(\frac{n^{t+1}(2e/t)^{t}}{C\log(n)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{Ct(\log(n))^{2}}{n}H_{m}(n,t).

Hence,
|\mathscr{F}|\leq 2^{\varepsilon H_{m}(n,t)},
for sufficiently large

. This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.8
.
∎

We can now state our main result on the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
1\leq n,2\leq m,1\leq t\leq 10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n},
then the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is asymptotically at most
2^{(1+o(1))H_{m}(n,t)},
for sufficiently large
n
.

Proof.

Let
0<\varepsilon
and
\mathscr{F}
be as in Lemma
3.8
, then the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathscr{F}}i(G[F])\leq|\mathscr{F}|2^{(1+\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}=2^{(1+2\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)},

since
\varepsilon
can be chosen arbitrarily small, the result follows.
∎

In the following we give an upper bound on the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes of given size. We will use this result for experimental results in Python and compare it with the upper bound obtained from bipartite graphs in Section

.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
2\leq m,1\leq t\leq 60,

0<\varepsilon
and let
1\leq n
be sufficiently large, then the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes of length
n
and size
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
is asymptotically at most

2^{\varepsilon H_{m}(n,t)}\binom{(1+\varepsilon)H_{m}(n,t)}{S}.

Proof.

For
1\leq t\leq 60
we let
\mathscr{F}
be the collection of all

obtained by Algorithm

. From Lemma

we have

i.e., an upper bound on the size of each container. Since any

∎

Remark 2
.

Note that due to Lemma
2.11
using supersaturation estimates depending on
H_{m}(n,t)
when considering
m
towards infinity, we would only consider the trivial code
\mathscr{C}=\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and end up with trivial counting results. The container method is therefore not suitable for considering the case of increasing
m
. In Section
4
we will use a different counting technique, based on bipartite graphs, to also estimate the number of codes with a prescribed distance as
m
tends to infinity.

Recall that
A_{m}(n,d)
denotes the maximum size of a code
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
of minimum distance
d
.
We start by showing that
A_{m}(n,2t+1)=o(H_{m}(n,t)/n)
for
10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}<t<n^{4/5}
. The following version of the Elias bound will be crucial in this section.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
1\leq d,n
be integers and
\theta_{m}
be the average Lee weight over
\mathbb{Z}_{m}

For every
r\leq\theta_{m}n
such that

0<r^{2}-2\theta_{m}nr+\theta_{m}nd,

the following upper bound holds

A_{m}(n,d)\leq\frac{\theta_{m}nd}{r^{2}-2\theta_{m}nr+\theta_{m}nd}\cdot\frac{m^{n}}{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)}.

Proof.

Similarly as in the Hamming metric, see
[
38
, Theorem 5.2.11]
.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\theta_{m}
be the average Lee weight over
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. If
10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}<t<n^{4/5},
we have

A_{m}(n,2t+1)=o\left(\frac{H_{m}(n,t)}{n}\right).

Proof.

We apply the Elias bound with
r=t+\alpha
, where
\alpha=7
. When
10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}<t
we satisfy the condition

(3.3)

r^{2}\lesssim r^{2}-\theta_{m}(2\alpha-1)n=r^{2}-2\theta_{m}nr+\theta_{m}n(2t+1).

In fact, when
10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}<t<n^{4/5}
we have
\theta_{m}(2\alpha-1)n=13\cdot\theta_{m}n
whereas
100\cdot\theta_{m}n<r^{2}.

Applying Theorem
3.13
, together with the second part of Lemma
2.12
gives

\displaystyle A_{m}(n,2t+1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta_{m}n(2t+1)}{r^{2}-2\theta_{m}nr+\theta_{m}n(2t+1)}\cdot\frac{m^{n}}{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,r)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:EliasCond}}}{{\lesssim}}\frac{nt}{(t+\alpha)^{2}}\cdot\frac{m^{n}}{\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,t+\alpha)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\frac{n}{t}\left(\frac{(t+\alpha)(n-\alpha+1)}{2n(n-\alpha+1-t)}\right)^{\alpha}H_{m}(n,t)&\,\textnormal{if $m$ is even}\\
\frac{n}{C_{m}}\left(\frac{(C_{m}+\alpha)(n-\alpha+1)}{n(n-\alpha+1-t)}\right)^{\alpha}H_{m}(n,t)&\textnormal{if $m$ is odd and $t=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil$,}\\
\frac{n}{t}\left(\frac{(t+\alpha)(n-\alpha+1)}{n(n-\alpha+1-t)}\right)^{\alpha}H_{m}(n,t)&\textnormal{in all other cases,}\end{cases}

where
0<C_{m}
is a sufficiently large constant.
Similar calculations to those used for
[
18
, Proposition 5.2]
lead in all three cases to
A_{m}(n,2t+1)=o(H_{m}(n,t)/n)
.
∎

This now implies a result about the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes when

.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
2\leq m,10\sqrt{\theta_{m}n}<t<n^{4/5},
then the number of

-Lee-error-correcting codes of length

in

is asymptotically at most

By Proposition

the number of

∎

In Theorem

we obtained different restrictions on

than Dong et al. in

for the Hamming metric. In the Hamming metric the region of

4
Counting Codes on Bipartite Graphs

In this section, we determine upper and lower bounds on the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes by estimating isolated nodes on bipartite graphs. In particular, we define the density function of these codes of given cardinality within the set of codes of the same cardinality. Using the bipartite graph model, we can even consider linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(for
p
prime). We determine the asymptotic behaviour of the density function and obtain density results for Lee metric codes that are optimal with respect to various bounds.
Let
1\leq t\leq\lfloor\frac{nm-2}{4}\rfloor
, and consider the density function

(4.1)

\delta_{m}^{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n},S,t):=\frac{|\{\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:|\mathscr{C}|=S,\,d^{\textnormal{L}}(\mathscr{C})\geq 2t+1\}|}{|\{\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}:|\mathscr{C}|=S\}|}

of (possibly) nonlinear t-Lee-error-correcting codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and the density function of linear t-Lee-error-correcting codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
with minimum Lee distance at least
2t+1
, which is denoted by

(4.2)

\delta_{p}^{L}[\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},S,t]:=\frac{|\{\mathscr{C}\leq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}:|\mathscr{C}|=S,\,d^{\textnormal{L}}(\mathscr{C})\geq 2t+1\}|}{|\{\mathscr{C}\leq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}:|\mathscr{C}|=S\}|}.

Note that nonlinear Lee metric codes can have any cardinality
2\leq|\mathscr{C}|\leq m^{n}
, whereas the cardinality of linear codes is a power of
p
.

To give upper and lower bounds on the densities (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) we use counting arguments on the number of isolated nodes in
bipartite graphs
that are regular with respect to certain maps defined on their left nodes. This approach was first proposed in
[
23
]
and applied to different metrics and different types of linearity in
[
22
]
.

We recall the concept of an
association
on a finite non-empty set
\mathscr{V}
of magnitude
r\geq 0
(see
[
23
]
), which is a function

\alpha:\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}\to\{0,...,r\}
satisfying the following:

(i)

\alpha(V,V)=r
for all
V\in\mathscr{V}
;

(ii)

\alpha(V,V^{\prime})=\alpha(V^{\prime},V)
for all
V,V^{\prime}\in\mathscr{V}
.

Given a finite bipartite graph
\mathscr{B}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{W},\mathscr{E})
and an association
\alpha
on
\mathscr{V}
of magnitude
r
, we call
\mathscr{B}

\alpha
-regular
if for all
(V,V^{\prime})\in\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}
the number of nodes
W\in\mathscr{W}
with
(V,W)\in\mathscr{E}
and

(V^{\prime},W)\in\mathscr{E}
depends only on
\alpha(V,V^{\prime})
. We then denote this number by
\mathscr{W}_{\ell}(\alpha)
, where
\ell=\alpha(V,V^{\prime})\in\{0,\dots,r\}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
23
, Lemma 3.2 & Lemma 3.5]

Let
\mathscr{B}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{W},\mathscr{E})
be a bipartite
\alpha
-regular graph, where
\alpha
is an association on
\mathscr{V}
of magnitude
r
.
Let
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{W}
be the collection of non-isolated nodes of
\mathscr{W}
. If
\mathscr{W}_{r}(\alpha)>0
, then

(i)

|\mathscr{F}|\leq|\mathscr{V}|\,\mathscr{W}_{r}(\alpha).

(ii)

|\mathscr{F}|\geq\frac{\mathscr{W}_{r}(\alpha)^{2}\,|\mathscr{V}|^{2}}{\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}\mathscr{W}_{\ell}(\alpha)\,|\alpha^{-1}(\ell)|}.

4.1
Nonlinear Lee Metric Codes

We begin by giving bounds on the number of (possibly) nonlinear Lee metric codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
whose minimum distance is bounded from below by
2t+1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
S\geq 1
and
1\leq t<+\infty
be integers. Define the quantities

\displaystyle\beta^{0}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}m^{n}(\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)-1)-2\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)+3,

\displaystyle\beta^{1}

\displaystyle:=2\textbf{v}_{m}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)-4,

\displaystyle:=1+\beta^{1}\frac{S-2}{m^{n}-2}+\beta^{0}\frac{(S-2)(S-3)}{(m^{n}-2)(m^{n}-3)},

The proof is analogous to

∎

From Theorem

.

.

4.2
Linear Lee Metric Codes

In this section we consider linear codes
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
of a certain dimension

. Again we begin by giving bounds on the number of linear Lee metric codes in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
whose minimum distance is bounded from below by

.
We consider the same bipartite graph as done in the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 2.3]
, except that – due to the fact that the Lee weight for an element

may increase under scalar multiplication –
we consider the whole set of elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
with Lee distance at most
2t
(except zero) instead of considering
1
-dimensional vector spaces as done in
[
22
]
. Hence, we let the left node set of the graph be
\mathbf{B}_{p}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t,0)\setminus\{0\}.
Furthermore, we will consider linear Lee metric codes only over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, i.e., fields of prime order. The reason for this is that the regularity conditions on the graph can no longer be fulfilled if the elements in the left node set of
\mathscr{B}
have different orders which is the case if we consider codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
where
s>1.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
1\leq k\leq n
and
1\leq t<+\infty
be integers and

\mathscr{F}:=\{\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}:\dim(\mathscr{C})=k,d^{\textnormal{L}}(\mathscr{C})\leq 2t\}.
We have

\frac{(\textbf{v}_{p}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)-1)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k-1}_{p}^{2}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-2}{k-2}_{p}(\textbf{v}_{p}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)-p)+\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k-1}_{p}(p-1)}\leq|\mathscr{F}|\leq(\textbf{v}_{p}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)-1)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k-1}_{p}.

Proof.

We consider the bipartite graph
\mathscr{B}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{W},\mathscr{E})
, where
\mathscr{V}=\mathbf{B}_{p}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t,0)\setminus\{0\}
,
\mathscr{W}=\{\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}:\dim(\mathscr{C})=k\}
and

\mathscr{E}=\{(c,\mathscr{C})\in\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{W}:c\in\mathscr{C}\}
.
We have

\displaystyle|\mathscr{V}|=\textbf{v}_{p}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)-1,\quad|\mathscr{W}|=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{p}.

It is easy to see that
\mathscr{B}
is left-regular of degree

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k-1}_{p}.

Applying Proposition
4.1
we obtain an upper bound on
|\mathscr{F}|
.

To prove the lower bound, we consider the association

\alpha:\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}\to\{0,1\},\quad(V,V^{\prime})\mapsto 2-\dim\langle V,V^{\prime}\rangle.

Here we obtain

\displaystyle|\alpha^{-1}(0)|=|\mathscr{V}|(|\mathscr{V}|-(p-1)),\quad|\alpha^{-1}(1)|=|\mathscr{V}|(p-1)

and
\mathscr{B}
is
\alpha
-regular. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}_{0}(\alpha)=\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\

As an immediate consequence we obtain the following bounds on the density function.

.

1\leq k\leq n
and

(4.3)

\displaystyle 1-\frac{(\textbf{v}_{p}^{\textnormal{L}}(n,2t)-1)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k-1}_{p}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{p}}\leq\delta_{p}^{L}[\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},p^{k},2t+1]

where

From Corollary

.

be an integer.

.

Hence, when fixing
n,k
and

1
as
p
tends to infinity.
From the corollary above we can conclude, that most of the codes will have a large distance.

However, codes that attain the bound in Theorem

are sparse as

using our more general technique.
The Plotkin bound is in the classical case a much looser bound than the Singleton bound. Its optimal linear codes are constant weight codes, while their nonlinear counterparts are called equidistant codes. They find several applications (e.g.

5
Concluding Remarks

From computational results in Python for small parameters, i.e.,
m=4,5,t\leq 3
and
n\leq 20
, we observe in all cases that the upper bound on the number of
t
-Lee-error-correcting codes obtained from the graph container is sharper than the upper bound obtained from the bipartite graph.
However, proving the stronger bound
2^{O(A_{m}(n,2t+1))}
whenever
2t+1<(1-m^{-1}-c)n
, for any fixed
0<c
is much harder because one has to show that sets of size much larger than
A_{m}(n,2t+1)
must have many pairs of elements with distance at most
2t+1
. In the context of
H
-free graphs this stronger bound is a well-known conjecture of Kleitman and Winston in
[
27
]
, where it served as a motivation for the development of the container algorithm.
We think that this algorithm is still very interesting for studying the number of codes that attain a certain bound. Indeed, one could extend and transform these results to other bounds, i.e., the Singleton- or Plotkin bound and use then these bounds as a proxy for the size of the code when looking for saturation estimates that can be used in the container algorithm. However, this would go beyond the scope of this article and we were initially interested in the characterization of the intersection of two balls with equal Lee radii and distance
\ell
between their respective centers. Since our upper bounds on the size of the intersection of two Lee metric balls only depend on the Lee volume for which explicit formulae are known, and hence can be computed fast, we suggest these results for considering covering properties of Lee metric codes.

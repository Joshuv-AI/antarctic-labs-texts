On some freeness-type properties for line arrangements
Takuro Abe, Denis Ibadula, Anca Măcinic

Abstract
. By way of Ziegler restrictions we study the relation between nearly free plane arrangements and combinatorics and we give a Yoshinaga-type criterion for plus-one generated plane arrangements.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32S22, 52C35

1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be a field and
V
a
3
-dimensional vector space over
\Bbbk
. Choose a basis
\{x,y,z\}
for the dual vector space
V^{*}
so as to describe the coordinate ring
S:=\Bbbk[x,y,z]
.
Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be a non-empty central arrangement of planes in
V
, see Section §
2
for details.
We will identify
{\pazocal{A}}
, whenever convenient, to the canonically associated arrangement of lines in the corresponding projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}={\rm Proj}(S)
. Let
\alpha_{H}\in V^{*}
be such that
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
is defined by
\alpha_{H}=0
.
We are mainly interested here in arrangements of planes. Consequently, we state only in this particular context, i.e. only for arrangements of planes, some notions and results that hold also in higher dimensions. We refer to
[
15
]
for a comprehensive presentation of the theory of arrangements of hyperplanes in general.

Define

D({\pazocal{A}})=\{\theta\in\Der S\;|\;\theta(\alpha_{H})\in S\alpha_{H},\;\forall H\in{\pazocal{A}}\}

the logarithmic derivation module of the arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
, a graded submodule of the
S
-module
\Der S=S\partial_{x}\oplus S\partial_{y}\oplus S\partial_{z}
of derivations of
S
.
D({\pazocal{A}})
is not generally a free
S
-module. When it is free, the arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}

is called
free
and the ordered sequence of degrees of a basis of
D({\pazocal{A}})
constitutes the
exponents
of
{\pazocal{A}}
, denoted
\exp({\pazocal{A}})
. Since the Euler derivation,
\theta_{E}=x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}+z\partial_{z}
, is always an element in
D({\pazocal{A}})
, the sequence of exponents always starts with
1
. We will omit from now on, unless explicitly stated, the first exponent. So, by a free arrangement of exponents
(a,b)
one actually means a free arrangement of exponents
(1,a,b)_{\leq}
.

We need to recall the definition of a notion that turned out to be important in the study of freeness,
multiarrangements
. Multiarrangements are, simply put, arrangements with each hyperplane labelled by a positive integer. More precisely,
a multiarrangement is a pair consisting of an arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
and a map
m:{\pazocal{A}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, called
multiplicity
.
The Ziegler restrictions defined in
1.1
are the examples of multiarrangements that we will work with.

For
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
, let

{\pazocal{A}}^{H}=\{H\cap K\;|\;K\in{\pazocal{A}}\setminus\{H\}\}

be the arrangement induced by
{\pazocal{A}}
on
H
. One has a multiarrangement structure on
{\pazocal{A}}^{H}
, called the
Ziegler restriction
(
[
19
]
), defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
m^{H}:{\pazocal{A}}^{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{>0}
be defined by the correspondence:

X\mapsto\#\{K\in{\pazocal{A}}\;|\;X\subset K\}-1.

We call the pair
({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
the Ziegler restriction of
{\pazocal{A}}
onto
H
.

The derivation module associated to the multiarrangement
({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is defined by

D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})=\{\theta\in Der\overline{S}\;|\;\ \theta(\alpha_{K})\in\overline{S}(\alpha_{K})^{m^{H}(K)},\;\forall K\in{\pazocal{A}}^{H}\},

where
\alpha_{K}\in\overline{S}
is the linear form that defines
K
and
\overline{S}=S/(\alpha_{H})
.

If
{\pazocal{A}}
is an arrangement in a
3
-dimensional vector space, then
D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is always free, of rank
2
. For
\{\theta_{1}^{H},\theta_{2}^{H}\}
homogeneous basis in
D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
, we call the set
(\deg(\theta_{1}^{H}),\deg(\theta_{2}^{H}))
the exponents of
({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
, denoted by
\exp({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
.

Working with Ziegler restrictions plays an important role in proving the main results of this paper.

Let
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
be defined by the linear form
\alpha_{H}
and

D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})=\{\theta\in D({\pazocal{A}})\;|\;\theta(\alpha_{H})=0\}.

Define the
Ziegler restriction map
:

\pi:D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})\rightarrow D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})

by taking
modulo

\alpha_{H}
, see
[
19
]
for details.

Hence we have the exact sequence:

0\rightarrow D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})\overset{\cdot\alpha_{H}}{\rightarrow}D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})

We recall a couple of important results due to Ziegler and Yoshinaga that link freeness and Ziegler restrictions.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Ziegler,
[
19
]
)
Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be free of exponents
(a,b)
. Then, for any
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
, the Ziegler restriction
({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is also free of exponents
(a,b)
and the Ziegler restriction map is surjective.

The converse result was proved by Yoshinaga.
Let

\chi_{0}({\pazocal{A}},t)=\chi({\pazocal{A}},t)/(t-1),

where
\chi({\pazocal{A}},t)
is the characteristic polynomial of the arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
, see
[
15
]
. Then

\chi_{0}({\pazocal{A}},t)=t^{2}-(|{\pazocal{A}}|-1)t+b_{2}^{0}({\pazocal{A}}),

and, when
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}
, we have that
b_{2}^{0}({\pazocal{A}})=b_{2}({\pazocal{A}})-(|{\pazocal{A}}|-1)
, where
b_{2}({\pazocal{A}})
is the second Betti number of the complement of the arrangement.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Yoshinaga’s criterion,
[
17
]
)

Let
({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
be the Ziegler restriction of
{\pazocal{A}}
onto
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
with
\exp({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(e_{1},e_{2})
. Then
b_{2}^{0}({\pazocal{A}})-e_{1}e_{2}=\dim_{\Bbbk}\Coker(\pi)<\infty
and
b_{2}^{0}({\pazocal{A}})-e_{1}e_{2}=0
if and only if
{\pazocal{A}}
is free with
\exp({\pazocal{A}})=(e_{1},e_{2})
.

The freeness property for arrangements is intensively studied from algebraic, geometric and combinatorial perspectives. A long standing conjecture in the field of hyperplane arrangements, concerning free arrangements, is the
Terao conjecture
, which states that whether an arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
is free or not depends only on its combinatorics.

We turn our attention in this paper to some recently introduced (weaker) notions of freeness:
nearly free
arrangements of lines in the projective plane, that were defined by Dimca and Sticlaru in
[
12
]
, and the
plus-one generated
arrangements introduced by the first author in
[
3
]
, see Section §
2
for the definitions.

In Section §
3
, we mostly look at nearly free arrangements through combinatorial perspective. We prove that, under certain restrictions, freeness and near freeness depend on the combinatorics.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be a plane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Let
\chi_{0}({\pazocal{A}},t)=(t-n)(t-n-r)+1
with
n,r\geq 0
. If
r\geq n>0
, then whether
{\pazocal{A}}
is free or nearly free depends only on
L({\pazocal{A}})
.

Consequently, if there is enough distance between the exponents, a nearly free version of the Terao conjecture holds. We apply this in Corollary
3.3
to show that if
{\pazocal{A}}
is a nearly free arrangement of exponents
(a,b)_{\leq}
(see Definition
2.1
) and
a\leq\frac{|{\pazocal{A}}|-1}{3}
, then all other arrangements in the lattice isomorphism class of
{\pazocal{A}}
are nearly free.

In Theorem
3.14
we show that a nearly free version of the Terao conjecture holds when limiting the values of the exponents. More precisely we prove that if
{\pazocal{A}}
is a nearly free arrangement of exponents
(a,b)
such that at least one of the exponents is most
5
, then all other arrangements in the lattice isomorphism class of
{\pazocal{A}}
are nearly free.

A Yoshinaga-type criterion for near freeness is given by the first author and Dimca in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

(
[
5
]
)

Let
\chi_{0}({\pazocal{A}},t)=t^{2}-(|{\pazocal{A}}|-1)t+b_{2}^{0}({\pazocal{A}})
. Then
{\pazocal{A}}
is nearly free if and only if there exists
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
b_{2}^{0}({\pazocal{A}})-e_{1}e_{2}=1
, where
(e_{1},e_{2})
is the splitting type onto
H
for the bundle of logarithmic vector fields associated to
{\pazocal{A}}
.

On the same note, we give in Theorem
1.6
a similar criterion for an arrangement to be plus-one generated. To state it we need to add a bit to the set-up.

Consider the following property:

[P]
Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be an arrangement for which there exists
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
such that one of the conditions below holds.

(1)

There exists
\alpha\in S
non-zero linear form such that
\langle\theta_{1}^{H},\theta_{2}^{H}\rangle\supsetneq Im(\pi)\supset\langle\theta_{1}^{H},\alpha\theta_{2}^{H}\rangle

(2)

There exists
\beta\in S
non-zero linear form such that
\langle\theta_{1}^{H},\theta_{2}^{H}\rangle\supsetneq Im(\pi)\supset\langle\theta_{2}^{H},\beta\theta_{1}^{H}\rangle

where
\{\theta_{1}^{H},\theta_{2}^{H}\}
is a basis in
D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H}),\;\pi
is the Ziegler restriction map and
\langle X\rangle
denotes the
S
-module generated by a set
X
.

Theorem 1.6
.

{\pazocal{A}}
satisfies property
[P]
if and only if it is plus-one generated.

The last section is generally devoted to results on plus-one generated arrangements. We show in Theorem
4.4
that the possible splitting types onto any projective line for the bundle of logarithmic vector fields associated to the arrangement are determined by the exponents.
See
[
5
,
14
]
for similar results on nearly free arrangements: in
[
5
]
the authors describe the possible splitting types for the vector field associated to nearly free curves, and the result is recovered by Marchesi-Vallès in
[
14
]
, who moreover give a geometric characterization of the set of jumping lines.
In Corollary
4.5
we prove that the exponents of a plus-one generated arrangement put strong restrictions on the exponents of its Ziegler restrictions. Along the way we generalize some other results known to hold for nearly free arrangements.

2.
Preliminaries

We will present in more detail some of the notions we will work with in the next chapters.

An arrangement of hyperplanes
{\pazocal{A}}
in a vector space
V
over a field
\Bbbk
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in
V
.
{\pazocal{A}}
is called
central
if
\cap_{H\in{\pazocal{A}}}H\neq\emptyset
.
Recall that we work with
{\pazocal{A}}\subset V=\Bbbk^{3}
a central non-empty arrangement and
S:=\Bbbk[x,y,z]
. We present here the definition of plus-one generated arrangements in
V=\Bbbk^{3}
(see
[
3
]
for the general definition).

Definition 2.1
.

The arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
is plus-one generated with exponents
(a,b)
and level
d
if there is a minimal set of homogeneous generators
\{\theta_{1}=\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\phi\}
for
D({\pazocal{A}})
with
\deg(\theta_{2})=a,\;\deg(\theta_{3})=b,\deg(\phi)=d
, and a unique homogeneous relation

f_{1}\theta_{E}+f_{2}\theta_{2}+f_{3}\theta_{3}+\alpha\phi=0

where
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},\alpha\in S,\;\deg(\alpha)=1
.

If moreover
b=d
in the previous definition, one gets a
nearly free
arrangement.

It is well known that, provided that
{\pazocal{A}}\neq\emptyset
, there is an isomorphism

D({\pazocal{A}})\cong S\theta_{E}\oplus D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})

and as a consequence the relation among the generators of the derivation module from the definition of the plus-one generated arrangement can be assumed to take place in
D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})
.

Plus-one generated arrangements are defined by taking one step further from the algebraic definition of free arrangements. As it turns out, they are quite literally next to free arrangements, as any free arrangement produces by deletion or addition either a free or a plus-one generated arrangement, see
[
3
]
. So, results that hold for free arrangements provide legitimate questions to explore for plus-one generated arrangements, and in particular for nearly free ones, as we shall see in the next chapters.

One of them concerns the splitting type of the bundle of logarithmic vector fields associated to the arrangement. The set-up is the following.

Say
{\pazocal{A}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
is defined as the zero set of the homogeneous polynomial
f^{{\pazocal{A}}}\in S=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
. Let
f^{{\pazocal{A}}}_{x},f^{{\pazocal{A}}}_{y},f^{{\pazocal{A}}}_{z}
be the partial derivatives of
f^{{\pazocal{A}}}
with respect to
x,y,z
. Consider the graded S-module of Jacobian syzygyes of
f^{{\pazocal{A}}},\;AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})
, defined by

AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})=\{(a,b,c)\in S^{3}\;|\;af^{{\pazocal{A}}}_{x}+bf^{{\pazocal{A}}}_{y}+cf^{{\pazocal{A}}}_{z}=0\},

connected by a natural isomorphism

(a,b,c)\mapsto a\partial_{x}+b\partial_{y}+c\partial_{z}

to the module of logarithmic derivations of the arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
that send
f^{{\pazocal{A}}}
to zero,

D_{0}({\pazocal{A}})=\{\theta\in\Der S\;|\;\theta(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})=0\}.

Moreover, there is a well known isomorphism

D_{0}({\pazocal{A}})\cong D_{H}({\pazocal{A}}),

for any
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
, defined by

\theta\mapsto\theta-(\theta(\alpha_{H})/\alpha_{H})\theta_{E},

see for instance
[
5
, Proposition 2.10]
for details.

Then the freeness of
{\pazocal{A}}
is immediately equivalent to the freeness of the S-module
AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})
.

Let
\widetilde{AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})}
be the associated sheaf of
AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})
over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Consider the graded S-module of sections associated to a sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2},

\Gamma_{*}({\mathcal{F}})=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{2},{\mathcal{F}}(k)).

\widetilde{AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})}
is a locally free sheaf, i.e. a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. By
[
7
]
, the natural homomorphism
AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})\rightarrow\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})})
, that sends
x\mapsto\frac{x}{1}
,
is in fact an isomorphism, hence we can assume the identification
\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})})=AR(f^{{\pazocal{A}}})
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
a line, we know that the restriction of
{\mathcal{E}}
to
L,\;{\mathcal{E}}|_{L}
, which is a vector bundle on
L\equiv\mathbb{P}^{1}
, always splits (by Grothendieck’s splitting theorem), say
{\mathcal{E}}|_{L}={\pazocal O}_{L}(-e_{1})\oplus{\pazocal O}_{L}(-e_{2})
. The pair of (non-negative) integers
(e_{1},e_{2})
is called
the splitting type
of
{\mathcal{E}}
along the line
L
.

Finally, let us recall a result of Yoshinaga on the splitting type of a vector bundle of rank
2
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
along a line.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
18
]
)

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
a line. Let
{\mathcal{E}}|_{L}={\pazocal O}_{L}(-e_{1})\oplus{\pazocal O}_{L}(-e_{2})
. Then
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})\geq e_{1}e_{2}
, furthermore

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})-e_{1}e_{2}=dim_{\Bbbk}Coker(\Gamma_{*}({\mathcal{E}})\overset{\pi_{L}}{\longrightarrow}(\Gamma_{*}({\mathcal{E}}|_{L}))

where
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})
is the second Chern number of
{\mathcal{E}}
and
\pi_{L}
is the morphism of graded modules induced by the restriction to
L
of the vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{E}}
is splitting if and only if
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=e_{1}e_{2}
.

A remarkable fact is that for the vector bundle
\widetilde{AR(f_{{\pazocal{A}}})}
, to determine the splitting type onto the lines in
{\pazocal{A}}
means to determine the exponents of the corresponding Ziegler restrictions, see
[
17
]
. Refer to

[
5
]
for a comprehensive analysis on the splitting types of bundles of logarithmic vector fields associated to reduced plane curves in general.
In particular, notice that the above result implies Theorem
1.3
.

The splitting type onto arbitrary projective lines, in particular for the lines in
{\pazocal{A}}
, for the vector bundle
\widetilde{AR(f_{{\pazocal{A}}})}
when
{\pazocal{A}}
is a nearly free arrangement, is described in
[
5
, Corollary 3.4, Theorem 5.3]
, see also
[
14
, Proposition 2.4]
.
Our Theorem
4.4
and Corollary
4.5
extend these results to plus-one generated arrangements.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
5
]
)Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be nearly free with exponents
(a,b)
. Then, for any
H\in{\pazocal{A}},\;\exp({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(a-1,b)
or
(b-1,a)
.

Let us now turn our attention to the combinatorics of line arrangements and recall some basic notions and results that we will use throughout the next sections.
For an arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
, let
L({\pazocal{A}})=\{\cap_{H\in\mathcal{B}}H\;|\;\mathcal{B}\subset{\pazocal{A}}\}
be the
intersection lattice
of
{\pazocal{A}}
, endowed with a poset structure with partial ordering given by reverse inclusion. An invariant associated to
{\pazocal{A}}
or a property of
{\pazocal{A}}
is
combinatorial
, or
combinatorially determined
, if it depends solely on the intersection lattice of
{\pazocal{A}}
.

For any
X\in L({\pazocal{A}})
, let
{\pazocal{A}}_{X}=\{H\in{\pazocal{A}}\;|\;X\subseteq H\}
. If
X\in L({\pazocal{A}})
is of codimension
2
, then set
m(X)=|{\pazocal{A}}_{X}|
. When
{\pazocal{A}}
is identified to an arrangement of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then
X
is just a point in the projective plane, and we call
m(X)
the
multiplicity
of the point
X
. Denote by
m({\pazocal{A}})
the maximal multiplicity over all multiple points of
{\pazocal{A}}
.

For
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
, denote
n_{H}:=|{\pazocal{A}}^{H}|
. Notice that if one looks at
{\pazocal{A}}
as an arrangement of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then
n_{H}
is just the number of intersection points on the line
H
.
We already know that if
{\pazocal{A}}
is a free arrangement, there are restrictions on the values of
n_{H}
in terms of the exponents of
{\pazocal{A}}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
2
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be a free arrangement with
\chi_{0}({\pazocal{A}},t)=(t-n)(t-n-r)
with
n,r\geq 0
. Then:

(1)

If
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
then
n_{H}\leq n+1
or
n_{H}=n+r+1
.

(2)

If
L\notin{\pazocal{A}}
, let
{\pazocal{B}}={\pazocal{A}}\cup\{L\}
. Then
|{\pazocal{B}}^{L}|=n+1
or
|{\pazocal{B}}^{L}|\geq n+r+1
.

We will see in the next section that similar types of weak combinatorics restrictions are imposed by near freeness.
In
[
5
]
sufficient conditions for near freeness are formulated in terms of weak combinatorics:

Theorem 2.5
.

(
[
5
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be an arrangement with
\chi_{0}({\pazocal{A}},t)=(t-a)(t-b)+1
with
a,b
real numbers and
a\leq b
. Then
{\pazocal{A}}
is nearly free if there is
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
such that one of the following holds:

(1)

n_{H}=b+1

(2)

n_{H}=a+1
and
b\neq a+2
.

Conditions on the weak combinatorics of an arrangement can determine the exponents of the Ziegler restrictions. This leads to partial positive answers for the Terao and near Terao conjectures.

Proposition 2.6
.

(
[
16
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be an arrangement and let
({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
be the Ziegler restriction of
{\pazocal{A}}
onto
H
, for some
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
.

(1)

If
n_{H}\geq(|{\pazocal{A}}|+1)/2
then
\exp({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(|{\pazocal{A}}|-n_{H},n_{H}-1)
.

(2)

If there is
X\in{\pazocal{A}}^{H}
with
m:=m^{H}(X)\geq(|{\pazocal{A}}|-1)/2
then
\exp({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(m,|{\pazocal{A}}|-1-m)
.

It is immediate that in the hypothesis from Proposition
2.6
(2), Theorem
1.3
implies at once that the Terao conjecture holds. It turns out that under the same hypothesis, the near Terao conjecture holds as well.

Proposition 2.7
.

(
[
5
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be an arrangement such that for some
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
there is
X\in{\pazocal{A}}^{H}
with
m^{H}(X)\geq(|{\pazocal{A}}|-1)/2
. Then the near freeness of
{\pazocal{A}}
depends only on
L({\pazocal{A}})
.

A multiplicity
m:{\pazocal{A}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is called
balanced
if
m(H)\leq(|{\pazocal{A}}|-1)/2
for all
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
, and
non-balanced
otherwise. An arrangement is called
balanced
if all its Ziegler restrictions are balanced and
non-balanced
otherwise.
Thus Proposition
2.7
states that near freeness depends only on the intersection lattice for non-balanced arrangements and Proposition
2.6
(2) implies the same for freeness, via the computation of the exponents for a Ziegler restriction.

One has an instrument to handle the exponents of the Ziegler restrictions, even in the case of balanced arrangements:

Theorem 2.8
.

(
[
1
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be an arrangement in a
2
-dimensional vector space over a field of characteristic zero with
|{\pazocal{A}}|>2
. If
m:{\pazocal{A}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is balanced and
\exp({\pazocal{A}},m)=(e_{1},e_{2})
, then
|e_{1}-e_{2}|\leq|{\pazocal{A}}|-2
.

For future references, we need to recall a result on the behaviour of near freeness to addition-deletion techniques.

Theorem 2.9
.

(
[
5
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be an arrangement,
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
and
\mathcal{B}:={\pazocal{A}}\setminus\{H\}
. Let
a\leq b
be two non-negative integers. Then, any two of the following imply the third:

(1)

{\pazocal{A}}
is nearly free with exponents
(a+1,b+1)
.

(2)

\mathcal{B}

.

(3)

|{\pazocal{A}}^{H}|=b+2
.

3.
Nearly free line arrangements and combinatorics

Throughout this section, unless otherwise stated, we will assume that
{\pazocal{A}}
is a central arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\chi_{0}({\pazocal{A}},t)=(t-n)(t-n-r)+1
with
n,r\geq 0
. If

4.1.
A Yoshinaga-type criterion

Let
{\pazocal{A}}\ni H
be an arrangement in
V=\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\{\theta_{1}^{H},\theta_{2}^{H}\}
a basis for
D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
. We may assume without loss of generality that
\alpha_{H}=z
. Then taking
modulo

\alpha_{H}
, for the Ziegler restriction map, sends
S=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
to
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
.

Corollary
4.5
lists the possible values for the exponents of the Ziegler restrictions for plus-one generated arrangements. Among them, if
b<d
, there is at most one Ziegler restriction with exponents
(a+b-d-1,d)
, as we can see in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be plus-one generated with exponents
(a,b)
and level
d
with
a\leq b<d
. If
H,L\in{\pazocal{A}}
are such that
exp({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})=exp({\pazocal{A}}^{L},m^{L})=(a+b-d-1,d)
, then
H=L
.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
be such that
exp({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(a+b-d-1,d)
and let
\pi=\pi_{H}:D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})\rightarrow D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
be the Ziegler restriction of
{\pazocal{A}}
onto
H
. Then
D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is generated by a set of derivations
\{\theta_{1}^{H},\theta_{2}^{H}\}
with
\deg(\theta_{1}^{H})=a+b-d-1
and
\deg(\theta_{2}^{H})=d
.
In the notations from Definition
2.1
with the subsequent remarks,
\{\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\phi\}
is a minimal set of generators for
D_{H}({\pazocal{A}}),\;\deg(\theta_{2})=a,\;\deg(\theta_{3})=b,\;\deg(\phi)=d
. Since
a\leq b<d,\;\pi(\theta_{2}),\pi(\theta_{3})
are in the submodule of
D({\pazocal{A}}^{H},m^{H})
generated by
\theta_{1}^{H}
. Let
\pi(\theta_{2})=f\theta_{1}^{H}
and
\pi(\theta_{3})=g\theta_{1}^{H},\;f,g\in S
. Then
\pi(g\theta_{2}-f\theta_{1})\in\Ker(\pi)
, hence the homogeneous relation of degree
d+1

g\theta_{2}-f\theta_{3}-\alpha_{H}\theta=0,

for some derivation
\theta\in D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})
.
Assume
L\in{\pazocal{A}}
is such that
exp({\pazocal{A}}^{L},m^{L})=(a+b-d-1,d)
. As before we get a homogeneous relation of degree
d+1

g^{\prime}\theta_{2}-f^{\prime}\theta_{3}-\alpha_{L}\theta^{\prime}=0,

for some derivation
\theta^{\prime}\in D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})
.
Since
b<d
, in the unique generating syzygy from Definition
2.1
, in the notations established there,
\alpha\neq 0
and both
\alpha
and
\phi
are uniquely determined (modulo multiplication by an element in
\Bbbk
). Just express
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
in the previous relations in terms of the generators of
D_{H}({\pazocal{A}})
and recall that both relations are homogeneous of degree

to arrive at the conclusion that
\alpha_{L}
and
\alpha_{H}
are linearly dependent, hence

.
∎

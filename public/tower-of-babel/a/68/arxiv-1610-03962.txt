A simple proof of Feuerbach’s Theorem
Franz Hofbauer

Abstract
. The theorem of Feuerbach states that the nine-point circle of a nonequilateral triangle is tangent to both its incircle and its three excircles. We give a simple proof of this theorem.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
51M04, 97G70

Let
\triangle ABC
be a triangle with circumradius
R
and sidelengths
a
,
b
and
c
.
We choose a coordinate system, which has its origin in the circumcenter
O
of the triangle.
We denote the vectors from
O
to the vertices
A
,
B
and
C
by
\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
respectively.
Then we have
a=\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\|
,
b=\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}\|
,
c=\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|
and

\|\mathbf{u}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{w}\|=R
.
The semiperimeter
\frac{1}{2}(a+b+c)
is denoted by
s
.

Let
N
be the nine-point center and
H
the orthocenter of the triangle.
Set
\mathbf{n}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}
. Then we have

\|\mathbf{n}-(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v})\|=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|=\frac{1}{2}R
. Similarly we get

\|\mathbf{n}-(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w})\|=\frac{1}{2}R
and
\|\mathbf{n}-(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w})\|=\frac{1}{2}R
.
Therefore
\mathbf{n}
is the vector from
O
to
N
und
\frac{1}{2}R
is the nine-point radius.

Let
I
be the incenter and
I_{c}
the excenter opposite
C
.
Set
\mathbf{m}=\frac{a}{2s}\mathbf{u}+\frac{b}{2s}\mathbf{v}+\frac{c}{2s}\mathbf{w}
.
The equations of the angle bisectors through
A
and
B
are

\mathbf{u}+\lambda(\tfrac{1}{c}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u})+\tfrac{1}{b}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{u}))\ \ \ \text{ und }\ \ \ \mathbf{v}+\mu(\tfrac{1}{c}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})+\tfrac{1}{a}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}))

Choosing
\lambda=\frac{bc}{2s}
and
\mu=\frac{ac}{2s}
we get
\mathbf{m}
in both cases.
This shows that
\mathbf{m}
is the vector from
O
to the incenter
I
.

Set
\mathbf{m}_{c}=\frac{a}{2(s-c)}\mathbf{u}+\frac{b}{2(s-c)}\mathbf{v}-\frac{c}{2(s-c)}\mathbf{w}
.
The equations of the external angle bisectors through
A
and
B
are

\mathbf{u}+\lambda(\tfrac{1}{c}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u})-\tfrac{1}{b}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{u}))\ \ \ \text{ und }\ \ \ \mathbf{v}+\mu(\tfrac{1}{c}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})-\tfrac{1}{a}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}))

Choosing
\lambda=\frac{bc}{2(s-c)}
and
\mu=\frac{ac}{2(s-c)}
we get
\mathbf{m}_{c}

in both cases.
This shows that
\mathbf{m}_{c}
is the vector from
O
to the excenter
I_{c}
.

We use the following lemma for the computation of distances.

Lemma
.

For any
d_{a}
,
d_{b}
and
d_{c}
in
\mathbb{R}
we have

\|d_{a}\mathbf{u}+d_{b}\mathbf{v}+d_{c}\mathbf{w}\|^{2}=R^{2}(d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c})^{2}-(a^{2}d_{b}d_{c}+b^{2}d_{a}d_{c}+c^{2}d_{a}d_{b})

Proof.

We have

c^{2}=\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^{2}=\langle\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v},\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\rangle=\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}+\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}-2\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle
.
Since we have also
\|\mathbf{u}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|=R
we get
\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle=R^{2}-\frac{1}{2}c^{2}
.
Similarly we get
\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}\rangle=R^{2}-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}
und
\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\rangle=R^{2}-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}
.
Using this and
\|\mathbf{u}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{w}\|=R
we compute

\displaystyle\|d_{a}\mathbf{u}+

\displaystyle d_{b}\mathbf{v}+d_{c}\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=d_{a}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}+d_{b}^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}+d_{c}^{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+2d_{a}d_{b}\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle+2d_{a}d_{c}\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}\rangle+2d_{b}d_{c}\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=R^{2}(d_{a}^{2}+d_{b}^{2}+d_{c}^{2}+2d_{a}d_{b}+2d_{a}d_{c}+2d_{b}d_{c})-c^{2}d_{a}d_{b}-b^{2}d_{a}d_{c}-a^{2}d_{b}d_{c}

This is the desired result.
∎

We use Heron’s formula
\Delta^{2}=s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)
for the area
\Delta
of the triangle.
This can also be written as
16\Delta^{2}=2a^{2}b^{2}+2a^{2}c^{2}+2b^{2}c^{2}-a^{4}-b^{4}-c^{4}
.
Furthermore, we use the formulas
r=\frac{\Delta}{s}
,
r_{c}=\frac{\Delta}{s-c}
and
R=\frac{abc}{4\Delta}

for the inradius
r
, the exradius
r_{c}
and the circumradius
R
.
In particular we have
rR=\frac{abc}{4s}
and
r_{c}R=\frac{abc}{4(s-c)}
.

Theorem 1
.

(Euler)
|OI|^{2}=R^{2}-2Rr

Proof.

We have
\overrightarrow{OI}=\mathbf{m}=d_{a}\mathbf{u}+d_{b}\mathbf{v}+d_{c}\mathbf{w}
with

d_{a}=\frac{a}{2s}
,
d_{b}=\frac{b}{2s}
and
d_{c}=\frac{c}{2s}
.
We get
d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c}=1
and
a^{2}d_{b}d_{c}+b^{2}d_{a}d_{c}+c^{2}d_{a}d_{b}=\frac{abc}{4s^{2}}(a+b+c)=\frac{abc}{2s}=2rR
.
The Lemma gives the result.
∎

Theorem 2
.

(Feuerbach)
|IN|=\frac{1}{2}R-r

Proof.

We have
\overrightarrow{IN}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}=d_{a}\mathbf{u}+d_{b}\mathbf{v}+d_{c}\mathbf{w}
with

d_{a}=\frac{s-a}{2s}
,
d_{b}=\frac{s-b}{2s}
and
d_{c}=\frac{s-c}{2s}
.
We get
d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c}=\frac{1}{2}
and

\displaystyle a^{2}d_{b}d_{c}

\displaystyle+b^{2}d_{a}d_{c}+c^{2}d_{a}d_{b}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{16s^{2}}(a^{2}(a^{2}-(b-c)^{2})+b^{2}(b^{2}-(a-c)^{2})+c^{2}(c^{2}-(a-b)^{2}))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{16s^{2}}(a^{4}+b^{4}+c^{4}-2a^{2}b^{2}-2a^{2}c^{2}-2b^{2}c^{2}+2abc(a+b+c))

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\Delta^{2}}{s^{2}}+\tfrac{abc}{4s}=-r^{2}+rR

The Lemma implies
|IN|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}R^{2}+r^{2}-rR=(\frac{1}{2}R-r)^{2}
. By Theorem
1
we have

\frac{1}{2}R\geq r
. Hence
|IN|=\frac{1}{2}R-r
follows.
∎

Theorem 3
.

(Feuerbach)
|I_{c}N|=\frac{1}{2}R+r_{c}

Proof.

We have
\overrightarrow{I_{c}N}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}_{c}=d_{a}\mathbf{u}+d_{b}\mathbf{v}+d_{c}\mathbf{w}
with

d_{a}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{a}{2(s-c)}=-\frac{s-b}{2(s-c)}
,
d_{b}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{b}{2(s-c)}=-\frac{s-a}{2(s-c)}

and
d_{c}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{c}{2(s-c)}=\frac{s}{2(s-c)}
.
We get
d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c}=\frac{1}{2}
and

\displaystyle a^{2}d_{b}d_{c}

\displaystyle+b^{2}d_{a}d_{c}+c^{2}d_{a}d_{b}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{16(s-c)^{2}}(a^{2}(a^{2}-(b+c)^{2})+b^{2}(b^{2}-(a+c)^{2})+c^{2}(c^{2}-(a-b)^{2}))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{16(s-c)^{2}}(a^{4}+b^{4}+c^{4}-2a^{2}b^{2}-2a^{2}c^{2}-2b^{2}c^{2}-2abc(a+b-c))

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\Delta^{2}}{(s-c)^{2}}-\tfrac{abc}{4(s-c)}=-r_{c}^{2}-r_{c}R

The Lemma implies
|I_{c}N|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}R^{2}+r_{c}^{2}+r_{c}R=(\frac{1}{2}R+r_{c})^{2}
. We get
|I_{c}N|=\frac{1}{2}R+r_{c}
.
∎

Theorem
2
implies that the nine-point circle is tangent to the incircle and
Theorem
3
implies that the nine-point circle is tangent to the excircle
opposite
C
.

We get also

Theorem 4
.

|OH|^{2}=9R^{2}-(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})

Proof.

Since
N
is the midpoint between
O
and
H
, we get that

\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w}
is the vector from
O
to
H
.
Hence the desired result follows from the Lemma with
d_{a}=d_{b}=d_{c}=1
.
∎

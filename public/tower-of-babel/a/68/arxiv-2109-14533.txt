Abstract
This is the third part in a series on a mass conserving, high order, mixed finite element method for Stokes flow. In this part, we study a block-diagonal preconditioner for the indefinite Schur complement system arising from the discretization of the Stokes equations using these elements. The underlying finite element method is uniformly stable in both the mesh size h h and polynomial order p p , and we prove bounds on the eigenvalues of the preconditioned system which are independent of h h and grow modestly in p p . The analysis relates the Schur complement system to an appropriate variational setting with subspaces for which exact sequence properties and inf-sup stability hold. Several numerical examples demonstrate agreement with the theoretical results.

By fixing bases for the spaces

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}\bm{A}_{EE}&\bm{B}_{Ee}&\bm{A}_{EI}&\bm{B}_{E\iota}\\
\bm{B}_{eE}&\bm{0}&\bm{B}_{eI}&\bm{0}\\

\vec{p}_{e}\\
\hline\cr\vec{u}_{I}\\

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c}\vec{f}_{E}\\
\vec{0}\\
\hline\cr\vec{f}_{I}\\
\vec{0}\end{array}\end{bmatrix}.

(4)

Interior Subspaces

We first examine the subspaces associated with the interior degrees of freedom given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{I}

\displaystyle=\{\phi\in\Sigma_{0}:\phi|_{\partial K}=\partial_{n}\phi|_{\partial K}=0,\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\}

\displaystyle:=\left\{q\in\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(K)\cap L^{2}_{0}(K):q(\bm{a})=0,\ \forall\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}\right\}.

\displaystyle 0\xrightarrow{\ \ \ \subset\ \ \ }\Sigma_{I}(K)\xrightarrow{\ \ \vcurl\ \ }\bm{V}_{I}(K)\xrightarrow{\ \ \dive\ \ }Q_{I}(K)\xrightarrow{\hskip 16.38895pt}0,\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T},

Proof.

In view of

Velocity Basis Functions

\displaystyle\vec{J}_{1}:=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{P_{k-3}^{(3,3)}(-1)}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{2}P_{k-3}^{(3,3)}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\\

\hat{\gamma}_{3}

\lambda_{1}^{2}

and their gradients vanish on the edge

Substituting the identities

We now define the global basis functions for the spaces

6.2.1
Pressure Basis Functions

The pressure space
Q
requires
C^{0}
continuity at all vertices except at corner vertices, where the functions are allowed to be discontinuous. This means that
each element has its own degree of freedom
at vertices
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C}
, whilst at the remaining vertices
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{C}
, all elements
share a single degree of freedom
at the common vertex as shown in
fig.

3(a)
. Consequently, any given vertex
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}
is associated with either (a)
a single
basis function supported on the patch
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}
if
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{C}

a collection
of basis functions, each of which is supported on a single element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}
if
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C}
. The set of supports of the pressure functions associated with a vertex
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\bm{a}}=\begin{cases}\{\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}\}&\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C}\\

That is, the cardinality of these sets is
|\Omega_{\bm{a}}|=1
for noncorner vertices (since there is only one vertex basis function associated to
\bm{a}
) whilst
|\Omega_{\bm{a}}|\geq 2
for corner vertices, thanks to the assumption that the mesh is corner-split into at least two elements. The corresponding global vertex functions
\{\psi_{\bm{a}}^{\omega}:\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V},\ \omega\in\Omega_{\bm{a}}\}
are defined to be pull-backs in the usual way:

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{a}}^{\omega}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\hat{\psi}_{i}\circ\bm{F}_{K}^{-1}&\text{on }K\subseteq\omega,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\psi_{K}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\hat{\psi}_{\hat{T}}\circ\bm{F}_{K}^{-1}&\text{on }K,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(42)

where
\hat{\bm{a}}_{i}=\bm{F}_{K}^{-1}(\bm{a})
.

6.2.2
Velocity Basis Functions

The velocity space
V

C^{1}
continuity at all vertices except at corner vertices, where only
C^{0}

. This means that at corner vertices
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C}

K
that meet at
\bm{a}
. To enforce continuity between two neighboring elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}
, the tangential derivative corresponding to the common edge must be
shared between the two elements
. In other words, each corner vertex
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C}
has
one derivative degree of freedom for each edge

\gamma\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{a}}
. For the remaining noncorner vertices
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{C}
, all elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}

share two degrees of freedom
at the common vertex, corresponding to any two linearly independent directional derivatives as in
fig.

3(b)

\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}
is associated with either (a)
two
basis functions supported on the patch
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}
if
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{C}
, or (b)
a collection
of basis functions, each of which is associated to an edge
\gamma\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{a}}
and supported on the pair of elements sharing the common edge
\gamma
if
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C}
.

The set of unit vectors defining the directional derivative degrees of freedom at a vertex
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}
are chosen as follows:

\displaystyle D_{\bm{a}}=\begin{cases}\{\hat{\bm{e}}_{1},\hat{\bm{e}}_{2}\}&\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{I}\\
\{\hat{\bm{t}},\hat{\bm{n}}\}&\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}\\
\{\hat{\bm{t}}_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\mathcal{E}_{\bm{a}}\}&\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C}\end{cases}

(43)

where
\hat{\bm{t}}
and
\hat{\bm{n}}
are the unit tangent and normal vectors at a noncorner boundary vertex
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}

\gamma\in\mathcal{E}

For a given vertex
\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}
and unit vector

has support

\displaystyle\supp\phi_{\bm{a}}^{\mu}=\begin{cases}\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}:\exists\gamma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\text{ with }\hat{\bm{t}}_{\gamma}=\pm\hat{\bm{\mu}}\}&\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{C},\\
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}}&\bm{a}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{C}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{\bm{a}}^{\mu_{1}}\\

\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{on }K\in\mathcal{T}_{\bm{a}},

(44)

where
\hat{\bm{a}}_{i}=\bm{F}_{K}^{-1}(\bm{a})

\bm{a}

\bm{a}

\phi_{\bm{a}}^{\mu_{1}}

\phi_{\bm{a}}^{\mu_{2}}

C^{1}

C^{0}

\phi_{\bm{a}}=0
otherwise,

\hat{\bm{a}}_{i}=\bm{F}_{K}^{-1}(\bm{a})

, supported on the patch of elements containing that edge
\{K\in\mathcal{T}:\gamma\in\mathcal{E}_{K}\}

\frac{1}{2}(k-1)(k-2)
interior functions per element
K\in\mathcal{T}

The above construction gives a basis for

where

all

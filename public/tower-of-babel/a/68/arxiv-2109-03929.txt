Independence inheritance and Diophantine approximation for systems of linear forms
Demi Allen, Felipe A. Ramírez

Abstract
The classical Khintchine–Groshev theorem is a generalization of Khintchine’s theorem on simultaneous Diophantine approximation, from approximation of points in ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} to approximation of systems of linear forms in ℝ n ​ m \mathbb{R}^{nm} . In this paper, we present an inhomogeneous version of the Khintchine–Groshev theorem which does not carry a monotonicity assumption when n ​ m > 2 nm>2 . Our results bring the inhomogeneous theory almost in line with the homogeneous theory, where it is known by a result of Beresnevich and Velani (2010) that monotonicity is not required when n ​ m > 1 nm>1 . That result resolved a conjecture of Beresnevich, Bernik, Dodson, and Velani (2009), and our work resolves almost every case of the natural inhomogeneous generalization of that conjecture. Regarding the two cases where n ​ m = 2 nm=2 , we are able to remove monotonicity by assuming extra divergence of a measure sum, akin to a linear forms version of the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture. When n ​ m = 1 nm=1 it is known by work of Duffin and Schaeffer (1941) that the monotonicity assumption cannot be dropped. The key new result is an independence inheritance phenomenon ; the underlying idea is that the sets involved in the ( ( n + k ) × m ) ((n+k)\times m) -dimensional Khintchine–Groshev theorem ( k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 ) are always k k -levels more probabilistically independent than the sets involved the ( n × m ) (n\times m) -dimensional theorem. Hence, it is shown that Khintchine’s theorem itself underpins the Khintchine–Groshev theory.

The Lebesgue measure theory

For a sequence of balls

Conjecture 1

Let

Conjecture 2

Let

\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{n,m}(\Psi)\right\rvert=1\qquad\textrm{if}\qquad\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{n-1}\lvert B_{q}\rvert=\infty\;.

The challenge in the above conjectures is an issue that causes

[

2

7

\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{1,2}^{\mathbf{y}}(\psi)\right\rvert=1\qquad\textrm{if}\qquad\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\psi(q)^{2}=\infty.

1.2
An “independence inheritance” theorem

The results described in this section are the main new tool for
establishing Theorems
1
and
2
. They also provide an instructive point of
view on all statements of Khintchine–Groshev type, and so we find
them interesting in their own right. To put it informally, they tell
us that statements in the
(n,m)
case imply statements in the

(n+k,m)
case, hence, we may take as a guiding principle that

\textrm{``Khintchine''}\qquad\implies\qquad\textrm{``Khintchine--Groshev''}

in all its various incarnations. In order to state this principle
precisely, some discussion is needed.

The set
\mathcal{A}_{n,m}(\Psi)
is the limsup set of a sequence of
measurable subsets of
{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
. As such, its measure is
0
whenever
the measures of those subsets have a converging sum — a simple
consequence of the First Borel–Cantelli lemma (see, for example,

[
22
, Lemma 1.2]
). Indeed, this is why, as we have mentioned,
the convergence parts of the theorems in the previous subsection are
straightforward to establish.

On the other hand, if the measure sum
diverges
, it takes much
more work to show that
\mathcal{A}_{n,m}(\Psi)
has full, or even positive,
measure. (In fact, it might not have positive measure, as
counterexamples in the
(n,m)=(1,1)
setting have shown.) Invariably,
that work has involved showing that the sequence of sets exhibits some
sort of “independence,” such as is necessary to apply a partial
converse of the Borel–Cantelli lemma. In fact, Beresnevich and Velani
have shown that, in a sense made precise in
[
12
]
, a limsup set
has positive measure
only if
there is some independence
present.

For us, the relevant notion of independence is that of
quasi-independence on average. A sequence
(A_{\mathbf{q}})_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
of
measurable subsets of a finite measure space
(X,\mu)
is said to be

quasi-independent on average
(or QIA for short) if

\limsup_{Q\to\infty}\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\mu(A_{\mathbf{q}})\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert,\lvert\mathbf{r}\rvert=1}^{Q}\mu(A_{\mathbf{q}}\cap A_{\mathbf{r}})}>0.

(2)

The importance of this concept is made clear by a well-known partial
converse to the first Borel–Cantelli lemma, stated here as
Lemma
1
. It guarantees that if a sequence is
quasi-independent on average, then the associated limsup set has
positive measure.

The sets we will be working with here are of the following form. Given
a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and
\mathbf{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, let
A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B)
denote the
set of
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
for which there exists a
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with

\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B
. When the meaning is clear from context, we may omit
the subscript and simply write
A(\mathbf{q},B)
. When
n=1
we customarily
write the non-bold
q
, i.e.
A(q,B)
. The sets
\mathcal{A}_{n,m}(\Psi)

appearing in our main results are limsup sets for sequences of sets of
this form, so the problem of establishing Theorems
1

and
2
naturally involves studying the
independence properties of the sets
A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B)
.

Figure 1:
On the left are three of the
16
possible sets

A_{2,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{q})
with
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q=2
. On the right is the set

A_{1,1}(\mathbf{q},qB_{q})\times[0,1]
. The areas of the
16
possible
blue regions sum to the area of the red region, up to a constant
which depends on the dimensions; in this case, that constant is
8
. One may imagine that the red region has been fractured into

q=2
pieces of equal area, (duplicated
8
times,) and shuffled
in order to make the blue
regions. Theorem
8
quantifies the
extra independence that is gained in this fracturing and
shuffling.

As a starting point, we take a naïve point of view, which we will
illustrate in the
(2,1)
case (see Figure
1
). The
divergence condition
\sum q\lvert B_{q}\rvert=\infty
should be understood as the
divergence of a measure sum. The summand
q\lvert B_{q}\rvert
is, up to a
constant, the sum of the measures of
A_{2,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})
as

\mathbf{q}
ranges through the sphere of radius
q
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. These sets
each have measure
\lvert B_{q}\rvert
, and for each
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q
, the set

A_{2,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{q})
can be visualized as an array of “stripes”
through the unit square, at an angle determined by the angle of

\mathbf{q}
. On the other hand one can interpret the sum
\sum q\lvert B_{q}\rvert
as

\sum\lvert qB_{q}\rvert
, where
qB_{q}
denotes the
\times q
-dilation of

B_{q}
around its center. This way, one may imagine that the sum is the
measure sum corresponding to the sets
A_{1,1}(q,qB_{q})
, each of
which is a union of intervals. Any metric properties of these sets
will be shared trivially by the vertically extended sets

A_{1,1}(q,qB_{q})\times[0,1]\subset{\mathbb{I}}^{2}
. Notice that this last set,
which is a union of thick vertical stripes, has the same measure (up
to a constant) as the
sum
of the measures of the sets

\left\{A_{2,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{q})\right\}_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}
, which are unions of thin
stripes arranged in many different directions. This leads to the naïve
suspicion that

the sets
\left\{A_{2,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\right\}_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
should be at least as independent as
the sets
\left\{A_{1,1}(q,qB_{q})\right\}_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
,

because it is as if the latter have each been fractured into
q

pieces which are then shuffled into a disordered arrangement to create
the former.

The next result confirms this intuition (and a stronger result, to be
discussed, quantifies it). It states that quasi-independence on
average for certain sequences of sets in
{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
is inherited by an
associated sequence of sets in
{\mathbb{I}}^{(n+k)m}
for any integer

k\geq 0
, and this allows us to prove full measure for the
corresponding limsup set in
{\mathbb{I}}^{(n+k)m}
.

Theorem 4

(Independence Inheritance)
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
. Suppose
(B_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of balls in

\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. If the sets

\left\lparen A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\right\rparen_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
are quasi-independent
on average, then so are the sets

\left\lparen A_{n+k,m}(\mathbf{q},\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert^{-k/m}B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\right\rparen_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+k}}
for
every
k\geq 0
. If in addition the sum
\sum\lvert B_{q}\rvert
diverges,
then for every
k\geq 0
, the set

\limsup_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\to\infty}A_{n+k,m}(\mathbf{q},\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert^{-k/m}B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})

has full Lebesgue measure.

The philosophy behind applications of Theorem
4
is
that Khintchine–Groshev-like statements in the case
(n+k,m)
are
weaker than in the case
(n,m)
. Indeed, by using
Theorem
4
, one can show that for
m\geq 2
, the
m
-dimensional
Khintchine theorem
implies
the
(n,m)
-dimensional
Khintchine–Groshev theorem, because the sets involved in Khintchine’s
theorem are quasi-independent on average in dimensions

\geq 2

[
19
]
. The same implication holds
inhomogeneously, as well as inhomogeneously
sans
monotonicity
in dimension
\geq 3
; that is, the inhomogeneous Khintchine theorem
in dimension
m\geq 3
implies the inhomogeneous Khintchine–Groshev
theorem in dimensions
(n,m)
for any
n
. The quasi-independence on
average of the relevant sets in the cases
(1,m)
with
m\geq 3
is
established in Proposition
2
as well as in Yu’s
proof of
[
34
, Theorem 1.8]
.

In fact, Theorem
4
follows from a stronger
inheritance phenomenon, Theorem
8
, which
allows us to quantify how much “more independent” the sets in the

(n+k,m)
case are than the sets in the
(n,m)
case. We define in
Section
4
(Definition
1
)
a hierachy of the form

\textrm{QIA}=0\textrm{-QIA}\quad\implies\quad 1\textrm{-QIA}\quad\implies\quad 2\textrm{-QIA}\quad\implies\quad 3\textrm{-QIA}\quad\implies\quad\cdots

where

-QIA stands for “
w
-weak quasi-independence on average”
and
0
-QIA coincides with the usual notion defined
by (
2
). Theorem
8
states that the

(n+k,m)
case inherits an independence that is
k
steps stronger
than the independence enjoyed by the
(n,m)
case. At this level of
detail, we can already say how Theorems
1

and
2
are proved:
we establish that the sets involved in the
(1,1)
case are

2
-QIA, the sets in the
(1,2)
case are
1
-QIA, and the sets in the

(1,m)
case (
m\geq 3
) are
0
-QIA (see

). Theorem

then gives us
0
-QIA for all
(n,m)
with
nm>2
.

The full measure statement then follows by a “mixing” argument
exploiting the periodicity inherent in the sets
A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B)
(see
Lemma
7
and Proposition
1
). This
argument enables us to overcome another perceived obstacle in our
general setting; namely, that the literature lacks a
“zero-one” law for inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation. In the
homogeneous setting, Beresnevich and Velani had
previously shown that the Lebesgue measure of the set

{\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{0}}(\psi)
is always either zero or one; that is, for
homogeneous approximation, these sets satisfy a so-called “zero-one”
law
[
10
]
. A consequence of this was that in proving the
homogeneous Khintchine–Groshev theorem without monotonicity for

nm>1
in
[
11
]
, to obtain the full measure statement, it was
enough for Beresnevich and Velani to show that the sets

{\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{0}}(\psi)
had
positive
measure subject to the
divergence of the appropriate sum. This is a common and effective
strategy that has been used countless times in this
field. Consequently, zero-one laws are seen as indispensable tools in
homogeneous approximation, the most well-known of them being the
zero-one laws of Cassels and
Gallagher
[
13
,
18
]
. Since we know
of no zero-one law for the general sets
{\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\psi)
, that
strategy is unavailable to us. However, our
Theorem
1
implies an
a posteriori
inhomogeneous zero-one
law when
nm>2
.

1.3
The Hausdorff measure theory

In addition to studying the Lebesgue measure of sets such as

\mathcal{A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\psi)
, much attention in Diophantine approximation is
also devoted to studying the more refined Hausdorff measures and
Hausdorff dimension of such sets. This is particularly fruitful when
the Lebesgue measure of such sets is zero, in which case Hausdorff
dimension and Hausdorff measures can often still provide us with a
means of differentiating the relative “sizes” of such sets. In
classical simultaneous approximation, these types of distinctions were
accomplished by the Jarník–Besicovitch theorem and Jarník’s
theorem. See
[
8
]
for a survey
discussion.

We conclude this introduction by recording Hausdorff measure
counterparts to Theorems
1
,

3
, and
2
. These are, respectively, Theorems
5
,
6
, and
7
. Theorems
5
and

6
can be obtained as a simple consequence of
combining the relevant Lebesgue measure statement (in our case,
Theorem
1
or
3
) with
[
4
, Theorem
5]
. The latter statement, appearing below as Theorem
AB, is essentially a general “transference principle,” proved by the
first author and Beresnevich, which allows us to more-or-less
immediately read off a Hausdorff measure analogue when given an
appropriate Lebesgue measure Khintchine–Groshev type
statement. Theorem AB is a consequence of the mass transference
principle for systems of linear forms
[
4
, Theorem
1]
which, informally speaking, allows us to transfer
Lebesgue measure statements for limsup sets determined by
neighbourhoods of planes (i.e. systems of linear forms) to Hausdorff
measure statements. The mass transference principle for systems of
linear forms is just one natural generalization of the original
mass transference principle due to Beresnevich and Velani

[
9
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem AB

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be an
approximating function, let
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{m}
, and let
f
and

g:r\to g(r):=r^{-m(n-1)}f(r)
be dimension functions such that

r^{-nm}f(r)
is monotonic. Let

\theta:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\qquad\text{be defined by}\qquad\theta(q)=q\,g\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\,.

Then

|{\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\theta)|=1\qquad\text{implies}\qquad{\cal H}^{f}({\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\psi))={\cal H}^{f}({\mathbb{I}}^{nm}).

Before presenting our Hausdorff measure results, we recall some
definitions. We will say that a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
is a

dimension function
if
f
is continuous, non-decreasing, and

f(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
. Given a set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and a real
number
\rho>0
, we will say that a countable collection of balls,
say
(B_{i}:=B(x_{i},r_{i}))_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, is a
\rho
-cover
for
F

if
F\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}{B_{i}}
and
r_{i}<\rho
for all

i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The
\rho
-approximate Hausdorff
f
-measure
of
F

is defined as

{\cal H}_{\rho}^{f}(F):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{f(r)}:\{B_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\text{ is a $\rho$-cover for $F$}\right\}.

The
Hausdorff
f
-measure
of
F
is then defined to be

{\cal H}^{f}(F):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}{{\cal H}_{\rho}^{f}(F)}=\sup_{\rho>0}{{\cal H}_{\rho}^{f}(F)}.

In the case that
f(r)=r^{s}
for some real
s\geq 0
,
{\cal H}^{f}
is the
perhaps more familiar
Hausdorff
s
-measure
. In this case we
write
{\cal H}^{s}
in place of
{\cal H}^{f}
. Although we will not require this
definition here, for completeness we remark that the
Hausdorff
dimension
of
F
is defined as

\dimh{F}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:{\cal H}^{s}(F)=0\}.

For further details on Hausdorff measures and Hausdorff dimension, we refer the reader to, for example,
[
17
,
28
,
30
]
.

Combining Theorem
1
with Theorem AB yields the
following Hausdorff measure analogue of Theorem

1
. This statement is essentially an inhomogeneous
Hausdorff
measure Khintchine–Groshev theorem
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
nm>2
. Let
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{m}
, and let

\psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be an approximating function. Let
f
and

g:r\to g(r):=r^{-m(n-1)}f(r)
be dimension functions such that

r^{-nm}f(r)
is monotonic. Then,

{\cal H}^{f}({\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\psi))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle 0&\mbox{ \text{if}\hskip 10.22217pt$\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{q^{n+m-1}g\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)}<\infty$\;,}\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle{\cal H}^{f}(\mathbb{I}^{nm})&\mbox{ \text{if}\hskip 10.22217pt$\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{q^{n+m-1}g\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)}=\infty$\;.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Analogously to the Lebesgue measure theory, the convergence part of Theorem
5
follows from a standard covering argument, and the statement is true in this case for any
n,m\geq 1
with no monotonicity requirements on
\psi
. We omit the details here but note that the required argument is given very explicitly in
[
3
]
.

The divergence part of the above statement would follow immediately
from Theorem AB provided that we could show that

\lvert{\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\theta)\rvert=1
for

\theta(q)=qg\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}
. Provided
that
nm>2
, it follows from
Theorem
1
that
\lvert{\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\theta)\rvert=1
if

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{q^{n-1}\theta(q)^{m}}=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{q^{n+m-1}g\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)}=\infty,

thus completing the proof.
∎

Remark
.

The proof of Theorem
5
, both the
convergence and divergence parts, is virtually identical to the
proof given of Theorem 3.7 in
[
3
]
. In all cases
except when
n=1
or
n=2
, Theorem
5
was
already known to be true without any monotonicity assumptions on

\psi
, see
[
4
, Theorem 3]
(which also appears
as
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
). In the case that
n=1
or

n=2
, the conclusion of Theorem
5
was
shown to be true in
[
4
, Theorem 3]
subject to
additional monotonicity assumptions. We refer the reader to

[
3
,
4
]
for detailed discussion of the
precise requirements, as well as for more details on the mass
transference principle for linear forms.

In line with how Theorem
1
demonstrates that
monotonicity is not required in the classical inhomogeneous
Khintchine–Groshev theorem whenever
nm>2
, Theorem
5
shows that the same is true in
the analogous inhomogeneous Hausdorff measure Khintchine–Groshev theorem. Moreover, any further progress towards removing monotonicity from the Lebesgue measure inhomogeneous Khintchine–Groshev theorem in the remaining
(1,2)
and
(2,1)
cases
can be transferred to analogous progress in the
Hausdorff measure setting via Theorem AB. For example, in the same
way that we can deduce the divergence part of Theorem
5
from Theorem
1
using Theorem AB,
we can deduce the following Hausdorff measure analogue of
Theorem
3
. We leave the details as an exercise for
the interested reader!

Theorem 6
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
nm=2
. Let
f
and

g:r\to g(r):=r^{-m(n-1)}f(r)
be dimension functions such that

r^{-nm}f(r)
is monotonic.
Then, for any
\psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, and any
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{m}
, we have

{\cal H}^{f}\left({\cal A}_{n,m}^{\mathbf{y}}(\psi)\right)={\cal H}^{f}({\mathbb{I}}^{nm})\qquad\textrm{if}\qquad\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}q^{n+m-1}g\left(\frac{\psi(q)}{q}\right)}=\infty.

Remark
.

Notice that the condition
nm=2
only permits the cases
(1,2)
and

(2,1)
.

Via a more direct application of the mass transference principle for
systems of linear forms
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, it is also
possible to prove a more general Hausdorff measure statement analogous
to the Lebesgue measure statement given by Theorem

2
. More precisely, recall that if

\Psi:=(B_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
and

n\geq 1
, we denote by
{\cal A}_{n,m}(\Psi)
the set of
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}

such that

\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B_{|\mathbf{q}|}

for infinitely many pairs

(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. By adapting the proof
of
[
4
, Theorem 2]
(specifically the proof of (11)
in
[
4
]
), one can apply
[
4
, Theorem
1]
directly to obtain the following Hausdorff
measure analogue of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
nm>2
.
Let
f
and
g:r\to g(r)=r^{-m(n-1)}f(r)
be dimension functions such that
r^{-nm}f(r)
is monotonic. Then,

{\cal H}^{f}({\cal A}_{n,m}(\Psi))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle 0&\mbox{ \text{if}\hskip 10.22217pt$\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{q^{n+m-1}g\left(\frac{|B_{q}|^{\frac{1}{m}}}{2q}\right)}<\infty$,}\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle{\cal H}^{f}(\mathbb{I}^{nm})&\mbox{ \text{if}\hskip 10.22217pt$\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{q^{n+m-1}g\left(\frac{|B_{q}|^{\frac{1}{m}}}{2q}\right)}=\infty$.}\end{array}\right.

Remark
.

Observe that
\frac{|B_{q}|^{\frac{1}{m}}}{2q}
is simply
\frac{r(B_{q})}{q}
, where
r(B_{q})
is the radius of the ball
B_{q}
.

2
Lemmas

The first three lemmas are standard facts from measure theory. Recall
that the
limsup
of a sequence of measurable sets

(A_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in a finite measure space
(X,\mu)
is

\displaystyle\limsup_{q\to\infty}{A_{q}}:

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in X:x\in A_{q}\text{ for infinitely many $q\in{\mathbb{N}}$}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{Q\geq 1}{\bigcup_{q\geq Q}{A_{q}}}.

Lemma 1

(Divergence Borel–Cantelli Lemma,
[
22
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Suppose
(X,\mu)
is a finite measure space and
(A_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset X
is a
sequence of measurable subsets such that
\sum\mu(A_{q})=\infty
. Then

\mu\left\lparen\limsup_{q\to\infty}A_{q}\right\rparen\geq\limsup_{Q\to\infty}\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})}.

Lemma 2

(Chung–Erdős Lemma,
[
14
, Equation 4]
)
.

Suppose
(X,\mu)
is a probability space and
(A_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset X
is a
sequence of measurable subsets. If
\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{q=1}^{Q}A_{q}\right\rparen>0
, then

\mu\left\lparen\bigcup_{q=1}^{Q}A_{q}\right\rparen\geq\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\mu(A_{q})\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\mu(A_{q}\cap A_{r})}.

Lemma 3

(
[
6
, Lemma 6]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space with a finite measure
\mu
such that
every open set is
\mu
-measurable. Let
A
be a Borel subset of
X

and let
f:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be an increasing function with
f(x)\to 0
as

x\to 0
. If for every open set
U\subset X
we have

\mu(A\cap U)\geq f(\mu(U)),

then
\mu(A)=\mu(X)
.

The next lemmas are specific to our problem. Recall that for
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
we define

A(\mathbf{q},B)=A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B)=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}:\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B\text{ for some }\mathbf{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\}

(3)

and note that it has measure
\lvert B\rvert
. Given two vectors
\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, we will use the notation
\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}
to denote that
\mathbf{q}_{1}
and
\mathbf{q}_{2}
are parallel and, similarly, we will write
\mathbf{q}_{1}\nparallel\mathbf{q}_{2}
to indicate that the vectors
\mathbf{q}_{1}
and
\mathbf{q}_{2}
are not parallel.

Lemma 4

(
[
32
, Lemma 9 in Chapter 1]
)
.

Suppose
B_{1},B_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
are balls, and
\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
\mathbf{q}_{1}\nparallel\mathbf{q}_{2}
. Then

\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert=\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{1})\right\rvert\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
B_{1},B_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
are balls, and that

\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbf{r}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{r}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n^{\prime}}
are
such that
\lvert\mathbf{q}_{i}\rvert=\lvert\mathbf{r}_{i}\rvert
and

\mathbf{q}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{2}=\mathbf{r}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{2}
(that is, the pairs either both
point in the same direction, or both point in opposite
directions). Then

\left\lvert A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{1})\cap A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert=\left\lvert A_{n^{\prime},m}(\mathbf{r}_{1},B_{1})\cap A_{n^{\prime},m}(\mathbf{r}_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert.

Remark
.

Throughout this work, we have allowed
\mathbf{q}
to take values in

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. As it turns out, all of our arguments work if we restrict

\mathbf{q}
to lie in the positive orthant
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Under that restriction, the question (seen for example in
the above lemma) of whether
\mathbf{q}_{1}
and
\mathbf{q}_{2}
point in the same
direction becomes irrelevant.

Proof of Lemma
5
.

We will show that

\left\lvert A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{1})\cap A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert=\left\lvert A_{1,m}(\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert,B_{1})\cap A_{1,m}(\pm\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert,B_{2})\right\rvert,

(4)

where the
\pm
is determined according to whether
\mathbf{q}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{2}
is
positive or negative. To see this, let
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and

k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
\mathbf{q}_{1}=k_{1}\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{q}_{2}=k_{2}\mathbf{q}
. By
possibly changing the sign on
\mathbf{q}
we may ensure that
k_{1}
is
positive. Now
A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{2})
is the preimage of

A(k_{1},B_{1})\cap A(k_{2},B_{2})
under the projection

\mathbf{x}\mapsto\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}(\bmod 1)
. This mapping is measure-preserving
(see
[
32
, Lemma 8 in Chapter 1]
), therefore

\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert=\left\lvert A(k_{1},B_{1})\cap A(k_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert.

But notice that
A(\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert,B_{1})\cap A(\sgn(k_{2})\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert,B_{2})
is the preimage of

A(k_{1},B_{1})\cap A(k_{2},B_{2})
under the “
\times\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert(\bmod 1)
”
map, which is also measure-preserving, so we have

\left\lvert A(k_{1},B_{1})\cap A(k_{2},B_{2})\right\rvert=\left\lvert A(\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert,B_{1})\cap A(\sgn(k_{2})\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert,B_{2})\right\rvert.

Combining this with the previous observation, we
have (
4
), where the
\pm
is determined
by
\sgn(k_{2})
, which is itself determined by whether
\mathbf{q}_{1}
and

\mathbf{q}_{2}
point in the same or opposite direction. Since the same
argument could have been carried out with
\mathbf{r}_{1}
and
\mathbf{r}_{2}
, we are
done.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
B_{1},B_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
are balls, and
q_{1},q_{2}
are
integers with
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}
. Then, for every
\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}
and

\lvert\mathbf{q}_{i}\rvert=q_{i}
,

\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},q_{1}^{-1/m}B_{1})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},q_{2}^{-1/m}B_{2})\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{q_{1}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})\right\rvert.

Proof.

Lemma
5
implies that it is enough to prove this
for
n=1
, that is, we must prove that

\left\lvert A(q_{1},q_{1}^{-1/m}B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},q_{2}^{-1/m}B_{2})\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{q_{1}}\left\lvert A(q_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})\right\rvert,

where the
\pm
is determined by whether
\mathbf{q}_{1}
and
\mathbf{q}_{2}
point in
the same or opposite direction. Seeing

A(q_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})
as a subset of

[0,1]^{m}
, we contract it by
q_{1}^{-1/m}
and see that

\left\lvert A(q_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})\right\rvert=q_{1}\left\lvert q_{1}^{-1/m}\left\lparen A(q_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})\right\rparen\right\rvert.

Notice that

q_{1}^{-1/m}\left\lparen A(q_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})\right\rparen=\underbrace{\left[q_{1}^{-1/m}\left\lparen A(q_{1},B_{1})\right\rparen\right]}_{C}\cap\underbrace{\left[q_{1}^{-1/m}\left\lparen A(\pm q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})\right\rparen\right]}_{D}.

Since
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are balls in
\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, they have diameter

\leq 1
, and so each of the square bracketed sets on the right-hand
side (labelled
C
and
D
) is a union of essentially disjoint balls
of volume
q_{1}^{-1}\lvert B_{1}\rvert
and
q_{2}^{-1}\lvert B_{2}\rvert
respectively. We
may see
C\cap D
as an intersection of several balls in

(q_{1}^{-1/m}\mathbb{R})^{m}/(q_{1}^{-1/m}\mathbb{Z})^{m}
(a shrunken torus) such that no
point is contained in more than two of them. The sets

A(q_{1},q_{1}^{-1/m}B_{1})
and
A(\pm q_{2},q_{2}^{-1/m}B_{2})
are obtained
from
C
and
D
by
translating
the disjoint balls so that
their centers lie at their corresponding points in

\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. Since those corresponding points can be obtained via a
scaling of the metric by
q_{1}^{1/m}\geq 1
, the pairwise distances
between centers has necessarily increased. Moreover, since the balls
making up
C
and
D
are only
translated
to their new
positions (not scaled), the measures of the pairwise intersections
cannot have increased. Therefore, we have

\left\lvert A(q_{1},B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})^{1/m}B_{2})\right\rvert\geq q_{1}\left\lvert A(q_{1},q_{1}^{-1/m}B_{1})\cap A(\pm q_{2},q_{2}^{-1/m}B_{2})\right\rvert,

proving the lemma.
∎

Lemma 7
.

For any
n,m\geq 1
there exists a constant
C:=C_{m}>0
such that for every open set
U\subset{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
the following holds: for all
\mathbf{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
of sufficiently large norm (
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\geq Q_{U}
),

\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\cap U\right\rvert\geq C\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\right\rvert\left\lvert U\right\rvert

holds for every ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. (Note that the subscripts are added to the constants here to indicate their dependencies, e.g.
C_{m}
indicates that
C
depends on
m
.)

Proof.

First, find within
U
a finite union of disjoint balls
V\subset U

such that
\lvert V\rvert\geq\lvert U\rvert/2
. (In principle, we could get as
close to the measure of
U
as we want.) We may even, and indeed
will, assume all the balls in
V
have the same radius,
r>0
.

Now let
W\subset{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
be any ball of radius
r
. It will be
enough to show that

\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\cap W\right\rvert\geq C^{\prime}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\right\rvert\left\lvert W\right\rvert

(5)

for all
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\geq Q_{r}
, where
C^{\prime}>0
is some absolute constant
which may depend on
n
and
m
, and
Q_{r}
only depends on

r
. Importantly for us,
Q_{r}
does not depend on

B
and so, given (
5
), one can deduce the lemma with
C=C^{\prime}/2
.

Let us write
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
as
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{m})

where
\mathbf{x}_{j}
are column vectors. Then for any
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we have
that
\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{m})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{m}
, and the
condition that

\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B\qquad\textrm{for}\qquad\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}

is equivalent to
\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{j}+p_{j}\in B_{j}
for each
j
, where

B_{j}
is the projection of
B
to the
j
th component. Therefore, we
have

A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B)\cap W=\left\lparen\prod_{j=1}^{m}A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})\right\rparen\cap W=\prod_{j=1}^{m}A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})\cap W_{j},

(6)

where
W_{j}
is the projection of
W
to the copy of
{\mathbb{I}}^{n}

corresponding to the
j
th column of
{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
.

Suppose for the moment that
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
is achieved in the first
coordinate of
\mathbf{q}
. For any
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{n-1}
(representing the last

n-1
coordinates), let

S_{\mathbf{z}}=\left\lparen A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})\cap W_{j}\right\rparen_{\mathbf{z}}=A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}\cap(W_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}

be the cross-section through
\mathbf{z}
parallel to the first
coordinate. Then

\displaystyle\left\lvert A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})\cap W_{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{I}}^{n-1}}\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{Y_{j}}\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\,d\mathbf{z}

where
Y_{j}
is the projection of
W_{j}
to the last
n-1

coordinates. Meanwhile,
(W_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}
is an interval of length
2r

and
A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}
is a union of
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
disjoint
intervals of length

\lvert B_{j}\rvert/\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=\lvert B\rvert^{1/m}/\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
with centers spaced

1/\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
apart, in
({\mathbb{I}}^{nm})_{\mathbf{z}}\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore,

(W_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}
fully contains at least
2r\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert-2
of the
intervals constituting
A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}
, so we have

\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\geq(2r\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert-2)\frac{\lvert B_{j}\rvert}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert},

which exceeds
r\lvert B_{j}\rvert
as soon as
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\geq 2/r
. In that case, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert A_{n,1}(\mathbf{q},B_{j})\cap W_{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\int_{Y_{j}}\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\geq r\lvert B_{j}\rvert\int_{Y_{j}}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=r\lvert B_{j}\rvert\lvert Y_{j}\rvert

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}r^{n}\lvert B_{j}\rvert.

Note that the argument in this paragraph did not depend on the
supposition that
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
was achieved in the first of the
n

coordinates.

Now, by (
6
) and recalling that

W\subset{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
was an arbitrary ball of radius
r
, we have

\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\cap W\right\rvert\geq 2^{nm-m}r^{nm}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\lvert B_{j}\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2^{m}}\lvert B\rvert\lvert W\rvert

for all
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\geq 2/r
. Since
\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\rvert=\lvert B\rvert
, we are
done.
∎

Lemma 8

(Overlap estimates)
.

Let
m\geq 1
. Suppose
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are balls in
\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and let
r,q\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\left\lvert A_{1,m}(r,B_{1})\cap A_{1,m}(q,B_{2})\right\rvert\ll\lvert B_{1}\rvert\lvert B_{2}\rvert+\lvert B_{2}\rvert\lvert q\rvert^{-m}\gcd(r,q)^{m},

where the implicit constant depends only on
m
. In fact, we can take
the implicit constant to be
2^{m}
.

Proof.

Since
A(q,B)=A(-q,-B)
, it is enough to prove this lemma for
r,q\geq 1
.

Suppose the radii of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are

\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, respectively. Let

\delta=2\min\left\{\frac{\psi_{1}}{r},\frac{\psi_{2}}{q}\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta=2\max\left\{\frac{\psi_{1}}{r},\frac{\psi_{2}}{q}\right\}.

Then

\left\lvert A(r,B_{1})\cap A(q,B_{2})\right\rvert\leq\delta^{m}N

where

N=\#\left\{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}:\lvert\mathbf{a}\rvert\leq q,\lvert\mathbf{b}\rvert\leq r,r\mathbf{a}-q\mathbf{b}\in B\right\},

and
B
is a ball of diameter
\Delta rq
whose center depends on

B_{1},B_{2},r,q
, but we do not need to specify it here. Essentially,

N
gives us an upper bound on the number of regions of intersection
we can possibly see between
A(r,B_{1})
and
A(q,B_{2})
, and
\delta^{m}

is a trivial upper bound for the measure of one of these regions of
intersection.

If
\Delta qr\geq\gcd(q,r)
then
B
can contain at most

\left(\frac{\Delta qr}{\gcd(q,r)}+1\right)^{m}

integer points of the form
r\mathbf{a}-q\mathbf{b}
.
Furthermore, an integer point can
be realized in the form
r\mathbf{a}-q\mathbf{b}
in at most

\gcd(q,r)^{m}
different ways, for if

r\mathbf{a}-q\mathbf{b}=r\mathbf{a}^{\prime}-q\mathbf{b}^{\prime},

then this implies

\frac{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}{q}=\frac{\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}{r},

and there are
\gcd(q,r)^{m}
rational points of this form in

{\mathbb{I}}^{m}
. So, in this case, we can bound

\displaystyle\left\lvert A(r,B_{1})\cap A(q,B_{2})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2\Delta qr}{\gcd(q,r)}\right)^{m}\gcd(q,r)^{m}\delta^{m}

\displaystyle=2^{m}\Delta^{m}\delta^{m}q^{m}r^{m}

\displaystyle=8^{m}\psi_{1}^{m}\psi_{2}^{m}

\displaystyle=2^{m}\lvert B_{1}\rvert\lvert B_{2}\rvert.

On the other hand, if
\Delta qr<\gcd(q,r)
, then
B
contains at
most one integer point of the form
r\mathbf{a}-q\mathbf{b}
which (if it exists) can be realized in that form
in at most
\gcd(q,r)^{m}
different ways. Therefore, in this case,

N\leq\gcd(q,r)^{m}
and we have

\left\lvert A(r,B_{1})\cap A(q,B_{2})\right\rvert\leq\delta^{m}\gcd(q,r)^{m}\leq 2^{m}\frac{\psi_{2}^{m}}{q^{m}}\gcd(q,r)^{m}=\lvert B_{2}\rvert q^{-m}\gcd(q,r)^{m}.

Combining the two possible cases,
\Delta qr<\gcd(q,r)
and

\Delta qr\geq\gcd(q,r)
, the proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

The following definition is the way of quantifying “weak

Definition 1
.

Given a sequence of balls
\Psi:=(B_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, we will say that the sets

\left\lparen A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\right\rparen_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
are
w
-
weakly
quasi-independent on average
(
w
-QIA, for short) if for
w\geq 0

\limsup_{Q\to\infty}\left\lparen\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\rvert\right\rparen^{2}\left\lparen\sum_{1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert\leq Q}\left\lparen\frac{\gcd(\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert,\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert)}{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{w}\left\lvert A\left\lparen\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert}\right\rparen\cap A\left\lparen\mathbf{q}_{2},\left\lparen\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert}{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert}\right\rparen^{\frac{w}{m}}B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert}\right\rparen\right\rvert\right\rparen^{-1}>0.

(7)

For tidiness, we use the notation

\Pi(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\Psi,w)=\Pi_{n,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\Psi,w):=\left\lvert A\left\lparen\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert}\right\rparen\cap A\left\lparen\mathbf{q}_{2},\left\lparen\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert}{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert}\right\rparen^{\frac{w}{m}}B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert}\right\rparen\right\rvert

when
\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert

q,r\geq 1

\Gamma(q,r)=\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{\min(q,r)}.

This way, the expression (
7
) becomes

\limsup_{Q\to\infty}\left\lparen\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\rvert\right\rparen^{2}\left\lparen\sum_{1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert\leq Q}\Gamma(\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert,\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert)^{w}\Pi(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\Psi,w)\right\rparen^{-1}>0.

(8)

Note that
w
-QIA implies
w^{\prime}
-QIA whenever
w\leq w^{\prime}
and that

0
-QIA coincides with QIA.

Theorem 8

(Strong Independence Inheritance)
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
and let
w\geq 0
. Suppose

(B_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. If the the
sets

\left\lparen A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\right\rparen_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

are
w
-QIA, then the sets

\left\lparen A_{n+k,m}(\mathbf{q},\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert^{-k/m}B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\right\rparen_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+k}}

are
\max(w-k,0)
-QIA for every integer
k\geq 0
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Denote
\Psi_{0}:=(B_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, the given sequence of balls in

\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, and for integer
k\geq 0
denote

\Psi_{k}:=(q^{-k/m}B_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
.

It suffices to prove the theorem with
k=1
. Furthermore, since

0
-QIA implies
1
-QIA, we may take
w\geq 1
. For

\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
with
\lvert\mathbf{q}_{i}\rvert=q_{i}
and
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
,
we are concerned with overlaps of the form

\Pi_{n+1,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\Psi_{1},w-1):=\left\lvert A_{n+1,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},q_{1}^{-1/m}B_{q_{1}})\cap A_{n+1,m}\left(\mathbf{q}_{2},\left\lparen\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right\rparen^{\frac{w-1}{m}}q_{2}^{-1/m}B_{q_{2}}\right)\right\rvert.

(9)

If
\mathbf{q}_{1}\nparallel\mathbf{q}_{2}
then

\displaystyle\Pi_{n+1,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\Psi_{1},w-1)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert A_{n+1,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},q_{1}^{-1/m}B_{q_{1}})\right\rvert\left\lvert A_{n+1,m}\left(\mathbf{q}_{2},\left\lparen\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right\rparen^{\frac{w-1}{m}}q_{2}^{-1/m}B_{q_{2}}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert A_{n+1,m}(\mathbf{q}_{1},q_{1}^{-1/m}B_{q_{1}})\right\rvert\left\lvert A_{n+1,m}(\mathbf{q}_{2},q_{2}^{-1/m}B_{q_{2}})\right\rvert

by Lemma
4
. In particular, we have

\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}_{1}\nparallel\mathbf{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\\

\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}\lvert A_{n+1,m}(\mathbf{q},\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert^{-1/m}B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\rvert\right\rparen^{2}.

(10)

On the other hand, for
\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}
, we have as a consequence
of Lemma
5
that

\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\\

\ll\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{r}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{r}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
\lvert\mathbf{r}_{i}\rvert=q_{i}\end{subarray}}\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})\Gamma(q_{1},q_{2})^{w-1}\Pi_{n,m}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\Psi_{1},w-1),

noting that in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the number of parallel pairs with norms

is comparable to
\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})^{d-1}
. Then by

\ll\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{r}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{r}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\

(11)

By assumption, there are infinitely many values of

Proposition 1

(Full measure)
.

Let

and suppose

3
.

Proposition 2

(Base case for Theorem
2
)
.

Suppose
(B_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}

such that
\sum\lvert B_{q}\rvert
diverges.

•

If

, then the sets
(A_{1,m}(q,B_{q}))_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
are
quasi-independent on average.

•

If
m\geq 2
, then the weaker estimate

\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{r}\left\lvert A_{1,m}(r,B_{r})\cap A_{1,m}(q,(r/q)^{1/m}B_{q})\right\rvert\ll\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert A_{1,m}(q,B_{q})\rvert\right\rparen^{2},

holds.

•

If
m\geq 1
, then the still weaker estimate

\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\left\lparen\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{r}\right\rparen^{2}\left\lvert A_{1,m}(r,B_{r})\cap A_{1,m}(q,(r/q)^{2/m}B_{q})\right\rvert\ll\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert A_{1,m}(q,B_{q})\rvert\right\rparen^{2},

holds.

Remark
.

In the language introduced in Definition
1
, this
proposition says that the sets are

\max\{(3-m),0\}
-QIA.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

First, suppose
m\geq 3
. By Lemma
8
,

\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\left\lvert A(r,B_{r})\cap A(q,B_{q})\right\rvert\ll\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\left(\lvert B_{r}\rvert\lvert B_{q}\rvert+\lvert B_{q}\rvert q^{-m}\gcd(q,r)^{m}\right).

The second sum on the right-hand side is

\displaystyle\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert q^{-m}\gcd(q,r)^{m}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert q^{-m}\gcd(q,r)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}q^{-m}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\underbrace{\sum_{r=1}^{q}\gcd(q,r)^{m}}.

The indicated
\gcd
sum is
\ll q^{m}
if
m\geq 3
. After all,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{q}\gcd(q,r)^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d\mid q}d^{m}(q/d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q}(q/d)^{m}d

\displaystyle\leq q^{m}\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{q}i^{-m+1}},

and the indicated sum is absolutely bounded for
m\geq 3
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\left\lvert A(r,B_{r})\cap A(q,B_{q})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{r}\rvert\lvert B_{q}\rvert+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\right\rparen^{2},

since the series in parentheses diverges.

Now let us suppose only that
m\geq 2
. Then a nearly identical
calculation can be done. By Lemma
8
, we have

\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{r}\left\lvert A(r,B_{r})\cap A(q,(r/q)^{1/m}B_{q})\right\rvert\ll\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\left(\lvert B_{r}\rvert\lvert B_{q}\rvert+\frac{r}{q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert q^{-m}r^{-1}\gcd(q,r)^{m+1}\right).

This time the second sum on the right-hand side is

\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert q^{-m-1}\gcd(q,r)^{m+1}\ll\sum_{q=1}^{Q}q^{-m-1}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\underbrace{\sum_{r=1}^{q}\gcd(q,r)^{m+1}}

and the
\gcd
sum is
\ll q^{m+1}
since we have assumed
m\geq 2
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{r}\left\lvert A(r,B_{r})\cap A(q,(r/q)^{1/m}B_{q})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\lvert B_{r}\rvert+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\right\rparen^{2},

as in the previous paragraph.

Finally, suppose only that
m\geq 1
. Again by Lemma

8
, we have

\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\left\lparen\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{r}\right\rparen^{2}\left\lvert A(r,B_{r})\cap A(q,(r/q)^{2/m}B_{q})\right\rvert\ll\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\left(\lvert B_{r}\rvert\lvert B_{q}\rvert+\left\lparen\frac{r}{q}\right\rparen^{2}\lvert B_{q}\rvert q^{-m}r^{-2}\gcd(q,r)^{m+2}\right).

This time the second sum on the right-hand side is

\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert q^{-m-2}\gcd(q,r)^{m+2}\ll\sum_{q=1}^{Q}q^{-m-2}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\underbrace{\sum_{r=1}^{q}\gcd(q,r)^{m+2}}

and the
\gcd
sum is
\ll q^{m+2}
since we have assumed
m\geq 1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\left\lparen\frac{\gcd(q,r)}{r}\right\rparen^{2}\left\lvert A(r,B_{r})\cap A(q,(r/q)^{2/m}B_{q})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{q,r=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\lvert B_{r}\rvert+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\right\rparen^{2},

as before. This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
nm>2
. The convergence part is easily disposed by an
application of the First Borel–Cantelli lemma, so let us focus on
the divergence part.

First, notice that we lose no generality by choosing some
c>0
and
assuming that

(\forall q\in\mathbb{N})\qquad q^{n-1}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\leq c.

(12)

After all, for any
q
where it does not hold, we can shrink the
ball until
q^{n-1}\lvert B_{q}\rvert=c
and work with the possibly-shrunk
sequence of balls instead. The divergence condition will still hold
for the smaller balls. Let us therefore assume that (
12
)
holds with a fixed
c<1
.

Now we may obtain a new sequence of balls

\widehat{B}_{q}=q^{(n-1)/m}B_{q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. Notice that the sum

\sum\lvert\widehat{B}_{q}\rvert=\sum q^{n-1}\lvert B_{q}\rvert

diverges by assumption, so Proposition
2

guarantees that the sets

A_{1,m}(q,\widehat{B}_{q})=A_{1,m}(q,q^{(n-1)/m}B_{q})

are
\max\{(3-m),0\}
-QIA, in the sense of
Definition
1
. Therefore, by
Theorem
8
the sets

A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert^{-(n-1)/m}\widehat{B}_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})=A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})

inherit
\max\{(3-m)-(n-1),0\}
-QIA, which, since

4-(m+n)\leq 0
, is
0
-QIA. Now, by
Proposition
1
,

\limsup_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\to\infty}A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})

has full measure, and this proves the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3

The following theorem is a more general version of
Theorem
3
that does not require the balls to be
concentric.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
nm=2
and
{\varepsilon}>0
. For any sequence of balls

\Psi:=(B_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, we have

\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{n,m}(\Psi)\right\rvert=1\qquad\textrm{if}\qquad\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{n-1}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\lvert B_{q}\rvert=\infty.

Remark
.

The above theorem only has two cases:
(1,2)
and
(2,1)
. The
corresponding divergence conditions are

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\lvert B_{q}\rvert=\infty\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}q\lvert B_{q}\rvert=\infty.

Remark
.

Following the arguments outlined in Section
1.3
, one could obtain a Hausdorff measure analogue of Theorem
9
akin to statements seen in Section
1.3
. We leave the details to the reader.

Proof of Theorem
9
for

(n,m)=(2,1)
.

Suppose
(B_{q})_{q\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of balls
such that for some
{\varepsilon}>0
the series

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}{\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}q|B_{q}|}\asymp\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{\infty}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\rvert=\infty.

By Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert\leq Q\\
\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert\leq Q\\
\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A_{1,1}(\left\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\right\rvert,B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert})\cap A_{1,1}(\pm\left\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\right\rvert,B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})\left\lvert A(q_{1},B_{q_{1}})\cap A(q_{2},B_{q_{2}})\right\rvert,

(13)

and by Lemma
6
, we have

\left\lvert A(q_{1},B_{q_{1}})\cap A(q_{2},B_{q_{2}})\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{q_{1}}\left\lvert A(q_{1},q_{1}B_{q_{1}})\cap A(q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})q_{2}B_{q_{2}})\right\rvert.

(14)

Combining (
13
) and (
14
) brings us to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert\leq Q\\
\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert})\right\rvert\ll\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\frac{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}{q_{1}}\left\lvert A(q_{1},q_{1}B_{q_{1}})\cap A(q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})q_{2}B_{q_{2}})\right\rvert,

and, by Lemma
8
, we have

\left\lvert A(q_{1},q_{1}B_{q_{1}})\cap A(q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})q_{2}B_{q_{2}})\right\rvert\ll q_{1}^{2}\lvert B_{q_{1}}\rvert\lvert B_{q_{2}}\rvert+q_{1}\lvert B_{q_{2}}\rvert q_{2}^{-1}\gcd(q_{1},q_{2}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\frac{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}{q_{1}}

\displaystyle\left\lvert A(q_{1},q_{1}B_{q_{1}})\cap A(q_{2},(q_{1}/q_{2})q_{2}B_{q_{2}})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}q\lvert B_{q}\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\sum_{1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq Q}\frac{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})^{2}}{q_{2}}\lvert B_{q_{2}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}q\lvert B_{q}\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\sum_{q_{2}=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q_{2}}\rvert q_{2}^{-1}\sum_{q_{1}=1}^{q_{2}}\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})^{2}.

Now,

\sum_{r=1}^{q}\gcd(r,q)^{2}=\sum_{d\mid q}d^{2}\varphi\left(\frac{q}{d}\right)=\sum_{d\mid q}\left\lparen\frac{q}{d}\right\rparen^{2}\varphi(d)=q^{2}\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d^{2}}\leq q^{2}\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{1}{d}=q\sum_{d\mid q}d\ll\frac{q^{3}}{\varphi(q)},

(15)

by
[
21
, Theorem 329]
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert\leq Q\\
\mathbf{q}_{1}\parallel\mathbf{q}_{2}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\left\lparen\sum_{q=1}^{Q}q\lvert B_{q}\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\frac{q^{2}}{\varphi(q)}

\displaystyle\ll\left\lparen\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\rvert\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)},

(16)

an estimate we can safely extend to the nonparallel pairs

\mathbf{q}_{1}\nparallel\mathbf{q}_{2}
, since they are genuinely pairwise independent.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{I}}^{2}
be an open set. Then by a combination
of (
16
) and Lemma
7
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert\leq Q}

\displaystyle\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q}_{1},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{1}\rvert})\cap A(\mathbf{q}_{2},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}_{2}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert\ll\left\lparen\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\rvert\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\lvert U\rvert^{2}}\left\lparen\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\frac{1}{\lvert U\rvert}\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\lvert U\rvert^{2}}\left[\left\lparen\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=1}^{Q}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}\right].

(17)

The implicit constant does not depend on
U
.

Following a strategy from the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 1.8]
, we let

D_{\ell}=\left\{q\in\mathbb{N}:2^{\ell}\leq\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}<2^{\ell+1}\right\}.

If there exists
\ell\geq 0
for which

\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\rvert=\infty,

then we can restrict our attention to
D_{\ell}
. In this case,
estimate (
16
) would immediately lead to 0-QIA, and we would then be done by
Proposition
1
. So assume that there is no such

\ell
, and put

\Sigma_{\ell}:=\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Sigma_{\ell,Q}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert.

Notice that
\Sigma_{\ell}<\infty
for every
\ell\geq 0
, by
assumption, and that the
\Sigma_{\ell}
’s form a divergent series, by
Lemma
7
. Then, by Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\lvert\mathbf{r}\rvert,\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\\
\lvert\mathbf{r}\rvert,\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap A(\mathbf{r},B_{\lvert\mathbf{r}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\gg\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert\right\rparen^{2}}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\lvert\mathbf{r}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\\
\lvert\mathbf{r}\rvert,\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap A(\mathbf{r},B_{\lvert\mathbf{r}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert}

for every
\ell
and
Q
for which the union on the left-hand side
has positive measure. This is guaranteed to be the case for
infinitely many
\ell
, since the measure sum
diverges. Then, for
Q\geq Q_{\ell}
where
Q_{\ell}
is
sufficiently large that the estimates in (
17
) take effect,
we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert

\displaystyle\gg\lvert U\rvert^{2}\left\lparen\frac{\left\lparen\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert\right\rparen^{2}}{\left\lparen\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\right\rparen^{2}+\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}}\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\lvert U\rvert^{2}\left\lparen 1+\displaystyle{\frac{\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}}{\left\lparen\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\right\rparen^{2}}}\right\rparen^{-1}

(18)

Note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}}{\left\lparen\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\right\rparen^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\frac{\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\left\lparen\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}\right\rparen^{2+{\varepsilon}}}{\left\lparen\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
1\leq\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}}\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\rvert\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\left\lparen\frac{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}{\varphi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}\right\rparen^{1+{\varepsilon}}\right\rparen^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2^{\ell+1})^{2+{\varepsilon}}}{2^{2\ell(1+{\varepsilon})}\Sigma_{\ell,Q}}.

Putting this into (
18
), we find that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert

\displaystyle\gg\lvert U\rvert^{2}\left\lparen\frac{1}{1+\frac{2^{2+{\varepsilon}}}{2^{{\varepsilon}\ell}\Sigma_{\ell,Q}}}\right\rparen.

Now, the fact that the
\Sigma_{\ell}
form a divergent series implies
that there are
\ell
and corresponding
Q
for which

2^{{\varepsilon}\ell}\Sigma_{\ell,Q}
is arbitrarily large. In particular,
there are infinitely many
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
(and corresponding

Q\in{\mathbb{N}}
) for which the above string of inequalities gives

\underbrace{\left\lvert\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\in D_{\ell}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q\end{subarray}}A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert}_{C_{\ell}}\geq\frac{\lvert U\rvert^{2}}{2C},

where
C
is the implicit constant in the above estimates. Since the
sets
C_{\ell}
all have measure at least
\lvert U\rvert^{2}/2C
, their
associated limsup set must have at least that
measure. Furthermore, since

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}C_{\ell}\subset\limsup_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\to\infty}A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U,

this implies that

\left\lvert\limsup_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\to\infty}A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap U\right\rvert\geq\frac{\lvert U\rvert^{2}}{2C}.

The theorem now follows by Lemma
3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

for
(n,m)=(1,2)

Suppose

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq r\leq q\leq Q}\left\lvert A_{1,2}(r,B_{r})\cap A_{1,2}(q,B_{q})\right\rvert

.

Pathwise uniform convergence of a full discretization for a three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise
Binjie Li, Qin Zhou

Abstract
This paper analyzes a full discretization of a three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise. The discretization combines the Euler scheme for temporal approximation and the finite element method for spatial approximation. A pathwise uniform convergence rate is derived, encompassing general spatial L q L^{q} -norms, by using discrete versions of deterministic and stochastic maximal L p L^{p} -regularity estimates. Additionally, the theoretical convergence rate is validated through numerical experiments. The primary contribution of this work is the introduction of a technique to establish the pathwise uniform convergence of finite element-based full discretizations for nonlinear stochastic parabolic equations within the framework of general spatial L q L^{q} -norms.



Keywords:

stochastic Allen-Cahn equation, Euler scheme, finite element method,
discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, pathwise uniform convergence

Introduction

Let
T\in(0,\infty)

\mathcal{O}
be a bounded, convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Let

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y(t)=\left(\Delta y+y-y^{3}\right)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+F(y(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
y(0)=v,\end{cases}

and

represents an
H
-cylindrical Brownian motion.
The diffusion coefficient
F

The Allen-Cahn equation is an important mathematical model in
materials science
[
1
]
and it is also of fundamental
importance for the geometric moving interface problem
[
10
]
.
By incorporating thermal fluctuations, impurities, and atomistic processes
that describe surface motion, the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation
naturally arises
[

,

Recently, there has been a growing interest in the numerical analysis
of the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation. A brief summary of some recent
works is given below. For the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation
with additive noise, Becker and Jentzen
[
4
]
obtained
strong convergence rates for a nonlinearity-truncated Euler-type
approximation. Becker et al.
[

[
5
]
analyzed an

[
35

method for spatial discretization and the Euler scheme for temporal discretization.
In addition, Wang
[
43

analyzed an efficient explicit

The numerical analysis of the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative
noise generally presents greater challenges. In this regard,
Feng et al.
[
11
]
derived the strong convergence of finite element
approximations of a stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with finite-dimensional
gradient-type multiplicative noise, Majee and Prohl
[
33
]
analyzed
a space-time discretization, and Liu and Qiao
[
32
]
investigated the strong convergence of Galerkin-based Euler approximations.
We also refer the reader to
[

,
17
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
36
,
38
]
and
the references therein for more related works.

So far, the numerical analysis of the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation in the literature has generally considered only the
L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})
-norm error estimates for fully discrete approximations. There is a lack of pathwise uniform convergence estimates and convergence estimates with general spatial
L^{q}
-norms. Notably, Bréhier et al.
[
5
]
derived some pathwise uniform convergence estimates for an explicit temporal splitting numerical scheme; however, their analysis might not be directly applicable to general domains (see
[
5
, pp. 2116]
). Extending this analysis to the full discretization of the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise is nontrivial.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We establish the stability estimate

\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{k-j}g_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t)\right\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\right]\leqslant C\left\|g_{h}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,L^{q}))}^{p},

for all
g_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,X_{h}))
,
where
p\in(2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
.
Here,
X_{h}
denotes the finite element space and
L^{q}
denotes
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
.
This estimate is useful for demonstrating pathwise uniform convergence of
fully discrete approximations based on finite elements for nonlinear stochastic parabolic equations,
within the framework of general spatial
L^{q}
-norms.
While van Neerven and Veraar
[
40
]
have developed analogous estimates for
the Euler scheme, their methodology does not directly apply to deriving the aforementioned
stability estimate, as detailed in Subsection
4.2
.

•

For a fully discrete approximation of
Eq.

1.1
,
using the Euler scheme for temporal discretization and the finite
element method for spatial discretization, we establish the following
pathwise uniform convergence estimate:

\Big\lVert{\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{y(t_{j})-Y_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant c\left(h^{2-\varepsilon}+\tau^{1/2}\right)

for all
p\in(2,\infty)
,
q\in[2,\infty)
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Notably, even for the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with additive noise,
to the best of our knowledge, such error estimates for fully discrete approximations using
the Euler scheme have not been reported previously. This finding fills an important gap in
the existing literature.

Our analysis is based on discrete deterministic and stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimates. We impose relatively mild regularity conditions on the diffusion coefficient
F
in
Eq.

1.1
. Although our analysis assumes that the initial value

is deterministic and belongs to
W_{0}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{O})\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathcal{O})

In addition, the pathwise uniform convergence of numerical approximations for
SPDEs within the framework of Banach spaces has recently attracted considerable
attention; see, for example,
[

,
8
,
21
,
40
]
.
However, existing research has primarily focused on temporal semidiscretizations. This work introduces a new approach for establishing pathwise uniform convergence for finite element-based full discretizations of nonlinear stochastic parabolic equations, within the general spatial
L^{q}
-norms framework.

In Section

Mathematical Setup

For any two Banach spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{2})
the space of all bounded linear operators from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
, and abbreviate
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{1})

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded, convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\mathcal{C}^{2}
(cf. Section 11.1 in Chapter 1 of
[
44
]

W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{O})

[

Let
\Delta
denote the Laplace operator subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. For
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, let
\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}
denote the domain of
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
in
L^{q}
, endowed with the norm

\lVert{u}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}}:=\lVert{(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u}\rVert_{L^{q}},\quad\forall u\in\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}.

We use
\dot{H}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}
to denote the dual space of
\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}
, where
q^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
q
, i.e.,
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
.
For any
q\in(1,\infty)
, it is well-known (see, e.g., Theorem 16.14 of
[
44
]
) that
\dot{H}^{2,q}
coincides with the intersection
W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{O})\cap W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})
, and is equipped with a norm equivalent to that of
W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})
. Moreover,
\dot{H}^{1,q}
is identical to
W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{O})
, with equivalent norms.

Assume that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a given
complete probability space with a right-continuous filtration
\mathbb{F}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, on which a sequence of
independent
\mathbb{F}
-adapted Brownian motions

(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are defined. For any
0\leqslant s<t<\infty
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the increment
\beta_{n}(t)-\beta_{n}(s)
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
.
Let
H
be a separable Hilbert
space with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
and an orthonormal basis
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Let the
H
-cylindrical Brownian motion be defined such that for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

W_{H}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(H,L^{2}(\Omega))
is given by

W_{H}(t)h:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\beta_{n}(t)(h,h_{n})_{H},\quad\forall h\in H.

We use the symbol
\mathbb{E}
to represent the expectation operator associated with the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
For any UMD Banach space
E
, let
\gamma(H,E)
denote the space of all
\gamma
-radonifying operators
from
H
to
E
. For any
p\in[2,\infty)
,
we denote by
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,E))

and
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H,E)))
the spaces
of all
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
\gamma(H,E)

in

When

is a Hilbert space,

coincides with the standard Hilbert-Schmidt space
from

to

, and we have the celebrated Itô’s isometry:

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t)\right\rVert_{E}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert{g(t)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,E)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t,

for all
g\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,E))
.
For an in-depth discussion of
\gamma
-radonifying operators,
the reader is referred to Chapter 9 of
[
16
]
.
An extensive treatment of stochastic integrals with respect to
W_{H}
in UMD Banach spaces can
be found in
[
41
]
.

Next, we define the diffusion coefficient
F
in

1.1
as follows.
Let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a sequence of continuous functions
from
\overline{\mathcal{O}}\times\mathbb{R}

such
that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{cases}f_{n}(x,0)=0,&\forall x\in\partial\mathcal{O},\\
\lvert{f_{n}(x,y)}\rvert\leqslant C_{F}(1+\lvert{y}\rvert),&\forall x\in\mathcal{O},\,\forall y\in\mathbb{R},\\
\lvert{f_{n}(x,y_{1})-f_{n}(x,y_{2})}\rvert\leqslant C_{F}\lvert{y_{1}-y_{2}}\rvert,&\forall x\in\mathcal{O},\,\forall y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\\
\lvert{f_{n}(x_{1},y)-f_{n}(x_{2},y)}\rvert\leqslant C_{F}(1+\lvert{y}\rvert)\lvert{x_{1}-x_{2}}\rvert,&\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{O},\,\forall y\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
C_{F}
is a positive constant independent of

,
x
,
y
,

x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,

and

.
Let
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers
satisfying the condition
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}<\infty
.
Finally, for any

define

where

\big(h_{n}\otimes f_{n}(\cdot,u(\cdot))\big)(w):=(w,h_{n})_{H}f_{n}(\cdot,u(\cdot)),\quad\forall w\in H.

By

),

\displaystyle\lVert{F(u)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,L^{2})}\leqslant c\big(1+\lVert{u}\rVert_{L^{2}}\big),\quad\forall u\in L^{2},

where

is a positive constant independent of

We call a process

Proposition 2.1

Suppose the initial value

3
Full Discretization

Let
J
be a positive integer, and set
t_{j}:=j\tau
for each
0\leqslant j\leqslant J
,
where
\tau:=T/J
is the time step. Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a conforming and quasi-uniform
triangulation of
\mathcal{O}
consisting of three-dimensional simplexes,
and let the spatial mesh size
h
denote the maximum diameter of the simplexes in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
.
Define

X_{h}:=\Big\{u_{h}\in C(\overline{\mathcal{O}})\,\big|\,u_{h}=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{h}\text{ and outside of }\mathcal{O}_{h},\text{ and }u_{h}|_{K}\in P_{r}(K)\text{ for each }K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\Big\},

where
C(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
is the set of all continuous functions
on the closure of
\mathcal{O}
,
\mathcal{O}_{h}
is the closure of
the union of the simplexes in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
,
r\geqslant 1

is an integer, and
P_{r}(K)
is the set of all polynomials on
K

with degree not exceeding
r
for each
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
.
Let
P_{h}
be the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection operator onto
X_{h}
,
and, for any
u_{h}\in X_{h}
, define
\Delta_{h}u_{h}\in X_{h}
by

\int_{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta_{h}u_{h})\cdot v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla u_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\quad\forall v_{h}\in X_{h},

where
\mu
denotes the three-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{O}
.

This study considers the following full discretization of the model problem
Eq.

1.1
:

\begin{cases}Y_{j+1}-Y_{j}=\tau\left(\Delta_{h}Y_{j+1}+Y_{j}-P_{h}Y_{j+1}^{3}\right)+P_{h}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}F(Y_{j})\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\quad 0\leqslant j<J,\\
Y_{0}=P_{h}v.\end{cases}

(3.1)

For this full discretization, we establish the following pathwise uniform convergence estimate; the proof is deferred to
Section

4
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the initial value
v
belongs to
W_{0}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{O})\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathcal{O})
,
and
\tau\leqslant h^{2}
. Let
y
be the mild solution of the model problem
Eq.

1.1
,
and let
(Y_{j})_{j=0}^{J}
be the solution of the full discretization
Eq.

3.1
.
Then for all
p\in(2,\infty)
,
q\in[2,\infty)
, and
\varepsilon>0
,
the following pathwise uniform convergence estimate holds:

\Big\lVert{\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{Y_{j}-y(t_{j})}\rVert_{L^{q}}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant c\big(h^{2-\varepsilon}+\tau^{1/2}\big),

(3.2)

where
c
denotes a positive constant independent of the spatial mesh size
h

and the time step
\tau
.

Remark 3.1
.

For
Theorem

3.1
, we have the following comments.

1.

The condition
\tau\leqslant h^{2}
can be relaxed by re-examining and refining the analysis in Subsection

.

2.

Assume that the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}^{2}f_{n}(x,y)\right|+\left|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}f_{n}(x,y)\right|+\left|\nabla_{y}^{2}f_{n}(x,y)\right|\leqslant C_{F}(1+|y|),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{O},\,\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

The authors in

\Big(\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{2,q}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leqslant c\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}.

Finally, we present some standard properties of the space
\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,q}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
.
For any
q\in(1,\infty)
, there exist two positive constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
, independent of
h
, such that for all
u_{h}\in X_{h}
, the following inequality holds:

c_{0}\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1,q}}\leqslant\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}\leqslant c_{1}\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1,q}}.

For any
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, there exist two positive constants
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
, independent of
h
, such that for all
u_{h}\in X_{h}
, the following inequality holds:

c_{2}\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,q}}\leqslant\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{[\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q},\dot{H}_{h}^{2,q}]_{\alpha/2}}\leqslant c_{3}\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,q}},

where
[\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q},\dot{H}_{h}^{2,q}]_{\alpha/2}
is the complex interpolation space between
\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}
and
\dot{H}_{h}^{2,q}
(see
[
44
, Section 5 in Chapter 1]
).
Furthermore, for any
\beta\in[3/2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
,
using the well-known estimate (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Section 8.5]
),

\lVert{\Delta^{-1}-\Delta_{h}^{-1}P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(L^{\beta})}\leqslant ch^{2},

combined with the continuous embedding of
\dot{H}^{2,\beta}
into
L^{q}
and the inverse estimate, it follows that the continuous embedding of
\dot{H}_{h}^{2,\beta}
into
L^{q}
is independent of
h
.
These results will be used throughout the subsequent analysis without further explicit reference.

4.2
A stability estimate for a discrete stochastic convolution

The objective of this subsection is to establish the stability estimate

\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\Big]\bigg)^{1/p}\leqslant c\lVert{g_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}))}

(4.8)

for the discrete stochastic convolution defined by

Z_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{k-j}g_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant J,

(4.9)

where
g_{h}
belongs to
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}))
,
with parameters
p\in(2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
.
This stability estimate is crucial for the derivation of
pathwise uniform convergence rates for the numerical approximations of a
broad class of nonlinear stochastic parabolic equations.

Within the framework of Hilbert spaces, we note that
Gyöngy and Millet
[
13
, Theorem 2.6]
have
established the following stability estimate:

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\|Z_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}\leqslant c\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,L^{2}))}.

Moreover, under the Banach space setting, a significant advancement was made by
van Neerven and Veraar
[
40
, Proposition 5.4]
,
who demonstrated the following sharp estimate for a discrete stochastic convolution
without spatial discretization:

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(I-\tau\Delta)^{k-j}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t)\Big\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}\leqslant c\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H,L^{q})))},

holds for all
g\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H,L^{q})))

with
p,q\in[2,\infty)
. The derivation of this estimate relies on
the contraction property of the operator
(I-\tau\Delta)^{-1}
on

L^{q}
for all
q\in[2,\infty)
. However, it is important to note
that
(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}
fails to possess
the contraction property on
\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}
for any
q\in(2,\infty)
.
Hence, Proposition 5.4 from
[
42
]
can not be directly applied to
bound the discrete stochastic convolution defined by
Eq.

4.9
.

To address the aforementioned challenge, we employ the discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity
estimate for

\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathbb{E}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{p},\quad p\in(2,\infty),\,q\in[2,\infty),

coupled with the approximation property of
(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}
to the identity operator

I
as presented in
Eq.

4.3
.
We formally state the discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate
in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
.
Assume that
(Z_{j})_{j=1}^{J}
is defined by
Eq.

4.9
,
where
g_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}))
.
Then the following discrete stochastic
maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate holds:

\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{p}\bigg]\Bigg)^{1/p}\leqslant c\lVert{g_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}))}.

Proof.

Note that
[
29
, Theorem 3.1]
has demonstrated that the

H^{\infty}
-calculus of
-\Delta_{h}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
the spatial mesh size
h
. Subsequently, applying
[
27
, Theorem 3.2]
,
we establish the desired discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate.
∎

Now we present the main result of this subsection in the form of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
.
Suppose that
g_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}))
,
and let
(Z_{j})_{j=1}^{J}
be defined by
Eq.

4.9
.
Then the stability estimate
Eq.

4.8
holds.

Proof.

Let
Z_{0}:=0
and, for any
t\in[0,T]
, define

\Psi(t):=(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}g_{h}(t),\quad\mathcal{G}(t):=p\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}(t)\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\left\langle\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j},\,\Psi(t)\right\rangle,

where
\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1})}
is the indicator function for
the time interval
[t_{j},t_{j+1})
.
For any
0\leqslant j<J
, we also define

\delta_{j}:=(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}Z_{j}-Z_{j},\quad M_{j}:=\delta_{j}+\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\Psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t).

It can be verified that

\displaystyle Z_{j+1}=Z_{j}+M_{j}\quad\text{for all $0\leqslant j<J$}.

The rest of the proof is divided into the following five steps.

Step 1
. Let us prove

\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{0\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\Big]\leqslant I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},

(4.10)

where

\hskip-165.00024ptI_{1}:=p\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\left|\left\langle\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j},\,\delta_{j}\right\rangle\right|\bigg],

\hskip-195.00029ptI_{2}:=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big\lvert{\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s)}\Big\rvert\bigg],

I_{3}:=p|p-q|\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{0}^{1}\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-2q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}(Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}),\,M_{j}}\big\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg],

\hskip-60.00009ptI_{4}:=p(q-1)\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{0}^{1}\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rvert^{q-2},\,M_{j}^{2}}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg].

Fix any
0\leqslant j<J
.
Define the function
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by

g(\theta):=\big\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\big\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p},\quad\theta\in[0,1].

A straightforward computation yields

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(0)=p\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j},\,M_{j}}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=p\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j},\,\delta_{j}}\big\rangle+\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\mathcal{G}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t)

and

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=p(p-q)\big\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\big\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-2q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}(Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}),\,M_{j}}\big\rangle^{2}+{}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad p(q-1)\big\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\big\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rvert^{q-2},\,M_{j}^{2}}\big\rangle,\quad\forall\theta\in[0,1].

Hence, by
g(1)=g(0)+g^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)g^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta
and the fact that

g(1)=\lVert{Z_{j+1}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}
and
g(0)=\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert{Z_{j+1}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}+p\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j},\,\delta_{j}}\big\rangle+\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\mathcal{G}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t)

\displaystyle\quad{}+p|p-q|\int_{0}^{1}\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-2q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}(Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}),\,M_{j}}\big\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad{}+p(q-1)\int_{0}^{1}\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rvert^{q-2},\,M_{j}^{2}}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Given that
Z_{0}=0
and the preceding inequality holds for all
0\leqslant j<J
,
we deduce the following bound for the supremum of the sequence
(Z_{j})_{j=0}^{J}
:

\displaystyle\max_{0\leqslant j\leqslant J}\left\|Z_{j}\right\|_{L^{q}}^{p}\leqslant p\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\left\|Z_{j}\right\|_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\left|\big\langle{\left|Z_{j}\right|^{q-2}Z_{j},\,\delta_{j}}\big\rangle\right|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{}+p|p-q|\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}\right\|_{L^{q}}^{p-2q}\big\langle{\left|Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}\right|^{q-2}(Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}),\,M_{j}}\big\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{}+p(q-1)\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}\right\|_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\left|Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}\right|^{q-2},\,M_{j}^{2}}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

By taking expectations on both sides of the inequality, we arrive at
the desired inequality (
4.10
).

Step 2
.
From the definition of
I_{1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant p\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\lVert{\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j}}\big\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1,q/(q-1)}}\lVert{\delta_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{-1,q}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\lVert{\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j}}\big\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1,q/(q-1)}}\lVert{\delta_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{-1,q}}\bigg].

For any
0\leqslant j<J
, we observe that

\displaystyle\|\delta_{j}\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{-1,q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}-I\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q},\dot{H}_{h}^{-1,q})}\|Z_{j}\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}

\displaystyle=\|(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}-I\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{h}^{2,q},\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q})}\|Z_{j}\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau\|Z_{j}\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}},

where the last inequality is justified by (
4.3
) with
\alpha=0
and
\beta=2
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|\,|Z_{j}|^{q-2}Z_{j}\|_{\dot{H}^{1,q/(q-1)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\|\nabla(|Z_{j}|^{q-2}Z_{j})\|_{L^{q/(q-1)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\|Z_{j}\|_{L^{q}}^{q-2}\|\nabla Z_{j}\|_{L^{q}}\quad\text{(by H\"{o}lder's inequality)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\|Z_{j}\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{q-1}.

Consequently, putting the above estimates together, we obtain

I_{1}\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tau\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{p}\bigg].

(4.11)

Step 3
.
By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\lVert{\mathcal{G}(t)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=c\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\Big\lVert{\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-q}\big\langle{\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j},\,\Psi(t)}\big\rangle}\Big\rVert_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{2p-2q}\big\lVert{\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert^{q-2}Z_{j}}\big\rVert_{L^{q/(q-1)}}^{2}\lVert{\Psi(t)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,L^{q})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=c\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{2p-2}\lVert{\Psi(t)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,L^{q})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]^{1/2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{0\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-1}\lVert{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H,L^{q}))}\Big].

By Young’s inequality, we then obtain

I_{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{0\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\Big]+c\lVert{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\gamma(H,L^{q})))}^{p}.

(4.12)

Step 4
.
We employ Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality as follows:

\displaystyle I_{3}+I_{4}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{0}^{1}\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p-2}\lVert{M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{1}\tau\lVert{Z_{j}+\theta M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}+\tau^{1-p/2}\lVert{M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tau\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\bigg]+c\tau^{1-p/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\bigg].

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{M_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(i)}}}{{\leqslant}}c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{\delta_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\bigg]+c\tau^{p/2-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\lVert{\Psi(t)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;\gamma(H,L^{q}))}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(ii)}}}{{\leqslant}}c\tau^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{p}\bigg]+c\tau^{p/2-1}\lVert{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,L^{q}))}^{p},

where in the first inequality (i), we invoke the definition
M_{j}:=\delta_{j}+\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\Psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t)
,
Lemma
4.3
, and Hölder’s inequality;
and in the second inequality (ii), we make use of the definition
\delta_{j}:=(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}Z_{j}-Z_{j}
,
along with the resolvent estimate from Lemma
4.3
with
\alpha=0
and
\beta=1
.
By combining these estimates, we obtain

I_{3}+I_{4}\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tau\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{p}\bigg]+c\lVert{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,L^{q}))}^{p}.

(4.13)

Step 5
. Combining
Eqs.

4.10
,
4.11
,
4.12
and
4.13
, and noting that
Z_{0}=0
,
we deduce that

\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\right]\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{p}\bigg]+c\lVert{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,L^{q}))}^{p}.

Applying
Proposition

4.1
, we infer that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\lVert{Z_{j}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,q}}^{p}\bigg]\leqslant c\lVert{g_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}))}^{p}.

Moreover, using inequality (
4.4
) with parameters

\alpha=\beta=0
, we establish

\lVert{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,L^{q}))}\leqslant c\lVert{g_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q}))}.

Aggregating these estimates, we conclude the desired stability estimate (
4.8
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Considering the maximal inequality established in

,

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\bigg]\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\lVert g(t)\rVert_{\gamma(H,L^{q})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{p/2},

).

(4.23)

3.

In Subsection

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lVert{\chi_{j}^{(2)}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\bigg]\leqslant c\tau^{p/2},\quad\forall p,q\in(2,\infty).

(4.35)

Finally, considering the decomposition (
4.32
) and the identity
Y_{0}=y_{h}(0)
,
we obtain the desired error estimate (
4.24
) by combining the bounds established in (
4.33
), (
4.34
), and (
4.35
).

4.3.4
Proof of the error estimate
Eq.

4.16

Fix any
p\in(2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
.
Let the sequences
(\chi_{j}^{(0)})_{j=0}^{J}
,

(\chi_{j}^{(1)})_{j=0}^{J}
, and
(\chi_{j}^{(2)})_{j=0}^{J}
be
constructed as in Subsection
4.3.3
.
By their construction, for any
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
,
the
L^{q}
-norm of
\chi_{j}^{(0)}
can be estimated almost surely as follows:

\displaystyle\lVert{\chi_{j}^{(0)}}\rVert_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big\lVert{(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{k-j}\big[\Delta_{h}\left(y_{h}(t_{k+1})-y_{h}(t)\right)+Y_{k}-y_{h}(t)\big]}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\lVert{(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{k-j}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{h}^{1/2+1/p,q},\dot{H}_{h}^{2,q})}\lVert{y_{h}(t)-y_{h}(t_{k+1})}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1/2+1/p,q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\lVert{(I-\tau\Delta_{h})^{k-j}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{h}^{0,q})}\lVert{Y_{k}-y_{h}(t)}\rVert_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Applying the resolvent estimate
Eq.

4.4
and employing Hölder’s inequality,
we obtain, for any
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
, almost surely,

\displaystyle\lVert{\chi_{j}^{(0)}}\rVert_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left[\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\lVert{y_{h}(t)-y_{h}(t_{k+1})}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1/2+1/p,q}}^{p}+\lVert{Y_{k}-y_{h}(t)}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Consequently, for the sequence
(\chi_{j}^{(0)})_{j=1}^{J}
, we derive the following bound almost surely:

\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{\chi_{j}^{(0)}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\leqslant c\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\lVert{y_{h}(t)-y_{h}(t_{j+1})}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1/2+1/p,q}}^{p}+\lVert{Y_{j}-y_{h}(t)}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Similarly, for
(\chi_{j}^{(1)})_{j=1}^{J}
, we have, almost surely,

\displaystyle\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{\chi_{j}^{(1)}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\leqslant c\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\lVert{P_{h}y_{h}^{3}(t)-P_{h}Y_{j+1}^{3}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Combining these results leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{\chi_{j}^{(0)}+\chi_{j}^{(1)}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\lVert{y_{h}(t)-y_{h}(t_{j+1})}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1/2+1/p,q}}^{p}+\lVert{Y_{j}-y_{h}(t)}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}+\lVert{P_{h}y_{h}^{3}(t)-P_{h}Y_{j+1}^{3}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\tau^{p/2}+c\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\lVert{P_{h}y_{h}^{3}(t)-P_{h}Y_{j+1}^{3}}\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr],

by
Eqs.

4.20
,
4.18
and
4.24
.
Furthermore, utilizing the stability property of
P_{h}
,
the error estimate
Eq.

4.24
, the inequality
Eq.

4.19
,
and the stability results in
Eq.

4.17
and
Eq.

4.23
,
we can establish the following inequality

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\big\lVert{P_{h}y_{h}^{3}(t)-P_{h}Y_{j+1}^{3}}\big\rVert_{L^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]\leqslant c\tau^{p/2}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\Big\lVert{\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\big\lVert{\chi_{j}^{(0)}+\chi_{j}^{(1)}}\big\rVert_{L^{q}}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant c\tau^{1/2}.

(4.36)

Next, for the sequence
(\chi_{j}^{(2)})_{j=1}^{J}
, by the stability estimate established
in
Proposition

4.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\|\chi_{j}^{(2)}\|_{L^{q}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\bigg[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\left\|P_{h}\big(F(y_{h}(t))-F(Y_{j})\big)\right\|_{\gamma(H,L^{q})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{1/p}.

Using the stability property of

from

4.1

4.7
,
and applying the results from

4.18

4.24

we further establish the following bound:

\Big\|\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\|\chi_{j}^{(2)}\|_{L^{q}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant c\tau^{1/2}.

(4.37)

Finally, given the decomposition

4.32
,

4.16
follows by

4.36

4.37
.

This section is dedicated to the numerical validation of the theoretical
findings presented in this paper.
The numerical experiments are conducted
using the following model equation:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y(t)=(\Delta y+y-y^{3})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+y(t)\,\mathrm{d}\beta(t),\quad 0<t\leqslant T,\\
y(0)=v.\end{cases}

Here,
\beta
represents a given Brownian motion,
the spatial domain
\mathcal{O}
is
(0,1)^{3}
,
and the initial condition is specified by the function
v
defined as:

v(x):=\sin(\pi x_{1})\sin(\pi x_{2})\sin(\pi x_{3})\quad\text{for all }x:=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{O}.

To verify the spatial accuracy as established in Theorem
3.1
,
we set the final time to
T=0.01
and the time step to

\tau=1\times 10^{-6}
. We then use the numerical solution
computed with a spatial mesh size of
h=\frac{1}{2^{8}}
as our
reference solution. Furthermore, we perform simulations across
500

independent paths. The numerical results presented in Figure

agrees well with the expected spatial convergence rate of

,
as outlined in
Eq.

3.2
,
for the cases of

To further affirm the temporal accuracy claimed in Theorem

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have utilized discrete deterministic and stochastic maximal
L^{p}
regularity
estimates to derive a pathwise uniform convergence rate of
O\big(h^{2-\epsilon}+\tau^{1/2}\big)

for a three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise.
This convergence estimate employs general spatial
L^{q}
-norms and imposes mild
conditions on the diffusion coefficient. Our work introduces a new approach to establishing
pathwise uniform convergence within the context of general spatial
L^{q}
-norms for
finite element-based full approximations of nonlinear stochastic parabolic equations.

Partial Optimality in Cubic Correlation Clustering
David Stein, Silvia Di Gregorio, Bjoern Andres

Abstract
The higher-order correlation clustering problem is an expressive model, and recently, local search heuristics have been proposed for several applications. Certifying optimality, however, is np -hard and practically hampered already by the complexity of the problem statement. Here, we focus on establishing partial optimality conditions for the special case of complete graphs and cubic objective functions. In addition, we define and implement algorithms for testing these conditions and examine their effect numerically, on two datasets.



1
Introduction

We study an optimization problem whose feasible solutions are all partitions of a finite set
S
.
Given a cost
c_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
for every (unordered) pair
p\in\tbinom{S}{2}
and a cost
c_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
for every (unordered) triple

t\in\tbinom{S}{3}
, the objective is to find a partition
\Pi
of
S
so as to minimize the sum of the costs of those pairs and triples whose elements all belong to the same set in
\Pi
:

Definition 1.1
.

The instance of the
cubic set partition
problem with respect to a finite set
S
, the set
P_{S}
of all partitions of
S
, and a function
c\colon\tbinom{S}{3}\cup\tbinom{S}{2}\cup\{\emptyset\}\to\mathbb{R}
is:

\displaystyle\min_{\Pi\in P_{S}}\quad\sum_{R\in\Pi}\sum_{t\in\tbinom{R}{3}}c_{t}+\sum_{R\in\Pi}\sum_{p\in\tbinom{R}{2}}c_{p}+c_{\emptyset}

(2)

The cubic set partition problem is
np
-hard, as it generalizes the
np
-hard clique partitioning problem for complete graphs
[
8
]
, specializing to the latter in the case that
c_{t}=0
for all
t\in\tbinom{S}{3}
.
Applications of cubic set partitioning include the tasks of fitting equilateral triangles to points in a plane (
Section

7.2
), and subspace clustering as discussed in
[
17
]
.

In this article, we ask whether we can compute a partial solution to the problem efficiently, i.e. to decide efficiently for some pairs or triples whether their elements are in the same set or distinct sets of an optimal partition.
In order to find such
partial optimality
, we characterize
improving maps
and state efficiently verifiable sufficient conditions of their improvingness,
a technique introduced by
Shekhovtsov 2013
; see also
[
21
,
22
]
.
In order to examine the effectiveness of these partial optimality conditions numerically, we implement algorithms for testing these, and conduct experiments, cf.
Figure

1
.

2
Related Work

We choose to state the cubic set partition problem
(
Definition

1.1
)
in the form of a non-linear binary program
(
Proposition

3.1
),
a special case of the higher-order correlation clustering problem introduced by
Kim et al. 2014
.
Combinatorial optimization problems like this involving higher-order objective functions
have interesting application as accurate models of intrinsically non-linear tasks

[
2
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
. In particular, higher-order correlation clustering has been used for subspace clustering in
[
17
, Section 5.1]
by introducing negative costs for points sufficiently close to a subspace.

Being able to efficiently fix some variables to an optimal value and thus reducing the size of the problem can be valuable in practice.
Consequently, much effort has been devoted to studying partial optimality for non-convex problems
[
1
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
21
,
22
]
.
In particular, we mention the impressive application of partial optimality conditions to Potts models for image segmentation in which more than 95% of the variables can be fixed
[
22
, Fig. 1]
.
In contrast to the customary approach of considering a convex, usually linear, relaxation and establishing partial optimality conditions regarding the variables in the extended formulation, we study such conditions directly in the original variable space.
Unlike the above-mentioned articles, we concentrate on taking advantage of the specific structure of the cubic clique partitioning problem.

To this end, we build on the works of
Alush and Goldberger 2012
and
Lange et al. 2018
,
Lange et al. 2019
who establish partial optimality conditions for problems equivalent to correlation clustering with a linear objective function.
Regarding their terminology, we remark that the correlation clustering problem, the clique partitioning problem, and the multicut problem are equivalent if the objective functions are linear.
The correlation clustering problem keeps attracting considerable attention by the community also in the context of approximation algorithms
[
24
]
.
The cubic set partition problem we consider here generalizes the specialization to complete graphs of both the correlation clustering problem and the multicut problem.
Note that correlation clustering for arbitrary, weighted graphs does not become more specific by considering only complete graphs. Instead, any such problem with respect to an arbitrary graph can be stated as a problem with respect to a complete graph and excessive edges having cost zero.

Here, we transfer all partial optimality conditions established by

Alush and Goldberger 2012
and
Lange et al. 2018
,
Lange et al. 2019

for the correlation clustering problem and the multicut problem to the cubic set partition problem.
In addition, we establish new results.
Unlike in
Lange et al. 2018
, the algorithm we define does not exploit the sparsity of edges with non-zero cost and, in this sense, is designed for complete graphs. Moreover, we do not contribute persistency conditions for the max cut problem.

3
Preliminaries

In order to establish partial optimality conditions for the cubic set partition problem (
Definition

1.1
), we state this problem in the form of the non-linear integer program introduced by
Kim et al. 2014
:

Proposition 3.1
.

The instance of the cubic set partition problem with respect to a finite set
S
and a function
c\colon\tbinom{S}{3}\cup\tbinom{S}{2}\cup\{\emptyset\}\to\mathbb{R}
has the form of the cubic integer program

\displaystyle\min_{x:\tbinom{S}{2}\to\{0,1\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{pqr\in\tbinom{S}{3}}c_{pqr}x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}+\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}}c_{pq}x_{pq}+c_{\emptyset}

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject~to}\ \

\displaystyle\forall p\in S\ \forall q\in S\setminus\{p\}\ \forall r\in S\setminus\{p,q\}\colon\quad x_{pq}+x_{qr}-x_{pr}\leq 1

(4)

Proof.

For each partition
\Pi
of the set
S
and every distinct
p,q\in S
, let
x_{pq}=1
if and only if
p
and
q
are in the same set of
\Pi
.
This establishes a one-to-one relation between the set
P_{S}
of all partitions of
S
and the feasible set
X_{S}
of all
x:\tbinom{S}{2}\to\{0,1\}
that satisfy the above inequalities
[
8
]
.
Under this bijection, the objective functions of
Definition

1.1
and
Proposition

3.1

are equivalent.
∎

Below, we let
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
denote this instance of the problem,
\phi_{c}
its objective function, and
X_{S}
its feasible set, i.e. the set of all
x:\tbinom{S}{2}\to\{0,1\}
that satisfy the above inequalities.

Our main technique is the construction of improving maps
[
20
]
, which is based on the following preliminary notions.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X\neq\emptyset
,
\phi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\colon X\to X
. If for every
x\in X
, we have
\phi(\sigma(x))\leq\phi(x)
, then

\sigma
is called
improving
for the problem
\min_{x\in X}\phi(x)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X\neq\emptyset
,
\phi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\colon X\to X
an improving map. Moreover, let
Q\subseteq X
.
If, for every
x\in X
,
\sigma(x)\in Q
,
then there is an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\min_{x\in X}\phi(x)
such that
x^{*}\in Q
.

Proof.

Let
x^{*}
be an optimal solution to
\min_{x\in X}\phi(x)
such that
x^{*}\not\in Q
. Then
\sigma(x^{*})
is also an optimal solution to
\min_{x\in X}\phi(x)
and
\sigma(x^{*})\in Q
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
S\neq\emptyset
,
X\subseteq\{0,1\}^{S}
,
\phi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\colon X\to X
an improving map.
Moreover, let
s\in S
and
\beta\in\{0,1\}
.
If for every
x\in X
,
\sigma(x)_{s}=\beta
,
then there is an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\min_{x\in X}\phi(x)
such that
x^{*}_{s}=\beta
.

Our construction starts from the elementary maps of
Lange et al. 2019
, i.e. the map
\sigma_{\delta(R)}
that cuts a set
R\subseteq S
from its complement, and the map
\sigma_{R}
that joins all sets intersecting with a set
R\subseteq S
:

Definition 3.5
.

For any finite, non-empty set
S
and
R\subseteq S
,
the
elementary cut map

\sigma_{\delta(R)}\colon X_{S}\to X_{S}
is such that for all
x\in X_{S}
and all

:

\sigma_{\delta(R)}(x)_{pq}:=\begin{cases}0&\textnormal{if $|\{p,q\}\cap R|=1$}\\
x_{pq}&\textnormal{otherwise}\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

(5)

Definition 3.6
.

For any finite, non-empty set
S
and
R\subseteq S
,
the
elementary join map

\sigma_{R}\colon X_{S}\to X_{S}
is such that for all
x\in X_{S}
and all
pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}
:

\sigma_{R}(x)_{pq}:=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{if $pq\in\tbinom{R}{2}$}\\
1&\textnormal{if $\forall p^{\prime}\in\{p,q\}\setminus R\;\exists q^{\prime}\in R\colon x_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}=1$}\\
x_{pq}&\textnormal{otherwise}\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

(6)

4.1
Cut Conditions

Here, we establish partial optimality conditions that imply the existence of an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
such that
x^{*}_{ij}=0
for some
ij\in\tbinom{S}{2}
or
x^{*}\in\{x\in X_{S}\mid x_{ij}x_{ik}x_{jk}=0\}

The following Proposition
4.1
generalizes to cubic objective functions the specialization for complete graphs of Theorem 1 of
Alush and Goldberger 2012
.
Intuitively, it says that if there exists a subset
R
for which joining any pair or triple that has some items in
R
and some outside of
R
leads to a penalty, then we can safely cut the whole set
R
from the rest.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S\neq\emptyset
, and let
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}_{S}}
.
If there exists

such that

\displaystyle c_{pq}

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\forall pq\in\delta(R)

\displaystyle c_{pqr}

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\forall pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}

(14)

then there is an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
such that
x^{*}_{ij}=0
for all
ij\in\delta(R)
.

Proof.

We define
\sigma\colon X_{S}\to X_{S}
such that for all
x\in X_{S}

\sigma(x):=\begin{cases}x&\text{ if }x_{ij}=0\>\>\forall ij\in\delta(R)\\
\sigma_{\delta(R)}(x)&\textnormal{otherwise}\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

For any

, let
x^{\prime}=\sigma(x)
.
Firstly, the map

is such that

.
Secondly, for any
x\in X_{S}
such that there exists

such that

\displaystyle\phi_{c}(x^{\prime})-\phi_{c}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}}c_{pqr}x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}-\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}c_{pq}x_{pq}

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}\cap T^{-}}c_{pqr}-\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)\cap P^{-}}c_{pq}

\displaystyle=0\kern 5.0pt.

The following

4.2
, together with

The idea behind this statement is the following: if there exists a pair

.

, and let

Let

\sigma\colon X_{S}\to X_{S}

(24)

For any
x\in X_{S}
, let
x^{\prime}=\sigma(x)
.
First of all, the map

.
Next, for any
x\in X_{S}
such that
x_{ij}=1

\displaystyle\phi_{c}(x^{\prime})-\phi_{c}(x)

\displaystyle=-c_{ij}-\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}}c_{pqr}x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}pq\in\delta(R)\\
pq\neq ij\end{subarray}}c_{pq}x_{pq}

(25)

\displaystyle\leq-c_{ij}+\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}}c_{pqr}^{-}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}pq\in\delta(R)\\

\displaystyle\leq 0\kern 5.0pt.

. ∎

4.2
Join Conditions

Next, we establish partial optimality conditions that imply the existence of an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
such that
x^{*}_{ij}=1
for some
ij\in\tbinom{S}{2}
.
This property can be used to simplify a given instance by joining the elements
i
and
j
.

As mentioned earlier, the following Proposition
4.4
transfers a result of
Lange et al. 2019
to the cubic set partition problem.
Condition (
35
) is rather restrictive.
The idea behind it is the following: if there exists a subset of items
R
that cuts
i
and
j
, and the total potential reward for joining
i
and
j
(meaning not only joining the pair

but also the triples that could end up together once

and its complement, then it is beneficial to put

.

Let
S\neq\emptyset
,
and let

.

.

x^{*}

\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}

Let
\sigma\colon X_{S}\to X_{S}

, we have

\sigma(x):=\begin{cases}x&\textnormal{if $x_{ij}=1$}\\

(36)

For any
x\in X_{S}
, let
x^{\prime}=\sigma(x)
.
The map
\sigma
is such that
x^{\prime}_{ij}=1
for all
x\in X_{S}
.
We show that
\sigma
is improving.
In particular, let
x\in X_{S}
such that
x_{ij}=0
.
We observe that

\displaystyle\phi_{c}(x^{\prime})-\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}}c_{pqr}\left(x^{\prime}_{pq}x^{\prime}_{pr}x^{\prime}_{qr}-x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}\right)+\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}c_{pq}\left(x^{\prime}_{pq}-x_{pq}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0\kern 5.0pt.

Proposition 4.5

Let

By construction of
R
, all coefficients on the left-hand side of (
130
) are non-positive.
By applying
Proposition

5.2
to the left-hand side of (
130
), this problem takes the form of a min cut problem with non-negative capacities that we solve exactly using the implementation in the C++ library
Boost 2022
of the algorithm by
Stoer and Wagner 1997
.

5.1
Reductions to Minimum Cut Problems

Here, we discuss how, for a given pair or triple, we reduce the search for subsets
R,R^{\prime}\subseteq S
that satisfy (
23
)–(
55
) maximally to the min
st
-cut problem.
In any of these cases, we have
S\neq\emptyset
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}_{S}}
such that
c_{pqr}\geq 0
for all
pqr\in\tbinom{S}{3}
, and
c_{pq}\geq 0
for all
pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}
.
Moreover, we have
i\in S
, a pair or triple
\{i\}\cup S_{0}\subseteq S
and a problem of the form

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\subseteq S\colon\\
i\in R,\\
j\not\in R,\forall j\in S_{0}\end{subarray}}\quad\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}}c_{pqr}+\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}c_{pq}\kern 5.0pt.

(133)

To begin with, we move costs of triples to costs of pairs:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
S\neq\emptyset
,
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}_{S}}
and
R\subseteq S
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}}c_{pqr}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}\sum_{r\in S\setminus\{p,q\}}c_{pqr}\kern 5.0pt.

(134)

Proof.

Let
R\subseteq S
.
Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{pqr\in T_{\delta(R)}}c_{pqr}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{R}{2}}\sum_{r\in S\setminus R}c_{pqr}+\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S\setminus R}{2}}\sum_{r\in R}c_{pqr}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in R}\sum_{q\in R\setminus\{p\}}\sum_{r\in S\setminus R}c_{pqr}

(135)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in S\setminus R}\sum_{q\in S\setminus\left(R\cup\{p\}\right)}\sum_{r\in R}c_{pqr}

(136)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in R}\sum_{q\in S\setminus R}\left(\sum_{r\in R\setminus\{p\}}c_{pqr}+\sum_{r\in S\setminus\left(R\cup\{q\}\right)}c_{pqr}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}\sum_{r\in S\setminus\{p,q\}}c_{pqr}.

(137)

∎

Consequently, (
133
) is equivalent to

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\subseteq S\colon\\
i\in R,\\
j\not\in R,\forall j\in S_{0}\end{subarray}}\quad\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}c^{\prime}_{pq},

(138)

where
c^{\prime}_{pq}=c_{pq}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in S\setminus\{p,q\}}c_{pqr}
for all
pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}
.
Next, we reduce (
138
) to a
quadratic unconstrained binary optimization problem
, by applying the following proposition:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
S\neq\emptyset
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\tbinom{S}{2}}
.
Moreover, let
i\in S
and
S_{0}\subseteq S\setminus\{i\}
.
Furthermore, let
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\left(S_{0}\cup\{i\}\right)
and
c^{\prime}\colon\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{2}\cup S^{\prime}\cup\{\emptyset\}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in S\setminus\{p\}}c_{pq}-2c_{pi}

\displaystyle\forall p\in S^{\prime}

(139)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{pq}

\displaystyle=-2c_{pq}

\displaystyle\forall pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{2}

(140)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in S\setminus\{i\}}c_{qi}\kern 5.0pt.

(141)

Then:

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\subseteq S\colon\\
i\in R,\\
j\not\in R,\forall j\in S_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}c_{pq}=\min_{y\in\{0,1\}^{S^{\prime}}}\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{2}}c^{\prime}_{pq}y_{p}y_{q}+\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}}c^{\prime}_{p}y_{p}+c^{\prime}_{\emptyset}\kern 5.0pt.

(142)

Proof.

Let
R\subseteq S
such that
i\in R
and
\forall j\in S_{0}\colon j\not\in R
.
We define
y\in\{0,1\}^{S}
such that
y=\mathbbm{1}_{R}
.
Then, we have
y_{i}=1
and
\forall j\in S_{0}\colon y_{j}=0
.
Moreover, it follows

\displaystyle\sum_{pq\in\delta(R)}c_{pq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}}c_{pq}\left(y_{p}(1-y_{q})+y_{q}(1-y_{p})\right)=\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}}c_{pq}\left(y_{p}+y_{q}-2y_{p}y_{q}\right)

(143)

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}}c_{pq}y_{p}y_{q}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in S\\
p\neq q\end{subarray}}c_{pq}y_{p}

(144)

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{2}}c_{pq}y_{p}y_{q}-2\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}}c_{pi}y_{p}+\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}}\sum_{q\in S\setminus\{p\}}c_{pq}y_{p}+\sum_{q\in S\setminus\{i\}}c_{qi}

(145)

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{2}}c_{pq}y_{p}y_{q}+\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}}\left(-2c_{pi}+\sum_{q\in S\setminus\{p\}}c_{pq}\right)y_{p}+\sum_{q\in S\setminus\{i\}}c_{qi}

(146)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{2}}c^{\prime}_{pq}y_{p}y_{q}+\sum_{p\in S^{\prime}}c^{\prime}_{p}y_{p}+c^{\prime}_{\emptyset}\kern 5.0pt.

(147)

This concludes the proof.
∎

For the instances of (
142
) that arise from testing (
23
)–(
55
), we have
\forall pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{2}\colon c^{\prime}_{pq}\leq 0
.
Thus, the right-hand side of (
142
) is
submodular
and can be minimized in strongly polynomial time
[
6
,
14
]
.
For completeness, we describe the reduction to an instance of min
st
-cut in detail in
Section

A.1
.

6
Combining Partial Optimality Conditions

Next, we discuss how we apply partial optimality conditions iteratively and why this requires special attention.
Let
S\neq\emptyset
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}_{S}}
.
Furthermore, let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\subseteq X_{S}
.
If there is an optimal solution
x^{*}_{1}\in X_{S}
to
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
such that
x^{*}_{1}\in Q_{1}
, and an optimal solution
x^{*}_{2}\in X_{S}
to
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
such that
x^{*}_{2}\in Q_{2}
, then there is not necessarily an optimal solution
x^{*}\in X_{S}
to
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
such that
x^{*}\in Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
.
For example, consider
S=\{1,2,3\}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}_{S}}
such that
c_{123}=5
,
c_{12}=c_{13}=c_{23}=-2
and
c_{\emptyset}=0
.
Then,
\min_{x\in X_{S}}\phi_{c}(x)=-2
.
Furthermore, let
Q_{1}=\{x\in X_{S}\mid x_{12}=1\}
and
Q_{2}=\{x\in X_{S}\mid x_{13}=1\}
.
It follows that
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}=\{x\in X_{S}\mid x_{12}=1,x_{13}=1\}=\{(1,1,1)\}
.
The set of optimal solutions is the set of all
x\in X_{S}
for which there is exactly one
pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}
with
x_{pq}=1
.
Thus, the feasible
x^{\prime}\in X_{S}
such that
x^{\prime}_{12}=x^{\prime}_{13}=x^{\prime}_{23}=1
is not optimal.

6.1
Cut Conditions

For any
T_{0}\subseteq\tbinom{S}{3}
,
P_{0}\subseteq\tbinom{S}{2}
, we define
X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}}\subseteq X_{S}
such that
x\in X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}}
if and only if
\forall pqr\in T_{0}\colon x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}=0
and
\forall pq\in P_{0}\colon x_{pq}=0
.
For any
R\subseteq S
, we have that
\sigma_{\delta(R)}
restricted to
X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}}
has image
X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}}
.
All our cut results use either
\sigma_{\delta(R)}
for some
R\subseteq S
or the identity.
Furthermore, the cut conditions do not change when applied to the restricted set
X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}}
.
Therefore, we can apply all our cut conditions simultaneously.
On the contrary,
\sigma_{R}
for some
R\subseteq S
restricted to
X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}}
does not necessarily have image
X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}}
, e.g. for the map
\sigma_{S}
we have
\sigma_{S}(X_{S}|_{T_{0},P_{0}})=\{\mathbbm{1}_{S}\}
.
Therefore, we cannot expect that partial optimality statements would hold when applying any join condition together with any another condition.

6.2
Join Conditions

Let us assume the existence of an optimal solution
x^{*}
to
\textnormal{SP3}_{S,c}
such that
x^{*}_{ij}=1
for some
ij\in\tbinom{S}{2}
.
We define
X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}=\{x\in X_{S}\mid x_{ij}=1\}
.
Then, we have

\min_{x\in X_{S}}\phi_{c}(x)=\min_{x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}}\phi_{c}(x)\kern 5.0pt.

(148)

Let
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{j\}
.
Now, we relate feasible vectors of
X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}
to feasible vectors of
X_{S^{\prime}}
.
We observe that for any
x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}
we have
\forall p\in S\setminus\{i,j\}\colon x_{pi}=x_{pj}
.
We define
\varphi_{ij}\colon X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}\to X_{S^{\prime}}
as

\displaystyle\varphi_{ij}(x)_{pi}

\displaystyle=x_{pi}

\displaystyle\forall x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}\quad\forall p\in S^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}\phantom{\kern 5.0pt..}

(149)

\displaystyle\varphi_{ij}(x)_{pq}

\displaystyle=x_{pq}

\displaystyle\forall x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}\quad\forall pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}}{2}\kern 5.0pt.

(150)

It is easy to see that
\varphi_{ij}
is bijective.
Proposition
6.1
below shows that solving the right-hand side of (
148
) is equivalent to solving a smaller instance of the original problem.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
S\neq\emptyset
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}_{S}}
.
Moreover, let
ij\in\tbinom{S}{2}
and
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{j\}
, and let
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{I}_{S^{\prime}}}
such that

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{pqr}

\displaystyle=c_{pqr}

\displaystyle\forall pqr\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}}{3}

(151)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{pqi}

\displaystyle=c_{pqi}+c_{pqj}

\displaystyle\forall pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}}{2}

(152)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{pq}

\displaystyle=c_{pq}

\displaystyle\forall pq\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}}{2}

(153)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{pi}

\displaystyle=c_{pi}+c_{pj}+c_{pij}

\displaystyle\forall p\in S^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}

(154)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+c_{ij}\kern 5.0pt.

(155)

Furthermore, let
\varphi_{ij}\colon X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}\to X_{S^{\prime}}
be the map that relates feasible vectors of
X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}
to feasible vectors of
X_{S^{\prime}}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}}\phi_{c}(x)=\min_{x\in X_{S^{\prime}}}\phi_{c^{\prime}}(x)\kern 5.0pt.

(156)

Moreover, if
x^{*}\in\argmin_{x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}}\phi_{c}(x)
, then
\varphi_{ij}(x^{*})\in\argmin_{x\in X_{S^{\prime}}}\phi_{c^{\prime}}(x)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}
.
We show that
\phi_{c}(x)=\phi_{c^{\prime}}(\varphi_{ij}(x))
.
Let
x^{\prime}=\varphi_{ij}(x)
.
We use the fact that
x_{pi}=x_{pj}
for all
p\in S\setminus\{i,j\}
, and
x_{ij}=1
.
It follows

\displaystyle\phi_{c^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{pqr\in\tbinom{S^{\prime}}{3}}c^{\prime}_{pqr}x^{\prime}_{pq}x^{\prime}_{pr}x^{\prime}_{qr}+\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S}{2}}c^{\prime}_{pq}x^{\prime}_{pq}+c^{\prime}_{\emptyset}

(157)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{2}}c^{\prime}_{pqi}x^{\prime}_{pi}x^{\prime}_{qi}x^{\prime}_{pq}+\sum_{pqr\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{3}}c^{\prime}_{pqr}x^{\prime}_{pq}x^{\prime}_{pr}x^{\prime}_{qr}+\sum_{p\in S\setminus\{i,j\}}c^{\prime}_{pi}x^{\prime}_{pi}

(158)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{V\setminus\{i,j\}}{2}}c^{\prime}_{pq}x^{\prime}_{pq}+c^{\prime}_{\emptyset}

(159)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{2}}(c_{pqi}+c_{pqj})x_{pi}x_{qi}x_{pq}+\sum_{pqr\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{3}}c_{pqr}x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}+\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{2}}c_{pq}x_{pq}

(160)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{p\in S\setminus\{i,j\}}(c_{pi}+c_{pj}+c_{pij})x_{pi}+c^{\prime}_{\emptyset}

(161)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{2}}c_{pqi}x_{pi}x_{qi}x_{pq}+\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{2}}c_{pqj}x_{pj}x_{qj}x_{pq}+\sum_{pqr\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{3}}c_{pqr}x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}

(162)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{p\in V}c_{pij}x_{ij}x_{pi}x_{pj}+\sum_{pq\in\tbinom{S\setminus\{i,j\}}{2}}c_{pq}x_{pq}+\sum_{p\in V\setminus\{i,j\}}c_{pi}x_{pi}+\sum_{p\in S\setminus\{i,j\}}c_{pj}x_{pj}+c_{ij}x_{ij}+c_{\emptyset}

(163)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pqr\in\tbinom{S}{3}}c_{pqr}x_{pq}x_{pr}x_{qr}+\sum_{pq\in S}c_{pq}x_{pq}+c_{\emptyset}=\phi_{c}(x).

(164)

Therefore, we have

\min_{x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}}\phi_{c}(x)=\min_{x\in X_{S}|_{x_{ij}=1}}\phi_{c^{\prime}}(\varphi_{ij}(x))=\min_{x\in X_{S^{\prime}}}\phi_{c^{\prime}}(x)

(165)

This concludes the proof.
∎

6.3
Mixing Cut and Join Conditions

Here, we describe how we apply the partial optimality properties recursively. As soon as a condition leads to a smaller instance, we start the procedure again on the smaller set (in case of a join) or sets (in case of a cut).
Firstly, we apply Proposition
4.1
, which leaves us with independent sub-problems.
Secondly, we apply our join conditions until we find a pair
ij\in\tbinom{S}{2}
or triplet
ijk\in\tbinom{S}{3}
to join, starting from
Corollary

4.12
and then moving on to
Propositions

4.4
and
4.6
,
Corollaries

4.9
and
4.10
and
Proposition

4.5
, in this order.
Thirdly, we apply the remaining cut conditions, which can be applied jointly, as we have seen in
Section

6.1
.
We remark, that the order in which we apply our join conditions is arbitrary, and we do not claim it to be optimal.

8
Conclusion

We establish partial optimality conditions for the cubic set partition problem, which can be seen as the special case of cubic correlation clustering for complete graphs.
In particular, we generalize all such conditions known for correlation clustering with a linear objective function to arbitrary cubic objective functions.
In addition, we establish new partial optimality conditions.
Furthermore, we define and implement exact algorithms and heuristics for testing all established conditions efficiently.
Lastly, we quantify the effect of these algorithms on two datasets.
Regarding these numerical experiments, we note that all cut conditions are effective on the tested datasets, whereas join conditions pose a bigger challenge.
In fact,
Proposition

4.11
, in its simplified form of
Corollary

4.12
, is the only join property that is beneficial in our numerical experiments.
Yet, in almost all cases, it is the one statement that fixes the most variables (
Figures

5
and
6
).
We remark that
Corollary

4.12
is one of the newly proposed conditions.
Perspectives for future work include the exploitation of sparsity of non-zero cost coefficients, as well as applications to subspace clustering and object recognition.

The classical framework of the monotone operator theory for SPDE with coefficients satisfying the local monotoncity conditions was extended in

The biggest drawback of this approach is that it cannot be extend in case of general multiplicative noise. Definition 2.1

Let us consider a measurable space

\displaystyle\pi:(\Omega,\mathscr{F})\to(\mathtt{M}_{\bar{\mathbb{N}}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathrm{Z}),\mathcal{M}_{\bar{\mathbb{N}}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathrm{Z}))

such that

(1)

(2)

the measure
\pi
is independently scattered, that is, if the sets

are disjoint, then the random variables
\pi(B_{i}),i=1,\ldots,m

(3)

for all

-valued process

defined by

\displaystyle N(t,K):=\pi((0,t]\times K),\;t\geq 0

Let us assume

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}(t,S):=\pi((0,1]\times S)-t\lambda(S),\;t\in(0,T]

2.2.
Assumptions

We start with some basic definitions related to the operators.

Definition 2.2
.

An operator
\mathrm{A}
from
\mathbb{V}
to
\mathbb{V}^{*}

if the following condition holds: for any sequence

\mathbb{V}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\to\infty}\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}),\boldsymbol{u}_{m}-\boldsymbol{u}\rangle\geq 0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}),\boldsymbol{u}_{m}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle\leq\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle,\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V}.

Remark 2.3
.

We know that if an operator
\mathrm{A}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}^{*}
is a bounded operator, that is,
\mathrm{A}
takes every bounded bounded set of
\mathbb{V}
to a bounded set of
\mathbb{V}^{*}
, then the pseudo-monotonicity of the operator
\mathrm{A}
is equivalent to the following condition:

For any sequence
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}\}
with the weak limit
\boldsymbol{u}
in
\mathbb{V}
and

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\to\infty}\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}),\boldsymbol{u}_{m}-\boldsymbol{u}\rangle\geq 0,

imply that
\{\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m})\}
converges to
\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u})
in weak-star topology of
\mathbb{V}^{*}
and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u}_{m}),\boldsymbol{u}_{m}\rangle=\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{u}\rangle.

The interested readers are refereed to Proposition 27.7 in
[
60
]
and Remark 5.2.12 in
[
31
]
.

Now, we introduce assumptions on the coefficients
\mathrm{A}
,
\mathrm{B}
and
\gamma
.

Hypothesis 2.4
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\beta\in(1,\infty)
.

(H.1)

(Hemi continuity). The map
\mathbb{R}\ni\lambda\mapsto\langle\mathrm{A}(t,\boldsymbol{u}+\lambda\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{w}\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
is continuous for any
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{V}
and for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
.

(H.2)

(Local monotonicity). There exist non negative constants
\zeta
and
C
such that for any
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V}
and a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle 2\langle\mathrm{A}(t,\boldsymbol{u})-\mathrm{A}(t,\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle+

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{B}(t,\boldsymbol{u})-\mathrm{B}(t,\boldsymbol{v})\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathrm{Z}}\|\gamma(t,\boldsymbol{u},z)-\gamma(t,\boldsymbol{v},z)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\lambda(\mathrm{d}z)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq\big[f(t)+\rho(\boldsymbol{u})+\eta(\boldsymbol{v})\big]\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

\displaystyle|\rho(\boldsymbol{u})|+|\eta(\boldsymbol{u})|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta})(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\zeta}),

where
\rho
and
\eta
are two measurable functions from
\mathbb{V}
to
\mathbb{R}
.

(H.2)
′

(General local monotonicity). For any constant

, there exists a function

(2.6)

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}(t,\boldsymbol{u})-\mathrm{A}(t,\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle\leq M_{t}(r)\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

(H.3)

(Coercivity). There exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{V}
and a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle 2\langle\mathrm{A}(t,\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{u}\rangle\leq f(t)(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2})-C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta}.

(H.4)

(Growth). There exist non-negative constants
\alpha
and
C
such that for any
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{V}
and a.e.
t\in[0,T]

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}(t,\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\mathbb{V}^{*}}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\leq(f(t)+C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{\beta})(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\alpha}).

(H.5)

For any sequence
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
and
\boldsymbol{u}
in
\mathbb{V}
with

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{B}(t,\boldsymbol{u}_{m})-\mathrm{B}(t,\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}\to 0,\ \text{ for a.e. }\ t\in[0,T].

Moreover, there exists

such that for any
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{V}
and a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{B}(t,\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\mathrm{L}_{2}}^{2}\leq g(t)(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}).

(H.6)

The jump noise coefficient
\gamma(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
satisfy:

(1)

The function
\gamma\in\mathfrak{L}^{2}_{\lambda,T}(\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{I},\mathrm{d}\otimes\mathbb{P}\otimes\lambda;\mathbb{H})

(2)

For any sequence
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
and
\boldsymbol{u}

\mathbb{V}
with
\|\boldsymbol{u}_{m}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\to 0

m\to\infty

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{Z}}\|\gamma(t,\boldsymbol{u}_{m},z)-\gamma(t,\boldsymbol{u},z)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\lambda(\mathrm{d}z)\to 0,\ \text{for a.e. }\ t\in[0,T].

(3)

(Growth). There exists functions

such that for any
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{V}
, a.e.
t\in[0,T]

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{Z}}\|\gamma(t,\boldsymbol{u},z)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{p}\lambda(\mathrm{d}z)\leq h_{p}(t)(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{p}).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{Y},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{Y}})
be a separable Banach space and let
\{\mathbf{Y}_{m}\}
be a sequence of
\mathbb{Y}
-valued random variables. Assume that for every
\epsilon,\eta>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that for every sequence
\{\tau_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathscr{F}
-stopping times with
\tau_{m}\leq T
, one has

(3.40)

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{0<\xi\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}((\tau_{m}+\xi)\wedge T)-\mathbf{Y}_{m}(\tau_{m})\|_{\mathbb{Y}}\geq\eta}}\right\}\leq\epsilon.

In this case, we say that the sequence
\{\mathbf{Y}_{m}\}
satisfies the Aldous condition.

If a sequence
\{\mathbf{Y}_{m}\}
satisfies the Aldous condition in the space
\mathbb{Y}
, then the laws of
\{\mathbf{Y}_{m}\}
form a tight sequence on
\mathrm{D}([0,T];\mathbb{Y})
endowed with the Skorokhod topology. By the Chebyshev inequality, the following result provides a sufficient condition for the verification of Aldous condition.

Lemma 3.4
.

Given any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that

(3.41)

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{0<\xi\leq\delta}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}((\tau_{m}+\xi)\wedge T)-\mathbf{Y}_{m}(\tau_{m})\|_{\mathbb{Y}}^{\zeta}\big]\leq\varepsilon,

for some
\zeta>0
, then the sequence
\{\mathbf{Y}_{m}\}
satisfies the Aldous condition in the space
\mathbb{Y}
.

One can see Theorem 13.2,
[
58
]
, where the author used a similar condition in the above lemma for the deterministic setting. Interested readers can see the works
[
36
,
45
]
, etc., where the authors established a similar condition to verify the Aldous condition for the stochastic setting.

Appendix A
Some Useful Results

In this section, we state some useful results for the tightness of the laws as well as the well-known Skorokhod’s representation theorem. Let
(\mathbb{Y},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Y}))
be a separable and complete metric space

Definition A.1
.

A family of probability measures
M
on
(\mathbb{Y},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Y}))
is tight if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
K_{\epsilon}\subset E
such that
\mu(K_{\epsilon})\geq 1-\epsilon
for all
\mu\in M
. A sequence of measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
on
(\mathbb{Y},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Y}))
is weakly convergent to a measure
\mu
if for all continuous and bounded functions
h
on
\mathbb{Y}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{Y}}h(x)\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{Y}}h(x)\mu(x)\mathrm{d}x.

Lemma A.2

(Prokhorov Theorem, section 5,
[
8
]
)
.

A sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
on
(\mathbb{Y},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Y}))
is tight if and only if it is relatively compact, that is, there exists a subsequence
\{\mu_{n_{k}}\}
which converges weakly to a probability measure
\mu
.

Lemma A.3

(Theorem 5,
[
56
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\mathbb{V},\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be Banach spaces satisfying the embedding
\mathbb{V}\subset\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{Y}
. Suppose that the embedding
\mathbb{V}\subset\mathbb{H}
is compact. If
\Gamma
is a bounded subset of
\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{V})
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\sup_{f\in\Gamma}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\|f(t+\delta)-f(t)\|_{\mathbb{Y}}^{p}\mathrm{d}t=0,

then
\Gamma
is a relatively compact subset of
\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
.

Based on the previous lemma, the following result has been established in the work
[
51
]
which prove the tightness of the laws in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
.

Lemma A.4

(Lemma 5.2,
[
51
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\mathbb{V},\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be Banach spaces satisfying the embedding
\mathbb{V}\subset\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{Y}
. Suppose that the embedding
\mathbb{V}\subset\mathbb{H}
is compact. Let
\{\mathbf{Y}_{m}\}
be a sequence of the stochastic processes. If

(A.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{p}\mathrm{d}t>N\bigg]=0,

and for any
\epsilon>0
,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\|\mathbf{Y}_{m}(t+\delta)-\mathbf{Y}_{m}(t)\|_{\mathbb{Y}}^{p}\mathrm{d}t>\epsilon\bigg]=0.

Then,
\{\mathbf{Y}_{m}\}
is tight in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
.

Let us now recall the following version of the Skorokhod’s representation theorem.

Theorem A.5

(Theorem A.1,
[
45
]
, Theorem C.1,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
\mathbb{Y}^{1},\mathbb{Y}^{2}
be two complete separable metric spaces. Let
\chi_{m}:\Omega\to\mathbb{Y}^{1}\times\mathbb{Y}^{2},\;m\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sequence of weakly convergent random variables with laws
\{\rho_{m}\}
. For
j=1,2
let
\Pi_{j}:\mathbb{Y}^{1}\times\mathbb{Y}^{2}\to\mathbb{Y}^{j}
denote the natural projection, that is,

\displaystyle\Pi_{j}(\chi^{1},\chi^{2})=\chi^{j},\ \text{ for all }\ (\chi^{1},\chi^{2})\in\mathbb{Y}^{1}\times\mathbb{Y}^{2}.

Finally, we assume that the random variables
\Pi_{1}(\chi_{m})=\chi_{m}^{1}
on
\mathbb{Y}^{1}
have the same law, independent of
m
.

Then, there exists a family of
\mathbb{Y}^{1}\times\mathbb{Y}^{2}
-valued random variables
\{\widehat{\chi}_{m}\}
on the probability space
(\widehat{\Omega},\widehat{\mathscr{F}},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}):=\big([0,1)\times[0,1),\mathcal{B}([0,1)\times[0,1)),\text{ Lebesgue measure}\big)
and a random variable
\widehat{\chi}_{\infty}
on
(\widehat{\Omega},\widehat{\mathscr{F}},\widehat{\mathbb{P}})
such that the following statements hold:

(1)

The law of
\{\widehat{\chi}_{m}\}

\{\rho_{m}\}

(2)

\widehat{\chi}_{m}\to\widehat{\chi}_{\infty}

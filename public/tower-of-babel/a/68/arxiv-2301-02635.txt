Abstract
. Let A A be a Noetherian local ring with canonical module K A \mathrm{K}_{A} . We characterize A A when K A \mathrm{K}_{A} is a torsionless, reflexive, or q q -torsionfree module for an integer q ≥ 3 q\geq 3 . If A A is a Cohen-Macaulay ring, H.-B. Foxby proved in 1974 that the A A -module K A \mathrm{K}_{A} is q q -torsionfree if and only if the ring A A is q q -Gorenstein. With mild assumptions, we provide a generalization of Foxby’s result to arbitrary Noetherian local rings admitting the canonical module. In particular, since the reflexivity of the canonical module is closely related to the ring being Gorenstein in low codimension, we also explore quasi-normal rings, introduced by W. V. Vasconcelos. We provide several examples as well.

1.
Introduction

This paper investigates the question of the structure of a Noetherian local ring
A
if its canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a torsionless, reflexive, or more generally,
q
-torsionfree
A
-module for an integer
q\geq 3
.
The notion of
q
-torsionfree modules was one of the central contributions of the famous research of M. Auslander and M. Bridger
[
1
]
, which was succeeded by H.-B. Foxby
[
11
]
to be a striking study of
q
-Gorenstein rings.
Among many interesting results, Foxby settled the above question in the case where
A
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring. More precisely, the
A
-module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
q
-torsionfree if and only if the ring
A
is
q
-Gorenstein, i.e.,
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Gorenstein ring for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A
with
\operatorname{depth}A_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
(see
[
10
, Proposition 3.2]
).
It remains unclear what happens if we do not assume the ring
A
is Cohen-Macaulay.
The theory of canonical modules nowadays has been developed mainly over Cohen-Macaulay rings in connection with the Gorenstein property; see e.g.,
[
6
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
19
]
.
However, over Noetherian local (not necessarily Cohen-Macaulay) rings, there are also remarkable preceding researches on canonical modules, including the study of their endomorphism algebras; see
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
.
Therefore, behaviors of canonical modules, even for non-Cohen-Macaulay rings, are interesting and the
q
-torsionfree property is well worth studying.
The motivation for the present research started with this question that arose while the second and fourth authors were writing the last paper with Vasconcelos concerning (torsionless) canonical modules
[
4
]
.

To explain our results more precisely, let us start from definitions which we will use throughout this paper. For a Noetherian local ring
A
of dimension
d
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, a
canonical module

K
of
A
is a finitely generated
A
-module satisfying

\widehat{A}\otimes_{A}K\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{A}}(\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}}(\widehat{A}),\widehat{E})

where
\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}}(\widehat{A})
denotes the
d^{\mbox{\tiny th}}
local cohomology module of the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\widehat{A}
of
A
with respect to its maximal ideal
{\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}}
and
{\displaystyle\widehat{E}}
is the injective hull of the
\widehat{A}
-module
\widehat{A}/\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}
(
[
14
, Definition 5.6]
).
Equivalently, a finitely generated
A
-module
K
is a canonical module of
A
if
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(K,E)\cong\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)}
, where
\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)
is the
d^{\mbox{\tiny th}}
local cohomology module of
A
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
and
{\displaystyle E}
is the injective hull of
A/\mathfrak{m}
(
[
5
, Definition 12.1.2, Remarks 12.1.3]
).
The canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is uniquely determined up to isomorphisms (
[
2
, (1.5)]
, see also
[
14
, Lemma 5.8]
) if it exists. Although the existence is not guaranteed even for Cohen-Macaulay local domains, provided
A
is Cohen-Macaulay, the ring
A
admits the canonical module if and only if
A
is a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein ring (
[
16
,
18
]
).
The fundamental theory of canonical modules over Cohen-Macaulay rings was developed in the monumental book
[
14
]
of J. Herzog and E. Kunz. We shall in this paper freely refer to
[
14
]
for basic results on canonical modules (see
[
6
, Chapter 3]
also).

We now continue to state our setup. Let
R
be a Noetherian (not necessarily local) ring. For an
R
-module
M
, we have a canonical homomorphism

\varphi:M\rightarrow M^{**}

defined by
{\displaystyle\big[\varphi(x)\big](f)=f(x)}
for each
{\displaystyle f\in M^{*}}
and
x\in M
, where
(-)^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,R)
denotes the
R
-dual functor. We say that
M
is
torsionless
(resp.
reflexive
) if
\varphi
is injective (resp. bijective). Torsionless modules are torsionfree, i.e., there is no nonzero torsion elements, and the converse holds if the total ring of fractions
\mathrm{Q}(R)
of
R
is Gorenstein (
[
21
, Theorem (A.1)]
).
Moreover, the
R
-module
M
is torsionless (resp. reflexive) if and only if
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(\mathrm{D}(M),R)=(0)
for
i=1
(resp.
i=1,2
), where
\mathrm{D}(M)
denotes the Auslander transpose of
M
(
[
1
]
). From this point of view, Auslander and Bridger introduced a
q
-torsionfree module

M
to be
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(\mathrm{D}(M),R)=(0)
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,q
.
In addition, for an integer
n
, we say that

•

M
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{n})
if
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\left\{n,\,\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right\}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
,

•

M
satisfies
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{n})
if
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\left\{n,\,\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right\}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
,

•

R
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{n})
if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
with
{\displaystyle\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq n}
,

•

R
satisfies
(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{n})
if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
with
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq n}
.

The condition
(\mathrm{S}_{n})
is known as Serre’s condition.
A Noetherian ring satisfying
(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{n})
coincides with
(n+1)
-Gorenstein ring in earlier publications such as
[
1
,
11
]
. The condition
(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{n})
is equivalent to saying that the ring satisfies both
(\mathrm{S}_{n+1})
and
(\mathrm{G}_{n})
.

Let us now state our results, explaining how this paper is organized.
In Section
2
, after recalling the necessary definitions and preliminaries, we give a criterion for a Noetherian local ring
A
to have the torsionless canonical module. We show that the
A
-module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionless if and only if
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A
, where
\operatorname{Assh}A=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A\mid\dim A/\mathfrak{p}=\dim A\}=\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}
(Proposition
2.3
).
Section
3
is devoted to the characterizations of local rings
A
with reflexive canonical modules. When
\dim A=1
, this is exactly the case where
A
is a Gorenstein ring (Proposition
3.3
). We elaborate on the one-dimensional case in Section
5
. For the higher dimensional case, the reflexivity of
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is characterized by the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
being Gorenstein for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{A}K_{A}
with
\dim A_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq 1
and
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
, where
U
denotes the unmixed component of
(0)
in
A
and
V(U)
is the set of all prime ideals in
A
containing
U
(Theorem
3.6
). This lead us to obtain Corollary
3.8
, which claims that
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive if and only if
A
satisfies
({\mathrm{G}}_{1})
, provided
\operatorname{Assh}A=\operatorname{Ass}A
. This indicates that the reflexivity of canonical modules is deeply related to the ring being Gorenstein in low codimension. Thus Section
4
is dedicated to quasi-normal rings, i.e., rings with
(\mathrm{S}_{2})
and
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
, which have been introduced by Vasconcelos.
In Section
6
, we generalize Foxby’s result on
q
-torsionfree canonical modules to arbitrary Noetherian local rings
A
admitting a canonical module. Our results of Sections
2
and
3
provide a complete generalization in case
q=1,2
. When
q\geq 3
, Theorem
6.6
states that the
A
-module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
q
-torsionfree if and only if the ring
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{q-1})
and
(\mathrm{S}_{q-1})
on
\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}
, provided that
\mathrm{K}_{A}
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
. In the final section we provide concrete examples of Cohen-Macaulay and
q
-Gorenstein rings in order to illustrate our theorems.

2.
Torsionless canonical modules

Throughout the section, let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
. We begin with some preliminaries. Let
{\displaystyle(0)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}A}Q(\mathfrak{p})}
denote a primary decomposition of
(0)
in
A
. We set

\operatorname{Assh}A=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A\mid\dim A/\mathfrak{p}=d\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad U=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A}Q(\mathfrak{p})

where
U
is called the
unmixed component
of
(0)
in
A
. Let
V(U)
denote the set of all prime ideals of
A
containing
U
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There is an embedding
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow A/U\rightarrow A}
of
A
-modules.

Proof.

We may assume that
U\neq(0)
. Then
\operatorname{Assh}A\subsetneq\operatorname{Ass}A
. Let

L=\bigcap_{\tiny\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}A\setminus\operatorname{Assh}A}Q(\mathfrak{p}).

We then have
{\displaystyle L\not\subseteq\bigcup_{\tiny\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A}\mathfrak{p}}
. Choose an element
a\in L
but
{\displaystyle a\not\in\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A}\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
a
is a non-zerodivisor on
A/U
and
aU\subseteq L\cap U=(0)
, we obtain
((0):_{A}a)=U
. Then
U
is the kernel of the homomorphism
\varphi:A\rightarrow A
given by
\varphi(1)=a
. Thus,
A/U\cong\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\hookrightarrow A
.
∎

In the rest of this section, we assume the ring
A
admits the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
.
We recall several known facts about
\mathrm{K}_{A}
which we will use throughout this article; see
[
2
, (1.6), (1.7), (1.8), (1.9), (1.10), Theorem 3.2]
and
[
14
, Korollar 6.3]
(also
[
5
, Chapter 12]
) for the proofs.

Proposition 2.2
.

The following assertions hold true.

(1)

The annihilator of
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
U
. In particular,
{\dim_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}=d}
and
{\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}=\operatorname{Assh}A}
.

(2)

If
{\displaystyle a\in\mathfrak{m}}
is
A
-regular, then
a
is
\mathrm{K}_{A}
-regular.

(3)

V(U)=\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A\mid\dim A=\dim A/\mathfrak{p}+\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}\}
.

(4)

Both
\mathrm{K}_{A}
and
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{K}_{A},\mathrm{K}_{A})}
satisfy
(\mathrm{S}_{2})
.

(5)

{\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}}
for every
{\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}}
.

(6)

\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}=\operatorname{Spec}A
if and only if
\operatorname{Min}A=\operatorname{Assh}A
.

(7)

\operatorname{Ass}A=\operatorname{Assh}A
if and only if
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a faithful
A
-module.

(8)

Suppose
A
is Cohen-Macaulay. If
a\in\mathfrak{m}
is
A
-regular, then
\mathrm{K}_{A/(a)}
exists and
\mathrm{K}_{A/(a)}\cong\mathrm{K}_{A}/a\mathrm{K}_{A}
.

(9)

Suppose that
\mathrm{K}_{A/U}
exists. Then
\mathrm{K}_{A/U}
, as an
A
-module, is the canonical module of
A
.

By Proposition
2.2
-(2), the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionfree as an
A
-module. In general, torsionless modules are torsionfree, and the converse holds if and only if
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Gorenstein local ring for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}A
(
[
21
, Theorem (A.1)]
). Therefore, if the total ring of fractions
\mathrm{Q}(A)
of
A
is Gorenstein, then
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionless. The following proposition shows the case where
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionless without assuming
\mathrm{Q}(A)
is Gorenstein. It is also a generalization of
[
4
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a torsionless
A
-module
;

(2)

A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Gorenstein ring for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A
;

(3)

\mathrm{K}_{A}\cong I
for some ideal
I
of
A
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Since
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionless, there exists an exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathrm{K}_{A}\rightarrow F
of
A
-modules, where
F
is a finitely generated free
A
-module.
Let
{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}}
. Then
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Artinian and
\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the canonical module of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Therefore, we may assume that
\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the injective hull of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The splitting monomorphism
0\rightarrow\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow F_{\mathfrak{p}}
induces that
\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a direct summand of the free
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Artinian, by the Matlis duality, we have
\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Hence
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Gorenstein ring.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) Let
{W=A\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A}\mathfrak{p}}
.
By assumption,
\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong A_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A
. Thus,
{\displaystyle W^{-1}\mathrm{K}_{A}\cong W^{-1}A}
. Moreover, we have
{\displaystyle W^{-1}A\cong W^{-1}(A/U)}
because
W^{-1}U=(0)
by the proof of Lemma
2.1
. Since every element of
W
is a non-zerodivisor on both
\mathrm{K}_{A}
and
A/U
, the isomorphism
{\displaystyle W^{-1}\mathrm{K}_{A}\cong W^{-1}(A/U)}
induces the embedding
{\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{A}\hookrightarrow A/U}
. By Lemma
2.1
, there is an embedding
{\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{A}\hookrightarrow A}
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) is clear.
∎

If
A
is
reduced
, which means there are no nonzero nilpotents, then the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a field for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}A
. Hence we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
A
is a reduced ring, then
\mathrm{K}_{A}\cong I
for some ideal
I
of
A
.

We recall that if
A
is Cohen-Macaulay, the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
has rank one if and only if the ring
A
is
generically Gorenstein
, i.e.,
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Gorenstein local ring for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Min}A
. When one of the equivalent conditions of
[
6
, Proposition 3.3.18]
is satisfied, the canonical module can be identified with an ideal of
A
(see also
[
14
, Satz 6.21]
).
The example below shows that
the assumption
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A
is necessary for Proposition
2.3
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
S=k[\![X,Y,Z]\!]
be the formal power series ring over a field
k
and set
A=S/[(X)\cap(Y,Z)^{2}]
. Let
x,y,z
denote the images of
X,Y,Z
in
A
, respectively. Then we have

U=(x)
and
\mathrm{K}_{A}\cong A/(x)\cong y^{2}A
.

By Proposition
2.3
,
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionless. However,
A
is
not generically Gorenstein. In fact,
A_{\mathfrak{q}}
is not a Gorenstein ring for
\mathfrak{q}=(y,z)\in\operatorname{Min}A
.

3.
Reflexive canonical modules

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
admitting the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
. We denote by
U
the unmixed component of
(0)
in
A
.
In this section we will show how the reflexivity of the canonical module is related to the Gorensteinness of the ring. We begin with the following simple but effective lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive. Then
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
.
In particular, if
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive and
\operatorname{depth}A=0
, then
\dim A=0
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from

V(U)\cap\operatorname{Ass}A=\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}^{*}\cap\operatorname{Ass}A=\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{K}_{A}^{*},A)=\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}^{**}=\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}=\operatorname{Assh}A.\qed

The example below shows that the reflexivity of
\mathrm{K}_{A}
may require a rather strong restriction on
A
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
S=k[\![X,Y]\!]
be the formal power series ring over a field
k
and set
A=S/[(X)\cap(X^{2},Y)]
. Let
x,y
denote the images of
X,Y
in
A
, respectively. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y)
be the maximal ideal of
A
. Then we have
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Assh}A=\left\{(x)\right\}}
,
{\displaystyle U=(x)}
, and
{\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{A}=A/U}
.

(1)

Let
\mathfrak{p}=(x)
. Since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a field, by Proposition
2.3
,
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionless.

(2)

Since
\operatorname{depth}A=0
and
\dim A=1
, by Lemma
3.1
,
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is not reflexive.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
d=1
.
Then
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module if and only if
A
is a Gorenstein ring.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive. By Lemma
3.1
,
A
is Cohen-Macaulay.
Since
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive, there exists an exact sequence

{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathrm{K}_{A}\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}}
, where
F_{0},F_{1}
are finite free
A
-modules
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
.
Let
a\in\mathfrak{m}
be an
A
-regular element. Since
A
is Cohen-Macaulay,
\mathrm{K}_{A}/a\mathrm{K}_{A}
is the canonical module of
A/aA
. Moreover, the embedding
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathrm{K}_{A}/a\mathrm{K}_{A}\rightarrow F_{1}/aF_{1}}
proves that
K_{A/aA}
is torsionless. Therefore, by Proposition
2.3
,
A/aA
is a Gorenstein ring. Thus,
A
is a Gorenstein ring. The converse is clear.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

There exist non-Cohen-Macaulay local rings with reflexive canonical module. Example
6.2
shows a two-dimensional non-Cohen-Macaulay local ring
A
with
\mathrm{K}_{A}
reflexive. The example also shows that, even if
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive, the equality
\operatorname{Ass}A=\operatorname{Assh}A
does not hold true in general.

Recall that a finitely generated
A
-module
M
is reflexive, i.e., the canonical map
\varphi:M\to M^{**}
is an isomorphism, if and only if there is at least one isomorphism
M\cong M^{**}
of
A
-modules.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that there is an exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{K}_{A}\to\mathrm{K}_{A}^{**}\to C\to 0

of
A
-modules. If
C\neq(0)
, then
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring with
\dim A_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}C
with
\operatorname{depth}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 1
. In particular,
\mathfrak{p}\in V(U)
and
U_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}C
such that
\operatorname{depth}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 1
. Then
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}=V(U)
.
Since
\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, by passing to the ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we may assume
\operatorname{depth}A>0
and
\operatorname{depth}_{A}C=0
. Since
\operatorname{Ass}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}^{**}\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}A
, we have

\operatorname{depth}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}^{**}\geq 1
. From the exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\to\mathrm{K}_{A}\to\mathrm{K}_{A}^{**}\to C\to 0}
, we obtain

0=\operatorname{depth}_{A}C\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}-1,\;\operatorname{depth}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}^{**}\}.

Thus,
\operatorname{depth}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}=1
. Since
\mathrm{K}_{A}
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{2})
, we have

1=\operatorname{depth}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}\geq\min\{2,\;\dim A\}.

Therefore,
A
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
1
. In particular,
U=(0)
.
∎

Now we aim to generalize Proposition
3.3
.

Theorem 3.6
.

The following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module
;

(2)

\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
, and
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}
with
\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}
with
\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
. If
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A
, then
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein by Proposition
2.3
. Otherwise, we have
\dim A_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
. Since
\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is a reflexive
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module, the ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein by Proposition
3.3
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
Since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A
, by Proposition
2.3
,
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is torsionless. Hence we have the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathrm{K}_{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{K}_{A}^{**}\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0

of
A
-modules, where
\varphi
is the canonical homomorphism.
Suppose that
C\neq(0)
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{A}C
.
Note that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}
and
\big[\mathrm{K}_{A}\big]_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
If
\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
, then by assumption
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein. Then
C_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
, which is a contradiction.
Thus,
\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}\geq 2
.
Since
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
, we have
\operatorname{depth}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 1
. This shows, by Lemma
3.5
, that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring with
\dim A_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
C=(0)
and
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module.
∎

We summarize some consequences of Theorem
3.6
.
Note that
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{1})
if and only if
\operatorname{Ass}A=\operatorname{Min}A
, and the latter condition implies
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{1})
and
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
, then
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module.

Recall that if
\operatorname{Assh}A=\operatorname{Ass}A
, then
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}A=\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}}
. Thus, we obtain the following as another direct consequence of Theorem
3.6
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
\operatorname{Assh}A=\operatorname{Ass}A
.
Then
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module if and only if
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
.

If
A
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring, the above corollary recovers
[
14
, Korollar 7.29]
.
Recall that
A
is a
generalized Cohen-Macaulay ring
, if the
i^{\mbox{\tiny th}}
local cohomology module
\mathrm{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(A)
is a finitely generated
A
-module for every
i\neq d
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that
A
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay ring and
d>0
.
Then
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module if and only if

\operatorname{depth}A>0
and
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
.

Proof.

By assumption, we have
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}A\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Assh}A}
. If
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive, then by Lemma
3.1
we see that
\operatorname{depth}A>0
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\operatorname{depth}A>0
. Hence
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}A=\operatorname{Assh}A}
. The assertion follows from Corollary
3.8
.
∎

It seems natural to ask for the relation between the reflexivity of the
A
-module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
and that of the
A/U
-module
\mathrm{K}_{A/U}
. As for this question, we have
the following.

Theorem 3.10
.

The following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module
;

(2)

\mathrm{K}_{A/U}
is a reflexive
A/U
-module and
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
.

Proof.

Let
B=A/U
. Then
{\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{A}=\mathrm{K}_{B}}
(
[
2
, 1.8]
). Also note that
\operatorname{Ass}B=\operatorname{Assh}B
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
. By Corollary
3.8
, it suffices to show that
B
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
. Let
{\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}\in\operatorname{Spec}B}
be a prime with
{\displaystyle\operatorname{ht}_{B}\mathfrak{P}\leq 1}
. We write
{\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{p}/U}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in V(U)
. Then
\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{ht}_{B}\mathfrak{P}\leq 1
. Moreover,
\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is a reflexive
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module. By Propositions
2.3
and
3.3
,
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein.
Since
{\displaystyle U_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0):_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}=(0)}
, we obtain
{\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{P}}=A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Thus,
{\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{P}}}
is a Gorenstein ring.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}
with
\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
. By Theorem
3.6
, it is enough to show that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein. Let
\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{p}/U
. Then by Corollary
3.8
,
B_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a Gorenstein ring. Since
\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A
, the ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen-Macaulay. In particular,
U_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
and
{\displaystyle A_{\mathfrak{p}}=B_{\mathfrak{P}}}
. Therefore
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Suppose that
A/U
is a Gorenstein ring. Then the following assertions hold true.

(1)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module if and only if
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)=\operatorname{Assh}A}
.

(2)

If
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{1})
, then
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive.

Proof.

Note that (1) follows directly from Theorem
3.10
. To prove (2), it is enough to show that
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)\subseteq\operatorname{Assh}A}
. Let
{\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}A\cap V(U)}
.
Since
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{1})
, we have
\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}=0
. Since
\mathfrak{p}\in V(U)
, we have
{\displaystyle\dim A=\dim A/\mathfrak{p}+\operatorname{ht}_{A}\mathfrak{p}=\dim A/\mathfrak{p}}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}A
.
∎

Closing this section, we provide the examples of (not necessarily Cohen-Macaulay) local rings admitting reflexive canonical modules.

Example 3.12
.

Let
S=k[\![X,Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n}]\!]

(n\geq 2)
be the formal power series ring over a field
k
and let
A=S/[(X^{m})\cap J]
where
m\geq 1
and
J
is a
(Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n})
-primary ideal of
S
.
Let
x
denote the image of
X
in
A
. Then
U=(x^{m})
,
\operatorname{Assh}A=\{(x)\}
, and
A/U
is a Gorenstein ring. By Corollary
3.11
, the
A
-module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is reflexive.

Example 3.13
.

Let
k
be a field and
R=k[\Delta]
be the Stanley-Reisner ring of a simplicial complex
\Delta
. Since
R
is reduced, the graded canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{R}
(see
[
13
,
20
]
) is torsionless. Moreover, if
\#(\operatorname{Assh}R)=1
, the ring
R/U
is Gorenstein, so that
\mathrm{K}_{R}
is reflexive as an
R
-module, where
U
stands for the unmixed component of
(0)
in
R
and
\operatorname{Assh}R=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\dim R/\mathfrak{p}=\dim R\}
.

4.
Quasi-normal rings

Quasi-normal rings were introduced by Vasconcelos (
[
21
, Definition 1.2]
) and they are exactly
2
-Gorenstein rings.

Definition 4.1
.

A Noetherian ring
R
is said to be
quasi-normal
if
R
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{2})
and
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
.

The following is a direct consequence of
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]
(for a local ring case, see also
[
8
, Theorem 3.8]
). Here we include our alternative proof specifically for quasi-normal rings.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
R
be a quasi-normal ring and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
If
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\{2,\;\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
, then
M
is reflexive.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\rightarrow X\rightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{**}\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0,

where
\varphi
denotes the canonical homomorphism.
Suppose
X\neq(0)
and choose
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}X
. By assumption, we have
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
. Since
R
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
, the local ring
{\displaystyle R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is Gorenstein, whence
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is reflexive. Hence
X_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
, which is a contradiction. So
X=(0)
, and we have the exact sequence

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{**}\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0.

Suppose
C\neq(0)
. Let
{\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}C}
. If
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
, then
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is reflexive, so
C_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
. This is a contradiction. Thus
{\displaystyle\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 1}
. As
R
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{2})
, we have
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\{2,\;\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}}
. Hence
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 1}
. Since

{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{**}\geq\min\{2,\;\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}}
, we then have
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{**}\geq 1}
. The exact sequence

0\rightarrow M_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{**}\rightarrow C_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow 0

gives that

0=\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}C_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}-1,\;\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}^{**}\}.

Hence
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq 1}
. By assumption, we have

1\geq\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\{2,\;\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}.

Therefore
{\displaystyle\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}=1}
and
{\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}=1}
. Since
R
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
, the ring
{\displaystyle R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is Gorenstein. By
[
21
, Corollary 2.3]
, we see that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is reflexive. Hence
C_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

A finitely generated
R
-module
\omega_{R}
is a
canonical module
of
R
, if
{\displaystyle(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{m}}}
is the canonical module of
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. In contrast to the local case, the canonical module is in general not unique up to isomorphisms; see e.g.,
[
6
, Remark 3.3.17]
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring with
d=\dim R>0
. Suppose that there exists a canonical module
\omega_{R}
and
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}R_{\mathfrak{m}}=\operatorname{Assh}R_{\mathfrak{m}}}
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Then
R
is quasi-normal if and only if
R
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{2})
and
\omega_{R}
is reflexive.

Proof.

Suppose
R
is quasi-normal. Since
\omega_{R}
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{2})
and
{\displaystyle\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}[\omega_{R}]_{\mathfrak{p}}=\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
, by Proposition
4.2
, we conclude that
\omega_{R}
is reflexive. For the converse, it remains to show that
R
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
.
Let
A=R_{\mathfrak{m}}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of
R
. Then
\mathrm{K}_{A}=(\omega_{R})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is reflexive. Therefore we have
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}A=\operatorname{Assh}A}
. By
Corollary
3.8
,
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
. Thus
R
satisfies
(\mathrm{G}_{1})
.
∎

We summarize some examples. First, we note examples of quasi-normal rings which are not normal. The simplest ones are non-normal Gorenstein rings.

Example 4.4
.

Suppose that
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring with canonical module
\omega_{R}
. We set
T=R\ltimes\omega_{R}
to be the idealization of
\omega_{R}
over
R
. Then
T
is a Gorenstein ring (
[
16
]
), but not normal because it is never a reduced ring.

For a commutative ring
R
, we denote by
\overline{R}
the integral closure of
R
in
\mathrm{Q}(R)
. We refer to
[
6
, p. 178]
for background on numerical semigroups.

Example 4.5
.

Let
H=\left<a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}\right>
be a symmetric numerical semigroup. We consider
R=k[s,t^{a_{1}},t^{a_{2}},\ldots,t^{a_{\ell}}]
, where
s,t
are indeterminates and
k
is a field. Then
R
is a two-dimensional Gorenstein ring with
\overline{R}=k[s,t]
, so that
R
is not normal if
1\not\in H
. As a special case, the ring
R=k[s,t^{2},t^{3}]
is quasi-normal, but not normal.

Next, we note examples of quasi-normal but non-normal Cohen-Macaulay rings which are moreover not Gorenstein.

Example 4.6
.

Let
k
be a field and
k[X,Y]
the polynomial ring over
k
. Let
H=\left<a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}\right>
be a symmetric numerical semigroup such that
1\not\in H
and let
k[H]=k[t^{a_{1}},t^{a_{2}},\ldots,t^{a_{\ell}}]
denote the semigroup ring of
H
over
k
, where
t
is an indeterminate. Let
T=k[X^{n},X^{n-1}Y,\ldots,XY^{n-1},Y^{n}]
, where
n\geq 3
is an integer. We set
R=T\otimes_{k}k[H]
. Then
R
is a quasi-normal Cohen-Macaulay ring with
\dim R=3
, which is neither Gorenstein nor normal. Indeed, because
\overline{R}=T\otimes_{k}k[t]
and
k[H]\neq k[t]
, the ring
R
is not normal. As
T
is normal, we see that
R
is a quasi-normal ring (see Proposition
7.3
(2)). Moreover,
R
is not a Gorenstein ring because
T
is not Gorenstein. The simplest example in this class is
R=k[X^{3},X^{2}Y,XY^{2},Y^{3}]\otimes_{k}k[t^{2},t^{3}]
.

Example 4.7
.

Let
T=k[X,Y,Z,V]
be the polynomial ring over a field
k
.
We denote by
\mathbf{I}_{2}(\mathbb{N})
the ideal of
T
generated by all the
2\times 2
minors of a matrix
\mathbb{N}
.
Let
I=\mathbf{I}_{2}(\mathbb{M})
where
\mathbb{M}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X^{a}&Y^{b}+V&Z^{c}\\
Y^{b^{\prime}}&Z^{c^{\prime}}&X^{a^{\prime}}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for some integers
a,b,c,a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\geq 1
.
We set
R=T/I
. Then
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
2
.
Let
x,y,z,v
denote the images of
X,Y,Z,V
in
R
, respectively. We first check the isomorphism
\omega_{R}\cong(x^{a},y^{b^{\prime}})R
. In fact, by setting

f=Z^{c+c^{\prime}}-X^{a^{\prime}}(Y^{b}+V),\ \ g=X^{a+a^{\prime}}-Y^{b^{\prime}}Z^{c},\ \ \text{and}\ \ h=-X^{a}Z^{c^{\prime}}+Y^{b^{\prime}}(Y^{b}+V),

we can consider the exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}^{t}\mathbb{M}}
\textstyle{T^{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\scriptsize\begin{pmatrix}f&g&h\end{pmatrix}}
\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

of
T
-modules. By taking the
T
-dual, we get the presentation of
\omega_{R}
of the form
T^{3}\overset{\mathbb{M}}{\to}T^{2}\to\omega_{R}\to 0
.
Therefore, the complex of
R
-modules

\textstyle{R^{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{M}}
\textstyle{R^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Y^{b^{\prime}}&-X^{a}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\hskip 8.19447pt\ }
\textstyle{(x^{a},y^{b^{\prime}})R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

induces a natural epimorphism

\varphi:\omega_{R}\twoheadrightarrow(x^{a},y^{b^{\prime}})R

of
R
-modules.
Moreover,
\varphi
is an isomorphism because
\omega_{R}
is a torsionfree
R
-module of rank one and
x^{a}
is a non-zerodivisor on
R
. Hence
\omega_{R}\cong(x^{a},y^{b^{\prime}})R
, as claimed.
We similarly have

\omega_{R}\cong(y^{b}+v,z^{c^{\prime}})R\cong(z^{c},x^{a^{\prime}})R.

We also note that the isomorphisms can be obtained by using the procedure of
[
22
, Section 6.1.2]
.
In particular,
R
is not a Gorenstein ring, since the type of
R
is two.

Next, we show that
R
is a quasi-normal ring.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
with
\operatorname{ht}_{R}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
.
If
x\not\in\mathfrak{p}
, then
[\omega_{R}]_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(x^{a},y^{b^{\prime}})R_{\mathfrak{p}}=R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a Gorenstein ring.
Assume that
x\in\mathfrak{p}
. Similarly, we may assume that
y,z\in\mathfrak{p}
.
Then,
v\notin\mathfrak{p}
, since
\operatorname{ht}_{R}\mathfrak{p}\leq 1
.
Therefore,
[\omega_{R}]_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(y^{b}+v,z^{c^{\prime}})R_{\mathfrak{p}}=R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so that

is a Gorenstein ring. Hence

is a quasi-normal ring.

Finally, we prove that
R
is a normal ring if and only if
a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}=c=1
.
Assume
a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}=c=1
and consider the ideal

is a normal ring.
Conversely, we assume

, or

, or

. By taking

we then have

.

. Then

, but

, we see that

\left(\dfrac{Z^{c+c^{\prime}}}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-X^{a^{\prime}},X^{a+a^{\prime}}-Y^{b^{\prime}}Z^{c},-\dfrac{X^{a}Z^{c^{\prime}}}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}+Y^{b^{\prime}}\right)\subseteq(Y)+(X,Z)^{2}&\text{(if $c=1$)}\end{cases}

in

cannot be a DVR. Hence

is not a normal ring.
As a special case,
R=k[X,Y,Z,V]/\mathbf{I}_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X&Y+V&Z\\
Y&Z&X^{2}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is a
quasi-normal ring, but not normal.

5.
Reflexive canonical modules in dimension one

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
\dim A=1
admitting the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
. In this section, we explore the question of when
A
has a reflexive canonical module. We denote by
\mathrm{Q}(A)
the total ring of fractions of
A
. Throughout this section, we assume that there exists an
A
-submodule
K
of
\mathrm{Q}(A)
such that
A\subseteq K\subseteq\overline{A}
and
K\cong\mathrm{K}_{A}
as an
A
-module, where
\overline{A}
denotes the integral closure of
A
in
\mathrm{Q}(A)
. Note that the assumption is automatically satisfied if
\mathrm{Q}(A)
is Gorenstein and the residue class field
A/\mathfrak{m}
is infinite; see
[
12
, Corollaries 2.8, 2.9]
.
For
A
-submodules
X
and
Y
of
\mathrm{Q}(A)
, let
X:Y=\{a\in\mathrm{Q}(A)\mid aY\subseteq X\}
. If we consider ideals

of

; hence

.

Proposition 5.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

A
is a Gorenstein ring
;

(2)

K^{2}:K=K
;

(3)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive

-module.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) See Proposition
3.3
.

(3)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) Since

(
[

, Bemerkung 2.5]
), we have

A:(A:K)=(K:K):(K:K^{2})=[K:(K:K^{2})]:K=K^{2}:K.

Therefore,

if and only if

, that is
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a reflexive
A
-module.
∎

Recall that an ideal

of

is called a

of

as an

-module. By

, there exists a canonical ideal

of

. We then have the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
I
be a canonical ideal of
A
. Then the following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

is a Gorenstein ring
;

(2)

I^{2}:_{A}I=I
;

(3)

I/I^{2}
is a free
A/I
-module
;

(4)

is a reflexive
A
-module.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
, it suffices to show
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Enlarging the residue class field
A/\mathfrak{m}
of

if necessary, we may assume that
A/\mathfrak{m}
is infinite. Let
I=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
(

) so that each
(x_{i})
is a reduction of
I
. We set
K_{i}=x_{i}^{-1}I
and choose a non-zerodivisor

of

so that
bK_{i}^{2}\subseteq A
for all

. Let

and
y_{i}=bx_{i}
for

. Then
(y_{i})
is a reduction of
J
. Notice that
A/I
and

are both Gorenstein rings, since

as
A
-modules.

Claim 1
.

J^{2}:_{A}J=J
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Suppose that
J^{2}:_{A}J\supsetneq J
. Then,
J:_{A}\mathfrak{m}\subseteq J^{2}:_{A}J
. Since
A/J
is a Gorenstein ring, we have
J:_{A}\mathfrak{m}=J+A\varphi
for some
\varphi\in(J:_{A}\mathfrak{m})\setminus J
. Hence,
\frac{\varphi}{b}\in\mathrm{Q}(A)
and
\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}\frac{\varphi}{b}\subseteq I
, so that
\frac{\varphi}{b}\in I:\mathfrak{m}
. Because
I\subsetneq I:_{A}\mathfrak{m}\subseteq I:\mathfrak{m}
and
\ell_{A}\left((I:\mathfrak{m})/I\right)=1
(since
A/I
is a Gorenstein ring), we get
\frac{\varphi}{b}\in I:_{A}\mathfrak{m}
, so that
\frac{\varphi}{b}\in A
. On the other hand,
\frac{\varphi}{b}{\cdot}I\subseteq I^{2}
, since
\varphi{\cdot}bI=\varphi J\subseteq J^{2}=b^{2}I^{2}
. Consequently,
\frac{\varphi}{b}\in I^{2}:_{A}I=I
, whence
\varphi\in bI=J
, which is impossible. Thus
J^{2}:_{A}J=J
.
∎

Let
\overline{y_{i}}
denote the image of

in

. We then have
J/J^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A/J){\cdot}\overline{y_{i}}
, and therefore,
(0):_{A/J}\overline{y_{i}}=(0)
for some

, since

is a Gorenstein ring and
(0):_{A/J}J/J^{2}=(0)
by Claim

. Without loss of generality, assume

. On the other hand, since

and

, we get

, whence

. Therefore,

. Hence,
J^{2}=y_{1}{\cdot}(J^{2}:_{A}y_{1})=y_{1}J
. Thus

is a Gorenstein ring (see
[

, Theorem 3.7]
).
∎

6.

-torsionfree canonical modules

The purpose of this section is to give a generalization of Proposition

and Theorem

, which characterize local rings with
q
-torsionfree canonical modules for
q=1,2
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian (not necessarily local) ring and
q
an integer.
Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module with a finite projective presentation
{\displaystyle P_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\rightarrow}}P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0}
.
By applying the
R
-dual functor
(-)^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,R)
, we obtain the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M^{*}\longrightarrow P_{0}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}P_{1}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\sigma}(M)\longrightarrow 0

of
R
-modules.
We set
{\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(M)=\mathrm{D}_{\sigma}(M)}
and call it the
Auslander transpose
of

.
Note that
\mathrm{D}(M)
is uniquely determined up to projective equivalence.

Definition 6.1

(
[
1
, Definition 2.15]
)
.

A finitely generated
R
-module
M
is said to be
q
-torsionfree
if
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(\mathrm{D}(M),R)=0
for all

.

By
[
1
, Proposition 2.6]
, there exists an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{R}(\mathrm{D}(M),R)\longrightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{**}\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}^{2}_{R}(\mathrm{D}(M),R)\rightarrow 0

of
R
-modules, where
\varphi
is the canonical homomorphism, and furthermore, we have

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i+2}(\mathrm{D}(M),R)\cong\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(M^{*},R)\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ i>0.

This shows
M
is torsionless (resp. reflexive) if and only if
M
is
1
-torsionfree (
2
-torsionfree). When
q\geq 3
, the
R
-module

is
q
-torsionfree if and only if
M
is reflexive and
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(M^{*},R)=(0)
for all

.

Example 6.2
.

Let

be the formal power series ring over a field

. Let

{\displaystyle\operatorname{Assh}A=\left\{(x)\right\}}
,
{\displaystyle U=(x)}
, and
{\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{A}=A/U}
, where

denotes the unmixed component of
(0)
in

.
By dualizing the exact sequence

Thus,
{\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}_{A})=\mathrm{K}_{A}}
. Consider the free resolution of

A^{5}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{4}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{3}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow 0,

{\displaystyle\tau_{2}=[y\;z],\;\tau_{3}=\left[\begin{array}[]{rrr}x&0&z\\

Dualize this free resolution to obtain

are the transposes of

, respectively. Let
{\displaystyle a\in\operatorname{Ker}\sigma_{2}}
. Then

Thus,
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{A}(D,A)=(0)}
. Let

{\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{Ker}\sigma_{3}}
.
Then
{\displaystyle a_{1},a_{2}\in(0):_{A}x=(y,z)}

{\displaystyle a_{1}z-a_{2}y=0}
.
Hence
{\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-a_{2}\\

\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{Ker}\tau_{2}=\operatorname{Im}(\tau_{3})}
.
Let
{\displaystyle-a_{2}=c_{1}x-c_{3}z}

{\displaystyle a_{1}=c_{2}x+c_{3}y}
for some

.
Then
{\displaystyle c_{1}x=-a_{2}+c_{3}z\in(y,z)}
.
Thus,

and

{\displaystyle a_{1}=c_{3}y}
.
We obtain
{\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}\\

Then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}^{2}_{A}(D,A)=(0)}
.
Hence

Note that

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\

,

Then

Definition 6.3

(
[
1
, Definition 2.15]
)
.

A finitely generated

-module

is called

-syzygy

-modules

and an exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\to M\to F_{1}\to F_{2}\to\cdots\to F_{q}}
of

Note that
(a)

is torsionless if and only if

is

-syzygy, (b) every

-torsionfree

-module is

-syzygy, and (c) if

is

Although the following theorem has been proved by Foxby in a more general setting involving Gorenstein modules, we restate it and give its proof in our context for the sake of completeness.
Recall that

is

-Gorenstein if

is Gorenstein for every prime

with

Theorem 6.4

(
[
10
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
A
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring admitting the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

is
q
-Gorenstein
;

(2)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
q
-torsionfree
;

(3)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
q
-syzygy.

Proof.

Since

is Cohen-Macaulay, we have
{\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}A=\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}}
and
{\displaystyle[\mathrm{K}_{A}]_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A
. Notice that
{\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay as an

-module.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Since every

-regular sequence is
\mathrm{K}_{A}
-regular, the

-module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
q
-torsionfree by
[

, Proposition 2.3]
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
This follows from
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A
with

.
Set

. When

, the ring

is Gorenstein. Assume

and choose a system

of parameters of

. Then it is an

-regular sequence, so that

is

.
Since

. This completes the proof.
∎

As a direct consequence of Theorem

, we have the following.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let

be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with

admitting the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
.
Then

is a Gorenstein ring if and only if
\mathrm{K}_{A}
is

-torsionfree.

Theorem

and Corollary

lead us to the question of the structure of a local ring

-torsionfree canonical module without the assumption that

For a subset

on

on

is Gorenstein for every

with

The main result of this section gives an answer to the above question.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian local ring admitting the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
. Suppose that
\mathrm{K}_{A}
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
. Then the following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

satisfies both
(\mathrm{G}_{q-1})
and
(\mathrm{S}_{q-1})
on
\operatorname{Supp}_{A}\mathrm{K}_{A}
;

(2)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
q
-torsionfree
;

(3)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is
q
-syzygy.

To show this, we need some auxiliaries.
The following plays an important role in our argument.

Lemma 6.7

(
[
7
, Lemma 4.9]
)
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian local ring and
M
a nonzero finitely generated
A
-module. Assume that

and

is
q
-syzygy. Then
\operatorname{depth}_{A}M=\operatorname{depth}A
.

We apply Lemma

to get the following.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module.
If

is
(q+1)
-syzygy, then one has

\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\{q,\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\}\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ \mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M.

In particular, if
M
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
, then

satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
on
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
.

Proof.

By localizing at
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
, it suffices to show
\operatorname{depth}R\geq\min\{q,\operatorname{depth}_{R}M\}
. If
\operatorname{depth}R\geq q
, the assertion is obvious. Otherwise, if
\operatorname{depth}R<q
, the assertion follows from Lemma

.
∎

As consequences of Theorems
6.4
,

, we get the following.

.

Let
A
be a Noetherian local ring with

admitting the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent
:

is Gorenstein
;

(2)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a

-torsionfree maximal Cohen-Macaulay

;

(3)

\mathrm{K}_{A}

.

Let

.

Remark 6.11

Let

be a Noetherian local ring admitting the canonical module

. We say that

is

Based on the above observation, it is natural to raise the following question.

Question 6.12

Let

be a Noetherian local ring with

admitting the canonical module
\mathrm{K}_{A}
. When are the following conditions equivalent?

(i)

is a quasi-Gorenstein ring, i.e.,

.

(ii)

\mathrm{K}_{A}
is a

-torsionfree

In what follows, let

be a Noetherian ring and

a finitely generated

-module.
The equivalence of
(1)
and
(2)
in the next theorem was essentially proved by Auslander and Bridger
[

]
. Notice that this is a

version of
[

, Proposition 1.4.1]
.

Theorem 6.13
.

The following conditions are equivalent
:

(1)

M
is
q
-torsionfree
;

(2)

M
satisfies the conditions below
:

(i)

is
q
-torsionfree for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
with
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
;

(ii)

satisfies
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{q})
;

(3)

M
satisfies the conditions below
:

(i)

M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
q
-torsionfree for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
with
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
;

(ii)

\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
with
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q-1
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume

.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
This follows from
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
(i) Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
with
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
. Since

satisfies
(\widetilde{\mathrm{S}}_{q})
, we have
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This implies
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
, and hence

is
q
-torsionfree. (ii) Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
with
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q-1
. Then

is
q
-torsionfree, so that
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
by Lemma

.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)

For each

, we set
E^{i}=\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(\mathrm{D}(M),R)
.
Suppose

and seek a contradiction. Take
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}E^{q}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
, by (i) we have
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq q
.
Then by (ii),
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq q-1
.
By passing to the localization
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
at
\mathfrak{p}
, we may assume

is a local ring,
\operatorname{depth}R\geq q-1
,
\operatorname{depth}_{R}M\geq q
, and
\operatorname{depth}_{R}E^{q}=0
.

We proceed by induction on

.
First, assume that

.
Since

is isomorphic to a submodule of

, it follows that
\operatorname{depth}_{R}M=0
, a contradiction. Thus

.
Next, we assume

.
Applying the depth lemma to the exact sequence

of

-modules, we get
\operatorname{depth}_{R}M=1
, as
\operatorname{depth}_{R}M^{**}\geq 1
. This is impossible, whence

.
Suppose

and the assertion holds for

, i.e.,
M
is
(q-1)
-torsionfree.
Hence
E^{1}=\cdots=E^{q-1}=(0)
.
Consider a free resolution
(F_{i},\partial_{i})
of
M^{*}
.
Applying the
R
-dual functor
(-)^{*}
, we get the exact sequence

0\to M^{**}\to F_{0}^{*}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}^{*}}F_{1}^{*}\to\cdots\to F_{q-3}^{*}\xrightarrow{\partial_{q-2}^{*}}F_{q-2}^{*}

of
R
-modules because
E^{3}=\cdots=E^{q-1}=(0)
.
Let
C
be the cokernel of
\partial_{q-2}^{*}
.
Since
M
is reflexive as an
R
-module, we obtain the exact sequence of the form

0\to M\to F_{0}^{*}\to\cdots\to F_{q-2}^{*}\to C\to 0.

Since
E^{q}=\mbox{\rm Ext}^{q-2}_{R}(M^{*},R)
may be regarded as a submodule of
C
, we see that
\operatorname{depth}_{R}C=0
. Hence
\operatorname{depth}_{R}M=q-1
. This gives a contradiction.
Hence we conclude that
E^{q}=(0)
, which shows
M
is
q
-torsionfree.
∎

Corollary 6.14
.

Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied
:

(a)

M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
q
-torsionfree for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
with
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
;

(b)

M
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
;

(c)

\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\min\{q-1,\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}-1\}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
.

Then
M
is
q
-torsionfree.

Proof.

We will check condition (3) in Theorem
6.13
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
.
(i) Assume that
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
. By (b),
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}=\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so that
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
. Therefore
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
q
-torsionfree by (a). (ii) Assume that
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q-1
. By (c),
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}-1
, so that
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
. Therefore
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
q
-torsionfree by (a), whence
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
by Lemma
6.7
.
∎

Corollary 6.15
.

Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied
:

(a)

M
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
;

(b)

R
satisfies both
(\mathrm{G}_{q-1})
and
(\mathrm{S}_{q-1})
on
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
.

Then
M
is
q
-torsionfree.

Proof.

By Corollary
6.14
, it suffices to show that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
q
-torsionfree on
\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M\mid\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q\}
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M
with
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}<q
.
Then by (b),
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein.
As
M
satisfies
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
,
\operatorname{depth}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Hence
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay as an
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module.
In particular,
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
q
-torsionfree.
∎

6.6
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)

.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
This follows from

7.
Examples of
q
-Gorenstein rings

Closing this paper, in order to illustrate our theorems, we provide additional examples of Cohen-Macaulay and
q
-Gorenstein rings, i.e., rings with
(\mathrm{S}_{q})
and
(\mathrm{G}_{q-1})
conditions, or equivalently, rings with
(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{q-1})
condition.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
A
be a Gorenstein local ring with
d=\dim A\geq 3
and let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}
be a system of parameters of
A
. Let
\mathfrak{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell})

(3\leq\ell\leq d)
and let

\mathcal{R}=A[a_{1}t,a_{2}t,\ldots,a_{\ell}t]\ \subseteq\ A[t]

be the Rees algebra of
\mathfrak{a}
, where
t
denotes an indeterminate. Then,
\mathcal{R}
is not a Gorenstein ring, but it is a Cohen-Macaulay
(\ell+1)
-Gorenstein ring of dimension
d+1
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{R}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
d+1
. Let
S=A[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell}]
be the polynomial ring over
A
and let
\varphi:S\to\mathcal{R}
denote the surjective homomorphism of
A
-algebras defined by
\varphi(X_{i})=a_{i}t
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
. The homomorphism
\varphi
preserves the grading and
{\rm Ker}(\varphi)=\mathbf{I}_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X_{1}&X_{2}&\ldots&X_{\ell}\\
a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{\ell}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)

is the perfect ideal of
S
of grade
\ell-1
generated by the
2\times 2
minors of the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X_{1}&X_{2}&\ldots&X_{\ell}\\
a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{\ell}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. We set
I={\rm Ker}(\varphi)
. We then have the following.

Claim 2
.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Spec}S
such that
I\subseteq P
but
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})+(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell})\not\subseteq P
. Then,
S_{P}/IS_{P}
is a Gorenstein ring.

Proof of Claim 2.

We may assume that
X_{1}\not\in P
. Let
\widetilde{S}=S[\frac{1}{X_{1}}]
,
\widetilde{A}=A[X_{1},\frac{1}{X_{1}}]
, and
Y_{i}=\frac{X_{i}}{X_{1}}
for
2\leq i\leq\ell
. Then,
\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{A}[Y_{2},Y_{3},\ldots,Y_{\ell}]
and
I\widetilde{S}=(a_{i}-a_{1}Y_{i}\mid 2\leq i\leq\ell)\widetilde{S}
. Because
a_{1}\widetilde{S}+(a_{i}-a_{1}Y_{i}\mid 2\leq i\leq\ell)\widetilde{S}=(a_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell)\widetilde{S}
and
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}
is an
\widetilde{S}
-regular sequence, the sequence
a_{2}-a_{1}Y_{2},a_{3}-a_{1}Y_{3},\ldots,a_{\ell}-a_{1}Y_{\ell}
is
\widetilde{S}_{P}
-regular, so that
S_{P}/IS_{P}
is a Gorenstein ring.
∎

Let
P\in\operatorname{Spec}S
and suppose that
I\subseteq P
. We set
\mathfrak{p}=\varphi(P)\in\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}
. Then,
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})+(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell})\not\subseteq P
if
\operatorname{ht}_{S}P<2\ell
, while

\operatorname{ht}_{\mathcal{R}}\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{ht}_{S/I}P/I=\operatorname{ht}_{S}P-(\ell-1).

Therefore, if
\operatorname{ht}_{\mathcal{R}}\mathfrak{p}<\ell+1
, then
\operatorname{ht}_{S}P-(\ell-1)<\ell+1
, that is
\operatorname{ht}_{S}P<2\ell
, so that
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell})+(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell})\not\subseteq P
, whence
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}=S_{P}/IS_{P}
is a Gorenstein ring by Claim 2. Thus,
\mathcal{R}
is an
(\ell+1)
-Gorenstein ring.
∎

Comment on "Anisotropic peridynamics for homogenized microstructured materials" by Vito Diana, Andrea Bacigalupo, Marco Lepidi and Luigi Gambarotta [Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Engrg. 392 (2022) 114704] Journal: Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering
Marcin Maździarz

Abstract




Recently, Vito Diana, Andrea Bacigalupo, Marco Lepidi and Luigi Gambarotta
[
1
]
proposed an anisotropic continuum-molecular model based on pair potentials for describing the linear behavior of
periodic heterogeneous Cauchy materials in the framework of peridynamic theory.

Unfortunately, the proposed model for an isotropic material may have zero or even negative shear stiffness and this will be the case for
Silicene
or
Germanene
, for example. In addition, 2D symmetries were confused with 3D symmetries, and hence the limits for the Poisson’s ratio.

In Ref.
[
1
, Eq.38]
two elastic quadratic potentials of pairwise axial (
{w}_{s}
) and shearing deformation measures (
{w}_{\gamma}
) in the special case of isotropic materials reduce to:

\displaystyle{w}_{s}=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\left|\mathbf{\xi}\right|k_{n}=6\pi{h}{\delta}^{3}s^{2}\left|\mathbf{\xi}\right|\left(C_{1111}-C_{1212}\right).

(1)

\displaystyle{w}_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}{\gamma}^{2}\left|\mathbf{\xi}\right|k_{t}=6\pi{h}{\delta}^{3}{\gamma}^{2}\left|\mathbf{\xi}\right|\left(3C_{1212}-C_{1111}\right).

(2)

The elasticity tensor for an ordinary 2D material with isotropic symmetry has a representation in the Voigt notation:

\displaystyle\centering{C}_{ijkl}\rightarrow\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}{{C}_{1111}}&{{C}_{1122}}&0\\
{{C}_{1122}}&{{C}_{1111}}&0\\
0&0&C_{1212}\\
\end{array}\right],\@add@centering

where
C_{1212}=\dfrac{C_{1111}-C_{1122}}{2}
, hence the number of independent components reduces here to 2. We can therefore write this as:

\displaystyle\centering{C}_{ijkl}\rightarrow\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}{{C}_{1111}}&{{C}_{1122}}&0\\
{{C}_{1122}}&{{C}_{1111}}&0\\
0&0&\dfrac{C_{1111}-C_{1122}}{2}\\
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}{{C}_{1111}}&{{C}_{1111}-2{C}_{1212}}&0\\
{{C}_{1111}-2{C}_{1212}}&{{C}_{1111}}&0\\
0&0&C_{1212}\\
\end{array}\right].\@add@centering

The mechanical stability condition requires the elasticity tensor
C_{ijkl}
to be positive definite, see
[
2
]
. This imposes conditions on the elastic constants and for any isotropic two-dimensional material (2D) means:

\displaystyle C_{1212}>0,

(12)

\displaystyle C_{1111}>C_{1212}.

(13)

We see that the condition
13
implies positivity of
w
s
in Eq.
1
but for

{C_{1111}}>3C_{1212}

w
γ
in Eq.
2
is negative, which is not acceptable as the stiffness cannot be negative.

This is not some contrived, theoretical case, e.g. for
Silicene

C_{1111}
=68.3 N/m,
C_{1212}
=22.5 N/m, what means that
\left(3C_{1212}-C_{1111}\right)
= -0.8 N/m, see
[
3
]
.

[
1

is written: "
… leads to the effective fourth order elasticity tensor
\mathbb{C}
having the elasticities of the two-dimensional cubic symmetry …
". No such symmetry exists for 2D materials, there is isotropy, tetragonal, orthotropy and anisotropy, see
[
2
]
.

In Ref.
[
1

A note on null distance and causality encoding
Gregory J. Galloway

Abstract
Under natural conditions, the null distance introduced by Sormani and Vega [ 10 ] is a metric space distance function on spacetime, which, in a certain precise sense, can encode the causality of spacetime. The null distance function requires the choice of a time function. The purpose of this note is to observe that the causality assumptions related to such a choice in results used to establish global encoding of causality, due Sakovich and Sormani [ 9 ] and to Burtscher and García-Heveling [ 2 ] , can be weakened.



In
[
10
]
, C. Sormani and C. Vega introduced a novel distance measure on spacetimes
(time-oriented Lorentzian manifolds), called
null distance
, which, under reasonable circumstances, has a number of desirable properties. In particular, under certain conditions, it is a metric on spacetime, in the standard sense of metric spaces, and, remarkably, at the same time, encodes the causal structure of spacetime. We will discuss what is meant by the latter shortly. The construction of such a distance function was motivated in part by the program to study the convergence theory of Lorentzian manifolds. The null distance function requires the choice of a time function on spacetime. The main purpose of this note is to observe that the causality assumptions related to such a choice, which have been used to establish
global
encoding of causality (see
[
9
,
2
]
) can be replaced by an
a priori
weaker causality condition.

We begin by fixing some notation and terminology. By a
spacetime
we
mean a smooth connected time orientable Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
, with
\dim M\geq 2
, where
g
has signature
(-,+\cdots+)
.
With regard to causal theoretic
notions such as
I^{\pm}
(timelike futures and pasts),

J^{\pm}
(causal futures and pasts),
D^{\pm}
(future and past domains of dependence), and
H^{\pm}
(future and past Cauchy horizons), we by and large follow the conventions in O’Neill
[
7
]
.

Recall, an edge point of an acausal set
A
is a point
p
in the closure of
A
such that every neighborhood
U
of
p
, contains a timelike curve from
I^{-}(p,U)

to
I^{+}(p,U)

that does not meet
A
. By an
acausal
C^{0}
spacelike hypersurface
in a spacetime
M
, we mean an acausal subset
S\subset M
that does not contain any of its edge points.
It follows that an acausal
C^{0}
spacelike hypersurface is, in fact,
a topological submanifold of
M
of co-dimension one (see
[
7
, Proposition 14.25]
).
An acausal
C^{0}
spacelike hypersurface
S
is a
Cauchy hypersurface
for
M
provided every inextendible causal curve in
M
meets
S
(and by acausality, does so exactly once). The existence of a Cauchy hypersurface for
M
is equivalent to
M
being globally hyperbolic.

A
time function
on a spacetime
M
is a continuous real-valued function
\tau:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
that is strictly increasing along all future-directed causal curves. A basic example of a time function is a smooth function on
M
which has a past pointing timelike gradient. However, there are types of time functions, such as the so-called cosmological time function
[
1
]
, that needn’t be smooth in general. The level sets
\tau=\tau_{0}
of a time function
\tau
on
M
are clearly acausal. Moreover, it is easy to see, using the continuity of
\tau
, that the level sets do not contain any edge points. In fact, since the level sets are closed subsets of spacetime (again by continuity of
\tau
), all the level sets are edgeless
(i.e.​ have no edge points); cf.​
[
7
, Corollary 14.26]
. Thus, the level sets of a time function are edgeless acausal
C^{0}
spacelike hypersurfaces. (Such hypersurfaces are sometimes referred to as
partial Cauchy surfaces
.) The level sets need not, in general, be connected.

The
null distance function
introduced by Sormani and Vega
[
10
]
is defined briefly as follows.
1
1

1

In
[
10
]
, the authors define null distance with respect to
generalized
time functions, which are not required to be continuous. Many results obtained in
[
10
,
12
,
9
]
apply to such time functions. Here we are only considering continuous time functions.

Suppose
\tau:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a time function on a spacetime
M
. The
null length
of a piecewise causal curve
\beta:[a,b]\to M
, with break points
x_{i}=\beta(s_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq m
, where each smooth causal segment may be either future pointing or past pointing, is defined by

\hat{L}_{\tau}(\beta):=\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{m}|\tau(x_{i})-\tau(x_{i-1})|\,.

The
null distance function

\hat{d}_{\tau}:M\times M\to{\mathbb{R}}
on
M
is then defined by taking the inf of the null lengths between any two points
p
and
q
,

\hat{d}_{\tau}(p,q):=\inf\{\hat{L}_{\tau}(\beta):\beta\textrm{\; is piecewise causal from \,}p\textrm{\, to \,}q\}\,.

See e.g.
[
10
,
12
,
9
,
2
]
for a discussion of fundamental properties and examples.

As observed in
[
10
]
, the null distance
\hat{d}_{\tau}:M\times M\to[0,\infty)
is in general a pseudometric,
but which can fail to be definite. However, there is a natural class of time functions
\tau
for which the null distance is definite, and hence is a true metric, in the sense of metric spaces. A time function

\tau:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be
locally anti-Lipschitz
provided for every point
p\in M
there is a neighborhood
U
of
p
that has a Riemannian metric with a distance function
d_{U}:U\times U\to[0,\infty)
such that for all
x,y\in U
one has

\ x\leq y\implies\tau(y)-\tau(x)\geq d_{U}(x,y)\ ,

(1)

where ‘
x\leq y
’ means that
y
is in the causal future of
x
, i.e.
y\in J^{+}(x)
(which includes
y=x
). As shown in
[
10
]
,
\hat{d}_{\tau}:M\times M\to[0,\infty)
is definite if and only if
\tau
is locally anti-Lipschitz. Moreover, in this case, the metric space topology induced by
\hat{d}_{\tau}
agrees with the manifold topology. Temporal time functions (smooth functions with past timelike gradient) and
regular
cosmological time functions
[
1
]
satisfy this locally anti-Lipschitz condtion
[
10
]
.

A remarkable feature of the null distance is that, under favorable circumstances, it encodes the causal structure of spacetime. We say that
\hat{d}_{\tau}:M\times M\to[0,\infty)
encodes causality provided the following holds

p\leq q\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\hat{d}_{\tau}(p,q)=\tau(q)-\tau(p)\,.

(2)

The ‘only if’ direction follows easily from the definition of null distance. As shown in
[
10
]
, the ‘if’ direction does not hold in general. However Sormani and Vega
[
10
, Theorem 3.25]
proved that the null distance encodes causality for a large class of generalized Robertson-Walker warped product spacetimes.
Sakovich and Sormani
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
proved a local causality encoding result for time functions
\tau
that obey the locally anti-Lipschitz condition. To obtain a global result, they imposed a further condition on the time function. We paraphrase their result here.

Theorem 1

(
[
9
]
, Theorem 4.1)
.

Let
\tau:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a
proper
locally anti-Lipschitz time function. Then
\hat{d}_{\tau}
globally encodes causality in
M
, i.e. (
2
) holds on
M
.

Here,
\tau
being proper means that for all
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\tau(M)
,with
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
,

\tau^{-1}([\tau_{1},\tau_{2}])
is compact. As observed in
[
2
]
, the assumption that

\tau
is proper
implies that its level sets
\tau=\tau_{0}
are compact Cauchy hypersurfaces, and hence that
M
is globally hyperbolic. It also follows that if all the level sets of
\tau
are compact Cauchy hypersurfaces then
\tau
is proper. Thus, in the statement of Theorem
1
, the assumption that
\tau
is proper can be replaced by the assumption that all level sets of
\tau
are compact Cauchy hypersurfaces.

From this perspective, A. Burtscher and L. García-Heveling have recently obtained a generalization of Theorem
1
. As described in
[
2
]
, this is accomplished by combining the approach of Sakovich and Sormani, together with the approach of Burtscher and García-Heveling in their proof of an earlier version of their global causality encoding result
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
, which required a temporal time function.

Theorem 2

(
[
2
]
, Theorem 1.9)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime and
\tau
a locally anti-Lipschitz time function such that all level sets are Cauchy. Then the null distance encodes causality, i.e., (
2
) holds on
M
.

As observed in
[
2
, Remark 3.6]
, in the statement of Theorem
2
, it is sufficient to assume that all level sets of
\tau
are
future Cauchy
. An acausal
C^{0}
spacelike hypersurface
S
in a spacetime
M
is future Cauchy if it has no future Cauchy horizon, i.e.
H^{+}(S)=\emptyset
. Similarly
S
is past Cauchy if
H^{-}(S)=\emptyset
. Future and past Cauchy hypersurfaces are necessarily connected.
If S is both future and past Cauchy then it’s a Cauchy hypersurface in
M
.

Here we wish to point out that this future Cauchy condition, under which Theorem
2
holds, can be replaced by a simple causal theoretic condition which
in general
is much weaker.
An acausal
C^{0}
spacelike hypersurface is said to be
future causally complete
provided for all points
p\in J^{+}(S)
, the set
J^{-}(p)\cap S
has compact closure in S. (Past causal completeness is defined in a time-dual manner.) As a very simple example, consider Minkowski space N, with standard coordinates
(t,x^{1},\cdots,x^{n})
, and let
M=N\setminus\{(1,0,\cdots,0)\}
. The time slices
t=t_{0}<1
are future causally complete, but not future Cauchy. Similarly, the spacelike hypersurfaces
t=\sqrt{a^{2}+\sum_{i}(x^{i})^{2}}
,
a\in(0,1)
are future causally complete, but neither future nor past Cauchy.

Note that all compact acausal
C^{0}
hypersurfaces, as well as all future Cauchy hypersurfaces, are future causally complete. (The latter follows from e.g.​
[
8
, Prop. 5.20]
. In fact, if
S
is future Cauchy then
J^{-}(p)\cap S
is compact for all
p\in J^{+}(S)
.) Future causal completeness was introduced in
[
3
]
, and used, for example, to extend Hawking’s cosmological singularity theorem to the nonspatially closed case in spacetimes not assumed to be globally hyperbolic. Further applications have been considered in e.g.
[
4
,
11
,
5
]
.

We have the following refinement of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\tau
be a locally anti-Lipschitz time function on a spacetime
(M,g)
such that all the level sets are future causally complete. Then the null distance encodes causality, i.e.​ (
2
) holds on
M
. Furthermore,
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic.

The theorem is an immediate consequence of
[
2
, Theorem 1.9, Remark 3.6]
and the following observation.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\tau
be a time function on a spacetime
(M,g)
such that all the level sets are future causally complete. Then all the level sets are future Cauchy. In particular,
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic.

Proof.

The lemma follows from a much more general result
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
concerning spacetime foliations. Here we give a simple direct proof applicable to spacetimes with time functions. Let
S_{a}
denote the level set
\tau=a
. It suffices to show that for each
a\in\tau(M)
,
H^{+}(S_{a})=\emptyset
. Suppose to the contrary, for some
a
there exists a point
q\in H^{+}(S_{a})
. Then
q
is the future endpoint of a past directed null geodesic
\eta
contained in
H^{+}(S_{a})
, which is either past inextendible or has a past end point on an edge point of
S_{a}
(
[
8
, Theorem 5.12]
). But
S_{a}
is edgless. Hence
\eta:[0,c)\to M
,
s\to\eta(s)
, is past inextendible in
H^{+}(S_{a})
. Let
\{s_{k}\}
be an increasing sequence of numbers such that
s_{k}\to c
, and let
q_{k}=\eta(s_{k})
. Then
\{\tau(q_{k})\}
is a decreasing sequence of numbers; set
b=\inf\{\tau(q_{k})\}\geq a
.

Consider the case
b>a
. Fix a smooth, past directed, timelike vector field
T
on
M
. Let
\gamma_{k}
be an integral curve of
T
, starting at
q_{k}
.
\gamma_{k}
meets
S_{a}
at a point
p_{k}
, say (
[
7
, Lemma 14.51]
). Since
\tau(p_{k})=a<b<\tau(q_{k})
, continuity of
\tau
implies that
\gamma_{k}
meets
S_{b}
at a point
r_{k}
, say. By the future causal completeness of
S_{b}
,
J^{-}(q_{1})\cap J^{+}(S_{b})
has compact closure in
S_{b}
. Hence, by passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
r_{k}
converges to a point
r\in S_{b}
.

Let
U
be a small neighborhood of
r
in
S_{b}

with compact closure.
Using the flow of the vector field
T
near
\overline{U}
, we obtain a small compact spacetime neighborhood
V\cong\overline{U}\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]
of
r
, which is contained in
D(S_{b})=D^{+}(S_{b})\cup D^{-}(S_{b})
. Let
A
be the set of all points
x\in M
such that
x
lies on an integral curve of
T
that passes through
\overline{U}
. Then, by continuity of
\tau
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\{x\in A:b-\delta<\tau(x)<b+\delta\}\subset V\,.

It follows that for
k
sufficiently large,
q_{k}\in V\cap J^{+}(S_{b})\subset D^{+}(S_{b})
. But this means that the null geodesic
\eta
must extend to
S_{b}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
in this case,
H^{+}(S_{a})~=~\emptyset
.

The argument for the case
b=a
is essentially the same. The future causal completeness of
S_{a}
, implies that the
p_{k}^{\prime}s
subconverge to a point
p\in S_{a}
. Now apply the same argument around the point
p
as was done around the point
r
above. Thus, in either case, we conclude that
S_{a}
is future Cauchy.
That
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic follows from the fact that
I^{+}(S_{a})={\rm int}\,D^{+}(S_{a})
is globally hyperbolic for all
a\in\tau(M)
.
∎

Theorem
3
immediate implies the following refinement of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\tau
be a locally anti-Lipschitz time function on a spacetime
(M,g)
such that all level sets are compact. Then the null distance encodes causality, i.e., (
2
) holds on
M
. Furthermore,
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic.

Remark.
In this case the level sets are Cauchy hypersurfaces. This can be seen, for example, by applying Lemma
4
and its time dual to each level surface.

Global Lipschitz stability for an inverse coefficient problem for a mean field game system


Abstract
For an inverse coefficient problem of determining a state-varying factor in the corresponding Hamiltonian for a mean field game system, we prove the global Lipschitz stability by spatial data of one component and interior data in an arbitrarily chosen subdomain over a time interval. The proof is based on Carleman estimates with different norms.

1
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary

\partial\Omega
, and let
T>0
, and
Q:=\Omega\times(0,T)
.
In article concerned a system of
the mean field game:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\Delta u(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}p(x)|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}-c_{0}(x)v(x,t)=0\quad\mbox{in $Q$},\\
&\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\Delta v(x,t)-\mbox{div}\thinspace(p(x)v(x,t)\nabla u(x,t))=0\quad\mbox{in $Q$},\\
&u|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}=v|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}=0.\end{array}\right.

[formula]

In (1.1),
x
and
t
are the state and the time variables,
and
u
and
v
denote the value of the game and the population
density of players respectively (e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
12
]
).
We note that
p(x)
specifies the Hamiltonian for (1.1).

We consider

Inverse coefficient problem.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega
be an arbitrarily chosen subdomain and

t_{0}\in(0,T)
,
\delta_{0}>0
be small.
One need to determine
p(x)
,
x\in\Omega
by

u|_{\omega\times(t_{0}-\delta_{0}\,t_{0}+\delta_{0})}
,

v|_{\omega\times(t_{0}-\delta_{0}\,t_{0}+\delta_{0})}
and

u(\cdot,t_{0})
in
\Omega
.

Henceforth we set
\partial_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq d
,
and define

H^{2,1}(Q):=\{u\in L^{2}(Q);\,\nabla u,\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u,\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}(Q),\,\,1\leq i,j\leq d\}
, and
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)

denotes the Sobolev space of functions whose first partial derivatives are in
L^{\infty}(\Omega).

We state our main result.

Theorem 1.

For
\ell=1,2
, let
(u_{\ell},v_{\ell})\in(H^{2,1}(Q))^{2}
satisfy (1.1)
with
p:=p_{\ell}
.
We assume that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\|p_{\ell}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M,\quad\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M,\\
&\|u_{\ell}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(Q)}+\|\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega))}\leq M,\,\|v_{\ell}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(Q)}+\|\partial_{t}v_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega))}\leq M,\\
&(\partial_{t}^{k}u_{\ell},\partial_{t}^{k}v_{\ell})\in(H^{2,1}(Q))^{2},\quad k=0,1,\,\ell=1,2\end{array}\right.

[formula]

and there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

|\nabla u_{1}(x,t_{0})|\geq\delta,\,\,x\in\Omega\quad\mbox{or}\quad|\nabla u_{2}(x,t_{0})|\geq\delta,\,\,x\in\Omega.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(M,\delta)>0
such that

\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|u_{1}(\cdot,t_{0})-u_{2}(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))}+\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))}).

We emphasize the following features of Theorem 1:

(1)

The observation subdomain
\omega\subset\Omega
can be
arbitrarily small.

(2)

Lipschitz stability over
\Omega
.

(3)

We need the positiveness of
|\nabla u_{1}|
or

|\nabla u_{2}|
only at one moment
t=t_{0}
.

(4)

We do not need spatial data neither
v_{1}
nor
v_{2}
in
\Omega\times\{t_{0}\}
.

The features (1) - (3) are inevitable consequences of our methodology (e.g.,

[
6
]
). The last (4) is a new aspect by that
the unknown is only one spatial function, so that we do not need

v(\cdot,t_{0})
but one spatial data are sufficient. We remark that
the stability and the uniqueness are open in the cases of
t_{0}=0
and
t_{0}=T
.

As for inverse problems for mean field games, see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,

[
7
]
-
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
-
[
16
]
. In particular,

[
8
]
proves Hölder stability with extra data compare to the above inverse problem for the case where
the equation contain special non-local term.

The proof of Theorem 1 is based on Carleman estimates: Lemma 2
in Section 2 and the modified argument in
[
6
]
.

2
Main Carleman estimate

Setting
y:=u_{1}-u_{2}
,
z:=v_{1}-v_{2}
and
f:=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}-p_{2})
,

g:=|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}
,
h:=2v_{1}\nabla u_{1}
,

r_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}p_{2}(\nabla u_{1}+\nabla u_{2})
,

r_{2}:=p_{2}\nabla u_{1}
and
r_{3}:=p_{2}v_{2}.
From (1.2) we obtain
the linearized system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}y(x,t)+\Delta y(x,t)=c_{0}z+r_{1}\cdot\nabla y+g(x,t)f(x)\quad\mbox{in $Q$},\\
&\partial_{t}z(x,t)-\Delta z(x,t)=\mbox{div}\thinspace(r_{2}z+r_{3}\nabla y)+\mbox{div}\thinspace(h(x,t)f(x))\quad\mbox{in $Q$}\end{array}\right.

[formula]

with
y=z=0
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
.
By (1.2) we see that

\partial_{t}^{j}r_{k},\partial_{t}^{j}g,\partial_{t}^{j}h\in L^{\infty}(Q)\quad\mbox{with $1\leq k\leq 3$ and $j=0,1$}.

[formula]

For the Carleman estimates, we introduce
\eta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\eta>0
in
\Omega
,
\eta|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and

|\nabla\eta|>0
on
\overline{\Omega\setminus\omega_{0}}
,
where
\omega_{0}
is some subdomain such that

\overline{\omega_{0}}\subset\omega
. (For existence of such a function see e.g.
[
2
]
.)
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
t_{0}=\frac{1}{2}T
and

(t_{0}-\delta_{0}\,t_{0}+\delta_{0})=(0,T)
.

Fixing a constant
\lambda>0
sufficiently large, we
set
\mu(t):=t(T-t)
,
Q_{\omega}:=\omega\times(0,T)
, and

\varphi(x,t):=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{\mu(t)},\quad\alpha(x,t):=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}-e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}}{\mu(t)},\quad(x,t)\in Q.

Then we state Carleman estimates for single parabolic equations, which
can be both backward and forward.

Lemma 1.

Let
\widetilde{y},\widetilde{z}\in H^{2,1}(Q)
and

\widetilde{y}|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}=\widetilde{z}|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}=0
.
There exist constants
s_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for all
s>s_{0}
, we have

\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\widetilde{y}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\widetilde{y}|^{2}\right)+s\varphi|\nabla\widetilde{y}|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|\widetilde{y}|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\leq C\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}\widetilde{y}+\Delta\widetilde{y}|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q_{\omega}}s^{3}\varphi^{3}|\widetilde{y}|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

[formula]

and

\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\nabla\widetilde{z}|^{2}+s\varphi|\widetilde{z}|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}|G|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q_{\omega}}s\varphi|\widetilde{z}|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

[formula]

where
\partial_{t}\widetilde{z}-\Delta\widetilde{z}=\mbox{div}\thinspace G
in
Q
.

The Carleman estimate (2.3) can be proved by
applying
\alpha(x,t)=\alpha(x,T-t)
and
\varphi(x,t)=\varphi(x,T-t)
for
(x,t)\in Q
to the Carleman estimate in
[
2
]
,
while the proof of (2.4) is
found in Imanuvilov and Yamamoto
[
5
]
.

Henceforth
C>0
denotes generic constants independent of
s>0
.
We define

D(y,z):=\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s^{3}\varphi^{3}|y|^{2}+s\varphi|z|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt
.
Setting
y_{1}:=\partial_{t}y
and
z_{1}:=\partial_{t}z
, and differentiating equations (2.1) respect to
t
we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}y_{1}+\Delta y_{1}=c_{0}z_{1}+r_{1}\cdot\nabla y_{1}+(\partial_{t}r_{1})\cdot\nabla y+(\partial_{t}g)f\quad\mbox{in $Q$},\\
&\partial_{t}z_{1}-\Delta z_{1}=\mbox{div}\thinspace(r_{2}z_{1}+r_{3}\nabla y_{1})+\mbox{div}\thinspace((\partial_{t}r_{2})z+(\partial_{t}r_{3})\nabla y)+\mbox{div}\thinspace((\partial_{t}h)f)\quad\mbox{in $Q$}.\end{array}\right.

[formula]

Applying (2.3) and (2.4) to the first and the second
equations in (2.1) respectively, in terms of (2.2) we have

\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}y|^{2}+s\varphi|\nabla y|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|y|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}|z|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD(y,z)

[formula]

and

\int_{Q}s\varphi|z|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}(|y|^{2}+|\nabla y|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD(y,z).

[formula]

Here in terms of (2.2), we estimate
\int_{Q}|r_{1}\cdot\nabla y|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}|\nabla y|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt
, and this
term can be absorbed into
\int_{Q}s\varphi|\nabla y|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

on the left-hand side of (2.3) for large
s>0
.
Throughout the proof, we repeat similar
estimation with absorption thanks to the large parameter
s>0
.

Adding (2.6) and (2.7), and choosing
s>0
large, we can absorb the
resulting term on the right-hand side
into the left-hand side, so that

\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}y|^{2}+s\varphi|\nabla y|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|y|^{2}+s\varphi|z|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD(y,z).

[formula]

Next, the application of Lemma 1 to (2.5) yields

\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}y_{1}|^{2}+s\varphi|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|y_{1}|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\leq C\int_{Q}(|\nabla y|^{2}+|z_{1}|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD(y_{1},z_{1})

[formula]

and

\int_{Q}s\varphi|z_{1}|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}(|z|^{2}+|\nabla y|^{2}+|\nabla y_{1}|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD(y_{1},z_{1}).

[formula]

Adding (2.9) and (2.10), we can absorb the terms
|z_{1}|^{2}
,

|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}
on the right-hand side
into the left-hand side, we can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}y_{1}|^{2}+s\varphi|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|y_{1}|^{2}+s\varphi|z_{1}|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(|\nabla y|^{2}+|z|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD(y_{1},z_{1}).

Substituting (2.8) into the first term on the right-hand side, we reach

Lemma 2 (key Carleman estimate.

There exist constants
s_{0}>0
and
C>
such that

\int_{Q}\biggl(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}^{2}y|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|\partial_{t}y|^{2})+s\varphi(|z|^{2}+|\partial_{t}z|^{2})\biggr)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C(D(y,z)+D(y_{1},z_{1}))

for all
s>s_{0}
.

3
Completion of the proof of Theorem 1.

By
e^{2s\alpha(x,0)}=0
for
x\in\Omega
, and

|\partial_{t}\varphi|\leq C\varphi^{2},|\partial_{t}\alpha|\leq C\varphi^{2}
in
Q
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,t_{0})^{-1}|\partial_{t}y(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx=\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{-1}|\partial_{t}y|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dx\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(-(\partial_{t}\varphi)\varphi^{-2}|\partial_{t}y|^{2}+2s\varphi^{-1}|\partial_{t}y|^{2}(\partial_{t}\alpha)+2\varphi^{-1}(\partial_{t}y)(\partial_{t}^{2}y))e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(|\partial_{t}y|^{2}+s\varphi|\partial_{t}y|^{2}+|\partial_{t}y||\partial_{t}^{2}y|)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}(s\varphi|\partial_{t}y|^{2}+|\partial_{t}y||\partial_{t}^{2}y|)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(s\varphi|\partial_{t}y|^{2}+\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}^{2}y|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt.

Here we used

|\partial_{t}y||\partial_{t}^{2}y|=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s\varphi}}|\partial_{t}^{2}y|\right)(\sqrt{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}y|)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}^{2}y|^{2}+s\varphi|\partial_{t}y|^{2}\right)
.

Therefore, by
\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,t_{0})^{-1}>0
,
Lemma 2 yields

\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}y(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,t_{0})^{-1}|\partial_{t}y(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C_{s}\mathcal{D}.

Here and henceforth we set

\mathcal{D}:=\|y\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))}+\|z\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))}
.

We can assume that
|\nabla u_{1}(x,t_{0})|>0
for

x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Then, since

|g(x,t_{0})|=|\nabla u_{1}(x,t_{0})|^{2}>0

for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and

g(x,t_{0})f(x)=\partial_{t}y(x,t_{0})+(\Delta y-r_{1}\nabla y)(x,t_{0})-c_{0}(x)z(x,t_{0})
for
x\in\Omega
,
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C_{s}\mathcal{D}+C_{s}\|y(\cdot,t_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+C\int_{\Omega}|z(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx.

[formula]

Next

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|z(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,t_{0})^{-1}|z(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{-1}|z|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dx\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(-(\partial_{t}\varphi)\varphi^{-2}|z|^{2}+\varphi^{-1}|z|^{2}2s(\partial_{t}\alpha)+\varphi^{-1}2z(\partial_{t}z))e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(s\varphi|z|^{2}+|z||\partial_{t}z|)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}(s\varphi|z|^{2}+|z|^{2}+|\partial_{t}z|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt.

Therefore Lemma 2 yields

\int_{\Omega}|z(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C_{s}\mathcal{D}
,
with which (3.1) implies

\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C_{s}(\mathcal{D}+\|y(\cdot,t_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)})

[formula]

for all large
s>0
.

On the other hand,

\int_{Q}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt=\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}\left(\int^{T}_{0}e^{2s(\alpha(x,t)-\alpha(x,t_{0}))}dt\right)dx.

Since
\mu(t_{0})>\mu(t)
for
t\neq t_{0}
, we verify

\alpha(x,t_{0})-\alpha(x,t)\geq C_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(t)}-\frac{1}{\mu(t_{0})}\right)

for
(x,t)\in Q
, where
C_{0}:=e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}
.

Hence,

\int^{T}_{0}e^{2s(\alpha(x,t)-\alpha(x,t_{0}))}dt\leq\int^{T}_{0}\exp\left(-2sC_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(t)}-\frac{1}{\mu(t_{0})}\right)\right)dt

for
x\in\Omega
.
Since

\lim_{s\to\infty}\exp\left(-2sC_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(t)}-\frac{1}{\mu(t_{0})}\right)\right)=0
if
t\neq t_{0}
and

\exp\left(-2sC_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(t)}-\frac{1}{\mu(t_{0})}\right)\right)\leq 1
for
s>0
and
0\leq t\leq T
, the Lebesgue convergence theorem yields

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int^{T}_{0}e^{2s(\alpha(x,t)-\alpha(x,t_{0}))}dt=o(1)
as
s\to\infty
,
and so

\int_{Q}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt=o(1)\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx
.
Substituting this into (3.2) and choosing
s>0
large, we reach

\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C_{s}(\mathcal{D}+\|y(\cdot,t_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)})
.

\blacksquare

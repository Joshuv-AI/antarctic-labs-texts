Analysis Accelerated Mirror Descent via High-resolution ODEs Thanks: This work was partially supported by grant 12288201 from NSF of China
Ya-xiang Yuan, Yi Zhang

Abstract
Mirror descent plays a crucial role in constrained optimization and acceleration schemes, along with its corresponding low-resolution ordinary differential equations (ODEs) framework have been proposed. However, the low-resolution ODEs are unable to distinguish between Polyak’s heavy-ball method and Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method. This problem also arises with accelerated mirror descent. To address this issue, we derive high-resolution ODEs for accelerated mirror descent and propose a general Lyapunov function framework to analyze its convergence rate in both continuous and discrete time. Furthermore, we demonstrate that accelerated mirror descent can minimize the squared gradient norm at an inverse cubic rate.

Introduction

During the era of big data, machine learning has emerged as a crucial method for discovering information in high-dimensional datasets. Reinforcement learning, as a key branch of machine learning, frequently involves solving optimization problems within specific convex sets. However, due to the curse of dimensionality, researchers have focused on simple first-order methods in both theoretical and practical contexts.

Throughout this paper, we will be considering constrained optimization problems,

\min_{x\in C}f(x),

(1)

where
f
is a smooth convex function and
C
is a closed convex set. For unconstrained optimization problem, perhaps gradient descent is the simplest first-order method. Mirror descent proposed in
[
14
]
provides an efficient generalization of gradient descent to non-Euclidean geometries in constrained optimization, and has variety applications in policy gradient
[
24
]
, as well as online optimization
[
6
,
7
]
. Fixing a step size
s
, mirror descent is given by the recursive rule

x_{k+1}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{x}\left\{s\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{D_{\varphi}(x,x_{k})}{\sigma}\right\},

(2)

where
D_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot)
represents Bergman divergence respect to a given
\sigma-
strongly convex function
\varphi
on
C
.

However, gradient descent methods always converge slowly in practice. About 40 years ago, Nesterov proposed the following accelerated gradient scheme
[
15
,
19

\begin{split}y_{k+1}&=x_{k}-\frac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&=y_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+3}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}).\end{split}

(3)

Where the parameter
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla f
. This scheme achieves the convergence rate

f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})\leqslant O\left(\frac{\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}}{k^{2}}\right),

compared to the convergence rate of gradient descent

f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})\leqslant O\left(\frac{\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}}{k}\right).

Above

[

]

[

,

[

. In
[

\begin{split}y_{k+1}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{y}\left\{\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),y-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\middle|y\in C\right\},\\
z_{k+1}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{z}\left\{\frac{L}{\sigma}\varphi(z)+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{i+1}{2}[f(x_{i})+\left\langle\nabla f(x_{i}),z-x_{i}\right\rangle]\middle|z\in C\right\},\\
x_{k+1}&=\frac{2}{k+3}z_{k+1}+\frac{k+1}{k+3}y_{k+1}.\end{split}

(4)

The initial feasible point
x_{0}
is the
\varphi
-center of
C

\varphi
over
C

O(1/k^{2})

Notations

We almost follow the notations of
[
16
]
. Let
E
denotes a finite-dimensional real vector spaces,
E^{*}
be the dual space of
E
. The value of function
s\in E^{*},x\in E
is denoted as
\left\langle s,x\right\rangle.
The norm of
E
is denoted as
\left\|\cdot\right\|
, the inner product of
E
is
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
, and
\left\|x\right\|_{2}
is
\sqrt{\left\langle x,x\right\rangle}
. Let
\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L}
be the class of
L-
smooth convex functions, i. e.
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L}
if and only if
f
is a differentiable convex function and
\nabla f
is
L-
Lipschitz continuous. Given a convex function
f
,
f^{*}:E^{*}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
denotes conjugate function of
f
, whose function value at
s\in E^{*}
is given by
f^{*}(s)=\sup_{x\in E}\{\left\langle s,x\right\rangle-f(x)\}.
For a convex function
\varphi
, the Bergman-divergence of
\varphi
is

D_{\varphi}(y,x)=\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)-\left\langle\varphi^{\prime}(x),y-x\right\rangle,

above
\varphi^{\prime}(x)\in\partial\varphi(x)
.
\varphi
is said to be
\sigma-
strongly convex if
D_{\varphi}(y,x)\geqslant\dfrac{\sigma}{2}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}.
The
x^{*}\in C
represents the minimizer of
f
over closed convex set
C
.

2
Mirror Descent

The mirror descent with constant step size is given by the recursive rule

x_{k+1}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{x}\left\{s\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{D_{\varphi}(x,x_{k})}{\sigma}\right\},

where
D_{\varphi}(y,x)
is the Bergman divergence respect to
\sigma-
strongly convex function
\varphi
defined on
C
. To derive ODEs for mirror descent, we need to obtain an explicit iteration rule like gradient descent rather than the recursive rule given by the solution of another optimization problem. One can use the well known theorem that
\varphi
is
\sigma-
strongly convex if and only if
\varphi^{*}\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{\sigma^{-1}}
, and then transform the above iteration rule into the following algorithm from mirror map view

\begin{split}z_{k+1}&=z_{k}-\sigma s\nabla f(x_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&=\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{k+1}),\end{split}

(7)

with initial
\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{0})=x_{0}
. As
s\to 0
, we achieve the continuous limit of (
7
):

\begin{split}\overset{\cdot}{Z}&=-\sigma\nabla f(X),\\
X&=\nabla\varphi^{*}(Z),\\
Z(0)&=z_{0},X(0)=x_{0}\text{ with }\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{0})=x_{0}.\end{split}

(8)

Next, we construct Lyapunov functions to analyze the dynamics of trajectories of (
8
). Enlightened by the Lyapunov functions in
[
22
]
for gradient flow and the Lyapunov function in
[
12
]
for accelerated mirror descent, we propose the following Lyapunov function for (
8
)

\mathcal{E}(t)=t\sigma[f(X(t))-f(x^{*})]+D_{\varphi^{*}}(Z(t),z^{*}),

(9)

where
\nabla\varphi^{*}(z^{*})=x^{*}
. The first term can be regraded as the potential term and the second term is a mix. The significant of mixed term in (
9
) is that the derivative of
Z
is equal to
-\nabla f(X)
. It’s worthwhile to mention that the mixed term should not be the Bergman-divergence on
E
. The main reason is that
\varphi
isn’t differentiable in general, which make
D_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot)
ill-defined. Right now, we can investigate the derivative of
\mathcal{E}(t)
that play a central role in characterizing properties of (
8
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
X(t),Z(t)
be the solution to (
8
). If
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L}
and
\varphi
is twice continuous differentiable, then

\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leqslant-t\sigma\|\overset{\cdot}{X}\|^{2}.

Proof.

By definition of the Bergman divergence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+t\sigma\left\langle\nabla f(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle+\left\langle\overset{\cdot}{Z},\nabla\varphi^{*}(Z)-\nabla\varphi^{*}(z^{*})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\sigma\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle-t\left\langle\overset{\cdot}{Z},\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle-\sigma\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-t\left\langle\nabla^{2}\varphi(X)\overset{\cdot}{X},\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant-t\sigma\|\overset{\cdot}{X}\|^{2}.

∎

From the above theorem, We can now analysis the convergence rate of trajectories of (
8
).

Corollary 1
.

Let
X(t),Z(t)
be the solution to (
8
). If
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L}
and
\varphi
is twice continuous differentiable, then

\displaystyle f(X(t))-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{0},z^{*})}{t\sigma},

\displaystyle\inf_{0\leqslant u\leqslant t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}(u)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
1
, we have
\mathcal{E}
is a non-increasing function respect to
t
, which leads to

\sigma[f(X(t))-f(x^{*})]\leqslant\mathcal{E}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{0},z^{*}).

Diving
\sigma
on both sides of inequality, we get the convergence rate of function value.

Inspired by
[
9
]
, we integrate the inequality in Theorem
1
from
\frac{t}{2}
to
t
, and have

\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(u)du\leqslant-\sigma\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}u\|\overset{\cdot}{X}(u)\|^{2}du\leqslant-\sigma\inf_{\frac{t}{2}\leqslant u\leqslant t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}(u)\|^{2}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}udu=-\frac{3\sigma t^{2}}{8}\inf_{\frac{t}{2}\leqslant u\leqslant t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}(u)\|^{2}.

Thus

\inf_{0\leqslant u\leqslant t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}(u)\|^{2}\leqslant\inf_{\frac{t}{2}\leqslant u\leqslant t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}(u)\|^{2}\leqslant\frac{8}{3\sigma t^{2}}\left[\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)-\mathcal{E}(t)\right].

Since the non-negative function
\mathcal{E}(t)
is non-increasing on
(0,+\infty)
,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)
exists. Thus by Cauchy’s criteria, we have
\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)-\mathcal{E}(t)=o(1)
. In conclusion,

\inf_{0\leqslant u\leqslant t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}(u)\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right).

∎

In practice, the conjugate of
\varphi
is hard to be calculated, which limits its application to estimate the upper bound of complexity of optimization problem. To overcome such problem, we translate the Bergman-divergence respect to the dual space
E^{*}
into the Bergman-divergence on
E
via the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

If
\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{0})=x_{0},\nabla\varphi^{*}(z^{*})=x^{*}
, then

D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{0},z^{*})=D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0}).

Proof.

Due to Theorem 23.5 in
[
20
]
, under the assumption of Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{0})+\varphi^{*}(z_{0})=\left\langle z_{0},x_{0}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\varphi(x^{*})+\varphi^{*}(z^{*})=\left\langle z^{*},x^{*}\right\rangle.

Thus

\displaystyle D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{0},z^{*})

\displaystyle=\varphi(x^{*})-\varphi(x_{0})-\left\langle z_{0}-z^{*},x^{*}\right\rangle+\left\langle z_{0},x_{0}\right\rangle-\left\langle z^{*},x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\varphi(x^{*})-\varphi(x_{0})-\left\langle z_{0},x^{*}-x_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0}).

∎

With such observation, we have

Corollary 2
.

Let
X(t),Z(t)
be the solutions to (
8
). If
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L}
and
\varphi
is twice continuous differentiable, then

\displaystyle f(X(t))-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0})}{t\sigma}.

Now we translate the continuous-time Lyapunov function into discrete-time Lyapunov function to analysis the mirror descent. Motivated by (
9
), we consider the following discrete-time Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{E}(k)\overset{\Delta}{=}\mathcal{E}(ks)=ks\sigma[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]+D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{k},z^{*}).

(10)

Analogous to the continuous-time Lyapunov functions, the difference of discrete-time Lyapunov functions
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
is also importance to investigate convergence results of mirror descent. Similar to continuous-time cases, the upper bound of
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
is dominated by
-\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}
. With such observation, we can conclude the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1,1}_{L},\varphi
is
\sigma-
strongly convex. If the step-size
s\in[0,\dfrac{2}{L})
, then the sequences
\{z_{k}\},\{x_{k}\}
generated by (
7
) satisfies

\displaystyle f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})\leqslant\frac{D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0})}{k\sigma s},

\displaystyle\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|x_{i+1}-x_{i}\right\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right).

The details of proof can be found in
A.1
. As we can see, Theorem
2
is the discrete version of Theorem
1
with time
t_{k}=ks
expect the assumptions they required.

3
Accelerated Mirror Descent

3.1
High-resolution ODE for accelerated mirror descent

The accelerated mirror descent proposed in
[
16
]
with fixed step-size
s
is given by the recursive rule

\begin{split}y_{k+1}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{y}\left\{s\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),y-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\middle|y\in C\right\},\\
z_{k+1}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{z}\left\{\frac{1}{\sigma s}\varphi(z)+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{i+1}{2}[f(x_{i})+\left\langle\nabla f(x_{i}),z-x_{i}\right\rangle]\middle|z\in C\right\},\\
x_{k+1}&=\frac{2}{k+3}z_{k+1}+\frac{k+1}{k+3}y_{k+1}.\end{split}

To build up the ODEs for it, we need to derive explicit solutions of optimization algorithms appear above under additional conditions. The solution of gradient map can be solve explicit if
C=E
and the norm is Euclidean norm. Under these assumption, we have

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\arg\min}_{z}\left\{\frac{1}{\sigma s}\varphi(z)+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{i+1}{2}[f(x_{i})+\left\langle\nabla f(x_{i}),z-x_{i}\right\rangle]\right\},

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{k+3}z_{k+1}+\frac{k+1}{k+3}[x_{k}-s\nabla f(x_{k})].

Next we use the mirror-map to derive the explicit formula for
z_{k+1}
. The first-order optimal condition for
z_{k+1}
is

-\sigma s\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{i+1}{2}\nabla f(x_{i})\in\partial\varphi(z_{k+1}).

Let
\tilde{z}_{k+1}=-\sigma s\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{i+1}{2}\nabla f(x_{i})
. The above relation can be rewritten as
\tilde{z}_{k+1}\in\partial\varphi(z_{k+1})
, or
z_{k+1}=\nabla\varphi^{*}(\tilde{z}_{k+1})
owing to that
\varphi^{*}
is differentiable and its derivative is the inverse of
\partial\varphi
. Noticing that
\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\tilde{z}_{k}=-\sigma s\nabla f(x_{k})
, we have the following reformed iteration rule.

\begin{split}\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\tilde{z}_{k}&=-\sigma s\frac{k+1}{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\
(k+3)x_{k+1}&=2\nabla\varphi^{*}(\tilde{z}_{k+1})+(k+1)[x_{k}-s\nabla f(x_{k})].\end{split}

(11)

Replace
\tilde{z}_{k+1}
by
z_{k+1}
, and slightly modify the iteration rule, we have

\begin{split}\frac{z_{k+1}-z_{k}}{\sqrt{s}}&=-\sigma\frac{(k+1)\sqrt{s}}{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\
(k+1)\sqrt{s}\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}{\sqrt{s}}&=2\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{k+1})-2x_{k+1}-[(k+1)\sqrt{s}]\sqrt{s}\nabla f(x_{k}).\end{split}

(12)

(
12
) can be seen as applying forward-backward Euler scheme to the high-resolution ODE:

\begin{split}\overset{\cdot}{Z}&=-\frac{t\sigma}{2}\nabla f(X),\\
\overset{\cdot}{X}&=\frac{2}{t}\nabla\varphi^{*}(Z)-\frac{2}{t}X-\underbrace{\sqrt{s}\nabla f(X)}_{\text{gradient correction term}},\\
X(0)&=x_{0},Z(0)=z_{0}\text{ with }\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{0})=x_{0},\overset{\cdot}{X}(0)=0,\end{split}

(13)

with time
t_{k}=(k+1)\sqrt{s}
and forward step to gradient correction term. Before taking a deep look at the behavior of (
13
), we discuss the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
13
). In the previous work
[
12
]
, they prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution to low-resolution ODEs by considering the equi-Lipschitz-continuous and uniformly bounded family of solution
(X_{\delta},Z_{\delta})|_{[0,t_{0}]}
to

\begin{split}\overset{\cdot}{Z}&=-\frac{t}{r}\nabla f(X),\\
\overset{\cdot}{X}&=\frac{r}{\max\{t,\delta\}}(\nabla\varphi^{*}(Z)-X),\\
X(0)&=x_{0},Z(0)=z_{0}\text{ with }\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{0})=x_{0},\overset{\cdot}{X}(0)=0.\end{split}

Due to Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, as
\delta\to 0
, the solution of low-resolution is obtained. In high-resolution ODEs case, we only introduce the term
\sqrt{s}\nabla f(X)
, which is Lipschitz continuous respect to
X
and ensure the existence of
(X_{\delta},Z_{\delta})
. The rest of the proof is analogous to the previous discussion.

Now we establish the convergence rate of (
13
). First, we consider the Lyapunov function

\mathcal{E}(t)=t^{2}\sigma[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+4D_{\varphi^{*}}(Z,z^{*}).

(14)

Before estimate the differentiability of
\mathcal{E}(t)
, we first remark that we prefer the following ODEs form

\begin{split}\overset{\cdot}{Z}&=-\frac{t}{2}\nabla f(X),\\
\nabla\varphi^{*}(Z)&=\frac{t}{2}\overset{\cdot}{X}+\frac{t}{2}\sqrt{s}\nabla f(X)+X,\end{split}

(15)

because the information of
\nabla\varphi^{*}(Z)
plays a central role in estimate the upper bound of
\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)
. The upper bound of
\mathcal{E}(t)
is given as followed.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X(t),Z(t)
be the solutions to (
13
), then

\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leqslant-{t^{2}\sigma}\left\|\nabla f(X)\right\|^{2}_{2}.

Proof.

The upper bound of derivative of (
14
) can be estimated by the following inequalities.

\displaystyle\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)=

\displaystyle 2t\sigma[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+t^{2}\sigma\left\langle\nabla f(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle+4\left\langle\overset{\cdot}{Z},\nabla\varphi^{*}(Z)-\nabla\varphi^{*}(z^{*})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2t\sigma\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle+{t^{2}\sigma}\left\langle\nabla f(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle-\sigma\left\langle t\nabla f(X),t\overset{\cdot}{X}+2X-2x^{*}+t\nabla f(X)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{2}\sigma\left\|\nabla f(X)\right\|^{2}_{2}.

∎

With Theorem
3
, we can characterize properties of trajectories of (
15
) by using technique similar to the proof of corollary
1
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
X(t),Z(t)
be the solutions to (
13
), then

\displaystyle f(X(t))-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0})}{t^{2}\sigma},

\displaystyle\inf_{0\leqslant u\leqslant t}\left\|\nabla f(X(u))\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}(t)
is non-increasing, we have

t^{2}\sigma[f(X(t))-f(x^{*})]\leqslant\mathcal{E}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=4D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{0},z^{*})=4D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0}).

The last equality is due to Lemma
1
. Thus we reach the convergence rate of function value. For the convergence rate of gradient norm, we integrate the inequality in Theorem
3
for
\frac{t}{2}
to
t
, and get

\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(u)du\leqslant-\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}t^{2}\sigma\left\|\nabla f(X(u))\right\|^{2}_{2}du\leqslant-\frac{7t^{3}\sigma}{24}\inf_{\frac{t}{2}\leqslant u\leqslant t}\left\|\nabla f(X(u))\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Or

\inf_{\frac{t}{2}\leqslant u\leqslant t}\left\|\nabla f(X(u))\right\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\frac{24}{7t^{3}\sigma}\left[\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)-\mathcal{E}(t)\right]

Since the non-negative value function
\mathcal{E}(t)
is non-increasing on
(0,+\infty)
,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)
exists. Thus by Cauchy’s criteria, we have
\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)-\mathcal{E}(t)=o(1)
, thus

\inf_{0\leqslant u\leqslant t}\left\|\nabla f(X(u))\right\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\inf_{\frac{t}{2}\leqslant u\leqslant t}\left\|\nabla f(X(u))\right\|^{2}_{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right).

∎

3.2
The discrete cases with
C=E
and Euclidean norm

This section is devoted to analyze the convergence rates of (
4
) under the assumption that
C=E
equipped with Euclidean norm, i. e. the recursive rule described in (
11
). It can be seen as the non-Euclidean extension of Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method, which inspired us to generalize the Lyapunov function in
[
23
]
for NAG-C with more refined convergence results. First, we construct the following Lyapunov function

\mathcal{E}(k)=(k+1)(k+2)\sigma s[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]+4D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{k+1},z^{*}).

(16)

The upper bound of the difference
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
is estimated by the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let the sequences
\{x_{k}\},\{z_{k}\}
generated by the recursive rule

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=z_{k}-\frac{(k+1)\sigma s}{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(k+3)x_{k+1}-(k+1)x_{k}+(k+1)s\nabla f(x_{k})\right].

If
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L}
,
\varphi
is
\sigma-
strongly convex function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<s\leqslant\frac{1}{L}
, then

\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant-\frac{(k+1)(k+2)\sigma s^{2}}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x_{k})\right\|^{2}.

The details of the proof can be found in
B.1
. With such important estimation, we have the following result.

Corollary 4
.

If
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L}
,
\varphi
is
\sigma-
strongly convex function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<s\leqslant\frac{1}{L}
, then the sequences
\{x_{k}\},\{z_{k}\}
generated by (
11
) satisfy

\displaystyle f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\sigma s[f(x_{0})-f(x^{*})]+4D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{1},z^{*})}{(k+1)(k+2)\sigma s},

\displaystyle\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|\nabla f(x_{i})\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right).

Proof.

Theorem
4
implies that
\{\mathcal{E}(k)\}
is non-increasing, which implies

(k+1)(k+2)\sigma s[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]\leqslant\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=2\sigma s[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]+4D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{1},z^{*}).

Diving
(k+1)(k+2)\sigma s
on both sides of the above inequality, we obtain the required inequality. Next, summing up the inequalities in Theorem
4
from
\left\lfloor\dfrac{k}{2}\right\rfloor
to
k
, we have

-\sum_{i=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor}^{k}\frac{(i+1)(i+2)\sigma s^{2}}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x_{i})\right\|^{2}\leqslant\sum_{i=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor}^{k}\mathcal{E}(i+1)-\mathcal{E}(i)=\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\right).

Thus

\frac{\sigma s^{2}}{6}\min_{\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|\nabla f(x_{i})\right\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\dfrac{\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\right)}{(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)-\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor+2\right)}

Since the non-negative sequence
\{\mathcal{E}(k)\}
is non-increasing,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(k)
exists. By Cauchy’s criteria,
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\right)=o(1)
. In conclusion, we have

\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|\nabla f(x_{i})\right\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\min_{\frac{\lfloor k\rfloor}{2}\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|\nabla f(x_{i})\right\|_{2}^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right).

∎

3.3
The general discrete cases

Now we analysis the original Nesterov’s accelerated mirror descent via Lyapunov function framework. Motivated by the convergence result proven in
[
16
]
and the continuous-time Lyapunov function, we build up the following Lyapunov function.

\mathcal{E}(k)=k(k+1)\sigma s[f(y_{k})-f(x^{*})]+4D_{\varphi^{*}}(z_{k},z^{*}).

(17)

The upper bound of the difference
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
is given by the below theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L},\varphi
be
\sigma-
stongly convex function defined on
C
. Let
\{x_{k}\},\{y_{k}\},\{z_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=z_{k}-\frac{(k+1)\sigma s}{2}\nabla f(x_{k}),

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\arg\min}_{y}\left\{s\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),y\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\|y-x_{k}\right\|^{2}\middle|y\in C\right\},

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{k+3}y_{k+1}+\frac{2}{k+3}\nabla\varphi^{*}(z_{k+1}),

then

\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant-\frac{(k+1)(k+2)(1-Ls)}{2}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}.

The details of the proof can be found in
B.2
. Now we can characterize the convergence results of (
4
).

Corollary 5
.

If
f\in\mathscr{F}^{1}_{L},\varphi
is
\sigma-
stongly convex function defined on
C
, let
\{x_{k}\},\{y_{k}\},\{z_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by (
4
). If
0<s\leqslant\dfrac{1}{L}
, then
\{x_{k}\},\{y_{k}\}
satisfy

\displaystyle f(y_{k})-f(x^{*})\leqslant\frac{4D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0})}{k(k+1)\sigma s}.

In addition,
0<s<\dfrac{1}{L}
,
\{x_{k}\},\{y_{k}\}
satisfy

\inf_{0\leqslant u\leqslant k}\left\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right).

Proof.

From Theorem
5
, we know that
0<s\leqslant\dfrac{1}{L}
is a sufficient condition for
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant 0
. Thus

k(k+1)\sigma s[f(y_{k})-f(x^{*})]\leqslant\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=4D_{\varphi}(x^{*},x_{0}).

By dividing
k(k+1)\sigma s
on both sides, we achieve the desired inequality. In addition, if we sum up the inequalities in Theorem
5
from
\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor
to
k
, then we have

\sum_{i=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor}^{k}\frac{(i+1)(i+2)(1-Ls)}{2}\left\|y_{i+1}-x_{i}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\right).

Thus

\min_{\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|y_{i+1}-x_{i}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\dfrac{6\mathcal{E}(k+1)-6\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{\lfloor k\rfloor}{2}\right)}{\left[(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)-\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor+2\right)\right](1-Ls)}.

Since the non-negative sequence
\{\mathcal{E}(k)\}
is non-increasing,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(k)
exists. By Cauchy’s criteria,
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\right)=o(1)
. In conclusion

\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|y_{i+1}-x_{i}\right\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant\min_{\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|y_{i+1}-x_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right).

∎

3.4
Higher-order mirror descent

In
[
27
]
, Wibisono et al. proposed a higher-order mirror descent, which is a generalization of Nesterov’s accelerated cubic Newton method. The higher-order mirror descent is described as followed:

\begin{split}x_{k+1}&=\frac{p}{k+p}z_{k}+\frac{k}{k+p}y_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&=\mathop{\arg\min}_{z}\left\{Cps(k+1)^{(p-1)}\left\langle\nabla f(y_{k+1}),z\right\rangle+D_{\varphi}(z,z_{k})\right\}.\end{split}

(18)

Where
p\geqslant 2
is an integer,
k^{(p)}=k(k+1)\cdots(k+p-1)
and the sequence
\{y_{k}\}
satisfies

\left\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\right\rangle\geqslant Ms^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left\|\nabla f(y_{k})\right\|_{*}^{\frac{p}{p-1}},

(19)

for some constant
M>0
. Also the Bergman divergence
D_{\varphi}
is required to be lower bounded by
p-
th power of of the norm

D_{\varphi}(x,y)\geqslant\frac{\sigma}{p}\left\|x-y\right\|^{p},\quad\forall x,y\in C.

(20)

Or we say
\varphi
is
\sigma-
strongly convex of order
p
.
[
27
]
proves that if
f
is
p-
times continuous differentiable and
\nabla^{(p-1)}f
is
L-
Lipschitz continuous, then the
y_{k}
generated by the
(p-1)-
th order gradient map

y_{k}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{y}\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{1}{i!}\nabla^{i}f(x)(y-x)^{i}+\frac{N}{sp}\left\|y-x\right\|^{p}\middle|y\in C\right\}

satisfies (
19
) for some chosen
N
. They used estimate-sequence technique to show that if
0<C\leqslant\dfrac{M^{p-1}}{p^{p}}
,
f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})\leqslant O\left(\dfrac{1}{sk^{p}}\right)
. In this paper, we exploit Lyapunov function framework to demonstrate the same convergence rate for function values of higher-order mirror descent with a wider range of parameters. Additionally, we show that the convergence rate of gradient norm minimization is
o\left(\dfrac{1}{k^{p+1}}\right)
. To make use of Lyapunov function framework, we need to translate the recursive rule into the following mirror-map form.

\begin{split}x_{k+1}&=\frac{p}{k+p}z_{k}+\frac{k}{k+p}y_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&=z_{k}-Csp(k+1)^{(p-1)}\nabla f(y_{k+1}).\end{split}

(21)

Before proving the convergence result of higher-order mirror descent, the smoothness of
\varphi^{*}
is required.

Lemma 2
.

If
\varphi
is
\sigma-
strongly convex of order
p
, then
\varphi^{*}
is differentiable. In addition, we have

\varphi^{*}(z^{\prime})\leqslant\varphi^{*}(z)+\left\langle\nabla\varphi^{*}(z),z^{\prime}-z\right\rangle+\frac{p-1}{p}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left\|z^{\prime}-z\right\|^{\frac{p}{p-1}},\quad\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(\varphi).

The proof of Lemma
2
is referred to the proof of Lemma 4.2.2 in
[
19
]
. Finally, we can describe and prove the convergence rate of (
21
).

Theorem 6
.

Let
f
be a continuous differentiable convex function,
\varphi
is
\sigma-
strongly convex of order
p
and
\{x_{k}\},\{y_{k}\},\{z_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by (
21
) and (
19
). If
0<C\leqslant\dfrac{\sigma M^{p-1}}{(p-1)^{p-1}p}
, then

f(y_{k})-f(x^{*})\leqslant\frac{D_{\varphi}(x,x^{*})}{Csk^{(p)}}.

In addition, if
0<C<\dfrac{\sigma M^{p-1}}{(p-1)^{p-1}p}
, then

\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|\nabla f(y_{k})\right\|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}_{*}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{p+1}}\right).

This theorem tells us that Lyapunov function framework is superior to estimate-sequence technique. The convergence result in
[
27
]
is hold when
0<C\leqslant\dfrac{\sigma M^{p-1}}{p^{p}}
, by contrast the assumption in theorem
6
is
0<C\leqslant\dfrac{\sigma M^{p-1}}{(p-1)^{p-1}p}
, which is wider than the previous one. Also estimate-sequence technique cannot tells us any information about the convergence rate of gradient norm, compared to
\min_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}\left\|\nabla f(y_{k})\right\|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}_{*}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{p+1}}\right)
in theorem
6
.

4
Discussion

In this paper, we generalized the second-order high-resolution ODEs for Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method to first-order high-resolution ODEs for Nesterov’s accelerated mirror descent. Also, we presented Lyapunov function framework for characterizing the convergence rates of accelerated schemes in both continuous-time and discrete-time. Finally, we generalized the Lyapunov function framework to higher-order mirror descent.

There are a good many open problems of high-resolution ODEs. First of all, there remains a significant number of algorithms that have not yet been transformed into discretizations of ordinary differential equations. The excessive gap technique proposed by Nesterov for solving primal-dual problems is one such example. In recent times, researchers have increasingly focused on optimization algorithms that involve solving optimization subproblems, such as alternative direction method of multipliers and augmented Lagrangian methods. It would be worthwhile to analyze these algorithms using ODEs and attempt to derive their accelerated variants.

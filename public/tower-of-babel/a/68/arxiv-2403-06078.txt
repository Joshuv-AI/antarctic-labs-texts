Decomposition of Persistent Homology and Spectral Sequences
Peiqi Yang, Yingfeng Hu, Hao Wu

Abstract
. We study the relation between the persistent homology and the spectral sequence of a filtered chain complex over a field. Our method is based on a decomposition of the persistent homology. We demonstrate that, under fairly general assumptions, these two algebraic structures capture the same information from the filtered chain complex.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Filtered chain complexes arise naturally in many applications of algebraic topology. For example, given a point cloud in a metric space, one can consider the abstract simplicial complex consisting of all the possible abstract simplexes with vertices in this cloud. This abstract simplicial complex gives rise a simplicial chain complex, which, by itself, has trivial homology. But, when equipped with the filtration induced by the diameters of simplexes, the filtered chain complex captures much topological information of the point cloud.

There are two main approaches to extracting homological information from a filtered chain complex. The more classical approach is via spectral sequences defined by Jean Leray
[
3
]
, which are hard to compute in most cases. More recently, in the study of point clouds, most researchers opt to use the persistent homology because there are efficient algorithms to compute it
[
2
,
9
]
.

A natural question is how different or similar are the homological data obtained by these two approaches. Saugata Basu and Laxmi Parida
[
1
]
established explicit relations between the spectral sequences and the persistent homology for filtered chain complexes over a field. In fact, similar relations are implicit in the work of the third author
[
8
]
in the context of the Khovanov-Rozansky homology in knot theory. In this paper, we apply the arguments in
[
8
]
to more general filtered chain complexes over fields and, among other things, recover the results in
[
1
]
. Our method is based on decomposition theorems of persistent homology and spectral sequences, which allow us to avoid complicated exact sequence arguments and provide a direct and intuitive explanation why the two approaches yield exactly the same information from a filtered chain complex over a field under fairly general assumptions.

{\mathcal{PC}_{n}=\mathbb{F}[x]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}C_{n}}

g^{(k-1)}(\alpha)=\pi_{x}\circ x^{2-k}(\alpha)=\pi_{x}\circ x^{1-k}(x\alpha)=(\pi_{x}\circ x^{1-k})(f^{(k-1)}(\alpha)).

Therefore
g^{(k)}(\alpha)=\pi_{x}\circ x^{1-k}(\alpha)
. Note that
d^{(k-1)}=g^{(k-1)}\circ h^{(k-1)}=(\pi_{x}\circ x^{2-k})\circ x^{m-1}=\pi_{x}\circ x^{m-k+1}=0
, thus
E^{(k)}\cong E^{(k-1)}\cong H(U_{n,s,m}/xU_{n,s,m})\cong\mathbb{F}||n+1||\{s+m\}\oplus\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
.

Finally consider
r=m+1
. The differential map
d^{(m)}
becomes
d^{(m)}=g^{(m)}\circ h^{(m)}=(\pi_{x}\circ x^{1-m})\circ x^{m-1}=\pi_{x}\circ 1=i
which is an isomorphism on
\mathbb{F}||n+1||\{s+m\}\oplus\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
, then
E^{(m+1)}=H(E^{(m)},i)\cong 0
. Hence
E^{(r)}\cong E^{(m+1)}\cong 0
for all
r>m
.
∎

Under our assumptions, the spectral sequence
\{E^{(r)}\}
does not necessarily collapse in the usual sense.
However, from Lemma
3.3
, each
E^{(r)}(U_{n,s,\infty})
and
E^{(r)}(U_{n,s,m})
both collapse after finite pages. Therefore, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\{E^{(r)}\}
be the spectral sequence in Theorem
1.5
.
Then
\{E^{(r)}\}
collapses locally to
E^{(\infty)}
, which means
\{E_{n,s}^{(r)}\}
collapses to
E_{n,s}^{(\infty)}
for every
(n,s)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

We have another observation from Lemma
3.3
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
K
be a chain complex of free graded
\mathbb{F}[x]
-module satisfying:

•

Its differential
d
is homogeneous and preserves the
\mathbb{F}[x]
-grading of
K
;

•

For each
n
, the
\mathbb{F}[x]
-grading of
K_{n}
is bounded below;

•

For each
n
,
K_{n}
is locally finite dimensional over
\mathbb{F}
.

Then we have that

E^{(1)}(K)\cong H(K/xK)

and that
\{E^{(r)}(K)\}
collapses locally to
E^{(\infty)}(K)
where

E^{(\infty)}(K)\cong H(K/(x-1)K).

In particular, assume that
K
is the persistent chain complex of a filtered chain complex
(C,d,\mathcal{F})
over
\mathbb{F}
satisfying:

(a)

For each
n
, the filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
C_{n}
is bounded below;

(b)

(C,d,\mathcal{F})
is locally finite dimensional over
\mathbb{F}
.

Then
K/xK
is isomorphic to the graded chain complex associated to
(C,d,\mathcal{F})
, and
K/(x-1)K
is isomorphic to the filtered chain complex
(C,d,\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

From Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
, we have that
E^{(1)}(K)\cong H(K/xK)
.
Since
K
satisfies all conditions in Proposition
2.6
, it admits the decomposition in Corollary
2.7
.
It remains to show that

E^{(\infty)}(U_{n,s,\infty})\cong H(U_{n,s,\infty}/(x-1)U_{n,s,\infty})

and

E^{(\infty)}(U_{n,s,m})\cong H(U_{n,s,m}/(x-1)U_{n,s,m}),

where
U_{n,s,\infty}
and
U_{n,s,m}
are given in Definition
1.3
.
From ring isomorphism
\mathbb{F}[x]/(x-1)\cong\mathbb{F}
, we have

U_{n,s,\infty}/(x-1)U_{n,s,\infty}\cong 0\to\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}\to 0

and

U_{n,s,m}/(x-1)U_{n,s,m}\cong 0\to\mathbb{F}||n+1||\{s+m\}\xrightarrow{1}\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}\to 0.

Hence
H(U_{n,s,\infty}/(x-1)U_{n,s,\infty})\cong\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
and
H(U_{n,s,m}/(x-1)U_{n,s,m})\cong 0
.
Comparing these to Lemma
3.3
, we know that
E^{(\infty)}(K)\cong H(K/(x-1)K)
.

Let
K
be the persistent chain complex of a filtered chain complex
(C,d,\mathcal{F})
over
\mathbb{F}
satisfying conditions (a) and (b).
From Definition
1.1
, recall that
x
is a homogeneous element of degree
1
that act on
K
as the natural inclusion map
i^{p}:\mathcal{F}^{p}C\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}^{p+1}C
for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Fix
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
x(\mathcal{F}^{p-1}C)\subset\mathcal{F}^{p}C
.
Thus

K/xK\cong\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}^{p}C/\mathcal{F}^{p-1}C.

From Definition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
, following their notations, we have the differential map
d_{x}
of
K
is given by
(d_{x})_{n}^{p}=\sum_{q\leq p}x^{p-q}d_{n}^{p,q}
.
Then
d_{x}
induces a differential map
d_{0*}
in
K/xK
given by

\bigl(d_{0*}\bigr)_{n}^{p}=d_{n}^{p,p}=\pi_{n-1}^{p,p}\circ d_{n}^{p},

where
\pi_{n-1}^{p,p}\circ d_{n}^{p}:G_{n}^{p}\to G_{n-1}^{p}
is the restriction of the differential map
d
on these
\mathbb{F}
-spaces.
Since each
\mathbb{F}
-space
G_{n}^{p}
is fixed by
\mathcal{F}^{p}C_{n}=\mathcal{F}^{p-1}C_{n}\oplus G_{n}^{p}
, we have
G_{n}^{p}\cong\mathcal{F}^{p}C_{n}/\mathcal{F}^{p-1}C_{n}
.
Therefore
K/xK
is isomorphic to the associated graded chain complex of
C
.

Notice that
x=1
in the quotient chain complex
K/(x-1)K
.
We know that the image of the homogeneous component of degree
p
on homological degree
n
under quotient map is

\Bigl(K/(x-1)K\Bigl)_{n}^{p}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}1^{k}G_{n}^{p-k}=\bigoplus_{q=-\infty}^{p}G_{n}^{q}=\mathcal{F}^{p}C_{n}.

Let
d_{1*}
be the induced differential map of
K/(x-1)K
, then by Definition
2.1
we have

\bigl(d_{1*}\bigr)_{n}^{p}=\sum_{q\leq p}1^{p-q}d_{n}^{p,q}=d_{n}^{p}

where
d_{n}^{p}
is the differential map
d_{n}:C_{n}\to C_{n-1}
of
(C,d,\mathcal{F})
restricted on
G_{n}^{p}
.
Thus

is isomorphic to the filtered chain complex
(C,d,\mathcal{F})
.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The decomposition follows from Theorem
1.4
. The structures of
E^{(r)}(U_{n,s,m})
and
E^{(r)}(U_{n,s,\infty})
are given in Lemma
3.3
. By Corollary
3.4
,
\{E^{(r)}\}
collapses locally.
∎

4.
Relations Between
E^{(r)}
and
PH

Next, we establish the relation between the spectral sequence and the persistent homology.
From the discussions in the above sections, we know that as
\mathbb{F}
-spaces, the spectral sequence is determined by the persistent chain complex.
Thus, it is straight forward to give a formula of the
\mathbb{F}
-dimension of the spectral sequence by counting the relevant components in the persistent homology.
Moreover, we will further show in this section that the persistent homology can also be recovered from the spectral sequence if the filtration is bounded below uniformly.

Corollary 4.1
.

Recall that
\mu_{n}^{i,j}
is the persistent multiplicities from
[
2
, VII.1]
for finite filtered chain complex, and
\nu_{n,s,n}
be defined in Definition
1.7
. Then

•

\mu_{n}^{i,j}=\nu_{n,i,j-i}
,

•

\nu_{n,s,m}=\mu_{n}^{s,s+m}
.

Also, the persistent Betti number
b_{n}^{i,j}
defined in
[
2
, VII.1]
can be expressed in terms of
\nu_{n,s,m}
as

b_{n}^{i,j}=\sum_{j-m<s\leq i}\nu_{n,s,m}.

Proof.

This follows directly from Definition
1.7
with an index changing.
The multiplicity
\nu_{n,s,m}
of
H(U_{n,s,m})
means the number of
n
-homology cycles generating at filtration level
s
and surviving for
m
filtration levels.
By
[
2
, VII.1]
, the persistent multiplicity
\mu_{n}^{i,j}
means numbers of
n
-homology cycles generating at filtration level
i
while getting killed at filtration level
j
.
Hence
\mu_{n}^{i,j}=\nu_{n,i,j-i}
and
\nu_{n,s,m}=\mu_{n}^{s,s+m}
.
Following the
fundamental lemma of persistent homology
from
[
2
, VII.1]
, we have
b_{n}^{i,j}=\sum_{s\leq i}\sum_{k>j}\mu_{n}^{s,k}
.
Therefore

b_{n}^{i,j}=\sum_{s\leq i}\sum_{k>j}\nu_{n,s,k-s}=\sum_{s\leq i}\sum_{m+s>j}\nu_{n,s,m}=\sum_{j-m<s\leq i}\nu_{n,s,m}.

∎

Recall that
E_{n,s}^{(r)}
is the homogeneous component of
E^{(r)}
with homological grading
n
and
\mathbb{F}[x]
-module grading
s
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
r>0
,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(r)}=\nu_{n,s,\infty}+\sum_{m\geq r}(\nu_{n,s,m}+\nu_{n-1,s-m,m}).

Proof.

From Theorem
1.5
, the spectral sequence decomposes uniquely as
E^{(r)}\cong\bigoplus_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{K_{l}}E^{(r)}(U_{l,s_{l}(i),m_{l}(i)})
. Consider an extended sequence
\{(l,s_{l}(i),m_{l}(i))\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{+,\infty}
and its subsequence
\{(l,s_{l}(i),m_{l}(i))\}_{(l,i)\in I_{n,s}}
where

I_{n,s}:=\{(l,i)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{+,\infty}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq K_{l},\ \mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}\subseteq E^{(r)}(U_{l,s_{l}(i),m_{l}(i)})\}.

Then
E^{(r)}_{n,s}\cong\bigoplus_{(l,i)\in I_{n,s}}\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
since each
E^{(r)}(U_{l,s_{l}(i),m_{l}(i)})
contributes at most one summand of
\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
in
E^{(r)}_{n,s}
from Lemma
3.3
.
Thus

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E^{(r)}_{n,s}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{F}^{\oplus|I_{n,s}|}=|I_{n,s}|.

It suffices to count
|I_{n,s}|
in terms of
\nu
.

We first separate
I_{n,s}=I_{n,s,\infty}\sqcup I^{\prime}_{n,s}
where
I_{n,s,\infty}:=\{(n,i)\ |\ 1\leq i\leq K_{n},\ s_{n}(i)=s,\ m_{n}(i)=\infty\}
and
I^{\prime}_{n,s}:=I_{n,s}\backslash I_{n,s,\infty}
, because we have
E^{(r)}(U_{n,s,\infty})\cong\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
for all
r\geq 1
in Lemma
3.3
.
Notice that
\nu_{n,s,\infty}=|I_{n,s,\infty}|
.

Next consider
I^{\prime}_{n,s}
. According to Lemma
3.3
,
E^{(r)}(U_{l,s,m})\cong 0
for all
m<r
. Also, any rest of the summand
\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
in
E_{n,s}^{(r)}
coming from either
E^{(r)}(U_{n,s,m})\cong\mathbb{F}||n+1||\{s+m\}\oplus\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}
or
E^{(r)}(U_{n-1,s-m,m})\cong\mathbb{F}||n||\{s\}\oplus\mathbb{F}||n-1||\{s-m\}
for all
m\geq r
.
Therefore
I^{\prime}_{n,s}=I_{n,s,m\geq r}\sqcup I_{n-1,s-m,m\geq r}
where

I_{n,s,m\geq r}:=\{(n,i)\ |\ 1\leq i\leq K_{n},\ s_{n}(i)=s,\ m_{n}(i)\geq r\}

and

I_{n-1,s-m,m\geq r}:=\{(n-1,i)\ |\ 1\leq i\leq K_{n-1},\ s_{n}(i)=s-m_{n}(i),\ m_{n}(i)\geq r\}.

Thus
|I_{n,s,m\geq r}|=\sum_{m\geq r}\nu_{n,s,m}
and
|I_{n-1,s-m,m\geq r}|=\sum_{m\geq r}\nu_{n-1,s-m,m}
.
It is clear that
I_{n,s,m\geq r}\cap I_{n-1,s-m,m\geq r}=\emptyset
since the index of
I_{n,s,m\geq r}
coming form homological degree
n
while the other coming from homological degree
n-1
.
Hence

|I_{n,s}|=|I_{n,s,\infty}|+|I_{n,s,m\geq r}|+|I_{n-1,s-m,m\geq r}|=\nu_{n,s,\infty}+\sum_{m\geq r}(\nu_{n,s,m}+\nu_{n-1,s-m,m}).

∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.8
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

The first part of the theorem follows from Proposition
4.2
.
If
\{E^{(r)}\}
is the spectral sequence of the persistent chain complex
(PC,d_{x})
satisfying conditions in Theorem
1.4
, it suffices to show that

•

\nu_{n,s,\infty}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(\infty)}
and

•

\nu_{n,s,m}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(m)}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(m+1)}-\nu_{n-1,s-m,m}
.

From Corollary
3.4
,
\{E^{(r)}\}
collapses locally to
E^{(\infty)}
.
Thus
E_{n,s}^{(\infty)}
exists for every
n,s\in\mathbb{Z}
and is also finite
\mathbb{F}
-dimensional.
It consists of the free part of the homology group with homogeneous grading.
Suppose
E_{n,s}^{(r)}
collapses at the
M
-th page, which means
E_{n,s}^{(r)}\cong E_{n,s}^{(r+1)}\cong E_{n,s}^{(\infty)}
for all
r\geq M
but
E_{n,s}^{(M-1)}\not\cong E_{n,s}^{(M)}
.
Consider
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(r)}=\nu_{n,s,\infty}+\sum_{m\geq r}(\nu_{n,s,m}+\nu_{n-1,s-m,m})
from Proposition
4.2
.
For all
r\geq M
, by
E_{n,s}^{(r)}\cong E_{n,s}^{(r+1)}
we have

\nu_{n,s,r}+\nu_{n-1,s-m,r}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(r)}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(r+1)}=0.

Thus
\sum_{m\geq M}(\nu_{n,s,m}+\nu_{n-1,s-m,m})=0
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(\infty)}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(M)}=\nu_{n,s,\infty}
. Hence
\nu_{n,s,\infty}
is determined by the infinite page
E_{n,s}^{(\infty)}
.

For general
\nu_{n,s,m}
, consider Proposition
4.2
again.
We have

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(m)}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(m+1)}=\nu_{n,s,m}+\nu_{n-1,s-m,m}.

Thus
\nu_{n,s,m}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(m)}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,s}^{(m+1)}-\nu_{n-1,s-m,m}
.

If the filtration of
(C,d,\mathcal{F})
is bounded below uniformly, the
\mathbb{F}[x]
-grading of
(PC,d_{x})
is therefore bounded below uniformly.
Then there exists
S\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that
\nu_{n,s,m}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+,\infty}
and
s<S
.
Thus all
\nu_{n,S,m}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,S}^{(m)}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E_{n,S}^{(m+1)}
are in terms of dimensions of spectral sequence.
Finally, each nonzero
\nu_{n,s,m}
is determined recursively by terms from only the spectral sequence.
∎

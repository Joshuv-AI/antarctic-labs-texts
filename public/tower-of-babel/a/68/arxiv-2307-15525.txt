Gradient Riesz potential estimates for a general class of measure data quasilinear systems
Iwona Chlebicka, Minhyun Kim, Marvin Weidner

Abstract
. We study the gradient regularity of solutions to measure data elliptic systems with Uhlenbeck-type structure and Orlicz growth. For any bounded Borel measure, pointwise estimates for the gradient of solutions are provided in terms of the truncated Riesz potential. This allows us to show a precise transfer of regularity from data to solutions on various scales.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Objectives

The goal of this article is to establish fine local regularity properties of solutions
{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}:\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
(
n\geq 2
,
m\geq 1
) to quasilinear elliptic systems with measure data of the following form

(1)

-{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{{\rm div}}}}}\left(\frac{g(|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}|)}{|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}|}{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}\right)={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}\quad\text{in}~\Omega\,,

where
\Omega
is open in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{{\rm div}}}}
stands for the
{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
-valued divergence operator,
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
is a bounded Borel measure, and
g
is a function satisfying certain Orlicz-type growth conditions. In particular, we are interested in the precise transfer of regularity properties from the data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}
to the solution
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}
. In order to achieve this, we establish a pointwise estimate for
{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}
in terms of the truncated Riesz potential of
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}
(see (
9
)) reading

(2)

g(|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}(x_{0})|)\lesssim{\mathcal{I}}^{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}|}_{1}(x_{0},r)+g\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}(x)|{\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)\,.

Our framework is fairly general and includes for instance the following special cases:
p
-Laplacian, when
g(t)=t^{p-1}
and
p>2
, as well as operators generated by Zygmund-type functions
g_{p,\alpha}(t)=t^{p-1}\log^{\alpha}(c+t)
for
p>2
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and sufficiently large
c
.

Potential estimates of the form (
2
) are an adequate nonlinear replacement of integral representation formulas in terms of fundamental solutions. Such formulas hold true for linear equations like the scalar Poisson equation
-\Delta u=\mu
but cannot be expected in the realm of nonlinear problems. A main achievement of the research during the past thirty years has been to establish nonlinear potential estimates in terms of Wolff and Riesz potentials for solutions and gradients of solutions to nonlinear problems of the form

(3)

-{\rm div}\,{\mathcal{A}}(Du)=\mu\quad\text{in}~\Omega\,

involving
{\mathcal{A}}
of various rate of degeneracy. Potential estimates can be applied to infer fine information about solutions and allow to investigate sharp regularity properties of solutions to (
3
) in terms of the inhomogeneity
\mu
. Another challenge comes from the presence of general measure data
\mu
, since in this setting the classical notion of weak solutions turns out not to be suitable for the analysis of (
3
). Instead, one employs the weaker solution concept of SOLA (solutions obtained as limits of approximations) introduced in
[
8
]
. These solutions are less controlled making their analysis more delicate.

The landmark paper
[
40
]
, where potential estimates have been established for equations driven by the
p
-Laplacian, has been followed by a stream of deep results, see e.g.
[
41
,
43
,
33
]
. Equations of non power-type growth like (
1
) are studied since
[
38
,
55
,
47
]
. Potential estimates for solutions to scalar problems with Orlicz growth were provided in
[
14
,
48
]
. On the other hand, one can analyze the regularity transfer of the inhomogeneity not only to the solution, but also to its gradient. In the super-quadratic case, gradient bounds for solutions to measure data equations were first provided in terms of nonlinear Wolff-type potentials
[
35
,
33
,
34
]
. Later, improved estimates in terms of Riesz potentials were found to hold true (see
[
43
]
). Gradient potential estimates for solutions to (
3
) with Orlicz growth functions
g
were established in
[
5
]
and extended in
[
58
]
. We refer to
[
44
,
45
]
for a rather complete account on the state of art.

While the nonlinear potential theory for scalar equations of the form (
3
) is by now fairly well-understood, some questions remained open in the development of an analogous theory for nonlinear systems. The analysis of systems comes along with some significant technical difficulties, which are for instance caused by the lack of a maximum principle. Moreover, controlling the energy of solutions is very hard if a system is not of a particular structure. The regularity theory for homogeneous nonlinear systems of quasi-diagonal structure modeled upon the
p
-Laplacian has been initiated by Uhlenbeck and Uralt’seva in their celebrated works
[
57
,
56
]
.

Despite the aforementioned serious challenges, the potential theory for nonlinear vectorial problems remains valid in some special cases. In particular, let us refer to
[
22
]
for Wolff potential estimates for solutions to
p
-Laplace systems with datum in divergence form, to
[
46
]
for estimates for solutions and their gradients to
p
-Laplace problems with measure datum in terms of Wolff and Riesz potentials, respectively, and to
[
16
]
for generalized Wolff potential estimates for solutions to measure data problems exposing Orlicz growth. The goal of this article is to extend the nonlinear potential theory for vectorial
p
-Laplace problems to nonlinear systems subject to more general growth conditions.

1.2.
Main results and their consequences

Complementing the study
[
5
,
35
,
43
,
46
,
22
,

]
, in this article we provide the Riesz potential estimate (
2
) for gradients of solutions to problems like (

) possessing Orlicz growth. We stress that such estimates are already known in the scalar case
[

and that our result is an exact and precise analogue of the work

for vectorial problems with power growth.

Let us define a vector field
{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}}
by

(4)

{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}):=\frac{g(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\,{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}\,.

With this definition (
1
) can be rewritten as a problem of the form
-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{{\rm div}}}}\,{\mathcal{A}}({D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}})={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}
. Throughout the rest of the article we will impose the following assumption on the function
g
.

Assumption (G)
.

We say that
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfies Assumption
(G)
if there exist
2<p\leq q<\infty
and an
N
-function
G\in C^{3}((0,\infty))\cap C({[0,\infty)})
, i.e.
G
is convex, vanishes only at
0
, and satisfies

\lim_{t\to 0}{G(t)}/{t}=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}{G(t)}/{t}=\infty
, such that
g=G^{\prime}
and such that for all
t\geq 0
:

(5)

\displaystyle pG(t)

\displaystyle\leq tg(t)\leq qG(t)\,,

(6)

\displaystyle(p-1)g(t)

\displaystyle\leq tg^{\prime}(t)\leq(q-1)g(t)\,,

(7)

\displaystyle(p-2)g^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq tg^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq(q-2)g^{\prime}(t)\,.

As is common in the literature on measure data problems, we consider solutions obtained as limits of approximations (SOLA), defined as follows:

Definition 1.1

(Solutions obtained as limits of approximations (SOLA))
.

Suppose that Assumption
(G)
holds true. A map
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}\in{W^{1,1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})}
such that
\int_{\Omega}g(|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}|){\,\mathrm{d}x}<\infty
is called a SOLA to (
1
) if there exists a sequence
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}_{h})\subset W^{1,G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
of weak solutions to the systems

-{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{{\rm div}}}}}\left(\frac{g(|D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}_{h}|)}{|D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}_{h}|}D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}_{h}\right)={{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}_{h}}\quad\text{in}~\Omega

such that
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}_{h}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}
locally in
W^{1,1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
, where
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}_{h})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
is a sequence
of maps that converges to
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}
weakly in the sense of measures and satisfies

(8)

\limsup_{h}|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}_{h}|(\overline{B})\leq|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}|(\overline{B})\quad\text{
for every ball $B\subset\Omega$\,.}

The existence of SOLA to (
1
) is established in
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
for any measure datum
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}\in{\mathcal{M}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})}
.

We provide pointwise gradient estimates in terms of truncated Riesz potentials given by

(9)

{\mathcal{I}}^{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}|}_{1}(x_{0},R):=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}|(B_{r}(x_{0}))}{r^{n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,.

As mentioned before, in the superquadratic case, Riesz potentials are in general smaller than Wolff-potentials. Therefore, estimates from above involving Riesz potential are more precise.

We are now ready to state the two main results of this article.

Theorem 1.2

(Pointwise Riesz potential estimates)
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
be a SOLA to (
1
) for some
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}\in{\mathcal{M}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})}
and suppose that Assumption
(G)
holds true. Let
B_{r}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
. If
{\mathcal{I}}^{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}|}_{1}(x_{0},r)
is finite, then
x_{0}
is a Lebesgue’s point of
{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}
and

(10)

g(|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}(x_{0})-({D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}})_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|)\leq C_{\mathcal{I}}{\mathcal{I}}^{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}|}_{1}(x_{0},r)+C_{{\mathcal{I}}}g\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}-({D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}})_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|{\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)

holds for some
C_{\mathcal{I}}=C_{\mathcal{I}}(n,m,p,q,G(1),g(1),g^{\prime}(1),g^{\prime\prime}(1))>0
. In particular, we have the following pointwise estimate:

(11)

\displaystyle g(|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}(x_{0})|)\leq C_{{\mathcal{I}}}{\mathcal{I}}^{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}|}_{1}(x_{0},r)+C_{{\mathcal{I}}}g\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}(x)|{\,\mathrm{d}x}\right)\,.

Note that the first claim in Theorem
1.2
is a consequence of the first part of the following theorem. The second part provides a criterion for the gradient of solution to be continuous.

Theorem 1.3

(VMO and continuity criteria)
.

Let

Suppose Assumption

A central advantage of Theorem

Suppose Assumption
(G)

{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}^{m}}

(i)

If

g(|{D{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}}}|)\in\Lambda^{\frac{\vartheta r}{\vartheta-r},\frac{\vartheta s}{\vartheta-r}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

1.3.
Methods

Many techniques that are commonly used for scalar equations cannot be adapted to systems of PDEs. This results from deep differences between scalar and vectorial problems like the lack of a comparison principle for solutions to systems. Moreover, continuity of a solution to an equation can be proven for measurable and bounded coefficients, whereas for systems one needs their continuity. In turn, despite it was expected since
[
43
]
that
p
-Laplace systems should enjoy a potential estimate of the type (
2
), it required a novel approach and was established in
[
46
]
. The main idea of the latter contribution is to employ several technical ideas from the classical potential theory concerning measure data problems and to use a special kind of
p
-harmonic approximation that is a common tool in the partial regularity theory, see
[
31
,
32
]
. In this article, we develop a general growth version of the results in
[
46
]
. Note that this problem is mentioned as an interesting and far from trivial task in
[
46
, Section 11]
.

The methods we apply in this paper can be seen as a careful refinement of the technique introduced in
[
46
]
. Adapting the approach to the Orlicz setting comes with several technical difficulties. One of the main tools is the
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic approximation result due to
[
16
]
, whose proof is also based on the ideas of
[
46
]
. Let us note that there are other
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic approximation lemmas in the literature for related problems with Orlicz growth, e.g.
[
36
,
30
]
, but they are not suitable for treating measure data problems. On the other hand, it should be stressed that the proofs not only allow for treating arbitrary measure data, but at the same time the results are obtained precisely in the scale relevant for the operator. The method we develop is delicate enough to keep such precision.

The proof goes as follows. In Section
3
, we provide some comparison results for weak solutions, which are key ingredients for the proofs of our main results. In order to establish the pointwise gradient estimate at a Lebesgue’s point
x_{0}
, we divide into two cases — degenerate and nondegenerate case — depending on the size of the gradient average at
x_{0}
compared to the so-called excess functional. When the size of the gradient average is smaller than the excess functional, the system (
1
) is considered as a degenerate system, and otherwise as a nondegenerate system. In each case, we establish comparison estimates, which provide regularity transfer from approximations to solutions. In the degenerate case, by using an
{\mathcal{A}}
-harmonic approximation result (Theorem
3.2
), we construct a solution to a nonlinear homogeneous equation (an
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic map) which is close to the solution in a suitable sense. In the nondegenerate case we compare the original solution with a solution to the linearized system. These elaborate estimates, taking the Orlicz growth into account, are of independent interest. Then, in Section
4
, we obtain the excess decay for SOLA by combining the two cases described above and passing to the limit. This requires the analysis of delicate estimates on concentric balls. Finally, we can conclude the proof of our main results Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
in Section
5
.

2.2.
On the vector field

We note that for

{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}})=\frac{g(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\xi_{i}^{\alpha}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{i}\,,\quad{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}}\,.

\partial{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}})=\frac{g(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}})\,,

Note that the vector field

(16)

|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}|^{2}\leq({\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}):{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}):{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|{\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}):{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}|\leq\frac{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|g^{\prime}(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)}{g(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)}|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}|\,

for every

|{\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}):{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}|\leq(q-1)|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}|\qquad\text{for every ${\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\zeta}}}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}}$}\,.

The second differential of

\displaystyle\partial^{2}{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|g^{\prime}(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)-g(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|^{2}}\left(\frac{\xi_{i}^{\alpha}}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\delta_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{jk}+\frac{\xi_{j}^{\beta}}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\delta_{\gamma\alpha}\delta_{ki}+\frac{\xi_{k}^{\gamma}}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{ij}\right)

2.3.
Orlicz spaces

Basic reference for this section is
[
2
]
, where the theory of Orlicz spaces is presented for scalar functions. The theory can be extended to
{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
-valued functions by obvious modifications.

We study solutions to PDEs in Orlicz–Sobolev spaces equipped with a modular function
G
satisfying Assumption
(G)
. Let us define a modular

\varrho_{G,\Omega}(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)=\int_{\Omega}G(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|){\,\mathrm{d}x}\,.

We define
{L}^{G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
to be the space of measurable functions endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}\|_{L^{G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\ \ \varrho_{G,\Omega}\left(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}|\right)\leq 1\right\}\,.

We define the Orlicz–Sobolev space
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
by

W^{1,G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})=\big\{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}\in{W^{1,1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})}:\ \ |{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}|\,,|D{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}}|\in L^{G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})\big\}\,,

where the gradient is understood in the distributional sense, endowed with the norm

\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}\|_{W^{1,G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})}=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\ \ \varrho_{G,\Omega}\left(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}|\right)+\varrho_{G,\Omega}\left(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}|D{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{f}}}}|\right)\leq 1\bigg\}\,.

By
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
we denote the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
under the above norm. Assumption
(G)
implies that the Orlicz–Sobolev space
W^{1,G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
is separable and reflexive. Moreover, one can apply arguments of
[
39
]
to infer density of smooth functions in
W^{1,G}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}})
.

2.4.
Solutions to problems with regular data

Although our main results are stated for SOLA to problems with measure data, we will first establish most auxiliary results in the framework of weak solutions and later run an approximation argument. For this purpose we provide the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\mu}}}\in(W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{m}}))^{\prime}

-harmonic map

For

due to the strict monotonicity of the operator

[

Remark 2.3

By Campanato’s characterization

-harmonic maps with any exponent

\displaystyle\leq cg^{\prime\prime}(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\eta}}}|+3|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)\leq c\frac{g^{\prime}(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\eta}}}|+3|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\eta}}}|+3|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\leq c\frac{g^{\prime}(3(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\eta}}}|+|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|))}{|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\eta}}}|+|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|}\leq cg^{\prime\prime}(|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\eta}}}|+|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\xi}}}|)\,,

where we also used monotonicity of
g^{\prime}
and (
7
). This proves the desired result.
∎

Appendix B
Function spaces

Before presenting the definitions of Lorentz, Morrey, and Lorentz–Morrey spaces, we recall the definitions of decreasing rearrangement and maximal rearrangement of a function (see
[
7
]
). The decreasing rearrangement
f^{*}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
of a measurable function
f:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is given by

f^{\ast}(t):=\sup\Big\{s\geq 0\colon\big|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|f(x)|>s\}\big|>t\Big\}\,,

where we assume that
\sup\emptyset=0
. Equivalently it can also be defined as the function, which is right-continuous, non-increasing, and equimeasurable with
f
, i.e.,
|\{x:|f(x)|>t\}|=|\{x:f^{*}(x)>t\}|
for all
t>0
.
The maximal rearrangement is defined by

f^{**}(t):=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{\ast}(s)\,ds\quad\text{and}\quad f^{\ast\ast}(0)=f^{\ast}(0)\,.

Let
0<r,s\leq\infty
. The Lorentz space
\Lambda^{r,s}(\Omega)
is the space of measurable functions
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\|f\|_{{\Lambda^{r,s}}(\Omega)}:=\big\|(\cdot)^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}}f^{**}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{s}(0,|\Omega|)}<\infty\,.

For
s=\infty
this space is usually called the Marcinkiewicz space.

We say that a measurable function
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
belongs to the Morrey space
L^{r}_{\vartheta}(\Omega)
for
r\geq 1
and
\vartheta\in[0,n]
,
if and only if

\|f\|_{L^{r}_{\vartheta}(\Omega)}:=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B(x_{0},R)\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\\
x_{0}\in\Omega\end{subarray}}R^{\frac{\vartheta-n}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{r}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}<\infty\,.

We say that
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
belongs to the Lorentz–Morrey space
\Lambda^{r,s}_{\vartheta}(\Omega)
for
r,s\in[1,\infty]
and
\vartheta\in[0,n]
,
if and only if

\|f\|_{\Lambda^{r,s}_{\vartheta}(\Omega)}:=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B(x_{0},R)\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\\
x_{0}\in\Omega\end{subarray}}R^{\frac{\vartheta-n}{r}}\|f\|_{\Lambda^{r,s}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}<\infty\,.

We define the space
L\log L(\Omega)
as a subset of integrable functions
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L\log L(\Omega)}&=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\quad\int_{\Omega}\Big|\tfrac{f}{\lambda}\Big|\log\left(e+\Big|\tfrac{f}{\lambda}\Big|\right)dx\leq 1\right\}<\infty\,.\end{split}

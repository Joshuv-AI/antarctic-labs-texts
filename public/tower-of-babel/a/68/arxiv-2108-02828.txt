Rank r r DT theory from rank 1 1
S. Feyzbakhsh and R. P. Thomas

Abstract
. Fix a Calabi-Yau 3-fold X X satisfying the Bogomolov-Gieseker conjecture of Bayer-Macrì-Toda, such as the quintic 3-fold. We express Joyce’s generalised DT invariants counting Gieseker semistable sheaves of any rank r r on X X in terms of those counting sheaves of rank 1. By the MNOP conjecture they are therefore determined by the Gromov-Witten invariants of X X .

Wall crossing formula

From Section

From small to large volume

The Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality gives a region in the space of weak stability conditions in which there are no semistable objects

Just as (

Outlook

Having dealt with the counting of sheaves, one could try to extend our result to counts of arbitrary Bridgeland semistable complexes of sheaves. After a possible shift to ensure rank
\geq 0
this reduces to another wall crossing problem — assuming the space of stability conditions is connected — to reach stability conditions dominated by the weak stability conditions considered in this paper and thus return to the problem of counting sheaves.

Applying Joyce’s new wall crossing formula for Fano 3-folds
[

\mathsf{J}\hskip 0.6pt(0,0,\beta,m)
in terms of stable pair invariants.

Now take general
c>0
. On each wall the semistable factors
E_{0},\,E_{1}
either have the classes
(1,0,0),\,(0,c,s)
again, or have
strictly smaller
c
. In the first case we are on
\theta_{\operatorname{JS}}
and again pick up the term on the right hand side of (
76
), plus higher order terms counting semistable factors of
E_{0}
; since these have smaller
c
we may assume by induction they are written in terms of stable pair invariants.

In the second case both
E_{0},\,E_{1}
have strictly smaller
c>0
, and
\theta
is to the right of the Joyce-Song wall of
E_{1}
by (
72
). By induction we may assume both
\mathsf{J}\hskip 0.6pt(0,0,\beta^{\prime},m^{\prime})
and
\mathsf{J}\hskip 0.6pt_{\theta}(1,\beta^{\prime},m^{\prime})
can be written in terms of stable pair invariants for all
\beta^{\prime}.H<c
and
\theta
to the right of the Joyce-Song wall of
(1,\beta^{\prime},m^{\prime})
, since the base case
\beta^{\prime}.H=c_{\min}
was shown above. After finitely many walls, we reach the large volume chamber where we get the stable pair invariant
P_{\beta,\,m+n\beta.H}(X)
equated with the right hand side of (
76
) plus a function of stable pair invariants with smaller

. Rearranging — and rewriting the stable pair invariants in terms of counts of ideal sheaves using the DT/PT wall crossing proved in
[
Br3
,
To1
]

— proves Theorem

for dimension 1 classes.

Finally we note that the working of
[

, Section 6.3]
shows that the same technique of using Joyce-Song pairs also works for dimension 0 sheaves.

Appendix A
Stable pairs

We explain here some results of Toda. Though they are all proved in
[
To3
, Section 3]
they are not stated in the generality we need, so we give a brief account (with slightly different proofs) for completeness.

Definition A.1
.

A pair
(F,s)
consisting of a 1-dimensional sheaf
F
and a section
s\in H^{0}(F)
is called a
stable pair

[
PT
]
if

•

F
is a
pure
1-dimensional sheaf, i.e. it has no 0-dimensional subsheaves, and

•

s\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow F
has zero-dimensional cokernel.

We often abuse notation and call the 2-term complex
\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ s\ }F
(with
\mathcal{O}_{X}
in degree 0) a stable pair. We also use the notation
E^{\vee}:=R\mathcal{H}\hskip-1.0ptom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})
for derived dual.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
E\in\mathcal{A}_{\hskip 0.6ptb}
is a
\nu_{b,w}
-semistable object of rank
-1
with
\nu_{b,w}(E)<+\infty
and
w\gg 0
. Set
L:=(\det E)^{-1}
. Then

E^{\vee}\otimes L[1]\,\text{ is a stable pair}.

Proof.

By
[
BMS
, Lemma 2.7(c)(iii)]
we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\in\operatorname{Coh}_{\leq 1}(X)
while
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)
is a
\mu_{H}
-semistable sheaf. In particular
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)
is torsion free, and rank 1 by Lemma
2.1
. Thus it is
L\otimes I_{Z}
for some subscheme
Z\subset X
of dimension
\leq 1
and line bundle
L
— the double dual of
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)
, which also equals
\det\!\big(E[1]\big)
since
Z
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)
have dimension
\leq
1.

Since
\mu_{H}(L)=\mu_{H}\big(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)\big)
the shifts by
[1]
of both sheaves are in
\mathcal{A}_{\hskip 0.6ptb}
. This gives two
\mathcal{A}_{\hskip 0.6ptb}
short exact sequences

(77)

L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)[1]\longrightarrow\hskip-15.649pt\longrightarrow L[1]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)[1]\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow E\longrightarrow\hskip-15.649pt\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)

and so the
\mathcal{A}_{\hskip 0.6ptb}
-injection
L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}\hookrightarrow E
. Since
\nu_{b,w}(L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z})=+\infty
this contradicts the
\nu_{b,w}
-semistability of
E
unless
Z=\emptyset
. Thus
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)=L
.

The derived dual of the second exact sequence of (
77
) is the exact triangle

\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)^{\vee}\longrightarrow E^{\vee}\longrightarrow L^{-1}[-1].

Setting
F:=\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)^{\vee}\otimes L[2]
and
I^{\bullet}:=E^{\vee}\otimes L[1]
this gives

(78)

I^{\bullet}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow F.

Since
\dim\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\leq 1
, we know
\mathcal{H}^{0}(F)=\mathcal{E}\hskip-1.0ptxt^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\otimes L^{-1},\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a pure 1-dimensional sheaf,
\mathcal{H}^{1}(F)=\mathcal{E}\hskip-1.0ptxt^{3}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\otimes L^{-1},\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a 0-dimensional sheaf, and the other
\mathcal{H}^{i}(F)
vanish. The long exact sequence of cohomology sheaves of (
78
) then shows that

(79)

\mathcal{H}^{\geq 3}(I^{\bullet})\,=\,0\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{H}^{2}(I^{\bullet})\,=\,\mathcal{H}^{1}(F)\ \text{ is 0-dimensional.}

Now we use the
\nu_{b,w}
-semistability of
E\in\mathcal{A}_{\hskip 0.6ptb}
and
\nu_{b,w}(E)<+\infty
to deduce

(80)

\operatorname{Hom}(G,E)\ =\ 0\ =\ \operatorname{Hom}(E^{\vee},G^{\vee})\ \text{ for any }\ G\,\in\,\mathrm{Coh}_{\leq 1}(X).

Setting
G=\mathcal{O}_{x}
for any point
x\in X
gives

0\ =\ \operatorname{Hom}\!\big(I^{\bullet}\otimes L^{-1}[-1],\mathcal{O}_{x}[-3]\big)\ \cong\ \operatorname{Hom}(I^{\bullet}[2],\mathcal{O}_{x})\ =\ \operatorname{Hom}\!\big(\mathcal{H}^{2}(I^{\bullet}),\mathcal{O}_{x}\big),

where the last equality follows from
\mathcal{H}^{\geq 3}(I^{\bullet})=0
(
79
). Combined with the second part of (
79
) this gives
\mathcal{H}^{2}(I^{\bullet})=0
, so in fact

(81)

\mathcal{H}^{\geq 2}(I^{\bullet})\,=\,0\,\text{ and }\,F\,\text{ is a pure 1-dimensional sheaf}.

Now let
G
be any pure 1-dimensional sheaf. Then
G^{\vee}\otimes L[2]
is also a pure 1-dimensional sheaf, which we may also subsitute into (
80
) in place of
G
. This gives

(82)

0\ =\ \operatorname{Hom}\!\big(E^{\vee},(G^{\vee}\otimes L[2])^{\vee}\big)\ =\ \operatorname{Hom}(I^{\bullet}[1],G)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ge2}}}{{=}}\ \operatorname{Hom}\!\big(\mathcal{H}^{1}(I^{\bullet}),G\big).

But by (
78
)
\mathcal{H}^{1}(I^{\bullet})
is a quotient of a pure 1-dimensional sheaf

, so by (
82
) it must be 0-dimensional. Thus by (
78
),
I^{\bullet}
is the cocone of a map
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to F
with 0-dimensional cokernel, i.e. it is a stable pair.
∎

For the converse let
(F,s)
be a stable pair and
I^{\bullet}=\{\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ s\ }F\}
the associated complex.

A note on the Bures-Wasserstein metric
Shravan Mohan

Abstract
In this brief note, it is shown that the Bures-Wasserstein (BW) metric on the space positive definite matrices lends itself to convex optimization. In other words, the computation of the BW metric can be posed as a convex optimization problem. In turn, this leads to efficient computations of (i) the BW distance between convex subsets of positive definite matrices, (ii) the BW barycenter, and (iii) incorporating BW distance from a given matrix as a convex constraint. Computations are provided for corroboration.



I
Introduction

Consider the set of positive definite matrices of dimension
n
given by
\mathcal{P}(n)
. The Bures-Wasserstein (BW) metric between
A
and
B
in
\mathcal{P}(n)
is given by the closed form
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(A,B)=\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(B)-2\mbox{Tr}\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{A}B\sqrt{A}}\right).

(1)

Here,
\sqrt{X}
denotes the unique symmetric square root of a positive definite matrix
X
. That is:

\displaystyle\sqrt{X}=U\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}U^{\top},

(2)

where
X=U\Sigma U^{\top}
is the singular value decomposition of
X
and
\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the
element-wise square root of
\Sigma
.

The BW metric is defined in the following way. The set of square roots of a positive definite matrix
X
is given by:

\displaystyle\left\{\sqrt{X}U:U\in O(n)\right\},

(3)

where
O(n)
is the set of real unitary matrices of dimension
n
. Then, for two positive definite matrices
A
and
B
, the Frobenius distance (
||.||_{F}
) between the sets of their respective square roots is defined as the BW metric. Mathematically, this gives:

\displaystyle\rho(A,B)=\min_{U,V\in O(n)}\left|\left|\sqrt{A}U-\sqrt{B}V\right|\right|_{F}

(4)

II
BW metric & Semidefinite Programming

Since the Frobenius norm is unitary invariant, the BW metric can also be written as:

\displaystyle\rho(A,B)=\min_{V\in O(n)}\left|\left|\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{B}V\right|\right|_{F},

(5)

Thus,

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(A,B)=\min_{U\in O(n)}\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(B)-2\mbox{Tr}\left(\sqrt{A}\sqrt{B}U\right).

(6)

Now, the following well-known lemma comes to the aid for solving the above optimization problem as a convex optimization problem
[
2
]
.

Lemma
: The linear SDP given by

\displaystyle\max_{U}~~~~\mbox{Tr}(KU)

(7)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~~}\begin{bmatrix}I&U^{\top}\\
U&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.

(8)

has an optimal solution
\tilde{U}
such that
\tilde{U}^{\top}\tilde{U}=I
.

Proof
: Firstly, note that if a feasible point
U
is such that some diagonal elements of
KU
are negative, then the matrix
UD
, where
D
is diagonal such that

D_{i,i}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }(KU)_{i,i}\geq 0\\
-1,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

would yield a higher cost function value. Thus, at optimality, the diagonal elements of
KU
are non-negative. Also note that
DU
satisfies the semidefinite constraint if
U
does. Secondly, suppose the optima
\tilde{U}
was such that
I\succ\tilde{U}^{\top}\tilde{U}
. Let
\tilde{U}=PSQ^{\top}
by SVD. By our assumption, some of the elements of
S
have to be zero or less than 1. Now consider the unitary matrix
U=PDQ^{\top}
, where
D
is a diagonal matrix (from the first observation) which makes all the diagonal elements of
KPDQ^{\top}
non-negative.
Also note that

\displaystyle\small\mbox{Tr}(KPDQ^{\top})=\mbox{Tr}(Q^{\top}KPD)\geq\mbox{Tr}(Q^{\top}KPS)=\mbox{Tr}\left(KU\right),

which finally implies that the convex relaxation is tight. The result also applies to the case where the constraint is:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}G&U^{\top}\\
U&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(9)

since this is equivalent to the constraint

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I&\sqrt{G^{-1}}U^{\top}\\
U\sqrt{G^{-1}}&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(10)

With the above lemma, the computation of BW distance can also be written as:

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(A,B)=~~

\displaystyle\min~~\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(B)-2\mbox{Tr}\left(\sqrt{A}U\right)

(11)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~~}\begin{bmatrix}B&U^{\top}\\
U&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.

(12)

1.

Computation of BW distance between convex subsets
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
of PD matrices:

•

Choose any matrix
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

•

Compute the following:

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle\mbox{~arg}\min_{X}\mbox{Tr}(X)-2\mbox{Tr}\left(\sqrt{A}K\right)

(13)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~}\begin{bmatrix}X&K^{\top}\\
K&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,~X\in\mathcal{B}.

(14)

•

Compute the following:

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\mbox{~arg}\min_{X}\mbox{Tr}(X)-2\mbox{Tr}\left(\sqrt{B}K\right)

(15)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~}\begin{bmatrix}X&K^{\top}\\
K&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,~X\in\mathcal{A}.

(16)

•

Repeat the above two steps till convergence.

2.

Computation of the BW barycenter:

\displaystyle\min_{X}~~\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\left(\mbox{Tr}(A_{i})+\mbox{Tr}(X)-2\mbox{Tr}(\sqrt{A_{i}}K_{i})\right)

(17)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~~}\begin{bmatrix}X&K_{i}^{\top}\\
K_{i}&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,~\forall i.

(18)

3.

Incorporating BW distance from a matrix as a constraint in a convex program:

\displaystyle\min_{X}~~~~f\left(X\right);~~f\mbox{~convex in $X$}

(19)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~~}X\in\mathcal{C},~~\begin{bmatrix}X&K^{\top}\\
K&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(20)

\displaystyle~~~~~~\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(X)-2\mbox{Tr}\left(\sqrt{A_{i}}K\right)\leq d_{A_{i}},\forall i.

(21)

BW distance between convex sets
: The convex routine to compute the BW metric can be used to find the BW distance between two convex subsets of positive definite matrices using alternating projections
[
3
]
. The alternating projections method proceeds by finding the distance of a point from one set to the other alternatively, while latching onto the latest iterate. Recall that this method converges to the distance between convex sets (and the corresponding matrices in the two subsets), given a metric on the point set. More precisely, consider two convex sets of positive definite matrices
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
. Then, the alternating steps would be:

\displaystyle\min_{K,A}~~\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(B)-2\mbox{Tr}(\sqrt{B}K)

(22)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~~}\begin{bmatrix}A&K^{\top}\\
K&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,~A\in\mathcal{A}.

(23)

and

\displaystyle\min_{K,B}~~\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(B)-2\mbox{Tr}(\sqrt{A}K)

(24)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~~}\begin{bmatrix}B&K^{\top}\\
K&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,~A\in\mathcal{A}.

(25)

BW barycenter
: The same idea can be applied towards finding a weighted BW barycenter. Conventionally, the BW barycenter is computed as a solution to a fixed point equation given by:

\displaystyle X=\sqrt{X^{-1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\sqrt{\sqrt{X}A_{i}\sqrt{X}}\right)^{2}\sqrt{X^{-1}}.

(26)

Although, the fixed point iteration converges to the BW barycenter, a convex approach is easy to understand and allows inclusion of convex constraints on the barycenter. Consider the convex optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{X}~~\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\left(\mbox{Tr}(A_{i})+\mbox{Tr}(X)-2\mbox{Tr}(\sqrt{A_{i}}K_{i})\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~~}\begin{bmatrix}X&K_{i}^{\top}\\
K_{i}&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,~\forall i.

(28)

Note that the optima in this case too has to lie on the boundary of each constraint (by the aforementioned lemma), and hence the BW barycenter can be calculated this way.

BW distance as convex constraint
: Consider the constraint set given by:

\displaystyle\left\{X\in\mbox{PD}(n)~~|~~\rho(A,X)\leq d\right\}.

(29)

This set can be represented as a convex constraint:

\displaystyle\left\{X\in PD(n),~K\in R^{n,n}~~|~~\begin{bmatrix}X&K^{\top}\\
K&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0\right.~\&~

(30)

\displaystyle\left.\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(X)-2\mbox{Tr}(\sqrt{A}K)\leq d^{2}\right\}.

(31)

Note that if
(X,K)
belongs to the constraint set, then obviously

\min_{X,K\in O(n)}~\left(\mbox{Tr}(A)+\mbox{Tr}(X)-2\mbox{Tr}(\sqrt{A}K)\right)=\rho^{2}(A,X)\leq d^{2}.

An example of convex optimization problem incorporating such a constraint would be:

\displaystyle\min_{X}~~~~||X||_{{F}}

(32)

\displaystyle\mbox{sub to~~}\rho(A,X)\leq d.

(33)

IV
Conclusion

In this paper it was shown that the computation of the BW metric can be done using convex optimization. This resulted in numerically efficient routines for calculating the BW distance between convex subsets of matrices, the BW barycenter of a finite set of positive definite matrices and incorporating BW distance from a matrix as a convex constraint in a convex optimization problem. Computational examples were provided for corroboration.

Solving the Transmission Problem for Open Wave-Guides, I Fundamental Solutions and Integral Equations Version 5.1
Charles L. Epstein

Abstract
We introduce a layer potential representation for the solution of the transmission problem defined by two dielectric channels, or open wave-guides, meeting along the straight-line interface, { x 1 = 0 } . \{x_{1}=0\}. The main observation is that the outgoing fundamental solution for the operator Δ + k 1 2 + q ⁡ ( x 2 ) , \Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2}), acting on functions defined in ℝ 2 , \mathbb{R}^{2}, is easily constructed using the Fourier transform in the x 1 x_{1} -variable and the elementary theory of ordinary differential equations. These fundamental solutions can then be used to represent the solution to the transmission problem in half planes. The transmission boundary conditions lead to integral equations along the intersection of the half planes, which, in our normalization, is the x 2 x_{2} -axis. We show that, in appropriate Banach spaces, these integral equations are Fredholm equations of second kind, which are therefore generically solvable. We analyze the representation of the guided modes in our formulation.

Introduction

This paper is the first part of a three part series on scattering problems for
open, or dielectric wave-guides. In these papers we focus on the scalar case, so
that an open wave-guide is specified by perturbations of the background
dielectric constant. We work in the time harmonic setting, i.e. solutions of the
form
U(x,t)=e^{-i\omega t}u(x),
where
\omega>0,
and
u
solves an equation
of the form

(\Delta+\omega^{2}\epsilon(x))u=0.

(1)

The permittivity function,
\epsilon(x),
is assumed to be a positive constant outside of a
compact set, union with tubular neighborhoods of a finite collection of
semi-infinite rays,
\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{N}\},
see Figure
4
. In each tubular neighborhood the
permittivity is independent of the distance along the ray, depending only on
variables in the orthogonal hyperplane. The solution is assumed to belong to

H^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
which means that it is continuous, and its gradient is
continuous across jumps in the permittivity. Conceptually this is quite similar
to the classical quantum-mechanical
N
-body problem,
see
[
12
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
.

In the first 2 parts of this series we consider a very simple, 2-dimensional
model case consisting of two semi-infinite strips of constant permittivity
meeting along a common perpendicular line, see Figure
1
. Outside the
strips the permittivity is a constant. The goal of this paper is to produce a
layer potential representation for the solution of this model problem, leading
to Fredholm integral equations of second kind. We assume that the refractive
index inside the wave-guide is larger than the surrounding space, which implies
that wave-guide modes exist, see (
12
). Using the layer potential representation, which is
practicably computable, we derive integral equations that lend themselves to
numerical implementation for the approximate solution of this basic, model
problem. In the second part of the series we show that our solution satisfies
the natural outgoing radiation conditions, suggested by similar results in

N
-body theory.

Finally, in the third part of this series, which is joint with Rafe Mazzeo, we
formulate the appropriate outgoing radiation conditions for the general

n
-dimensional, open wave-guide problem, show that they imply uniqueness, and
are satisfied by the limiting absorption solution. Finally we show that the
solutions found using our method satisfy these conditions, and therefore agree
with the limiting absorption solutions. We refer to the
subsequent parts of this series as Part II, and Part III, which are the
papers
[
7
]
, and
[
8
]
.

[

]
, the fundamental electro-magnetic problem we are
considering is that of scattering in

by a non-magnetic, dielectric
sheet, which is independent of the
x_{3}
-variable in the TM, or transverse
magnetic mode. The electric field then takes the form
\boldsymbol{E}=(0,0,u(x_{1},x_{2}))

where

satisfies an appropriate Helmholtz-like equation

(\Delta+k^{2}(x))u=0,\text{ with }k^{2}(x)=\omega^{2}\epsilon(x).

(2)

For the case of two semi-infinite, dielectric channels meeting along a common
perpendicular line, the precise formulation is in terms of a
pair operators:

\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l,r}(x_{2}),

(3)

see Figure
1
.
Here, and throughout the paper,
l
refers to
\{x_{1}<0\},
and
r
refers to

\{x_{1}>0\}.
Though our method of solution applies to
piecewise continuous
q(x_{2})
with bounded support, for simplicity we usually assume that

q_{l,r}(x_{2})=(k^{2}_{2;l,r}-k^{2}_{1})\chi_{[d^{-}_{l,r},d^{+}_{l,r}]}(x_{2}),

(4)

with
k_{2;l,r}\geq k_{1}.
This is essentially the same problem as that considered
in
[
2
]
, but rather different from that considered
in
[
5
]
. Chandler-Wilde and Zhang consider more general
potentials, but make assumptions that preclude the existence of wave-guide
modes. They also focus on incoming data similar to plane waves, whereas we
consider incoming fields that decay as
|x_{2}|\to\infty,
which includes
wave-guide modes as well as point sources and smooth wave packets formed from
plane waves, see Section
6
.

For time harmonic solutions, with time dependence
e^{-i\omega t},
the wave
numbers,
\{k_{1},k_{2;l},k_{2;r}\},
are related to permittivity by

k_{1}=\omega\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}},

k_{2;l,r}=\omega\sqrt{\epsilon_{2;l,r}},
with

\epsilon
denoting the permittivities of the materials, which we assume are
positive, real numbers. We use a symmetric formulation of the transmission problem, which
is suggested by the symmetry of the operator itself.

Suppose that there are incoming solutions
u^{\operatorname{in}}_{l,r},
which satisfy

(\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l,r}(x_{2}))u^{\operatorname{in}}_{l,r}=0,

(5)

in a neighborhood of
\{x_{1}=0\},
we then look for “outgoing” solutions

u^{l},u^{r},
to the equations

(\Delta+k^{2}_{1}+q_{l,r})u^{l,r}=0\text{ where }x_{1}<0,\text{
resp. }x_{1}>0,

(6)

with
u^{l}\in H^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}((-\infty,0]\times\mathbb{R})
and
u^{r}\in H^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}),
satisfying the transmission
boundary conditions

\begin{split}u^{r}(0^{+},x_{2})-u^{l}(0^{-},x_{2})&=u^{\operatorname{in}}_{l}(0,x_{2})-u^{\operatorname{in}}_{r}(0,x_{2})=g(x_{2}),\\
\partial_{x_{1}}u^{r}(0^{+},x_{2})-\partial_{x_{1}}u^{l}(0^{-},x_{2})&=\partial_{x_{1}}u^{\operatorname{in}}(0,x_{2})-\partial_{x_{1}}u^{\operatorname{in}}_{r}(0,x_{2})=h(x_{2}).\end{split}

(7)

The regularity and boundary conditions then imply that the field

u^{\operatorname{tot}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{cases}&u^{l}(x_{1},x_{2})+u^{\operatorname{in}}_{l}(x_{1},x_{2})\text{ for }x_{1}\leq 0,\\
&u^{r}(x_{1},x_{2})+u^{\operatorname{in}}_{r}(x_{1},x_{2})\text{ for }x_{1}>0,\end{cases}

(8)

is a weak solution to

(\Delta+q(x_{1},x_{2})+k_{1}^{2})u^{\operatorname{tot}}=0,\\
\text{ where }q(x_{1},x_{2})=q_{l}(x_{2})\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(x_{1})+q_{r}(x_{2})\chi_{(0,\infty)}(x_{1}),

(9)

which belongs to
H^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Our solutions are outgoing in a sense rather different from that in earlier
papers on
this
problem. The outgoing condition we establish in Part III requires the scattered
fields to satisfy more or less classical Sommerfeld radiation conditions outside
the channels, with appropriate outgoing conditions within the channels,
see (
14
). Among
other things this implies that along the line
\{x_{1}=0\}
the solutions must
satisfy an estimate like

(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})u^{l,r}(0,x_{2})=O((\pm x_{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}).

(10)

The transmission boundary condition then implies that

(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})g(x_{2})=O((\pm x_{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}),

(11)

which shows that some conditions are required on the data for the solution to
this problem to be outgoing. This is extensively discussed in Parts II and III.

Under a different definition of outgoing, the existence and uniqueness of
solutions to this problem is established in
[
2
]
. We give a
very different formulation of the solution that we believe is better suited to
numerical approximation. That said, in this paper we only prove existence
generically, and have not considered the relationship between our solution and
that found in
[
2
]
. In Part III we consider radiation
conditions for open wave-guides, and the problem of uniqueness using tools from
microlocal analysis pioneered in
[
10
,
11
,
14
]
. Our
solutions will turn out to satisfy the radiation conditions given in Part III,
and are uniquely determined according to this notion of outgoing. This will also
imply that our integral equations have a trivial null-space and are therefore
always solvable.

For the applications of most immediate interest, the incoming field is
a sum of
wave-guide modes
. A single wave-guide mode for

\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l}(x_{2}),
is given by
u^{\operatorname{in}}_{l}=e^{i\xi_{0}x_{1}}v_{0}(x_{2}),u^{\operatorname{in}}_{r}=0

where
v_{0}(x_{2})
is an
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
–solution to

[\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}-\xi_{0}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l}(x_{2})]v_{0}(x_{2})=0

(12)

that is exponentially decaying outside the channel, which is the
\operatorname{supp}q_{l}.
If

\xi_{0}>0
then this mode is rightward moving, and if
\xi_{0}<0,
leftward
moving. For such a single mode, the jump data is given by

g(x_{2})=v_{0}(x_{2}),\quad h(x_{2})=i\xi_{0}v_{0}(x_{2}).

(13)

For the data of this type we show, in Part II, that the solution found using our method
satisfies an analogue of the Sommerfeld radiation condition suggested by the
analysis of the
N
-body Schrödinger equation given by Isozaki, Melrose, Vasy,
et al., see
[
10
,
11
,
14
]
. We show
that along rays
r\eta=r(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),
with
\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}=1,

\eta_{1}\cdot\eta_{2}\neq 0,

the scattered fields satisfy

\begin{split}&u^{l,r}(r\eta)=O(r^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\\
&(\partial_{r}-ik_{1})u^{l,r}(r\eta)=O(r^{-\frac{3}{2}}),\end{split}

(14)

as
r\to\infty,
uniformly as
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\to 0^{\pm}.
Within the channels
(
\eta_{2}=0
) we also have contributions from the guided modes, which do not
decay, but are outgoing in an appropriate sense: on the left a guided mode is
outgoing if it is proportional to
v(x_{2})e^{-i\xi_{0}x_{1}},
with
\xi_{0}>0,
and
outgoing to the right if is proportional to
v(x_{2})e^{i\xi_{0}x_{1}},
with

\xi_{0}>0.

A Layer Potential Approach

The starting point for our approach is the formula for the solution of
the classical transmission problem: find a function
u
that solves

(\Delta+k^{2})u=0,\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{x_{1}=0\},

and the transmission boundary condition

\begin{split}u(0^{+},x_{2})-u(0^{-},x_{2})&=g(x_{2})\\
\partial_{x_{1}}u(0^{+},x_{2})-\partial_{x_{1}}u(0^{-},x_{2})&=h(x_{2}).\end{split}

(16)

The outgoing solution to this problem is given by

u(x)=-\mathcal{D}_{k}g+\mathcal{S}_{k}h,

(17)

where the single and double layers are given by

\mathcal{S}_{k}f(x)=\int\limits_{\{y_{1}=0\}}g_{k}(x-y)f(y_{2})dy_{2},\text{ and }\mathcal{D}_{k}f(x)=\int\limits_{\{y_{1}=0\}}\partial_{y_{1}}g_{k}(x-y)f(y_{2})dy_{2},

(18)

with

g_{k}(x-y)=\frac{i}{4}H^{(1)}_{0}(k|x-y|),

the outgoing fundamental solution to
\Delta+k^{2}.

That this gives a solution follows from the classical jump formulæ
for layer potentials, see
[
6
]
.

Our method for solving the transmission problem for 2 wave
guides (
6
), and (
7
), uses this general
approach. Let
\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}(x;y)
denote the outgoing fundamental solutions for the
operators
\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l,r}(x_{2}),
acting on the
whole
plane
. The kernels of these
operators take a rather special form:

\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}(x;y)=\frac{i}{4}H^{(1)}_{0}(k_{1}|x-y|)+w^{l,r}(x;y),

(19)

where
w^{l,r}
satisfies the equation

(\Delta_{x}+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l,r}(x_{2}))w^{l,r}(x;y)=-\frac{i}{4}q_{l,r}(x_{2})H^{(1)}_{0}(k_{1}|x-y|).

(20)

The right hand sides of (
20
) are compactly supported in the

x_{2}
-variable. As we shall see, these equations can be solved quite
explicitly by taking the Fourier transform in the
x_{1}
-variable, and
solving frequency-by-frequency. The correction terms,
w^{l,r}(x;y),

are smooth away from the diagonal, and 2 orders smoother along the diagonal, as
distributions, than
H^{(1)}_{0}(k|x-y|).
Suppose that
\operatorname{supp}q_{l,r}\subset[-d,d],
then, where
x_{1}=y_{1}=0,
the singularities of
w^{l,r}

are contained within the compact set
B_{d},
where, for
d>0,

B_{d}\overset{d}{=}[-d,d]\times[-d,d].

(21)

Once the fundamental solutions are constructed we can express the right and left
portions of the solution as

u^{l,r}=-\mathcal{E}^{l,r\,^{\prime}}\sigma+\mathcal{E}^{l,r}\tau,

(22)

where

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{l,r}f(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}(x;0,y_{2})f(y_{2})dy_{2}=\mathcal{S}_{k_{1}}f+\mathcal{W}^{l,r}f(x)\\
&\mathcal{E}^{l,r\,^{\prime}}f(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[\partial_{y_{1}}\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}](x;0,y_{2})f(y_{2})dy_{2}=\mathcal{D}_{k_{1}}f+\mathcal{W}^{l,r\,^{\prime}}f(x),\end{split}

(23)

with

\begin{split}\mathcal{W}^{l,r}f(x)=&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}w^{l,r}(x;0,y_{2})f(y_{2})dy_{2},\\
\mathcal{W}^{l,r\,^{\prime}}f(x)=&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[\partial_{y_{1}}w^{l,r}](x;0,y_{2})f(y_{2})dy_{2}.\end{split}

(24)

Using this representation we derive a system of equations for

(\sigma,\tau),
which are somewhat better behaved than usual
in so far as the singularities of
w^{l,r}
behave like

|x-y|^{2}\log|x-y|.
The restrictions to the boundary,
x_{1}=y_{1}=0,
are
given by

\begin{split}u^{r}(0,x_{2})-u^{l}(0,x_{2})&=\sigma(x_{2})+\\
&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[\partial_{y_{1}}w^{l}-\partial_{y_{1}}w^{r}]{(0,x_{2};0;y_{2})}\sigma(y_{2})dy_{2}+\\
&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[w^{r}-w^{l}]{(0,x_{2};0;y_{2})}\tau(y_{2})dy_{2}=g(x_{2}),\\
\partial_{x_{1}}u^{r}(0,x_{2})-\partial_{x_{1}}u^{l}(0,x_{2})&=\tau(x_{2})+\\
&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[\partial_{x_{1}}w^{r}-\partial_{x_{1}}w^{l}]{(0,x_{2};0;y_{2})}\tau(y_{2})dy_{2}+\\
&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[\partial^{2}_{x_{1}y_{1}}w^{l}-\partial^{2}_{x_{1}y_{1}}w^{r}]{(0,x_{2};0;y_{2})}\sigma(y_{2})dy_{2}=h(x_{2}).\end{split}

(25)

Only the
H^{(1)}_{0}
-terms have jumps across
\{x_{1}=0\};
as we show in Section
4.1

\partial_{y_{1}}w^{l,r}(0,x_{2};0,y_{2})=\partial_{x_{1}}w^{l,r}(0,x_{2};0,y_{2})=0,

(26)

so these equations take the very simple form

\left(\begin{matrix}\operatorname{Id}&D\\
C&\operatorname{Id}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}\sigma\\
\tau\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}g\\
h\end{matrix}\right).

(27)

The behavior of these equations hinges on the analytic properties of the functions

w^{l,r}
along the plane where
x_{1}=y_{1}=0,
which we analyze in the following 2
sections. It is an interesting feature of this approach, via the fundamental
solutions of
\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l,r}(x_{2}),
that if
q_{l}=q_{r},
then these
equations reduce to

\begin{split}u^{r}(0,x_{2})-u^{l}(0,x_{2})&=\sigma(x_{2})=g(x_{2})\\
\partial_{x_{1}}u^{r}(0,x_{2})-\partial_{x_{1}}u^{l}(0,x_{2})&=\tau(x_{2})=h(x_{2}),\end{split}

(28)

exactly as in (
17
).

at the

\{\xi_{n}\}.
The
\{v_{n}(x_{2})\}
are normalized to have
L^{2}
-norm 1. The
coefficients of the wave-guide terms are given by

a_{n}(y_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-d}^{d}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}|y_{2}-z_{2}|}q(z_{2})v_{n}(z_{2})dz_{2}}{\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}.

(38)

These contributions and their
\partial_{x_{1}}
-derivatives are in

\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}).
Using the fact that

(\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}-\xi_{n}^{2})v_{n}(x_{2})=-q(x_{2})v_{n}(x_{2}),
and integration by parts,
we show that

a_{n}(y_{2})=v_{n}(y_{2}),

(39)

and therefore

w^{g}_{0+}(x;y)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}(x_{2})v_{n}(y_{2})e^{i\xi_{n}(x_{1}-y_{1})}\text{ for }x_{1}>0.

(40)

Hence this term and its
x_{1}
-derivatives decay exponentially as

|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|\to\infty.
The fundamental solution for
\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2})
is
given by

\mathfrak{E}(x;y)=g_{k_{1}}(|x-y|)+w_{0+}^{c}(x;y)+w_{0+}^{g}(x;y);

(41)

the guided modes are entirely captured by
w^{g}_{0+}.
This and the following
section describes the construction in the right half plane; the left half plane
is obtained by replacing
(x_{1},y_{1})
with
(-x_{1},-y_{1}),
and
\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}

with
\Gamma_{\nu}^{-}.

We need to analyze
w_{0+},
and certain of its derivatives,
along the set
x_{1}=y_{1}=0.
First observe that outside the strip

|x_{2}|\leq d,
this function satisfies the homogeneous elliptic
equation
(\Delta_{x}+k_{1}^{2})w_{0+}=0,
and is therefore a

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function of
x.
From (
30
) and (
33
) it is
clear that

\partial_{x_{1}}w^{c}_{0+}(x;y)=-\partial_{y_{1}}w^{c}_{0+}(x;y),
hence it suffices to
analyze the smoothness and decay properties of

\partial_{x_{1}}^{j}w^{c}_{0+}(x;y),
for
j=0,1,2.

As noted, away from
x_{2}=y_{2},
the functions
\partial_{x_{1}}^{j}w_{0+}(0,x_{2};0,y_{2})

are
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions provided
x_{2}\neq\pm d
or
y_{2}\neq\pm d.
The
function
w_{0+}
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
in a neighborhood of these points with higher
regularity determined by the regularity of
q.
If
q
were a smooth
function, then it would follow that

(\Delta_{x}+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2}))q(x_{2})|x-y|^{2}H^{(1)}_{0}(k_{1}|x-y|)=\\
4q(x_{2})H^{(1)}_{0}(k_{1}|x-y|)+O(1+|x-y|\log|x-y|),

(42)

showing that the principal singularity along the diagonal would be given by

w_{0+}(x;y)=-i\frac{q(x_{2})}{16}|x-y|^{2}H^{(1)}_{0}(k_{1}|x-y|)+O(|x-y|^{3}\log|x-y|).

(43)

Even with
q
given by (
34
) this is essentially correct.

4
Estimates for the Boundary Kernel

In this section we analyze the behavior of the functions

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})=\left[\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x_{1}}\right]^{j}w^{c}_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};y_{1},y_{2})\upharpoonright_{x_{1}=y_{1}=0},\,j=0,1,2.

(44)

With these functions one can easily construct the kernel functions
appearing in the equations in (
25
). In the following
section we explain why similar estimates hold for

w_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};0,y_{2}),
and its derivatives

\partial_{y_{1}}w_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};0,y_{2}),\partial_{x_{1}}w_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};0,y_{2}),\text{ and
}\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{y_{1}}w_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};0,y_{2}),

where
x_{1}>0.
These estimates
require tedious, but rather standard analysis, using the properties of solutions
to second order ODEs, stationary phase and integration by parts. A reader only
interested in the final results can safely skip to Section
4.5
.

4.1
Estimates on the Wronskian and Basic Solutions of
L_{\xi}

We first spell out the properties of the basic solutions,

\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,0+;x_{2}),
and their Wronskian,
W(\xi),
in greater
detail. For these calculations we assume that
q
is given
in (
34
). Similar estimates hold for any piecewise
continuous, bounded potential, which is supported in an interval, though we can
no longer rely on explicit formulæ within the support of
q.

The Wronskian is given by

W(\xi)=\begin{cases}&-e^{2iBd}\left[(2\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})\frac{\sin 2dA}{A}-2iB\cos 2dA\right]\text{ for }|\xi|<k_{1},\\
&-e^{-2\widetilde{B}d}\left[(2\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})\frac{\sin 2dA}{A}+2\widetilde{B}\cos 2dA\right]\text{ for }k_{1}<|\xi|<k_{2},\\
&-e^{-2\widetilde{B}d}\left[(2\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})\frac{\sinh 2d\widetilde{A}}{\widetilde{A}}+2\widetilde{B}\cosh 2d\widetilde{A}\right]\text{ for }k_{2}<|\xi|,\end{cases}

(45)

where

\begin{split}A=\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}},\,&B=\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}},\\
\widetilde{A}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}},\,&\widetilde{B}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}.\end{split}

(46)

These formulæ and those for
\widetilde{u}_{+}
below are provided for the convenience of the reader, as all can be
derived from the formula for
|\xi|<k_{1}
by choosing the correct branch of the
square root:
\sqrt{z}
is defined in
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0],

with
\sqrt{x}\in(0,\infty)
for
x\in(0,\infty).

The Wronskian is analytic in a neighborhood of
\pm k_{2},
(where
A,\widetilde{A}

vanish), but has square root singularities at
\pm k_{1}.
It never vanishes at

\pm k_{2},
and does not vanish at
\pm k_{1},
provided that

2d\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\neq n\pi\text{ for any }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(47)

As
|\xi|\to\infty,
we have the lower bound

|W(\xi)|>M|\xi|.

(48)

Since
q
is supported in
[-d,d]
the asymptotics of
\tilde{w}_{0+}
as
|x_{2}|\to\infty

are given by

\begin{split}\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};y)&=-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;x_{2})e^{-iy_{1}\xi}}{2W(\xi)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}\times\\
&\int_{-d}^{d}\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;-z_{2})q(z_{2})e^{-|z_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}dz_{2},\text{ for }x_{2}>d,\\
\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};y)&=-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;-x_{2})e^{-iy_{1}\xi}}{2W(\xi)\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}\times\\
&\int_{-d}^{d}\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;z_{2})q(z_{2})e^{-|z_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}dz_{2},\text{ for }x_{2}<-d,\end{split}

(49)

which shows that we need formulæ for
\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;z_{2})
for all

z_{2}\geq-d.
We include the formula for
z_{2}<-d,
with
|\xi|<k_{1},

as we need the analytic properties of
\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;z_{2})
in the
appendices. For
|\xi|<k_{1},

\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;z_{2})=\begin{cases}&e^{iBz_{2}},\text{ for }z_{2}>d,\\
&e^{iBd}\left[\cos A(d-z_{2})-iB\frac{\sin A(d-z_{2})}{A}\right]\text{ for }|z_{2}|<d,\\
&e^{iBd}\Big[\left(\cos 2dA\cos B(d+z_{2})+A\sin 2dA\frac{\sin B(d+z_{2})}{B}\right)+\\
&iB\left(\cos 2dA\frac{\sin B(d+z_{2})}{B}-\frac{\sin 2dA}{A}\cos B(d+z_{2})\right)\Big]\text{ for }z_{2}<-d.\end{cases}

(50)

Note that for all
z_{2}<d,

\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;z_{2})=e^{i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}d}\left[\theta(\xi,z_{2})+\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}\varphi(\xi,z_{2})\right],

where
\theta
and
\varphi
are entire functions
\xi.
For the
convenience of the reader we include certain formulæ for
k_{1}<|\xi|<k_{2},

\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;z_{2})=\begin{cases}&e^{-\widetilde{B}z_{2}},\text{ for }z_{2}>d\\
&e^{-\widetilde{B}d}\left[\cos A(d-z_{2})+\widetilde{B}\frac{\sin A(d-z_{2})}{A}\right]\text{ for }|z_{2}|<d;\end{cases}

(51)

and for
k_{2}<|\xi|,

\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;z_{2})=\begin{cases}&e^{-\widetilde{B}z_{2}},\text{ for }z_{2}>d\\
&e^{-\widetilde{B}d}\left[\cosh\widetilde{A}(d-z_{2})+\widetilde{B}\frac{\sinh\widetilde{A}(d-z_{2})}{\widetilde{A}}\right]\text{ for }|z_{2}|<d.\end{cases}

(52)

If
\{\xi_{n}\}
are the positive roots of the Wronskian, then

\gamma^{-}_{n}=\{-\xi_{n}+\nu e^{i\theta}:\>\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}\text{ and }\gamma^{+}_{n}=\{\xi_{n}+\nu e^{i\theta}:\>\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}

(53)

are circles of radius
\nu
centered in these roots. We let
-\gamma^{\pm}_{n+}

denote the intersection of these circles with the upper half plane, oriented from left to
right. Before proceeding with this analysis, we observe that

\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})=\tilde{w}_{0+}(-\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2}),
which implies that, in the integral defining

\mathfrak{w}^{[1]},
the contributions from
\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}
cancel exactly, and
therefore

\mathfrak{w}^{[1]}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\int_{-\gamma^{-}_{n+}}\xi\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})d\xi+\int_{-\gamma^{+}_{n+}}\xi\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})d\xi\right].

(54)

Using the symmetries of the integrand it is not difficult to show that

\mathfrak{w}^{[1]}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{-\gamma^{+}_{n}}\xi\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})d\xi;

(55)

where
-\gamma^{+}_{n}
indicates that the circles about the
\{\xi_{n}\}
are oriented in
the clockwise direction. This gives
-i
times the sum of the residues of

\xi\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})
at the
\{\xi_{n}\}.
It is not difficult to see that

i\mathfrak{w}^{[1]}+\partial_{x_{1}}w_{0+}^{g}(0,x_{2};0,y_{2})=0,

(56)

as the function
\partial_{x_{1}}w_{0+}^{g}
is
i
times the sum of the residues of

i\xi\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})
at the
\{\xi_{n}\}.
Hence the kernel

\mathfrak{w}^{[1]}
does not play any role in the integral equations derived
in Section
5
, nonetheless we include estimates for its behavior as we need
these results for the representation formula, which involves
\partial_{y_{1}}w_{0+}(x;0,y_{2}).

4.2
Asymptotics for
|x_{2}|,|y_{2}|>d

For
x_{2}>d,
we write these functions in the form

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\xi^{j}\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})d\xi\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\xi^{j}\frac{\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;x_{2})\mathfrak{A}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}d\xi,

(57)

where, for
|\xi|<k_{1},

\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}=-i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}
and we write

\mathfrak{A}(\xi,\eta;y_{2})=\int_{-d}^{d}q(z_{2})e^{-\eta|y_{2}-z_{2}|}e^{-\eta d}\left[\cos A(d+z_{2})+\eta\frac{\sin A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]dz_{2}.

(58)

We rewrite the Wronskian as
W(\xi)=\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}),
with

\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\eta)=-e^{2i\eta d}\left[(2\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})\frac{\sin 2dA}{A}-2i\eta\cos 2dA\right]\text{ for }|\xi|<k_{1}.

(59)

These formulæ hold for other ranges of
\xi
by using the correct
branch of
B=\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}};
as noted these formulæ do not
depend on the choice of branch for
A=\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}}.
We use this
formulation, as
\mathfrak{A}(\xi,\eta;y_{2}),
and
\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\eta)
are entire
functions of
(\xi,\eta),
which allows us to better keep track of the
square root singularities at
\pm k_{1}
in our subsequent computations.

To analyze
\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}
we split the integral over
\xi
into the segment where

|\xi|<k_{1},
a segment with
k_{1}<|\xi|<k_{2},
and finally the segment where

k_{2}<|\xi|.
The leading order behavior is largely determined, via stationary
phase, by the integral over small neighborhood of
\xi=0;
whereas the diagonal
singularities come from large
|\xi|.

We begin with
\xi\in[-k_{1},k_{1}],
and denote these contributions by

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{0}(x_{2},y_{2}):

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{0}(x_{2},y_{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-k_{1}}^{k_{1}}\xi^{j}\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})d\xi\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-k_{1}}^{k_{1}}\xi^{j}\frac{\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0+;x_{2})\mathfrak{A}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}d\xi.

(60)

We first consider
x_{2},y_{2}>d,
so that

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{0}(x_{2},y_{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-k_{1}}^{k_{1}}\xi^{j}\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})d\xi\\
=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-k_{1}}^{k_{1}}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,-i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}d\xi,

(61)

where

\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\eta)=\int_{-d}^{d}q(z_{2})e^{\eta z_{2}}e^{-\eta d}\left[\cos A(d+z_{2})+\eta\frac{\sin A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]dz_{2}.

(62)

We split the integral in (
61
) into the part near to
\xi=0,
denoted
\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{00},

where the phase is stationary and the part near to
\xi=\pm k_{1},
denoted

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{01},
where the integrand appears to have a singularity. As we will
see, this singularity is removable by changing variables. Let

\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})),
be a non-negative, even function with

\phi(t)=1
for
|t|<\frac{1}{4}.
Using a standard stationary phase argument we
see that there are complete asymptotic expansions

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{00}(x_{2},y_{2})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-k_{1}}^{k_{1}}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,-i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\phi(\xi/k_{1})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}d\xi\\
\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}(x_{2}+y_{2})}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[M_{j0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{jl}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{l}}\right]\text{ for }j=0,1,2.

(63)

To treat the other part of the integral we let
s=\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}},
to obtain
a smooth integrand that can be integrated by parts giving

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{01}(x_{2},y_{2})=\\
\frac{i}{\pi}\int_{0}^{k_{1}}(k_{1}^{2}-s^{2})^{\frac{j}{2}}\frac{e^{is(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-s^{2}},-is)[1-\phi(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-s^{2}}/k_{1})}{\mathfrak{W}(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-s^{2}},s)\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-s^{2}}}ds=\\
-\frac{k_{1}^{j-1}}{\pi(x_{2}+y_{2})}\frac{\mathfrak{A}_{0}(k_{1},0)}{\mathfrak{W}(k_{1},0)}+O\left((x_{2}+y_{2})^{-2}\right).

(64)

The leading term here will cancel with the leading contribution from this
endpoint for the integrals over
set
k_{1}<|\xi|<k_{2}.
So overall, the contribution of this endpoint will be

O\left((x_{2}+y_{2})^{-2}\right).

We next consider,
\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{1},
the contributions from
k_{1}<|\xi|<k_{2},
which
includes small semi-circles around the zeros of the Wronskian. We first consider
the portions of these integrals from near to
\xi=\pm k_{1},
which we denote

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{10}(x_{2},y_{2}).
By evenness of the integrand it suffices to consider

\xi\in[k_{1},k_{1}+\mu].
Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}([0,\mu)),
for an

0<\mu\ll\xi_{1}-k_{1},
with
\phi(t)=1
for
t\in[0,\mu/2].
Set

s=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}},
and integrate by parts to get

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{10}(x_{2},y_{2})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{k_{1}}^{k_{1}+\mu}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\phi(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k^{2}_{1}})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi=\\
\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2k_{1}\mu+\mu^{2}}}(s^{2}+k_{1}^{2})^{\frac{j}{2}}\frac{e^{-s(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}},s)\phi(s)}{\mathfrak{W}(\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}},is)\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}}ds=\\
\frac{k_{1}^{j-1}}{\pi(x_{2}+y_{2})}\frac{\mathfrak{A}_{0}(k_{1},0)}{\mathfrak{W}(k_{1},0)}+O\left((x_{2}+y_{2})^{-2}\right).

(65)

As stated above,

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{10}(x_{2},y_{2})+\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{01}(x_{2},y_{2})=O\left((x_{2}+y_{2})^{-2}\right).

(66)

In fact the sum of these terms vanishes to all orders. This can be proved by using
analyticity to deform the portions of the contour near to
\pm k_{1}:
we replace
an interval
[-\epsilon-k_{1},\epsilon-k_{1}]
with a smooth contour meeting the
real axis smoothly, lying in the upper half plane and an interval

[-\epsilon+k_{1},\epsilon+k_{1}]
with a smooth contour meeting the real axis
smoothly, lying in the lower half plane. We call this contour

\Gamma^{+}_{\nu,\epsilon}.
An example is shown in Figure
3
. On this
contour the analytic continuation of
\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}
is smooth, has a
positive imaginary part, and no critical points. Hence

e^{i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}
is exponentially decaying, and therefore

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{10}(x_{2},y_{2})+\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{01}(x_{2},y_{2})=O\left((x_{2}+y_{2})^{-N}\right)\text{
for any }N>0.

(67)

A similar argument applies in all cases considered in the subsequent
sections. To treat the right half plane we use the contour

\Gamma^{-}_{\nu,\epsilon},
which is obtained by modifying
\Gamma^{-}_{\nu}
is
the same way, i.e. the change to the contour in the left half plane lies in

\operatorname{Im}\xi>0,
and that in the right half plane lies in
\operatorname{Im}\xi<0.
Note:
deforming the contours from
\Gamma^{\pm}_{\nu}
to

\Gamma^{\pm}_{\nu,\epsilon}
does not change the value of the integral, and
therefore it follows that all finite order contributions from near to
\pm k_{1}

along
\Gamma^{\pm}_{\nu}
also cancel.

Figure 3
:
The blue contour is
\Gamma^{+}_{\nu,\epsilon}
showing the smooth curves
replacing intervals

[-\epsilon-k_{1},\epsilon-k_{1}]\cup[-\epsilon+k_{1},\epsilon+k_{1}].
The roots
of the Wronskian are shown as asterisks, and
\pm k_{1}
as diamonds.

Now we need to estimate the contributions from the remainder of

\Gamma_{\nu,\epsilon}^{+}
lying over
[k_{1}+\mu/2,k_{2}]\cup[-k_{2},-(k_{1}+\mu/2)].
Other than the semi-circles centered on the
roots of the Wronskian,

\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}\{\pm\xi_{n}+\nu e^{i\theta}:\>\theta\in[\pi,0]\}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}C^{\pm}_{n,\nu},

(68)

we use the evenness of the integrand to restrict our attention to

[k_{1}+\mu/2,k_{2}].

To estimate the contributions from the semi-circles we need to
estimate both
|\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})|,
from above, and

|\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})|,
from below, on these
arcs. As the roots of the Wronskian are all simple, there
is a constant
M
so that

|\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})|\geq M\nu\text{ for }\xi\in C^{\pm}_{n,\nu}.

(69)

On the other hand, for
\xi\in C^{\pm}_{n,\nu},

|\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\eta)|\leq\int_{-d}^{d}q(z_{2})e^{\alpha(z_{2}-d)}\left[\exp\beta(d-z_{2})+|\eta|\frac{\exp\beta(d-z_{2})}{|\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}}|}\right]dz_{2},

(70)

where

\alpha=\operatorname{Re}\left[\sqrt{(\pm\xi_{n}+\nu e^{i\theta})^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\right]\text{ and }\beta=\left|\operatorname{Im}\left[\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-(\pm\xi_{n}+\nu e^{i\theta})^{2}}\right]\right|.

(71)

There is a constant,
m
so that, for
\theta\in[0,2\pi],
we have

\alpha>\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(1-m\nu)\text{ and }\beta<m\nu.

(72)

These inequalities rely on the fact that
k_{1}<\xi_{j}<k_{2}.
If we choose
0<\nu

sufficiently small so that

m\nu-\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(1-m\nu)<0\text{ for }n=1,\dots,N,

(73)

then it follows that there exists a constant,
M,
so that

|\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\eta)|\leq M\text{ for }\xi\in C^{\pm}_{n,\nu}\text{
for }n=1,\dots,N.

(74)

For sufficiently small
\nu
we therefore have the estimates

\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{C_{n,\nu}^{\pm}}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi\right|\leq\\
M\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(1-m\nu)(x_{2}+y_{2})}}{\nu}.

(75)

To treat
\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}\cap[k_{1}+\mu/2,k_{2}],
we see that
|\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\eta)|
is
bounded by a constant on this set and therefore

\left|\,\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}\cap[k_{1}+\mu/2,k_{2}]}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi\right|\leq\\
M\frac{e^{-\mu k_{1}(x_{2}+y_{2})/2}}{\nu}.

(76)

To summarize, we have shown that for
x_{2},y_{2}>d,
the contributions to the
integrals defining
\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2}),
from frequencies
\xi\in[-k_{2},k_{2}]

are given by the asymptotic expansions in (
63
), with errors

O((x_{2}+y_{2})^{-N}),
for any
N>0.
We conclude the discussion of this case by
estimating the contribution from
|\xi|>k_{2},
which we denote by

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{2}(x_{2},y_{2}).
As before, by evenness, it suffices to consider

[k_{2},\infty).
Using the formulæ above we see that

\mathfrak{w}_{2}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})=\int_{k_{2}}^{\infty}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi,

(77)

where

\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})=\int_{-d}^{d}q(z_{2})e^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(z_{2}-d)}\times\\
\left[\cosh\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}(d+z_{2})+\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}(d+z_{2})}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}}\right]dz_{2}.

(78)

From this formula we can easily show that there is a constant
M
so that

|\mathfrak{A}_{0}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})|\leq\frac{Me^{2d\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}}{1+|\xi|}\text{ for }\xi\in[k_{2},\infty),

(79)

and therefore, for
x_{2},y_{2}>d,\,|\xi|>k_{2}
we have the estimate

|\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})|\leq M\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2}-2d)}}{(1+|\xi|)^{3}}.

(80)

Hence

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{w}_{2}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})|&\leq M\int_{k_{2}}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{j}e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2}-2d)}}{(1+|\xi|)^{3}}d\xi\\
&\leq\begin{cases}&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\text{ for }j=0,1\\
&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}+y_{2})}\log\left(\frac{2(x_{2}+y_{2})}{x_{2}+y_{2}-2d}\right)\text{
for }j=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

(81)

The calculation above indicates that the diagonal singularity should be

|x-y|^{2}\log|x-y|,
and this implies that we should expect a
\log
-singularity
in
\mathfrak{w}^{[2]}
where
x_{2}+y_{2}=2d.

Altogether we have shown that, for
x_{2},y_{2}>d
we have the asymptotic
formulæ

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\sim\\
\frac{e^{ik_{1}(x_{2}+y_{2})}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[M_{j0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{jl}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{l}}\right]\text{ for }j=0,1,2.

(82)

We can also analyze

\partial_{x_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})-ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2}),\text{ and }\partial_{y_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})-ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2}),

(83)

which are only significantly different in the contribution from

\xi\in[-k_{1},k_{1}].
In this interval we get an additional factor
of
i(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}-k_{1})=O(\xi^{2})
in the integrand. From the
usual stationary phase argument it will follow that, for
j=0,1,2,

\begin{split}\partial_{x_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})-ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})&\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}(x_{2}+y_{2})}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{\frac{j+3}{2}}}\left[M^{\prime}_{j0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{M^{\prime}_{jl}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{l}}\right],\\
\partial_{y_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})-ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})&\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}(x_{2}+y_{2})}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{\frac{j+3}{2}}}\left[M^{\prime\prime}_{j0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{M^{\prime\prime}_{jl}}{(x_{2}+y_{2})^{l}}\right].\end{split}

(84)

It is not difficult to see that the same arguments apply if

\pm x_{2},\pm y_{2}>d

to show that the same expansions hold with
(x_{2},y_{2})
replaced with

(|x_{2}|,|y_{2}|),
for example:

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)}}{(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[M^{\pm,\pm}_{j0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{M^{\pm,\pm}_{jl}}{(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)^{l}}\right],\text{ for }j=0,1,2.

(85)

We can also show

\partial_{x_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\mp ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2}),\text{ for }\pm x_{2}>d,\text{ and }\\
\partial_{y_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\mp ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\text{ for }\pm y_{2}>d,

(86)

have asymptotic expansions like those in (
84
), with
(x_{2},y_{2})

replaced by
(|x_{2}|,|y_{2}|),
obtained by applying the appropriate operator,

\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1},
or
\partial_{y_{2}}\mp ik_{1},
to the expansions
in (
85
). The function
\mathfrak{w}^{[0]}(x_{2},y_{2})
is continuous as

(x_{2},y_{2})\to(\pm d,\pm d),
and

|\mathfrak{w}^{[2]}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq M|\log(|x_{2}+y_{2}|-2d)|,\text{ as }|x_{2}+y_{2}|\to 2d.

(87)

There is no singularity as
(x_{2},y_{2})\to(\pm d,\mp d).
Similar conclusions
hold for
w_{0+}=w_{0+}^{c}+w_{0+}^{g},
as the contribution of the wave-guide
modes is exponentially decaying as
|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|\to\infty.

4.3
Asymptotics for
|x_{2}|
or
|y_{2}|<d

We now consider what happens if either
|x_{2}|<d,
or
|y_{2}|<d.
We start by
assuming that
y_{2}>d,
but
|x_{2}|<d.
For this case the integrals defining

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}
take the form

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\nu,\epsilon}^{+}}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}(y_{2}+2d)}\mathfrak{B}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}};x_{2})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}d\xi,

(88)

where

\mathfrak{B}(\xi,\eta;x_{2})=\int\limits_{-d}^{x_{2}}\Bigg[\cos A(d-x_{2})\cos A(d+z_{2})-i\frac{\eta}{A}\sin A(2d+z_{2}-x_{2})-\\
\frac{\eta^{2}}{A^{2}}\sin A(d-x_{2})\sin A(d+z_{2})\Bigg]e^{-iz_{2}\eta}q(z_{2})dz_{2}+\\
\int\limits_{x_{2}}^{d}\Bigg[\cos A(d+x_{2})\cos A(d-z_{2})-i\frac{\eta}{A}\sin A(2d-z_{2}+x_{2})-\\
\frac{\eta^{2}}{A^{2}}\sin A(d+x_{2})\sin A(d-z_{2})\Bigg]e^{-iz_{2}\eta}q(z_{2})dz_{2}.

(89)

Again
\mathfrak{B}(\xi,\eta;x_{2})
is an entire
function of
(\xi,\eta).
In the integral over
\Gamma_{\nu,\epsilon}^{+}
we
take
\eta=\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}=i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}
for
k_{1}<|\xi|.

As before the principal contribution to
\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})
, as

y_{2}\to\infty,
comes from the stationary phase at
\xi=0.
The
function
|\mathfrak{B}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}};x_{2})|
is easily seen to be
uniformly bounded where
|\xi|<k_{1},|x_{2}|<d.

We separate the contributions from the stationary point at zero, and
the contributions from endpoints
\pm k_{1}.
The leading contributions from
the stationary point are

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{00}(x_{2},y_{2})=C^{\prime}_{j}k_{1}^{\frac{j-1}{2}}\frac{e^{ik_{1}y_{2}}\mathfrak{B}(0,k_{1};x_{2})}{\mathfrak{W}(0,k_{1})\,y_{2}^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}+O\left(|y_{2}|^{-{\frac{j+3}{2}}}\right)\text{
for }j=0,1,2.

(90)

As before, there are complete asymptotic expansions of the form

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{00}(x_{2},y_{2})=k_{1}^{\frac{j-1}{2}}\frac{e^{ik_{1}y_{2}}}{\mathfrak{W}(0,k_{1})\,y_{2}^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{jl}(x_{2})}{y_{2}^{l}}\right],\text{ for }j=0,1,2.

(91)

The contributions from near to
\pm k_{1}
are estimated as before by using the
deformed contour
\Gamma^{+}_{\nu,\epsilon}.
The portions from
\delta>0
to

k_{1}+\mu,
and from
-k_{1}-\mu
to
-\delta
are easily seen to be

O(y_{2}^{-N}),
for any
N>0.
We leave the details to the reader.

We next consider the integral over
\Gamma_{\nu,\epsilon}^{+}
lying above

k_{1}+\mu<|\xi|<k_{2},
which is of the form

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{11}(x_{2},y_{2})=\\
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\{k_{1}+\mu<|\xi|<k_{2}\}\cap\Gamma_{\nu,\epsilon}^{+}}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(y_{2}+2d)}\mathfrak{B}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};x_{2})\widetilde{\phi}(\xi)}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi,

(92)

for a suitable cut-off function
\widetilde{\phi}.
As before we use the lower bound on
the Wronskian,
|\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})|>m\nu.
We also need to bound

\mathfrak{B}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};x_{2})
from above. For

|x_{2}|<d,
we can easily show that there is a constant
M
so that

|\mathfrak{B}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};x_{2})|\leq Me^{\rho(\xi)d},\text{
where }\rho(\xi)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\right).

(93)

It then follows that

|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{11}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{\nu}\int\limits_{k_{1}+\mu<|\xi|<k_{2}}e^{-\rho(\xi)(y_{2}+d)}d\xi

(94)

If we set

\alpha=\min\{\rho(\xi):\>\xi\in\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}\text{ with }k_{1}+\mu<|\xi|<k_{2}\},

(95)

then
\alpha>0,
provided that
\nu
is sufficiently small,
from which it follows easily that

|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{11}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq\frac{Me^{-\alpha y_{2}}}{\nu}.

(96)

This leaves the integral over the set
\{|\xi|>k_{2}\}.
In this set
we can prove the estimate

\left|\mathfrak{B}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};x_{2})\right|\leq Me^{(2d+x_{2})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}\left[\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}+(d-x_{2})\right],

(97)

and therefore, if either
|x_{2}|<d
or
|y_{2}|<d,
and
|\xi|>k_{2},
we have the estimate

|\tilde{w}_{0+}(\xi,x_{2};0,y_{2})|<M\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}{(1+|\xi|)^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|)}+|y_{2}-x_{2}|\right],

(98)

and therefore for
|x_{2}|<d<y_{2},
we have

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})|&=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{k_{2}<|\xi|}\xi^{j}\frac{e^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(y_{2}+2d)}\mathfrak{B}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};x_{2})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi\right|\\
&\leq M\int_{k_{2}}^{\infty}e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(y_{2}-x_{2})}\left[\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|)^{3-j}}+\frac{y_{2}-x_{2}}{(1+|\xi|)^{2-j}}\right]d\xi.\end{split}

(99)

An elementary calculation now shows that

|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq\begin{cases}&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(y_{2}-x_{2})}\text{ for }j=0,1\\
&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(y_{2}-x_{2})}\log\left(\frac{2y_{2}}{y_{2}-x_{2}}\right)\text{ for }j=2.\end{cases}

(100)

We can again show that this analysis applies equally well
if
|x_{2}|<d,y_{2}<-d,
and therefore we have the asymptotic expansions

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\sim k_{1}^{\frac{j-1}{2}}\frac{e^{ik_{1}|y_{2}|}}{|y_{2}|^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{b^{\pm}_{jl}(x_{2})}{|y_{2}|^{l}}\right],\text{ for }\pm y_{2}>d,\,j=0,1,2.

(101)

The functions
\mathfrak{w}^{[0]}(x_{2},y_{2}),\mathfrak{w}^{[1]}(x_{2},y_{2})
are
continuous as
y_{2}\to x_{2},
and

|\mathfrak{w}^{[2]}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq M|\log|y_{2}-x_{2}||,\text{ as }y_{2}\to x_{2}.

(102)

As before we can show that

\partial_{y_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\mp ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})=O\left(|y_{2}|^{-\frac{j+3}{2}}\right),

(103)

in fact these functions have complete asymptotic expansion obtained by
applying the operator
\partial_{y_{2}}\mp ik_{1}
to the expansions above.

The final case we need to treat is
|y_{2}|<d,|x_{2}|>d.
We begin with
x_{2}>d.

For this case we write

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\nu,\epsilon}^{+}}\frac{\xi^{j}e^{i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}x_{2}}\mathfrak{C}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}};y_{2})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}d\xi,

(104)

where

\mathfrak{C}(\xi,\eta;y_{2})=e^{i\eta(y_{2}+d)}\int_{-d}^{y_{2}}e^{-i\eta z_{2}}\left[\cos A(d+z_{2})-i\eta\frac{\sin A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]q(z_{2})dz_{2}+\\
e^{i\eta(d-y_{2})}\int_{y_{2}}^{d}e^{i\eta z_{2}}\left[\cos A(d+z_{2})-i\eta\frac{\sin A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]q(z_{2})dz_{2},\text{
with }A=\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}}.

(105)

As in the earlier cases, there is a stationary phase contribution
from
\xi=0,
which we denote by
\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{00}(x_{2},y_{2}).
The function

\mathfrak{C}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}};y_{2})
is an analytic function of
\xi
in

|\xi|<k_{1}-\epsilon,
for any
\epsilon>0.
The stationary phase
contributions are

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{00}(x_{2},y_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}x_{2}}}{\mathfrak{W}(0,k_{1})\,x_{2}^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{jl}(y_{2})}{x_{2}^{l}}\right]\text{ for }j=0,1,2.

(106)

The analysis for the contribution from
\delta<|\xi|<\sqrt{k^{2}_{1}+\epsilon^{2}}
is as before,
and can be shown to be
O(x_{2}^{-N}),
for any
N>0.

The remaining portion for
\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}<|\xi|<k_{2}
is given by

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{11}(x_{2},y_{2})=\\
\frac{1}{i\pi}\int\limits_{\{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}<|\xi|<k_{2}\}\cap\Gamma_{\nu,\epsilon}^{+}}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}x_{2}}\,\xi^{j}\mathfrak{C}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})(1-\widetilde{\phi}(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}))}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi,

(107)

where

\mathfrak{C}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})=e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(y_{2}+d)}\times\\
\int_{-d}^{y_{2}}e^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}z_{2}}\left[\cos A(d+z_{2})+\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\frac{\sin A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]q(z_{2})dz_{2}+\\
e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(d-y_{2})}\times\\
\int_{y_{2}}^{d}e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}z_{2}}\left[\cos A(d+z_{2})+\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\frac{\sin A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]q(z_{2})dz_{2}\\
\text{ with }A=\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}}.

(108)

It is easy to see that for
|y_{2}|<d,

|\mathfrak{C}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})|
is bounded, and the Wronskian satisfies

|\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})|>M\nu,
on the part of

\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}
lying over
\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}<|\xi|<k_{2}.
Moreover

\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}>\alpha>0,
therefore

|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{11}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq\frac{Me^{-\alpha x_{2}}}{\nu}.

(109)

Where
|\xi|>k_{2}
we have

\mathfrak{C}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})=e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(y_{2}+d)}\times\\
\int_{-d}^{y_{2}}e^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}z_{2}}\left[\cosh A(d+z_{2})+\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\frac{\sinh A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]q(z_{2})dz_{2}+\\
e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(d-y_{2})}\times\\
\int_{y_{2}}^{d}e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}z_{2}}\left[\cosh A(d+z_{2})+\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\frac{\sinh A(d+z_{2})}{A}\right]q(z_{2})dz_{2}\\
\text{ with }A=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}.

(110)

The first term in (
110
) is bounded by

M\frac{e^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}y_{2}}}{1+|\xi|};

(111)

the other term requires somewhat more care. It is bounded by

Me^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}y_{2}}\int_{y_{2}}^{d}e^{(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})z_{2}}dz_{2}=\\
Me^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}y_{2}}\left[\frac{e^{(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})d}-e^{(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})y_{2}}}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}\right].

(112)

An elementary argument then shows that the expression in the bracket is
bounded by
|d-y_{2}|
and therefore

|\mathfrak{C}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})|\leq Me^{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}y_{2}}\left[\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}+|d-y_{2}|\right].

(113)

These estimates show that

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})|&=\frac{1}{\pi}\left|\int_{k_{2}<|\xi|}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}x_{2}}\,\xi^{j}\mathfrak{C}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};y_{2})}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}d\xi\right|\\
&\leq M\int_{k_{2}}^{\infty}e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}-y_{2})}\xi^{j-2}\left[\frac{1}{\xi}+(x_{2}-y_{2})\right]d\xi.\end{split}

(114)

In the second line we use the fact that
x_{2}-y_{2}>d-y_{2}.
It is easy to show
that the second term is bounded by
Me^{\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}-y_{2})}
for
j=0,2.

We estimate the contribution from the first term, as before, to obtain that
for
|y_{2}|<d,x_{2}>d,
we have

|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq\begin{cases}&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}-y_{2})}\text{ for }j=0,1\\
&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}(x_{2}-y_{2})}\cdot\log\frac{2x_{2}}{x_{2}-y_{2}}\text{ for }j=2.\end{cases}

(115)

It is not difficult to show that the analogous estimates hold with

x_{2}<-d,
so that

|\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq\begin{cases}&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}\text{ for }j=0,1\\
&Me^{-\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}\cdot\log\frac{2|x_{2}|}{|x_{2}-y_{2}|}\text{ for }j=2.\end{cases}

(116)

Altogether we have, for
|y_{2}|<d,|x_{2}|>d,

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}|x_{2}|}}{|x_{2}|^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{c^{\pm}_{jl}(y_{2})}{|x_{2}|^{l}}\right],\text{ for }\pm x_{2}>d,\,j=0,1,2.

(117)

The function
\mathfrak{w}^{[0]}(x_{2},y_{2})
is continuous as

|x_{2}-y_{2}|\to 0,
and

|\mathfrak{w}^{[2]}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq M|\log|x_{2}-y_{2}||.

(118)

As before we can show that

\partial_{x_{2}}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\mp ik_{1}\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})=O\left(|x_{2}|^{-\frac{j+3}{2}}\right)\text{ as }\pm x_{2}\to\infty,

(119)

have asymptotic expansions obtained by applying
\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1}
to the
expansions in (
117
).

4.4
The Diagonal Singularity

The singularities of the function
w(x;y)
are confined to the set
x=y

where
|x_{2}|,|y_{2}|\leq d.
In this section we use the Fourier representation
to study the nature of this singularity. As expected from the discussion
surrounding (
42
), the principal singularity behaves like

(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}\log|x_{2}-y_{2}|.
Note that the discussion leading
to (
42
) assumed that the potential
q(x_{2})
is smooth, whereas
here we continue working with
q
as defined in (
34
).

We start by assuming that
-d<y_{2}<x_{2}<d,
so that

Definition 1
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},
let

denote

(139)

These spaces are somewhat like Hölder spaces, in that, for

is not dense in

with respect to

It is important to have
a criterion for when a bounded linear operator
A:\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})

is compact. We give a simple sufficient condition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
0<\alpha<\beta,
and let
A:\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R})

be a bounded linear operator. Let

B_{r}=\{f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}):\>|f|_{\alpha}<r\}.
If, for any
0<X,
the
image
AB_{r}
restricted to
[-X,X]
is a uniformly equicontinuous family of
functions, then
A:\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
is a compact
operator.

Proof.

To prove the proposition we need to show that if
\{f_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
is a bounded sequence, then
\{Af_{n}\}
has a

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
-convergent subsequence. The hypotheses of the
proposition imply that there are positive constants
M,r
so that

\{f_{n}\}\subset B_{r}
and

|Af|_{\beta}\leq M|f|_{\alpha}\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}).

(140)

The restriction of
\{Af_{n}\}
to any interval
[-X,X]
is a bounded,
uniformly equicontinuous family. Hence a simple diagonal argument using
the Arzela-Ascoli theorem produces a subsequence
\{f_{n_{j}}\}
so that

\{Af_{n_{j}}\}
converges to
g\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R})
uniformly on any interval

[-X,X].
In fact this sequence also converges in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}).

For the terms of the sequence we have the estimate

|Af_{n_{j}}(x)|(1+|x|)^{\beta}\leq Mr.

(141)

Letting
j\to\infty
shows that

|g(x)|(1+|x|)^{\beta}\leq Mr

(142)

as well. These estimates and the triangle inequality show that

|Af_{n_{j}}(x)-g(x)|(1+|x|)^{\alpha}\leq 2Mr\frac{(1+|x|)^{\alpha}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}.

(143)

For an
\epsilon>0,
we can therefore choose
X
so that

|Af_{n_{j}}(x)-g(x)|(1+|x|)^{\alpha}\leq\epsilon\text{ if }|x|>X.

(144)

As
\{Af_{n_{j}}\upharpoonright_{[-X,X]}\}
converges uniformly to

g\upharpoonright_{[-X,X]},
there is a
J
so that if
j>J,
then

|Af_{n_{j}}(x)-g(x)|(1+|x|)^{\alpha}\leq\epsilon\text{ if }|x|\leq X.

(145)

Together these estimates show that

|Af_{n_{j}}-g|_{\alpha}<\epsilon\text{ if }j>J,

(146)

which completes the proof of the proposition.

Admissible Data

In general, our method for solving the transmission problem specified

If the incoming fields

In this setting, point sources will arise from taking a fundamental solution for a
bi-infinite strip,
\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}(x;y),
which is precisely what we have constructed
above. Using our representation, we have

\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}(x;y)=g_{k_{1}}(x-y)+w^{l,r}(x:y).

(175)

y_{0}
with

then we can use

as point source in the left half plane at
y_{0};
we
can let
u^{\operatorname{in}}_{r}=0.
The data for the transmission
problem is then

The analysis in Section
4.6
is easily adapted to show that

and

\begin{split}&g(x_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}|x_{2}|}}{\sqrt{|x_{2}|}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}^{\pm}}{|x_{2}|^{j}},\\
&h(x_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}|x_{2}|}}{|x_{2}|}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{j}^{\pm}}{|x_{2}|^{j}}.\end{split}

(177)

This sort of asymptotic behavior is needed in order for the solutions we

(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})g(x_{2})=(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})u^{l}(0^{-},x_{2})-(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})u^{r}(0^{+},x_{2})=O((\pm x_{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}).

(178)

k_{1}<k_{2},

solution to the transmission problem produced by our method can also expected to

with

for a

then the incoming wave

v_{\mu}^{\operatorname{in}}(r\eta)=O(r^{-N})
for any

if

.

The Projections onto Wave-Guide Modes

In the foregoing pages we have explained a method to find and
represent solutions to the transmission problem that results from two
semi-infinite wave-guides meeting along a common perpendicular
line. The solution is represented in each half plane by layer
potentials along this line, with sources
(\sigma,\tau),

see (
168
). The solutions in each half plane can be split
into a contribution from the wave-guide modes and “radiation,”

u^{l,r}(x)=u^{l,r}_{g}(x)+u^{l,r}_{\operatorname{rad}}(x).

(186)

If
\{(v_{n}^{l,r},\xi_{n}^{l,r}):\>n=1,\dots,N_{l,r}\}
are the guided modes,
normalized to have
L^{2}
-norms 1, then
the projection into the guided modes is given by

u^{l,r}_{g}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{l,r}}v_{n}^{l,r}(x_{2})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u^{l,r}(x_{1},y_{2})v_{n}^{l,r}(y_{2})dy_{2}

(187)

This has a very simple expression in terms of our representation, coming
entirely from the
w^{g}_{0+}
-term in the expression for
\mathfrak{E}^{l,r},
see (
41
).

We assume that
u^{l,r}
is given by (
168
), with sources

(\sigma,\tau)\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}).
As noted
in Section
5
the representations for
u^{l,r}
and
\partial_{x_{1}}u^{l,r}
are
in terms of absolutely convergent integrals. The key observation is the
following

Proposition 4
.

Let
(\sigma,\tau)\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
and
let
v_{n}^{l,r}(x_{2})e^{i\xi_{n}^{l,r}x_{1}}
be a wave-guide mode for

\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q_{l,r}(x_{2}),
then, for all
\pm x_{1}>0,

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}[g_{k_{1}}(|(x_{1},x_{2}-y_{2})|)+w^{c;l,r}_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};0,y_{2})]\tau(y_{2})v_{n}^{l,r}(x_{2})dy_{2}dx_{2}=0,\\
&\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{y_{1}}[g_{k_{1}}(|(x_{1}-y_{1},x_{2}-y_{2})|)+\\
&\phantom{mmmmmmmmmmmm}w^{c;l,r}_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};y_{1},y_{2})]_{y_{1}=0}\sigma(y_{2})v_{n}^{l,r}(x_{2})dy_{2}dx_{2}=0.\end{split}

(188)

Proof.

We give the details for the right half plane. The estimates proved in the
previous sections show that these integrals are absolutely convergent and
therefore we can change the order of the integrations. To prove the
proposition we show that, for each
y_{2}
and
x_{1}>0,
we have

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}[g_{k_{1}}(|(x_{1},x_{2}-y_{2})|)+w^{c;r}_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};0,y_{2})]v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}=0,\\
&\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{y_{1}}[g_{k_{1}}(|(x_{1}-y_{1},x_{2}-y_{2})|)+w^{c;r}_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};y_{1},y_{2})]_{y_{1}=0}v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}=0.\end{split}

(189)

To prove these statements we use the Sommerfeld integral representation for
the free space fundamental solution, see (
30
).

We begin with the single layer term, which can be written as the iterated
integral:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}+\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\tilde{w}_{0+}^{c;r}(\xi,x_{2};y_{2})\right]e^{ix_{1}\xi}d\xi\,v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}.

(190)

We would like to change the order of integrations in this integral, which
would be easily justified if the integral in
\xi
were over any finite
interval. Note that

\int\limits_{k_{1}+1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{ie^{-|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}e^{-iy_{1}\xi}}{2\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}\right|d\xi\leq M[1+|\log|x_{2}-y_{2}|].

(191)

Combining this with the estimates (
80
) and (
98
), and the fact that

|v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})|\leq Me^{-|x_{2}|\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{r\,2}-k_{1}^{2}}}
shows that these integrals are
absolutely convergent and therefore we can interchange the order of the
integrations. By analyticity we can also replace the integral in the first term with an
integral over
\Gamma_{\nu}^{+},
to obtain

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\left[\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}+\tilde{w}_{0+}^{c;r}(\xi,x_{2};y_{2})\right]e^{ix_{1}\xi}v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}d\xi.

(192)

Using the fact that
\xi^{r\,2}_{n}v_{n}^{r}=(\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}+q_{r}(x_{2}))v_{n}^{r}

integrating by parts it follows that

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}+\tilde{w}_{0+}^{c;r}(\xi,x_{2};y_{2})\right]e^{ix_{1}\xi}v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}=\frac{ie^{ix_{1}\xi}v_{n}^{r}(y_{2})}{\xi_{n}^{r\,2}-\xi^{2}}.

(193)

Hence the double integral is

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\frac{ie^{ix_{1}\xi}v_{n}^{r}(y_{2})d\xi}{\xi^{2}-\xi_{n}^{r\,2}},

(194)

which, using Cauchy’s theorem, is easily seen to vanish for
x_{1}>0.

The double layer is almost the same; the double integral in (
190
) is
replaced by

\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\,\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\left[\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}+\tilde{w}_{0+}^{c;r}(\xi,x_{2};y_{2})\right]\xi e^{ix_{1}\xi}d\xi\right]v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}.

(195)

The integral involving
\tilde{w}_{0+}^{c;r}(\xi,x_{2};y_{2})
is again easily seen to be
absolutely convergent, but the additional factor of
\xi
makes the other
term more subtle. We need an estimate for this term that takes account of
the fact that
x_{1}>0.

Lemma 2
.

For
x_{1}>0,\lambda>0
and
R>k_{1}
let

f^{\pm}_{R}(x_{1},\lambda)=\pm\int\limits_{\pm R}^{\pm\infty}\frac{e^{ix_{1}\xi-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}\xi d\xi}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}.

(196)

These functions satisfy the estimates

|f^{\pm}_{R}(x_{1},\lambda)|\leq\frac{Me^{-\lambda\sqrt{R^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}}{x_{1}}

(197)

Proof.

As
f^{-}_{R}(x_{1},\lambda)=\overline{f^{+}_{R}(x_{1},\lambda)}
it suffices to do the
+
-case.
If we let
s=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}},
then

f^{+}_{R}(x_{1},\lambda)=\int\limits_{\sqrt{R^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}^{\infty}e^{ix_{1}\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}-\lambda s}ds.

(198)

Noting that

\partial_{s}\left[\frac{\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}}{s}e^{ix_{1}\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}}\right]=\\
ix_{1}e^{ix_{1}\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}}-\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{s^{2}\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}}e^{ix_{1}\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}},

(199)

integration by parts shows that

f^{+}_{R}(x_{1},\lambda)=\frac{1}{ix_{1}}\Bigg[\frac{k_{1}e^{-\lambda\sqrt{R^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}+ix_{1}R}}{\sqrt{R^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}+\\
\int\limits_{\sqrt{R^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\lambda\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}}{s}+\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{s^{2}\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}}\right]e^{ix_{1}\sqrt{s^{2}+k_{1}^{2}}-\lambda s}ds\Bigg].

(200)

The integral is easily seen to be
O(e^{-\lambda\sqrt{R^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}),
which
completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We rewrite the integral in (
195
) as

\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\left[\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}\right]\xi e^{ix_{1}\xi}d\xi\,v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}=\\
\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\,\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}\cap D_{R}}\left[\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}\right]\xi e^{ix_{1}\xi}d\xi+f_{R}^{+}(x_{1},|x_{2}-y_{2}|)+f_{R}^{-}(x_{1},|x_{2}-y_{2}|)\right]v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}.

(201)

In the part of the integral over
\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}\cap D_{R}
we can
interchange the order of the integrations. We use the lemma to estimate
the other terms

\begin{split}\Bigg|\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}[f_{R}^{+}(x_{1},|x_{2}-y_{2}|)&+f_{R}^{-}(x_{1},|x_{2}-y_{2}|)]v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}\Bigg|\\
&\leq\frac{M}{x_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\sqrt{R^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}e^{-\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{r\,2}-k_{1}^{2}}|x_{2}|}dx_{2}\\

Thus we see that

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\,\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\left[\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}+\tilde{w}_{0+}^{c;r}(\xi,x_{2};y_{2})\right]\xi e^{ix_{1}\xi}d\xi\right]v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}=\\

(203)

The

-integral is computed in (

), giving

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\,\int\limits_{\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}\left[\frac{ie^{i|x_{2}-y_{2}|\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}}{2\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}+\tilde{w}_{0+}^{c;r}(\xi,x_{2};y_{2})\right]\xi e^{ix_{1}\xi}d\xi\right]v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})dx_{2}=\\

(204)

The last equality follows from Cauchy’s theorem. It completes the proof
of (

) and also of the proposition.
∎

Using this proposition we can compute the projections of
u^{l,r}
to the
respective right and left wave-guide modes given in (
187
). In our
representation

u^{l,r}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}(x;0,y_{2})\tau(y_{2})dy_{2}-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{y_{1}}\mathfrak{E}^{l,r}(x;0,y_{2})\sigma(y_{2})dy_{2}.

(205)

We give the details for
\{x_{1}>0\};

Proposition
4
and (

) show that

u_{g}^{r}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{r}}[\langle\tau,v_{n}^{r}\rangle+i\xi_{n}^{r}\langle\sigma,v_{n}^{r}\rangle]v_{n}^{r}(x_{2})e^{i\xi_{n}^{r}x_{1}},

(206)

where

\langle f,g\rangle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx.

(207)

Hence the projections of
u^{l,r}
onto the wave-guide modes are completely
determined by the projections,
\{\langle\tau,v_{n}^{l,r}\rangle,\langle\sigma,v_{n}^{l,r}\rangle:\>n=1,\dots,N_{l,r}\},
of the source terms onto these modes.

If these projections could be determined directly from the data, then we could
determine the scattering relation, from incoming wave-guide modes to outgoing
modes, without having to solve the complete problem. Starting with the
equation (
138
) we can almost find equations for the coefficients
in (
206
). Projecting these equations into span of the wave-guide
modes we obtain

\begin{split}P^{l,r}_{g}\sigma+P^{l,r}_{g}D\tau&=P^{l,r}_{g}g\\
P^{l,r}_{g}C\sigma+P^{l,r}_{g}\tau&=P^{l,r}_{g}h,\end{split}

(208)

where we let

P^{l,r}_{g}f=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{l,r}}\langle f,v_{n}^{l,r}\rangle v_{n}^{l,r}(x_{2}).

(209)

These equations can be rewritten as

\begin{split}P^{l,r}_{g}\sigma+P^{l,r}_{g}DP_{g}^{l,r}\tau&=P^{l,r}_{g}g-P^{l,r}_{g}D(\operatorname{Id}-P_{g}^{l,r})\tau\\
P^{l,r}_{g}CP^{l,r}_{g}\sigma+P^{l,r}_{g}\tau&=P^{l,r}_{g}h-P^{l,r}_{g}C(\operatorname{Id}-P^{l,r}_{g})\sigma.\end{split}

(210)

While this is not quite a system of equations for the projections

(P^{l,r}_{g}\sigma,P^{l,r}_{g}\tau),
the facts that
P^{l,r}_{g}(\operatorname{Id}-P^{l,r}_{g})=0,

and the norms of
\|D\|
and
\|C\|
are proportional to

suggests
that, at least for two channels with small contrast, dropping these terms
leads to equations

\begin{split}P^{l,r}_{g}\widetilde{\sigma}+P^{l,r}_{g}DP_{g}^{l,r}\widetilde{\tau}&=P^{l,r}_{g}g\\
P^{l,r}_{g}CP^{l,r}_{g}\widetilde{\sigma}+P^{l,r}_{g}\widetilde{\tau}&=P^{l,r}_{g}h,\end{split}

(211)

whose solutions,
(P^{l,r}_{g}\widetilde{\sigma},P_{g}^{l,r}\widetilde{\tau}),
should be very close to

(P^{l,r}_{g}\sigma,P_{g}^{l,r}\tau).

8
Some Concluding Remarks

In the foregoing pages we have constructed outgoing fundamental
solutions for operators of the form
\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2}),
and shown
how to use them to represent the solution to the transmission problem
defined by two semi-infinite wave-guides meeting along a common
perpendicular line. The construction of the “outgoing” fundamental
solution is in a form that should lend itself to numerical
implementation. We have shown that the resultant system of integral
equations is Fredholm of second kind on the spaces

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}),
with

0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2},
and are therefore generically solvable.

We have only presented a detailed analysis of this problem for the case of
potentials given by (
4
), though it is clear that our approach will
apply,
mutatis mutandis
, if
q(x_{2})
is a bounded, measurable function
with bounded support. The principal difference will be that the basic solutions,

\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,0^{+};x_{2}),
of the ODE, and their Wronskian no longer have
explicit formulæ in terms of elementary functions within the support of
q.

These formulæ need to be replaced by (standard) estimates. To implement the
method numerically, the functions
\widetilde{u}_{\pm}
have to be computed numerically
within the support of
q.
The problem of having 2 open wave-guides that are of
the form considered here outside a compact set, is a relatively compact
perturbation which, while requiring further analysis, should not pose serious
additional difficulties.

In Part II we show that under reasonable hypotheses on the data, which are satisfied by
wave-guide modes, points sources and wave-packets, the sources found by solving
the integral equations along
\{x_{1}=0\}
satisfy many additional estimates and
even admit asymptotic expansions, that is

\begin{split}&\sigma(x_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}|x_{2}|}}{|x_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\frac{a^{\pm}_{l}}{|x_{2}|^{l}}+O\left(|x_{2}|^{-N-\frac{3}{2}}\right),\\
&\tau(x_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}|x_{2}|}}{|x_{2}|}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\left[\frac{b^{\pm}_{l}}{|x_{2}|^{l+\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{c^{\pm}_{l}}{|x_{2}|^{l}}\right]+O\left(|x_{2}|^{-N-2}\right),\text{ as }|x_{2}|\to\infty.\end{split}

(212)

In some cases the coefficients
c^{\pm}_{l}=0.

Using these asymptotic expansions we show that the solutions given by
u^{l,r}

also have complete expansions that are uniformly correct as
\eta_{1},\,\eta_{2}\to 0^{\pm}.
The existence of these expansions implies that the solutions satisfy
precisely the sort of outgoing radiation condition that one expects from the
work of Isozaki, Melrose, Vasy et al. The proofs of the asymptotic expansions
use fairly classical techniques, combined with a novel contour deformation
argument. To complete this analysis, and prove the uniqueness of the solutions
found using our method, requires a much more sophisticated, microlocal analysis
of this class of problems, which is given in Part III.

In these appendices we collect a variety of background results, and study the
outgoing properties of the limiting absorption solution in the case of a

Appendix A
The Planar Case

In order to estimate the correction terms
w^{l,r}(x;y)
we need to
have a good description of the limit of the kernels for

(D_{q}+i\delta)^{-1}
as
\delta\to 0^{+},
where

D_{q}=\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2}).

(213)

We are employing the limiting absorption principle limit, which,
more or less by definition,
gives the outgoing solution to

(\Delta+k_{1}^{2}+q)u=f,

(214)

for certain functions
f,
which includes, but is not limited to compactly
supported functions.

In this section we use the Fourier transform in the
x_{1}
-variable and basic ODE theory to construct
the kernels for these operators where we usually take

q(x_{2})=(k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2})\chi_{[-d,d]}(x_{2}).

(215)

It would be more standard to consider the spectral theory and
resolvent of the operator
\Delta+q(x_{2}).
However our analysis
relies on detailed analyticity properties of the kernel of

(\Delta+q+k_{1}^{2}+i\delta)
for
\delta>0,
which is why we consider
the shifted operator. The substance of these results generalizes
easily to piecewise continuous functions,
q(x_{2}),
with support in

[-d,d].

With
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as the domain,
D_{q}
defines an unbounded self
adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).
The spectrum of this operator,

\sigma(D_{q}),
is well known to lie in the interval

(-\infty,k_{2}^{2}].
In this section we present a construction for the resolvent
kernel of this operator, which allows for the construction of the
perturbation terms
w^{l,r},
by taking for
q
either
q_{l}
or

q_{r}.
To that end we need compute the kernel of the limit

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}(D_{q}+i\delta)^{-1},

which we denote by

\mathcal{R}_{0+}(x;y).
This is the operator arising from limiting
absorption principle applied to this case. By a small abuse of
terminology, in the sequel we refer to
\mathcal{R}_{0+}
as the

resolvent kernel
, or outgoing resolvent kernel.

Our construction of the resolvent kernel uses the partial Fourier transform in
the
x_{1}
-variable, which we denote by

\widetilde{u}(\xi,x_{2})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x_{1},x_{2})e^{-i\xi x_{1}}dx_{1}.

(216)

To construct the resolvent kernel we the need
kernels for inverses of the operators

L_{\xi}+i\delta=\partial^{2}_{x_{2}}+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2})-\xi^{2}+i\delta,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R},

with domain
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

These kernels are constructed from the
basic solutions
to

\partial^{2}_{x_{2}}\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})+(k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2})-\xi^{2}+i\delta)\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})=0,

(217)

which satisfy

\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})=e^{\pm ix_{2}\sqrt{k^{2}_{1}-\xi^{2}+i\delta}}\text{
for }\pm x_{2}>d.

(218)

The
\sqrt{z}
is defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0],
to be positive
on
(0,\infty);
for
\delta,\xi\in\mathbb{R},

\operatorname{sgn}\operatorname{Im}\sqrt{k^{2}-\xi^{2}+i\delta}=\operatorname{sgn}\delta,

and therefore, for
\delta>0,
and
\xi^{2}>k_{1}^{2},

\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}+i\delta}=i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}-i\delta}.

(219)

Hence, for
\delta>0,

\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})
decays
exponentially as
x_{2}\to\infty,
whereas
\widetilde{u}_{-}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})

decays exponentially as
x_{2}\to-\infty.

Taking
\delta\to 0^{+}
we get the basic solutions that
satisfy

\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,0^{+};x_{2})=\begin{cases}&e^{\pm ix_{2}\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}\text{ for }|\xi|\leq k_{1},\\
&e^{\mp x_{2}\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}\text{ for }|\xi|>k_{1},\text{ as }\pm x_{2}\to\infty.\end{cases}

(220)

The solutions
\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,0;x_{2})
are outgoing as
x_{2}\to\infty,
and

\widetilde{u}_{-}(\xi,0;x_{2})
are outgoing as
x_{2}\to-\infty.
If
q(-x_{2})=q(x_{2}),
then it
is easy to show that

\widetilde{u}_{-}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})=\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,\delta;-x_{2}).

(221)

Remark 5
.

It should be noted that while the families

\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})
are analytic as functions of
\xi^{2},

their analyticity properties as functions of
\xi
are more
complicated. This is because, with our choice of square-root, the
composition
\xi\mapsto\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}
is analytic and
single-valued in
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,-k_{1}]\cup[k_{1},\infty).

We see that letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
implies that, for
\xi
real
with for
|\xi|>k_{1},

\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}=i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}},

where

\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}\in(0,\infty).
Fortunately our applications
only require analyticity for
\xi
in small neighborhoods of the
intervals
(k_{1},k_{2}),(-k_{2},-k_{1})\subset\mathbb{R}.
Since this avoids the
branch points at
\pm k_{1},
the
\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}
has a single
valued, analytic determination in a neighborhood of these open
intervals.

As solutions to an ODE,
the functions
\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\xi,0^{+};x_{2})
are specified by their
behavior for
\pm x_{2}>d,
and therefore have analytic extensions to

\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\zeta,0^{+};x_{2}),
for
\zeta
in a neighborhood,

\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{C},
of
(k_{1},k_{2})\cup(-k_{2},-k_{1}).
As these
solutions are also determined by their asymptotics, for small enough

\delta,
these extensions satisfy

\widetilde{u}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})=\widetilde{u}(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-i\delta},0^{+};x_{2}).

Note also that

\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(\zeta,0^{+};x_{2})=\widetilde{u}_{\pm}(-\zeta,0^{+};x_{2}),
for
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}.
This does not require
q(x_{2})=q(-x_{2}).

The simple case of the
\Delta+k^{2}
is instructive. Constructing
the outgoing solution to
(\Delta+k^{2})u=f,
via a 1-dimensional
Fourier transform gives the formula

u(x_{1},x_{2})=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k^{2}}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}e^{i\xi x_{1}}\tilde{f}(\xi,y_{2})dy_{2}d\xi}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k^{2}}},

(222)

where
\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k^{2}}=-i\sqrt{k^{2}-\xi^{2}}
if

The
resolvent kernel of
\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}-\xi^{2}+k^{2}
is an even function of
\xi,

and has an analytic extension to a neighborhood of

(-\infty,-k)\cup(k,\infty).
This is
not
the restriction of its
analytic extension to the upper, or lower half plane.

In order to satisfy the equation and boundary conditions at
x_{2}=\pm d,
implied by
\operatorname{Dom}(L_{\xi})=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
it is necessary for
\widetilde{u}_{+}

to have the form

\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})=\begin{cases}&a_{0}e^{ix_{2}\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}+i\delta}}+b_{0}e^{-ix_{2}\sqrt{k^{2}_{2}-\xi^{2}+i\delta}}\text{ for }|x_{2}|<d,\\
&a_{-}e^{ix_{2}\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}+i\delta}}+b_{-}e^{-ix_{2}\sqrt{k^{2}_{1}-\xi^{2}+i\delta}}\text{ for }x_{2}<-d.\end{cases}

(223)

The claim for
|x_{2}|<d
assumes that
q
is given
by (

section. If
\delta=0^{+},
and
|\xi|<k_{1},
then
\widetilde{u}_{+}
oscillates as

|x_{2}|\to\infty,
whereas if

then this solution decays

x_{2}\to\infty,
but typically grows exponentially as

x_{2}\to-\infty.

Using the solutions described in (

construct the inverse for
L_{\xi}+i\delta.
The inverse is given by

R_{\xi,\delta}(\tilde{f})(x_{2})=\frac{1}{W(\xi,\delta)}\Bigg[\int_{-\infty}^{x_{2}}\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})\widetilde{u}_{-}(\xi,\delta;y_{2})\tilde{f}(y_{2})dy_{2}+\\
\int_{x_{2}}^{\infty}\widetilde{u}_{-}(\xi,\delta;x_{2})\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,\delta;y_{2})\tilde{f}(y_{2})dy_{2}\Bigg],

(224)

where

(225)

is the Wronskian, which is independent of
x_{2}.
This operator agrees
with the bounded inverse of
L_{\xi}+i\delta,
where it is defined,
but is also defined as an operator from

even for
\delta=0^{+},|\xi|<k_{1}.
We denote the
operator by

with

R_{\xi,0^{+}}=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}R_{\xi,\delta}.

(226)

The operator
\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2}),
acting on

is self adjoint and its spectrum is
easily shown lie in the interval
(-\infty,k_{2}^{2}].
Thus

is invertible on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
provided
\delta\neq 0,
and also if
\delta=0,
but

|\xi|>k_{2}.
Indeed it is also invertible with
\delta=0,
for all
but finitely
\xi

k_{1}<|\xi|<k_{2}.
Because it is self adjoint we
have the norm estimate for

(L_{\xi}+i\delta)^{-1}=R_{\xi,\delta}

\|R_{\xi,\delta}\|\leq\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(\xi^{2}-i\delta,(-\infty,k_{2}^{2}])}\leq\begin{cases}&\frac{1}{|\delta|}\text{ for }|\xi|<k_{2},\\
&\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2})^{2}+\delta^{2}}}\text{ for }|\xi|\geq k_{2}.\end{cases}

(227)

If

then

-solution to

(229)

Using Plancherel’s formula and (

(230)

If

then the limit of (

is just

as a Fourier transform in the

-variable.

Relation (

(231)

(232)

(233)

Suppose that

(234)

for some non-zero constant

For

(235)

If

As

\delta=0.

If

The

-eigenfunction of

\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}+q(x_{2})

\xi_{0}^{2}.

Definition 2

Theorem 1
.

If

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R},

\{\pm\xi_{n}:\>n=1,\dots,N\},

(260)

all of which satisfy
\delta=0^{+},
and

k_{1}<|\xi_{n}|<k_{2}.

(261)

These are the only solutions to equation (
260
). For

0<k_{1}<k_{2},
and any

Proof.

From the discussion preceding the statement of the theorem in the
previous section, it is clear that we only need to consider the case
that
k_{1}^{2}<\xi^{2}<k_{2}^{2};
we let

A=\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}},\widetilde{B}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k^{2}_{1}}.

(262)

Both
A
and
\widetilde{B}
are positive real numbers. To find

\left(\begin{matrix}e^{iAd}&e^{-iAd}\\
iAe^{iAd}&-iAe^{-iAd}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}a_{0}\\
b_{0}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-\widetilde{B}d}\\

(263)

Solving we see that

\left(\begin{matrix}a_{0}\\

(A-i\widetilde{B})e^{iAd}\end{matrix}\right).

Since
W(\xi,0^{+})=2iA(a_{0}^{2}-b_{0}^{2});

But for the points

\tan 2d\sqrt{k^{2}_{2}-\xi^{2}}=\frac{2\sqrt{(k^{2}_{2}-\xi^{2})(\xi^{2}-k^{2}_{1})}}{k^{2}_{1}+k^{2}_{2}-2\xi^{2}}.

(266)

Since this is an analytic equation (at least on a Riemann surface covering

\mathbb{C}
) the set of solutions is discrete. Indeed, for a given

there is a
finite set of points

\{k_{1}\leq\xi_{j}\leq k_{2}:\>j=1,\dots,N\},

which solve this equation. Clearly
\{-\xi_{j}\}
are also solutions.

The right hand side of (

) goes from

to
+\infty
for

\xi\in[k_{1},\sqrt{(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})/2}),
and from
-\infty
to
0

\xi\in(\sqrt{(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})/2},k_{2}].
The left hand side of (
266
) vanishes
at
\xi=k_{2},
and therefore, no matter how small

is, the
graph of the left hand side crosses that of the right hand side for some value
of
\xi\in(k_{1},k_{2}).

If

(267)

then

are roots of

It is easy to
see that
\xi=\pm k_{2}
are never solutions to

It is possible

\xi=\pm k_{1}
are roots, but only if

2d\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}=n\pi\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(268)

the left and right hand

for
k_{1}<|\xi|<k_{2},
we have the
following estimate

|R_{\xi,0^{+}}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq C\frac{e^{-\widetilde{B}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}}{\sqrt{|\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}|}\prod_{n=1}^{N}|\xi^{2}-\xi_{n}^{2}|},

(270)

with analogous estimates for
x_{2}<y_{2}.
If
|\xi|<k_{1},
then

|R_{\xi,0^{+}}(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{|\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}|}}.

(271)

Here
C
are constants independent of
x_{2},y_{2},\xi,
and

\widetilde{A}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}},\,\widetilde{B}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}.

(272)

Proof.

Since
R_{\xi,0^{+}}(x_{2},y_{2})=\overline{R_{\xi,0^{+}}(y_{2},x_{2})},
it suffices to consider the
case
y_{2}<x_{2}.
Using the calculations above we see that the Wronskian is given
by (
265
):

W(\xi,0^{+})=e^{2id\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}}\Bigg[(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}-2\xi^{2})\frac{\sin 2Ad}{A}+\\
2i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}\cos 2dA\Bigg]\text{ for }|\xi|<k_{1},

(273)

along with
\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}=i\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}
for
|\xi|>k_{1}.
We see
that the Wronskian has square root singularities at
\pm k_{1},
though not at

\pm k_{2}.
For
|\xi|>k_{2}

W(\xi,0^{+})=\frac{(3k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}-4\xi^{2})e^{2d(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})}}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}}+O(e^{-2d(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}+\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}})}).

(274)

Since

\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}=\frac{k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}+\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}},

(275)

we see that there is a constant
C>0
so that

|W(\xi,0^{+})|>C|\xi|\text{ for }|\xi|>k_{2}.

(276)

As we are principally interested in the behavior of these solutions as

|\xi|\to\infty,
we begin by assuming that
|\xi|>k_{2},
and rewrite
the relations defining these coefficients as

\widetilde{u}_{+}(\xi,,0^{+};x_{2})=\begin{cases}&e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{B}}\text{ for }x_{2}>d,\\
&a_{0}e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{A}}+b_{0}e^{x_{2}\widetilde{A}}\text{ for }|x_{2}|<d,\\
&a_{-}e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{B}}+b_{-}e^{x_{2}\widetilde{B}}\text{ for }x_{2}<-d.\end{cases}

(277)

As
|\xi|>k_{2},

\widetilde{A}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}},\,\widetilde{B}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}}.

(278)

are positive real numbers. The jump
relations imply

\left(\begin{matrix}a_{0}\\
b_{0}\end{matrix}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\widetilde{A}}\left(\begin{matrix}(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})e^{(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})d}\\
(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})e^{-(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})d}\end{matrix}\right),

(279)

and

\left(\begin{matrix}a_{-}\\
b_{-}\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{1}{4\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}\left(\begin{matrix}(\widetilde{A}^{2}-\widetilde{B}^{2})e^{-2(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})d}-(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{2(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})d}\\
(\widetilde{A}^{2}-\widetilde{B}^{2})e^{2\widetilde{A}d}-(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{-2\widetilde{A}d}\end{matrix}\right).

(280)

From (
279
)–(
280
) we see that these coefficients
are functions asymptotically homogeneous of degree 0, as

|\xi|\to\infty,
times exponentials, which together with

1/W(\xi,0^{+}),
are bounded by the function on the first line
of (
269
). Below we refer to these sorts of algebraic terms
as “prefactors.”

We need to consider the products

u_{+}(\xi,,0^{+};x_{2})u_{+}(\xi,,0^{+};-y_{2})
for
y_{2}<x_{2}
in various
ranges. To simplify the notation we largely suppress explicit mention of

\xi
in the computations below. We start with 2 observations:

1.

\widetilde{A}<\widetilde{B}.

2.

0<\widetilde{B}-\widetilde{A}=\frac{(k^{2}_{2}-k^{2}_{1})}{\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}}\leq\frac{K}{|\xi|},
for a positive constant
K.

We start with the case
y_{2}<x_{2}<-d,
where

u_{+}(x_{2})u_{+}(-y_{2})=e^{y_{2}\widetilde{B}}(a_{-}e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{B}}+b_{-}e^{x_{2}\widetilde{B}}),

(281)

which gives

\frac{1}{4\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}\Big[e^{-(x_{2}-y_{2})\widetilde{B}}[(\widetilde{A}^{2}-\widetilde{B}^{2})e^{-2(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})d}-(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{2(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})d}]+\\
e^{(x_{2}+y_{2})\widetilde{B}}[(\widetilde{A}^{2}-\widetilde{B}^{2})e^{2\widetilde{A}d}-(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{-2\widetilde{A}d}]\Big].

(282)

Since
x_{2}-y_{2}>0,

x_{2}+y_{2}<-2d,
it follows easily from 1. and
2. that, away from
x_{2}=y_{2},
all terms are bounded by a prefactor
times
e^{-\widetilde{B}(x_{2}-y_{2})}.

We now consider
y_{2}<-d<x_{2}<d:

u_{+}(x_{2})u_{+}(-y_{2})=e^{y_{2}\widetilde{B}}(a_{0}e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{A}}+b_{0}e^{x_{2}\widetilde{A}})=\\
-\frac{1}{2\widetilde{A}}\Big[e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{A}+y_{2}\widetilde{B}}(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})e^{(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})d}+e^{x_{2}\widetilde{A}+y_{2}\widetilde{B}}(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})e^{-(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})d}\Big]

(283)

The first term is exponentially decaying away from
x_{2}=y_{2}=-d,
as
\widetilde{A}<\widetilde{B}

and
d-x_{2}\leq 2d,

y_{2}-d\leq-2d.
The second term is exponentially
decaying as
x_{2}-d\leq 0.
Indeed all terms are bounded by a prefactor
times
e^{-\widetilde{A}(x_{2}-y_{2})}.

Next we consider
-d<y_{2}<x_{2}<d:

\begin{split}u_{+}(x_{2})u_{+}(-y_{2})&=(a_{0}e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{A}}+b_{0}e^{x_{2}\widetilde{A}})(a_{0}e^{y_{2}\widetilde{A}}+b_{0}e^{-y_{2}\widetilde{A}})\\
&=a^{2}_{0}e^{-(x_{2}-y_{2})\widetilde{A}}+a_{0}b_{0}[e^{(x_{2}+y_{2})\widetilde{A}}+e^{-(x_{2}+y_{2})\widetilde{A}}]+b^{2}_{0}e^{(x_{2}-y_{2})\widetilde{A}}.\end{split}

(284)

Inserting the coefficients gives

\frac{1}{4\widetilde{A}^{2}}\Big[(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{2(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})d}e^{-(x_{2}-y_{2})\widetilde{A}}+\\
(\widetilde{A}^{2}-\widetilde{B}^{2})e^{-2\widetilde{B}d}[e^{(x_{2}+y_{2})\widetilde{A}}+e^{-(x_{2}+y_{2})\widetilde{A}}]+(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{-2(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})d}e^{(x_{2}-y_{2})\widetilde{A}}\Big].

(285)

A careful examination shows that this term remains bounded as
\widetilde{A}\to 0.

Fact 2. above and
y_{2}<x_{2}
shows that first term is exponentially
decreasing away from
x_{2}=y_{2}.
As
|x_{2}\pm y_{2}|\leq d
and
\widetilde{A}<\widetilde{B}

the second term is exponentially decaying, as is the third term. All
terms are bounded by a prefactor times
e^{-\widetilde{A}(x_{2}-y_{2})}.

In case
y_{2}<-d<d<x_{2},

u_{+}(x_{2})u_{+}(-y_{2})=e^{-\widetilde{B}(x_{2}-y_{2})},
which obviously satisfies the
desired estimate.

Next we turn to
-d<y_{2}<d<x_{2}
which gives

u_{+}(x_{2})u_{+}(-y_{2})=e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{B}}(a_{0}e^{y_{2}\widetilde{A}}+b_{0}e^{-y_{2}\widetilde{A}})\\
=-\frac{1}{2\widetilde{A}}\Big[e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{B}+y_{2}\widetilde{A}}(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})e^{(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})d}+e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{B}-y_{2}\widetilde{A}}(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})e^{-(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})d}\Big].

(286)

The first term decays exponentially as
\widetilde{A}<\widetilde{B}
and
x_{2}+d>y_{2}+d;
as
does the second term since
y_{2}+d\geq 0.
Indeed all terms are bounded
by a prefactor times
e^{-\widetilde{A}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}.

This leaves the final case

d<y_{2}<x_{2}:

u_{+}(x_{2})u_{+}(-y_{2})=e^{-x_{2}\widetilde{B}}(a_{-}e^{y_{2}\widetilde{B}}+b_{-}e^{-y_{2}\widetilde{B}})\\
=\frac{1}{4\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}\Big[e^{-(x_{2}-y_{2})\widetilde{B}}[(\widetilde{A}^{2}-\widetilde{B}^{2})e^{-2(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})d}-(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{2(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})d}]+\\
e^{-(x_{2}+y_{2})\widetilde{B}}[(\widetilde{A}^{2}-\widetilde{B}^{2})e^{2\widetilde{A}d}-(\widetilde{A}-\widetilde{B})^{2}e^{-2\widetilde{A}d}]\Big]

(287)

The first term is exponentially decaying away from
x_{2}=y_{2};
the
second term is as well because
\widetilde{A}<\widetilde{B}
and
2d\leq x_{2}+y_{2}.
Indeed all terms are bounded
by a prefactor times
e^{-\widetilde{B}|x_{2}-y_{2}|}.

Altogether we have shown that

u_{+}(\xi,,0^{+};x_{2})u_{+}(\xi,,0^{+};-y_{2})\chi(\{y_{2}<x_{2}\})
satisfies
the estimates in (
269
). The estimate for the other term in
the resolvent kernel,

u_{+}(\xi,,0^{+};-x_{2})u_{+}(\xi,,0^{+};y_{2})\chi(\{y_{2}>x_{2}\}),
follows
from the symmetry of the kernel.
Using these formulæ we easily establish the estimate
in (
270
). The estimate for
|\xi|<k_{1}
follows easily from
the fact the eigenfunctions
\widetilde{u}_{\pm}
are bounded and oscillatory in
this range.
∎

Pointwise Properties of Fourier-Jacobi-Forms


Abstract
Jacobi-Forms can be decomposed as a linear combination of Thetafunctions with modular forms as coefficients. It is shown that the space of these coefficient modular forms of Fourier-Jacobi-Forms, which come from Siegel cusp forms, has full rank in every point of the Satake boundary, if the index is 1, the weight is sufficiently large and the Satake boundary point has trivial stabilizer in Γ n − 1 \Gamma_{n-1} . This yields a local automorphic embedding of the Siegel modular variety. Klingen-Poincare series are the main tool. Despite of this richness it is proved that there are more Jacobi index 1 cusp forms than Fourier-Jacobi index 1 cusp forms for all sufficiently large weights extending a result of Dulinski.

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\frac{2}{3}\pi i((3q+1)^{2}+(3q+1))z_{11}+\pi i(2(3q+1)+1)z_{12}}+

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,3}(z_{11})\cdot\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\frac{2}{3}\pi i((3q+2)^{2}+(3q+2))z_{11}+\pi i(2(3q+2)+1)z_{12}}

or written in Theta-functions

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1}(z_{11},z_{12})=\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}\Big(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12}\Big)+

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{3}}\Big(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12}\Big)+

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,3}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{2}{3}}\Big(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12}\Big)

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1}(z_{11},z_{12})=\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{6}}\Big(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12}\Big)\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{6}\pi iz_{11}}+

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}\Big(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12}\Big)\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{6}\pi iz_{11}}+

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,3}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{5}{6}}\Big(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12}\Big)\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{6}\pi iz_{11}}

There are six coefficient-functions in
z_{11}
occuring but due to the relation

\displaystyle\psi_{(a_{1},\frac{1}{2}),1}(z_{11},z_{12}+z_{11})e^{\frac{3}{2}\pi i(z_{11}+2z_{12})}=\psi_{(a_{1}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1}(z_{11},z_{12})

only three can be chosen independently. In fact we have the relations

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})=\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,3}(z_{11})\cdot e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi iz_{11}}

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})=\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi iz_{11}}

\displaystyle\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}),1,3}(z_{11})=\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi iz_{11}}

There are further restrictions coming from the unimodular transformation

\displaystyle U=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right)

which implies together with
F(U^{t}ZU)=F(Z)
the further relations

\displaystyle\psi_{(a_{1},a_{2}),1}(z_{11},-z_{12})=\psi_{(a_{1},a_{2}),1}(z_{11},z_{12})

Here we used that

\displaystyle\Theta_{(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}z_{11}&-z_{12}\\
-z_{12}&z_{22}\end{array}\right),z_{13},-z_{23}\right)=\Theta_{(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}z_{11}&z_{12}\\
z_{12}&z_{22}\end{array}\right),z_{13},z_{23}\right)

We apply the decomposition of
\psi_{(a_{1},a_{2}),1}(z_{11},z_{12})
into 1-dim.
Theta-functions. In case of
a_{2}=0
this does not yield any further restrictions but
in case of
a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
we have

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})=\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})

\displaystyle\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})=\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})

\displaystyle\Theta_{\frac{1}{3}}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})=\Theta_{\frac{2}{3}}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})

\displaystyle\Theta_{\frac{1}{6}}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})=\Theta_{\frac{5}{6}}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})

From uniqueness of Theta-decomposition this implies

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})=\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,3}(z_{11})

So in fact there are only two independent coefficient functions

\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11}),\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})
in case of
a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
.

We develop the two-dimensional Theta-functions as well and obtain

\displaystyle\Theta_{(a_{1},\frac{1}{2})}(\widehat{Z},\widehat{z})=e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi iz_{22}}\cdot\Big(\sum_{m_{1}}e^{2\pi i((m_{1}+a_{1})^{2}z_{11}+(m_{1}+a_{1})z_{12})+4\pi i((m_{1}+a_{1})z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{23})}+

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}}e^{2\pi i((m_{1}+a_{1})^{2}z_{11}-(m_{1}+a_{1})z_{12})+4\pi i((m_{1}+a_{1})z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{23})}\Big)+e^{\frac{9}{2}\pi iz_{22}}\cdot...=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi iz_{22}}\cdot\Big(\Theta_{a_{1}}(z_{11},z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{12})\cdot e^{2\pi iz_{23}}+\Theta_{a_{1}}(z_{11},z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{12})\cdot e^{-2\pi iz_{23}}\Big)+e^{\frac{9}{2}\pi iz_{22}}\cdot...

Putting all expansions together we obtain an expansion for the Fourier-Jacobi cusp form
of index 1

\displaystyle\sum_{a=(a_{1},a_{2}),a_{j}\in\{0,\frac{1}{2}\}}\phi_{a}(\widehat{Z})\cdot\Theta_{a}(\widehat{Z},\widehat{z})=e^{2\pi iz_{22}}\cdot\Delta(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13},z_{23})+e^{4\pi iz_{22}}\cdot...

where

\displaystyle\Delta(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13},z_{23})=

\displaystyle=\Big(\psi_{(0,0),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{12})+\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12})\Big)\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13})+

\displaystyle\Big(\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{12})+\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12})\Big)\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13})+

\displaystyle\Big(\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot

\displaystyle\Big(\Theta_{\frac{1}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+\Theta_{\frac{2}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\Big)\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle\Big(\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{12})\cdot e^{2\pi iz_{23}}+\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{12})\cdot e^{-2\pi iz_{23}}\Big)+

\displaystyle\Big(\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot

\displaystyle\Big(\Theta_{\frac{1}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+\Theta_{\frac{5}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\Big)\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle\Big(\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{12})\cdot e^{2\pi iz_{23}}+\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{12})\cdot e^{-2\pi iz_{23}}\Big)

Now the interesting point is that this expansion also holds true more generally for all
Jacobi cusp forms of scalar index 1 on
\mathbb{H}_{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
But in case of a Fourier-Jacobi cusp form so stemming from a Siegel cusp form
F(Z)
on

\mathbb{H}_{3}
we have an additional symmetry because of the unimodular identity

\displaystyle F\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}z_{11}&z_{12}&z_{13}\\
z_{12}&z_{22}&z_{23}\\
z_{13}&z_{23}&z_{33}\end{array}\right)=F\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}z_{11}&z_{13}&z_{12}\\
z_{13}&z_{33}&z_{23}\\
z_{12}&z_{23}&z_{22}\end{array}\right)

This additional symmetry implies

\displaystyle\Delta(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13},z_{23})=\Delta(z_{11},z_{13},z_{12},z_{23})

As the torus variable
z_{23}
may vary free this
translates into the following extra conditions on the coefficient
function
\psi_{...}(z_{11})
:

\displaystyle\big(\psi_{(0,0),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{12})+\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12})\big)\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13})+

\displaystyle\big(\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{12})+\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12})\big)\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13})=

\displaystyle=\big(\psi_{(0,0),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13})+\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13})\big)\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{12})+

\displaystyle\big(\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13})+\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13})\big)\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12})

and for the
a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
part

\displaystyle\Big(\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{2}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\Big)\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}\pm\frac{1}{2}z_{12})+

\displaystyle\Big(\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{5}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\Big)\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13}\pm\frac{1}{2}z_{12})=

\displaystyle=\Big(\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{13})+\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{13})+

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{2}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{13})\Big)\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{12}\pm\frac{1}{2}z_{13})+

\displaystyle\Big(\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{13})+\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,3}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{13})+

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{5}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{13})\Big)\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12}\pm\frac{1}{2}z_{13})

The first equation yields

\displaystyle(\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(z_{11})-\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(z_{11}))\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13})=

\displaystyle(\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(z_{11})-\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(z_{11}))\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{12})

As the torus parameter
z_{12},z_{13}
may vary free we conclude from
linear independence of the theta-functions

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(z_{11})=\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(z_{11})

In the appendix we prove that

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,\pm}(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13})=\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}\pm\frac{1}{2}z_{12})+\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13}\pm\frac{1}{2}z_{12})

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{2,\pm}(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13})=\Big(\Theta_{\frac{1}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})+\Theta_{\frac{1}{3}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\Big)\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}\pm\frac{1}{2}z_{12})+

are symmetric in

does not yield any
further restrictions on
\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,1}(z_{11}),\psi_{(0,\frac{1}{2}),1,2}(z_{11})
.

Now we construct a Jacobi cusp form of index 1 and arbitrary high weight
r
for
which the symmetry relation
\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(z_{11})=\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(z_{11})

is not fulfilled. Let
J_{2,1}\subset\Gamma_{3}
be the Jacobi-subgroup. Let

\displaystyle T=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\frac{1}{2}&0\\
\frac{1}{2}&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{r}(T,Z)=\sum_{[M]\in J_{2,1}\backslash\{Transl.\}}\det(CZ+D)^{-r}\cdot e^{2\pi i\sigma(TM(Z))}

\displaystyle=\widehat{F}_{r}(T,\widehat{Z},\widehat{z})\cdot e^{2\pi iz_{33}}

with a Jacobi-cusp form
\widehat{F}_{r}(T,\widehat{Z},\widehat{z})
of weight
r

and scalar index 1. We fix a Minkowski reduced
Y>0
with
1<<Y_{11}<Y_{22}<Y_{33}
and

Y_{11}
so large that for all
C\neq(0)
we have

|\det(CZ+D)|>2
. Now we have

\displaystyle\phi_{(a_{1},0)}(\widehat{F}_{r})(\widehat{Z})=\frac{1}{\Theta_{(a_{1},0)}(2\widehat{Y},2\widehat{y})}\cdot e^{2\pi y_{33}}\cdot

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{F}_{r}(T,Z)\cdot\overline{\Theta_{(a_{1},0)}(\widehat{Z},\widehat{z})}\cdot e^{-2\pi ix_{33}}dx_{13}dx_{23}dx_{33}

and employing this expression we get

\displaystyle\psi_{(a_{1},0),1,1}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})=\frac{1}{\Theta_{0}(2Y_{11},2Y_{12})}\cdot e^{2\pi y_{22}}\cdot

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{(a_{1},0)}(\widehat{F}_{r})(\widehat{Z})\cdot\overline{\Theta_{0}(Z_{11},Z_{12})}\cdot e^{-2\pi ix_{22}}dx_{12}dx_{22}

and a similar expression for
\psi_{(a_{1},0),1,2}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})
. From this we see
that all terms of
\widetilde{F}_{r}(T,Z)
with
C\neq(0)
will have a vanishing contribution
to
\psi_{(a_{1},0),1,1}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})
,
\psi_{(a_{1},0),1,2}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})

for
r\longrightarrow\infty
. So for disproving the equality

\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})=\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})

it is sufficient to restrict to the unimodular subseries of
\widetilde{F}_{r}(T,Z)
given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(T,Z)=\sum_{U}e^{2\pi i\sigma(UTU^{t}Z)}

where

\displaystyle U=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}U_{11}&U_{12}&m_{1}\\
U_{21}&U_{22}&m_{2}\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right)\mbox{ and }\widehat{U}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}U_{11}&U_{12}\\
U_{21}&U_{22}\end{array}\right)\in GL(2,\mathbb{Z})

and
m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
vary free. So from

\displaystyle\sigma(UTU^{t}Z)=\sigma(\widehat{U}\widehat{T}\widehat{U}^{t}\widehat{Z})+m^{t}\widehat{Z}m+2m^{t}\widehat{z}+z_{33}

we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(T,Z)=\left(\sum_{\widehat{U}\in GL(2,\mathbb{Z})}e^{2\pi i\sigma(\widehat{U}\widehat{T}\widehat{U}^{t}\widehat{Z})}\right)\cdot e^{2\pi iz_{33}}\cdot\Theta_{(0,0)}(\widehat{Z},\widehat{z})

Furthermore a direct calculation shows

\displaystyle\sum_{\widehat{U}\in GL(2,\mathbb{Z})}e^{2\pi i\sigma(\widehat{U}\widehat{T}\widehat{U}^{t}\widehat{Z})}=12\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi iz_{11}+2\pi iz_{22}}\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{12})+e^{4\pi iz_{22}}\cdot...

and so

\displaystyle\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(\widetilde{G})(z_{11})=12\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi iz_{11}}\mbox{ and }\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(\widetilde{G})(z_{11})=0

This proves

\psi_{(0,0),1,2}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})\neq\psi_{(\frac{1}{2},0),1,1}(\widehat{F}_{r})(z_{11})

for all large
r
.

Theorem 2

For all large weights
r
there are more Jacobi cusp forms of scalar index 1 than Fourier-Jacobi cusp forms of index 1.

Appendix

First we notice that from symmetry of
\Delta_{1,+}

we immediately also obtain the corresponding symmetry for
\Delta_{1,-}
as

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,+}(z_{11},-z_{12},z_{13})=\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{12})+

\displaystyle\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},-\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{12})=

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{12})+

\displaystyle\Theta_{\frac{3}{6}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13}-\frac{1}{2}z_{12})=\Delta_{1,-}(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13})

and so

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,-}(z_{11},z_{13},z_{12})=\Delta_{1,+}(z_{11},-z_{13},z_{12})=\Delta_{1,+}(z_{11},z_{12},-z_{13})=

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,+}(z_{11},-z_{12},z_{13})=\Delta_{1,-}(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13})

A similar calculation holds for
\Delta_{2,-}
. For
b=0,\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}
we set
by abuse of notation

\displaystyle\Delta_{b}(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13})=\Theta_{b}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{12})+\Theta_{b+\frac{1}{2}}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})\cdot\Theta_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{11},z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{12})

We claim

\displaystyle(z_{12},z_{13})\longmapsto\Delta_{b}(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13})

is a symmetric function.

We have

\displaystyle\Theta_{b}(3z_{11},\frac{3}{2}z_{12})=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi i(q+b)^{2}\cdot 3z_{11}+2\pi i(q+b)\cdot 3z_{12}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{1}e^{6\pi i(2q+j+b)^{2}z_{11}+6\pi i(2q+j+b)z_{12}}

and

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}(z_{11},z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{12})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi im^{2}z_{11}+4\pi im(z_{13}+\frac{1}{2}z_{12})}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k=0}^{5}e^{2\pi i(6m+k)^{2}z_{11}+2\pi i(6m+k)(2z_{13}+z_{12})}

So multiplying we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{b}(z_{11},z_{12},z_{13})=\sum_{q,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{5}e^{2\pi i(3(2q+j+b)^{2}+(6m+k)^{2})z_{11}+2\pi i(6q+3j+3b+6m+k)z_{12}+2\pi i(12m+2k)z_{13}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{q,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{5}e^{2\pi i(3(2q+j+b+\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(6m+k+\frac{1}{2})^{2})z_{11}+2\pi i(6q+3j+3b+\frac{3}{2}+6m+k+\frac{1}{2})z_{12}+2\pi i(12m+2k+1)z_{13}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{5}e^{2\pi i(3(2q-2m+j+b)^{2}+(6m+k)^{2})z_{11}+2\pi i(6q+3j+3b+k)z_{12}+2\pi i(12m+2k)z_{13}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{q,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{5}e^{2\pi i(3(2q-2m+j+b+\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(6m+k+\frac{1}{2})^{2})z_{11}+2\pi i(6q+3j+3b+2+k)z_{12}+2\pi i(12m+2k+1)z_{13}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j,l=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{5}e^{2\pi i(3(4q-2m+2l+j+b)^{2}+(6m+k)^{2})z_{11}+2\pi i(12q+6l+3j+3b+k)z_{12}+2\pi i(12m+2k)z_{13}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{q,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j,l=0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{5}e^{2\pi i(3(4q-2m+2l+j+b+\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(6m+k+\frac{1}{2})^{2})z_{11}+2\pi i(12q+6l+3j+3b+k+2)z_{12}+2\pi i(12m+2k+1)z_{13}}

For such an expression to be symmetric in
z_{12},z_{13}
it is necessary that for every
choice of
j,l,k
there is a corresponding tripel
\widetilde{j},\widetilde{l},\widetilde{k}

with either

\displaystyle 6l+3j+3b+k\equiv 2\widetilde{k}\mbox{ mod }12

\displaystyle 6\widetilde{l}+3\widetilde{j}+3b+\widetilde{k}\equiv 2k\mbox{ mod }12

or

\displaystyle 6l+3j+3b+k\equiv 2\widetilde{k}+1\mbox{ mod }12

\displaystyle 6\widetilde{l}+3\widetilde{j}+3b+\widetilde{k}+2\equiv 2k\mbox{ mod }12

So let
j,l,k
be fixed and assume first that

6l+3j+3b+k=2\widetilde{k}
is even. Then we conclude

\displaystyle k+3b-2\widetilde{k}=-6l-3j\equiv 0\mbox{ mod }3

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 4k-6b-2\widetilde{k}\equiv 0\mbox{ mod }3

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 2k-3b-\widetilde{k}\equiv 0\mbox{ mod }3

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\exists\widetilde{j},\widetilde{l}\mbox{ with }6\widetilde{l}+3\widetilde{j}+3b+\widetilde{k}\equiv 2k\mbox{ mod }12

In case

6l+3j+3b+k=2\widetilde{k}+1
is odd we conclude

\displaystyle k+3b-2\widetilde{k}-1=-6l-3j\equiv 0\mbox{ mod }3

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 4k-6b-2\widetilde{k}-4\equiv 0\mbox{ mod }3

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 2k-3b-\widetilde{k}-2\equiv 0\mbox{ mod }3

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\exists\widetilde{j},\widetilde{l}\mbox{ with }6\widetilde{l}+3\widetilde{j}+3b+\widetilde{k}+2\equiv 2k\mbox{ mod }12

So for every pair of Fourier-coefficients

(12q+6l+3j+3b+k,12m+2k)
or
(12q+6l+3j+3b+2+k,12m+2k+1)

of
z_{12},z_{13}
there is a
corresponding pair
(12\widetilde{m}+2\widetilde{k},12\widetilde{q}+6\widetilde{l}+3\widetilde{j}+3b+\widetilde{k})
or

(12\widetilde{m}+2\widetilde{k}+1,12\widetilde{q}+6\widetilde{l}+3\widetilde{j}+3b+\widetilde{k}+2)

with the same coefficients but reversed roles of
z_{12},z_{13}
.

In the next step we have to prove that for such corresponding pairs the
coefficients of
z_{11}
are identical. So assume first we have

\displaystyle 6l+3j+3b+k=2\widetilde{k}\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle 6\widetilde{l}+3\widetilde{j}+3b+\widetilde{k}=2k

We calculate the
z_{11}
-coefficient

\displaystyle 3(4q-2m+2l+j+b)^{2}+(6m+k)^{2}=48q^{2}-48qm+48m^{2}+

\displaystyle 12(4l+2j+2b)q+12(k-(2l+j+b))m+3(2l+j+b)^{2}+k^{2}

So we have to show that

\displaystyle 4l+2j+2b=\widetilde{k}-(2\widetilde{l}+\widetilde{j}+b)

\displaystyle 4\widetilde{l}+2\widetilde{j}+2b=k-(2l+j+b)

\displaystyle 3(2l+j+b)^{2}+k^{2}=3(2\widetilde{l}+\widetilde{j}+b)^{2}+\widetilde{k}^{2}

But from our two relations above we infer

\displaystyle 2l+j+b=\frac{2}{3}\widetilde{k}-\frac{1}{3}k

\displaystyle 2\widetilde{l}+\widetilde{j}+b=\frac{2}{3}k-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{k}

Plugging these terms in we directly obtain equality of
z_{11}
-coefficients. The calculation
for the remaining cases is similar.

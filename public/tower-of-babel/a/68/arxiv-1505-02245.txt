Abstract
. We study the contraction properties (up to shift) for admissible Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuities of n × n n\times n systems of conservation laws endowed with a convex entropy. We first generalize the criterion developed in [ 47 ] , using the spatially inhomogeneous pseudo-distance introduced in [ 50 ] . Our generalized criterion guarantees the contraction property for extremal shocks of a large class of systems, including the Euler system. Moreover, we introduce necessary conditions for contraction, specifically targeted for intermediate shocks. As an application, we show that intermediate shocks of the two-dimensional isentropic magnetohydrodynamics do not verify any of our contraction properties. We also investigate the contraction properties, for contact discontinuities of the Euler system, for a certain range of contraction weights. All results do not involve any smallness condition on the initial perturbation, nor on the size of the shock.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we develop criteria for the existence of contraction properties of admissible Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuities (typically entropic shocks and contact discontinuities) of a wide class of systems of
n
conservation laws endowed with a convex entropy. Consider a
n\times n
system of conservation laws

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}f(u)=0,\quad t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R},\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{aligned}

endowed with a strictly convex entropy
\eta
.

For the scalar case (
n=1
), Kru
\check{\mbox{z}}
kov’s theory
[
27
]
shows that the semi-group associated with (
1.1
) is contractive for the
L^{1}
norm. For the system case, under small BV perturbation, Bressan, Liu and Yang in
[
11
,
36
]
constructed a
L^{1}
semi-group of solutions. However, the
L^{1}
-contraction property does not hold, generally, for systems (See Temple
[
48
]
).

The Kru
\check{\mbox{z}}
kov’s semi-group is not contractive in
L^{p}
for
p>1
, unless the flux is linear. However, Leger showed in
[
31
]
, that any perturbation of an entropic shock wave is contractive in
L^{2}
, up to shift. More precisely, for a strictly convex flux function
f
, and an associated entropic shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma)
(i.e.,
u_{l}>u_{r}
), and for any bounded entropy solution
u
of this scalar conservation law, there exists a Lipschitz shift
t\mapsto h(t)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x+h(t))-S(t,x)|^{2}dx

is not increasing in time, where
S(t,x)
is the traveling wave associated to
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma)

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{l}&\mbox{if $x<\sigma t$},\\
u_{r}&\mbox{if $x>\sigma t$}.\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

The shift
h(t)
depends on the solution
u
. This contraction property can be extended from
L^{2}
, to any relative entropy
\eta(u|S)
associated to a convex entropy
\eta
(see next section). Some extension to
L^{p}
, for
1<p<\infty
, can be found in Adimurthi, Goshal and Veerappa Gowda
[
1
]
.

In the case of systems (
n>1
), this kind of contraction property has been studied in
[
47
,
50
]
. In
[
47
]
the authors developed, in the case of systems, a criterion for contraction of admissible (Rankine-Hugoniot) discontinuities. Their criterion is satisfied, for instance, by the Keyfitz-Kranzer system with a rotationally symmetric flux. However, it is not applicable to many cases, including the Euler system. In
[
50
]
, the notion of contraction was extended to a family of non-homogenous pseudo-norms, defined for a fixed
a>0
, as

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d(u(t,x),S(t,x))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{l})&\mbox{if $x<\sigma t$},\\
a\eta(u(t,x)|u_{r})&\mbox{if $x>\sigma t$},\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

where
S
is the traveling wave associated to the studied entropic shock. Notice that the case of
a=1
corresponds to the case studied in
[
47
]
. In
[
50
]
, it is shown, that any extremal shock (i.e.
1
-shock or
n
-shock) verifies a contraction property, up to a shift, for such pseudo-norms with suitable weights
a>0
.
This pseudo-distance (
1.3
) (determined by the weight
a
) does not depend on on the solution
u
. It depends only on the system and the traveling wave
S
.

The purpose of this article is to generalize the criterion developed in
[
47
]
to the spatially inhomogeneous pseudo-distance introduced in
[
50
]
. We first apply our generalized criterion to the case of extremal shocks. Then, we develop criteria specific for intermediate shocks, and intermediate contact discontinuities.
We present two applications of those criteria.

First, we show that intermediate shocks of the two-dimensional isentropic MHD (which is a
4\times 4
system), do not verify the contraction property, for any weight
a>0
. For inviscid and viscous stability issues for the MHD, we refer to
[
5
,
6
,
24
,
39
]
.

For the contact discontinuities of the Euler system, it is shown in
[
46
]
, that the contraction property holds for the specific value
a=\theta_{r}/\theta_{l}
, the ratio of temperatures on the right, and on the left of the contact discontinuity. We show that this cannot hold for a large range of other weights
a
.

Our criteria depend only on the structure of the system (
1.1
), and the fixed admissible discontinuity. Contrary to the analysis in
[
50
]
, it does not involve the study of every solutions
u
, nor the construction of the shift. This simplifies a lot its applicability. The main difficulty of the analysis of
[
50
]
, for the contraction, is due to the construction of the suitable shift and weights
a
.

The theory of contraction, based on the relative entropy, is valid for large perturbation, without smallness conditions. We consider any bounded entropy weak solutions
u
to (
1.1
) verifying a
BV_{loc}
property. The
BV_{loc}
property is stronger than the strong trace property used in
[
32
,
50
]
. All our results still hold under the assumption of the strong trace property instead of
BV_{loc}
. However, following
[
47
]
, we restrict ourselves to the
BV_{loc}
case to simplify the exposition. Note that the existence theory of entropy weak solutions to the system (
1.1
) (when
n\geq 3
) for large data is open. Strong trace property in the case of scalar conservation laws have been widely studied
[
15
,
20
,
28
,
29
,
41
,
40
,
52
]
. However, in the case of systems, the validity of the strong trace property is mainly an open problem. This has been shown only for the particular case of isentropic gas dynamics with
\gamma=3
, for traces in time, in
[
51
]
.

Our analysis is based on the relative entropy method. It has been first used by Dafermos
[
19
]
and DiPerna
[
21
]
to show the weak-strong uniqueness and stability of Lipschitz regular solution to conservation laws. (See also
[
17
,
18
]
)
We refer to
[
16
,
25
,
31
,
32
,
43
,
46
]
for applications of the relative entropy method to the stability of large perturbation in various contexts. This method is also an important tool in the study of asymptotic limits to conservation laws. Applications of the relative entropy method in this context began with the work of Yau
[
55
]
and have been studied in various context (See for instance
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
23
,
26
,
34
,
37
,
38
,
42
]
).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
, we present the criteria for the contraction of admissible discontinuities, and then identify the necessary conditions for the contraction. As an application of our criteria, in Section
4
, we prove the contraction property of extremal shocks. In Section
5
, using the necessary condition developed in section
3
, we construct two kinds of criteria preventing the contraction property for intermediate entropic shocks, and then present an application to MHD. It turns out that one of the criteria still hold true for intermediate contact discontinuities. In Section
6
, as an application of this criterion to gas dynamics, we find a range of weights, for which there is no contraction for 2-contact discontinuities of the full Euler system.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present our framework, and basic concepts and properties, needed for the analysis in the following sections.

2.1.
General framework

We consider a
n\times n
system of conservation laws:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}f(u)=0,\quad t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R},\end{aligned}

which is endowed with a strictly convex entropy
\eta
, thus the system is hyperbolic on the state space where
\eta
exists. Here, the flux
f
, the entropy
\eta
and associated entropy flux
q
are assumed to be all defined on an open convex state space
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{V})
, and the following compatibility relation holds on
\mathcal{V}
:

\partial_{j}q=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\eta\partial_{j}f_{i},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

which is conventionally rewritten as

\nabla q=\nabla\eta\nabla f,

where the matrix
\nabla f
denotes
(\partial_{j}f_{i})_{i,j}
.

As already mentioned, we have in mind the application of our criteria to the gas dynamics.
For this reason, we need to extend the phase space
\mathcal{V}
to a suitable subset of the boundary of
\mathcal{V}
, to handle the points corresponding to vacuum states. Thus, we introduce as in
[
53
]
:

\mathcal{U}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~|~\exists u_{k}\in\mathcal{V},\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}=u,\quad\limsup_{k\to\infty}\eta(u_{k})<\infty\},

and extend the entropy functional
\eta
on
\mathcal{U}
by

\eta(\bar{u})=\liminf_{u\in\mathcal{V},~u\to\bar{u}}\eta(u).

In
3\times 3
full Euler system,
\mathcal{V}=(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
denotes a set of non-vacuum states of density, momentum and energy, while
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}\cup\{(0,0,0)\}
includes the vacuum state
(0,0,0)
. In general case,
\mathcal{U}
is still convex and
\eta
is convex on
\mathcal{U}
(See
[
53
]
). We here restrict our study to bounded entropy solutions
u
to (
2.4
), whose values are in a convex bounded subset
\mathcal{U}_{K}\subset\mathcal{U}
, on which the functions
f
,
\eta
and
q
are continuous.

2.2.
Relative entropy

For the strictly convex entropy
\eta
of
(
2.4
)
, we define the relative entropy function by

\eta(u|v)=\eta(u)-\eta(v)-\nabla\eta(v)\cdot(u-v),

for any
u\in\mathcal{U},v\in\mathcal{V}
.

Since
\eta
is convex on
\mathcal{U}
and strictly convex in
\mathcal{V}
, we have (see
[
53
]
)

\eta(u|v)\geq 0,\quad u\in\mathcal{U},v\in\mathcal{V},

and

\eta(u|v)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u=v.

Thus, the relative entropy
\eta(u|v)
is positive-definite and convex in the first variable
u
. However it looses the symmetry unless
\eta(u)=|u|^{2}
. Nevertheless the relative entropy is comparable to the square of
L^{2}
distance on any bounded subset of
\mathcal{U}
as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any bounded set
B\subset\mathcal{U}
and compact set
\Omega\subset\mathcal{V}
, there exists
C_{1},C_{2}>0
depending on
B
and
\Omega
such that for any
u\in B
with
v\in\Omega
,

C_{1}|u-v|^{2}\leq\eta(u|v)\leq C_{2}|u-v|^{2}.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
[
32
,
53
]
. Notice that this lemma also holds for all
(u,v)\in\Omega^{2}
, for any compact set
\Omega\in\mathcal{V}
.

As mentioned in Introduction, we are interested in studying the contraction property of a bounded entropy weak solution as any perturbation of admissible Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuity.

We say that
u
is an entropy (weak) solution of (
1.1
) if a weak solution
u
to (
1.1
) satisfies the entropy inequality

(2.5)

\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\partial_{x}q(u)\leq 0,

in the sense of distributions. On the other hand, the equality above holds when
u
is a Lipschitz solution to (
1.1
).

For given
u_{l}\neq u_{r}
, we say that
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma)
is an admissible Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuity if there exists
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &f(u_{r})-f(u_{l})=\sigma(u_{r}-u_{l})\\
&q(u_{r})-q(u_{l})\leq\sigma(\eta(u_{r})-\eta(u_{l})).\end{aligned}

Equivalently, this means that the discontinuous function
u(t,x)
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{l}&\mbox{if $x<\sigma t$},\\
u_{r}&\mbox{if $x>\sigma t$},\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

is an entropy weak solution to (
1.1
).

For any constant vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
u
is an entropic weak solution of (
1.1
), then
\eta(u|v)
is a solution in the sense of distributions to

(2.7)

\partial_{t}\eta(u|v)+\partial_{x}q(u,v)\leq 0,

where
q(u,v)
is the relative entropy flux defined by

q(u,v)=q(u)-q(v)-\nabla\eta(v)\cdot(f(u)-f(v)).

This can be derived directly from (
1.1
) and (
2.5
).

2.3.
Spatially inhomogeneous pseudo-distance

For a given weight
a>0
, using the relative entropy, we consider the pseudo-distance
d
by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d(u(t,x),S(t,x))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{l})&\mbox{if $x<\sigma t$},\\
a\eta(u(t,x)|u_{r})&\mbox{if $x>\sigma t$},\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

where
S(t,x)
denotes the fixed shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma)
. This pseudo-distance is spatially inhomogeneous for
a\neq 1
.
Based on this pseudo-distance, it has been shown in
[
50
]
that there exists suitable weight
a>0
such that
contraction of extremal shocks holds up to Lipschitz shift
\alpha(t)
in the spatially inhomogeneous pseudo-distance:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d(u(t,x+\alpha(t)),S(t,x))dx,

which is equal to

\int_{-\infty}^{\alpha(t)+\sigma t}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{l})dx+a\int_{\alpha(t)+\sigma t}^{\infty}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{r})dx.

From now on, we study the contraction properties by using this pseudo-distance denoted by (
2.8
) as

(2.8)

E_{a}(u(t),h(t)):=\int_{-\infty}^{h(t)}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{l})dx+a\int_{h(t)}^{\infty}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{r})dx.

This pseudo-distance (
2.8
) (determined by the weight
a
) does not depend on
\mathcal{U}_{K}
. That is, it does not depend on any quantitative property of bounded entropy weak solution perturbed from admissible discontinuities. On the other hand, the shift
h(t)
depends on the perturbation and is estimated by

|h^{\prime}(t)|\leq C_{K},\quad|h(t)-\sigma t|\leq C_{K}\sqrt{t}\|u_{0}-S\|_{L^{2}},

where
C_{K}
is a constant depending on
\mathcal{U}_{K}
.

3.
Entropy criteria for contractions

In this section, we present a general theory for contraction of admissible discontinuity for any characteristic fields. First of all, we generalize the criteria developed in
[
47
]
via the spatially inhomogeneous pseudo-distance (
2.8
). We then give the necessary condition for the contraction property in Theorem
3.2
. Following the heuristic observation of Serre and Vasseur in
[
47
]
, we consider the generalized conditions for contraction as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

For a positive constant
a>0
, we say that an entropic Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuity
(u_{l},u_{r})
is relative entropy stable with respect to weight a (in short, a-RES) if
(u_{l},u_{r})
satisfies the following entropy conditions:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bullet(\mathcal{H}1):~\mbox{For any}~u~\mbox{in}~\Sigma_{a}:=\{u~|~\eta(u|u_{l})=a\eta(u|u_{r})\},\\
&\hskip 42.67912ptD_{sm}(u_{l,r};u):=aq(u,u_{r})-q(u,u_{l})\leq 0.\\
&\bullet(\mathcal{H}2):~\mbox{For any entropic discontinuity}~(u_{-},u_{+})~\mbox{of speed}~\sigma_{\pm}~\mbox{satisfying}\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})<a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})~\mbox{and}~\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})>a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r}),\\
&\hskip 42.67912ptD_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm}):=aq(u_{+},u_{r})-q(u_{-},u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm}(a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{-}|u_{l}))\leq 0.\end{aligned}

As a variant of a-RES, we say that an entropic Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuity
(u_{l},u_{r})
is strongly relative entropy stable with respect to weight a (in short, a-SRES) if
(u_{l},u_{r})
satisfies
(\mathcal{H}1)
and a slightly stronger condition
(\mathcal{H}2^{*})
than
(\mathcal{H}2)
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bullet(\mathcal{H}2^{*}):~\mbox{For any entropic discontinuity}~(u_{-},u_{+})~\mbox{of speed}~\sigma_{\pm}~\mbox{such that}\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})~\mbox{and}~a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})~\mbox{have the same sign},\\
&\hskip 42.67912ptD_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})\leq 0.\end{aligned}

Remark 3.1
.

1. For the unit weight
a=1
, the meaning of 1-SRES is the exactly same as one of the terminology ‘RES’ used in
[
47
]
. Thus, the above definition of a-SRES is a generalization of ‘RES’.

2. The condition that
\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})
and
a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})
have the same sign has the same meaning as that
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
are separated by the
(n-1)
-dimensional surface
\Sigma_{a}
. Note that the convexity and
C^{2}
-regularity of
\eta
implies the convexity and
C^{2}
-regularity of

R:=\{u~|~\eta(u|u_{l})\leq a\eta(u|u_{r})\},

thus
C^{2}
-regularity of surface
\Sigma_{a}=\partial R
.

Definition 3.2
.

For a positive constant
a>0
, we say that an entropic Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuity
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma)
satisfies
a
-contraction if for any bounded convex subset
\mathcal{U}_{K}
of
\mathcal{U}
, there exists a constance
C_{K}
(depending on
\mathcal{U}_{K}
) such that the following holds true. For any entropy weak solution
u\in\mathcal{U}_{K}\cap BV_{loc}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})^{n}
(with possibly
T=\infty
) of the system (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}
satisfying
E(u_{0},0)<\infty
, there exists a Lipschitz function
h(t)
with
h(0)=0
such that the pseudo distance
E_{a}(u(t),h(t))
is non increasing in time, i.e.,

(3.9)

E_{a}(u(t),h(t))\leq E_{a}(u(s),h(s)),\quad\mbox{a.e.}~t>s>0,

moreover, for every
0<t<T
,

(3.10)

|h^{\prime}(t)|\leq C_{K},\quad|h(t)-\sigma t|\leq C_{K}\sqrt{t}\|u_{0}-S\|_{L^{2}},

where
S(x)=u_{l}
for
x<0
and
S(x)=u_{r}
for
x>0
.

Remark 3.2
.

As already mentioned, the weight
a>0
defines the pseudo distance
E_{a}(u(t),h(t))
for the contraction, which does not depend on any quantitative property of the perturbation, i.e.,
\mathcal{U}_{K}
, but only depends on the system (
1.1
) and the fixed discontinuity
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma)
. On the other hand, the boundedness of the set
\mathcal{U}_{K}
is for the control of Lipschitz shift
h(t)
such as (
3.10
). Indeed by Lemma
2.1
,
E_{a}(u(t),h(t))
is equivalent to
\|u(t,\cdot+h(t))-S\|_{L^{2}}
, thus the contraction property (
3.9
) implies

\|u(t,\cdot+h(t))-S\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{K}\|u_{0}-S\|_{L^{2}}.

This induces the estimate (
3.10
). Its proof can be found in
[
50
]
. Therefore, we will not mention about (
3.10
) in the sequel.

3.1.
The
a-SRES
implies the
a
-contraction

We here show that for a given weight
a>0
, the
a-SRES
is a sufficient condition for the
a
-contraction. This is a generalization of the main theorem (corresponding to
a=1
) in
[
47
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
a>0
, if the entropic discontinuity
(u_{l},u_{r})
is a-SRES, then
(u_{l},u_{r})
satisfies
a
-contraction.

Proof.

The proof is almost same as that of the main theorem in
[
47
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we give a variant of the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
47
]
. For given
\varepsilon>0
, we define a function
V_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} V_{\varepsilon}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{[aq(u|u_{r})-q(u|u_{l})-\varepsilon]_{+}}{a\eta(u|u_{r})-\eta(u|u_{l})}&\mbox{if $u\notin\Sigma_{a}$},\\
0&\mbox{if $u\in\Sigma_{a}$},\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

where
\Sigma_{a}=\{u~|~\eta(u|u_{l})=a\eta(u|u_{r})\}
.

Since
(u_{l},u_{r})
is
a-SRES
,
V_{\varepsilon}
is Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, that is true by the continuity of
q
and the definition of
a-SRES
. More precisely, since
aq(u|u_{r})-q(u|u_{l})-\varepsilon\leq-\varepsilon
for
u\in\Sigma_{a}
,
V_{\varepsilon}=0
on a neighborhood of
\Sigma_{a}
.

We now consider an entropic weak solution
u\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})^{n}\cap BV_{loc}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})^{n}
with
E(u_{0},0)<\infty
. Then we define an approximated curve
h_{\varepsilon}
as a solution to the ODE

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)=V_{\varepsilon}(u(t,h_{\varepsilon}(t))),\\
&h_{\varepsilon}(0)=0,\end{aligned}

in the Filippov sense
[
22
]
.

For solvability of (
3.12
), we have the following lemma as in
[
47
]
:

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a Lipschitz solution
h_{\varepsilon}
to (
3.12
) such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}},\\
&\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)\in I(V_{\varepsilon}(u_{-}),V_{\varepsilon}(u_{+})),\quad\mbox{a.e.}~t>0,\end{aligned}

where
u_{\pm}:=u(t,h_{\varepsilon}(t)\pm)
, and
I(a,b)
denotes the interval with endpoints
a
and
b
. Moreover, if
u_{-}\neq u_{+}
, the
(u_{-},u_{+},\dot{h}_{\varepsilon})
is an admissible entropic discontinuity, that is,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &f(u_{+})-f(u_{-})=\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(u_{+}-u_{-})\\
&q(u_{+})-q(u_{-})\leq\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(\eta(u_{+})-\eta(u_{-})),\quad\mbox{a.e.}~t>0.\end{aligned}

The proof of this lemma is the exactly same as that in
[
47
]
, because its proof only need the regularity of
u
and
V_{\varepsilon}
, but not the definition of
V_{\varepsilon}
itself. Indeed in
[
31
,
32
,
47
]
, it is shown that (
3.12
) has a solution satisfying (
3.13
). We refer to
[
47
]
for details of its proof.

We use (
2.7
) to derive that the entropic weak solution
u\in BV_{loc}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})^{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E_{a}(u(t),h_{\varepsilon}(t))&=\int_{-\infty}^{h_{\varepsilon}(t)}\partial_{t}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{l})dx+a\int_{h_{\varepsilon}(t)}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{r})dx\\
&\quad+\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)\Big(\eta(u(t,h(t)-)|u_{l})-a\eta(u(t,h(t)+)|u_{r})\Big)\\
&\leq-\int_{-\infty}^{h_{\varepsilon}(t)}\partial_{x}q(u(t,x)|u_{l})dx-a\int_{h_{\varepsilon}(t)}^{\infty}\partial_{x}q(u(t,x)|u_{r})dx\\
&\quad+\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)\Big(\eta(u(t,h(t)-)|u_{l})-a\eta(u(t,h(t)+)|u_{r})\Big)\\
&\leq aq(u_{+}|u_{r})-q(u_{-}|u_{l})-\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)(a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{-}|u_{l}))\\
&=:D_{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}

where
u_{\pm}:=u(t,h_{\varepsilon}(t)\pm)
. Let us show that
D_{\varepsilon}\leq 0
.

For a.e.
t>0
such that
u_{-}=u_{+}
, by (
3.13
), we have

\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)=V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\pm}),

which implies together with (
3.11
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{\varepsilon}&=aq(u_{+}|u_{r})-q(u_{-}|u_{l})-V_{\varepsilon}(u_{\pm})(a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{-}|u_{l}))\\
&=aq(u_{+}|u_{r})-q(u_{-}|u_{l})-[aq(u_{+}|u_{r})-q(u_{-}|u_{l})-\varepsilon]_{+}\\
&\leq\varepsilon.\end{aligned}

On the other hand, for a.e.
t>0
such that
u_{-}\neq u_{+}
, there are two cases as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &i)~\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})~\mbox{and}~a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})~\mbox{have the same sign,}\\
&ii)~a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})~\mbox{and}~a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})~\mbox{have the same sign.}\end{aligned}

Concerning the first case
i)
, we use the fact that
(u_{l},u_{r})
satisfies
(\mathcal{H}2^{*})
and
(u_{-},u_{+},\dot{h}_{\varepsilon})
is the entropic discontinuity by (
3.14
), then it follows from
(\mathcal{H}2^{*})
that

D_{\varepsilon}=D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})\leq 0,

where note that
\sigma_{\pm}=\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}
.

For the second case
ii)
, since
a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})
,
a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})
,
V_{\varepsilon}(u_{-})
and
V_{\varepsilon}(u_{+})
have the same sign, thus
\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}\in I(V_{\varepsilon}(u_{-}),V_{\varepsilon}(u_{+}))
also has the same sign. This implies together with (
3.14
) that for both
v=u_{-}
and
v=u_{+}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{\varepsilon}&\leq aq(v|u_{r})-q(v|u_{l})-\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(a\eta(v|u_{r})-\eta(v|u_{l}))\\
&=aq(v|u_{r})-q(v|u_{l})-|\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}||a\eta(v|u_{r})-\eta(v|u_{l})|.\end{aligned}

If we consider
v
satisfying
|V_{\varepsilon}(v)|=\mbox{inf}(|V_{\varepsilon}(u_{-})|,|V_{\varepsilon}(u_{+})|)
, since
|\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}|\geq|V_{\varepsilon}(v)|
by (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{\varepsilon}&\leq aq(v|u_{r})-q(v|u_{l})-|V_{\varepsilon}(v)||a\eta(v|u_{r})-\eta(v|u_{l})|\\
&=aq(v|u_{r})-q(v|u_{l})-V_{\varepsilon}(v)(a\eta(v|u_{r})-\eta(v|u_{l}))\\
&=aq(v|u_{r})-q(v|u_{l})-[aq(v|u_{r})-q(v|u_{l})-\varepsilon]_{+}\\
&\leq\varepsilon.\end{aligned}

Therefore, it follows from the estimates above that for a.e.
t>s>0
,

E_{a}(u(t),h_{\varepsilon}(t))\leq E_{a}(u(s),h_{\varepsilon}(s))+(t-s)\varepsilon.

Since
\|V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
thanks to
u\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})^{n}
and (
3.11
), by (
3.13
),
\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}
is also uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. Thus up to a subsequence,
h_{\varepsilon}
uniformly converges to a Lipschitz function
h
. Hence we conclude that a.e.
t>s>0
,

E_{a}(u(t),h(t))\leq E_{a}(u(s),h(s)).

∎

3.2.
The
a
-contraction implies the
a-RES

The following theorem says that the
a-RES
is the necessary condition for the
a
-contraction.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
a>0
, if the entropic discontinuity
(u_{l},u_{r})
satisfies the
a
-contraction, then
(u_{l},u_{r})
is the a-RES discontinuity.

Proof.

Suppose that the entropic discontinuity
(u_{l},u_{r})
is not
a-RES
, by the definition of
a-RES
, at least one of
(\mathcal{H}1)
and
(\mathcal{H}2)
dose not hold. That is, we assume that one of the following conditions holds.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bullet(\sim\mathcal{H}1):~\exists~\bar{u}\quad\mbox{such that}\quad\eta(\bar{u}|u_{l})=a\eta(\bar{u}|u_{r})~\mbox{and}~D_{sm}(u_{l,r};\bar{u})>0.\\
&\bullet(\sim\mathcal{H}2):~\exists~\mbox{entropic discontinuity}~(u_{-},u_{+},\sigma_{\pm})\quad\mbox{such that}\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})<a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r}),~\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})>a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})~\mbox{and}~D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})>0.\end{aligned}

We may show that both cases above provide a contradiction with the contractivity (
3.9
).

\bullet

Case of
(\sim\mathcal{H}1)
: Consider a smooth initial data
u_{0}
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{u}&\mbox{if $x\in(-R,R)$ for some $R>0$},\\
u_{l}&\mbox{if $x\in(-\infty,-2R)$},\\
u_{r}&\mbox{if $x\in(2R,\infty)$},\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

where
\bar{u}
is a constant vector appeared in
(\sim\mathcal{H}1)
.

Then,
E_{a}(u_{0},0)<\infty
and the system (
1.1
) admits the smooth solution
u
for some small time
t<T_{0}
,
which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E_{a}(u(t),h(t))&=\int_{-\infty}^{h(t)}\partial_{t}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{l})dx+a\int_{h(t)}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\eta(u(t,x)|u_{r})dx\\
&\quad+\dot{h}(t)\Big(\eta(u(t,h(t))|u_{l})-a\eta(u(t,h(t))|u_{r})\Big).\end{aligned}

Since
u
is smooth for such short time,
u
satisfies the entropy equality

\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\partial_{x}q(u)=0.

Thus, for any constant vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the smooth solution
u
verifies

(3.15)

\partial_{t}\eta(u|v)+\partial_{x}q(u|v)=0.

We use (
3.15
) to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E_{a}(u(t),h(t))&=-\int_{-\infty}^{h(t)}\partial_{x}q(u(t,x)|u_{l})dx-a\int_{h(t)}^{\infty}\partial_{x}q(u(t,x)|u_{r})dx\\
&\quad+\dot{h}(t)\Big(\eta(u(t,h(t))|u_{l})-a\eta(u(t,h(t))|u_{r})\Big)\\
&=aq(u(t,h(t))|u_{r})-q(u(t,h(t))|u_{l})\\
&\quad+\dot{h}(t)\Big(\eta(u(t,h(t))|u_{l})-a\eta(u(t,h(t))|u_{r})\Big).\end{aligned}

For any Lipschitz curve
h(t)
with
h(0)=0
, since we can choose
T_{0}
small enough such that

u(t,h(t))=\bar{u}\quad\mbox{for all}~t\in[0,T_{0}],

it follows from
(\sim\mathcal{H}1)
that for all
t\in[0,T_{0}],

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E_{a}(u(t),h(t))&=aq(\bar{u}|u_{r})-q(\bar{u}|u_{l})+\dot{h}(t)\Big(\eta(\bar{u}|u_{l})-a\eta(\bar{u}|u_{r})\Big)\\
&=aq(\bar{u}|u_{r})-q(\bar{u}|u_{l})\\
&=D_{sm}(u_{l,r};\bar{u})>0,\end{aligned}

which contradicts with (
3.9
).

\bullet

Case of
(\sim\mathcal{H}2)
: For the entropic discontinuity
(u_{-},u_{+},\sigma_{\pm})
in
(\sim\mathcal{H}2)
, we consider a initial data
u_{0}
that is discontinuous at
x=0
and smooth on
(-\infty,0)
and
(0,\infty)
, and satisfies that for some
R>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{-}&\mbox{if $x\in(-R,0)$},\\
u_{+}&\mbox{if $x\in(0,R)$},\\
u_{l}&\mbox{if $x\in(-\infty,-2R)$},\\
u_{r}&\mbox{if $x\in(2R,\infty)$},\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

In order to freeze the shock speed
\sigma_{\pm}
, we consider a new flux
A
defined by

(3.16)

A(u)=f(u)-\sigma_{\pm}u.

Indeed, by Rankine-Hugoniot condition, we have

A(u_{+})-A(u_{-})=f(u_{+})-f(u_{-})-\sigma_{\pm}(u_{+}-u_{-})=0,

which means that the speed of entropic shock
(u_{-},u_{+})
to the system

(3.17)

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}A(u)=0

is zero. Let
G
be a
C^{2}
-entropy flux such that
G^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}A^{\prime}
. Then, since

G^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}(f^{\prime}-\sigma_{\pm})=q^{\prime}-\sigma_{\pm}\eta^{\prime},

we have

(3.18)

G=q-\sigma_{\pm}\eta+C\quad\mbox{for some constant}~C.

Let us consider a weak entropic solution
w\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})^{n}\cap BV_{loc}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})^{n}
to the system (
3.17
) with initial data
u_{0}
. Then, there exists small time
T_{*}>0
such that the weak entropic solution
w
is smooth on both
(-\infty,0)
and
(0,\infty)
, and has a shock
(u_{-},u_{+})
with zero speed at
x=0
, and satisfies that for all
t\leq T_{*}
,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{-}&\mbox{if $x\in(-R/2,0)$},\\
u_{+}&\mbox{if $x\in(0,R/2)$},\\
u_{l}&\mbox{if $x\in(-\infty,-3R)$},\\
u_{r}&\mbox{if $x\in(3R,\infty)$}.\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

Let
h(t)
be any Lipschitz curve with
h(0)=0
. Then, we can choose
T_{*}>0
small enough such that

|h(t)|<\frac{R}{4}\quad t\in[0,T_{*}].

By the continuity of
h
, we use the Weierstrass approximation theorem to choose a sequence
(p_{n})
of polynomials such that

(3.20)

\|p_{n}-h\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{*}])}~\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

which yields that for sufficiently large
N
,

(3.21)

|p_{n}(t)|<\frac{R}{4},\quad n\geq N,\quad t\in[0,T_{*}].

We first show that

(3.22)

E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))\geq E_{a}(u_{0},p_{n}(0))+tD_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm}),\quad t\in(0,T_{*}].

Let us begin by noticing the fact that

(3.23)

w(t,x)~\mbox{is constant in time}~t\in(0,T_{*}],\quad\mbox{for}~x\in(-\frac{R}{2},\frac{R}{2}),

thus
w(t,w)
is differentiable with respect to
t\in(0,T_{*}]
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. This yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))&=\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{p_{n}(t)}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{a\int_{p_{n}(t)}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx}_{I_{2}}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\dot{p}_{n}(t)\Big(\eta(w(t,p_{n}(t)-)|u_{l})-a\eta(w(t,p_{n}(t)+)|u_{r})\Big)}_{I_{3}}.\end{aligned}

(Case of non-constant polynomial
p_{n}
)
: If the polynomial
p_{n}
is not constant for some
n\geq N
,
p_{n}
has a finite number of zeros as
t_{1},t_{2}\cdots,t_{k}
with

0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}\leq T_{*}.

Then
p_{n}
is either negative or positive on each interval
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
, where
i
belongs to one of the following classes

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &1\leq i\leq k-1\quad\mbox{if $t_{1}=0$ and $t_{k}=T_{*}$},\\
&0\leq i\leq k-1\quad\mbox{if $t_{1}>0$ and $t_{k}=T_{*}$ where $t_{0}:=0$},\\
&1\leq i\leq k\quad\mbox{if $t_{1}=0$ and $t_{k}<T_{*}$ where $t_{k+1}:=T_{*}$},\\
&0\leq i\leq k\quad\mbox{if $t_{1}>0$ and $t_{k}<T_{*}$}.\end{aligned}

First of all, let us consider the case that
p_{n}(t)<0
for all
t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1})
for some
l
. Then the solution
w
is smooth on
(0,T_{*}]\times(-\infty,p_{n}(t)]
. Thus, we apply (
3.15
) to the system (
3.17
) to get

I_{1}=-\int_{-\infty}^{p_{n}(t)}\partial_{x}G(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx.

Since by (
3.19
) and (
3.21
),

(3.24)

w(t,p_{n}(t))=u_{-}\quad\mbox{ for all}~t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1}),

we have

I_{1}=-G(u_{-}|u_{l}).

Since
w
is discontinuous at
x=0
for
0<t\leq T_{*}
, we rewrite
I_{2}
as

I_{2}=a\int_{p_{n}(t)}^{\frac{R}{4}}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx+a\int_{\frac{R}{4}}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx.

By (
3.23
), we have

\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{r})=0,\quad(t,x)\in(0,T_{*}]\times[p_{n}(t),\frac{R}{4}].

Since
w
is smooth on
(0,T_{*}]\times[\frac{R}{4},\infty)
and
w(t,\frac{R}{4})=u_{+}
for
0<t\leq T_{*}
, we have

I_{2}=-a\int_{\frac{R}{4}}^{\infty}\partial_{x}G(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx=aG(u_{+}|u_{r}).

For
I_{3}
, we use (
3.24
) to get

I_{3}=\dot{p}_{n}(t)\Big(\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})\Big).

Thus, we have shown

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))=aG(u_{+}|u_{r})-G(u_{-}|u_{l})+\dot{p}_{n}(t)\Big(\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})\Big),\quad t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1}).\end{aligned}

We here use (
3.18
) to reduce

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &aG(u_{+}|u_{r})-G(u_{-}|u_{l})\\
&\quad=a\Big[q(u_{+})-\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{+})-q(u_{r})+\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{r})-d\eta(u_{r})(f(u_{+})-\sigma_{\pm}u_{+}-f(u_{r})-\sigma_{\pm}u_{r})\Big]\\
&\qquad-q(u_{-})+\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{-})+q(u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{l})+d\eta(u_{l})(f(u_{-})-\sigma_{\pm}u_{-}-f(u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm}u_{l})\\
&\quad=aq(u_{+}|u_{r})-q(u_{-}|u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm}\Big(a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})\Big)\\
&\quad=D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm}),\end{aligned}

which provides

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))=D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})+\dot{p}_{n}(t)\Big(\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})\Big),\quad t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1}).\end{aligned}

For any
\tau,t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1})
with
\tau<t
, integrating it over
[\tau,t]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{a}(w(t),{p}_{n}(t))&=E_{a}(w(\tau),{p}_{n}(\tau))+(t-\tau)D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})\\
&\quad+({p}_{n}(t)-{p}_{n}(\tau))\Big(\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})\Big).\end{aligned}

Since
E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))
is continuous in time
t
and
{p}_{n}(t_{l})=0
, taking
\tau\to t_{l}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))&=E_{a}(w(t_{l}),p_{n}(t_{l}))+(t-t_{l})D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})\\
&\quad+{p}_{n}(t)\Big(\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})\Big).\end{aligned}

Since
{p}_{n}(t)<0
for all
t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1})
, we use the assumption
(\sim\mathcal{H}2)
to get

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))&\geq E_{a}(w(t_{l}),p_{n}(t_{l}))+(t-t_{l})D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm}),\quad t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1}],\end{aligned}

where note that this inequality also holds at
t=t_{l+1}
by the time-continuity of
E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))
.

On the other hand, for the case where
p_{n}(t)>0
for all
t\in(t_{m},t_{m+1})
for some
m
, we follow the same argument as above. More precisely for all
t\in(t_{m},t_{m+1})
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}&=\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{R}{4}}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx+\int_{-\frac{R}{4}}^{p_{n}(t)}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{R}{4}}\partial_{x}G(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx=-G(u_{-}|u_{l}),\\
I_{2}&=-a\int_{p_{n}(t)}^{\infty}\partial_{x}G(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx=aG(u_{+}|u_{r}),\\
I_{3}&=\dot{p}_{n}(t)\Big(\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})\Big),\end{aligned}

which provides that for all
t\in(t_{m},t_{m+1})
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))&=E_{a}(w(t_{m}),p_{n}(t_{m}))+(t-t_{m})D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})\\
&\quad+{p}_{n}(t)\Big(\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})-a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})\Big).\end{aligned}

Since
{p}_{n}(t)>0
for all
t\in(t_{m},t_{m+1})
, we use the assumption
(\sim\mathcal{H}2)
to get

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))&\geq E_{a}(w(t_{m}),p_{n}(t_{m}))+(t-t_{m})D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm}),\quad t\in(t_{m},t_{m+1}].\end{aligned}

Therefore, combining (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), we can conclude (
3.22
).

(Case of constant polynomial
p_{n}
)
: If polynomial
p_{n}
is constant for some
n\geq N
, using (
3.21
), we have

p_{n}(t)=p_{n}(0)\in(-\frac{R}{4},\frac{R}{4})\quad\mbox{for all}~t\in[0,T_{*}].

First of all, for the case of
p_{n}(0)\neq 0
, the claim (
3.22
) follows directly from the previous arguments.
If
p_{n}(0)=0
, we also combine the previous arguments to have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}&=\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{R}{4}}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx+\int_{-\frac{R}{4}}^{0}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{R}{4}}\partial_{x}G(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx=-G(u_{-}|u_{l}),\\
I_{2}&=a\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{4}}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx+a\int_{\frac{R}{4}}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx\\
&=-a\int_{\frac{R}{4}}^{\infty}\partial_{x}G(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx=aG(u_{+}|u_{r}),\\
I_{3}&=0,\end{aligned}

which provides (
3.22
).

Since
tD_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})
in (
3.22
) independent of
n
and
(\sim\mathcal{H}2)
implies

tD_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})>0\quad\mbox{for}~t>0,

using (
3.20
), (
3.22
) and
w\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
, we show

(3.27)

E_{a}(w(t),h(t))>E_{a}(u_{0},0),\quad t\in(0,T_{*}].

Indeed, since

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|E_{a}(w(t),h(t))-E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))|\\
&\quad=\Big|\int_{p_{n}(t)}^{h(t)}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{l})dx+a\int_{h(t)}^{p_{n}(t)}\eta(w(t,x)|u_{r})dx\Big|\\
&\quad\leq\|p_{n}-h\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{*}])}\Big(\|\eta(w|u_{l})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})}+a\|\eta(w|u_{r})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})}\Big)\\
&\quad\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}~n\to\infty,\end{aligned}

we have

E_{a}(w(t),p_{n}(t))\rightarrow E_{a}(w(t),h(t))\quad\mbox{uniformly in}~t\in[0,T_{*}],

which implies that for
t\in(0,T_{*}]
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{a}(w(t),h(t))\geq E_{a}(u_{0},0)+\frac{t}{2}D_{RH}(u_{l,r},u_{\pm})>E_{a}(u_{0},0).\end{aligned}

Since the Lipschitz shift
h
is arbitrary, the weak entropic solution
u
of (
1.1
) with
u_{0}
also satisfies

E_{a}(u(t),h(t))>E_{a}(u_{0},0),\quad 0<t\leq T_{*}.

which provides the contradiction with (
3.9
).

∎

4.
On
a
-contractions for extremal shocks

In this section, we are going to show that extremal shocks (i.e.
1
-shock or
n
-shock) is
a-SRES
for some
a
, which implies that they satisfies
a
-contraction by Theorem
3.1
. Even though the
a
-contraction for extremal shocks has been shown by Vasseur in
[
50
]
, we intend to here give an alternate proof as a direct application of the criteria built in Theorem
3.1
. This result goes beyond the known results valid in the class of
BV
solutions under small perturbation in
BV
. In the case of small perturbation in
L^{\infty}\cap BV
, Bressan, Crasta and Piccoli in
[
10
]
developed a powerful theory of
L^{1}

Regarding the hypotheses for the

For any fixed
u_{r}\in\mathcal{V}
and

, there exists a neighborhood
B\subset\mathcal{V}
of
u_{r}
such that for any
u\in B

i
-th Hugoniot curve
S_{u}^{i}(s)\in\mathcal{U}

f(S_{u}^{i}(s))-f(u)=\sigma_{u}^{i}(s)(S_{u}^{i}(s)-u),

where

Remark 4.1
.

1. Note that a system (
1.1
) verifies the hypotheses
(\mathcal{H})
relative to
u_{l}\in\mathcal{V}
if and only if the system

\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}f(u)=0

verifies
(\mathcal{H}^{*})
relative to
u_{r}\in\mathcal{V}
. In other words,
(\mathcal{H})
and
(\mathcal{H}^{*})
are dual in this way. Thus it is enough to show the
a
-contraction for 1-shocks, because the result of
n
-shock is obtained by applying the case of
1
-shock to
\tilde{u}(t,x)=u(t,-x)
, which is also entropic solution to (
1.1
). From now on, we will restrict our arguments to the case of a 1-shock.

2. The assumptions (a) in (
4.28
), (
4.29
) and (
4.30
) are just due to Liu and Lax entropy conditions. The only additional requirement is (b) in (
4.28
), which is a condition on the growth of the shock along
S_{u}^{1}(s)
measured through the pseudo-distance. This condition arises naturally in the study of admissibility criteria for systems of conservation laws. In particular, it ensures that Liu admissible shocks are entropic even for moderate to strong shocks. Indeed, this fact follows
from the important formula : (See also
[
17
,
30
,
35
]
)

q(S_{u}^{1}(s))-q(u)=\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)(\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)-\eta(u))+\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d}{dt}\sigma^{1}_{u}(t)\eta(u|S_{u}^{1}(t))dt.

In
[
4
]
, Barker, Freistühler, and Zumbrun showed that the stability (and so the contraction as well) fails to hold for the full Euler system if hypothesis (b) in (
4.28
) is replaced by

\frac{d}{ds}\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s))>0,\quad s>0.

This shows that the strength of the shock is better measured by the relative entropy rather
than the entropy itself.

3. In
[
49
]
, Texier and Zumbrun showed that the hypotheses
(\mathcal{H})
implies the Lopatinski condition of Majda.

4.2.
Structural lemmas

We first present the following structural Lemmas treated in
[
50
]
. The first lemma provides a kind of triangle inequality for the pseudo metric induced by
\eta(\cdot|\cdot)
and its analogous inequalities, which are useful tools in the following proofs.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
u,v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(4.33)

\eta(u|w)+\eta(w|v)=\eta(u|v)+(\nabla\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(v))\cdot(w-u),

and

(4.34)

q(u|w)+q(w|v)=q(u|v)+(\nabla\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(v))\cdot(f(w)-f(u)).

Therefore, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4.35)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} q(u|v)-\sigma\eta(u|v)&=\Big(q(u|w)-\sigma\eta(u|w)\Big)+\Big(q(w|v)-\sigma\eta(w|v)\Big)\\
&\quad-(\nabla\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(v))\cdot\Big(f(w)-f(u)-\sigma(w-u)\Big)\end{aligned}

Proof.

The proof follows directly from the definition of the relative entropy
\eta(\cdot|\cdot)
and its flux
q(\cdot|\cdot)
. Indeed, the following computations hold:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \eta(u|w)+\eta(w|v)&=\Big(\eta({u})-\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(w)\cdot(u-w)\Big)\\
&\quad+\Big(\eta(w)-\eta(v)-\nabla\eta(v)\cdot(w-v)\Big)\\
&=\eta(u)-\eta(v)-\nabla\eta(v)\cdot(u-v)+(\nabla\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(v))\cdot(w-u)\\
&=\eta(u|v)+(\nabla\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(v))\cdot(w-u),\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} q(u|w)+q(w|v)&=\Big(q({u})-q(w)-\nabla\eta(w)\cdot(f(u)-f(w))\Big)\\
&\quad+\Big(q(w)-q(v)-\nabla\eta(v)\cdot(f(w)-f(v))\Big)\\
&=q(u)-q(v)-\nabla\eta(v)\cdot(f(u)-f(v))+(\nabla\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(v))\cdot(f(w)-f(u))\\
&=q(u|v)+(\nabla\eta(w)-\nabla\eta(v))\cdot(f(w)-f(u)).\end{aligned}

∎

The following lemma gives an explicit formula concerning the entropy lost at an entropic discontinuity
(u,S^{i}_{u}(s))

For any Rankine-Hugoniot discontinuity

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
be an entropic
1
-shock such that
u_{r}=S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})
,
\sigma_{l,r}=\sigma_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})
for some

, and the corresponding conditions in (

Then, there exists
\sigma_{0}\in(\sigma_{l,r},\lambda_{1}(u_{l}))
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
\beta>0
and
a_{*}>0
verifying the following properties:

•

For any
u\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})
,

(4.39)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sigma_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}(u),\\
&-q(u,u_{l})+\sigma_{0}\eta(u|u_{l})\leq-\beta\eta(u|u_{l}),\\
&q(u,u_{r})-\sigma_{0}\eta(u|u_{r})\leq-\beta\eta(u|u_{r}).\end{aligned}

•

For any
0<a<a_{*}
,

(4.40)

\mbox{the ball $B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})$ contains the convex set}~{R}_{a}:=\{u~|~\eta(u|u_{l})\leq a\eta(u|u_{r})\},

and for any
u\in{R}_{a}
,

(4.41)

a\Big(q(S_{u}^{1}(s),u_{r})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|u_{r})\Big)-q(u|u_{l})+\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(u|u_{l})\leq 0,\quad s>0.

Proof.

Step A) proof for (
4.39
)
:
We begin by using (
4.37
) with
u=u_{l}
and
s=0
to get

q(u_{l},u_{r})-\lambda_{1}(u_{l})\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})<0,

where we have used the hypothesis (
4.28
). Since this inequality is strict, we can choose
\sigma_{0}
sufficiently close to
\lambda_{1}(u_{l})
with
\sigma_{0}\in(\sigma_{l,r},\lambda_{1}(u_{l}))
and
\beta>0
sufficiently small such that

q(u_{l},u_{r})-(\sigma_{0}-\beta)\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})<0,

which gives

q(u_{l},u_{r})-\sigma_{0}\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})<-\beta\eta(u_{l}|u_{r}).

Then, using the continuity of
q(\cdot,u_{r})
,
\eta(\cdot|u_{r})
and
\lambda_{1}(\cdot)
, we can choose
\varepsilon_{0}
sufficiently small such that for all
u\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})
,

q(u,u_{r})-\sigma_{0}\eta(u|u_{r})<-\beta\eta(u|u_{r}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}(u).

On the other hand, we use Taylor expansion at
u_{l}
to get

-q(u,u_{l})+\sigma_{0}\eta(u|u_{l})=(u-u_{l})^{T}\nabla^{2}\eta(u_{l})(\sigma_{0}I-\nabla f(u_{l}))(u-u_{l})+\mathcal{O}(|u-u_{l}|^{3}).

Here, since the entropy
\eta
is strictly convex,
\nabla^{2}\eta(u_{l})
is symmetric and strictly positive, and
\nabla^{2}\eta(u_{l})\nabla f(u_{l}))
is symmetric. Thus those matrices are diagonalizable in the same basis, which gives

\nabla^{2}\eta(u_{l})\nabla f(u_{l}))\geq\lambda_{1}(u_{l})\nabla^{2}\eta(u_{l}).

This and
\lambda_{1}(u_{l})>\sigma_{0}
imply that for all
u\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -q(u,u_{l})+\sigma_{0}\eta(u|u_{l})&\leq-(\lambda_{1}(u_{l})-\sigma_{0})(u-u_{l})^{T}\nabla^{2}\eta(u_{l})(u-u_{l})+\mathcal{O}(|u-u_{l}|^{3})\\
&\leq-(\lambda_{1}(u_{l})-\sigma_{0})\eta(u|u_{l})+\mathcal{O}(|u-u_{l}|^{3})\\
&\leq-\frac{\lambda_{1}(u_{l})-\sigma_{0}}{2}\eta(u|u_{l}),\end{aligned}

where we have used the smallness of
\varepsilon_{0}
in the last inequality.

Step B) proof for (
4.40
)
: Notice that

(4.42)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mbox{for}~a<1,\quad\eta(u|u_{l})\leq a\eta(u|u_{r})\quad\mbox{is equivalent to}\\
&\eta(u)\leq\frac{1}{1-a}(\eta(u_{l})-a\eta(u_{r})-\nabla\eta(u_{l})\cdot u_{l}+a\nabla\eta(u_{r})\cdot u_{r}+(\nabla\eta(u_{l})-a\nabla\eta(u_{r}))\cdot u),\end{aligned}

where the right hand side of the second inequality above is linear in
u
. Thus, the convexity of
\eta
implies the convexity of
{R}_{a}=\{u~|~\eta(u|u_{l})\leq a\eta(u|u_{r})\}
. We take
a_{*}<\frac{1}{2}
to rewrite the second inequality above as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \eta(u|u_{l})&\leq\frac{a}{1-a}(\eta(u_{l})-\eta(u_{r})-\nabla\eta(u_{l})\cdot u_{l}+\nabla\eta(u_{r})\cdot u_{r}+(\nabla\eta(u_{l})-\nabla\eta(u_{r}))\cdot u)\\
&\leq Ca(1+|u|),\quad\mbox{for all}~0<a<a_{*}.\end{aligned}

This yields together with Lemma
2.1
that for all
u\in R_{a}\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})
,

|u-u_{l}|^{2}\leq Ca(1+|u|)\leq C_{*}a.

Taking
a_{*}
sufficiently small as
a_{*}<\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{2C_{*}}
such that for any
a<a_{*}
and
u\in R_{a}\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})

|u-u_{l}|^{2}\leq C_{*}a<\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{2},

which implies that
B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})
strictly contains
R_{a}\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})
. Since
R_{a}
is convex, so connected, thus we have

R_{a}=R_{a}\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l}).

Therefore, for any
a<a_{*}
,

R_{a}\subset B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l}).

Step C) proof of (
4.41
)
:
First of all, we take
\sigma_{0}
closer to
\lambda_{1}(u_{l})
and
\varepsilon_{0}
smaller than those chosen in Step A, such that for all
u\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{l})

(4.43)

\sigma^{1}_{u}(s)\leq\sigma_{0},\quad s\geq\frac{s_{0}}{2},

and

(4.44)

\lambda_{1}(u)-\sigma_{0}<\frac{k}{4C_{1}}|\sigma_{u}^{1}(\frac{s_{0}}{2})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|,

where

is the constant as in (

) and

Indeed, (
4.43

, in which

is
C^{1}
-function, and
\frac{d}{ds}\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)<0
with

Let us first show (
4.41
) for all
s\geq\frac{s_{0}}{2}
. We use (
4.35
) to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &q(S_{u}^{1}(s),u_{r})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|u_{r})\\
&\quad=-\Big(q(u_{r},S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(u_{r}|S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\Big)+\Big(q(S_{u}^{1}(s),S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\Big)\\
&\qquad+\Big(\nabla\eta(u_{r})-\nabla\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\Big)\Big(f(u_{r})-f(S_{u}^{1}(s))-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)(u_{r}-S_{u}^{1}(s))\Big),\end{aligned}

where
u_{r}=S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})
. By using Rankine-Hugoniot conditions

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &f(u_{r})-f(u_{l})=\sigma_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})(u_{r}-u_{l}),\\
&f(S_{u}^{1}(s))-f(u)=\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)(S_{u}^{1}(s)-u),\\
\end{aligned}

we rewrite it as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &q(S_{u}^{1}(s),u_{r})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|u_{r})\\
&\quad=\Big(-q(u_{r},S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))+\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(u_{r}|S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\Big)+\Big(q(S_{u}^{1}(s),S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\Big)\\
&\qquad+\Big(\nabla\eta(u_{r})-\nabla\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\Big)\Big(f(u_{l})-f(u)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)(u_{l}-u)+(\sigma_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s))(u_{r}-u_{l})\Big)\\
&\quad=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{aligned}

Since

and

are

, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|q(S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0}),S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))|\leq C|S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})-S_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|^{2}\leq C|u_{l}-u|^{2},\\
&\eta(S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})|S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\leq C|S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})-S_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|^{2}\leq C|u_{l}-u|^{2},\\
&|\nabla\eta(S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0}))-\nabla\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))|\leq C|S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})-S_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|\leq C|u_{l}-u|,\\
&|\sigma_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|\leq C|u_{l}-u|.\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}&=-q(S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0}),S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))+\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})\eta(S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})|S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))+(\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\eta(S_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})|S_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))\\

\end{aligned}

&\quad+(\sigma_{u_{l}}^{1}(s_{0})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))(u_{r}-u_{l})-(\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0}))(u_{r}-u_{l})\Big),\\
&\leq C|u_{l}-u|(|u_{l}-u|+|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})||u_{l}-u|+|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|).\end{aligned}

&\quad\leq-k|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|^{2}\\
&\qquad+C|u-u_{l}|^{2}(1+|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|)+C|u-u_{l}||\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|\\
&\quad\leq C(|u-u_{l}|^{2}+|u-u_{l}|^{4})\leq C|u-u_{l}|^{2},\end{aligned}

where we have used Young’s inequality and

.

If

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &q(S_{u}^{1}(s),u_{r})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|u_{r})\\
&\quad\leq-k|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|\\
&\qquad+C|u-u_{l}|^{2}(1+|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|)+C|u-u_{l}||\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|\\
&\quad\leq C|u-u_{l}|^{2},\end{aligned}

where we have used smallness of
\varepsilon_{0}
as
\varepsilon_{0}\ll k
.

Therefore, we use (
4.39
) and (
4.43
) to get

&\leq aC|u-u_{l}|^{2}-q(u|u_{l})+\sigma_{0}\eta(u|u_{l})\\
&\leq aC|u-u_{l}|^{2}+\beta\eta(u|u_{l}).\end{aligned}

.

On the other hand, when

, since

as in (

), we have

. Thus using smallness of

, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &q(S_{u}^{1}(s),u_{r})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|u_{r})\\
&\quad\leq-k|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|\\
&\qquad+C|u-u_{l}|^{2}(1+|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|)+C|u-u_{l}||\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|\\
&\quad\leq-\frac{k}{2}|\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|+C|u-u_{l}|^{2}.\end{aligned}

Using (

) and smallness of
\varepsilon_{0}
again, we have that for all

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &q(S_{u}^{1}(s),u_{r})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|u_{r})\\
&\quad\leq-\frac{k}{2}|\sigma_{u}^{1}(\frac{s_{0}}{2})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|+C|u-u_{l}|^{2}\\
&\quad\leq-\frac{k}{4}|\sigma_{u}^{1}(\frac{s_{0}}{2})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|.\end{aligned}

Since

, we use (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -q(u|u_{l})+\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(u|u_{l})&\leq-q(u|u_{l})+\lambda_{1}(u)\eta(u|u_{l})\\
&=-q(u|u_{l})+\sigma_{0}\eta(u|u_{l})+(\lambda_{1}(u)-\sigma_{0})\eta(u|u_{l})\\
&\leq(\lambda_{1}(u)-\sigma_{0})\eta(u|u_{l})\\
&\leq a(\lambda_{1}(u)-\sigma_{0})\eta(u|u_{r})\\
&\leq aC_{1}(\lambda_{1}(u)-\sigma_{0}).\end{aligned}

Therefore, for all

,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &a\Big(q(S_{u}^{1}(s),u_{r})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(S_{u}^{1}(s)|u_{r})\Big)-q(u|u_{l})+\sigma_{u}^{1}(s)\eta(u|u_{l})\\
&\quad\leq-a\Big(\frac{k}{4}|\sigma_{u}^{1}(\frac{s_{0}}{2})-\sigma_{u}^{1}(s_{0})|-C(\lambda_{1}(u)-\sigma_{0})\Big).\end{aligned}

Hence we use (
4.44
) to conclude (
4.41
).

(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
does not satisfies the first condition
(\mathcal{H}1)
of
a-RES
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{sm}(u_{l,r};\bar{u})=aq(\bar{u},u_{r})-q(\bar{u},u_{l})>0.\end{aligned}

For this end, we consider a differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

(5.62)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F(s)&=a\Big(q(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s),u_{r})-\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{r})\Big)\\
&\qquad-q(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s),u_{l})+\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{l}).\end{aligned}

Note that we have

F(\bar{s})=D_{sm}(u_{l,r};\bar{u}),

because
a\eta(\bar{u}|u_{r})=\eta(\bar{u}|u_{l})
.

Since the
i
-th shock
(u_{l},S_{u_{l}}^{i}(s),\sigma_{u_{l}}^{i}(s))
satisfies (
5.60
) and (
5.61
), by the inequality
(
4.36
) and the fact
\lambda_{j}(u_{l})<\lambda_{i}(u_{l})
, we have

(5.63)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &q(u_{l},u_{r})-\sigma_{l,r}\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})=-\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\frac{d}{dt}\sigma^{i}_{u_{l}}(t)\eta(u_{l}|S_{u_{l}}(t))dt>0,\\
&\sigma_{l,r}\geq\lambda_{i-1}(u_{l})\geq\lambda_{j}(u_{l}),\end{aligned}

which implies the positivity of
f(0)
by

(5.64)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F(0)&=a\Big(q(u_{l},u_{r})-\lambda_{j}(u_{l})\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\Big)\\
&=a\Big(q(u_{l},u_{r})-\sigma_{l,r}\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\Big)+a(\sigma_{l,r}-\lambda_{j}(u_{l}))\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\\
&>0.\end{aligned}

Let us show

(5.65)

F^{\prime}(s)>0,\quad 0<s<\bar{s}.

First of all, since
\frac{d}{ds}R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)=r_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))
, we have

\nabla f(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\frac{d}{ds}R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)=\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\frac{d}{ds}R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s),

which yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F^{\prime}(s)&=a\Big[(d\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))-d\eta(u_{r}))\Big(\nabla f(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))-\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))I\Big)\frac{d}{ds}R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)\Big]\\
&\quad-(d\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))-d\eta(u_{l}))\Big(\nabla f(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))-\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))I\Big)\frac{d}{ds}R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)\\
&\quad+\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\Big(\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{l})-a\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{r})\Big)\\
&=\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\Big(\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{l})-a\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{r})\Big).\end{aligned}

Here, since
\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))
is continuous and
\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))<\lambda_{j}(u_{l})
besides

\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))=\nabla\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\cdot r_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))\neq 0,

we have

\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s))<0,\quad s>0.

Since
R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(\bar{s})=\bar{u}
is the first intersection point of the continuous curve
R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)
and the surface
\Sigma_{a}
, we have

\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{l})-a\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}(s)|u_{r})<0,\quad 0<s<\bar{s}.

Thus we have shown (
5.65
), which implies together with (
5.64
) that

F(\bar{s})=D_{sm}(u_{l,r};\bar{u})>0.

Hence the shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
is not
a-RES
, which provides the conclusion by Theorem
3.2
.

(2) Case of
a>1
:
We follow the same argument as above by considering
R_{u_{r}}^{k,+}
instead of
R_{u_{l}}^{j,-}
. Indeed, we use the same computations to have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f(0)&=-q(u_{r},u_{l})+\lambda_{k}(u_{r})\eta(u_{r}|u_{l})\\
&=-\Big(q(u_{r},u_{l})-\sigma_{l,r}\eta(u_{r}|u_{l})\Big)+(\lambda_{k}(u_{r})-\sigma_{l,r})\eta(u_{r}|u_{l})\\
&>0,\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f^{\prime}(s)&=\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{j}(R_{u_{r}}^{k,+}(s))\Big(\eta(R_{u_{r}}^{j,+}(s)|u_{l})-a\eta(R_{u_{l}}^{j,+}(s)|u_{r})\Big)\\
&>0,\quad 0\leq s\leq\tilde{s},\end{aligned}

where
\tilde{u}:=R_{u_{r}}^{k,+}(\tilde{s})
is the first intersection point of the forward curve
R_{u_{r}}^{k,+}(s)
and the surface
\Sigma_{a}
. Thus we have the same conclusion as

f(\tilde{s})=D_{sm}(u_{l,r};\tilde{u})>0.

(3) Case of
a=1
:
Since the proof of two cases above does not depend on the strength of
a
, if one of the assumptions of (1) and (2) is satisfied, we end up with no
a
-contraction of the entropic shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
.

5.2.
Application to magnetohydrodynamics

As an application of Theorem
5.1
, we here show that there is no contraction property of certain intermediate shocks for two-dimensional (planar) isentropic MHD in Lagrangian coordinates:

(5.66)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v-\partial_{x}u=0\\
\partial_{t}(vB)-\beta\partial_{x}w=0\\
\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}(p+\frac{1}{2}B^{2})=0\\
\partial_{t}w-\beta\partial_{x}B=0,\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

where
v
denotes specific volume, and two-dimensional fluid velocity
(u,w)
and magnetic field
(\beta,B)
only depend on a single direction
e_{1}
measured by
x
. This behavior of two-dimensional vector fields with spatially one-dimensional dependence is achieved when the initial condition is so. Thus the divergence-free condition of magnetic field of full MHD reduces that
\beta
is constant (See for example
[
6
]
for study on (
5.66
)). As a perfect fluid, the pressure
p
is assumed to satisfies

(5.67)

p(v)=v^{-\gamma},\quad\gamma>1.

This system has an entropy
\eta
as

\eta(U)=\int_{v}^{\infty}p(s)ds+\frac{1}{2}(u^{2}+w^{2})+\frac{q^{2}}{2v}

in terms of the conservative variables
U:=(v,q,u,w)
where
q:=vB
.

For simplicity of computation, we use non-conservative variable
W:=(v,B,u,w)
and rewrite (
5.66
) as a quasilinear form:

\partial_{t}W+A\partial_{x}W=0,

where the
4\times 4
matrix
A
is given by

A:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&-1&0\\
0&0&\frac{B}{v}&-\frac{\beta}{v}\\
-c^{2}&B&0&0\\
0&-\beta&0&0\end{matrix}\right).

where
c:=\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v)}
denotes the sound speed.

Since the eigenvalues of
A
solves the characteristic polynomial

\lambda^{4}-\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}+c^{2}\Big)\lambda^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{v}c^{2}=0,

we have four eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}=-\sqrt{\alpha_{+}},\quad\lambda_{2}=-\sqrt{\alpha_{-}},\quad\lambda_{3}=\sqrt{\alpha_{-}},\quad\lambda_{4}=\sqrt{\alpha_{+}},

where

\alpha_{\pm}:=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}+c^{2}\pm\sqrt{\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}+c^{2}\Big)^{2}-4\beta^{2}\frac{c^{2}}{v}}\Big].

By a straightforward computation, we have the corresponding eigenvectors

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &r_{1}=\Big(1,-\frac{(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{B},\sqrt{\alpha_{+}},-\frac{\beta(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{B\sqrt{\alpha_{+}}}\Big)^{T},\\
&r_{2}=\Big(1,-\frac{(\alpha_{-}-c^{2})}{B},\sqrt{\alpha_{-}},-\frac{\beta(\alpha_{-}-c^{2})}{B\sqrt{\alpha_{-}}}\Big)^{T},\\
&r_{3}=\Big(-1,\frac{(\alpha_{-}-c^{2})}{B},\sqrt{\alpha_{-}},-\frac{\beta(\alpha_{-}-c^{2})}{B\sqrt{\alpha_{-}}}\Big)^{T},\\
&r_{4}=\Big(-1,\frac{(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{B},\sqrt{\alpha_{+}},-\frac{\beta(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{B\sqrt{\alpha_{+}}}\Big)^{T}.\end{aligned}

Here, we restrict our study to the case of
B\neq 0
. Using the strict convexity of pressure
p(v)
,
d\lambda_{i}\cdot r_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq 4
, which means that all characteristic fields are genuinely non-linear. Indeed since

\alpha_{\pm}-c^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}\pm\sqrt{\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}\Big)^{2}+4\frac{B^{2}c^{2}}{v}}\Big]

yields

(5.68)

\alpha_{-}<c^{2}<\alpha_{+},

thus we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d\lambda_{1}\cdot r_{1}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha_{+}}}\Big[-\partial_{v}\alpha_{+}+\partial_{B}\alpha_{+}\frac{\alpha_{+}-c^{2}}{B}\Big],\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\partial_{v}\alpha_{+}&=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v^{2}}+p^{\prime\prime}+\frac{(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2})(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v^{2}}-p^{\prime\prime})+\frac{2B^{2}c^{2}}{v^{2}}+\frac{2B^{2}p^{\prime\prime}}{v}}{\sqrt{\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}\Big)^{2}+4\frac{B^{2}c^{2}}{v}}}\Big]\\
&>\frac{1}{2}\frac{\frac{2B^{2}c^{2}}{v^{2}}+\frac{2B^{2}p^{\prime\prime}}{v}}{\sqrt{\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}\Big)^{2}+4\frac{B^{2}c^{2}}{v}}}>0,\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{B}\alpha_{+}\frac{\alpha_{+}-c^{2}}{B}&=\Big[\frac{1}{v}+\frac{(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2})\frac{1}{v}+\frac{2c^{2}}{v}}{\sqrt{\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}\Big)^{2}+4\frac{B^{2}c^{2}}{v}}}\Big](\alpha_{+}-c^{2})\\
&>\frac{\frac{2c^{2}}{v}}{\sqrt{\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}\Big)^{2}+4\frac{B^{2}c^{2}}{v}}}(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})>0.\end{aligned}

Similarly we have
d\lambda_{i}\cdot r_{i}>0
for
i=2,3,4
.

Let
(U_{l},U_{r},\sigma_{2})
be the 2-shock wave satisfying the Rankine-Hugoniot condition:

(5.69)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -[u]&=\sigma_{2}[v],\\
-\beta[w]&=\sigma_{2}[q],\\
[p]+\Big[\frac{q^{2}}{2v^{2}}\Big]&=\sigma_{2}[u],\\
-\beta\Big[\frac{q}{v}\Big]&=\sigma_{2}[w],\end{aligned}

where
[f]:=f_{r}-f_{l}
.

By Lax condition,
d\lambda_{2}\cdot r_{2}>0
implies that
-r_{2}(U_{l})
is a tangent vector at
U_{l}
of the 2-shock curve
S_{U_{l}}^{2}
issuing from
U_{l}
. Thus since
dv\cdot(-r_{2})<0
and
du\cdot(-r_{2})<0
, we have

(5.70)

[v]<0\quad\mbox{and}\quad[u]<0.

Since

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} dB\cdot(-r_{2})=\frac{(\alpha_{-}-c^{2})}{B}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}<0\quad\mbox{if}~B>0\\
>0\quad\mbox{if}~B<0,\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

we have that
[B]<0
for
B_{l}>0
, and
[B]>0
for
B_{l}<0
. In particular, we here consider the case where the 2-shock wave satisfies

(5.71)

\mbox{either}\quad B_{l}>B_{r}>0\quad\mbox{or}\quad B_{l}<B_{r}<0.

Similarly for a given 3-shock wave
(\tilde{U}_{l},\tilde{U}_{r},\sigma_{3})
, we have
[\tilde{v}]>0
,
[\tilde{u}]<0
, and

[\tilde{B}]>0
for
\tilde{B}_{l}>0
, and
[\tilde{B}]<0
for
\tilde{B}_{l}<0
. Also, we consider the case where the 3-shock wave
satisfies

(5.72)

\mbox{either}\quad\tilde{B}_{r}>\tilde{B}_{l}>0\quad\mbox{or}\quad\tilde{B}_{r}<\tilde{B}_{l}<0.

We are now ready to show that for any
a>0
, there is no
a
-contraction of such intermediate shocks as follows.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(U_{l},U_{r},\sigma_{2})
be a given 2-shock wave of the system (
5.66
)-(
5.67
)
satisfying (
5.71
). Then there is no weight
a>0
such that
(u_{l},u_{r})
satisfies
a
-contraction. Likewise, this result holds for a given 3-shock wave
(\tilde{U}_{l},\tilde{U}_{r},\sigma_{3})
satisfying (
5.72
).

Proof.

First of all, we show that for any
0<a<1
, the backward 1-rarefaction wave
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
issuing from
U_{l}
intersects with the three dimensional surface
\Sigma_{a}
, i.e.,

\Sigma_{a}:=\{U~|~\eta(U|U_{l})=a\eta(U|U_{r})\}.

Since
dv\cdot r_{1}=1>0
,
v
is strictly monotone along the integral curve of
r_{1}
, which means that the 1-rarefaction wave can be parameterized by
v
. Moreover since
d\lambda_{1}\cdot r_{1}>0
,
-r_{1}
is the tangent vector of the backward 1-rarefaction wave
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
, which implies that
v
decreases along
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
. That is,
v_{+}\leq v_{l}
for all parameters
v_{+}
of
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
. Notice that
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
is well-defined for all
v_{+}\in(0,v_{l}]
, because
-r_{1}(W)
is smooth for all
W\in(0,\infty)\times(\mathbb{R}-\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Indeed, since

(5.73)

\alpha_{+}-c^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}+\sqrt{\Big(\frac{B^{2}+\beta^{2}}{v}-c^{2}\Big)^{2}+4\frac{B^{2}c^{2}}{v}}\Big]>0,

we have

(5.74)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} dB\cdot(-r_{1})=\frac{(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{B}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}>0\quad\mbox{if}~B>0\\
<0\quad\mbox{if}~B<0,\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

which implies that
B_{+}\neq 0
along
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
due to
B_{l}\neq 0
, thus
-r_{1}(W)
is smooth for all
W\in(0,\infty)\times(\mathbb{R}-\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We now use the fact that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mbox{for}~a<1,\quad\eta(U|U_{l})\leq a\eta(U|U_{r})\quad\mbox{is equivalent to}\\
&\eta(U)\leq\frac{1}{1-a}(\eta(U_{l})-a\eta(U_{r})-\nabla\eta(U_{l})\cdot U_{l}+a\nabla\eta(U_{r})\cdot U_{r}+(\nabla\eta(U_{l})-a\nabla\eta(U_{r}))\cdot U),\end{aligned}

which is rewritten as

(5.75)

\int_{v}^{\infty}p(s)ds+\frac{1}{2}(u^{2}+w^{2})+\frac{q^{2}}{2v}\leq c_{1}+c_{2}(v+q+u+w),

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}
. This implies that

\eta(U|U_{l})\leq a\eta(U|U_{r})\Longleftrightarrow v>c_{*}~\mbox{and}~|q|+|u|+|w|\leq c^{*}\quad\mbox{for some constants}~c_{*},~c^{*}>0,

since
\int_{0}^{\infty}p(s)ds=+\infty
, and the positive terms on
u
,
w
and
q
are quadratic in the left-hand side of (
5.75
). Therefore there exists
0<v_{*}\ll c_{*}
such that

\eta(R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}(v_{*})|U_{l})>a\eta(R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}(v_{*})|U_{r}),

which implies that
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
intersects with
\Sigma_{a}
for
a<1
, because
R_{U_{l}}^{1,-}
is a continuous curve issuing from
U_{l}\in\{U~|~\eta(U|U_{l})<a\eta(U|U_{r})\}
.

On the other hand, we show that the forward 4-rarefaction wave
R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}
issuing from
U_{r}
intersects with the surface
\Sigma_{a}
for any
a\geq 1
. Since
d\lambda_{4}\cdot r_{4}>0
and
dv\cdot r_{4}<0
,

(5.76)

r_{4}~\mbox{ is the tangent vector of the forward 4-rarefaction wave}~R_{U_{r}}^{4,+},

and the parameter
v_{+}
decreases along
R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}
. Moreover
R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}
is well-defined for all
v_{+}\in(0,v_{r}]
by the same reason as above.

Let us consider a continuous function

F_{a}(U):=\eta(U|U_{l})-a\eta(U|U_{r}).

We claim that

(5.77)

F_{1}(R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}(v_{*}))<0\quad\mbox{for some}~v_{*}\in(0,v_{r}].

Using (
5.76
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{dF_{1}(R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}(v_{+}))}{dv_{+}}&=(\nabla\eta(U_{r})-\nabla\eta(U_{l}))\cdot\frac{dR_{U_{r}}^{4,+}(v_{+})}{dv_{+}}\\
&=[p]+\Big[\frac{q^{2}}{2v^{2}}\Big]+\Big[\frac{q}{v}\Big]\frac{v(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{q_{+}}+[u]\sqrt{\alpha_{+}}-[w]\frac{v_{+}\beta(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{q_{+}\sqrt{\alpha_{+}}}.\end{aligned}

And (
5.69
) yields

\frac{dF_{1}(R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}(v_{+}))}{dv_{+}}=\underbrace{(\sigma_{2}+\sqrt{\alpha_{+}})[u]}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\frac{v_{+}(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{q_{+}}\Big(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\sigma_{2}\sqrt{\alpha_{+}}}\Big)\Big[\frac{q}{v}\Big]}_{I_{2}}.

Since (
5.73
) yields

(5.78)

\sqrt{\alpha_{+}}>\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{+})}=\sqrt{\gamma v_{+}^{-\gamma-1}}\to\infty\quad\mbox{as}~v_{+}\to 0+,

it follows from (
5.70
) that
I_{1}\to-\infty
as
v_{+}\to 0+
.

To control
I_{2}
, we use the condition (
5.71
). Since

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} dB\cdot r_{4}=\frac{(\alpha_{+}-c^{2})}{B}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}>0\quad\mbox{if}~B>0\\
<0\quad\mbox{if}~B<0,\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

if
B_{l}>B_{r}>0
,
\Big[\frac{q}{v}\Big]=[B]<0
and
q_{+}>0
. Since
\alpha_{+}\to+\infty
by (
5.78
), we have

I_{2}<0\quad\mbox{for}~v_{+}\ll 1.

This is also true in the case of
B_{l}\leq B_{r}<0
because of
\Big[\frac{q}{v}\Big]>0
and
q_{+}<0
.

Thus we have

\frac{dF(R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}(v_{+}))}{dv_{+}}\to-\infty\quad\mbox{as}~v_{+}\to 0+,

which implies (
5.77
).

Therefore we conclude that
R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}
intersects with
\Sigma_{a}
for any
a\geq 1
, because
F_{a}(U_{r})>0
and
F_{a}(R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}(v_{*}))<F_{1}(R_{U_{r}}^{4,+}(v_{*}))<0
for all
a\geq 1
.

Hence for all
a>0
, the 2-shock wave
(U_{l},U_{r},\sigma_{2})
does not satisfies
a
-contraction property thank to Theorem
5.1
.

Similarly we use the same arguments as above to show non-contraction for 3-shock wave
(\tilde{U}_{l},\tilde{U}_{r},\sigma_{3})
satisfying (
5.72
). More precisely, we can show that the backward 1-rarefaction wave
R_{\tilde{U}_{l}}^{1,-}
intersects with

\tilde{\Sigma}_{a}:=\{U~|~\eta(U|\tilde{U}_{l})=a\eta(U|\tilde{U}_{r})\}\quad\mbox{for any}~0<a\leq 1,

and the forward 4-rarefaction wave
R_{\tilde{U}_{r}}^{4,+}
intersects with
\tilde{\Sigma}_{a}
for any
a>1
. We omit the details.
∎

5.3.
Conditions via neighboring linearly degenerate fields

The following criterion is on the case where there are linearly degenerate fields as neighboring families of the intermediate entropic shock.

Theorem 5.3
.

Consider the system (
1.1
) satisfying
(\mathcal{A}_{1})
. Let
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
be a given
i
-th the entropic shock such that
u_{r}=S_{u_{l}}^{i}(s_{0})

\sigma_{u_{l}}(s_{0})=\sigma_{l,r}

s_{0}>0

•

(1) For

, we assume that for some

-th characteristic field is linearly degenerate, thus there exists the

-th Hugoniot curve

with

(5.81)

\frac{d}{ds}\sigma_{u_{r}}^{k}(s)=0,\quad\sigma_{u_{r}}^{k}(0)=\lambda_{k}(u_{r}).

•

(3) For

, we assume that one of the assumptions of (1) and (2) is satisfied. Then, the entropic shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
does not satisfy

-contraction.

Remark 5.2
.

For example, the condition (
5.80
) (resp. (
5.82
)) is geometrically satisfied by a case that
C^{1}
curve
S_{u_{l}}^{j}(s)
(resp.

(n-1)
-dimensional surface

.

Proof of Theorem
5.3

(1) Case of
0<a<1
:

Let us put
u_{-}:=u_{l}
,
u_{+}:=S_{u_{-}}^{j}(s_{1})
and
\sigma_{\pm}:=\sigma_{u_{-}}^{j}(s_{1})
, then the contact discontinuity
(u_{-},u_{+},\sigma_{\pm})
satisfies

\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})=0<a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})>a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r}).

We are going to show that the shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
does not satisfies the second condition
(\mathcal{H}2)
of
a-RES
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm}):=aq(u_{+},u_{r})-q(u_{-},u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm}(a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})-\eta(u_{-},u_{l}))>0.\end{aligned}

In fact, since
q(u_{-},u_{l})=\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})=0
by
u_{-}=u_{l}
, we have

D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})=a\Big(q(u_{+},u_{r})-\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{+}|u_{r})\Big).

We use the identity (
4.35
) and the Rankine-Hugoniot condition for the discontinuity
(u_{-},u_{+},\sigma_{\pm})
, to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})&=a\Big[q(u_{+},u_{l})+q(u_{l},u_{r})+(d\eta(u_{l})-d\eta(u_{r}))\cdot(f(u_{+})-f(u_{l}))\\
&\quad-\sigma_{\pm}\Big(\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})+\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})+(d\eta(u_{l})-d\eta(u_{r}))\cdot(u_{+}-u_{l})\Big)\Big]\\
&=a\Big[q(u_{+},u_{l})+q(u_{l},u_{r})-\sigma_{\pm}\Big(\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})+\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\Big)\Big],\end{aligned}

where the fact
u_{-}=u_{l}
is used above.

We decompose the above relation into three parts by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})&=a\Big(q(u_{l},u_{r})-\lambda_{j}(u_{l})\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\Big)+a(\lambda_{j}(u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm})\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\\
&\quad+a\Big(q(u_{+},u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})\Big)\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{aligned}

Following the same argument as (
5.63
) by using (
4.36
) and the fact
\lambda_{j}(u_{l})<\lambda_{i}(u_{l})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}=a\Big[\Big(q(u_{l},u_{r})-\sigma_{l,r}\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\Big)+(\sigma_{l,r}-\lambda_{j}(u_{l}))\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})\Big]>0.\end{aligned}

Since
\lambda_{j}(u_{l})=\sigma_{u_{l}}^{j}(0)=\sigma_{u_{l}}^{j}(s_{1})=\sigma_{\pm}
by (
5.79
), we have

I_{2}=a(\lambda_{j}(u_{l})-\sigma_{\pm})\eta(u_{l}|u_{r})=0.

We use (
4.36
) together with (
5.79
) to get

I_{3}=a\Big(q(u_{+},u_{-})-\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{+}|u_{-})\Big)=a\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\frac{d}{ds}\sigma_{u_{-}}^{j}(s)\eta(u_{-}|S_{u_{-}}^{j}(s))ds=0.

Therefore we have

D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})>0.

Hence the shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
is not
a-RES
, which provides the conclusion by Theorem
3.2
.

(2) Case of
a>1
:
We follow the same argument as above by considering
S_{u_{r}}^{k}
instead of
S_{u_{l}}^{j}
. By (
5.82
), we have

\eta(u_{-}|u_{l})<a\eta(u_{-}|u_{r})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta(u_{+}|u_{l})>0=a\eta(u_{+}|u_{r}).

for the contact discontinuity
(u_{-},u_{+},\sigma_{\pm})
where
u_{+}:=u_{r}
,
u_{-}:=S_{u_{+}}^{k}(s_{2})
and
\sigma_{\pm}:=\sigma_{u_{+}}^{k}(s_{2})
.

Since
q(u_{+},u_{r})=\eta(u_{+},u_{r})=0
by
u_{+}=u_{r}
, we have

D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})=-q(u_{-},u_{l})+\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{-},u_{l}).

Using (
4.35
) and the Rankine-Hugoniot condition of the discontinuity
(u_{-},u_{+},\sigma_{\pm})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})=-q(u_{-},u_{+})-q(u_{r},u_{l})+\sigma_{\pm}\Big(\eta(u_{-}|u_{+})+\eta(u_{r}|u_{l})\Big),\end{aligned}

where the fact
u_{+}=u_{r}
is used above.

We decompose the above relation into three parts by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})&=-\Big(q(u_{r},u_{l})-\lambda_{k}(u_{r})\eta(u_{r}|u_{l})\Big)+(\sigma_{\pm}-\lambda_{k}(u_{r}))\eta(u_{r}|u_{l})\\
&\quad-\Big(q(u_{-},u_{+})-\sigma_{\pm}\eta(u_{-}|u_{+})\Big)\\
&=:J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.\end{aligned}

By the same argument as the first case, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{1}=-\Big(q(u_{r},u_{l})-\sigma_{l,r}\eta(u_{r},u_{l})\Big)+(\lambda_{k}(u_{r})-\sigma_{l,r})\eta(u_{r},u_{l})>0.\end{aligned}

Since
\lambda_{k}(u_{r})=\sigma_{u_{r}}^{k}(0)=\sigma_{u_{r}}^{k}(s_{2})=\sigma_{\pm}
by (
5.81
), we have
J_{2}=0
.

We use (
4.36
) together with (
5.81
) to get

J_{3}=-\int_{0}^{s_{2}}\frac{d}{ds}\sigma_{u_{+}}^{k}(s)\eta(u_{+}|S_{u_{+}}^{k}(s))ds=0.

Therefore we have

D_{RH}(u_{l,r};u_{\pm})>0.

(3) Case of
a=1
:
Since the proof of two cases above does not depend on the strength of
a
, if one of the assumptions of (1) and (2) is satisfied, we end up with no
a
-contraction of the entropic shock
(u_{l},u_{r},\sigma_{l,r})
.

Application to gas dynamics
As an application of Theorem
6.1
, we here find weights
a>0
, on which the contact discontinuity of full Euler system for a perfect gas does not satisfy
a
-contraction. The
3\times 3
full Euler system reads

(6.85)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho v)=0\\
\partial_{t}(\rho v)+\partial_{x}(\rho v^{2}+p)=0\\
\partial_{t}(\rho(\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+e))+\partial_{x}(\rho(\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+e)v+pv)=0,\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

Here, the equation of state for a perfect gas is given by

(6.86)

p=(\gamma-1)\rho e,

where
\gamma>1
.

We consider the conservative variables
\rho
,
q:=\rho v
and
\mathcal{E}:=\rho(\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+e)
and put
u:=(\rho,q,\mathcal{E})
, and the entropy

(6.87)

\eta(u)=(\gamma-1)\rho\mbox{ln}\rho-\rho\mbox{ln}e,

where
e
is given by
e=\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\rho}-\frac{q^{2}}{2\rho^{2}}
in conservative variables.

By a straightforward computation (See for example
[
45
]
), the three characteristic fields are given as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{1}=v-c,\quad r_{1}=\Big(-\rho,c,-\rho^{-1}p\Big)^{T},\\
&\lambda_{2}=v,\quad r_{2}=\Big(-\partial_{e}p,0,-\partial_{\rho}p\Big)^{T},\\
&\lambda_{3}=v+c,\quad r_{3}=\Big(\rho,c,\rho^{-1}p\Big)^{T},\end{aligned}

where
c:=\sqrt{\gamma(\gamma-1)e}
denotes the sound speed.

Thus since
d\lambda_{i}\cdot r_{i}>0
for
i=1,3
and
d\lambda_{2}\cdot r_{2}=0
, the first and third characteristic fields are genuinely non-linear, whereas the second characteristic field is linearly degenerate. Let
(u_{l},u_{r})
be 2-contact discontinuity, then we have

(6.88)

v_{l}=v_{r},\quad p_{l}=p_{r}.

We refer to
[
45
]
for these relations.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(u_{l},u_{r})
be the 2-contact discontinuity for the system (
6.85
)-(
6.86
). Then, there is no weight
a
for the range of either

(6.89)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &0<a<\frac{e_{r}}{e_{l}},~a>1,\quad\mbox{when}~e_{l}>e_{r},\quad\mbox{or}\\
&a<1,~a>\frac{e_{r}}{e_{l}},\quad\mbox{when}~e_{l}<e_{r},\end{aligned}

such that
(u_{l},u_{r})
satisfies
a
-contraction.

Proof.

We show that the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
are satisfied for
a
belonging to (
6.89
). For that, we separate the range of weight
a>0
into two cases.

i) Case of either
0<a<\frac{e_{r}}{e_{l}}
when
e_{l}>e_{r}
or
a<1
when
e_{l}<e_{r}
.
For such
a
, we are going to show that the first backward rarefaction wave
R_{u_{l}}^{1,-}
issuing from
u_{l}
intersects with the surface
\Sigma_{a}
.
As in
[
45
]
, it turned out that the first backward rarefaction wave
R_{u_{l}}^{1,-}
starting from
u_{l}
can be parametrized by the pressure
p_{+}
as follows.

(6.90)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &R_{u_{l}}^{1,-}~\mbox{is parametrized as}~p_{l}\leq p_{+},\\
&\rho_{+}=\phi(p_{+};\rho_{l},p_{l}),\quad\mbox{where $\phi$ is increasing in the first argument},\\
&v_{+}=v_{l}-\int_{p_{l}}^{p_{+}}\frac{dp}{\rho c},\end{aligned}

where
c
is the speed of sound, in particular
c=\sqrt{\gamma(\gamma-1)e}
for a perfect gas. That is, along
R_{u_{l}}^{1,-}
, the pressure
p_{+}
and density
\rho_{+}
increase while the velocity
v_{+}
decrease. Then, using

(6.91)

\rho c=\rho\sqrt{\gamma(\gamma-1)e}=\sqrt{\gamma\rho p}\quad\mbox{by \eqref{pressure} and \eqref{1-rare}},

we have

v_{+}=v_{l}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\int_{p_{l}}^{p_{+}}\frac{dp}{\sqrt{\rho p}}.

Multiplying by
\sqrt{\rho_{+}}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{+}&=\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{l}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\int_{p_{l}}^{p_{+}}\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{+}}{\rho}}\frac{dp}{\sqrt{p}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{l}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\int_{p_{l}}^{p_{+}}\frac{dp}{\sqrt{p}}\\
&=\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{l}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\gamma}}(\sqrt{p_{+}}-\sqrt{p_{l}}),\end{aligned}

where we have used
\rho_{+}\geq\rho
thanks to (
6.90
).

If
\rho_{+}
is bounded in
p_{+}
, then we have

\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{+}\rightarrow-\infty\quad\mbox{as}~p_{+}\rightarrow\infty,

which implies

(6.92)

\mbox{either}\quad\rho_{+}\rightarrow\infty\quad\mbox{or}\quad\mathcal{E}_{+}\rightarrow\infty\quad\mbox{as}~p_{+}\rightarrow\infty.

This means that the either density or energy should be unbounded along the first backward rarefaction wave
R_{u_{l}}^{1,-}
issuing from
u_{l}
.

On the other hand, it turns out that
u_{l}
is enclosed by the surface
\Sigma_{a}
in the case of either
0<a<\frac{e_{r}}{e_{l}}
when
e_{l}>e_{r}
or
a<1
when
e_{l}<e_{r}
as follows.

Recall (
4.42
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mbox{for}~a<1,\quad\eta(u|u_{l})\leq a\eta(u|u_{r})\quad\mbox{is equivalent to}\\
&\eta(u)\leq\frac{1}{1-a}(\eta(u_{l})-a\eta(u_{r})-\nabla\eta(u_{l})\cdot u_{l}+a\nabla\eta(u_{r})\cdot u_{r}+(\nabla\eta(u_{l})-a\nabla\eta(u_{r}))\cdot u).\end{aligned}

Since the entropy (
6.87
) can be rewritten as

(6.93)

\eta(u)=\gamma\rho\mbox{ln}\rho-\rho\mbox{ln}(\mathcal{E}-\frac{q^{2}}{2\rho}),

using
\rho e=\mathcal{E}-\frac{q^{2}}{2\rho}
, we have

(6.94)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{\rho}\eta(u)=\gamma(\mbox{ln}\rho+1)-\mbox{ln}(\rho e)-\frac{v^{2}}{2e},\\
&\partial_{q}\eta(u)=\frac{v}{e},\\
&\partial_{\mathcal{E}}\eta(u)=-\frac{1}{e}.\end{aligned}

We use (
6.86
), (
6.88
) and (
6.94
) to compute

(6.95)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\nabla\eta(u_{l})-a\nabla\eta(u_{r}))\cdot u&=(\partial_{\rho}\eta(u_{l})-a\partial_{\rho}\eta(u_{r}))\rho\\
&\quad+(\partial_{q}\eta(u_{l})-a\partial_{q}\eta(u_{r}))q+\Big(\frac{a}{e_{r}}-\frac{1}{e_{l}}\Big)\mathcal{E}.\end{aligned}

By (
4.42
), (
6.93
) and (
6.95
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Big(\gamma\mbox{ln}\rho-\mbox{ln}(\mathcal{E}-\frac{q^{2}}{2\rho})-c_{1}\Big)\rho+c_{3}\mathcal{E}\leq c_{2}q+c_{4},\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &c_{1}=\frac{1}{1-a}(\partial_{\rho}\eta(u_{l})-a\partial_{\rho}\eta(u_{r})),\\
&c_{2}=\frac{1}{1-a}(\partial_{q}\eta(u_{l})-a\partial_{q}\eta(u_{r})),\\
&c_{3}=-\frac{1}{1-a}\Big(\frac{a}{e_{r}}-\frac{1}{e_{l}}\Big),\\
&c_{4}=\frac{1}{1-a}(\eta(u_{l})-a\eta(u_{r})-\nabla\eta(u_{l})\cdot u_{l}+a\nabla\eta(u_{r})\cdot u_{r}).\end{aligned}

Since
c_{3}>0
in the case of either
0<a<\frac{e_{r}}{e_{l}}
when
e_{l}>e_{r}
or
a<1
when
e_{l}<e_{r}
, we use
c_{2}q\leq\frac{c_{3}\rho v^{2}}{4}+c_{5}\rho
by Young’s inequality, to get

\underbrace{\Big(\gamma\mbox{ln}\rho-\mbox{ln}(\mathcal{E}-\frac{q^{2}}{2\rho})-c_{1}-c_{5}\Big)\rho+\frac{c_{3}}{2}\mathcal{E}}_{L}\leq c_{4}.

Notice that
c_{4}
is constant while

L\to\infty\quad\mbox{as}~\mathcal{E}\to\infty~\mbox{or}~\rho\to\infty,

which implies that
\{u~|~\eta(u|u_{l})\leq a\eta(u|u_{r})\}
is bounded. Thus
u_{l}
is enclosed by
\Sigma_{a}
.
Therefore by this and (
6.92
), we conclude that the first backward rarefaction wave
R_{u_{l}}^{1,-}
has to intersect with the
(n-1)
-dimensional surface
\Sigma_{a}
.

ii) Case of either
a>1
when
e_{l}>e_{r}
or
a>\frac{e_{r}}{e_{l}}
when
e_{l}<e_{r}
.
For such
a
, we use the same argument as previous to show that the third forward rarefaction wave
R_{u_{r}}^{3,+}
issuing from
u_{r}
intersects with the surface
\Sigma_{a}
. Since the third forward rarefaction wave
R_{u_{r}}^{3,+}
starting from
u_{r}
can be parametrized by the pressure
p_{+}
as:

(6.96)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &R_{u_{r}}^{3,+}~\mbox{is parametrized as}~p_{r}\leq p_{+},\\
&\rho_{+}=\phi(p_{+};\rho_{r},p_{r}),\quad\mbox{where $\phi$ is increasing in the first argument},\\
&v_{+}=v_{r}+\int_{p_{r}}^{p_{+}}\frac{dp}{\rho c},\end{aligned}

the pressure
p_{+}
, density
\rho_{+}
and the velocity
v_{+}
all increase along
R_{u_{r}}^{3,+}
.
Then by (
6.91
),

v_{+}-v_{r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\int_{p_{r}}^{p_{+}}\frac{dp}{\sqrt{\rho p}}.

Multiplying by
\sqrt{\rho_{+}}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{+}&=\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{r}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\int_{p_{r}}^{p_{+}}\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{+}}{\rho}}\frac{dp}{\sqrt{p}}\\
&\geq\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{r}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\int_{p_{r}}^{p_{+}}\frac{dp}{\sqrt{p}}\\
&=\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{r}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\gamma}}(\sqrt{p_{+}}-\sqrt{p_{r}}),\end{aligned}

where we have used
\rho_{+}\geq\rho
thanks to (
6.96
). If
\rho_{+}
is bounded in
p_{+}

\sqrt{\rho_{+}}v_{+}\rightarrow\infty\quad\mbox{as}~p_{+}\rightarrow\infty,

which implies

(6.97)

\mbox{either}\quad\rho_{+}\rightarrow\infty\quad\mbox{or}\quad\mathcal{E}_{+}\rightarrow\infty\quad\mbox{as}~p_{+}\rightarrow\infty.

This means that the either density or energy should be unbounded along the third forward rarefaction wave

u_{r}

On the other hand, by the same computation as previous case,

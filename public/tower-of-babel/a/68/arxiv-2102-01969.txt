Greatest HITs: Higher inductive types in coinductive definitions via induction under clocks Conference: 37th Annual ACM/IEEE Symposium on Logic in Computer Science (LICS); August 2–5, 2022; Haifa, Israel 37th Annual ACM/IEEE Symposium on Logic in Computer Science (LICS) (LICS ’22), August 2–5, 2022, Haifa, Israel
Magnus Baunsgaard Kristensen, Rasmus Ejlers Møgelberg, Andrea Vezzosi

Abstract
. We present Clocked Cubical Type Theory, the first type theory combining multi-clocked guarded recursion with the features of Cubical Type Theory. Guarded recursion is an abstract form of step-indexing, which can be used for construction of advanced programming language models. In its multi-clocked version, it can also be used for coinductive programming and reasoning, encoding productivity in types. Combining this with Higher Inductive Types (HITs) the encoding extends to coinductive types that are traditionally hard to represent in type theory, such as the type of finitely branching labelled transition systems. Among our technical contributions is a new principle of induction under clocks , providing computational content to one of the main axioms required for encoding coinductive types. This principle is verified using a denotational semantics in a presheaf model.



1.
Introduction

Homotopy type theory
(
Univalent Foundations Program 2013
)
is the extension of
Martin-Löf type theory
(
Martin-Löf 1984
)
with
the univalence axiom and higher inductive types. It can be seen as a
foundation for mathematics, allowing for synthetic approaches to
mathematics, as exemplified by synthetic homotopy theory, as well
as more direct formalisations of mathematics, closer to the traditional set-theory
based developments. Higher Inductive Types (HITs) play a key role in both approaches:
In synthetic homotopy theory these are used to describe spaces
such as the spheres and the torus, and in the more direct formalisations,
HITs express free structures and quotients, both of which have traditionally
been hard to represent and use in type theory.

The kind of mathematics that is particularly important to the LICS community
often involves reasoning about recursion, and in particular programming with
and reasoning about coinductive types. Doing this in type theory has historically
been difficult for a number of reasons. One is that the definitions of the functors
of interest in coalgebra often involve constructions such as finite or
countable powerset (for modelling non-determinism),
or quotients. All of these are known to be representable as HITs
(
Frumin et al. 2018
;
Chapman et al. 2019
;
Univalent Foundations Program 2013
)
.
Another reason is that programming and reasoning about coinductive types
involves productivity checking, which in most proof assistants is implemented
as non-modular syntactic checks that can lead to considerable overhead
for programmers
(
Danielsson 2010
)
. A third reason is that bisimilarity, which is the
natural notion of identity for coinductive types, rarely (at least provably)
coincides with the built-in identity types of type theory, and so proofs
cannot be transported along bisimilarity proofs.

Multi-clocked guarded recursion
(
Atkey and McBride 2013
)
allows for the productivity
requirement for coinductive definitions to be encoded in types. The key ingredient
in this is a modal operator
\triangleright
(pronounced ‘later’) indexed by clocks
\kappa
,
encoding a notion of time-steps in types. Guarded recursive types such as

\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}\simeq\mathbb{N}\times\triangleright^{\kappa}\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}
are recursive types in which the
recursion variable occurs only under a
\triangleright
.
In the case of
\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}
the type equivalence above
states that the head of a guarded stream is available now, but the tail only after
a time step on clock
\kappa
. Guarded streams can be defined using a fixed
point operator
\mathsf{fix}^{\kappa}
of type
(\triangleright^{\kappa}A\to A)\to A
. In the case of

\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}
the delay in the domain of the input precisely captures the productivity
requirement for programming with streams. Guarded recursive types can themselves
be encoded as fixed points on a universe. Using quantification over clocks, the coinductive type of streams can be encoded as

\mathsf{Str}\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\forall\kappa.\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}
.

Guarded recursion
(
Nakano 2000
)
can also be used as an abstract
approach to step-indexing techniques
(
Appel and McAllester 2001
;
Appel et al. 2007
)
.
In particular,
the type variables in guarded recursive types can also appear in negative positions,
and so can be used to provide models of untyped lambda calculus or solve the
advanced type equations needed for modelling higher-order store
(
Birkedal et al. 2012
)

and advanced notions of separation logic
(
Jung et al. 2018
)
.
A type theory combining guarded recursion with homotopy type theory will
therefore provide a powerful framework, not just for coinductive reasoning
but also for reasoning about recursion and advanced programming language
features beyond the reach of traditional domain theory.

1.1.
Clocked Cubical Type Theory

This paper presents Clocked Cubical Type Theory (CCTT), the first type theory
to combine all the above mentioned features: Multi-clocked guarded recursion,
HITs and univalence. Rather than basing this on the original
formulation of homotopy type theory we build on Cubical Type
Theory (CTT)
(
Cohen et al. 2018
)
, a variant of HoTT based on the cubical model of
type theory, and implemented in the Cubical Agda
proof assistant
(
Vezzosi et al. 2019
)
. One reason for this choice is that
the operational properties of CTT as well as the concrete implementation
give hope for an implementation of our type theory.
In fact we have already extended the implementation of Guarded Cubical
Agda
(
Veltri and Vezzosi 2020
)
to support most of the new constructions of CCTT.
Another is that the path types of
CTT make for a simple
implementation of the extensionality principle for
the
\triangleright^{\kappa}
modality
(
Birkedal et al. 2019
)
.

CCTT extends CTT with the constructions
of Clocked Type Theory (CloTT)
(
Bahr. et al. 2017
)
, a type theory for multi-clocked
guarded recursion. CloTT uses a Fitch-style approach to programming with the
\triangleright

modality. This means that elements of modal type are introduced by abstracting
over tick variables
\alpha:\kappa
and eliminated by application to ticks. One benefit of this
is that let-expressions are avoided, and these do not interact well with dependent types in general.
For example, one can define a dependently typed generalisation of applicative action

(
McBride and Paterson 2008
)
operator of type

(1)

\displaystyle\triangleright^{\kappa}({{\textstyle\prod\left(x:A\right).B}})\to{\textstyle\prod\left(y:\triangleright^{\kappa}A\right).{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).B[y\,[\alpha]/x]}

In this term the tick variable
\alpha
should be thought of as evidence that time has passed, which
is used in subterms like
y\,[\alpha]
to access the element of type
A
delivered by
y:\triangleright^{\kappa}A
in
the next time step. CloTT has a single tick constant
\diamond
that can be used for a controlled
elimination of
\triangleright
.
Bahr. et al. 2017
define a strongly normalising operational semantics for
CloTT, in which
\diamond
unfolds fixed point operations. Since tick variables
\alpha
can be substituted
by
\diamond
, their identity is crucial for normalisation. For reasoning in the type theory, however, it is often
necessary that
y\,[\alpha]
does not depend on
\alpha
up to path equality. This is referred to as the

tick irrelevance axiom
.

When adding such axioms to a univalent type theory one should be take care to avoid
introducing extra structure that one will eventually need to reason about.
Ideally, an axiom like tick irrelevance
should state that the type of ticks is propositional, i.e., that any two elements can be identified, any two
paths between elements can be identified, and so on for higher dimensions.
In CCTT, there appears to be no such way of formulating the axiom,
because ticks do not form a type. This paper shows how to obtain tick irrelevance
by enriching the language of ticks
with paths
\mathsf{tirr}\,u\,v
between any pair of ticks
u,v
. This has the additional benefit that it allows for
rules giving computational content to the axiom.

1.2.
Induction under clocks

The encoding of coinductive streams from guarded streams mentioned above generalises to an
encoding of coinductive solutions to type equations of the form
X\simeq F(X)
for all functors
F

commuting with clock quantification in the sense that the canonical map

(2)

F(\forall\kappa.X)\to\forall\kappa.F(X)

is an equivalence of types.
Note that this is a semantic condition, rather than the more standard grammars for
coinductive types. For this to be useful, one must have a large supply of such functors,
including functors formed using inductive and higher inductive types.
This property has previously been obtained via axioms
(
Atkey and McBride 2013
)
.
Here we formulate a principle of

induction under clocks
, which gives computational content to these
axioms also in the case of higher inductive types.

Consider for example the finite powerset
{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}
which
can be defined as a HIT given by constructors for empty set, singletons, union,
equalities stating associativity, commutativity and idempotency of union as well as an
axiom forcing
{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}(X)
to be a hset
(
Frumin et al. 2018
)
. In this case induction under clocks
states that to inhabit a family
over an element of type
\forall\kappa.{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}{(X)}

it suffices to inductively describe the cases for elements of the form

\lambda\kappa.\mathsf{con}_{i}(p)
for each constructor
\mathsf{con}_{i}
for
{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}
, i.e., for

\lambda\kappa.\emptyset,\lambda\kappa.\{a\},\lambda\kappa.x\,[\kappa]\cup y\,[\kappa]

and for equality
constructors such as
\lambda\kappa.\mathsf{idem}(x\,[\kappa],r)
, where

\mathsf{idem}
implements idempotency of union. In these cases,
x,y
are
assumed to be of type
\forall\kappa.{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}(X)
. Using this, one can construct
an inverse to (
2
) for
F={\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}
, and also prove that both
compositions are identities. As a consequence, the coinductive solution to

{\mathsf{LTS}}\simeq{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}(A\times{\mathsf{LTS}})
can be encoded as

{\mathsf{LTS}}\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\forall\kappa.{\mathsf{LTS}^{\kappa}}

where
{\mathsf{LTS}^{\kappa}}\simeq{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}(A\times\triangleright^{\kappa}{\mathsf{LTS}^{\kappa}})

is a guarded recursive type.
{\mathsf{LTS}}
is the type of
A
-labelled finitely
branching transition systems. We moreover prove that path equality
for this type coincides with bisimilarity.

The results for
{\mathsf{LTS}}
mentioned above hold for all types of labels
A
that are
clock-irrelevant
,
meaning that the map
A\to\forall\kappa.A
is an equivalence for
\kappa
fresh.
In previous type theories for multi-clocked guarded recursion clock-irrelevance of all types
has been taken as an axiom. Most types are clock-irrelevant, but ensuring this for universes
requires special care both syntactically and semantically
(
Bizjak and Møgelberg 2020
)
. In this paper
we opt for a simpler solution, taking clock-irrelevance to be a side condition to the
correctness of the encoding of coinductive types. Using the principle of induction
under clocks we show that HITs constructed using clock-irrelevant types are themselves
clock-irrelevant, an important step towards reintroducing clock irrelevance as an axiom.

1.3.
Denotational semantics

Our type theory is justified by a denotational semantics in the form
of a presheaf model. This model combines previous models of
CTT
(
Cohen et al. 2018
;
Orton and Pitts 2018
)
and CloTT
(
Mannaa et al. 2020
)
. A key challenge for
the construction of this model is the definition of a composition
structure on the operation modelling
\triangleright
. We give a general theorem
for defining composition structures for dependent
right adjoints
(
Clouston et al. 2020
)
which can be used to model other type theories
combining Fitch-style modal operators
(
Clouston 2018
)
with Cubical Type Theory.

Overview of paper

The paper is organised as follows. We start by recalling
Cubical Type Theory in
section 2
, and
section 3
extends this
to CCTT.
The encoding of coinductive types using guarded
recursion is recalled in
section 4

which also details the example of labelled transition systems.
The syntax for HITs in CCTT is presented in
section 5
, which also states
the principle of induction under clocks.
The denotational semantics of Clocked
Cubical Type Theory is sketched in
section 6
.
Finally, we discuss related work in
section 7
, and
conclude and discuss future work in
section 8
.

2.
Cubical Type Theory

Cubical Type Theory
(
Cohen et al. 2018
)
is an extension of
Martin-Löf type theory which gives a computational interpretation
to extensionality principles like function extensionality and
univalence. It does so by internalizing the notion from Homotopy Type
Theory that equalities are paths, by explicitly representing them as
maps from an interval object.
The interval,
\mathbb{I}
, is not a type but contexts can contain
i:\mathbb{I}
assumptions and there is a judgement,
\Gamma\vdash r:\mathbb{I}
which specifies that
r
is built according to this grammar

r,s::=0\mid 1\mid i\mid 1-r\mid r\wedge s\mid r\vee s

where
i
is taken from the assumptions in
\Gamma
. Equality of two
interval elements,
\Gamma\vdash r\equiv s:\mathbb{I}
corresponds to the
laws of De Morgan algebras. A good intuition is to think of

\mathbb{I}
as the real interval
[0,1]
with
r\wedge s
and
r\vee s

given by minimum and maximum operations.

Given
x,y:A
, we write
\mathsf{Path}_{A}(x,y)
for the type of paths between
x
and
y
, which correspond to maps from
\mathbb{I}
into
A
which are equal to
x
or
y
when applied to the endpoints
0,1
of the interval
\mathbb{I}
.

\frac{\Gamma,i:\mathbb{I}\vdash t:A}{\Gamma\vdash\lambda i.\,t:\mathsf{Path}_{A}(t[0/i],t[1/i])}\frac{\Gamma\vdash p:\mathsf{Path}_{A}(a_{0},a_{1})\quad\Gamma\vdash r:\mathbb{I}}{\Gamma\vdash p\,r:A}

We sometimes write
=_{A}
or simply
=
as infix notation for path equality, and use
\equiv
for judgemental equality.
Path types support the
\beta
and
\eta
equalities familiar from function types, but we also have that a path applied to
0
or
1
reduces according to the endpoints specified in its type.

(\lambda i.\,t)\,r\equiv t[r/i]p\equiv(\lambda i.\,p\,i)p\,0\equiv a_{0}p\,1\equiv a_{1}

The path witnessing reflexivity is defined as
\mathsf{refl}\,x\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\lambda i.\,x:\mathsf{Path}_{A}(x,x)
, given any
x:A
.
It is also straightforward to provide a proof of function extensionality, as it is just a matter of reordering arguments:

\frac{\Gamma\vdash p:\Pi(x:A).\,{\mathsf{Path}_{B}(f\,x,g\,x)}}{\Gamma\vdash\lambda i.\,\lambda x.\,p\,x\,i:\mathsf{Path}_{\Pi(x:A).{B}}(f,g)}

Other constructions like transitivity, or more generally transport along path, require a
further primitive operation called composition, which we now recall.

Define first the
face lattice

\mathbb{F}
, as the free
distributive lattice with generators
(i=0)
and
(i=1)
, and
satisfying the equality
(i=0)\wedge(i=1)=0_{\mathbb{F}}
. Explicitly, elements
\Gamma\vdash\varphi:\mathbb{F}
are given by the following grammar, where
i
ranges over the interval variables from
\Gamma
:

\varphi,\psi::=0_{\mathbb{F}}\mid 1_{\mathbb{F}}\mid(i=0)\mid(i=1)\mid\varphi\wedge\psi\mid\varphi\vee\psi

Given
\Gamma\vdash\varphi:\mathbb{F}
we can form the restricted context

\Gamma,\varphi
. Types and terms in context
\Gamma,\varphi
are called

partial
, matching the intuition that they are only defined for
elements of
\Gamma
that satisfy the constraints specified by
\varphi
.
For example while a type
i:\mathbb{I}\vdash A\,\operatorname{type}
corresponds to a whole
line, a type
i:\mathbb{I},(i=0)\vee(i=1)\vdash B\,\operatorname{type}
is merely two
disconnected points. Given a partial element
\Gamma,\varphi\vdash u:A
we write
\Gamma\vdash t:A[\varphi\mapsto u]
to mean the
conjunction of
\Gamma\vdash t:A
and
\Gamma,\varphi\vdash t\equiv u:A
. Likewise we write
\Gamma\vdash A[\varphi\mapsto B]\,\operatorname{type}
to mean

\Gamma\vdash A\,\operatorname{type}
and
\Gamma,\varphi\vdash A\equiv B\,\operatorname{type}
.

Composition is given by the following typing rule:

\frac{\Gamma\vdash\varphi:\mathbb{F}\quad\Gamma,i:\mathbb{I}\vdash A\,\operatorname{type}\quad\Gamma,\varphi,i:\mathbb{I}\vdash u:A\quad\Gamma\vdash u_{0}:A[0/i][\varphi\mapsto u[0/i]]}{\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{comp}^{i}_{A}\,[\varphi\mapsto u]\,u_{0}:A[1/i][\varphi\mapsto u[1/i]]}

the inputs
u
and
u_{0}
represent the sides and the base of an open
box, while composition gives us the lid, i.e. the side opposite to the
base. The behaviour of composition is specified by
u
whenever
\varphi

is satisfied, and otherwise depends on the type
A
, consequently when
we introduce new type formers we will also give a corresponding
equation for
\mathsf{comp}
.
As an example we can prove transitivity for paths with the following term:

\frac{\Gamma\vdash p:\mathsf{Path}_{A}(x,y)\quad\Gamma\vdash q:\mathsf{Path}_{A}(y,z)}{\Gamma\vdash\lambda i.\,\mathsf{comp}^{j}\,[i=0\mapsto x,i=1\mapsto q\,j]\,(p\,i):\mathsf{Path}_{A}(x,z)}

The standalone syntax for partial elements is as a list of faces and
terms
[\varphi_{1}~u_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}~u_{n}]
, but we will write
[\vee_{i}\varphi_{1}\mapsto[\varphi_{1}~u_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}~u_{n}]]

as
[\varphi_{1}\mapsto u_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}\mapsto u_{n}]
.
We refer to
Cohen et al. 2018
for more details on partial elements,
composition, and the
glueing construction
.
The latter is what allows to derive the univalence principle as a
theorem, by turning a partial equivalence into a total one.
Writing
A\simeq B
for the type stating that
A
and
B
are
equivalent
(
Univalent Foundations Program 2013
)
, univalence states that the canonical map of type
A=_{\mathsf{U}}B\to A\simeq B
is itself an equivalence for any types
A,B:\mathsf{U}
.
We will use univalence in the rest of the paper, but we will not need
to refer to a specific implementation, so we omit the details in this
brief overview.

From Homotopy Type Theory
(
Univalent Foundations Program 2013
)
we also recall the notion of homotopy

proposition
which are types for which any two elements are path
equal, and homotopy
set
which are types whose path types are
propositions.
Given any type
A
its propositional truncation
\lVert{A}\rVert_{0}
is the
least proposition that admits a map from
A
.
It can be defined as an Higher Inductive Type (HIT), which are inductive
types defined by providing generators for both the type itself and its
path equality. Another example of a HIT is the finite powerset
(
Frumin et al. 2018
)
of
a type
A
mentioned in the introduction.
We will discuss HITs in more detail in
section 5
.

CCTT extends CTT with the constructions of
CloTT
(
Bahr. et al. 2017
;
Mannaa et al. 2020
)
, a type theory with
Nakano style guarded recursion, multiple clocks and ticks. This means that
the delay type operator is associated with clocks:
\triangleright^{\kappa}A
is the type of data
of type
A
which is available in the next time step on clock
\kappa
. There are no
operations on clocks, they can only be assumed by placing assumptions such as

\kappa:\mathsf{clock}
in the context, and abstracted to form elements of type

\forall\kappa.A
. The status of
\mathsf{clock}
is similar to
\mathbb{I}
in the sense
that it is not a type, and so, e.g.,
\mathsf{clock}\to\mathsf{clock}
is not a
wellformed type. It is sometimes convenient to assume a single
clock constant
\kappa_{0}
, and this can be achieved by a precompilation adding this
to the context. Similarly to function types, path equality in
\forall\kappa.\,A
is equivalent
to pointwise equality, and clock quantification preserves truncation levels.

Ticks are evidence that time has passed on a given clock. Tick assumptions in a context
separate a judgement such as
\Gamma,\alpha:\kappa,\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash t:A

into a part (
\Gamma
) of assumptions arriving before the time tick
\alpha
on the
clock
\kappa
and the rest of the judgement that happens after. The introduction
rule for
{{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).A}
in
Figure 1
can therefore be read
as stating that if
t
has type
A
one time step after the assumptions in
\Gamma

then
\char 12\relax\alpha\kappa t
is delayed data of type
A
. Because terms can appear
in types,
\alpha
can appear in

, and must therefore
be mentioned in the type of
\char 12\relax\alpha\kappa t
. In many cases, however,
it does not, and we will then write simply
\triangleright^{\kappa}A
for
{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).A
.

Elimination for
{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).A
is by application to ticks. There are two forms of ticks:
simple ticks and forcing ticks. The judgement for simple ticks

{\Gamma}\vdash{u}:{\kappa}\leadsto{\Gamma^{\prime}}

states that

is a tick on the clock
\kappa
in context
\Gamma
with

\Gamma^{\prime}
. The residual context consists of the assumptions that were available
before the tick

\alpha

the left of
\alpha
as well as the

assumptions to the right of
\alpha

Timeless assumptions are interval and clock assumptions (

and
\kappa:\mathsf{clock}
) as well as all faces, and
{\mathsf{TL}(\Gamma)}

computes the timeless assumptions of
\Gamma
. The assumption
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\sqsubseteq\Gamma

defined by the rule

\Gamma,{\mathsf{TL}(\Gamma^{\prime})}\sqsubseteq\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}

allows for further trimming of the residual context.
The term

applies

to the simple tick
u
. The assumption that

is well-typed in the
residual context
\Gamma^{\prime}
should be read as stating that it has the type
{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).A

before the tick
u
, and can therefore be opened after the tick

to produce something of

. We will often omit the residual context

from the term, writing

As basic examples of programming

and the unit

The timelessness of the interval is needed to type

3.1.
Forcing ticks

Unrestricted elimination of
\triangleright
is unsound, since any type
A
with a map

\triangleright^{\kappa}A\to A
can be inhabited using fixed points. However, it is safe to
eliminate
\triangleright^{\kappa}
in contexts of the form
\Gamma,\kappa:\mathsf{clock}
,
i.e., where no variables mention
\kappa
in their type. To close such an elimination
rule under substitutions, the clock variable
\kappa
must be abstracted in the term.

Application to forcing ticks does exactly that:
If
\kappa^{\prime}
is a clock in
\Gamma
then
{\Gamma}\vdash({\kappa^{\prime}},{\diamond})\leadsto{\Gamma}

is a forcing tick and so if
\Gamma,\kappa:\mathsf{clock}\vdash t:\triangleright(\alpha:\kappa).A

then

\Gamma\vdash(\kappa.t)\,_{\Gamma}[({\kappa^{\prime}},{\diamond})]:A[(\diamond:\!\kappa^{\prime})/(\alpha\!:\!\kappa)]

This construction binds
\kappa
in
t
, and
A[(\diamond:\!\kappa^{\prime})/(\alpha\!:\!\kappa)]
uses a special simultaneous substitution of the pair
(\diamond,\kappa^{\prime})

for the pair
(\alpha,\kappa)
. In particular
[(\diamond:\!\kappa^{\prime})/(\alpha\!:\!\kappa)]
commutes as expected
with type and term formers, replacing each of
\alpha
and
\kappa
with
\diamond
and
\kappa^{\prime}
as intended,
but it will also have to turn a simple tick application like
t\,[\alpha]
into a forcing tick application

(\kappa.t)\,[({\kappa^{\prime}},{\diamond})]
to preserve typing. This substitution operation was originally
described by
Mannaa et al. 2020
.

Forcing ticks can be used to define the
\mathsf{force}
operator of
Atkey and McBride 2013

(5)

\mathsf{force}\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\lambda x.\lambda\kappa.(\kappa.x\,[\kappa])\,[({\kappa},{\diamond})]:\forall\kappa.\triangleright^{\kappa}A\to\forall\kappa.A

which is used in the encoding of coinductive types. Replacing the delayed substitution of
\kappa^{\prime}

for
\kappa
in
(\kappa.t)\,_{\Gamma}[({\kappa^{\prime}},{\diamond})]
with an actual substitution,
gives the elimination rule used by
Bahr. et al. 2017
.
Here we opt for an explicit substitution because it is easier to type check and give semantics to, which is
why
Mannaa et al. 2020
made a similar choice.

Bahr. et al. 2017
designed their application rule for
\diamond
to obtain a normalisation result for their theory.
We expect that using the present rule, one can prove the same property for CCTT.

3.2.
Tick irrelevance

Since fixed points unfold when applied to
\diamond

for the operational properties of Clocked Type Theory, in particular for ensuring
strong normalisation. However, on the extensional level the identity of ticks is
irrelevant, as

\mathsf{tirr}^{\kappa}:\Pi(x:\triangleright^{\kappa}A).{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).{\triangleright}\,(\beta\!:\!\kappa).\mathsf{Path}_{A}(x\,[\alpha],x\,[\beta])

\mathsf{tirr}^{\kappa}(t)\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\char 12\relax\alpha\kappa{\char 12\relax\beta\kappa{\lambda i.t\,[\mathsf{tirr}(\alpha,\beta,i)]}}

\displaystyle\equiv\lambda\kappa.\char 12\relax\alpha\kappa{(\kappa.{x\,[\kappa]})\,[({\kappa},{\diamond})]}

3.3.
Fixed points

The operator
\mathsf{dfix}^{\kappa}:(\triangleright^{\kappa}A\to A)\to\triangleright^{\kappa}A
computes fixed
points of productive functions. Using this, one can construct Nakano’s fixed point operator
as

(7)

\mathsf{fix}^{\kappa}\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\lambda f.f(\mathsf{dfix}^{\kappa}f):(\triangleright^{\kappa}A\to A)\to A

To ensure termination, fixed points do not unfold judgementally, except
when applied to
\diamond
. As in Guarded Cubical Type Theory
(
Birkedal et al. 2019
)
we add a path
\mathsf{pfix}^{\kappa}

between a fixed point and its unfolding.
Note that the right hand endpoint of
\mathsf{pfix}^{\kappa}t
is
\mathsf{fix}^{\kappa}t
and so,

(8)

\mathsf{fix}^{\kappa}t\equiv t(\char 12\relax\alpha\kappa{\mathsf{dfix}^{\kappa}t\,[\alpha]})=t(\char 12\relax\alpha\kappa{\mathsf{fix}^{\kappa}t})

With this we can prove that fixed points are unique, in fact the following stronger statement is true.

Lemma 3.2.

The types
\Sigma(x:A).\mathsf{Path}_{A}(x,f\,(\char 12\relax\alpha\kappa{x}))

and

\Sigma(x:\triangleright^{\kappa}A).{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).{\mathsf{Path}_{A}(x\,[\alpha],f(x))}

are contractible for every
f:\triangleright^{\kappa}A\to A
.

By lemma
3.2
the pair
(\mathsf{dfix}^{\kappa}t,\mathsf{pfix}^{\kappa}t)
is uniquely determined up to path, as it witnesses that
\mathsf{dfix}^{\kappa}t
is the fixed point of
\lambda x.\char 12\relax\_\kappa t\,x
.
Moreover both
\mathsf{dfix}^{\kappa}
and
\mathsf{pfix}^{\kappa}
unfold when applied to
\diamond
, which means they will not be stuck terms in a context with no tick variables.

The principles above can be used to encode guarded recursive types as fixed points on the universe.
For example, assuming codes for products and natural numbers, we define

\overline{\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}}(\mathbb{N})\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\mathsf{fix}^{\kappa}(\lambda X.\overline{\mathbb{N}}\overline{\times}\overline{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).X\,[\alpha])

and then by (
8
)

\overline{\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}}(\mathbb{N})=\overline{\mathbb{N}}\overline{\times}\overline{\triangleright}\,(\alpha\!:\!\kappa).\overline{\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}}(\mathbb{N})

so, defining
\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{N})\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\mathsf{El}\,(\overline{\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}}(\mathbb{N}))
gives the type equivalence

(9)

\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{N})\simeq\mathbb{N}\times\triangleright^{\kappa}\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{N})

Note that we do not assume the
clock irrelevance axiom

(10)

\frac{\Gamma\vdash t:\forall\kappa.A\quad\kappa\notin\mathsf{fc}(A)}{\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{cirr}^{\kappa}t:\forall{\kappa^{\prime}}.\forall{\kappa^{\prime\prime}}.\mathsf{Path}_{A}(t[\kappa^{\prime}],t[\kappa^{\prime\prime}])}

often assumed
in type theories with multi-clock guarded recursion
(
Bizjak et al. 2016
;
Bahr. et al. 2017
)
.
Instead we will use the following definition.

Definition 3.3.

A type
A
is
clock-irrelevant
if the canonical map
A\to\forall\kappa.A

(for
\kappa
fresh) is an equivalence.

If
A
is clock-irrelevant it satisfies axiom (
10
), and assuming a clock constant
the two statements are equivalent.
Clock irrelevance is needed for the correctness of encodings of coinductive types.
For example, using (
9
) one can prove

\forall\kappa.\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{N})\simeq(\forall\kappa.\mathbb{N})\times\forall\kappa.(\triangleright^{\kappa}\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{N}))\simeq\mathbb{N}\times\forall\kappa.\mathsf{Str}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{N})

assuming
\mathbb{N}
clock-irrelevant, and using that
\mathsf{force}
is an equivalence.
In this paper, rather than assuming all types clock irrelevant, we will prove clock-irrelevance
for a large class of types and use this to construct final coalgebras for a correspondingly large
class of functors.

\displaystyle=\mathsf{squash}(\alpha(x_{0}),\alpha(x_{1}),r)

Finally, this principle shows that there is an equivalence
{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}(\forall\kappa.A)\simeq\forall\kappa.{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}(A)
as required for the encoding of labelled transition systems.
The concrete calculations are analogous to those for the pushout or trunctation
depending on the constructor.

As a step towards reintroducing clock-irrelevance as an axiom, one would need to
show that the collection of clock irrelevant types is closed under HIT formation:

Theorem 5.3.

Let

\mathsf{con}_{\ell}:(\gamma\!:\!\Gamma_{\ell}[\delta])(x\!:\!\Xi_{\ell}[\delta,\gamma]\to\textsf{H}\,{\delta})(r\!:\!\Psi_{\ell})\to\textsf{H}\,{\delta}[\varphi_{\ell}\mapsto e_{\ell}]

As a special case

6.
Denotational semantics

Previous work has defined a model of CTT in presheaves over a cube category

(
Cohen et al. 2018
;
Orton and Pitts 2018
)
and a model of CloTT in covariant presheaves over a category
of time objects
\mathcal{T}

(
Mannaa et al. 2020
)
. We combine these
by considering the category
\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{T})
of
covariant presheaves over
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{T}
,
where
\mathcal{C}
is the opposite category of the usual choice of cube
category. Let
\mathsf{U}
be a Hofmann-Streicher universe
(
Hofmann and Streicher 1999
)
in

\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{T})
.

Following the approach of
Licata et al. 2018
, we construct the model using the internal type theory
of
\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{T})
.
For this, we must provide an interval object
\mathbb{I}
and a cofibration object
\mathbb{F}
satisfying certain axioms.
We refer to this data as a
cubical model
, and recall that
presheaves over
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}

admits a cubical model
(
Coquand et al. 2019b
)
for any small
\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathbb{I}
and

\mathbb{F}
are taken to be the inclusion of those
from
\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})
.

Recall
(
Orton and Pitts 2018
)
that a
CCHM fibration

(A,\alpha)
over a context
\Gamma:\mathsf{U}
is a family

A:\Gamma\to\mathsf{U}
equipped with a fibration structure
\alpha
.
The denotational semantics is given by the category with families (CwF)
(
Dybjer 1995
)

\mathsf{Fib}
constructed as
follows:
Contexts are global elements of
\mathsf{U}
, families are global
CCHM fibrations, and elements of
(A,\alpha)
are elements of
A
.

To model HITs
we follow
Coquand et al. 2018
. The principle of induction under clocks is justified semantically using that

\forall\kappa.(-)

can be modelled as an
{{\omega}^{\mathrm{op}}}
-limit
(
Bizjak and Møgelberg 2020
)
and that the structure maps of this limit
cannot change the outermost constructor in HITs, allowing for an inductive proof.

To model a Fitch-style modality like
\triangleright
we must define a dependent adjunction
(
Clouston et al. 2020
)
.
A dependent adjunction consists of
an endofunctor
L
and an action on types
R
such that elements of

L(\Gamma)\vdash A\,\operatorname{type}
correspond bijectively to elements of
\Gamma\vdash R(A)\,\operatorname{type}
.
Both
R
and the bijective correspondence are required to be natural in
\Gamma
.

Mannaa et al. 2020
define a dependent right adjoint on the slice of
\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{T})

over an object of clocks in that model, capturing the dependence of
\triangleright
on a clock.
This construction extends to
\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{T})
. To extend
to
\mathsf{Fib}
we show how to lift CCHM fibration structures from
A
to
R(A)
by a general construction.

Definition 6.1.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be a cubical model, and let

be an endofunctor on
\mathbb{C}
which preserves
finite limits. We say that

preserves the interval
if there is an isomorphism

which preserves endpoints. We say that
L

preserves cofibrations

if it maps cofibrations to cofibrations and preserves pullbacks where the vertical
maps are cofibrations.

Writing
\Gamma.A
for the comprehension object for the family
A
over
\Gamma
,
and

for the family classified by a cofibration
\varphi

the condition of preserving cofibrations can be presented in the internal language as an operation
mapping cofibrations

on

to
cofibrations
\mathsf{L}\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{L}_{\mathbb{F}}(\varphi):\mathbb{F}
such that

\mathsf{L}\Gamma.[\mathsf{L}_{\mathbb{F}}(\varphi)]\cong\mathsf{L}(\Gamma.[\varphi])
as
subobjects of
\mathsf{L}\Gamma
and satisfying
\mathsf{L}_{\mathbb{F}}(\varphi[\sigma])=\mathsf{L}_{\mathbb{F}}(\varphi)[\mathsf{L}\sigma]

7.
Related work

Guarded Cubical Type Theory
(
Birkedal et al. 2019
)
combines Cubical Type Theory
with single-clocked guarded recursion.
While this case is useful for many purposes, it cannot be used to encode
coinductive types.

Møgelberg and Veltri 2019

extend Guarded Cubical Type Theory with ticks as in CloTT.
They give a model of this calculus including HITs, and
show that bisimilarity coincides with path equality for a large class
of guarded recursive types. This paper can be seen as an extension of
that with multiple clocks, allowing for these results to be lifted from guarded
recursive types to coinductive types. Note that modelling the multiclocked case
is much more complex than the single clock case. In particular, equipping

\triangleright
with a composition structure is much more challenging, because the simple
description of the left adjoint in the single clock case allowed for a simple construction.
The extended language of ticks giving computation rules for clock irrelevance
presented here is also new.

The encoding of coinductive types using guarded recursion was first
described by
Atkey and McBride 2013
in the simply typed
setting. Since then a number of dependent type theories have been
developed for programming and reasoning with
these
(
Clouston et al. 2015
;
Møgelberg 2014
)
, of which CloTT is the most
advanced.
Bahr. et al. 2017
prove syntactic properties of
CloTT including strong normalisation and canonicity, but only for a
pure calculus without identity or path types. The model of CloTT
constructed by
Mannaa et al. 2020
considers extensional
identity types, but no cubical features.
Coinductive types can also be encoded using a combination of guarded
recursion and a
\Box
-modality
(
Clouston et al. 2015
)
. This approach
has not been studied in combination with CTT yet, and
also appears to be less flexible, e.g., it does not seem possible
to define nested coinductive types.

Sized types
(
Hughes et al. 1996
)
is another approach to encoding
productivity in types, by annotating (co)inductive types
with sizes indicating a bound on the size of the allowed elements.
Specifically, sized types reduce both termination and productivity to (well-founded) induction on sizes.
They have been extensively studied from the syntactic
perspective
(
Abel and Pientka 2013
;
Abel et al. 2017
;
Sacchini 2013
)
but
are not well understood from the perspective of denotational semantics.
Our view
is that sized types are closer to working in the models
of type theory, like the one provided here, and guarded recursion is
a more abstract, principled perspective. This is supported by the model of
guarded recursion in sized types constructed by
Veltri and van der Weide 2019
.
To our knowledge sized types have never been used to
solve equations with negative occurrences, which is an important application
of guarded recursion.

The coincidence of bisimilarity and
path equality for streams as coinductive records has been proved
in Cubical Agda
(
Vezzosi et al. 2019
)
, and it is likely that the proof can be extended to general M-types.

Veltri 2021
proves that bisimilarity implies path equality for the
final coalgebra for the
{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}
using sized types in Cubical Agda.
This coincidence should therefore be seen as
a feature of Cubical Type Theory, rather than guarded recursion.
On the other hand, when proving such results guarded recursion is a powerful
framework for ensuring productivity of definitions, as illustrated by the examples
in this paper.

The final coalgebra for the finite powerset functor can be constructed in set theory
as a limit of an
\omega+\omega
-indexed sequence
(
Worrell 2005
)
.
This construction has been formalised in Cubical Agda by
Veltri 2021

using the lesser limited principle of omniscience, a weak choice
principle.
From this perspective it is
interesting that our model uses
\omega
-indexed step-indexing only,
and therefore
{\mathsf{LTS}}
as constructed in
section 4
is realised
in the model as an
\omega
-limit. This construction works because
of the formulation of
{\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{f}}}
as a HIT and because the
\omega
-chain is constructed

externally
, using judgemental equality. In particular, the counter example
constructed in Proposition 5 of
(
Veltri 2021
)
uses an
\omega
-chain
of elements whose projections are only equal up to path equality.

Multimodal dependent type theory
(
Gratzer et al. 2020
)
is a general framework
for dependent modal type theories parametrised over
mode theories
.
By instantiating this
appropriately, one can recover e.g., the basic modal framework needed for internalising
parametricity in type theory
(
Cavallo and Harper 2020
;
Bernardy et al. 2015
)
, or
for the combination of
\triangleright
and
\Box
used for guarded recursion by

Clouston et al. 2015
. Generalising this to multiple clocks seems to require a notion
of dependent mode theory, as for example, the modal operator
\triangleright
depends on the object of
clocks.

8.
Conclusion and future work

We have presented the type theory CCTT, and shown that the principle of induction under clocks
can be used to construct a
rich supply of functors for which coinductive types can be encoded using guarded recursion.
This allows for simple programming with a wide range of coinductive types, including ones constructed
using higher inductive types.
We have seen by example how to prove coincidence
of path equality with bisimilarity for these types.
We believe this type theory is useful not just for coinductive reasoning, but also for reasoning about
advanced programming language features using a form of synthetic guarded domain
theory
(
Møgelberg and Paviotti 2019
;
Paviotti et al. 2015
)
. In fact, an earlier version of CCTT has already been used for a semantic proof of applicative simulation being
a congruence for a lambda calculus with finite non-determinism
(
Møgelberg and Vezzosi 2021
)
.

We are currently developing a prototype implementation of CCTT as an extension of Cubical Agda.
The current implementation
3
3

3

https://github.com/agda/guarded/tree/forcing-ticks

includes clock, ticks,
\triangleright
(and its composition structure), and fixed points, but not
the principle of induction under clocks. We believe this can be simulated using Agda’s rewrite
features
(
Cockx 2020
)
,
but should in the long run be build into Agda’s pattern matching. The proof of Theorem
4.3

has been verified in this.

We would also like to prove canonicity for CCTT, building on similar
results for Cubical Type Theory
(
Coquand et al. 2019a
;
Huber 2019
;
Sterling and Angiuli 2021
)

and Clocked Type Theory
(
Bahr. et al. 2017
)
.
For Cubical Type Theory
canonicity is proved for terms in a context of only interval variables.

Bahr. et al. 2017
prove that in Clocked Type Theory, terms of type
\mathbb{N}
or

\mathsf{Bool}
(the only inductive types considered in that paper)
in contexts with free clock variables, but no other assumptions, reduce to introduction forms.
Generalising this result to general HITs and contexts also containing interval variables should allow
for terms of type
\forall\overline{\kappa}.H(\delta\,[\overline{\kappa}])
to reduce
to a form in which the
\beta
-rule for induction under clocks can be applied. On the other hand,
we believe that it should not be necessary to include free tick variables, and so the only tick that
needs to be considered in reductions is
\diamond
. Our equational rules have been designed with
this in mind.

Unlike this paper, other type theories for multi-clocked guarded
recursion
(
Bizjak et al. 2016
;
Mannaa et al. 2020
)
take clock irrelevance
(
10
) as an axiom. This requires that universes be indexed by clock contexts, and the

\triangleright
modality be restricted to
\triangleright^{\kappa}:\mathsf{U}_{\Delta}\to\mathsf{U}_{\Delta}
for

\kappa\in\Delta
, because an unrestricted
\triangleright^{\kappa}
breaks clock
irrelevance
(
Bizjak and Møgelberg 2020
)
.
Bizjak and Møgelberg 2020
show how to
construct such universes of
types that are clock-irrelevant in the sense of the map
A\to\forall\kappa.A
being an isomorphism,
rather than an equivalence. Future work includes constructing larger
universes of types clock-irrelevant in the more liberal sense used in this paper.

A.6.1.
Modelling Cubical Type Theory

The standard model of Cubical Type Theory
(
Cohen et al. 2018
)
uses presheaves over the

category of cubes
. Here, since we use covariant presheaves, we will write
\mathcal{C}

for the opposite of the category used by

\mathcal{C}
has as objects
finite sets

and as morphisms from

to

maps

where

\mathsf{dM}(J)
is the free de Morgan algebra on the set

. Composition is the standard Kleisli
composition, using the fact that
\mathsf{dM}(-)
is a monad.
From the model construction we recall in particular the interval
object
\mathbb{I}(I)\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\mathsf{dM}(I)
, representing the singleton set, and the face lattice

\mathbb{F}
, which at stage

is the free distributive lattice generated by
elements
(i=0)
and
(i=1)
for each

(i=0)\wedge(i=1)=\bot_{\mathbb{F}}
.

This model construction can be extended to work for any
category of the form
\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathbb{D})
.
To do so we rely on the Orton and Pitts’ axiomatisation of models of CTT
(
Orton and Pitts 2018
;
Licata et al. 2018
)
.
Rather than recalling these axioms, we recall the sufficient conditions summarised by

Coquand et al. 2019b
for a presheaf topos to satisfy these axioms.
These conditions are as follows.

•

The interval object
\mathbb{I}
is connected, and a bounded distributive
algebra structure with distinct 0 and 1 elements.
Exponentiation by
\mathbb{I}
has a right adjoint.

•

The canonical map from
\mathbb{F}
to the subobject classifier is a
monomorphism, and the universal cofibration
\top:1\to\mathbb{F}
is a levelwise
decidable inclusion. Monomorphisms that can be described as pullbacks
of the latter are referred to as
cofibrations
.
The interval endpoint inclusions
0,1:1\to\mathbb{I}
must be cofibrations, and cofibrations must be closed under finite
union (finite disjunction), composition (dependent conjunction), and
universal quantification over
\mathbb{I}
.

A

is a presheaf category satisfying the axioms above.
In
\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathbb{D})
, defining both objects as constant
on the
\mathbb{D}
component, using the corresponding objects in

\mathsf{PSh}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(I,d)

\displaystyle\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\mathbb{I}(I)

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}(I,d)

\displaystyle\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}\mathbb{F}(I)

make

a cubical model,
as also observed by
Coquand et al. 2019b
.

Given such a model,
Orton and Pitts 2018
express
the structure sufficient to model CTT using the internal language
of the presheaf topos as an extensional type theory.
We recall here some definitions that will be relevant later. We assume a

\omega+1

corresponding hierarchy of universes a la Russell
\mathsf{U}_{i}
in the
internal language, each classifying presheaves of the appropriate size.

\displaystyle\mathsf{isFib}\,\Gamma\,A\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}

\displaystyle\,(e:\{0,1\})(p:\mathbb{I}\to\Gamma)\to\mathsf{Comp}\,e\,(A\circ p)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Comp}\,e\,A\mathbin{\overset{\textsf{def}}{=}}

\displaystyle\,(\varphi:\mathbb{F})(u:[\varphi]\to(i:\mathbb{I})\to A\,i)

Notice

•

\Gamma

•

\Gamma

•

Remark 2.

Cubical Type Theory as presented by

One way to deal with this is to use

Since these equalities are mainly relevant for operational

, as long as these are fresh meaning that the environment variable is of the form
\iota\cdot\rho
.

The action of morphisms on HIT’s in the model can only change the constructor type by acting on the cube
component. As a consequence of this, the structure of elements in
{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}
are determined at each level by their structure at 0.

It is sufficient to work with
d:{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\Delta\rrbracket}(I,x)
, with the proof for
general
\Gamma
following by pulling back along the term
\Gamma\vdash d:\forall\kappa.\Delta
.
We start by making explicit the data of the assumed terms
u_{i}
. The input to a
u_{i}
is as
follows:

(a)

We have a family
t=(t_{n})\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\Gamma_{i}\,[\kappa]\rrbracket}(I,x,d)
.

(b)

For each
j
we have a family

a=(a^{j}_{n})\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\Xi_{i,j}[\delta\,[\kappa],\gamma\,[\kappa]]\to\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(I,x,d,t).

This can be further unfolded as follows: We have for each
n
,

f:(I,x+\lambda_{n})\to(J,x^{\prime})
and

z\in{\llbracket\Xi_{i,j}[\delta\,[\kappa],\gamma\,[\kappa]]\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},f\cdot d_{n},f\cdot t_{n},f(\lambda))

an element
a^{j}_{n,f,z}\in{\llbracket\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},f\cdot d_{n},f(\lambda))
.

(c)

The next family is indexed by
j
as above, a morphism
g:(I,x)\to(J,x^{\prime})
and a family

z:=(z_{n})\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\Xi_{i,j}[\delta\,[\kappa],\gamma\,[\kappa]]\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},g\cdot d,g\cdot t).

Note here that this means we have a family
h:=(h_{n})\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},g\cdot d)
given by
h_{n}:=a^{j}_{n,g+\lambda_{n},z_{n}}
. We then have an element

b^{j}_{n,z}\in{\llbracket D\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},g\cdot d,h)
, and we denote the family by
b
.

(d)

An element
r\in\Psi_{i}(I)
.

When given this, we have an element

(\mathsf{con}_{i}(t_{n},a_{n},r))_{n}\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(I,x,d)

and we assume that we can inhabit
D
over this element, i.e., that we have

u_{i}(t,a,b,r)\in{\llbracket D\rrbracket}(I,x,d,(\mathsf{con}_{i}(t_{n},a_{n},r))_{n}).

We need to inhabit

{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
at an arbitrary element

h\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(I,x,d).

Observe that the structure of the family
h

is completely determined by the structure of
h_{0}
. This holds because the family
is compatible with the tick maps, and these are trivial in the cube component which
means in particular that they cannot change the outer constructor of an element of
the HIT. This means that we only have to inhabit it at families either of the form

(\mathsf{con}_{i}(t_{n},a_{n},r))
or of the form
(\mathsf{hcomp}[\varphi\mapsto u^{n}]\,u^{n}_{0})
. We can
do this by induction on the structure, since it is the same for each component of the
family, and can therefore assume that we can inhabit
{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
at structurally simpler
families. Explicitly, the induction is done on the structure of the 0 component in the
family, so that the inductive hypothesis says that whenever we have a family
(h^{\prime}_{n})

such that
h^{\prime}_{0}
is structurally simpler than
h_{0}
, we can inhabit
{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
over this
family. We denote the element over a family
h
by
\alpha(h)
.

We consider first the case where we are given a family

(\mathsf{con}_{i}(t_{n},a_{n},r))_{n}\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(I,x,d).

Note that
(t_{n})
is forced to be compatible with tick maps and that
r
is constant.
This means that we have
(t_{n})_{n}\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\Gamma_{i}[\delta\,[\kappa]]\rrbracket}(I,x,d)

and
r\in\Psi(I)
, and we have the data of (a) and (d).
The implied typing of
a^{j}_{n}
is a family over
f:(I,x+\lambda_{n})\to(J,x^{\prime})
and
z\in{\llbracket\Xi_{i,j}[\delta\,[\kappa],\gamma\,[\kappa]]\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},f\cdot d_{n},f\cdot t_{n},f(\lambda))

of elements
a^{j}_{n,f,z}\in{\llbracket\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},f\cdot d_{n},f(\lambda))
. This is exactly
the typing of (b), so we need only to construct the family in (c). We need this to be a family over

g:(I,x)\to(J,x^{\prime})
and
z:=(z_{n})\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\Xi_{i,j}[\delta\,[\kappa],\gamma\,[\kappa]]\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},g\cdot d,g\cdot t)
. Given this, we again define
the family

h:=(h_{n})\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},g\cdot d)

by
h_{n}:=a^{j}_{n,g+\lambda_{n},z_{n}}
. Note that this family is compatible with ticks, and
hence we obtain
\alpha((h_{n}))\ in{\llbracket D\rrbracket}(J,x^{\prime},g\cdot d,h)
by inductive hypothesis,
since
h_{0}
is given by a component of the structurally simpler
a
. We define

\alpha((\mathsf{con}_{i}(t_{n},a_{n},r))_{n})=u_{i}(t,a,\alpha((h_{n})),r).

Consider now the
\mathsf{hcomp}
case. Since
{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
is a type, it carries a composition structure.
The strategy for inhabiting
{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
will be to construct an appropriate composition problem
and apply the inductive hypothesis. Consider a family
h=(\mathsf{hcomp}[\varphi\mapsto u^{n}]\,u^{n}_{0})\in{\llbracket\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(I,x,d)
. Here the implicit typing is as follows:
For each
n
, we have for each
f:I\to J
such that
f\cdot\varphi=\top
and
s\in\Psi(J)

an element
u^{n}_{f,s}\in{\llbracket\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(J,x+\lambda_{n},f\cdot d_{n},\lambda)
and
an element
u^{n}_{0}\in{\llbracket\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}(J,x+\lambda_{n},d_{n},\lambda)
. The
u^{n}_{0}

family has the right shape for us to employ our induction hypothesis to inhabit
{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
over
it. Because the shape of
f
and
s
does not depend on the time component, we also have for each

f
a family in
n
given by
(u^{n}_{f,s})
. All these families have 0 component structurally simpler,
hence we can inhabit
{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
over each of them by
\alpha((u_{0}^{n}))
and
\alpha((u_{f,i}^{n}))
. We will
write
\alpha((u^{n}))
for the family given at
f,i
by
\alpha((u_{f,i}^{n}))
.
We can then define
\alpha(h)=\mathsf{comp}_{D[v]}[\varphi\mapsto\alpha((u^{n}))]\,\alpha((u^{n}_{0}))
where
v=(\mathsf{hfill}_{{\llbracket\textsf{H}\,{\delta\,[\kappa]}\rrbracket}}[\varphi\mapsto u^{n}]\,u^{n}_{0})
.

It must be verified that
\alpha
is natural in morphisms of the category while
defining it. Both because we need
\alpha
to be natural for the desired conclusion
and because the recursive calls used to define
\alpha
require it. Let
f:(I,x)\to(J,x^{\prime})
. Naturality means that
f\cdot\alpha(h)=\alpha(f\cdot h)
, which unfolds as follows:

•

Consider a family
h=(\mathsf{con}_{i}(t_{n},a_{n},r))
. By definition
\alpha(h)=u_{i}(t,a,b,r)
where

b_{g,z}=\alpha((a^{j}_{n,g+\lambda_{n},z_{n}}))
.
If
f
does not trigger a boundary condition for
\mathsf{con}_{i}
we have

f\cdot\alpha(h)=u_{i}(f\cdot t,f.a,f.b,f\cdot r)\text{ and }

(f\cdot h)_{n}=\mathsf{con}_{i}((f+\lambda_{n})\cdot t_{n},(f+\lambda_{n}).a_{n},(f+\lambda_{n})\cdot r).

Recall here that
g.a_{n}
is defined to be the family
a_{n}
is given by composing the map index
with
g
and acting on the input index.
Applying
\alpha
to this yields
u_{i}(((f+\lambda_{n})\cdot t_{n}),((f+\lambda_{n}).a_{n}),b^{\prime},f\cdot r)
where
b^{\prime}
is defined from
((f+\lambda_{n}).a_{n})
as above, i.e.,

b^{\prime}_{g,z}=\alpha((((f+\lambda_{n}).a)_{n,g+\lambda_{n},z_{n}})).

By definition of
the morphism action on a limit we have
((f+\lambda_{n})\cdot t_{n})=f\cdot(t_{n})
and
((f+\lambda_{n}).a_{n})=f.(a_{n})
. The action of
f
and
f+\lambda_{n}
are the same on
r
, since they have the same cubical
component. It remains to argue that
f.b
coincides with the
b^{\prime}
defined from the family
f.a
, which
already a part of the naturality of the
u_{i}
’s. We check this componentwise for the family, so we have to
show that
(f.b)_{g,z}=b^{\prime}_{g,z}
. The left hand side is by definition equal to
b_{gf,f\cdot z}=\alpha((a^{j}_{n,gf+\lambda_{n},(f+\lambda_{n})\cdot z_{n}}))
, while the right hand side is equal to

\alpha((((f+\lambda_{n}).a)^{j}_{g+\lambda_{n},z_{n}}))
. Unfolding the right hand side we get

\alpha(((a)^{j}_{(g+\lambda_{n})(f+\lambda_{n}),(f+\lambda_{n})\cdot z_{n}}))
, which is the same as the left hand
side since
(g+\lambda_{n})(f+\lambda_{n})=gf+\lambda_{n}
.

If
f
triggers a boundary condition of
\mathsf{con}_{i}
we have by assumption that

f\cdot\alpha(h)=f\cdot u_{i}(t,a,b,r)={\llbracket\llparenthesis{e}\rrparenthesis^{\delta}_{\mathcal{E},\overline{x}\mapsto\overline{y}}\rrbracket}(f\cdot t,f\cdot a,f\cdot b,f\cdot r)

where
e
is the boundary term of
\mathsf{con}_{i}
.
On the other hand, the structure of
H
means that
\alpha(f\cdot h)=\alpha((\lVert e\rVert((f+\lambda_{n})\cdot t_{n},(f+\lambda_{n}).a_{n},(f+\lambda_{n})\cdot r))
where

\lVert e\rVert
is the boundary evaluation for
\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}
. Equality of these two can be shown
by induction on the structure of
e
.

•

Consider a family
h=(\mathsf{hcomp}[\varphi\mapsto u^{n}]\,u_{0}^{n})
. By definition
\alpha(h)=\mathsf{comp}_{D[v]}[\varphi\mapsto\alpha((u^{n}))]\,\alpha((u_{0}^{n}))
. If
f
does not make
\varphi
true
we have to verify that

f\cdot\mathsf{comp}_{D[v]}[\varphi\mapsto\alpha((u^{n}))]\,\alpha((u_{0}^{n}))=\alpha(f\cdot h).

We unfold the right hand side:

\displaystyle\alpha(f\cdot h)=

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}==}\alpha((\mathsf{hcomp}[f\cdot\varphi\mapsto(f+\lambda_{n}).u^{n}]\,(f+\lambda_{n})\cdot u_{0}^{n}))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{comp}_{D[f\cdot v]}[f\cdot\varphi\mapsto\alpha(((f+\lambda_{n}).u^{n}))]

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}====================}\alpha(((f+\lambda_{n})\cdot u_{0}^{n}))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{comp}_{D[f\cdot v]}[f\cdot\varphi\mapsto f.\alpha((u^{n}))]\,f\cdot\alpha((u_{0}^{n}))

\displaystyle=f\cdot\mathsf{comp}_{D[v]}[\varphi\mapsto\alpha((u^{n}))]\,\alpha((u_{0}^{n}))

The second to last step above follows by the inductive hypothesis that
\alpha
is natural on
simpler families, and the last line is exactly the left hand side of the original equation.

If
f
makes
\varphi
true, both composition in
D
and
\mathsf{hcomp}
reduce to appropriate
components of the input, i.e., we have
\alpha(f\cdot h)=\alpha((u_{(f+\lambda),1}^{n}))

and
f\cdot\alpha(h)=\alpha((u^{n}))_{f,1}
for the right and left hand sides of the
equality we are trying to show. These agree by definition of
\alpha((u^{n}))_{f,1}
.

This means that
\alpha
assembles into a term of
{\llbracket D\rrbracket}
over
{\llbracket\Gamma.\forall\kappa.\textsf{H}\,{(\delta\,[\kappa])}\rrbracket}
as desired. It is moreover clear from the definition of
\alpha

that it will validate the computation rules of the induction principle, since the value on
constructor families was given directly by the appropriate

and the value on
\mathsf{hcomp}

families was given by the appropriate composition in

.
∎

Minimum degree stability of H H -free graphs
Freddie Illingworth

Abstract
Given an ( r + 1 ) (r+1) -chromatic graph H H , the fundamental edge stability result of Erdős and Simonovits says that all n n -vertex H H -free graphs have at most ( 1 − 1 / r + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ ( n 2 ) (1-1/r+o(1))\binom{n}{2} edges, and any H H -free graph with that many edges can be made r r -partite by deleting o ⁡ ( n 2 ) o(n^{2}) edges. Here we consider a natural variant of this – the minimum degree stability of H H -free graphs. In particular, what is the least c c such that any n n -vertex H H -free graph with minimum degree greater than c ​ n cn can be made r r -partite by deleting o ⁡ ( n 2 ) o(n^{2}) edges? We determine this least value for all 3-chromatic H H and for very many non-3-colourable H H (all those in which one is commonly interested) as well as bounding it for the remainder. This extends the Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem and work of Alon and Sudakov.

Introduction

A central theme in extremal graph theory is the structure of graphs which do not contain some fixed subgraph
H
. Some of the oldest questions in the area are what is the greatest number of edges such a graph can have, what is the structure of these extremal graphs and how stable is this structure. The classical result is that of Erdős and Simonovits.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős & Simonovits,
[
Erd67
,
Erd68
,
Sim68
]
)
.

Fix a graph
H
with chromatic number
r+1
. If
G
is an
H
-free graph with
n
vertices, then
e(G)\leqslant(1-1/r+o(1))\binom{n}{2}
. Furthermore, if
G
has
(1-1/r+o(1))\binom{n}{2}
edges, then
G
can be obtained from the
r
-partite Turán graph,
T_{r}(n)
, by deleting and adding
o(n^{2})
edges.

In particular, all
H
-free graphs with close to the extremal number of edges are close to
(\chi(H)-1)
-partite. In place of the number of edges, it is natural to consider the structure of
H
-free graphs with large minimum degree and whether they are close to
(\chi(H)-1)
-partite. In 1974, Andrásfai, Erdős and Sós
[
AES74
]
did this for
H
being an
(r+1)
-clique. Their seminal result was the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós,
[
AES74
]
)
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
and
G
be a
K_{r+1}
-free graph with
n
vertices and minimum degree greater than

\biggl(1-\frac{1}{r-1/3}\biggr)n.

Then
G
is
r
-colourable. Furthermore
1-1/(r-1/3)
is tight.

Note that the
K_{r+1}
-free graph with greatest minimum degree (and most edges), the Turán graph,
T_{r}(n)
, has minimum degree
(1-1/r)n
so the theorem gives information about graphs whose minimum degrees are
\Omega(n)
away from that of the extremal graph – we are far from the regime of
Theorem

1.1
.

We now consider
Theorem

1.2
from the perspective of minimum degree stability. The graph consisting of a 5-cycle joined to a complete
(r-2)
-partite graph with three vertices in each part shows that
1-1/(r-1/3)
is tight. Furthermore,
n
-vertex balanced blow-ups (defined in
Section

1.3
) of this graph are
K_{r+1}
-free, have minimum degree
\lfloor(1-1/(r-1/3))n\rfloor
, and require the deletion of
\Omega(n^{2})
edges to be made
r
-partite. This suggests the most basic problem for the minimum degree stability of
H
-free graphs: given a
(r+1)
-chromatic graph
H
, determine

\begin{split}\delta_{H}=\inf\{c\colon&\textrm{if }\lvert G\rvert=n,\,\delta(G)\geqslant cn\textrm{ and }G\textrm{ is }H\textrm{-free},\\
&\textrm{then }G\textrm{ can be made }r\textrm{-partite by deleting }o(n^{2})\textrm{ edges}\}.\end{split}

This is the analogue of the edge stability question answered by
Theorem

1.1
. The reason for allowing the omission of
o(n^{2})
edges is the same for both questions: there are many
H
for which the
H
-free graph with most edges or greatest minimum degree is not
r
-partite (but of course is very close to
r
-partite – it is close to the Turán graph). This is explored further in
Section

1.1
.

It follows from our previous discussion that

\delta_{K_{r+1}}=1-\frac{1}{r-1/3}.

As pointed out by Alon and Sudakov
[
AS06
]
, a standard application of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma
[
Sze78
]
shows that

\delta_{K_{r+1}(t)}=1-\frac{1}{r-1/3},

where
K_{r+1}(t)
is the complete
(r+1)
-partite graph with
t
vertices in each part (so is a Turán graph). In fact, Alon and Sudakov showed that one can do better than deleting
o(n^{2})
edges.

Theorem 1.3

(Alon-Sudakov,
[
AS06
]
)
.

Let

and
t\geqslant 1

\varepsilon>0
, and set
\rho=1/(4r^{2/3}t)
. The following holds for all sufficiently large

. If

is a
K_{r+1}(t)
-free graph on
n
vertices and with minimum degree at least
(1-1/(r-1/3)+\varepsilon)n
, then one can delete
\mathcal{O}_{r,t,\varepsilon}(n^{2-\rho})
edges to make

r
-colourable.

Subsequently, Allen
[
All10
]
found a more direct proof (with no use of the regularity lemma) that yields optimal (to within a constant factor) estimates. Given the central role played by
K_{r+1}(t)
in edge stability, it might be tempting to believe that this should determine
\delta_{H}
for general non-bipartite
H
. Of course, if
H
is a graph with chromatic number
\chi(H)=r+1\geqslant 3
, then
H
is a subgraph of
K_{r+1}(t)
for some

and so any
H
-free graph is
K_{r+1}(t)
-free. Thus,

\delta_{H}\leqslant\delta_{K_{r+1}(t)}=1-\frac{1}{r-1/3}.

(1)

However, the inequality may be strict. In the case of edge stability, the Turán graph
T_{r}(n)
has
(1-1/r)\binom{n}{2}
edges and does not contain
H
, as
H
has chromatic number
r+1
. Here, however, it is blow-ups of
K_{r-2}+C_{5}
(mentioned above) which show that
\delta_{K_{r+1}(t)}\geqslant 1-1/(r-1/3)
. These are
K_{r+1}
-free, but need not be
H
-free – for a simple example, consider

and
H
an odd cycle. This observation highlights the following important notion. For two graphs
H
and

we say there is a
homomorphism
H\to F
, writing

for short, if

is a subgraph of a blow-up of
F
– this is discussed more comprehensively in
Section

1.3
. Minimum degree stability is nuanced:
\delta_{H}
is determined not just by the chromatic number of

but also by its finer structural properties. For 3-chromatic graphs, the situation is fairly straightforward.

Theorem 1.4

(
\delta_{H}
for 3-chromatic
H
)
.

Let
H
be a 3-chromatic graph. There is a smallest positive integer
g
for which there is
no
homomorphism
H\to C_{2g+1}
. Then

\delta_{H}=\frac{2}{2g+1}.

Thus, for 3-chromatic
H
,
\delta_{H}
is determined by the first odd cycle to which there is not a homomorphism from
H
. Next we turn to graphs which are not 3-colourable. We will determine
\delta_{H}
for very many

(indeed, all those in which one is commonly interested) and bound it for the remainder.

Theorem 1.5

(
\delta_{H}
for
H
not 3-colourable)
.

There is a sequence of eleven graphs
(F_{g})_{1\leqslant g\leqslant 11}

(
described explicitly in
Section

1.2
)
and constants
(c_{g})_{1\leqslant g\leqslant 11}
such that the following holds. Let

be an integer. If

(r+1)
-chromatic graph and
g
is minimal such that there is
no

H\to K_{r-3}+F_{g}
, then

\delta_{H}=1-\frac{1}{r-1+c_{g}}.

If there is a homomorphism from
H
to each of the eleven
K_{r-3}+F_{g}
, then there is a least
g
for which there is
no
homomorphism
H\to K_{r-2}+C_{2g+1}
:

\delta_{H}
satisfies the bounds

1-\frac{1}{r-1}<1-\frac{1}{r-1+2/(2g-1)}\leqslant\delta_{H}\leqslant 1-\frac{1}{r-1+1/7}.

Again, there is a sequence of graphs such that
\delta_{H}
is determined by the first one to which there is no homomorphism from
H
. We will define the
F_{g}
and
c_{g}
explicitly in
Section

1.2
– we mention for now that the

are all 4-chromatic graphs on few vertices.

The only
(r+1)
-chromatic graphs whose

are those which have a homomorphism to each of the eleven
K_{r-3}+F_{g}

1.1
Wider context – chromatic profiles

We give a second view of
\delta_{H}
, placing it within a whole spectrum of structural constants relating to
H
-free graphs. The
chromatic profile of a graph
H
is the sequence of values for
k=1,2,\dotsc
of

\delta_{\chi}(H,k)=\inf\{c\colon\text{if }\delta(G)\geqslant c\lvert G\rvert\text{ and }G\text{ is }H\text{-free},\text{then }G\text{ is $k$-colourable}\}.

The question of determining this was first asked by Erdős and Simonovits
[
ES73
]
in 1973. Note that the Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem says that
\delta_{\chi}(K_{r+1},r)=1-1/(r-1/3)
. The chromatic profile of triangles has been studied extensively
[
AES74
,
Bra99
,
CJK97
,
Häg82
,
Jin95
,
Łuc06
,
Tho02
]
and was finally settled by Brandt and Thomassé
[
BT05
]
. These results were extended to general cliques
[
GL10
,
Nik10
]
.

Much less is known about the chromatic profile of non-complete graphs with Erdős and Simonovits describing the behaviour as “too complicated”. In part this is because the exact structure and chromatic number of the
n
-vertex
H
-free graphs with highest minimum degree or most edges is unknown. Moreover, degenerate examples abound. Consider
H=K_{3}(2)
and let
G
be the complete bipartite Turán graph
T_{2}(n)
with a graph
F
inserted into one of the parts. Now if
F
does not contain a 4-cycle, then
G
is
H
-free. Furthermore,
\chi(G)\geqslant\chi(F)
and
\delta(G)\geqslant\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. Thus, taking
F
to have girth at least five and arbitrarily large chromatic number shows that
\delta_{\chi}(H,k)\geqslant 1/2
for all
k
. But, of course, all
H
-free graphs have at most
(1/2+o(1))\binom{n}{2}
edges and so minimum degree at most
(1/2+o(1))n
. In particular,
1/2=\delta_{\chi}(H,k)
for all
k
. This is unsatisfying, failing to capture the macroscopic behaviour of
H
-free graphs with large minimum degree. All these graphs are close to (within
o(n^{2})
edge of) being bipartite and their high chromatic number is rather artificial. For these reasons, another natural notion of the structure of
H
-free graphs with large minimum degree is the
approximate chromatic profile
. This is

\begin{split}\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)=\inf\{c\colon&\textrm{if }\lvert G\rvert=n,\,\delta(G)\geqslant cn\text{ and }G\textrm{ is }H\textrm{-free},\\
&\textrm{then }G\textrm{ can be made }k\textrm{-colourable by deleting }o(n^{2})\textrm{ edges}\}.\end{split}

Note in passing that
\delta_{\chi}(H,k)\geqslant\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)
, although equality need not occur. Indeed, we have just seen that the chromatic profile of
H=K_{3}(2)
is the constant
1/2
sequence, while
Theorem

1.6
below shows that
H
’s approximate chromatic profile is the same as the chromatic profile of triangle-free graphs.

If
H
is
(r+1)
-chromatic, then the
r
-partite Turán graph
T_{r}(n)
is
H
-free and cannot be made
(r-1)
-colourable without deleting
\Omega(n^{2})
edges. Also, any
n
-vertex
H
-free graph has at most
(1-1/r+o(1))\binom{n}{2}
edges and so has minimum degree at most
(1-1/r+o(1))n
. Thus, for all
k\leqslant r-1
we have
\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)=1-1/r
. In particular, the first interesting threshold in the approximate chromatic profile of
H
is

\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,\chi(H)-1)

which is exactly
\delta_{H}
.

For a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, one can make the more general definition

\begin{split}\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(\mathcal{F},k)=\inf\{c\colon&\textrm{if }\lvert G\rvert=n,\,\delta(G)\geqslant cn\text{ and }G\in\mathcal{F},\\
&\textrm{then }G\textrm{ can be made }k\textrm{-colourable by deleting }o(n^{2})\textrm{ edges}\}.\end{split}

This again satisfies the inequality
\delta_{\chi}(\mathcal{F},k)\geqslant\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(\mathcal{F},k)
. There is a natural class of families where equality occurs. We say
\mathcal{F}
is closed under taking blow-ups
if any blow-up of any member of
\mathcal{F}
is also in
\mathcal{F}
. Examples of such families include
K_{t}
-free graphs and
t
-colourable graphs.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of graphs that is closed under taking blow-ups. For any positive integer
k
,

\delta_{\chi}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F},k)=\delta_{\chi}(\mathcal{F},k).

For non-complete
H
, the family of
H
-free graphs is not closed under taking blow-ups. However, there is a natural family of graphs which is closed under taking blow-ups and whose chromatic profile is the same as the approximate chromatic profile of
H
. Define
H
-hom
to be the family of
H
-homomorphism-free graphs
, that is, those graphs to which there is no homomorphism from
H
. Then

\begin{split}\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)=\inf\{c\colon&\text{if }\delta(G)\geqslant c\lvert G\rvert\text{ and there is no homomorphism }H\to G,\\
&\text{then }G\text{ is $k$-colourable}\},\end{split}

is the chromatic profile of this family. In
Section

5
, we will show that this is identical to the approximate chromatic profile of
H
and so
\delta_{H}=\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},\chi(H)-1)
.

Theorem 1.7
.

For any graph
H
and any positive integer
k
,

\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)=\delta_{\chi}(H\text{\emph{-hom}},k).

1.3
Notation

Given a graph
G
, a
blow-up
of
G
, is a graph obtained by replacing each vertex
v
of
G
by a non-empty independent set
I_{v}
and each edge
uv
by a complete bipartite graph between classes
I_{u}
and
I_{v}
. We say we have
blown-up a vertex
v
by
n
if
\lvert I_{v}\rvert=n
. It is often helpful to think of this as weighting vertex
v
by
n
.

A blow-up is
balanced
if the independent sets
(I_{v})_{v\in G}
are as equal in size as possible. We use
G(t)
to denote the graph obtained by blowing-up each vertex of
G
by
t
, i.e.
G(t)
is the balanced blow-up of
G
on
t\lvert G\rvert
vertices. For example, the balanced blow-up of the
r
-clique,
K_{r}
, on
n
vertices is exactly the Turán graph,
T_{r}(n)
. We note in passing that a graph has the same chromatic and clique number as any of its blow-ups. Furthermore, if
H
is a blow-up of
G
, then
G
is
a
-locally bipartite if and only if
H
is.

Given two graphs
G
and
H
, the
join
of
G
and
H
, denoted by
G+H
, is the graph obtained by taking disjoint copies of
G
and
H
and joining each vertex of the copy of
G
to each vertex of the copy of
H
. Note that the chromatic and clique numbers of
G+H
are the sum of the chromatic and clique numbers of
G
and
H
.

There is a homomorphism from a graph
G
to a graph
H
, written
G\to H
, if there is a map
\varphi\colon V(G)\to V(H)
such that for any edge
uv
of
G
,
\varphi(u)\varphi(v)
is an edge of
H
. There is a homomorphism
G\to H
if and only if
G
is a subgraph of some blow-up of
H
. In particular, if
G\to H
, then
\chi(G)\leqslant\chi(H)
and moreover if
H
is
a
-locally bipartite, then
G
is also.

1.4
Tools

We will make great use of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma
[
Sze78
]
together with some associated machinery which we describe here. Let
(X,Y)
be a pair of vertex subsets of graph
G
. We use
d(X,Y)=e(X,Y)\lvert X\rvert^{-1}\lvert Y\rvert^{-1}
to denote the
density
between
X
and
Y
. The pair
(X,Y)
is
\varepsilon
-regular
if

\lvert d(U,V)-d(X,Y)\rvert\leqslant\varepsilon

for all
U\subset X
,
V\subset Y
with
\lvert U\rvert\geqslant\varepsilon\lvert X\rvert
and
\lvert V\rvert\geqslant\varepsilon\lvert Y\rvert
. A partition
\mathcal{P}=V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup V_{k}
of
V(G)
is an
\varepsilon
-regular partition
if:

•

V_{1}
,
V_{2}
, …,
V_{k}
all have equal size and
\lvert V_{0}\rvert\leqslant\varepsilon\lvert G\rvert
,

•

for all but at most
\varepsilon\binom{k}{2}
pairs
ij
(
i<j
), the pair
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
\varepsilon
-regular.

Szemerédi’s celebrated result is that, for every positive integer
\ell
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is some
L=L(\ell,\varepsilon)
such that every graph with at least
\ell
vertices has an
\varepsilon
-regular partition into at least
\ell
but at most
L
parts. We will need a version of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma which works well with minimum degrees.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and some
\lambda\geqslant 0
. Suppose we have a graph
G
with some
\varepsilon
-regular partition
\mathcal{P}=V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup V_{k}
. These induce what is called a
reduced graph

R(\mathcal{P},\varepsilon,\lambda)
: this has vertex set
\{1,2,\dotsc,k\}
with vertex
i
joined to vertex
j
exactly if the pair
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
\varepsilon
-regular and

. Note when

\mathds{1}(v\in G_{K})\geqslant\mathds{1}(vx_{1}\in E(G))+\dotsb+\mathds{1}(vx_{r-2}\in E(G))-(r-3)

and so summing over all the vertices gives

\lvert G_{K}\rvert\geqslant(r-2)\delta(G)-(r-3)\lvert G\rvert>\bigl((r-2)k_{g}-(r-3)\bigr)\cdot\lvert G\rvert.

Note that
\delta(G_{K})\geqslant\delta(G)-(\lvert G\rvert-\lvert G_{K}\rvert)=\lvert G_{K}\rvert-(\lvert G\rvert-\delta(G))
so

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(G_{K})}{\lvert G_{K}\rvert}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-\frac{\lvert G\rvert-\delta(G)}{\lvert G\rvert}\cdot\frac{\lvert G\rvert}{\lvert G_{K}\rvert}>1-\biggl(1-\frac{\delta(G)}{\lvert G\rvert}\biggr)\cdot\frac{1}{(r-2)k_{g}-(r-3)}

\displaystyle>1-\frac{1-k_{g}}{(r-2)k_{g}-(r-3)}=1-\frac{1}{1+c_{g}}.

For
g=1
:
G_{K}
is not bipartite and
\delta(G_{K})>2/5\cdot\lvert G_{K}\rvert
so, by
Lemma

2.1
,
G_{K}
contains a triangle and so
G
contains a
K_{r-2}+K_{3}=L_{0}
. Similarly for
g=2
,
G
contains a copy of
L_{0}
or
L_{1}
and for
g=3
,
G
contains one of
L_{0}
,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
. More generally, for
g
even,
G
contains
L_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\{0,1,2,4,6,\dotsc,g-2\}
and for
g
odd,
G
contains
L_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\{0,1,2,4,6,\dotsc,g-1\}
.

Now suppose that
G
is
(r-2)
-locally bipartite. For
g=1,2,3
:

\delta(G)\lvert G\rvert^{-1}>1-\frac{1}{r-1+c_{3}}=1-\frac{1}{r-2+4/3},

so, by
Theorem

1.8
,
G
is
r
-colourable. For
g=4,5
:

\delta(G)\lvert G\rvert^{-1}>1-\frac{1}{r-1+c_{5}}=1-\frac{1}{r-2+5/4},

so, by
Theorem

1.8
,
G
is
r
-colourable or contains
K_{r-3}+\overline{C}_{7}=L_{3}
. For
g=6,7
:

\delta(G)\lvert G\rvert^{-1}>1-\frac{1}{r-1+c_{7}}=1-\frac{1}{r-2+6/5},

so, by
Theorem

1.8
,
G
is
r
-colourable or contains
K_{r-3}+\overline{C}_{7}=L_{3}
or contains
K_{r-3}+H_{2}^{+}=L_{5}
. For
g=8,9
:

\delta(G)\lvert G\rvert^{-1}>1-\frac{1}{r-1+c_{9}}=1-\frac{1}{r-2+7/6},

so, by
Theorem

1.8
,
G
is
r
-colourable or contains
K_{r-3}+H_{2}=L_{7}
. Finally, for
g=10,11
:

\delta(G)\lvert G\rvert^{-1}>1-\frac{1}{r-1+c_{11}}=1-\frac{1}{r-2+8/7},

so, by
Theorem

1.8
,
G
is
r
-colourable or contains
K_{r-3}+H_{2}=L_{7}
or contains
K_{r-3}+T_{0}=L_{9}
. Hence, the sequences do indeed satisfy
3
, as required.

5
Approximate chromatic profile and homomorphism-free graphs

In
Section

1.1
we introduced two chromatic profiles related to
\delta_{H}
. The first was the approximate chromatic profile which for a family
\mathcal{F}
is,

\begin{split}\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(\mathcal{F},k)=\inf\{c\colon&\textnormal{if }\lvert G\rvert=n,\delta(G)\geqslant cn,G\in\mathcal{F},\\
&\textnormal{then }G\textnormal{ can be made }k\textnormal{-colourable by deleting at most }o(n^{2})\textnormal{ edges}\},\end{split}

and the second was the chromatic profile of the family of
H
-homomorphism-free graphs,

\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)=\inf\{c\colon\text{if }\delta(G)\geqslant c\lvert G\rvert\text{ and }H\nrightarrow G,\text{then }G\text{ is $k$-colourable}\},

We first prove
Theorem

1.6
, showing that families which are closed under blow-ups have nicely behaved chromatic profiles.

See
1.6

Proof
.

Plainly the definitions of the approximate and normal chromatic profiles give

\delta_{\chi}(\mathcal{F},k)\geqslant\delta_{\chi}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F},k),

as being
k
-colourable implies being within
o(n^{2})
edges of
k
-colourable.

Let
c<\delta_{\chi}(\mathcal{F},k)
. By definition, there must be a graph
G\in\mathcal{F}
which is not
k
-colourable and has minimum degree at least
c\lvert G\rvert
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the balanced blow-up of
G
on
n
vertices: as
\mathcal{F}
is closed under taking blow-ups,
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
. Furthermore,
G^{\prime}
has minimum degree at least
c\lvert G\rvert\lfloor n/\lvert G\rvert\rfloor=(c-o(1))n
. Finally, just as in
Section

3
, it is easy to check by counting copies of
G
that to make
G^{\prime}

k
-colourable requires the deletion of
\Omega(n^{2})
edges. In particular,
\delta_{\chi}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F},k)\geqslant c
, as required.

Finally, we prove
Theorem

1.7
showing that the chromatic profile of
H
-homomorphism-free graphs and the approximate chromatic profile of
H
-free graphs are one and the same.

See
1.7

Proof
.

We first show that
\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)\geqslant\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)
. Any graph to which there is no homomorphism from
H
must be
H
-free, so
\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)\geqslant\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)
. But
H
-hom is closed under taking blow-ups so, by
Theorem

1.6
,
\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)=\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)
, as required.

We now show that
\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)\leqslant\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)
. Let
\gamma>0
,
c=\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)+2\gamma
and let
G
be an
n
-vertex
H
-free graph with
\delta(G)\geqslant cn
. Let

\mathcal{H}=\{H^{\prime}\colon H\to H^{\prime},\lvert H^{\prime}\rvert\leqslant\lvert H\rvert\},

and note that this is finite and that there is a homomorphism from
H
to a graph if and only if that graph contains some
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
. There is some
t\leqslant\lvert H\rvert
such that
H\subset H^{\prime}(t)
for every
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Fix
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
. The graph
G
is
H
-free so does not contain
H^{\prime}(t)
. By Erdős’s result on the extremal function for complete
\ell
-uniform
\ell
-partite hypergraphs
[
Erd64
]
,
G
must contain
o(n^{\lvert H^{\prime}\rvert})
copies of
H^{\prime}
(see for example
[
ABG+17
, Lemma 6.2]
). By the graph removal lemma (see for example
[
KS96
, Theorem 2.9]
),
G
can be made
H^{\prime}
-free by deleting
o(n^{2})
edges. As
\mathcal{H}
is finite, there is a spanning subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
with
e(G)-e(G^{\prime})=o(n^{2})
which contains no
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Take
G^{\prime}
and sequentially delete vertices of degree less than
(c-\gamma)n
until no more remain. Provided
n
is large enough, so that
(e(G)-e(G^{\prime}))/n^{2}
is sufficiently small, this process will terminate with the deletion of at most
o(n)
vertices. Let the resulting graph be
G^{\prime\prime}
. Then
G^{\prime\prime}
satisfies
\delta(G^{\prime\prime})\geqslant(c-\gamma)\lvert G^{\prime\prime}\rvert
and
G^{\prime\prime}
contains no
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
. In particular, there is no homomorphism
H\to G^{\prime\prime}
. But
c-\gamma>\delta_{\chi}(H\text{-hom},k)
so
G^{\prime\prime}
is
k
-colourable. Furthermore
G^{\prime\prime}
was obtained from
G
by the deletion of
o(n^{2})
edges. Thus
\delta^{\ast}_{\chi}(H,k)\leqslant c
, as required.

6
Concluding remarks

While
Theorem

1.5
determines
\delta_{H}
for most
H
, many minimum degree stability questions remain. Firstly how do those
\delta_{H}
not determined by the theorem behave? Is
1-1/(r-1)
the only accumulation point of
\{\delta_{H}\colon\chi(H)=r+1\}
(as it is for
r=2
) or is there more exotic behaviour? To extend
Theorem

1.5
one would need to extend
Theorem

1.8
below
1-1/(a+8/7)
and so we would need to further our knowledge of the structure of locally bipartite and more generally locally colourable graphs – for more information see
[
Ill22
]
. All the graphs appearing in
Theorem

1.5
are either an
(r-2)
-clique joined to an odd cycle or an
(r-3)
-clique joined to a 4-chromatic locally bipartite graphs. Are these the only graphs that appear? The motivation in
Section

1.2
suggests that this is the case for
r=3
: the major obstacles to being close to tripartite are containing either a blow-up of an odd wheel of the blow-up of some 4-chromatic locally bipartite graph. However, for greater
r
other graphs could appear. For example, containing a blow-up of some 5-chromatic locally tripartite graph could be an obstacle for being close to 4-partite.

In
Section

1.1
we placed
\delta_{H}
as the first non-trivial threshold within the approximate chromatic profile. It would be interesting to determine the next threshold, that is, to understand the behaviour of
\delta_{\chi}^{\ast}(H,\chi(H))
. For triangles and cliques, this has already been done
[
BT05
,
GL10
,
Nik10
]
.

Another natural direction is to consider the number of edges that need deleting to make an
n
-vertex
H
-free graph,
G
, with minimum degree at least
(\delta_{H}+\varepsilon)n

r
-partite. Are
o(n^{2})
edges really required, or, as is often the case, can one get away with
\mathcal{O}(n^{2-\rho})
for some
\rho=\rho(H)>0
? This has precedent. Erdős and Simonovits
[
Erd67
,
Erd68
,
Sim68
]
showed that the
H
-free graph with most edges can be made
r
-partite by deleting
\mathcal{O}(n^{2-\rho})
edges. Also Alon and Sudakov’s result,
Theorem

1.3
, gives an affirmative answer for
H=K_{r+1}(t)
. The heuristic here is that if more than
n^{2-\rho}
edges are required, then, by the theorem of Kővári, Sós and Turán
[
KST54
]
,
G
is an
r
-partite graph with some large
K_{t,t}
appearing inside one of the parts. Joining these together ought to give some blow-up of an
F_{g}
and so a copy of
H
. In both the cases of Erdős-Simonovits and Alon-Sudakov, the minimum degree of
G
was large and so
G
was well connected. For our present situation the following would be the most basic question. Although an affirmative answer seems plausible (and I have a proof for
g=3
) the smaller minimum degree casts doubt for large
g
.

Question 6.1
.

For positive integers
g
and
t
is there some
\rho>0
such that every
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree at least
(2/(2g+1)+\varepsilon)n
either contains
C_{2g-1}(t)
or can be made bipartite by deleting
\mathcal{O}(n^{2-\rho})
edges?

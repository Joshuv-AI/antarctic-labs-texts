Quadratic covariations for the solution to a stochastic heat equation ∗

Abstract
. Let u ⁡ ( t , x ) u(t,x) be the solution to a stochastic heat equation ∂ ∂ t ​ u = 1 2 ​ ∂ 2 ∂ x 2 ​ u + ∂ 2 ∂ t ​ ∂ x ​ X ​ ( t , x ) , t ≥ 0 , x ∈ ℝ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}u+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial x}X(t,x),\quad t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}} with initial condition u ⁡ ( 0 , x ) ≡ 0 u(0,x)\equiv 0 , where X X is a time-space white noise. 1.
Introduction

Let
u(t,x)
denote the solution to the stochastic heat equation

(1.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}u+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial x}X(t,x),\quad t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}

with initial condition
u(0,x)\equiv 0
, where
\dot{X}
is a
time-space white noise on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}
. That is,

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-r,x-y)X(dr,dy),

where
p(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}
is the heat kernel. Then, these processes
(t,x)\mapsto u(t,x)
,
t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)
and
x\mapsto u(\cdot,x)
are Gaussian. Swanson
[
32
]
has showed that

(1.2)

E\left[u(t,x)u(s,x)\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left((t+s)^{1/2}-|t-s|^{1/2}\right),\qquad t,s\geq 0,

and the process
t\mapsto u(t,x)
has a nontrivial quartic
variation. This shows that for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the process
t\mapsto u(t,x)
coincides with the bi-fractional Brownian motion and it is not a semimartingale, so a stochastic integral with respect to the process
t\mapsto u(t,x)
cannot be defined in the classical Itô sense. Some surveys and complete literatures for bi-fractional Brownian motion could be found in Houdré and Villa
[
14
]
, Kruk
et al

[
15
]
, Lei and Nualart
[
19
]
, Russo and Tudor
[
28
]
, Tudor and Xiao
[
35
]
and Yan
et al

[
38
]
, and the references therein. It is important to note for a large class of parabolic SPDEs, one obtains better regularity results when the solution
u
is viewed as a process
t\mapsto u(t,x)
taking values in Sobolev space, rather than for each fixed
x
. Denis
[
4
]
and Krylov
[
16
]
considered a class of stochastic partial differential equations driven by a multidimensional Brownian motion and showed that the solution is a Dirichlet processes. These inspire one to consider stochastic calculus with respect to the solution to the stochastic heat equation. It is well known that many authors have studied some Itô analysis questions of the solutions of some stochastic partial differential equations and introduced the related Itô and Tanaka formula (see, for examples, Da Prato
et al

[
3
]
, Deya and Tindel
[
5
]
, Gradinaru
et al

[
13
]
, Lanconelli
[
17
,
18
]
, León and Tindel
et al

[
20
]
, Nualart and Vuillermot
[
23
]
, Ouahhabi and Tudor
[
24
]
, Pardoux
[
25
]
, Torres
et al

[
33
]
, Tudor
[
34
]
, Tudor and Xiao
[
36
]
, Zambotti
[
41
]
, and the references therein). Almost all of these studies considered only the process in time, and there is a little discussion about the process
x\mapsto u(\cdot,x)
. This paper is an attempt to study stochastic analysis questions of the solution
u(t,x)
.

On the other hand, we shall see (in Section
3
) that
the process
x\mapsto u(\cdot,x)
admits a nontrivial finite quadratic variation coinciding with the classical Brownian motion in any finite interval, and moreover we shall also see (in Section
4
) that the forward integral of some adapted processes with respect to
x\mapsto u(\cdot,x)
coincides with ”Itô’s integral”. As a noise, the stochastic process
u=\{u(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is
very rough
in time and it is not white in space. However, the process
x\mapsto u(\cdot,x)
admits some characteristics similar to Brownian motion. These results, together with the works of Swanson
[
32
]
, point out that the process
u=\{u(t,x)\}
as a noise admits the next special structures:

•

It is very rough in time and similar to fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
H=\frac{1}{4}
, but it has not stationary increments.

•

It is not white in space, but its quadratic variation coincides with the classical Brownian motion and it is not self-similar.

•

The process in space variable is not a semimartingale, but the forward integral of some adapted processes with respect to the process in space variable coincides with ”Itô’s integral”.

•

The process
u=\{u(t,x)\}
admits a simple representation via Wiener integral with respect to Brownian sheet.

•

Though the process
u=\{u(t,x)\}
is Gaussian, as a noise, its time and space parts are farraginous. We can not decompose its covariance as the product of two independent parts. This is very different from fractional noise and white noise. In fact, we have

\displaystyle Eu(t,x)u(s,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t+s-2r)}\right\}dr

for all
t\geq s>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Therefore, it seems interesting to study the integrals

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)u(t,dx),\quad\int_{0}^{t}f(s)u(ds,x),\quad\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s,x)u(ds,dx),

and some related stochastic (partial) differential equations. For example, one can consider the following ”iterated” stochastic partial differential equations:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u^{j}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}u^{j}+f(u^{j})+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial x}u^{j-1}(t,x),\quad t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,

where
u^{0}
is a time-space white noise. Of course, one can also consider some sample path properties and singular integrals associated with the solution process
u=\{u(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
. We will carry out these projects in some forthcoming works. In the present paper our start points are to study the quadratic variations of the two processes
x\mapsto u(\cdot,x),t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)
. Our objects are to study the quadratic covariations of
x\mapsto u(\cdot,x)
and
t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)
, and moreover, we shall also introduce some generalized Itô formulas associated with
\{u(\cdot,x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
and
\{u(t,\cdot),t\geq 0\}
, respectively, and to consider their local times and Bouleau-Yor’s identities.

To expound our aim, let us start with a basic definition. An elementary calculation can show that (see Section
2
)

(1.3)

E[(u(t,x)-u(s,y))^{2}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\sqrt{2|t-s|}+\Delta(s,t,x-y)\right)

for all
t,s>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where

\Delta(s,t,z)=\int_{0}^{s\wedge t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(t-r)}}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{z^{2}}{2(t+s-2r)}\right\}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(s-r)}}\right)dr

for
t,s>0
and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
. This simple estimate inspires us to consider the following limits:

(1.4)

\begin{split}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}E[(u(t,x+\varepsilon)&-u(t,x))^{2}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4r}}\right)dr\\
&=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4}}\right)ds=1\end{split}

and

(1.5)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}E[(u(t+\varepsilon,x)-u(t,x))^{2}]=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}

for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. That is,

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\delta}E[(u(t+\varepsilon,x+\delta)-u(t,x))^{2}]=1,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\delta}E[(u(t+\varepsilon,x+\delta)-u(t,x))^{2}]=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}

for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. However, it is easy to see that the limit

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\to 0\\
\delta\to 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\delta}E[(u(t+\varepsilon,x+\delta)-u(t,x))^{2}]

does not exist for all
t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
by taking
\varepsilon=k\delta^{2}
with
k>0
. Thus, the next definition is natural.

Definition 1.1
.

Denote
B:=\{B_{t}:=u(t,\cdot),t\geq 0\}
and
W:=\{W_{x}:=u(\cdot,x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
. Let
I_{x}=[0,x]
for
x\geq 0
and
I_{x}=[x,0]
for
x\leq 0
. Define the integrals

\displaystyle I_{\delta}^{1}(f,x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{I_{x}}\left\{f(W_{y+\delta})-f(W_{y})\right\}(W_{y+\delta}-W_{y})dy,

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(f,x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{f(B_{s+\varepsilon})-f(B_{s})\right\}(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{s})\frac{ds}{2\sqrt{s}}

for all
t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}},\varepsilon,\delta>0
, where
f
is a measurable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
. The limits
\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}I_{\delta}^{1}(f,t,x)
and
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(f,t,x)
are called the partial quadratic covariations (PQC, in short) in space and in time, respectively, of
f(u)
and
u
, provided these limits exist in probability. We denote them by
[f(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}
and
[f(B),B]^{(TQ)}_{t}
, respectively.

Clearly, we have (see Section
3
)

[f(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}=\int_{I_{x}}f^{\prime}(W_{y})dy

and
[W,W]^{(SQ)}_{x}=|x|
for all
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We also have (see Section
6
)

[f(B),B]^{(TQ)}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(B_{s})\frac{ds}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}

and
[B,B]^{(TQ)}_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}t}
for all
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. These say that the process
W=\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
admits a nontrivial finite quadratic variation in any finite interval
I_{x}
. This is also a main motivation to study the solution of (
1.1
).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we establish some technical estimates associated with the solution, and as some applications we introduce Wiener integrals with respect to the two processes
B=\{B_{t}=u(t,\cdot),t\geq 0\}
and
W=\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
, respectively. In Section
3
we show that the quadratic variation
[W,W]^{(SQ)}
exists in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and equals to
|x|
in every finite interval
I_{x}
. For a given
t>0
, by estimating in
L^{2}

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{I_{x}}f(W_{y+\varepsilon})(W_{y+\varepsilon}-W_{y})dy\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}})

and

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{I_{x}}f(W_{y})(W_{y+\varepsilon}-W_{y})dy\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}})

for all
\varepsilon>0
, respectively, we construct a Banach space
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
of measurable functions such that the PQC
[f(W),W]^{(SQ)}
in space exists in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for all
f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
, and in particular we have

[f(W),W]_{x}^{(SQ)}=\int_{I_{x}}f^{\prime}(W_{y})dy

provided
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. In Section
4
, as an application of Section
3
, we show that the Itô’s formula

\displaystyle F(W_{x})=F(W_{0})+\int_{I_{x}}f(W_{y})\delta W_{y}+\frac{1}{2}[f(W),W]_{x}^{(SQ)}

holds for all
t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where the integral
\int_{I_{x}}f^{\prime}(W_{y})\delta W_{y}
denotes the Skorohod integral,
F
is an absolutely continuous function with the derivative
F^{\prime}=f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
. In order to show that the above Itô formula we first introduce a standard Itô type formula

(1.6)

F(W_{x})=F(W_{0})+\int_{I_{x}}F^{\prime}(W_{y})\delta W_{y}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{x}}F^{\prime\prime}(W_{y})dy

for all
F\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying some suitable conditions. It is important to note that the Gaussian process

W=\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
does not satisfy the condition in Alós
et al

[
1
]
since

\displaystyle E\left[u(t,x)^{2}\right]=\sqrt{\frac{t}{\pi}},\quad\frac{d}{x}E\left[u(t,x)^{2}\right]=0

for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We need to give the proof of the formula (
1.6
). Moreover, we also show that the forward integral (see Russo-Vallois
[
30
,
31
]
)

\int_{I_{x}}f(W_{y})d^{-}W_{y}:={\rm ucp}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{I_{x}}f(W_{y})\left(W_{y+\varepsilon}-W_{y}\right)dy

coincides with the Skorohod integral
\int_{I_{x}}f(W_{y})\delta W_{y}
, if
f
satisfies the growth condition

(1.7)

|f(y)|\leq Ce^{\beta{y^{2}}},\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}

with
0\leq\beta<\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{t}}
, where the notation
{\rm ucp}\lim
denotes the uniform convergence in probability on each compact interval. This is very similar to Brownian motion, but the process
W=\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is not a semimartingale. In Section
5
we consider some questions associated with the local time

{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(x,a)=\int_{0}^{x}\delta(W_{y}-a)dy

of the process
W=\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\geq 0\}
. In particular, we show that the Bouleau-Yor type identity

[f(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(v){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(x,dv)

holds for all
f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
. In Section
6
we consider some analysis questions associated with the quadratic covariation of the process
B=\{B_{t}=u(t,\cdot),t\geq 0\}
.

2.
Some basic estimates and divergence integrals

In this section we will establish divergence integral and some technical estimates associated with the solution

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-r,x-y)X(dr,dy),\quad t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
p(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}
is the heat kernel. For simplicity throughout this paper we let
C
stand for a positive constant depending only on the subscripts and its value may be different in different appearance, and this assumption is also adaptable to
c
. Moreover, we assume that the notation
F\asymp G
means that there are positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}G(x)\leq F(x)\leq c_{2}G(x)

in the common domain of definition for
F
and
G
.

The first object in this section is to introduce some basic estimates for the solution process
\{u(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
. We have

\displaystyle R_{x,y}(s,t):

\displaystyle=Eu(t,x)u(s,y)=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-r,x-z)p(s-r,y-z)dzdr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(t-r)(s-r)}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{2(t-r)}-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{2(s-r)}\right\}dzdr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t+s-2r)}\right\}dr

for all
t\geq s>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Denote

\Delta(s,t,u)=\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(t-r)}}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2(t+s-2r)}\right\}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(s-r)}}\right)dr

for
t\geq s
. Then, we have

(2.1)

E[(u(t,x)-u(s,y))^{2}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\sqrt{2(t-s)}+\Delta(s,t,x-y)\right)

for all
t>s>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
t\geq s>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

(2.2)

E\left[(u(t,x)-u(s,y))^{2}\right]\leq C\left(\sqrt{t-s}+|x-y|\right).

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\frac{2}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}

\displaystyle\left(1-\exp\left\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2(t+s-2r)}\right\}\right)dr

\displaystyle=|u|\int_{\frac{|u|}{\sqrt{t+s}}}^{\frac{|u|}{\sqrt{t-s}}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\right)\frac{dr}{r^{2}}\leq|u|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\right)\frac{dr}{r^{2}}=|u|\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}

for all
t\geq s>0
and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We get

\displaystyle\Delta(s,t,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(t-r)}}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(s-r)}}\right)dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{s}\frac{2}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}\left(1-\exp\left\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2(t+s-2r)}\right\}\right)dr

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2t}+\sqrt{2s}+(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{t-s}-2\sqrt{t+s}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{s}\frac{2}{\sqrt{t+s-2r}}\left(1-\exp\left\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2(t+s-2r)}\right\}\right)dr

\displaystyle\leq C\left((3-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{t-s}+|u|\right)

for all
t\geq s>0,u\in{\mathbb{R}}
by the next estimate:

\displaystyle 0\leq\sqrt{2t}+\sqrt{2s}

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{t-s}-2\sqrt{t+s}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{t-s}+\left(\sqrt{2t}-\sqrt{t+s}\right)+\sqrt{2s}-\sqrt{t+s}\leq 3\sqrt{t-s}.

It follows from (
2.1
) that

(2.3)

E[(u(t,x)-u(s,y))^{2}]\leq C\left(\sqrt{t-s}+|x-y|\right)

for all
t\geq s>0,x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
t,s,r>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\displaystyle|E\left[u(r,x)(u(t,x)-u(s,x))\right]|

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{|t-s|}.

Proof.

For all
t,s,r>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle E[u(r,x)(u(t,x)

\displaystyle-u(s,x))]=Eu(r,x)u(t,x)-Eu(r,x)u(s,x)

\displaystyle=R_{x,x}(r,t)-R_{x,x}(r,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\sqrt{t+r}-\sqrt{|t-r|}-\sqrt{s+r}+\sqrt{|s-r|}\right),

which gives

\displaystyle|E[u(r,x)

\displaystyle(u(t,x)-u(s,x))]|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sqrt{t+r}-\sqrt{s+r}\right|+\left|\sqrt{|t-r|}-\sqrt{|s-r|}\right|\leq 3\sqrt{|t-s|}

for all
t,s,r>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
t>0
and
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\displaystyle|E\left[u(t,x)(u(t,y)-u(t,z))\right]|

\displaystyle\leq C|y-z|.

Proof.

For all
t>0
and
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle E[

\displaystyle u(t,x)(u(t,y)-u(t,z))]=Eu(t,x)u(t,y)-Eu(t,x)u(t,z)

\displaystyle=R_{x,y}(t,t)-R_{x,z}(t,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right\}dr-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right\}dr\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(|x-y|\int_{|x-y|}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds-|x-z|\int_{|x-z|}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds\right).

Consider the function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
defined by

f(x)=x\int_{x}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds=e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}-\frac{x}{2t}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}dr

Then, by Mean value theorem we have

\displaystyle\left|f(u)-f(v)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2t}|u-v|\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2t}|u-v|\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds\leq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{t}}|u-v|

for all
u,v\geq 0
and some
\xi
between
u
and
v
. It follows that

|E[u(t,x)(u(t,y)-u(t,z))]|\leq\frac{1}{4}||x-y|-|x-z||\leq\frac{1}{4}|y-z|

for all
t>0
and
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
t>s>t^{\prime}>s^{\prime}>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

(2.4)

|E\left[(u(t,x)-u(s,x))(u(t^{\prime},x)-u(s^{\prime},x))\right]|\leq\frac{C(t^{\prime}-s^{\prime})\sqrt{t-s}}{\sqrt{ts(s-s^{\prime})(t-t^{\prime})}}

Proof.

For all
t>s>t^{\prime}>s^{\prime}>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\displaystyle E[(u(t,x)

\displaystyle-u(s,x))(u(t^{\prime},x)-u(s^{\prime},x))]

\displaystyle=R_{x,x}(t,t^{\prime})-R_{x,x}(s,t^{\prime})-R_{x,x}(t,s^{\prime})+R_{x,x}(s,s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\sqrt{t+t^{\prime}}-\sqrt{t-t^{\prime}}-\sqrt{s+t^{\prime}}+\sqrt{s-t^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\sqrt{t+s^{\prime}}+\sqrt{t-s^{\prime}}+\sqrt{s+s^{\prime}}-\sqrt{s-s^{\prime}}\right).

Consider the function

f(x)=\sqrt{t+x}-\sqrt{t-x}-\sqrt{s+x}+\sqrt{s-x}

with
x\in[0,s]
. Then, we have

\displaystyle E[(u(t,x)-u(s,x))(u(t^{\prime},x)-u(s^{\prime},x))]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(f(t^{\prime})-f(s^{\prime})\right),

and by Mean value theorem

\displaystyle\left|f(t^{\prime})-f(s^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(t^{\prime}-s^{\prime})\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+\xi}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-\xi}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s+\xi}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s-\xi}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(t^{\prime}-s^{\prime})\left(\frac{\sqrt{t+\xi}-\sqrt{s+\xi}}{\sqrt{t+\xi}\sqrt{s+\xi}}+\frac{\sqrt{t-\xi}-\sqrt{s-\xi}}{\sqrt{s-\xi}\sqrt{t-\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(t^{\prime}-s^{\prime})\sqrt{t-s}}{\sqrt{ts(s-s^{\prime})(t-t^{\prime})}}

for some
s^{\prime}\leq\xi\leq t^{\prime}
, which shows the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
t>0
and

we have

(2.5)

|E\left[(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(u(t,x^{\prime})-u(t,y^{\prime}))\right]|\leq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{t\pi}}(x-y)(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})e^{-\frac{(y-x^{\prime})^{2}}{4t}}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle E[

\displaystyle(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(u(t,x^{\prime})-u(t,y^{\prime}))]

\displaystyle=R_{x,x^{\prime}}(t,t)-R_{x,y^{\prime}}(t,t)-R_{y,x^{\prime}}(t,t)+R_{y,y^{\prime}}(t,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}{4r}\right\}dr-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y^{\prime})^{2}}{4r}\right\}dr\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(y-x^{\prime})^{2}}{4r}\right\}dr+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(y-y^{\prime})^{2}}{4r}\right\}dr\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left((x-x^{\prime})\int_{(x-x^{\prime})}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds-(x-y^{\prime})\int_{(x-y^{\prime})}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-(y-x^{\prime})\int_{(y-x^{\prime})}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds+(y-y^{\prime})\int_{(y-y^{\prime})}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds\right)

for all

and
x>y>x^{\prime}>y^{\prime}
. Similar to the proof of Lemma

Then, we have

\displaystyle|E[

\displaystyle(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(u(t,x^{\prime})-u(t,y^{\prime}))]|

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left|f(x-x^{\prime})-f(x-y^{\prime})-f(y-x^{\prime})+f(y-y^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(x-y)\left|f^{\prime}(\xi-x^{\prime})-f^{\prime}(\xi-y^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{t}\sqrt{\pi}}(x-y)\left|\int_{\xi-x^{\prime}}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds-\int_{\xi-y^{\prime}}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds\right|

Lemma 2.6
.

For all
t>s>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
denote

\sigma^{2}_{t,x}=E\left[u(t,x)^{2}\right]
,
\sigma^{2}_{s,x}=E\left[u(s,x)^{2}\right]
,
\mu_{t,s,x}=E\left[u(t,x)u(s,x)\right]
. Then we have

(2.6)

\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{s(t-s)}\leq\sigma^{2}_{t,x}\sigma^{2}_{s,x}-\mu_{t,s,x}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{\pi}\sqrt{s(t-s)}.

Proof.

Given
t>s>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We have

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{t,x}\sigma^{2}_{s,x}-\mu_{t,s,x}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(2\sqrt{ts}-\left((t+s)^{1/2}-(t-s)^{1/2}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\sqrt{ts}-t+\sqrt{t^{2}-s^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\pi}\left(\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}-1\right)

with
z=\frac{s}{t}
. Clearly,

\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}-1=\sqrt{z}-\left(1-\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\right)\geq\sqrt{z}-\sqrt{z^{2}}\geq\sqrt{z(1-z)}

for all
0\leq z\leq 1
. Conversely, we have also that

\displaystyle 0\leq\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{1-z}-1

\displaystyle=\sqrt{z(1-z)}+\sqrt{z}+\left(\sqrt{1-z}-\sqrt{z(1-z)}\right)-1

\displaystyle=\sqrt{z(1-z)}+\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{1-z}\left(1-\sqrt{z}\right)-1

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{z(1-z)}+\left(\sqrt{z}-z\right)\leq 2\sqrt{z(1-z)},

which gives

\displaystyle\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{1-z^{2}}-1

\displaystyle=\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{1-z+z-z^{2}}-1

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{1-z}-1+\sqrt{z(1-z)}\leq 3\sqrt{z(1-z)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For all
t>0
and
x>y
denote

\mu_{t,x,y}=E\left[u(t,x)u(t,y)\right]
. Then, under the conditions of Lemma
2.6

(2.7)

\sigma^{2}_{t,x}\sigma^{2}_{t,y}-\mu_{t,x,y}^{2}\asymp\frac{(x-y)t}{\sqrt{t}+x-y}.

In particular, we have

(2.8)

0\leq\sigma^{2}_{t,z}-\mu_{t,x,y}\asymp\frac{(x-y)\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{t}+x-y}

for all
t>0
and
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Given
t>0
and
x>y
. We have

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{t,x}\sigma^{2}_{t,y}-\mu_{t,x,y}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\pi}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4r}\right\}dr\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(4t-\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4r}\right\}dr\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{r}}\left(1-\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4r}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{r}}\left(1+\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4r}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\sqrt{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{r}}\left(1-\exp\left\{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4r}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{t}(x-y)\int_{\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{t}}}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4}}\right)\frac{ds}{s^{2}}.

\displaystyle=F_{n}(W_{y})-F_{n}(W_{0})-\frac{1}{2}[f_{n}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow F(W_{y})-F_{n}(W_{0})-\frac{1}{2}[f(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, as
n
tends to infinity. This completes the proof since the integral is closed in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Now, similar to proof of Theorem
4.1
one can introduce Theorem
4.2
. But, we need to give the following standard Itô type formula:

(4.9)

F(W_{x})=F(W_{0})+\int_{I_{x}}F^{\prime}(W_{y})\delta W_{y}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{x}}F^{\prime\prime}(W_{y})dy

for all
F\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying the condition

(4.10)

|F(y)|,|F^{\prime}(y)|,|F^{\prime\prime}(y)|\leq Ce^{\beta{y^{2}}},\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}

with
0\leq\beta<\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{t}}
. It is important to note that one have given a standard Itô formula for a large class of Gaussian processes in Alós
et al

[
1
]
. However, the process

does not satisfy the condition in Alós
et al

[

]
since

\displaystyle E\left[u(t,x)^{2}\right]=\sqrt{\frac{t}{\pi}},\quad\frac{d}{dx}E\left[u(t,x)^{2}\right]=0

for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. So, we need to give the proof of the formula (
4.9
) in order to prove Theorem
4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let
x^{n}_{j}=\frac{jx}{n};j=0,1,\ldots,n
. Then we have

(4.11)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}}\right)^{2}\longrightarrow|x|,

in
L^{2}
, as
n
tends to infinity.

Proof.

Similar to proof of Proposition
3.1
one can introduce the lemma, so we omit it.
∎

Proof of (
4.9
).

Let us fix
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let
\pi\equiv\{x^{n}_{j}=\frac{jx}{n};j=0,1,\ldots,n\}
be a partition of
I_{x}
. Clearly, the growth condition (
4.4
) implies that

(4.12)

E\left[\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|G(W_{x})|^{p}\right]\leq c^{p}E\left[e^{p\beta\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|W_{x}|}\right]<\infty

for some constant
c>0
and all
p<\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\beta\sqrt{t}}
, where
G\in\{F,F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime}\}
. In particular, the estimate (
4.12
) holds for
p=2
. Using Taylor expansion, we have

(4.13)

\begin{split}F(W_{x})&=F(W_{0})+\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{j}(\theta_{j}))(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})^{2}\\
&\equiv F(W_{0})+I^{n}+J^{n}\end{split}

where

W_{j}(\theta_{j})=W_{x^{n}_{j-1}}+\theta_{j}(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})

with
\theta_{j}\in(0,1)
being a random variable. By (
2.1
) we have

\displaystyle I^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})(\delta^{t}(1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}))

\displaystyle=\delta^{t}\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}f^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x_{j}]}(\cdot)\right)+\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})\langle 1_{(0,x^{n}_{j-1}]},1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}

\displaystyle\equiv I^{n}_{1}+I^{n}_{2}.

Now, in order to end the proof we claim that the following convergences in
L^{2}
hold:

(4.14)

\displaystyle I^{n}_{2}\longrightarrow-\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})dy,

(4.15)

\displaystyle I^{n}_{1}\longrightarrow\int_{I_{x}}F^{\prime}(W_{y})\delta W_{y},

(4.16)

\displaystyle J^{n}\longrightarrow\int^{t}_{0}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})dy,

as
n
tends to infinity.

To prove the first convergence, it is enough to establish that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}:=E\left|I^{n}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j}})(x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1})\right|^{2}\longrightarrow 0,

as
n
tends to infinity. By Minkowski inequality we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Lambda_{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(E\left|\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})\left\{\langle 1_{(0,x^{n}_{j-1}]},1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1})\right\}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum^{n}_{j=1}\left|\langle 1_{(0,x^{n}_{j-1}]},1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1})\right|

\displaystyle=C\sum^{n}_{j=1}\left|\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{1}{4r}(x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1})^{2}}\right)dr-\frac{1}{2}(x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1})\right|

\displaystyle=C\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1}|\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\frac{x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1}}{\sqrt{2t}}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\right)ds-\frac{1}{2}\right|

\displaystyle=C\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1}|\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\frac{x^{n}_{j}-x^{n}_{j-1}}{\sqrt{2t}}}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\right)ds\right|

\displaystyle=C|x|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\frac{|x|}{n\sqrt{2t}}}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\right)ds\longrightarrow 0,

as
n
tends to infinity by the fact

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}\right)ds=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds=\frac{1}{2}.

Now, we prove the third convergence. We have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(2):

\displaystyle=E\left|J^{n}-\int_{I_{x}}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})dy\right|

\displaystyle=E\left|\frac{1}{2}\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{j}(\theta_{j}))(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})^{2}-\int_{I_{x}}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})dy\right|.

Suppose that
n\geq m
, and for any
j=1,\ldots,n
let us denote by
x^{m(n)}_{j}
the point of the
m
th partition that is closer to
x^{n}_{j}
from the left. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}E\left|\sum^{n}_{j=1}\left(F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{j}(\theta_{j}))-F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{x^{m(n)}_{j}})\right)(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}E\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{x^{m(n)}_{j}})\sum_{\{j:x^{m(n)}_{k-1}\leq x^{m(n)}_{j-1}<x^{m(n)}_{k}\}}\left((W_{x_{j}^{n}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})^{2}-(x_{j}^{n}-x^{n}_{j-1})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+E\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{x^{n}_{k-1}}^{x^{n}_{k}}\left(F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{x^{m(n)}_{j}})-F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{n}(2,1)+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{n}(2,2)+\Lambda_{n}(2,3).

Clearly, we have that
\Lambda_{n}(2,2)\to 0
(
n,m\to\infty
) by Lemma
4.1
and the estimate (
4.12
),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(2,3)

\displaystyle\leq|x|E\sup_{|z-y|\leq\frac{|x|}{m}}|F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{z})-F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})|\longrightarrow 0\quad(m\to\infty)

and

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(2,1)

\displaystyle=E\left|\sum^{n}_{j=1}\left(F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{j}(\theta_{j}))-F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{x^{m(n)}_{j}})\right)(W_{x_{j}}-W_{x_{j-1}})^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq CE\left\{\sup_{|z-y|\leq\frac{|x|}{n}}|F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{z})-F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})|\sum^{n}_{j=1}(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{E\sup_{|z-y|\leq\frac{|x|}{n}}|F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{z})-F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})|^{2}E\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}(W_{x^{n}_{j}}-W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})^{2}\right)^{2}\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C|x|\left\{E\sup_{|z-y|\leq\frac{|x|}{n}}|F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{z})-F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})|^{2}\right\}^{1/2}\longrightarrow 0\quad(n\to\infty)

by (
2.2
) and the estimate (
4.12
). Thus, we obtain the third convergence, i.e.,
J^{n}\to\int^{t}_{0}F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(W_{y})dy
in
L^{1}
.

Finally, to end the proof we show that the second convergence:

I^{n}_{1}=\delta^{t}\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}(\cdot)\right)\longrightarrow\int_{I_{x}}F^{\prime}(W_{y})\delta W_{y}\quad(n\to\infty).

We need to show that

A_{n}:=\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}(\cdot)\longrightarrow F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{\cdot})1_{I_{x}}(\cdot)

in
L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathcal{H}}_{t})
, as
n
tends to infinity. We have

\displaystyle E\|A_{n}

\displaystyle-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{\cdot})1_{I_{x}}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{t}}

\displaystyle=E\Bigl\|\sum^{n}_{j=1}\left(F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x_{j-1}})-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{\cdot})\right)1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}(\cdot)\Bigr\|^{2}_{|\mathcal{H}_{t}|}

\displaystyle=E\sum^{n}_{j,l=1}\int^{x^{n}_{j}}_{x^{n}_{j-1}}\int_{x^{n}_{l-1}}^{x^{n}_{l}}\left|F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{y})\right|\left|F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{z})\right|\phi(y,z)dydz

\displaystyle\leq E\sup_{|y-z|\leq\frac{|x|}{n}}\left|F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{y})-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{z})\right|^{2}\int_{I_{x}}\int_{I_{x}}\phi(y,z)dydz

\displaystyle=E\sup_{|y-z|\leq\frac{|x|}{n}}\left|F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{y})-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{z})\right|^{2}\int_{I_{x}}\int_{I_{x}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4t}}dydz

\displaystyle\leq E\sup_{|y-z|\leq\frac{|x|}{n}}\left|F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{y})-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{z})\right|^{2}\int_{I_{x}}dz\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4t}}dy

\displaystyle\leq|x|E\sup_{|y-z|\leq\frac{|x|}{n}}\left|F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{y})-F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{z})\right|^{2}\longrightarrow 0,

as
n
tends to infinity by the estimate (
4.12
), which implies that

A_{n}:=\sum^{n}_{j=1}F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{x^{n}_{j-1}})1_{(x^{n}_{j-1},x^{n}_{j}]}(\cdot)\longrightarrow F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{\cdot})1_{I_{x}}(\cdot)

in
L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathcal{H}}_{t})
, as
n
tends to infinity. The above steps prove that

\displaystyle I^{n}_{1}=F(W_{x})-F(W_{0})-I^{n}_{2}-J^{n}\longrightarrow F(W_{x})-F(W_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{x}}F^{\prime\prime}(W_{y})dy

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, as
n
tends to infinity. This completes the proof since the integral
\int_{0}^{\cdot}u_{s}\delta W_{s}
is closed in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

5.
The Bouleau-Yor identity of
\{u(\cdot,x),x\geq 0\}

In this section, we consider the local time of the process
\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\geq 0\}
. Our main object is to prove that the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(a){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(x,da)

is well-defined and that the identity

(5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(a){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(x,da)=-[g(u(t,\cdot)),u(t,\cdot)]_{x}^{(SQ)}

holds for all
g\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
and
t>0
, where

{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(x,a)=\int_{I_{x}}\delta(u(t,y)-a)dy.

is the local time of
\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\geq 0\}
. The identity (
5.1
) is called the Bouleau-Yor identity. More works for this can be found in Bouleau-Yor
[
2
]
, Eisenbaum
[
6
,
7
]
, Föllmer
et al

[
10
]
, Feng–Zhao
[
8
,
9
]
,
Peskir
[
26
]
, Rogers–Walsh
[
27
]
,
Yan
et al

[
39
,
40
]
, and the references therein.

Recall that for any closed interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and for any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the local time
L(a,I)
of
u
is defined as the density of the occupation measure
\mu_{I}
defined by

\mu_{I}(A)=\int_{I}1_{A}(W_{x})dx

It can be shown (see Geman and Horowitz
[
11
]
, Theorem 6.4) that the following occupation density formula holds:

\int_{I}g(W_{x},x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}da\int_{I}g(a,x)L(a,dx)

for every Borel function
g(a,x)\geq 0
on
I\times{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus, some estimates in Section
2
and Theorem 21.9 in Geman-Horowitz
[
11
]
together imply that the following result holds.

Corollary 5.1
.

The local time
{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x):=L(a,[0,x])
of
W=\{W_{x}=u(\cdot,x),x\geq 0\}
exists and
{\mathscr{L}}^{t}\in L^{2}(\lambda\times P)
for all
x\geq 0
and
(a,x)\mapsto{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x)
is jointly continuous, where
\lambda
denotes Lebesgue measure. Moreover, the occupation formula

(5.2)

\int_{0}^{t}\psi(W_{x},x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}da\int_{0}^{t}\psi(a,x){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,dx)

holds for every continuous and bounded function
\psi(a,x):{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and any
x\geq 0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
f_{\triangle}=\sum_{j}f_{j}1_{(a_{j-1},a_{j}]}\in{\mathscr{E}}
, we define

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\triangle}(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(dy,x):=\sum_{j}f_{j}\left[{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a_{j},x)-{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a_{j-1},x)\right].

Then the integral is well-defined and

(5.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\Delta}(y)\mathscr{L}^{t}(dy,x)=-\bigl[f_{\triangle}(W),W\bigr]^{(SQ)}_{x}

almost surely, for all
x\geq 0
.

Proof.

For the function
f_{\triangle}(y)=1_{(a,b]}(y)
we define the sequence of smooth functions
f_{n},\;n=1,2,\ldots
by

(5.4)

\displaystyle f_{n}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\triangle}(y-z)\zeta_{n}(z)dz=\int_{a}^{b}\zeta_{n}(y-z)dz

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\zeta_{n},n\geq 1
are the so-called mollifiers given in (
4.7
). Then
\{f_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\cap{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
and
f_{n}
converges to
f_{\triangle}
in
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
, as
n
tends to infinity. It follows from the occupation formula that

\displaystyle[f_{n}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}f^{\prime}_{n}(W_{y})dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{n}^{\prime}(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\zeta_{n}^{\prime}(y-z)dz\right){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)\left(\zeta_{n}(y-b)-\zeta_{n}(y-a)\right)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)\zeta_{n}(y-a)dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)\zeta_{n}(y-b)dy

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x)-{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(b,x)

almost surely, as
n\to\infty
, by the continuity of
y\mapsto{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)
. On the other hand, we see also that there exists a subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\}
such that

[f_{n_{k}}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}\longrightarrow[1_{(a,b]}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}

for all
x\geq 0
, almost surely, as
k\to\infty
since
f_{n}
converges to
f_{\triangle}
in
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
. It follows that

[1_{(a,b]}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}=\left({\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x)-{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(b,x)\right)

for all
x\geq 0
, almost surely. Thus, the identity

\sum_{j}f_{j}[{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a_{j},x)-{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a_{j-1},x)]=-[f_{\triangle}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}

follows from the linearity property, and the lemma follows.
∎

As a direct consequence of the above lemma, for every
f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{\triangle,n}(y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{\triangle,n}(x)=f(y)

in
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
, where
\{f_{\triangle,n}\},\{g_{\triangle,n}\}\subset{\mathscr{E}}
, we then have that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle f_{\triangle,n}(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)dy=-\lim_{n\to\infty}[f_{\triangle,n}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}=-[f(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{n\to\infty}[g_{\triangle,n}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\triangle,n}(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)dy

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus, by the denseness of
{\mathscr{E}}
in
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
we can define

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(dy,x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\triangle,n}(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(dy,x)

for any
f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
, where
\{f_{\triangle,n}\}\subset{\mathscr{E}}
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{\triangle,n}=f

in
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
. These considerations are enough to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
, the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(dy,x)

is well-defined in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and the Bouleau-Yor type identity

(5.5)

[f(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\mathscr{L}^{t}(dy,x)

holds, almost surely, for all
x\geq 0
.

Corollary 5.2

(Tanaka formula)
.

For any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\displaystyle(W_{x}-a)^{+}=(W_{0}-a)^{+}+\int_{0}^{x}{1}_{\{W_{y}>a\}}\delta W_{y}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x),

\displaystyle(W_{x}-a)^{-}=(W_{0}-a)^{-}-\int_{0}^{x}{1}_{\{W_{y}<a\}}\delta W_{y}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x),

\displaystyle|W_{x}-a|=|W_{0}-a|+\int_{0}^{x}{\rm sign}(W_{x}-a)\delta W_{y}+{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x).

Proof.

Take
F(y)=(y-x)^{+}
. Then
F
is absolutely continuous and

F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{y}1_{(x,\infty)}(y)dy.

It follows from the identity (
5.3
) and Itô’s formula (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(a,x)

\displaystyle=[1_{(a,+\infty)}(W),W]^{(SQ)}_{x}

\displaystyle=2(W_{x}-a)^{+}-2(-a)^{+}-2\int_{0}^{x}{1}_{\{W_{y}>a\}}\delta W_{y}

for all
x\geq 0
, which gives the first identity. In the same
way one can obtain the second identity, and by subtracting the last identity from the previous one, we get the third identity.
∎

According to Theorem
5.1
, we get an analogue of the Itô formula (Bouleau-Yor type formula).

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
be a left continuous function with right limits. If
F
is an absolutely continuous function with
F^{\prime}=f
, then the following Itô type formula holds:

(5.6)

F(W_{x})=F(W_{0})+\int_{0}^{x}f(W_{y})\delta W_{y}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(dy,x).

Recall that if
F
is the difference of two convex functions, then

F
is an absolutely continuous function with derivative of bounded
variation. Thus, the Itô-Tanaka formula

\displaystyle F(W_{x})

\displaystyle=F(0)+\int_{0}^{x}F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{y})\delta W_{y}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathscr{L}}^{t}(y,x)F^{\prime\prime}(dy)

\displaystyle\equiv F(0)+\int_{0}^{x}F^{{}^{\prime}}(W_{y})\delta W_{y}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime}(y){\mathscr{L}}^{t}(dy,x)

holds.

6.
The quadratic covariation of process
\{u(t,\cdot),t\geq 0\}

In this section, we study the existence of the
PQC
[f(u(\cdot,x)),u(\cdot,x)]^{(TQ)}
. Recall that

I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(f,x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{f(u(s+\varepsilon,x))-f(u(s,x))\right\}(u(s+\varepsilon,x)-u(s,x))\frac{ds}{2\sqrt{s}}

for
\varepsilon>0,t\geq 0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

(6.1)

[f(u(\cdot,x)),u(\cdot,x)]^{(TQ)}_{t}=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(f,x,t),

provided the limit exists in probability. In this section, we study some analysis questions of the process
\{u(t,\cdot),t\geq 0\}
associated with the quadratic covariation
[f(u(\cdot,x)),u(\cdot,x)]^{(TQ)}
, and the researches include the existence of the
PQC
[f(u(\cdot,x)),u(\cdot,x)]^{(TQ)}
, the Itô and Tanaka formulas. Recall that

\displaystyle E\left[u(t,x)^{2}\right]=\sqrt{\frac{t}{\pi}}

for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Denote

B_{t}=u(t,\cdot),\quad t\in[0,T].

It follows from Alós
et al

[
1
]
that the Itô formula

(6.2)

f(B_{t})=f(0)+\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(B_{s})\delta B_{s}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime\prime}(B_{s})\frac{ds}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}

for all
t\in[0,T]
and

Proof.

The proof is similar to Proposition

\displaystyle\qquad-\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon}{\pi}}E\left((B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{s})^{2}+(B_{r+\varepsilon}-B_{r})^{2}\right).

Sandpile groups of supersingular isogeny graphs
Nathanaël Munier, Ari Shnidman

Abstract
. Let p p and q q be distinct primes, and let X p , q X_{p,q} be the ( q + 1 ) (q+1) -regular graph whose nodes are supersingular elliptic curves over 𝔽 ¯ p \overline{{\rm\mathbb{F}}}_{p} and whose edges are q q -isogenies. For fixed p p , we compute the distribution of the ℓ \ell -Sylow subgroup of the sandpile group (i.e. Jacobian) of X p , q X_{p,q} as q → ∞ q\to\infty . We find that the distribution disagrees with the Cohen-Lenstra heuristic in this context. Our proof is via Galois representations attached to modular curves. As a corollary of our result, we give an upper bound on the probability that the Jacobian is cyclic, which we conjecture to be sharp.



1.
Introduction

Attached to any finite undirected graph
X
is a finite abelian group
J(X)
, called the Jacobian or sandpile group of
X
.
1
1

1

Other names for it are the critical group and the divisor class group. See Section
2
for the definition.
One may think of
J(X)
as the class group of the graph, in analogy with the divisor class group of an algebraic curve or the ideal class group of a number field.

For any prime number
\ell
, let
J(X)[\ell^{\infty}]
denote the subgroup of elements killed by some power of
\ell
. As with the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics for ideal class groups
[
CL84
]
, we can ask about the distribution of the finite abelian
\ell
-group
J(X)[\ell^{\infty}]
, as
X
varies over a countable family of graphs.
2
2

2

For the families we consider, the size of
J(X)
grows, so we cannot make sense of the analogous question for the entire group
J(X)
.
Recently, Wood
[
Woo17
]
has verified a Cohen-Lenstra-type heuristic for Erdős-Rényi random graphs, and Mészáros proved analogous results for random regular graphs
[
M2́0
]
.

In this paper, we compute these distributions for certain families of Ramanujan graphs, namely supersingular isogeny graphs.
To define these graphs, let
p
and
q
be distinct prime numbers, and assume
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, for simplicity. Let
X_{p}
be the set of isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves
E
over
\overline{{\rm\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
, a set of size
n:=(p-1)/12
. See
[
Sil86
, §5.4]
for background on supersingular elliptic curves. Each
E\in X_{p}
contains
q+1
distinct subgroups of order
q
, and hence admits
q+1
degree
q
isogenies
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
, up to isomorphism. Moreover, each
q
-isogeny
E\to E^{\prime}
admits a dual
q
-isogeny
E^{\prime}\to E
in the opposite direction. Let
X_{p,q}
denote the undirected
(q+1)
-regular graph whose vertex set is
X_{p}
and whose edges correspond to
q
-isogenies. Set
J_{p,q}:=J(X_{p,q})
.

Our main results determine the distribution of the finite abelian groups
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
, when
p
and
\ell
are fixed and
q\to\infty
. Thus, the number of vertices is fixed, while the degree of the graph goes to infinity. Our proof makes use of the link between the graphs
X_{p,q}
and the Galois representation attached to the modular curve
X_{0}(p)
, as we shall explain. It would be interesting to consider families where
q
is fixed and
p\to\infty
, but this would require different methods.

1.1.
Results

When
p
is fixed, the groups
J_{p,q}
carry an extra structure which heavily influences their distribution. Namely, they are each modules for a certain ring
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
, called the Hecke algebra. To define
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
, let
L_{q}
be the Laplacian of
X_{p,q}
, viewed as a linear operator on the space of functions
{\rm Hom}(X_{p},{\rm\mathbb{Z}})
. Note that as
q
varies, the operators
L_{q}
act on the same space of functions. Crucially, they commute with each other. Define the submodule of degree-zero functions

M=\{f\in{\rm Hom}(X_{p},{\rm\mathbb{Z}})\colon\sum_{E\in X_{p}}f(E)=0\},

on which
L_{q}
acts as well. Then
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
is the commutative subring of
{\rm End}_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}(M)
generated by the endomorphisms
L_{q}
. It is known to be semisimple and of rank
n-1={\rm rk}_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}M
as a
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
-module, which means that
M
is rank 1 (not necessarily free) as a
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-module. The Jacobian
J_{p,q}
is then a module for the Hecke algebra
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
. In fact,
J_{p,q}
is essentially the cokernel
M/L_{q}M
. More precisely, for
\ell\nmid n
, we have

J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq M_{\ell}/L_{q}M_{\ell},

(1.1)

where
M_{\ell}=M\otimes_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
(see Proposition
9
). Given this, one might naively guess that the groups
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
should be distributed as a random
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-module of the form
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/a{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
, where
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}={\rm\mathbb{T}}\otimes_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
and
a\in{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
is sampled according to Haar measure. However, this turns out to be incorrect, because it ignores the particular form of the elements
L_{q}
.

By definition
L_{q}=q+1-T_{q}
, where
T_{q}\in{\rm\mathbb{T}}
is the image of the adjacency matrix of
X_{p,q}
, thought of as an element of
{\rm End}_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}\,{\rm Hom}(X_{p},{\rm\mathbb{Z}})
. Thus
L_{q}
is (formally) the determinant of
x-1
acting on the free rank two
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-module
{\rm\mathbb{T}}[x]/(x^{2}-T_{q}x+q)
. This rank two module arises naturally in the context of Hashimoto’s edge-adjacency operator, whose characteristic polynomial computes the zeta function of the graph. This suggests a different model for the groups
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
, namely as random cokernels of the form
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/\det(g-1){\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
, where
g
is sampled from the group

{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}:=\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell})\colon\det(g)\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}\},

endowed with its
\ell
-adic topology and its probability Haar measure.

Our first result states that for all but finitely many primes
\ell
, this is indeed the correct distribution. For an arbitrary
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-module
G
, define

{\rm\mathbb{P}}(J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq G):=\lim_{X\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{q<X\colon J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq G\}}{\#\{q<X\}},

which is the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is isomorphic to
G
as
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-modules (or
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
-modules). Let
{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
be the discriminant of the ring
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
.

Theorem 1
.

Fix a prime
\ell
not dividing
6n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
, and let
G
be a finite
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
-module. Let
\mu
be the Haar probability measure on the group
{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}
. Then

{\rm\mathbb{P}}(J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq G)=\mu(g\in{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}\colon{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/\det(g-1){\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\simeq G).

Before discussing the proof of Theorem
1
, we address the natural follow-up questions:
what is the distribution in Theorem
1
, in concrete terms? The first thing to observe is that the distribution behaves differently depending on the
\ell
-adic valuation of
q-1
. Indeed, the cokernel
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/\det(g-1){\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
measures the
\ell
-adic distance from 1 to the two eigenvalues of
g
. If
\det g
(which we imagine is
q
) is itself
\ell
-adically close to
1
, then both of the eigenvalues can be close to 1, whereas if
\det g
is not close to 1, then at most one eigenvalue can be close to
1
.

With this phenomenon in mind, we only give an explicit formula for the distribution as
q
varies through primes
q\not\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
; the case of
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
is more complicated. We may write
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
, where each
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
is a finite free ring extension of
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
of degree
d_{i}
. Since
\ell
does not divide the discriminant of
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
, each
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal
\ell{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
and residue field
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/\ell{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
of size
\ell^{d_{i}}
. The following result computes the probability
{\rm\mathbb{P}}(J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq G)
in Theorem
1
, in terms of the integers
d_{1},\ldots,d_{t}
. Since
M_{\ell}
is a rank one
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
-module, this probability is 0 unless
G
is isomorphic to
G_{\rm{\bf k}}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/\ell^{k_{i}}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
, for some tuple
{\rm{\bf k}}=(k_{i})\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}^{t}_{\geq 0}
.

Theorem 2
.

Assume
\ell
does not divide
6n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
. Let
\delta_{ij}
be Kronecker’s delta function.
As
q\to\infty
varying through primes
q\not\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
, we have

{\rm\mathbb{P}}(J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq G_{\rm{\bf k}})=\frac{1}{\#G_{\rm{\bf k}}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{d_{i}}-1}\right)^{\delta_{0k_{i}}}.

Note that
G_{\rm{\bf k}}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}({\rm\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{k_{i}}{\rm\mathbb{Z}})^{d_{i}}
, as abstract abelian groups. Thus, one can easily determine from Theorem
2
the explicit distribution of the abstract abelian groups
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
. To compute
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
(and hence the integers
d_{i}
), one can use sage or Magma. See Section 6 for some worked out examples.

It is interesting to compare the distribution in Theorem
2
with a naive Cohen-Lenstra-type heuristic. As explained in
[
CLP15
]
, because the Jacobian of a graph
X
is naturally endowed with a perfect symmetric bilinear form, the Cohen-Lenstra heuristic in the setting of sandpile groups predicts that for each finite abelian group
G
,

{\rm\mathbb{P}}(J(X)\simeq G)\propto\frac{\#\{\mbox{perfect symmetric bilinear }G\times G\to{\rm\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\}}{\#G\#{\rm Aut}(G)}.

This is what Wood proves in the context of Erdos-Renyi random graphs. In our case,
J_{p,q}
is a rank one
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-module, and the number of perfect
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-linear pairings of a rank one
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-module is equal to the size of its automorphism group. Thus the naive Cohen-Lenstra heuristic would predict

{\rm\mathbb{P}}(J_{p,q}\simeq G_{\rm{\bf k}})\propto\frac{1}{\#G_{\rm{\bf k}}},

which also agrees with the naive guess that
J(X_{p,q})[\ell^{\infty}]
is modeled by the groups
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/a{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
for
a\in{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
. This is nearly what we find in Theorem
2
, except there is an extra factor of
1-\frac{1}{\ell^{d_{i}}-1}
for each trivial component of
G_{\rm{\bf k}}
(i.e. for each
i
such that
k_{i}=0
). We see that for Jacobians of supersingular isogeny graphs, there is a slight and unexpected bias towards
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-modules with non-trivial components.

For any fixed
t\geq 0
, one can give explicit formulas for the distribution of the groups
J_{p,q}
as
q\to\infty
varies through primes such that
v_{\ell}(q-1)=k
, but these formulas seem quite complicated for
k>0
. This is somewhat analogous to the complications that arise in the Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet heuristics when the ground field contains
\ell
-th roots of unity
[
Gar15
]
.

One statistical quantity which we found accessible without any restriction on
q
, is the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is cyclic.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\ell
be a prime not dividing
6n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
. Recall
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
, with each
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
unramified over
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
of degree
d_{i}
. Let
t_{1}
be the number of factors with
d_{i}=1
. Then the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is a cyclic abelian group is

\frac{\ell-2+t_{1}}{\ell-1}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{d_{i}}-1}\right)+\left(\frac{\ell^{2}+(t_{1}-1)\ell-1}{\ell^{3}-2\ell^{2}+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\ell^{d_{i}}}{\ell^{2d_{i}}-1}\right).

As a function of
\ell
, this expression is
1-O(\ell^{-2})
, with implied constant depending only on
p
.

We can prove similar results for the subgroup
J_{p,q}[L^{\infty}]
, where
L
is any squarefree integer coprime to
6n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
. We find that the
\ell
-Sylow subgroups are independent of each other, as one might expect (see Theorem
26
). Taking
L\to\infty
, we deduce the following upper bound on the proportion of primes
p
for which
J_{p,q}
is cyclic.

Theorem 4
.

Let
S=\prod_{\ell\mid 6n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})}\ell
and let
J_{p,q}[1/S]
be the prime-to-
S
part of
J_{p,q}
. Then as
q\to\infty
, the probability that
J_{p,q}[1/S]
is cyclic is at most

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\notin S}\left(\frac{\ell-2+t_{1}(\ell)}{\ell-1}\prod_{i=1}^{t(\ell)}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{d_{i}}-1}\right)+\left(\frac{\ell^{2}+(t_{1}(\ell)-1)\ell-1}{\ell^{3}-2\ell^{2}+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t(\ell)}\left(1-\frac{\ell^{d_{i}}}{\ell^{2d_{i}}-1}\right)\right)>0,

where
t(\ell)
is the number of factors in
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
and
t_{1}(\ell)
is the number of degree one factors of
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
. In particular, this is also an upper bound for the probability that
J_{p,q}
is cyclic.

We conjecture that this upper bound is also the correct lower bound, which would imply that a positive proportion of the groups
J_{p,q}[1/S]
are cyclic.

1.2.
Proofs

The first step in the proof of Theorem
1
is to identify a rank two
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
-module
V_{\ell}
,
which does not depend on
q
, and operators
g_{q}\in{\rm End}_{{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell})
such that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/\det(g_{q}-1){\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
.

To find
V_{\ell}
, we use the deep connection between supersingular isogeny graphs and Galois representations that are familiar in number theory. Indeed, the algebra
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
is also known to act by correspondences on the modular curve
X_{0}(p)
; see e.g.
[
Eme02
]
. Moreover, there is a
({\rm\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
-linear isomorphism between
M\otimes{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
and the space
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p),{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
of weight two cusp forms of level
\Gamma_{0}(p)
, which may be viewed as the space of regular differentials on
X_{0}(p)
. This is proved either by using the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence or via theta series as in
[
Eme02
]
. Let
J_{0}(p)
be the Jacobian of
X_{0}(p)
, an abelian variety of dimension
n-1
. Then the Tate module

V_{\ell}=\lim_{\leftarrow}J_{0}(p)[\ell^{k}]

is free of rank two over
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
and admits an action
\rho_{\ell}\colon G_{\rm\mathbb{Q}}\to{\rm Aut}_{{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell})
by the absolute Galois group
G_{\rm\mathbb{Q}}={\rm Gal}(\bar{\rm\mathbb{Q}}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
. In conjunction with (
1.1
) and the
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-module isomorphism
M\otimes{\rm\mathbb{Q}}\simeq S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p),{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
, the Eichler-Shimura relation
[
Shi58
]
implies that for
\ell\nmid pN
, we have

J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]\simeq{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/\det(\rho_{\ell}(\mathrm{Frob}_{q})-1){\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell},

(1.2)

where
\mathrm{Frob}_{q}
is a choice of Frobenius automorphism at
p
(see Proposition
12
). Thus
V_{\ell}
is the sought after
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
-module. The elements
\rho_{\ell}(\mathrm{Frob}_{q})
lie in
{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}
because the determinant of
\rho_{\ell}
is the cyclotomic character, and hence
\det(\rho_{\ell}(\mathrm{Frob}_{q}))=q
. As an aside, it was observed in
[
Li19
, §4.3]
that for
\ell\nmid n
, we have

\#J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]=\#J_{0}(p)({\rm\mathbb{F}}_{q})[\ell^{\infty}],

which is a weaker version of (
1.2
). In fact, one deduces from (
1.2
) that the groups
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
and
J_{0}(p)({\rm\mathbb{F}}_{p})[\ell^{\infty}]
are
not
isomorphic in general, despite having the same cardinality.

To prove that the Frobenius cokernels
\mu
-equidistribute, we need two more ingredients. The first is the fact that
\rho_{\ell}
maps
G_{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
surjectively onto
{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}
. General results for Galois representation attached to modular curves imply that this surjectivity holds for all but finitely many
\ell
. To get precise control of the allowed values of
\ell
, we use Ribet’s strong result in the case where the level
p
is prime
[
Rib97
]
, which proves surjectivity for all
\ell\nmid n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
. The second ingredient in our proof is Cebotarev’s density theorem.

The proofs of Theorems 2-4 amount to some elaborate
\ell
-adic computations. There may be a slicker proof of Theorem
2
using more group theory, but we prefer our elementary proof, which may be helpful for readers interested in computing the full distribution, including primes
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
.

1.3.
Remarks and further directions

For
\ell\mid n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
, the distribution of the groups
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
may very well differ from those in Theorem
1
and
2
. For example, if there is a mod
\ell
congruence between two newforms of level
p
, then
\ell
will divide
{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
and the distribution will not agree with Theorem
2
. In that case, the distribution could conceivably agree with the abstract distribution in Theorem
1
, but Ribet’s results no longer apply, so we are not sure.

It is natural to wonder about the distribution of the groups
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
with
q
fixed and
p\to\infty
. The authors are not sure what the distribution should be in this case. What is needed is an
\ell
-adic version of the results of Serre
[
Ser97
]
and Conrey-Duke-Farmer
[
CDF97
]
, but controlling the
\ell
-adic norms of the terms in the trace formula seems difficult.

2.
Jacobians of graphs

Let
X
be a finite undirected graph with vertex set
V(X)
and edge set
E(X)
. Self-loops and multi-edges are allowed. The Jacobian of
X
is defined in terms of the group of divisors on
G

{\rm Div}(X)=\left\{\sum_{v\in V(X)}a_{v}v\colon a_{v}\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}\right\},

which is the free abelian group on the set
V(X)
. We may of course identify
{\rm Div}(X)
with the group
{\rm Hom}(V(X),{\rm\mathbb{Z}})
from the introduction.

The
degree
of a divisor
\sum a_{v}v
is the integer
\sum a_{v}
. We write
{\rm Div}^{0}(X)\subset{\rm Div}(X)
for the subgroup of degree 0 divisors. Each function
f\colon V(X)\to{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
determines a
principal divisor

{\rm div}(f)=\sum_{v}\sum_{e=vw\in E(X)}(f(v)-f(w))v.

Two divisors
D,D^{\prime}\in{\rm Div}(X)
are
linearly equivalent
if their difference is principal, or, in other words, if
D-D^{\prime}={\rm div}(f)
for some function
f
. Principal divisors have degree 0, so equivalent divisors have the same degree.

Definition 5
.

The
Jacobian

J(X)
is the group of linear equivalence classes in
{\rm Div}^{0}(X)
, i.e. the quotient of
{\rm Div}^{0}(X)
by the subgroup of principal divisors.

Remark 6
.

The group of all functions
f\colon V(X)\to{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
is generated by the indicator functions
\delta_{v}(w)=\delta_{vw}
, for each
v\in V(X)
. The linear equivalence relation is therefore generated by the relations
\sum_{e=vw}(v-w)
, one for each
v
.

3.
Jacobians of supersingular isogeny graphs

Recall that
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
is a prime and
X_{p}
is the set of supersingular elliptic curves over
\bar{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Let
q
be any prime different from
p
, and let
X=X_{p,q}
be the supersingular
q
-isogeny graph with vertex set
X_{p}
. Then
G
is a
(q+1)
-regular graph on
n:=(p-1)/12
vertices.

The
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
-module
{\rm Div}(X)
is free of rank
n
, with basis given by the
n
supersingular elliptic curves
E\in X_{p}
. The Hecke operator
T_{q}\colon{\rm Div}(X)\to{\rm Div}(X)
sends
E
to
\sum_{E^{\prime}\sim E}E^{\prime}
, where the sum is over every edge
E^{\prime}\sim E
in
X_{p,q}
.

Example 7
.

When
p=37
, there are three supersingular elliptic curves, with
j
-invariants
8
and the roots
\alpha
and
\bar{\alpha}
of
x^{2}-6x-6\in{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{37}[x]
. For
q=2
, we compute in sage that

•

T_{2}E_{8}=E_{8}+E_{\alpha}+E_{\bar{\alpha}}
,

•

T_{2}E_{\alpha}=E_{8}+0E_{\alpha}+2E_{\bar{\alpha}}
, and

•

T_{2}E_{\bar{\alpha}}=E_{8}+2E_{\alpha}+0E_{\bar{\alpha}}
.

The Hecke operator
T_{2}
is degree
3
, since the graph
X_{37,2}
is
3
-regular.

In general,
T_{q}
sends divisors of degree
n
to divisors of degree
(q+1)n
. In particular, it preserves the subgroup
{\rm Div}^{0}(X)
. The latter sits in an exact sequence

0\to{\rm Div}^{0}(X)\longrightarrow{\rm Div}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\deg}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm\mathbb{Z}}\to 0,

(3.1)

of abelian groups. For varying primes
q
, the operators
T_{q}\in{\rm End}_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}{\rm Div}(X)
commute with each other. They therefore generate a commutative algebra
\tilde{\rm\mathbb{T}}
, called the Hecke algebra, which is itself free of rank
n
as a
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
-module
[
Eme02
]
. Then (
3.1
) is an exact sequence of
\tilde{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-modules, with
T_{q}
acting on
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
by multiplication by
q+1
. Let
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
be the subalgebra of
{\rm End}_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}\,{\rm Div}^{0}(X)
generated by the action of the
T_{q}
. Then
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
is a quotient of
\tilde{\rm\mathbb{T}}
and has rank
n-1
.

Lemma 8
.

There is an isomorphism of
{\rm\mathbb{T}}
-modules
J_{p,q}\simeq{\rm Div}^{0}(X)/(q+1-T_{q}){\rm Div}(X)
.

Proof.

From the definitions, the subgroup of principal divisors is precisely

A=2\lambda^{2k}-2\lambda^{k}-2(\lambda-1)k\lambda^{k-1}-2(\lambda-1)^{2}\lambda^{2k-2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle N(k,D)=

\displaystyle 2(k+1)\lambda^{k}-2k\lambda^{k-1}+\lambda^{2k-2}(\lambda-2)(\lambda-1)2\lambda^{2k}-2\lambda^{k}-2(\lambda-1)k\lambda^{k-1}-2(\lambda-1)^{2}\lambda^{2k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2k}+\lambda^{2k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2k-1}(\lambda+1),

as desired.
∎

For any
D\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1}
, define the set

\mathcal{M}(k,D)=\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathcal{O}}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1})\colon v(\det(g-1))=k\mbox{ and }\det(g)=D\}

and let
M(k,D)=\#\mathcal{M}(k,D)
.

Proposition 17
.

Fix
k\geq 0
and
D\in({\rm\mathcal{O}}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1})^{\times}
. If
v(1-D)=0
, then

M(k,D)=\begin{cases}\lambda(\lambda-2)(\lambda+1)&\mbox{ if }k=0\\
\lambda^{2k+1}(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)&\mbox{ if }k>0\\
\end{cases}.

Proof.

First assume
k=0
. Then
M(0,D)
is the number of
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda})
of determinant
D
such that
\det(g-1)\neq 0
. Now, the number of
g
with determinant equal to
D
is

\dfrac{\#{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda})}{\lambda-1}=(\lambda+1)\lambda(\lambda-1).

Thus,
M(0,D)=(\lambda+1)\lambda(\lambda-1)-N(1,D)
, and by Proposition
14
, this is

(\lambda-1)\lambda(\lambda+1)-\lambda(\lambda+1)=\lambda(\lambda+1)(\lambda-2).

Now assume
k\geq 1
. Let
\pi\colon{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathcal{O}}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1})\to{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathcal{O}}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}^{k})
. Recall that
N(k,\pi(D))
is the cardinality of the set

\mathcal{N}(k,\pi(D))=\{h\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathcal{O}}/\pi^{k})\colon\det(h)=\pi(D)\mbox{ and }\det(h-1)=0\}.

Then
\pi
sends
\mathcal{M}(k,D)
to
\mathcal{N}(k,\pi(D))
. Conversely, given
h\in\mathcal{N}(k,\pi(D))
, there is at least one
g\in\pi^{-1}(h)
with
\det(g)=D
. Any other element of
\pi^{-1}(h)
has the form
g+\ell^{k}M
for some matrix
M
. The determinant of
g+\ell^{k}M
is equal to

(a+\ell^{k}m_{1})(d+\ell^{k}m_{4})-(b+\ell^{k}m_{2})(c+\ell^{k}m_{3})=\det(g)+\ell^{k}(am_{4}+dm_{1}-cm_{2}-bm_{3})

and hence equals
D=\det(g)
if and only if
M
lies in the kernel of a certain non-zero linear functional
\mathrm{Mat}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda})\to{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda}
. There are therefore
\lambda^{3}
pre-images
g^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(h)
with
\det(g^{\prime})=D
. Not all of them satisfy
v(\det(g-1))=k
, but we have

M(k,D)=\lambda^{3}N(k,\pi(D))-N(k+1,D).

By Proposition
14
, we have

\displaystyle M(k,D)=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2k+2}(\lambda+1)-\lambda^{2(k+1)-1}(\lambda+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2k+1}(\lambda(\lambda+1)-\lambda-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2k+1}(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1),

as claimed.
∎

Now let
{\rm{\bf k}}=(k_{i})\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}^{t}_{\geq 0}
. Recall
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
and each
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
is a finite unramified extension of
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
of degree
d_{i}\geq 1
. The residue field
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
of
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
is isomorphic to
{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda_{i}}
, where
\lambda_{i}=\ell^{d_{i}}
. Recall that
{\rm{\bf k}}
determines a finite
\ell
-primary
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
-module
G_{\rm{\bf k}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/\ell^{k_{i}}
. If
g\in{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}
, then
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/\det(g-1){\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\simeq G_{\rm{\bf k}}
if and only if
v(\det(g-1))={\rm{\bf k}}
, where here
v\colon{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\to{\rm\mathbb{Z}}^{t}
is the combined valuation from Section
4
.

Recall the ideals
I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1}
and the sets
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)
.
Having fixed
{\rm{\bf k}}
for the time being, we will abuse notation and write
v\colon{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1}\to\prod_{i=1}^{t}\{0,1,\ldots,k_{i}+1\}
for the “combined reduced valuation”. Let
k=\max_{i}k_{i}
, and observe that we may view
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}/\ell^{k+1}{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
as a subring of
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1}
. Define
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\diamondsuit}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)
to be the subset of elements
D
of this subring such that
v(D(1-D))=0
.
Now define

\mathcal{M}({\rm{\bf k}})=\left\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1})\colon\det(g)\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\diamondsuit}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)\mbox{ and }v(\det(g-1))={\rm{\bf k}}\right\}.

Finally, set
M({\rm{\bf k}})=\#\mathcal{M}({\rm{\bf k}})
.

Proposition 18
.

Let
\delta_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
be Kronecker’s delta. Then

M({\rm{\bf k}})=\ell^{k}(\ell-2)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{2k_{i}+1}(\lambda_{i}-1-\delta_{0k_{i}})(\lambda_{i}+1).

Proof.

Recall that we view
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}/\ell^{k+1}{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
as a subring of
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1}
in the natural way. We have

M({\rm{\bf k}})=\sum_{D\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\diamondsuit}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)}M({\rm{\bf k}},D)

where

M({\rm{\bf k}},D)=\#\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1})\colon\det(g)=D\mbox{ and }v(\det(g-1))={\rm{\bf k}}\}.

On the other hand,

M({\rm{\bf k}},D)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}M(k_{i},D_{i}),

where
D_{i}
is the image of
D
in
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}/\ell^{k_{i}+1}
and

M(k_{i},D_{i})=\#\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{k_{i}+1})\colon\det(g)=D_{i}\mbox{ and }v_{i}(\det(g-1))=k_{i}\}.

Then by Proposition
17
, we have

M({\rm{\bf k}},D)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{2k_{i}+1}(\lambda_{i}-1-\delta_{0k_{i}})(\lambda_{i}+1),

where
\lambda_{i}=\#{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
. Thus,

\displaystyle M({\rm{\bf k}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\diamondsuit}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)}M({\rm{\bf k}},D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{k}(\ell-2)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{2k_{i}+1}(\lambda_{i}-1-\delta_{0k_{i}})(\lambda_{i}+1).

as claimed.
∎

Let
{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}^{\prime}=\{g\in{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}\colon\det(g)-1\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}\}
. The following is an equivalent formulation of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 19
.

Fix
{\rm{\bf k}}\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}^{t}_{\geq 0}
and let
\delta_{i}=1
if
k_{i}=0
and
\delta_{i}=0
if
k_{i}>0
. Then the proportion of
g\in{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}^{\prime}
such that
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/\det(g-1){\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}\simeq G_{\rm{\bf k}}
is

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{-k_{i}}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right)^{\delta_{i}}=\frac{1}{\#G_{\rm{\bf k}}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right)^{\delta_{i}}.

Remark 20
.

The distribution above is a product of
t
independent probability distributions, since

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-k}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{\lambda-1}\right)^{\delta_{0k}}=1-\frac{1}{\lambda-1}+\frac{1}{1-1/\lambda}-1=1

Proof.

Define

{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}^{\prime}({\rm{\bf k}}+1):=\left\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1})\colon\det(g)\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\diamondsuit}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle\#{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}^{\prime}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)=

\displaystyle\#{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\diamondsuit}({\rm{\bf k}}+1)\cdot\#\ker\left({\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1})\to({\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}/I_{{\rm{\bf k}}+1})^{\times}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{k}(\ell-2)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\#\ker\left({\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{k_{i}+1})\to({\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{k_{i}+1})^{\times}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{k}(\ell-2)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{4k_{i}}\frac{(\lambda_{i}^{2}-1)(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{k_{i}}(\lambda_{i}-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{k}(\ell-2)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{3k_{i}+1}(\lambda_{i}^{2}-1)

Thus, by Proposition
18
, the proportion of
g\in{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{\ell}^{\prime}
giving rise to
G_{\rm{\bf k}}
is

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\dfrac{\lambda_{i}^{2k_{i}+1}(\lambda_{i}-1-\delta_{0k_{i}})}{\lambda_{i}^{3k_{i}+1}(\lambda_{i}-1)}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{-k_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right)^{\delta_{i}}.

∎

For the next two corollaries, we fix a prime
\ell\nmid 6n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
.

Corollary 21
.

As
q\to\infty
through primes
q\not\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
, we have

{\rm\mathbb{P}}(J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]=0)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right).

Corollary 22
.

As
q\to\infty
through primes
q\not\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
, the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is a cyclic abelian group is

\left(1+\frac{t_{1}}{\ell-2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right),

where
t_{1}
is the number of
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
which are isomorphic to
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}

(
i.e. with
d_{i}=1)
.

Proof.

The
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
-component of
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
can only be non-trivial and cyclic if
d_{i}=1
, or in other words
\lambda_{i}=\ell
. Indeed,
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}}\simeq{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda_{i}}
is isomorphic to
({\rm\mathbb{Z}}/\ell)^{d_{i}}
as an abelian group. Let us index the factors
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
so that
d_{i}=1
for
1\leq i\leq t_{1}
. Then the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is cyclic is

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{i}-2}{\lambda_{i}-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{t_{1}}\frac{1}{\ell-1}\prod_{j\neq i}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{j}-2}{\lambda_{j}-1}

=\kappa+\sum_{i=1}^{t_{1}}\kappa\cdot\dfrac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}\cdot\frac{1}{\ell-1}=\kappa+\frac{t_{1}}{\ell-2}\kappa=\left(1+\frac{t_{1}}{\ell-2}\right)\kappa,

where
\kappa=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{i}-2}{\lambda_{i}-1}
.
∎

What about analogues of the results for
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
? In principle one can write a closed formula for the distribution of
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
(as in Theorem
19
) as
q\to\infty
varies through primes such that
v_{\ell}(p-1)=k>0
, for any fixed
k
, but the formulas seemed very complicated to us. For our purposes, we will be happy to simply give analogues of Corollaries
21
and
22
.

Proposition 23
.

As
q\to\infty
through
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
, the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]=0
is

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\dfrac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}\right).

Proof.

First let
{\rm\mathcal{O}}={\rm\mathcal{O}}_{i}
and
\lambda=\lambda_{i}
. Then the number of
M\in{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathcal{O}}/{\rm\mathfrak{m}})
with
\det(M)=1
and
\det(M-1)=0
is
(\lambda-1)^{2}+(\lambda-1)+\lambda=\lambda^{2}
, by (
5.1
). The total number of
M
with determinant
1
is

\dfrac{\#{\rm GL}_{2}({\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda})}{\#{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\lambda}^{\times}}=\dfrac{(\lambda^{2}-1)(\lambda^{2}-\lambda)}{\lambda-1}=\lambda(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)

Thus the probability that such an
M
has
\det(M-1)\neq 0
is
1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}-1}
. The proposition now follows by taking the product over all
i
.
∎

Proposition 24
.

As
q\to\infty
through
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
, the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is cyclic is

\left(1+\frac{t_{1}\ell}{\ell^{2}-\ell-1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}\right)

Proof.

The probability is

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{i}-1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{t_{1}}\frac{\ell}{\ell^{2}-1}\prod_{j\neq i}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\lambda_{j}-1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-1}

=\kappa+\sum_{i=1}^{t_{1}}\kappa\cdot\dfrac{\ell^{2}-1}{\ell^{2}-\ell-1}\cdot\frac{\ell}{\ell^{2}-1}=\kappa+\frac{t_{1}\ell}{\ell^{2}-\ell-1}\kappa=\left(1+\frac{t_{1}\ell}{\ell^{2}-\ell-1}\right)\kappa,

where
\kappa=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{i}-1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}
.
∎

Putting together Corollary
22
and Proposition
24
, we obtain the following, which is Theorem
3
.

Corollary 25
.

As
q\to\infty
, the probability that
J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is cyclic is

\frac{\ell-2+t_{1}}{\ell-1}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right)+\left(\frac{\ell^{2}+(t_{1}-1)\ell-1}{\ell^{3}-2\ell^{2}+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}\right).

As
\ell\to\infty
, this is
1-O(\ell^{-2})
, with the implied constant depending only on
p
.

Proof.

Call the desired probability
P
. Then by Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, we have
P=(1-\frac{1}{\ell-1})P_{A}+\frac{1}{\ell-1}P_{B}
, where
P_{A}
and
P_{B}
are the probabilities computed in Corollary
22
and
24
, respectively. This gives the claimed formula. To estimate the first term, we may ignore the factors with
\lambda_{i}=\ell^{d_{i}}>\ell
. Taking
x=\ell-1
, we compute

P_{A}\approx\left(1+\frac{t_{1}}{x-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{x}\right)^{t_{1}}=1+O(x^{-2})=1+O(\ell^{-2}).

Thus

P=\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell-1}\right)+O(\ell^{-2})+\frac{1}{\ell-1}\left(1+O(\ell^{-1})\right)=1+O(\ell^{-2}),

as desired.
∎

Since
\prod_{\ell}(1-O(\ell^{-2}))>0
, this gives an explicit positive upper bound on the proportion of primes
p
such that
J_{p,q}
is cyclic. It also suggests that a positive proportion of the groups
J_{p,q}
are cyclic, but we cannot deduce this from our results. We at least have the following, which is Theorem
4
.

Theorem 26
.

Let
S
be the set of primes dividing
6n{\rm Disc}({\rm\mathbb{T}})
. The probability that
\bigoplus_{\ell\notin S}J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is cyclic is at most

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\notin S}\left(\frac{\ell-2+t_{1}(\ell)}{\ell-1}\prod_{i=1}^{t(\ell)}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right)+\left(\frac{\ell^{2}+(t_{1}(\ell)-1)\ell-1}{\ell^{3}-2\ell^{2}+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t(\ell)}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}\right)\right),

where
t(\ell)
is the number of factors in
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
and
t_{1}(\ell)
is the number of degree one factors of
{\rm\mathbb{T}}_{\ell}
. In particular, the above Euler product is also an upper bound on the probability that
J_{p,q}
is cyclic.

Proof.

Technically speaking, this does not follow from the previous results. However, Ribet’s results apply not just to a single prime, but to any set of primes not contained in
S

[
Rib97
]
. This shows that for any finite set of primes
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
not in
S
, the distributions of the groups
J_{p,q}[\ell_{i}^{\infty}]
are independent of each other. Taking a limit as
m\to\infty
leads to the desired upper bound.
∎

In fact, Ribet’s result applies even to infinite sets of primes not contained in
S
. This suggests that with some extra work one might be able to prove a uniformity estimate and hence prove:

Conjecture 27
.

The probability that
\bigoplus_{\ell\notin S}J_{p,q}[\ell^{\infty}]
is cyclic is positive and equal to

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\notin S}\left(\frac{\ell-2+t_{1}(\ell)}{\ell-1}\prod_{i=1}^{t(\ell)}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-1}\right)+\left(\frac{\ell^{2}+(t_{1}(\ell)-1)\ell-1}{\ell^{3}-2\ell^{2}+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t(\ell)}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}-1}\right)\right)>0.

Arithmetic level raising on triple product of Shimura curves and Gross–Kudla–Schoen Diagonal cycles II: Bipartite Euler system
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we study the Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycle on the triple product of Shimura curves at a place of good reduction and prove an unramified arithmetic level raising theorem for the cohomology of this triple product. We deduce from it a reciprocity law which relates the image of the diagonal cycle under the Abel–Jacobi map to certain period integral of Gross–Kudla type. Combing this with the first reciprocity law we proved in a previous work, we show that the Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycles along with the Gross–Kudla periods form a bipartite Euler system for the symmetric cube motive of a modular form. As an application we provide some evidence for the rank one case of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the symmetric cube motive of a modular form.

1.
Introduction

In a seminal work of Bertolini–Darmon
[
BD3
]
, the authors constructed a Kolyvagin type Euler system using Heegner points on various Shimura curves. The cohomology classes in this system satisfy beautiful reciprocity laws that resemble the so-called Jochnowitz’s congruence. More precisely, these reciprocity laws relate the Kummer images of the Heegner points to certain toric period integrals via level-raising congruences. One can consider these Heegner point classes as the incarnations of the first derivatives of certain Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions at the central critical points in light of the Gross–Zagier type formula while the toric period integrals are the algebraic parts of the central critical values of the Rankin–Selberg type
L
-functions as shown by the Gross’ formula. The reciprocity laws alluded above are reflections of the congruences between the algebraic parts of the first derivatives of the Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions and the algebraic parts of the central critical values of the Rankin–Selberg type
L
-functions observed by Jochnowitz using computational method. Using these reciprocity laws, Bertolini–Darmon are able to verify the one-sided divisibility of the Iwasawa main conjecture for an elliptic curve over the anticyclotomic extension of an imaginary quadratic field. These reciprocity laws are also the backbone of Wei Zhang’s work
[
Zhang
]
on verifying the Kolyvagin’s conjecture. The method of Bertolini–Darmon is axiomatized by
[
How
]
and is given the name of a bipartite Euler system. In
[
How
]
, it was also mentioned that the cycle on the triple product of Shimura curves studied in
[
GK
]
and
[
GS
]
known as the Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycle might form another example of such a bipartite Euler system. The main theme of this article is to investigate Howard’s conjecture for the Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycle.

The present article completes what was started in
[
Wang
]
where we verified one of the reciprocity laws (the first reciprocity law). We will show below that the Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycles and the Gross–Kudla periods on the triple product of Shimura curves satisfy all the reciprocity laws needed to form a bipartite Euler system for the triple product motive of modular forms. However these cycles do not give a bipartite Euler system in the strict sense of
[
How
]
for obvious rank reasons. On the other hand, when we consider the symmetric cube component of the triple tensor product motive, then the image of these Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycles in the symmetric cube component do form a bipartite Euler system. Using this bipartite Euler system, we provide some evidence towards the rank one case of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the symmetric cube motive of a modular form. A refinement of the method in this article should also be able to handle the more general case of the triple product motive of modular forms. We decide to treat this in another occasion where we also plan to extend the results here to an Iwasawa theoretic setting.

Note that the Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycle is also studied extensively using the
p
-adic method. This was initiated in
[
DR1
]
and
[
DR2
]
where certain
p
-adic reciprocity laws in the style of Perrin-Riou are proved. The reciprocity laws in this article can be viewed as the counter part of those in
[
DR1
]
and
[
DR2
]
in the level raising situation. We will refer to
[
BDRSV
]
and
[
CH
]
for further development and arithmetic applications in this direction. It also should be possible to combine the results in this article with the
p
-adic methods to prove interesting Iwasawa theoretic results as was done in
[
BD3
]
. In a parallel direction, Liu and Tian
[
Liu1
]
,
[
Liu2
]
and
[
LT
]
have constructed the desired bipartite Euler system for the triple product motive realized on certain Hilbert modular threefold.

The symmetric cube motive

\mathbf{A}^{\infty}
.

When

is a local field, we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
its valuation ring and by

its residue field. We let

be the inertia subgroup of

. For a

-module

, we have the following exact sequence of Galois cohomology groups

0\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K,\mathrm{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\mathrm{M})\xrightarrow{\partial_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sin}}(K,\mathrm{M})\rightarrow 0

where

. The natural quotient map

2.

Let

We will consider now the curve

We define an integral model

over
\mathbf{Z}[1/Nm]
which represents the following functor. Let
S
be a locally Noetherian test scheme over
\mathbf{Z}[1/Nm]

\mathfrak{X}_{m}(p)(S)
classifies the tuples

(A_{1},A_{2},\iota_{1},\iota_{2},\pi,C_{N^{+}},\alpha_{m})

up to isomorphism where

(1)

A_{i}
for

is an
S
-abelian scheme of relative dimension

;

(2)

\iota_{i}:\mathcal{O}_{B}\hookrightarrow\End_{S}(A_{i})

\mathcal{O}_{B}
on
A_{i}
for

;

(3)

is an isogeny of degree

By
[
Buz
, Theorem 4.7]
,
\mathfrak{X}_{m}(p)
is regular and proper over

. Again when we consider the base-change of

to

, we use the same symbol for this base-change. We denote by

the generic fiber of

and
\overline{X}_{m}(p)
the special fiber of
\mathfrak{X}_{m}(p)
. We have the following descriptions of

over
\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The scheme
\overline{X}_{m}(p)
consists of two irreducible components both isomorphic to
\overline{X}_{m}
, crossing transversally at the supersingular locus of
\overline{X}_{m}(p)
which can be identified with the supersingular locus of
\overline{X}_{m}
.

Proof.

This is well-known and see
[
Buz
, Theorem 4.7(v)]
for a proof of this.
∎

Let
\pi_{1}:\mathfrak{X}_{m}(p)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{m}
be the morphism given by

(A_{1},A_{2},\iota_{1},\iota_{2},\pi,C_{N^{+}},\alpha_{m})\mapsto(A_{1},\iota_{1},C_{N^{+}},\alpha_{m})

and
\pi_{2}:\mathfrak{X}_{m}(p)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{m}
be the morphism given by

(A_{1},A_{2},\iota_{1},\iota_{2},\pi,C_{N^{+}},\alpha_{m})\mapsto(A_{2},\iota_{2},C_{N^{+}},\alpha_{m}).

We can define two closed immersions

and

as in the proof of
[

such that

(2.1)

\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{1}\circ i_{1}&\pi_{1}\circ i_{2}\\
\pi_{2}\circ i_{1}&\pi_{2}\circ i_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}&\mathrm{Frob}_{p}\\
S^{-1}_{p}\mathrm{Frob}_{p}&\mathrm{id}\\
\end{pmatrix}

where
S_{p}
corresponds to the central element in the spherical Hecke algebra of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
.

We will need the following result known as the Ihara’s lemma. This is proved for the case of classical modular curves by Ribet
[
Rib1
]
and Diamond–Taylor
[
DT
]
for Shimura curves. See also
[
MS21
]
for an interesting approach using the Taylor–Wiles patching method to prove an analogue result for a Shimura curve over a totally real field. Recall that a Hecke module is
Eisenstein
if its support consists of Eisenstein maximal ideals. A maximal ideal is Eisenstein if its associated Galois representation is reducible.

Theorem 2.3

(Ihara’s lemma)
.

We have the following statements.

(1)

The kernel of the pull-back map

(\pi^{*}_{1}+\pi^{*}_{2}):\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{m,\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},k_{\lambda})^{\oplus 2}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{m}(p)_{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},k_{\lambda})

is Eisenstein.

(2)

The cokernel of the push-forwad map

(\pi_{1*},\pi_{2*}):\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{m}(p)_{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},k_{\lambda})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{m,\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},k_{\lambda})^{\oplus 2}

is Eisenstein.

2.3.
Unramified level raising for Shimura curves

Let
f
be a normalized newform in
S^{\mathrm{new}}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. Let
E=\mathbf{Q}(f)
be the Hecke field of
f
and let
\lambda
be a place above
l
in
E
. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
the valuation ring of
E_{\lambda}
and by
k_{\lambda}
its residue field. Let
\varpi
be a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
and
\lambda=(\varpi)
be the maximal ideal.
Then by the construction of Eichler–Shimura, we can attach a Galois representation

\rho_{f,\lambda}:G_{\mathbf{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}({E_{\lambda}})=\mathrm{GL}(\mathrm{V}_{f,\lambda})

to
f
such that
\mathrm{tr}(\rho_{f,\lambda}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}))=a_{p}(f)
where
a_{p}(f)
is the Fourier coefficient of
f
at
p
for every
p\nmid N
and such that the determinant
\mathrm{det}(\rho_{f,\lambda})=\epsilon_{l}
is the
l
-adic cyclotomic character. We denote by

\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}:G_{\mathbf{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k_{\lambda})

the residual representation of
\rho_{f,\lambda}
.

We fix an auxiliary prime
m=d\geq 5
considered in the last subsection. Let
\mathbf{T}
be the abstract Hecke algebra unramified away from
Nd
. This means
\mathbf{T}
is the restricted tensor product of the spherical Hecke algebra
\mathbf{T}_{v}
for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})
with
v\nmid Nd
. The spherical Hecke algebra
\mathbf{T}_{v}
is generated by the operators
T_{v},S^{\pm 1}_{v}
. The Hecke eigensystem of
f
gives rise to a map

\phi_{f}:\mathbf{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
sending
T_{v}
to
a_{v}(f)
and sending
S_{v}
to
1
for
v\nmid Nd
.

For a finite set of primes
S
away from
Nd
, let
\mathbf{T}^{[S]}
be the Hecke algebra containing all the Hecke operators away from
Nd
and away from the primes in
S
. We introduce the following ideals of
\mathbf{T}^{[S]}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{[S]}=\mathrm{ker}[\mathbf{T}\xrightarrow{\phi_{f}}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda]\cap\mathbf{T}^{[S]}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{[S]}_{n}=\mathrm{ker}[\mathbf{T}\xrightarrow{\phi_{f}}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda^{n}]\cap\mathbf{T}^{[S]}.

If
S
is empty, then we omit it from all the notations. We say
\mathfrak{m}
is residually irreducible if
\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
is absolutely irreducible.

Definition 2.4
.

We say the auxiliary prime
d
is
clean
for
\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
if the degeneracy maps
(\pi_{1,d,\ast},\pi_{2,d,\ast})
on
X_{d}
induces an isomorphism

(\pi_{1,d,\ast},\pi_{2,d,\ast}):\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{d,\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{m}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})^{\oplus 2}_{/\mathfrak{m}}

and the degeneracy maps
(\pi_{1,d,\ast},\pi_{2,d,\ast})
on
Z_{d}(\overline{B})
induces an isomorphism

(\pi_{1,d,\ast},\pi_{2,d,\ast}):\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{\mathrm{B}}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(Z(\overline{\mathrm{B}}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})^{\oplus 2}_{/\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}.

From here on, we will always fix a clean
d
for
\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
. Furthermore, we will impose the following assumptions on
\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
.

Assumption 2.5
.

We make the following assumptions on
\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
and thus on
\mathfrak{m}
:

(1)

\bar{\rho}_{f,\lambda}|_{G_{\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{l})}}
is absolutely irreducible;

(2)

\bar{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
is minimally ramified at primes in
\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma^{-}_{\mathrm{ram}}
and is ramified at primes in
\Sigma^{-}_{\mathrm{ram}}
;

(3)

The image of
\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
contains
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{l})
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Under Assumption
2.5
, we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{/\mathfrak{m}}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}

which induces an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{d,\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{/\mathfrak{m}}\cong\overline{\rho}^{\oplus 2}_{f,\lambda}.

Proof.

By
[
BLR2
]
, it follows that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{/\mathfrak{m}}
is isomorphic to
\overline{\rho}^{\oplus d_{\mathfrak{m}}}_{f,\lambda}
for some
d_{\mathfrak{m}}\geq 1
. Therefore
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{d,\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{/\mathfrak{m}}
is isomorphic to
2d_{\mathfrak{m}}
copies of
\overline{\rho}_{f,\lambda}
by the cleaness of
d
. On the other hand, we know that
\Gamma(Z(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{m}}
is one dimensional over
k_{\lambda}
under the Assumption
2.5
by
[
CH1
, Proposition 6.8]
(Note that our assumption is stronger than the (CR+) assumption in
[
CH1
, Proposition 6.8]
). By the same argument of
[
LTXZZ
, Lemma 6.4.2]
,
d_{\mathfrak{m}}
is equal to the dimension of
\Gamma(Z(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{m}}
over
k_{\lambda}
and hence is equal to
1
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Note that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}}
provide a

-stable
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-lattice
\rho_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}

the natural reduction of

Let
\mathrm{cl}:\mathrm{CH}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(\overline{X}_{d},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))

\overline{X}_{d}

Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}
satisfies Assumption
2.5

\mathrm{H}^{0}
and

of

\mathrm{H}^{2}(\overline{X}_{d},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}).

Then the cycle class map
\mathrm{cl}
induces the Abel–Jacobi map

\mathrm{AJ}_{f}:\mathrm{CH}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}).

The Abel–Jacobi image
\mathrm{AJ}_{f}([\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d}])
of the class of the supersingular locus
[\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d}]\in\mathrm{CH}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d})
can be calculated by the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow 0.

More precisely, the above exact sequence induces the following coboundary map

\Phi:\mathrm{H}^{0}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})^{G_{\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}}}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}})=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}}).

with
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}
and will identify
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{/\mathfrak{p}_{n}}
with
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1))_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{n}}
, these identifications follow from Proposition
2.6
and will be used elsewhere in this article without further remark. The Abel–Jacobi image
\mathrm{AJ}_{{f}}([\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d}])
of
[\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d}]
is represented by the image of the characteristic function
\mathbf{1}_{\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d}}
of the supersingular locus
\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d}
under
\Phi
. On the other hand, we can consider the excision exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{c}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow 0

\Phi^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})^{G_{\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}}}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{0}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}).

3.2.
Unramified level raising for triple product of Shimura curves

We recall next the definition of an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
introduced in
[

, Definition 4.3]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. We say that a prime
p
is
n
-admissible
for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
if

(1)

p\nmid Nl
;

(2)

l\nmid p^{2}-1
;

(3)

\varpi_{i}^{n}\mid p+1-\epsilon_{p,i}a_{p}(f_{i})
with
\epsilon_{p,i}=\pm 1
for

;

(4)

\epsilon_{p,1}\epsilon_{p,2}\epsilon_{p,3}=1
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. Since the integral model
\mathfrak{X}^{3}_{d}
of
X^{3}_{d}
aquires good reduction at
p
,
\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
is unramified at
p
as an
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\lambda},n}[G_{\mathbf{Q}}]
-module and therefore it makes sense to consider
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))
. We define the
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\lambda},n}
-module
Z_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
by

Z_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)=\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}}.

Let
\overline{X}_{i}
be the
i
-th copy of
\overline{X}_{d}
in the triple product
\overline{X}^{3}_{d}
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. Let
\mathrm{T}_{i,n}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{i,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}}(1))_{/\mathfrak{p}_{i,n}}
. By Proposition
2.11
, we have a decomposition

\mathrm{T}_{i,n}=\mathrm{T}^{+}_{i,n}\oplus\mathrm{T}^{-}_{i,n}

where

•

\mathrm{T}^{+}_{i,n}
is given by
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{i,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}_{i,n}}^{G_{\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}}}(1)
which is identified with
\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}}(1))_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}}
via the level raising map
\Phi^{\ast}_{i,n}
;

•

\mathrm{T}^{-}_{i,n}
is given by
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\overline{X}_{i,\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{ac}}_{p}},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}}(1))_{/\mathfrak{p}_{i,n}})
which is identified with
\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}}

\Phi_{i,n}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the Poincaré duality between
\mathrm{T}_{i,n}
and
\mathrm{T}_{i,n}(-1)
,
\mathrm{T}^{-}_{i,n}
is dual to
\mathrm{T}^{+}_{i,n}(-1)
and
\mathrm{T}^{+}_{i,n}
is dual to
\mathrm{T}^{-}_{i,n}(-1)
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the constructions in Proposition
2.11
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible
prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. Then
\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
is unramified at

and we have a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)\cong Z_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)\oplus Z^{\oplus 3}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(1)\oplus Z^{\oplus 3}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(2)\oplus Z_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(3)

between
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\lambda},n}[G_{\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}}]
-modules.

Proof.

This follows from the definition of an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
and the split exact sequence in Proposition
2.11
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{T}_{1,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}_{2,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}_{3,n}

\displaystyle=\underset{?_{\ast}\in\{\pm\}}{\oplus}\mathrm{T}^{?_{1}}_{1,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}^{?_{2}}_{2,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}^{?_{3}}_{3,n}

\displaystyle=Z_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)\oplus Z^{\oplus 3}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(1)\oplus Z^{\oplus 3}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(2)\oplus Z_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(3).

∎

The following theorem will be referred to as the unramified arithmetic level raising theorem for the triple product of Shimura curves. We call it the unramified arithmetic level raising theorem as we are interested in the cohomology of the triple product of Shimura curves at a place of good reduction.

Theorem 3.5

(Unramified level raising)
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. We assume that each maximal ideal in the triple
\mathfrak{m}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}=(\mathfrak{m}_{1},\mathfrak{m}_{2},\mathfrak{m}_{3})
satisfies Assumption
2.5
. Then we have the following isomorphism

(3.1)

\Phi_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))\cong\overset{3}{\underset{j=1}{\oplus}}(\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}}).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{Z}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1)\oplus\mathrm{Z}^{\oplus 3}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)\oplus\mathrm{Z}^{\oplus 3}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(1)\oplus\mathrm{Z}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(2))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{Z}^{\oplus 3}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d))

\displaystyle\cong\overset{3}{\underset{j=1}{\oplus}}(\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}}).

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. We assume that each maximal ideal in the triple
\mathfrak{m}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}=(\mathfrak{m}_{1},\mathfrak{m}_{2},\mathfrak{m}_{3})
satisfies Assumption
2.5
. Then we have the following isomorphism

\Phi_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}:\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))\cong\overset{3}{\underset{j=1}{\oplus}}(\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}}).

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
is unramified, we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1)).

By
[
Rib2
, Proposition 3.8]
, the non-trivial element in
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}/\mathbf{F}_{p})
acts on
\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}})
by
\epsilon_{i}
. Therefore the non-trivial element in
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}/\mathbf{F}_{p})
acts on

\overset{3}{\underset{j=1}{\oplus}}(\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}})

by the product of the sign
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})
which is
1
by the definition of an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. Since the map
\Phi_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}
commutes with the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}}/\mathbf{F}_{p})
by construction, the conclusion immediately follows.
∎

3.3.

In this subsection, we will always fix an
n
-admissible prime
p
for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}=(\mathfrak{m}_{1},\mathfrak{m}_{2},\mathfrak{m}_{3})
be the triple of maximal ideals that all satisfy Assumption
2.5
. Recall we have the Shimura curve
X_{d}
over
\mathbf{Q}
and its integral model
\mathfrak{X}_{d}
over
\mathbf{Z}[1/Nd]

\theta:X_{d}\rightarrow X^{3}_{d}

\mathrm{AJ}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}:\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X^{3}_{d})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{H}^{3}(X^{3}_{d,\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathbf{Z}_{l}(2))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}}\otimes_{\mathbf{T}^{\otimes 3}_{\mathfrak{m}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}}}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\lambda}}).

We can compose it further with the natural map
\mathrm{M}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1)\rightarrow\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1)
.
We will denote the composite map by

(3.2)

\mathrm{AJ}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}:\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X_{d}^{3})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1)).

Let
\Theta_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))
be the image of
\Delta_{d}=\theta_{*}[X_{d}]
under
\mathrm{AJ}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}
. Recall that

\overline{B}

\overline{X}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{d}
of the special fiber
\overline{X}_{d}

\theta

We have a bilinear pairing

(\hphantom{a},\hphantom{b}):\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})\times\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),E_{\lambda_{i}}/\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\lambda}}

Let

Theorem 3.8

(The second reciprocity law)
.

Let
p
be an
n
-admissible prime for
\underline{\mathbf{f}}

\mathfrak{m}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}

Proof.

Let
\theta:\overline{X}_{d}\rightarrow\overline{X}^{3}_{d}
be the map induced by
\theta:\mathfrak{X}_{d}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{3}_{d}
on the special fiber. Consider the Abel–Jacobi map
{\mathrm{AJ}}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}:\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\overline{X}^{3}_{d})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))
constructed similarly as the one on
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X^{3}_{d})
. Let
\overline{\Delta}_{d}=[\theta_{\ast}\overline{X}_{d}]\in\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\overline{X}^{3}_{d})
be the diagonal element, we denote by
\overline{\Theta}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
the class given by
\mathrm{AJ}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}(\overline{\Delta}_{d})\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))
and define
\overline{\Theta}_{n}^{(j)}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
to be the projection to the

-th component of
\overset{3}{\underset{j=1}{\oplus}}(\overset{3}{\underset{i=1}{\otimes}}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{i,n}})
using the isomorphism

for

. Since
\mathfrak{X}^{3}_{d}
has good reduction at
p
and thus
\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1)
is unramified, the class
\overline{\Theta}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
agrees with
\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\Theta_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d))
. Similarly, we can identify
\overline{\Theta}^{(j)}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
with
\mathrm{loc}^{(j)}_{p}(\Theta_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d))
for every

.
To finish the proof, we will show without loss of generality

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\bar{\Theta}^{(1)}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d),\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{3})=\sum_{z\in\Delta_{d}(\overline{B})}\phi_{1}(z)\otimes\phi_{2}(z)\otimes\phi_{3}(z).

To prove this claim, we are free to take a base change of coefficients for the cohomologies involved. Therefore we can assume that
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{i}}=\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
for a discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
with maximal ideal
\lambda
. We write
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda,n}
for
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda^{n}
with any
n\geq 1
. Recall that the class
\overline{\Theta}^{(1)}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
is the image of
\overline{\Theta}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)
under the following maps

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{M}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)(-1))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{T}_{1,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}_{2,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}_{3,n}(-1))

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}},\mathrm{T}^{+}_{1,n}(-1)\otimes\mathrm{T}^{-}_{2,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}^{-}_{3,n})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{T}^{+}_{1,n}(-1)\otimes\mathrm{T}^{-}_{2,n}\otimes\mathrm{T}^{-}_{3,n}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{1,n}}\otimes\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{2,n}}\otimes\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B}),\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{3,n}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B})^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}}

We need to prove that the element
\overline{\Theta}^{(1)}_{n}(\underline{\mathbf{f}},d)\in\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B})^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}}
agrees with the characteristic function
\mathbf{1}_{\overline{B}}\in\Gamma(Z_{d}(\overline{B})^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{/\mathfrak{p}^{[p]}_{\underline{\mathbf{f}},n}}

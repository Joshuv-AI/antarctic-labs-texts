Abstract
We consider the triality fusion category discovered in the c = 1 c=1 Kosterlitz-Thouless theory [ 1 ] . We analyze this fusion category using the tools from the group theoretical fusion category and compute the simple lines, fusion rules and F F -symbols. We then studied the physical implication of this fusion category including deriving the spin selection rule, computing the asymptotic density of states of irreducible representations of the fusion category symmetries, and analyzing its anomaly and constraints under the renormalization group flow. There is another set of F F -symbols for the fusion categories with the same fusion rule known in the literature [ 2 ] . We find these two solutions are different as they lead to different spin selection rules. This gives a complete list of the fusion categories with the same fusion rule by the classification result in [ 3 ] .

Introduction

Global symmetry is an important guideline for constructing and analyzing quantum field theories. In modern language, the global symmetries, whether continuous or discrete, can be represented as invertible topological operators supported on a codimensions-1 (codim-1) surface. Any correlation functions with the topological surface operator insertion are invariant under the small deformation of the codim-
1

2.1
TDLs in 2d CFT

In 2-dimensional conformal field theory, the symmetry defects of the ordinary symmetry are line operators and they are the very first examples of TDLs. For symmetry
G
of a CFT, the corresponding TDLs are denoted as
\mathcal{L}_{g},g\in G
. The juxtaposition of two such TDLs satisfies the group algebra,
\mathcal{L}_{g}\times\mathcal{L}_{h}=\mathcal{L}_{gh}
. The TDL corresponds to the identity
\mathds{1}
in the group
G
is the identity line which we will also denote as
\mathds{1}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\mathds{1}}
. Since every group element has its inverse, these TDLs also have their inverse in the sense that there exists another line such that they fuse into a single identity line
\mathds{1}
, i.e. they are invertible TDLs.

However, TDLs are ubiquitous in the CFT, and the way they fuse is beyond the group algebra, more generally satisfying the fusion algebra. These TDLs then generate the non-invertible symmetries or the fusion category symmetries, since the TDLs may not have their inverse. The Kramer-Wannier duality line in the Ising CFT and most of the Verlinde lines in rational CFT are examples of the non-invertible TDLs.

The TDL
\mathcal{L}
can be deformed locally without changing the correlation functions
\langle\mathcal{L}\cdots\rangle
where
\cdots
denotes any other operator insertions. The topological nature of these TDLs imply that they commute with stress energy tensor, therefore can be algebraically expressed as,

[L_{n},\mathcal{L}_{a}]=[\bar{L}_{n},\mathcal{L}_{a}]=0,

(2.1)

where
L_{n},\bar{L}_{n}
are the generators of the Virasoro algebra. When the TDL
\mathcal{L}_{a}
acts on a state
\ket{\phi}
in Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the resulting state
\mathcal{L}_{a}\ket{\phi}
is still in
\mathcal{H}
.

When two TDLs are brought close to each other, their juxtaposition satisfies the fusion rule,

\mathcal{L}_{a}\times\mathcal{L}_{b}=\bigoplus_{c}N_{ab}^{c}\mathcal{L}_{c}

(2.2)

where
N_{ab}^{c}
is the fusion multiplicity which can only be non-negative integers. More specifically, the TDLs can join at the point-like junction, which is equipped with a Hilbert space. For example, the fusion of TDLs
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}

\mathcal{L}_{3}

2.3
Asymptotic density of states

As an application of the modular bootstrap reviewed above, one can derive the asymptotic density of states of a CFT
[
63
,
66
]
. Intuitively, the modular bootstrap equation,

Z^{\mathcal{L}}\left(-\frac{1}{\tau},-\frac{1}{\overline{\tau}}\right)=Z_{\mathcal{L}}(\tau,\overline{\tau}),

(2.11)

relates the high-temperature limit of the partition function
Z^{\mathcal{L}}
which computes the action of the TDL
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{H}
to the low-temperature limit of the partition function
Z_{\mathcal{L}}
which computes the spectrum of the defect Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}
and vice versa. Since in the low-temperature limit, the partition function is always dominated by the ground state, (
2.11
) essentially allows us to determine the partition function
Z^{\mathcal{L}}
and
Z_{\mathcal{L}}
in the high-temperature limit. In the high-temperature limit, states with different energy would contribute equally to the partition sum, knowing
Z_{\mathcal{L}}
in such limit would allow us to derive an approximation of the density of states for the defect Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}
. This idea is made into a rigorous mathematical statement in
[
40
]
using the techniques in
[
66
]
, and we will only mention the result below.

Let
F_{\mathcal{L}}(\Delta)
denote the total number of states with scaling dimension
\Delta^{\prime}<\Delta
in the defect Hilbert space. By asymptotic density of states, we mean a continuous function
\rho_{0,\mathcal{L}}(\Delta)
which approximates the actual density of states in the sense that

F_{\mathcal{L}}(\Delta)=\int_{0}^{\Delta}d\Delta^{\prime}\,\rho_{0,\mathcal{L}}(\Delta^{\prime})+O(\Delta^{-1/2}),\quad\Delta\rightarrow\infty.

(2.12)

Let

\rho_{0}(\Delta)=\left(\frac{c}{48\Delta^{3}}\right)^{1/4}\exp\left[2\pi\sqrt{\frac{c\Delta}{3}}\right],

(2.13)

it is shown in
[
40
]
that the asymptotic density of states
\rho_{0,\mathcal{L}}
of the defect Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}
is simply given by

\rho_{0,\mathcal{L}}(\Delta)=\langle 0|\mathcal{L}|0\rangle\rho_{0}(\Delta),

(2.14)

where
|0\rangle
is the ground state and
\langle 0|\mathcal{L}|0\rangle
is the quantum dimension of the TDL
\mathcal{L}
.

Furthermore, let’s consider the theory with finite global symmetry
G
. Then the states in the Hilbert space will organize into irreducible representations of
G
. For simplicity, we assume
G
acts faithfully. Recall that a particular type of irreducible representation
\alpha
can be counted by
\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{\alpha}(g)^{*}\text{Tr}g
in a reducible representation of
G
where
\chi_{\alpha}(g)
is the character function of the irrep
\alpha
and
|G|
is the order of the group, then the partition function counts the number of irrep
\alpha
in the Hilbert space is given by

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{\alpha}(g)^{*}Z^{g}(\tau,\overline{\tau}),

(2.15)

whose high-temperature limit is known since each
Z^{g}
’s high-temperature limit is known. Moreover, the assumption that
G
acts faithfully implies that the ground state in the defect Hilbert space has
h+\overline{h}>0
, therefore (
2.15
) is dominated by
Z^{\mathds{1}}(\tau,\overline{\tau})=Z(\tau,\overline{\tau})
in the high temperature limit. This allows us to derive the following result. Every irreducible representation has to appear in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and they also have a Cardy-like growth. Specifically, we can consider the asymptotic growth
\rho_{0,\alpha}(\Delta)
of the occurrence of a particular irrep
\alpha
of
G
as a function of the scaling dimension
\Delta
takes the form,

\rho_{0,\alpha}(\Delta)=\frac{d_{\alpha}}{|G|}\rho_{0}(\Delta).

(2.16)

This result will be useful for us later.

\displaystyle g\times\mathcal{L}_{Q}=\mathcal{L}_{Q}\times g=\mathcal{L}_{Q},\quad g\times\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}}=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}}\times g=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Q}\times\mathcal{L}_{Q}=2\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}}\times\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}}=2\mathcal{L}_{Q},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}}\times\mathcal{L}_{Q}=\mathcal{L}_{Q}\times\mathcal{L}_{\overline{Q}}=\sum_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\hat{\sigma}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\eta}}g.

3.2
Group theoretical fusion category

Let’s consider the fusion category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
, where
G
is a finite group and

Generically, when

4.3
Constraints on RG flow

To study the constraints on the RG flow, we want to determine if the fusion category symmetry
\mathcal{C}
is anomalous, in the sense that if it obstructs a
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric trivial gapped phase.
10
10

10

Here, by
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric gapped phase we mean the Hamiltonian or Lagrangian preserves the
\mathcal{C}
-symmetry while it could be spontaneously broken etc. By
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric trivial gapped phase, we mean there’s a unique ground state in this phase.
As pointed out in
[
4
]
, module categories
\mathcal{M}
over
\mathcal{C}
are in bijection with
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric gapped phases such that the ground states are in bijection with the simple objects in
\mathcal{M}
. Therefore, to check whether a fusion category symmetry
\mathcal{C}
has a trivial gapped phase is to check if it has a module category
\mathcal{M}
with a single simple object. Equivalently, one can check if
\mathcal{C}
admits a fiber functor.

In general, this is not an easy problem. For the case of the group-theoretic fusion category, this is relatively easy because all the indecomposable module categories over
\mathcal{C}(G,\omega,H,\psi)
can be explicitly constructed as pointed out in
[
51
]
. In fact, the indecomposable module categories over
\mathcal{C}(G,\omega,H,\psi)
and the indecomposable module categories over
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
are in a canonical bijection. However, as we will see in a concrete example below that this bijection does not preserve the number of simple elements in the module category.
11
11

11

The authors thank the referee for pointing out a mistake in our draft which leads to this discussion.
Hence, in general, the existence of a module category with a single simple object over
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}
does not imply the existence of the a module category with a single simple object over
\mathcal{C}(G,1,H,\psi)
. Therefore, the fact that
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}
has trivial anomaly does not necessarily imply the symmetry
\mathcal{C}(G,1,H,\psi)
has a symmetric trivial gapped phase.

Physically, this bijection between symmetric gapped phases of
\mathcal{C}(G,\omega,H,\psi)
and
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
can be seen as follows. Consider a theory
\mathcal{T}
with global symmetry
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
with a relevant operator

preserving the
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-symmetry driving the
\mathcal{T}
to some gapped phase
A
. Since
A
is
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-symmetric, we can consider gauge the anomaly free subgroup
H
with discrete torsion
\psi
to get a
\mathcal{C}(G,\omega,H,\psi)
-symmetric gapped phase denoted as
A/H_{\psi}
. On the other hand, since the operator
O(x)
preserve the entire
G
-symmetry, it will survive the
H_{\psi}
-gauging and remain a local operator in the theory
\mathcal{T}/H_{\psi}
which we get from gauging
H_{\psi}
in the theory
\mathcal{T}
.

will trigger the RG flow in the theory
\mathcal{T}/H_{\psi}
and the theory would flow to the
\mathcal{C}(G,\omega,H,\psi)
-symmetric gapped phase

. In this way, we construct a map from the symmetric gapped phases of the theories with
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
-symmetry to the symmetric gapped phases of the theories with
\mathcal{C}(G,\omega,H,\psi)

Kodaira-Spencer Anomalies with Stora-Zumino Method
Davide Rovere

Abstract
Holomorphic diffeomorphism anomalies of 2 ​ n 2\,n -dimensional gravitational theories in Beltrami parametrisation (Kodaira-Spencer anomalies) are computed in the brst framework, using an extension of the Stora-Zumino method. This method, which allows to compute anomalies in a very concise way, makes manifest the topological origin of anomalies. They have a clear geometric interpretation, since they are expressed in terms of Chern polynomials and Pontryagin invariants. The key ingredient is the formulation of the brst transformations in terms of polyforms, whose total degree is the sum of the form degree and of the ghost number. This approach simplifies significantly the analysis available in literature and it allows to compute many other solutions. Namely, an anomaly, which was computed using different methods, is proved to be a consistent brst anomaly, thereby supplementing a conclusion in a previous analysis.

7
Conclusions

We computed the Kodaira-Spencer anomalies in the
brst
formulation of
2\,n
-dimensional gravitational theories in Beltrami parametrisation, using an extension of the Stora-Zumino method. This is an example of application of the Stora-Zumino method in a context in which the breaking of the horizontality condition of the polycurvature is maximal: The polycurvature has components of ghost number one and two, as in (
1.5
), generalising the previously known cases, in which the polycurvature is horizontal (
1.3
), or it has only a non-vanishing ghost number one component (
1.4
).

The result of the computation is the equation (
6.39
). The Stora-Zumino approach simplifies significantly the analysis in
Bandelloni:1998vp
. The anomalies we found have a clear geometric interpretation, since they are expressed in terms of Chern polynomials and Pontryagin invariants. The method was able to capture all the solutions found in equations (5.52) and (5.53) of
Bandelloni:1998vp
, but many other possibilities were obtained, since our solutions are classified by the partitions of
n+1
, instead of those of
n
. Namely, the anomalies obtained in
Bandelloni:1998vp
are those which are related, in our approach, to the invariants containing a factor
\Tr\mathcal{R}
. In other terms, only the anomalies
(\omega_{1k_{2}\dots k_{p}})_{1}^{(2n)}
are considered. In particular, the anomaly corresponding to the partition
k_{l}=0
,
l<p
,
k_{p}=n+1
, that is

(\omega_{n+1})^{(2n)}_{1}\simeq\Tr(\de\,c\,(\diff\,\de\,\mu)^n),

(7.1)

which is not captured in
Bandelloni:1998vp
, corresponds to the one proposed in equation (1.11) in
Losev:1996up
. So, we proved that this anomaly is a consistent anomaly in the
brst
sense too.

Appendix A
Homotopy operator and polyforms

A topological theory is characterised by the existence of an homotopy operator
\ell
, which allows to state that the
s
-cohomology and the
\delta
-cohomology are isomorph. But the
\delta
-cohomology is equivalent to the
s
-cohomology modulo
\mathrm{d}
. This shows immediately that the forms
\omega^{(p)}_{d+1-p}
involved in the
d
-dimensional descent can be obtained knowing the elements
{\omega^{\natural}}^{(p)}_{d+1-p}
in the
s
-cohomology on
p
-form of ghost number
d+1-p

Sorella:1992dr
;
Sorella:1993kq
;
Blaga:1995rv
;
Tataru:1995ru
.

Let us perform the direct computation, starting from the descent (for definiteness in two dimensions):

\displaystyle s\,\omega_{1}^{(2)}=-\mathrm{d}\omega_{2}^{(1)},

(A.1a)

\displaystyle s\,\omega_{2}^{(1)}=-\mathrm{d}\omega_{3}^{(0)},

(A.1b)

\displaystyle s\,\omega_{3}^{(0)}=0

(A.1c)

Denote the element in the cohomology with
\natural
. Arbitrary forms are denoted by
\sim
. The most general solution of the bottom equation is

\omega_{3}^{(0)}={\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}+s\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)}.

(A.2)

Compute the exterior differential:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\omega_{3}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(\ell\,s-s\,\ell)\,\omega_{3}^{(0)}=-s\,\ell\,\omega_{3}^{(0)}=-s\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-s\,\ell\,s\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)}=

\displaystyle=-s\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-s\,(s\,\ell+\mathrm{d})\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)}=-s\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-s\,\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)}.

(A.3)

Plugging into the second equation (
A.1b
),

s\,(\omega_{2}^{(1)}-\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)})=0,

(A.4)

whose general solution is

\omega_{2}^{(1)}={\omega^{\natural}}_{2}^{(1)}+\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}+\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)}+s\,\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{(1)}.

(A.5)

Compute its exterior differential:

\mathrm{d}\,\omega_{2}^{(1)}=(\ell\,s-s\,\ell)\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{2}^{(1)}+\mathrm{d}\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-s\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{(1)}.

(A.6)

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(\ell\,s-s\,\ell)\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}=\ell\,s\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-s\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}=

\displaystyle=\ell\,(\ell\,s-\mathrm{d})\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-s\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}=-\mathrm{d}\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-s\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)},

(A.7)

so that

\mathrm{d}\,\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,s\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}.

(A.8)

Therefore, we get

\mathrm{d}\,\omega_{2}^{(1)}=-s\,(\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{2}^{(1)}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}+\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{(2)}).

(A.9)

Plugging into the top equation (
A.1a
),

s\,(\omega_{1}^{(2)}-\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{2}^{(1)}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}-\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{(2)})=0,

(A.10)

whose general solution is

\omega_{1}^{(2)}={\omega^{\natural}}_{1}^{(2)}+\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{2}^{(1)}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}+\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{(2)}+s\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{(2)}.

(A.11)

Summarising, the solution is

\displaystyle\ignorespaces\omega_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle={\omega^{\natural}}_{1}^{(2)}+\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{2}^{(1)}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}+\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{(2)}+s\,\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{(2)},

(A.12a)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}^{(1)}

\displaystyle={\omega^{\natural}}_{2}^{(1)}+\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}+\mathrm{d}\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)}+s\,\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{(1)},

(A.12b)

\displaystyle\omega_{3}^{(0)}

\displaystyle={\omega^{\natural}}_{3}^{(0)}+s\,\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{(0)}.

(A.12c)

This result can be readly generalised to
d
dimensions:

\displaystyle\omega^{(p)}_{d+1-p}

\displaystyle={\omega^{\natural}}^{(p)}_{d+1-p}+\ell\,{\omega^{\natural}}^{(p-1)}_{d+2-p}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell^{2}\,{\omega^{\natural}}^{(p-2)}_{d+3-p}+\dots\,+

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{p!}\,\ell^{p}\,{\omega^{\natural}}^{(0)}_{d+1}+\mathrm{d}\tilde{\omega}_{d+1-p}^{(p-1)}+s\,\tilde{\omega}_{d-p}^{(p)},

(A.13)

for any
0\leqslant p\leqslant d
.

The whole solution fits nicely into a polyform, as
p
varies:

\displaystyle\omega^{(d)}_{1}+\dots+\omega^{(0)}_{d+1}

\displaystyle=(1+\ell+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell^{2}+\dots+\tfrac{1}{d!}\,\ell^{d})\,{\omega^{\natural}}^{(0)}_{d+1}\,+

\displaystyle+(1+\ell+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\ell^{2}+\dots+\tfrac{1}{(d-1)!}\,\ell^{d-1})\,{\omega^{\natural}}^{(1)}_{d}+\dots\,+

\displaystyle+{\omega^{\natural}}^{(d)}_{1}+(\mathrm{d}+s)\,(\tilde{\omega}^{(d)}_{0}+\dots+\tilde{\omega}^{(0)}_{d}),

(A.14)

that is,

\omega_{d+1}=e^{\ell}\,\omega^{\natural}_{d+1}+\delta\,\tilde{\omega}_{d},

(A.15)

having defined

\displaystyle\ignorespaces\delta

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{d}+s,

(A.16a)

\displaystyle\omega_{d+1}

\displaystyle:=\omega_{1}^{(d)}+\dots+\omega_{d+1}^{(0)},

(A.16b)

\displaystyle\omega^{\natural}_{d+1}

\displaystyle:={\omega^{\natural}}^{(d)}_{1}+\dots+{\omega^{\natural}}^{(0)}_{d+1},

(A.16c)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{d}

\displaystyle:=\tilde{\omega}^{(d)}_{0}+\dots+\tilde{\omega}^{(0)}_{d}.

(A.16d)

This is in agreement with the isomorphism between
s
- and
\delta
-cohomology: if
\omega^{\natural}_{d+1}
is an
s
-cocycle, then
e^{\ell}\,\omega^{\natural}_{d+1}
is a
\delta
-cocycle.

Appendix C
A counterterm for an anomaly

The computation of local
\delta
-cohomology on the space of finite polynomials of
c^{i}
and
\mu^{i}
was performed throughout the paper. Nevertheless, if we extend the space of fields and if we allow non-polynomial or non-finite polynomial expressions, it would be possible in principle to find local counterterms which eliminate some anomalies. As an example, in this appendix it is shown that the Kodaira-Spencer anomaly corresponding to the partition
k_{l}=1,l=1,\dots,p
can be eliminated by means of a logarithmic counterterm if the integrating matrix
\lambda_{i}{}^{I}
is included in the field space. This fact was pointed out in
Bandelloni:1998vp
, but in that context it is a starting point, whereas it seems more natural that it is an arrival one.

The result has an analogue in the general covariant formulation. Taking into account only the diffeomorphisms, the
brst
variation of the metric is

s\,g_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\,\xi_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\,\xi_{\mu}.

(C.1)

where
\xi^{\mu}
is the diffeomorphism anticommuting ghost. As shown in
Bonora:1984pz
, the following cocycle

\partial_{\mu}\,\xi^{\mu}\,e_{2n}(R),

(C.2)

where
e_{2n}(R)
is the
2\,n
-dimensional Euler form, becomes trivial by taking into account the logarithm of the determinant of the metric. Indeed, there exists a one-weighted vector density
K^{\mu}
, such that
Chern:1944

e_{2n}(R)=-\partial_{\mu}\,K^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}^{2n}x.

(C.3)

Using (
C.1
), one can show that

s\,\sqrt{|g|}=\partial_{\mu}\,(\xi^{\mu}\,\sqrt{|g|}).

(C.4)

Since
K^{\mu}
is a one-weighted vector density, its
s
-variation reads

s\,K^{\mu}=\partial_{\nu}\,(\xi^{\nu}\,K^{\mu})-K^{\nu}\,\partial_{\nu}\,\xi^{\mu},

(C.5)

Now, consider that

s\,(K^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}\,\log{\sqrt{|g|}})=\partial_{\mu}\,(K^{\mu}\,\partial_{\nu}\,\xi^{\nu})-\partial_{\mu}\,\xi^{\mu}\,\partial_{\nu}\,K^{\nu}.

(C.6)

Therefore, integrating at both sides, one finds an expression whose
s
-variation gives the integrated anomaly:

s\int d^{2n}x\,K^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}\,\log{\sqrt{|g|}}=\int\partial_{\mu}\,\xi^{\mu}\,e_{2n}(R).

(C.7)

Let us proceed similarly for the Kodaira-Spencer anomaly
(\omega_{1\dots 1})^{(2n)}_{1}
. Observe that

\displaystyle(\omega_{1\dots 1})^{(2n)}_{1}

\displaystyle\simeq\Tr(\de\,c)\,\Tr(\hat{\de}\,(\de\,\mu))\dots\Tr(\hat{\de}\,(\de\,\mu))=

\displaystyle=\Tr(\de\,c)\,\hat{\partial}\,[\Tr(\de\,\mu)\,\Tr(\hat{\de}\,(\de\,\mu))\dots\Tr(\hat{\de}\,(\de\,\mu))]=:-\mathrm{d}^{n}z\,\mathrm{d}^{n}\bar{z}\,\Tr(\de\,c)\,\partial_{i}\,K^{i}.

(C.8)

Taking the determinant of the
s
-variation of the integrating matrix,

s\,\det\lambda=\partial_{i}\,(c^{i}\,\det\lambda).

(C.9)

Thus, similarly to (
C.6
),

\displaystyle s\,(\mathrm{d}^{n}z\,\mathrm{d}^{n}\bar{z}\,K^{i}\,\partial_{i}\,\log{\det\lambda})

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\,(\mathrm{d}^{n}z\,\mathrm{d}^{n}\bar{z}\,K^{i}\,\Tr(\de\,c))-\mathrm{d}^{n}z\,\mathrm{d}^{n}\bar{z}\,\partial_{j}\,c^{j}\,\partial_{i}\,K^{i}=

\displaystyle=-\hat{\partial}\,[\Tr(\de\,\mu)\,\Tr(\hat{\de}\,(\de\,\mu))\dots\Tr(\hat{\de}\,(\de\,\mu))\,\Tr(\de\,c)]+(\omega_{n+1})^{(2n)}_{1}=

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{d}\,[\Tr(\de\,\mu)\,\Tr(\diff\,(\de\,\mu))\dots\Tr(\diff\,(\de\,\mu))\,\Tr(\de\,c)]+(\omega_{n+1})^{(2n)}_{1},

(C.10)

where the last step follows from the same trick as in (
6.26
). Finally, integrating the previous relation,

s\int\mathrm{d}^{n}z\,\mathrm{d}^{n}\bar{z}\,K^{i}\,\partial_{i}\,\log{\det\lambda}=\int(\omega_{n+1})^{(2n)}_{1}.

(C.11)

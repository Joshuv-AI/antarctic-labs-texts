Abstract
. We prove that any Lipschitz map that satisfies a condition inspired by the work of David may be decomposed into countably many bi-Lipschitz pieces.



Fix complete metric spaces
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
and
s>0
.
A map
f\colon X\to Y
is
Lipschitz
if there exists
L\geq 0
such that
\rho(f(x),f(y))\leq Ld(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
.
An injective Lipschitz map is
bi-Lipschitz
if
f^{-1}
is Lipschitz.
Let
\mathcal{H}^{s}
denote the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff (outer) measure on a metric space and, for
S
a subset of a metric space, let

\Theta^{*,s}(S,x):=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{s}((B(x,r)\cap S))}{(2r)^{s}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta^{s}_{*}(S,x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{s}((B(x,r)\cap S))}{(2r)^{s}}.

Theorem
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be Lipschitz,
\mathcal{H}^{s}(X)<\infty
and, for
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-a.e.
x\in X
, suppose that

(1)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{s}(B(f(x),\lambda_{x}r)\setminus f(B(x,r)))}{(2\lambda_{x}r)^{s}}<\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{*}^{s}(Y,f(x))

for some
0<\lambda_{x}\leq 1
.
Then there exists a countable Borel decomposition
X=N\cup\bigcup_{i}X_{i}
with
\mathcal{H}^{s}(N)=0
such that each
f|_{X_{i}}
is bi-Lipschitz.

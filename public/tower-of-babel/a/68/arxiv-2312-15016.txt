Neutrinoless Double Beta Decay without Vacuum Majorana Neutrino Mass
Lukáš Gráf, Sudip Jana, Oliver Scholer, Nele Volmer

Abstract
We present a proof-of-concept extension to the Standard Model that can generate a non-vanishing neutrinoless double beta decay ( 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta ) signal without the existence of Majorana neutrinos or lepton number violation in the zero-density vacuum-ground-state Lagrangian. We propose that the 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta can be induced by the capture of an ultralight scalar field, a potential dark matter candidate, that carries two units of lepton number. This makes the observable 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta spectrum indistinguishable from the usual 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta mechanisms by any practical means. We find that a non-zero 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta rate does not require neutrinos to be fundamentally Majorana particles. However, for sizeable decay rates within the range of next-generation experiments, the neutrino will, generally, acquire an (effective) Majorana mass as the scalar field undergoes a transition to the Bose-Einstein condensate phase. We also discuss the distinction between the aforementioned scenario and the case in which the emission of a lepton-number-two scalar leads to 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta , exhibiting discernible qualitative features that make it experimentally distinguishable from the conventional scenario.

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset

\displaystyle m_{\phi}^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\phi+\lambda_{\phi}(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)^{2}+\lambda_{H\phi}(H^{\dagger}H)(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)

\displaystyle+Y_{ij}^{\nu}\overline{L}_{i}\tilde{H}\nu_{R,j}+g_{ij}\overline{\nu}_{R,i}\nu_{R,j}^{C}\phi+\mathrm{h.c.}

Other Phenomenological Implications
.
—
While our extension to the SM is rather simple, it comes with a variety of new phenomenological features that should be taken into account.
Numerous processes across various energy scales can detect the presence of the scalar

Conclusion
.
—
The evidence for lepton number violation that would be implied by a future observation of neutrinoless double beta decay is usually automatically assumed to be tied to the Majorana nature of neutrinos, as it stems from the black-box theorem. The purpose of this work is to point out several subtleties that may arise in this connection for certain scenarios. Specifically, if an extra particle carrying lepton number takes part in
0\nu\beta\beta
, the entire process can preserve the
B-L
symmetry. This is, for instance, the case for
0\nu\beta\beta
with emission of a scalar field with
L=2
, but even more intriguingly, for
0\nu\beta\beta
triggered by a hypothetical capture of such a scalar from a cosmic background. As discussed above, assuming a small momentum of the incoming scalar particle, this
0\nu\beta\beta
mechanism would lead to a signature in principle indistinguishable from the usual mass mechanism. Although this observation is certainly interesting, the corresponding rate of the
0\nu\beta\beta
induced by the scalar capture would be beyond the scope of any future experiment unless the number density of the considered scalar field is assumed to be large. In such a case, however, the scalar field forms a Bose-Einstein condensate, inducing an in-medium expectation value that effectively breaks the
B-L
symmetry. While the
B-L
number remains conserved in the vacuum ground state, neutrinos acquire an effective in-medium Majorana mass, which in turn triggers
0\nu\beta\beta
. In this way, the implication pointing from lepton number violation towards Majorana neutrino mass is restored for testable
0\nu\beta\beta
half-lives, but the reasons for its validity, as pointed out, are quite distinct and more subtle than in usually considered scenarios.

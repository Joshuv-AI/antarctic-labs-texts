Dilaton Solutions for Laboratory Constraints and Lunar Laser Ranging
Philippe Brax, Hauke Fischer, Christian Käding, Mario Pitschmann

Abstract
We derive approximate analytical solutions to the environment-dependent dilaton field theory equations in the presence of a one or two mirror system or a sphere. The one-dimensional equations of motion are integrated for each system. The solutions obtained herein can be applied to q BOUNCE experiments, neutron interferometry and for the calculation of the dilaton field induced ”Casimir force” in the Cannex experiment as well as for Lunar Laser Ranging. They are typical of the Damour-Polyakov screening mechanism whereby deviations from General Relativity are suppressed by a vanishingly small direct coupling of the dilaton to matter in dense environments.

I
Introduction

The accelerated expansion of the Universe may require the introduction of additional degrees of freedom (see
Joyce:2014kja
for a recent review). Such new degrees of freedom, in particular light scalars, are theoretically well motivated irrespective of their role for the acceleration of the expansion of the Universe. If they exist in Nature, they must either only be feebly coupling to other matter or appear in some screened form in order to
prevent detection in all past experiments and observations involving scalar fifth forces. A number of screening mechanisms exist
Joyce:2014kja
, the chameleon
Khoury:2003rn
;
Khoury:2003aq
;
Brax:2004qh
and Damour-Polyakov mechanisms
Damour:1994zq
, the K-mouflage
Babichev:2009ee
;
Brax:2012jr
;
Brax:2014wla
and Vainshtein ones
Vainshtein:1972sx
, allowing such hypothetical fields to remain unseen in local tests of gravity.

In the companion paper
Pitschmann:2018aa
, gravity resonance spectroscopy
Abele:2009dw
;
Jenke:2011zz
, a Casimir force experiment
Sedmik:2018kqt
and Lunar Laser Ranging (LLR)
nordtvedt2001lunar
are used for the first time to put new bounds on the environment-dependent dilaton
Brax:2010gi
;
Brax:2011ja
. The LLR bounds have already been discussed for chameleon
PhysRevD.97.104044
and symmetron scalar fields
PhysRevD.84.103521
. The dilaton model conjugates two ingredients. First of all, it involves an exponentially decreasing potential as expected in the strong coupling limit of string theory
Gasperini:2001pc
;
Damour:2002nv
;
Damour:2002mi
and second it is the simplest realisation of the least coupling principle as advocated in
Damour:1994zq
. Hence this model is a prime example, with the symmetron, of a model subject to the Damour-Polyakov screening mechanism
PhysRevD.82.063519
.
The experimental analysis in
Pitschmann:2018aa
depends heavily on the field profile of dilatons in both the one mirror or two mirror setups, where the field is either present over an infinite plane of high density or is confined between two such parallel planes. This is the purpose of this paper to provide a detailed analysis of the environment-dependent dilaton in realistic situations which can be tested using current experiments. For instance the analysis of the LLR results as well as the screening of neutrons in Q-bounce experiments employ the dilaton solution around and inside a sphere as derived here.
For a similar study applied to chameleon field theories see
Ivanov:2016rfs
. The symmetron field equations have been studied in a similar fashion in
Brax:2017hna
;
Pitschmann:2020ejb
. Testing screened models can be tackled from the laboratory to cosmological scales as reviewed in
Brax:2021wcv
.

In section
II
we will provide some background information on dilatons, which will provide the relevant definitions for the field theory analysis. We consider a two-dimensional parameter subspace of the dilaton model, which is of special cosmological significance as noted in section
III
. Then, in section

V

VI

VII
provides relevant information on the
q

VIII
the induced pressure in Casimir experiments due to the dilaton field between two mirrors of the experimental setup is derived, while in section
IX
bounds due to LLR are given. A conclusion in section
X
will be followed by Appendix
A
, in which the precession of the lunar perigee induced by fifth forces is obtained.

II
Background

Following
Brax:2018iyo
, the dilaton potential is given by

\displaystyle V(\phi)=V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi/m_{\text{Pl}}}\>,

(1)

where
V_{0}
is an energy scale related to the dark energy of the Universe and
\lambda
a numerical constant. This potential corresponds to the string theory dilaton potential in the strong coupling limit
Damour:1994zq
;
Damour:2002nv
. Together with the coupling to matter this induces an effective potential

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho)=V(\phi)+A(\phi)\,\rho\>,

(2)

where for the environment-dependent dilaton we have
Brax:2010gi

\displaystyle A(\phi)=1+\frac{A_{2}}{2m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi^{2}\>,

(3)

and hence

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho)=V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi/m_{\text{Pl}}}+\frac{A_{2}\rho}{2m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi^{2}\>.

(4)

Here, we have neglected an additional term
\rho
, which does not affect the equations of motion.
The minimum value
\phi_{\rho}
in the presence of a density
\rho
is given by
V_{\text{eff},\phi}(\phi;\rho)\big|_{\phi=\phi_{\rho}}=0
and reads

\displaystyle\phi_{\rho}=\frac{m_{\text{Pl}}}{\lambda}\,W\bigg(\frac{\lambda^{2}V_{0}}{A_{2}\rho}\bigg)

(5)

with the Lambert
W
-function

\displaystyle W(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-n)^{n-1}}{n!}\,x^{n}

\displaystyle=x-x^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\,x^{3}-\frac{8}{3}\,x^{4}+\frac{125}{24}\,x^{5}-\frac{54}{5}\,x^{6}+\ldots\>.

(6)

For large arguments the approximative relation holds:

\displaystyle W(x)\simeq\ln x\>.

(7)

The mass
\mu_{\rho}
of the quantum fluctuation is therefore

\displaystyle\mu_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{V_{\text{eff},\phi\phi}(\phi_{\rho};\rho)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{\rho}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho}\>.

(8)

We employ the metric signature (
+---
), for which the stress-energy tensor of a scalar field is

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\alpha}\phi\,\partial^{\alpha}\phi-V(\phi;\rho)\Big)\>,

(9)

while the equations of motion read

\displaystyle\Box\phi+V_{\text{eff},\phi}(\phi;\rho)=0\>

(10)

in the presence of matter.

III
The ”Cosmological” Dilaton

The parameter space of the dilaton model is 3-dimensional (
V_{0}
,
\lambda
,
A_{2}
). Since we are interested only in ”cosmological” dilatons, having significance in the
cosmological domain, we consider only the 2-dimensional parameter subspace, for which

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{V};\rho_{V})=3\Omega_{\Lambda 0}m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}H_{0}^{2}=6.73\times 10^{-34}\>\text{MeV}^{4}\>,

(11)

where
\phi_{V}
,
\rho_{V}
are the corresponding vacuum values and the density parameter
\Omega_{\Lambda 0}\sim 0.73
. This choice neglects the possible instability of the potential under radiative corrections. We take the parameter
V_{0}
as a function of
A_{2}
and
\lambda
such that Eq. (
11

It is important to notice that the coupling function involves the strong coupling scale

M=\frac{m_{\rm Pl}}{\sqrt{A}_{2}}.

(12)

In the spirit of an effective field theory expansion of the coupling function, one must require that
\phi/M\ll 1
to guarantee that terms of higher order in
\phi
can be neglected. Similarly the exponential potential involves the scale

\Lambda=\frac{m_{\rm Pl}}{\lambda}

(13)

and one must make sure that typically
\phi/\Lambda\gg 1
to guarantee that higher order string corrections in

can be neglected. We will make sure that these conditions are satisfied in the following.

It is straightforward to show that the condition (
11

\displaystyle V_{0}(A_{2},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{2}\rho_{V}}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{A_{2}\rho_{V}}\,3\Omega_{\Lambda 0}m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}H_{0}^{2}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left\{\sqrt{1+\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{A_{2}\rho_{V}}\,3\Omega_{\Lambda 0}m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}H_{0}^{2}}-1\right\}\>.

(14)

The dilaton parameter space becomes effectively 2-dimensional (
\lambda
,
A_{2}
) simplifying also the representation of experimental constraints.

We may summarize the findings of the tables as follows. For roughly
\lambda\propto\sqrt{A_{2}}
we expect finite limits to be obtainable from experiments as long as
\lambda
is not too large since otherwise
\phi_{\rho}
decreases, i.e. the dilaton effectively disappears and the interaction range
1/\mu_{\rho}
vanishes as well. For

we expect no limits from experiments since
\phi_{\rho}
becomes small. On the other hand, for
\lambda\gg A_{2}
we also do not expect bounds since in this case either

\phi_{\rho}
diverges and cannot act dynamically anymore. Hence, we find that the dilaton has a physical impact only within a restricted region in the

\lambda

\lambda

Pitschmann:2018aa

IV
one mirror

In this section, we treat the case of a single mirror filling the infinite half-space
z\leq 0
.
The 1-dimensional equation of motion reads (see Eq. (
10
))

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\phi}{dz^{2}}=V_{\text{eff},\phi}(\phi;\rho)\>.

(16)

Multiplication by
\phi^{\prime}
and integration with respect to
z
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\phi}{dz}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\phi}{dz}\right)^{2}\bigg|_{z=z_{0}}=V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho)-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho)\big|_{z=z_{0}}\>,

(17)

with the integration constant
z_{0}
and leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi(z)}\frac{d\phi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho)-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho)\big|_{z=z_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\phi}{dz}\right)^{2}\bigg|_{z=z_{0}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\pm\sqrt{2}\left(z-z_{0}\right),

(18)

where
\phi_{0}=\phi(z_{0})
, the
+
sign is for
\phi^{\prime}\geq 0
, while the
-
sign holds for
\phi^{\prime}\leq 0
.

IV.1
The Vacuum Region

First, we consider the case of low density
\rho_{V}
corresponding to the medium above the mirror and
search for a solution that asymptotically for
z\to\infty
goes as
\phi(z)\to\phi_{V}
with

\displaystyle\phi_{V}=\frac{m_{\text{Pl}}}{\lambda}\,W\bigg(\frac{\lambda^{2}V_{0}}{A_{2}\rho_{V}}\bigg)\>,

(19)

implying
\phi^{\prime}\to 0
. We find for
z_{0}=0
and
z\to\infty
from Eq. (
17
)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\phi}{dz}\right)^{2}\bigg|_{z=0}=V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{V};\rho_{V})-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho_{V})\big|_{z=0}\>.

(20)

Using Eq. (
20
) in Eq. (
IV
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi(z)}\frac{d\phi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho_{V})-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{V};\rho_{V})}}=\sqrt{2}\,z\>,

(21)

respectively

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi(z)}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\displaystyle V_{0}\left(e^{-\lambda\phi/m_{\text{Pl}}}-e^{-\lambda\phi_{V}/m_{\text{Pl}}}\right)+\frac{A_{2}\rho_{V}}{2m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\left(\phi^{2}-\phi_{V}^{2}\right)}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,z\>.

(22)

Approximating the effective potential around its minimum at the vacuum value
\phi_{V}
, we find to leading order

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{V}}\int_{(\phi_{V}-\phi(z))/m_{\text{Pl}}}^{(\phi_{V}-\phi_{0})/m_{\text{Pl}}}\frac{d\tilde{\phi}}{\tilde{\phi}}=z

(23)

with the mass of the quantum fluctuation in vacuum

\displaystyle\mu_{V}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{V_{\text{eff},\phi\phi}(\phi;\rho_{V})}\Big|_{\phi=\phi_{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{V}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{V}}\>.

(24)

Inverting the relation straightforwardly leads to

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=\phi_{V}+\left(\phi_{0}-\phi_{V}\right)e^{-\mu_{V}z}\>.

(25)

IV.2
The High Density Region

Here, we consider the case of high density
\rho_{M}
as inside the mirror.
Clearly, for
z\to-\infty
we have
\phi(z)\to\phi_{M}
with

\displaystyle\phi_{M}=\frac{m_{\text{Pl}}}{\lambda}\,W\bigg(\frac{\lambda^{2}V_{0}}{A_{2}\rho_{M}}\bigg)\>,

(26)

and hence
\phi^{\prime}\to 0
. Therefore, we find for
z_{0}=0
and
z\to-\infty
from Eq. (
17
)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\phi}{dz}\right)^{2}\bigg|_{z=0}=V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{M};\rho_{M})-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho_{M})\big|_{z=0}\>.

(27)

Using Eq. (
27
) in Eq. (
IV
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi(z)}\frac{d\phi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho_{M})-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{M};\rho_{M})}}=\sqrt{2}\,z\>.

(28)

Proceeding analogously to the case of vacuum, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=\phi_{M}+\left(\phi_{0}-\phi_{M}\right)e^{\mu_{M}z}\>,

(29)

with the mass of the quantum fluctuation in the mirror

\displaystyle\mu_{M}=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{M}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{M}}\>.

(30)

IV.3
Boundary Conditions

Using the boundary conditions

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi}{dz}\bigg|_{z=0_{-}}=\frac{d\phi}{dz}\bigg|_{z=0_{+}}\>,

(31)

we find

\displaystyle\phi_{0}=\frac{\mu_{V}\,\phi_{V}+\mu_{M}\,\phi_{M}}{\mu_{V}+\mu_{M}}\>.

(32)

The second boundary condition

\displaystyle\phi(0_{-})=\phi(0_{+})\>,

(33)

is trivially satisfied.

IV.4
Final Solution

Summarising, we obtain the solution

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=\Theta(+z)\,\big(\phi_{V}+\left(\phi_{0}-\phi_{V}\right)e^{-\mu_{V}z}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\Theta(-z)\,\big(\phi_{M}+\left(\phi_{0}-\phi_{M}\right)e^{\mu_{M}z}\big)\>,

(34)

with

\displaystyle\mu_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{V}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{V}}\>,

\displaystyle\mu_{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{M}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{M}}\>,

(35)

and

\displaystyle\phi_{0}=\frac{\mu_{V}\,\phi_{V}+\mu_{M}\,\phi_{M}}{\mu_{V}+\mu_{M}}\>.

(36)

A prototype solution with values
A_{2}=10^{35}
,
\lambda=10^{25}
and
V_{0}=1.00034\times 3\Omega_{\Lambda 0}m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}H_{0}^{2}=6.73\times 10^{-10}
meV
4
,
\rho_{V}=10^{-15}
MeV
4
and
\rho_{M}=1.082\times 10^{-5}
MeV
4
is plotted in Fig.

V
two mirrors

In this section, we treat the case of two parallel infinitely thick mirrors separated at distance
2d
in
z
-direction with
z=0
being the center between the two mirrors.

V.1
The Vacuum Region

Here, we consider the case of low density
\rho_{V}
, as between the mirrors and choose
z_{0}=0
. Due to the symmetry of the setup, the derivative of the field has to vanish there yielding for Eq. (
IV
)

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi(z)}\frac{d\phi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho_{V})-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{0};\rho_{V})}}=\sqrt{2}\,z\>,

(37)

where
\phi_{0}=\phi(0)
. Approximating the potential around
\phi_{0}
yields

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(\phi;\rho_{V})-V_{\text{eff}}(\phi_{0};\rho_{V})\simeq-\mathfrak{D}_{0}\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right)+\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{2}\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right)^{2}\>,

(38)

with the positive constants

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda V_{0}}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{0}/m_{\text{Pl}}}-\frac{A_{2}\rho_{V}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi_{0}\>,

\displaystyle\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{0}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{V}}\>,

(39)

leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi(z)}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\displaystyle-\mathfrak{D}_{0}\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right)+\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{2}\left(\phi-\phi_{0}\right)^{2}}}=-\sqrt{2}\,|z|\>.

(40)

Substituting
\displaystyle x=1+\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}\left(\phi_{0}-\phi\right)
leads to the elementary integral

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\int_{1}^{1+\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}\,(\phi_{0}-\phi(z))}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\displaystyle x^{2}-1}}=|z|\>.

(41)

Integration and some transformations lead to the solution

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\phi_{0}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\,\big(1-\cosh(\mu_{0}z)\big)\>.

(42)

V.2
The High Density Region

We can read off the solution inside the mirrors directly from the corresponding solution in the one mirror case Eq. (
IV.4
)

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=\phi_{M}+\left(\phi_{d}-\phi_{M}\right)e^{-\mu_{M}(|z|-d)}\>,

(43)

where
\phi_{d}=\phi(d)=\phi(-d)
is the field value at the surfaces of the mirrors.

V.3
Boundary Conditions

Using the boundary conditions at the mirror surface

\displaystyle\phi(d_{-})=\phi(d_{+})\>,

(44)

gives
\phi_{d}
as a function of
\phi_{0}

\displaystyle\phi_{d}=\phi_{0}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\cosh(\mu_{0}d)\right)\>,

(45)

which together with the second boundary condition

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi}{dz}\bigg|_{z=d_{-}}=\frac{d\phi}{dz}\bigg|_{z=d_{+}}

(46)

gives an equation, which defines
\phi_{0}
implicitly

\displaystyle\phi_{M}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}\mu_{M}}\sinh(\mu_{0}d)=\phi_{0}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\cosh(\mu_{0}d)\right)

(47)

since
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
both are functions of
\phi_{0}
.

V.4
Final Solution

Summarising, we obtain the solution

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=\Theta(d-|z|)\left\{\phi_{0}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\,\big(1-\cosh(\mu_{0}z)\big)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\Theta(|z|-d)\left\{\phi_{M}+\left(\phi_{d}-\phi_{M}\right)e^{-\mu_{M}(|z|-d)}\right\}\>,

(48)

where

\displaystyle\mu_{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{M}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{M}}\>,

\displaystyle\mu_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{0}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{V}}\>,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda V_{0}}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{0}/m_{\text{Pl}}}-\frac{A_{2}\rho_{V}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi_{0}\>,

(49)

\phi_{d}
as a function of
\phi_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{d}=\phi_{0}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\cosh(\mu_{0}d)\right)\>,

(50)

and
\phi_{0}
is solution to

\displaystyle\phi_{M}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}\mu_{M}}\sinh(\mu_{0}d)=\phi_{0}+\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\cosh(\mu_{0}d)\right)\>.

(51)

A prototype solution is plotted in Fig.
3
. The induced pressure inside the planes is
\displaystyle P=-4.73\times 10^{-4}
pN/cm
2
(see section
VII
).

VI
Dilaton Field of a Sphere

In this section we consider the dilaton field as caused by a static massive sphere surrounded by vacuum with radius
R
and a homogenous high density
\rho_{S}
, which is used for the analysis of the
q
BOUNCE and Lunar Laser Ranging bounds.

Since neutrons are used in the search for dilatons in
q
BOUNCE experiments and neutron interferometry, it is important to understand their interaction with the dilaton. For a certain parameter regime this interaction between neutron and dilaton becomes strong. In this case, the neutron affects the background dilaton field as generated by the mirrors of the experimental setup in a non-negligible way, which in turn weakens the effect on the neutron,
viz.
screening of the neutron sets in (see also
Burrage_2021
).
Since we treat the dilaton as a classical field theory, a consistent description of its coupling to a quantum mechanical system is beyond our reach. Therefore, we employ a semi-classical treatment in which the neutron’s probability distribution times its mass acts as the source of the dilaton as defined in Eq. (
81
).

Concerning Lunar Laser Ranging, we approximate the Sun, Earth and Moon as spherical sources of dilatons with homogenous density and employ the solutions derived in this section.

The spherically symmetric field equation is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\phi}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d\phi}{dr}

\displaystyle=V_{\text{eff},\phi}(\phi;\rho)\>,

(52)

with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=0\>,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\phi(r)

\displaystyle\to\phi_{V}\>.

(53)

Inside the sphere we have density
\rho_{S}
. The corresponding minimum of the field value is

\displaystyle\phi_{S}=\frac{m_{\text{Pl}}}{\lambda}\,W\bigg(\frac{\lambda^{2}V_{0}}{A_{2}\rho_{S}}\bigg)\>.

(54)

We expand

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff},\phi}(\phi;\rho_{S})

\displaystyle\simeq V_{\text{eff},\phi\phi}(\phi_{S};\rho_{S})\left(\phi-\phi_{S}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{S}^{2}\left(\phi-\phi_{S}\right)

(55)

with

\displaystyle\mu_{S}=\frac{1}{m_{\text{Pl}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi_{S}/m_{\text{Pl}}}+A_{2}\rho_{S}}\>.

(56)

For the field equation we find

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\phi}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d\phi}{dr}\simeq\mu_{S}^{2}\left(\phi-\phi_{S}\right).

(57)

Introducing the field
\varphi

\displaystyle\phi-\phi_{S}=\frac{\varphi}{r}\>,

(58)

Eq. (
57
) takes on the simpler form

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\varphi}{dr^{2}}\simeq\mu_{S}^{2}\,\varphi\>.

(59)

From the general solution for
\varphi
we obtain the particular solution for
\phi
, which is convergent for
r\to 0
and satisfies the boundary condition
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
:

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\phi_{S}+\mathfrak{C}_{I}\frac{\displaystyle\sinh(\mu_{S}r)}{r}\>,

(60)

where
\mathfrak{C}_{I}
is some constant to be determined later.

In the vacuum outside the sphere we approximate

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff},\phi}(\phi;\rho_{V})

\displaystyle\simeq V_{\text{eff},\phi\phi}(\phi_{V};\rho_{V})\left(\phi-\phi_{V}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{V}^{2}\left(\phi-\phi_{V}\right),

(61)

where
\mu_{V}
is given by Eq. (
IV.1
).

For the field equation we find

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\phi}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d\phi}{dr}\simeq\mu_{V}^{2}\left(\phi-\phi_{V}\right).

(62)

With the field
\varphi

\displaystyle\phi-\phi_{V}=\frac{\varphi}{r}\>,

(63)

the field equation again simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\varphi}{dr^{2}}\simeq\mu_{V}^{2}\,\varphi\>.

(64)

The general solution of which provides the
\phi
solution convergent for
r\to\infty
and satisfying the boundary condition
\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\phi(r)\to\phi_{V}

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\phi_{V}+\mathfrak{C}_{O}\frac{e^{-\mu_{V}(r-R)}}{r}\>,

(65)

where
\mathfrak{C}_{O}
is some constant to be determined next.

The dilaton field has to satisfy the following boundary conditions at the surface of the sphere

\displaystyle\phi(R_{-})

\displaystyle=\phi(R_{+})\>,

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(R_{-})

\displaystyle=\phi^{\prime}(R_{+})\>,

(66)

which provides explicit expressions for
\mathfrak{C}_{I}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{O}
.
After some elementary algebraical manipulations we obtain the relations

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{V}-\phi_{S}}{\cosh(\mu_{S}R)}\frac{1+\mu_{V}R}{\mu_{S}+\mu_{V}\tanh(\mu_{S}R)}\>,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{O}

\displaystyle=-R\,\frac{1-\frac{1}{\mu_{S}R}\tanh(\mu_{S}R)}{1+\frac{\mu_{V}}{\mu_{S}}\tanh(\mu_{S}R)}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{S}\right)\>.

(67)

In order to quantify the amount of ”screening” of a sphere, we introduce a formfactor, which we interpret as a ”screening charge”
\mathfrak{Q}
.
The interaction of the sphere with its surrounding is given by its outer field. Clearly, with decreasing radius
R
the sphere becomes increasingly ”unscreened” since the dilaton field inside the sphere decreases towards its minimum throughout the volume of the sphere. On the other hand, for large sphere radii the dilaton field reaches
its minimum already before the center of the sphere, it is no longer ”sourced” by the whole volume of the sphere, which becomes ”screened”.

An unscreened sphere has
R\ll 1/\mu_{S}
. Hence, we expand
\mathfrak{C}_{O}
in powers of
\mu_{S}R
to obtain for an ”unscreened” sphere

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{O}^{u}=-\frac{\mu_{S}^{2}R^{3}}{3}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{S}\right)\>.

(68)

We define the ”screening charge”
\mathfrak{Q}
as the ratio

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}=\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{O}}{\mathfrak{C}_{O}^{u}}=\frac{3}{\mu_{S}^{2}R^{2}}\frac{1-\frac{1}{\mu_{S}R}\tanh(\mu_{S}R)}{1+\frac{\mu_{V}}{\mu_{S}}\tanh(\mu_{S}R)}\>.

(69)

With this definition, see Fig.
4
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}\to\begin{cases}0\>,&\quad\text{for "screened" bodies with }\mu_{S}R\to\infty\>,\\
1\>,&\quad\text{for "unscreened" bodies with }\mu_{S}R\to 0\>.\end{cases}

Finally, we find for the solution

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\begin{cases}&\phi_{S}+\frac{\phi_{V}-\phi_{S}}{\cosh(\mu_{S}R)}\frac{1+\mu_{V}R}{\mu_{S}+\mu_{V}\tanh(\mu_{S}R)}\\

The acceleration experienced by a pointlike test body, which does not disturb the field, in the outer field of the sphere has been derived in
Pitschmann:2020ejb
for a generic scalar
\phi
in the non-relativistic limit as

\displaystyle\vec{f}_{\phi}=-m\vec{\nabla}\ln A(\phi)\>.

(70)

In all models of consideration
A(\phi)=1+\delta A(\phi)
with
\delta A(\phi)\ll 1
, such that
\ln A(\phi)\simeq\delta A(\phi)
. Consequently, we find for the force on a particle caused by a scalar
\phi
to leading order

\displaystyle\vec{f}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-m\vec{\nabla}A(\phi)\>.

(71)

In the case of dilatons Eq. (
3

\displaystyle\vec{f}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-m\,\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi\,\vec{\nabla}\phi\>,

(72)

and, respectively, for the acceleration

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi\,\vec{\nabla}\phi\>.

(73)

Asymptotically, for large

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{\phi}\to-\mathfrak{Q}\,\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\mu_{V}\mu_{S}^{2}R^{3}}{3}\,\phi_{V}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{S}\right)\frac{e^{-\mu_{V}(r-R)}}{r}\frac{\vec{r}}{r}\>,

(74)

justifying the definition of

VII
Dilaton-induced Frequency Shift in
q
BOUNCE

Here, we derive a discrete set of limits for the
q
BOUNCE experiment
Abele:2009dw
;
Jenke:2011zz
;
Jenke:2014yel
using the solutions obtained herein. In this experiment, ultra-cold neutrons are dropped in Earth’s gravitational potential and reflected by a neutron mirror, which has been reported for the first time in
Nesvizhevsky:2002ef
. The energy eigenstates are discrete and allow to apply the method of resonance spectroscopy. In
Jenke:2011zz
the basic setup is described. In the Rabi version of the experiment, spectroscopy has been realized with energy resolution 3
\times
10
-15
peV
Cronenberg:2018qxf
.

The experimental setup is such, that ultracold neutrons pass three regions, while being reflected on polished glass mirrors. In
Cronenberg:2018qxf
, the resonance spectroscopy transitions between the energy ground state
E_{1}=1.41
peV and the excited states
E_{3}=3.32
peV as well as
E_{4}=4.08
peV have been demonstrated. First, the neutrons pass region I which acts as a state selector for the ground state
|1\rangle
having energy
E_{1}
. A polished mirror at the bottom and a rough absorbing scatterer on top at a height of about 20
\mu
m serve to select the ground state. Neutrons in higher, unwanted states are scattered out of the system. This region has a length of 15 cm. Subsequently, in region II, a horizontal mirror performs harmonic oscillations with a tunable frequency
\omega
, which drives the system into a coherent superposition of ground and excited states. The length of this region is 20 cm. Finally, region III is identical to the first region and hence acts again as a ground state selector.

The quantum mechanical description of a neutron above a mirror in the gravitational potential is given by the Schrödinger equation
Westphal:2006dj
.
After separation into free transversal and bound vertical states

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}^{(0)}(\textbf{x},t)=\frac{e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}(p_{\perp}\cdot x_{\perp}-E_{\perp}t)}}{2\pi\hbar v_{\perp}}\,\psi_{n}^{(0)}(z)\,e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{n}t}\>,

(75)

it reads

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{N}}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{n}^{(0)}(z)}{\partial z^{2}}+m_{N}gz\,\psi_{n}(z)=E_{n}\psi_{n}^{(0)}(z)\>.

(76)

The characteristic length scale

\displaystyle z_{0}=\sqrt[3]{\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{N}^{2}g}}=5.87\,\mu\text{m}\>,

(77)

and energy scale
E_{0}
=
\sqrt[3]{\hbar^{2}m_{N}g^{2}/2}
are given by the mass
m_{N}
of the neutron and the acceleration of the Earth
g
. With the substitution

\displaystyle\sigma=\sqrt[3]{\frac{2m_{N}^{2}g}{\hbar^{2}}}\left(z-\frac{E_{n}}{m_{N}g}\right)\equiv\frac{z-z_{n}}{z_{0}}\>,

(78)

Eq. (
76
) becomes Airy’s equation

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{n}(\sigma)}{d\sigma^{2}}-\sigma\,\tilde{\psi}_{n}(\sigma)=0\>.

(79)

From the effective dilaton potential

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(\phi)=V_{0}\,e^{-\lambda\phi/m_{\text{Pl}}}+\frac{A_{2}\rho}{2m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi^{2}\>,

(80)

we can deduce the semi-classical neutron-dilaton coupling

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}=\frac{A_{2}}{2}\frac{m_{N}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\psi^{*}\psi\,\phi^{2}\>.

(81)

There are some subtleties involved here of a nature similar as in the case of the symmetron. We refer to
Brax:2017hna
for further information.

\displaystyle\textrm{V}=\frac{A_{2}}{2}\frac{m_{N}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\phi^{2}\>,

(82)

and leads to a resonance frequency shift to first order (see e.g.

\displaystyle\delta E_{mn}^{(1)}

Likewise, the first order correction to the wavefunctions reads (see e.g.

(84)

Hence, the correction to the density
\displaystyle\varrho_{n}^{(0)}(z)=\psi_{n}^{(0)*}(z)\psi_{n}^{(0)}(z)
to first order is given by

\displaystyle\varrho_{n}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle=2\,\mathfrak{Re}\big(\psi_{n}^{(0)*}(z)\psi_{n}^{(1)}(z)\big)

\displaystyle=A_{2}\,\frac{m_{N}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,\mathfrak{Re}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz^{\prime}\,\psi_{m}^{(0)*}(z^{\prime})\,\psi_{n}^{(0)}(z^{\prime})\,\phi(z^{\prime})^{2}}{E_{n}^{(0)}-E_{m}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\psi_{n}^{(0)*}(z)\psi_{m}^{(0)}(z)\>,

(85)

where
\mathfrak{Re}
denotes the real part.

In the one mirror case
the unperturbed normalized wavefunction for

Pitschmann:2019boa

\displaystyle\psi_{n}^{(0)}(z)=\frac{\text{Ai}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{z-z_{n}}{z_{0}}\Big)}{\sqrt{z_{0}}\,\text{Ai}^{\prime}\Big(-\displaystyle\frac{z_{n}}{z_{0}}\Big)}

(86)

with

Outside this region the wavefunction vanishes.
For a single mirror filling the region

\displaystyle\delta E_{mn}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{2}}{2}\frac{m_{N}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{1}{z_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\Big(\phi_{V}+\left(\phi_{0}-\phi_{V}\right)e^{-\mu_{V}z}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigg\{\frac{\displaystyle\text{Ai}\Big(\frac{z-z_{m}}{z_{0}}\Big)^{2}}{\text{Ai}^{\prime}\Big(-\displaystyle\frac{z_{m}}{z_{0}}\Big)^{2}}-\frac{\displaystyle\text{Ai}\Big(\frac{z-z_{n}}{z_{0}}\Big)^{2}}{\text{Ai}^{\prime}\Big(-\displaystyle\frac{z_{n}}{z_{0}}\Big)^{2}}\Bigg\}\>.

VIII
Dilaton-induced Pressure in CASIMIR experiments

Here, we consider limits that can be obtained by the Casimir And Non-Newtonian force EXperiment (
Cannex
)
Sedmik:2018kqt
(see also
Klimchitskaya:2019fsm
;
Klimchitskaya:2019nzu
).
This experiment consists of two parallel plates in a vacuum chamber and has been devised to measure the Casimir force and hypothetical fifth forces.
A dilaton field would induce a pressure between those plates, which can be measured with high precision.

We approximate the setup in one dimension along the
z
-axis as follows. Between the upper surface of the fixed lower mirror at
z=0
and the lower surface of the movable upper mirror at
z=d
there is vacuum. Then follows the upper mirror with thickness
D
and above that at
z>d+D
there is vacuum again.
In order to obtain the induced pressure for the
Cannex
setup, we can apply the force induced on a point particle by dilatons in Eq. (
71
). We obtain

\displaystyle\vec{f}_{\phi}=-\rho_{M}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\int_{d}^{d+D}dz\,\partial_{z}\ln A(\phi)\,\vec{e}_{z}\>.

(89)

Consequently, the pressure in
z
-direction is given by

\displaystyle P_{z}=-\rho_{M}\int_{d}^{d+D}dz\,\partial_{z}\ln A(\phi)\>.

(90)

The corresponding integral is a surface term and hence trivially carried out with the final result

\displaystyle P_{z}=\rho_{M}\,\Big(\ln A\big(\phi(d)\big)-\ln A\big(\phi(d+D)\big)\Big)\>.

(91)

This agrees with (see e.g.
Pitschmann:2020ejb
)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\nu}\ln A\,T\>,

(92)

where
T=g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}
and which reduces for a static field configuration of
\phi
to

\displaystyle\partial^{z}T_{zz}=-\partial_{z}P_{z}=\partial_{z}\ln A\,\rho_{M}\>.

(93)

In all models of consideration
A(\phi)=1+\delta A(\phi)
with
\delta A(\phi)\ll 1
, such that
\ln A(\phi)\simeq\delta A(\phi)
. For dilatons Eq. (
3
) holds and we finally obtain the dilaton-induced pressure

\displaystyle P_{z}=\rho_{M}\,\frac{A_{2}}{2m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\left(\phi^{2}(d)-\phi^{2}(d+D)\right).

(94)

For
\phi(d)
we employ the value
\phi_{d}
at the mirror surface of the corresponding two mirror solution given in Eq. (
50
), while for
\phi(d+D)
we can use the value
\phi_{0}
at the mirror surface of the one mirror solution given in Eq. (
36
) instead. Finally, we obtain for the pressure

\displaystyle P_{z}=\rho_{M}\,\frac{A_{2}}{2m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\left\{\phi_{d}^{2}-\left(\frac{\mu_{V}\,\phi_{V}+\mu_{M}\,\phi_{M}}{\mu_{V}+\mu_{M}}\right)^{2}\right\}.

(95)

This results can be used to carry out a numerical analysis
Pitschmann:2018aa
where the exclusion regions of the dilaton parameter space are obtained from the
Cannex
experiment as we expect a sensitivity of
|P_{z}|\leq 1
nPa.

IX
Dilaton Constraints by Lunar Laser Ranging

In this section we analyze bounds on dilatons due to Lunar Laser Ranging (LLR), which provides three separate constraints
nordtvedt2001lunar
due to

1.

the Nordvedt effect, which relates to a difference between the free fall acceleration of the Earth and Moon towards the Sun (equivalence principle violations),

2.

deviations from the inverse-square law at distances comparable to the Earth-Moon separation and

3.

time-variation of
G
.

Since the dilaton interaction is time-independent, it cannot induce a time-variation of the effective coupling
G
. Hence, only the first two constraints can lead to dilaton bounds and will be analyzed here.

IX.1
Constraints due to the Nordvedt Effect

For the first LLR test we consider the acceleration
of the Earth (
{\earth}
) and Moon (
{\leftmoon}
) towards the Sun (
{\astrosun}
).
For the Earth we find
74

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{\earth}

\displaystyle=\vec{a}_{G}+\vec{a}_{\phi{\earth}}

\displaystyle\simeq\vec{a}_{G}-\mathfrak{Q}_{\astrosun}\mathfrak{Q}_{\earth}\,\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\mu_{V}\mu_{\astrosun}^{2}R_{\astrosun}^{3}}{3}\,\phi_{V}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{\astrosun}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{e^{-\mu_{V}(r_{AU}-R_{\astrosun})}}{r_{AU}}\frac{\vec{r}_{AU}}{r_{AU}}\>,

(96)

where
\vec{r}_{AU}
is the vector pointing from Sun to Earth,
\vec{a}_{G}
is the acceleration due to the Sun’s gravitational field and
\vec{a}_{\phi{\earth}}
the acceleration due to the dilaton field of the Sun and given by Eq. (
74
), which
holds for a test particle in an outer field without screening. The screening of the Earth is taken into account by the second charge factor
\mathfrak{Q}_{\earth}
.
Likewise, the acceleration of the Moon is given by

\displaystyle\vec{a}_{\leftmoon}

\displaystyle=\vec{a}_{G}+\vec{a}_{\phi{\leftmoon}}

\displaystyle\simeq\vec{a}_{G}-\mathfrak{Q}_{\astrosun}\mathfrak{Q}_{\leftmoon}\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\mu_{V}\mu_{\astrosun}^{2}R_{\astrosun}^{3}}{3}\,\phi_{V}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{\astrosun}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{e^{-\mu_{V}(r_{AU}-R_{\astrosun})}}{r_{AU}}\frac{\vec{r}_{AU}}{r_{AU}}\>,

(97)

where
\vec{r}_{AU}
is again the vector pointing from Sun to Earth, which is approximately the vector pointing from Sun to Moon. Then,
\vec{a}_{G}
is the acceleration due to the Sun’s gravitational field and taken equal for Earth and Moon, such that any difference in acceleration between Earth and Moon,
viz.
an equivalence principle violation, is attributed to
the dilaton field.
For the equivalence principle violation quantified by the Eötvos parameter

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{em}}=2\frac{|\vec{a}_{\earth}-\vec{a}_{\leftmoon}|}{|\vec{a}_{\earth}+\vec{a}_{\leftmoon}|}\simeq\frac{|\vec{a}_{\phi{\earth}}-\vec{a}_{\phi{\leftmoon}}|}{|\vec{a}_{G}|}\>,

(98)

we find in the case of dilatons

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{em}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\mathfrak{Q}_{\astrosun}|\mathfrak{Q}_{\earth}-\mathfrak{Q}_{\leftmoon}|}{|\vec{a}_{G}|}\,\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\mu_{V}\mu_{\astrosun}^{2}R_{\astrosun}^{3}}{3}\,\phi_{V}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{\astrosun}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{e^{-\mu_{V}(r_{AU}-R_{\astrosun})}}{r_{AU}}\>.

(99)

Typically this is constrained at the
\eta_{\rm em}\leq 2\times 10^{-13}
level by the LLR experiment
Williams:2012nc
.

IX.2
Constraints due to Deviations from the Inverse-Square Law

For the second LLR test we consider the precession of the lunar perigee caused by fifth forces. The corresponding Eq. (
124
) is derived in Appendix
A
and reads

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Omega}{\Omega}\simeq-\frac{R^{2}}{GM_{\earth}}\left(\delta f(R)+\frac{R}{2}\,\delta f^{\prime}(R)\right).

(100)

It is derived in Appendix
A
. Here,
\delta f(R)
is the acceleration caused by dilatons at the maximum Earth-Moon separation
R
, and given by
74

\displaystyle\delta f(R)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathfrak{Q}_{\earth}\mathfrak{Q}_{\leftmoon}\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\mu_{V}\mu_{\earth}^{2}R_{\earth}^{3}}{3}\,\phi_{V}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{\earth}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{e^{-\mu_{V}(R-R_{\earth})}}{R}\>.

(101)

Using Eq. (
IX.2
) in Eq. (
100
) leads to the central relation

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Omega}{\Omega}

\displaystyle\simeq-\mathfrak{Q}_{\earth}\mathfrak{Q}_{\leftmoon}\frac{A_{2}}{m_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\frac{\mu_{V}\mu_{\earth}^{2}R_{\earth}^{3}}{3}\frac{R}{GM_{\earth}}\,\phi_{V}\left(\phi_{V}-\phi_{\earth}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1-\frac{\mu_{V}R}{2}\right)e^{-\mu_{V}(R-R_{\earth})}\>,

(102)

to be compared with experimental results of order
6\times 10^{-12}
.

The results of the numerical analysis for both tests are reproduced in the accompanying paper
Pitschmann:2018aa
.

X
Conclusion

We have derived approximate analytical solutions to the dilaton field theory in the presence of a one or two mirror system as well as for a sphere. The 1-dimensional equations of motion have been integrated in each case. The analytical results obtained herein provide the necessary input for the numerical study carried out in parallel to this work
Pitschmann:2018aa
. The latter provides bounds on dilatons by using results from three ”experiments”. First, from
q
BOUNCE we obtain results by employing bouncing ultracold neutrons, second,
Cannex
provides bounds by measuring induced pressures between parallel plates and third, from Lunar Laser Ranging we obtain additional bounds in the astrophysical regime.

Appendix A
Precession of the Lunar Perigee induced by Fifth Forces

In this Appendix we derive the precession of the lunar perigee induced by an arbitrary ”fifth force”. Historically, the following result is essentially due to
Isaac Newton
(
newton1999principia
, Propositions XLIII - XLV). For a modern derivation see
chandrasekhar1995newton
.
In this Appendix, any ”force” is understood as the absolute value of the centripetal force per mass

\displaystyle f(r)=-\frac{\vec{r}}{r}\cdot\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}\>.

(103)

We recall the Binet equation governing
u=1/r=g(\varphi)
(see e.g.
chandrasekhar1995newton
)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=h^{2}u^{2}\frac{d^{2}u}{d\varphi^{2}}+h^{2}u^{3}

\displaystyle=h^{2}g^{2}(\varphi)\frac{d^{2}g}{d\varphi^{2}}(\varphi)+h^{2}g^{3}(\varphi)\>,

(104)

where
h
is the absolute value of
\vec{h}=\vec{r}\times\vec{v}
.

First, we address the following question. Given that
u=1/r=g(\varphi)
is the inverse polar equation of an orbit described under the action of a centripetal force
f(r)
with a constant of areas
h
, what is the centripetal force
\tilde{f}(r)
under which a revolving orbit
u=1/r=g(\tilde{\varphi}/\alpha)
with
\tilde{\varphi}=\alpha\varphi
and a constant of areas
\tilde{h}=\alpha h
would be described, where
\alpha
is some assigned constant?
The polar form of an ellipse with its center at one focus and
\varphi
defined such that
\varphi=0
corresponds to the point nearest to this focus, is given by

\displaystyle\frac{p}{r}=1+e\cos\varphi

(105)

with eccentricity
e
and semi-latus rectum
p
. Hence, we have for an ellipse

\displaystyle f(r)=\frac{h^{2}}{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\>.

(106)

We now require the centripetal force
\tilde{f}(r)
under which the orbit

\displaystyle r=g(\tilde{\varphi}/\alpha)

(107)

will be described with a constant of areas

\displaystyle\tilde{h}=\alpha h\>.

(108)

We find

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(r)

\displaystyle=\tilde{h}^{2}u^{2}\frac{d^{2}u}{d\tilde{\varphi}^{2}}+\tilde{h}^{2}u^{3}

\displaystyle=\tilde{h}^{2}g^{2}(\tilde{\varphi}/\alpha)\frac{d^{2}g}{d\tilde{\varphi}^{2}}(\tilde{\varphi}/\alpha)+\tilde{h}^{2}g^{3}(\tilde{\varphi}/\alpha)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}h^{2}g^{2}(\varphi)\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{d^{2}g}{d\varphi^{2}}(\varphi)+\alpha^{2}h^{2}g^{3}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=h^{2}g^{2}(\varphi)\frac{d^{2}g}{d\varphi^{2}}(\varphi)+\alpha^{2}h^{2}g^{3}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=f(r)-h^{2}g^{3}(\varphi)+\alpha^{2}h^{2}g^{3}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=f(r)-h^{2}u^{3}+\alpha^{2}h^{2}u^{3}\>,

(109)

and finally
Newton’s theorem of revolving orbits

whittaker1988treatise

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(r)-f(r)=\frac{\tilde{h}^{2}-h^{2}}{r^{3}}\>.

(110)

In order to relate fifth forces to the precession of the Lunar perigee, we compare the elliptic orbit described under an inverse-square law of attraction with a nearly circular orbit caused by an inverse-square law of attraction together with a small fifth force. The elliptic orbit described by Eq. (
105
) is not revolving, while an additional fifth force leads to a deformation of the orbit and also lets the orbit revolve. Actually, the orbit of the Moon is to first order an ellipse with manifold corrections at higher orders. These higher orders also lead to corrections of the relation between fifth force and precession of the Lunar perigee, which are sub-leading and hence will be neglected.

For an elliptic orbit we have Eq. (
106
)
and find for the force causing the revolving orbit

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}}{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{h}^{2}-h^{2}}{r^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\frac{1}{r^{3}}\left[rh^{2}+p\left(\tilde{h}^{2}-h^{2}\right)\right].

(111)

For a nearly circular orbit, we may write

\displaystyle r=R-\delta r\>,

(112)

where
R
is the maximum distance, which for an ellipse is
R=a(1+e)
. Hence, we find

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(r)=\frac{1}{p}\frac{1}{r^{3}}\left[\left(R-\delta r\right)h^{2}+p\left(\tilde{h}^{2}-h^{2}\right)\right].

(113)

The new force we describe in the form

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(r)=\frac{1}{p}\frac{C(r)}{r^{3}}\>,

(114)

where
C(r)
is an arbitrary function. Since we consider nearly circular orbits, we perform a Taylor expansion

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(r)\simeq\frac{1}{p}\frac{C(R)-C^{\prime}(R)\delta r}{r^{3}}\>,

(115)

where
\displaystyle C^{\prime}(R)=\frac{dC}{dR}
. Eq. (
113
) leads to

\displaystyle\left(R-\delta r\right)h^{2}+p\left(\tilde{h}^{2}-h^{2}\right)=C(R)-C^{\prime}(R)\delta r\>.

(116)

Comparing coefficients gives

\displaystyle Rh^{2}+p\left(\tilde{h}^{2}-h^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=C(R)\>,

\displaystyle h^{2}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}(R)\>.

(117)

For nearly circular orbits we have to leading order
p=R
and hence

\displaystyle R\tilde{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=C(R)\>,

\displaystyle h^{2}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}(R)\>.

(118)

For
\alpha
we find

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{\tilde{h}}{h}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{R}\frac{C(R)}{C^{\prime}(R)}}\>.

(119)

Using Eq. (
114
) we can express
\alpha
in terms of
\tilde{f}(r)

\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{f}(R)}{3\tilde{f}(R)+R\tilde{f}^{\prime}(R)}}\>.

(120)

Next, we consider the case of gravitational attraction due to the Earth and an additional small fifth force
\delta f(r)
acting in radial direction and caused by the Earth. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(r)=\frac{GM_{{\earth}}}{r^{2}}+\delta f(r)\>.

(121)

To leading order we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha\simeq 1-\frac{R^{2}}{GM_{{\earth}}}\,\delta f(R)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{R^{3}}{GM_{{\earth}}}\,\delta f^{\prime}(R)\>.

(122)

The correction to the precession of the Lunar perigee is obviously given by

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Omega}{\Omega}=\alpha-1\>,

(123)

so we can finally connect the fifth force with the precession of the Lunar perigee

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Omega}{\Omega}\simeq-\frac{R^{2}}{GM_{{\earth}}}\left(\delta f(R)+\frac{R}{2}\,\delta f^{\prime}(R)\right).

(124)

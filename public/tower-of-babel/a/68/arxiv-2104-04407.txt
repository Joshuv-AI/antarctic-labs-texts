A branching process with deletions and mergers that matches the threshold for hypercube percolation Alternative title - Infanticide and Incest: a branching process with deletions and mergers
Laura Eslava, Sarah Penington, Fiona Skerman

Abstract
We define a graph process 𝒢 ⁡ ( p , q ) \mathcal{G}(p,q) based on a discrete branching process with deletions and mergers, which is inspired by the 4-cycle structure of both the hypercube Q d Q_{d} and the lattice ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} for large d d . Individuals have Poisson offspring distribution with mean 1 + p 1+p and certain deletions and mergers occur with probability q q ; these parameters correspond to the mean number of edges discovered from a given vertex in an exploration of a percolation cluster and to the probability that a non-backtracking path of length four closes a cycle, respectively. We prove survival and extinction under certain conditions on p p and q q that heuristically match the known expansions of the critical probabilities for bond percolation on the lattice ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} and the hypercube Q d Q_{d} . These expansions have been rigorously established by Hara and Slade in 1995, and van der Hofstad and Slade in 2006, respectively. We stress that our method does not constitute a branching process proof for the percolation threshold. The analysis of the graph process survival is considerably more challenging than for branching processes in discrete time, due to the interdependence between the descendants of different individuals in the same generation. In fact, it is left open whether the survival probability of 𝒢 ⁡ ( p , q ) \mathcal{G}(p,q) is monotone in p p or q q ; we discuss this and some other open problems regarding the new graph process.

Introduction

We propose an extension to inhomogeneous branching processes in discrete generations by defining a process
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
in which individuals may have more than one parent and the offspring distribution of each vertex depends on its genealogy which, in turn, is no longer represented by a tree but by a graph. Our main theorem gives sufficient conditions for extinction and survival of this graph process.

The motivation for the construction of
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
lies in the local, structural properties of percolation clusters on the hypercube
Q_{d}
and the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Briefly explained, under suitable choices of
p
and
q
, the graph process we propose approximates the exploration of a cluster in bond percolation, by taking into account only 4-cycles. The survival and extinction conditions we obtain shed some light on the influence the local structure of these graphs has on the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion of the critical probability for percolation. We postpone the discussion of the heuristic interpretation of our model to Section
2
.

1.2
Main result

Recall that branching processes in discrete generations (Galton-Watson processes) have the following survival threshold: the process survives with positive probability (resp. dies out almost surely) if the mean number of offspring is greater than (resp. less than or equal to) one (except for the special case in which each individual has exactly one offspring); see for example
[
2
]
.

In the case of the graph process
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
, we can define a notion of survival by saying that
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
dies out if there exists
n\geq 1
for which
Z_{n}=0
; otherwise we say that the process survives. It is easy to show that the generation sizes
Z_{n}
are stochastically dominated by the generation sizes of a Galton-Watson process with Poisson offspring distribution with mean
1+p
, and so if
p\leq 0
then
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
trivially dies out almost surely. Our main theorem concerns the survival threshold of
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist
C>0
and
p_{0}\in(0,1)
with
Cp_{0}<1
such that the graph process
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
with
0<p\leq p_{0}
exhibits (at least) two phases:

•

If
q<\frac{2}{5}p(1-Cp)
, then the process survives with positive probability:

{\mathbf{P}}\left(Z_{n}>0\text{ for all }n\geq 1\right)>0.

•

If
q>\frac{2}{5}p(1+Cp)
, then the process dies out almost surely:

{\mathbf{P}}\left(Z_{n}>0\text{ for all }n\geq 1\right)=0.

We do not attempt to obtain an optimal value for
C
, but we conjecture that in fact there is a critical value
q_{c}=q_{c}(p)

such that the process
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
survives with positive probability for
q<q_{c}
and dies out for
q>q_{c}
.
We also leave open the question of monotonicity of the survival probability in
p
and
q
(this monotonicity would imply the existence of such a critical

).
It seems reasonable to expect that the survival probability increases with
p
(since larger values of

correspond to larger numbers of offspring) and decreases with
q
(since a larger value of
q
corresponds to more deletions and mergers).
However, under the most natural coupling of the processes
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
and
\mathcal{G}(p^{\prime},q)
with
p>p^{\prime}
, survival of
\mathcal{G}(p^{\prime},q)
does not imply survival of
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
, and similarly, under the most natural coupling of the processes
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
and
\mathcal{G}(p,q^{\prime})
with
q^{\prime}>q
, survival of
\mathcal{G}(p,q^{\prime})
does not imply survival of
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
(see Figures
A.1
and
A.2
and the discussion in the Appendix).

Take
\Omega
a large positive integer and
\rho\in(0,1)
, and let
p(\rho):=(\Omega-1)\rho-1
and
q_{b}:=(\Omega-1)^{-2}
.
In Section
2.2
below, we will show heuristically that the process
\mathcal{G}(p(\rho),q_{b})
can be seen as a toy model of an exploration of a bond percolation cluster with edge probability

in the hypercube

(and the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\Omega/2}
, if

is even).
The following corollary, which is a direct consequence of Theorem

shows that the survival and extinction phases of

match the expansion in

of
the critical probability for percolation up to the first three coefficients
(see (
1
) in Section

We note in passing that it will become clear from the proof of Theorem

that the

Corollary 1.2
.

Take

as in Theorem
1.1
.
There exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that the following holds for
\Omega\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.
For any
\rho\in(0,(1+p_{0}\wedge(2C)^{-1})(\Omega-1)^{-1}]
, let
p(\rho):=(\Omega-1)\rho-1
and let

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{c}

\displaystyle:=(\Omega-1)^{-1}+\frac{5}{2}(\Omega-1)^{-3}.

Letting
q_{b}:=(\Omega-1)^{-2}
,

•

If
\rho>\hat{\rho}_{c}+C^{\prime}\Omega^{-5}
, then
\mathcal{G}(p(\rho),q_{b})
survives with positive probability.

•

If
\rho<\hat{\rho}_{c}-C^{\prime}\Omega^{-5}

A similar heuristic explanation for a threshold appeared in the case of the
k
-core in Erdős-Rényi random graphs; that is, the unique maximal subgraph with minimum degree

[
4
]
. The threshold for the appearance of the giant

Heuristic link to percolation in
Q_{d}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the hypercube
Q_{d}
and the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are graphs with vertex sets
\{0,1\}^{d}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, respectively. Their edge sets are given by nearest neighbours; that is,
\{u,v\}
is an edge if and only if

, where
|w|:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|w_{i}|
. In what follows we write
\mathbb{G}
to refer to either

or
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(for large values of
d
) and write
\Omega
for the number of neighbours per vertex; that is,
\Omega=d
if
\mathbb{G}=Q_{d}
and
\Omega=2d
if
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Results and heuristics are then given in terms of
\Omega
.

For a graph
G:=(V^{G},E^{G})
and
\rho\in[0,1]
, we define the bond percolation graph
G_{\rho}
as the subgraph of
G
in which each edge is present with probability
\rho
, independently from all the other edges.
One of the central objects of study in percolation theory is the critical probability
p_{c}(G)
, which is rigorously defined for bond percolation on infinite graphs
[
14
]
, and on (classes of) finite graphs; see e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
22
,
21
]
. For an infinite graph

p_{c}(G):=\inf\{\rho\in[0,1]:\exists\text{ an infinite connected component in }G_{\rho}\text{ a.s.}\}.

For finite graphs, we follow the definition in
[
8
]
and assume that
G
is connected and transitive. In this case, the critical probability consists of a critical window rather than a precise point. For
\lambda>0
let

p_{c}(G)=p_{c}(G,\lambda):=\inf\{\rho\in[0,1]:\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[|\mathcal{C}|]\geq\lambda|V^{G}|^{1/3}\},

where
|\mathcal{C}|
is the size of the connected component containing a fixed root vertex and

denotes expectation with respect to

. The choice of (small)

is rather flexible in the case

for large

[
19
]
.

The first three terms in the asymptotic expansion of

in terms of

are known, and rigorous bounds on the error terms have been proved. The critical probability is given by

p_{c}(\mathbb{G})=\Omega^{-1}+\Omega^{-2}+\frac{7}{2}\Omega^{-3}+O(\Omega^{-4})=(\Omega-1)^{-1}+\frac{5}{2}(\Omega-1)^{-3}+O(\Omega^{-4});

In the remainder of this section, we demonstrate the heuristic connection between our graph process

and an exploration process of a percolation cluster in the hypercube

2.1
Exploration of a percolation cluster

We first define an exploration algorithm that constructs a percolation cluster in a graph
G
, i.e. the connected component of
G_{\rho}
containing some root vertex.
Each time an edge is explored by the algorithm, the edge will be added to the percolation cluster with probability
\rho\in[0,1]
.
Unlike in the usual exploration algorithms such as Breadth-first search and Depth-first search,
where the exploration is performed by sequentially selecting a vertex from a given set and exploring its incident edges, instead we will explore all neighbouring unexplored edges at once, mimicking the generations of offspring in a branching process in discrete time.

For

1.

We construct

Heuristic link between exploration process and

In step 2 of the construction of

, note that a pair of edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}
‘merge’ (have a common end-vertex in
S_{n}
) if and only if there exist
\ell\geq 2
and a cycle
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{2\ell},e_{2\ell+1}=e_{1}
of edges with
e_{3},\ldots,e_{2\ell}\in E_{n-1}
.
As above, we ignore the ‘lower order’ effect of cycles of length greater than four, and use the fact that a non-backtracking random walk of length four on
\mathbb{G}
forms a cycle with probability
c_{b}
, to give us the heuristic that a pair of edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E_{n}\setminus E_{n-1}
from
u_{1},u_{2}\in S_{n-1}
merges only if the graph distance from
u_{1}
to
u_{2}
in
H_{n-1}
is two, in which case the edges merge with probability
q_{b}
. (We assume for our toy model that each pair of edges merges independently from other pairs, neglecting the effect of correlations.)

By comparing with the definition in Section
1.1
, this means that
\mathcal{G}(p(\rho),q_{b})
mimics the exploration process of a percolation cluster on
\mathbb{G}_{\rho}
by incorporating the effect of the local 4-cycle structure of
\mathbb{G}
.

2.3
Open questions

The process
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
is a generalisation of branching processes in which mergers may occur; the corresponding loss of the tree structure substantially increases the challenges in the analysis of the survival probability. In particular, establishing the monotonicity of the survival probability in
p
and
q
is left open (see the discussion after Theorem
1.1
).

As we saw in Section
2.2
,
the particular forms of the (inhomogeneous) offspring distribution and merger probabilities are tuned so that for suitable
p
and
q
, the process
\mathcal{G}(p,q)
is a toy model for the exploration of bond percolation clusters on both
Q_{d}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for large
d
. We expect that if the model
was modified to take into account the effect of
the cycle structure of
Q_{d}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
up to cycles of length
2k
, for some
k\geq 3
,
then Corollary
1.2
would still hold for such a model.

For integers
d,n\geq 1
, the Hamming graph
H(d,n)
has vertex set
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}^{d}
and two vertices
u
and
v
are adjacent if they differ in exactly one coordinate. In other words,
H(d,n)
is the Cartesian product of
d
complete graphs on
n
vertices; in particular
H(d,2)=Q_{d}
. The first three terms in the expansion of the critical percolation probability for Hamming graphs, as
n\to\infty
, are obtained in
[
12
]
; the first two terms were established in
[
9
]
. Similarly, the first three terms in the expansion of the critical probability for site percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as
d\to\infty
have been rigorously established in
[
15
]
.

It would be interesting to define a branching process with deletions and mergers (with adjusted offspring distribution and merge probabilities) that
i)
serves as a toy model of cluster exploration for Hamming graphs
H(d,n)
for large
n
(respectively, site percolation of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for large
d
) and
ii)
has a survival threshold that matches the known expansion of the critical probability.

we have that
vi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v^{\prime}i^{\prime}
if and only if

,

and

.
Hence

can be constructed from

using the collection of random variables in (

).
This completes the proof of the claim.

From the description of the construction above, and the construction of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(p,q)
in Section

, it is not difficult to verify that conditional on

,
\mathcal{G}^{u}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(p,q)
.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

Fix

; then define a sequence

of sets of renewed vertices iteratively as follows.
Let

; for

5
Merger stage and upper bounds

From now on, we assume
p,q\in[0,1]
.
In this section, we establish upper and lower bounds on
Z_{n}
, and then prove upper bounds on the expectations of some relevant quantities.
For
n\geq 2
, let
M_{n}
denote the number of mergers that occur when
I_{n}
is constructed from
\tilde{I}_{n}
, i.e. let

M_{n}:=|\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in\tilde{I}_{n},u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v\}|.

(30)

Recall the definition of
\tilde{J}^{(2)}_{n}
in (
24
).
For
n\geq 2
, let

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n}

\displaystyle:=|\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)},u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v\}|

(31)

\displaystyle\text{and }\quad\widetilde{L}_{n}

\displaystyle:=|\{(u,v,w)\in\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}\times\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}\times\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}:u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v,v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}w,u\neq w\}|.

(32)

For
n\geq 1
, let

Y_{n}:=\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}|\mathcal{C}_{u}|=|\tilde{I}_{n}|.

(33)

Lemma 5.1
.

For all
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n}-\widetilde{L}_{n}\leq Y_{n}-Z_{n}\leq M_{n}.

(34)

Proof.

For
u\in I_{n}
, recall from (
22
) that we let

\sigma(u):=\{v\in\tilde{I}_{n}:u\sim v\}
, the equivalence class of offspring that merges into the single individual
u
.
Let

M_{n,u}:=|\{\{v,v^{\prime}\}:v,v^{\prime}\in\sigma(u),v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v^{\prime}\}|,

the number of mergers within the equivalence class
\sigma(u)
.
Then for each
u\in I_{n}
, by the definition of the equivalence relation
\sim
in Section
4.1
we have
M_{n,u}\geq|\sigma(u)|-1
, and so

M_{n}=\sum_{u\in I_{n}}M_{n,u}\geq\sum_{u\in I_{n}}|\sigma(u)|-|I_{n}|=Y_{n}-Z_{n},

by the definition of
Y_{n}
in (
33
),
which establishes the second inequality in (
34
).

For the first inequality in (
34
), for
u\in I_{n}
, let

\widetilde{L}_{n,u}:=|\{(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}):v_{i}\in\sigma(u)\cap\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}\;\forall 1\leq i\leq 3,\,v_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v_{2},v_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v_{3},v_{1}\neq v_{3}\}|,

and let

\widetilde{M}_{n,u}:=|\{\{v,v^{\prime}\}:v,v^{\prime}\in\sigma(u)\cap\tilde{J}^{(2)}_{n},v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v^{\prime}\}|.

If
|\sigma(u)\cap\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}|=0
, then
\widetilde{M}_{n,u}-\widetilde{L}_{n,u}=0\leq|\sigma(u)|-1
.
Suppose instead that
|\sigma(u)\cap\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}|>0
.
Then consider the graph
G^{u}=(V^{u},E^{u})
, where

\displaystyle V^{u}

\displaystyle=\sigma(u)\cap\tilde{J}^{(2)}_{n}

\displaystyle\text{and }\quad E^{u}

\displaystyle=\{\{v,v^{\prime}\}:v,v^{\prime}\in V^{u},v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v^{\prime}\}.

Note that
G^{u}
contains exactly
\widetilde{L}_{n,u}
directed paths of length two.
It is a straightforward fact that a simple, non-empty graph with
t
vertices,
m
edges and
\ell
directed paths of length two satisfies
m-\ell<t
(see Lemma
A.1
in the Appendix). Thus,
\widetilde{M}_{n,u}-\widetilde{L}_{n,u}\leq|\sigma(u)\cap\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}|-1\leq|\sigma(u)|-1.

It follows that

\widetilde{M}_{n}-\widetilde{L}_{n}=\sum_{u\in I_{n}}(\widetilde{M}_{n,u}-\widetilde{L}_{n,u})\leq\sum_{u\in I_{n}}|\sigma(u)|-|I_{n}|=Y_{n}-Z_{n},

as required.
∎

Note that we could have proved by the same argument that
Y_{n}-Z_{n}\geq M_{n}-L_{n}
, where

L_{n}:=|\{(u,v,w)\in\tilde{I}_{n}\times\tilde{I}_{n}\times\tilde{I}_{n}:u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v,v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}w,u\neq w\}|,
but it will turn out that proving a suitable lower bound on
{\mathbf{E}}\left[\widetilde{M}_{n}-\widetilde{L}_{n}\right]
is easier than proving such a bound on
{\mathbf{E}}\left[M_{n}-L_{n}\right]
.

5.1
Preliminary results

Let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}
be non-negative integers. For
n\geq 0
and a vertex
u\in I_{n}
, let

\mathcal{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u):=\{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\in\prod_{i=1}^{k}I_{n+j_{i}}:\,u\in\alpha(v_{i}),\alpha(v_{i})\cap\alpha(v_{i^{\prime}})\subseteq\cup_{m\leq n}I_{m}\text{ for all }i\neq i^{\prime}\},

(35)

the set of ordered
k
-tuples of vertices
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
with
v_{i}\in I_{n+j_{i}}
for each
i
which do not have any pairwise ancestors more recently than their common ancestor
u
.
Let

\displaystyle N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u):=|\mathcal{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)|\quad\text{and}\quad N_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}:=\sum_{u\in I_{n}}N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u).

(36)

The following simple upper bound on the expectation of
N_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}
will be used several times in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}
be non-negative integers. For any
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[N_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}\right]\leq(1+p)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n}\right].

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
j_{1}\geq\ldots\geq j_{k}\geq 0.

Note first that it suffices to prove the uniform bound

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\leq(1+p)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}}

(37)

for any
n\geq 0
and
u\in I_{n}
.
Indeed, assuming (
37
),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}_{n}\right]={\mathbf{E}}\left[{\mathbf{E}}\left[N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}_{n}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+p)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n}\right],

as required.
We will prove (
37
) by induction on
j_{1}=\max_{\ell}\{j_{\ell}\}
.

We begin by considering the case
j_{k}=0
; note that by the definition of
\mathcal{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)
in (
35
), in this case we have

N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)=N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k-1})}(u),

and
N^{(0)}(u)=1
.
Hence from now on we may assume that
j_{k}\geq 1
.

Let us now consider the case
j_{1}=j_{k}=1
, where
N^{(1,\ldots,1)}(u)
counts the number of ordered
k
-tuples of distinct offspring of
u
.
By (
18
) and (
19
), the number of offspring of
u
is

|I^{u}_{1}|\leq\xi_{u}.

Since
\xi_{u}
has Poisson distribution with mean
1+p
, and
\xi_{u}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.N^{(1,\ldots,1)}(u)\right|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[(\xi_{u})_{k}\right]=(1+p)^{k}.

(Here, we write
(x)_{k}:=x(x-1)\ldots(x-k+1)
for the
k
th falling factorial.)

For the induction step, suppose that
j_{1}\geq 2
and let
\ell:=\max\{i:\,j_{i}=j_{1}\}
. Set
j^{\prime}_{i}:=j_{i}-{\mathbf{1}}_{[i\leq\ell]}\geq 1
for
i\leq k
.
Then for each
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\in\mathcal{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)
,
there exists

(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\mathcal{N}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)

such that
w_{i}=v_{i}

\forall i>\ell
and
w_{i}\in\alpha_{1}(v_{i})

\forall i\leq\ell
.
Moreover, for each
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\mathcal{N}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)
,
by (
19
) again,

|\{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\in\mathcal{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u):w_{i}=v_{i}\;\forall i>\ell,w_{i}\in\alpha_{1}(v_{i})\;\forall i\leq\ell\}|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi_{w_{i}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)\right|\mathcal{F}_{n+j_{1}-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\mathcal{N}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi_{w_{i}}\right|\mathcal{F}_{n+j_{1}-1}\right]

\displaystyle=N^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)(1+p)^{\ell},

since
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in I_{n+j_{1}-1}
are distinct for each
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\mathcal{N}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)
.
By taking expectations, this establishes the induction step for (
37
).
∎

We now define a similar quantity to
N_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}
which will also be used several times later in the paper.
Let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}
be non-negative integers. For
n\geq 0
and a vertex
u\in I_{n}
, let

\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u):=\{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\in\prod_{i=1}^{k}I_{n+j_{i}}:\,u\in\alpha(v_{i})\text{ for all }i\},

(38)

the set of ordered
k
-tuples of vertices
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
with
v_{i}\in I_{n+j_{i}}
for each
i
and with common ancestor
u
.
Let

\displaystyle\overline{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u):=|\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)|\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{N}_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}:=\sum_{u\in I_{n}}\overline{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u).

(39)

We can prove a similar upper bound to Lemma
5.2
for the expectation of
\overline{N}_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}
be non-negative integers with
m=\max_{i}\{j_{i}\}
. Let
\xi
be a Poisson random variable with mean
1+p
. For any
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\overline{N}_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{k}\right]^{m}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n}\right].

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
j_{1}\geq\ldots\geq j_{k}\geq 0.

By the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
, it suffices to prove the uniform bound

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\overline{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{k}\right]^{m}

(40)

for any
n\geq 0
and
u\in I_{n}
.
We will prove (
40
) by induction on
j_{1}=\max_{\ell}\{j_{\ell}\}
.

In the case
j_{k}=0
, by the definition of
\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)
in (
38
), we have

\overline{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)=\overline{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k-1})}(u),

and
\overline{N}^{(0)}(u)=1
.
From now on we will assume that
j_{k}\geq 1
.

We now consider the case
j_{1}=j_{k}=1
, where
\overline{N}^{(1,\ldots,1)}(u)
counts the number of ordered
k
-tuples of (not necessarily distinct) offspring of
u
.
By (
18
) and (
19
),
the number of offspring of
u
is

|I^{u}_{1}|\leq\xi_{u},

and, conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
,
\xi_{u}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\xi
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\overline{N}^{(1,\ldots,1)}(u)\right|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.(\xi_{u})^{k}\right|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]={\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{k}\right].

As in the proof of Lemma
5.2
,
for the induction step, suppose that
j_{1}\geq 2
and let
\ell=\max\{i:\,j_{i}=j_{1}\}
. Set
j^{\prime}_{i}=j_{i}-{\mathbf{1}}_{[i\leq\ell]}\geq 1
for
i\leq k
.
Then for each
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)
,
there exists

(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)

such that
w_{i}=v_{i}

\forall i>\ell
and
w_{i}\in\alpha_{1}(v_{i})

\forall i\leq\ell
.
Moreover, for each
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)
, by (
19
),

|\{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u):w_{i}=v_{i}\;\forall i>\ell,w_{i}\in\alpha_{1}(v_{i})\;\forall i\leq\ell\}|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi_{w_{i}}.

Since
j_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,j_{k}^{\prime}\leq j_{1}-1
, it follows that

{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\overline{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(u)\right|\mathcal{F}_{n+j_{1}-1}\right]\leq\sum_{(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi_{w_{i}}\right|\mathcal{F}_{n+j_{1}-1}\right].

(41)

(Note that for
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{(j^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{k})}(u)
,

need not be distinct.) By Jensen’s inequality, we have

{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{\ell^{\prime}}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{\ell}\right]^{\ell^{\prime}/\ell}\;\text{for each }\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell,

and therefore, for

,

{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi_{w_{i}}\right|\mathcal{F}_{n+j_{1}-1}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{\ell}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{k}\right]^{\ell/k}\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{k}\right],

(42)

where the second inequality follows by Jensen’s inequality and since
\ell\leq k
, and the last inequality follows since
{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{k}\right]\geq(1+p)^{k}\geq 1
.
By substituting into (
41
) and taking expectations, this establishes the induction step for (
40
).
∎

5.2
Upper bounds

We now use the results in Section
5.1
to prove some useful upper bounds on expectations of the random variables in Lemma
5.1
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[M_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q}{2}(1+p)^{4}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right]

(43)

\displaystyle\text{and }\quad{\mathbf{E}}\left[\widetilde{L}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4q^{2}(1+p)^{6}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right].

(44)

Proof.

By the definition of
M_{n}
in (
30
) and our construction in Section
4.1
, and then since
{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\mu_{\{u,v\}}\right|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\right]=q
for each
u\neq v\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[M_{n}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in\tilde{I}_{n},d_{\tilde{G}_{n}}(u,v)=4\}}\mu_{\{u,v\}}\right]

\displaystyle=q{\mathbf{E}}\left[|\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in\tilde{I}_{n},d_{\tilde{G}_{n}}(u,v)=4\}|\right].

By the construction of
\tilde{G}_{n}
in Section
4.1
(and recalling the definition of
\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}
in (
23
)), it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[M_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=q{\mathbf{E}}\left[|\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in\tilde{I}_{n},u|_{n-1}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v|_{n-1}\}|\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}q{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in I_{n-1}\times I_{n-1}:u^{\prime}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}|\mathcal{C}_{u^{\prime}}||\mathcal{C}_{v^{\prime}}|\right].

Then since for each
u\in I_{n-1}
,
|\mathcal{C}_{u}|\leq\xi_{u}
, and since
(\xi_{u})_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{n-1}}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
,
it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[M_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}q{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in I_{n-1}\times I_{n-1}:u^{\prime}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi_{u^{\prime}}\xi_{v^{\prime}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}q(1+p)^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[|\{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in I_{n-1}\times I_{n-1}:u^{\prime}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}q(1+p)^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[N^{(1,1)}_{n-2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}q(1+p)^{4}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right],

where the third line follows by the definition of
N_{n}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}
in (
36
), and the last inequality follows
by Lemma
5.2
.

Now recall the definitions of
\widetilde{L}_{n}
in (
32
) and
\tilde{J}^{(2)}_{n}
in (
24
), and note that for each triple
(u,v,w)\in\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}\times\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}\times\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}
with
u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}v
and
v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}w
,
we must have
u|_{n-1}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v|_{n-1}
,
v|_{n-1}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}w|_{n-1}
, and
\mu_{\{u,v\}}=1=\mu_{\{v,w\}}
. Since each of
u,v
and
w
has exactly one grandparent, we must have
|\alpha_{1}(u|_{n-1})|=1
and

\alpha_{1}(u|_{n-1})=\alpha_{1}(v|_{n-1})=\alpha_{1}(w|_{n-1})
.
Therefore, since conditional on
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
,
(\mu_{\{u_{1},u_{2}\}})_{u_{1}\neq u_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{n}}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables with mean
q
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\widetilde{L}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[|\{(u,v,w)\in\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}\times\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}\times\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}:u|_{n-1}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v|_{n-1},v|_{n-1}\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}w|_{n-1}\}|\right]

\displaystyle=q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}:\alpha_{1}(u^{\prime})=\alpha_{1}(v^{\prime})=\alpha_{1}(w^{\prime}),u^{\prime}\neq v^{\prime},v^{\prime}\neq w^{\prime}\}}|\mathcal{C}_{u^{\prime}}||\mathcal{C}_{v^{\prime}}||\mathcal{C}_{w^{\prime}}|\right].

(45)

Note that for
u^{\prime},v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in I_{n-1}
distinct,

{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.|\mathcal{C}_{u^{\prime}}||\mathcal{C}_{v^{\prime}}||\mathcal{C}_{w^{\prime}}|\right|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\xi_{u^{\prime}}\xi_{v^{\prime}}\xi_{w^{\prime}}\right|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]=(1+p)^{3},

and for
u^{\prime}\neq v^{\prime}\in I_{n-1}
,

{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.|\mathcal{C}_{u^{\prime}}|^{2}|\mathcal{C}_{v^{\prime}}|\right|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.(\xi_{u^{\prime}})^{2}\xi_{v^{\prime}}\right|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]=(1+p)^{2}(2+p).

Hence by conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
in (
5.2
) and splitting the sum according to whether or not we have
u^{\prime}=w^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\widetilde{L}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-3}\right]

(54)

\displaystyle\text{and }\quad{\mathbf{E}}\left[D_{n}^{s}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-3}\right].

(55)

Proof.

A_{2}\subseteq I_{n-3}
denote the set of individuals such that at least one pair of descendants in
\tilde{I}_{n}
and at least one pair of descendants in
\tilde{I}_{n-1}

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in I_{n-3}:\exists\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{v}_{2}\in\tilde{I}_{n},\tilde{w}_{1},\tilde{w}_{2}\in\tilde{I}_{n-1}\text{ s.t.~}u\in\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{v}_{1})\cap\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{v}_{2})\cap\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{w}_{1})\cap\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{w}_{2}),

\displaystyle\hskip 327.20668pt\tilde{v}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{w}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{w}_{2}\}.

By Lemma
6.2
, if
v\in I_{n}
has a distant relative (i.e. if

.

in (

\displaystyle D_{n}=\sum_{v\in I_{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{[v\in\mathcal{D}_{n}]}\leq\sum_{u\in I_{n-3}}|I^{u}_{3}|{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{1}\cup A_{2}]}.

(56)

Moreover, for a pair of siblings
v,v^{\prime}\in I_{n}
with

and

,

, and since

\displaystyle D_{n}^{s}=\sum_{v,v^{\prime}\in I_{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{[v\in\mathcal{D}_{n}\cap J_{n}^{(1)}]}{\mathbf{1}}_{[v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}]}\leq\sum_{u\in I_{n-3}}|I^{u}_{3}|(|I^{u}_{3}|-1){\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{1}\cup A_{2}]}.

(57)

We will now bound the expectation of

, conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{n-3}

.

\displaystyle\leq|\tilde{I}^{u}_{3}|^{2}\sum_{\tilde{v}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{v}_{4}\in\tilde{I}^{u}_{3},\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{v}_{4}\text{ distinct}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.{\mathbf{1}}_{[\tilde{v}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}_{2},\tilde{v}_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}_{4}]}\right|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\right]

(58)

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[|I^{u}_{3}|^{2}{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{1}]}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]

\xi

(60)

Let
A^{\prime}_{2}\subseteq I_{n-3}
denote the set of individuals such that at least one pair of descendants in
\tilde{I}_{n-1}
merge, i.e.

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in I_{n-3}:\exists\tilde{w}_{1},\tilde{w}_{2}\in\tilde{I}_{n-1}\text{ s.t.~}u\in\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{w}_{1})\cap\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{w}_{2}),\tilde{w}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{w}_{2}\}.

A_{2}

Hence by conditioning on
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
, and since
|I^{u}_{3}|\leq|\tilde{I}^{u}_{3}|
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[|I^{u}_{3}|^{2}{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{2}]}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]

by Jensen’s inequality and the same argument as in (

\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
, and since

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[|I^{u}_{3}|^{2}{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{2}]}\Big|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq q{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{4}\right]|\tilde{I}^{u}_{2}|^{4}\sum_{\tilde{w}_{1}\neq\tilde{w}_{2}\in\tilde{I}^{u}_{2}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.{\mathbf{1}}_{[\tilde{w}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{w}_{2}]}\right|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{4}\right]|\tilde{I}^{u}_{2}|^{6}.

Therefore by conditioning on
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[|I^{u}_{3}|^{2}{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{2}]}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{4}\right]\sum_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{6}\in I^{u}_{1}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left.\prod_{i=1}^{6}|\mathcal{C}_{x_{i}}|\right|\mathcal{F}_{n-2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{4}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{6}\right]|I^{u}_{1}|^{6}

)).
Hence by (

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[|I^{u}_{3}|^{2}{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{1}\cup A_{2}]}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-3}\right]

\displaystyle\leq q^{2}\Big({\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{6}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[|I^{u}_{2}|^{6}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-3}\right]+{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{4}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{6}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[|I^{u}_{1}|^{6}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-3}\right]\Big)

\displaystyle=q^{2}\Big({\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{6}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\overline{N}^{(2,2,2,2,2,2)}(u)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-3}\right]+{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{4}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{6}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\overline{N}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1)}(u)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-3}\right]\Big),

by the definition of
\overline{N}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}(w)
in (

).
Therefore, summing over

,

{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n-3}}|I^{u}_{3}|^{2}{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in A_{1}\cup A_{2}]}\right]\leq q^{2}\Big({\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{6}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\overline{N}_{n-3}^{(2,2,2,2,2,2)}\right]+{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{4}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi^{6}\right]{\mathbf{E}}\left[\overline{N}_{n-3}^{(1,1,1,1,1,1)}\right]\Big),

and the result follows by (
56
), (
57
) and Lemma

.

The following decomposition and lower bound will be used several times.
Recall (

)-(

k_{u}=k^{(g)}_{u}+k^{(a)}_{u}+k^{(r)}_{u}

For any

,
u\in I_{n}
and
c\in\mathbb{N}
, using that

for

\displaystyle(1-q)^{ck_{u}}

\displaystyle=(1-q)^{c(k_{u}^{(g)}+k_{u}^{(a)})}-(1-q)^{c(k_{u}^{(g)}+k_{u}^{(a)})}\left(1-(1-q)^{ck_{u}^{(r)}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-qc(k_{u}^{(g)}+k_{u}^{(a)})-{\mathbf{1}}_{[k_{u}^{(r)}\neq 0]}

\displaystyle=1-qc(k_{u}^{(g)}+k_{u}^{(a)})-{\mathbf{1}}_{[u\in\mathcal{D}_{n}]}

(61)

by the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
in (

For
n\geq 1
, let

(62)

the number of ordered pairs of siblings in the

In order to prove a lower bound on

in Lemma

below, we need a lower bound on

.

Lemma 7.1
.

There exists
C_{4}>0
such that for
p,q\in[0,1]
and
n\geq 4
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[S_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1+p)^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-1}\right]-C_{4}q\max_{2\leq k\leq 4}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-k}\right].

(63)

Proof.

Note first that by our construction in Section
4.1
, for
u\in I_{n-1}
and
i\neq i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{u}
, the pair
(ui,ui^{\prime})
contributes to
S_{n}
unless there exists
\tilde{v}\in\tilde{I}_{n}
such that
ui\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}
or
ui^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}
.
Hence

S_{n}\geq\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}|\mathcal{C}_{u}|(|\mathcal{C}_{u}|-1)-\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}2|\{(i,i^{\prime},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{C}_{u}\times\mathcal{C}_{u}\times\tilde{I}_{n}:i\neq i^{\prime},ui\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}\}|.

(64)

We now need an upper bound on the second term on the right hand side.
For
u\in I_{n-1}
and
i,i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{u}
,
\tilde{v}\in\tilde{I}_{n}
, if
ui\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}
then we must have
\alpha_{1}(u)\cap\tilde{\alpha}_{2}(\tilde{v})\neq\emptyset
and
u\notin\tilde{\alpha}_{1}(\tilde{v})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}|\{(i,i^{\prime},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{C}_{u}\times\mathcal{C}_{u}\times\tilde{I}_{n}:i\neq i^{\prime},ui\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w\in I_{n-2}}\sum_{u\neq u^{\prime}\in I^{w}_{1}}\sum_{i\neq i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{u},\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{C}_{u^{\prime}}}\mu_{\{ui,\tilde{v}\}}.

Therefore, by conditioning first on
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
and then on
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
, and since, for
u\neq u^{\prime}\in I_{n-1}
, we have

{\mathbf{E}}\left[|\mathcal{C}_{u}|(|\mathcal{C}_{u}|-1)|\mathcal{C}_{u^{\prime}}||\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]\leq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\xi_{u}(\xi_{u}-1)\xi_{u^{\prime}}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]=(1+p)^{3}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}|\{(i,i^{\prime},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{C}_{u}\times\mathcal{C}_{u}\times\tilde{I}_{n}:i\neq i^{\prime},ui\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m}}{{\sim}}\tilde{v}\}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq q(1+p)^{3}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{w\in I_{n-2}}|I^{w}_{1}|(|I^{w}_{1}|-1)\right]

\displaystyle=q(1+p)^{3}{\mathbf{E}}\left[N^{(1,1)}_{n-2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq q(1+p)^{5}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right],

where the second line follows by the definition of
N^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})}_{n}
in (
36
), and the last line follows by Lemma
5.2
.

Recall from our construction in Section
4.1
that conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
, for
u\in I_{n-1}
,
|\mathcal{C}_{u}|
is Poisson distributed with mean
(1+p)(1-q)^{k_{u}}
. It follows from (
64
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[S_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[|\mathcal{C}_{u}|(|\mathcal{C}_{u}|-1)|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]\right]-2q(1+p)^{5}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1+p)^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}(1-q)^{2k_{u}}\right]-64q{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right]

since
p\leq 1
.
Therefore it suffices to show that there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}(1-q)^{2k_{u}}\right]\geq{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-1}\right]-C^{\prime}q\max_{2\leq k\leq 4}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-k}\right].

(65)

Since
|I_{n-1}|=Z_{n-1}
and
|\mathcal{D}_{n-1}|=D_{n-1}
, we obtain from (
61
) with
c=2
that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}(1-q)^{2k_{u}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-1}\right]-{\mathbf{E}}\left[D_{n-1}\right]-2q{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{u\in I_{n-1}}(k_{u}^{(a)}+k_{u}^{(g)})\right]

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-1}\right]-C_{3}q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-4}\right]-2q(1+p)^{3}({\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-3}\right]+{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-4}\right]),

where in the last inequality we use (
54
) from Lemma
6.3
and (
49
) from Lemma
5.5
(using that

); this establishes (
65
) and completes the proof.
∎

below.

Lemma 7.2
.

There exists
C_{5}>0
such that for
p,q\in[0,1]
and

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\widetilde{M}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q}{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right]-C_{5}q^{2}\max_{3\leq k\leq 5}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-k}\right]

(66)

\displaystyle\text{and }\quad{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(v,v^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}\times J^{(1)}_{n-1}:v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}|\mathcal{C}_{v}|\right]

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right]-C_{5}q\max_{3\leq k\leq 5}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-k}\right].

(67)

Proof.

Recall from (
31
) that
\widetilde{M}_{n}
counts the number of (unordered) pairs
\{vi,v^{\prime}i^{\prime}\}\subseteq\tilde{J}_{n}^{(2)}
such that

{\mathbf{E}}\left[\widetilde{M}_{n}\right]=\frac{q}{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(v,v^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}\times J^{(1)}_{n-1}:v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}|\mathcal{C}_{v}|\cdot|\mathcal{C}_{v^{\prime}}|\right].

4.1
that conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(v,v^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}\times J^{(1)}_{n-1}:v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}|\mathcal{C}_{v}|\right]=(1+p){\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(v,v^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}\times J^{(1)}_{n-1}:v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}(1-q)^{k_{v}}\right].

\displaystyle\sum_{\{(v,v^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}\times J^{(1)}_{n-1}:v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}(1-q)^{mk_{v}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\{(v,v^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}\times J^{(1)}_{n-1}:v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}(1-mq(k_{v}^{(g)}+k_{v}^{(a)})-{\mathbf{1}}_{[v\in\mathcal{D}_{n-1}]})

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{\{(v,v^{\prime})\in J^{(1)}_{n-1}\times J^{(1)}_{n-1}:v\overset{\mathrm{s}}{\sim}v^{\prime}\}}(1-q)^{mk_{v}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\ (1+p)^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-2}\right]-C_{4}q\max_{3\leq k\leq 5}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-k}\right]-mq(1+p)^{4}({\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-3}\right]+{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-4}\right])-C_{3}q^{2}{\mathbf{E}}\left[Z_{n-4}\right]

By substituting into (

), this completes the proof.

We can now use (

Lemma 7.3
.

There exists
C_{6}>0
such that for
p,q\in[0,1]
and

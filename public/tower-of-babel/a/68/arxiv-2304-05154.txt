Quantum detection of millimeter wave electric fields with driving surface-state electrons
Miao Zhang, Y. F. Wang, X. Y. Peng, X. N. Feng, S. R. He, Y. F. Li, L. F. Wei

Abstract
We introduce a spin-based receiver to sensitively detect the electric fields of millimeter (mm) waves by using quantum interferometric approach. The proposed quantum sensor consists of many surface-state electrons trapped individually on liquid helium by an electrode-network at the bottom of the liquid helium film. A dc-current in this chip is biased to generate a strong spin-orbit coupling of each of the trapped electrons. The mm wave signals are conducted to non-dissipatedly drive the orbital motions of the trapped electrons and result in the Stark shifts of the spin-orbit dressed states of the electrons. As a consequence, the electric fields of the conducted mm waves could be detected sensitively by using the Hahn echo interferometry with the long-lived spin states of the electrons trapped on liquid helium.

\displaystyle=\Omega_{sz\uparrow}-\Omega_{sz\downarrow}=\frac{2\Omega_{12}^{2}\Delta_{s}}{\Delta_{a}^{2}-\Delta_{s}^{2}}\,.

(18)

This is nothing but the ac-Stark effect of spin generated by the electric dipole interaction between the mm wave and the 1D hydrogen-like atom.

IV.3
The sensitivity of mm wave electric field detection

It is emphasized that, as the Rabi frequency
\Omega_{12}
is required to be comparable to the orbital decay rate, the above orbital excitation of surface-state electron is suitable to detect the spin state, instead of the very weak mm wave directly. Given the coherent time of the spin qubit is generically longer than that of atomic qubit, we use the spin ac-Stark effect (
17

Firstly, Eq. (

\displaystyle P=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\Gamma t}\cos(\Omega_{sz}t+\theta_{0})\,,

due to the practical existing interference damping, i.e.,

For the spin qubit of trapped electron on liquid helium, the dephasing is the major factor resulting in decoherence

Schuster2010

Secondly, supposing the mm wave is very weak, i.e.,

\displaystyle P\approx\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\Gamma t}\Omega_{sz}t\,,

According to Eq. (

\displaystyle I_{0}(\omega)=Nq_{0}\omega\rho_{22}(\omega)\,.

As an experimentally detectable quantity, signal (

to denote such a noise and letting

\displaystyle\left[\Omega_{sz}\right]_{\text{min}}\approx\left(\frac{2e^{\Gamma t}}{t}\right)_{\text{min}}\frac{\tilde{I}(\omega)}{I_{0}(\omega)}=\frac{2\mathcal{N}}{t_{c}}\,.

This means that, to improve the detecting sensitivity, one can use a relatively weak spin-orbit coupling during the stage of Hahn echo sequence, and consequently enhance the spin-orbit coupling for implementing the efficient detection for spins.
The operations would be complex in practical experiments, as the adjustable bias-current is required. We hope this is not a fundamental problem.

V
Conclusion

In summary, we have introduced a conceptually new approach to implement the sensitive detection of mm wave electric field by using the spin ac-Stark effect of trapped electrons on liquid helium. To achieve the proposal, we designed a circuit chip to trap multiple electrons individually on superfluid helium film, and then, a dc-current is loaded very close to every electron for generating the spin qubit and also the strong spin-orbit coupling for each of the trapped electrons.
The proposal is based on the fact that the transition frequency between two orbital states of the surface-binding electron is in the mm wave band. Therefore, with the above spin-orbit coupling, the mm wave electric field can affect spin effectively, for example, results in its ac-Stark shift. During the driving for this effect, the orbital state of trapped electron is not excited by the mm wave signal.
Therefore, without the decoherence of orbital qubit, the mm wave electric field could be precisely measured by using the spin echo interferometry.

Practical experiments for implementing this interferometry would be complex, as the bias-current (for generating spin qubits) and the RF pulses (for manipulating qubits) are required to be precise enough. Given the fundamental limitations such as Johnson noise and the qubit decoherence are weaker than that within the usual systems, the present work showed still a promising platform for mm wave sensitive detection.
Hopefully, the presented circuit chip and ac-Stark effect as well as the spins detection may be also feasible for the system of electrons on solid neon

Schuster2023
;

\textbf{B}_{w}=(\textbf{e}_{x}\times\textbf{E}_{w})/c
. As usual,
E_{w}
,
\omega_{T}

k_{T}

are the amplitude, frequency, wave number, and the propagating speed of the mm wave, respectively. The electromagnetic potentials of the mm wave are described by the fundamental equations
\textbf{E}_{w}=-\nabla\phi_{w}-\partial_{t}\textbf{A}_{w}
and
\textbf{B}_{w}=\nabla\times\textbf{A}_{w}

\phi_{w}=0

\textbf{A}_{w}=-(E_{w}\textbf{e}_{z}/\omega_{T})\sin(\omega_{T}t-k_{T}x)

Using the presented solution of
\textbf{A}_{w}

to Schrödinger equation, then the Pauli Hamiltonian (

\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{g}^{\dagger}\hat{H}_{\rm PL}\hat{U}_{g}-i\hbar\hat{U}_{g}^{\dagger}\partial_{t}\hat{U}_{g}

(A1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\hat{\textbf{p}}-e\textbf{A}^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{e}}+e\phi+e\textbf{r}\cdot\partial_{t}\textbf{A}_{w}+u_{b}\hat{\textbf{S}}\cdot\textbf{B}\,,

with

Note that,
\textbf{A}_{0}
is the vector potential (
6

\phi=\Phi_{0}+\phi_{w}
(with
\phi_{w}=0

Also, we note that the relation
\hat{U}_{g}^{\dagger}\hat{\textbf{p}}\hat{U}_{g}=\hat{\textbf{p}}+e\textbf{A}_{w}+ez(\textbf{e}_{z}\times\textbf{B}_{w})
is used to exactly derive the second line in Eq. (

Neglecting the

-related terms can significantly simply the above Hamiltonian. This is a reasonable approximation, and called usually electric dipole approximation. Formally, Hamiltonian (

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}_{0}-e\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{E}_{w}+u_{b}\hat{\textbf{S}}\cdot\textbf{B}+\hat{H}_{A^{\prime}}\,,

(A2)

the Hamiltonian relating to

6

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{A^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{eB_{0}z\hat{p}_{y}}{m_{e}}+\frac{e|\textbf{B}_{w}|z\hat{p}_{x}}{m_{e}}+\mathcal{O}(h_{0}^{-1})+\mathcal{O}(e^{2})\,.

(A3)

Indeed, all the couplings presented in

For the similar reason, we can write the term related to the electronic spin as

APPENDIX B: THE SPIN-BOSON MODEL USED TO ESTIMATE COHERENCE TIMES

In this proposal, a
y
-directional dc current
I_{\rm dc}
beneath the liquid helium surface of depth
h_{0}
is used to generate the desired spin-orbit coupling of a trapped electron.
Meanwhile, the current causes also decoherence into the spin superposition state due to the current fluctuation
\delta I
and the height fluctuation
\delta h
(capillary wave) of helium surface. With such fluctuations, the magnetic field
\textbf{B}_{0}
in Eq. (
5
) is
corrected as

\displaystyle\textbf{B}_{\text{c}}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{0}(I_{\rm dc}+\delta I)}{2\pi(h_{0}+\delta h)}\left[\left(1-\frac{z}{h_{0}+\delta h}\right)\textbf{e}_{x}-\frac{x}{h_{0}+\delta h}\textbf{e}_{z}\right]

(B1)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\textbf{B}_{0}+B_{0}\left(\frac{\delta I}{I_{\rm dc}}-\frac{\delta h}{h_{0}}\right)\textbf{e}_{x}\,,

with
B_{0}=\mu_{0}I_{\rm dc}/(2\pi h_{0})
, and where the high orders of small quantities, such as
z\delta h/h_{0}^{2}
and
x\delta h/h_{0}^{2}
, are neglected.
Therefore, the noise reduces to the famous spin-boson model
Oxford1996
;
PRA1998
;
book2007
;
PRA2024

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm sb}=\hbar\omega_{0}\left(\frac{\delta I}{I_{\rm dc}}-\frac{\delta h}{h_{0}}\right)\hat{S}_{z}\,.

(B2)

Here,
\omega_{0}=\omega_{s}/2=\mu_{b}B_{0}/\hbar
and
\omega_{0}/I_{\rm dc}=\mu_{0}\mu_{b}/(2\pi h_{0}\hbar)
, with
\omega_{s}
being the transition frequency between two spin-states used in Eq. (
10
).

The spin-boson model explains decoherence by the following equation,

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\phi_{I}(t)}\rangle\langle e^{i\phi_{h}(t)}\rangle=e^{-\Gamma(t)}\,,

(B3)

with

\displaystyle i\phi_{I}(t)=i\frac{\omega_{0}}{I_{\rm dc}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\delta I{\rm d}t-\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\delta I{\rm d}t\right)\,,

(B4)

and

\displaystyle i\phi_{h}(t)=i\frac{\omega_{0}}{h_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\delta h{\rm d}t-\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\delta h{\rm d}t\right)\,,

(B5)

due to the unitary evolution of Hamiltonian (
B2
) before any measurements performed to the system. Above,

and
T_{2}=2t
are the times of the two-stage “free evolution” in the Hahn echo interferometry.

V.1
The spin-boson model for Johnson-Nyquist noises

Using the second quantization approach, the 1D current can be written as

\displaystyle\delta I\approx i\sum_{k}I_{k}[\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}e^{i(\omega_{k}t-ky)}-\hat{b}_{k}e^{-i(\omega_{k}t-ky)}]\,,

(B6)

and
\omega_{k}=|k|/\sqrt{l_{0}c_{0}}
being the amplitude and the frequency of
k
-th boson mode, respectively. Note that,

, and
\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{b}_{k}
are the corresponding creation and annihilation operators.
l_{0}
is the inductance per unit length,

is the capacitance per unit length, and

a temporary length of the transmission line

;
RMP-circuit

As a consequence, the current-related Eq. (
B4

\displaystyle i\phi_{I}(t)=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{k}^{*}\hat{b}_{k}\,,

(B7)

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\frac{i\omega_{0}I_{k}e^{-iky}}{I_{\rm dc}\omega_{k}}\left[\left(e^{i\omega_{k}t}-1\right)-\left(e^{i2\omega_{k}t}-e^{i\omega_{k}t}\right)\right]\,.

(B8)

The spin-boson model
Oxford1996
;
PRA1998
;
book2007
;
PRA2024
says

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\phi_{I}(t)}\rangle=\sum_{k}\langle e^{\alpha_{k}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{k}^{*}\hat{b}_{k}}\rangle=e^{-\Gamma_{I}}\,,

(B9)

with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{I}=\sum_{k}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}[\bar{n}(\omega_{k})+\frac{1}{2}]\,,

(B10)

and
\bar{n}(\omega_{k})=\langle\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}\rangle=[\exp(\hbar\omega_{k}/k_{b}T)-1]^{-1}
.

According to Eq. (
B8
), we have

\displaystyle|\alpha_{k}|^{2}=\frac{4\omega_{0}^{2}I_{k}^{2}}{I_{\rm dc}^{2}}\left[\frac{\sin^{2}(\omega_{k}t/2)}{(\omega_{k}/2)^{2}}-\frac{\sin^{2}(\omega_{k}t)}{\omega_{k}^{2}}\right]\,,

(B11)

and therefore,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{I}=\frac{4\omega_{0}^{2}}{I_{\rm dc}^{2}}\sum_{k}I_{k}^{2}[\bar{n}(\omega_{k})+\frac{1}{2}]Y(\omega_{k}t)\,,

(B12)

with the time dependent function

\displaystyle Y(\omega_{k}t)=\frac{\sin^{2}(\omega_{k}t/2)}{(\omega_{k}/2)^{2}}-\frac{\sin^{2}(\omega_{k}t)}{\omega_{k}^{2}}\,.

(B13)

Now, we rewrite the quadratic amplitude as
I_{k}^{2}=\hbar\omega_{k}\omega_{\text{min}}/(2\pi R_{0})
, with
\omega_{\text{min}}=2\pi/(L\sqrt{l_{0}c_{0}})
and the impedance
R_{0}=\sqrt{l_{0}/c_{0}}
. As a consequence, for

B12

\displaystyle\Gamma_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\omega_{0}^{2}}{\pi I_{\rm dc}^{2}R_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hbar\omega[\bar{n}(\omega)+\frac{1}{2}]Y(\omega t){\text{d}}\omega\,.

(B14)

Note that, the following mathematical equation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}(\tau\omega)}{(\pi\tau/2)\omega^{2}}\text{d}\omega=1

(B15)

holds for arbitrary
\tau
. Therefore, Eq. (
B14
)
can be further written as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\omega_{0}^{2}t}{I_{\rm dc}^{2}R_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hbar\omega[\bar{n}(\omega)+\frac{1}{2}]\delta(\omega){\text{d}}\omega\,,

(B16)

with
t\rightarrow\infty

Furthermore, we note that,
\bar{n}(\omega)\approx 0
with
\hbar\omega\gg k_{b}T

\bar{n}(\omega)\approx k_{b}T/(\hbar\omega)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{I}\approx t\frac{2k_{b}T}{R_{0}}\left(\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{I_{\rm dc}^{2}}\right)=t\frac{2k_{b}T}{R_{0}}\left(\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{b}}{2\pi h_{0}\hbar}\right)^{2}\,,

with

APPENDIX C: TIME CORRELATION FUNCTIONS FOR THE SYMMETRY BREAKING TWO-LEVEL ATOM

As mentioned in Sec. III-A, the positional operator of a surface-state electron can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{z}

\displaystyle=z_{11}\hat{\sigma}_{11}+z_{22}\hat{\sigma}_{22}+z_{12}\hat{\sigma}_{12}+z_{21}\hat{\sigma}_{21}

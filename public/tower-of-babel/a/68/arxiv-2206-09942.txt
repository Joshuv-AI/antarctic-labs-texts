A globally convergent method to accelerate large-scale optimization using on-the-fly model hyperreduction: application to shape optimization
Tianshu Wen, Matthew J. Zahr

Abstract
We present a numerical method to efficiently solve optimization problems governed by large-scale nonlinear systems of equations, including discretized partial differential equations, using projection-based reduced-order models accelerated with hyperreduction (empirical quadrature) and embedded in a trust-region framework that guarantees global convergence. The proposed framework constructs a hyperreduced model on-the-fly during the solution of the optimization problem, which completely avoids an offline training phase. This ensures all snapshot information is collected along the optimization trajectory, which avoids wasting samples in remote regions of the parameters space that are never visited, and inherently avoids the curse of dimensionality of sampling in a high-dimensional parameter space. At each iteration of the proposed algorithm, a reduced basis and empirical quadrature weights are constructed precisely to ensure the global convergence criteria of the trust-region method are satisfied, ensuring global convergence to a local minimum of the original (unreduced) problem. Numerical experiments are performed on two fluid shape optimization problems to verify the global convergence of the method and demonstrate its computational efficiency; speedups over 18 × 18\times (accounting for all computational cost, even cost that is traditionally considered “offline” such as snapshot collection and data compression) relative to standard optimization approaches that do not leverage model reduction are shown.

an empirical quadrature procedure with additional constraints tailored to the optimization setting,

(ii)

(iii)

a trust-region method with hyperreduced approximation models (EQP/TR), and

(iv)

), the adjoint residual and QoI gradient function can be written in unassembled form as

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}^{\lambda}({\bm{\lambda}},{\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})

\displaystyle=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\left[\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{\mu}})-{\bm{\lambda}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}})\right]

(24)

\displaystyle{\bm{r}}^{\lambda}({\bm{\lambda}},{\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})

\displaystyle=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\left[-{\bm{P}}_{e}\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}+{\bm{P}}_{e}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\lambda}}_{e}+{\bm{P}}_{e}^{\prime}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\lambda}}_{e}\right],

where
{\bm{\lambda}}_{e}={\bm{P}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{\lambda}}
.
The unassembled form will be used to construct optimization-informed hyperreduced models in the next section.

We close this section by introducing sensitivity analysis terms that mirror the adjoint terms above.
The HDM sensitivity problem is: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding primal solution
{\bm{u}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}

satisfying (
1
), find the sensitivity solution
\partial_{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{u}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
satisfying

{\bm{r}}^{\partial}(\partial_{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{u}}^{\star},{\bm{u}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0},

(25)

where the sensitivity residual,
{\bm{r}}^{\partial}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
, is defined as

{\bm{r}}^{\partial}:({\bm{w}},{\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{w}}+\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(26)

Assuming the residual Jacobian is well-defined and invertible for every
({\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})
pair with
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
,
then there exists a unique sensitivity solution

\partial_{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})=-\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{-1}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}}).

(27)

making the implicit map
{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\partial_{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})
well-defined for all
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
.
From the unassembled form of the residual function (
2
), the sensitivity residual can be written in unassembled
form as

{\bm{r}}^{\partial}({\bm{w}},{\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\left[{\bm{P}}_{e}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{w}}_{e}+{\bm{P}}_{e}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{w}}_{e}^{\prime}+{\bm{P}}_{e}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}}),\right],

(28)

where
{\bm{w}}_{e}={\bm{P}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{w}}
and
{\bm{w}}_{e}^{\prime}=({\bm{P}}_{e}^{\prime})^{T}{\bm{w}}
.

Remark 3
.

There are two reasons sensitivity analysis is included in this work. First, the sensitivity method can be used in the place of the
adjoint method in the proposed framework to compute gradients if appropriate for a given application (i.e., few parameters). The
use of sensitivity information to construct reduced-order models has been shown to lead to more robust, predictive ROMs
[
48
]
,
although this approach is not practical when there are many parameters. Secondly, our numerical experiments (Section
5
)
found it is beneficial to include reduced sensitivity constraints in the EQP training when the adjoint method is used to compute
gradients.

2.4
Application: shape optimization

To close this section, we specialize the developments thus far to shape optimization as this will be an emphasis
of the numerical experiments (Section
5
). Recall the PDE in (
3
) and
(
7
), and observe that in the discrete setting, the PDE domain
\Omega
is approximated
using the computational mesh
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, which we denote

\Omega_{h}\coloneqq\cup_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\Omega_{e}\approx\Omega

{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{x}}
denote the
coordinates of the nodes associated with the mesh
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, which naturally parametrizes the discrete
domain as
\Omega_{h}=\Omega_{h}({\bm{x}})
. Then the algebraic PDE residual and QoI can be written explicitly
in terms of the PDE state and the nodal coordinates of the mesh. Let
{\bm{R}}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}

denote the PDE residual and
J:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the QoI with

{\bm{R}}:({\bm{u}},{\bm{x}})\mapsto{\bm{R}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}),\qquad J:({\bm{u}},{\bm{x}})\mapsto J({\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}).

(29)

In practice, it is rarely convenient to directly use these quantities to pose a shape optimization problem because
directly optimizing the nodal coordinates leads to many parameters and is difficult to enforce smoothness
of the resulting geometry.
In this work, we partition the nodal coordinates as
{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{o}},{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}})
,
where
{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{o}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}}
are the DoFs that will be optimized
and
{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}}
are the constrained DoFs that will be determined via linear elasticity
(considering the mesh to be a pseudo-structure with displacement-driven deformation) to obtain
a well-conditioned mesh
[
19

27
]
.
That is, we define the constrained DoFs in terms of the optimized DoFs as

{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}=-{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{c}\mathtt{c}}^{-1}{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{c}\mathtt{o}}{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{o}}

(30)

where
{\bm{K}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}\times N_{\bm{x}}}
is the linear elasticity stiffness matrix and

{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{c}\mathtt{c}}
,
{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{c}\mathtt{o}}
indicate its restriction to the rows
corresponding to constrained DoFs and columns corresponding to the constrained and optimized
DoFs, respectively.

Next, we parametrize the nodal coordinates by introducing a mapping from
the parameter domain

{\bm{\phi}}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}},\qquad{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{I}}\\
-{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{c}\mathtt{c}}^{-1}{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{c}\mathtt{o}}\end{bmatrix}{\bm{\phi}}_{\mathtt{o}}({\bm{\mu}}),

(31)

where
{\bm{\phi}}_{\mathtt{o}}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}}
directly parametrizes the optimized nodal DoFs.
Then, for a given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}}
, the corresponding nodal coordinates are defined as

{\bm{x}}={\bm{\phi}}({\bm{\mu}})
. In this work, the optimized DoFs correspond to the nodal positions on the surface whose shape is being
optimized and the mapping
{\bm{\phi}}_{\mathtt{o}}
is a Bezier parametrization of the nodal coordinates.

From these definitions, we define the
{\bm{\mu}}
-parametrized PDE residual
(
{\bm{r}}
) in (
1
) and
{\bm{\mu}}
-parametrized QoI (
j
) as

{\bm{r}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})={\bm{R}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\phi}}({\bm{\mu}})),\qquad j({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=J({\bm{u}},{\bm{\phi}}({\bm{\mu}})).

(32)

With these definitions, the shape optimization problem exactly fits the form of the optimization (
16
).
The unassembled form of the residual and quantity of interest are

{\bm{r}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}{\bm{R}}_{e}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}})),\qquad j({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}J_{e}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}})),

(33)

where
{\bm{R}}_{e}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{e}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{e^{\prime}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{e}}
with

{\bm{R}}_{e}:({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{x}}_{e})\mapsto{\bm{R}}_{e}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{x}}_{e})
is the residual
contribution of element
e
,
{\bm{u}}_{e}
and
{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}
are defined in Section
2.1
,

{\bm{x}}_{e}={\bm{Q}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{e}}
are the mesh coordinate DoFs associated with
element
e
, and
{\bm{\phi}}_{e}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{e}}
with

{\bm{\phi}}_{e}:{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto{\bm{Q}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{\phi}}({\bm{\mu}})
and
{\bm{Q}}_{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}\times N_{\bm{x}}^{e}}

is the assembly operator that maps element mesh coordinate DoFs to global mesh coordinate DoFs.
The adjoint residual, sensitivity residual, and gradient reconstruction are defined similarly with the
observation that derivatives of
{\bm{R}}_{e}
and
J_{e}
with respect to
{\bm{\mu}}
must use the chain
rule, e.g.,

\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}\left[{\bm{R}}_{e}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}}))\right]=\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{x}}_{e}}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}}))\frac{\partial{\bm{\phi}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\mu}}).

(34)

3
Hyperreduction

In this section, we enhance the empirical quadrature procedure (EQP) developed by Yano
[
43
]
with additional constraints required in the optimization setting that will be used to prove global convergence when embedded in a trust-region framework. To this end, we introduce standard projection-based model reduction (Section
3.1
), the adapted EQP method (Section
3.2
), and a technique to accelerate mesh motion in the shape optimization setting (Section
3.4
).

3.1
Projection-based model reduction

Projection-based model reduction begins with the ansatz that the primal state lies in a low-dimensional affine subspace

\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{\Phi}}=\{{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}}\mid\hat{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}
, where
{\bm{\Phi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times n}
with
n\ll N_{\bm{u}}
is the reduced basis. That is, we approximate the primal state
{\bm{u}}^{\star}
as

{\bm{u}}^{\star}\approx\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\coloneqq{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}}^{\star},

(35)

where
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{\Phi}}
is the subspace approximation of the primal
state
{\bm{u}}_{\star}
and
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
contains the corresponding reduced coordinates.
The construction of
{\bm{\Phi}}
will be deferred to Section
4.2
.
The primal reduced coordinates are implicitly defined as the solution of the Galerkin reduced-order model:
given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
, find
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0},

(36)

where
\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto{\bm{\Phi}}^{T}{\bm{r}}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),

(37)

which is obtained by substituting the ROM ansatz (
35
) into the governing equation (
1
)
and projecting the resulting residual onto the columnspace of the reduced basis (Galerkin projection). We assume that for
every
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a unique primal solution
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}=\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})

satisfying (
36
) and that the implicit map
{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})
is continuously
differentiable. The reduced residual (
\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}
) inherits an unassembled structure from the HDM

\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(38)

where
{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\coloneqq{\bm{P}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{e}\times n}
and

{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\coloneqq({\bm{P}}_{e}^{\prime})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{e^{\prime}}\times n}
are the
reduced bases restricted to the elemental degrees of freedom.

The QoI is reduced by substituting the ROM approximation into the original QoI function. Let

\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the reduced quantity of interest
and
\hat{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the corresponding quantity restricted
to the ROM solution manifold, where

\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto j({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),\qquad\hat{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}}).

(39)

The reduced QoI inherits an unassembled structure from the HDM

\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}j_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(40)

The reduced adjoint residual,
\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is defined as

\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}\hat{\bm{z}}-\frac{\partial\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}={\bm{\Phi}}^{T}{\bm{r}}^{\lambda}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{z}},{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),

(41)

where the equality follows directly from the definitions in (
20
), (
37
), and (
39
).
The reduced adjoint problem is: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding reduced primal solution

\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}
satisfying (
36
), find the reduced adjoint solution
\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

satisfying

\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0}.

(42)

Assuming the reduced residual Jacobian is well-defined and invertible for every
(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})

pair with
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a unique reduced adjoint solution

\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})=\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{-T}\frac{\partial\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{T},

(43)

making the implicit map
{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})
well-defined for all
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
.
The reduced adjoint residual inherits an unassembled structure from the HDM using
the relationships in (
24
) and (
41
)

\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\left[-{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{z}}+({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime})^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{z}}\right].

(44)

From the primal-adjoint pair (
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}
), the gradient of the reduced QoI (
\hat{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}
),

\nabla\hat{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{\mu}}}}
, is computed as

\nabla\hat{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\lambda}(\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{T}=\frac{\partial\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{T}-\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{T}\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),

(45)

where the operator that reconstructs the reduced QoI gradient from the reduced adjoint solution,

\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
, is

\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\lambda}:(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial\hat{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-\hat{\bm{z}}^{T}\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})={\bm{g}}^{\bm{\lambda}}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{z}},{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(46)

The reduced gradient operator also inherits an unassembled structure from the HDM

\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\lambda}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\left[\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-\hat{\bm{z}}^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right].

(47)

The reduced sensitivity residual,
\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
, is defined as

\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\hat{\bm{w}}+\frac{\partial\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})={\bm{\Phi}}^{T}\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{w}},{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),

(48)

where the equality follows directly from the definitions in (
26
) and (
37
).
The reduced sensitivity problem is: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding reduced primal solution

\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}
satisfying (
36
), find the reduced sensitivity solution
\partial_{\bm{\mu}}\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}

satisfying

{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\partial_{\bm{\mu}}\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0}.

(49)

Assuming the reduced residual Jacobian is well-defined and invertible for every
(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})

pair with
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a unique reduced sensitivity solution

\partial_{\bm{\mu}}\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})=-\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{-1}\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}}),

(50)

making the implicit map
{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\partial_{\bm{\mu}}\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})
well-defined for all
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
.
The reduced sensitivity residual inherits an unassembled structure from the HDM using
the relationships in (
28
) and (
48
)

\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\left[{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{w}}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{w}}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right].

(51)

Despite the potentially significant reduction in degrees of freedom between the HDM system (
1
) and the
reduced system (
36
), computational efficiency will not necessarily be achieved due to the cost of
constructing the nonlinear terms. This can be clearly seen from the unassembled form of the primal residual
(
38
), QoI (
40
), adjoint residual (
41
), sensitivity residual (
48
), and QoI gradient (
47
), namely, each
of these operations requires elemental operations
for all elements
. For systems comprised of many elements,
e.g., high-fidelity discretizations of PDEs, this can be a serious bottleneck. To accelerate formation of the nonlinear
terms, we turn to EQP hyperreduction.

Remark 4
.

One source of computational efficiency in the model reduction setting is the reduced-order model in
(
36
) can usually be solved directly using Newton’s method initialized from the snapshots used to
train the basis
{\bm{\Phi}}
. In the HDM setting, Newton’s method can rarely be used directly for
fluid applications; usually, some form of continuation is required for robust convergence, which
can require a significant number of iterations. This difference will be leveraged and discussed
further in Section
5
.

3.2
An optimization-aware empirical quadrature procedure

To accelerate the assembly of the nonlinear terms in the various reduced quantities introduced in
Section
3.1
, we use the empirical quadrature procedure
[
46
,
43
]
. In the adjoint-based
optimization setting, we use EQP to accelerate any operation that involves assembly over all elements (Section
3.2.1
),
i.e., evaluation of the primal and adjoint residuals, quantity of interest, and gradient reconstruction.
To ensure all hyperreduced quantities are accurate with respect to their reduced counterparts, we include additional
constraints on the original EQP linear program introduced in
[
43
]
(Section
3.2.2
).

3.2.1
Formulation

The EQP construction replaces the unassembled form of the reduced primal residual with a weighted (hyperreduced) version,

\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}
, where

\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})\mapsto\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\rho_{e}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{r}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})

(52)

and
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
is the vector of weights with
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}
the set of admissible weights
such that
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{R}
(
\mathbf{1}
is the vector with each entry equal to one). For each element
\Omega_{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
with
\rho_{e}=0
, the operations on the element can be completely skipped so computational efficiency is achieved when the vector of weights is highly sparse; construction of
{\bm{\rho}}
is deferred to Section
3.2.2
and
3.3
. The primal EQP problem reads: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
, find
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})=\mathbf{0}.

(53)

For each
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
, we assume there is a unique primal solution

\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}=\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})
satisfying (
53
) for every
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
.

Remark 5
.

The standard and weighted reduced primal residual are related as

\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};\mathbf{1})

(54)

for any
\hat{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
. Therefore, the corresponding reduced coordinates are equal

\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})=\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};\mathbf{1}).

(55)

Remark 6
.

Uniqueness of the primal solution will not hold for all
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
because

, which means any
\tilde{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies (

).
Such cases are not encountered in practice because
{\bm{\rho}}
is not chosen randomly, rather it is
the solution of a linear program (Section

) that promotes sparsity while retaining accuracy with respect to
\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}
.

In the optimization setting, computational efficiency is also required for the evaluation of the QoI, the adjoint residual
(similar to
[
44
,
15
]
), and the gradient reconstruction. We use the same approach of introducing weights
(
{\bm{\rho}}
) into the unassembled form; efficiency is achieved when
{\bm{\rho}}
is sparse.
The hyperreduced QoI,
\tilde{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
its restriction to the EQP solution manifold,
\tilde{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
are defined as

\tilde{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})\mapsto\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\rho_{e}j_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),\qquad\tilde{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})\mapsto\tilde{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}),{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}).

(56)

The hyperreduced adjoint residual,
\tilde{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is defined as

\tilde{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\tilde{\bm{z}},\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})\mapsto\frac{\partial\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\tilde{\bm{y}}}(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})^{T}\tilde{\bm{z}}-\frac{\partial\tilde{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\tilde{\bm{y}}}(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})^{T},

(57)

or, in unassembled form,

\tilde{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{z}},\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\rho_{e}\left[-{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{z}}+({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime})^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{z}}\right].

(58)

The hyperreduced adjoint problem is: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding hyperreduced primal solution

\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}
satisfying (
53
), find the hyperreduced adjoint solution

satisfying

(59)

For each
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
, we assume the hyperreduced Jacobian is well-defined and invertible for every
(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}),{\bm{\mu}})
pair with
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
so there exists a unique hyperreduced adjoint solution

(60)

making the implicit map

From the primal-adjoint pair (

.

Remark 13
.

Due to the additional constraints in the proposed optimization-aware variant of EQP relative to the original

.

.

) is used, there is an issue with (

3.4
Application: shape optimization

To close this section, we return to the shape optimization setting. Because we have cast the shape optimization problem
as the generic form of the discretized PDE (
1
), all reduced and hyperreduced terms follow accordingly. However, as written,
the mesh motion is a potential bottleneck in the reduced workflow because it requires high-dimensional operations. To see
this, we write the unassembled residual and quantity of interest

\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\rho_{e}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{R}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}})),\qquad\tilde{j}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\rho_{e}J_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}}))

(78)

from which we can see both terms depend on
{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}})
, i.e., for each value of
{\bm{\mu}}
encountered
online,
{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}})={\bm{Q}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{\phi}}({\bm{\mu}})
must be computed for each
e\in\{1,\dots,N_{\mathtt{e}}\}

where
\rho_{e}>0
. Expanding
{\bm{\phi}}({\bm{\mu}})
using (
31
), we have

{\bm{\phi}}_{e}({\bm{\mu}})={\bm{A}}_{e}{\bm{\phi}}_{\mathtt{o}}({\bm{\mu}}).

(79)

where
{\bm{A}}_{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{e}\times N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}}
is defined as

{\bm{A}}_{e}={\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{o},e}^{T}-{\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{c},e}^{T}{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}^{-1}{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}

(80)

and the rows of
{\bm{Q}}_{e}
are partitioned into optimized and unconstrained mesh coordinate
degrees of freedom (as described in Section
2.4
) as
{\bm{Q}}_{e}=({\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{o},e},{\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{c},e})

with
{\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{o},e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}\times N_{\bm{x}}^{e}}
and

{\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{c},e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}\times N_{\bm{x}}^{e}}
.
The cost and feasibility of evaluating
{\bm{A}}_{e}
using (
80
) strongly depends on the size of
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}
.
If
{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}
is small enough to be store and factorized, then
{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}^{-1}{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}

can be computed at the cost of a factorization of
{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}
and
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}
forward/backward
substitutions. On the other hand, if
{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}
is too large for direct solvers to be feasible (often the case
for CFD), then
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}
iterative system solves of the form
{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}{\bm{v}}={\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}

are required, which can be very expensive depending on the size of
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}
and
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}
.
Often in aerodynamic shape optimization,
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}
is proportional to the number of nodes
on the surface being optimized and
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}
proportional to the number of nodes in the
fluid mesh not on the surface being optimized. While both of these can be quite large (particularly for turbulent
flows), usually
N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}\gg N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}
.

To avoid this potentially significant cost in the hyperreduced model for shape optimization, we propose an
alternate approach to mesh deformation that uses projection-based model reduction. First, we recast the
equation in (
30
) as

{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}=-{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{o}}

(81)

and assume the constrained degrees of freedom can be well-approximated in a low-dimensional subspace

{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}\approx\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}\coloneqq{\bm{\Psi}}\hat{\bm{\varpi}},

(82)

where
\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}}
is the subspace approximate to
{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}
,

{\bm{\Psi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}\times r}
is the reduced basis for the constrained mesh coordinate
DoFs with
r\ll N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}
, and
\hat{\bm{\varpi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
are the corresponding reduced coordinates.
To close the system, we apply a Galerkin projection to yield a linear system of equations for the reduced coordinates

\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}{\bm{\varpi}}=-\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{o}},

(83)

where
\hat{\bm{K}}_{cc}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
and
\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}}
are
the reduced elasticity stiffness matrix terms

\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}={\bm{\Psi}}^{T}{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}{\bm{\Psi}},\qquad\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}={\bm{\Psi}}^{T}{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}.

(84)

By solving this system and substituting into (
83
), we have the expression for the constrained mesh coordinate DoFs

\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}=-{\bm{\Psi}}\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}^{-1}\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{co}}{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{o}}.

(85)

Finally, we use the subspace approximation
\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}
in place of the
true constrained mesh coordinate DoFs
{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}
in (
30
) to define an reduced version of the
mesh motion mapping
\hat{\bm{\phi}}_{e}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{e}}
with

\hat{\bm{\phi}}_{e}:{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\hat{\bm{A}}_{e}{\bm{\phi}}_{\mathtt{o}}({\bm{\mu}}),

(86)

where
\hat{\bm{A}}_{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{x}}^{e}\times N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{o}}}
is defined as

\hat{\bm{A}}_{e}={\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{o},e}^{T}-{\bm{\Psi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{K}}_{cc}^{-1}\hat{\bm{K}}_{co}

(87)

and
{\bm{\Psi}}_{e}={\bm{Q}}_{\mathtt{c},e}^{T}{\bm{\Psi}}
. The reduced stiffness matrix
\hat{\bm{K}}_{\mathtt{cc}}

is small (
r\ll N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}}
) and can be factorized efficiently, keeping the overall cost
computing
\hat{\bm{A}}_{e}
low.

The reduced basis
{\bm{\Psi}}
is constructed once-and-for-all using a simple training strategy that
is effective for a small to moderate number of parameters. The basis is defined by compressing perturbations
of each shape parameter about a nominal configuration
{\bm{\mu}}_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
(in this work, we
use the original shape used to initialize the optimization iterations), i.e.,

{\bm{\Psi}}=\mathtt{POD}_{N_{\bm{x}}^{\mathtt{c}},2N_{\bm{\mu}}}^{r}\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{\mu}}_{0}+\epsilon\mathbf{1}_{1}&{\bm{\mu}}_{0}-\epsilon\mathbf{1}_{1}&\cdots&{\bm{\mu}}_{0}+\epsilon\mathbf{1}_{N_{\bm{\mu}}}&{\bm{\mu}}_{0}-\epsilon\mathbf{1}_{N_{\bm{\mu}}}\end{bmatrix}\right),

(88)

where
\mathtt{POD}_{m,n}^{k}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
applies the SVD to the argument
(snapshot matrix of size
m\times n
) and extracts the
k
left singular vectors, and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
is the
magnitude of the perturbation. The reduced mesh motion is demonstrated in Figures

-
3
using a NACA0012 airfoil parametrized using Bezier curves.
Figures

and

show the original shape and mesh of the airfoil,

88
) with
\epsilon=0.5
and

r=35

Remark 18
.

Linear, physics-based mesh motion problem is ideally suited for projection-based model reduction for several reasons.
The governing equations are linear and thus significant speedups are easily obtained without hyperreduction. Most importantly,
a reduced-order model is built of
{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}
(constrained mesh coordinates DoFs, e.g., nodes away
from surface being optimized), which has no impact on the shape of the optimized surface
(completely controlled by
{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{o}}

perfectly predict
{\bm{x}}_{\mathtt{c}}
(from full linear elasticity) as long as it leads to meshes that are
well-conditioned.

Remark 19
.

In the optimization setting,
\hat{\bm{A}}_{e}
is computed at the first iteration for each element in the entire mesh

{\bm{\rho}}

{\bm{\rho}}

\rho_{e}>0

makes amortizing the initial

unreduced mesh motions required to train

in (

Globally convergent trust-region method using hyperreduced models

In this section, we use the hyperreduced models introduced in Section
3
to accelerate the optimization problem
of interest (
18
). To ensure the hyperreduced optimization problem converges to a local minimum of the original
(unreduced) problem, we embed it in trust-region algorithm that allows for models with inexact gradients at trust-region
centers and asymptotic error bounds
[
25
]
. To this end, we introduce the general trust-region method in
which the hyperreduced model will be embedded (Section
4.1
), as well as the proposed approach to train
and leverage hyperreduced models in the trust-region framework to ensure global convergence (Section
4.2
).

4.1
A trust-region method with inexact model gradients and asymptotic error bounds

Recall the optimization problem in (
18
), where the objective function
f
satisfies Assumption
1
below.
A trust-region method constructs a sequence of trust-region centers

\{{\bm{\mu}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
whose limit will be a local solution of (
18
). Trust-region methods
construct a smooth approximation model
m_{k}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
at each trust-region center
such that
m_{k}({\bm{\mu}})\approx f({\bm{\mu}})
for all
{\bm{\mu}}\in\{{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}|\left\|{\bm{\mu}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{k}\right\|\leq\Delta_{k}\}
,
where
\Delta_{k}>0
is the trust-region radius. A candidate step
\check{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
is produced by
approximately solving the trust-region subproblem

\displaystyle\underset{{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle m_{k}({\bm{\mu}})

(89)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{\mu}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{k}\right\|_{2}\leq\Delta_{k}.

The trust-region subproblem does not need to be solved exactly; rather, it must only satisfy the
fraction of Cauchy decrease condition
[
13
]
. The step candidate is evaluated using the actual-to-predicted reduction ratio,

\varrho_{k}
in (
93
), to determine whether to accept the step and how to adjust the trust-region radius.

While there are many trust-region methods available in the literature, we base our hyperreduced optimization
framework on the method in
[
25
]
because it only requires an asymptotic error condition of the form

\left\|\nabla m_{k}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})-\nabla f({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|\leq\xi\min\{\left\|\nabla m_{k}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|,\Delta_{k}\},

(90)

where
\xi>0
is any arbitrary constant (independent of
k

\xi

bound is not particularly meaningful for a fixed

model gradient to become increasingly accurate as

m_{k}

\left\|\nabla m_{k}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})-\nabla f({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|\leq\xi\varphi_{k}({\bm{\mu}}_{k}),

(91)

where

denotes a
computable
error indicator for the model gradient,
then the gradient condition can be stated solely in terms of the error bound as

\varphi_{k}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\leq\kappa_{\varphi}\min\{\left\|\nabla m_{k}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|,\Delta_{k}\},

(92)

where
\kappa_{\varphi}>0

4.2
Accelerated optimization using trust regions and on-the-fly model hyperreduction

With the individual components—the hyperreduced models and the globally convergent trust-region method based on
asymptotic error bounds—in place, we combine them to define the proposed trust-region method accelerated with
model hyperreduction, to be referred to as EQP/TR in the remainder. We begin by defining the trust-region model (
m_{k}
)
at the
k
th trust-region center
{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
as the quadratic approximation to the hyperreduced quantity of interest

m_{k}({\bm{\mu}})=\tilde{f}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}}({\bm{\mu}}_{k};{\bm{\rho}}_{k})+\nabla\tilde{f}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}}({\bm{\mu}}_{k};{\bm{\rho}}_{k})^{T}({\bm{\mu}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{k})+\frac{1}{2}({\bm{\mu}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{k})^{T}\tilde{\bm{H}}_{k}({\bm{\mu}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{k}),

(94)

where
{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times n_{k}}
is the reduced basis,
{\bm{\rho}}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k},{\bm{\Xi}}_{k},{\bm{\delta}}_{k}}
is the weight
vector, and
\tilde{\bm{H}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{\mu}}\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
is the Hessian of
\tilde{f}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}}

\tilde{\bm{H}}_{k}\coloneqq\nabla^{2}\tilde{f}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}}({\bm{\mu}}_{k};{\bm{\rho}}_{k}),

(95)

all at the
k
th trust-region center. Hessian-vector products are computed using a finite difference approximation involving
the gradient

\tilde{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{v}}\approx\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left[\nabla\tilde{f}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}}({\bm{\mu}}_{k}+\varepsilon{\bm{v}};{\bm{\rho}}_{k})-\nabla\tilde{f}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}}({\bm{\mu}}_{k};{\bm{\rho}}_{k})\right],

(96)

where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is the finite difference step size (
\varepsilon=10^{-6}
in this work).
Assuming the reduced basis dimension
n_{k}
is small and the weight vector
{\bm{\rho}}_{k}
is sparse, the model
m_{k}
and
its gradient
\nabla m_{k}
will be much less expensive to query than the HDM
f
.

The reduced basis is chosen as

{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}=\mathtt{GramSchmidt}\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})&{\bm{\lambda}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})&{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}^{\mathtt{p}}&{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}^{\mathtt{a}}\end{bmatrix}\right),

(97)

to guarantee
{\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{k}),{\bm{\lambda}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\in\mathrm{Ran}~{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}

(required for Corollaries
1.1
-
1.3
to hold), where

{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}^{\mathtt{p}}\coloneqq\mathtt{POD}_{N_{\bm{u}},k}^{p_{k}}({\bm{U}}_{k-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times p_{k}},\qquad{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}^{\mathtt{a}}\coloneqq\mathtt{POD}_{N_{\bm{u}},k}^{q_{k}}({\bm{V}}_{k-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times q_{k}}

(98)

are optimal compressions of state and adjoint snapshots from

Remark 22
.

The trust-region subproblems do not need to be solved exactly, rather they must guaranteed a fraction of the Cauchy decrease
condition
[
13
]
. In this work, we leverage this opportunity for efficiency by using the Steihaug-Toint Truncated Conjugate Gradient
method, which guarantees the candidate step will satisfy the fraction of Cauchy decrease condition.

Remark 23
.

We choose the quadratic model in (
94
) rather than the more obvious choice of directly using the hyperreduced model,
i.e.,
m_{k}({\bm{\mu}})=\tilde{f}_{{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}_{k})
, as done in other work
[
48
,
32
,
45
]

because the trust-region subproblems are less expensive to solve. For example, a truncated conjugate
gradient method can be used rather than a more sophisticated solver for a general nonlinear subproblem. Even though the trust-region
subproblem only requires hyperreduced model queries, it can become a bottleneck (especially at later iterations when the
reduced basis is large) and the solver can be prone to failure. In some cases these issues are supplanted because this approach
can lead to models with higher predictive capability, which leads to more successful iterations and fewer HDM queries
[
48
,
32
]
.

Remark 24
.

With the choice of the reduced basis
{\bm{\Phi}}_{k}
in (
97
), the basis size is bounded as
n_{k}\leq 2(k+1)
leading to a very small
hyperreduced model in the early iterations, which can limit its predictive capability. Therefore, following the work in
[
49
]
,
we will also consider a related approach that includes the HDM sensitivities at the starting point

(111)

which leads to a basis of size

(bounded by
n_{k}\leq 2(k+1)+N_{\bm{\mu}}
).

.

Remark 26
.

The only requirement on the EQP training set
{\bm{\Xi}}_{k}
is that it contains the trust-region center, i.e.,
{\bm{\mu}}_{k}\in{\bm{\Xi}}_{k}
.
In this work, we take

Remark 27
.

As stated, the tolerances

5.2
Inverse design of an airfoil in inviscid, subsonic flow

Next, we consider aerodynamic inverse shape design whereby we aim to recover an RAE2822 airfoil from only
its flow field starting from a NACA0012 airfoil. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2
) be the region around a NACA0012
airfoil with cord length
L=1
that extends
8L
from the leading edge, and consider steady, inviscid, compressible
flow governed by the Euler equations

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\rho v_{j}\right)=0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\rho v_{i}v_{j}+P\delta_{ij}\right)=0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\left[\rho E+P\right]v_{j}\right)=0,

(119)

where
i=1,\dots,d
and summation is implied over
the repeated index
j=1,\dots,d
. The density of the fluid

\rho:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the velocity of
the fluid
v_{i}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
in the
x_{i}

direction for
i=1,\dots,d
, and the total energy of the fluid

E:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are implicitly defined
as the solution of (
119
).
For a calorically ideal fluid, the pressure of the fluid,

P:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, is related to the energy
via the ideal gas law

P=(\gamma-1)\left(\rho E-\frac{\rho v_{i}v_{i}}{2}\right),

(120)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is the ratio of specific heats. We stack the conservative variables into a
state vector
U:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d+2}
with
U:x\mapsto(\rho(x),\rho(x)v(x),\rho(x)E(x))
.
A farfield boundary condition is applied to the cutoff surface of the domain and a slip wall condition
(
v\cdot n=0
) is applied on the airfoil surface.

The objective of this problem is to match the RAE2822 flow field, denoted
U_{\mathrm{RAE2822}}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d+2}

by adjusting the airfoil surface, which is parametrized using a Bezier curve with 18 control points and the deformation is extended
to the rest of the domain using linear elasticity (Section
30
). The parameter vector
{\bm{\mu}}
denotes the collection of Bezier
control points. This leads to the following PDE-constrained shape optimization problem

\underset{U,{\bm{\mu}}}{\text{minimize}}~~\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega({\bm{\mu}})}\left\|U-U_{\mathrm{RAE2822}}\right\|^{2}~dV,\quad\text{subject to:}~~\mathcal{L}(U;{\bm{\mu}})=0,

(121)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the differential operator that includes the Euler equations (
119
) and appropriate boundary conditions.
The starting point and optimal solution for this problem are shown in Figure
11
. Because of this choice of objective
function, the optimal value of the objective function is zero so we use the first definition of
S_{k}
in (
115
) throughout
this section.

j({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\frac{1}{2}({\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{RAE2822}})^{T}{\bm{M}}({\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{RAE2822}}),

(122)

where

Therefore, in this section, we directly use the form of the QoI in (

6
Conclusion

In this work, we introduce a numerical method to efficiently solve optimization problems governed by large-scale nonlinear systems
using projection-based reduced-order models accelerated with hyperreduction (empirical quadrature) and globalized with a
trust-region method based on inexact gradient evaluations and asymptotic error bounds
[
25
]
. The proposed method is globally
convergent because hyperreduced models are constructed at each trust-region center precisely to satisfy the conditions
for global convergence. In addition to the primary contribution of the EQP/TR method, this work also contributes:
(i) an empirical quadrature procedure with additional constraints tailored to the optimization setting,
(ii) an approach to accelerate physics-based mesh motion (linear elasticity, springs system, etc) using linear model reduction, and
(iii) global convergence theory for the proposed EQP/TR method.

Two fluid shape optimization problems are employed to verify global convergence of the method and
demonstrate the efficiency of the method; speedups over 18 are attained relative to standard optimization approaches
(even when accounting for all sources of computational cost, i.e., HDM evaluations to gather snapshots and assess trust-region
steps, reduced basis construction, linear program solves to construct EQP weights, trust-region subproblems solves, etc).
Several studies were performed to understand the sensitivity of the method relative to algorithmic parameters, e.g.,
the gradient tolerance, the basis truncation size, and the EQP constraints to include. We found mild sensitivity
with respect to the gradient tolerance, although a moderate value of
\hat{\kappa}/\kappa=10^{-4}
proved effective
throughout. Furthermore, there seemed to be little benefit to truncating the snapshot matrix and including
all optimization-based EQP constraints was most effective. Currently, the dominant cost of the proposed
method comes from the HDM evaluations. This suggests further
investigation into hyperreduced models with better predictive capabilities could reduce the frequency
in which the model is reconstructed and further improve the computational efficiency of the method.
Other interesting research directions include the extension to unsteady problems, extension to quadrature-based
(instead of element-based) EQP
[
15
,
16
]
for integration with higher order finite element
discretizations, and using the methodology to solve relevant optimization problems.

(AH2)

\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{D}

(AH3)

The Jacobian matrix

\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{u}}}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{\mu}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times N_{\bm{u}}},\qquad\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{u}}}:({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})

.

The state derivative

\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\bm{u}}}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{\mu}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1\times N_{\bm{u}}},\qquad\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\bm{u}}}:({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\bm{u}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})

(125)

is Lipschitz continuous with respect to its first argument on the domain

.

The parameter Jacobian matrix

\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{\mu}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times N_{\bm{\mu}}},\qquad\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}:({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})

(126)

is Lipschitz continuous with respect to its first argument on the domain
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{D}
.

(AH7)

The parameter derivative

\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{\mu}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1\times N_{\bm{\mu}}},\qquad\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}:({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})

(127)

is Lipschitz continuous with respect to its first argument on the domain
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{D}

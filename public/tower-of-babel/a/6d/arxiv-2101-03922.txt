Dirac Hamiltonian in a supersymmetric framework
Bijan Bagchi, Rahul Ghosh

Abstract
We investigate the most general form of the one-dimensional Dirac Hamiltonian H D H_{D} in the presence of scalar and pseudoscalar potentials. To seek embedding of supersymmetry (SUSY) in it, as an alternative procedure to directly employing the intertwining relations, we construct a quasi-Hamiltonian 𝒦 \mathcal{K} , defined as the square of H D H_{D} , to explore the consequences. We show that the diagonal elements of 𝒦 \mathcal{K} under a suitable approximation reflects the presence of a superpotential thus proving a useful guide in unveiling the role of SUSY. For illustrative purpose we apply our scheme to the transformed one-dimensional version of the planar electron Hamiltonian under the influence of a magnetic field. We generate spectral solutions for a class of isochronous potentials.

Principles of SQM

The
N=2

SQM
is described

by a pair of fermionic-like supercharges

Q^{\dagger}
related by Hermitian conjugation. The SUSY Hamiltonian is given by

\mathcal{H}=\{Q,Q^{\dagger}\}

(2.1)

The essential properties of
Q

Q^{\dagger}
are

\displaystyle(Q)^{2}=0=(Q^{\dagger})^{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle[Q,\mathcal{H}]=0=[Q^{\dagger},\mathcal{H}]

(2.3)

Representing
Q
and
Q^{\dagger}
through

Q=\mathcal{L}\otimes\sigma_{-},\quad Q^{\dagger}=\quad\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\otimes\sigma_{+}

(2.4)

where
\sigma_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{1}\pm i\sigma_{2})
,
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
being the usual Pauli matrices and
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}
are a set of linear differential operators, and adopting a first-order realization of them namely (
\partial\equiv\frac{d}{dx}
)

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\partial+W(x),\quad\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}=-\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\partial+W(x)

(2.5)

where
m
is the mass of a non-relativistic particle and
W(x)\in\mathbb{R}
is called the superpotential of the system, project
Q
and
Q^{\dagger}
in a
2\times 2
matrix forms

Q=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\partial+W(x)&0\end{array}\right),\quad Q^{\dagger}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\partial+W(x)\\
0&0\end{array}\right)

(2.6)

\mathcal{H}
is thus diagonal

\mathcal{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{+}&0\\
0&\mathcal{H}_{-}\end{array}\right)

(2.7)

where the component Hamiltonians
H_{\pm}
appear as the Schrödinger operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{+}=\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\mathcal{L}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\partial^{2}+V^{(+)}(x)-\Lambda

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{-}=\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}=--\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\partial^{2}+V^{(-)}(x)-\Lambda

(2.9)

where
\Lambda
is some arbitrary point of energy. The corresponding potentials

and
V^{(-)}
read explicitly

{}V_{\pm}(x)=W^{2}(x)\mp\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}W^{\prime}(x)+\Lambda

(2.10)

where the prime denotes derivative with respect to the variable
x
.The above set of equations immediately implies that the spectrum of
\mathcal{H}
is doubly degenerate except for the ground state
|0\rangle
. We will consider
SQM
to be unbroken in which case the supercharges

and
Q^{\dagger}
annihilates
|0\rangle
implying

. In such a scenario the ground state wavefunction

is non-degenerate which, for our purpose, is taken to be associated with the
\mathcal{H}_{+}
component

\mathcal{L}\psi_{0}^{+}(x)=0\quad\implies\quad\psi_{0}^{+}(x)\propto\exp\left(-\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{\hbar}\int^{x}W(t)dt\right)

(2.11)

The normilazibility of the ground-state is assured if the superpotential is so chosen that
\int^{x}W(t)dt
remains positive as
x\rightarrow\infty
.
The double degeneracy of
\mathcal{H}
follows from the following

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{H}_{+}=\mathcal{H}_{-}\mathcal{L}\quad\mathcal{H}_{+}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{-}

(2.12)

These provide isospectral connections between the partner SUSY Hamiltonians. The above equations match with the forms
(2.8)
and
(2.9)
. It is worthwhile to mention here the relevance of our approach with other formalisms that address Dirac equation through SQM
[

Dirac equation with scalar and pseudoscalar

Because of the stationary character

is non-relativistic. In equation
(3.1)
,
\lx@paragraphsign_{2}
is the block-diagonal unit matrix,
m_{0}
is the electron rest-mass, and apart from the kinetic energy operator term, the associated potential, in general, consists of a combination of the electrostatic potential

, the scalar potential
S(x)
, a pseudo-scalar potential

and in principle an additional one

. However the latter can be eliminated
[
31
]
by a simple phase transformation. The solution of the non-relativistic version of the Dirac equation following from

is known for long in the context of an external magnetic field
[
33
,
34
]
. The Hamiltonian
H_{D}
operates on a two-component spinor wave function which is to be defined later. In principle, apart from

one can also make
[
30
]
alternative choices like

or

by moving in the cyclic order. Indeed, as Junker
[

H^{(2)}_{D}=-i\hbar c\sigma_{y}\partial+V(x)\lx@paragraphsign_{2}+W\sigma_{z}+(m_{0}c^{2}+S)\sigma_{x}

•
H^{(3)}_{D}=-i\hbar c\sigma_{z}\partial+V(x)\lx@paragraphsign_{2}+W(x)\sigma_{x}+(m_{0}c^{2}+S(x))\sigma_{y}

For concreteness, let us concentrate on
H_{D}
only.
Inserting the standard forms of the Pauli matrices

\sigma_{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|c}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma_{y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|c}0&-i\\
i&0\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma_{z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|c}1&0\\

(3.2)

and keeping in mind the analyses of

H_{D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m_{0}c^{2}+S&-i\hbar c\partial-iW\\
-i\hbar c\partial+iW&-m_{0}c^{2}-S\end{array}\right)

(3.3)

In
(3.3)
we have allowed
S
to remain an arbitrary function of

.

To illustrate the connection
[
35
,
36
]

with SQM let us introduce a quasi-Hamiltonian
\mathcal{K}
operator by projecting it as a second degree polynomial in

\mathcal{K}=H_{D}^{2}+2\gamma H_{D}+\delta\equiv(H_{D}+a)^{2}+d

(3.4)

where
\delta
and
\gamma
are constants and

satisfy
\gamma=a,\delta=a^{2}+d
. The concept of a quasi-Hamiltonian has been pioneered by Andrianov and his collaborators for higher-dimensional models of SQM
[
35
,
36
]
. It has seen applicability
[
35
]
in a number of situations like, for example,
coupled channel problems and in cases of transparent matrix potentials. In the present case, the Dirac Hamiltonian contains only a first-order derivative term and so going for
\mathcal{K}
would mean we have to deal with just Schrödinger-like Hamiltonians.

Inserting the above form of
H_{D}
, the quasi-Hamiltonian
\mathcal{K}
reads

\mathcal{K}=H_{D}^{2}+2\gamma H_{D}+(\delta-m_{0}^{2}c^{4})\lx@paragraphsign_{4}

(3.5)

which can be expressed in the following matrix form

\mathcal{K}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{K}_{11}&\mathcal{K}_{12}\\
\mathcal{K}_{21}&\mathcal{K}_{22}\end{array}\right)

(3.6)

The various elements
\mathcal{K}_{ij},\quad i,j=1,2
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{11}=-\hbar^{2}c^{2}\partial^{2}+W^{2}+\hbar cW^{\prime}+2(m_{0}c^{2}+\gamma)S+S^{2}+2\gamma m_{0}c^{2}+\delta

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{12}=i\hbar cS^{\prime}-2i\gamma(\hbar c\partial+W)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{21}=-i\hbar cS^{\prime}-2i\gamma(\hbar c\partial-W)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{22}=-\hbar^{2}c^{2}\partial^{2}+W^{2}-\hbar cW^{\prime}+2(m_{0}c^{2}-\gamma)S+S^{2}-2\gamma m_{0}c^{2}+\delta

(3.10)

where a prime stands for the derivative with respect to
x
.

As a case study, let us set
\delta
by choosing
\delta=\gamma^{2}+m_{0}^{2}c^{4}
. Then, from
(3.5)
,
\mathcal{K}
becomes a perfect square and one has the transformation

\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=(H_{D}+\gamma)^{2}

(3.11)

which represents the

matrix structure of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{ij}
whose

)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{11}=-\hbar^{2}c^{2}\partial^{2}+W^{2}+\hbar cW^{\prime}+(S+m_{0}c^{2}+\gamma)^{2}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{12}=i\hbar c(S^{\prime}-2\gamma\partial)-2i\gamma W

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{21}=-i\hbar c(S^{\prime}+2\gamma\partial)+2i\gamma W

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{22}=-\hbar^{2}c^{2}\partial^{2}+W^{2}-\hbar cW^{\prime}+(S+m_{0}c^{2}-\gamma)^{2}

(3.15)

Note that the elements are above are basically a reduced version of the corresponding ones of
(3.7-3.10)
.

can be interpreted to be the underlying superpotential of the Dirac system.

Let us go for a further reduction by assuming as a typical choice
\gamma=0,\delta=m^{2}c^{4}
. For

, where

is a constant, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{11}=-c^{2}\hbar^{2}\partial^{2}+W^{2}+\hbar cW^{\prime}+\mathcal{E}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{22}=-c^{2}\hbar^{2}\partial^{2}+W^{2}-\hbar cW^{\prime}+\mathcal{E}_{0}

(3.17)

where

contains the mass parameter

. The function

is arbitrary. We now seek an application of

and

4
Planar electrons in magnetic fields

In recent times, two-dimensional quantum kinetic models describing the features of graphene has been a subject of active research
[
37
]
. Apart from obvious theoretical interests , one of the reasons is due to the exotic behavior of graphene pertaining to its

mechanical properties.
As is well known, at low energies, the two-dimensional form of the Dirac Hamiltonian for a zero-mass electron minimally coupled to an external electromagnetic field is

H_{P}=v_{F}\vec{\sigma}.\bigg(\vec{p}+\frac{e\vec{A}}{c}\bigg)+S(x)\sigma_{z}

\vec{A}

Assuming a Landau gauge choice of

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=(H_{P})^{2}+2\gamma H_{P}+\delta

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{11}=(v_{F})^{2}\Bigg[-\hbar^{2}\partial_{X}^{2}+\big(\frac{eA_{y}}{c}-i\hbar\partial_{y}\big)^{2}+\frac{\hbar e}{c}\partial_{x}(A_{y})\Bigg]+S^{2}+2\gamma S+\delta

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{12}=v_{F}\Bigg[i\hbar\big(\partial_{X}-i\partial_{y}\big)S\Bigg]-2i\hbar v_{F}\gamma\bigg(\partial_{X}-i\partial_{y}+\frac{eA_{y}}{c\hbar}\bigg)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{21}=v_{F}\Bigg[-i\hbar\big(\partial_{X}+i\partial_{y}\big)S\Bigg]-2i\hbar v_{F}\gamma\bigg(\partial_{X}+i\partial_{y}-\frac{eA_{y}}{c\hbar}\bigg)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{22}=(v_{F})^{2}\Bigg[-\hbar^{2}\partial_{X}^{2}+\big(\frac{eA_{y}}{c}-i\hbar\partial_{y}\big)^{2}-\frac{\hbar e}{c}\partial_{x}(A_{y})\Bigg]+S^{2}-2\gamma S+\delta

Restricting to a constant scalar field i.e

and assuming

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{11}=(v_{F})^{2}\Bigg[-\hbar^{2}\partial_{X}^{2}+\big(\frac{eA_{y}}{c}-i\hbar\partial_{y}\big)^{2}+\frac{\hbar e}{c}\partial_{x}(A_{y})\Bigg]+\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{22}=(v_{F})^{2}\Bigg[-\hbar^{2}\partial_{X}^{2}+\big(\frac{eA_{y}}{c}-i\hbar\partial_{y}\big)^{2}-\frac{\hbar e}{c}\partial_{x}(A_{y})\Bigg]+\varepsilon_{0}

where

and
\mathcal{K}_{22}
.

It is worth emphasizing that the simple case when the scalar filed vanishes with

but

casts

to the reduced form

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=(H_{P})^{2}-\frac{C^{2}}{4}

An equivalent strategy to study the two-dimensional electrons in graphene with magnetic fields was undertaken some time ago by Castillo-Celeita and Fernández
[
37
]
by employing first-order intertwining relations. Because the vector potential is static, choosing a two-component separated form of the corresponding wave function

5
Isochronous potentials

Let us examine the implications of a planar electron in the presence of a linear vector potential

-\hbar^{2}\partial^{2}\psi^{q}(x)+\Big[\big(2\beta-q-\mathcal{E}\big)+x^{2}+\frac{1}{x^{2}}\big(\beta^{2}+q\beta\big)\Big]\psi^{q}(x)=0,\quad q=\pm

Comparing with the general differential equation

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}-\frac{a^{2}x^{4}+bx^{2}+c}{x^{2}}=0

whose general solution is given by

\displaystyle y=a^{\frac{1}{2}(1\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+c})}x^{(\frac{1}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+c})}e^{-\frac{ax^{2}}{2}}{}_{1}U_{1}\Bigg[{\frac{b}{4a}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(1\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+c}\Big),1\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+c};ax^{2}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\frac{b}{4a}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(1\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+c}\Big)=-n\ \textit{where $n=0,1,2,......$ }

In the present case the relevant connection provided by

\displaystyle a=1,\quad b=\big(2\beta-q-\mathcal{E}\big),\quad c=\big(\beta^{2}+q\beta\big)

As a result the energy eigenvalues are determined to be

6
Concluding remarks

Taking into account the presence of scalar and pseudoscalar potentials, the Dirac Hamiltonian
H_{D}
is explored from a supersymmetric point of view. The main highlight of our approach is that we refrain from using the intertwining relations as hitherto been generally probed in the literature, but instead, noting that the Dirac equation is only a first-order differential equation, construct a quasi-Hamiltonian
\mathcal{K}
, defined as the square of
H_{D}
, that offers the typical
N=2
features of
SQM
. To be specific, the quasi-Hamiltonian
\mathcal{K}
has a
2\times 2
structure that reveals a SUSY-like diagonal form when certain suitable approximations are made. Its elements are then seen to be dictated by an underlying superpotential W making our procedure easily tractable. We apply our scheme to the transformed one-dimensional zero-mass Dirac Hamiltonian, which describes the behaviour of graphene under the action of an external magnetic field, to read out the operating partner Hamiltonians. We illustrate our approach for the specific cases of isochronous potentials by projecting out the supersymmetric component Hamiltonians and evaluate the accompanying energy spectra.

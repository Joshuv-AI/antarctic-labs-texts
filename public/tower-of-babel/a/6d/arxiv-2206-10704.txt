Abstract
. In the first part of this paper, we generalize Dirac reduction to the extent of non-local Poisson vertex superalgebra and non-local SUSY Poisson vertex algebra cases. Next, we modify this reduction so that we explain the structures of classical W-superalgebras and SUSY classical W-algebras in terms of the modified Dirac reduction.



1.
Introduction

Dirac reductions of a Poisson algebra were first introduced in the paper
[
17
]
of Dirac. For a Poisson algebra
(P,\{\ ,\ \})
and a finite subset
\{\theta_{i}|\,i\in I\}\subset P
, if the
|I|\times|I|
matrix
C
with the
ij
-entry
C_{ij}=\{\theta_{i},\theta_{j}\}
is invertible, then

(1.1)

\{a,b\}^{D}:=\{a,b\}-\sum_{i\in I}\{a,\theta_{i}\}(C^{-1})_{ij}\{\theta_{j},b\},\quad a,b\in P

defines a new Poisson bracket called Dirac reduced bracket. Since this new bracket satisfies
\{\theta_{i},P\}^{D}=0
for any
i\in I
, it induces a Poisson bracket on
P/I_{P}
, where
I_{P}
is the associative algebra ideal generated by
\{\theta_{i}|i\in I\}
.
Moreover, in
[
15
]
, De Sole-Kac-Valeri generalized the notion to Poisson vertex algebras (PVAs) cases. The Dirac reduced bracket of a Poisson vertex algebra
(\mathcal{P},\{\ {}_{\lambda}\ \})
associated with the finite subset
\{\theta_{i}|i\in I\}\subset\mathcal{P}
is defined by

(1.2)

\left\{a\,_{\lambda}\,b\right\}^{D}=\left\{a\,_{\lambda}\,b\right\}-\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I}\left\{\theta_{j}\,{}_{\lambda+\partial}\,b\right\}_{\rightarrow}(C^{-1})_{ji}(\lambda+\partial)\left\{a\,_{\lambda}\,\theta_{i}\right\},\quad a,b\in\mathcal{P},

provided that the
|I|\times|I|
-matrix
C(\lambda)=(\{\theta_{j}{}_{\lambda}\theta_{i}\})_{i,j\in I}
is invertible with respect to the product
A(\lambda)\circ B(\lambda)=A(\lambda+\partial)B(\lambda)
and
(C^{-1})(\lambda)
is the inverse of
C(\lambda)
. Similarly to the Poisson algebra case, the bracket
\{\ {}_{\lambda}\ \}^{D}
induces a Poisson vertex algebra bracket on
\mathcal{P}/I_{\mathcal{P}}
, where
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is the differential algebra ideal generated by
\{\theta_{i}|i\in I\}
.

Integrable Hamiltonian systems have been studied in the framework of PVAs by the authors in
[
4
,
12
,
14
]
and
Dirac reductions are useful to obtain new systems
[
10
]
. For example, the bi-Poisson structure of the Gelfand-Dickey hierarchy associated with the Lax operator
L=\partial^{n}+u_{2}\partial^{n-2}+\cdots+u_{n}
can be understood as a Dirac reduction of the Poisson structure induced from
\tilde{L}=\partial^{n}+u_{1}\partial^{n-1}+u_{2}\partial^{n-2}+\cdots+u_{n}
(see
[
1
,
21
,
20
]
). Moreover, if we admit non-local Poisson structures for Hamiltonian equations, more systems can be understood by Dirac reductions. See
[
10
]
for detailed explanations and examples.

The third author described in
[
34
]
integrable Hamiltonian systems associated with a certain family of Poisson vertex superalgebras (PVSAs). Hence a natural question is whether we can get reduced systems via super-analogue of non-local PVAs and Dirac reductions. All the notions and properties for non-local PVAs in
[
10
]
can be generalized to the super-analogue by Koszul-Quillen sign rule. On the other hand, we introduce in Definition
2.6

the Dirac reduction for PVSA
\mathcal{P}
.
The main idea is that we consider
C(\lambda)=(\{\theta_{j}{}_{\lambda}\theta_{i}\})_{i,j\in I}
as an element in
\mathfrak{gl}_{(m|n)}\otimes\mathcal{P}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)
where
m
and
n
are the number of even and odd indices in
I
. If
C(\lambda)
is invertible
via the multiplication in (
2.7
), then the Dirac reduction of the Poisson
\lambda
-bracket
\left\{\ \,{}_{\lambda}\,\ \right\}
on
\mathcal{P}
associated with
\theta_{I}=\{\theta_{i}|i\in I\}
is given by

(1.3)

\left\{a\,_{\lambda}\,b\right\}^{D}=\left\{a\,_{\lambda}\,b\right\}-\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(p(a)+p(j))(p(b)+p(i))+p(i)+p(j)}\left\{\theta_{j}\,{}_{\lambda+\partial}\,b\right\}_{\rightarrow}(C^{-1})_{ji}(\lambda+\partial)\left\{a\,_{\lambda}\,\theta_{i}\right\},

where
a,b
are homogeneous elements in
\mathcal{P}
and
p(i)=p(\theta_{i})
. In Theorem
2.9
and Theorem
2.10
, we show the following statements.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\left<\theta_{I}\right>
be the differential superalgebra ideal of
\mathcal{P}
generated by
\theta_{I}
.

(1)

The bracket
\left\{\ \,{}_{\lambda}\,\ \right\}^{D}
in (
1.3
) gives another PVSA structure on
\mathcal{P}
. In other words, it satisfies the sesquilinearity, skewsymmetry, Jacobi identity and Leibniz rule for PVSAs.

(2)

The Dirac reduced bracket induces a PVSA
\lambda
-bracket on
\mathcal{P}/\left<\theta_{I}\right>
.

Furthermore, we also consider supersymmetric (SUSY) PVAs, which are PVSAs with an odd derivation
D
. SUSY vertex algebras and SUSY PVAs introduced in

22
]
to provide algebraic frameworks for SUSY conformal field theory. Moreover, some superintegrable Hamiltonian systems can be understood in terms of SUSY PVAs. See
[

\frac{du}{dt}=D^{6}u+3D^{2}uDu+3uD^{3}u=\{uDu\,{}_{\Lambda}\,u\}|_{\chi=\lambda=0},

where

In spite of the fact that the second Poisson structure of the super-KdV has a non-local property, non-local super Hamiltonian operators have not been studied in the theory of SUSY PVAs. See
[
27
,
29
,
31
]
for the SUSY bi-Poisson structure of the super KdV.

\Lambda

\mathscr{P}

(1.5)

\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{D}=\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}-\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(p(a)+j)(p(b)+i)}\{\theta_{j}\,{}_{\Lambda+\nabla}\,b\}_{\rightarrow}(C^{-1})_{ji}(\Lambda+\nabla)\{a\,{}_{\Lambda}\,\theta_{i}\}

for homogeneous

holds. See Theorem

Let
\left<\theta_{I}\right>

\mathscr{P}

\theta_{I}
.

(1)

The bracket
\left\{\ \,{}_{\Lambda}\,\ \right\}^{D}
in (

\mathscr{P}
. In other words, it satisfies the sesquilinearity, skewsymmetry, Jacobi identity and Leibniz rule for SUSY PVAs.

(2)

\mathscr{P}

In the second part of this paper, we deal with classical W-algebras. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra with a Dynkin grading
\bigoplus_{i\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}}\mathfrak{g}(i)
associated with an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{E,H,F\}
and a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form
(\,|\,)
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}{(i)}
, where
\mathcal{L}
is a Lagrangian subspace with respect to the skew symmetric bilinear form
\left<\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\right>:=(F|[\cdot,\cdot])
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. The classical finite W-algebras
\mathcal{W}^{\text{fin}}(\mathfrak{g},F)
is a Hamiltonian reduction of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\simeq S(\mathfrak{g})
via the the moment map
\mu:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{m}^{*}
at the regular value
\chi|_{\mathfrak{m}}
for
\chi=(F|\,\cdot)\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. It is known that, for any isotropic subspace
\ell
, if we take
\mathfrak{n}:=\ell^{\perp}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}{(i)}
and replace
\mathfrak{m}
by
\mathfrak{m}:=\ell\oplus\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}{(i)}
then

\mathcal{W}^{\text{fin}}(\mathfrak{g},F)=\{w\in S(\mathfrak{p})\,|\,\rho(\{n,w\})=0\text{ for }n\in\mathfrak{n}\}.

Here
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
and
\rho(a)=a_{\mathfrak{p}}+(F|a)
for
a\in\mathfrak{g}
,
a_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{p}
and
a_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
a=a_{\mathfrak{m}}+a_{\mathfrak{p}}
. As an affine analogue, Drinfeld-Sokolov
[
18
]
introduced the classical affine W-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},F)
as a Hamiltonian reduction of the affine PVA
\mathcal{V}^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Note that, in this paper, we fix
\ell=0
. Hence,
\mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{i>0}\mathfrak{g}(i)
and
\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}(i)
.

Kac-Roan-Wakimoto
[
24
]
introduced W-algebras associated with Lie superalgebras and the corresponding classical finite W-algebras were established in
[
9
]
, which we call W-superalgebras in this paper.
For a finite simple Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a nondegenerate supersymmetric invariant even bilinear form
(\ |\ )
, let us take an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(E,H,F)
, which gives rise to the Dynkin grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}}\mathfrak{g}(i)
.
The classical W-superalgebra

\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},F)=\{\,w\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{p})\,|\,\rho\{n\,{}_{\lambda}\,w\}=0\text{ for }n\in\mathfrak{n}\,\}

is a PVSA endowed with the
\lambda
-bracket induced from
\mathcal{V}^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
. Here
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{p})
is the differential superalgebra generated by
\mathfrak{p}
. In order to describe free generators of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},F)
, consider a homogeneous basis
\{\,v_{i}\,|\,i\in J^{F}\}
of
\ker(\text{ad}F)=:\mathfrak{g}^{F}
.
Then there is a unique element
\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},F)
such that
\omega_{i}-v_{i}\in\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{g}^{F})\otimes(\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes[E,\mathfrak{g}_{<0}])^{\otimes n}
for the differential superalgebra
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{g}^{F})
generated by
\mathfrak{g}^{F}
. Then the subset
\{\omega_{i}\,|\,i\in J_{F}\}
freely generates
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},F)
as a differential superalgebra.
Moreover, in
[
35
,
36
]
, the third author computed the summand
\gamma_{i}^{1}
of
\omega_{i}-v_{i}
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{g}^{F})\otimes(\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes[E,\mathfrak{g}_{<0}])
. Using the explicit formula of
\gamma_{i}^{1}
, the
\lambda
-bracket between the generators can be expressed with elements in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda]\otimes\mathbb{C}[\partial^{n}\omega_{i}\,|\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,i\in I]
.

2.
Dirac reduction of Poisson (vertex) superalgebras

A
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is called a
vector superspace
and its homogeneous element
a\in V_{\bar{\imath}}
is called
even
(resp.
odd
) if
i=0
(resp.
i=1
). Denote the
parity
of
a\in V_{\bar{\imath}}
by
\tilde{a}:=i
. A linear operator
\phi:V\rightarrow V
is called
even
if
\phi(V_{\bar{\imath}})\subset V_{\bar{\imath}}
and
odd
if
\phi(V_{\bar{\imath}})\subset V_{\bar{\imath}+\bar{1}}
. A
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A
is called a
superalgebra
if
A
is a vector superspace satisfying
A_{\bar{\imath}}A_{\bar{\jmath}}\subset A_{\bar{\imath}+\bar{\jmath}}
for
i,j\in\{0,1\}
. If there is a linear operator
d
on a superalgebra
A
with satisfying

d(ab)=d(a)b+(-1)^{\tilde{d}\tilde{a}}ad(b)

for
a,b\in A
, then
A
is called a
differential superalgebra
with a
derivation

d
. Here and further, for simplicity, we just call the tuple
(A,d)
a
differential algebra
. In the rest of the paper, whenever the parity
\tilde{a}
of an element
a
in a vector superspace is considered, we assume that
a
is homogeneous even though it is not mentioned.

2.1.
Non-local Poisson vertex superalgebras

A vector superspace
\mathfrak{g}
endowed with a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a
Lie superalgebra
if it satisfies

•

(skewsymmetry)
[a,b]=-(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}[b,a]
,

•

(Jacobi identity)
(-1)^{\tilde{c}\tilde{a}}[a,[b,c]]+(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}[b,[c,a]]+(-1)^{\tilde{b}\tilde{c}}[c,[a,b]]=0

for
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}
. If a Lie superalgebra
(P,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a unital supercommutative associative algebra with the Leibniz rule:

(2.1)

\left\{a,bc\right\}=\left\{a,b\right\}c+(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}b\left\{a,c\right\}

for
a,b,c\in P
, then
P
is called a
Poisson superalgebra.

To define a non-local Poisson vertex superalgebra in a similar manner, let us introduce an admissible non-local
\lambda
-bracket. See
[
10
]
for detailed properties of non-local Poisson vertex algebras.
On a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{R}
, a
non-local
\lambda
-bracket
is a parity preserving bilinear map
[\cdot\,{}_{\lambda}\,\cdot]:\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)
satisfying the
sesquilinearity:

(2.2)

[\partial a_{\lambda}b]=-\lambda[a_{\lambda}b],\ [a_{\lambda}\partial b]=(\lambda+\partial)[a_{\lambda}b]

for
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
. We denote by
[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{n}a_{(n)}b
for
a_{(n)}b\in\mathcal{R}
.

Consider a superspace

(2.3)

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\mu}:=\mathcal{R}[\![\lambda^{-1},\mu^{-1},(\lambda+\mu)^{-1}]\!][\lambda,\mu]

and let
\iota_{\mu,\lambda}:\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)(\!(\mu^{-1})\!)
be defined by the geometric expansion of
(\lambda+\mu)^{m}
for an integer
m
in the domain
|\mu|>|\lambda|
.
Then we can identify
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\mu}
with the image
\iota_{\mu,\lambda}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\mu})\subset\mathcal{R}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)(\!(\mu^{-1})\!)
.
If a non-local
\lambda
-bracket on
\mathcal{R}
satisfies

[a{}_{\lambda}[b{}_{\mu}c]]\in\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\mu}

for all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{R}
, then it is said to be
admissible
.
If a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{R}
and an admissible
\lambda
-bracket
[\cdot\,{}_{\lambda}\,\cdot]
are given to satisfy the following axioms:

•

(skewsymmetry)
[b_{\lambda}a]=-(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}{}_{\leftarrow}[a\,_{-\lambda-\partial}\,b]
in
\mathcal{R}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!),

•

(Jacobi identity)
[a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]]=[[a_{\lambda}b]\,_{\lambda+\mu}\,c]+(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}[b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]]
in
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\mu}

for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{R}
. We call
\mathcal{R}
a
non-local Lie conformal superalgebra
(
LCA
). The skewsymmetry can be rewritten as

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda^{n}b_{(n)}a=-(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-\lambda-\partial)^{n}a_{(n)}b=-(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\binom{n}{r}(-1)^{n}(\partial^{r}a_{(n)}b)\lambda^{n-r}

and the three terms in the Jacobi identity are in
\mathcal{R}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)(\!(\mu^{-1})\!)
,
\mathcal{R}(\!((\lambda+\mu)^{-1})\!)(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)
and
\mathcal{R}(\!(\mu^{-1})\!)(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)
, respectively. By the admissibility, the LHS and RHS of Jacobi identity can be compared in
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda,\mu}
via the identification
\iota_{\mu,\lambda}
,
\iota_{\lambda,\lambda+\mu}
and
\iota_{\mu,\lambda}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
non-local Poisson vertex superalgebra
(
non-local PVSA
or just

(left Leibniz rule)

The supersymmetric algebra generated by a superspace

(Master formula of non-local PVSA).
For the differential algebra

W-superalgebras and modified Dirac reduction of PVSAs

In this section, inspired by the definition of Dirac reduced bracket, we define a modified Dirac reduced bracket on a quotient space of PVSA. We also describe Poisson structures of W-superalgebras as modified Dirac reductions of affine PVSAs. For the further properties of W-superalgebras, we refer to

,

]

4.1.
Modified Dirac reduction of PVSAs

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a differential algebra freely generated by a finite homogeneous set
\theta_{J}:=\{\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in J\}
with an even derivation
\partial
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}\simeq\mathbb{C}[\partial^{n}\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in J,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}]
.
To simplify notations, we assume that the index set
J
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
and
j\in J
is even (resp. odd) if
\theta_{j}
is even (resp. odd).
Consider the differential algebra ideal
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{P}
generated by
\theta_{I}:=\{\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in I\subset J\}
and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}:=\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{I}
be the quotient algebra. Then we can identify

(4.1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\simeq\mathbb{C}[\partial^{n}\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in J\setminus I,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}]

as differential algebras.

From now on we simply write as
\text{Mat}_{I}=\text{Mat}_{(r|s)}
and
\text{Id}_{I}=\text{Id}_{(r|s)}
,
where
r
(resp.
s
) denotes the number of even (resp. odd) elements in
\theta_{I}
. Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda):=\text{Mat}_{I}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)
is a unital associative algebra with respect to the product
\circ
in (
2.7
). Then
\text{Id}_{I}\otimes 1\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
for the identity matrix
\text{Id}_{I}\in\text{Mat}_{I}
is the unity and an element
\widetilde{A}(\lambda)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
is called
invertible
if there exists
\widetilde{A}^{-1}(\lambda)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
such that

(4.2)

\widetilde{A}(\lambda)\circ\widetilde{A}^{-1}(\lambda)=\widetilde{A}^{-1}(\lambda)\circ\widetilde{A}(\lambda)=\text{Id}_{I}\otimes 1.

Now we suppose
\mathcal{P}
is a PVSA endowed with the Poisson bracket
\{\cdot{}_{\lambda}\cdot\}
and consider

(4.3)

\widetilde{C}(\lambda):=\sum_{i,j\in I}e_{ij}\otimes\pi\{\theta_{j}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\theta_{i}\}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda),

where
\pi:\mathcal{P}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)
is the canonical quotient map.

Definition 4.1
.

Assume that
\widetilde{C}(\lambda)
in (
4.3
) is invertible and
\widetilde{C}^{-1}(\lambda)=\left(\widetilde{C}_{ij}^{-1}(\lambda)\right)_{i,j\in I}
is its inverse. Then the

\pi\{\cdot{}_{\lambda}\cdot\}^{D}:\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)

associated with

(4.4)

\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}^{D}=\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}-\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(\tilde{a}+i)(\tilde{b}+j)+i+j}\pi\{\theta_{i}{}_{\lambda+\partial}b\}_{\rightarrow}\,(\widetilde{C}^{-1})_{ij}(\lambda+\partial)\,\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}\theta_{j}\}

. By (

), the RHS of (

) is computed regarding

Note that the bracket

in (

) is not necessarily a PVSA
\lambda
-bracket. Especially, the Jacobi identity is not guaranteed
since
\pi
is not a LCA homomorphism. In other words,

may not be same as

However, other axioms of PVSA hold for

Proposition 4.2
.

If the modified Dirac reduced bracket in (
4.4
) satisfies the Jacobi identity then

is a PVSA.

Proof.

We need to show that (

) satisfies the sesquilinearity, skew-symmetry and Leibniz rule. Since

is differential algebra homomorphism, these properties can be proved by the similar proof to Theorem
2.9
.
∎

Now we close this section with the following lemma, which is used to describe
the PVSA structures of W-superalgebras using modified Dirac reductions.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\theta^{\prime}_{I}:=\{\theta^{\prime}_{j}\,|\,j\in I\}
be another basis of a vector superspace

spanned by
\theta_{I}
. Suppose

in (

) is invertible, then
\overline{C}(\lambda)=\big(\pi\{\theta_{j}{}_{\lambda}\theta^{\prime}_{i}\}\big)_{i,j\in I}
is invertible and

) can be written as

(4.5)

\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}^{D}=\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}-\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(\tilde{a}+i)(\tilde{b}+j)+i+j}\pi\{\theta_{i}{}_{\lambda+\partial}b\}_{\rightarrow}\,\overline{C}^{-1}_{ij}(\lambda+\partial)\,\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}\theta^{\prime}_{j}\}.

Proof.

Take the invertible matrix

with

satisfying

\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes\theta^{\prime}_{i}=K\,\Big(\sum_{j\in I}e_{j}\otimes\theta_{j}\Big).

Observe that

and
\widetilde{C}(\lambda)
is invertible. Hence the matrix

is also invertible and

(4.7)

\overline{C}^{-1}(\lambda+\partial)=\widetilde{C}^{-1}(\lambda+\partial)K^{-1}.

By (
4.6
) and (

), we have

(4.8)

\sum_{j\in I}\big(\widetilde{C}^{-1}(\lambda+\partial)K^{-1}\big)\big(K(e_{j}\otimes\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}\theta_{j}\})\big)=\sum_{j\in I}\overline{C}^{-1}(\lambda+\partial)(e_{j}\otimes\otimes\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}\theta^{\prime}_{j}\}).

By (
4.8
), we obtain

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{\tilde{a}\tilde{b}+\tilde{a}j}\left(e_{i}^{T}\otimes\pi\{\theta_{i}{}_{\lambda+\partial}b\}_{\rightarrow}\right)\widetilde{C}^{-1}(\lambda+\partial)\left(e_{j}\otimes\pi\{a{}_{\lambda}\theta_{j}\}\right)

The last terms of (

) and (
4.5

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

5.
SUSY W-algebras and modified Dirac reduction of SUSY PVAs

In this section, we define a modified Dirac reduced bracket on a quotient space of SUSY PVA. We also show that SUSY W-algebras can be described by modified Dirac reductions of SUSY affine PVAs. For further properties of SUSY W-algebras, we refer to
[
30
,
35
]
.

5.1.
Modified Dirac reduction of SUSY PVAs

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a differential algebra freely generated by a finite homogeneous set
\theta_{J}:=\{\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in J\}
with an odd derivation
D
, i.e.,
\mathscr{P}\simeq\mathbb{C}[D^{n}\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in J,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}]
. Assume that the index set
J
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
and
j\in J
is even (resp. odd) if
\theta_{j}
is even (resp. odd).
Consider the differential algebra ideal
\mathscr{I}
of
\mathscr{P}
generated by
\theta_{I}:=\{\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in I\subset J\}
and let
\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}:=\mathscr{P}/\mathscr{I}
be the quotient algebra. Then we can identify

(5.1)

\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}\simeq\mathbb{C}[D^{n}\theta_{j}\,|\,j\in J\setminus I,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}]

as differential algebras.
The space
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}(\Lambda):=\text{Mat}_{I}\otimes\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}(\!(\Lambda^{-1})\!)
is an associative algebra with unity
\text{Id}_{I}\otimes 1
for the product defined in (
3.10
). An invertible element in
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}(\Lambda)
is also defined as in (
3.11
). Now we suppose that
\mathscr{P}
is a SUSY PVA with the
\Lambda
-bracket
\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}
and consider the following odd element

(5.2)

\widetilde{C}(\Lambda):=\sum_{i,j\in I}e_{ij}\otimes\pi\{\theta_{j}{}_{\Lambda}\theta_{i}\}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}(\Lambda)

where
\pi:\mathscr{P}(\!(\Lambda^{-1})\!)\to\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}(\!(\Lambda^{-1})\!)
is the canonical quotient map of differential algebras.

Definition 5.1
.

Assume that
\widetilde{C}(\Lambda)
in (
5.2
) is invertible and let
\widetilde{C}^{-1}(\Lambda)\in\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}(\Lambda)
be its inverse. Then the
modified Dirac reduced bracket on
\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}
associated with
\theta_{I}
is a bilinear map

(5.3)

\pi\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}^{D}:\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}\times\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}\,\longrightarrow\,\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}(\!(\Lambda^{-1})\!)

given by

(5.4)

\pi\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{D}=\pi\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}-\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(\tilde{a}+j)(\tilde{b}+i)}\pi\{\theta_{j}\,{}_{\Lambda+\nabla}\,b\}_{\rightarrow}\widetilde{C}^{-1}_{ji}(\Lambda+\nabla)\pi\{a\,{}_{\Lambda}\,\theta_{i}\},

where
\widetilde{C}^{-1}_{ij}(\Lambda)
denotes the

-entry of
\widetilde{C}^{-1}(\Lambda)
and
a,b
are elements in
\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}.
Regard
a,b\in\mathbb{C}[D^{n}\theta_{j}\,|j\in J\setminus I,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}]
, when we compute the RHS.

In general, the modified bracket (
5.4
) does not give a SUSY PVA structure on
\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}
. More precisely, the modified
\Lambda
-bracket satisfies the sesquilinearity, skew-symmetry and Leibniz rule, but Jacobi identity for SUSY PVAs. That is because the quotient map
\pi
is only a differential algebra homomorphism, not a SUSY LCA algebra homomorphism.

Now we introduce the SUSY analogue of Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\theta^{\prime}_{I}:=\{\theta^{\prime}_{j}\,|\,j\in I\}
be another basis of a vector superspace

spanned by

and suppose

If

in (

) is invertible, then

is invertible and

\pi\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{D}=\pi\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}-\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(\tilde{a}+j)(\tilde{b}+i)}\pi\{\theta_{j}{}_{\Lambda+\nabla}b\}_{\rightarrow}\,\overline{C}^{-1}_{ji}(\Lambda+\nabla)\,\pi\{a{}_{\Lambda}\theta^{\prime}_{i}\}.

Proof.

A similar proof to Lemma
4.3
works.
∎

5.2.

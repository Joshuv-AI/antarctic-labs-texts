ACTION OF WEYL GROUP ON EQUIVARIANT K-THEORY OF FLAG VARIETIES
MIESZKO BASZCZAK

Abstract
We describe the action of the Weyl group of a semi simple linear group G G on cohomological and K-theoretic invariants of the generalized flag variety G / B G/B . We study the automorphism s i s_{i} , induced by the reflection in the simple root, on the equivariant K K -theory ring K T ​ ( G / B ) K_{T}(G/B) using divided difference operators. Using the localization theorem for torus action and Borel presentation for the equivariant K-theory ring, we calculate the formula for this automorphism. Moreover, we expand this formula in the basis consisting of structure sheaves classes of Schubert varieties. We provide effective formula (applying properties of Weyl groups) for the approximation of this expansion, more specifically for the part corresponding to Schubert varieties with the fixed dimension, which in the case of G G being a special linear group is more exact. Finally, we discuss the above-mentioned formula in the basis of motivic Chern classes of Schubert varieties.

Let
G
be a complex semi-simple Lie group. Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
and
G/B
the associated full flag variety. The fundamental classes of Schubert varieties in

form a basis of (equivariant) cohomology, treated as an algebra over the (equivariant) cohomology of a point. Similarly, the equivariant

-theory ring

[
Uma14
]
, has a basis consisting of structure sheaves classes of Schubert varieties, treated as an algebra over
K_{T}(pt)

In both of these rings, there are many positivity properties. Such properties depict different invariants in different bases. From the most fundamental ones (
[
Bri02
]
in

-theory and
[
AGM11
]
in equivariant

-theory), through Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson classes positivity conjecture in ordinary cohomology (
[

]

[

]
) as well as motivic Chern classes positivity conjecture (
[

]
), both strongly related to the automorphism
s_{i}
, which is induced by the reflection in a simple root of the Weyl group. Hence, an interesting question arises: how to describe this action on the basis of Schubert varieties? In cohomology, the answer is known and uses the Chevalley formula (as in
[
AM16
]
). In

-theory, however, the situation is a bit more complicated.

In this paper, we show that using the Chevalley formula one can calculate a part of the support of the automorphism

\displaystyle s_{i}^{coh}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}+c_{1}([\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{i})])\partial_{i}^{coh}

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]\cdot\mathrm{id}-([\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]-1)\partial_{i}^{K}

s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])=\begin{cases}a_{\omega s_{i}}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]+\sum_{\beta\in C_{i}}a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}]+l.o.t.&\text{if } $l(\omega s_{i})>l(\omega)$\\
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where l.o.t. stands for lower order terms (see the explanation of the notion in Theorem

). Moreover, for the special case of

we derive formula (Equation (

))

\displaystyle s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])

\displaystyle=(1-e^{\omega(\alpha_{i})})[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]

\displaystyle-\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha_{i}\text{ positive root such that }l(\omega s_{i}s_{\beta})=l(\omega)\text{ and }\langle\alpha_{i},\beta\rangle>0}e^{-\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha_{i}\text{ positive root such that }l(\omega s_{i}s_{\beta})=l(\omega)\text{ and }\langle\alpha_{i},\beta\rangle<0}e^{\omega(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}]

1
Preliminaries

1.1
Equivariant cohomology and equivariant
K
-theory

Let
X
be a quasi-projective complex algebraic variety. Let
H_{*}(Y)
denote the Borel-Moore homology group and
H^{*}(X)
the cohomology ring, both with rational coefficients. Every subvariety
Y\subseteq X
of complex dimension
k
defines
a fundamental class

[Y]\in H_{2k}(X)
(
[
Ful97
, Appendix B.3]
). For
X
smooth we identify the Borel-Moore homology and cohomology via the Poincare duality.

Suppose a torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
acts on the variety
X
.
The
T
-equivariant cohomology ring of
X
is defined as the cohomology of the quotient
H_{T}^{*}(X)=H^{*}(ET\times^{T}X,\mathbb{Q})
and
ET
is the total space of the universal
T
-bundle. This ring is an algebra over the ring
H^{*}_{T}(pt)
, which is equal to

H^{*}_{T}(pt)=H^{*}(ET/T,\mathbb{Q})=H^{*}((\mathbb{P}^{\infty})^{n},\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}].

Moreover, this ring can be identified with the polynomial ring
Sym_{\mathbb{Q}}\Xi(T)
via the map
\lambda\to c_{1}(\mathcal{K}(\lambda))
, where
\Xi(T)
is the group of characters of
T
and
\mathcal{K}(\lambda)
is the linear bundle
ET\times^{T}\mathbb{C}\to ET/T
. Given a
T
-invariant subvariety
Y
we define its fundamental class
[Y]_{T}
as the class of the subvariety
E_{n}\times^{T}Y
of
E_{n}\times^{T}X
for sufficiently large
n
, where
E_{n}
is an approximation of the universal
T
-bundle.

We can consider three
K
-theories on
X
- the
K
-theory of coherent equivariant sheaves, the
K
-theory of locally free equivariant sheaves and the
K
-theory of equivariant topological vector bundles. If
X
is smooth, then the first and the second are isomorphic (
[
Tho87
, Theorem 5.7]
). Moreover, if
X
is a finite union of orbits, the natural map (forgetting the structure of a vector bundle) is an isomorphism between the second and the third (
[
FRW21
, Theorem 9.1]
). In this case, we denote by
K_{T}(X)

the equivariant
K
-theory ring of
X
.
K_{T}(X)
is an algebra over
K_{T}(pt)
, which is equal to
R(T)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\Xi(T)]
.

1.2
Flag varieties, Schubert varieties and their classes

Let
G
be a connected, complex, semi-simple Lie group,
B_{+}
a fixed Borel subgroup,
B_{-}
the opposite Borel subgroup and
T\subseteq B_{+}
a maximal torus. From now on, we denote
B_{+}
by
B
. Let
W=N(T)/T
be the Weyl group and
l\text{: }W\to\mathbb{N}
the length function. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
denote the associated set of simple roots. We denote by
s_{i}
the reflection in the root
\alpha_{i}
and by
P_{i}
the minimal parabolic subgroup corresponding to the simple root
\alpha_{i}
.

The quotient
X:=G/B
is called
the full flag variety
for
G
and
B
defined as above. The isomorphism
G/B\simeq G_{comp}/G_{comp}\cap T
, where
G_{comp}
is a suitably chosen maximal compact subgroup of
G
, implies that the Weyl group acts on
G/B
by right multiplication. The foundation of the Schubert calculus is a well known partition called the Bruhat decomposition (
[
Bor91
, Section 14.12]
),

G/B=\bigsqcup_{\omega\in W}B\tilde{\omega}B/B

for
\tilde{\omega}
being a representative of
\omega\in NT/T
in
NT
. The
Schubert cell
X_{\omega}^{\circ}
is defined as
B\tilde{\omega}B/B
. The
Schubert varieties
X_{\omega}
are defined as
\overline{B\tilde{\omega}B/B}
(closure in Zariski topology). Because these varieties are
T
-invariant, one can take their fundamental classes
[X_{\omega}]_{T}
, and these classes form a
H_{T}^{*}(pt)
-basis of
H_{T}^{*}(G/B)
. The situation is similar when we study
K_{T}(G/B)
. The structure sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{\omega}
of
X_{\omega}
(which are
T
-equivariant) define classes
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]
in
K_{T}(G/B)
that form a
K_{T}(pt)
-basis of
K_{T}(G/B)
.

1.3
Equivariant cohomology and equivariant
K
-theory of flag varieties

Definition 1.1
.

The Berstain-Gelfand-Gelfand operator
\partial_{i}^{coh}:H^{*}_{T}(G/B)\to H^{*}_{T}(G/B)
is defined as

\partial_{i}^{coh}(u)=p_{i}^{*}p_{i*}(u),

where
p_{i}\text{: }G/B\to G/P_{i}
is the projection and
u
is an element of
H^{*}_{T}(G/B)
.

The Demazure operators
\partial_{i}^{K}
on
K_{T}(G/B)
were defined in
[
Dem74
]
. They are analogs of the Bernstain-Gelfand-Gelfand operators.

Definition 1.2
.

The Demazure operator
\partial_{i}^{K}:K_{T}(G/B)\to K_{T}(G/B)
is defined as

\partial_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{U}])=p_{i}^{*}p_{i*}([\mathcal{U}]),

where
p_{i}:G/B\to G/P_{i}
is the projection and
[\mathcal{U}]
is an element of
K_{T}(G/B)
.

The Berstain-Gelfand-Gelfand operator and the Demazure operator satisfy the following.

Proposition 1.3

(
[
BGG73
, Theorem 3.12]
)
.

\partial_{i}^{coh}([X_{\omega}])=\begin{cases}[X_{\omega s_{i}}]&\text{if $l(\omega s_{i})>l(\omega)$},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proposition 1.4

(
[
Dem74
, Theorem 2]
)
.

\partial_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])=\begin{cases}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]&\text{if $l(\omega s_{i})>l(\omega)$},\\
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let us denote the ring
H^{*}_{T}(pt)
by
S
and take an element
\lambda\in\Xi(T)
. This element can be extended to a character
\tilde{\lambda}
of
B
by composing it with the projection
B\to T
. One can take a line bundle
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)=G\times\mathbb{C}/B=G\times\mathbb{C}/(gb,z)\sim(g,\tilde{\lambda}(b)z)
over
G/B
which is
G
-equivariant, hence
T
-equivariant. Furthermore, there is a morphism

\displaystyle\Xi(T)

\displaystyle\to H^{*}_{T}(X)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\to c_{1}^{T}(\mathcal{L}(\lambda)).

This assignment induces a morphism of rings
c^{T}\text{: }S\to H_{T}^{*}(G/B)
.

Lemma 1.5
.

[
AS17
, Section 1.3]

There is an isomorphism called the Borel presentation of
H^{*}_{T}(G/B)

S\otimes_{S^{W}}S\to H^{*}_{T}(G/B)

defined as
f\otimes g\to f\cdot c^{T}(g)
.

The map
\gamma:R(T)\to K_{T}(G/B)
induced by the assignment
\mathbb{Z}[\Xi(T)]\supseteq\Xi(T)\ni\lambda\to[\mathcal{L}(\lambda)]\in K_{T}(G/B)
, leads to the following lemma called
the Borel’s presentation
of
K_{T}(G/B)
.

Lemma 1.6
.

[
HLS10
, Theorem 4.4]

There is an isomorphism

R(T)\otimes_{R(T)^{W}}R(T)\to K_{T}(G/B)

defined as
f\otimes g\to f\cdot\gamma(g)
.

1.4
Motivic Chern classes

In this section, we restrict our attention only to equivariant motivic Chern classes of Schubert cells. For a general definition see e.g.
[
FRW21
]
. We define the equivariant motivic Chern class of Schubert cell
X_{\omega}^{\circ}
in equivariant
K
-theory as follows.

Definition 1.7
.

The equivariant motivic Chern class of Schubert’s cell
X_{\omega}^{\circ}
for the reduced presentation
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
of
\omega
is defined as

\text{MC}_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})=\mathcal{T}_{i_{1}}\mathcal{T}_{i_{2}}\ldots\mathcal{T}_{i_{n}}([\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]),

where
the
K
-theoretic Demazure-Lusztig operator
\mathcal{T}_{i}
on
K_{T}^{*}(G/B)[y]
is defined as

\mathcal{T}_{i}=\partial_{i}^{K}+y[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{i})]\partial_{i}^{K}-\mathrm{id}.

Remark 1.8

(
[
AMSS22
, Proposition 3.4, Remark 5.4]
)
.

The above defintion does not depend on the choice of presentation of element
\omega
.

The following equality holds (
[
AMSS22
, Corollary 5.2]
):

\mathcal{T}_{i}(\text{MC}_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))=\begin{cases}\text{MC}_{y}(X_{\omega s_{i}}^{\circ})&$\text{ if }l(\omega s_{i})>l(\omega)$\\
-(y+1)\text{MC}_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})-y\text{MC}_{y}(X_{\omega s_{i}}^{\circ})&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

These classes depend on
y
and after setting
y=const
form the basis of
K_{T}^{*}(G/B)
localized in the ideal generated by the elements of the form
(1+ye^{-\alpha_{i}})
. Two important special cases are
y=-1
and
y=0
. In the first case these classes form a
fixed point basis
defined as
[\mathcal{O}_{e_{\omega}}]
where
e_{\omega}:=\widetilde{\omega}B\in(G/B)^{T}
is the fixed point induced by the element
\omega\in W
(
[
AMSS22
, Remark 5.3, Lemma 3.8]
). In the second case the class
\text{MC}_{0}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})
is equal to
the ideal sheaf of the boundary of the Schubert variety
X_{\omega}
defined as
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]-[\mathcal{O}_{\partial X_{\omega}}]
(
[
AMSS22
, Lemma 3.8]
). We will discuss the automorphism
s_{i}
in these bases in Chapter
5
.

2
Action of Weyl group

Remark 2.1
.

Let us treat
\Xi(T)
as a lattice in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is the Cartan subalgebra of the Lie algebra of
G
. By
(-,-)
we denote an inner product induced by the Killing form. Moreover, we define
the coroot
\beta^{\vee}
of the root
\beta
as
2\frac{\beta}{(\beta,\beta)}
. We use the notation
\langle\alpha,\beta^{\vee}\rangle:=2\frac{(\alpha,\beta)}{(\beta,\beta)}
.

For every element
\omega\in W
, we choose its representative
\widetilde{\omega}
in
N(T)
. We define
m_{\omega}\in\Aut(G_{comp}/(G_{comp}\cap T))
as a right hand side multiplication by
\widetilde{\omega}
. This automorphism, which is
T
-equivariant, combined with a homeomorphism
G_{comp}/(G_{comp}\cap T)\approx G/B
, induces (by pullback) automorphism
\omega^{coh}\in\Aut(H^{*}_{T}(G/B))
(
[
MNS22
, Section 3.1]
) and
\omega^{K}\in\Aut(K^{*}_{T}(G/B))
(
[
MNS22
, Section 5.1]
). In next two sections, we will prove formulas for both of these actions for
\omega=s_{i}
.

Remark 2.2
.

There are other actions of
W
on the cohomology ring and
K
-theory ring. Instead of right hand side multiplication, one can take the left hand side multiplication. The induced action is completly described in
[
MNS22
]
. Further, instead of taking pullback above, one can take the pushforward map, but we can easily switch from one case to another, because pullback is adjoint to pushforward.

2.1
Equivariant cohomology

Let
\omega^{S}\text{: }S\to S
be the automorphism acting on the generators of
S
in the same manner as
\omega
acts on the set of simple roots.

Definition 2.3
.

The divided difference operator
\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{coh}\text{: }S\to S
is defined as

\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{coh}(f)=\frac{f-s_{i}^{S}(f)}{-\alpha_{i}}.

Remark 2.4
.

This operator is sometimes defined as
\frac{f-s_{i}^{S}(f)}{\alpha_{i}}
, but for the flag variety
G/B_{-}
. By changing
\alpha_{i}
for
-\alpha_{i}
, we translate this definition to our case.

Remark 2.5
.

The expression
\frac{f-s_{i}^{S}(f)}{-\alpha_{i}}
formally belongs to a field of fractions, but we know that
f-s_{i}^{S}(f)
is divisible by
\alpha_{i}
.

The Bernstain-Gelfand-Gelfand operator is closely related to the divided difference operator. The relation between them is given by the isomorphism from Lemma
1.5

\Phi:S\otimes_{S^{W}}S\to H_{T}^{*}(G/B),

which satisfies the following (
[
Ara89
]
,
[
BGG73
]
for non-equivariant case):

\Phi\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{coh})=\partial_{i}^{coh}\circ\Phi.

Using that fact, we can rewrite the Definition
2.3
:

\displaystyle s_{i}^{S}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}+\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{coh}

\displaystyle\Phi\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes s_{i}^{S})

\displaystyle=\Phi+c_{1}([\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{i})])\partial_{i}^{coh}\circ\Phi

\displaystyle\Phi\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes s_{i}^{S})\circ\Phi^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}+c_{1}([\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{i})])\partial_{i}^{coh}

We give a topological proof of the following key formula (another proof of it is shown in
[
Knu03
]
).

Lemma 2.6
.

\Phi\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes s_{i}^{S})\circ\Phi^{-1}=s_{i}^{coh}
in
\Aut(H_{T}^{*}(G/B))
.

Proof.

The ring
H_{T}^{*}(G/B)
can be treated as a subring of the localized ring
H_{T}^{*}(G/B)_{loc}
, which is defined as the localization of
H_{T}^{*}(G/B)
in

[x]

2.2
Equivariant K-theory

Defining the isobaric divided difference operator on the representation ring
R(T)
is analogous to equivariant cohomology case. Let
\omega^{R}\text{: }R(T)\to R(T)
be the automorphism acting on generators
e^{\lambda}
of
R(T)
by
\omega^{R}(e^{\lambda})=e^{\omega^{S}(\lambda)}
.

Definition 2.7

(
[
HLS10
, Chapter 1]
)
.

The isobaric divided difference operator

\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{K}:R(T)\to R(T)
is defined as

\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{K}(u)=\frac{u-e^{\alpha_{i}}s_{i}^{R}(u)}{1-e^{\alpha_{i}}}.

Remark 2.8
.

In
[
HLS10
]
this operator is defined as
\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{K}(u)=\frac{u-e^{-\alpha_{i}}s_{i}^{R}(u)}{1-e^{-\alpha_{i}}}
, but for the flag variety
G/B_{-}
. By changing
-\alpha_{i}
for
\alpha_{i}
, we translate this definition to our case.

Remark 2.9
.

As in Remark
2.5
, the element
\frac{u-e^{-\alpha_{i}}s_{i}^{R}(u)}{1-e^{-\alpha_{i}}}
formally belongs to the field of fractions of

, but since

\frac{e^{\lambda}-e^{\alpha_{i}}s_{i}^{R}(e^{\lambda})}{1-e^{\alpha_{i}}}=\frac{e^{\lambda}-e^{\alpha_{i}}e^{\lambda-\frac{2(\lambda,\alpha_{i})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\alpha_{i}}}{1-e^{\alpha_{i}}}=e^{\lambda}\frac{1-e^{\alpha_{i}(-\frac{2(\lambda,\alpha_{i})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}+1)}}{1-e^{\alpha_{i}}}

it belongs to

, because
\frac{2(\lambda,\alpha_{i})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}
is an integer.

Analogously to what we have already described in the cohomology case (Section

), the isomorphism (Lemma
1.6
)

\Psi:R(T)\otimes_{R(T)^{W}}R(T)\to K_{T}(G/B)

results in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
HLS10
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

The following equation is satisfied

\partial_{i}^{K}\circ\Psi=\Psi\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{K}).

Rewriting Definition

leads to the equation:

\displaystyle s_{i}^{R}

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha_{i}}-(e^{-\alpha_{i}}-1)\widetilde{\partial}_{i}^{K}

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathrm{id}\otimes s_{i}^{R})

\displaystyle=\Psi(1\otimes e^{-\alpha_{i}})-(\Psi(1\otimes e^{-\alpha_{i}})-\Psi(1\otimes 1))\cdot\partial_{i}^{K}\circ\Psi

\displaystyle(\Psi(\mathrm{id}\otimes s_{i}^{R})\Psi^{-1})([\mathcal{U}])

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{U}]-([\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]-1)\partial_{i}([\mathcal{U}])

Lemma 2.11
.

The equation
\Psi(\mathrm{id}\otimes s_{i}^{R})\Psi^{-1}=s_{i}^{K}
is satisfied in

.

Proof.

Analogous to the proof of Lemma
2.6
. The formula

3
Chevalley formula

Our next goal is to express the action of the Weyl group in basis of Schubert varieties, both in cohomology and
K
-theory. In both formulas for these actions (Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.11
), multiplication by a linear bundle (respectively its Chern class) appears, which is described by
the Chevalley formula
. This knowledge, along with Proposition
1.3
and Proposition
1.4
, allows us to understand the way in which actions
s_{i}^{coh}
and
s_{i}^{K}
look like in the bases of Schubert varieties.

First, let us introduce the Chevalley formula in equivariant cohomology.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Kum02
, Theorem 11.1.7, part e]
)
.

Let
[X_{\omega}]
denote class of the Schubert variety and
\mathcal{L}(\alpha)
a linear bundle for
\alpha\in\Xi(T)
. Then

c_{1}(\mathcal{L}(\alpha))[X_{\omega}]=\omega(\alpha)[X_{\omega}]-\sum_{\beta\text{ positive root such that }l(\omega s_{\beta})=l(\omega)}\langle\alpha,\beta^{\vee}\rangle[X_{\omega s_{\beta}}].

The presentation of the Chevalley formula for an equivariant
K
-theory requires more preparation. To start, we define a family of operators
T_{\beta}^{0}
and
T_{\beta}^{1}
on
R(T)
for
\beta
being a root.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\beta
be a root.

\displaystyle T_{\beta}^{1}(e^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=e^{s_{\beta}\lambda};

\displaystyle T_{\beta}^{0}(e^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{if } $\langle\lambda,\beta^{\vee}\rangle=0$,\\
e^{\lambda}+\ldots+e^{\lambda-(\lambda(\beta^{\vee})-1)\beta}&\text{if } $\langle\lambda,\beta^{\vee}\rangle>0$,\\
-e^{\lambda+\beta}-\ldots-e^{\lambda-\lambda(\beta^{\vee})\beta}&\text{if } $\langle\lambda,\beta^{\vee}\rangle<0$.\end{cases}

We define a monoid
W^{\prime}
with the same set of generators and the same braid relations as a Weyl group
W
but instead of relations
s_{i}^{2}=1
, relations
s_{i}^{2}=s_{i}
hold. There is an obvious map
\Delta
from
W^{\prime}
to
W
, which for the element
x\in W^{\prime}
return the element of
W
with the same reduced decomposition in
W
as
x
have in
W^{\prime}
. For example
\Delta(s_{i}^{2})=s_{i}
. Let
\omega
have the reduced decomposition
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
and
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. By
x(\epsilon,\omega)
we denote element
\Delta(s_{i_{j_{1}}}\ldots s_{i_{j_{k}}})
for
j_{l}
such that
\epsilon_{j_{l}}=1
. For example, if
\omega=s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}
and
\epsilon=\{1,0,1\}
we have
x(\epsilon,\omega)=s_{1}s_{3}
. The following Theorem describes the action of left multiplication by
[\mathcal{L}(\alpha)]
.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Wil07
, Chapter 1]
)
.

Let
\omega
have the reduced decomposition
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
. Then, for every
\alpha\in\Xi(T)
the formula

[\mathcal{L}(\alpha)][\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]=\sum_{(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\ldots,\epsilon_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}}T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{\epsilon_{1}}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{n}}}^{\epsilon_{n}}(e^{\alpha})[\mathcal{O}_{x(\epsilon,\omega)}]

holds.

Remark 3.4
.

In
[
Wil07
]
, the Chevalley formula is considered on flag variety
G/B_{-}
and that is the reason why operators
T_{-\alpha_{i}}
appear in this formula instead of operators
T_{\alpha_{i}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

To check the correctness of the above-mentioned remark let us examine an example of a flag variety
SL_{2}/B
. It is exactly the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It consists of two Schubert cells - a point and a one-dimensional disc. Both of them correspond to the fixed points of the action of the Weyl group. Let us name these points
0
and
\infty
. We know that

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{0}}=1-e^{\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\infty}}=0

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{0}}=1

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\infty}}=1

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{1})][\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{0}}=e^{\alpha_{1}}-e^{2\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{1})][\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\infty}}=0

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{1})][\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{0}}=e^{\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{1})][\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}}]\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\infty}}=e^{-\alpha_{1}}

Classes in the equivariant
K
-theory are determined by their restricions to the fixed point basis. That gives us equalities

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{1})][\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha_{1}}[\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{1})][\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}}]

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha_{1}}[\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}}]-(1+e^{-\alpha_{1}})[\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}].

These calculations coincide with the formulas from Theorem
3.3
.

4
Properties of action of Weyl group in
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]
basis

Our goal is to study the
s_{i}^{K}
action in the basis
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]
in equivariant
K
-theory. From now on, this basis will be denoted by
\mathcal{B}
.

4.1
Proof of the main theorem

In this subsection we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\omega\in W
. Then

s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])=\begin{cases}a_{\omega s_{i}}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]+\sum_{\beta\in C_{i}}a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}]+l.o.t.&\text{if } $l(\omega s_{i})>l(\omega)$\\
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
C_{i}=\{\gamma\text{: }\gamma\neq\alpha_{i}\text{ is a positive root such that }l(\omega s_{i}s_{\gamma})=l(\omega)\text{ and }\langle\alpha_{i},\gamma\rangle\neq 0\}
,
a_{\omega^{\prime}}
are non-zero elements of
R(T)
and
l.o.t.
is the sum of terms
a_{\omega^{\prime}}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega^{\prime}}]
for
l(\omega^{\prime})<l(\omega)
.

The support
of action for fixed
\omega
is the set of elements
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega^{\prime}}]
, that in formula for this action in the basis
\mathcal{B}
have non-zero coefficient
a_{\omega^{\prime}}
. Part of the support, which consist of elements
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega^{\prime}}]
such that
l(\omega^{\prime})=n
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
is called
the support of lenght
n
of this action.

4.1.1
Part I - preliminaries

Lemma
2.11
and Proposition
1.4
imply that

s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]-([\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]-1)[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]

(1)

for
l(\omega s_{i})>l(\omega)
and that

s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]-([\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]-1)[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]=[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]

for
l(\omega s_{i})<l(\omega)
. The second equation proves case
l(\omega s_{i})\leq l(\omega)
of Theorem
4.1
. From now on we consider the first case only. The first step in our proof is simplifying Equation
1
using the Chevalley formula. It is obvious that

[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]=e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]+l.o.t.

and

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]

\displaystyle=e^{\omega s_{i}(-\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]+\text{ terms of length }l(\omega)+l.o.t.

\displaystyle=e^{\omega(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]+\text{ terms of length }l(\omega)+l.o.t.

where "terms of length
l(\omega)
" is a linear combination (over
R(T)
) of the elements from the support of length
l(\omega)
of left-multiplication by
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]
. Applying above equations to Equation (
1
) we obtain:

s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])=(1-e^{\omega(\alpha_{i})})[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]+e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]-\text{ terms of length }l(\omega)+l.o.t.

(2)

What needs to be understood is which elements belong to the support of length
l(\omega)
of left-multiplication by
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]
.

4.1.2
Part II - properties of Weyl group

Let us fix a reduced decomposition
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
of an element
\omega
of the Weyl group. The Chevalley formula implies that elements
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega^{\prime}}]
in the support of length
l(\omega)-1
of left-multiplication by
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]
are in correspondence with the subwords
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{j}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
of
\omega
. Moreover, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
be a fixed representation of the word
\omega
. Then the words
\omega_{k}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
and
\omega_{l}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{l}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
are different.

Proof.

Assume that
k=1
and
l=n
. Otherwise, we can consider the word
s_{i_{k-1}}\ldots s_{i_{1}}\omega s_{i_{n}}\ldots s_{i_{l+1}}
. The following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=\omega_{l}

\displaystyle s_{i_{1}}\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=s_{i_{1}}\omega_{l}

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\omega^{\prime}

where
\omega^{\prime}
is
\omega
with the first and the last element removed. Nevertheless
l(\omega^{\prime})=l(\omega)-2\neq l(\omega)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

The next step is to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\omega=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
. The subwords of
\omega
of length
l(\omega)-1
obtained by removing one element from
\omega
are in bijection with the words
\omega s_{\beta}
such that
l(\omega s_{\beta})=l(\omega)-1
for
\beta
being a positive root.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)

Lemma 4.4
.

[
Hum90
, Theorem 5.8]
[Strong Exchange Condition]
If
\omega=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
is a reduced word and
t
is a reflection satisfying
l(\omega t)<l(\omega)
then there exist
k
such that
\omega t=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
.

(\Rightarrow)

We must prove that
s_{i_{1}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
is equal to
\omega t
for reflection in the positive root
\beta
. Let
\beta=\omega^{\prime}(\alpha_{k})
where
\omega^{\prime}
is defined as
s_{i_{n}}\ldots s_{i_{k+1}}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The root
\beta
defined as above is a positive root.

Proof.

From
[
Hum90
, Theorem 5.4]
it follows that if
l(\omega s_{\alpha})>l(\omega)
for a simple root
\alpha
, then
\omega(\alpha)
is a positive root. We know that
\omega^{\prime}s_{i_{k}}
is a reduced word, because
\omega
is as well, so
l(\omega^{\prime}s_{i_{k}})>l(\omega^{\prime})
, so
\omega^{\prime}(\alpha_{k})
is a positive root.
∎

Moreover, the element
\omega^{\prime}s_{\alpha_{k}}(\omega^{\prime})^{-1}
is a reflection in a positive root
\beta
(
[
Hum90
, Lemma 5.7]
), so when we take
t=s_{\beta}
, the desired equality holds:

\omega t=\omega s_{\beta}=\omega\omega^{\prime}s_{i_{k}}(\omega^{\prime})^{-1}=s_{i_{1}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}.

∎

4.1.3
Part III - support of Chevalley formula

Let
\mathrm{sgn}\text{: }\mathbb{Z}\to\{+,-,0\}
denote the function of the sign of a number. The following lemma holds.

Lemma 4.6
.

For
\beta
,
\omega^{\prime}
and
\alpha_{k}
defined as above and for
\alpha
a root, the following equality holds

\mathrm{sgn}\langle(\omega^{\prime})^{-1}(\alpha),\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle=\mathrm{sgn}(\beta,\alpha).

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}\langle(\omega^{\prime})^{-1}(\alpha),\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sgn}\frac{2((\omega^{\prime})^{-1}(\alpha),\alpha_{k})}{(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k})}=\mathrm{sgn}(2((\omega^{\prime})^{-1}(\alpha),\alpha_{k}))=

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sgn}((\alpha,\omega^{\prime}(\alpha_{k})))=\mathrm{sgn}((\omega^{\prime}(\alpha_{k}),\alpha))=\mathrm{sgn}(\beta,\alpha)

∎

From Definition
3.2
we know, that for a fixed
\omega
and a fixed
i
the support of length
l(\omega)
of left-multiplication
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]
by
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]
consist of elements
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega^{\prime\prime}}]
such that
\omega^{\prime\prime}=s_{i_{1}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}s_{i}
and
\langle(\omega^{\prime})^{-1}(\alpha_{i}),\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle\neq 0
for
\omega^{\prime}
defined as in the Subsection
4.1.2
. By Lemma
4.2
this is equivalent to
\omega^{\prime\prime}=\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}
for positive roots
\beta
satisfying
(\beta,\alpha_{i})\neq 0
and
l(\omega s_{i}s_{\beta})=l(\omega)
. The only detail demanding explanation now is that the coefficient of
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]
in
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]
is not equal to
e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})}
. If that was a case, the support of
s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])
would not contain
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]
from Equation (
2
) (because
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]=e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]+l.o.t.
). The Chevalley formula implies that this coefficient is equal to
e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})}+1
. Therefore, Theorem
4.1
is proved.

4.2
Example of
SL_{n}/B

In this section, the main goal is to study the automorphism
s_{i}^{K}
on
K_{T}(SL_{n}/B)
. In this example, we can calculate coefficients
a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}
from Theorem
4.1
.

Every two different roots
\alpha,\beta
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
satisfy
(\alpha,\beta)\in\{-1,0,1\}
, so the operator
T^{0}_{\beta}
in the Chevalley formula (Definition
3.2
) simplifies to

\displaystyle T_{\beta}^{0}(e^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{if } $\langle\lambda,\beta^{\vee}\rangle=0$,\\
e^{\lambda}&\text{if } $\langle\lambda,\beta^{\vee}\rangle=1$,\\
e^{\lambda}+1&\text{if } $\lambda=\beta$\\
-1-e^{-\lambda}&\text{if } $\lambda=-\beta$\\
-e^{\lambda+\beta}&\text{if } $\langle\lambda,\beta^{\vee}\rangle=-1$.\end{cases}

(3)

First, we calculate the coefficient of the element
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]
in
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]
. Equation (
3
) and Lemma
4.2
show that this coefficient is equal to
1+e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})}
. Equation (
2
) gives

s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])=e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]-(1+e^{-\omega(\alpha_{i})})[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]+\ldots=-[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]+\ldots.

Since we know
a_{\omega}=-1
, we calculate the coefficient
a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}
of the element
[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}]
in
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]
. Let us assume that
\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}
is the subword of
\omega s_{i}=s_{i_{1}}\ldots\ldots s_{i_{n}}s_{i}
with one element removed as follows:
s_{i_{1}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}s_{i}
. Let
\lambda=s_{i_{k+1}}\ldots s_{i}(-\alpha_{i})
. By the Chevalley formula,
a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}
is equal to

\displaystyle T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{n}}}^{1}T_{-\alpha_{i}}^{1}(e^{-\alpha_{i}})

\displaystyle=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}(e^{s_{i_{k+1}}\ldots s_{i}(-\alpha_{i})})=

\displaystyle=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}(e^{\lambda}).

We will prove, that
\lambda\neq\pm\alpha_{i_{k}}
. Firstly, let us assume that
i_{k}\neq i
. By
[
Hum90
, Theorem 5.4]
if
l(\omega s_{\alpha})>l(\omega)
for a simple root
\alpha
, then
\omega(\alpha)
is a positive root. Applying this to
\omega^{\prime}=s_{i_{k+1}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
and
\omega^{\prime\prime}=s_{i_{k}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}
implies that both
\lambda
and
-\lambda
are simple roots, which is a contradiction. If
i_{k}=i
, then Lemma
4.2
implies that
s_{i_{1}}\ldots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\ldots s_{i_{n}}s_{i}=\omega
and this case is already calculated. From Equation (
3
), three options arise.

•

\langle\lambda,-\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle=1
. From Equation (
3
)

\displaystyle a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}(e^{\lambda})=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k-1}}}^{1}(e^{\lambda})=

\displaystyle=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k-1}}}^{1}(e^{s_{i_{k+1}}\ldots s_{i}(-\alpha_{i})})=e^{-\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}(\alpha_{i})}.

•

\langle\lambda,-\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle=0
, which implies that
T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}(e^{\lambda})
is equal to zero and therefore
a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}=0
.

•

\langle\lambda,-\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle=-1
. Equation (
3
) gives

T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}(e^{\lambda})=-e^{\lambda-\alpha_{k}}

Now, let us establish the conditions for
\lambda
to satisfy the equation
\langle\lambda,-\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle=-1
. In
\mathfrak{sl_{n}}
, presenting an element as the sum of simple roots makes every coefficient equal to either
-1
,
0
or
1
. Moreover, if coefficients of
\alpha_{i-1}
and
\alpha_{i+1}
are non-zero, then so is the coefficient of
\alpha_{i}
and all of them are equal. If we want the equality
\langle\lambda,-\alpha_{k}^{\vee}\rangle=-1
to hold, then
\lambda
written as a sum of simple roots must look as follows:
\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\alpha_{k-1}+\alpha_{k}
or
\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{k+1}+\ldots+\alpha_{j}
. In both cases
s_{k}(\lambda)=\lambda-\alpha_{k}
, because

s_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\begin{cases}\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}&\text{ if } $i=j\pm 1$\\
-\alpha_{j}&\text{ if } $i=j$\\
\alpha_{j}&\text{ otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

That gives us the following

T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}(e^{\lambda})=-e^{\lambda-\alpha_{k}}=-e^{s_{k}(\lambda)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle a_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}^{0}(e^{\lambda})=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k-1}}}^{1}(-e^{s_{k}(\lambda)})

\displaystyle=T_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}^{1}\ldots T_{-\alpha_{i_{k-1}}}^{1}(-e^{s_{k}(s_{i_{k+1}}\ldots s_{i}(-\alpha_{i}))})=-e^{\omega s_{i}(-\alpha_{i})}=-e^{\omega(\alpha_{i})}.

Combining these results with the Equation (
2

4.6

\displaystyle s_{i}^{K}([\mathcal{O}_{\omega}])

\displaystyle=(1-e^{\omega(\alpha_{i})})[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}}]-[\mathcal{O}_{\omega}]

\displaystyle-\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha_{i}\text{ positive root such that }l(\omega s_{i}s_{\beta})=l(\omega)\text{ and }\langle\alpha_{i},\beta\rangle>0}e^{-\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha_{i}\ \text{ positive root such that }l(\omega s_{i}s_{\beta})=l(\omega)\text{ and }\langle\alpha_{i},\beta\rangle<0}e^{\omega(\alpha_{i})}[\mathcal{O}_{\omega s_{i}s_{\beta}}]

5
Properties of action of Weyl group in
\text{MC}_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})
basis

In this chapter we study the automorphism
s_{i}^{K}
in the
mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})
basis of
K_{T}(G/B)_{loc}
. Using Lemma
2.11
.

\displaystyle s_{i}^{K}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})-([\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]-1)\partial_{i}^{K}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))

Since
[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{i})][\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]=\mathrm{id}
(because
e^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot e^{-\alpha_{i}}=1
), we get

\displaystyle s_{i}^{K}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]\left(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})-(1-[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{i})])\partial_{i}^{K}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))\right).

(5)

From Definition
1.7
, the following holds for
y\neq 0
:

[\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{i})]\partial_{i}^{K}=\frac{\mathcal{T}_{i}-\partial_{i}^{K}+\mathrm{id}}{y}.

That leads to the equation

\displaystyle s_{i}^{K}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))=

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]\left(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})-\partial_{i}^{K}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))+\frac{\mathcal{T}_{i}-\partial_{i}^{K}+\mathrm{id}}{y}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))\right)=

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{i})]\frac{mC_{y}(X_{\omega s_{i}}^{\circ})-(y+1)\partial_{i}^{K}(mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ}))+(y+1)mC_{y}(X_{\omega}^{\circ})}{y}.

Unfortunately, in this basis no easy description of the action
\partial_{i}^{K}
is known, which makes such presentation unuseful. For
y=-1
the situation is much easier as we will see further in this chapter.

Another basis worth considering is the basis of motivic Chern classes with
y=0
, which is the basis of classes

6
Conclusions

In order to calculate the coefficients of the automorphism
s_{i}^{K}
, we must multiply the class of Schubert variety with the class of a linear bundle twice, which means using the Chevalley formula twice, which is computationally challenging. Switching to the motivic Chern classes basis changes the Chevalley formula (shown in
[
MNS24
]
), but the formula is still combinatorically too complicated to simplify these calculations. As we saw in Chapter
3
, the problem with the Chevalley formula is that the support of it is not described in terms of the elements of the Weyl group, but the monoid
W^{\prime}
. We only know that the support is subset of
\{[\mathcal{O}_{\omega^{\prime}}]\}
for
\omega^{\prime}
being the subword of
\omega s_{i}
. That is because, for example, a certain subword can be constructed in two ways in the Weyl group, but in three ways in the monoid
W^{\prime}
, as we explain below.

Let us take a word
s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}
in the Weyl group of
SL_{n}
. It has a subword
s_{1}
. It can be constructed by removing
s_{2}
and the last
s_{1}
or by removing
s_{2}
and the first
s_{1}
. However, in this monoid a relation
s_{1}s_{1}=s_{1}
holds, so there are three constructions possible - the two mentioned above and one additional, executed by only removing
s_{2}
. These additional combinations can transform element from being in the support to not being in the support and the other way around. For example the coefficient of
[\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{id}}]
in
s_{1}^{K}[\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}s_{2}}]
is equal to zero (Figure
1
), but would be non-zero if we would consider only subwords in the Weyl group because one non-zero term of
[\mathcal{L}(-\alpha_{1})][\mathcal{O}_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}}]
is equal to

T_{\alpha_{1}}^{1}T_{\alpha_{2}}^{0}T_{\alpha_{1}}^{0}(e^{-\alpha_{1}}).

A possible solution to this problem might be a different choice of bases or indexing elements of basis
\mathcal{B}
not by elements of the Weyl group
W
, but by elements of the monoid (or, in fact, the distinguished basis of Hecke algebra)
W^{\prime}
. These two objects have the same elements, but the fact that the relations holding in the monoid are different from the Weyl group could help to describe the support of the automorphism
s_{i}^{K}
explicitely.

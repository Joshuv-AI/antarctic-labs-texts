Abstract
. Given a flat vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold, Corlette [ 7 ] and Donaldson [ 11 ] proved that it admits harmonic metrics if and only if it is semi-simple. In this paper, we extend this equivalence to arbitrary vector bundles without any additional hypothesis, the result can be viewed as a Riemannian Hitchin-Kobayashi correspondence. Furthermore, we also prove an equivalence of categories in Sasakian geometry, relating the category of projective flat complex vector bundles to the category of Higgs bundles.

53C07, 53C25, 32L05, 14J60.

1.
Introduction

A unifying principle in geometric analysis predicts that existences of canonical metrics should be closely related to appropriate algebraic stability conditions on underlying geometric objects, one may refer to the works on Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture for extremal metrics in Kähler geometry and Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau characterization(also referred as Hitchin-Kobayashi correspondence or Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence) for Hermitian-Einstein metrics in holomorphic vector bundles. It is our aim in present paper to investigate the issue on general vector bundles. Unless indicated explicitly otherwise, vector bundles could be real or complex, whose metrics are Riemannian or Hermitian respectively.

Suppose that
E
is a smooth vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and
\nabla
is a connection on
E
. We call
(E,\nabla)
semi-simple(also called completely reducible or reductive in the literature) if it splits as a direct sum of simple sub-bundles, where simple(also called irreducible or stable in the literature) means that there exists no nontrivial
\nabla
-invariant sub-bundle. A vector bundle
E
is said to be semi-simple if
(E,\nabla)
is semi-simple for some
\nabla
. Naturally there arises a fundamental problem:
Does there exist certain best canonical metrics on
(E,\nabla)
?
On the other hand, motivated by calculating the Euler characteristic number
\chi(E)
via the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula and its ramifications, one may ask:
How to find metric compatible connections on
E
?
For this purpose, we write for a metric
K
on
E
,

(1.1)

\begin{split}\nabla=\nabla_{K}+\psi_{K},\end{split}

where
\nabla_{K}
is a connection preserving
K
and
\psi_{K}
is a
\End(E)
-valued
1
-form. We define

(1.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\nabla}(K)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\psi_{K}|^{2}\dvol_{g},\end{split}

and the point is to minimize
\mathcal{E}_{\nabla}(K)
when
K
varies.

The canonical metric concerned in this paper is the critical point of
\mathcal{E}_{\nabla}
which means

(1.3)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}=0,\end{split}

and we then call
H
a harmonic metric. Another reason for this candidate lies in the scenario of nonabelian Hodge theory in Kähler geometry
[
35
]
, in which
\nabla
is assumed to be flat and the existence of harmonic metrics leads to the existence of Higgs structures. It is remarked that the flatness means any metric
H
corresponds to an equivariant map

(1.4)

\begin{split}f_{H}:\tilde{M}\rightarrow GL(r)/U(r),\end{split}

where
\tilde{M}
is the universal covering space of
M
and
r=\rank(E)
, Moreover, it is known that
H
being harmonic iff
f_{H}
being harmonic. So in general case, lacking of flatness may block off methods used in equivariant harmonic maps. Detecting harmonic metrics on vector bundles is a nonlinear system generalization of solving Laplace equations and obstructions would appear.

In this paper, we prove(see Theorem
2.1
)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(E,\nabla)
be a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold with an arbitrary connection
\nabla
, then it admits harmonic metrics if and only if it is semi-simple.

Note that Theorem
1.1
is a Riemannian counterpart of the Hitchin-Kobayashi correspondence in Kähler geometry
[
9
,
10
,
39
]
. Concerning Theorem
1.1
, there are a few previous works in the presence of extra assumptions which we should mention. As a postscript to Hitchin’s paper
[

on self-duality equations, borrowing ideas from harmonic maps, Donaldson
[

]
proved that any vector bundle over a compact Riemannian surface equipped with a semi-simple flat connection must admit harmonic metrics. Deforming a semi-simple flat connection via an evolution equation and utilizing Uhlenbeck compactness
[
38
]
, it is Corlette who proved Theorem
1.1
for flat vector bundles in
[
7
]
. Many works are constantly engaged in generalizations and applications on Corlette-Donaldson’s tremendous criterion since then, see
[

) that the existence of harmonic metrics is equivalent to a kind of stability condition, see

.

Given an automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(E)
, it acts on a connection
\nabla
by
\nabla\mapsto\sigma\circ\nabla\circ\sigma^{-1}
and the space
\mathcal{M}_{S}
of isomorphism classes of simple connections is realized as the quotient

(1.5)

\begin{split}\{Simple\ connections\ on\ E\}/\Aut(E).\end{split}

We say a connection
\nabla
irreducible if there is no nontrivial

-invariant endomorphism and the space
\mathcal{M}_{I,K}
of isomorphism classes of irreducible connections with

Using Theorem

2.
Harmonic metrics on vector bundles

2.1.
Preliminaries

We assume
E
is a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and set
A^{p}(M,E)=\Gamma(\Lambda^{p}T^{\ast}M\otimes E)
, the space of
p
-forms on
M
with values in
E
. Associated to a connection
\nabla
on
E
, the exterior differential operator

(2.1)

\begin{split}D:A^{p}(M,E)\rightarrow A^{p+1}(M,E)\end{split}

is defined by requiring
D(\alpha\otimes u)=d\alpha\otimes u+(-1)^{p}\alpha\wedge\nabla u
for any
\alpha\in A^{p}(M)
and
u\in\Gamma(E)
. Then the curvature is given by
F_{\nabla}=D\circ\nabla\in A^{2}(M,\End(E))
. For
\omega\in A^{p}(M,E)
, we have

(2.2)

\begin{split}D\omega(e_{0},...,e_{p})&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{k}\nabla_{e_{k}}(\omega(e_{0},e_{1},...,\hat{e_{k}},...,e_{p}))\\
&+\sum\limits_{0\leq k<l\leq p}(-1)^{k+l}\omega([e_{k},e_{l}],e_{1},...,\hat{e_{k}},...,\hat{e_{l}},...,e_{p}),\end{split}

where
e_{1},...,e_{p}\in\Gamma(TM)
and symbols covered by
\wedge
are omitted. If the tangent bundle
TM
is equipped with a connection
\nabla^{TM}
, the action of
\nabla
may be further extended to tensorial combinations of
TM
and
E
as well as their duals. Then it also holds

(2.3)

\begin{split}D\omega(e_{0},...,e_{p})&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{k}(\nabla_{e_{k}}\omega)(e_{0},...,\hat{e_{k}},...,e_{p})\\
&-\sum\limits_{0\leq k<l\leq p}(-1)^{k+l}\omega(T_{\nabla^{TM}}(e_{k},e_{l}),e_{1},...,\hat{e_{k}},...,\hat{e_{l}},...,e_{p}),\end{split}

where
T_{\nabla^{TM}}
denotes the torsion. For a metric
K
on
E
, we have the pointwise inner-product

(2.4)

\begin{split}(\bullet,\bullet)_{K}:A^{p}(M,E)\times A^{p}(M,E)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M),\end{split}

(2.5)

\begin{split}(\omega,\theta)\mapsto\sum\limits_{i_{1}<...<i_{p}}K(\omega(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}}),\theta(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}})),\end{split}

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\dim M}
is an orthogonal unit basis of
TM
. Henceforth, we will omit the subscript
K
if there is no ambiguity. The generalized inner-product

(2.6)

\begin{split}K(\bullet,\bullet):A^{p}(M,E)\times A^{q}(M,E)\rightarrow A^{p+q}(M)\end{split}

is defined by requiring
K(\alpha\otimes u,\beta\otimes v)=(u,v)\alpha\wedge\beta
for any
\alpha\in A^{p}(M)
,
\beta\in A^{q}(M)
and
u,v\in\Gamma(E)
. In particular, for
\omega,\theta\in A^{p}(M,E)
we have
K(\omega,\ast\theta)=(\omega,\theta)\dvol_{g}
, where
\ast
is the Hodge star operator acting on the form component.

Given above
\nabla
and
K
, we define
\psi_{K}\in A^{1}(M,\End(E))
by

(2.7)

\begin{split}K(\psi_{K}(u),v)=\frac{1}{2}\left(K(\nabla u,v)+K(u,\nabla v)-dK(u,v)\right),\end{split}

where
u,v\in\Gamma(E)
. One can easily check
\psi_{K}
is self-adjoint and

(2.8)

\begin{split}\nabla_{K}=\nabla-\psi_{K}:A^{0}(M,E)\rightarrow A^{1}(M,E)\end{split}

is a connection preserving
K
. The co-differential operator

(2.9)

\begin{split}D_{K}^{\ast}:A^{p}(M,E)\rightarrow A^{p-1}(M,E)\end{split}

is determined by
D_{K}
, the exterior differential operator of
\nabla_{K}
,

(2.10)

\begin{split}\int_{M}(D_{K}\omega,\theta)\dvol_{g}=\int_{M}(\omega,D_{K}^{\ast}\theta)\dvol_{g},\end{split}

where
\omega\in A^{p-1}(M,E),\theta\in A^{p}(M,E)
. Then it holds

(2.11)

\begin{split}D_{K}^{\ast}\theta(e_{1},..,e_{p-1})=-\tr_{g}\nabla_{K,\bullet}\theta(\bullet,e_{1},...,e_{p-1}),\end{split}

where for notational simplicity,
\nabla_{K}
also denotes the connection on
\Lambda^{p}T^{\ast}M\otimes E
which acts on
\Lambda^{p}T^{\ast}M
by the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
.

For two metrics
K
and
H
on
E
, we denote by
f=K^{-1}H
the endormorphism given by

(2.12)

\begin{split}H(\bullet,\bullet)=K(f(\bullet),\bullet).\end{split}

By
(\ref{metricdecom})
and the fact that
f
is self-adjoint with respect to
K
and
H
, we have

(2.13)

\begin{split}H(\psi_{H}(u),v)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(H(\nabla u,v)+H(u,\nabla v)-dH(u,v)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(K((f\circ\nabla)u,v)+K(f(u),\nabla v)-dK(f(u),v)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(K((\nabla\circ f-\nabla f)u,v)+K(f(u),\nabla v)-dK(f(u),v)\right)\\
&=K((\psi_{K}\circ f)u,v)-\frac{1}{2}K((\nabla f)u,v)\\
&=H((f^{-1}\circ\psi_{K}\circ f)u,v)-\frac{1}{2}H((f^{-1}\nabla f)u,v)\\
&=H(\psi_{K}(u),v)+H((f^{-1}[\psi_{K},f])u,v)-\frac{1}{2}H((f^{-1}\nabla f)u,v)\\
&=H(\psi_{K}(u),v)-\frac{1}{2}H((f^{-1}\delta_{K}f)u,v),\end{split}

where
\delta_{K}=\nabla_{K}-\psi_{K}
. It follows

(2.14)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H}=\nabla_{K}+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\delta_{K}f,\ \psi_{H}=\psi_{K}-\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\delta_{K}f.\end{split}

According to the definition, we deduce

(2.15)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}&=-\tr_{g}\nabla_{H}\psi_{H}\\
&=-\tr_{g}(\nabla_{K}+\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\delta_{K}f)(\psi_{K}-\frac{1}{2}f^{-1}\delta_{K}f)\\
&=-\tr_{g}\nabla_{K}\psi_{K}+\frac{1}{2}\tr_{g}\nabla_{K}(f^{-1}\delta_{K}f)-\frac{1}{2}\tr_{g}[f^{-1}\delta_{K}f,\psi_{K}]\\
&=\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{K}^{\ast}(f^{-1}\delta_{K}f)+\frac{1}{2}\tr_{g}[\psi_{K},f^{-1}\delta_{K}f]\\
&=\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}+\frac{1}{2}\tr_{g}\nabla(f^{-1}\delta_{K}f).\end{split}

Set
f=h^{\ast K}h
and
H(\bullet,\bullet)=K(h(\bullet),h(\bullet))
. Consider the gauge action
h(\bullet)=h\circ\bullet\circ h^{-1}
and write
h(\nabla)=h(\nabla)_{K}+\psi_{h(\nabla),K}
for the decomposition of
h(\nabla)
in term of
K
, then

(2.16)

\begin{split}dK(u,v)&=dH(h^{-1}(u),h^{-1}(v))\\
&=H((\nabla_{H}\circ h^{-1})u,h^{-1}(v))+H(h^{-1}(u),(\nabla_{H}\circ h^{-1})v)\\
&=K(h(\nabla_{H})u,v)+K(u,h(\nabla_{H})v),\end{split}

(2.17)

\begin{split}K(\psi_{h(\nabla),K}(u),v)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(K(h(\nabla)u,v)+K(u,h(\nabla)v)-dK(u,v)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}H((\nabla\circ h^{-1})u,h^{-1}(v))+\frac{1}{2}H(h^{-1}u,(\nabla\circ h^{-1})v)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}dH(h^{-1}(u),h^{-1}(v))\\
&=H((\psi_{H}\circ h^{-1})u,h^{-1}(v))\\
&=K(h(\psi_{H})u,v).\end{split}

Namely, we have
h(\nabla)_{K}=h(\nabla_{H})
,
\psi_{h(\nabla),K}=h(\psi_{\nabla,H})
, and hence it holds

(2.18)

\begin{split}h(\nabla)^{\ast}_{K}\psi_{h(\nabla),K}&=-\tr_{g}h(\nabla)_{K}\psi_{h(\nabla),K}\\
&=-\tr_{g}h(\nabla_{H})h(\psi_{H})\\
&=-h(\tr_{g}\nabla_{H}\psi_{H})\\
&=h(\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}).\end{split}

Moreover, by
(\ref{exteriord})
we also know
h(D_{H})
is the exterior differential operator relative to
h(\nabla)_{K}
.

2.2.
Harmonic metrics

We shall apply the method of continuity to detect harmonic metrics on a simple vector bundle
(E,\nabla)
over a compact Riemannian manifold
(M.g)
. Let us fix a background metric
K
and consider the following family of equations:

(2.19)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}=\epsilon\log f_{\epsilon},\ f_{\epsilon}=K^{-1}H_{\epsilon}.\end{split}

Pick a metric
K_{0}
and a function
u
with
\Delta_{g}u=-2\rank(E)^{-1}\tr\nabla_{K_{0}}^{\ast}\psi_{K_{0}}
, where
\Delta_{g}
denotes the Beltrami-Laplace. Define
\hat{K}=e^{u}K_{0}
,
K=\hat{K}\exp(-\nabla_{\hat{K}}^{\ast}\psi_{\hat{K}})
, and then by
(\ref{DHKstarpsiHK})
, we obtain

(2.20)

\begin{split}\tr\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}=\tr\nabla_{\hat{K}}^{\ast}\psi_{\hat{K}}=0\end{split}

Moreover, one easily concludes
f_{1}=\exp\nabla_{\hat{K}}^{\ast}\psi_{\hat{K}}
must solve
(\ref{continuitypath})
with
\epsilon=1
.

We denote by
I
the set of
\epsilon\in[0,1]
such that
(\ref{continuitypath})
is solvable and thus
1\in J
. For the openness of
I
, we consider the operator

(2.21)

\begin{split}\hat{L}:(0,1]\times L_{k}^{p}(S^{+}_{K})\rightarrow L^{p}_{k-2}(S_{K}),\ (\epsilon,f)\mapsto f\circ(\nabla_{Kf}^{\ast}\psi_{Kf}-\epsilon\log f),\end{split}

where
L_{k}^{p}(S_{K})
(
L_{k}^{p}(S^{+}_{K})
) is the Sobolev space of (positive)self-adjoint sections of
\End(E)
. Set

(2.22)

\begin{split}\nabla^{f}=\Ad f^{\frac{1}{2}}\circ\nabla\circ\Ad f^{-\frac{1}{2}},\ \delta_{K}^{f}=\Ad f^{-\frac{1}{2}}\circ\delta_{K}\circ\Ad f^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where
\Ad f^{\frac{1}{2}}(\bullet)=f^{\frac{1}{2}}\circ\bullet\circ f^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
\Ad f^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is defined accordingly. In the normal coordinate,

(2.23)

\begin{split}\tr_{g}\nabla^{f}\delta_{K}^{f}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta ff^{-\frac{1}{2}})&=\tr_{g}\nabla^{f}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{K}(\delta ff^{-1})f^{\frac{1}{2}})\\
&=\tr_{g}\Ad f^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nabla(f^{-1}\delta_{K}(\delta ff^{-1})f))\\
&=\tr_{g}\Ad f^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nabla(f^{-1}\delta_{K}\delta f)+\nabla(f^{-1}\delta f\delta_{K}f^{-1}f))\\
&=\tr_{g}\Ad f^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nabla(f^{-1}\delta_{K}\delta f)+\nabla(\delta f^{-1}\delta_{K}f))\\
&=2\Ad f^{\frac{1}{2}}(\delta\nabla_{Kf}^{\ast}\psi_{Kf}).\end{split}

It follows for any
(\epsilon,f_{\epsilon})
with
\nabla_{Kf_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}\psi_{Kf_{\epsilon}}-\epsilon\log f_{\epsilon}=0
,

(2.24)

\begin{split}\delta_{2}\hat{L}(\epsilon,f_{\epsilon})&=\delta f_{\epsilon}(\nabla_{Kf_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}\psi_{Kf_{\epsilon}}-\epsilon\log f_{\epsilon})+f\delta(\nabla_{Kf}^{\ast}\psi_{Kf}-\epsilon\log f)\\
&=f\delta\nabla_{Kf_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}\psi_{Kf_{\epsilon}}-\epsilon f_{\epsilon}\delta\log f_{\epsilon}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\tr_{g}\nabla^{f_{\epsilon}}\delta_{K}^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\delta f_{\epsilon}f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})f^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}-\epsilon f_{\epsilon}\delta\log f_{\epsilon}.\end{split}

On the other hand, since
d(u,v)=(\nabla^{f}u,v)+(u,\delta^{f}_{K}v)
for
u,v\in\Gamma(\End(E))
, we have for any
\phi\in S_{K}
(where
S_{K}
is the space of self-adjoint sections of
\End(E)
) with
\delta_{2}\hat{L}(\epsilon,f_{\epsilon})(\phi)=0
,

(2.25)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}|f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}|^{2}&=(\tr_{g}\nabla^{f_{\epsilon}}\delta_{K}^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}),f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})\\
&+(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon},\tr_{g}\delta_{K}^{f_{\epsilon}}\nabla^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}))\\
&+|\nabla^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})|^{2}+|\delta_{K}^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})|^{2}\\
&\geq(\tr_{g}\nabla^{f_{\epsilon}}\delta_{K}^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}),f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})\\
&+(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon},(\tr_{g}\nabla^{f_{\epsilon}}\delta_{K}^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}))^{\ast})\\
&=2(\tr_{g}\nabla^{f_{\epsilon}}\delta_{K}^{f_{\epsilon}}(f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}),f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})\\
&=2(\epsilon f^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\delta\log f_{\epsilon}(\phi)f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon},f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})\\
&\geq 2\epsilon|f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}|^{2},\end{split}

where we have used the following pointwise inequality(see
[
23
, pp.69-70]
):

(2.26)

\begin{split}(f^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\delta\log f_{\epsilon}(\phi)f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon},f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon})\geq|f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}\phi f^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\epsilon}|^{2}.\end{split}

So the maximum principle shows
\phi=0
and therefore one easily concludes that
\delta_{2}\hat{L}(\epsilon,f_{\epsilon})
is an isomorphism. By the standard implicit function theorem, we get

Proposition 2.1
.

I
is a nonempty open subset.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
||\log f_{\epsilon}||_{C^{0}(M)}
are uniform bounded, so are
||f_{\epsilon}||_{C^{k}(M)}
for any
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

In the following, we shall use
C_{j}(j=1,2,3...)
to denote uniform constants which may depend on
||\log f_{\epsilon}||_{C^{0}(M)}
and also observe

(2.27)

\begin{split}|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}^{2}\leq C_{1}(|\psi_{K}|^{2}+|\nabla f_{\epsilon}|^{2}),\end{split}

(2.28)

\begin{split}|\nabla f_{\epsilon}|^{2}\leq C_{2}(|\psi_{K}|^{2}+|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}).\end{split}

First of all, we have

(2.29)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}\tr f_{\epsilon}&=\tr_{g}dK(f^{-1}_{\epsilon}\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon},f_{\epsilon})\\
&=(\tr_{g}\nabla(f^{-1}_{\epsilon}\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon}),f_{\epsilon})+(f^{-1}_{\epsilon}\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon},\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon})\\
&=2(\nabla_{\epsilon}^{\ast}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}-\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K},f_{\epsilon})+(f^{-1}_{i}\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon},\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon})\\
&\geq-C_{3}+|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, we compute

(2.30)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}&=-2(\nabla^{\ast}_{H_{\epsilon}}\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}},\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}+2|\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}\\
&=-2g^{ij}(F_{\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}}(\bullet,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})(\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}})),\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}\\
&+2g^{ij}(\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}(F_{\nabla^{g}}(\bullet,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}})),\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}+2|\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}^{2}\\
&-2(\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}},\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}-2(D_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}D_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}},\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}.\end{split}

We note the relation

(2.31)

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{2}(F_{\nabla}+F_{\nabla}^{\ast H_{\epsilon}}),\\
F_{\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}}+\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}\wedge\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{2}(F_{\nabla}-F_{\nabla}^{\ast H_{\epsilon}}).\end{array}\right.\end{split}

It follows

(2.32)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}&=(g^{ij}[[\psi_{H_{\epsilon}},\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]-(F_{\nabla}-F_{\nabla}^{\ast H_{\epsilon}})(\bullet,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}})],\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}\\
&+2(\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}\circ\Ric_{g},\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}+2|\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}^{2}\\
&-2\epsilon(\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\log f_{\epsilon},\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}-(D_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}(F_{\nabla}+F_{\nabla}^{\ast H_{\epsilon}}),\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}\\
&\geq|[\psi_{H_{\epsilon}},\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}]|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}-(g^{ij}[(F_{\nabla}-F_{\nabla}^{\ast H_{\epsilon}})(\bullet,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}})],\psi_{H_{\epsilon}})_{H_{\epsilon}}\\
&-C_{4}|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}+2|\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}^{2}\\
&+|\nabla_{H_{\epsilon}}\log f_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}+|D_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\ast}(F_{\nabla}+F_{\nabla}^{\ast H_{\epsilon}})|_{H_{\epsilon}}|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}\\
&\geq-C_{5}-C_{6}|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}.\end{split}

Therefore it holds for suitable constants
A
and
B
,

(2.33)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}(A\tr f_{\epsilon}+|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}^{2})\geq-C_{7}+B|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}},\end{split}

and thus
|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}(x)\leq C_{8}
, where
A\tr f_{\epsilon}+|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}^{2}
attains its maximum at
x
. This implies

(2.34)

\begin{split}\max\limits_{M}|\nabla f_{\epsilon}|^{2}\leq C_{9}(1+|\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}^{2}(x))\leq C_{10}.\end{split}

Now for the Laplician
\Delta_{\nabla_{K}}=\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\nabla_{K}+\nabla_{K}\nabla_{K}^{\ast}
, we have

(2.35)

\begin{split}\Delta_{\nabla_{K}}f_{\epsilon}&=-\frac{1}{2}\tr_{g}(\nabla\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon}+\delta_{K}\nabla f_{\epsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\tr_{g}[\psi_{K},[\psi_{K},f_{\epsilon}]].\end{split}

The elliptic
L^{p}
-theory indicates

(2.36)

\begin{split}||f_{\epsilon}||_{L_{2}^{p}}&\leq C_{11}(||f_{\epsilon}||_{L^{p}}+||\Delta_{\nabla_{K}}f_{\epsilon}||_{L^{p}})\\
&\leq C_{12}(1+||\tr_{g}\nabla\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon}||_{L^{p}})\\
&\leq C_{13},\end{split}

where we have used
\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K}+\frac{1}{2}\tr_{g}(\nabla f_{\epsilon}^{-1}\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon}+f_{\epsilon}^{-1}\nabla\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon})=\epsilon\log f_{\epsilon}
. Finally, the higher order estimates follow from the elliptic regularity.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\lim\sup\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}||\log f_{\epsilon}||_{L^{2}}=\infty
, there is a nontrivial
\nabla

.

Proof.

We may assume

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}|\log f_{\epsilon}|^{2}&=2\tr_{g}dK(\delta_{K}\log f_{\epsilon},\log f_{\epsilon})\\

&=2(\tr_{g}\nabla(f^{-1}_{\epsilon}\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon}),\log f_{\epsilon})+2(f^{-1}\delta_{K}f_{\epsilon},\delta_{K}\log f_{\epsilon})\\

where

\begin{split}l\Phi(lx,ly)&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}l,\ x\leq y,\\

(2.43)

\begin{split}\int_{M}(\rho[\widetilde{\log f_{\epsilon}}](\nabla\widetilde{\log f_{\epsilon}}),\nabla\widetilde{\log f_{\epsilon}})\dvol_{g}\leq 2\int_{M}(\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K},\widetilde{\log f_{\epsilon}})\dvol_{g}.\end{split}

for any

and

is small enough. Due to the zeroth order estimate of

\begin{split}\widetilde{\log f_{\epsilon}}\rightarrow u_{\infty}\in L_{1}^{2}S_{K},\end{split}

weakly in

-topology. Meanwhile, we have

\begin{split}\int_{M}(\rho[u_{\infty}](\nabla u_{\infty}),\nabla u_{\infty})\dvol_{g}\leq 2\int_{M}(\nabla_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{K},u_{\infty})\dvol_{g}<\infty.\end{split}

As long as
(\ref{Simpson})
is established, by using a similar discussion as
[
33

we know the eigenvalues of
u_{\infty}
are constants almost everywhere. Since
u_{\infty}\neq 0
and

(2.46)

\begin{split}\int_{M}\tr u_{\infty}\dvol_{g}&=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{M}\tr\widetilde{\log f_{\epsilon}}\dvol_{g}\\
&=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\epsilon^{-1}l_{\epsilon}^{-1}\int_{M}\tr\nabla^{\ast}_{H_{\epsilon}}\psi_{H_{\epsilon}}\dvol_{g}\\
&=0,\end{split}

it admits at least two distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
with

and

(we may assume

(2.47)

\begin{split}\Pi_{s}^{\ast}=\Pi_{s}=\Pi_{s}^{2}.\end{split}

Applying

and a similar discussion as

\begin{split}0&=(\id_{E}-\Pi_{s})\circ\nabla\Pi_{s}\\
&=(\id_{E}-\Pi_{s})\circ\nabla\circ\Pi_{s}\\
&=\nabla(\id_{E}-\Pi_{s})\circ\Pi_{s},\end{split}

and taking adjoint gives

(2.49)

\begin{split}0=&\delta_{K}\Pi_{s}\circ(\id_{E}-\Pi_{s})\\
&=-\Pi_{s}\circ\delta_{K}\circ(\id_{E}-\Pi_{s})\\

2.3.
Consequences

A triple
(E,\nabla,H)
will be called a harmonic bundle if
H
is harmonic. The semi-simpleness of a harmonic bundle follows from a vector bundle analogue of Gauss-Codazzi equations. In fact, for any
\nabla
-invariant sub-bundle
F
, we write
E=F\oplus F^{\perp}
orthogonally and
\nabla
takes the following form

(2.53)

\begin{split}\nabla=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla_{F}&\beta_{F}\\
0&\nabla_{F^{\perp}}\end{array}\right),\end{split}

where
\nabla_{F}
(
\nabla_{F^{\perp}}
) is the induced connection on
F
(
F^{\perp}
) and
\beta_{F}\in A^{1}(M,\Hom(F^{\perp},F))
is referred as the second fundamental form. It is easily known that

(2.54)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla_{F,H}&\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}^{\ast}&\nabla_{F^{\perp},H}\end{array}\right),\ \psi_{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\psi_{F,H}&\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}\\
\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}^{\ast}&\psi_{F^{\perp},H}\end{array}\right),\end{split}

where we decompose
\nabla_{F}=\nabla_{F,H}+\psi_{F,H}
and

\nabla_{F^{\perp}}=\nabla_{F^{\perp},H}+\psi_{F^{\perp},H}
.
We may choose a local normal coordinate and deduce

(2.55)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}&=\sum_{i=1}^{\dim M}[\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\psi_{F,H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})&\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})\\
\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}^{\ast}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})&\psi_{F^{\perp},H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla_{F,H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}&\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})\\
-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}^{\ast}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})&\nabla_{F^{\perp},H,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}\end{array}\right)]\\
&=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla_{F,H}^{\ast}\psi_{F,H}&\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{(F^{\perp})^{\ast}\otimes F,H}^{\ast}\beta_{F}\\
\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{F^{\ast}\otimes F^{\perp},H}^{\ast}\beta_{F}^{\ast}&\nabla_{F^{\perp},H}^{\ast}\psi_{F^{\perp},H}\end{array}\right)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{\dim M}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})\circ\beta_{F}^{\ast}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})&\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{(F^{\perp})^{\ast}\otimes F,H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),\beta_{F}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]\\
-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{F^{\ast}\otimes F^{\perp},H}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}),\beta_{F}^{\ast}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})]&\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}^{\ast}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})\circ\beta_{F}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})\end{array}\right),\end{split}

By the harmonicity of
H
, we have

(2.56)

\begin{split}2\nabla_{F,H}^{\ast}\psi_{F,H}=\sum_{i=1}^{\dim M}\beta_{F}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}})\circ\beta_{F}^{\ast}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}).\end{split}

Taking trace on both sides and integrating over
M
indicate
\beta_{F}=0
, therefore

(2.57)

\begin{split}(E,\nabla,H)=(F,\nabla_{F},H)\oplus(F^{\perp},\nabla_{F^{\perp}},H)\end{split}

splits as harmonic bundles. By an induction argument we conclude that
(E,\nabla)
is semi-simple and combing this with Proposition
2.2
yields

Theorem 2.1
.

The existence of harmonic metrics and the semi-simpleness are equivalent.

Corollary 2.1
.

Given a Hermitian vector bundle
(E,K)
, we have a natural bijection:

(2.58)

\begin{split}I_{K}:\mathcal{M}_{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{I,K}.\end{split}

Proof.

Given a simple connection
\nabla
with
\nabla f=0
for
f\in\Gamma(\End(E))
. Let

(2.59)

\begin{split}g(\lambda)=(\lambda-\lambda_{1})^{m_{1}}...(\lambda-\lambda_{s})^{m_{s}}\end{split}

be the characteristic polynomial of
f
, where
\lambda_{j}
are the
s
mutually different eigenvalues of
f
. It is known that
\tr f^{k}
must be constants since
d\tr f^{k}=\tr\nabla f^{k}=0
. In particular, we conclude
\lambda_{j}
,
m_{j}
are constants and we decompose
E
into the eigenspaces of
f
:

(2.60)

\begin{split}(E,\nabla)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}(E_{\lambda_{i}},\nabla|_{E_{\lambda_{i}}}).\end{split}

If
s\geq 2
one finds at least one nontrivial
\nabla
-invariant sub-bundle, contradicting to the simpleness. Therefore
f
only has one eigenvalue, saying
\lambda
. However the simpleness again implies
\ker(f-\lambda\id_{E})=E
,
f=\lambda\id_{E}
and hence
\nabla
is irreducible. Furthermore, there is a connection in the
\Aut(E)
-orbit through
\nabla
such that
K
is harmonic. In fact, we pick
H
, the harmonic metric given in Theorem
2.1
and it follows for
K^{-1}H=h^{\ast K}h
,

(2.61)

\begin{split}h(\nabla)_{K}^{\ast}\psi_{h(\nabla),K}=h(\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H})=0.\end{split}

If
K^{-1}H=\tilde{h}^{\ast K}\tilde{h}
,
\tilde{h}(\nabla)
is isomorphic to
h(\nabla)
via
\tilde{h}\circ h^{-1}\in\Unitary(E,K)
. Moreover, let
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
be two harmonic metrics and
h_{12}=H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}
, we have

(2.62)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}\tr h_{12}=(h_{12}^{-1}\delta_{H_{1}}h_{12},\delta_{H_{1}}h_{12})_{H_{1}}=|h_{12}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{H_{1}}h_{12}|_{H_{1}}^{2},\end{split}

from which and the simpleness we know that
h_{12}
is a constant mutiple of
\id_{E}
. Consequently,
\nabla
uniquely corresponds to an element, saying
I_{K}(\nabla)=[h(\nabla)]\in\mathcal{M}_{I,K}
. In addition, assuming
I_{K}(\nabla)=[h_{1}(\nabla)]
and
I_{K}(g(\nabla))=[h_{2}(g(\nabla))]
, it is not hard to see

(2.63)

\begin{split}h_{2}(g(\nabla))=(h_{2}\circ g\circ h_{1}^{-1})(h_{1}(\nabla)),\ h_{2}\circ g\circ h_{1}^{-1}\in\Unitary(E,K).\end{split}

That is,
I_{K}
desends to a bijection from
\mathcal{M}_{S}
to
\mathcal{M}_{I,K}
, as required.
∎

3.
An application in Sasakian geometry

3.1.
Sasakian manifolds in pseudo-Hermitian geometry

We start with some basic facts on Sasakian manifolds from the viewpoint of pseudo-Hermitian geometry, more details can be found in
[
5
,
12
]
. Let
M
be a
2n+1
-dimensional smooth manifold, a CR structure on
M
is an integrable rank
n
complex sub-bundle

(3.1)

\begin{split}T_{1,0}M\subseteq T^{\mathbb{C}}M=TM\otimes\mathbb{C}\end{split}

satisfying
T_{1,0}M\cap T_{0,1}M=\{0\}
for
T_{0,1}M=\overline{T_{1,0}M}
. We then call
(M,T_{1,0}M)
a CR manifold and its maximal complex or Levi, distribution is the real rank
2n
real sub-bundle

(3.2)

HM=Re\{T_{1,0}M\oplus T_{0,1}M\}\subseteq TM.

It carries a complex structure
J:HM\rightarrow HM
given by

(3.3)

J(X+\overline{X})=\sqrt{-1}(X-\overline{X}),\ X\in T_{1,0}M.

Assume
M
to be orientable, we define

(3.4)

E_{x}=\{\omega\in T^{\ast}_{x}M,HM_{x}\subseteq\ker\omega\}\subseteq T_{x}^{\ast}M,

then
E
a real line bundle and any globally defined nowhere vanishing section
\eta
is called a pseudo-Hermitian structure and the associated Levi form
L_{\eta}
is defined by

(3.5)

\begin{split}L_{\eta}(X,\overline{Y})=-\sqrt{-1}d\eta(X,\overline{Y}),\ X,Y\in T_{1,0}M.\end{split}

An orientable CR manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M)
is nondegenerate if
L_{\eta}
is nondegenerate for a pseudo-Hermitian structure
\eta
and
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
is called a pseudo-Hermitian manifold. Moreover,
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
is said to be strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold if
L_{\eta}
is positive definite.

For a pseudo-Hermitian manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
,
d\eta
is nondegenerate on
HM
and thus there is a unique nonvanishing vector field
\xi
(referred to as the characteristic direction) such that

(3.6)

\begin{split}\eta(\xi)=1,\ d\eta(\xi,\bullet)=0,\end{split}

and
\xi
is transverse to the Levi distribution(that is
TM=HM\oplus\mathbb{R}\xi
). Define a bilinear form

(3.7)

\begin{split}G_{\eta}(X,Y)=d\eta(X,JY),\ X,Y\in HM,\end{split}

the integrability of
T_{1,0}M
implies that
G_{\eta}
is
J
-invariant and thus symmetric. One may extend
G_{\eta}
to a semi-Riemannian(Riemannian if strictly pseudoconvex CR) metric via

(3.8)

\begin{split}g_{\eta}(X,Y)=G_{\eta}(X,Y),\ g_{\eta}(X,\xi)=0,\ g_{\eta}(\xi,\xi)=1,X,Y\in HM,\end{split}

and we call it the Webster metric.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
36
,
40
]
, see also
[
12
]
)
.

For a pseudo-Hermitian manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
, we extend
J
to an endomorphism of
TM
by requiring
J\xi=0
. There exists a unique affine connection
\nabla^{TM}
(called Tanaka-Webster connection) on
TM
such that

(1)

HM
is parallel with respect to
\nabla^{TM}
,

(2)

\nabla^{TM}g_{\eta}=0
,
\nabla^{TM}J
=0 and
\nabla^{TM}\xi=0
,

(3)

The torsion
T_{\nabla^{TM}}
is pure:

(a)

\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}\circ J+J\circ\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}=0
,

(b)

T_{\nabla^{TM}}(X,Y)=0
,
T_{\nabla^{TM}}(X,\overline{Y})=d\eta(X,\overline{Y})\xi
,

where
\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}=T_{\nabla^{TM}}(\xi,\bullet)
(called the pseudo-Hermitian torsion) and
X,Y\in T_{1,0}M
.

As an endomorphism, it is known that

(3.9)

\begin{split}\tr\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}=0,\ \tau_{\nabla^{TM}}(T_{1,0}M)\subseteq T_{0,1}M.\end{split}

On the other hand, the relation between the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g_{\eta}}
and the Tanaka-Webster connection
\nabla^{TM}
is given by(see
[
12
]
)

(3.10)

\begin{split}\nabla^{g_{\eta}}_{X}Y-\nabla^{TM}_{X}Y&=\left(g_{\eta}(X,J(Y))-g_{\eta}(\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}(X),Y)\right)\xi\\
&+\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}(X)\eta(Y)+\eta(X)J(Y)+\eta(Y)J(X),\end{split}

for two vector fields
X,Y
. Define a vector field
U=\tr_{g_{\eta}}(\nabla^{g_{\eta}}-\nabla^{TM})
and it follows

Lemma 3.1
.

For a strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
, we have
U=0
.

Definition 3.1
.

A strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
with vanishing pseudo-Hermitian torsion
\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}
is called a Sasakian manifold.

3.2.
A vanishing result

In what follows we always assume
\{e_{1}=\xi,e_{2},...,e_{2n+1}\}
is a local frame of
TM
and
\{e^{1}=\eta,e^{2},...,e^{2n+1}\}
is a local frame of
T^{\ast}M
. For a vector bundle
E
over
M
with a connection
\nabla
and a metric
K
on
E
, we shall use
\nabla_{K}
to denote the connection on
\Lambda^{\bullet}T^{\ast}M\otimes E
which acts on
\Lambda^{\bullet}T^{\ast}M
by
\nabla^{TM}
and acts on
E
by
\nabla_{K}
.
We take the notation

(3.11)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H,X,Y}^{2}\omega=\nabla_{H,X}\nabla_{H,Y}\omega-\nabla_{H,\nabla^{TM}_{X}Y}\omega,\end{split}

for two vector fields
X,Y
and
\omega\in A^{\bullet}(E)
.

A connection
\nabla
on a complex vector bundle
E
is called projective flat if the curvature satisfies
\sqrt{-1}F_{\nabla}=\alpha\otimes\id_{E}
for a
2
-form
\alpha
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(E,\nabla)
be a projective flat complex vector bundle over a compact Sasakian manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
and
H
be a metric on
E
with
\nabla_{H,\xi}\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}=0
, then
\nabla_{H}(\psi_{H}(\xi))=0
.

Proof.

We first note Lemma
3.1
implies

(3.12)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,e_{i}}(\psi_{H}(e_{j}))-g_{\eta}^{ij}\psi_{H}(\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}e_{j})\\
&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,e_{i}}(\psi_{H}(e_{j}))-g_{\eta}^{ij}\psi_{H}(\nabla^{TM}_{e_{i}}e_{j})\end{split}

The projective flatness means
D_{H}\psi_{H}=0,\ \sqrt{-1}(F_{\nabla_{H}}+\psi_{H}\wedge\psi_{H})=\alpha\otimes\id_{E}
for
\alpha\in A^{2}(M)
. Next we deduce the symmetry

(3.13)

\begin{split}\nabla_{H,e_{i}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))&=\nabla_{H,e_{i}}\psi_{H}(\xi)+\psi_{H}(\nabla^{TM}_{e_{i}}\xi)\\
&=\nabla_{H,\xi}\psi_{H}(e_{i})-\psi_{H}(\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}(e_{i}))-D_{H}\psi_{H}(\xi,e_{i})\\
&=\nabla_{H,\xi}(\psi_{H}(e_{i})),\end{split}

where we haved used
\nabla^{TM}\xi=0
and the vanishing of pseudo-Hermitian torsion. Using these properties again,
(\ref{new2})
and
(\ref{new1})
repeatedly, we have

(3.14)

\begin{split}\tr_{g_{\eta}}\nabla_{H}^{2}(\psi_{H}(\xi))&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,e_{i}}\nabla_{H,e_{j}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))-g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,\nabla^{TM}_{e_{i}}e_{j}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))\\
&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{H,e_{i},\xi}(\psi_{H}(e_{j}))-g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,\nabla^{TM}_{e_{i}}e_{j}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))\\
&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{H,\xi,e_{i}}(\psi_{H}(e_{j}))-g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,\nabla^{TM}_{e_{i}}e_{j}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))\\
&+g_{\eta}^{ij}\left([F_{\nabla_{H}}(e_{i},\xi),\psi_{H}(e_{j})]+\nabla_{H,\tau_{\nabla^{TM}}(e_{i})}(\psi_{H}(e_{j}))\right)\\
&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{H,\xi,e_{i}}(\psi_{H}(e_{j}))-g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,\nabla^{TM}_{e_{i}}e_{j}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))\\
&-\frac{1}{2}g_{\eta}^{ij}[[\psi_{H}(e_{i}),\psi_{H}(\xi)],\psi_{H}(e_{j})]\\
&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,\xi}\nabla_{H,e_{i}}(\psi_{H}(e_{j}))-g_{\eta}^{ij}\nabla_{H,\xi}(\psi_{H}(\nabla^{TM}_{e_{i}}e_{j}))\\
&-\frac{1}{2}g_{\eta}^{ij}[[\psi_{H}(e_{i}),\psi_{H}(\xi)],\psi_{H}(e_{j})]\\
&=-\nabla_{H,\xi}\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\eta}^{ij}[[\psi_{H}(e_{i}),\psi_{H}(\xi)],\psi_{H}(e_{j})].\end{split}

By Lemma
3.1
and the assumption on
H
, it follows

(3.15)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g_{\eta}}|\psi_{H}(\xi)|^{2}_{H}&=\Div(\nabla^{TM}|\psi_{H}(\xi)|^{2}_{H})\\
&=2(\tr_{g_{\eta}}\nabla^{2}_{H}(\psi_{H}(\xi)),\psi_{H}(\xi))_{H}+2|\nabla_{H}(\psi_{H}(\xi))|^{2}_{H}\\
&=-(g_{\eta}^{ij}[[\psi_{H}(e_{i}),\psi_{H}(\xi)],\psi_{H}(e_{j})],\psi_{H}(\xi))_{H}^{2}+2|\nabla_{H}(\psi_{H}(\xi))|^{2}_{H}\\
&=|[\psi_{H}(\xi),\psi_{H}]|_{H}^{2}+2|\nabla_{H}(\psi_{H}(\xi))|^{2}_{H},\end{split}

and we thus conclude
\nabla_{H}(\psi_{H}(\xi))=0
by maximum principle.
∎

Under the setting in Lemma
3.2
, we define
\mathcal{D}=g_{\eta}^{ij}\left(e^{i}\wedge\nabla_{H,e_{j}}-\iota(e_{i})\nabla_{H,e_{j}}\right)
, the Dirac operator on
\Lambda^{\ast}T^{\ast}M\otimes\End(E)
. By
(\ref{new2})
and the projective flatness, we have

(3.16)

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}\psi_{H}=g_{\eta}^{ij}e^{i}\wedge\nabla_{H,e_{j}}\psi_{H}+\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H},\end{split}

and for any
X,Y\in TM
,

(3.17)

\begin{split}(g_{\eta}^{ij}e^{i}\wedge\nabla_{H,e_{j}}\psi_{H})(X,Y)&=\nabla_{H,X}\psi_{H}(Y)-\nabla_{H,Y}\psi_{H}(X)\\
&=\psi_{H}(T_{\nabla^{TM}}(X,Y))+D_{H}\psi_{H}(X,Y)\\
&=\psi_{H}(T_{\nabla^{TM}}(X,Y)).\end{split}

Therefore we have
\mathcal{D}\psi_{H}=d\eta\otimes\psi_{H}(\xi)+\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}
due to Proposition
3.1
and the vanishing of pseudo-Hermitian torsion. Furthermore it holds

(3.18)

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{2}\psi_{H}&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\left(e^{i}\wedge\nabla_{H,e_{j}}(d\eta\otimes\psi_{H}(\xi))-(\nabla_{H,e_{j}}(d\eta\otimes\psi_{H}(\xi)))(e_{i})\right)\\
&=g_{\eta}^{ij}\left(e^{i}\wedge d\eta\otimes\nabla_{H,e_{j}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))-d\eta(e_{i},\bullet)\otimes\nabla_{H,e_{j}}(\psi_{H}(\xi))\right),\end{split}

where we have used
\nabla^{TM}d\eta=0
. Thanks to Lemma
3.2
, we find
\mathcal{D}\psi_{H}=0
since
\mathcal{D}
is formal self-adjoint(see
[
31
, Proposition 2.1]
). Hence, we have the following vanishing result which is essential to construct basic Higgs structures.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(E,\nabla)
be a projective flat complex vector bundle over a compact Sasakian manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
and
H
be a metric on
E
with
\nabla_{H,\xi}\nabla_{H}^{\ast}\psi_{H}=0
, then
H
is exactly harmonic and satisfies
\psi_{H}(\xi)=0
.

3.3.
Correspondence between projective flat bundles and Higgs bundles

Below we assume the base space is a compact Sasakian manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
and denote by
A_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{\ast}(M)
the space of basic forms, meaning that any
\omega\in A_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{\ast}(M)
satisfies
\iota_{\xi}\omega=0
and
L_{\xi}\omega=0
. Since
\ker\eta\otimes\mathbb{C}=T_{1,0}M\oplus T_{0,1}M
, we similar have the decomposition

(3.19)

\begin{split}A_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{k}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{p+q=k}A_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{p,q}(M),\end{split}

where
A_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{p,q}(M)
is the sub-space of basic forms of type
(p,q)
. It is easy to see the exterior differential preserves basic forms and hence
d=\partial_{\xi}+\overline{\partial}_{\xi}
on
A_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{k}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}
, such that

(3.20)

\begin{split}\partial_{\xi}:A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q}(M)\rightarrow A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p+1,q}(M),\ \overline{\partial}_{\xi}:A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q}(M)\rightarrow A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q+1}(M).\end{split}

In the presence of the CR structure
T_{1,0}M
and the complex structure
J
, there is a transverse holomorphic structure on
M
(with the transverse Kähler structure
d\eta
) as well as the transverse Hodge theory on the foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F}_{\xi})
, see
[
37
, Chapter 7]
and
[
2
, pp.275]
.

Let
E
be a complex vector bundle over
M
, it is called transverse holomorphic if it can be trivialized by an open cover
M=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}
so that each transition function
f_{\alpha\beta}
is basic and holomorphic(that is,
\overline{\partial}_{\xi}f_{\alpha\beta}=0
). We have the canonical Dolbeault operator

(3.21)

\begin{split}\overline{\partial}_{E}:A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q}(M.E)\rightarrow A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q+1}(M,E),\end{split}

which satisfies
\overline{\partial}_{E}^{2}=0
and for
\alpha\in A^{p,q}_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(M)
,
u\in A^{0}_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(M,E)
,

(3.22)

\begin{split}\overline{\partial}_{E}(\alpha\wedge u)=\overline{\partial}_{\xi}\alpha\otimes u+(-1)^{p+q}\alpha\wedge\overline{\partial}_{E}u.\end{split}

A basic Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
consists of a transverse holomorphic bundle
E
and a Higgs field
\theta\in A_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{1,0}(M,\End(E))
satisfying
\overline{\partial}_{E}\theta=0
,
\theta\wedge\theta=0
. Assume further
E
admits a basic Hermitian metric
H
, one may take all nontrivial sub-Higgs sheaves to define the notion of poly-stability on
(E,\theta)
in the manner of
[
1
, Section 3.3]
. We say a connection
\nabla
on a vector bundle
E
is basic if the associated
D
restricts to a homomorphism

(3.23)

\begin{split}D:A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{k}(M,E)\rightarrow A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{k+1}(M,E),\end{split}

and the pair
(E,\nabla)
is said to be a basic vector bundle. For a basic connection
\nabla
on
E
, we may define
c_{k,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(E,\nabla)\in A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{2k}(M,E)
by

(3.24)

\begin{split}\det(I+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}F_{\nabla})=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}c_{k,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(E,\nabla).\end{split}

For each
k
, the class
c_{k,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(E,\nabla)
in
H^{\ast}_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(M)
, the cohomology of complex

(3.25)

\begin{split}(A^{\ast}_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(M),d|_{A^{\ast}_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(M)}),\end{split}

is independent of the choice of basic connections and we just write
c_{k,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(E)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
be a compact Sasakian manifold of dimension
2n+1
and
(E,\nabla)
be a rank
r
semi-simple basic projective flat complex vector bundle over
M
, then it determines a poly-stable basic Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
over
M
such that

(3.26)

\begin{split}\int_{M}\left(c_{2,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(E)-\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{2}(E)\right)\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\wedge\eta=0.\end{split}

Proof.

We employ Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
3.2
to find a harmonic metric
H
on
(E,\nabla)
satisfying
\psi_{H}(\xi)=0
. It follows that
\nabla_{H}
induces a homomorphism

(3.27)

\begin{split}D_{H}:A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{k}(M,E)\rightarrow A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{k+1}(M,E).\end{split}

We decompose
D_{H}=\partial_{H,\xi}+\overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}
and
\psi_{H}=\theta_{H,\xi}+\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}
such that

(3.28)

\begin{split}\partial_{H,\xi}:A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q}(M,E)\rightarrow A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p+1,q}(M,E),\ \overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}:A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q}(M,E)\rightarrow A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{p,q+1}(M,E),\end{split}

and
\theta_{H,\xi}\in A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{1,0}(M,\End(E))
,
\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}\in A_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{0,1}(M,\End(E))
.

The projective flatness implies

(3.29)

\begin{split}\partial_{H,\xi}\theta_{H,\xi}=0,\ \overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}=0,\ \partial_{H,\xi}\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}+\overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}\theta_{H,\xi}=0,\ \end{split}

(3.30)

\begin{split}\partial_{H,\xi}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}[\theta_{H,\xi},\theta_{H,\xi}],\ \overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}[\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi},\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}],\ \end{split}

(3.31)

\begin{split}[\partial_{H,\xi},\overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}]=-[\theta_{H,\xi},\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}]+\frac{\tr F_{\nabla}}{r}\otimes\id_{E}.\end{split}

Set
D^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{H,\xi}=\overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}+\theta_{H,\xi}
and
G_{H,\xi}=D^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{H,\xi}\circ D^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{H,\xi}
, it follows

(3.32)

\begin{split}\tr(G_{H,\xi}\wedge G_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}&=-2\tr(\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}\wedge\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}\wedge\theta_{H,\xi}\wedge\theta_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\\
&-\tr(\overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}\theta_{H,\xi}\wedge\partial_{H,\xi}\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\\
&=-2\tr(\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}\wedge\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}\wedge\theta_{H,\xi}\wedge\theta_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\\
&-\overline{\partial}\tr(\theta_{H,\xi}\wedge\partial_{H,\xi}\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\\
&+\tr(\theta_{H,\xi}\wedge[[\theta_{H,\xi},\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}]-\frac{\tr F_{\nabla}}{r}\otimes\id_{E},\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}])\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\\
&=-\overline{\partial}\left(\tr(\theta_{H,\xi}\wedge\partial_{H,\xi}\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\right).\end{split}

The harmonicity of
H
is equivalent to
\Lambda_{\xi}G_{H,\xi}=0
, meaning
G_{H,\xi}
is primitive, where
\Lambda_{\xi}
is the adjoint of the multiplication of
d\eta
. Thus

(3.33)

\begin{split}\tr(G_{H,\xi}\wedge G_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}&=2C_{1}\tr(G_{H,\xi}^{2,0}\wedge\ast_{\xi}G_{H,\xi}^{0,2})-C_{1}\tr(G_{H,\xi}^{1,1}\wedge\ast_{\xi}G_{H,\xi}^{1,1})\\
&=2C_{1}\tr(G_{H,\xi}^{2,0}\wedge\ast_{\xi}(G_{H,\xi}^{2,0})^{\ast H})+C_{1}\tr(G_{H,\xi}^{1,1}\wedge\ast_{\xi}(G_{H,\xi}^{1,1})^{\ast H})\\
&=C_{1}\tr\left(G_{H,\xi}\wedge\ast_{\xi}G_{H,\xi}^{\ast H}\right),\end{split}

for a constant
C_{1}
, where
\ast_{\xi}
is the basic Hodge star operator given by
\ast_{\xi}\beta=\ast(\eta\wedge\beta)
for
\beta\in A^{\ast}_{B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(M)
. Therefore integrating
\tr(G_{H,\xi}\wedge G_{H,\xi})\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\wedge\eta
over
M
yields
G_{H,\xi}=0
, meaning
(E,\theta)
is a basic Higgs bundle with canonical Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}_{H,\xi}
. In addition, the calculation in
[
33
, Proposition 3.4]
shows for a constant
C_{2}
,

(3.34)

\begin{split}&\int_{M}\left(c_{2,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(E)-\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{2}(E)\right)\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\wedge\eta\\
&=C_{2}\int_{M}\left(|F_{\nabla_{H}+\theta_{H,\xi}+\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}}^{\perp}|^{2}_{H}-|\Lambda_{\xi}F_{\nabla_{H}+\theta_{H,\xi}+\overline{\theta}_{H,\xi}}^{\perp}|^{2}_{H}\right)\dvol_{g_{\eta}}\\
&=0,\end{split}

by the projective flatness. Using the basic metric
H
, the stability of basic Higgs bundle can be well-defined and one easily concludes the poly-stability, see
[
1
, Theorem 4.7]
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
be a compact Sasakian manifold of dimension
2n+1
and
(E,\theta)
be a rank
r
poly-stable basic Higgs bundle satisfying
(\ref{Chernnumber})
over
M
, then it determines a semi-simple basic projective flat complex vector bundle
(E,\nabla)
over
M
.

Proof.

The Sasakian analogue of
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
shows that there exists a basic Hermitian metric
H
on
(E,\theta)
such that(see
[
2
]
)

(3.35)

\begin{split}\Lambda_{\xi}F_{\nabla}^{\perp}=0,\ \nabla=\nabla_{H}+\theta+\theta^{\ast H},\end{split}

where
\nabla_{H}
is the canonical basic Chern connection. Now by
(\ref{Chernnumber2})
it follows

(3.36)

\begin{split}0&=\int_{M}\left(c_{2,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}(E)-\frac{r-1}{2r}c_{1,B_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}}^{2}(E)\right)\wedge(d\eta)^{n-2}\wedge\eta\\
&=C_{2}\int_{M}\left(|F_{\nabla}^{\perp}|^{2}_{H}-|\Lambda_{\xi}F_{\nabla}^{\perp}|^{2}_{H}\right)\dvol_{g_{\eta}}\\
&=C_{2}\int_{M}|F_{\nabla}^{\perp}|^{2}_{H}\dvol_{g_{\eta}}.\end{split}

This means
(E,\nabla)
is a semi-simple basic projective flat complex vector bundle.
∎

Suppsoe that
(F,\nabla)
is a flat vector bundle with a harmonic metric
H
, over a compact Sasakian manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
. Define
H_{DR,\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{0}(M,F)
to be the space of
\nabla
-parallel elements of
A^{0}_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}(M,F)
and
H_{Dol,\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{0}(M,F)
to be the space of
D_{H}^{0,1}+\psi_{H}^{1,0}
-parallel elements of
A^{0}_{\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}(M,F)
.

In view of Proposition
3.3
, Proposition
3.4
and the natural isomorphism

(3.37)

\begin{split}H_{DR,\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{0}(M,F)\cong H_{Dol,\mathcal{F}_{\xi}}^{0}(M,F),\end{split}

via transverse Kähler identities(just as
[
35
, Lemma 1.2]
), we conclude

Theorem 3.1
.

For a compact Sasakian manifold
(M,T_{1,0}M,\eta)
of dimension
2n+1
, there exists an equivalence between the following two categories:

•

Semi-simple basic projective flat complex vector bundles over
M
.

•

Poly-stable basic Higgs bundles satisfying
(\ref{Chernnumber})
over
M
.

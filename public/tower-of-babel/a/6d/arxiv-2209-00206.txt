Free Fermion Cyclic/Symmetric Orbifold CFTs and Entanglement Entropy
Tadashi Takayanagi, and Takashi Tsuda

Abstract
In this paper we study the properties of two-dimensional CFTs defined by cyclic and symmetric orbifolds of free Dirac fermions, especially by focusing on the partition function and entanglement entropy. Via the bosonization, we construct the twist operators which glue two complex planes to calculate the partition function of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} orbifold CFT on a torus. We also find an expression of ℤ N \mathbb{Z}_{N} cyclic orbifold in terms of Hecke operators, which provides an explicit relation between the partition functions of cyclic orbifolds and those of symmetric ones. We compute the entanglement entropy and Renyi entropy in cyclic orbifolds on a circle both for finite temperature states and for time-dependent states under quantum quenches. We find that the replica method calculation is highly non-trivial and new because of the contributions from replicas with different boundary conditions. We find the full expression for the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} orbifold and show that the periodicity gets doubled. Finally, we discuss extensions of our results on entanglement entropy to symmetric orbifold CFTs and make a heuristic argument towards holographic CFTs.

Introduction

Symmetric orbifold CFTs play important roles to explain black hole entropy microscopically, especially for D1-D5 systems
Strominger:1996sh
.
Such a microscopic model is typically described by a two-dimensional sigma model, whose target space is an instanton moduli space
Vafa:1995bm
;
Vafa:1995zh
;
Douglas:1995bn
. Although the geometry of this moduli space is complicated, the space is a singularity-resolved manifold of a symmetric product of the moduli space of a single instanton
Dijkgraaf:1998gf
;
Aspinwall:1995zi
. One specific type of the resolution is given by the symmetric orbifold CFTs. This can be regarded as a special example of AdS/CFT
Maldacena:1997re
. In order to have a genuine dual of classical gravity (namely holographic CFTs), we need to introduce strong interactions in the symmetric orbifolds via a suitable marginal deformation
Larsen:1999uk
;
Avery:2010er
;
Carson:2014ena
. Nevertheless, the free symmetric orbifold CFT before the deformation acquires the reputation of “something similar to two-dimensional holographic CFTs”. Indeed, the match of the BPS spectrum was pointed out
deBoer:1998us
for example.

Generally, to define a symmetric orbifold CFT, we start with an arbitrary seed CFT
\mathcal{C}
with a central charge
c
. The
N
-th symmetric orbifold CFT
\mathcal{C}_{N,S}
is defined by orbifolding the product
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes N}
by the symmetric group
S_{N}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{N,S}=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{C}^{\otimes N}/S_{N},

(1)

which has a central charge
Nc
. Every quantity in
\mathcal{C}_{N,S}
is completely determined by the choice of seed theory
\mathcal{C}
in principle.

Interestingly, symmetric orbifold CFTs are equipped with some important properties which are necessary conditions for 2-dimensional CFTs to be holographic
Heemskerk:2009pn
;
El-Showk:2011yvt
. The most significant feature is the presence of the Hagedorn transition in the large
N
limit regardless of the choice of the seed theory, as shown in
Keller:2011xi
;
Hartman:2014oaa
. The reason for this is that each of them has a sparse low-energy spectrum in addition to that they obviously have a large central charge
Nc
. Moreover, some multi-point correlation functions in symmetric orbifold CFTs obey the large
N
factorization
Pakman:2009zz
;
Belin:2015hwa
.

However, it has been known that symmetric orbifold CFTs are not exactly holographic. This is almost clear because the CFTs do not include interactions other than the orbifold projections. Thus they are not chaotic, which can be explicitly seen from the Lyapunov exponent calculations
Perlmutter:2016pkf
and the evolution of entanglement entropy
Caputa:2017tju
. Refer also to

Giusto:2014aba
;
Balasubramanian:2014sra
;
Balasubramanian:2016ids
;
Balasubramanian:2018ajb
for other quantum information theoretic approaches to the symmetric product CFTs. Moreover, although symmetric orbifold CFTs have such sparse spectra that make Hagedorn transition possible, the spectra are still too dense to have classical gravity duals
Apolo:2022fya
. In the “bulk dual” point of view, the spectrum of symmetric orbifold CFTs contains too many higher-spin modes and thus the bulk dual is not an ordinary classical (super)gravity even if bulk dual exists.

Despite the above differences from what we expect for the holographic theory, investigating symmetric orbifold CFTs further is still very intriguing. This is partly because they provide tractable toy models of holographic CFTs. The mechanism of how holographic CFTs admit their AdS gravity duals is not yet fundamentally understood, and the emergence of bottom-up construction of holographic CFTs will aid in understanding. As we referred above, some specific marginal deformations are believed to remove unnecessary higher-spin modes and to deform the theory into an exact holographic CFT with a classical gravity dual. For the case of a symmetric orbifold CFT with an
\mathcal{N}=2
minimal model as the seed theory, the marginal operator that removes such higher-spin modes, is constructed recently
Belin:2020nmp
;
Apolo:2022fya
.
The study of symmetric orbifolds is also intriguing partly because they are an interesting class of CFTs whose dynamical properties have not been understood completely. For instance, recently, the complete set of boundary states in symmetric orbifold CFT was constructed and their typical behaviors were compared with those expected from gravity duals in an interesting way
Belin:2020nmp
;
Belin:2021nck
. In addition to these two motivations, specific symmetric orbifold theories alone, without any deformations, can admit higher-spin gravity dual: tensionless string theory on AdS geometry
Gaberdiel:2014cha
;
Giribet:2018ada
;
Gaberdiel:2018rqv
;
Eberhardt:2018ouy
;
Eberhardt:2019ywk
. Research in this direction may provide a more clear understanding of string geometry and ensemble average
Eberhardt:2021jvj
.

Every element of symmetric group
S_{N}
can be decomposed into products of elements of cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{{}_{i}}
, where
1\leq i\leq N
.
We can find a strong connection between a cyclic orbifold CFT and a symmetric orbifold CFT.
The
N
-th cyclic orbifold CFT
\mathcal{C}_{N,\mathbb{Z}}
can be obtained by orbifolding the tensor product of seed theory
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes N}
by the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{N,\mathbb{Z}}=

\displaystyle~\mathcal{C}^{\otimes N}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}.

(2)

The structure of cyclic orbifold CFT is much simpler than symmetric one, it is natural to start with a study of cyclic orbifold CFTs.
We can assemble the results of cyclic orbifolds to obtain quantities in symmetric ones.

We must clarify here the strong connection we referred above.
As for one of the well-known results of symmetric orbifold CFTs, their partition functions on a torus can be expressed in terms of so-called Hecke operators.
We will show that the cyclic orbifold CFT partition function on the torus can also be expressed in terms of Hecke operators. The fact that Hecke operators can describe both orbifold theories offers a strong connection.

The main purpose of the present paper is to explore the dynamical properties of cyclic and symmetric orbifold CFTs, motivated by the above observations. A useful quantity that characterizes the dynamical aspects is the entanglement entropy and its cousin, called Renyi entropy. These entropies are defined in terms of the reduced density matrix obtained by tracing out a subsystem.
These quantities estimate the degrees of freedom in a given quantum many-body system for static states. In particular, for two-dimensional CFTs, these entropies follow universal rules for simple choices of subsystems
Holzhey:1994we
;
Calabrese:2004eu
. However, for more non-trivial setups such as the entropies for double interval subsystems
Furukawa_2009
;
Headrick:2010zt
and those for a single interval subsystem in a finite size system at finite temperature, the results highly depend on the details of a given CFT, i.e. its spectrum and its interaction, as is manifest in the free Dirac fermion case
Azeyanagi:2007bj
;
Ogawa:2011bz
;
Mukhi:2017rex
;
Takayanagi:2010wp
. Moreover, they are helpful to characterize how a quantum state gets thermalized, being sensible to whether the theory is chaotic or not, under non-equilibrium processes such as the quantum quenches
Calabrese:2005in
.
If we apply the AdS/CFT correspondence
Maldacena:1997re
, such a non-equilibrium process is typically dual to a black hole formation
Abajo-Arrastia:2010ajo
;
Hartman:2013qma
, whose behavior of entanglement entropy can be computed from the holographic entanglement entropy
Ryu:2006bv
;
Ryu:2006ef
;
Hubeny:2007xt
and from its generalization to boundary conformal field theories
Takayanagi:2011zk
;
Fujita:2011fp
. In this paper, we will study the entanglement entropy and Renyi entropy for the above setups in cyclic and symmetric orbifold CFTs.

This paper is organized as follows.
In section 2, we briefly review the symmetric/cyclic orbifold CFT partition functions on a torus and then introduce Hecke operators. A symmetric orbifold CFT partition function on a torus can be expressed in terms of Hecke operators.
In section 3, we review the replica methods in 2-dimensional CFTs, and develop the method on Dirac fermion cyclic orbifold CFTs. We explicitly construct replica twist operators for this cyclic orbifold. We also discuss the relation between the second Renyi entropy for double intervals on a plane and the torus partition function.
In section 4, we calculate the Renyi and entanglement entropy in a finite size system at a finite temperature. For the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
cyclic orbifold CFT, we evaluate them by limiting to the ”diagonal sectors” in the CFT on replicated tori to Renyi and entanglement entropy analytically and show that they reproduce the expected part of the thermal entropy by taking the limit where the subsystem approaches the total system. However, the full contribution to the Renyi entropy can be obtained only after summing over both the diagonal and non-diagonal sectors. Even though this is difficult for general
N
, we solve this problem by focusing on the simplest case of the second Renyi entropy at
N=2
and obtain the full expression. We also find that a time-like 2-point function in
N
-th cyclic orbifold CFT can have a periodicity
N
times longer than that in the seed CFT.
In section 5, we extend the analysis in section 4 for cyclic orbifolds to the dynamical process of quantum quenches described by the CFT on replicated cylinders. We find that the periodicity of time-dependent entanglement entropy in the
N
-th cyclic orbifold CFT gets
N
times larger than that in the seed CFT.
In section 6, we introduce a new type of Hecke operator (we call square-free Hecke operator) by deforming the ordinary Hecke operator. This new operator is necessary for constructing cyclic orbifold CFT partition functions in terms of Hecke operators. Here, through Hecke operators, the connection between partition functions of symmetric orbifold CFTs and those of cyclic ones, becomes very clear.
By using this connection, we show the calculation of diagonal Renyi entropy in Dirac fermion symmetric orbifold theory.
In section 7, we discuss the connection between symmetric orbifold CFTs and holographic CFTs. We provide evidence for Dirac fermion symmetric orbifold theory to have
\exp N
recurrence time, which is a significantly long time even for such an integrable CFT.
In section 8, we summarize our conclusions and discuss future problems.
In appendix A, we present the definitions and properties of the theta functions. In appendix B, we present examples of Hecke operators. In appendix C, we explain the construction of orbifold partition functions.

Orbifold CFT preliminaries

In this section, we briefly review orbifold CFTs, especially so-called symmetric orbifold CFTs and cyclic orbifold CFTs.
We mainly focus on the partition functions of these orbifold CFTs, on a torus with complex structure

\displaystyle Z(\tau)=\sum_{m,\bar{m}\in I}d(m,\bar{m})q^{m}\bar{q}^{\bar{m}}.

(3)

2.1
General orbifold partition functions

The partition function of CFT orbifolded by some discrete group
G
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,h\in G\\
gh=hg\end{subarray}}{\scriptstyle h}\,\underset{\scriptstyle g}{\Box}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,h\in G\\
gh=hg\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Tr}_{(g)}\left[hq^{H_{L}}\bar{q}^{H_{R}}\right],

(6)

where
H_{L}
and
H_{R}
are the Hamiltonian in the left and right moving sectors, respectively.
Here,
g
labels the (un)twisted sectors, and running

corresponds to the projection.

2.2
Symmetric orbifold partition function

The generating function of the
N
-th symmetric orbifold partition function

is well-known

;
Dijkgraaf:1996xw
;
Hartman:2014oaa
;

\sum_{N\geq 0}p^{N}Z_{N,S}(\tau)=\prod_{n>0}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\bar{m}\in I\\

Here, product over

(8)

This insertion can be interpreted as performing an orbifold projection. We can calculate the effect of this projection explicitly by replacing

Alternatively, there is another expression of the generating function

where the partition of

\displaystyle(N_{1},\dots,N_{N})~\mathrm{s.t.}~\sum_{k=1}^{N}kN_{k}=N
.
Here we introduced the Hecke operator

T_{k}Z\left(\tau\right)\equiv\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i|k}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}Z\left(\frac{k\tau}{i^{2}}+\frac{j}{i}\right).

(10)

We will discuss the Hecke operator in detail in subsection
6.1
. Yet another expression of the generating function
Haehl:2014yla
reads:

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq 0}p^{N}Z_{N,S}(\tau)=\exp\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p^{k}T_{k}Z(\tau)\right].

(11)

Twist operators

A basic and powerful way to calculate the entanglement entropy in two-dimensional CFTs is to employ the replica method

Calabrese:2004eu

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{A}=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{A})^{n}],

(16)

where
\rho_{A}
is the reduced density matrix obtained by tracing out the complement of
A
for a given quantum state.
In the Euclidean path-integral, the quantity Tr
[(\rho_{A})^{n}]
is equal to the partition function on an
n
-sheeted Riemann surface
where the cuts are introduced on the subsystem
A
as sketched in Fig.

.
This partition function can be computed as a correlation function of twist operators, denoted by
\sigma_{n}
and
\bar{\sigma}_{n}
, inserted on the boundaries

. When the subsystem

is a union of

[\xi_{1},\eta_{1}]\cup[\xi_{2},\eta_{2}]\cdot\cdot\cdot\cup[\xi_{s},\eta_{s}]
, then we find that Tr
[(\rho_{A})^{n}]

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{A})^{n}]=\langle\sigma_{n}(\xi_{1})\bar{\sigma}_{n}(\eta_{1})\cdot\cdot\cdot\sigma_{n}(\xi_{s})\bar{\sigma}_{n}(\eta_{s})\rangle.

(17)

Here the correlation function in the right-hand side is normalized such that
\langle 1\rangle=1
and this corresponds to the normalization of density matrix Tr

The twist operator

creates a cut at

is

. The other twist operator
\bar{\sigma}_{n}(\xi)
has the opposite winding such that it absorbs the cut created by

. In this section below, we would like to study the properties of the twist operators in the Dirac fermion CFT and its cyclic orbifolds.

3.1
Twist operators in Dirac fermion CFT

For the free massless Dirac fermion CFT with

;
Azeyanagi:2007bj

a complex plane whose coordinate is expressed as

(\psi_{L}(z),\bar{\psi}_{L}(z))
for the left-moving mode
and
(\psi_{R}(\bar{z}),\bar{\psi}_{R}(\bar{z}))
for the right-moving mode. They satisfy the OPE relation

\displaystyle\psi_{L}(z)\bar{\psi}_{L}(0)\sim\frac{1}{z},\ \ \ \psi_{R}(z)\bar{\psi}_{R}(0)\sim\frac{1}{\bar{z}}.

(18)

In the replica calculation, we write

\psi_{L,[a]}\ \ (a=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,n)
(and similarly in the right-moving sector). If the fermion goes around a point (simply we choose

) where the twist operator is inserted, the fermion will go back to the original sheet after

. This can be found by conformally mapping from the

\psi_{L}(z)=z^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\psi_{L}(w)

\displaystyle\psi_{L,[a]}\to e^{\pi i\left(\frac{1-n}{n}\right)}\psi_{L,[a+1]}.

(19)

Now we perform the discrete Fourier transformation

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{L,q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{a=1}^{n}e^{-2\pi i\frac{qa}{n}}\psi_{L,[a]}.

(20)

By introducing

by shifting

as
p=q+\frac{1-n}{2}

rotation

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{L,p}\to e^{2\pi i\frac{p}{n}}\tilde{\psi}_{L,p}.

(21)

We can choose

to be

. Note that

takes integer values (or half-integer values) when

is odd (or even), respectively. Finally, the twist operator inserted at a point

can be explicitly written in the form
1
1

1

Equivalently we can choose
\prod_{p=-\frac{n-1}{2}}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{i\frac{p}{n}\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{L,p}(z_{*})+\tilde{\varphi}_{R,p}(\bar{z}_{*})\right)}
by replacing

Azeyanagi:2007bj

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(z_{*},\bar{z}_{*})=\prod_{p=-\frac{n-1}{2}}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{i\frac{p}{n}\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{L,p}(z_{*})-\tilde{\varphi}_{R,p}(\bar{z}_{*})\right)},

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leftrightarrow\mbox{Tr}_{NS}(q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}})=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}.

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{2}(0)\bar{\sigma}_{2}(x)\bar{\sigma}_{2}(1)\sigma_{2}(\infty)\rangle=\frac{\theta_{4}}{\theta_{2}}=x^{-\frac{1}{4}}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leftrightarrow\mbox{Tr}_{NS}((-1)^{F}q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}})=\left|\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}.

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{2}(0)\sigma_{2}(x)\bar{\sigma}_{2}(1)\bar{\sigma}_{2}(\infty)\rangle=\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{4}}=x^{\frac{1}{4}}(1-x)^{-\frac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leftrightarrow\mbox{Tr}_{R}(q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}})=\left|\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}.

(29)

Note that the sector Tr
{}_{R}(-1)^{F}
simply vanishes and thus is absent in the above. Our conventions of theta functions and eta function, used in the above, are summarized in Appendix
A
.

The above identification (

and
z=x
and the one which does
z=x
and

) corresponds to the NS sector. In this paper, we will focus on the NS sector contribution below.

3.3
Twist operators in
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
cyclic orbifold CFT

Now we would like to turn to the cyclic orbifold CFT constructed from

Dirac fermions, denoted by

We can diagonalize again by taking the discrete Fourier transformation

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(k)}_{L,R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-2\pi i\frac{kj}{N}}\psi^{j}_{L,R},\ \ \ \ (k=0,1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N-1),

so that the

(\rho_{A})^{n}
in the cyclic orbifold CFT, we consider the replicated fermions, denoted by

\psi^{j}_{L,[a]}(z),\ \ \ \bar{\psi}^{j}_{L,[a]}(z),\ \ \ \psi^{j}_{R,[a]}(\bar{z}),\ \ \ \bar{\psi}^{j}_{R,[a]}(\bar{z}),

(32)

where

where

The partition functions in NS sector look like (from (

\displaystyle Z^{NS}_{N,\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\mbox{Tr}^{(l)}_{NS}\left[\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\frac{g^{l^{\prime}}}{N}q^{H_{L}}\bar{q}^{H_{R}}\right]

We can easily confirm the invariance under the

\tau\to-1/\tau

In order to calculate the entanglement entropy, we again need to examine the twist operator
\sigma_{n}
in the replica of the orbifold CFT. By bosonizing the
Nn
fermions

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(k)}_{L,p}(z)=e^{i{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(k)}_{L,p}(z)},\ \ \ \ \tilde{\psi}^{(k)}_{R,p}(\bar{z})=e^{i{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(k)}_{R,p}(\bar{z})},

3.4
Twist operator in

As the simplest example among non-trivial cyclic orbifolds, we consider the

In this case, the NS sector partition function takes the form:

If we compare this with

\displaystyle Z\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)=\left|\frac{\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}{\eta\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\right|^{2}=2^{4/3}x^{1/6}(1-x)^{-1/3},

\displaystyle Z\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)=0.

(40)

In terms of the twist operator four-point functions, we find

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}[\rho_{A}^{2}]=2^{-\frac{4}{3}}x^{-\frac{1}{6}}(1-x)^{-\frac{1}{6}}Z^{NS}_{2,\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\langle\sigma_{2}(0)\bar{\sigma}_{2}(x)\tilde{\sigma}_{2}(1)\bar{\tilde{\sigma}}_{2}(\infty)\rangle,

(41)

where we defined the twist operators as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}=e^{\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(0)}_{L,\frac{1}{2}}(z_{*})-\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(0)}_{R,\frac{1}{2}}(\bar{z}_{*})}\!\cdot\!e^{-\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(0)}_{L,-\frac{1}{2}}(z_{*})+\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(0)}_{R,-\frac{1}{2}}(\bar{z}_{*})}\cdot e^{\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(1)}_{L,\frac{1}{2}}(z_{*})-\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(1)}_{R,\frac{1}{2}}(\bar{z}_{*})}\!\cdot\!e^{-\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(1)}_{L,-\frac{1}{2}}(z_{*})+\frac{i}{4}{\tilde{\varphi}}^{(1)}_{R,-\frac{1}{2}}(\bar{z}_{*})},

(42)

Also,
\bar{\sigma}_{2}

\sigma_{2}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}

4.1
Entanglement entropy in cyclic orbifolds

Consider entanglement entropy in the cyclic orbifold CFTs.

4.1.3

Now let us calculate the entanglement entropy for a fixed diagonal sector. The

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-n}\left[\log\langle\sigma_{n}(L)\bar{\sigma}_{n}(0)\rangle_{(l,l^{\prime})}\right]

\displaystyle S^{(n)1}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}+S^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}.

Here, we defined the first term

\displaystyle S^{(n)1}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}=\frac{N}{6}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log\left|\frac{\theta_{1}(L|\tau)}{2\pi\delta\eta(\tau)^{3}}\right|,

The second contribution is from the other terms:

\displaystyle~S^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\log\left[\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau+\frac{p}{n}L|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right]-\frac{n}{1-n}\log\left[Z^{NS}_{(l,l^{\prime})}\right].

We are interested in the difference between the Renyi entropy for the total system

\sigma\to 0
:

\displaystyle~\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\left[S^{(n)}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}(1-\sigma)-S^{(n)}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}(\sigma)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\left[S^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}(1-\sigma)-S^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}(\sigma)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\log\left[\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau+\frac{p}{n}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\log\left[\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right].

(60)

We would like to argue

\displaystyle~\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\left[S^{(n)}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}(1-\sigma)-S^{(n)}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}(\sigma)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{1-n}\log\frac{Z_{(l,nl^{\prime})}^{NS}(n\tau)}{Z_{(l,l^{\prime})}^{NS}(\tau)^{n}},

(61)

where
Z_{(l,l^{\prime})}^{NS}(\tau)
is

\displaystyle Z_{(l,l^{\prime})}^{NS}(\tau)=

\displaystyle~\mbox{Tr}_{(l)}\left[g^{l^{\prime}}q^{H_{L}}\bar{q}^{H_{R}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau|\tau\right)\right|^{2},

(62)

\displaystyle Z_{(l,nl^{\prime})}^{NS}(n\tau)=

\displaystyle~\mbox{Tr}_{(l)}\left[g^{nl^{\prime}}q^{nH_{L}}\bar{q}^{nH_{R}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}n\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(n\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}nl^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}ln\tau|n\tau\right)\right|^{2}.

(63)

We can show (
61
) by employing the formula (
178
) to perform the sum over
p
.
Note that when considering
Z_{(l,nl^{\prime})}^{NS}(n\tau)
, not only
\tau
but also the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
action
g^{l^{\prime}}
get multiplied by
n
.

We can extend this procedure to the full diagonal Renyi entropy
S^{(n)}_{A,diag}
.
The first contribution is universal and thus is the same as the one in the fixed diagonal sector:

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{A,diag}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{1-n}\left[\log\langle\sigma_{n}(L)\bar{\sigma}_{n}(0)\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle~S^{(n)1}_{A,diag}+S^{(n)2}_{A,diag},

(64)

\displaystyle S^{(n)1}_{A,diag}=

\displaystyle~S^{(n)1}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}=\frac{N}{6}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log\left|\frac{\theta_{1}(L|\tau)}{2\pi\delta\eta(\tau)^{3}}\right|.

(65)

The second contribution is from the other terms:

\displaystyle S^{(n)2}_{A,diag}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau+\frac{p}{n}L|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right].

(66)

Although this equality is somewhat complicated, the following quantity is relatively understandable:

\displaystyle~\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\left[S^{(n)}_{A,diag}(1-\sigma)-S^{(n)}_{A,diag}(\sigma)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau+\frac{p}{n}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{-2\pi\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}\tau_{2}}}{|\eta(\tau)^{2}|}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}Z_{(l,nl^{\prime})}^{NS}(n\tau)}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\left(Z_{(l,l^{\prime})}^{NS}(\tau)\right)^{n}}\right].

(67)

We employ the formula (
178
) again to perform the product over
p
. As we can see, only the first terms of
S^{(n)2}_{A,diag}(1-\sigma)
and
S^{(n)2}_{A,diag}(\sigma)
contribute.

We should notice again that the above calculation is for the diagonal sectors. It is necessary to consider off-diagonal sectors—applying different twist-boundary conditions (labeled by
(l,l^{\prime})
) for different replica sheets. Summing over all possible boundary condition will yield the complete Renyi entropy. However, naive construction of replica-twist operators cannot formulate the different boundary conditions for arbitrary

and

.
If we could calculate the complete Renyi entropy
S^{(n)}_{A}
including contributions from both diagonal and off-diagonal sectors, then we expect instead of (

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\left[S^{(n)}_{A}(1-\sigma)-S^{(n)}_{A}(\sigma)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\frac{Z(n\tau)}{Z(\tau)^{n}},

(68)

where

is the full partition function of any given two-dimensional CFT, including both the cyclic and symmetric orbifolds. Later we will explicitly confirm this relation (

) for
N=n=2
.

For reference, we perform here the high-temperature expansion of
S^{(n)}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}
.
By performing the modular S transformation to
S^{(n)1}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}
, we obtain (we set
\tau=i\beta)

\displaystyle S^{(n)1}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}=\frac{N}{6}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log\left[\frac{\beta}{\pi\delta}e^{-\frac{\pi L^{2}}{\beta}}\sinh\left(\frac{\pi L}{\beta}\right)\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e^{\frac{2\pi L}{\beta}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}m})(1-e^{-\frac{2\pi L}{\beta}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}m})}{(1-e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}m})^{2}}\right].

To evaluate
S^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}
, we employ the following formula:

\displaystyle~\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l\tau+\frac{p}{n}L\biggl|\tau\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\beta\cdot e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\left(\frac{kl^{\prime}}{N}+\frac{pL}{n}\right)^{2}}\cdot e^{2\pi\beta\frac{k^{2}l^{2}}{N^{2}}}\left|\theta_{3}\left(-\frac{k}{N\tau}l^{\prime}+\frac{k}{N}l+\frac{p}{n\tau}L\biggl|-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)\right|^{2}.

(70)

This leads to

\displaystyle S^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\left(-\frac{2\pi N}{\beta}\right)\frac{p^{2}L^{2}}{n^{2}}+\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi N}{6\beta}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)L^{2}+\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})},

where the contribution
\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}
is evaluated as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\log\left[\frac{e^{-\frac{2\pi k^{2}l^{\prime 2}}{N^{2}\beta}}e^{-\frac{4\pi kpl^{\prime}L}{Nn\beta}}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{2}}\left|\theta_{3}\left(\frac{k}{N}l+i\frac{k}{N\beta}l^{\prime}-i\frac{pL}{n\beta}\biggl|\frac{i}{\beta}\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\frac{n}{1-n}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\log\left[\frac{e^{-\frac{2\pi k^{2}l^{\prime 2}}{N^{2}\beta}}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{2}}\left|\theta_{3}\left(\frac{k}{N}l+i\frac{k}{N\beta}l^{\prime}\biggl|\frac{i}{\beta}\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\log\left[\left|\theta_{3}\left(\frac{k}{N}l+i\frac{k}{N\beta}l^{\prime}-i\frac{pL}{n\beta}\biggl|\frac{i}{\beta}\right)\right|^{2}\cdot\left|\theta_{3}\left(\frac{k}{N}l+i\frac{k}{N\beta}l^{\prime}\biggl|\frac{i}{\beta}\right)\right|^{-2}\right].

4.1.4
Von-Neumann entanglement entropy in diagonal sectors

Next, we compute the entanglement entropy by taking the von-Neumann limit
n=1
in the replica method. We again focus on the diagonal sectors.
The part of the Renyi entropy

\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}
can be explicitly written as

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}=\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\log|F_{k,p}|^{2},

\displaystyle F_{k,p}\equiv\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+e^{\frac{2\pi ikl}{N}}e^{-2\pi\frac{kl^{\prime}}{N\beta}}e^{2\pi\frac{pL}{n\beta}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}(m-1/2)})(1+e^{\frac{-2\pi ikl}{N}}e^{2\pi\frac{kl^{\prime}}{N\beta}}e^{-2\pi\frac{pL}{n\beta}}e^{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}(m-1/2)})}{(1+e^{\frac{2\pi ikl}{N}}e^{-2\pi\frac{kl^{\prime}}{N\beta}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}(m-1/2)})(1+e^{-\frac{2\pi ikl}{N}}e^{2\pi\frac{kl^{\prime}}{N\beta}}e^{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}(m-1/2)})}.

Then we can expand the logarithm as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}}{s}e^{-2\pi\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{s}{\beta}}\left[G_{s,m,p,k}-G_{s,m,0,k}\right],

\displaystyle G_{s,m,p,k}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle e^{2\pi\left(i\frac{lks}{N}-\frac{l^{\prime}ks}{N\beta}+\frac{Lps}{n\beta}\right)}+e^{2\pi\left(-i\frac{lks}{N}-\frac{l^{\prime}ks}{N\beta}+\frac{Lps}{n\beta}\right)}+e^{2\pi\left(i\frac{lks}{N}+\frac{l^{\prime}ks}{N\beta}-\frac{Lps}{n\beta}\right)}

(74)

\displaystyle+e^{2\pi\left(-i\frac{lks}{N}+\frac{l^{\prime}ks}{N\beta}-\frac{Lps}{n\beta}\right)}.

By performing the summation over
m
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{(n)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-n}\sum_{p=-(n-1)/2}^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}}{s\cdot\sinh\left(\frac{\pi s}{\beta}\right)}\cdot\left[\frac{\sinh\left(\frac{\pi Ls}{\beta}\right)}{\sinh\left(\frac{\pi Ls}{n\beta}\right)}-n\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \times\ \left[\frac{\sinh\left[N\left(\pi i\frac{ls}{N}-\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right)\right]}{\sinh\left[\pi i\frac{ls}{N}-\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right]}+\frac{\sinh\left[N\left(\pi i\frac{ls}{N}+\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right)\right]}{\sinh\left[\pi i\frac{ls}{N}+\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right]}\right].

By taking the von-Neumann limit
n\to 1
, we find

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{(1)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{s\cdot\sinh\left(\frac{\pi s}{\beta}\right)}\cdot\left[\frac{\pi Ls}{\beta}\coth\left(\frac{\pi sL}{\beta}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\left[\frac{\sinh\left[N\left(\pi i\frac{ls}{N}-\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right)\right]}{\sinh\left[\pi i\frac{ls}{N}-\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right]}+\frac{\sinh\left[N\left(\pi i\frac{ls}{N}+\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right)\right]}{\sinh\left[\pi i\frac{ls}{N}+\frac{\pi l^{\prime}s}{N\beta}\right]}\right].

The final expression of the entanglement entropy for the
(l,l^{\prime})
sector is given by

\displaystyle S^{(1)}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{3}\!\log\!\left[\frac{\beta}{\pi\delta}\sinh\left(\frac{\pi L}{\beta}\right)\right]\!+\!\frac{N}{3}\!\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\log\!\left[\frac{\left(1-e^{\frac{2\pi L}{\beta}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}m}\right)\left(1-e^{-\frac{2\pi L}{\beta}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}m}\right)}{\left(1-e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}m}\right)^{2}}\right]\!+\!\tilde{S}^{(1)2}_{A(l,l^{\prime})}.

4.2
Time-like correlation function in cyclic orbifolds

Before we proceed to study entanglement entropy further, we would like to stop here to explore the periodicity under the time evolution in cyclic orbifolds. First note that in the
k
-th fermion,
l
-th twisted sector is defined by
the twisted boundary condition

\displaystyle\psi^{(k)}_{L,R}(x+1)=e^{\frac{2\pi ikl}{N}}\psi(x)^{(k)}_{L,R}

(76)

for both the left
(L)
and right
(R)
moving sectors. Then the quantized fermionic modes look like

\displaystyle\left(\psi^{(k)}_{L}\right)_{-n+\frac{kl}{N}},\ \ \ \left(\psi^{(k)}_{R}\right)_{-n-\frac{kl}{N}},\ \ \ \ \ (n\in\mathbb{Z}).

(77)

Now we set

\displaystyle O(z,\bar{z})=\psi^{(k)}_{L}(z)\bar{\psi}^{(k)}_{R}(\bar{z})=e^{i\varphi^{(k)}_{L}(z)-i\varphi^{(k)}_{R}(\bar{z})},

(78)

which has a non-trivial phase under the time translation as

\displaystyle e^{i2\pi Ht}\left[\left(\psi^{(k)}_{L}\right)_{-n+\frac{kl}{N}}\left(\bar{\psi}^{(k)}_{R}\right)_{-m+\frac{kl}{N}}\right]e^{-2\pi iHt}=e^{2\pi i(n+m-\frac{2kl}{N})t}\left[\left(\psi^{(k)}_{L}\right)_{-n+\frac{kl}{N}}\left(\bar{\psi}^{(k)}_{R}\right)_{-m+\frac{kl}{N}}\right].

(79)

We would like to evaluate the two-point function
\langle O(z,\bar{z})O(0,0)\rangle
, by choosing the time-like separation:
(z,\bar{z})=(T,-T)
. Or if we set
z_{12}=x_{12}+iy_{12}
we set
y_{12}=iT
. By plugging this and
(e,m)=(0,1)
into (
48
), we obtain

\displaystyle~Z_{(l,l^{\prime})}\cdot\langle O(T,-T)O(0,0)\rangle_{(l,l^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~Z_{(l,l^{\prime})}~e^{-4\pi i\frac{klT}{N}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta^{3}}{\theta_{1}(T|\tau)}\right|^{2}\frac{\theta_{3}(\frac{kl\tau-kl^{\prime}}{N}-T|\tau)\overline{\theta_{3}(\frac{kl\tau-kl^{\prime}}{N}+T|\tau)}}{\left|{\theta_{3}(\frac{kl\tau-kl^{\prime}}{N}|\tau)}\right|^{2}}.

(80)

The phase factor
e^{-4\pi iT\frac{kl}{N}}
is consistent with (
79
). Summation over
(l,l^{\prime})
gives the two-point function
Z\cdot\langle O(T,-T)O(0,0)\rangle
. This shows that the periodicity in the time direction is
T\sim T+N
for odd
N
and
T\sim T+N/2
for even
N
.

Second Renyi entropy at

Thus we find the difference

where

4.3.1

We already computed the diagonal case i.e.

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(0,0)}_{(0,0)}(0,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{3}(\tau)|^{4}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}},\ \ \ \ \tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(0,1)}(0,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{4}(\tau)|^{4}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}},

In the limit

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(0,0)}_{(0,0)}(1,\tau)=\tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(0,1)}(1,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{3}(2\tau)|^{2}}{|\eta(2\tau)|^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,0)}_{(1,0)}(1,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{2}(2\tau)|^{2}}{|\eta(2\tau)|^{2}},\ \ \ \ \tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(1,1)}(1,\tau)=0.

4.3.2
Off-diagonal parts

The off-diagonal partition functions are new. First, note the symmetry

\displaystyle Z^{(a,b)}_{(c,d)}(L,\tau)=Z^{(c,d)}_{(a,b)}(L,\tau).

(94)

Let us start with

(l_{1},l^{\prime}_{1})=(0,0)
and
(l_{2},l^{\prime}_{2})=(1,0)
. In this case, the
fermion follows the boundary condition in the real
z
-direction:

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(0)}_{\pm}(z+1)=\tilde{\psi}^{(0)}_{\pm}(z),

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z+1)=\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\mp}(z).

(95)

Therefore, in the
k=1
sector, the periodicity in the Re
z
direction gets doubled as
\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z+2)=\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z)
, which makes the moduli of the torus halved i.e.
\frac{\tau}{2}
.
Therefore
\tilde{Z}^{(0,0)}_{(1,0)}(L,\tau)
is computed by the four-point function rather than a square of two-point functions:

\displaystyle Z^{(1,0)}_{(0,0)}(L,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e^{\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{+}(L)}e^{-\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{-}(L)}e^{\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{+}(0)}e^{-\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{-}(0)}\rangle

(96)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e^{\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{+}(L)}e^{-\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{+}(L+1)}e^{\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{+}(0)}e^{-\frac{i}{4}\tilde{\varphi}^{(-)}_{+}(1)}\rangle.

This is evaluated as follows

\displaystyle Z^{(1,0)}_{(0,0)}(L,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\eta\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)|^{2}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{L}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{L+1}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{2}.

(97)

Here the final factor
\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{2}
comes from the winding mode summation.

This leads to

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,0)}_{(0,0)}(L,\tau)=\frac{1}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{L}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(98)

By adding the diagonal projection
l^{\prime}_{1}=l^{\prime}_{2}=1
to the above result, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(0,1)}(L,\tau)=\frac{1}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{L}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

(99)

Next consider the asymmetric projection case

(l_{1},l^{\prime}_{1})=(0,0)
and
(l_{2},l^{\prime}_{2})=(0,1)
.
In this case, the imaginary direction Im
z
gets doubled due to the twisted boundary condition

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(0)}_{\pm}(z+\tau)=\tilde{\psi}^{(0)}_{\pm}(z),

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z+\tau)=\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\mp}(z).

(100)

Therefore the moduli of the torus is given by
2\tau
.

\displaystyle Z^{(0,1)}_{(0,0)}(L,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\eta\left(2\tau\right)|^{2}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(2\tau\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(L|2\tau\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(2\tau\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(L+\tau|2\tau\right)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(2\tau\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\tau|2\tau\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\theta_{4}\left(2\tau\right)\right|^{2}.

(101)

This leads to

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(0,0)}(L,\tau)=\frac{1}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}}\left|\theta_{4}\left(2\tau\right)\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}\left|\theta_{4}\left(\frac{L}{2}|2\tau\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(1,0)}(L,\tau)=\frac{1}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(2\tau\right)\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{L}{2}|2\tau\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},

(102)

where in the latter we added a diagonal twist in Re
z
direction.

Finally we consider the case
(l_{1},l^{\prime}_{1})=(1,0)
and
(l_{2},l^{\prime}_{2})=(0,1)
.
In this case, the both Re
z
and Im
z
for
k=1
are twisted as

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z+1)=\tilde{\psi}^{(0)}_{\mp}(z),

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z+\tau)=\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\mp}(z).

(103)

Since
\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z+\tau+1)=\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(z)
, we can regard the torus as defined by the identification
z\sim z+2
and

z\sim z+\tau+1
. Therefore the moduli is given by
\frac{\tau+1}{2}
.
This is evaluated as follows

\displaystyle Z^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(L,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)|^{2}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{L}{2}|\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{L+1}{2}|\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left|\frac{2\pi\eta\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)\right|^{2}.

(104)

This leads to

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(L,\tau)=\frac{1}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{L}{2}|\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(L,\tau)=\frac{1}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}\left|\theta_{2}\left(\frac{L}{2}|\frac{\tau+1}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(105)

If we take the limit
L\to 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,0)}_{(0,0)}(0,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{2}(\tau)\theta_{3}(\tau)|^{2}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}},\ \ \ \tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(0,0)}(0,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{3}(\tau)\theta_{4}(\tau)|^{2}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}},

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(0,1)}(0,\tau)=\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(1,0)}(0,\tau)=\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(0,\tau)=0,\ \ \ \ \tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(0,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{2}(\tau)\theta_{4}(\tau)|^{2}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{4}}.

(106)

In the limit
L\to 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,0)}_{(0,0)}(1,\tau)=\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(0,1)}(1,\tau)=0,\ \ \ \tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(0,0)}(1,\tau)=\frac{|\theta_{4}(2\tau)|^{2}}{|\eta(2\tau)|^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(1,0)}(1,\tau)=\tilde{Z}^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(1,\tau)=\tilde{Z}^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(1,\tau)=0.

(107)

These results (

), (

), (

), and (

) confirm the identity (
89
) we wanted to show.

5
Entanglement entropy in cyclic orbifold CFTs under quantum quenches

An excited state produced by a global quantum quench can be modeled by a regularized boundary state
Calabrese:2005in
:

|\Psi(t)\rangle={\cal N}e^{-iHt}e^{-\epsilon H}|B\rangle,

(108)

where
|B\rangle
is a boundary state (or Cardy state) in a given CFT. In this section, we would like to study the evolution of entanglement entropy for such excited states in the cyclic orbifold CFT on a circle. At
N=1
i.e. the
c=1
Dirac fermion CFT, this was studied in
Takayanagi:2010wp
and we will closely follow the convention in that paper below. We write the complex coordinate of the cylinder by
(y,\bar{y})=(\tau+i\sigma,\tau-i\sigma)
, where the spacial coordinate
\sigma
is compactified as
\sigma\sim\sigma+2\pi
.
We choose subsystem
A
to be an interval with length
\sigma
at the real-time
t
as depicted in Fig.
5
. The endpoints of
A
, denoted by
(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})
and
(y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})
, are given by

(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})=(\epsilon+it,\epsilon+it),\ \ \ (y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})=(\epsilon+it+i\sigma,\epsilon+it-i\sigma).

(109)

Figure 5:
A sketch of the calculation of entanglement entropy on a cylinder.

The entanglement entropy can be computed from the partition function of
n
sheeted cylinder

\displaystyle Z^{cyl}_{n}=\langle B_{n}|\sigma_{n}(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\bar{\sigma}_{n}(y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})|B_{n}\rangle,

(110)

where
|B_{n}\rangle
is the boundary state of the
n
folded CFT.

5.1
Boundary state of a single fermion CFT

Consider the
N=1
case, i.e. the
c=1
free Dirac fermion. We can bosonize the fermion into a scalar and this leads to the following expression of boundary state

\displaystyle|B\rangle_{N=1}={\cal N}_{1}\cdot e^{-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\alpha_{L,-n}\alpha_{R,-n}}\sum_{w\in Z}|w\rangle,

(111)

where
\alpha_{L,n}
and
\alpha_{R,n}
are the left and right-moving parts of the oscillators in the scalar field
\tilde{\varphi}=\tilde{\varphi}_{L}(y)+\tilde{\varphi}_{R}(\bar{y})
.
They satisfy the familiar commutation relation

\displaystyle[\alpha_{L,n},\alpha_{L,m}]=[\alpha_{R,n},\alpha_{R,m}]=n\delta_{n+m,0}.

(112)

The two-point functions of typical primary fields can be computed as follows
(note that we set
\tau=\frac{2i\epsilon}{\pi}
and that we have
k_{R}=-k_{L}
for winding modes)
Takayanagi:2010wp
:

\displaystyle\langle B|e^{-2\epsilon H}e^{ik_{L}(\tilde{\varphi}_{L}(y_{1})-\tilde{\varphi}_{R}(\bar{y}_{1}))}e^{-ik_{L}(\tilde{\varphi}_{L}(y_{2})-\tilde{\varphi}_{R}(\bar{y}_{2}))}|B\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{w}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{2}\epsilon w^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\eta(\tau)}\cdot\left[\frac{\eta(\tau)^{6}\theta_{1}\left(\frac{y_{1}+\bar{y}_{2}}{2\pi i}\right)\theta_{1}\left(\frac{y_{2}+\bar{y}_{1}}{2\pi i}\right)}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{2\pi i}\right)\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\bar{y}_{2}-\bar{y}_{1}}{2\pi i}\right)\theta_{1}\left(\frac{y_{1}+\bar{y}_{1}}{2\pi i}\right)\theta_{1}\left(\frac{y_{2}+\bar{y}_{2}}{2\pi i}\right)}\right]^{k_{L}^{2}}.

(113)

We choose the free fermion radius i.e.
R=2
as before. Then the summation over
w
leads to the NS sector partition function

\displaystyle\sum_{w}e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{2}\epsilon w^{2}}=\theta_{3}(\tau).

(114)

To calculate Tr
(\rho_{A})^{n}
, we choose
k_{L}=\frac{p}{n}
and take the product as follows

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{A})^{n}]=\prod_{p=-\frac{n-1}{2}}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\langle B_{n}|e^{-2\epsilon H}e^{i\frac{p}{n}(\tilde{\varphi}_{L}(y_{1})-\tilde{\varphi}_{R}(\bar{y}_{1}))}e^{-i\frac{p}{n}(\tilde{\varphi}_{L}(y_{2})-\tilde{\varphi}_{R}(\bar{y}_{2}))}|B_{n}\rangle.

(115)

Since we have
\sum_{p=-\frac{n-1}{2}}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{p^{2}}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{12}\left(n-\frac{1}{n}\right)
, we find the
n
-th Renyi entropy is given by
Takayanagi:2011zk
:

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{A}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\log\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{A})^{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log\frac{\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}}{\left|\eta\left(\frac{2i\epsilon}{\pi}\right)\right|^{6}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}+\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}-\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|\delta^{2}},

(116)

where we inserted the infinitesimally small parameter
\delta
as the UV cut-off, which is a standard prescription in the CFT calculation of entanglement entropy (e.g. see
Calabrese:2004eu
). This time evolution of entanglement entropy has the periodicity by
\pi
as

S^{(n)}_{A}(t+\pi,\sigma)=S^{(n)}_{A}(t,\sigma).

(117)

Refer to the numerical plot shown in Fig.
6
. We find the linear growth
until
t=\frac{\sigma}{2}
and then the plateau later until
t=\pi-\frac{\sigma}{2}
as the usual evolution of entanglement entropy in quantum quenches
Calabrese:2005in
, which can be explained by the propagation of entangled pairs created by the quench at
t=0
.

Figure 6:
The plot of entanglement entropy as a function of time
t
for the
c=1
Dirac fermion CFT under quantum quenches.
We set
n=2
(2nd Renyi entropy),
\epsilon=0.2
and
\delta=1
. The blue (bottom), orange, green, and red (top) curves describe the entanglement entropy for
\sigma=\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{4}
and

\pi
, respectively.

5.2
Entanglement entropy in cyclic orbifold CFTs

Next, we calculate entanglement entropy in the cyclic orbifolds using the boundary state formalism.
The boundary state in the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
cyclic orbifold can be written in the form

\displaystyle|B(\alpha)\rangle_{N}={\cal N^{\prime}}\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i\alpha l}{N}}|B^{(k)}(l)\rangle,

(118)

where
\alpha=0,1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N-1
labels the type of the boundary state, so-called the fractional branes
Diaconescu:1997br
;
Billo:1999nf
;
Diaconescu:1999dt
;
Billo:2000yb
;
Takayanagi:2001aj
. Also, the state
|B^{(k)}(l)\rangle
is the boundary state for the fermion
\tilde{\psi}^{(k)}_{L,R}
and
\tilde{\tilde{\psi}}^{(k)}_{L,R}
in the
l
-th twisted sector
in terms of their bosonized scalar fields.

To find the entanglement entropy we need to compute the replicated partition function which is written in terms of a two-point function of twist operators on a cylinder (
110
). For example, when we consider the second Renyi entropy
n=2
, this is given by a summation over the twisted sectors of the boundary states as depicted in Fig.
7
. For general
N
, it looks quite difficult to calculate each contribution and perform the summation. However, it is straightforward to calculate the entanglement entropy for the diagonal sectors
l_{1}=m_{1}=l_{2}=m_{2}=\cdot\cdot\cdot=l_{n}=m_{n}(\equiv l)
as we did in the finite temperature case. In this case, the zero mode of the winding mode is shifted by
w\to w+\frac{lk}{N}
.
Since the winding mode summation just gives an overall factor that does not depend on
t
and
\sigma
, the final entanglement entropy is simply
N
times that of the single fermion result (
116
) i.e.

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{A}|_{diagonal}=\frac{N}{12}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log\frac{\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}}{\left|\eta\left(\frac{2i\epsilon}{\pi}\right)\right|^{6}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}+\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}-\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|\delta^{2}}.

(119)

Note that this is again periodic under the time translation with the same periodicity, given by (
117
). Its profile is identical to Fig.
6
.

However, if we add all sectors including off-diagonal ones, we expect that the periodicity gets
N
times longer such that
S_{A}(t+N\pi)=S_{A}(t)
. This is because the twisted sector, which describes long strings, is equivalent to an
N
folded string i.e. the spatial coordinate has a larger periodicity:
\sigma\sim\sigma+2\pi N
. In the next subsection, we will confirm this in the
N=2
case by an explicit calculation.

The replica method calculation of second Renyi entropy on a cylinder for the
Z_{N}
orbifold CFT.

5.3
Full analysis in
N=n=2
case

We can explicitly evaluate the replicated partition function (
110
) for the second Renyi entropy of
N=2
cyclic orbifold. In this
N=n=2
case, the two-point function of twist operators
is written as follows

\displaystyle\langle B(\alpha)|e^{-2\epsilon H}\sigma_{2}(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\bar{\sigma}_{2}(y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})|B(\alpha)\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2},m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{1}C^{(l_{2},m_{2})}_{(l_{1},m_{1})}(\sigma,t).

(120)

First, we note the symmetry which exchanges the two replicas:

\displaystyle C^{(l_{2},m_{2})}_{(l_{1},m_{1})}(\sigma,t)=C^{(l_{1},m_{1})}_{(l_{2},m_{2})}(\sigma,t).

(121)

The
k=0
fermion
\tilde{\psi}^{(0)}_{p}
follows the untwisted boundary condition. Thus for any values of
(l_{1},m_{1})
and
(l_{2},m_{2})
of the summation depicted in Fig.
7
, the
k=0
partition function is simply given by
\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}
. The
k=1
fermion
\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{p}
follows the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
twisted boundary condition in the
l=1
sector, while it is untwisted in the
l=0
sector.

Let us start with the diagonal cases. When
(l_{1},m_{1})=(l_{2},m_{2})=(0,0)
, the partition function can be computed as the square of (
113
):

\displaystyle C^{(0,0)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{4}\cdot\left[\frac{\left|\eta\left(\frac{2i\epsilon}{\pi}\right)\right|^{6}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}+\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}-\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|}{\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(122)

When
(l_{1},m_{1})=(l_{2},m_{2})=(1,1)
, the
k=1
sector is twisted leading to the
k=1
partition function
\left|\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}
. By combining with the
k=0
part we obtain

\displaystyle C^{(1,1)}_{(1,1)}(\sigma,t)=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}\cdot\left|\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}\cdot\left[\frac{\left|\eta\left(\frac{2i\epsilon}{\pi}\right)\right|^{6}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}+\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}-\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|}{\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(123)

In the off-diagonal case
(l_{1},m_{1})=(0,0)
and
(l_{2},m_{2})=(1,1)
, the periodicity of the Im
y
direction gets doubled
y\sim y+4\pi i
due to the twisted boundary condition

\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\pm}(y+2\pi i)=\tilde{\psi}^{(1)}_{\mp}(y)
. Therefore the two-point function should be regarded as a four-point function on a cylinder as we have already seen in (
96
). In the end, we obtain the following expression

\displaystyle C^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)^{3}\theta_{2}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{4}}\right|\cdot\left[\frac{\left|\eta\left(\tau\right)\right|^{6}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}+\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}-\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|}{\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{\pi i}|\tau\right)\right|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{8}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \times\left|\frac{\eta(\frac{\tau}{2})^{6}~\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{2\pi i}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{2\pi i}+\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{2\pi i}-\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}{4\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}\theta_{2}\left(0|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{2\pi i}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{2\pi i}+\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\theta_{2}\left(\frac{\epsilon+it}{2\pi i}-\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}|\frac{\tau}{2}\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{8}}.

(124)

We can also obtain the result for
(l_{1},m_{1})=(1,0)
and
(l_{2},m_{2})=(0,1)

by performing transformation
\sigma\to 2\pi-\sigma

\displaystyle C^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(\sigma,t)=C^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(2\pi-\sigma,t).

(125)

All other components of
C^{(l_{2},m_{2})}_{(l_{1},m_{1})}(\sigma,t)
are vanishing.

The total partition function takes the form

\displaystyle\langle B(\alpha)|e^{-2\epsilon H}\sigma_{2}(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\bar{\sigma}_{2}(y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})|B(\alpha)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left[C^{(0,0)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)+C^{(1,1)}_{(1,1)}(\sigma,t)+2C^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)+2C^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(\sigma,t)\right].

(126)

It is easy to confirm

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}C^{(0,0)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)=\lim_{\sigma\to 2\pi}C^{(0,0)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)^{4}}{\eta(\tau)^{4}}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}C^{(1,1)}_{(1,1)}(\sigma,t)=\lim_{\sigma\to 2\pi}C^{(1,1)}_{(1,1)}(\sigma,t)=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)^{2}\theta_{2}(\tau)^{2}}{\eta(\tau)^{4}}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}C^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)^{3}\theta_{2}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{4}}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 2\pi}C^{(1,1)}_{(0,0)}(\sigma,t)=\lim_{\sigma\to 0}C^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(\sigma,t)=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 2\pi}C^{(0,1)}_{(1,0)}(\sigma,t)=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)^{2}\theta_{2}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{4}}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we employed the identities in appendix
A
.
Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\langle B(\alpha)|e^{-2\epsilon H}\sigma_{2}(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\bar{\sigma}_{2}(y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})|B(\alpha)\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\sigma\to 2\pi}\langle B(\alpha)|e^{-2\epsilon H}\sigma_{2}(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\bar{\sigma}_{2}(y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})|B(\alpha)\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}|\tau\right)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)^{2}}{\eta(\tau)^{2}}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)+\theta_{2}(\tau)}{2\eta(\tau)}\right|^{2}.

(128)

This result is consistent with the fact that the partition function should be factorized into the product of two cylinder partition functions in the limit
\sigma\to 0
and

\sigma\to 2\pi
. Indeed, the final term is a square of cylinder amplitudes of the
N=2
cyclic orbifold CFT in the NS sector:

\displaystyle Z^{N=2}_{cyl}(\tau)=\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau)+\theta_{2}(\tau)}{2\eta(\tau)}\right|.

(129)

Thus the final expression of the second Renyi entropy reads

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{A}(\sigma,t)=-\log\frac{\langle B(\alpha)|e^{-2\epsilon H}\sigma_{2}(y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\bar{\sigma}_{2}(y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})|B(\alpha)\rangle\cdot\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{Z^{N=2}_{cyl}(\tau)^{2}},

(130)

where we again inserted the UV cut-off
\delta
dependence.
It is clear that the periodicity in the time direction is now doubled:

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{A}(\sigma,t+2\pi)=S^{(2)}_{A}(\sigma,t),

(131)

as we argued in the last subsection. We can also confirm the pure state identity

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{A}(\sigma,t)=S^{(2)}_{A}(2\pi-\sigma,t).

(132)

In the small
\sigma
limit, we reproduced the well-known behavior
Calabrese:2004eu
(at the central charge
c=N=2
)

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{A}(\sigma,t)\simeq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\sigma}{\delta}.

(133)

Refer to Fig.
8

6
Connection to symmetric orbifold CFTs

In this section, we examine the extension of results in cyclic orbifolds to symmetric orbifolds.
We start with re-analyzing their partition functions, in terms of Hecke operators.
By introducing deformed Hecke operators (we named square-free Hecke operators), we can relate these partition functions.
In addition to the connection in partition functions, by studying the structure of twisted sectors in detail, we can calculate quantities in symmetric orbifold CFTs through cyclic ones.

Cyclic orbifold CFT revisited: connection to symmetric orbifold

Recall that the

(136)

Conlusions and discussions

In this paper, we developed the methods of calculations in cyclic/symmetric orbifold CFTs with the Dirac fermion as a seed theory.
We started with a bosonization of the Dirac fermion cyclic theory and constructed replica-twist operators. For the twist operators for the second Renyi entropy, we confirmed that their four-point functions correctly reproduce the torus partition function for
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
cyclic orbifold CFT, which can also be regarded as the S
2
symmetric orbifold CFT. In this analysis, we found that we need to modify the replica-twist operator to calculate the contributions from twisted sectors of the orbifold.

To conclude this paper, we clarify some future tasks and open problems.

\bullet
Developing the replica method

As we indicated iteratively, the naive replica method excludes contributions from off-diagonal parts.
To be precise, the off-diagonal contributions can be evaluated in principle by applying different twist-boundary conditions for different replica sheets.
This problem can be rephrased: orbifold-twist (not replica-twist) operators should also be replicated as ordinary excitation e.g.
\psi(z)
.
Hence, the key to developing the methods of calculating the off-diagonal parts might be to consider orbifold-twist operator correlations for arbitrary
n
.

\bullet
Extension to symmetric orbifolds on cylinder

By using the result of the recent research on boundary states in symmetric orbifolds
Belin:2021nck
, we can extend our calculations to arbitrary
N
symmetric orbifolds on a cylinder. This extension will be one of the concrete approaches to AdS/BCFT.

\bullet
Application to general symmetric orbifold CFTs

We hope to extend our observation to the case with other seed CFTs. The most straightforward and beneficial extension will be the orbifold theory with
T^{4}
(rectangular,
R=2
) SCFT as a seed CFT.
This
T^{4}
orbifold theory is much closer to real holographic CFT than the Dirac fermion orbifold theory.
Marginal deformations to eliminate higher-spin modes and matching the spectrum to classical supergravity dual should be revisited.

\bullet
Heavy operators that contribute to the BTZ black hole

In the Dirac fermion cyclic orbifolds, we found examples of correlation functions that have a long periodicity. Although we conjectured that symmetric orbifold CFT has the periodicity
\exp N
, we did not explicitly specify operators/correlators that show this periodicity, analogous to the BTZ black hole entropy. Specifying such excitation will be a novel approach to investigating the structure of black holes.

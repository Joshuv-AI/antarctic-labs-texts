Extremal points of total generalized variation balls in 1D: characterization and applications 0 0 footnotetext: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 46A55, 90C49, 65J20, 52A40. 0 0 footnotetext: Keywords: total generalized variation, regularization, extremal points, non-smooth optimization, sparsity.
José A. Iglesias, Daniel Walter

Abstract
The total generalized variation (TGV) is a popular regularizer in inverse problems and imaging combining discontinuous solutions and higher order smoothing. In particular, empirical observations suggest that its order two version strongly favors piecewise affine functions. In the present manuscript, we formalize this statement for the one-dimensional TGV-functional by characterizing the extremal points of its sublevel sets with respect to a suitable quotient space topology. These results imply that 1D TGV-regularized linear inverse problems with finite dimensional observations admit piecewise affine minimizers. As further applications of this characterization we include precise first-order necessary optimality conditions without requiring convexity of the fidelity term, and a simple solution algorithm for TGV-regularized minimization problems.



The total variation has been a widely considered prior for regularization of inverse problems for several decades. A particularly popular application is denoising of natural images since the seminal work
[
26
]
. However, it is arguably even better suited to more involved situations where nearly piecewise constant solutions are expected, like recovery of material inclusions in various physical inverse problems (see for example
[
17
,
13
]
). The reasons for its success are varied. On the one hand, it typically produces solutions with discontinuities, which can be thought to correspond to edges of objects in the recovered images, or sharp changes of material parameters. On the other, its formulation is purely local and in terms of derivatives, which in particular enables a great amount of rigorous analysis of the resulting solutions.
One of the main drawbacks of the total variation, however, is the effect generally known as staircasing. Inputs which are not piecewise constant but otherwise very smooth tend to produce solutions with many discontinuities of small amplitude, particularly in the presence of significant noise. A popular cure for this problem is the use of regularizers involving higher order derivatives, which for example can be made to not penalize linear functions. Among such approaches, arguably the most successful is the total generalized variation (TGV) first introduced in
[
7
]
.

A clearly desirable feature of TGV is that it manages to introduce higher order regularization and avoid staircasing while still allowing sharp discontinuities in the solutions. Also of paramount importance is that, unlike other models satisfying these first requirements, it admits a relatively straightforward predual formulation. In turn, this allows the use of convex nonsmooth optimization tools for its numerical solution, like the ubiquitous
[
11
]
which was published around the same time. On the analysis side, topics like existence of minimizers and relations to regularization theory are well explored, see
[
9
,
5
,
6
]
. However, more detailed characterizations of the behavior of solutions are significantly more scarce and with few exceptions
[
28
]
they treat the one dimensional case and denoising only
[
8
,
23
,
25
]
.

In this article, we consider the one-dimensional
\operatorname{TGV}
-functional

\displaystyle\operatorname{TGV}(u)=\inf_{w}\alpha\|Du-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M}

on an interval
(0,T)
from a convex geometry point of view. Here,
\alpha,\beta>0
are suitable regularization parameters and
\|\cdot\|_{M}
is the Radon norm on
(0,T)
, i.e., the dual to the uniform norm. Denoting by
\mathcal{L}
the space of affine linear functions, we show that the set of equivalence classes

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\left\{\,u\;|\;\operatorname{TGV}(u)\leq 1\,\right\}+\mathcal{L}

is compact with respect to the quotient space topology and can thus be written as the closure of the convex hull of its extremal points
\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}
, by the Krein-Milman theorem.
The main result of the manuscript gives a precise characterization of
\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}
as the equivalence classes corresponding to two distinct sets of functions. More in detail, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}=\big(\left\{\,u\;|\;Du=\pm\delta_{x}/\alpha,\penalty\ x\in(0,T)\,\right\}\cup\left\{\,u\;|\;D^{2}u=\pm\delta_{x}/\beta,\penalty\ \operatorname{dist}(x,\partial(0,T))>\beta/\alpha\,\right\}\big)+\mathcal{L}.

Most surprisingly,
\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}
contains the equivalence classes of
all
piecewise constant functions with a single jump, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\{\,u\;|\;Du=\pm\delta_{x}/\alpha,\penalty\ x\in(0,T)\,\right\}+\mathcal{L}

while those corresponding to piecewise affine ones are only included if the corresponding kink has a large enough distance to the boundary,

\displaystyle\left\{\,u\;|\;D^{2}u=\pm\delta_{x}/\beta,\penalty\ \operatorname{dist}(x,\partial(0,T))>\beta/\alpha\,\right\}+\mathcal{L}.

This characterization has consequences in both the analysis and optimization aspects related to the
\operatorname{TGV}
-regularizer. Within the first, we derive precise necessary first-order optimality conditions and sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of
sparse
, i.e., piecewise affine, solutions to minimization problems incorporating the
\operatorname{TGV}
-functional. In particular, based on convex representer theorems, we show that linear inverse problems with finite-dimensional observation and
\operatorname{TGV}
-regularization admit such minimizers. These results are not constrained to denoising problems and confirm the intuition that TGV-regularized solutions are encouraged to be piecewise affine.

On the optimization side, this characterization enables us to formulate and analyze a minimization algorithm that iteratively approximates solutions to (

P

) by conic combinations of representatives of extremal points. This means that every iterate is piecewise affine. In every iteration, the method alternates between “adding” a new extremal point to the current iterate and updating the nonnegative weights in the conic combination via solving a finite dimensional problem with
\ell_{1}
-regularization. In particular, the proposed method completely avoids, possibly complicated, evaluations of the
\operatorname{TGV}
-functional. Moreover, its practical realization does not require a discretization of the underlying interval, meaning that the positions for the discontinuities of the function and its first derivative are found directly and not constrained to be on a discrete grid. We give a preliminary convergence analysis for the method showing subsequential convergence of iterates and a convergence rate in terms of an adequate measure of non-stationarity which dominates the suboptimality of the iterates in the convex case.

As mentioned, at present we are only able to treat the one-dimensional case. In the same spirit as previous works, this serves as a simplified setting in which to infer characteristics of the solutions with the hope that these could be generalized to higher dimensions. However, the one-dimensional case also finds applications in its own right, like in
[
12
]
where it is applied for data assimilation for the Burgers equation, whose solutions may contain shocks. Moreover, in a number of works in signal processing
[
27
,
22
,
19
,
18
]
priors favoring discontinuous piecewise affine functions are considered for time-domain signals. This motivates the numerical example in our last section. In it, our solution algorithm is applied to the inverse problem of reconstructing a piecewise affine signal from the values of its Fourier transform at a small number of frequencies. This is an archetypical example of a regularization problem with few measurements where a very sparse solution exists in light of our theory, but with the added challenge that the measurements made and the features to be reconstructed are of very different nature.

1
Functional space framework

Throughout the paper, we work with the total generalized variation of order two with parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
, which is defined as

\operatorname{TGV}_{\alpha,\beta}(u):=\sup\left\{\int_{0}^{T}u\,v^{\prime\prime}\,\middle|\,v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,T),\,\|v\|_{C}\leq\alpha,\,\|v^{\prime}\|_{C}\leq\beta\right\},

(1.1)

where
\|\cdot\|_{C}
denotes the uniform norm on the space
C_{0}(0,T)
of continuous functions vanishing at the boundary. Whenever possible we omit the dependence on the parameters in the notation of
\operatorname{TGV}
for the sake of brevity. Moreover, according to
[
9
, Thm. 3.1]
, we have

\operatorname{TGV}(u)=\inf_{w\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}\alpha\|Du-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M},

(1.2)

where by
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
we refer to the space of functions of bounded variation on the interval
(0,T)
and
\|\cdot\|_{M}
denotes the canonical norm on the space of Radon measures
M(0,T)=(C_{0}(0,T))^{\ast}
. Note that
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
can be seen as the space of
L^{1}
functions
u
whose distributional derivatives
Du
are elements of
M(0,T)
. Abbreviating
\operatorname{TV}(u)\coloneqq\|Du\|_{M}
, the corresponding norm is given by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\operatorname{BV}}=\|u\|_{L^{1}}+\operatorname{TV}(u)\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T).

For further information on these spaces we refer the reader to Chapters 1 and 3 of
[
1
]
.

Directly from the definition (
1.1
), we see that for all
c,d\in\mathbb{R}
one has
\operatorname{TGV}(u+cx+d)=\operatorname{TGV}(u)
. Accordingly, we denote the subspaces of constant and affine linear functions by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\,u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)\;\middle|\;u=c\cdot 1_{(0,T)},\penalty\ c\in\mathbb{R}\,\right\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big\{\,u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)\;\big|\;u(x)=cx+d,\,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\big\}=\big\{\,u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)\;\big|\;Du\in\mathcal{C}\,\big\},

so that
\operatorname{TGV}(u+\ell)=\operatorname{TGV}(u)
for all
u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
and
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
. As we will see it is beneficial to consider
\operatorname{TGV}
on the corresponding quotient space
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
:

Proposition 1
.

The total generalized variation
\operatorname{TGV}
is an equivalent norm on the quotient space
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
, which admits the dual representation

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}=\big(C_{0,a}(0,T)\big)^{\ast},\text{ with }C_{0,a}(0,T):=\left\{p\in C_{0}(0,T)\;\middle|\;\int_{0}^{T}p\,\mathrm{d}x=0\right\}\text{ and }\\
\langle u+\mathcal{L},p\rangle_{\big(BV(0,T)/\mathcal{L}\,,\,C_{0,a}(0,T)\big)}=\int_{(0,T)}p\,\mathrm{d}Du.\end{gathered}

(1.3)

In particular, the unit ball

\mathcal{B}:=\big\{u+\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}\;\big|\;\operatorname{TGV}(u)\leq 1\big\}

(1.4)

is sequentially compact with respect to the corresponding weak-* topology in
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

To begin with, we notice that both
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{L}
are closed subspaces of
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
with respect to the weak-* topology. Moreover, we may write

\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}=\big(\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{C}\big)/(\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{C}).

In the one dimensional situation we consider, the map

\displaystyle\Lambda:BV(0,T)/\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\to M(0,T)

\displaystyle u+\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\mapsto Du

is clearly continuous by the definition of the corresponding norms, and has as an inverse the map assigning to each
\mu\in M(0,T)
the equivalence class
\big[x\mapsto\mu\big((0,x)\big)\big]+\mathcal{C}
. Using the bounded inverse theorem, this implies that it is an isomorphism of Banach spaces. Furthermore, the image
\Lambda(\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{C})=\mathbb{R}\mathscr{L}
is the set of scalar multiples of the Lebesgue measure restricted to
(0,T)
, which we denote by
\mathscr{L}
. By results for general Banach spaces (an application of Hahn-Banach, essentially) we have
[
16
, Prop. 2.7]
that if
C_{0,a}(0,T)^{\perp}=\mathbb{R}\mathscr{L}
then
M(0,T)/(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{L})=\big(C_{0,a}(0,T)\big)^{\ast}
. To see the equality for the annihilator, consider

\mu\in M(0,T)\text{ satisfying }\int_{(0,T)}p\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0\text{ for all }p\in C_{0,a}(0,T),

and fix an arbitrary function
q_{0}\in C_{0}(0,T)
with
\int_{0}^{T}q_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. Now, for each
q\in C_{0}(0,T)
, just consider the function

C_{0,a}(0,T)\ni p=q-\left(\int_{0}^{T}q\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,q_{0},\text{ so that }\int_{(0,T)}q\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\left(\int_{0}^{T}q\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{(0,T)}q_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right).

Since the number
\int_{(0,T)}q_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
is independent of
q\in C_{0}(0,T)
and by the Riesz representation theorem
M(0,T)=\big(C_{0}(0,T)\big)^{\ast}
, this means that

\mu=\left(\int_{(0,T)}q_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)\mathscr{L}.

To see that
\operatorname{TGV}
is an equivalent norm on
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
, fix
u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
as well as
w_{u}
with

\displaystyle\inf_{w\in\mathrm{BV}}\alpha\|Du-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M}

\displaystyle=\alpha\|Du-w_{u}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{u}\|_{M}.

Now, we can first use the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality
[
1
, Thm. 3.44, Rem. 3.45]
in
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
twice to obtain that

\displaystyle\operatorname{TGV}(u)

\displaystyle=\inf_{w\in\mathrm{BV}}\alpha\|Du-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M}

(1.5)

\displaystyle=\alpha\|Du-w_{u}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{u}\|_{M}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|Du-w_{u}\|_{M}+CT\beta\big\|w_{u}-T^{-1}\textstyle{\int_{0}^{T}}w_{u}\big\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\min(\alpha,CT\beta)\big(\|Du-w_{u}\|_{M}+\big\|w_{u}-T^{-1}\textstyle{\int_{0}^{T}}w_{u}\big\|_{L^{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\min(\alpha,CT\beta)\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\big(\|Du-w_{u}\|_{M}+\big\|w_{u}-c\big\|_{L^{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\min(\alpha,CT\beta)\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|D(u-cx)\|_{M}

\displaystyle\geq CT\min(\alpha,CT\beta)\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\big\|u-cx-T^{-1}\textstyle{\int_{0}^{T}}(u-cx)\big\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\geq CT\min(\alpha,CT\beta)\inf_{c,d\in\mathbb{R}}\big\|u-cx-d\big\|_{L^{1}},

with which we can estimate

\displaystyle\|u+\mathcal{L}\|_{\mathrm{BV}/\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|u-\ell\|_{\mathrm{BV}}=\inf_{c,d\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-cx-d\|_{\mathrm{BV}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{c,d\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-cx-d\|_{L^{1}}+\|D(u-cx)\|_{M}\leq\frac{2}{CT\min(\alpha,CT\beta)}\operatorname{TGV}(u).

On the other hand, testing the definition of
\operatorname{TGV}
with constant
w\equiv c
tells us that

\operatorname{TGV}(u)\leq\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\alpha\|Du-c\|_{M}=\alpha\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|D(u-cx)\|_{M}=\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|D(u-\ell)\|_{M}\leq\|u+\mathcal{L}\|_{\mathrm{BV}/\mathcal{L}}.

We remark that this equivalence of norms is not specific to the one-dimensional case, and in fact a similar estimate is proved in
[
9
, Thm. 3.3]
.

With the equivalence of norms and having identified a separable predual space, compactness of
B
can be characterized by sequences and follows by an application of the Banach-Alaoglu theorem.
∎

2
Extremal points of
\operatorname{TGV}

In this section, we consider the extremal points of the set
\mathcal{B}
:

Definition 1
.

An element
u\in\mathcal{B}
is called an extremal point of
\mathcal{B}
if

\displaystyle u=u_{1}+s(u_{2}-u_{1})\ \text{for some}\penalty\ u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }s\in(0,1)\text{ implies }u=u_{1}=u_{2}.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is weak* compact in
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
, the set of all extremal points of
\mathcal{B}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}
is nonempty due to the Krein-Milman theorem.
We prove the following characterization:

Theorem 1
.

The extremal points of
\mathcal{B}=\big\{u+\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}\;\big|\;\operatorname{TGV}(u)\leq 1\big\}
satisfy

\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}=\pm\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\alpha}S_{x_{0}}\;\Big|\;x_{0}\in(0,T)\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{1}{\beta}K_{x_{1}}\;\Big|\;\operatorname{dist}(x_{1},\partial(0,T))>\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right\}\right)+\mathcal{L},

(2.1)

where, denoting by
1_{(a,b)}
the characteristic function of an interval
(a,b)
,

S_{x_{0}}=1_{(x_{0},T)}\text{ and }K_{x_{1}}=\begin{cases}1_{(0,x_{1})}(x_{1}-x)&\text{ if }x_{1}<T/2\\
1_{(x_{1},T)}(x-x_{1})&\text{ if }x_{1}\geq T/2.\end{cases}

The strategy is centered around the fact that since in one dimension we can always find primitives,
\operatorname{TGV}
can be rewritten as an infimal convolution:

Lemma 1
.

For every
\bar{u}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
we have

\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,h\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T),\\
\bar{u}=v+h\end{subarray}}\left[\,\alpha\operatorname{TV}(v)+\beta\operatorname{TV}(Dh)\,\right],

(2.2)

and if
\bar{w}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
is such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})=\alpha\|D\bar{u}-\bar{w}\|_{M}+\beta\|D\bar{w}\|_{M}

then the infimum is attained for

\bar{v}=\bar{u}-\int^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}_{0}\bar{w}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t,\penalty\ \bar{h}=\int^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}_{0}\bar{w}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t.

(2.3)

Moreover we can define
\operatorname{TGV}
also in the quotient space
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
, and there it can also be written as an infimal convolution:

\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})=\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u}+\mathcal{L})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v+\mathcal{L},h+\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L},\\
\bar{u}=v+h\end{subarray}}\left[\,\alpha\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\operatorname{TV}(v+\ell)+\beta\operatorname{TV}(Dh)\,\right].

(2.4)

Proof.

First note that since
Dh\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
we have, denoting by
D^{a}
and
D^{s}
the absolutely continuous and singular parts with respect to the Lebesgue measure, that
D^{s}h=0
and thus

\displaystyle D^{s}h=0,\penalty\ D^{s}\bar{u}=D^{s}v,\penalty\ D^{a}v=D^{a}\bar{u}-Dh.

As a consequence we can rewrite

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,h\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T),\\
\bar{u}=v+h\end{subarray}}\left[\,\alpha\operatorname{TV}(v)+\beta\operatorname{TV}(Dh)\,\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{Dh\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}\alpha\|D^{s}\bar{u}\|_{M}+\alpha\|D^{a}\bar{u}-Dh\|_{M}+\beta\operatorname{TV}(Dh)

\displaystyle=\inf_{w\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}\alpha\|D\bar{u}-w\|_{M}+\beta\operatorname{TV}(w)

where we use that for every
w\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
there is
W\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
with
DW=w
. Moreover we have
\bar{u}=\bar{v}+\bar{h}
and

\displaystyle\alpha\operatorname{TV}(\bar{v})+\beta\operatorname{TV}(D\bar{h})=\alpha\|D\bar{u}-\bar{w}\|_{M}+\beta\|D^{2}\bar{w}\|_{M}=\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})

finishing the proof of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
). Finally, to deduce (
2.4
) from (
2.2
) we just notice that by definition
\operatorname{TV}(Dh)=\operatorname{TV}\big(D(h+\ell)\big)
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
.
∎

Then we have the following more general negative result, which allows us to reduce the possible candidates for extremal points:

Lemma 2
.

Let
f,g
be convex positively one-homogeneous functionals defined on some Banach space
X
, and

(f\,\Box\,g)(u):=\inf_{u=v+w}f(v)+g(w)

(2.5)

their infimal convolution. Given
u_{0}\in X
, assume that either
f(u_{0})\neq 1
or
g(u_{0})\neq 1
, and
v_{0},w_{0}
are such that

(f\,\Box\,g)(u_{0})=1,\quad u_{0}=v_{0}+w_{0},\quad f(v_{0})+g(w_{0})=1,\quad f(v_{0})\neq 0,\quad g(w_{0})\neq 0

so (
2.5
) is exact at
u_{0}
with the infimum attained by
v_{0}
and
w_{0}
. Then
u_{0}
cannot be an extremal point of the convex set

\left\{u\in X\,\middle|\,(f\,\Box\,g)(u)\leq 1\right\}.

Proof.

First, notice that we must have
f(v_{0})\leq g(v_{0})
. To see this, assume for the sake of contradiction that
g(v_{0})<f(v_{0})
. Being convex and one-homogeneous
g
is sublinear, so we have that

g(v_{0}+w_{0})\leq g(v_{0})+g(w_{0})<f(v_{0})+g(w_{0})

and the pair
(v_{0},w_{0})
was not optimal for the infimal convolution, since
(0,v_{0}+w_{0})
has lower energy. Similarly we conclude that
g(w_{0})\leq f(w_{0})
.

Moreover, we claim that

(f\,\Box\,g)(v_{0})=f(v_{0})\in(0,1),\quad(f\,\Box\,g)(w_{0})=g(w_{0})\in(0,1).

(2.6)

To see this, notice that if on the contrary we had for some
v_{1},w_{1}
with
v_{0}=v_{1}+w_{1}
that

(f\,\Box\,g)(v_{0})=f(v_{1})+g(w_{1})<f(v_{0})

we would end up, using the sublinearity, with

f(v_{0})+g(w_{0})>f(v_{1})+g(w_{0})+g(w_{1})\geq f(v_{1})+g(w_{0}+w_{1}),

and again the pair
(v_{0},w_{0})
could not have been optimal for
(f\,\Box\,g)(u_{0})
. The claim for
w_{0}
is again entirely similar.

Using in (
2.6
) that the infimal convolution is one-homogeneous as well we arrive at

(f\,\Box\,g)\left(\frac{v_{0}}{f(v_{0})}\right)=1,\quad(f\,\Box\,g)\left(\frac{w_{0}}{g(w_{0})}\right)=1,

which means that we can write

u_{0}=f(v_{0})\frac{v_{0}}{f(v_{0})}+g(w_{0})\frac{w_{0}}{g(w_{0})}.

Now if
v_{0}/f(v_{0})\neq w_{0}/g(w_{0})
, these two points and
u_{0}
are collinear in
\left\{u\in X\,\middle|\,(f\,\Box\,g)(u)=1\right\}
, and
u_{0}
could not be extremal. Otherwise

v_{0}=c\,w_{0}\text{ with }c:=f(v_{0})/g(w_{0}),\text{ so }u_{0}=(1+c)w_{0}=(1+c^{-1})v_{0}.

By one-homogeneity and the first part of the proof

\displaystyle g(u_{0})=(1+c)g(w_{0})

\displaystyle\leq(1+c)f(w_{0})=f(u_{0})\text{ and }

\displaystyle f(u_{0})=(1+c^{-1})f(v_{0})

\displaystyle\leq(1+c^{-1})g(v_{0})=g(u_{0}),

from which we conclude
f(u_{0})=g(u_{0})=(f\,\Box\,g)(u_{0})=1
, contrary to our assumption.
∎

Applied to our case, the above lemma yields:

Proposition 2
.

Denoting
\mathcal{B}:=\{u+\mathcal{L}\;\big|\;\operatorname{TGV}(u)\leq 1\}
we have

\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}\subset\pm\left(\left\{\,u\;\Big|\;\|Du\|_{M}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\right\}\cup\left\{\,u\;\Big|\;\|D^{2}u\|_{M}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\,\right\}\right)+\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{B},

(2.7)

and every element in
\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}
is either of the form
\pm\{S_{x}/\alpha+\mathcal{L}\}
or
\pm\{K_{x}/\beta+\mathcal{L}\}
.

Proof.

We consider
\operatorname{TGV}
as an infimal convolution in
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
as in (
2.4
) of Lemma
1
. First, we notice that for each equivalence class
u+\mathcal{L}
there exist representatives
\bar{u}
for which

\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|D(u+\ell)\|_{M}=\|D\bar{u}\|_{M},

(2.8)

since this is a convex and coercive functional in
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{C}
. This also implies that the functional
u\mapsto\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|D(u+\ell)\|_{M}
is convex, since if
u+\mathcal{L}=(1-\lambda)(u_{1}+\mathcal{L})+\lambda(u_{2}+\mathcal{L})
and
\bar{u_{1}},\bar{u_{2}}
are such representatives we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|D(u+\ell)\|_{M}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|D\big((1-\lambda)\bar{u_{1}}+\lambda\bar{u_{2}}\big)\big\|_{M}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\|D\bar{u_{1}}\|_{M}+\lambda\|D\bar{u_{2}}\|_{M}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|D(u_{1}+\ell)\|_{M}+\lambda\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|D(u_{2}+\ell)\|_{M}.

Moreover since
\mathcal{L}
is a linear subspace the functional above is also positively one-homogeneous, so we can apply Lemma
2
. In view of it, to arrive at (
2.7
) we just need to check that

\left\{u+\mathcal{L}\,\Big|\,\alpha\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|D(u+\ell)\|_{M}\leq 1\right\}=\left\{\,u\;\Big|\;\|Du\|_{M}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\right\}+\mathcal{L},

which again follows directly by the existence of minimal representatives (
2.8
).

Notice that the assumptions of Lemma

are not a restriction since the case
\|Du\|_{M}/\alpha=\|D^{2}u\|_{M}/\beta=\operatorname{TGV}(u)=1
(for

an optimal representative as above) is also included in (

).
∎

For the positive direction, the piecewise constant functions in (
2.1
) can be handled directly:

Proposition 3
.

Let
u\in\{S_{x}/\alpha\}+\mathcal{L}
,
x\in(0,T)
, be given. Assume that there are
u_{1}+\mathcal{L},u_{2}+\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}
as well as
\lambda\in(0,1)
with
u=(1-\lambda)u_{1}+\lambda u_{2}
. Then we have
u_{1},u_{2}\in\{S_{x}/\alpha\}+\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Again we may assume that
\mathrm{TGV}(u_{1})=\mathrm{TGV}(u_{2})=1
. For every
i=1,2
there exists
w_{i}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
with

\displaystyle\alpha\|Du_{i}-w_{i}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{i}\|_{M}=\alpha\|D^{s}u_{i}\|_{M}+\alpha\|D^{a}u_{i}-w_{i}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{i}\|_{M}=\operatorname{TGV}(u_{i})=1.

In particular, this implies

\displaystyle\alpha\|D^{s}u_{i}\|_{M}\leq\alpha\|D^{s}u_{i}\|_{M}+\alpha\|D^{a}u_{i}-w_{i}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{i}\|_{M}=\operatorname{TGV}(u_{i})=1.

(2.9)

By assumption we have that

\displaystyle Du=\delta_{x}/\alpha+c=(1-\lambda)Du_{1}+\lambda Du_{2}

for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, separating into singular and absolutely continuous parts, we get

\displaystyle\delta_{x}/\alpha=(1-\lambda)D^{s}u_{1}+\lambda D^{s}u_{2},\penalty\ c=(1-\lambda)D^{a}u_{1}+\lambda D^{a}u_{2}.

By extremality of
\delta_{x}
for the unit ball in
M(0,T)
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the first equality is only possible if
D^{s}u_{1}=D^{s}u_{2}=\delta_{x}/\alpha
. Now, since
\alpha|D^{s}u_{i}|=1
, (
2.9
) yields

\displaystyle\alpha\|D^{a}u_{i}-w_{i}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{i}\|_{M}=\inf_{w\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}\left[\,\alpha\|D^{a}u_{i}-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M}\,\right]=0.

which is only possible if
D^{a}u_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. Combining the previous observations we get that
Du_{i}\in\{\delta_{x}/\alpha\}+\mathcal{C}
and thus
u_{i}\in\{S_{x}/\alpha\}+\mathcal{L}
.
∎

To proceed further, we need to examine in detail the behaviour of
\operatorname{TGV}
for piecewise affine functions of the form
K_{x_{1}}
.

Proposition 4
.

Consider the functions
K_{x_{1}}
defined in Theorem
1
. Assuming

, if

\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\leq T-x_{1}

(2.10)

we have
\operatorname{TGV}_{\alpha,\beta}(K_{x_{1}})=\beta\|D^{2}u\|_{M}=\beta
, and otherwise
\operatorname{TGV}_{\alpha,\beta}(K_{x_{1}})=\alpha\|Du\|_{M}=\alpha(T-x_{1})
. For

analogous statements hold, in which each appearance of
T-x_{1}
is replaced by
x_{1}
. Moreover, as long as
\beta/\alpha\neq\min(x_{1},T-x_{1})
the inner minimization problem

\argmin_{w\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}\alpha\|DK_{x_{1}}-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M}

(2.11)

w_{K_{x_{1}}}
, where the uniqueness is understood in terms of almost everywhere equality.

Proof.

By definition, in case
x_{1}\in[T/2,T)
we have
K_{x_{1}}(x)=(x-x_{1})^{+}
and if
x_{1}\in(0,T)
instead
K_{x_{1}}(x)=(x-x_{1})^{+}-x+x_{1}
. Since (
2.11
) is invariant by sign changes and constant shifts in
DK_{x_{1}}
, we can consider without loss of generality just the function
(\cdot-x_{1})^{+}
. We then notice that since
D(\cdot-x_{1})^{+}(x)\in\{0,1\}
, as in
[
15
, Prop. 4.5]
by comparing with truncations one sees that any minimizer of (
2.11
) takes values in the interval
[0,1]
almost everywhere. Moreover, by
[
1
, Thm. 3.27]
we can compute one-dimensional
\operatorname{TV}
through the essential (pointwise) variation

\operatorname{TV}(w)=\inf_{z=w\penalty\ a.e.}\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|z(t_{i+1})-z(t_{i})|\,\middle|\,n\geq 2,\ 0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<T\right\},

(2.12)

and the infimum is achieved for a particular representative
z_{w}
, which will be assumed in the rest of the proof (that is, from here on

). In particular (
2.12

\operatorname{TV}(w)\geq\sup_{x\in(0,T)}w(x)-\inf_{x\in(0,T)}w(x)=\sup w-\inf w.

(2.13)

To see this, assume
w
is not constant (since otherwise there is nothing to prove) and consider sequences
\{x_{k}\}_{k}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k}
for which
w(x_{k})\to\inf w
and
w(y_{k})\to\sup w
. Since
w
is not constant, for all

, which allows to use these points for partitions in (
2.12

\displaystyle\alpha\|DK-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha x_{1}\inf w+\alpha(T-x_{1})(1-\sup w)+\beta(\sup w-\inf w)

\displaystyle=(\alpha x_{1}-\beta)\inf w+(\beta-\alpha(T-x_{1}))\sup w+\alpha(T-x_{1}),

where we note that the last term does not depend on
w
. Our strategy is to first minimize the right hand side with respect to
\inf w
and
\sup w
and then produce functions with these extrema for which (
2.14
) holds with equality, which must then be minimizers. To accomplish this for every
\alpha
,
\beta
and
x_{1}
we distinguish four cases:

•

If
x_{1}\geq\beta/\alpha
and
(T-x_{1})\geq\beta/\alpha
, then the right hand side of (

) is clearly lowest if
\inf w=0
and
\sup w=1
, in which case it reaches the value
\beta
. Moreover, when

we have equality in (
2.14
), so it is a minimizer with cost
\beta
.

•

For
x_{1}<\beta/\alpha
and
(T-x_{1})<\beta/\alpha
, the first term compels us to maximize
\inf w
while the second term enforces minimizing
\sup w
. In case
x_{1}-\beta/\alpha>(T-x_{1})-\beta/\alpha
(that is,
x_{1}>T/2
) the second term is stronger, so it is advantageous to choose

x_{1}\leq T/2

\alpha

•

The two remaining cases

or

respectively. Again, there is equality in (

\alpha

Finally, we notice that the only possibility that does not lead to a unique minimizer is when one of the terms of the right hand side of (

x_{1}=\beta/\alpha
in which case

is a minimizer for all

, or that
(T-x_{1})=\beta/\alpha
with

c\in[0,1]
.
∎

With this, we can prove that all of the piecewise affine functions in (

Proposition 5
.

Let
u\in\{K_{x}/\beta\}+\mathcal{L}
,
\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial(0,T))>\beta/\alpha
, be given. Assume that there are
u_{1},u_{2}\in B
as well as
\lambda\in(0,1)
with

. Then we have

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\mathrm{TGV}(u_{1})=\mathrm{TGV}(u_{2})=1
. For
i=1,2
there exists

with

\displaystyle\beta\|Dw_{i}\|_{M}\leq\alpha\|Du_{i}-w_{i}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{i}\|_{M}=\operatorname{TGV}(u_{i})=1.

Moreover, by Proposition
4

\displaystyle\min_{w\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}\left[\,\alpha\|Du-w\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw\|_{M}\,\right]

. Now, by assumption, we have

\displaystyle 1=\operatorname{TGV}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\|Du-w_{\lambda}\|_{M}+\beta\|Dw_{\lambda}\|_{M}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\operatorname{TGV}(u_{1})+\lambda\operatorname{TGV}(u_{2})=1.

Thus we conclude

and

\displaystyle\delta_{x}/\beta=(1-\lambda)Dw_{1}+\lambda Dw_{2}

Again, by extremality of
\delta_{x}
for the unit ball in

w_{i}\in\{S_{x}/\beta\}+\mathcal{C}
. Now, since

\displaystyle 1=\beta\|Dw_{i}\|_{M}\leq\operatorname{TGV}(u_{i})=1

we arrive at

which finally yields
u_{i}\in\{K_{x}/\beta\}+\mathcal{L}
.
∎

On the other hand the equality case can be resolved manually:

Remark 1
.

If
\operatorname{dist}(x_{1},\partial(0,T))=\beta/\alpha
then

is not in

\frac{1}{\beta}K_{x_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}u_{1}+\frac{1}{2}u_{2}\,\text{ with }\,u_{1}=\frac{2}{\beta}K_{x_{1}}-\frac{2}{\beta}K_{(T+x_{1})/2}\text{ and }\,u_{2}=\frac{2}{\beta}K_{(T+x_{1})/2},

and

\operatorname{TGV}(u_{1})=\alpha\|Du_{1}\|_{M}=\operatorname{TGV}(u_{2})=\alpha\|Du_{2}\|_{M}=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(T-x_{1})=1.

Finally, putting the above facts together we get:

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By Proposition
2
, elements in
\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}
can only be equivalence classes corresponding to either piecewise constant functions with one jump in
\pm\{S_{x}/\alpha\}+\mathcal{L}

∎

3
Optimization problems with
\operatorname{TGV}
-regularization

In the following, we discuss the consequences of Theorem
1
on minimization problems of the form

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}J(u),\quad J(u)=f(u)+\operatorname{TGV}_{\alpha,\beta}(u).

(
\mathcal{P}
)

Here,
f\colon L^{2}(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
denotes a smooth but not necessarily convex fidelity term and the
\operatorname{TGV}
-functional plays the role of a regularizer. From a practical point of view, problems of this form are interesting since the inclusion of the
\operatorname{TGV}
-functional is expected to favor piecewise affine minimizers. This section aims at formalizing this observation. In particular, we present sufficient conditions for the existence of a solution
\bar{u}
which is
sparse
in the sense that, up to a shift in
\mathcal{L}
, it can be written as a finite conic combination of piecewise constant and continuous piecewise affine functions, i.e., there are sets
\{\bar{x}^{S}_{j}\}^{N_{S}}_{j=1},\penalty\ \{\bar{x}^{K}_{j}\}^{N_{K}}_{j=1}
in
(0,T)
such that

\displaystyle\bar{u}=(1/\alpha)\sum^{N_{S}}_{j=1}\bar{\sigma}^{S}_{j}\bar{\lambda}^{S}_{j}S_{\bar{x}^{S}_{j}}+(1/\beta)\sum^{N_{K}}_{i=j}\bar{\sigma}^{K}_{j}\bar{\lambda}^{K}_{j}K_{\bar{x}^{K}_{j}}+\bar{a}x+\bar{b}.

(3.1)

for some
\bar{\sigma}^{S}_{j},\bar{\sigma}^{K}_{j}\in\{-1,1\}
,
\bar{\lambda}^{S}_{j},\bar{\lambda}^{K}_{j}\geq 0,\bar{a},\bar{b}\in\mathbb{R}
. Two characteristic cases are discussed. First, we show that the kinks and jumps of a solution
\bar{u}
correspond to maximizers and minimizers of certain dual variables. This can be derived from necessary first-order optimality conditions for Problem (

P

) which are provided in Section
3.2
. Consequently, if these only admit
finitely many
global extrema, the function
\bar{u}
is of the form (
3.1
). Second, we consider structured objective functionals of the form
f(u)=F(Hu)
where
F
is convex and
H
is a linear operator with finite range. In this case, the existence of a sparse solution follows from
convex representer theorems
for abstract minimization problems, see
[
3
,
2
]
. Loosely speaking, applied to the present problem, these state the existence of a minimizer
\bar{u}
which is given by a finite conic combination of functions
\bar{u}_{j}
with
\bar{u}_{j}+\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}
. Thus, in virtue of Theorem
1
, it will be of the form (
3.1
).

3.1
Linear minimization over
\operatorname{TGV}
-balls

For abbreviation set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\left\{\,\pm S_{x}/\alpha\;|\;x\in(0,T)\,\right\},\penalty\ \mathcal{K}\coloneqq\left\{\,\pm K_{x}/\beta\;|\;x\in(\beta/\alpha,T-\beta/\alpha)\,\right\}

throughout the following sections. Moreover, with abuse of notation, define

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}B\coloneqq\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{K}\subset\mathrm{BV}(0,T),\text{ for }B:=\big\{u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)\;|\;\operatorname{TGV}(u)\leq 1\big\}.

Note that for the unit ball
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
, see (
1.4
), and its set of extremal points, Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\left\{\,u+\mathcal{L}\;|\;u\in B\,\right\},\penalty\ \operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}=\left\{\,u+\mathcal{L}\;|\;u\in\operatorname{Ext}B\,\right\}.

Now, given
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T)
, consider the infimizing problem

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in B}(\varphi,v)_{L^{2}}.

(3.2)

Problems of this form will play a vital role in the following sections. We point out that the set
B
is unbounded in
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
, i.e., (
3.2
) does not necessarily admit a minimizer. In the following lemma we show that it is well-posed if
\varphi
is orthogonal to the kernel of the
\operatorname{TGV}
-functional. Moreover, in order to find a solution, it suffices to minimize over the smaller set
\operatorname{Ext}B
.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T)
satisfies
(\varphi,\ell)_{L^{2}}=0
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
. Then (
3.2
) admits a minimizer in
B
and there holds

\displaystyle\min_{v\in B}(\varphi,v)_{L^{2}}=\min_{v\in\operatorname{Ext}B}(\varphi,v)_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
v\in B
be arbitrary but fixed. Using that
T<+\infty
and the Sobolev embedding
[
1
, Thm. 3.47]
for
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
there holds

\displaystyle(\varphi,v)_{L^{2}}=(\varphi,v+\ell)_{L^{2}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|v+\ell\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|v+\ell\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c\,\|v+\ell\|_{\mathrm{BV}}\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ \ell\in\mathcal{L}

for some
c>0
independent of
v
and
\ell
. In particular, this implies

\displaystyle(\varphi,v)_{L^{2}}\leq c\inf_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\|v+\ell\|_{\mathrm{BV}},

i.e.,
\varphi
induces a linear and continuous functional
\Phi\colon\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle\Phi(u+\mathcal{L})=(\varphi,u)_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ u+\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}.

Now by Proposition
1
and the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, the set
\mathcal{B}
is weak* compact in
\mathrm{BV}(0,T)/\mathcal{L}
. Hence there exists
\bar{v}\in B
with

\displaystyle\Phi(\bar{v}+\mathcal{L})=\min_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L}).

(3.3)

Now we claim that

\displaystyle\min_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L})=\min_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Ext}(\mathcal{B})}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L}).

(3.4)

In order to prove this, consider the convex and compact image set

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{B})\coloneqq\left\{\,\Phi(v+\mathcal{L})\;|\;v\in B\,\right\}\subset\mathbb{R},

for which it is readily verified that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}\Phi(\mathcal{B})=\left\{\min_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L}),\max_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L})\right\}.

By invoking
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
we finally get

\displaystyle\min_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{B}}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L})\in\left\{\min_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L}),\max_{v+\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Ext}\mathcal{B}}\Phi(v+\mathcal{L})\right\}.

which proves (
3.4
). Now, let
\bar{v}\in\operatorname{Ext}B
be such that (
3.3
) holds. Then we have

\displaystyle(\varphi,\bar{v})_{L^{2}}=\Phi(\bar{v}+\mathcal{L})\leq\Phi(v+\mathcal{L})=(\varphi,v)_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ v\in B.

Together with
\operatorname{Ext}B\subset B
the proof is finished.
∎

3.2
Necessary first-order optimality conditions

The aim of this section is to prove first-order necessary optimality conditions for Problem (

P

). Later, these will be used to infer structural properties of its minimizers. For this purpose, start by defining the

-subdifferential of the
\operatorname{TGV}

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})\coloneqq\left\{\,\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T)\;|\;(\varphi,u-\bar{u})_{L^{2}}+\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})\leq\operatorname{TGV}(u)\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)\;\,\right\}.

Note that
\partial_{2}\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})

Theorem 3
.

Let
\bar{u}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)

\bar{w}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)

\displaystyle\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})=\alpha\|D\bar{u}-\bar{w}\|_{M}+\beta\|D\bar{w}\|_{M}.

\displaystyle\bar{p}=-\int^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}_{0}\bar{\varphi}(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x,\penalty\ \bar{P}=-\int^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}_{0}\bar{p}(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x.

•

There holds

(3.7)

•

We have

\begin{split}\operatorname{supp}(D\bar{u}-\bar{w})_{\pm}&\subset\left\{\,x\in(0,T)\;|\;\bar{p}(x)=\pm\alpha\,\right\},\\
\operatorname{supp}(D\bar{w})_{\pm}&\subset\left\{\,x\in(0,T)\;|\;\bar{P}(x)=\pm\beta\,\right\}.\end{split}

(3.8)

We break down the proof into smaller steps. The following proposition serves as a starting point.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\bar{u}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
and
\bar{\varphi}\in L^{2}(0,T)
. Then there holds
\bar{\varphi}\in\partial_{2}\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})

\displaystyle(\bar{\varphi},\ell)_{L^{2}}=0,\penalty\ (\bar{\varphi},\bar{u})_{L^{2}}=\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u}),\penalty\ (\bar{\varphi},v)_{L^{2}}\leq\operatorname{TGV}(v)

Proof.

By definition, there holds
\bar{\varphi}\in\partial_{2}\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})

\displaystyle(\bar{\varphi},u-\bar{u})_{L^{2}}+\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u})\leq\operatorname{TGV}(u)\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ u\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)

(3.9)

Of course, this is equivalent to
\bar{u}
being a minimizer of

\displaystyle\min_{v\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)}\left[-(\bar{\varphi},v)_{L^{2}}+\operatorname{TGV}(v)\right].

(3.10)

Since
\operatorname{TGV}(\cdot)
is positively one-homogeneous and
\operatorname{TGV}(u+\ell)=\operatorname{TGV}(u)
for all

and
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, this holds (with value zero) if and only if

\displaystyle(\bar{\varphi},\ell)_{L^{2}}=0,\penalty\ (\bar{\varphi},\bar{u})_{L^{2}}=\operatorname{TGV}(\bar{u}),\penalty\ (\bar{\varphi},v)_{L^{2}}\leq\operatorname{TGV}(v)

(3.11)

for all

and

.
∎

Now we first prove that
\bar{\varphi}
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{L}
if and only if
\bar{p}
and

Lemma 3
.

Let
\bar{\varphi}\in L^{2}(0,T)
be given and let
\bar{p}
and
\bar{P}
be defined as in (

The following statements are equivalent:

•

There holds

•

We have
\bar{p}\in H^{1}_{0}(0,T)

Proof.

By construction we have
\bar{p}\in H^{1}(0,T)

\bar{p}(0)=\bar{P}(0)=0

\bar{p}(T)=\bar{P}(T)=0

with

In this appendix we finally give the counterexample announced in Remark
4

\mathcal{A}=\{u_{j}\}^{N}_{j=1}
in
\operatorname{Ext}B
as well as a convex and smooth function
f\colon L^{2}(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
with
\min\eqref{eq:altsubprob}<\min\eqref{def:subprob}

are constructed. For this purpose, choose

and
\alpha,\beta>0
with
\beta/\alpha=1/2
. Moreover set
\bar{x}_{1}=6
,
\bar{x}_{2}=6.25
and fix
\bar{\lambda}_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\bar{\lambda}_{2}-\bar{\lambda}_{1}|<0.25,\penalty\ 3.75<\bar{\lambda}_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}.

Under this prerequisites, the function
\bar{u}=\bar{\lambda}_{1}u_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}u_{2}
with

The following proposition provides an outline for the construction of a counterexample.

Proposition 13
.

Assume that there is
\bar{\varphi}\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that

\displaystyle(u_{j},\bar{\varphi})_{L^{2}}+1=0\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ j=1,2,\penalty\ (\ell,\bar{\varphi})_{L^{2}}=0\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ \ell\in\mathcal{L}.

(A.1)

Then
(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathcal{L}
is a minimizer of

\displaystyle\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int^{10}_{0}\Big|\sum^{2}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}u_{j}(x)-u_{d}(x)\Big|^{2}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x+\sum^{2}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\right]

(
\mathfrak{P}
)

for
u_{d}=\bar{u}-\bar{\varphi}
.

Proof.

First, note that the objective functional of (

P

) is convex in
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ell)
.
Calculating its partial derivatives at
(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2},0)
reveals

\displaystyle(u_{j},\bar{u}-u_{d})_{L^{2}}+1=(u_{j},\bar{\varphi})_{L^{2}}+1=0\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ j=1,2

as well as

\displaystyle(\ell,\bar{u}-f)_{L^{2}}=(\ell,\bar{\varphi})_{L^{2}}=0\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ \ell\in\mathcal{L}.

(A.2)

Consequently,
(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\bar{\lambda}_{2},0)
is a stationary point of (

P

) and thus a minimizer.
∎

In particular, if
\bar{\varphi}
exists we have

\displaystyle\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int^{10}_{0}\Big|\sum^{2}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}u_{j}(x)-u_{d}(x)\Big|^{2}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}x+\operatorname{TGV}\left(\sum^{2}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}u_{j}\right)\right]<\min\eqref{probcounter}

due to

\displaystyle w_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\bar{x}_{1}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{2}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases},\penalty\ w_{2}(x)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{3}{8}K_{\bar{x}_{1}}(x)&\bar{x}_{1}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{2}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases},\penalty\ \ell_{1}(x)=1,\penalty\ \ell_{2}(x)=x-5.

Note that

\displaystyle(u_{1},w_{1})_{L^{2}}\neq 0,\penalty\ (u_{1},w_{2})_{L^{2}}=0,\penalty\ (u_{2},w_{2})_{L^{2}}\neq 0,\penalty\ (u_{2},w_{1})_{L^{2}}=0

\displaystyle(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})_{L^{2}}=0,\penalty\ \operatorname{span}\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}=\mathcal{L}.

\displaystyle\varphi(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4})\coloneqq\gamma_{1}w_{1}+\gamma_{2}w_{2}+\gamma_{3}\ell_{1}+\gamma_{4}\ell_{4}.

(A.3)

Proposition 14
.

There is
(\bar{\gamma}_{1},\bar{\gamma}_{2},\bar{\gamma}_{3},\bar{\gamma}_{4})

\bar{\varphi}\coloneqq\varphi(\bar{\gamma}_{1},\bar{\gamma}_{2},\bar{\gamma}_{3},\bar{\gamma}_{4})

Proof.

Inserting the ansatz (
A.3

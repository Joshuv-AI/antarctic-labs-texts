Action-angle coordinates for motion in a straight magnetic field with constant gradient
Alain J. Brizard

Abstract
The motion of a charged particle in a straight magnetic field 𝐁 = B ⁡ ( y ) ​ 𝗓 ^ {\bf B}=B(y)\,\widehat{\sf z} with a constant perpendicular gradient is solved exactly in terms of elliptic functions and integrals. The motion can be decomposed in terms of a periodic motion along the y y -axis and a drift motion along the x x -axis. The periodic motion can be described as a particle trapped in a symmetric quartic potential in y y . The canonical transformation from the canonical coordinates ( y , P y ) (y,P_{y}) to the action-angle coordinates ( J , θ ) (J,\theta) is solved explicitly in terms of a generating function S ⁡ ( θ , J ) S(\theta,J) that is expressed in terms of Jacobi elliptic functions. The presence of a weak constant electric field 𝐄 = E 0 ​ 𝗒 ^ {\bf E}=E_{0}\,\widehat{\sf y} introduces an asymmetric component to the quartic potential, and the associated periodic motion is solved perturbatively up to second order.



I
Introduction

The motion of a charged particle in a straight magnetic field
{\bf B}=B(y)\,\widehat{\sf z}
with constant perpendicular gradient was considered recently
Brizard_2017
in order to explore the validity of the guiding-center approximation
Cary_Brizard_2009
in the present of strong gradients. There, a single orbit in the
(x,y)
plane was solved exactly in terms of elliptic functions and integrals
footnote
, and the orbit-averaged position
\langle y\rangle
and drift velocity
\langle\dot{x}\rangle
were compared, respectively, with the polarization shift and magnetic-drift velocity predicted by guiding-center Hamiltonian theory
Cary_Brizard_2009
. An excellent agreement was found between exact analytical results and guiding-center predictions, even in the presence of strong magnetic gradients.

The purpose of the present paper is to consider all orbit types associated with the straight magnetic field
{\bf B}=B_{0}\,(1-y/L)\,\widehat{\sf z}
considered in Ref.
Brizard_2017
, where
B_{0}
is the field magnitude at
y=0
and
|\nabla\ln B(y)|_{y=0}\equiv 1/L
defines a constant gradient length scale
L
. The equations of motion in the
(x,y)
-plane perpendicular to the magnetic field are

\left.\begin{array}[]{rc l}\ddot{x}&=&\Omega_{0}\,(1-y/L)\,\dot{y}\\
\ddot{y}&=&-\,\Omega_{0}\,(1-y/L)\,\dot{x}\end{array}\right\},

(1)

where
\Omega_{0}=qB_{0}/M
denotes the constant gyrofrequency for a particle of charge
q
and mass
M
. By using the canonical momentum conservation law

P_{x}=M\,\left[\dot{x}\;-\frac{}{}\Omega_{0}\left(y-y^{2}/2L\right)\right]\equiv M\Omega_{0}L\,\left(u-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(2)

we can now express the
y
-dynamics in terms of the energy conservation law
E=M\dot{y}^{2}/2+V(y,u)
, where the potential energy
V(y,u)\equiv M\dot{x}^{2}/2
represents a quartic potential in
y
, with minima
(\dot{x}=0)
at

y/L=1\pm\sqrt{2u}
and a local maximum at
y=L
, where
V(L,u)=\frac{1}{2}\,M\,(u\,\Omega_{0}L)^{2}
.

The study of charged particle motion in the presence of a magnetic neutral sheet in a magnetized space plasma has been carried out elsewhere
Speiser_1965
;
Rothwell_Yates_1984
;
Parks_2003
. In the present paper, we expand the recent work of Kabin
Kabin_2021
on explicit solutions of this system and calculate, for each orbit type, the action-angle coordinates and the generating function for the canonical transformation to these coordinates. Because the associated 2D orbits are labeled by two exact invariants (energy and canonical momentum along the
x
-axis), the motion is exactly integrable and the construction of the magnetic moment is not required.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, the equations of motion (
1
) in the
(x,y)
plane are transformed into the problem of a particle moving in a symmetric quartic potential, whose solutions in terms of the Jacobi elliptic functions and integrals are well known
Reichl_1984
;
Brizard_Westland_2017
. In Sec.
III
, these orbital solutions are used to calculate an action integral
J\equiv(1/2\pi)\oint P_{y}\,dy
for each particle orbit in terms of elliptic integrals. In addition, orbital periods are recovered from these action integrals in accordance to standard definitions
Goldstein_2002
. In Sec.
IV
, an angle coordinate
\theta
is introduced for each orbit and these angles are shown to be canonical to the action coordinates introduced in Sec.
III
. In Sec.
V
, the function
S
that generates the canonical transformation
(P_{y},y)\rightarrow(J,\theta)
is derived from the relation
Arnold_1989

P_{y}\,dy=J\,d\theta+dS
. In Sec.
VI
, we investigate the problem of a particle moving in an asymmetric quartic potential in the presence of a constant electric field. Finally, our work is summarized in Sec.
VII
, while useful definitions of the Jacobi epsilon and zeta functions are presented in App. A.

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}

III
Action Integrals and Orbital Periods

The area enclosed by each phase-space curve in Fig.
6
is related to the action integral
J(E)=(1/2\pi)\oint y^{\prime}\,dy
, from which the orbital period can be derived as
T(E)\equiv 2\pi\,dJ(E)/dE
. In our model, the energy is expressed as

E\;=\;u^{2}{\sf e}^{2}/2,

(24)

so that the orbital period
T(u,{\sf e})
for each orbit type can be calculated as

T(u,{\sf e})\;\equiv\;\frac{2\pi}{u^{2}{\sf e}}\;\frac{\partial J(u,{\sf e})}{\partial{\sf e}}\;\equiv\;\frac{2\pi}{\omega(u,{\sf e})},

(25)

where
\omega(u,{\sf e})
denotes the angular frequency for the orbit.

III.1
Action Integrals

The action integral for a type I orbit
({\sf e}<1)
is calculated from the solution (
10
) as

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint y^{\prime}\,dy\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{T}y^{\prime 2}\;d\tau

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\nu^{3}}{\pi}\;m^{2}\int_{0}^{2\,{\sf K}}{\rm cn}^{2}\zeta\;{\rm sn}^{2}\zeta\;d\zeta,

where
u\,(1+{\sf e})=2\,\nu^{2}
and
m=2{\sf e}/(1+{\sf e})<1
. Using standard properties of the the Jacobi elliptic functions [see Eq. (
83
) in App. A], we find

J(u,{\sf e})\;=\;\frac{4\,\nu^{3}}{3\pi}\left[(2-m)\,{\sf E}(m)\;-\frac{}{}2\,(1-m)\,{\sf K}(m)\right].

(27)

As
{\sf e}\rightarrow 1

(m\rightarrow 1)
, we find
J(u,1)=4u^{\frac{3}{2}}/(3\pi)
, while the action
J(u,{\sf e})\sim(u/2)^{\frac{3}{2}}{\sf e}^{2}
vanishes as
{\sf e}\rightarrow 0
.

The action integral for a type II orbit (
{\sf e}>1
), on the other hand, is calculated from the solution (
16
) as

\displaystyle\overline{J}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint\overline{y}^{\prime}\,dy\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\overline{T}}\overline{y}^{\prime 2}\;d\tau

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\nu^{3}}{\pi}\;\sqrt{m}\int_{0}^{4\,\overline{\sf K}}{\rm sn}^{2}\xi\;{\rm dn}^{2}\xi\;d\xi.

Using standard properties of the the Jacobi elliptic functions [see Eq. (
85
) in App. A], we find

\overline{J}(u,{\sf e})\;=\;\frac{8\,\nu^{3}}{3\pi}\;\sqrt{m}\left[(2-m)\,\overline{\sf E}\;-\frac{}{}(1-m)\;\overline{\sf K}\right].

(29)

As
{\sf e}\rightarrow 1

(m\rightarrow 1)
, we find
\overline{J}(u,1)=8u^{\frac{3}{2}}/(3\pi)
, which is double the limit for
J(u,1)
since only one separatrix well is considered in the type I case (see Fig.
6
). On the other hand, as
{\sf e}\rightarrow\infty

(m\rightarrow 2)
, we find
\overline{J}(u,{\sf e})\rightarrow 4\,(u\,{\sf e})^{\frac{3}{2}}{\sf K}(\frac{1}{2})/(3\pi)
.

IV
Angle Coordinates and Canonical Condition

The canonical transformation from the canonical coordinates
(y,P_{y})
to the action-angle coordinates
(\theta,J)
satisfies the canonical condition

\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}\,\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial J}\;-\;\frac{\partial y}{\partial J}\,\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\theta}\;=\;1,

(32)

where
y(\tau;u,{\sf e})\equiv y[\theta(\tau;u,{\sf e}),J(u,{\sf e})]
and
P_{y}(\tau;u,{\sf e})\equiv\partial y(\tau;u,{\sf e})/\partial\tau=[\partial\theta(\tau;u,{\sf e})/\partial\tau]\,\partial y(\theta,J)/\partial\theta
. By definition, using the Hamiltonian
H=u^{2}{\sf e}^{2}/2
, the angle coordinate evolves according to the canonical Hamilton equation

\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tau}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial J}\;=\;u^{2}{\sf e}\;\frac{\partial\sf e}{\partial J}\;=\;\omega(J),

(33)

which was shown in Eq. (
25
) to be correct for both orbital types. Since the Hamiltonian is independent of the canonical angle
\theta
, we obtain
\partial J/\partial\tau=-\,\partial H/\partial\theta\equiv 0
.

We note that the canonical transformation from the canonical coordinates
(y,P_{y})
to the action-angle coordinates
(\theta,J)
can be inverted, yielding the canonical condition

\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial y}\,\frac{\partial J}{\partial P_{y}}\;-\;\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial P_{y}}\,\frac{\partial J}{\partial y}\;=\;1,

(34)

for the transformation from the action-angle coordinates
(\theta,J)
to the canonical coordinates
(y,P_{y})
.

IV.1
Angle coordinates

For type I orbits, we use the definition
\zeta(\theta,J)=\nu\tau\;=\;2\,{\sf K}(m)\,\theta/(2\pi)
, which yields the angle coordinate

\theta\;=\;\pi\zeta/{\sf K}(m)\;\equiv\;\omega(u,{\sf e})\,\tau,

(35)

with
\partial\theta/\partial\tau=\pi\nu/{\sf K}(m)\equiv\omega(u,{\sf e})
. Hence,

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}y(\theta,J)&=&1\;-\;2\nu\;{\rm dn}\,\zeta(\theta,J)\\
&&\\
P_{y}(\theta,J)&=&2u\,{\sf e}\,{\rm cn}\,\zeta(\theta,J)\;{\rm sn}\,\zeta(\theta,J)\end{array}\right\},

(36)

where the action dependence appears through
J(u,{\sf e})
.

For type II orbits, on the other hand, we use the definition
\xi(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})=\overline{\nu}\tau\;=\;4\,\overline{\sf K}\,\overline{\theta}/(2\pi)
, which yields the angle coordinate

\overline{\theta}\;=\;\pi\xi/(2\,\overline{\sf K})\;\equiv\;\overline{\omega}(u,{\sf e})\,\tau,

(37)

with
\partial\overline{\theta}/\partial\tau=\pi\overline{\nu}/(2\overline{\sf K})\equiv\overline{\omega}(u,{\sf e})
. Hence,

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}\overline{y}(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})&=&1\;-\;2\nu\;{\rm cn}\,\xi(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})\\
&&\\
\overline{P_{y}}(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})&=&2u\,{\sf e}\;\sqrt{\overline{m}}\,{\rm sn}\,\xi(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})\;{\rm dn}\,\xi(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})\end{array}\right\},

(38)

where the action dependence appears through
\overline{J}(u,{\sf e})
.

IV.2
Canonical condition

IV.2.1
Type I orbit

We now verify the canonical condition (
32
) for orbit type I. First, we note that the derivatives with respect to the action coordinate must be calculated more carefully according to the expression

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\right|_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\partial\sf e}{\partial J}\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\sf e}\right|_{\theta}\;+\;\frac{1}{\Omega_{\sf e}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega(u,{\sf e})}{u^{2}\,{\sf e}}\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\sf e}\right|_{\theta}\;+\;\frac{1}{\Omega_{\sf e}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\right),

where
\Omega_{\sf e}^{-1}\equiv\partial\tau/\partial{\sf e}|_{\theta}
, with

d\theta\;=\;(\pi\nu/{\sf K})\,d\tau\;+\;\pi\tau\,d{\sf e}\;\partial(\nu/{\sf K})/\partial{\sf e}\;=\;0,

so that we find

\frac{1}{\Omega_{\sf e}}\;=\;-\;\frac{\tau\,{\sf K}}{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sf e}\left(\frac{\nu}{\sf K}\right)\;=\;\frac{\tau}{2{\sf e}\,(1-{\sf e})}\left(\frac{\sf E}{\sf K}-(1-{\sf e})\right).

Second, the angular derivatives can be evaluated as
\partial/\partial\theta=\omega^{-1}\,\partial/\partial\tau
, so that Eq. (
32
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}\,\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial J}-\frac{\partial y}{\partial J}\,\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega^{-1}\frac{\partial y}{\partial\tau}\frac{\omega}{u^{2}{\sf e}}\left(\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\sf e}+\frac{1}{\Omega_{\sf e}}\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\tau}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle-\frac{\omega}{u^{2}{\sf e}}\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial\sf e}+\frac{1}{\Omega_{\sf e}}\frac{\partial y}{\partial\tau}\right)\omega^{-1}\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial\tau}\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial E}-\frac{\partial y}{\partial E}\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\tau},

where
\partial/\partial E=(u^{2}{\sf e})^{-1}\partial/\partial{\sf e}
. Here, using Eq. (
36
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial\tau}\;\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\,{\rm sn}^{2}\zeta\;{\rm cn}^{2}\zeta+m\,(2-m)\;\frac{\partial}{\partial m}\left({\rm sn}^{2}\zeta\frac{}{}{\rm cn}^{2}\zeta\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial E}\;\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\rm cn}^{2}\zeta\;-\frac{}{}{\rm sn}^{2}\zeta\right){\rm dn}^{2}\zeta

\displaystyle+(2-m)\left({\rm cn}^{2}\zeta\;-\frac{}{}{\rm sn}^{2}\zeta\right)\;\frac{\partial\,{\rm dn}^{2}\zeta}{\partial m},

where we used
{\sf e}=m/(2-m)
and the
m
-derivatives of
{\rm pq}\,\zeta\equiv{\rm pq}(\zeta|m)={\rm pq}({\sf K}(m)\theta/\pi|m)
are evaluated at constant
\theta
. Next, using the identities
{\rm cn}^{2}\zeta+{\rm sn}^{2}\zeta=1
and

{\rm dn}^{2}\zeta+m\,{\rm sn}^{2}\zeta=1
, the canonical condition (
40
) becomes

\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}\,\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial J}-\frac{\partial y}{\partial J}\,\frac{\partial P_{y}}{\partial\theta}\;=\;1,

(41)

where the final result is obtained by cancellations without evaluating the
m
-derivatives.

IV.2.2
Type II orbit

Next, we verify the canonical condition (
32
) for orbit type II. Here, we replace Eq. (
39
) with the expression

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{J}}\right|_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\partial\sf e}{\partial\overline{J}}\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\sf e}\right|_{\overline{\theta}}\;+\;\frac{1}{\overline{\Omega}_{\sf e}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\right)

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\omega}(u,{\sf e})}{u^{2}\,{\sf e}}\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\sf e}\right|_{\overline{\theta}}\;+\;\frac{1}{\overline{\Omega}_{\sf e}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\right),

where
\overline{\Omega}_{\sf e}^{-1}\equiv\partial\tau/\partial{\sf e}|_{\overline{\theta}}
, with

d\overline{\theta}\;=\;(\pi\overline{\nu}/2\overline{\sf K})\,d\tau\;+\;(\pi\tau/2)\,d{\sf e}\;\partial(\overline{\nu}/\overline{\sf K})/\partial{\sf e}\;=\;0,

so that we find

\frac{1}{\overline{\Omega}_{\sf e}}\;=\;-\;\frac{\tau\,\overline{\sf K}}{\overline{\nu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sf e}\left(\frac{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\sf K}}\right)\;=\;\frac{\tau}{2(1-{\sf e}^{2})}\left(\frac{\overline{\sf E}}{\overline{\sf K}}-(1-{\sf e})\right).

Second, the angular derivatives can be evaluated as
\partial/\partial\overline{\theta}=\overline{\omega}^{-1}\,\partial/\partial\tau
, so that Eq. (
32
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\overline{\theta}}\,\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\overline{J}}-\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\overline{J}}\,\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\overline{\theta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}^{-1}\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\tau}\frac{\overline{\omega}}{u^{2}{\sf e}}\left(\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\sf e}+\frac{1}{\overline{\Omega}_{\sf e}}\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\tau}\right)

(43)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\overline{\omega}}{u^{2}{\sf e}}\left(\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\sf e}+\frac{1}{\overline{\Omega}_{\sf e}}\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\tau}\right)\;\overline{\omega}^{-1}\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\tau}\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial E}-\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial E}\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\tau}.

Here, using Eq. (
36
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\tau}\;\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\overline{m}+1)\,\overline{\rm sn}^{2}\xi\,\overline{\rm dn}^{2}\xi

\displaystyle-\;\overline{m}\,(2\overline{m}-1)\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{m}}\left(\overline{\rm sn}^{2}\xi\frac{}{}\overline{\rm dn}^{2}\xi\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial E}\;\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\rm cn}^{2}\xi\;\left(\overline{\rm dn}^{2}\xi\;-\frac{}{}\overline{m}\;\overline{\rm sn}^{2}\xi\right)

\displaystyle+\;\overline{m}\,(2\overline{m}-1)\;\left(\overline{\rm dn}^{2}\xi\;-\frac{}{}\overline{m}\;\overline{\rm sn}^{2}\xi\right)\;\frac{\partial\,\overline{\rm cn}^{2}\xi}{\partial\overline{m}},

where we used
{\sf e}=1/(2\overline{m}-1)
and the
\overline{m}
-derivatives of
\overline{\rm pq}\,\xi\equiv{\rm pq}(\xi|\overline{m})={\rm pq}(2{\sf K}(\overline{m})\overline{\theta}/\pi|\overline{m})
are evaluated at constant
\overline{\theta}
. Next, using the identities
\overline{\rm cn}^{2}\xi+\overline{\rm sn}^{2}\xi=1
and
\overline{\rm dn}^{2}\xi+\overline{m}\,\overline{\rm sn}^{2}\xi=1
, the canonical condition (
43
) becomes

\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\overline{\theta}}\,\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\overline{J}}-\frac{\partial\overline{y}}{\partial\overline{J}}\,\frac{\partial\overline{P}_{y}}{\partial\overline{\theta}}\;=\;1,

(44)

where the final result is obtained by cancellations without evaluating the
\overline{m}
-derivatives.

V
Canonical Transformation

The canonical condition (
32
) proves the existence of a generating function
S
for the canonical transformation
Goldstein_2002

(y,P_{y})\rightarrow(\theta,J)
. Here, the periodic function
S
is defined from the expression
P_{y}\;dy=J\;d\theta+dS
, which leads to two complementary equations

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}J\;+\;\partial S/\partial\theta&=&P_{y}\;\partial y/\partial\theta\\
&&\\
\partial S/\partial J&=&P_{y}\;\partial y/\partial J\end{array}\right\},

(45)

which are consistent with the canonical condition (
32
). We note that the expression
P_{y}\,dy-dS=J\,d\theta
is also consistent with the inverse canonical condition (
34
).

In Eq. (
45
), the partial derivative
\partial/\partial J
is understood in the sense of Eq. (
39
). The first equation in Eq. (
45
) yields the action-integral definition

J\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\;\int_{0}^{2\pi}P_{y}\,\frac{\partial y}{\partial\theta}\;d\theta\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{T}\left(y^{\prime}\right)^{2}d\tau.

This equation also yields a defining expression for the generating function

\displaystyle S(\theta,J)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta/\omega}P_{y}\,\frac{\partial y}{\partial\tau}\;d\tau\;-\;J\,\theta

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta/\omega}\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial\tau}\right)^{2}\;d\tau\;-\;J\,\theta,

where
S(0,J)\equiv 0
and we will show that
S(\theta,J)
is explicitly dependent on the angle coordinate
\theta
, i.e., the orbit average
\langle S\rangle=0
vanishes for both orbit types.

V.1
Type I Orbit

For an orbit of type I, we use the orbit velocity (
11
) and Eq. (
46
) yields

S(\theta,J)=4\nu^{3}\,m^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}{\rm sn}^{2}(u|m)\;{\rm cn}^{2}(u|m)\,du\;-\;J\,\theta.

(47)

Next, using the Jacobi zeta function (
87
), the integral can be evaluated as

\displaystyle 4\nu^{3}\,m^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}{\rm sn}^{2}(u|m)\;{\rm cn}^{2}(u|m)\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\,\nu^{3}\left[(2-m)\,{\mathcal{Z}}(\zeta,m)\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}{\rm dn}^{2}(\zeta|m)\right]\;+\;J\,\theta,

and the canonical generating function for orbit type I is

\displaystyle S(\theta,J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\,\nu^{3}\left[(2-m)\,{\mathcal{Z}}(\zeta,m)\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}{\rm dn}^{2}(\zeta|m)\right]

(48)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\,u\,(1+{\sf e})\,\omega(u,{\sf e})\;\frac{\partial R(\theta,J)}{\partial\theta},

where the periodic function

\displaystyle R(\theta,J)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(2-m)\,\ln\vartheta_{4}(\theta/2,{\sf q}(m))

(49)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{1}{2}\,(1-m)^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\frac{\vartheta_{3}^{2}(\theta/2,{\sf q}(m))}{\vartheta_{4}^{2}(\theta/2,{\sf q}(m))}

is expressed in terms of the theta functions
Lawden_1989
;
NIST_Jacobi

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vartheta_{3}(u,{\sf q})&=&1+2\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\sf q}^{n^{2}}\,\cos(2nu)\\
&&\\
\vartheta_{4}(u,{\sf q})&=&1+2\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}{\sf q}^{n^{2}}\,\cos(2nu)\end{array}\right\},

(50)

which are defined in terms of the nome

{\sf q}(m)\;=\;\exp[-\pi{\sf K}(1-m)/{\sf K}(m)].

We note that Eq. (
48
) automatically yields the vanishing orbit average
\langle S\rangle=0
.

V.2
Type II Orbit

For an orbit of type II, we use the orbit velocity (
17
) and Eq. (
46
) yields

\overline{S}(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})=4\nu^{3}\,\sqrt{m}\int_{0}^{\xi}{\rm sn}^{2}(u|\overline{m})\;{\rm dn}^{2}(u|\overline{m})\,du-\overline{J}\,\overline{\theta}.

(51)

Next, using the Jacobi zeta function (
88
), the integral can be evaluated as

\displaystyle 4\nu^{3}\,\sqrt{m}\int_{0}^{\xi}{\rm sn}^{2}(u|\overline{m})\;{\rm dn}^{2}(u|\overline{m})\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\,\nu^{3}\,\sqrt{m}\left[(2-m)\,{\mathcal{Z}}(\xi,\overline{m})\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}{\rm cn}^{2}(\xi|\overline{m})\right]\;+\;\overline{J}\,\overline{\theta},

and the canonical generating function for orbit type II is

\displaystyle\overline{S}(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\,\nu^{3}\,\sqrt{m}\left[(2-m)\,{\mathcal{Z}}(\xi,\overline{m})\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}{\rm cn}^{2}(\xi|\overline{m})\right]

(52)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\,u\,(1+{\sf e})\,\overline{\omega}(u,{\sf e})\;\frac{\partial\overline{R}(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})}{\partial\overline{\theta}},

where the periodic function

\displaystyle\overline{R}(\overline{\theta},\overline{J})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(2-m)\,\ln\vartheta_{4}(\overline{\theta},{\sf q}(\overline{m}))

(53)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{1}{2}(m-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\frac{\vartheta_{2}^{2}(\overline{\theta},{\sf q}(\overline{m}))}{\vartheta_{4}^{2}(\overline{\theta},{\sf q}(\overline{m}))}

is expressed in terms of the theta function
\vartheta_{4}(\overline{\theta},{\sf q}(\overline{m}))
and the theta function

\vartheta_{2}(\overline{\theta},\overline{\sf q})\;=\;2\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\sf q}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\,\cos[(2n+1)\,\overline{\theta}],

(54)

which are defined in terms of the nome

\overline{\sf q}\;=\;{\sf q}(\overline{m})\;=\;\exp[-\pi{\sf K}(1-\overline{m})/{\sf K}(\overline{m})].

Once again, we note that Eq. (
52
) automatically yields the vanishing orbit average
\langle S\rangle=0
.

VI
Motion in the presence of an electric field

In this Section, we consider the impact of a constant electric field on the dynamics of a charged particle in a straight magnetic field with constant perpendicular gradient. The case of a constant electric field
{\bf E}=E_{0}\,\widehat{\sf x}
in the ignorable

x
-direction has been studied in Ref.
Parks_2003
and will not be considered here.

Instead, we consider the constant electric field
{\bf E}=E_{0}\,\widehat{\sf y}
, so that the equations of motion (
1
) become

\left.\begin{array}[]{rc l}\ddot{x}&=&\Omega_{0}\,(1-y/L)\,\dot{y}\\
\ddot{y}&=&qE_{0}/M\;-\;\Omega_{0}\,(1-y/L)\,\dot{x}\end{array}\right\}.

(55)

These equations still conserve the canonical momentum (
2
) while the energy conservation law

E\;=\;M(\dot{x}^{2}+\dot{y}^{2})/2-qE_{0}\,(y-1)

is now expressed in terms of an electrostatic potential that is assumed to vanish at
y=1
. The normalized equation of motion (
4
) is now replaced with

y^{\prime\prime}\;=\;w\;-\;(1-y)\,x^{\prime}\;=\;w\;-\;(1-y)\,u\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,(1-y)^{3},

(56)

where
w\equiv qE_{0}/(M\Omega_{0}^{2}L)
is the normalized
E\times B
velocity.

VI.1
Asymmetric quartic potential

We now set
s=1-y
, so that Eq. (
56
) becomes

s^{\prime\prime}\;=\;-\,w\;+\;u\,s\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,s^{3}\;\equiv\;-\,V^{\prime}(s),

(57)

where the asymmetric quartic potential

\displaystyle V(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w\,s\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\left(u-\frac{1}{2}\,s^{2}\right)^{2}

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,u^{2}\;+\;w\,s\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,u\,s^{2}\;+\;\frac{1}{8}\,s^{4}

has extrema points
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
at

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}s_{1}&=&2\,\sigma\;\cos(\pi/6+\psi/3)\\
s_{2}&=&2\,\sigma\;\sin(\psi/3)\\
s_{3}&=&-\,2\,\sigma\;\cos(\pi/6-\psi/3)\end{array}\right\},

(59)

where
\sigma^{2}=2\,u/3
and
\psi=\sin^{-1}(w/\sigma^{3})
. These points are real when
-\sigma^{3}\leq w\leq\sigma^{3}
, so that the quartic potential (
58
) has two minima
(s_{1},s_{3})
and a local maximum
(s_{2})
, where
s_{3}<s_{2}<s_{1}
. As shown in Fig.
7
(where
-\sigma^{3}<w<0
), a particle can now be trapped either in a shallow well (left) or a deep well (right) if the energy level falls below the barrier height
V_{2}=V(s_{2})
.

Figure 7:
Plot of the asymmetric quartic potential (
58
) in the range
-2\leq s\leq 2
for
u=1/2
and
w=-0.08
(solid). The symmetric quartic potential, with
w=0
, is shown as a dashed curve.

The problem of a particle moving in the asymmetric quartic potential (
58
) was solved exactly in terms of Jacobi and Weierstrass elliptic functions in Ref.
Brizard_Westland_2017
. There, it was shown that a particle trapped in the shallow well at a given energy level has exactly the same orbital period as a particle trapped in the deep well at the same energy level. While it should be possible to construct action-angle coordinates for the trapped motion in the shallow well and the deep well separately, as well as for the trapped motion above the potential barrier, we will not pursue this avenue in the present paper.

VI.2
Electric field perturbation

In previous work, Reichl and Zheng
Reichl_1984
considered a symmetric double-well potential perturbed by a small time-dependent (monochromatic) electric field represented in dimensionless form as
w=\epsilon\,\cos(\Omega\,\tau)
, which allows a fraction of particles trapped in one well to migrate over the top of the potential barrier. The analysis of this perturbed Duffing equation can also be found in Ref.
Nayfeh_1973
.

In this Section, we consider the case of a weak time-independent electric field
w=\epsilon\,\sigma^{3}
, with
\epsilon\ll 1
, so that the asymmetry in the quartic potential is weak. This perturbed dynamical problem can be represented as a Hamiltonian perturbation problem, where, using the action-angle coordinates
(J,\theta)
, the Hamiltonian is expressed as

H(J,\theta)\;=\;H_{0}(J)\;+\;\epsilon\,\sigma^{3}\,s(J,\theta),

(60)

where
H_{0}(J)=\frac{1}{2}\,u^{2}\,{\sf e}^{2}(J)
and
s(J,\theta)
describes a solution of the unperturbed (symmetric) quartic potential problem. Because of the electric-field perturbation, the unperturbed action coordinate
J
is no longer an invariant

\frac{\partial J}{\partial\tau}\;=\;-\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta}(J,\theta)\;=\;-\;\epsilon\,\sigma^{3}\,\frac{\partial s}{\partial\theta}(J,\theta),

(61)

while the angle coordinate evolves according the Hamilton equation

\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\tau}\;=\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial J}(J,\theta)\;=\;\omega_{0}(J)\;+\;\epsilon\,\sigma^{3}\,\frac{\partial s}{\partial J}(J,\theta),

(62)

where
\omega_{0}(J)=u^{2}{\sf e}\,\partial{\sf e}/\partial J
is obtained from Eq. (
25
).

According to standard perturbation methods
Nayfeh_1973
, we now seek a canonical transformation
(J,\theta)\rightarrow({\mathcal{J}},\Theta)
, where the new action-angle coordinates
({\mathcal{J}},\Theta)
are constructed by Lie-transform methods (see App. C of Ref.
Brizard_2015
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J\;+\;\epsilon\,\{S_{1},J\}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\{S_{2},J\}

(63)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}\{S_{1},J\}\right\}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\;+\;\epsilon\,\{S_{1},\theta\}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\{S_{2},\theta\}

(64)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}\{S_{1},\theta\}\right\}+\cdots.

Here, the generating functions
S_{1}(J,\theta)
and
S_{2}(J,\theta)
are used to remove the angular dependence from the new Hamiltonian up to second order

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(J)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle H(J,\theta)\;-\;\epsilon\,\{S_{1},H\}\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\,\{S_{2},H\}

(65)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\;\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}\{S_{1},H\}\right\}\;+\;\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(J)\;+\;\epsilon\,{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(J)\;+\;\epsilon^{2}{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(J)+\cdots,

and the action-angle Poisson bracket
\{\;,\;\}
is defined as

\{f,\;g\}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\;\frac{\partial g}{\partial J}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial J}\;\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\left(g\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(g\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial J}\right).

By definition, the zeroth-order Hamiltonian is
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(J)\equiv H_{0}(J)
, and the lowest-order (unperturbed) angular velocity is
\omega_{0}(J)\equiv{\mathcal{H}}_{0}^{\prime}(J)
.

From Eq. (
65
), the new first-order Hamiltonian is defined by the relation

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(J)\;=\;\sigma^{3}\,s(J,\theta)\;-\;\omega_{0}(J)\;\frac{\partial S_{1}}{\partial\theta},

(66)

and, since the left side of Eq. (
66
) is independent of the angle
\theta
, we readily find the solution for the new first-order Hamiltonian

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(J)\;\equiv\;\sigma^{3}\,\langle s\rangle\;=\;\frac{\sigma^{3}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}s(J,\theta)\,d\theta,

(67)

while the first-order generating function is defined as the non-secular solution
S_{1}(J,\theta)
obtained from the angle-dependent part of Eq. (
66
):

\frac{\partial S_{1}}{\partial\theta}\;=\;\frac{\sigma^{3}}{\omega_{0}}\left(s\;-\frac{}{}\langle s\rangle\right)\;\equiv\;\frac{\sigma^{3}}{\omega_{0}}\,\widetilde{s}.

(68)

At second order, the new Hamiltonian is defined from the expression

{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(J)=-\;\omega_{0}\;\frac{\partial S_{2}}{\partial\theta}-\omega_{1}\;\frac{\partial S_{1}}{\partial\theta}-\frac{\sigma^{3}}{2}\left\{S_{1},\;\widetilde{s}(J,\theta)\right\},

(69)

where
\omega_{1}(J)\equiv{\mathcal{H}}_{1}^{\prime}(J)
. Since the left side of Eq. (
69
) is
\theta
-independent, the new second-order Hamiltonian is then defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\sigma^{3}}{2}\left\langle\left\{S_{1},\frac{}{}s(J,\theta)\right\}\right\rangle\equiv-\;\frac{\sigma^{3}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\left\langle\widetilde{s}\;\frac{\partial S_{1}}{\partial\theta}\right\rangle

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\sigma^{6}}{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\left[\frac{1}{\omega_{0}}\left(\langle s^{2}\rangle\;-\frac{}{}\langle s\rangle^{2}\right)\right].

The second-order generating function is defined as the non-secular solution
S_{2}(J,\theta)
obtained from the angle-dependent part of Eq. (
69
).

Once the new Hamiltonian (
65
) is constructed up to any desired order in
\epsilon
(say
\epsilon^{n}
), the new action
{\mathcal{J}}
is conserved up the
n
th-order, i.e.,
d{\mathcal{J}}/d\tau={\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{n+1})
, and the angular motion
d\Theta/d\tau={\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{J}})\equiv\omega({\mathcal{J}})
is exactly solved as
\Theta(\tau,{\mathcal{J}})=\omega({\mathcal{J}})\,\tau
.

VI.2.1
Type I orbit

We begin our perturbation analysis of the particle motion in an asymmetric quartic potential with type-I particle orbit, where
s(J,\theta)=2\nu\,{\rm dn}(\theta\,{\sf K}/\pi|m)
. Using the definition for the quasi-periodic Jacobi amplitude function
NIST_Jacobi

\int_{0}^{\zeta}{\rm dn}(u|m)\,du\;=\;\phi(\zeta|m)\;\equiv\;\sin^{-1}\left({\rm sn}(\zeta|m)\right),

with
\phi(\zeta+2{\sf K}|m)=\phi(\zeta|m)+\pi
, we find the orbit average

\langle s\rangle\;=\;\frac{\nu}{\sf K}\int_{0}^{2{\sf K}}{\rm dn}\,\zeta\;d\zeta\;=\;\frac{\pi\nu}{\sf K}\;\equiv\;\omega_{0}(J),

so that the new first-order Hamiltonian (
67
) becomes

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(J)\;=\;\sigma^{3}\,\omega_{0}(J).

(71)

The generating function
S_{1}
can be obtained from Eq. (
66
) by direct integration

\displaystyle S_{1}(J,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\nu\,\sigma^{3}}{\omega_{0}}\left(\int{\rm dn}(\theta\,{\sf K}/\pi|m)\,d\theta\;-\;\frac{\pi\theta}{2{\sf K}}\right)

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{3}\left[2\,\phi(\theta\,{\sf K}/\pi|m)\;-\frac{}{}\theta\right],

which can be used in the first-order canonical transformation in Eqs. (
63
)-(
64
).

Next, we calculate the new second-order Hamiltonian from Eq. (
70
), where
\langle s\rangle=\omega_{0}
and

\langle s^{2}\rangle\;=\;\frac{2\nu^{2}}{\sf K}\int_{0}^{2{\sf K}}{\rm dn}^{2}\zeta\,d\zeta\;=\;4\nu^{2}\,{\sf E}/{\sf K}=(4\nu/\pi)\,\omega_{0}{\sf E},

so that Eq. (
70
) yields

{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(J)\;=\;-\,\frac{2\,\sigma^{6}}{\pi}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\left(\nu\,{\sf E}\;-\;\frac{\pi}{4}\;\omega_{0}\right),

(73)

which yields the new Hamiltonian (
65
) up to second order.

VI.2.2
Type II orbit

Next, we consider the perturbation analysis of the particle motion in an asymmetric quartic potential with type-II particle orbits, where
s(\overline{J},\overline{\theta})=2\nu\,{\rm cn}(2\overline{\theta}\,\overline{\sf K}/\pi|\overline{m})
. Using the integral

\int_{0}^{\xi}{\rm cn}(u|\overline{m})\,du\;=\;\sqrt{m}\;\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\overline{m}}\frac{}{}{\rm sd}(\xi|\overline{m})\right),

we find
\langle s\rangle=0
and the new first-order Hamiltonian is
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(\overline{J})=0
. The first-order generating function is thus defined by the relation

\frac{\partial S_{1}}{\partial\overline{\theta}}\;=\;\frac{2\nu\sigma^{3}}{\overline{\omega}_{0}}\;{\rm cn}(2\overline{\theta}\,\overline{\sf K}/\pi|\overline{m}),

(74)

which yields the solution

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\nu\sigma^{3}}{\overline{\omega}_{0}}\int_{0}^{\overline{\theta}}{\rm cn}(2\theta\,\overline{\sf K}/\pi|\overline{m})\;d\theta=\frac{\pi\nu\sigma^{3}}{\overline{\sf K}\overline{\omega}_{0}}\int_{0}^{\xi}{\rm cn}(u|\overline{m})\,du

(75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\sigma^{3}\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\overline{m}}\frac{}{}{\rm sd}(\xi|\overline{m})\right),

which can be used in the first-order canonical transformation in Eqs. (
63
)-(
64
).

Next, we calculate the new second-order Hamiltonian from Eq. (
70
), where

\langle s^{2}\rangle\;=\;\frac{\nu^{2}}{\overline{\sf K}}\int_{0}^{4\overline{\sf K}}{\rm cn}^{2}\xi\,d\xi\;=\;\frac{4m\nu^{2}}{\overline{\sf K}}\,\left[\overline{\sf E}\;-\frac{}{}(1-\overline{m})\,\overline{\sf K}\right],

so that Eq. (
70
) yields

{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(\overline{J})\;=\;-\,\frac{4\,\sigma^{6}}{\pi}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{J}}\left[\overline{\nu}\,\left(\overline{\sf E}\;-\frac{}{}(1-\overline{m})\,\overline{\sf K}\right)\right],

(76)

which yields the new Hamiltonian (
65
) up to second order.

VII
Summary

In this paper, we presented in Sec.
II
all orbital solutions for the equations of motion (
1
) describing the motion of a charged particle in a straight magnetic field with constant gradient. In addition, we derived expressions for the action-angle coordinates for each orbit type in Secs.
III
-
IV
and obtained an explicit expression for the generating function for each canonical transformation in Sec.
V
. This work follows in the steps of previous work on the trapped and passing particle orbits in simple tokamak geometry
Brizard_Duthoit_2014
, based on the canonical transformation for the pendulum problem
Brizard_2013
.

Next, we considered the case of motion in an asymmetric quartic potential due to the presence of a constant electric field in Sec.
VI
, which is solved perturbatively for the case of a weak electric field. In future work, we will construct the canonical transformation to action-angle coordinates based on the exact solution in terms of elliptic functions presented in Ref.
Brizard_Westland_2017
.

Appendix A
Integrals of Jacobi Elliptic Functions

In this Appendix, we present a brief review of the Jacobi epsilon and zeta functions
Lawden_1989
;
NIST_Jacobi
.

A.1
Jacobi Epsilon Function

In calculating action integrals in Sec.
III
, we need to compute integrals of even powers of
{\rm sn}(u|m)
:

I_{2n}(z|m)\;\equiv\;\int_{0}^{z}{\rm sn}^{2n}(u|m)\,du

for
n\geq 0
. First,
I_{0}(z|m)=z
and

I_{2}(z|m)\;=\;\int_{0}^{z}{\rm sn}^{2}(u|m)du\;=\;m^{-1}\left[z\;-\;{\mathcal{E}}(z|m)\right],

(79)

where
{\mathcal{E}}(z|m)
is the Jacobi epsilon function, which is quasi-periodic:
{\mathcal{E}}(z+2{\sf K}|m)={\mathcal{E}}(z|m)+2\,{\sf E}(m)
. Next, we use the recurrence relation
(n\geq 1)

Lawden_1989

\displaystyle m\,(1+2n)\,I_{2n+2}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+m)\,2n\;I_{2n}(z)

(80)

\displaystyle-\;(2n-1)\,I_{2n-2}(z)

\displaystyle+\;{\rm sn}^{2n-1}(z)\,{\rm cn}(z)\,{\rm dn}(z),

which, for
n=1
, yields

\displaystyle 3\,m\,I_{4}(z|m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1+m)\,I_{2}(z|m)\;-\;I_{0}(z|m)

\displaystyle+\;{\rm sn}(z|m)\,{\rm cn}(z|m)\,{\rm dn}(z|m),

or

\displaystyle m\int_{0}^{z}{\rm sn}^{4}(u|m)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\,\left({\rm sn}(z|m)\,{\rm cn}(z|m)\,{\rm dn}(z|m)\;-\frac{}{}z\right)

(81)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{2}{3m}(1+m)\left(z\;-\frac{}{}{\mathcal{E}}(z|m)\right).

First, using the identity
{\rm cn}^{2}(z|m)=1-{\rm sn}^{2}(z|m)
, we find

\displaystyle m^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}{\rm sn}^{2}u\;{\rm cn}^{2}u\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\left({\rm sn}^{2}u\;-\frac{}{}{\rm sn}^{4}u\right)du

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}\left[I_{2}(\zeta|m)\;-\frac{}{}I_{4}(\zeta|m)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\left(\zeta\;-\frac{}{}{\mathcal{E}}(\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{m}{3}\,\left({\rm sn}\,\zeta\;{\rm cn}\,\zeta\;{\rm dn}\,\zeta\;-\frac{}{}\zeta\right)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{2}{3}\,(1+m)\left(\zeta\;-\frac{}{}{\mathcal{E}}(\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\left[(2-m)\,{\mathcal{E}}(\zeta)\;-\frac{}{}2\,(1-m)\,\zeta\right]

\displaystyle-\;\frac{m}{3}\;{\rm sn}\,\zeta\;{\rm cn}\,\zeta\;{\rm dn}\,\zeta.

When evaluated at
\zeta=2\,{\sf K}(m)
, this expression yields

\displaystyle m^{2}\int_{0}^{2{\sf K}}{\rm sn}^{2}u\,{\rm cn}^{2}u\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}(2-m)\,{\sf E}(m)

(83)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{4}{3}\,(1-m)\,{\sf K}(m),

where we used
{\mathcal{E}}(2{\sf K})=2\,{\sf E}
.

Second, using the identity
{\rm dn}^{2}(\xi|\overline{m})=1-\overline{m}\,{\rm sn}^{2}(\xi|\overline{m})
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\xi}\overline{\rm sn}^{2}u\;\overline{\rm dn}^{2}u\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\xi}\left(\overline{\rm sn}^{2}u\;-\;\overline{m}\int_{0}^{\xi}\overline{\rm sn}^{4}u\right)du

(84)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{2}(\xi|\overline{m})\;-\;\overline{m}\;I_{4}(\xi|\overline{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\left(\xi\;-\frac{}{}\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}\left(\overline{\rm sn}\,\xi\;\overline{\rm cn}\,\xi\;\overline{\rm dn}\,\xi\;-\frac{}{}\xi\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}(1+m)\left(\xi\;-\frac{}{}\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\left[(2-m)\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\xi)\;-\frac{}{}(1-m)\,\xi\right]

\displaystyle-\;\frac{1}{3}\;\overline{\rm sn}\,\xi\;\overline{\rm cn}\,\xi\;\overline{\rm dn}\,\xi,

where
\overline{m}\equiv m^{-1}
. When evaluated at
\xi=4\,\overline{\sf K}
, this expression yields

\int_{0}^{4\overline{\sf K}}\overline{\rm sn}^{2}u\;\overline{\rm dn}^{2}u\,du\;=\;\frac{4}{3}\left[(2-m)\,\overline{\sf E}\;-\frac{}{}(1-m)\,\overline{\sf K}\right],

(85)

where we used
\overline{\mathcal{E}}(4\overline{\sf K})=4\,\overline{\sf E}
.

Quenched large deviations in renewal theory
Frank den Hollander Marco Zamparo

Abstract
In this paper we introduce and study renewal-reward processes in random environments where each renewal involves a reward taking values in a Banach space. We derive quenched large deviation principles and identify the associated rate functions in terms of variational formulas involving correctors. We illustrate the theory with three examples: compound Poisson processes in random environments, pinning of polymers at interfaces with disorder, and returns of Markov chains in dynamic random environments. MSC 2020 subject classifications. 60F10; 60K05; 60K10; 60K37; 82D60. Key words and phrases. Random environments, Renewal-reward processes, Quenched large deviations, Rate functions, Compound Poisson processes, Pinned polymers, Returns of Markov chains. Acknowledgment. The purpose of the present paper is to develop

Specifically, we assume that each renewal involves a reward taking a value in a real separable Banach space, and we characterise the quenched fluctuations of the total reward over time, i.e., fluctuations conditional on a typical realisation of the environment. In the absence of disorder, the total reward over time defines a so-called
renewal-reward process

[
29

[
53

55

The problem we address fits naturally into the context of large deviations for random walks in random environments and random potentials, but it differs from the mainstream literature in two respects. First, the renewal processes we consider allow for renewal times with possibly unbounded and heavy-tailed increments, in contrast to the random walks in random environments and random potentials with bounded increments that are typically considered in the literature. Second, motivated by queuing theory
[
1
]

[
41

[
52

[
16

24

[
42

In order to identify rate functions, we consider a class of models where the rate function in the quenched LDP can be related via convex conjugation to a cumulant generating function, which itself is traced back to a quenched free energy. Since the latter in general is known only as a subadditive limit, we also provide a variational formula for the quenched free energy, which involves correctors (gradients of sorts) and is deduced as a variational solution for the growth rate of a renewal equation in a random environment. This variational formula applies, in particular, to the free energy of the random pinning model and appears to be new in that context. Similar variational formulas have been obtained previously for random walks in random environments and random potentials

1.2
Basic ingredients

Random environment.

The random environment, also called
disorder
, is sampled from a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
endowed with an ergodic measure-preserving transformation
f
. Expectation under
\mathbb{P}
is denoted by
\mathbb{E}
.

Renewals and rewards.

Put
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
, and
\overline{\mathbb{N}}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Let
p_{\omega}
be a probability mass function on
\overline{\mathbb{N}}
parametrised by
\omega\in\Omega
in such a way that
\omega\mapsto p_{\omega}(s)
is a measurable function for all
s
. In addition, let
(\mathcal{X},\|{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\|)
be a real separable Banach space equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and, for
s\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
be a probability measure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
parametrised by
\omega
in such a way that
\omega\mapsto\lambda_{\omega}(A|s)
is measurable for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
. We call
p_{\omega}
the
waiting-time distribution
and
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
the
reward probability measure
. In fact, for a given disorder
\omega
, we consider a sequence
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots
of
waiting times
taking values in
\overline{\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
of
rewards
taking values in
\mathcal{X}
whose joint law
P_{\omega}
satisfies

P_{\omega}\Big[S_{1}=s_{1},\ldots,S_{n}=s_{n},\,X_{1}\in A_{1},\ldots,X_{n}=A_{n}\Big]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})\,\lambda_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(A_{i}|s_{i})

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, and
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
, where
t_{0}:=0
and
t_{i}:=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. This law describes some phenomenon that occurs at the
renewal times

T_{i}:=S_{1}+\cdots+S_{i}
, with
T_{0}:=0
, while an initial environment
\omega
evolves over time by successive applications of the transformation
f
. The
i
th renewal involves the reward
X_{i}
, which can depend on
S_{i}
. Expectation under
P_{\omega}
is denoted by
E_{\omega}
.

The number of renewals up to time
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is the largest non-negative integer
N_{t}
such that
T_{N_{t}}\leq t
, and the
total reward
up to time
t
is

W_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}X_{i}

(empty sums are equal to zero). The process
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a
renewal-reward process in a random environment
, which turns out to be the classical renewal-reward process in the absence of disorder
[
29
]
. We emphasise that
W_{t}
is a random variable with respect to any probability space associated with the law of waiting times and rewards, because
\mathcal{X}
is separable
[
28
]
.

Pinning models.

Let
v_{\omega}
be a real function on
\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, which we call the
potential
, parametrised by
\omega\in\Omega
in such a way
that
\omega\mapsto v_{\omega}(s)
is measurable for all
s
. For
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define the
pinning model
as the law
P_{\omega,t}
determined by the Gibbs change of measure

\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\omega,t}}{\mathrm{d}P_{\omega}}:=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}}{E_{\omega}[\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}]},

where
H_{\omega,t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}v_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega}(S_{i})
is the
Hamiltonian
. This definition generalises the standard pinning model
[
19
,
25
]
, which will be recalled in Section
1.5
, and contains as the special case
v_{\omega}=0
the above renewal-reward system in a random environment.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}
the random set
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Together with the pinning model, we consider the
constrained pinning model
corresponding to the law
Q_{\omega,t}
obtained via the change of measure

\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{\omega,t}}{\mathrm{d}P_{\omega}}:=\frac{\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}}{E_{\omega}[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}]}.

The constrained pinning model turns out to be a useful tool to investigate the pinning model. We generically refer to
P_{\omega,t}
or
Q_{\omega,t}
as pinning models.

Conditional independence.

We aim to analyse the quenched large deviations of the renewal-reward process
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, taking advantage of the fact that a renewal process at every renewal starts afresh in the current environment. This fact is formalised by the identity

\displaystyle P_{\omega}

\displaystyle\Big[S_{1}=s_{1},\ldots,S_{n+n^{\prime}}=s_{n+n^{\prime}},\,X_{1}\in A_{1},\ldots,X_{n+n^{\prime}}\in A_{n+n^{\prime}}\,\Big|\,T_{n}=t\Big]

\displaystyle=P_{\omega}\Big[S_{1}=s_{1},\ldots,S_{n}=s_{n},\,X_{1}\in A_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in A_{n}\,\Big|\,T_{n}=t\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad P_{f^{t}\omega}\Big[S_{1}=s_{1},\ldots,S_{n^{\prime}}=s_{n^{\prime}},\,X_{1}\in A_{1},\ldots,X_{n^{\prime}}\in A_{n^{\prime}}\Big]

(1.1)

for
n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n+n^{\prime}}\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, and
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n+n^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
(with the convention
\frac{0}{0}:=0
).

Subadditive ergodic theorem.

To describe the asymptotic behaviour of probabilities, we use Kingman’s subadditive ergodic theorem
[
44
]
:

(\star)

Let
\{F_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of measurable functions on
\Omega
such that
\mathbb{E}[\max\{0,F_{1}\}]<+\infty
and
F_{t+t^{\prime}}\leq F_{t}+F_{t^{\prime}}\circ f^{t}
for all
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
\mathcal{L}:=\inf_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\{\mathbb{E}[\frac{F_{t}}{t}]\}<+\infty
. The following hold:

(i)

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\frac{F_{t}}{t}]=\mathcal{L}
.

(ii)

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{F_{t}(\omega)}{t}=\mathcal{L}

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
.

Note that the condition
\mathbb{E}[\max\{0,F_{1}\}]<+\infty
can be replaced by the condition
\mathbb{E}[\max\{0,F_{t}\}]<+\infty
for all
t\geq t_{o}
for some
t_{o}\in\mathbb{N}
. In that case
\mathcal{L}:=\inf_{t\geq t_{o}}\{\mathbb{E}[\frac{F_{t}}{t}]\}
.

1.3
Main results

We investigate quenched large deviations for the process
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
under the following assumptions about the waiting-time distribution
p_{\omega}
, the reward probability measures
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and the potential
v_{\omega}
(which are assumed to be in force throughout the paper). Denote by
B_{w,\delta}
the open ball of radius
\delta>0
centred at
w\in\mathcal{X}
. For
r\in\mathcal{X}
and
A\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, let
r+A
be the set of points

.

Assumption 1.1
.

There exist
m

\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(\sigma_{i})]>-\infty
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

\spadesuit

Assumption 1.2
.

There exist a finite-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and, for

, measurable functions
\omega\mapsto r_{\omega,s}
valued in
\mathcal{V}
and points

such that
\mathbb{E}[\log\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},s}+B_{x_{s},\delta}|s)]>-\infty
for all
\delta>0
and
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,\|r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},s}\|-\eta s\}]<+\infty
for some
\eta\geq 0
. Moreover, for each
s\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a compact set
K_{s}\subset\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\log\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},s}+K_{s}|s)]>-\infty
.

\spadesuit

Assumption 1.3
.

\mathbb{E}[\min\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)\}]>-\infty
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)-\eta s\}]<+\infty
for some real number
\eta\geq 0

\spadesuit

Our first main result concerns the large deviations of the total reward
W_{t}
with respect to the constrained pinning model
Q_{\omega,t}
for a typical disorder
\omega

\omega\mapsto\mu_{\omega,t}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})

\mu_{\omega,t}:=E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg].

(1.2)

The following result about the cumulant generating function associated with
\mu_{\omega,t}
comes before an LDP. Let
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
be the
topological dual of
\mathcal{X}
, and let
\mathcal{R}_{+}
be the set of positive random variables on
\Omega
that are almost surely finite. For
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}

\Upsilon_{\varphi}(\zeta):=\infd_{R\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}\mathbb{P}\,\mbox{-}\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}\bigg\{\log E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})-\zeta S_{1}+R(f^{S_{1}}\omega)-R(\omega)}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}<\infty\}}\Big]\bigg\}.

(1.3)

Finally, denote by
z
the function that maps
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
in
z(\varphi):=\inf\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\colon\,\Upsilon_{\varphi}(\zeta)\leq 0\}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

The following hold:

(i)
z
is proper convex, with
z(0)
finite, and is lower semi-continuous.

(ii)
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)}\mu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}w)=z(\varphi)

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
.

The variational definition of the function
z
, which in (
1.3
) involves the gradient
R\circ f^{S_{1}}-R
of auxiliary random variables

as a corrector, is our variational formula for the quenched free energy. The number
\zeta
is reminiscent of a renewal equation, and accommodates for a Cramér-Lundberg parameter to describe its growth rate
[
29
]
. We will comment further on this formula below. For now, we note that Proposition

suggests as a putative rate function the Legendre transform of

, which is the convex lower semi-continuous function
J
that maps

in

J(w):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\big\{\varphi(w)-z(\varphi)\big\}.

The following theorem states a quenched LDP for the family of measures
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the weak LDP with rate function

, i.e.,

(i)

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(G)\geq-\inf_{w\in G}J(w)
for all
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
open.

(ii)

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(K)\leq-\inf_{w\in K}J(w)
for all
K\subset\mathcal{X}
compact.

It is desirable (for example, in order to be able to apply Varadhan’s lemma

16
,
24

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family

holds for all closed sets, and not only for compact sets, and that

has compact level sets, i.e., the sets

Corollary 1.6
.

If
\mathcal{X}

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\bigg\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\bigg\}\bigg]<+\infty,

then
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the full LDP with good rate function

.

Remark 1.7
.

Since

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega

1.4
Discussion

Variational free energy.

Assumption
1.1
about the waiting-time distribution formulates a notion, suitable for the exponential scale of LDPs, of aperiodic probability mass function in a context with disorder. Assumption
1.3
on the potential is motivated by and applies to the polymer pinning model, discussed below in some detail, and its generalisations, such as the spatially extended pinning model
[
11
]
. We note that a homogeneous version, i.e., without disorder, of these assumptions is already present in
[
53
]
, where LDPs for the total reward are established within the framework of homogeneous pinning models. According to
(ii)
of Proposition
1.4
with
\varphi=0
, Assumptions
1.1
and
1.3
suffice to obtain the following limit, which shows that
z(0)
is the quenched free energy of the (constrained)
pinning model:

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(\mathcal{X})=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log E_{\omega}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\big]=z(0)\penalty\ \mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega.

We stress that the instance
\varphi=0
puts the reward probability measures
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
out of the picture. One of the services of Assumptions
1.3
is to ensure that
z(0)
is finite through the requirement
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)-\eta s\}]<+\infty
for some
\eta\geq 0
.

Our variational definition of the function
z
based on (
1.3
) turns out to be a variational formula for the quenched free energy:
z(0)=\inf\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\colon\,\Upsilon_{0}(\zeta)\leq 0\}
with

\Upsilon_{0}(\zeta):=\infd_{R\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}\mathbb{P}\,\mbox{-}\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}\bigg\{\log E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{v_{\omega}(S_{1})-\zeta S_{1}+R(f^{S_{1}}\omega)-R(\omega)}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}<\infty\}}\Big]\bigg\}.

It is interesting to note that the quantity
\Upsilon_{0}(\zeta)
can itself be interpreted as the quenched free energy of a random walk. In fact, supposing for a moment that the waiting-time distribution
p_{\omega}
has bounded support to make contact with the existing literature,
\Upsilon_{0}(\zeta)
is the quenched free energy for the random walk
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of renewal times in a random environment when
v_{\omega}=0
and
\zeta=0

[
40
,
45
,
37
]
, or the quenched free energy for the random walk
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
in the random potential
\omega\mapsto v_{\omega}-\zeta\mathrm{id}
when
p_{\omega}
is independent of
\omega

[
37
,
18
,
12
,
38
]
. These connections, together with the findings of
[
38
]
, suggest that the formula for
\Upsilon_{0}(\zeta)
does not always have a minimiser over
\mathcal{R}_{+}
. Recasting this expression in terms of centered cocycles could lead to a minimiser, as shown for a class of random walks in random potentials
[
18
,
12
,
38
]
. This issue, which we leave for future work, may open a new perspective in the study of the random pinning model.

Reward laws with disorder.

Assumption
1.2
on the distribution of rewards raises a new problem, namely, that of incorporating generic rewards into a renewal model with disorder. We are not aware of seeing this in similar or other contexts. The assumption is always verified by probability measures
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
that do not depend on the disorder
\omega
. In this case we can take
r_{\omega,s}=0
. In fact, for any probability measure
\lambda
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
there exists a point
x\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\lambda(B_{x,\delta})>0
for all
\delta>0
. On the contrary, if for every
y\in\mathcal{X}
it were possible to find a number
\delta_{y}>0
such that
\lambda(B_{y,\delta_{y}})=0
, then the open covering
\{B_{y,\delta_{y}}\}_{y\in\mathcal{X}}
of
\mathcal{X}
would contain a countable subcollection covering
\mathcal{X}
by Lindelöf’s lemma, with the consequence that
\lambda(\mathcal{X})=0
instead of
\lambda(\mathcal{X})=1
. Furthermore, there exists a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{X}
such that
\lambda(K)>0
, because
\lambda
is tight as
\mathcal{X}
is separable (see
[
10
]
, Theorem 7.1.7).

In the presence of disorder, what Assumption
1.2
requires is basically that the random probability measures
\omega\mapsto\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
satisfy the properties of homogeneous laws, in mean and on the exponential scale of LDPs, under possibly a shift by the vectors
r_{\omega,s}
. The points
r_{\omega,s}
are incorporated in the theory to account for a disorder-dependent deterministic component of rewards, which becomes dominant in the limiting case
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)=\delta_{r_{\omega,s}}
, with
\delta_{x}
the Dirac measure centered at
x
. This limiting case allows, for instance, to study the large deviations of the Hamiltonian
H_{\omega,t}
, which is the total reward
W_{t}
in a model where
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)=\delta_{v_{\omega}(s)}
. The vectors
r_{\omega,s}
are required not to vary too much, in particular, to lie in a common finite-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{V}
. We make use of this assumption, together with the hypothesis
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,\|r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},s}\|-\eta s\}]<+\infty
for some
\eta\geq 0
, to ensure that the disorder-dependent deterministic component
\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}r_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega,S_{i}}
of
the scaled total reward is in a compact set
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
. We stress that Assumption
1.2
simplifies when the reward space
\mathcal{X}
is finite-dimensional, as in this case
\mathcal{V}
can be taken equal to
\mathcal{X}
and the compact sets
K_{s}
can be taken equal to the closure of an open ball.

Difficulties and open problems.

Since Assumption
1.2
allows for any homogeneous reward law, our LDPs contain as a particular case the LDPs established in
[
53
]
under optimal hypotheses for homogeneous pinning models. Our variational definition of the function
z
generalises to a setting with disorder the variational definition given in
[
53
]
for the same function. We note that, in the homogeneous setting, the subadditivity properties of renewal models allow among others to extend the large deviation upper bounds from compact sets to open and closed convex sets under the same assumptions of the weak LDPs
[
53
]
. In the presence of disorder, our main assumptions and methods appear to be unable to reach the same result, because we exploit subadditivity properties after an approximation argument, as explained in Section
2.3
. We also note that, in the homogeneous setting, conditions for the full LDP to hold for infinite-dimensional rewards are known
[
55
]
. Such conditions come from large deviation theory of sums of i.i.d. random variables and are formulated in terms of exponential moments. We leave the investigation of these conditions to models with disorder as an open problem.

Examples

Compound Poisson processes in random environments.

Suppose that, in an economic scenario
\omega\in\Omega
, a customer can arrive in a shop with probability
\rho_{\omega}
, and can spend an amount of money smaller than or equal to
x\in\mathbb{R}
with probability
F_{\omega}(x)
. If the economic scenario evolves according to an ergodic measure-preserving transformation
f
, then the customer arrives at time
s\in\mathbb{N}
with probability

p_{\omega}(s):=\prod_{t=1}^{s-1}(1-\rho_{f^{t}\omega})\rho_{f^{s}\omega}

(empty products are equal to one), and spends an amount of money with probability distribution
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
over
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
defined, for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, by

\lambda_{\omega}((-\infty,x]|s):=F_{f^{s}\omega}(x).

Note that we are implicitly assuming that
F_{\omega}(x)
is non-decreasing and right-continuous with respect to
x
. We also assume that
\omega\mapsto\rho_{\omega}
and
\omega\mapsto F_{\omega}(x)
are measurable. In this way, when other customers arrive progressively after the first customer, all of them are described by the law
P_{\omega}
associated with the waiting time distribution
p_{\omega}
and the reward probability measures
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
over
\mathcal{X}:=\mathbb{R}
. The total reward
W_{t}
turns out to be the amount of money earned by the shop up to time
t
.

We are able to characterise the large deviations of
W_{t}
via our theory for a pinning model with potential
v_{\omega}
equal to zero. Assumption
1.1
is met if
\mathbb{E}[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(1)]>-\infty
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\log\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}]>-\infty
, while Assumption
1.3
holds trivially. Since the reward probability measures
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
depend on
s
and
\omega
only through
f^{s}\omega
, Assumption
1.2
is satisfied if there exists a measurable function
\omega\mapsto r_{\omega}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbb{E}[|r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}|]<+\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[\log\{F_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}+\delta)-F_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}-\delta)\}]>-\infty
for all
\delta>0
. The hypothesis of Corollary
1.6
becomes

\mathbb{E}[\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi|x|}\mathrm{d}F_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(x)]<+\infty
for some real number
\xi>0
. Thus, Theorem
1.8
and Corollary
1.9
give the following result after we show that the condition (
1.5
) is verified with
\ell=\mathbb{E}[\log(1-\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})]
.

Corollary 1.11
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}[\log\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}]>-\infty
and that there exists a real measurable function
\omega\mapsto r_{\omega}
such that
\mathbb{E}[|r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}|]<+\infty
and

\mathbb{E}[\log\{F_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}+\delta)-F_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}-\delta)\}]>-\infty
for all
\delta>0
. Then the following hold:

(i)

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, associated with the total profit, satisfies the weak LDP with rate function
I_{\ell}
, where
\ell:=\mathbb{E}[\log(1-\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})]\in[-\infty,0]
.

(ii)
If, moreover,
\mathbb{E}[\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi|x|}\mathrm{d}F_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(x)]<+\infty
for some number
\xi>0
, then
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the full LDP with
good rate function
I_{\ell}
.

Proof of Corollary
1.11
.

It remains to verify that the condition (
1.5
) holds with
\ell:=\mathbb{E}[\log(1-\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})]
. The waiting-time tail probability is now, for
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

P_{\omega}[S_{1}>s]=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1-\rho_{f^{i}\omega}).

First we prove that (
1.5
) is fulfilled with the above
\ell
when
\mathbb{E}[\log(1-\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})]>-\infty
. To this aim, we note that Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem allows us to find a set
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that, for
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
,
\lim_{s\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\log P_{\omega}[S_{1}>s]=\ell
. Thus, for a given
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and for each
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
k
such that the bounds
-k+(\ell-\epsilon/2)s\leq\log P_{\omega}[S_{1}>s]\leq k+(\ell+\epsilon/2)s
are valid for all
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. These bounds imply

\displaystyle\log P_{f^{t}\omega}[S_{1}>s]

\displaystyle=\log P_{\omega}[S_{1}>t+s]-\log P_{\omega}[S_{1}>t]

\displaystyle\leq 2k+(\ell+\epsilon/2)(t+s)-(\ell-\epsilon/2)t\leq 2k+(\ell+\epsilon)s+\epsilon t,

which shows that the first line of (
1.5
) holds. A similar argument proves the second line.

In the case
\ell:=\mathbb{E}[\log(1-\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})]=-\infty
, the second line of (
1.5
) is trivial. Regarding the first, put
\rho_{\omega}^{k}:=\min\{\rho_{\omega},1-1/k\}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
P_{\omega}[S_{1}>s]\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1-\rho_{f^{i}\omega}^{k})
for all
s
and
k
, and
\mathbb{E}[\log(1-\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}^{k})]>-\infty
, we can conclude as before that there exists
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that

\adjustlimits{\lim}_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}{\limsup}_{s\uparrow\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{s}\log P_{f^{t}\omega}[S_{1}>s]-\epsilon\frac{t}{s}\bigg\}\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\log(1-\rho_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}^{k})\big]

for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Letting
k\uparrow\infty
, we see the monotone convergence theorem demonstrates the first line of (
1.5
).

Pinning of polymers at interfaces with disorder.

We recover the standard pinning model
[
19
,
25
]
as follows. Suppose that the disorder
\omega:=\{\omega_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a real sequence, which is sampled from a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
in such a way that the canonical projections
\omega\mapsto\omega_{\tau}
form a sequence of i.i.d. random variables, and that the transformation
f
is the left-shift acting on
\omega
. Next, suppose that the waiting-time distribution is independent of
\omega
and is defined, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, by

p_{\omega}(s)=p(s):=\frac{L(s)}{s^{\alpha+1}}

with an index
\alpha\geq 0
and a slowly varying function at infinity
L
. Finally, given parameters
h,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the potential
v_{\omega}(s):=h+\beta\omega_{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, whose associated Hamiltonian turns out to be

H_{\omega,t}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}(h+\beta\omega_{T_{i}}).

This model represents a heteropolymer consisting of
t
monomers that interacts with a substrate through the monomers
T_{1},\ldots,T_{N_{t}}
. Monomer
\tau
has binding energy
h+\beta\omega_{\tau}
, and the spacing along the polymer chain of pinned units has a heavy-tailed distribution. If the last monomer is forced to be bound to the substrate, then the polymer is described by the constrained Gibbs measure
Q_{\omega,t}
, otherwise the law is the Gibbs measure
P_{\omega,t}
.

Our theory allows us to study the fluctuations of the number
N_{t}
of pinned monomers. In fact, we have
W_{t}=N_{t}

P_{\omega}
-a.s. when
\mathcal{X}:=\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s):=\delta_{1}
for each
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Our theory also allows us to investigate the fluctuations of the polymer excursions between pinned units. To this aim, we may suppose that, e.g.,
\mathcal{X}
is the Hilbert space of square-summable real functions on
\mathbb{N}
endowed with the Euclidean inner product
\langle\boldsymbol{\cdot},{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\rangle
and the corresponding norm
\|{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\|
. The space
\mathcal{X}
is separable, and so is its topological dual
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. Then, given an orthonormal basis
\{e_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{X}
, we can put
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s):=\delta_{e_{s}}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}
. In this framework, we have
W_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}e_{S_{i}}=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\#_{t}(s)e_{s}

P_{\omega}
-a.s., where
\#_{t}(s)
is a non-negative integer random variable that counts the number of times that a waiting time of size
s
has occurred up to time
t
.

Both for the number of pinned monomers and the number of polymer excursions between pinned units, the reward probability measures do not depend on
\omega
, so that Assumption
1.2
is automatically satisfied, as seen in Section
1.4
. Assumption
1.1
is trivial because
p(s)>0
for all sufficiently large
s
by definition, and the condition in (
1.5
) is met with
\ell:=0
. Assumption
1.3
requires that
\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{0}|\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
. Thus, Theorems
1.5
and
1.8
provide quenched weak LDPs with respect to the constrained and non-constrained model, respectively. Regarding the number of pinned monomers, the LDP is actually full and the rate function is good according to Corollaries
1.6
and
1.9
. We note, in general, that if
\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{0}|\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
and rewards are bounded by a common constant
\rho>0
, i.e.,
\lambda_{\omega}(B_{0,\rho}|s)=1
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in\Omega
as in the above cases, then
z(\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, so that
z_{\ell}=z
and
I_{\ell}=J
. This follows from
(ii)
of Proposition
1.4
since, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)}\mu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}w)

\displaystyle=E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{T_{1}=t\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle=p(t)\!\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(x)+h+\beta\omega_{t}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|t)\geq p(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\|\varphi\|\rho+h+\beta\omega_{t}}.

On the other hand, the hypothesis
\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{0}|\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
implies
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{\omega_{t}}{t}=0

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
, which can be easily justified by means of the strong law of large numbers. In conclusion, Theorems
1.5
and
1.8
lead to the following results.

Corollary 1.12
.

Suppose that
\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{0}|\,\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]<+\infty
. Then the following hold:

(i)

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the families
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, associated with the number of pinned monomers, satisfy the full LDP with good rate
function
J
.

(ii)

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the families
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, associated with the polymer excursions between pinned units, satisfy the weak LDP with rate function
J
.

The example of the number of pinned units allows to appreciate the effect of disorder on rate functions. The following proposition makes use of a known smoothing effect of disorder in the pinning model (under more restrictive hypotheses on disorder than necessary for the sake of simplicity) to unveil some properties of the rate function
J
of the number of pinned monomers. Note that, as
\frac{N_{t}}{t}\in[0,1]
for all
t
, the large deviation lower bound for open sets implies
J(w)=+\infty
for
w\notin[0,1]
. Put
u:=\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}p(s)}{\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}sp(s)}\in(0,1)
if
\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}sp(s)<+\infty
and
u:=0
otherwise.

Proposition 1.13
.

Assume that
p(s)>0
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
and that either
\omega_{0}
is bounded with full probability or is Gaussian distributed. Then the following hold:

(i)
If
\beta=0
and
u>0
, then the rate function
J
of the number of pinned monomers has an affine stretch on
(0,u]
, whereas it is strictly convex and infinitely differentiable on
(u,1)
.

(ii)
If either
\beta=0
and
u=0
or
\beta\neq 0
, then
J
is strictly convex and infinitely differentiable on
(0,1)
.

Thus, for a model with
u>0
, the rate function
J
of the number of pinned monomers has an affine stretch terminating at the point
u
when disorder is absent, i.e., when
\beta=0
. But as soon as disorder comes into play, i.e., when
\beta\neq 0
, the rate function
J
loses such an affine stretch. We note that the phenomenon of affine stretches in rate functions of homogeneous pinning models have been investigated in detail in
[
52
,
54
]
.

Proof of Proposition
1.13
.

Let us denote the free energy of the constrained pinning model
z(0)
by
\mathsf{F}(h)
to highlight the dependence on the parameter
h
. Since we are considering a problem where
W_{t}=N_{t}

P_{\omega}
-a.s., so that
\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)}\mu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}w)=E_{\omega}[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}]=E_{\omega}[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(1)N_{t}+H_{\omega,t}}]
,
(ii)
of Proposition
1.4
shows that
z(\varphi)=\mathsf{F}(h+k)
with
k:=\varphi(1)
. It therefore follows that, for
w\in\mathbb{R}
,

J(w):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\big\{\varphi(w)-z(\varphi)\big\}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\big\{wk-\mathsf{F}(h+k)\big\}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\big\{wk-\mathsf{F}(k)\big\}-wh.

(1.6)

The overall features of the free energy under the hypotheses of the proposition on disorder have been characterised and are now needed. In order to make contact with the existing literature, we suppose without loss of generality that
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=0
and
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}^{2}\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{d}\omega]=1
. To begin with, we recall that there exists a number
h_{c}
such that
\mathsf{F}(h)=0
for
h\leq h_{c}
and
\mathsf{F}(h)>0
for
h>h_{c}
(see
[
19
]
, Chapter 5). The function that maps
h
in
\mathsf{F}(h)
is convex on the real line and is infinitely differentiable and strictly convex on the open interval
(h_{c},+\infty)
(see
[
21
]
, Theorem 2.1 and
[
20
]
, Theorem B.1). In the absence of disorder, i.e., when
\beta=0
, we have that
h_{c}=-\log\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}p(s)
and that
\mathsf{F}(h)
for
h>h_{c}
is the unique positive real number that solves the equation
\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}p(s)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathsf{F}(h)s}=\mathrm{e}^{-h}
(see
[
19
]
, Proposition 1.1). Then, when
\beta=0
, we can easily verify that
\lim_{h\downarrow h_{c}}\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(h)=u
. This limit is affected by disorder, which has a smoothing effect. In fact, when
\beta\neq 0
, it is known that
\lim_{h\downarrow h_{c}}\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(h)=0
(see
[
22
]
, Theorem 2.1). In all cases, we find
\lim_{h\uparrow+\infty}\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(h)=1
since
p(1)>0
. To prove this claim, we note that, for
h>0
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{i=1}^{t}(h+\beta\omega_{i})+t\log p(1)}=E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=1,\ldots,S_{t}=1\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{ht}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}-hN_{t}}\Big],

so that
h+\log p(1)\leq\mathsf{F}(h)\leq h+F(0)
by Proposition
1.4
and Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem. The latter bounds imply
\lim_{h\uparrow+\infty}\frac{F(h)}{h}=1
. In this way, since convexity of
\mathsf{F}
entails
\frac{\mathsf{F}(h)-\mathsf{F}(0)}{h}\leq\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(h)\leq\frac{\mathsf{F}(k)-\mathsf{F}(h)}{k-h}
for
\max\{0,h_{c}\}<h<k
, we get
\lim_{h\uparrow+\infty}\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(h)=1
from here by letting
k\uparrow+\infty
first and then
h\uparrow+\infty
.

Coming back to formula (
1.6
), the listed overall features of the free energy allow us to conclude that

J(w)=\begin{cases}w(h_{c}-h)&\mbox{if $\beta=0$ and $w\in[0,u]$}\\
w(k-h)-\mathsf{F}(k)&\mbox{if $\beta=0$ and $w\in(u,1)$ or $\beta\neq 0$ and $w\in(0,1)$}\end{cases},

where
k>h_{c}
is the unique real number that satisfies
\mathsf{F}^{\prime}(k)=w
. The implicit function theorem demonstrates that
J
is strictly convex and infinitely differentiable on the interval
(u,1)
for
\beta=0
and on the interval
(0,1)
for
\beta\neq 0
.

Returns of Markov chains in dynamic random environments.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite set and let
\omega\mapsto K_{\omega}:=\{K_{\omega}(a,b)\}_{a,b\in\mathcal{C}}
be a random stochastic matrix over
\mathcal{C}
, i.e., a random non-negative matrix that satisfies
\sum_{b\in\mathcal{C}}K_{\omega}(a,b)=1
for all
a
and
\omega
. If the environment
\omega
evolves by successive applications of an ergodic measure-preserving transformation
f
, then the random matrix
\omega\mapsto K_{\omega}
defines a Markov chain in a dynamic random environment, which at time
t
jumps from state
a
to state
b
with probability
K_{f^{t}\omega}(a,b)
.

Given a distinguished state
c\in\mathcal{C}
, the returns of the Markov chain to the state
c
define a renewal process in a random environment. Its waiting-time distribution reads, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

p_{\omega}(s):=\begin{cases}K_{\omega}(a_{0},a_{s})&\mbox{if }s=1\\
\sum_{a_{1}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}\cdots\sum_{a_{s-1}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}K_{f^{i}\omega}(a_{i},a_{i+1})&\mbox{if }s\geq 2\end{cases}

with
a_{0}=a_{s}:=c
. We can use our theory to investigate, e.g., the number of distinct states that the Markov chain explores during its excursions from the state
c
. To this aim, for each
s\geq 2
such that
p_{\omega}(s)>0
, denoting by
\#\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
the number of distinct elements in the collection
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
, we introduce over
\mathcal{X}:=\mathbb{R}
the reward probability measure

\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s):=\frac{1}{p_{\omega}(s)}\sum_{a_{1}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}\cdots\sum_{a_{s-1}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}\delta_{\#\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}}\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}K_{f^{i}\omega}(a_{i},a_{i+1})

with
a_{0}=a_{s}:=c
. We put
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|1):=\delta_{1}
if
p_{\omega}(1)>0
and
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s):=\delta_{0}
whenever
p_{\omega}(s)=0
. Under the law
P_{\omega}
associated with
p_{\omega}
and
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
, the total reward
W_{t}
is almost surely the number of visited states in all excursions from the state
c
up to time
t
.

Assumption
1.1
immediately holds in the easy case
\mathbb{E}[\log K_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(a,b)]>-\infty
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
, which gives
\mathbb{E}[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)]>-\infty
for every

. Assumption
1.3
is trivial, since there is no potential in this example. Assumption
1.2
is verified as follows. Fix
s\in\mathbb{N}
and, for

, denote by

the set of all
\omega\in\Omega

. The sets

are measurable, because they inherit this property from the random stochastic matrix

, and are disjoint. Moreover,
\cup_{n=0}^{s}\Omega_{n}=\Omega
since
\sum_{n=0}^{s}\lambda(\{n\}|s)=1
. With such sets, consider the measurable function
\omega\mapsto r_{\omega,s}
that takes value

over
\Omega_{n}
. This function leads to the fulfilment of Assumption
1.2
, since
|r_{\omega,s}|\leq s
and
\lambda_{\omega}(\{r_{\omega,s}\}|s)\geq\frac{1}{s+1}
for all

by construction. Finally, we note that the hypothesis of Corollary
1.6
is satisfied with, e.g.,
\xi=1
and

since
\lambda_{\omega}([0,s]|s)=1
for all

and
\omega
. Thus, Theorem
1.8
and Corollary
1.9
give the following result after we show that the condition (

) holds with some
\ell
.

Corollary 1.14
.

Assume that
\mathbb{E}[\log K_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(a,b)]>-\infty
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
. Then there exists a finite number
\ell\leq 0
such that
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, associated with the total number of visited states in the excursions from the distinguishable state

I_{\ell}
.

Proof of Corollary
1.14
.

It remains to verify that condition (
1.5
) holds with a finite number
\ell
. The waiting-time tail probability reads, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

P_{\omega}[S_{1}>s]=\sum_{a_{1}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}\cdots\sum_{a_{s}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}K_{\omega}(a_{0},a_{1})\cdots K_{f^{s-1}\omega}(a_{s-1},a_{s})

(1.7)

with
a_{0}:=c
.

Denoting by
M_{\omega}
the restriction of
K_{\omega}
to
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}
and putting
M_{\omega}^{t}:=M_{f\omega}\cdots M_{f^{t}\omega}
for
t\in\mathbb{N}
with
M_{\omega}^{0}
the identity matrix, we can recast (
1.7
) as

P_{\omega}[S_{1}>s]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}K_{\omega}(c,a)M^{s-1}_{\omega}(a,b).

Since
\mathbb{E}[\log M_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(a,b)]=\mathbb{E}[\log K_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(a,b)]>-\infty
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}
, there exist a finite number
\ell
and a set
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log M_{\omega}^{t}(a,b)=\ell
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
(see
[
27
]
, Theorem 5). We can choose
\Omega_{o}
in order to also have
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log K_{f^{t}\omega}(c,a)=0
for all
a\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. A simple way to justify this claim involves Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem. Let us show that (
1.5
) holds for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
with such
\ell
.

Pick
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
\epsilon>0
. By construction, there exists a constant
k
such that
(\ell-\epsilon/3)t-k\leq\log M_{\omega}^{t}(a,b)\leq(\ell+\epsilon/3)t+k
and
\log K_{f^{t}\omega}(c,a)\geq-(\epsilon/3)t-k
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
. The latter can be extended to also include the case
t=0
. In this way, given any

, the identity

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}M^{t}_{\omega}(a_{o},a)M^{s-1}_{f^{t}\omega}(a,b)=M^{t+s-1}_{\omega}(a_{o},b)

shows that, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}

\mathrm{e}^{(\ell-\epsilon)(s-1)-(2\epsilon/3)t-2k}\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}M^{s-1}_{f^{t}\omega}(a,b)\leq|\mathcal{C}|\mathrm{e}^{(\ell+\epsilon)s+\epsilon t+2k}.

We also have, for
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
a\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}

\mathrm{e}^{-(\epsilon/3)t-k}\leq K_{f^{t}\omega}(c,a)\leq 1.

We note that

P_{f^{t}\omega}[S_{1}>s]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{c\}}K_{f^{t}\omega}(c,a)M^{s-1}_{f^{t}\omega}(a,b)\leq|\mathcal{C}|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{(\ell+\epsilon)s+\epsilon t+2k}

and

P_{f^{t}\omega}[S_{1}>s]\geq\mathrm{e}^{(\ell-\epsilon)(s-1)-\epsilon t-3k}

for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
and

. These bounds show that

\limsup_{s\uparrow\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{s}\log P_{f^{t}\omega}[S_{1}>s]-\epsilon\frac{t}{s}\bigg\}\leq\ell+\epsilon

and

\liminf_{s\uparrow\infty}\inf_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{s}\log P_{f^{t}\omega}[S_{1}>s]+\epsilon\frac{t}{s}\bigg\}\geq\ell-\epsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\epsilon
proves (
1.5
).

2.1
Supermultiplicativity

Supermultiplicativity.

The identity in (
1.1
) leads to a supermultiplicativity property for the random measures

defined by (

), as shown in the following lemma. For
A,A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

and
A+A^{\prime}:=\{w+w^{\prime}:w\in A,\,w^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\}

Lemma 2.1
.

For

and

,
\mu_{\omega,t+t^{\prime}}(\alpha A+\alpha^{\prime}A^{\prime})\geq\mu_{\omega,t}(A)\mu_{f^{t}\omega,t^{\prime}}(A^{\prime})

\alpha:=\frac{t}{t+t^{\prime}}
and
\alpha^{\prime}:=\frac{t^{\prime}}{t+t^{\prime}}

\mu_{\omega,t+t^{\prime}}(A)\geq\mu_{\omega,t}(A)\mu_{f^{t}\omega,t^{\prime}}(A)

is convex.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Writing

\displaystyle\geq E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in A,\,t\in\mathcal{T},\,\frac{W_{t+t^{\prime}}-W_{t}}{t^{\prime}}\in A^{\prime},\,t+t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t+t^{\prime}}}\bigg],

where the last bound accounts for the constraint

\displaystyle E_{\omega}

\displaystyle\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in A,\,t\in\mathcal{T},\,\frac{W_{t+t^{\prime}}-W_{t}}{t^{\prime}}\in A^{\prime},\,t+t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t+t^{\prime}}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\min\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s_{i})\}\big]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\log p_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s_{i})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\log\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},s_{i}}+K_{i})\big]>-\infty.\qed

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, put

z_{o}(\varphi):=\sup_{t\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{t}\log Z_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}(\varphi)\bigg]\bigg\}.

Hölder’s inequality shows that the functions that associate
\varphi
with

that maps
\varphi
in

2.2
and

with

.

Corollary 2.4
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
,
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{t}\log Z_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}(\varphi)]=z_{o}(\varphi)

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
.

A similar result holds under truncation and will be used in Section
2.4
to work out some technical steps. With Lemmas

2.2
and
2.3
, the following follows from
(\star)
with
F_{t}(\omega)=-\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,t}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)
.

Corollary 2.5
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
and
\delta\geq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
, the supremum
e:=\sup_{t\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}}\{\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)]\}
is finite and
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,t}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)=e

.

We now go back to the random measures
\omega\mapsto\mu_{\omega,t}
. Although the sequence of term

convex enjoys supermultiplicativity according to Lemma

for all

and

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that

(ii)

\displaystyle\mu_{\omega,t}(C)

\displaystyle:=E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

2.2
A renewal equation in a random environment

In this section we prove that
z_{o}=z
, with
z
the function defined in Section
1.3
by the variational formula
z(\varphi):=\inf\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\colon\,\Upsilon_{\varphi}(\zeta)\leq 0\}
with

\Upsilon_{\varphi}(\zeta):=\infd_{R\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}\mathbb{P}\,\mbox{-}\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}\bigg\{\log E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})-\zeta S_{1}+R(f^{S_{1}}\omega)-R(\omega)}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}<\infty\}}\Big]\bigg\}.

To this aim, we resort to the following renewal equation in a random environment: for
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
,

\displaystyle Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=s,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=s\}}\Big]\,Z_{f^{s}\omega,t-s}(\varphi).

This equation is due to the fact that, conditional on
S_{1}=s
, the random variables
S_{2},S_{3},\ldots,X_{2},X_{3},\ldots
are independent of
X_{1}
and jointly distributed as
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
in the environment
f^{s}\omega
. The equality
z_{o}=z
demonstrates Proposition
1.4
thanks to Corollary
2.4
, and the property that
z_{o}
is proper convex, with
z_{o}(0)
finite and lower semi-continuous. Lower semi-continuity of
z_{o}
will be verified in Section
2.4
.

Fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. We first verify that
z_{o}(\varphi)\leq z(\varphi)
. To this aim we assume that
z(\varphi)<+\infty
, otherwise there is nothing to prove. Pick real numbers
\zeta>z(\varphi)
and
\epsilon>0
. Since
\Upsilon_{\varphi}(\zeta)\leq 0
as
\zeta>z(\varphi)
, there exist a random variable
R\in\mathcal{R}_{+}
and a set
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
and

\log E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})-\zeta S_{1}+R(f^{S_{1}}\omega)-R(\omega)}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}<\infty\}}\Big]\leq\epsilon

for every
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. By changing
\Omega_{o}
with
\cap_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}f^{-t}\Omega_{o}
if necessary, we may suppose that
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
implies
f^{t}\omega\in\Omega_{o}
for any
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Below we will prove that
Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)\leq\mathrm{e}^{t(\zeta+\epsilon)+R(\omega)}
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. This gives
z_{o}(\varphi)\leq\zeta+\epsilon
since, by Corollary
2.4
, there exists at least one point
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
such that
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)=z_{o}(\varphi)
. The arbitrariness of
\zeta
and
\epsilon
demonstrates that
z_{o}(\varphi)\leq z(\varphi)
.

We prove that
Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)\leq\mathrm{e}^{t(\zeta+\epsilon)+R(\omega)}
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
by induction. The bound is true when
t=0
, as
Z_{\omega,0}(\varphi)=1
and
R(\omega)>0
. Suppose that it holds for every
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
up to
t-1
with a positive
t
. Pick
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. Then the renewal equation and the fact that
f^{s}\omega\in\Omega_{o}
for
s=1,\ldots,t
show that

\displaystyle Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=s\}}\Big]\,Z_{f^{s}\omega,t-s}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=1}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=s\}}\Big]\,\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)(\zeta+\epsilon)+R(f^{s}\omega)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{t(\zeta+\epsilon)+R(\omega)}\sum_{s=1}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})-\zeta S_{1}+R(f^{S_{1}}\omega)-R(\omega)}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=s\}}\Big]\,\mathrm{e}^{-s\epsilon_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{t(\zeta+\epsilon)+R(\omega)}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})-\zeta S_{1}+R(f^{S_{1}}\omega)-R(\omega)}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}<\infty\}}\Big]\,\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon}\leq\mathrm{e}^{t(\zeta+\epsilon)+R(\omega)}.

Next, let us show the opposite bound
z_{o}(\varphi)\geq z(\varphi)
. If
z_{o}(\varphi)=+\infty
, then there is nothing to prove. If instead
z_{o}(\varphi)<+\infty
, then pick a real number
\zeta>z_{o}(\varphi)
. By Corollary
2.4
, the number
\zeta
makes the series
\sum_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta t}
convergent
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
. We have
\sum_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta t}>1

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
, since
Z_{\omega,0}(\varphi)=1
and
Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)>0
for all
t\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
, the latter being implied by
(i)
of Lemma
2.3
. Thus, the random variable
\omega\mapsto R(\omega):=\log\sum_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta t}
is almost surely finite and positive. Given any
\omega\in\Omega
such that
0<R(\omega)<+\infty
, the renewal equation yields

\displaystyle 1>\sum_{t\in\mathbb{N}}Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta t-R(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=s\}}\Big]\,Z_{f^{s}\omega,t}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta t-\zeta s-R(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}=s\}}\Big]\,\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta s+R(f^{s}\omega)-R(\omega)}

\displaystyle=E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(X_{1})+v_{\omega}(S_{1})-\zeta S_{1}+R(f^{S_{1}}\omega)-R(\omega)}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{1}<\infty\}}\Big].

This shows that
\Upsilon_{\varphi}(\zeta)<0
, and consequently
z(\varphi)\leq\zeta
. We get
z(\varphi)\leq z_{o}(\varphi)
by letting
\zeta\downarrow z_{o}(\varphi)
.

2.3
The weak LDP

The quenched weak LDP for the family
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is obtained after replacing the probability measure
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
by convenient probability measures
\lambda_{\omega}^{1}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s),\lambda_{\omega}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s),\ldots
that satisfy the integrability condition of Lemma
2.6
. The following lemma introduces such probability measures, resulting from a coupling argument that underlies an exponential
approximation.

Lemma 2.7
.

There exist a real number
L\geq 0
and, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, a sequence
\Lambda_{\omega}^{1}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s),\Lambda_{\omega}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s),\ldots
of probability measures on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})
such that the following hold for all
k
:

(i)

\lambda_{\omega}(A\times\mathcal{X}|s)=\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(A\times\mathcal{X}|s)
and
\omega\mapsto\lambda_{\omega}^{k}(A|s):=\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(A\times\mathcal{X}|s)
is measurable for each
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
.

(ii)

\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{k\|x-x^{\prime}\|}\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\mathrm{d}(x,x^{\prime})|s)\leq\mathrm{e}^{L}
.

(iii)

\mathbb{E}[\log\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}^{k}(G|s)]>-\infty
for all
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
open and nonempty.

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}:=\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable dense subset of
\mathcal{X}
, which exists by separability, and let

q_{n}:=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-n-\|u_{n}\|}}{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{e}^{-i-\|u_{i}\|}}

define a probability mass function on
\mathbb{N}
. We have
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{e}^{\|u_{n}\|}q_{n}<+\infty
. Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
be a basis of the subspace
\mathcal{V}
in Assumption
1.2
, and let
g
be the function that maps
\zeta:=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
g(\zeta):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\zeta_{i}e_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
. With
g
, construct on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{V})
the probability measure

\rho:=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathds{1}_{\{g(\zeta)\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,\}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\|g(\zeta)\|}\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\|g(\zeta)\|}\mathrm{d}\zeta},

where
\mathrm{d}\zeta
is the Lebesgue measure. We have
\int_{\mathcal{V}}e^{\|v\|}\rho(\mathrm{d}v)<+\infty
and
\rho(v+A)\geq\mathrm{e}^{-2\|v\|}\rho(A)
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{V})
, the latter being a consequence of the translation invariance of the Lebesgue measure. We claim that the lemma holds with the number

L:=\log\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{e}^{\|u_{n}\|}q_{n}+\log\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{e}^{\|v\|}\rho(\mathrm{d}v)

and the probability measures
\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
defined for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathsf{A}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})
by

\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\mathsf{A}|s):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}q_{n}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\bigg[\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathds{1}_{\big\{(x,u_{n}/k+x-v/k)\in\mathsf{A}\big\}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\bigg]\rho(\mathrm{d}v).

For
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
, we have

\lambda_{\omega}^{k}(A|s):=\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\mathcal{X}\times A|s)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}q_{n}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{\omega}\big(v/k+A-u_{n}/k\big|s\big)\rho(\mathrm{d}v).

(i)
and
(ii)
Fix
s
and
k
in
\mathbb{N}
. It is manifest that
\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(A\times\mathcal{X}|s)=\lambda_{\omega}(A|s)
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
, and that

\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{k\|x-x^{\prime}\|}\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\mathrm{d}(x,x^{\prime})|s)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}q_{n}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{e}^{\|u_{n}-v\|}\rho(\mathrm{d}v)\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{e}^{\|u_{n}\|}q_{n}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{e}^{\|v\|}\rho(\mathrm{d}v)=\mathrm{e}^{L}.

(iii)
Fix
s
and
k
in
\mathbb{N}
and pick a set
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
open and nonempty,
x\in G
, and
\delta>0
such that
B_{x,3\delta}\subseteq G
. Recalling that
\mathcal{S}
is dense in
\mathcal{X}
, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\|x-u_{m}/k\|<\delta
. Note that if
y\in B_{r_{\omega,s}+x_{s},\delta}
and
v\in B_{kr_{\omega,s}+kx_{s},k\delta}
with
r_{\omega,s}
and
x_{s}
as in Assumption
1.2
, then

\big\|y-v/k-x+u_{m}/k\big\|\leq\big\|y-r_{\omega,s}-x_{s}\big\|+\big\|r_{\omega,s}+x_{s}-v/k\big\|+\big\|x-u_{m}/k\big\|<3\delta,

so that
r_{\omega,s}+B_{x_{s},\delta}=B_{r_{\omega,s}+x_{s},\delta}\subseteq B_{v/k+x-u_{m}/k,3\delta}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\omega}^{k}(G|s)\geq\lambda_{\omega}^{k}(B_{x,3\delta}|s)

\displaystyle\geq q_{m}\int_{\mathcal{V}\cap B_{kr_{\omega,s}+kx_{s},k\delta}}\lambda_{\omega}\big(B_{v/k+x-u_{m}/k,3\delta}\big|s\big)\rho(\mathrm{d}v)

\displaystyle\geq q_{m}\,\lambda_{\omega}(r_{\omega,s}+B_{x_{s},\delta}|s)\,\rho(\mathcal{V}\cap B_{kr_{\omega,s}+kx_{s},k\delta}).

In this way, since
\rho(\mathcal{V}\cap B_{kr_{\omega,s}+kx_{s},k\delta})=\rho(kr_{\omega,s}+\mathcal{V}\cap B_{kx_{s},k\delta})\geq\mathrm{e}^{-2k\|r_{\omega,s}\|}\rho(\mathcal{V}\cap B_{kx_{s},k\delta})
because
r_{\omega,s}\in\mathcal{V}
, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\log\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}^{k}(G|s)\big]

\displaystyle\geq\log q_{m}+\mathbb{E}\big[\log\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},s}+B_{x_{s},\delta}|s)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad-2k\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,\|r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}\|-\eta t\big\}\Big]-2k\eta+\log\rho(\mathcal{V}\cap B_{kx_{s},k\delta})>-\infty

by Assumption
1.2
.
∎

Thanks to
(iii)
of Lemma
2.7
, Lemma
2.6
applies to the random measures
\omega\mapsto\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}
associated with

\lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
: for each open convex set
C

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(C)=\mathcal{L}^{k}(C):=\sup_{t\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}^{k}(C)\bigg]\bigg\}\qquad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega.

The function
J_{o}
that maps
w\in\mathcal{X}
in

J_{o}(w):=-\inf_{\delta>0}\bigg\{\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\delta})\bigg\}

turns out to be the rate function in a quenched weak LDP for the family
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, as we will show below. The following lemma provides two general properties of
J_{o}
. We note that
J_{o}(w)\geq-z_{o}(0)>-\infty
.

Lemma 2.8
.

The function
J_{o}
is lower semi-continuous and convex.

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

Pick
w\in\mathcal{X}
and a sequence
\{w_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{X}
that converges to
w
. Given
\delta>0
, the monotonicity of
\mathcal{L}^{k}
inherited from the measures
\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}
entails that
-J_{o}(w_{i})\leq\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w_{i},\delta/2})\leq\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\delta})
for all sufficiently large

satisfying
B_{w_{i},\delta/2}\subseteq B_{w,\delta}
. This implies the bound
\liminf_{i\uparrow\infty}J_{o}(w_{i})\geq-\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\delta})
and proves the lower semi-continuity of
J_{o}
by the arbitrariness of
\delta
.

As far as the convexity of
J_{o}
is concerned, due to lower semi-continuity it suffices to verify that
J_{o}(\alpha w+\alpha^{\prime}w^{\prime})\leq\alpha J_{o}(w)+\alpha^{\prime}J_{o}(w^{\prime})
for every fixed
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
and rational numbers
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}>0
such that
\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}=1
. Given
\delta>0
and observing that
B_{\alpha w+\alpha^{\prime}w^{\prime},\delta}\supseteq\alpha B_{w,\delta}+\alpha^{\prime}B_{w^{\prime},\delta}
,
(ii)
of Lemma
2.6
shows that
\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{\alpha w+\alpha^{\prime}w^{\prime},\delta})\geq\mathcal{L}^{k}(\alpha B_{w,\delta}+\alpha^{\prime}B_{w^{\prime},\delta})\geq\alpha\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\delta})+\alpha^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w^{\prime},\delta})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. From here, letting
k\uparrow\infty
, we get
-J_{o}(\alpha w+\alpha^{\prime}w^{\prime})\geq-\alpha J_{o}(w)-\alpha^{\prime}J_{o}(w^{\prime})
because
\delta
is arbitrary.
∎

We next show that
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies a weak LDP with rate function
J_{o}
by resorting to a coupling with new rewards distributed according to
\lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
. In fact, with the sequence of probability measures
\Lambda_{\omega}^{1}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s),\Lambda_{\omega}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s),\ldots
introduced by Lemma
2.7
, we consider waiting times
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots
and pairs of rewards
(X_{1},X_{1}^{\prime}),(X_{2},X_{2}^{\prime}),\ldots
distributed according to the joint law

\mathsf{P}_{\omega}^{k}\Big[S_{1}=s_{1},\ldots,S_{n}=s_{n},\,(X_{1},X_{1}^{\prime})\in\mathsf{A}_{1},\ldots,(X_{n},X_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathsf{A}_{n}\Big]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})\,\Lambda_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}^{k}(\mathsf{A}_{i}|s_{i})

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\mathsf{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{A}_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})
, where
t_{0}:=0
and
t_{i}:=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
W_{t}^{\prime}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}X_{i}^{\prime}
be the total reward associated with the new rewards
X_{1}^{\prime},X_{2}^{\prime},\ldots
and denote by
\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}
expectation under
\mathsf{P}_{\omega}^{k}
. Since
\lambda_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
and
\lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
are
marginals of
\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
, we have

\mu_{\omega,t}=\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg],\qquad\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}=\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}^{\prime}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg].

Importantly, the properties of
\Lambda_{\omega}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}|s)
make
W_{t}^{\prime}
an exponential approximation of
W_{t}
, in the sense that, for

t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\delta>0
,

\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\|W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime}\|>\delta t,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\Big]\leq\mathrm{e}^{Lt-k\delta t}Z_{\omega,t}(0)

(2.6)

with
L
as in Lemma
2.7
. Indeed, a Chernoff-type bound, in combination with
(ii)
of Lemma
2.7
, gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}

\displaystyle\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\|W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime}\|>\delta t,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-k\delta t}\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{k\|W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime}\|+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-k\delta t}\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}k\|X_{i}-X_{i}^{\prime}\|+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-k\delta t}\sum_{n=1}^{t}\sum_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}}\cdots\sum_{s_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{t_{n}=t\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{v_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})}p_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{k\|x-x^{\prime}\|}\Lambda_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}^{k}(\mathrm{d}(x,x^{\prime})|s_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-k\delta t}\sum_{n=1}^{t}\sum_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}}\cdots\sum_{s_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{t_{n}=t\}}\mathrm{e}^{Ln}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{v_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})}p_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})\leq\mathrm{e}^{Lt-k\delta t}Z_{\omega,t}(0).

The bound in (
2.6
) is the basis of the following proposition, which states the desired LDP.

Proposition 2.9
.

The following bounds hold
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
:

(i)

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\geq-J_{o}(w)
for all
w\in\mathcal{X}
and
\delta>0
.

(ii)

\inf_{\delta>0}\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\}\leq-J_{o}(w)
for all
w\in\mathcal{X}
.

Consequently,
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the weak LDP with rate function
J_{o}
.

Proof of Proposition
2.9
.

Suppose for the moment that
(i)
and
(ii)
hold for a certain
\omega\in\Omega
. Then, the family
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is shown to satisfy the weak LDP with rate function
J_{o}
as follows. Given an open set
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, a point
w\in G
, and a real number
\delta>0
such that
B_{w,\delta}\subseteq G
,
(i)
implies
\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(G)\geq\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\geq-J_{o}(w)
. The arbitrariness of
w
yields the large deviation lower bound
\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(G)\geq-\inf_{w\in G}J_{o}(w)
. At the same time, given a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{X}
and a real number
\lambda<\inf_{w\in K}J_{o}(w)
, by
(ii)
for each
w\in K
there exists
\delta_{w}>0
with the property
\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta_{w}})\leq-\lambda
. As
\{B_{w,\delta_{w}}\}_{w\in K}
is an open cover of
K
, there exist finitely many points
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
in
K
such that
K\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}}
. Thus,
\mu_{\omega,t}(K)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}})
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
, giving
\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(K)\leq-\lambda
. The arbitrariness of
\lambda
implies the large deviation upper bound
\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(K)\leq-\inf_{w\in K}J_{o}(w)
.

Let us next verify
(i)
and
(ii)
. By separability,
\mathcal{X}
contains a countable dense subset
\mathcal{S}
. Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
the set of positive rational numbers. Thanks to
(iii)
of Lemma
2.7
, Lemma
2.6
ensures that there exists
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{u,\alpha})=\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{u,\alpha})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in\mathcal{S}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
, and
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. By Corollary
2.4
, we may also suppose that
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log Z_{\omega,t}(0)=z_{o}(0)<+\infty
for every
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. We prove that
(i)
and
(ii)
hold for any given
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. To this aim, we make use of the bound in (
2.6
) to state that, for
w\in\mathcal{X}
and
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,2\delta})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w,2\delta},\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}^{\prime}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta},\,\|W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime}\|\leq\delta t,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}^{\prime}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta},\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]-\mathsf{E}_{\omega}^{k}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\|W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime}\|>\delta t,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{w,\delta})-\mathrm{e}^{Lt-k\delta t}Z_{\omega,t}(0).

(2.7)

Exchanging
W_{t}
and
W_{t}^{\prime}
, we also have

\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\leq\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{w,2\delta})+\mathrm{e}^{Lt-k\delta t}Z_{\omega,t}(0).

(2.8)

(i)
Fix
w\in\mathcal{X}
such that
J_{o}(w)<+\infty
(otherwise there is nothing to prove) and
\delta>0
. Pick
\epsilon>0
. We show that

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\geq-J_{o}(w)-2\epsilon,

(2.9)

which yields
(i)
by the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and
u\in\mathcal{S}
have the properties
5\alpha\leq\delta
and
\|u-w\|<\alpha
, so that
B_{w,\alpha}\subseteq B_{u,2\alpha}\subseteq B_{u,4\alpha}\subseteq B_{w,5\alpha}\subseteq B_{w,\delta}
. By construction,
J_{o}(w)
satisfies
\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\alpha})\geq-J_{o}(w)
, which allows us to find
k\in\mathbb{N}
so large that
\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\alpha})\geq-J_{o}(w)-\epsilon
and
z_{o}(0)\leq 2k\alpha-L-J_{o}(w)-4\epsilon
with
L
as in Lemma
2.7
. Thus, since
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{u,2\alpha})=\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{u,2\alpha})\geq\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\alpha})
and
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln Z_{\omega,t}(0)=z_{o}(0)
, we can state that
\frac{1}{t}\ln\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{u,2\alpha})\geq-J_{o}(w)-2\epsilon
and
\frac{1}{t}\ln Z_{\omega,t}(0)\leq 2k\alpha-L-J_{o}(w)-3\epsilon
for all sufficiently large
t
. For such
t
, the bound in (
2.7
) gives

\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\geq\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{u,4\alpha})\geq\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{u,2\alpha})-\mathrm{e}^{Lt-2k\alpha t}Z_{\omega,t}(0)\geq e^{-J_{o}(w)t-2\epsilon t}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\epsilon t}),

which demonstrates (
2.9
).

(ii)
Pick
w\in\mathcal{X}
and a real number
\lambda<J_{o}(w)
. We prove that

\inf_{\delta>0}\bigg\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\bigg\}\leq-\lambda,

(2.10)

which demonstrates
(ii)
thanks to the arbitrariness of
\lambda
. As
J_{o}(w):=-\inf_{\delta>0}\{\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,\delta})\}
, there exists an
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
such that
\liminf_{k\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,4\alpha})<-\lambda
. Hence there exists a
k\in\mathbb{N}
so large that
\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,4\alpha})\leq-\lambda
and
z_{o}(0)\leq k\alpha-L-\lambda
. Finally, there exists a
u\in\mathcal{S}
with the property
\|w-u\|<\alpha
. We have
B_{w,2\alpha}\subseteq B_{u,3\alpha}\subseteq B_{w,4\alpha}
. The bound in (
2.8
) gives

\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\alpha})\leq\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{w,2\alpha})+\mathrm{e}^{Lt-k\alpha t}Z_{\omega,t}(0)\leq\mu_{\omega,t}^{k}(B_{u,3\alpha})+\mathrm{e}^{Lt-k\alpha t}Z_{\omega,t}(0)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
, so that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{u,3\alpha}),\,L-k\alpha+z_{o}(0)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\mathcal{L}^{k}(B_{w,4\alpha}),\,L-k\alpha+z_{o}(0)\Big\}\leq-\lambda.

This proves (
2.10
).
∎

2.4
Legendre transform of the free energy

In this section we show that
J_{o}
is the Legendre transform
z_{o}^{\star}
of
z_{o}
defined, for
w\in\mathcal{X}
, by
z_{o}^{\star}(w):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\{\varphi(w)-z_{o}(\varphi)\}
. Since
z_{o}=z
, this proves that
J_{o}
is the rate function
J
introduced in Section
1.3
as the Legendre transform of
z
. Thus, Proposition
2.9
demonstrates Theorem
1.5
.

In order to show that
J_{o}=z_{o}^{\star}
, it suffices to verify that
J_{o}^{\star}=z_{o}
with
J_{o}^{\star}
the Legendre transform of
J_{o}
defined, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, by
J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi):=\sup_{w\in\mathcal{X}}\{\varphi(w)-J_{o}(w)\}
. In fact, a general result from convex analysis states that
J_{o}(w)=J_{o}^{\star\star}(w):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\{\varphi(w)-J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)\}
if
J_{o}
is proper convex and lower semi-continuous (see
[
51
]
, Theorem 2.3.3). We already know from Lemma
2.8
that
J_{o}
is convex and lower semi-continuous. If
J_{o}^{\star}=z_{o}
, then
J_{o}
is proper convex because
\inf_{w\in\mathcal{X}}J_{o}(w)=-J_{o}^{\star}(0)=-z_{o}(0)
with
z_{o}(0)
finite implies that
J_{o}(w)>-\infty
for all
w\in\mathcal{X}
and
J_{o}(w)<+\infty
for some
w\in\mathcal{X}
. We note that the identity
J_{o}^{\star}=z_{o}
also proves that
z_{o}
is lower semi-continuous, as claimed in Section
2.1
.

To verify that
J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)=z_{o}(\varphi)
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, we first demonstrate that
J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)\leq z_{o}(\varphi)
, and afterwards that
J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)\geq z_{o}(\varphi)
. The latter is harder to obtain than the former.

Lower bound.

Fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
,
w\in\mathcal{X}
, and
\delta>0
. Note that if
\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta}
, then
\varphi(W_{t})-t\varphi(w)=\varphi(W_{t}-tw)\geq-\|\varphi\|\|W_{t}-tw\|\geq-\|\varphi\|\delta t
, namely
\varphi(W_{t})\geq t\varphi(w)-t\|\varphi\|\delta
. It follows that

\displaystyle E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta},\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)-t\|\varphi\|\delta}\,E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta},\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]=\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)-t\|\varphi\|\delta}\,\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})

for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking logarithms, dividing by
t
, and letting
t\uparrow\infty
, we get
z_{o}(\varphi)\geq\varphi(w)-J_{o}(w)-\|\varphi\|\delta
thanks to Corollary
2.4
and
(i)
of Proposition
2.9
. Thus, appealing to the arbitrariness of
w
and
\delta
, we find the bound
z_{o}(\varphi)\geq\sup_{w\in\mathcal{X}}\{\varphi(w)-J_{o}(w)\}=:J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)
.

Upper bound.

Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
and real numbers
\zeta\leq z_{o}(\varphi)
and
\epsilon>0
. The goal is to show that
\zeta\leq J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)+3\epsilon
. This gives
J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)\geq z_{o}(\varphi)
by the arbitrariness of
\zeta
and
\epsilon
. The random variables
\omega\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{\omega,t}(A,\varphi)
defined in Section
2.1
come into play here.

As
\zeta\leq z_{o}(\varphi)
, Corollary
2.4
implies that there exists an integer
\tau\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}
such that

\zeta\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{\tau}\log Z_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(\varphi)\bigg]+\epsilon.

The monotone converge theorem shows that
\lim_{\delta\uparrow\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)=Z_{\omega,\tau}(\varphi)
. Since
\mathbb{E}[\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)]
exists and is finite for all
\delta\geq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
by Lemma
2.3
, a second application of the monotone converge theorem to the non-negative random variables
\omega\mapsto\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)-\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(B_{0,\delta_{\mathrm{F}}},\varphi)
entails existence of a number
\delta\geq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
such that

\zeta\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)\bigg]+2\epsilon.

(2.11)

The crucial point is that we can replace the open ball
B_{0,\delta}
by a compact set, according to the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

For every
\tau\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}
,
\delta\geq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(B_{0,\delta},\varphi)]\leq\mathbb{E}[\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K,\varphi)]+\epsilon
.

Proof of Lemma
2.10
.

Fix
\tau\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}
,
\delta\geq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, and
\epsilon>0
. Recall that there exists a
K_{o}\subset\mathcal{X}
compact such that
\mathbb{E}[\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)]
exists and is finite, as seen in Section
2.1
. Let
\delta_{o}\geq\delta
be a number that satisfies
B_{0,\delta_{o}}\supset K_{o}
and denote by
F_{o}
the closure of
B_{0,\delta_{o}}
for brevity. Clearly,
B_{0,\delta}\subseteq B_{0,\delta_{o}}\subset F_{o}\subset B_{0,2\delta_{o}}
and
K_{o}\subset F_{o}
. Since
\min\{0,\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)\}\geq\min\{0,\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(B_{0,\delta_{o}},\varphi)\}
and
\max\{0,\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)\}\leq\max\{0,\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(B_{0,2\delta_{o}},\varphi)\}
, the expectation
\mathbb{E}[\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)]
exists and is finite by Lemma
2.3
. The present lemma is demonstrated once we show that

\mathbb{E}\big[\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K,\varphi)\big]+\epsilon

(2.12)

for some compact set
K\subset\mathcal{X}
.

For
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}
, put

\Delta_{\omega,i}(A):=E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{Y_{\omega,1}\in F_{o},\ldots,Y_{\omega,N_{\tau}}\in F_{o},\,Y_{\omega,i}\in A,\,N_{\tau}\geq i,\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{\tau})+H_{\omega,\tau}}\Big]\leq\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi).

We note that the measure that associates
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
with

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg\{1+\log\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)}\bigg\}\frac{\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},i}(A)}{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}\Bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[1+\log\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)}\bigg]

is finite. Therefore, due to the separability of
\mathcal{X}
, such a measure is tight (see
[
10
]
, Theorem 7.1.7), so that there exists a
K_{i}\subset\mathcal{X}
compact with the property that

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg\{1+\log\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)}\bigg\}\frac{\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},i}(K_{i}^{c})}{\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}\Bigg]\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\tau}.

(2.13)

Define
K:=(K_{o}\cup K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{\tau})\cap F_{o}
. We claim that
K
is the desired compact set. In fact, we resort to the bound
\log(1+\zeta)\leq\{1+\log(1+\zeta)\}\frac{\zeta}{1+\zeta}
valid for
\zeta\geq 0
, to write down

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)

\displaystyle=\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)+\log\bigg\{1+\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)-\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)+\bigg\{1+\log\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)}\bigg\}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)-\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)\geq\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)\geq\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)
, we can even replace
\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)
by
\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)
in the second logarithm to obtain

\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)\leq\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)+\bigg\{1+\log\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)}\bigg\}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)-\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}.

(2.14)

Next, we observe that either
Y_{\omega,i}\in K
for
i=1,\ldots,N_{\tau}
or there exists a
i\leq N_{\tau}
such that
Y_{\omega,i}\in K^{c}
. We also observe that
\Delta_{\omega,i}(K^{c})=\Delta_{\omega,i}(K^{c}\cap F_{o})\leq\Delta_{\omega,i}(K_{i}^{c})
, as
\Delta_{\omega,i}(A)=\Delta_{\omega,i}(A\cap F_{o})
for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
. These arguments give

\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)\leq\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\Delta_{\omega,i}(K^{c})\leq\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\Delta_{\omega,i}(K_{i}^{c}).

Combining this inequality with (
2.14
), we get the bound

\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)\leq\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K,\varphi)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\bigg\{1+\log\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(K_{o},\varphi)}\bigg\}\frac{\Delta_{\omega,i}(K_{i}^{c})}{\mathcal{E}_{\omega,\tau}(F_{o},\varphi)}.

After taking expectation, this bound becomes (
2.12
) thanks to (
2.13
).
∎

By the bound in (
2.11
) and Lemma
2.10
, there exists a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{X}
such that

\zeta\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K,\varphi)\bigg]+3\epsilon.

Replacing
K
by the closed convex hull of
\{0\}\cup K
if necessary, we can treat
K
as a compact convex set that contains
0
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, Lemma
2.2
implies that
\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},\tau}(K,\varphi)]\leq\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{n\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},n\tau}(K,\varphi)]
, so that

\zeta\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{n\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},n\tau}(K,\varphi)\bigg]+3\epsilon.

If
\rho\geq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
is a number such that
K\subset B_{0,\rho}
, then, defining
e:=\sup_{t\geq t_{\mathrm{F}}}\{\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}(B_{0,\rho},\varphi)]\}
, we can even conclude that

\zeta\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{n\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},n\tau}(K,\varphi)-\frac{1}{n\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},n\tau}(B_{0,\rho},\varphi)\bigg]+e+3\epsilon.

In this way, as the random variables
\omega\mapsto\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,n\tau}(B_{0,\rho},\varphi)-\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,n\tau}(K,\varphi)

are non-negative, Fatou’s lemma gives

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{n\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},n\tau}(K,\varphi)-\frac{1}{n\tau}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},n\tau}(B_{0,\rho},\varphi)\bigg]+e+3\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}(K,\varphi)-\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},t}(B_{0,\rho},\varphi)\bigg\}\bigg]+e+3\epsilon.

Since
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,t}(B_{0,\rho},\varphi)=e

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
by Corollary
2.5
, from here we get the desired bound
\zeta\leq J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)+3\epsilon
once we prove that

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathcal{E}_{\omega,t}(K,\varphi)\leq J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)\qquad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega.

(2.15)

To this aim, we note that the conditions
Y_{\omega,1}\in K,\ldots,Y_{\omega,N_{t}}\in K
entail
\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}Y_{\omega,i}=(1-\frac{N_{t}}{t})0+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}Y_{\omega,i}\in K
, since
K
is convex with
0\in K
. It follows that, for
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\omega,t}(K,\varphi)

\displaystyle:=E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{Y_{\omega,1}\in K,\ldots,Y_{\omega,N_{t}}\in K,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}Y_{\omega,i}\in K,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\bigg].

(2.16)

The following lemma concludes the proof because it yields (
2.15
) through (
2.16
).

Lemma 2.11
.

For any compact set
K\in\mathcal{X}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
,

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}Y_{\omega,i}\in K,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]\leq J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)\qquad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega.

Proof of Lemma
2.11
.

Fix
K\subset\mathcal{X}
compact and
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. Denote by
F
the closed ball of center
0
and radius
2\,\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,\|r_{\boldsymbol{\cdot},s}\|-\eta s\}]+2\eta<+\infty
, with
\eta
the number in Assumption
1.2
. We will prove that
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\mathcal{V}\cap F+K,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]\leq J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi),

(2.17)

where
\mathcal{V}
is the finite-dimensional subspace in Assumption
1.2
. This bound gives us the lemma as follows. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}r_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega,S_{i}}\in\mathcal{V}
and

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}r_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega,S_{i}}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\max\big\{0,\|r_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega,S_{i}}\|-\eta S_{i}\big\}+\eta T_{N_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,\|r_{f^{\tau}\omega,s}\|-\eta s\big\}+\eta t,

Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem entails that
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the vector
\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}r_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega,S_{i}}
belongs to
\mathcal{V}\cap F
for all sufficiently large
t
. Thus,
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the condition
\frac{W_{t}}{t}-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}r_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega,S_{i}}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}Y_{\omega,i}\in K
implies that
\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\mathcal{V}\cap F+K
for all sufficiently large
t
.

The bound in (
2.17
) relies on the compactness of
\mathcal{V}\cap F+K
, for which the dimension of
\mathcal{V}
must
be finite. In order to demonstrate this bound, recall that, by
(ii)
of Proposition
2.9
, there exists an
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}

with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that

\inf_{\delta>0}\bigg\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\bigg\}\leq-J_{o}(w)

for all
w\in\mathcal{X}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. Fix
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
\epsilon>0
. Then, for each
w
there exists
\delta_{w}>0
such that
\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta_{w}})\leq-J_{o}(w)+\epsilon
. The number
\delta_{w}
can be chosen so small that
\|\varphi\|\delta_{w}\leq\epsilon
. By compactness, there exist finitely many points
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
in
\mathcal{V}\cap F+K
such that
\mathcal{V}\cap F+K\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}}
. This gives

\displaystyle E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\mathcal{V}\cap F+K,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}},\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w_{i})+t\|\varphi\|\delta_{w_{i}}}\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w_{i})+t\epsilon}\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}}),

and it therefore follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\log E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\mathcal{V}\cap F+K,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\varphi(w_{1})-J_{o}(w_{1}),\ldots,\varphi(w_{n})-J_{o}(w_{n})\Big\}+2\epsilon\leq J_{o}^{\star}(\varphi)+2\epsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\epsilon
demonstrates (
2.17
).
∎

2.5
The full LDP

It is well known that a weak LDP gives a full LDP with good rate function when it is combined with exponential tightness (see
[
16
]
, Lemma 1.2.18). Hence Corollary
1.6
is a consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.12
.

Under the hypotheses of Corollary
1.6
,
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family of measures
\{\mu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is exponentially tight, i.e., for each number
\lambda>0
there exists a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{X}
such that

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(K^{c})\leq-\lambda.

Proof of Lemma
2.12
.

Pick a number
\rho>0
, and let
\xi>0
be as in Corollary
1.6
. For
t\in\mathbb{N}
, a Chernoff-type bound, together with the bound in (
2.1
), gives

\displaystyle\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{0,\rho}^{c})

\displaystyle:=E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{0,\rho}^{c},\,t\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\|X_{i}\|\geq\rho t,\,t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\xi\rho t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\|X_{i}\|+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,v_{f^{\tau}\omega}(s)-\eta s\}+\eta t-\xi\rho t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\|X_{i}\|}\Big].

(2.18)

At the same time, with
M
as in Corollary
1.6
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\omega}

\displaystyle\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{t\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\|X_{i}\|}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{t}\sum_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}}\cdots\sum_{s_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{t_{n}=t\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|}\lambda_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|s_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{Mt}\sum_{n=1}^{t}\sum_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}}\cdots\sum_{s_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{t_{n}=t\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms_{i}}\lambda_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|s_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{Mt}\sum_{n=1}^{t}\sum_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}}\cdots\sum_{s_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathds{1}_{\{t_{n}=t\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(s_{i})\,\mathrm{e}^{\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{f^{t_{i-1}}\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\big\}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{Mt+\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{f^{\tau}\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\big\}}P_{\omega}[t\in\mathcal{T}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{Mt+\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{f^{\tau}\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\big\}},

(2.19)

where
t_{0}:=0
and
t_{i}:=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)-\eta s\}]<+\infty
by Assumption
1.3
and

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\}]<+\infty
by hypothesis, combining (
2.18
) with (
2.19
), taking logarithm, dividing by
t
, and letting
t\uparrow\infty
, we find

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{0,\rho}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)-\eta s\big\}\Big]+\eta-\xi\rho

\displaystyle\qquad+M+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\bigg\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\bigg\}\bigg]\qquad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega

thanks to Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem. In this way, given
\lambda>0
, the lemma is proved by choosing
\rho
so large that the r.h.s. is
smaller than
\lambda
and
K
equal to the closure of
B_{0,\rho}
.
∎

3
Free LDPs

In this section we deduce Theorem
1.8
and Corollary
1.9
from our results on the constrained pinning model. To this aim, we note that, for
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0\leq n\leq\tau\leq t
, the disjoint events
\{T_{n}=\tau,\,T_{n+1}>t\}=\{T_{n}=\tau,\,S_{n+1}>t-\tau\}
exhaust the entire space of configurations. According to (
1.1
), the conditions
T_{n}=\tau
and
S_{n+1}>t-\tau
entail that
H_{\omega,t}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{f^{T_{i-1}}\omega}(S_{i})=H_{\omega,\tau}
and
W_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}=W_{\tau}
are independent of
S_{n+1}
, which in turn is distributed as
S_{1}
in the environment
f^{\tau}\omega
. In this way, we find the identity of measures

\displaystyle\nu_{\omega,t}:=E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\tau}E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,,\,T_{n}=\tau,\,S_{n+1}>t-\tau\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\tau}E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{\tau}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,,\,T_{n}=\tau\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau}}\bigg]P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>t-\tau]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{\tau}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,,\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau}}\bigg]P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>t-\tau].

(3.1)

Similarly, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)}\nu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}w)=E_{\omega}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{t})+H_{\omega,t}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi(W_{\tau})+H_{\omega,\tau}}\Big]P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>t-\tau]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}Z_{\omega,\tau}(\varphi)P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>t-\tau].

(3.2)

These formulas connect the free setting with the constrained setting, and represent the starting point to prove Theorem
1.8
and
Corollary
1.9
.

From now on we assume that (
1.5
) holds. We define the rate function
I_{\ell}
as the Legendre transform of
z_{\ell}:=\max\{z,\ell\}
. Recall that
z=z_{o}
is proper convex and lower semi-continuous. The identity in (
3.2
) immediately gives
(i)
of Theorem
1.8
according to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
,
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)}\nu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}w)=z_{\ell}(\varphi)

\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. Corollary
2.4
states that there exists a set
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi)=z_{o}(\varphi)=z(\varphi)
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. We can choose
\Omega_{o}
so that also (
1.5
) is satisfied for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. Pick
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. Since
\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{t\varphi(w)}\nu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}w)\geq\max\{Z_{\omega,t}(\varphi),P_{\omega}[S_{1}>t]\}

) and

and
\epsilon>0

The proof that the family

3.1
The weak LDP for infinite exponential tail constant

When

, we have

. Thus,

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\ell=-\infty
, then
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the weak LDP with rate function
J_{o}
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

The large deviation lower bound for open sets is immediate from Proposition
2.9
, as
\nu_{\omega,t}(A)\geq\mu_{\omega,t}(A)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
. We verify the large deviation upper bound for compact sets.

According to Corollary
2.4
,
(ii)
of Proposition
2.9
, and the first line of (
1.5
) with
\ell=-\infty
, there exists a set
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
, we have
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log Z_{\omega,t}(0)=z_{o}(0)
with
z_{o}(0)
finite,
\inf_{\delta>0}\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\}\leq-J_{o}(w)
for
w\in\mathcal{X}
, and

\limsup_{s\uparrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{s}\log P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>s]-\frac{\tau}{s}\bigg\}=-\infty.

(3.3)

We will show that, for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
w\in\mathcal{X}
,

\inf_{\delta>0}\bigg\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\bigg\}\leq-J_{o}(w).

(3.4)

This gives the quenched large deviation upper bound for compact sets with rate function
J_{o}
, as in the proof of Proposition
2.9
.

Pick
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
,
w\in\mathcal{X}
, and a real number
\lambda<J_{o}(w)
. Then there exists an

such that
\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,4\eta})<-\lambda

C
that provides the inequalities
\mu_{\omega,\tau}(B_{w,4\eta})\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\tau}
and
Z_{\omega,\tau}(0)\leq C\mathrm{e}^{z_{o}(0)\tau+\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Given
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
such that

\epsilon\|w\|\leq\eta
, the conditions
t/2\leq(1-\epsilon)t<\tau\leq t
and
\frac{W_{\tau}}{t}\in B_{w,\eta}
imply
\frac{W_{\tau}}{\tau}\in B_{w,4\eta}
, since

. Hence it follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\eta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{\tau}}{t}\in B_{w,\eta},\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau}}\bigg]P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>t-\tau]

\displaystyle\leq tC\mathrm{e}^{|z_{o}(0)|t+2t}\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\big\{P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>\epsilon t]\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\big\}+tC\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t+\epsilon|\lambda|t}.

t
, letting

, and invoking (

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\eta})

\leq-\lambda+\epsilon|\lambda|
. Letting
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, this gives
\inf_{\delta>0}\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\}\leq-\lambda
, which proves (
3.4
) thanks to the arbitrariness of
\lambda
.
∎

3.2
The weak LDP for finite exponential tail constant

The following lemma shows that the family
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the quenched large deviation lower bound for open sets
with rate function
I_{\ell}
even when
\ell>-\infty
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
\ell>-\infty
. Then
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the large deviation lower bound with rate function
I_{\ell}
, i.e.,
\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(G)\geq-\inf_{w\in G}I_{\ell}(w)
for all
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
open.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

According to
(i)
of Proposition
2.9
, there exists an event
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that
\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\geq-J_{o}(w)
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
,
w\in\mathcal{X}
, and
\delta>0
. By the second line of (
1.5
), we can even suppose that
\Omega_{o}
satisfies
\liminf_{s\uparrow\infty}\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\{\frac{1}{s}\log P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>s]+\epsilon\frac{\tau}{s}\}\geq\ell
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
with any number
\epsilon>0
. Pick
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. We verify that

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\geq-I_{\ell}(w)

(3.5)

for all
w\in\mathcal{X}
and
\delta>0
, which implies the quenched large deviation lower bound for open sets, as we have seen in the proof of
Proposition
2.9
.

Fix
w\in\mathcal{X}
and
\delta>0
. We will prove that

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\geq-\sup_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z}\Big\{\varphi(w)-\beta z(\varphi)-(1-\beta)\ell\Big\}

(3.6)

for all
\beta\in[0,1]
, where
\mbox{dom}\,z:=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}:z(\varphi)<+\infty\}
is the effective domain of the proper convex lower semi-continuous function
z=z_{o}
. This gives (
3.5
) as follows. The function that maps
(\beta,\varphi)\in[0,1]\times\mbox{dom}\,z
to the real number
\varphi(w)-\beta z(\varphi)-(1-\beta)\ell
is concave and upper semi-continuous with respect to
\varphi
for each fixed
\beta\in[0,1]
, and is convex and continuous with respect to
\beta
for each fixed
\varphi\in\mbox{dom}\,z
. Due to compactness of the closed interval
[0,1]
, Sion’s minimax theorem allows us to exchange the infimum over
\beta\in[0,1]
and the supremum over
\varphi\in\mbox{dom}\,z
, to conclude that

\displaystyle I_{\ell}(w):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\Big\{\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\adjustlimits{\sup}_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z}{\inf}_{\beta\in[0,1]}\Big\{\varphi(w)-\beta z(\varphi)-(1-\beta)\ell\Big\}

\displaystyle=\adjustlimits{\inf}_{\beta\in[0,1]}{\sup}_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z}\Big\{\varphi(w)-\beta z(\varphi)-(1-\beta)\ell\Big\}.

In this way, (
3.6
) demonstrates (
3.5
) after we take the supremum over
\beta
.

We prove (
3.6
), considering the case
\beta>0
first. Pick
\beta\in(0,1]
and denote by
\tau_{t}
the largest integer less than or equal to
\beta t
. Focus on the sufficiently large integers
t
satisfying
\tau_{t}>0
and
\|w\|<\beta\delta t/2
. Then the condition
\frac{W_{\tau_{t}}}{\tau_{t}}\in B_{w/\beta,\delta/2}
implies
\frac{W_{\tau_{t}}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta}
. Indeed, observing that
0\leq t-\tau_{t}/\beta<1/\beta
, if
\|W_{\tau_{t}}-\tau_{t}w/\beta\|<\delta\tau_{t}/2
, then
\|W_{\tau_{t}}-tw\|\leq\|W_{\tau_{t}}-\tau_{t}w/\beta\|+(t-\tau_{t}/\beta)\|w\|<\delta\tau_{t}/2+\|w\|/\beta<\delta t
as
\tau_{t}\leq t
and
\|w\|<\beta\delta t/2
. Thus, keeping only the term corresponding to
\tau=\tau_{t}>0
in the r.h.s. of (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})

\displaystyle\geq E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{\tau_{t}}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta},\,\tau_{t}\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau_{t}}}\bigg]P_{f^{\tau_{t}}\omega}\big[S_{1}>t-\tau_{t}\big]

\displaystyle\geq E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{\tau_{t}}}{\tau_{t}}\in B_{w/\beta,\delta/2},\,\tau_{t}\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau_{t}}}\bigg]P_{f^{\tau_{t}}\omega}\big[S_{1}>t-\tau_{t}\big]

\displaystyle=\mu_{\omega,\tau_{t}}\big(B_{w/\beta,\delta/2}\big)P_{f^{\tau_{t}}\omega}\big[S_{1}>t-\tau_{t}\big].

(3.7)

Since
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\tau_{t}/t=\beta
, we have
\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu_{\omega,\tau_{t}}(B_{w/\beta,\delta/2})\geq-\beta J_{o}(w/\beta)
and

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log P_{f^{\tau_{t}}\omega}\big[S_{1}>t-\tau_{t}\big]\geq(1-\beta)\ell.

The latter limit is trivial for
\beta=1
because
P_{\omega}[S_{1}>0]=1
, whereas for
\beta<1
it follows from the fact that, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log P_{f^{\tau_{t}}\omega}\big[S_{1}>t-\tau_{t}\big]

\displaystyle\geq(1-\beta)\liminf_{s\uparrow\infty}\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{s}\log P_{f^{\tau}\omega}\big[S_{1}>s\big]+\epsilon\frac{\tau}{s}\bigg\}-\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq(1-\beta)\ell-\epsilon.

These arguments, in combination with (
3.7
), show that

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})

\displaystyle\geq-\beta J_{o}(w/\beta)+(1-\beta)\ell

\displaystyle=-\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\big\{\varphi(w)-\beta z_{o}(\varphi)\big\}+(1-\beta)\ell

\displaystyle=-\sup_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z}\Big\{\varphi(w)-\beta z(\varphi)-(1-\beta)\ell\Big\},

which is (
3.6
) under the hypothesis
\beta>0
.

In order to settle the case
\beta=0
, we observe that, as
z
is proper convex and lower semi-continuous, there exist a point
u\in\mathcal{X}
and a real number
c
such that
z(\varphi)\geq\varphi(u)-c
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
(see
[
51
]
, Theorem 2.2.6). For all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that
\epsilon\|u\|<\delta/2
we have
B_{w+\epsilon u,\delta/2}\subseteq B_{w,\delta}
. Thus, the bound (
3.6
) with
\epsilon
in place of
\beta
and
w+\epsilon u
in place of
w
gives

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w+\epsilon u,\delta/2})

\displaystyle\geq-\sup_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z}\Big\{\varphi(w+\epsilon u)-\epsilon z(\varphi)-(1-\epsilon)\ell\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq-\sup_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z}\big\{\varphi(w)-\ell\big\}-\epsilon(c+\ell).

From here we obtain (
3.6
) corresponding to
\beta=0
by letting
\epsilon\downarrow 0
.
∎

In the case
\ell>-\infty
, our derivation of the large deviation upper bound for compact sets requires that
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
is separable. In fact, due to the difficulty to deal with the measure
E_{\omega}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{W_{\tau}}{t}\in\,\boldsymbol{\cdot}\,,\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau}}\big]
in (
3.1
) when
\tau
grows slower than
t
, we resort to an estimate based on the cumulant generating function. The latter needs that
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
is not too large in order to ensure uniformity with respect to the disorder. The following lemma shows what the separability of

\mathcal{X}^{\star}
is needed for.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
is separable, then there exists a countable subset
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{X}^{\star}
such that, for
w\in\mathcal{X}
,

I_{\ell}(w)=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{G}}\big\{\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)\big\}.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

By hypothesis, there exists a countable dense subset
\mathcal{S}^{\star}
of
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. Denote by
\mathbb{Q}
the collection of rational numbers and, for
\vartheta\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, define

\delta_{\vartheta,\alpha}:=\inf_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z_{\ell}}\max\Big\{\|\vartheta-\varphi\|,|\alpha-z_{\ell}(\varphi)|\Big\},

where
\mbox{dom}\,z_{\ell}:=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}:z_{\ell}(\varphi)<+\infty\}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, pick
g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n}\in\mbox{dom}\,z_{\ell}
in such a way that
\max\{\|\vartheta-g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n}\|,|\alpha-z_{\ell}(g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n})|\}\leq\delta_{\vartheta,\alpha}+\frac{1}{n}
. The countable family
\mathcal{G}:=\{g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n}\}_{\vartheta\in\mathcal{S}^{\star},\alpha\in\mathbb{Q},n\in\mathbb{N}}
has the following useful property: for any
\varphi\in\mbox{dom}\,z_{\ell}
and
\epsilon>0
there exists a
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that

\max\{\|\varphi-g\|,|z_{\ell}(\varphi)-z_{\ell}(g)|\}<3\epsilon.

In fact, given
\varphi\in\mbox{dom}\,z_{\ell}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\vartheta\in\mathcal{S}^{\star}
such that
\|\varphi-\vartheta\|<\epsilon
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
|z_{\ell}(\varphi)-\alpha|<\epsilon
. It follows that
\delta_{\vartheta,\alpha}<\epsilon
. Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

\frac{1}{n}<\epsilon
, we find

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\|\varphi-g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n}\|,|z_{\ell}(\varphi)-z_{\ell}(g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n})|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\|\varphi-\vartheta\|,|z_{\ell}(\varphi)-\alpha)|\Big\}

\displaystyle+\max\Big\{\|\vartheta-g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n}\|,|\alpha-z_{\ell}(g_{\vartheta,\alpha,n})|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\|\varphi-\vartheta\|,|z_{\ell}(\varphi)-\alpha)|\Big\}+\delta_{\vartheta,\alpha}+\frac{1}{n}<3\epsilon.

The family
\mathcal{G}
is the desired countable subset of
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. Indeed, for
w\in\mathcal{X}
,

I_{\ell}(w)=\sup_{\varphi\in\scriptsize{\mbox{dom}\,}z_{\ell}}\big\{\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)\big\}\leq\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{G}}\big\{\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)\big\},

(3.8)

and hence
I_{\ell}(w)=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{G}}\{\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)\}
. To prove (
3.8
), we note that, given
w\in\mathcal{X}
and real numbers
\lambda\leq I_{\ell}(w)
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\varphi\in\mbox{dom}\,z_{\ell}
such that
\lambda\leq\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)+\epsilon
. Hence there exists a
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\max\{\|\varphi-g\|,|z_{\ell}(\varphi)-z_{\ell}(g)|\}<3\epsilon
, as seen above. It follows that

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq g(w)-z_{\ell}(g)+\|\varphi-g\|\|w\|+|z_{\ell}(\varphi)-z_{\ell}(g)|+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq g(w)-z_{\ell}(g)+3\epsilon\|w\|+4\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{G}}\big\{\varphi(w)-z_{\ell}(\varphi)\big\}+3\epsilon\|w\|+4\epsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\lambda
and
\epsilon
demonstrates (
3.8
).
∎

We are now in a position to deduce the large deviation upper bound for compact sets, obtaining the following proposition, which is

(ii)
of Theorem
1.8
for
\ell>-\infty
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
\ell>-\infty
and
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
is separable. Then
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the weak
LDP with rate function
I_{\ell}
.

Proof of Proposition
3.5
.

In view of Lemma
3.3
, it remains to verify the quenched large deviation upper bound for compact sets. Let
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{X}^{\star}
be the countable set in Lemma
3.4
. By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a set
\Omega_{o}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{o}]=1
such that
\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{tg(v)}\nu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}v)=z_{\ell}(g)
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Pick
\omega\in\Omega_{o}
. Below we show that

\inf_{\delta>0}\bigg\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\bigg\}\leq z_{\ell}(g)-g(w)

(3.9)

for all
w\in\mathcal{X}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}
. This gives
\inf_{\delta>0}\{\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})\}\leq-I_{\ell}(w)
for every
w
by Lemma
3.4
, which in turn demonstrates the large deviation upper bound for compact sets as in the proof of Proposition
2.9
.

Given
w\in\mathcal{X}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
, and
\delta>0
, the bound
g(v)-g(w)\geq-\|g\|\|v-w\|\geq-\|g\|\delta
for
v\in B_{w,\delta}
implies, for
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{w,\delta})=\int_{B_{w,\delta}}\nu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}v)\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{tg(v)-tg(w)+t\|g\|\delta}\nu_{\omega,t}(\mathrm{d}v).

Taking logarithm, dividing by
t
, letting first
t\uparrow\infty
and afterwards
\delta\downarrow 0
, we find (
3.9
).
∎

3.3
The full LDP

One of the hypothesis of Corollary
1.9
is that
\mathcal{X}
is finite-dimensional. In particular, this implies that
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
is separable, so that
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family of measures
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the weak LDP with rate function
I_{\ell}
when either
\ell=-\infty
or
\ell>-\infty
. Thus, similarly to Corollary
1.6
, the full LDP stated by Corollary
1.9
is a consequence of the following quenched
exponential tightness.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under the hypotheses of Corollary
1.6
,
\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega
the family of measures
\{\nu_{\omega,t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is exponentially tight.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Pick a number
\rho>0
, and let
\xi>0
be as in Corollary
1.6
. For
t\in\mathbb{N}
, a Chernoff-type bound, together with the identity
in (
3.1
) and the bound in (
2.1
), gives

\displaystyle\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{0,\rho}^{c})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{W_{\tau}}{t}\in B_{0,\rho}^{c},\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}\big\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau}}\bigg]P_{f^{\tau}\omega}[S_{1}>t-\tau]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\tau}}\|X_{i}\|\geq\rho t,\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{H_{\omega,\tau}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\xi\rho t}\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\tau}}\|X_{i}\|+H_{\omega,\tau}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,v_{f^{\tau}\omega}(s)-\eta s\}+\eta t-\xi\rho t}\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\tau}}\|X_{i}\|}\Big].

(3.10)

On the other hand, with
M
as in Corollary
1.6
, (
2.19
) implies

\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}E_{\omega}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\tau}}\|X_{i}\|}\Big]\leq(t+1)\,\mathrm{e}^{Mt+\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{f^{\tau}\omega}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\big\}}.

(3.11)

Combining (
3.10
) with (
3.11
), taking the logarithm, dividing by
t
, and letting
t\uparrow\infty
, we find

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu_{\omega,t}(B_{0,\rho}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\big\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)-\eta s\big\}\Big]+\eta-\xi\rho

\displaystyle\qquad+M+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\bigg\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\bigg\}\bigg]\qquad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.e.\ }\omega

(3.12)

thanks to Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem. Since
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,v_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(s)-\eta s\}]<+\infty
by Assumption
1.3
and
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{0,\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi\|x\|-Ms}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(\mathrm{d}x|s)\}]<+\infty
by hypothesis, given
\lambda>0
, the lemma follows by choosing
\rho
so large that the r.h.s. of (
3.12
) is smaller than
\lambda
and
K
is equal to the closure of
B_{0,\rho}
.
∎

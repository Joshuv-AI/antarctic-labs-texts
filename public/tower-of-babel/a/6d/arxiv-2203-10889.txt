ON CONJUGATION INVARIANT NORMS, ASYMPTOTIC CONES, METRIC ULTRAPRODUCTS AND CONTRACTIBILITY
BASTIEN KARLHOFER

Abstract
In the present paper we prove lemmata on strong contractibility in asymptotic cones and metric ultraproducts which we apply to both the case of finitely generated word norms and the case of conjugation invariant norms. We recover classically known contractibility results on free products and prove the contractibility of the asymptotic cone of the infinite symmetric group S ∞ \Sym_{\infty} equipped with a conjugation invariant norm. Furthermore, we give examples of contractible metric ultraproducts arising from subgroups of general linear groups. Additionally, we discuss algebraic properties of groups arising as asymptotic cones for conjugation invariant norms. For example, we show that the asymptotic cone of the infinite symmetric group is itself an algebraically simple group relating strongly to the universally sophic groups defined by Elek and Szabo.



1
Introduction

The study of asymptotic cones on a given group
G
originates from work of Gromov in
[
Gro81
]
to prove that groups of polynomial growth are virtually nilpotent. Other notable early work on asymptotic cones includes
[
dDW84
]
. Since then the study of asymptotic cones and their properties has become a central topic in geometric group theory with asymptotic cones encoding rich information about the underlying structure of the group
G
.

In general, the asymptotic cone depends strongly on the particular choice of ultrafilter. It is shown in
[
KSTT05
]
that the size of the continuum is the maximal number of pairwise non-isometric asymptotic cones for a finitely generated group if the continuum hypothesis is true. If the continuum hypothesis is not true, then
[
KSTT05
]
shows that
\SL(n,\mathbb{R})
for
n\geq 3
is an example of a group with
2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}
pairwise non-homeomorphic asymptotic cones. An example of a finitely presented group with two non-homeomorphic asymptotic cones that is independent of the continuum hypothesis is given in
[
OS07
, Thm. 1.1]
.

It has first been noted in
[
CZ11
, Prop. 3.3]
that the multiplication of representative sequences turns the asymptotic cone into a group whenever the underlying metric is bi-invariant. In the context of symplectic geometry where the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphism carries a naturally bi-invariant norm, called the Hofer norm, asymptotic cones have been used by Álvarez-Gavela et Al. in
[
AKKK19
]
to study embedding questions of the free group.

The growth behaviour of conjugacy classes has been a classical field of study. This growth behaviour is analysed in the theory of finite simple groups under the term covering number (see for example
[
LP97
]
,
[
LS01
]
) and has been studied more recently under the terminology of (strong/uniform) bounded generation in algebraic groups
[
KLM21
]
.

The construction of asymptotic cones for a sequence of spaces
X_{n}
and metric ultraproducts are very closely related. However, in the latter case it is common practice to consider spaces
X_{n}
of finite diameter and normalise the norms such that
\diam(X_{n})=1
for all
n
, which makes every sequence of elements admissible. Background on the study of metric ultraproducts for sequences of classical groups can be found in
[
TS14
]
,
[
TW14
]
and
[
Wil17
]
. In
[
TW14
]
it is shown that metric ultraproducts of finite simple groups are simple, thus extending the results of Elek and Szabo in
[
ES05
]
.

In Section 2 we discuss preliminaries on conjugation invariant norms, asymptotic cones on groups and recall that asymptotic cones form groups themselves if the underlying metrics are bi-invariant. For completeness, we prove that the classical study of finitely generated word norms and word norms generating by finitely many conjugacy classes has quasi-isometrically trivial intersection. Moreover, we give an example of a word norm associated to an infinite generating set on the integers and show that the asymptotic cone with respect to this word norm has non-trivial 2-torsion.

Proposition

(
2.34
)
.

The asymptotic cone
\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z},\|.\|_{S})
contains non-trivial elements of order two. In particular,
\Cone_{\omega}(Z,\|.\|_{S})

are not isomorphic.

This example visualises that passing to an infinite set of conjugacy classes can create very wild asymptotic behaviour.

In Section 4 we prove a general contraction lemma for the asymptotic cone in Lemma

that we apply to direct sums, the infinite symmetric group and free products of groups. As an application to the infinite symmetric group we obtain the following theorem.

(

In Section 5 we turn to algebraic properties of the asymptotic cones of the infinite symmetric group

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Basics on conjugation invariant word norms

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group. A map
\psi\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a
quasimorphism
if there exists a constant
D\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle|\psi(gh)-\psi(g)-\psi(h)|\leq D\hskip 2.84526pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84526ptg,h\in G.

The
defect
of
\psi
is defined to be the smallest number
D(\psi)
with the above property.
A quasimorphism is
homogeneous
if it satisfies
\psi(g^{n})=n\psi(g)
for all
g\in G
and all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By induction we have
|\psi(g_{1}\cdots g_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi(g_{i})|\leq(n-1)D
for all
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in G
. It follows from the definition that sums and scalar multiples of (homogeneous) quasimorphisms are (homogeneous) quasimorphisms again. It is immediate that homogeneous quasimorphisms vanish on elements of finite order and are unbounded if there exists an element
g\in G
such that
\psi(g)\neq 0
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\psi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
be a quasimorphism. The
homogenisation

\bar{\psi}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
of
\psi
is defined to be
\bar{\psi}(g)=\lim_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\psi(g^{n})}{n}
for all
g\in G
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Cal09
, p.18]
)
.

The homogenisation
\bar{\psi}
of a quasimorphism
\psi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
is a homogeneous quasimorphism. Moreover, it satisfies
|\bar{\psi}(g)-\psi(g)|\leq D(\psi)
for any
g\in G
.

Homogeneous quasimorphisms are constant on conjugacy classes by
[
Cal09
, p.19]
. A perfect group
G
is called
uniformly perfect
if there exists a constant
K
such that every
g\in G
can be expressed as a product of at most
K
commutators. It follows by elementary calculation, that a uniformly perfect group
G
does not admit an unbounded quasimorphism.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
\nu\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
norm
on
G
if
\nu
satisfies for all
g,h\in G
that

•

\nu(g)\geq 0
,

•

\nu(g)=0
if and only if
g=1
,

•

\nu(gh)\leq\nu(g)+\nu(h)
,

If in addition
\nu
satisfies for all
g,h\in G

•

\nu(hgh^{-1}))=\nu(g)
,

then it is called
conjugation invariant
. The supremum
\nu(G)=\sup\{\nu(g)\mid g\in G\}
is called the
diameter
of the norm
\nu
. If
\nu(G)=\infty
, then
\nu
is called
unbounded
. If there exists
g\in G
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\nu(g^{n})}{n}>0
, then
\nu
is called
stably unbounded
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
G
be a group together with a generating set
S
. The
word norm
generated by
S
is the norm on
G
defined by

\displaystyle\nu_{S}(g)=\min\{n\mid g=s_{1}\cdots s_{n}\hskip 2.84526pt\text{where}\hskip 2.84526ptn\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 2.84526pt\text{and}\hskip 2.84526pts_{i}\in S\hskip 2.84526pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84526pti\}.

If we assume additionally that the set
S
is invariant under conjugation, then
\nu_{S}
is a conjugation invariant norm.

Note that, if
H
is a subgroup of
G
and a normal generating set
S
of
H
normally generates
G
as well, the norms generated by
S
on
H
and on
G
can differ significantly from one another. One example is
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
inside
SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
for
n\geq 3
. Then
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
generates all higher
SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
under conjugation, but
SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
is bounded for
n\geq 3
(see for example
[
Mor07
]
, originally due to
[
CK83
]
). However,
\SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
admits unbounded conjugation invariant norms since it possesses many unbounded quasimorphisms. In fact,
\SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
even admits unbounded quasimorphisms that are invariant under all automorphisms by
[
Kar21
, Example 6.6]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Two conjugation invariant norms
\|.\|_{1}
and
\|.\|_{2}
are called
equivalent
if there exist Lipschitz-constants
A,B>0
such that
\|.\|_{1}\leq A\|.\|_{2}
and
\|.\|_{2}\leq B\|.\|_{1}
. Furthermore,
\|.\|_{1}
and
\|.\|_{2}
are
coarsely equivalent
if there exist constants
A>0
and
B\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\|.\|_{1}-B\leq\|.\|_{2}\leq A\|.\|_{1}+B

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
S\subset G
be a generating set and
\nu_{S}
be the conjugation invariant word norm defined by conjugacy classes of
S
. Then any other conjugation invariant norm
\mu\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for which
C_{\mu}=\sup_{s\in S}\|s\|<\infty
satisfies
\mu\leq C_{\mu}\nu_{S}
. In particular, this always holds if
S
is finite.

Proof.

Given
g\in G
with
\nu_{S}(g)=n
we write
g=s_{1}\cdots s_{n}
for
s_{i}
conjugates of elements in
S
and calculate

\displaystyle\mu(g)=\mu(s_{1}\cdots s_{n})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu(s_{i})\leq nC_{\mu}=C_{\mu}\nu(g).

∎

Definition 2.8
.

A group
G
is called
bounded
if
G
does not admit a conjugation invariant norm that has infinite diameter.

By Lemma
2.7
any finitely normally generated group
G
is bounded if there exists one word norm generated by finitely many conjugacy classes that has finite diameter on
G
.
Another immediate consequence of Lemma
2.7
is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

On a group
G
any two generating sets
S
and
R
consisting of finitely many conjugacy classes define equivalent conjugation invariant word norms
\nu_{S}
and
\nu_{R}
. ∎

This means that although generating sets consisting of finitely many conjugacy classes usually have infinite cardinality, there is an analogue of the classic statement that any two finite generating sets on a group
G
will yield quasi-isometric Cayley graphs. However, if one relaxes this assumption further to allow generating sets
S
consisting of infinitely many conjugacy classes, many problems arise even if the group remains unbounded with respect to
S
. A particularly pathological example is given in Proposition
2.34
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\psi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
be a quasimorphism with unbounded image, but bounded on a generating set
S
of
G
. Then the word norm
\|.\|_{S}
is stably unbounded on
G
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
we can assume that
\psi
is homogeneous. Let
K
be a positive bound for the absolute value of
\psi
on
S
. Write
g\in G
as a product of these generators
g=s_{1}\cdots s_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
where
s_{i}\in S
. Then

\displaystyle|\psi(g)|=|\psi(s_{1}\cdots s_{n})|\leq|\psi(s_{1})|+\dots+|\psi(s_{n})|\hskip-0.28453pt+(n-1)D(\psi)\leq n(K+D(\psi))

shows that
\|g\|_{S}\geq\frac{|\psi(g)|}{K+D(\psi)}
for all
g\in G
. Since
\psi
is homogeneous,
\|g^{k}\|_{S}\geq k\cdot\frac{|\psi(g)|}{K+D(\psi)}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
g\in G
. Since
\psi
does not vanish everywhere,
\|.\|_{S}
is stably unbounded on
G
.
∎

2.2
Conjugation invariant norms on the infinite symmetric group

Define the infinite symmetric group
\Sym_{\infty}
to be the colimit over the inclusions of the symmetric groups
\Sym_{n}\hookrightarrow\Sym_{n+1}
. That is,
\Sym_{\infty}
is the group of finitely supported permutations of the natural numbers. Analogously, define the infinite alternating group
\A_{\infty}
as the group of finitely supported alternating permutations. Since
\A_{n}
is simple for any
n
it follows that the colimit
\A_{\infty}
, which has index 2 in
\Sym_{\infty}
, is as well. Recall, that every permutation has a unique
cycle decomposition
. We follow the classical convention that in cycle notation cycles are performed from
left to right
, that is
(x\kern 5.0pty)(y\kern 5.0ptz)=(x\kern 5.0ptz\kern 5.0pty)
.

Definition 2.11
.

Define the support norm
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
on
\Sym_{\infty}
and
\A_{\infty}
by

\displaystyle\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}=\begin{cases}|\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\kern 5.0pt\sigma(n)\neq n\}|&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\sigma\neq id,\\
0&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\sigma=id.\end{cases}

It is elementary to verify that this defines a conjugation invariant norm on both
\Sym_{\infty}
and
\A_{\infty}
. Moreover, the inclusion
\A_{\infty}\hookrightarrow\Sym_{\infty}
is an isometric embedding and quasi-surjective with constant 1 with respect to the support norms. Hence, it will follow in Proposition
2.26
that
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})=\Cone_{\omega}(A_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
.

Remark 2.12
.

If one considers
\Sym_{\infty}
as a subgroup of the group
\GL_{\infty}(k)
arising as the colimit over the finite general linear groups for any field
k
, then the restriction of the rank norm defined by
\|g\|_{\rk}=\rk(g-id)
for any
g\in GL_{\infty}
is equivalent to the support norm on
\Sym_{\infty}
. In fact, it is clear that for any
\sigma\in\Sym_{\infty}
we have
\|\sigma\|_{\rk}\leq\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}
and Lemma
5.4
can be used to obtain
\|.\|_{\rk}\geq\frac{1}{3}\|.\|_{\rm supp}
. This implies the equivalence of the norms
\|.\|_{\rk}
and
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
on
\Sym_{\infty}
.

Definition 2.13
.

Define the transposition norm
\|.\|_{tr}\colon\Sym_{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
to be the word norm generated by the set
T
consisting of all transpositions. Since
T
consist of a single conjugacy class in
\Sym_{\infty}
, the norm
\|.\|_{tr}
is conjugation invariant.

Lemma 2.14
.

The two norms
\|.\|_{tr}
and
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
on
S_{\infty}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|.\|_{tr}\leq\|.\|_{\rm supp}\leq 2\|.\|_{tr}.

Consequently,
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
is equivalent to any word norm
\nu
on
\Sym_{\infty}
that is generated by finitely many conjugacy classes.

Proof.

Since the support of every transposition equals 2, the second inequality is immediate. We now prove
\|.\|_{tr}\leq\|.\|_{\rm supp}
inductively on the size of the support. Let
\sigma\in S_{\infty}
with
\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}=n
be given. If
n\leq 1
then
\sigma
is in fact the identity and the inequality holds trivially. Now assume
n>1
. Let
x\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\sigma(x)=y\neq x
. and let
z=\sigma^{-1}(x)
. Then
z\neq x
. Recall, that we use the convention of multiplying permutations from left to right, which mean first the left one, then the right one. If
z=y
, write
\sigma=(x\kern 5.0pty)\sigma^{\prime}
for some
\sigma^{\prime}
with
\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{\rm supp}\leq n-2
and apply the induction hypothesis. If
z\neq y
, consider
(y\kern 5.0ptz)\sigma
which has the property that it maps
y
to
x
and
x
to
y
whence
(y\kern 5.0ptz)\sigma=(x\kern 5.0pty)\sigma^{\prime}
for some
\sigma^{\prime}
with
\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{\rm supp}\leq n-2
which shows by induction that
\|\sigma\|_{tr}=\|(y\kern 5.0ptz)(x\kern 5.0pty)\sigma^{\prime}\|_{tr}\leq 2+n-2=n
. Therefore,
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
and
\|.\|_{tr}
are equivalent and the result follows from Lemma
2.9
.
∎

Definition 2.15
.

Any two 3-cycles are conjugate in
\A_{\infty}
and the set of 3-cycles normally generates
\A_{\infty}
. Define
\|.\|_{3}
to denote the conjugation invariant word norm on
\A_{\infty}
generated by the single conjugacy class of 3-cycles.

The following lemma verifies that under the restriction of conjugation invariant norms to the subgroup
\A_{\infty}
of
\Sym_{\infty}
we still stay within the equivalence class of word norms generated by finitely many conjugacy classes. Hence, by
[
DK18
, Lemma 10.83]
, which will be recalled in Proposition
2.26
, it will follow that all asymptotic cones of
\A_{\infty}
and
\Sym_{\infty}
are Lipschitz equivalent as metric spaces and isomorphic as groups.

Lemma 2.16
.

The two norms
\|.\|_{3}
and
\|.\|_{tr}
on
A_{\infty}
are equivalent satisfying
\|.\|_{tr}\leq 2\|.\|_{3}
and
\|.\|_{3}\leq\frac{3}{2}\|.\|_{tr}
. Moreover,
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
is equivalent to any word norm
\nu
that is generated by finitely many conjugacy classes on
\A_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Given
\sigma\in\A_{\infty}
with
\|\sigma\|_{3}=n
. Write
\sigma=c_{1}...c_{n}
as a product of 3-cycles
c_{i}
and calculate:

\|\sigma\|_{tr}=\|c_{1}...c_{n}\|_{tr}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}\|_{tr}\leq 2n=2\|\sigma\|_{3}\hskip 2.84526pt,

where we just used that any 3-cycle is the product of two transpositions.

For the other inequality we first recall that
the product of any two transpositions
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
satisfies
\|\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\|_{3}\leq 3
. Namely, since every transposition has support norm equal to two, we have that if the support of the two transpositions is equal, then their product is the identity. If their supports do not agree and are not disjoint either then the product yields a 3-cycle. If the supports are disjoint then we can write
\tau_{1}=(x_{1}\kern 5.0pty_{1})
and
\tau_{2}=(x_{2}\kern 5.0pty_{2})
and pick
z\in\mathbb{N}
different from all of them. We have that

\displaystyle\tau_{1}\tau_{2}=(x_{1}\kern 5.0pty_{1})(x_{2}\kern 5.0pty_{2})=(z\kern 5.0pty_{1}\kern 5.0ptx_{1})(x_{2}\kern 5.0ptz\kern 5.0pty_{1})(y_{2}\kern 5.0ptx_{2}\kern 5.0ptz)

is a product of three 3-cycles. So now suppose that
we are given
\sigma\in\A_{\infty}
with
\|\sigma\|_{tr}=2n
. Then
\sigma=\tau_{1}...\tau_{2n}
and the following calculation shows the other inequality

\|\sigma\|_{3}=\|\tau_{1}...\tau_{2n}\|_{3}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\tau_{2i-1}\tau_{2i}\|_{3}\leq 3n=\frac{3}{2}\|\sigma\|_{tr}\hskip 2.84526pt.

The second statement follows from the equivalence of
\|.\|_{tr}
and
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
on
\Sym_{\infty}
together with the fact that all word norms on
\A_{\infty}
generated by finitely many conjugacy classes are equivalent by Lemma
2.9
.
∎

2.3
Ultrafilters

Definition 2.17
.

Let
I
be a set. A
filter

\omega
on
I
is a set
\omega
consisting of subsets of
I
such that

(i)

\emptyset\notin\omega
,

(ii)

A\subset B\subset I
and
A\in\mathfrak{B}
implies
B\in\omega
,

(iii)

A,B\in\omega
implies
A\cap B\in\omega
.

The filter
\omega
is called an
ultrafilter
if it additionally satisfies

(iv)

\forall A\subset I
either
A\in\omega
or
(I\setminus A)\in\omega
.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter on the set
I
and let
Y
be a topological space. Let
f\colon I\rightarrow Y
be a function. Then a point
y\in Y
is called ultralimit of the function
f
, denoted by
y=\lim_{\omega}f(i)
if it satisfies that for all
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\{i\in I\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054ptd(f(i),y)<\epsilon\}\in\omega.

From now onwards we will always take
I=\mathbb{N}
, identify the function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to Y
with its image sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
and just write
y=\lim_{\omega}x_{n}
for the ultralimit.

Remark 2.19
.

Given
p\in I
one can define an ultrafilter by setting
A\in\omega
iff
p\in A
. Filters arising in this way are called principal. For such a princial ultrafilter the limit of every function
f\colon I\to Y
is the image
f(p)
(compare
[
DK18
, p.339]
).

We will require all ultrafilters to be
non-principal
from now onwards. The following properties of ultralimits are well-known (see e.g.
[
DK18
, p.338]
).

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and let
Y
be a compact metric space. Then:

(i)

For every function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow Y
the ultralimit exists and is unique ,

(ii)

For any convergent function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow Y
the ultralimit agrees with the usual limit,

(iii)

The ultralimit of any function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow Y
is an accumulation point of
f
,

(iv)

If
g\colon Y\rightarrow Z
is a continuous function to a metric space
Z
then for every
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow Y
it holds that

\lim_{\omega}g(f(i))=g\big(\lim_{\omega}f(i)\big)
.

Moreover, ultralimits satisfy the usual rules of calculus
[
DK18
, p.338]
. For example, let
(a_{n})_{n},(b_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}
be two sequences such that
\lim_{\omega}a_{n}
and
\lim_{\omega}b_{n}
are both real numbers. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}a_{n}+b_{n}

\displaystyle=\big(\lim_{\omega}a_{n}\big)+\big(\lim_{\omega}b_{n}\big),

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}a_{n}b_{n}=\big(\lim_{\omega}a_{n}\big)\cdot\big(\lim_{\omega}b_{n}\big).

Also, if two sequences
(x_{n})_{n},(y_{n})_{n}\subset Y
agree for a subset of indices
A\in\omega
, then the ultralimits
\lim_{\omega}x_{n}=\lim_{\omega}y_{n}
agree. As an example of how to work with ultralimits, let us very they respect closed inequalities on
\omega
-heavy subsets.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
(a_{n}),(b_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
be two sequences of real numbers. Assume there is a set
A\in\omega
such that for all
n\in A
we have
a_{n}\leq b_{n}
. Then it holds that
\lim_{\omega}a_{n}\leq\lim_{\omega}b_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
a=\lim_{\omega}a_{n}
and
b=\lim_{\omega}b_{n}
. Assume that
a>b
and both are real numbers. Then set
\epsilon=\min\{\frac{a-b}{2},1\}
. By the definition of ultralimits the sets

\displaystyle A_{\epsilon}=\{n\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pt|a_{n}-a|<\epsilon\}\hskip 5.69054pt,\hskip 5.69054ptB_{\epsilon}=\{n\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pt|b_{n}-b|<\epsilon\}

both lie in
\omega
. We have
a-\epsilon\geq b+\epsilon
and since ultrafilters are closed under taking supersets we conclude

\displaystyle\{n\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pt|a_{n}-a|<\epsilon\}\subset\{n\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pta_{n}\geq b+\epsilon\}\in\omega.

Intersection of the latter set with
A
yields

\displaystyle B=\{n\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526ptb_{n}\geq a_{n}\geq b+\epsilon\}\in\omega.

However, this implies
\emptyset=B\cap B_{\epsilon}\in\omega
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
a\leq b
.

If
a=\infty
then for all
C>0
we have
A_{C}=\{n\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pta_{n}\geq C\}\in\omega
. Hence
A\cap A_{C}\in\omega
which yields
B_{C}=\{n\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526ptb_{n}\geq C\}\in\omega
. Thus
b=a=\infty
and the statement follows.
∎

2.4
Asymptotic Cones

Let us recall the construction of an
asymptotic cone
of a sequence of metric spaces
(X_{n},d_{n})
. The closely related construction of
metric ultraproducts
is stated in the beginning of Section 6 to keep notation consistent for now.

Pick an ultrafilter
\omega
and a scaling sequence
(s_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\lim_{\omega}s_{n}=\infty
. Pick a sequence of basepoints
p_{n}
. Let
X_{n}^{b}(\omega,s_{n},p_{n})
be the set of sequences
(x_{n})\subset\in X_{n}
such that the sequence
\left(\frac{d_{n}(x_{n},p_{n})}{s_{n}}\right)
is bounded. Any sequence that belongs to
X_{n}^{b}(\omega,s_{n},p_{n})
is called
admissible
. Then an
asymptotic cone
is defined to be the metric space

\displaystyle\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n})=X_{n}^{b}(\omega,s_{n},p_{n})/\sim

with
(x_{n})\sim(y_{n})
if
\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})}{s_{n}}=0
. The equivalence class of an admissible sequence
(x_{n})
is denoted by
[x_{n}]
. An asymptotic cone carries the induced metric arising as the ultralimit
d([x_{n}],[y_{n}])=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})}{s_{n}}
.

The asymptotic cone depends on the particular choice of ultrafilter independently of the continuum hypothesis
[
OOS09
, Thm 1.1]
. For a single metric space
(X_{n},d_{n})=(X,d)
the space
\Cone_{\omega}(X,s_{n},p_{n})
is called an asymptotic cone of
(X,d)
. Our canonical choice of scaling sequence will be
s_{n}=n
and in this case we will omit the scaling sequence from the notation. Moreover, if
p_{n}=p\in X
is a fixed basepoint then the asymptotic cone is independent of the particular choice of fixed basepoint.

Iterating the construction of an asymptotic cone just amounts to a change of ultrafilter
[
DK18
, Cor. 10.79]
. In the case of group equipped with norms the homogeneity of the spaces makes the construction of an asymptotic cone independent from the choice of basepoint by the following Lemma
2.22
(compare
[
DK18
, Exercise 10.66]
). Thus, basepoints will be suppressed in the notation after this lemma. Lemma
2.22
and
2.23
should be well-known, but are recalled here for completeness.

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
G_{n}
be a sequence of groups together with norms
\|.\|_{n}
. Let
s_{n}
be a scaling sequence and
p_{n}
be a sequence of elements in
G
. Then

\displaystyle\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},s_{n},p_{n})\cong\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},s_{n},1)

are isometric.

Proof.

Indeed, the right invariance of a metric induced by a norm means that we can just use the group multiplication to translate one sequence into another.

Spelled out in more detail this means that we define maps

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\colon\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},s_{n},1)\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},s_{n},p_{n}),

\displaystyle[x_{n}]\rightarrow[x_{n}p_{n}],

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\colon\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},s_{n},p_{n})\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},s_{n},1),

\displaystyle[y_{n}]\rightarrow[y_{n}p_{n}^{-1}].

Then,
d_{n}(\varphi(x_{n}),p_{n})=\|x_{n}p_{n}p_{n}^{-1}\|_{n}=\|x_{n}\|_{n}=d_{n}(x_{n},1)
and
d_{n}(\psi(y_{n}),1)=\|y_{n}p_{n}^{-1}\|=d_{n}(y_{n},p_{n})
, which shows that both maps are well defined on the level of sequences. Similarly, it is immediate from

d_{n}(\varphi(x_{n}),\varphi(z_{n}))=\|x_{n}p_{n}(z_{n}p_{n})^{-1}\|_{n}=\|x_{n}z_{n}^{-1}\|_{n}=d_{n}(x_{n},z_{n})

that
\varphi
is an isometry and analogously for
\psi
. Consequently, both maps are well defined and continuous on the respective asymptotic cones. By definition
\varphi
and
\psi
are inverse to one another.
∎

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
(X_{n},d_{n},p_{n})
be a sequence of pointed metric spaces. Let
(s_{n}),(r_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be two scaling sequences with
\lim_{\omega}\frac{s_{n}}{r_{n}}=K
with
K\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then
\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n})
and
\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},r_{n},p_{n})
are bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic with Lipschitz constants
K
and
\frac{1}{K}
. In particular, for
K=1
the cones
\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n})
and
\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},r_{n},p_{n})
are isometric.

Proof.

First, let
A=\{n|\frac{s_{n}}{r_{n}}\in[\frac{K}{2},2K]\}
. Since
\lim_{\omega}\frac{s_{n}}{r_{n}}=K
, it holds by definition of the ultralimit that
A\in\omega
.
Let
[g_{n}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n})
and
C_{g}
be such that
d_{n}(g_{n},p_{n})\leq C_{g}s_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Define

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}g_{n},n\in A,\\
p_{n},n\notin A.\end{cases}

Then for
n\notin A
we have
d_{n}(g_{n}^{\prime},p_{n})=0
and for
n\in A
we have

\displaystyle d_{n}(g_{n}^{\prime},p_{n})=d_{n}(g_{n},p_{n})\leq C_{g}s_{n}=C_{g}\frac{s_{n}}{r_{n}}r_{n}\leq 2KC_{g}r_{n}.

So, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that
d_{n}(g_{n}^{\prime},p_{n})\leq 2KC_{g}r_{n}
, which means that
[g_{n}^{\prime}]
yields an element in
\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},r_{n},p_{n})
. Define

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n})\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},r_{n},p_{n})\hskip 14.22636pt\text{by}\hskip 14.22636pt\varphi([g_{n}])=[g_{n}^{\prime}].

Claim
.

The map
\varphi
is well-defined an Lipschitz continuous with constant
K
.

Let
[g_{n}],[h_{n}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n})
. Then, by Lemma
2.21
and the product rule of ultralimits it holds that

\displaystyle d(\varphi([g_{n}]),\varphi([h_{n}]))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(g_{n}^{\prime},h_{n}^{\prime})}{r_{n}}\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(g_{n},h_{n})}{r_{n}}=\lim_{\omega}\left(\frac{d_{n}(g_{n},h_{n})}{s_{n}}\cdot\frac{s_{n}}{r_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(g_{n},h_{n})}{s_{n}}\right)\cdot\left(\lim_{\omega}\frac{s_{n}}{r_{n}}\right)=Kd([g_{n}],[h_{n}]).

In particular,
\varphi
does not depend on the particular choice of representative sequence.

Conversely, define for
[g_{n}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},r_{n},p_{n})
the sequence

\displaystyle\widehat{g_{n}}=\begin{cases}g_{n},n\in A,\\
p_{n},n\notin A.\end{cases}

Let
D_{g}
be such that
d_{n}(g_{n},p_{n})\leq D_{g}r_{n}
for all
n
. Then, for all
n\in A
it holds that

\displaystyle d_{n}(\widehat{g_{n}},p_{n})=d_{n}(g_{n},p_{n})\leq D_{g}r_{n}=D_{g}\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}}s_{n}\leq\frac{2}{K}D_{g}s_{n}.

Since
\widehat{g_{n}}=p_{n}
for all
n\notin A
the above equality is in fact true for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We set

\displaystyle\psi\colon\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},r_{n},p_{n})\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n}).

As above,
\psi
is well-defined and Lipschitz continuous with constant
\frac{1}{K}
.

If
[g_{n}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},s_{n},p_{n})
, then
(\psi\circ\varphi)([g_{n}])=[\widehat{g_{n}^{\prime}}]
. For all
n\in A
we have
\widehat{g_{n}^{\prime}}=g_{n}
and so
\frac{d_{n}(\widehat{g_{n}^{\prime}},g_{n})}{s_{n}}=0
. Since
A\in\omega
, it follows that
d((\psi\circ\varphi)([g_{n}]),[g_{n}])=0
and so
\psi\circ\varphi=id
. The equality
\varphi\circ\psi=id
follows similarly.
∎

The previous lemma shows that the asymptotic cone only depends on the growth rate of a scaling sequence and not the sequence itself. Thus, we may assume all scaling sequences to consist of natural numbers from now onwards.

Example 2.24
.

The asymptotic cone of
(\mathbb{Z},|.|)
is
(\mathbb{R},|.|)
.

Definition 2.25
.

Let
(X,d)
and
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
be metric spaces. A map
f\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is called a quasi-isometric embedding if there exist constants
A\geq 1
and
B\geq 0
such that for all
x,y\in X
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}d(x,y)-B\leq d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))\leq Ad(x,y)+B.

We call
f
a quasi-isometry if it additionally is quasi-surjective. That is, there exists a third constant
C\geq 0
such that for all
z\in X
there exists
z^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
with
d^{\prime}(f(z),z^{\prime})\leq C
.

Proposition 2.26

(
[
DK18
, Lemma 10.83]
)
.

Let
f\colon(X,d)\rightarrow(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
be a quasi-isometric embedding of metric spaces. Then this gives rise to a bi-Lipschitz embedding on the level of asymptotic cones
\Cone_{\omega}(X^{\prime},d^{\prime},s_{n})\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(X,d,s_{n})
. If
f
is additionally quasi-surjective then
f
is surjective and so
f
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism.

In fact, the asymptotic cone of any metric space is complete
[
dDW84
, Prop. 4.2]
. Furthermore, the asymptotic space of a geodesic space is geodesic itself and the asymptotic cone of a product of metric spaces
X\times Y
is the product of the asymptotic cones
X
and the asymptotic cone of
Y

[
DK18
, Cor. 1068]
.

2.5
Asymptotic cones for bi-invariant metrics

Our primary interest lies in the case where
X_{n}=G
is a group and the bi-invariant metric
d_{n}=d
is induced by a conjugation invariant norm
\|.\|
on
G
. In this case the sequence of identity elements is a canonical choice of basepoint (compare Lemma
2.22
). Then multiplication of representative sequences gives rise to a well-defined multiplication on
\Cone_{\omega}(G,||.||)
, which turns the latter into a group
[
CZ11
, Prop. 3.3]
. For completeness we will verify the continuity of the multiplication and inversion maps here to conclude that
\Cone_{\omega}(G,||.||)
is a topological group. Recall, that a metric on a group
G
is called bi-invariant if it is both right and left invariant under multiplication in the group.

Lemma 2.29
.

Let
(G_{n},d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of groups
G_{n}
together with bi-invariant metrics
d_{n}\colon G_{n}\times G_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then
\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})
is a topological group for which the induced metric is bi-invariant.

Proof.

Let us verify that the inversion and multiplication maps are continuous since the rest follows as in
[
CZ11
, Prop. 3.3]
.
In fact, the inversion map
\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})
is an isometry with respect to the induced bi-invariant metric on
\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})
, since for all
x,y\in\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})

\displaystyle d(x^{-1},y^{-1})=d(x^{-1}y,1)=d(y,x)=d(x,y).

Isometries are continuous maps. Furthermore, given
\epsilon>0
the multiplication map
m
satisfies
m\big(B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(x)\times B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(y)\big)\subset B_{\epsilon}(xy)
for all
x,y\in\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})
. Indeed, for
x^{\prime}\in B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(x)
and
y^{\prime}\in B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(y)
we calculate

\displaystyle d(x^{\prime}y^{\prime},xy)

\displaystyle=d(x^{-1}x^{\prime}y^{\prime},y)=d(x^{-1}x^{\prime},y(y^{\prime})^{-1})\leq d(x^{-1}x^{\prime},1)+d(1,y(y^{\prime})^{-1})

\displaystyle=d(x,x^{\prime})+d(y,y^{\prime})<\epsilon.

This implies that preimages of open sets are open which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.30
.

If
G_{n}
carries a conjugation invariant norm
\|.\|_{n}
which induces the metric
d_{n}
for all
n
, then the bi-invariant metric on the asymptotic cone is induced by the conjugation invariant norm
\|.\|\colon\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
defined for
x=[(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})
by

\displaystyle\|x\|=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\|x_{n}\|}{n},

where
\omega
is the non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
used to define
\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})
.

A few immediate observations are as follows. If
G
is abelian, then so are all asymptotic cones of
G
for any conjugation invariant norm on
G
. If
H\leq G
is a subgroup and
\|.\|_{G}
a conjugation invariant norm on

, then
\Cone_{\omega}(H,\|.\|_{G})
is a subgroup of
\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|_{G})
. If

is a group homomorphism which is a quasi-isometry, then
f
induces a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism which is an isomorphism of groups on the level of asymptotic cones.

Proposition 2.31
.

Let
G
be a group together with coarsely equivalent conjugation invariant norms
\|.\|_{1}
and
\|.\|_{2}
. Then the respective cones
\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|_{1})
and

are bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic.

Proof.

This statement immediately follows from the definition of coarse equivalence and Proposition
2.26
.

Lemma 2.32
.

Let
G
be a group with conjugation invariant word norm
\|.\|
and let
(s_{n})_{n}
be a scaling sequence. Then for any non-trivial

, there exists a constant
K>0
and an admissible sequence
(g_{n})_{n}\subset G
such that
\|g_{n}\|\geq Ks_{n}
for all

and
g=[(g_{n})_{n}]
.

Proof.

Let
g
be a non-trivial element represented by a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
and let
\epsilon>0
. Set
a:=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\|a_{n}\|}{s_{n}}
, where the limit is taken with respect to the ultrafilter
\omega
. Then

\displaystyle\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054pt\left|\frac{\|a_{n}\|}{s_{n}}-a\right|<\epsilon\right\}\in\omega.

Since filters are closed under taking supersets, this implies for

using the inverse triangle inequality

that

\displaystyle A:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054pt\|a_{n}\|>s_{n}(a-\epsilon)=\frac{s_{n}a}{2}\right\}\in\omega.

For
n\in A

and for

to be some element in

is an unbounded word norm since

possesses a non-trivial element. Then all
g_{n}
satisfy
\|g_{n}\|\geq Ks_{n}
with

. Moreover,
(g_{n})_{n}
defines an admissible sequence which still represents

in
\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
since by definition

and
(a_{n})_{n}

∎

Lemma 2.33
.

Let
G
be a group and let

be a conjugation invariant norm on

. Then for all

and

above, but we include it for completeness. Let

2.6
A bad example for a word norm generated by infinitely many conjugacy classes on the integers

Let us construct an example of a word norm on
\mathbb{Z}
generated by infinitely many conjugacy classes that despite remaining unbounded behaves very differently from the standard absolute value which is the word norm generated by
\{\pm 1\}
. Recall, that
\Cone_{\omega}(Z,\|.\|)=\mathbb{R}
for all norms
\|.\|
generated by finitely many conjugacy classes by Lemma
2.9
.

Consider the word norm
\|.\|_{S}
on
\mathbb{Z}
generated by the set

S=\{\pm 2^{m}m!\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

Claim
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence
x_{n}=2^{n-1}n!
for
n\geq 1
and
x_{0}=0
. Then
\|x_{n}\|_{S}=n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Clearly,
x_{n}=n(2^{n-1}(n-1)!)
and so
\|x_{n}\|_{S}\leq n
. Now assume that
x_{n}=s_{1}+\dots+s_{\ell}
is a presentation of
x_{n}
of minimal length
\ell
where
s_{i}\in S
for all
i
. Reordering this expression we can assume that there is
k\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
such that the absolute values of the generators satisfy

|s_{i}|\leq 2^{n-1}(n-1)!\hskip 5.69054pt\forall i\leq k\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69054pt|s_{i}|\geq 2^{n}n!\hskip 5.69054pt\forall i>k.

Note that for all
i>k
the generators are of the form
(2^{n}n!)\bar{s}_{i}
for a positive integer
\bar{s}_{i}
. Thus, we calculate

\left|s_{1}+\dots+s_{k}\right|=\left|x_{n}-(s_{k+1}+\dots+s_{\ell})\right|=\left|2^{n-1}n!-(2^{n}n!)(\bar{s}_{k+1}+\dots+\bar{s}_{\ell})\right|\geq 2^{n-1}n!.

However, since
|s_{i}|\leq 2^{n-1}(n-1)!\hskip 5.69054pt\forall i\leq k
, it follows that
\left|s_{1}+\dots+s_{k}\right|\leq k\cdot 2^{n-1}(n-1)!
. Therefore,
k\geq n
, which implies that
\ell=n
since
\ell
was the minimal length of an expression of
x_{n}
in generators to begin with. This establishes the claim.

Therefore, the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defines a non-trivial element in
\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z},\|.\|_{S})
for which we have
\|2x_{n}\|_{S}=\|2^{n}n!\|=1
for all
n
. Thus, we have seen that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
represents a non-trivial element of order 2 in
\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z},\|.\|_{S})
.

Proposition 2.34
.

The asymptotic cone
\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z},\|.\|_{S})
contains non-trivial elements of order two. In particular,
\Cone_{\omega}(Z,\|.\|_{S})
and
\mathbb{R}
are not isomorphic.

Remark 2.35
.

A similar argument shows that for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\geq 2
there exists an infinite generating set such
S_{t}
such that
\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z},\|.\|_{S_{t}})
has non-trivial elements which are

2.7

Proposition 2.37
.

Let
G
be a group generated by a finite set
S\subset G
which is invariant under conjugation. Then
G
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
where
n=\rk(G^{ab})
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon G\to G^{ab}
be the abelianisation map and equip
G^{ab}
with the word norm
\|.\|_{f(S)}
generated by the set
f(S)
, which is equivalent to the standard norm on
G^{ab}
since
f(S)
is a finite generating set of
G^{ab}
. It is clear, that
\|f(g)\|_{f(S)}\leq\|g\|_{S}
for all
g\in G
and hence
f
is Lipschitz continuous with constant one with respect to the norms
\|.\|_{S}
and
\|.\|_{f(S)}
. Conversely, let
g\in G
be arbitrary and
\|f(g)\|_{f(S)}=n
. Write
f(g)=f(s_{1})\cdots f(s_{n})
and define

. Then
\|z\|_{S}\leq n
by definition of the norm,

and since

is Lipschitz with constant 1 it follows that
\|z\|_{s}=n=\|f(g)\|_{f(S)}
. Moreover,

, which is finite. So,
\|g\|_{S}=\|az\|_{S}\leq\|a\|_{S}+\|z\|_{S}\|a\|_{S}+\|f(g)\|_{f(S)}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\|f(g)\|_{f(S)}\geq\|g\|_{S}-\max_{a\in[G,G]}\|a\|_{S}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for all }g\in G.

is surjective,

is a quasi-isometry.
∎

4
General Contraction Lemma for a single group

In the proof of the continuity of our contracting homotopy on the asymptotic cone in Lemma
4.2
we will use the following statement from calculus to derive the continuity of a map from a product space from continuity on the individual factors.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be metric spaces together with a map
F\colon X\times Y\rightarrow Z
. Assume that for any
x\in X
there is a constant
L_{x}
such that
F(x,-)\colon Y\rightarrow Z
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{x}
, the map assigning
L_{x}
to
x
is continuous and further assume that for all
y\in Y
the map
F(-,y)\colon X\rightarrow Z
is continuous. Then
F
is continuous on
X\times Y
.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)\in X\times Y
and let
\epsilon>0
be given. Since the assignment of Lipschitz constants is continuous, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that
|L_{x^{\prime}}-L_{x}|<1
for all
x^{\prime}
with
d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})<\delta_{0}
. This implies
L_{x^{\prime}}<L_{x}+1
for all such
x^{\prime}
. By continuity of
F(-,y)
there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that for all
x^{\prime}
with
d(x,x^{\prime})<\delta_{1}
we have
d_{Z}(F(x,y),F(x^{\prime},y))<\epsilon/2
. For the product metric it holds for any
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
that
d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\leq d_{X\times Y}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))
and
d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})\leq d_{X\times Y}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))
. Choose
\delta=\min\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\frac{\epsilon}{2(L_{x}+1)}\}>0
. So for all
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in B_{\delta}((x,y))
we calculate

\displaystyle d_{Z}(F(x,y),F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq d_{Z}(F(x,y),F(x^{\prime},y))+d_{Z}(F(x^{\prime},y),F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))

\displaystyle<\epsilon/2+L_{x^{\prime}}d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})

\displaystyle<\epsilon/2+(L_{x}+1)\delta

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2=\epsilon.

Therefore,
F
is continuous.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Contraction Lemma)
.

Let
G
be a group together with a norm
\|.\|
that induces the metric
d
. Assume there exist constants
K,L
and projection maps
p_{k}\colon G\rightarrow G
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
p_{0}=id
such that

(i)

d(p_{k}(g),p_{k}(h))\leq Kd(g,h)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
g,h\in G
,

(ii)

d(p_{k}(g),p_{\ell}(g))\leq L|k-\ell|
for all
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
g\in G
,

(iii)

\|p_{k}(g)\|\leq\Big|\|g\|-k\Big|
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
g\in G
.

Then all asymptotic cones of
G
are contractible. That is,
\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
is contractible for all ultrafilters
\omega
and scaling sequences
s_{n}
.

Proof.

Denote the equivalence class of the identity sequence by
1\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
and write
\|[g_{n}]\|=d([g_{n}],1)=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\|g_{n}\|}{s_{n}}
for
[g_{n}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
.
We want to use the projection maps to collapse a representative sequence with respect to a time parameter. For this we set our contracting homotopy to be

\displaystyle H\colon

\displaystyle\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})\times[0,1]\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n}),

\displaystyle\big(

\displaystyle[x_{n}],t\big)\rightarrow[p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n})]\hskip 8.53581pt\text{with}\hskip 2.84526pt\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 2.84526pt\text{such that}\begin{cases}\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=t&\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pt[x_{n}]\neq 1,\\
\lambda_{n}=0\hskip 2.84526pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84526ptn&\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pt[x_{n}]=1.\end{cases}

Claim
.

H
is well defined.

First, we show that such a sequence
(\lambda_{n})
can be chosen. Recall, that for
x\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
with
x\neq 1
any representative sequence is nonzero on an
\omega
-heavy subset. Namely, by definition of the ultralimit for all
\epsilon>0
it holds that

\displaystyle\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054pt\left|\frac{\|x_{n}\|}{s_{n}}-\|x\|\right|<\epsilon\right\}\in\omega.

Since filters are closed under taking supersets, this implies by the inverse triangle inequality and setting
\epsilon=\frac{\|x\|}{2}
that

\displaystyle A:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054pt\|x_{n}\|>s_{n}(\|x\|-\epsilon)=\frac{s_{n}\|x\|}{2}>0\right\}\in\omega.

This implies that any representative sequence of any non-trivial
x\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
admits for any
t\in[0,1]
a choice of a positive real sequence
(\lambda_{n}^{\prime})
such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{\prime}}{\|x_{n}\|}=t
. Since
\|x_{n}\|\rightarrow\infty
, we have for
\lambda_{n}=\lceil\lambda_{n}^{\prime}\rceil
that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=t
as well.

Second, we verify the independence of the choice of such a sequence
(\lambda_{n})
while keeping the representative sequence
(x_{n})
of
x\neq 1
fixed. Suppose
(\tau_{n})
is a second sequence such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=t=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
. Both limits
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\|x_{n}\|}{s_{n}}=\|x\|
and
\lim_{\omega}\frac{s_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=\frac{1}{\|x\|}
exist and belong to the open interval
(0,\infty)
. Then, calculate using the multiplicativity of ultralimits and condition
(ii)
that

\displaystyle d([p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n})],[p_{\tau_{n}}(x_{n})])

\displaystyle=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d(p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{\tau_{n}}(x_{n}))}{s_{n}}\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{L|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{s_{n}}

\displaystyle=L\cdot\left(\lim_{\omega}\frac{|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{\|x_{n}\|}\cdot\frac{\|x_{n}\|}{s_{n}}\right)=L\cdot\left|\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}\right|\cdot\|x\|=0.

Third, we verify the independence of
H
of the choice of representative sequence
(x_{n})
of
x
while keeping the sequence
\lambda_{n}
fixed. For this let
(y_{n})
be another choice of representative sequence for
x\neq 1
. Then, calculate using condition
(i)
that

\displaystyle d([p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n})],[p_{\lambda_{n}}(y_{n})])=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d(p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{\lambda_{n}}(y_{n}))}{s_{n}}\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{Kd(x_{n},y_{n})}{s_{n}}=0.

This concludes the proof that
H
is indeed well defined.

Clearly,
H(-,0)=id_{\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})}
using for example
\lambda_{n}=0
for all
x\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, condition
(iii)
forces
H(-,1)\equiv 1
using
\lambda_{n}=\lceil\|x_{n}\|\rceil
for some representative sequence of
x
. It only remains to check continuity of
H
to show that
H
is the desired contracting homotopy.

Claim
.

H
is continuous.

Since
H
is a map from a product of metric spaces it suffices by Lemma
4.1
to verify continuity in each argument separately and see that for each
x\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
the map
H(x,-)
is Lipschitz with constant
L\|x\|
since this is a continuous assignment.

We start by checking continuity in the time parameter. By definition
H(1,-)\equiv 1
is constant. Let
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,1]
and
x=[x_{n}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
with
x\neq 1
. Let
(\lambda_{n})
be such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=t
and
(\tau_{n})
be such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=t^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle d(H(x,t),H(x,t^{\prime}))=

\displaystyle d([p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n})],[p_{\tau_{n}}(x_{n})])=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d(p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{\tau_{n}}(x_{n}))}{s_{n}}\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{L|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{s_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\lim_{\omega}\frac{|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{\|x_{n}\|}\cdot\frac{\|x_{n}\|}{s_{n}}=L\lim_{\omega}\frac{|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{\|x_{n}\|}\cdot\lim_{\omega}\frac{\|x_{n}\|}{s_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\|x\|\cdot|t-t^{\prime}|,

which establishes Lipschitz continuity with constant
L\|x\|
for
H(x,-)
.

Next we need to show for fixed
t
continuity in
x\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
. Since
H(-,1)
is the identity map and
H(-,1)
is constant we can assume that
t\in(0,1)
. Moreover, since the projection maps are all strictly norm decreasing the continuity at
1\in\Cone_{\omega}(G,s_{n})
is immediate. So let
x=[x_{n}]\neq 1
be given and let
y=[y_{n}]
be a different element converging to
x
.
We calculate for
(\lambda_{n})
and
(\tau_{n})
such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{\|y_{n}\|}=t=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
the following

\displaystyle d(H(x,t),H(y,t))

\displaystyle=d([p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n})],[p_{\tau_{n}}(y_{n})])

\displaystyle=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d(p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{\tau_{n}}(y_{n}))}{s_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{d(p_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{\tau_{n}}(x_{n}))}{s_{n}}+\frac{d(p_{\tau_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{\tau_{n}}(y_{n}))}{s_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{L|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{s_{n}}+\lim_{\omega}\frac{Kd(x_{n},y_{n})}{s_{n}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\omega}\frac{L|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{s_{n}}+Kd(x,y).

Clearly
Kd(x,y)\xrightarrow{y\to x}0
, so it remains to show that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{s_{n}}\xrightarrow{y\to x}0
as well. For this we first note that by continuity of the norm we have
\|y\|\xrightarrow{y\to x}\|x\|>0
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{\|y_{n}\|}\cdot\frac{\|y_{n}\|}{\|x_{n}\|}=\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{\|y_{n}\|}\cdot\lim_{\omega}\frac{\|y_{n}\|}{\|x_{n}\|}=t\cdot\lim_{\omega}\frac{\|y_{n}\|}{\|x_{n}\|}\xrightarrow{y\to x}t.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}\frac{|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{s_{n}}=\lim_{\omega}\frac{|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{\|x_{n}\|}\cdot\frac{\|x_{n}\|}{s_{n}}\leq\left|\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}-\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}\right|\cdot\|x\|\xrightarrow{y\to x}\left|t-t\right|=0,

which establishes
H(y,t)\xrightarrow{y\to x}H(x,t)
and concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a group with a norm
\|.\|
that induces the metric

. Then the conditions of the Contraction Lemma

are fulfilled for
(G,\|.\|)
if there is a single map

and a constant

such that

(i)

for all

(ii)

for all
g\in G
,

(iii)

\|p(g)\|\leq\|g\|-1
for all

of norm
\|g\|\geq 1
,

(iv)

, where

g\in G

.

Proof.

We set
p_{k}
to be the

p
for all

using the convention that

. By induction it is immediate that the first condition

0\hskip 34.1433pt\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

4.1
Direct sums

Proposition 4.5

Let
I
be any totally ordered set. Let

and projections

(iii)

\displaystyle d(p(g),g)

Definition 4.7

Lemma 4.8

Corollary 4.11

Let

any norm on
F
. For any totally ordered set
I
all asymptotic cones of the
I
-fold direct sum
G=\bigoplus_{i\in I}F
with the
\ell_{1}
-norm
\|.\|=\Sigma_{i}\|.\|_{F}
are contractible.

Proof.

Any norm in a finite group assumes its infimum and supremum on the set of non-trivial elements. Hence, it is equivalent to the support norm by Remark
4.9
. It follows from Proposition
4.10
that the infinite direct sum only possesses contractible asymptotic cones.
∎

Let us discuss one more example of a direct sum in which the norms on the factors are unbounded but Proposition
4.5
still applies.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
I
be a totally ordered set. Let
G=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}
be equipped with the word norm
\|.\|
coming from the standard basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i}
and their inverses. Then all asymptotic cones of
(G,\|.\|)
are contractible.

Proof.

On every copy of the integers we have the projection map

\displaystyle p\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\hskip 14.22636ptp(n)=\begin{cases}n-1&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptn\geq 1,\\
0&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptn=0,\\
n+1&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptn\leq-1.\\
\end{cases}

This map clearly fixes the identity element. Moreover,
p
is distance decreasing for the standard word metric generated by
\{\pm 1\}
, satisfies
d(p(n),n)\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and reduces the norm of any non-trivial element. Hence, it follows from Proposition
4.5
, that any asymptotic cone of
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}
is contractible with respect to the word norm given by the sum of all generators of the individual summands.
∎

4.2
Infinite Symmetric Group

In the spirit of the projection maps before we now define cutting maps
c_{k}\colon\Sym_{\infty}\rightarrow\Sym_{\infty}
that cut off the last
k
numbers of the support of any given permutation. Then natural numbers
n
that are sent by
\sigma
to the last
k
numbers of the support of
\sigma
will in
c_{k}(\sigma)
be sent to their first return point not belonging to the last
k
-numbers of the support.

More precisely, let
\sigma\in\Sym_{\infty}
where
\rm supp(\sigma)=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}\}
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{\ell}
. Define
c_{k}\colon\Sym_{\infty}\rightarrow\Sym_{\infty}
by

\displaystyle\sigma\rightarrow c_{k}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}c_{k}(\sigma)(i)=i&\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pti>i_{\ell-k},\\
c_{k}(\sigma)(i)=\sigma(i)&\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pti\leq i_{\ell-k}\hskip 2.84526pt\text{and}\hskip 2.84526pt\sigma(i)\leq i_{\ell-k},\\
c_{k}(\sigma)(i)=\sigma^{m}(i)&\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pti\leq i_{\ell-k}\hskip 2.84526pt\text{and}\hskip 2.84526pt\sigma(i)>i_{\ell-k},\\
\text{where}\hskip 2.84526ptm\hskip 2.84526pt\text{minimal such that}\hskip 2.84526pt\sigma^{m}(i)\leq i_{\ell-k}.&\end{cases}

Note, that
c_{k}(\sigma)
is indeed a permutation satisfying
\rm supp(c_{k}(\sigma))\subset\{i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell-k}\}
by definition and for
k\geq\ell
we have
c_{k}(\sigma)=1
.

Lemma 4.13
.

The cutting maps satisfy
d(c_{k}(\sigma),c_{m}(\sigma))\leq 2|k-m|
for all
\sigma\in\Sym_{\infty}
and all
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
d
denotes the induced metric by
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
.

Proof.

Consider the case
m=k+1
. Writing
\sigma
as a product of cycles we see that by definition
c_{k}(\sigma)
and
c_{k+1}(\sigma)
agree on all
i\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell-k}\}
which are not
i_{\ell-{k}}
or its preimage
\sigma^{-1}(i_{\ell-k})
. Hence, we have
d(c_{k}(\sigma),c_{k+1}(\sigma))\leq 2
and the lemma follows by induction.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\sigma,\tau\in\Sym_{\infty}
such that
\rm supp(\sigma)=\rm supp(\tau)
. Then
d(c_{k}(\sigma),c_{k}(\tau))\leq d(\sigma,\tau)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume that
\rm supp(\sigma)=\rm supp(\tau)=\{1,\dots,n\}
. Set

\displaystyle Z=\{z|\sigma(z)\neq\tau(z)\}

\displaystyle Z_{k}=\{z|c_{k}(\sigma)(z)\neq c_{k}(\tau)(z)\},

where by definition
|Z|=\|\sigma\tau^{-1}\|_{supp}=d(\sigma,\tau)
and

|Z_{k}|=\|c_{k}(\sigma)c_{k}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\rm supp}=d(c_{k}(\sigma),c_{k}(\tau)).

Our goal is to find a distinct corresponding element in
Z
for every single element of
Z_{k}
, thus showing that
|Z_{k}|\leq|Z|
.

For
x>n-k
we have
c_{k}(\tau)(x)=c_{k}(\sigma)(x)=x
by definition of the cutting maps
c_{k}
. Let
x\leq n-k
and distinguish the following cases

1.

\sigma(x)\leq n-k
and
\tau(x)\leq n-k
. Then
x\in Z_{k}
if and only if
x\in Z
.

2.

\sigma(x)\leq n-k
and
\tau(x)>n-k
. Then
x\in Z
and
x
may or may not lie in
Z_{k}
.

3.

\sigma(x)>n-k
and
\tau(x)\leq n-k
. Then
x\in Z
and
x
may or may not lie in
Z_{k}
.

4.

\sigma(x)>n-k
and
\tau(x)>n-k
. Then write out both
\sigma
and
\tau
in their unique cycle decompositions. There exists a cycle in which
x=i_{k}=j_{k^{\prime}}
is appearing of the form

\displaystyle(i_{1}\dots i_{k}i_{k+1}\dots i_{\ell}i_{\ell+1}\dots)\subset\sigma,

\displaystyle(j_{1}\dots j_{k^{\prime}}j_{k^{\prime}+1}\dots j_{\ell^{\prime}}j_{\ell^{\prime}+1}\dots)\subset\tau,

such that
i_{\ell+1}
and
j_{\ell^{\prime}+1}
are the first natural numbers
\leq n-k
which appear and all numbers in between are natural numbers larger than
n-k
. Then
c_{k}(\sigma)(x)=i_{\ell+1}
and
c_{k}(\tau)(x)=j_{\ell^{\prime}+1}
. Now if
x\in Z_{k}
then
c_{k}(\sigma)(x)\neq c_{k}(\tau)(x)
and this means that there exists a first letter in
\{i_{k},\dots,i_{\ell}\}
and
\{j_{k^{\prime}},\dots,j_{\ell^{\prime}}\}
on which
\sigma
and
\tau
disagree. This is either
x
itself or a letter
>n-k
. In both cases this yields a unique
x^{\prime}\in Z
which we have not counted in any of the other cases yet.

This shows every
x\in Z_{k}
corresponds to some distinct
x^{\prime}\in Z
which implies that
|Z_{k}|\leq|Z|
. Hence, statement of the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

The maps satisfy
d(c_{k}(\sigma),c_{k}(\tau))\leq 2d(\sigma,\tau)
for all
\sigma,\tau\in\Sym_{\infty}
and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
d
denotes the induced metric by
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
.

Proof.

The supports of
\sigma
and
\tau
can be written uniquely as disjoint unions

\displaystyle\rm supp(\sigma)=A\cup(\rm supp(\sigma)\cap\rm supp(\tau))

\displaystyle\rm supp(\tau)=B\cup(\rm supp(\sigma)\cap\rm supp(\tau)),

As in Lemma
4.14
before use the notation

\displaystyle Z=\{z|\sigma(z)\neq\tau(z)\}

\displaystyle Z_{k}=\{z|c_{k}(\sigma)(z)\neq c_{k}(\tau)(z)\}.

Clearly, we have
|Z|\geq\max\{|A|,|B|\}
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the cutting map
c_{k}
. By definition of
c_{k}
we erase
k
numbers from both
\rm supp(\sigma)
and
\rm supp(\tau)
separately adhering to the total order on the natural numbers. Let
a\in A
be the maximal element among all numbers in
\rm supp(\sigma)\cup\rm supp(\tau)
, then clearly
d(c_{1}(\sigma),\tau)\leq d(\sigma,\tau)-1
, since we just erased an element on which they were not the same. Consequently, in this case we have
d(c_{1}(\sigma),c_{1}(\tau))\leq d(\sigma,\tau)+1
. When erasing a letter from
B
we have the same behaviour. So whenever we erase a letter from
A
or
B
in the process of defining the cutting map we will increase distances by at most 1.

However, we erase at least
\min\{k-|A|,k-|B|\}
common elements from
\rm supp(\sigma)\cap\rm supp(\tau)
in both
\sigma
and
\tau
, since there is a total order on these elements. For these the same argument of Lemma
4.14
above shows that the elements of
Z_{k}\cap\rm supp(\sigma)\cap\rm supp(\tau)
correspond to elements from
Z
. We deduce that
|Z_{k}|\leq|Z|+\max\{|A|,|B|\}
. Since
\max\{|A|,|B|\}\leq|Z|
it follows that
d(c_{k}(\sigma),c_{k}(\tau))\leq 2d(\sigma,\tau)
for all
k
.
∎

Recall, that the equivalence of the norms and the quasi-isometry for
\Sym_{\infty}
and
\A_{\infty}
are discussed in Section 2.2. Moreover, coarsely equivalent norms and quasi-isometric spaces yield Lipschitz-isomorphic asymptotic cones by Proposition
2.26
and Proposition
2.31
.

Theorem 4.16
.

All asymptotic cones of
(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
are contractible.

Proof.

It is clear by definition of
c_{k}\colon\Sym_{\infty}\rightarrow\Sym_{\infty}
that for all
\sigma\in\Sym_{\infty}
we have
c_{k}(\sigma)=1
if
\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}\leq k
and that
\|c_{k}(\sigma)\|_{\rm supp}\leq\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}-k
whenever
\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}\geq k
. This yields the third condition of the Contraction Lemma
4.2
. The first two conditions are guaranteed by Lemma
4.13
and Lemma
4.15
. So the Contraction Lemma
4.2
applies and the result follows.
∎

4.3
Free Products

The aim of this section is to apply our contraction lemma to recover classically known results regarding the contractibility of asymptotic cones of free products with respect to word norms associated to a finite generating set that is not invariant under conjugation. We derive very visual contraction maps from projection maps onto prefixes of words in this case. Thus, Section 4.3 and Proposition
7.4
in the appendix constitute the only parts in this paper in which the norms in consideration are
not
conjugation invariant. An extensive study of asymptotic cones of hyperbolic groups with respect to word norms given by a finite generating set can be found in
[
BDS11
]
,
[
DK18
]
and
[
OOS09
]
. In fact, for a finitely presented group
G
, hyperbolicity is equivalent to the existence of such an asymptotic cone that is a tree by
[
DK18
, Thm. 11.170]
.

Definition 4.17
.

Let
G=\ast_{i\in I}G_{i}
be a free product of a family of groups
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for some indexing set
I
. For each
i
the factor
G_{i}
is a subgroup of
G
via the canonical inclusion. An element of
G
that belongs to one of the factors is called a
letter
of
G
. Any product of letters is called a
word
in
G
. The product of any two letters belonging to the same factor in
G
can be replaced by the letter that represents their product in that factor. Moreover, any identity letters appearing in a word can be omitted without changing the element the word represents in
G
. Recall that any element
g\in G
has a unique presentation as a word, where no two consecutive letters lie in the same factor and no identity letters appear. Such a word is called
reduced
.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i})\}
be a collection of groups together with norms for some indexing set
I
. Define a norm
\|.\|_{\ell_{1}}
on the free product
G=\ast_{i\in I}G_{i}
as follows. Write
g\in G
uniquely as a reduced word
g=g_{1}\dots g_{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
where
g_{j}\in G_{i_{j}}
for all
j
. Then set

\|g\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|g_{1}\|_{i_{1}}+\dots+\|g_{k}\|_{i_{k}}.

This defines a norm on the free product
G
which we will refer to as the
\ell_{1}
-norm

associated to the collection

\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i})\}
.

It follows from the definition of the
\ell_{1}
-norm
associated to a collection
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i})\}
that the projection onto the factor
G_{j}
is Lipschitz-continuous with constant one with respect to the norms
\|.\|_{\ell_{1}}
and
\|.\|_{j}
.

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i})\}
be a collection of groups together with norms for some set
I
. Then for any
j\in I
the inclusion
\varphi_{j}\colon(G_{j},\|.\|_{i})\hookrightarrow(\ast_{i\in I}G_{i},\|.\|_{\ell_{1}})
is an isometry.

Proof.

The inclusion
\varphi_{j}
maps any letter
g\in G_{j}
to itself in
\ast_{i\in I}G_{i}
and this is its unique presentation as a reduced word in
\ast_{i\in I}G_{i}
. Hence, we have
\|\varphi_{j}(g)\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|g\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|g\|_{j}
.
∎

Recall, that the discrete norm on a group is the norm that takes value 0 on the identity element and value 1 on all other all other elements in the group. Then the associated
\ell_{1}
-norm is denoted by
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
and measures the length of a reduced expression in terms of letters. The norm
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
may be viewed as an analogue of the support norm before. Similar to the case of direct sums we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i})\}
be any collection of groups
G_{i}
together with norms
\|.\|_{i}
of bounded diameter and bounded fineness. That is,

\sup_{i}\sup_{g_{i}\in G_{i}}\|g_{i}\|_{i}<\infty\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\inf_{i}\inf_{g_{i}\in G_{i}^{\circ}}\|g_{i}\|_{i}>0,

where
G_{i}^{\circ}
is the set of non-identity elements in
G_{i}
.
Then the
\ell_{1}
-norm
\|.\|_{\ell_{1}}
associated to
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i})\}
is equivalent on
\ast_{i\in I}G_{i}
to the
\ell_{1}
-norm
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
associated to
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{d_{i}})\}
, where
d_{i}
is the discrete norm on
G_{i}
for all
i
.

Proof.

Let
g=g_{1}\dots g_{k}
as a reduced word in
\ast_{i\in I}G_{i}
with
g_{j}\in G_{i_{j}}
. Then
\|g\|_{\rm supp}=k
and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|g_{1}\|_{i_{1}}+\dots\|g_{k}\|_{i_{k}}\leq\sup_{h_{1}\in G_{i_{1}}}\|h_{1}\|_{i_{1}}+\dots+\sup_{h_{k}\in G_{i_{k}}}\|h_{k}\|_{i_{k}}\leq k\sup_{i}\sup_{h_{i}\in G_{i}}\|h_{i}\|_{i},

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|g_{1}\|_{i_{1}}+\dots\|g_{k}\|_{i_{k}}\geq\inf_{h_{1}\in G_{i_{1}}^{\circ}}\|h_{1}\|_{i_{1}}+\dots+\inf_{h_{k}\in G_{i_{k}}^{\circ}}\|h_{k}\|_{i_{k}}\geq k\inf_{i}\inf_{h_{i}\in G_{i}^{\circ}}\|h_{i}\|_{i}.

Consequently,
\|.\|_{\ell_{1}}
and
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
are equivalent.
∎

Proposition 4.21
.

Let
I
be a set. Let
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i},p_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a collection of groups
G_{i}
together with integer valued norms
\|.\|_{i}
and maps
p_{i}\colon G_{i}\rightarrow G_{i}
which satisfy for all
i\in I

(i)

d_{i}(p_{i}(g),p_{i}(h))\leq d_{i}(g,h)
for all
g,h\in G_{i}
,

(ii)

d_{i}(p_{i}(g),g)\leq 1
for all
g\in G_{i}
,

(iii)

\|p_{i}(g)\|_{i}<\|g\|_{i}
for all non-trivial
g\in G_{i}
,

(iv)

p_{i}(1)=1
, where
1\in G_{i}
is the trivial element,

where
d_{i}
denotes the metric induced by
\|.\|_{i}
.
Then all asymptotic cones of
G=\ast_{i\in I}G_{i}
with respect to the
\ell_{1}
-norm associated to the collection
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{i},p_{i})\}_{i\in I}
are contractible.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
and let
g=g_{1}\dots g_{k}
be its unique presentation as a reduced word. Let
i_{1}
be such that
g_{1}\in G_{i_{1}}
. Define

\displaystyle p\colon G\rightarrow G,\hskip 14.22636ptp(g)=p_{i_{1}}(g_{1})g_{2}\dots g_{k}

with
p(1)=1
, where
1
is the identity element of
G
. Note, that
p_{i_{1}}(g_{i_{1}})g_{2}\dots g_{k}
is a reduced word after potentially omitting
p_{i_{1}}(g_{1})
if it is equal to the identity in
G_{i_{1}}
. For all non-trivial
g\in G
it holds that

\displaystyle\|p(g)\|_{\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle=\|p(g_{1})\|_{i_{1}}+\|g_{2}\|_{i_{2}}+\dots\|g_{k}\|_{i_{k}}<\|g_{1}\|_{i_{1}}+\|g_{2}\|_{i_{2}}+\dots\|g_{k}\|_{i_{k}}=\|g\|_{\ell_{1}}

and

\displaystyle d(p(g),g)

\displaystyle=\|p(g)g^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|p_{i_{1}}(g_{1})g_{2}\dots g_{k}(g_{1}\dots g_{k})^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}}=\|p_{i_{1}}(g_{1})g_{1}^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle=\|p_{i_{1}}(g_{1})g_{1}^{-1}\|_{i_{1}}=d_{i_{1}}(p_{i_{1}}(g_{1}),g_{1})\leq 1.

It remains to check that
p
is distance decreasing since then by Lemma
4.3
the Contraction Lemma
4.2
applies and the result follows. Let
g,h\in G
with presentations as reduced words given by
g=g_{1}\dots g_{k}
and
h=h_{1}\dots h_{\ell}
. The only operations to obtain the reduced presentation of
gh^{-1}
from the product of their respective reduced expressions are multiplying adjacent elements in the same group and omitting identities. So, if
g_{k}
and
h_{\ell}
are letters from different groups then
g_{1}\dots g_{k}h_{\ell}^{-1}\dots h_{1}^{-1}
is already reduced. If
g_{k}h_{\ell}^{-1}
is not the identity, then there are no cancellations in
g_{1}\dots g_{k}h_{\ell}^{-1}\dots h_{1}^{-1}
. After possibly performing a sequence of cancellations there is a positive integer
a\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle gh^{-1}=g_{1}\dots g_{k}h_{\ell}^{-1}\dots h_{1}^{-1}=g_{1}\dots g_{k-a}h_{\ell-a}^{-1}\dots h_{1},

where the latter is a reduced word. Clearly
a\leq\min\{k,\ell\}
. Distinguish the following cases.

If
a<k-1
then

\displaystyle d(p(g),h)

\displaystyle=\|p(g)p(h)^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle=\|p_{i_{1}}(g_{1})g_{2}\dots g_{k-a}h_{\ell-a}^{-1}\dots h_{1}\|_{\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle=\|p_{i_{1}}(g_{1})\|_{i_{1}}+\|g_{2}\dots g_{k-a}h_{\ell-a}^{-1}\dots h_{1}\|_{\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{1}\|_{i_{1}}-1+\|g_{2}\dots g_{k-a}h_{\ell-a}^{-1}\dots h_{1}\|_{\ell_{1}}

\displaystyle=\|g_{1}g_{2}\dots g_{k-a}h_{\ell-a}^{-1}\dots h_{1}\|_{\ell_{1}}-1

\displaystyle=d(g,h)-1,

where we used the fact that
p_{i_{1}}
decreases the integer valued norm
\|.\|_{i_{1}}
. Consequently,

d(p(g),p(h))\leq d(p(g),h)+d(h,p(h))\leq d(g,h)-1+1=d(g,h).

Similarly, if
a<\ell-1
we obtain
d(p(g),p(h))\leq d(g,h)
with the same argument.

Finally, if
a\geq\max\{k-1,\ell-1\}
then
gh^{-1}=g_{1}h_{1}^{-1}
. If
g_{1}
and
h_{1}
belong to different groups, then

d(p(g),p(h))=\|p(g_{1})\|_{i_{1}}+\|p(h_{1})^{-1}\|_{j_{1}}<\|g_{1}\|_{i_{1}}+\|h_{1}^{-1}\|_{j_{1}}=\|gh^{-1}\|_{\ell_{1}}=d(g,h).

If both of them belong to the same group, then

d(p(g),p(h))=d_{i_{1}}(p_{i_{1}}(g_{1}),p_{i_{1}}(h_{1}))\leq d_{i_{1}}(g_{1},h_{1})=d(g,h).

Thus
p
is distance decreasing in all cases and the result follows from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.22
.

Let
\{(G_{i},\|.\|_{d_{i}})\}
be any collection of groups
G_{i}
all of which are equipped with the discrete norm

to be the projection sending the whole group to the identity element. Then the collection

4.21
.
∎

Algebraic properties of the asymptotic cone of the infinite symmetric group

Let us make a remark on the subgroups of
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. First, any finite group
F
is a subgroup of some finite symmetric group
\Sym_{k}
. Using disjoint copies of
\Sym_{k}
in
\Sym_{\infty}
one constructs for each
g\in F
the sequence of elements
g_{n}=(g,\dots,g)\in F^{s_{n}}\leq(\Sym_{k})^{s_{n}}\leq\Sym_{\infty}
. The sequence
(g_{n})_{n}
has support norm growing asymptotically like

and this construction realises
F
as a subgroup of
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. Moreover, let
h_{n}=(1\kern 5.0pt2\kern 5.0pt...\kern 5.0pts_{n})
be the standard

-cycle. Then
h=[(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}]
has infinite order in
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. Disjointly arranging such

-cycles one can realise any finite free abelian group as a subgroup of
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. Additionally,
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},s_{n},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
contains the group
\mathfrak{S}(s_{n}/\omega)=\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{s_{n}},s_{n},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
that is universally sophic according to
[

5.1
Perfectness of the Cone

Proposition 5.2
.

\Cone_{\omega}(\A_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
is a perfect group of commutator length one.

Proof.

Let
a=[(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(\A_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
be an arbitrary element. For every
n
there exists
k(n)
such that
a_{n}\in A_{k(n)}
. In fact,
a_{n}
can be viewed as an element of
A_{k}
where
k=\max\{\|a_{n}\|_{\rm supp},5\}
since
a_{n}
only permutes the elements within its support. Since every element in
A_{k}
is a commutator for
k\geq 5
(originally due to
[
Mil99
, Thm. 1]
, but reproved in
[
Ore51
, Thm. 7]
), there exist
b_{n},c_{n}\in A_{k}
such that
[b_{n},c_{n}]=a_{n}
. Then
\|b_{n}\|_{\rm supp}
and
\|c_{n}\|_{\rm supp}
are bounded by
\|a_{n}\|_{\rm supp}+5
. Hence, the sequences
(b_{n})
,
(c_{n})
are are admissible and represent elements
b
,
c
in the asymptotic cone
\Cone_{\omega}(\A_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. Then
a=[b,c]
in
\Cone_{\omega}(\A_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
, which in turn also implies non-triviality of
b
and
c
whenever
a
is non-trivial.
∎

Since every element in
\Cone_{\omega}(\A_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
is an elementary commutator, there is no non-trivial homogeneous quasimorphism on
\Cone_{\omega}(\A_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
.

5.2
Simplicity

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a group. If for all non-trivial
g\in G
there exists a subgroup
H<G
such that
[H,H]
normally generates
G
and
H
commutes with
gHg^{-1}
, then
G
is a simple group.

Proof.

Assume
N
is a non-trivial normal subgroup of
G
and let
g\neq 1
be an element of
N
. Given any elementary commutator
[a,b]\in[H,H]
it holds that

\displaystyle[[a,g],b]=aga^{-1}g^{-1}bgag^{-1}a^{-1}b^{-1}=aga^{-1}g^{-1}gag^{-1}ba^{-1}b^{-1}=[a,b].

That is, every elementary commutator is a product of conjugates of
g
. Therefore,
[H,H]\leq N
and since
[H,H]
normally generates the whole group, so does
N
. Consequently,
G
does not admit any proper normal subgroups apart from the trivial one.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Any
\sigma\in\Sym_{\infty}
with
\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}=n
displaces a set of size at least
\frac{n}{3}
from itself.

Proof.

First, write
\sigma
uniquely as a product of non-intersecting cycles
\sigma=\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}
. Any cycle of size
k
is conjugate to the
k
-cycle
(1\hskip 2.84526pt...\hskip 2.84526ptk)
. If
k
is even, the set containing all odd natural numbers smaller than
k
is displaced from itself. If
k
is odd, then the set of all odd numbers strictly smaller than
k
is displaced from itself. Hence, for any cycle of length
k
we find a set of cardinality
\geq k/3
which is displaced from itself and the same holds for
\sigma
since all cycles
\tau_{i}
have disjoint support.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\sigma\in\Sym_{\infty}
with
\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}=n
. Then for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
with
\|\sigma_{1}\|_{\rm supp}\leq k
,
\|\sigma_{2}\|_{\rm supp}\leq n-k+1
and
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}
.

Proof.

Again, use the unique disjoint cycle decompostion of
\sigma
to write
\sigma=\tau_{1}\cdot\tau_{\ell}
for some
\ell
. Consider the
k
-th number appearing in this cycle decomposition. If this happens to be the last element of a cycle, then the statement is immediate. Otherwise consider that cycle
\tau_{i}
containing the
k
-th number appearing in the cycle decomposition. Without loss of generality we can assume
\tau_{i}=(1...j)
. It can be split up at any
m\leq j
to the two cycles

\displaystyle(1...j)=(m...j)(1...m)

and we have that their supports add up to
m+1
. The result follows.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

All asymptotic cones of
(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
are simple groups.

Proof.

Let
g\in\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
be non-trivial. By Lemma
2.32
there exists a representative sequence
(g_{n})
of
g
with
\|g_{n}\|_{\rm supp}\geq Ks_{n}
for some
K>0
. By Lemma
5.4
every
g_{n}
displaces some set
D_{n}
of size at least
\frac{Ks_{n}n}{3}
from itself. Define

\displaystyle H:=\left\{h=[(h_{n})]\in\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})\hskip 2.84526pt\mid\hskip 2.84526pt\rm supp(h_{n})\subset D_{n}\right\}.

Then
H
and
gHg^{-1}
commute by construction.

It remains to show that the commutator
[H,H]
normally generates
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. Let
a=[(a_{n})]\in\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
be arbitrary. By definition of the asymptotic cone there exists
C>0
such that
\|a_{n}\|\leq Cs_{n}
for all
n
. Apply Lemma
5.5
to write
a_{n}=b_{1,n}c_{n}
where the support of
b_{1,n}
is of size at most
|D_{n}|
and the size of the support of
c_{n}
is bounded by
Cn-|D_{n}|+1
. Proceed by applying Lemma
5.5
again to chop up
c_{n}
further. Inductively, after
m\leq\frac{Cs_{n}}{Ks_{n}/3}=\frac{3C}{K}
iterations we have factored
a_{n}=b_{1,n}...b_{m,n}b^{\prime}_{n}
where the supports of each
b_{i_{n}}
is smaller than
|D_{n}|
and the support of
b^{\prime}_{n}
is at most of size of the number of iterations of our chopping process which is bounded by
\frac{3C}{K}
. For
n
large enough
\frac{3C}{K}\leq|D_{n}|
and since changing finitely many elements of a sequence does not change the ultralimit we just assume this is true for all
n
from here onwards. Set
b^{\prime}_{n}=b_{m+1,n}
. Then for each
b_{i,n}
there exists a
\gamma_{i,n}\in\Sym_{\infty}
which swaps the support of
b_{i,n}
with a subset of
D_{n}
. Moreover,
\rm supp(\gamma_{i,n})\subset\rm supp(b_{i,n})\cup D_{n}
and so
(\gamma_{i,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an admissable sequence for all
i
.

Finally,
\gamma_{i,n}b_{i,n}\gamma_{i,n}^{-1}
is supported in
D_{n}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\gamma_{i,n}b_{i,n}\gamma_{i,n}^{-1}
is an even permutation since we can multiply it by some transposition supported in
D_{n}
without changing the ultralimit. By
[
Mil99
, Thm. 1]
(or
[
Ore51
, Thm. 7]
)
\gamma_{i,n}b_{i,n}\gamma_{i,n}^{-1}
can be written as an elementary commutator
[c_{i,n},d_{i,n}]
of elements
c_{i_{n}},d_{i,n}
supported in
D_{n}
. The sequences
(b_{i,n})_{n}
,
(\gamma_{i,n})_{n}
,
(c_{i,n})_{n}
and
(d_{i,n})_{n}
are all admissible and represent elements
b_{i},\gamma_{i},c_{i},d_{i}
in
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
for all
i
. Then

\displaystyle g=b_{1}...b_{m+1}=\gamma_{1}^{-1}[c_{1},d_{1}]\gamma_{1}...\gamma_{m+1}^{-1}[c_{m+1},d_{m+1}]\gamma_{m+1}\hskip 2.84526pt.

Thus,
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
is simple by Lemma

.
∎

5.3
Coarse equivalence of support norm on the cone

By abuse of notation we denote the norm induced by
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
on
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
by
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
again. The norm
\|.\|_{\rm supp}\colon\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and surjective on the positive real axis and thus not equivalent to any norm generated by finitely many conjugacy classes. Moreover, the norm
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
is stably bounded on
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. This section shows that
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
is coarsely equivalent to word norms generated by finitely many conjugacy classes in a very explicit way and concludes that every conjugation invariant norm is stably bounded on
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
despite the fact that it contains many elements of infinite order.

Theorem 5.9
.

[
Bre78
, Theorem 3.05]

Let
\sigma\in\A_{n}
. Suppose that
\sigma
has an orbit of length two and
n-2r\geq-1
, where
r
is the number of orbits of
\sigma
. Then
C_{\sigma}^{4}=A_{n}
, where
C_{\sigma}
is the conjugacy class of
\sigma
. ∎

Note, that
\sigma\in\A_{n}
with
\|\sigma\|_{\rm supp}=n
has at most
n/2
orbits. If
\sigma
has an orbit of length two then Theorem
5.9
implies that
C_{\sigma}^{4}=A_{n}
.

Proposition 5.10
.

On
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
the induced norm
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
is coarsely equivalent to every word norm generated by finitely many conjugacy classes.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
it suffices to show this for a single word norm generated by finitely many conjugacy classes. Let
g\in\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
be non-trivial. Since
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
is simple, the conjugacy classes of
g
and
g^{-1}
define a conjugation invariant norm
\|.\|_{g}
on
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
.
By Lemma
2.7
it suffices to show that there exist
A,B>0\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\|.\|_{g}\leq A\|.\|_{\rm supp}+B
to conclude that
\|.\|_{g}
and
\|.\|_{\rm supp}
are coarsely equivalent.

By Lemma
2.32
there exists a representative sequence
(g_{n})_{n}
of
g
such that
\|g_{n}\|_{\rm supp}\geq Ks_{n}
for some
K>0
. Furthermore, modifying
g_{n}
by at most two transpositions outside its support we may assume additionally that each of the
g_{n}
has an orbit of length 2 and all
g_{n}
are elements in
\A_{\infty}
. Since this modification still represents
g
in
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
, assume
g_{n}
to be element in
\A_{\infty}
and contain an orbit of order two for all
n
.

Let
h\in\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. By Lemma
2.33

h
is represented by a sequence
(h_{n})
satisfying
\|h_{n}\|_{\rm supp}\leq 2\|h\|_{\rm supp}\cdot s_{n}
. As before in the proof of Proposition
5.6
we now invoke Lemma
5.5
in order to chop up
h_{n}
in
m
steps into pieces. That is, after
m
chops we obtain a product decomposition

h_{n}=b_{1,n}...b_{m,n}b^{\prime}_{n}

where
\|b_{i,n}\|_{\rm supp}\leq Ks_{n}
for all
i
and
\|b^{\prime}_{n}\|_{\rm supp}\leq m
. The number of steps
m
in the chopping procedure is bounded by

m\leq\frac{2\|h\|_{\rm supp}\cdot s_{n}}{Ks_{n}}=\frac{2\|h\|_{\rm supp}}{K}.

Thus
\|b^{\prime}_{n}\|_{\rm supp}\leq\frac{2C}{K}\leq Ks_{n}
for large
n
and so we may assume without loss of generality that this is the case for all
n
. Set
b_{m+1,n}=b^{\prime}_{n}
. For all
i,n
there exists
c_{i,n}
interchanging the support of
b_{i,n}
with a subset of the first
Ks_{n}
natural numbers which themselves have support bounded by
\|c_{i,n}\|_{\rm supp}\leq 2Ks_{n}
. Similarly, there exist
d_{n}
interchanging the support of
g_{n}
with the first
\|g_{n}\|_{\rm supp}
natural numbers and having support norm
\|d_{n}\|_{\rm supp}\leq 2\|g_{n}\|_{\rm supp}
.

Summarising, for all
i,n
the elements
c_{i,n}b_{i,n}c_{i,n}^{-1}
are supported on a subset of the first
Ks_{n}
natural numbers which is a subset of the support of
d_{n}g_{n}d_{n}^{-1}
. Thus, apply Proposition
5.9
and deduce that
c_{i,n}b_{i,n}c_{i,n}^{-1}
is a product of at most four conjugates of
g_{n}
which all have support norm bounded by the support of
g_{n}
. That is

c_{i,n}b_{i,n}c_{i,n}^{-1}=\gamma_{1,n}g_{n}\gamma_{1,n}^{-1}\gamma_{2,n}g_{n}\gamma_{2,n}^{-1}\gamma_{3,n}g_{n}\gamma_{3,n}^{-1}\gamma_{4,n}g_{n}\gamma_{4,n}^{-1}

where
\|\gamma_{j,n}\|_{\rm supp}\leq\|g_{n}\|_{\rm supp}
for all

.
Consequently, since all involved sequences of elements are admissible, they yield elements in the asymptotic cone
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
. Then
h
in
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
is the product

h=\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}b_{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}c_{i}^{-1}\gamma_{1}g\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma_{2}g\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{3}g\gamma_{3}^{-1}\gamma_{4}g\gamma_{4}^{-1}c_{i},

which is a product of at most
4m+4\leq\frac{8\|h\|_{\rm supp}}{K}+4
conjugates of

. Since

was arbitrary to begin with this shows that

\displaystyle\|.\|_{g}\leq\frac{8}{K}\|.\|_{\rm supp}+4.

∎

.

\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{\rm supp})
does not admit a stably unbounded norm. ∎

6
More examples: some classical metric ultraproducts

Let us recall the definition of a metric ultraproduct. For more background see
[
TS14
]
,
[
TW14
]
and
[
Wil17
]
. Given a sequence
(G_{n},d_{n})
of groups equipped with bi-invariant metrics; that is metrics that are invariant under left and right multiplication in
G_{n}
. Assume that the diameters
\sup_{n}\{d_{n}(1,g_{n})\mid g_{n}\in G_{n}\}
are bounded independently of
n
. Then define the metric ultraproduct to be the quotient of the cartesian product
\prod_{n}G_{n}
by the subgroup of elements with vanishing ultralimit

\displaystyle\prod_{n\to\omega}(G_{n},d_{n})=\prod_{n}G_{n}\Big/\{(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\hskip 2.84526pt\mid\hskip 2.84526pt\lim_{\omega}d_{n}(1,g_{n})=0\}.

It becomes a metric space by setting
d=\lim_{\omega}d_{n}
. It is immediate from the definition that for any scaling sequence
s_{n}
setting
d^{\prime}_{n}=s_{n}\cdot d_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\Cone_{\omega}(G_{n},d^{\prime}_{n},s_{n})=\prod_{n\to\omega}(G_{n},d_{n}).

Example 6.1
.

Consider
(\Sym_{n},d_{n})
, where
d_{n}
is the so-called Hamming distance induced by
\frac{\|.\|_{\rm\rm supp}}{n}
. Then
\prod_{n\to\omega}(\Sym_{n},d_{n})=\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{n},\|.\|_{supp})
is the asymptotic cone with respect to the standard scaling sequence
s_{n}=n
. It is one of the universally sophic groups considered in
[
ES05
]
and thus simple. The contraction map of
\Cone_{\omega}(\Sym_{\infty},\|.\|_{supp})
in Theorem
4.16
restricts to a contraction map of
\prod_{n\to\omega}(\Sym_{n},d_{n})
. This remains true for
\prod_{n\to\omega}(\Sym_{s_{n}},d_{s_{n}})
and any other scaling sequence
(s_{n})
.

6.1
Contraction Lemma for sequences of embedded spaces

For simplicity of notation we will assume
s_{n}=n
from here onwards.

Lemma 6.2

(Contraction Lemma for Sequences)
.

Let
(X_{n},d_{n})
be a sequence of subspaces such that
X_{k}\subset X_{n}
for all
k\leq n
,
d_{n}
restricts to
d_{k}
on
X_{k}
for all
k\leq n
. Assume that
X_{0}
consists of a single point
x_{0}
. Finally, assume that the exist maps
p_{n}\colon X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n-1}
satisfying

(i)

there exits
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n
and all
x_{n}\in X_{n}
we have
d_{n}(p_{n}(x_{n}),x_{n})\leq K
,

(ii)

d_{n-1}(p_{n}(x_{n}),p_{n}(y_{n}))\leq d_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})
for all
x_{n},y_{n}\in X_{n}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then
\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},d_{n})
is contractible.

Proof.

Define maps
p_{n,k}\colon X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n-k}
by the concatenation
p_{n,k}=p_{n-k+1}\circ\dots\circ p_{n}
where we use the convention that for
k>n
the maps
p_{n,k}
map every element in
X_{n}
to the single point in
X_{0}
.

Claim
.

These maps satisfy the following properties

(i)

d_{n}(p_{n,k}(x),p_{n,\ell}(x))\leq K|k-\ell|
for all natural numbers
n,k,\ell
and all
x\in X_{n}
,

(ii)

d_{n-k}(p_{n,k}(x),p_{n,k}(y))\leq d_{n}(x,y)
for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and all
x,y\in X_{n}
.

For
\ell<k
it holds that

\displaystyle d_{n}(p_{n,k}(x),p_{n,\ell}(x))

\displaystyle\leq d_{n}(p_{n,k}(x),p_{n,\ell+1}(x))+d_{n}(p_{n,\ell+1}(x),p_{n,\ell}(x))

\displaystyle=d_{n}(p_{n,k}(x),p_{n,\ell+1}(x))+d_{n}\big(p_{n-\ell}(p_{n,\ell}(x)),p_{n,\ell}(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leq d_{n}(p_{n,k}(x),p_{n,\ell+1}(x))+K,

and so
(i)
follows by induction. The second part of the claim follows from assumption
(ii)
by induction on
k
fixing
n
since all inclusions are isometries. This establishes the claim.

Define the contracting homotopy
H\colon\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},d_{n})\times[0,1]\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},d_{n})
by

\displaystyle\big([x_{n}],t\big)\rightarrow[p_{n,\lambda_{n}}(x_{n})],\hskip 2.84526pt\text{where}\hskip 2.84526pt(\lambda_{n})\subset\mathbb{N}\hskip 2.84526pt\text{is such that}\hskip 2.84526pt\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}=t.

First, let us verify that
H
is well-defined. Let
[x_{n}]=[y_{n}]
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}\frac{1}{n}d_{n}(p_{n,\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{n,\lambda_{n}}(y_{n}))\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{1}{n}d_{n}(x_{n}y_{n})=0.

Clearly, for any
t\in[0,1]
a sequence
(\lambda_{n})\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}=t
can be chosen. Moreover, for another sequence
(\tau_{n})
such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{n}=t=\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}
it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega}\frac{1}{n}d_{n}(p_{n,\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{n,\tau_{n}}(x_{n}))\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{K}{n}|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|=K\left|\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}-\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{n}\right|=K|t-t|=0,

which shows independence of the particular choice of sequence
(\lambda_{n})
. Thus,
H
is well-defined.

Claim
.

H
is continuous.

By Lemma
4.1

H
is continuous if it is continuous when restricted to each factor and
H
is globally Lipschitz continuous with respect to one of the factors. Let us start with the time parameter. Denote the induced metric on
\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},d_{n})
simply by
d
. Fix
x=[x_{n}]
. By property
(i)
we calculate for
\lambda_{n}
and
\tau_{n}
such that
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}=t
and
\lim_{\omega}\frac{\tau_{n}}{n}=t^{\prime}
that

\displaystyle d(H(x,t),H(x,t^{\prime}))=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(p_{n,\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{n,\tau_{n}}(x_{n}))}{n}\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{K|\lambda_{n}-\tau_{n}|}{n}=K|t-t^{\prime}|.

For Lipschitz continuity with respect to a fixed time
t
we calculate using
(ii)

\displaystyle d(H(x,t),H(y,t))=\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(p_{n,\lambda_{n}}(x_{n}),p_{n,\lambda_{n}}(y_{n}))}{n}\leq\lim_{\omega}\frac{d_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})}{n}=d(x,y)

This establishes continuity of
H
.

Finally,
H(-,0)=id
letting
\lambda_{n}=0
for all
n
. Respectively, letting
\lambda_{n}=n
for all
n
.
H(-,1)\equiv x_{0}
, the unique point
x_{0}\in X_{0}
. Therefore,
H
is the desired contracting homotopy.

∎

6.2
Upper triangular matrices

Definition 6.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Define the rank norm of an element
g
in the infinite general linear group
\GL_{\infty}(R)=\colim_{n}\GL_{n}(R)
to be

\|g\|_{rk}=\rk(g-id).

Moreover, for
g\in\GL_{n}(R)
we define the projectivised rank norm to be
\|g\|_{\overline{\rk}}=\frac{\|g\|_{\rk}}{n}
.

Observe, that the (projectivised) rank norms are conjugation invariant and that by definition
\|g\|_{\rk}=\dim(\im(g-id))=n-\dim\Fix(g)
where
\Fix(g)
denotes the subspace of fixed points of
g
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\B_{n}(R)
be the group of invertible upper triangular matrices with entries in a commutative ring
R
. Then the metric ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\omega}(B_{n},\|.\|_{\overline{\rk}})
is contractible.

Proof.

Clearly, the diameter of
B_{n}
with the projectivised rank norm is bounded by 1. The statement is equivalent to showing that the cone
\Cone_{\omega}(\B_{n}(R),\|.\|_{\rk})
for the standard scaling sequence
s_{n}=n
is contractible. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define the projection maps
p_{n}\colon\B_{n}(R)\rightarrow\B_{n-1}(R)
to be the projections onto the upper left block with the convention that
\B_{0}(R)
is the trivial group. These maps satisfy

(i)

p_{n}
is a group homomorphism for all
n
,

(ii)

\|p_{n}(g)g^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq 1
for all
n
,

(iii)

\|p_{n}(g)p_{n}(h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}
for all
g,h
and all
n
.

Indeed, it is clear that
p_{n}
is a group homomorphism and that
\|p_{n}(g)g^{-1}\|_{|rk}\leq 1
since
p_{n}(g)g^{-1}
differs from the identity in at most one column. Moreover, for
g\in\B_{n}
it holds that

\|p_{n}(g)\|_{\rk}=\rk(p_{n}(g)-id)\leq\rk(g-id)=\|g\|_{\rk},

Then it follows for
h,g\in\B_{n}
using the fact that
p_{n}
is a group homomorphism that

\|p_{n}(g)p_{n}(h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}=\|p_{n}(gh^{-1})\|_{\rk}\leq\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}.

Consequently, the contractibility of
\Cone_{\omega}(B_{n}(R),\|.\|_{\rk})
follows from Lemma
6.2
.
∎

6.3
Symmetric positive definite matrices

Let
\Po_{n}
be the set of real symmetric positive definite matrices equipped with the rank norm
\|.\|_{\rk}
as a subspace
\Po_{n}\subset\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. For
n\in N
define the projection map

\displaystyle p_{n}\colon\Po_{n}\rightarrow\Po_{n-1},\hskip 14.22636ptA\rightarrow A^{(n-1)}

by forgetting the last row and column. The projection maps
p_{n}
are well-defined since
A^{(n-1)}
is clearly symmetric and by the criterion of positive principle minors
A^{(n-1)}
is still positive definite hence invertible.

Proposition 6.5
.

The metric ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\omega}(\Po_{n},\|.\|_{\overline{\rk}})
is contractible.

Proof.

Again, we will verify the conditions of Lemma
6.2
to conclude the contractibility of
\Cone_{\omega}(\Po_{n},\|.\|_{\rk})=\prod_{n\to\omega}(\Po_{n},\|.\|_{\overline{\rk}})
. Let
A\in\Po_{n}
. Then

\|p_{n}(A)A^{-1}\|_{\rk}=\|A^{(n-1)}A^{-1}\|_{\rk}=\rk(A^{(n-1)}-A)\leq 2

since the only possible non-zero entries of
A^{(n-1)}-A
are in the
n
-th row and column. Furthermore,
A,B\in\Po_{n}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|p_{n}(A)p_{n}(B)^{-1}\|_{\rk}=\rk(A^{(n-1)}-B^{(n-1)})\leq\rk(A-B)=\|AB^{-1}\|_{\rk}

since any collection of linearly independent vectors of
A^{(n-1)}-B^{(n-1)}
stays linearly independent in
A-B
. Thus, Lemma
6.2
applies and it follows that
\Cone_{\omega}(\Po_{n},\|.\|_{\rk})
is contractible.
∎

6.4
Special orthogonal group

Call an element
g\in\SO(n)
that belongs to a subgroup conjugate to
\SO(2)\leq SO(n)
an
elementary rotation
. That is, elementary rotations in
\SO(n)
are precisely the elements that fix a subspace of codimension two.

Lemma 6.6
.

The inclusions
\iota_{n}\colon\SO(n)\rightarrow\Or(n)
are all isometries which are quasi-surjective with constant 1 with respect to the rank norm.

Proof.

Since all spaces are equipped with the same subspace norm of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
all inclusions are isometries by definition. For any
g\in\Or(n)
we have
|\det(g)|=1
and so the matrix
a=(\det(g))
lies in
\Or(1)
. Hence,
h=ag
satisfies
\|hg^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq 1
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

\Cone_{\omega}(\SO(n),\|.\|_{\rk})=\Cone_{\omega}(\Or(n),\|.\|_{\rk})
. That is, the metric ultraproducts of the special orthogonal groups and the orthogonal groups with the projectivesed rank metric are equal.

Lemma 6.8
.

There is the following identification of the rank norm using the standard maximal tori of
\SO(n)
. For
g\in\SO(n)
the norm
\|g\|_{\rk}
is twice the number of non-trivial rotations of a representative
hgh^{-1}
in the maximal torus of
\SO(n)
. In particular,
\|g\|_{\rk}
is always even.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle hgh^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}R_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&R_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix},

where all
R_{i}
are rotations belonging to
\SO(2)
. An element in
\SO(2)
has a fixed point if and only if it is the identity. Thus, the largest subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
without any fixed point of
g
has dimension twice the number of non-trivial rotations. Consequently,
\|g\|_{\rk}
is twice the number of non-trivial rotations
R_{i}
.
∎

Recall, that the action of
\SO(n)
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
gives rise to a fibration with fibre
\SO(n-1)
. In this spirit we want to define projection maps
\SO(n)\rightarrow\SO(n-1)
that rotate vectors back into this fibre and do not increase the norm
\|.\|_{\rk}
.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
g,h\in\SO(n)
such that
g(e_{n})\neq h(e_{n})
. Let
R_{\alpha}
and
R_{\beta}
be elementary rotations such that
R_{\alpha}g(e_{n})=e_{n}=R_{\beta}h(e_{n})
and such that
R_{\alpha}
(and
R_{\beta}
respectively) is the identity if
g(e_{n})=e_{n}
(or
h(e_{n})
respectively). Then
\|R_{\alpha}g(R_{\beta}h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}
.

Proof.

By conjugation invariance
\|R_{\alpha}g(R_{\beta}h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}=\|gh^{-1}R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha}\|_{\rk}
. By our assumptions the rotations
R_{\alpha}
and
R_{\beta}^{-1}
either both act non-trivially on
e_{n}
or one of them is the identity. In both cases their product is again an elementary rotation since every element of
\SO(3)
is an elementary rotation. Consequently,
\|R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha}\|_{\rk}\leq 2
. Since

R_{\alpha}g(e_{n})=e_{n}=R_{\beta}h(e_{n})
, it holds that
g(e_{n})\in\Fix(gh^{-1}R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha})
, but
R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha}(g(e_{n}))=h(e_{n})\neq g(e_{n})
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be the norm
k=\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}
. Then
\dim(\Fix(gh^{-1}))=n-k
and thus

\displaystyle\dim\Big(\Fix(gh^{-1}R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha})\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\dim\Big(\big(\Fix(gh^{-1})\cap\Fix(R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha})\big)\oplus\langle g(e_{n})\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\Big(\Fix(gh^{-1})\cap\Fix(R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha})\Big)+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim(\Fix(gh^{-1}))+\dim(\Fix(R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha}))-\dim(\Fix(gh^{-1})

\displaystyle+\Fix(R_{\beta}^{-1}R_{\alpha}))+1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n-k+n-2-n+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-(k+1).

Therefore,
\|R_{\alpha}g(R_{\beta}h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}+1
. However, by Lemma
6.8
the rank norms
\|R_{\alpha}g(R_{\beta}h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}
and
\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}
are both even, so
\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}+1
is odd. Consequently, we must have
\|R_{\alpha}g(R_{\beta}h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}
.
∎

With these preliminary considerations at hand we will define our desired projection maps
p_{n}\colon\SO(n)\rightarrow\SO(n-1)
as follows. For every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
we fix an elementary rotation
R_{x}\in\SO(n)
which rotates
x
to
e_{n}
and set
R_{e_{n}}=id
. Note, that this choice is unique when
x\neq\pm e_{n}
since
x
and
e_{n}
span a 2 dimensional subspace in that case. Define

\displaystyle p_{n}\colon\SO(n)\rightarrow\SO(n-1)\hskip 5.69054pt\text{by}\hskip 5.69054ptp_{n}(g)=R_{g(e_{n})}g.

Note, that
R_{g(e_{n})}g
is indeed an element of
\SO(n-1)
since it belongs to
SO(n)
and satisfies
R_{g(e_{n})}g(e_{n})=e_{n}
. Thus, it follows by orthogonality that the last entry in all other columns of
R_{g(e_{n})}g
vanishes and it lies in the image of
\SO(n-1)
.

Proposition 6.10
.

The metric ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\omega}(\SO(n),\|.\|_{\overline{\rk}})
of the special orthogonal groups with the projectivised rank metric is contractible.

Proof.

We will verify the conditions of Lemma
6.2
for the cone with respect to the standard scaling sequence
\Cone_{\omega}(\SO(n),\|.\|_{\rk})=\prod_{n\to\omega}(SO(n),\|.\|_{\overline{\rk}})
to obtain a contracting homotopy.

Clearly,
\|p_{n}(g)g^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq 2
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in\SO(n)
. Additionally, by Lemma
6.9
all
g,h\in\SO(n)
satisfy
\|p_{n}(g)p_{n}(h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}\leq\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}
if
g(e_{n})\neq h(e_{n})
. However, if
g(e_{n})=h(e_{n})
then
R_{g(e_{n})}=R_{h(e_{n})}
and so

\displaystyle\|p_{n}(g)p_{n}(h)^{-1}\|_{\rk}=\|R_{g(e_{n})}gh^{-1}R_{h(e_{n})}^{-1}\|_{\rk}=\|gh^{-1}\|_{\rk}.

Therefore, the result follows from Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

The analogeous statement for unitary groups does not follow since the rank norm on
\SU(n)
can assume odd values.

Appendix: Non-contractible metric ultraproducts and asymptotic cones

The purpose of this appendix is to give some immediate examples for groups that have non-contractible asymptotic cones stemming directly from the metric ultraproduct of cyclic groups of order
n
. More background and much more involved theory focussing mainly on the case of finitely generated groups can be found in
[
Bur99
]
,
[
Ril03
]
and
[
EO05
]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
G_{n}=\mathbb{Z}/n
be equipped with the word norm
\|.\|_{n}
generated by
1\in\mathbb{Z}/n
for all
n
and let
\|.\|_{\overline{n}}
be the projectivisation. Then
\prod_{n\to\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{\overline{n}})
is Lipschitz isomorphic to the unit circle
S^{1}
with its standard metric.

Proof.

Recall, that the standard subspace metric on
S^{1}
is Lipschitz equivalent to the metric given by the arclength
d_{\arc}
. For each
n
consider the homomorphism

\varphi_{n}\colon\mathbb{Z}/n\rightarrow S^{1}\hskip 14.22636pt,\hskip 14.22636ptk\rightarrow e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{n}}

with image equal to the
n
-th roots of unity. Then
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/n
with
\|ab^{-1}\|_{n}=k
satisfy
d_{\arc}(\varphi_{n}(a),\varphi_{n}(b))=2\pi\frac{k}{n}
. Hence, the sequence
(\varphi_{n})
gives rise to a Lipschitz continuous homomorphism
\varphi\colon\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})\rightarrow S^{1}
setting
\varphi(x)=\lim_{\omega}\varphi_{n}(x_{n})
for
x=[x_{n}]
.

Conversely, define
\theta_{n}\colon S^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/n
by
\theta_{n}(x)=k
where
k
is a fixed choice for each
x
such such that
d_{\arc}(x,e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{n}})
is minimal. These (non-continuous) maps satisfy

\|\theta_{n}(x)\theta_{n}(y)^{-1}\|_{n}\leq d_{\arc}(x,y)n+2.

Consequently, setting
\theta(x)=[\theta_{n}(x)]
yields a map
\theta\colon S^{1}\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})
that is Lipschitz continous. Since for each
n
the choice of
k
-th root of unity in the definition of
\theta_{n}(x)
is distance minimising, this sequence converges again to
x\in S^{1}
and so
(\varphi\circ\theta)=id
.

For
[x_{n}]\in\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})
let
x^{\prime}=\lim_{\omega}\varphi_{n}(x_{n})
and let
y_{n}=\theta_{n}(x^{\prime})
for all
n
. We claim that
[y_{n}]=[x_{n}]
.
Let
\epsilon>0
be given. Then the following sequence of implications holds where the first line follows from the definition of the ultralimit
x^{\prime}=\lim_{\omega}\varphi_{n}(x_{n})
.

\displaystyle\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054ptd_{\arc}(e^{2\pi i\frac{x_{n}}{n}},x^{\prime})<\epsilon\right\}\in\omega

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054pt\|\theta_{n}(e^{2\pi i\frac{x_{n}}{n}})\theta_{n}(x^{\prime})^{-1}\|_{n}<\epsilon n+2\right\}\in\omega

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{\|x_{n}y_{n}^{-1}\|_{n}}{n}<\frac{\epsilon n+2}{n}\right\}\in\omega

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{\|x_{n}y_{n}^{-1}\|_{n}}{n}<2\epsilon\right\}\in\omega.

Therefore,
[x_{n}]=[y_{n}]
and so
(\theta\circ\varphi)=id
. Thus,
\varphi
and
\theta
are Lipschitz isomorphisms and
\prod_{n\to\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{\overline{n}})
is Lipschitz isomorphic to the unit circle
S^{1}
with its standard metric.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G=\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{Z}/n
be equipped with the word norm
\|.\|
generated by the unit vectors
\{1_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/n\}_{n}
. Then
\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|)
is Lipschitz isomorphic to
S^{1}\times\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},\|.\|_{n})
for suitable sequence
(X_{n},\|.\|_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
p_{n}\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/n
be the projection on the
\mathbb{Z}/n
factor and let
\iota_{n}\colon\mathbb{Z}/n\rightarrow G
be the inclusion. Then for all
n
the inclusion
\iota_{n}
is an isometric embedding,
p_{n}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant 1 and
p_{n}\circ\iota_{n}=id_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
. On the level of asymptotic cones this induces the maps
p\colon\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|)\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})
and
\iota\colon\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|)
. Thus, the exact sequence of abelian groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(p)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

splits as a product of groups. The maps

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|)\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})\times\ker(p),

\displaystyle g\rightarrow\left(p(g),g-p(g)\right),

\displaystyle\psi\colon\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})\times\ker(p)\rightarrow\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|),

\displaystyle(a,b)\rightarrow\iota(a)+b.

display the product structure on the level of groups. However, all these involved maps are identities or Lipschitz continuous and so
\varphi
and
\psi
are Lipschitz continuous as well. The result follows from Lemma
7.1
.

The kernel
\ker(p)
is given precisely by equivalence classes having a representative sequence
(x_{n})
such that
x_{n}\notin\mathbb{Z}/n
for all
n
. Hence, setting
X_{n}=\bigoplus_{k\neq n}\mathbb{Z}/k
and equipping this space with the natural subspace metric
d_{n}
generated by
\{1_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}/k\ :k\neq n\}_{k}
it holds that
\ker(p)=\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},d_{n})
. Finally, it follows that
\psi
is an isometric isomorphism.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
G=\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{Z}/n
be equipped with the word norm generated by
\{1_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/n\}_{n}
. There is an injective map
\pi_{1}(S^{1})\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|))
. In particular,
\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|)
is not contractible.

Proof.

Every map collapsing
S^{1}
to a point and including it in another topological space
Y
is continuous. Therefore, by the universal property of the product there are continuous maps
S^{1}\hookrightarrow S^{1}\times\Cone_{\omega}(X_{n},\|.\|_{n})\rightarrow S^{1}
whose composition is the identity. This induces an injective map
\pi_{1}(S^{1})\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(\Cone_{\omega}(G,\|.\|))
on the level of fundamental groups.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}/n
be equipped with the word norm
\|.\|_{n}
generated by
1\in\mathbb{Z}/n
for all
n
and let the free product
\ast_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}/k
be equipped with the associated
\ell_{1}
-norm. Then
S^{1}
is a topological retract of
\Cone_{\omega}(\ast_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}/k,\|.\|_{\ell_{1}})
.

By Lemma
4.19
the inclusions
\iota_{n}\colon\mathbb{Z}/n\rightarrow\ast_{k}\mathbb{Z}/k
are all isometries. The projections
p_{n}\colon\ast_{k}\mathbb{Z}/k\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/n

\displaystyle\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})\xrightarrow{\iota=[\iota_{n}]}\Cone_{\omega}(\ast_{k}\mathbb{Z}/k,\|.\|_{\ell_{1}})\xrightarrow{p=[p_{n}]}\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n}).

By Lemma
7.1
identify
\Cone_{\omega}(\mathbb{Z}/n,\|.\|_{n})
with the unit circle. Since
p_{n}\circ\iota_{n}=id_{\mathbb{Z}/n}
for all
n

and so
\Cone_{\omega}(\ast_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}/k,\|.\|_{\ell_{1}})
retracts onto
S^{1}
via
p
.
∎

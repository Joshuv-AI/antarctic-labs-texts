Derived Delooping Levels and Finitistic Dimension
Ruoyu Guo and Kiyoshi Igusa

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop new ideas regarding the finitistic dimension conjecture, or the findim conjecture for short. Specifically, we improve upon the delooping level by introducing three new invariants called the effective delooping level edell \mathrm{edell}\,\! , the sub-derived delooping level sub ​ - ​ ddell \mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\! , and the derived delooping level ddell \mathrm{ddell}\,\! . They are all better upper bounds for the opposite Findim. Precisely, we prove Findim ​ Λ op = edell ​ Λ ≤ ddell ​ Λ ​ (or sub ​ - ​ ddell ​ Λ ) ≤ dell ​ Λ \mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\text{ (or $\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda$)}\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda and provide examples where the last inequality is strict (including the recent example from [ 16 ] where dell ​ Λ = ∞ \mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=\infty , but ddell ​ Λ = 1 = Findim ​ Λ op \mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=1=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}} ). We further enhance the connection between the findim conjecture and tilting theory by showing finitely generated modules with finite derived delooping level form a torsion-free class ℱ \mathcal{F} . Therefore, studying the corresponding torsion pair ( 𝒯 , ℱ ) (\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}) will shed more light on the little finitistic dimension. Lastly, we relate the delooping level to the ϕ \phi -dimension ϕ dim \phi\dim , a popular upper bound for findim, and give another sufficient condition for the findim conjecture.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

The finitistic dimension conjectures have been an active area of research for decades since they were first proposed by Rosenberg and Zelinsky in
[
2
]
. The original conjectures stated that, for any ring
\Lambda
,

1.

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda
, and

2.

\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda<\infty
,

where

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{pd}\,M\mid M\in\mathrm{Mod}\,\Lambda,\,\mathrm{pd}\,M<\infty\},

\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{pd}\,M\mid M\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda,\,\mathrm{pd}\,M<\infty\},

\mathrm{pd}\,M=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\text{ is projective}\}.

The first part of the conjectures is shown to fail in many situations, including even monomial algebras
[
13
]
.
On the other hand, there is no known counterexample for the second part of the conjecture for finite dimensional
\Lambda
. In the infinite-dimensional case, however, if
\Lambda
is a commutative Noetherian ring that is regular of infinite Krull dimension, then there exists an algebra
\tilde{\Lambda}
related to
\Lambda
such that
\mathrm{findim}\,\tilde{\Lambda}=\infty
and
\mathrm{findim}\,\tilde{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{op}}=0

[
17
]
.

We will call the second part of the finitistic dimension conjectures restricted to finite dimensional algebras the
findim conjecture
for short. The finiteness of findim bears much homological significance. Some consequences of the findim conjecture are

1.

Wakamatsu tilting conjecture

[
18
]

(a)

Gorenstein symmetry conjecture
, a consequence of the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture

2.

Strong Nakayama conjecture
, which is equivalent to the Nunke condition, by
[
11
]

(a)

Generalized Nakayama conjecture
and
Auslander-Reiten conjecture
are equivalent
[
22
]
and are both implied by the strong Nakayama conjecture

(b)

Nakayama conjecture
, implied by the generalized Nakayama conjecture
[
1
]

Many methods were developed in an attempt to solve the findim conjecture, a lot of which involve the analysis of syzygies. This turns out to be the center of our attention in this paper as well. Another method to solve the findim conjecture involves injective generation
[
19
]
. For a survey of some existing results and more implications among these conjectures, see
[
8
,
10
,
14
]
.

While the findim conjecture holds for many important algebras, such as monomial algebras
[
6
]
, radical cube zero algebras
[
7
,
15
]
, representation dimension three algebras
[
15
]
, and special biserial algebras (since its representation dimension is 3 shown in
[
3
]
) and so on, it remains a very difficult question in general. New ideas arose when Gélinas introduced the delooping level
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
of an algebra
\Lambda
and showed
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda

[
4
]
. Therefore, one way to solve the findim conjecture amounts to showing the delooping level is always finite. So naturally, we have a few questions.

1.

Is the delooping level finite for all
\Lambda
?

2.

Is the difference
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda-\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
or
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda-\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
finite, zero, or arbitrarily large?

For Question 1, the answer is negative due to a recent counterexample by Kershaw and Rickard
[
16
]
, but we will show their example has finite derived delooping level, our improved invariant, in Section
3
. For Question 2, it is known that
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda-\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda
can be arbitrarily large
[
21
]
, so the same is true for
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda-\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
. On the other hand, Gélinas asked whether
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
is always true in Question 4.2 of
[
4
]
, and we show that this is false with a motivating counterexample (Example
2.11
) of a monomial algebra in Section
2
, where
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}+1
. The difference
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda-\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
can indeed be arbitrarily large due to the same example in
[
16
]
.

In order to close the gap between
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
and
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
, we introduce three new invariants called the
effective delooping level

\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda
, the
sub-derived delooping level

\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
, and the
derived delooping level

\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
as improvements to the original delooping level. The improvement lies in the fact that computing
\mathrm{ddell}\,
and
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,
involves considering all modules; on the other hand, the delooping level only considers syzygies of simple modules
S
and modules of the form
\Omega^{n+1}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\Omega$}}^{n+1}\Omega^{n}S

[
4
, Corollary 1.12]
, where
(\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\Omega$}},\Omega)
is an adjoint pair on
\underline{\mathrm{mod}}\,\Lambda
. Our first theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any finite dimensional algebra
\Lambda
over a field
\mathbb{K}
,

(1)

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\text{ (or $\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda$)}\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda.

The upper bound given by the sub-derived delooping level
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
considers all modules that every simple module
S
can embed into. Specifically, we define for every
\Lambda
-module
M

\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M=\inf\{\mathrm{dell}\,N\mid M\xhookrightarrow{}N\},

and prove a new upper bound
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
for
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}

\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,S\mid\text{$S$ is a simple $\Lambda$-module}\}.

On the other hand, the upper bound given by the derived delooping level
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
considers all exact sequences that end in a simple module

. We define for every
\Lambda
-module
M
the more general
k
-delooping level

the derived delooping level

\displaystyle\mathrm{ddell}\,M=\inf\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid

\displaystyle\,\exists n\leq m\text{ and an exact sequence in $\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda$ of the form}

\displaystyle\,\,0\to C_{n}\to C_{n-1}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\to C_{0}\to M\to 0,

\displaystyle\text{ where $(i+1)$-$\mathrm{dell}\,C_{i}\leq m-i$, }i=0,1,\dots,n\},

and prove another new upper bound
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
for
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}

\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{ddell}\,S\mid\text{$S$ is a simple $\Lambda$-module}\}.

Moreover, the set

of finitely generated

. However, the set of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules with finite delooping level is not closed under extensions by

]
. See Remark

We also remark that the inequalities
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
and
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
can be strict as we will show in Examples

and
2.26
. More general definitions and theorems involving the derived delooping level and sub-derived delooping level are stated in Definitions

,
2.22
and Theorems

and
2.27
.

For any finite dimensional algebra

2.
Old and New Invariants

We start with two well-known lemmas which will be used repeatedly throughout the section. We also include the proofs here for completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

If a module
X
has finite injective dimension
n
, then there exists a simple module
S
such that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n}(S,X)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let the minimal injective resolution of
X
be

0\to X\to I_{0}\to I_{1}\to\cdots\to I_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}I_{n}\to 0,

and let
S
be a simple direct summand of
\mathrm{soc}\,I_{n}
, the socle of
I_{n}
.

By definition,
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n}(S,X)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(S,I_{n})/(f_{n})_{*}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(S,I_{n-1}))
. If
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n}(S,X)=0
, then the inclusion
i
from
S
to
I_{n}
factors as
i=S\xhookrightarrow{j}I_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}I_{n}
. Since
I_{n-1}
is injective, there is a map
g:I_{n}\to I_{n-1}
such that
j=gi
. This implies
f_{n}gi=i
, so
f_{n}g:I_{n}\to I_{n}
sends a copy of
I_{S}
identically to itself. This holds for all simple summands of
\mathrm{soc}\,I_{n}
, so
0\to\ker f_{n}\to I_{n-1}\to I_{n}\to 0
splits, contradicting the fact that the injective resolution is minimal.

In particular, the proof shows that all simple summands of
\mathrm{soc}\,I_{n}
satisfy the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For positive integers
n_{1},n_{2}
and modules
M,N
,

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(M,N)=\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}}(\Omega^{n_{2}}M,N).

Dually,

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(M,N)=\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}}(M,\Sigma^{n_{2}}N).

Proof.

Since the dual statement is not used explicitly in the paper and the proofs of the two statements are similar, we only prove the first statement.

If
\mathrm{pd}\,M\leq n_{2}
, then
\Omega^{n_{2}}M
is either projective or zero, so both
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(M,N)
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}}(\Omega^{n_{2}}M,N)
are zero.
Suppose
\mathrm{pd}\,M>n_{2}
. Then we have the following exact sequence

0\to\Omega^{n_{2}}M\to P_{n_{2}-1}\to\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to M\to 0.

and
n_{2}
short exact sequences

(2)

0\to\Omega^{n}M\to P_{n-1}\to\Omega^{n-1}M\to 0

for
n=1,2,\dots,n_{2}
.

We look at the following portions of long exact sequences induced by
\mathrm{Ext}(-,N)
:

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}}(P_{n_{2}-1},N)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}}(\Omega^{n_{2}}M,N)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+1}(\Omega^{n_{2}-1}M,N)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+1}(P_{n_{2}-1},N),

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+1}(P_{n_{2}-2},N)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+1}(\Omega^{n_{2}-1}M,N)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+2}(\Omega^{n_{2}-2}M,N)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+2}(P_{n_{2}-2},N),

and so on. Therefore,

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}}(\Omega^{n_{2}}M,N)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+1}(\Omega^{n_{2}-1}M,N)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+2}(\Omega^{n_{2}-2}M,N)\cong\cdots\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(M,N).\qed

We recall the definition of the delooping level and use the notation
M\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}N
to mean
M
is a
direct summand
of
N
.
Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of nonnegative integers. The infimum of the empty set is

.

[

]

Let

\Lambda
-module.

1.

The delooping level of

\mathrm{dell}\,M=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega^{n+1}N\text{ up to projective summands for some $N\in\underline{\mathrm{mod}}\,\Lambda$}\}.

2.

The delooping level of

\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{dell}\,S\mid\text{$S$ is a simple $\Lambda$-module}\}.

Remark 2.4
.

The original definition of the delooping level is

\displaystyle\mathrm{dell}\,M

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\text{ is a stable retract of }\Omega^{n+1}N\text{ for some $N\in\underline{\mathrm{mod}}\,\Lambda$}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\xrightarrow{s}\Omega^{n+1}N\xrightarrow{\pi}\Omega^{n}M=\mathrm{id}\,\!_{\Omega^{n}M}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\text{the short exact sequence }0\to\Omega^{n}M\xrightarrow{s}\Omega^{n+1}N\to\mathrm{coker}\,{s}\to 0\text{ splits}\},

A natural extension of Definition

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda

The definition of the effective delooping level aims to pinpoint and generalize the key idea of this proof.

Definition 2.7
.

Let

The

The

It turns out this definition perfectly captures the big finitistic dimension when

Proposition 2.8
.

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda=\infty
, or equivalently,
\mathrm{edell}\,S=\infty
for some simple module
S
, then by definition, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is some module
X
with
\mathrm{id}\,X=n+k^{\prime}>n
such that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k^{\prime}}(S,X)\neq 0
. This trivially guarantees there are modules with arbitrarily high injective dimension.

Now suppose
\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda=n<\infty
. Then there exists a simple module
S
such that
\mathrm{edell}\,S=n
,
i.e.
, for every module

such that
\mathrm{id}\,X=n+k^{\prime}>n
,
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k^{\prime}}(S,X)=0
. It is immediate that there can be no module

with
\mathrm{id}\,X>n
as it would contradict Lemma

. On the other hand, if every module

with finite injective dimension has
\mathrm{id}\,X<n
, then
\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda\leq n-1
by definition since it is vacuously true. Therefore, there must exist a module

with
\mathrm{id}\,X\geq n
. Since
\mathrm{id}\,X>n
is impossible,
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=n
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

If
k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda=n
for

, we may use the last argument in the previous proof to show the lower bound
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\geq n+k-1
. However, this is not useful in practice when

since in that case,

is always harder to compute than
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
.

We can easily compare

and
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,M
. In fact, this will be a consequence of certain later theorems in the paper, but we present a proof here first.

Lemma 2.10
.

k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,M\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,M
.

Proof.

Assume
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,M=n<\infty
so that there exists a module
N
such that
\Omega^{n}M\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega^{n+k}N
. To show
k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,M\leq n
, we pick any module
X
such that
\mathrm{id}\,X=n+k-1+k^{\prime}>n+k-1
and want to show that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k^{\prime}}(M,X)=0
. By Lemma
2.2
,

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k^{\prime}}(M,X)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n}M,X)\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n+k}N,X)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k+k^{\prime}}(N,X)=0.

∎

As expected, the effective delooping level is difficult to compute in general as a reinterpretation of the big finitistic dimension since we need to consider all
X
with high injective dimension and compute extensions. Nonetheless, we use this reinterpretation to improve the upper bound of the Findim by establishing relationships between the effective delooping level and our other new invariants.

Coming back to Gelinas’ question about whether

, we want to motivate our new definitions with a counterexample and demonstrate the shortcoming of concentrating on the delooping level of simple modules instead of all modules. This example is particularly interesting because the algebra is monomial, one of the nicest and well-understood classes of algebras to consider and one that guarantees the finiteness of both findim and Findim.

.

Proceed inductively until the short exact sequence
0\to\mathrm{coker}\,f_{0}\to D_{1}\to\mathrm{coker}\,f_{1}\to 0
to obtain
(k-1)
-
\mathrm{edell}\,\mathrm{coker}\,f_{0}\leq m+1
. Lastly, apply Lemma
2.21
again to
0\to S\to D_{0}\to\mathrm{coker}\,f_{0}\to 0
to conclude
k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,S\leq m
.

If
n=k
, then there is no condition on
D_{n}
and
\mathrm{dell}\,D_{n-1}\leq m+n-1
. So we can apply Lemma
2.20
to
\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n-2}\xhookrightarrow{}D_{n-1}
to obtain
\mathrm{edell}\,\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n-2}\leq m+n-1
. Similar to the case of
n<k
, we apply Lemma
2.21

(k-1)
more times to conclude
k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,S\leq m
.

The same argument works for all simple modules
S
, so
k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
and the theorem is the special case when
k=1
. ∎

It is natural to consider the definition and theorem dual to those of the sub-derived delooping level. Hence, we introduce the derived delooping level, the important definition of the paper.

Definition 2.22
.

Let
M
be a
\Lambda
-module.

1.

The
k
-derived delooping level
of
M
is

\displaystyle k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M=\inf\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid

\displaystyle\,\exists n\leq m\text{ and an exact sequence in $\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda$ of the form}

\displaystyle\,\,0\to C_{n}\to C_{n-1}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\to C_{0}\to M\to 0,

\displaystyle\text{ where $(i+k)$-$\mathrm{dell}\,C_{i}\leq m-i$, }i=0,1,\dots,n\},

where we drop the
k
when it is 1 and write
\mathrm{ddell}\,M
instead of
1\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M
. We say
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M
is
equal to
m
using
n
.

2.

The
k
-derived delooping level
of
\Lambda
is

k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\sup\{k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,S\mid S\text{ is simple}\}

We drop the
k
when it is 1. Thus,
\mathrm{ddell}\,M:=1\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M
and the derived delooping level of
\Lambda
is defined to be the
1
-derived delooping level of
\Lambda
.

In order to better understand this new definition and its connection to other invariants, we make three remarks like when we introduced
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
.

Remark 2.23
.

Again, we immediately see that
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
from the definitions. If
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,S=n<\infty
for some simple module
S
, then we can truncate the projective resolution of
S
at
P_{n}
and form the exact sequence

0\to\Omega^{n}S\to P_{n-1}\to\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to S\to 0.

We see immediately that
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,S\leq n
.

Remark 2.24
.

In general, for any
k>0
and any module
M
, if
(k+1)
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,M=m
using
n
is finite, then
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M\leq m
. This is because the exact sequence

0\to C_{n}\to\cdots\to C_{0}\to M\to 0

used to determine
(k+1)
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,M
has
(i+k+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,C_{i}\leq m-i
for
i=0,1,\dots,n
. This means
(i+k)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,C_{i}\leq m-i
for
i=0,1,\dots,n
, so
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M
is at most
m
.

Remark 2.25
.

The asymmetry in the definitions of the derived and sub-derived delooping levels is necessary. It might make sense to define the sub-derived delooping level as

\displaystyle\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mid M\text{ fits in an exact sequence in $\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda$ of the form}

\displaystyle\,\,0\to M\to D_{0}\to D_{1}\to\cdots\to D_{n-1}\to D_{n}\to 0,

\displaystyle\text{ where $(i+1)$-$\mathrm{dell}\,D_{i}\leq n-i$}\}.

However, we saw in Lemma
2.20
that the exact sequence
0\to M\to D_{0}
is already sufficient in showing
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
. Similarly, the derived delooping level cannot be defined concisely. That is, it is insufficient to define
\mathrm{ddell}\,M=\inf\{\mathrm{dell}\,N\mid N\twoheadrightarrow M\}
and
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{ddell}\,S\mid S\text{ is simple}\}
and show
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
. Otherwise, we could just take
N
to be the projective cover and the delooping level of any projective is zero.

The derived delooping level is also strictly better than the delooping level in Example
2.11
.

Example 2.26

(Example
2.11
revisited)
.

We easily find that
\mathrm{ddell}\,S_{1}=1
due to the short exact sequence
0\to S_{2}\to\begin{matrix}1\\
2\end{matrix}\to S_{1}\to 0
and both
S_{2}
and
\begin{matrix}1\\
2\end{matrix}
being infinitely deloopable, which was pointed out in Example
2.18
. For other values of
k
, the same short exact sequence shows
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=1
using 1 for all
k
.

Therefore, for the monomial algebra
\Lambda
defined in Example
2.11
,

\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=1<\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=2.

A main feature of
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
and
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
is that they are usually easier to compute, as we are allowed to explore all modules that have a map to or from simple modules and more. In fact, we conjecture that
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
for all
\Lambda
. The statement
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
is not true, as we will show the example from
[
16
]
is a counterexample in Section
3
.

To finish the discussion of our new invariants, we prove
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
is another upper bound for
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Theorem 2.27
.

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
.

Like the case for
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
, we prove the more general statement

k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,\Lambda\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda,

so that the theorem is the special case when
k=1
.

Lemma 2.28

(
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
Base Case)
.

If
0\to A\to B\to C\to 0
is a short exact sequence in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda
and for
k>1
,

•

k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,A\leq n-1

•

(k-1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,B\leq n
,

then
(k-1)
-
\mathrm{edell}\,C\leq n
.

Lemma 2.29

(
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
Inductive Step)
.

If
0\to A\to B\to C\to 0
is a short exact sequence in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda
and for
k>1
,

•

k\text{-}\mathrm{edell}\,A\leq n-1

•

(k-1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,B\leq n
,

then
(k-1)
-
\mathrm{edell}\,C\leq n
.

Note that Lemma
2.28
implies Lemma
2.29
due to Lemma
2.10
, so we only prove Lemma
2.28
.

Proof of Lemma
2.28
.

Applying Lemma
2.13
repeatedly, we get the short exact sequence

(11)

0\to\Omega^{n}B\to\Omega^{n}C\to\Omega^{n-1}A\to 0.

To show
(k-1)
-
\mathrm{edell}\,C\leq n
, we take any module
X
with
\mathrm{id}\,X=n+k-2+k^{\prime}>n+k-2
and apply the contravariant functor
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(-,X)
to (
11
) to get the exact sequence

(12)

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n}B,X)\leftarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n}C,X)\leftarrow\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n-1}A,X).

Since
(k-1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,B\leq n
,
\Omega^{n}B\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega^{n+k-1}N_{B}
for some module
N_{B}
, so

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n}B,X)\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n+k-1}N_{B},X)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k-1+k^{\prime}}(N_{B},X)=0.

Since
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,A\leq n-1
,
\Omega^{n-1}A\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega^{n+k-1}N_{A}
for some module
N_{A}
, so

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n-1}A,X)\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n+k-1}N_{A},X)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k-1+k^{\prime}}(N_{A},X)=0.

This makes the first and last term of the exact sequence (
12
) both 0, so
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{k^{\prime}}(\Omega^{n}C,X)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n+k^{\prime}}(C,X)=0
. This shows the
(k-1)
-effective delooping level of
C
is at most
n
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.27
.

Let
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=m<\infty
. For any simple
S
and exact sequence

(13)

0\xrightarrow{f_{n+1}}C_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}C_{n-1}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}C_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}S\to 0

such that
(i+k)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,C_{i}\leq m-i
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
, we can split (
13
) into
n
short exact sequences:

0\to\mathrm{coker}\,f_{i+1}\xrightarrow{f_{i}}C_{i-1}\xrightarrow{f_{i-1}}\mathrm{coker}\,f_{i}\to 0,

where
i=1,2,\dots,n
, and in particular
\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n+1}=C_{n}
,
\mathrm{coker}\,f_{1}=S
.

For
i=n
, we have the short exact sequence
0\to C_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}C_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n}\to 0
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
2.28
, so
(n+k-1)
-
\mathrm{edell}\,(\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n})\leq m-n+1
.

For
i=n-1
, the short exact sequence
0\to\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}C_{n-2}\xrightarrow{f_{n-2}}\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n-1}\to 0
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
2.29
, so
(n+k-2)
-
\mathrm{edell}\,(\mathrm{coker}\,f_{n-1})\leq m-n+2
. Inductively, we apply Lemma
2.29
to
i=n-2,n-3,\dots,1
to conclude that
k
-
\mathrm{edell}\,S\leq m
. Therefore, the theorem follows when
k=1
.
∎

We obtain Theorem
1.1
by combining Proposition
2.8
, Theorem
2.19
, and Theorem
2.27
when
k=1
.

3.
Derived delooping level of all modules

In
[
16
]
, the authors use the construction in
[
20
]
to give an example of an algebra with infinite delooping level. Here we will show, as a consequence of very general lemmas, that the example of
[
16
]
has finite
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
for all
k\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
0\to A\to B\to C\to 0
is a short exact sequence with
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,A=m_{1}
,
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,C=m_{2}
. Then
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,B\leq m_{1}+m_{2}+1
.

Proof.

By assumption, we have two exact sequences

(14)

0\to D_{n_{1}}\to D_{n_{1}-1}\to\cdots\to D_{0}\to A\to 0,

(15)

0\to E_{n_{2}}\to E_{n_{2}-1}\to\cdots\to E_{0}\to C\to 0,

where
(i+k)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,D_{i}\leq m_{1}-i
for
i=0,\dots,n_{1}
and
(i+k)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,E_{i}\leq m_{2}-i
for
i=0,\dots,n_{2}
.

We also need the first
n_{1}+1
steps of the minimal projective resolution of
C

(16)

0\to\Omega^{n_{1}+1}C\to P_{n_{1}}\to\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to C\to 0,

and another exact sequence

(17)

0\to\Omega^{n_{1}+1}E_{n_{2}}\to\Omega^{n_{1}+1}E_{n_{2}-1}\to\cdots\to\Omega^{n_{1}+1}E_{0}\to\Omega^{n_{1}+1}C\to 0

by applying Lemma
2.13
to (
15
).

Combining (
14
), (
16
), and (
17
), it is straightforward to check that we get the long exact sequence

(18)

0\to\Omega^{n_{1}+1}E_{n_{2}}\to\cdots\to\Omega^{n_{1}+1}E_{0}\overset{f}{\to}D_{n_{1}}\oplus P_{n_{1}}\to\cdots\to D_{1}\oplus P_{1}\to D_{0}\oplus P_{0}\overset{g}{\to}B\to 0,

where
f
maps into
P_{n_{1}}
, which can be factored as
\Omega^{n_{1}+1}E_{0}\twoheadrightarrow\Omega^{n_{1}+1}C\xhookrightarrow{}P_{n_{1}}
, and
g
can be factored as
D_{0}\oplus P_{0}\twoheadrightarrow A\oplus P_{0}\twoheadrightarrow B
and the last surjective map
P_{0}\twoheadrightarrow B
is
g^{\prime}
in Lemma
2.13
.

Now, for
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,B\leq m_{1}+m_{2}+1
, it remains to check

1.

(i+k)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,D_{i}\leq m_{1}+m_{2}+1-i
for
i=0,\dots,n_{1}
,

2.

(i+n_{1}+1+k)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega^{n_{1}+1+i}E_{i}\leq m_{1}+m_{2}-i-n_{1}
for
i=0,\cdots,n_{2}
.

A stronger version of the first statement holds due to the conditions in (
14
). For the second statement, it is equivalent to show
\Omega^{m_{1}+m_{2}+1}E_{i}\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega^{m_{1}+m_{2}+1+k}N_{i}
for some
N_{i}
and
i=0,\dots,n_{2}
, but this is immediately implied from the conditions in (
15
).
∎

The previous lemma implies that if
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda<\infty
for some
k
, then
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M<\infty
for all finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules
M
, while the same statement is not true for the delooping level by Remark
3.7
and
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
M
has bounded
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
, then
\Omega M
has bounded
(k+1)\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
and therefore bounded
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
.

Proof.

Suppose
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M=m
using
n
is finite. Assume
m\neq n
and
m\neq 0
. Then there is an exact sequence

(19)

0\to C_{n}\to C_{n-1}\to\cdots\to C_{0}\to M\to 0,

where
(i+k)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,C_{i}\leq m-i
for
i=0,\dots,n
, or equivalently,
(i+k+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega C_{i}\leq m-i-1
for
i=0,\dots,n
.

Applying Lemma
2.13
, we obtain another exact sequence

0\to\Omega C_{n}\to\cdots\to\Omega C_{0}\to\Omega M\to 0,

which implies
(k+1)
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Omega M\leq m-1
.

If
m=n
, instead of having
(i+k+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega C_{i}\leq m-i-1
, we have
(i+k+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega C_{i}\leq m-i
in (
19
), so
(k+1)
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Omega M\leq m
.

If
m=0
,
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M=k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,M=0
, so
(k+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega M=(k+1)
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Omega M=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
of any local algebra
\Lambda
is finite for all
k
.

Proof.

Local algebras only have one simple module
S
, so
\mathrm{dell}\,S=\mathrm{ddell}\,S=0
. By Lemma
3.1
,
\mathrm{ddell}\,M
is finite for all finitely generated modules
M
. Take
M=\Lambda/S
. Then
S=\Omega M
, so
2
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,S
is finite by Lemma
3.2
. This implies
2
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,M
is finite for all
M
. Proceeding like so, we get
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M<\infty
for all
\Lambda
-modules
M
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
M
be any (finitely generated) module over a local algebra
A
and consider the one-point extension
\Lambda=A[M]
. Then
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
is finite for all
k
.

Proof.

Let
S
be the unique simple
A
-module and let
T
be the other simple
\Lambda
-module. We know that
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,S<\infty
for all
k
. Also,
(k+1)
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,M<\infty
for all
k
. This implies that
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,T<\infty
for all
k
.
∎

More generally, this same proof shows that, for

to have finite

, it suffices for
A
to have finite
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
and for

to have finite
(k+1)
-
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
.

Remark 3.5
.

The example of

4.
Sufficient condition for
\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda<\infty

In this section, we provide a sufficient condition for the finiteness of findim by comparing
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
and
\phi\dim\Lambda
. Note that this comparison is done on the same algebra
\Lambda
instead of opposite algebras. Both invariants focus on the structure of syzygies of simple
\Lambda
-modules, so it is not surprising that they may be related.

We repeat the setup in
[
15
]
in order to recall the definition of the
\phi
-dimension. Let
K_{0}
be the free abelian group generated by all symbols
[M]
, where
M
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module, modulo the subgroup generated by
[P]
for projective modules
P
and by
[M]-[N]-[N^{\prime}]
for
M\cong N\oplus N^{\prime}
. Thus
K_{0}
has a free basis given by those
[M]
where
M
is indecomposable. The elements of
K_{0}
are
[M]-[N]
where
M,N
are
\Lambda
-modules.

Define the endomorphism

L:K_{0}\to K_{0}\text{ by }[M]\mapsto[\Omega M].

Let
\mathrm{add}\,M
be the additive category of the module
M
and let
\langle\mathrm{add}\,M\rangle
denote the subgroup of
K_{0}
generated by
[N]
for all
N\in\mathrm{add}\,M
. More generally, for any finite set of modules
T
, we denote by
\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle
the subgroup of
K_{0}
generated by
[M]
for all
M\in\mathrm{add}\,T
. Then, we define

\phi(M)=\inf\{n\mid L^{m}(\langle\mathrm{add}\,M\rangle)\cong L^{m+1}(\langle\mathrm{add}\,M\rangle)\,\forall m\geq n\},

\phi\dim\Lambda=\sup\{\phi(M)\mid M\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda\}.

We extend the definition slightly for when the additive category is generated by some subset
T
of
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda
, that is,

\displaystyle\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\mid L^{m}(\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle)\cong L^{m+1}(\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle)\,\forall m\geq n\}

By Fitting’s Lemma,
\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda
is always finite if
T
is a finite set.

We could do the same for the delooping level and define
\mathrm{dell}_{T}\Lambda:=\sup\{\mathrm{dell}\,M\mid M\in T\}
. However, note that when
T
contains all simple modules and is contained in all indecomposable summands of syzygies of simple modules, as in the case of Theorem
4.1
,
\mathrm{dell}_{T}\Lambda=\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
, so this definition is not useful.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any finite dimensional algebra
\Lambda
over a field
K
, let
T_{\Lambda}
be the set of non-projective indecomposable summands of syzygies of simple
\Lambda
-modules, including all simple modules. If
T_{\Lambda}
is a finite set, then

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\text{ or }\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda\leq\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda\leq\phi\dim\Lambda,

and in particular, since
\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda
is finite, the finitistic dimension conjecture holds for
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Proof.

Let
T=\{T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{N}\}
. Note that we can restrict
L
to be an endomorphism of
\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle
since
T
is closed under taking syzygies.
Thus,
L\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle\subset\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle
which implies
L^{n+1}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle\subset L^{n}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle
. It is clear that
\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda
is finite since
T
is finite, so let
\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda=n
. Then
L^{n}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle\cong L^{n+1}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle
as free abelian groups of finite rank. This makes the quotient
L^{n}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle/L^{n+1}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle
into a finite abelian group of order, say
m
. Then
mL^{n}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle\subset L^{n+1}\langle\mathrm{add}\,T\rangle
.

This implies that, for all
T_{i}\in T
,
m[\Omega^{n}T_{i}]=[\Omega^{n+1}A]-[\Omega^{n+1}B]
for some
A,B\in\mathrm{add}\,T
. So,
\Omega^{n}T_{i}
is a direct summand of
\Omega^{n+1}A
which makes
\mathrm{dell}\,T_{i}\leq n=\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda
for all
i
.
So
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda\leq\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda<\infty
. Since
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
,
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}<\infty
.
∎

We would like to point out that there is a similar concept in Example 1.22 of
[
4
]
called “
n
-syzygy finiteness.” It is straightforward to show that if
\Lambda
is
n
-syzygy finite, then
\phi\dim\Lambda<\infty
, but the converse might not be true. Investigations on their relationships will be the topic of a future paper.
Another similar concept is “finite cosyzygy type” which appears in Definition 7.1 of
[
19
]
, where Rickard shows in Lemma 6.1 and Proposition 7.2 that if all simple
\Lambda
-modules have finite cosyzygy type, then
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda<\infty
. This is equivalent to our previous Theorem
4.1
. Our theorem has the slight improvement that in addition to knowing
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda
is finite, we can find an upper bound
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda\leq\phi_{T}\dim\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
if
T
is finite.

Theorem
4.1
immediately recovers the result that
\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda<\infty
if
\Lambda

Corollary 4.2

Proof.

We write the monomial algebra

∎

5.
Future Directions

The introduction of new invariants brings forth more questions. First of all, we can still explore more of their relationships among themselves. For example,

Question 5.1
.

Regarding the two new sub-derived and derived delooping levels,

1.

Can we compare
k\text{-}\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
and
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
? We saw in Section
3
that
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda<\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
is possible. Does the inequality
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
hold for all Artin algebras
\Lambda
and for general
k
? To what extent can we compare
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
and
\mathrm{sub}\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
?

2.

Is
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
true? There is no example that we know of where this is false.

3.

If the answer to the previous question is negative, can we quantify the difference
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda-\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
?

4.

Can we use these new invariants to prove the findim conjecture in other settings?

Moreover, we would like to point out that there is an associated torsion pair.

Theorem 5.2
.

The class of modules
\mathcal{F}
with finite derived delooping level forms a torsion-free class. If this class contains all
\Lambda
-modules, then
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}<\infty
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.1
shows
\mathcal{F}
is closed under extensions. The next lemma shows that
\mathcal{F}
is closed under submodules. Thus
\mathcal{F}
is a torsion-free class. If
\mathcal{F}
contains all modules, it contains the simple modules making
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda<\infty
. Then,
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}<\infty
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Note that
\mathcal{F}
contains all
\Lambda
-modules if and only if the corresponding torsion class
\mathcal{G}=\,^{\perp}\mathcal{F}
is zero.

Lemma 5.4
.

The set
\mathcal{F}
is closed under submodules.

Proof.

If
N\in\mathcal{F}
and
M
is a submodule of
N
, then
0\to M\overset{f}{\to}N\to\mathrm{coker}\,f\to 0
rotates to
0\to\Omega\mathrm{coker}\,f\to M\to N\to 0
by Lemma
2.13
, where
\mathrm{ddell}\,N<\infty
and
\mathrm{ddell}\,(\Omega\mathrm{coker}\,f)\leq\mathrm{dell}\,(\Omega\mathrm{coker}\,f)=0
. Therefore,
M\in\mathcal{F}
by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

If
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
is finite, then all simple
\Lambda
-modules belong to
\mathcal{F}
, so the torsion-free class
\mathcal{F}
contains all finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules. The corresponding torsion class
\mathcal{T}
would be empty. In general, we could ask if
\mathcal{T}
is finitely generated. In
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
, we will have the torsion pair
(D\mathcal{F},D\mathcal{T})
. This connection to tilting theory allows us to potentially answer questions about findim using the derived delooping level for the same or opposite algebra.

Since
\mathcal{F}
contains all finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules if and only if
D\mathcal{F}
contains all finitely generated
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules and
D\mathcal{F}
contains all finitely generated injective
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules, we have the following corollary of Theorem
5.2
.

Corollary 5.5
.

If the torsion class generated by all finitely generated injective
\Lambda
-modules contains all finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules, then
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be the torsion class generated by all injective
\Lambda
-modules. If
\mathcal{J}=\mathrm{mod}\,\text{-}\Lambda
, then the torsion-free class
D\mathcal{J}
in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
cogenerated by all projective
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules contains all
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules and in particular all simple
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules. Since
\mathcal{F}
contains all projective
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules, we have
D\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and
D\mathcal{J}=\mathrm{mod}\,\text{-}\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
. So, by Theorem
5.2
,
\mathrm{Findim}\,(\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda<\infty
.
∎

This leads us to a comparison of derived delooping level and injective generation of the derived category. Let
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
be the unbounded
derived category
of complexes over
\Lambda
-modules. Let
M^{\perp}
be the
right perpendicular category
of
M
, defined as the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)}(M,X[t])=0
for all
X
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. The left perpendicular category
\prescript{\perp}{}{M}
of
M
is defined similarly. In
[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
, Rickard proves that if the localizing subcategory generated by all injective
\Lambda
-modules (the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
that contains all injectives and is closed under coproducts), denoted by
\langle\text{Inj-}\Lambda\rangle
, is the entire
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
, then
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda<\infty
. In that case, we say that
injectives generate
for
\Lambda
. In particular, if all simple stalk complexes are in
\langle\text{Inj-}\Lambda\rangle
, then injectives generate. The paper presents several methods for showing injective generation, but it is difficult in general to determine whether a (simple) stalk complex is in
\langle\text{Inj-}\Lambda\rangle
. In light of our new invariant, we present two possible candidates for
\langle\text{Inj-}\Lambda\rangle
in terms of
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
.

Suppose
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\infty
and
\mathrm{ddell}\,{DS}=\infty
for some simple
\Lambda
-module
DS_{\Lambda}
. Then we would like to ask whether the dual simple module
S
is in the localizing subcategory generated by all injective
\Lambda
-modules
I
. Since we know
\prescript{\perp}{}{S}
is a localizing subcategory, the question becomes whether
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)}(I,S[t])=\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{t}(I,S)=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. It is clear that this is true for
t=0
, but there does not seem to be an easy way to determine the case when
k>0
.

Define a class of
\Lambda
-modules
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\{DM\mid k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,M<\infty\text{ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$}\}
. Another candidate for
\langle\text{Inj-}\Lambda\rangle
is
\langle\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\rangle
. Note that all projective
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules have finite
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
for all
k
, so
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
contains all injective
\Lambda
-modules. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
satisfies the following.

Lemma 5.6
.

If
0\to A\to B\to C\to 0
is a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
, then if two of the terms are in
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
then so is the third, where
A,B,C
are all considered as stalk complexes.

Proof.

If
B,C
are in
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
, then so is
A
by Theorem
2.19
. If
A,B
are in
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
, then so is
C
by Theorem
2.27
. Finally, if
A,C\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
, then so is
B

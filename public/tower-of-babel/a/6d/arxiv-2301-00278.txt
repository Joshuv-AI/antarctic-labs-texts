Isometric path complexity of graphs A preliminary version of this paper appeared as [ 7 ] in the proceedings of MFCS 2023.
Dibyayan Chakraborty, Jérémie Chalopin, Florent Foucaud, Yann Vaxès

Abstract
A set S S of isometric paths of a graph G G is “ v v -rooted”, where v v is a vertex of G G , if v v is one of the end-vertices of all the isometric paths in S S . The isometric path complexity of a graph G G , denoted by i ​ p ​ c ​ o ​ ( G ) ipco\left(G\right) , is the minimum integer k k such that there exists a vertex v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) satisfying the following property: the vertices of any isometric path P P of G G can be covered by k k many v v -rooted isometric paths. First, we provide an O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ m ) O(n^{2}m) -time algorithm to compute the isometric path complexity of a graph with n n vertices and m m edges. Then we show that the isometric path complexity remains bounded for graphs in three seemingly unrelated graph classes, namely, hyperbolic graphs , (theta, prism, pyramid)-free graphs , and outerstring graphs . Hyperbolic graphs are extensively studied in Metric Graph Theory . The class of (theta, prism, pyramid)-free graphs are extensively studied in Structural Graph Theory , e.g. in the context of the Strong Perfect Graph Theorem . The class of outerstring graphs is studied in Geometric Graph Theory and Computational Geometry . Our results also show that the distance functions of these (structurally) different graph classes are more similar than previously thought. There is a direct algorithmic consequence of having small isometric path complexity. Specifically, we show that if the isometric path complexity of a graph G G is bounded by a constant, then there exists a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation algorithm for Isometric Path Cover , whose objective is to cover all vertices of a graph with a minimum number of isometric paths. This applies to all the above graph classes. Keywords: Shortest paths, Isometric path complexity, Hyperbolic graphs, Truemper Configurations, Outerstring graphs, Isometric Path Cover

The main technical contribution of this paper are as follows. First we prove that the isometric path complexity can be computed in polynomial time.

Given a graph

n

G

(a)

If the hyperbolictiy of

(b)

If

(c)

u,v
in
\overrightarrow{G_{r}}(P)
. Now
suppose that there exists an isometric
r
-rooted path containing

u,v
in
\overrightarrow{G_{r}}(P)
,
i.e.
,

\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)-\mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(u,v\right).
Then, along any path from
u

to
v
, we need to traverse at least
\mathsf{d}\left(u,v\right)
edges increasing
the distance to
r
. Since
P
is an isometric
(u,v)
-path, it
contains exactly
\mathsf{d}\left(u,v\right)
edges. Hence,

\mathsf{d}\left(r,v_{i+1}\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v_{i}\right)+1
for every

i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
.
∎

3.1
Notations and preliminary observations

We now introduce some notations that will be used to describe the
algorithm and prove its correctness. Consider three vertices
r,x,v

of
G
such that
x\neq v
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
denote the set
of all isometric
(x,v)
-paths
P
containing a vertex
u
that is
adjacent to
v
and satisfies

\mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)-1
. Analogously, let
\mathcal{P}_{\rightarrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)

denote the set of all isometric
(x,v)
-paths
P
containing a vertex

u
that is adjacent to
v
and satisfies
\mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)

and let
\mathcal{P}_{\nearrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
denote the set of all isometric

(x,v)
-paths
P
containing a vertex
u
that is adjacent to
v
and
satisfies
\mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)+1
. Observe that the set of
isometric
(x,v)
-paths is precisely

\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\cup\mathcal{P}_{\rightarrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\cup\mathcal{P}_{\nearrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)

and that some of these sets may be empty.

Given a path
P
, we denote by
|S_{r}\left(P\right)|
the minimum size of a
set of isometric
r
-rooted paths covering the vertices of
P
. We
denote by
\gamma^{r}_{\searrow}(x,v)
and
\beta^{r}_{\searrow}(x,v)

respectively the minimum of
|S_{r}\left(P\right)|
and

|S_{r}\left(P-\{v\}\right)|
over all paths
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
. More
formally,

\displaystyle\gamma^{r}_{\searrow}(x,v)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{|S_{r}\left(P\right)|\colon P\in\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\right\},

\displaystyle\beta^{r}_{\searrow}(x,v)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{|S_{r}\left(P-\{v\}\right)|\colon P\in\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\right\}.

Note that if
\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
is empty, we have

\gamma^{r}_{\searrow}(x,v)=\beta^{r}_{\searrow}(x,v)=0
. We define
similarly
\gamma^{r}_{\nearrow}(x,v)
,
\beta^{r}_{\nearrow}(x,v)
, and

\gamma^{r}_{\rightarrow}(x,v)
:

\displaystyle\gamma^{r}_{\nearrow}(x,v)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{|S_{r}\left(P\right)|\colon P\in\mathcal{P}_{\nearrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\right\},

\displaystyle\beta^{r}_{\nearrow}(x,v)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{|S_{r}\left(P-\{v\}\right)|\colon P\in\mathcal{P}_{\nearrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\right\},

\displaystyle\gamma^{r}_{\rightarrow}(x,v)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{|S_{r}\left(P\right)|\colon P\in\mathcal{P}_{\rightarrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\right\}.

Finally, let

\gamma^{r}(x,v)=\max\left\{\gamma^{r}_{\searrow}(x,v),\gamma^{r}_{\rightarrow}(x,v),\gamma^{r}_{\nearrow}(x,v)\right\}
be
the maximum of
|S_{r}(P)|
over all isometric
(x,v)
-paths
P
.
In our algorithm, we will need also to consider the case where
v=x

as an initial case. For practical reasons, we let

\gamma^{r}(x,x)=\gamma^{r}_{\searrow}(x,x)=\gamma^{r}_{\rightarrow}(x,x)=\gamma^{r}_{\nearrow}(x,x)=1
and

\beta^{r}_{\searrow}(x,x)=\beta^{r}_{\nearrow}(x,x)=0
.
Based on the above notations and Lemma
7
, we have the following observation.

Observation 11
.

For any graph
G
and any vertex
r
of
G
, we have

ipco\left(\overrightarrow{G_{r}}\right)=ipacc\left(\overrightarrow{G_{r}}\right)=\max_{x,v}\gamma^{r}(x,v)
and

ipco\left(G\right)=ipacc\left(G\right)=\min_{r}\max_{x,v}\gamma^{r}(x,v)
.

Observation
11
implies that to compute the isometric
path complexity of a graph it is enough to compute the parameter

\gamma^{r}(x,v)
for all
r,x,v\in V(G)
in polynomial time. In the
next section, we focus on achieving this goal without computing
explicitly any of the sets
\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
,
\mathcal{P}_{\rightarrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)

or
\mathcal{P}_{\nearrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
. (Note that the size of these sets could be
exponential in the number of vertices of the graph).

Observation 12

If

the source vertex, and

distinct from

\displaystyle\beta_{\searrow}(v)

\displaystyle=\max\{\gamma(u):u\in I(x,v)\cap N(v);\ \mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)-1\},

\displaystyle\beta_{\nearrow}(v)

Lemma 13
.

If
r
is the root vertex,
x
the source vertex, and
v
is
distinct from

, then

\gamma_{\rightarrow}(v)=\max\{1+\gamma(u):u\in I(x,v)\cap N(v);\ \mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)\}

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{P}_{\rightarrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
is empty if and only if there is
no vertex

such that

\mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)
. If
\mathcal{P}_{\rightarrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)

Suppose now that
\mathcal{P}_{\rightarrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\neq\emptyset

be a path such that

\mathsf{d}\left(r,v_{i-1}\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v_{i}\right)
. Let
Q=(v_{0},\dots,v_{i-1})

and consider a set
S
of isometric

-rooted paths covering the

of size

and a
(r,v_{i})
-shortest

. Observe that

is a set of isometric

r
-rooted paths covering the vertices of

. Consequently

\gamma_{\rightarrow}(v_{i})=|S_{r}\left(P\right)|\leq|S_{r}\left(Q\right)|+1\leq\gamma(v_{i-1})+1
.

Consider now an isometric
(x,v_{i-1})
-path

such that

\gamma(v_{i-1})=|S_{r}\left(Q^{\prime}\right)|
. Let
P^{\prime}
be the isometric

(x,v_{i})
-path obtained by appending

to
Q^{\prime}
. Consider a set

S^{\prime}
of isometric

-rooted paths covering the vertices of

of

be a path of

\gamma_{\rightarrow}(v_{i})=\gamma(v_{i-1})+1

Lemma 14

If
r
is the root vertex,
x
the source vertex, and
v
is a
vertex distinct from
x
, then

\gamma_{\searrow}(v)=\max\{\max\{\gamma_{\searrow}(u),\gamma_{\rightarrow}(u),\beta_{\nearrow}(u)+1\}:u\in I(x,v)\cap N(v);\ \mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)-1\}

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)
is empty if and only if there is
no vertex

such that

\mathsf{d}\left(r,u\right)=\mathsf{d}\left(r,v\right)-1

\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)

\gamma_{\searrow}(v)=0

\mathcal{P}_{\searrow}^{r}\left(x,v\right)\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\left(x|y\right)_{r}

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}\left(x,r\right)+\mathsf{d}\left(y,r\right)-\mathsf{d}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}\left(y,r\right)-\mathsf{d}\left(z,r\right)+2\delta

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}\left(y,r\right)-\mathsf{d}\left(z,r\right)+2\delta

(a)

.
First, we shall define the notions of

-theta

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have introduced the new graph parameter
isometric path complexity
. We have shown that the isometric path complexity of a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges can be computed in
O(n^{2}m)
-time. It would be interesting to provide a faster algorithm to compute the isometric path complexity of a graph. We have derived upper bounds on the isometric path complexity of three seemingly (structurally) different classes of graphs, namely hyperbolic graphs, (theta,pyramid,prism)-free graphs and outerstring graphs. An interesting direction of research is to generalise the properties of hyperbolic graphs or (theta,pyramid,prism)-free graphs to graphs with bounded isometric path complexity.

Note that, in our proofs we essentially show that, for any graph
G
that belongs to one of the above graph classes, any vertex
v
of
G
, and any isometric path
P
of
G
, the path
P
can be covered by a small number of
v
-rooted isometric paths. This implies our “choice of the root” is arbitrary. This motivates the following definition. The
strong isometric path complexity
of a graph
G
is the minimum integer
k
such that for each vertex
v\in V(G)
we have that the vertices of any isometric path
P
of
G
can be covered by
k
many
v
-rooted isometric paths. Our proofs imply that the strong isometric path complexity of graphs from all the graph classes addressed in this paper are bounded.
We also wonder whether one can find other interesting graph classes with small (strong) isometric path complexity.

Our results imply a constant-factor approximation algorithm for
Isometric Path Cover
on hyperbolic graphs, (theta, pyramid, prism)-free graphs and outerstring graphs. However, the existence of a constant-factor approximation algorithm for
Isometric Path Cover
on general graphs is not known (an
O(\log n)
-factor approximation algorithm is designed in
[
27
]
).

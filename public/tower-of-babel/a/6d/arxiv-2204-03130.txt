Abstract
. If G G is a finite group, we have proposed new conjectures on the interaction between different primes and their corresponding Brauer principal blocks. In this paper, we give strong support to the validity of these conjectures.

1.
Introduction

Meaningful interaction between the representation theory of finite groups from the perspective of different primes
is extremely rare. However, in
[
NRS21
]
, we proposed the following three plausible conjectures, which
extended work of several authors (see
[
LWXZ20
]
,
[
NWi97
]
,
[
BNOT07
]
,
[
MN20
]
).

If
p
is a prime and
G
is a finite group, we denote by
B_{p}(G)
the principal
p
-block of
G
.
The main subject of our work is the set
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))
of the irreducible
complex characters in the principal
p
-block of
G
whose degree is not divisible by
p
.
This set seems to possess remarkable properties.

Conjecture A
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
and
q
be different primes.
If

{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))\cap{\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))=\{1_{G}\},

then there are a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G

and a Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
of
G
such that
xy=yx
for all

x\in P
and
y\in Q
.

Conjecture B
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
and
q
be primes dividing the order of
G
.
If
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))
, then
p=q
.

Conjecture C
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
p
and
q
be different primes.
Then
q
does not divide
\chi(1)
for all
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))

and
p
does not divide
\chi(1)
for all
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))

if and only if there are a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G

and a Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
of
G
such that
xy=yx
for all

x\in P
and
y\in Q
.

The main result of
[
NRS21
]
was to reduce Conjecture A to a problem on almost simple groups and to prove it in the case that one of the primes is
2
. To prove Conjecture A for almost simple groups in the case
where
p
and
q
are both odd
remains quite a challenge.

In the present paper, we focus on Conjectures B and C.
Using the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, in
our first main theorem we prove the following.

Theorem D
.

Conjecture C implies Conjecture B.

After proving Theorem D, therefore, we concentrate our efforts in the remainder of the paper towards Conjecture C.

Theorem E
.

Conjecture C holds for finite simple groups.

Besides Conjecture C being true for simple groups,
in Theorem
3.3
below, we shall also prove that Conjecture C is true for

p
-solvable groups, assuming the inductive Alperin–McKay condition.
This gives strong support to the validity of
this conjecture.

Unfortunately, at the time of this writing, we still do not know how to reduce Conjecture C to a question on almost simple groups.

2.
Theorem D

In this Section we prove that Conjecture C implies Conjecture B. This will require the following result on simple groups.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p,q
be different primes and let
S
be a non-abelian simple group with
pq\mid|S|
. Assume that
[P,Q]=1
for some
Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
S
and a Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
of
S
.
Then one of the following holds:

(a)

There exists
\alpha\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(S))-{\rm Irr}(B_{q}(S))
which
is
{\rm Aut}(S)_{p}
-invariant, where
{\rm Aut}(S)_{p}
is some Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(S)
.

(b)

There exists
\alpha\in{\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(S))-{\rm Irr}(B_{p}(S))
which
is
{\rm Aut}(S)_{q}
-invariant, where
{\rm Aut}(S)_{q}
is some Sylow
q
-subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(S)
.

Proof.

First, note that by
[
MN20
, Lemma 3.1]
, the condition
[P,Q]=1
implies that
[\bar{P},\bar{Q}]=1
for some Sylow
p
- and
q
- subgroups
\bar{P},\bar{Q}
of any covering group of
S
. If
S
is one of the sporadic groups
J_{1}
or
J_{4}
, then we may use GAP and its Character Table Library to see that the statement holds.
Then
[
MN20
, Propositions 3.2-3.4]
further imply that we may assume
S
to be a simple group of Lie type that is not isomorphic to a sporadic or alternating group and is defined in characteristic
r_{0}\not\in\{p,q\}
.

So, let
S
be of the form
S=G/{\bf Z}(G)
for
G
a group of Lie type of simply connected type defined in characteristic
r_{0}\neq p,q
. In this case, we may further assume that
p
and
q
are both odd and that the Sylow
p
- and
q
-subgroups of
G
are abelian, using
[
MN20
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Since
p
and
q
are odd, note that we may therefore assume without loss that
p\geq 5
. Further, using
[
Ma14
, Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 2.2]
, we have
p\nmid|{\bf Z}(G)|
. Together, this implies that
p
does not divide the order of any diagonal or graph outer automorphism, so that

\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{p}
may be taken as a subgroup of
S\rtimes\langle F\rangle
, where
F
is a generating field automorphism.

Now, in
[
NRS21
, Proposition 3.5]
, building off of
[
GSV19
, Theorem 3.5]
, we have shown that there is a character
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(S))
such that
\chi
is the deflation of a so-called semisimple character
\chi_{t}
of
G
with
t\neq 1
an element of the dual group
G^{\ast}
of order a power of
p
. In particular,
\chi_{t}
lies in the Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(G,t)
. Since
B_{q}(G)
contains only characters lying in Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
with
|s|
a power of
q
(see
[
CE04
, Theorem 9.12]
), we see that
\chi
does not lie in
{\rm Irr}(B_{q}(S))
.

It now suffices to argue that
t
can have been chosen so that
\chi_{t}
is invariant under
\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{p}
. Following the proof of
[
GSV19
, Theorem 3.5]
, we see that
t
was chosen to lie in
{\bf Z}(P^{\ast})
, where
P^{\ast}
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of the dual group
G^{\ast}
, and that we may further choose
t
to have order
p
without losing the properties we have already discussed.

Now, since
p\nmid|{\bf Z}(G)|
, we have
{\bf C}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is connected, where
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
is the ambient reductive group whose fixed points under an appropriate Frobenius endomorphism yields
G^{\ast}
(see
[
MT11
, Exercise 20.16]
). Then for
\varphi\in\langle F\rangle
, we have
\chi_{t}^{\varphi}=\chi_{t^{\varphi^{\ast}}}
, where
\varphi^{\ast}
is an appropriate field automorphism of
G^{\ast}
(see
[
NTT08
, Corollary 2.5]
). Since
t^{\varphi^{\ast}}
must be
G^{\ast}
-conjugate to another element of order
p
in
{\bf Z}(P^{\ast})
, we have
\chi_{t}^{\varphi}
is also of the form
\chi_{t^{\prime}}
with
t^{\prime}\in{\bf Z}(P^{\ast})
of order
p
, is still trivial on
{\bf Z}(G)
, and still lies in
B_{p}(S)
but not
B_{q}(S)
under deflation. Hence
\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{p}
acts on such characters and we let
\alpha
be a fixed point under this action, since the number of such elements
t^{\prime}
is relatively prime to
p
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, which is a direct product of the different

G
-conjugates of a non-abelian simple group
S
. Let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Suppose that
\alpha\in{\rm Irr}(S)

is
{\rm Aut}(S)_{p}
-invariant, where
{\rm Aut}(S)_{p}
is some Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(S)
.
Then there are
g_{i}\in G,h_{i}\in P
and
\sigma_{i}\in{\rm Aut}(S^{g_{i}})
such that

(\alpha^{g_{1}})^{\sigma_{1}h_{1}}\times\cdots\times(\alpha^{g_{m}})^{\sigma_{m}h_{m}}\in{\rm Irr}(N)
is
P
-invariant.

Proof.

Suppose that
N
is the direct product of
\Omega=\{S^{g}|\,g\in G\}
.
Now, write

\Omega=\mathcal{O}(S_{1})\cup\ldots\cup\mathcal{O}(S_{t}),

where
\mathcal{O}(S_{i})
is the
P
-orbit of some
S_{i}=S^{x_{i}}
, for some
x_{i}\in G
.
Then
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{t}
, where
N_{i}
is the product of the elements in
\mathcal{O}(S_{i})
.
Of course,
N_{i}
is
P
-invariant.
Let
\alpha_{i}=\alpha^{x_{i}}\in{\rm Irr}(S_{i})
. Suppose that
\{S_{i}^{y_{1}},\ldots S_{i}^{y_{r}}\}
are
the different
P
-conjugates of
S_{i}
, where
y_{i}\in P
. Hence

N_{i}=S_{i}^{y_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{i}^{y_{r}}
, and

P=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}{\bf N}_{P}(S_{i})y_{j}

is a disjoint union.

By hypothesis,
\alpha
is invariant under
X\in{\rm Syl}_{p}({\rm Aut}(S))
.
Therefore
\alpha_{i}
is invariant under
X_{i}=X^{x_{i}}\in{\rm Syl}_{p}({\rm Aut}(S_{i}))
.
Let
M_{i}={\bf N}_{G}(S_{i})
and
C_{i}={\bf C}_{G}(S_{i})
. We have that
M_{i}/C_{i}
embeds into
{\rm Aut}(S_{i})
.
Then
{\bf N}_{P}(S_{i})C_{i}/C_{i}
is a
p
-subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(S_{i})
, and therefore, there
is
\sigma_{i}\in{\rm Aut}(S_{i})
such that
({\bf N}_{P}(S_{i})C_{i}/C_{i})\subseteq X_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}
.
Since
\alpha_{i}
is
X_{i}
-invariant, it follows that
\beta_{i}=(\alpha_{i})^{\sigma_{i}}\in{\rm Irr}(S_{i})
is
X_{i}^{\sigma_{i}}
-invariant, and therefore

{\bf N}_{P}(S_{i})
-invariant. We claim that
\gamma_{i}=\beta_{i}^{y_{1}}\times\cdots\times\beta_{i}^{y_{r}}\in{\rm Irr}(N_{i})
is
P
-invariant.
Indeed, if
x\in P
, then
y_{k}x=w_{k}y_{\sigma(k)}
for some
w_{k}\in{\bf N}_{P}(S_{i})
,
1\leq k\leq r
,
and
\sigma
a permutation of
\sf S_{r}
.
Now, if
u\in S_{i}
, then

\gamma_{i}^{x^{-1}}(u^{y_{k}})=\gamma_{i}(u^{y_{k}x})=\alpha(1)^{r-1}\beta_{i}^{y_{\sigma(k)}}(u^{w_{k}y_{\sigma(k)}})=\alpha(1)^{r-1}\beta_{i}(u^{w_{k}})=\alpha(1)^{r-1}\beta_{i}(u)=\gamma_{i}(u^{y_{k}})\,.

This proves that
\gamma_{i}
is
P
-invariant. Hence
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\gamma_{t}\in{\rm Irr}(N)

is
P
-invariant.
∎

.

Suppose that
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, with
G/N
a
p^{\prime}
-group.
Let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
and assume that
G=N{\bf C}_{G}(P)
. Then restriction of characters defines
a natural bijection between the irreducible characters of the principals blocks
of
G
and
N
.

Proof.

The case where
G/N
is solvable was proved in
[
Al76
]
and the general case
in
[

]
. ∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that Conjecture C is true for all finite groups.
Let
p
and
q
be primes. Assume that
G
is a finite group of order divisible by
p
and
q
. If
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))
, then
p=q
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
|G|
. Assume that
p\neq q
.
By Conjecture C, we know that
[P,Q]=1
for some
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
and
Q\in{\rm Syl}_{q}(G)
.

Step 0. If
1\neq N\lhd G
, then
p
divides
|N|
or
q
divides
|N|
.

Otherwise by
[
Na98
, Theorem 9.9(c)]

{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G/N))={\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G/N))

and by induction we are done.

Step 1. Let
L
be a proper normal subgroup of
G
.
Then
G/L
has order divisible by
p
or
q
.

Suppose that
G/L
has
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
-order.
We claim that
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(L))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(L))
. Indeed, let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(L))
.
Then there exists
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{q}(G))
over
\theta
. Then
\chi

has
q^{\prime}
-degree by
[
I08
, Theorem 11.29]
and
therefore
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))
, by hypothesis.
Therefore
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(L))
.
By symmetry, the claim is proved. Therefore,
the proof of Step 1 follows by using the inductive hypothesis.

Step 2. Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
and suppose that
N
is
an elementary abelian
p
-group. Then
G={\bf C}_{G}(N){\bf C}_{G}(Q)
.

Write
M={\bf C}_{G}(N)
and
L=M{\bf C}_{G}(Q)
. We have that

Q\subseteq{\bf C}_{G}(P)\subseteq{\bf C}_{G}(N)=M
and
G/M
is a
q^{\prime}
-group. Also by the Frattini argument
G=M{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
and
L\lhd G
. Notice that
G/L
is a
q^{\prime}
-group and a
p^{\prime}
-group (because
P\subseteq{\bf C}_{G}(Q)\subseteq L
). Then we use Step 1.

Step 3. Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
and suppose that
N

is abelian. Then
G={\bf C}_{G}(N)
.

By Step 0, we may assume that
N
is a
p
-group or
a
q
-group. By symmetry, assume that
N
is a
p
-group. Write
M={\bf C}_{G}(N)
.
We prove first that
B_{p}(G)
is the only
p
-block of
G
covering
B_{p}(M)
. Let
B
be a
p
-block of
G
covering
B_{p}(M)
and let
D
be a defect group of
B
. Then
N\subseteq D
by
[
Na98
, Theorem 4.8]
and
{\bf C}_{G}(D)\subseteq M={\bf C}_{G}(N)
. Then
B
is regular with respect to
M
(
[
Na98
, Lemma 9.20]
) and hence by
[
Na98
, Theorem 9.19]
we have that
B=B_{p}(M)^{G}=B_{p}(G)
by the third main theorem (see
[
Na98
, Theorem 6.7]
). Hence
B_{p}(G)
is the only
p
-block of
G
covering
B_{p}(M)
.
In particular, we have that
{\rm Irr}(G/M)\subseteq{\rm Irr}(B_{p}(G))
.

Next, we prove that
G=M
. Recall that
G/M
is a
q^{\prime}
-group, because
[Q,N]=1
.
By Step 2, we have
G=M{\bf C}_{G}(Q)
. Hence by Theorem
2.3
, we have
that the restriction map

{\rm res}:{\rm Irr}(B_{q}(G))\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(B_{q}(M))

is a bijection.
We claim that
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G/M)=\{1_{G}\}
. Indeed, if
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G/M)
, then
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))
. By Theorem
2.3
,
\chi_{M}
is irreducible and since
\chi
lies over
1_{M}
we have
\chi_{M}=1_{M}
. Hence
\chi=1_{G}
by the uniqueness
of the restriction map. Thus
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G/M)=\{1_{G/M}\}
and
G=M
by
[
NRS21
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Step 4. Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, then
N
is not abelian.

Suppose the contrary and assume without loss of generality that
N
is an elementary abelian
p
-group, so by Step 3 we have
G={\bf C}_{G}(N)
and
N\subseteq{\bf Z}(G)
. By
[
Na98
, Theorem 9.10]
we have that
B_{p}(G/N)
is the unique
p
-block of
G/N
contained in
B
. We claim that
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G/N))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G/N))
. Indeed, we have that

{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G/N))\subseteq{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G/N))

where we have used
[
Na98
, Theorem 9.9]
in the last equality. On the other hand, let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G/N))
, so
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))={\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G))
and

. Since

is the only

-block of
G/N
contained in

, we have that
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G/N))
and the claim is proven. By using the inductive hypothesis,
we have that

does not divide
|G/N|
. Therefore,

\{1_{G}\}={\rm Irr}(B_{p}(G/N))={\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(G/N))={\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G/N))
and

by
[

, Lemma 2.1]
). Hence

and this is a contradiction.

Step 5. Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, then
pq
divides
|N|
.

Suppose that
N
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. We claim first that
NQ
does not have a normal

-complement. Indeed, suppose the contrary and let

be a normal

-complement. Then

is a normal

-complement of

and by the minimality of

we have that either

or

. If

,

is

and

, contradiction with Step 0. If

then

is a

-group, which is a contradiction with Step 4. Therefore
NQ
does not
have a normal
q
-complement. By
[
IS76
, Corollary 3]
there is
\tau\in{\rm Irr}(B_{q}(QN))

non-linear of

-degree. Therefore

. By
[
Mu94
, Lemma 4.3]
we have that there is some
\gamma\in{\rm Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(B_{q}(G))
lying over
\tau_{N}
.
By hypothesis, we have that
\gamma
is in the principal

-block
of
G
, and therefore
\tau_{N}
is in the principal

-block of
N
, which is a contradiction since

is a
p^{\prime}
-group and
\tau_{N}\neq 1
.

Final Step.
If
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, then
N
is semisimple by Step 4.
Suppose that
N
is a direct product of all the different
G
-conjugates of a simple group

S
of order divisible by
pq
. Suppose that (a) of Theorem
2.1
holds and let
\alpha
be the character in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(S))
(not in
{\rm Irr}(B_{q}(S))
) which is

-invariant, given by Theorem

.
Notice that any

-conjugate of

-block os

.
By Lemma

, there exists

-invariant, and such that each of its

by

.

. Then

3.
Conjecture C and
p
-solvable groups

As of the writing of this article, we can only prove Conjecture C for
p
-solvable
groups if the following (expected) consequence of the Alperin–McKay conjecture is true.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Suppose that
N
is normal in
G
, and let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(N)
.
Then there is a bijection

{}^{*}:{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(N))\rightarrow{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}({\bf N}_{N}(P)))

such that for each
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{p}(N))
, there is a bijection

f_{\theta}:{\rm Irr}(B_{p}(G)|\theta)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(B_{p}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|\theta^{*})
such that

\chi(1)/\theta(1)=f_{\theta}(\chi)/\theta^{*}(1)
for all
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
.

(In fact, it is expected that
∗
is
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
-equivariant, and that
the character triples
(G_{\theta},N,\theta)
and
({\bf N}_{G}(P)_{\theta},{\bf N}_{N}(P),\theta^{*})

are isomorphic.)

In any case, we shall only need Conjecture
3.1
in the case where

G/N
is a
p^{\prime}
-group.

In our proof, we shall also need a McKay divisibility theorem, which was made possible
after M. Geck proved a remarkable conjecture on Glauberman correspondents.

In the following, we follow the proof of
[
Ri19
, Theorem A]
, and then use Geck’s result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group and let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Then there is a bijection

{}^{*}:{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))

such that
\chi^{*}(1)
divides
\chi(1)
and
\chi(1)/\chi^{*}(1)
divides
|G:{\bf N}_{G}(P)|
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
|G|
. As in the proof of
[
Ri19
, Theorem A]
we may assume that
{\bf O}_{p}(G)=1
, and hence
K={\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)>1
.

Let
S/K={\bf O}_{p}(G/K)
, and notice that
P_{0}=P\cap S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
S
.
By the Frattini argument we have that
G=K{\bf N}_{G}(P_{0})
and
{\bf N}_{G}(P_{0})<G
since
{\bf O}_{p}(G)=1
. Let
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s}
be a complete set of representatives of the orbits of the action of
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
on the
P
-invariant irreducible characters of
K
. By
[
Na18
, Lemma 9.3]
, we have that

{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)={\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G\,|\,\theta_{1})\cup\cdots\cup{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G\,|\,\theta_{s})

is a disjoint union. Fix
\theta_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(K)
and observe that
\theta_{i}
is also
P_{0}
-invariant. Let
\theta_{i}^{*}\in{\rm Irr}({\bf C}_{K}(P_{0}))
be the Glauberman correspondent of
\theta_{i}
(see
[
Na18
, Theorem 2.9]
, for instance) and let

be the stabilizer of
\theta_{i}
in

. Since the Glauberman correspondence and the action of
{\bf N}_{G}(P_{0})
commute (see
[
Na18
, Lemma 2.10]
), it follows that
{\bf N}_{T_{i}}(P_{0})=T_{i}\cap{\bf N}_{G}(P_{0})
is the stabilizer of
\theta_{i}^{*}
in
{\bf N}_{G}(P_{0})
.

Again as in the proof of
[
Ri19
, Theorem A]
we obtain a bijection

{}^{*}:{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(T_{i}\,|\,\theta_{i})\rightarrow{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{T_{i}}(P_{0})\,|\,\theta_{i}^{*})

satisfying
\psi(1)/\psi^{*}(1)=\theta_{i}(1)/\theta_{i}^{*}(1)
. Hence we have that
\psi^{*}(1)
divides
\psi(1)
by the main result of
[
Ge20
]
. Moreover, since

we have that
|K:{\bf C}_{K}(P_{0})|=|T_{i}:{\bf N}_{T_{i}}(P_{0})|
and since

divides
|K:{\bf C}_{K}(P_{0})|
we conclude that
\psi(1)/\psi^{*}(1)
divides
|T_{i}:{\bf N}_{T_{i}}(P_{0})|
.

The remaining part of the proof proceeds exactly as in the proof of
[
Ri19
, Theorem A]
.
∎

Setting notation:

if

or
p\nmid(m-1)
. Hence we may now assume that further

and
p\mid(m-1)
.
Then setting

yields
\chi_{\lambda}\in{\rm Irr}(B_{p}(\mathfrak{S}_{n}))
and
\chi_{\lambda}(1)=Y^{\prime}
, which is prime to

, we have

, which forces

.

So we are reduced to the case

,
b=(q-a_{1})q^{t_{1}}=p-1
, and
p\mid(m-1)
. Then the partition
(1^{mp-1},1+b)
gives a character in

with degree

but is not divisible by

Finally, note that in all cases, the

described is not self-conjugate, and hence the characters restrict irreducibly to

, completing the proof.
∎

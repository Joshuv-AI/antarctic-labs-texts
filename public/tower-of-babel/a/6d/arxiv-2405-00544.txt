1.
Introduction and main results

The objective of this paper is to improve the results of
[
12
]
on averages of short and maximal sums of a Dirichlet character whose (group-theoretic) order is large. In
[
12
]
, the first author considered primitive Dirichlet characters
\chi
modulo a prime
q
with order
d
, under the assumption that
d=d(q)\rightarrow\infty
as
q\rightarrow\infty
.
In that work the first author investigated how this assumption on
d
influenced the sizes of the short sums

S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x):=\sum_{n\leq x}\chi^{\ell}(n),\quad x>q^{\delta}

for arbitrary fixed
\delta>0
, and the maximal sums

M(\chi^{\ell}):=\max_{1\leq t\leq q}\left|\sum_{n\leq t}\chi^{\ell}(n)\right|

for
1\leq\ell\leq d-1
. The methods of
[
12
]
had the defect that they only yielded non-trivial results under the assumption that the least prime factor of
d
was also assumed to be large.

In this paper, we rectify this shortcoming by presenting (quantitatively stronger) analogues of the theorems in
[
12
]
in which assumptions on the size of the prime factors of
d
are removed. Moreover, the results in this paper apply to general moduli
q
, rather than just to prime
q
.

Our first main theorem is an alternative bound, which states that in the régime that
d\rightarrow\infty
with
q
, either
|S_{\chi}(x)|=o(x)
or else the mean-square average of the short character sums
S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)
with length
x=q^{\delta}
exhibits cancellation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\geq 3
and let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q
with order
d\geq 2
. Then there is an absolute constant
c>0
such that if
\tau\in(0,1/2)
,

\delta:=\max\left\{\left(\frac{\log\log(ed)}{c\log(ed)}\right)^{1/2},(\log q)^{-c}\right\},

and

then at least one of the following is true:

(1)

\chi
itself satisfies

\frac{1}{x}\left|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\right|\ll_{\tau}\frac{1}{(\log\log(ed))^{1/6-\tau}};

(2)

we have the average bound

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d}\left|\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}\chi^{\ell}(n)\right|^{2}\ll_{\tau}\frac{1}{(\log\log(ed)^{1/6-\tau}}.

This should be compared with
[

, Thm. 2]
, in which the averaged bound was only non-trivial under the assumption that
P^{-}(d)\rightarrow\infty
with

, and the savings only comparable if
P^{-}(d)\gg\log\log d
.

As in
[
12
]
, a mean-square bound like Theorem

(together with some additional inputs) may be used to prove a paucity phenomenon for the level sets of
\chi
. In this direction, our second main theorem provides a non-trivial upper bound for the cardinality of the set of solutions

with
\chi(n)=\alpha
, for any fixed

, whenever

and

, wherein the condition that

Theorem 1.2
.

There are absolute constant

such that the following holds.

Let

and let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo

with order

Remark 1
.

Let us show that Theorem

indeed generalises
[
12
, Thm. 1]
. If

is squarefree and

then by the prime number theorem,

d/d_{z}\leq\prod_{p\leq z}p\leq e^{2z}\asymp(\log d)^{2},

provided

is thus of quality

, which is comparable (albeit with a less explicit

power) with
[
12
, Thm. 1]
.

Remark 2
.

To explain the form of the upper bound given in Theorem

it is helpful to consider a case, not covered in
[
12
, Thm. 1]
, where
d
is a prime power, say
d=2^{k}
. In this case,
d_{2}=1
, so that, taking

, the upper bound provided by Theorem

is precisely

(2)

\max_{\alpha^{2^{k}}=1}\frac{1}{x}|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|\leq\frac{1}{2}+o_{k\rightarrow\infty}(1)

(and indeed this is the worst-possible bound that (

implies the bound

\max_{\alpha^{2^{k}}=1}\frac{1}{x}|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|\ll\frac{1}{(\log k)^{1/13}}\text{ whenever }|S_{\chi}(x)|>\frac{x}{(\log k)^{2/13}},

which is of course much stronger as

. In the converse case that
|S_{\chi}(x)|
is small, however, the following heuristically plausible scenario is consistent with (
2
).

Suppose that
\chi
has order
2^{k}
, but satisfies
\chi(p)=\pm 1
for all
p\leq x
and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\chi(p)=-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

Thus,
\chi
is a real-valued multiplicative function on
[1,x]
. By a theorem of Hall and Tenenbaum
[
7
]
, we obtain

\left|S_{\chi}(x)\right|\ll x\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1-\chi(p)}{p}\right)=o_{k\rightarrow\infty}(x).

Since
\chi(n)\in\{-1,+1\}
for all
n\leq x
, this is equivalent to

\max_{\alpha\in\{-1,+1\}}\frac{1}{x}|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|=\frac{1}{2}+o_{k\rightarrow\infty}(1),

which is precisely of the form (
2
).

We obtain the following straightforward consequence of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume the notation and hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
, and let

\delta:=\max\left\{\left(\frac{\log\log(eP^{+}(d))}{c_{1}\log(eP^{+}(d))}\right)^{1/2},(\log q)^{-c_{1}}\right\}.

Then if

we get

\max_{\alpha^{d}=1}\frac{1}{x}|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|\ll\frac{1}{(\log\log(eP^{+}(d)))^{c_{2}}}.

Corollary

shows that as long as
P^{+}(d)\rightarrow\infty
with
d
, the level sets
|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|
are sparse as soon as
x>q^{\delta}
, for any fixed, but otherwise arbitrary,
\delta>0
. Note that this property is fairly generic, only excluding orders
d
that are very smooth (and hence rare).

In
[
12
]
the first author also gave a non-trivial average bound for the maximal character sums
M(\chi^{\ell})
,
1\leq\ell\leq d-1
.
The Pólya-Vinogradov inequality states that for a non-principal character
\psi
of modulus
m
we have
M(\psi)\ll\sqrt{m}\log m
. It is a long-standing open problem to obtain unconditional improvements (as
m\rightarrow\infty
) to this bound for general
\psi
. In
[
12
]
(see Theorem 3 there), the bound

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d-1}M(\chi^{\ell})\ll\left(\sqrt{q}\log q\right)\left(\frac{1}{P^{-}(d)}+\sqrt{\frac{\log\log\log q}{\log\log q}}\right)

was obtained by appealing to combinatorial arguments. Clearly, this bound is non-trivial only when
d
has no small prime factors and therefore must be
odd
. Well-known work of Granville and Soundararajan
[
5
]
(with refinements in
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
) previously showed that
M(\chi)=o(\sqrt{q}\log q)
whenever
\chi
has
odd
order
d=o(\sqrt{\log\log q})
, so that this result is
only new
when

d\gg\sqrt{\log\log q}\text{ and }P^{-}(d)\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }d\rightarrow\infty.

Our next theorem remedies this situation, providing non-trivial bounds as soon as
d\rightarrow\infty
(including the case that
d
is even).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
q\geq 3
and let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q
with order
d\geq 2
. Then at least one of the following statements is true:

(i)

\chi
itself satisfies

M(\chi)\ll\frac{\sqrt{q}\log q}{(\log\log(ed))^{1/8}}

(ii)

we have

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d-1}M(\chi^{\ell})\ll\frac{\sqrt{q}\log q}{(\log\log(ed))^{1/8}}.

1.1.
Proof strategy

Let us describe separately the strategy of proof of each of the main theorems in this paper.

1.1.1.
On averages of short character sums

The proof of Theorem
1.1
largely follows the line of attack of
[

]
, introducing refinements of the key lemmas at several junctures.

Ideally, we would like to prove that

As in

, given small parameters

\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon):=\{1\leq\ell\leq d-1:|S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)|\geq\varepsilon x\},\quad x>q^{\delta}.

If

Suppose instead that

1.1.2.
A new bound for level sets of
\chi

To prove Theorem
1.2
we employ three observations (see Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
below), two of which are already present in
[
12
]
. Firstly, if
b|d
,
\alpha
is a
d
th order root of unity and
\beta:=\alpha^{b}
then we have the trivial inclusion

\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}\subseteq\{n\leq x:\chi^{b}(n)=\beta\}.

This allows us to replace a bound for the level sets of
\chi
of order
d
by those of
\psi:=\chi^{b}
of some order
d^{\prime}=d/b
dividing
d
. As discussed below, this sometimes presents an advantage.

Secondly, the level sets of
\chi
can be linked to the
L^{2}
averages of the powers
\chi^{\ell}
. This follows by orthogonality modulo
d
from the formula

S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\chi^{\ell}(n)=\sum_{\alpha^{d}=1}\alpha^{\ell}|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|.

Therefore, whenever a non-trivial bound is available for the
L^{2}
average

(5)

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d}|S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)|^{2},

we obtain correspondingly non-trivial bounds for all level sets
|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|
. By Theorem
1.1
this is true as long as
|S_{\chi}(x)|
is large.

The third key observation concerns the converse case, namely when
|S_{\chi}(x)|
is small (and therefore non-trivial bounds for (
5
) do not follow from Theorem
1.1
). In this case, we may arrive at a bound for the level sets by interpreting the event
\chi(n)=\alpha=e(a/d)
,
0\leq a\leq d-1
, as a constraint on the distribution of the complex argument
\theta_{n}\in[0,1)
of
\chi(n)
(provided
(n,q)=1
), i.e.,

\chi(n)=e(\theta_{n})\text{ with }\theta_{n}\in\left[\frac{a}{d},\frac{a+1}{d}\right).

Since this interval has measure
1/d
, if
\theta_{n}
were uniformly distributed we would expect the number of such
n\leq x
to have size
\sim x/d
. Using the Erdős-Turán inequality to control the deviation from this heuristic, we show that
|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|
may be bounded above by

(6)

\frac{x}{d}+\frac{x}{K+1}+O\left(\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\frac{1}{k}|S_{\chi^{k}}(x)|\right)\text{ for any }K\geq 1.

Knowing that
|S_{\chi}(x)|
is small, we use the pretentious theory of multiplicative functions to show (in certain key cases where upper bounds for (
5
) are not available) that in fact
|S_{\chi^{k}}(x)|
is also small whenever
1\leq k<P^{-}(d)
. This can be understood as being due to

\chi^{k}(p)\neq 1\text{ whenever }\chi(p)\neq 1\text{ and }1\leq k<P^{-}(d),

so that
\chi^{k}
retains much of the oscillation exhibited when
\chi
has small partial sums. The upshot of this is that we may then select
K=P^{-}(d)-1
in (
6
). This bound presents no advantage when
P^{-}(d)
is quite small, but can be strengthened in the case that the contribution to
d
from its small prime factors is small. More precisely, applying the first observation above with

d^{\prime}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}||d\\
p>z\end{subarray}}p^{k}\text{ for any }1\leq z\leq\log\log(ed)

allows us (after replacing
d
and
\chi
by
d^{\prime}
and
\chi^{d/d^{\prime}}
, respectively) to apply (
6
) with
K=P^{-}(d^{\prime})-1>z-1
instead. This improves the bound, as long as
d^{\prime}
is sufficiently large.

1.1.3.
On averages of maximal sums

Our expectation is that the first alternative of Theorem
1.4
always holds, but here we will mainly focus on the consequences if it fails. As in
[
12
]
, given a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
, we investigate the structure of

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon):=\{1\leq\ell\leq d-1:|M(\chi^{\ell})|\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{q}\log q\}.

If
|\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon)|\leq\varepsilon d
then the average size of
M(\chi^{\ell})
is
\ll\varepsilon\sqrt{q}\log q
. In other words, for most
1\leq\ell\leq d-1
,
M(\chi^{\ell})
admits a sharper upper bound than what the Pólya-Vinogradov inequality provides. Our goal is to show that
|\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon)|
is indeed of size

.

By Proposition
2.1
and Lemma

, bounding

reduces to the estimation of a logarithmic sum

\displaystyle L_{\chi^{\ell}\overline{\gamma}_{\ell}}(N):=\sum\limits_{n\leq N}\frac{\chi^{\ell}\overline{\gamma}_{\ell}(n)}{n},

where
\gamma_{\ell}
is some Dirichlet character of small conductor determined by
\chi^{\ell}
, and
N=N_{\ell}\in[1,q]
. In turn, this can be related via standard estimates for logarithmic averages of multiplicative functions, to the prime sum

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p\leq q}\frac{1-\text{Re}(\chi^{\ell}\overline{\gamma}_{\ell}(p))}{p}.

In the same vein as the structure theorem for
\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon)

to which the characters
\chi^{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon)

such that

is a subgroup

, where
|H|=d/g\gg_{\varepsilon}d
. Assuming
\chi^{g}
“pretends to be” a primitive Dirichlet character
\xi

also “pretends to be”

(in a manner that is uniform in

\displaystyle\max_{\ell\in H}\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{1-\text{Re}(\psi^{\ell}(p))}{p}=O_{\varepsilon}(1).

which is of the same shape as (

We have thus reduced matters in this problem to a situation similar to that of the short character sums problem, replacing
\chi^{\ell}
by

In particular, there must exist
\ell\in\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon)
for which
\|j_{0}\ell/d\|>\theta/m
. Note that by our parameter choices, we have

\frac{m^{2}\log(1/\varepsilon)}{\sigma_{j_{0}}(z)}<\frac{2m^{2}\eta^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)}{\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\ll\varepsilon^{6+\tau-4-\tau/2}=\varepsilon^{2+\tau/2}\leq\varepsilon^{\tau/4}\frac{\theta}{m}<\varepsilon^{\tau/4}\left\|\frac{j_{0}\ell}{d}\right\|.

Taking
\rho=1/4
and applying Proposition
4.2
we thus find that if

1\leq k\leq\min\left\{\frac{M}{3},\sqrt{\eta\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right\}

then for any
1\leq\ell\leq r
for which
r\nmid j_{0}\ell
we have

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}|S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)|\ll k!\left\|\frac{j_{0}\ell}{r}\right\|^{-k}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\eta\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right)^{k/2}+\frac{1}{\eta M^{1/4}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}+x^{-1/6}\right).

By Proposition
3.1
,
\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon)\subseteq m\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
. We deduce using
k!\leq k^{k}
that

\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{x}|S_{\chi^{g\ell}}(x)|\ll\left(\left(\frac{(km)^{2}}{\eta\theta^{2}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right)^{k/2}+\frac{(2km/\theta)^{k}}{\eta M^{1/4}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right).

As
m\leq\varepsilon^{-2}
, we get that

(29)

\varepsilon\ll\left(\left(\frac{k^{2}\varepsilon^{-4}}{\eta\theta^{2}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right)^{k/2}+\frac{(2k/(\varepsilon^{2}\theta))^{k}}{\eta M^{1/4}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right).

Set now
\eta=\theta=\varepsilon^{\tau/30}
, choose
M
so that
\log M=\frac{\eta}{3}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)
and select

k:=\left\lfloor\frac{10}{\tau}\sqrt{\varepsilon^{4+\tau/2}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right\rfloor.

We then find that if
d
is sufficiently large relative to
\tau
then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k^{2}\varepsilon^{-4}}{\eta\theta^{2}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\right)^{k/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(100\tau^{-2}\varepsilon^{2\tau/5}\right)^{\frac{5}{\tau}}\ll_{\tau}\varepsilon^{2},

on the one hand, and also

\displaystyle\frac{(2k/(\varepsilon^{2}\theta))^{k}\varepsilon^{-1}}{\eta M^{1/4}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{31/30}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{31/30}}{12}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)+10\frac{\varepsilon^{5/2}}{\tau}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)^{1/2}\log\Sigma_{\chi}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\ll\Sigma_{\chi}(x)^{-2/3}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{24}\Sigma_{\chi}(x)}\ll\Sigma_{\chi}(x)^{-100}

\displaystyle\ll\varepsilon^{600},

on the other. It follows that (
29
) yields a contradiction.

We conclude, therefore, that
|\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon)|\leq\varepsilon d
, and therefore

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d}|S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)|^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{d}(\varepsilon)|}{d}\leq\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon=\Sigma_{\chi}(x)^{-1/(6+\tau)}\ll(\log\log d)^{-\tfrac{1}{6}+\tau}
by Proposition
2.5
, the proof of the theorem follows.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
. For convenience, we introduce the notation

M_{d,\chi}(x):=\max_{\alpha^{d}=1}|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|.

We begin by establishing a couple of lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

M_{d,\chi}(x)\leq\min_{r|d}M_{r,\chi^{d/r}}(x).

Proof.

It clearly suffices to prove that
M_{d,\chi}(x)\leq M_{r,\chi^{d/r}}(x)
for each
r|d
. Thus, fix
r|d
and let
\psi:=\chi^{d/r}
. Let
\alpha
be a
d
th order root of unity for which

M_{d,\chi}(x)=|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|,

and let
\beta:=\alpha^{d/r}
. Then
\psi(n)=\beta
whenever
\chi(n)=\alpha
. Since
\beta
is a root of unity of order
r
, we obtain

M_{d,\chi}(x)\leq|\{n\leq x:\psi(n)=\beta\}|\leq M_{r,\psi}(x),

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\chi
be a character modulo
q
of order
d
, and let
K\geq 1
. Then

M_{d,\chi}(x)\leq\min\left\{\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq d}|S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}},\,\frac{x}{d}+\frac{x}{K+1}+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\frac{|S_{\chi^{k}}(x)|}{k}\right\}.

Proof.

We prove each of the above bounds in sequence. The proof of the first, which is already invoked in
[
12
, Sec. 3.2]
, is as follows. Given any
d
th root of unity
\alpha
we have

\displaystyle|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|=|\{n,m\leq x:\chi(n)=\chi(m)=\alpha\}|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq|\{n,m\leq x:\chi(n)=\chi(m)\}|^{1/2}

(30)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}|S_{\chi^{\ell}}(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Maximising over
\alpha
, the first alternative bound for
M_{d,\chi}(x)
holds.

For the second bound,
set
N:=|\{n\leq x:(n,q)=1\}
and for each
(n,q)=1
write
\chi(n)=e(\theta_{n})
for some
\theta_{n}\in[0,1]
. Specifying that
\chi(n)=\alpha=e(a/d)
is equivalent to

\theta_{n}\in\left[\frac{a}{d},\frac{a+1}{d}\right).

By the Erdős-Turán inequality (in the form given in
[
14
, I.6.15]
), for any
K\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{d}+\sup_{I\subseteq[0,1)}\left|\,|\{n\leq x:\,(n,q)=1,\,\theta_{n}\in I\}|-|I|N\,\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\frac{1}{d}+\frac{1}{K+1}+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\frac{1}{k}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(k\theta_{n})\right|\right)

(31)

\displaystyle\leq x\left(\frac{1}{d}+\frac{1}{K+1}+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\frac{1}{k}\left|\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)^{k}\right|\right).

This implies the claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
c_{1}>0
be a sufficiently small constant to be determined later. Given
1\leq z\leq\log\log d
, write

\delta_{z}:=\max\left\{\left(\frac{\log\log(ed_{z})}{c_{1}\log(ed_{z})}\right)^{1/2},(\log q)^{-c_{1}}\right\},\quad d_{z}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}||d\\
p>z\end{subarray}}\text{ and }r_{z}:=d/d_{z}.

By Lemma
5.1
we have

M_{d,\chi}(x)\leq\min_{r|d}M_{r,\chi^{d/r}}(x)\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\log\log d\\
x>q^{\delta_{z}}\end{subarray}}M_{d_{z},\chi^{r_{z}}}(x).

For ease of notation, we write
\tilde{d}:=d_{z_{0}}
and
\tilde{\chi}:=\chi^{r_{z_{0}}}
, where
1\leq z_{0}\leq\log\log d
minimises this latter upper bound, subject to the condition that
x>q^{\delta_{z_{0}}}
. In the remainder of the proof, we show that

(32)

M_{\tilde{d},\tilde{\chi}}(x)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{P^{-}(\tilde{d})}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log\log\tilde{d})^{c}}\right)\right),

and since
P^{-}(\tilde{d})>z_{0}
this provides the required upper bound.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, let
\varepsilon:=\Sigma_{\chi}(x)^{-1/7}

Case 1:
Assume first that

\phi(q)/q<\frac{1}{(\log d)^{1/10}}
. Picking
K:=\lfloor(\log d)^{1/10}\rfloor
in (
31
), together with the uniform upper bound

\left|S_{\tilde{\chi}^{k}}(x)\right|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{\phi(q)}{q}x\leq\frac{x}{(\log d)^{1/5}}

(using
[
6
]
as before), we deduce that

M_{\tilde{d},\tilde{\chi}}(x)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{d}}+\varepsilon+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log d)^{1/5}}\sum_{1\leq k\leq(\log d)^{1/10}}\frac{1}{k}\right)\right)\ll\frac{x\log\log d}{(\log\tilde{d})^{1/5}},

which is more than enough suffices for the bound we seek. In the remaining cases we shall assume that
\phi(q)/q>\frac{1}{(\log d)^{1/10}}
, and in particular by Proposition
2.5
,
\Sigma_{\chi}(x)>c\log\log d
for some absolute constant
c>0
. We assume this lower bound henceforth.

Case 2:
Assume next that
|\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)|\leq\varepsilon\tilde{d}
. By (
30
), we have

\displaystyle M_{\tilde{d},\tilde{\chi}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{d}}\sum_{\ell\notin\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)}|S_{\tilde{\chi}^{\ell}}(x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{\tilde{d}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)}|S_{\tilde{\chi}^{\ell}}(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\varepsilon^{2}x^{2}+x^{2}\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)|}{\tilde{d}}\right)^{1/2}\leq 2\sqrt{\varepsilon}x\ll\frac{x}{(\log\log\tilde{d})^{1/14}},

which is more than sufficient.

Case 3:
Assume
|\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)|>\varepsilon\tilde{d}
and

\varepsilon^{-7}=\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)\geq c\log\log\tilde{d}
.
We consider several subcases below.

Case 3.(i):
Assume that
g>1
in Proposition 3.1. Since
g|\tilde{d}
, we have
g\geq P^{-}(\tilde{d})
, and as
\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)\subseteq\{g\ell:1\leq\ell<\tilde{d}/g\}
we have that
|S_{\tilde{\chi}^{k}}(x)|\leq\varepsilon x
for all
1\leq k<g
. Applying (
31
) with
K=g-1
and recalling that
g\leq\varepsilon^{-1}
, we get

M_{\tilde{d},\tilde{\chi}}(x)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{d}}+\frac{1}{g}+O\left(\varepsilon\log g\right)\right)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{P^{-}(\tilde{d})}+O\left(\frac{\log\log\log\tilde{d}}{(\log\log\tilde{d})^{1/7}}\right)\right),

which implies the bound (
32
) in this case.
We will assume for the remaining subcases that
g=1
.

Case 3.(ii)
If
|S_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)|>\varepsilon x
then as
x>q^{\delta_{z_{0}}}
, Theorem
1.1
immediately implies that

\frac{1}{\tilde{d}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\tilde{d}-1}|S_{\tilde{\chi}^{\ell}}(x)|^{2}\ll\frac{x}{(\log\log\tilde{d})^{1/7}},

and the claimed bound (with
c=1/14
) follows from (
30
).

Case 3. (iii)
If
|S_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)|\leq\varepsilon x
and (recalling
g=1
)
\max_{1\leq j\leq\tilde{d}-1}\sigma_{j}(x)\leq\eta\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)
then we obtain a contradiction to
|\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)|>\varepsilon\tilde{d}
as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(as this part of the argument did not require
|S_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)|>\varepsilon x
). We deduce that
|\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)|\leq\varepsilon\tilde{d}
, and the claim follows from Case 2 above.

Case 3.(iv)
Assume that
|S_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)|\leq\varepsilon x
,
\sigma_{j_{0}}(x)=\max_{1\leq j\leq\tilde{d}-1}\sigma_{j}(x)>\eta\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)
and (as

)
(j_{0},\tilde{d})\leq\theta\tilde{d}
, noting that here

. From this, we deduce by Proposition
4.3

that there exists an

such that

. Following the proof of Theorem
1.1
, this was enough to deduce a contradiction to the assumption
|\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)|>\varepsilon\tilde{d}
, and so the claim of the current proposition follows once again from Case 2.

Case 3. (v)
Finally, we assume the following data:

•

g=1
, so
r=\tilde{d}

•

|S_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)|\leq\varepsilon x
,

•

\sigma_{j_{0}}(x)>\eta\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)

•

\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)>c\log\log\tilde{d}

•

(j_{0},\tilde{d})>\theta\tilde{d}
.

Set
R:=\tilde{d}/(j_{0},\tilde{d})<\theta^{-1}
, and note that for each
1\leq k\leq R-1
we have

\left\|\frac{j_{0}k}{\tilde{d}}\right\|=\left\|\frac{k(j_{0}/(j_{0},\tilde{d}))}{R}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{R},

since
j/(j_{0},\tilde{d})
is coprime to

. It follows from equation (

) that for every
1\leq k\leq R-1
,

\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},1;x)^{2}\geq 8\sum_{1\leq j\leq\tilde{d}-1}\left\|\frac{jk}{\tilde{d}}\right\|^{2}\sigma_{j}(x)\geq 8\left\|\frac{j_{0}k}{\tilde{d}}\right\|^{2}\sigma_{j_{0}}(x)>\frac{8\eta}{R^{2}}\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)\geq 8\eta\theta^{2}\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x).

Now, either (a)
k\notin\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)
, or else (b)
k\in m\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{d}}(\varepsilon)
. In case (a) we immediately have
|S_{\tilde{\chi}^{k}}(x)|\leq\varepsilon x
. Thus, assume that (b) holds.
From the proof of Proposition 3.1, if

is a minimiser for
\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},n^{it};x)
from

then

Now, by the pretentious triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},n^{it_{k}};x)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},1;x)-\mathbb{D}(1,n^{it_{k}};x).

Given the bound
\eta,\theta>\varepsilon^{1/8}
, we have

\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},1;x)>\left(8\eta\theta^{2}\right)^{1/2}\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)^{1/2}>2\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{3/16-7/2}\geq 2\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{-3}>20\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\varepsilon)\geq 2\mathbb{D}(1,n^{it_{k}};x).

whenever
\tilde{d}
is sufficiently large. Hence we have

\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},n^{it_{k}};x)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},1;x)^{2}>2\eta\theta^{2}\Sigma_{\tilde{\chi}}(x)>2c\varepsilon^{-6}.

Applying Halász’ theorem (with
T=m/\varepsilon^{2}
), we obtain, for each

|S_{\tilde{\chi}^{k}}(x)|\ll x\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},n^{it_{k}};x)^{2}e^{-\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\chi}^{k},n^{it_{k}};x)^{2}}\right)\ll x\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{-6}\exp\left(-c\varepsilon^{-6}\right)\right)\ll\varepsilon^{2}x.

It follows that in either of cases (a) and (b),
|S_{\chi^{k}}(x)|\ll\varepsilon x
for all
1\leq k\leq R-1
.

Applying this in (
31
) with
K=R\leq\theta^{-1}
, we get

M_{\tilde{d},\tilde{\chi}}(x)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{d}}+\frac{1}{R}+O\left(\varepsilon^{2}\log R\right)\right)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{R}+O\left(\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)\right)\right).

Since
j_{0}<\tilde{d}
, we have
(j_{0},\tilde{d})\leq\tilde{d}/P^{-}(\tilde{d})
, and therefore as
\varepsilon\gg(\log\log\tilde{d})^{-1/6}
, we obtain

M_{\tilde{d},\tilde{\chi}}(x)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{P^{-}(\tilde{d})}+O\left(\frac{\log\log\log\tilde{d}}{(\log\log\tilde{d})^{1/7}}\right)\right).

Conclusion:
Summarising all of the above cases, we obtain, for some
c\in(0,1/14]
,

M_{\tilde{d},\tilde{\chi}}(x)\leq x\left(\frac{1}{P^{-}(\tilde{d})}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log\log\tilde{d})^{c}}\right)\right),

as claimed.
∎

. Taking

from below, and letting

,

\max_{\alpha^{d}=1}\frac{1}{x}|\{n\leq x:\chi(n)=\alpha\}|\leq\left(\frac{1}{P^{+}(d)}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log\log d_{z})^{c_{2}}}\right)\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log\log P^{+}(d))^{c_{2}}},

6.
Averaged Maximal Character Sums: Proof of Theorem
1.4

Our strategy towards proving Theorem
1.4
will be similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, recall that

\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon):=\{1\leq\ell\leq d:M(\chi^{\ell})>\varepsilon\sqrt{q}\log q\}.

Assuming that
|\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon)|\geq\varepsilon d
, Proposition
3.2
shows that there are positive integers
m\leq\varepsilon^{-2}
,
g\leq\varepsilon^{-1}
and
k\leq e^{-3m}
, and a Dirichlet character
\xi\pmod{k}
of order dividing
r=d/g
such that

\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon)\subseteq\{g\ell:1\leq\ell\leq r\}=m\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon),

and furthermore

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq r}\mathbb{D}(\chi^{g\ell},\xi^{\ell};x)^{2}\ll m^{2}\log(1/\varepsilon).

Set
\psi:=\chi^{g}\overline{\xi}
, so that
\psi^{r}
is principal. Write
\omega=e(1/r)
and put

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\psi}(q)=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\tilde{\sigma}_{j}(q),\text{ where }\tilde{\sigma}_{j}(q)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq q,\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}.

We will study the influence of the sizes of the prime sums
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}(q)
on the maximal sums
M(\chi^{\ell})
. Throughout this section, fix
\eta\in(0,1)
to be a small parameter.

6.1.
Small
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}
case

We assume first of all that
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}(q)\leq\tfrac{\eta}{g}\Sigma_{\psi}(q)
for all
1\leq j\leq r
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\eta>0
be sufficiently small, and suppose
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}(q)\leq\frac{\eta}{g}\Sigma_{\psi}(q)
for all
1\leq j\leq r-1
.
Then there are elements
1\leq\ell\leq r
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\chi^{g\ell},\xi^{\ell};q)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\psi}(q).

Proof.

Since
\psi=\chi^{g}\overline{\xi}
has order dividing
r
and

\mathbb{D}(\chi^{g\ell},\xi^{\ell};q)=\mathbb{D}(\psi^{\ell},1;q)

for each
1\leq\ell\leq r
, the result follows upon applying Proposition
4.1
to
\psi
in place of
\chi^{g}
(as
\Sigma_{\chi}(x)\geq\Sigma_{\chi^{g}}(x)
there).
∎

6.2.
Large
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}
case

Next, we assume that
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}(q)>\tfrac{\eta}{g}\Sigma_{\psi}(q)
for some
j=j_{0}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose there is
1\leq j_{0}\leq r-1
such that
\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(q)>\frac{\eta}{g}\Sigma_{\psi}(q)
. For each
1\leq\ell\leq r
let
1\leq N_{\ell}\leq q
satisfy

|L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})|=\max_{1\leq N\leq q}|L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N)|.

Then for any
1\leq\ell\leq r
,

\displaystyle\frac{|L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})|}{\log q}\ll\min\Bigg\{1,\Big\|\frac{j_{0}g\ell}{d}\Big\|^{-1}\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\log q)\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}}\Bigg\}.

Proof.

For
g\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{d}(\varepsilon)
, by the trivial bound

\displaystyle\varepsilon\log q\ll|L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})|\leq\log N_{\ell},

we have
N_{\ell}\gg q^{\varepsilon}
. We introduce the completely additive function

\displaystyle\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}|n,\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}1.

By the complete multiplicativity of
\psi
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})}{\log q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log q}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{\psi^{\ell}(n)}{n}\cdot\frac{\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)+\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})-\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}

(33)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})\log q}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}mp^{k}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi^{\ell}(p)^{k}}{p^{k}}\frac{\psi^{\ell}(m)}{m}+O\Bigg(\frac{1}{\log q}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{|\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)/\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})-1|}{n}\Bigg).

We first estimate the error term above. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(34)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{|\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)/\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})-1|}{n}\leq\Bigg(\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{|\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)/\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})-1|^{2}}{n}\Bigg)^{1/2}\sqrt{\log N_{\ell}}.

We will show that

(35)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{|\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)/\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})-1|^{2}}{n}=O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\right).

After expanding the square on the left-hand side, to prove (
35
) it suffices to show that for
j=0,1,2
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{(\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)/\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell}))^{j}}{n}=\log N_{\ell}+O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\right).

This bound clearly holds when
j=0
. When
j=1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}mp^{k}=n,\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{mp^{k}}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{k}}\sum\limits_{m\leq N_{\ell}/p^{k}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{k}}(\log N_{\ell}-k\log p+O(1)).

Using Mertens’ theorem, we find that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{k\log p}{p^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\log N_{\ell}+\sum\limits_{2\leq k\leq\log N_{\ell}}k\sum\limits_{2\leq n\leq N_{\ell}^{1/k}}\frac{1}{n^{k-1/2}}

\displaystyle\ll\log N_{\ell}+\sum\limits_{2\leq k\leq\log N_{\ell}}\frac{k}{k-3/2}\ll\log N_{\ell}.

We also note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell}\\
k\geq 2\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{p^{k}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell}\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{k}}\ll\log N_{\ell}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)}{n}=\log N_{\ell}+O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\right).

Finally, when
j=2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})^{2}}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{\Omega_{j_{0}}(n)^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})^{2}}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}^{k_{1}}|n,\\
\psi(p_{1})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}^{k_{2}}|n,\\
\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})^{2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p_{1})=\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N_{\ell},\\
p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}|n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})^{2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}]\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p_{1})=\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}]}\left(\log(N_{\ell}/[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}])+O(1)\right).

Using Mertens’ theorem, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}]\leq N_{\ell}\\
\psi(p_{1})=\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}]}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[p_{1},p_{2}]\leq N_{\ell}\\
\psi(p_{1})=\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[p_{1},p_{2}]}+O(\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell}))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\leq N_{\ell}\\
\psi(p_{1})=\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[p_{1},p_{2}]}+O(\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}]\leq N_{\ell}\\
\psi(p_{1})=\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}]}{[p_{1}^{k_{1}},p_{2}^{k_{2}}]}\ll\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}^{k_{1}}\leq N_{\ell}\\
\psi(p_{1})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{k_{1}}}\right)\left(\sum_{p_{2}^{k_{2}}\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{\log p_{2}^{k_{2}}}{p_{2}^{k_{2}}}\right)\ll\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})\log N_{\ell}.

Inserting these bounds in the previous estimates, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell}))^{2}}\sum\limits_{n\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{(\Omega_{j_{0}}(n))^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell}))^{2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p_{1}),\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[p_{1},p_{2}]}+O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell}))^{2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\\
p_{2}\leq N_{\ell},p_{1}\neq p_{2}\\
\psi(p_{1}),\psi(p_{2})=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p_{1}p_{2}}+O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\right)

\displaystyle=\log N_{\ell}+O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}\right),

as required.

Combining (
35
) with (
34
) and combining the result into (
33
), we find that

\displaystyle\frac{L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})}{\log q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})\log q}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi^{\ell}(p)^{k}}{p^{k}}\sum\limits_{m\leq N_{\ell}/p^{k}}\frac{\psi^{\ell}(m)}{m}+O\Bigg(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\log q)\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})\log q}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\omega^{j_{0}k\ell}}{p^{k}}L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell}/p^{k})+O\Bigg(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\log q)\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}}\Bigg).

Since whenever
p^{k}\leq N_{\ell}
we have

\displaystyle L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell}/p^{k})=L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})+O\left(\sum_{N_{\ell}/p^{k}<m\leq N_{\ell}}\frac{1}{m}\right)=L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})+O(k\log p),

we obtain using Mertens’ theorem again that

\displaystyle\frac{L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})}{\log q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})\log q}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\omega^{j_{0}k\ell}}{p^{k}}\Big(L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})+O(k\log p)\Big)+O\Bigg(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\log q)\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{j_{0}\ell}L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})}{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})\log q}\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq N_{\ell},\\
\psi(p)=\omega^{j_{0}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)+O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\log q)\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega^{j_{0}\ell}\frac{L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})}{\log q}+O\left(\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\log q)\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}}\right).

Rearranging this expression and using the bound

, we deduce that

\frac{|L_{\psi^{\ell}}(N_{\ell})|}{\log q}\ll\left\|\frac{j_{0}\ell}{r}\right\|^{-1}\frac{\log N_{\ell}}{(\log q)\sqrt{\tilde{\sigma}_{j_{0}}(N_{\ell})}},

as claimed.
∎

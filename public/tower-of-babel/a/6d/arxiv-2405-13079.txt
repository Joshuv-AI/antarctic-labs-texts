On optical appearance of Einstein-Maxwell-Æther black holes surrounded by various accretions
Mitra Darvishi, Malihe Heydari-Fard and Morteza Mohseni

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the effects of the æther field and the electric charge on the observed shadow of two types of charged black holes in the Einstein-Maxwell-Æther theory. By considering that the Einstein-Maxwell-Æther black holes surrounded by the static/infalling spherical accretion flows, as well as an optically and geometrically thin disk accretion flow, we study the shadow luminosities and the observed specific intensity of the image for these various profiles of accretion flows. We find that in the thin disk accretion model the location and the emitted model of the accretion gas affect on the optical appearance of charged Einstein-Æther black holes in contrast to the spherical accretion flows. For a thin disk profile, we show that the observer will receive more intensity for an emitted model as Gaussian function when the innermost radiation radius lies in the innermost stable circular orbit. Finally, comparing the results of the charged Einstein-Æther black holes with neutral Einstein-Æther black holes, we show that the charged Einstein-Æther black holes have smaller dark area, whereas wider lensed ring and photon ring. Comparing the charged Einstein-Æther black holes with Reissner-Nordstrom black hole gives a reverse effect. Keywords : Black hole shadow, The accretion flow, Modified theories of gravity

Einstein-Maxwell-Æther theory

Let us briefly review the Einstein-Maxwell-Æther theory of gravity. The action of the theory is defined
as

{\cal{S}}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{16\pi G_{{\ae}}}\left(R+{\cal L}_{{\ae}}\right)+{\cal L}_{M}\right],

Here,

(8)

(9)

(10)

Charged BHs in Einstein-Maxwell-Æther theory

The static spherically symmetric space-time for the Einstein-Maxwell-Æther theory can be written as

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+f^{-1}(r)dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}\right).

(15)

Depending on the choice of coupling constants there are two types of solutions corresponding to
c_{123}\neq 0
and
c_{123}=0

[
55
]
,

\bullet

First type

c_{123}\neq 0
:

For the first type of the Einstein-Maxwell-Æther BH, we have

f_{1}(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{c_{13}B}{\left(1-c_{13}\right)r^{4}},

(16)

where
M
is the BH mass,
q
is the BH charge and
B
is defined as

B=\frac{\left(2M-\sqrt{-32q^{2}+36M^{2}}\right)\left(6M+\sqrt{-32q^{2}+36M^{2}}\right)^{3}}{4096}.

(17)

Obviously, one can obtain the Schwarzschild BH solution when
c_{13}=q=0
. For
c_{13}=0
,
q\neq 0
and
c_{13}\neq 0
,
q=0
the above solution reduces to Reissner-Nordstrom (RN) BH and the first neutral Einstein-Æther BH solutions, respectively. The event horizon
r_{h}
can be obtained by solving
f_{1}(r)=0
, which for this type has a complicated form and therefore we will not bring it here.

\bullet

Second type

c_{123}=0
:

The metric function for the second type of the charged Einstein-Æther BH is given by

f_{2}(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r_{u}\left(2M+r_{u}\right)}{r^{2}},

(18)

where

r_{u}=M\left[\sqrt{\frac{\left(2-c_{14}\right)}{2\left(1-c_{13}\right)}-\frac{q^{2}}{M^{2}\left(1-c_{13}\right)}}-1\right].

(19)

Since the expression under square root in Eq. (
19
) should be always positive, we obtain the following constraints

q\leq M\sqrt{c_{13}-\frac{c_{14}}{2}},\hskip 14.22636ptc_{13}\geq\frac{c_{14}}{2}.

(20)

By solving
f_{2}(r)=0
, we obtain the event horizon of the second charged Einstein-Æther BH solution as

r_{h}=M\left[1+\sqrt{\frac{c_{14}-2+\frac{2q^{2}}{M^{2}}}{2\left(c_{13}-1\right)}}\right],

(21)

where for
q=0
reduces to equation (20) from Ref.
[
62
]
. For
c_{13}=c_{14}=q=0
and
c_{13}=c_{14}=0
, we recover the schwarzschild event horizon,
r_{h}=2M
, and the RN event horizon,
r_{h}=M+\sqrt{M^{2}-q^{2}}
, respectively. In what follows, we consider two specific cases of the second charged Einstein-Æther BHs; when
c_{14}=0
and when
c_{13}=0
.

{\rm ergs^{-1}cm^{-2}str^{-1}Hz^{-1}}

I(\nu^{\rm s}_{\rm ob})=\int_{\gamma}{g^{\rm s}}^{3}j(\nu^{\rm s}_{\rm em})dl_{p},

{\nu^{\rm s}}_{\rm ob}

{\nu^{\rm s}}_{\rm em}

Infalling spherical accretion

Now, we consider charged Einstein-Æther BHs surrounded by infalling spherical accretion flow. The observed intensity in this case is still expressed by Eq. (
41
) with this difference that the redshift factor is defined as

{g^{\rm i}}=\frac{k_{\alpha}{u^{\rm i}}_{\rm ob}^{\alpha}}{k_{\beta}{u^{\rm i}}_{\rm em}^{\beta}},

(42)

in which
k^{\mu}\equiv{\dot{x}}^{\mu}
,
{u^{\rm i}}_{\rm ob}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,0)
and
{u^{\rm i}}_{\rm em}^{\mu}
are the photon four-velocity, the observer four-velocity and four-velocity of the accretion flow, respectively. According to Eq. (
25
),
k_{t}=1/b
is a constant and
k_{r}
comes from condition
k_{\mu}k^{\mu}=0
, so we have

\frac{k_{r}}{k_{t}}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{f(r)^{2}}-\frac{b^{2}}{f(r)r^{2}}},

(43)

here the sign “
\pm
” refers to the case that the photons approach
(+)
or away
(-)
from the charged Einstein-Æther BHs, respectively. The four-velocity of the infalling accretion gas is

({u^{\rm i}}_{\rm em}^{t},{u^{i}}_{\rm em}^{r},{u^{\rm i}}_{\rm em}^{\theta},{u^{\rm i}}_{\rm em}^{\varphi})=(\frac{1}{f(r)},-\sqrt{1-f(r)},0,0).

(44)

Now, use of Eq. (
44
) into Eq. (
42
) leads to the following redshift factor

g^{\rm i}=\frac{1}{{u^{\rm i}}_{\rm em}^{t}+\left(\frac{k_{r}}{k_{t}}\right){u^{\rm i}}_{\rm em}^{r}}.

(45)

Also, the infinitesimal proper distance is

dl_{\rm p}=k_{\alpha}{u^{\rm i}}^{\alpha}_{\rm em}d\tau=\frac{k_{t}}{{g^{i}}\mid k_{r}\mid}dr,

(46)

where
\tau
is the affine parameter. For a monochromatic specific emissivity, the specific intensity
I(\nu_{\rm ob})
can be written as

I({\nu^{\rm i}}_{\rm ob})=\int\frac{{g^{\rm i}}^{2}}{r^{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{f(r)^{2}}-\frac{b^{2}}{f(r)r^{2}}}}dr.

(47)

5.3.2
The transfer function and the total observed intensity

According to the Liouville’s theorem, we have

\frac{I_{\rm em}(r)}{\nu_{\rm em}^{3}}=\frac{I_{\rm ob}(r)}{\nu_{\rm ob}^{3}}.

(48)

From above equation for the charged Einstein-Æther BHs the observed intensity can be written as

I_{\rm ob}(r)=f^{\frac{3}{2}}(r)I_{\rm em}(r),

(49)

where the observed frequency is
\nu_{\rm ob}\equiv f^{1/2}(r)\nu_{\rm em}
. By integrating the specific intensity with different frequencies we can obtain the total specific intensity for the first charged Einstein-Æther BH

I_{\rm total}(r)=\int I_{\rm ob}(r)d\nu_{\rm ob}=\left[1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{c_{13}B}{\left(1-c_{13}\right)r^{4}}\right]^{2}I_{\rm emit}(r),

(50)

and for the second charged Einstein-Æther BH as

I_{\rm total}(r)=\int I_{\rm ob}(r)d\nu_{\rm ob}=\left[1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r_{u}\left(2M+r_{u}\right)}{r^{2}}\right]^{2}I_{\rm emit}(r),

(51)

where

I_{\rm emit}(r)=\int I_{\rm em}(r)d\nu_{\rm em},

(52)

is the total emitted specific intensity of accretion disk. As we mentioned above, due to intersections of light rays with the disk plane, the total observed intensity is the sum of extra intensities from each intersection, that is

I_{\rm total}(r)=\sum_{n}f^{2}(r)I_{\rm emit}(r)|_{r=r_{\rm n}(b)},

(53)

where
r_{\rm n}(b)
is the transfer function that indicates the radial position of the
n_{\rm th}
intersection between the light rays with a given impact parameter and the thin accretion disk
[
11
]
. We have plotted the first three transfer functions for the RN BH (left panel), first neutral Einstein-Æther BH (middle panel) and the first charged Einstein-Æther BH (right panel) in Fig.

c_{13}=0
have been plotted in Fig.
11

n=1
described by the gray lines corresponds to “direct emission”. The second and third transfer function with
n=2
and
n=3
represented by the red and yellow lines correspond to “lensed ring” and “photon ring”, respectively.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we briefly reviewed the Einstein-Maxwell-Æther theory and introduced two types of the charged Einstein-Æther BHs including the first type of charged Einstein-Æther BH with
c_{123}\neq 0
and the second type of charged Einstein-Æther BHs with
c_{123}=0
; when
c_{14}=0
and when
c_{13}=0

[
55
]
. The effects of the coupling constants on the optical appearance of the Einstein-Æther BHs have been discussed in Ref.
[
62
]
. Here we investigated the effects of both the coupling constants and charge parameter on the BH event horizon
r_{\rm h}
, photon sphere
r_{\rm ph}
and impact parameter
b_{\rm ph}
of these BHs. We found that the increase of charge leads to the decrease of these BH parameters in both the first and the second charged Einstein-Æther BHs. The results were summarized in Tab.
1
,
2
and
3
. So, the presence of the charge parameter in two types of Einstein-Æther BHs caused that the BH shadow radius and photon rings is shrunk inward the charged Einstein-Æther BHs.

Then, we studied the charged Einstein-Æther BHs surrounded by static/infalling accretion flows and calculated the observed specific intensity. Figs.
4
and
6
show the peak of intensity decreases by increasing
c_{13}
for the first charged Einstein-Æther BH and the second charged Einstein-Æther BH when
c_{14}=0
, whereas for the second charged Einstein-Æther BH when
c_{13}=0
the peak of intensity increase by increasing
c_{14}
. For two types of BHs the charge parameter shrunk the BH shadow radius inward the BH and so increases the peak of intensity in comparison to the neutral Einstein-Æther BH. The results also show that for an infalling accretion, due to the Doppler effect of infalling matter, the dark central region is darker than the static case. Two dimensional image of shadow and photon rings have been plotted in Figs.
5
and
7
.

For the thin disk accretion model, following the method proposed by Gralla et al.
[
11
]
, we studied the photon trajectories with different total number of orbits. The behavior of the photon for the first charged Einstein-Æther BH and the second charged Einstein-Æther BH when
c_{14}=0
is shown in Fig.
8
and Fig.
9
, respectively. The total observed intensity as a function of the impact parameter has been plotted in Fig.
12
and Fig.
13
for the first and second charged Einstein-Æther BHs. We found that in the thin disk accretion flow the location and the emitted model of the accretion gas affect on the optical appearance of charged Einstein-Æther BHs in contrast to the spherical accretion flows. Moreover, the results indicate that in all three emitted models the direct emission has the dominant contribution to the optical appearance of the BH image.

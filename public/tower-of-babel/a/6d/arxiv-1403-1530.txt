Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a regular domain (not necessary bounded) of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
),
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
d\omega(x)=\frac{dx}{1+|x|^{N+2\alpha}}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{M}^{b}(\Omega)
the space of the Radon measures
\nu
in
\Omega

such that
\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{b}(\Omega)}:=|\nu|(\Omega)<+\infty
and by
\mathfrak{M}^{b}_{+}(\Omega)
the nonnegative cone.
The purpose of this paper is to study the existence of nonnegative weak solutions to semilinear fractional elliptic equations

\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+h(u)=\nu&{\rm in}\quad\Omega,\\
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{\alpha}+h(u)}u=0&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}

(1.1)

where
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a continuous nondecreasing function and
(-\Delta)^{\alpha}

denotes the fractional Laplacian of exponent
\alpha
defined by

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}u(x),

where for
\epsilon>0
,

(-\Delta)_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}u(x)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(z)-u(x)}{|z-x|^{N+2\alpha}}\chi_{\epsilon}(|x-z|)dz

and

\chi_{\epsilon}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}0,&{\rm if}\quad t\in[0,\epsilon],\\[5.69054pt]
1,&{\rm if}\quad t>\epsilon.\end{array}\right.

In the pioneering work
[
5
]
(also see
[
3
]
), Brezis studied the existence of weak solutions to second order elliptic problem

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+h(u)=\nu&{\rm in}\quad\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta+g(u)}u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{array}

(1.2)

where
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 3)
,
\nu
is a bounded Radon measure in
\Omega
, and the function
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is nondecreasing,
positive on
(0,+\infty)
and satisfies the subcritical assumption:

\int_{1}^{+\infty}(h(s)-h(-s))s^{-2\frac{N-1}{N-2}}ds<+\infty.

In particular case that
0\in\Omega
,
h(s)=s^{q}
and
\nu=k\delta_{0}
with
k>0
, Brezis et al showed that
(
1.2
) admits a unique weak solution
v_{k}
for
1<q<N/(N-2)
, while no solution exists if
q\geq N/(N-2)
. Later on,
Véron in
[
29
]
proved that if
1<q<N/(N-2)
, the limit of
v_{k}
is a strong singular solution of

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+u^{q}=0&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta+u^{q}}u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{array}

(1.3)

After that,
Brezis-Véron in
[
8
]
found that the problem (
1.3
)
admits only the zero solution if
q\geq N/(N-2)
. Much advances in the study
of semilinear second order elliptic equations involving measures see references
[
2
,
6
,
22
,
23
]
.

During the last years, there has also been a renewed and increasing interest in the study of linear and nonlinear integro-differential
operators, especially, the fractional Laplacian, motivated by various applications in physics and by important links on the theory
of Lévy processes, refer to
[
9
,
11
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
25
,
26
]
. In a recent work, Karisen-Petitta-Ulusoy in
[
20
]
used the duality approach to study the fractional elliptic
equation

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}v=\mu\quad{\rm{in}}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\mu
is a Radon measure with compact support.
More recently, Chen-Véron in
[
17
]

studied the semilinear fractional elliptic problem (
1.1
) when

\Omega
is an open bounded regular set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

and
\nu
is a Radon measure such that
\int_{\Omega}d^{\beta}d|\nu|<+\infty
with
\beta\in[0,\alpha]
and
d(x)=dist(x,\partial\Omega)
.
The existence and uniqueness of weak
solution are obtained when the function
h
is nondecreasing and satisfies

\int_{1}^{+\infty}(h(s)-h(-s))s^{-1-k_{\alpha,\beta}}ds<+\infty,

(1.4)

where

k_{\alpha,\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{N}{N-2\alpha},&{\rm if}\quad\beta\in[0,\frac{N-2\alpha}{N}\alpha],\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2\alpha+\beta},&{\rm if}\quad\beta\in(\frac{N-2\alpha}{N}\alpha,\alpha].\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

Motivated by these results and in view of the non-local character of the fractional Laplacian we are interested in
the existence of weak solutions to problem (
1.1
) when
\Omega
is a general regular domain, including
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Before stating our main results in this paper, we introduce the definition of weak solution to
(
1.1
).

Definition 1.1

A function
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega)
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) if
h(u)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}[u(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\xi+h(u)\xi]dx=\int_{\Omega}\xi d\nu,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{X}_{\Omega},

(1.6)

where
\mathbb{X}_{\Omega}\subset C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the space of functions

\xi
satisfying:

(i) the support of
\xi
is a compact set in
\bar{\Omega}
;

(ii)
(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\xi(x)
exists for any
x\in\Omega
and there exists
C>0
such that

|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\xi(x)|\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{N+2\alpha}},\quad\forall x\in\Omega;

(iii) there exist
\varphi\in L^{1}(\Omega,\rho^{\alpha}dx)

and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
|(-\Delta)_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\xi|\leq\varphi
a.e. in
\Omega
, for all

\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
, here
\rho(x)=\min\{1,dist(x,\partial\Omega)\}
if
\Omega\not=\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
\rho\equiv 1
if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

We notice that
\mathbb{X}_{\Omega}
coincides with the test function space
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
if
\Omega
is bounded, see
[
17
, Definition 1.1]
.
Moreover, the test function space
\mathbb{X}_{\Omega}
is used as
C^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
if
\Omega
is bounded and
\alpha=1
, see
[
30
]
.
We denote by
G_{\Omega}
the Green kernel of
(-\Delta)^{\alpha}
in

\Omega\times\Omega
and by
\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\cdot]
the Green operator
defined as

\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\nu](x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)d\nu(y),\quad\forall\nu\in\mathfrak{M}^{b}(\Omega).

Now we are ready to state our first theorem on the existence of weak solutions for problem (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1

Assume that
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\Omega
is a regular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 2)
and
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

is a continuous nondecreasing function satisfying

\int_{1}^{+\infty}h(s)s^{-1-\frac{N}{N-2\alpha}}ds<+\infty.

(1.7)

Then for any
\nu\in\mathfrak{M}^{b}_{+}(\Omega)
, problem
(
1.1
) admits a weak solution
u_{\nu}
such that

0\leq u_{\nu}\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\nu]\quad\rm{a.e.\ in}\ \Omega.

(1.8)

If
\nu
is a nonnegative bounded Radon measure, (
1.4
) with
\beta=0
and
(
1.7
) have the same critical value
\frac{N}{N-2\alpha}
.

In the case that
\Omega
is bounded, the authors of
[
17
]
took a sequence of
C^{1}
functions
\{\nu_{n}\}
converging to
\nu
in the weak star sense, then they considered the solutions
u_{n}
of (
1.1
) replacing
\nu
by
\nu_{n}
. By the compact imbedding theorem, they showed that the limit of
\{u_{n}\}
exists, up to subsequence. While for the case that
\Omega
is unbounded, the difficulty is that Sobolev imbedding may not be compact. To overcome the difficulty, we truncate the measure
\nu
by
\nu\chi_{B_{R}(0)}
and use the increasing monotonicity of corresponding solutions

\{u_{R}\}
of solutions to (
1.1
) in related bounded domains. Taking the limit as
R\to\infty
, we achieve the desired weak solution.

The second purpose in this paper is to study properties of weak solution to problem (
1.1
) when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
h(u)=u^{p}
and
\nu=k\delta_{0}
, that is,

\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+u^{p}=k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}

(1.9)

where

,

and
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac mass at the origin.

Theorem 1.2

Assume that
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

.
Then for any

, problem
(
1.9
) admits a unique weak solution
u_{k}
such that

\lim_{x\to 0}u_{k}(x)|x|^{N-2\alpha}=c_{1}k,

(1.10)

where
c_{1}>0
.
Moreover,

(i)

\{u_{k}\}_{k\in(0,\infty)}
are classical solutions of

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+u^{p}=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\};

(1.11)

(ii)

the mapping:
k\mapsto u_{k}
is increasing.

We consider the asymptotic behavior of
u_{1}
at
\infty
when
p\in(1,\frac{N}{N-2\alpha})
.

Theorem 1.3

Assume that
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
u_{1}
is the solution of (
1.9
) with
k=1
.
Then there exist

and
R>2
such that for
|x|\geq R
,

(i)

if

,

\frac{1}{c_{2}}|x|^{-\frac{N+2\alpha}{p}}\leq u_{1}(x)\leq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{N+2\alpha}{p}};

(1.12)

(ii)

if
p=1+\frac{2\alpha}{N}
,

\frac{1}{c_{2}}|x|^{-N}\log^{\frac{N}{2\alpha}}(|x|)\leq u_{1}(x)\leq c_{2}|x|^{-N}\log^{\frac{N}{2\alpha}}(|x|);

(1.13)

(iii)

if
p\in(1+\frac{2\alpha}{N},\frac{N}{N-2\alpha})
,

\frac{1}{c_{2}}|x|^{-\frac{2\alpha}{p-1}}\leq u_{1}(x)\leq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{2\alpha}{p-1}}.

(1.14)

According to Theorem
1.3
, we know that the decaying power of

shifts at the point

;
while for
\alpha=1
and

From now on, we denote that

Since the mapping:

2
Preliminary

The purpose of this section is to introduce some preliminaries and prove
Marcinkiewicz type estimate.

2.1
Marcinkiewicz type estimate

In this subsection,
we recall the definition of Marcinkiewicz space and prove
Marcinkiewicz type estimate.

Definition 2.1

Let
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a domain and
\mu
be a positive
Borel measure in
\Theta
. For
\kappa>1
,

\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa/(\kappa-1)
and
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Theta,d\mu)
, we
set

\|u\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Theta,d\mu)}=\inf\left\{c\in[0,\infty]:\int_{E}|u|d\mu\leq c\left(\int_{E}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}},\ \forall E\subset\Theta,\,E\ \rm{Borel}\right\}

(2.1)

and

M^{\kappa}(\Theta,d\mu)=\{u\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Theta,d\mu):\|u\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Theta,d\mu)}<\infty\}.

(2.2)

M^{\kappa}(\Theta,d\mu)
is called the Marcinkiewicz space of
exponent
\kappa
, or weak
L^{\kappa}
-space and

\|.\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Theta,d\mu)}
is a quasi-norm.

Proposition 2.1

[
4
]

Assume that
1\leq q<\kappa<\infty
and
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Theta,d\mu)
.
Then there exists
c_{4}>0
dependent of
q,\kappa
such that

\int_{E}|u|^{q}d\mu\leq c_{4}\|u\|_{M^{\kappa}(\Theta,d\mu)}\left(\int_{E}d\mu\right)^{1-q/\kappa},

for any Borel set
E
of
\Theta
.

Now we are ready to state Marcinkiewicz type estimate as follows.

Proposition 2.2

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{M}^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then there exists
c_{5}>0
such that

\|\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[|\nu|]\|_{M^{p^{*}_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega)}\leq c_{5}\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

(2.3)

where
d\omega(x)=\frac{dx}{1+|x|^{N+2\alpha}}
and
p^{*}_{\alpha}=\frac{N}{N-2\alpha}
.

Proof
. For
\lambda>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we set

A_{\lambda}(y)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{y\}:G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)>\lambda\right\},\quad m_{\lambda}(y)=\int_{A_{\lambda}(y)}d\omega.

We observe that there exists a positive constant
c_{N,\alpha}
such that

G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)=\frac{c_{N,\alpha}}{|x-y|^{N-2\alpha}},\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\ x\neq y,

which implies that

A_{\lambda}(y)\subset\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x-y|\leq c_{N,\alpha}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{N-2\alpha}}\right\}.

(2.4)

As a consequence,

m_{\lambda}(y)=\int_{A_{\lambda}(y)}\frac{dx}{1+|x|^{N+2\alpha}}\leq|A_{\lambda}(y)|\leq c_{6}\lambda^{-p_{\alpha}^{*}},

(2.5)

where
c_{6}>0
independent of
y
and
\lambda
and
p_{\alpha}^{*}=\frac{N}{N-2\alpha}
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a Borel set and
\lambda>0
, then

\displaystyle\int_{E}G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)d\omega(x)\leq\int_{A_{\lambda}(y)}G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)d\omega(x)+\lambda\int_{E}d\omega.

By (
2.5
), we have that

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\lambda}(y)}G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)d\omega(x)=\lambda m_{\lambda}(y)+\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}m_{s}(y)ds\leq c_{7}\lambda^{1-p_{\alpha}^{*}},

for some
c_{7}>0
, then it results that

\displaystyle\int_{E}G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)d\omega(x)\leq c_{7}\lambda^{1-p_{\alpha}^{*}}+\lambda\int_{E}d\omega.

Choosing
\lambda=(\int_{E}d\omega)^{-\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}^{*}}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{E}G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)d\omega(x)\leq(c_{7}+1)\left(\int_{E}d\omega\right)^{\frac{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}{p_{\alpha}^{*}}},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{E}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[|\nu|](x)d\omega(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{E}G_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y)d\omega(x)d|\nu(y)|\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{---------}\displaystyle\leq(c_{7}+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}d|\nu(y)|\left(\int_{E}d\omega\right)^{\frac{p^{*}_{\alpha}-1}{p^{*}_{\alpha}}}\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{---------}\displaystyle\leq(c_{7}+1)\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left(\int_{E}d\omega\right)^{\frac{p^{*}_{\alpha}-1}{p^{*}_{\alpha}}}.\end{array}

(2.6)

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[|\nu|]\|_{M^{p^{*}_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega)}\leq c_{5}\|\nu\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

which ends the proof.
\Box

Now we use Marcinkiewicz type estimate to prove the following lemma, which is the key-stone in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.1

Assume that
\nu\in\mathfrak{M}_{+}^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

is a continuous nondecreasing function satisfying
(

).
Then

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu],\ h(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu])\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega).

Proof.
On the one hand, using Fubini’s lemma, we have that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu]\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega)}=c_{N,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{N+2\alpha}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2\alpha}}d\nu(y)dx\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{--------\ }=c_{N,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{N+2\alpha}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2\alpha}}dxd\nu(y)\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{--------\ }\leq c_{N,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\int_{B_{1}(y)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2\alpha}}dx+\int_{B_{1}^{c}(y)}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{N+2\alpha}}dx]d\nu(y)\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{--------\ }<+\infty,\end{array}

that is,
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu]\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega).

On the other hand, let
S_{\lambda}=\{x\in B_{R}(0):\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu](x)>\lambda\}
and

g(\lambda)=\int_{S_{\lambda}}d\omega
, where
\lambda\geq 1
. We observe that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu])d\omega=\int_{S^{c}_{\lambda}}h(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu])d\omega+\int_{S_{\lambda}}h(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu])d\omega\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{-\int_{E}|g(u_{n_{k}})|\rho^{\beta}dx}\leq h(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}d\omega+\int_{S_{\lambda}}h(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu])d\omega\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{-\int_{E}|g(u_{n_{k}})|\rho^{\beta}dx}=h(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}d\omega+h(\lambda)g(\lambda)+\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}g(s)dh(s).\end{array}

(2.7)

Since

\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}g(s)dh(s)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\lambda}^{T}g(s)dh(s)

and
\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu]\in M^{p^{*}_{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega)
, it derives from Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.1
with
q=1
,
\kappa=p^{*}_{\alpha}
,
E=S_{\lambda}

and
d\mu=d\omega
that

g(s)\leq c_{8}s^{-p^{*}_{\alpha}}
and for
T>\lambda
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}h(\lambda)g(\lambda)+\int_{\lambda}^{T}g(s)dh(s)\leq c_{8}\lambda^{-p^{*}_{\alpha}}h(\lambda)+c_{8}\int_{\lambda}^{T}s^{-p^{*}_{\alpha}}dh(s)\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{-----------\ \ }\displaystyle\leq c_{8}T^{-p^{*}_{\alpha}}h(T)+c_{8}p^{*}_{\alpha}\int_{\lambda}^{T}s^{-1-p^{*}_{\alpha}}h(s)ds,\end{array}

where
c_{8}>0
. By the nondecreasing monotonicity of
h
, we have that

\displaystyle T^{-p^{*}_{\alpha}}h(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{1+p_{\alpha}^{*}}h(T)(2T)^{-1-p^{*}_{\alpha}}\int_{T}^{2T}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1+p_{\alpha}^{*}}\int_{T}^{2T}h(t)t^{-1-p^{*}_{\alpha}}dt,

then it infers by (
1.7
) that
that

\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-p^{*}_{\alpha}}h(T)=0.

Therefore, by (
1.7
) and we take
\lambda=1
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu])d\omega\leq h(1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}d\omega+c_{8}p^{*}_{\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-1-p^{*}_{\alpha}}h(s)ds<+\infty,

i.e.
h(\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nu])\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},d\omega).

We complete the proof.
\Box

3
Existence of weak solutions

In this section, we show the existence of solutions of problem (

), that is, we will prove Theorem

. We first give an auxiliary lemma as follows.

Lemma 3.1

Assume that
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\eta\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with support in
\bar{\mathcal{O}}
.
Then there exists

such that

where

Proof.
For

and

we assert (
3.1
) holds, which ends the proof.

Now we are in the position to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let
\{\mathcal{O}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of

By Proposition

) with

admits a unique weak solution, denoting it by

. We divide the proof in following steps.

Step 1. We claim that

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

In fact, let

be the solution of (

) with

and

.
By Proposition
2.3
,

\tilde{v}_{n+1}\leq v_{n+1}\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3.2)

Choosing a sequence nonnegative functions
\{f_{n,m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{n})

such that

as

into

implies that

Together with the facts that

(3.3)

It follows by (

,

v_{n}\leq v_{n+1}\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3.4)

Step 2. Uniform bounds of

.
We deduce by (

.
Therefore, by (

Proposition 4.1

.

\displaystyle(u_{1}-t_{0}c_{39}w_{\tau_{p}})(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{x\in B_{R}^{c}(0)}(u_{1}-t_{0}c_{39}w_{\tau_{p}})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}(u_{1}-t_{0}c_{39}w_{\tau_{p}})(x)<0,

since

which is a contradiction.

Upper bound.

,

Since

Proposition 5.1

Proof.
For

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha p}{p-1}}[(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u_{k}(\lambda x)+u_{k}^{p}(\lambda x)]

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Moreover, for

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\tilde{u}_{\lambda}+\tilde{u}_{\lambda}^{p},f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha p}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u_{k}(\lambda x)+u_{k}^{p}(\lambda x)]f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha p}{p-1}-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u_{k}(z)+u_{k}^{p}(z)]f(\frac{z}{\lambda})dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha p}{p-1}-N}kf(0),

where

Thus,

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\tilde{u}_{\lambda}+\tilde{u}_{\lambda}^{p}=\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha p}{p-1}-N}k\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

(5.3)

We observe that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\tilde{u}_{\lambda}(x)=0

and
u_{k\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha p}{p-1}-N}}
is a unique weak solution of
(
1.9

replaced by
\lambda^{\frac{2\alpha p}{p-1}-N}k
,
then for

(5.4)

and letting

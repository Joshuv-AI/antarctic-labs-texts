On the Nonlinear Ψ \Psi -Hilfer Fractional Differential Equations


Abstract
We consider the nonlinear Cauchy problem for Ψ \Psi - Hilfer fractional differential equations and investigate the existence, interval of existence and uniqueness of solution in the weighted space of functions. The continuous dependence of solutions on initial conditions is proved via Weissinger fixed point theorem. Picard’s successive approximation method has been developed to solve nonlinear Cauchy problem for differential equations with Ψ \Psi - Hilfer fractional derivative and an estimation have been obtained for the error bound. Further, by Picard’s successive approximation, we derive the representation formula for the solution of linear Cauchy problem for Ψ \Psi -Hilfer fractional differential equation with constant coefficient and variable coefficient in terms of Mittag-Leffler function and Generalized (Kilbas–Saigo) Mittag-Leffler function.

Let

h

\Delta

C_{1-\zeta;\,\Psi}(\Delta,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})=\left\{h:\left(a,b\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}~\big|~\left(\Psi\left(t\right)-\Psi\left(a\right)\right)^{1-\zeta}h\left(t\right)\in C(\Delta,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})\right\},\text{ }0<\zeta\leq 1

\left\|h\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta;\,\Psi}\left(\Delta,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)}=\underset{t\in\Delta}{\max}\left|\left(\Psi\left(t\right)-\Psi\left(a\right)\right)^{1-\zeta}h\left(t\right)\right|.

(2.1)

Definition 2.1

Let
\eta>0~(\eta\in\mbox{\Bbb R})
,
h\in L_{1}(\Delta,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
and
\Psi\in C^{1}(\Delta,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
be an increasing function wth
\Psi^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
, for all
\,t\in\Delta
. Then, the
\Psi
-Riemann–Liouville fractional integral of a function
h

\Psi

\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}h\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\eta\right)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta}_{\Psi}(t,\sigma)h(\sigma)\,d\sigma.

(2.2)

where,
\mathrm{Q}^{\eta}_{\Psi}(t,s)=\Psi^{\prime}(s)(\Psi(t)-\Psi(s))^{\eta-1},~t,s\in\Delta.

Definition 2.2

Let
n-1<\eta<n\in\mbox{\Bbb N}
and
h,\Psi\in C^{n}(\Delta,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})

\Psi
is increasing with
\Psi^{\prime}(t)\neq 0,

\,t\in\Delta

(\mbox{left-sided})

\Psi
-Hilfer fractional derivative
{}^{H}\mathcal{D}^{\eta,\,\nu\,;\,\Psi}_{a+}(\cdot)
of a function
h

\eta
and type
0\leq\nu\leq 1
, is defined by

^{H}\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\eta,\,\nu\,;\,\Psi}h\left(t\right)=\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\nu\left(n-\eta\right)\,;\,\Psi}\left(\frac{1}{\Psi^{\prime}\left(t\right)}\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{n}\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\left(1-\nu\right)\left(n-\eta\right)\,;\,\Psi}h\left(t\right).

(2.3)

Lemma 2.3

Theorem 2.4

Let
\tilde{u},

\tilde{v}\in L_{1}(\Delta,\,\mathbb{R})
and
\tilde{g}\in C(\Delta,\,\mathbb{R}).
Let
\Psi\in C^{1}(\Delta,\,\mathbb{R})

Existence and uniqueness results are proved via following fixed point theorems.

Theorem 2.5
(
[
11
]
, Schauder)

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space, let
\mathcal{U}
be a nonempty convex bounded closed subset of
\mathcal{X}
and let
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
be a completely continuous operator. Then
\mathcal{A}
has at least one fixed point.

Theorem 2.6
(
[
11
]
, Weissinger)

Assume
(\mathcal{U},d)
to be a non empty complete metric space and let
\eta_{j}\geq 0
for every
j\in\mbox{\Bbb N}_{0}
such that
\overset{\infty}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\eta_{j}
converges. Furthermore, let the mapping
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
satisfy the inequality

d(\mathcal{A}^{j}u,\mathcal{A}^{j}v)\leq\eta_{j}\,\,d(u,v)

for every
j\in\mbox{\Bbb N}
and every
u,v\in\mathcal{U}.
Then,
\mathcal{A}
has a unique fixed point
u^{*}.
Moreover, for any
u_{0}\in\mathcal{U},
the sequence
(\mathcal{A}^{j}u_{0})_{j=1}^{\infty}
converges to this fixed point
u^{*}.

Definition 2.3
(
[
4
]
)

Let
\eta>0,\,\nu>0~(~\eta,\nu\in\mbox{\Bbb R})
. Then the two parameter Mittag-Leffler function is defined as

\mathcal{E}_{\eta,\,\nu}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(k\eta+\nu)}.

Definition 2.4
(
[
4
]
)

Let
\eta,\,m\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
and

such that
\eta>0,~m>0
and
\eta(jm+l)+1\neq-1,-2,-3,\cdots(j=0,1,2,\cdots)
. Then generalized (Kilbas–Saigo) Mittag–Leffler type function of three parameters is defined by

\mathcal{E}_{\eta,\,m,\,l}\,(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\,z^{k}

where

c_{0}=1,~c_{k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\Gamma(\eta[jm+l]+1)}{\Gamma(\eta[jm+l+1]+1)}\,\,(k=1,2,\cdots)

and an empty product is assumed to be equal to one.

3
Existence, Uniqueness and Continuous Dependence

The forthcoming theorem establishes the existence of solution along with the interval of existence of the initial value problem (IVP) (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) using its equivalent fractional Volterra integral equation(VIE) in the weighted space
C_{1-\zeta;\,\Psi}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
.

Theorem 3.1
(Existence and interval of existence)

Let
\zeta=\eta+\nu(1-\eta),0<\eta<1
and
0\leq\nu\leq 1
. Define

R_{0}=\left\{(t,y):a\leq t\leq a+\xi,\left|y-\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}\right|\leq k\right\},~\xi>0,~k>0.

where,
\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a):=\dfrac{(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}
.
Let
f:R_{0}\rightarrow\mbox{\Bbb R}
is continuous function such that
f(\cdot\,,y(\cdot))\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
for every
y\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
.
Let
\Psi\in C^{1}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
be an increasing bijective function with
\Psi^{\prime}(t)\neq 0,
for all
\,t\in\Lambda
.
Then the IVP
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
possesses at least one solution
y
in the space
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}[a,a+\chi]
of weighted functions, where

\chi=\min\left\{\xi,~\Psi^{-1}\left[\Psi(a)+\left(\frac{k\,\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}{\Gamma(\zeta)\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\xi\right]}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\right]-a\right\}.

Proof:

The equivalent fractional VIE to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in the space
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
is derived in
[
31
]
and it is given by

y(t)=\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t,\sigma)f(\sigma,y(\sigma))\,d\sigma,~t\in\Lambda.

(3.1)

Consider the set defined by,

\mathcal{U}=\left\{y\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]:\left\|y-\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(\cdot,a)\,y_{a}\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}\leq k\right\}.

Define
\tilde{y}(t)=\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a},~t\in[a,a+\chi]
. Then
(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\,\tilde{y}(t)=\dfrac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}\in C[a,a+\chi]
and hence we have,
\tilde{y}(t)\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
. Further,

\left\|\tilde{y}-\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(\cdot,a)\,y_{a}\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}=0\leq k.

Thus,
\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{U}
and hence
\mathcal{U}
is non-empty set. Clearly,
\mathcal{U}
is a convex, bounded and closed subset
of space
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right].

We define an operator
\mathcal{A}
on the set
\mathcal{U}
by

\mathcal{A}y(t)=\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t,\sigma)f(\sigma,y(\sigma))\,\,d\sigma.

By definition of an operator
\mathcal{A}
, the equation (
3.1
) can be written as

y=\mathcal{A}y.

We analyze the properties of
\mathcal{A}
so that it admit at least one fixed point.

First we show that,
\mathcal{A}\,\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
. In the view of Lemma
2.1
,
\Psi
-Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator
\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}\left(\cdot\right)
maps
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
to
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
, and hence
\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}f(\cdot,y(\cdot))\in C_{{1-\zeta};\,\Psi}[a,a+\chi]
for any

y\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
.
Further,

(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\,\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}=\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}\in C[a,a+\chi],

and hence
\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
.

From the above arguments it follows that
\mathcal{A}y\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
. Next, for any
y\in\mathcal{U}
and any
x
,
a<x\leq\chi
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}y-\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(\cdot,a)\,y_{a}\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}

\displaystyle=\underset{t\in\left[a,a+x\right]}{\max}\left|(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t,\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\times\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}f(\sigma,y(\sigma))\,d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\underset{\varsigma\in\left[a,\,\sigma\right]}{\max}\left|(\Psi(\varsigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}f(\varsigma,y(\varsigma))\right|\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\,\,\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\eta+\zeta-1}

\displaystyle=\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}.

Taking

\left\|\mathcal{A}y-\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(\cdot,a)\,y_{a}\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}

(3.2)

Since,

\chi=\min\left\{\xi\,\,,\Psi^{-1}\left[\Psi(a)+\left(\frac{k\,\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}{\Gamma(\zeta)\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\xi\right]}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\right]-a\right\}

\chi\leq\Psi^{-1}\left[\Psi(a)+\left(\frac{k\,\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}{\Gamma(\zeta)\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\xi\right]}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\right]-a.

Further, since
\Psi\in C^{1}\left(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
is a bijective function,
\Psi^{-1}:\mbox{\Bbb R}\to\Lambda

(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}\leq\frac{k\,\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}{\Gamma(\zeta)\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\xi\right]}}.

(3.3)

Using Eq.(
3.3

3.2

\left\|\mathcal{A}y-\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(\cdot,a)\,y_{a}\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}}\leq k.

We have proved that
\mathcal{A}y\in\mathcal{U}\,\,\text{for any}\,\,y\in\mathcal{U}

\mathcal{A}\,\,\text{maps}\,\,\mathcal{U}\,\,\text{to itself}

is a continuous operator. Let any
\varepsilon>0

is continuous and

is compact set,
f
is uniformly continuous on

. Thus, there exists

such that

\left|f(t,y)-f(t,z)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon\,\,\Gamma(\eta+1)}{(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta-\zeta+1}},~~\text{whenever}~|y-z|<\tilde{\delta}.

Since,
\Psi
is continuous on compact set

, we can choose
\delta>0
such that
(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}|y-z|<\delta
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left|f(t,y)-f(t,z)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon\,\,\Gamma(\eta+1)}{(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta-\zeta+1}},~\text{whenever}~(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}|y-z|<\delta.

(3.4)

Let any
y,z\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\left\|y-z\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}<\delta
. Then in the view of (

\left|f(t,y(t))-f(t,z(t))\right|<\frac{\varepsilon\,\,\Gamma(\eta+1)}{(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta-\zeta+1}},~t\in[a,a+\chi].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}y-\mathcal{A}z\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{t\in\left[a,a+\chi\right]}{\max}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t,\sigma)\left|f(\sigma,y(\sigma))-f(\sigma,z(\sigma))\right|\,d\sigma

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon\,\,\Gamma(\eta+1)}{(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta-\zeta+1}}\,\,\,\underset{t\in\left[a,a+\chi\right]}{\max}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t,\sigma)\,\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon\,\,\Gamma(\eta+1)}{(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta-\zeta+1}}\,\,\,\underset{t\in\left[a,a+\chi\right]}{\max}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta+1)}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta-\zeta+1}}\,\,(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a))^{\eta-\zeta+1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon.

Thus, the operator
\mathcal{A}
is continuous on
\mathcal{U}
. Now, for any
z\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})=\{\mathcal{A}y:y\in\mathcal{U}\}
and any
x
such that
a<x\leq\chi
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}=\left\|\mathcal{A}y\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}+\underset{t\in\left[a,a+x\right]}{\max}\left|\frac{(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t,\sigma)\times\right.

\displaystyle\qquad(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}f(\sigma,y(\sigma))\,d\sigma\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}+\frac{(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)\times

\displaystyle\qquad(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\underset{\varsigma\in[a,\sigma]}{\max}\left|(\Psi(\varsigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}f(\varsigma,y(\varsigma))\right|\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}+{\left\|f\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}}\,\,\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}+\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}\,\,\left(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a)\right)^{1-\zeta}\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\eta+\zeta-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}+\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}\,\,\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}.

This shows that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})
is uniformly bounded subset of
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
and hence it is point wise bounded also. Next, we prove that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})
is equicontinuous. For any
t_{1},t_{2}
such that
a<t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq a+\chi
and any
y\in\mathcal{U},
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}y(t_{2})-\mathcal{A}y(t_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}\left\{\left(\Psi(t_{2})-\Psi(a)\right)^{\zeta-1}-(\Psi(t_{1})-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\,\,+\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t_{2}}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t_{2},\sigma)\left|f(\sigma,y(\sigma))\right|\,d\sigma-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t_{1}}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t_{1},\sigma)\left|f(\sigma,y(\sigma))\right|\,d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}\left\{\left(\Psi(t_{2})-\Psi(a)\right)^{\zeta-1}-(\Psi(t_{1})-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t_{2}}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t_{2},\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\underset{\varsigma\in[a,\sigma]}{\max}\left|(\Psi(\varsigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}f(\varsigma,y(\varsigma))\right|\,d\sigma\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t_{1}}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t_{1},\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\underset{\varsigma\in[a,\sigma]}{\max}\left|(\Psi(\varsigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}f(\varsigma,y(\varsigma))\right|\,d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}\left\{\left(\Psi(t_{2})-\Psi(a)\right)^{\zeta-1}-(\Psi(t_{1})-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}[a,a+\chi]}\left|\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(t_{2})-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}-\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(t_{1})-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{a}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}\left|\left(\Psi(t_{2})-\Psi(a)\right)^{\zeta-1}-(\Psi(t_{1})-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}[a,a+\chi]}\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}\left|(\Psi(t_{2})-\Psi(a))^{\eta+\zeta-1}-(\Psi(t_{1})-\Psi(a))^{\eta+\zeta-1}\right|.

Observe that, the right hand part in the preceding inequality is free from
y
. Thus, using the continuity of
\Psi
,
|t_{2}-t_{1}|\rightarrow 0
implies that
|\mathcal{A}y(t_{2})-\mathcal{A}y(t_{1})|\rightarrow 0
. This proves that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})
is equicontinuous. In the view of Arzela-Ascoli Theorem
[
11
]
, it follows that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{U})
is relatively compact. Therefore, by Schauder’s fixed point Theorem
2.5
, operator
\mathcal{A}
has at least one fixed point
y\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
which is the solution of IVP (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

In the next theorem, using Lipschitz condition on

and the Weissinger theorem, we establish another existence result for the IVP (

)–(

), which gives in addition the uniqueness of solution also.

Theorem 3.2
(Uniqueness)

Let
\zeta=\eta+\nu(1-\eta),0<\eta<1
,
0\leq\nu\leq 1
and

R_{0}=\left\{(t,y):a\leq t\leq a+\xi,\left|y-\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}\right|\leq k\right\},~\xi>0,~k>0.

where,
\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)=\frac{\left(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a)\right)^{\zeta-1}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}
.
Let
f:R_{0}\rightarrow\mbox{\Bbb R}
be a function such that
f(\cdot\,,y(\cdot))\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
for every
y\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
and satisfies the Lipschitz condition

\left|f(t,u)-f(t,v)\right|\leq L|u-v|,\,\,u,v\in\mbox{\Bbb R},~L>0.

Let
\Psi\in C^{1}(\Lambda,\,\mbox{\Bbb R})
be an increasing bijective function with
\Psi^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in\Lambda
. Then, the IVP (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has a unique solution

y
in the weighted space
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}[a,a+\chi]
, where

\chi=\min\left\{\xi,~\Psi^{-1}\left[\Psi(a)+\left(\frac{k\,\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}{\Gamma(\zeta)\left\|f\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\xi\right]}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\right]-a\right\}.

Proof:
Consider the operator
\mathcal{A}
used in Theorem
3.1
. It is as of now demonstrated that
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
is continuous on closed, convex and bounded subset
\mathcal{U}
of Banach space
C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]
. We now prove that, for every
n\in\mbox{\Bbb N}
and every
x
such that
a<x\leq\chi
, we have

\left\|\mathcal{A}^{n}y-\mathcal{A}^{n}z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}\leq\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(n\eta+\zeta)}\left[L\left(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a)\right)^{\eta}\right]^{n}\left\|y-z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}.

(3.5)

We provide the proof of above inequality by using mathematical induction. By using Lipschitz condition on
f
, for any
x\in(a,\chi]
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}y-\mathcal{A}z\right\|_{{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}

\displaystyle\leq(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)\left|f(\sigma,y(\sigma))-f(\sigma,z(\sigma))\right|\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq L(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)\times

\displaystyle\qquad(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\left|y(\sigma)-z(\sigma)\right|\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq L(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)\times

\displaystyle\qquad(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\underset{\varsigma\in\left[a,\sigma\right]}{\max}\left|(\Psi(\varsigma)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}[y(\varsigma)-z(\varsigma)]\right|\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq L\left\|y-z\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\,\,\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(\eta+\zeta)}\left[L(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}\right]\left\|y-z\right\|_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}.

Hence, the inequality (
3.5
) is true for
n=1
. Let us assume that, it is true for
n=k-1
. Now, we prove that inequality (
3.5
) is also true for
n=k
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k}y-\mathcal{A}^{k}z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}

\displaystyle=\underset{t\in\left[a,a+x\right]}{\max}\left|(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(t,\sigma)\left[f\left(\sigma,\mathcal{A}^{k-1}y(\sigma)\right)-f\left(\sigma,\mathcal{A}^{k-1}z(\sigma)\right)\right]\,d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq L\,\,(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)\left|\mathcal{A}^{k-1}y(\sigma)-\mathcal{A}^{k-1}z(\sigma)\right|\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq L\,\,(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)\times

\displaystyle\qquad(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k-1}y-\mathcal{A}^{k-1}z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,\sigma\right]}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq L\,\,(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{a+x}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta\,;\,\psi}(a+x,\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma((k-1)\eta+\zeta)}\left[L\left(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a)\right)^{\eta}\right]^{k-1}\left\|y-z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,\sigma\right]}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq L^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma((k-1)\eta+\zeta)}\,\,(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\left\|y-z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\eta(k-1)+\zeta-1}

\displaystyle=L^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma((k-1)\eta+\zeta)}\,\,(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{1-\zeta}\left\|y-z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{\Gamma((k-1)\eta+\zeta)}{\Gamma(k\eta+\zeta)}(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{k\eta+\zeta-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(k\eta+\zeta)}\left[L(\Psi(a+x)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}\right]^{k}\left\|y-z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}\left[a,a+x\right]}}.

Hence, the inequality (
3.5
) is true for
n=k
. By mathematical induction the proof of the inequality (
3.5
) is concluded. Taking
x\to\chi
, we obtain

\left\|\mathcal{A}^{n}y-\mathcal{A}^{n}z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta;\,\psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}}\leq\frac{\Gamma(\zeta)}{\Gamma(n\eta+\zeta)}\left[L\left(\psi(a+\chi)-\psi(a)\right)^{\eta}\right]^{n}\left\|y-z\right\|_{{}_{C_{1-\zeta;\,\psi}\left[a,a+\chi\right]}}.

Note that, the operator
\mathcal{A}
satisfy all the conditions of the Weissinger’s theorem
2.6
, with

\eta_{n}=\frac{\left[L\left(\Psi(a+\chi)-\Psi(a)\right)^{\eta}\right]^{n}}{\Gamma(n\eta+\zeta)}.

Further, by definition of two parameter Mittag–Leffler function, we have

which is convergent series. Thus, by using Weissinger’s fixed point theorem

has a unique fixed point

)-(

).

Theorem 4.1

f(\cdot\,,y(\cdot))\in C_{1-\zeta\,;\,\Psi}[a,a+\chi]

\displaystyle=y_{a}\sum_{k=0}^{j+1}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{\Gamma(k\eta+\zeta)}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{k\eta+\zeta-1}+\int_{a}^{t}\Psi^{\prime}(\sigma)\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{\Gamma((k+1)\eta)}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(\sigma))^{(k+1)\eta-1}f(\sigma)\,d\sigma.

This proves equation (
5.2
) is true for
m=j+1
. By mathematical induction the equation (
5.2
) is true for all
m\in\mbox{\Bbb N}
.
Taking limit as
m\rightarrow\infty
, on both sides of (
5.2
), we get the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) as

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}y_{m}(t)

\displaystyle=y_{a}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{k\eta+\zeta-1}}{\Gamma(k\eta+\zeta)}+\int_{a}^{t}\Psi^{\prime}(\sigma)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(\sigma))^{(k+1)\eta-1}}{\Gamma((k+1)\eta)}f(\sigma)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=y_{a}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}\mathcal{E}_{\eta,\,\zeta}\left(\lambda(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta}_{\Psi}(t,\sigma)\mathcal{E}_{\eta,\,\eta}\left(\lambda(\Psi(t)-\Psi(\sigma))^{\eta}\right)f(\sigma)\,d\sigma.

\Box

Theorem 5.2

(5.3)

We consider the sequences
\{y_{m}\}
of successive approximation defined by

\displaystyle y_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}

\displaystyle y_{m}(t)

\displaystyle=y_{0}(t)+\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta}_{\Psi}(t,\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\mu-1}y_{m-1}(\sigma)\,d\sigma,~(m=1,2,\cdots),

and derive the solution of (

\displaystyle y_{m}(t)=\mathcal{H}^{\Psi}_{\zeta}(t,a)\,y_{a}\left\{1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,\left(\lambda(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\eta+\mu-1}\right)^{k}\right\},

where

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle y_{1}(t)

) for
m=1

\displaystyle y_{j+1}(t)

\displaystyle=y_{0}(t)+\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta}_{\Psi}(t,\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\mu-1}y_{j}(\sigma)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=y_{0}(t)+\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(\eta)}\int_{a}^{t}\mathrm{Q}^{\eta}_{\Psi}(t,\sigma)(\Psi(\sigma)-\Psi(a))^{\mu-1}\times

\displaystyle=y_{0}(t)+y_{a}\frac{\lambda}{{\Gamma(\zeta)}}\,\,\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\mu+\zeta-2}

\displaystyle\qquad+y_{a}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\frac{c_{k}\,\,\lambda^{k+1}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}\,\,\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\eta\,;\,\Psi}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{(k+1)\mu+k\eta+\zeta-k-2}

\displaystyle=y_{a}\frac{(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\zeta-1}}{\Gamma(\zeta)}+y_{a}\frac{\lambda\,\Gamma(\mu+\zeta-1)}{\Gamma(\zeta)\Gamma(\eta+\mu+\zeta-1)}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(a))^{\eta+\mu+\zeta-2}

6
Concluding remarks

In the present paper, we are able to provide a brief study of the theory of FDEs by means of the
\Psi
-Hilfer fractional derivative. We examined the existence along with the interval of existence, uniqueness, dependence of solutions and Picard’s successive approximations in cases: nonlinear and linear.

The existence results pertaining to Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), was obtained through the theorems of Schauder and Arzela-Ascoli. On the other hand, we can prove the uniqueness and continuous dependence of the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) by making use of mathematical induction and Weissinger’s fixed point theorem. In addition, we investigate Picard’s successive approximation in the nonlinear case for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and in the linear cases: (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) with constant coefficients and (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) with variable coefficients, making use of the Gronwall’s inequality and mathematical induction. It should be noted that the results obtained in the space of the weighted functions
C_{1-\zeta;\,\Psi}\left(\Delta,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
are contributions to the fractional calculus field, in particular, the fractional analysis.

The question that arises is: will it be possible to perform the same study in space
L_{p}([0,1],\mathbb{R})
with the norm
||(\cdot)||_{p,\,\eta}
, involving
\Psi
-Hilfer fractional derivative and Banach’s fixed point theorem
[
39
]
? If the answer is yes. What are the restrictions for such results, if any? These issues and others, are studies that are in progress and will be published in the future.

Explicit construction of recurrent neural networks effectively approximating discrete dynamical systems
Chikara Nakayama, Tsuyoshi Yoneda

Abstract
. We consider arbitrary bounded discrete time series originating from dynamical system with recursivity. More precisely, we provide an explicit construction of recurrent neural networks which effectively approximate the corresponding discrete dynamical systems.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 37N30; Secondary 11B50

1.
Introduction

In this paper we provide an explicit construction of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) which effectively approximate discrete dynamical systems.
In general, Lyapinov indexes for reconstructed and original systems have a gap (see Dechert-Gençay
[
2
]
and Berry-Das
[
1
]
), and our explicit construction of RNNs at least minimize the maximal Lyapunov exponent.
As far as the authors are aware, none of the numerous works to date attempted to
explicitly construct RNNs in terms of an elementary algebraic approach, and in this paper we initiate it (for investigating the universality of periodic points based on the similar algebraic approach, see
[
4
]
).

First we define the dynamical system in a delay coordinate.
For the dimension of the dynamical system
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
let
\Phi:[-1,1]^{L}\to[-1,1]^{L}
(
(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{L})^{T}\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{L})^{T}
) be such that

(1)

w_{1}=x_{2},\quad w_{2}=x_{3},\cdots,w_{L-1}=x_{L}

(i.e. discrete dynamical system in a delay coordinate, see
[
5
]
, and see also
[
3
]
).
Let us assume that a time series
y:\mathbb{Z}\to[-1,1]
satisfies

Y_{t+1}=\Phi(Y_{t})\quad\text{for}\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

where

Y_{t}:=(y(t),y(t-1),\cdots,y(t-L+1))^{T}.

From this
\Phi
, we define
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
to be the maximum Lyapunov exponent, and assume it is finite, as follows:

e^{\lambda}:=\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W,W^{\prime}\in[-1,1]^{L},}}{{W\not=W^{\prime}}}}\frac{|\Phi(W)-\Phi(W^{\prime})|}{|W-W^{\prime}|}<\infty.

Also we assume recursivity to
y
:
For any
\epsilon>0
,
there is
\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}

(
t_{j}\to-\infty
for
j\to\infty
) such that

|Y_{0}-Y_{t_{j}}|<\epsilon.

Now we define the conventional RNNs.
For initial hidden state vector
r(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, initial data
\hat{y}(0)\in[-1,1]
,
recurrent weight matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
input weight matrix (vector)
W^{in}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
, output weight matrix (vector)
W^{out}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times N}

and activate function
h:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
,
the RNNs can be expressed as follows:

(2)

\begin{split}&\begin{cases}r(t+1)&:=h(Wr(t)+W^{in}\hat{y}(t)),\\
\hat{y}(t+1)&:=W^{out}r(t+1)\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq 0.\end{cases}\end{split}

The main theorem is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
y:\mathbb{Z}\to[-1,1]
be a time series generated by the discrete dynamical system in a delay coordinate
\Phi:[-1,1]^{L}\to[-1,1]^{L}
(
Y_{t-1}\mapsto Y_{t}
)
with recursivity and a finite
\lambda\geq 0
.
Then for any large
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and for any constant
C
which is slightly larger than
1
, there exist
N\leq K^{L}
,
h
, explicit
W
,
W^{in}
and
W^{out}
which are
composed of
y(t)
(
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
),
an initial hidden state vector
r(0)
, and initial data
\hat{y}(0)

such that the following holds:

(3)

\displaystyle|\hat{y}(t)-y(t)|\leq(2t+1)e^{\lambda t}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq 0.

This is only on the order of log worse (since
t=e^{\log t}
). Thus, this RNNs construction seems effective for re-expressing the original system.

2.
Proof of main theorem

First let us discretize the range
[-1,1]
as follows:
For
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we choose
\{a_{k}^{K}\}_{k=1}^{K}\subset[-1,1]
such that

•

-1<a_{1}^{K}<a_{2}^{K}<\cdots<a_{K}^{K}<1
,

•

a_{i}^{K}\not=0
for any
1\leq i\leq K
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{a_{k^{\prime}_{\ell}}^{K}}{a_{k_{\ell}}^{K}}\not=L
for each
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{L})\not=(k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{L})
,

•

\sup\left\{\frac{a_{k+1}^{K}-a_{k-1}^{K}}{2}\ (1<k<K),\frac{a_{1}^{K}+a_{2}^{K}}{2}+1,1-\frac{a_{K-1}^{K}+a_{K}^{K}}{2}\right\}\leq C/K

•

For any
t
with
-L+1\leq t\leq 0
and for any
k
with
1\leq k<K
,

(4)

y(t)\not=\frac{a_{k}^{K}+a_{k+1}^{K}}{2}.

We need the last condition for appropriately separating
the time series
y
into training and inference phases.
Note that the above second condition is almost always satisfied in the following sense.

Proposition 2
.

There is a closed set
Z
of
[-1,1]^{K}
whose Lebesgue measure is zero such that the above second condition is satisfied as soon as
(a^{K}_{1},\ldots,a^{K}_{K})
is not in
Z
.

Proof.

We drop the superscipt
K
for simplicity.
Regard
a_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq K
) as indeterminates.
Clearly the measure of the set where
a_{i}=0
is zero.
Under the assumption that any
a_{i}
is not zero, the condition
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{a_{k^{\prime}_{\ell}}}{a_{k_{\ell}}}\not=L
is equivalent to

\sum_{\ell}a_{k_{1}}\cdots a_{k^{\prime}_{\ell}}\cdots a_{k_{L}}-L\prod_{\ell}a_{k_{\ell}}\not=0
, the left-hand-side of which is a nonzero polynomial whenever
k_{\ell}\not=k^{\prime}_{\ell}
for some
\ell
(then the term
\prod_{\ell}a_{k_{\ell}}
does not vanish).
Hence by the following simple fact, the non-equality holds for almost all values of
a_{i}
:

\bullet
Let
f
be a nonzero polynomial in
M
variables. Then
the Lebesgue measure of the zero point set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}:f(x)=0\}
is zero.

∎

We now discretize
y
(in the training phase) as follows:

(5)

\bar{y}_{K}(t):=\argmin_{a\in\{a^{K}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}}|y(t)-(a-0)|\quad\text{for}\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0},

where
a-0:=a-\varepsilon
for any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
From this discretization, we define “dictionary”, in other word, “key-value pairs”.
Let
\sigma_{n}
(
n=1,2,\cdots,N
) be a permutation operator, namely, a map

\sigma_{n}^{K}:\{1,2,\cdots,L\}\to\{a_{1}^{K},a_{2}^{K},\cdots,a_{K-1}^{K},a_{K}^{K}\}

(
\ell\mapsto\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)
) with
\sigma_{n}^{K}\not=\sigma_{n^{\prime}}^{K}
(
n\not=n^{\prime}
),
and we impose the following two conditions for determining
N
:

(6)

\begin{cases}\text{For any $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}$, there is $n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}$ such that}\\
\qquad\text{$\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t-\ell)$ for $\ell=1,2,\cdots,L$},\\
\text{For any $n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}$ there is $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}$ such that}\\
\qquad\text{$\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t-\ell)$ for $\ell=1,2,\cdots,L$.}\end{cases}

Note that
N\leq K^{L}
due to the sequence with repetition.
We now define the dictionary
\{(\sigma_{n},a_{k(n)})\}_{n=1}^{N}
, in other word, key-value pairs (i.e.
\sigma_{n}
is the “key” and
a_{k(n)}
is the corresponding “value”).
By (
6
),
for any key
\sigma_{n}^{K}
, there exists
a
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
and then we choose such a
t^{\prime}
and the corresponding
k(n)\in\{1,2,\cdots,K\}
such that

(7)

\text{key:}\quad\sigma_{n}^{K}(\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime}-\ell)\quad(\ell=1,2,\cdots,L)\quad\text{and}\quad\text{value:}\quad a_{k(n)}^{K}:=\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime}).

Now, by using these key-value pairs, we first construct a time series
y^{*}(t)

(t\geq 0)
, and then show an estimation analogous to (
3
), that is, the inequality obtained by replacing
\hat{y}
in (
3
) by
y^{*}
.
After that, we give
h,W,W^{in},W^{out},r(0)
and
\hat{y}(0)

such that
\hat{y}=y^{*}
, which completes the proof.

We define
y^{*}(t)
by induction.
Let
y^{*}(t)=\bar{y}_{K}(t)
for
t=0,-1,\ldots,-L
.
Then the permutation operator
\ell\mapsto y^{*}(1-\ell)
is in the dictionary by recursivity and (
4
).
Let
t_{0}>0
.
Assume that we have defined
y^{*}(t)
for
-L\leq t<t_{0}
and assume that
we can find the permutation operator
\ell\mapsto y^{*}(t_{0}-\ell)
is in the dictionary, that is, it is
\sigma_{n}
for some
n
.
Define
y^{*}(t_{0}):=a_{k(n)}
.
Then we see that the permutation operator
\ell\mapsto y^{*}(t_{0}-\ell+1)
also in the dictionary as follows.
Recall that for some
t^{\prime}\leq 0
,
\sigma_{n}(\ell)=\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime}-\ell)
(
\ell=1,2,\cdots,L
)
and
a_{k(n)}=\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime})
.
Hence the permutation operator concerned coincides with
\ell\mapsto\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime}+1-\ell)
.
If
t^{\prime}<0
, there is
\tilde{n}
such that

\bar{y}_{K}(t^{\prime}-\ell+1)=\sigma_{\tilde{n}}(\ell)
(
\ell=1,2,\cdots,L
) by (
6
).
If
t^{\prime}=0
, we just apply recursivity and (
4
).
Thus the induction goes and we have defined
y^{*}
.

We estimate the difference of
y^{*}(t)
and
y(t)
, which eventually implies
(
3
).
The case
t=0
is by the third condition in the beginning of this section.
Let

\begin{split}\overline{Y}_{t}&:=(\bar{y}_{K}(t),\bar{y}_{K}(t-1),\cdots,\bar{y}_{K}(t-L+1))^{T}\quad(t\leq 0),\\
Y^{*}_{t}&:=(y^{*}(t),y^{*}(t-1),\cdots,y^{*}(t-L+1))^{T}\quad(t\geq 0).\end{split}

Then we see that, for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there exists
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
such that

(8)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{t^{\prime}}=Y^{*}_{t},\quad|Y_{t^{\prime}}-Y^{*}_{t}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K},\\
Y_{t^{\prime}}=\Phi(Y_{t^{\prime}-1}),\\
\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{t^{\prime}-1}=Y^{*}_{t-1},\quad|Y_{t^{\prime}-1}-Y^{*}_{t-1}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}.\end{cases}

Recall
e^{\lambda}\geq 1
, and then we have

\begin{split}|y^{*}(t)-y(t)|\leq&|Y^{*}_{t}-Y_{t}|\\
\leq&|Y_{t^{\prime}}-Y_{t}|+\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&e^{\lambda}|Y_{t^{\prime}-1}-Y_{t-1}|+\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&e^{\lambda}|Y^{*}_{t-1}-Y_{t-1}|+e^{\lambda}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}+\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&e^{\lambda}|Y^{*}_{t-1}-Y_{t-1}|+2e^{\lambda}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\leq\cdots\\
\leq&e^{\lambda t}|Y^{*}_{0}-Y_{0}|+2te^{\lambda t}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}\\
\leq&(2t+1)e^{\lambda t}\frac{\sqrt{L}C}{K}.\end{split}

Next we explicitly construct effective
recurrent neural networks.
Let
\sigma^{*}_{n}
(
n=1,2,\cdots,N
) be an adjoint type of permutation operator, more precisely, let

(9)

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}_{n}(\ell-1):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}(\ell)}

for
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,L\}
and
n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.
The definition of the following
N\times N
matrix
X

is the crucial to prove the main theorem:

X:=\left[h\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sigma^{*}_{i}(\ell-1)\sigma_{j}(\ell)\right)\right]_{i,j}

for some
h:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
.
Also let
G
be such that

G:=\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sigma^{*}_{i}(\ell-1)\sigma_{j}(\ell):i,j\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}\right\}.

Note that
L\in G
.

Lemma 3
.

X
is a regular matrix for almost all
h\colon\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
in
the following sense.
Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{M}
be the all elements of
G
.
Then there is a closed set
Z
of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
whose Lebesgue measure is
zero such that

X
is regular as soon as
(h(\gamma_{1}),\ldots,h(\gamma_{M}))
is not in
Z
.

Proof.

Take a bijection
x\colon\gamma_{m}\mapsto x_{m}
from
G

to the set of indeterminates
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\}
.
Consider the determinant
D
of the matrix
[x(\sum\sigma_{i}^{*}(\ell-1)\sigma_{j}(\ell))]_{i,j\in N}
with polynomial
coefficients.
Then
D
is a nonzero polynomial because it is a monic polynomial of
degree
N
with respect to the indeterminate
x(L)
(the leading term

x(L)^{N}
comes from the diagonal).
Now we apply the simple fact in the proof of Proposition
2
to
D
.
Let
Z
be the zero point set of
D
. Assume that

(h(\gamma_{1}),\ldots,h(\gamma_{M}))
is not in
Z
.
Then
\det X
defined by
h
is not zero so that
X
is regular.
∎

With the aid of the inverse of
X
, we can provide
the
N\times N
recurrent weight matrix
W
as follows:

(10)

W:=YX^{-1}\quad\text{with}\quad Y:=\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\sigma_{i}^{*}(\ell)\sigma_{j}(\ell)\right]_{i,j}.

Remark 1
.

The rank of
W
is less than
L-1
, because Y is the product of the
N\times(L-1)
-matrix:
[\sigma_{i}^{*}(\ell-1)]_{i,\ell}
and
(L-1)\times N
-matrix:

[\sigma_{j}(\ell)]_{\ell,j}
.

We now define the input weight vector
W^{in}
and the the output weight matrix (vector)
W^{out}
as follows:

\begin{split}W^{in}&:=\sigma^{*}(0),\\
W^{out}&:=(a_{k(1)},a_{k(2)},\cdots,a_{k(N)})X^{-1},\end{split}

where
\sigma^{*}(\ell):=(\sigma^{*}_{1}(\ell),\sigma^{*}_{2}(\ell),\cdots,\sigma_{N}^{*}(\ell))^{T}

for
0\leq\ell\leq L-1
.
Let us set the initial hidden state vector
r(0)
and the initial data
\hat{y}(0)
as follows:

\begin{split}r(0)&=\left[h\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sigma^{*}_{i}(\ell-1)\bar{y}_{K}(-\ell)\right)\right]_{i},\\
\hat{y}(0)&:=\bar{y}_{K}(0),\end{split}

then we have the desired RNNs.
Let
\hat{y}
be the time series generated by these initial data.
Now we certify
\hat{y}=y^{*}
, which completes the proof of Theorem.
For
t\geq 0
, the permutation operator
\ell\mapsto y^{*}(t-\ell)
is
\sigma_{n}
for some
n
.
Let
n_{t}
be this
n
.
Then we define the column vector

e_{n_{t}}:=(\underbrace{\overbrace{0,0,\cdots,0,1}^{n_{t}},0\cdots,0}_{N})^{T}.

We prove
\hat{y}(t)=y^{*}(t)
for
t\geq 0
together with
r(t)=Xe_{n_{t}}
by induction.
The case
t=0
is by the definition.
Assume that
t\geq 0
and they hold for
0,1,\ldots,t
.
Then we have

\text{key:}\quad y^{*}(t-\ell)=\sigma_{n_{t}}(\ell)\quad\text{for}\quad\ell=1,2,\cdots,L.

Thus we have

Wr(t)=WXe_{n_{t}}=Ye_{n_{t}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\sigma^{*}(\ell)y^{*}(t-\ell),

r(t+1)=h\left(Ye_{n_{t}}+\sigma^{*}(0)y^{*}(t)\right)=Xe_{n_{t+1}}{\text{ and}}\\

\hat{y}(t+1)=W^{out}r(t+1)=(a_{k(1)},\ldots,a_{k(N)})e_{n_{t+1}}=a_{k(n_{t+1})}=y^{*}(t+1).

Hence they hold also for
t+1
.

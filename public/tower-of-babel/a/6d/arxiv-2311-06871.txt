An inexact q q -order regularized proximal Newton method for nonconvex composite optimization Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Ruyu Liu, Shaohua Pan, Yitian Qian

Abstract
This paper concerns the composite problem of minimizing the sum of a twice continuously differentiable function f f and a nonsmooth convex function. For this class of nonconvex and nonsmooth problems, by leveraging a practical inexactness criterion and a novel selection strategy for iterates, we propose an inexact q q -order regularized proximal Newton method for q ∈ [ 2 , 3 ] q\in[2,3] , which becomes an inexact cubic regularization (CR) method for q = 3 q=3 . We prove that the whole iterate sequence converges to a stationary point for the KL objective function; and when the objective function has the KL property of exponent θ ∈ ( 0 , q − 1 q ) \theta\in(0,\frac{q-1}{q}) , the convergence has a local Q Q -superlinear rate of order q − 1 θ ​ q \frac{q-1}{\theta q} . In particular, under a local Hölderian error bound of order γ ∈ ( 1 q − 1 , 1 ] \gamma\in(\frac{1}{q-1},1] on a second-order stationary point set, we show that the iterate and objective value sequences converge to a second-order stationary point and a second-order stationary value, respectively, with a local Q Q -superlinear rate of order γ ⁡ ( q − 1 ) \gamma(q\!-\!1) , specified as the Q Q -quadratic rate for q = 3 q=3 and γ = 1 \gamma=1 . This is the first practical inexact CR method with Q Q -quadratic convergence rate for nonconvex composite optimization. We validate the efficiency of the CR method with ZeroFPR as the inner solver by applying it to composite optimization problems with highly nonlinear f f .

1.2
Main contribution

We propose an inexact
q
-order regularized proximal Newton method with
q\in\![2,3]
for problem (
1
) by utilizing a practical inexactness criterion and a novel selection strategy for the iterates. When
q=3

(1)
For the proposed algorithm, we establish the full convergence of the iterate sequence under the KL property of
F
by combining the sufficient decrease of the objective values with the KL inequality on a potential function skillfully. Based on the full convergence analysis and the proof of
[
39

, under the KL property of
F

\theta\in\!(0,\frac{q-1}{q})
, we obtain the superlinear convergence rate of order
\frac{q-1}{\theta q}
for the iterate and objective value sequences, which are consistent with those of
[
39
, Theorem 4.1 (ii)]
for the algorithm of only theoretical values. KL functions are ubiquitous in nonconvex and nonsmooth optimization by
[
3
, Section 4]
, and KL property of exponent
\theta\!\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{q-1}{q})
for
q\in\!(2,3]
is weaker than that of exponent
1/2
. Thus, our convergence results have a wide range of potential applications. Also, the superlinear convergence result of the cost value sequence improves the one got with
p=2
in
[
35
]
for (
1

[
35
]
.

(2)
Under a local Hölderian error bound of order

on a second-order stationary point set that reduces to the ordinary one used in
[
23
,
47
]
if

This implies that the iterate and objective value sequences of our inexact CR method converge quadratically to a second-order stationary point and a second-order stationary value, respectively, under a local Lipschitzian error bound. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first practical inexact CR method for (
1

[

under the KL property of

(3)
We apply the proposed algorithm armed with ZeroFPR

is said to have the KL property at a point
\overline{x}\in{\rm dom}\,\partial h
if there exist
\delta>0,\varpi\in(0,\infty]
and
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{\!\varpi}
such that

\varphi^{\prime}(h(x)\!-\!h(\overline{x})){\rm dist}(0,\partial h(x))\geq 1

for all
x\in\mathbb{B}(\overline{x},\delta)\cap[h(\overline{x})<h<h(\overline{x})+\varpi]
; and it is said to have the KL property of exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
at
\overline{x}
if there exist
c>0,\,\delta>0
and
\varpi\in(0,\infty]
such that

c(1\!-\!\theta)\,{\rm dist}(0,\partial h(x))\geq(h(x)\!-\!h(\overline{x}))^{\theta}

for all
x\in\mathbb{B}(\overline{x},\delta)\cap[h(\overline{x})<h<h(\overline{x})+\varpi]
.
If
h
has the KL property (of exponent
\theta
) at each point of
{\rm dom}\,\partial h
, it is called a KL function (of exponent
\theta
).

By
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
, to show that a proper lsc function has the KL property (of exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
), it suffices to check if the property holds at its critical points. The discussions in
[
30
, Section 2.2]
show that the KL property of
h
with exponent
\theta\in(0,1)
has a close relation with the
\gamma
-subregularity of its subdifferential mapping
\partial h
for
\gamma\in(0,1]
. As discussed in
[
3
, Section 4]
, the KL property is ubiquitous and the functions definable in an o-minimal structure over the real field admit this property.

3
Inexact
q
-order regularized proximal Newton method

Let
x^{k}
be the current iterate. When
\nabla^{2}\!f
is Hölder calm with exponent
q\!-\!2
at
x^{k}
, i.e., there exist
\overline{L}_{k}>0
and
\delta_{k}>0
such that
\|\nabla^{2}\!f(z)\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\|\leq\overline{L}_{k}\|z-x^{k}\|^{q-2}
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}(x^{k},\delta_{k})
, it is easy to verify that
|f(z)\!-\!\vartheta_{k}(z)|\leq({\overline{L}_{k}}/{q})\|z\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}(x^{k},\delta_{k})
. This means that the function
z\mapsto\vartheta_{k}(z)+(L_{k}/q)\|z-x^{k}\|^{q}+g(z)
with
L_{k}\geq\overline{L}_{k}
is a local majorization of
F
at
x^{k}
. Based on this, our method first constructs a local majorization of
F
at the current iterate
x^{k}
by adding the
q
-order regularization term
({L_{k}}/{q})\|\cdot-x^{k}\|^{q}
to the local quadratic approximation, and then computes inexactly

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\Theta_{k}(x):=f_{k}(x)+g(x)\ \ {\rm with}\ f_{k}(x)\!:=\vartheta_{k}(x)+({L_{k}}/q)\|x\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}

(7)

to seek an inexact stationary point
y^{k}
satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle r_{k}(y^{k})\leq\varrho L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}\ {\rm for}\ \varrho\in(0,1)\ {\rm with}\ \Theta_{k}(y^{k})\leq\Theta_{k}(x^{k}),

(8a)

\displaystyle F(y^{k})\leq F(x^{k})-\sigma(L_{k}/q)\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}\ \ {\rm for}\ \sigma\in(0,1),

(8b)

where
r_{k}\!:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is the KKT residual function of subproblem (
7
) defined by

r_{k}(x)\!:=\|R_{k}(x)\|\ \ {\rm with}\ \ R_{k}(x)\!:=x-{\rm prox}_{g}\big(x-\nabla\!f_{k}(x)\big)\ {\rm for}\ x\in\mathbb{X}.

(9)

The
q
-order regularization term
({L_{k}}/{q})\|\cdot-x^{k}\|^{q}
accounts for
f_{k}
being a better approximation than
\vartheta_{k}
to
f
at
x^{k}
. Although problem (
7
) is still a nonconvex composite optimization one, it is much simpler than the original problem (
1
) because its nonconvexity is only caused by the quadratic term. The above condition (
8a
) aims at seeking an approximate stationary point
y^{k}
of subproblem (
7
) with its objective value no worse than the current one
\Theta_{k}(x^{k})
, while condition (
8b
) requires the approximate stationary point
y^{k}
to have an objective value
F(y^{k})
lower than the current one of problem (
1
). The computation of subproblem (
7
) with an appropriate
L_{k}
is not as difficult as it seems to be. Indeed, at the
k
th iteration, by the expression of
f_{k}
, we have
\nabla^{2}\!f_{k}(y)\succeq\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})+L_{k}\|y-x^{k}\|^{q-2}\mathcal{I}
for
y\in\mathbb{X}
. Hence, as long as
x^{k}
is not a stationary point of (
7
), for an appropriately large
L_{k}
, it is possible for
f_{k}
and then
\Theta_{k}
to be strongly convex in a neighborhood of the stationary point set of (
7
). In addition, for an appropriately large
L_{k}
, the function
\Theta_{k}
is coercive, so subproblem (
7
) has an optimal solution, which by Lemma
4
later ensures the existence of the desired
y^{k}
. Motivated by this, our method at each iteration employs a line-search procedure to capture an appropriate
L_{k}
and a desired
y^{k}
simultaneously.

With
y^{k}
, the next iterate
x^{k+1}
is set to be either
y^{k}
or
y^{k}\!-\!R_{k}(y^{k})
depending on which one has a lower objective value. Note that
y^{k}\!-\!R_{k}(y^{k})={\rm prox}_{g}(y^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k}(y^{k}))
is a potential stationary point of (
1
) because it can be viewed as an approximate iterate produced by applying the iterative formula
x^{k+1}={\rm prox}_{g}(x^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f(x^{k}))
. Hence, such a selection strategy takes into account the objective values of potential stationary points. As a consequence, it is possible to gain a sufficient decrease of the objective values as well as guarantee the convergence of the iterate sequence. A similar selection strategy was adopted in the variable metric inexact proximal gradient method
[
8
,
10
]
and the inexact proximal Newton method
[
30
]
for nonconvex composite optimization, but it is done between
y^{k}
and a point on the line segment joining
x^{k}
and
y^{k}
, and does not involve the information on the objective values of potential stationary points.

The iteration process of our inexact
q
-order regularized proximal Newton method is described as follows, where for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
r_{k,j}
denotes the KKT residual of subproblem (
10
) defined as in (
9
) except that
f_{k}
is replaced with
f_{k,j}
, and
f_{k,j}
is the function defined by
f_{k,j}(x):=\vartheta_{k}(x)+(L_{k,j}/q)\|x-x^{k}\|^{q}
for
x\in\mathbb{X}
.

Algorithm 1

(Inexact
q
-order regularized proximal Newton method)

Input:

q\in[2,3],0<\!L_{m}<L_{\!M},\varrho\in(0,1),\sigma\in(0,1),\tau>1
and
x^{0}\in{\rm dom}\,g
.

For

k=0,1,2,\ldots

do

1.

Choose
L_{k,0}\in[L_{m},L_{\!M}]
and
L_{k,0}>\max(0,-\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})))
if
q=2
.

2.

For

j=0,1,2,\ldots

do

(2a)

Compute an inexact stationary point
y^{k,j}
of the composite problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\ \Theta_{k,j}(x)\!:=f_{k,j}(x)+g(x)

(10)

such that
y^{k,j}
satisfies the following inexactness criterion

r_{k,j}(y^{k,j})\leq\varrho L_{k,j}\|y^{k,j}\!-x^{k}\|^{q-1}\ \ {\rm with}\ \ \Theta_{k,j}(y^{k,j})\leq\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k}).

(11)

(2b)

If
F(y^{k,j})\leq F(x^{k})-(\sigma L_{k,j}/q)\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{q}
, then set
j_{k}=j
and go to step 3; else let
L_{k,j+1}=\tau L_{k,j}
and go to step (2a).

end (For)

3.

Write
L_{k}\!:=L_{k,j_{k}},\,y^{k}\!:=y^{k,j_{k}}
and
v^{k}\!:=R_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k})
. Let

x^{k+1}\!:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}y^{k}&{\rm if}\ F(y^{k})<F(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k}),\\
y^{k}\!-\!v^{k}&{\rm otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(12)

and go to step 1.

end (For)

Remark 3
.

(a)
Recall that
r_{k,j}(y^{k,j})=0
if and only if
y^{k,j}
is a stationary point of (
10
). The inexactness criterion (
11
) along with
\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})=F(x^{k})
means that step (2a) of Algorithm
1
is searching for an inexact stationary point of (
10
) with its objective value no worse than the current one of (
1
). Obviously, any efficient algorithms developed recently for nonconvex composite optimization such as ZeroFPR
[
43
]
can be used as an inner solver. Together with the checkability of the inexactness criterion (
11
), Algorithm
1
is a practical
q
-order regularized proximal Newton method. This is the most significant difference from the one proposed in
[
39
]
, which requires computing an exact stationary point of subproblem at each iteration. In addition, Algorithm
1
involves a novel selection strategy for the next iterate
x^{k+1}
which, as will be shown in Section
4
, ensures that the iterate sequence generated by Algorithm
1
is convergent. When
q=2
, Algorithm
1
reduces to a regularized proximal Newton method for solving problem (
1
), but unlike the one developed in
[
30
]
its subproblems are not necessarily convex, and the adopted inexactness criterion and selection strategy on the next iterate are also different from those used in
[
30
]
.

(b)
Algorithm
11
is well-defined by Lemma
4
later. When
x^{k+1}\!=x^{k}
occurs,
x^{k}
is a stationary point of (
1
) because now
x^{k}=y^{k}
holds and the inexactness criterion (
11
) implies that
r(x^{k})=r_{k}(y^{k})=0
. Indeed, from (
12
), either
x^{k+1}=y^{k}
or
x^{k+1}=y^{k}-v^{k}
. For the former,
x^{k}=y^{k}
automatically holds; while for the latter,
x^{k}=y^{k}-v^{k}
, which by step (2b) implies that

\sigma(L_{k}/q)\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}\leq F(x^{k})\!-\!F(y^{k})\leq F(x^{k})\!-\!F(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})=0
, so
x^{k}=y^{k}
holds by
L_{k}\geq L_{m}>0
. In view of this, we suggest that
r(x^{k+1})\leq\epsilon
or
\|x^{k+1}\!-\!x^{k}\|\leq\epsilon
for an accuracy tolerance
\epsilon\in(0,1)
as the stopping criterion of Algorithm
1
.

(c)
For the smooth unconstrained problem
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\!f(x)
, we claim that as
k
is large enough, our inexactness condition (
8a
) with
g\equiv 0
and
q=3
is weaker than

f_{k}(y^{k})\leq f_{k}(y^{k}_{C})\ {\rm and}\ \|\nabla\!f_{k}(y^{k})\|\leq\kappa_{\theta}\min(1,\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|)\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|\ {\rm for}\ \kappa_{\theta}\!\in(0,1),

(13)

the one adopted by Cartis et al.
[
12
,
13
]
for the adaptive cubic regularization methods with
m_{k}\equiv f_{k}
, where
y^{k}_{C}=x^{k}\!-\!\alpha_{k}^{C}\nabla\!f(x^{k})
with
\alpha_{k}^{C}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\alpha\geq 0}m_{k}(x^{k}\!-\!\alpha\nabla\!f(x^{k}))
. Indeed, from the first inequality of (
13
) and the definition of
\alpha_{k}^{C}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

f_{k}(y^{k})\leq f_{k}(y_{C}^{k})\leq f_{k}(x^{k}),

which along with
g\equiv 0
implies that
\Theta_{k}(y^{k})\leq\Theta_{k}(x^{k})
. While from the second inequality of (
13
), we have
\|\nabla f_{k}(y^{k})\|\leq\kappa_{\theta}\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|
, which in turn implies that

(1\!-\!\kappa_{\theta})\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|\leq\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\!-\!\nabla f_{k}(y^{k})\|\leq\|\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\|\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|+L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}.

Along with the boundedness of
\{\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{L_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|=0
(see Proposition
6
later),
it follows that
\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|\leq\widetilde{c}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|
with some
\widetilde{c}>0
for all
k
large enough, which by the second inequality of (
13
) means that
\|\nabla\!f_{k}(y^{k})\|\leq\kappa_{\theta}\widetilde{c}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}
, i.e., the first inequality of (
8a
) holds. Thus, the claimed relation holds. By following the same arguments, we conclude that as
k
is sufficiently large, our inexactness condition (
8a
) with
g\equiv 0
and
q=5/2
is also weaker than

f_{k}(y^{k})\leq f_{k}(y^{k}_{C})\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|\nabla f_{k}(y^{k})\|\leq\kappa_{\theta}\min(1,\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|^{1/2})\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|,

which is another one used by Cartis et al.
[
12
,
13
]
for the proposed methods.

Note that
v^{k}=R_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k})
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By the expression of
R_{k,j_{k}}
, we have

y^{k}\!-\!v^{k}={\rm prox}_{g}(y^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k}))\ \ {\rm and}\ \ y^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k})\in\partial g(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(14)

These two relations will be frequently used in the convergence analysis in Section
4
.

Before analyzing the properties of the iterate sequence yielded by Algorithm
1
, we need to show that it is well-defined. This is implied by the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Fix any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
r(x^{k})\neq 0
. The following assertions hold.

(i)

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, subproblem (
10
) has a stationary point
\overline{y}^{k,j}
satisfying
\Theta_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})<\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})
.

(ii)

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, condition (
11
) is satisfied by any
y-R_{k,j}(y)
with
y
close enough to a stationary point
\overline{y}^{k,j}
of (
10
) with
\Theta_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})<\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})
.

(iii)

The inner for-end loop stops within a finite number of steps.

Proof.

(i)
As
g
is convex and its domain is nonempty, we have
{\rm ri}({\rm dom}\,g)\neq\emptyset
. Pick any
\widehat{x}\in{\rm ri}({\rm dom}\,g)
. Since
\partial g(\widehat{x})\neq\emptyset
, we choose any
\widehat{\xi}\in\partial g(\widehat{x})
. Then,

g(x)\geq g(\widehat{x})+\langle\widehat{\xi},x-\widehat{x}\rangle\quad\ \forall x\in{\rm dom}\,g,

(15)

which means that
f_{k,j}(z)+g(z)\geq\vartheta_{k}(z)+\langle\widehat{\xi},z\rangle+(L_{k,j}/q)\|z-x^{k}\|^{q}+g(\widehat{x})-\langle\widehat{\xi},\widehat{x}\rangle
for any
z\in\mathbb{X}
. Note that
L_{k,0}>\max(0,-\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})))
when
q=2
. Together with
q\in[2,3]
and the expression of
\vartheta_{k}
, it follows that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, as
\|z\|\to\infty
,

\vartheta_{k}(z)+\langle\widehat{\xi},z\rangle+(L_{k,j}/q)\|z-x^{k}\|^{q}+g(\widehat{x})-\langle\widehat{\xi},\widehat{x}\rangle\to\infty.

That is, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, subproblem (
10
) is coercive, so has an optimal solution.
Fix any
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\overline{y}^{k,j}
be an optimal solution of (
10
). Clearly,
\overline{y}^{k,j}
is a stationary point of subproblem (
10
) and
\Theta_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})\leq\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})
. The rest only argues that
\Theta_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})\neq\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})
. If not, then
x^{k}
is an optimal solution of subproblem (
10
), and
0\in\nabla\!f_{k,j}(x^{k})+\partial g(x^{k})=\nabla\!f(x^{k})+\partial g(x^{k})
, a contradiction to
r(x^{k})\neq 0
.

(ii)
Fix any
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\overline{y}^{k,j}
be a stationary point of (
10
) with
\Theta_{k}(\overline{y}^{k,j})<\Theta_{k}(x^{k})
. Clearly,
\overline{y}^{k,j}\neq x^{k}
. From
r_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})=0
, we have

r_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})-\varrho L_{k,j}\|\overline{y}^{k,j}\!-x^{k}\|^{q-1}<0
.
Note that
r_{k,j}(\cdot)
is continuous because
{\rm prox}_{g}
is nonexpansive and
\nabla\!f_{k,j}
is continuous. Then, for any
y
sufficiently close to
\overline{y}^{k,j}
,
r_{k,j}(y)\!-\!\varrho L_{k,j}\|y\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}\leq 0
. The rest only argues that for any
y
close enough to
\overline{y}^{k,j}
,
\Theta_{k}(y\!-\!R_{k,j}(y))\leq\Theta_{k}(x^{k})
. Indeed, from the continuity of
R_{k,j}
and
R_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})=0
, we have
\big(y\!-\!R_{k,j}(y),R_{k,j}(y)\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j}(y)\big)\to\big(\overline{y}^{k,j},-\nabla\!f_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})\big)
as
y\to\overline{y}^{k,j}
.
In addition, by the expression of
R_{k,j}
, for any
z\in\mathbb{X}
,
R_{k,j}(z)-\nabla\!f_{k,j}(z)\in\partial g(z\!-\!R_{k,j}(z))
. Then, the subdifferential continuity of
g
at
\overline{y}^{k,j}
by
[
41
, Example 13.30]
implies that
g(y\!-\!R_{k,j}(y))\to g(\overline{y}^{k,j})
as
y\to\overline{y}^{k,j}
. Thus,
\Theta_{k,j}(y-\!R_{k,j}(y))\to\Theta_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})
as
y\to\overline{y}^{k,j}
. Together with
\Theta_{k,j}(\overline{y}^{k,j})<\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})
, we have
\Theta_{k,j}(y-R_{k,j}(y))\leq\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})
. The above arguments show that for any
y
close enough to
\overline{y}^{k,j}
, the vector
y-R_{k,j}(y)
satisfies condition (
11
).

(iii)
Suppose on the contrary that the result does not hold. For all
j
large enough,

F(y^{k,j})>F(x^{k})-\sigma(L_{k,j}/q)\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{q}.

(16)

From
\Theta_{k,j}(y^{k,j})\leq\Theta_{k,j}(x^{k})
and the expression of
\Theta_{k,j}
, for each
j
large enough,

\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j})+(L_{k,j}/q)\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{q}+g(y^{k,j})\leq F(x^{k}).

(17)

Invoking inequality (
15
) with
x=y^{k,j}
and inequality (
17
) leads to

\displaystyle F(x^{k})

\displaystyle\geq\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j})+(L_{k,j}/q)\|y^{k,j}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}+\langle\widehat{\xi},y^{k,j}\!-\!\widehat{x}\rangle+g(\widehat{x}),

\displaystyle\geq f(x^{k})-\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|\,\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|-\|\widehat{\xi}\|\|y^{k,j}\!-\!\widehat{x}\|+g(\widehat{x})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\rm min}(\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k}))\|y^{k,j}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}+(L_{k,j}/q)\|y^{k,j}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q},

where the second inequality is due to the expression of
\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j})
. Recall that
L_{k,j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
. The above inequality, along with
q\in[2,3]
, implies that
\lim_{j\to\infty}y^{k,j}=x^{k}
. Next we claim that
\liminf_{j\to\infty}L_{k,j}\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{q-1}>0
. If not, there exists
J\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\liminf_{J\ni j\to\infty}L_{k,j}\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{q-1}=0
, which by the first inequality of (
11
) implies that
\lim_{J\ni j\to\infty}r_{k,j}(y^{k,j})=0
. Along with the expression of
R_{k,j}
, we have

0=\lim_{J\ni j\to\infty}\big[y^{k,j}\!-\!{\rm prox}_{g}(y^{k,j}\!-\!\nabla\!\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j})\!-\!L_{k,j}\|y^{k,j}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}(y^{k,j}\!-\!x^{k}))\big],

which by
\lim_{j\to\infty}y^{k,j}=x^{k}
implies that
r(x^{k})=0
, a contradiction to
r(x^{k})\neq 0
. Thus,
\liminf_{j\to\infty}L_{k,j}\|y^{k,j}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}>0
,
which implies that for all sufficiently large
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1\!-\!\sigma)({L_{k,j}}/{q})\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{q}\geq o(\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{2})
. Together with
\lim_{j\to\infty}y^{k,j}=x^{k}
, the second-order Taylor expansion of
f
at
x^{k}
and
q\in[2,3]
, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
large enough,

\displaystyle F(y^{k,j})-F(x^{k})

\displaystyle=\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j})+o(\|y^{k,j}-x^{k}\|^{2})+g(y^{k,j})-F(x^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j})+g(y^{k,j})-F(x^{k})+(1\!-\!\sigma)({L_{k,j}}/{q})\|y^{k,j}\!-x^{k}\|^{q}.

Combining this with (
17
) yields that
F(y^{k,j})-F(x^{k})\leq-\sigma(L_{k,j}/q)\|y^{k,j}\!-x^{k}\|^{q}
, a contradiction to inequality (
16
). The conclusion then follows.

For convenience, in the rest of this paper, we let
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. Next we take a closer look at the properties of the iterate and objective value sequences of Algorithm

.

Proposition 5
.

Under Assumption
1
, the following assertions hold.

(i)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
F(x^{k+1})\leq F(y^{k})\leq F(x^{k})-\sigma(L_{m}/q)\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}
; consequently, the sequence
\{F(x^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is nonincreasing and convergent.

(ii)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}<\infty
and hence
\lim_{k\to\infty}(y^{k}-x^{k})=0
.

(iii)

If
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, then the sequence

Proposition 6

Proof.

(i)
Suppose on the contrary that the result does not hold. There exists
\mathcal{K}\!\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\lim_{\mathcal{K}\ni k\to\infty}L_{k}\!=\infty

\widetilde{L}_{k}\!:=\tau^{-1}L_{k}
. Clearly,

(18)

Note that
\Theta_{k,j_{k}-1}(y^{k,j_{k}-1})\leq\!\Theta_{k,j_{k}-1}(x^{k})\!=F(x^{k})

\displaystyle q^{-1}\widetilde{L}_{k}\|y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq F(x^{k})\!-\!g(y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1})-\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1})

\displaystyle\leq F(x^{0})\!-\!g(y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1})-\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1})

\displaystyle\leq F(x^{0})-g(\widehat{x})+\langle\widehat{\xi},\widehat{x}\!-\!y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1}\rangle-\vartheta_{k}(y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1})

\displaystyle\quad+\|\nabla\!f(x^{k})\|\|y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1}\!-\!x^{k}\|\!+\!\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\|\|y^{k,j_{k}\!-\!1}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}

(ii)
By Proposition

\displaystyle\leq g(\overline{x})+\limsup_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}\ni\nu\to\infty}\,\langle v^{k_{\nu}\!-\!1}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k_{\nu}\!-\!1}(y^{k_{\nu}\!-\!1}),(y^{k_{\nu}\!-\!1}\!-\!v^{k_{\nu}\!-\!1})-\overline{x}\rangle

4
Convergence analysis of Algorithm
1

We shall focus on the full convergence of
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, the local superlinear convergence rates of
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{F(x^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and the global complexity bounds in terms of the first-order optimality conditions. First, we discuss the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla\!f
and
\nabla^{2}\!f
on a compact convex set under Assumptions
1
-
2
. Recall that
\omega(x^{0})
is nonempty and compact by Proposition
6
(iii). From the openness and convexity of
\mathcal{N}\supset\omega(x^{0})
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}{\rm dist}(x^{k},\omega(x^{0}))=0
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=0
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|x^{k}\!-\!y^{k}\|=0
, there necessarily exist a compact convex set
\Gamma\subset\mathcal{N}
, a constant
\overline{\varepsilon}>0
and an index
\overline{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

,

\displaystyle x^{k}\in\!\!\bigcup_{x\in\omega(x^{0})}\!\!\mathbb{B}(x,\overline{\varepsilon}/2)\subset\!\!\bigcup_{x\in\omega(x^{0})}\!\!\mathbb{B}(x,\overline{\varepsilon})\subset\Gamma\ \ {\rm and}\ \ y^{k},\,y^{k}\!-\!v^{k}\in\Gamma.

(21)

Recall that
\mathcal{N}\!\subset\mathcal{O}
. From
\Gamma\!\subset\mathcal{N}
and Assumption

(i), there exists

such that

\|\nabla\!f(y)-\nabla\!f(x)\|\leq L_{g}\|y-x\|\quad\forall x,y\in\Gamma.

By Assumption

(ii), there exists a constant

such that for all

(23)

Along with the convexity of

and the integral expression of

\|\nabla\!f(y)\!-\!\nabla\!f(x)\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x)(y\!-\!x)\|\leq({L_{H}}/{2})\|y-x\|^{2}\quad\ \forall x,y\in\Gamma.

(24)

4.1
Global convergence

To establish the full convergence of the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
we need to introduce the following potential function

\Phi(w)\!:=F(y-v)\quad\ \forall\,w=(y,v)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}.

(25)

By
[
41

,
\partial\Phi(w)=\{(\xi;-\xi)\ |\ \xi\in\partial F(y-v)\}
for any

. Hence, if
w^{*}=(y^{*},v^{*})
is a critical point of
\Phi
, then
x^{*}=y^{*}-v^{*}
is a critical point of
F
; and conversely, if
x^{*}
is a critical point of
F
, then
w^{*}=(x^{*},0)
is a critical point of
\Phi
. By the expression of
\Phi
and the proof of
[
28
, Theorem 3.2]
, the following fact holds.

Fact 1

If
F
is a KL function (of exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
), then so is the function
\Phi
.

In the rest of this section, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, write
w^{k}\!:=(y^{k},v^{k})
. By invoking condition (
11
), we can prove that
{\rm dist}(0,\partial\Phi(w^{k}))
is upper bounded by
\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}
.

Lemma 7
.

Under Assumptions
1
-
2
, we have
{\rm dist}(0,\partial\Phi(w^{k}))\!\leq\!\beta\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}
for all

, where
\beta:=\!\sqrt{2}[\varrho(1\!+\!L_{g})\!+\!1]\widehat{L}+\!\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}L_{H}
with
\widehat{L}
from Proposition
6

Proof.

By using previous (
21
) and (
24
) with
y=y^{k}
and

, for all

,

\|\nabla\!f(y^{k})-\nabla\!f(x^{k})-\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})(y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\|\leq({L_{H}}/{2})\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}.

(26)

Fix any

. From the inclusion in (
14

\partial\Phi
, it holds that

\zeta^{k}\!:=\left(\begin{matrix}\nabla\!f(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})\!+\!v^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k})\\
-\nabla\!f(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})\!-\!v^{k}\!+\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k})\end{matrix}\right)\in\partial\Phi(w^{k}).

By using the expression of

and (

\displaystyle\|\zeta^{k}\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\big\|\nabla\!f(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})\!+\!v^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k})\big\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\|\nabla\!f(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})\!+\!v^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f(x^{k})\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})(y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\!-\!L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}(y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\big[\|v^{k}\|+\|\nabla\!f(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})-\nabla\!f(y^{k})\|+L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\|\nabla\!f(y^{k})-\nabla\!f(x^{k})-\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})(y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\|\big]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq-quad}}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{2}\big[(1\!+\!L_{g})\|v^{k}\|+({L_{H}}/{2})\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}+L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}\big].

Note that

(if necessary by increasing

.
Note that the sequence
\{w^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

the set of its accumulation points. Recall that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|=0

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v^{k}\|=0

for all

. Note that
\Phi

[
7

and

\varphi^{\prime}(\Phi(w)\!-\!\overline{F}){\rm dist}(0,\partial\Phi(w))\geq 1.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}

\Phi

12

\Phi(w^{k})-\overline{F}=F(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})-\overline{F}\geq F(x^{k+1})-\overline{F}>0.

(28)

20

for some

\{y^{k}\!-\!v^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

k\geq\widehat{k}:=\overline{k}\!+\!1
,

\beta\varphi^{\prime}\big(\Phi(w^{k-1})-\overline{F}\big)\|y^{k-1}\!-\!x^{k-1}\|^{q-1}\geq 1.

(29)

Fix any
k\geq\widehat{k}
. As
\varphi^{\prime}
is nonincreasing on
(0,\varpi)
, combining (
28
) and (
29
) leads to

\varphi^{\prime}(F(x^{k})\!-\!\overline{F})\geq\varphi^{\prime}(\Phi(w^{k-1})\!-\!\overline{F})\geq\frac{1}{\beta\|y^{k-1}\!-\!x^{k-1}\|^{q-1}}.

(30)

Together with the concavity of
\varphi
and Proposition
5
(i), it follows that

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,k+1}

\displaystyle:=\varphi(F(x^{k})\!-\!\overline{F})-\varphi(F(x^{k+1})\!-\!\overline{F})\geq\varphi^{\prime}(F(x^{k})-\overline{F})(F(x^{k})\!-\!F(x^{k+1}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{F(x^{k})\!-\!F(x^{k+1})}{\beta\|y^{k-1}-x^{k-1}\|^{q-1}}\geq\frac{\sigma L_{m}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q}}{\beta q\|y^{k-1}\!-\!x^{k-1}\|^{q-1}},

which can be rearranged as
\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|\leq\|y^{k-1}\!-\!x^{k-1}\|^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\big[\beta q(\sigma L_{m})^{-1}\Delta_{k,k+1}\big]^{\frac{1}{q}}
.
Using the Young’s inequality
u^{\frac{q-1}{q}}v^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{q-1}{q}u+\frac{1}{q}v
for
u\geq 0,v\geq 0
yields that

\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|\leq q^{-1}(q-1)\|y^{k-1}\!-\!x^{k-1}\|+\beta(\sigma L_{m})^{-1}\Delta_{k,k+1}.

Summing this inequality from
\widehat{k}
to any
l>\widehat{k}
and using the nonnegativity of
\varphi
leads to
q{\textstyle\sum_{i=\widehat{k}}^{l}}\|y^{i}-x^{i}\|\leq(q\!-\!1)\big[{\textstyle\sum_{i=\widehat{k}}^{l}}\|y^{i}\!-\!x^{i}\|+\|y^{\widehat{k}-1}\!-\!x^{\widehat{k}-1}\|\big]+\frac{\beta q\varphi(F(x^{\widehat{k}})-\overline{F})}{\sigma L_{m}}
. Then,

{\textstyle\sum_{i=\widehat{k}}^{l}}\|y^{i}-x^{i}\|\leq(q\!-\!1)\|y^{\widehat{k}-1}\!-\!x^{\widehat{k}-1}\|+\beta q(\sigma L_{m})^{-1}\varphi(F(x^{\widehat{k}})-\overline{F}).

Passing the limit
l\to\infty
to this inequality results in
\sum_{i=\widehat{k}}^{\infty}\|y^{i}-x^{i}\|<\infty
. From
q-1\geq 1
, it follows that
\sum_{i=\widehat{k}}^{\infty}\|y^{i}-x^{i}\|^{q-1}<\infty
.
Recall that
\|v^{k}\|\leq\varrho L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}
and
L_{k}\leq\widehat{L}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

{\textstyle\sum_{i\geq\widehat{k}}}\|v^{i}\|<\infty
. Now, from (
12
), we have

\displaystyle{\textstyle\sum_{i\geq\widehat{k}}}\|x^{i+1}-x^{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq{\textstyle\sum_{i\geq\widehat{k}}}\|y^{i}-x^{i}\|+{\textstyle\sum_{i\geq\widehat{k}}}\|v^{i}\|<\infty.

This shows that the desired result holds. The proof is completed.

Theorem 9
.

\!\alpha k^{\frac{1-\theta}{1-(q\theta)/(q-1)}}&{\rm if}\ \theta\in(\frac{q-1}{q},1).\end{array}\right.

Lemma 10

\displaystyle\quad+(L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-3}\!+\!L_{H})\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\big(1\!+\!L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\!+\!\|\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\|\big)\|v^{k}\|\big],

\displaystyle F(y^{k}\!-\!v^{k})-F(x^{*})\leq\frac{2L_{H}}{3}\|y^{k}\!-\!v^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|^{3}+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}\|y^{k}\!-\!v^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|.

where the constant

is the same as the one in Lemma

Proof.

Fix any

. Let

. From the equality in (

,
which by

and the definition of

means that

\langle z^{k}-x^{*},\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*})\rangle\geq 0.

(31)

In addition, combining the inclusion in (
14
) and the expression of

leads to

v^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k})\!=v^{k}-\!\nabla\!f(x^{k})-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})(y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\!-\!L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}(y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\!\in\partial g(z^{k}).

Together with

and the monotonicity of

, it follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle z^{k}-x^{*},\nabla\!f(x^{*})+v^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f(x^{k})-\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})(y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\langle z^{k}-x^{*},y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\rangle,

which, after a suitable rearrangement, can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\big[L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\mathcal{I}+\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\big](y^{k}\!-\!x^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\nabla\!f(x^{*})\!-\!\nabla\!f(x^{k})\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})(x^{*}-x^{k})\rangle+\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},v^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}(x^{*}\!-\!x^{k})+\big[\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\big](y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\rangle.

Adding the term
\langle x^{*}\!-\!z^{k},\big[L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\mathcal{I}+\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\big]v^{k}\rangle
to this inequality leads to

\displaystyle\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\big[L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\mathcal{I}+\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\big](z^{k}\!-\!x^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\nabla\!f(x^{*})-\nabla\!f(x^{k})-\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})(x^{*}-x^{k})\rangle+\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},v^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}(x^{*}\!-\!x^{k})+\big[\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\big](y^{k}\!-\!x^{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\big[L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\mathcal{I}+\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\big]v^{k}\rangle.

Together with the above (
31
) and the triangle inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\|z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|\big[\|\nabla\!f(x^{*})-\nabla\!f(x^{k})-\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})(x^{*}\!-\!x^{k})\|

\displaystyle\quad+L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\|x^{*}\!-\!x^{k}\|+\|\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\|\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|+\|v^{k}\|

\displaystyle\quad+L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-2}\|v^{k}\|+\|\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\|\|v^{k}\|\big].

Recall that
x^{k},x^{*}\in\Gamma
. Invoking (
23
)-(
24
) with

results in

\displaystyle\|\nabla\!f(x^{*})\!-\!\nabla\!f(x^{k})\!+\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})(x^{k}\!-\!x^{*})\|+\|\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\|\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq(L_{H}/2)\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|^{2}+L_{H}\|x^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|.

From the above two inequalities, we obtain the first desired inequality.
For the second one, combining

with the convexity of

leads to

g(z^{k})\leq g(x^{*})+\langle v^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k}),z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\rangle.

(32)

In addition, from Assumption
1
(i) and the integral formula, it follows that

\displaystyle f(z^{k})\!-\!f(x^{*})

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\!f(x^{*}),z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\rangle+\Big\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*}\!+\!s(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}))(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*})dsdt\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\!=\!\Big\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*}\!+\!s(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}))(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*})dsdt\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\nabla\!f(z^{k}),z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\rangle-\Big\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}f(x^{*}\!+t(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}))(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*})dt\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq-spoint}}}{{\leq}}\!\Big\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\big[\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*}\!+\!s(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}))\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\big](z^{k}\!-\!x^{*})dsdt\Big\rangle.

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big\langle z^{k}\!-\!x^{*},\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\big[\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*})\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{*}\!+\!t(z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}))\big](z^{k}\!-\!x^{*})dt\Big\rangle

Adding this inequality to the above (

\displaystyle\leq({2L_{H}}/{3})\|z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|^{3}+\langle\nabla\!f(z^{k})+v^{k}\!-\!\nabla f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k}),z^{k}\!-\!x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle r(x^{k+1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{yk-vk}}}{{=}}\|x^{k+1}-{\rm prox}_{g}(x^{k+1}\!-\!\nabla\!f(x^{k+1}))+v^{k}-y^{k}+{\rm prox}_{g}(y^{k}\!-\!\nabla\!f_{k,j_{k}}(y^{k}))\|

(39)

\{r(x^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

(43)

Note that

4.3
Complexity bounds

We derive a global complexity bound for Algorithm
1
with
q\in[2,3]
in terms of the first-order optimality conditions by replacing Assumption
2
(ii) with the strict continuity of
\nabla^{2}\!f
on a compact convex set
\Omega
containing

. Note that
\Omega
does not necessarily contain the compact convex set

.

Theorem 16
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
2
(i) holds, and that
\nabla^{2}\!f
is strictly continuous on a compact convex set
\Omega
containing
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

Then, for any given
\epsilon\in(0,1)

with

\widehat{\eta}\!=\!(\varrho(3\!+\!L_{g})\!+\!1)\widehat{L}\!+\!{L_{H}}/{2}
, where
L_{H}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla^{2}\!f
on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
\nabla^{2}\!f(\cdot)
is assumed to be strictly continuous on the compact convex set
\Omega
, it is Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
by
[
41
, Theorem 9.2]
. We first derive an upper bound of
r(x^{k+1})
in terms of
\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|
. It is easy to check (
4.2
) holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. From the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla^{2}\!f(\cdot)
on
\Omega
and
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega
, inequality (
26
) holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

\|\nabla\!f(x^{k+1})\!-\!\nabla\vartheta_{k}(y^{k})\|\leq L_{g}\|v^{k}\|+(L_{H}/2)\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}.

By combining this inequality with (
4.2
) and (
11
), for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

\displaystyle r(x^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq 3\|v^{k}\|+L_{g}\|v^{k}\|+(L_{H}/2)\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2}+L_{k}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq(\varrho(3\!+\!L_{g})\!+\!1)\widehat{L}\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{q-1}+(L_{H}/2)\|y^{k}\!-\!x^{k}\|^{2},

(48)

where the second inequality is due to
L_{k}\leq\widehat{L}
by Proposition
6
(i).
As
r(x^{K+1})\leq\epsilon
and Algorithm
1
stops at the
(K\!+\!1)
th iteration, we have
r(x^{i})>\epsilon
for
i=1,\ldots,K
. Fix any
i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
. When
\|y^{i-1}\!-\!x^{i-1}\|>1
, combining the above (
4.3
) with
r(x^{i})>\epsilon
and the definition of
\widehat{\eta}
leads to
\|y^{i-1}\!-\!x^{i-1}\|\geq\widehat{\eta}^{-1/2}\epsilon^{1/2}\geq\widehat{\eta}^{-1/2}\epsilon^{1/(q-1)}
. When
\|y^{i-1}\!-\!x^{i-1}\|\leq 1
, the above (
4.3
) along with

and the definition of
\widehat{\eta}
implies that
\|y^{i-1}\!-\!x^{i-1}\|\geq\widehat{\eta}^{-1/(q-1)}\epsilon^{1/(q-1)}
. Thus,
\|y^{i-1}\!-\!x^{i-1}\|\geq\min\{\widehat{\eta}^{-1/2},\widehat{\eta}^{-1/(q-1)}\}\epsilon^{1/(q-1)}
. Together with Proposition
5
(i), it follows that

F(x^{i-1})-F(x^{i})\geq\sigma L_{m}q^{-1}\|y^{i-1}\!-\!x^{i-1}\|^{q}\geq\sigma L_{m}q^{-1}\min\{\widehat{\eta}^{-q/2},\widehat{\eta}^{-q/(q-1)}\}\epsilon^{\frac{q}{q-1}}.

Recall that
F(x^{K})
is bounded below by
\overline{F}
by Proposition
5
(i). Then, it holds that

F(x^{0})-\overline{F}\geq{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}}(F(x^{i-1})\!-\!F(x^{i}))\geq K\sigma L_{m}q^{-1}\min\{\widehat{\eta}^{-q/2},\widehat{\eta}^{-q/(q-1)}\}\epsilon^{\frac{q}{q-1}},

which, after a suitable rearrangement, yields the desired result.

When
g\equiv 0
, the complexity bound of Theorem
16
with

matches the one obtained in
[
37
]
for the CR method and those obtained in
[
13
]
for its variants, namely,
O(\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}})
. When

for a closed convex set

, the complexity bound of Theorem

also fits with the one established in
[

.

and common nonsmooth convex
g
. All tests are performed on a desktop running on Matlab R2020b and 64-bit Windows System with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i9-10850K CPU 3.60GHz and 32.0 GB RAM.

5.1
Implementation of Algorithm
1

The core of Algorithm
1
is the inexact computation of subproblem (
10
), the minimization of the sum of the
q
-order regularized quadratic approximation of
f
at
x^{k}
and the nonsmooth convex
g
. Based on Remark 3.3 in the archive version of this work, we choose ZeroFPR as a solver to subproblems. ZeroFPR is a line-search algorithm proposed in
[
43
]
for the composite problem of form (
1
) with a general nonsmooth
g
by seeking a critical point of the forward-backward envelope of
F
with the limited-memory BFGS (lbfgs). When calling ZeroFPR to solve (
10
), we adopt its default setting except that the inexactness criterion (
11
) is used as the stopping condition and the maximum number of iterations is set as
10^{3}
. For the subsequent tests, the parameters of Algorithm
1
are chosen as

\varrho={0.9}/{(1.1\!+\!L_{0})},\ \tau=10,\ \delta=10^{-5},\ L_{m}=10^{-12}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ L_{\!M}=10^{8},

where
L_{0}
is an estimate for the constant
L_{g}
appearing in (
22
), i.e., a positive constant such that
f(x^{0,*})\leq f(x^{0})+\langle\nabla\!f(x^{0}),x^{0,*}\!-\!x^{0}\rangle\!+\!\frac{L_{0}}{2}\|x^{0,*}\!-\!x^{0}\|^{2}
holds with
x^{0,*}\!=\!{\rm prox}_{L_{0}^{-1}g}(x^{0}\!-\!L_{0}^{-1}\nabla\!f(x^{0}))
. Notice that the constant
L_{k,0}
in step 1 of Algorithm
1
is an initial upper estimation of the Hölder modulus of exponent
q-2
for
\nabla^{2}\!f
at
x^{k}
. Motivated by the Barzilai-Borwein rule
[
6
]
for estimating the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla\!f
(see
[
45
]
), we always choose
L_{0,0}=L_{0}
, and
L_{k,0}
for
k\geq 1
by the following formula

\max\Big\{\min\Big\{\frac{\|[\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k})-\nabla^{2}\!f(x^{k-1})](x^{k}\!-\!x^{k-1})\|}{\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{q-1}},L_{\!M}\Big\},L_{m}\Big\}.

5.3
Numerical comparisons

Based on the convergence curves in Figure
1
, we test the performance of Algorithm
1
with
q=3
(i.e. the CR method), and compare its performance with that of ZeroFPR and IRPNM on three classes of problems of form (
1
) to validate its efficiency. As mentioned above, ZeroFPR is a first-order line-search algorithm that is applicable to (
1
), and its code can be downloaded from
http://github.com/kul-forbes/ForBES

[
30
]
, whose each iteration involves minimizing the sum of a strongly convex quadratic approximation of
f
and the nonsmooth convex function
g
with the solver dALMSN, a dual augmented Lagrangian method armed with the semismooth Newton. The numerical results reported in
[
30
]
and
[
16
]
indicate that IRPNM is superior to GIPN, an inexact proximal Newton-type method proposed in
[
26
]
by solving subproblem with FISTA (named GIPN-F) or the KKT system with the semismooth Newton method (named GIPN-S), and AC-FISTA proposed in
[
29
]
. Here, we do not compare the performance of our CR method with that of GIPN and AC-FISTA separately. For ZeroFPR and IRPNM to solve problem (
1
), we adopt their default setting. For fairness of numerical comparisons, the three solvers start from the same initial point
x^{0}\in{\rm dom}\,g
and stop under the same condition
r(x^{k})\leq\epsilon
.

This class of problem takes the form of (

was introduced in
[

]
to deal with the data contaminated by heavy-tailed Student-

[

]
. Specifically, we generate a true sparse signal

of length

with

x^{\rm true}_{i}=\eta_{1}(i)10^{d\eta_{2}(i)/20}
, where
\eta_{1}(i)\in\{1,-1\}

\eta_{2}(i)
is uniformly distributed in
[0,1]
. The signal has a dynamic range of
d
dB with

. The matrix

takes
m={n}/{8}
random cosine measurements, i.e.,
Ax=(\verb"dct"(x))_{J}
, where
\verb"dct"
is the discrete cosine transform and
J\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
|J|=m
is an index set chosen randomly. The vector
b
is obtained by adding Student’s
t
-noise with degree of freedom
4
and rescaled by
0.1
to
Ax^{\rm true}
. We choose
\nu=0.25
and
\lambda=c_{\lambda}\|\nabla\!f(0)\|_{\infty}
with

for numerical tests.

We run three solvers from the starting point
x^{0}\!=A^{\top}b
with the stopping accuracy
\epsilon=10^{-5}
for
10

(A,b)
generated randomly. Table
3
reports the average objective values, KKT residuals, and running time over
10
independent trials. We see that the three solvers yield the same objective values for all test examples, and our CR method and IRPNM return comparable KKT residuals for all test examples, which are superior to those returned by ZeroFPR. Compared with IRPNM, our CR method requires fewer iterations and needs less running time for
d=80
but a little more time for other
d
. Notice that the test examples with
d=80
are more difficult than those with

5.3.3
Portfolio decision with higher moments

In this example, we focus on the higher moment portfolio selection problem described in
[
38
,
48
]
. Let
\xi
be a random vector to denote the return rate of
n
assets. It is known that the mean and variance of
\xi
have been widely used to assess the expected profit and risk of the portfolio, and the associated standard mean-variance portfolio model is tailored to Gaussian distributed return. When the return distribution exhibits heavy tail and asymmetry, it is reasonable to extend the classical mean-variance optimization to higher moments
[
25
]
and consider the MVSK model of form (
1
) with
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

f(x)=-\omega_{1}E(x)+\omega_{2}V(x)-\omega_{3}S(x)+\omega_{4}K(x)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ g(x)=\chi_{\Delta}(x),

where
\omega_{i}\in[0,1]
with
\sum_{i=1}^{4}\omega_{i}=1
are the investor’s preference levels corresponding to the four moments, and
\Delta=\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\ |\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=1\}
denotes the feasible set of portfolio weights. Here,
E,V,S,K
represent four moments of the portfolio, and for their definitions and gradient and Hessian computation formulas, please refer to
[
48
]
.

We choose the weekly returns
R\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times T}
of NASDAQ100 index during the period 26/08/2022-25/08/2023 as the test dataset, where
N
is the total number of stocks and
T
is the number of weeks during the selected period. The test data is obtained by the following two steps:
(1)
randomly select
n\leq N
stocks from the dataset and compute the return rate of each selected stock via
\xi_{it}=100[\log(R_{i,t+1})-\log(R_{i,t})]
for
t=1,2,\ldots,T\!-\!1
;
(2)
compute four sample moments of the selected
n
stocks as in
[
38
]
by using the return rate and the weight vector
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\omega_{4})=(0.29,0.21,0.4,0.1)
. Table
4
reports the numerical results of three solvers with the stopping accuracy
\epsilon=10^{-5}
and the starting point
x^{0}=\frac{1}{n}(1,1,\ldots,1)^{\top}
. We see that the CR method is significantly superior to ZeroFPR in terms of running time, and it returns the comparable objective values and a little better KKT residuals for all test examples. For this class of problems, the computation of function and gradient values of
f
is expensive. The time difference between CR method and ZeroFPR is attributed to the fact that Algorithm
1
needs fewer calls of function and gradient values of
f
. Our CR method has the comparable performance with IRPNM except that the former needs a little fewer iterations and returns a little better objective values. For this class of test problems, IRPNM with the default setting exhibits poor numerical performance, failing to return a solution with the desired accuracy within the maximum number of iterations. The results of IRPNM in Table
4
are obtained by modifying IRPNM with a tighter approximation as suggested in
[
30
, Section 4.2]
to construct the subproblem as well as choosing the initial penalty parameter of dALMSN again.

Conclusions

We proposed a practical inexact
q
-order regularized proximal Newton method for
q\in[2,3]
to solve nonconvex composite problem (
1
), which becomes an inexact CR method for
q=3
. We proved the full convergence of the generated iterate sequence under the KL property of
F
, and the local superlinear rate of order
\frac{q-1}{\theta q}
under the KL property of
F
with exponent
\theta\in(0,\frac{q-1}{q})
. Furthermore, the iterate and objective value sequences were proved to have a local superlinear rate of order
\gamma(q\!-\!1)
under a local Hölderian error bound of order
\gamma
on the second-order stationary point set
\mathcal{X}^{*}
. These results improve those of exact or inexact CR methods obtained in
[
17
,
20
]
for convex composite optimization, and they are first extended to nonconvex composite problems. Numerical tests have shown that Algorithm
1
armed with ZeroFPR as the inner solver is very promising for those problems with highly nonlinear
f
. An interesting topic is to develop a robust and fast inner solver, i.e., algorithm to solve the composite optimization problem of minimizing the sum of a
q
-order regularized nonconvex quadratic function and a nonsmooth convex function.

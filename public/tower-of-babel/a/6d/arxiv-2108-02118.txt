The volume-of-tube method for Gaussian random fields with inhomogeneous variance
Satoshi Kuriki, Akimichi Takemura, Jonathan E. Taylor

Abstract
. The tube method or the volume-of-tube method approximates the tail probability of the maximum of a smooth Gaussian random field with zero mean and unit variance. This method evaluates the volume of a spherical tube about the index set, and then transforms it to the tail probability. In this study, we generalize the tube method to a case in which the variance is not constant. We provide the volume formula for a spherical tube with a non-constant radius in terms of curvature tensors, and the tail probability formula of the maximum of a Gaussian random field with inhomogeneous variance, as well as its Laplace approximation. In particular, the critical radius of the tube is generalized for evaluation of the asymptotic approximation error. As an example, we discuss the approximation of the largest eigenvalue distribution of the Wishart matrix with a non-identity matrix parameter. The Bonferroni method is the tube method when the index set is a finite set. We provide the formula for the asymptotic approximation error for the Bonferroni method when the variance is not constant.



1.
Introduction: Tube with non-constant radius

Let
M\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be a
d
-dimensional
C^{2}
-closed submanifold of the (
n-1
)-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=S(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\|u\|=1\}

We assume that the linear-hull of
M
is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
M
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
(that is,

).
Define a Gaussian random field on
M
by

(1.1)

X(u)=\sigma(u)\langle u,\xi\rangle,\quad u\in M\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

where
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a random vector distributed as the standard Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}_{n}(0,I_{n})
,

(

The purpose of this study is to provide a formula for approximating the tail probability of the maximum

(1.2)

\mathbb{P}\biggl(\max_{u\in M}X(u)>c\biggr)\quad\mbox{when $c$ is large}.

When the variance is constant, say,
\sigma(u)\equiv 1
,
the recipes to approximate the tail probability (

) are well established and known as the volume-of-tube method, or simply, the tube method (
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

,

]
,
[
TK02
,

]

The tail probability of (

such as construction of simultaneous confidence bands,
likelihood ratio test in singular models,

projection pursuit/testing Gaussianity, and so on
(e.g.,
[
Nai86
,

]
,
[

]

[

]

The tube method provides sufficiently accurate

In this study, we extend the tube formula to Gaussian random fields with inhomogeneous variance.
For later purposes, we define a standardized random field on
M
by

Y(u)=\sigma(u)\bigl\langle u,\xi/\|\xi\|\bigr\rangle,\ \ u\in M,\ \ \xi\sim\mathcal{N}_{n}(0,I_{n}).

We sometimes use the abbreviations

Note that the support of

Because

Moreover, because

, the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{max}}>b\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\biggl(\max_{u\in M}\sigma(u)\langle u,\xi/\|\xi\|\rangle>b\biggr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\exists u\in M,\,\langle u,\xi/\|\xi\|\rangle>b/\sigma(u)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}}\Vol_{n-1}\bigl((M)_{b/\sigma(\cdot)}\bigr),

where

(1.5)

\displaystyle(M)_{f}=

\displaystyle\bigl\{w\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\mid\exists u\in M,\,\langle u,w\rangle>f(u)\bigr\},

\Vol_{n-1}(\cdot)
is the (
n-1
)-dimensional volume, and

)-dimensional unit sphere.
Further, we observe that the distribution of

is derived from that of

(It is shown that the relation between

As a related problem,

formulated a test statistic for a global null hypothesis in a regularized regression
as the maximum of a Gaussian random field with inhomogeneous variance:

\max_{\eta\in K}\langle\eta,\xi\rangle,\quad\xi\sim\mathcal{N}_{n}(0,I_{n}),

where

.
In our notation, (

Differently from

, we treat the unconditional distribution of the maximum.
Another difference is the assumption of convexity.
In this paper,

We will examine the effects of the non-convexity of the set

{\sigma}M=\bigl\{\sigma(u)u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid u\in M\bigr\}.

It will be shown that only the supporting points of

.

The outline of the paper is as follows.
The main results are summarized in Section

.
We present the approximate formulas for the tail probability of

and

.
To evaluate their approximation error,

Consequently, based on the resulting formula, we provide its Laplace approximation under the assumption that the set

forms a closed

-manifold.
In addition, we discuss the Bonferroni method, which is the tube method when the index set is finite.
We provide the formula for the asymptotic approximation error for the Bonferroni method when the variance is not constant.
Examples are provided in Section
3
.
One of them is the tail probability of the largest eigenvalue of the Wishart matrix when the parameter matrix is not an identity matrix, which will be of independent interest.
All proofs for the main results are provided in Section
4
.

2.
Main Results

2.1.
Preliminary and notations

The index set
M
is treated as a
C^{2}
-submanifold endowed with the metric induced by the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Suppose that each point
u\in M
is represented by the local coordinates
t=(t^{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}
as
u=\varphi(t)
.
We use conventions
\partial/\partial t^{i}=\partial_{i}
,

\varphi_{i}=\partial_{i}\varphi
,

\varphi_{ij}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi
,
etc
.
The tangent space of
M
at
u\in M
is

T_{u}(M)=\spn\{\varphi_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d\}.

The metric tensor is

g_{ij}=\langle\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\rangle
,
and the affine connection is

\Gamma_{ij,k}=\langle\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{k}\rangle=(1/2)(\partial_{i}g_{jk}+\partial_{j}g_{ik}-\partial_{k}g_{ij})
.
The volume element of
M
at
u\in M
is
\det(G)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i}\mathrm{d}t^{i}|_{u}
, where
G=(g_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
is the metric tensor in
d\times d
matrix form.
When needed, we write
G=G_{u}
to indicate the point
u\in M
where the metric is defined.
The covariant derivative in the direction of
\partial/\partial t^{i}
is denoted by
\nabla_{i}
.
The inverse of the metric matrix
G
is denoted by
G^{-1}=(g^{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
.
Let

(2.1)

R_{ij;kl}=\partial_{i}\Gamma_{jk,l}-\partial_{j}\Gamma_{ik,l}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}(\Gamma_{ik,\alpha}\Gamma_{jl,\beta}-\Gamma_{il,\alpha}\Gamma_{jk,\beta})g^{\alpha\beta}

be the curvature tensor.

Define the normal space of
M
at
u
by

(2.2)

N_{u}=T_{u}(M)^{\perp}\cap T_{u}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Note that

\mathbb{R}^{n}=T_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\spn\{u\}\oplus T_{u}(M)\oplus N_{u},

where
\oplus
is the orthogonal direct sum.

Let

(2.3)

\ell=\ell(u)=\log\sigma(u),

and let

(2.4)

\displaystyle C=C_{u}=(c_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq d},

\displaystyle c_{ij}=-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\ell+\nabla_{i}\ell\nabla_{j}\ell=-\biggl(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\ell-\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\Gamma_{ij,k}\partial_{l}\ell g^{kl}\biggr)+(\partial_{i}\ell)(\partial_{j}\ell).

The gradient of
\ell(u)
in (
2.3
) in the tangent space
T_{u}(M)
is

\nabla\ell=\nabla\ell(u)=\nabla|_{T_{u}(M)}\ell(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\nabla_{i}\ell\varphi_{j}g^{ij}.

The squared length of the gradient is

\|\nabla\ell\|^{2}=\|\nabla\ell(u)\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\nabla_{i}\ell)(\nabla_{j}\ell)g^{ij}
.

The
k
th elementary symmetric function of the eigenvalues of a matrix is denoted by
\tr_{k}(\cdot)
(
[
Mui82
]
) or
\detr_{k}(\cdot)
(
[
Wor95
]
).
Note that
\tr_{1}(\cdot)=\tr(\cdot)
, and
\tr_{d}(A)=\det(A)
if
A
is
d\times d
, and let
\tr_{0}(\cdot)=1
.
We use the relation

\det(I_{d}+A)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\tr_{k}(A).

For a finite set
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\}
(
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}
), let

(2.5)

\Sigma(I)=\{(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{n})\mid\{\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{n}\}=I\}

be the set of all permutations of elements of
I
.
For
I
such that
n=|I|
is even, let

(2.6)

\Pi(I)=\bigl\{(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{n})\in\Sigma(I)\mid\pi_{1}<\pi_{2},\,\pi_{3}<\pi_{4},\,\ldots,\pi_{n-1}<\pi_{n}\bigr\}

be the set of ordered parings of
I
.
Let
\Sigma_{n}=\Sigma(\{1,\ldots,n\})
and
\Pi_{n}=\Pi(\{1,\ldots,n\})
.

2.2.
Volume of tube formula

To state main theorems, we introduce the notion of self-overlap of tube.
Figure
1
depicts tube
(M)_{b/\sigma(\cdot)}
.
Suppose we let
b
be small.
Subsequently, the width of the tube becomes large, and therefore causing self-overlap.
More formally,
(M)_{b/\sigma(\cdot)}
is said to have a self-overlap if there exists a point
w\in(M)_{b/\sigma(\cdot)}
such that the function
u\mapsto\sigma(u)\langle u,w\rangle
has two local maxima at the points
u^{*},u^{**}\in M
and

\sigma(u^{*})\langle u^{*},w\rangle=\sigma(u^{**})\langle u^{**},w\rangle
.
The threshold
b=b_{\mathrm{cri}}
causing such overlap is defined by

(2.7)

b_{\mathrm{cri}}=\sup\bigl\{b\geq 0\mid(M)_{b/\sigma(\cdot)}\mbox{ has a self-overlap}\bigr\}.

For
x\geq 0
, let

\bar{B}_{a,b}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\,\Gamma(b)}\int_{x}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}t&(0\leq x\leq 1),\\
0&(x>1),\end{cases}

be the upper probability of the beta distribution with parameter
(a,b)
.
Now, we state the following main results.

Theorem 2.1

(Volume of tube)
.

Let
\mathrm{d}u
be the volume element of
M
at
u\in M
.
Let
b_{\mathrm{cri}}
be the threshold defined in (
2.7
).
For
b\geq b_{\mathrm{cri}}
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{max}}>b\bigr)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}}\Vol_{n-1}\bigl((M)_{b/\sigma(\cdot)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e=0}^{d}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{e/2}\Omega_{d-e+1}}\int_{M}\frac{\det(I_{d}+C_{u}G_{u}^{-1})}{(1+\|\nabla\ell(u)\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}(d-e+1)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\bar{B}_{\frac{1}{2}(d-e+1),\frac{1}{2}(n-d+e-1)}\biggl(\frac{1+\|\nabla\ell(u)\|^{2}}{\sigma(u)^{2}}b^{2}\biggr)\,\zeta_{e}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

where
\Omega_{k}
is defined in (
1.6
),

G_{u}
is the metric matrix at
u
,
C_{u}
is defined in (
2.4
),

(2.9)

\zeta_{e}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,d\},\,|I|=e}\frac{1}{(e/2)!}\sum_{\pi,\tau\in\Pi(I)}\sgn(\pi,\tau)\,\widetilde{R}_{\pi_{1}\pi_{2}}^{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}}\cdots\widetilde{R}_{\pi_{e-1}\pi_{e}}^{\tau_{e-1}\tau_{e}}\,&(\mbox{if $e$ is even}),\\
0&(\mbox{if $e$ is odd}),\end{cases}

with

(2.10)

\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{kl}=\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{kl}(u)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\Bigl(R_{ij;\alpha\beta}-\bigl(g_{i\alpha}g_{j\beta}-g_{i\beta}g_{j\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr)\widetilde{g}^{\alpha k}\widetilde{g}^{\beta l},

\widetilde{g}^{ij}
the
(i,j)
th element of the inverse matrix
\widetilde{G}_{u}^{-1}
of
\widetilde{G}_{u}=G_{u}+C_{u}
,

\sgn(\pi,\tau)=\sgn\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{1}&\cdots&\pi_{e}\\
\tau_{1}&\cdots&\tau_{e}\end{pmatrix}

the sign of permutation.
In particular,
\zeta_{0}(u)=1
and

\zeta_{2}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{ij}(u)
.

Moreover, (
2.8
) holds even if the range
M
of the integral is replaced with

(2.11)

M_{\mathrm{cri}}=\biggl\{u\in M\mid\frac{1+\|\nabla\ell(u)\|^{2}}{\sigma(u)^{2}}b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{2}<1\biggr\}.

Remark 2.2

(Weyl’s tube formula)
.

When
\sigma(u)\equiv 1
, (
2.8
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{max}}>b\bigr)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}}\Vol_{n-1}\bigl((M)_{b}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e=0}^{d}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{e/2}\Omega_{d-e+1}}\int_{M}\,\zeta_{e}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\times\bar{B}_{\frac{1}{2}(d-e+1),\frac{1}{2}(n-d+e-1)}\bigl(b^{2}\bigr),

where
\zeta_{e}(u)
is defined by (

) with

\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{kl}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}R_{ij;\alpha\beta}g^{\alpha k}g^{\beta l}-\bigl(\delta_{i}^{k}\delta_{j}^{l}-\delta_{i}^{l}\delta_{j}^{k}\bigr),\qquad\delta_{i}^{j}=\begin{cases}1&(i=j),\\
0&(i\neq j).\end{cases}

This is original Weyl’s tube formula
[

]
.

The theorem below characterizes the threshold

.
This is also useful for numerical calculation (Section

).

Theorem 2.3
.

The threshold

appearing in Theorem

is

(2.12)

b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{2}=\sup_{u\in M}b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(u)^{2},\qquad b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(u)^{2}=\sup_{w\in M\setminus\{u\}}\frac{\sigma(u)^{2}}{1+h(u,w)},

where

(2.13)

h(u,w)=\|\nabla\ell(u)\|^{2}+\biggl(\frac{\{\sigma(u)/\sigma(w)-\langle w,u-\nabla\ell(u)\rangle\}_{+}}{\|P_{u}^{\perp}w\|}\biggr)^{2}.

Here
x_{+}=\max\{x,0\}
, and

P_{u}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal projection in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto the subspace
N_{u}
defined in (
2.2
).
That is,

\|P_{u}^{\perp}w\|^{2}=1-\langle u,w\rangle^{2}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\langle\varphi_{i},w\rangle\langle\varphi_{j},w\rangle g^{ij}.

Remark 2.4

(Critical radius)
.

When
\sigma(u)\equiv 1
, (
2.12
) becomes

b_{\mathrm{cri}}=\cos\theta_{\mathrm{cri}}=\sup_{u\in M}\cos\theta_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(u),\quad\mbox{where}\ \ \tan\theta_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(u)=\inf_{w\in M\setminus\{u\}}\frac{1-\langle w,u\rangle}{\|P_{u}^{\perp}w\|}.

and

are referred to as the global and local critical radius of the tube about

, respectively.
They are positive when

-closed manifold (e.g.,
[

We generalize the local critical radius

\tan^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(u)=\inf_{w\in M\setminus\{u\}}h(u,w).

Then,

b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{2}=\sup_{u\in M}\bigl[\sigma(u)^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(u)\bigr].

In the formula (

), if

.
Therefore, Theorem

holds as follows.

Proposition 2.5
.

The threshold

in Theorem

From Proposition

, we immediately have

and

) and (

2.3.
Supporting points of
{\sigma}M

In this subsection, we focus on the shape of
{\sigma}M
in (
1.8
).
Define a half space and its boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\displaystyle L_{u}

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle z,u-\nabla\ell(u)\rangle\leq\sigma(u)\},

\displaystyle\partial L_{u}

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle z,u-\nabla\ell(u)\rangle=\sigma(u)\}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\langle\sigma(u)u,u-\nabla\ell(u)\rangle=\sigma(u),\quad\biggl\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{i}}\bigl[\sigma(u)u\bigr],u-\nabla\ell(u)\biggr\rangle=0,

that is,
the hyperplane
\partial L_{u}
is tangent to
{\sigma}M
at
\sigma(u)u
.
Let

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{supp}}=\bigl\{u\in M\mid\partial L_{u}\mbox{ is a supporting hyperplane of }{\sigma}M\mbox{ with }L_{u}\supset{\sigma}M\bigr\}

be the set of supporting points.

Proposition 2.6
.

(i)

u\in M_{\mathrm{supp}}\iff\sigma(u)/\sigma(w)-\langle w,u-\nabla\ell(u)\rangle\geq 0,\,\forall w\in M
.

(ii)

M_{\mathrm{supp}}\supset M_{\mathrm{cri}}
.

(iii)
When
u\notin M_{\mathrm{supp}}
, the tube
(M)_{b/\sigma(\cdot)}
,
b\geq b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(u)
, is degenerated at
u
.

(i) means that, when
u\in M_{\mathrm{supp}}
, the argument of the truncation function
\{\cdot\}_{+}
in (
2.13
) is always nonnegative.
(ii) implies that the non-supporting points do not make contribution to the tube-volume formula (
2.8
).
The inclusion in (ii) is strict in general.

2.4.
Tail probability of the maximum

Let

\bar{G}_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu/2)}\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{\nu/2-1}e^{-t/2}\,\mathrm{d}t

be the upper probability of the chi-square distribution
\chi^{2}_{\nu}
with
\nu

Let

\bar{\Phi}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}t

be the upper probability of the standard normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

Theorem 2.7

(Tube method)
.

Let
G
,
C
,
\mathrm{d}u
, and
b_{\mathrm{cri}}
be defined as in Theorem

.
Then,

(2.14)

\mathbb{P}\bigl(X_{\mathrm{max}}>c\bigr)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{tube}}\bigl(X_{\mathrm{max}}>c\bigr)+O(\bar{G}_{n}(c^{2}/b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{2})),\quad c\to\infty,

\displaystyle\sigma(u)=\sqrt{v^{\top}\Lambda v},\qquad u=\varphi(v,w)=\frac{(\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes I_{\nu})(v\otimes w)}{\sqrt{v^{\top}\Lambda v}},

\displaystyle M=\{\varphi(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{p\nu}\mid\|v\|=\|w\|=1\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{vec}(\Xi)=(\xi_{11},\xi_{21},\ldots,\xi_{p,1},\xi_{12},\ldots,\xi_{p,\nu})^{\top}.

The maximizer of
\sigma(u)
is

M_{0}=\biggl\{\varphi\biggl(\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
0\end{pmatrix},w\biggr)\in\mathbb{R}^{p\nu}\mid v_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{q},\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu},\,\|v_{1}\|=\|w\|=1\biggr\}.

The dimensions of
M
and

are

,

,

,

\bigl(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}q|_{u_{0}}\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq d}=C_{0}+C_{0}G_{0}^{-1}C_{0}

and about the point
u_{0}=u(t)|_{t=0}
,

(4.28)

q=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\bigl(1+t^{\top}(C_{0}+C_{0}G_{0}^{-1}C_{0})t\bigr)+o(\|t\|^{2}),\ \ t=(t^{1},\ldots,t^{d})^{\top},

where
u_{0}=\varphi(0)
.
The volume element at
u_{0}
is
\mathrm{d}u=\det(G_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathrm{d}t_{i}
.
Therefore, according to the standard arguments of Laplace’s method (e.g.,
[
Erd56
]
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{tube}}\bigl(X_{\mathrm{max}}>c\bigr)\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}(d+1)}}\det(I_{d}+C_{0}G_{0}^{-1})\int_{r>c}\frac{\mathrm{d}r\,r^{d}}{\sigma_{0}^{d+1}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{r}{\sigma_{0}}\bigr)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{d/2}\det(C_{0}+C_{0}G_{0}^{-1}C_{0})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\det(G_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\frac{r}{\sigma_{0}}\biggr)^{-d}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\det(I_{d}+C_{0}G_{0}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\det(C_{0}G_{0}^{-1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bar{\Phi}\biggl(\frac{c}{\sigma_{0}}\biggr),

where

C_{0}=(-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\ell(u_{0}))_{1\leq i,j\leq d}=(-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\log\sigma(u_{0}))_{1\leq i,j\leq d}.

Case (ii).
When
\dim M_{0}=d_{0}>0
, the matrix
C+CG^{-1}C
evaluated at
u\in M_{0}
in (
4.28
) is of rank
d-d_{0}
.
By redefining the local coordinates
t=(t^{i})
, we can assume that
C
at
u
is of the form
C=\diag(0,C_{22})
where
C_{22}
is
(d-d_{0})\times(d-d_{0})
.
Accordingly, we write

G=\begin{pmatrix}G_{11}&G_{12}\\
G_{21}&G_{22}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \mbox{and let}\ \ G_{22\cdot 1}=G_{22}-G_{21}G_{11}^{-1}G_{12}.

Then, for
u\approx u_{0}=u(t)|_{t=0}\in M_{0}
,

q(u)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\bigl(1+\bar{t}^{\top}(C_{22}+C_{22}G_{22\cdot 1}^{-1}C_{22})|_{u_{0}}\bar{t}\bigr)+o(\|\bar{t}\|^{2}),\ \ \bar{t}=\bigl(t^{d_{0}+1},\ldots,t^{d}\bigr)^{\top},

\mathrm{d}u=\mathrm{d}u_{0}\prod_{i=d_{0}+1}^{d}\mathrm{d}t^{i}\det(G_{22\cdot 1})^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mathrm{d}u_{0}=\det(G_{11})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\mathrm{d}t^{i},

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{tube}}\bigl(X_{\mathrm{max}}>c\bigr)\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}(d+1)}}\int_{M_{0}}\mathrm{d}u_{0}\det(I_{d-d_{0}}+C_{22}G_{22\cdot 1}^{-1})\int_{r>c}\frac{\mathrm{d}r\,r^{d}}{\sigma_{0}^{d+1}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{r}{\sigma_{0}}\bigr)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}(d-d_{0})}\det(C_{22}+C_{22}G_{22\cdot 1}^{-1}C_{22})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\det(G_{22\cdot 1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\frac{r}{\sigma_{0}}\biggr)^{-(d-d_{0})}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{d_{0}+1}}\int_{M_{0}}\mathrm{d}u_{0}\frac{\det(I_{d-d_{0}}+C_{22}G_{22\cdot 1}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\det(C_{22}G_{22\cdot 1}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\times\bar{G}_{d_{0}+1}\biggl(\frac{c^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{d_{0}+1}}\int_{M_{0}}\mathrm{d}u_{0}\frac{\det(I_{d}+CG^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\tr_{d-d_{0}}(CG^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\times\bar{G}_{d_{0}+1}\biggl(\frac{c^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\biggr).

The asymptotic equivalence on the last line is due to the uniformity of the Laplace approximation on the compact set

.

4.7.
Approximation error of the Bonferroni method

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
2.11
and Proposition
2.12
.
For this purpose, we will provide analogues to Theorems
2.1
and
2.3
when
M
is a finite set.

Proof of Theorem
2.11
.

Recall that

X_{i}=\sigma_{i}\langle\varphi^{(i)},\xi\rangle
,

Y_{i}=\sigma_{i}\langle\varphi^{(i)},\xi/\|\xi\|\rangle
,

\xi\sim\mathcal{N}_{n}(0,I_{n})
,
and
\rho_{ij}=\langle\varphi^{(i)},\varphi^{(j)}\rangle
.
If
b
is sufficiently large, say
b\geq b_{\mathrm{cri}}
, the events
\{Y_{i}>b\}
are mutually exclusive, and

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{K}\}>b\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}>b),

which is (
2.18
).

To identify the threshold
b_{\mathrm{cri}}
, we will follow the proof in Section
4.3
.
Let
i
be fixed.
Let

N_{i}=T_{\varphi^{(i)}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

be the space in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
orthogonal to
\varphi^{(i)}
.
Almost all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is written as

z=r\varphi^{(i)}+sv,\ \ (r,s,v)\in Q_{i},

where

Q_{i}=\{(r,s,v)\mid r>0,\ s\geq 0,\ v\in S(N_{i})\}.

Let

V_{i}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\sigma_{j}\langle\varphi^{(j)},z\rangle<\sigma_{i}\langle\varphi^{(i)},z\rangle,\ z=r\varphi^{(i)}+sv,\ \forall j\neq i\}.

Instead of (
4.11
), we will find the threshold
b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(i)
of
b
satisfying

A_{i}(b)\subset B_{i}
a.s., where

\displaystyle A_{i}(b)=

\displaystyle\,\{(r,s,v)\in Q_{i}\mid\sigma_{i}\langle\varphi^{(i)},z/\|z\|\rangle>b,\,z=r\varphi^{(i)}+sv,\ \forall j\neq i\},

\displaystyle B_{i}=

\displaystyle\,\{(r,s,v)\in Q_{i}\mid z=r\varphi^{(i)}+sv\in V_{i}\}.

The threshold
b_{\mathrm{cri}}
in Theorem
2.11
is given by
\max_{i\in I}b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(i)
.

As in (
4.12
), the set
A_{i}(b)
is

A_{i}(b)=\bigl\{(r,s,v)\in Q_{i}\mid(s/r)^{2}<\sigma_{i}^{2}/b^{2}-1\bigr\}.

Let
I=\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
We divide the index set
I\setminus\{i\}
as
I_{i}^{+}\sqcup I_{i}^{-}
, where

I_{i}^{+}=\bigl\{j\in I\mid\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij}>0\bigr\},\qquad I_{i}^{-}=\bigl\{j\in I\mid\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij}\leq 0\bigr\}\setminus\{i\}.

(i) When
I_{i}^{-}=\emptyset
,

\displaystyle B_{i}=

\displaystyle\biggl\{(r,s,v)\in Q_{i}\mid\frac{s}{r}<\min_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}:\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle>0}\frac{\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij}}{\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle}\biggr\}.

Therefore,
A_{i}(b)\subset B_{i}
iff

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{b^{2}}-1}<

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in S(N_{i})}\min_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}:\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle>0}\frac{\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij}}{\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}\inf_{v\in S(N_{i}):\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle>0}\frac{\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij}}{\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle}=\min_{j:j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij}}{\sqrt{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}}.

Here we used

\sup_{v\in S(N_{i}):\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle>0}\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle=\sqrt{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}.

(ii) When
I_{i}^{-}\neq\emptyset
,

B_{i}=\biggl\{(r,s,v)\in Q_{i}\mid\mbox{``}\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle<0\ \mbox{and}\ \frac{s}{r}>\frac{-\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}+\rho_{ij}}{-\langle\varphi^{(j)},v\rangle}\mbox{''},\ \ \forall j\in I\setminus\{i\}\biggr\}.

A_{i}(b)\subset B_{i}
holds iff
A_{i}(b)=\emptyset
.
That is,

\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{b^{2}}-1\leq 0.

Combining (i) and (ii),
b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(i)
is given by

\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(i)^{2}}-1}=\min_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}\frac{(\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij})_{+}}{\sqrt{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}},

or equivalently,

b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(i)^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{1+\min_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}h(i,j)},

where
h(i,j)
is given in (
2.20
).
The threshold in Theorem
2.11
is
b_{\mathrm{cri}}=\max_{i\in i}b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{\prime}(i)
.
In (
2.18
), the term
\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}>b)=(1/2)\bar{B}_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}(n-1)}(b^{2}/\sigma_{i}^{2})
for
\sigma_{i}<b_{\mathrm{cri}}
is always zero and can be omitted.

Finally, we apply the proof of Theorem
2.7
in Section
4.5
to (
2.18
) to prove (
2.19
) of Theorem
2.11
.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.12
.

We evaluate

(4.29)

\frac{1+h(i,j)}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{(\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij})_{+}^{2}}{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}\biggr).

(i) In the case
\rho_{ij}<1\leq\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}
,

(\ref{(1+h)/s})\geq\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{(1-\rho_{ij})^{2}}{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}\biggr)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\frac{2}{1+\rho_{ij}}.

(ii) In the case
\rho_{ij}<\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}<1
,

\displaystyle(\ref{(1+h)/s})=\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{(\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}-\rho_{ij})^{2}}{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}\biggr)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{(\sigma_{j}/\sigma_{i}-\rho_{ij})^{2}}{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}\biggr)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{(1-\rho_{ij})^{2}}{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}\biggr)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\frac{2}{1+\rho_{ij}}.

(iii) In the case
\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}\leq\rho_{ij}<1

(\ref{(1+h)/s})=\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}\rho_{ij}^{2}}>\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\frac{2}{1+\rho_{ij}}.

In each case,

(\ref{(1+h)/s})\geq\min\biggl\{\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\biggr\}\frac{2}{1+\rho_{ij}},

and hence

\frac{1}{b_{\mathrm{cri}}^{2}}\geq\min_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\frac{2}{1+\rho_{ij}}\geq\biggl(\min_{i}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}\biggr)\biggl(\min_{i<j}\frac{2}{1+\rho_{ij}}\biggr).

∎

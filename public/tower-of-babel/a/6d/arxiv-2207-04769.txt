Gaussian curvature of minimal graphs in M × ℝ M\times\mathbb{R}
David Kalaj

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider minimal graphs in the three-dimensional Riemannian manifold M × ℝ M\times\mathbb{R} . We mainly estimate the Gaussian curvature of such surfaces. We consider the minimal disks and minimal graphs bounded by two Jordan curves in parallel planes. The key to the proofs is the Weierstrass representation of those surfaces via ℘ − \wp- harmonic mappings. We also prove some Schwarz lemma type results and some Heinz type results for harmonic mappings between geodesic disks in Riemannian surfaces.



1.
Introduction

Assume that
\Omega
is a domain in the complex plane. Moreover, assume that
\wp
is a smooth positive metric defined in
\Omega
with bounded Gauss
curvature
\mathcal{K}
where

(1.1)

\mathcal{K}_{\wp}(z)=-\frac{\Delta\log\wp(z)}{\wp^{2}(z)}.

We assume
\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\mathcal{K}_{\wp}(z)|<\infty
and
\wp
has a finite area defined by

\mathcal{A}(\wp)=\int_{\Omega}\wp^{2}(w)dudv,\ \ w=u+iv.

Then we define the Riemannian manifold
M=M_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
M_{0}=(\Omega,\wp)
, where the metric of
M
is

(1.2)

d\hbar^{2}(z)=\wp^{2}(z)|dz|^{2}+dt^{2}.

Let
N_{0}=(D,\mathbf{1})
and
M_{0}=(\Omega,\wp)
be
Riemann surfaces with metrics
\mathbf{1}
and
\wp
, respectively, where
D
and
\Omega
are planar domains. Here
\mathbf{1}
is the Euclidean metric. If
a mapping
f:N_{0}\to M_{0},
is
C^{2}
, then
f
is said to be harmonic (to make difference with Euclidean harmonic we will sometimes say
\wp
-harmonic) if

(1.3)

f_{z\overline{z}}+{(\log\wp^{2})}_{w}\circ f\cdot f_{z}\,f_{\bar{z}}=0,

where
z
and
w
are the local parameters on
N_{0}
and
M_{0}

respectively. Also
f
satisfies (
1.3
) if and only if its Hopf
differential

(1.4)

\mathrm{Hopf}(f)=\wp^{2}\circ ff_{z}\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}

is a
holomorphic quadratic differential on
M
. Let

|\partial f|^{2}:=\wp^{2}(f(z))\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\text{ and }|\bar{\partial}f|^{2}:={\wp^{2}(f(z))}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}\right|^{2}

where
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}

and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}
are standard complex
partial derivatives. The
\wp-
Jacobian is defined by

J(z,f):=|\partial f(z)|^{2}-|\partial\bar{f}(z)|^{2}.

If
f
is sense preserving,
then its
\wp-
Jacobian is positive. For
g:N_{0}\mapsto M_{0}
the
\wp-

Dirichlet energy
is defined by

(1.5)

\mathcal{E}^{\wp}[g]=\int_{N_{0}}(|\partial f|^{2}+|\bar{\partial}f|^{2})dx\wedge dy=\int_{D}\wp^{2}(f(z))(|f_{z}|^{2}+|f_{\bar{z}}|^{2})dxdy.

Assume that the energy integral of
f
is bounded. Then a
stationary point
f
of
\mathcal{E}^{\wp}[g_{t}]
, i.e. the solution of the equation
\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}^{\wp}[g_{t}]|_{t=0}=0
,
g_{t}=g+th
,
t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
, where
h
has a compact support in
D
, is a harmonic
mapping i.e. it satisfies equation (
1.3
). A different variation produces the so-called Noether harmonic maps in Section
3.1
. Recall that
N_{0}=(D,\mathbf{1})
. For the last
definition and some important properties of harmonic maps see

[
10
, Chapter 8]
and
[
9
]
.

It follows from the definition that, if
a
is conformal
and
f
is harmonic, then
f\circ a
is harmonic. Moreover, if
b
is
conformal,
b\circ f
is also harmonic but with respect to
(possibly) an another metric
\wp_{1}
.

The Bochner formula
[
9
, p. 113]
for a harmonic mapping
h
are

\Delta\log|\partial h|^{2}=-\mathcal{K}_{\wp}J(h)

and

\Delta\log|\bar{\partial}h|^{2}=\mathcal{K}_{\wp}J(h).

In this paper, we will prove some curvature estimates of a minimal graph
\Sigma
in
M_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
. Firstly we will derive an analogous divergence formula for its non-parametric parametrization (Propositions
3.2
and (
1.8
)). Further, we consider disk-type minimal graphs and estimate the ”central” curvature (Theorem

). To prove Theorem

we previously prove some Schwarz lemma type estimates of the dilatation of
\wp-
harmonic mappings, which we believe are interesting on their own (Theorem

). Also, we prove a Schwarz lemma type estimate for the
\wp-
distance of the
\wp-
harmonic mapping (Theorem

). We also need some Heinz type estimate proved in the same theorem. The role of
\wp-
harmonic mappings in a minimal surface
\Sigma
in
(\Omega,\wp)\times\mathbb{R}
concerning Weierstrass representation is analogous to the role of Euclidean harmonic mappings in Euclidean minimal surfaces (cf.
[
20
]
).

Moreover, minimizers of the
\wp-
energy of the diffeomorphic mappings between certain doubly connected domains produce minimal surfaces spanning two Jordan curves lying in parallel planes, provided the original annulus has a modulus bigger than the image annulus. This is done in Section
3
, where is made a connections with minimizers of
\wp-
energy and minimal surfaces in Riemannian manifolds.

By using the results in Section

\Sigma
(see below Theorem

for its reformulation ).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
is a double-connected minimal graph bounded by two Jordan curves
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
, lying in two horizontal planes
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
whose distance is
\mathbf{d}

\Pi

|\mathcal{K}_{\Sigma}(\zeta)|\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathbf{d}^{2}}.

Moreover, the tangential plane
T\Sigma_{\zeta}
at a point
\zeta\in\Sigma\setminus(\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2})
is never horizontal nor vertical.

At the end at Subsection

1.1.
Conformal minimal immersion

A mapping
\chi(z)=(f(z),h(z)):\Omega\to(M,\wp)\times\mathbb{R}
is conformal minimal if and only if
(h_{w})^{2}=-(\wp\circ h)^{2}f_{w}\bar{f}_{w}
. Furthermore, the induced metric is given by

ds^{2}=(\wp\circ h)^{2}(|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|)^{2}|dz|^{2}.

Moreover, the unit normal
N
at the tangent plane of the surface
\Sigma=\{\chi(z):z\in\Omega\}
is given by the formula

N=\frac{1}{1+|F|^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{\wp}\Re F,\frac{2}{\wp}\Im F,|F|^{2}-1\right)

where

(1.6)

F^{2}=-\frac{f_{w}}{\bar{f}_{w}}.

For those facts we refer to
[
20
]
. We want to mention that a class of minima surfaces on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R}
has been considered in
[
16
]
and
[
19
]
.

1.2.
Minimal graphs

For a surface
S=\{(u,v,\omega(u,v)):(u,v)\in\Omega\}\subset M=(\Omega,\wp)\times\mathbb{R}
, the
\wp
-area is defined by

(1.7)

\|\omega\|:=\mathrm{Area}_{\wp}(S)=\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\wp^{2}(u,v)+|\nabla\omega(u,v)|^{2}}dudv.

By setting
\left(\frac{d}{dt}\|\omega_{t}\|\right)_{t=0}=0
, where
\omega_{t}
is the outer variation of the form
\omega_{t}=\omega+t\varpi
,
t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
, where
\varpi
has a compact support in
\Omega
, the following non-linear elliptic partial differential equation comes out

(1.8)

\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla\omega}{\sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla\omega(u,v)|^{2}}{\wp^{2}(u,v)}}}\right)=0.

Therefore, the graph

\Sigma=\{(u,v,\omega(u,v)):(u,v)\in\Omega\}\subset M\times\mathbb{R}

is called minimal if
\omega
solves the
the equation (
1.8
). For
\wp\equiv 1
, the equation is the standard minimal surface equation.

We will relate the minimal graphs and conformal minimal immersions in Proposition
3.2
.

2.
Preliminary results from harmonic mappings and related topics

2.1.
Schwarz lemma for harmonic mappings

Let
o\in\Omega
and let
D_{R}(o)=D(o,R)\subset(\Omega,\wp)
be a geodesic disk of radius
R>0
centered at
o
and assume that the cut-locus of
o
is outside of
D_{R}(o)
and
2R\kappa<\pi
, where
\kappa^{2}
is an upper bound of the Gaussian curvature and define

\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o,R)=\{f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega:f(\mathbb{D})\subset D_{R}(o),f\text{ is }\wp-\text{ harmonic }\}.

One of main results in
[
8
]
of Hildebrandt, Jost and Widman for harmonic maps between two-dimensional manifolds reads.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
8
, Theorem 3]

The class
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o,R)
is Hölder continuous on every compact subset of the unit disk, i.e. for every
s<1
there are constants
C>0
and
0<\alpha<1
depending on
s
,
R
and
\kappa
such that

(2.1)

d_{\wp}(f(z),f(w))\leq C|z-w|^{\alpha}

for
|z|,|w|<s
,
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o,R)
.

Further we use the following theorem of the author

Theorem 2.2
.

[
14
]
Let

\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o,R)=\{f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega:f(\mathbb{D})\subset D_{R}(o),\,2R\kappa<\pi,f\text{ is }\wp-\text{ harmonic }\}.

a) Then there is an absolutely continuous homeomorphism

\phi_{\wp}=\phi_{o,R}:[0,1]\to[0,R]

convex in
\log r
, with
\phi^{\prime}(1^{-})>0
and
0<\phi^{\prime}(0^{+})<\infty
, such that

d_{\wp}(f(z),f(0))\leq\phi_{\wp}\left(\left|z\right|\right),

for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o,R)
with
f(0)=o
. Then
{\mathbb{D}}\ni z\to\phi_{\wp}(|z|)
is subharmonic.

b) Assume that the cut-locus of
o
is outside of
D(o,R)
. Then there is an absolutely continuous homeomorphism

\varphi_{\wp}=\varphi_{R}:[0,1]\to[0,2R]

convex in
\log r
, with
\varphi_{\wp}^{\prime}(1^{-})>0
and
0<C^{\prime}_{\wp}=\varphi_{\wp}^{\prime}(0^{+})<\infty
, such that

d_{\wp}(f(z),f(0))\leq\varphi_{\wp}\left(\left|z\right|\right),

for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o,R)
. Moreover

\wp(f(z))|\nabla f(z)|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{\wp}}{1-|z|^{2}},\ z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Here
|\nabla f(z)|:=|f_{z}(z)|+|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa}
be the family of all smooth metrics
\wp
in
\Omega
so that
|\mathcal{K}_{\wp}|\leq\kappa^{2}
, and so that the geodesic disk
D_{1}(o)=D_{\wp}(o,1)\subset\Omega
is disjoint from its cut-locus and
2\kappa<\pi
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o):=\{f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega:f(\mathbb{D})\subset D_{1}(o),\,2\kappa<\pi,f\text{ is }\wp-\text{ harmonic for some }\wp\in\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa}\text{ and }f(0)=o\}.

Define

(2.2)

\phi_{\kappa}(r)=\sup\{\phi_{\wp}(r),\wp\in\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa}\}.

The continuity of
\phi_{\kappa}
follows from Theorem
2.2
, because the constant
C
in (
2.1
) depends on
\kappa
only. To do so, observe that

\phi_{\wp}(r_{j})=\sup_{|z|=r_{j},f\in H_{\wp}}d_{\wp}(f(z),f(0)),\ j=1,2,

and assume without loss of generality that
\phi_{\wp}(r_{2})\geqslant\phi_{\wp}(r_{1})
. Then

\begin{split}|\phi_{\wp}(r_{2})-\phi_{\wp}(r_{1})|&=(\phi_{\wp}(r_{2})-\phi_{\wp}(r_{1}))\\
&=\sup_{f}d_{\wp}(f(r_{1}),o)-\sup_{g}d_{\wp}(g(r_{2}),o)\\
&\leq\sup_{f}\left[d_{\wp}(f(r_{1}),o)-d_{\wp}(f(r_{2}),o)\right]\leq\sup_{f}d_{\wp}(f(r_{1}),f(r_{2}))\\
&\leq C|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Similarly we get

|\phi_{\kappa}(r_{2})-\phi_{\kappa}(r_{1})|\leq C|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{\alpha}.

Further
\phi_{\kappa}
is not constant, because
\phi_{\kappa}(0)=0
.

Then by well-known result for subharmonic functions,
\mathbb{D}\ni z\to\phi_{\kappa}(|z|)
is a subharmonic absolutely continuous function on
r=|z|
and
\phi_{\kappa}(r)=\phi_{o}(r):[0,1]\to[0,1]
is convex in
\log r
, with

(2.3)

\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(1^{-})>0

and

(2.4)

0<\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(0^{+})<\infty,

such that

d_{\wp}(f(z),f(0))\leq\phi_{\kappa}\left(\left|z\right|\right),

for
f\in\mathcal{H}(o)
with
o=f(0)
and
\wp\in\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa}
.

Notice that
\phi_{\kappa}
is a nonconstant function. Since
\phi_{\kappa}
is an
increasing convex function of
\log r
, it follows that for
r>s

r\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(r^{+})\geqslant r\phi^{\prime}(r^{-})\geqslant s\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(s^{+})\geqslant s\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(s^{-})\geqslant 0.

Since it is non-constant, it satisfies in
particular that
\phi^{\prime}(1^{-})>0
which proves (
2.3
). The relation (
2.4
) can be proved in the same way as the analogous relation for
\phi_{\wp}
proved in
[
14
]
.

Moreover, from Schwarz lemma for harmonic Euclidean harmonic functions (
[
3
, p. 76]
) we have
\phi_{k}(r)\geqslant\phi_{0}(r)=\frac{4}{\pi}\arctan r
. Since
\phi_{k}(1)=1
, we get

\frac{1-\phi_{k}(r)}{1-r}\leq\frac{1-\frac{4}{\pi}\arctan r}{1-r}.

By letting
r\to 1
we get

(2.5)

\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(1^{-})\leq\frac{2}{\pi}.

So we have proved the first part of the following Schwarz lemma type estimate and Heinz type estimate for harmonic mappings.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
w=f(z)
be a harmonic mapping of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
into the geodesic disk
D(o,1)\subset(\mathcal{M},\wp)
of the Reimannian surface
(\mathcal{M},\wp)
with

-\kappa^{2}\leq\mathcal{K}_{\wp}\leq\kappa^{2}
and assume that
2\kappa<\pi
and that the cut-locus of
o
is outside of
D(o,1)
. Let
\varrho
be the distance function from the

. Define
\rho(z)=\varrho(f(z))
.
Assume also that
o=f(0)
.

a) Then we have the Schwarz lemma type inequality

(2.6)

|\rho(z)|\leq\phi_{\kappa}(|z|),

where
\phi_{\kappa}:[0,1]\xrightarrow{{}_{\!\!\textnormal{onto}\!\!}}[0,1]
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism with
\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(1^{-})<2/\pi
.
Moreover,

|\nabla\rho(0)|\leq C_{\kappa},

where
C_{\kappa}=\phi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(0)\geqslant\frac{4}{\pi}
and
\phi_{\kappa}
depend on
\kappa
only.

a^{\prime}
) If the cut-locus of
o
is outside of
D(o,1)
we have the inequality

(2.7)

d_{\wp}(f(z),f(0))|\leq\varphi_{\kappa}(|z|),\ z\in{\mathbb{D}}

where
\varphi_{\kappa}:[0,1]\xrightarrow{{}_{\!\!\textnormal{onto}\!\!}}[0,2]
is a homeomorphism locally Lipschitz in
[0,1)
.
Moreover,

(2.8)

\wp(f(z))|\nabla f(z)|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{\kappa}}{1-|z|^{2}},\ z\in{\mathbb{D}}

where
C^{\prime}_{\kappa}=\varphi_{\kappa}^{\prime}(0)<\infty
and
\varphi_{\kappa}
depend on
\kappa
only.

b) If in addition the function
f:\mathbb{D}\xrightarrow{{}_{\!\!\textnormal{onto}\!\!}}D(o,1)
is a diffeomorphism with
f(0)=o
, and
\wp
has a non-negative Gaussian curvature, then

(2.9)

|\partial f(z)|\geqslant c_{\kappa}>0,\ \ z\in\mathbb{D}.

The function
\kappa\to c_{\kappa}
is non-increasing.

Remark 2.4
.

a)
Instead of

, we can assume that

In this case we get from (

A similar remark can be stated for

.

To prove

\wp(f(e^{it}))|\partial_{r}f(re^{it})|_{r=1}\geqslant 2\tilde{c}_{\kappa}>0,

(2.12)

\max_{|z|\leq 1}\log\frac{1}{|\partial f|}=\max_{|z|=1}\log\frac{1}{|\partial f|}.

So

\min_{|z|\leq 1}{|\partial f|}=\min_{|z|=1}{|\partial f|}\geqslant\tilde{c}_{\kappa}.

If

f_{n}(z)=\Psi^{-1}_{n}(f(\Phi_{n}(z)))
, where

,

and

. Then

Thus

|\partial f_{n}(z)|\geqslant\tilde{c}_{\kappa},\ z\in\mathbb{D}

and therefore

because

and
\Phi_{n}
converges to identity in compacts. Now we define

c_{\kappa}=\inf\{|\partial f(z)|,z\in{\mathbb{D}},f\in\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}(o)\}.

Then

.
Moreover, in view of (

2.2.
Schwarz lemma for dilatation of harmonic mappings

Proposition 2.5
.

[

, p. 10-11]
Assume that

is harmonic with respect to a non-vanishing

. Then the functions
|\partial h|
and
|\bar{\partial}h|
are identically zero or they have the isolated zeros with well defined orders. More precisely
|\partial h|=|z-z_{0}|^{n}u(z)
and
|\bar{\partial}h|=|z-z_{0}|^{m}v(z)

and

.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
M=(\Omega,\wp)
be a non-negatively curved Riemannian surface.
Assume that

harmonic mapping that preserves the orientation. Let

|m(z)|\leq|z|^{2},

|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|\leq|z|^{2}|f_{z}(z)|.

Further if
\mu=\sqrt{m}

(2.14)

|\mu(z)|\leq|z|,\text{ and }|\nabla\mu(0)|\leq 1.

If
\mu(0)\neq 0
,
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to D_{1}(o)

for a constant
C_{\kappa}^{\ast}\geqslant 1
depending on
\kappa
.

Proof.

Prove first (
2.15
).
Let
r\in(0,1)
,
P_{r}(z)=\frac{r^{2}-|z|^{2}}{|z-r|^{2}}
. By using the well-known formula we have

(2.16)

\mu(z)=h_{r}(z)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{r{\mathbb{D}}}\log\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\frac{1}{r^{2}}z\overline{w}}\right|\Delta\mu(w)dudv,\ \ w=u+iv,

where
h_{r}:r{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
is the harmonic function defined by

h_{r}(z)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}P_{r}(ze^{it})\mu[re^{it}]\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Further

\Delta\mu=K_{\wp}\wp^{2}(f(z))(|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}|^{2}).

From Schwarz lemma for Euclidean harmonic functions (
[
3
, p. 76]
) we obtain that
|\nabla h_{r}(0)|\leq\frac{4}{r\pi}
.

On the other hand

\left|\nabla_{z}\log\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\frac{1}{r^{2}}z\overline{w}}\right|\right|_{z=0}=\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}-|w|^{2}}}{r^{2}|w|}.

Thus by (
2.8
) we have

|\nabla\mu(0)|\leq\frac{4}{r\pi}+\kappa^{2}(C^{\prime}_{\kappa})^{2}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{r{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}-|w|^{2}}}{r^{2}|w|}\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})^{2}}dudv.

Then for
r=1/2
we obtain

(2.17)

|\nabla\mu(0)|\leq C^{*}_{\kappa}:=\frac{8}{\pi}+\pi\kappa^{2}(C^{\prime}_{\kappa})^{2}(5\coth^{-1}(2)-2).

Now we improve the constant for the case
\mu(0)=0
.
If
\mathcal{K}_{\wp}\geqslant 0
, and
f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
, then by Bochner formula we have

\Delta\log\mu(z)=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\wp}}{2}J\geqslant 0.

Since

\mu=\sqrt{\frac{|f_{\bar{z}}|}{|f_{z}|}}=\frac{|\Phi(z)|}{\wp(f(z))|f_{z}|},

on account of Proposition
2.5
, it follows that also
\mu
has well-defined order of the zero. So
\log\left(\mu(z)/|z|\right)
is subharmonic.

Assume now that
\mu
is continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. By the maximum principle

\log(\mu(z)/|z|)\leq 0.

Thus

\mu(z)/|z|\leq 1.

Therefore,

|\mu(z)|\leq|z|.

Further

(2.18)

\mu(u,v)=\mu(0,0)+\partial_{x}\mu(0,0)u+\partial_{y}\mu(0,0)+o(\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}}).

If
\partial_{x}\mu(0,0)=\partial_{y}\mu(0,0)=0
we have nothing to prove. If the last relation is not true then we choose
u=t\partial_{x}\mu(0,0)/|\nabla\mu(0,0)|
and
v=t\partial_{y}\mu(0,0)/|\nabla\mu(0,0)|.

By inserting
u
and
v
in (
2.18
), dividing by
t
and letting

we get

|\nabla\mu(0,0)|\leq 1.

If
\mu(z)/|z|
is only upper-semicontinuous in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then by
[
6
, Theorem 1.4]
, there exists a decreasing sequence of mappings

continuous in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
so that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(z)=\mu(z)/|z|.

Thus the proof can be reduced to the proof when
\mu
is continuous up to the boundary.

∎

3.
Minimizers and minimal surfaces in Riemannian manifold

Assume that
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
are double connected domains and that
\mathscr{W}_{\wp}^{1,2}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})

and
\mathbb{Y}

Let
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\wp}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})

{\mathcal{H}}^{\wp}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})

3.1.
Stationary mappings and Noether harmonic maps

We call a mapping

stationary
if

(3.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}{\mathcal{E}^{\wp}}[h\circ\phi_{t}^{-1}]=0

for every family of diffeomorphisms

which depend smoothly on the parameter

and satisfy

\phi_{0}=\id
. The latter mean that the mapping
\mathbb{X}\times[0,\epsilon_{0}]\ni(z,t)\to\phi_{t}(z)\in\mathbb{X}
is a smooth mapping for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
.
We now have.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
11
]

Let
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{A}(r,R)
be a circular annulus,

, and
assume that
\mathbb{Y}
is a doubly connected domain. If

where

is a constant. Moreover,
\mathbf{c}\geqslant 0
if

and
\mathbf{c}<0
if

. Further every stationary mapping that is a diffeomorphism is a minimizer.

Notice first that a change of variables

in (

)
yields

(3.3)

{\mathcal{E}^{\wp}}[f]=2\int_{\mathbb{X}}\wp^{2}(f(z))J_{f}(z)\,dz+4\int_{\mathbb{X}}\wp^{2}(f(z))\lvert f_{\bar{z}}\rvert^{2}dz\geqslant 2\mathcal{A}(\wp)

where

is the Jacobian determinant and

is
the area of
\mathbb{Y}
and

is the area element
w.r. to Lebesgue measure on the complex plane. A conformal mapping
of

for the minimizer of (

harmonic mappings comes to the stage.

The following lemma makes a relation with minimizers and minimal surfaces in manifold

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that

is a diffeomorphism so that

\wp^{2}(f(z))f_{z}\bar{f}_{z}=\Phi^{2}(z),

where

\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla\omega}{\sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla\omega(u,v)|^{2}}{\wp^{2}(u,v)}}}\right)=0.

In other words,

4.1.
Gaussian curvature

Assume that
\Sigma=\{(f(z),h(z)):z\in\Omega\}
is a minimal surface in the manifold

From a corresponding formula in
[
5
]

ds^{2}_{\Sigma}=\wp(f(z))^{2}(|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|)^{2}|dz|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(|\mu|+|\mu|^{-1}\right)^{2}|\eta|^{2}|dz|^{2}=\lambda|dz|^{2},

where

\eta=\pm 2\rho(f(z))\sqrt{f_{z}\bar{f}_{z}}

and

\mu=\sqrt{\left|\frac{\bar{f}_{z}}{f_{z}}\right|}.

Let

m=\mu^{2}.

Bochner formula implies that

\Delta\log m=\mathcal{K}_{\wp}J_{f},

where

J(f)=|\partial_{\wp}f|^{2}-|\bar{\partial}_{\wp}f|^{2}=\wp(f(z))^{2}(|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}|^{2}).

Then the Gaussian curvature of
\Sigma
at
\zeta=\chi(z)=(f(z),h(z))
can be expressed as

\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{\Sigma}(\zeta)=-\frac{\vartriangle\log\lambda(z)}{2\lambda(z)}.

Thus

(4.5)

\mathcal{K}=\frac{-\Delta\log\lambda}{2\lambda}=\frac{-2A+X\mu^{2}\left(1-\mu^{4}\right)}{2\lambda\mu^{2}(1+\mu^{2})^{2}}

where

A=|\nabla m|^{2}

and

X=2\Delta\log m=2\mathcal{K}_{\wp}J(f).

So

\mathcal{K}=\frac{-A}{\lambda\mu^{2}(1+\mu^{2})^{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\wp}J(f)\left(1-\mu^{4}\right)}{\lambda(1+\mu^{2})^{2}}.

Observe that, if
\wp
has negative Gaussian curvature, then
\Sigma
is also negatively curved minimal surface.
Furthermore

0\leq\frac{J(f)\left(1-\mu^{4}\right)}{\lambda(1+\mu^{2})^{2}}=\frac{\left(1-\mu^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1+\mu^{2}\right)^{3}}\leq 1.

Further

\frac{-A}{\lambda\mu^{2}(1+\mu^{2})^{2}}=\frac{-B}{\wp^{2}(f(z))(|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|)^{2}\left(1+\mu^{2}\right)^{4}},

where

(4.6)

B=4|\nabla\mu|^{2}.

Thus we get

(4.7)

|\mathcal{K}|\leq\max\left\{\left|\frac{-B}{\wp^{2}(f(z))(|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|)^{2}}\right|,\left|\frac{-B}{\wp^{2}(f(z))(|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|)^{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{\wp}\right|\right\}.

By
[
20
, Proposition 4]
, see (
1.1
) above, we know that a conformal parametrization
\chi(z)=(f(z),h(z))
of a minimal surface
\Sigma\subset M\times\mathbb{R}
defines the unit normal at a given point. Moreover, if
\bar{\partial}f(0)=0
, then the unit normal
N
at a point
\chi(0)=o
is
N=(0,0,1)
and it defines a horizontal tangential plane. Now we formulate the following Finn-Osserman type result (
[

]
):

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
D_{\wp}(o,R)\subset M_{0}=(\Omega,\wp)

4.2.
The curvature estimate of minimal graphs between parallel planes

In this case, as it is discussed in Propositions
4.1
and
4.3
we have

\eta=\pm 2\rho(f(z))\sqrt{f_{z}\bar{f}_{z}}=\pm 2\imath\sqrt{\frac{\mathbf{c}}{z^{2}}}.

For our surface

\mathcal{K}=\frac{-A|z|^{2}}{|c|\left(1+\mu^{2}\right)^{4}}+\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\wp}J(f)\left(1-\mu^{4}\right)}{2\lambda(1+\mu^{2})^{2}}.

If
\wp\equiv 1
, then
\mathcal{K}_{\wp}=0
and

\frac{-\Delta\log\lambda}{\lambda}=\frac{-A}{2\lambda\mu^{2}(1+\mu^{2})^{2}}.

In this case
m=|\mathbf{q}(z)|^{2}
, where
\mathbf{q}
is a holomorphic function so that
|\mathbf{q}(z)|<1
, and so

|\nabla m|^{2}=4|\mathbf{q}(z)|^{2}\cdot|\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}.

Moreover,

\lambda=(1+|\mathbf{q}|^{2})^{2}|\mathbf{p}|^{2}.

In this case we get

\mathcal{K}=-\frac{4|\mathbf{q}^{\prime}|^{2}}{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}(1+|\mathbf{q}|^{2})^{4}}.

We also have for
\omega=\mathbf{q}^{2}
, (
[
3
, p. 184]
)

(4.8)

\mathcal{K}=-\frac{|\omega^{\prime}|^{2}}{|f_{z}\bar{f}_{z}|(1+|\omega|)^{4}}.

Now we have the make the following equivalent formulation of Theorem
1.1

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
is a double-connected minimal graph bounded by two Jordan curves in parallel planes
z=\pm\mathbf{d}/2.
Then for every point
\zeta\in\Sigma\bigcap\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\}
we have

|\mathcal{K}(\zeta)|\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathbf{d}^{2}}.

Moreover, the tangential plane
T\Sigma_{\zeta}
at a point
\zeta\in\Sigma\setminus\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{-\mathbf{d}/2,\mathbf{d/2}\}
is never horizontal nor vertical.

Proof.

Assume that
\Sigma
is defined over a double connected domain
\Omega
and assume that
\chi(z)=(f(z),h(z)):\mathbb{A}(1/R,R)\to\Sigma
is its conformal minimal parameterisation. By (
4.4
)
h
is given by

(4.9)

h(z)=\frac{\mathbf{d}}{\log\left[R^{2}\right]}\log|z|.

Let
\omega(z)=(\mathbf{q}(z))^{2}
.
From (
4.8
) we have

(4.10)

\mathcal{K}(\chi(z)))=-\frac{|\omega^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{|f_{z}\bar{f}_{z}|(1+|\omega|)^{4}}=-\frac{|\omega^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|z|^{2}}{|\mathbf{c}|(1+|\omega|)^{4}}.

Schwarz-Pick inequality for analytic functions of the annulus
\mathbb{A}(1/R,R)
into the unit disk,
R>1
can be formulated as (
[
2
]
)

(4.11)

\left|\omega^{\prime}(z)\right|<\frac{\pi\left(1-\left|\omega(z)\right|^{2}\right)\sec\left[\frac{\pi\log|z|}{2\log R}\right]}{4|z|\log R}.

(Notice that: For
\omega(z)=z^{2}/R^{2}
the above inequality is sharp for
s=|z|\to R
. For
\omega(z)=1/(R^{2}z^{2})
it is sharp for
s=|z|\to 1/R
.)

So

|\mathcal{K}|\leq\frac{|\omega^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|z|^{2}}{|\mathbf{c}|(1+|\omega|)^{4}}\leq\left(\pi\frac{(1-|\omega(z)|)}{1+|\omega(z)|}\frac{\sec\left[\frac{\pi\log|z|}{2\log R}\right]}{4\log R}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{c}|}.

So for every
\zeta\in\Sigma
we have

|\mathcal{K}(\zeta)|\leq\frac{|\omega^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|z|^{2}}{|\mathbf{c}|(1+|\omega|)^{4}}\leq\left(\pi\frac{\sec\left[\frac{\pi\log|z|}{2\log R}\right]}{4\log R}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{c}|}.

So for
|z|=1
, on account of (
4.9
), we have that
\zeta=\chi(z)\in\Sigma\bigcap\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\}
. For such

(4.12)

|\mathcal{K}(\zeta)|\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{4\log R}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{c}|}.

From (
4.12
) and (
4.1
) we obtain

\begin{split}|\mathcal{K}(\zeta)|&\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{4\log R}\right)^{2}\frac{4\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\mathbf{d}^{2}}\\
&=\left(\frac{\pi}{2\log R^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{4\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\mathbf{d}^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathbf{d}^{2}}.\end{split}

To prove the last assertion of the theorem, observe that
\omega^{\prime}(z)=2\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(z)\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(z)
.
Then the unit normal at
\zeta=\chi(z)\in\Sigma
, in view of
[

Further

is horizontal if and only if

∎

4.3.
The curvature of rotationally symmetric minimal surface

We continue the notation of Example (
3.5
).
Assume that

is a function defined by

s=\exp\int_{1}^{p(s)}\frac{\rho(y)}{\sqrt{4\mathbf{c}+y^{2}\rho^{2}(y)}}dy

Define for
\mathbf{c}<0

\chi(z)=(p(s)\cos\theta,p(s)\sin\theta,2\sqrt{-\mathbf{c}}\log\frac{1}{s})

and for
\mathbf{c}>0

\chi(z)=(p(s)\cos\theta,p(s)\sin\theta,2\sqrt{\mathbf{c}}\theta),

where

\chi(z)

. Observe that for
\rho\equiv 1
, i.e. for
\wp-
being an Euclidean metric, the surface

\mathbf{c}<0

\mathbf{c}>0

If we set

,

\rho-
harmonic mapping between
\mathbb{A}(r,1)

\mathbb{A}(R,1).

p(s)^{2}-(sp^{\prime}(s))^{2}=-\frac{4\mathbf{c}}{\rho(p(s))^{2}},

we get for

\mu_{f}=\frac{|f_{\bar{z}}|}{|f_{z}|}=\frac{p(s)-sp^{\prime}(s)}{p(s)+p^{\prime}(s)}.

By having in mind the equation

|\eta|=\frac{|4\mathbf{c}|}{|z|^{2}},

and using the formula (
4.5

we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{c}}(\chi(z))&=-\frac{\sign(\mathbf{c})}{t^{4}\rho^{6}}\bigg(8\mathbf{c}t^{2}\rho^{\prime 2}+\rho^{2}\left(4\mathbf{c}+t^{4}\rho^{\prime 2}\right)\\
&+4\mathbf{c}t\rho\left(\rho^{\prime}-t\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)-t^{3}\rho^{3}\left(\rho^{\prime}+t\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)\bigg)\end{split}

For

\mathcal{K}=-\frac{4\mathbf{c}\left(\rho+t\rho^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{\left(4\mathbf{c}\rho+t^{2}\rho^{3}\right)^{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{\wp}\frac{t^{2}\rho^{2}}{4\mathbf{c}+t^{2}\rho^{2}}.

For

\mathcal{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{4\mathbf{c}}{t^{4}},&\hbox{if $\mathbf{c}<0$;}\\
-\frac{4\mathbf{c}}{\left(4\mathbf{c}+t^{2}\right)^{2}},&\hbox{if $\mathbf{c}>0$.}\end{array}\right.

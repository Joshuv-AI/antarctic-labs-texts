One-shot quantum state redistribution and quantum Markov chains This paper was presented at the sixteenth conference on the Theory of Quantum Computation, Communication, and Cryptography (TQC 2021) and 2021 IEEE International Symposium on Information Theory (ISIT 2021). An extended abstract of it is published in the proceedings of ISIT 2021 [ 3 ] . This work was also part of S.B.’s PhD thesis.
Anurag Anshu, Shima Bab Hadiashar, Rahul Jain, Ashwin Nayak, Dave Touchette

Abstract
We revisit the task of quantum state redistribution in the one-shot setting, and design a protocol for this task with communication cost in terms of a measure of distance from quantum Markov chains. More precisely, the distance is defined in terms of quantum max-relative entropy and quantum hypothesis testing entropy. Our result is the first to operationally connect quantum state redistribution and quantum Markov chains, and can be interpreted as an operational interpretation for a possible one-shot analogue of quantum conditional mutual information. The communication cost of our protocol is lower than all previously known ones and asymptotically achieves the well-known rate of quantum conditional mutual information. Thus, our work takes a step towards an optimal characterization of the resources required for one-shot quantum state redistribution, an important open problem in quantum Shannon theory.

The protocol we design (for redistributing an arbitrary state) is a more sophisticated version of the above protocol. The key technique underlying this protocol is a reduction procedure using embezzling quantum states, that allows us to use a protocol due to Anshu, Jain, and Warsi
[

\psi^{RBC}

\sigma^{RBC}
such that

\sigma^{RBC}
of
\psi^{RB}

\sigma^{RBC}
is in

1.3
Organization of the paper

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

2.1
, we review basic concepts and results from quantum information theory. In Section
2.2

In Section
2.3
, we define the task of quantum state redistribution formally, and present two key primitives, namely Coherent Rejection Sampling (implicit in the Convex-Split Lemma) and Position-Based Decoding. We then describe how these are used by Anshu, Jain, and Warsi
[
7
]
to design a one-shot protocol for quantum state redistribution.

Next we present some of the other components of the new protocol we develop. In Section
2.4
, we introduce a technique for decoupling classical-quantum states via embezzlement
[
40
]
and a flattening technique designed in ref.
[
4
]
.

We develop the new protocol for one-shot quantum state redistribution in Section
3
. We first explain the intuition behind the protocol in detail by considering the example of the

We conclude with a summary of the results and an outlook in Section

Throughout Sections

2.2
Quantum Markov states

A tripartite quantum state
\sigma^{RBC}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{RBC})
is
called a
quantum Markov state
of the form
R\!-\!B\!-\!C
if there exists a quantum
operation
\Lambda:{\mathsf{L}}\!\left({\mathcal{H}}^{B}\right)\rightarrow{\mathsf{L}}\!\left({\mathcal{H}}^{BC}\right)

such that
({\mathbb{1}}\otimes\Lambda)(\sigma^{RB})=\sigma^{RBC}
. This is equivalent to the condition that
\rI(R:C\,|\,B)_{\sigma}=~0
, and is the quantum analogue of the notion of
Markov chains
for classical registers.
Classical registers
YXM
form a
Markov chain
in this order (denoted as
Y\!-\!X\!-\!M
) if registers
Y
and
M
are independent given
X
.
Hayden, Josza, Petz, and Winter
[
20
]
showed that an analogous property holds for quantum Markov states.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
20
]
)
.

A state
\sigma^{RBC}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{R}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{C})
is a quantum Markov state of the form
R\!-\!B\!-\!C
if and only if there is a decomposition of the space
{\mathcal{H}}^{B}
into a direct sum of tensor products as

{\mathcal{H}}^{B}\quad=\quad\bigoplus_{j}{\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{R}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{C}}\kern 5.0pt,

(2.4)

such that

\sigma^{RBC}\quad=\quad\bigoplus_{j}p(j)\,\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\kern 5.0pt,

(2.5)

where
\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\in{\mathsf{D}}\left({\mathcal{H}}^{R}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{R}}\right)
,
\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\in{\mathsf{D}}\left({\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{C}\right)
and
p
is a probability distribution over the direct summands.

For a state
\psi^{RBC}
, we say that
\sigma^{RBC}
is a
Markov extension
of
\psi^{RB}
if
\sigma^{RB}=\psi^{RB}
and
\sigma^{RBC}
is a Markov state. We denote the set of all Markov extensions of
\psi^{RB}
by
{\mathsf{QMC}}_{R-B-C}^{\psi}
. Note that
{\mathsf{QMC}}_{R-B-C}^{\psi}
is non-empty, as it contains the state
\sigma^{RBC}\coloneqq\psi^{RB}\otimes\psi^{C}
. The following lemma relates the quantum conditional mutual information to quantum Markov extensions. The proof of this lemma is implicit in ref.
[
12
, Lemma 1]
, but we provide a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2.9

(Implicit in
[
12
]
, Lemma 1)
.

For any tripartite quantum state
\psi^{RBC}
, and any quantum Markov extension
\sigma^{RBC}\in{\mathsf{QMC}}_{R-B-C}^{\psi}
, it holds that

\rI(R:C\,|\,B)_{\psi}\quad=\quad\rD\left(\psi^{RBC}\|\sigma^{RBC}\right)-\rD\left(\psi^{BC}\|\sigma^{BC}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

Proof:

For sake of clarity, in this proof, we suppress tensor products with the identity in expressions involving sums or products of quantum states over different sequences of registers. For example, we write
\omega^{XY}+\tau^{YZ}
to represent the sum
\omega^{XY}\otimes{\mathbb{1}}^{Z}+{\mathbb{1}}^{X}\otimes\tau^{YZ}
, and
\omega^{XY}\tau^{YZ}
to represent the product
\big(\omega^{XY}\otimes{\mathbb{1}}^{Z}\big)\big({\mathbb{1}}^{X}\otimes\tau^{YZ}\big)
. All the expressions involving entropy and mutual information are with respect to the state
\psi
.

Consider any quantum Markov chain
\sigma^{RBC}
satisfying
\sigma^{RB}=\psi^{RB}
. From Eq. (
2.5
), we have

\log\sigma^{RBC}\quad=\quad\bigoplus_{j}\left(\log\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\right)+\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\right)\kern 5.0pt,

and similarly,

\log\sigma^{BC}\quad=\quad\bigoplus_{j}\left(\log\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{R}}\right)+\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

Thus, we can evaluate

D(
ψ
^RBC∥
σ
^RBC)-D(
ψ
^BC∥
σ
^BC)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\psi^{RBC}\right)-\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\sigma^{RBC}\right)-\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\psi^{BC}\right)+\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\sigma^{BC}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rS(BC)-\rS(RBC)-\sum_{j}\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\right)\right)-\sum_{j}\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\>\sum_{j}\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{R}}\right)\right)+\sum_{j}\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

Since
\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\right)=\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\right)
, the above equation can be simplified to obtain

D(
ψ
^RBC∥
σ
^RBC)-D(
ψ
^BC∥
σ
^BC)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rS(BC)-\rS(RBC)-\sum_{j}\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\right)\right)+\sum_{j}\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{R}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rS(BC)-\rS(RBC)-\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\left(\bigoplus_{j}p(j)\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\right)\right)+\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\left(\bigoplus_{j}p(j)\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{R}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rS(BC)-\rS(RBC)-\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\bigoplus_{j}\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\>\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\bigoplus_{j}\left(p(j)\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{R}}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}}\right)\right)\kern 5.0pt,

where the last equality above follows by noting that

\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}}\right)\quad=\quad\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

Since
\psi^{RB}=\sigma^{RB}
, we get that

\displaystyle\rD\left(\psi^{RBC}\|\sigma^{RBC}\right)-\rD\left(\psi^{BC}\|\sigma^{BC}\right)\quad

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rS(BC)-\rS(RBC)-\trace\left(\psi^{RBC}\log\sigma^{RB}\right)+\trace\left(\psi^{BC}\log\sigma^{B}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rS(BC)-\rS(RBC)-\trace\left(\psi^{RB}\log\psi^{RB}\right)+\trace\left(\psi^{B}\log\psi^{B}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rS(BC)-\rS(RBC)+\rS(RB)-\rS(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rI\left(R:C\,|\,B\right)\kern 5.0pt.

This completes the proof.

For a Markov extension
\sigma\in{\mathsf{QMC}}_{R-B-C}^{\psi}
, let
\Pi_{j}^{\sigma}
be the orthogonal projection operator onto the
j
-th subspace of the register

given by the decomposition corresponding to the Markov state
\sigma
as described above. In other words,

is the projection onto the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{R}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{C}}
in Eq. (
2.4
). For a quantum
state
\psi^{RBC}
, we define

{\mathsf{ME}}_{R-B-C}^{\epsilon,\psi}\quad\coloneqq\quad\left\{\left.\sigma\in{\mathsf{QMC}}_{R-B-C}^{\psi}~\right|~\text{ for all }j,~\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\in{\mathsf{B}}^{\epsilon}\left(\trace_{B_{j}^{R}}\left[(\Pi_{j}^{\sigma}\otimes{\mathbb{1}})\psi^{BC}(\Pi_{j}^{\sigma}\otimes{\mathbb{1}})\right]\right)\right\}\kern 5.0pt.

(2.6)

Informally, this is the subset of Markov extensions

of

such that the restrictions of
\sigma
and
\psi
to the
j
-th subspace in the decomposition of

agree well on the registers

. Again, the state
\sigma^{RBC}\coloneqq\psi^{RB}\otimes\psi^{C}
belongs to

2.3
Quantum state redistribution

Consider a pure state
\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}
shared between
Ref (
R
), Alice (
AC
) and Bob (
B
). In an
\epsilon
-error

quantum state redistribution
protocol, Alice and Bob share an
entangled state
\left|\theta\right\rangle^{E_{A}E_{B}}
, where register
E_{A}
is with Alice and register
E_{B}
with Bob. Alice applies an encoding
operation
{\mathcal{E}}:{\mathsf{L}}({\mathcal{H}}^{ACE_{A}})\rightarrow{\mathsf{L}}({\mathcal{H}}^{AQ})
, and sends the
register
Q
to Bob.
Then, Bob applies a decoding operation
{\mathcal{D}}:{\mathsf{L}}({\mathcal{H}}^{QBE_{B}})\rightarrow{\mathsf{L}}({\mathcal{H}}^{BC})
. The output of the protocol is the state
\phi^{RABC}
with the property that
\rP(\psi^{RABC},\phi^{RABC})\leq\epsilon
. The communication cost of the protocol is
\log\left|Q\right|
.

To derive the bound in Theorem
1.1
, we use a protocol due to Anshu, Jain, and Warsi
[
7
]
, which we call the AJW protocol in the sequel.
The AJW protocol is based on the
Convex-Split Lemma
introduced by Anshu, Devabathini, and Jain
[
2
]
,
and the technique of
Position-Based Decoding
introduced by Anshu, Jain, and Warsi
[
6
]
.

Let
n
be an integer,
\rho^{AB}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{AB})
and
\sigma^{B}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{B})
. Consider the quantum state
\tau^{AB_{1}\ldots B_{n}}
derived by adding
n-1
independent copies of
\sigma^{B}
in tensor product with
\rho^{AB}
and swapping the
(i-1)
-th copy of
\sigma^{B}
with
\rho^{B}
for uniformly random
i\in[n-1]
. The convex-split lemma states that the state
\tau^{AB_{1}\ldots B_{n}}
is almost indistinguishable from the product state
\rho^{A}\otimes(\sigma^{B})^{\otimes n}
, provided that
n
is large enough.

Lemma 2.10

(Convex-Split Lemma
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\rho^{AB}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{AB})
and
\sigma^{B}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{B})
be
quantum states with
{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}(\rho^{AB}\|\rho^{A}\otimes\sigma^{B})=k
for
some finite number
k
. Let
\delta>0
and
n\coloneqq{\left\lceil\tfrac{2^{k}}{\delta}\right\rceil}
.
Define the following states on
n+1
registers
A,B_{1},B_{2},\dotsc,B_{n}
:

\displaystyle\tau^{AB_{1}B_{2}\dotsb B_{n}}\quad

\displaystyle\coloneqq\quad\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho^{AB_{j}}\otimes\sigma^{B_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma^{B_{j-1}}\otimes\sigma^{B_{j+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma^{B_{n}}\kern 5.0pt,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\tau}}^{AB_{1}B_{2}\dotsb B_{n}}\quad

\displaystyle\coloneqq\quad\rho^{A}\otimes\sigma^{B_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\sigma^{B_{n}}\kern 5.0pt,

where for all
i\in[n]
, we have
\left|B_{i}\right|=\left|B\right|
,
\rho^{AB_{i}}=\rho^{AB}
, and
\sigma^{B_{i}}=\sigma^{B}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\rP\left(\tau^{AB_{1}\dotsb B_{n}},\;{\widetilde{\tau}}^{AB_{1}\dotsb B_{n}}\right)\quad\leq\quad\sqrt{\delta}\kern 5.0pt.

We may think of the Convex-Split Lemma as providing a sufficient condition under which the correlations between registers
A
and
B
in
\rho
can be “hidden” by taking a certain convex combination of quantum states. A dual problem is to find conditions sufficient for
identifying
the location of desired correlations in a convex combination. This task is achievable via the position-based decoding technique, which in turn uses quantum hypothesis testing.

Lemma 2.11

(Position-Based Decoding
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\epsilon>0
, and
\rho^{AB}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{AB})
and
\sigma^{B}\in{\mathsf{D}}({\mathcal{H}}^{B})
be quantum states such that
\support(\rho^{B})\subseteq\support(\sigma^{B})
. Let
n\coloneqq{\left\lceil\epsilon\,2^{{\mathrm{D}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\epsilon}(\rho^{AB}\|\rho^{A}\otimes\sigma^{B})}\right\rceil}
, and for every
j\in[n]
,

\tau_{j}^{AB_{1}\ldots B_{n}}\quad\coloneqq\quad\rho^{AB_{j}}\otimes\sigma^{B_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma^{B_{j-1}}\otimes\sigma^{B_{j+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma^{B_{n}}\kern 5.0pt.

There exists a measurement
(\Lambda_{j}:j\in[n+1])
on registers
AB_{1}B_{2}\dotsb B_{n}
, i.e., operators
\Lambda_{i}\succeq 0
with

\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\Lambda_{j}\quad=\quad{\mathbb{1}}\kern 5.0pt,

such that for all
j\in[n]
,

\trace\left[\Lambda_{j}\tau_{j}^{AB_{1}\ldots B_{n}}\right]\quad\geq\quad 1-6\epsilon\kern 5.0pt.

The above statement is slightly different from the one in ref.
[
6
]
because of a minor difference in defining quantum hypothesis testing relative entropy.

Let
\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}
be a quantum state shared between Alice, Bob, and Ref where registers
AC
are with Alice, register
B
is with Bob and register
R
is with Ref, and
\psi^{\prime RBC}\in{\mathsf{B}}^{\epsilon}(\psi^{RBC})
. The AJW protocol works as follows.

The AJW protocol:

1.

Alice and Bob initially share
m\coloneqq{\left\lceil 2^{\beta}/\epsilon^{2}\right\rceil}
copies of a purification
\left|\sigma\right\rangle^{LC}
of
\sigma^{C}
where
\beta\coloneqq{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}\left(\psi^{\prime RBC}\|\psi^{\prime RB}\otimes\sigma^{C}\right)
. Their global state is
\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}\otimes\left|\sigma\right\rangle^{L_{1}C_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\left|\sigma\right\rangle^{L_{m}C_{m}}
, where
\left|L_{i}\right|=\left|L\right|
and
\left|C_{i}\right|=\left|C\right|
for all
i\in[m]
. The registers
ACL_{1}L_{2}\dotsb L_{m}
are with Alice and the registers
BC_{1}C_{2}\dotsb C_{m}
are with Bob.

2.

Let
b
be the smallest integer such that
\log b\geq{\mathrm{D}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\epsilon^{2}}(\psi^{\prime BC}\|\psi^{\prime B}\otimes\sigma^{C})-\log\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\;
. By performing a suitable isometry on her registers, Alice transforms the global state into a state close to the state

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|{\lfloor(j-1)/b\rfloor}\right\rangle^{J_{1}}

\displaystyle\left|j-1{\@displayfalse\pmod{b}}\right\rangle^{J_{2}}\left|0\right\rangle^{L_{j}}\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC_{j}}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\otimes\left|\sigma\right\rangle^{L_{1}C_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\left|\sigma\right\rangle^{L_{j-1}C_{j-1}}\otimes\left|\sigma\right\rangle^{L_{j+1}C_{j+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\left|\sigma\right\rangle^{L_{m}C_{m}}\kern 5.0pt.

This is possible due to the Uhlmann theorem, the Convex-Split Lemma, and the choice of
m
.

3.

Alice sends register
J_{1}
to Bob with communication cost at most
(\log m-\log b)/2
using superdense coding.

4.

Then, for each
j_{2}\in[b]
, Bob swaps registers
C_{j_{2}}
and
C_{j_{2}+bj_{1}}
, conditioned on register
J_{1}
being in state
\left|j_{1}\right\rangle
. At this point, registers
RBC_{1}\ldots C_{b}
are in a state close to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b}\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{b}\psi^{RBC_{j_{2}}}\otimes\sigma^{C_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\sigma^{C_{j_{2}-1}}\otimes\sigma^{C_{j_{2}+1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\sigma^{C_{b}}\kern 5.0pt.

5.

Then, Bob uses position-based decoding to determine the index
j_{2}

for which register
C_{j_{2}}
is correlated with registers
RB
. This
is possible by the choice of

Embezzlement

3
The new protocol

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{R}\otimes|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{B}\otimes|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{C}\kern 5.0pt.

The reduction broadly follows the description we gave in Section
1.2
, and is a two-step process. We expand on these steps below.

(1) Coherent measurement of register
B
.

By “coherent measurement”, we mean the application of the isometry given by a Steinspring representation of the measurement. For the GHZ state, this corresponds “copying” the content of register
B
into a fresh register, in superposition. The state of the fresh register is chosen so as to facilitate the redistribution protocol.
Let
T
be a register with
|T|=d
, and
\left|\Psi\right\rangle^{TT^{\prime}}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{t}\left|tt\right\rangle
be the maximally entangled state over registers
T
and
T^{\prime}
.

Define the unitary operator
U_{1}\in{\mathsf{U}}({\mathcal{H}}^{BT})
as
U_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{j}|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{B}\otimes P_{j}^{T}
, where
P_{j}
is the Heisenberg-Weyl operator as defined in Section
2.1
. Let
\left|\kappa_{1}\right\rangle^{RBCTT^{\prime}}
and
\tau_{1}^{RBCT}
be the states obtained by applying
U_{1}
to
\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}\otimes\left|\Psi\right\rangle^{TT^{\prime}}
and
\sigma^{RBC}\otimes\Psi^{T}
, respectively. We have

\displaystyle\left|\kappa_{1}\right\rangle^{RBCTT^{\prime}}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left|j\right\rangle^{R}\left|j\right\rangle^{B}\left|j\right\rangle^{C}\otimes\sum_{t=1}^{d}\left|t\oplus j\right\rangle^{T}\left|t\right\rangle^{T^{\prime}}\kern 5.0pt.

Since the set of Heisenberg-Weyl operators
\left\{P_{a}\right\}
is closed under multiplication, and each
P_{a}
is traceless unless
a=d
, the states
(P_{a}\otimes{\mathbb{1}})\left|\Psi\right\rangle
are mutually orthogonal. So the unitary operator
U_{1}
coherently measures register
B
in
\psi^{RBC}
while it acts trivially on
\sigma
. Moreover, the reduced state on
T
remains maximally mixed. So

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{RBC}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{1}{d}\quad\sum_{j}|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{R}\otimes|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{B}\otimes|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{C}\kern 5.0pt,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\tau_{1}^{RBCT}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sigma^{RBC}\otimes\frac{{\mathbb{1}}^{T}}{d}\kern 5.0pt.

(2) Decoupling
C
from
RB
in
\sigma
.

Let
U_{2}\in{\mathsf{U}}({\mathcal{H}}^{BC})
be a unitary operator that is read-only on
B

and maps
\left|j\right\rangle^{C}
to
\left|0\right\rangle^{C}
if system
B
is in the
state
\left|j\right\rangle
. Let
\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle^{RBCTT^{\prime}}
and
\tau_{2}^{RBCT}

be the states after applying
U_{2}
to
\left|\kappa_{1}\right\rangle^{RBCTT^{\prime}}

and
\tau_{1}^{RBCT}
, respectively. We have

\displaystyle\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle^{RBCTT^{\prime}}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{1}{d}\quad\sum_{j}\left|j\right\rangle^{R}\otimes\left|j\right\rangle^{B}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle^{C}\otimes\sum_{t=1}^{d}\left|t\oplus j\right\rangle^{T}\left|t\right\rangle^{T^{\prime}}\kern 5.0pt,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\tau_{2}^{RBCT}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\psi^{RB}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|^{C}\otimes\frac{{\mathbb{1}}^{T}}{d}\kern 5.0pt.

In particular, since register
B
is classical in
\kappa_{1}^{RBC}
and
U_{2}
is read-only on
B
, we get
\kappa_{2}^{RB}=\psi^{RB}
.

The reduction procedure uses the above two steps to (effectively) add the maximally mixed state
\Psi^{T}
and apply the unitary operator
U_{2}U_{1}
. Note that running this procedure on both
\psi
and
\sigma
does not change their max-relative entropy and the hypothesis testing entropy. We have

{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}\left(\psi^{RBC}\|\sigma^{RBC}\right)-{\mathrm{D}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\epsilon_{2}^{2}}\left(\psi^{BC}\|\sigma^{BC}\right)\quad=\quad{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}\left(\kappa_{2}^{RBCT}\|\tau_{2}^{RBCT}\right)-{\mathrm{D}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\epsilon_{2}^{2}}\left(\kappa_{2}^{BCT}\|\tau_{2}^{BCT}\right)

(3.3)

where
\tau_{2}^{RBCT}=\kappa_{2}^{RB}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|^{C}\otimes\tfrac{{\mathbb{1}}^{T}}{d}
. Hence, if Alice and Bob locally map
\left|\psi\right\rangle
to
\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle
, then they can run the AJW protocol to transfer registers
CT
to Bob and finally retrieve
\left|\psi\right\rangle
by applying
U_{1}^{-1}U_{2}^{-1}
. A hitch here is that the reduction procedure cannot be implemented directly (i.e., as described above) for the local transformation of
\left|\psi\right\rangle
to
\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle
. This is because register
C
is initially with Alice and
register
B
is with Bob. However, since
\psi^{RB}=\kappa_{2}^{RB}
, there is an isometry
V:{\mathcal{H}}^{AC}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{ACTT^{\prime}}
which maps
\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}
to
\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle^{RABCTT^{\prime}}
, as guaranteed by the Uhlmann theorem.
Alice
can thus implement the local transformation from
\left|\psi\right\rangle
to
\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle
.

In summary, the simplified version of the protocol for the GHZ state works as follows:

1.

Alice applies the isometry
V
on her registers
AC
, and transforms the global state to the state
\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle^{RABCTT^{\prime}}
such that registers
(ACTT^{\prime})
,
(B)
, and
(R)
are with Alice, Bob and Ref, respectively.

2.

Choosing
\sigma^{CT}\coloneqq|0\rangle\!\langle 0|^{C}\otimes\tfrac{{\mathbb{1}}^{T}}{d}
, Alice and Bob run the AJW protocol on
\left|\kappa_{2}\right\rangle
to transfer registers
CT
to Bob with error at most
9\epsilon_{2}
. Let
\widehat{\kappa}_{2}^{RABCTT^{\prime}}
be the joint state of the registers
RABCTT^{\prime}
at the end of this step.

3.

Bob applies
U_{1}^{-1}U_{2}^{-1}
on the registers
BCT
, which are now in his possession.

4.

The output of the protocol is the final state in registers
RABC
.

By Theorem
2.12
and Eq. (
3.3
), the cost of the above protocol is at most

{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}\left(\psi^{RBC}\|\sigma^{RBC}\right)-{\mathrm{D}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\epsilon_{2}^{2}}\left(\psi^{BC}\|\sigma^{BC}\right)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon_{2}^{2}}\kern 5.0pt,

and
\rP(\kappa_{2}^{RABCTT^{\prime}},\widehat{\kappa}_{2}^{RABCTT^{\prime}})\leq 9\epsilon_{2}\;
.
Let
\phi^{RABC}
be the final state of the registers
RABC
. We have

\displaystyle\rP\left(\psi^{RABC},\phi^{RABC}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\quad\rP\left(\psi^{RABC}\otimes\Psi^{TT^{\prime}},\phi^{RABCTT^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\rP\left(\kappa_{2}^{RABCTT^{\prime}},\widehat{\kappa}_{2}^{RABCTT^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\quad 9\epsilon_{2}\kern 5.0pt,

where the first inequality is obtained by considering extensions of states in
RABC
to those in
RABCTT^{\prime}
and the monotonicity of purified distance under quantum operations, and the second step follows by the invariance of purified distance under unitary operations (in this case
U_{2}U_{1}
).

3.2
The protocol for arbitrary states

Now consider an arbitrary state
\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}
and a quantum Markov extension
\sigma^{RBC}\in{\mathsf{ME}}_{R-B-C}^{\epsilon_{2}^{4}/4,\psi}
. As explained in Section
2.2
, there exists a decomposition of register
B
as
{\mathcal{H}}^{B}=\bigoplus_{j}{\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{R}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{B_{j}^{C}}
such that

{\psi}^{RB}\quad=\quad\sigma^{RB}\quad=\quad\bigoplus_{j}p(j)\,{\psi}_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\otimes{\psi}_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}}\kern 5.0pt,

(3.4)

and

\sigma^{RBC}\quad=\quad\bigoplus_{j}p(j)\,\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\kern 5.0pt,

(3.5)

where
\sigma_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}={\psi}_{j}^{RB_{j}^{R}}
,
\sigma_{j}^{B_{j}^{C}C}\in{\mathsf{B}}^{\epsilon_{2}^{4}/4}\left(\trace_{B_{j}^{R}}\left((\Pi_{j}\otimes{\mathbb{1}}){\psi}^{BC}(\Pi_{j}\otimes{\mathbb{1}})\right)\right)
and
\Pi_{j}
is the projection operator over the
j
-th subspace in the direct sum decomposition of
{\mathcal{H}}^{B}
. This special structure of
\sigma^{RBC}
makes it possible to design the reduction procedure. As in the case of the GHZ state, the reduction procedure consists of the two main steps of coherent measurement and decoupling. These are preceded by two pre-processing steps. The pre-processing steps unitarily transform
\psi
and
\sigma
to the states
\kappa
and
\tau
which are easier to handle. In step (i), we apply a local isometry transforming
\sigma^{RBC}
to a classical-quantum state.

(i) Viewing
\sigma^{RBC}
as a classical-quantum state.

Let
B^{R}
and
B^{C}
be two quantum registers with
\left|B^{R}\right|\coloneqq\max_{j}\left|B_{j}^{R}\right|

and
\left|B^{C}\right|\coloneqq\max_{j}\left|B_{j}^{C}\right|
. As a consequence of Eq. (
3.5
),
there exists an isometry
U_{{\mathrm{i}}}:{\mathcal{H}}^{B}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{B^{R}JB^{C}}
which takes
\sigma^{RBC}
to the
state

{\widetilde{\sigma}}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}C}\quad\coloneqq\quad\sum_{j}p(j)\,\sigma_{j}^{RB^{R}}\otimes|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{J}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{B^{C}C}\kern 5.0pt.

(3.6)

Let
\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}C}
be the state obtained by applying the same operation on
\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}C}\quad

\displaystyle\coloneqq\quad U_{{\mathrm{i}}}\left|\psi\right\rangle^{RABC}\quad=\quad\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}|j\rangle\!\langle j^{\prime}|^{J}\otimes\psi_{j,j^{\prime}}^{RAB^{R}B^{C}C}\kern 5.0pt,

(3.7)

for some sub-normalized, rank
1
states
\psi_{j,j^{\prime}}
.
It is sufficient to design a protocol for redistributing register
C
in
\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}C}
when initially registers
(AC)
are held by Alice,
(B^{R}JB^{C})
are held by Bob and
R
is held by Ref.
Notice that
\psi_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}}=\sigma^{RB^{R}JB^{C}}
since
\psi^{RB}=\sigma^{RB}
. So
\psi_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}}
is a quantum Markov state of the form
RB^{R}\!-\!J\!-\!B^{C}
. So, Alice and Bob can use the folklore protocol for redistributing quantum Markov states explained in Fig.
2
and transfer
B^{C}
to Alice. This is done in step (ii) of pre-processing.

(ii) Transferring
B^{C}
from Bob to Alice without communication.

Note that
\psi_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}}
is purified by systems
(AC)
which are with Alice. So by applying a suitable isometry, Alice can prepare the following purification of
\psi_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}}
:

\left|\widehat{\psi}_{1}\right\rangle^{RB^{R}JJ^{\prime}B^{C}GH}\quad\coloneqq\quad\sum_{j}\sqrt{p(j)}\left|\sigma_{j}\right\rangle^{RB^{R}G}\otimes\left|j,j\right\rangle^{JJ^{\prime}}\otimes\left|\sigma_{j}\right\rangle^{B^{C}H}\kern 5.0pt,

where registers
J^{\prime}GH
are held by Alice. Let
\delta_{1}\in(0,1)
,
n_{1}\coloneqq\left|B^{C}H\right|^{2/\delta_{1}^{2}}
, and
D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime}
be registers with
|D_{1}|=|D_{1}^{\prime}|=n_{1}
. Conditioned on register
J
, Alice and Bob use the embezzling state
\left|\xi\right\rangle^{D_{1}D_{1}^{\prime}}
(as defined in Eq. (
2.7
)) and the reverse of the van Dam-Hayden protocol
[
40
]
to embezzle out
\left|\sigma_{j}\right\rangle^{B^{C}H}
in superposition. They thus obtain a state
\widetilde{\psi}_{1}
such that

\rP\left(\widetilde{\psi}_{1}^{RB^{R}GJJ^{\prime}D_{1}D_{1}^{\prime}},~\sum_{j}\sqrt{p(j)}\left|\sigma_{j}\right\rangle^{RB^{R}G}\otimes\left|j,j\right\rangle^{JJ^{\prime}}\otimes\left|\xi\right\rangle^{D_{1}D_{1}^{\prime}}\right)\quad\leq\quad\delta_{1}\kern 5.0pt.

Finally, conditioned on register
J
, Alice locally generates
\left|\sigma_{j}\right\rangle^{B^{C}H}
in superposition with registers
B^{C}H
on her side, and applies an Uhlmann unitary operator to her registers in order to prepare the purification
\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}C}
. Let
U_{{\mathrm{ii}},A}
and
U_{{\mathrm{ii}},B}
denote the overall unitary operators applied by Alice and Bob, respectively, in this step. After applying
U_{{\mathrm{ii}},A}
and
U_{{\mathrm{ii}},B}
, the global state is
\left|\psi_{2}\right\rangle
satisfying

\rP\left(\psi_{2}^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}CD_{1}D_{1}^{\prime}},~|\psi_{1}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{1}|^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}C}\otimes|\xi\rangle\!\langle\xi|^{D_{1}D_{1}^{\prime}}\right)\quad\leq\quad\delta_{1}\kern 5.0pt,

where registers
AB^{C}C
are with Alice, registers
B^{R}J
are with Bob and register
R
is with Ref.
Thus, the problem reduces, up to a purified distance
\delta_{1}
, to the case where the global state is
\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle
and the register
B^{C}
is with Alice. Henceforth, we assume that this is indeed the case. We account for the inaccuracy introduced by this assumption in the error analysis of the protocol. This completes the second step and the pre-processing stage of the protocol.

Due to the pre-processing steps, we may suppose that the global state is
\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}C}
such that
registers
(AB^{C}C)
,
(B^{R}J)
, and
R
are held by Alice, Bob, and Ref,
respectively. It then remains for Alice to send
B^{C}C

to Bob. To achieve this, we follow a two-step unitary procedure (as in the case of the GHZ state) which
decouples registers
RB^{R}J
and
B^{C}C
in
{\widetilde{\sigma}}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}C}

while keeping the state of registers

unchanged. This operation
transforms
{\widetilde{\sigma}}
to a product state and allows us to use the AJW

To decouple

from

in
{\widetilde{\sigma}}

2.16

using an additional
maximally entangled state. This operation transforms
\psi_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}C}

, and keeps
{\widetilde{\sigma}}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}C}
intact. The following two steps contain the detailed construction of these unitary procedures.

(1) Coherent measurement of register

.

Let

be a register with
|F|=|J|

. Define

\left|\kappa_{1}\right\rangle^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}CFF^{\prime}}\quad\coloneqq\quad U_{1}\left(\left|\psi_{1}\right\rangle^{RAB^{R}JB^{C}C}\otimes\left|\Psi\right\rangle^{FF^{\prime}}\right)\kern 5.0pt,

and

\tau_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}CF}\quad\coloneqq\quad U_{1}\left({\widetilde{\sigma}}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}C}\otimes\frac{{\mathbb{1}}^{F}}{|F|}\right)U_{1}^{\dagger}\kern 5.0pt,

(3.8)

where
\left|\Psi\right\rangle^{FF^{\prime}}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{f=1}^{d}\left|ff\right\rangle

F
and

\tau_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}CF}\quad=\quad{\widetilde{\sigma}}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}C}\otimes\frac{{\mathbb{1}}^{F}}{|F|}\kern 5.0pt,

(3.9)

\kappa_{1}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}C}\quad=\quad\sum_{j}|j\rangle\!\langle j|^{J}\otimes\psi_{j,j}^{RB^{R}B^{C}C}\kern 5.0pt.

(3.10)

from

in

.

By Eqs. (

\displaystyle{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}\left(\left.\kappa_{2}^{RB^{R}JB^{C}CE_{2}D_{2}F}\right\|~\kappa_{2}^{RB^{R}J}\otimes\nu_{2}^{B^{C}CE_{2}}\otimes\xi_{1:n_{2}}^{D_{2}}\otimes\frac{{\mathbb{1}}^{F}}{|F|}\right)

Now we have all the ingredients for the new state redistribution protocol. We describe the steps systematically below. Let

\beta\quad\coloneqq\quad{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}\left(\psi^{RBC}\|~\sigma^{RBC}\right)+5\delta_{2}\kern 5.0pt,

and

.
Let

4
Conclusion and outlook

In this article, we revisited the task of one-shot quantum state redistribution, and introduced a new protocol achieving this task with communication cost

\frac{1}{2}\min_{\psi^{\prime}\in{\mathsf{B}}^{\epsilon}(\psi^{RBC})}\min_{\sigma^{RBC}\in{\mathsf{ME}}_{R-B-C}^{\epsilon^{2}/4,\psi^{\prime}}}\left[{\mathrm{D}}_{\max}\left(\psi^{\prime RBC}\|\sigma^{RBC}\right)-{\mathrm{D}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\epsilon}\left(\psi^{\prime BC}\|\sigma^{BC}\right)\right]+\Order\!\Big(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big)\kern 5.0pt,

(4.1)

with error parameter
\epsilon
. This is the first result connecting the communication cost of state redistribution with Markov chains. It provides an operational interpretation for a one-shot representation of quantum conditional mutual information as explained in Sec
1
. In the special case where
\psi^{RBC}
is a quantum Markov chain, our protocol leads to near-zero communication which was not known for the previous protocols designed for arbitrary states. Moreover, the communication cost of our protocol is lower than that of all previously known one-shot protocols and we show that it achieves the optimal cost of
\tfrac{1}{2}\,\rI(R:C\,|\,B)
in the asymptotic i.i.d. setting. Our protocol also achieves the near-optimal result of ref.
[
5
]
in the case when
\psi^{RBC}
is classical.

A question of interest is whether the communication cost of our one-shot protocol can be bounded with
\rI(R:C\,|\,B)
. In the quantum communication complexity setting, such a bound would imply the possibility of compressing the communication of bounded-round quantum protocols to their information content. This would lead to a
direct-sum theorem
for bounded-round quantum communication complexity
[
38
]
.

Another question that we have not addressed in this article is whether our bound is near-optimal. There are several known lower bounds in the literature for the communication cost of entanglement-assisted quantum state redistribution, such as in ref.
[
11
, Proposition 6]
and ref.
[
25
, Theorem 3.2, Eq. (3.17)]
. However, it is not clear if our bound matches any of them. Obtaining a near-optimal bound for one-shot quantum state redistribution remains a major open question.

Sign sequences of log-atomic numbers
Vincent Bagayoko

Abstract
. Log-atomic numbers are surreal numbers whose iterated logarithms are monomials, and consequently have a trivial expansion as transseries. Presenting surreal numbers as sign sequences, we give the sign sequence formula for log-atomic numbers. To that efect, we relate log-atomic numbers to fixed-points of certain surreal functions.



Introduction

Each surreal number is precisely described by a sequence of signs giving the
path to that number in the surreal tree. Yet the relation between this sign sequence and properties of numbers in the ordered, differential exponential field of surreal numbers is still complex and mysterious. Originally, Conway’s class

\mathbf{No}
of
surreal numbers

[

Thus

whereas
1:=\{\{0\}|\varnothing\}
has birthday
1
and
1/2:=\{\{0\}|\{1\}\}

Conway defined the arithmetic on surreal numbers,
turning

into an ordered field extension of the
reals, naturally containing the class
\mathbf{On}
of ordinal numbers. He also discovered that

The sign sequence presentation of surreal numbers, studied by Gonshor

The birthdate coincides with the domain, called

1.
Numbers and sign sequences

1.1.
Numbers as sign sequences

Definition 1.1
.

A surreal number is a map
x:\ell(x)\rightarrow\{-1,1\};\alpha\mapsto x[\alpha]
, where
\ell(x)\in\mathbf{On}
is an ordinal number. We call
\ell(x)
the

length
of
x
.

Given a surreal number
x\in\mathbf{No}
, we extend its sign sequence with

x[\alpha]=0
for all
\alpha\geqslant\ell(x)
. Given
x\in\mathbf{No}
and
\alpha\in\mathbf{On}
, we also introduce the

restriction

y={x\upharpoonleft\alpha}\in\mathbf{No}
to

\alpha
as being the initial segment of
x
of length
\alpha
, i.e.
y[\beta]=x[\beta]
for
\beta<\alpha
and
y[\beta]=0
for
\beta\geqslant\alpha
.

The ordering
<
on
\mathbf{No}
is lexicographical: given distinct elements

x,y\in\mathbf{No}
, there exists a smallest ordinal
\alpha
with
x[\alpha]\neq y[\alpha]
, and
x<y
if and only if
x[\alpha]<y[\alpha]
.

For
x,y\in\mathbf{No}
, we say that
x
is

simpler
than
y
, and write
x\sqsubseteq y
, if
x=y\upharpoonleft\ell(x)
. We write
x\sqsubset y
if
x\sqsubseteq y
and
x\neq y
. For
x\in\mathbf{No}
, we
write

x_{\sqsubset}:=\{a\in\mathbf{No}:a\sqsubset x\}

for the set of numbers that are strictly simpler than
x
. The partially
ordered class
(\textbf{}\mathbf{No},\sqsubset)
is well-founded, and each
set
(x_{\sqsubset},\sqsubset)
is well-ordered with order type
\operatorname{ord}(x_{\sqsubset},\sqsubset)=\ell(x)
. Moreover,
x_{\sqsubset}
is the union
of the sets

x_{L}:=\{y\in\mathbf{No}:y\sqsubset x,y<x\}\text{\qquad and\qquad$x_{R}:=\{y\in\mathbf{No}:y\sqsubset x,y>x\}$.}

Every linearly ordered subset
C
of
(\textbf{}\mathbf{No},\sqsubset)
has
a
supremum

s=\sup_{\sqsubset}C
in
\textbf{}\mathbf{No}
. Indeed, we have
\ell(s)=\sup\ell(Z)
, and
s[\alpha]=x[\alpha]
for all
x\in C
with
\alpha<\ell(x)
. Numbers
x
that are
equal to
\sup_{\sqsubseteq}x_{\sqsubset}
are called
limit
numbers
; other numbers are called

successor numbers
. Limit numbers
are exactly the numbers whose length is a limit ordinal.

Note that
\mathbf{No}
is a proper class. In particular, the linearly ordered
class
(\mathbf{On},\in)
is embedded into the partial order
(\mathbf{No},\sqsubset)
through the map
\mathbf{On}\longrightarrow\mathbf{No}\>;\>\alpha\mapsto(1)_{\beta<\alpha}
. We will identify ordinals numbers with
their surreal image.

1.2.
Monomials

For
x\in\mathbf{No}^{>}
we define

\mathcal{H}[x]:=\{y\in\mathrm{K}^{>}:\exists q\in\mathbb{Q}^{>},q^{-1}x<y<qx\}.

The class
\mathcal{H}[x]
has a unique
\sqsubset
-minimal element denoted

\mathfrak{d}_{x}
, and the class
\mathbf{Mo}:=\{\mathfrak{d}_{x}:x\in\mathbf{No}^{>}\}
is a
subgroup of
(\mathbf{No}^{>},\cdot)
.

Definition 1.2
.

A
monomial
is a number
\mathfrak{m}
which is
simplest in the class
\mathcal{H}[\mathfrak{m}]
, i.e. a number of the
form
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{d}_{x}
for a certain
x\in\mathbf{No}^{>}
.

Conway defined
[
9
, Chapter 3]
a parametrisation
z\mapsto\dot{\omega}^{z}
of
\mathbf{Mo}
. This is an
isomorphism
(\mathbf{No},+,0,<)\longrightarrow(\mathbf{Mo},\cdot,1,<)
which also preserves simplicity:

\forall x,y\in\mathbf{No},x\sqsubset y\Longleftrightarrow\dot{\omega}^{x}\sqsubset\dot{\omega}^{y}.

The function
\mathbf{On}\longrightarrow\mathbf{No}\>;\>\alpha\mapsto\dot{\omega}^{\alpha}
coincides with the ordinal exponentiation with basis

\omega
.

1.3.
Arithmetic on sign sequences

For ordinals
\alpha,\beta
, we will denote their ordinal sum, product, and
exponentiation by
\alpha\dotplus\beta
,
\alpha\mathbin{\dot{\times}}\beta
and
\dot{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
Here, we consider the operations
\dotplus
and
\mathbin{\dot{\times}}
of

[
4
, Section 3.2]
on
\mathbf{No}
which extend
ordinal arithmetic to
\mathbf{No}
.

For numbers
x,y
, we write
x\dotplus y
for the number
whose sign sequence is the concatenation of that of
y
at the end of that of

x
. So
x\dotplus y
is the number of length
\ell(x\dotplus y)=\ell(x)\dotplus\ell(y)
, which satisfies

\displaystyle(x\dotplus y)[\alpha]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x[\alpha]\hskip 0.0pt(\alpha<\ell(x))

\displaystyle(x\dotplus y)[\ell(x)\dotplus\beta]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y[\beta]\hskip 0.0pt(\beta<\ell(y))

The operation
\dotplus
clearly extends ordinal sum. We have
x\dotplus 0=0\dotplus x=x
for all
x\in\mathbf{No}
.

We write
x\mathbin{\dot{\times}}y
for the number of length
\ell(x)\mathbin{\dot{\times}}\ell(y)
whose sign sequence is defined by

\displaystyle(x\mathbin{\dot{\times}}y)[\ell(x)\mathbin{\dot{\times}}\alpha\dotplus\beta]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y[\alpha]x[\beta]\hskip 0.0pt(\alpha<\ell(y),\beta<\ell(x))

The operation
\mathbin{\dot{\times}}
extends ordinal product. Given
x\in\textbf{}\mathbf{No}
and
\alpha\in\mathbf{On}
, the number
x\mathbin{\dot{\times}}\alpha
is the concatenation of
x
with
itself to the right “
\alpha
-many times”, whereas
\alpha\mathbin{\dot{\times}}x
is
the number obtained from
x
by replacing each sign by itself
\alpha
-many
times. We have
x\mathbin{\dot{\times}}1=x
and
x\mathbin{\dot{\times}}(-1)=-x
for all
x\in\mathbf{No}
.

The operations also enjoy properties which extend that of their ordinal
counterparts as is illustrated hereafter. We refer to

[
4
, Section 3.2]
for more details.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
4
, Lemma 3.1]

THe laws
\dotplus
and
\mathbin{\dot{\times}}
are associative with identities
0
and
1
respectively, and
\mathbin{\dot{\times}}
distribues with
\dotplus
on the left.
Moreover, for
x,y\in\textbf{}\mathbf{No}
where
y
is a limit, we have

a)

x\dotplus y=\sup_{\sqsubset}(x\dotplus y_{\sqsubset})
.

b)

x\mathbin{\dot{\times}}y=\sup_{\sqsubset}\left(x\mathbin{\mathbin{\dot{\times}}}y_{\sqsubset}\right)
.

1.4.
Sign sequences formulas

If
\alpha
is an ordinal and
f
is a function
\alpha\longrightarrow\mathbf{No}
, then
(\alpha,f)
uniquely determines a surreal number

[\alpha,f]
defined inductively by the rules

\bullet

[0,f]=0
,

\bullet

[\gamma\dotplus 1,f\upharpoonleft(\gamma+1)]=[\gamma,f\upharpoonleft\gamma]\dotplus f(\gamma)
for
\beta<\alpha
,

\bullet

[\gamma,f\upharpoonleft\gamma]=\sup_{\sqsubseteq}\{[\mu,f\upharpoonleft\mu]:\mu<\gamma\}
for limit ordinals
\gamma\leqslant\alpha
.

We say that
(\alpha,f)
is a
sign sequence formula
for
[\alpha,f]
, which we identify with the informal
expression

\dot{\sum}_{\gamma<\alpha}f(\gamma)=f(0)\dotplus f(1)\dotplus\mathord{\cdots\dotplus f(\beta)\dotplus\mathord{\cdots}}

where
\gamma
ranges in
\alpha
. For instance, the ordered pair
(\omega,((-1)^{n})_{n<\omega})
is a sign sequence formula of
1/2\mathbin{\dot{\times}}\omega=1\dotplus(-1)\dotplus 1\dotplus(-1)\dotplus\cdots=2/3
. In
general, we look for such formulas where
f
alternates between

\mathbf{On}^{>}
and
-\mathbf{On}^{>}
, that is, where
f
ranges in

\mathbf{On}^{>}\cup-\mathbf{On}^{>}
and where, for each ordinal
\beta

with
\beta\dotplus 1<\alpha
, we have
f(\beta)f(\beta\dotplus 1)<0
. It is easy to see that every surreal number admits a unique such
alternating sign sequence formula. We refer to this formula as
the

sign sequence formula of said number.

If
F:\textbf{}\mathbf{No}\longrightarrow\textbf{}\mathbf{No}
is a
function, we may look for a function
\Psi
whose value at each number
x
is
a sign sequence formula of
F(x)
. We then consider
\Psi
as a
sign
sequence formula
for
F
.

As an example, we now state Gonshor’s results regarding the sign sequences of
monomials. For
z\in\mathbf{No}
, we write
\tau_{z}
for the order type of

(z_{L},\sqsubset)
, that is the order type
z^{-1}[\{1\}]
when
z
is
seen as a sign sequence, or equivalently the ordinal number of signs
1
in
the sign sequence of
z
. We have
\tau_{z}=0
if and only if
z\in-\mathbf{On}
. Gonshor found the following sign sequence formula for monomials:

Proposition 1.4
.

[
11
, Theorem 5.11]
Let
z
be
a number. The sign sequence formula for
\dot{\omega}^{z}
is

\dot{\omega}^{z}=1\dotplus\dot{\sum}_{\beta<\ell(x)}(z[\beta]\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{z\upharpoonleft(\beta+1)}+1}).

For instance, we have

\sqrt{\omega}=\dot{\omega}^{1/2}\;=\;\omega\dotplus(-\omega^{2})\text{{\hskip 30.00005pt}and{\hskip 30.00005pt}$\log\omega=\dot{\omega}^{\omega^{-1}}\;=\;\omega\dotplus(-\omega^{3}).$}

Let us also specify a consequence that we will use often in what follows.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbf{No}
be such that the maximal
ordinal
\alpha
with
\alpha\sqsubseteq y
is a limit. We
have
\dot{\omega}^{x\dotplus y}=\dot{\omega}^{x}\dotplus\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{x}\dotplus y}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be maximal with
\alpha\sqsubseteq y
and write
y=\alpha\dotplus z
. Since
y
is strictly positive, we have
\alpha>0
, whence

\alpha\geqslant\omega
. Define

\mathfrak{m}:=\left(z[0]\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{\tau_{x}\dotplus\alpha\dotplus z\upharpoonleft 1}+1}\right)\dotplus\cdots\dotplus\left(z[\gamma]\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{\tau_{x}\dotplus\alpha\dotplus(z\upharpoonleft(\gamma+1))}+1}\right)\dotplus\cdots

where
\gamma
ranges in
\ell(z)
. Proposition
1.4

yields
\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{x}\dotplus y}=\omega^{\tau_{x}\dotplus\alpha}\dotplus\mathfrak{m}
. We also have
\omega^{x\dotplus y}=\omega^{x}\dotplus\mathfrak{n}\dotplus\mathfrak{m}
where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(y[0]\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{x\dotplus 1}+1})\dotplus\cdots\dotplus(y[\beta]\omega^{\tau_{x\dotplus\beta}+1})\dotplus\cdots,\beta<\alpha.

For
\beta<\alpha
, we have
y[\beta]=1
and
\tau_{x\dotplus\beta}=\tau_{x}\dotplus\beta
, so

\mathfrak{n}=\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{x}\dotplus 1+1}\dotplus\cdots\dotplus\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{x}\dotplus\beta+1}\dotplus\cdots=\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{x}\dotplus\alpha}.

It follows that
\dot{\omega}^{x\dotplus y}=\dot{\omega}^{x}\dotplus\dot{\omega}^{\tau_{x}\dotplus y}
.
∎

2.2.
Convexity

We fix a surreal substructure
\mathbf{S}
. Asubclass

Proposition 2.3
.

[
4

A convex subclass
\mathbf{C}
of

\mathbf{S}
is a surreal substructure if and only if each subset of

\mathbf{C}
has strict upper and lower bounds in
\mathbf{C}

In particular, non-empty open intervals
(a,b)\cap\mathbf{S}

following proposition gives a sign sequence formula for
\Xi_{(a,b)}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha
be a non-zero ordinal. Let

and

a)

We have
\Xi_{(a,a\dotplus\alpha)}z=a\dotplus 1\dotplus z

z\ngtr\alpha_{L}
,

and
\Xi_{(a,a\dotplus\alpha)}(\alpha\dotplus z)=a\dotplus\alpha\dotplus(-1)\dotplus z
.

b)

We have
\Xi_{(b\dotplus(-\alpha),b)}z=b\dotplus(-1)\dotplus z
if
z\nless-(\alpha_{L})
,

and
\Xi_{(b\dotplus(-\alpha),b)}((-\alpha)\dotplus z)=b\dotplus(-\alpha)\dotplus 1\dotplus z
.

Proof.

First note that
\alpha\sqsubseteq z
if and only if
z>(\alpha_{L})
and

(-\alpha)\sqsubseteq z
if and only if
z<-(\alpha_{L})
so the given
descriptions cover all cases. We will only derive the formulas for the

(a,a\dotplus\alpha)
, the other ones being symmetric. The

is the simplest element of
(a,a\dotplus\alpha)

\Xi_{(a,a\dotplus\alpha)}z=a\dotplus 1\dotplus u_{z}

\mathbf{No}^{\ngtr\alpha_{L}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{No}\setminus\mathbf{No}^{\ngtr\alpha_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{No}^{\sqsupseteq\alpha},\quad\operatorname{and}

\displaystyle(a,a\dotplus\alpha)\setminus(a\dotplus 1\dotplus\mathbf{No}^{\ngtr\alpha_{L}})

We have

Proof.

The first result is a consequence of the identity
\mathbf{No}_{\succ}=\omega\mathbin{\dot{\times}}\mathbf{No}

[

∎

Recall that
\kappa_{a}=\dot{\omega}^{\dot{\omega}^{\iota_{a}}}
so

\rho_{\theta_{a}}=\iota_{a}
.
[
15

gives
\tau_{\iota_{a}}=\varepsilon_{\flat\tau_{a}}

\displaystyle\varsigma_{a}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tau_{\iota_{a}}\in\mathbf{On},\text{\quad and}

\displaystyle\delta_{a}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\dot{\omega}^{\varsigma_{a}\dotplus 1}\hskip 8.00003pt=\;\dot{\omega}^{\varsigma_{a}}\mathbin{\dot{\times}}\omega\in\mathbf{On},

Lemma 4.2

For

\rho_{\theta_{a}-n}=\rho_{\theta_{a}}-n=\rho_{\theta_{a}}\dotplus(-n)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle(\rho_{\theta_{a}-n-1},\rho_{\theta_{a}-n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\rho_{\theta_{a}}-n-1,\rho_{\theta_{a}}-n)

\displaystyle\mathbf{No}^{\sqsupseteq\rho_{\theta_{a}}-n-1/2}.

(

Lemma 4.3
.

For

, we have

We see that

is the surreal isomorphism

Proposition 5.2
.

The structure
\mathbf{La}
is closed.

Proof.

Let
C
be a non-empty chain in
\mathbf{La}
. We want to prove that

\sup_{\sqsubset}C
is log-atomic. We may assume that
C
has no

\sqsubset
-maximum.

If the set

for which there is a

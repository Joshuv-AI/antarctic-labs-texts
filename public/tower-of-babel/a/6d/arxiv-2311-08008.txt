1.
Introduction

The study of Schur functors and determinantal ideals has a long history that
goes back to the seminal work of Lascoux
[
13
]
who used Schur functors to
construct (in characteristic 0) a free graded resolution of determinantal
ideals. More precisely, let
\varphi:F\longrightarrow G
be a graded

R
-morphism between free
R
-modules of rank
t
and
t+c-1
, respectively.
Set
M=\coker(\varphi^{*})
. It is an interesting open problem in commutative
algebra to determine a graded free minimal

R
-resolution of the cokernel
of the natural map,

\varphi_{r,s}^{*}:\bigwedge^{r}G^{*}\otimes\bigwedge^{s}F^{*}\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{r+s}F^{*},\quad f\otimes g\mapsto\bigwedge^{r}\varphi^{*}(f)\wedge g,

and in particular to find a graded free minimal
R
-resolution of the

p
-exterior power,
\bigwedge^{p}M:=\coker(\varphi^{*}_{1,p-1})
, of
M
, as
well as to describe a graded free minimal
R
-resolution of any Schur power

\Sigma^{I}M
. So far only few particular cases have been described, and the
most relevant contribution to this problem is due to Lascoux who using Schur
complexes constructed a minimal free resolution of

\coker(\varphi^{*}_{r,t-r})=R/I_{r}(\varphi)
. In
[
2
]
,
Buchsbaum and Eisenbud solves the case
\coker(\varphi^{*}_{p,0})
and in
pg. 299 they predict the first terms of a graded free minimal

R
-resolution of
\coker(\varphi^{*}_{p-1,1})
. No contributions have been
achieved until now and we devote this paper to study the first open cases and solve the case
p=c=2
as well as the case
p=2
and
c=3
up to an explicitly described summand which seems to split off. Also the case
p=2
,
c\geq 4
is solved under a certain depth condition, up to a concrete summand. More precisely, this note has two goals: (1) To
show that the canonical module of any determinantal ring
R/I_{i}(\varphi)
is up to twist equal to a
suitable Schur power of the cokernel
M
of
\varphi^{*}
, thus equal to
\wedge^{t+1-i}M
if
c=2
; and (2) To construct
a free resolution of
\wedge^{2}M
for the cases
c=2,3
and, hence, solve Buchsbaum-Eisenbud question for
p=c=2
, and for
p=2
and
c=3
up to a concrete summand. Moreover, for
c=2
, we find a minimal
R
-free resolution of
\wedge^{p}M
for any
p
.

As a main tool we use the resolution of determinantal ideals
I_{i}(\varphi)

constructed by Lascoux
[
13
]
using Schur functors. For sake of completeness
we quickly review the construction. As a byproduct we obtain the minimal
resolution of the normal module of a standard determinantal ideal of
codimension 3. We end the paper with a couple of conjectures based on our results and some explicit computations using Macaulay2 (Conjectures 5.1 and 5.3).

3.
Schur powers of the cokernel of a graded morphism

Let
\varphi:F:=\oplus_{i=1}^{t}R(b_{i})\longrightarrow G:=\oplus_{j=1}^{t+c-1}R(a_{j})
be a graded
R
-morphism between free
R
-modules of rank
t
and
t+c-1
, respectively. Set
M=\coker(\varphi^{*})
.
It is a longstanding problem in commutative algebra to determine a graded free
minimal
R
-resolution of the
p
-exterior power,
\bigwedge^{p}M
, of

or,
even more generally, a graded free minimal
R
-resolution of the cokernel of

\varphi_{r,s}^{*}:\bigwedge^{r}G^{*}\otimes\bigwedge^{s}F^{*}\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{r+s}F^{*},\quad f\otimes g\mapsto\bigwedge^{r}\varphi^{*}(f)\wedge g.

Note that
\bigwedge^{p}M=\coker(\varphi^{*}_{1,p-1})
and, in particular,

M=\coker(\varphi^{*}_{1,0})
. So far, only few particular cases have been
described. To our knowledge, an explicit finite free
R
-resolution has been
computed for
\coker(\varphi^{*}_{1,0})
under the assumption that

\depth_{I_{t}(\varphi)}R
has the largest possible value, namely

\depth_{I_{t}(\varphi)}R=c
. It is known that this value is
achieved if the entries of
\varphi
are general enough. A minimal graded
free
R
-resolution of
\coker(\varphi^{*}_{r,t-r})=R/I_{r}(\varphi)
is known
under the assumption that

\depth_{I_{r}(\varphi)}R=(t-r+1)(t-r+c)
by
[
13
]
. Again we
know that this value is achieved if the entries of
\varphi
are general
enough. Finally, in
[
2
]
; Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2, an explicit
finite free
R
-resolution of
\coker(\varphi^{*}_{p,0})
is given under the
hypothesis that
\depth_{I_{t}(\varphi)}R=c
, and in Proposition
6.1 of the same paper a minimal free
R
-resolution of
\bigwedge^{2}M
is
given in the case
c=1
under the assumption

\depth_{I_{t-1}(\varphi)}R=4
.

In
[
2
]
, pg. 299, Buchsbaum and Eisenbud predict the first terms of a graded free minimal
R
-resolution of
\coker(\varphi^{*}_{p-1,1})
and, in particular, of

. To state their guess we need to fix some notation. Given a graded free
R
-module

, we define

(3.1)

L_{p}^{q}F=\ker(\delta_{p+1}^{q-1})\ \ \text{ where }\\
\delta_{k}^{\ell}:S_{k-1}F\otimes\bigwedge^{\ell}F\longrightarrow S_{k}F\otimes\bigwedge^{\ell-1}F.

It holds (see, for instance,
[
2
]
; Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 2.5):

(1)

.

(2)

for all

.

(3)

for all

.

(4)

(5)

(6)

Given the graded morphism

(See

We will often use

Let

Let

(I)^{*}

Proposition 3.5
.

Set
R=k[x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}]
. Let
\varphi^{*}:G^{*}\longrightarrow F^{*}
be a graded minimal morphism between free
R
-modules of rank
t+1
and
t
, respectively. Set
B_{t-p+1}:=R/I_{t+1-p}(\varphi)
,
M=\coker(\varphi^{*})
and
\ell:=\sum_{j=1}^{t+1}a_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_{i}
and assume that
B_{t+1-p}
is determinantal. Then, we have:

K_{B_{t+1-p}}(n+1-p\ell)\cong\bigwedge^{p}M.

Proof.

By hypothesis
M=\coker(\varphi^{*})
. Therefore, a minimal graded
R
-free resolution of
\bigwedge^{p}M
starts out as:

\cdots\longrightarrow G^{*}\otimes\bigwedge^{p-1}F^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\varphi^{*}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigwedge^{p}F^{*}\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{p}M\longrightarrow 0

with
\overline{\varphi^{*}}(\eta\otimes\rho)=\varphi^{*}(\eta)\wedge\rho
. So,
\bigwedge^{p}M=\coker(\overline{\varphi^{*}})
.

On the other hand, we know that
B_{t-p+1}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring and
\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t-p+1,\varphi)
is a graded minimal free
R
-resolution of
B_{t-p+1}
of length
p(p+1)
(see Theorem
3.4
). Applying
\Hom_{R}(-,R)
to
\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t+1-p,\varphi)
we get a minimal graded free
R
-resolution of
\Ext_{R}^{p(p+1)}(B_{t-p+1},R)\cong K_{B_{t+1-p}}(n+1)
. Let us explicitly compute
\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)}(t+1-p,\varphi)
,
\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+1}(t+1-p,\varphi)
and the morphism linking them.

\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)}(t+1-p,\varphi)
corresponds to the unique partition
I=(\overbrace{t+1,\cdots,t+1}^{p})
for which the Durfee square
p_{I}=p
,
n(I)=p(t-p)
and
I^{\prime}=(\overbrace{p,\cdots,p}^{t-p},\overbrace{p+1,\cdots,p+1}^{p})
. Thus, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)}(t+1-p,\varphi)&:=&\Sigma^{(I)^{*}}G^{*}\otimes\Sigma^{I^{\prime}}F\\
&\cong&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes p}\otimes(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{\otimes p}\otimes\bigwedge^{p}F\\
&\cong&\bigwedge^{p}F\otimes R(-p\ell).\end{array}

Analogously, we apply
[
13
]
; Corollaire 5.10 and we get that the
R
-free module

\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+1}(t+1-p,\varphi)
corresponds to a unique partition, namely,
I=(t,\overbrace{t+1,\cdots,t+1}^{p-1})
for which
p_{I}=p
,
n(I)=p(t-p)
,
I^{\prime}=(\overbrace{p,\cdots,p}^{t-p+1},\overbrace{p+1,\cdots,p+1}^{p-1})
and
I^{*}=(p-1,\overbrace{p,\cdots,p}^{t})
. Thus, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+1}(t+1-p,\varphi)&:=&\Sigma^{(I)^{*}}G^{*}\otimes\Sigma^{I^{\prime}}F\\
&\cong&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes(p-1)}\otimes\bigwedge^{t}(G^{*})\otimes(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{\otimes p}\otimes\bigwedge^{p-1}F\\
&\cong&G\otimes\bigwedge^{p-1}F\otimes R(-p\ell)\end{array}

where the last isomorphism follows from the well known fact that
\bigwedge^{t}(G^{*})\cong G\otimes\bigwedge^{t+1}(G^{*})
.
To finish the proof it is enough to observe that for the unique partitions
I=(\overbrace{t+1,\cdots,t+1}^{p})
and
H=(t,\overbrace{t+1,\cdots,t}^{p-1})
associated to
\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)}(t+1-p,\varphi)
and
\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+1}(t+1-p,\varphi)
, respectively,
we have
\psi_{(I,H)}^{*}\cong\overline{\varphi^{*}}\otimes 1_{R(p\ell)}
, whence
K_{B_{t+1-p}}(n+1-p\ell)\cong\bigwedge^{p}(M).

∎

By
[
15
]
, Proposition 4, we know that

is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R/I_{t+1-p}(\varphi)
-module. As a consequence of the above Proposition we recover Vetter’s result and even more. We have:

. Let
\varphi^{*}:G^{*}\longrightarrow F^{*}
be a graded

R
-modules of rank

and
t
,
respectively, and let

be the cokernel of

. Assume that

is a Gorenstein

Since

Theorem 3.7
.

Set
R=k[x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}]
. Let

\varphi^{*}:G^{*}\longrightarrow F^{*}
be a graded minimal morphism between
free
R
-modules of rank
t+1
and
t
, respectively. Set

\ell:=\sum_{j=1}^{t+1}a_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_{i}
and assume

\depth_{I_{t+1-p}(\varphi)}R=p(p+1)
. Then, the
R
-dual

\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t+1-p,\varphi)^{*}
of
\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t+1-p,\varphi)
is a
minimal free
R
-resolution of
\bigwedge^{p}M(p\ell)
.

In particular, Conjecture
3.1
(with
\varphi
minimal) is true
for

.

Proof.

We know that
\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t+1-p,\varphi)
is a minimal graded free
R
-resolution of

. We also know that

is a Cohen-Macaulay ring. Therefore, the
R
-dual
\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t+1-p,\varphi)^{*}
of
\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t+1-p,\varphi)
gives us a minimal graded free
R
-resolution of
K_{B_{t+1-p}(\varphi)}(n+1)
and we now conclude using Proposition
3.5
.

To prove Conjecture
3.1
for
p=c=2
, we only have to identify the last terms of
\mathbb{L}^{\bullet}(t+1-p,\varphi)
. By the proof of Proposition
3.5
we know that

\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)}(t+1-p,\varphi)=(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{p}\otimes(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{p}\otimes\bigwedge^{p}F\cong\bigwedge^{p}F(-p\ell)

and

\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+1}(t+1-p,\varphi)=(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{p}\otimes G\otimes(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{p}\otimes\bigwedge^{p-1}F\cong G\otimes\bigwedge^{p-1}F(-p\ell).

Let us compute
\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+2}(t+1-p,\varphi)
.

\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+2}(t+1-p,\varphi)
corresponds to two different partitions:

•

I_{1}:=(p-1,\overbrace{t+1,\cdots,t+1}^{p-1})
with Durfee square
p_{I_{1}}=p-1
,
n(I_{1})=(p-1)(t-p)
and
I_{1}^{\prime}=(\overbrace{p-1,\cdots,p-1}^{t-p+1},\overbrace{p+1,\cdots,p+1}^{p-1})
.

•

I_{2}:=(t,t,\overbrace{t+1,\cdots,t+1}^{p-2})
for which
p_{I_{2}}=p
,
n(I_{2})=p(t-p)
and
I_{2}^{\prime}=(\overbrace{p,\cdots,p}^{t-p+2},\overbrace{p+1,\cdots,p+1}^{p-2})

Therefore, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Sigma^{I_{1}^{\prime}}F&=&(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{\otimes p-1}\otimes\Sigma^{(0,\cdots,0,\overbrace{2,\cdots,2}^{p-1})}F,\\
\Sigma^{I_{2}^{\prime}}F&=&(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{\otimes p}\otimes\Sigma^{(0,\cdots,0,\overbrace{1,\cdots,1}^{p-2})}F\\
&\cong&(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{\otimes p}\otimes\bigwedge^{p-2}F,\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Sigma^{(I_{1})^{*}}G^{*}&=&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes p-1}\otimes\Sigma^{(0,\cdots,0,\overbrace{1,\cdots,1}^{p-1})}G^{*}\\
&\cong&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes p-1}\otimes\bigwedge^{p-1}G^{*},\text{ and }\\
\Sigma^{(I_{2})^{*}}G^{*}&=&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes p-2}\otimes\Sigma^{(0,2,\cdots,2)}G^{*}\\
&\cong&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes p}\otimes\Sigma^{(-2,0,\cdots,0)}G^{*}\\
&\cong&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes p}\otimes S_{2}G.\end{array}

In particular, for
p=c=2
, we obtain:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Sigma^{I_{1}^{\prime}}F&=&\bigwedge^{t}F\otimes S_{2}F\cong(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{\otimes 2}\otimes\bigwedge^{t}F^{*}\otimes S_{2}F,\\
\Sigma^{I_{2}^{\prime}}F&=&(\bigwedge^{t}F)^{\otimes 2},\\
\Sigma^{(I_{1})^{*}}G^{*}&=&\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*}\otimes G^{*}\cong(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes 2}\otimes\bigwedge^{t}G,\text{ and }\\
\Sigma^{(I_{2})^{*}}G^{*}&=&(\bigwedge^{t+1}G^{*})^{\otimes 2}\otimes S_{2}G.\end{array}

Thus, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{L}^{-p(p+1)+2}(t+1-p,\varphi)&:=&(\Sigma^{(I_{1})^{*}}G^{*}\otimes\Sigma^{I_{1}^{\prime}}F)\oplus(\Sigma^{(I_{2})}G^{*}\otimes\Sigma^{I_{2}^{\prime}}F)\\
&\cong&\big(S_{2}G\oplus\bigwedge^{t}G\otimes S_{2}F\otimes\bigwedge^{t}F^{*}\big)\otimes R(-2\ell).\end{array}

Since, for
c=p=2
, we have
A^{2}(F)=\coker(\bigwedge^{t-2}F\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{t-1}F\otimes F^{*})\cong S_{2}F^{*}\otimes\bigwedge^{t}F
, we have got what we wanted to prove.
∎

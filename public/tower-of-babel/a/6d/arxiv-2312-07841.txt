On the Dynamics Under the Unhinged Loss and Beyond Thanks: The corresponding author
Xiong Zhoucszx@hit.edu.cn, Xiangyang Ji xyji@tsinghua.edu.cn

Abstract
Recent works have studied implicit biases in deep learning, especially the behavior of last-layer features and classifier weights. However, they usually need to simplify the intermediate dynamics under gradient flow or gradient descent due to the intractability of loss functions and model architectures. In this paper, we introduce the unhinged loss, a concise loss function, that offers more mathematical opportunities to analyze the closed-form dynamics while requiring as few simplifications or assumptions as possible. The unhinged loss allows for considering more practical techniques, such as time-vary learning rates and feature normalization. Based on the layer-peeled model that views last-layer features as free optimization variables, we conduct a thorough analysis in the unconstrained, regularized, and spherical constrained cases, as well as the case where the neural tangent kernel remains invariant. To bridge the performance of the unhinged loss to that of Cross-Entropy (CE), we investigate the scenario of fixing classifier weights with a specific structure, (e.g., a simplex equiangular tight frame). Our analysis shows that these dynamics converge exponentially fast to a solution depending on the initialization of features and classifier weights. These theoretical results not only offer valuable insights, including explicit feature regularization and rescaled learning rates for enhancing practical training with the unhinged loss, but also extend their applicability to other loss functions. Finally, we empirically demonstrate these theoretical results and insights through extensive experiments.

Deep learning with neural networks has achieved great success in a variety of tasks

)
,
which, however, is not entirely understood in the interpolation and generalization of the learned models

;

;
Zeiler 2012

, play a crucial role in the training of deep neural networks, but lack convincing explanations due to the complexity of multilayered architectures. Recent works are devoted to simplifying modeling to better understand the behavior of DNNs,

and then to gain insights for new algorithms, theoretical, and experimental investigations.

To better understand the implicit regularization that improves the generalization of trained models in deep learning, many studies have investigated the implicit bias of gradient descent
(
Hardt et al. 2016
;
Sekhari et al. 2021
)
, with an emphasis on the behavior of linear predictors (or called classifiers) over linearly separable data
(
Soudry et al. 2018
;
Gunasekar et al. 2018
;
Nacson et al. 2019
;
Ji and Telgarsky 2019
;
Ji et al. 2020
;
Shamir 2021
)
. In particular,
Soudry et al. 2018
demonstrated that gradient descent iterates under exponentially-tailed loss minimization on separable data are biased toward
\ell_{2}
-maximum-margin solutions and that continuing to optimize can still lead to performance improvements, even if the validation loss increases. Further,
Ji et al. 2020
showed that the gradient descent path and the algorithm-independent regularization path converge to the same direction for general losses.
Shamir 2021
formally proved that standard gradient methods never overfit on separable data. These works impressively expose the implicit regularization induced by optimization algorithms and help to understand the generalization of the learned models, but they
mainly focus on the behavior of linear classifiers
that is only the last layer of neural networks, while the classifier actually interacts strongly with the features produced by many nonlinear layers and parameterized layers. Thus, the relevant conclusions do not always apply to deep learning. For example, in
(
Soudry et al. 2018
)
, the convergence rate of gradient descent is rather slow, wherein for almost all datasets, the distance to the maximum-margin solution decreases only as
O(1/\log t)
, and in some degenerate datasets, the rate further slows down to
O(\log\log t/\log t)
.
However, the training of DNNs typically takes only a few hundred epochs. In this paper, we show that exponential convergence is more realistic.

This paper is closely related to another research line which emerged after the empirical discovery of Neural Collapse by
Papyan et al. 2020
. This phenomenon precisely characterizes a pervasive inductive bias of both features and linear classifiers at the terminal phase of training, and has opened a rich area of exploring this area with simplified mathematical frameworks
(
Mixon et al. 2022
;
Fang et al. 2021b
;
Lu and Steinerberger 2022
;
Galanti et al. 2021
;
Fang et al. 2021a
;
Zhu et al. 2021
;
Hui et al. 2022
;
Tirer and Bruna 2022
;
Lu and Steinerberger 2022
;
Kothapalli et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2022b
)
. Neural collapse provides a clear view of how the last-layer features and linear classifiers behave after interpolation and enables us to understand the benefit of achieving zero training error in terms of generalization and robustness
(
Poggio and Liao 2020
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Kornblith et al. 2021
;
Thrampoulidis et al. 2022
;
Gao et al. 2023
)
, but the intermediate dynamics have remained challenging to analyze due to the intractability of cross entropy (CE). To alleviate this issue, some studies
(
Mixon et al. 2022
;
Han et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2022a
;
Tirer and Bruna 2022
;
Kothapalli et al. 2022
;
Xu et al. 2023
)
have explored the more tractable mean squared error (MSE) loss, which performs comparably to those trained with CE
(
Demirkaya et al. 2020
;
Hui and Belkin 2021
)
. However, these studies
still need to make additional simplifications or assumptions for intermediate dynamics
. For instance,
Mixon et al. 2022
formulate the gradient flow of the unconstrained feature model as a nonlinear ordinary differential equation and then linearize the equation by claiming that nonlinear terms are negligible for models initialized near the origin.
Han et al. 2022
assume that the gradient flow is along the central path which requires the linear classifier to stay MSE-optimal for features throughout the dynamics. Furthermore,
Tirer et al. 2023
delve into a more practical model, demonstrating the exponential decay of within-class variability. They assume that features remain in close proximity to predefined features under MSE loss function, but note that the gradient flow is still essential along the “central path”. Therefore, MSE is still not simple enough to derive exact dynamics in certain mathematical frameworks, making it difficult to grasp and bridge the gap between the modeling and practical optimization.

Main Theoretical Results

In this section, we begin with the commonly used assumption that treats last-layer features as free optimization variables. We then conduct a comprehensive analysis of the closed-form dynamics of last-layer features and prototypes under the unhinged loss in various scenarios, including unconstrained, regularized, prototype-anchored, spherical constrained, and NTK-invariant cases. Additionally, we provide convergence analyses for each case, revealing a surprising result: all cases exhibit exponential convergence.

3.5
The NTK-Invariant Case

We further perform some analysis in the case where the neural tangent kernel
\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}{\bm{H}}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}{\bm{H}}

(
Jacot et al. 2018
;
Chizat et al. 2019
;
Yang and Hu 2020
;
Yang and Hu 2021
)
is assumed to be invariant during training. We prove that the unhinged loss has more potential to derive exact dynamics. Specifically, consider the last-layer feature
{\bm{h}}_{i,c}={\bm{f}}_{\bm{\Theta}}({\bm{x}}_{i,c})
extracted from the example
{\bm{x}}_{i,c}
. When DNNs are trained using gradient descent to minimize the composition
\mathcal{L}\circ\bm{W}\circ\bm{f}_{\bm{\Theta}}
, we have

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}(t+1)-\bm{\Theta}(t)

\displaystyle=-\eta\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\mathcal{L}\big|_{\bm{\Theta}=\bm{\Theta}(t)},

(22)

\displaystyle\bm{W}(t+1)-\bm{W}(t)

\displaystyle=-\eta\nabla_{\bm{W}}\mathcal{L}\big|_{\bm{W}=\bm{W}(t)}.

According to the first-order Taylor expansion, we obtain

\bm{H}(t+1)-\bm{H}(t)\approx\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{H}^{\top}[\bm{\Theta}(t+1)-\bm{\Theta}(t)].

(23)

For the unhinged loss, we can obtain the following closed-form dynamics

Theorem 8
.

Assume that the neural tangent kernel
\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{H}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{H}
remains invariant during iterations. Let
{\bm{z}}(t)
denote the row-first vectorization of
\begin{pmatrix}\bm{H}(t)&0\\
0&\bm{W}(t)\end{pmatrix}
,
\bm{B}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\bm{M}^{\top}\\
\bm{M}&0\end{pmatrix}
, and
\bm{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{H}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{H}\\
\mathbf{I}_{C}&0\end{pmatrix}
. Considering the eigendecomposition
\bm{A}=\bm{U}_{\bm{A}}\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{A}}\bm{U}_{\bm{A}}^{-1}
and
\bm{B}=\bm{U}_{\bm{B}}\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{B}}\bm{U}_{\bm{B}}^{-1}

{\bm{C}}\bm{z}(t)=\exp[(\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{A}}\otimes\bm{\Lambda}_{\bm{B}})t]{\bm{C}}\bm{z}(0),

(24)

where
{\bm{C}}=\bm{U}_{\bm{B}}^{-1}\bm{U}_{\bm{A}}^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{I}

Though Theorem
8
does not imply properties of Neural Collapse, it reveals a specific dynamics under the unhinged loss, which exhibits an exponential convergence rate, contingent upon the assumption that the neural tangent kernel remains constant. To demonstrate the mathematical simplicity of the unhinged loss, we consider the dynamics of the MSE loss
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{CN}\|\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{H}+\bm{b}\mathbf{1}_{CN}^{\top}-\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{N}^{\top}\|_{F}^{2}
for comparison, and similarly derive that

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{H}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{\Theta}}\bm{H}\bm{W}(t)\bm{C}(t),\quad\bm{W}^{\prime}(t)

Through the above analysis, it is clear to see that the unhinged loss offers more mathematical opportunities than the MSE loss to analyze the closed-form dynamics under the assumption of an invariant neural tangent kernel.

4.3
Rescaled Learning Rate for the Spherical Case

In this subsection, we propose a strategy of rescaling the learning rate for the spherical case in which the features are performed
\ell_{2}
normalization.

As shown in Theorem
7
and its proof, the convergence behavior in the spherical constrained case is characterized by the term
\frac{\eta(t)}{\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}(t)\|_{2}}
, when
\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\|_{2}
is monotonically increasing and thus leads to a slower convergence rate. This result underscores the influence of the feature norm on the gradient dynamics in spherical case. Also, we observe that the training plots in the spherical case exhibit a slower rate in comparison to the CE loss in Figure 2. To bridge the performance of the unhinged loss to that of CE, we propose a straightforward approach—rescaling the learning rates based on feature norms, that is, scaling up the learning rate
\eta(t)
with the feature norm
\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\|_{2}
for each example
15
15

15

We can also implement this strategy by rescaling the loss, but the multiplicative feature norm stops gradients.
. Though we change the learning rates, we still guarantee the convergence analysis in Theorem
7
if
\eta(t)
is non-increasing and satisfies
\frac{\eta(0)(1+\gamma)}{CN}\leq\frac{1}{\|{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}}
.

Experimental Results

To confirm the efficacy of the rescaled learning rate in accelerating convergence for the spherical case, we experiment with the unhinged loss (FNPAL). As illustrated in Figure
2
, the utilization of the unhinged loss (FNPAL) with the rescaled learning rate (RLR) clearly demonstrates accelerated convergence in comparison to its counterpart without RLR.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the unhinged loss as a surrogate to analyze the behavior of last-layer features and prototypes. Due to the conciseness of the unhinged loss, we derived exact dynamics under gradient descent in various scenarios, including unconstrained, regularized, and spherical constrained cases, as well as the case with an invariant neural tangent kernel. Furthermore, we demonstrated that these dynamics converge exponentially to a specific solution depending on the initialization. Inspired by these results, we proposed additional insights for improvements, such as using prototype-anchored learning with the unhinged loss to bridge its performance to that of cross-entropy loss, employing explicit feature regularization to address over-confidence, and implementing a rescaled learning rate to accelerate convergence in the spherical case. Finally, we validated these theoretical results and insights through extensive experiments, covering numerical analysis, visual classification, imbalanced learning, and out-of-distribution detection. We anticipate that the unhinged loss will serve as a valuable tool for the community to gain a deeper understanding of the behavior of deep neural networks, extending beyond the scope of this paper.

Appendix A
Clarification for the Unhinged Loss

In this section, we provide some clarification for the unhinged loss as follows.

•

About the contribution.
As stated in the body of this paper, the unhinged loss serves as an alternative object of study, which has the advantage of being linear in the outputs of the model. Thus, it can provide more opportunities to analyze more concrete behaviors and explore the shortcomings of the existing modeling. The main contribution of this work is to glimpse into deep learning through a concise form under the unhinged loss, which enjoys the great advantage to simplify theoretical research very much. Moreover, it is not just a tool for theoretical analysis. To make the unhinged loss practical, we further provide several insights theoretically and empirically, including using prototype-anchored learning with the unhinged loss (Sec.
4.1
), applying explicit regularization on features (Sec.
4.2
), rescaling the learning rates for the spherical constrained case (Sec.
4.3
), and other potential insights (Sec.
D
).

•

About the criterion of correct classification.
The value of the unhinged loss in
Equation

1
cannot be directly used as the criterion to judge whether a sample is classified correctly since the unhinged loss can be arbitrary negative in unconstrained cases, since the gradient of the unhinged loss if
\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial{\bm{h}}}=-({\bm{w}}_{y}-\gamma\sum_{i\neq y}{\bm{w}}_{i})
cannot converge to zero unless
{\bm{w}}_{y}-\gamma\sum_{i\neq y}{\bm{w}}_{i}=0
for all
y
. This is also the main reason why the unhinged loss cannot be used directly for practical training. To overcome this drawback, we turn to limit the rapid growth of norms. For instance, in the spherical constrained case, the features are constrained to the unit sphere, and class prototypes are fixed as a simplex equiangular tight frame (ETF). Moreover, under this case, the unhinged loss would be lower bounded by
-(1+\gamma)
, and thus can be used in practical training (as shown in
Figure

2
). We can also derive that
L_{\gamma}<-(1+\gamma)\sqrt{\frac{C-2}{2(C-1}}\Rightarrow{\bm{w}}_{y}^{\top}{\bm{h}}>\max_{i\neq y}{\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{h}}
when these prototypes are fixed as a simplex ETF, so the sample can be correctly classified if the value of the unhinged loss is less than
(1+\gamma)\sqrt{\frac{C-2}{2(C-1}}
.

•

About other losses.

1) For the CE and multi-binary CE loss, we have the following inequality.

\displaystyle L_{\gamma}({\bm{W}}{\bm{h}}+{\bm{b}},y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\log(1+\exp(-{\bm{w}}_{y}^{\top}{\bm{h}}-b_{y}))+\gamma\sum_{j\neq y}\log(1+\exp({\bm{w}}_{j}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{j})),

\displaystyle(\gamma(C-1)-1)({\bm{w}}_{y}^{\top}+b_{y}){\bm{h}}-\gamma(C-1)\log\frac{\exp({\bm{w}}_{y}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{y})}{\sum_{i=1}^{C}\exp({\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{i})}\bigg\}.

2) For the Wasserstein loss in
Arjovsky et al. 2017
, we can find that the empirical form of the unhinged loss
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}({\bm{w}}_{y}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{y}-\gamma\sum_{i\neq y}({\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{i}))
resembles the Wasserstein loss
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_{r}}[f_{w}(x)]-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim P_{\theta}}[f_{w}(x)]
if we regard positive logits
{\bm{w}}_{y}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{y}
as scores from
P_{r}
and negative logits
{\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{i}
(
i\neq y
) as scores from
P_{\theta}
. The main difference is that in the unhinged loss the two logits
{\bm{w}}_{y}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{y}
and
{\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{h}}+b_{i}
(
i\neq y
) are computed from
{\bm{h}}
(
i.e.
, the same input), rather than being sampled separably from two distributions as done in Wasserstein loss. Moreover, the unhinged loss does not constrain the values of these scores, in contrast the Wasserstein loss requires
f_{w}
to be Lipschitz continuous.

Lemma
1
(Neural Collapse of The Unhinged Loss).

For norm-bounded prototypes and features, i.e.,
\|{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}\leq E_{1}
and
\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\|_{2}\leq E_{2}
,
\forall i\in[N],\forall c\in[C]
, the global minimizer of
\frac{1}{CN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{C}L_{\gamma}({\bm{W}}{\bm{h}}_{i,c},y_{i,c})
implies neural collapse when
p\geq C-1
. More specifically, the global minimizer is uniquely obtained at
\frac{{\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{j}}{\|{\bm{w}}_{i}\|_{2}\|{\bm{w}}_{j}\|_{2}}=-\frac{1}{C-1}
,
\forall i\neq j
,
\frac{{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c}}{\|{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}\|_{2}\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\|_{2}}=1
,
\|{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}=E_{1}
, and
\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\|_{2}=E_{2}
,
\forall i\in[N],

\forall c\in[C]
.

Proof.

The proof is based on lower bounding the objective
\frac{1}{CN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{C}L_{\gamma}({\bm{W}}{\bm{h}}_{i,c},y_{i,c})
by a sequence of inequalities that holds if and only if the solution forms Neural Collapse
(
Papyan et al. 2020
)
. Let
\hat{\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C}{\bm{w}}_{c}
, according to the definition of
L_{SM}
, we have

\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{CN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{C}L_{SM}({\bm{W}}{\bm{h}}_{i,c},y_{i,c})\\
=&\frac{1}{CN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{C}(-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c}+\gamma\sum_{j\neq y_{i,c}}{\bm{w}}_{j}^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c})\\
\geq&\frac{1}{CN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{C}(-E_{1}E_{2}+\gamma(C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}})^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c})\\
\geq&-\frac{\gamma E_{2}}{CN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\|C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}\|_{2}-E_{1}E_{2}\\
\geq&-\gamma E_{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{CN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\|C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}\|_{2}^{2}}-E_{1}E_{2}\\
=&-\gamma E_{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\|{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}^{2}-C^{2}\|\hat{{\bm{w}}}\|_{2}^{2}}-E_{1}E_{2}\\
\geq&-(1+\gamma)E_{1}E_{2}\end{aligned},

where the first and second inequalities are based on the facts that
{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\leq E_{1}E_{2}
and
(C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}})^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\geq-E_{2}\|C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}\|_{2}
, respectively. In the third equality, we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the last inequality we use the facts that
\|{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}\leq E_{1}
and
\|\hat{{\bm{w}}}\|_{2}\geq 0
.

According the above derivation, the equality holds if and only if
\forall i\in[N]
,
\forall c\in[C]
,
{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c}=E_{1}E_{2}
,
(C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}})^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c}=-E_{2}\|C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}\|_{2}
,
\|C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}=\|C\hat{{\bm{w}}}-{\bm{w}}_{C}\|_{2}
,
\|{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}=E_{1}
, and
\|\hat{{\bm{w}}}\|_{2}=0
. These equations can be simplified as
\frac{{\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{j}}{\|{\bm{w}}_{i}|_{2}\|{\bm{w}}_{j}\|_{2}}=-\frac{1}{C-1}
,
\forall i\neq j
,
\frac{{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}^{\top}{\bm{h}}_{i,c}}{\|{\bm{w}}_{y_{i,c}}\|_{2}\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\|_{2}}=1
,
\|{\bm{w}}_{c}\|_{2}=E_{1}
, and
\|{\bm{h}}_{i,c}\|_{2}=E_{2}
,
\forall i\in[N],

\forall c\in[C]
, which also implies neural collapse.
∎

The following five subspaces are orthogonal to each other and satisfy
{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times C(N+1)}={\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{+}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{-}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{+}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{-}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{3}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{\epsilon}:=\{({\bm{H}},{\bm{W}}):{\bm{H}}={\epsilon}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}({\bm{W}}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}^{\top}),{\bm{W}}\bm{1}_{C}=0,{\bm{W}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times C}\},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{\epsilon}:=\{({\bm{H}},{\bm{W}}):{\bm{H}}={\epsilon}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{h}}\bm{1}^{\top}_{CN},{\bm{W}}={\bm{h}}\bm{1}_{C}^{\top},{\bm{h}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{E}}_{3}:=\{({\bm{H}},{\bm{W}}):{\bm{H}}({\bm{I}}_{C}\otimes\bm{1}_{N})=0,{\bm{W}}=0,{\bm{H}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times CN}\}.

{\epsilon}\in\{\pm 1\}

Proof.

For

For
({\bm{H}}_{1},{\bm{W}}_{1})=(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{h}}_{1}\bm{1}_{CN}^{\top},{\bm{h}}_{1}\bm{1}_{C}^{\top})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{+}
and
({\bm{H}}_{2},{\bm{W}}_{2})=(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{h}}_{2}\bm{1}_{CN}^{\top},{\bm{h}}_{2}\bm{1}_{C}^{\top})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{-}
, we have

{\bm{H}}_{1}{\bm{H}}_{2}^{\top}+{\bm{W}}_{1}{\bm{W}}_{2}^{\top}=-\frac{1}{N}{\bm{h}}_{1}\bm{1}_{CN}^{\top}\bm{1}_{CN}{\bm{h}}_{2}^{\top}+{\bm{h}}_{1}\bm{1}_{C}^{\top}\bm{1}_{C}{\bm{h}}_{2}^{\top}=0.

For
({\bm{H}}_{1},{\bm{W}}_{1})=({\epsilon}_{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}({\bm{W}}_{1}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}^{\top}),{\bm{W}}_{1})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{\epsilon}
and
({\bm{H}}_{2},{\bm{W}}_{2})=({\epsilon}_{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{h}}_{2}\bm{1}_{CN}^{\top},{\bm{h}}_{2}\bm{1}_{C}^{\top})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{\epsilon}
, since
{\bm{W}}_{1}\bm{1}_{C}=0

, we have

{\bm{H}}_{1}{\bm{H}}_{2}^{\top}+{\bm{W}}_{1}{\bm{W}}_{2}^{\top}=\frac{{\epsilon}_{1}{\epsilon}_{2}}{N}({\bm{W}}_{1}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}^{\top})\bm{1}_{CN}{\bm{h}}_{2}^{\top}+{\bm{W}}_{1}\bm{1}_{C}{\bm{h}}_{2}^{\top}=0.

For
({\bm{H}}_{1},{\bm{W}}_{1})=({\epsilon}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}({\bm{W}}_{1}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}^{\top}),{\bm{W}}_{1})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{\epsilon}
and
({\bm{H}}_{2},{\bm{W}}_{2})=({\bm{H}}_{2},0)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{3}

{\bm{H}}_{1}{\bm{H}}_{2}^{\top}+{\bm{W}}_{1}{\bm{W}}_{2}^{\top}=\frac{{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{N}}({\bm{W}}_{1}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}^{\top}){\bm{H}}_{2}^{\top}=\frac{{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{W}}_{1}({\bm{H}}_{2}({\bm{I}}_{C}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}))^{\top}=0.

({\bm{H}}_{1},{\bm{W}}_{1})=({\epsilon}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{h}}_{1}\bm{1}_{CN}^{\top},{\bm{h}}_{1}\bm{1}_{C}^{\top})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{\epsilon}
and
({\bm{H}}_{2},{\bm{W}}_{2})=({\bm{H}}_{2},0)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{3}

{\bm{H}}_{2}({\bm{I}}_{C}\otimes\bm{1}_{N})=0
, we have

{\bm{H}}_{1}{\bm{H}}_{2}^{\top}+{\bm{W}}_{1}{\bm{W}}_{2}^{\top}=\frac{{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{h}}_{1}\bm{1}_{CN}^{\top}{\bm{H}}_{2}^{\top}=\frac{{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{h}}_{1}({\bm{H}}_{2}({\bm{I}}_{C}\otimes\bm{1}_{N})\bm{1}_{C}^{\top})^{\top}=0

\mathop{\text{dim}}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{+}=\mathop{\text{dim}}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{-}=p(C-1),\quad\mathop{\text{dim}}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{+}=\mathop{\text{dim}}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{-}=p,\quad\mathop{\text{dim}}{\mathcal{E}}_{3}=pC(N-1).

Since these dimensions sum to

{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times C(N+1)}={\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{+}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{1}^{-}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{+}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{-}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{3}
.

.

Proof.

As aforementioned, due to the existence of the limit of
\alpha_{t}
, we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{t}=0
or
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{t}=1
. Thus, we just need to prove that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{t}=1
as
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{t}=0
.

When
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{t}=0
, then there exists
\tau
, such that
\forall t>\tau
,
\alpha_{t}\leq 1
.

According to the iterations in
Equation

84
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\beta_{t+1}^{2}}{\alpha_{t+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}-(\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}^{2})^{2}}{\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}-\beta_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{4}-2\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}+2\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}+2\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}^{3}}{\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\beta_{t}^{2}+\alpha_{t}^{2}(\beta_{t}^{2}-\beta_{t}^{4})-2\alpha_{t}(\beta_{t}-\beta_{t}^{3})}{\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\beta_{t}^{2}}{\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}}\cdot(1+\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}-2\alpha_{t}\beta_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\beta_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\cdot\frac{(1-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t})^{2}}{\xi_{t}},

then
\forall t>\tau
,

\displaystyle 1-\beta_{t+1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{t+1}\cdot\frac{1-\beta_{0}^{2}}{\alpha_{0}}\prod_{i=0}^{t}\frac{(1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}}{\xi_{i}}

(86)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{t+1}\cdot\frac{\eta(0)(1-\beta_{0}^{2})}{\alpha_{0}\eta(t+1)}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{\tau}(1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}\cdot\prod_{i=\tau+1}^{t}(1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{t+1}\cdot\frac{\eta(0)(1-\beta_{0}^{2})}{\varepsilon\alpha_{0}}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{\tau}(1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})^{2},

where the inequality is based on the fact that
1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\in(0,1]
when
0\leq\beta_{0}\leq\beta_{i}\leq 1
,
\eta(t+1)\geq\varepsilon
, and
\alpha_{i}\leq 1
for
i>\tau
. Since
\frac{\eta(0)(1-\beta_{0}^{2})}{\varepsilon\alpha_{0}}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{\tau}(1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}
is a constant, we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}1-\beta_{t+1}^{2}\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{t+1}\cdot\frac{\eta(0)(1-\beta_{0}^{2})}{\varepsilon\alpha_{0}}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{\tau}(1-\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}=0,

as
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{t+1}=0
. This reveals
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{t}^{2}=1
. Furthermore, since
\beta_{t}\geq 0
, we then have
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{t}=1
.
∎

Next, we are going to prove
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{t}=1
when
-1<\beta_{0}<0
. According to Lemma
11
, we just need prove that
\exists\tau>0
,
s.t.
,
\beta_{\tau}\geq 0
.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
\beta_{t}<0
for all
t>0
, we then have
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{t}=0
. As a consequence, we obtain

\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}^{2}<0,\ \forall t\geq 0,

(87)

and we know that
\exists t^{\prime}>0
, such that

\alpha_{t}<\frac{\varepsilon}{\eta(0)}\alpha_{0}(1-\beta_{0}^{2}),\ \forall t\geq t^{\prime}.

(88)

According to the iterations in
Equation

84
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\alpha_{t+1}(1-\beta_{t+1}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}}{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{(\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}^{2})^{2}}{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}-(\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}^{2})^{2}}{(1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}(1-\beta_{t}^{2})\left(\frac{1-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}}{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\xi_{t}\alpha_{t}(1-\beta_{t}^{2})\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{t}(\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}^{2})}{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}}\right)^{2},

Since
1-\frac{\alpha_{t}(\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\beta_{t}^{2})}{1+\alpha_{t}^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}}\geq 1
, then for
t\geq t^{\prime}
,

\alpha_{t}\geq\alpha_{t}(1-\beta_{t}^{2})\geq\xi_{t-1}\alpha_{t-1}(1-\beta_{t-1}^{2})\geq\ldots\geq\alpha_{0}(1-\beta_{0}^{2})\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\xi_{i}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{\eta(0)}\alpha_{0}(1-\beta_{0}^{2}),

(89)

which contradicts the fact in
Equation

88
. Thus,
\exists\tau>0
, s.t.
\beta_{\tau}\geq 0
. Consider the dynamical system with an initial time
\tau
, we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{t}=1
according to Lemma
11
.

To sum up, we have proven that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{t}=1
when
\beta_{0}>-1
and
\alpha_{0}>0
.
∎

\displaystyle\argmin_{{\bm{Z}}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\|{\bm{Z}}\|_{F}^{2}-\tfrac{2{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle{\bm{Z}},{\bm{H}}_{i}\rangle+\|{\bm{H}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\bm{Z}}-{\bm{W}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\argmin_{{\bm{Z}}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\|{\bm{Z}}-\tfrac{{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bm{H}}_{i}\|_{F}^{2}-\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bm{H}}_{i}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\bm{H}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\bm{Z}}-{\bm{W}}\|_{F}^{2}

.

{\mathcal{S}}_{b}
is the set of samples whose prediction scores fall into Bin-
b

\mathop{\text{acc}}(\cdot)
and
\mathop{\text{conf}}(\cdot)
denote the accuracy and predicted confidence of
{\mathcal{S}}_{b}
, respectively.

Appendix D
Other Potential Insights

D.1
A Good Initialization of Prototypes

As depicted in
Section

3
and
B.9
, the dynamics under the unhinged loss is dependent on the initialization of both features and prototypes, such as
\Pi_{1}^{+}{\bm{Z}}_{0}=(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}({\bm{P}}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}^{\top}),{\bm{P}})
, where
{\bm{P}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}{\bm{H}}_{0}({\bm{I}}_{C}\otimes\bm{1}_{N})+{\bm{W}}_{0}\right)({\bm{I}}_{C}-\frac{1}{C}\bm{1}_{C}\bm{1}_{C}^{\top})
. However, these features
{\bm{H}}_{0}
extracted from a dataset by some nonlinear layers and parameterized layers are practically intractable, but we can elaborately initialize
{\bm{W}}_{0}
and highlight its role in the whole. To do this, we consider two ways: (1) Initializing the structure of
{\bm{W}}_{0}
. Inspired by the neural collapse solution that maximizes class separation, we can initialize
{\bm{W}}_{0}
as this structure,
i.e.
,
\hat{{\bm{w}}}_{i}^{\top}\hat{{\bm{w}}}_{j}=\frac{-1}{C-1}
,
\forall i\neq j
; (2) Increasing the importance of
{\bm{W}}_{0}
. A simple strategy is scaling up
{\bm{W}}_{0}
, thereby implicitly weakening the importance of
{\bm{H}}_{0}
.
However, it is difficult to handle the initialization of features because they are obtained by a complex processing a large dataset, thus we seek to initialize the prototypes in the last layer of the network.

D.2
Refined Decision-makings

Recalling the rule—
\argmax_{c^{\prime}}\langle{\bm{w}}_{c^{\prime}},{\bm{h}}\rangle+b_{c^{\prime}}
that makes decision by selecting the class with the largest logit (where the inner product
\langle{\bm{w}}_{c^{\prime}},{\bm{h}}\rangle
is dominant), which may not be good to directly use the learned features and prototypes, since learning with the unhinged loss within limited iterations (that means
\zeta_{1}(t)<\infty
) will introduce some residual
\bm{\Delta}(t)
caused by gradient descent regardless of the unconstrained case or regularized case.

Example 18
.

If we add a perturbation
\Delta
for all features while adding
s\Delta
for all prototypes, then the perturbed decision-making will be
\arg\max_{c^{\prime}}\langle{\bm{w}}_{c^{\prime}}+s\Delta,{\bm{h}}+\Delta\rangle+b_{c^{\prime}}
, which may not be equivalent to
\argmax_{c^{\prime}}\langle{\bm{w}}_{c^{\prime}},{\bm{h}}\rangle+b_{c^{\prime}}
.

D.3
Adjusted Sample Margin Loss

As aforementioned in
Section

3.4
and the proof of Theorem
7
, we will encounter zero gradients when the cosine similarity
\hat{{\bm{w}}}_{y}^{\top}\hat{{\bm{h}}}
is
-1
or
1
, so we can adjust the loss to avoid the issue by to
{\bm{w}}_{y}
and accelerate convergence:

L_{\gamma}^{\prime}({\bm{W}}\hat{{\bm{h}}},y)=\begin{cases}L_{\gamma}({\bm{W}}\hat{{\bm{h}}},y)&\text{if }\hat{{\bm{w}}}_{y}^{\top}\hat{{\bm{h}}}\geq-1+\varepsilon,\\
-(1+\gamma)({\bm{w}}_{y}+\bm{\delta})^{\top}\hat{{\bm{h}}}&\text{if }\hat{{\bm{w}}}_{y}^{\top}\hat{{\bm{h}}}<-1+\varepsilon,\end{cases}

(104)

where
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is a hyperparameter and
\bm{\delta}=-\left(1+\frac{\hat{{\bm{w}}}_{y}^{\top}\hat{{\bm{h}}}\sqrt{1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}}{(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{1-(\hat{{\bm{w}}}_{y}^{\top}\hat{{\bm{h}}})^{2}}}\right)({\bm{w}}_{y}+\hat{{\bm{h}}}\hat{{\bm{h}}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}_{y})
(performed with a stop-gradient) satisfying
\frac{({\bm{w}}_{y}+\bm{\delta})^{\top}\hat{{\bm{h}}}}{\|{\bm{w}}_{y}+\bm{\delta}\|_{2}}=-1+\varepsilon
.

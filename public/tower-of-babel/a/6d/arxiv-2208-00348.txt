Random Networks with Heterogeneous Reciprocity
Tiandong Wang, Sidney Resnick

Abstract
Users of social networks display diversified behavior and online habits. For instance, a user’s tendency to reply to a post can depend on the user and the person posting. For convenience, we group users into aggregated behavioral patterns, focusing here on the tendency to reply to or reciprocate messages. The reciprocity feature in social networks reflects the information exchange among users. We study the properties of a preferential attachment model with heterogeneous reciprocity levels, give the growth rate of model edge counts, and prove convergence of empirical degree frequencies to a limiting distribution. This limiting distribution is not only multivariate regularly varying, but also has the property of hidden regular variation.

1
Introduction

Social networks have grown rapidly and users are exhibiting different
interaction and behavioral patterns on platforms like Facebook and
Twitter. Reciprocity is one such pattern and helps characterize
information exchange between social network users

[
12
,
18
]
. For instance, consider
the network of Facebook wall posts: a directed edge from user A to
user B is formed when user A leaves a message on the Facebook wall of
user B, and a reciprocal edge from user B to user A is created if
and when user B replies to the message. Depending on both users’
behavioral features and networking habits (e.g. the closeness of
friendship, whether user A is broadcasting, the obsessiveness of user
B’s replying habits), the probability of generating a reciprocal edge
may vary across different pairs of users, and network modeling should
include such heterogeneity. A study in
[
11
]
shows
that online social networks tend to have a high proportion of
reciprocal edges, compared to other types of networks such as
biological networks, communication networks, software call graphs and
peer-to-peer networks. Due to the large and diversified user groups on
social networks, it is essential to incorporate heterogeneous
reciprocity levels into the modeling.

For the modeling of dynamic networks, the preferential attachment (PA)
model
[
5
,
14
]
is an appealing starting point. This model
captures the scale-free property of complex networks, where both in-
and out-degree distributions have Pareto-like tails

[
22
,
21
,
23
,
24
]
. Recently,

[
25
]
show that for a wide and realistic
range of model parameters,
the standard PA model in
[
5
]

generates networks with very small proportion of reciprocal edges,
deviating from the empirical observations in
[
11
]
.
This led to two consecutive studies
[
26
,
6
]
on the theoretical properties and the
estimation of a PA model with homogeneous reciprocity, where
reciprocal edges are generated by simply flipping a two-sided coin.
To capture the heterogeneous reciprocal patterns, we extend here the
model in
[
26
]
by dividing users into
K
different
behavioral groups, and the probability of generating a reciprocal
edge from a user in group
m
to a user in group
r
is

\rho_{m,r}\in(0,1)
,
m,r\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
.

We study three properties of the proposed PA model with heterogeneous
reciprocity. Under modest assumptions, we first analyze the
growth of the edge counts by identifying the almost sure limit for the
scaled number of edges emanating from and pointing to nodes of group
type
m
. Secondly, by embedding in- and out-degree sequences
into a family of multitype branching processes whose particles
are given group labels, we prove that the empirical frequencies of
nodes with in-degree
i
and out-degree
j
converge to a limiting
distribution
p_{i,j}
. Third, we show that the asymptotic limiting
distribution,
p_{i,j}
, is multivariate regularly varying with limit
measure concentrating on a ray and after removing large in- and
out-degree pairs close to the concentrating ray, we also detect hidden
regular variation
[
16
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. We start with a detailed description of the PA model with heterogeneous reciprocity levels in Section
1.1
. Section
2
studies the growth of edge counts, and in order
to ensure the convergence of scaled edge counts, sufficient
assumptions are imposed.
Also, we extend the embedding technique in
[
26
]
to a family of multitype branching processes with different group labels, and derive the limit of empirical degree frequencies in Section
3
. We then characterize the asymptotic dependence structure of large in- and out-degrees in Section
4
, and give concluding remarks in Section
5
.
Technical proofs of results in Section
2
are collected in Section
6
.

1.1
PA Model with Heterogeneous Reciprocity

The proposed model extends the directed preferential attachment (PA)
model studied in
[
14
,
5
,
23
,
24
]
by amending a mechanism that generates
a reciprocal edge with probabilities depending on characteristics of the node pair being connected by the edge. Because these
characteristics can be specific to the node pair, the model incorporates heterogeneous responding patterns of users in social networks.

We now specify a growing sequence of graphs.
Let
G(n)
be the graph after
n
steps with
V(n)
being the
set of nodes and
E(n)
being the set of edges in
G(n)
.
Attach to each node
v
a communication type
W_{v}
, where
\{W_{v},v\geq 1\}
are iid random variables, independent from the growth mechanism of the graph, with

\mathbb{P}(W_{v}=r)=\pi_{r},\qquad\text{for}\quad\sum_{r=1}^{K}\pi_{r}=1.

We imagine that
when a node is born, it flips a multi-sided coin to determine its communication type. Think of

\pi_{r}
as the percent of the users in a social network that have communication habits labeled as type
r
.
Let
W(n):=\{W_{v}:v\in V(n)\}
denote the set of group types for all nodes in
G(n)
.
Throughout we assume that the communication group of
node
v
is always observed upon its creation, and
remains unchanged afterwards.
In the rest of this paper, we assume
G(n)=(V(n),E(n),W(n))
, for
n\geq 0
.

Denote the cardinality of a discrete set
S
by
|S|
and initialize the model with graph
G(0)
, which consists of one node
(labeled as node 1) and a self-loop, with
V(0)=\{1\}
,

|V(0)|={1}
,
W(0)=\{W_{1}\}
, and
E(0)=\left\{(1,1)\right\}
.
For each new edge
(u,v)
with
W_{u}=r,W_{v}=m
, the reciprocity mechanism adds its reciprocal
counterpart
(v,u)
instantaneously with probability
\rho_{m,r}\in[0,1]
, for
m,r\in\{1,2,\ldots,K\}
.
Here
\rho_{m,r}
measures the probability of adding a reciprocal edge from a node in group
m
to a node in group
r
.
Note that the matrix
\bm{\rho}:=(\rho_{m,r})_{m,r}
is not
necessarily a stochastic matrix, but can be an arbitrary matrix in

M_{K\times K}([0,1])
, the set of all
K\times K
matrices with
entries belonging to
[0,1]
.
Later in Section
2
, we will give particular regularity conditions on
\bm{\rho}
to facilitate theoretical analysis.

Let
\bigl(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n),D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)\bigr)

be the in- and out-degrees of node
v\in V(n)
in
G(n)
, and
we use the convention that
D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)=D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)=0
if

v\notin V(n)
.
Let
\delta>0
be an offset parameter.
The evolution of the network
G(n+1)
from
G(n)
is described
as follows.

1.

With probability
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
add a new node
|V(n)|+1
with a directed edge
(|V(n)|+1,v)
,
where
v\in V(n)
is chosen with probability

\frac{D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{\sum_{v\in V(n)}(D^{\text{in}}_{w}(n)+\delta)}=\frac{D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|},

(1)

and update the node set
V(n+1)=V(n)\cup\{|V(n)|+1\}
and

W(n+1)=W(n)\cup\{W_{|V(n)|+1}\}
.
The new node
|V(n)|+1
belongs to group
r
with probability
\pi_{r}
.
If node
v
belongs to group
m
, then
a reciprocal edge
(v,|V(n)|+1)
is added
with probability
\rho_{m,r}
. Upon reciprocation, update the edge set as

E(n+1)=E(n)\cup\{(|V(n)|+1,v),(v,|V(n)|+1)\}
.
If the reciprocal edge is not created, set

E(n+1)=E(n)\cup\{(|V(n)|+1,v)\}
.

2.

With probability
\gamma\equiv 1-\alpha\in(0,1)
,
add a new node
|V(n)|+1
with a directed edge
(v,|V(n)|+1)
,
where
v\in V(n)
is chosen with probability

\frac{D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{\sum_{v\in V(n)}(D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta)}=\frac{D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|},

(2)

and update the node set
V(n+1)=V(n)\cup\{|V(n)|+1\}
,

W(n+1)=W(n)\cup\{W_{|V(n)|+1}\}
.
The new node
|V(n)|+1
belongs to group
r
with probability
\pi_{r}
.
If node
v
belongs to group
m
, then
a reciprocal edge
(|V(n)|+1,v)
is added
with probability
\rho_{r,m}
.
Upon reciprocation, update the edge set as

E(n+1)=E(n)\cup\{(v,|V(n)|+1,v),(|V(n)|+1,v)\}
.
If the reciprocal edge is not created, set
E(n+1)=E(n)\cup\{(v,|V(n)|+1)\}
.

Note that
|V(n)|=n+1
,
n\geq 0
,
since a new node is added at each step, and
the offset parameter
\delta
is assumed to be the same for
both in- and out-degrees.

2
Growth of Edge Counts

Heterogeneous reciprocity levels make analysis of the convergence of

|E(n)|/n
more complicated compared to the homogeneous case.
In Theorem
2.2
, we show the concentration of
|E(n)|
around

\mathbb{E}[|E(n)|]
, and then prove the convergence of
\mathbb{E}[|E(n)|]/n
,
thereby showing linear growth of the number of edges. We now
prepare for this result.

Write

\displaystyle\rho_{m\bullet}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{r=1}^{K}\rho_{m,r}\pi_{r}=\mathbb{P}[\text{Type
$m$ node sends reciprocal edge}],

\displaystyle\rho_{\bullet m}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{r=1}^{K}\rho_{r,m}\pi_{r}=\mathbb{P}[\text{Type $m$
node receives reciprocal edge}].

Let
\mathcal{G}_{n}
be denote the
\sigma
-field generated by observing the network evolution up to
n
steps, i.e.

\mathcal{G}_{n}=\sigma\left\{(V(k),E(k),W(k)):k=0,\ldots,n\right\}.

For
v\in V(n)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n+1)-D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\frac{D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}+\gamma\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{\bullet m}\frac{D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}\bm{1}_{\{W_{v}=m\}},

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n+1)-D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\frac{D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}+\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{m\bullet}\frac{D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}\bm{1}_{\{W_{v}=m\}}.

(4)

Let
V_{m}(n):=\{v\in V(n):W_{v}=m\}
, and note that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(n)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n+1)-D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)\right)\bm{1}_{\{W_{v}=m\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl(E(n+1)=E(n)\cup\{(|V(n)|+1,v)\},v\in V_{m}(n)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl(E(n+1)=E(n)\cup\{(v,|V(n)|+1),(|V(n)|+1,v)\},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad v\in V_{m}(n),\text{$(|V(n)|+1,v)$ is due to reciprocation.}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((|V(n)|+1,v)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),v\in V_{m}(n)\bigr).

(5)

Write
E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n):=\{(u,v)\in E(n):W_{v}=m\}
, and
E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n):=\{(v,u)\in E(n):W_{v}=m\}
.
We see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}

\displaystyle\left(|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n+1)|-|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((|V(n)|+1,v)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),v\in V_{m}(n)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((v,|V(n)|+1)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),W_{|V(n)|+1}=m,v\in V(n)\bigr),

and applying (
3
) and (
5
) gives

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(n)}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n+1)-D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)\right)\bm{1}_{\{W_{v}=m\}}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((v,|V(n)|+1)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),W_{|V(n)|+1}=m,v\in V(n)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\alpha\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((v,|V(n)|+1)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),W_{|V(n)|+1}=m,v\in V(n)\bigr).

(6)

For the third term in (
6
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((v,|V(n)|+1)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),W_{|V(n)|+1}=m,v\in V(n)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((v,|V(n)|+1)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),W_{|V(n)|+1}=m,v\in V(n),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{$(v,|V(n)|+1)$ is not due to reciprocation}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\bigl((v,|V(n)|+1)\in E(n+1)\setminus E(n),W_{|V(n)|+1}=m,v\in V(n),

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\text{$(v,|V(n)|+1)$ is due to reciprocation},W_{v}=r,\text{ for some $r$}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\gamma\pi_{m}+\alpha\pi_{m}\sum_{r=1}^{K}\rho_{r,m}\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{r}(n)|+\delta|V_{r}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}.

(7)

Therefore, combining (
6
) and (
7
) shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}

\displaystyle\left(|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n+1)|-|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}

\displaystyle+\gamma\pi_{m}+\alpha\pi_{m}\sum_{r=1}^{K}\rho_{r,m}\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{r}(n)|+\delta|V_{r}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}.

(8)

Following a similar argument, we use the relationship in (
4
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}

\displaystyle\left(|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n+1)|-|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}+\alpha\rho_{m\bullet}\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}

\displaystyle+\alpha\pi_{m}+\gamma\pi_{m}\sum_{r=1}^{K}\rho_{m,r}\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{r}(n)|+\delta|V_{r}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}.

In addition, summing over
m
in (
8
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}

\displaystyle\left(|E(n+1)|-|E(n)|\right)

\displaystyle=1+\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{m\bullet}\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}+\gamma\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{\bullet m}\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}.

(9)

As remarked at the beginning of Section
2
,
the proof of Theorem
2.2
requires showing
the convergence
of
\mathbb{E}[|E(n)|]/n
and for this it suffices to check

\mathbb{E}[|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|]/n\to x_{m}
and
\mathbb{E}[|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|]/n\to y_{m}
,
for some
x_{m},y_{m}\in[0,1]
,
which is implied by the approximation

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n+1)|-|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|\right)\approx x_{m}
, and

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n+1)|-|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|\right)\approx y_{m}
.
Therefore, we expect
x_{m},y_{m}
,
m=1,\ldots,K
, to be solutions to the following system of equations:

\displaystyle x_{m}

\displaystyle=\alpha\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}x_{r}+\delta}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}y_{r}+\delta}+\gamma\pi_{m}+\alpha\pi_{m}\sum_{r}\rho_{r,m}\frac{x_{r}+\delta\pi_{r}}{\sum_{r}x_{r}+\delta},

(10)

\displaystyle y_{m}

\displaystyle=\gamma\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}y_{r}+\delta}+\alpha\rho_{m\bullet}\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}x_{r}+\delta}+\alpha\pi_{m}+\gamma\pi_{m}\sum_{r}\rho_{m,r}\frac{y_{r}+\delta\pi_{r}}{\sum_{r}y_{r}+\delta},

(11)

for
m=1,\ldots,K
.
Summing over
m
in (
10
) and (
11
) shows

\sum_{m=1}^{K}x_{m}=\sum_{m=1}^{K}y_{m}
and from
(
10
) we get

\sum_{m=1}^{K}x_{m}\geq\sum_{m=1}^{K}\left(\alpha\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}x_{r}+\delta}+\gamma\pi_{m}\right)=1,

and therefore

\sum_{m=1}^{K}y_{m}\geq 1
.
Also, since

\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}x_{r}+\delta}\leq 1,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}y_{r}+\delta}\leq 1,

the right hand side of (
10
) is upper bounded by

\alpha+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}+\gamma\pi_{m}+\alpha\pi_{m}\text{max}_{r}\rho_{r,m}\leq 2.

Similarly, the right hand side of (
11
) is bounded by 2.

Lemma
2.1
shows that under some regularity
conditions, the system of equations (
10
) and (
11
)
has a unique solution,

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{K},y_{1},\ldots,y_{K})
in

\mathcal{Z}:=\{\bm{z}\in[0,2]^{2K}:\sum_{i=1}^{K}z_{i}\geq 1,\sum_{i=K+1}^{2K}z_{i}\geq 1\}.

The proof relies on the contraction mapping theorem

[
13
, Theorem 1.2.2]
. We proceed by defining a function

\bm{f}:\mathcal{Z}\mapsto\mathcal{Z}
, using the right-hand-side
expressions in (
10
) and (
11
). Applying the mean
value theorem to
\bm{f}
, we deduce a sufficient condition for

\bm{f}
to be a contraction by deriving an upper bound for the
1-norm of the Jacobian matrix. Due to the linked definition of

(x_{m})
and
(y_{m})
in (
10
) and (
11
), we write the
Jacobian matrix as a block matrix, and find the upper bounds block by
block.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\odot
and
\otimes
denote the Hadamard and Kronecker products of matrices, respectively. Denote the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{K}
as
\bm{I}_{K}
, and use
\bm{1}_{K}
to denote the vector in
\mathbb{R}^{K}
with all entries being 1.
Set
\bm{\pi}:=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{K})
, and the reciprocity matrix,
\bm{\rho}
, whose
(i,j)
-th entry is
\rho_{i,j}
.
Consider the four matrices:

\displaystyle J^{*}({1,1})=\alpha\left(\bm{I}_{K}+(\bm{\pi}\bm{1}_{K}^{T})\odot\bm{\rho}^{T}\right),

\displaystyle\quad J^{*}({1,2})=\gamma(\bm{\rho}^{T}\bm{\pi})\otimes\bm{1}_{K}^{T},

\displaystyle J^{*}({2,1})=\alpha(\bm{\rho}\bm{\pi})\otimes\bm{1}_{K}^{T},

\displaystyle\quad J^{*}({2,2})=\gamma\left(\bm{I}_{K}+(\bm{\pi}\bm{1}_{K}^{T})\odot\bm{\rho}\right),

and the block matrix

J^{*}:=\begin{bmatrix}J^{*}({1,1})&J^{*}({1,2})\\
J^{*}({2,1})&J^{*}({2,2})\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\|J^{*}\|_{1}
denote the 1-norm of the matrix
J^{*}
. Then as long as

\displaystyle\delta>\text{max}\{\|J^{*}\|_{1}-1,0\},

(12)

the system of equations in (
10
) and (
11
) has a unique solution
in
\mathcal{Z}
.

The proof of Lemma
2.1
is deferred to Section
6.1
.
Continuing to assume (
12
) holds, the next Theorem
2.2
gives the a.s. convergence of
|E(n)|/n
,
|E^{\text{in}}(n)|/n
and
|E^{\text{out}}(n)|/n
.
Define a constant

\displaystyle C_{\delta}=\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{m\bullet}\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}x_{r}+\delta}+\gamma\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{\bullet m}\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{r}y_{r}+\delta},

(13)

and a matrix

\displaystyle\bm{H}:=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\alpha}{1+\delta}\left(1+\bigvee_{m}\rho_{m\bullet}\right)&\frac{\gamma}{1+\delta}\bigvee_{m}\rho_{\bullet m}&\frac{1}{1+\delta}\left(\alpha+C_{\delta}\right)\\
\frac{\alpha}{1+\delta}\bigvee_{m}\rho_{m\bullet}&\frac{\gamma}{1+\delta}\left(1+\bigvee_{m}\rho_{\bullet m}\right)&\frac{1}{1+\delta}\left(\gamma+C_{\delta}\right)\\
\frac{\alpha}{1+\delta}\bigvee_{m}\rho_{m\bullet}&\frac{\gamma}{1+\delta}\bigvee_{m}\rho_{\bullet m}&\frac{C_{\delta}}{1+\delta}\end{bmatrix}.

(14)

The proof of Theorem
2.2
requires supposing

\lambda_{H}
, the largest eigenvalue of
\bm{H}
, satisfies
\lambda_{H}<1
to ensure the convergence of
\mathbb{E}[|E(n)|]/n
,
\mathbb{E}[|E^{\text{in}}(n)|]/n
and
\mathbb{E}[|E^{\text{out}}(n)|]/n
.

Define
\Delta^{\text{in}}_{m}(n):=|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|-n(x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m})
,
\Delta^{\text{out}}_{m}(n):=|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|-n(y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m})
, and

\Delta(n):=|E(n)|-n\sum_{m}x_{m}
.
We will prove Theorem
2.2
by deriving iterative upper bounds such that
for the matrix
\bm{H}
given in (
14
),

\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{m}|\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{\text{in}}_{m}(n+1))|\\
\sum_{m}|\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{\text{out}}_{m}(n+1))|\\
|\mathbb{E}(\Delta(n))|\end{bmatrix}\leq\left(\bm{I}+\frac{1}{n}\bm{H}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{m}|\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{\text{in}}_{m}(n))|\\
\sum_{m}|\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{\text{out}}_{m}(n))|\\
|\mathbb{E}(\Delta(n))|\end{bmatrix}.

Hence, provided that all elements in
\bm{H}
are strictly positive, applying the
Perron-Frobenius theorem suggests restricting the largest eigenvalue of
\bm{H}
will be sufficient to control the growth of the expected edge counts. In addition, the assumption of positive elements in
\bm{H}
leads to the requirement of
\alpha,\gamma>0
,
\bigvee_{m}\rho_{m\bullet}>0
, and
\bigvee_{m}\rho_{\bullet m}>0
.

Recall we may
interpret
\rho_{m\bullet}
and
\rho_{\bullet m}
, them as the likelihood of sending and attracting
reciprocal edges for a user in group
m
, respectively.
The assumption
\bigvee_{m}\rho_{m\bullet}>0
and

\bigvee_{m}\rho_{\bullet m}>0
requires that there exists at least one
group whose probability of generating or attracting reciprocal edges
is strictly positive.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
\alpha,\gamma>0
,
\bigvee_{m}\rho_{m\bullet}>0
,
\bigvee_{m}\rho_{\bullet m}>0
,

\delta>\|J^{*}\|_{F}-1
and
\lambda_{H}<1
.
Suppose that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{K},y_{1},\ldots,y_{K})
is the solution to the system of equations given in (
10
) and (

).
We have:

(i)

For
m=1,\ldots,K
,
\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}x_{m}

satisfies (

as well.

.

3
Growth of Degree Counts

We next focus on the asymptotic behavior of the joint in- and out-degree counts:

N_{k,l}(n):=\sum_{v\in V(n)}\bm{1}_{\left\{(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n),D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n))=(k,l)\right\}}.

We extend the homogeneous reciprocity techniques in
[
26
,
6
]

to obtain the convergence of

N_{k,l}(n)/n
under heterogeneous reciprocity. The way forward is via
embedding the in- and out-degree
sequences in a family of multi-type Markov
branching processes with immigration (MBI processes).

3.1
Markov Branching with Immigration

To pursue count asymptotics using embedding,
we need a linked family of two-type Markov branching processes
(
[
3
]
) where each process is a Markov Branching
with Immigration (MBI) process. See
Section 2.1.1 of
[
26
]
.
For each
m\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
,

\{\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(t,m)\equiv\bigl(\xi^{(1)}_{\delta}(t,m),\xi^{(2)}_{\delta}(t,m)\bigr):t\geq 0\},

is an MBI process whose branching structure
depends on
m
; this will be specified more precisely in
(
16
) and (
17
).
In the rest of the paper, we refer to
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(\cdot,m)
as a
MBI process with group label
m
.
The process
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(\cdot,m)
is designed to mimic evolution of in- and out-degrees of a fixed node with communication type
m
.
The general setup
of continuous-time multitype branching processes without immigration
is reviewed in
[
2
, Chapter V]
and discussions on
the MBI process are included, for instance, in

[
19
,
26
]
.

We assume life time parameters of
\xi^{(1)}_{\delta}(\cdot,m)
and

\xi^{(2)}_{\delta}(\cdot,m)
to be
\alpha
and
\gamma\equiv 1-\alpha
, respectively.
For
\bm{s}=(s_{1},s_{2})\in[0,1]^{2}
, the branching structure of
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(\cdot,m)
is specified through
offspring generating functions:

\displaystyle f^{(1)}_{v}(\bm{s},m)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\rho_{m\bullet}\right)s_{1}^{2}+\rho_{m\bullet}s_{1}^{2}s_{2},

(16)

\displaystyle f^{(2)}_{v}(\bm{s},m)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\rho_{\bullet m}\right)s_{2}^{2}+\rho_{\bullet m}s_{1}s_{2}^{2},

(17)

According to (
16
), when a group-
m
type I particle’s
lifetime ends,
with
probability
1-\rho_{m\bullet}
, it splits into two group-
m
type I particles, increasing the total number of group-
m

type I particles by 1, and with probability
\rho_{m\bullet}
,
it splits into two group-
m
type I
particles and one group-
m
type II particle. This last eventuality
increases the total numbers of group-
m
type I and II particles both
by 1.
Immigration events arrive following a homogeneous Poisson process with
rate
\delta>0
.
When an immigration event happens,

\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(\cdot,m)
is incremented by
(1,0)
,
(0,1)
, or

(1,1)
following the distribution

\displaystyle p_{0}(\bm{x},m)=\left(\alpha(1-\rho_{m\bullet})\right)^{\bm{1}_{\{\bm{x}=(1,0)\}}}\left(\gamma(1-\rho_{\bullet m})\right)^{\bm{1}_{\{\bm{x}=(0,1)\}}}\left(\alpha\rho_{m\bullet}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}\right)^{\bm{1}_{\{\bm{x}=(1,1)\}}}.

(18)

All immigrants are of group
m
, and have the same branching structure as given in (
16
) and (
17
).
Following the discussion in Chapter V.7.2 of
[
2
]
,
we use (
16
)–(
17
) to obtain a matrix that helps specify the branching structure of
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(\cdot,m)
:

A_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\alpha\rho_{m\bullet}\\
\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}&\gamma\end{bmatrix},\qquad m\in\{1,\ldots,K\}.

(19)

Applying the Perron-Frobenius theorem,
A_{m}
has a largest positive eigenvalue with multiplicity 1, i.e.

\displaystyle\lambda_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{(\alpha-\gamma)^{2}+4\alpha\gamma\rho_{m\bullet}\rho_{\bullet m}}\right)=:\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{D_{0}(m)}\right).

(20)

Also, the smaller eigenvalue of
A_{m}
is

\lambda^{\prime}_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{D_{0}(m)}\right),

which plays an important role in the discussion of hidden regular variation in Theorem
4.2
.
Let
\bm{v}{(m)}\equiv(v^{(1)}{(m)},v^{(2)}{(m)})
,
\bm{u}{(m)}\equiv(u^{(1)}{(m)},u^{(2)}{(m)})
be the
left and right eigenvectors associated with
\lambda_{m}
respectively, with all coordinates strictly positive, and
\bm{u}(m)^{T}\bm{1}=1
,
\bm{u}(m)^{T}\bm{v}(m)=1
.
Applying Theorem 1 in
[
26
]
gives that
there exists some finite positive random variable
Z(m)
such that

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda_{m}t}\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(t,m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z(m)\bm{v}(m),\qquad m\in\{1,\dots,K\}.

(21)

The PA model with heterogeneous reciprocity assumes that
the communication group of a fixed node is determined at the node’s creation
by flipping a

K
-sided coin and a corresponding feature must also be present in the embedding
process.
Let
L
be a random variable with

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L=m)=\pi_{m},\qquad m=1,\ldots,K,

(22)

and
for
t\geq 0
, the generating function of
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(t,L)
is

\mathbb{E}\left(s_{1}^{\xi^{(1)}_{\delta}(t,L)}s_{2}^{\xi^{(2)}_{v,\delta}(t,L)}\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{K}\pi_{m}\mathbb{E}\left(s_{1}^{\xi^{(1)}_{\delta}(t,m)}s_{2}^{\xi^{(2)}_{\delta}(t,m)}\right),\qquad s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1].

Imagine
L
assigns a label to a MBI which remains unchanged
throughout the evolution of
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(\cdot,\cdot)
.
The initial value,
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(0,L)
, is a random vector on
\{(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}
, whose distribution depends on the label
L
and will be specified during the embedding process.
Conditioning on
(\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(0,L),L)
,
\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(\cdot,L)
behaves like a MBI process with fixed group label
L
.
In the sequel, we refer to

\{\bm{\xi}_{\delta}(t,L):t\geq 0\}
as a two-type MBI process with random group label.

3.2
Embedding

Let
\{L_{v}:v\geq 1\}
be random variables with common pmf’s as in (
22
).
We now explain how to embed the degree sequence

\{(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n),D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)):v\in V(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
into a family of linked two-type MBI processes with random group labels,

\{\bm{\xi}_{v,\delta}(t,L_{v}):t\geq 0\}_{v\geq 1}
.
We assume
\{\bm{\xi}_{v,\delta}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{v\geq 1}
have the same parameters but possibly different initializations.

At
T_{0}=0
,
flip a
K
-sided coin with outcome
L_{1}
. If
L_{1}=m_{1}
, which occurs
with probability
\pi_{m_{1}}
,
initiate the MBI process
\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(\cdot,m_{1})
with
\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(0,m_{1})=(1,1)
.
Let
T_{1}
be the first jump time of

\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(\cdot,m_{1})
.Then for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(T_{1}>t,L_{1}=m_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{m_{1}}\exp\left\{-t\left(\alpha\left(\bm{\xi}^{(1)}_{1,\delta}(0,m_{1})+\delta\right)+\gamma\left(\bm{\xi}^{(2)}_{1,\delta}(0,m_{1})+\delta\right)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\pi_{m_{1}}e^{-t(1+\delta)},

and
\mathbb{P}(T_{1}>t)=e^{-t(1+\delta)}
.

At
T_{1}
,
flip another
K
-sided coin with outcome
L_{2}
and
start the process

\{\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(t-T_{1},L_{2}):t\geq T_{1}\}
. The initial value of

\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(0,L_{2})
is set depending on which of the following
four cases happens:

(i)

If
L_{1}=m_{1}
and at time
T_{1}
the process

\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(\cdot,{m_{1}})
increases by
(1,0)
,
then we have
L_{2}=m_{2}
with (conditional) probability
\pi_{m_{2}}(1-\rho_{m_{1},m_{2}})/(1-\rho_{m_{1}\bullet})
,
and set
\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(0,m_{2})=(0,1)
.

(ii)

If the
\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(\cdot,L_{1})
with
L_{1}=m_{1}
is increased by
(0,1)
,
then we have
L_{2}=m_{2}
with (conditional) probability
\pi_{m_{2}}(1-\rho_{m_{2},m_{1}})/(1-\rho_{\bullet m_{1}})
,
and
set
\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(0,m_{2})=(1,0)
.

(iii)

If one type I particle in
\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(\cdot,L_{1})
with
L_{1}=m_{1}
splits into
2 type I and 1 type II particles at
T_{1}
,
then we have
L_{2}=m_{2}
with (conditional) probability
\pi_{m_{2}}\rho_{m_{1},m_{2}}/\rho_{m_{1}\bullet}
,
and
set
\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(0,m_{2})=(1,1)
.

(iv)

If one type II particle in
\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(\cdot,L_{1})
with
L_{1}=m_{1}
splits into
1 type I and 2 type II particles at
T_{1}
,
then we have
L_{2}=m_{2}
with (conditional) probability
\pi_{m_{2}}\rho_{m_{2},m_{1}}/\rho_{\bullet m_{1}}
,
and
set
\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(0,m_{2})=(1,1)
.

Therefore, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L_{1}=m_{1},L_{2}=m_{2},T_{2}-T_{1}>t)

\displaystyle=\pi_{m_{1}}e^{-t(1+\delta)}\left(\alpha(1-\rho_{m_{1}\bullet})\frac{\pi_{m_{2}}(1-\rho_{m_{1},m_{2}})}{1-\rho_{m_{1}\bullet}}+\alpha\rho_{m_{1}\bullet}\frac{\pi_{m_{2}}\rho_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{\rho_{m_{1}\bullet}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\gamma(1-\rho_{\bullet m_{1}})\frac{\pi_{m_{2}}(1-\rho_{m_{2},m_{1}})}{1-\rho_{\bullet m_{1}}}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m_{1}}\frac{\pi_{m_{2}}\rho_{m_{2},m_{1}}}{\rho_{\bullet m_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{m_{1}}\pi_{m_{2}}e^{-t(1+\delta)},

which shows that
(L_{1},L_{2})
are independent.
Also,
\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(0,L_{2})
is a 2-dimensional random vector whose generating function
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(s_{1}^{\xi^{(1)}_{2,\delta}(0,L_{2})}s_{2}^{\xi^{(2)}_{2,\delta}(0,L_{2})}\middle|L_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha(1-\rho_{L_{1}\bullet})s_{2}+\gamma(1-\rho_{\bullet L_{1}})s_{1}+(\alpha\rho_{L_{1}\bullet}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet L_{1}})s_{1}s_{2},

so that for
\rho_{0}:=\sum_{m}\pi_{m}\rho_{m\bullet}=\sum_{m}\pi_{m}\rho_{\bullet m}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(s_{1}^{\xi^{(1)}_{2,\delta}(0,L_{2})}s_{2}^{\xi^{(2)}_{2,\delta}(0,L_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{K}\pi_{m}\left(\alpha(1-\rho_{m\bullet})s_{2}+\gamma(1-\rho_{\bullet m})s_{1}+(\alpha\rho_{m\bullet}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m})s_{1}s_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha(1-\rho_{0})s_{2}+\gamma(1-\rho_{0})s_{1}+\rho_{0}s_{1}s_{2},\qquad s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1].

(23)

Define
R_{1}:=\bm{1}_{\{\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(T_{1},L_{1})=\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(0,L_{1})+(1,1)\}}
, so that

\mathbb{P}(R_{1}=1|L_{1}=m)=\alpha\rho_{m\bullet}+\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}=1-\mathbb{P}(R_{1}=0|L_{1}=m),

and
\mathbb{P}(R_{1}=1)=\rho_{0}=1-\mathbb{P}(R_{1}=0)
.
Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{T_{1}}:=

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\{L_{k}\}_{k=1,2};\left\{\bm{\xi}_{k,\delta}(t-T_{k-1},L_{k}):t\in[T_{k-1},T_{1}]\right\}_{k=1,2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sigma\left(\{L_{k}\}_{k=1,2};\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(t,L_{1}),t\in[0,T_{1}];\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(0,L_{2})\right)},

we see that
R_{1}
is
\mathcal{F}_{T_{1}}
-measurable.

In general, for
n\geq 1
, suppose that we have initiated
n+1
MBI processes with group labels
\{L_{k}:1\leq k\leq n+1\}
at time
T_{n}
,

\displaystyle\{\bm{\xi}_{k,\delta}(t-T_{k-1},L_{k}):t\geq T_{k-1}\}_{1\leq k\leq n+1}.

(24)

Define
T_{n+1}
as the first time when one of the processes in (
24

J_{n+1}
be the index of the process that jumps at
T_{n+1}
.
Define the
\sigma
-field

\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}:=\sigma\left(\{L_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n+1};\left\{\bm{\xi}_{k,\delta}(t-T_{k-1},L_{k}):t\in[T_{k-1},T_{n}]\right\}_{1\leq k\leq n+1}\right),

and

R_{n+1}:=\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{J_{n+1},\delta}(T_{n+1}-T_{J_{n+1}-1},L_{J_{n+1}})=\bm{\xi}_{J_{n+1},\delta}(T_{n}-T_{J_{n+1}-1},L_{J_{n+1}})+(1,1)\right\}}.

Then we see that
\{R_{k}:1\leq k\leq n\}
are
\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}
-measurable.

At
T_{n+1}
, we initiate the MBI process with group label

\bm{\xi}_{n+2,\delta}(0,m_{n+2})=(0,1)
.

(ii)

If the
\bm{\xi}_{J_{n+1},\delta}(\cdot,L_{J_{n+1}})
with

is increased by
(0,1)
,

,
and

\bm{\xi}_{n+2,\delta}(0,m_{n+2})=(1,0)

(iii)

If one type I particle in

with

splits into

,
then we have

with (conditional) probability
\pi_{m_{n+2}}\rho_{m_{n+1},m_{n+2}}/\rho_{m_{n+1}\bullet}

(iv)

If one type II particle in

with

splits into
1 type I and 2 type II particles at

and

\bm{\xi}_{n+2,\delta}(0,m_{n+2})=(1,1)
.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi^{(1)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi^{(2)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1})=n+1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{r=1}^{K}\left[\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(\xi^{(1)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\delta)\bm{1}_{\{L_{k}=r\}}}{(1+\delta)(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}\left(\alpha(1-\rho_{r\bullet})\frac{\pi_{m}(1-\rho_{r,m})}{1-\rho_{r\bullet}}+\alpha\rho_{r\bullet}\frac{\pi_{m}\rho_{r,m}}{\rho_{r\bullet}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}

\displaystyle(\bm{\xi}_{n+2,\delta}(0,L_{n+2})=(1,0),L_{J_{n+1}}=m,L_{n+2}=r)

\displaystyle=\gamma\pi_{r}(1-\rho_{r,m})\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\delta\right)\bm{1}_{\{L_{k}=m\}}}{(1+\delta)(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}

\displaystyle(\bm{\xi}_{n+2,\delta}(0,L_{n+2})=(1,1),L_{J_{n+1}}=m,L_{n+2}=r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\pi_{r}\rho_{m,r}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(\xi^{(1)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\delta\right)\bm{1}_{\{L_{k}=m\}}}{(1+\delta)(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma\pi_{r}\rho_{r,m}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\delta\right)\bm{1}_{\{L_{k}=m\}}}{(1+\delta)(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}.

\displaystyle p_{n+2,0}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{K}\frac{\alpha(1-\rho_{m\bullet})\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(\xi^{(1)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\delta\right)\bm{1}_{\{L_{k}=m\}}}{(1+\delta)(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}\right)^{\bm{1}_{\{\bm{x}=(0,1)\}}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{m=1}^{K}\frac{\gamma(1-\rho_{\bullet m})\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\delta\right)\bm{1}_{\{L_{k}=m\}}}{(1+\delta)(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}\right)^{\bm{1}_{\{\bm{x}=(1,0)\}}}

\displaystyle\left.\left.\quad+\frac{\gamma\rho_{\bullet m}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\delta\right)\bm{1}_{\{L_{k}=m\}}}{(1+\delta)(n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}\right)\right)^{\bm{1}_{\{\bm{x}=(1,1)\}}}.

Meanwhile,

\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}
-measurable, we then have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}\left(R_{n+1}=1,J_{n+1}=w,T_{n+1}-T_{n}>t\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\rho_{L_{w}\bullet}\left(\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})+\delta\right)+\gamma\rho_{\bullet L_{w}}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})+\delta\right)}{\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi^{(1)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi^{(2)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+(n+1)\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left\{-t\left(\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi^{(1)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi^{(2)}_{k,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{k-1},L_{k})+(n+1)\delta\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\rho_{L_{w}\bullet}\left(\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})+\delta\right)+\gamma\rho_{\bullet L_{w}}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})+\delta\right)}{(n+1)(1+\delta)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}e^{-t((n+1)(1+\delta)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k})}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}\left(R_{n+1}=1,J_{n+1}=w\right)\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}\left(T_{n+1}-T_{n}>t\right).

(26)

Therefore,
\bigl(R_{n+1},J_{n+1}\bigr)
is independent from
T_{n+1}-T_{n}
under

\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}
.

Define for
n\geq 0
,

{\bm{\xi}}^{*}_{\delta}\left(T_{n},L_{[n+1]}\right):=\left(\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(T_{n},L_{1}),\bm{\xi}_{2,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{1},L_{2}),\ldots,\bm{\xi}_{n+1,\delta}(0,L_{n+1}),(0,0),\ldots\right),

then the embedding framework just described shows that

\{{\bm{\xi}}^{*}_{\delta}\left(T_{n},L_{[n+1]}\right):n\geq 0\}
is Markov with state space
\left(\mathbb{N}^{2}\right)^{\infty}
.
The next theorem gives the embedding of in- and out-degree sequences in the PA model with heterogeneous reciprocity into a linked system of delayed MBI processes.

Theorem 3.1
.

In
\left(\mathbb{N}^{2}\right)^{\infty}
, define the in- and out-degree sequences as

\bm{D}(n):=\left(\bigl(D^{\text{in}}_{1}(n),D^{\text{out}}_{1}(n)\bigr),\ldots,\bigl(D^{\text{in}}_{n+1}(n),D^{\text{out}}_{n+1}(n)\bigr),(0,0),\ldots\right)\qquad n\geq 0.

Then for
\{T_{k}:k\geq 0\}
and
\{\bm{\xi}_{k,\delta}(t-T_{k-1},L_{k}):t\geq T_{k-1}\}_{k\geq 1}
constructed above,
we have that in
\left(\left(\mathbb{N}^{2}\right)^{\infty}\right)^{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\bigl\{\bm{D}(n):n\geq 0\bigr\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\left\{{\bm{\xi}}^{*}_{\delta}\left(T_{n},L_{[n+1]}\right):n\geq 0\right\}.

By the model description in Section
1.1
,
\{\bm{D}(n):n\geq 0\}
is Markovian on
\left(\mathbb{N}^{2}\right)^{\infty}
, so
it suffices to check the transition probability from
\bm{D}(n)
to
\bm{D}(n+1)
agrees with that from

{\bm{\xi}}^{*}_{\delta}\left(T_{n},L_{[n+1]}\right)
to
{\bm{\xi}}^{*}_{\delta}\left(T_{n+1},L_{[n+2]}\right)
.
Write

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{v}^{(1)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\bigl(0,0\bigr),\ldots,\bigl(0,0\bigr),\underbrace{\bigl(1,0\bigr)}_{\text{$v$-th entry}},\bigl(0,0\bigr),\ldots\right),

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{v}^{(2)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\bigl(0,0\bigr),\ldots,\bigl(0,0\bigr),\underbrace{\bigl(0,1\bigr)}_{\text{$v$-th entry}},\bigl(0,0\bigr),\ldots\right),

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{v}^{(3)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\bigl(0,0\bigr),\ldots,\bigl(0,0\bigr),\underbrace{\bigl(1,1\bigr)}_{\text{$v$-th entry}},\bigl(0,0\bigr),\ldots\right),

and we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(\bm{D}(n+1)=\bm{D}(n)+\bm{e}_{v}^{(1)}+\bm{e}_{|V(n)|+1}^{(2)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta)}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}\sum_{m=1}^{K}(1-\rho_{W_{v},m}\pi_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta)}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}(1-\rho_{W_{v}\bullet});

(27)

similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(\bm{D}(n+1)=\bm{D}(n)+\bm{e}_{v}^{(2)}+\bm{e}_{|V(n)|+1}^{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta)}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}(1-\rho_{\bullet W_{v}}),

(28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\left(\bm{D}(n+1)=\bm{D}(n)+\bm{e}_{v}^{(3)}+\bm{e}_{|V(n)|+1}^{(3)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\rho_{W_{v}\bullet}(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta)+\gamma\rho_{\bullet W_{v}}(D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta)}{|E(n)|+\delta|V(n)|}.

(29)

Note that
|V(n)|=n+1
for all
n\geq 0
, and
from (
26
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}

\displaystyle\left({\bm{\xi}}^{*}_{\delta}\left(T_{n+1},L_{[n+2]}\right)={\bm{\xi}}^{*}_{\delta}\left(T_{n},L_{[n+1]}\right)+\bm{e}_{v}^{(3)}+\bm{e}_{|V(n)|+1}^{(3)}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}\left(R_{n+1}=1,J_{n+1}=v\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\rho_{L_{v}\bullet}\left(\xi^{(1)}_{v,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{v-1},L_{v})+\delta\right)+\gamma\rho_{\bullet L_{v}}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{v,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{v-1},L_{v})+\delta\right)}{(n+1)(1+\delta)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}.

(30)

So it remains to check
whether
\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}
has the same distribution as
|E(n)|-(n+1)
.

Again, applying (
26
) gives that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}}}

\displaystyle\left(R_{n+1}=1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha\rho_{L_{v}\bullet}\left(\xi^{(1)}_{v,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{v-1},L_{v})+\delta\right)+\gamma\rho_{\bullet L_{v}}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{v,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{v-1},L_{v})+\delta\right)}{(n+1)(1+\delta)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}}.

Also, we obtain from (
8
) that
|E(n)|-(n+1)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{G}_{n}}

\displaystyle\left(|E(n+1)|-|E(n)|-1=1\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{m\bullet}\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta(n+1)}+\gamma\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{\bullet m}\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(n)|+\delta|V_{m}(n)|}{|E(n)|+\delta(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha\rho_{W_{v}\bullet}\left(D^{\text{in}}_{v}(n)+\delta\right)+\gamma\rho_{\bullet W_{v}}\left(D^{\text{out}}_{v}(n)+\delta\right)}{|E(n)|+\delta(n+1)}.

(31)

By (
25
), we see that
\{L_{v}:v\geq 1\}
are iid random variables with

, agreeing with the distributional property of

.
Therefore, we conclude from (
3.2
) that
\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{k}
has the same distribution as

, which implies the agreement between the transition probabilities in

) and (

).
∎

3.3
Asymptotic Growth of Empirical Degree Frequencies

We now use the embedding results in Theorem
3.1
to prove the convergence of

N_{k,l}(n)/n
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(t,L^{*}):t\geq 0\}
be a MBI process with
random group label
L^{*}
, where
L^{*}

satisfies
\mathbb{P}(L^{*}=m)=\pi_{m}
,
m=1,\ldots,K
.
Suppose that the regularity condition (
15
) holds, and
the initialization,

\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(0,L^{*})
, satisfies that for

s_{i}\in[0,1]
,
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left(s_{1}^{\widetilde{\xi}^{(1)}_{\delta}(0,L^{*})}s_{2}^{\widetilde{\xi}^{(2)}_{\delta}(0,L^{*})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{K}\pi_{r}\left[\alpha\left(1-\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{m,r}\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}x_{s}+\delta}\right)s_{2}+\gamma\left(1-\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{r,m}\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}y_{s}+\delta}\right)s_{1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{m,r}\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}x_{s}+\delta}+\gamma\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{r,m}\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}y_{s}+\delta}\right)s_{1}s_{2}\right].

(32)

For
L^{*}=m
, the branching structure of
\{\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(t,m):t\geq 0\}
is given by
A_{m}
(cf. (
19
)).
Write

\rho^{*}:=\sum_{m=1}^{K}x_{m}-1=\sum_{m=1}^{K}y_{m}-1>0\text{
and }c^{*}:=1+\rho^{*}+\delta,

(33)

then as
n\to\infty
, we have
for
k,l\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{N_{k,l}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\pi_{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}c^{*}e^{-c^{*}t}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(t,m)=(k,l)\right)\differential t.

(34)

Let
T^{*}
be an exponential random variable with rate
c^{*}
, independent from

\{\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(\cdot,m):m=1,\ldots,K\}
, then the integral on the right hand side of (
34
) is

\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(T^{*},m)=(k,l)\right),\qquad m=1,\ldots,K,

representing the limiting in- and out-degree frequencies for nodes of communication group
m
.
In the sequel, set
(\mathcal{I}_{m},\mathcal{O}_{m})=\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(T^{*},m)
to denote the limit random
variables so that
(
34
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{N_{k,l}(n)}{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\pi_{m}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\big(\mathcal{I}_{m},\mathcal{O}_{m}\bigr)=(k,l)\Bigr].

(35)

By the embedding results in Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{N_{k,l}(n)}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})=(k,l)\right\}}+\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{1,\delta}(T_{n},L_{1})=(k,l)\right\}},

(36)

where the second term goes to 0 a.s. as
n\to\infty
.
Then we only need to consider the limit of the first term in (
36
).

Let
\{\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(t,\widetilde{L}_{w}):t\geq 0\}_{w\geq 1}
be a sequence of iid MBI processes with random labels, which have the same distributional properties as
\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(\cdot,L^{*})
and satisfy the initialization condition in (
32
).
Then we divide the first term in (
36
) into different parts:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})=(k,l)\right\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\left(\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}\left(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w}\right)=(k,l)\right\}}-\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}\bigl(\log(n/w)/c^{*},L_{w}\bigr)=(k,l)\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\,+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\left(\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}\bigl(\log(n/w)/c^{*}\bigr)=(k,l)\right\}}-\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}\left[\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),L_{w}\right)=(k,l)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\,+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}\left[\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),L_{w}\right)=(k,l)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)=(k,l)\middle|\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\,+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)=(k,l)\middle|\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad-\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}\left(-\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log t,L^{*}\right)=(k,l)\right]\differential t\right)

\displaystyle\,+\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}\left(-\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log t,L^{*}\right)=(k,l)\right]\differential t

\displaystyle=:A_{1}(n)+A_{2}(n)+A_{3}(n)+A_{4}(n)+A_{5}.

Note that by a change of variable argument,
A_{5}
is identical to the right hand side of (
34
),
and we now show that
A_{j}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
, for
j=1,2,3,4
.

For
A_{1}(n)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|A_{1}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}\left|\bm{1}_{\left\{\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})=k\right\}}-\bm{1}_{\{\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}\bigl(\log(n/w)/c^{*},L_{w}\bigr)=k\}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}\left|\bm{1}_{\{\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})=l\}}-\bm{1}_{\{\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}\bigl(\log(n/w)/c^{*},L_{w}\bigr)=l\}}\right|.

(37)

Since both
\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}(\cdot,L_{w})
and
\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}(\cdot,L_{w})

have finite number of jumps in any finite time interval
[0,K]
a.s., then
applying Lemma 3.1 in
[
1
]
gives that for all
K>0
and
w\geq 2
,

\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,K]}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{(i)}_{w,\delta}(t+\epsilon,L_{w})-\xi^{(i)}_{w,\delta}\bigl((t-\epsilon)\wedge 0,L_{w})\bigr)\geq 1\right)=0,\qquad i=1,2.

Also, we see from
[
1
, Corollary 2.1(iii)]
that for
\eta>0
,

\sup_{n\eta\leq w\leq n}\left|T_{n}-T_{w-1}-\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Then using techniques from
[
1
, Theorem 1.2, pp 489–490]
further gives

\displaystyle\left|\bm{1}_{\{\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})=k\}}-\bm{1}_{\{\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}\bigl(\log(n/w)/c^{*},m\bigr)=k\}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{w\geq 1}\sup_{1\leq m\leq K}\sup_{t\in[0,-\log\eta/c^{*}]}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}(t+\epsilon,m)-\xi^{(1)}_{w,\delta}\bigl((t-\epsilon)\wedge 0,m)\bigr)\geq 1\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\eta\leq w\leq n}\left|T_{n}-T_{w-1}-\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w)\right|\geq\epsilon\right)=:p_{1}(\epsilon,\eta).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left|\bm{1}_{\{\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}(T_{n}-T_{w-1},L_{w})=l\}}-\bm{1}_{\{\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}\bigl(\log(n/w)/c^{*},L_{w}\bigr)=l\}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{w\geq 1}\sup_{1\leq m\leq K}\sup_{t\in[0,-\log\eta/c^{*}]}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}(t+\epsilon,m)-\xi^{(2)}_{w,\delta}\bigl((t-\epsilon)\wedge 0),m\bigr)\geq 1\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\eta\leq w\leq n}\left|T_{n}-T_{w-1}-\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w)\right|\geq\epsilon\right)=:p_{2}(\epsilon,\eta).

Then by (
37
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|A_{1}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\frac{1}{n}\cdot n\eta+\frac{1}{n}(1-\eta)n\bigl(p_{1}(\epsilon,\eta)+p_{2}(\epsilon,\eta)\bigr),

which implies
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|A_{1}(n)|=0
. Therefore,
A_{1}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.

For
A_{2}(n)
,
define

X_{w}:=\bm{1}_{\left\{\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),L_{w}\right)=(k,l)\right\}}-\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}\left(\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),L_{w}\right)=(k,l)\right),

then we see that
\mathbb{E}(X_{w})=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}(X_{w})\right)=0
.
Also, for
u<w
, since

\mathbb{E}(X_{w}X_{u})=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}(X_{w}X_{u})\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}(X_{w})\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}(X_{u})\right)=0,

then by the weak law of large numbers, we have
A_{2}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.

For
A_{3}(n)
, we first note that for
w\geq 2
and
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{F}_{T_{w-1}}}\left(\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(t,L_{w})=(k,l)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(t,L_{w})=(k,l)\middle|\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(0,L_{w}),L_{w}\right).

Then we have

\displaystyle|A_{3}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\bm{1}_{\left\{\left(\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(0,L_{w}),L_{w}\right)\neq\left(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)\right\}}.

Hence, to prove
A_{3}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
, it suffices to show

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\mathbb{P}\left[\left(\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(0,L_{w}),L_{w}\right)\neq\left(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)\right]\to 0.

(38)

Let
\mathcal{X}:=\{(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}\times\{1,\ldots,K\}
, and we note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(0,L_{w}),L_{w}\right)\neq\left(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left[\left(\bm{\xi}_{w,\delta}(0,L_{w}),L_{w}\right)=\bm{x}\right]-\mathbb{P}\left[\left(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),L_{w}\right)=\bm{x}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{m\bullet}\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(w-1)|+\delta|V_{m}(w-1)|}{|E(w-1)|+\delta|V(w-1)|}\right)-\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}x_{s}+\delta}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+2\gamma\sum_{m=1}^{K}\rho_{\bullet m}\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(w-1)|+\delta|V_{m}(w-1)|}{|E(w-1)|+\delta|V(w-1)|}\right)-\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}y_{s}+\delta}\right|.

From the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we see that there exist constants
C_{1}(\delta),C_{2}(\delta)>0
such that

\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|E^{\text{in}}_{m}(w-1)|+\delta|V_{m}(w-1)|}{|E(w-1)|+\delta|V(w-1)|}\right)-\frac{x_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}x_{s}+\delta}\right|\leq C_{1}(\delta)w^{\lambda_{H}-1},

and

\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|E^{\text{out}}_{m}(w-1)|+\delta|V_{m}(w-1)|}{|E(w-1)|+\delta|V(w-1)|}\right)-\frac{y_{m}+\delta\pi_{m}}{\sum_{s}y_{s}+\delta}\right|\leq C_{2}(\delta)w^{\lambda_{H}-1}.

Then the left hand side of (
38
) is bounded by

\frac{2}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\left(\rho_{m\bullet}C_{1}(\delta)+\rho_{\bullet m}C_{2}(\delta)\right)w^{\lambda_{H}-1},\qquad\lambda_{H}<1,

which goes to 0 as
n\to\infty
, thus proving the claim in (
38
).

For
A_{4}(n)
, since
\{(\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),\widetilde{L}_{w}):w\geq 1\}
are iid
random vectors in
\mathcal{X}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)=(k,l)\middle|\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}(0,\widetilde{L}_{w}),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad-\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)=(k,l)\right]\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Also, by the definition of
\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}
,
w\geq 1
, we see that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{w,\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),\widetilde{L}_{w}\right)=(k,l)\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w=2}^{n+1}\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{c^{*}}\log(n/w),L^{*}\right)=(k,l)\right].

Since the function
\mathbb{P}[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(t,L^{*})=(k,l)]
is bounded and continuous in
t
,
then we conclude that
A_{4}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
by applying the Riemann integrability of
\mathbb{P}\left[\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{\delta}(-\log t/c^{*},L^{*})=(k,l)\right]
,
which completes the proof of (
34
).
∎

4
Power Laws and Asymptotic Dependence of Degree Frequencies

In this section, we study the dependence between large in- and

\mathbb{P}[(\mathcal{I}_{m},\mathcal{O}_{m})\in\cdot]
, for each

.

4.1
Multivariate and Hidden Regular Variation

To formalize our analysis, we provide some useful definitions related
to

(MRV) and
hidden
regular variation
(HRV) of distributions.

Suppose that
\mathbb{C}_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
are two closed cones, and we provide the definition of
\mathbb{M}
-convergence in Definition
4.1

(cf.
[
16
,
10
,
7
,
15
,
4
]
) on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{C}_{0}
, which lays the theoretical foundation of regularly varying measures (cf. Definition
4.2
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{M}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{C}_{0})
be the set of Borel
measures on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{C}_{0}
which are finite on
sets bounded away from

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we propose a preferential attachment model with heterogeneous reciprocity levels and study its theoretical properties. Using the MBI embedding technique, we find that the distribution of large in- and out-degrees is jointly regularly varying, concentrating along a specific ray. Additionally, after deleting large in- and out-degree pairs close to the ray, we further have hidden regular variation with limit measure concentrating around another ray.

We now outline some open problems related to this model, which will be left as future research.

Estimation.

The fitting of the proposed model remains open at this point. For a given dataset, we need to first decide how many communication groups (
K
) should be assumed. One may consult classical clustering methods such as
k
-means and
k
-nearest neighbors to determine a proper
K
beforehand, but their applications to the network framework needs rigorous justification.
Once
K
is chosen, we can also derive proper tools (similar to those in
[
9
]
) to detect the existence of hidden regular variation under the network setup.

Varying group labels.

So far we have assumed that the communication group of a user (node) is determined upon its creation and remains unchanged afterwards. For real-world applications, however, this may be a naive assumption, since users’ interaction patterns can change over time. A possible extension is to assume that the group label for each node follows another Markov chain with finite state space. One may consider applying variational Bayesian inference methods (cf.
[
17
]
) to these extended models.

Covariate-dependent reciprocity probabilities.

The model studied in this paper assumes a deterministic matrix,
\bm{\rho}
, to characterize the reciprocation between different communication groups.
If more node-specific information (covariates) is available, e.g. various demographic, socio-economic, and behavioral factors, then we can generalize the definition of the matrix
\bm{\rho}
to be covariate-dependent. This may possibly lead to heterogeneous extremal behaviors.

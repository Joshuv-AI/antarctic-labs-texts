Structural stability of the RG flow in the Gross-Neveu model
J. Dimock, Cheng Yuan, SUNY at Buffalo, NY 14260, USA

Abstract
We study flow of renormalization group (RG) transformations for the massless Gross-Neveu model in a non-perturbative formulation. The model is defined on a two dimensional Euclidean space with a finite volume. The quadratic approximation to the flow stays bounded after suitable renormalization. We show that for weak coupling this property also is true for the complete flow. As an application we prove an ultraviolet stability bound for the model. Our treatment is an application of a method of Bauerschmidt, Brydges, and Slade. The method was developed for an infrared problem, and is now applied to an ultraviolet problem.



1
Introduction

In a quantum field theory model a renormalization group transformation replaces the given action by an effective action on a larger length scale.
When iterated one gets a flow of the effective actions which distill information about the large scale structure of model. At each stage one identifies
an effective coupling constant and a few other parameters like field strength and mass. The flow of the coupling constant is particularly important
as it drives the other parameters. In weakly coupled models in critical dimension
it has the form

g_{k+1}=g_{k}\pm\beta_{k}g_{k}^{2}+{\cal O}(g_{k}^{3})\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\beta_{k}>0

(1)

Structural stability is the statement that this quadratic flow
g_{k+1}=g_{k}\pm\beta_{k}g_{k}^{2}
, together with the quadratic flow of the other parameters, is the
dominant behavior in complete flow of the effective actions. In particular with a minus sign the flow is toward zero, the effective actions become free, and infrared (long distance) problems become tractable. With a plus sign the flow
is away from the origin and, after renormalization to tiny initial values, ultraviolet (short distance) problems become tractable.

[
5
]
have developed a method for proving structural stability in the infrared case. They are
interested in an
n
-component scalar field
\phi
on a four dimensional unit lattice with a
|\phi|^{4}
interaction term
[
2
]
,
as well as the self-avoiding random walk which is formally the case
n=0

[
3
]
,
[
4
]
.
In these models the minus sign occurs in (
1
) and they are able to use the structural stability result
to prove results on the bulk behavior of the model.

In this paper we show that this method works for an ultraviolet problem and in the continuum. We consider the massless Gross-Neveu model in dimension two. It features
anti-commuting fermi fields
\psi,\bar{\psi}

with
n

S(\psi)=\int\Big(-\varepsilon+(1+z)\ \bar{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi-g\ (\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}\Big)dx

(2)

We work in a finite volume and add a high momentum cutoff to provide a short distance regularization of the model. Precise definitions to follow shortly. We scale up to a large volume and unit momentum cutoff. We take initial values
g_{0},z_{0},\varepsilon_{0}
, depending on the cutoff, and make a sequence of RG transformations.
After

In particular one has bounds on the effective actions uniform in the cutoff, and this gives a uniform

The method could lead to a complete construction of the model which would involve taking the limit as the cutoff is removed both in the partition function
and correlation functions, and taking the infinite volume limit.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We start in a finite volume by working on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{M}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}/L^{M}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

with volume
L^{2M}
. Here
L
is a fixed positive integer and
M
is
the long distance cutoff. The fields are elements on a Grassmann algebra generated by independent. elements

\bar{\psi}^{i}_{a}(x),\bar{\psi}^{i}_{a}(x)
where
x\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M}
, the spinor index is
a=1,2
, and
i=1,\dots,n
is the internal index.
This is an infinite dimensional Grassmann algebra; we are more precise about its exact
definition in section
2.3
.

The partition function is interpreted as an integral on the Grassmann algebra and has the form (up to an overall constant)

\mathsf{Z}=\int\exp\Big(\int\Big[\varepsilon-z\bar{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi+g(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2})\Big]\Big)d\mu_{G}(\psi)

(5)

where bilinears have the form
(\bar{\psi}\psi)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1,2}\bar{\psi}^{i}_{a}(x)\psi^{i}_{a}(x)
, etc.
With self-adjoint Dirac matrices satisfying
\{\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}\}=\delta_{\mu\nu}
,
the Euclidean Dirac operator is
\not{\partial}=\sum_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}=\gamma_{0}\partial_{0}+\gamma_{1}\partial_{1}
.
Further
\int[\cdots]d\mu_{G}
is the Grassmann Gaussian integral
with covariance
G=\not{\partial}^{-1}
.
Formally the kernel
G(x-y)
is defined by the Fourier series
1
1

1

See Appendix
A
for our Fourier series conventions

G(x-y)=L^{-2M}\sum_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M}^{*}}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}

(6)

where
{\mathbb{T}}_{M}^{*}=2\pi L^{-M}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. More precisely if we include internal indices it is
G(x-y)\delta_{ij}
.

We regularize this by introducing a smooth momentum cutoff at approximately
|p|=L^{N}

and we also exclude
p=0
. The modified covariance is

G(x-y)=L^{-2M}\sum_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M}^{*},p\neq 0}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-p^{2}/L^{2N}}

(7)

is now well defined pointwise. Our goal is to get uniform bounds as
N\to\infty
.

It is convenient to scale up to the torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{N+M}
with a unit momentum cutoff.
This is accomplished replacing
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M}
by
\psi_{L^{-N}}
for
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N}
where

\psi_{L^{-N}}(x)=L^{N/2}\psi(L^{N}x)\hskip 28.45274pt\bar{\psi}_{L^{-N}}(x)=L^{N/2}\bar{\psi}(L^{N}x)

(8)

Then we find (up to an overall constant)

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S_{0}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{0}}(\psi)

(9)

The action becomes

S_{0}=\int\Big(\varepsilon_{0}-z_{0}\bar{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi+g_{0}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}\Big)

(10)

with new parameters
\varepsilon_{0}=L^{-3N}\varepsilon,z_{0}=z,g_{0}=g
.
The covariance is now

G_{0}(x-y)=L^{-2(M+N)}\sum_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N}^{*},p\neq 0}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-p^{2}}

(11)

Then
N\to\infty
limit is now a long distance problem. Instead of potential singularities as
p\to\infty
, we have potential singularities as
p\to 0
.

2.2
Renormalization group

The renormalization group method is a technique to find alternate expressions for
\mathsf{Z}
on coarser momentum lattices, thereby softening the
singularities. The improvement comes in a sequence of steps. After
k
steps one has fields
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}
and the expression

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S_{k}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{k}}(\psi)

(12)

Here the new covariance is essentially the same as
G_{0}
and is given
by

G_{k}(x-y)={\sum}^{\prime}_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}^{*}_{N+M-k}}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-p^{2}}

(13)

The primed sum is a weighted sum with
p\neq 0
defined by

{\sum}^{\prime}_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}^{*}_{N+M-k}}[\cdots]=L^{-2(M+N-k)}\sum_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}^{*}_{N+M-k},p\neq 0}[\cdots]

(14)

The new
S_{k}
is yet to be specified, but should have leading terms similar to
S_{0}
.

Given such an expression on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}
we generate the expression on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k-1}
as follows.
We split off the highest momentum piece of the covariance by

G_{k}=G_{k+1,L}+C_{k}

(15)

where
G_{k+1,L}(x,y)=L^{-1}G_{k+1}(L^{-1}x,L^{-1}y)
. Then we have

\begin{split}G_{k+1,L}(x-y)=&{\sum}^{\prime}_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}^{*}}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}e^{-L^{2}p^{2}}\\
C_{k}(x-y)=&{\sum}^{\prime}_{p\in{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}^{*}}e^{ip(x-y)}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}\Big(e^{-p^{2}}-e^{-L^{2}p^{2}}\Big)\\
\end{split}

(16)

The integral splits as

\mathsf{Z}=\int\Big[\int e^{S_{k}(\psi+\eta)}d\mu_{C_{k}}(\eta)\Big]d\mu_{G_{k+1},L}(\psi)

(17)

The expression in brackets is the fluctuation integral, and one searches for a new action
\tilde{S}_{k+1}

such that

e^{\tilde{S}_{k+1}(\psi)}=\int e^{S_{k}(\psi+\eta)}d\mu_{C_{k}}(\eta)

(18)

Replace
\psi(x)
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k}
by
\psi_{L}(x)=L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi(L^{-1}x)
now with
\psi
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{M+N-k-1}
.
Under this scaling
G_{k+1,L}
scales to
G_{k+1}
and
\tilde{S}_{k+1}
scales to

S_{k+1}(\psi)=\tilde{S}_{k+1}(\psi_{L})
.
We have then

\mathsf{Z}=\int e^{S_{k+1}(\psi)}d\mu_{G_{k+1}}(\psi)

(19)

We are back to a unit momentum cutoff, but the volume has been reduced. The issue is to keep track the effective actions
S_{k}
.

In the main text will use a modification of the above in which the field strength is absorbed into
the measure before studying the fluctuation integral.

2.3
The Grassmann algebra

We give a more precise definition of the Grassmann algebras (see also
[
17
]
,
[
8
]
).
The algebras are formally generated by elements
\psi(\xi)
satisfying
\psi(\xi)\psi(\xi^{\prime})=-\psi(\xi^{\prime})\psi(\xi)
. Here the index
\xi=(x,a,i,\omega)
consists of a point

x
in some torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{j}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}/L^{j}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, a Dirac index

, an internal index
1\leq i\leq n
, and

\omega=0,1
which distinguishes between
\psi,\bar{\psi}
. We have

\psi(x,a,i,0)=\psi_{a,i}(x)\hskip 28.45274pt\psi(x,a,i,1)=\bar{\psi}_{a,i}(x)

(20)

A general element of the Grassman algebra is given by the formal expression

F=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\int F_{\ell}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{\ell})\psi(\xi_{1}),\cdots\psi(\xi_{\ell})d\xi

(21)

and we assume that the kernels
F_{\ell}
are anti-symmetric to match the anti-symmetry of
\psi(\xi_{1}),\cdots\psi(\xi_{\ell})
. So we assume

\textrm{Alt}F_{\ell}=F_{\ell}

where

(\textrm{Alt}F)(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi}\textrm{sgn}\ \pi\ F(\xi_{\pi(1)},\dots,\xi_{\pi(n)})

(22)

and the sum is over a permutations
\pi
of
1,2,\dots,n
.

The kernels

are allowed to be distributions and we assume they are in a Banach space of distributions
{\cal C}^{\prime}

to be specified. Then we define a norm depending on a parameter

\|F\|_{h}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{h^{\ell}}{\ell!}\|F_{\ell}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}

(23)

Let
{\cal G}_{h}
be Banach space of all formal expressions (
21

is finite.

A product of two elements is formally

\begin{split}FG=\frac{1}{n!m!}\int F_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})G_{m}(\xi_{n+1},\dots,\xi_{m})\psi(\xi_{1}),\cdots\psi(\xi_{n+m})d\xi\\
\end{split}

(24)

But
\psi(\xi_{1}),\cdots\psi(\xi_{n+m})
is anti-symmetric. So if we take account that

<H_{1},\textrm{Alt}H_{2}>=<\textrm{Alt}H_{1},H_{2}>

(25)

we can formally transfer an
Alt

FG=\sum_{n,m}\frac{1}{n!m!}\int\Big(\textrm{Alt}(F_{n}\otimes G_{m})\Big)(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+m})\psi(\xi_{1})\cdots\psi(\xi_{n+m})d\xi\\

(26)

The next result shows that under certain conditions on the space
{\cal C}^{\prime}
the space
{\cal G}_{h}
is a Banach algebra.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that

\|\textrm{Alt}(F_{n}\otimes G_{m}))\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\leq\|F_{n}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\|G_{m}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}

(27)

Then if
F\in{\cal G}_{h}
and

\|FG\|_{h}\leq\|F\|_{h}\|G\|_{h}

Proof
. Let
H=FG
.
Then

H=\sum_{\ell}\frac{1}{\ell!}\int H_{\ell}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\ell})\psi(\xi_{1}),\cdots\psi(\xi_{\ell})d\xi\\

(28)

where

\begin{split}\|H_{\ell}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\leq&\sum_{n+m=\ell}\frac{\ell!}{n!m!}\|\textrm{Alt}(F_{n}\otimes G_{m})\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\leq\sum_{n+m=\ell}\frac{\ell!}{n!m!}\|F_{n}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\|G_{m}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\\
\end{split}

(30)

and we have the announced

\|H\|_{h}=\sum_{\ell}\frac{h^{\ell}}{\ell!}\|H_{\ell}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\leq\sum_{n,m}\frac{h^{n+m}}{n!m!}\|F_{n}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\|G_{m}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}=\|F\|_{h}\|G\|_{h}

Now we define the space
{\cal C}^{\prime}={\cal C}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}}_{j}^{\ell})
. It is the dual space to a Banach space

defined as
all functions

on

in each of the
\ell
variables
separately. With
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{\ell})
and

the norm is

Here the multi-index

\partial^{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\alpha_{\ell}}_{x_{\ell}}\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\partial^{\alpha_{i}}_{x_{i}}=\partial^{\alpha_{i,0}}_{x_{i,0}}\partial^{\alpha_{i,1}}_{x_{i,1}}

Then

Now we show that this satisfies the conditions of lemma 1.

Lemma 2

\|\textrm{Alt}(F_{n}\otimes G_{m}))\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\leq\|F_{n}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}\|G_{m}\|_{{\cal C}^{\prime}}

3
A single RG transformation

3.1
Some definitions

After
k
RG transformations we will have an action which contains a term

V_{k}=g_{k}\int(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}

(124)

with a new coupling constant
g_{k}
, as well as many other terms. If this were the only term in the next step we would want to compute
the fluctuation integral

\Big\langle e^{V_{k}}\Big\rangle_{C_{k}}(\psi)\equiv\int e^{V_{k}(\psi+\eta)}d\mu_{C_{k}}(\eta)

(125)

We would like to write this as the exponential of something. To find the leading term of this something, one might look at the logarithm and expand in
V_{k}

by

\log\Big\langle e^{V_{k}}\Big\rangle=\Big\langle V_{k}\Big\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\Big\langle V_{k},V_{k}\Big\rangle^{T}+\dots

(126)

where
\Big\langle V_{k},V_{k}\Big\rangle^{T}=\Big\langle V_{k}^{2}\Big\rangle-\Big\langle V_{k}\Big\rangle^{2}
. This is just formal because
V_{k}
is not small (due to the large volume)
and so the definition of the logarithm is problematic. Nevertheless
\Big\langle V_{k}\Big\rangle
and
\frac{1}{2}\Big\langle V_{k},V_{k}\Big\rangle^{T}

are the correct leading terms and will play an important roll in the more detailed analysis.

In computing these integrals it is useful to use the following fact. If

\Big\langle F\Big\rangle_{C}(\psi)\equiv\int F(\psi+\eta)d\mu_{C}(\eta)

(127)

then

\Big\langle F\Big\rangle_{C}=e^{\Delta_{C}}F

where

is the
formal differential operator

\Delta_{C}=\int dxdy\ C_{ab}(x-y)\ \frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\psi}_{b}(y)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi_{a}(x)}

(128)

In the case at hand
\Big\langle V_{k}\Big\rangle=e^{\Delta_{C}}V_{k}
. Any derivatives give terms proportions to
C_{k}(0)
which vanishes
since
C_{k}
is an odd function. (This is special to the massless model.) Thus we
have

\begin{split}\Big\langle V_{k}\Big\rangle_{C_{k}}=&V_{k}\\
\Big\langle V_{k},V_{k}\Big\rangle^{T}_{C_{k}}=&\Big\langle V_{k}^{2}\Big\rangle_{C_{k}}-V_{k}^{2}=(e^{\Delta_{C_{k}}}-1)V_{k}^{2}\\
\end{split}

(129)

Terms like this accumulate after
k
steps and we will find that the quadratic term has the form

Q_{k}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\Big\langle V_{k},V_{k}\Big\rangle^{T}_{w_{k}}=\frac{1}{2}(e^{\Delta_{w_{k}}}-1)V_{k}^{2}

(130)

where

w_{k}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}C_{j,L^{-(k-j}}(x)={\sum}^{\prime}_{p}e^{ipx}\frac{-i\not{p}}{p^{2}}\Big(e^{-p^{2}/L^{2k}}-e^{-p^{2}}\Big)

(131)

In subsequent steps we cannot avoid allowing local terms not present in the original action.
With
\gamma_{5}=i\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}
there may be pseudo-scalar terms
of the form

(132)

with

and
p_{0}=0
.
Again we have
\Big\langle V^{p}_{k}\Big\rangle=V^{p}_{k}
and
there are associated quadratic terms
<V_{k},V^{p}_{k}>^{T}={\cal O}(g_{k}^{3})
and
\frac{1}{2}<V^{p}_{k},V^{p}_{k}>^{T}={\cal O}(g_{k}^{4})
.
It is only the first that we need to track in any detail.
After
k
steps these will accumulate to

Q^{p}_{k}\equiv\Big\langle V_{k},V^{p}_{k}\Big\rangle^{T}_{w^{\prime}_{k}}=(e^{\Delta_{w^{\prime}_{k}}}-1)V_{k}V^{p}_{k}

(133)

where

is

In addition there may be vector terms
of the form

V^{v}_{k}=v_{k}\int\sum_{\mu}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi)^{2}

After
k

In fact it is not

which occur, but normalized versions

which have relevant terms removed and have good bounds. The precise definition is yet to come.
In the following we group together the rogue terms by

V_{k}^{\prime}=V^{p}_{k}+V^{v}_{k}\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274ptQ_{k}^{\prime}=Q^{p}_{k}+Q^{v}_{k}\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt{Q_{k}^{\prime}}^{\textrm{reg}}=Q^{p,\textrm{reg}}_{k}+Q^{v,\textrm{reg}}_{k}

Here the

This occurs in a term like

However we again replace

The terms in

and the proof for

is the same.

\delta E_{k}(X,\psi,\eta)=E_{k}(\psi+\eta)-E_{k}(\psi)

Proof

For second result integrate

3.9
Cluster expansion

To complete the analysis of the terms in the fluctuation integral (

We still work on the torus
\mathbb{T}_{N+M-k}
, but consider general localized functions

. The covariance
C
is either
C_{k}
or
C_{k}^{z}
.

The following is a version of the cluster expansion was developed by Gawedski and Kupiainen
[
19
]
for bosons, and
extended to fermions by Feldman, Magnen, Rivasseau, Seneor
[
15
]
.
Technical details related to the norm we are using go back to Brydges and Yau
[
9
]
(although they have a rather different treatment of fluctuation integrals).
Earlier references can be found in the cited papers.

Theorem 2
.

Let
h
be sufficiently large depending on
L
, and
\|E\|_{h,\Gamma_{4}}\leq c_{0}
with

sufficiently small. Then there are complex numbers

such that

(279)

with the estimate

|E^{\#}|_{\Gamma}\equiv\sum_{X\supset\square}|E^{\#}(X)|\Gamma(X)\leq{\cal O}(1)\|E\|_{h,\Gamma_{4}}

(280)

Proof
.
part I:

First we make a Mayer expansion. The
\exp(\sum_{X}E(X))
is written as
\prod_{X}\Big((e^{E(X)}-1)+1\Big)
.
The product is expanded and intersecting terms are grouped together.
We have

\exp\Big(\sum_{X}E(X)\Big)=\sum_{\{Y_{\beta}\}}\prod_{\beta}K(Y_{\beta})

(281)

where the sum is over disjoint paved sets
\{Y_{\beta}\}
, and where we have defined

K(Y)=\sum_{\{X_{i}\}\to Y}\prod_{i}\Big(e^{E(X_{i})}-1)\Big)

(282)

Here the sum is over distinct overlap connected paved sets
\{X_{i}\}
whose union is
Y
.

3
3

3

overlap connected means the graph on the
\{X_{i}\}
consisting of all pairs
\{X_{i},X_{j}\}
such that

X_{i}\cap X_{j}\neq\emptyset
is connected. The
X_{i}
themselves may not be connected.

To estimate this we use

\|e^{E(X_{i})}-1\|_{h}\leq 2\|E(X_{i})\|_{h}

(283)

and so

\|K(Y)\|_{h}\leq\sum_{\{X_{i}\}}\prod_{i}2\|E(X_{i})\|_{h}

(284)

We also have from (

\Gamma_{3}(Y)\leq\prod_{i}\Gamma_{3}(X_{i})
and then

\|K\|_{h,\Gamma_{3}}=\sum_{Y\supset\square}\|K(Y)\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(Y)\leq\sum_{\{X_{i}\}:\cup_{i}X_{i}\supset\square}\prod_{i}2\|E(X_{i})\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(X_{i})

(285)

We replace the sum over unordered paved sets by a sum over ordered paved sets and have

\|K\|_{h,\Gamma_{3}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\prod_{i}2\|E(X_{i})\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(X_{i})

(286)

with the restrictions that
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
are overlap connected and that
\cup_{i}X_{i}\supset\square
.

But

1(\cup_{i}X_{i}\supset\square)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}1(X_{j}\supset\square)

(287)

and when summed over
X_{i}
this gives a contribution independent of
j
. Thus we can assume
j=1
. Then
\sum_{j=1}^{n}=n
and we have

\|K\|_{h,\Gamma_{3}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}1(X_{1}\supset\square)\prod_{i}2\|E(X_{i})\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(X_{i})

(288)

still with the overlap connected condition. But if
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
are overlap connected there is also a tree graph connecting these elements.
Thus we have

1((X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\textrm{ overlap connected })\leq\sum_{\tau}1(\tau\textrm{ connects }(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}))

(289)

We can regard the tree graph
\tau
as rooted on
X_{1}
and specified by
a mapping on
(1,2,\dots,n)
satisfying
\tau(j)<j
. The statement that

X_{j}\cap X_{\tau(j)}\neq\emptyset

for

.
Now we have.

\|K\|_{h,\Gamma_{3}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\tau}\sum_{(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):X_{j}\cap X_{\tau(j)}\neq\emptyset}1(X_{1}\supset\square)\prod^{n}_{i=1}\|E(X_{i})\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(X_{i})

(290)

We estimate successively the sums over
X_{n},X_{n-1},\dots

\sum_{X_{n}\cap X_{\tau(n)}\neq\emptyset}\|E(X_{n})\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(X_{n})\leq\sum_{\square\in X_{\tau(n)}}\sum_{X_{n}\supset\square}\|E(X_{n})\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(X_{n})=|X_{\tau(n)}|\|E\|_{h,\Gamma_{3}}

(291)

In the

where the incidence number

is the number of elements in

\leq&(d_{j}-1)!|X_{\tau(j)}|\|E\|_{h,\Gamma_{4}}\\
\end{split}

(292)

The last step is just

\begin{split}\sum_{X_{1}\cap\square\neq\emptyset}|X_{1}|^{d_{1}-1}\|E(X_{1})\|_{h}\Gamma_{3}(X_{1})\leq&(d_{1}-1)!\sum_{X_{1}\cap\square\neq\emptyset}\|E(X_{1})\|_{h}\Gamma_{4}(X_{1})\\

Altogether then we have

(296)

(and hence for

The other terms are either linear or quadratic in

, and it is better to evaluate derivatives directly. The term

is proportional to

, the derivative reduces this to

, and as in lemma

.
Fourth order terms in (

) are estimated in (

). The derivative in

reduces the bounds by

and they are all less than

Altogether the bound is the announced

We study the flow of the renormalization group equations (

Our basic structural stability result is the following.

.

be chosen as in theorem

Lemma 23
.

Let
G,h
satisfy the above conditions

.

Remarks
. The determinant is the Fredholm determinant, see Simon
[
27
]
. This can also be written

\int e^{-<\bar{\psi},J\psi>}d\mu=\det(I+JG)

(473)

Proof
. First we note that

is trace class so the determinant is well defined. In fact
G
alone is trace class
since

and each of these convolutions is Hilbert-Schmidt. Indeed the kernel of

has Hilbert-Schmidt norm squared

\int|G_{i}(x-y)|^{2}dxdy=\|G_{i}\|^{2}_{2}|{\mathbb{T}}_{j}|<\infty

(474)

Now we compute

\begin{split}\int e^{<\psi,J\bar{\psi}>}d\mu_{G}=&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int J(x_{1},y_{1})\cdots J(x_{n},y_{n})\det\Big\{G(x_{i},y_{i})\Big\}dxdy\\
=&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int\det\Big\{(J^{T}G)(x_{i},x_{j})\Big\}dx\\
=&\det(I+J^{T}G)\\
\end{split}

(475)

The last step is the Fredholm formula
[
27
]
.

For the converse suppose that

\begin{split}&\int\Big[\int\psi(x_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{n})\ d\mu\Big]\prod_{i=1}^{n}J(x_{i},y_{i})dxdy\\

\end{split}

(476)

.
Buy the kernel theorem the associated kernels are equal as distributions and hence as functions.
Thus

\int\psi(x_{1})\bar{\psi}(y_{1})\cdots\psi(x_{n})\bar{\psi}(y_{n})\ d\mu=\det\Big\{G(x_{i},y_{j})\Big\}

which is our result.

Gamma matrices

Let
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
be
2\times 2
traceless matrices satisfying
\{\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}\}=2\delta_{\mu\nu}

[
10
]

[
11
]

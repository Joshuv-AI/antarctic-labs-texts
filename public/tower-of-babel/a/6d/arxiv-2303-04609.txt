On Card guessing with two types of cards
Markus Kuba, Alois Panholzer

Abstract
. We consider a card guessing strategy for a stack of cards with two different types of cards, say m 1 m_{1} cards of type red (heart or diamond) and m 2 m_{2} cards of type black (clubs or spades). Given a deck of M = m 1 + m 2 M=m_{1}+m_{2} cards, we propose a refined counting of the number of correct color guesses, when the guesser is provided with complete information, in other words, when the numbers m 1 m_{1} and m 2 m_{2} and the color of each drawn card are known. We decompose the correct guessed cards into three different types by taking into account the probability of making a correct guess, and provide joint distributional results for the underlying random variables as well as joint limit laws.

1.
Introduction

Card guessing games have been considered in the literature in many articles
[
4
,
11
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
. As the basic setting a randomized deck of
M
cards is considered.
A person is asked to guess the card on top of the deck. Afterwards, the top card is revealed to the guesser and then discarded.
This process is continued until no more cards are left. If the person guessing the cards knows in advance the composition of the
deck before randomization, say the number of hearts, diamonds, clubs and spades, respectively, one is interested in the number of correct guesses, its distribution, expectation and limit laws, under the assumption that the guesser uses this information to maximize the chance of a correct guess in each step. This is sometimes generalized to
n\geq 2
types of different cards
[
4
,
11
,
20
]
, but so far only in terms of the expected value.
There are also variations of the card guessing procedure with only partial information revealed, we refer to the works of Diaconis et al.
[
3
,
5
,
6
]
, and also to
[
2
,
8
]
for applications in clinical trials.

We consider the setting where two different kinds of cards are considered, say red (heart or diamond) and black (clubs or spades).
Their numbers are given by non-negative integers
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
, with
M=m_{1}+m_{2}
. We are interested in the random variable
C_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, counting the number of correct guesses. This quantity has been studied in
[
15
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
:
Sulanke
[
23
]
obtained the probability mass function of
C_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, which was later rederived by Knopfmacher and Prodinger
[
15
]
. Zagier
[
24
]
considered the distribution and the expectation of
C_{m,m}
. The epected value is also covered by the before mentioned general results for
n\geq 2
different types. A more general result for the expected value
for arbitrary
m_{1},m_{2}
was obtained in
[
15
]
. We also mention the work
[
16
]
,
which rederives results for
C_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, but seems to be unaware of the previous works. Moreover, limit laws for
C_{m_{1},m_{2}}
have been derived in
[
18
]
, however without stating proofs, leading to phase transitions according to the different growths of
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
in the general case
\max\{m_{1},m_{2}\}\to\infty
.

In the following we gain more insight into the number of correct guesses, its structure, as well as its limit laws.
We decompose the correct guessed cards
C_{m_{1},m_{2}}
into three different types according to the probability
p\in[0.5,1]
of a correct guess. Given
m_{1}
red and
m_{2}
black cards, where without loss of generality we restrict ourselves to
0\leq m_{2}\leq m_{1}
, we are interested in the number of boundary cases, say
p=0.5
, or
p=1
, or the cases in between,
0.5<p<1
. In more detail, this means that we consider draws, where the guesser knows for sure he will be correct,
p=1
,
second, draws, where the guesser has a pure luck fifty-fifty chance, and third, draws, where the guesser has a chance for a correct guess strictly between fifty and one hundred percent,
0.5<p<1
.

Let
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
denote the number of certified correct guesses,
L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
denote the number of more likely guesses,
and
P_{m_{1},m_{2}}
the number of pure luck guesses. These random variables refine the total number of correct guesses, as we have the identity

C_{m_{1},m_{2}}=T_{m_{1},m_{2}}+L_{m_{1},m_{2}}+P_{m_{1},m_{2}}.

Moreover, the three random variables will give a detailed insight in how big the advantage of an educated guesser
is compared to just random guessing. We note in advance that intuition dictates for
m_{1}\gg m_{2}
, the number of trivially correct guesses
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
should dominate the asymptotics of
C_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, whereas for
m_{1}\sim m_{2}
, of the same order, we should observe an
interplay between more likely guesses
L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and pure luck
P_{m_{1},m_{2}}
.

Interestingly, it will turn out that the random variable
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
links the card guessing game directly to so-called
diminishing urn models
[
7
,
12
,
17
]
: one interprets the card guessing game as an urn containing two types of balls,
say red and black. At random, balls are drawn from the urn, their color inspected and then removed. This is well known as a
sampling without replacement urn:
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
One is interested in the number of balls, when one type is emptied. Using a geometric interpretation via lattice paths, cf. Section
2.2
, this corresponds to an absorption at the coordinate axes
[
12
]
, similar to
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
.

We propose a generating function approach, based on Prodinger and Knopfmacher’s analysis
[
15
]
,
to determine the distribution and limit laws of the aforehand stated random variables.
This allows to obtain a detailed insight into the nature of the limit laws
[
18
]
,
as well as the precise relation of the card guessing game to so-called diminishing urn models.

As a remark concerning notation used throughout this work, we always write
X\law Y
to express equality in distribution of two random variables (r.v.)
X
and
Y
, and
X_{n}\claw X
for the weak convergence (i.e., convergence in distribution) of a sequence
of random variables
X_{n}
to a r.v.
X
. Furthermore we use
x^{\underline{s}}:=x(x-1)\dots(x-(s-1))
for the falling factorials, and
x^{\overline{s}}:=x(x+1)\dots(x+s-1)
for the rising factorials,
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Moreover,
f_{n}\ll g_{n}
denotes that a sequence
f_{n}
is asymptotically smaller than a sequence
g_{n}
, i.e.,
f_{n}=o(g_{n})
,
n\to\infty
.

2.
Distributional analysis

2.1.
Decomposition of pure luck

In order to analyze the number of pure luck guesses, we actually turn to another random variable,

W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, counting the number of times during the card guessing process when the number of red and black cards are equal and non-zero. This random variable allows to obtain a decomposition of pure luck guesses.

Lemma 1

(Decomposition of pure luck guesses)
.

Let
B(n,p)
denote a Binomial distribution with success probability
p
and
n
trials. The random variable
P_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, counting the number of pure luck guesses,
is distributed as a Binomial distribution with a random number of trials:

P_{m_{1},m_{2}}\law B\big(W_{m_{1},m_{2}},\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

Proof.

By definition of
P_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, each individual such guess happens when we have a success probability of
p=0.5
, thus, when both types of cards are equally many. At any time reaching a composition
(j,j)\neq(0,0)
(for the pair consisting of the number of cards of each color), we thus can toss a fair coin, independent of what has happened before. Since
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
counts the (random) number of times we reach such a state
(j,j)\neq(0,0)
with equal numbers, the result follows.
∎

2.2.
Geometric interpretation via lattice paths and decompositions

Lemma 2
.

The random variables
L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, counting the more likely guesses and the trivial guesses, satisfy

L_{m_{1},m_{2}}+T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=m_{1}.

(1)

Consequently, the total number of correct guesses satisfies

C_{m_{1},m_{2}}=m_{1}+P_{m_{1},m_{2}}\law m_{1}+B\big(W_{m_{1},m_{2}},\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

(2)

Remark 1
.

We remark that the latter relation gives an explanation to the occurrence of a shift by
m_{1}
, appearing in
[
18
]
when stating the limiting distribution behaviour of
C_{m_{1},m_{2}}
.

Proof.

For a geometric interpretation of the card guessing process, we think of the numbers
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
as the
(x,y)
-coordinates of a particle in the wedge
x\geq y\geq 0
.
The evolution of the process is described via weighted lattice paths from
(m_{1},m_{2})
to the origin with step sets “left”
(-1,0)
and “down”
(0,-1)
.

Namely, when
m_{1}>m_{2}\geq 0
, a left step
(m_{1},m_{2})\to(m_{1}-1,m_{2})
and a down step
(m_{1},m_{2})\to(m_{1},m_{2}-1)
, resp., reflect the draw of a card of the first or second type, resp., and appear with probabilities
\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}
and
\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}
, respectively. Consequently, a left step corresponds to a correct guess, either, if
m_{2}=0
, a certified correct guess increasing the counter of
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, or, if
m_{2}\geq 1
, a more likely correct guess increasing the counter of
L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
. In our generating functions approach, this results in respective weights of the steps. Note that apart from introducing certain weighted left steps, this description simply corresponds to the aforehand mentioned interpretation of diminishing urns in terms of lattice paths.

However, when
m_{1}=m_{2}\geq 1
, a down step
(m_{1},m_{1})\to(m_{1},m_{1}-1)
occurs with probability
1
, which actually combines the draw of a card of the second type and the draw of a card of the first type, both appearing with probability
\frac{1}{2}
, where in the latter case we may think of exchanging the rôles of the first and second color, such that the amount of cards of the second type never exceeds that one of the first type.
A down step starting at the diagonal increases the counter of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and, in the generating functions approach, results in a respective weight. As pointed out in Lemma
1
, each down step from the diagonal corresponds to a Bernoulli trial, which yields with probability
\frac{1}{2}
a correct pure luck guess.

It follows from this geometric interpretation that each step to the left contributes either one to the number of certified correct guesses, which happens exactly if this step is on the
x
-axis, or one to the number of more likely correct guesses, if it is above the
x
-axis, whereas down-steps do not contribute to them. This implies the stated relation between the r.v.
L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
.
Moreover, together with Lemma
1
, yields the stated representation of the number of correct guesses.
∎

2.3.
Generating function approach

We define the multivariate generating function

\varphi_{m_{1},m_{2}}:=\varphi_{m_{1},m_{2}}(u_{1},u_{2},w)
as the multivariate probability generating function of

L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
,
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
as well as
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
:

\varphi_{m_{1},m_{2}}(u_{1},u_{2},w)={\mathbb{E}}\big(u_{1}^{L_{m_{1},m_{2}}}u_{2}^{T_{m_{1},m_{2}}}w^{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}\big).

By distinguishing the cases according to the first card drawn, we obtain the recurrence relation

\varphi_{m_{1},m_{2}}=u_{1}\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}\varphi_{m_{1}-1,m_{2}}+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}\varphi_{m_{1},m_{2}-1},

with
M=m_{1}+m_{2}
, for
m_{1}>m_{2}>0
, and with initial values
\varphi_{m,0}=u_{2}^{m}
,
m\geq 0
. Moreover,

\varphi_{m,m}=w\varphi_{m,m-1},\quad m\geq 1.

In order to simplify the analysis, we consider the quantity

\Phi_{m_{1},m_{2}}(u_{1},u_{2},w)=\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}\varphi_{m_{1},m_{2}}(u_{1},u_{2},w),

leading to the simplified recurrence relations

\Phi_{m_{1},m_{2}}=u_{1}\Phi_{m_{1}-1,m_{2}}+\Phi_{m_{1},m_{2}-1},\quad m_{1}>m_{2}>0,

(3)

with
\Phi_{m,0}=u_{2}^{m}
,
m\geq 0
. Furthermore,

\Phi_{m,m}=2w\Phi_{m,m-1},\quad m\geq 1.

(4)

Next, we introduce additional generating functions

F(z,x):=F(z,x,u_{1},u_{2},w)=\sum_{m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq 0}\Phi_{m_{1},m_{2}}z^{m_{1}}x^{m_{1}-m_{2}}.

We use the additional notation

F_{0}(z)=F(z,0)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\Phi_{m,m}z^{m},

as well as

F_{1}(z)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\Phi_{m,m-1}z^{m}.

Proposition 1
.

The generating function
F(z,x)
of weighted multivariate probability generating functions
satisfies the functional equation

\Big(1-u_{1}zx-\frac{1}{x}\Big)F(z,x)=\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})zx}{1-u_{2}zx}+F_{0}(z)\Big(1-\frac{1}{x}\Big)-F_{1}(z),

(5)

and

F_{0}(z)=1+2wF_{1}(z).

Proof.

The basic recurrence relation (
3
) is readily translated into a functional equation for
F(z,x)
. Summation leads to the equation

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}\Phi_{m_{1},m_{2}}z^{m_{1}}x^{m_{1}-m_{2}}

\displaystyle=u_{1}\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}\Phi_{m_{1}-1,m_{2}}z^{m_{1}}x^{m_{1}-m_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}\Phi_{m_{1},m_{2}-1}z^{m_{1}}x^{m_{1}-m_{2}}.

After standard arguments, index shifts and splitting the sum, the left hand side evaluates to

F(z,x)-F_{0}(z)-\frac{u_{2}zx}{1-u_{2}zx}.

The two sums on the right hand side simplify as follows:

u_{1}\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}\Phi_{m_{1}-1,m_{2}}z^{m_{1}}x^{m_{1}-m_{2}}=u_{1}zxF(z,x)-\frac{u_{1}zx}{1-u_{2}zx}

and

\sum_{m_{1}>m_{2}>0}\Phi_{m_{1},m_{2}-1}z^{m_{1}}x^{m_{1}-m_{2}}=\frac{1}{x}\big(F(z,x)-F_{0}(z)\big)-F_{1}(z).

Finally, we note that (
4
) directly implies
F_{0}(z)=1+2wF_{1}(z)
. Simplifications then lead to the stated functional equation.
∎

Lemma 3
.

The generating function
F(z,x)
of weighted multivariate probability generating functions
is given by

F(z,x)=\frac{1}{1-x+u_{1}zx^{2}}\bigg(\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})zx^{2}}{1-u_{2}zx}+1-x+F_{1}(z)\big(2w(1-x)+x\big)\bigg),

with

F_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{2w(1-\lambda)+\lambda}\cdot\Big(\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})z\lambda^{2}}{1-u_{2}z\lambda}+\lambda-1\Big)

and

\lambda:=\lambda(z,u_{1})=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4zu_{1}}}{2u_{1}z}.

Proof.

We use a basic application of the so-called kernel method, see, e.g.,
[
1
,
21
]
. First, we multiply the whole equation (
5
) by
(-x)
.
The kernel
K(z,x,u_{1})=1-x+u_{1}zx^{2}
is canceled by the power series
x=\lambda=\lambda(z,u_{1})
stated in the formulation of the lemma:

K\big(z,\lambda(z,u_{1}),u_{1}\big)=0.

Thus, by plugging
x=\lambda
into the equation, the left-hand side and so also the right-hand side vanish, which implies the additional relation

0=\frac{(u_{1}-u_{2})z\lambda^{2}}{1-u_{2}z\lambda}+\big(1-\lambda\big)F_{0}(z)+\lambda F_{1}(z).

Using
F_{0}(z)=1+2wF_{1}(z)
then directly leads to the stated results.
∎

Due to the dependence of the r.v.
L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
stated in (
1
), we may restrict ourselves to a joint analysis of
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
. For extracting coefficients, we find it convenient to introduce the generating function

\hat{F}(z,y):=\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=\sum_{m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq 0}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\big(u^{T_{m_{1},m_{2}}}w^{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}\big)z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}\\
=\sum_{m_{1}\geq 0}\sum_{m_{2}=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m_{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_{1}}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}{\mathbb{P}}\big\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\big\}w^{k}u^{\ell}z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}},

(6)

which is obtained from
F(z,x,u_{1},u_{2},w)
via
\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=F(zy,y^{-1},1,u,w)
. Note that the natural restrictions
0\leq\ell\leq m_{1}
,
0\leq k\leq m_{2}
easily follow from the geometric interpretation given in Section
2.2
.

Starting with Lemma
3
, we easily get the following representation of
\hat{F}(z,y)
:

\begin{split}\hat{F}(z,y)&=\frac{1-z}{(1-y-z)(1-uz)}-\frac{y}{1-y-z}\\
&\quad\mbox{}+\frac{uzy(2w(1-y)-1)}{(1-y-z)(1-2wP(zy))(1-uP(zy))},\end{split}

(7)

with

P(q)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4q}}{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\binom{2n-2}{n-1}q^{n}

denoting the generating function of shifted Catalan numbers, satisfying the functional equation

P(q)=\frac{q}{1-P(q)}.

Extracting coefficients from
\hat{F}(z,y)
yields an explicit formula for the joint distribution of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
(and, in view of the stated dependency (
1
), also of
L_{m_{1},m_{2}}
) given next.

Theorem 4
.

The joint distribution of the random variables
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
is, for
0\leq m_{2}\leq m_{1}
,
0\leq\ell\leq m_{1}
, and
0\leq k\leq m_{2}
, given as follows, outside this range the probabilities are zero, anyway.

{\mathbb{P}}\big\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\big\}=\\
\begin{cases}\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}-1}-\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}},&\quad\text{$k=0$ and $m_{2},\ell\geq 1$},\\[8.61108pt]
\frac{2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-k}{m_{1}-1}-2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-k-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}},&\quad\text{$\ell,k\geq 1$ and $(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(\ell,k)$},\\[8.61108pt]
1,&\quad\text{$k=m_{2}=0$ and $\ell=m_{1}$},\\[2.15277pt]
1,&\quad\text{$m_{1}=m_{2}=k=\ell=1$},\\[2.15277pt]
0,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

A key rôle in our approach to get sum-free expressions when extracting coefficients from equation (
7
) plays the following split of the bivariate generating function
\frac{1}{1-y-z}=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0}f_{m_{1},m_{2}}z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}
, with
f_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}
, into generating functions
S^{+}(z,y)
and
S^{-}(z,y)
, where
S^{+}(z,y)
only contains the terms
f_{m_{1},m_{2}}z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}
with
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq 0
, and
S^{-}(z,y)
only the ones with
m_{2}>m_{1}\geq 0
:

\frac{1}{1-y-z}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{(1-2P(zy))\big(1-\frac{z}{1-P(zy)}\big)}}_{=:S^{+}(z,y)}+\underbrace{\frac{P(zy)}{z(1-2P(zy))\big(1-\frac{P(zy)}{z}\big)}}_{=:S^{-}(z,y)}.

(8)

This representation can be obtained easily by setting
q:=zy
and carrying out a partial fraction expansion of the resulting factorization:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-y-z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\frac{q}{z}-z}=\frac{-z}{z^{2}-z+q}=\frac{-z}{(z-P(q))\big(z-(1-P(q))\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-2P(q))\big(1-\frac{z}{1-P(q)}\big)}+\frac{P(q)}{z(1-2P(q))\big(1-\frac{P(q)}{z}\big)}.

Since we are interested in the coefficients of
z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}
, with
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq 0
, of the generating functions occurring, we may reduce the task of extracting coefficients to expressions, where we replace
\frac{1}{1-y-z}
by
S^{+}(z,y)
. In particular, we use this replacement together with an application of the formal residue calculus (or alternatively, Cauchy’s integration formula), see, e.g.,
[
10
]
, to extract coefficients of the following expression.

Let
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq b+r\geq b\geq a\geq 0
. Setting
q=zy
and also taking into account
q=P(q)(1-P(q))
and
\frac{dq}{dP}=1-2P
, we get:

\displaystyle[z^{m_{1}-a}y^{m_{2}-b}]\frac{P(zy)^{r}}{1-y-z}=[z^{m_{1}-m_{2}+b-a}q^{m_{2}-b}]\frac{P(q)^{r}}{1-y-z}

\displaystyle\quad=[z^{m_{1}-m_{2}+b-a}q^{m_{2}-b}]\frac{P(q)^{r}}{(1-2P(q))\big(1-\frac{z}{1-P(q)}\big)}

\displaystyle\quad=[q^{m_{2}-b}]\frac{P(q)^{r}}{(1-2P(q))\big(1-P(q)\big)^{m_{1}-m_{2}+b-a}}

\displaystyle\quad=[q^{-1}]\frac{1}{P(q)^{m_{2}-b+1-r}(1-2P(q))(1-P(q))^{m_{1}-a+1}}

\displaystyle\quad=[P^{-1}]\frac{1-2P}{P^{m_{2}-b+1-r}(1-2P)(1-P)^{m_{1}-a+1}}=[P^{m_{2}-b-r}]\frac{1}{(1-P)^{m_{1}-a+1}}

\displaystyle\quad=\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-a-b-r}{m_{1}-a}.

(9)

With the latter result, we are ready to extract coefficients
[z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}w^{k}u^{\ell}]
from (
7
), where we recall the natural restrictions
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq 0
,
0\leq\ell\leq m_{1}
, and
0\leq k\leq m_{2}
. We distinguish between the cases
k\geq 1
and
k=0
.

For
k\geq 1
, it necessarily also must hold
\ell\geq 1
in order to get non-zero coefficients, where we obtain

\displaystyle[u^{\ell}w^{k}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)

\displaystyle=[u^{\ell}w^{k}]\frac{uzy(2w(1-y)-1)}{(1-y-z)(1-2wP(zy))(1-uP(zy))}

\displaystyle=[w^{k}]\frac{zy(2w(1-y)-1)P(zy)^{\ell-1}}{(1-y-z)(1-2wP(zy))}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k}zy}{1-y-z}\Big((1-y)P(zy)^{\ell+k-2}-P(zy)^{\ell+k-1}\Big),

thus

[z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}u^{\ell}w^{k}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=2^{k}[z^{m_{1}-1}y^{m_{2}-1}]\frac{P(zy)^{\ell+k-2}}{1-y-z}\\
\mbox{}-2^{k}[z^{m_{1}-1}y^{m_{2}-2}]\frac{P(zy)^{\ell+k-2}}{1-y-z}-2^{k}[z^{m_{1}-1}y^{m_{2}-1}]\frac{P(zy)^{\ell+k-1}}{1-y-z}.

Using (
9
), one gets the explicit formula

[z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}u^{\ell}w^{k}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-k}{m_{1}-1}-2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-k-1}{m_{1}-1},

(10)

which holds for
1\leq\ell\leq m_{1}
,
1\leq k\leq m_{2}
and
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(\ell,k)
. For
(m_{1},m_{2})=(\ell,k)
one can check easily that
[z^{1}y^{1}u^{1}w^{1}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=2
, and zero, otherwise.

For
k=0
, we first obtain

\displaystyle[w^{0}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)

\displaystyle=\hat{F}(z,y,u,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z}{(1-y-z)(1-uz)}-\frac{y}{1-y-z}-\frac{uzy}{(1-y-z)(1-uP(zy))}.

We further distinguish between
\ell=0
and
\ell\geq 1
. The case
\ell=0
yields

[u^{0}w^{0}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=\hat{F}(z,y,0,0)=1,

thus
[z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}u^{0}w^{0}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=1
, for
m_{1}=m_{2}=0
, and the coefficients are zero, otherwise.

For the case
\ell\geq 1
, we proceed with

[u^{\ell}w^{0}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=\frac{(1-z)z^{\ell}}{1-y-z}-\frac{zyP(zy)^{\ell-1}}{1-y-z}.

Since

\displaystyle[z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}]\frac{(1-z)z^{\ell}}{1-y-z}

\displaystyle=[z^{m_{1}-\ell}y^{m_{2}}]\frac{1}{1-\frac{y}{1-z}}=[z^{m_{1}-\ell}]\frac{1}{(1-z)^{m_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}-1},

and, by an application of (
9
),

[z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}]\frac{zyP(zy)^{\ell-1}}{1-y-z}=\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1},

we thus get the explicit formula

[z^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}u^{\ell}w^{0}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}-1}-\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1},

(11)

which holds for
\ell,m_{2}\geq 1
. Moreover,
[z^{m_{1}}y^{0}u^{m_{1}}w^{0}]\hat{F}(z,y,u,w)=1
, and the coefficients are zero, otherwise.

The exact formulæ for the joint distribution of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
follow then from (
10
), (
11
), together with the particular cases mentioned, by taking into account the defining equation (
6
).
∎

From this explicit result the marginal distributions can be obtained easily.

Corollary 1
.

The exact distributions of the random variables
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, respectively, are, for
0\leq m_{2}\leq m_{1}
, and
0\leq\ell\leq m_{1}
or
0\leq k\leq m_{2}
, respectively, given as follows, outside this range the probabilities are zero, anyway.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big\{T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\big\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}-1}+\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}},&\quad\text{$m_{2},\ell\geq 1$},\\[8.61108pt]
1,&\quad\text{$m_{2}=0$ and $\ell=m_{1}$},\\[2.15277pt]
0,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\big\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k}{m_{1}}-2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-1}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}},&\quad\text{$m_{1}\geq 1$},\\[8.61108pt]
1,&\quad\text{$m_{1}=m_{2}=k=0$},\\[2.15277pt]
0,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The stated results for the marginal distributions follow from Theorem
4
by summation. For the probability mass function of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
one just has to apply the basic summation formula
\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{\ell}{m}=\binom{n+1}{m+1}
, whereas the corresponding result for
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
follows from the fact that the sum running over
k
telescopes.
∎

3.

3.1.
Limiting behaviour of the marginal distributions

We first state the limiting behaviour of the individual r.v.
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, respectively, for
m_{1}\to\infty
and depending on the growth behaviour of
m_{2}

m_{2}=0
, since then
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
have deterministic distributions.

Theorem 5
.

The limiting distribution behaviour of the random variable
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
is, for
0\leq m_{2}\leq m_{1}
and
m_{1}\to\infty
, depending on the growth behaviour of
m_{2}
given as follows.

•

m_{2}\geq 1
fixed: suitably scaled,
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
converges in distribution to a r.v.
Y^{[m_{2}]}
, which is characterized via the distribution function

F(y)=1-(1-y)^{m_{2}},\quad 0<y<1.

It holds that
Y^{[m_{2}]}
is Beta distributed with parameters
1
and
m_{2}
, or alternatively, distributed as the minimum of
m_{2}
independent uniform distributed r.v. on the interval
[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\frac{T_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{m_{1}}\claw Y^{[m_{2}]},\quad Y^{[m_{2}]}\law\BetaDist(1,m_{2})\law\min\{U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{m_{2}}\},

\displaystyle\text{where $U_{i}\law\Unif[0,1]$, $1\leq i\leq m_{2}$, and the $U_{i}$ mutually independent}.

•

m_{2}=o(m_{1})
, but
m_{2}\to\infty
: suitably scaled,
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
is asymptotically exponential distributed with parameter
1
:

\displaystyle\frac{m_{2}\cdot T_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{m_{1}}\claw Y,\quad Y\law\Exp(1),

\displaystyle\text{i.e., $Y$ has the distribution function $F(y)=1-e^{-y}$, $y>0$}.

•

m_{2}\sim\rho\,m_{1}
, with
0<\rho\leq 1
:
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
converges in distribution to a discrete r.v.
Y_{\rho}
, whose probability mass function is given as follows:

p_{\ell}^{[Y]}:={\mathbb{P}}\{Y_{\rho}=\ell\}=\frac{\rho+\rho^{\ell}}{(1+\rho)^{\ell+1}},\quad\ell\geq 1.

It holds that
Y_{\rho}
is the mixture of two geometrically distributed random variables:

\displaystyle Y_{\rho}\law I_{1}\cdot\Geom\Big(\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\Big)+(1-I_{1})\cdot\Geom\Big(\frac{1}{1+\rho}\Big),

\displaystyle\text{with $I_{1}\law$ $\Bernoulli\Big(\frac{1}{1+\rho}\Big)$}.

Remark 2
.

Note that in the case
m_{2}\sim m_{1}
, above characterization yields a mixture of two identically distributed geometric random variables, thus for
\rho=1
it simply holds

T_{m_{1},m_{2}}\claw Y_{1},\quad\text{with $Y_{1}\law$ $\Geom\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\big)$}.

Remark 3
.

As discussed at the beginning, the limit laws of
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
closely resemble the limit laws of the sampling without replacement urn absorbed at the boundaries
[
17
]
, as one might expect. A heuristic explanation for the limit laws: for small
m_{2}
, i.e.
m_{2}\geq 1
fixed or
m_{2}=o(m_{1})
, but
m_{2}\to\infty
, the influence of the diagonal
y=x
is negligible, but it gets more significant once
m_{2}
and
m_{1}
are of comparable size. In contrast to sampling without replacement, the limit law of
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
cannot degenerate (which happens for the sampling urn, if
m_{2}\gg m_{1}
), as we stay in the cone
x\geq y\geq 0
. This heuristic can be made precise, since
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
is distributed as the distance from the origin, where a particle, starting at
(m_{1},m_{2})
and moving according to a sampling without replacement urn, either hits the
x
-axis or the
y
-axis.

Theorem 6
.

The limiting distribution behaviour of the random variable
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
is, for
0\leq m_{2}\leq m_{1}
and
m_{1}\to\infty
, depending on the growth behaviour of
m_{2}
given as follows.

•

m_{2}=o(m_{1})
:
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
has a degenerate limiting distribution,
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}\claw\>0
.

•

m_{2}\sim\rho\,m_{1}
, with
0<\rho<1
:
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
converges in distribution to a (shifted) geometrically distributed r.v.
X_{\rho}
with success probability
\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}

W_{m_{1},m_{2}}\claw X_{\rho},\quad\text{with $X_{\rho}\law$ $\Geom\Big(\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}\Big)-1$},

thus with probability mass function

p_{k}^{[X]}:={\mathbb{P}}\{X_{\rho}=k\}=\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}\cdot\Big(\frac{2\rho}{1+\rho}\Big)^{k},\quad k\geq 0.

•

m_{2}\sim m_{1}
, where the difference
d:=m_{1}-m_{2}

\sqrt{m_{1}}\ll d\ll m_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{d\cdot W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{m_{1}}\claw X,\quad X\law\Exp\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\big),

\displaystyle\text{i.e., $X$ has the distribution function $F(x)=1-e^{-\frac{x}{2}}$, $x>0$}.

•

m_{2}\sim m_{1}
, where the difference

satisfies
d\sim\alpha\sqrt{m_{1}}

, which is characterized via the distribution function

Thus,

\frac{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}\claw X_{\alpha},\quad X_{\alpha}\law\LinExp\big(\textstyle{\frac{\alpha}{2}},\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

converges in distribution to a Rayleigh distributed r.v.
X
with parameter
\sqrt{2}

\displaystyle\frac{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}\claw X,\quad X\law\Rayleigh\big(\sqrt{2}\big),

\displaystyle\text{thus $X$ has the distribution function $F(x)=1-e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4}}$, $x>0$}.

Remark 4

(Properties of linear exponential distributions)
.

The so-called linear exponential distribution
\LinExp(\lambda,\nu)
, see
[
13
, p. 480]
, is a two-parametric family of continuous distributions, with support
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, characterized via the distribution function

F(x)=1-e^{-\big(\lambda x+\frac{\nu x^{2}}{2}\big)},\quad x>0.

It generalizes the exponential distribution as well as the Rayleigh distribution.
Alternatively,
\LinExp(\lambda,\nu)
can be represented in term of an Exponential distribution
Z\law\text{Exp}(1)
:

Y\law-\frac{\lambda}{\mu}+\frac{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+2\mu Z}}{\mu}.

Remark 5
.

The range

Our results in Lemmata

but without proofs. Here, we only additionally note in passing a very simple relation between the moments of

Corollary 2

(Moments of the total number of correct guesses)

Let

.

, where the difference

Proof.

The joint limiting behaviour of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, for

, follows from the explicit formulæ for the joint probability mass function given in Theorem

by distinguishing between various cases according to the growth behaviour of
m_{2}
, using suitable estimates, and applying Stirling’s formula (
12
). For some regions of

we find it slightly more convenient to start with explicit formulæ for the joint or one-sided cumulative distribution functions, which are obtained easily from Theorem

,

and

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq\ell\}\\
=1-\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}}+\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}}+2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-1}{m_{1}}-2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-\ell-1}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}},

(13)

as well as (we omit stating the cases

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\}\\
=\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}-1}+\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1}-2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}.

(14)

Next we sketch the asymptotic considerations for the different cases.

•

m_{2}\geq 1
fixed: setting

in (

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=0\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq\ell\}=1-\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{2}}}-\frac{2\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}+\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}.

(15)

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}=\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\big),

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}^{\underline{\ell+1}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\underline{\ell+1}}}=0,\quad\text{for $\ell\geq m_{2}$},

the asymptotic contribution relevant for
m_{2}
fixed is stemming from

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}^{\underline{\ell+1}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\underline{\ell+1}}}=\frac{(m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1)^{\underline{m_{2}}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\underline{m_{2}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(m_{1}-\ell)^{m_{2}}\cdot\big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{1}{m_{1}-\ell}\big)\big)}{m_{1}^{m_{2}}\cdot\big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{1}{m_{1}}\big)\big)}=\Big(1-\frac{\ell}{m_{1}}\Big)^{m_{2}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{m_{1}-\ell}}\big)\Big).

Thus, for
m_{2}\geq 1
fixed,
m_{1}\to\infty
, and, e.g.,
\sqrt{m_{1}}\leq\ell\leq m_{1}-\sqrt{m_{1}}
, it holds

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=0\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq\ell\}\to 1-\Big(1-\frac{\ell}{m_{1}}\Big)^{m_{2}},

i.e., by setting

,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=0\wedge\frac{T_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{m_{1}}\leq y\Big\}\to F(y)=1-(1-y)^{m_{2}},\quad\text{for $0<y<1$}.

is the distribution function of a Beta distribution or, alternatively, the distribution function of the minimum of
m_{2}
independent uniformly on
[0,1]
distributed r.v.

•

m_{2}=o(m_{1})
, but
m_{2}\to\infty

), where, as a refinement to the considerations of the previous case, we get by assuming

\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\leq\Big(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}\Big)^{\ell+1}=e^{-(\ell+1)\ln\big(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\big)}\leq e^{-\ell},\quad\text{for $\ell\geq 1$},

thus asymptotically negligible contributions for

. Again, the relevant contribution for the asymptotic behaviour is coming from

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}!\cdot(m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1)!}{(m_{1}-\ell-1)!\cdot(m_{1}+m_{2})!}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\ell m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{\ell m_{2}^{2}}{m_{1}^{2}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{\ell^{2}m_{2}}{m_{1}^{2}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{\ell}{m_{1}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\big)\Big),

where the asymptotic evaluation is obtained by applying Stirling’s formula.
Thus, for
m_{1},m_{2}\to\infty
,
m_{2}=o(m_{1})
, and say
\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\leq\ell\leq\min\Big\{\big(\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\big)^{\frac{3}{2}},\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}^{\frac{3}{4}}}\Big\}
, it holds

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=0\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq\ell\}\to 1-e^{-\frac{\ell m_{2}}{m_{1}}},

i.e., by setting

,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=0\wedge\frac{m_{2}\cdot T_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{m_{1}}\leq y\Big\}\to 1-e^{-y},\quad\text{for $y>0$}.

•

m_{2}\sim\rho m_{1}
, with
0<\rho<1
: in this range one considers the asymptotic behaviour of the probability mass function given in Theorem
4
, for fixed
k\geq 0
,
\ell\geq 1
. The case

has to be treaten separately, where we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}m_{1}^{\underline{\ell}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\underline{\ell+1}}}=\frac{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}{\big(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\big)^{\ell+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{m_{1}}}\big)\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}^{\underline{\ell}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\underline{\ell+1}}}=\frac{(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}})^{\ell}}{\big(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\big)^{\ell+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{m_{2}}}\big)\Big).

Thus, for
m_{1}\to\infty
,
m_{2}\sim\rho m_{1}
, with
0<\rho<1
, and
\ell\geq 1
fixed, it holds

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=0\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\}\to\frac{\rho-\rho^{\ell}}{(1+\rho)^{\ell+1}}.

Similarly, for
k\geq 1
one gets

\displaystyle\frac{2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-k}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k}m_{1}m_{2}^{\underline{\ell+k-1}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\underline{\ell+k}}}=\frac{2^{k}(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}})^{\ell+k-1}}{\big(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\big)^{\ell+k}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{m_{2}}}\big)\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-k-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k+1}m_{1}m_{2}^{\underline{\ell+k}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\underline{\ell+k+1}}}=\frac{2^{k+1}(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}})^{\ell+k}}{\big(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\big)^{\ell+k+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{m_{2}}}\big)\Big),

which implies, for
m_{1}\to\infty
,
m_{2}\sim\rho m_{1}
, with
0<\rho<1
, and
k,\ell\geq 1
fixed,

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\}\to\frac{2^{k}\rho^{\ell+k-1}}{(1+\rho)^{\ell+k}}-\frac{2^{k+1}\rho^{\ell+k}}{(1+\rho)^{\ell+k+1}}\\
=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho(1+\rho)}\cdot\Big(\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\Big)^{\ell}\cdot\Big(\frac{2\rho}{1+\rho}\Big)^{k}.

•

m_{2}\sim m_{1}
, where the difference
d:=m_{1}-m_{2}
satisfies
\sqrt{m_{1}}\ll d\ll m_{1}
: we start with (
14
) and examine its asymptotic behaviour for
\ell\geq 1
fixed, where at first we only assume
d=o(m_{1})
. Analogous to previous considerations we get

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{2}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}{\big(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\big)^{\ell+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{m_{1}}}\big)\Big)=\frac{1}{2^{\ell+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{d}{m_{1}}}\big)\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}})^{\ell}}{\big(1+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\big)^{\ell+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{1}{m_{1}}}\big)\Big)=\frac{1}{2^{\ell+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{d}{m_{1}}}\big)\Big),

and

\frac{2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-\ell-1}{m_{1}-1}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}=\frac{2m_{1}(m_{2}-k)^{\underline{\ell}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2}-k)^{\underline{\ell+1}}}\cdot\frac{2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}.

It easily follows that, for
\ell\geq 1
fixed,

\frac{2m_{1}(m_{2}-k)^{\underline{\ell}}}{(m_{1}+m_{2}-k)^{\underline{\ell+1}}}=\frac{2m_{1}m_{2}^{\ell}}{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\ell+1}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{k}{m_{2}}}\big)\Big)=\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{d}{m_{1}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{k}{m_{1}}}\big)\Big),

whereas, by an application of Stirling’s formula, one obtains

\frac{2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}=e^{-\frac{k(2d+k)}{4m_{1}}}\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{kd^{2}}{m_{1}^{2}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{k^{3}}{m_{1}^{2}}}\big)\Big).

Thus, putting things together, we obtain, for
\ell\geq 1
fixed, the following asymptotic result, valid uniformly for say
k\leq\min\big\{m_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}-\epsilon},(\frac{m_{1}}{d})^{2-\epsilon}\big\}
, for a fixed
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\}

\displaystyle\quad=2^{-\ell}\cdot\left(1-e^{-\frac{k(2d+k)}{4m_{1}}}\right)\cdot\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{d}{m_{1}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{k}{m_{1}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{kd^{2}}{m_{1}^{2}}}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(\textstyle{\frac{k^{3}}{m_{1}^{2}}}\big)\Big).

(16)

Now we restrict our considerations to the range
\sqrt{m_{1}}\ll d\ll m_{1}
. For
m_{1}\to\infty
,
\ell\geq 1
fixed, and say
k\leq\min\Big\{\big(\frac{m_{1}}{d}\big)^{\frac{3}{2}},\frac{m_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\sqrt{d}}\Big\}
, which in addition implies that
k=o(d)
, it holds

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\}\to 2^{-\ell}\cdot\Big(1-e^{-\frac{kd}{2m_{1}}}\Big),

i.e., by setting
x=\frac{kd}{m_{1}}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{\frac{d\cdot W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{m_{1}}\leq x\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\Big\}\to 2^{-\ell}\cdot\Big(1-e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\Big),\quad\text{for $x>0$}.

•

m_{2}\sim m_{1}
, where the difference
d:=m_{1}-m_{2}
satisfies
d\sim\alpha\sqrt{m_{1}}
, with
\alpha>0
: using (
16
), we obtain, for
m_{1}\to\infty
,
\ell\geq 1
fixed, and
k=\mathcal{O}\big(m^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\big)
, with
\epsilon>0
, when setting
x=\frac{k}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{\frac{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}\leq x\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\Big\}\to 2^{-\ell}\cdot\Big(1-e^{-\frac{x(2\alpha+x)}{4}}\Big),\quad\text{for $x>0$}.

•

m_{2}\sim m_{1}
, where the difference
d:=m_{1}-m_{2}
satisfies
d=o(\sqrt{m_{1}})
: For
m_{1}\to\infty
,
\ell\geq 1
fixed, and say
k\leq\min\Big\{m_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon},\frac{m_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\sqrt{d}}\Big\}
, it holds

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}\leq k\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\}\to 2^{-\ell}\cdot\Big(1-e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4m_{1}}}\Big),

i.e., by setting
x=\frac{k}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{\frac{\cdot W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}\leq x\wedge T_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\ell\Big\}\to 2^{-\ell}\cdot\Big(1-e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4}}\Big),\quad\text{for $x>0$}.

∎

3.3.
Correlation in central region

As a consequence of Theorem
7
, the discrete r.v.
\hat{X}_{\rho}
and
\hat{Y}_{\rho}
occurring as limits of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
T_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, resp., in the central region
m_{2}\sim\rho m_{1}
,
0<\rho<1
, are not independent. In order to get some insight into the joint variability of
\hat{X}_{\rho}
and
\hat{Y}_{\rho}
and their linear correlation we compute the covariance between
\hat{X}_{\rho}
and
\hat{Y}_{\rho}
as well as the correlation coefficient.

To this aim, let us introduce the joint moment generating function

\hat{P}(s,t):={\mathbb{E}}\big(e^{s\hat{X}_{\rho}+t\hat{Y}_{\rho}}\big)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}{\mathbb{P}}\big\{\hat{X}_{\rho}=k\wedge\hat{Y}_{\rho}=\ell\big\}e^{ks+\ell t}.

Using
\hat{p}_{k,\ell}={\mathbb{P}}\big\{\hat{X}_{\rho}=k\wedge\hat{Y}_{\rho}=\ell\big\}
as defined in Theorem
7
, we get, after distinguishing
k=0
and
k\geq 1
, via basic summation and simple manipulations,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\hat{p}_{0,\ell}e^{\ell t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{\rho-\rho^{\ell}}{(1+\rho)^{\ell+1}}e^{\ell t}=\frac{\rho(1-\rho)e^{2t}}{(1+\rho)(1+\rho-e^{t})(1+\rho-\rho e^{t})},

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\hat{p}_{k,\ell}e^{ks+\ell t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1-\rho}{\rho(1+\rho)}\Big(\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\Big)^{\ell}\Big(\frac{2\rho}{1+\rho}\Big)^{k}e^{ks+\ell t}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\rho(1-\rho)e^{s+t}}{(1+\rho)(1+\rho-2\rho e^{s})(1+\rho-\rho e^{t})},

and, by combining these expressions, the following result for the joint moment generating function:

\hat{P}(s,t)=\frac{\rho(1-\rho)e^{t}(e^{t}+2e^{s}-2e^{s+t})}{(1+\rho-e^{t})(1+\rho-\rho e^{t})(1+\rho-2\rho e^{s})}.

(17)

Carrying out a series expansion of (
17
) around
(s,t)=(0,0)
and taking into account

{\mathbb{E}}\big(e^{s\hat{X}_{\rho}+t\hat{Y}_{\rho}}\big)=1+{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho})s+{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho})t+{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho}^{2})\frac{s^{2}}{2}+{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho}\hat{Y}_{\rho})st+{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho}^{2})\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}\big((s+t)^{3}\big),

yields the first moments of
\hat{X}_{\rho}
and
\hat{Y}_{\rho}
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho})=\frac{2\rho}{1-\rho},\quad{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho})=\frac{\rho^{2}+1}{\rho},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho}^{2})=\frac{2\rho(1+3\rho)}{(1-\rho)^{2}},\quad{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho}^{2})=\frac{2\rho^{4}+\rho^{3}+\rho+2}{\rho^{2}},\quad{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho}\hat{Y}_{\rho})=\frac{2\rho(1+\rho)}{1-\rho}.

From these expressions, the variances and the covariance immediately follow:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{V}}(\hat{X}_{\rho})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho}^{2})-\big({\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho})\big)^{2}=\frac{2\rho(1+\rho)}{(1-\rho)^{2}},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{V}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho}^{2})-\big({\mathbb{E}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho})\big)^{2}=\frac{1+\rho-2\rho^{2}+\rho^{3}+\rho^{4}}{\rho^{2}},

\displaystyle\Cov(\hat{X}_{\rho},\hat{Y}_{\rho})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho}\hat{Y}_{\rho})-{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{X}_{\rho}){\mathbb{E}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho})=-2.

Thus, the covariance
\Cov(\hat{X}_{\rho},\hat{Y}_{\rho})
does not depend on the ratio
\rho
. Furthermore, the correlation coefficient
C_{\rho}:=\Corr(\hat{X}_{\rho},\hat{Y}_{\rho})
is given by

C_{\rho}=\Corr(\hat{X}_{\rho},\hat{Y}_{\rho})={\textstyle{\frac{\Cov(\hat{X}_{\rho},\hat{Y}_{\rho})}{\sqrt{{\mathbb{V}}(\hat{X}_{\rho})}\,\sqrt{{\mathbb{V}}(\hat{Y}_{\rho})}}}}=-\frac{\sqrt{2\rho}\,(1-\rho)}{\sqrt{(1+\rho)(1+\rho-2\rho^{2}+\rho^{3}+\rho^{4})}}.

Simple calculations show that
C_{\rho}
takes its minimum for
\rho=\tilde{\rho}
, the unique root of
\kappa(\rho)=1-3\rho-3\rho^{2}+3\rho^{3}-6\rho^{4}-2\rho^{5}+2\rho^{6}
in the interval
[0,1]
, numerically,
\tilde{\rho}\approx 0.269187
, with corresponding minimal correlation coefficient
C_{\tilde{\rho}}\approx-0.444039
. The correlation coefficient
C_{\rho}
, considered as a function of the ratio
\rho
, is illustrated in Figure
1
.

C_{\rho}=\Corr(\hat{X}_{\rho},\hat{Y}_{\rho})
as a function of
\rho
in the interval
[0,1]

3.4.
Expectation and higher moments: equal number of cards

At the end we compare our limit laws to the result of Diaconis and Graham
[
4
]
for the expected value.
They considered the more general case of
n
different colours, but restricted themselves there to the special case

m_{1}=m_{2}=\dots=m_{n}=m
, i.e., to the r.v.
C_{m,m,\dots,m}
counting the number of correct guesses when starting with a random deck of
nm
cards containing each of the
n
colours exactly
m
times.

Theorem 8

(Diaconis-Graham
[
4
]
)
.

The expected value
S_{m,n}={\mathbb{E}}(C_{m,m,\dots,m})
of the number of correct guesses satisfies for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
n\geq 2
, and
m\to\infty
the expansion

S_{m,n}=m+\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot M_{n}\cdot\sqrt{m}+o(\sqrt{m}),

where
M_{n}={\mathbb{E}}(\max\{N_{1},\dots,N_{n}\})
and the random variables
N_{k}
are iid standard normal distributed.

In the special case
n=2
we observe

{\mathbb{E}}(C_{m,m})=S_{m,2}=m+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{m}+o(\sqrt{m}),

using the explicit value
1/\sqrt{\pi}
of
M_{2}
. Our results in Theorem
6
suggest that the expected value
and higher moments for
n=2
actually stem from the Rayleigh distribution. We obtain the following result,
which is in accordance with Theorem
8
, as
\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt{\pi}/2
, and extends it from
s=1
to arbitrary high integer moments.

Theorem 9
.

The raw moments of the centered random variable
\hat{C}_{m,m}=C_{m,m}-m
,
counting the total number of correct guesses, satisfy for
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\to\infty
the asymptotic expansion

{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{C}_{m,m}^{s})\sim\frac{1}{2^{s}}{\mathbb{E}}(R^{s})\cdot m^{\frac{s}{2}}=\Gamma\big(\frac{s}{2}+1\big)\cdot m^{\frac{s}{2}},\quad\text{with}\quad R\law\Rayleigh\big(\sqrt{2}\big).

In order to prove this result, we need the asymptotics of the factorial moments of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
in the special case
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
. We use the following result, relating the factorial moments to a Cauchy integral.

Lemma 10
.

For
s\geq 1
the
s
th factorial moment of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
is given by

{\mathbb{E}}(W_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{\underline{s}})=\frac{s!2^{s}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{1}{(1-x)^{s}}\cdot(1+x)^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\frac{dx}{x^{m_{2}+1-s}},

with
\mathcal{C}
a positively oriented simple closed curve around the origin.

Remark 6
.

The result above and Corollary
2
allow to study moment convergence of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
\hat{C}_{m_{1},m_{2}}
not only for
m_{1}=m_{2}=m\to\infty
, but also for the different cases considered in Theorem
6
. However, the analysis gets much more involved and we refrain from going into details.

Proof of Lemma
10
.

It is convenient to consider the binomial moments
{\mathbb{E}}\binom{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{s}
, such that

{\mathbb{E}}(W_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{\underline{s}})=s!\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\binom{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{s}.

From the explicit result in Corollary
1
we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\binom{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=s}^{m_{2}}\binom{k}{s}{\mathbb{P}}(W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\sum_{k=s}^{m_{2}}\binom{k}{s}\Big(2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k}{m_{1}}-2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-1}{m_{1}}\Big).

By the standard property
\binom{k+1}{s}=\binom{k}{s}+\binom{k}{s-1}
we obtain further

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\binom{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{s}=\sum_{k=s}^{m_{2}}\binom{k}{s}{\mathbb{P}}(W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k)

\displaystyle\,=\frac{1}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\sum_{k=s}^{m_{2}}\Big(\binom{k}{s}2^{k}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k}{m_{1}}-\binom{k+1}{s}2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-1}{m_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\sum_{k=s}^{m_{2}-1}\binom{k}{s-1}2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-1}{m_{1}}.

The telescoping sum simplifies to
\frac{2^{s}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-s}{m_{1}}}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}
.
Further simplification gives

{\mathbb{E}}\binom{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{s}=\frac{1}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\sum_{k=s-1}^{m_{2}-1}\binom{k}{s-1}2^{k+1}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-1}{m_{1}}\Big).

Using the standard expansion for integers
n,j\geq 0
:

[t^{n}]\frac{1}{(1-2t)^{j+1}}=2^{n}\binom{n+j}{j},

we obtain

[t^{m_{2}-1}]\frac{t^{s-1}}{(1-2t)^{s}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-t)^{m_{1}+1}}=\sum_{k=s-1}^{m_{2}-1}\binom{k}{s-1}2^{k+1-s}\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-k-1}{m_{1}}.

Thus, by an application of Cauchy’s integration formula we obtain the following contour integral representation of the
s
th factorial moments of
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
:

{\mathbb{E}}(W_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{\underline{s}})=\frac{2^{s}\cdot s!}{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{1}{(1-2t)^{s}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-t)^{m_{1}+1}}\frac{dt}{t^{m_{2}-s+1}}.

Finally, we use the substitution
t=\frac{x}{x+1}
to get the stated result.
∎

Next we are going to asymptotically evaluate the integral for
m_{1}=m_{2}=m\to\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
9
.

Due to the relation between factorial moments and raw moments

{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{C}_{m,m}^{s})=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{s}{j}{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{C}_{m,m}^{\underline{j}}),

where
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{s}{j}
denote the Stirling numbers of the second kind, it suffices to prove the stated expansion for the factorial moments.
By Corollary
2
and Lemma
10
we have

{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{C}_{m,m}^{\underline{s}})=\frac{s!}{\binom{2m}{m}2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{1}{(1-x)^{s}}\cdot(1+x)^{2m}\frac{dy}{x^{m+1-s}}.

Here, we are in a situation similar to the example considered by Flajolet and Sedgewick
[
9
, VIII.39, page 590]
:
the saddle point method is not directly applicable due to the singularity at
x=1
.
However, we can circumvent a more difficult analysis using a trick
[
9
]
, namely another substitution

y=\frac{x}{(1+x)^{2}},\quad x(y)=\frac{1-2y-\sqrt{1-4y}}{2y}\sim 1-2\sqrt{1-4y}+\mathcal{O}(1-4y),

where the latter local expansion holds in a slit neighbourhood of the dominant singularity
y=1/4
.
This leads to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{C}_{m,m}^{\underline{s}})

\displaystyle=\frac{s!}{\binom{2m}{m}2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\big(1+x(y)\big)x(y)^{s}}{(1-x(y))^{s+1}}\frac{dy}{y^{m+1}}.=\frac{s!}{\binom{2m}{m}}[y^{m}]\frac{\big(1+x(y)\big)x(y)^{s}}{(1-x(y))^{s+1}}.

We can use now standard singularity analysis
[
9
]
. An expansion around
y=1/4
gives

\frac{\big(1+x(y)\big)x(y)^{s}}{(1-x(y))^{s+1}}\sim\frac{1}{2^{s}(1-4y)^{\frac{s+1}{2}}}.

Thus, singularity analysis and the classical expansion

\frac{1}{\binom{2m}{m}}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{m}}{4^{m}}

yield

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{C}_{m,m}^{\underline{s}})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{s!m^{\frac{s}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{s}\Gamma(\frac{s+1}{2})}.

By the Legendre duplication formula

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(s+1)}{2^{s}\Gamma(\frac{s+1}{2})}=\Gamma\big(\frac{s}{2}+1\big),

this leads to the stated result

{\mathbb{E}}(\hat{C}_{m,m}^{\underline{s}})\sim\Gamma\big(\frac{s}{2}+1\big)m^{\frac{s}{2}}.

∎

At the very end, we also sketch an alternative approach to Theorem
9
,
based on a local limit theorem; compare also with Zagier’s analysis
[
24
]
for the expected value.
Following our previous analysis of the joint limit laws and using

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m,m}=k\}=\frac{2^{k}\binom{2m-k-1}{m-1-1}\frac{k}{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}},\quad 0\leq k\leq m,

we observe that, for
k
of order
\sqrt{m}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m,m}=k\}\sim\frac{k}{2m}e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4m}}.

Consequently, by Euler’s summation formula

{\mathbb{E}}(W_{m,m}^{s})\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{s}\frac{k}{2m}e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4m}}\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s}\cdot\frac{x}{2m}e^{-x^{2}/4m}dx.

As
f(x)=\frac{x}{2m}e^{-x^{2}/4m}
is the density of a Rayleigh-distributed random variable with parameter
\sigma=\sqrt{2m}
,
we obtain further

{\mathbb{E}}(W_{m,m}^{s})\sim\sigma^{s}\big(\sqrt{2}\big)^{s}\Gamma\big(\frac{s}{2}+1\big)=2^{s}\Gamma\big(\frac{s}{2}+1\big)m^{\frac{s}{2}}.

Appendix: Additional combinatorial models

In the appendix we relate the card guessing game with two types of cards to different combinatorial problems.
This allows to directly translate the results for the random variable
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, see Corollary
1
, Theorems
6
,
7
and Lemma
10
,
to the different random variables stated in the following.

First we recall the definition of the sampling without replacement urn.
An urn contains two types of balls, say
m_{1}
red and
m_{2}
black balls, with
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
The urn evolves by successive draws of random balls at discrete instance according to the transition matrix

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, which means that the colour of the drawn ball is inspected and then the ball is discarded.
Usually, one is interested in the composition of the urn once a colour is fully depleted,
but here we continue this process until the urn is completely empty.

Let
G_{m_{1},m_{2}}
denote the random variable counting the number of times there are equally many red and black balls in the sampling without replacement urn process, except for the empty urn, starting with
m_{1}
red and
m_{2}
black balls,
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq 0
.

Proposition 2

(Card guessing and equality in the sampling without replacement urn)
.

The random variable
G_{m_{1},m_{2}}
has the same distribution as the random variable
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
, counting the number of times during the card guessing process when the number of red and black cards are equal and non-zero.

Remark 7
.

Time reversal
[
7
,
14
]
also allows to obtain a relation of to the number of equalities in
a standard Pólya urn with ball transition matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Proof.

Similar to the geometric interpretation of the card guessing game in Subsection
2.2
,
one also thinks of evolution of the sampling urn in terms of lattice paths
[
7
,
12
]
.
However, we note that the state spaces are different: for sampling without replacement we consider
\Omega_{S}=\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
whereas for the card guessing game we consider only
\Omega_{C}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\colon x\geq y\geq 0\}
.
In order to prove
{\mathbb{P}}\{G_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\}={\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\}
, we proceed by constructing a surjection
\psi
from

\Omega_{S}
to
\Omega_{C}
. Each sample path
\omega\in\Omega_{S}
from the sampling without replacement urn, contributing to
{\mathbb{P}}\{G_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\}
, is either directly contained in
\Omega_{C}
, or it has a subpath going above the diagonal
y=x
.

Figure 2.
A sample path
\sigma\in\Omega_{S}
and its image
\psi(\sigma)=\omega\in\Omega_{C}
.

The surjection
\psi
maps all paths
\sigma\in\Omega_{S}\setminus\Omega_{C}
to a path
\omega
in
\Omega_{C}
by mirroring the parts above the diagonal. Moreover, its restriction is the identity:
\psi|_{\Omega_{C}}=\text{id}_{\Omega_{C}}
. Comparing the weight of a path
\omega\in\Omega_{C}
in the sampling without replacement urn and the card guessing process
with

W_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\omega)=G_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\omega)=k,

we observe
{\mathbb{P}}_{S}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2^{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{C}(\omega)
. All
2^{k}-1
different path
\sigma\in\Omega_{S}\setminus\Omega_{C}

with
\psi(\sigma)=\omega
have the same probability (weight) as
\omega
, and thus

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\{W_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Omega_{C}\\
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\omega)=k\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}_{C}(\omega)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Omega_{C}\\
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\omega)=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\sigma\in\psi^{-1}(\omega)}{\mathbb{P}}_{S}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\Omega_{S}\\
G_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\sigma)=k\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}_{S}(\sigma)={\mathbb{P}}\{G_{m_{1},m_{2}}=k\}.

∎

Next, we relate the problem to classical Dyck paths
[
1
]
.

Proposition 3

(Card guessing and Dyck paths)
.

The random variable
W_{m_{1},m_{2}}
has the same distribution
as the random variable
X_{m_{1}+m_{2}}
, counting the number of returns to zero in a Dyck
walk of length
m_{1}+m_{2}
, with final altitude
m_{2}-m_{1}
.
In the special case
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
we observe a Dyck bridge of length
2m
.

Remark 8
.

The relation between Card guessing, Sampling without replacement and Dyck paths in the Proposition above and
2
also allows
to interpret hitting times considered in
[
18
]
in terms of Dyck paths.

Proof.

We actually show that
G_{m_{1},m_{2}}\law X_{m_{1}+m_{2}}
, which by Proposition
2
leads to the stated result.
First, we observe that for each path from
\sigma\in\Omega_{S}
it holds that

\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}\{\sigma\}=1.

Thus, instead of considering the step by step evolution of the urn we can enumerate all paths
from
(m_{1},m_{2})
to
(0,0)
, touching (or crossing) the diagonal exactly
k
times. Then,
the probability is determined all such paths divided by their total number
\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}
.

Figure 3.
A sample path
\omega\in\Omega_{S}
starting at
(6,3)
and the corresponding Dyck walk of length
9
with final altitude
-3
.

After rotation of the coordinate system, we reverse the direction of paths.
The new steps are
(1,1)
and
(1,-1)
and the length of the walks is given by the sum
m_{1}+m_{2}
. Thus, we observe the relation to the stated classical Dyck walks, where the final altitude is given by

m_{2}-m_{1}
.

Figure 4.
A sample path
\omega\in\Omega_{S}
starting at
(4,4)
and the corresponding Dyck bridge of length
8
.

In particular, for
m_{2}-m_{1}=0
one obtains ordinary Dyck bridges of length
2m
. This can be used to give in the special case
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
a complete different proof of the Rayleigh limit law
for both
W_{m,m}
, as well as
\hat{C}_{m,m}
.
∎

Asymptotic properties for a general class of Szász-Mirakjan-Durrmeyer operators
Ulrich Abel, Ana Maria Acu, Margareta Heilmann, Ioan Raşa

Abstract
In this paper we introduce a general family of Szzász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer type operators depending on an integer parameter j ∈ ℤ j\in\mathbb{Z} . They can be viewed as a generalization of the Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators [ 9 ] , Phillips operators [ 11 ] and corresponding Kantorovich modifications of higher order. For j ∈ ℕ j\in{\mathbb{N}} , these operators possess the exceptional property to preserve constants and the monomial x j x^{j} . It turns out, that an extension of this family covers certain well-known operators studied before, so that the outcoming results could be unified. We present the complete asymptotic expansion for the sequence of these operators. All its coefficients are given in a concise form. In order to prove the expansions for the class of locally integrable functions of exponential growth on the positive half-axis, we derive a localization result which is interesting in itself.

Introduction

For fixed
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we consider sequences of positive linear
operators
S_{n,j}

The operators

continuity. For

The idea of this paper was to construct a family of operators of

It turns out, that an extension of this family covers certain well-known

\left(\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)\right)_{n\geq 1}
even for simultaneous approximation. All its
coefficients are given in a concise form. In order to prove the expansions

For the special case

\displaystyle-\left(j-1\right)\left(j-2\right)e^{-t}+\left[-2-\left(j-1\right)\left(j-2\right)\right]e^{-t}\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h_{2}\left(t\right)\leq-\left(j-1\right)e^{-t}\left(j-2+2\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\right)\leq 0.

Since
\left(S_{n,j}\psi_{x}^{2}\right)\left(x\right)\geq 0
, the
desired estimate of the second central moment follows.

4
Localization result

The purpose of this section is to estimate the rate of convergence of the
sequence of functions
S_{n,j}f
at a point
x>0
if
f\in E_{A}
has the
specific property to vanish in a neighborhood of
x
. Though we need such an
estimate in deriving the asymptotic expansions for the sequence
\left(S_{n,j}f\right)
, this so-called localization result is interesting in
itself.

Theorem 17

Let
f\in E
,
x\geq 0
and
\delta>0
. If
f\left(t\right)=0
, for
t\in\left(x-\delta,x+\delta\right)\cap\left[0,\infty\right)
, then there is a positive constant
c
, such that
\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=O\left(e^{-cn}\right)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

A similar result for a general family of operators can be found in
[
8
, Theorem 8]
. For locally smooth functions
f
, the
conclusion is the weaker relation
\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=O\left(n^{-q}\right)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, for any positive
constant
q
.

For the proof of Theorem
17
we need several
auxiliary results. We start with the following technical lemma.

Lemma 18

The logarithm function satisfies the estimates

\displaystyle\log t

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle t-1-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-t\right)^{2}\text{ \hskip 20.44434pt}\left(0<t<1\right),

\displaystyle\log t

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle t-1-\frac{1}{2}\left(t-1\right)^{2}\left(2-t\right)\text{
\hskip 20.44434pt}\left(1<t<2\right).

Proof.
Let
g(t):=t-1-\dfrac{1}{2}(1-t)^{2}-\log t
,
0<t\leq 1
. Then
g(1)=0
and

g^{\prime}(t)=-\dfrac{(t-1)^{2}}{t}<0
,
0<t<1
. This proves the first
inequality.

Now let
h(t):=t-1-\dfrac{1}{2}(t-1)^{2}(2-t)-\log t
,
1\leq t\leq 2
. Then

h(1)=0
and
h^{\prime}(t)=\dfrac{(t-1)^{2}(3t-2)}{2t}>0
,
1<t\leq 2
,
which proves the second inequality.

We use the following standard inequality
[
5
, Theorem 137, Eq. (9.1.4)]
.

Lemma 19

Given
x,\delta>0
, there exists a constant
c>0
such
that

e^{-x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\left|k-\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor\right|>\delta x\end{subarray}}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}=O\left(e^{-cx}\right)\qquad\left(x\rightarrow\infty\right),

where
c=\delta^{2}/3
.

Lemma 20

Let
\left(b_{n}\right)
be a sequence of
positive numbers with
b_{n}\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Given
x,\beta>0
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\left|k/n-x\right|>\beta\end{subarray}}s_{n,k}\left(x\right)b_{n}^{k}=O\left(e^{-cn}\right)\qquad\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Proof.
Let
b>1
such that
b-1<\beta/\left(2x\right)
and
b<12/\left(12-\beta^{2}\right)
. Put
\gamma:=\beta/2
. Then
\left|k-nx\right|>\beta n
implies
\left|k-\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor\right|>\gamma n
, for sufficiently large
n
. This can be seen as follows.
Firstly, we have
n\left(x-\beta\right)<n\left(x-\beta\right)+\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor-nxb+1=\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor-n\gamma+nx-nxb+1-n\gamma<\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor-n\gamma
, for
n>1/\gamma
. Secondly, we have
n\left(x+\beta\right)>n\left(x+\beta\right)+\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor-nxb=\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor+n\gamma+n\gamma-nx\left(b-1\right)>\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor+n\gamma+n\gamma-nx\beta/\left(2x\right)=\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor+n\gamma
. For sufficiently large
n
, we have
b_{n}<b
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\left|k/n-x\right|>\beta\end{subarray}}s_{n,k}\left(x\right)b_{n}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-nx}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\left|k-\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor\right|>\gamma n\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(nxb_{n}\right)^{k}}{k!}\leq e^{-nx}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\left|k-\left\lfloor nxb\right\rfloor\right|>\gamma n\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(nxb\right)^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\left(b-1\right)nx}O\left(e^{-\left(\gamma^{2}/3\right)nxb}\right)\qquad\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right),

by Lemma
19
. Noting that
\left(b-1\right)-b\cdot\gamma^{2}/3=b\left(1-\gamma^{2}/3\right)-1<12/\left(12-\beta^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\beta^{2}/12\right)-1<0
completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
17
.

Let
f\in E_{A}
. First consider the special case
x=0
. Since
\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(0\right)=0
, for
j\geq 1
, we assume that
j\leq 0
. If
f\left(t\right)=0
, for
t\in\left[0,x+\delta\right)
, then

\displaystyle\left|\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(0\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(0)n\int_{\delta}^{\infty}s_{n,k-j}(t)\left|f(t)\right|dt\leq\frac{K}{\left(-j\right)!}n\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(nt\right)^{-j}e^{-\left(n-A\right)t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K}{\left(-j\right)!}\int_{n\delta}^{\infty}t^{-j}e^{-t}dt=O\left(e^{-cn}\right)\qquad\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right),

for each positive
c<\delta
.

Now let us turn to the case
x>0
. Let
f\left(t\right)=0
, if
x-\delta<t<x+\delta
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\delta<x/3
.
Furthermore, put
\beta=\delta/2
. We have

\displaystyle\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(0\right)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}s_{n,k}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\left|k/n-x\right|>\beta\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\left|k/n-x\right|\leq\beta\end{subarray}}\right)s_{n,k+j}\left(x\right)\cdot n\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)dt

\displaystyle=T_{0}+T_{1}+T_{2},

(8)

say. Obviously,

T_{0}=f\left(0\right)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}s_{n,k}\left(x\right)=O\left(n^{j-1}e^{-nx}\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Secondly, we estimate
\left|T_{1}\right|
. Since
\left|f\left(t\right)\right|\leq Ke^{At}
, for
t\geq 0
, we have, for

\displaystyle\left|n\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq Kn\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}\left(nt\right)^{k}e^{At}dt=K\left(1+\frac{A}{n-A}\right)^{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq K\left(1+\frac{A}{n-A}\right)e^{kA/\left(n-A\right)}.

Hence, for sufficiently large

, there is a constant

, such that

\displaystyle\left|T_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2K\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\

\displaystyle=2K\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\

\displaystyle\leq 2K\left(nx\right)^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\

by an application of Lemma
20
with
b_{n}=e^{2A/n}
. Now, we are going to estimate
\left|T_{2}\right|

in
\left(\ref{W_n,j-composition}\right)
. We consider values
k=n\left(x+\vartheta\right)
with

.
Then, for

,

U_{n,k}:=\left|n\int_{0}^{x-\delta}s_{n,k}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)dt\right|\leq K\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{n}{n-A}\right)^{k+1}\int_{0}^{\left(x-\delta\right)\left(n-A\right)}e^{-t}t^{k}dt

and

\displaystyle V_{n,k}

\displaystyle:=\left|n\int_{x+\delta}^{\infty}s_{n,k}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq K\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{n}{n-2A}\right)^{k+1}\int_{\left(x+\delta\right)\left(n-2A\right)}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{k}e^{-At/\left(n-2A\right)}dt.

Define

. Since

with a unique maximum at

and

since

\int_{\left(x+\delta\right)\left(n-2A\right)}^{\infty}e^{-At/\left(n-2A\right)}dt=\frac{n-2A}{A}e^{-A\left(x+\delta\right)}\leq\frac{n-2A}{A}.

Using Stirling’s formula

k!=\sqrt{2\pi}k^{k+1/2}\exp\left(-k+\frac{\sigma}{12k}\right),\text{
\qquad}\left(k>0,\text{ }0<\sigma<1\right)

for

\displaystyle U_{n,k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K\left(x-\delta\right)ne^{k}}{\sqrt{2\pi}k^{k+1/2}}\left(\frac{n}{n-A}\right)^{k}g_{k}\left(\left(x-\delta\right)\left(n-A\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K\left(x-\delta\right)ne^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\left(n\left(x+\vartheta\right)\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)+1/2}}\left(\frac{n}{n-A}\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\left(x-\delta\right)\left(n-A\right)}\left(\left(x-\delta\right)\left(n-A\right)\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K\left(x-\delta\right)e^{A\left(x-\delta\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi\left(x+\vartheta\right)}}\sqrt{n}e^{n\left(\delta+\vartheta\right)}\left(\frac{x-\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}

and

\displaystyle V_{n,k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K}{A}\frac{ne^{k}}{\sqrt{2\pi}k^{k+1/2}}\left(\frac{n}{n-2A}\right)^{k}g_{k}\left(\left(x+\delta\right)\left(n-2A\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K}{A}\frac{ne^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\left(n\left(x+\vartheta\right)\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)+1/2}}\left(\frac{n}{n-2A}\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\left(x+\delta\right)\left(n-2A\right)}\left(\left(x+\delta\right)\left(n-2A\right)\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K}{A}\frac{e^{2A\left(x+\delta\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi\left(x+\vartheta\right)}}\sqrt{n}e^{-n\left(\delta-\vartheta\right)}\left(\frac{x+\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)^{n\left(x+\vartheta\right)}.

Rewriting and applying Lemma
18
we obtain, with
t=\left(x-\delta\right)/\left(x+\vartheta\right)
,

\displaystyle e^{\delta+\vartheta}\left(\frac{x-\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)^{x+\vartheta}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\delta+\vartheta+\left(x+\vartheta\right)\log\frac{x-\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle<\exp\left(\delta+\vartheta+\left(x+\vartheta\right)\frac{-\delta-\vartheta}{x+\vartheta}-\frac{\left(x+\vartheta\right)}{2}\left(\frac{\delta+\vartheta}{x+\vartheta}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\delta+\vartheta\right)^{2}}{2\left(x+\vartheta\right)}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\delta-\beta\right)^{2}}{2\left(x+\beta\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4\left(2x+\delta\right)}\right)=:C_{1}\left(x,\delta\right)

and, with
t=\left(x+\delta\right)/\left(x+\vartheta\right)
,

\displaystyle e^{-\delta+\vartheta}\left(\frac{x+\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)^{x+\vartheta}=\exp\left(-\delta+\vartheta+\left(x+\vartheta\right)\log\frac{x+\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle<\exp\left(-\delta+\vartheta+\left(x+\vartheta\right)\frac{\delta-\vartheta}{x+\vartheta}-\frac{\left(x+\vartheta\right)}{2}\left(\frac{\delta-\vartheta}{x+\vartheta}\right)^{2}\left(2-\frac{x+\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\delta-\vartheta\right)^{2}}{2\left(x+\vartheta\right)}\frac{x+2\vartheta-\delta}{x+\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\delta-\beta\right)^{2}\left(x-2\beta-\delta\right)}{2\left(x+\beta\right)^{2}}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}\left(x-2\delta\right)}{2\left(2x+\delta\right)^{2}}\right)=:C_{2}\left(x,\delta\right).

Without loss of generality, one can choose
\delta>0
so small that both
constants are less than
1
. Now we can estimate
T_{2}
in
\left(\ref{W_n,j-composition}\right)
. With

\displaystyle\left|T_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\

Observing that

concludes the proof.

5
Asymptotic expansions

In this section we derive the complete asymptotic expansion for the sequence
of operators
S_{n,j}
in the form

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)\sim f(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k,j}\left(f,x\right)\;n^{-k}\qquad(n\rightarrow\infty),

(9)

provided that
f
admits derivatives of sufficiently high order at
x>0
.
Formula
\left(\ref{complete-asymptotic-expansion}\right)
means that, for
all
q=0,1,2,\ldots
, it holds

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{q}c_{k,j}\left(f,x\right)\text{ }n^{-k}+o(n^{-q})\qquad(n\rightarrow\infty)

where
c_{0,j}\left(f,x\right)=f\left(x\right)
. The coefficients
c_{k,j}\left(f,x\right)
, which are independent of
n
, will be given in a
concise form using the differential operator
\mathcal{D}_{j}
as defined in
Eq. (
1
). To this end, recall definition (
2
), i.e.,

\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2k}=e_{j}D^{k}(e_{k-j}D^{k}),\,\text{\qquad}k\in{\mathbb{N}},

and relation (
3
), i.e.,
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2k}=\left(\mathcal{D}_{j}^{2}\right)^{k}
.

Theorem 21

Let
q
be a positive integer. For each
function
f\in E
having the derivative
f^{\left(2q\right)}\left(x\right)
, the operators
S_{n,j}
possess the asymptotic expansion

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=f\left(x\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{q}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{2k}f\right)\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\text{ \hskip 20.44434pt}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Remark 22

For a function
f\in E
having all derivatives at
x>0
, we have the
complete asymptotic expansion

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)\sim f\left(x\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{2k}f\right)\left(x\right)\text{ \hskip 20.44434pt}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Note that
\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{2k}e_{j}\right)\left(x\right)=0
,
for all

. This reflects the fact that
S_{n,j}
preserves

.

, a general

[

will be

, let

Proof of Theorem

\left[0,+\infty\right)\setminus U_{2\delta}\left(x\right)

\left(S_{n,j}\psi_{x}^{2q}\right)\left(x\right)=O\left(n^{-q}\right)

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=\sum_{s=0}^{2q}\frac{f^{\left(s\right)}\left(x\right)}{s!}\left(S_{n,j}\psi_{x}^{s}\right)\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\text{ }\qquad\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

By Proposition
8

\displaystyle\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{2q}\frac{f^{\left(s\right)}\left(x\right)}{s!}s!\sum_{k=\left\lfloor\left(s+1\right)/2\right\rfloor}^{s}\frac{x^{s-k}}{\left(s-k\right)!n^{k}}\binom{k-j}{2k-s}+o\left(n^{-q}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=k}^{2k}\frac{f^{\left(s\right)}\left(x\right)}{\left(s-k\right)!}\binom{k-j}{2k-s}x^{s-k}+o\left(n^{-q}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=0}^{k}\frac{f^{\left(k+s\right)}\left(x\right)}{s!}\binom{k-j}{k-s}x^{s}+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\text{ }\qquad\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Interchanging the order of summation, we obtain

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=k}^{2k}\frac{f^{\left(s\right)}\left(x\right)}{\left(s-k\right)!}\binom{k-j}{2k-s}x^{s-k}+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\text{ }\qquad\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Observing that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=k}^{2k}\frac{f^{\left(s\right)}\left(x\right)}{\left(s-k\right)!}\binom{k-j}{2k-s}x^{s-k}=\sum_{s=0}^{k}\frac{f^{\left(k+s\right)}\left(x\right)}{s!}\binom{k-j}{k-s}x^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{j}}{k!}\sum_{s=0}^{k}\binom{k}{s}f^{\left(k+s\right)}\left(x\right)\left(x^{k-j}\right)^{\left(k-s\right)}=\frac{x^{j}}{k!}\left(x^{k-j}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k\right)},

where the last equation follows from Leibniz rule for differentiation, we
obtain

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=x^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}\left(x^{k-j}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k\right)}+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\text{ }\qquad\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Using the notation from Eq.
\left(\ref{eq.X3}\right)
we get the result
in the desired form, which completes the proof.

Now, we study simultaneous approximation by the operators
S_{n,j}
. It
turns out that
\left(S_{n,j}f\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)

possess as asymptotic expansion which arises from the asymptotic expansion
for
\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)
by differentiating its
coefficient term-by-term with respect to the variable
x
.

Theorem 26

Let
x>0
and
q,m
non-negative integers. If the function
f\in E
admits
the derivative
f^{\left(2q+2m\right)}\left(x\right)
, then the
derivatives of
S_{n,j}f
possess the asymptotic expansion

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{q}c_{k,j}^{\left(m\right)}\left(f,x\right)n^{-k}+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\text{ \hskip 20.44434pt}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right),

(10)

where

c_{k,j}\left(f,x\right)=\frac{1}{k!}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{2k}f\right)\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{k!}x^{j}\left(x^{k-j}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k\right)}.

In a more explicit form it holds

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{1}{i!}\binom{k+m-j}{k-i}x^{i}f^{\left(k+m+i\right)}\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q}\right)

(11)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

For the classical Szász–Mirakjan–Durrmeyer operators, i.e., for
j=0
,
this result can be found in
[
1
, Corollary 2.4]
.

Proof.
Let
f\in E
. Using

s_{n,k}^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)=\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{m}\left(e^{-nx}\frac{\left(nx\right)^{k}}{k!}\right)=n^{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{m-\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}s_{n,k-\ell}\left(x\right)

it follows that

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)=n^{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{m-\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}\left(S_{n,j-\ell}f\right)\left(x\right).

If
f^{\left(2q+2m\right)}\left(x\right)
exists, Theorem
21
yields the asymptotic expansion

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)\left(x\right)=f\left(x\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{q+m}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j}^{2k}f\right)\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q-m}\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(S_{n,j}f\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=n^{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{m-\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{q+m}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j-\ell}^{2k}f\right)\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q-m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{q+m}\frac{1}{k!n^{k-m}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{m-\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j-\ell}^{2k}f\right)\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\,\,\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{m-\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}\left({\mathcal{D}}_{j-\ell}^{2k}f\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{m-\ell}\binom{m}{\ell}x^{j-\ell}\left(x^{k-j+\ell}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j}\left(\left(\frac{x}{t}-1\right)^{m}x^{k-j}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k\right)}\mid_{t=x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{j}\binom{k}{m}m!x^{-m}\left(x^{k-j}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k-m\right)},

which vanishes, for
0\leq k<m
. Hence,

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}x^{j-m}\left(x^{k+m-j}f^{\left(k+m\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k\right)}+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\,\,\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right).

Application of the Leibniz rule yields

\left(S_{n,j}f\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{1}{k!n^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{k-i}}x^{i}f^{\left(k+m+i\right)}\left(x\right)+o\left(n^{-q}\right)\,\,\left(n\rightarrow\infty\right),

which proves
\left(\ref{Asymptotic-expansion-simultaneous-explicit}\right)
. Now we calculate

\displaystyle k!c_{k,j}^{\left(m\right)}\left(f,x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x^{j}\left(x^{k-j}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k\right)}\right)^{\left(m\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}j^{\underline{m-\ell}}x^{j-m+\ell}\left(x^{k-j}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\left(k+\ell\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}j^{\underline{m-\ell}}x^{j-m+\ell}\sum_{i=0}^{k+\ell}\binom{k+\ell}{i}\left(k-j\right)^{\underline{k+\ell-i}}x^{-\ell+i-j}f^{\left(k+i\right)}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k+m}x^{i-m}f^{\left(k+i\right)}\left(x\right)d\left(k,j,m,i\right),

where

d\left(k,j,m,i\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}\binom{k+\ell}{i}j^{\underline{m-\ell}}\left(k-j\right)^{\underline{k+\ell-i}}.

Application of Vandermonde convolution

\binom{k+\ell}{i}=\sum_{p=0}^{i}\binom{k}{i-p}\binom{\ell}{p}

and the binomial identity
\binom{m}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{p}=\binom{m}{p}\binom{m-p}{\ell-p}
leads to

\displaystyle d\left(k,j,m,i\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{i}\binom{k}{i-p}\binom{m}{p}\sum_{\ell=p}^{m}\binom{m-p}{\ell-p}j^{\underline{m-\ell}}\left(k-j\right)^{\underline{k+\ell-i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{i}\binom{k}{i-p}\binom{m}{p}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-p}\binom{m-p}{\ell}j^{\underline{m-p-\ell}}\left(k-j\right)^{\underline{k+\ell+p-i}}.

A second application of Vandermonde convolution shows that the inner sum is
equal to

\left(k-j\right)^{\underline{k+p-i}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-p}\binom{m-p}{\ell}j^{\underline{m-p-\ell}}\left(i-p-j\right)^{\underline{\ell}}=\left(k-j\right)^{\underline{k+p-i}}\left(i-p\right)^{\underline{m-p}}.

By a third application of Vandermonde convolution

\left(k-j\right)^{\underline{k+p-i}}=\sum_{\mu=0}^{k+p-i}\binom{k+p-i}{\mu}\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{q}}\left(-m\right)^{\underline{k+p-i-q}}

and reversing the summation order of the first sum we obtain

d\left(k,j,m,i\right)=\sum_{p=0}^{i}\binom{k}{p}\binom{m}{i-p}p^{\underline{m-i+p}}\sum_{\mu=0}^{k-p}\binom{k-p}{\mu}\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{q}}\left(-m\right)^{\underline{k-p-q}}.

Observing that
p^{\underline{m-i+p}}=0
if
i<m
, we infer that

k!c_{k,j}^{\left(m\right)}\left(f,x\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}x^{i}f^{\left(k+m+i\right)}\left(x\right)d\left(k,j,m,i+m\right),

(12)

with

d\left(k,j,m,i+m\right)=

\sum_{\mu=0}^{k}\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{\mu}}\sum_{p=0}^{i+m}\binom{k}{p}\binom{k-p}{\mu}\binom{m}{i+m-p}p^{\underline{p-i}}\left(-m\right)^{\underline{k-p-\mu}}.

Since
\binom{m}{i+m-p}=0
, if
i>p
, and
\binom{m}{i+m-p}=\binom{m}{p-i}
,
for
i\leq p
, we obtain

d\left(k,j,m,i+m\right)=

\sum_{\mu=0}^{k}\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{\mu}}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{k}{p+i}\binom{k-i-p}{\mu}\binom{m}{p}\left(p+i\right)^{\underline{p}}\left(-m\right)^{\underline{k-i-p-\mu}}.

Using
\binom{k}{p+i}\left(p+i\right)^{\underline{p}}i!=k^{\underline{p+i}}=k^{\underline{i}}\left(k-i\right)^{\underline{p}}
this equation can be
rewritten in the form

d\left(k,j,m,i+m\right)=

\sum_{\mu=0}^{k}\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{\mu}}\binom{k}{i}\sum_{p=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{p}\binom{k-i-p}{\mu}\binom{m}{p}\left(-m\right)^{\underline{k-i-p-\mu}}.

Because
\binom{k-i-p}{\mu}=0
, if
\mu>k-i-p
, the inner sum of
\left(\ref{formula-for-d(k,j,m,i+m)}\right)
vanishes if
k-i<\mu\leq k
. In the
case
0\leq\mu<k-i
, we use
p!\binom{m}{p}\left(-m\right)^{\underline{k-i-p-\mu}}=\left(-1\right)^{k-i-p-\mu}m\left(m+k-i-p-\mu-1\right)^{\underline{k-i-\mu-1}}
to see that the inner sum of
\left(\ref{formula-for-d(k,j,m,i+m)}\right)
is equal to

\sum_{p=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{p}\binom{k-i-p}{\mu}m\left(m+k-i-p-\mu-1\right)^{\underline{k-i-\mu-1}}.

Since
\binom{k-i-p}{\mu}m\left(m+k-i-p-\mu-1\right)^{\underline{k-i-\mu-1}}
is a polynomial of degree
k-i-1
in the variable
p
, this sum
vanishes. Finally, if
\mu=k-i
, the inner sum of
\left(\ref{formula-for-d(k,j,m,i+m)}\right)
has the value

\sum_{p=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{p}\binom{k-i-p}{k-i}\binom{m}{p}\left(-m\right)^{\underline{-p}}=1.

Summarizing, we obtain

d\left(k,j,m,i+m\right)=\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{k-i}}\binom{k}{i}.

By
\left(\ref{intermediate-formula-for-koeff-derivatives}\right)
, we get

c_{k,j}^{\left(m\right)}\left(f,x\right)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\left(k+m-j\right)^{\underline{k-i}}x^{i}f^{\left(k+m+i\right)}\left(x\right),

and comparison with
\left(\ref{Asymptotic-expansion-simultaneous-explicit}\right)
proves
\left(\ref{Asymptotic-expansion-simultaneous}\right)
.

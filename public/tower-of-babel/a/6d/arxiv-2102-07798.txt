A space-time isogeometric method for the partial differential-algebraic system of Biot’s poroelasticity model
Jeremias Arf, Bernd Simeon

Abstract
Biot’s equations of poroelasticity contain a parabolic system for the evolution of the pressure, which is coupled with a quasi-stationary equation for the stress tensor. Thus, it is natural to extend the existing work on isogeometric space-time methods to this more advanced framework of a partial differential-algebraic equation (PDAE). A space-time approach based on finite elements has already been introduced. But we present a new weak formulation in space and time that is appropriate for an isogeometric discretization and analyze the convergence properties. Our approach is based on a single variational problem and hence differs from the iterative space-time schemes considered so far. Further, it enables high-order convergence. Numerical experiments that have been carried out confirm the theoretical findings.



Keywords—
Biot’s poroelasticity model, isogeometric analysis, space-time discretization, high-order convergence

1
Introduction

Poroelasticity describes the coupling of mechanical deformation with the flow of a fluid in a porous medium and has numerous applications in engineering. In the quasi-static case, which is most widely adopted, the model takes the form of a partial differential-algebraic equation (PDAE) that calls for appropriate discretizations in space and time. The conventional approach relies so far on the method of lines, starting with a semi-discretization by finite elements in space followed by a time integration
of the resulting differential-algebraic system. We introduce here a novel scheme that treats space and time simultaneously and that applies isogeometric analysis (IGA) as discretization.
In this way, we extend the framework of space-time methods to PDAE problems and show how
the powerful algorithmic machinery of IGA with its spline-based function spaces can be applied to such
coupled models.

For more background on poroelasticity, we refer to the example of reservoir engineering where
oil and gas reservoirs, as well as more sustainable energy resources like geothermal reservoirs, are subject of research

On the other hand sometimes specific variables like the fluid flux are of exceptional interest and a formulation in which such variables are incorporated is preferred. But three- or four-field formulations suffer from the drawback of an increased number of degrees of freedom.

A different class of methods are the ones based on IGA. In IGA, geometric approximations are avoided, and at the same

In this paper we also follow the idea of space-time discretizations and use the approach of
[
26
]
for a parabolic
evolution equation as starting point. The extension to
the Biot two-field system is not straightforward since we are facing here several challenges. First, an appropriate weak formulation for both pressure and displacement variables has to be derived. Second, the treatment of the elastic momentum balance requires special care as it represents a constraint with respect to the time axis. And third, finally, the
pressure variable is known to be sensitive to oscillations that call for additional measures.
A space-time method leads directly to a linear system that includes all time steps and hence is much larger than
a spatial discretization alone. However, by closer inspection of the linear system one notices a staircase structure that reflects the propagation of the solution over time and that results in a very sparse matrix. With appropriate fast iterative
and parallel solvers, the linear system can be tackled as a whole, but in our work, the focus is on the
discretization itself. We also point out that in principal, the space-time scheme can be employed to introduce full adaptivity
in space and time, which comprises in particular local time step changes in combination with local spatial refinement.

The Biot system

In this section we outline the coupled model of Biot, following the references

The porous medium consists of a solid skeleton and permeable voids (pores) that are filled by some fluid. It is identified with a connected and bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\ d\in\{1,2,3\}

Model variables are the time-dependent mechanical displacement field
\boldsymbol{u}(t)\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

\ \boldsymbol{q}(t)\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{q}=-\frac{\boldsymbol{K}}{\eta_{f}}\big(\nabla_{x}p-\rho_{f}\boldsymbol{g}\big),\,

which gives a linear relation between the pressure gradient and the flux. In the last equation

the fluid viscosity,

the fluid density and

The first governing equation of the Biot system can be derived by means of the balance of momentum and linear elasticity, namely

\displaystyle-\nabla_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tilde{\sigma}}(\boldsymbol{u},p)=\boldsymbol{f},\,

(1)

where

The second governing equation is obtained from a conservation law
for the fluid phase, which reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\big(c_{0}p+b\nabla_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}\big)+\nabla_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}=g.

(2)

Here, we can interpret

as the fluid content and

is the

\displaystyle-\nabla_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tilde{\sigma}}(\boldsymbol{u},p)=\boldsymbol{f},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\big(c_{0}p+b\nabla_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}\big)-\nabla_{x}\cdot\frac{\boldsymbol{K}}{\eta_{f}}\nabla_{x}p=g.

(4)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{t}_{n}\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\textup{on}\hskip 14.22636pt\Sigma_{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Gamma_{t}\times(0,T),\ \ \ \ \ (\textup{tension BC.})

(5)

\displaystyle\vskip 1.70709pt\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\nabla_{x}p\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{x}

\displaystyle=v_{f}\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\textup{on}\hskip 14.22636pt\Sigma_{f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Gamma_{f}\times(0,T),\ \ \ \ \ (\textup{flux BC.})

\displaystyle\vskip 1.70709pt\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\textup{on}\hskip 14.22636pt\Sigma_{u}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Gamma_{u}\times(0,T),

(7)

\displaystyle\vskip 1.70709ptp

\displaystyle=0\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\textup{on}\hskip 14.22636pt\Sigma_{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Gamma_{p}\times(0,T).

(8)

Here

is the time interval of interest and

Moreover,

Space-time discretization and discrete variational formulation

Regarding the discretization of the Biot system, we look first at the basics of isogeometric analysis and proceed with the
derivation of a discrete space-time variational method for it.

3.1
Isogeometric analysis

Introduced by Hughes et al.
[
23
]
, the concept of IGA developed in the last 15 years to a powerful tool in numerical analysis. The basic idea is the simultaneous use of spline functions for the geometric modelling and the definition of discrete spaces. IGA is able to represent various complex and curved-boundary domains exactly and furthermore one has the possibility to easily increase or lower the smoothness of functions in the discrete spaces. Following
[
14
,
5
,
6
]
for a brief
exposition, we call an increasing sequence of real numbers
\Xi\coloneqq\{\xi_{1}\leq\xi_{2}\leq\dots\leq\xi_{n+r+1}\}
for some
r\in\mathbb{N}

knot vector
, where we assume
0=\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=\dots=\xi_{r+1},\ \xi_{n+1}=\xi_{n+2}=\dots=\xi_{n+r+1}=1
, and call such knot vectors
r
-open.
Further, the multiplicity of the
j
-th knot is denoted by
m(\xi_{j})
.
Then the univariate B-spline functions
\hat{B}_{j,r}(\cdot)
of degree
r
corresponding to a given knot vector
\Xi
is defined recursively by the
Cox-DeBoor formula
:

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{j,0}(\zeta)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\ \ \textup{if}\ \zeta\in[\xi_{j},\xi_{j+1})\\
0,\ \ \textup{else},\end{cases}

and if
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\ \textup{we set}

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{j,r}(\zeta)\coloneqq\frac{\zeta-\xi_{j}}{\xi_{j+r}-\xi_{j}}\hat{B}_{j,r-1}(\zeta)+\frac{\xi_{j+r+1}-\zeta}{\xi_{j+r+1}-\xi_{j+1}}\hat{B}_{j+1,r-1}(\zeta),

where one puts
0/0=0
to obtain well-definedness. The multivariate extension of the last spline definition is achieved by a tensor product construction. In other words, we set for a given tensor knot vector
\boldsymbol{\Xi}\coloneqq\Xi_{1}\times\dots\times\Xi_{d}
, where the
\Xi_{l}=\{\xi_{1}^{l},\dots,\xi_{n_{l}+r_{l}+1}^{l}\},\ l=1,\dots,d
are
r_{l}
-open, and a given
degree vector

\boldsymbol{r}\coloneqq(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
for the multivariate case

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{r}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\coloneqq\prod_{l=1}^{d}\hat{B}_{i_{l},r_{l}}(\zeta_{l}),\ \ \ \ \boldsymbol{\zeta}\coloneqq(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{d}),\ \ \forall\,\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathit{\mathbf{I}},

with
d
as the underlying dimension of the parametric domain
\hat{\Omega}=(0,1)^{d}
and
\mathit{\mathbf{I}}
the multi-index set
\mathit{\mathbf{I}}\coloneqq\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})\ |\ 1\leq i_{l}\leq n_{l},\ l=1,\dots,d\}
.
To enlarge the possibilities of the representation of geometric objects, one can generalize the definition of B-splines
to rational B-splines. Namely, choosing strictly positive weights
0<w_{\boldsymbol{i}},\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathit{\mathbf{I}}
and exploiting the notation from above we introduce the weight function

W(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\coloneqq\sum_{\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathit{\mathbf{I}}}w_{\boldsymbol{i}}\,\hat{B}_{\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{r}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}).

We define the non-uniform rational B-spline (NURBS) basis functions
\hat{N}_{\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{r}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})
w.r.t. to the weight function
W
as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{r}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\coloneqq w_{\boldsymbol{i}}\,\hat{B}_{\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{r}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\big(W(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\big)^{-1},\ \ \ \ \forall\,\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathit{\mathbf{I}}.

B-splines (and the same for NURBS) fulfil several properties and for our purposes the most important ones are:

•

If for all internal knots the multiplicity satisfies
1\leq m(\xi_{j}^{l})\leq m\leq r\leq r_{l},\ \forall l
, then the B-spline basis functions
\hat{B}_{\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{r}}
are globally
C^{r-m}
-continuous.

•

The B-splines
\{\hat{B}_{\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{r}}\ |\ \ \boldsymbol{i}\in\mathit{\mathbf{I}}\}
are linearly independent.

the space-time cylinder

This so-called physical domain is assumed to be parametrized by means of NURBS or B-splines, respectively.

\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\colon(0,1)^{d+1}\eqqcolon\hat{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}\ ,\ \tilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\mapsto\sum_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{i}}\in{\tilde{\mathit{\mathbf{I}}}}}\tilde{C}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{i}}}\,\hat{N}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{i}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\zeta}})\ ,

and

3.2
Discrete space-time variational formulation

Starting point for the discretized variational formulation is the classical Biot two-field model.
For the derivation we consider the solution to satisfy
p\in H^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
and
\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{3}(\mathcal{Q})

while the right-hand sides fulfil
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\mathcal{Q}),\ g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})

\displaystyle-h_{T}\,\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tilde{\sigma}}(\boldsymbol{u},p)=h_{T}\,\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{f}.

(11)

This last differentiation step is inspired by the differentiation procedure used in DAE (differential algebraic equation) theory in order to obtain underlying ODEs (ordinary differential equations); see e.g.
[
35
]
.
To be more precise, we choose a differentiated test function
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\ \boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}
and multiply the sum (
3
)
+
(
11
) of the two equations by this test function. Integration over the whole space-time cylinder yields

\displaystyle\langle-\nabla_{x}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-b\,p\boldsymbol{I})-h_{T}\,\partial_{t}[\nabla_{x}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-b\,p\boldsymbol{I})]\ ,\ \partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}=\langle\boldsymbol{f}+h_{T}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{f},\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})},

and integration by parts along with the Einstein summation convention leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\ C_{ijkl}\,{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u}+h_{T}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u})\,{\varepsilon}_{ij}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\ d\boldsymbol{x}dt-b\,\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\,(p+h_{T}\partial_{t}p)\,\nabla_{x}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\,d\boldsymbol{x}dt

(12)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int_{\partial\mathcal{Q}}\,\big(\underbrace{(-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+b\,p\boldsymbol{I})}_{=-\boldsymbol{\tilde{\sigma}}}+h_{T}\partial_{t}(-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+b\,p\boldsymbol{I})\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{x}\ \partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\,ds=\langle\boldsymbol{f}+h_{T}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{f},\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}.

On the other hand, the multiplication of the evolution equation (
4
) for the pressure by a time-upwind test function
q_{h}+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}q_{h}
gives, again using integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\,c_{0}\,\partial_{t}p\ (q_{h}+h_{T}\partial_{t}q_{h})\,d\boldsymbol{x}dt+b\,\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\nabla_{x}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}\ (q_{h}+h_{T}\partial_{t}q_{h})\,d\boldsymbol{x}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt-\int_{\partial\mathcal{Q}}\big(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\nabla_{x}p\,(q_{h}+\partial_{t}q_{h})\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{x}\,ds+\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\nabla_{x}p\ \nabla_{x}(q_{h}+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}q_{h})\,d\boldsymbol{x}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt=\langle g,q_{h}+h_{T}\partial_{t}q_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}.

Remark 1
.

For the derivation above, the product structure of the parametrization
and the smoothness of the isogeometric basis functions in each mesh element
\mathcal{K}
lead to the well-definedness of the mixed derivatives
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\ \partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}q_{h}
. The
\partial_{x_{i}},\ i=1,\dots,d
, denote the derivatives w.r.t. spatial coordinates.

Both equations (

3.2

Definition 1
.

(Discrete variational formulation)

Find
{u}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}
and
p_{h}\in\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}
s.t.

\displaystyle\tilde{e}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+h_{T}\,{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\,,\,{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-b\langle p_{h}+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{p}_{h}\,,\,\nabla_{x}\cdot{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}=l_{1}({\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}),

(14)

\displaystyle c_{0}\langle\partial_{t}{p}_{h}\,,\,q_{h}+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}+b\langle\nabla_{x}\cdot{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\,,\,q_{h}+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}+\tilde{a}(p_{h}\,,\,q_{h}+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}_{h})

\displaystyle=l_{2}(q_{h}+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}_{h}),

(15)

for all
{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{V}}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}
and
q_{h}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}
,

with linear forms

\begin{cases}l_{1}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq\langle\boldsymbol{f}+h_{T}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{f}\,,\,\boldsymbol{v}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}+\langle\boldsymbol{t}_{n}+h_{T}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{t}_{n}\,,\,\boldsymbol{v}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Sigma_{t})},\vskip 2.84544pt\\
l_{2}(q)\coloneqq\langle g\,,\,q\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}+\langle v_{f}\,,\,q\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{f})},\end{cases}

and bilinear forms

\begin{cases}\tilde{e}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{Q}}C_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u}){\varepsilon}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{v})\ d\boldsymbol{x}dt,\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\tilde{a}(p,q)\coloneqq\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\nabla_{x}p\,,\,\nabla_{x}q\rangle_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}.\end{cases}

For later considerations, we use instead of (
14
)-(
1
) the equivalent formulation

\displaystyle\textup{Find}\ \boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\ \ \textup{s.t.}\ \ b_{ST}([\boldsymbol{u}_{h},

\displaystyle p_{h}],[\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}])=l([\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}]),\ \ \forall\ \boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}},\ q_{h}\in\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}},

(16)

\displaystyle\textup{with}\hskip 8.5359ptb_{ST}([\boldsymbol{u},p],[\boldsymbol{v},q])

\displaystyle\coloneqq\tilde{e}(\boldsymbol{u}+h_{T}\,{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}\,,\,{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}})-b\langle p+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{p}\,,\,\nabla_{x}\cdot{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

(17)

\displaystyle\hskip 4.26773pt+c_{0}\langle\partial_{t}{p}\,,\,q+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}+b\langle\nabla_{x}\cdot{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}\,,\,q+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+\tilde{a}(p\,,\,q+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}),

\displaystyle l([\boldsymbol{v},q])

\displaystyle\coloneqq l_{1}({\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}})+l_{2}(q+h_{T}\,\partial_{t}{q}).

The discrete formulation is consistent in the following way.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that there exists a solution to (

)-(
4
) satisfying
\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{3}(\mathcal{Q})
and
p\in H^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
and let
v_{f}\in L^{2}(\Sigma_{f})
and
\boldsymbol{t}_{n}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Sigma_{t})
the restriction of a function in
\boldsymbol{t}_{e}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\partial\mathcal{Q})

\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\mathcal{Q}),\ g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})

Then
b_{ST}([\boldsymbol{u},p],[\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}])=l([\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}])\ \ \textup{for all}\ \boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}},\,q_{h}\in\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}
.

Proof.

This is clear due to the equations (

), the assumed boundary conditions and the fact that for the Lipschitz domain
\mathcal{Q}
the trace operator restricted to

defines a linear and continuous operator

; see, e.g.,
[

.
∎

Note the well-definedness of the terms on the right-hand side of (

) if
\boldsymbol{u},\,\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{q\in H^{1}(\mathcal{Q})\ |\ \nabla_{x}\partial_{t}q\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q}),\ q=0\ \textup{on}\ \Sigma_{p}\cup\Sigma_{0}\}.

3.3
Existence of solutions

Next we check if there exists a solution to the discrete problem.
For this purpose we prove the coercivity of the bilinear form
b_{ST}(\cdot,\cdot)
w.r.t. to the space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}\times\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}
endowed with the auxiliary norm

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\boldsymbol{v},q]\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\coloneqq h_{T}\left\lVert\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Sigma_{T})}^{2}+h_{T}c_{0}\left\lVert\partial_{t}q\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}

(18)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+c_{0}\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{T})}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{x}q\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2},\hskip 14.22636pt\textup{where}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i,j}\big(\partial_{x_{i}}v_{j}\big)^{2}\,d\boldsymbol{x}dt+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2},\ \ \boldsymbol{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\ \ \textup{and}\ \ \Sigma_{T}=\Omega\times\{T\}.

Exploiting the continuity, the piecewise smoothness and the boundary conditions of the test functions one can check easily that
\left\lVert[\cdot,\cdot]\right\rVert_{h}
is indeed a norm in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}\times\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}
. Before we prove the coercivity we insert here two auxiliary results.

Lemma 2
.

Let the elasticity tensor
\boldsymbol{C}
satisfy

\mu(\boldsymbol{C})\ \sum_{i,j}\,x_{ij}^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j,k,l}C_{ijkl}\,x_{ij}{x}_{kl}

for all
x_{ij},\ x_{kl}\in\mathbb{R},\ i,j,k,l\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and some constant
0<\mu(\boldsymbol{C})
.
Then the bilinear form

\displaystyle e(\cdot,\cdot)\colon\boldsymbol{V}\times\boldsymbol{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ ,\ (\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})\mapsto\,\int_{\Omega}C_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u}){\varepsilon}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{v})\,d\boldsymbol{x}

is symmetric and coercive w.r.t. the
\boldsymbol{H}^{1}
-norm in
\boldsymbol{V}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega)\ |\ \boldsymbol{v}=0\ \textup{on}\ \Gamma_{u}\}
.
Thus there exists a constant
0<c_{e}
such that

\displaystyle e(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})\geq c_{e}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2},\ \ \ \ \forall\ \boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V}.

(19)

The constant depends only on
\Omega,\ \Gamma_{u}
and
\boldsymbol{C}
.

Proof.

The inequality (
19
) follows by
[
1
]
, Corollary 5.9, for both cases
d=2,3
. The one-dimensional case is a consequence of the Poincaré inequality; see Example 3 in
[
20
]
. And the symmetry of
e(\cdot,\cdot)
is clear due to the symmetry properties of the elasticity tensor.
∎

Lemma 3
.

There exist constants
0<c_{a},C_{a}<\infty
which only depend on
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}},\ \Gamma_{p}
and
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle a(q,q)\geq c_{a}\,\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\ \ \ \textup{and}\ \ \ |a(p,q)|\leq C_{a}\,\left\lVert p\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\ \textup{for all}\ \ \ p,\,q\in W\coloneqq\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega)\ |\ w=0\ \textup{on}\ \Gamma_{p}\}\ \ \textup{and with}

\displaystyle\ \ \ a\colon W\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ ,\ (p,q)\mapsto\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\nabla_{x}p\,,\,\nabla_{x}q\rangle_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \

Proof.

An application of the Poincaré inequality and the assumption that the
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})
are uniformly elliptic and bounded symmetric positive definite matrices give the assertion.
∎

Remark 2
.

In the context of the space-time discretization we interpret the derivatives
\partial_{x_{i}},\ \partial_{t}
etc., as weak derivatives w.r.t. to the domain
\mathcal{Q}
. Nevertheless due to the piecewise smoothness of the test functions and their continuity, we obtain their weak derivatives as piecewise defined classical derivatives and in particular one can assume for
s\in[0,T]
that
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(\cdot,s)\in\boldsymbol{V}
and
q_{h}(\cdot,s)\in W
. The product structure of the parametrization and hence of the test spaces further gives us
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(\cdot,s)\in\boldsymbol{V}
and
\partial_{t}q_{h}(\cdot,s)\in W
; see (
10
).

Now we arrive at the mentioned coercivity result.

Lemma 4
.

The bilinear form
b_{ST}
, defined by (
17
), is coercive in the sense that there exists a constant
0<\mu_{c}
independent from the mesh sizes such that

\displaystyle\mu_{c}\,\left\lVert[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}]\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\leq b_{ST}([\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}],[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}]),\ \ \forall\ [\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}]\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}\times\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}.

The constant
\mu_{c}
can be chosen independently from
c_{0}
.

Proof.

For reasons of clarity we estimate different terms in the definition of
b_{ST}
, i.e., (
17
), separately, starting with the non-mixed terms in which either only
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{v}
or only
p,\ q
occur.

•

Consider the
\tilde{e}(\cdot,\cdot)
term and note that
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,t=0)=\boldsymbol{0}
. Observe the symmetry of the bilinear form
\tilde{e}(\cdot,\cdot),
that is obvious from the symmetry of
e(\cdot,\cdot)
; see Lemma
2
. By means of these properties and Green’s formula we can write

\displaystyle\tilde{e}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}C_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}){\varepsilon}_{ij}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})d\boldsymbol{x}dt=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\partial_{t}\big(C_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}){\varepsilon}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\big)\,dtd\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{Q}}C_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}){\varepsilon}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\cdot n_{t}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{T})}C_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}){\varepsilon}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\,d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,e(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,T),\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,T))\geq\frac{c_{e}}{2}\,\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,T)\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We used here the piecewise smoothness of the test functions, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,t)\in\boldsymbol{V}
, and the coercivity of the elasticity form
e(\cdot,\cdot)
(Lemma
2
) with
(\boldsymbol{n}_{x},n_{t})
denoting the outer unit normal vector of the space-time domain.

•

In view of Remark
2
and the coercivity of
e(\cdot,\cdot)
it is

\displaystyle h_{T}\,\tilde{e}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle=h_{T}\,\int_{\mathcal{Q}}C_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}){\varepsilon}_{ij}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})d\boldsymbol{x}dt

\displaystyle=h_{T}\,\int_{I}{e}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,t),\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,t))\,dt

\displaystyle\geq h_{T}\,\int_{I}\,c_{e}\left\lVert\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=h_{T}\,c_{e}\,\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j}(\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}u_{h,j})^{2}+\sum_{j}(\partial_{t}u_{h,j})^{2}\,d\boldsymbol{x}dt

\displaystyle=h_{T}\,c_{e}\,\left\lVert\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}.

One notices Fubini’s theorem and the notation
u_{h,i}
for the i-th component of
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
.

•

By the chain rule and the zero initial conditions we get

\displaystyle\langle c_{0}\,\partial_{t}p_{h},p_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\frac{c_{0}}{2}\partial_{t}\big(p_{h}^{2}\big)dtd\boldsymbol{x}=\frac{c_{0}}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{Q}}\,p_{h}^{2}\cdot n_{t}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{0}}{2}\,\left\lVert p_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{T})}^{2}.

•

Obviously,

\displaystyle h_{T}\,\langle c_{0}\,\partial_{t}p_{h},\partial_{t}p_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}=h_{T}\,c_{0}\,\left\lVert\partial_{t}p_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}.

•

Moreover, with Remark
2
as well as with Lemma
3
and Fubini’s theorem one can estimate

\displaystyle\tilde{a}(p_{h},p_{h})=\int_{\Omega}\int_{I}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\nabla_{x}p_{h}\ \nabla_{x}p_{h}\,dtd\boldsymbol{x}=\int_{I}\,a(p_{h}(\cdot,t),p_{h}(\cdot,t))\,dt

\displaystyle\geq c_{a}\left\lVert\nabla_{x}p_{h}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}.

•

Finally the last non-mixed term yields by the symmetry of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
:

\displaystyle h_{T}\,\tilde{a}(p_{h},\partial_{t}p_{h})

\displaystyle=h_{T}\,\int_{I}a(p_{h}(\cdot,t),\partial_{t}p_{h}(\cdot,t))\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{T}}{2}\int_{I}\partial_{t}a(p_{h}(\cdot,t),p_{h}(\cdot,t))\,dt=\frac{h_{T}}{2}\,a(p_{h}(\cdot,T),p_{h}(\cdot,T))\geq 0.

For the first, third and last point above, we used the assumption that
p_{h}=0,\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=0
on
\Sigma_{0}
.
Next we sum up all the remaining terms in the definition of
b_{ST}([\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}],[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}])
, i.e. in the sum of the right-hand side of (
17
). We get

\displaystyle b\ \big[-\langle p_{h},\nabla_{x}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}-h_{T}\,\langle\partial_{t}p_{h},\nabla_{x}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\langle\nabla_{x}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}+h_{T}\,\langle\nabla_{x}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\partial_{t}p_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}\big]=0.

Thus the mixed terms vanish. So it is obvious by the above estimates that

\displaystyle b_{ST}([\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}],[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}])

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{c}\,\Big(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Sigma_{T})}^{2}+h_{T}\,\left\lVert\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}+c_{0}\,\left\lVert p_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt+h_{T}\,c_{0}\,\left\lVert\partial_{t}p_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{x}p_{h}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mu_{c}\,\left\lVert[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}]\right\rVert_{h}^{2},

for
\mu_{c}\coloneqq\min\{\frac{c_{e}}{2},c_{a},\frac{1}{2}\}.

∎

If we now choose bases
\{\psi_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,N^{p}_{h}\}
and
\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}\ |\ j=1,\dots,N^{u}_{h}\}
of the test spaces
\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}
respectively. Then the coefficient vectors
P^{h}\coloneqq(\hat{p}_{1},\dots,\hat{p}_{N^{p}_{h}})
,
U^{h}\coloneqq(\hat{u}_{1},\dots,\hat{u}_{N^{u}_{h}})
, with

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}\approx\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N^{u}_{h}}\hat{u}_{j}\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j},

\displaystyle p\approx p_{h}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N^{p}_{h}}\hat{p}_{i}\,\psi_{i},

which define the discretized solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}
are obtained by solving one linear system of the type

\displaystyle S^{h}\begin{bmatrix}U^{h}\\
P^{h}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}R^{1}\\
R^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

The shown coercivity of the bilinear form
b_{ST}
implies the positive definiteness of the system matrix
S^{h}
.
Thus the existence of a unique solution is clear.

Theorem 1
.

Existence of a discretized solution

There exists a unique solution to the variational problem (
16
).

Latter theorem guarantees the well-definedness of our numerical scheme. But for a useful method a convergence statement is an important aspect, too. Consequently we face this issue in the next part.

4
Error analysis

The main objective of this section is the derivation of an error estimate for the numerical approximation of
the displacement
\boldsymbol{u}
and the pressure
p
in the setting of Lemma
1
. For reasons of simplification we set in the whole chapter w.l.o.g.
b=1
and remark that
0.5\leq b\leq 1
in most applications.

We start with a result from the IGA theory that will be used below.

Lemma 5
.

(Inverse inequality)

Let the space-time mesh be regular with polynomial degrees
r_{T},\,r_{S}
greater than zero. Then for
i=1,\dots,d

it holds

\displaystyle h_{T}\,\left\lVert\partial_{t}\partial_{x_{i}}v_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{inv,1}\,\left\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}v_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})},

(20)

\displaystyle h_{T}\,\left\lVert\partial_{t}v_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{inv,2}\,\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})},\ \ v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h,r_{S},r_{T}}\cap C^{0}(\mathcal{Q}),

(21)

where
C_{inv,j}
are constants independent of the mesh sizes and
v_{h}
.

Proof.

We remark that
\partial_{x_{i}}v_{h}
is piecewise smooth and for
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{Q}
the function
\partial_{x_{i}}v_{h}(\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)
is continuous in time. By the product structure of the space
\mathcal{V}_{h,\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}
and
\mathcal{Q}
one sees that
\partial_{x_{i}}v_{h}(\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)
is an element of a univariate spline space
V_{h_{T},r_{T}}
with mesh size
h_{T}
. Due to the regularity of the mesh and as a consequence of Theorem 4.2 in
[
5
]
we find a constant
C
independent of
\boldsymbol{x}
s.t. the estimate

h_{T}\left\lVert\partial_{t}\partial_{x_{i}}v_{h}(\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\leq C\left\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}v_{h}(\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}.

is fulfilled. Integration over
\Omega
yields the assertion for inequality (
20
). The second estimate (
21
) can be proven analogously.
∎

Next we define for the space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{0}\times\mathcal{W}_{0}
another auxiliary norm

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\boldsymbol{u},p]\right\rVert_{h,\star}^{2}\coloneqq\left\lVert\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}+\frac{1}{h_{T}}\big(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}+\left\lVert p\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}\big)+\left\lVert\partial_{t}p\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{x}p\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}^{2}

(22)

and state a boundedness result for
b_{ST}
.

Lemma 6
.

The bilinear form
b_{ST}
is continuous w.r.t. the norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{h}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{h,\star}
in the sense

b_{ST}([\boldsymbol{u},p],[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}])\leq\mu_{b}\,\left\lVert[\boldsymbol{u},p]\right\rVert_{h,\star}\left\lVert[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}]\right\rVert_{h}

for all
[\boldsymbol{u},p]\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{0}\times\mathcal{W}_{0}
and
[\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}]\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h,r_{u},r_{T}}\times\mathcal{W}_{h,r_{p},r_{T}}
and some constant
\mu_{b}
independent of
c_{0}
and the mesh size.

Proof.

We first look at the different terms appearing in the definition of
b_{ST}
and estimate them separately. Doing so, we also introduce some auxiliary constants
C_{1},\dots,C_{8}
.

•

By the definition of the elasticity bilinear form
\tilde{e}(\cdot,\cdot)
(see Definition
1
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have:

\displaystyle S_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\tilde{e}(\boldsymbol{u},\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}C_{ijkl}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{kl}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{ij}(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\,d\boldsymbol{x}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{4}\,C_{ijkl}\big(\partial_{x_{k}}u_{l}\,\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}u_{h,j}+\partial_{x_{l}}u_{k}\,\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}u_{h,j}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\partial_{x_{k}}u_{l}\,\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{t}u_{h,i}+\partial_{x_{l}}u_{k}\,\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{t}u_{h,i}\ \big)d\boldsymbol{x}dt

In an analogous manner to the first point it holds

\displaystyle\leq C_{\tilde{e}}\,h_{T}\,\left\lVert\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}\left\lVert\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathcal{Q})}

A further term can be bounded similarly as in point 2:

Next we introduce NURBS projections, i.e. projections onto NURBS spaces, which can be used to measure the approximation properties of the test function spaces.

Lemma 7
.

Let
v\in H^{s}(\mathcal{Q}),\ s\geq\max\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
with
1\leq s_{1}\leq r_{T}+1,\ 1\leq s_{2}\leq r_{S}+1
and
\mathcal{V}_{h,r_{S},r_{T}}
the space-time NURBS space with an underlying regular mesh.

Then there exists a projection
\Pi_{h}\colon H^{1}(\mathcal{Q})\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{h,r_{S},r_{T}}
and constants
C_{\Pi,j}
not depending on
h_{S},\,h_{T}
and
v
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Pi_{h}v-v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Pi,1}(h_{T}^{s_{1}}+h_{S}^{s_{2}})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{Q})},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}\big(\Pi_{h}v-v\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Pi,2}(h_{T}^{s_{1}-1}+h_{S}^{s_{2}-1})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{Q})},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{t}\big(\Pi_{h}v-v\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Pi,3}(h_{T}^{s_{1}-1}+h_{S}^{s_{2}-1})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{Q})}.

If additionally
s\geq\max\{s_{1}+1,s_{2}+1\}
, it holds moreover

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}\big(\Pi_{h}v-v\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Pi,4}(h_{T}^{s_{1}-1}+h_{S}^{s_{2}-1})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{Q})},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}\big(\Pi_{h}v-v\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Pi,5}(h_{T}^{s_{1}}+h_{S}^{s_{2}-1})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{Q})}.

The approximation results are also valid in case of homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on the whole or on a part of the boundary.

Proof.

In the following,
C
denotes a constant that depends only on the parametrization but may change at different occurrences.
The main idea of the proof is the application of IGA approximation results presented in
[
14
]
(Part 3). For a better understanding we define the auxiliary derivatives

\displaystyle D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{\boldsymbol{s}}u\coloneqq\big(\partial_{\zeta_{1}}^{s_{1}}\dots\partial_{\zeta_{d}}^{s_{d}}\partial_{\zeta_{d+1}}^{s_{d+1}}(u\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}})\big)\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1},\ \ \ \boldsymbol{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d+1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1},

(23)

for sufficiently regular mappings
u
. Here
\partial_{\zeta_{i}}
stands for the derivative w.r.t. the
i
-th coordinate in the parametric domain
\hat{\mathcal{Q}}
. Latter definition is analogous to (56) in Part 3 of the mentioned reference.
Let
v\in H^{2}(\mathcal{K})
and
\mathcal{K}
an element of the physical mesh
\mathcal{M}
. The next step relates the weak derivatives to the definition (
23
).
By the chain rule and the regularity of
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
we have

\partial_{t}v=\partial_{t}\big[\big(v\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\big)\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}\big]=\hat{\nabla}\big(v\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\big)\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}\cdot\partial_{t}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}.

We denote with
\hat{\nabla}
the gradient w.r.t. to the coordinates
\ \zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{d+1}
of the parametric domain
\hat{\mathcal{Q}}
.
Further rearrangements yield with the structure of
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
(see (
9
)),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=\frac{1}{T}\,\partial_{\zeta_{d+1}}\big(v\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\big)\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}=\frac{1}{T}D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{(0,\dots,0,1)}v.

(24)

Moreover, using again the chain rule it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\partial_{x_{i}}\big[\partial_{\zeta_{d+1}}\big(v\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\big)\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}\big]=\frac{1}{T}\hat{\nabla}\big[\partial_{\zeta_{d+1}}\big(v\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\big)\big]\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}\cdot\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big[\partial_{\zeta_{j}}\partial_{\zeta_{d+1}}\big(v\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\big)\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}\big]\,\big(\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}\big)_{j}\ .

Let
\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
be the

Then we can choose a constant

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}v|\leq C\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big|\partial_{\zeta_{j}}\partial_{\zeta_{d+1}}\big(v\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\big)\circ\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-1}\big|=C\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big|D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{\boldsymbol{e}_{j}+(0,\dots,0,1)}v\big|.

(25)

Consequently, (
24
) and
(
25
)
imply for some (new) constant

, only depending on the

parametrization, that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{t}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\left\lVert D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{(0,\dots,0,1)}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})},

(26)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\lVert D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{\boldsymbol{e}_{j}+(0,\dots,0,1)}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}.

(27)

Similarly one gets

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\lVert D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{\boldsymbol{e}_{j}}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})},

(28)

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{(0,\dots,0)}v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}.

(29)

Again by the chain rule and an induction argument one gets a reverse estimate, namely

\displaystyle\left\lVert D_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}^{\boldsymbol{s}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K})}\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{K})},\ \ \textup{for}\ \ v\in H^{s}(\mathcal{K})\ \ \textup{with}\ \ s=s_{1}+\dots+s_{d+1}.

denote the mesh sizes in the spatial domain and in the time interval, the assertion follows from the inequalities (

How does this convergence result compare with the vertical method of lines? In the latter case, the
spatial discretization leads to an ODE or DAE in time which is then solved by specific integration schemes.
In Theorem
2
, the spatial and temporal influences show up in terms of the type
h_{S}^{a}
,
h_{T}^{a}
respectively. If we assume
h_{S}
of order
\mathcal{O}(h_{T})
and require a regular exact solution then the error w.r.t. the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{h}
is of order
\mathcal{O}(h_{T}^{r_{T}}+h_{S}^{r_{p}}+h_{T}^{-0.5}(h_{S}^{r_{p}+1}+h_{S}^{r_{u}}))=\mathcal{O}(h_{T}^{r_{T}}+h_{S}^{r_{p}}+h_{S}^{r_{u}-0.5})
.
Error estimates for the method of lines typically have the form
\textup{error}\leq\textup{const.}\cdot\big(\Delta t^{m}+h_{S}^{k}\big)
, where
m
denotes the convergence order of the time integrator,
\Delta t
the step size and
k
the approximation order of the spatial discretization.

From a theoretical point of view it is possible to obtain high convergence orders in both ways. But for the space-time method increasing the convergence order can be achieved by raising polynomial degrees, which becomes very efficient in the context of IGA.
Variable order multistep formulae also allow to do this, in particular the BDF, while
for implicit and linear-implicit one-step methods such as Runge-Kutta, the order is fixed. Besides, for multistep methods the choice of good starting values is of relevance and order reduction problems might occur for Runge-Kutta type algorithms; compare Remark 6 and 7 in
[
18
]
.

The case of the continuous P1-spline discretization in time in the space-time method deserves a further remark. For illustration, we apply the method based on the time-upwind test functions introduced in
[
26
]
, which is similar to our discretization
of the Biot system, to the semi-discretized homogeneous heat equation
\boldsymbol{M}_{h}\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{w}}=\boldsymbol{A}_{h}\boldsymbol{w}
.
Here we have an spd mass matrix
\boldsymbol{M}_{h}
and a semi-definite right-hand side matrix
\boldsymbol{A}_{h}
. More precisely, we make the ansatz
\boldsymbol{w}=\sum_{k}\boldsymbol{w}_{k}\ \phi_{k}(t)
, where
\phi_{k}
are P1-splines, and multiply the system by test functions
\big(\phi_{k}(t)+\Delta t\partial_{t}\phi_{k}(t)\big)\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
, where
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th canonical basis vector, and integrate w.r.t. time. Instead of a simultaneous space-time discretization, we split the procedure thus into two discretization steps and obtain, after some straightforward computations, the recursion formula

\boldsymbol{M}_{h}\left(-\frac{3}{4}\boldsymbol{w}_{k-1}+\boldsymbol{w}_{k}-\frac{1}{4}\boldsymbol{w}_{k+1}\right)=\Delta t\boldsymbol{A}_{h}\left(\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{w}_{k-1}+\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{w}_{k}-\frac{1}{6}\boldsymbol{w}_{k+1}\right)

(37)

for the time steps
k=1,2,\ldots
. We observe the structure of an implicit 2-step method, and the consistency order is readily checked to be
m=3
. The difference to a classical multistep approach that would proceed step by step lies in the first step
k=1
where both
\boldsymbol{w}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{2}
are unknowns as there is no starting procedure for
\boldsymbol{w}_{1}
.
If we arrange all time steps in a large linear system, it will have a tridiagonal staircase block structure that reflects the propagation of information.Thus, the P1-spline temporal discretization is inappropriate for a simple sequential processing in time. The same reasoning applies to the full space-time method for the Biot system. It is thus natural to treat the fully discretized system en bloque
by suitable sparse direct or iterative methods that in the end take implicitly advantage of the staircase structure.

Finally, the factor
h_{T}^{-0.5}
in the estimate (
36
) suggests an order reduction effect.
Our numerical experiments below, however, did not reveal this, which indicates that our estimate might be too pessimistic.

6
Concluding remarks

We have introduced and analyzed a novel isogeometric method for the Biot two-field system. It is based on a space-time discretization and allows to use the spline machinery to achieve arbitrary high convergence order, given sufficient regularity of the exact solution. By several numerical examples we have validated the theory and demonstrated the applicability, even for a
3D geometry. Moreover, the well-known problem of pressure oscillations has been addressed.

In our view, there are two major issues which should be treated in future work. On the one hand, although mixed methods lead to substantial improvements, a closer look at the pressure instability from a theoretical point of view might be advisable in the
context of the space-time approach.
On the other hand it is desirable to consider the linear algebra for the resulting large-scale linear system in detail. Eventually, this may make the space-time approach also very competitive with respect to computing times.

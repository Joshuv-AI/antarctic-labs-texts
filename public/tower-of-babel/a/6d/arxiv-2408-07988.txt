Exploring learning environments for label-efficient cancer diagnosis
Samta Rani, Tanvir Ahmad, Sarfaraz Masood, Chandni Saxena

Abstract
Despite significant research efforts and advancements, cancer remains a leading cause of mortality. Early cancer prediction has become a crucial focus in cancer research to streamline patient care and improve treatment outcomes. Manual tumor detection by histopathologists can be time-consuming, prompting the need for computerized methods to expedite treatment planning. Traditional approaches to tumor detection rely on supervised learning, necessitates a large amount of annotated data for model training. However, acquiring such extensive labeled data can be laborious and time-intensive. This research examines the three learning environments—supervised learning (SL), semi-supervised learning (Semi-SL), and self-supervised learning (Self-SL)—to predict kidney, lung, and breast cancer. Three pre-trained deep learning models (Residual Network-50, Visual Geometry Group-16, and EfficientNetB0) are evaluated based on these learning settings using seven carefully curated training sets. To create the first training set (TS1), SL is applied to all annotated image samples. Five training sets (TS2-TS6) with different ratios of labeled and unlabeled cancer images are used to evaluate Semi-SL. Unlabeled cancer images from the final training set (TS7) are utilized for Self-SL assessment. Among different learning environments, outcomes from the Semi-SL setting show a strong degree of agreement with the outcomes achieved in the SL setting. The uniform pattern of observations from the pre-trained models across all three datasets validates our research’s methodology and techniques. Based on modest number of labeled samples and minimal computing cost, our study suggests that the Semi-SL option can be a highly viable replacement for the SL option under label-annotation constraint scenarios.

Related Work

Role of Supervised Learning (SL):

SL is a widely used method in the medical field for disease diagnosis, including cancer prediction. Labeled clinical data and imaging data play a critical role in training machine learning models
van2020survey
;
requa2023high
;
khosravan2018semi
.

In their study, Chen et al.
chen2023comparative
utilize four transfer learning models with varying complexities - AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet50, and Inception-v3 - for lung cancer detection. The findings indicate that despite being pre-trained on the ImageNet database, these models demonstrate significantly improved accuracy, superior initial performance, and faster convergence compared to models trained from random initialization. Sayin et al.
sayin2023comparative
employ the BreakHis dataset to evaluate the effectiveness of deep learning models in diagnosing breast cancer. The primary objective of their research is to offer a thorough comprehension of the possible utilization of deep learning methodologies in enhancing the precision of breast cancer diagnoses. By doing so, this study makes a valuable contribution to the overall enhancement of healthcare quality. Rani et al.
rani2023deep
propose a deep-learning approach for identifying breast cancer in CT images. Their approach incorporates a variety of architectural options, including combinations of different CNNs and RNNs, LSTMs, and similar models. In their study, Rana et al.
rana2023classifying
applied seven transfer learning models to the BreakHis dataset to categorize tumors. The findings of these researches assist in selecting an appropriate supervised learning model for tumor classification, even when working with an imbalanced dataset.

Role of Semi-Supervised Learning (Semi-SL):

Process flow generalization for supervised learning (SL), semi-supervised learning (Semi-SL), and self-supervised learning (Self-SL)

Semi-SL is a hybrid approach that combines elements of both unsupervised and supervised learning approaches. In order for supervised learning algorithms to train models capable of making accurate predictions, a significant amount of labeled data is usually necessary. However, in real-world scenarios such as diagnosis of medical conditions, recognition of images, recognition of speech, and document classification, there exists a vast amount of unlabeled data that is challenging to incorporate into the model. Semi-supervised learning methods can provide a solution to this problem, narrowing the gap between labeled and unlabeled data
nguyen2023active
;
schmidt2022efficient
;
yang2023semi
;
ouali2020overview
;
gao2023semi
.

Jaiswal et al.
jaiswal2019semi
introduce an advanced model based on deep convolutional neural networks, which has been thoroughly evaluated using the PatchCamelyon (PCam) benchmark dataset. This model is specifically designed for advanced research in machine learning applied to histopathology diagnosis. The proposed model, trained at the PCam level using a semi-supervised learning strategy and pseudo-labels, demonstrates significant performance improvements over a strong CNN baseline, as measured by the AUC metric. In their study, Jiang et al.
jiang2023semi
introduce Semi-His-Net, a CNN-based semi-supervised learning framework for tumor histological image processing. The framework incorporates techniques like regularization for stability and pseudo-labeling, which encourage the model to predict consistent distributions for unlabeled images with different perturbations. The proposed model is trained using a limited number of labeled and unlabeled images, providing increased flexibility and competitiveness compared to supervised CNN models during training and application.
The study conducted by Al et al.
al2021comparing
utilizes a total of nine machine learning classification algorithms to train on both SL and Semi-SL settings. Models were evaluated and trained using the “Wisconsin Diagnosis Cancer dataset”
mohammad2022diagnosis
. In order to enhance the resilience of the model, the author implemented K-fold cross-validation and fine-tuned the hyperparameters. The outcomes of all models are motivating when employing both SL and Semi-SL. The research by Wu et al.
wu2023semi
introduces a straightforward and efficient approach for classifying medical images. To improve the precision of pseudo-labels, the model predictions of samples augmented with diverse data augmentations were averaged in this study. In addition, the authors incorporate a temporal refinement method in order to improve the model’s performance and the credibility of the pseudo-labels. This technique involves linearly combining the model’s prior stage pseudo-labels with its current stage predictions.

Role of Self-Supervised Learning (Self-SL):
Self-SL is a specific type of unsupervised learning that was developed to extract significant characteristics from an enormous number of unlabeled data instances, without relying on human-annotated labels. This methodology obviates the necessity for the expensive and laborious procedure of amassing and annotating a substantial quantity of instances
bengar2021reducing
.

In their work, Gui et al.
gui2023survey
endeavors to offer a thorough review of a wide range of self-supervised techniques. The focus of their analysis includes algorithms, theory, applications, three major trends, and unanswered questions. Zhang et al.
zheng2022improving
suggest a technique to enhance the identification of breast cancer metastases by employing self-supervised contrastive learning approach. By incorporating this learning methodology as a supplementary task during the detection procedure, the feature extractor is capable of acquiring more significant representations, even in scenarios where the quantity of annotated images is restricted. Ozan et al.
ciga2022self
tackle the challenge of autonomously learning domain-specific features to improve the performance of various tasks in the field of digital histopathology. To achieve this, the authors collected and pre-trained 57 unlabeled histopathology datasets, implementing contrastive self-supervised learning on digital histopathology.
Chippa et al.
chhipa2023magnification
propose a method called “Magnification Prior Contrastive Similarity (MPCS)” for self-supervised learning of models without labels. The MPCS method incorporates the magnification factor, inductive transfer, and the reduction of human prior to enable self-supervised learning on the BreakHis small-scale breast cancer dataset. Niu et al.
niu2022unsupervised
present a novel autonomous region-centric 3D transformer model for identifying lung nodules within a set of prospective regions. In order to enhance the model’s performance on a comparatively limited dataset, the authors utilize the region-based contrastive learning technique. This method involves pre-training the 3D transformer using publicly available CT images.

Based on the review of the related work, it can be inferred that learning-based analysis and prediction are highly preferred in the field of machine learning for cancer diagnosis. This also highlights the commonly used label prediction techniques for histopathology image cases, such as the pseudo-labeling approach for Semi-SL and the contrastive learning approach for Self-SL.

Methodology

Figure 2:
Workflow for exploring three learning settings (Setting 1, Setting 2, and Setting 3) and deploying models on three datasets for predicting breast cancer, lung cancer, and kidney cancer

The workflow, datasets, and training process utilised to choose seven training sets based on three different learning settings are described in this section.

3.1
Overall Workflow

This subsection elucidates the workflow employed to analyze and assess the proportion between labeled and unlabeled data, a crucial aspect in tackling a multitude of real-world medical challenges, leveraging the abundance of available unlabeled data. The entire workflow is structured based on three distinct learning settings. Setting 1 is built upon the principles of supervised learning (SL), Setting 2 is built upon the principles of semi-supervised learning (Semi-SL), and Setting 3 is built upon the principles of self-supervised learning (Self-SL). Figure
2
provides a comprehensive overview of the entire process, accompanied by detailed explanations of each step, as described below.

•

Data Collection:
In the initial step, we gather three cancer datasets from reputable research-oriented websites, each pertaining to distinct organs. Specifically, the first dataset focuses on breast cancer, the second dataset on lung cancer, and the final dataset on kidney cancer. It is noteworthy that all three datasets utilized in this study comprise histopathological images of varying dimensions.

•

Data Preprocessing:
During this step, various image augmentation methods are applied, including rotation with a 40-degree angle, horizontal and vertical flipping, shearing with a value of 0.2, and scaling to a size of 224 by 224, as suggested by Shorten and Khoshgoftaar
shorten2019survey
.

•

Data Splitting:
The dataset is partitioned into training, testing, and validation sets at a ratio of 8:2.

•

Labeled and Unlabeled ratio:
Following the partitioning, the training data is further divided into labeled (L) and unlabeled (U) samples using seven different ratios. These divided subsets are referred to as training set 1 to 7 (TS1 to TS7). The allocation of labeled and unlabeled samples is done randomly within each ratio.

•

Label Prediction:
Subsequently, the unlabeled image samples undergo label prediction using a pseudo-labeling approach, derived from the Semi-SL setting, and a contrastive learning approach, implemented from the Self-SL setting. The choice between these approaches depends on the availability of labeled image samples within the designated ratio.

•

Training sets:
Once the label predictions for the unlabeled image samples are generated, the training sets are organized by merging the labeled image samples with the predicted-label image samples.

•

Classification:
During this step, the seven curated training sets (TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5, TS6, and TS7) are employed to train or employ three deep learning models that have already been trained: ResNet50, VGG16, and EfficientNetB0. The primary aim is to accurately classify cancer cases into the distinct categories of benign and malignant.

•

Evaluation:
In the final stage, a comprehensive evaluation and comparison of the performance of all three pre-trained models, trained on the seven carefully curated training sets, is conducted, considering the diverse learning settings.

Contrastive Learning approach:
The main goal of contrastive learning is to acquire significant representations by utilizing the comparison of positive and negative pairs of occurrences. The underlying principle is that similar instances should be embedded closely in a learned representation space, while dissimilar instances should be pushed farther apart. Experimental Design

This section examines the application of pre-trained models that were specifically developed to tackle a binary classification task involving the differentiation between two unique labels, Benign (B) and Malignant (M), present in every cancer dataset.
We also provide a description of the evaluation parameters that are necessary for assessing the performance of these models.

4.1
Models

After predicting the labels of samples in both the Semi-SL and Self-SL settings, we curate seven training sets: TS1, TS2, TS3, TS4, TS5, TS6, and TS7. As the initial step, we use these training sets for fine-tuning the pre-trained models (Resnet50, VGG16, and EfficientNetB0) that have been specifically optimized
tsuneki2022transfer
;
rana2023classifying
for the detection of histopathological images. We employ these models for training, testing, and comparing outcomes.

Resnet50:
Residual Networks are based on the concept of incorporating shortcut connections that perform identity mappings. This approach helps reduce computational time and does not introduce additional parameters to the model. In addition to 48 convolutional layers, ResNet50 comprises an average pooling layer, a completely connected layer, and a max pooling layer
chen2023comparative
;
liu2023enhancing
. The convolutional model utilized in this case employs a 7x7 kernel size and consists of a single layer. The subsequent convolutional layer includes 1x1 kernels and 64 kernels, followed by another layer with 3x3 kernels and 64 kernels. Additionally, there is one more convolutional layer with 256 1x1 kernels. In order to enhance the model’s efficacy, each of the aforementioned three layers is iterated three times
aluka2023comparative
.

VGG16:
The VGG16 architecture is widely adopted in the field due to its inclusion of 16 layers, which consists of a convolutional base layer comprising a sequence of conv2D, max pooling, and conv2D pooling operations. Following the convolutional base, a flattened layer is utilized to convert the feature matrix into a vector, and dense layers are added for categorization purposes. The architecture comprises both a convolutional base and a classifier component. The system leverages classification features and is equipped with pre-trained weights derived from the ImageNet dataset
aluka2023comparative
;
zargar2023using
.

EfficientNetB0:
The EfficientNet series, including EfficientNetB0 – EfficientNetB7, was introduced by the “Google AI” research team in 2019 as a powerful backbone architecture for tasks including classifying images in ImageNet, segmenting images, and performing transfer learning. These models surpassed several cutting-edge deep CNN architectures, including Inception-V3, ResNet50, Inception-ResNetV2, and DenseNet. The EfficientNet models achieve enhanced performance by scaling the architecture using specific coefficients, following a consistent compound scaling mechanism
raza2023lung
;
ravi2023multichannel
. Trained on an extensive ImageNet dataset containing more than a million images, the models offer accurate categorization into 1000 different object types, enabling them to learn diverse and rich image representations.

Conclusion

In this work, we present a methodology that conducts a comprehensive investigation into the influence of annotated and non-annotated cancer images on the performance of pre-trained models. The learning process is categorized into three distinct settings: SL, Semi-SL, and Self-SL, based on the presence of labeled or unlabeled samples. To validate our methodology, we leverage three datasets encompassing diverse image types, sizes, and cancer variants. Our methodology involves the utilization of seven curated training sets, comprising varying proportions of labeled and unlabeled histopathological cancer images. We train three pre-trained models (ResNet50, VGG16, and EfficientNetB0) on these sets (TS1-TS7) for cancer prediction. In the Semi-SL setting, we employ the pseudo-labeling approach to predict labels for unlabeled images, while in the Self-SL setting, we adopt the contrastive learning approach. Throughout our investigation, the EfficientNetB0 model consistently demonstrates the highest accuracy among the three learning settings. Notably, the outcomes obtained in the Semi-SL setting closely resemble those achieved in the SL setting, and the results in the self-supervised learning setting are also comparable. The present research makes a valuable contribution by enabling cancer prediction using unlabeled or minimum-sized labeled image data. The framework can be adapted to other modalities without class labels. Using a combination of labeling algorithms that utilise various deep learning classification architectures can improve the direction of future research to increase the accuracy of inferencing the diagnosis.

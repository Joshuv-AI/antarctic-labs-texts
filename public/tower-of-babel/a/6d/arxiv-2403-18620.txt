Triple product p p -adic L L -functions for finite slope families and a p p -adic Gross–Zagier formula
Ting-Han Huang

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize two results of H. Darmon and V. Rotger on triple product p p -adic L L -functions attached to Hida families to finite slope families. We first prove a p p -adic Gross–Zagier formula, then demonstrate an application to a special case of the equivariant Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for supersingular elliptic curves.



1
Introduction

In
[
DR14
]
, a
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula is proved for the triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions for ordinary families of modular forms.
More precisely, the formula relates

-

the special values of the Garrett–Rankin triple product
p
-adic
L
-function at classical points lying outside the region of interpolation, to

-

the
p
-adic Abel–Jacobi images of
generalized diagonal cycles
in the product of three Kugo–Sato varieties, evaluated at certain differentials.

The fist part of this paper aims to generalize the above result to triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions for finite slope families of modular forms.

Let

f\in S_{k}(N_{f},\chi_{f})
,
g\in S_{\ell}(N_{g},\chi_{g})
,
h\in S_{m}(N_{h},\chi_{h})
be a triple of normalized primitive cuspidal eigenforms and set
N:=\ell\mathrm{cm}(N_{f},N_{g},N_{h})
.
We will assume that
\chi_{f}\cdot\chi_{g}\cdot\chi_{h}=1
, which implies that
k+\ell+m
is even.

The triple
(k,\ell,m)
is said to be
balanced
if the largest number is strictly less than the sum of the other two.
A triple that is not balanced is called
unbalanced
, and the largest number in an unbalanced triple will be called the
dominant weight
.

For such a triple
(f,g,h)
, one has the (complex) Garrett–Rankin
L
-function
L(f,g,h;s)
.
It can be viewed as the
L
-function attached to the tensor product

V(f,g,h)=V(f)\otimes V(g)\otimes V(h)

of the (compatible systems of)
p
-adic Galois representations associated with
f,g
and
h
respectively.
This
L
-function admits a functional equation relating its values at
s
and
k+\ell+m-2-s
.
As a consequence, the order of vanishing of
L(f,g,h;s)
at the central point
c:=\frac{k+\ell+m-2}{2}
is determined by the root number
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
of the functional equation.
The root number
\varepsilon
can be further written as a product
\prod_{v\mid N\infty}\varepsilon_{v}
of local root numbers
\varepsilon_{v}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
We will assume the following assumption throughout this article as in
[
DR14
]
.

Assumption
H: The local root numbers
\varepsilon_{v}=+1
for all finite primes
v\mid N
.

As indicated in
[
DR14
, § 1]
, this condition is satisfied in a broad collection of settings.
Under Assumption H,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\infty}
depends only on the triple of weights
(k,\ell,m)
:

\varepsilon_{\infty}=\begin{cases}-1\quad\textrm{if }(k,\ell,m)\textrm{ is balanced;}\\
+1\quad\textrm{if }(k,\ell,m)\textrm{ is unbalanced.}\end{cases}

In particular, the
L
-function necessarily vanishes at the central point
c
when
(k,\ell,m)
is balanced.
In this situation, instead of studying the values of
L(f,g,h;s)
, one usually studies the values of its derivatives or its associated
p
-adic
L
-functions and expects them to encode arithmetic information.

Choose a prime
p\nmid N
such that
f,g,h
are of (sufficiently small) finite slope at
p
.
We may find (
p
-adic) Coleman families
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{g}
,
\mathbf{h}
passing through
f,g,h
, defined over certain weight spaces
\Omega_{f},\Omega_{g},\Omega_{h}
, respectively.
In the language of rigid analytic geometry, the space
\Omega_{\bullet}
for
\bullet\in\{f,g,h\}
is a finite rigid analytic cover over a subset of the weight space
\mathcal{W}:=\Hom_{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
which always contains the integers
\mathbb{Z}
via the identification
k\mapsto(\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{k})
.

When
\mathbf{f}
is of slope
a\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
,
a point
x\in\Omega_{f}
is said to be classical if its image in
\Omega
, denoted by
\kappa(x)
, belongs to
\mathbb{Z}_{>a+1}
.
We will often identify a classical weight
x
with
\kappa(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
by an abuse of notation.
We denote the set of classical points in
\Omega_{f}
by
\Omega_{f,\mathrm{cl}}
.
For almost all
x\in\Omega_{f,\mathrm{cl}}
, the specialization of
\mathbf{f}
at
x\in\Omega_{f,\mathrm{cl}}
, denoted by
\mathbf{f}_{x}
(or simply
f_{x}
), is a normalized eigenform of weight
\kappa(x)
on
\Gamma_{1}(N,p):=\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma_{0}(p)
.
For all but finitely many such
x
,
\mathbf{f}_{x}
is a (finite slope)
p
-stabilization of a normalized eigenform of the same weight on
\Gamma_{1}(N)
, denoted by
f_{x}^{0}
.

When
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{g}
,
\mathbf{h}
are Hida (ordinary) families, one can view them as maps

\mathbf{f}:\Omega_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}\llbracket q\rrbracket,\ \mathbf{g}:\Omega_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}\llbracket q\rrbracket,\ \mathbf{h}:\Omega_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}\llbracket q\rrbracket.

The triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions for Hida families in
[
DR14
]
is defined as

\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}):=\frac{(\mathbf{f}^{*},e_{\ord}(\theta^{\bullet}\mathbf{g}^{[p]}\times\mathbf{h}))}{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\mathbf{f}^{*})},

where
\mathbf{f}^{*}
is the Atkin–Lehner involution of
\mathbf{f}
,
e_{\ord}
is Hida’s ordinary projector,
\theta=q\frac{d}{dq}
is Serre’s operator,
\mathbf{g}^{[p]}:=(1-VU)\mathbf{g}
is the
p
-depletion of
\mathbf{g}
, and
(\phantom{e},\phantom{e})
is the Petersson product.

For finite slope families, one needs a different approach.
A more geometric construction of triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions for finite slope families is developed by F. Andreatta and A. Iovita in
[
AI21
]
.
Section §
2
is dedicated to recall their construction.

Roughly speaking, one wants to define the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function as the ratio

\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}):=\frac{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\nabla^{\bullet}\mathbf{g}^{[p]}\times\mathbf{h})}{(\mathbf{f}^{*},\mathbf{f}^{*})}.

In order for this expression to make sense, one needs to
p
-adically iterate the Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla
.
When
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}
are Hida families, as we saw in above definition, one may replace
\nabla
by
\theta=q\frac{d}{dq}
and works only on the
q
-expansions.
Then there is a straightforward way to iterate
\theta
to
p
-adic powers, provided that the form it acts on lies in the kernel of
U
(e.g. the
p
-depletion
\mathbf{g}^{[p]}
).
The case for
\nabla
is more complicated and requires a careful study on the convergence.
However, we would like to refer the detailed computations to
[
AI21
]
.

The triple product
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
can be viewed as a three-variable function

\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}):\Omega_{f}\times\Omega_{g}\times\Omega_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p},

whose value at
(x,y,z)\in\Omega_{f}\times\Omega_{g}\times\Omega_{h}
is

\frac{(\mathbf{f}_{x}^{*},\nabla^{-t}\mathbf{g}_{y}^{[p]}\times\mathbf{h}_{z})}{(\mathbf{f}_{x}^{*},\mathbf{f}_{x}^{*})},

where
t\in\Omega
satisfies
\kappa(x)=\kappa(y)+\kappa(z)-2t
.
As one can observe, the form
\mathbf{f}
plays a distinct role in
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}
.
What is not so obvious is that
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
is symmetric, up to a
\pm
sign, in the last two components.
Similarly, one can define
\mathscr{L}^{g}_{p}=\mathscr{L}^{g}_{p}(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{h})
and
\mathscr{L}^{h}_{p}=\mathscr{L}^{h}_{p}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
.

Set
\Sigma=\Omega_{f}\times\Omega_{g}\times\Omega_{h}
and
\Sigma_{\operatorname{cl}}=\Omega_{f,\operatorname{cl}}\times\Omega_{g,\operatorname{cl}}\times\Omega_{h,\operatorname{cl}}
.
The set
\Sigma_{\operatorname{cl}}
can be naturally divided into four disjoint subsets:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bal}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{cl}}\mid(\kappa(x),\kappa(y),\kappa(z))\textrm{ is balanced}\};

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{cl}}\mid\kappa(x)\geq\kappa(y)+\kappa(z)\};

\displaystyle\Sigma_{g}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{cl}}\mid\kappa(y)\geq\kappa(x)+\kappa(z)\};

\displaystyle\Sigma_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{cl}}\mid\kappa(z)\geq\kappa(x)+\kappa(y)\}.

The
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}
on
\Sigma_{f}
interpolates the
square roots
of
central critical values
of the classical
L
-functions
L(f_{x}^{0},g_{y}^{0},h_{z}^{0};s)
, which is a combined result of the Ichino formula,
[
DR14
, Theorem 4.7]
and
[
AI21
, Corollary 5.13]
.

Points in
\Sigma_{\bal}
, on the other hand, lie outside this region of interpolation, and we are interested in the values of
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}
at these points.
By definition, the value of
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
at a balanced point
(x,y,z)
involves a ‘negative’ power
\nabla^{-t}
, which is originally defined as a
p
-adic limit.
On the other hand, the interpolating property of
\nabla
implies the relation
\nabla^{s}\circ\nabla^{-t}=\nabla^{-t+s}
.
In other words, this negative power also serves as a
t
-th anti-derivative with respect to
\nabla
.
Hence, we may expect to express these special values via a
p
-adic integration theory.
A suitable candidate is Coleman
p
-adic integration or its generalization, Besser’s finite polynomial cohomology.
Furthermore, these
p
-adic integration theories allow us to compute
p
-adic Abel–Jacobi maps, which will be discussed bellow.

Let
E\rightarrow X=X_{1}(N)
be the universal elliptic curve over the modular curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(or a finite extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
).
For any
n\geq 0
, we let
W_{n}
be the
n
-th Kuga–Sato variety over
X
.
It is an
(n+1)
-dimensional variety obtained by desingularizing the
n
-fold fiber product
E^{n}
over
X
(c.f.
[
BDP13
, Apeendix]
).

Now fix a balanced weight
(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\bal}
.
We write
(x,y,z)=(r_{1}+2,r_{2}+2,r_{3}+2)
,
r:=\frac{1}{2}(r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3})
, and set

W:=W_{r_{1}}\times W_{r_{2}}\times W_{r_{3}}.

As in
[
DR14
, § 3.1]
, one can construct a homologically trivial cycle

\Delta_{x,y,z}\in\CH^{r+2}(W)_{0}:=\ker(\CH^{r+2}(W)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{cl}}H^{2r+4}_{\dR}(W/\mathbb{C}_{p})),

and study its image under the
p
-adic Abel–Jacobi map

\AJ_{p}:\CH^{r+2}(W)_{0}\rightarrow[\Fil^{r+2}H^{2r+3}_{\dR}(W/\mathbb{C}_{p})]^{\vee}.

Suppose that the family
\mathbf{f}
is of slope
a
with
a<x-1
, and
assume that the specializations
\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}
are
p
-stabilizations of classical modular forms
f_{x}^{0},g_{y}^{0},h_{z}^{0}
, respectively.
For simplicity, we will further assume that
f_{x}^{0},g_{y}^{0},h_{z}^{0}
are newforms of the same level
N
in the introduction.

Associated with the triple
(f_{x}^{0},g_{y}^{0},h_{z}^{0})
, we construct an element

\displaystyle\eta_{f}^{a}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h}

\displaystyle\in H^{r_{1}+1}_{\dR}(W_{r_{1}})\otimes\Fil^{r_{2}+1}H^{r_{2}+1}_{\dR}(W_{r_{2}})\otimes\Fil^{r_{3}+1}H^{r_{3}+1}_{\dR}(W_{r_{3}})

\displaystyle\subset\Fil^{r+2}(H^{r_{1}+1}_{\dR}(W_{r_{1}})\otimes H^{r_{2}+1}_{\dR}(W_{r_{2}})\otimes H^{r_{3}+1}_{\dR}(W_{r_{3}}))

\displaystyle\subset\Fil^{r+2}H^{2r+3}_{\dR}(W/\mathbb{C}_{p}),

where the first inclusion is by the balancedness assumption, and the second one is from the Künneth decomposition of
W=W_{r_{1}}\times W_{r_{2}}\times W_{r_{3}}
.
In particular,
\eta_{f}^{a}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h}
lies inside the domain of
\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{x,y,z})
.

Before stating the Gross–Zagier formula, we need to introduce several Euler factors.
For any
\phi\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N),\chi)
, we shall write the Hecke polynomial as

x^{2}-a_{p}(\phi)x+\chi(p)p^{k-1}=(x-\alpha_{\phi})(x-\beta_{\phi})

with
\ord_{p}(\alpha_{\phi})\leq\ord_{p}(\beta_{\phi})
.
For the modular form
f_{x}^{0}
associated with
\mathbf{f}_{x}
, we also assume that
a=\ord_{p}(\alpha_{f_{x}^{0}})
.
To simplify the notations, we will write
\alpha_{f}
for
\alpha_{f_{x}^{0}}
and similarly for the modular forms
g
and
h
when no confusion might occur.

The first result of this paper is the theorem below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\bal}
.
Let
c:=(x+y+z-2)/2
and write
x=y+z-2t
with
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then

\mathscr{L}_{p}^{f}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})(x,y,z)=(-1)^{t-1}\frac{\mathscr{E}(f_{x}^{0},g_{y}^{0},h_{z}^{0})}{(t-1)!\mathscr{E}_{0}(f_{x}^{0})\mathscr{E}_{1}(f_{x}^{0})}\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{x,y,z})(\eta_{f}^{a}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h}),

(1)

where the Euler factors are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathscr{E}_{0}(f_{x}^{0})&:=1-\beta_{f}^{2}\chi_{f}^{-1}(p)p^{1-x},\\
\mathscr{E}_{1}(f_{x}^{0})&:=1-\beta_{f}^{2}\chi_{f}^{-1}(p)p^{-x},\\
\mathscr{E}(f_{x}^{0},g_{y}^{0},h_{z}^{0})&:=(1-\beta_{f}\alpha_{g}\alpha_{h}p^{-c})(1-\beta_{f}\alpha_{g}\beta_{h}p^{-c})(1-\beta_{f}\beta_{g}\alpha_{h}p^{-c})(1-\beta_{f}\beta_{g}\beta_{h}p^{-c}).\end{split}

(2)

After establishing the
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula, we proceed to generalize two results in the paper
[
DR17
]
.
Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and

\rho:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\Aut_{L}(V_{\rho})\cong\GL_{n}(L)

be an Artin representation
with coefficient in a finite extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}
, which factors through a finite Galois extension
H/\mathbb{Q}
.
Then one has
L(E,\rho,s)
, the Hasse–Weil–Artin
L
-function of
E
twisted by
\rho
.
Let
E(H)^{\rho}:=\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(V_{\rho},E(H)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L)
be the
\rho
-isotypic component of the Mordell–Weil group.
One defines the analytic and algebraic rank of the twist of
E
by
\rho
as

r_{\mathrm{an}}(E,\rho):=\ord_{s=1}L(E,\rho,s),\quad r(E,\rho):=\dim_{L}(E(H))^{\rho}.

The Galois-equivariant Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture then predicts that

r_{\mathrm{an}}(E,\rho)=r(E,\rho).

The triple product
L
-function appears in the special case
\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
, where

\rho_{1},\rho_{2}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(L)

are two-dimensional odd irreducible Artin representation with
\chi=\det(\rho_{1})=\det(\rho_{2})^{-1}
.
As the elliptic curve
E
is associated with a cuspidal newform
f\in S_{2}(N_{f})
, and the representations
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are associated with cuspidal newforms
g\in S_{1}(N_{g},\chi),h\in S_{1}(N_{h},\chi^{-1})
, respectively,

L(E,\rho;s)
is equal to the triple product
L
-function
L(f,g,h;s)
.

Assuming that the modular forms
f,g,h
are ordinary at a fixed prime
p
, and let
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}
be three Hida families passing through respectively
f,g,h
, Darmon–Rotger proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
DR17
, Theorem B]
)
.

If
L(f,g,h;1)=0
and
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{g}(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{h})(1,2,1)\neq 0
for some choice of test vectors, then

\dim_{L_{p}}\Sel_{p}(E,\rho)\geq 2

where
L_{p}
is the completion of
L
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
,
\Sel_{p}(E,\rho)
is the
\rho
-isotypic component of the Bloch–Kato Selmer group (c.f. §6.1 in loc. cit.).
The statement stays true if we replace
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{g}(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{h})
by
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{h}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
.

The technique used in
[
DR17
]
was later improved by Bertolini–Seveso–Venerucci in
[
BSV22
]
.
We will give an over-simplified illustration of their approach below.

First, one constructs an element (called the
diagonal class
)

\underline{\kappa}=\underline{\kappa}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},V(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}))

in the cohomology group of a certain big Galois representation
V(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
,
such that the image of
\underline{\kappa}_{p}:=res_{p}(\underline{\kappa})
under the Perrin-Riou big logarithm

\mathscr{L}og_{\xi}
is the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{\xi}
for
\xi\in\{f,g,h\}
, where
res_{p}
denotes the restriction to
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

Second, one studies the specialization
\underline{\kappa}_{p}(\mathbf{f_{2}},\mathbf{g_{1}},\mathbf{h_{1}})
of the class
\underline{\kappa}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
to weight
(2,1,1)
.
It turns out that the complex
L
-function encodes certain information about this specialization.
In particular, one has

Theorem 1.3

(
[
DR14
, Theorem 6.4]
and
[
BSV22
, Theorem B]
)
.

The class
\underline{\kappa}_{p}(\mathbf{f_{2}},\mathbf{g_{1}},\mathbf{h_{1}})
is crystalline if and only if the complex
L
-function
L(f,g,h;s)
vanishes at the central point
s=1
.

Remark 1.4
.

The above theorem actually holds for specializations at general unbalanced weights
(x,y,z)
, with the central point being
c=\frac{x+y+z-2}{2}
.

Lastly, one can construct, out of the crystalline class
\kappa(\mathbf{f_{2}},\mathbf{g_{1}},\mathbf{h_{1}})
, at least two linearly independent elements in
\Sel_{p}(E,\rho)
and completes the proof.

Remark 1.5
.

Since
g,h
are modular forms of weight
1
, they are necessarily ordinary at
p
,
the two
p
-adic
L
-functions
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{g}(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{h})
and
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{h}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
are already defined in
[
DR14
]
.
The only remaining obstacle for the finite slope case is about
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{f}
and the existence of the class
\underline{\kappa}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
that gives rise to
L_{p}^{f}
,
whose proof will rely on our
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula for finite slope families.

To summarize, our main result in the second part is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
be as in the setting of Theorem
1.1
.
Then there is a unique element
\underline{\kappa}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},V(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}))
such that

\mathscr{L}og_{f}(\underline{\kappa}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}))=\mathscr{L}_{p}^{f}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}).

Moreover, when specialized to a unbalanced triple
(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{f}
, the class
\underline{\kappa}_{p}(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})
is crystalline if and only if the classical
L
-function
L(f_{x}^{0},g_{y}^{0},h_{z}^{0};s)
vanishes at the central point
c=\frac{x+y+z-2}{2}
.

Put in other words, the second part aims to answer the question raised in
[
BSV22
, Remark 1.3]
;
namely, whether their two main results can be generalized to the finite slope case.
Most of the materials are already provided in their paper, except for various Galois cohomology groups and the big logarithm map for finite slope families.
Once these objects are carefully introduced, the proof of Theorem
1.6
will follow identically the arguments in
[
BSV22
, § 8 and § 9]
.
For this reason, we will mainly adopt their notations and omit most of the proofs in §
4
.

With Theorem
1.6
established, it is not hard to see that one can prove an analogous result of Theorem
1.2
by the same strategy as in
[
DR17
, § 6]
.

Remark 1.7
.

We would like to mention that we do not generalize or improve
[
DR17
, Theorem A]
in any sense, since one can always find a prime
p
such that the elliptic curve
E
and the modular forms
g,h
are ordinary at
p
.

2
Triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions

In this section, we will recall the definition of the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function for finite slope families.
The main reference is
[
AI21
]
, but we will follow closely
[
AI19
]
for simpler setups.
Before going through the construction, we would like to briefly explain the idea of their work.

Fix an integer
N\geq 5
and a prime
p\nmid N
.
Let
X=X_{1}(N)
be the modular curve defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with the universal generalized elliptic curve
\pi:E\rightarrow X
.
Let
Y\subset X
be the affine modular curve and
C\subset X
be the closed subscheme of cusps.
Then the two sheaves

\underline{\omega}:=\pi_{*}(\Omega^{1}_{E/Y})\textrm{ and }\mathcal{H}:=\mathbb{R}^{1}\pi_{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{{E}/Y})

on
Y
have canonical extensions to
X
(c.f.
[
BDP13
, § 1]
), which will still be denoted by the same notations
\underline{\omega}
and
\mathcal{H}
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r})
can be identified as the space of modular forms of weight
r
.
For a strict neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of the ordinary locus in
X
,
H^{0}(\mathcal{U},\underline{\omega}^{r})
can be identified as the space of overconvergent modular forms of weight
r
.

The sheaf
\mathcal{H}
is equipped with a (increasing) filtration given by the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\underline{\omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\underline{\omega}^{-1}\rightarrow 0

and a Gauss–Manin connection

\nabla:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log C).

For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
\mathcal{H}^{r}:=\Sym^{r}\mathcal{H}
be its
r
-th symmetric power.
The filtration and the connection on
\mathcal{H}
then extend naturally to
\mathcal{H}^{r}
.
By composing with the Kodaira–Spencer isomorphism

\mathrm{KS}:\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log C)\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{\omega}^{2},

the Gauss–Manin connection may be viewed as a map from
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to
\mathcal{H}^{r+2}
.

The idea is to construct a sheaf
\mathfrak{w}_{k}
(over a certain strict neighborhood) which
p
-adically interpolates the collection
\{\underline{\omega}^{r}\}_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}
, such that a
p
-adic family of overconvergent modular forms may be viewed as a section of
\mathfrak{w}_{k}
over some strict neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of the ordinary locus.

There is also a sheaf
\mathbb{W}_{k}
which
p
-adically interpolates
(\mathcal{H}^{n},\Fil_{\bullet},\nabla)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular, it allows us to take certain
p
-adic powers of the connection
\nabla
, which is crucial for defining the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
as explained in the introduction.

Since their construction is highly technical, we prefer not to provide detailed proofs in this section.
Instead, we will only set up notations and statements that are sufficient for our application.

2.1
The modular sheaf
\mathfrak{w}_{k}
and the de Rham sheaf
\mathbb{W}_{k}

Let
N,p
be as before and
X=X_{1}(N)
be the modular curve defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
We write
\mathfrak{X}
for the formal completion of
X
along its special fiber.
We denote by
\operatorname{Hdg}
be the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-ideal that is locally generated by a lift of the Hasse invariant.

For any integer
n\geq 2
, we denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
the formal open subscheme in the formal admissible blow-up of
\mathfrak{X}
with respect to the ideal
\mathcal{J}=(p,\operatorname{Hdg}^{n})
, defined such that
\mathcal{J}
is generated by
\operatorname{Hdg}^{n}
over
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
.
The generalized elliptic curve
{E}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{n}
has a canonical subgroup of order
p^{r}
, where this
r
depends on
n
(c.f.
[
AI21
, § 3.1]
).
We will write
\mathcal{X}_{n}
for the adic generic fiber of
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a
p
-adically complete and separated ring, and
k:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\times}
a continuous homomorphism.
Then
k
is called an analytic weight of radius
r\in\mathbb{N}
if there exists an element
u=u_{k}\in p^{1-r}\Lambda
such that
k(t)=\exp(u\log(t))
for all
t\in 1+p^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
k:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\times}
be an analytic weight.
By saying ‘specialized to a classical weight
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,’ we mean that we take the composition
\gamma\circ k
for some homomorphism
\gamma:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
\gamma\circ k:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
is the
\ell
-th power map.

One has the following results (c.f.
[
AI21
, § 3.2 & § 3.3]
):

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
k
be an analytic weight of radius
r
as above, and take an integer
n
such that
{E}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{n}
admits a canonical subgroup of order
p^{r}
.
Then there are
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}_{n}}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Lambda
-modules
\mathfrak{w}_{k}
and
\mathbb{W}_{k}
with the following properties:

1.

\mathfrak{w}_{k}
is an invertible
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}_{n}}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Lambda
-module.
Over the generic fiber
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, the global sections of
\mathfrak{w}_{k}
can then be identified as
p
-adic families of overconvergent modular forms of weight
k
;

2.

\mathbb{W}_{k}
has a natural increasing filtration
(\Fil_{n})_{n\geq 0}
by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}_{n}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda
-modules with
\Fil_{0}=\mathfrak{w}_{k}
.
Moreover, when specializing to a classical weight
r\geq 1
,
\Fil_{r}\mathbb{W}_{r}\cong\mathcal{H}^{r}
over
\mathcal{X}_{n}
and is compatible with the Hodge filtration of
\mathcal{H}^{r}
.

3.

There is a Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla_{k}
(with poles at
V(\operatorname{Hdg})
) on
\mathbb{W}_{k}
that satisfies Griffiths transversality.
By composing with the Kodaira–Spencer map, the connection
\nabla_{k}
can be viewed as a map from
\mathbb{W}_{k}
to
\mathbb{W}_{k+2}
.

The
q
-expansions.

As explained in
[
AI21
, § 3.5]
, we have the
q
-expansion map

H^{0}(\mathfrak{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})\rightarrow\Lambda((q))

obtained by first restricting to the ordinary locus then applying the unit root splitting.
On the other hand, the
q
-expansion map can also be described by using the Tate curve.

Let
T=\mathrm{Tate}(q^{N})
be the Tate curve over
\Spf(S)
, where
S=\Lambda((q))
.
We fix the canonical basis
(\omega_{\can},\eta_{\can}=\nabla(\theta)(\omega_{\can}))
of
\cal H
on
T
.
Let
\mathbb{W}_{k}(q)
be the module of
\mathbb{W}_{k}
over the Tate curve, then we have the following local description:
\mathbb{W}_{k}(q)=S\langle\frac{Y}{1+p^{r}Z}\rangle(1+p^{r}Z)^{k}

(c.f.
[
AI21
, § 3.5]
).
If we set
V_{k,i}:=Y^{i}(1+p^{r}Z)^{k-i}
, then the filtration of
\mathbb{W}_{k}
is given by
\Fil_{h}\mathbb{W}_{k}(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{h}S\cdot V_{k,i}
.
The
q
-expansion map then corresponds to the map
\sum_{i}a_{i}(q)V_{k,i}\mapsto a_{0}(q)
.

Remark 2.4
.

Since
k
is analytic, the element
k(1+p^{r}Z)
is well-defined in
S\langle Z\rangle
and we sometimes denote it by
(1+p^{r}Z)^{k}
.
The description above comes directly from the explicit construction of vector bundles with marked sections (c.f.
[
AI21
, § 2]
).
One should think of
1+p^{r}Z
(resp.
Y
) as an element that parametrizes the deformation of
\omega_{\can}
(resp.
\eta_{\can})
.
In particular, the specialization of
V_{k,i}
to a classical weight
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
can be identified as the element
\omega_{\can}^{\ell-i}\eta_{\can}^{i}
of
\mathcal{H}^{\ell}
on the Tate curve when
i\leq\ell
.

The operators
U
and
V
.

Similar to the classical case, we can define Hecke operators
U
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})
and
V:H^{0}(\mathfrak{X}_{n},\mathfrak{w}_{k})\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},\mathfrak{w}_{k})
, whose actions on the
q
-expansions are the usual ones
(c.f.
[
AI21
, § 3.6, § 3.7]
).
Moreover,
U
is a compact operator.
In particular, one has the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
AI21
, Cor. 3.26]
)
.

The operator
U
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})
admits a Fredholm determinant
P(T)\in\Lambda\llbracket T\rrbracket
, and for any non-negative rational number
a
the space
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})
admits a slope
a
-decomposition

H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})=H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})^{\leq a}\oplus H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})^{>a}.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{f}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathfrak{w}_{k})
.
We define
\mathbf{f}^{[p]}:=(1-VU)\mathbf{f}
and call it the
p
-depletion of
\mathbf{f}
.
If
\mathbf{f}(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(\mathbf{f})q^{n}V_{k,0}
is the
q
-expansion of
\mathbf{f}
, then the
q
-expansion of
\mathbf{f}^{[p]}
is
\mathbf{f}^{[p]}(q)=\sum_{n=0,\ p\nmid n}^{\infty}a_{n}(\mathbf{f})q^{n}V_{k,0}
.

p
-adic iterations of the Gauss–Manin connection.

Another important property of the sheaf
\mathbb{W}_{k}
is that it is possible to iterate the Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla
to certain
p
-adic powers and can be described explicitly in terms of
q
-expansions.

We first need an assumption on the weights.

Assumption 2.7
.

Let
k
and
s
be two analytic weights for the same ring
\Lambda
that satisfy

k(t)=\chi(t)t^{a}\exp(u_{k}\log(t)),\quad s(t)=\chi^{\prime}(t)t^{b}\exp(u_{s}\log(t))\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

where

\bullet

a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
;

\bullet

\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
are finite character of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and
\chi
is even;

\bullet

u_{k}\in p\Lambda
and
u_{s}\in p^{2}\Lambda
.

Under this assumption, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
AI21
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

Suppose
\mathbf{g}\in H^{0}(\mathfrak{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})^{U=0}
and let
\mathbf{g}(q)=\sum_{h}\mathbf{g}_{h}(q)V_{k,h}
be its
q
-expansion.
Then there are positive integers
b
and
\gamma
depending on
k,s,n
and an element

\nabla^{s}(\mathbf{g})\in\mathrm{Hdg}^{-\gamma}H^{0}(\mathfrak{X}_{b},\mathbb{W}_{k+2s})
such that

\nabla^{s}(\mathbf{g})(q):=\sum_{h}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{u_{s}}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(u_{k}+u_{s}-h-1-i)\theta^{s-j}(\mathbf{g}_{h}(q))V_{k+2s,j+h},

where
\theta=q\frac{d}{dq}
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
\gamma:R\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a homomorphism such that
\gamma\circ s:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
is a positive classical weight
\ell
.
Then the specialization of
\nabla^{s}(\mathbf{g})
via
\gamma
is
\nabla^{\ell}(\mathbf{g})
, the usual
\ell
-th iteration of
\nabla
.

Remark 2.10
.

The requirement that
u_{k}
and
u_{s}
are divisible by powers of
p
is for convergence reason.
We should mention that an improved version is developed by Ananyo Kazi in his Ph.D. thesis
[
Kaz22
]
, which slightly releases the conditions.

The overconvergent projections.

We have seen how to define
\nabla^{s}(\mathbf{g}^{[p]})
for a suitable weight
s
.
However, it is only a section of
\mathbb{W}_{k+2s}
instead of
\mathfrak{w}_{k+2s}
.
In order to define the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function via Petersson product of families of overconvergent modular forms, we need the overconvergent projection, which sends
\nabla^{s}(\mathbf{g}^{[p]})
to a certain section of
\mathfrak{w}_{k+2s}
.

We first consider the following commutative diagram of sheaves on
\mathcal{X}_{n}

(

, Lemma 3.32]

.

For any

(
[
AI21
, Theorem 3.34]
)
.

Fix a positive slope
a
.
Then we have an integer

and an isomorphism

H^{\dagger,\leq a}:H^{1}_{\dR}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathbb{W}_{k})^{\leq a}\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda\left[\prod_{i=0}^{m_{a}}(u_{k}-i)\right]\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathfrak{w}_{k+2})^{\leq a}\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda\left[\prod_{i=0}^{m_{a}}(u_{k}-i)\right]

where the superscript

subspace with respect to the

The maps

and

above are referred as the overconvergent projections.

Remark 2.13
.

When specialized to a classical weight
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, one can see that the map

for

coincides with the overconvergent projection defined in

.

We shall now give the description of the map

on the

Recall that on

\nabla_{k}(aV_{k,i})=\theta(a)V_{k+2,i}+a(u_{k}-i)V_{k+2,h+1},\quad a\in\Lambda((q)).

By definition, the overconvergent projection of a class

for

is the unique element in

that represents the same class modulo

.
Hence, after shifting the numbers, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
AI21
, Prop. 3.37]
)
.

Consider an element
\gamma\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{n},\Fil_{m}\mathbb{W}_{k})
with its class

be its

-expansion.
Then the

-expansion of

is

\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\frac{\theta^{i}\gamma_{i}(q)}{(u_{k}-2-i+1)(u_{k}-2-i+2)\cdots(u_{k}-2)}V_{k,0}.

2.2
Definition of the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function

We are now able to define triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions for finite slope families.
We first recall some results in the complex case.
For any newform
f
of level
N_{f}
and nebentypus
\chi_{f}
, we write
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
for the number field generated by all Hecke eigenvalues of
f
.
We denote by
\pi_{f}
the automorphic representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by
f
.
If
N
is a multiple of
N_{f}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{f}\subset L
, then we let
S_{k}(N,L)[\pi_{f}]
be the
f
-isotypic subspace of
S_{k}(N,L)
.

Now let
f\in S_{k}(N_{f},\chi_{f})
,
g\in S_{\ell}(N_{g},\chi_{g})
,
h\in S_{m}(N_{h},\chi_{h})
be a triple of normalized primitive cuspidal eigenforms.
We set
N=\operatorname{\ell\textrm{cm}}(N_{f},N_{g},N_{h})
and
\mathbb{Q}_{f,g,h}=\mathbb{Q}_{f}\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{g}\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{h}
.
We assume that
\chi_{f}\cdot\chi_{g}\cdot\chi_{h}=1
and the triple
(k,\ell,m)
is unbalanced with
k=\ell+m+2t^{\prime}
for some
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In this situation, we have the following result of M. Harris and S. Kulda
[
HK91
]
, refined by A. Ichino
[
Ich08
]
and T. C. Watson
[
Wat02
]
, which is usual known as the Ichino formula.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
f,g,h
be as above, then there exist holomorphic modular forms

\breve{f}\in S_{k}(N,\mathbb{Q}_{f,g,h})[\pi_{f}],\ \breve{g}\in S_{k}(N,\mathbb{Q}_{f,g,h})[\pi_{g}],\ \breve{h}\in S_{k}(N,\mathbb{Q}_{f,g,h})[\pi_{h}]

and constants
C_{q}\in\mathbb{Q}_{f,g,h}
depending only on the local components of
\breve{f},\breve{g},\breve{h}
for all
q\mid N\infty
such that

\frac{\prod_{q}C_{q}}{\pi^{2k}}L\left(\breve{f},\breve{g},\breve{h};\frac{k+l+m-2}{2}\right)=|I(\breve{f},\breve{g},\breve{h})|^{2}.

Moreover, there exists a choice of
\breve{f},\breve{g},\breve{h}
such that all
C_{q}\neq 0
.

In the above theorem,
L(f,g,h;s)
is the complex Garrett-Rankin triple product
L
-function and

I(\breve{f},\breve{g},\breve{h}):=(\breve{f}^{*},\delta^{t}(\breve{g})\cdot\breve{h})

where

is the Petersson inner product,
\delta
is the Maass-Shimura differential operator, and
\breve{f}^{*}=\breve{f}\otimes\chi_{f}^{-1}
is the Atkin–Lehner involution of
\breve{f}
.

Let

be as before and suppose that

We fix modular forms

.

Suppose the weight

).

Remark 2.19

As explained in

In fact, given

Equivalently in a fancier language, one wants to restrict to a subset of the weight space over which the eigencurve giving rise to

3
The
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula

In this section, we will prove the
p

We examine one side of the equation,

, the special values of the

-adic

Then we recall some computations on the

3.1
Special values at balanced classical weights

We first specify at which classical points we want to study the values of
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
.

Suppose
\mathbf{f}
is a family of finite slope
a=a_{f}
, and
\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}
are of slopes
a_{g},a_{h}
respectively.
We are interested in the values of
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
at classical weights
(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\bal}
such that

i.

The specializations of
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}
at
(x,y,z)
are
p
-old,
i.e.
,
\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}
are
p
-stabilizations of some classical modular forms
f^{0}_{x},g^{0}_{y},h^{0}_{z}
on
X_{1}(N)
;

ii.

The triple
(x,y,z)
is balanced with
x=y+z-2t
for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
;

iii.

x>2a+1
,
y>2a_{g}+1
and
z>2a_{h}+1
(c.f. Remark
2.19
).

We denote the set consisting of all such weights by
\Sigma_{\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}}
.
To simplify the notations, we will write
f_{x}:=\mathbf{f}_{x}
,
g_{y}:=\mathbf{g}_{y}
,
h_{z}:=\mathbf{h}_{z}
and denote the classical forms
f^{0}_{x},g^{0}_{y},h^{0}_{z}
by
f,g,h
.
Notice that the new
f,g,h
are different from the modular forms (of unbalanced weights
k,\ell,m
) which the families
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}
deform respectively.

As we will proceed our proof by studying the
q
-expansions of various modular forms, we here recall several definitions.
On the Tate curve, one has the canonical basis
\{\omega_{\can},\eta_{\can}\}
of
\mathcal{H}
that satisfies

\nabla(\omega_{\can})=\eta_{\can}\otimes\frac{dq}{q},\quad\nabla(\eta_{\can})=0.

As mentioned in Remark
2.4
, the element
V_{r,i}
of
\mathbb{W}_{r}(q)
corresponds to the element
\omega_{\can}^{r-i}\eta_{\can}^{i}
when
r
is a positive integer and
i\leq r
.
From now on, we will make no difference between these two notations and use them interchangeably.

We now examine the
q
-expansion of
H^{\dagger}(\nabla_{k_{g}}^{\nu}(\mathbf{g}^{[p]})\times\mathbf{h})
at
(y,z)
.
Let

g_{y}^{[p]}(q)V_{y,0}\textrm{ and }h_{z}(q)V_{z,0}

be the
q
-expansions of the specializations of
\mathbf{g}^{[p]}
and
\mathbf{h}
at
y
and
z
respectively.

Since we assume the triple
(x,y,z)
to be balanced,
b:=y-t
is a positive integer.
Apply the formula in Theorem
2.8
,
we see that

\displaystyle\nabla^{-t}(g_{y}^{[p]}V_{y,0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}^{\infty}\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(y-t-1-i)\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)V_{y-2t,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(y-t-1-i)\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)V_{y-2t,j}

is in fact a finite sum.
We simplify the above formula as

\nabla^{-t}(g_{y}^{[p]}V_{y,0})=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}C_{j}\cdot\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)V_{y-2t,j}

by letting
C_{j}:=\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(y-t-1-i)
.

The
q
-expansion of the product
\nabla^{-t}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)\times h_{z}(q)
can now be expressed as

\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}C_{j}\cdot\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)V_{y-2t,j}\times h_{z}(q)V_{z,0}=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}C_{j}\cdot\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)\times h_{z}(q)V_{x,j}.

We here remark that
b-1\leq x
.
Since if
b-1=y-t-1>x
, by using the balancedness assumption
x+z-y>0
, we have
z>t+1
.
But this would imply
x=y-t+z-t>y-t+1>y-t-1
, which is a contradiction.
As a result, one has the identification

\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}C_{j}\cdot\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)\times h_{z}(q)V_{x,j}=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}C_{j}\cdot\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)\times h_{z}(q)\omega_{\can}^{x-j}\eta_{\can}^{j}.

Applying the overconvergent projection formula in Prop.
2.14
with

\gamma_{j}(q)=C_{j}\cdot\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)\times h_{z}(q),

we get

\displaystyle H^{\dagger}(\nabla^{-t}(g_{y}^{[p]}V_{y,0})\times h_{z}V_{z,0})(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}(-1)^{j}C_{j}\cdot\frac{\theta^{j}(\theta^{-t-j}g_{y}^{[p]}(q)\times h_{z}(q))}{(x-2-j+1)(x-2-j+2)\cdots(x-2)}V_{x,0}.

Kuga–Sato varieties and the generalized diagonal cycles.

For any

, we have the Kuga–Sato variety

, which is the desingularization (c.f.
[

Then one may see the parabolic cohomolgy

as a subspace in a correct degree of the de Rham cohomology of

.
This is illustrated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
BDP13
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Assume that
r\geq 1
. Then there is a idempotent
\epsilon_{r}\in\mathbb{Q}[\Aut(W_{r}/X)]
, defined in
[
DR14
, §3.1]
, such that we have

\epsilon_{r}H^{j}_{\dR}(W_{r}/K)=\begin{cases}0&j\neq r+1\\
H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r})&j=r+1\end{cases}.

Furthermore, the Hodge filtration on
\epsilon_{r}H^{r+1}_{\dR}(W_{r}/K)
is given by

\displaystyle\Fil^{0}=H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r}),

\displaystyle\Fil^{1}=\Fil^{2}=\cdots=\Fil^{r+1}=H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}),

\displaystyle\Fil^{r+2}=0.

Remark 3.2
.

We here recall that the parabolic cohomology
H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r})
is defined to be the hypercohomology of the complex

0\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{r}\rightarrow\left(\mathcal{H}^{r}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}+\nabla(\mathcal{H}^{r})\right)\rightarrow 0.

As we are mainly interested in cusp forms, the parabolic cohomology has the advantage that it comes with the exact sequence (c.f.
[
Sch85
, § 2]
)

0\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})\rightarrow H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r})\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\underline{\omega}^{-r})\rightarrow 0.

Suppose now we have a triple of balanced weights
(x,y,z)=(r_{1}+2,r_{2}+2,r_{3}+2)
.
We further assume that
r_{1}>0,r_{2}>0,r_{3}>0
and
r:=\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Set
W=W_{r_{1}}\times W_{r_{2}}\times W_{r_{3}}
, which is of dimension
2r+3
.
We now briefly recall the definition of the generalized diagonal cycle
\Delta_{x,y,z}\in\mathrm{CH}^{r+2}(W)
(c.f.
[
DR14
, Definition 3.3]

Choose three subsets

A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r_{1}}\},\quad B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r_{2}}\},\quad C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{r_{3}}\}

of
\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that
A\cap B\cap C=\emptyset
and
A\cup B=B\cup C=A\cup C=\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
One can see that the balancedness assumption guarantees the existence of such sets.
Then we consider the map

\displaystyle\varphi_{ABC}:W_{r}

\displaystyle\rightarrow W:=W_{r_{1}}\times W_{r_{2}}\times W_{r_{3}}

\displaystyle(x;P_{1},\ldots P_{r})

\displaystyle\mapsto((x;P_{a_{1}},\ldots,P_{a_{r_{1}}}),(x;P_{b_{1}},\ldots P_{b_{r_{2}}}),(x;P_{c_{1}},\ldots P_{c_{r_{3}}})),

which is a closed embedding of
W_{r}
into
W
.

Definition 3.3
.

The generalized diagonal cycle is defined by

\Delta_{x,y,z}=(\epsilon_{r_{1}},\epsilon_{r_{2}},\epsilon_{r_{3}})\varphi_{ABC}(W_{r})\in\mathrm{CH}^{r+2}(W).

Remark 3.4
.

Here we assume that
x,y,z>2
for simplicity.
The definition of
\Delta_{x,y,z}
when some of the weights are equal to
2
can be found in
[

By using the Künneth decomposition on

and examining the image of the idempotents

is homologous to zero.
That is,

\Delta_{x,y,z}\in\mathrm{CH}^{r+2}(W)_{0}:=\ker(\operatorname{cl}:\mathrm{CH}^{r+2}(W)\rightarrow H^{2r+4}_{\dR}(W)).

In particular, one may consider the image of
\Delta_{x,y,z}

under the
p
-adic Abel–Jacobi map (c.f.
[

]

\AJ_{p}:\mathrm{CH}^{r+2}(W)_{0}\rightarrow\Fil^{r+2}H^{2r+3}_{\dR}(W)^{\vee}.

Explicit computations of the Abel–Jacobi maps.

We now study the value of
\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{x,y,z})
at a certain element
\eta\otimes\omega_{2}\otimes\omega_{3}

H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})\otimes H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r_{2}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})\otimes H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r_{3}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}).

First of all, by the balancedness assumption, we see that

H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})\otimes H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r_{2}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})\otimes H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r_{3}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})\subset\Fil^{r+2}H^{2r+3}_{\dR}(W).

We now specify the forms
\eta,\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
we will encounter later.
Let
f,g,h
be a triple of cusp forms on
X_{1}(N)
of balanced weights
(x=r_{1}+2,\ y=r_{2}+2,\ z=r_{3}+2)
such that
x=y+z-2t
as before.
The cusp form
f
then corresponds to a section
\omega_{f}\in H^{0}(X,\underline{\omega}^{r_{1}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})
and similar for
g
and

.
We also reserve the notation
\mathcal{U}

X

We write the Hecke polynomial of

T^{2}-a_{p}(f)T+\chi_{f}(p)p^{x-1}=(T-\alpha_{f})(T-\beta_{f})

with

, and similarly for

.
We also write

\displaystyle P_{g}(T)

\displaystyle=1-a_{p}(g)p^{1-y}T+\chi_{g}(p)p^{1-y}T^{2}=(1-\alpha_{g}p^{1-y}T)(1-\beta_{g}p^{1-y}T),

\displaystyle P_{h}(T)

\displaystyle=1-a_{p}(h)p^{1-z}T+\chi_{h}(p)p^{1-z}T^{2}=(1-\alpha_{h}p^{1-z}T)(1-\beta_{h}p^{1-z}T).

The polynomial
P_{g}(T)
is defined such that

In fact,

Similarly as in
[

We want to find a suitable lift of

in

First recall that Poincaré pairing on

H^{1}_{\para}(X_{K},\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})[f]/\Fil^{r_{1}+1}\times S_{k}(N;K)[{f}^{*}]\rightarrow K.

Hence, one can viewed
\overline{\eta_{f}}

.
We would also like to remark that on the

\phi

Notice that

Suppose that

H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})[f]=\Fil^{r_{1}+1}H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})[f]\oplus H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})[f]^{\phi=\alpha_{f^{*}}}.

\alpha_{f^{*}}

We now turn our attention to the modular forms

and

As

Remark 3.5
.

We here explain the projection
\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}

Set

We may embed

\mathcal{H}^{r_{3}}

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Delta_{x,y,z}
be the general diagonal cycle defined as above, and view
\eta_{f}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h}
as elements in the de Rham cohomology
\Fil^{r+2}H^{2r+3}_{\dR}(W)
.
Then we have

\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{x,y,z})(\eta_{f}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h})=\frac{\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)}{\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)}\cdot\langle\eta_{f},e^{\leq a}\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times\omega_{h})\rangle

where
\langle\phantom{e},\phantom{e}\rangle
is the Poincaré pairing between
H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})
and
H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}}(1-t))
and
e^{\leq a}
is the projection to the slope
\leq a
part with respect to the operator
U
.
Note that on the right hand side, we identify
e^{\leq a}\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times\omega_{h})
with its class in
H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}}(1-t))
.

The terms
\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)
and
\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)
are Euler factors defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)

\displaystyle=(1-\beta^{2}\alpha_{g}^{\prime}\beta_{g}^{\prime}\alpha_{h}^{\prime}\beta_{h}^{\prime})=(1-\beta_{f}^{2}\chi_{f}^{-1}(p)p^{-x}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)

\displaystyle=(1-\beta\alpha_{g}^{\prime}\alpha_{h}^{\prime})(1-\beta\alpha_{g}^{\prime}\beta_{h}^{\prime})(1-\beta\beta_{g}^{\prime}\alpha_{h}^{\prime})(1-\beta\beta_{g}^{\prime}\beta_{h}^{\prime})

where
\beta:=p^{x-1+t-1}/\alpha_{f^{*}}
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is essentially identical to the proof in
[
DR14
, § 3.4]
.
The main difference is that instead of the ordinary projection
e_{\ord}
, we use the finite slope projection
e^{\leq a}
.

Notice that the projection
e^{\leq a}
can be written as a power series in
U
without constant term (c.f. Remark
5.12
).
Consequently, one has the following analogous result of
[
DR14
, Lemma 2.17]
:

e^{\leq a}(G^{[p]}\cdot V\omega_{h})=0.

In addition, one needs the following lemma (c.f.
[
DR14
, Proposition 2.11]
), which will be proved in the Appendix.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
\eta\in H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r})
is an element for which the Frobenius
\phi
acts with eigenvalue
\alpha
such that
v_{p}(\alpha)\leq a
.
Then for any
\omega\in H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r})
, we have

\langle\eta,\omega\rangle=\langle\eta,e^{\leq a}\omega\rangle

where the pairing is the Poincaré pairing as in Theorem
3.6
.

With these two facts, one then follow the exact same argument in
[
DR14
, § 3.4]
and conclude the proof.
∎

, it is showed that the proof of the above theorem in the case
(x,y,z)=(2,2,2)
can be greatly simplified by using the explicit cup product formula for finite polynomial cohomology on
X
.
It is expected that the general case can also be proved in a similar manner if one has a suitable theory with coefficients.
Currently, there is a joint work of me and Ju-Feng Wu
[
HW22
]
aiming to develop a theory of finite polynomial cohomology and
p
-adic Abel–Jacobi maps with coefficients.
Alternatively, it is also proved in my Ph.D. thesis
[
Hua24
]
.

3.3
Proof of the
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula

After studying the two ingredients, we are now able to prove the
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula.
Before doing so, we first recall our notations.

Let
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}
be three finite slope families and
(x,y,x)\in\Sigma_{\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}}
be a triple of balanced weights as in Section
3.1
.
Their specializations
f_{x},g_{y},h_{z}
are classical modular forms on
X_{1}(N,p)
and will be viewed as sections over the strict neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
in
X
.
The forms
f_{x},g_{y},h_{z}
are the
p
-stabilizations of modular forms
f,g,h
on
X_{1}(N)
and
f_{x}=(1-\beta_{f}V)f
by our assumption.
We also have the elements
\eta_{f},\omega_{g},\omega_{h}
associated with
f,g,h
in their respective cohomology groups as defined in Section
3.2
.

Theorem 3.9
.

(
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula)
Let notations be as above.
We have

\mathscr{L}_{p}^{f}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})(x,y,z)=(-1)^{t-1}\frac{\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)}{(t-1)!\mathscr{E}_{0}(f)\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)}\times\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{x,y,z})(\eta_{f}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h}),

where the Euler factors
\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)
and
\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)
are as Theorem
3.6
, and

\mathscr{E}_{0}(f)=(1-\beta_{f}^{2}\chi_{f}^{-1}(p)p^{1-x}).

The strategy of the proof is the following:
from the two overconvergent forms
g_{y},h_{z}
, there are two ways to construct an overconvergent form of weight
x
(or an element in
H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})
).

One is
H^{\dagger}(\nabla^{-t}(g_{y}^{[p]}V_{y,0})\times h_{z}V_{z,0})
, which appears in the triple product
L
-function.
The other is
H^{\dagger}(\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times\omega_{h}))
, which is related to the
p
-adic Abel-Jacobi image.
Hence, proving the
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula is essentially equal to relating the two overconvergent forms (or cohomological classes) mentioned above.

The crucial observation is the following lemma, which should be considered the main novelty of this paper.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let notations be as before, then we have

(-1)^{t-1}(t-1)!\cdot H^{\dagger}(\nabla^{-t}(g_{y}^{[p]}V_{y,0})\times h_{z}V_{z,0})=H^{\dagger}(\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times h_{z}))V_{x,0}.

Proof.

We prove this equality by examining their
q
-expansions.

First, we observe that the two forms
g
and
g_{y}=(1-\beta_{g}V)g
has the same
p
-depletion.
So we may replace
g_{y}^{[p]}
with
g^{[p]}
.

Let
b:=y-t
be as before, recall that

H^{\dagger}(\nabla^{-t}(g^{[p]}V_{y,0})\times hV_{z,0})=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(y-t-1-i)\frac{\theta^{j}(\theta^{-t-j}g^{[p]}\times h)}{(x-2-j+1)\cdots(x-2)}V_{x,0}.

(3)

On the other hand, recall that we have

G^{[p]}=\sum_{s=0}^{r_{2}}(-1)^{s}s!\binom{r_{2}}{s}\theta^{-s-1}g^{[p]}(q)\omega_{\can}^{r_{2}-s}\eta_{\can}^{s}

as the primitive of the overconvergent form
g^{[p]}
with respect to the Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla
.
We also view
h_{z}
as a section of
\mathcal{H}^{r_{3}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}
over
\mathcal{U}

and write its
q
-expansion as
h_{z}(q)\omega_{\can}^{r_{3}}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}
.

Following the recipe in Remark
3.5
, we have the polynomial
q
-expansion

\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times h_{z})=\sum_{s=t-1}^{r_{2}}(-1)^{s}s!\binom{r_{2}-(t-1)}{s-(t-1)}\theta^{-s-1}g^{[p]}\times h_{z}\ \omega_{\can}^{r_{1}-(s-(t-1))}\eta_{\can}^{s-(t-1)}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}.

After a change of variable
\alpha:=s-(t-1)
and noticing that
r_{2}-(t-1)=b-1
, it can be written as

\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times h_{z})=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{b-1}(-1)^{\alpha+(t-1)}(\alpha+t-1)!\binom{b-1}{\alpha}\theta^{-t-\alpha}g^{[p]}\times h_{z}\ \omega_{\can}^{r_{1}-\alpha}\eta_{\can}^{\alpha}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}.

The overconvergent projection
H^{\dagger}(\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times h_{z}))
then takes the form

\sum_{\alpha=0}^{b-1}(-1)^{2\alpha+(t-1)}(\alpha+t-1)!\binom{b-1}{\alpha}\frac{\theta^{\alpha}(\theta^{-t-\alpha}g^{[p]}\times h_{z})}{(k-2-\alpha+1)\cdots(k-2)}\ \omega_{\can}^{r_{1}}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}.

(4)

The lemma is then reduced to comparing the numbers

(-1)^{j}\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(b-1-i)

and

(-1)^{t-1}(j+t-1)!\binom{b-1}{j}.

Observe that

\displaystyle(-1)^{j}\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(b-1-i)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}\frac{(-t)(-t-1)\cdots(-t-j+1)}{j!}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(b-1-i)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{2j}\cdot t(t+1)\cdots(t+j-1)\cdot\frac{1}{j!}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(b-1-i)

\displaystyle=t(t+1)\cdots(t+j-1)\frac{1}{j!}\cdot j!\binom{b-1}{j}

\displaystyle=t(t+1)\cdots(t+j-1)\cdot\binom{b-1}{j}.

Hence we have

(-1)^{t-1}(t-1)!\cdot\left((-1)^{j}\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(b-1-i)\right)=(-1)^{t-1}(j+t-1)!\binom{b-1}{j}

for all
0\leq j\leq b-1
.
The lemma now follows.
In fact, one sees that the equality holds even without applying the overconvergent projection.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

In the ordinary case (c.f.
[
DR14
, Prop 2.9]
), the proof is easier as one may replace the overconvergent projection with the unit root splitting.
In other words, one only needs to prove the equality between the first terms of the two polynomial
q
-expansions.

Corollary 3.12
.

With notations as before, we have

\displaystyle\AJ(\Delta_{x,y,z})(\eta_{f}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)}{\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)}\cdot\langle\eta_{f},e^{\leq a}(\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times h)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)}{\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)}\cdot\langle\eta_{f},e^{\leq a}(\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times h_{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{t-1}(t-1)!\frac{\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)}{\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)}\cdot\langle\eta_{f},e^{\leq a}H^{\dagger}(\nabla^{-t}(g^{[p]})\times h_{z})\rangle.

Proof.

It only suffices to prove the second equation.
As
U(G^{[p]}\cdot Vh)=0
, and
e^{\leq a}
has no constant term as a power series in
U
,

e^{\leq a}
annihilates
\operatorname{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times Vh)
.
Hence we may replace
h
by one of its
p
-stabilization
h_{z}
.
The second equality then follows.
∎

Now back to the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}^{f}_{p}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
.
By definition, its value at the point
(x,y,z)
is

\frac{(f_{x}^{*},H^{\dagger,\leq a}(\nabla^{-t}(g^{[p]}_{y})\times h_{z}))_{N,p}}{(f_{x}^{*},f_{x}^{*})_{N,p}}

where the Petersson product is on
X_{1}(N,p)
.

We need to translate the Petersson product on
X_{1}(N,p)
back to
X_{1}(N)
.
Recall that
f_{x}
is the
p
-stabilization of
f
.
In other words, as classes in
H^{1}_{\dR}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})
, we have
f_{x}=(1-\beta_{f}^{\prime}\phi)f=(1-\beta_{f}V)f
and
f^{*}_{x}=(1-\beta_{f^{*}}V)f^{*}
.

We now use the formula in
[
DR14
, Lemma 2.19]
.
Let
\omega
be any modular form on
X_{1}(N,p)
of weight
x
, we have

\frac{(f_{x}^{*},\omega)_{N,p}}{(f_{x}^{*},f_{x}^{*})_{N,p}}=\frac{(f_{x}^{*},e_{f_{x}^{*}}\omega)_{N,p}}{(f_{x}^{*},f_{x}^{*})_{N,p}}=\frac{(f^{*},\omega^{0})_{N}}{(f^{*},f^{*})_{N}}=\langle\eta_{f},\omega^{0}\rangle_{\dR}.

Here
e_{f_{x}^{*}}
is the projection to the
f_{x}^{*}
-isotypic component, and
\omega^{0}
is a modular form on
X_{1}(N)
such that

e_{f*}\omega=(1-\beta_{f^{*}}^{\prime}\phi)\omega^{0}=(1-\beta_{f^{*}}V)\omega^{0}.

Now we take
\omega
to be the modular form
H^{\dagger,\leq a}(\nabla^{-t}(g^{[p]}_{y})\times h_{z})
, which we also view as a class in
H^{1}_{\para}(X,\mathcal{H}^{r_{1}})
.
Then,

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{f},\omega\rangle=\langle\eta_{f},e_{f^{*}}\omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta_{f},(1-\beta_{f^{*}}V)\omega^{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta_{f},\omega^{0}\rangle-\beta_{f^{*}}\langle\eta_{f},V\omega^{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta_{f},\omega^{0}\rangle-\beta_{f^{*}}\langle\phi^{-1}\eta_{f},\omega^{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{\beta_{f^{*}}}{\alpha_{f^{*}}})\langle\eta_{f},\omega^{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathscr{E}_{0}(f)\langle\eta_{f},\omega^{0}\rangle

where
\mathscr{E}_{0}(f):=(1-\frac{\beta_{f^{*}}}{\alpha_{f^{*}}})=(1-\frac{\beta_{f}}{\alpha_{f}})=(1-\beta_{f}^{2}\chi_{f}^{-1}(p)p^{1-x})
.
As a result,

\mathscr{L}_{p}^{f}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{\mathscr{E}_{0}(f)}\langle\eta_{f},H^{\dagger,\leq a}(\nabla^{-t}(g^{[p]}_{y})\times h_{z})\rangle_{\dR}.

This equality, together with Corollary
3.12
, then imply

\mathscr{L}_{p}^{f}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})(x,y,z)=(-1)^{t-1}\frac{\mathscr{E}(f,g,h)}{(t-1)!\mathscr{E}_{0}(f)\mathscr{E}_{1}(f)}\times\AJ(\Delta_{x,y,z})(\eta_{f}\otimes\omega_{g}\otimes\omega_{h}).

4

In this section, we will show an application of the

4.1
Coleman families and big Galois representations

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the weight space defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
as before.
In this section, we will focus on families defined over wide open disks in
\mathcal{W}
as in
[
LZ16

.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{V}
is a open disk in
\mathcal{W}
defined over a finite extension
L
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
We denote by
\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
(resp.
\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}
) the ring of bounded (resp. bounded by
1
) rigid analytic functions on
\mathcal{V}
.
In particular, if
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
is a center of
\mathcal{V}
,

\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{L}\llbracket\bm{k}-k_{0}\rrbracket
for some formal variable
\bm{k}
and
\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{V}}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}[\frac{1}{p}]
.
We will always endow
\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}
with the topology induced by its maximal ideal
m_{\mathcal{V}}
.
We let
\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}^{\times}
be the universal character (shifted by
-2
) on
\mathcal{V}
.
In other words,
\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}(t)=\omega(t)^{k_{0}-2}\langle t\rangle^{\mathbf{k}-2}
for
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, where
\omega
is the Teichmüller character and
\langle t\rangle:=t/\omega(t)\in 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{W}
be an open disk such that the classical weights
\mathcal{V}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is dense in
\mathcal{V}
.
A Coleman family
\mathbf{f}
(of level
N_{f}
and nebentypus character
\chi_{f}
) defined over
\mathcal{V}
of slope
a\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
is a power series

\mathbf{f}=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(\mathbf{f})q^{n}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}\llbracket q\rrbracket

such that
a_{1}(\mathbf{f})=1
,
a_{p}(\mathbf{f})
is invertible in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
with norm
p^{-a}
, and for all but finitely many classical weights
k\in\mathcal{V}
, the specialization
\mathbf{f}_{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{L}\llbracket q\rrbracket
is the
q
-expansion of a classical normalized eigenform of weight
k
, level
\Gamma_{1}(N_{f},p)
and nebentypus
\chi_{f}
.

We will often write
\mathcal{V}_{f}=\mathcal{V}
and similarly
\mathscr{O}_{f}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{V}_{f}}
,
\Lambda_{f}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{f}}
,
\kappa_{f}=\kappa_{\mathcal{V}_{f}}
to specify their dependence to the family
\mathbf{f}
.

One now wants to construct a
p
-adic family of Galois representations attached to a finite slope family.
It turns out that this can be achieved by considering certain big étale sheaf on modular curves (c.f.
[
AIS15
]
and
[
AS08
]
).
We shall briefly recall several notations and results without any proofs, mainly following the setups in
[
BSV22
, § 4]
.

As we will consider both families and their specializations to classical weights,
we use the terminology ‘big’ to refer to anything defined for families and ‘small’ for the specialized case.

Locally analytic functions and distributions.

Definition 4.3
.

For any integer
m\geq 0
, we write
LA_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}})
for the space of functions
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}
that are analytic on every open disk
z+p^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

We define
T:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
T^{\prime}=p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

One has right actions of
M_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
on
T
and
T^{\prime}
.
An easy calculation shows that
T
is preserved by

\Sigma_{0}(p)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
p\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\end{pmatrix}

and

is preserved by

\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}(p)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
p\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, they are both preserved by scalar multiplication by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and the subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(p\mathbb{Z}_{p}):=\Sigma_{0}(p)\cap\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}(p)
.

Definition 4.4
.

For any
m\geq 0
, we let
A_{\mathcal{V},m}(T)
be the space of functions

f:T\rightarrow\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}

such that

f(\gamma\cdot t)=\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}(\gamma)f(t)

for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and

.
This space is a
\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}
-module and we endow it with the topology induced by
m_{\mathcal{V}}
.
Similarly, one defines
A_{\mathcal{V},m}(T^{\prime})
to be the space of functions
f:T^{\prime}\rightarrow\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}
with
f(\gamma\cdot t)=\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}(\gamma)f(t)
for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and
f(pz,1)\in LA_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}})
.

Definition 4.5
.

We define

D_{\mathcal{V},m}(T):=\Hom_{\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}}(A_{\mathcal{V},m}(T),\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}})

and
D_{\mathcal{V},m}(T^{\prime})
in a similar manner.
We endow
D_{\mathcal{V},m}(T)
with the weak-
\ast
topology, which is the weakest topology such that the evaluation-at-
f
maps are continuous for all
f\in A_{\mathcal{V},m}(T)
.

Remark 4.6
.

As we will frequently give statements that work both for
T
and T
′
,
we will use the convention

as in

Remark 4.7

Lemma 4.8

-power order at

Following

\displaystyle=H^{1}_{\operatorname{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}(Y_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}},D_{\mathcal{V},m}(T^{\cdot})^{\operatorname{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}})(\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\operatorname{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}(Y_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}},A_{\mathcal{V},m}(T^{\cdot})(\chi)^{\operatorname{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}})

\displaystyle=H^{1}_{\operatorname{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}(Y_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}},A_{\mathcal{V},m}(T^{\cdot})^{\operatorname{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}})(\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime})

4.2
On

This subsection aims to recall several facts about

A brief reference on

For the definitions of various Selmer groups for

Since we are interested in the case of modular forms, we will also recall several useful results in

Let
\mathscr{R}
be the Robba ring of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

It is the ring consists of formal Laurent series over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in one variable

\varphi
and an action of

on

, defined by

\displaystyle\tau(\pi)

\displaystyle=(1+\pi)^{\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\tau)}-1,\quad\tau\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle\varphi(\pi)

\displaystyle=(1+\pi)^{p}-1,

where

is the cyclotomic character.
There is also a left inverse
\psi
of

[
Ber03
, § I.2]

Definition 4.10
.

By
p
-adic representation of

-vector space with continuous linear action of

-module

over
\mathscr{R}

\mathscr{R}
-module of finite rank with
\mathscr{R}
-semilinear actions of
\varphi
and
\Gamma
.

Given a reduced affinoid algebra
A
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, let
\mathscr{R}_{A}:=\mathscr{R}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}A
.
By an
A
-representation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, we means a finitely generated locally free
A
-module
M
with a continuous
A
-linear action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.
One can also define
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules over
\mathscr{R}_{A}

As is well known, there is a functor
\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\operatorname{rig}}
from the category of
p
-adic representations of

(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules over
\mathscr{R}
.
A similar functor also exists from

-representations to

-modules over
\mathscr{R}_{A}

[

]

Proposition 4.11

(
[

.

Let notations be as before, then we have the following results.

4.3
The Balanced Selmer group

Let
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}
be three Coleman families with tame characters satisfying the relation
\chi_{f}\cdot\chi_{g}\cdot\chi_{h}=1
,
and let
a_{f},a_{g},a_{h}
be their respective slopes.
Suppose
\mathcal{V}_{f},\mathcal{V}_{g},\mathcal{V}_{h}
are defined over some finite extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
We fix centers
k_{0},\ell_{0},m_{0}
and uniformizers
\mathbf{k}-k_{0},\bm{\ell}-\ell_{0},\mathbf{m}-m_{0}
for
\mathcal{V}_{f},\mathcal{V}_{g},\mathcal{V}_{h}
respectively.
We set
\mathscr{O}_{fgh}:=\mathscr{O}_{f}\hat{\otimes}_{L}\mathscr{O}_{g}\hat{\otimes}_{L}\mathscr{O}_{h}
and
\mathscr{R}_{fgh}:=\mathscr{R}_{\mathscr{O}_{fgh}}
.

Consider the character
\Xi:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{fgh}^{\times}
defined by

\displaystyle\Xi(g):

\displaystyle=(\omega^{(4-k_{0}-l_{0}-m_{0})/2}\cdot\langle\phantom{e}\rangle^{(4-\mathbf{k}-\bm{\ell}-\mathbf{m})/2})\circ\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(-\kappa_{f}-\kappa_{g}-\kappa_{h}-2)\circ\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g).

We set

V(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}):=V(\mathbf{f})\hat{\otimes}_{L}V(\mathbf{g})\hat{\otimes}_{L}V(\mathbf{h})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{fgh}}\Xi,

which is Kummer self-dual, and set
M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}):=\operatorname{\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\operatorname{rig}}}(V(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}))
.
One defines a filtration on
M(\mathbf{f})
by
F^{0}M(\mathbf{f})=M(\mathbf{f})
,
F^{1}M(\mathbf{f})=F^{+}M(\mathbf{f})
,
F^{2}M(\mathbf{f})=0
, and similarly for
M(\mathbf{g}),M(\mathbf{h})
.
These filtrations induce a filtration on
M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
, defined by

F^{n}M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}):=\left[\sum_{p+q+r=n}F^{p}M(\mathbf{f})\hat{\otimes}_{L}F^{q}M(\mathbf{g})\hat{\otimes}_{L}F^{r}M(\mathbf{h})\right]\{\Xi\}

where by
\{\Xi\}
we mean the image of
\Xi
under the functor
\operatorname{\mathbf{D}^{\dagger}_{\operatorname{rig}}}
(c.f.
[
Ben11
, § 1.5]
).

Definition 4.19
.

Analogous to
[
BSV22
, § 7.2]
, we define the balanced Selmer group to be

H^{1}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})):=H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},F^{2}M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}))\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})).

(7)

Remark 4.20
.

We remark that by examining the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module structure of
\textrm{gr}^{0}M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
, the natural map
F^{2}M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})\hookrightarrow M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})
induces an injection on
H^{1}
(c.f.
[
BSV22
, (148)]
).

One important property of this balanced Selmer group is the following lemma.

Lemma 4.21

(c.f.
[
BSV22
, Lamma 7.2]
)
.

If
(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{bal}}
is a balanced triple of classical weights, then

H^{1}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))=H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))=H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))

(8)

where the last one is the Bloch–Kato finite Selmer group.
As a consequence, one has the Bloch–Kato exponential map

\exp_{p}:D_{\dR}(V(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))/\Fil^{0}\cong H^{1}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))

and we denote its inverse by
\log_{p}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially identical to that of
[
BSV22
, Lamma 7.2]
, which boils down to compute the Hodge–Tate weights, or the actions of
\Gamma
in the case of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules.
In particular, one has

\Fil^{0}\mathcal{D}_{\dR}(F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))=0\textrm{ and }\Fil^{0}\mathcal{D}_{\dR}(M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})/F^{2})=\mathcal{D}_{\dR}(M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})).

The second identity implies that
H^{1}_{e}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})/F^{2})=0
.
Notice that
F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})
is Kummer dual to
M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})/F^{2}
.
So one has

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))=H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})).

By
[
BSV22
, Lemma 3.5]
,
H^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))=H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))
.
One then deduces that
H^{1}_{\bal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))\subset H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))
.
For the other direction, it follows similarly by using the fact that
\Fil^{0}\mathcal{D}_{\dR}(F^{2}M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))=0
.
∎

If we set

M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})_{f}:=F^{-}M(\mathbf{f})\hat{\otimes}F^{+}M(\mathbf{g})\hat{\otimes}F^{+}M(\mathbf{h})\{\Xi\}

and similarly for
M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})_{g}
and
M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})_{h}
, then
the graded piece
F^{2}/F^{3}
decomposes as

F^{2}/F^{3}=M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})_{f}\oplus M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})_{g}\oplus M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})_{h}.

(9)

As a result, there are natural projections

F^{2}M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})\rightarrow M(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h})_{\xi}

for
\xi\in\{f,g,h\}
.

4.4
The three-variable big logarithm

Write
V_{\dR}(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}):=D_{\dR}(V(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))
and similarly for its Kummer dual
V_{\dR}^{*}(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})

If
w=(x,y,z)\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{bal}}
, one can construct an element
\eta^{a}_{\mathbf{f}_{x}}\otimes\omega_{\mathbf{g}_{y}}\otimes\omega_{\mathbf{h}_{z}}\in\Fil^{0}V_{\dR}^{*}(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})
similarly to the construction in §
3.2
(c.f.
[
BSV22
, § 3.1]

The Bloch–Kato logarithm

\log_{p}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))\xrightarrow{\sim}D_{\dR}(V(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))\cong[\Fil^{0}V_{\dR}^{*}(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z})]^{\vee}

then enables us to define
\log_{p,f}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},M(\mathbf{f}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y},\mathbf{h}_{z}))\rightarrow L
as the composition of
\log_{p}
and evaluation at
\eta^{a}_{\mathbf{f}_{x}}\otimes\omega_{\mathbf{g}_{y}}\otimes\omega_{\mathbf{h}_{z}}

One important result of compact operators on
p
-adic Banach spaces is the following proposition.

Proposition 5.11
.

(Riesz theory)
If
\lambda\in K
is a zero of
P(T)=\det(1-Tu)
of order
h
.
Then the space
E
can be decomposed uniquely as a direct sum of
u
-invariant closed subspaces

E=N(\lambda)\oplus F(\lambda)

such that

i.

(1-\lambda u)
is invertible on
F(\lambda)

ii.

(1-\lambda u)
is nilpotent on
N(\lambda)
.
More precisely,
(1-\lambda u)^{h}N(\lambda)=0
and
N(\lambda)
is of dimension
h
.

In order to understand the projection
E\twoheadrightarrow N(\lambda)
, we recall part of the proof from
[
Ser62
]
.

Proof.

By assumption, we have
\Delta^{s}P(\lambda)=0
for all
s<h
, and
\Delta^{h}P(\lambda)=c\neq 0
.
Consider the identity

(1-Tu)R(T,u)=P(T)

and apply
\Delta^{s}
, we get

(1-Tu)\Delta^{s}R(T,u)-u\Delta^{s-1}R(T,u)=\Delta^{s}P(T)

Now put
w_{s}=\Delta^{s}R(\lambda,u)
, we have the equations

\displaystyle(1-\lambda u)w_{0}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(1-\lambda u)w_{1}-uw_{0}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(1-\lambda u)w_{h-1}-uw_{h-2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(1-\lambda u)w_{h}-uw_{h-1}

\displaystyle=c

We deduce from these equations that
(1-\lambda u)^{s+1}w_{s}=0
for
s<h
.

Set
e=c^{-1}(1-\lambda u)w_{h}
and
f=-c^{-1}uw_{h-1}
.
Then
e+f=1
and
fe^{h}=0
since
(1-\lambda u)^{h}w_{h-1}=0
(
w_{s}
is power series in
u
, so it commutes with
u
).

Consider the equation
(e+f)^{h}=1
, and let

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=e^{h}

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=he^{h-1}f+\cdots+hef^{h-1}+f^{h}

Then
p+q=1
and
pq=qp=0
.
The maps
p
and
q
are the desired projections to
F(a)
and
N(a)
.
More precisely,
\ker p=\im q=N(\lambda)
and
\ker q=\im p=F(\lambda)
.
Hence we have the decomposition

E=N(\lambda)\oplus F(\lambda)

As
(1-\lambda u)^{h}q=0
, we see that
(1-\lambda u)^{h}=0
on
N(\lambda)
.
On the other hand,
(1-\lambda u)^{h}(w_{h})^{h}=c^{h}p
, so
1-\lambda u
is invertible on
F(\lambda)=\im p
.

The rest of the proof will be skipped.
A proof for
\dim N(\lambda)=h
can be found in
[
Buz07
]
.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

Examining the above proof carefully, we see that the projector
q:E\rightarrow N(\lambda)
is a power series without constant term in
u
.
This fact plays an important role when we compute the
p
-adic Abel–Jacobi maps.

We are now able to prove lemma
5.3

Proof.

By assumption, for any power series
G(T)
such that
G(u)
converges, we have

\langle m_{1},G(u)m_{2}\rangle=\langle G(\phi)m_{1},m_{2}\rangle

as long as the expression
G(\phi)m_{1}
also converges.
So it suffices to show that
G(\phi)\eta=\eta
when
G(T)
is a power series such that
G(u)
is the projector
e^{u=\alpha}
on
M
.

Suppose that
u
has
\alpha
as one of its eigenvalues, then
\alpha^{-1}
is a root of
P(T)=\det(1-Tu)
.
On the finite dimensional subspace
N(\alpha^{-1})
,
u
has characteristic polynomial
(T-\alpha)^{h}
.
We will assume that
(T-\alpha)^{h}
is also the minimal polynomial for simplicity.

By Proposition
5.8
, the resolvant
R(T,u)
is an entire function of
T
.
In particular,
R(\alpha^{-1},u)
converges in
\mathcal{L}(E,E)
.
We write
R(T)
for the power series
R(\alpha^{-1},T)
.
For any eigenvector
\omega
of
u
with eigenvalue
\alpha
, the element
R(\alpha^{-1},u)\omega=R(\alpha)\omega
converges.
Hence, the power series
R(T)
a least converges for
|T|\leq|\alpha|=p^{-a}
.

In other words, we may view
R(T)
as an element in the Tate algebra
K\langle T/p^{a}\rangle
, and
so are
w_{s}(T)=\Delta^{s}R(\alpha^{-1},T)
and the power series
G(T)
that satisfies

G(u)=q=he^{h-1}f+\cdots+hef^{h-1}+f^{h}
.

As the polynomial
(T-\alpha)^{h}
is regular, we can apply
Weierstrass division on the Tate algebra
K\langle T/p^{a}\rangle
and write

G(T)=(T-\alpha)^{h}S(T)+r(T)

where
S(T)\in K\langle T/p^{a}\rangle
and
r(T)
is a polynomial of degree
<h
.
Since
G(u)
is a projector to
N(\alpha^{-1})
, we must have
G(u)|_{N(\alpha^{-1})}=1
.
This implies
r(u)=1
on
N(\alpha^{-1})
.
Since we assume
(T-\alpha)^{h}
is the minimal polynomial on
N(\alpha^{-1})
, we must have
r(T)=1
.

Now, for every
m\in M
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\eta,e^{u=\alpha}m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta,G(u)m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle G(\phi)\eta,m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[(\phi-\alpha)^{h}S(\phi)+1]\eta,m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta,m\rangle.

Note that the expression
[(\phi-\alpha)^{h}S(\phi)+1]\eta
converges.

The lemma then follows as the projector
e^{\leq a}
is just the finite sum of all
e^{u=\gamma}
with
\ord_{p}(\gamma)\leq a
.
∎

A new parameterized entanglement monotone
Xue Yang 1 , Ming-Xing Luo 1 , Yan-Han Yang 1 , Shao-Ming Fei 2,3

Abstract
Entanglement concurrence has been widely used for featuring entanglement in quantum experiments. As an entanglement monotone it is related to specific quantum Tsallis entropy. Our goal in this paper is to propose a new parameterized bipartite entanglement monotone which is named as q q -concurrence inspired by general Tsallis entropy. We derive an analytical lower bound for the q q -concurrence of any bipartite quantum entanglement state by employing positive partial transposition criterion and realignment criterion, which shows an interesting relationship to the strong separability criteria. The new entanglement monotone is used to characterize bipartite isotropic states. Finally, we provide a computational method to estimate the q q -concurrence for any entanglement by superposing two bipartite pure states. It shows that the superposition operations can at most increase one ebit for the q q -concurrence in the case that the two states being superposed are bi-orthogonal or one-sided orthogonal. These results reveal a series of new phenomena about the entanglement, which may be interesting in quantum communication and quantum information processing.



I
Introduction

Quantum entanglement as one of the most remarkable phenomena of quantum mechanics, reveals the fundamental insights into the nature of quantum correlations. It is key of many interesting quantum tasks such as quantum teleportation
Bennett1
, quantum dense coding
Bennett2
, quantum secret sharing
Hillery
, and quantum cryptography
Gisin
. An fundamental problem is to justify whether a given quantum composite system state is entangled or separable. So far, there are two important entanglement criteria for the bipartite entanglement. One is positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion
Peres(1996)
which implies the partial transposition satisfying
\rho^{T_{A}}\geq 0
for any separable state
\rho_{AB}
. The PPT criterion is a necessary and sufficient condition of entanglement for pure states,
2\otimes 2
and
2\otimes 3
mixed states, but in
general not sufficient for higher dimensions
Peres(1996)
;
Horodecki(1996)
. The other is complementary operational criterion which is called the realignment criterion
Horodecki(1999)
;
Rudolph (2005)
;
Chen(2002)
. For a separable
\rho_{AB}
, the realignment operation
{\cal{R}}(\rho)
satisfies
\|{\cal{R}}(\rho)\|_{1}\leq 1
. Both entanglement criteria are widely used in quantum experiments and quantum applications
3H(2009)
.

Entanglement measure as another approach is also used to quantify entanglement
Vedral(1997)
;
Vedral(1997)1
. There are some interesting entanglement measures for bipartite entangled systems, such as the concurrence
Hill(1997)
;
Wootters(1998)
;
Rungta(2001)
, entanglement of formation
Bennett(1996)
;
Horodeck(2001)
, negativity
Zyczkowski(1998)
;
Vidal(2002)
, Tsallis-
q
entropy of entanglement
Kim(2010)T
, and Rényi-
\alpha
entropy of entanglement
Gour(2007)
;
Kim(2010)R
. Howbeit, the explicit computation of these measures for arbitrary states is a formidable task because of the extremization for mixed states. So far, analytical results are only available for special measures and two-qubit states or special higher-dimensional mixed states
Lee(2003)
;
Wootters(1998)
;
Rungta(2003)
;
Vollbrecht(2001)
;
Terhal(2000)
;
Buchholz(2016)
. Moreover, these entanglement measures are also related to the PPT criterion and the realignment criterion. Recently, some efforts have been made towards the analytical lower bounds of concurrence
Chen(2005)
;
L.G.Liu(2009)
;
M. Li(2018)
. Thereby, the development of analytical lower bounds for the various entanglement measures is of great interest.

In fact, as one reason of the difficult quantification of mixed states, any entanglement generated by superposing two pure states cannot be simply featured by two individual states being superposed. Entanglement of superpositions is firstly introduced by Linden et al.
Linden(2006)
who found an upper bound on the entanglement of formation for the superposition in terms of the entanglement of two individual states being superposed. Their bound is then improved by Gour
Gour(2007)1
. So far, the entanglement of superpositions has been addressed in terms of different entanglement measures
K.H. Ma(2010)
;
Akhtarshenas(2011)
;
Yu(2006)
;
Song(2007)
;
Xiang(2007)
;
Ou(2007)
;
Ma(2014)
. Although it is difficult to exactly estimate the entanglement measure of the superposed entanglement from individual states being superposed, however, it may be helpful for exploring the nature of quantum entanglement by investigating the superposed entanglement. Additionally, it provides direct results for the approximate quantification of mixed entanglement
Lohmayer(2006)
;
Osterloh(2008)
;
Eltschka(2008)
.

The concurrence for a bipartite pure state
|\psi\rangle_{AB}
is defined by
C(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=\sqrt{2(1-{\rm{Tr}}\rho^{2}_{A})}

Hill(1997)
. It plays a major role in entanglement distributions such as entanglement swapping and remote preparation of bipartite entangled states
Gour(2004)
. In fact, the concurrence for pure states is related to specific Tsallis entropy
Tsallis(1988)
;
Landsberg(1998)
as
C(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=\sqrt{2T_{2}(\rho_{A}})
for
q=2
. Noteworthily, Tsallis entropy provides a generalization of traditional Boltzmann-Gibbs statistical mechanics and enables us to find a consistent treatment of dynamics in many nonextensive physical systems such as long-range interactions, long-time memories, and multifractal structures
Tsallis(2001)1
. Tsallis entropy also provides many intriguing applications in the realms of quantum information theory
Abe(2001)
;
Tsallis(2001)
;
Rossignoli(2002)
;
Rajagopal (2005)
. Hence, a natural problem is how to construct an entanglement measure from general Tsallis entropy with
q\geq 2
. Our goal in this paper is to solve this problem.

The outline of the rest is as follows. In Sec.II, we propose a new bipartite entanglement monotone which is related to general Tsallis entropy for any
q\geq 2
. The so-called
q
-concurrence is actually an entanglement monotone. We prove an analytical lower bound for the
q
-concurrence by using the PPT and realignment criteria. Moreover, we valuate the
q
-concurrence for isotropic states. In Sec.III, we investigate the entanglement of the superposition of two pure states by using the
q
-concurrence in terms of two states being superposed. The entanglement of the superposed state can be expressed explicitly when two input states are bi-orthogonal or one-sided orthogonal. As a result, the superposing operation can only increase at least one ebit in terms of the
q
-concurrence in both cases. The last section concludes the paper.

II
A new entanglement monotone

Before giving our definition, we recall a well-known bipartite entanglement monotone. For any arbitrary bipartite pure state
|\psi\rangle_{AB}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, the concurrence
Hill(1997)
;
Rungta(2001)
is given by

\displaystyle C(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=\sqrt{2(1-{\rm{Tr}}\rho^{2}_{A})}

(1)

where
\rho_{A}={\rm{Tr}}_{B}(|\psi\rangle_{AB}\langle\psi|)
is the reduced density matrix of the subsystem
A
by tracing out the subsystem
B
.

The concurrence defined in Eq.(
1
) can be regarded as a function of specific Tsallis entropy of
q=2

Tsallis(1988)
;
Landsberg(1998)
, i.e.,
C(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=\sqrt{2T_{2}(\rho_{A}})
for
q=2
. In this section, we define another new parameterized entanglement monotone named the
q
-concurrence which is related to general Tsallis entropy for any
q\geq 2
.

II.1
The
q
-concurrence

Definition 1.
For an arbitrary bipartite pure state
|\psi\rangle_{AB}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, the
q
-concurrence is defined as

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=1-{\rm{Tr}}\rho^{q}_{A}

(2)

for any
q\geq 2
, where
\rho_{A}
is the reduced density operator of the subsystem
A
.

It is clear that
C_{q}(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=0
if and only if
|\psi\rangle_{AB}
is a separable state, i.e.,
|\psi\rangle_{AB}=|\psi\rangle_{A}\otimes|\psi\rangle_{B}
. The
q
-concurrence may be concerned with Schatten
q
-norm for the positive semidefinite matrices, where the Schatten
q
-norm
Bhatia(1997)
is defined as

\displaystyle\|A\|_{q}=({\rm Tr}A^{q})^{1/q}

(3)

It will be a useful tool to prove the subadditivity inequality in the following Lemma 1.

Suppose a pure state
|\psi\rangle_{AB}
defined on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
has the Schmidt decomposition

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum^{m}_{i=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|a_{i}\rangle_{A}|b_{i}\rangle_{B}

(4)

It is apparent that the reduced density matrices
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
have the same spectra of
\{\lambda_{i}\}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\psi\rangle_{AB})=1-{\rm{Tr}}\rho^{q}_{A}=1-{\rm{Tr}}\rho^{q}_{B}

(5)

This implies that

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\psi\rangle)=1-\sum^{m}_{i=1}{\lambda^{q}_{i}}

(6)

where
C_{q}(|\psi\rangle)
satisfies
0\leq C_{q}(|\psi\rangle)\leq 1-m^{1-q}
. The lower bound is obtained for product states while the upper bound is achieved for the maximally entangled pure states
\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\sum^{m}_{i=1}|ii\rangle
.

For a mixed state
\rho_{AB}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, we define its
q
-concurrence via the convex-roof extension as follows:

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho_{AB})=\inf_{\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle\}}\sum_{i}p_{i}C_{q}(|\psi_{i}\rangle_{AB})

(7)

where the infimum is taken over all the pure-state decompositions of
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
with
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
, and
p_{i}\geq 0
.

So far, several results have been made for the requirements that a reasonable measure of entanglement should fulfill
Popescu(1997)
;
Vedral(1997)1
;
Vidal(1999)
. Specially, it has been proposed in
Vidal(2000)
that the monotonicity under local operations and classical communication (LOCC) has to satisfy as the only requirement of any entanglement measure. This kind of entanglement measure is then defined as entanglement monotone. In fact, Vidal
Vidal(2000)
states that it is an entanglement monotone
E
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

E(\rho)\geq 0
for any state
\rho
, and
E(\rho)=0
if
\rho
is fully separable;

(ii)

For a pure state
|\Psi\rangle
, the measure is a function of
the reduced density operator
\rho_{A}={\rm{Tr}}_{B}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)
, i.e.,
E(|\Psi\rangle)=f(\rho_{A})
, where the function
f
has the following properties: (a)
f
is invariant under any unitary transformation
U
, i.e.
f(U\rho_{A}U^{\dagger})=f(\rho_{A})
. (b)
f
is concave, i.e.,
f(\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})\geq\lambda f(\rho_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(\rho_{2})
for
\lambda\in(0,1)
;

(iii)

For a mixed state
\rho
, the measure
E(\rho)
is defined as the convex-roof extension, i.e.,

\displaystyle E(\rho)=\inf_{\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle\}}\{\sum_{i}p_{i}C(|\psi_{i}\rangle)|\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|=\rho\}

(8)

where the minimum is taken over all possible pure-state decompositions of
\rho
.

These conditions (i)-(iii) formalize intuitive properties of an entanglement monotone. From this point of view any entanglement monotone could be regarded as a measure of entanglement. We present the following Lemma 1 for verifying that the
q
-concurrence defined in Eq.(
7
) is a proper entanglement monotone.

Lemma 1
. Define the function

\displaystyle F_{q}(\rho)=1-{\rm{Tr}}\rho^{q}

(9)

for any density matrix
\rho
and
q\geq 2
.
F_{q}(\rho)
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

Nonnegativity
:
F_{q}(\rho)\geq 0
for any density operator
\rho
, where the equality holds for pure states;

(ii)

Symmetry
:
F_{q}(\rho_{A})=F_{q}(\rho_{B})
for a pure state
\rho_{AB}
of the composite system
AB
;

(iii)

Subadditivity
: For a general bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
,
F_{q}(\rho_{AB})
satisfies the inequalities:

\displaystyle|F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B})|\leq F_{q}(\rho_{AB})\leq F_{q}(\rho_{A})+F_{q}(\rho_{B})

(10)

(iv)

Concavity and quasi-convexity
:
F_{q}
is concave, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}p_{i}F_{q}(\rho_{i})\leq F_{q}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})

(11)

where
\{p_{i}\}
is a probability distribution, and
\rho_{i}
s are density matrices. The equality holds iff
\rho_{i}
s are identical for all
p_{i}>0
. Moreover,
F_{q}
is quasi-convex, i.e.,

\displaystyle F_{q}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})\leq\sum_{i}p^{q}_{i}F_{q}(\rho_{i})+1-\sum_{i}p^{q}_{i}

(12)

where the equality holds iff
\rho_{i}
have supports on orthogonal subspaces, i.e.,
\rho_{i}=|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
, and
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}
are orthogonal.

The proof of the Lemma 1 is provided in Appendix A. Next we prove
C_{q}(\rho)
is a proper entanglement monotone.

Proposition 1
. The
q
-concurrence
C_{q}(\rho)
in Eq.(
7
) is an entanglement monotone.

Proof.
From the non-negativity in Lemma 1 and Eq.(
7
), it follows that
C_{q}(\rho)\geq 0
for any density matrix
\rho
, where the equality holds iff
\rho
is separable. Furthermore, from the concavity in Lemma 1, we know that
F_{q}(\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})\geq\lambda F_{q}(\rho_{1})+(1-\lambda)F_{q}(\rho_{2})
for any density matrices
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Thus,
C_{q}(|\phi\rangle_{AB})
is a concave function of
\rho_{A}
. Finally,
C_{q}(|\phi\rangle_{AB})
is invariant under local unitary transformations from the invariance of
{\rm{Tr}}\rho^{q}
. Then, the convex-roof extension of the
q
-concurrence
C_{q}(\rho)
for mixed states is a proper entanglement monotone
Vidal(2000)
.
\Box

Note that for a given bipartite entanglement
|\psi\rangle
, the entanglement monotone

in Eq.(
2
) are invariant under the local unitary operations. From Cayley-Hamilton Theorem, the reduced density matrix

II.2
A lower bound on the
q
-concurrence

In contrast to the simple case of pure entangled states in Eq.(
2
), the quantification of mixed states is still challenging due to the optimization procedures
3H(2009)
. Fortunately, we present an effective operational way to detect the
q
-concurrence for any bipartite quantum state, which manifests an essential quantitative relation among the
q
-concurrence, PPT criterion and realignment criterion. Before presenting the lower bound, we recall two separability criteria.

PPT criterion
Peres(1996)
;
Horodecki(1996)
. Given a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{ijkl}\rho_{ij,kl}|ij\rangle\langle kl|
. If
\rho_{AB}
is separable, then the partial transposition
\rho^{T_{A}}
with respect to the subsystem
A
has the nonnegative spectrum, i.e.,
\rho^{T_{A}}\geq 0
. The partial transpose
\rho^{T_{A}}
is given by
\rho^{T_{A}}=[\sum_{ijkl}\rho_{ij,kl}|ij\rangle\langle kl|]^{T_{A}}=\sum_{ijkl}\rho_{ij,kl}|kj\rangle\langle il|
, where the subscripts
i
and
j
are the row and column indices for the subsystem
A
, respectively, while
k
and
l
are such indices for the subsystem
B
.

Realignment criterion

Horodecki(1999)
;
Rudolph (2005)
;
Chen(2002)
. Let
{\cal{R}}
be the realignment operation on the joint system
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{ijkl}\rho_{ij,kl}|ij\rangle\langle kl|
. The output is given by
{\cal{R}}(\rho)=\sum_{ijkl}\rho_{ij,kl}|ik\rangle\langle jl|
. If
\rho_{AB}
is separable, then
\|{\cal{R}}(\rho)\|_{1}\leq 1
, where
\|X\|_{1}
denotes the trace norm defined by
\|X\|_{1}=\rm{Tr}\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}

Rudolph(2004)
.

According to these criteria, a given state
\rho
is entangled if the trace norms
\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{1}
or
\|{\cal{R}}(\rho)\|_{1}
are strictly larger than 1.

For the pure state defined in Eq.(
4
), it is straightforward to prove
K.Chen(2005)
:

\displaystyle\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{1}=\|{\cal{R}}(\rho)\|_{1}=(\sum^{m}_{i=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}})^{2}

(13)

Besides, we know that

\displaystyle(\sum^{m}_{i=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}})^{2}\leq m\sum^{m}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}=m

(14)

from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, where
\{\lambda_{i}\}
is a probability distribution.

Theorem 1
. For any mixed entanglement state
\rho
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
with the dimension of
m
and
n
(
m\leq n
), respectively, the
q
-concurrence
C_{q}(\rho)
satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho)\geq\frac{(\max{\{\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1},\|{\cal{R}}(\rho)\|_{1}^{q-1}\}}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

(15)

Proof
. Consider the optimal decomposition of
\rho
as
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}
in order to achieve the infimum of
C_{q}(\rho)
in Eq.(
7
), where
\rho_{i}
s are pure states with
\rho_{i}=|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
. Firstly, we will prove that

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho_{i})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(\|\rho_{i}^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

(16)

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho_{i})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(\|{\cal{R}}(\rho_{i})\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

(17)

In fact, note that the function
g(\lambda)=\lambda^{q}
is convex for
q\geq 2
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. It means that
h(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}^{q}
is Schur-convex
Bhatia(1997)
. Since the uniform distribution of
\{\frac{1}{m},\cdots,\frac{1}{m}\}
is majorized by any other distribution
\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}\}
, i.e.,
\{\frac{1}{m},\cdots,\frac{1}{m}\}\prec\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}\}
. For the Schur-convex function

it follows that
Bhatia(1997)
:

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{k=1}\lambda^{q}_{k}\geq\frac{1}{m^{q-1}}

(18)

From Eq.(
6
), we have

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum^{m}_{k=1}\lambda_{ik}^{q}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\sum^{m}_{j=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ij}})^{2q-2}-\sum^{m}_{k=1}\lambda_{ik}^{q}(\sum^{m}_{j=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ij}})^{2q-2}}{(\sum^{m}_{j=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ij}})^{2q-2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ik}})^{2q-2}-1}{(\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ik}})^{2q-2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{((\sum^{m}_{j=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ij}})^{2q-2}-1)^{2}}{(\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ik}})^{2q-2}((\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ik}})^{2q-2}-1)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(\|\rho_{i}^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

(20)

where the inequality (
19
) is due to the inequality:
-\sum^{m}_{k=1}\lambda_{ik}^{q}(\sum^{m}_{j=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ij}})^{2q-2}\geq-1
which can be proved by using the inequalities (
14
) and (
18
). Moreover, for a pure state
\rho_{i}=|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
, we have
\|\rho_{i}^{T_{A}}\|_{1}=(\sum^{m}_{k=1}\sqrt{\lambda_{ik}})^{2}\leq m
as shown in Eq.(
13
). This implies the inequality (
20
).

From Eq.(
20
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}p_{i}C(\rho_{i})\geq\frac{\sum_{i}p_{i}(\|\rho_{i}^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

In fact, for

\displaystyle(\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}

\displaystyle(\|\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\sum_{i}p_{i}\|\rho_{i}^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}p_{i}\|\rho_{i}^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{2q-2}

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho)\geq\frac{(\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

From Eq.(

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho)\geq\frac{(\|{\cal{R}}(\rho)\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

Combining Eqs.(

) and (

), we get that

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho)\geq\max\{\frac{(\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}},\frac{(\|{\cal{R}}(\rho)\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}\}

Example 1
. Isotropic states are a class of mixed states on

\rho_{F}
with respect to
\rho_{\Psi}=|\Psi^{+}\rangle\langle\Psi^{+}|
, i.e.,
F=f_{\Psi^{+}}(\rho_{F})=\langle\Psi^{+}|\rho_{F}|\Psi^{+}\rangle
which satisfies
0\leq F\leq 1
.

For isotropic states
\rho_{F}
, there are lots of analytic results in the entanglement of formation
Terhal(2000)
, the tangle and concurrence
Rungta(2003)
, and Rényi
\alpha
-entropy entanglement
Wang(2016)
. Inspired by the techniques
Terhal(2000)
;
Rungta(2003)
;
Wang(2016)
, the
q
-concurrence
C_{q}(\rho_{F})
for these states will be derived by an extremization as follows.

Lemma 2
. The
q
-concurrence for isotropic states
\rho_{F}
on Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}

(d\geq 2)
is given by

\displaystyle C_{q}(\rho_{F})=co(\xi(F,q,d))

(31)

where
F\in(1/d,1]
and
co()
denotes the largest convex function that is upper bounded by a given function
\xi(F,q,d)
defined as

\displaystyle\xi(F,q,d)=1-\gamma^{2q}-(d-1)\delta^{2q}

(32)

with
\gamma=\sqrt{F}/\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{(d-1)(1-F)}/\sqrt{d}
and
\delta=\sqrt{F}/\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{1-F}/\sqrt{d(d-1)}
.

The evaluation is essentially algebraic and quite tedious, as shown in Appendix B. For convenience, take
q=2
as an example. The
2
-concurrence of an two-qubit isotropic state
\rho_{F}
is given by
Rungta(2003)
:

\displaystyle C_{2}(\rho_{F})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&0\leq F\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
&\frac{(1-2F)^{2}}{2},&&\frac{1}{2}\leq F\leq 1\end{aligned}\right.

(33)

Moreover, from Theorem 1 it implies
C_{2}(\rho_{F})\geq(1-2F)^{2}/2=C_{2}(\rho_{F})
. This implies that the upper bound gives exact value of the
2
-concurrence for this qubit systems. For arbitrary
d\geq 3
, the
2
-concurrence
C_{2}(\rho_{F})
is given by

\displaystyle C_{2}(\rho_{F})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&F\leq\frac{1}{d}\\
&\xi(F,2,d),&&\frac{1}{d}\leq F\leq\frac{4(d-1)}{d^{2}}\\
&\frac{dF-d}{d-1}+\frac{d-1}{d},&&\frac{4(d-1)}{d^{2}}\leq F\leq 1\end{aligned}\right.

(34)

Moreover, it is shown that
\|\rho^{T_{A}}_{F}\|_{1}=\|{\cal{R}}(\rho_{F})\|_{1}=dF
for
F>1/d

Vidal(2002)
;
Rudolph (2005)
. From Theorem 1 we get that

\displaystyle C_{2}(\rho_{F})\geq\frac{(dF-1)^{2}}{d^{2}-d}

(35)

For simplicity,
C_{2}(\rho_{F})
in Eq.(
34
) and its lower bounds in Eq.(
35
) are shown in Fig.1 with
3\leq d\leq 10
. Especially, it illustrates that the present lower bound is very close to the exact value of the
2
-concurrence, which shows of the tightness of our bounds.

The lower bound in Theorem 1 can be used to detect the
q
-concurrence for all the entangled states of two-qubit or qubit-qutrit system because the PPT criterion is necessary and sufficient for the separability in both cases
Peres(1996)
;
Horodecki(1996)
. Unfortunately, it cannot detect all the other entangled states due to the limitation of the PPT criterion
Peres(1996)
;
Horodecki(1996)
and the realignment criterion
Horodecki(1999)
;
Rudolph (2005)
. Thus, it is intriguing to explore new bounds for the
q
-concurrence of general mixed states.

III
The
q
-concurrence of superposition states

Assume that a state
|\Gamma\rangle
is generated by superposing two pure states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
, i.e.,
|\Gamma\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle+\beta|\Psi\rangle
. Our goal in this section is to explore the
q
-concurrence for these superposition states. We discuss how the entanglement of superpositions of some given pure states is related to the entanglement contained in input states. In detail, we consider four cases: two component states in the superposition are bi-orthogonal states, one-sided orthogonal states, orthogonal states or arbitrary states.

III.1
Bi-orthogonal states

Definition 2
. Two bipartite states
|\Phi\rangle_{AB}
and
|\Psi\rangle_{AB}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
are bi-orthogonal if they satisfy

\displaystyle{\rm{Tr}}_{B}[{\rm{Tr}}_{A}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|){\rm{Tr}}_{A}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)]=0

(36)

\displaystyle{\rm{Tr}}_{A}[{\rm{Tr}}_{B}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|){\rm{Tr}}_{B}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)]=0

(37)

For two bi-orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
we get up to local unitary transformations
Gour(2007)1
that

\displaystyle|\Phi\rangle=\sum^{d_{1}}_{i=1}a_{i}|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\sum^{d}_{i=1}b_{i}|i+d_{1}\rangle_{A}|i+d_{1}\rangle_{B}

(38)

where
a_{i},b_{i}
are positive constants. In this case, the
q
-concurrence of the superposition state
|\Gamma\rangle
will be evaluated as follows.

Theorem 2
. Given two bi-orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
, then the
q
-concurrence of the superposition
|\Gamma\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle+\beta|\Psi\rangle
satisfies

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)=|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

(39)

where
h_{q}(t)=1-t^{q}-(1-t)^{q}
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
.

Proof
. From Eq.(
38
) the reduced states of the system
A
for
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
are diagonal in the same basis. More specifically, from Eq.(
2
), we have
C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)=F_{q}(\rho_{A})
with
\rho_{A}={\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Gamma\rangle\langle\Gamma|)
. It follows that the first
d_{1}
eigenvalues of
\rho_{A}
are given by
\{|\alpha|^{2}{a^{2}_{i}},i=1,2,\cdots,d_{1}\}
, and all the rest eigenvalues are given by
\{|\beta|^{2}{b^{2}_{j}},j=1,2,\cdots,d\}
. Thus, from Definition 1 we get

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{i}(|\alpha|^{2}{a^{2}_{i}})^{q}-\sum_{j}(|\beta|^{2}{b^{2}_{j}})^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}(1-\sum_{i}a^{2q}_{i})+|\beta|^{2q}(1-\sum_{j}b^{2q}_{i})

\displaystyle+1-|\alpha|^{2q}-|\beta|^{2q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

From Lemma 1 for any density matrices
\rho
and
\sigma
we get the following inequalities:

\displaystyle F_{q}(|\alpha|^{2}\rho+|\beta|^{2}\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}F_{q}(\rho)+|\beta|^{2}F_{q}(\sigma)

(41)

\displaystyle F_{q}(|\alpha|^{2}\rho+|\beta|^{2}\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}F_{q}(\rho)+|\beta|^{2q}F_{q}(\sigma)

(42)

\displaystyle+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

Moreover, from Lemma 1 the Eq.(
42
) holds iff
\rho
and
\sigma
are orthogonal. Since
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
are bi-orthogonal, their reduced density matrices
\rho_{A}
and
\sigma_{A}
are orthogonal. Thus, from Eq.(
42
) we get Eq.(
39
).
\square

Note that the entanglement of the superposition is related to the average of the entanglement of two states being superposed. For convenience, define the increase of
q
-concurrence entanglement for the superposition state
|\Gamma\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle+\beta|\Psi\rangle
as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)=C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)-(|\alpha|^{2}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle))

(43)

For the bi-orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
, we obtain the following Corollary 1.

Corollary 1
. Given two bi-orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
, the increase of the
q
-concurrence of the superposition state
|\Gamma\rangle
is upper bounded by one ebit, i.e.,
\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)\leq 1
.

Proof
. From Theorem 2, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)-[|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)]=h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

(44)

Moreover, since
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 1
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)=

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)-(|\alpha|^{2}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)-(|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle))

(45)

Thereby, combing Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) we get

\displaystyle\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)\leq h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})\leq 1

(46)

which implies that the increase of the
q
-concurrence for the superposition state
|\Gamma\rangle
cannot be greater than one ebit.
\Box

Example 2
. Consider the superposition state

\displaystyle|\Gamma\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle+\beta|\Psi\rangle

(47)

with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
, where
|\Phi\rangle=\cos\theta|00\rangle+\sin\theta|11\rangle
and

are generalized bipartite entangled states for
\theta,\phi\in(0,\pi/2)
. Note that
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
are bi-orthogonal states. From Eq.(
2
) it is easy to check that
C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)=1-\cos^{2q}\theta-\sin^{2q}\theta
,
C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)=1-\cos^{2q}\phi-\sin^{2q}\phi
and

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-|\alpha|^{2q}(\cos^{2q}\theta+\sin^{2q}\theta)

(48)

\displaystyle-|\beta|^{2q}(\cos^{2q}\phi+\sin^{2q}\phi)

Moreover, we have
h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})=1-|\alpha|^{2q}-|\beta|^{2q}
. Thus, Eq.(
39
) holds for the superposition state
|\Gamma\rangle
in Eq.(
47
), which is consistent with Theorem 2. From Eq.(
43
) we have

\displaystyle\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)-(|\alpha|^{2}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle))

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\alpha|^{2}-|\alpha|^{2q})(\cos^{2q}\theta+\sin^{2q}\theta)

\displaystyle+(|\beta|^{2}-|\beta|^{2q})(\cos^{2q}\phi+\sin^{2q}\phi)

To show the result in Corollary 1, we take the special case of
\alpha=\beta=1/\sqrt{2}
and
q=4
. As shown in Fig.2, it is apparent that the function
\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)
of
\theta
and
\phi
satisfies
\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)\leq 1
.

-concurrence for the superposition state

III.2
One-side orthogonal states

Definition 3
. Two bipartite states
|\Phi\rangle_{AB}
and
|\Psi\rangle_{AB}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
are one-side orthogonal if they satisfy only one of Eqs.(
36
) and (
37
).

Without loss of generality, we assume that two one-side orthogonal states satisfy Eq. (
36
). Up to local unitary transformations
Gour(2007)1
, we have

\displaystyle|\Phi\rangle=\sum^{d_{1}}_{i=1}a_{i}|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B}

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\sum^{d}_{i=1}b_{i}|i\rangle_{A}|i+d_{1}\rangle_{B}

(50)

where
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are positive constants.

Now, consider the case of
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
being one-sided orthogonal but not necessarily bi-orthogonal, i.e., they satisfy Eq. (
50
).

Theorem 3
. Given two one-side orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
, the
q
-concurrence of the superposition state
|\Gamma\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle+\beta|\Psi\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)

(51)

\displaystyle+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})-|F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B})|

where
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
are reduced density matrices of
\rho_{AB}
,
\rho_{AB}:=|\alpha|^{2}|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|+|\beta|^{2}|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|
with
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
, and
h_{q}
is defined in Theorem 2.

Proof
. Note that
\||\Gamma\rangle\|=\sqrt{\langle\Gamma|\Gamma\rangle}=\sqrt{|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}}=1
. It means that
|\Gamma\rangle
is normalized. From Eq.(
50
) we know

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Gamma\rangle\langle\Gamma|)=|\alpha|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)+|\beta|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)

(52)

which is also defined as the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
by tracing
\rho_{AB}
over the subsystem
B
. Hence, we get that
{\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Gamma\rangle\langle\Gamma|)=\rho_{A}
. From Eq.(
2
) it follows that

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)=F_{q}(\rho_{A})

(53)

Note that
{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Gamma\rangle\langle\Gamma|)\neq\rho_{B}
because
\rho_{B}
is defined as the reduced density matrix by tracing
\rho_{AB}
over the subsystem
A
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\rho_{B}=|\alpha|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)+|\beta|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)

(54)

while

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Gamma\rangle\langle\Gamma|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)+\alpha\beta^{\ast}{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Psi|)

(55)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{\ast}\beta{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Phi|)

\displaystyle+|\beta|^{2}{\rm Tr}_{A}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)

From Eqs.(
50
) and (
54
) the first
d_{1}
eigenvalues of
\rho_{B}
are given by
\{|\alpha|^{2}{a^{2}_{i}}|i=1,2,\cdots,d_{1}\}
, and all the rest are shown as
\{|\beta|^{2}{b^{2}_{j}}|j=1,2,\cdots,d\}
. Thus, according to the definition of
F_{q}(\rho)
in Eq.(
9
) we get

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!F_{q}(\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{i}(|\alpha|^{2}{a^{2}_{i}})^{q}-\sum_{j}(|\beta|^{2}{b^{2}_{j}})^{q}

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}(1-\sum_{i}a^{2q}_{i})+|\beta|^{2q}(1-\sum_{j}b^{2q}_{i})

\displaystyle+1-|\alpha|^{2q}-|\beta|^{2q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

By utilizing Eqs.(
42
) and (
52
) we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!F_{q}(\rho_{A})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}F_{q}({\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|))

(57)

\displaystyle+|\beta|^{2q}F_{q}({\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|))+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

From Eqs.(
56
) and (
57
)
F_{q}(\rho_{B})\geq F_{q}(\rho_{A})
for the one-side orthogonal states defined in Eq.(
36
). Due to Eqs.(
53
) and (
56
) we obtain

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)

(58)

\displaystyle+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})-(F_{q}(\rho_{B})-F_{q}(\rho_{A}))

In a similar manner, for one-side orthogonal states in Eq.(
37
) we get that

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)

(59)

\displaystyle+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})-(F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B}))

Combining Eqs.(
58
) and (
59
), we have completed the proof.
\square

Note that
F_{q}(\rho_{A})=F_{q}(\rho_{B})
holds for bi-orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
. Thus we can obtain Eq.(
39
) for Theorem 2. Moreover, since
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
are orthogonal pure states, we have
F_{q}(\rho_{AB})=h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})
. This implies
h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})\geq|F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B})|
from the triangle inequality of
F_{q}(\rho_{AB})
in Lemma 1.

Similar to Corollary 1, we get the following result for one-side orthogonal states.

Corollary 2
. Given two one-sided orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
, the increase of the
q
-concurrence for the superposition state
|\Gamma\rangle
is no more than one ebit, i.e.,
\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)\leq 1
.

Proof
. From Theorem 3 we have

\displaystyle\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)-(|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle))

(60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})-|F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B})|

Note that

\displaystyle h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})-|F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B}|\leq H_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

(61)

where the equality holds for the bi-orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
.

Clearly, Eq.(
45
) holds for one-side orthogonal states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
. Thus, combining Eqs.(
60
), (
61
) and (
45
), we obtain
\Delta C_{q}(|\Gamma\rangle)\leq h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})\leq 1
. This completes the proof.
\Box

III.3
Arbitrary states

For general cases, two pure states that are superposed are not orthogonal. This means that the superposition state is not normalized. If we define
|\Gamma^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{1}{c_{+}}|\Gamma\rangle
with
|\Gamma\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle+\beta|\Psi\rangle
as the normalized state of
|\Gamma\rangle
, where
c_{+}
is the normalization constant. By using Theorem 1, we prove the following inequality for its
q
-concurrence.

Theorem 4
. Given any two different states
|\Phi\rangle
and
|\Psi\rangle
, then the
q
-concurrence of the superposition
|\Gamma\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle+\beta|\Psi\rangle
satisfies

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma^{\prime}\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{c_{+}^{2}}(|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)

(66)

\displaystyle+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})-|F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B})|)

or

\displaystyle C_{q}(|\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{c_{+}^{2}}(|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)

(67)

\displaystyle+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})-|F_{q}(\rho_{A})-F_{q}(\rho_{B})|)

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{-}^{2}}{c_{+}^{2}}\frac{(\|\sigma^{T_{A}}\|_{1}^{q-1}-1)^{2}}{m^{2q-2}-m^{q-1}}

where
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
, and
\sigma
is the density operator of
|\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\rangle
, i.e.,
\sigma=|\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}\rangle\langle\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}|
.
\sigma^{T_{A}}
stands for a partial transpose with respect to the subsystem
A
.
\|X\|_{1}
denotes the trace norm.

Proof
. By introducing an auxiliary state
|0\rangle_{a}
and
|1\rangle_{a}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{a}
, consider the state

\displaystyle|\Delta\rangle=\alpha|\Phi\rangle|0\rangle_{a}+\beta|\Psi\rangle|1\rangle_{a}

(68)

The reduced state of
|\Delta\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{B}=|\alpha|^{2}{\rm{Tr}}_{A}(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)+|\beta|^{2}{\rm{Tr}}_{A}(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)

(69)

According to Eq.(
42
), we get

\displaystyle F_{q}(\rho_{B})\leq|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

(70)

Similarly, for the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
we have

\displaystyle F_{q}(\rho_{A})\leq|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

(71)

Thus, from Eqs.(
70
) and (
71
) we have

\displaystyle\max\{F_{q}(\rho_{A}),F_{q}(\rho_{B})\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Phi\rangle)+|\beta|^{2q}C_{q}(|\Psi\rangle)

(72)

\displaystyle+h_{q}(|\alpha|^{2})

Additionally,
\rho_{B}
may be written into

IV
Conclusion

Given an entanglement, how much is it entangled? The entanglement monotone has been introduced to solve this problem by quantifying the degree of entanglement. In this paper, inspired by general Tsallis entropy, we define a parameterized
q
-concurrence as a new entanglement monotone for any
q\geq 2
. We prove a lower bound of the
q
-concurrence for general states. The present bound is exact for two-qubit isotropic states. The new entanglement monotone is finally applied for characterizing the superposition state in terms of two states being superposed, especially for bi-orthogonal or one-sided orthogonal states. It shows that the increase of the
q
-concurrence for the superposition state is upper bounded by one ebit in both cases. These results are interesting in the entanglement theory, quantum information processing, quantum communication, and quantum many-body theory.

Effective non-vanishing and the Mukai-type conjecture
Yoshinori Gongyo

Abstract
. We introduce some invariants of Fano varieties and propose a Mukai-type conjecture which characterizes the product of projective spaces. Moreover, we prove that the Ambro–Kawamata effective non-vanishing conjecture implies the Mukai-type conjecture.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this article, we will work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.
We will make use of the standard notation as in
[
KaMM
]
,
[
Ko
]
,

[
BiCHM
]
, and
[
KoMo
]
.
In this short article, we study the characterization of the product of the projective spaces for Fano varieties. The following is a very famous conjecture by Shigeru Mukai:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
M
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional smooth Fano varieties,
\rho(X)
the Picard number, and

i_{X}:=\max\{r\in\mathbb{Z}|-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}rH\text{ for some Cartier divisor $H$}\}

be the Fano index. Then it holds that

d+\rho(X)-i(X)\cdot\rho(X)\geq 0.

Moreover the above is equal if and only if
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{i(X)-1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{i(X)-1}.

The classical approach to the Mukai conjecture is to study the geometry of rational curves cf.
[
W
]
,
[
BCDD
]
… In fact, in the series of these studies, they prove some generalization of the above Mukai conjecture, where Fano index is replaced by the
pseudo index
which is the minimal length of rational curves on the variety.

In this article, we introduce some invariants of Fano varieties and propose the new Mukai-type conjecture from the motivation of the Mukai conjecture. We call the invariant the
total index
, which is the maximal length of nef-partitions on Toric geometry that appeared in
[
Ba
]
,
[
BaN
]

(cf. Section
3
). The total index coincides with the Fano index when the Picard number is one. We propose the new conjecture by replacing
\rho(X)\cdot i(X)
with the total index
\tau_{X}
in Conjecture
1.1
as follows:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional smooth Fano varieties,
\rho(X)
the Picard number, and
\tau_{X}
be the total index in Definition
3.1
. Then it holds that

\mathrm{dim}\,X+\rho(X)-\tau_{X}\geq 0

and the equality holds if and only if the product of projective spaces, i.e.
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{d_{\rho(X)}}
for some
d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{\rho(X)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

Note that Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
coincide with the Kobayashi–Ochiai theorem
[
KoO
]
when the Picard number is one.

The main ingredient of this article is to prove Conjecture
1.2
under assuming that the following Ambro–Kawamata effective non-vanishing conjecture is true:

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
(X,B)
be a projective klt pair and
L
a nef line bundle on
X
such that
L-(K_{X}+B)
is ample.

Then
H^{0}(X,L)\not=0
.

Namely the following is the main theorem in this article:

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that Conjecture
1.3
holds. Then Conjecture
1.2
holds.

We note that we study Conjecture
1.2
from the viewpoint of Shokurov’s complexity for generalized pairs in
[
GM
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some notations of generalized pairs and quasi-log schemes.
For more detail, we refer the reader to
[
Bi
]
and
[
Am2
]
,
[
F-book
]
. First, we recall the definition of generalized log canonical and kawamata log terminal.

Definition 2.1
.

A generalized pair
(X,B+M)
consists of

•

a normal projective variety
X
,

•

an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B
on
X
, and

•

a b-
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier b-divisor over
X
represented by some projective birational morphism
\varphi:X^{\prime}\to X
and
a nef
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
M^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}

such that
M=\varphi_{*}M^{\prime}
and
K_{X}+B+M
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a generalized pair.
Let
Y\rightarrow X
be a projective birational morphism.
We can write

K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M),

for some divisor
B_{Y}
on
Y
.
The generalized log discrepancy
of
(X,B+M)
at a prime divisor
E\subset Y
, denoted by
a_{E}(X,B+M)

is defined to be
1-{\rm coeff}_{E}(B_{Y})
.

A generalized pair
(X,B+M)
is said to be generalized log canonical (or glc for short) if all its generalized log discrepancies are non-negative.
A generalized pair
(X,B+M)
is said to be generalized Kawamata log terminal (or gklt for short) if all its generalized log discrepancies are positive.
In the previous definitions, if the analogous statement holds for
M=0
, then we drop the word “generalized”.

Moreover, we recall the notion of the quasi-log structure by Ambro–Fujino:

Definition 2.3

(Quasi-log canonical pairs)
.

Let
X
be a scheme and let
\omega
be an

\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor (or an
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle) on
X
.
Let
f:Z\to X
be a proper morphism from a globally embedded
simple normal
crossing pair
(Z,\Delta_{Z})
, i.e.
Z
is a simple normal crossing divisor on some smooth variety
M
, and
\Delta_{Z}=\Delta_{M}|_{Z}
, where
\Delta_{M}
is some divisor on
M
such that
\mathrm{Supp}\,(\Delta_{M}+Z)
is simple normal crossing and
\mathrm{Supp}\,\Delta_{M}
does not contain any components of
Z
. Note that
\Delta_{Z}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor which is not necessarily effective. If the natural map

\mathcal{O}_{X}\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\lceil-(\Delta_{Z}^{<1})\rceil)
is an
isomorphism,
\Delta_{Z}=\Delta^{\leq 1}_{Z}
,
and
f^{*}\omega\sim_{\mathbb{R}}K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}
,
then
\left(X,\omega,f:(Z,\Delta_{Z})\to X\right)

is called a quasi-log canonical pair
(qlc pair, for short). Let
C
be a closed subvariety of
X
. We say that
C
is a qlc center if
C
is not an irreducible component of
X
and an image of some stratum of
(Z,\lfloor\Delta_{Z}\rfloor)
.
We denote the union of all qlc centers by
\mathrm{Nqklt(X,\omega)}
.

We call the triple quasi-kawamata log terminal pair
(qklt pair, for short) when
\mathrm{Nqklt(X,\omega)}=\emptyset

. If there is no danger of confusion,
we simply say that
[X,\omega]
is a qlc pair (resp. qklt pair).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
[X,\omega]
be a projective qklt, then
X
is normal and there exists an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B+M
such that
\omega\sim_{\mathbb{R}}K_{X}+B+M
and
(X,B+M)
is generalized klt. Moreover, when
\omega
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, so are
B
and
M
.

Proof.

From the definition of qklt, it follows that
\mathrm{Nqklt(X,\omega)}=\emptyset
.
By
[
F-book
, Lemma 6.9]
, we see that
X
is normal. The latter assertion follows from
[
F2
, Theorem 1.3]
.

∎

3.
Total indices

In this section, we define the total index of Fano varieties and compute some examples. In
[
Ba
]
,
[
BaN
]
, the invariants for Toric varieties appear in the context of the Mirror symmetry.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety. We define the total index
\tau_{X}
of
X
as the maximal of
\sum a_{i}
for a decomposition

-K_{X}=\sum a_{i}L_{i},

where
L_{i}
are nef line bundles which is not numerically trivial and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Note that we allow
L_{i}=L_{j}
for
i\not=j
.

It seems to be no direct relationship with the product of the Fano index and the Picard number although we propose Conjecture
1.2
motivated with Conjecture
1.1
;

Example 3.2
.

Let
X
be a three point blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then
\tau_{X}=3
but
\rho(X)=4
and the Fano index
i(X)=1
. Thus
\tau_{X}<\rho(X)i(X)
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
X
be a one point blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then
\tau_{X}=3
but
\rho(X)=2
and the Fano index
i(X)=1
. Thus
\tau_{X}>\rho(X)i(X)
.

4.
Ambro–Kawamata’s effective non-vanishing conjecture

In this section, we discuss extending Conjecture
1.3
beyond kawamata log terminal singularities.

We expect that Conjecture
1.3
is valid for generalized klt pairs or quasi-log canonical pairs:

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that Conjecture
1.3
holds. Let
[X,\omega]
be a projective quasi-log canonical pair (resp.
(X,B+M)
is a projective generalized klt pair) and
L
a nef line bundle on
X
such that
L-\omega
(resp.
L-(K_{X}+B+M)
) is ample.
Then
H^{0}(X,L)\not=0
.

Proof.

We shall prove by induction on dimension. By the adjunction and vanishing theorem
[
F-book
, Theorem 6.3.4(2)]
, we may assume that
[X,\omega]
has no quasi-lc center, then
[X,\omega]
has generalized klt structure by Lemma
2.4
, i.e.
\omega\sim_{\mathbb{R}}K_{X}+B+M
for some generalized klt pair
(X,B+M)
. Moreover, take a general effective
\mathbb{R}
-ample divisor
H
such that there exists an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B^{\prime}
with
B+M+\varepsilon H\sim_{\mathbb{R}}B^{\prime}
for some small
\varepsilon>0
and that
(X,B^{\prime})
is klt. Thus, we may assume that
M=0
. Thus the conclusion holds.

∎

Now we discuss some version of the ladder arguments for Fano varieties cf.
[
Am1
]
,
[
Ka2
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
[X,\omega]
be a normal quasi-log canonical pair and
D
be an effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
. Let
l
be the quasi-log canonical thresholds of
D
for
[X,\omega]
, i.e.

l=\sup\{s\geq 0|[X,\omega+sD]\text{ has quasi-log canonical structure}\}.

Then for an associated generalized log canonical structure
(X,B+M)
of
[X,\omega]
, the divisors

B^{\prime}=B+lD\text{ and }M^{\prime}=M

give a generalized log canonical structure
(X,B^{\prime}+M^{\prime})
on
[X,\omega+lD].

Proof.

See the proof of
[
F1
, Theorem 1.7]
.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that Conjecture
1.3
holds. Let
[X,\omega]
be a projective normal quasi-log canonical scheme such that
-\omega
is nef with a generalized lc structure
(X,B+M)
such that
\omega\sim_{\mathbb{R}}K_{X}+B+M
and
X
is of Fano type, i.e. there exists a big
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-K_{X}
such that
(X,\Delta)
is klt. Suppose that we have a decomposition

-(K_{X}+B+M)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}L_{i},

where
L_{i}
are nef line bundles such that
L_{i}\not\equiv 0
for any
i
. Then there exists a reduced divisor
S_{i,j}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
for
j=1,\cdots,\lceil a_{i}\rceil
and for any
i
such that

(X,B+M+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor a_{i}\rfloor}S_{i,j}+\sum_{i}\{a_{i}\}S_{i,\lceil a_{i}\rceil})

is log canonical.

Proof.

First we shall show the proposition when
-(K_{X}+B+M)
is ample.
By Conjecture
1.3
and Lemma
4.1
,we have some effective divisor
S_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{1}
. Let
l
be the log canonical thresholds of
S_{1}
for the pair
(X,B+M)
. We consider the case of
a_{1}\geq 1
.
We assume that
l<1
. Then

L_{1}-(K_{X}+B+M+lS_{1})\equiv(1+a_{1}-l)S_{1}+\sum_{i>1}a_{i}L_{i}

is still ample since
1+a_{1}-l>0.
Let
C
be a minimal lc center of
(X,B+M+lS_{1})
.
By applying the vanishing theorem
[
F-book
, Theorem 6.3.4(2)]
cf.
[
CLX
]
, we obtain the surjectivity of

H^{0}(X,L_{1})\to H^{0}(C,L_{1}).

Note that this minimal lc center is a minimal qlc center from Lemma
4.2
and
[
F1
, Theorem 1.7]
.
By the adjunction formula of
[
FuH
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have
-(K_{X}+B+M+lS_{1})|_{C}=-(K_{C}+B_{C}+M_{C})
for some
B_{C}+M_{C}
such that
(C,B_{C}+M_{C})
is generalized klt. Since
a_{1}-l>0,

-(K_{C}+B_{C}+M_{C})
is ample.

By Conjecture
1.3
(cf. Lemma
4.1
) for
C
,

H^{0}(C,L_{1})\not=0.

Thus if we choose
S_{1}
as general,
C
must be contained in the base locus of
|L_{1}|
. Therefore this is the contradiction and it holds that
l=1
. Thus by induction on
\lfloor a_{1}\rfloor
, we may assume that
a_{1}<1
. Then, for the log canonical thresholds
l
of
S_{1}
for the pair
(X,B+M)
, if
l<a_{1}
, we make the contradiction from the same arguments as the above. So
l\geq a_{1}
. Thus by induction on
r
, we see the desired proposition.

Next, we discuss the general case. By the base point free theorem for quasi-log pairs (cf.
[
F-book
, Theorem 6.5.1]
), we have the semi-ample fibration
\varphi:X\to Y
of
-\omega.
Indeed let
f:(Z,B_{Z})\to X
be a quasi-log structure of
(X,\omega)
. Then we have an ample
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
-\omega_{Y}
such that
-\omega=\varphi^{*}(-\omega_{Y}).
Thus the induced morphism
f\circ\varphi:(Z,B_{Z})\to Y
gives a quasi-log structure on
(Y,\omega_{Y^{\prime}}).
Note that we give a generalized log canonical structure
(Y,B_{Y}+M_{Y})
on
\omega_{Y}
from
[
F1
, Theorem 1.7]
. Moreover, since
L_{i}\sim_{f}0
by the base point free theorem, we have some nef Cartier divisor
L_{i,Y}
on
Y
such that
L_{i}=\varphi^{*}L_{i,Y}
for any
i
.
Since
-\omega_{Y}
is ample, we have
a reduced divisor
S_{i,j,Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{i,Y}
for
j=1,\cdots,\lceil a_{i}\rceil
and for any
i
such that

(Y,B_{Y}+M_{Y}+\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor a_{i}\rfloor}S_{i,j,Y}+\sum_{i}\{a_{i}\}S_{i,\lceil a_{i}\rceil,Y})

is log canonical. Now since

K_{X}+B+M+\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor a_{i}\rfloor}S_{i,j}+\sum_{i}\{a_{i}\}S_{i,\lceil a_{i}\rceil}=\varphi^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}+\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor a_{i}\rfloor}S_{i,j,Y}+\sum_{i}\{a_{i}\}S_{i,\lceil a_{i}\rceil,Y})

by letting
S_{i,j}=\varphi^{*}S_{i,j,Y}
, we apply the inversion of adjunction for the fibration cf.
[
Fil
, Proposition 4.16]
and see that

(X,B+M+\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor a_{i}\rfloor}S_{i,j}+\sum_{i}\{a_{i}\}S_{i,\lceil a_{i}\rceil})

is log canonical. Thus we prove the proposition.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
[X,\omega]
be a projective normal quasi-log canonical scheme such that
-\omega
is nef with a generalized lc structure
(X,B+M)
such that
\omega\sim_{\mathbb{R}}K_{X}+B+M
and
X
is of Fano type. Suppose that we have a decomposition

-(K_{X}+B+M)\equiv\sum a_{i}L_{i},

where
L_{i}
are nef line bundles. If
X
is a smooth Fano
3
-fold or
\mathrm{dim}\,X=2
, then there exists a reduced divisor
S_{i,j}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
for
j=1,\cdots,\lceil a_{i}\rceil
and for any
i
such that

(X,B+M+\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor a_{i}\rfloor}S_{i,j}+\sum_{i}\{a_{i}\}S_{i,\lceil a_{i}\rceil})

is log canonical.

Proof.

When
\mathrm{dim}\,X=2
, the fully general case of Conjecture
1.3
is known in
[
Ka2
, Theorem 5.1]
. And for smooth Fano
3
-folds, it is known by
[
BrH
, Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

.

Lemma
4.1
, Proposition
4.3
, and Corollary
4.4
can be proved for generalized log canonical pairs by using the results in
[

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathrm{dim}\,X+\rho_{X}-\tau_{X}\leq 0.
By Proposition
4.3
, we obtain a reduced divisor
S_{i,j}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
for
j=1,\cdots,a_{i}
and for any
i
such that

(X,\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{a_{i}}S_{i,j})

is log canonical. Let
\Delta=\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{a_{i}}S_{i,j}
. Then the global complexity of
(X,\Gamma)
is not less than
\mathrm{dim}\,X+\rho_{X}
. Thus by
[
BMSZ
]
,

(X,\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{a_{i}}S_{i,j})

is a Toric pair and

\mathrm{dim}\,X+\rho_{X}-\tau_{X}=0.

Since
\sum a_{i}=\mathrm{dim}\,X+\rho_{X}
, any torus invariant divisors appear in
\{S_{i,j}\}
. Thus we conclude every effective divisor is nef on
X
. Such a smooth Toric variety is only a product of projective spaces (cf.
[
FS
, Proposition 5.3]
).
∎

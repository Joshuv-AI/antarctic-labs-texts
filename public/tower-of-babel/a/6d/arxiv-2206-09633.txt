Page curve and symmetries Preprint: KOBE-COSMO-22-07
Pak Hang Chris Lau, Toshifumi Noumi, Yuhei Takii, Kotaro Tamaoka

Abstract
Motivated by the quantum process of black hole evaporation and its implications for symmetries, we consider a qubit system with a random dynamics as a toy model of black hole. We compute its symmetry-resolved entropies and discuss its implications. We first consider the case where charges are conserved and compute the symmetry-resolved entropies. We derive a symmetry-resolved analogue of the Page curve. We then consider the case where symmetry is explicitly broken and charges are no longer conserved. It serves as a toy model for global symmetry breaking in black hole evaporation. Despite the simple framework, the symmetry-resolved entropies capture various interesting features during the analogous process of black hole evaporation in our qubit model.



I
Introduction

Thought experiments on black hole evaporation offer various lessons on symmetries in quantum gravity. The most famous one is on the absence of global symmetries, which states that symmetries in quantum gravity have to be either gauged or explicitly broken
Banks and Dixon 1988
;
Banks and Seiberg 2011
;
Harlow and Ooguri 2021
. It is then natural to ask how large gauge interactions have to be and also how much symmetries have to be broken. Such quantitative questions are crucial when exploring phenomenological implications of quantum gravity constraints. A lot of research activities are ongoing toward this direction, especially along the line of the conjectured swampland criteria
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2007
;
Ooguri and Vafa 2007
. See, e.g.,
Palti 2019
;
van Beest et al. 2021
;
Harlow et al. 2022
for review articles.

More recently, significant progress has been made on the long-standing problem in quantum gravity, whether or not black hole evaporation follows the unitary time evolution
Hawking 1975
;
Hawking 1976
, in the light of entanglement entropy and gravitational path integral
Penington 2020
;
Almheiri et al. 2019
;
Almheiri et al. 2020
;
Penington et al. 2022
.
Notably, this progress also made implications for global symmetries in quantum gravity
Harlow and Shaghoulian 2021
;
Chen and Lin 2021
;
Hsin et al. 2021
;
Belin et al. 2022
;
Milekhin and Tajdini 2021
. It is then of great interests if one can use these developments to quantify required gauge interactions or symmetry breaking. We would like to set up a framework for such a quantitative problem.

According to the progress mentioned above, the time evolution of the entanglement entropy in the evaporation process follows the so-called unitary Page curve. The original Page curve
Page 1993
;
Page 2013
was introduced as entanglement entropy with random unitary time evolution, assuming that the dynamics of black holes are highly chaotic.
Such random time evolution is also used in the Hayden-Preskill protocol
Hayden and Preskill 2007
, which gives insight into information recovery in the evaporation process. Several recent studies
Nakata et al. 2020
;
Tajima and Saito 2021
have discussed information recovery in the Hayden-Preskill protocol when there are conserved quantities.

In this paper, inspired by these developments, we discuss a quantity called symmetry-resolved (entanglement) entropy
Laflorencie and Rachel 2014
;
Goldstein and Sela 2018
;
Xavier et al. 2018
in similar random systems as a toy model for studying the relationship between symmetries in quantum gravity and the dynamics of black holes. See
Milekhin and Tajdini 2021
for a seminal work in this direction in the context of two dimensional gravity.
The symmetry-resolved entropy is a finer decomposition of the entanglement entropy into superselection sectors of conserved charges. We will find that it is useful to introduce an analogue of the Page curve in the presence of symmetries. We also find that this perspective is useful to quantify effects of symmetry breaking on entanglement entropy.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In section
II
, we review the entanglement measures relevant in this paper, especially the symmetry-resolved entropy. In section
III
, we introduce our toy model of black hole evaporation with conserved charges and then derive a symmetry-resolved analog of the Page curve. In section
IV
, we introduce explicit symmetry breaking and study its effects on the Page curve. In section
V
, we conclude by discussing the implications of our results for the evaporation process with charged Hawking radiation.

II
Symmetry-resolved entropy

This section reviews the necessary entanglement measures used in this paper. Let us consider a system in a pure state represented by a density matrix
\rho=\left|\psi\right\rangle\left\langle\psi\right|
. We split the total system into a subregion
A
and its complement
\bar{A}
. A basic entanglement measure between the two regions is the entanglement entropy. It is defined to be the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix,

\displaystyle S_{A}=-{\text{Tr}}\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A}\,,

(1)

where
\rho_{A}={\text{Tr}}_{\bar{A}}\,\rho
is the reduced density matrix of the subregion
A
constructed by tracing the total density matrix over the complement region
\bar{A}
. Entanglement entropy has the property that
S_{A}=S_{\bar{A}}
for a system in a pure state. When the two regions are maximally entangled, the entanglement entropy attains its maximal value of
S_{A}=\min(\log d_{A},\log d_{\bar{A}})
, where
d_{A}
and
d_{\bar{A}}
are the dimensions of the Hilbert space of the region
A
and
\bar{A}
, respectively. A powerful tool to compute the entanglement entropy is the replica trick. We first compute a generalization of the entanglement entropy called the Rényi entropy,

\displaystyle S_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{1-n}\log{\text{Tr}}[\rho_{A}^{n}]\,.

(2)

Then the entanglement entropy can be recovered by analytic continuation and taking the limit
S_{A}=\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}S_{n}
. The Rényi entropy is non-increasing in
n
, i.e.,
S_{n_{1}}\geq S_{n_{2}},\ \forall\ n_{1}>n_{2}
. In this paper we utilize this property of the Rényi entropy and study the second Rényi entropy (
S_{2}
) instead of the entanglement entropy. It provides the tightest lower bound to the entanglement entropy and allows us to compute the Page curves.

Another focus of this paper is the existence of symmetry in the system which leads to conserved charges. The Hilbert space in such a system naturally splits into superselection sectors, labelled by the charge. We will explore the consequence of symmetry to the entanglement properties of the system. By assuming that the dynamics of the system is random, we can model the reduced density matrix using a probability distribution and decompose it into the form,

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}p(q)\rho_{A}(q)+\Delta\rho\,,\quad\text{Tr}_{q}\rho_{A}(q)=1\,,

(3)

where
\rho_{A}(q)
is the reduced density matrix in each charge sector and
p(q)
is the probability of finding the reduced density matrix with charge
q
.
\text{Tr}_{q}
is the trace over the charge
q
subspace of
A
. We also include scenarios of explicit symmetry breaking which leads to violation of charge conservation. The term
\Delta\rho
encodes such violation effects and allows transition between different charge sectors. We will first consider the case with
\Delta\rho=0
in Section
III
and then study the case with
\Delta\rho\neq 0
in Section
IV
.

The existence of conserved charges allows one to define a finer entanglement measure for each charge sector. Let us set
\Delta\rho=0
and plug in the form of (
3
) into the definition of entanglement entropy,

\displaystyle S_{A}

\displaystyle=-{\text{Tr}}\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{q}p(q){\text{Tr}}_{q}\rho_{A}(q)\log\rho_{A}(q)-\sum_{q}p(q)\log p(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}p(q)S_{A}(q)+S_{\text{c.f.}}\,,

(4)

where the first term contains the symmetry-resolved entanglement entropy
S_{A}(q)\equiv-{\text{Tr}}_{q}\rho_{A}(q)\log\rho_{A}(q)
defined as the von Neumann entropy for the reduced density matrix in the charge
q
sector. The second term
S_{\text{c.f.}}\equiv-\sum_{q}p(q)\log p(q)
is the charge fluctuation entropy
Lukin et al. 2019
. Since each term in
S_{A}
is semi-positive definite, there is an obvious relation
S_{A}\geq S_{\text{c.f.}}
. A symmetry-resolved Rényi entropy can similarly be defined and the symmetry-resolved entanglement entropy can be recovered by taking the limit
n\to 1
:

\displaystyle S_{n}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log{\text{Tr}}[\rho_{A}(q)^{n}]\,,

(5)

\displaystyle S_{A}(q)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}S_{n}(q)\,.

(6)

III
Symmetry-resolved Page curves

We first compute the symmetry resolved entropy in a random system with a conserved charge. Although our results are applicable to general quantum systems with finite dimensional Hilbert space, we will illustrate the calculation using a simple qubit model.

III.1
Setup

We consider an
N
qubits model with each qubit representing either a
+1
or
-1
charged particle. The allowed charge in this toy model is bounded by the number of qubits as
Q=-N,-N+2,\ldots,N
. To model the time evolution of a Page curve, we consider a time dependent bipartition of the system. At the initial time
t=0
, the region
A
is empty and the whole system is in the complement region
\bar{A}
.
At each time step, we perform a random time-evolution and then one qubit of the system is selected to become part of
A
. We interpret this setup as a toy model of black hole evaporation by regarding
A
and
\bar{A}
as Hawking radiation and the black hole, respectively.

More explicitly, at an intermediate time
t<N
, the state
\left|\psi\right\rangle
is split into a subsystem
A
with
t
qubits and a complement subsystem
\bar{A}
with
N-t
qubits. For a state with charge
Q
and a bipartition of the system into charge
q
and charge
\bar{q}
subregions, we employ the following notation:

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{q+\bar{q}=Q}\sum_{a,\bar{a}}\frac{e^{i\delta(q_{a},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}})}}{\sqrt{d(Q)}}|q_{a}\rangle_{A}\otimes|\bar{q}_{\bar{a}}\rangle_{\bar{A}}\,,

(7)

where
|q_{a}\rangle_{A}
denotes a charge
q
state labeled by
a
in the region
A
and similarly for
|\bar{q}_{\bar{a}}\rangle_{\bar{A}}
. Note that the charge
q
of the subregion
A
takes the value
q=-t,-t+2,\ldots,t
and similarly for
\bar{q}.
The labels
a
and
\bar{a}
account for the degeneracy of the states in each charge sector.
\delta(q_{a},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}})
is a random phase whose average will be taken in the computation of entropy afterwards.
d(Q)
is the dimension of the charge
Q
superselection sector. More explicitly,

\displaystyle d(Q)=\sum_{q+\bar{q}=Q}d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})\,,

(8)

where
d_{A}(q)
is the number of charge
q
states in
A
and similarly for
d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})
. In our present qubit setup, we have

\displaystyle d_{A}(q)=\binom{t}{\frac{t+q}{2}}\,,\quad d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})=\binom{N-t}{\frac{N-t+\bar{q}}{2}}\,,

\displaystyle d(Q)=\binom{N}{\frac{N+Q}{2}}\,.

(9)

The advantage of the notation (
7
) is that it can be easily modified to accommodate a more general situation, e.g., the scenario with violation of charge conservation studied in Section
IV
.

III.2
Calculation of Page curve

We consider an initial state with a total charge
Q
. The state after a random time-evolution populates equally over all the charge
Q
states and takes the form of (
7
).

The reduced density matrix of the subregion
A
reads

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=\sum_{q}p(q)\rho_{A}(q)

(10)

with
p(q)
and
\rho_{A}(q)
given by

\displaystyle p(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(Q-q)}{d(Q)}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\rho_{A}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{A}(q)}\boldsymbol{1}_{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{a\neq b}\sum_{\bar{a}}\frac{e^{i(\delta(q_{a},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}})-\delta(q_{b},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}}))}}{d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(Q-q)}|q_{a}\rangle\langle q_{b}|\,.

(12)

Here
\boldsymbol{1}_{q}
is the identity matrix in the charge
q
subspace of
A
.

Then, the symmetry-resolved second Rényi entropy is

\displaystyle e^{-S_{2}(q)}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}(q)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{A}(q)}+\frac{1}{d_{\bar{A}}(Q-q)}-\frac{1}{d_{\bar{A}}(Q-q)d_{A}(q)}\,,

(13)

where we have taken an average over the random phases
1
1

1

Such dephasing is an analog of the average over unitary operators with the Haar measure.
To be precise, details of the sub-leading term (the third term with a minus sign) under the thermodynamic limit depend on the choice of ensemble. Refer to recent work
Freivogel et al. 2021
;
Stanford et al. 2022
;
Goto et al. 2021
that mentioned these sub-leading terms in the context of AdS/CFT correspondence.
.
In this language, the ordinary second Rényi entropy reads

\displaystyle e^{-S_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}p(q)^{2}e^{-S_{2}(q)}\,.

(14)

Note that the expressions (
13
)-(
14
) in terms of the dimension of each Hilbert space are applicable generally, independent of fine details of the Hilbert space.
In FIG.

and FIG.

, we plot the symmetry-resolved second Rényi entropy (
13
) and the second Rényi entropy (
14
) in our concrete qubit model with (
9
). In particular, the former gives a symmetry-resolved analogue of the Page curve.

III.3
Thermodynamic limit

It is easy to derive an analytic expression of the symmetry-resolved second Rényi entropy (
13
) in the thermodynamic limit defined by
N\to\infty
with
t/N
,
q/N
, and
Q/N
fixed. For this, we introduce a symmetry-resolved analogue of the Page time
t_{*}(q)
for each
q
such that
d_{A}(t_{*}(q))=d_{\bar{A}}(N-t_{*}(q))
. Then, for
t<t_{*}(q)
, we find

\displaystyle S_{2}(q)

\displaystyle\simeq\log d_{A}(q)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{(t+q)}{2}\log\frac{2t}{t+q}+\frac{(t-q)}{2}\log\frac{2t}{t-q}\,.

(15)

Similarly, for
t>t_{*}(q)
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}(q)

\displaystyle\simeq\log d_{\bar{A}}(Q-\bar{q})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{(N-t+Q-q)}{2}\log\frac{2(N-t)}{N-t+Q-q}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(N-t-Q+q)}{2}\log\frac{2(N-t)}{N-t-Q+q}\,.

(16)

IV
Explicit symmetry breaking

Next we generalize the analysis to systems with explicit symmetry breaking. As we have mentioned, violation of charge conservation induces a charge-block-off-diagonal component
\Delta\rho
of the reduced density matrix, which leads to a new contribution to the second Rényi entropy:

\displaystyle e^{-S_{2}}=\text{Tr}\,\rho_{A}^{2}=\sum_{q}p(q)^{2}e^{-S_{2}(q)}+\text{Tr}\left(\Delta\rho^{2}\right)\,,

(17)

where the second term quantifies entanglement between different charge sectors. Our main focus here is to clarify when and how each contribution becomes dominant.

IV.1
Setup

Let us generalize our previous setup (
7
) as follows:

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{q,\bar{q}}\sqrt{w(q,\bar{q})}\sum_{a,\bar{a}}\frac{e^{i\delta(q_{a},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}})}}{\sqrt{d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}}|q_{a}\rangle_{A}\otimes|\bar{q}_{\bar{a}}\rangle_{\bar{A}}\,,

(18)

where the weight function
w(q,\bar{q})\geq 0
gives a probability of finding a state in the charge
(q,\bar{q})
sector and quantifies violation of charge conservation. It is normalized as

\displaystyle\sum_{q,\bar{q}}w(q,\bar{q})=1\,.

(19)

A convenient parametrization of
w(q,\bar{q})
for our calculations in this paper is

\displaystyle w(q,\bar{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}{\cal N}f(q,\bar{q})\,,

(20)

\displaystyle{\cal N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q,\bar{q}}d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})f(q,\bar{q})\,,

(21)

where
{\cal N}
is the normalization constant and
f(q,\bar{q})
is the distribution function controlling the degree of symmetry violation.
For example, the weight function
w(q,\bar{q})
for the charge conserving case (
7
) is localized in the subspace
q+\bar{q}=Q
and the corresponding distribution function is

\displaystyle f(q,\bar{q})

\displaystyle=\delta_{q+\bar{q},Q}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle{\cal N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(Q-q)\equiv d(Q)\,.

(23)

In contrast, if the symmetry is completely broken, every state appears randomly independent of
q+\bar{q}
, so that

\displaystyle f(q,\bar{q})

\displaystyle=1\,,

(24)

\displaystyle{\cal N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q,\bar{q}}d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})\equiv d

(25)

where
d
is the dimension of the total Hilbert space, i.e.,

\displaystyle d=\sum_{Q}d(Q)\,.

(26)

In our qubit model
d=2^{N}
. We will introduce a family of weight functions interpolating the two profiles (
22
) and (
24
), but we keep
w(q,\bar{q})
general to derive a general formula of entropy in the meantime.

IV.2
General formula

First, the reduced density matrix reads

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\bar{A}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=\sum_{q}p(q)\rho_{A}(q)+\Delta\rho

(27)

with
p(q)
,
\rho_{A}(q)
and
\Delta\rho
given by

\displaystyle p(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{q}}w(q,\bar{q})\,,

(28)

\displaystyle\rho_{A}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{q}}{d_{A}(q)}

(29)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{p(q)}\sum_{a\neq b}\sum_{\bar{q},\,\bar{a}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})e^{i(\delta(q_{a},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}})-\delta(q_{b},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}}))}}{d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}|q_{a}\rangle\langle q_{b}|\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\rho

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\neq q^{\prime}}\sum_{a,b}\sum_{\bar{q},\bar{a}}\sqrt{\frac{w(q,\bar{q})w(q^{\prime},\bar{q})}{d_{A}(q)d_{A}(q^{\prime})d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})^{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times e^{i(\delta(q_{a},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}})-\delta(q^{\prime}_{b},\bar{q}_{\bar{a}}))}|q_{a}\rangle\langle q^{\prime}_{b}|\,.

(30)

Then, the two contributions to the second Rényi entropy (
17
) are

\displaystyle\sum_{q}p(q)^{2}e^{-S_{2}(q)}=\sum_{q,\bar{q},\bar{q}^{\prime}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})w(q,\bar{q}^{\prime})}{d_{A}(q)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{q,\bar{q}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})^{2}}{d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}\left[1-\frac{1}{d_{A}(q)}\right]\,,

(31)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\Delta\rho)^{2}=\sum_{q,q^{\prime},\bar{q}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})w(q^{\prime},\bar{q})}{d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}-\sum_{q,\bar{q}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})^{2}}{d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}\,,

(32)

where we took an average over the random phases.
Note that the total contribution is

\displaystyle e^{-S_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q,\bar{q},\bar{q}^{\prime}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})w(q,\bar{q}^{\prime})}{d_{A}(q)}+\sum_{q,q^{\prime},\bar{q}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})w(q^{\prime},\bar{q})}{d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{q,\bar{q}}\frac{w(q,\bar{q})^{2}}{d_{A}(q)d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})}\,.

(33)

IV.3
Illustrative examples

For illustration, let us consider several examples for the weight function
w(q,\bar{q})
. We begin by the extreme case (
24
), where the symmetry is completely broken and time-evolution happens randomly without depending on
q+\bar{q}
. Then, the two contributions (
31
)-(
32
) read

\displaystyle\sum_{q}p(q)^{2}e^{-S_{2}(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{A}}\left[1+\frac{\sum_{q}d_{A}(q)^{2}}{d_{A}d_{\bar{A}}}-\frac{1}{d_{\bar{A}}}\right]\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\Delta\rho)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{\bar{A}}}\left[1-\frac{\sum_{q}d_{A}(q)^{2}}{d_{A}^{2}}\right]\,,

(35)

where we introduced

\displaystyle d_{A}=\sum_{q}d_{A}(q)\,,\quad d_{\bar{A}}=\sum_{\bar{q}}d_{\bar{A}}(\bar{q})\,.

(36)

We also have

\displaystyle e^{-S_{2}}=\frac{1}{d_{A}}+\frac{1}{d_{\bar{A}}}-\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{\bar{A}}}\,.

(37)

Note that (
34
)-(
35
) and (
37
) do not depend on the initial value
Q
since the symmetry is completely broken.
In Fig.
3
, we show the
t
-dependence of the contribution of (
35
) to the entropy (
37
) with a curved line. There we find that the charge-off-diagonal contribution
\text{Tr}(\Delta\rho)^{2}
becomes dominant after the Page time. The reason is actually simple: Before the Page time, diagonal components of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
control the entropy, so that
\Delta\rho
does not play a role. In contrast, after the Page time, off-diagonal components become important. In models without charge conservation, the number of charge-off-diagonal components is much larger than that of off-diagonal components in the charge-diagonal sector, hence the charge-off-diagonal contribution
\text{Tr}(\Delta\rho)^{2}
becomes dominant. See also FIG.
4
for comparison of
S_{2}
in the present model and the symmetry preserving case studied in the previous section.

).

V
Discussion

To conclude, we discuss possible implications of the results of our qubit model for black hole evaporation and symmetries. First, we consider the model with charge conservation in Section
III
. Also, we set the total charge
Q=0
and assume the number of qubits
N\gg 1
, having neutral black holes in mind. At the earlier time
t
satisfying
1\ll t\ll N
, the probability (
11
) of finding a charge
q
state is well approximated as

\displaystyle p(q)\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{q^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\,,\quad\sigma=\sqrt{t}\,,

(39)

which reproduces the Gaussian charge distribution of free Hawking particles. Note that our model did not include neutral particles such as gravitons and photons, but the generalization is straightforward and the result in the qubit model remains qualitatively the same.

Here it is instructive to explore the consequence if we naively extrapolate the Gaussian charge distribution (
39
) to late time. This is analogous to extrapolating semiclassical analysis of Hawking radiation to late time of black hole evaporation. Assuming charge conservation, hence
\Delta\rho=0
, we find that

\displaystyle e^{-S_{2}}=\sum_{q}p(q)^{2}e^{-S_{2}(q)}\leq\sum_{q}p(q)^{2}\sim t^{-1/2}\,.

(40)

Here we have neglected a
t
-independent constant that gives a subleading correction to the entropy, at the most right-hand-side. This implies that the second Rényi entropy monotonically increases in
t
:

\displaystyle S_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log t\,,

(41)

which implies unitarity violation since
S_{2}
is bounded by the system size as

\displaystyle S_{2}\leq\min\left(\log d_{A},\log d_{\bar{A}}\right)\sim\min\left(t,N-t\right)\,.

(42)

Note that unitarity violation in this argument happens much later than the Page time
t\sim N/2
, because the second inequality in (
40
) is a loose bound (recall that we typically have
S_{2}(q)\gg 1
for
t,N\gg 1
) and accordingly the monotonic growth (
41
) is logarithmic.

This observation implies that either (A) the Gaussian charge distribution has to be modified or (B) the assumption of charge conservation has to be relaxed, in order to recover unitarity. See a recent interesting paper
Milekhin and Tajdini 2021
for earlier discussion on this point in the context of two dimensional gravity. There, the charge distribution of the matter field (charged radiation) was essentially given by (
39
). Indeed, our qubit model in Section
III
provides an example for the case (A), where the charge distribution (
11
) is no more Gaussian, once we take into account the finite
N
effects.

On the other hand, our qubit model with the distribution function (
24
) studied in Section
IV
provides an illustrative example for the case (B). There, the charge distribution
p(q)
is Gaussian simply because the symmetry is completely broken and the time-evolution is random in the charge space. However, the new contribution,
\text{Tr}\left(\Delta\rho^{2}\right)
in (
17
), originating from the charge-off-diagonal part
\Delta\rho
of the reduced density matrix is responsible for decreasing the entanglement entropy to make it consistent with unitarity. We also studied a one-parameter family (
38
) of the weight functions that interpolates the two extreme cases.

We believe that our analysis in the qubit toy models offers insights on black hole evaporation and symmetries. We hope to revisit this issue from the gravity side and report our progress in near future.

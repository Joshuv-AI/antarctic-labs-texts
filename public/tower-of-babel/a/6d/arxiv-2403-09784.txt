Eigenvariety for partially classical Hilbert modular forms
Mladen Dimitrov, Chi-Yun Hsu

Abstract
. For each subset of primes in a totally real field above a rational prime p p , there is the notion of partially classical Hilbert modular forms, where the empty set recovers the overconvergent forms and the full set of primes above p p yields classical forms. Given such a set, we p p -adically interpolate the classical modular sheaves to construct families of partially classical Hilbert modular forms with weights varying in appropriate weight spaces and construct the corresponding eigenvariety, generalizing the construction of Andreatta–Iovita–Pilloni–Stevens.



1.
Introduction

Coleman and Mazur
[
CM98
]
constructed the eigencurve, a rigid analytic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
parametrizing finite-slope Hecke eigensystems appearing in the space of overconvergent elliptic modular forms of tame level one. It is endowed with a natural map to a one dimensional weight space parametrizing continuous characters on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
.

A general method for constructing eigenvarieties, known as the
eigenvariety machine
, was formulated by Buzzard
[
Buz07
]
.
Roughly speaking, given an orthonormalizable Banach module
{\mathcal{M}}
over an admissibly covered rigid analytic space
{\mathcal{W}}
and a commutative algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
acting on
{\mathcal{M}}
with a distinguished compact operator
U\in{\mathcal{H}}
, the eigenvariety machine returns an equidimensional eigenvariety
{\mathcal{E}}
with a weight map
{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}
.
The Banach modules, whose importance goes beyond their auxiliary role in the construction of
{\mathcal{E}}
as highlighted by Bellaïche
[
Bel24
]
, come in different flavors.

(1)

Coherent cohomology of degree zero
. The theory of canonical subgroups allows one to
p
-adically interpolate classical modular sheaves and then take global sections. This was done by Andreatta–Iovita–Pilloni in the case of Siegel modular forms
[
AIP15
]
and Hilbert modular forms
[
AIP16
]
, by Brasca
[
Bra16
]
in the case of PEL Shimura varieties with dense ordinary locus, and by Hernandez
[
Her19
]
for Picard modular forms. The key technical ingredient is the vanishing of higher cohomologies coming from the affineness of the ordinary locus, which implies that the relevant cohomology is concentrated in the
\rH^{0}
.
For Hilbert modular forms, there is also a second method of
p
-adically interpolating the classical modular sheaves, using the Hilbert modular varieties at infinite level and the Hodge–Tate period map
[
BHW23
]
.

(2)

Betti cohomology of distribution sheaves
. The spreading of Betti cohomology over multiple degrees
requires a mechanism beyond Buzzard’s eigenvariety machine. This is done for reductive groups whose real points admit discrete series by Urban
[
Urb11
]
by interpolating the alternating trace on the total cohomology, or by
Hansen
[
Han17
]
where the resulting eigenvariety might not be equidimensional.
In the case of Hilbert modular forms, an equidimensional eigenvariety using the middle degree cohomology was constructed by Barrera–Dimitrov–Jorza
[
BDJ22
]
and by Bergdall–Hansen
[
BH24
]
,
both based on slight variations of Stevens’ overconvergent cohomology.

The eigenvarieties mentioned so far, aside from
[
BDJ22
]
, focus on the
p
-adic interpolation of classical automorphic forms over a weight space of maximal dimension, where the distinguished operator
U
is the Hecke operator with respect to the Iwahori subgroup at
p
.
More generally, one would be interested in the
p
-adic interpolation of classical automorphic forms with respect to other parahoric subgroups, which is motivated by constructing families of forms having infinite slope at some of the places above
p
.
This was first investigated by Loeffler
[
Loe11
]
in the situation of reductive groups whose real points are compact modulo center. In this situation the associated modular variety is
0
-dimensional, so the theory has no geometric input. The case of Hilbert modular forms was worked out in
[
BDJ22
]
. Further research
for representations of
\GL(2n)
admitting a Shalika model was taken up by Barrera–Dimitrov–Williams, where one considers the parahoric subgroup corresponding to the maximal
(n,n)
-parabolic.
More generally, Barrera–Williams
[
BSW21
]
constructed parabolic eigenvarieties via overconvergent cohomology.

In this paper, we are interested in the construction of partial Hilbert eigenvarieties using coherent cohomologies.
We
p
-adically interpolate the classical modular sheaves, following the approach of Andreatta–Iovita–Pilloni.
Because the partial ordinary locus is not affine, the
\rH^{0}
cannot be proven to be orthonormalizable.
This prevents from applying Buzzard’s eigenvariety machine to construct the partial eigenvariety.

To address this issue, we propose an alternative path inspired by the Ash–Stevens method.
We consider the total cohomology complex of the modular sheaf.
There is a natural admissible covering of the partial ordinary locus by affinoids, obtained from the pulling back a natural covering on the flag varieties via the Hodge–Tate period map
which is stable by the relevant Hecke operators.
The Čech complex associated to the admissible covering consists of projective modules endowed with a compact operator and is quasi-isomorphic to the total cohomology complex. The Ash–Stevens method, as adapted by Boxer–Pilloni
[
BP21
]
, allows one to
deduce that the total cohomology admits slope decomposition
and to construct the corresponding eigenvariety.

While this works sets the stage for studying the
p
-adic variations of partially classical Hilbert modular forms, including the fascinating case of partial weight
1
, it also leaves many natural questions unanswered. For example, it
remains unclear whether every partially classical form appears in the partial eigenvariety, namely whether it deforms in a partial family. We hope to investigate this and related
questions in a subsequent work.

Let

(7)

The group

for
K/L
a finite extension instead of over

The exact sequence

shows that

\omega_{A[\mathfrak{p}^{n}]}=\omega_{A,\mathfrak{p}}/p^{n}\omega_{A,\mathfrak{p}}

On the other hand, we also have the exact sequence

4.3.
Slope decomposition

Let
(A,A^{+})
be a complete Tate algebra over a non-archimedean field.
Let
M
be an
A
-module and let
T
be an
A
-linear endomorphism of
M
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
h\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. An
h
-slope decomposition of
M
with respect to
T
is a direct sum decomposition of
A
-modules
M=M^{\leqslant h}\oplus M^{>h}

such that

(1)

M=M^{\leqslant h}
and
M^{>h}
are stable under the
T
-action.

(2)

M^{\leqslant h}
is a finite
A
-module

(3)

There is a unitary polynomial
Q\in A[X]
with slope
\leqslant h
such that
Q^{\ast}(T)
is zero on
M^{\leqslant h}

(4)

For any unitary polynomial
Q\in A[X]
with slope
\leqslant h
, the restriction of
Q^{\ast}(T)
to
M^{>h}
is an invertible endomorphism

Let
S=\Spa(A,A^{+})
, and
{\mathcal{M}}
a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-modules.
Let
T\in\End_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Definition 4.6
.

We say that
{\mathcal{M}}
has a slope decomposition with respect to
T
if for any
x\in S
and
h\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, there exists an affinoid neighborhood
U
of
x
in
S
,
h^{\prime}\geqslant h
, and a
T
-stable decomposition of
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
-modules

\left.{\mathcal{M}}\right|_{U}=(\left.{\mathcal{M}}\right|_{U})^{\leqslant h^{\prime}}\oplus(\left.{\mathcal{M}}\right|_{U})^{>h^{\prime}},

where
(\left.{\mathcal{M}}\right|_{U})^{\leqslant h^{\prime}}
is a coherent sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
-modules and for any affinoid open
V=\Spa(B,B^{+})\subseteq U
,

{\mathcal{M}}(V)=(\left.{\mathcal{M}}\right|_{U})^{\leqslant h^{\prime}}(V)\oplus(\left.{\mathcal{M}}\right|_{U})^{>h^{\prime}}(V)

is a
h^{\prime}
-slope decomposition of the
B
-module
{\mathcal{M}}(V)
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}
has a slope decomposition with respect to
T
.
A section
s\in{\mathcal{M}}(U)
is said to
have infinite slope
if for any affinoid open
V\subseteq U
and
h\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
{\mathcal{M}}(V)
admits an
h
-slope decomposition,
\left.s\right|_{V}\in{\mathcal{M}}(V)^{>h}
.
The infinite slope sections form a subsheaf
{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}
.
The
finite slope part of
{\mathcal{M}}
is defined to be
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{fs}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{M}}/{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}
.
We can also construct the spectral variety
{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
as follows:
For any affinoid open
U=\Spa(B,B^{+})
for which there is a slope decomposition
{\mathcal{M}}(U)={\mathcal{M}}(U)^{\leqslant h}\oplus{\mathcal{M}}(U)^{>h}
, define a map
B[X]\rightarrow\End_{B}({\mathcal{M}}(U)^{\leqslant h})
by sending
X
to
T^{-1}
. Let
I_{h}
be the kernel of the map.
Note that
I_{h}
is generated by
Q
guaranteed by (3) in Definition
4.5
.
Let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{U,h}\hookrightarrow S\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
be
\Spa(B[X]/I_{h},(B[X]/I_{h})^{+})
, where
(B[X]/I_{h})^{+}
is the integral closure of
B^{+}
in
B[X]/I_{h}
.
If
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
is an open affinoid, the flatness of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(U)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(U^{\prime})
implies that
{\mathcal{Z}}_{U,h}\times_{U}U^{\prime}={\mathcal{Z}}_{U^{\prime},h}
. If
h^{\prime}\leqslant h
and
{\mathcal{M}}(U)
also has an
h^{\prime}
-slope decomposition, then

is a union of connected components because

Then
{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\coprod_{U,h}{\mathcal{Z}}_{U,h}/\sim
, where
\sim
is the equivalence relation identifying the domain with the image for the injections

{\mathcal{Z}}_{U,h}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Z}}_{U,h^{\prime}}
.
The projection

There is a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{fs}}

{\mathcal{M}}

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{fs}}({\mathcal{Z}}_{U,h})={\mathcal{M}}(U)^{\leqslant h}

4.4.
The partial eigenvariety

Let
A
be an affinoid integral domain.
We say
A
is
relatively factorial
if for any
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}X^{n}\in A\langle X\rangle
with
a_{0}=1
,
(f)
factors uniquely as a product of principal prime ideals
(f_{i})
where each
f_{i}
may be chosen with constant term
1
.
A rigid analytic space
\mathscr{W}
is relatively factorial if it has an admissible covering by relatively factorial affinoids.

A
Fredholm series
is a global section
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(\mathscr{W}\times\mathbb{A}^{1})
such that under the map

{\mathcal{O}}(\mathscr{W}\times\mathbb{A}^{1})\xrightarrow{j^{\ast}}{\mathcal{O}}(\mathscr{W})

induced by
j\colon\mathscr{W}\times\{0\}\rightarrow\mathscr{W}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
we have
j^{\ast}f=1
.
A
Fredholm hypersurface
is a closed immersion
{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq\mathscr{W}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
such that the ideal sheaf of
{\mathcal{Z}}
is generated by a Fredholm series
f
, in which case we write
{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathcal{Z}}(f)
.

Definition 4.8

(
[
Han17
, Definition 4.2.1]
)
.

An
eigenvariety datum
is a tuple
{\mathcal{D}}=(\mathscr{W},{\mathcal{Z}},{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{T}},\psi)
where

•

\mathscr{W}
is a separated, reduced, equidimensional, relatively factorial rigid analytic space,

•

{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq\mathscr{W}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
is a Fredholm hypersurface,

•

{\mathcal{M}}
is a coherent analytic sheaf on
{\mathcal{Z}}
,

•

{\mathcal{T}}
is a commutative
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-algebra, and

•

\psi\colon{\mathcal{T}}\rightarrow\End_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{M}})
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism.

(
[
Han17
, Thm. 4.2.2]
)
.

Given an eigenvariety datum
{\mathcal{D}}
, there exists a separated rigid analytic space

{\mathcal{M}}\cong\pi_{\ast}{\mathcal{M}}^{\dagger}

and

.
The points of
{\mathcal{E}}

.
Let

.

There exists a separated rigid space

\overline{I}

Remark 4.11
.

After the construction of partial eigenvarieties, it is desirable to have functorialities between them for different
I\subseteq\Sigma_{p}
, such as

{\mathcal{E}}_{I}^{\kappa_{\overline{I}},I-\mathrm{fs}}\hookrightarrow(\iota^{G}_{\kappa_{\overline{I}}})^{\ast}{\mathcal{E}}_{\Sigma_{p}}

where
\iota^{G}_{\kappa_{\overline{I}}}
is the inclusion of weight spaces
{\mathcal{W}}_{I}^{G,\kappa_{\overline{I}}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}^{G}
.
Even though we have natural maps between complexes of partially classical Hilbert modular forms by equation (
1
), it does not automatically provide functoriality between the partial eigenvarieties.
This is because the eigenvarieties are constructed by maximal dimension families, and it is not clear whether the specialization map of partially classical Hilbert modular families to lower dimensional families is surjective. Indeed, the corresponding result for overconvergent Hilbert modular forms makes crutial use of the fact that the ordinary locus in the minimal compactification
{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}
is affine. In the finite slope case, one possible approach would be to generalize the partial classicality theorem
[
Hsu22
]
from classical weights to
partially
classical weights, which would allow a comparison of the (finite slope part of the) partial eigenvariety to the full eigenvariety whose geometry is better understood.

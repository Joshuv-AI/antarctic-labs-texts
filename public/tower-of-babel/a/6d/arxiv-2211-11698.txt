Diagonal restriction of Eisenstein series and Kudla-Millson theta lift
Romain Branchereau

Abstract
We consider the Kudla-Millson theta series associated to a quadratic space of signature ( N , N ) (N,N) . By combining a ‘see-saw’ argument with the Siegel-Weil formula, we show that its (regularized) integral along a torus attached to a totally real field of degree N N is the diagonal restriction of an Eisenstein series. It allows us to express the Fourier coefficients of the diagonal restriction as intersection numbers, which generalizes one of the results of Darmon-Pozzi-Vonk [ DPV21 ] to totally real fields.



1
Introduction

The starting point of this paper is a result of Darmon-Pozzi-Vonk, that relates the diagonal restriction of an Eisenstein series to intersection numbers of geodesics. Let
\psi
be a finite order odd Hecke character on the narrow class group
\textrm{Cl}(F)^{+}
of a real quadratic field
F
of discriminant
d_{F}
. To such a character one can associate an Eisenstein series
E(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\psi)
which is a Hilbert modular form of parallel weight one and level
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathscr{O})
. Its diagonal restriction
E(\tau,\tau,\psi)
is a modular form of weight two and level
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. As such, it is zero, since this is the only such form. Instead one can look at the
p
-stabilization
E^{(p)}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\psi)
for some prime
p
. The diagonal restriction
E^{(p)}(\tau,\tau,\psi)
is now a form of weight two and level
\Gamma_{0}(p)
. Moreover, it is non-zero when
p
is split.

Suppose that
p
is a split prime and let
Y_{0}(p)
be the modular curve
\Gamma_{0}(p)\backslash\mathbb{H}
. To a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in
F
and a square root
r
of
d_{F}
modulo
p
one can associate a closed geodesic
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathfrak{a},r}
in
Y_{0}(p)
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}(\psi)
be the
1
-cycle defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Q}}(\psi)\coloneqq\sum_{[\mathfrak{a}]\in\textrm{Cl}(F)^{+}}\psi(\mathfrak{a})(\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathfrak{a},r}+\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathfrak{a},-r})\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(Y_{0}(p)),

(1.1)

and let
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}(0,\infty)
be the image in
Y_{0}(p)
of the geodesic joining
0
to
\infty
. The following identity is proved in
[
DPV21
, Theorem. A]

\displaystyle E^{(p)}\left(\tau,\tau,\psi\right)=L^{(p)}(\psi,0)-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl<\overline{\mathcal{Q}}(0,\infty),T_{n}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}(\psi)\bigr>_{Y_{0}(p)}e^{2i\pi n\tau}

(1.2)

where
L^{(p)}(\psi,0)=(1-\psi(\mathfrak{p}))(1-\psi({\mathfrak{p}^{\sigma}}))L(\psi,0)
and
T_{n}
is a Hecke operator defined by double cosets. In
loc. cit.
the equality (
1.2
) is proved by computing the intersection numbers and comparing them with the Fourier coefficients of
E^{(p)}(\tau,\tau,\psi)
.

The work of Kudla and Millson provides a very natural framework to prove (
1.2
). The goal of this paper is to recover the result of Darmon-Pozzi-Vonk by Kudla-Millson theory and to generalize it to totally real fields. For this we define a torus
C\otimes\psi
such that the integral of the Kudla-Millson theta series over this torus gives the diagonal restriction of the Eisenstein series.

Kudla-Millson theta lift.

The Kudla-Millson theta lift is a lift from the cohomology of locally symmetric spaces attached to orthogonal or unitary groups to modular forms. In our case we want to work with orthogonal groups of certain quadratic spaces of signature
(N,N)
.

Let
F
be a totally real field of degree

with ring of integers
\mathscr{O}
. Let

be the
2
-dimensional quadratic

-space with the quadratic form
Q^{0}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=xy^{\prime}+x^{\prime}y

\mathbf{x}=(x,x^{\prime})
and
\mathbf{y}=(y,y^{\prime})
are vectors in
F^{2}
. At a place

of
\mathbb{Q}
let
F_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}\coloneqq F_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{v}
, where we write

instead of

to emphasize that we view

as a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. We fix a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis of
F
so that we identify
F_{\mathbb{Q}}
with
\mathbb{Q}^{N}
. Let
X=F^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the
2N
-dimensional quadratic
\mathbb{Q}
space with the quadratic form
Q\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}_{F\mid\mathbb{Q}}Q^{0}
. Let
H(\mathbb{Q})\coloneqq\operatorname{SO}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}})
be its orthogonal group. The real vector space
F^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
is of signature
(N,N)
. Let
H(\mathbb{R})^{+}
be the connected component of the identity of its real points
H(\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{SO}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\varphi_{f}
in
\mathscr{S}(F_{\mathbb{A}_{f}}^{2})
be a finite Schwartz function, which is
K_{f}
-invariant by the Weil representation for some open compact subgroup
K_{f}
of
H(\mathbb{A}_{f})
. We define the double coset space

\displaystyle M_{K}\coloneqq H(\mathbb{Q})\backslash H(\mathbb{A})/K,

(1.3)

where
K=K_{\infty}K_{f}
, and
K_{\infty}
is a maximal connected compact subgroup of
H(\mathbb{R})^{+}
that is isomorphic to
\operatorname{SO}(N)\times\operatorname{SO}(N)
. The space
M_{K}
is a disjoint union of locally symmetric spaces of dimension
N^{2}
, in general non-compact. If
M_{K}
is non compact let

be any compactification, for example the Borel-Serre compactification. On

there is a natural family of

of codimension
N

\displaystyle C_{n}(\varphi_{f})\in\mathcal{Z}_{N^{2}-N}(\overline{M_{K}},\partial\overline{M_{K}};\mathbb{R})

indexed by positive rational numbers

. In

,

]
, Kudla and Millson used the work of Weil

\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f})\in\Omega^{N}(M_{K})

. The function

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\longmapsto\int_{C}\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f})

is a holomorphic modular form of weight
N
. Moreover, we have the Fourier decomposition

\displaystyle\int_{C}\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f})=\kappa\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}}\left(\int_{C}\Theta_{n}(\varphi_{f})\right)e^{2in\pi\tau},

where

to the cycle

, when

, and

Since

is compact, the Fourier coefficients for positive

, see Section

.

Limitations of the work of Kudla-Millson.

The integral of
\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f})
along a compact cycle is a modular form of weight
N
whose Fourier coefficients are intersection numbers. If we replace the compact cycle by a non-compact cycle
C
, then the results of Kudla and Millson do not apply and the following problem may arise. First the integral (
1.5
) might diverge. Secondly, even if the integral does converge the resulting function in
\tau
can be non-holomorphic. This is for example what happens in
[
Fun02
]
: the Kudla-Millson theta series associated to a quadratic space of signature
(1,2)
is integrated over the whole modular curve and the resulting integral converges to a non-holomorphic modular form. Thirdly, it is not immediate that the Fourier coefficients
\int_{C}\Theta_{n}(\varphi_{f})
can be interpreted as an intersection number between the two non-compact cycles
C_{n}(\varphi_{f})
and
C
, since a priori such a number is not well-defined; see Remark
3.3
.

A non-compact cycle
C\otimes\psi
.

Let
\psi\colon F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a totally odd unitary Hecke character of finite order. To simplify the notation let us suppose in the introduction that
\psi
is unramified. Hence the finite part of this Hecke character can be viewed as a character on the narrow class group, as in the beginning of the introduction.

Let
\operatorname{SO}(F^{2})\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)
be the orthogonal group of the quadratic space
X_{F}^{0}=F^{2}
with the quadratic form defined above. The group
F^{\times}
can be identified with
\operatorname{SO}(F^{2})
by

\displaystyle F^{\times}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(F^{2})

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&t^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(1.7)

On the other hand the group
\operatorname{SO}(F^{2})
can naturally be embedded in
\operatorname{SO}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(\mathbb{Q})
by restriction of scalars. Composing the two and passing to the adèles gives an embedding

\displaystyle h\colon\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}})\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(\mathbb{A}),

(1.8)

where
F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}=F^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}
. For
K_{f}
large enough we have that
h(\widehat{\mathscr{O}}^{\times})
is contained
K_{f}
. The embedding
h
then induces an immersion of

M_{\mathscr{O}}
in
M_{K}
where

\displaystyle M_{\mathscr{O}}\coloneqq F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}^{N-1}\times\widehat{\mathscr{O}}^{\times}.

(1.9)

The space
M_{\mathscr{O}}
is not connected. There is a bijection between classes in the narrow class group
\textrm{Cl}(F)^{+}
and connected components of
M_{\mathscr{O}}
. More precisely it can be written as a disjoint union

\displaystyle M_{\mathscr{O}}=\bigsqcup_{[\mathfrak{a}]\in\textrm{Cl}(F)^{+}}\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}

(1.10)

where
\Gamma\coloneqq\mathscr{O}^{\times,+}
are the totally positive units in
\mathscr{O}
. The connected components are
N
-dimensional tori. The image by the immersion in
M_{K}
of the connected component of
M_{\mathscr{O}}
corresponding to the ideal class
[\mathfrak{a}]
in
\textrm{Cl}(F)^{+}
is a torus
C_{\mathfrak{a}}
. We twist it by the Hecke character

to obtain a relative cycle

\displaystyle C\otimes\psi\coloneqq\sum_{[\mathfrak{a}]\in\textrm{Cl}(F)^{+}}\psi(\mathfrak{a})C_{\mathfrak{a}}\in\mathcal{Z}_{N}(\overline{M_{K}},\partial\overline{M_{K}};\mathbb{R}),

(1.11)

where we view
\psi
as a character on the ray class group.

In our case, the integral of
\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f})
over
C\otimes\psi
does not converge, but can be regularized by adding a parameter
t^{s}
with
s
a complex number and isolating some singular terms as it is done in
[
Kud82
]
. Moreover, although the cycles
C\otimes\psi
and
C_{n}(\varphi_{f})
are both non-compact we show that the intersection number
\bigl<C_{n}(\varphi_{f}),C\otimes\psi\bigr>
is well-defined, see (
4.81
).

Eisenstein series and diagonal restriction.

For a Hecke character
\psi
as above and a finite Schwartz function
\phi_{f}
in
\mathscr{S}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{A},f})
we can define an Eisenstein series

\displaystyle E(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N},\phi_{f},\psi)={\left.\kern-1.2ptE(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N},\phi_{f},\psi,s)\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{s=0}}

(1.12)

by analytic continuation, where
(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})
is a point in
\mathbb{H}^{N}
; see (
2.46
). It is a Hilbert modular form of parallel weight one. If we take
\tau=\tau_{1}=\cdots=\tau_{N}
, then the diagonal restriction
E(\tau,\dots,\tau,\phi_{f},\psi)
is a modular form of weight
N
.

Let
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
be the isotropic lines spanned by the isotropic vectors
\mathbf{e}_{1}\coloneqq\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut(1,0)}
and
\mathbf{e}_{2}\coloneqq\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut(0,1)}
in
F^{2}
. For a Schwartz function on
F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}_{f}}
let
\varphi_{1}(x)\coloneqq\varphi_{f}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
0\end{pmatrix}
and
\varphi_{2}(y)\coloneqq\varphi_{f}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
y\end{pmatrix}
be the Schwartz functions in
\mathscr{S}(F_{\mathbb{A},f})
obtained by restricting
\varphi_{f}
to
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
.

Theorem
.

(Theorem
4.12
) Let
\varphi_{f}
be any Schwartz function in
\mathscr{S}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}_{f}})
such that
\varphi_{1}
or
\varphi_{2}
vanishes. The diagonal restriction has the Fourier expansion

E(\tau,\dots,\tau,\phi_{f},\psi)=\zeta_{f}(\varphi_{1},\psi^{-1},0)+\zeta_{f}(\varphi_{2},\psi,0)+(-1)^{N}2^{N-1}\kappa\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\bigl<C_{n}(\varphi_{f}),C\otimes\psi\bigr>_{M_{K}}e^{2i\pi n\tau},

where
\phi_{f}=\mathscr{F}\varphi_{f}
is a partial Fourier transform of
\varphi_{f}
and
\zeta_{f}
is a zeta integral (see (
2.25
)).

The equality is proved by showing that both sides of the equality are equal to the regularized integral

2^{N-1}\int_{C\otimes\psi}\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f}).

Note that the intersection takes place in
M_{K}
and is between the
N
-dimensional cycle
C\otimes\psi
of dimension
N
and the cycle
C_{n}(\varphi_{f})
of codimension
N
(and dimension
N^{2}-N
).
The constant term consists of the values at
s=0
of the analytic continuation of two zeta functions converging on two disjoint half planes
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
and
\operatorname{Re}(s)<-1
. Hence the condition of vanishing of
\varphi_{1}
or
\varphi_{2}
is used to make sure that one of the two terms is zero and that an analytic continuation exists.

A seesaw.

The theta series
\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f})
is a theta kernel for the dual pair
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\times\operatorname{SO}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. The theorem can be summarized by the following seesaw

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SO}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}),}

that relates the theta kernel
\Theta_{KM}(\tau,\varphi_{f})
to a theta kernel for
F^{\times}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
. The vertical arrow on the left correspond to the cycle
C\otimes\psi
, that is obtained from the group embedding of
F^{\times}
in
\operatorname{SO}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. The vertical arrow on the right correspond to the diagonal restriction of the Hilbert-Eisenstein series of parallel weight one.

Preliminaries

Intersection numbers.

Let
M
be an arbitrary oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
m
with a Riemannian metric
q_{z}
. Let
o(T_{z}M)
in
\bigwedge^{m}T_{z}M
denote the orientation at a point

in
M
. Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be two immersed oriented submanifolds of complementary dimensions
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, with orientations that we denote by
o(T_{z}M_{1})
and

. Let

be an intersection point in

. We say that the intersection is transversal if
T_{z}M=T_{z}M_{1}\oplus T_{z}M_{2}
, equivalently if

is empty. In this case

is a multiple of

and we define

\displaystyle I_{z}(M_{1},M_{2})\coloneqq\operatorname{sgn}\left(\frac{o(T_{z}M_{1})\wedge o(T_{z}M_{2})}{o(T_{z}M)}\right).

(2.1)

Furthermore, if
M_{1}\cap M_{2}
is finite we define the
topological intersection number
on
M

\displaystyle\bigl<M_{1},M_{2}\bigr>_{M}\coloneqq\sum_{z\in M_{1}\cap M_{2}}I_{z}(M_{1},M_{2}).

(2.2)

In particular, if one of the two submanifolds is compact, then the intersection is finite.

Viewing the Riemannian metric
q_{z}
as a bilinear pairing on

we get a bilinear pairing

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{k}}{\bigwedge}T_{z}M\otimes\sideset{}{{}^{k}}{\bigwedge}T_{z}M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}\otimes w_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{k}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\det q_{z}(v_{i},w_{j})_{ij}

(2.3)

for every positive integer

. Let

be the normal space, which is the orthogonal complement of

with respect to

. We fix an orientation
o(N_{z}M_{1})
of
N_{z}M_{1}
via the rule
o(T_{z}M_{1})\wedge o(N_{z}M_{1})=o(T_{z}M).
One can check that the intersection number is also given by

\displaystyle I_{z}(M_{1},M_{2})=\operatorname{sgn}q_{z}\bigl(o(T_{z}M_{2}),o(N_{z}M_{1})\bigr).

(2.4)

(2.12)

where
\alpha_{w}
is the image of

F_{w}
. It induces an isomorphism between

at the finite places; see

We fix a

-basis of

diving

by

. Then there is a matrix

in
\operatorname{Hom}(F_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},F_{v})\simeq\operatorname{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})

\gamma(\varsigma^{-1}t)=g_{v}^{-1}g(t)g_{v}

as in (

F_{\mathbb{A}}\coloneqq F_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathbb{A}

Haar measures.

Let
F
be a totally real number field and
F_{w}
the completion at a place
w
. We identify
F_{w}
with its dual
F^{\vee}_{w}
by the pairing
\operatorname{tr}_{F_{w}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}}(xy)
. We fix the additive character
\chi_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
defined by

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}e^{2i\pi x}&\textrm{if}\;v=\infty\\
e^{2i\pi\{x\}_{p}}&\textrm{if}\;v=p,\end{cases}

(2.13)

where
\{x\}_{p}
is the fractional part of
x
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
k
be an étale algebra extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
. We can extend this character to a character
\chi_{k}
on
k
by setting
\chi_{k}\coloneqq\chi_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}\circ\operatorname{tr}_{k\mid\mathbb{Q}_{v}}
.

There is a unique choice of Haar measure
dx_{w}
on
F_{w}
which is self dual with respect to
\chi_{F_{w}}
. This is the Haar measure normalized such that
\operatorname{vol}(\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{-1})=1
, where

\mathfrak{d}_{w}^{-1}=\left\{x\in F_{w}|\,\operatorname{tr}_{F_{w}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}}(xy)\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}\,\textrm{for all}\,y\in\mathscr{O}_{w}\right\}

is the inverse different ideal. This is equivalent to
\operatorname{vol}(\mathscr{O}_{w})=\sqrt{\operatorname{N}(\mathfrak{d}_{w})}
. We obtain a measure
dx\coloneqq\prod_{w}dx_{w}
on
\mathbb{A}_{F}
which satisfies
\operatorname{vol}(\widehat{\mathscr{O}})=d_{F}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
For a Schwartz function
\Phi_{w}
in
\mathscr{S}(F_{w})
let
\Phi^{\vee}_{w}
in
\mathscr{S}(F_{w})
be the Fourier transform defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{w}^{\vee}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}\Phi_{w}(u)\chi_{w}(-tu)du,

(2.14)

where
du
is the self-dual measure. On
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
we define the Haar measure

\displaystyle dt_{w}^{\times}\coloneqq m_{w}\frac{dt_{w}}{|t_{w}|_{w}},

(2.15)

where
m_{w}=1
if
w
is archimedean. After fixing a character
\psi
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
the constant
m_{w}
for
w
non-archimedean will be choosen such that
\operatorname{vol}^{\times}(\widehat{U}(\mathfrak{f}))=1
.

Class groups.

For an ideal
\mathfrak{f}
of
\mathscr{O}
let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{f}}(F)
be the set of fractional ideals in

coprime to
\mathfrak{f}
and

the set of principal ideals in

to the finite adèle

(2.16)

Hecke characters and
L
-functions.

Let
\psi\colon F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a finite order Hecke character. We can write
\psi=\psi_{\infty}\otimes\psi_{f}
where the finite part
\psi_{f}
is a character on
F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{\times}
. The conductor of
\psi
is the largest ideal
\mathfrak{f}
of
\mathscr{O}
such that
\psi_{f}
vanishes on

\displaystyle\widehat{U}(\mathfrak{f})=\prod_{w}U_{w}(\mathfrak{f})

(2.17)

where

\displaystyle U_{w}(\mathfrak{f})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathscr{O}_{w}^{\times}&\textrm{if}\;w(\mathfrak{f})=0\\
1+\mathfrak{p}^{w(\mathfrak{f})}&\textrm{if}\;w(\mathfrak{f})>0.\end{cases}

(2.18)

We say that
\psi
is
unramified
if the conductor is
\mathscr{O}

i.e.

\psi_{f}
is trivial on
\widehat{\mathscr{O}}^{\times}
. At an archimedean places
\psi_{\sigma}
is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{\sigma}(t)=|t_{\sigma}|^{a_{\sigma}}\left(\frac{t_{\sigma}}{|t_{\sigma}|}\right)^{b_{\sigma}}

(2.19)

where
a_{\sigma}
can be any complex number and
b_{\sigma}
is
0
or
1
.
We say that
\psi
is
totally odd
if
b_{\sigma}=1
at all places.
The finite part of a Hecke character of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
can also be seen as a character

\displaystyle\psi\colon\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\psi(1,\mathfrak{a})

(2.20)

such that at principal ideals in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{f}}^{+}(F)
we have

\displaystyle\psi((\alpha))=\prod_{\sigma}|\sigma(\alpha)|^{a_{\sigma}}\left(\frac{\sigma(\alpha)}{|\sigma(\alpha)|}\right)^{b_{\sigma}},

(2.21)

where the product is taken over all real places and pairs of complex embeddings. To such a character we can attach an
L
-function

\displaystyle L^{\mathfrak{f}}(\psi,s)\coloneqq\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathscr{O}\\
\gcd(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{a})=1\end{subarray}}}\frac{\psi(\mathfrak{a})}{\operatorname{N}(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}.

(2.22)

It converges for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, admits a meromorphic continuation and a functional equation. Moreover, it has the Euler product

\displaystyle L^{\mathfrak{f}}(\psi,s)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathscr{O}\;\textrm{prime}\\
\gcd(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{f})=1\end{subarray}}L_{\mathfrak{p}}(\psi,s)

(2.23)

where the local factors are
L_{\mathfrak{p}}(\psi,s)\coloneqq(1-\psi(\mathfrak{p})\operatorname{N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1}
. For more on
L
-functions we refer to
[
Iwa19
]
and
[
Bum97
]
.

Zeta functions.

For a Schwartz function
\Phi_{w}
in
\mathscr{S}(F_{w})
we define the
local Zeta integrals

\displaystyle\zeta_{w}(\Phi_{w},\psi,s)\coloneqq\int_{F_{w}^{\times}}\Phi_{w}(t_{w})\psi_{w}(t_{w})\lvert t_{w}\rvert^{s}dt_{w}^{\times},

(2.24)

which converge for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
. For a Schwartz function
\Phi
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
we define the
Zeta integral

\displaystyle\zeta(\Phi,\psi,s)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\Phi(t)\psi(t)\lvert x\rvert^{s}dt^{\times}.

(2.25)

This function converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
by
[
Bum97
, Proposition. 3.1.4 (iii)]
. For a decomposable Schwartz function
\Phi=\otimes\Phi_{w}
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
the decomposition

\displaystyle\zeta(\Phi,\psi,s)=\prod_{w}\zeta_{w}(\Phi_{w},\psi,s)

(2.26)

is valid, where the product is taken over the places of
F
. We will also denote by
\zeta_{f}
the finite part of the zeta integral:

\displaystyle\zeta_{f}(\Phi_{f},\psi,s)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{F,f}}\Phi_{f}(t_{f})\psi_{f}(t_{f})\lvert t_{f}\rvert^{s}dt_{f}^{\times}.

(2.27)

The zeta function
\zeta(\Phi,\psi,s)
admits a meromorphic continuation to the complex plane and a functional equation
\zeta(\Phi,\psi,s)=\zeta(\Phi^{\vee},\psi^{-1},1-s),

where
\Phi^{\vee}
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
is the Fourier transform of
\Phi
defined in (
2.14
). In our case, at an archimedean place
\sigma
, the Schwartz function will be
\Phi_{\sigma}(t)=e^{-\pi t^{2}}t
and the character
\psi_{\sigma}(t)=\operatorname{sgn}(t)
. With this choice we then have

\displaystyle\zeta_{\infty}(\Phi,\psi,s)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(s),

(2.28)

where
\Lambda(s)\coloneqq\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)^{N}\pi^{-\frac{N(1+s)}{2}}
.
At the places where
\Phi_{w}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{O}_{w}}
and the character
\psi
is unramified we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{w}(\Phi,\psi,s)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{vol}^{\times}(\mathscr{O}^{\times}_{w})L_{w}(\psi,s)

(2.29)

where
L_{w}(\psi,s)
are the local factors as in Subsection
2
for
\mathfrak{p}
corresponding to
w
.

Weil representation.

Let
L
be a number field (we will consider
L=\mathbb{Q}
or
L=F
totally real) and
(X_{L},Q)
be a
2m
-dimensional quadratic space over
L
. Let
(W_{L},B)
be the symplectic space
W_{L}=X_{L}\oplus X_{L}
with the symplectic form

\displaystyle B\left(\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{x}\\
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{y}\\
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right)=Q(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})-Q(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}).

(2.30)

Let
k
be the completion of
L
at some place, let
X_{k}\coloneqq X\otimes_{L}k
and
W_{k}\coloneqq W_{L}\otimes_{L}k
. Let
\chi_{k}
be the additive character of
k
defined in Subsection
2
. Let

\displaystyle g=\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}&g_{2}\\
g_{3}&g_{4}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Sp}(W_{k})\subset\operatorname{GL}(W_{k})

(2.31)

with
g_{1}
and
g_{4}
in
\operatorname{GL}(X_{k})
, and
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
in
\operatorname{End}(X_{k})
. For
\varphi
a Schwartz function in
\mathscr{S}(X_{k})
we define the
local (projective) Weil representation

[
MVW87
, p. 40]

\displaystyle\omega\colon\operatorname{Sp}(W_{k})\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{S}(X_{k}))

(2.32)

by the operator

\displaystyle\omega(g)\varphi(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\int_{X_{k}/\ker(g_{3})}\varphi(\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{1}}\mathbf{x}+\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{3}}\mathbf{y})\chi_{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}Q(\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{1}}\mathbf{x},\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{2}}\mathbf{x})+Q(\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{2}}\mathbf{x},\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{3}}\mathbf{y})+\frac{1}{2}Q(\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{3}}\mathbf{y},\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g_{4}}\mathbf{y})\right)d\mu(\mathbf{y}),

(2.33)

where
\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g}
is defined by
Q(g\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=Q(\mathbf{x},\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g}\mathbf{y})
. The Haar measure
d\mu(\mathbf{y})
on
X_{k}/\ker(g_{3})
is the unique one that makes this operator unitary with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm on
\mathscr{S}(X_{k})
. We can then extend the local Weil representation to a global Weil representation
\mathscr{S}(X_{\mathbb{A}_{L}})
of
\operatorname{Sp}(W_{\mathbb{A}_{L}})
.

The operator (
2.33
) only defines a projective representation of
\operatorname{Sp}(W_{k})
in the sense that
\omega(g_{1}g_{2})=c(g_{1},g_{2})\omega(g_{1})\omega(g_{2})
for some complex cocycle
c(g_{1},g_{2})
of norm
1
; see
[
Rao93
]
. However, on some subgroups of
\operatorname{Sp}(W_{L})
the cocycle
c(g_{1},g_{2})
becomes trivial and we have a true representation of those subgroups.

Dual pairs and theta kernels.

A
dual pair

(G,H)
is a pair of subgroups
G
and
H
of
\operatorname{Sp}(W_{L})
that centralize each other in
\operatorname{Sp}(W_{L})
. This allows us to view
G\times H
as a subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}(W_{L})
and to restrict the Weil representation to this product. If
\varphi
is a Schwartz function in
\mathscr{S}(X_{\mathbb{A}_{L}})
we define the theta kernel

\Theta(g,h,\varphi)\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in X(L)}\omega_{os}(g,h)\varphi(\mathbf{x})

for
(g,h)
in
G(\mathbb{A}_{L})\times H(\mathbb{A}_{L})
. A
seesaw pair
is a pair of dual pairs
(G^{0},H^{0})
and
(G,H)
such that
H^{0}
is contained in
H
and
G
is contained in
G^{0}
. Such a pair is represented by a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G.}

The seesaw principle relies on the observation that the theta kernel associated to these two dual pairs pairs agree on the common subgroup
H^{0}\times G
. We will be interested in a seesaw that is obtained by restriction of scalars. It is the example
(2.19)
given by Kudla in
[
Kud84
]
and will be explained in Section
4
.

There are two types of dual pairs that will be involved. Let
k
be one of the completions of
L
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{2m}
a
k
-basis of
X_{k}
. Then we have an isomorphism between
W_{k}
and
k^{4m}
with the symplectic form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&A(Q)\\
-A(Q)&0\end{pmatrix}

(2.34)

where
A(Q)
in
M_{2m}(k)
is the symmetric matrix
(A(Q))_{ij}=Q(f_{i},f_{j})
. In this basis the symplectic group is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sp}(W_{k})=\left\{g\in\operatorname{GL}_{4m}(k)\left|\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g}\begin{pmatrix}&A(Q)\\
-A(Q)&\end{pmatrix}g=\begin{pmatrix}&A(Q)\\
-A(Q)&\end{pmatrix}\right.\right\}.

(2.35)

1.

We view
X=k^{2m}
with the quadratic form
A(Q)
. We can embedd
\operatorname{GL}_{2m}(k)
in
\operatorname{Sp}(W_{k})
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{GL}_{2m}(k)

\displaystyle\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(W_{k})

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}g&\\
&\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g}^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

(2.36)

where
\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\ast\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g}\coloneqq A(Q)^{-1}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g}A(Q)

\operatorname{GL}_{1}(k)
of

\displaystyle\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(W_{k})

Hilbert modular forms and Eisenstein series.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite index subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathscr{O})
. Let
(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})
be a point in
\mathbb{H}^{N}\coloneqq\mathbb{H}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}
. The group
\Gamma
acts on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
by
\gamma(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})=(\gamma_{1}\tau_{N},\dots,\gamma_{N}\tau_{N}),
where
\gamma_{i}
is the image in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
of
\gamma
by the real embedding
\sigma_{i}
. A holomorphic function
f
on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is a Hilbert modular form of weight
(k_{1},\dots,k_{N})
and of level
\Gamma
if

1.

f(\gamma_{1}\tau_{N},\dots,\gamma_{N}\tau_{N})=(c_{1}\tau_{1}+d_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(c_{N}\tau_{N}+d_{N})^{k_{N}}f(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})
, where
\gamma_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{pmatrix}
,

2.

if
F=\mathbb{Q}
then
f
is holomorphic at the cusps.

We say that
f
has parallel weight
k
if all the
k_{i}
’s are equal to
k
. If
f
is a Hilbert modular form then its diagonal restriction
f(\tau,\dots,\tau)
is a modular form of weight
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{N}
and of level
\Gamma\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\phi_{\sigma}
be the Schwartz function in
\mathscr{S}(F_{\sigma}^{2})
defined by

\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{x}_{\sigma})=(-i)e^{-\pi\lvert z_{\sigma}\rvert^{2}}z_{\sigma}

where
\mathbf{x}_{\sigma}=(x_{\sigma},x^{\prime}_{\sigma})
and
z_{\sigma}\coloneqq x_{\sigma}+ix^{\prime}_{\sigma}
. Let
\phi=\phi_{\infty}\otimes\phi_{f}
in
\mathscr{S}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}})
where
\phi_{\infty}=\prod_{\sigma}\phi_{\sigma}
is in
\mathscr{S}(F_{\infty}^{2})
and
\phi_{f}
is an arbitrary finite Schwartz function in
\mathscr{S}(F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}_{f}})
. For a totally odd finite order Hecke character
\psi
we define the function

\displaystyle Z\left(g,\mathbf{x},\phi,\psi,s\right)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\omega_{l}(g,t)\phi(\mathbf{x})\psi(t)\lvert t\rvert^{s}dt^{\times},

(2.44)

which converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
. For
\underline{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})
in
\mathbb{H}^{N}
let

\displaystyle g_{\underline{\tau}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\underline{v}}&\nicefrac{{\underline{u}}}{{\sqrt{\underline{v}}}}\\
0&\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{\underline{v}}}}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F_{\infty})\simeq\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{N}

(2.45)

where
\underline{\tau}=\underline{u}+i\underline{v}
. It is the matrix in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{N}
that sends
(i,\dots,i)
to
(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})
by Möbius transformation. We define the Eisenstein series

\displaystyle E(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N},\phi_{f},\psi,s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\gamma\in P(F)\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)}(v_{1}\cdots v_{N})^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z\left(g_{\underline{\tau}},\gamma_{0}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{0},\phi,\psi,s\right),

(2.46)

where

is the stabilizer of
\mathbf{x}_{0}\coloneqq\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-3.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut(1,0)}
and

in

is one of the following representatives for

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\;\textrm{or}\;\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\;\textrm{with}\;\lambda\in F^{\times}.

(2.47)

The Eisenstein series converges termwise absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>N-1
, see
[
Wie85
, Lemme p. 106]
. It admits an analytic continuation to the whole plane by
[

and we set

\displaystyle E(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N},\phi_{f},\psi)\coloneqq{\left.\kern-1.2ptE(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N},\phi_{f},\psi,s)\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{s=0}}.

Since the Schwartz function decomposes as
\phi=\phi_{\infty}\otimes\phi_{f}
we can decompose the integral

\displaystyle Z\left(g_{\underline{\tau}},\begin{pmatrix}m\\
n\end{pmatrix},\phi,\psi,s\right)=Z_{\infty}\left(g_{\underline{\tau}},\begin{pmatrix}m\\
n\end{pmatrix},\phi,\psi,s\right)Z_{f}\left(1,\begin{pmatrix}m\\
n\end{pmatrix},\phi,\psi,s\right).

(2.49)

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

Z_{\infty}\left(g_{\underline{\tau}},\begin{pmatrix}m\\
n\end{pmatrix},\phi,\psi,s\right)=\frac{\Lambda(1+s)}{\left(i\pi\right)^{N}}\frac{(v_{1}\cdots v_{N})^{\frac{1}{2}+s}}{\operatorname{N}(m-n\underline{\tau})|\operatorname{N}(m-n\underline{\tau})|^{s}}.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{\sigma}=u_{\sigma}+iv_{\sigma}
and
g_{\tau_{\sigma}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{v_{\sigma}}&\nicefrac{{u_{\sigma}}}{{\sqrt{v_{\sigma}}}}\\
0&\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{v_{\sigma}}}}\end{pmatrix}
, then
g_{\tau_{\sigma}}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}m\\
n\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\sigma}\\
\beta_{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}
with
\alpha_{\sigma}=\frac{m-nu_{\sigma}}{\sqrt{v_{\sigma}}}
and
\beta_{\sigma}=n\sqrt{v_{\sigma}}
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{F_{\infty}^{\times}}\omega_{l}(g_{\underline{\tau}})\phi_{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}mt_{\infty}\\
nt_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\psi_{\infty}(t_{\infty})\lvert t_{\infty}\rvert^{1+s}dt^{\times}_{\infty}=2^{N}\prod_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{\sigma}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\sigma}t_{\sigma}\\
\beta_{\sigma}t_{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}t_{\sigma}^{1+s}\frac{dt_{\sigma}}{t_{\sigma}},

(2.50)

since
\phi_{\sigma}
and
\psi_{\sigma}
are both odd functions. At the place
\sigma
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{\sigma}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\sigma}t_{\sigma}\\
\beta_{\sigma}t_{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}t_{\sigma}^{1+s}\frac{dt_{\sigma}}{t_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=-iz_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi t_{\sigma}^{2}|z_{\sigma}|^{2}}t_{\sigma}^{2+s}\frac{dt_{\sigma}}{t_{\sigma}},

(2.51)

where
z_{\sigma}=\alpha_{\sigma}+i\beta_{\sigma}=\frac{\overline{m-n\tau_{\sigma}}}{\sqrt{v_{\sigma}}}
. The right hand side converges for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>-2
to

\displaystyle-\frac{iz_{\sigma}}{2|z_{\sigma}|^{2+s}}\Gamma\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)\pi^{-\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\Gamma\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{v_{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}+s}}{(m-n\tau_{\sigma})|m-n\tau_{\sigma}|^{s}}.

(2.52)

Hence

\displaystyle Z_{\infty}\left(g_{\underline{\tau}},\begin{pmatrix}m\\
n\end{pmatrix},\phi,\psi,s\right)=\frac{\Lambda(1+s)}{\left(i\pi\right)^{N}}\frac{(v_{1}\cdots v_{N})^{\frac{1}{2}+s}}{\operatorname{N}(m-n\underline{\tau})|\operatorname{N}(m-n\underline{\tau})|^{s}}.

(2.53)

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For

c&d\end{pmatrix}
in

and

In particular, if
\Gamma

\Gamma

.

We have

(5.98)

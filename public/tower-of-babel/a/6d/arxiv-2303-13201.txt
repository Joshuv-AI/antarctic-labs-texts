Positivity and base loci of vector bundles revisited
Mihai Fulger, Nabanita Ray

Abstract
. We give equivalent descriptions for the augmented and diminished base loci of vector bundles in characteristic zero. We show that these base loci behave well under pullback, tensor product, and direct sum. Pathological behavior is observed on some nonsplit exact sequences.



1.
Introduction

It is classical to study the geometry of a projective variety
X
by using positivity properties of line bundles on it. The standard generalization to higher rank bundles
E
is to impose the analogous positivity property to the Serre line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
on the projective bundle of 1-dimensional quotients of
E
. This approach has been studied intensely for ampleness or nefness. Its generalizations to bigness and pseudo-effectivity are called
L-bigness
and
L-pseudo-effective
respectively.
However there are stronger generalizations due to Viehweg that we define below.

Fix any ample divisor polarization
A
of
X
. For a vector bundle
E
on
X
we may define the
augmented base locus

\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)
by

X\setminus\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)=\bigl\{x\in X\ \mid\ \exists\ m,q\geqslant 1,\ \Sym^{m}(\Sym^{q}E(-A))\text{ is generated by global sections at }x\bigr\}.

Then say that
E
is
Viehweg-big
(or
V-big
) for short if
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)\neq X
. In rank 1 this agrees with the usual bigness notion for line bundles. For ranks
r>1
however it is a more restrictive notion than L-bigness. For instance a direct sum of line bundles on a curve is L-big precisely when one of the summands is ample, but V-big precisely when all the summands are ample.
We may also define the
diminished base locus

\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
by

X\setminus\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)=\bigl\{x\in X\ \mid\ \forall\ q\geqslant 1\ \exists\ m\geqslant 1,\ \Sym^{m}(\Sym^{q}E(A))\text{ is generated by global sections at }x\bigr\}.

Say that
E
is
V-pseudo-effective
(
V-psef
for short) if
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)\neq X
. Again for line bundles this agrees with the usual pseudo-effectivity notion. However for ranks
r>1
V-pseudo-effecitivity is usually stronger than L-pseudo-effectivity.

These base loci were also introduced and studied in
[
4
]
. They prove that
\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E)=\pi(\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)))
, where
\pi:\mathbb{P}(E)\to X
is the bundle projection. Our work complements theirs.
In our first result we offer equivalent descriptions for these notions of base locus.

Theorem 1.1

(
2.10
,
2.11
)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Fix
x\in X
and
A
an ample Cartier divisor on
X
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exist
q,m\geqslant 1
such that
\bigotimes^{mq}E(-mA)
is generated by global sections at
x
.

(2)

x\not\in\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)
.

(3)

There exist
q,m\geqslant 1
such that
\Sym^{mq}E(-mA)
is generated by global sections at
x
.

Similar equivalent interpretations exist for
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
. The implications
(1)\to(2)\to(3)
are straightforward without restriction on the characteristic. The idea of
(3)\to(1)
is that in characteristic zero tensor powers are direct sums of Schur functors each of which is a direct summand of a tensor product of symmetric powers. This idea was also employed in
[
11
]
to prove that
(1)
and
(2)
are equivalent, and earlier by
[
14
]
to prove that if
E
is an ample vector bundle, then
\bigotimes^{m}E
is eventually globally generated.
The arguments for
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
are new.

As a consequence we see in Corollary
2.12
that
\mathbb{B}_{\pm}
base loci are homogeneous for symmetric (or tensor) powers. In particular
E
is V-big if and only if
\Sym^{m}E
is V-big for some (or any)
m\geqslant 2
. In Example
3.4
we see that the converse fails for L-bigness.

We also study the behavior of base loci under pullback of vector bundles by surjective morphisms:

Theorem 1.2

(
2.31
,
2.30
)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a surjective morphism of normal projective varieties over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
E
be a vector bundle on
Y
. Then

(1)

We have
\mathbb{B}_{+}(f^{*}E)=f^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)\bigcup\text{NF}(f)
, where
\text{NF}(f)
is the union of the curves
C\subset X
that are contracted by
f
.

(2)

If
Y
is smooth, or if
f
is equidimensional, then
\mathbb{B}_{-}(f^{*}E)=f^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
.

In their generality, these results appear new even for line bundles. The focus in the literature has been on finite or birational morphisms. For instance
[
6
]
prove the finite case of
(1)
and
(2)
for line bundles (or
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors) when
X
and
Y
are smooth using complex geometric methods, while
[
2
]
prove with algebraic methods the birational case of
(1)
for line bundles. Our methods are also algebraic. Furthermore we also consider
\mathbb{R}
-twisted bundles.

Finally we observe a pathology of augmented and diminished base loci. A vector bundle
E
is ample (resp. nef) if and only if
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)=\emptyset
(resp.
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)=\emptyset
). This motivates calling
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)
(resp.
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
) the
nonample
(resp.
nonnef
) locus of
E
. In
[
10
]
it is proved that an extension of ample (resp. nef) coherent sheaves is again ample (resp. nef).
In Lemma
2.20
we show that augmented and diminished base loci are “additive” for direct sums.
These suggest that positivity of vector bundles does not worsen in extensions. Unfortunately this is not the case.

We construct Examples
2.21
and
2.22

of short exact sequences
0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0
on surfaces where
\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E)\not\subset\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E^{\prime})\bigcup\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E^{\prime\prime})
. We have not found any example where
E^{\prime}
and
E^{\prime\prime}
are V-big (resp. V-psef), but
E
is not. On the other hand, a bundle that contains an L-big bundle as a subsheaf is necessarily L-big (cf. Proposition
3.2
).

The last section is more informal. We explain how we can define cones of V-big and V-psef vector bundles inside the real numerical ring
N^{*}(X)
of a complex projective manifold.

2.
Augmented and diminished base loci

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a projective variety over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
E
be a locally free sheaf (or
vector bundle) over
X
. For a point
x\in X
,
E_{x}=E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
denotes the stalk of
E
at the point
x
and

E(x)=E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\kappa(x)
the fiber of
E
at
x
, where
\kappa(x)
is the residue field of
x
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{P}(E)\to X
be the projective bundle of rank 1 quotients of the fibers
E(x)
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
be the relative Serre bundle. We have
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m)=S^{m}E
, where we write
S^{m}E=\Sym^{m}E
for short. Denote
\xi\coloneqq c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1))
. Also denote
T^{n}E\coloneqq\bigotimes^{n}E=E^{\otimes n}
.

Definition 2.1

(Base loci)
.

The
base locus
of
E
is

\text{Bs}(E)=\{x\in X\mid H^{0}(X,E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\overset{ev}{\rightarrow}E(x)\text{ is not surjective }\},

the support of the cokernel
Q
of the evaluation map
ev
. The
stable base locus
of
E
is

\mathbb{B}(E)=\bigcap\limits_{m>0}\text{Bs}(S^{m}E).

It is equal to
\text{Bs}(S^{m}E)
for all sufficiently divisible positive
m
.
Let
A
be any ample line bundle on
X
. Then the
augmented base locus
of
E
is

\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)=\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{B}(S^{q}E\otimes A^{-1})=\bigcap\limits_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\text{Bs}(S^{m}S^{q}E\otimes A^{-m}).

The
diminished base locus
of
E
is

\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)=\bigcup\limits_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{B}(S^{q}E\otimes A)=\bigcup\limits_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\text{Bs}(S^{m}S^{q}E\otimes A^{m}).

The definitions are independent of the choice of the polarization
A
. Note that
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)
is always a closed subset of
X
but already for line bundles it is not known whether
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
is closed.
[
17
]
gives an example of an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor class whose diminished base locus is dense and not closed, Again, no such example of a Cartier
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor seems to be known.

For line bundles
L
, the augmented and diminished base loci are homogeneous numerical invariants that are furthermore well defined on the real Néron–Severi space
N^{1}(X)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Say that
E
is

•

V-big (or Viehweg-big)
if
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)\neq X
.

•

L-big
if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
is big on
\mathbb{P}(E)
, i.e.,
\mathbb{B}_{+}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1))\neq\mathbb{P}(E)
.

•

V-psef (or Viehweg-pseudo-effective)
if
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)\neq X
.

•

L-psef
if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
is pseudo-effective on
\mathbb{P}(E)
, i.e.,
\mathbb{B}_{-}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1))\neq\mathbb{P}(E)
.

We use
V-positive
as a fixed choice of either V-big or V-psef. Similarly we use
L-positive
.

Following
[
4
]
, since the diminished base locus is not known to be closed, we can also define
weak positivity
by the requirement that the diminished base locus is not dense in
X
(resp.
\mathbb{P}(E)
). This positivity notion is weaker than bigness and possibly stronger than pseudo-effectivity. We will not use it in our work.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
E
and
F
be two vector bundles. Then

(1)

\text{Bs}(E\oplus F)=\text{Bs}(E)\bigcup\text{Bs}(F)
;

(2)

\text{Bs}(E\otimes F)\subseteq\text{Bs}(E)\bigcup\text{Bs}(F)
;

(3)

\text{Bs}(E)\supseteq\text{Bs}(E^{\otimes m})\supseteq\text{Bs}(S^{m}E)
for
m\geqslant 1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f:E\rightarrow F
be a map between two vector bundles, surjective over an open subset
U\subseteq X
. Then

\text{Bs}(F)\cap U\subseteq\text{Bs}(E)\cap U\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{B}(F)\cap U\subseteq\mathbb{B}(E)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(F)\cap U\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E)\cap U

In particular, if
E
is V-positive, then
F
is also V-positive.

Proof.

The first inclusion is clear. Tensor products and symmetric powers preserve surjections. The other inclusions then follow from the first.
∎

Example 2.5
.

If
X
is a curve and
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)\neq X
, then
E
is nef, so
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)=\emptyset
. Similarly, if
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)\neq X
, then
E
is ample and
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)=\emptyset
. In particular on curves V-bigness is equivalent to ampleness and V-pseudo-effectivity is equivalent to nefness.
(Indeed if some
x
is not in
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
, then for all
q\geqslant 1
there exists
m=m(E,q)
such that
S^{mq}E\otimes A^{m}
is globally generated at
x
. Since
X
is a curve, it follows that
S^{mq}E\otimes A^{m}
is nef, for instance by
[
16
]
*Example 6.4.17. By using the relative Veronese embedding
\mathbb{P}(E)\subset\mathbb{P}(S^{mq}E)
, we deduce that
mq\xi+m\pi^{*}A
is nef for all
q\geqslant 1
. Since the nef cone of
\mathbb{P}(E)
is closed, we obtain that
\xi
is nef, thus so is
E
. See also
[
16
]
*Example 6.2.13.)
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The situation for L-bigness is very different. A direct sum
E=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}
of line bundles on a curve is L-big if and only if one of the
B_{i}
is ample, and it is V-big if and only if all the
B_{i}
are ample.

2.2.
Base loci via tensor powers

In general
S^{mn}E\not\simeq S^{m}S^{n}E\not\simeq S^{n}S^{m}E
. This makes it nontrivial to translate between positivity properties of
E
and positivity properties of
S^{n}E
.
This issue is also observed in
[
19
]
. To bypass it, we use tensor powers
T^{n}E=\bigotimes^{n}E=E^{\otimes n}
. These do satisfy
T^{mn}E\simeq T^{n}T^{m}E\simeq T^{m}T^{n}E
. The price to pay is that they are not related by one geometric object like
\mathbb{P}(E)
.

Definition 2.7
.

Put
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(E)\coloneqq\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}\text{Bs}(T^{m}E)
. For
A
ample put

\mathbb{B}_{+,A}^{\otimes}\coloneqq\bigcap_{q\geqslant 1}\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}\text{Bs}(T^{mq}E\otimes A^{-m})=\bigcap_{q\geqslant 1}\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(T^{q}E\otimes A^{-1})

and

\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}_{-,A}(E)\coloneqq\bigcup_{q\geqslant 1}\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}\text{Bs}(T^{mq}E\otimes A^{m})=\bigcup_{q\geqslant 1}\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(T^{q}E\otimes A).

For sufficiently divisible
m
we have that
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(E)=\text{Bs}(T^{m}E)
by noetherianity. In particular
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(E)=\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(T^{n}E)
for all
n\geqslant 1
.
Due to the natural surjections
T^{mq}E\to S^{m}S^{q}E
, we have
\mathbb{B}(S^{q}E)\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(T^{q}E)=\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(E)
.
Furthermore
\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E)\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}_{\pm,A}(E)
by Lemma
2.4
.
We will prove that (at least in characteristic 0) the inclusion is an equality. First we check that the definition is independent of
A
.

Remark 2.8
.

If
A
and
B
are ample divisors, then
\mathbb{B}_{\pm,A}^{\otimes}(E)=\mathbb{B}_{\pm,B}^{\otimes}(E)
.
(Fix
p
such that
pB-A
is globally generated. For
q\geqslant 0
fix
m=m(q)
sufficiently divisible such that
\text{Bs}(T^{m\cdot(pq)}E\otimes A^{m})=\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}\text{Bs}(T^{m\cdot(pq)}E\otimes A^{m})
and
\text{Bs}(T^{(mp)q}E\otimes B^{mp})=\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}\text{Bs}(T^{mq}E\otimes B^{m})
. Then
\text{Bs}(T^{mpq}E\otimes B^{mp})\subseteq\text{Bs}(T^{m(pq)}E\otimes A^{m})
by Remark
2.3
.
Taking unions over all
q
, we obtain
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}_{-,B}(E)\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}_{-,A}(E)
. The reverse inclusion follows by swapping
A
and
B
. The case of
\mathbb{B}_{+}
is similar and also done in
[
11
]
.)

Definition 2.9
.

Denote then the common value
\mathbb{B}_{\pm}^{\otimes}(E)\coloneqq\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}_{\pm,A}(E)
.

Lemma 2.10
.

\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E)=\mathbb{B}_{\pm}^{\otimes}(E)
.

Proof.

[
11
]
proved the statement about augmented base loci. The argument for the diminished base loci is similar as we show here.
Since
S^{m}S^{q}E
is a quotient of
T^{mq}E
, we deduce that
\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}_{-}(E)
by Remark
2.3
. Conversely, assume that
x\not\in\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)
. Then for every
q\geqslant 1
there exists
m=m(q)\geqslant 1
such that
S^{m}S^{q}E\otimes A^{m}
is generated by global sections at
x
. In particular
S^{mq}E\otimes A^{m}
is also generated by global sections at
x
. Using
\mathbb{B}_{-,A}^{\otimes}(E)=\mathbb{B}_{-,2A}^{\otimes}(E)
from Remark
2.8
, we want to prove that for all
q
we can find
M=M(q)
such that
T^{M\cdotp q}E\otimes A^{2M}
is globally generated at
x
.
Since we restrict ourselves to characteristic zero, we have a direct sum decomposition

T^{Mq}E=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}E

where
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}E
denotes the Schur functor corresponding to the partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\ldots)
with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots=Mq
. Put
r\coloneqq{\rm rk}\,E
. If
\lambda_{r+1}>0
, then
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}E=0
. Thus we can restrict our attention to partitions
\lambda
with at most
r
nonzero parts
\lambda_{i}
.
In characteristic zero we have that
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}E
is also a direct summand of
S^{\lambda_{1}}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{\lambda_{r}}E
by Pieri’s rule (
[
13
]
*(6.8) p. 79)).

Write
\lambda_{i}=a_{i}(mq)+b_{i}
with
0\leqslant b_{i}<mq
. Then
S^{\lambda_{1}}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{\lambda_{r}}E
is a direct summand of
S^{a_{1}mq}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{a_{r}mq}E\otimes(S^{b_{1}}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{b_{r}}E)
by Pieri’s rule.
Let
N=N(q)
be sufficiently large so that
S^{b_{1}}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{b_{r}}E\otimes A^{n}
is globally generated for all possible
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\in\{0,\ldots,mq-1\}^{r}
and all
n\geqslant N
. We have

S^{a_{1}mq}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{a_{r}mq}E\otimes(S^{b_{1}}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{b_{r}}E)\otimes A^{2M}=\bigl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}(S^{a_{i}mq}E\otimes A^{a_{i}m})\bigr)\otimes(S^{b_{1}}E\otimes\ldots\otimes S^{b_{r}}E\otimes A^{2M-m\sum_{i}a_{i}}).

Since
2M-m\sum_{i}a_{i}=M+\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i}b_{i}\geqslant M
, any value
M\geqslant N
will do.
∎

.

Similarly

\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)=\bigcup_{q\geqslant 1}\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}\text{Bs}(S^{mq}E\otimes A^{m})\text{ and }\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)=\bigcap_{q\geqslant 1}\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}\text{Bs}(S^{mq}E\otimes A^{-m}).

\text{Bs}(S^{mq}E\otimes A^{\pm m})

Corollary 2.12

(Homogeneity)
.

We have
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(E)=\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(T^{c}E)
, and

\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E)=\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(S^{c}E)=\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(T^{c}E)

for all
c\geqslant 1
.
In particular
T^{c}E\text{ is V-positive}\Leftrightarrow E\text{ is V-positive}\Leftrightarrow S^{c}E\text{ is V-positive}
for any
c\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

Both
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(E)
and
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes}(T^{c}E)
equal
\text{Bs}(T^{m}E)
for
m
sufficiently divisible, in particular divisible by
c
. All homogeneity properties for
T^{c}E
follow.
From the surjections
T^{c}E\to S^{c}E
we deduce by Lemma
2.4
that
\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(S^{c}E)\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(T^{c}E)=\mathbb{B}_{\pm}(E)
. The reverse inclusion is a consequence of the surjections
T^{mq}S^{c}E\to S^{(mc)q}E
and Remark
2.11
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

We have inclusions

\mathbb{B}_{-}(E)\subseteq\mathbb{B}(E)\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{+}(E).

In particular, if
E
is V-big, then it is also V-psef.

Proof.

For every
q\geqslant 1
and very ample
A
we can find
n
sufficiently divisible such that
\bigcap_{m\geqslant 1}{\rm Bs}(S^{mq}E\otimes A^{\pm m})={\rm Bs}(S^{nq}E\otimes A^{\pm n})
and such that
\mathbb{B}(E)={\rm Bs}(S^{nq}E)
. Clearly
{\rm Bs}(S^{nq}E\otimes A^{n})\subseteq{\rm Bs}(S^{nq}E)\subseteq{\rm Bs}(S^{nq}E\otimes A^{-n})
. Conclude by Remark
2.11
.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\lambda
be any partition with at most
r={\rm rk}\,E
parts. If
E
is a V-positive vector bundle, then so are all Schur functors
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}E
, in particular so is
\det E
.

Proof.

We have surjective maps
T^{r}E\rightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}E
.
The result follows from Lemma
2.4
. Note that
\det E=S_{(1^{r})}E
.
∎

2.3.
Positvity via the Serre line bundle

Classically, positivity properties like ampleness or nefness for
E
can be defined by the corresponding property for
\xi
. We have seen that the situation is not the same for (V-)bigness. Here we investigate the relation between base loci of
E
and of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
x\in X
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
is generated by global sections at all points of
\pi^{-1}\{x\}=\mathbb{P}(E(x))\subset\mathbb{P}(E)
if and only if
E
is globally generated at
x
. In particular
\text{Bs}(E)=\pi(\text{Bs}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)))
.

Proof.

If
E
is globally generated at
x
, then
\pi^{*}E
is globally generated along
\mathbb{P}(E(x))
, hence so is its quotient
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
.
Conversely, if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
is globally generated along
\mathbb{P}(E(x))
,
consider the evaluation sequence
0\to K\to H^{0}(X,E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}(E)
. By assumption there exists
x\in W\subset X
an open neighborhood such that the evaluation sequence is also exact on the right over
\pi^{-1}W
. For
z\in W
we have that the sequence restricts as the exact sequence
0\to K|_{\mathbb{P}(E(z))}\to H^{0}(X,E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E(z))}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E(z))}(1)\to 0
. The resulting map
H^{0}(X,E)\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(E(z)),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E(z))}(1))
must be surjective since no proper subspace of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1))
generates
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
. Note that this fails for
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(m)
with
m>1
1
1

1

This fact was missed in the proof of
[
10
]
*Proposition 6.4, but the proof there can be corrected with the arguments of
[
4
]

For the failure of L-pseudo-effectivity, it is sufficient to check that for all
n>1
and all
\ell>0
, the bundle
(S^{n\ell}E)(\ell)
has no nonzero sections. We have exact sequences

0\to S^{n\ell-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus 3}(-1))(\ell-4)\to S^{n\ell}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus 3}(-1))(\ell)\to S^{n\ell}E(\ell)\to 0.

The totally split bundles on the left and in the middle of the exact sequence are direct sums of line bundles of degrees
2\ell-n\ell-4
and
\ell-n\ell
respectively. Since the intermediary cohomology of all line bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in vanishes, we deduce that
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},S^{n\ell}E(\ell))=0
for all
n>1
and
\ell>0
.∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
E
be a L-psef vector bundle and
F
be an L-big bundle on
X
. Then
E\otimes F
is an L-big vector bundle.

Proof.

The natural surjection
T^{m}(E\otimes F)=T^{m}E\otimes T^{m}F\twoheadrightarrow S^{m}E\otimes S^{m}F
descends to a surjective
S^{m}(E\otimes F)\twoheadrightarrow S^{m}E\otimes S^{m}F
. By dualizing the corresponding surjection for the duals we also obtain a natural inclusion
S^{m}E\otimes S^{m}F\subset S^{m}(E\otimes F)
in characteristic zero. Thus we also have inclusions
(S^{m}E(H))\otimes(S^{m}F(-2H))\subset S^{m}(E\otimes F)(-H)
for any ample
H
on
X
.
∎

4.
Cones of big and of pseudoeffective vector bundles

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
.
Let
N^{*}(X)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}N^{i}(X)
be the real numerical ring of
X
.
Let
E
be a vector bundle. Let
\textup{ch}(E)
be its
Chern character
, and also consider its
log-Chern character
,

\textup{lc}(E)\coloneqq\log\textup{ch}(E)=\log r+\frac{1}{r}c_{1}(E)-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}(2rc_{2}(E)-(r-1)c_{1}^{2})+\ldots\in N^{*}(X).

We have
\textup{ch}(E\otimes F)=\textup{ch}(E)\cdot\textup{ch}(F)
and
\textup{ch}(E\oplus F)=\textup{ch}(E)+\textup{ch}(F)
. Furthermore
\textup{lc}(E\otimes F)=\textup{lc}E+\textup{lc}F
.

[
12
]
considered the convex cones
{\rm Amp}_{\textup{lc}}(X)\subset\Nef_{\textup{lc}}(X)\subseteq N^{*}(X)
generated by
\textup{lc}(E)
where
E
is ample, respectively nef. The first author then proved that the two cones are the same up to closure. A related construction was carried out using the Chern character
ch
.

One can similarly define positive cones of V-positive bundles using characteristic classes of V-big and respectively V-pseudoeffective bundles.
In this way we obtain
{\rm Big}^{V}_{\textup{lc}}(X)
and
{\rm Psef}^{V}_{\textup{lc}}(X)
.
As in
[
12
]
one can prove that they are full-dimensional cones in
N^{*}(X)
that agree up to closure and surject onto the classical cones
{\rm Big}(X)\subset\Nef(X)\subset N^{1}(X)
via projection onto the degree 1 component
N^{*}(X)\to N^{1}(X)
. This is an application of Proposition
2.19
.

These cones exhibit pathological behavior. For instance they are not necessarily pointed cones, ample divisors do not determine interior classes, and the big cone is not necessarily open.

Many of the classical positivity notions for divisors are numerical, e.g., ampleness, nefness, bigness, pseudoeffectivity. This is not the case in higher rank. For instance
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
have the same classes, but the first is not V-positive.

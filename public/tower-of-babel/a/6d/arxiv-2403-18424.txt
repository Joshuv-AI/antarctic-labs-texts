1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set with smooth enough boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider the following mixed local-nonlocal perturbed critical semilinear elliptic problem:

(
\mathrm{P}_{\varepsilon}
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u=\lambda\,u^{p}+u^{2^{\ast}-1}&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\textrm{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\lambda>0
is a positive real parameter,
p\in(0,1)
and

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}:=-\Delta+\varepsilon\,(-\Delta)^{s},\qquad s\in(0,1),\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Along the paper it will sometimes be useful to denote the above problem as (

P
ε

)
λ

to make it clear the choice of the parameter.
Here,
(-\Delta)^{s}
with
s\in(0,1)
denotes the fractional Laplacian which acts in smooth enough functions as

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=2\,\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy=2\,\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\{|x-y|\geq\delta\}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy.

We neglect the normalization constant
C_{n,s}>0
usually appearing in front of the integral because we are not interested in asymptotics as
s\to 1^{-}
.

The above boundary value problem falls in the framework of the so called
concave-convex problems
whose model and most famous example is the one considered in the paper
[
1
]
:

(ABC)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\Delta u=\lambda\,u^{p}+u^{2^{\ast}-1}&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\textrm{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Without any aim of completeness, we mention that (
ABC
) has been generalized to various directions, either allowing for different operators either considering different boundary conditions, see e.g.
[
32
,
24
,
22
,
7
]
.

Before entering into the details related to (

P
ε

)
λ
, let us spend a few more words on
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
.
The operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}
(i.e. with
\varepsilon=1
) is a special instance of a wide more general class of operators whose study began in the ’60s, see
[
14
]
and
[
21
]
for generalizations, in connection with the validity of a maximum principle. On the other hand, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}
can be seen as the infinitesimal generator of a stochastic process obtained as a superposition of a Brownian motion and a Lévy flight, and hence there is a vast literature which establishes several regularity properties

adopting probabilistic techniques
, see e.g.
[
23
]
and the references therein.

More recently, the study of regularity properties related to this operator (and its quasilinear generalizations) has seen an increasing interest, mainly adopting more analytical and PDEs approaches, see, e.g.,

[
8
,
20
,
27
,
28
,
30
,
31
,
37
,
4
,
38
]
.
It is worth mentioning that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
(usually with
\varepsilon=1
) seems to be of interest in biological applications, see, e.g.
[
29
]
and the references therein.

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}
has a also variational nature and it is associated to the energy

E(u):=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}\,dx+\dfrac{1}{2}\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

defined on a suitable space functions for which we refer to Section
2
.
It is clear that

E(\lambda u)=\lambda^{2}E(u),\quad\textrm{for every }\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

but there is a lack of scaling invariance, namely,

E(u_{t})=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+\dfrac{t^{2s-2}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\quad\textrm{for every }t>0,

where
u_{t}(x):=t^{(n-2)/2}u(x)
, which typically preserves the
L^{2}
-gradient norm.

Among other aspects, this phenomenon is responsible of the following fact: the best Sobolev constant in the natural mixed Sobolev inequality is never achieved and it coincides with the one coming from the purely local one. We notice that something similar happens when dealing with mixed Hardy-type inequalities, see
[
11
]
. An interesting consequence of the absence of mixed Aubin-Talenti functions naturally affects PDEs with critical term. Indeed, when dealing with critical variational problems for
-\Delta
, it is well known that a major role is played by the best Sobolev constant, for example being a threshold of validity of Palais-Smale condition, and this is usually achieved by testing the critical equation with the Aubin-Talenti functions. An analogous procedure can be followed in the mixed setting as well, but the lack of invariance previously mentioned can create troubles, see e.g
[
10
,
13
,
25
]
. We stress that something similar may happen with non-homogeneous operators like the
(p,q)
-Laplacian, see
[
33
]
.

Following the approach recently used in
[
13
]
for the case of mild singular and critical mixed problems, we are now interested in the following classical problem:

Find values of the parameter
\lambda
for which (

P
ε

)
λ
admits one or more positive weak solutions.

We refer to Definition
2.2
for the precise definition of
weak solution
of (

P
ε

)
λ
.
Differently from
[
10
]
and
[
13
]
, here we are in presence of a sublinear perturbation (
\lambda\,u^{p}
with
p\in(0,1)
) of the critical term (
u^{2^{\ast}-1}
).

For
\varepsilon=1
, a first step towards an answer has been recently made in
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
where the following has been proved:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(
with
n\geq 3
)
be a bounded open set
with smooth enough boundary, and let
p\in(0,1)
. Then, there exists
\Lambda>0

such that

a)

problem
(\mathrm{P}_{1})_{\lambda}
admits at least one weak solution for every
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
;

b)

problem
(\mathrm{P}_{1})_{\lambda}
does not admit weak solutions
for every
\lambda>\Lambda
.

Moreover, for
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
, the solution is minimal and increasing w.r.t. to
\lambda
.

We stress that the the above results actually holds in the supercritical case as well.
For sake of completeness, we recall that the existence of the first positive solution in
[
2
]
is obtained by means of a sub/supersolution scheme as in
[
1
]
. We also note that, if we fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, the above result holds for a threshold
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
now depending on
\varepsilon
.

We also mention that concave-convex problems in the mixed local-nonlocal setting has been previously studied in
[
26
]
: there, the leading operator is a combination of
p
-Laplacian and fractional
p
-Laplacian and the existence of weak solutions is proved under the assumption
p>n
, being the interest of the authors to study the limiting problem as
p\to+\infty
.

Our first result is somehow complementary to Theorem
1.1
stated above and it is the counterpart of
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
in the mixed local-nonlocal setting.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be fixed. Then, there exists a constant
M_{\varepsilon}>0
such that, for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{\varepsilon})
, problem (

P
ε

)
λ,ε
has at most one solution
u_{\lambda}
with

\|u_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq M_{\varepsilon}.

Note that the if the unique solution of Theorem
1.2
exists, it has to be the minimal one found in Theorem
1.1
.

Following the seminal paper
[
1
]
, it is natural to wonder whether a second positive solution exists or not for all
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
. A similar question has been answered in
[
36
]
for sublinear perturbations of a subcritical nonlinearity, so leaving the critical case unsolved.

To better understand the difficulties one has to face in the case of mixed operators like
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
, let us briefly recall the method employed in
[
1
]
to find a second positive solution of (
ABC
):

i)

show that the first solution found is a minimizer of the functional naturally associated with (
ABC
) in
C^{1}
-topology;

ii)

thanks to the famous result by Brezis and Nirenberg
[
18
]
, one inherits that the first solution is actually a minimizer in the
H^{1}
-topology;

iii)

prove the validity of the Palais-Smale condition under a certain value proportional to the best Sobolev constant, here making use of the Aubin-Talenti functions;

iv)

find a second positive solution of mountain-pass-type.

Despite i) can still be proved in our case, the above scheme finds a first obstacle once a result like the one in
[
18
]
is needed. To this aim, and thanks to the recent regularity results proved in
[
3
]
and
[
38
]
, we establish the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

(1.1)

\Phi(u):=\dfrac{1}{2}\,\rho(u)^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx,

where
F(x,u):=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,s)\,ds
and with
f
satisfying that

(1.2)

|f(x,u)|\leq C_{f}\,(1+|u|^{2^{*}-1}).

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
be a local minimizer of
\Phi
in the
C^{1}
-topology. Then,
u_{0}
is a local minimizer in the
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}
-topology.

Theorem
1.3
allows to achieve ii) as well,
but a new difficulty arises once iii) has to be faced, the main reason
being the lack of scaling invariance, and its consequences on the mixed Sobolev inequality, previously discussed.
A similar issue occurred in the recent
[
13
]
when dealing with mild singular problems, and it is the main
reason to consider
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
instead of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
because, roughly speaking, the presence of the parameter
\varepsilon
allows us to adjust the lack of scaling invariance, at least for small enough
\varepsilon
. In this
way, we are able to fully follow the above scheme proving the following

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(
with
n\geq 3
)
be a bounded open set
with smooth enough boundary, and let
p\in(0,1)
be fixed.

Then, there exist
\lambda_{\star}>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)

such that problem (

P
ε

)
λ

admits a second positive solutions for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and for every
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\star})
.

As it clearly appears from the statement, there is a first price to pay once considering
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
, because the existence is not proved for every
\lambda
. Less evident is the second fee we have to pay. Let us briefly describe it: the first solution found in Theorem
1.1
can still be found once
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is replaced with
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
but now it implicitly depends on
\varepsilon
and this forces to carefully keep track of the dependence on
\varepsilon
of many ingredients like
L^{\infty}
-bounds and so on.

As in
[
13
]
, the key result to be proved is Lemma
5.2
and we want to explicit mention that a careful inspection of its proof shows that a better result can be obtained, this time with big restrictions on both the dimension
n
and the fractional parameter
s
:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
n=3
and
s\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
. Then problem (

P
ε

)
λ
admits at least two positive weak solutions for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
.

Finally, once the existence of (at least) two solutions of problem
(

P
ε

)
λ
has been established (at least for
\varepsilon
small enough),
by exploiting Theorem
1.2
and by proceeding essentially
as in
[
1
]
we can prove the following qualitative result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be fixed, and assume that
\Omega
is
star-shaped
. Then,

\text{$\|v_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to+\infty$ as $\lambda\to 0^{+}$},

where

v_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
is
any weak solution of

problem (

P
ε

)
λ
distinct from its minimal solution

u_{\lambda,\varepsilon}

(
see Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
2.6
)
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some preliminary definitions and results which
will be used throughout the rest of the paper.
First of all, we review some basic properties
of the fractional Sobolev spaces, and
we properly
introduce the adequate functional setting for the study
of mixed local-nonlocal operators; we then give the precise definition
of
weak sub/supersolution
of problem (

P
ε

)
λ
,
and we establish some qualitative properties of the solutions of this problem
(provided they exist).
Finally, we spend a few words the applicability
of Theorem
1.1
(established in the case
when
\varepsilon=1
) to our
\varepsilon
-dependent operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
.

i)  Sobolev spaces of fractional order.

We begin this section by collecting a few basic facts
fractional Sobolev spaces, which are naturally
related to the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
; we refer
to
[
34
]
for a thorough introduction to this topic.

Let
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary open set.
The

fractional Sobolev space

H^{s}(\mathcal{O})
(of order
s\in(0,1)
) is the subset
of
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
defined
as follows

H^{s}(\mathcal{O}):=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}):\,[u]^{2}_{s,\mathcal{O}}=\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty\Big\}.

We then list the few basic properties of
H^{s}(\mathcal{O})

we will exploit in this paper.

a)

H^{s}(\mathcal{O})
is a real Hilbert space, with the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle_{s,\,\mathcal{O}}:=\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\qquad(u,v\in H^{s}(\mathcal{O})).

b)

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
is a
linear subspace of
H^{s}(\mathcal{O})
; in addition,
in the particular case when
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
dense
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

c)

If
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
or if
\mathcal{O}
has
bounded boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}\in C^{0,1}
,
we have the
continuous embedding

H^{1}(\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\mathcal{O})
,
that is, there exists
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}(n,s)>0
s.t.

(2.1)

\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leq\mathbf{c}\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\quad\text{for every $u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{O})$}.

In particular, if
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a

bounded open set
(with no regularity assumptions on
\partial\mathcal{O}
) and if

u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
, setting
\hat{u}=u\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{O}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

(2.2)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|\hat{u}(x)-\hat{u}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leq\beta\,\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

where
\beta>0
is a suitable constant depending on
n,s
and on
|\Omega|
. Here
and throughout,
|\cdot|
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

ii)  The space
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

Now we have briefly recalled some basic facts
regarding fractional Sobolev spaces, we are in a position
to introduce the adequate functional setting for the study of mixed local-nonlocal operators.

Let then
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
.
We define the space

\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
as the completion
of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the

global norm

\rho(u):=\left(\||\nabla u|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+[u]^{2}_{s,\mathbb{R}^{n}}\right)^{1/2},\qquad u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Due to its relevance in the sequel, we also introduce a distinguished notation
for the
cone of the non-negative functions
in
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
: we set

\mathcal{X}^{1,2}_{+}(\Omega):=\{u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega):\,\text{$u\geq 0$ a.e.\,in $\Omega$}\}.

Since this norm
\rho
is induced by the scalar product

\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\langle u,v\rangle_{s,\mathbb{R}^{n}}

(where
\cdot
denotes the usual scalar product in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
), the space
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)

is a real
Hilbert space
; most importantly, since
\Omega
is bounded
and
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz, by combining the above
(
2.1
) with the classical Poincaré
inequality we infer that

\vartheta^{-1}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\rho(u)\leq\vartheta\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\text{for every $u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)$},

where
\vartheta>1
is a suitable constant depending on
n,s
and on
|\Omega|
.
Thus,
\rho(\cdot)
and the full
H^{1}
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

are
actually equivalent
on the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, so that

(2.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)&=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\,\,\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\\
&=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\text{$u|_{\Omega}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)$ and
$u\equiv 0$ a.e.\,in $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}\}.\end{split}

We explicitly observe that, on account of (
2.3
),
the functions in
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
naturally
satisfy the nonlocal Dirichlet condition
prescribed in problem (

P
ε

)
λ
, that is,

\text{$u\equiv 0$ a.e.\,in $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$ for every $u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)$}.

Remark 2.1

(Properties of the space
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
)
.

For a future reference, we list in this remark some properties
of the function space
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
which will be repeatedly
exploited in the rest of the paper.

1)

Since both
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
are
closed
under the
maximum/minimum operation, it is readily seen that

u_{\pm}\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)\quad\text{for every $u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)$},

where
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
and
u_{-}=\max\{-u,0\}
.

2)

Since we are assuming that
\partial\Omega
is smooth, from
(
2.3
) we see that a function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

belongs to the space
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)

if and only if

\text{$u\equiv 0$ pointwise on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}.

3)

On account of (
2.2
), for every

u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
we have

(2.4)

[u]_{s,\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leq\beta\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

As a consequence,
the norm
\rho
is
globally equivalent
on
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
to the

H_{0}^{1}
-norm: in fact, by (
2.4
) there exists a constant
\Theta>0
such that

(2.5)

\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\rho(u)\leq\Theta\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for every $u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)$}.

4)

By the (local) Sobolev inequality, for every
u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)

we have

\displaystyle S_{n}\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\rho(u)^{2}.

This, together with Hölder’s inequality
(recall that
\Omega
is
bounded
), proves the
continuous embedding

\text{$\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)$ for every $1\leq q\leq 2^{*}$}.

5)

By combining (
2.5
) with the
compact embedding
of

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
(holding true for every
1\leq q<2^{*}
),
we derive that also the embedding

\text{$\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)$ is compact
for every $1\leq q<2^{*}$}.

As a consequence, if
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, it is possible
to find a (unique) function
u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that (up to a sub-sequence)

a)

u_{n}\to u
weakly in
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
;

b)

u_{n}\to u

strongly
in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq q<2^{*}
;

c)

u_{n}\to u
pointwise a.e. in
\Omega
.

Clearly, since both
u_{n}
(for all
n\geq 1
) and
u

identically vanish

out of
\Omega
, see
(
2.3
), we can replace

\Omega
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the above assertions b)-c).

We will exploit these properties without any further comment.

We now observe that, since the leading operator
of (

P
ε

)
λ
is given by the
\varepsilon
-dependent operator

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=-\Delta+\varepsilon(-\Delta)^{s}
, it follows
that the bilinear form naturally associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
is the following

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\varepsilon\,\langle u,v\rangle_{s,\mathbb{R}^{n}};

in its turn, this form
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
induces the
\varepsilon
-dependent quadratic form

\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)=\||\nabla u|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\varepsilon\,[u]^{2}_{s}\qquad(\text{for $u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)$}).

While in this perspective it should seem more
natural
to use
the
norm
\rho_{\varepsilon}
in place of
\rho
on the space
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
,
it is readily seen that these two norms
are indeed
equivalent on
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)

(and equivalent to the
H_{0}^{1}
-norm),
uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon
:
in fact, taking into account (
2.5
) (and since
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
), we have

(2.6)

\||\nabla u|\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\rho(u)\leq\Theta\||\nabla u|\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

for some
\Theta>0
only depending on
n,s
. On account of (
2.6
),
we can indifferently use
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\cdot),\,\rho(\cdot)
and the
H_{0}^{1}
-norm
to define the topology of the space
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, and this choice
does not produce any dependence on
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Notation.
We conclude this second part of the section

Theorem 2.3

Theorem 2.4

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n>3
and
h=h(x)\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
p>\tfrac{n}{2}
.
Assume that there exists a weak solution

u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
of the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u=h&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\textrm{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Then
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\,\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

for some positive constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

It is enough to closely follow the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 4.7]
in the case

\varepsilon=1
. Take
\delta>0
to be chosen later on and define the functions

\tilde{u}:=\dfrac{\delta\,u}{K}\quad\textrm{and }\quad\tilde{v}:=\dfrac{\delta\,h}{K},

where
K=K(u,h,\Omega,p,n):=\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
. One can now perform the same computations made in
[
8
, Theorem 4.7]
, getting rid of the nonlocal part thanks to
[
8
, Equation (4.47)]
, reaching the following:

(2.15)

u(x)\leq\dfrac{K}{\delta}=\dfrac{\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\delta},\quad\textrm{for every }x\in\Omega,

where
\delta>0
has been chosen conveniently small but independently of
\varepsilon
.
Now, by Sobolev and Hölder inequalities, and testing the equation with
u
itself, we get

\mathcal{S}_{n}\,\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|^{1/q}\,\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\Omega)}\,\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
\tfrac{1}{2^{\ast}}+\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{q}=1
are the Hölder exponents. Therefore,

\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\Omega)}\leq\dfrac{|\Omega|^{1/q}}{\mathcal{S}_{n}}\,\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

which combined with (
2.15
) gives the desired conclusion.
∎

3.
Purely sublinear problems and the proof of Theorem

In this short section we investigate

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u=\lambda\,u^{p}&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\

To be more precise, we establish/review some results which will be used (as fundamental tools)
in the proof of Theorem

, and we demonstrate our first main result,
namely Theorem

.

To begin with, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be fixed. Moreover, let
\lambda>0
and

.

Then, the following assertions hold.

i)

There exists a
unique weak solution

of problem (

), which is the unique

Moreover,

.

ii)

w_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for every

, and

\partial_{\nu}w_{\lambda,\varepsilon}<0
on
\partial\Omega
.

iii)

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0

with the following property:

given any ball

and any

we have

w_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\geq c_{2}\quad\text{a.e.\,on $B_{r}(x_{0})$},

for some constant

independent of
\varepsilon
.

We explicitly stress that, since problem (

) is of the form
(
2.7
) (with

), the definition of weak solution of
(

)
is given in Definition

Proof.

We prove separately the three assertions.

i)  To begin with, by using
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
with the choice

(notice that this theorem certainly
applies to the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
, the role of
\varepsilon
being immaterial)
we immediately get the existence of a
unique weak solution

w_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)

of problem (
3.1
); moreover, owing to

[

, Proposition 6.2]
(which also
can be applied to the

satisfies

ii)  Since

clearly satisfies
the growth condition (
1.2
) (with

),
the regularity up the boundary of

follows
from Theorem

in

, allows us to apply

iii)  The proof of this assertion
is split into three steps, and it
is very similar to that of

[

, Proposition 3.7]
; we present it in detail
for the sake of completeness.

Step 1).
In this first step, we prove that

\|w_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,c\quad\text{for all $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$}.

for some constant

only depending on

and on

.

Indeed, since

of problem (

as a test

\displaystyle\|w_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}

4.
Proof of Theorem

In this section we show that the famous result
[

, Theorem 1]
still holds in the mixed local-nonlocal setting.
The proof is a quite simple adaptation of the original proof by Brezis and Nirenberg and the major role is played by the recent regularity results established in
[

]
and
[

]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By assumption,
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
is a local minimizer for
\Phi
in the
C^{1}
-topology; this
means, precisely, that there exists some
r>0
such that

(4.1)

\Phi(u_{0})\leq\Phi(u_{0}+v),\quad\textrm{for all }v\in C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\,\textrm{ s.t. }\|v\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)}\leq r.

It follows that the Euler-Lagrange equation satisfied by
u_{0}
is

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}v=f(x,v)&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\\
v=0&\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega;\end{cases}

as a consequence, since (by assumption)
f
satisfies the growth condition (
1.2
), from
Theorem
2.3
we derive that
u_{0}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In view of this fact,
from now on we may assume without loss of generality that

u_{0}=0.

We now argue by contradiction, assuming that
u_{0}

is not a local minimizer for
\Phi
in the
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}
-topology
; thus,
for every
\delta>0
there exists
v_{\delta}\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that

(4.2)

\rho_{\varepsilon}(v_{\delta})<\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi(v_{\delta})<\Phi(0).

We explicitly notice that use of
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
to define an open neighborhood
of
u_{0}=0
in (
4.2
) is motivated by the fact that, since
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
by (
2.6
) we have

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\rho(u)\leq\Theta\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

for some
\Theta>0
only depending on
n,s
; as a consequence,
the norm
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
is globally equivalent to
\rho(\cdot)
(and to the

H_{0}^{1}
-norm), uniformly w.r.t.
\varepsilon
.

Now, since the closed set
K_{\delta}=\{u\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega):\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\delta\}\subseteq

is weakly sequentially compact, and since
\Phi

is weakly lower semincontinuous and bounded from below
(by the growth assumption (
1.2
) on
f
),
we can find
w_{\delta}\in K_{\delta}
such that

\Phi(w_{\delta})=\min_{u\in K_{\delta}}\Phi(u).

In particular, by (
4.2
) we get

(4.3)

\Phi(w_{\delta})\leq\Phi(v_{\delta})<\Phi(0)\quad\text{for every $\delta>0$}.

We then claim that

(4.4)

\text{$\mathrm{i)}\,\,w_{\delta}\in C_{0}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})$ \quad and\quad$\mathrm{ii)}\,\,\lim_{\delta\to 0}w_{\delta}=0$ in the $C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})$-topology}.

Taking this claim for granted for a moment, we can immediately
complete the proof of the theorem: in fact, owing to
(
4.4
), we see that there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\|w_{\delta}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq r\quad\text{for every $0<\delta<\delta_{0}$},

where

is as in (
4.1
); thus, by (
4.1
)
(and since we are assuming

) we infer that

\Phi(0)\leq\Phi(w_{\delta})
for every
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
,
but this clearly contradicts (
4.3
).

We then turn to prove (
4.4
), and we proceed by steps.

Step I).
In this first step we prove that there exist
\delta_{0}>0

and a constant
c_{0}>0
,
independent of
\delta
, such that
the following estimate holds:

(4.5)

\|w_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{0}\quad\text{for every $0<\delta<\delta_{0}$}.

To this end we first notice that, since

is a global minimizer for

\Phi
on
K_{\delta}

by the Lagrange Multiplier Theorem, there exists
\mu_{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\delta},\varphi)-\int_{\Omega}f(x,w_{\delta})\varphi\,dx=\mu_{\delta}\,\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\delta},\varphi)\quad\textrm{for all }\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega).

If

(that is, if

then

; if, instead,

\rho_{\varepsilon}(v_{\delta})=\delta
(that is, if

in (

\displaystyle\mu_{\delta}\,\delta^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\delta},w_{\delta})=\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\delta},w_{\delta})-\int_{\Omega}f(x,w_{\delta})w_{\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle=\Phi(w_{\delta})+\int_{\Omega}F(x,w_{\delta})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x,w_{\delta})w_{\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle(\text{using \eqref{eq:tocontradictH1C1}, and since $\Phi(0)=0$})

where we have used the growth condition (

)

Lemma 5.3

With (

To this end, we arbitrarily choose a sub-sequence

Let then

thus, since

\text{$0\leq|f_{j_{k}}|^{\vartheta}|\varphi|\leq h^{\vartheta}|\varphi|$ a.e.\,in $A$ and for every $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Now, by (

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}(v_{k}-v_{\lambda})^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\|v_{k}-v_{\lambda}\|^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\big({\rho_{\varepsilon}}(v_{k})^{2}-{\rho_{\varepsilon}}(v_{\lambda})^{2}\big)-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\big(\|v_{k}\|^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}-\|v\|^{2^{*}}_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}\big)+o(1)\\[4.26773pt]
&\qquad=I_{\lambda}(v_{k})-I_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})+o(1)=\gamma_{0}-I_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})+o(1)\\
&\qquad<\frac{1}{n}S_{n}^{n/2}-\delta_{0},\end{split}

for some
\delta_{0}>0
such that
1/n\,S_{n}^{n/2}-\delta_{0}>0
(provided that
k
is large enough).

Gathering (
5.46
)-(
5.48
),
and arguing as in
[
39
, Proposition 3.1]
, it is then easy to recognize that

v_{k}\to v_{\lambda}

strongly in
\mathcal{X}^{1,2}(\Omega)
;
as a consequence, by the continuity of the functional

I_{\lambda}
and by (
5.43
)-(
5.47
), we get

I_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})<\gamma_{0}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}I_{\lambda}(v_{k})=I_{\lambda}(v_{\lambda}),

and this finally proves that
v_{\lambda}\not\equiv u_{\lambda}
, as desired.
∎

ε

)

)

.

Proof (of Theorem
1.6
).

We closely follow the approach in
[
1
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Arguing by contradiction,
we assume that there exist a sequence
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}\subseteq(0,\Lambda_{\varepsilon})

and a family
\{v_{j}=v_{\lambda_{j},\varepsilon}\}_{j}
of weak solutions
of problem (

P
ε

)
{}_{\lambda_{j}}
such that

a)

\lambda_{j}\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
;

b)

v_{j}\not\equiv\bar{u}_{\lambda_{j},\varepsilon}
(where
\bar{u}_{\lambda_{j},\varepsilon}

is the unique minimal solution of (

P
ε

)
{}_{\lambda_{j}}
), and

(6.1)

for some constant
\mathbf{c}>0
(possibly depending on
\varepsilon
).

In particular, we have

(6.2)

\begin{split}\bullet)\,\,&\text{$v_{j}>0$ pointwise in $\Omega$ and $u=0$ pointwise in $\partial\Omega$};\\
\bullet)\,\,&\text{$\partial_{\nu}v_{j}<0$ pointwise on $\partial\Omega$}\end{split}

We then observe that, since
v_{j}
is a weak solution of
problem (

P
ε

)
{}_{\lambda_{j}}

ε

)
{}_{\lambda_{j}}

1.2

On the other hand, by combining (

Two-current correlations in the pion on the lattice Preprint: DESY 18-105, CERN-TH-2018-147
Gunnar S. Bali, Peter C. Bruns, Luca Castagnini, Markus Diehl, Jonathan R. Gaunt, Benjamin Gläßle, Andreas Schäfer, André Sternbeck, and Christian Zimmermann

Abstract
We perform a systematic study of the correlation functions of two quark currents in a pion using lattice QCD. We obtain good signals for all but one of the relevant Wick contractions of quark fields. We investigate the quark mass dependence of our results and test the importance of correlations between the quark and the antiquark in the pion. Our lattice data are compared with predictions from chiral perturbation theory.

Introduction

The study of hadronic form factors has a long history in QCD and remains an active field of study. Defined from the matrix elements of single currents, form factors contain information about the spatial distribution of charge inside a hadron. Going beyond this, the matrix elements of
two
currents at different points in space are sensitive to charge
correlations
and thus yield qualitatively new information about QCD bound states and their many-body structure.

Hadronic matrix elements of one or two currents can be calculated in lattice QCD. In fact, it was realised long ago that two-current correlators on the lattice can be regarded as gauge invariant probes of hadron wave functions. Following earlier work

Last but not least, increased computing power has allowed us to use larger lattices and a smaller pion mass than previous studies. Indeed, we will see that finite volume effects can be appreciable for the quantities we are interested in.

At large distances between the two currents, the correlation functions we consider can be evaluated in chiral perturbation theory, which describes the low-energy limit of QCD in terms of mesons and their interactions. A leading-order calculation, supplemented by an estimate of higher orders using resonance exchange graphs, has been performed in

An extension of the work presented here allows us to compute matrix elements that, as explained in

An additional challenge in this case is that one must compute correlation functions for all vector or tensor components of the inserted currents and then extract their twist-two part. Corresponding results for the pion will be presented in a forthcoming paper. The ultimate goal is to extend such studies to double parton distributions in the nucleon, which is of acute interest for the precise description of high-multiplicity final states at the LHC and at possible future hadron colliders.

The lattice computation of the matrix elements (
1
) involves a considerable number of different Wick contractions between the quark fields in the two currents and in the pion source and sink. This is to be contrasted with, e.g., the computation of single-current matrix elements, where there is only one connected and one disconnected graph. In the present section, we give details about the lattice contractions for two-current correlators, their relation with the physical pion matrix elements, and their implementation in the lattice simulation.

3.1
Lattice contractions and their symmetry properties

The different lattice contractions are pictorially represented in figure

2
. We have two fully connected graphs
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, a graph
A
in which the pion in annihilated by one current and created again by the second current, two graphs
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
with one disconnected quark loop, and a doubly disconnected graph
D
with two quark loops.

The symbols
C_{1}^{ij}(y)
,
C_{2}^{ij}(y)
, etc. denote contributions to the physical matrix elements (
1
), i.e. it is understood that

•

the contractions shown are averaged over the gauge ensemble and divided by the ensemble average of the pion two-point function,

•

the two currents are inserted at the same time slice
\tau
(i.e.
y^{0}=0
), and the ratio of four-point and two-point functions is evaluated at a plateau in
\tau
. To use time reversal invariance, the plateau must be symmetric between source and sink times,

•

both source and sink are projected on definite momentum
p
. By translation invariance one can thus shift their spatial positions by a common amount.

Details are given in section
3.3
below. For brevity, we do not indicate the dependence of
C_{1}^{ij}(y)
etc. on the pion momentum
p
.

\displaystyle M^{ij}(y)

\displaystyle\text{for $M=A,C_{2}$},

3.2
Physical matrix elements

The form of the relation between pion matrix elements and lattice contractions depends on the product of
C
parities. Using the shorthand notation

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{ij}(y)\,,

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigl[C_{2}^{ij}(y)+C_{2}^{ji}(-y)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigl[A^{ij}(y)+A^{ji}(-y)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigl[S_{1}^{ij}(y)+S_{1}^{ji}(-y)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=S_{2}^{ij}(y)\,,

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=D^{ij}(y)\,,

(28)

we have for
\eta_{C}^{ij}=+1

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{dd}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}+\bigl[2S_{1}+D\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=\bigl[2C_{2}+S_{2}\bigr]+\bigl[2S_{1}+D\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{0}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{dd}(0)\ket{\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle=\bigl[2S_{1}+D\bigr]-A\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{0}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}+\bigl[2S_{1}+D\bigr]+\bigl[2C_{2}+S_{2}\bigr]+A\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{0}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{du}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{ud}(0)\ket{\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle={}-C_{1}+\bigl[2C_{2}+S_{2}\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{-}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{du}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{du}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=2C_{1}+2A\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{du}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{ud}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=2C_{2}^{ij}(y)+S_{2}+A^{ij}(y)\,,

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\mskip 1.5mu\bra{\pi^{0}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{du}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}+\bigl[C_{2}^{ij}(y)-C_{2}^{ji}(-y)\bigr]+A^{ij}(y)\,.

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}F_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}+\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{dd}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}+2\mskip 1.5mu\bigl[2S_{1}+D\bigr]+[2C_{2}+S_{2}]\,,

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}F_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}-\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{dd}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle={}-C_{1}+\bigl[2C_{2}+S_{2}\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}F_{2}(y)

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bra{\pi^{-}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{du}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{du}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}+A\,,

(30)

where
F_{0}
,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are the isospin amplitudes defined in (

of matrix elements can e.g. be isolated by

\displaystyle iF_{3}(y)

\displaystyle=\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{du}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{ud}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}-\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{ud}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{du}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=2\bigl[C_{2}^{ij}(y)-C_{2}^{ji}(-y)\bigr]+\bigl[A^{ij}(y)-A^{ji}(-y)\bigr]\,.

(31)

In the present work, we only study the real valued combinations in (

For
\eta_{C}^{ij}=-1
we have three nonzero lattice contractions,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
S_{1}
, and two
independent matrix elements, which may be taken as

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{dd}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}+S_{1}^{ij}(y)-S_{1}^{ji}(y)\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=2C_{2}+S_{1}^{ij}(y)+S_{1}^{ji}(y)\,.

Among all contractions,

has typically the smallest statistical uncertainties in the lattice simulation and thus plays a special role. The above equations show that it does not appear in isolation in any physical pion matrix element. However,

can be regarded as “approximately physical” in the following sense. Consider QCD with

(instead of

) mass degenerate quarks, where SU(4) flavour symmetry is exact. It is easy to see that in this theory

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uc}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{j}^{sd}(0)\ket{D_{s}^{+}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{ij}(y)\,,

(33)

where the quark content of the
D_{s}^{+}
meson is

C_{1}
computed in our study does not exactly correspond to this matrix element, because our lattice action has
n_{F}=2
and not
n_{F}=4
sea quark flavours. We therefore must interpret (

Details on the contractions.

We now give details on the calculation of the different lattice contractions for

\displaystyle S_{1}^{ij,\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{a^{3}}{V}\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu}\left\langle G_{3}^{i,\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}(\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu+\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau,t)\,L_{1}^{j}(\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau)\right\rangle+\frac{a^{3}}{V}\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu}\left\langle G_{3}^{i,\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}(\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau,t)\right\rangle\left\langle\!\left\langle L_{1}^{j}(\tau)\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\,,

\displaystyle L_{1}^{j}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{ins}}}\sum_{\ell}^{N^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{ins}}}\left[\eta_{\tau}^{\dagger(\ell)}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau)\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{j}\mskip 1.5mu\chi_{\tau}^{(\ell)}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau)\right]

(44)

and

\displaystyle\left\langle\!\left\langle L_{1}^{j}(\tau)\right\rangle\!\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{3}}{V}\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu}\left\langle L_{1}^{j}(\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau)\right\rangle\,.

(45)

Note that for symmetry reasons, the vacuum subtraction for

with the sequential propagator

\displaystyle\Bigl(\mathcal{D}{X}_{0\mskip 1.5mut,\mathrm{oet}}^{(\ell),\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}\Bigr)(\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu^{\prime},t^{\prime})

The contraction

has a similar structure as the connected part of

\tau
. To reduce statistical noise, we make the following improvements. We consider the hopping parameter expansion of the propagator
Thron:1997iy
;
Bali:2005fu
;
Bali:2009hu
, writing
\mathcal{D}=(\mathbb{1}-H)/(2\kappa)
with the hopping term
H

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{D}^{-1}=2\kappa\left(\mathbb{1}-H\right)^{-1}=2\kappa\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{n}=2\kappa\!\!\sum_{n=0}^{n(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)-1}H^{n}+2\kappa\!\!\sum_{n=n(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)}^{\infty}H^{n}\,.

(48)

Since for the Wilson-clover action,

involves at most nearest neighbours on the lattice, one needs at least

(49)

hopping terms to obtain a non-vanishing contribution to the propagator from a point
\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu
to
\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu+\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu
on a periodic lattice of spatial size
La

48

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=2\kappa\!\!\sum_{n=n(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)}^{\infty}H^{n}=H^{n(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)}\;2\kappa\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{n}=H^{n(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)}\mathcal{M}

for propagation from
\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu
to
\vec{\mskip 0.5mux}\mskip 1.5mu+\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu

(50)

where in the last step we used (

Taking

instead of

itself, we implicitly omit those terms that do not contribute to the propagation but may add to the stochastic noise. The expression to be evaluated for the

\displaystyle C_{2}^{ij,\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu,\tau,t)

3.4
Renormalisation

We convert all lattice currents, which are defined in the lattice
scheme at a lattice spacing
a
, into the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme at the renormalisation scale
\mu=2\operatorname{GeV}
. The corresponding renormalisation constants depend on the gauge coupling
g^{2}=6/\beta
, where in our case
\beta=5.29
. For currents with nonzero anomalous dimension, such as in the scalar and pseudoscalar cases, there is also a dependence on
a\mu
. The conversion factors used for the correlator (
36
) of currents
i
and
j
read

\displaystyle R_{ij}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{Z}_{i}\mskip 1.5mu\tilde{Z}_{j}\mskip 1.5muR_{ij}^{\mathrm{lat}}

\displaystyle\text{ \penalty\ with\penalty\ }\tilde{Z}_{i}

\displaystyle=Z^{\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$}}_{i}\left(1+am_{q}\mskip 1.5mub_{i}\right)\,,

(57)

see e.g.
Bali:2014nma
. Here we have included the coefficients
b_{i}
for the mass-dependent order
a
improvement. They become particularly relevant at the charm quark mass. In the vector and axial vector cases, there are additional mass-independent order
a
improvement terms (accompanied by
c_{V}
and
c_{A}
, respectively), which we ignore in the present work. Note that some of the matrix elements listed in section
3.2
receive contributions from flavour singlet current combinations. In these cases our renormalisation procedure should be regarded as approximate, because we do not take into account operator mixing.

The renormalisation factors

(updated in

have been computed in lattice perturbation theory
Capitani:2000xi
;
Sint:1997jx
;
Taniguchi:1998pf

(58)

with an uncertainty of about 5%. We can determine

4.2
Anisotropy effects

In the continuum, the correlation functions
\langle V^{0}V^{0}\rangle,\langle A^{0}A^{0}\rangle,\langle SS\rangle
and
\langle PP\rangle
in a pion at rest can only depend on the modulus
y=|\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu|
. Lattice regularisation breaks rotational invariance, so that an anisotropy in the
\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu
dependence of our correlators is a clear indication of lattice artefacts.

At large distances
y/a\sim L/2
we see clear signs of anisotropy, as shown in figure
6
(a) to (c). As explained in detail in
Burkardt:1994pw

Benefiting from the larger size of our lattices, we take a less sophisticated approach here and consider only data for distances
\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu

The points thus combined are not necessarily related by an exact lattice symmetry operation, and we checked that restricting the data combination to points that are equivalent on the lattice gives results consistent with those of the more inclusive combination.

A different type of anisotropy effect appears at small

5.3
Test of factorisation

The two-current matrix elements
\langle V^{0}V^{0}\rangle
and
\langle SS\rangle
quantify the distribution of vector or scalar charges at two separate points in the pion. By contrast, the corresponding single-current matrix elements
\bra{\pi(p^{\prime})}V^{0}\ket{\pi(p)}
and
\bra{\pi(p^{\prime})}S\ket{\pi(p)}
give the vector or scalar charge distribution at one point in the pion if one performs a Fourier transform from the momentum transfer
\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu-\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu^{\prime}
to three-dimensional space.
3
3

3

As is well known and discussed e.g. in
Burkardt:2000za
, this simple interpretation receives relativistic corrections, which become relevant for distances of order
1/m_{\pi}
or below. Our treatment here is fully relativistic and not affected by this limitation.

In the absence of correlations, the two-current distribution can be computed from the single-current one. A detailed analysis of this has already been given in
Burkardt:1994pw
, with some focus on the non-relativistic limit. A way to express the expectation for the two-current distribution in the absence of correlations is to insert a complete set of intermediate states between the two currents and to keep only the pion ground state
Burkardt:1994pw
;
Diehl:2011yj
. In momentum space, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{ii}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu^{2})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}{y}\;e^{i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu}\,\bra{\pi^{+}(p)}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\mathcal{O}_{i}^{dd}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}(p)}

\displaystyle\overset{?}{=}\int d^{3}{y}\;e^{i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu}\int\frac{d^{3}{p}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}\mskip 1.5mu2p^{\prime\mskip 1.5mu0}}\bra{\pi^{+}(p)}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(y)\ket{\pi^{+}(p^{\prime})}\,\bra{\pi^{+}(p^{\prime})}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{dd}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}(p)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{i}_{C}}{2E_{q}}\;\bigl|\bra{\pi^{+}(E_{q},-\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}(p)}\bigr|^{2}\,,

(63)

where

and it is understood that
\vec{\mskip 0.5mup}\mskip 1.5mu=\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu
. The question mark above the equality sign indicates an assumption, which will be tested in the following.
In the last step of (

\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{dd}\ket{\pi^{+}}=\eta^{i}_{C}\,\bra{\pi^{+}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{uu}\ket{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}(p^{\prime})}V_{uu}^{\mu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}(p)}

\displaystyle=(p^{\prime}+p)^{\mu}\mskip 1.5muF_{V}(Q^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\bra{\pi^{+}(p^{\prime})}S_{uu}(0)\ket{\pi^{+}(p)}

\displaystyle=F_{S}(Q^{2})

with

\displaystyle\overset{?}{=}{}\frac{(m_{\pi}+E_{q})^{2}}{2E_{q}}\,\Bigl[\mskip 1.5muF_{V}\bigl(2m_{\pi}E_{q}-2m_{\pi}^{2}\bigr)\mskip 1.5mu\Bigr]^{2}\,,

5.4
Root mean square radii

In figure
12
we noted a clear difference between the decrease of the two-current correlators for light and heavy quarks, reflecting the different size of the pseudoscalar bound state in the two cases. In the present section, we make this observation more quantitative. We focus on the current combinations
\langle V^{0}V^{0}\rangle
and
\langle A^{0}A^{0}\rangle
and on graph
C_{1}
, which can be interpreted as a physical matrix element in the sense discussed at the end of section
3.2
.
Following
Wilcox:1986ge
and later work, we quantify the characteristic length scale of the two-current correlators via root mean square (rms) radii
r_{VV}
and
r_{AA}
, defined by

\displaystyle r^{2}_{VV}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int d^{3}y\;{y}^{2}\,C_{1}^{\mskip 1.5muVV}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)}{\int d^{3}y\;C_{1}^{\mskip 1.5muVV}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)}

(71)

for
\langle V^{0}V^{0}\rangle
and in analogy for
\langle A^{0}A^{0}\rangle
. For the sake of legibility, we omit superscripts
0
in the labels
VV
and
AA
in the remainder of this section.

One might think of evaluating (
71
) from the ratio

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu\in E(\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu)}y^{2}\,C_{1}^{\mskip 1.5muVV}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)\Bigg/\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu\in E(\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu)}C_{1}^{\mskip 1.5muVV}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)

(72)

of lattice data, with the sum running over all
\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu
in the elementary lattice cell
E(\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu)
defined by
-L/2<y^{i}/a\leq L/2
for
i=1,2,3
. However, the weight
y^{2}
in the numerator of (
72
) enhances the importance of large distances to the extent that for our
L=40
lattice, the contribution of points with
y/a>L/2
to the sum over all
\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu
amounts to 39% for light and to 16% for strange quarks. At such distances, the effect of periodic images discussed in section
4.2
is considerable. Moreover, the slow decrease of
y^{2}\,C_{1}^{\mskip 1.5muVV}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)
with
y
implies that the numerator of (
72
) misses important contributions from distances
y/a>\sqrt{3}\mskip 1.5muL/2
that are not included in the elementary cell.

In the following we present a method to deal with this situation. Let us assume that the correlation function computed on the lattice has the form

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\text{lat}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu+\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5muLa)\,,

(73)

where
C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)
is the correlation function in the physical limit and the terms with
\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5mu\neq\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu
are due to the periodic boundary conditions on the lattice
Burkardt:1994pw
. The rms radii can be computed from the Fourier transform

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}y\;e^{i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu}\,C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)

(74)

as

\displaystyle r^{2}

\displaystyle={}-6\mskip 1.5mu\bigl[\widetilde{C}_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu)\bigr]^{-1}\,\frac{d\mskip 1.5mu\widetilde{C}_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)}{d\mskip 1.5muq^{2}}\biggl|_{q^{2}=0}\,,

(75)

where we used that
\widetilde{C}_{1}
depends on
\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu
only via
q=|\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu|
. On the lattice we can evaluate the discrete Fourier transform

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}^{\text{lat}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)

\displaystyle=a^{3}\!\!\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu\in E(\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu)}e^{i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu}\,C_{1}^{\text{lat}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)

\displaystyle\text{ for }\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu=\vec{\mskip 0.5muk}\mskip 1.5mup_{0}

(76)

with
\vec{\mskip 0.5muk}\mskip 1.5mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
and
p_{0}=2\pi/(La)
. We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1}^{\text{lat}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)

\displaystyle=a^{3}\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\;\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu\in E(\smash{\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu})}e^{i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu}\,C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu+\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5muLa)=a^{3}\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}\;\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu\in E(\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5mu)}e^{i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu-i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5muLa}\,C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)

\displaystyle=a^{3}\!\!\sum_{\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu/a\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}e^{i\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu}\,C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)\,,

(77)

where
E(\vec{\mskip 0.5mun}\mskip 1.5mu)
denotes the shifted lattice cell defined by
-L/2<y^{i}/a+n^{i}\leq L/2
for
i=1,2,3
, and in the last step we have used the condition
\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu=\vec{\mskip 0.5muk}\mskip 1.5mup_{0}
. We thus find that for
a\to 0
the discrete Fourier transform (
76
) becomes equal to the infinite-volume expression (
74
). The periodic images included in
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{\text{lat}}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)
provide the contribution of the infinite-volume correlator
C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)
at distances outside the elementary cell
E(\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu)
.

To evaluate the rms radii from (
75
), we need to construct a smooth function of
\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu
out of
\widetilde{C}_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)
at the points
\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu=\vec{\mskip 0.5muk}\mskip 1.5mup_{0}
. Whilst direct computation of (
71
) would require us to
extrapolate

C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)
to large values of
y
and to remove the contributions from periodic images, we now need to
interpolate
between discrete values of
\widetilde{C}_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)
in the vicinity of
\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu=\vec{\mskip 0.5mu0}\mskip 1.5mu
. The reliability of this interpolation is of course higher for a higher density of points
\vec{\mskip 0.5muk}\mskip 1.5mup_{0}=2\pi\vec{\mskip 0.5muk}\mskip 1.5mu/(La)
and thus for larger physical lattice size
La
.

In figure
16
we show
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{\mskip 1.5muVV}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)
obtained from our lattice data for light and charm quarks. The results for strange quarks and those for
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{\mskip 1.5muAA}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu)
look qualitatively similar.
At values
q\sim\pi/a
we see a clear anisotropy in the Fourier transform (which in the continuum limit depends on the length but not on the direction of
\vec{\mskip 0.5muq}\mskip 1.5mu
). This is to be expected and reflects discretisation effects in
C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)
at distances
y
of a few lattice units

. With decreasing

, the anisotropy gradually disappears and we have a very clear and smooth signal.

7
Summary

This paper contains a detailed study of two-current correlation functions in the pion in lattice QCD. We use two gauge ensembles with a pion mass of
m_{\pi}\approx 300\operatorname{MeV}
, a lattice spacing of
a\approx 0.07\operatorname{fm}
and spatial extensions of
L=32
and
L=40
, respectively. Additional results for heavier quarks (strange or charm) are obtained in a partially quenched setup.

We derive several general theoretical results concerning symmetry properties and the connection between lattice graphs and physical amplitudes. In the remainder of our study, we focus on correlation functions of two currents
V^{0}
,
A^{0}
,
S
or
P
in a pion at rest.
In our lattice simulations, we make extensive use of stochastic sources, which allows us to obtain satisfactory (and in many cases excellent) signals for all lattice contractions except for the double disconnected graph
D
, where vacuum subtractions entail large cancellations and a loss of the signal in statistical noise.

We study a number of lattice artefacts in our data. The comparison of results for two different time differences between the pion source and sink gives no indication for large contributions from excited states. Comparing the Lorentz invariant correlation functions
\langle SS\rangle
and
\langle PP\rangle
for different pion momenta, we find that our lattice results are fully consistent with boost invariance. We observe several types of anisotropic behaviour, indicating a loss of rotational invariance due to either the periodic boundary conditions or to discretisation effects. We find that these effects are significantly reduced by selecting distances
\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu
between the two currents that are close to the spatial diagonals of the lattice. Finally, the comparison of data for two lattices shows moderate volume effects in several channels, but large ones in others.

In the connected graph
C_{1}
, the “valence quark” and “valence antiquark” in the pion are probed individually by the two currents. One might naively expect this contraction to be dominant. An important result of our study is that this is not true: we find that several lattice contractions are important for
m_{\pi}\approx 300\operatorname{MeV}
. This concerns especially the connected graph
C_{2}
with two current insertions on the same quark line, but in the case of
\langle SS\rangle
and
\langle PP\rangle
also the disconnected graph
S_{1}
and the annihilation contribution
A
. For heavier quark masses, the importance of these graphs is reduced, although
C_{2}
remains prominent in
\langle A^{0}A^{0}\rangle
and
\langle PP\rangle
even for charm quarks. Of course, not every contraction contributes to every physical matrix element, as shown in (
3.2
).

For the connected graph
C_{1}
, we test the hypothesis that the two-current density can be computed in terms of the single-current density, assuming the absence of correlation effects between the quark and antiquark in the pion. We find that this hypothesis clearly fails in a large range of current separations
\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu
, both for the vector and for the scalar current.

We also extract rms radii
r_{VV}
and
r_{AA}
of the correlator
C_{1}(\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu)
for the vector and for the axial current. We find that a Fourier transform w.r.t.
\vec{\mskip 0.5muy}\mskip 1.5mu
mitigates finite-size effects. The radius
r_{VV}
shows a clear decrease with the quark mass. Interestingly, it turns out that
r_{AA}<r_{VV}
for light and strange quarks.

Combining the different lattice graphs to physical amplitudes in an isospin basis, we can compare our lattice results with the computation in chiral perturbation theory performed in
Bruns:2015yto
, which includes the contributions from the leading-order chiral Lagrangian and an estimate of higher-order contributions using resonance exchange graphs. We find rather good agreement between the lattice data and the chiral calculation for vector and axial currents, whereas the comparison for scalar and pseudoscalar currents is poor in five out of six channels. Since the exchanged resonances are
\rho
and
a_{1}
in the former case and
\sigma
,
a_{0}
,
\eta
in the latter, our findings are consistent with the hypothesis that the exchange of the lowest-mass vector and axial vector mesons often gives a good estimate of higher orders in the chiral expansion, whereas the same does not necessarily hold for the exchange of spin-zero mesons. This is in line with the conclusions drawn in
Donoghue:1988ed
;
Ecker:1988te
and the general success of models based on vector meson dominance.

The lattice methods presented in this work are suitable for the study of two-current correlators that can be related to double parton distributions in the pion, thus providing a connection with an active field of research in collider physics. This will be presented in a forthcoming paper.

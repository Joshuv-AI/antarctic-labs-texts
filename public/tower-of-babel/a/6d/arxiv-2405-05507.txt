Abstract
. Using Galois representations, we analyze fields of definition of cyclic isogenies on elliptic curves to prove the following uniformity result: for any number field F F which has no rational CM, under GRH there exists an effectively computable constant B := B ⁡ ( F ) ∈ ℤ + B:=B(F)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+} such that for any finite extension L / F L/F whose degree [ L : F ] [L:F] is coprime to B B , one has for all elliptic curves E / F E_{/F} that any L L -rational isogeny on E E is F F -rational. For any number field F F , under GRH we also prove results for the mod- ℓ \ell Galois representations of non-CM elliptic curves with an F F -rational isogeny of uniformly large prime degree ℓ \ell .

Introduction

Given a number field
F
, it is of great interest to understand isogenies of elliptic curves which are rational over
F
, in analogy to understanding rational torsion points. When
F=\mathbb{Q}
, a classic theorem of Mazur
[
Maz78
]
shows that the only possible prime degrees of
\mathbb{Q}
-rational isogenies are
\ell\in\{2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,37,43,67,163\}
. Following Mazur’s work, Kenku
[
Ken82
]
has shown that all possible degrees of
\mathbb{Q}
-rational isogenies with cyclic kernel are the integers
n\in[1,19]\cup\{21,25,27,37,43,67,163\}
.
For general number fields
F
, if
F
has no rational
complex multiplication
(henceforth CM), then there exists a uniform, effectively computable bound on degrees of
F
-rational isogenies under the
generalized Riemann hypothesis
(henceforth GRH); see
[
LV14
]
and
[
Ban23
]
. However, it is currently unknown whether a uniform bound can hold unconditionally.

Nonetheless, one can prove uniformity results on isogenies of elliptic curves defined over number fields
of a particular degree
. For example, if
L
is a number field of odd degree, then for any elliptic curve
E_{/L}
that is
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isogenous to a
\mathbb{Q}
-rational elliptic curve, all
L
-rational isogenies on
E
of prime degree
\ell
must have
\ell\leq 37
; see
[
CN21
, Theorem 1.1]
. This type of result can be leveraged to study torsion from the family of
\mathbb{Q}
-curves, see also
[
BN
]
.

In this paper, we prove uniformity results for cyclic isogenies of elliptic curves base-changed from
F
. We study such isogenies through analyzing the curve’s Galois representations. Recall that for an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
F
, for each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
there exists an action of
G_{F}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F)
on the
n
-torsion subgroup
E[n]
of
E
. The information of this Galois action is encoded in the
mod-
n
Galois representation of
E
.
Once we fix a basis of
E[n]
, we can write this representation as
\rho_{E,n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
. This action extends to an action on the set of cyclic order
n
subgroups of
E
. If
\phi
is an isogeny from
E
whose kernel
C
is cyclic, then the the field of definition of
\phi
, written as
F(C)
, is the fixed field of the stabilizer of
C
. One key observation towards understanding the rationality of
\phi
is that the degree
[F(C):F]
is equal to the size of the orbit of
C
under the action of
G_{F}
, and so
\phi
is
F
-rational if and only if the orbit of
C
is trivial.

Studying rationality of isogenies through this action of Galois can be useful. As an example, combinative work of several mathematicians, culminating in recent work of Furio and Lombardo
[
FL
]
, shows that if
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
is a non-CM elliptic curve, then for any prime
\ell>37
, the mod-
\ell
Galois representation
\rho_{E,\ell}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is either surjective,
or its image equals the normalizer of a nonsplit Cartan group mod-
\ell
. This fact, combined with a classification of possible images for
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
when
\ell\leq 37
(see e.g.
[
GJN20
]
), implies that the orbit of any cyclic order
n
subgroup of
E
is either trivial, or has a size that is a multiple of
2,3,5,7,11,13,17
or
37
. Consequently, for any number field
L
whose degree over
\mathbb{Q}
is coprime to
2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7\cdot 11\cdot 13\cdot 17\cdot 37=18888870
, for any non-CM elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, an isogeny
\phi
from
E
is
L
-rational if and only if
\phi
is
\mathbb{Q}
-rational. In particular, when
\phi
is cyclic, the degree of
\phi
is bounded uniformly by Mazur and Kenku’s results.

With the above example in mind, let us introduce some terminology. Given an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
and a cyclic
n
-isogeny with kernel
C\subseteq E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, we say that
C
(and the isogeny) is
new
if
C
is not
F
-rational.
For an extension
L/F
, we say that
C
is
new for
L/F
if
C
is
L
-rational but not
F
-rational; equivalently,
C
is new for
L/F
if
F(C)\subseteq L
and
[F(C):F]>1
. For example, the preceding paragraph shows that if
L
is a number field with
[L:\mathbb{Q}]
coprime to
18888870
, then for any non-CM elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, there are no new isogenies on
E
for
L/\mathbb{Q}
.

The main theorem of this paper is a uniformity result for new isogenies on elliptic curves for
L/F
, where
F
is any number field with no rational CM.
This theorem can be viewed as a generalization of
[
Gen24
, Theorem 1]
, which concerns new torsion points on elliptic curves for
L/F
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
F
be a number field with no rational CM. Then under GRH, there exists an effectively computable constant
B:=B(F)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for any finite extension
L/F
whose degree
[L:F]
is coprime to
B
, there are no new isogenies for
L/F
on any elliptic curve over
F
.

As will be made precise in the following section, the degree of the field of definition of an
\ell
-isogeny on
E_{/F}
is determined by the size of its kernel’s orbit under the action of
G_{F}
. When
F
has no rational CM and
\ell
is sufficiently large with respect to
F
, under GRH the group
E[\ell]
of
\ell
-torsion points on
E
is an irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-module, which restricts the possible images
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})
of the mod-
\ell
Galois representation of
E
over
F
. This implies that the image
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})
is “relatively uniformly large” in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, as described in Theorem
2
below.
This theorem can be viewed as progress towards Serre’s uniformity question for number fields larger than
\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
F
be a number field with no rational CM. Then under GRH, there exists an effectively computable constant
c:=c(F)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all primes
\ell>c
, one has the following: for an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
, if the image
G:=\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})
does not equal
\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, then it is contained in the normalizer of a Cartan group in
\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
up to conjugacy.

Let
N_{s}(\ell)
and
N_{ns}(\ell)
denote the normalizer of the split and nonsplit Cartan group mod-
\ell
, respectively, and let
G(\ell)
be as defined in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{Sect_SubgpsOfGL2}
. Then up to a choice of basis for
E[\ell]
, one of the following holds:

(1)

If
G\subseteq N_{s}(\ell)
then
C_{s}(\ell)^{e}\subseteq G
for some
e\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
, and
G
contains the subgroup of scalar matrices. Furthermore, the index of
G
in
N_{s}(\ell)
satisfies

[N_{s}(\ell):G]\mid\gcd(\ell-1,e).

(2)

If
G\subseteq N_{ns}(\ell)
then
G=N_{ns}(\ell)
or
G(\ell)
. If
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{3}
then
G=N_{ns}(\ell)
.

The conclusion of Theorem
2
is an improvement of
[
Gen24
, Theorem 2]
in the case where
G
is contained in the normalizer
N_{ns}(\ell)
of the nonsplit Cartan group mod-
\ell
, up to conjugacy. More specifically, when
\ell
is large, if up to conjugacy
G
is also contained in the nonsplit Cartan group
C_{ns}(\ell)
, then it follows that
G
is diagonalizable over the quadratic extension
\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
. Thus,
E[\ell]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}
has a degree 1 associated character with values in
\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}
. By Theorem
6
, this implies that
F
has rational CM, or that GRH fails for
F[\sqrt{-\ell}]
. Therefore, assuming both GRH and that
F
has no rational CM, we may conclude that
G
cannot be contained in
C_{ns}(\ell)
up to conjugacy when
\ell
is large; thus, Theorem
2
follows at once from
[
Gen24
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem
1
is in part a consequence of the following result, which can be viewed as a generalization of
[
CN21
, Proposition 3.3]
from
\mathbb{Q}
to
F
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
F
be a number field with no rational CM. Then under GRH, there exists an effectively computable constant
c:=c(F)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all primes
\ell>c

and for all elliptic curves
E_{/F}
, the field of definition for an
\ell
-isogeny of
E
has even degree over
F
.

In the final section of this paper, we will prove a uniformity result for elliptic curves defined over a number field
F
, where we do not assume that
F
has no rational CM. In this theorem, part a. is complementary to Theorem
2
, providing a result towards Serre’s uniformity question over
F
; part b. is a consequence of part a., and provides a necessary condition for the existence of new isogenies of large prime degree.

Theorem 4
.

Let
F
be a number field. Assume GRH, and fix a prime
\ell\geq\max\{c(F),72[F:\mathbb{Q}]\}
with
c(F)
as in Proposition
3
. If
E_{/F}
is an elliptic curve with exactly one
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny, then
E
must be non-CM. Furthermore, one has the following:

(1)

Up to conjugacy, the image
G:=\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})
is contained in
B(\ell)
, the Borel subgroup of
\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. If
G\subseteq B(\ell)
, then one has

[B(\ell):G]\mid 864[F:\mathbb{Q}].

(2)

For an order
\ell
subgroup
C\subseteq E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, either
[F(C):F]=1
or
[F(C):F]=\ell
.

Remark 1
.

The theorems and proposition above use the same constant
c(F)
: this is the least positive integer such that for all primes
\ell\geq c(F)
, one has
\ell\geq\max\{74,15[F:\mathbb{Q}]+2\}
,
\ell
is unramified in
F
and
\ell\not\in S_{F}
, where
S_{F}
is defined in Theorem
6
.

2.
Galois Action on Torsion and Isogenies

In this section, we will set up some notation and terminology for the rest of this paper, as well as describe and prove some basic results.

2.1.
The action of Galois on
n
-torsion points of elliptic curves

For an integer
n>1
, we let
\textrm{GL}_{2}(n):=\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
be the group of
2\times 2
invertible matrices over
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. If we let
e_{1}:=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}
and
e_{2}:=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix}
be the unit column vectors over
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, then
\textrm{GL}_{2}(n)
acts by left multiplication on the
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-module
V
generated by
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, which is free of rank two.

Once and for all, fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Given an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
and an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the
n
-torsion subgroup
E[n]:=E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})[n]
is a free rank two
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-module
[
Sil09
, Corollary 6.4]
; a choice of basis
\{P,Q\}
for
E[n]
gives a
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-module isomorphism
E[n]\cong V
.
In fact, the absolute Galois group
G_{F}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F)
acts
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-linearly on
E[n]
. With respect to a basis
\{P,Q\}
of
E[n]
, this action is explicitly described by the
mod-
n
Galois representation

\rho_{E,n,P,Q}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(n).

This map is as follows: if
\sigma\in G_{F}
acts on
E[n]
via
P^{\sigma}=aP+cQ
and
Q^{\sigma}=bP+dQ
, then one has
\rho_{E,n,P,Q}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
. By the Weil pairing, the image of the determinant of
\rho_{E,n,P,Q}(G_{F})
is equal to the image of the mod-
n
cyclotomic character on
G_{F}
.

We will usually write
G:=\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})
instead of
\rho_{E,n,P,Q}(G_{F})
for the image of the mod-
n
Galois representation over
F
; this notation is well-defined up to conjugacy, but we will often work with an implicitly fixed basis. One always has
G\cong\textrm{Gal}(F(E[n])/F)
, where
F(E[n])
is the
n
-division field of
E
over
F
.

2.2.
Cyclic isogenies of elliptic curves

Given an algebraic extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
and two elliptic curves
{E_{1}}_{/F}
and
{E_{2}}_{/F}
, an
F
-isogeny from
{E_{1}}
to
E_{2}
is a nontrivial
F
-rational morphism of curves
\phi\colon E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
which fixes the point at infinity:
\phi(O_{E_{1}})=O_{E_{2}}
. Such an isogeny induces a group homomorphism
\phi\colon E_{1}(F)\rightarrow E_{2}(F)

[
Sil09
, Theorem 4.8]
. Being
F
-isogenous defines an equivalence relation on elliptic curves over
F
, and the equivalence class of an elliptic curve is called its
F
-isogeny class.

Given an isogeny
\phi\colon E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
, the kernel
C:=\ker\phi
is a finite subgroup of
E_{1}[\textrm{tors}]:=E_{1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})[\textrm{tors}]
; if
C
is cyclic of order
n
, we say that
\phi
is a
cyclic
n
-isogeny.
Since
\phi
is
F
-rational, it follows that
C
is stable under the action of
G_{F}
: that is, for all
\sigma\in G_{F}
one has
C^{\sigma}=C
. Conversely, any cyclic order
n
subgroup
C\subseteq E_{1}[\textrm{tors}]
which is stable under the action of
G_{F}
induces an
F
-rational isogeny on
E_{1}
whose kernel is
C

[
Sil09
, Exercise 3.13.e]
. For this reason, we sometimes refer to cyclic order
n
subgroups of
E_{1}[n]
as cyclic
n
-isogenies; when
n=\ell
is prime, an
\ell
-isogeny is automatically cyclic.

For an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
, the action of
G_{F}
on
E[n]
extends to an action on its set of cyclic order
n
subgroups. In general, if we let
\textrm{Cyc}(V)
denote the set of nontrivial cyclic
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-submodules of the free rank two
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-module
V:=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
, then the action of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(n)
on
V
extends to an action on
\textrm{Cyc}(V)
: for
C=\langle v\rangle\in\textrm{Cyc}(V)
and
m\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(n)
, we set
m\cdot C:=\langle m\cdot v\rangle
. We will denote the set of cyclic order
n
subgroups in
E
by
\textrm{Cyc}(E,n):=\textrm{Cyc}(E[n])
.
Matters simplify in the case where
n=\ell
is prime: one has
\#\textrm{Cyc}(V)=\ell+1
, since the nontrivial order
\ell
subgroups of
V
are
\langle e_{1}\rangle
and
C_{k}:=\langle ke_{1}+e_{2}\rangle
, where
0\leq k<\ell
.

To prove Proposition
3
, we will need the following small but useful lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Fix an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and let
V
be the
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-span of unit column vectors
e_{1},e_{2}
over
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Then for all integers
1\leq k,m<n
, one has that
\langle ke_{1}+e_{2}\rangle=\langle me_{1}+e_{2}\rangle
if and only if
k=m
.

Proof.

If
\langle ke_{1}+e_{2}\rangle=\langle me_{1}+e_{2}\rangle
, then
me_{1}+e_{2}\in\langle ke_{1}+e_{2}\rangle
, and so for some
x\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

me_{1}+e_{2}=x(ke_{1}+e_{2})=xke_{1}+xe_{2}.

By
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-linear independence of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, it follows that
m\equiv xk\pmod{n}
and
1\equiv x\pmod{n}
; we deduce that
m\equiv k\pmod{n}
, whence from
1\leq k,m<n
we conclude that
k=m
.
∎

2.3.
Orbits and fields of definition

Given an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
and a torsion point
R\in E[\textrm{tors}]
, the
field of definition of
R
over
F
,
denoted by
F(R)
, is the extension of
F
of least degree over which
R
is rational. Equivalently, it is the fixed field of the stabilizer of
R
under the action of
G_{F}
on
E[n]
.

Say
R\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
has order
n
, and let
G:=\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})
. We will write
\mathcal{O}_{G}(R)
to denote the orbit of
R
under
G
(also called the
G
-orbit
of
R
). A key observation regarding orbits and fields of definition is the following. Since the stabilizer of
R
under
G
is
\textrm{Gal}(F(E[n])/F(R))
, the orbit-stabilizer theorem implies that

\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(R)=[F(R):F].

This size is invariant under a change of basis for
E[n]
.

A similar description applies to the field of definition of a cyclic isogeny. Given an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
and a cyclic
n
-isogeny
C\in\textrm{Cyc}(E,n)
, the field of definition of
C
over
F
, denoted by
F(C)
, is the least extension of
F
over which
C
is stable under the action of Galois. By Galois theory,
F(C)
is also the fixed field of the stabilizer of
C
under the action of
G_{F}
on
\textrm{Cyc}(E,n)
. The orbit-stabilizer theorem then implies that

\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(C)=[F(C):F],

which is invariant under a change of basis for
E[n]
.

2.4.
Subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)

In this subsection, we will define the groups which appear in Theorem
2
.

For
\ell\geq 3
, let us define the split Cartan group mod-
\ell
as the subgroup of diagonal matrices,

C_{s}(\ell):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)\right\}.

Its normalizer is denoted as
N_{s}(\ell)
; explicitly, we have

N_{s}(\ell)=C_{s}(\ell)\cup\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}C_{s}(\ell).

We will also define the nonsplit Cartan group mod-
\ell
, denoted
C_{ns}(\ell)
, as follows. For a prime
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we will write
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}:=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
.
Fix the least positive integer
\epsilon
which generates
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
: then we have

C_{ns}(\ell)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\epsilon\\
b&a\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)\right\}.

This is the regular representation of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[\sqrt{\epsilon}]^{\times}
acting on itself via multiplication with respect to the basis
\{1,\sqrt{\epsilon}\}
. Its normalizer is

N_{ns}(\ell)=C_{ns}(\ell)\cup\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}C_{ns}(\ell).

Let us also define the Borel subgroup mod-
\ell
as the subgroup of upper triangular matrices,

B(\ell):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)\right\}.

Also appearing in Theorem
2
is the mod-
\ell
subgroup

G(\ell):=\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix},C_{ns}(\ell)^{3}\right\rangle,

which has index
3
in
N_{ns}(\ell)
when
\ell\geq 5
. Finally, we will use
Z(\ell)
to denote the subgroup of scalar matrices in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
.

2.5.
Orbits under a subgroup

In this subsection, we will describe how orbits under the action of a finite group compare to orbits under its subgroups. This is particularly important for Theorem
4
(and the part of the proof of Theorem
2
from
[
Gen24
]
). Given a finite group
G
which acts on a set
X
, one has for any subgroup
H\subseteq G
that

(1)

\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(x)\mid[G:H]\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{H}(x).

As noted in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{Subsect_FieldsOfDefn}
, for an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
and an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, for any cyclic subgroup
C\in\textrm{Cyc}(E,n)
one has
\#\mathcal{O}_{\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})}(C)=[F(C):F]
. Thus, for any subgroup
N\subseteq\textrm{GL}_{2}(n)
with
\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})\subseteq N
, we have by Equation (
1
) that

\#\mathcal{O}_{N}(C)\mid[N:\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})]\cdot[F(C):F].

In particular, if a prime
p\mid\#\mathcal{O}_{N}(C)
does not divide
[N:\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})]
, then
p\mid[F(C):F]
.

2.6.
The isogeny character

In this subsection, we will describe some results on the
isogeny character
of an elliptic curve. This is necessary for the proofs of Theorems
1
and
4
, and Proposition
3
.

Recall that for an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
with a finite cyclic subgroup
C\subseteq E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
of order
n
, if
C
is
G_{F}
-stable, then the action of
G_{F}
on
C
induces a homomorphism
r\colon G_{F}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
called the
isogeny character of
C
.
This character is described as follows: writing
C:=\langle P\rangle
, for
\sigma\in G_{F}
one has

P^{\sigma}=r(\sigma)\cdot P.

In Theorem
4
,
part b. will quickly follow from part a. The proof of part a. analyzes the isogeny character of an elliptic curve over
F
, using the following uniformity result from Larson and Vaintrob. Recall that a CM elliptic curve
E_{/F}
has
rationally defined CM over
F
if its CM field is contained in
F
; in such a case, we say that
F
has
rational CM.

Theorem 6
.

[
LV14
, Theorem 1]

Let
F
be a number field. Then there exists a finite set
S_{F}
of prime numbers such that the following holds. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be a prime not in
S_{F}
, and let
E_{/F}
be an elliptic curve. Suppose that

Remark 3

For a prime

The following short lemma concerns the degree of a field of definition of a torsion point, over the field of definition of its cyclic subgroup. It will be used in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be an algebraic extension,
E_{/F}
an elliptic curve and
P\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
a point of order
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Setting
C:=\langle P\rangle
, one has

[F(P):F(C)]\mid\varphi(n),

where
\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is Euler’s totient function.

Proof.

Over the extension
F(C)
, the subgroup
C
is Galois stable. This induces an isogeny character

r\colon G_{F(C)}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

One checks that the fixed field of this character is
F(P)
. Thus, modding out by the kernel gives a faithful representation

r\colon\textrm{Gal}(F(P)/F(C))\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times},

from which we conclude that
[F(P):F(C)]\mid\#(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}=\varphi(n)
.

3.
The Proofs of Theorem
1
and Proposition
3

3.1.
The proof of Theorem
1

First, we will prove Theorem
1
using Proposition
3
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Given the constant
c:=c(F)
from Proposition
3
, let
B:=B(F)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be the product of the primes
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
p\leq c
.

Let
E_{/F}
be an elliptic curve. Fix an extension
L/F
such that
[L:F]
is coprime to
B
. Let
C\subseteq E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
be a finite cyclic subgroup that is
L
-rational; we will show that
C
must be
F
-rational. Write out the prime factorization
|C|=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\ell_{i}^{a_{i}}
; then we have a primary decomposition
C=\langle C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{r}\rangle
, where
C_{i}
is the unique subgroup of
C
whose order is
\ell_{i}^{a_{i}}
. We see that if each
C_{i}
is
G_{F}
-stable, then so is
C
.
Thus, without loss of generality we will assume that
|C|=\ell^{n}
is a prime power.

We will proceed by induction on
n\geq 1
. If
n=1
, then
C
is an
L
-rational
\ell
-isogeny, and the degree
[F(C):F]\leq\ell+1
. If
\ell\leq c
, then each prime
p
dividing
[F(C):F]
is at most
c
, which by definition implies
p\mid B
. However, since
F(C)\subseteq L
and
[L:F]
is coprime to
B
, so is
[F(C):F]
; this forces
[F(C):F]=1
. If
\ell>c
, then by Proposition
3
we have that
2\mid[F(C):F]
, and thus
2\mid[L:F]
, which is impossible since
B
is even and
[L:F]
is coprime to
B
. We conclude that
C
is
F
-rational when
n=1
. (We have also just shown that the prime degrees of
L
-rational isogenies are uniformly bounded by
c(F)
.)

Assume next that
n\geq 2
and the result is true for cyclic
\ell^{k}
-isogenies, where
1\leq k<n
. This inductive hypothesis implies that
C_{\ell^{n-1}}=\ell C
is
F
-rational, so that
[F(C):F]=[F(C):F(\ell C)]
. Thus, to show that
C
is
F
-rational, it suffices to show that if
[F(C):F(\ell C)]\neq 1
, then
[F(C):F(\ell C)]
is divisible by a prime
p\leq c
, an impossibility. Let us fix a generator
P
of
C
. Then we have the following diagram of field extensions:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\ell P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\ell C)}

It follows that

[F(C):F(\ell C)]=\frac{[F(P):F(\ell P)]\cdot[F(\ell P):F(\ell C)]}{[F(P):F(C)]}.

By Lemma
7
we have
[F(\ell P):F(\ell C)]\mid\varphi(\ell^{n-1})=\ell^{n-2}(\ell-1)
, and by
[
GJN20
, Proposition 4.6]
we know that

[F(P):F(\ell P)]\mid\ell^{2}(\ell-1)
, whence it follows that

[F(C):F(\ell C)]\mid\ell^{n}(\ell-1)^{2}.

Suppose for contradiction that
F(C)\neq F
. Thus, there exists a common prime divisor
p
of
\ell^{n}(\ell-1)^{2}
and
[L:F]
, i.e., there exists prime
p\mid\ell(\ell-1)
with
p\mid[L:F]
. If
\ell\leq c
then
p\leq c
, which by definition implies
p\mid B
– this is impossible since
[L:F]
is coprime to
B
. We then must have
\ell>c
, which by Proposition
3
implies that
[F(\ell^{n-1}C):F]
is even; however, this is impossible since
\ell^{n-1}C
is
F
-rational by the inductive hypothesis.
We thus conclude that
C
is
F
-rational. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
using Proposition
3
.
∎

3.2.
The proof of Proposition
3

To finish the proof of Theorem
1
, we must now prove Proposition
3
.

Proof of Proposition
3
.

Fix a prime
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
\ell\geq\max\{74,15[F:\mathbb{Q}]+2\}
,
\ell
is unramified in
F
and
\ell\not\in S_{F}
.
We must show that for an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
, for any cyclic subgroup
C\in\textrm{Cyc}(E,\ell)
one has that
[F(C):F]
is even.
By Theorem
2
, our assumptions on
\ell
imply that, up to conjugacy,
G:=\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})
either equals one of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
,
N_{ns}(\ell)
or
G(\ell)
, or that
G
is contained in
N_{s}(\ell)
. The groups
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
and
N_{ns}(\ell)
act transitively on
E[\ell]\smallsetminus\{O\}
, and thus act transitively on Cyc
(E,\ell)
, which implies that
[F(C):F]=\ell+1
for all
C\in
Cyc
(E,\ell)
when
G\in\{\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell),N_{ns}(\ell)\}
. If
G=G(\ell)
, then
[N_{ns}(\ell):G]=3
, so by Equation (
1
) we have
\ell+1\mid 3\cdot[F(C):F]
for all
C\in\textrm{Cyc}(E,\ell)
, which implies that each
[F(C):F]
is even.

Let us assume then that
G\subseteq N_{s}(\ell)
with respect to a basis
\{P,Q\}
. By Theorem
2
, we find that
C_{s}(\ell)^{e}\subseteq G
for some
e\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
, and that
G
contains
Z(\ell)
, the subgroup of scalars of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
. Note that
G\not\subseteq C_{s}(\ell)
since otherwise
E
has (two distinct)
F
-rational
\ell
-isogenies, which contradicts
\ell\not\in S_{F}
.

Observe that the subgroups
\langle P\rangle
and
\langle Q\rangle
have quadratic fields of definition over
F
, since the subgroup
H:=G\cap C_{s}(\ell)
has index two in
G
; consequently, both
[F(\langle P\rangle):F]
and
[F(\langle Q\rangle):F]
are even. Thus, we will restrict our attention to showing that the remaining order
\ell
subgroups
C_{k}:=\langle kP+Q\rangle
, where
0<k<\ell
, have even degree fields of definition over
F
.

Since
G\not\subseteq C_{s}(\ell)
, there exists an antidiagonal matrix
\gamma:=\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{bmatrix}\in G
. The action of
\gamma
on
\textrm{Cyc}(E,\ell)
sends
C_{k}
to
C_{bc^{-1}k^{-1}}
; thus we have
C_{bc^{-1}k^{-1}}\in\mathcal{O}_{G}(C_{k})
. One can check that if
\gamma\cdot C_{k}=\gamma\cdot C_{m}
, then
C_{bc^{-1}k^{-1}}=C_{bc^{-1}m^{-1}}
, which by Lemma
5
implies that
k=m
. Thus, if
C_{k}\neq C_{bc^{-1}k^{-1}}
for all
0<k<\ell
, then all orbits
\mathcal{O}_{G}(C_{k})
have even size, as each pair of distinct subgroups
C_{k}
and
\gamma\cdot C_{k}
lie in the same
G
-orbit; this would then imply that the field of definition of each
C_{k}
has even degree over
F
, which finishes the proof. Suppose then that for some
k
we have
C_{k}=C_{k^{-1}bc^{-1}}
; by Lemma
5
, this forces
c\equiv bk^{-2}\pmod{\ell}
, whence we have
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
bk^{-2}&0\end{bmatrix}
. Since
Z(\ell)\subseteq G
, we can scale
\gamma
by
b^{-1}k
and deduce that
\begin{bmatrix}0&k\\
k^{-1}&0\end{bmatrix}\in G
.

To this end, we may assume that
\gamma:=\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
b^{-1}&0\end{bmatrix}\in G
for some
1\leq b<\ell
. We check that
\gamma
sends any subgroup
C_{k}
to
C_{b^{2}k^{-1}}
. By Lemma
5
, we know that
C_{k}=C_{b^{2}k^{-1}}
if and only if
k\equiv\pm b\pmod{\ell}
. Therefore, to show that all orbits of
\textrm{Cyc}(E,\ell)
have even size, it is enough to show that
C_{b}
and
C_{-b}:=C_{\ell-b}
are in the same
G
-orbit (note that
C_{-b}
is distinct from
C_{b}
).

With some calculation, one can show that
C_{b}
and
C_{-b}
are in the same
G
-orbit if and only if
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\in G
or
\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
-b^{-1}&0\end{bmatrix}\in G
. Since
\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
-b^{-1}&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\cdot\gamma
, we deduce that
C_{b}
and
C_{-b}
are in the same
G
-orbit if and only if
\gamma_{0}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\in G
.

We will show that
\gamma_{0}\in G
. To do this, we will make a few more reductions. Let us set
H:=G\cap C_{s}(\ell)
.
By Theorem
2
, we have
C_{s}(\ell)^{e}\subseteq H
for some
e\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
. It follows that
\gamma_{0}\in H
if
-1
is an
e
’th power modulo
\ell
. This latter part is true if and only if
\ell=2
or
\frac{\ell-1}{\gcd(e,\ell-1)}
is even. Thus,
-1
is an
e
’th power modulo
\ell
unless
e\in\{2,4,6\}
and
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4},
or
e=4
and
\ell\equiv 5\pmod{8}
.

We will show that in either of these two cases, we still have
\gamma_{0}\in H
. Let
\det(H)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
denote the subgroup of determinants of matrices in
H
, and let
\mu_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
denote the subgroup of the fourth roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
. First, we will show that if
\mu_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\subseteq\det(H)
then
\gamma_{0}\in H
.

Assume first that
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.
Observe that
-1\in\det(H)
if and only if there exists
m=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&-a^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\in H
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
. When this happens, it follows that
am=\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\in H
, noting that
H
contains
Z(\ell)
. Since
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, we find that
a^{2}
has odd order. Writing this order as
|a^{2}|
, it follows that
(am)^{|a^{2}|}=\gamma_{0}\in H
. Next, assume that
e=4
and
\ell\equiv 5\pmod{8}
; let us write
i\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
for a primitive fourth root of unity. Then we have
i\in\det(H)
iff there exists
m=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&ia^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\in H
, and in such a case we have
am=\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}&0\\
0&i\end{bmatrix}\in H
. Thus
(am)^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}a^{4}&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\in H
. Since
8\nmid\ell-1
we have
8\nmid|a|
, and thus
|a^{4}|
is odd, so that
(am)^{2|a^{4}|}=\gamma_{0}\in H
. We conclude that in any case, if
\mu_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\subseteq\det(H)
then
\gamma_{0}\in G
.

We will show that
\det(H)=\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
.
Fix a prime
\mathcal{L}\mid\ell
in
F
and let
K:=(F)_{\mathcal{L}}
denote the
\mathcal{L}
-adic completion of
F
at
\mathcal{L}
. Let us use
I_{K}
to denote the inertia group of
K
(throughout this, we are working with a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
). Since
\det\rho_{E,\ell}(I_{K})=\chi_{\ell}(I_{K})
and
\ell
is unramified in
F
, it follows that
\det\rho_{E,\ell}(I_{K})
surjects onto
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
.

We will argue that the same holds once we replace
F
by the fixed field of
H
, which we denote by
M_{H}
.
Fix a prime
\mathcal{Q}\mid\mathcal{L}
in
M_{H}
, and let
L:=(M_{H})_{\mathcal{Q}}
denote the completion of
M_{H}
at
\mathcal{Q}
. Then the inertia group
I_{L}
is contained in
H
. In particular, it follows that
\ell
is unramified in
M_{H}
, see also
[
Ser72
, Lemme 2]
. Consequently,
L/K
is unramified, and so
I_{K}\subseteq I_{L}
. Thus, from
\det\rho_{E,\ell}(I_{K})=\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
we deduce that
\det\rho_{E,\ell}(I_{L})=\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
, whence we conclude that
\det(H)=\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
.

From the work above, we conclude that
\gamma_{0}\in G
. In particular,
C_{b}
and
C_{-b}
are distinct and in the same
G
-orbit, and so all
G
-orbits of
\textrm{Cyc}(E,\ell)
have even size, which is equivalent to the degree
[F(C):F]
being even for all
C\in\textrm{Cyc}(E,\ell)
. This concludes our proof.
∎

4.
The Proof of Theorem
4

In this section we will prove Theorem
4
, which allows us to describe the mod-
\ell
Galois representation of a non-CM elliptic curve over a number field
F
with an
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny, when
\ell
is uniformly large. Unlike Theorems
1
and
2
, these results do not exclude the possibility of
F
having rational CM. A similar analysis of isogeny characters can be found in
[
Gen22
]
.

We first prove part a. of Theorem
4
, which provides a relative uniform bound on the index of the mod-
\ell
Galois representation.
Since
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})
is non-diagonalizable,
E
must be non-CM.
Let
\langle P\rangle
be an
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny on
E
. Then we can choose
Q\in E[\ell]
such that
\{P,Q\}
is a basis for
E[\ell]
, and with respect to this basis we have

G:=\rho_{E,\ell,P,Q}(G_{F})=\textrm{im}\thinspace\begin{bmatrix}r&*\\
0&\chi_{\ell}r^{-1}\end{bmatrix},

where
\chi_{\ell}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
is the mod-
\ell
cyclotomic character, and
r
is the isogeny character of
\langle P\rangle
.
Let
G^{\textrm{ss}}
be the semisimplification of
G
.
Explicitly, we have

G^{\textrm{ss}}=\textrm{im}\thinspace\begin{bmatrix}r&0\\
0&\chi_{\ell}r^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
E
has exactly one
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny, we know that
G
is not diagonalizable, thus by
[
Gen22
, Lemma 4]
we have
G^{\textrm{ss}}\subseteq G
. It also follows by the proof of
[
Gen22
, Lemma 4]
that
\gamma:=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\in G
, and thus
G=\langle\gamma,G^{\textrm{ss}}\rangle
. Since we also have
B(\ell)=\langle\gamma,C_{s}(\ell)\rangle
, we deduce that

(2)

[B(\ell):G]=[C_{s}(\ell):G^{\textrm{ss}}].

The rest of this proof will be showing that
[C_{s}(\ell):G^{\textrm{ss}}]\mid 864[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Since we are assuming that GRH is true, by Theorem
6
the existence of the
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny
\langle P\rangle
implies the following: there exists a CM elliptic curve
E^{\prime}_{/F}
whose CM field
K
is contained in
F
, such that
E^{\prime}
has an associated character
\psi\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}^{\times}
which satisfies

(3)

r^{12}=\psi^{12}.

By our initial hypotheses,
\ell
is unramified in the ring
\mathcal{O}:=\textrm{End}(E^{\prime})
, regarded as an order of the CM field
K
. By
[
BC20
, Corollary 1.5]
, up to conjugation we have the bound

(4)

[C_{\ell}(\mathcal{O}):G^{\prime}]\mid 6[F:\mathbb{Q}]

where
G^{\prime}:=\rho_{E^{\prime},\ell}(G_{F})
and
C_{\ell}(\mathcal{O})
is either
C_{s}(\ell)
or
C_{ns}(\ell)
depending on whether
\ell
is split or inert in
\mathcal{O}
, respectively.

We will show that
\ell
must be split in
\mathcal{O}
. Assume to the contrary that
\ell
is inert in
\mathcal{O}
; then as noted in Remark
3
, the elements of
G^{\prime}
have eigenvalues over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}:=F_{\ell}[\sqrt{\epsilon}]
which are Galois conjugates of one another. Writing

G^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}=\textrm{im}\thinspace\begin{bmatrix}\psi&0\\
0&\psi^{\prime}\end{bmatrix},

it follows that

(5)

\#G^{\prime}=\#\psi(G_{F}).

Now, for any nonzero torsion point
R^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}[\ell]
, since
G^{\prime}\subseteq C_{ns}(\ell)
we have by Equations (
1
) and (
4
) the orbit size divisibility

\#\mathcal{O}_{C_{ns}(\ell)}(R^{\prime})\mid 6[F:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot[F(R^{\prime}):F],

noting that
\#\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(R)=[F(R^{\prime}):F]
.
Since the action of
C_{ns}(\ell)
on
E^{\prime}[\ell]\smallsetminus\{O\}
is transitive, this implies that

\ell^{2}-1\mid 6[F:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot[F(R^{\prime}):F].

However, from
r^{12}=\psi^{12}
we find that

\gcd(\#\psi(G_{F}),12)\cdot\#r(G_{F})=\gcd(\#r(G_{F}),12)\cdot\#\psi(G_{F}),

and thus

\#\psi(G_{F})\mid 12\cdot\#r(G_{F})\mid 12(\ell-1).

Since
[F(R^{\prime}):F]\mid\#G^{\prime}
, and because
\#G^{\prime}=\#\psi(G_{F})
by Equation (
5
), we deduce that

\ell^{2}-1\mid 6[F:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot 12(\ell-1),

and so

\ell+1\mid 72[F:\mathbb{Q}].

However, this is impossible since
\ell\geq 72[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. We deduce that
\ell
splits in
\mathcal{O}
.

Since
\ell
splits in
\mathcal{O}
, we can choose a basis for
E^{\prime}[\ell]
such that
G^{\prime}
has the form

G^{\prime}=\textrm{im}\thinspace\begin{bmatrix}\psi&0\\
0&\chi_{\ell}\psi^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus, from Equation (
3
) it follows that
(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{12}=(G^{\prime})^{12}
.
With this, we have the containments
C_{s}(\ell)\supseteq G^{\textrm{ss}}\supseteq(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{12}=(G^{\prime})^{12}
, whence it follows that

[C_{s}(\ell):G^{\textrm{ss}}]\mid[C_{s}(\ell):(G^{\prime})^{12}].

Since
[G^{\prime}:(G^{\prime})^{12}]\mid 144
, this shows that

[C_{s}(\ell):G^{\textrm{ss}}]\mid 144[C_{s}(\ell):G^{\prime}].

By Equation (
4
),
we thus conclude that

[C_{s}(\ell):G^{\textrm{ss}}]\mid 864[F:\mathbb{Q}].

Since
[B(\ell):G]=[C_{s}(\ell):G^{\textrm{ss}}]
by Equation (
2
), this concludes our proof of part a.

Next, we will prove part b. of Theorem
4
.
With our assumptions, we can choose a basis
\{P,Q\}
of
E[\ell]
such that
G:=\rho_{E,\ell,P,Q}(G_{F})\subseteq B(\ell)

E

\ell

G

1

\#\mathcal{O}_{B(\ell)}(C)\mid[B(\ell):G]\cdot[F(C):F].

By part a., this implies that

\#\mathcal{O}_{B(\ell)}(C)\mid 864[F:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot[F(C):F].

The orbits of
\textrm{Cyc}(E,\ell)
under
B(\ell)

\mathcal{O}_{B(\ell)}(\langle P\rangle)=\{\langle P\rangle\}

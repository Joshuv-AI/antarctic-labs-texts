Spin-momentum Locking and Topological Vector Charge Response with Conserved Spin
Yoonseok Hwang, Penghao Zhu, Taylor L. Hughes

Abstract
Spin-momentum locking plays a fundamental role in spintronics and, more broadly, is an important concept in condensed matter physics. In 2D and 3D, spin-momentum locking typically does not allow spin-conservation because the spin-1/2 operators of electrons anticommute. Instead, here we study spin-momentum locking terms with conserved, commuting pseudospins built from a combination of spin and orbitals. We find that 2D spin-momentum locking terms with conserved pseudospins generally lead to linearly dispersing modes at low-energy with anomalous charge and pseudospin currents. To cure the anomaly we show that such anomalous modes can be realized on the surface of a 3D Weyl semimetal (or an associated weak topological insulator) with a nonzero mixed spin-momentum quadrupole moment, which is determined by the momentum location and pseudospin eigenvalues of Weyl points at the Fermi level. Crucially, this mixed quadrupole moment captures a mixed pseudospin-charge bulk response that cancels the anomaly of surface modes, and can generate a giant 3D spin Hall effect, among other phenomena.



Spintronics, and in general the electrical control of spin, is built upon the relativistic phenomenon of spin-orbit coupling (SOC)
Dresselhaus 1955
;
Bychkov and Rashba 1984a
;
Bychkov and Rashba 1984b
;
Winkler 2003
;
Manchon et al. 2015
;
Soumyanarayanan et al. 2016
.
In solids, one remarkable, low-energy manifestation of atomic SOC is the concept of spin-momentum locking, where the orientation of an electron’s spin is inherently tied to its direction of motion.
Spin-momentum locking is the foundation for many of the most exciting spintronic phenomena
Žutić et al. 2004
in two dimensional (2D) electron systems with Rashba SOC
Bychkov and Rashba 1984b
;
Bychkov and Rashba 1984a
, the 2D surface states of topological insulators (TIs)
Liu et al. 2010
;
Qi and Zhang 2011
, the 1D helical edge states of quantum spin Hall insulators
Kane and Mele 2005
;
Bernevig and Zhang 2006
;
Wu et al. 2006
;
Xu and Moore 2006
;
Roushan et al. 2009
.

Previous studies of spin-momentum locking have focused on couplings between momenta and the intrinsic spin-1/2 of the electrons.
Because of the anticommuting nature of the spin-1/2 Pauli matrices, spin is not conserved in 2D and 3D systems exhibiting spin-momentum locking as more than one Pauli matrix generally appears in the low-energy Bloch Hamiltonian.
This is not ideal for spintronics applications as the nonconserved spins have a finite lifetime and any stored information will decay as the spins undergo unconstrained equilibration.
With this problem in mind, it is important to ask if we can have both spin-momentum locking and conserved spins.

Below we will find that the answer to this question is yes, and furthermore, that in some cases these systems are anomalous in the sense that they have a mixed spin-charge ‘t-Hooft anomaly, which can be remedied when attached to the boundary of a higher dimensional bulk.
In addition, we find that in some crystal symmetry classes, the higher dimensional bulk response that compensates for the surface anomaly is captured by an inherently non-relativistic, 3D rank-2 symmetric or antisymmetric axial vector charge Chern-Simons term, where the axial vector charge is the conserved spin.
Interestingly, we show that the bulk response is naturally generated by a topological Weyl semimetal with a nodal configuration that generates a mixed spin-momentum Weyl quadrupole.
This is in analogy to the recently proposed rank-2 chiral fermions which carry a crystal momentum vector charge and a momentum-momentum Weyl quadrupole
Dubinkin et al. 2021
;
Zhu et al. 2023
.

Explicitly, here we study 2D spin-momentum locking terms in
nonmagnetic
crystalline systems with rotation and mirror symmetries. We focus on effective
k\cdot p
Hamiltonians having the form

\displaystyle h_{\mathbf{k}}=\sum_{i=x,y}\sum_{A=X,Y}g_{iA}k_{i}S_{A},

(1)

where
(S_{X},S_{Y})
are spin operators satisfying
S_{A}^{\dagger}=S_{A}
, and
{\rm Tr}[S_{A}]=0
, and

is

Note that we work in units
\hbar=c=1
and the electron charge is
-e

In the neighborhood of a TRIM, time-reversal symmetry simply flips both spin and momentum in Eq. (
1
), and hence the effective Hamiltonian is manifestly time-reversal invariant.

The simplest case of spin-momentum locking with conserved spin in Eq. (
1
) is when only one spin operator appears.
This happens in the 1D helical edge states of the quantum spin Hall insulator, but in 2D, this case is allowed only in lower-symmetry systems having 2D point groups
C_{1,2}
or
C_{1v,2v}
(and even then would require extreme anisotropy or fine-tuning)

;
Samokhin 2022
.
We note that for
C_{3}

C_{3v}
symmetries, there can also be spin-momentum locking terms with one conserved spin operator
S_{Z}

;
Samokhin 2022
, but we leave further analysis of higher-order terms as a future work.

Our primary focus will instead be on systems with
C_{3,4,6}
or
C_{3v,4v,6v}
, where, as shown in the Supplementary Material (SM) Sec. S1
sup
, the spin-momentum locking terms that are

S_{X}
and
S_{Y}

In these cases the spin is conserved only when
S_{X}
and
S_{Y}

[S_{X},S_{Y}]=0

Although the intrinsic spin-1/2 of the electrons cannot satisfy this condition on their own, we will show that commuting operators consisting of both orbital- and spin-degrees of freedom of electrons can be constructed.
Hence, we study generalized spin-momentum locking terms in Eq. (
1
) with commuting pseudospin operators
S_{X}
and
S_{Y}

Rashba term with commuting pseudospins.—

For our primary example we focus on the point group
C_{4v}
, which is promoted to the
P4mm
space group for lattice Hamiltonians.
We further limit our study to nonmagnetic systems having time reversal symmetry (TRS)
\mathcal{T}
with
\mathcal{T}^{2}=-1

The relevant symmetry elements in
C_{4v}
act on the position-space coordinates as
M_{x}:(x,y,z)\to(-x,y,z)
,
M_{y}:(x,y,z)\to(x,-y,z)

C_{4z}:(x,y,z)\to(-y,x,z)

We choose the following representation of the symmetry elements:

\displaystyle M_{x,y}=-i\tau_{0}\sigma_{x,y},\,C_{4z}=\tau_{z}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{z}},\,\mathcal{T}=-i\tau_{0}\sigma_{y}\mathcal{K},

(2)

where

-orbital) representations of

From these degrees of freedom we can construct commuting pseudospin operators

\displaystyle S_{X}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\tau_{z}+\tau_{x})\sigma_{x},\quad S_{Y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\tau_{z}-\tau_{x})\sigma_{y}.

(3)

We see that
S_{X}
and
S_{Y}

(

(
S_{X}
),

flips both

and
S_{Y}

to
(S_{Y},-S_{X})

Using this construction we can now consider spin-momentum locking phenomena with commuting spins.
Independent of the spin commutation relations,

in Eq.

Without loss of generality, we set

Thus, Eq. (

\displaystyle h_{{\mathbf{k}}}=k_{x}S_{Y}-k_{y}S_{X},

(4)

which is an analog of the Rashba term
Bychkov and Rashba 1984b
but with commuting, instead of anticommuting, spin operators. For simplicity, we call the Hamiltonian described by Eq. (
4

The spectrum of the CSR term has four bands
E_{{\mathbf{k}}}=\pm k_{x}\mp k_{y}
and they, and the Fermi-surface contours of

, are shown in Figs.
1

We see that

has independent symmetries

Hence, each of the four open Fermi-surfaces has a fixed spin polarization that determines the Fermi-velocity

In comparison, instead of having four separate Fermi-surfaces that transform into each other under

the conventional Rashba Fermi-surface is a single closed circle where the spin-polarization and group velocity continuously wind around the Fermi-surface.

Anomalous charge and pseudospin currents.—

We will now show that the CSR Fermi surfaces have a mixed anomaly between the electric charge
U(1)_{\rm EM}
and the spin
U(1)_{A}
symmetries.
To demonstrate this, we view the CSR mode as a family of 1D chiral modes propagating in the
x
-direction (
y
-direction) with chirality
\chi_{x}(s_{Y})={\rm sgn}(s_{Y})
[
\chi_{y}(s_{X})=-{\rm sgn}(s_{X})
] parameterized by each
k_{y}
(
k_{x}
). For example, at a fixed value of
k_{y}
there are four linearly-dispersing modes propagating in the
x
-direction with chirality
{\rm sgn}(s_{Y}),
each with an effective chemical potential proportional to the fixed
k_{y}.

Hence, the charge and pseudospin anomalies of the CSR modes can be straightforwardly determined from the anomalies of 1D chiral modes.

Hence, to determine the anomalies of the CSR modes, let us first recall the anomaly of a 1D chiral mode with dispersion
E_{k_{x}}=\chi_{x}k_{x}.
Let us turn on an electric field by adiabatically shifting the
vector potential,
A_{x}

over a time period

Explicitly, we have

for

goes from

to

, the number of occupied right-moving modes (
\chi_{x}=+1
) increases by one, and the number of occupied left-moving modes (
\chi_{x}=-1
) decreases by one. To interpret this as an anomalous conservation law we define the charge density
J^{0}=-\frac{e}{V}\sum_{n\in occ.}1
where
V
is the volume (area in 2D, length in 1D) of the system. Thus, during this process
\Delta J^{0}=-e\chi_{x}/V,
and the corresponding anomalous conservation law is

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=\chi_{x}\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi}E_{x}.

(5)

Equipped with Eq. (
5

(

couples with the CSR mode as:

\displaystyle h_{\mathbf{k}}[A]=(k_{x}+eA_{x})S_{Y}-(k_{y}+eA_{y})S_{X}.

(6)

Considering the CSR mode as a family of 1D chiral modes, we see that the net chiralities along both the

Therefore, the electric field will not induce an anomaly for the electric charge.
However, we can define the pseudospin density

A=X,Y

Interestingly, we find that the pseudospin density changes by a nonzero amount during an adiabatic shift of

As illustrated in Figs.

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{X}=\frac{2e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}E_{y},\quad\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{Y}=-\frac{2e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}E_{x},

Realization of the CSR on the surface of a 3D semimetal.—

The anomalies discussed above imply that the CSR modes cannot exist in isolation in a 2D lattice system.
There are two natural ways to treat this issue: (i) introduce symmetry preserving terms that generate closed, non-anomalous Fermi surfaces intrinsically in 2D, or (ii) realize the CSR on the surface of a 3D system such that the bulk cancels the anomalous current via the Callan-Harvey anomaly inflow
Callan Jr and Harvey 1985
.
For (i) we can consider a Hamiltonian

\displaystyle h_{{\mathbf{k}}}=\alpha(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})\mathds{1}_{4\times 4}+vk_{x}S_{Y}-vk_{y}S_{X},

(10)

where we restored
v
.
The
\alpha
-dependent term preserves all of the symmetries and modifies the energy bands as
E_{{\mathbf{k}}}=\alpha(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})\pm vk_{x}\pm vk_{y}.
These bands generate four closed, circular Fermi surfaces centered around
(\pm v/2\alpha,\pm v/2\alpha).
Furthermore these Fermi-surfaces will not be anomalous if
|\Lambda|>|\tfrac{v}{2\alpha}|+\sqrt{\tfrac{\mu}{\alpha}+\tfrac{v^{2}}{2\alpha^{2}}}
,
i.e., if the region inside the momentum cutoff includes all four closed circles generated at the chemical potential
\mu
.
This is what we would expect for a 2D lattice model Bloch Hamiltonian that realizes Eq. (
4
) when expanded around a TRIM.
Note that the
\alpha
-term is not the only possible symmetry preserving perturbation, and discussions of another allowed term can be found in SM Sec. S2
sup
.

For a more interesting resolution to the anomaly let us consider mechanism (ii). To realize the anomalous CSR mode as a boundary mode we consider a 3D tight-binding (TB) model,

\displaystyle H_{3D}({\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle=\sin k_{x}\mu_{x}S_{Y}-\sin k_{y}\mu_{x}S_{X}+\sin k_{z}\mu_{y}

This model has space group

In addition,

Bulk response and Weyl quadrupole.—

Because of the anomalous surface response we expect the Weyl semimetal to produce a compensating bulk response. We calculated the quasi-topological bulk charge and pseudo-spin responses to external fields and found (detailed Kubo formula derivations can be found in SM Sec. S3
sup

\displaystyle J^{\mu}_{3D}=-\frac{e}{4\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu i\nu\rho}Q_{iA}\partial_{\nu}a^{A}_{\rho},

Conclusions.—

Here we studied
C_{4v}
-symmetric spin-momentum locking terms with conserved spin, and discussed the dynamics and anomalies of a commuting spin Rashba term.
We showed that the CSR is anomalous by itself and can appear on the surface of a 3D topological semimetal. Such a semimetal will have a vector charge Chern-Simons theory bulk response with a response coefficient determined by the Weyl spin-momentum quadrupole moment of the Fermi surface.
Beyond the specific crystalline symmetry group studied in this Letter, our approach can be applied to general commuting spin-momentum locking terms in Eq. (
1
).
Interested readers can find relevant discussions in SM Sec. S4
sup
. This work opens an important new direction in the quasi-topological semimetal responses characterized by mixed multipole moments of the nodal Fermi surfaces. Furthermore, we expect this work to be relevant for both quantum materials and metamaterials. Indeed, a pre-cursor to this work
Dubinkin et al. 2021
, where the vector charge was realized by momentum instead of spin, has recently been explored experimentally in a topo-electric circuit material
Zhu et al. 2023
. Additionally it would be interesting to investigate connections between our work and the recently proposed spin-resolved topology
Lin et al. 2022
.

S2
Perturbations effect on the commuting-spin Rashba mode

In this section, we discuss the symmetric perturbation terms for the CSR mode.

\displaystyle h_{\mathbf{k}}=

\displaystyle vk_{x}S_{Y}-vk_{y}S_{X}

\displaystyle+\alpha(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})\mathds{1}_{4\times 4}+\beta k_{x}k_{y}S_{X}S_{Y}.

\displaystyle\frac{e}{V}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}},a,\bar{b}}\frac{\Delta_{\bar{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})}{\nu_{m}^{2}+\Delta^{2}_{\bar{b}a}}S_{a,A}[v_{ia\bar{b}}({\mathbf{k}})v_{j\bar{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})+(i\leftrightarrow j)].

(S37)

Replacing
\nu_{m}
by
-i\omega
and replacing the sum over
{\mathbf{k}}
by an integral, the symmetric part of
\Pi_{A}^{ij}
contributes to the charge-spin mixed conductivity as

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\sum_{a,\bar{b}}

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{i\omega}\int\frac{d{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\Delta_{\bar{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})S_{a,A}

\displaystyle\times[A_{ia\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})A_{j\overline{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})+(i\leftrightarrow j)].

(S38)

where we define the non-Abelian Berry connection

\displaystyle A_{iab}({\mathbf{k}})=-i\langle a,{\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{i}|b,{\mathbf{k}}\rangle

(S39)

and
v_{ia\bar{b}}({\mathbf{k}})=i\Delta_{\bar{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})A_{ia\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})
is used.
Next, we show explicitly that Eq. (
S3.1
) cancels Eq. (
S38
).
First, we use
\sum_{a\in\rm occ}\langle a,{\mathbf{k}}|S_{A}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{{\mathbf{k}}}|a,{\mathbf{k}}\rangle
in Eq. (
S3.1
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\rm occ}S_{a,A}[\partial_{i}v_{jaa}({\mathbf{k}})-\langle\partial_{i}a,{\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{j}h_{{\mathbf{k}}}|a,{\mathbf{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle a,{\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{j}h_{{\mathbf{k}}}|\partial_{i}a,{\mathbf{k}}\rangle],

(S40)

where the first term vanishes after integrating over
{\mathbf{k}}
due to the periodicity.
Ignoring the first term, the above equation becomes

\displaystyle i\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in\rm occ}S_{a,A}[A_{iaa^{\prime}}({\mathbf{k}})v_{ja^{\prime}a}({\mathbf{k}})-v_{jaa^{\prime}}({\mathbf{k}})A_{ia^{\prime}a}({\mathbf{k}})]

\displaystyle+i\sum_{a,\overline{b}}S_{a,A}[A_{ia\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})v_{j\overline{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})-v_{ja\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})A_{i\overline{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})],

(S41)

where the first two lines vanish because
a
and
a^{\prime}
are symmetric.
Using
v_{ja\bar{b}}({\mathbf{k}})=i\Delta_{\bar{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})A_{ja\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})
again, we eventually have

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\rm occ}\langle a,{\mathbf{k}}|S_{A}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{{\mathbf{k}}}|a,{\mathbf{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,\bar{b}}\Delta_{\bar{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})S_{a,A}[A_{ia\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})A_{j\overline{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})+(i\leftrightarrow j)].

(S42)

Substituting the above equation into Eq. (
S3.1
), it is straightforward to see that
\sigma_{A,\rm diag}^{ij}
cancels the symmetric part from the Kubo formula.

Therefore, only the antisymmetric part of the Kubo formula is important for the linear response, which takes the form

\displaystyle\Pi^{ij}_{A}(i\nu_{m})=\frac{e}{V}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}},a,\overline{b}}\,\frac{i\nu_{m}}{[\epsilon_{a}({\mathbf{k}})-\epsilon_{\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})]^{2}}S_{a,A}

\displaystyle\times[v_{ia\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})v_{j\overline{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})-(i\leftrightarrow j)].

(S43)

Note that the Abelian Berry connection and curvature are defined by

\displaystyle A_{i,a}({\mathbf{k}})=A_{iaa}({\mathbf{k}}),

(S44)

\displaystyle F^{a}_{ij}({\mathbf{k}})=\partial_{i}A_{j,a}({\mathbf{k}})-\partial_{j}A_{i,a}({\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{\overline{b}\notin\rm occ}\left[A_{ia\overline{b}}({\mathbf{k}})A_{j\overline{b}a}({\mathbf{k}})-(i\leftrightarrow j)\right].

(S45)

Then, Eq. (
S43
) can be simplified to

\displaystyle\Pi^{ij}_{A}(i\nu_{m})=-\frac{e}{V}\nu_{m}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}},a\in\rm occ}S_{a,A}F_{ij}^{a}({\mathbf{k}}).

(S46)

Thus, the conductivity
\sigma_{A}^{ij}
becomes

\displaystyle\sigma^{ij}_{A}=

\displaystyle\lim_{\nu_{m}\to 0}\frac{1}{\nu_{m}}\Pi^{ij}_{A}({\mathbf{k}})

(S47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{a\in\text{occ}}\int d{\mathbf{k}}S_{a,A}F_{ij}^{a}({\mathbf{k}}).

(S48)

From the Stokes theorem,

\displaystyle\int d{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{a\in\text{occ}}S_{a,A}F_{ij}^{a}({\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}\int_{FS_{\gamma}}\epsilon_{ijk}k_{k}\sum_{a\in\text{occ}}S_{a,A}\left(\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}({\mathbf{k}})dS_{\mu}\wedge dS_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\epsilon_{ijk}\sum_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}K_{\alpha,k}S_{\alpha,A}=2\pi\epsilon_{ijk}Q_{kA}.

(S49)

Here
FS_{\gamma}
denotes a set of disjoint Fermi surfaces.
The index
\alpha=(\gamma,a)
in the last line labels both the pseudospin sector and the location in momentum space of the Fermi surfaces.
Note that we set the Fermi level slightly above/below the Weyl points. Hence, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sigma^{ij}_{A}=-\frac{e}{4\pi^{2}}\epsilon_{ijk}Q_{kA}.

(S50)

Then, from
J^{i}_{\rm bulk}=\sigma^{ij}_{A}\mathcal{E}^{j}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{J}^{i}_{A}=\sigma^{ij}_{A}E_{j}
, it is straightforward to derive the bulk responses shown in the main text.

S3.2

In the main text, we showed that the Weyl spin-momentum quadrupole

In this subsection we discuss the symmetry constraints on

We first note that the chiral charge

S4
Anomalies of the generalized spin-momentum locked mode

S4.1
Current anomaly

In this section, we explicitly write down the anomalous conservation laws for the generalized Hamiltonian in Eq. (
S2
).
To simplify the equations, we define
g_{i\pm}=g_{iX}\pm g_{iY}
, and
\sigma_{i\pm}={\rm sgn}(g_{i\pm})
.
The anomalous conservation laws for charge and spin currents can be obtained by inserting gauge fluxes in the same way described in the main text.
Then, the divergence of charge and spin currents are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{X}=-\frac{e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{x+}+\sigma_{x-})E_{x}-\frac{e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{y+}+\sigma_{y-})E_{y},

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{Y}=-\frac{e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{x+}-\sigma_{x-})E_{x}-\frac{e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{y+}-\sigma_{y-})E_{y},

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=-\frac{e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}\large[\sigma_{y+}(\mathcal{E}^{X}_{y}+\mathcal{E}^{Y}_{y})+\sigma_{y-}(\mathcal{E}^{X}_{y}-\mathcal{E}^{Y}_{y})\large]

\displaystyle-\frac{e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}\large[\sigma_{x+}(\mathcal{E}^{X}_{x}+\mathcal{E}^{Y}_{x})+\sigma_{x-}(\mathcal{E}^{X}_{x}-\mathcal{E}^{Y}_{x})\large].

(S55)

The last equation can be further simplified if we introduce
\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{i}=\mathcal{E}^{X}_{i}\pm\mathcal{E}^{Y}_{i}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=-\frac{e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{y+}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{y}+\sigma_{y-}\mathcal{E}^{-}_{y})

\displaystyle-\frac{e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{x+}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{x}+\sigma_{x-}\mathcal{E}^{-}_{x}).

(S56)

This reproduce the anomalous conservation laws discussed in the main text.
By substituting
g_{xY}=1
,
g_{yX}=-1
, and
g_{xX}=g_{yY}=0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{X}=\frac{2e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}E_{y},\quad\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{Y}=-\frac{2e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}E_{x},

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=\frac{2e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{X}_{y}-\frac{2e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{Y}_{x}.

(S57)

Note that
\Lambda_{x}=\Lambda_{y}=\Lambda
is set in the main text because of
C_{4v}
symmetry.

S4.2
Anomaly cancellation and spin-momentum quadrupole

Generalized spin-momentum locked modes cannot exist solely because of the current anomalies.
As discussed in the main text, they can live on the surface of 3D semimetals with proper spin-momentum quadrupole
Q_{iA}
.
We now discuss what the word
proper
means in terms of the Callan-Harvey anomaly cancellation mechanism
Callan Jr and Harvey 1985
.
The bulk response term determined by the spin-momentum quadrupole generally takes the form

\displaystyle S_{CS}[A,a]=\int d^{4}x\alpha\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}Q_{\mu A}\partial_{\nu}a^{A}_{\rho}A_{\sigma},

(S58)

\displaystyle J^{\mu}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta A_{\mu}}=-\alpha\epsilon^{\mu i\nu\rho}Q_{iA}\partial_{\nu}a^{A}_{\rho},

(S59)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{A}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta a^{A}_{\mu}}=-\alpha\epsilon^{\mu i\nu\rho}Q_{iA}\partial_{\nu}A_{\rho}.

(S60)

When the bulk has a constant spin-momentum quadrupole that is denoted as
Q_{0,iA}
, then the presence of boundary with
Q_{iA}(z)=Q_{0,iA}\theta(z)
,
this contributes
\partial_{\mu}J_{b}^{\mu}=\int_{0_{-}}^{0^{+}}dz\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}
and
\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}_{b,A}^{\mu}=\int_{0_{-}}^{0^{+}}dz\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{J}_{b,A}^{\mu}
.
Hence, considering the contributions from both surface and bulk, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(J_{b}^{\mu}+J_{s}^{\mu})=-f_{iA}\mathcal{E}^{A}_{i},

(S61)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(J_{b,A}^{\mu}+J_{s,A}^{\mu})=-f_{iA}E_{i},

(S62)

where the coefficients
f_{iA}
are given by

\displaystyle f_{xX}=\alpha Q_{0,yX}+\frac{e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{x+}+\sigma_{x-}),

(S63)

\displaystyle f_{xY}=\alpha Q_{0,yY}+\frac{e\Lambda_{y}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{x+}-\sigma_{x-}),

(S64)

\displaystyle f_{yX}=-\alpha Q_{0,xX}+\frac{e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{y+}+\sigma_{y-}),

(S65)

\displaystyle f_{yY}=-\alpha Q_{0,xY}+\frac{e\Lambda_{x}}{\pi^{2}}(\sigma_{y+}-\sigma_{y-}).

(S66)

All of the four
f_{iA}
must be zero to have the anomaly cancellation between bulk and surface.
This is equivalent to say that generalized spin-momentum locked modes can only live on the surface of 3D systems with bulk spin-momentum quadrupole satisfying
f_{iA}=0
.

Finally, we comment that, in the presence of symmetries,
Q_{iA}
and
g_{iA}
are further constrained by Eqs. (
S3
) and (
S51
).

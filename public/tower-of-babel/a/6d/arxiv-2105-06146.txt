On quasilinear Maxwell equations in two dimensions Thanks: *Corresponding author
Robert Schippa*, Roland Schnaubelt

Abstract
. New sharp Strichartz estimates for the Maxwell system in two dimensions with rough permittivity and non-trivial charges are proved. We use the FBI transform to carry out the analysis in phase space. For this purpose, the Maxwell equations are conjugated to a system of half-wave equations with rough coefficients. For this system, Strichartz estimates are proved similarly as in previous work by Tataru on scalar wave equations with rough coefficients. We use the estimates to improve the local well-posedness theory for quasilinear Maxwell equations in two dimensions.

1.
Introduction and main results

The Maxwell equations in three spatial dimensions govern the propagation of electromagnetic fields. We refer to the physics literature with its many
excellent accounts (e.g.
[
10
,
20
]
) for explaining the role of

electric and magnetic fields

(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{B}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and

displacement and magnetizing fields

(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The
electric charges

\rho_{e}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
act as sources of the displacement field.
In the following space-time coordinates are denoted by

x=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(t,x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the dual variables in Fourier space
by
\xi=(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In the absence of currents, the Maxwell system in media is given by

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathcal{D}&=\nabla\times\mathcal{H},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=\rho_{e},\\
\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}&=-\nabla\times\mathcal{E},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{B}=0,\\
\mathcal{E}(0,\cdot)&=\mathcal{E}_{0},\quad\mathcal{B}(0,\cdot)=\mathcal{B}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

These equations have to be supplemented with material laws linking, e.g.,
\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathcal{H}
with
\mathcal{B}
.
We consider the
constitutive relations

(2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(x)&=\varepsilon(x)\mathcal{E}(x),\quad\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\\
\mathcal{B}(x)&=\mu(x)\mathcal{H}(x),\quad\mu:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\end{split}

which are linear, pointwise, and instantaneous. The coefficient
\varepsilon
is referred to as

permittivity
and
\mu
as
permeability
. We aim to describe dispersive properties
of electromagnetic fields in possibly anisotropic and inhomogeneous media, so that we allow for

x
-dependent and matrix-valued coefficients. In the following we consider
\mu\equiv 1

for simplicity. The relations (
2
) with

\mu(x)\equiv\mu_{0}
are frequently used to model phenomena in optics (cf.
[
28
]
).
We remark that our arguments extend to a variable permeability provided it satisfies
the same ellipticity and regularity assumptions as the permittivity.

We first focus on rough permittivity
\varepsilon
with coefficients in
C^{s}
for
0<s\leq 2

as an intermediate step to quasilinear Maxwell equations, where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathcal{E})
.
A prominent example is the
Kerr nonlinearity
given by

(3)

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathcal{E})=(1+|\mathcal{E}|^{2}).

In this paper the Maxwell system in two spatial dimensions is considered, which can be derived taking
\mathcal{E}

to be perpendicular to media interfaces (cf.
[
4
,
25
]
). Actually, if
\varepsilon_{j3}=\varepsilon_{3j}=0

for
j\in\{1,2\}
, if
\mathcal{E}_{0}
,
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and
\rho_{e}
in (
1
) only depend on
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
if the components
\mathcal{E}_{03}
,
\mathcal{H}_{01}
and
\mathcal{H}_{02}
vanish, then the solutions
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H})
to (
1
)
have the same properties.
Hence, the resulting Maxwell system in two spatial dimensions is given by

(4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathcal{D}&=\nabla_{\perp}\mathcal{H},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=\rho_{e},\\
\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}&=-\nabla\times\mathcal{E},\\
\mathcal{D}(0,\cdot)&=\mathcal{D}_{0},\quad\mathcal{H}(0,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

In the above display we have
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathcal{H},\rho_{e}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
and we set
\nabla_{\perp}=(\partial_{2},-\partial_{1})^{t}
. We suppose that
\varepsilon
is a matrix-valued
function
\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
such that for some constants
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}>0

and all
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have

(5)

\Lambda_{1}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\varepsilon^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda_{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},\quad\varepsilon^{ij}(x)=\varepsilon^{ji}(x).

Throughout the paper we shall use sum convention and sum over indices appearing twice, e.g.,

\varepsilon^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\varepsilon^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}.

Using the matrix

(6)

P(x,D)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}&0&-\partial_{2}\\
0&\partial_{t}&\partial_{1}\\
-\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{11}\cdot)+\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{21}\cdot)&\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{22}\cdot)-\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{12}\cdot)&\partial_{t}\end{pmatrix}

with rough symbols, the PDEs in (
4
) can be rewritten as

(7)

P(x,D)(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{H})=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=\rho_{e},

where we set

\varepsilon^{-1}(x)=\big(\varepsilon_{ij}(x)\big)_{i,j=1,2}.

Let
P(x,D)(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{H})=(g_{1},g_{2},h)
, where
\mathcal{J}=-(g_{1},g_{2})
is the electric current and
h
has no physical meaning.
Then the electric charges in (
7
) are given by

(8)

\rho_{e}(t)=\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\cdot(g_{1},g_{2})ds.

There is a large body of literature for Maxwell equations on smooth space-times, investigating more fundamental decay properties in higher dimensions (cf.
[
24
,
26
]
). In these works, local energy decay
is proved, which implies Strichartz estimates.

Note that well-posedness of (
7
) with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathcal{E})
in Sobolev spaces

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s>2
can be established by the energy method for hyperbolic systems (cf.
[
2
,
3
,
22
]
). We revisit the argument in the last section and show how Strichartz estimates yield improvements.
For a detailed account on local well-posedness results for the Maxwell system on spatial domains in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with nonlinear material laws, we refer to the PhD thesis
[
34
]
by M. Spitz. In
[
35
]
Spitz showed local well-posedness of the Maxwell equations with perfectly conducting boundary conditions in
H^{3}(G)
for domains
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This approach neglects dispersive effects.

We are not aware of works on Maxwell equations with rough coefficients in the anisotropic case taking
advantage of dispersion. In the isotropic case, i.e.,
\varepsilon(x)=e(x)1_{3\times 3}
with

e:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
e\in C^{2}
, the second author proved global-in-time Strichartz estimates
from local energy decay, jointly with P. D’Ancona
[
7
]
. Local-in-time estimates
for smooth scalar coefficients were treated in
[
9
]
.

We remark that in the constant-coefficient case, Liess
[
21
]
(see also
[
23
]
) showed decay estimates by Fourier analytic methods. Liess pointed out that in three spatial dimensions, the time-decay of
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
is weaker if
\varepsilon
has three different eigenvalues. In this case, the time-decay corresponds to the one of solutions to the two-dimensional wave equations. Hence, in three dimensions additional hypotheses are necessary to recover Strichartz estimates for the three-dimensional wave equation. This will be subject of future work.

In the present paper, we analyze the dispersive properties of the Maxwell system on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for rough
pointwise material laws in the anisotropic case. We prove Strichartz estimates by linking Maxwell equations
to half-wave equations. The connection is established by analysis in phase space.

To relate our problem to the scalar wave equation, let
\mathcal{H}
satisfy (
4
) and

\varepsilon
be time-independent. Differentiating (
4
) in time, we infer

(9)

\partial^{2}_{t}\mathcal{H}=\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{11}\partial_{2}\mathcal{H})-\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{12}\partial_{2}\mathcal{H})-\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{21}\partial_{1}\mathcal{H})+\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{22}\partial_{1}\mathcal{H})=:\Delta_{\varepsilon^{-1}}\mathcal{H}.

Hence,
\mathcal{H}
solves a wave equation with rough coefficients, for which Strichartz estimates are known (cf.
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
). However, we aim to show Strichartz estimates directly for the first-order system (
4
) because taking additional derivatives typically gives rise to loss in regularity.

To put our results into perspective, we review Strichartz estimates for wave equations.
For
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
let

\|u\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x_{0},x^{\prime})|^{q}dx^{\prime}\Big)^{p/q}dx_{0}\Big)^{1/p}.

We will frequently omit to indicate space and time integration in
L^{p}L^{q}
-norms and set
L^{p}=L^{p}L^{p}
.
Keel and Tao
[
17
]
established the sharp range for solutions to the wave equation
with constant coefficients in Euclidean space. Let

u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\Box u=0,\quad u(0)=u_{0},\quad u_{t}(0)=u_{1}.

Then the estimate

(10)

\|u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\rho}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\rho-1}}

holds provided that

\displaystyle 2\leq p,q\leq\infty,\quad\rho=n\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)-\frac{1}{p},

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}+\frac{n-1}{q}\leq\frac{n-1}{2},\quad(p,q,n)\neq(2,\infty,3).

Tupels
(\rho,p,q,n)
satisfying these relations will be called Strichartz pairs. If

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{n-1}{q}=\frac{n-1}{2},

then
(\rho,p,q,n)
will be referred to as sharp Strichartz pair.
Also note that the sharp Strichartz pairs imply the other ones by Sobolev’s embedding.

An important question is how Strichartz estimates extend to variable metrics, i.e.,
g=g(x)
. On the one hand, it is known (cf.
[
15
,
16
,
27
,
29
]
) that solutions to the variable-coefficient wave equation

\Box_{g(x)}u=\partial_{i}g^{ij}\partial_{j}u=0,

with uniformly hyperbolic
g\in C^{\infty}
have the same dispersive properties, at least locally in time, from which Strichartz estimates (
10
) follow.

In view of non-smooth coefficients, Smith and Sogge
[
31
]
pointed out that for merely Hölder-continuous metrics
g\in C^{s}
with
0<s<2
Strichartz estimates (
10
) fail.
On the other hand, Smith
[
30
]
showed that Strichartz estimates remain valid for
C^{2}
-coefficients in low dimensions.
In a series of papers
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
Tataru then recovered Strichartz estimates
(
10
) for wave equations with
C^{2}
-coefficients for all dimensions; see also the
preceding paper
[
1
]
by Bahouri and Chemin and the related work by Klainerman
[
18
]
.
Tataru showed corresponding sharp estimates with additional derivative loss for
C^{s}
-coefficients as a
minor variation of the
C^{2}
-case in
[
38
]
. Smith and Tataru proved sharpness in
[
32
]
.

We show the following theorems for the Maxwell system on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. To state the results, let

\displaystyle(|D|^{\alpha}f)\widehat{\,}(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi),\quad(|D^{\prime}|^{\alpha}f)\widehat{\,}(\xi)=|\xi^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi).

For the sake of simplicity, we suppose the fields to be smooth and understand the Strichartz estimates as a priori estimates. This makes no difference for the application to quasilinear equations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a matrix-valued function with coefficients in
C^{2}
satisfying (
5
). Let
u=(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},H):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\partial_{1}\mathcal{D}_{1}+\partial_{2}\mathcal{D}_{2}=\rho_{e}
, and
P
as in (
6
). Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

(11)

\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\kappa\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\kappa^{-1}\|Pu\|_{L^{2}}+\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}

provided that the right hand-side is finite,
(\rho,p,q,2)
is a Strichartz pair, and

\|\partial^{2}_{x}\varepsilon\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\kappa^{4}.

The space
C^{s}=C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
for
s\geq 0
is equipped with its standard norm.
For vectors the norms are given by
\|u\|_{X}=\|u_{1}\|_{X}+\|u_{2}\|_{X}+\|u_{3}\|_{X}
. The additional parameter
\kappa
is crucial to control the size of coefficients when dealing with quasilinear problems.

Note that (
11
) implies the estimate with
\||D^{\prime}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}
on the right-hand side. Moreover, if
\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}\sim\||D|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{D}\|_{L^{2}}
, the estimate for
the displacement field
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
in
(
11
) already follows from Sobolev’s embedding. Hence, the Strichartz estimates are most
relevant for charges with the additional regularity
\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\|_{L^{2}}
, since in
this case our results point out that the Maxwell system with
C^{2}
-coefficients exhibits the same dispersive properties
as scalar wave equations. This includes the important charge-free case, of course. Furthermore, we note that already in the constant-coefficient case, the Strichartz estimates for the wave equation

\|(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\lesssim\|(\mathcal{D}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{0})\|_{H^{s}}\quad\big(s=2\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\big)-\frac{1}{p}\big)

fail in general: Consider a Strichartz pair
(s,p,q,2)
,
\varepsilon=e1_{2\times 2}
,
e>0
,
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\nabla\varphi_{0}
for
\varphi_{0}\in H^{s+1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}=0
: Then,
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})=(\mathcal{D}_{0},0)
is a stationary solution, but there are
\varphi_{0}\in H^{s+1}
such that
\mathcal{D}_{0}\notin L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
p\neq\infty
.

Truncating the frequencies of
\varepsilon_{ij}
appropriately and using
the above theorem, we can show Strichartz estimates for
C^{s}
-coefficients
if we allow for a loss of derivatives compared to (
11
) as in
[
38
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a matrix-valued function with coefficients in
C^{s}
,
0\leq s<2
, satisfying (
5
). Let
u=(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{H}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\partial_{1}\mathcal{D}_{1}+\partial_{2}\mathcal{D}_{2}=\rho_{e}
. Then, we obtain the estimate

(12)

\||D|^{-\rho-\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\kappa\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\kappa^{-1}\|Pu\|_{\dot{H}^{-\sigma}}+\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}

provided that the right hand-side is finite,
(\rho,p,q,2)
is a Strichartz pair,

\sigma=\frac{2-s}{2+s},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\varepsilon^{ij}\|_{\dot{C}^{s}}\leq\kappa^{4}.

The conclusion of Theorem
1.1
remains true with small modifications if the second
derivatives of the coefficients belong to
L^{1}L^{\infty}
. The motivation for this setup is the quasilinear
case
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathcal{E})
, as discussed below.
For (
7
) we prove the following variant of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a matrix-valued function with Lipschitz coefficients, satisfying (
5
) and
\partial^{2}_{x}\varepsilon\in L^{1}L^{\infty}
. Let
u=(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{H}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\partial_{1}\mathcal{D}_{1}+\partial_{2}\mathcal{D}_{2}=\rho_{e}
, and
(\rho,p,q,2)
be a Strichartz pair. Then,

(13)

\begin{split}\||D^{\prime}|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}&\lesssim\kappa^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\kappa^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|P(x,D)u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}\\
&\quad+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\||D^{\prime}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\||D^{\prime}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{t}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}},\end{split}

whenever the right-hand side is finite, provided that
\kappa\geq 1
and

T\|\partial^{2}_{x}\varepsilon\|_{L^{1}L^{\infty}}\leq\kappa^{2}.

Observe that
\rho_{e}(0)=\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}_{0}
and
\partial_{t}\rho_{e}=\partial_{1}(Pu)_{1}+\partial_{2}(Pu)_{2}

due to (
8
). Compared to (
11
), above one thus takes the
L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}
norm
into sum and integral apearing in (
8
). This happens when passing to
L^{\infty}L^{2}
- and
L^{1}L^{2}
-norms
on the right-hand side of (
13
),
see Paragraph
3.4.2
. These norms are better suited for the application to
quasilinear problems than the
L^{2}
-norms appearing on the right-hand side of (
11
).

But for these applications one still needs a version for coefficients with less regularity.
To state it, let
(S_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}
denote a homogeneous Littlewood–Paley decomposition in space-time, and
(S_{\lambda}^{\prime})_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}
one in the spatial variables only. To avoid problems when summing norms on Littlewood–Paley blocks, the regularity of solutions is measured in homogeneous Besov-type spaces
\dot{B}^{pqr}_{s}
with norms

\|u\|^{r}_{\dot{B}^{pqr}_{s}}=\sum_{\lambda}\lambda^{rs}\|S_{\lambda}u\|^{r}_{L^{p}L^{q}},

and the usual modification for
r=\infty
. For the coefficients,
following
[
40
,
39
]
we use the microlocalizable scale of spaces
\mathcal{X}^{s}
given by

\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}^{s}}=\sup\nolimits_{\lambda}\lambda^{s}\|S_{\lambda}v\|_{L^{1}L^{\infty}}.

The
\mathcal{X}^{s}
-regularity is an adequate substitute for the
C^{s}
-regularity of the coefficients in our setting.
For these regularities we prove the following Strichartz estimate.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{X}^{s}
,
0\leq s<2
, and
u
,
(\rho,p,q,2)
and
\sigma
be as in the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

(14)

\begin{split}\||D|^{-\rho-\frac{\sigma}{p}}u\|_{\dot{B}^{pq\infty}_{0}}&\lesssim\kappa^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\kappa^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\||D|^{-\sigma}Pu\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}\\
&\quad+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\partial_{t}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}\end{split}

for all
u
compactly supported in
[0,T]
and
\kappa,T
satisfying

T^{s}\|\varepsilon\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{s}}\lesssim\kappa^{2+s}.

Estimates involving the norm of
P(x,D)u
in
L^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}
follow as in

[
38
,
39
]
. We sketch the proof for the next result with
C^{2}
coefficients.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\varepsilon\in C^{2}
and
(\rho,p,q,2)
be a Strichartz pair. Then, we have

(15)

\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\kappa\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\kappa^{-1}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\||D|^{\rho}f_{2}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}+\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}

whenever

P(x,D)u=f_{1}+f_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varepsilon\|_{\infty}\leq\kappa^{4}.

One can establish versions for
C^{s}
-coefficients with
0\leq s<2
, cf.
[
38
, Theorem 4]
, and for

\mathcal{X}^{s}
-coefficients, cf.
[
39
, Corollary 1.6]
, using arguments from these papers.

On a finite time interval, the homogeneous problem (
4
) can easily be treated
by the above results if
\partial_{t}\varepsilon\in L^{1}L^{\infty}
, since a standard energy estimate yields

(16)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim e^{c\|\partial_{t}\varepsilon\|_{L^{1}L^{\infty}}}\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

As in
[
38
, Corollary 5]
, we can also prove estimates with two different Strichartz pairs. We provide such a result on a finite time interval
(0,T)
, fixing
\delta
and
T
, and we further suppose that the solutions are charge-free and
\varepsilon
is isotropic. These limitations stem from the use of duality in the proof. Perhaps the latter assumptions can be weakened by modifying the proof of Theorem
1.5
to treat inhomogeneous terms
\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{\tilde{\rho}}f\in L^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}
. This is not pursued presently.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\varepsilon=eI_{2\times 2}\in C^{s}
,
1\leq s\leq 2
,
P(x,D)u=f
,
\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}=0
, and
(\rho,p,q,2)
,
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{p},\tilde{q},2)
be Strichartz pairs. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

(17)

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\rho-\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\lesssim_{T,\kappa}\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{\tilde{\rho}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}f\|_{L^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}(0,T;L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}})}.

The proof relies on a now standard application of the Christ–Kiselev lemma,
[
6
]
. However, as the time-dependent generators of the Maxwell system are not self-adjoint in
L^{2}
, additional considerations are necessary.

To study quasilinear equations, we use a similar result in the context of
Theorem
1.4
, cf. Corollary 1.7 in
[
39
]
.
The quantity
\|\partial_{x}\varepsilon\|_{L^{2}L^{\infty}}
can be controlled for coefficients

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathcal{E})
arising in a bootstrap argument.

Corollary 1.7
.

Assume that
\|\partial_{x}\varepsilon\|_{L^{2}L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1
and for some
\tilde{s}\in[1,2)
, suppose that
\|\varepsilon\|_{\mathcal{X}^{\tilde{s}}}\lesssim 1
. Let
(\rho,p,q,2)
be a Strichartz pair. Then the solution
u
to

\left\{\begin{aligned} P(x,D)u&=f,\qquad\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}=\rho_{e},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

satisfies

\begin{split}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}&\lesssim_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2})}\\
&\qquad+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\rho_{e}(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\partial_{t}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2})}\end{split}

for
\alpha>\rho+\frac{\sigma}{p}
and
\sigma=\sigma(\tilde{s})=\frac{2-\tilde{s}}{2+\tilde{s}}

For the proof of Theorem

we shall make use of the FBI transform, conjugating the problem to phase space. Prior to the phase space analysis, we make the following reductions already pointed out by Tataru
[

]
and also in earlier work

in the context of wave equations with rough coefficients. The first reduction consists in a direct estimate of low frequencies
\lesssim 1

\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
and with coefficients of the permittivity truncated at frequencies
<\lambda^{1/2}
. Finally, we reduce to the case of frequencies
|\xi_{0}|\lesssim|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|
by estimating the contributions from the region
|\xi_{0}|\gg|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|
off the light cone using properties of the FBI transform.

After these reductions, we can diagonalize (

\|u\|_{2}
and the divergence of
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})

L^{2}
-estimates, since we cannot spare several derivatives in the spatial variables. In our opinion the diagonalization procedure is the main novelty of the paper. It quantifies the hyperbolic degeneracy of the Maxwell system through the electric charges and allows to recover wave Strichartz estimates in the charge-free case. The method of proof possibly extends to other first-order systems like Dirac equations with variable coefficients (cf.
[

2.
Pseudo-differential operators with rough symbols and properties of the FBI transform

This section is devoted to preliminaries on the encountered pseudo-differential operators and the FBI transform. As in Tataru’s works
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
, we make use of the latter to find suitable conjugates of pseudo-differential operators in phase space. In these references, the key application was to use the conjugate of the rough wave operator as weight in phase space to prove Strichartz estimates. In the present paper, rough symbols additionally come up when conjugating Maxwell equations to a diagonal system of scalar half-wave equations. Thus, we have to analyze the
L^{2}
-boundedness and compositions of rough symbols. Taylor’s monograph
[
40
]
contains many results for symbols which are not smooth in the spatial variables. Here we state the results in the form needed in the present context, and we shall revisit some of the arguments as these will be used in later sections.

2.1.
The FBI transform

We first recall basic facts about the FBI transform (cf.
[
8
,
37
]
). For
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, the FBI transform of an integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is defined by

\begin{split}T_{\lambda}f(z)&=C_{m}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(z-y)^{2}}f(y)dy,\quad z=x-i\xi\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{m}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{2m},\\
C_{m}&=2^{-\frac{m}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{3m}{4}}.\end{split}

We have the isometric mapping property
T_{\lambda}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\Phi}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
\Phi(z)=e^{-\lambda\xi^{2}}
. It is natural to write
z=x-i\xi
since
T_{\lambda}f
is in fact holomorphic.
The connection with the Fourier transform is emphasized by writing

T_{\lambda}f(z)=C_{m}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{4}}e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\xi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(x-y)^{2}}e^{i\lambda\xi.(x-y)}f(y)dy.

An inversion formula for the FBI transform is given by the adjoint in
L^{2}_{\Phi}
:

T_{\lambda}^{*}F(y)=C_{m}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(\overline{z}-y)^{2}}\Phi(z)F(z)dxd\xi.

We recall the following identities for conjugating symbols with the FBI transform.
In the following we consider symbols
a(x,\xi)\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}
compactly supported in
\xi
. More specifically, we shall assume

a(x,\xi)=0\text{ for }\xi\notin B(0,2).

Let
a_{\lambda}(x,\xi)=a(x,\xi/\lambda)
denote the scaled symbol supported at frequencies
\lesssim\lambda
, and

A_{\lambda}=A_{\lambda}(x,D)
be the corresponding pseudo-differential operator.

As in
[
37
,
38
]
, the idea is to find an ‘approximate conjugate’
\tilde{A}_{\lambda}
of
A_{\lambda}
such that

T_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}(y,D)\approx\tilde{A}_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}.

We record the basic identities

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}(yf)(z)

\displaystyle=(x+\frac{1}{-i\lambda}(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda\xi))T_{\lambda}f,

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}(\frac{D}{\lambda}f)(z)

\displaystyle=(\xi+\frac{1}{\lambda}(\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x}-\lambda\xi))T_{\lambda}f,

yielding the formal asymptotics

T_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}(x,D)\approx\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda\xi)^{\alpha}\frac{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)}{|\alpha|!|\beta|!(-i\lambda)^{|\alpha|}\lambda^{|\beta|}}(\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x}-\lambda\xi)^{\beta}T_{\lambda}.

We recall error bounds for the truncated approximations

\tilde{a}^{s}_{\lambda}=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|<s}(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda\xi)^{\alpha}\frac{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)}{|\alpha|!|\beta|!(-i\lambda)^{|\alpha|}\lambda^{|\beta|}}(\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x}-\lambda\xi)^{\beta}.

For
s\leq 1
, we have

\tilde{a}^{s}_{\lambda}=a,

and for
1<s\leq 2
,

(21)

\tilde{a}^{s}_{\lambda}=a+\frac{1}{-i\lambda}a_{x}(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda\xi)+\frac{1}{\lambda}a_{\xi}(\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x}-\lambda\xi)=a+\frac{2}{\lambda}(\overline{\partial}a)(\partial-i\lambda\xi),

where
\partial=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}+i\partial_{\xi})
and
\overline{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{\xi})
.
We will not need higher approximations because for coefficients in
C^{2}
the Strichartz estimates for the Euclidean (half-)wave equation hold true, which are known to be optimal (cf.
[
17
]
).
To prove Theorem
1.1
, it will be enough to use the first-order approximation from the previous display.

Consider the remainder

(22)

R^{s}_{\lambda,a}=T_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}-\tilde{a}^{s}_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}.

In
[
37
,
38
]
the following approximation result was proved.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
38
, Theorem 5, p. 393]
)
.

Suppose that
a\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|R^{s}_{\lambda,a}\|_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}_{\Phi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-s/2},

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda)R^{s}_{\lambda,a}\|_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}_{\Phi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1/2-s/2}.

To prove our main results, we use the following multiplier theorem for
T_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
a\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
a(x,\xi)=0
for
\xi\notin B(0,2)
, and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq m+1}\|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}a(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}}\leq C.

Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

\|T_{\lambda}^{*}a(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}f\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\lesssim C\|f\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}.

Proof.

We start with the special case
b\in L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
c\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
a(x,\xi)=b(x)c(\xi)
. It can be treated by a straight-forward kernel estimate. We first compute

(23)

\begin{split}&T_{\lambda}^{*}(b(x)c(\xi))T_{\lambda}f(y)\\
&=C_{m}^{2}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(\overline{z}-y)^{2}}e^{-\lambda\xi^{2}}b(x)c(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(z-y^{\prime})^{2}}f(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}dxd\xi\\
&=C_{m}^{2}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(x+i\xi-y)^{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(x-i\xi-y^{\prime})^{2}}e^{-\lambda\xi^{2}}b(x)c(\xi)dxd\xi f(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\\
&=C_{m}^{2}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}((x-y)^{2}+(x-y^{\prime})^{2})}b(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{i\lambda\xi.(y-y^{\prime})}c(\xi)d\xi f(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\\
&=:C_{m}^{2}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}B_{\lambda}(y,y^{\prime})\hat{c}(\lambda(y-y^{\prime}))f(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}.\end{split}

Note that

|B_{\lambda}(y,y^{\prime})|\leq\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\lambda x^{2}}dx\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{m}{2}}\|b\|_{L^{\infty}},

and further,

|\hat{c}(\lambda(y-y^{\prime}))|\leq C_{N}(1+\lambda|y-y^{\prime}|)^{-N}\quad\text{ for any }N\in\mathbb{N}.

Write
y=(y_{1},y_{r})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-1}
and likewise for
y^{\prime}
. Two successive applications of Young’s inequality imply

\begin{split}&\quad\lambda^{m}\|\int(1+\lambda|y-y^{\prime}|)^{-N}f(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda^{m}\|\int(1+\lambda|y_{1}-y_{1}^{\prime}|)^{-\frac{N}{2}}(1+\lambda|y_{r}-y_{r}^{\prime}|)^{-\frac{N}{2}}f(y_{1}^{\prime},y_{r}^{\prime})dy^{\prime}\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda\int(1+\lambda|y_{1}-y_{1}^{\prime}|)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|f(y_{1}^{\prime},\cdot)\|_{L^{q}_{y_{r}^{\prime}}}dy_{1}^{\prime}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\end{split}

for large
N
. The two estimates for
B_{\lambda}
and
\hat{c}
yield

\|T_{\lambda}^{*}(b(x)c(\xi))T_{\lambda}f\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\lesssim\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\|c\|_{C^{N}}\|f\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}.

We turn to the case of general
a(x,\xi)
according to the assumptions. Let
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
\beta\equiv 1
for
\{|\xi|\leq 2\}
and
\text{supp}(\beta)\subseteq B(0,3)
. The main reduction is an expansion into the rapidly converging Fourier series

a(x,\xi)=\beta(\xi)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}e^{ik\xi}\hat{a}_{k}(x),\quad\hat{a}_{k}(x)=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{n}}e^{-ik\xi}a(x,\xi)d\xi,\quad\beta_{k}(\xi)=e^{ik\xi}\beta(\xi)

(cf.
[
40
]
). Hence,

\|T_{\lambda}^{*}a(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}_{x_{0}}L^{q}_{x^{\prime}}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|T_{\lambda}^{*}(\hat{a}_{k}(x)\beta_{k}(\xi))T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}_{x_{0}}L^{q}_{x^{\prime}}}.

Integration by parts yields

|\hat{a}_{k}(x)|\lesssim_{\ell}(1+|k|)^{-\ell}\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq\ell}\|D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}}.

Take
\ell=m+1
so that
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}(1+|k|)^{-\ell}\lesssim_{m}1
. In this case, in (
23
) we estimate the kernel
B_{\lambda}(y,y^{\prime})
by

|B_{\lambda}(y,y^{\prime})|\lesssim_{m}(1+|k|)^{-\ell}\lambda^{-\frac{m}{2}}\sup_{x}\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq\ell}\|D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\cdot)\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\lesssim_{m}(1+|k|)^{-\ell}C\lambda^{-\frac{m}{2}},

and find
\hat{c}(\lambda(y-y^{\prime}))=\hat{\beta}(k+\lambda(y-y^{\prime}))
. Taking absolute values, we infer

T_{\lambda}^{*}(\hat{a}_{k}(x)\beta_{k}(\xi)T_{\lambda}f)(y)\lesssim C_{a}(1+|k|)^{-\ell}\lambda^{m}\int|\hat{\beta}(k+\lambda(y-y^{\prime}))f(y^{\prime})|dy^{\prime}.

Since

\|\int|\hat{\beta}(k+\lambda(\cdot-y^{\prime}))||f(y^{\prime})|dy^{\prime}\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}=\|\int|\hat{\beta}(\lambda(\cdot-y^{\prime}))||f(y^{\prime})|dy^{\prime}\|_{L_{x_{0}}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}},

we can finish the proof by

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|T_{\lambda}^{*}(\hat{a}_{k}(x)e^{ik.\xi}\beta(\xi))T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim_{m}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}(1+|k|)^{-\ell}C\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim_{m}C\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}.\qed

2.2.
Properties of rough symbols

In this subsection compositions of pseudo-differential operators are recalled and their
L^{2}
-boundedness is quantified. The theory for smooth symbols is vast (cf.
[
13
,
33
,
40
]
). For instance, the
L^{p}
-boundedness of symbols
a\in S_{1,\delta}^{0}
,
0\leq\delta<1
, is well-known, see
[
40
, Section 0.11]
. Here we give a proof which quantifies in particular the
L^{2}
-boundedness of symbols
a\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}
, see
[
40
, Chapter 2]
. The argument is detailed as it becomes important in later sections.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, and
a\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
a(x,\xi)=0
for
\xi\notin B(0,2)
. Suppose that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq m+1}\|D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\cdot)\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\leq C.

Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

\|a(x,D)f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim C\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}.

Proof.

We first consider the special case of separated variables
a(x,\xi)=b(x)c(\xi)
. Hölder’s and Young’s inequality give

\begin{split}\|a(x,D)f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}&=\|b(x)c(D)f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\leq\|b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\|c(D)f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\\
&\leq\|b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\|\check{c}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}.\end{split}

Integrating by parts to estimate
\|\check{c}\|_{L^{1}}
yields
\|b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\|\check{c}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim C
.

In the general case, we write

\begin{split}a(x,D)f&=(2\pi)^{-m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi=(2\pi)^{-m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\beta(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\end{split}

for
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{c}
with support in
[-\pi,\pi]^{m}
and
\beta(\xi)\equiv 1
on
B(0,2)
. We expand
a(x,\xi)\beta(\xi)
into the Fourier series in
\xi

a(x,\xi)\beta(\xi)=\beta(\xi)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}a_{k}(x)e^{ik\xi},

with
a_{k}(x)=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{m}}e^{-ik\xi}a(x,\xi)d\xi
.

We shall estimate every single term
a_{k}(x)e^{ik\xi}\beta(\xi)
via the above argument and then sum over
k
using decay from the regularity in
\xi
. We have

\int a_{k}(x)e^{ik\xi}\beta(\xi)e^{ix\xi}\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi=a_{k}(x)(\beta(D)f)(x+k).

We find
|a_{k}(x)|\leq\|a(x,\cdot)\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}
, and
\|\beta(D)f(\cdot+k)\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}

by Young’s inequality and translation invariance. This estimate does not decay in
k
sufficiently.
For decay in
k
, we integrate by parts in
\xi
obtaining

|a_{k}(x)|\lesssim_{\ell}(1+|k|)^{-\ell}\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq\ell}\|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}a(x,\cdot)\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}.

For
\ell=m+1
this is summable in
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, and we estimate

\displaystyle\|a(x,D)f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|a_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}(1+|k|)^{-(m+1)}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\big(\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq m+1}\|D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\cdot)\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\big)\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}.\qed

We turn to compound symbols. As we shall see in Section
3
, it suffices to prove dyadic estimates
1
1

1

Here we suppose that we are in the charge-free case for simplicity of exposition.

\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|P^{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}},

where
S_{\lambda}
localizes to frequencies of size
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
P^{\lambda}
denotes the operator

P^{\lambda}(x,D)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}&0&-\partial_{2}\\
0&\partial_{t}&\partial_{1}\\
-\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{11}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)+\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{21}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)&\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{22}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)-\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{12}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)&\partial_{t}\end{pmatrix}.

For the components of the permittivity,
\varepsilon_{ij}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}
means that the frequencies are truncated to size at most
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Recall the symbol classes for
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq\delta<\rho\leq 1
:

S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}=\{a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})\,:\,|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\rho|\beta|+\delta|\alpha|}\}.

Hence, the pseudo-differential operators we encounter are smooth in
x
and the considered symbols are in

S^{m}_{1,\frac{1}{2}}
. Boundedness
P(x,D):H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{k-m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is proved in
[
40
, Prop. 0.5E]
.
Using Lemma
2.3
, we will show that the estimates are independent of the dyadic frequency

\lambda

(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N})_{22}&=-\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}[\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{11}\cdot)\partial_{t}\partial_{2}-\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{12}\cdot)\partial_{t}\partial_{2}+\partial_{1}\partial_{t}(\varepsilon_{22}\cdot)\partial_{1}-\partial_{1}\partial_{t}(\varepsilon_{12}\cdot)\partial_{2}]\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}},\\
(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N})_{23}&=\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{1}D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}},\\
(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N})_{31}&=D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}((\varepsilon_{12}\cdot)\partial_{1}-(\varepsilon_{11}\cdot)\partial_{2})\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}},\\

3.2.
Reductions for

-coefficients

Next, we carry out the reductions for
u
and
\varepsilon_{ij}
needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
, that is

•

localization to a cube of size
1
and to high frequencies,

•

•

•

Before these steps, by scaling we can assume that

3.2.1.
Localization to a cube of size 1 and to high frequencies

We first show that it is sufficient to estimate
\|\langle D\rangle^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}
instead of
\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}
.
Let
s(\xi)
be a symbol supported in
\{1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
such that

\sum_{j}s(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\{0\}.

For
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, let
S_{\lambda}=S(D/\lambda)
denote the Littlewood–Paley multiplier, which localizes to frequencies of size
\lambda
and
S_{0}=1-\sum_{j\geq 0}S_{2^{j}}
. Write
u=S_{0}u+(1-S_{0})u
.

Sobolev’s embedding and the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality yield

\||D|^{-\rho}S_{0}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|S_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

For the contribution of
(1-S_{0})u
in (
11
) we observe that

\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}(1-S_{0})u)=\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}u)-\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}S_{0}u)

and

\begin{split}\|\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}S_{0}u)\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{ij}\partial_{k}S_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{k}\varepsilon_{ij})S_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|S_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{k}\varepsilon_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|S_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{ij}\|_{C^{1}}\|S_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Hence,
\|P(1-S_{0})u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|Pu\|_{L^{2}}+\|\varepsilon_{ij}\|_{C^{1}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.

This means that low frequencies can always be estimated by the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and Sobolev’s embedding, so that we can assume that
u
has only large frequencies. It suffices to prove

\|(1+|D|^{2})^{\frac{-\rho}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|Pu\|_{L^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}.

Take a smooth partition of unity

1=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}}\chi_{j}(x),\quad\chi_{j}(x)=\chi(x-j),\quad\text{supp}(\chi)\subseteq B(0,2),

and let
\rho_{j}=\partial_{1}(\chi_{j}u_{1})+\partial_{2}(\chi_{j}u_{2})
. By considering commutators, we first note

\sum_{j}\Big(\|\chi_{j}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|P(\chi_{j}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Pu\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, since
p,q\geq 2
we have

(36)

\|(1+|D|^{2})^{\frac{-\rho}{2}}u\|^{2}_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\sum_{j}\|(1+|D|^{2})^{\frac{-\rho}{2}}\chi_{j}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}^{2}.

To prove the latter estimate, we write

(1+|D|^{2})^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}u=\sum_{j,k}\chi_{j}(x)(1+|D|^{2})^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\chi_{k}(x)u.

If
|j-k|\geq 100
, for the distributional kernel of
\chi_{j}(1+|D|^{2})^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\chi_{k}

we find the bound

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}|G(x,y)|

\displaystyle=|\int e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\chi_{j}(x)\chi_{k}(y)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}d\xi|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}(1+|j-k|)^{-N}(1+|x-y|)^{-N}

since
|x-y|\gtrsim|j-k|\geq 1
. Hence, we can estimate
\chi_{j}(1+|D|^{2})^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\chi_{k}u

for
|j-k|\geq 10n
using Young’s inequality and obtain

\|(1+|D|^{2})^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}u\|^{2}_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sum_{j,k:|j-k|\leq 10n}\chi_{k}(1+|D|^{2})^{-\rho/2}\chi_{j}u\|^{2}_{L^{p}L^{q}}.

Let
\tilde{\chi}_{j}=\sum_{|k-j|\leq 10n}\chi_{k}
. Due to the pointwise bound

\sum_{j}{\tilde{\chi}_{j}}(1+|D|^{2})^{-\rho/2}\chi_{j}u\lesssim\big(\sum_{j}|\tilde{\chi}_{j}(1+|D|^{2})^{-\rho/2}\chi_{j}u|^{2}\big)^{1/2},

and Minkowski’s inequality for
p,q\geq 2
, we conclude the proof of (
36
). It remains to show

(37)

\sum_{j}\|\langle D\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\langle D\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This fact is a consequence of the inequality
\sum_{j}\|\chi_{j}v\|^{2}_{H^{-1/2}}\leq\|v\|^{2}_{H^{-1/2}}
which is dual to

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\sum_{j}\|\chi_{j}u\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

The above estimate follows by interpolation from its elementary variants in
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
. This concludes the reduction to compact support, and we suppose in the following that
u
is supported in the unit cube.

3.2.2.
Reduction to dyadic estimates

Here we shall see that it is enough to prove

(38)

\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|PS_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}

for
\lambda\geq 1
. To reduce to the above display, we have to show the commutator estimate

\sum_{\lambda=2^{j}\geq 1}\|[P,S_{\lambda}]u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Set
\tilde{S}_{\lambda}=\sum_{|j|\leq 2}S_{2^{j}\lambda}
as a mildly enlarged version of
S_{\lambda}
, and
write
[P,S_{\lambda}]=[P,S_{\lambda}]\tilde{S}_{\lambda}-S_{\lambda}P(1-\tilde{S}_{\lambda})
. We need the inequalities

\begin{split}\|[P,S_{\lambda}]v\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}},\\
\|S_{\lambda}P(1-\tilde{S}_{\lambda})u\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

for some
\delta>0
. Since the commutator

[\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}\cdot),S_{\lambda}]v=\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}S_{\lambda}v)-S_{\lambda}\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}v)=\partial_{k}[\varepsilon_{ij},S_{\lambda}]v

has the kernel
K(x,y)=\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}(x)-\varepsilon_{ij}(y))\hat{s}(\lambda(x-y))\lambda^{n+1}
,
the
L^{2}
-boundedness follows from
\varepsilon\in C^{2}
.
For the second term note that only frequencies of
\varepsilon_{ij}
of size
\lambda
and higher matter as

S_{\lambda}\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}(1-\tilde{S}_{\lambda})u)=S_{\lambda}\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}^{\gtrsim\lambda}(1-\tilde{S}_{\lambda})u).

We thus find

\|S_{\lambda}\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}^{\gtrsim\lambda}(1-\tilde{S}_{\lambda})u)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda\|\varepsilon_{ij}^{\gtrsim\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|\varepsilon_{ij}\|_{C^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}},

due to the standard estimate

\sup_{\lambda}\lambda^{2}\|\varepsilon_{ij}^{\geq\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{ij}\|_{C^{2}}.

Observe that
S_{\lambda}u
is no supported in the unit cube anymore. It is still rapidly decreasing, which suffices
for the arguments in Section
4
, cf. (
55
).

3.2.3.
Truncating the coefficients of
P
at frequency
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}

We check that it is enough to prove (
38
) when the coefficients have Fourier transform supported in
\{|\xi|\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
. We stress that the uniform ellipticity for
\varepsilon
after frequency truncation will still be important. For that purpose, we observe that
\|\varepsilon_{ij}^{\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|\varepsilon_{ij}^{\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{C^{2}}
, and thus

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ij}^{\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}-\varepsilon_{ij}^{\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq(\Lambda_{1}-C\lambda^{-1})\|\xi^{\prime}\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{1}\|\xi^{\prime}\|^{2}

for sufficiently large
\lambda\geq 1
.

The error term coming from frequency truncation in (
38
) is estimated by

\begin{split}\|\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}^{\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}S_{\lambda}u)\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\|\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}^{\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\varepsilon^{\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{ij}\partial_{k}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{ij}\|_{C^{1}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda\|\varepsilon_{ij}^{\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

which is bounded by the first term on the right-hand side of (
38
). In the following we write
\varepsilon^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{ij}=\varepsilon^{\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{ij}
. Consequently, it is enough to prove

(39)

\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|P^{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}},

with the operator

P^{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}&0&-\partial_{2}\\
0&\partial_{t}&\partial_{1}\\
-\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{11}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)+\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{12}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)&\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{22}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)-\partial_{2}(\varepsilon_{12}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot)&\partial_{t}\end{pmatrix},

whose coefficients are truncated at frequency
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
. If there is no possibility of confusion,
the frequency truncation of the coefficients will be implicit in the following, and we write
P
instead of
P^{\lambda}
.

3.2.4.
Reduction to half-wave equations

We shall consider the two refined regions
\{|\xi_{0}|\gg|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\}
and

\{|\xi_{0}|\lesssim|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\}
. As noticed above, the first region is away from the characteristic
surface. To deal with it, we first apply Theorem
2.1
obtaining

\|T_{\lambda}\left(\frac{P(x,D)}{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u\right)-p(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}.

Suppose that
u
has frequencies in
\{|\xi_{0}|\gg|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\}
. By Theorem
2.1
,
the same is true for
v_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u
up to an acceptable error

\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}
. For such
v_{\lambda}
it is easy to see that

\|v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}\lesssim\|p(x,\xi)v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}

because

p(x,\xi)=m(x,\xi)d(x,\xi)m^{-1}(x,\xi)\quad\text{ and }\quad|d_{ii}|\gtrsim|\xi_{0}|\gtrsim 1.

Using also the
L^{2}
-mapping properties of
T_{\lambda}
and the triangle inequality, we deduce

\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}=\|v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-1}\|P(x,D)S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}.

Sobolev’s embedding thus yields

\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\rho}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|P(x,D)S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}.

So (
39
) is true if the frequencies of
u
are confined to

\{|\xi_{0}|\gg|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\}
.

We turn to the main contribution coming from
\{|\xi_{0}|\lesssim|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|\}
where the
characteristic surfaces are contained. In the following we assume that the space-time Fourier transform of
u

is supported in this region. We first prove

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}w\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{D}S_{\lambda}w\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{D}S_{\lambda}w\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}},

where
w=\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u
and as above

\mathcal{D}=\begin{pmatrix}i\partial_{t}&0&0\\
0&i\partial_{t}-D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}&0\\
0&0&i\partial_{t}+D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\end{pmatrix}.

The estimates of the second and third component of
S_{\lambda}w
are a consequence of Proposition
1.8
to be established in Section
4
.
Here the charge
\rho_{e}
does not enter. For the first component, as in Subsection
3.3

below one shows that the product
\partial_{1}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}S_{\lambda}
has the symbol
i\xi_{j}^{*}s_{\lambda}(\xi)

up to an error bounded by
c\lambda^{-1/2}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}
. Combined with Theorem
2.1
, we deduce

\|T_{\lambda}w_{1}-[m^{-1}(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u]_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}.

By
\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}=\rho_{e}
and Theorem
2.1
, we find

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle[m^{-1}(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u]_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}+\|T_{\lambda}(\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{1}S_{\lambda}u_{1}+\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{2}S_{\lambda}u_{2})-i\xi_{1}^{*}T_{\lambda}u_{1}-i\xi_{2}^{*}T_{\lambda}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}.

Recall that the ultimate estimate for the first term is a consequence of Lemma
2.3
and the previous frequency localization
\{|\xi^{\prime}|\sim|\xi|\}
.

Hence, using Sobolev’s embedding and the
L^{2}
-mapping properties of the FBI-transform,
the contribution of
S_{\lambda}w_{1}
can be estimated by

\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}w_{1}\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{\rho+\frac{1}{2}-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}w_{1}\|_{L^{2}}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|T_{\lambda}w_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\Phi}}\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}},

which gives the estimate for
w_{1}
. So far, we have proved that

\begin{split}\lambda^{-\rho}\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}&\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{D}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\end{split}

For (
38
), we yet have to show that

(40)

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-\rho}\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}+\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}},

(41)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{M}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{D}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}},

and (
42
) below.
We start with the proof of (
41
). Let
\tilde{w}=\mathcal{D}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u
. We can as well consider
\tilde{w}=\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{D}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}S_{\lambda}u
as
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{N}
respect frequency localization up to negligible errors. Theorem
2.1
and calculations as in the next subsection yield

\|T_{\lambda}^{*}m(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{w}-\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{M}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}}.

\begin{split}\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}}&=\|T_{\lambda}^{*}m^{-1}(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}^{*}m(x,\xi)T_{\lambda}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}}\\

where

For the proof of (

Consequently,

-\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\Delta_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}=S_{\lambda}^{\prime}+E_{N^{\prime}}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}\quad\text{ with \ }\|E_{N^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}.

Finally, for time-dependent
\tilde{\varepsilon}
, we compute as above

\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\partial_{t}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}=\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{t}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}+O_{L^{2}}(\lambda^{-1}),

and in the same venue,

\|\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{i}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\partial_{t}(\tilde{\varepsilon}^{ij}_{\lambda^{1/2}})\partial_{j}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1.

Estimate for

(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N}\big)_{13}
: We have to prove

\|\big(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N}\big)_{13}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}-P_{13}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=\|{-}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{2}D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}+\partial_{2}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1.

First note that

\|{-}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{2}\tilde{S}_{\mu}^{\prime}D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{N}(\mu\vee\lambda)^{-N}

for
\mu\ll\lambda
or
\mu\gg\lambda
because of the estimates

\|S^{\prime}_{\mu}D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{N}(\mu\vee\lambda)^{-N}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{i}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\mu,

see Lemma
2.5
. Hence, it is enough to show

\|{-}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\partial_{2}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}+\partial_{2}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1.

The first operator is composed of the two operators with symbols

p(x,\xi)=-\frac{1}{\|\xi\|_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}(i\xi_{2})\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(\xi)\quad\text{ and }\quad q(x,\xi)=\|\xi\|_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}a_{\lambda}(\xi).

We find

(P\circ Q)(x,D)=Op(-i\xi_{2}a_{\lambda}(\xi))+\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq N^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}Op(D_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(x,\xi)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}q(x,\xi))+R_{N^{\prime}}

with
\|R_{N^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{N^{\prime}}\lambda^{-N}
.
The operators
E^{\alpha}
in the sum over
\alpha
can be bounded by collecting powers of
\lambda
and
exploiting homogeneity, where one obtains
\lambda^{-|\alpha|}
from
D_{\xi}^{\alpha}p

and
\lambda^{\frac{|\alpha|+1}{2}}
from
D_{x}^{\alpha}q
. In total, we find the inequality

\|E^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1-|\alpha|}{2}}
, and conclude the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq N^{\prime}}E^{\alpha}
.
The proof is complete since

Op(i\xi_{2}a_{\lambda}(\xi))=\partial_{2}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}.

Estimate for
\big(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N}\big)_{31}
:
Below we show

\|\big[\big(\mathcal{M}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N}\big)_{31}+\partial_{2}\big(\varepsilon_{11}^{\lambda^{1/2}}\cdot\big)-\partial_{1}(\varepsilon_{12}^{\lambda^{1/2}}\cdot)\big]S_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1.

First,
((\partial_{2}\varepsilon_{11})-(\partial_{1}\varepsilon_{12}))(\cdot)
is bounded in
L^{2}
. As above the contribution of
\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\mu}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
can be neglected for
\lambda\ll\mu
or
\mu\ll\lambda
. It remains to verify

\|{-}D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\tilde{S^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\varepsilon_{11}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial_{2}\tilde{S^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}+\varepsilon^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{11}\partial_{2}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1

since the terms containing
\varepsilon_{12}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}
can be estimated in the same venue.

The first operator we perceive as composition of the operators associated with the symbols

-\|\xi\|_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(\xi),\quad\varepsilon^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{11}(-i\xi_{2})\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(\xi),\quad\frac{1}{\|\xi\|_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}a_{\lambda}(\xi).

For the composite of the first and second symbol, we find

Op(\varepsilon_{11}^{\lambda^{1/2}}(i\xi_{2})\|\xi\|_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\tilde{a}_{\lambda})+\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq N^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}Op(D_{\xi}^{\alpha}(-\|\xi\|_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\tilde{a}_{\lambda})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varepsilon_{11}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}(-i\xi_{2})\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(\xi)\big)+R_{N^{\prime}}

with
\|R_{N^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-N}
. The operators
E^{\alpha}
satisfy

\|E^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{\alpha}\lambda^{1-|\alpha|}\lambda^{\frac{|\alpha|-1}{2}}\lambda=\lambda^{\frac{3-|\alpha|}{2}}.

The error in

is thus bounded by
\lambda
. Since
\|\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}
, this gives an
L^{2}
-bounded contribution.

Next, we compute the composite
C

Op(\varepsilon^{\lambda^{1/2}}_{11}(i\xi_{2})\|\xi\|_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\tilde{a}_{\lambda}(\xi))\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1}{D_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}S_{\lambda}^{\prime}.

We find

where

. The error estimate

\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq N^{\prime}}\|E^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1

3.4.1.
Reduction to the case

For this we can follow the argument from

3.4.2.
Reduction to a cube of size

If
\kappa=1

by rescaling. It is enough to show

(45)

\begin{split}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1;L^{q})}&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|P(x,D)u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}\\

)
and (

Indeed, if one applies this inequality to solutions
u
of the homogenous problem with a cut-off
in time, one obtains

\displaystyle\||D^{\prime}|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1;L^{q})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\!+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

using also the basic energy estimate. Combined with Duhamel’s formula and Minkowski’s inequality,

(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})

Inequality (

3.4.3.
Estimate away from the characteristic surface

Next, we argue that it is enough to prove the stronger estimate

\||D|^{-\rho}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|P(x,D)u\|_{L^{2}}+\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}.

3.4.4.
Reduction to a dyadic estimate

We replace (

\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|P(x,D)S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{r}L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}.

\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\|[P,S_{\lambda}]u\|^{2}_{L^{r}L^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

We rewrite

in non-divergence form, where the error terms are easily estimated in

[
39

3.4.2
the inequality (

\lambda^{-\rho}\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|S_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|PS_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\lambda}\rho_{e}\|_{L^{2}}.

Truncating the coefficients at frequencies

Finally, the Fourier coefficients of
\tilde{\varepsilon}
are truncated to the region

U_{\lambda}=\sum_{j\geq 0}S_{2^{j}\lambda}.

We estimate the contribution of

in

,
with

. To pass to the summand

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}(\varepsilon_{ij}^{\geq c\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}S_{\lambda}u)\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}

4.1.1.

Let

4.1.5.
The regularity of the Hamilton flow

We revisit the proof of the regularity of the Hamilton flow. We note that the Fourier truncation yields

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\tilde{\varepsilon}|\leq c_{\alpha}\lambda^{\frac{|\alpha|-2}{2}},\quad|\alpha|\geq 2.

A word of clarification regarding the following estimates: Seemingly, the half wave symbol

and

We record Lemmas 9 and 10 of
[

that estimate the flow and give an expansion.

Lemma 4.3
.

In

from (

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}x_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\alpha}t(1+t\sqrt{\lambda})^{|\alpha|-1},\quad|\alpha|\geq 1,

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\xi_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\alpha}(1+t\sqrt{\lambda})^{|\alpha|-1},\quad|\alpha|\geq 1.

.

For the Hamilton flow in

we have the representation

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=x+tq_{\xi}+t^{2}g(t,x,\xi),

\displaystyle\xi_{t}

\displaystyle=\xi+th(t,x,\xi),

4.2.1.
Gradient and Hamilton flow equations

As in (

Proof of Theorem

We follow the argument from

Here we need that

Hence we will suppose that the Fourier transform of

to a

, since the coefficients are

\displaystyle\|[\varepsilon_{ij}^{\lesssim\nu},S_{\lambda}]v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\lambda^{2-2\sigma}\|S_{\lambda}(\varepsilon_{ij}^{\gtrsim\nu}v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for some

, which is suppressed. Because of (

|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u,\tilde{C}(u)(\partial_{t}u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle|\lesssim c(A)B\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}.

The first term in (
77

\displaystyle\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\partial_{j}u),C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)(\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\partial_{j}u)+(\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)-\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s})\partial_{j}u,C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)(\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\partial_{j}u),C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle+II.

Paraproduct/Moser estimates yield

II\leq\|(\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)-\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s})\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}\|C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{A}B\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}.

Integrating by parts, the other summand becomes

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)&\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\partial_{j}u,C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=-\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u,\partial_{j}(\mathcal{A}_{j}(u)^{*}C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u)\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=-\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u,\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)^{*}C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\partial_{j}u\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{O}_{A}(B\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}).\end{split}

The second summand in (
77
) can be treated analogously. We seek to cancel the highest-order terms.
This gives the condition

\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)^{*}C(u)=C(u)^{*}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u).

Solving the system of equations and setting
C_{33}=1
, we find

(78)

C(u)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi+2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{1}^{2}&2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{1}u_{2}&0\\
2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{1}u_{2}&\psi+2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{2}^{2}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We thus conclude

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{E^{s}}\lesssim_{A}B(t)\|u(t)\|^{2}_{E^{s}},

and Grønwall’s lemma implies (
74
). To check that

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\approx_{A}\|u\|^{2}_{E^{s}},

it is enough to show that
C
is uniformly elliptic. This follows from Young’s inequality
in the computation

\displaystyle\Big\langle\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle,\begin{pmatrix}\psi+2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{1}^{2}&2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{1}u_{2}\\
2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{1}u_{2}&\psi+2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\geq(\psi+2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{1}^{2})\xi_{1}^{2}-4\psi^{\prime}\cdot(\frac{u_{1}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{u_{2}^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}}{2})+(\psi+2\psi^{\prime}\cdot u_{2}^{2})\xi_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\psi\cdot|\xi|^{2}.

The second claim of the proposition is a consequence of Sobolev’s embedding for

In the third step, we show continuous dependence by means of the frequency envelope argument detailed in Ifrim–Tataru
[
14
]
. The envelopes represent the dyadically localized Sobolev energy. We use the following tailored version, where
P_{k}
is the standard Littlewood–Paley projector at frequency
2^{k}
.

Definition 6.3
.

(c_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\ell^{2}
is called a
frequency envelope
for a function
u
in
H^{s}
if it has the following properties:

a)

Energy bound:

\|P_{k}u\|_{H^{s}}\leq c_{k}.

b)

Slowly varying: There is
\delta>0
such that for all
j,k\in\mathbb{N}

\frac{c_{k}}{c_{j}}\lesssim 2^{-\delta|j-k|}.

The envelopes are called
sharp

if they also
\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\approx\sum_{k}c_{k}^{2}
for a family of functions.

As noted in
[
14
]
such envelopes always exist.
The idea is to show that for a solution not only the
H^{s}
-norm is propagated, but also the frequency envelope for a time depending on the control parameters. This allows to infer continuous dependence. We give the details.

Regularization.
Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
(c_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sharp frequency envelope for
u_{0}
in
H^{s}
. The regularized initial data
u_{0}^{n}=P_{\leq n}u_{0}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
have the following properties.

i)

Uniform bounds:

\|P_{k}u_{0}^{n}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim c_{k},

ii)

High frequency bounds:

\|u_{0}^{n}\|_{H^{s+j}}\lesssim 2^{jn}c_{n},

iii)

Difference bounds:

\|u_{0}^{n+1}-u_{0}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-sn}c_{n},

iv)

Limit as
n\to\infty
:

u_{0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{0}^{n}\text{ in }H^{s}.

The regularized initial data give rise to a family of smooth solutions.

Uniform bounds:
Propositions
6.1
and
6.2

now yield a time interval of length
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
on which the solutions exist, and also
L^{2}
-bounds their differences:

i)

High frequency bounds:

\|u^{n}\|_{C([0,T],H^{s+j})}\lesssim 2^{nj}c_{n},

ii)

Difference bounds:

\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|_{C([0,T],L^{2})}\lesssim 2^{-sn}c_{n}.

Interpolation implies

\|u^{n+1}-u^{n}\|_{C([0,T],H^{m})}\lesssim c_{n}2^{-(s-m)n}

for
m\geq 0
. By the
L^{2}
-bound, we have

\|u-u^{n}\|_{C([0,T],L^{2})}\lesssim 2^{-sn}.

This gives convergence in
L^{2}
since

u-u^{n}=\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}(u^{j+1}-u^{j}).

The frequency localization of the summands at
2^{j+1}
and the error bound show that

\|u-u^{n}\|_{C([0,T],H^{s})}\lesssim c_{\geq n}=\big(\sum_{j\geq n}c_{j}^{2}\big)^{1/2},

which gives convergence in
C([0,T],H^{s})
. We can now show Theorem
1.9
.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Consider a sequence of initial data

u_{0k}\rightarrow u_{0}\text{ \ in }H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad s>\frac{11}{6}.

Propositions
6.1
and
6.2
show that the
solutions
u_{k}
with inital values
u_{0,k}
exist on a common time interval
[0,T]
with
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})

and that
u_{k}
coverges to
u
in
C([0,T],L^{2})
. For the solutions
u^{n}_{k}
and
u^{n}
with regularized initial data

u^{n}_{0k}
and
u^{n}
, we have
u^{n}_{0,k}\to u^{n}_{0}
in
H^{s}
and hence

\displaystyle u^{n}_{k}\to u^{n}\text{ \ in }C([0,T],H^{s})

as
k\to\infty
by the above reasoning. We then derive

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-u\|_{C([0,T],H^{s})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{n}_{k}-u^{n}\|_{C([0,T],H^{s})}+\|u^{n}-u\|_{C([0,T],H^{s})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|u^{n}_{k}-u_{k}\|_{C([0,T],H^{s})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{k}^{n}-u^{n}\|_{C([0,T],H^{s})}+c_{\geq n}+c^{k}_{\geq n}.

The convergence
u_{0k}\to u_{0}
in
H^{s}
allows us to choose a sequence of frequency envelopes
c^{k}\to c
in
\ell^{2}
.
We can thus take
n
uniformly in
k
such that the second and third term become arbitrarily small, and then let
k\to\infty
.
∎

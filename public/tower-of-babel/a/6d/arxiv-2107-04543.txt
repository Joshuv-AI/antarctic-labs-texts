1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Background

Interacting particle systems evolving according to a Metropolis dynamics associated with an energy functional called the Hamiltonian, may be trapped for a long time near a state that is a local minimum of the free energy, but not a global minimum. The deepest local minima are called
metastable states
, the global minimum is called the
stable state
. The transition from a metastable state to the stable state marks the relaxation of the system to equilibrium. To describe this relaxation, one needs to identify the set of critical configurations the system must attain in order to achieve this transition and to compute the crossover time. These critical configurations correspond to saddle points in the free energy landscape.

Metastability for interacting particle systems on
lattices
has been studied intensively in the past. For a summary, we refer the reader to the monographs by Olivieri and Vares
[
13
]
, and Bovier and den Hollander
[
6
]
. Successful attempts towards understanding metastable behaviour in random environments were made for the random field Curie-Weiss model, by Mathieu and Picco
[
12
]
, Bovier, Eckhoff, Gayrard and Klein
[
3
]
and Bianchi, Bovier and Ioffe
[
1
,
2
]
. Recently, there has been interest in metastability for interacting particle systems on
random graphs
. This is challenging, because the crossover times typically depend on the realisation of the graph. In den Hollander and Jovanovksi
[
11
]
and Bovier, Marello and Pulvirenti
[
7
]
, Glauber dynamics on dense
Erdős-Rényi
random graphs was analysed. Earlier work on metastability for Glauber dynamics on sparse random graphs can be found in Dommers
[
8
]
(random regular graph) and Dommers, den Hollander, Jovanovski and Nardi
[
10
]
(configuration model). The present paper is a first step towards the study of metastability for Glauber dynamics on
Chung-Lu
-like random graphs.

To the best of our knowledge, Tindemans and Capel
[
14
]
and Dommers, Giardinà, Giberti, van der Hofstad and Prioriello
[
9
]
are the only references where the model with the interaction Hamiltonian in (
1.2
) below has been studied in detail. Both focus on equilibrium properties only.

1.2.
Glauber dynamics on the complete graph with coupling disorder

Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}
be the complete graph on
n
vertices. Each vertex carries an Ising spin that can take the values
-1
or
+1
. Let
S_{n}=\{-1,+1\}^{[n]}
denote the set of spin configurations on
\mathcal{K}_{n}
, where
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
be an abstract probability space, and let
J=(J(i))_{i\in[n]}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables on this probability space taking values in a
finite
set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}\subset[0,\infty)
of cardinality
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The distribution of these random variables is given by

(1.1)

\mathcal{P}\left(J(i)=a_{\ell}\right)=\omega_{\ell}\in(0,1),\quad i\in[n],\ell\in[k],

with
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}=1
.

Let
H_{n}\colon\mathcal{S}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the interaction Hamiltonian defined by

(1.2)

H_{n}(\sigma)\equiv-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[n]\\
i<j\end{subarray}}J(i)J(j)\,\sigma(i)\sigma(j)-h\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma(i),\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n},

where
h\in[0,\infty)
is the magnetic field. We consider
Glauber dynamics
on
\mathcal{S}_{n}
, defined as the continuous-time Markov process with transition rates

(1.3)

r_{n}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta[H_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})-H_{n}(\sigma)]_{+}},&\mbox{if }\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n},

where
\beta\in(0,\infty)
is the inverse temperature,
\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma
means that
\sigma^{\prime}
differs from
\sigma
by a single spin-flip and
[\cdot]_{+}
is the positive part. This dynamics is
reversible
with respect to the
Gibbs measure

(1.4)

\mu_{n}(\sigma)\equiv\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta H_{n}(\sigma)},\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n},

where the normalising constant
Z_{n}
is called the partition sum. Note that the reference measure for (
1.4
) is the
counting measure
on
\mathcal{S}_{n}
. We write

(1.5)

(\sigma_{t})_{t\geq 0},\qquad\sigma_{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{n},

to denote a path of the Glauber dynamics on
\mathcal{S}_{n}
, and
\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}
to denote probability and expectation on path space given
\sigma_{0}=\sigma
(we suppress
J,h,\beta
and
n
from the notation).

For fixed
n
, if
h=0
the Hamiltonian in (
1.2
) has two global minima, at
\sigma\equiv+1
and
\sigma\equiv-1
, while if
h>0
it achieves a global minimum at
\sigma\equiv+1
and a local minimum at
\sigma\equiv-1
. The latter is the deepest local minimum not equal to the global minimum (at least for
h
small enough). However, in the limit as
n\to\infty
, these do
not
form a metastable pair of configurations because
entropy
comes into play.

1.3.
Metastability on the complete graph with coupling disorder

In this section we state our main results.

1.3.1.
Empirical magnetisations

The relevant quantity to monitor in order to characterise the metastable behaviour is the
disorder weighted magnetisation

(1.6)

K_{n}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}J(i)\sigma(i),\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}.

The following quantities will be essential for
coarse-graining
. Define the
level sets

(1.7)

A_{\ell,n}\equiv\{i\in[n]\colon\,J(i)=a_{\ell}\},\qquad\ell\in[k],

and the
level magnetisations

(1.8)

m_{\ell,n}(\sigma)\equiv\frac{1}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}\sum_{i\in A_{\ell,n}}\sigma(i),\qquad\ell\in[k],\,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}.

Put

(1.9)

m_{n}(\sigma)=(m_{\ell,n}(\sigma)\big)_{\ell\in[k]}\in[-1,1]^{k},\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n},

and note that
K_{n}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|\,m_{\ell,n}(\sigma)
depends on
\sigma
only through
m_{n}(\sigma)
. Thus, with abuse of notation, we may define

(1.10)

K_{n}(m)\equiv\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|m_{\ell},\qquad m=(m_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}\in[-1,1]^{k},

so that
K_{n}(\sigma)=K_{n}(m_{n}(\sigma))
.

1.3.2.
Thermodynamic limit

As
n\to\infty
, by the law of large numbers the random function
K_{n}
converges uniformly in probability to a deterministic function
K
given by

(1.11)

K(m)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,m_{\ell},\qquad m=(m_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}\in[-1,1]^{k}.

Similarly, the random free energy function
F_{n}
converges uniformly in probability to a deterministic function
F_{\beta,h}
(see (
2.15
) and (
2.26
) below for explicit formulas). In Section
3
, we show that the stationary points of
F_{\beta,h}
are given by
\mathbf{m}=(\mathbf{m}_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}
, where

(1.12)

\mathbf{m}_{\ell}=\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{m})+h]),\qquad\ell\in[k].

Note that, via (
1.12
), the
k
-dimensional vector
\mathbf{m}
is fully determined by the real number
K(\mathbf{m})
. Therefore, finding the stationary points of
F_{\beta,h}
reduces to finding the solutions of the equation

(1.13)

K=T_{\beta,h}(K),\qquad T_{\beta,h}(K)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h]).

1.3.3.
Metastable regime

It turns out that the critical inverse temperature
\beta_{c}
is given by

(1.14)

\beta_{c}=\left[\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}^{2}\omega_{\ell}\right]^{-1}.

Namely, if
\beta\in(0,\beta_{c}]
, then the system is not in the metastable regime for any
h\in[0,\infty)
, while if
\beta\in(\beta_{c},\infty)
, then, for
h\in[0,\infty)
small enough, it is in the metastable regime (i.e., (
1.13
) has more than one solution at which
T_{\beta,h}
is not tangent to the diagonal). Given
\beta\in(\beta_{c},\infty)
, the critical magnetic field
h_{c}(\beta)
is the minimal value of
h
for which the system is not metastable. The
metastable regime
is thus

(1.15)

\beta\in(\beta_{c},\infty),\qquad h\in[0,h_{c}(\beta)).

In Section
3
, we show that
\beta\mapsto h_{c}(\beta)
is continuous on
(\beta_{c},\infty)
, with

(1.16)

\lim_{\beta\downarrow\beta_{c}}h_{c}(\beta)=0,\qquad\lim_{\beta\to\infty}h_{c}(\beta)=C\in(0,\infty),

where the explicit value of
C
is given in (
3.12
) below.
Interestingly,
\beta\mapsto h_{c}(\beta)
is not necessarily monotone, i.e., the metastable crossover may be
re-entrant
.

It turns out that there exists an
\ell\in[k]
(depending on
\beta,h
and on the law of the components of
J
), such that
F_{\beta,h}
has
2\ell+1
stationary points.

1.3.4.
Metastable crossover

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the set of minima of
F_{n}
. Given
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
, define

(1.17)

\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m})\equiv\{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\backslash\mathbf{m}\colon\,F_{n}(m)\leq F_{n}(\mathbf{m})\}.

Let
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
be the gate between two disjoint subsets
A
and
B
of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. We refer to
[
6
, Section 10.1]
for a precise definition of the gate.

Fix
\mathbf{m}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
as the initial magnetisation.
Throughout the paper we assume that the following hypotheses hold for
\mathbf{m}_{n}
.

Hypothesis 1
.

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})
is non-empty.

(2)

The Hessian of
F_{n}
has only non-zero eigenvalues at
\mathbf{m}_{n}
and at all the points in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{m}_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))
.

(3)

There is a
unique
point
\mathbf{t}_{n}
in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{m}_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))
, which will often be called simply saddle point.

(4)

The saddle point
\mathbf{t}_{n}
is such that
r_{\ell}[\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|(1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2})]^{-1}
takes distinct values for different
\ell\in[k]
, where
r_{\ell}
is defined in (
4.9
) below.

Hypothesis
1
(
2
) and (
3
) are made to avoid complications. Hypothesis
1
(
4
) is needed in the proof of Lemma
4.1
below (as in
[
6
, Lemma 14.9]
). Neither is very restrictive: if for some parameter choice they fail, then after an infinitesimal parameter change they hold. Moreover, if Hypothesis
1
(
3
) fails, it is sufficient to compute separately the contribution to the crossover time of the various saddle points in the gate.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}]
and
\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]
denote the sets of configurations in
\mathcal{S}_{n}
for which the level magnetisations are
\mathbf{m}_{n}
and are contained in
\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})
, respectively. For
A\subset\mathcal{S}_{n}
, write

(1.18)

\tau_{A}=\{t\geq 0\colon\,\sigma_{t}\in A,\,\sigma_{t-}\notin A\}

to denote the first hitting time or return time of
A
.

We next state our main results for the crossover time. Theorem
1.1
provides a sharp asymptotics for the average crossover time from any metastable state to the set of states with lower free energy. Theorem
1.2
shows that asymptotically the crossover time is exponential on the scale of its mean, a property that is standard for metastable behaviour.

Theorem 1.1

(
Average crossover time with coupling disorder).

Let
\mathbb{A}_{n}(\cdot)
be the
k\times k
Hessian matrix defined in (
4.2
) below, and
\gamma_{n}
the unique negative solution of the equation in (
4.20
) below.
For every
\mathbf{m_{n}}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
satisfying Hypothesis
1
and within the metastable regime (
1.15
), uniformly in
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m_{n}}]
, and with
\mathcal{P}
-probability tending to
1
,

(1.19)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left[\tau_{\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m_{n}})]}\right]&=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\sqrt{\frac{[-\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}))]}{\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2\beta(-\gamma_{n})}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\beta n[F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]}.\end{split}

Theorem 1.2

(
Exponential law with coupling disorder).

For every
\mathbf{m_{n}}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
satisfying Hypothesis
1
and within the metastable regime (
1.15
), uniformly in
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}]
and with
\mathcal{P}
-probability tending to
1
,

(1.20)

\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}\left(\tau_{\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]}>t\,\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left[\tau_{\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]}\right]\right)=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\mathrm{e}^{-t},\qquad t\geq 0.

As the average crossover time estimated in Theorem
1.1
is a random variable, we next provide more information on the randomness of the quantity in the right-hand side of (
1.19
), which depends on the realisation of the random variable
J
. The prefactor in (
1.19
) converges with
\mathcal{P}
-probability tending to
1
to a deterministic limit, which depends on the law of
J
but not on the realisation of
J
. However, the exponent does not converge to a deterministic limit. In Theorem
1.3
we compute the exponent up to order
O(1)
. Recall that
F_{n}\to F_{\beta,h}
,
\mathbf{m}_{n}\to\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{t}_{n}\to\mathbf{t}
as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 1.3

(
Randomness of the exponent).

For every
\mathbf{m_{n}}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
satisfying Hypothesis
1
and within the metastable regime (
1.15
), in distribution,

(1.21)

n[F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]=n[F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m})]+Z\sqrt{n}+O(1),

where
Z
is a normal random variable with mean zero and variance in
(0,\infty)
, defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
and independent of
J
.

The variance of
Z
turns out to be a complicated function of
\beta
,
h
and the distribution of
J
. We refer to Section
6.3
for further details. Computing the exponent up to order
1
is in principle possible, but the formulas become rather complicated. Without this precision the prefactor in (
1.19
) is asymptotically negligible. Still, knowing this prefactor allows us to determine what the leading order behaviour of the randomness is.

1.4.
Discussion on the continuous case

Bianchi, Bovier and Ioffe
[
1
,
2
]
study the Curie-Weiss model with a
random magnetic field
whose distribution is continuous. Lumping techniques work for discrete distributions but not for continuous distributions. The latter require coarse-graining techniques to approximate the continuous distribution by a sequence of discrete distributions. In the present paper we consider pair interaction random variables with a discrete distribution only. It seems hard to obtain results with a similar precision for continuous distributions. The techniques employed in
[
1
,
2
]
do not carry over, because the error introduced by the coarse-graining turns out to be quadratic rather than linear.

1.5.
Techniques and outline

In order to prove Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
we use the potential-theoretic approach to metastability developed in Bovier, Eckhoff, Gayrard and Klein
[
4
,
5
]
. More specifically, we first find a sharp approximation of the Dirichlet form associated with the coarse-grained dynamics. We use these results, together with lumpability properties and well-known variational principles, to obtain sharp capacity estimates that are key quantities in the proof. For a more detailed overview of the methods, we refer the reader to the monograph by Bovier and den Hollander
[
6
]
.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section
2
provides quantities and notations that are needed throughout the paper. Section
3
identifies the metastable regime. Section
4
provides a sharp approximation of the Dirichlet form associated with the Glauber dynamics in the presence of the disorder. Section
5
provides estimates on capacity and on the metastable valley measure. Section
6
proves Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
. Appendix
A
contains a brief overview on known results for the standard CW model, which corresponds to the setting without disorder. Appendix
B
gives numerical evidence for the presence of multiple metastable states for suitable choices of
\beta
,
h
and of the law of the components of
J
. Appendix
C
contains an example in which
\beta\mapsto h_{c}(\beta)
is not increasing, implying the possibility of a re-entrant metastable crossover. Appendix
D
provides the limit as
n\to\infty
of the prefactor in (
1.19
).

Preparations

2.1
introduces further notation and writes the Hamiltonian in terms of the level magnetisations. Section

2.1.
Hamiltonian

Recall (
1.7
). Abbreviate

(2.1)

\omega_{\ell,n}=\frac{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}{n}.

Since, by the law of large numbers,

as

with
\mathcal{P}
-probability tending to
1
, we may and will assume that
A_{\ell,n}\neq\emptyset
for all
\ell\in[k]
and all
n
large enough. Recall (
1.8
)–(
1.9
). Note that
m_{\ell,n}(\sigma)
takes values in the set

(2.2)

\Gamma_{\ell,n}=\left\{-1,-1+\tfrac{2}{|A_{\ell,n}|},\dots,1-\tfrac{2}{|A_{\ell,n}|},1\right\}.

Hence
m_{n}(\sigma)
takes values in the set

(2.3)

\Gamma_{n}=\bigtimes_{\ell\in[k]}\Gamma_{\ell,n}.

The configurations corresponding to
M\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
are denoted by

(2.4)

\mathcal{S}_{n}[M]=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\colon\,m_{n}(\sigma)\in M\}.

For singletons

we write
\mathcal{S}_{n}[m]
instead of
\mathcal{S}_{n}[\{m\}]
.

Let

(2.5)

H_{n}(\sigma)=-\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i,j\in[n]}J(i)J(j)\,\sigma(i)\sigma(j)-h\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma(i),\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n},

which is the Hamiltonian in (

), except for the diagonal term

, which is a constant shift. Using the notation above, we can write the Hamiltonian in (

H_{n}(\sigma)=-n\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,m_{\ell,n}(\sigma)\right)^{2}+h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell,n}\,m_{\ell,n}(\sigma)\right]=nE_{n}(m_{n}(\sigma)),

where we abbreviate

(2.7)

E_{n}(m)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,m_{\ell}\right)^{2}-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,m_{\ell},\qquad m=(m_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}\in\Gamma_{n}.

2.2.
Dirichlet form and mesoscopic dynamics

By (

)–(

), the

associated with the Glauber dynamics equals

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(h,h)&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\mu_{n}(\sigma)r_{n}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\,[h(\sigma)-h(\sigma^{\prime})]^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2Z_{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n},\\

is a test function on
\mathcal{S}_{n}
taking values in

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(h,h)

\displaystyle\times\big[\,\bar{h}(m)-\bar{h}(m^{\prime})\big]^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n},\\
m_{n}(\sigma)=m\end{subarray}}\,\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n},\,\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma,\\
m_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})=m^{\prime}\end{subarray}}1,

where

. If

, then

for some

obtained from

(2.10)

m_{\ell,n}(\sigma^{i})=\begin{cases}m_{\ell^{\prime},n}(\sigma)\mp\frac{2}{\left|{A_{\ell^{\prime},n}}\right|},&\ell=\ell^{\prime},\\
m_{\ell,n}(\sigma),&\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}.\end{cases}

For
m,m^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{n}
, we write
m\sim m^{\prime}
when there exists an
\ell^{\prime}\in[k]
such that
m^{\prime}=m^{\ell^{\prime},+}
or
m^{\prime}=m^{\ell^{\prime},-}
, where

(2.11)

m_{\ell}^{\ell^{\prime},\pm}=\begin{cases}m_{\ell^{\prime}}\pm\frac{2}{\left|{A_{\ell^{\prime},n}}\right|},&\ell=\ell^{\prime},\\
m_{\ell},&\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
\ell\in[k]
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
with
m_{n}(\sigma)=m
, the cardinality of the set
\{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\colon\,\sigma^{\prime}\sim\sigma,\,m_{n}(\sigma^{\prime})=m^{\ell,\pm}\}
equals
\frac{1\mp m_{\ell}}{2}|A_{\ell,n}|
, namely, the number of
(\mp 1)
-spins in
\sigma
with index in
A_{\ell,n}
. Furthermore,

(2.12)

\left|\{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}\colon\,m_{n}(\sigma)=m\}\right|=\prod_{\ell\in[k]}\binom{|A_{\ell,n}|}{\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{2}|A_{\ell,n}|},\qquad m\in\Gamma_{n},

as is seen by counting the number of
(-1)
-spins with label in
A_{\ell,n}
of a configuration with
\ell
-th level magnetisation
m_{\ell}
. Using these observations, we can rewrite (
2.9
) as

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(h,h)=\frac{1}{2Z_{n}}\sum_{m\in\Gamma_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\,nE_{n}(m)}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\,n[E_{n}(m^{\prime})-E_{n}(m)]_{+}}\big[\,\bar{h}(m)-\bar{h}(m^{\prime})\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\ell\in[k]}\binom{|A_{\ell,n}|}{\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{2}|A_{\ell,n}|}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}|A_{\ell,n}|\left[\frac{1-m_{\ell}}{2}\,\mathds{1}(m^{\prime}=m^{\ell,+})+\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{2}\,\mathds{1}(m^{\prime}=m^{\ell,-})\right].

Next, abbreviate

(2.14)

I_{n}(m)=-\frac{1}{n}\log\left[\prod_{\ell\in[k]}\binom{|A_{\ell,n}|}{\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{2}|A_{\ell,n}|}\right],\qquad m\in\Gamma_{n},

and put

(2.15)

F_{n}(m)=E_{n}(m)+\frac{1}{\beta}I_{n}(m)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,m_{\ell}\right)^{2}-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,m_{\ell}+\frac{1}{\beta}I_{n}(m),\quad m\in\Gamma_{n},

where
E_{n}(m)
is defined in (
2.7
). Moreover, define

(2.16)

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{n}(m,m^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta n[E_{n}(m^{\prime})-E_{n}(m)]_{+}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\ell\in[k]}|A_{\ell,n}|\left[\frac{1-m_{\ell}}{2}\,\mathds{1}(m^{\prime}=m^{\ell,+})+\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{2}\,\mathds{1}(m^{\prime}=m^{\ell,-})\right].

With this notation, we can write the
mesoscopic measure

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\cdot)=\mu_{n}\circ m_{n}^{-1}(\cdot)
on
\Gamma_{n}
, with
\mu_{n}
defined in (
1.4
), as

(2.17)

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(m)=\mu_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{n}[m])=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(m)},\qquad m\in\Gamma_{n},

and so the Dirichlet form in (
2.13
) becomes

(2.18)

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(h,h)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in\Gamma_{n}}\mathcal{Q}_{n}(m)\,\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{n}}\bar{r}_{n}(m,m^{\prime})\,\big[\,\bar{h}(m)-\bar{h}(m^{\prime})\big]^{2}.

2.3.
Gradients and Hessians

Denote the Cramér entropy by

(2.19)

I_{\mathbf{C}}(x)=\frac{1-x}{2}\log\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)+\frac{1+x}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+x}{2}\right).

Define

(2.20)

\bar{I}_{n}(m)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell,n}I_{\mathbf{C}}(m_{\ell}).

Since
\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\omega_{\ell}n
, we can use Stirling’s formula
N!=[1+o_{N}(1)]\,N^{N}\mathrm{e}^{-N}\sqrt{2\pi N}
to obtain

(2.21)

I_{n}(m)=\bar{I}_{n}(m)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{1}{2n}\log\left(\frac{\pi(1-m_{\ell}^{2})|A_{\ell,n}|}{2}\right)+o(n^{-1})=\bar{I}_{n}(m)+O(n^{-1}\log n),

where the error term is
uniform
in
m\in\Gamma_{n}
. For
\ell,\bar{\ell}\in[k]
, we compute

(2.22)

\frac{\partial\bar{I}_{n}(m)}{\partial m_{\ell}}=\frac{\omega_{\ell,n}}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{1-m_{\ell}}\right)

and

(2.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{I}_{n}(m)}{\partial{m_{\ell}}\partial{m_{\bar{\ell}}}}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\ell\neq\bar{\ell},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{I}_{n}(m)}{\partial{m_{\ell}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\ell,n}}{1-m_{\ell}^{2}}.

Recalling (
2.7
), we compute

(2.24)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial E_{n}(m)}{\partial m_{\ell}}&=-a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,m_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)-\omega_{\ell,n}h.\end{split}

Define

(2.25)

\bar{F}_{n}(m)=E_{n}(m)+\frac{1}{\beta}\bar{I}_{n}(m)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,m_{\ell}\right)^{2}-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell,n}m_{\ell}+\frac{1}{\beta}\bar{I}_{n}(m).

Remark 2.1
.

By (
2.21
),
F_{n}(m)=\bar{F}_{n}(m)+O(n^{-1}\log n)
, where
F_{n}
is defined in (
2.15
).

\spadesuit

For
m\in[-1,1]^{k}
, define

(2.26)

\begin{split}F_{\beta,h}(m)&=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,m_{\ell}\right)^{2}-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\,\omega_{\ell}\,m_{\ell}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}I_{\mathbf{C}}(m_{\ell}),\end{split}

which corresponds to the uniform limit in probability of
F_{n}
as
n\to\infty
. Compute

(2.27)

\frac{\partial\bar{F}_{n}(m)}{\partial m_{\ell}}=\omega_{\ell,n}\left[\frac{1}{2\beta}\log\left(\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{1-m_{\ell}}\right)-a_{\ell}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,m_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)-h\right]

and

(2.28)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{F}_{n}(m)}{\partial{m_{\ell}}\,\partial{m_{\ell^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle=-a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n},\qquad\ell\neq\ell^{\prime},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{F}_{n}(m)}{\partial{m_{\ell}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\ell,n}}{\beta}\frac{1}{1-m_{\ell}^{2}}-a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}.

The same formulas apply for
I_{n},F_{n}
, with an error term
O(n^{-1})
.

2.4.
Additional computation

We conclude with a computation that will be useful later on. Recalling (
2.11
), we write

(2.29)

\begin{split}&n\big[\bar{I}_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-\bar{I}_{n}(m)\big]\\
&=n\,\omega_{\ell,n}\Bigg[\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{2}\log\left(1\pm\frac{2}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|(1+m_{\ell})}\right)+\frac{1-m_{\ell}}{2}\log\left(1\mp\frac{2}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|(1-m_{\ell})}\right)\pm\frac{1}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}A_{\ell,n}^{\pm}\Bigg]\\
&=n\,\omega_{\ell,n}\left[\pm\frac{1}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}\mp\frac{1}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}+O(n^{-2})\pm\frac{1}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}\right]=\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}+O(n^{-1}),\end{split}

where

(2.30)

\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}=\log\left(1+\frac{2m_{\ell}\pm\tfrac{4}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}}{1-m_{\ell}\mp\tfrac{2}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}}\right).

The same formula applies for
I_{n}
with an error term of order
O(n^{-1})
, and hence

(2.31)

n\big[{I}_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-{I}_{n}(m)\big]=\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}+O(n^{-1}).

Note that
\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}=O(1)
. Therefore, using (
2.15
), we get

(2.32)

\begin{split}n\left[E_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-E_{n}(m)\right]&=n\left[F_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-F_{n}(m)\right]-\frac{1}{\beta}n\left[I_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-I_{n}(m)\right]\\
&=n\left[F_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-F_{n}(m)\right]-\frac{1}{\beta}\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}+O(n^{-1}).\end{split}

3.
Metastable regime

Section
3.1
identifies the stationary points of
\bar{F}_{n}
. Section
3.2
identifies the metastable regime.
Section
3.3
provides details on the
1
-dimensional metastable landscape.

3.1.
Stationary points of
\bar{F}_{n}
and
F_{\beta,h}

By (
2.27
), the critical points
m=(m_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}
of
\bar{F}_{n}
solve the system of equations (with
\omega_{\ell,n}\neq 0
)

(3.1)

0=\frac{\partial\bar{F}_{n}(m)}{\partial m_{\ell}}=\omega_{\ell,n}\left[\frac{1}{2\beta}\log\left(\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{1-m_{\ell}}\right)-a_{\ell}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,m_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)-h\right],\quad\ell\in[k].

Hence

(3.2)

\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+m_{\ell}}{1-m_{\ell}}\right)=\beta\left[a_{\ell}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,m_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)+h\right].

Since
\mathrm{arctanh\,}x=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}
,
x\in(-1,+1)
, (
3.2
) can be rewritten as

(3.3)

m_{\ell}=\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,m_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)+h\right]\right),\qquad\ell\in[k].

Similarly, the critical points
m=(m_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}
of
F_{\beta,h}
solve the deterministic equation

(3.4)

m_{\ell}=\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,m_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)+h\right]\right),\qquad\ell\in[k].

Note that this can also be obtained directly from (
3.3
) after replacing
\omega_{\ell,n}
by its mean value
\omega_{\ell}
.

3.2.
Metastable regime

We are interested in identifying the metastable regime, i.e., the set of pairs
(\beta,h)
for which
F_{\beta,h}
has more than one minimum. Put

(3.5)

K=K(m)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,m_{\ell}.

From the characterisation of the critical points of
F_{\beta,h}
in (
3.4
) it follows that

(3.6)

K=T_{\beta,h}(K),\qquad T_{\beta,h}(K)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h]).

Note that any critical point
m=(m_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}\in[-1,1]^{k}
of
F_{\beta,h}
is uniquely determined by
K(m)\in\mathbb{R}
. Consequently, the problem of solving the
k
-dimensional system in (
3.4
) can be reduced to solving the
1
-dimensional equation (
3.6
). Recalling Hypothesis
1
(
2
), the system is in the metastable regime if and only if (
3.6
) has more than one solution that is not tangent to the diagonal.

Compute

(3.7)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{\beta,h}(K)

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a^{2}_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\big(1-\tanh^{2}(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h])\big),

\displaystyle T^{\prime\prime}_{\beta,h}(K)

\displaystyle=-2\beta^{2}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a^{3}_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h])\,\big(1-\tanh^{2}(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h])\big).

For
h=0
, the system is metastable when

(3.8)

\beta>\frac{1}{\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell}},

in which case
T_{\beta,h}
has a unique inflection point at
K=0
, implying that (
3.6
) has three solutions
K\in\{-K^{*},0,+K^{*}\}
with
K^{*}>0
. Otherwise (
3.6
) has only one solution
K=0
.

We proceed with the more interesting case
h>0
.

3.2.1.
Number of solutions

Lemma 3.1

(
Number of solutions).

For
h>0
, the number of critical points of
F_{\beta,h}
, i.e., solutions of (
3.6
), varies in
\{1,3,\dots,2\ell+1\}
, where
\ell\in[k]
and
2\ell-1
is the number of inflection points of
T_{\beta,h}
.

Proof.

For
h>0
and
K
positive and large enough,
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime\prime}(K)<0
. Moreover, for
h>0
and
K
negative with
\left|{K}\right|
large enough,
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime\prime}(K)>0
. Therefore,
T_{\beta,h}
has at least one inflection point and that the number of inflection points of
T_{\beta,h}
cannot be even: it takes values in
\{1,3,\dots,2k-1\}
depending on
\beta,h
and the law of the components of
J
. Consequently, if
2\ell-1
(
\ell\in[k]
) is the number of inflection points, then the cardinality of the solutions of (
3.6
) takes values in
\{1,3,\dots,2\ell+1\}
depending on
\beta,h
and on the law of the components of
J
.
∎

We conjecture that for any finite
k
there exist
\beta,h
and a law of the components of
J
such that (
3.6
) has any number of solutions in the set
\{1,3,\dots,2k+1\}
. We found numerical evidence for this fact for
k\in\{2,3,4\}
. See Appendix
B
.

Lemma 3.2

(
Unique strictly positive solution).

For every
\beta>0
and
h>0
, (
3.6
) has exactly one strictly positive solution.

Proof.

Put
W(K)=T_{\beta,h}(K)-K
. The solutions of
(
3.6
)
are the roots of
W
. Clearly,
W(0)>0
. Moreover,
\lim_{K\to\infty}W(K)=-\infty
because
\lim_{K\to\infty}T_{\beta,h}(K)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}>0
is finite. Therefore, by continuity, a root of
W(K)
exists in
(0,\infty)
.

Let
\tilde{K}
be the smallest positive root of
W
. Next we will prove that this root is unique. Indeed,
W(K)^{\prime\prime}<0
when
K\in[0,\infty)
, meaning that
K\mapsto W(K)^{\prime}
is strictly decreasing. By continuity, since
W(K)>0
for all
K\in[0,\tilde{K})
, we have
W(\tilde{K})^{\prime}\leq 0
and
\lim_{K\to\infty}W(K)^{\prime}=-1
. Therefore,
W(K)^{\prime}<0
for all
K\in(\tilde{K},\infty)
, and so
W
is strictly decreasing. Moreover,
W(K)<W(\tilde{K})=0
for all
K\in(\tilde{K},\infty)
. Thus,
\tilde{K}
is the only positive root of
W
.
∎

3.2.2.
Metastable regime

Lemma 3.3

(
Characterisation of the metastable regime).

(
3.6
) has at least three solutions not tangent to the diagonal if and only if there exists
\bar{K}<0
such that
\bar{K}>T_{\beta,h}(\bar{K})
, i.e.,

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\bar{K}>\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}\bar{K}+h]).\end{split}

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.2
, we see that (
3.6
) has at least three solutions if and only if it has at least two strictly negative solutions. As above, we define
W(K)=T_{\beta,h}(K)-K
. The solutions of (
3.6
) are the roots of
W
. Now,
assume
that there exists a
\bar{K}<0
such that
\bar{K}>T_{\beta,h}(\bar{K})
. Since
W(\bar{K})<0
and
W(0)>0
,
W(K)
has a root in
(\bar{K},0)
, implying that (
3.6
) has at least one solution in
(\bar{K},0)
. Moreover, since
\lim_{K\to-\infty}T_{\beta,h}(K)=-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}
is finite, we have
\lim_{K\to-\infty}W(K)=\infty
. Because
W(\bar{K})<0
, it follows that
W
has at least one root in
(-\infty,\bar{K})
. With the same argument it can be shown that the negative roots of
W
are always even. The opposite implication is trivial.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Applying the intermediate value theorem to the derivative of
W(K)=T_{\beta,h}(K)-K
, we get that if the condition in Lemma
3.3
is satisfied, then there exists a
\bar{K}<0
such that
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime}(\bar{K})=1
and
\bar{K}>T_{\beta,h}(\bar{K})
.

\spadesuit

Theorem 3.4

(
Metastable regime).

Define, as in (
1.14
),

(3.10)

\beta_{c}=\frac{1}{\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a^{2}_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}}.

The metastable regime is

(3.11)

\beta\in(\beta_{c},\infty),\qquad h\in\big[0,h_{c}(\beta)\big),

with
\beta\mapsto\beta h_{c}(\beta)
non-decreasing on
[\beta_{c},\infty)
. Furthermore, if the support of the law of the components of
J
is put into increasing order, i.e.,
a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{k}
, then

(3.12)

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}h_{c}(\beta)=\min_{\ell\in[k]^{*}}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{k}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}-\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell-1}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\right),

where the minimum is over all
\ell\in[k]
such that the quantity between brackets is positive.

Proof.

Recalling Lemma
3.3
, we look for conditions for the existence of a
K<0
satisfying (
3.9
). If such a
K
exists, then by Remark
3.1
there exists a
\bar{K}<0
satisfying (
3.9
) such that
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime}(\bar{K})=1
, which reads

(3.13)

\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a^{2}_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\tanh^{2}(\beta[a_{\ell}\bar{K}+h])=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a^{2}_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}-\frac{1}{\beta}.

Since the left-hand side of (
3.13
) is positive, it admits solutions only if

(3.14)

\frac{1}{\beta}<\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a^{2}_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}=\frac{1}{\beta_{c}}.

Therefore, (
3.14
) is a necessary condition for the metastable regime.

Now assume (
3.14
). Since
\tanh x\sim x
,
x\to 0
, for
\left|{K}\right|\ll\beta(\max_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell})^{-1}
and
h\downarrow 0
, we have

(3.15)

\begin{split}K&=T_{\beta,h}(K)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h])\sim\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\beta[a_{\ell}K+h],\end{split}

which reads

(3.16)

K\sim-\left(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{c}}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{-1}h

and proves the existence of a negative solution. A positive solution is guaranteed by Lemma
3.2
. The existence of a third (strictly negative) solution of (
3.4
), for every
\beta>\beta_{c}
and for
h\downarrow 0
, follows as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
. Therefore, the lower bound on
\beta_{c}
is sharp.

Since
h\mapsto T_{\beta,h}(K)
is strictly increasing for every fixed
\beta>0
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique critical curve
\beta\mapsto h_{c}(\beta)
such that the system is metastable for
0\leq h<h_{c}(\beta)
and not metastable for
h\geq h_{c}(\beta)
. We know that
h_{c}(\beta)>0
for
\beta>\beta_{c}
. By passing to the parametrisation
g=h\beta
, we get that
\beta\mapsto T_{\beta,g}(K)
is strictly decreasing for every
g
and for every
K<0
, from which it follows that
\beta\mapsto g_{c}(\beta)=\beta h_{c}(\beta)
is non-decreasing.

We next focus on the limit of
h_{c}(\beta)
as
\beta\to\infty
. By Lemma
3.3
, we may focus on the existence of
\bar{K}
satisfying (
3.9
). In the limit as
\beta\to\infty
,
\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}\bar{K}+h])\to 2\Theta_{-h/a_{\ell}}(\bar{K})-1
, where
\Theta_{x}(\cdot)
is the Heaviside function centred in
x
. Thus, for all
\ell\in[k+1]
,

(3.17)

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell^{\prime}}K+h])=-\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{k}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}},\qquad K\in\left(-\frac{h}{a_{\ell-1}},-\frac{h}{a_{\ell}}\right),

and, for all
\ell\in[k]
,

(3.18)

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell^{\prime}}K+h])=-\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell+1}^{k}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}},\qquad K=-\frac{h}{a_{\ell}},

where we set
-\frac{h}{a_{0}}=-\infty
and
-\frac{h}{a_{k+1}}=\infty
. Thus, for
\bar{K}\in\left(-\frac{h}{a_{\ell-1}},-\frac{h}{a_{\ell}}\right)
, (
3.9
) can be written as

(3.19)

\bar{K}>-\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{k}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}.

Therefore, (
3.9
) has a solution if and only if there exists an
\ell\in[k]
such that

(3.20)

-\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{k}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}<-\frac{h}{a_{\ell}},

in which case a solution
\bar{K}
of (
3.9
) exists in

(3.21)

\left(-\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell}^{k}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}},-\frac{h}{a_{\ell}}\right).

Note that the quantity between brackets in (
3.12
) is always positive for
\ell=1
. Thus, the minimum is always finite.

The proof is complete after we show why we may drop the case where
\bar{K}=-\frac{h}{a_{\ell}}
for some
\ell\in[k]
. In this case the condition for
\bar{K}
to satisfy (
3.9
) is

(3.22)

-\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=\ell+1}^{k}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell-1}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}<-\frac{h}{a_{\ell}},

which implies (
3.20
). Thus, if
\bar{K}=\frac{-h}{a_{\ell}}
satisfies (
3.9
), then also some other
K
in (
3.21
) satisfies (
3.9
). Therefore, the condition in (
3.20
) is equivalent to having metastability.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(
Re-entrant crossover).

The function
\beta\mapsto h_{c}(\beta)
is not necessarily non-decreasing.

Proof.

In Appendix
C
we provide an example of
\beta\mapsto h_{c}(\beta)
that is not increasing.
∎

3.2.3.
Bounds on the inflection points and on the critical curve

Lemma 3.6

(
Bounds on inflection points).

All solutions of
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime\prime}(K)=0
are contained in the interval

(3.23)

\left[-\frac{h}{\min_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}},-\frac{h}{\max_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}}\right].

In particular, they are all strictly negative.

Proof.

If
K>-\frac{h}{\max_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}}
, then
\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h])>0
for all
\ell\in[k]
, which implies
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime\prime}(K)<0
. If
K<-\frac{h}{\min_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}}
, then
\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}K+h])<0
for all
\ell\in[k]
, which implies
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime\prime}(K)>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(
Upper bound on
h_{c}
).

\sup_{\beta\in(\beta_{c},\infty)}h_{c}(\beta)<\left(\max_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\right)\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}
.

Proof.

Use Lemma
3.3
to characterise the metastable regime and Remark
3.1
. We
claim
that if a solution
\bar{K}
of (
3.9
) with
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime}(\bar{K})=1
exists, then it must be negative and strictly less than an inflection point. Using this fact, together with Lemma
3.6
and the inequality in (
3.9
), we obtain a necessary upper bound on
h
:

(3.24)

\begin{split}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}\bar{K}+h])<-\frac{h}{\max_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}}.\end{split}

Using that
\tanh(\beta[a_{\ell}\bar{K}+h])>-1
, we conclude the proof.

We are left to prove the claim. By Lemma
3.6
, all inflection points are negative, and
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime\prime}(K)<0
for
K\geq 0
. Assume, by contradiction, that
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime\prime}(K)<0
for all
K\in(\bar{K},\infty)
. Then
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime}
is strictly decreasing. Therefore,
T_{\beta,h}^{\prime}(K)<1
for all
K\in(\bar{K},\infty)
, which implies
T_{\beta,h}(K)-T_{\beta,h}(0)<K
. Since
T_{\beta,h}(0)>0
, there exists a
\tilde{K}\in(\bar{K},0)
such that
T_{\beta,h}(\tilde{K})>0>\tilde{K}
. Thus,
T_{\beta,h}(\tilde{K})-T_{\beta,h}(0)>\tilde{K}
, which contradicts what we have proved for all
K\in(\bar{K},\infty)
.
∎

3.3.
Quasi 1-dimensional landscape

Given

4.
Approximation of the Dirichlet form near the saddle point

In this section we approximate the Dirichlet form associated with the coarse-grained dynamics near the saddle point. This is a key step to obtain capacity estimates in the following section. Further details and examples on the techniques we use here can be found in
[
6
, Chapters 9, 10 and 14]
.

Section
4.1
introduces some key quantities that are needed to express the mesoscopic measure. Section
4.2
introduces an approximate mesoscopic measure that leads to an approximate dynamics. Section
4.3
approximates the harmonic functions associated with this dynamics. Section
4.4
computes an approximate Dirichlet form. Section
4.5
uses the latter to approximate the full Dirichlet form.

4.1.
Key quantities

Let
\mathbf{m}_{n}=(\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n})_{\ell\in[k]}
and
\mathbf{t}_{n}=(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})_{\ell\in[k]}
in
\Gamma_{n}
be a local minimum of
F_{n}
and the correspondent saddle point, respectively, as defined in Section
1.3.4
. Note that both
\mathbf{m}_{n}
and
\mathbf{t}_{n}
satisfy (
3.3
). Consider the neighbourhood of
\mathbf{t}_{n}
defined by

(4.1)

\mathcal{D}_{n}=\left\{m\in\Gamma_{n}\colon\,d(m,\mathbf{t}_{n})\leq C^{\prime}n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\right\},

where
d
is the Euclidean distance and
C^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
is a constant. Abbreviate the Hessian of
F_{n}

(4.2)

\mathbb{A}_{n}(m)=(\nabla^{2}F_{n})(m),\qquad m\in\Gamma_{n},

and put

(4.3)

\mathbb{A}_{n}=\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}).

By (
2.28
),

(4.4)

\displaystyle(\mathbb{A}_{n}(m))_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}+O(n^{-1}),\qquad\ell\neq\ell^{\prime},

\displaystyle(\mathbb{A}_{n}(m))_{\ell,\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\ell,n}}{\beta}\frac{1}{1-m_{\ell}^{2}}-a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}+O(n^{-1})=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{I}_{n}(m)}{\partial{m_{\ell}}^{2}}-a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}+O(n^{-1}).

Note that
\mathbb{A}_{n}(m)
is a diagonal matrix minus a rank one matrix. Compute

(4.5)

\begin{split}\det\mathbb{A}_{n}(m)&=\left(1-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\beta\,a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}[1-m_{\ell}^{2}]\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell^{\prime}\in[k]\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}}{1-m_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}}[1+O(n^{-1})].\end{split}

4.2.
Approximate dynamics and Dirichlet form

For any two vectors
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{w}}\right\rangle
denote their scalar product. For any
k\times k
matrix
\mathbf{M}
and any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{v}
denote their matrix product, as
\mathbf{v}
was in
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times 1

For

, define

(4.6)

\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\left[-\tfrac{\beta n}{2}\big\langle[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}],\mathbb{A}_{n}\cdot[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}]\big\rangle\right]\exp\left[-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})\right],

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{n}\big(m,m^{\prime}\big)=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\bar{r}_{n}\big(\mathbf{t}_{n},\mathbf{t}_{n}^{\ell,+}\big),&m^{\prime}=m^{\ell,+},\\[5.69046pt]
\bar{r}_{n}\big(\mathbf{t}_{n}^{\ell,-},\mathbf{t}_{n}\big)\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m^{\ell,-})}{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)},&m^{\prime}=m^{\ell,-},\\[5.69046pt]
0,&\text{else,}\end{cases}

where
\bar{r}_{n}
is defined in (
2.16
). The transition rates
\tilde{r}_{n}

\mathcal{D}_{n}
that is reversible with respect to the mesoscopic measure
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}
. The corresponding

(4.8)

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(u,u)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\tilde{r}_{n}\big(m,m^{\ell,+}\big)\left[u(m)-u(m^{\ell,+})\right]^{2},

where
u
is a test function on
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Put

(4.9)

r_{\ell}=\tilde{r}_{n}\big(m,m^{\ell,+}\big)=\bar{r}_{n}\big(\mathbf{t}_{n},\mathbf{t}_{n}^{\ell,+}\big).

Using (
2.7
) and (
2.16

(4.10)

r_{\ell}={\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}\,\frac{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}}{2}\,\exp\left[-2\beta\left(-h-a_{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{\ell}}{n}+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}\right)\right)_{+}\right].

4.2.1.
Approximation estimates

Next we estimate how close the pairs

and
(\mathcal{Q}_{n},\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n})

In particular,

(4.12)

\begin{split}&F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}^{\ell,\pm})-F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{4}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|^{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{n})_{\ell,{\ell}}+O\big(\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|^{-3}\big)\\
&=\frac{2}{n^{2}\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}}\left[\frac{\omega_{\ell,n}}{\beta}\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2}}-a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}+o\left((n\,\omega_{\ell,n})^{-1}\right)\right]+O\big((n\,\omega_{\ell,n})^{-3}\big)\\
&=\frac{2}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\beta\,\omega_{\ell,n}(1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2})}-a_{\ell}^{2}\right)+O\big((n\,\omega_{\ell,n})^{-3}\big),\end{split}

where the second equality uses (
4.4
). Moreover, for
m\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
(
\mathrm{e}_{\ell}
is the unitary vector in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
whose

(4.13)

\begin{split}&F_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-F_{n}(m)\\

&=\pm\frac{2}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}(\mathbb{A}_{n})_{\ell,{\ell^{\prime}}}(m_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n})+\frac{2}{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|^{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{n})_{\ell,{\ell}}+O\big(d(m,\mathbf{t}_{n})^{3}\big)\\
&=\left(\pm\frac{2}{n\omega_{\ell,n}}(m_{\ell}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})+\frac{2}{n^{2}\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\omega_{\ell,n}}{\beta}\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2}}-a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}+o(n^{-1})\right)\\
&\quad\pm\frac{2}{n\omega_{\ell,n}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k],\,\ell^{\prime}\neq\ell}(-a_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell,n}\,a_{{\ell^{\prime}}}\,\omega_{{\ell^{\prime}},n})(m_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n})+O\big(n^{-3/2}\log^{3/2}n\big)\\
&=\mp\frac{2}{n}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{{\ell^{\prime}}}\,\omega_{{\ell^{\prime}},n}(m_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n})\pm\frac{2(m_{\ell}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})}{\beta\,n(1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2})}+O\big(n^{-3/2}\log^{3/2}n\big),\end{split}

where the third equality uses (
4.4
). For
m\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, we have
d(m,\mathbf{t}_{n})^{3}=O(n^{-3/2}\log^{3/2}n)
. Therefore, combining (
2.17
), (
4.6

4.11

(4.14)

\left|{\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{n}(m)}{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)}-1}\right|\leq C^{\prime\prime}n^{-1/2}\log^{3/2}n,\qquad m\in\mathcal{D}_{n},

for some
C^{\prime\prime}\in(0,\infty)
constant. Using (
2.16
) and (
2.32

(4.15)

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{n}(m,m^{\ell,\pm})

\displaystyle=\exp{\left[-\beta\left[n\left[F_{n}(m^{\ell,\pm})-F_{n}(m)\right]-\frac{1}{\beta}\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}+O\left(n^{-1}\right)\right]_{+}\right]}\frac{1\mp m_{\ell}}{2},

where

Using (

4.12
), (
4.13
) and (

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{\bar{r}_{n}\left(m,m^{\ell,+}\right)}{\tilde{r}_{n}\left(m,m^{\ell,+}\right)}-1}\right|=\left|{\frac{\bar{r}_{n}\left(m,m^{\ell,+}\right)}{\bar{r}_{n}\big(\mathbf{t}_{n},\mathbf{t}_{n}^{\ell,+}\big)}-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\frac{(1-m_{\ell})\,\exp{\left\{-\left[I_{1}+O(n^{-1/2}\log^{3/2}n)-\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}+o_{n}(1)\right]_{+}\right\}}}{(1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})\,\exp{\left\{-\left[I_{2}+O(n^{-2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}^{-3})-\Delta_{\ell,n}^{\pm}+o_{n}(1)\right]_{+}\right\}}}-1}\right|

(4.17)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-2\beta\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{{\ell^{\prime}}}\,\omega_{{\ell^{\prime}},n}(m_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n})+\frac{2(m_{\ell}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})}{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2}},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{\ell,n}(1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2})}-\beta\,a_{\ell}^{2}\right).

&\quad\qquad\times\left(1-\frac{1}{\omega_{\ell,n}}v_{\ell}(-\gamma_{n})\beta\left\langle{v},{m-\mathbf{t}_{n}}\right\rangle+O(\omega_{\ell,n}^{-2}\,n^{-1}\log n)\right).\end{split}

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(g,g)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)\sum_{\ell\in[k]}r_{\ell}\left[g(m^{\ell,+})-g(m)\right]^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\exp\left[-\tfrac{\beta n}{2}\big\langle[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}],\mathbb{A}_{n}\cdot[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}]\big\rangle\right]\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})}\\
&\quad\times\sum_{\ell\in[k]}r_{\ell}v_{\ell}^{2}\,\frac{2(-\gamma_{n})\beta}{\pi n\,\omega_{\ell,n}^{2}}\exp\left(-\beta n(-\gamma_{n})\left\langle{v},{m-\mathbf{t}_{n}}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\times\left(1-\frac{v_{\ell}}{\omega_{\ell,n}}(-\gamma_{n})\beta\left\langle{v},{m-\mathbf{t}_{n}}\right\rangle+O(\omega_{\ell,n}^{-2}\,n^{-1}\log n)\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\frac{2(-\gamma_{n})\beta}{\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\exp\left[-\tfrac{\beta n}{2}\big\langle[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}],\mathbb{A}_{n}\cdot[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}]\big\rangle\right]\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})}\\
&\quad\times\exp\left(-\beta n(-\gamma_{n})\left\langle{v},{m-\mathbf{t}_{n}}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\left[1+O\left(\omega_{\ell,n}^{-1}\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\,\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\frac{2(-\gamma_{n})\beta}{\pi}\left[1+O\left(\omega_{\ell,n}^{-1}\,\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\,\right)\right]\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}{2}\right)\\
&\quad\times\int_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}\mathrm{d}m\exp\left[-\tfrac{\beta n}{2}\big\langle[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}],\mathbb{A}_{n}\cdot[m-\mathbf{t}_{n}]\big\rangle\right]\exp\left(-\beta n(-\gamma_{n})\left\langle{v},{m-\mathbf{t}_{n}}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})}\frac{(-\gamma_{n})n}{\sqrt{[-\det\mathbb{A}_{n}]}}\left(\frac{\pi n}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right)\left[1+O\left(\omega_{\ell,n}^{-1}\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\,\right)\right],\end{split}

where we use
[
6
, Eq. (10.2.33)]
with
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{\beta n}
and
d=k
. Here
\tfrac{1}{2}\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|
is the inverse of the step in the
\ell
–direction, while in
[
6
, Eq. (10.2.33)]
the step is
\varepsilon
.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that

(4.30)

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(g,g)=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(\tilde{g},\tilde{g})\,[1+o(1)]

is proportional to

or

, respectively.

We are now ready to compare
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}
with
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}
. Let
h\colon\mathcal{S}_{n}\to[0,1]
be such that

,

. We split the sum in (

) into four subsets of

,

,

m\in W_{0}\cap\mathcal{D}_{n}
,

\begin{split}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(h,h)&=O(n^{-c\beta})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in W_{0}\cap\mathcal{D}_{n}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in W_{0}\cap\mathcal{D}_{n}}\mathcal{Q}_{n}(m)\,\bar{r}_{n}\big(m,m^{\prime}\big)\,\big[\,\tilde{g}(m)-\tilde{g}(m^{\prime})\big]^{2}.\end{split}

\begin{split}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(h,h)&=O(n^{-c\beta})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in W_{0}\cap\mathcal{D}_{n}}\left[1+O\big(n^{-1/2}\log^{3/2}n\big)\right]\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)\\

5.
Capacity and valley estimates

Section
5.1
provides sharp asymptotic upper bounds and lower bounds on the capacity of the metastable pair between which the crossover is being considered. These estimates use the results of the Section
4
together with the Dirichlet principle and the Berman-Konsowa principle, which are variational representations of capacity. Section
5.2
provides a sharp asymptotic estimate for the mesoscopic measure of the valleys of the minima of
F_{n}
, which leads to a sharp asymptotic estimate for
F_{n}
inside this valley.

5.1.
Capacity estimates

Given a Markov process
(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with state space
S
, a key quantity in the potential-theoretic approach to metastability is the
capacity

\hbox{\rm cap}(A,B)
of two disjoint subsets
A,B
of
S
. This is defined by (see
[
6
, Eq. (7.1.39)]
)

(5.1)

\hbox{\rm cap}(A,B)=\sum_{x\in A}\mu(x)\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{B}<\tau_{A}),

where
\mu
is the invariant measure and
\mathbb{P}_{x}
is the probability distribution of the Markov process starting in
x
.

Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is the set of local minima of
F_{n}
.

Proposition 5.1

(
Asymptotics of the capacity).

Let
\mathbf{m}_{n}=(\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n})_{\ell\in[k]}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
M_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}\backslash\mathbf{m}_{n}
, such that the gate
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{m}_{n},M_{n})
consists of a unique point
\mathbf{t}_{n}=(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})_{\ell\in[k]}
. Suppose that
\beta\in(\beta_{c},\infty)
and
h\in[0,h_{c}(\beta))
. Then, as
n\to\infty
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\hbox{\rm cap}(\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}],\mathcal{S}_{n}[M_{n}])

\displaystyle=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})}\frac{(-\gamma_{n})\,n}{\sqrt{[-\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}))]}}\left(\frac{\pi n}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right).

Remark 5.1
.

Proposition
5.1
holds for any subset
M_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{n}\backslash\mathbf{m}_{n}
, separated from
\mathbf{m}_{n}
by
\mathbf{t}_{n}
, independently on the values of
F_{n}
on
M_{n}
.

\spadesuit

5.1.1.
Upper bound: Dirichlet principle

An important characterisation of the capacity between two disjoint sets is given by the
Dirichlet principle
. For our quantity of interest this states that

(5.3)

\hbox{\rm cap}(\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}],\mathcal{S}_{n}[M_{n}])=\inf_{u\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(u,u),

where
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is the set of functions from
\mathcal{S}_{n}
to
[0,1]
that are equal to
1
on
\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}]
and
0
on
\mathcal{S}_{n}[M_{n}]
.

Given that, by assumption,
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{m}_{n},M_{n})=\{\mathbf{t}_{n}\}
, we use the Dirichlet principle in (
5.3
) to obtain an upper bound on the capacity. We take as test function
h\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
defined in Section
4.5
and, using (
4.32
), we obtain

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\hbox{\rm cap}(\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}],\mathcal{S}_{n}[M_{n}])\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(h,h)\\
&=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})}\frac{(-\gamma_{n})n}{\sqrt{[-\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}))]}}\left(\frac{\pi n}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right).\end{split}

5.1.2.
Lower bound: Berman-Konsowa principle

We first note that the process
(\sigma_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is lumpable. Indeed, the process
(m_{n}(\sigma_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
is Markovian because the Hamiltonian
H_{n}(\sigma)
depends on
m_{n}(\sigma)
only (see (
2.6
)). Therefore, for
\mathbf{A}=S_{n}[{A}]
and
\mathbf{B}=S_{n}[{B}]
with
A
and
B
disjoint subsets of
\Gamma_{n}
,

(5.5)

\hbox{\rm cap}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})=\hbox{\rm cap}_{\Gamma}(A,B),

where
\hbox{\rm cap}_{\Gamma}
denotes the capacity for the process
(m_{n}(\sigma_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
, i.e., the projection of the process
(\sigma_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on the magnetisation space
\Gamma_{n}
. We write
\mathbb{P}^{\Gamma}
and
\mathbb{E}^{\Gamma}
to denote the law of
(m_{n}(\sigma_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
induced by the law
\mathbb{P}
of
(\sigma_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and its expectation, respectively. By the lumpability, we can focus on the dynamics on
\Gamma_{n}
.

Following the line of argument in
[
6
, Section 10.3]
(with
\varepsilon=\frac{2}{n}
and
d=k
), we obtain the lower bound

(5.6)

\begin{split}&\hbox{\rm cap}(\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}],\mathcal{S}_{n}[M_{n}])=\hbox{\rm cap}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{m}_{n},M_{n})\geq\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(\tilde{g},\tilde{g})\left[1+O(\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})}\frac{(-\gamma_{n})n}{\sqrt{[-\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}))]}}\left(\frac{\pi n}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right)\left[1+o_{n}(1)\right],\end{split}

where we use (
4.29
) and (
4.30
).

We sketch the proof. The main idea is to use the Berman-Konsowa principle for a suitable defective flow. More precisely, given disjoint subsets
A,B
of the state space, for any
defective loop-free unit flow

f_{A,B}
from
A
to
B
with defect function
\delta
(as defined in
[
6
, Definition 9.2]
), we can estimate (see
[
6
, Lemma 9.4]
, and notation therein)

(5.7)

\hbox{\rm cap}(A,B)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{M}\left(1+\left[\max_{y\in A_{i}}\frac{\delta(y)}{\mathcal{F}(y)}\right]_{+}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\gamma}\mathbb{P}^{f_{A,B}}(\gamma)\left[\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in\gamma}\frac{f_{A,B}((x,y))}{\mu(x)p(x,y)}\right)^{-1}\right],

where
[\cdot]_{+}
denotes the positive part and
\gamma
is a self-avoiding path from
A
to
B
. It turns out that, with a suitable choice of the flow
f
, the product in the right-hand side of (
5.7
) is bounded from below by
1+O(\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n)
, and the sum over
\gamma
from below by
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(\tilde{g},\tilde{g})[1+o_{n}(1)]
. This proves (
5.6
).

We give a sketch of the test flow definition in our setting. Here
A=\{\mathbf{m}_{n}\}
and
B=M_{n}
. Let
v^{*}
be the eigenvector corresponding to the unique negative eigenvalue of the Hessian of
F_{n}
at the saddle point
\mathbf{t}_{n}
(unique gate point in
\mathcal{G}(\{\mathbf{m}_{n}\},M_{n})
). Let
G_{n}
be the cylinder in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
intersected with
\Gamma_{n}
, centred at
\mathbf{t}_{n}
, with axis
v^{*}
, radius
\rho=C\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n
and length
\rho^{\prime}=C^{\prime}\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n
. We will denote by
\partial_{B}G_{n}
the base facing
B
and by
\partial_{A}G_{n}
the central part of radius
C^{\prime\prime}\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n
of the base facing
A
, with
C^{\prime\prime}<C
. Choose the constants so that
G_{n}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}_{n}
defined in (
4.1
).

We define a defective flow
f_{A,B}
from
A
to
B
consisting of three parts:
f_{A}
, a unitary flow from
A
to
\partial_{A}G_{n}
;
f
, a defective loop-free unit flow from
\partial_{A}G_{n}
to
\partial_{B}G_{n}
inside
G_{n}
;
f_{B}
, a unitary flow from
\partial_{B}G_{n}
to
B
. This choice implies that the sum over
\gamma
in (
5.7
) is relevant only on the paths entering
G_{n}
in
\partial_{A}G_{n}
, exiting
G_{n}
in
\partial_{B}G_{n}
, and afterwards reaching
B
without going back to
G_{n}
. For this purpose we choose
f_{A}
and
f_{B}
such that
f_{A}((x,y))
and
f_{B}((x,y))
are proportional to
\mathcal{Q}_{n}(x)
. For
m\in G_{n}
such that
m^{\ell,+}\in G_{n}
, define

(5.8)

f((m,m^{\ell,+}))=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)r_{\ell}\left[g(m^{\ell,+})-g(m)\right]_{+}}{N(g)},

where
g
is defined in (
4.24
),
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}
in (
4.6
),
r_{\ell}
in (
4.9
) and

(5.9)

N(g)=\sum_{m\in\partial_{A}G_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in[k]:\\
m^{\ell,+}\in G_{n}\end{subarray}}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{n}(m)r_{\ell}\left[g(m^{\ell,+})-g(m)\right]_{+}.

The contribution to the sum in brackets in (
5.7
) turns out to be negligible outside
G_{n}
. Therefore, no further conditions on the flows
f_{A}
and
f_{B}
are necessary, provided the total flow out of
A
is
1
and the total flow
f_{A,B}
is defective and loop-free.

5.2.
Measure of the valley

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we need the following estimate on the measure of the valley of the minima of
F_{n}
. For
\mathbf{m}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
, let
A(\mathbf{m}_{n})\subset\Gamma_{n}
be the valley of
\mathbf{m}_{n}
as defined in
[
6
, Eq. (8.2.10)]
.

Lemma 5.2

(
Gibbs weight of the valley).

Given
\mathbf{m}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
,

(5.10)

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n}))=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\frac{\exp\left(-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})\right)}{\sqrt{\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}}\left(\frac{n\pi}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right)\left[1+O(\,n^{-1/2}\log^{3/2}n)\right],

where
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
is the mesoscopic measure defined in (
2.17
), and
\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})
is the
k\times k
Hessian matrix defined in (
4.2
).

Proof.

The proof follows that of
[
6
, Lemma 10.12 and (10.2.33)]
. The relevant contribution to
\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n}))
is given by the measure of a ball
B_{\rho}
of radius
\rho=C\,n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n
centred in
\mathbf{m}_{n}
, with
C
constant, contained in
A(\mathbf{m}_{n})
. Indeed, if
y\in A(\mathbf{m}_{n})
and
d(\mathbf{m}_{n},y)>\rho
, then by Taylor expansion of
F_{n}
around
\mathbf{m}_{n}
we have

(5.11)

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}_{n}(y)&=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp[-\beta nF_{n}(y)]=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\left[-\beta n[F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})+c\,d(\mathbf{m}_{n},y)^{2}]\right]\\
&\leq\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\left[-\beta n[F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})+c\rho^{2}]\right]=\frac{n^{-\beta cC^{2}}}{Z_{n}}\exp\left[-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})\right],\end{split}

where
c
is a constant. The condition
y\in A(\mathbf{m}_{n})
is needed to ensure that
F_{n}(y)>F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})
, implying that
c
is positive. Therefore, we obtain the rough estimate

(5.12)

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n})\backslash B_{\rho})\leq n^{k}\frac{n^{-\beta cC^{2}}}{Z_{n}}\exp\left[-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})\right],

where we use that
|\Gamma_{n}|\leq n^{k}
. The bound in (
5.12
) is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n})\backslash B_{\rho})
is negligible in
\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n}))
.

Compute

(5.13)

\begin{split}&Z_{n}\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n})\cap B_{\rho})=Z_{n}\mathcal{Q}_{n}(B_{\rho})=Z_{n}\sum_{y\in B_{\rho}}\mathcal{Q}_{n}(y)=\sum_{y\in B_{\rho}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(y)}\\
&=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})}\sum_{y\in B_{\rho}}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta n}{2}\left\langle{y-\mathbf{m}_{n}},{(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))\cdot(y-\mathbf{m}_{n})}\right\rangle+O(n\rho^{3})\right]\\
&=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})}[1+O(n\rho^{3})]\sum_{y\in B_{\rho}}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta n}{2}\left\langle{y-\mathbf{m}_{n}},{(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))\cdot(y-\mathbf{m}_{n})}\right\rangle\right]\\
&=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{\left|{A_{\ell,n}}\right|}{2}\right)[1+O(n\rho^{3})]\\
&\times\int_{B_{\rho}}\mathrm{d}y\exp\left[-\frac{\beta n}{2}\left\langle{y-\mathbf{m}_{n}},{(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))\cdot(y-\mathbf{m}_{n})}\right\rangle\right]\\
&=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{k}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right)[1+O(n\rho^{3})]\left(\frac{2\pi}{n\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}}\\
&=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})}}{\sqrt{\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}}\left(\frac{n\pi}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right)[1+O(n\rho^{3})],\end{split}

where we use the Taylor expansion

(5.14)

F_{n}(y)=F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{y-\mathbf{m}_{n}},{(\nabla^{2}F_{n})(\mathbf{m}_{n})\cdot(y-\mathbf{m}_{n})}\right\rangle+O(\rho^{3}),\qquad y\in B_{\rho},

and the approximation of the sum by an integral is correct up to an error
1+O(\rho)
. In the last lines we approximated the Gaussian integral on intervals
[-\rho,\rho]
by the Gaussian integral on
\mathbb{R}
, with an error
1+O(n^{-c})
. We conclude by looking at (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), and noting that for
C
large enough
\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n})\backslash B_{\rho})
is negligible compared to
\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n})\cap B_{\rho})
.
∎

6.1.
Average crossover time

Let us return to the notation of Theorem
1.1
, where
\mathbf{m}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}

\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})=\{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\backslash\mathbf{m}_{n}\colon\,F_{n}(m)\leq F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})\}
. To prove Theorem
1.1
we use the relation

(6.1)

\mathbb{E}^{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{m}_{n}}(\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})})=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\frac{\mu(A(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}{\hbox{\rm cap}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{m}_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))},

Recall notation introduced in Section
5.1.2
.
Because

for all

, (
6.1
) follows from
[
6
, Theorem 8.15]
after proving that

is a set of metastable points in the sense of
[

[

, where similar values of capacities and invariant measures occur.

Using (
6.1
) in combination with Proposition
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, we obtain that, for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}]
,

(6.2)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}(\tau_{\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]})&=\mathbb{E}^{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{m}_{n}}(\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})})=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}{\hbox{\rm cap}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{m}_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}\\
&=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{n}(A(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}{\hbox{\rm cap}(\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}],\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})])}\\
&=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\frac{\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\frac{\exp\left(-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})\right)}{\sqrt{\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}}\left(\frac{n\pi}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right)}{\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\left[-\beta nF_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})\right]\frac{(-\gamma_{n})n}{\sqrt{[-\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}))]}}\left(\frac{\pi n}{2\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\left(\prod_{\ell\in[k]}{\omega_{\ell,n}}\right)}\\
&=[1+o_{n}(1)]\,\sqrt{\frac{[-\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n}))]}{\det(\mathbb{A}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2\beta(-\gamma_{n})}\right)\exp\left[\beta n(F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n}))\right],\end{split}

where we use that the dynamics depends on the starting configuration
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}]
only, through its level magnetisations
m_{n}(\sigma)=\mathbf{m}_{n}
(see (
2.6
)), and also use the lumpability.

6.2.
Exponential law

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
. Since the dynamics depends on the starting configuration
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathbf{m}_{n}]
through its level magnetisation
m_{n}(\sigma)=\mathbf{m}_{n}
only (see (
2.6
)), we have

(6.3)

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma}\left(\tau_{\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]}>t\,\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left[\tau_{\mathcal{S}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]}\right]\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{m}_{n}}\left(\bar{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})}>t\,\mathbb{E}^{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{m}_{n}}\left[\bar{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})}\right]\right),\end{split}

where
\bar{\tau}
is the hitting time of the process projected on
\Gamma_{n}
. Given the non-degeneracy hypothesis (Hypothesis
1
in Section
1.3.4
) and the one-dimensional landscape analysis (in Section
3.3
), we can apply
[
6
, Theorem 8.45]
to the right-hand side of (
6.3
) and conclude the proof.

6.3.
Randomness of the exponent

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
. In particular, we compute
F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})-[F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m})]
to leading order.

Recalling definitions (
2.26
) and (
3.5
),
we have

(6.4)

F_{\beta,h}(m)=-\frac{1}{2}K(m)^{2}-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\,\omega_{\ell}\,m_{\ell}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}I_{\mathbf{C}}(m_{\ell}).

Let
\mathbf{m}=(\mathbf{m}_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]},\mathbf{t}=(\mathbf{t}_{\ell})_{\ell\in[k]}\in[-1,1]^{k}
be the critical points of
F_{\beta,h}
closest to
\mathbf{m}_{n},\mathbf{t}_{n}
(i.e., the critical points of
F_{n}
defined above), respectively.
Note that
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{t}
satisfy (
3.4
), while
\mathbf{m}_{n}
and
\mathbf{t}_{n}
satisfy (
3.3
).
Using (
2.21
), we get

(6.5)

\begin{split}&F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t}_{n})=-\frac{1}{2}[K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})^{2}-K(\mathbf{t}_{n})^{2}]-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\beta}\left[\sum_{\ell\in[k]}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})+\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{1}{2n}\log\left(\frac{\pi(1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2})}{2}\right)\omega_{\ell,n}-\frac{k}{2n}+o\left(n^{-1}\right)\right]\end{split}

and

(6.6)

\begin{split}&F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})=-\frac{1}{2}[K(\mathbf{t}_{n})^{2}-K(\mathbf{t})^{2}]+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}[I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})-I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell})].\end{split}

By (
3.2
), we have

(6.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}}{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta\left[a_{\ell}K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})+h\right],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\mathbf{t}_{\ell}}{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{t})+h\right].

Thus,

(6.8)

\begin{split}I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})-I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell})&=(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})I_{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell})+O((\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})^{2})\\
&=(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\mathbf{t}_{\ell}}{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}}\right)+O((\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})^{2})\\
&=(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})\beta\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{t})+h\right]+O((\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})^{2}).\end{split}

Moreover,

(6.9)

\begin{split}&K(\mathbf{t}_{n})^{2}-K(\mathbf{t})^{2}=\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]\\
&=\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}[\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]+\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}]+[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}][\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]\right)\end{split}

and

(6.10)

\begin{split}&K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})^{2}-K(\mathbf{t}_{n})^{2}=\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,[\omega_{\ell,n}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}]\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}\\
&=\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}\left(\omega_{\ell}[\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}]+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]+[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}][\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}]\right).\end{split}

Similar equalities hold after we replace
\mathbf{t}
by
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{t}_{n}
by
\mathbf{m}_{n}
. Using the previous computations, we obtain

(6.11)

\begin{split}&F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})-[F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m})]\\
&=F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t}_{n})+F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})-[F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m}_{n})+F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m}_{n})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m})]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\\
&\times\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime},n}\right]\left(\omega_{\ell}[\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}]+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]+[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}][\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}]\right)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\mathbf{m}_{\ell}\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime},n}\right]\\
&\quad-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}\right]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]\left[I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})-I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n})\right]+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{1}{2n}\log\left(\frac{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2}}{1-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}^{2}}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}\left[I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})-I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell})+I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{m}_{\ell})-I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n})\right]+o\left(n^{-1}\right).\end{split}

Using(
6.8
), we find

(6.12)

\begin{split}&[F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]-[F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m})]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime},n}\right]\\
&\quad\times\left(\omega_{\ell}[\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}]+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]+[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}][\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}]\right)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\mathbf{m}_{\ell}\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime},n}\right]\\
&\quad-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}\right]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}[\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}]\left[I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})-I_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n})\right]+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{1}{2n}\log\left(\frac{1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}^{2}}{1-\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}^{2}}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}\Big[(\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})\beta\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{t})+h\right]+O((\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell})^{2})\\
&\qquad-(\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell})\beta\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{m})+h\Big]+O((\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell})^{2})\right]\\
&\quad+o\left(n^{-1}\right).\end{split}

Since

(6.13)

\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}=\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}[\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]+\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}]+[\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}][\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]\right),

we focus on estimating
\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}
.

From Taylor expansion, we get

(6.14)

\begin{split}&\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}=\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}+h\right]\right)-\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+h\right]\right)\\
&=\beta\,a_{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}[\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]\left[1-\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+h\right]\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\quad-\beta^{2}\,a_{\ell}^{2}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}[\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]\right)^{2}\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+h\right]\right)\\
&\quad\times\left[1-\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+h\right]\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\quad+O\left(a_{\ell}^{3}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}[\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}]\right)^{3}\right).\end{split}

Since

(6.15)

\begin{split}\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}&=(\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}),\end{split}

we have

(6.16)

\begin{split}\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}&=\beta\,a_{\ell}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}[(\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}})]\\
&\quad-\beta^{2}\,a_{\ell}^{2}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}[(\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}})]\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+O\left(a_{\ell}^{3}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}[(\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}\,-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}(\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime},n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}})]\right)^{3}\right).\end{split}

Suppose that
\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\sim\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}
. By the Central Limit Theorem,
\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}\sim\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}
, where
Z_{\ell}
is the normal random variable
N(0,\omega_{\ell}(1-\omega_{\ell}))
. Hence

(6.17)

\begin{split}\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}&=\beta\,a_{\ell}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\left(\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]\\
&\quad-\beta^{2}\,a_{\ell}^{2}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\left(\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+O\left(a_{\ell}^{3}\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}\,\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\left(\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]\right)^{3}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\beta\,a_{\ell}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}Z_{\ell^{\prime}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{n}\beta\,a_{\ell}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}Z_{\ell^{\prime}}\left(Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}-\beta\,a_{\ell}\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}\in[k]}\,a_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}\omega_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}Y_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}\right)+o(n^{-1})\end{split}

and so

(6.18)

\begin{split}Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}&=\beta\,a_{\ell}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]\frac{\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{1-\beta\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\right]}+O(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\end{split}

where the denominator does not vanish because of Remark
4.2
. Thus, up to a factor
O(n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
,
Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}
is a normal random variable with mean
0
and variance

(6.19)

\beta^{2}\,a_{\ell}^{2}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}^{2}\right]^{2}\frac{\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}(1-\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})}{\left(1-\beta\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}\right]\right)^{2}}.

Similar results hold after we replace
\mathbf{t}
by
\mathbf{m}
.

Going back to (
6.12
), using (
6.13
) and (
6.18
), and inserting
\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\sim\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}
and
\mathbf{m}_{\ell,n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell}\sim\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}
and
\omega_{\ell,n}-\omega_{\ell}\sim\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}
, we obtain

(6.20)

\begin{split}&[F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]-[F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m})]\\
&\sim-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\left(\mathbf{m}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\left(\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime}}+\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]\\
&\qquad\times\left(\omega_{\ell}\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{Z_{\ell}Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{n}\right)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}}{n}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell}\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{m}}}{n}\right)\\
&\quad-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell}-\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}\left[I_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-I_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathbf{m}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{1}{2n}\log\left(\frac{1-\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}}{1-\left(\mathbf{m}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}\left[\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\beta\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{t})+h\right]+O\left(\frac{(Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}})^{2}}{n}\right)-\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{m})+h\right]+O\left(\frac{(Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}})^{2}}{n}\right)\right]\\
&\qquad+o\left(n^{-1}\right).\\
\end{split}

Thus,

(6.21)

\begin{split}&[F_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})-F_{n}(\mathbf{m}_{n})]-[F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{t})-F_{\beta,h}(\mathbf{m})]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell}\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime}}\right]\left(\omega_{\ell}\frac{Z_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}a_{\ell}\,a_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\omega_{\ell}\,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\mathbf{t}_{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell}\frac{Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\\
&\quad-h\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\left[\mathbf{t}_{\ell}-\mathbf{m}_{\ell}\right]\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{Z_{\ell}}{\sqrt{n}}\left[I_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathbf{t}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-I_{\mathbf{C}}\left(\mathbf{m}_{\ell}+\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]\\
&\quad+\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\omega_{\ell}\left[\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}}{\sqrt{n}}\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{t})+h\right]-\frac{Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}}{\sqrt{n}}\left[a_{\ell}K(\mathbf{m})+h\right]\right]+O\left(n^{-1}\right).\end{split}

Since the random variables
Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{t}}
,
Y_{\ell}^{\mathbf{m}}
,
Z_{\ell}
are centred normal, this concludes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From (
6.21
) it is possible to compute explicitly the variance of
Z
defined in Theorem
1.3
, because the variances of all the random variables involved are known (at least to leading order).

Appendix A
Metastability on the complete graph without disorder

We give a brief overview of well-known results for the standard Curie-Weiss model. We refer to
[
6
, Chapter 13]
for more details.

The Glauber dynamics is defined as in Section
1.2
, but with
J\equiv 1
. For convenience we write the Curie-Weiss Hamiltonian as

(A.1)

H_{n}(\sigma)=-\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i,j\in[n]}\sigma(i)\sigma(j)-h\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma(i),\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n},

which is as (
2.5
) when
J\equiv 1
. What makes this case easier than the one with disorder is that the interaction is
mean-field
. Indeed, we may write

(A.2)

H_{n}(\sigma)=n\big[-\tfrac{1}{2}m_{n}(\sigma)^{2}-hm_{n}(\sigma)\big],

with

(A.3)

m_{n}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}\sigma(i)\in[-1,1]

the magnetisation. In this case the magnetisation process
(m_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, defined by

(A.4)

m_{n}(t)=m_{n}(\sigma_{t}),

is Markovian. More specifically, it is a nearest-neighbour random walk on the grid

(A.5)

\Gamma_{n}=\left\{-1,-1+\tfrac{2}{n},\ldots,+1-\tfrac{2}{n},+1\right\}.

In the limit as
n\to\infty
, (
A.4
) converges to a Brownian motion on
[-1,+1]
in the potential
F_{\beta,h}
given by

(A.6)

F_{\beta,h}(m)=-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}-hm+\frac{1}{\beta}I(m),

with

(A.7)

I(m)=\frac{1-m}{2}\log\left(\frac{1-m}{2}\right)+\frac{1+m}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+m}{2}\right)

Appendix D
Limit of the prefactor

Below Theorem
1.2
we stated that the prefactor in (
1.19
) converges. For completeness, in this Appendix we compute its limit, although, as we mentioned after Theorem
1.3
, it is negligible because of the order of approximation of the exponent.

We focus first on
\gamma_{n}
. Recall notation in (
1.10
), (
1.11
) and (
2.1
). Then (
4.20
) can be written as

(D.1)

\begin{split}&1+O(n^{-1})=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{a_{\ell}^{2}\omega_{\ell,n}(1-\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})\exp\left[-2\beta\left(-a_{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{\ell}}{n}+K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})\right)-h\right)_{+}\right]}{\frac{\exp\left[-2\beta\left(-a_{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{\ell}}{n}+K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})\right)-h\right)_{+}\right]}{\beta(1+\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n})}-2\gamma_{n}}\\
&=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\frac{a_{\ell}^{2}\omega_{\ell,n}\left(1-\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}\,K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})+h\right]\right)\right)\exp\left[-2\beta\left(-a_{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{\ell}}{n}+K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})\right)-h\right)_{+}\right]}{\frac{\exp\left[-2\beta\left(-a_{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{\ell}}{n}+K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})\right)-h\right)_{+}\right]}{\beta\left(1+\tanh\left(\beta\left[a_{\ell}K_{n}(\mathbf{t}_{n})+h\right]\right)\right)}-2\gamma_{n}}.\end{split}

In the first equality we use (
3.3
) for
\mathbf{t}_{n}
, i.e., the approximation of the stationary points of

by the stationary points of
\bar{F}_{n}
. This makes
\mathbf{t}_{\ell,n}

. Thus, we obtain that
\gamma_{n}
converges to
\gamma

(D.2)

) is similar to

We are left to find the limit of the determinants ratio. By (

(D.3)

\begin{split}\det\mathbb{A}_{n}(m)&=\left(1-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\beta\,a_{\ell}^{2}\,\omega_{\ell,n}[1-(m_{\ell})^{2}]\right)\prod_{\ell^{\prime}\in[k]}\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\omega_{\ell^{\prime},n}}{1-(m_{\ell^{\prime}})^{2}}\left[1+O(n^{-1})\right].\end{split}

Using the law of large numbers as above and with the same notation, we find

where

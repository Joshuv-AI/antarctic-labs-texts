N N -jettiness soft function at next-to-next-to-leading order in perturbative QCD
Prem Agarwal, Kirill Melnikov, and Ivan Pedron

Abstract
We derive a compact representation of the renormalized N N -jettiness soft function that is free of infrared and collinear divergences through next-to-next-to-leading order in perturbative QCD. The number of hard partons N N is a parameter in the formula for the finite remainder. Cancellation of all infrared and collinear singularities between the bare soft function and its renormalization matrix in color space is demonstrated analytically.

2
Definitions of basic quantities

We consider partonic processes with an arbitrary number of hard color-charged partons
that we denote by
N

\begin{split}&0\to h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}+h_{4}+\cdots h_{N},\\
&h_{1}\to h_{2}+h_{3}+h_{4}+\cdots h_{N},\\
&h_{1}+h_{2}\to h_{3}+h_{4}+\cdots h_{N}.\end{split}

(1)

The first process refers to
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation, the second to deep-inelastic scattering and the third to
hadron collisions.
Each parton in
Eq. (
1
) carries a four-momentum
p_{i}
and a color
charge
{\bf T_{i}}
. All partons are assumed to be massless.

The cross sections of processes shown in Eq. (
1
) are affected by the radiation of soft quarks and gluons, both real and virtual. To quantify their impact,
an observable that controls
the softness of the radiation is needed.
In this paper, we will use
the
N
-jettiness
{\cal T}

[
42
]
for this purpose.

To define
{\cal T}
, we proceed as follows. For a generic process, we split all final-state partons that we have
to consider in a particular
order of perturbation theory
into resolved
{\cal R}
and unresolved
{\cal U}
ones. We define a list

L_{x}=\left\{\frac{2p_{x}p_{h_{1}}}{P_{h_{1}}},\frac{2p_{x}p_{h_{2}}}{P_{h_{2}}},\frac{2p_{x}p_{h_{3}}}{P_{h_{3}}}...\right\},

(2)

where
x\in{\cal U}
and
h_{i}\in{\cal R}
. Quantities

P_{h_{i}}
are arbitrary normalization constants.
We then define the
N
-jettiness variable
as
[
42
]

{\cal T}({\cal R},{\cal U})=\sum\limits_{x\in{\cal U}}{\rm min}\left[L_{x}\right].

(3)

To compute radiative corrections to processes in Eq. (
1
), we need to consider virtual and real-emission
corrections. Since we work through next-to-next-to-leading order,
we need to include virtual corrections up to two loops, one-loop corrections with and without one soft parton and the real emission processes with up to two soft partons.
For virtual corrections to processes without additional soft radiation,

{\cal U}
is an empty set and so
{\cal T}
is zero. The same is true
for leading order processes shown in Eq. (
1
).

We note that virtual corrections do not need to be computed explicitly. Indeed, since infrared and collinear singularities of a generic two-loop amplitude are
known
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
, it is customary to separate them from the short-distance
contribution to the two-loop amplitude known as the “hard function” and include them into the renormalization constant
of the soft function, that we discuss in the next section.
Hence, to compute the soft function at NNLO we need to study contributions with one or two soft partons to cross sections
of processes in Eq. (
1
), subject to
N
-jettiness
constraint.

The perturbative expansion of the
bare
soft function can be written as

S_{\tau}(\tau)=\delta(\tau)+a_{s}S_{\tau,1}(\tau)+a_{s}^{2}S_{\tau,2}(\tau)+\cdots,

(4)

where
\tau
is the value of
N
-jettiness and
a_{s}=\alpha_{s}(\mu)/(2\pi)
is the renormalized QCD coupling constant. The quantities
S_{\tau,i}(\tau)
,
i=1,2
,
can be computed starting from universal functions that describe the behaviour of QCD matrix elements
when some of the
radiated partons are soft.

Before proceeding, it is useful to point out that it is very convenient to work with the Laplace transform
of the soft function
[
37
]
. The Laplace transform is defined as follows

S(u)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\;S_{\tau}(\tau)e^{-u\tau}.

(5)

As we will see from the explicit computations described below, the dependence of functions
S_{\tau,i}
,
i=1,2
, on
\tau
is very particular and is given by
simple powers
\tau^{-1-n\epsilon}
,
where
n=2,4
etc. For these functions, the Laplace transform evaluates to

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\;\tau^{-1-n\epsilon}e^{-u\tau}=-u^{n\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma(1-n\epsilon)}{n\epsilon}=-\frac{{\bar{u}}^{n\epsilon}e^{-\epsilon n\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}}\Gamma(1-n\epsilon)}{n\epsilon}.

(6)

Note that in the last step we introduced a variable
\bar{u}=ue^{\gamma_{E}}
to simplify
the expansion of the corresponding
\Gamma
-functions in
\epsilon
.
In what follows we will use Eq. (
6
) to compute the
Laplace transform of the perturbative expansion of the bare soft function.

3
Renormalization of the soft function

Perturbative computations of the soft function lead to expressions that contain soft and collinear divergences
that manifest themselves through
1/\epsilon
poles.
These divergences are removed by a dedicated renormalization
[
16
]
.

The renormalization of the soft function is a multiplicative (matrix) renormalization in the Laplace space
[
37
]
. We denote the Laplace transforms of the bare and renormalized
soft functions by

S
and
\tilde{S}
, respectively. The two functions
are related by the following equation

S=Z\tilde{S}Z^{+},

(7)

where
Z
is a matrix in color space, see Appendix
A
.
While it is entirely possible to compute the unrenormalized soft function
S
and then remove the divergences
by using Eq. (
7
), it is
beneficial to study suitable combinations of
Z
and

S
that are actually needed for
computing
\tilde{S}
.

To find these combinations,
we write expansions of
Z
,
S
and
\tilde{S}
in powers of
\alpha_{s}

\begin{split}&Z=1+Z_{1}+Z_{2},\\
&S=1+S_{1}+S_{2},\\
&\tilde{S}=1+\tilde{S}_{1}+\tilde{S}_{2},\end{split}

(8)

substitute them into Eq. (
7
) and solve for
{\tilde{S}}_{1,2}
.
We find

\begin{split}&\tilde{S}_{1}=S_{1}-Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{+},\\
&\tilde{S}_{2}=S_{2}-Z_{2}-Z_{2}^{+}+Z_{1}Z_{1}+Z_{1}^{+}Z_{1}^{+}-Z_{1}S_{1}-S_{1}Z_{1}^{+}+Z_{1}Z_{1}^{+}.\end{split}

(9)

It was recently pointed out
in Ref.
[
38
]
, in the context of the application of the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme to arbitrary multi-jet processes, that it is beneficial to
separate iterations of

{\cal O}(\alpha_{s})
soft, soft-collinear and virtual contributions to cross sections from the rest of NNLO
contributions. Following the same logic, we write

{S}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}{S}_{1}{S}_{1}+{S}_{2,r}.

(10)

It is useful to do the same for the NNLO contribution to the renormalization matrix

Z_{2}=\frac{1}{2}Z_{1}Z_{1}+Z_{2,r}.

(11)

It is then easy
to see that

\tilde{S}_{2}

can be written
as

\tilde{S}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{S}_{1}\tilde{S}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}[Z_{1},Z_{1}^{+}]+\frac{1}{2}\left[S_{1},Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{+}\right]+S_{2,r}-Z_{2,r}-Z_{2,r}^{+}.

(12)

We will show below that
this representation is
beneficial for computing the renormalized
N
-jettiness
soft function. Indeed, when
representation in Eq. (
12
)
is used, the required effort is minimized because cancellations of
1/\epsilon
poles between the different quantities are identified relatively early in the course of the computation. However,
before discussing how the NNLO computation is performed,
we will illustrate our approach by calculating the NLO
contribution to the
N
-jettiness soft function.

4
N
-jettiness soft function at NLO

We will explain main ideas of our approach
by considering
N
-jettiness soft function at NLO.
Since one-loop virtual corrections to
N
-jettiness soft function do not need to be considered, we focus on the single real-emission contribution below.
We use label
\mathfrak{m}
to denote a soft gluon and
write its momentum as
p_{\mathfrak{m}}
. The gluon
\mathfrak{m}
is the only unresolved parton, therefore
{\cal U}=\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.

We choose
P_{h_{i}}=E_{i}
, where

E_{i}
is the energy of the parton
h_{i}
in the chosen
reference frame,
and obtain (c.f. Eqs. (
2
,
3
))

\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{m})=E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(13)

In Eq. (
13
)
E_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the energy of the unresolved gluon
\mathfrak{m}
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a function defined as

\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}={\rm min}\{\rho_{1\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{2\mathfrak{m}},\rho_{3\mathfrak{m}},...,\rho_{N\mathfrak{m}}\},

(14)

where
\rho_{ij}=1-\vec{n}_{i}\cdot\vec{n}_{j}
and

\vec{n}_{i}

is a unit vector which points in the direction of the three-momentum of parton
i
.

The real-emission single-gluon soft contribution at fixed
N
-jettiness
\tau
reads

S_{\tau,1}(\tau)=-\sum\limits_{(ij)}{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\;I_{S,ij}^{(1)}(\tau),

(15)

where we use the notation

(ij)
to indicate that the sum runs over all
i,j\in{\cal R}
with the constraint
i\neq j
.
Furthermore,

{\bf T}_{i}
is the color charge operator of parton
i
,

I_{S,ij}^{(1)}=g_{s}^{2}\int[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{m}}]\;\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}})\;S_{ij}(\mathfrak{m}),

(16)

g_{s}
is the bare QCD coupling constant
and

S_{ij}(\mathfrak{m})=\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{(p_{i}p_{\mathfrak{m}})(p_{j}p_{\mathfrak{m}})}=\frac{1}{E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}}\;\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}},

(17)

is the soft eikonal function.
It is convenient to write the
phase space element of the gluon

\mathfrak{m}
as follows

[{\rm d}p_{m}]=\frac{{\rm d}\Omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(d-1)}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}\;\frac{{\rm d}E_{\mathfrak{m}}}{E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1+2\epsilon}}\;E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=\frac{\Omega^{(d-2)}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}\;[{\rm d}\Omega^{(d-1)}_{\mathfrak{m}}]\;\frac{{\rm d}E_{\mathfrak{m}}}{E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1+2\epsilon}}\;E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}.

(18)

We then combine the
coupling constant squared
with the remnant of phase space
normalization factor

g_{s}^{2}\frac{\Omega^{(d-2)}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}=[\alpha_{s}],

(19)

and note that

[\alpha_{s}]=a_{s}(\mu)\;\mu^{2\epsilon}\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(1+{\cal O}(a_{s})\right),

(20)

where
a_{s}(\mu)=\alpha_{s}(\mu)/(2\pi)
and

\alpha_{s}(\mu)
is the QCD coupling constant renormalized in the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme.

We work to first order in

a_{s}
and use Eq. (
18
) to
integrate over energy of the gluon
E_{\mathfrak{m}}
in Eq. (
16
). We find

I_{S,ij}^{(1)}(\tau)=\frac{[\alpha_{s}]}{\tau^{1+2\epsilon}}\;\int[{\rm d}\Omega^{(d-1)}_{\mathfrak{m}}]\;\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\;\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}=\frac{[\alpha_{s}]}{\tau^{1+2\epsilon}}\;\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\;\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}\;,

(21)

where
\langle..\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}

indicates integration over directions of the vector

\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

To proceed further, we compute the
Laplace transform
of the soft function and obtain

S_{1}=\frac{{\bar{u}}^{2\epsilon}e^{-2\epsilon\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}}\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{2\epsilon}\;[\alpha_{s}]\sum\limits_{(ij)}{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(22)

Integration over directions of the vector
\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}
induces collinear singularities when

\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}||\vec{n}_{i}
and
\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}||\vec{n}_{j}
. To isolate them, we make use
of the fact that collinear
singularities of the
integrand are fully controlled by the remnants of the eikonal function in Eq. (
22
)
and that the collinear limits of the
function
\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}
can be predicted
for an
arbitrary hard process
.

Indeed, consider the collinear limit

\mathfrak{m}||i
as an example.
In that limit,
\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}
is the smallest
\rho
-value so that

\lim_{\mathfrak{m}||i}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}=\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}.

(23)

It turns out useful to rewrite the integrand in
Eq. (
21
) as follows

\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\;\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}=\left(\frac{\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right)^{2\epsilon}\;\frac{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{ij}}{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{j\mathfrak{m}}}.

(24)

We then define

\left(\frac{\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right)^{2\epsilon}=1+2\epsilon g^{(2)}_{ij,\mathfrak{m}},

(25)

where the function
g^{(\alpha)}_{ij,\mathfrak{m}}
contains higher powers of
\epsilon
in addition to the first power shown explicitly.
Hence,
we write

\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=\left\langle\left(1+2\epsilon g^{(2)}_{ij,\mathfrak{m}}\right)\frac{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{ij}}{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(26)

Since the function
\epsilon g^{(2)}_{ij,m}
vanishes in all collinear limits and is
{\cal O}(\epsilon)
, it only provides
a finite contribution to the
N
-jettiness soft function that will have to be computed numerically for a given configuration of hard partons.
On the contrary,
the first integral in the right-hand side of Eq. (
26
)
can be
computed explicitly, see Eq. (
194
).
We write

\left\langle\frac{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{ij}}{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=\frac{2\eta_{ij}^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}K_{ij}^{(2)},

(27)

where
\eta_{ij}=\rho_{ij}/2

and

K_{ij}^{(2)}=\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(1+2\epsilon)}\;{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\epsilon,\epsilon,1-\epsilon,1-\eta_{ij}\right).

(28)

Putting everything together, we find

S_{1}=a_{s}\;(\mu\bar{u})^{2\epsilon}\;\frac{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}\sum\limits_{(ij)}{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\;\left[\frac{\eta_{ij}^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{2}}K_{ij}^{(2)}+\left\langle\;g^{(2)}_{ij,\mathfrak{m}}\;\frac{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{ij}}{\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho^{1-2\epsilon}_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}\;\right],

(29)

It follows from the above equation that all
1/\epsilon
poles have been separated
from the finite remainder
and that the remnant of the complicated
N
-jettiness function appears in the finite remainder
only
.

It remains to combine

S_{1}
with the
renormalization matrices
Z_{1}
and

Z_{1}^{+}
, c.f.
Eq. (
9
).
The expression
for
Z_{1}
can be found in Appendix
A
; similar to the NLO soft function
shown in Eq. (
29
),
it is given by the sum of products of the color-charge operators
with some coefficients. Combining the different contributions
and discarding all terms beyond
{\cal O}(\epsilon^{0})
, we find

{\tilde{S}}_{1}=a_{s}\sum\limits_{(ij)}{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\left[2L_{ij}^{2}+{\rm Li}_{2}(1-\eta_{ij})+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}+\left\langle\ln\left(\frac{\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right)\;\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}+{\cal O}(\epsilon)\right],

(30)

where

L_{ij}=\ln\left(\mu\bar{u}\sqrt{\eta_{ij}}\right).

(31)

This result can be easily evaluated numerically for an arbitrary number of hard
partons
N
. The logarithm
\ln(\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{ij}/(\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}))
provides an infrared regulator for an integral that would have exhibited
collinear divergencies without it.

5
N
-jettiness soft function at NNLO

We will now explain how to extend the above
approach to compute the NNLO contribution to the
N
-jettiness soft function. It is clear that the NNLO computation is significantly more complex than the NLO one. However, we will show that it is possible to split the calculation into several independent parts making the entire problem simpler.

The NNLO contribution to the bare soft function reads

S_{2}=S_{2,RR}+S_{2,RV}-a_{s}\;\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}S_{1},

(32)

where
S_{2,RR}

is the double real-emission contribution,

S_{2,RV}
is the real-virtual contribution and the
last term arises because of the renormalization of the strong coupling constant in the NLO soft function. The leading order QCD
\beta
-function is defined as

\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{6}C_{A}-\frac{2}{3}n_{F}T_{R},

(33)

where
C_{A}=N_{c}
is the Casimir
of the group
SU(N_{c})
,
T_{R}=1/2

and
n_{f}
is the number of massless quarks.

The double-real emission contribution
is obtained by integrating the double-soft
eikonal current
[
46
]

over the phase space of two soft gluons subject to the
N
-jettiness constraint.
Following Ref.
[
46
]
,
we further split the double-real
contribution into
correlated and uncorrelated pieces

S_{2,RR,\tau}=S_{2,RR,\tau,T^{4}}+S_{2,RR,\tau,T^{2}}.

(34)

We note that the subscript
\tau
indicates
that the Laplace transform of the
corresponding quantities has not yet been computed.

The uncorrelated contribution reads

S_{2,RR,\tau,T^{4}}=\frac{1}{2}\;\sum\limits_{(ij),(k,l)}\{{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j},{\bf T}_{k}\cdot{\bf T}_{l}\}\;I_{T^{4},ij,kl},

(35)

where

I_{T^{4},ij,kl}=\frac{g_{s}^{4}}{2}\int[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{m}}]\;[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{n}}]\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}-E_{\mathfrak{n}}\psi_{\mathfrak{n}})\;S_{ij}(\mathfrak{m})\;S_{kl}(\mathfrak{n}),

(36)

and the eikonal functions
S_{ij}(\mathfrak{m})
and

S_{kl}(\mathfrak{n})
are defined in
Eq. (
17
).
The correlated contribution
S_{2,RR,T^{2}}

reads

S_{2,RR,\tau,T^{2}}=-\frac{C_{A}}{2}\sum\limits_{(ij)}\;{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\;I_{T^{2},ij},

(37)

where

I_{T^{2},ij}=\frac{g_{s}^{4}}{2}\;\int[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{m}}][{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{n}}]\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}-E_{\mathfrak{n}}\psi_{\mathfrak{n}})\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}).

(38)

The eikonal function

{\tilde{S}}_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})

can be found in Appendix
C
.

The real-virtual
contribution reads
[
46
]

\begin{split}S_{2,RV,\tau}&=\frac{[\alpha_{s}]\;2^{-\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{2}}C_{A}A_{K}(\epsilon)\sum\limits_{(ij)}\;{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\;I_{RV,ij}+[\alpha_{s}]\frac{4\pi N_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}\sum\limits_{(kij)}\kappa_{ij}F^{kij}I_{kij}.\end{split}

(39)

where

\kappa_{ij}=\lambda_{ij}-\lambda_{i\mathfrak{m}}-\lambda_{j\mathfrak{m}},

(40)

and
\lambda_{ij}=1
if
both
i
and
j
refer to incoming or outgoing partons and zero otherwise.
Furthermore, the normalization
factors in Eq. (
39
)
read

A_{K}(\epsilon)=\frac{\Gamma^{3}(1+\epsilon)\Gamma^{5}(1-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1+2\epsilon)\Gamma^{2}(1-2\epsilon)},\;\;\;N_{\epsilon}=\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma^{3}(1-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)},

(41)

and we have defined

F^{kij}=f_{abc}T^{a}_{k}T^{b}_{i}T^{c}_{j}

to describe
anti-symmetrized product of three color charge operators. Finally,
the functions
I_{RV,ij}

and
I_{kij}
read

I_{RV,ij}=g_{s}^{2}\int[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{m}}]\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}})(S_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m}))^{1+\epsilon},

(42)

and

I_{kij}=g_{s}^{2}\int[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{m}}]\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}})S_{ki}(\mathfrak{m})(S_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m}))^{\epsilon}.

(43)

In the next section we will show that

S_{2,RR,T^{4}}=\frac{1}{2}S_{1}S_{1}.

(44)

Therefore, the quantity
S_{2,r}

introduced in Eq. (
10
) reads

S_{2,r}=S_{2,RR,T^{2}}+S_{2,RV}-\frac{a_{s}\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}S_{1}.

(45)

6
Uncorrelated emissions of two soft gluons

We have argued that
S_{2}
contains an iterated contribution of the next-to-leading order soft function

S_{1}
and that it is beneficial to separate it from the
rest of
S_{2}
, c.f. Eq. (
10
). We will now show
that this iteration
can be identified
with the Laplace transform of the
term
S_{2,RR,\tau,T^{4}}

in Eq. (
35
).
To this end, we write

I_{T^{4},ij,kl}=\frac{[\alpha_{s}]^{2}}{2}\left\langle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}E_{\mathfrak{m}}}{E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1+2\epsilon}}\frac{{\rm d}E_{\mathfrak{n}}}{E_{\mathfrak{n}}^{1+2\epsilon}}\;\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}-E_{\mathfrak{n}}\psi_{\mathfrak{n}})\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\frac{\rho_{kl}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{l\mathfrak{n}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(46)

It is straightforward to
integrate over energies of two gluons
E_{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}}
. Indeed, the first integration
(say over
E_{\mathfrak{n}}
) is elementary since it just removes the
\delta
-function. The second integration over

E_{\mathfrak{m}}
extends from
0
to

E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\rm max}=\tau/\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}
, i.e. the energy of the gluon
\mathfrak{m}
which corresponds to
E_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
.
The integral turns out to be of a Beta-function type, and we obtain

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}E_{\mathfrak{m}}}{E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1+2\epsilon}}\frac{{\rm d}E_{\mathfrak{n}}}{E_{\mathfrak{n}}^{1+2\epsilon}}\;\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}-E_{\mathfrak{n}}\psi_{\mathfrak{n}})=\frac{\tau^{-1-4\epsilon}}{\Gamma(-4\epsilon)}\frac{\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{2\epsilon}\frac{\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2\epsilon}\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{2\epsilon}.

(47)

Using this result,
and performing the
Laplace transform,
we derive

\begin{split}S_{2,RR,T^{4}}&=\frac{[\alpha_{s}]^{2}}{4}\sum\limits_{(ij),(kl)}\{{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j},{\bf T}_{k}\cdot{\bf T}_{l}\}\left(\frac{u^{2\epsilon}\Gamma^{(}1-2\epsilon)}{2\epsilon}\right)^{2}\\
&\times\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}\;\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{kl}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{l\mathfrak{n}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\;S_{1}S_{1},\end{split}

(48)

where the last step follows from
the symmetry between

(ij)
and
(kl)

summation indices,
and from the comparison with the results for the NLO
N
-jettiness soft function derived in Section
4
.
As we already explained
in Section
3
, it is straightforward to combine the above result with the iterated contribution of the
renormalization constants to arrive at the relevant contribution to the renormalized soft function.

7
Terms with three color charges

We will next discuss the computation of the contribution that depends on triple products of color charges. It originates from the
following terms
in the expression for
{\tilde{S}}_{2}

{\tilde{S}}_{2}^{tc}=\frac{1}{2}\left[Z_{1},Z_{1}^{+}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[S_{1},Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{+}\right]+S_{RV,tc},

(49)

where the
real-virtual triple-color correlated
contribution is given by the Laplace transform
of the last term
in Eq. (

The commutators
are easy to compute using a general formula in
Ref.
[
38
]
.
2
2

2

See Eqs. (4.142, 4.143).
We find

where the last step

L_{ij}
in
Eq. (

and the (anti)-symmetry of

The second commutator reads

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}[S_{1},Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{+}]&=\frac{a^{2}_{s}\pi(\mu u)^{2\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{2}}\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}\epsilon}\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\;\sum\limits_{(kij)}\lambda_{kj}\;\left\langle\psi^{2\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}F^{kij}\\

(51)

where in the last step we replaced

The first of these equation holds because

Hence, we can write

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}[S_{1},Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{+}]=-\frac{a^{2}_{s}\pi(\mu u)^{2\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{2}}\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}\epsilon}\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\;\sum\limits_{(kij)}\kappa_{kj}\;\left\langle\psi^{2\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}\frac{\rho_{ki}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}F^{kij}.\end{split}

(53)

The real-virtual

S_{RV,tc}=\frac{a_{s}^{2}\pi(\mu\;\bar{u})^{4\epsilon}N_{\epsilon}2^{-\epsilon}}{2\epsilon^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(1-4\epsilon)}{\Gamma^{2}(1-\epsilon)e^{2\gamma_{E}\epsilon}}\sum\limits_{(kij)}\kappa_{kj}\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{4\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{ki}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}}\left(\frac{\rho_{kj}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right)^{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}\;F^{kij}\;.

We need to
combine the three
contributions to

{\tilde{S}}_{2}^{tc}

shown above, demonstrate that
all
1/\epsilon

contributions that exhibit dependence on the
N
-jettiness function.

53
)
and
S_{RV,tc}

\Sigma_{\text{tcc}}^{\psi}=\frac{1}{2}[S_{1},Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{+}]+S_{RV,tc}.

(55)

Both terms in Eq. (

\rho_{ij}

\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}
over the
directions of the
momentum of the gluon
\mathfrak{m}
. Following the discussion of the NLO contribution
to the soft function,
we write

\begin{split}&\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{ki}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=\left\langle(1+2\epsilon g^{(2)}_{ki,\mathfrak{m}})\frac{\rho_{ki}^{1-2\epsilon}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}^{1-2\epsilon}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}^{1-2\epsilon}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}},\\
&\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{4\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{ki}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}}\left(\frac{\rho_{kj}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right)^{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=\left\langle\left(1+4\epsilon g^{(4)}_{ki,\mathfrak{m}}\right)\frac{\rho_{ki}^{1-4\epsilon}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}^{1-4\epsilon}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}^{1-4\epsilon}}\left(\frac{\rho_{kj}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}\right)^{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}.\end{split}

(56)

Using Eq. (
56
), and focusing on the
divergent

and
g^{(4)}

\Sigma_{\text{tcc}}^{\psi}\to-\frac{2a_{s}^{2}\pi}{\epsilon}\sum\limits_{(kij)}\kappa_{kj}\left\langle\frac{\rho_{ik}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}}\left(g_{ki,\mathfrak{m}}^{(2)}-g_{ki,\mathfrak{m}}^{(4)}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}F^{kij}={\cal O}(\epsilon^{0}),

through order

Extending the above computation in

N
-jettiness-dependent triple-color correlated remainder. We obtain

\Sigma_{\text{tcc}}^{\psi}\to 2a_{s}^{2}\pi\sum\limits_{(kij)}\kappa_{kj}\left\langle\frac{\rho_{ki}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}}\ln\left(\frac{\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{ki}}{\rho_{k\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}}\right)\ln\left(\frac{({\bar{u}}\mu)^{2}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{kj}}{2\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{ki}}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}F^{kij}.

(58)

Next, we discuss triple-color correlated

The second contribution is

It is slightly more challenging to calculate

(62)

9
Correlated emissions of two soft gluons: the strongly ordered limit

We consider the remaining contribution to
S_{2,RR,T^{2}}
.
It reads

S_{2,RR,T^{2},\tau}=-\frac{C_{A}}{2}\sum\limits_{(ij)}{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\;I_{ij,\tau},

(68)

where

I_{ij,\tau}=\frac{g_{s}^{4}}{2}\int[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{m}}][{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{n}}]\;\;\delta\left(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}-E_{\mathfrak{n}}\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}\right)\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}).

(69)

Since the function
\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}
is symmetric under the permutation of
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
, we can introduce the
energy-ordering
E_{\mathfrak{n}}<E_{\mathfrak{m}}
(
E_{\mathfrak{n}}=\omega E_{\mathfrak{m}}
) and use the fact that the scaling of
\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}
with
the common gluon energy “scale”

E_{\mathfrak{m}}
is uniform. We then write

\begin{split}I_{ij,\tau}&=g_{s}^{4}\int[{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{m}}][{\rm d}p_{\mathfrak{n}}]\;\theta(E_{\mathfrak{m}}-E_{\mathfrak{n}})\;\delta\left(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}-E_{\mathfrak{n}}\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}\right)\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\\
&=[\alpha_{s}]^{2}\;\;\int\limits_{0}^{1}\;\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\epsilon}}\left\langle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}E_{\mathfrak{m}}}{E_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1+4\epsilon}}\delta(\tau-E_{\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}})\;\left[\omega^{2}\;\tilde{S}^{gg}_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]_{E_{\mathfrak{m}}\to 1,E_{\mathfrak{n}}\to\omega}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\\
&=\frac{[\alpha_{s}]^{2}}{\tau^{1+4\epsilon}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\;\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\epsilon}}\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}^{4\epsilon}\;\left[\omega^{2}{\tilde{S}}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},\end{split}

(70)

where
\psi_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}=\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}+\omega\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}
. We note that from now on, for simplicity of notation, we will always assume that
in the evaluation of

{\tilde{S}}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})
the
energy of the gluon
\mathfrak{m}
is set to
1
and the energy
of the gluon
\mathfrak{n}
is set to
\omega
.
Finally, we
perform the Laplace transform and find

I_{ij}=\frac{N_{u}}{\epsilon}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\;\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\epsilon}}\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}^{4\epsilon}\;\left[\omega^{2}\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(71)

where

N_{u}=-\frac{[\alpha_{s}]^{2}{\bar{u}}^{4\epsilon}e^{-4\epsilon\gamma_{E}}\Gamma(1-4\epsilon)}{4}.

(72)

The representation of function

I_{ij}
in
Eq. (
71
) is the starting point of our analysis
of the correlated emissions of two gluons.
To compute
I_{ij}
we
identify and subtract various singular limits
of the integrand in Eq. (
71
). We begin with the
\omega\to 0

limit which we will refer to as
the “strongly-ordered” one,
in the sense of the energy ordering.
The strongly-ordered limit of
{\tilde{S}}^{gg}_{ij}
evaluates to

\lim_{\omega\to 0}\;\left[\omega^{2}\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]=\left[\frac{2\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{n}}}+\frac{2\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}-\frac{2\rho_{ij}^{2}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{n}}}\right]=F_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}).

(73)

We then need to compute

\begin{split}I_{ij}^{\rm so}=\frac{N_{u}}{\epsilon}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\;\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\epsilon}}\;\left\langle\left(\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}+\omega\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}\right)^{4\epsilon}\;F_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.\end{split}

(74)

To integrate over
\omega
,
we change the integration variable
\omega\to 1/\omega

and find

I_{ij}^{\rm so}=\frac{N_{u}}{\epsilon}\;\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\;\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\epsilon}}\left\langle\left(\omega\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}+\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}\right)^{4\epsilon}\;F_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(75)

We then rename
\mathfrak{m}\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{n}
and obtain

I_{ij}^{\rm so}=\frac{N_{u}}{\epsilon}\;\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\;\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\epsilon}}\left\langle\left(\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}+\omega\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}\right)^{4\epsilon}\;F_{ij}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.

(76)

Since
F_{ij}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{m})=F_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})
, we finally find

\begin{split}I_{ij}^{\rm so}&=\frac{N_{u}}{2\epsilon}\;\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\;\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{\omega^{1+2\epsilon}}\left\langle\left(\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}+\omega\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}\right)^{4\epsilon}\;F_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\\
&=-\frac{N_{u}\Gamma[1-2\epsilon]^{2}}{2\epsilon^{2}\Gamma[1-4\epsilon]}\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\;\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2\epsilon}\;F_{ij}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\\
&=-\frac{N_{u}\Gamma[1-2\epsilon]^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}\Gamma[1-4\epsilon]}\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\;\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2\epsilon}\left[\frac{2\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{n}}}-\frac{\rho_{ij}^{2}}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{n}}}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}.\end{split}

(77)

The last term in Eq. (
77
) is fully factorized and can be computed in the same way as the uncorrelated contribution.
We only need to understand how to deal with the first term in
Eq. (
77
).

To this end, we note that the angular integral

\left\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2\epsilon}\;\psi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{2\epsilon}\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\;\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\;\rho_{j\mathfrak{n}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}},

(78)

is singular in the three
collinear limits

\mathfrak{m}||i
,
\mathfrak{n}||j
and
\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}
.

10.1
The double-collinear

\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}

N
-jettiness constraint

To proceed, we need to compute the

In principle, this is straightforward, except for the fact that the
\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}

\begin{split}\\

where the three-vector
\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}
is defined through the following equation

(103)

with the additional constraints

\vec{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\vec{\kappa}_{\mathfrak{n}}=0,\;\;\;{\vec{\kappa}_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{2}=1.

(104)

We also need the collinear limit of the phase space in
b
-
and
d
-sectors. The corresponding parametrization
can be found in
Refs.
[
1
,
38
]
.
We obtain

\begin{split}&C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\;[{\rm d}\Omega_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}]\;\theta^{(b)}\;w^{\mathfrak{m}i,\mathfrak{n}i}\cdots\\
&=N_{\epsilon}^{(b,d)}\;w_{\mathfrak{m}||\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathfrak{m}i,\mathfrak{n}i}\;\eta_{i\mathfrak{m}}^{-\epsilon}\;(1-\eta_{i\mathfrak{m}})^{\epsilon}\;\left[{\rm d}\Omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(d-1)}\right]\;\left[\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\frac{{\rm d}x_{4}}{x_{4}^{1+2\epsilon}}\frac{[{\rm d}\Omega_{a}^{(d-3)}]}{[\Omega^{(d-3),a}]}{\rm d}\Lambda\right]\;C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\cdots,\end{split}

(105)

where

N_{\epsilon}^{(b,d)}=\frac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)\Gamma(1+2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)}.

(106)

As the next step, we need to integrate over azimuthal angles
parameterized by
{\rm d}\Omega_{a}^{(d-3)}

and
{\rm d}\Lambda
in Eq. (
105

To facilitate this,
we write

\left(\frac{(n_{j}\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}})}{\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}}}-\frac{(n_{i}\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}})}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}}\right)^{2}=v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\mu}\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\nu},

(107)

where
v^{\mu}=(n_{i}^{\mu}/\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}-n_{j}^{\mu}/\rho_{j\mathfrak{m}})
, and the four-vector
\kappa
satisfies

\kappa_{\mu}t^{\mu}=0,\;\;\;\;\kappa_{\mu}e_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mu}=0,

(108)

where

and

The averaging of

\langle\kappa^{\mu}\kappa^{\nu}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}g_{\perp,d-2}^{\mu\nu}+\epsilon r_{i}^{\mu}r_{i}^{\nu}.

(109)

The derivation of this formula as well as the definition of vector

are given in Appendix F in Ref.

To further simplify this expression, we write the metric tensor of the transverse space as follows

(110)

Since

(111)

we find

(112)

This allows us to write

A straightforward computation gives

(114)

and

We note that the function

\mathfrak{m}||j

Finally, we note that the contributions of sectors

We begin by considering the following integral

(139)

Using the result for the soft limit of the eikonal

(140)

The two terms that need to be integrated in Eq. (
140

are quite different. The second one is easy to compute because
integrations over directions of
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}

For each of the integrals we use

(141)

(142)

Integration of the first term

We first use the

over
\mathfrak{n}
, then write the

\mathfrak{m}

\begin{split}\left\langle\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{n}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}&=\frac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\Gamma(-2\epsilon)}\int\frac{{\rm d}z_{2}}{2\pi i}\;\frac{\rho_{ij}^{1+z_{2}}}{2^{1+z_{2}+4\epsilon}}\;\Gamma(-z_{2})\Gamma(1+z_{2})\\

(143)

The integration over

\begin{split}&\left\langle\frac{\rho_{ij}}{\rho_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}\rho_{j\mathfrak{n}}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}=-\frac{6\;\eta_{ij}\;2^{-4\epsilon}\Gamma^{5}(1-\epsilon)}{\epsilon\ \Gamma(1-3\epsilon)\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\;\frac{x^{-1-2\epsilon}{}_{2}F_{1}(-2\epsilon,-\epsilon,-3\epsilon,x)}{x+(1-x)\eta_{ij}},\end{split}

(144)

where
\eta_{ij}=\rho_{ij}/2
.
The resulting integral over
x
is straightforward to compute
using the program
Hyperint

[
50
]
.

Performing the integration, combining the results for the two integrals
that appear in Eq. (
140
) and expanding
in powers of
\epsilon
, we find

\begin{split}J_{ij}^{\rm so}&=[\alpha_{s}]^{2}\;\frac{E_{\rm max}^{-4\epsilon}\;2^{-4\epsilon}}{2\epsilon^{4}}\Bigg[1-2\epsilon\ln(\eta_{ij})+\epsilon^{2}\left(4\text{Li}_{2}(1-\eta_{ij})+2\ln^{2}(\eta_{ij})-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}\right)\\

&-\frac{2}{3}\ln^{3}(\eta_{ij})\ln(1-\eta_{ij})+10\ln^{2}(\eta_{ij})\ln^{2}(1-\eta_{ij})-\frac{10}{3}\pi^{2}\ln(\eta_{ij})\ln(1-\eta_{ij})\\
&+2[\pi^{2}+2\ln(\eta_{ij})\ln(1-\eta_{ij})+\ln^{2}(\eta_{ij})]\text{Li}_{2}(\eta_{ij})+2\text{Li}_{2}^{2}(\eta_{ij})-28\text{Li}_{4}(\eta_{ij})\\

&+4\ln(\eta_{ij})[\text{Li}_{3}(\eta_{ij})+6\text{Li}_{3}(1-\eta_{ij})+5\zeta_{3}]\Big)+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{5})\Bigg].\end{split}

(145)

In addition to standard polylogarithms, also the

\mathfrak{m}

\mathfrak{n}

(150)

The next step is to compute
the double-collinear limit
of the triple-collinear subtraction
term without the
N
-jettiness constraint. The calculation
follows the same steps as what is discussed in the previous
Section where the
N
-jettiness constraint was taken into account.
We find the following representation of the corresponding

\begin{split}&\left\langle C_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}[{\rm d}\Omega_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}]\theta^{(b+d)}C_{i\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\;\omega^{tc}\;{\bar{S}}_{\omega}\;\left[\omega^{2}\tilde{S}_{ij}^{gg}(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}\\

(158)

where we have to set

to one and

to
\omega

when computing

are identical to the ones in the gluon case except that the

eikonal function does not possess a strongly-ordered singular limit. Accounting for this and following what has

14
Numerical implementations and checks

We have implemented the above formulas into a
Fortran

code. The implementation is, in principle, straightforward. To compute terms that require integration over directions of gluons’ momenta, we use the phase space parametrization described in Ref.
[
9
,
10
,
1
]
. A possible choice
of partition functions can be found in
Ref.
[
52
]
.

As we already mentioned, the
N
-jettiness soft function was recently discussed in Ref.
[
37
]

where a plethora of numerical results for various kinematic configurations have been presented. We have checked many of the presented results for various values of the parameter
N
and found excellent agreement.
We present some examples of such a comparison in Appendix
D
.

We note that to obtain the high-precision numbers shown in Appendix
D
, we have used a very large number of sample points which
results in runtimes that can be as large as a few minutes per dipole per kinematic point. However, to obtain the same numbers with a better-than-percent precision, we need a relatively small number of sampling points which results in runtimes of an order of a few seconds per dipole per phase space point. We note that runtimes for individual dipoles are largely
independent
of
N
. Since
(N+2)(N+1)/2
dipoles need to be calculated to determine
the
N
-jettiness soft function for a given phase space point,
{\cal O}(1-2)
minutes will be required to do that in case of three- or four-jet production at a hadron collider, or six-jet production at an

e^{+}e^{-}
collider.

The situation with triple-color correlated terms is quite similar. Since in this case integration over the direction of a single gluon is required,
the integration converges rather fast.
In general, for an
N
-jettiness case,
the evaluation of
(N+2)(N+1)N
independent functions
G_{kij}^{\rm triple}
is required from which a smaller number of independent color-correlated contributions can be constructed.
We need about twenty seconds to compute
all the required
G_{kij}^{\rm triple}
functions for
N=2
and,
therefore, we will need about a minute to compute them for
N=3
and about two minutes for
N=4
.

15
Conclusions

We have described the computation of the
N
-jettiness
soft function through NNLO in QCD. Keeping
N
as the parameter, we demonstrated the cancellation of all
1/\epsilon

poles
analytically

against the soft-function renormalization matrix. Furthermore,
we derived a simple representation for
the finite, jettiness-dependent
remainder valid for an arbitrary number of hard partons
N
. We compared our numerical results for
N=1,2
and
N=3
with the ones recently presented in Ref.
[
37
]
and found excellent agreement. We have also found that the representation of the finite remainder that is derived in this paper leads to fast and rapidly convergent integration which is important if the results for
N
-jettiness soft function are to be used for the
computation of higher-order corrections for multi-jet production at colliders.

From a more general perspective, this study was motivated by a question of how advances in developing NNLO subtraction schemes for generic processes at the LHC can be used to derive representations for the building blocks
of modern slicing methods that rely on choosing observables to
define suitable slicing variables. We have shown that, at least for
N
-jettiness, the benefits of applying subtraction-inspired methods are significant. We note that the application of the subtraction-inspired methods for computing the
N
-jettiness soft function becomes possible if one departs from the (by now) standard approach
[
16
,
18
,
34
]
to such computations where hemisphere soft functions are used as elementary building blocks and, instead, interprets
N
-jettiness as one of the many infrared safe observables that can be studied using available subtraction schemes.

Appendix A
Renormalization of the soft function

A suitable expression for the renormalization of the
N
-jettiness soft function was recently given in Ref.
[
37
]
. It reads

Z=1+Z_{1}+\frac{1}{2}Z_{1}Z_{1}+Z_{2,r},

(184)

where

Z_{1}=a_{s}\sum\limits_{(ij)}{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{2L_{ij}+i\pi\lambda_{ij}}{2\epsilon}\right),

(185)

and

Z_{2,r}=a_{s}^{2}\sum\limits_{(ij)}{\bf T}_{i}\cdot{\bf T}_{j}\left(-\frac{3\beta_{0}}{8\epsilon^{3}}+\frac{\Gamma_{1}-4\beta_{0}(2L_{ij}+i\pi\lambda_{ij})}{16\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{1}(2L_{ij}+i\pi\lambda_{ij})+\gamma^{S}_{1}}{8\epsilon}\right),

(186)

with

L_{ij}=\ln(\bar{u}\sqrt{\eta_{ij}}\mu),

(187)

\Gamma_{1}=\left(\frac{67}{9}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\right)C_{A}-\frac{20}{9}T_{R}n_{f},

(188)

and

\gamma^{S}_{1}=\left(\frac{202}{27}-\frac{11\pi^{2}}{36}-7\zeta_{3}\right)C_{A}+\left(-\frac{56}{27}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{9}\right)T_{R}n_{f}.

(189)

where
\Gamma_{1}
is the cusp anomalous dimension and the non-cusp anomalous dimension
\gamma^{S}_{1}
is known to two-loop order
[
53
]
.
Furthermore,
\lambda_{ij}=1
if both

i
and
j
are incoming or outgoing and

\lambda_{ij}=0
otherwise.

Useful phase space integrals

We define measures of angular integration as

[{\rm d}\Omega^{(d-1)}]=\frac{{\rm d}\Omega^{(d-1)}}{\Omega^{(d-2)}}.

(190)

Then

\frac{{\rm d}\Omega^{(d-1)}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}=\frac{\Omega^{(d-2)}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}\;[{\rm d}\Omega^{(d-1)}],

(191)

where

\frac{\Omega^{(d-2)}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}=\left[\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{8\pi^{2}\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\right].

(192)

[

\left\langle\frac{1}{\rho_{i\mathfrak{m}}^{\alpha}}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}=2^{1-\alpha-2\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\alpha-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(2-\alpha-2\epsilon)},

(193)

where brackets

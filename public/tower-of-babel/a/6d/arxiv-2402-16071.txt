Four-gluon vertex in collinear kinematics
A. C. Aguilar, M. N. Ferreira, J. Papavassiliou, L. R. Santos

Abstract
To date, the four-gluon vertex is the least explored component of the QCD Lagrangian, mainly due to the vast proliferation of Lorentz and color structures required for its description. In this work we present a nonperturbative study of this vertex, based on the one-loop dressed Schwinger-Dyson equation obtained from the 4PI effective action. A vast simplification is brought about by resorting to “collinear” kinematics, where all momenta are parallel to each other, and by appealing to the charge conjugation symmetry in order to eliminate certain color structures. Out of the fifteen form factors that comprise the transversely-projected version of this vertex, two are singled out and studied in detail; the one associated with the classical tensorial structure is moderately suppressed in the infrared regime, while the other diverges logarithmically at the origin. Quite interestingly, both form factors display the property known as “planar degeneracy” at a rather high level of accuracy. With these results we construct an effective charge that quantifies the strength of the four-gluon interaction, and compare it with other vertex-derived charges from the gauge sector of QCD.

VI
Results: Emergence of Planar degeneracy

In this section we solve the SDE of the four-gluon vertex
under certain simplifying assumptions, and
check the extent of validity of the planar degeneracy
at the level of the solutions obtained.

VI.1
Preliminary considerations

For the SDE treatment,
we employ appropriate inputs for the various functions entering in the kernels of Eqs. (
56
) and (
57
). In particular,
for the gluon propagator,
\Delta(r^{2})
,
and the ghost dressing function,
F(r^{2})
,
we use fits given by Eqs. (C11) and (C6) of
Aguilar et al. 2022
, respectively, to the lattice results of
Bogolubsky et al. 2009
;
Aguilar et al. 2021a
.

Regarding the three-gluon vertex,
we retain only its tree-level tensorial structure, and resort to
the planar degeneracy approximation
for the associated form factor.
Specifically,

\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
can be accurately approximated by the compact form

\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(s^{2})\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,,\qquad s^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}+r^{2}+p^{2})\,,

(50)

where
\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\ P_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(q)P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(p)\Gamma_{\!0}^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)
, with
\Gamma_{\!0}
denoting the three-gluon vertex at tree-level
Eichmann et al. 2014
;
Ferreira and Papavassiliou 2023
;
Pinto-Gómez et al. 2023
;
Aguilar et al. 2023a
.
The function
\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(s^{2})
is
the form factor associated with the soft-gluon limit of the three-gluon
vertex,
(q=0,r=-p)
, and has been accurately determined from various lattice simulations
Athenodorou et al. 2016
;
Duarte et al. 2016
;
Boucaud et al. 2017b
;
Pinto-Gómez et al. 2023
;
Aguilar et al. 2020b
;
Aguilar et al. 2021c
;
Aguilar et al. 2021a
. Concretely, we use the fit given by Eq. (C12) of
Aguilar et al. 2022
, to the lattice data of
Aguilar et al. 2023b
;
Ferreira and Papavassiliou 2023
, and convert to the renormalization scheme of Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) using Eq. (
111
).

As for the ghost-gluon vertex,
its Lorentz decomposition reads

{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\mu}(q,r,p)=r_{\mu}B_{1}(q,r,p)+q_{\mu}B_{2}(q,r,p)\,;

(51)

at tree-level,
B_{1}^{0}=1
and
B_{2}^{0}=0
. Only
B_{1}
contributes to the transversely-projected form,
\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\mu}(q,r,p)
, so we have

\displaystyle\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\mu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle:=r^{\mu^{\prime}}P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(q)B_{1}(q,r,p)\,.

(52)

The form employed for

B_{1}(q,r,p)
is taken from the SDE results of
Aguilar et al. 2023b
;
Ferreira and Papavassiliou 2023
(see Fig. 13 of
Ferreira and Papavassiliou 2023
), and has been consistently renormalized using Eq. (
111
).

Finally, for the coupling constant we use the value
\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2})=g^{2}/4\pi=0.23
[see Eq. (
111
)],
which corresponds to the renormalization scheme of Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) and
\mu=4.3
GeV.

It is clear that the arguments of the
G_{i}
appearing in the integrands of the SDE
are comprised by combinations
x_{i}p
and the loop momentum

a fact that
complicates the numerical treatment.
However, a considerable
simplification occurs if (
\it i
) the form factor

is assumed to be dominant, making negligible
all dependence on other form-factors, in particular
G_{2,3}
, and (
\it ii
)
the

is assumed to display “planar degeneracy” at some notable level of accuracy. In such a case, one implements the substitution

\displaystyle\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^{abcd}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})

\displaystyle\approx G_{1}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0\penalty\ \!\!\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^{abcd}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})\,,

(53)

and

\displaystyle G_{1}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})\approx G^{*}_{1}(\bar{s}^{2})\,,

(54)

where, in general,

\displaystyle\bar{s}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+p_{3}^{2}+p_{4}^{2})\,.

(55)

The form of

G^{*}_{1}(\bar{s}^{2})
will be determined by means of an iterative procedure, outlined in the next subsection.
Note that, for a given
G^{*}_{1}(\bar{s}^{2})
,
all four-gluon vertices
in the integrands of
Eq. (
49
) will be
replaced by Eqs. (
53
) and (
54
), and evaluated at the value of

\bar{s}^{2}
corresponding to each vertex. For example, the

\bar{s}^{2}
assigned to the
vertex

\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{admn}_{\mu\sigma\alpha\beta}(p_{1},p_{4},k,-k_{3})
in the diagram
\left(\bar{d}_{3}\right)
in Eq. (

by
\bar{s}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}^{2}+p_{4}^{2}+k^{2}+k_{3}^{2})
, where
k_{3}:=k+p_{1}+p_{4}
.

There are two main consequences stemming
from the implementation of these two approximations.

First, the form factor
G_{2}(x,p)
vanishes identically. Indeed, we find that the diagrams
(\bar{d}_{1,2})
of Eq. (
V
) cannot generate the particular color combination associated with

[cf. Eq. (
33
)], independently of the approximation used for the ghost-gluon and three-gluon vertices. On the other hand, the diagrams
(\bar{d}_{3,4})
can contribute to
G_{2}
, but only if non-tree-level tensor structures of the four-gluon vertices therein are dressed. Since a nonzero
G_{2}
can only arise from the effect of dressing non-tree level tensor structures, or including higher loop diagrams in the SDE, it is reasonable to expect it to be subleading in comparison to
G_{1,3}
. In particular, it is evident that, perturbatively,

can only be nonzero at two loops or higher.

Second, the remaining equations for

G_{1}(x,p)

and

decouple
,
assuming the
schematic form

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!G_{1}(x,p)

\displaystyle=1+\left[\int_{k}\!\!K_{1}+\!\!\int_{k}\!\!G^{*}_{1}K_{2}+\!\!\int_{k}\!\!G^{*2}_{1}K_{3}\right]-\left[\int_{k}\!\!K_{1}+\!\!\int_{k}\!\!G^{*}_{1}K_{2}+\!\!\int_{k}\!\!G^{*2}_{1}K_{3}\right]_{\begin{subarray}{c}\!\!\!p\to\mu\\
x\to x_{0}\end{subarray}}\,,

(56)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!G_{3}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{k}\!\!K_{4}+\!\!\int_{k}\!\!G^{*}_{1}K_{5}+\!\!\int_{k}\!\!G^{*2}_{1}K_{6}\right]\,,

[10^{-4},10^{4}]\penalty\ \text{GeV}^{2}

VIII
Ultraviolet Finiteness of the form factors
G_{1}
and
G_{3}

Even though
the numerical evaluation of the
SDEs presented in the previous section gave rise
to results
that are free of ultraviolet
divergences, it would be clearly
advantageous to devise an analytic
demonstration of this fact.
In this section, we
show that when the SDE undergoes the
renormalization procedure outlined in Sec.
V
,
and under the assumptions discussed in Sec.
VI.1
,
one obtains ultraviolet finite
form factors
G_{1}
and
G_{3}
. In particular,

G_{1}
becomes finite after a single
subtraction, while
G_{3}
turns out
to be
automatically free of ultraviolet
divergences,
exactly as expected.

We begin by writing the
SDEs in Eq. (
49
) in
Euclidean space using hyperspherical
coordinates; then, integrating
over the angle between
k
and
p
,
and setting
y:=k^{2}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle G_{i}(x,p)=\int_{0}^{\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!dy\,f_{i}(y,p^{2},x)\,,\qquad i=1,3\,,

(71)

where
\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{2}
is an ultraviolet cutoff,
and
f_{i}(y,p^{2},x)
denotes the sum of the integrands
of the projected diagrams. The closed expressions for the
f_{i}(x,p)
are rather complicated;
however, for the purposes of renormalization,
only their behavior for asymptotic
y
is
relevant.

At this point we introduce an arbitrary
mass scale
M
, with
p^{2},\mu^{2}\ll M^{2}
,
and split the integrals in
Eq. (
71
) into a finite
and a potentially divergent part,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\!\!G_{i}(x,p)=\int_{0}^{M^{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!dy\,f_{i}(y,p^{2},x)\,+\,\underbrace{\int_{M^{2}}^{\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!dy\,f_{i}(y,p^{2},x)}_{\rm potentially\,\,divergent}\,.

(72)

The type of divergence contained
in the second integral may be
established by performing a Taylor expansion of
f_{i}(y,p^{2},x)
around
p^{2}/M^{2}=0
,

\displaystyle f_{i}(y,p^{2},x)

\displaystyle=f_{i}(y)+p^{2}\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}(y,p^{2},x)}{\partial p^{2}}\right]_{\!p^{2}=0}+\ldots\,,

(73)

where the ellipsis denotes higher derivatives.
Note that, upon setting
p^{2}=0
,
all reference to the
parameter
x:=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

drops out from
f_{i}(y)
.

Evidently, for the renormalization to go through, the only potential divergence must
be isolated in the term
f_{i}(y)
, whereas
the terms with the derivatives
must yield finite integrals when substituted into Eq. (
72
).

To check if this indeed so,
let us first focus on
f_{3}(y)
;
a detailed calculation reveals that
it consists of four contributions,

I_{i}(y)
, one
from each diagram
(\bar{d}_{i}^{s})

[i=1,2,3,4]
, namely

\displaystyle f_{3}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\alpha_{s}}{64\pi}\left[I_{1}(y)-24\,I_{2}(y)+40\,I_{3}(y)-17\,I_{4}(y)\right]\,,

(74)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}(y)=y^{-1}B_{1}^{4}(y,y,\pi)F^{4}(y)\,;\qquad\quad

\displaystyle I_{2}(y)=y^{3}\Delta^{4}(y)\mathit{L}_{{sg}}^{4}(y)\,;

(75)

\displaystyle I_{3}(y)=y^{2}\Delta^{3}(y)\mathit{L}_{{sg}}^{2}(y)G^{*}_{1}(y)\,;\qquad\quad

\displaystyle I_{4}(y)=y\Delta^{2}(y)G^{*2}_{1}(y)\,.

To further evaluate the
I_{i}(y)
,
we assume that, in the limit
y\to\infty
, the functions comprising them
reproduce their one-loop resummed forms
von Smekal et al. 1998
;
Fischer et al. 2002
;
Pennington and Wilson 2011
;
Huber 2020a

i.e.
,

\displaystyle F(y)

\displaystyle\sim\mathit{L}_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{-9/44}(y)\,,

\displaystyle\Delta(y)\sim y^{-1}\mathit{L}_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{-13/22}(y)\,,

\displaystyle B_{1}(y,y,\pi)\sim 1\,,

\displaystyle\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(y)

\displaystyle\sim\mathit{L}_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{17/44}(y)\,,

\displaystyle G^{*}_{1}(y)\sim\mathit{L}_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{2/11}(y)\,,

\displaystyle\mathit{L}_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}(y):=c\,\ln(y/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})\,,

(76)

where
c=1/\ln(\mu^{2}/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})
, and
\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}
is the QCD mass-scale in the MOM scheme.
Note that this assumption
is built into the
fits employed for all quantities
that serve as inputs for the SDE:
indeed, the expressions for
F(y)
,
\Delta(y)
, and
\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(y)

given respectively in Eqs. (C6), (C11) and (C12) of
Aguilar et al. 2022
are designed to
capture the correct one-loop resummed anomalous dimension, while the numerical results in Fig. 13 of
Ferreira and Papavassiliou 2023
satisfy
B_{1}(k,-k,0)\to 1
at large
k
.

The anomalous dimension of
G_{1}
can be obtained by standard renormalization group analysis
Altarelli 1982
from the one-loop form of the renormalization constant
Z_{4}
, given in Eq. (
106
). Specifically, the exponent
d_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}
of the logarithm in
G_{1}
is given by
d_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}=\gamma_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}^{(0)}/\beta_{0}
, where
\beta_{0}=11/(4\pi)
and
\gamma_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}^{(0)}
are the lowest order coefficients of the beta function,
\beta(\alpha_{s})
, and the four-gluon anomalous dimension,
\gamma_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(\alpha_{s})
, respectively,
i.e.
,

\beta(\alpha)=\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial\ln\mu^{2}}=-\beta_{0}\alpha_{s}+\ldots\,,\qquad\gamma_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(\alpha_{s})=\frac{1}{Z_{4}}\frac{\partial Z_{4}}{\partial\ln\mu^{2}}=-\gamma_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}^{(0)}\alpha_{s}+\ldots\,,

(77)

with ellipses denoting higher powers of
\alpha_{s}
. Then, Eq. (
106
) entails
\gamma_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}^{(0)}=1/(2\pi)
and, hence
d_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}=2/11
.

Using Eq. (
VIII
) in Eq. (
75
),
we find that all
I_{i}(y)
are equal,
namely

I_{i}(y)=\frac{1}{c^{9/11}y\ln^{9/11}(y/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})}:=I(y)\,,\qquad\forall i\,.

(78)

Note that the individual contributions
from each diagram diverge, since
the integral

\displaystyle{\cal I}:=\int_{\!M^{2}}^{\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{2}}\!\!dy\,I(y)=\frac{11}{2}c^{-9/11}\left[\ln^{2/11}(\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{2}/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})-\ln^{2/11}(M^{2}/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})\right]\,,

(79)

diverges as
\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle UV}}}^{2}\to\infty
.
However, the substitution of Eq. (
78
) into Eq. (
74
) reveals that

\displaystyle f_{3}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\alpha_{s}}{64\pi}\left[1-24+40-17\right]I(y)=0\,,

(80)

namely that the full asymptotic integrand

f_{3}(y)
vanishes before carrying out any integration.

Turning to the form factor
G_{1}
, note that
only
(\bar{d}_{3}^{s})
and

(\bar{d}_{4}^{s})
contribute to it, yielding

f_{1}(y)=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\left[I_{3}(y)-2I_{4}(y)\right]=-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}I(y)\,,

(81)

which upon integration yields
the divergent contribution of

,
given by the constant

G_{1}^{\rm div}=-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\,{\cal I}\,.

(82)

Clearly, the subtraction prescribed by the
first relation in Eq. (
49
) will cancel
the divergence in Eq. (
82
), giving rise to
a finite
G_{1}
. Specifically,
the exact same steps leading from
Eq. (
71
) to Eq. (
82
) may be repeated
for
f_{1}(y,\mu^{2},x_{0})
, expanding around

\mu^{2}/M^{2}=0
;
x_{0}
denotes
the reference configuration
(0,1,1,-2)

in Eq. (
45
).

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}(y,p^{2},x_{i})}{\partial p^{2}}\right]_{\!p^{2}=0}=\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}\log(y/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})+c_{i}\log^{2}(y/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})}{y^{2}\log^{31/11}(y/\Lambda_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle MOM}}}^{2})}+\ldots\,,

IX
Four-gluon effective charge

Typically, the quantitative
description of the strength of a
given interaction proceeds through
the construction of the corresponding

renormalization group invariant
(RGI) effective charge
Binosi and Papavassiliou 2003
;
Alkofer et al. 2005
;
Kellermann and Fischer 2008
;
Aguilar et al. 2009
;
Aguilar et al. 2010
;
Boucaud et al. 2012
;
Deur et al. 2016
;
Binosi et al. 2017a
;
Cui et al. 2020
;
Deur et al. 2024
.
In the case of the four-gluon
interaction, the
appropriate RGI combination
is obtained with the aid of
the relation

Z_{g}^{-1}=Z_{4}^{-1/2}Z_{A}

[see Eq. (
42
)]. Specifically,
introducing the gluon dressing
function
{\cal Z}(p^{2}):=p^{2}\Delta(p^{2})
,
it is straightforward to establish that
the combination

\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x,p)=\,\alpha_{s}G_{1}(x,p){\cal Z}^{2}(p^{2})=\alpha^{{\scriptscriptstyle R}}_{s}(\mu^{2})G_{1}^{{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(x,p,\mu){\cal Z}^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(p^{2},\mu^{2})\,,

(84)

retains the same form before and after renormalization,
and thus, does not depend on the renormalization point

\mu
, event though the individual components are

\mu
-dependent. Then, the quantity

\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x,p)
will be constructed using inputs renormalized at a given
\mu
, which we choose

\mu=4.3
GeV; evidently, any other choice of
\mu

would be equally good, since the value of

\alpha^{{\scriptscriptstyle R}}_{s}(\mu^{2})
is adjusted to
compensate precisely the
\mu
-dependence
in the other components.

In order to obtain a concrete realization of
a vertex-derived effective charge, one normally specifies a particular kinematic configuration for
the vertex form factor entering in it.
For instance, in the case of the effective charges

\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle cg}}}(p)
and

\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 3g}}}(p)
,
obtained from the ghost-gluon and three-gluon vertices,
respectively
Alkofer et al. 2005
;
Kellermann and Fischer 2008
;
Aguilar et al. 2009
;
Aguilar et al. 2010
;
Boucaud et al. 2012
,

\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle cg}}}(p)=\,\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2})B_{sg}^{2}(p^{2})F^{2}(p^{2}){\cal Z}(p^{2})\,,\qquad\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 3g}}}(p)=\,\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2})\mathit{L}_{{sg}}^{2}(p^{2}){\cal Z}^{3}(p^{2})\,,

(85)

where
B_{sg}(p^{2}):=B_{1}(p,-p,0)
and
\mathit{L}_{{sg}}(p^{2})
are the “soft-gluon” limits of the classical form factors of the ghost-gluon and three-gluon vertices, respectively. Instead,
in order to obtain a more representative
picture for the strength of the
four-gluon interaction,
we evaluate

\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x,p)
using
not one, but all fourteen collinear
configurations listed
in Eq. (
64
). Thus, we generate
a sequence of effective charges, which end up
comprising the blue band shown in
Fig.
11
.

For comparison, in the same figure
we show also the ghost-gluon and three-gluon effective charges of Eq. (
85
). For momenta
p\lessapprox 1.5\penalty\ \text{GeV}
there is a clear hierarchy, which is expressed by the inequality
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 3g}}}(p)<\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x,p)<\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle cg}}}(p)
, independently of the collinear configuration chosen for
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x,p)
. In fact, the same hierarchy was obtained in
Cyrol et al. 2015
;
Huber 2020a
for charges defined by the three- and four-gluon vertices in their totally symmetric configurations, and the soft-ghost kinematics for the ghost-gluon vertex.

Figure 11:
The effective charges
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x,p)
,
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle cg}}}(p)
, and
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 3g}}}(p)
, defined in Eqs. (

) and (
85
), respectively.

In the ultraviolet, the different charges slowly merge into each order. Nevertheless, even at
p=5
GeV, the
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}
,
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 3g}}}
and
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle cg}}}

p\gtrapprox 3.5
GeV, the charge hierarchy is modified

\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x,p)<\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle cg}}}(p)<\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 3g}}}(p)

Perturbatively, one charge can be expanded in powers of any other
Celmaster and Gonsalves 1979
; in particular, taking
x=x_{0}

\alpha{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 3g}}}(p)=\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x_{0},p)+a_{1}\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}^{2}(x_{0},p)+\ldots\,,\qquad\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle cg}}}(p)=\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}(x_{0},p)+b_{1}\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}^{2}(x_{0},p)+\ldots\,,

(86)

where the ellipses denote higher powers of
\alpha_{{\rm{\scriptscriptstyle 4g}}}
. Then, our task reduces to determining the one-loop coefficients

and

Pascual and Tarrach 1984
for

,

, and

given in Eq. (

we find that

\displaystyle a_{1}=\frac{1}{96\pi}\left[161+28\ln(2)\right]\approx 0.60\,,

\displaystyle b_{1}=\frac{1}{96\pi}\left[47+28\ln(2)\right]\approx 0.22\,.

(87)

Since
a_{1}>b_{1}>0
, Eq. (

In fact, it is straightforward to
confirm that the agreement

Conclusions

We have presented a comprehensive
nonperturbative study of the
transversely-projected
four-gluon vertex by means of the SDE obtained within the 4PI effective action formalism.
For the purposes of this work we have restricted our attention to the case of the collinear kinematics,
which simplify considerably the
complexity associated with this vertex. This kinematic choice,
in conjunction with the elimination
of color terms of the type
f^{abx}d^{cdx}

by virtue of the charge conjugation symmetry, reduces the number of
possible form factors down to fifteen. A special subset of three
form factors, including the one
associated with the tree-level
(classical) tensorial structure,
is subsequently singled out and studied in detail.
Our treatment of the SDE
uses as
external inputs results obtained
from lattice QCD and previous
continuous studies.

The most outstanding nonperturbative
feature that emerges from
our analysis is the “planar degeneracy” of the
form factors considered.
Specifically, at a high
level of accuracy, all kinematic
configurations that lie on a
given plane share the same form factors in the infrared. This special property, first observed at the level
of the three-gluon vertex
Eichmann et al. 2014
;
Ferreira and Papavassiliou 2023
;
Pinto-Gómez et al. 2023
;
Aguilar et al. 2023a
,
appears to be shared by the
four-gluon vertex, at least
in the context of the collinear configurations that we have studied.
It would be clearly
important to probe the extent of validity of
the planar degeneracy for
more complicated
configurations of momenta,
such as general soft-gluon, where one momentum vanishes and the other three are completely arbitrary;
calculations in this direction are already in progress.

It would be of course particularly interesting to confront our findings
with lattice QCD, as has been the
case with all other
fundamental vertices of the theory.
Unfortunately, the
preliminary data
available
Catumba 2021
;
Colaço et al. 2024a
;
Colaço et al. 2024b

are not sufficiently conclusive
for confirming any
of the main features
encountered in the present study.
It would be interesting to refine the lattice simulations, in order for a meaningful comparison with the
results of functional methods to
become feasible. In this context, the assumption of planar degeneracy might prove particularly useful,
because it would increase
significantly the available statistics.

Let us finally point out
that in our analysis
we have not considered
dynamical quarks. The
inclusion of quarks may proceed straightforwardly,
by making two basic modifications to the treatment
presented here. First,
in the diagrammatic representation of the SDE
in Fig.
3
,
one has to include
additional diagrams containing quark-loops
Oliveira et al. 2019
;
Kızılersü et al. 2021
. Second, the inputs employed, such as
gluon and ghost propagators, as well as
ghost-gluon and three-gluon vertices,
must be replaced by their “unquenched” counterparts,
see
Ayala et al. 2012
;
Binosi et al. 2017b
;
Aguilar et al. 2020b
,

Ilgenfritz et al. 2007
;
Williams et al. 2016
;
Aguilar et al. 2023c
,
and
Aguilar et al. 2020b
, respectively.
We hope to be able to present a
study of the four-gluon vertex with
2+1
quark flavors
in the near future.

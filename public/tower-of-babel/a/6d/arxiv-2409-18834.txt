Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a computably strongly self-absorbing C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra and show that the following C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras are computably strongly self-absorbing: the Cuntz algebras 𝒪 2 \mathcal{O}_{2} and 𝒪 ∞ \mathcal{O}_{\infty} , the UHF algebra M 𝔫 ​ ( ℂ ) M_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathbb{C}) and the tensor product M 𝔫 ​ ( ℂ ) ⊗ 𝒪 ∞ M_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\infty} , where 𝔫 \mathfrak{n} is a supernatural number of infinite type with computably enumerable support, and the Jiang-Su algebra 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} . In connection with the last example, we show that 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} has a computable presentation. The results above are a special instance of a computable version of the standard approximate intertwining argument due to Elliott.

Presentations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

Let

\{a_{n}\ :\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra).
Elements of the sequence

of the presentation while elements of the form
p(a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{k}})
for

*

\mathbb{N}

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra we obtain an effective enumeration of the rational points of the presentation. If

We say that

and

|\|p\|-q|<2^{-k}

\|p\|

A^{\dagger}
is a computable, left-c.e., or right-c.e. presentation, then by a
code
for
A^{\dagger}
we mean a natural number which codes the finite sequence of strings that describes the algorithm.

An element

is a
computable point
of the presentation
A^{\dagger}
if there is an algorithm which, upon input

q
of
A^{\dagger}

. Once again, one can speak of the code of a computable point of a presentation.

If

-algebras

and

A
to

{\left\lVert\varphi(p)-q\right\rVert}<2^{-k}
; in other words,

2.2.
Universal presentations of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

Operator algebraists might be familiar with a different notion of a presentation of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. In this subsection, we clarify the relationship between these two notions of presentation.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a set of noncommuting indeterminates, which we call

relations
for
\mathcal{G}
we mean a set of relations of the form
\|p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\|\leq a

*

n

are elements of
\mathcal{G}
, and

is a nonnegative real number. We also require that, for every generator

\|x\|\leq M
in
\mathcal{R}

representation
of

is a function

is a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, such that
\|p(j(x_{1}),\ldots,j(x_{n}))\|\leq a
for every relation
\|p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\|\leq a
in
\mathcal{R}

The

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
of

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

along with a representation
\iota:\mathcal{G}\to A
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})
such that, for all other representations
j:\mathcal{G}\to B
of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})

such that
\varphi(\iota(x))=j(x)
for all

. If the universal
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})
exists, then it is unique up isomorphism and will be denoted by
C^{*}\langle\mathcal{G}|\mathcal{R}\rangle

is generated by the image of the generators. If
\mathcal{G}
is a sequence
\bar{x}
, then we may write
C^{*}\langle\bar{x}|\mathcal{R}\rangle
instead of
C^{*}\langle\mathcal{G}|\mathcal{R}\rangle
. Given that we remain in the context of unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras throughout this paper, we implicitly assume that we have a distinguished generator for the unit and include relations stating that it is a self-adjoint idempotent which acts as a multiplicative identity.

If the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

is isomorphic to a universal
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})

as a

of

-algebra might admit many generator-relation presentations. Given a generator-relation presentation

of

A
(in the sense of the previous subsection) with
\bar{x}
as the sequence of special points. Since we always assume that in any generator-presentation we have an indeterminate for the identity element, it follows that the identity is a special point of any universal presentation of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.

Some
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras admit “canonical” generator-relation presentations. For example, the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is most commonly defined as the universal (unital)
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by two contractions
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
subject to the following relations:
s_{1}^{*}s_{1}=s_{2}^{*}s_{2}=1

s_{1}s_{1}^{*}+s_{2}s_{2}^{*}=1

A

A relation

of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

2.3.
Tensor product and inductive limit presentations

Consider two presentations

-)tensor product

\langle m,p\rangle=n

Call a presentation

bounded

Lemma 2.3
.

If
A^{\dagger}
is a bounded presentation of

and
1
is a computable point of

\id_{A}\otimes 1_{B}:A^{\dagger}\to(A\otimes_{\alpha}B)^{\dagger\otimes\#}

Proof.

Given a rational point

and

(which is possible since the presentation is bounded) and find a rational point

of

such that
\|b-1\|<2^{-k}M

\|a\otimes 1-a\otimes b\|<2^{-k}
. It remains to note that

is a rational point of
(A\otimes_{\alpha}B)^{\dagger\otimes\#}
whose code can be computed from codes for
a
and
b
.
∎

Suppose now that
A^{\dagger}
and
B^{\#}
are universal presentations of
A
and
B

of the maximal tensor product

and

Lemma 2.4
.

If

and
B^{\#}
are universal presentations of

We first note that the identity map is computable. To see this, note that any special point of

is either a special point of

(A\otimes_{\max}B)^{\dagger\otimes\#}

∎

The following fact is immediate from the definitions:

Lemma 2.5

Suppose that
A^{\dagger}
and
B^{\#}
are c.e. presentations of

B

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
A^{\dagger}
is a computable presentation of

(A\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))^{u(\dagger\otimes\operatorname{st})}
is computable. Moreover, from

Proof.

First, since

is computable, it is right-c.e., hence c.e. by Fact

It remains to show that

is left-c.e. To see this, we recall that, for any element

is left-c.e. and the entries of

and the algorithm outlined here, this algorithm is uniform in

A^{\dagger}

2.4.
Computable unitaries

We begin this subsection with a result on “almost unitaries” that will be used in the following section.

Fix a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
. Recall that, for each
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, if
a\in A
is an
\epsilon
-almost unitary
, by which we mean that
\|a^{*}a-1\|,\|aa^{*}-1\|<\epsilon
, then
a
is invertible and, letting
a=\omega(a)(a^{*}a)^{1/2}
denote the polar decomposition of
a
, we have that
\omega(a)
is a unitary and
\|a-\omega(a)\|<\epsilon
.

Recall also the Taylor expansion

x^{-1/2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k-1/2}{k}(1-x)^{k},\quad|x|<1.

For each
n\geq 1
, set
s_{n}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k-1/2}{k}(1-x)^{k}
. By Taylor’s theorem, there is a computable function
\delta\mapsto N(\delta):\mathbb{Q}^{>0}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\|x^{-1/2}-s_{N(\delta)}(x)\|<\delta
for all
x\in[1/2,3/2]
. Consequently, if
a
is an
\epsilon
-almost unitary with
\epsilon<1/2
and
\|a\|\leq 1
, then since
\omega(a)=a(a^{*}a)^{-1/2}
, we have that
\|\omega(a)-as_{N(\delta)}(a^{*}a)\|<\delta
.

The upshot of this discussion is the following:

Lemma 2.8
.

There is a computable function such that: for any presentation
A^{\dagger}
of a
\mathrm{C}^{*}

Lemma 2.9

There is a computable map

such that, if

Proof.

Recall that the Schur factorization of a matrix

Consequently, we can effectively find a diagonalization

Note also that one can compute a rational number

3.
Computable approximate intertwining

In this section, we prove the computable analog of the approximate intertwining result of Elliott, following Rørdam’s exposition
[
6
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Two computable
*
-homomorphisms
\varphi,\psi:A^{\dagger}\to B^{\#}
are
computably approximately unitarily equivalent
if there is a computable sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
from
B^{\dagger}
consisting of unitaries such that there is an algorithm for which, given inputs a code for a rational point
a
of
A^{\dagger}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, returns
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|v_{n}\varphi(a)v_{n}^{*}-\psi(a)\|<2^{-m}
.

Remarks 3.2
.

(1)

There are ways of altering the previous definition that can lead to different notions of computably approximately unitarily equivalent morphisms. For example, one might not require the sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be computable. Alternatively, one might not require that
\varphi
and
\psi
be computable maps; however, with the rest of the definition unchanged, note that, if
\varphi
and
\psi
are computably approximately unitarily equivalent, then
\varphi
is computable if and only if
\psi
is computable.

(2)

If
\varphi,\psi:A^{\dagger}\to B^{\#}
are computable maps,
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a computable sequence of unitaries witnessing that
\varphi
and
\psi
are approximately unitarily equivalent, and the presentation
B^{\#}
is computable, then the last part of the definition is automatic.

The following is the main result of this section and is the computable analog of
[
6
, Proposition 2.3.5]
:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be separable, unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and let
\varphi:A\to B
be an injective
*
-homomorphism. Equip
A
and
B
with computable presentations
A^{\dagger}
and
B^{\#}
respectively. Suppose the following conditions hold:

(1)

1
is a computable point of
B^{\#}
.

(2)

\varphi:A^{\dagger}\to B^{\#}
is a computable map.

(3)

There is a sequence of unitaries
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
from
B
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w_{n}\varphi(a)-\varphi(a)w_{n}\|=0\text{ and }\lim_{n\to\infty}d(w_{n}^{*}bw_{n},\varphi(A))=0

for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
.

Then there is a computable isomorphism
\psi:A^{\dagger}\to B^{\#}
that is computably approximately unitarily equivalent to
\varphi
.

Proof.

For ease of exposition, we assume that the
\varphi
-image of every rational point of
A^{\dagger}
is actually a rational point of
B^{\#}
; the general case just involves an extra approximation step in what follows. Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be effective enumerations of the rational points of
A^{\dagger}
and
B^{\#}
respectively.

Begin by effectively choosing small rational numbers
\epsilon_{1}
and
\eta_{1}
(to be determined below) and searching for the first
\epsilon_{1}
-almost unitary rational point
\tilde{v}_{1}
of
B^{\#}
with
\|\tilde{v}_{1}\|\leq 1
and first rational point
a_{1,1}
of
A^{\dagger}
such that

\max(\|\tilde{v}_{1}^{*}b_{1}\tilde{v_{1}}-\varphi(a_{1,1})\|,\|\tilde{v}_{1}\varphi(a_{1})-\varphi(a_{1})\tilde{v}_{1}\|)<\eta_{1}.

That such rational points exist follows from assumption (3) and that such computations can be done effectively follows from (1), (2) and the fact that the presentations are computable. (We will omit such justifications in the sequel.) Set
v_{1}:=\omega(\tilde{v}_{1})
, so
\|v_{1}-\tilde{v}_{1}\|<\epsilon_{1}
. It follows that

\|v_{1}^{*}b_{1}v_{1}-\varphi(a_{1,1})\|<2\epsilon_{1}\|b_{1}\|+\eta_{1}

and

\|v_{1}\varphi(a_{1})-\varphi(a_{1})v_{1}\|<2\epsilon_{1}\|a_{1}\|+\eta_{1}.

Consequently, taking
\epsilon_{1}<1/(2^{3}\max(\|a_{1}\|,\|b_{1}\|))
and
\eta_{1}<2^{-2}
we have that

\max(\|v_{1}^{*}b_{1}v_{1}-\varphi(a_{1,1})\|,\|v_{1}\varphi(a_{1})-\varphi(a_{1})v_{1}\|)<2^{-1}.

Next effectively choose small rational numbers
\epsilon_{2}
and
\eta_{2}
and a sufficiently large integer
k(2)
(again, to be determined below) and search for the first
\epsilon_{2}
-almost unitary rational point
\tilde{v}_{2}
of
B^{\#}
with
\|\tilde{v}_{2}\|\leq 1
and first rational points
a_{1,2},a_{2,2}
of
A^{\dagger}
such that:

•

\max_{j=1,2}\|\tilde{v}_{2}^{*}(\omega_{k(2)}(\tilde{v}_{1})^{*}b_{j}\omega_{k(2)}(\tilde{v}_{1}))\tilde{v}_{2}-\varphi(a_{j,2})\|<\eta_{2}
.

•

\|\tilde{v}_{2}\varphi(a_{1,1})-\varphi(a_{1,1})\tilde{v}_{2}\|<\eta_{2}
.

•

\max_{j=1,2}\|\tilde{v}_{2}\varphi(a_{j})-\varphi(a_{j})\tilde{v}_{2}\|<\eta_{2}
.

Set
v_{2}:=\omega(\tilde{v}_{2})
, so
\|v_{2}-\tilde{v}_{2}\|<\epsilon_{2}
.
For
j=1,2
, we then have:

•

\|v_{2}(v_{1}^{*}b_{j}v_{1})v_{2})-\varphi(a_{j,2})\|<(2\epsilon_{2}+2^{-k(2)+1})\|b_{j}\|+\eta_{2}
.

•

\|v_{2}\varphi(a_{1,1})-\varphi(a_{1,1})v_{2}\|<2\epsilon_{2}\|a_{1,1}\|+\eta_{2}
.

•

\|v_{2}\varphi(a_{j})-\varphi(a_{j})v_{2}\|<2\epsilon_{2}\|a_{j}\|+\eta_{2}
.

One can computably choose
\epsilon_{2}
and
\eta_{2}
sufficiently small and
k(2)
sufficiently large so that all of the bounds in the previous three bullets can be taken to be
2^{-2}
.

Inductively suppose that, for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
and
k=1,\ldots,j
, one has found
\epsilon_{j}
-almost unitary rational points
\tilde{v}_{j}
of
B^{\#}
with
\|\tilde{v}_{j}\|\leq 1
and rational points

of

in the manner above. Once again, effectively choose small rational numbers
\epsilon_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
and a sufficiently large integer

and search for the first
\epsilon_{n}
-almost unitary rational point
\tilde{v}_{n}
of

with
\|\tilde{v}_{n}\|\leq 1
and first rational points

of

with

such that the following quantities are bounded by
\eta_{n}
, for all

and
1\leq k\leq j
:

•

\|\tilde{v}_{n}^{*}(\omega_{k(n)}(\tilde{v}_{n-1})^{*}\cdots\omega_{k(n)}(\tilde{v}_{1}^{*})b_{j}\omega_{k(n)}(\tilde{v}_{1})\cdots\omega_{k(n)}(\tilde{v}_{n-1}))\tilde{v}_{n}-\varphi(a_{j,n})\|

•

\|\tilde{v}_{n}\varphi(a_{k,j})-\varphi(a_{k,j})\tilde{v}_{2}\|

•

\|\tilde{v}_{n}\varphi(a_{j})-\varphi(a_{j})\tilde{v}_{n}\|
.

Set
v_{n}:=\omega(\tilde{v}_{n})
so
\|v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}\|<\epsilon_{n}
. We then have the following inequalities for all
1\leq j\leq n
and all
1\leq k\leq j

•

\|v_{n}^{*}(v_{n-1}^{*}\cdots v_{1}^{*}b_{j}v_{1}\cdots v_{n-1})v_{n}-\varphi(a_{j,n})\|<(2\epsilon_{n}+2^{-k(n)+1})\|b_{j}\|+\eta_{n}
.

•

\|v_{n}\varphi(a_{k,j})-\varphi(a_{k,j})v_{n}\|<2\epsilon_{n}\|a_{k,j}\|+\eta_{n}
.

•

\|v_{n}\varphi(a_{j})-\varphi(a_{j})v_{n}\|<2\epsilon_{n}\|a_{j}\|+\eta_{n}
.

One can computably choose
\epsilon_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
sufficiently small and

sufficiently large so that all of the bounds in the previous three bullets can be taken to be

.

For each

and

, set

. Note that
\|w_{n}(a)-w_{n+1}(a)\|=\|\varphi(a)-v_{n+1}\varphi(a)v_{n+1}^{*}\|<2^{-(n+1)}
for all rational points

of

and consequently for all

. Setting

, the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 2.3.5]
shows that

is an isomorphism. We claim that

is a computable map.

Fix

. Notice that for any

, we have

\|w_{m+1}(a_{j})-\omega_{p}(\tilde{v}_{1})\cdots\omega_{p}(\tilde{v}_{m+1})\varphi(a_{j})\omega_{p}(v_{m+1})^{*}\cdots\omega_{p}(v_{1})^{*}\|<(m+1)2^{-p+1}\|a_{j}\|.

Consequently, taking

, and noting that

, we have that
\omega_{p}(\tilde{v}_{1})\cdots\omega_{p}(\tilde{v}_{n})\varphi(a_{j})\omega_{p}(v_{n})^{*}\cdots\omega_{p}(v_{1})^{*}
is a rational point of

within

of

. It follows that

is a computable map.

It is clear from construction that the sequence

is computable; as shown in the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 2.3.5]
, the sequence also witnesses that
\varphi
and
\psi
are approximately unitarily equivalent. By Remark

(2), the sequence witnesses that
\varphi
and

are computably approximately unitarily equivalent.
∎

with its image in

.

Suppose that
A
and

are separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

and

\mathrm{C}^{*}

5.
A computable presentation of the Jiang-Su algebra

In this section, we show how the original construction of the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
given in
[
5
]
yields a “standard” computable presentation
\mathcal{Z}^{\operatorname{st}}
of
\mathcal{Z}
, whence, by the results of the previous section, shows that
\mathcal{Z}
is also computably strongly self-absorbing.

Given integers
p,q\geq 2
, we let
\mathcal{Z}_{p,q}
denote the functions

such that

and

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of

called a

; when

and

are relatively prime,

is called a

dimension drop algebra.

By
[
5
, Proposition 7.3]
,
\mathcal{Z}_{p,q}
admits a generator-relations presentation consisting of finitely many generators and relations; as usual, we let
\mathcal{Z}_{p,q}^{\operatorname{st}}
denote the associated standard presentation of
\mathcal{Z}_{p,q}
. The exact details of the presentation are not relevant for us; the only thing we will need is the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

Viewing
\mathcal{Z}_{p,q}
as a subalgebra of
C[0,1]\otimes M_{p}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{q}(\mathbb{C})
, the generators of
\mathcal{Z}_{p,q}^{\operatorname{st}}
are computable points of
(C[0,1]\otimes M_{p}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{q}(\mathbb{C}))^{u(\operatorname{st}\otimes\operatorname{st}\otimes\operatorname{st})}
, uniformly in

and

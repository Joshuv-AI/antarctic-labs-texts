Only quadrics have pseudo-caustics – on caustics of Riemannian, pseudo-Euclidean and projective billiards in higher dimensions
Corentin Fierobe

Abstract
. This papers studies properties of caustics of different billiard models in dimension d d at least 3 3 , namely of billiards in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} whose law of reflection is defined by 1) a Riemannian metric projectively equivalent to the Euclidean one; 2) a constant non-degenerate quadratic form ( pseudo-Euclidean billiards ); 3) a smooth field of transverse lines to the boundary defining a law of reflection ( projective billiards ). Case 1) and 2) are particular cases of 3). The paper gives a necessary and sufficient condition so that if such billiards have a caustic then the latter is a quadric. In the case of pseudo-Euclidean billiards, we even show that the only billiards having a caustic are the quadrics, for which the caustics are pseudo-confocal quadrics.



1 Introduction

The study of the light’s behaviour in a homogeneous environment bounded with mirrors is of great interest and has fundamental applications, for example in optical engineering or in fundamental physics,
e.g.
to control laser beams of particles, or to study the existence of regions with no light, but also the opposite phenomenon, the so-called
caustics
, which are spots along curves with an intense concentration of light.

These systems can be modelized by mathematical billiards, as domains in which one can imagine a ray of light evolving by either going straight forward when no obstacles are on its way, or bouncing on such an obstacle according to a certain law of reflection, for example the classical law of optics
angle of incidence = angle of reflection
.

This law of reflection takes various expressions, depending on which physical phenomenon one decides to study. One can for example consider that the mirrors are not perfect anymore, or that the environment is inhomogeneous which would give a
twisted
version of the classical law of reflection. One can also study, instead of the trajectory of a ray of light, the behaviour of an electron submitted to a magnetic field.

The object of interest of this paper are billiard models with a generalized law of reflection, the so-called
projective billiards
. They unify various laws, including the classical one in a Euclidean or Riemannian metric whose geodesics are lines. The former models were introduced and studied by Sergey Tabachnikov
[
30
,
32
]
. They roughly consist of a domain endowed with a smooth field of lines tranverse to the boundary: the latter defines at each point on the boundary a law of reflection passing through this point. We investigate particular fields of transverse line
L
, for which a domain is what is called
L
-symmetric
(defined below, see Definition
16
).

The paper studies these projective billiard models in dimension
d\geq 3
and focuses on the existence and properties of caustics,
i.e. hypersurfaces to which any light trajectory remains tangent after successive reflections
. It questions for which billiards a caustic has to be a quadric, and relates the answer to the class of
L
-symmetric billiards which we mentionned above. More precisely we prove the following

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a strictly convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
, whose boundary is endowed with a smooth field of transve lines
L
. Assume that the corresponding projective billiard has a caustic
\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma
is a quadric if and only if
\partial\Omega
is
L
-symmetric.

Interestingly, projective billiards contain the class of ususal Euclidean billiards, but more generally billiards in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a metric whose geodesics are lines, and also pseudo-Euclidean or Finsler billiards (to see this, one can associate to a domain its field of orthogonal lines for the corresponding notion of "
metric
"). Pseudo-Euclidean billiards are billiard for which the law of reflection is defined by a constant non-degenerate quadratic form. For Finsler billiards, the law is defined by a Finsler metric which does not depend on the fiber. Hence Theorem
1
applies to all of these billiards:

Theorem 2
.

Theorem
1
applies to ususal Euclidean billiards, to billiards in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a metric whose geodesics are lines, to pseudo-Euclidean billiards and to Finsler billiards.

For pseudo-Euclidean billiards, Theorem
1
can be made more precise, since 1) it can be shown that any such billiard is
L
-symmetric and 2) it is known that for two quadrics
Q_{1},Q_{2}
of some pencil of quadrics called pseudo-confocal, one is a caustic of the other one for the pseudo-Euclidean law of reflection. We will explain in more details these arguments (see Section
5
), which allow to prove the

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Omega
be a pseudo-Euclidean billiard in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
whose field of normal line to the boundary is transverse to it. If
\Omega
has a caustic
\Gamma
, then both
\Gamma
and
\Omega
are pseudo-confocal quadrics.

Theorems
1
,
2
and
3
are described more precisely in Section
2
.

The study of caustics, and more generally of invariant curves, in billiards is an intense subsject of research. A famous example is the billiard in the ellipse (resp. ellipsoid) for which it is known
[
31
]
that smaller confocal ellipses (resp. ellipsoids) are caustics. Applying a KAM scheme, Lazutkin
[
28
]
showed that if the boundary of a planar Euclidean billiard is striclty convex and sufficiently smooth, then there exists a large set of caustics accumulating to the boundary. Mather
[
29
]
proved that in a planar Euclidean billiard if the curvature of the boundary vanishes, then the latter has no caustics. Gutkin and Katok
[
18
]
gave effective conditions in such billiards for which there exists caustics-free regions. These results were extended to the
2
-sphere and hyperbolic plane by Florentin, Ostrover and Rosen
[
17
]
. Let us mention the famous Birkhoff-Poritzky’s conjecture:

Conjecture (Birkhoff-Poritzky)
.
If a convex domain contains an open set of invariant curves then it is an ellipse.

The conjecture is still open, although some partial positive answers have been given, see for example
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
25
,
27
]
.

For a billiard in dimension at least
3
, having a caustic is a more rigid property. Berger
[
4
]
proved that if such a Euclidean billiard has a caustic, then both are confocal quadrics. This result was later extended by Glutsyuk to billiards in spaces of constant curvature
[
24
]
. Billiards in pseudo-Euclidean spaces and Minkowski metrics have been intensively studied by Dragović and Radnović
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. More generally, projective billiards have been studied in any dimensions
[
16
,
13
,
15
,
14
,
22
,
23
,
32
,
30
]
. The results which can be found here extend previously known results
[

]
found by the author.

2 Riemannian, pseudo-Euclidean and projective billiards

A
classical billiard
on a manifold
M
of dimension
d\geq 2
given with a complete Riemannian metric
g
is a domain
\Omega\subset M
with piecewise smooth boundary. The reflection law at a smooth point
p
of the boundary is defined as follows, where
\gamma
is an oriented geodesic inside
\Omega
hitting the boundary at
p
with velocity
v\in T_{p}M
:

Definition 4

(Riemannian billiard law of reflection)
.

We define the
reflected geodesic
from
\gamma
at
p
as the oriented geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}
passing through
p
with velocity
\tilde{v}\in T_{p}M
such that
\tilde{v}
is obtained from
v
by the linear orthogonal symmetry of
T_{p}M
with respect to
T_{p}\Omega
- that is the only linear non-trivial involution which preserves the hyperplane
T_{p}\partial\Omega
and its
g
-orthogonal line.

This defines a
billiard flow

\varphi^{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
: given any point
p\in\Omega
and a non-zero vector
v\in T_{p}M
, the billiard flow
\varphi^{t}(p,v)
is obtain by following the geodesic with initial conditions
(p,v)
until it eventually reflects on the boundary, and continue its trajectory, etc. It might eventually be not defined for all
t
.

Example 5

(
d
-dimensional sphere)
.

Consider a domain
\Omega
on the
d
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
equipped with the classical spherical metric
g
: the billiard flow inside
\Omega
follow a trajectory along broken pieces of great circles -
ie
intersection of hyperplanes of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Note that the canocical projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}P^{d}
restricts as diffeomorphism between a halph-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{+}
and a standard affine chart
\mathbb{R}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}P^{d}
which sends geodesics of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
to lines of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. However the pushforward
\pi_{\ast}g
of
g
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is not the Euclidean metric, and for any hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, its
\pi_{\ast}g
-orthogonal line differs from its Euclidean orthogonal line.

Example 6

(Metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
projectively equivalent to the Euclidean one)
.

A metric
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be projectively equivalent to the Euclidean metric if the geodesics of
g
are supported by straight lines. For example, the metric
\pi_{\ast}g
obtained in Example
5
is projectively equivalent to the Euclidean metric. We can therefore define a billiard flow inside an domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for the metric
g
which follows broken lines. Note that in this case also, the
g
-orthogonal line of an hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
may differ from its Euclidean orthogonal.

In this paper we study the property of so-called caustics of a billiard. They can be defined as follows:

Definition 7
.

Let
\Omega
be a billiard in a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. A
caustic
is a smooth hypersurface
\Gamma\subset\Omega
such that if the billiard flow in
\Omega
is once tangent to
\Gamma
, then it remains tangent to it after successive reflections (as long as it is defined).

We will define two other types of billiards, namely the
pseudo-Euclidean
and
projective billiards
for which this notion is relevant, and will state each of our result for this different models.

In the following we consider the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
endowed with its Euclidean metric
\left\langle\,\cdot\left|\,\cdot\right.\right\rangle
. Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth hypersurface. We fix
n:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a field of Euclidean unit normal vectors to
S
.

Riemannian billiards

To state our first result, we need another definition. Let
\nu:S\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the field of
g
-orthogonal vectors to
S
such that
\left\langle\,\nu\left|\,n\right.\right\rangle\equiv 1
.

Definition 8
.

We say that
S
is
g
-symmetric
at
p\in S
if for any
u,v\in T_{p}S
, we have

(1)

\left\langle\,dn_{p}(u)\left|\,d\nu_{p}(v)\right.\right\rangle=\left\langle\,dn_{p}(v)\left|\,d\nu_{p}(u)\right.\right\rangle.

We simply say that
S
is
g
-symmetric
if the latter holds for all
p\in S
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
g
be a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose geodesics are supported by lines. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth domain such that
\Omega
has a caustic
\Gamma
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\Gamma
is a quadric;

(ii)

\partial\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
g
-symmetric.

In fact Theorem
9
has a analogous versions for so-called pseudo-Euclidean and projective billiards which we are going to define.

Pseudo-Euclidean billiards

Let
Q
be a non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain with piecewise smooth boundary. We define the pseudo-Euclidean reflection of oriented lines with respect to
Q
at point
p\in\partial\Omega
as in Definition
4
, where

(i)
the geodesics are replaced by oriented lines;

(ii)
the notion of
g
-orthogonality is replaced by the notion of
Q
orthogonality: since
Q
is non-degenerate, the
Q
-orthogonal line
L_{p}
to
T_{p}\partial\Omega
in
T_{p}\mathbb{R}^{d}
is well-defined. Hence if
L_{p}\nsubseteq T_{p}\Omega
, there is a unique non-trivial linear involution of
T_{p}\mathbb{R}^{d}
preserving
T_{p}\Omega
and
L_{p}
, and the latter defines the pseudo-Euclidean reflection at
p
with respect to
T_{p}\Omega
as in Definition
4
. When
L_{p}\subset T_{p}\Omega
, the reflection is not defined.

Theorem 10
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
Q
be a non-degenerate quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain whose boundary is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth space-time hypersurface. Then
\Omega
has a caustic
\Gamma
if and only if
\Omega
and
\Gamma
are quadrics, which are pseudo-confocal.

Remark 11
.

The notion of pseudo-confocal quadrics extends the usual notion of confocal quadrics as a specific pencil of quadrics depending on the quadratic form associated to the space (see
[
16
]
for more details). For example if the space is endowed with the quadratic form

dx_{1}^{2}+\ldots+dx_{r}^{2}-dx_{r+1}^{2}-\ldots-dx_{d}^{2}

then the family of quadrics depending on a parameter
\lambda
and defined by the equations

\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}:\quad\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{a_{1}-\lambda}+\ldots+\frac{x_{r}^{2}}{a_{r}-\lambda}+\frac{x_{r+1}^{2}}{a_{r+1}+\lambda}+\ldots+\frac{x_{d}^{2}}{a_{d}+\lambda}=1

where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
is a pseudo-confocal pencil of quadrics.

Projective billiards

The two previous billiard models lead to a more general definition of billiard, called
projective billiards
, introduced and studied by Tabachnikov
[
30
,
32
]
. Note that given an oriented line
\ell
passing through a point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be seen as a geodesic, hence one can associates to it its unit velocity vector
v\in T_{p}\mathbb{R}^{d}

Definition 12

(Projective billiards and projective law of reflection)
.

A
projective billiard
is a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with piecewise smooth boundary, equipped with a field of transverse line
L
: that is for any smooth point
p
of
\partial\Omega
,
L_{p}
defines a line passing through
p
and transverse to
\partial\Omega
at
p
. We denote it by the pair
(\Omega,L)
.

Let
\ell
be an oriented line
\ell
hitting the boundary transversally at a point
p
with unit velocity
v\in T_{p}\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The line
\ell
is said to be
reflected into the oriented line
\ell^{\prime}
by the projective law of reflection at
p
if

(i)

\ell^{\prime}
contains
p
;

(ii)
Let
w
be the unit velocity of
L_{p}
. The unit velocity vectors
v^{\prime}
of
\ell^{\prime}
is obtained as the image of the unit velocity vector
v
of
\ell
by the only non-trivial linear involution of
T_{p}\mathbb{R}^{d}
which preserves
T_{p}\partial\Omega
and
\mathbb{R}w
.

This defines, as in the case of the usual billiards, a
projective billiard flow
in
\Omega
.

Remark 13
.

The projective law of reflection
12
is equivalent to the following items:

(i)
the lines
\ell
,
\ell^{\prime}
and
L_{p}
are contained in a
2
-dimensional plane
\mathcal{P}
transverse to
T_{p}{\Omega}
;

(ii)
if
T
is the line
T_{p}\cap\mathcal{P}
, then the quadruple of lines
(\ell,\ell^{\prime};T,L_{p})
are in involution (meaning that it is a harmonic quadruple of points in the space of lines contained in
\mathcal{P}
and passing through the point
p
, identified with
\mathbb{R}P^{1}
: their cross-ratio in this order is
-1
).

Remark 14
.

Let
g
be a metric projectively equivalent to the Euclidean one,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a domain with piecewise smooth boundary, and
L_{p}
the
g
orthogonal line to
T_{p}\partial\Omega
passing through

. Then by definition the billiard flow for

. The same statement hold for pseudo-Euclidean billiards with space-time boundary.

Let a smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with

, equipped with a smooth field of transverse lines to the boundary

. We assume that the corresponding porjective billiard

has a caustic

We observe

that we can study the problem locally by considering an open subset

of the boundary and two open subsets

of the caustic such that there is at least an oriented line tangent to

and reflected on

into an oriented line tangent to

. Note that here we consider the same projective law of reflection induced by the restriction of

to

. Denote this structure by

and call it

. This leads to the

Definition 15
.

Let

be embedded hypersurfaces of

and

be a field of transverse lines to

. We say that the pair

if the set of oriented lines tangent to

, intersecting

is a non-empty open subset of the set of lines tangent to

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a hypersurface endowed with a smooth field of transverse line
L
. As in the Riemannian case, we define
\nu:S\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a field of vectors such that
\left\langle\,\nu\left|\,n\right.\right\rangle\equiv 1
and
\nu_{p}
as the same direction as

.

.

We say that
S
is
L
-symmetric
at

, if for any

, we have

(2)

\left\langle\,dn_{p}(u)\left|\,d\nu_{p}(v)\right.\right\rangle=\left\langle\,dn_{p}(v)\left|\,d\nu_{p}(u)\right.\right\rangle.

Theorem 17
.

Let

,

Comments on the previous results

As explained, billiards in Riemannian metric projectively equivalent to the Euclidean one and pseudo-Euclidean billiards are particular cases of projective billiards: given
\Omega
, one of the two former billiards, the corresponding field of normal lines - either for the Riemannian metric or for the pseudo-Euclidean structure - defines a ŝmooth field of lines
L_{p}
on
\partial\Omega
. Moreover it is transverse to
\partial\Omega
in the Riemannian case, and also in the pseudo-Euclidean case when
\partial\Omega
is space-time.

Therefore, Theorem
9
and
10
in these two cases will be corollaries of Theorem
17
in the projective case: Theorem
9
is just the translation of Theorem
17
in a Riemannian metric, and Theorem
10
will use the additional result that caustics of pseudo-Euclidean billiards in quadrics are well understood.

I strongly believe that Theorem
17
applies to Minkowski billiards: the latter can be seen as projective billiard wich are symmetric in the sense of Definition
16
. The result is in preparation. For more details about Minkowski billiards and billiards in Finsler metrics see
[
2
,
19
]
.

To go beyond the results presented here, I am thinking about two interesting and relevant question. The first is to ask
whether a projective billiard which is not
L
symmetric can have a caustic
. The negative answer to this question would allow to conclude that if a projective billiard has a caustic, then the latter is a quadric. The positive answer would provide interesting examples of billiards with a caustic.

For the second question, note that, in the plane, given a convex domain
\Omega^{\prime}
nested in another one
\Omega
, one can endow
\Omega
with a field of transverse lines such that
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
is a caustic for the induced projective billiard in
\Omega
. This is true in particular in the case when
\Omega^{\prime}
is bounded by a conic. Hence if a projective billiard has a conic as a caustic, then itself can be something else than a conic. This construction is however more delicate in dimension greater than
2
. However, following Definition
16
and Theorem
17
, a natural question arises:
if a projective billiard is
L
-symmetric and has a quadric as a caustic, is it a quadric itself?
Answering this question is probably difficult, and the study of billiards in quadrics for different billiard structures might help, as it does for pseudo-Euclidean billiards.

3 Admissible hyperplanes

Having a caustic
\Gamma
for a projective billiard
(\Omega,L)
is a very strong property, which has important geometric consequences on the cones of lines tangent to
\Gamma
and intersecting
\partial\Omega
at a same point
q
: they are integrable surfaces of a certain distribution of hyperplanes of
T_{p}\partial\Omega
(see
[
4
]
for the case of Euclidean billiards). This observation still holds for projective billiards, as we will show in Proposition
20
.

We consider the following
important
situation. Let a line framed-hypersurface
(S,L)
having a piece of caustic
(U,V)
. Fix a triple of points
(p,q,r)\in U\times S\times V
such that
p
is the tangency point of an oriented line reflected on
(S,L)
at the point
q
into an oriented line tangent to
V
at
r
.
The hyperplanes
T_{p}U
and
T_{r}V
intersects
T_{q}S
into a hyperplane
H
of
T_{q}S
(a linear subspace of codimension
1
).

Hence we can do the following trivial remark: any smooth path
(p(t),q(t),r(t))\in U\times S\times V
passing through
(p,q,r)
at
0
is such that
p^{\prime}(0)
belongs to the hyperplane
T_{p}U
, generated by
H
and the direction of the line
pq
, and an analogous statement for
r^{\prime}(0)
. This remark is of high importance when we don’t assume the existence of a piece of caustic. This leads to the following

Definition 18
.

Let
(S,L)
be a line-framed hypersurface,
\ell
an oriented line intersecting
S
transversally at a point

and
H
a hyperplane of
T_{q}S
. We say that

is
\ell
-
admissible
if the orthogonal projection of
\ell
to
T_{q}S
is transverse to

, and for any smooth path
\ell(t)
passing through
\ell
at
0
and intersecting
S
transversally at

, there exists a curve
(p(t),q(t))
satisfying the following:

(i)

p(0)\in\ell
and the points

are not colinear;

(ii)

\ell(t)
is reflected into the oriented line from

to

by the projective law of reflection on
(S,L)
;

(iii)

p^{\prime}(0)
belongs to the hyperplane containing the line
\ell
and
H
;

(iv)

q^{\prime}(0)
belongs to the hyperplane containing the line
q(0)r(0)
and

.

As discussed, in the situation when
(S,L)
admits a piece of caustic
(U,V)
, we have the

Proposition 19
.

Suppose that
(S,L)
admits a piece of caustic
(U,V)
. Then the intersection

of the tangent space

with
T_{p}U
or with

, such that the line

, is

The main result of this section is described by the next proposition. We say that a line
\ell
intersecting
S
at
q
is
above
a vector
\xi\in T_{q}S
if it projects orthogonally to
T_{q}S
in a line containing
\xi
.

Proposition 20
.

Suppose
S
has a non-degenerate second fundamental form at
q
. Then for all line
\ell

5 Only quadrics have pseudo-caustics

This Section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

Consider

containing

Lemma 28
.

The hypersurface
S
is

-symmetric.

Proof.

Let

be a field of unit normal vector to

The reader can check that

-\lambda(q)^{2}\left\langle\,Mn(q)\left|\,dn_{q}(x)\right.\right\rangle\left\langle\,Mn(q)\left|\,dn_{q}(y)\right.\right\rangle,

from which we deduce

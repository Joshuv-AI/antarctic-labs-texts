Tackling the infamous g 6 g^{6} term of the QCD pressure
*

Abstract
We report on ongoing efforts to tackle an important open problem in QCD thermodynamics, namely an evaluation of the pressure to order g 6 g^{6} in a weak-coupling expansion, corresponding to four loops. In particular, we identify a class of contributing Feynman sum-integrals with lower-loop factors, describe the formalism to tensor decompose those, and manage to map them onto scalar master sum-integrals that have already been evaluated in the literature.

1
Introduction and Motivation

QCD thermodynamics has been a niche, but recurrent theme at this (mainly zero-temperature) conference series,
and over the years we have witnessed useful interactions that go both ways.
For example, explorations of the number-theoretic content of perturbative expansions in quantum field theory
such as reported in
[
1
]
have allowed to simplify fundamental constants arising in contributions to hot QCD
from 3d lattice perturbation theory
[
2
]
;
while algorithmic methods that had been implemented in the finite-temperature (
T
) setting
[
3
]

have subsequently been lifted to and applied in the
T=0
world of particle phenomenology
[
4
,
5
]
.

In weak-coupling expansions within finite-temperature field theory
one has to deal with the so-called Linde problem
[
6
]
, which arises from the non-perturbative nature of gluon confinement:
while strong interactions in the thermal plasma lead to an exponential screening of the color-electric part of massless
gauge fields at large distances (in full analogy to Debye screening in an electromagnetic plasma),
their color-magnetic part remains unscreened in perturbation theory.
In naive loop expansions this causes an infrared problem, which can be evaded within effective
theories, as has been worked out for QCD
[
7
,
8
]
as well as the Standard Model
[
9
]
.

Interestingly, for one of the most important thermodynamic quantities such as the QCD pressure (the negative free energy;
with phenomenological applications ranging from heavy-ion collisions via astrophysics to early-universe cosmology),
the potentially large effects of the long-range gluonic sector enter the weak-coupling expansion for the first time at four loops.
The program of how to employ a hierarchy of effective theories to consistently account for all contributions at this order,
as sketched in
[
10
]
,
has seen major efforts towards completion, see e.g.
[
11
]
and references therein.
These efforts have reduced the problem to a single – difficult but well-defined – perturbative computation within
thermal field theory, which consists in evaluating the hard-scale four-loop contributions within a naive loop expansion
of the pressure.

So far, results have been obtained only for scalar theory
[
12
]

and in the large-
N_{{\mbox{\scriptsize f}}}
limit of QCD
[
13
]
.
Here, we report some technical details necessary for attacking the core of the remaining open problem,
mainly considering pure Yang-Mills theory.

A compact and elegant method to generate all diagrams needed for the perturbative expansion of the pressure

While the method is generic, for the sake of illustration we will here keep fully gluonic contributions only,
omitting all diagrams that contain ghost (and fermion) lines.

4
Decomposition of tensor structure

As one marked difference with zero-temperature computations, we have 3d rotational symmetry only,
such that in tensor decompositions one has to account for an extra four-vector
U=(1,\vec{0})
.
For example, the integral of Eq. (
7
) has a 1-loop factor (line 3) in its propagator structure,
such that all numerator structures that contain the loop momentum
P_{3}
should be decoupled before
integrating the remaining 3-loop factor.
The price to pay is to introduce zero-components
U\!\cdot\!P=P_{0}
of loop momenta
P
into the numerator, thereby generalizing
the class of scalar sum-integrals to contain such numerators. This can easily be taken into account by adding
L
more indices to
the list representation of an
L
-loop integral.

Consider an
L
-loop tensor tadpole with denominator
{\cal D}
and (scalar part of) numerator
{\cal N}

\displaystyle T_{\cal DN}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P_{1}}$}}\dots{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P_{L}}$}}\frac{{\cal N}(P_{1},\dots,P_{L},U)}{{\cal D}(P_{1},\dots,P_{L})}\,\big[P_{i_{1}}^{\mu_{1}}\dots P_{i_{n}}^{\mu_{n}}\big]_{\mbox{\footnotesize symm in $\mu_{i}$}}\;.

(8)

We will deal with numerators that are symmetric in the
P^{\mu}
as indicated, so
the result has to be fully symmetric in the Lorentz indices
\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}
, and can only depend on
U^{\mu}
and
g^{\mu\nu}
.
We use an orthogonal tensor basis (trading
g^{\mu\nu}
for
G^{\mu\nu}=U^{\mu}U^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}
with
U^{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}=0
)

\displaystyle A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\cdots G^{\mu_{2k-1}\mu_{2k}}\,U^{\mu_{2k+1}}\cdots U^{\mu_{n}}\;\big|_{\mbox{\footnotesize symm in $\mu$}}

(9)

where
A_{nk}A_{nj}\sim\delta_{kj}
(see Eqs. (
10
) and (
13
) below).
There are
{n\choose 2k}=\frac{n!}{(2k)!\,(n-2k)!}
partitions of the
\mu
into those two groups,
and
\frac{(2k)!}{2^{k}\,k!}
terms in each product
G\cdots G
,
such that
A_{nk}
has
c_{nk}=\frac{n!}{2^{k}\,k!\,(n-2k)!}
terms.
Some examples are (abbreviating Lorentz indices as in
U^{12}\equiv U^{\mu_{1}}U^{\mu_{2}}
etc.)

\displaystyle n=0

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A_{00}=1\;;

\displaystyle n=1

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A_{10}=U^{1}\;;

\displaystyle n=2

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A_{20}=U^{12}\;,\;\;A_{21}=G^{12}=U^{12}-g^{12}\;;

\displaystyle n=3

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A_{30}=U^{123}\;,\;\;A_{31}=U^{1}G^{23}+U^{2}G^{31}+U^{3}G^{12}=3U^{123}-U^{1}g^{23}-U^{2}g^{31}-U^{3}g^{12}\;.

Contractions of the tensor basis elements of Eq. (
9
) can be computed for the general-
n
case as

\displaystyle{\rm Tr\,}(A_{nk}A_{nj})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}A_{nj}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\big]\;=\;\delta_{kj}\,p_{nk}

(10)

\displaystyle p_{nk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\big]\;=\;{n\choose 2k}\,p_{k}

(11)

\displaystyle p_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[A_{2k,k}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}A_{2k,k}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\big]\;;\quad p_{0}=1\;,\quad p_{k}=(2k-1)(D-3+2k)p_{k-1}

(12)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow p_{nk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{nk}\,\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(D-1+2j)\;,

(13)

whence all traces can be seen to reduce to simple polynomials in
D=g^{\mu\mu}=4-2\varepsilon
.

In the tensor basis of Eq. (
9
) any symmetric tensor tadpole of Eq. (
8
) now decomposes as

\displaystyle T_{\cal DN}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}t_{nk}\,A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\;.

(14)

To project out the coefficients
t_{nk}
, we contract with all basis tensors (
j=0..\lfloor n/2\rfloor
) as

\displaystyle T_{\cal DN}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\,A_{nj}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P_{1}}$}}\dots{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P_{L}}$}}\frac{{\cal N}(P_{1},\dots,P_{L},U)}{{\cal D}(P_{1},\dots,P_{L})}\,\big[P_{i_{1}}^{\mu_{1}}\dots P_{i_{n}}^{\mu_{n}}\,A_{nj}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\big]

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}t_{nk}\,\big[A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\,A_{nj}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\big]\;=\;t_{nj}\,p_{nj}\;,

where in the last step we have profited from using an orthogonal basis, such that altogether

\displaystyle T_{\cal DN}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}}{p_{nk}}\;{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P_{1}}$}}\dots{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P_{L}}$}}\frac{{\cal N}(P_{1},\dots,P_{L},U)}{{\cal D}(P_{1},\dots,P_{L})}\,\big[P_{i_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}\dots P_{i_{n}}^{\nu_{n}}\,A_{nk}^{\nu_{1}\dots\nu_{n}}\big]\;.

(16)

4.1
Special case: 1-loop tensor tadpoles

As a special case, let us apply Eq. (
16
) to 1-loop tensor tadpoles, i.e.
L=1
.
Then the basis tensors are contracted fully symmetrically as

\displaystyle\big[P^{\nu_{1}}\dots P^{\nu_{n}}\,A_{nk}^{\nu_{1}\dots\nu_{n}}\big]=c_{nk}\,\big[UP\big]^{n-2k}\,\big[PGP\big]^{k}=c_{nk}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k\choose j}(-1)^{j}\big[UP\big]^{n-2j}[P^{2}]^{j}\;,

(17)

such that the most general 1-loop tensor tadpole maps onto scalar sum-integrals as

\displaystyle T^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}_{st}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P}$}}\frac{(UP)^{t}}{[P^{2}]^{s}}\,P^{\mu_{1}}\cdots P^{\mu_{n}}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tiny(\ref{eq:dec})}}{{=}}\;\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}}{p_{nk}}\;{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P}$}}\frac{(UP)^{t}}{[P^{2}]^{s}}\,\big[P^{\nu_{1}}\dots P^{\nu_{n}}\,A_{nk}^{\nu_{1}\dots\nu_{n}}\big]

(18)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tiny(\ref{eq:contract})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}}{p_{nk}}\;c_{nk}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k\choose j}(-1)^{j}{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P}$}}\frac{(UP)^{t+n-2j}}{[P^{2}]^{s-j}}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}}{p_{nk}}\,c_{nk}\;\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k\choose j}(-1)^{j}\,\frac{I_{s-j}^{t+n-2j}}{I_{s}^{t+n}}\,I_{s}^{t+n}\,\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}}{p_{nk}}\,c_{nk}\;f_{ks}\,I_{s}^{t+n}.\quad\mbox{}

(20)

In the last step we have used the known result for the scalar one-loop tadpole (here
d=3-2\varepsilon
)

\displaystyle I_{s}^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hbox{$\sum$}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\,}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\raise-0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{P}$}}\frac{|P_{0}|^{a}}{[P^{2}]^{s}}\;=\;\frac{2T\,\zeta(2s-a-d)}{(2\pi T)^{2s-a-d}}\,\frac{\Gamma(s-d/2)}{(4\pi)^{d/2}\,\Gamma(s)}\;,

(21)

which allowed to perform the sum over
j
as

\displaystyle i_{sj}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{I_{s-j}^{t+n-2j}}{I_{s}^{t+n}}\;=\;\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s-j)}\,\frac{\Gamma(s-d/2-j)}{\Gamma(s-d/2)}\;=\;\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s-j)}\,\frac{\Gamma(s+1/2-D/2-j)}{\Gamma(s+1/2-D/2)}\;,

(22)

\displaystyle f_{ks}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k\choose j}(-1)^{j}\,i_{sj}\;=\;\frac{\Gamma((D-1)/2+k)\,\Gamma((D+1)/2-s)}{\Gamma((D-1)/2)\,\Gamma((D+1)/2+k-s)}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{D-1+2j}{D+1-2s+2j}\;.\quad\mbox{}

(23)

Using Eq. (
13
), we observe substantial cancellations and finally obtain

\displaystyle T^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}_{st}=I_{s}^{t+n}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}A_{nk}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\,\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{D+1-2s+2j}\;.

(24)

Particular cases of Eq. (
24
) are

\displaystyle T_{st}=I_{s}^{t}\;,\quad T_{st}^{\mu}=I_{s}^{t+1}\,U^{\mu}\;,\quad T_{st}^{\mu\nu}=I_{s}^{t+2}\,\Big\{U^{\mu}U^{\nu}+\frac{U^{\mu}U^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}}{D+1-2s}\Big\}\;,\quad\mbox{etc.}

(25)

Eq. (
24
) can now be employed to decouple some sectors into [1-loop]
\times
[3-loop], with scalar factors.
As mentioned above, owing to the appearance of
U^{\mu}
in the tensor basis
A_{nk}
, this introduces factors of
P_{0}
into the numerators,
even if initially one starts from
P_{0}
-free sum-integrals.

6
Conclusions and outlook

We have outlined techniques that allow to confront the evaluation of a specific missing four-loop contribution
to the pressure of a hot Yang-Mills gas.
It derives its relevance from the fact that only at this order, potentially large effects from infrared logarithms that
arise from unscreened color-magnetic gluonic modes can be accounted for in a systematic way.

Due to its technical difficulty, the problem has stood open for almost two decades now,
and we have presented first steps towards completing the task. In particular, we have demonstrated the
necessary tensor decomposition methods, which allow for evaluations of a class of factorizable 4-loop sum-integrals
that contribute to the pressure.

As an open problem, the remaining genuine 4-loop sum-integrals have to be evaluated up to the constant term.
One of those can be extracted from
[
12
]
, where it had contributed to the pressure of scalar theory.
Furthermore, one can explore linear IBP relations to search for simplifications.
However, since such relations act only on the 3d part of the sum-integrals required here, they are much less powerful than at zero temperature.

Concerning the feasibility of the remaining evaluations of 4-loop sum-integrals, let us note that a sizable number of them
contain one-loop self-energies, for which one should be able to generalize systematic subtraction methods that have been developed
on the 3-loop level, see
[
19
,
21
]
.
On the other hand, evaluating sum-integrals from sectors 511, 1022 and 1011 (cf. Fig.
1
) might represent a challenge that requires
the development of entirely new methods.

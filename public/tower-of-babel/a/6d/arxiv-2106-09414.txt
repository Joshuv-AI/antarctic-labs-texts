Saeid Azam, Amir Farahmand Parsa, Mehdi Izadi Farhadi

Abstract
. We construct certain integral structures for the cores of reduced tame extended affine Lie algebras of rank at least 2. One of the main tools to achieve this is a generalization of Chevalley automorphisms in the context of extended affine Lie algebras. As an application, groups of extended affine Lie type associated to the adjoint representation are defined over arbitrary fields.

17B67, 17B65, 19C99, 20G44, 22E65

1.
Introduction

K. Saito introduced
extended affine root systems
(EARS) in 1985 to model the universal deformation of a simple elliptic singularity (see
[
37
]
). Then in 1990, Høegh-Krohn and Torresani constructed a certain class of complex Lie algebras called
quasi-simple Lie algebras
whose roots fall into the class of extended affine root systems (see
[
20
]
). These algebras are now known as
extended affine Lie algebras
(EALAs) due to their root systems. EALAs and their root systems were axiomatically defined and thoroughly studied in
[
1
]
.

It is straightforward to define EALAs over fields of characteristic 0 by the same axioms (see
[
32
]
), however, for positive characteristics this requires more subtle considerations. Recall that the core of an EALA is its subalgebra generated by non-isotropic root spaces. By analogy with the arguments in classical Lie theory, here we introduce certain integral forms for the cores of EALAs, by means of which, we are able to define a counterpart for these algebras over fields of positive characteristic through reducing structure constants and then extending scalars.

In this paper, we consider the cores of reduced tame extended affine Lie algebras of rank at least 2. To construct our desired integral form for such an algebra, we present an appropriate spanning set which is obtained from a Chevalley system (Definition
4.9
). To achieve this, the procedure for isotropic and non-isotropic root spaces is different. For non-isotropic root spaces, we use arguments that are mostly local by means of nilpotent pairs and their corresponding rank
2
finite dimensional simple local subalgebras, together with certain local affine subalgebras. Then following the same approach as in classical Lie theory, to make our arguments global in a compatible way, we employ the concept of a Chevalley automorphism (Definition
3.22
).

For the isotropic root spaces the situation is more subtle due to the fact that our knowledge of these spaces is limited. We overcome this by considering the concept of a reflectable base (see Definition
2.9
) and analyzing the action of inner automorphisms based on root vectors corresponding to the ambient reflectable base. We emphasize that our integral forms enjoy the very important property of being invariant under the action of the associated extended affine Weyl group which is, for instance, essential for constructing the corresponding groups of extended affine Lie type over arbitrary fields.

In
[
8
]
we produced groups of extended affine Lie type over complex numbers associated with the tame reduced extended affine Lie algebras generalizing the results in
[
24
]
from simply-laced to arbitrary type. In this direction, there is also a work by Morita and Sakaguchi
[
31
]
. Here, as an application of integral forms, we are able to define groups of extended affine Lie type over arbitrary fields. Then we show that when the underlying field is finite, such groups under certain mild conditions are finitely generated; this generalizes the concept of arithmetic groups in non-Archimedean semisimple groups (see, e.g.,
[
35
]
).

Integral forms, in turn, are very important objects in modular form theory. Further, they appear in the modular moonshine program of Borcherds and Ryba (see e.g.,
[
36
,
15
,
14
]
and for more recent works, see
[
28
,
27
]
). With these integral forms in hand, one might expect the emergence of a modular form theory in the context of EALAs. Moreover, integral forms for EALAs can furnish the ground for defining integral cohomology of extended affine Lie algebras (see e.g.,
[
16
,
18
]
).

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
basic definitions and notation from the theory of EALAs are provided. Section
3
deals with the concept of extended affinization where we introduce certain Chevalley automorphisms and
\sigma
-Chevalley pairs for extended affine Lie algebras in Definition
3.22
. Then we show that a
\sigma
-Chevalley pair for an EALA induces a Chevalley automorphism on its affinization (see Proposition
3.26
below) and then apply it to construct explicit Chevalley automorphisms for toroidal Lie algebras (Corollary
3.27
), affine Lie algebras (Corollary
3.29
) and elliptic Lie algebras (Corollary
3.30
), starting from a Chevalley automorphism of a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra or an affine Lie algebra. Section
4
is an auxiliary section to Section
5
where we provide some preliminary results
which are higher nullity extensions of properties of integral forms for finite and affine Lie algebras. In particular we define the notion of a Chevalley system (Definition
4.9
) for an extended affine Lie algebra. In Section
5
, the integral structure of the core of an EALA equipped with a Chevalley automorphism is studied and via a Chevalley system and a reflectable base, an integral form for the core is introduced (see Theorems
5.8
and
5.9
). Section
6
is to show the independence of our integral forms from the choice of the Chevalley system (see Theorem
6.2
). In Section
7
, multi-loop algebras based on extended affine Lie algebras and the algebra of Laurent polynomials are studied. It is shown in Theorem
7.4
that with certain conditions on the involved automorphisms, the affinization procedure, when applied to a multi-loop algebra leads to an extended affine Lie algebra. We also investigate how a Chevalley automorphism on the underlying extended affine Lie algebra can be lifted to a Chevalley automorphism on the multi-loop affinization through Proposition
7.9
. Finally in Section
8
, we present two rather immediate applications of integral forms. First these integral forms are used to define extended affine Lie algebras over fields of positive characteristic (see Definition
8.1
) then define certain groups associated with them. It is shown in Theorem
8.13
that the adjoint forms of groups of extended affine Lie type are always infinite and that over finite fields are finitely generated.

Since our arguments for integral forms are essentially correspond to local rank
2
subalgebras, we have excluded type
A_{1}

which seems to require a different approach; we will address this type in a separate work.
We also emphasize that since our main motive for the present work is to define groups of extended affine Lie type over an arbitrary field, and on the group level EALAs and their cores have isomorphic associated groups (see
[
8
]
), we have restricted our study in this work to the cores.
We conclude the introduction by emphasizing that
one might be able to extend the integral forms from the core to the whole EALA with techniques presented in
[
32
]
; this also will be addressed in our future works.

2.
Extended affine Lie algebras

In this section we provide some basic concepts, definitions, and notations which will be used in the sequel. In particular we recall definitions of extended affine Lie algebras and root systems.
We also give some basic results on extended affine theory.

All vector spaces and algebras are assumed to be over field of complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
unless
otherwise stated. By
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
, we denote the dual spaces of a vector space
\mathfrak{h}
and by
\langle T\rangle
, we denote the subgroup generated by a subset
T
of the ground group or vector space.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{h}
be a non-trivial
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
be a symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
. The triple
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is called an
extended affine Lie algebra
if the following five axioms hold:

(A1)
(\cdot,\cdot)
is invariant and non-degenerate on
\mathfrak{g}
,

(A2)
\mathfrak{h}
is a finite-dimensional Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Axiom (A2) means that

\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\hbox{ where }\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in\mathfrak{h}\},\hbox{ and }\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}.

Let
R
be the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely
R=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}\mid\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\neq\{0\}\}
.
It follows from (A1)-(A2) that the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricted to
\mathfrak{h}
is non-degenerate and so it can be transferred
to
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
by
(\alpha,\beta):=(t_{\alpha},t_{\beta})
where
t_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}

is the unique element satisfying
\alpha(h)=(h,t_{\alpha})
,
h\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Let
{\mathcal{V}}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R
and denote the radical of the form on
{\mathcal{V}}
by
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
. We set

R^{0}:=\{\alpha\in R\mid(\alpha,\alpha)=0\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad R^{\times}:=R\setminus R^{0}.

Then
R=R^{0}\uplus R^{\times}
is regarded as the decomposition of roots into

isotropic
and
non-isotropic
roots, respectively.

(A3) For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
,
\hbox{ad}(x)
acts locally nilpotently on
\mathfrak{g}
.

(A4) The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of
R
in
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
is a free abelian group of rank
\dim{\mathcal{V}}
.

(A5) (a)
R^{\times}
is indecomposable,

(b)
R^{0}
is
non-isolated
, meaning that
R^{0}=(R^{\times}-R^{\times})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{0}.

[

Definition 2.2

3.
Chevalley automorphism and extended affinization

In this section, we first introduce the concept of a loop algebra of an extended affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a finite order automorphism
\sigma
, based on a quantum torus. We use this to construct a new extended affine Lie algebra out of
\mathfrak{g}
, called the extended affinization of
\mathfrak{g}
. We introduce the concepts of Chevalley automorphisms and
\sigma
-Chevalley pairs for extended affine Lie algebras and show that a
\sigma
-Chevalley pair for
\mathfrak{g}
induces a Chevalley automorphism on its affinization. This will be used then to construct explicit Chevalley automorphisms for toroidal Lie algebras, affine Lie algebras and elliptic Lie algebras, starting from a Chevalley automorphism of a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra.

Throughout this section we assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a quantum torus based on a free abelian group
\Lambda
of finite rank. More precisely,
\mathcal{A}
has a
{\mathbb{C}}
-basis
\{a^{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
satisfying

(3.1)

\mathcal{A}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{\oplus}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}={\mathbb{C}}a^{\lambda}\quad\hbox{and}\quad a^{\lambda}\cdot a^{\gamma}=\mu(\lambda,\gamma)a^{\lambda+\gamma}

where
\mu:\Lambda\times\Lambda\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is a
2
-cocycle satisfying

\mu(\lambda,\tau)=\mu(\tau,\lambda)=\mu(-\lambda,-\tau)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mu(0,0)=1.

We consider the
\Lambda
-graded invariant non-degenerate symmetric
bilinear form
\epsilon:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},
given by

\epsilon(a^{\lambda},a^{\tau})=\mu(\lambda,\tau)\delta_{\lambda,-\tau}\qquad(\lambda,\tau\in\Lambda).

(Here
\delta_{\sigma,-\tau}
denotes the Kronecker Delta.)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
.
Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
of period
m
, and
\omega:=e^{2\pi i/m}.

Then
\sigma
induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
-grading

\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{\bar{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{i}}

where

\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{i}}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\sigma(x)=\omega^{i}x\}.

We note that
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{j}}=\pi_{j}(\mathfrak{g})
, where

(3.2)

\pi_{j}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\omega^{-ij}\sigma^{i}.

(The scalar
1/m
is inserted for some normalization on the level of roots.)
Moreover,

(3.3)

\sigma\pi_{j}=\pi_{j}\sigma=\omega^{-j}\pi_{j}\quad\hbox{and}\quad[\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{j}(y)]=\pi_{i+j}[x,\pi_{j}(y)],

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
5
, Section 3]
). We set
\pi:=\pi_{0}
.
Note that

\pi(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
is the set of fixed points of
\mathfrak{g}
, under
\sigma
.

Assume further that
\sigma(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}
. Then we also have

\pi(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}^{0}=\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma}
.
The automorphism
\sigma
also induces an automorphism, denoted again by
\sigma
, on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by

\sigma(\alpha)(h)=\alpha(\sigma^{-1}(h))
,
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
,
h\in\mathfrak{h}
. As above
we denote the map
\pi_{0}\in\hbox{End}(\mathfrak{h}^{\star})
by
\pi
. One observes that for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
,
\pi(\alpha)
can be identified with
\alpha_{|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma}}}
.
It is easy to check that

(3.4)

(\pi(\alpha),\pi(\beta))=(\alpha,\pi(\beta))\quad\hbox{for}\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}.

We now set

\pi(R):=\{\pi(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in R\}.

Recall from Section
2
that the form on
\mathfrak{h}
transfers to a form on
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
.

Throughout this section, we assume that
\sigma
satisfies

(3.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}\sigma^{m}=\hbox{id},\\
\sigma(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h},\\
(\sigma(x),\sigma(y))=(x,y)\hbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\\
C_{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma},\\
(\alpha,\pi(\alpha))\not=0,\hbox{ for some }\alpha\in R.\end{array}

While the first four conditions given above looks natural for people familiar with the passage from finite dimensional theory to affine theory, the last less familiar condition is inserted in order to guarantee the existence of some non-isotropic vector
\pi(\alpha)
.

Next, we consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
with bracket defined by

[x\otimes a,y\otimes b]:=[x,y]\otimes{\mu(\lambda,\tau)}ab

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
a\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda},\;b\in\mathcal{A}^{\tau}
.
Now we define a form on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
by linear extension of

(3.6)

(x\otimes a,y\otimes b)=(x,y)\epsilon(a,b),

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
. It is easy to check that this is a
\Lambda
-graded invariant symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\rho:\Lambda\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
be a group epimorphism and set
\bar{\lambda}:=\rho(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
. The subalgebra

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}:=L_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A}):=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda})

of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is called the
loop algebra
of
\mathfrak{g}

relative to
\rho
and
\mathcal{A}
. In the case that
\sigma=\hbox{id}
and
\rho=0,
we denote

L_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
by

L(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
and note that
L(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
. The Lie algebra
L(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
is refereed to as a
toroidal Lie algebra.

From definition, it is clear that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is a

\Lambda
-graded Lie algebra with homogeneous spaces

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda}:=\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
,

\lambda\in\Lambda
.

The following result is standard, see for example
[
5
, Lemma 7.5]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

The form on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
restricted to
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is a

\Lambda
-graded invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form.

For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
, let
\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)}
be the sum of all root spaces
\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
with
\pi(\beta)=\pi(\alpha)
. For
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\pi(\alpha)}=\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\lambda)}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
. Then we have

(3.9)

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda}=\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\pi(\alpha)\in\pi(R)}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\pi(\alpha)}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}).

Next, we set
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}:=\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma}\otimes 1
, and we embed
(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma})^{\star}
in
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{\star}
.
The adjoint action of
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
on
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
stabilizes the spaces
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Let

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pi(\alpha)}=\{x\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\mid[x,h]=\pi(\alpha)(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}\}
. It then follows that

(3.10)

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{\pi(\alpha)\in\pi(R)}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pi(\alpha)}.

Moreover, we have

(3.11)

{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\pi(\alpha)}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\pi(\alpha)}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}),

namely we have a compatible
(\langle\pi(R)\rangle,\Lambda)
-grading

(3.12)

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\gamma\in\langle\pi(R)\rangle}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\gamma}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda},

where for
{\lambda\in\Lambda}
,
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\gamma}^{\lambda}=\{0\}
if
\gamma\notin\pi(R)
.

Next,
\Lambda
being free abelian allows us to embed it in the
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space

\mathcal{V}:={\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda.
We set

(3.13)

\hat{L}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})=\hat{\mathfrak{g}}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\star}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\hat{\mathfrak{h}}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\star}=(\mathfrak{h}^{0}\otimes 1)\oplus\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\star},

and make
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
into a Lie algebra with the Lie bracket:

(3.14)

\begin{array}[]{l}\;[d,x]=d(\lambda)x,\quad d\in\mathcal{V}^{\star},\;x\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda},\;\lambda\in\Lambda,\\
\;[\mathcal{V},\hat{\mathfrak{g}}]=\{0\},\\
\;[x,y]=[x,y]_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}+(x,y)\lambda,\;x\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\lambda},\;y\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\end{array}

where by

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
, we mean the Lie
bracket and the bilinear form on
{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}},
respectively.
Now the form on

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
extends
to a non-degenerate form on
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
by

(3.15)

\begin{array}[]{l}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})=(\mathcal{V}^{\star},\mathcal{V}^{\star})=(\mathcal{V},\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})=(\mathcal{V}^{\star},\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}):=\{0\},\\
(v,d)=(d,v):=d(v),\quad d\in\mathcal{V}^{\star},v\in\mathcal{V}.\end{array}

Definition 3.16
.

We call the Lie algebra
(\hat{L}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A}),(\cdot,\cdot),\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
an extended affinization of
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
.

By imbedding
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow(\mathcal{V}^{\star})^{\star}
via the natural paring, we may identify
\Lambda
inside
{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}^{\star}
.
We also identify
(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma})^{\star}
inside
(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})^{\star}
.
Then we have

(3.17)

\hat{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{\hat{\alpha}\in\hat{R}}{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\hat{\alpha}},

where

{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\hat{\alpha}}:=\{x\in\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\mid[\hat{h},x]=\hat{\alpha}(\hat{h})x\hbox{ for all }\hat{h}\in\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\},

and

\hat{R}=\{\hat{\alpha}\in(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})^{\star}\mid\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{\alpha}}\neq 0\}\subseteq\pi(R)\oplus\Lambda.

We have, for
\alpha\in R
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

(3.18)

\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\pi(\alpha)}+\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}&\mbox{if }{\pi(\alpha)}+\lambda\not=0,\\
(\mathfrak{g}^{0}_{\pi(0)}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{0})\oplus\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\star}&\hbox{if
}{\pi(\alpha)}+\lambda=0.\end{array}\right.

Finally, we mention that

(3.19)

\hat{R}=\cup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\pi(R_{\bar{\lambda}})+\lambda),\quad{where}\quad R_{\bar{\lambda}}=\{\alpha\in R\mid\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\alpha}\not=\{0\}\}.

Theorem 3.20
.

The extended affinization
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},(\cdot,\cdot),\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
of
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system

\hat{R}
.

Proof.
The fifth condition of (
3.5
) guarantees the existence of non-isotropic roots in
\hat{R}
. Then the result follows from
[
5
, Theorem 7.21]
.

Remark 3.21
.

One observes that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is in fact the fixed point subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
under the automorphism
\sigma\otimes\eta
where
\eta
is the automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
given by
\eta(a^{\lambda})=\omega^{-\bar{\lambda}}a^{\lambda}.

In what follows we investigate how a Chevalley automorphism of an
extended affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be promoted to an automorphism of its affinization
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Definition 3.22
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
be an extended affine Lie algebra and
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying (
3.5
).

(i) We call an automorphism
\tau
of
\mathfrak{g}
a
Chevalley automorphism
if

-
\tau
is of finite order,

-
\tau(h)=-h
for
h\in\mathfrak{h}
.

(ii) A pair
(\tau,\psi)
of finite order automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
is called a
\sigma
-Chevalley pair if

-
\tau
is a Chevalley automorphism,

-
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
,

-
\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{j}})=\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{j}}
, for
j=0,\ldots,m-1
,

-
\psi
preserves the form,

-
\psi(h)=h
for
h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{0}}
.

Lemma 3.23
.

Assume that
\tau
is a Chevalley automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then

(i)
\tau(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})=\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
.

(ii)
(\tau(x),\tau(y)=(x,y)
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)
\tau
is of even order.

Proof.
Since
\tau_{|_{\mathfrak{h}}}=-\hbox{id}
, we have for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}
,

[h,\tau(x)]=\tau([-h,x])=-\alpha(h)\tau(x)
, so (i) holds.

Next, let
\alpha\not=0
,
x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
x_{\beta}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
, then
by (i),

(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})=(\tau(x_{\alpha}),\tau(x_{\beta}))=0
if
\alpha+\beta\not=0
.
If
\beta=-\alpha,
then

t_{-\alpha}(\tau(x_{\alpha}),\tau(x_{-\alpha}))=[\tau(x_{\alpha}),\tau(x_{-\alpha})]=\tau([x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}])=-t_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}).

Thus
(\tau(x_{\alpha}),\tau(x_{-\alpha}))=(x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha})
. If
\alpha=0
, then for
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}
we have

(\tau(h),\tau(h^{\prime}))=(-h,-h^{\prime})=(h,h^{\prime})
. All together, we get (ii). Part (iii) is clear from (i).
\Box

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
(\tau,\psi)
be
\sigma
-Chevalley pair for
\mathfrak{g}
. Then

\psi\tau(\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}})=\mathfrak{g}_{-\pi(\alpha)}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\lambda}}
, for
\alpha\in R
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Proof.
Since
\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)}=\sum_{\{\beta\in R\mid\pi(\beta)=\pi(\alpha)\}}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
, it follows from Lemma
3.22
(i) that
\tau(\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)})=\mathfrak{g}_{-\pi(\alpha)}
. Moreover, since
\tau
commutes with
\sigma
, it preserves the grading spaces
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
. On the other hand since

\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for }h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{0}}\}
, we get
\psi(\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\pi(\alpha)}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\lambda}}
. The result now is immediate from the above facts.
\Box

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
\psi
be an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\{X_{\beta}\mid\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq R\}
be a set of Lie algebra generators for the extended affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. If for each
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}
, there exists
\beta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\psi\sigma^{i}(X_{\beta})=\sigma^{-i}X_{\beta^{\prime}}
for all
0\leq i\leq m-1
, then
\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{j}})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{j}}
, for each

j
.

Proof.
Let
X=[X_{\beta_{1}},\ldots,X_{\beta_{t}}]
,
\beta_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Then

\displaystyle\psi\pi_{j}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\omega^{-ij}\psi\sigma^{i}[X_{\beta_{1}},\ldots,X_{\beta_{t}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\omega^{-ij}[\sigma^{-i}X_{\beta^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,\sigma^{-i}X_{\beta^{\prime}_{t}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}\omega^{ij}\sigma^{i}[X_{\beta^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,X_{\beta^{\prime}_{t}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\pi_{-j}([X_{\beta^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,X_{\beta^{\prime}_{t}}])\in\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{j}}.

\Box

Proposition 3.26
.

Assume that the extended affine Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
admits
a
\sigma
-Chevalley pair
(\tau,\psi)
.
Then the assignment

\pi_{\lambda}(x)\otimes a^{\lambda}+\gamma+d\mapsto\psi\tau(\pi_{\lambda}(x))\otimes a^{-\lambda}-\gamma-d,

(
x\in\mathfrak{g},

\lambda\in\Lambda,

\gamma\in{\mathcal{V}},

d\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}
)
defines an automorphism
{\bar{\tau}}_{\psi}
of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
which restricts to a Chevalley automorphism of
{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.
The first statement follows from the fact that for any form preserving automorphism
\Phi
of
\mathfrak{g}
, the map

\bar{\Phi}
induced by the assignment

\pi_{j}(x)\otimes a^{\lambda}+\gamma+d\mapsto\Phi(\pi_{j}(x))\otimes a^{-\lambda}-\gamma-d,

defines an automorphism of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
with inverse

\overline{\Phi^{-1}}
. To be more precise, for

x\in\mathfrak{g},\lambda\in\Lambda
, we have

{\bar{\Phi}}(x\otimes a^{\lambda})=\sum_{j}{\bar{\Phi}}(\pi_{j}(x)\otimes a^{\lambda})=\sum_{j}\Phi(\pi_{j}(x))\otimes a^{-\lambda})=\Phi(x)\otimes a^{-\lambda}.

So for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
,
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
and
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{\star}
, we have

\begin{split}{\bar{\Phi}}[x\otimes a^{\lambda}+\gamma+d,y\otimes a^{\lambda^{\prime}}+\gamma^{\prime}+d^{\prime}]=&{{\bar{\Phi}}\big([x,y]\otimes a^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}}+(x,y)\delta_{\lambda,-\lambda^{\prime}}\lambda\big)\\
&+{\bar{\Phi}}\big(d(\lambda^{\prime})y\otimes a^{\lambda^{\prime}}-d^{\prime}(\lambda)x\otimes a^{\lambda}\big)\\
=&[\Phi(x),\Phi(y)]\otimes{a^{-\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}}\\
&+(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))\delta_{-\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(-\lambda)\\
&+d(\lambda^{\prime})\Phi(y)\otimes a^{-\lambda^{\prime}}-d^{\prime}(\lambda)\Phi(x)a^{-\lambda}\\
=&[{\bar{\Phi}}(x\otimes a^{\lambda}+\gamma+d),{\bar{\Phi}}(y\otimes a^{\lambda^{\prime}}+\gamma^{\prime}+d^{\prime})],\end{split}

where to get the above equalities we have used the facts that
\Phi
preserves the form and that
\mu(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=\mu(-\lambda,-\lambda^{\prime})
.
This shows that
\bar{\Phi}
is an automorphism of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
, with inverse
\overline{\Phi^{-1}}
.

We now know that
\bar{\tau}_{\psi}
is a finite order automorphism of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
. Moreover, since
\tau
commutes with each
\pi_{\lambda}
, and
\psi
sends the subspace
\pi_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
to
\pi_{-\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
, we get
for
x\in\mathfrak{g},\lambda\in\Lambda
, that

\bar{\tau}_{\psi}(\pi_{\lambda}(x)\otimes a^{\lambda})=\psi(\tau(\pi_{\lambda}(x)))\otimes a^{-\lambda}=\psi(\pi_{\lambda}(\tau(x)))\otimes a^{-\lambda}\in\pi_{-\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes a^{-\lambda}.

So
\bar{\tau}_{\psi}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A}))\subseteq\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
. Furthermore, since
\psi_{|_{\pi_{0}(\mathfrak{h})}}=\hbox{id}_{\mathfrak{h}}
, we have for
h\in\mathfrak{h}
,

{\bar{\tau}}_{\psi}(\pi_{0}(h))\otimes 1+d+\lambda=\psi\tau(\pi_{0}(h))\otimes 1-d-\lambda=-\pi_{0}(h)\otimes 1-d-\lambda
. Thus

\bar{\tau}_{\psi}
is a Chevalley automorphism of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
.
\Box

Corollary 3.27
.

(Toroidal Lie algebras)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra, and
\tau
be a Chevalley automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then

\bar{\tau}:={\bar{\tau}}_{\hbox{id}}
is a Chevalley automorphism of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
. In particular, if
\mathcal{A}
is the algebra of Laurent polynomials in
\nu
variables, then
\bar{\tau}
is a Chevalley automorphism
of the toroidal Lie algebra
{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.
Apply Proposition
3.26
with
\sigma=\psi=\hbox{id}
.

\Box

Corollary 3.28
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})

\sigma
be an automorphism of order
2
. If
\mathfrak{g}
admits a Chevalley automorphism

commuting with

, then
\bar{\tau}:={\bar{\tau}}_{\hbox{id}}

.

.
\Box

Corollary 3.29
.

(Affine Lie algebras)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra,
\tau
a Chevalley automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\sigma
a non-trivial graph automorphism. Then
{\bar{\tau}}_{\psi}
is a Chevalley automorphism for
{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
where
\psi=\hbox{id}
if
\sigma
has order
2
, and
\psi
is a graph automorphism of order
2
otherwise.
In particular if
\mathcal{A}

is the algebra of Laurent polynomials in one variable, then
{\bar{\tau}}_{\psi}
is a Chevalley automorphism of the affine Lie algebra
{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.
One knows that for
\sigma
and
\tau
as in the statement
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
.
If
\sigma
has order
2
, then we are done by Corollary
3.28
.

Suppose next that
\sigma
has order
3
, namely
\sigma
is the automorphism of
D_{4}
induced by Dynkin diagram automorphism:

2
1
3
4

We take
\psi
to be be the order
2
diagram automorphism of
D_{4}
induced by the diagram automorphism:
2
1
3
4

Now if
X\in\{h_{\alpha_{i}},E_{\pm i}\mid 1\leq i\leq 4\}
is a standard Chevalley generator for
\mathfrak{g}
, then we simply check that for

\psi\sigma^{k}(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma^{-k}X&\hbox{if }X\in\{h_{\alpha_{i}},E_{\pm\alpha_{i}}\mid i=1,2\},\\

Thus by Lemma

,

is

-Chevalley pair (see also

Corollary 3.30
.

(Elliptic Lie algebras)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an affine Lie algebra,
\sigma
be a non-identity graph automorphism and
\tau
be a Chevalley automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then

{\bar{\tau}}_{\psi}
is a Chevalley automorphism of
{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
for some graph automorphism
\psi
. In particular if
\mathcal{A}
is the algebra of Laurent polynomials in one variable then
{\bar{\tau}}_{\psi}
is a Chevalley automorphism of the elliptic Lie algebra
{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.
First, one observes that
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
. There are
22
non-identity graph automorphisms of affine type, from which
17
are of order
2
for them Corollary
3.28
applies. Assume next that
m>2
, where
m
is the order of
\sigma
. If
m=3
, then
\sigma
is the automorphism induced by the order

Remark 4.3

The existence of a positivity decomposition can be guaranteed in several ways. We present two of them here. Let

[x_{\beta},x^{\alpha}_{\delta}]=cx_{\beta+\delta}
, for some complex constant

. We must show that

.

such that
\gamma+\beta+\delta=\gamma+\alpha+\eta+\delta\in R^{\times}
and
\gamma\pm\alpha\not\in{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
.
Then we have

[x_{\gamma},[x_{\beta},[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}]]]=[x_{\gamma},cx_{\beta+\delta}]=cc_{\gamma,\beta+\delta}x_{\beta+\gamma+\delta},

where
cc_{\gamma,\beta+\delta}
is non-zero. On the other hand, using the Jacobi identity we see that

\displaystyle[x_{\gamma},[x_{\beta},[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}]]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}],[x_{\gamma},x_{\alpha+\eta}]]+[x_{\alpha+\eta},[x_{\gamma},[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}]]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c_{\gamma,\alpha+\eta}[[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}],x_{\gamma^{\prime}}]+[x_{\alpha+\eta},[x_{\gamma},[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}]]]

where
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma+\alpha+\eta.
Now we consider finite root systems

\dot{R}_{1}:=({\mathbb{Z}}\alpha+{\mathbb{Z}}\gamma^{\prime})\cap R,\qquad\dot{R}_{2}:=({\mathbb{Z}}\alpha+{\mathbb{Z}}\gamma)\cap R,

and affine Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}^{a}_{{\dot{R}}_{1},\delta}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{a}_{{\dot{R}}_{2},\delta}
, see Lemma
4.10
. The Chevalley automorphism
\tau

then restricts to a Chevalley automorphism on
\mathfrak{g}^{a}_{{\dot{R}}_{1},\delta}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{a}_{{\dot{R}}_{2},\delta}
.
Thus using realizations of affine Lie algebras as in
[
29
]
, we see that

(4.17)

[[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}],x_{\gamma^{\prime}}]=kx_{\gamma^{\prime}+\delta}\quad\hbox{and}\quad[x_{\gamma},[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}]]=k^{\prime}x_{\gamma+\delta}

for some
k,k^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that
k+k^{\prime}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then combining with (
4.16
), we get

\displaystyle cc_{\gamma,\beta+\delta}x_{\gamma+\beta+\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-kc_{\gamma,\alpha+\eta}x_{\gamma^{\prime}+\delta}+k^{\prime}[x_{\alpha+\eta},x_{\gamma+\delta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-kc_{\gamma,\beta}x_{\gamma+\beta+\delta}+k^{\prime}c_{\beta,\gamma+\delta}x_{\gamma+\beta+\delta}.

Since
c_{\gamma,\beta+\delta},c_{\gamma,\beta},c_{\beta,\gamma+\delta}
are equal up to
\pm
signs, we get

c\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
as required.

(ii) By (i), we may assume
\hbox{rank }\mathfrak{g}=1
. If
\mathfrak{g}
is finite dimensional simple, then
\beta=\pm\alpha
and the result is immediate. If
\mathfrak{g}
is affine, then one can check from the realizations of affine Lie algebras that the result holds.

5.
Integral structure of the core

In this section, we use the results given in Section
4
and the concept of a reflectable base to give a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form for the core of an extended affine Lie algebra equipped with a Chevalley automorphism. This leads to a a notion of Chevalley basis for the core. As a byproduct, we give an upper bound for the dimension of the isotropic root spaces of the core.

We continue with the assumptions and notations as in Section
4
.
In particular,
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is a tame reduced
extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
, equipped with a Chevalley automorphism
\tau
. In this section, we further assume that
\mathfrak{g}
has rank
>1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(\hbox{ad}x_{\beta})^{k}}{k!}
, where
\beta\in R^{\times}
. Then for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
\delta\in R^{0}
, the following hold:

(i) If
\alpha+\beta\in R^{\times}
, then
\phi(x_{\alpha})\in\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{\alpha-d\beta},\ldots,x_{\alpha+u\beta}\}
.

(ii) If
\alpha+\beta\in R^{0}
, then
\phi(x_{\alpha})\in\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{\alpha},x_{\alpha+\beta}^{-\alpha},x_{\alpha+2\beta}\}
.

(iii)
\phi(x_{\delta}^{\alpha})\in\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{\delta}^{\alpha},x_{\beta+\delta}\}.

(iv)
\phi(h_{\alpha})\in\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{h_{\alpha},x_{\beta}\}
, where
h_{\alpha}:=[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}].

Proof.
(i) If
\alpha+\beta\in R^{\times}
, namely
\{\alpha,\beta\}
is a nilpotent pair, then the statement is a relation inside the finite dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha,\beta}
(see (
4.8
)). Thus the result follows from the classical finite dimensional theory, see
[
19
]
.

(ii) If
\alpha+\beta\in R^{0}
, then
\alpha=-\beta+\lambda
for some
\lambda\in R^{0}
, and so

\phi(x_{\alpha})=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{(\hbox{ad}x_{\beta})^{k}}{k!}(x_{\alpha})=x_{\alpha}+x_{\lambda}^{-\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}[x_{\beta},x_{\lambda}^{-\alpha}].

Now the result follows from Lemma
4.14
.

(iii) If
\alpha+\beta\in{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
or
\alpha-\beta\in{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
, then we are done by Lemma
4.14
. Assume now that
\alpha\pm\beta\not\in{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
.
Then
\phi(x^{\alpha}_{\delta})=x_{\delta}^{\alpha}+[x_{\beta},x_{\delta}^{\alpha}]
. Moreover,

(5.2)

[x_{\beta},x^{\alpha}_{\delta}]=[x_{\beta},[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}]]=-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\overbrace{[x_{-\alpha},[x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\delta}]]}}}-\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\overbrace{[x_{\alpha+\delta},[x_{-\alpha},x_{\beta}]]}}}.

If
A\not=0
, then

(5.3)

A=[x_{-\alpha},c_{\beta,\alpha+\delta}x_{\beta+\alpha+\delta}]=c_{\beta,\alpha+\delta}c_{-\alpha,\beta+\alpha+\delta}x_{\beta+\delta},

and if
B\not=0
, then

(5.4)

B=[x_{\alpha+\delta},c_{-\alpha,\beta}x_{-\alpha+\beta}]=c_{\alpha+\delta,-\alpha+\beta}c_{-\alpha,\beta}x_{\beta+\delta}

as required.

(iv) We have
\phi(h_{\alpha})=h_{\alpha}+[x_{\beta},h_{\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}-\beta(h_{\alpha})x_{\beta}
.

\Box

We set

\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}:=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{\alpha},x_{\delta}^{\alpha},h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times},0\not=\delta\in R^{0},\alpha+\delta\in R\}.

We note that

\mathfrak{g}_{c}\cong{\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}},

as isomorphism of vector spaces.

Corollary 5.5
.

For each
\beta\in R^{\times}
,
the automorphism
\Phi_{\beta}
stabilizes
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{c}
.

Proof.
It immediately follows from Lemma
5.1
.
\Box

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\Pi
be a reflectable base for
R
.

(i) For
0\not=\delta\in R^{0}
,

\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{\delta}^{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Pi,\beta+\delta\in R\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{c})\leq|\Pi|.

(ii)
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{\alpha},t_{\beta},x_{\delta}^{\beta}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times},\delta\in R^{0},\beta\in\Pi\}.

Proof.
(i) We must show that for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
,
x_{\delta}^{\alpha}\in\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{\delta}^{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Pi\}
.
Now for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, we have
\alpha=w_{\beta_{1}}\cdots w_{\beta_{k-1}}(\beta_{k})
for some
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\in\Pi
. Then taking
w=w_{\beta_{1}}\cdots w_{\beta_{k-1}}
, we get

x_{\delta}^{\alpha}=x_{\delta}^{w(\beta_{k})}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\Phi_{\beta_{1}}\cdots\Phi_{\beta_{k-1}}(x_{\delta}^{\beta_{k}}).

Then the fact that automorphisms
\Phi_{\beta_{i}}
preserve the root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}
together with Lemma
5.1
gives the result.

(ii) Since
\langle\Pi\rangle=\langle R\rangle
, for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, we have

t_{\alpha}\in\langle t_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Pi\rangle
. This together with (i) now completes the proof.
\Box

Theorem 5.8
.

Let

be a tame reduced extended affine Lie algebra with root system

of rank
>1
, admitting a Chevalley automorphism
\tau
. Then

is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Lie algebra. In fact
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form of

, generated as a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Lie algebra by the Chevalley system

Proof.
By Proposition

(ii),

Thus considering Lemma

, we only need to show that
[x_{\delta}^{\alpha},x_{\eta}^{\beta}]\in\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}
for
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
and
\delta,\eta\in R^{0}
. Now

\displaystyle[x_{\delta}^{\alpha},x_{\eta}^{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}],x_{\eta}^{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[[x_{\eta}^{\beta},x_{\alpha+\delta}],x_{-\alpha}]-[[x_{-\alpha},x_{\eta}^{\beta}],x_{\alpha+\delta}]

\displaystyle(\hbox{Lemma \ref{lemassump2}(iii)})

\displaystyle k[x_{\alpha+\delta+\eta},x_{-\alpha}]+k^{\prime}[x_{-\alpha+\eta},x_{\alpha+\delta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kx_{\delta+\eta}^{\alpha}+k^{\prime}x_{\delta+\eta}^{-\alpha-\delta}

\displaystyle(\hbox{Proposition \ref{protemp2}}(i))

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}.

\Box

Theorem 5.9
.

(

[x_{\alpha},x_{\delta}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}x_{\alpha+\delta}

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}

(\mathfrak{g},\tau)
. Since non-isotropic root spaces are

\mathcal{C}
forms a basis for the subspace of

consisting of non-isotropic root spaces. We next find a basis for
\mathfrak{g}_{c}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.
We know that

\mathfrak{g}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{c}

,

. Now for

(see

,
1\leq i\leq\ell
,

1\leq j\leq\nu
. So the elements

{2t_{\sigma_{j}}}/{(\beta,\beta)}&\hbox{ if }t+1\leq j\leq\nu,\end{array}\right.

where
\beta
is a root of maximum length, form a basis for
\mathfrak{g}_{c}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
(here

is the twist number of the root system, introduced prior to (

)).

Next, using Proposition
5.6
, we consider a basis

\mathcal{X}_{\delta}\subseteq\{x_{\delta}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Pi,\;\alpha+\delta\in R\}

{\mathcal{B}}

{\mathbb{C}}
-basis for
\mathfrak{g}_{c}

\mathfrak{h}
is abelian, we have

\alpha\in R^{\times}

On the Uniqueness of integral structure

The goal in this section is to show that the integral structure provided for the core of an extended affine Lie algebra given in Section
5
is independent of the choice of the Chevalley system.

We continue with the notations and terminologies as in Sections
4
and
5
. As before
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is a tame reduced extended affine Lie algebra of rank
>1
, with root system
R
. By Theorem
5.8
,
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{c}
is generated as a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Lie algebra
by the choice of a Chvalley system
\{x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times}\}

\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}(x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times})

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{c}(x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times})

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{c}(\bar{x}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times})
for two choices of Chevalley systems
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}
and

\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}

\bar{x}_{\alpha}=\eta_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
, for some
\eta_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Since
[x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}=[\bar{x}_{\alpha},\bar{x}_{-\alpha}]

\eta_{\alpha}\eta_{-\alpha}=1
for each
\alpha\in R^{\times}
. We recall from Proposition
4.7
part (ii) that for a nilpotent pair
\{\alpha,\beta\}
of roots in
R

[x_{\alpha},x_{\beta}]=c_{\alpha,\beta}x_{\alpha+\beta}

[\bar{x}_{\alpha},\bar{x}_{\beta}]=\bar{c}_{\alpha,\beta}\bar{x}_{\alpha+\beta}
and
c_{\alpha,\beta}=\pm\bar{c}_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

(i) If
\{\alpha,\beta\}
is a nilpotent pair in
R
then

c_{\alpha,\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\beta}=\bar{c}_{\alpha,\beta}\eta_{\alpha}\eta_{\beta}

and

\eta_{\alpha+\beta}=\pm\eta_{\alpha}\eta_{\beta}
.

(ii) Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
,
\delta\in R^{0}
,
\alpha+\delta,\beta+\delta\in R
.
Then

\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}=\pm\eta_{\beta+\delta}\eta_{-\beta}
.

Proof.
(i) By
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
,
R_{\alpha,\beta}=({\mathbb{Z}}\alpha\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\beta)\cap R

is an irreducible reduced finite root system. Then
[
19
, §25.3]
gives the result.

(ii) Assume first that
\alpha
and
\beta
have the same length.
Let
\dot{\theta}
be the highest long (short) root of
\dot{R}

if
\alpha
is long (short). We have
\alpha=\dot{\alpha}+\sigma
for some

\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}
and
\sigma\in R^{0}
. We may assume without loos
of generality that
\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}^{+}
. We set

\theta:=\dot{\theta}+\sigma
. Since
\dot{\alpha}
and
\dot{\theta}
have the same length, we have
\theta\in R
. It is enough to show that

\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}=\eta_{\theta+\delta}\eta_{-\theta}
.
Assume
\dot{\alpha}\not=\dot{\theta}
.
Since

\theta+\dot{\alpha}\not\in\dot{R}
, we get from the Jacobi identity that

\displaystyle[\bar{x}_{\theta},[\bar{x}_{\alpha+\delta},\bar{x}_{-\alpha}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\bar{x}_{\alpha+\delta},[\bar{x}_{-\alpha},\bar{x}_{\theta}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{c}_{-\alpha,\theta}\bar{c}_{\alpha+\delta,\theta-\alpha}\bar{x}_{\theta+\delta}=-\eta_{\theta+\delta}\bar{c}_{-\alpha,\theta}\bar{c}_{\alpha+\delta,\theta-\alpha}x_{\theta+\delta}

On the other hand

\displaystyle[\bar{x}_{\theta},[\bar{x}_{\alpha+\delta},\bar{x}_{-\alpha}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\theta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}[x_{\theta},[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta_{\theta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}[x_{\alpha+\delta},[x_{-\alpha},x_{\theta}]]

\displaystyle-c_{-\alpha,\theta}c_{\alpha+\delta,\theta-\alpha}\eta_{\theta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}x_{\theta+\delta}.

Comparing the above equalities we get,

\eta_{\theta+\delta}=\pm\eta_{\theta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}

To complete the proof, it remains to show that if

\alpha

\displaystyle\eta_{\beta+\delta}\eta_{-\beta}

\displaystyle\eta_{{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}+\dot{\alpha}_{2}+\sigma+\delta}\eta_{-\dot{\alpha}_{1}-\dot{\alpha}_{2}-\sigma}

\displaystyle\eta_{({\dot{\alpha}}_{1}+\delta)+(\dot{\alpha}_{2}+\sigma)}\eta_{-\dot{\alpha}_{1}-(\dot{\alpha}_{2}+\sigma)}

\displaystyle\pm\eta_{{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}+\delta}\eta_{-\dot{\alpha}_{1}}\underbrace{\eta_{\dot{\alpha}_{2}+\sigma}\eta_{-\dot{\alpha}_{2}-\sigma}}_{1}

\displaystyle\pm\eta_{{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}+\delta}\eta_{-\dot{\alpha}_{1}}.

Theorem 6.2
.

(
Uniqueness Theorem
)
Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a reduced tame extended affine Lie algebra of rank
>1
. Then, as
{\mathbb{Z}}
-Lie algebras we have

\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}(x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times})\cong\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\bar{x}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times})
, for two choices of Chevalley systems
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}
and

\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}
.

Proof.
In what follows we always assume that
1\leq i\leq\ell
,
1\leq j\leq\nu
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
\delta\in R^{0}\setminus\{0\}
. From Theorem
5.9
, we know that the set
\{x_{\alpha},h_{i},{c_{j}}\}

is a basis for the subspace

(\mathfrak{g}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{c})\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
We consider an assignment

\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}(x_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\bar{x}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in R^{\times})

\begin{array}[]{c}h_{i}\mapsto h_{i},\quad c_{j}\mapsto c_{j},\quad x_{\alpha}\mapsto\eta_{\alpha}\bar{x}_{\alpha},\\
\sum_{t=1}^{n}k_{t}x^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta}\mapsto\sum_{t=1}^{n}k_{t}\eta_{\alpha_{t}+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha_{t}}\bar{x}^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta},\end{array}

where
\bar{x}^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta}:=[\bar{x}_{\alpha_{t}+\delta},\bar{x}_{-\alpha_{t}}]
,
\alpha_{t}\in R^{\times}
,
k_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>}0
.
The first three assignments induces a group homomorphism

\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\{x_{\alpha},h_{i},c_{j}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\{\bar{x}_{\alpha},h_{i},c_{j}\}
. To extend
f
to a group homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}(x_{\alpha},h_{i},c_{j})
onto

\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\bar{x}_{\alpha},h_{i},c_{j})
, it is enough to show that
if
\sum_{t=1}^{n}k_{t}x^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta}=0
,
\alpha_{t}\in R^{\times}
,
then

\sum_{t=1}^{n}k_{t}\eta_{\alpha_{t}+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha_{t}}\bar{x}^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta}=0
. But

\sum_{t=1}^{n}k_{t}\eta_{\alpha_{t}+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha_{t}}\bar{x}^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}k_{t}(\eta_{\alpha_{t}+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha_{t}})^{2}x^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta}=\eta\sum_{t=1}^{n}k_{t}x^{\alpha_{t}}_{\delta}=0,

where
\eta=(\eta_{\alpha_{t}+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha_{t}})^{2}
, for
1\leq t\leq n
, see Lemma
6.1
.
This shows that
f
is well defined, and is a group homomorphism.

We now check that
f
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Let
\alpha,\beta\in R^{\times}
and
\delta,\sigma\in R^{0}
. Then by Lemma
6.1
part (i),

\displaystyle f([x_{\alpha},x_{\beta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\alpha,\beta}f(x_{\alpha+\beta})=c_{\alpha,\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\beta}\bar{x}_{\alpha+\beta}=\bar{c}_{\alpha,\beta}\eta_{\alpha}\eta_{\beta}\bar{x}_{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\eta_{\alpha}\bar{x}_{\alpha},\eta_{\beta}\bar{x}_{\beta}]=[f(x_{\alpha}),f(x_{\beta})].

Next, we consider brackets of the form

[x_{\beta},x_{\delta}^{\alpha}]
. First note that

[\bar{x}_{\beta},\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\delta}]=\eta_{\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}[x_{\beta},x_{\delta}^{\alpha}]=c\eta_{\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}x_{\beta+\delta}=\bar{c}\eta_{\beta+\delta}x_{\beta+\delta},

where
c,\bar{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, see Theorem
5.9
(vi). On the other hand we get from Lemma
6.1
part (ii) that
\eta_{\beta+\delta}=\pm\eta_{\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}
. This together with the last equality gives
c\eta_{\beta+\delta}=\bar{c}\eta_{\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle f([x_{\beta},x^{\alpha}_{\delta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle cf(x_{\beta+\delta})=c\eta_{\beta+\delta}\bar{x}_{\beta+\delta}=\bar{c}\eta_{\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}\bar{x}_{\beta+\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\eta_{\beta}\bar{x}_{\beta},\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\delta}]=[f(x_{\beta}),f(x^{\alpha}_{\delta})].

Finally for brackets of the form
[x^{\alpha}_{\delta},x^{\beta}_{\sigma}]
we have

\displaystyle f([x_{\delta}^{\alpha},x_{\sigma}^{\beta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f([[x_{\alpha+\delta},x_{-\alpha}],x_{\sigma}^{\beta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(-[[x_{\sigma}^{\beta},x_{\alpha+\delta}],x_{-\alpha}]-[[x_{-\alpha},x_{\sigma}^{\beta}],x_{\alpha+\delta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[[f(x_{\sigma}^{\beta}),f(x_{\alpha+\delta})],f(x_{-\alpha})]-[[f(x_{-\alpha}),f(x_{\sigma}^{\beta})],f(x_{\alpha+\delta})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\beta+\sigma}\eta_{-\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}(-[[\bar{x}_{\sigma}^{\beta},\bar{x}_{\alpha+\delta}],\bar{x}_{-\alpha}]-[[\bar{x}_{-\alpha},\bar{x}_{\sigma}^{\beta}],\bar{x}_{\alpha+\delta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\beta+\sigma}\eta_{-\beta}\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}([[\bar{x}_{\alpha+\delta},\bar{x}_{-\alpha}],\bar{x}_{\sigma}^{\beta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\eta_{\alpha+\delta}\eta_{-\alpha}\bar{x}^{\alpha}_{\delta},\eta_{\beta+\sigma}\eta_{-\beta}\bar{x}^{\beta}_{\sigma}]=[f(x_{\delta}^{\alpha}),f(x_{\sigma}^{\beta})].

To check that
f
preserves the bracket for remaining terms is clear.
This completes the proof.

\Box

\bar{\lambda}_{j}:=\lambda_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{j}}

\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\sigma_{j}(x)=\omega^{\lambda_{j}}_{j}x\hbox{
for }1\leq j\leq\nu\}.

Let
\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
denote the projection onto
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
. The subalgebra

(7.2)

M(\mathfrak{g},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}):=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes z^{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes z^{\lambda}

of
L(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is called the
\nu
-step multi-loop
algebra
of
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}
based on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Consider the multi-loop algebra
M(\mathfrak{g},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu})
and
assume that automorphisms
\sigma_{i}
’s preserve
\mathfrak{h}
.
As before, we set
{\mathcal{V}}:=\Lambda\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
. We extend each automorphism
\sigma_{i}
of
\mathfrak{g}
to
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}
by

(7.3)

\bar{\sigma}_{i}(x\otimes z^{\lambda}+d+\gamma)=\sigma_{i}(x)\otimes\omega_{i}^{-\bar{\lambda}_{i}}z^{\lambda}+d+\gamma,

for

x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})\in\Lambda
,
d\in{\mathcal{V}}
and

\gamma\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}
. Then

\hat{M}:=\hat{M}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}):=M(\mathfrak{g},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu})\oplus{\mathcal{V}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}

is the fixed point subalgebra
of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
under automorphisms
\bar{\sigma}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\sigma}_{\nu}
. We set
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}:=(\mathfrak{h}^{\bar{0}}\otimes 1)\oplus{\mathcal{V}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}
.

From Section
3
we know that
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
has a root space decomposition with respect to
\mathfrak{h}\otimes 1\oplus{\mathcal{V}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}
. So it has a weight space decomposition with respect to
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}\otimes 1\oplus{\mathcal{V}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}
. Then it follows from
[
30
, Proposition II.1]
that
\hat{M}
also has a weight space decomposition with respect to
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
. We denote the set of weights of
\hat{M}
with respect to
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
by
\hat{R}
. Recall from Section
2
that the form on
\mathfrak{h}
transfers to a form on
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
, and also that the form on
\mathfrak{g}
extends to a non-degenerate symmetric invariant form on
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
, see
(
3.15
).

We denote the set of fixed points of

\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}
on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
by

\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}}
, respectively.
Finally, we denote the map
\pi=\pi_{0}
defined by (
3.2
) corresponding to
\sigma_{i}
by
\pi^{i}
.

Theorem 7.4
.

Suppose that
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R

and
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu})
is an ordered tuple of commuting finite order automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying for

1\leq i\leq\nu
,

-
\sigma_{i}(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h},

-
(\sigma_{i}(x),\sigma_{i}(y))=(x,y)
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

-
C_{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}},

-
(\alpha,\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha))\neq 0
, for some
\alpha\in R.

Then
(\hat{M},(\cdot,\cdot),\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
\hat{R}
, where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the form (
3.15
) on

\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
restricted to
\hat{M}
. Moreover

\hat{R}=\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(R)
, mod Rad
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Proof.
We recall that the form on
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A})
defined by (
3.15
) is symmetric, invariant and non-degenerate.
We use induction
on the number of automorphisms
\sigma_{i}
. For this we fix a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\nu}
of
\Lambda
. Then for
1\leq k\leq\nu
, we set

\begin{array}[]{l}\Lambda_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{i},\\
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}:=\Lambda_{k}\otimes{\mathbb{C}},\\
M_{k}:=M(\mathfrak{g},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}),\\
\hat{M}_{k}:=M_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\star},\end{array}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\mathfrak{g}_{k}:=\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}},\\
\mathfrak{h}_{k}:=\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}},\\
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{k}:=\mathfrak{h}_{k}\otimes 1\oplus{\mathcal{V}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\star}.\end{array}

We consider the natural embeddings
\Lambda_{k}\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{k+1}
,
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}
. We note that each automorphism

\bar{\sigma}_{i}
of
\hat{M}
defined by (
7.3
) restricts to an automorphism
of
\hat{M}_{k}
. We denote the set of weights of
\hat{M}_{k}
with respect to
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{k}
by
\hat{R}_{k}
.

Before starting the induction, we claim that for each
1\leq k\leq\nu
,

(7.5)

(\beta_{k},\pi^{k}(\beta_{k}))\not=0,\hbox{for }\beta_{k}:=\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{k-1}(\alpha),

where
\alpha
is as in the statement. In fact by
3.4
we have

(\beta_{k},\pi^{k}(\beta_{k}))=(\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{k}(\alpha),\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{k}(\alpha)).

On the other hand applying (
3.4
) repeatedly we see that

\displaystyle(\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha),\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi^{2}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha),\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi^{2}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha),\pi^{2}\pi^{1}\pi^{3}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi^{3}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha),\pi^{3}\pi^{1}\pi^{2}\pi^{4}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha,\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{\nu}(\alpha))\neq 0.

Now we get
(\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{k}(\alpha),\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{k}(\alpha))\neq 0
since each
\pi^{i}
maps Rad
(\cdot,\cdot)
into Rad
(\cdot,\cdot)
. So
(\beta_{k},\pi^{k}(\beta_{k}))\neq 0
, as was claimed.

Now if we have just one automorphism, then by Theorem
3.20
,
\hat{M_{1}}=\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma_{1})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
\hat{R}_{1}=\pi^{1}(R)
, mod Rad
(\cdot,\cdot)
. To proceed with the induction step, assume next that
(\hat{M}_{k-1},(\cdot,\cdot),{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}_{k-1})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system

\pi^{1}\cdots\pi^{k-1}(R)
, mod Rad
(\cdot,\cdot)
. We note that our assumptions on
\sigma_{i}
’s guarantee that,

-
\bar{\sigma}_{k}(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{k-1})=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{k-1},

-
(\bar{\sigma}_{k}(x),\bar{\sigma}_{k}(y))=(x,y)
for
x,y\in\hat{M}_{k-1},

-
C_{\hat{M}_{k-1}^{\bar{\sigma}_{k}}}(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{k-1}^{\bar{\sigma}_{k}})\subseteq\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{k-1}^{\bar{\sigma}_{k}}
.

These together with (
7.5
) therefore implies that the automorphism
{\bar{\sigma}}_{k}
and the extended affine Lie algebra

\hat{M}_{k-1}
satisfy (
3.5
). Now since
\hat{M}_{k}=\hat{L}_{\rho_{k}}(\hat{M}_{k-1},\bar{\sigma}_{k}),

where here
\rho_{k}:{\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{k}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{k}}
is given by

\lambda\delta_{k}\mapsto\bar{\lambda}
, we conclude from
Theorem
3.20
that
(\hat{M}_{k},(\cdot,\cdot),\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{k})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with the required root system.
\Box

Example 7.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
be the finite dimensional simple Lie algebra of type
E_{7}
and
\{h_{i},E_{\pm i}\mid 1\leq i\leq 7\}
be the Chevalley generators of
\mathfrak{g}
. We consider the order
2
diagonal automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
defined on the Chevalley generators by

\sigma_{1}(E_{\pm 7})=-E_{\pm 7},\quad\sigma_{1}(E_{\pm i})=E_{\pm i},\quad\text{for}\;i\neq 7,

and also the order
2
automorphism
\sigma_{2}
of
\mathfrak{g}
defined on the Chevalley generators by

\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma_{2}(E_{\pm 1})=E_{\pm 6},\quad\sigma_{2}(E_{\pm 3})=E_{\pm 5},\\
\sigma_{2}(E_{\pm 7})=E_{\mp\theta},\quad\sigma_{2}(E_{\pm i})=E_{\pm i},\quad\text{for}\;i=2,4,\end{array}

where
\theta
is the highest root and
E_{\theta},E_{-\theta}
are root vectors corresponding to highest and lowest roots, respectively. Clearly
\sigma_{i}(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}
, for
i=1,2
. Since
(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta})=\{0\}
unless
\alpha+\beta=0
, then

(\sigma_{i}(x),\sigma_{i}(y))=(x,y),\quad\text{for}\;x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\;i=1,2.

Since
\sigma_{1}(\alpha)=\alpha
, for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we get from
[
4
, Proposition 3.25]
that
C_{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1}}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1}})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1}}
. Next we show that
C_{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}
. First we note that
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}
is the simple Lie algebra of type
F_{4}
, see
[
38
, Section 4]
. So it is enough to prove that
\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}=\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}
. By
[
23
, Lemma 8.1]
,
C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}})
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}})
we have
\mathfrak{h}=C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}})
. If
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}}

\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}

\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}})=\mathfrak{h}
. So
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}=\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}}

\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma_{2}}

(\alpha_{2},\pi^{2}\pi^{1}(\alpha_{2}))=(\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2})\neq 0

Definition 7.8

-

\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}})=\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\lambda}}
, for

,

-

-
\psi(h)=h

.

(

) defines a finite order automorphism

of

.

Proof.

.

Remark 7.11
.

One knows that the centerless cores of almost all extended affine Lie algebras can be realized as multi-loop algebras based on a finite dimensional central simple Lie algebra; the exception is for type

A_{\ell}
(see
[
2
]
and
[
33
]
). The results in Sections
3
and
7

8.
Applications

In this section we present some natural applications of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-forms obtained for EALAs in the previous sections. We always consider those EALAs that admit a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form in this section. First we use these
{\mathbb{Z}}
-forms to define extended affine Lie algebras over fields of positive characteristic then we define certain groups associated to them.

8.1.
EALAs over arbitrary fields

Extended affine Lie algebras are originally defined over the field of complex numbers while, up to a tameness condition, the theory can also be developed over arbitrary fields of characteristic zero (see
[
32
]
). Here we define EALAs over arbitrary fields via
{\mathbb{Z}}
-forms that is a generalization of the same procedure in the classical context.

For this, we show how one can use the integral structure (Chevalley construction) constructed in Section
5
to reduce the coefficients of an extended affine Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
to arbitrary fields.

Assume that
(\mathfrak{g},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathfrak{h})
is a tame extended affine Lie algebra of reduced type and of rank
>1
.
We recall from Section
5
that
the core
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is equipped with an integral structure
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{c}
which is unique up to isomorphism, see Theorem
6.2
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the prime field of characteristic
p
. Since
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is closed under the Lie bracket,
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{F}_{p}}:=\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}
has a Lie algebra structure in a natural way. Now for any field extension
\mathbb{K}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we define
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}:=\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{K}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{K}
. Considering Remark
6.3
, we call (the natural extension of)
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
an
extended affine Lie algebra over
\mathbb{K}.

8.2.
Groups of extended affine Lie type over fields (Adjoint type)

In
[
8
]
we studied certain groups associated to tame reduced extended affine Lie algebras. A method used in
[
8
]
was the integration procedure (see e.g.,
[
21
,
22
,
26
]
). This integration is not applicable while working with fields of positive characteristic. To overcome this issue, one can use the
\mathbb{Z}
-form
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}
(see Theorem
5.8
) to construct groups of extended affine Lie type over arbitrary fields. Also recall from
[
8
, Proposition 4.22]
that the groups that are associated to
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
via integration are isomorphic. Therefore, in this section, we only consider
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
and the group associated to it.

Throughout this subsection, we assume
\mathfrak{g}
to be an EALA with the conditions in
[
8
]
such that there exists a group associated to it via integration. Let
G
denote the group associated to
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
. More precisely, if
\hat{G}:=\ast_{\alpha\in R^{\times}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
denotes the free product of abelian additive groups
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, then
G
is the quotient of
\hat{G}
over the intersection of kernels of all integrable representations of
\mathfrak{g}
(see e.g.,
[
24
]
,
[
21
]
for more details). Recall from
[
8
, §4.2]
that the adjoint form of
G
is defined as follows.

(8.1)

\text{Ad}(G):=\langle\text{exp}(\text{ad}(cx_{\alpha}))~|~\alpha\in R^{\times},c\in{\mathbb{C}}\rangle\leq G\leq\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}).

Also,
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
is integrable in the sense of
[
21
]
and we have

(8.2)

\text{ad}(\text{exp(\text{ad}(y))}(x))=\text{exp}(\text{ad}(x))\text{ad}(y)\text{exp}(-\text{ad}(x)).

In Subsection
8.1
we saw that
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
only depends on
(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{K})
. Therefore, here we consider a fixed
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form and start constructing the desired groups associated to
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
by turning every
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
-module into a
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
-module. For this, we start off by considering
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
as a
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
-module via the adjoint action. Let
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{\alpha}
denote the rank one subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
for every non-isotropic root
\alpha
. Then recall that by
[
8
, Proposition 4.13]
the adjoint map from
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{\alpha}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
integrates to the following map:

(8.3)

\\
\text{Ad}^{\alpha}:\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})^{\alpha}\to\text{Ad}(G)\leq\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}_{c}),

such that for all
t\in{\mathbb{C}}

(8.4)

\text{Ad}^{\alpha}\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&t\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right)\right)=\exp~t~\text{ad}(x_{\alpha}),~~~\text{Ad}^{\alpha}\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
t&1\\
\end{array}\right)\right)=\exp~t~\text{ad}(x_{-\alpha}).

But from the construction of
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, it is clear that if we restrict the above argument to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{\alpha}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

(8.5)

\\
\text{Ad}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\alpha}:\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})^{\alpha}\to\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\leq\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}_{c}),

where

(8.6)

\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{Z}}}:=\langle\text{exp}(\text{ad}(cx_{\alpha}))~|~\alpha\in R^{\times},c\in{\mathbb{Z}}\rangle<\text{Ad}(G)\leq\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}_{c}).

Note that the image of
\text{Ad}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\alpha}
lies in
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
since by Lemma
5.1
,
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is stable under
\text{exp}(\text{ad}(x_{\alpha}))
for
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.

In order to define the adjoint form on arbitrary fields, we consider
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
as a
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
-module via the adjoint action as described above where
{\mathbb{K}}
is considered as an extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
for some prime number
p
. More precisely, the natural adjoint action
\text{ad}^{{\mathbb{K}}}
of
x\otimes r\in\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}
is defined by

\text{ad}(x)(v)\otimes r\cdot s

for every
v\otimes s\in\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{K}.
Now by the same integration procedure as above, we obtain the adjoint form over
\mathbb{K},
namely, we have

(8.7)

\\
\text{Ad}_{{\mathbb{K}}}^{\alpha}:\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{K}})^{\alpha}\to\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}\leq\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}),

where

(8.8)

\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}:=\langle\text{exp}(\text{ad}^{{\mathbb{K}}}(x_{\alpha}\otimes c))~|~\alpha\in R^{\times},c\in{\mathbb{K}}\rangle\leq\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}_{c}^{\mathbb{K}}).

We nominate the resulting group
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}
as the adjoint form of the group associated to
\mathfrak{g}_{c}
over
{\mathbb{K}}.
Note again that the image of
\text{Ad}_{{\mathbb{K}}}^{\alpha}
lies in
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}
by Lemma
5.1
.

Remark 8.9
.

The above procedure suggests a similar construction of groups of extended affine Lie type associated to integrable highest weight representations (for such representations see e.g.,
[
17
,
40
]
). We intent to investigate this in a separate study. Furthermore, note that since the representation theory of extended affine Lie algebras has not been comprehensively characterized by integrable highest weight representations, finding a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form in any integrable representation is another open problem and hence, at this stage, we are not able to define a group of the universal type
G
over arbitrary fields.

Remark 8.10
.

Note that the above construction works for rings as well but in the case that the underlying ring is not Euclidean, instead of
\text{SL}_{2}
one has to work with the group generated by transvections, namely
E_{2}
.

Next we investigate groups of extended affine Lie type over finite fields. For this first recall from
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
that for a unital associative ring
A
, the Steinberg group of type
\dot{R}
over
A
is denoted by
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}(A)
where
\dot{R}
is as in Section
2
. In particular, if
{\mathbb{C}}_{\sigma}
denotes the coordinate ring of
\mathfrak{g}
as defined in Section
3
for some 2-cocycle
\sigma
, then the Steinberg group of type
\dot{R}
over
{\mathbb{C}}_{\sigma}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{C}}_{\sigma})
. We further assume that
\sigma
is elementary, meaning, the image of
\sigma
lies in
\{\pm 1\}
. Therefore, one can readily define
{\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma}
for any field
{\mathbb{K}}.

Lemma 8.11
.

The Steinberg group
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma})
for an elementary 2-cocycle
\sigma
over a finite field
{\mathbb{K}}
with characteristic at least 5, is finitely generated.

Proof.
Let
\nu
be the nullity of
\mathfrak{g}.
One knows from
[
1
, Chapter II]
that the lattice generated by isotropic roots contains a basis
\delta_{i}\in R^{0}
(
1\leq i\leq\nu
), for simplicity of the notation in calculations, let
a_{i}
denote a multiplicative change of variables corresponding to
\delta_{i}
in the coordinate ring
{\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma}
, e.g., we have
a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m}=n\delta_{i}+m\delta_{j}
in this setting. Moreover, since the 2-cocycle is assumed to be elementary, the following calculations are accurate up to a sign and without loss of generality we assume that
\sigma\cong 1
.

First assume
\text{rank}(\dot{R})=1.
We claim that the finite set

\mathcal{{X}}:=\{\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}({\mathbb{K}}\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}a^{n_{i}}_{i})~|{~n_{i}\in\{\pm 1,0\},~\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}}\},

generates
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma})
. First we show that the higher powers
\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{\pm n}_{i})
, (
n>1,1\leq i\leq\nu
) are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
. Higher powers of negative sign can be generated similarly.

Recall from
[
8
]
that for every unit
a\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma}

\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a):=\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a)\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(-a^{-1})\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a).

Now by
(StF
\text{2}^{\prime}
)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-1}_{i})\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-1)\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-1}_{i})^{-1}=\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{2}),

and

\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-1}_{i})\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i})\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-1}_{i})^{-1}=\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{3}).

Then

\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{2})\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)^{-1}=\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i}^{2}),

and

\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{3})\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)^{-1}=\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i}^{3}).

Higher powers can be generated inductively with the above algorithm.
For generating elements
\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m})
(
n,m\in\mathbb{Z},\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}
) by
(StF
\text{2}^{\prime}
)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-n}_{i})\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{j})\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-n}_{i})^{-1}=\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{2n}a_{j}),

and

\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-n}_{i})\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i}a_{j})\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-n}_{i})^{-1}=\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{2n+1}a_{j}).

Therefore, elements
\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j})
(
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
and hence by the relations

\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j})\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)^{-1}=\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i}^{n}a_{j}),

elements
\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j})
(
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
as well.

Again by
(StF
\text{2}^{\prime}
)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-m}_{j})\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i}^{n})\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-m}_{j})^{-1}=\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{2m}),

and

\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-m}_{j})\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i}^{n}a_{j})\hat{n}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{-m}_{j})^{-1}=\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{2m+1}).

Therefore, elements
\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m})
(
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
) are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
and hence by the relations

\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)\hat{x}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m})\hat{n}_{-\dot{\alpha}}(1)^{-1}=\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(-a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m}),

elements
\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m})
(
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
as well. Now note that the above procedure can be applied to any
n
-tuple (
2\leq n\leq\nu
) of elements
a_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq\nu
). Therefore, all higher powers
\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}a^{n_{i}}_{i}
(
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
) are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
.

Assume now that
\text{rank}(\dot{R})>1.
We claim that the finite set

\mathcal{{X}}:=\{\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}({\mathbb{K}}a^{n}_{i})~|~n\in\{\pm 1,0\},~1\leq i\leq\nu,~\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}\},

generates
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma})
. First we show that the higher powers
\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{\pm n}_{i})
, (
n>1,1\leq i\leq\nu
) are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
. Higher powers with negative sign can be generated similarly.

By the locality of our arguments, it suffices to only consider rank 2 cases, namely, types
A_{2}
,
B_{2}
and
G_{2}.
Here we only check type
G_{2}
since the other two types can be checked similarly and they have simpler relations. Also, the structure constants appear here which are modulo the characteristic of
{\mathbb{K}}
. By assuming that the characteristic is at least 5, all the structure constants survive. Let
\dot{\alpha},\dot{\beta}
be a base for
G_{2}.
Then by
(StF2)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

(\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}),\hat{x}_{\dot{\beta}}(c^{-1}_{32}a^{-1}_{i}))=\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{11}c^{-1}_{32})\hat{x}_{2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{21}c^{-1}_{32}a_{i})\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{2}),

and therefore
\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{2})
is generated. Similarly
\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{-2})
can also be generated. Note that the subgroup
\dot{W}:=\langle\hat{n}_{\dot{\gamma}}({\mathbb{K}})~|~\dot{\gamma}\in\dot{R}\rangle
is clearly generated by
\mathcal{{X}}.
Now by
(StF3)
in
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
we can move
a_{i}^{2}
to the entry
\hat{x}_{\dot{\beta}}
and by
(StF2)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

(\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}),\hat{x}_{\dot{\beta}}(c_{11}^{-1}a^{2}_{i}))=\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{3}).

With the above procedure and by the help of the action of
\dot{W}
as in
(StF3)
in
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
all powers of
a^{n}_{i}
for all the entries corresponding to the long roots, namely
\pm\dot{\beta}
,
\pm(3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta})
and
\pm(3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta})
, are generated.

For the entries corresponding to the short roots, again by
(StF2)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

(\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}),\hat{x}_{\dot{\beta}}(c_{21}^{-1}))=\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{11}c_{21}^{-1}a_{i})\hat{x}_{2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{2})\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}}(c_{32}c_{21}^{-1}a_{i}^{3}).

We showed above that
\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}}(c_{32}c_{21}^{-1}a_{i}^{3})
is generated by
\mathcal{X}
, hence
\hat{x}_{2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{2})
is also generated by
\mathcal{X}
and by the action of
\dot{W}
as in
(StF3)
in
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
, the power 2, namely
a_{i}^{2}
is generated for all the entries corresponding to the short roots;
\pm\dot{\alpha}
,
\pm(\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta})
and
\pm(2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta})
and, in particular, for the entry
\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}
. Again by
(StF2)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

(\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{2}_{i}),\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{11}^{-1}a_{i}))=\hat{x}_{2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a^{3}_{i})\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{21}c_{11}^{-1}a_{i}^{5}).

Since we showed that
\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{21}c_{11}^{-1}a_{i}^{5})
is generated by
\mathcal{X}
, therefore, it follows from the above equation that
\hat{x}_{2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a^{3}_{i})
is also generated by
\mathcal{X}.
Now by repeating this procedure and by the help of the action of
\dot{W}
as in
(StF3)
in
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
all powers of
a^{n}_{i}
for all the entries corresponding to the short roots, namely
\dot{\alpha}
,
\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}
and
2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}
, are also generated.

For generating elements
\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m})
(
n,m\in\mathbb{Z},\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{R}
) by
(StF2)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

(\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{n}),\hat{x}_{\dot{\beta}}(c_{11}^{-1}a_{i}^{m}))=\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}}(a_{i}^{n}a_{j}^{m}).

Hence by
(StF3)
in
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
all products
a^{n}_{i}a_{j}^{m}
for all the entries corresponding to the long roots, namely
\dot{\beta}
,
3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}
and
3\dot{\alpha}+2\dot{\beta}
, are generated by
\mathcal{{X}}
. Again by
(StF2)
in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
we have

(\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(a^{n}_{i}),\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{11}^{-1}a_{j}^{m}))=\hat{x}_{2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a^{n}_{i}a_{j}^{m})\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{21}c_{11}^{-1}a_{i}^{2n}a_{j}^{m}).

Now since we showed that
\hat{x}_{3\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(c_{21}c_{11}^{-1}a_{i}^{2n}a_{j}^{m})
are generated by
\mathcal{X}
, therefore, it follows from the above equation that
\hat{x}_{2\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}}(a^{n}_{i}a_{j}^{m})
are also generated by
\mathcal{X}
and hence in view of
(StF3)
in
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
all products
a^{n}_{i}a_{j}^{m}
for all the entries corresponding to the short roots are generated by
\mathcal{X}
as well. The above procedure is valid for every pair
a_{i},a_{j}
(
1\leq i,j\leq\nu
) and we can extend it to
\nu
-tuples step by step.

\Box

Theorem 8.13
.

Let
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}
be the adjoint form of the group of extended affine Lie type associated to an EALA
\mathfrak{g}
of nullity at least one whose coordinate ring 2-cocycle is elementary. Then the following hold:

(i)

The group
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}
is infinite.

(ii)

If
{\mathbb{K}}
is finite with characteristic at least 5, then
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}
is finitely generated.

Proof.

(i) Since every EALA
\mathfrak{g}
of nullity at least one contains certain copies of affine Kac-Moody algebras, the group
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}
contains copies of the corresponding affine Kac-Moody groups. The result then follows from the loop realization of affine Kac-Moody groups.

(ii) Let
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma})
denote the Steinberg group of type
\dot{R}
over
{\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma}
as in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
. By Lemma
8.11
,
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma})
is finitely generated. Now by the similar methods used in
[
8
, Proposition 4.12]
to define an epimorphism from
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{C}}_{\sigma})
to
G
, one can show that the assignment

(8.14)

\varpi(\hat{x}_{\dot{\alpha}}(kc_{\delta}))=\text{Ad}(\exp(\text{ad}(c_{\delta}X_{\dot{\alpha}})\otimes k)),

defines an epimorphism from
\mathfrak{St}_{\dot{R}}({\mathbb{K}}_{\sigma})
to
\text{Ad}(G)_{{\mathbb{K}}}
, hence the result.

\Box

This sets a pretext to generalizing the concept of arithmetic groups in non-Archimedean semisimple groups (see
[
35
]
).

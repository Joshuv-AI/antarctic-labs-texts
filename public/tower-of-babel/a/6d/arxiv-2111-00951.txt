Flatness-based Quadcopter Trajectory Planning and Tracking with Continuous-time Safety Guarantees
Victor Freire, Xiangru Xu

Abstract
This work presents a convex optimization framework for the planning and tracking of quadcopter trajectories with continuous-time safety guarantees. Using B-spline basis functions and the differential flatness property of quadcopters, a second-order cone program is formulated to generate optimal trajectories that respect safe state and input constraints in the continuous-time sense. A quadratic program (QP) based on control barrier functions is proposed to guarantee bounded trajectory tracking in continuous time by filtering a nominal controller, where the QP is shown to be always feasible. Furthermore, conditions that ensure the safe tracking controller respects thrust, roll angle, and pitch angle constraints are also proposed. The effectiveness of the proposed framework is demonstrated by real-world experiments using a Crazyflie2.1 nano quadcopter. The video for the experiments of Section VI is available at https://xu.me.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1196/2021/10/continuous-safety.mp4 .

Although numerous trajectory planning/tracking methods have been proposed for quadcopters, a systematic approach that provides a formal safety guarantee in the continuous-time sense is still largely lacking.

The trajectory planning problem is usually split into finding discrete waypoints that avoid obstacles, and generating a smooth curve that respects the system dynamics through the waypoints. However, a trajectory generated this way is normally only safe at the discrete waypoints without a formal safety guarantee in-between. The inter-sampling safety can be improved by increasing the number of samples at the expense of computation, which works well in practice but lacks a formal continuous-time guarantee

[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14

The main challenge with the flatness-based method lies in the highly nonlinear mapping between state/input spaces and flat output space, which makes the consideration of safety constraints in the state/input space challenging. II-B

B-Spline Curves

B-splines are widely used to parameterize trajectories because of their nice properties and ease of use. For example, the smoothness of B-splines makes the differentiability constraint of the flat trajectory
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\sigma}}(t)
easy to satisfy, the convexity property allows one to explore state and input constraints in continuous time, and the locality property is useful for re-planning.

Given a
knot vector

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\tau}}=(\tau_{0},\ldots,\tau_{v})^{T}
satisfying
\tau_{i}\leq\tau_{i+1}
where
i=0,\ldots,v-1
, a
d
-th degree
B-spline basis
with
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is computed recursively as
[
30
]
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,0}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\tau_{i}\leq t<\tau_{i+1},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,d}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{t-\tau_{i}}{\tau_{i+d}-\tau_{i}}\lambda_{i,d-1}(t)+\frac{\tau_{i+d+1}-t}{\tau_{i+d+1}-\tau_{i+1}}\lambda_{i+1,d-1}(t).

In this work, we consider the
clamped, uniform B-spline basis
, which is defined over knot vectors satisfying:

(clamped)

\displaystyle\tau_{0}=\ldots=\tau_{d},\quad\tau_{v-d}=\ldots=\tau_{v},

(5a)

(uniform)

\displaystyle\tau_{d+1}-\tau_{d}=\ldots=\tau_{N+1}-\tau_{N},

(5b)

where
N=v-d-1
. A
d
-th degree
B-spline curve

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}(t)
is a
m
-dimensional parametric curve built by linearly combining
control points

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}(i=0,\ldots,N)
and B-spline basis functions of the same degree. Noting
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(0)}(t)=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}(t)
, we generate a B-spline curve and its
r
-th order derivative by:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{r,i+1}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d-r}(t)=PB_{r}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d-r}(t),

(6)

where the control points are grouped into a matrix

P=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0}&\dots&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(N+1)},

(7)

the basis functions are grouped into a vector

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d-r}(t)=\left[\lambda_{0,d-r}(t),\dots,\lambda_{N+r,d-r}(t)\right]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+r+1},

(8)

and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{r,j}^{T}
is the
j
-th row of a time-invariant matrix
B_{r}
whose construction is given in Appendix
-A
.

Definition 1
.

The columns of
P^{(r)}\triangleq PB_{r}
are called the
r
-th order virtual control points
(VCPs) of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)
and denoted as
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}^{(r)}
where
i=0,1,\ldots,N+r
, i.e.,

P^{(r)}=PB_{r}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0}^{(r)}&\dots&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N+r}^{(r)}\end{bmatrix}.

(9)

Note that
P^{(0)}=P
and that the first and last
r
columns of
P^{(r)}
are zero vectors because of the form of
B_{r}
.

Some properties of B-splines that will be used in the following sections are listed below (see
[
30
,
31
]
for more details).

P1)

Continuity
. Given a clamped knot vector satisfying (
5a
),
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}(t)
is
C^{\infty}
for any
t\notin\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\tau}}
and
C^{d-1}
for any
t\in\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\tau}}
.

P2)

Convexity
. The B-Spline basis functions have the “partition of unity” property, meaning that:

(i)

\lambda_{i,d}(t)\geq 0
;

(ii)

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\lambda_{i,d}(t)=1,\ \forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

P3)

Local Support
. Any B-spline basis is only nonzero over a local set of knots:
\lambda_{i,d}(t)\neq 0
iff
t\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+d+1})
.

P4)

Strong Convexity
. Segments of the B-spline curve are contained within the convex hull of a limited set of control points. Combining each segment we obtain

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}(t)\in\bigcup_{i=d}^{N}\text{Conv}\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i-d},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\}.

II-C

Control Barrier Functions

The concept of (zeroing) control barrier functions (CBFs) was first proposed in
[
32
]
, and was proven to be a powerful control design method to ensure the safety (in the sense of controlled invariance) of control affine systems
[

,
33
]

[
32
]
. In this work, we will use time-varying CBFs with relative degree 2 and their control sharing property
[
34
]
.

Consider a time-varying affine control system

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}}=f(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})+g(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})u,

(10)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

and

are piecewise continuous in
t
and locally Lipschitz in
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}
.
Given a sufficiently smooth time-varying function
h(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the modified Lie derivative of
h(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})
along
f
as
\bar{L}_{f}^{i}h:=(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L_{f})^{i}h
where
i
is a non-negative integer.

Definition 2
.

[
34
]

Given a control system (
10
), a

with a relative degree

is called a CBF if there exists a column vector

such that for all
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

,

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in U}[L_{g}\bar{L}_{f}^{r-1}h(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})u+\bar{L}_{f}^{r}h(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})+{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{a}}}^{T}\eta(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})]\geq 0,

(11)

where
\eta(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})=(\bar{L}_{f}^{r-1}h,\bar{L}_{f}^{r-2}h,\ldots,h)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
, and the roots of
p_{0}^{r}(\lambda)=\lambda^{r}+a_{1}\lambda^{r-1}+...+a_{r-1}\lambda+a_{r}
are negative reals
-\lambda_{1},...,-\lambda_{r}<0
.

Define a series of functions:

\displaystyle\nu_{0}(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=h(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}),\;\nu_{k}=\Big(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}+\lambda_{k}\Big)\nu_{k-1},\;1\leq k\leq r.

(12)

For any

\mathcal{K}_{h}(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}):=\{u\in U|L_{g}\bar{L}_{f}^{r-1}h(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})u+\bar{L}_{f}^{r}h(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{a}}^{T}\eta(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})\geq 0\}
.

.

[

]

Given a control system (

II-D

Second-order Cone Constraints

A
second-order cone program
(SOCP) is a type of convex optimization problem of the following form
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
:

\displaystyle\min\quad

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{f}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}

(13)

s.t.

\displaystyle\|A_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{i}\|_{2}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{c}}_{i}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}+d_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the optimization variable, and the problem parameters are
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{f}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},A_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{i}-1)\times n},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}-1},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{c}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Constraints of the form:

\|A\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}\|_{2}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{c}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}+d

(14)

are called
second-order cone
(SOC) constraints of dimension
n
. Many commonly-seen convex constraints can be formulated as SOC; for example, ellipsoidal constraints (
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}^{T}Q\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\leq r,\ Q\succeq 0
) and polyhedral constraints (
A\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}
)
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
. SOCP encompasses many types of important convex programs as special cases, such as linear programs, convex QPs and convex quadratically constrained QPs. SOCP can be solved in polynomial time via the interior-point
method and off-the-shelf solvers such as MOSEK exist
[
38
,
39
]
.

III
Continuous-Time Set Inclusion of B-spline Curves & Their Derivatives

In this section, continuous-time convex set inclusion of B-spline curves and their derivatives will be considered. These results provide an intuitive description of the strong convexity property of B-spline curves and their derivatives, which is the key to enforcing the continuous-time state and input constraint satisfaction of quadcopter trajectories.

Lemma 2
.

The
r
-th derivative of a clamped B-spline curve defined as in (
6
) is contained within the convex hull of its
r
-th order virtual control points such that:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)\in\text{Conv}\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0}^{(r)},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N+r}^{(r)}\},\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v}).

(15)

Furthermore, for
d\leq i\leq N
,

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)\in\text{Conv}\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i-d+r}^{(r)},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}^{(r)}\},\quad t\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}).

(16)

By (
16
), condition (
15
) can be reduced to:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)\in\text{Conv}\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{r}^{(r)},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N}^{(r)}\},\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v}).

(17)

Proof.

Applying
the definition of VCP to (
6
) we obtain:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)=P^{(r)}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d-r}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{N+r}\lambda_{i,d-r}(t)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}^{(r)}_{i}.

(18)

Applying P2) of B-splines shown in Section
II-B
, we determine that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)
is a convex combination of
r
-th order VCPs, which is equivalent to (
15
). Using P3) of B-splines we have

\sum_{j=i-d+r}^{i}\lambda_{j,d-r}(t)=1,\quad t\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}),\quad d\leq i\leq N.

Leveraging this result, (
18
) reduces to:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)=\sum_{j=i-d+r}^{i}\lambda_{j,d-r}(t)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}^{(r)}_{j},\forall t\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}),d\leq i\leq N,

which is equivalent to (
16
). Taking the union of all nonempty knot segments in the knot vector
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\tau}}
results in (
17
). ∎

Proposition 1
.

Given a convex set
\mathcal{S}
and the
r
-th derivative of a clamped B-spline curve
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)
defined as in (
6
), if

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(r)}\in\mathcal{S},\quad j=r,\ldots,N

(19)

holds, then
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)\in\mathcal{S},\ t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. If

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}^{(r)}_{j}\in S,\quad j=i-d+r,\dots,i,\quad i\in\{d,\ldots,N\}

(20)

holds, then
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)\in\mathcal{S},\ t\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1})
.

Proof.

From (
17
) and the convexity property, it follows that containing
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)
in a convex set
\mathcal{S}
for
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
can be achieved by containing
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(r)}
in
\mathcal{S}
for
j=r,\ldots,N
. This results in (
19
). Furthermore, containing
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{s}}^{(r)}(t)
in
\mathcal{S}
for a nonempty time interval
[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1})
reduces to containing
d-r+1

r
th order VCPs in
\mathcal{S}
(i.e., those that form the convex hull in (
16
)). This is equivalent with (
20
).
∎

Remark 1
.

Proposition
1
above generalizes Proposition 1 in
[
11
]
from an interval set inclusion to a more general convex set inclusion. This will be key when considering spherical and conic sets to ensure the safety of quadcopters as will be shown in the following sections.

IV
Trajectory Planning with Continuous-Time Safety Guarantee

This section presents a SOCP framework to solve a safe trajectory planning problem that includes various state and input safety constraints, such that the reference trajectory satisfies the safety constraints rigorously in the continuous-time sense.
Since the flat output
\psi
is irrelevant to the state and input constraints considered in this paper, we set it to zero and limit ourselves to finding a sufficiently smooth, position flat output trajectory defined as

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}x(t)&y(t)&z(t)\end{bmatrix}^{T}.

IV-A

Encoding Safety Specifications as SOC Constraints

Consider the following state and input safety constraints that have to be satisfied rigorously in continuous-time:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{p}\cap\mathcal{X}_{v}\cap\mathcal{X}_{\xi},

(21a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{T}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\omega},

(21b)

where the state
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)
and input
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)
are defined in (
2
), and
\mathcal{X}_{p},\mathcal{X}_{v},\mathcal{X}_{\xi},\mathcal{U}_{T},\mathcal{U}_{\omega}
are the constraint sets for the position, linear velocity, roll and pitch angles, total thrust, and angular velocity, respectively. For notation simplicity we first consider (
21
) over the entire time interval
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. The procedure will be to formulate each constraint in flat space and then use Proposition
1
to derive finitely many sufficient conditions for (
21
). In Section
IV-C
, we will discuss how to use Proposition
1
to achieve obstacle avoidance by satisfying the constraints shown in (
21
) on sub-intervals instead. Apart from the continuous-time safety constraints, we will also consider waypoint constraints that have to be satisfied at certain discrete time instances.

IV-A
1
Position Constraints
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}_{p}

The position constraint set
\mathcal{X}_{p}
encodes limited flight volumes as follows:

\mathcal{X}_{p}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}\mid\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}\},

(22)

where
\mathcal{K}_{p}
is a given SOC.
Proposition
1
immediately provides sufficient conditions for
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{p},\ t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}\in\mathcal{K}_{p},\quad j=0,\dots,N.

(23)

Note that we restrict ourselves to SOC sets to preserve the overall SOCP structure that will arise from the rest of the constraints.

IV-A
2
Linear Velocity Constraints
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}_{v}

Limiting flight velocities can reduce the risk of injury or equipment damage. Consider the linear velocity constraint set defined as:

\mathcal{X}_{v}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}:\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}\|_{2}\leq\overline{v}\},

(24)

where
\overline{v}\geq 0
denotes the allowable bound of the maximal speed. Using Proposition 1, the constraint
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{v},\ t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
can be formulated as a constraint on the
1
-st order VCPs:

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(1)}\|_{2}\leq\overline{v},\ j=1,\ldots,N,

which can be expressed equivalently as a SOC constraint on the control points:

\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{1,i+1,j+1}\bigg\|_{2}\leq\overline{v},\quad j=1,\ldots,N,

(25)

where
b_{r,i,j}
is the
(i,j)
-th entry of matrix
B_{r}
.

IV-A
3
Angular Constraints
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\xi}

Providing bounds for the roll and pitch angles can enhance system stability and improve the accuracy of the dynamics model (
1
) by limiting aerodynamic effects. This is especially relevant when implementing controllers that leverage linearizations (e.g., LQR or linear MPC) or small-angle approximations. Consider the angular constraints set defined as follows:

\mathcal{X}_{\xi}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}:|\phi|,|\theta|\leq\epsilon\},

(26)

where
\epsilon<\pi/2
is a given bound for the roll and pitch angles. We begin by exposing the geometric meaning of a result from literature on quadcopter differential flatness.

Lemma 3
.

Given a positive scalar
0<\epsilon<\pi/2
, and the following SOC:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\triangleq\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\|A_{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}\|_{2}\leq p_{3}+g\},

(27)

\displaystyle A_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=|\cot\epsilon|\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{W}&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{W}&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{3\times 1}\end{bmatrix},

if the acceleration signal
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)\in\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
,
then
|\phi(t)|,|\theta(t)|\leq\epsilon
regardless of the yaw angle
\psi(t)
.

Proof.

Let
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)\in\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
and expand the SOC (
27
) to arrive at:

\ddot{x}^{2}(t)\cot^{2}\epsilon+\ddot{y}^{2}(t)\cot^{2}\epsilon\leq(\ddot{z}(t)+g)^{2}.

(28)

Let
k_{1}(t)\triangleq\ddot{x}(t)
,
k_{2}(t)\triangleq\ddot{y}(t)
and
k_{3}(t)\triangleq\ddot{z}(t)+g
. Dividing the inequality above by
\cot^{2}{\epsilon}\neq 0
yields:

\frac{k_{1}^{2}(t)+k_{2}^{2}(t)}{k_{3}^{2}(t)}\leq\tan^{2}\epsilon.

Then, the conclusion follows from Proposition 2 in
[
40
]
.
∎

Note that (
27
) describes the interior of a conic surface in
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}
coordinates with apex at
(0,0,-g)
. We have the following result for angular constraints satisfaction.

Lemma 4
.

Consider B-spline curve
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
defined as in (
6
). If

\displaystyle\bigg\|A_{\epsilon}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{2,i+1,j+1}\bigg\|_{2}\leq g+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{2,i+1,j+1}

(29)

holds for
j=2,\ldots,N
with
A_{\epsilon}
given as in (
27
), then
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{\xi},\forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Proof.

Applying the definition of
2
nd order VCPs given in (
9
) to condition (
29
) yields:

\|A_{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(2)}\|_{2}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(2)}+g,\quad j=2,\dots,N.

(30)

By Proposition
1
, we have:

\|A_{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{(2)}(t)\|_{2}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{(2)}(t)+g,\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v}).

(31)

Since condition (
31
) is equivalent to
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)\in\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
with
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
given as in (
27
), it follows from Lemma
3
that
|\phi(t)|,|\theta(t)|\leq\epsilon
,
\forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
, which means that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{\xi},\forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.
∎

IV-A
4
Thrust Constraints
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathcal{U}_{T}

Upper-bounding the total thrust is needed to prevent actuator saturation, while lower-bounding the total thrust is common in aerospace systems, e.g., to facilitate soft-landing
[
41
]
. Consider the thrust constraints set as follows:

\mathcal{U}_{T}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\mid\underline{T}\leq T\leq\overline{T}\},

(32)

where
\underline{T},\overline{T}
are given constants satisfying
0\leq\underline{T}\leq g\leq\overline{T}
.

Lemma 5
.

Consider B-spline curve
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
defined as in (
6
). If

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{2,i+1,j+1}+g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}\bigg\|_{2}\leq\overline{T},\quad j=2,\ldots,N,

(33a)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{2,i+1,j+1}\geq\underline{T}-g,\quad j=2,\ldots,N,

(33b)

hold, then
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)\in\mathcal{U}_{T},\;\forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Proof.

Applying the definition of
2
nd order VCPs (
9
) to (
33a
) yields:

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(2)}+g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}\|_{2}\leq\overline{T},\quad j=2,\dots,N.

(34)

By Proposition
1
, and expanding the norm shown in (
34
), we have:

\sqrt{\ddot{x}^{2}(t)+\ddot{y}^{2}(t)+\big(\ddot{z}(t)+g\big)^{2}}\leq\overline{T},\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v}),

(35)

which constrains the acceleration to lie within a sphere in
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}
coordinates centered at
(0,0,-g)
. Note that by the flatness map
(
4c
)
, (
35
) is equivalent to
T(t)\leq\overline{T},\ t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. We now apply the same initial steps to (
33b
) to get

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(2)}\geq\underline{T}-g,\quad j=2,\ldots,N,

(36)

and applying Proposition 1 to get

\ddot{z}(t)\geq\underline{T}-g,\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v}),

(37)

which lower-bounds the
z
-component of acceleration. Therefore,

\underline{T}\leq\sqrt{\ddot{x}^{2}(t)+\ddot{y}^{2}(t)+\big(\ddot{z}(t)+g\big)^{2}}=T(t)

(38)

holds over
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

When the inequality
\underline{T}\leq T(t)
is expressed in terms of the acceleration
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)
by (
4c
), it signifies exclusion from a spherical region. As a result, it is a nonconvex constraint in terms of the B-spline control points.
Such a constraint was converted to a mixed-integer constraint in
[
11
]
, and its conservative relaxation was also considered in
[
42
]
. The spherical region (
35
) is bigger than typical quadcopter operation requires: the lower half (
\ddot{z}<-g
) of sphere (
35
) is accessed only when the quadcopter is “upside-down” (
|\phi|\geq\pi/2
or
|\theta|\geq\pi/2
) and this is only required in very aggressive flights. The proof of Lemma
5
reveals that some generally unused feasible region is sacrificed by lower-bounding
\ddot{z}(t)
which results in a lower-bound for
T(t)
.

IV-A
5
Angular Velocity Constraints
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\omega}

Angular velocities are the inputs to the quadcopter model considered and are usually subject to saturation. Limits in angular velocities are also desirable to ensure integrity of gyroscope data. Consider the angular velocity constraints set defined as follows:

\mathcal{U}_{\omega}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:|p|,|q|\leq\overline{\omega}\},

(39)

where
\overline{\omega}
is a given bound for the angular velocities. Recall that
r(t)=0
because of the assumption
\psi(t)=0
.

Lemma 6
.

Consider B-spline curve
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
defined as in (
6
). For any vector
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\zeta}}=(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{N-d+1})^{T}
whose entries are positive constants, if the following conditions:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{2,i+1,j+1}

\displaystyle\geq\zeta_{l-d+1}-g,\quad j=l-d+2,\ldots,l,

(40a)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{3,i+1,j+1}\bigg\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\omega}\ \zeta_{l-d+1},\quad j=l-d+3,\ldots,l,

(40b)

hold for
l=d,\ldots,N
, then
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)\in\mathcal{U}_{\omega},\;\forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Proof.

Applying the definitions of
2
nd order VCPs to (
40a
) and
3
rd order VCPs to (
40b
) yields:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(2)}\geq\zeta_{l-d+1}-g,\quad j=l-d+2,\dots,l,

(41a)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}^{(3)}_{j}\|_{2}\leq\zeta_{l-d+1}\overline{\omega},\quad j=l-d+3,\dots,l.

(41b)

Applying now (
20
) from Proposition
1
allows us to establish:

T(t)\geq\zeta_{l-d+1},\;\|\dddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)\|_{2}\leq\overline{\omega}\ \zeta_{l-d+1},\quad t\in[\tau_{l},\tau_{l+1}),

(42)

which lower-bounds the thrust and upper-bounds the magnitude of the jerk over the
l
-th knot sub-interval. Recalling that the entries of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\zeta}}
are positive, we can combine inequalities to write:

\frac{\|\dddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)\|_{2}}{T(t)}\leq\frac{\|\dddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)\|_{2}}{\zeta_{l-d+1}}\leq\overline{\omega},\quad t\in[\tau_{l},\tau_{l+1}).

Now examine the flat maps (
4d
) and (
4e
) and take the absolute value of both sides to arrive at:

|p|=|-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{B}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathbf{h}}_{\omega}|,\quad|q|=|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{B}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathbf{h}}_{\omega}|.

Since
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{B}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{B}
are unit vectors, we can write
|p|,|q|\leq\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{h}}_{\omega}\|_{2}
by the definition of the dot product. Furthermore, examining
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{h}}_{\omega}
we note that the numerator is a projection of the jerk to a plane perpendicular to the
\ddot{z}
-axis which only reduces its norm, and that the denominator is exactly the thrust
T
. In particular, the following holds:

|p(t)|,|q(t)|\leq\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{h}}_{\omega}(t)\|_{2}\leq\frac{\|\dddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)\|_{2}}{T(t)}\leq\overline{\omega},\quad t\in[\tau_{l},\tau_{l+1}).

Finally, taking
l=d,\ldots,N
and letting
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\tau}}
be a clamped knot vector satisfying (
5a
) allows to establish
|p(t)|,|q(t)|\leq\overline{\omega}
over the whole interval
[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. This completes the proof.
∎

IV-A
6
Waypoint Constraints

Waypoint constraints are commonly used for shaping the trajectory. Assume that there are
n_{wp}
waypoints
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}_{1}^{wp},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}_{n_{wp}}^{wp}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
near which the trajectory must pass at certain discrete times
t_{i},(i=1,\ldots,n_{wp})
. Specifically, the following constrains are enforced for the position:

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t_{i})-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}^{wp}_{i}\|_{2}\leq d_{wp},\quad i=1,\dots,n_{wp},

(43)

where
d_{wp}\geq 0
is the desired radius indicating the closeness of the trajectory to the waypoints. Note that the waypoint constraints are exact when
d_{wp}=0
. Recalling that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
is considered a B-spline curve, constraint (
43
) is a SOC constraint in terms of its control points:

\bigg\|\sum_{j=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}\lambda_{j}(t_{i})-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}_{i}^{wp}\bigg\|_{2}\leq d_{wp},\quad i=1,\dots,n_{wp}.

(44)

IV-B

Safe Trajectory Planning as SOCP

Suppose that the initial (resp. final) conditions of the trajectory are fixed and given as
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}^{0}_{r}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}^{f}_{r}
) where
r=0,\dots,n_{0}
(resp.
r=0,\dots,n_{f}
). For example, the initial (resp. final) position of the trajectory is denoted as
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}_{0}^{0}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}_{0}^{f}
) for
r=0
, and the initial (resp. final) velocity is denoted as
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}^{0}_{1}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}^{f}_{1}
) for
r=1
.
These initial/final constraints can be expressed as:

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{r,i+1}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d-r}(t_{m})=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{p}}^{m}_{r},\quad r=0,\dots,n_{m},

(45)

where
m\in\{0,f\}
represents initial and final labels, respectively, and
0\leq n_{0},n_{f}\leq d
. Note that
n_{0}
may be different from
n_{f}
and that
t_{0}=\tau_{0}
and
t_{f}=\tau_{v}
.

In this work we choose an objective function that minimizes
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{v}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{(4)}(t)\|^{2}_{2}\mathrm{d}t
where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{(4)}(t)
denotes the “snap” of the trajectory
[
6
]
.
Taking
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{(r)}
to be a B-spline curve constructed as in (
6
), the (simple) objective function is given as

J_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N})=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{f}}\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{d,4,i+1}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{d-4}(t)\bigg\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

where the decision variables are the control points
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
of the B-spline curve
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
. By examining (
42
) we observe that it is desirable to make each entry
\zeta_{k}
large to maximize the feasible region. Therefore, we introduce
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\zeta}}
into the objective function such that it is maximized. Specifically, the modified objective function is chosen to be:

J(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\zeta}})=J_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N})-\sum_{k=1}^{N-d+1}\zeta_{k}.

(46)

Summarizing results above, the safe trajectory planning problem can now be expressed as the following SOCP:

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\zeta}}}\quad

\displaystyle J(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\zeta}})

(FLAT-SOCP)

s.t.

\displaystyle\eqref{cp_pos},\eqref{cp_vel},\eqref{cp_ang},\eqref{cp_thrust},\eqref{cp_omega},\eqref{cp_wp},\eqref{extremes}\;\text{hold},

where “FLAT” means that the solutions parameterize the flat output trajectory
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\sigma}}(t)=(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t),0)
. The trajectory in flat space is defined by control points generated from (
FLAT-SOCP
), and the corresponding state/input trajectory satisfies all the safety constraints. This is summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that (
FLAT-SOCP
) has a feasible solution
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
. Let
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
be a B-spline curve defined by the control points
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
. Then, the quadcopter state
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}(t)
and input
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}(t)
trajectories, which are obtained from the flat map (
3
) with
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\sigma}}(t)=(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t),0)
,
satisfy the safety constraints (
21
) for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Problem (
FLAT-SOCP
) can be relaxed to a QP if one replaces the convex regions described by the cone constraints in (
FLAT-SOCP
) by their polytopic inner approximations. For example, for the SOC constraint (
33a
), if we find a polytopic inner approximation as follows:

\{\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:A_{T}\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{T}\}\subset\{\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\eqref{T_sphere}\;\mbox{holds}\},

then we can replace the SOC constraint (
33a
) with the linear constraint

A_{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j}^{(2)}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{b}}_{T},\quad j=2,\dots,N,

in (
FLAT-SOCP
). Other replacements can be done in a similar way for all SOC constraints.

Remark 3
.

The optimization formulation shown in (
FLAT-SOCP
) is based on B-spline parameterization of the flat trajectory, which is more convenient than the parameterization using piece-wise polynomials as in
[
6
]
. By property P1), the smoothness requirement from the flatness map is satisfied automatically by choosing
d\geq 4
, without requiring additional smoothness constraints as in
[
6
]
or objective function reformulation as in
[
7
]
.

Remark 4
.

The feasible region of (
FLAT-SOCP
) may be expanded at the expense of computational burden by increasing
N
(number of control points), which must always respect
v=N+d+1
.

Remark 5
.

In
[
12
]
, the problem of ensuring continuous-time safety for thrust and Euler angles is considered via solving a nonlinear optimization problem, for which only local minimum can be found. In contrast, (
FLAT-SOCP
) is a convex program whose optimal solution can be found in polynomial time.

Remark 6
.

Theorem
1
ensures that the trajectory generated respects the safety constraints (
21
) for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.
However, Proposition
1
provides a framework for satisfying the constraints for
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
as long as we can find two knots
\tau_{i},\tau_{j}
such that
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq[\tau_{i},\tau_{j})
. This type of local constraint satisfaction may be of interest in applications where safety is needed only at certain times as well as in obstacle avoidance which will be shown in the next subsection.

Remark 7
.

Constraint (
40a
) is compatible with (
33a
) and (
33b
). When
\underline{T}
is provided, it follows that
\underline{T}\leq\zeta_{k}\leq\overline{T},k=1,\dots,N-d+1
. In fact, the present formulation may lower-bound
T(t)
more tightly than required to expand the feasible region for (
42
), but never so much that it violates the upper bound.
Additionally, while constraint (
40
) increases optimality by expanding the feasible region, it comes at the expense of computational cost. A possible compromise is by replacing the vector
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\zeta}}
with a scalar
\zeta_{\omega}
in the objective function (
46
) and replacing constraint (
40
) with the following global constraint:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}^{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{2,i+1,j+1}

\displaystyle\geq\zeta_{\omega}-g,\quad

\displaystyle j=2,\dots,N,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{N}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{i}b_{3,i+1,j+1}\bigg\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\omega}\ \zeta_{\omega},\quad

\displaystyle j=3,\dots,N.

IV-C

Obstacle Avoidance with Continuous-time Guarantees via SOCP

The safe flight space is usually non-convex which makes the obstacle avoidance problem difficult
[
14
]
. Obstacle avoidance with continuous-time safety guarantees was studied in
[
13
]
where each obstacle is expressed as a polytope and the trajectory planning problem is formulated as a mixed-integer QP, which is an NP-hard problem and renders the online trajectory re-planning computationally infeasible. In this subsection, we show that, by leveraging the locality property of B-splines, (
FLAT-SOCP
) can be modified to achieve obstacle avoidance with a rigorous continuous-time safety guarantee.

Suppose that the safe flight space
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a set defined as
\mathcal{F}\triangleq\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\cup...\cup\mathcal{O}_{n_{o}}\right)
where
\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the overapproximation of the
i
-th obstacle. Although
\mathcal{F}
is generally nonconvex, mild assumptions about the distribution of the obstacles allow us to determine the existance of convex subsets that, together, form a “safe corridor” in
\mathcal{F}
for the trajectory (see, for example,
[
43
]
). The union of convex subsets can approximate
\mathcal{F}
arbitrarily well. In practice, these convex subsets are easily found with sensors such as stereo cameras and lidars
[
44
]
. The time-optimality of trajectories generated using “safe corridors” remains an active research topic
[
45
,
46
]
.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose that there exist
n_{s}
overlapping convex sets
\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{n_{s}}\}
inside the safe flight space, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}_{l}\subset\mathcal{F},\ l=1,\ldots,n_{s},
satisfying
\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap\mathcal{S}_{i+1}\neq\emptyset,\ i=1,\ldots,n_{s}-1
and consider B-spline curve
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
defined as in (
6
) with
N=n_{s}+d-1
. If the following conditions hold:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{P}}_{j-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{l},\quad j=l,\ldots,l+d,\quad l=1,\ldots,n_{s},

(47)

then
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)\in\mathcal{F}
for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
is at least
C^{d-1},\ \forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
1
to the inclusion constraint of the
l
-th group of control points results in
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)\in\mathcal{S}_{l},\ t\in[\tau_{l+d-1},\tau_{l+d})
, which means that the
l
-th segment of the B-spline curve
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
is contained within
\mathcal{S}_{l}
. Taking now
l=1,\ldots,n_{s}
and recalling that we consider a clamped knot vector (
5a
) results in
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)\in\mathcal{F},\ t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. Further, note that
\mathcal{S}_{l}\cap\mathcal{S}_{l+1}\neq\emptyset
allows
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)
to be continuous despite the segment-wise constraints.
∎

Convex sets
\mathcal{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{n_{s}}
in Proposition
2
play a significant role in the properties of the resulting B-spline curve (such as the magnitude of its derivatives). Finding these sets is considered the task of a high-level planner, and is out of the scope of this work. The reader is referred to
[
45
,
46
]
for more detail. Note that the constraints shown in (
47
) can be readily added to (
FLAT-SOCP
) without compromising the problem structure if the convex sets
\{\mathcal{S}_{l}\}
are restricted to SOC form, as will be demonstrated with experiments in Section
VI
.

Remark 8
.

In Section
V
we will establish safety guarantees for bounded reference position trajectory tracking in the infinity-norm sense. This bound at the tracking level can be incorporated when finding the sets
\{\mathcal{S}_{l}\}
so that obstacle avoidance is guaranteed also during tracking. Alternatively, one can appropriately enlarge the size of obstacles
\{\mathcal{O}_{i}\}
.

V
Trajectory Tracking with Continuous-Time Safety Guarantee

In this section, we propose a trajectory tracking method for quadcopters with position safety guarantees in continuous-time. The idea is to use CBFs as a safety filter to guarantee boundedness between the real trajectory and the nominal safe trajectory
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
generated from (
FLAT-SOCP
). The safe tracking controller is obtained by solving a convex QP online, whose feasibility will be ensured by the control-sharing property of multiple CBFs.

V-A

6-Dim Quadcopter Model & Feedback Linearization

We assume now that the angular velocity dynamics are regulated by some high-bandwidth controller and consider the reduced state and input vectors:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x&y&z&\dot{x}&\dot{y}&\dot{z}\end{bmatrix}^{T},

(48a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T&\phi&\theta&\psi\end{bmatrix}^{T},

(48b)

subject to (
1a
). Then, we can consider a set of virtual inputs
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})^{T}\triangleq(\ddot{x},\ddot{y},\ddot{z})^{T}
found as in (
1a
):

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}=\Psi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}})\triangleq T\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{B}-g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}.

(49)

Note that the map is invertible if we recover the yaw angle such that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}=\Psi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}},\psi)
where
\Psi^{-1}
is given as:

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mu_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}+(\mu_{3}+g)^{2}}=\frac{\mu_{3}+g}{\cos\phi\cos\theta},

(50a)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\arctan\Big(\frac{\mu_{1}\sin\psi-\mu_{2}\cos\psi}{(\mu_{3}+g)\cos\theta}\Big),

(50b)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=\arctan\Big(\frac{\mu_{1}\cos\psi+\mu_{2}\sin\psi}{\mu_{3}+g}\Big).

(50c)

With the virtual inputs, the quadcopter dynamics becomes a double-integrator
[
18
,
23
]
:

\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}}(t)=f(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}(t))+g(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}(t))\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}(t)=A\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}(t)+B\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}(t)

(51)

where

A=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{3}&I_{3}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{3}&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{3}\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{3}\\
I_{3}\end{bmatrix}.

Assume that a reference state
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
and a reference input
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
are generated as in Sec.
IV
and note that we can immediately extract
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
from them. The dynamic feasibility of the reference trajectory will be necessary to establish the forthcoming results. Assume also a
nominal controller

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}=\pi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}})
is given, which is potentially unsafe. In the following subsection, a CBF-QP-based tracking controller will be designed such that the real trajectory lies within a prescribed tube of the nominal trajectory while the tracking control is close to the nominal controller as much as possible.

V-B

Safe Trajectory Tracking via CBF-QP Controller

Consider the following time-varying safe set:

\mathcal{S}_{h}(t)=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}:\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\delta\},\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v}),

(52)

where
\delta>0
represents the maximum allowable deviation in each axis. That is, the trajectory is considered as safe if it is within a
safe tube
of radius
\delta
around the nominal trajectory. Note that it is straightforward to generalize the following results to having different maximum bounds for each axis.

To ensure
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}(t)\in\mathcal{S}_{h}(t),\;\forall t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
, we define the following 6 candidate CBFs for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
:

\begin{bmatrix}h_{\overline{q}}\\
h_{\underline{q}}\end{bmatrix}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\delta+q^{\text{ref}}(t)-q(t)\\
\delta+q(t)-q^{\text{ref}}(t)\end{bmatrix}.

(53)

Note that all functions
h_{\overline{q}},h_{\underline{q}},\ q\in\{x,y,z\}
have relative degree 2. According to Definition
2
, we choose constants
a_{1},a_{2}
such that the roots of
\lambda^{2}+a_{1}\lambda+a_{2}
are reals and negative, and the CBF conditions of relative degree 2 are satisfied for
h_{\overline{q}},h_{\underline{q}},\forall q\in\{x,y,z\}
. For example, the CBF condition for
h_{\overline{x}}
is

\displaystyle L_{g}\bar{L}_{f}h_{\overline{x}}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}+\bar{L}_{f}^{2}h_{\overline{x}}+a_{1}\bar{L}_{f}h_{\overline{x}}+a_{2}h_{\overline{x}}\geq 0,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\phi_{\overline{x}}^{1}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}+\phi_{\overline{x}}^{2}\leq 0,

(54)

where

\displaystyle\phi_{\overline{x}}^{1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{W}^{T},

\displaystyle\phi_{\overline{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\ddot{x}^{\text{ref}}(t)+a_{1}(\dot{x}-\dot{x}^{\text{ref}}(t))+a_{2}(x-x^{\text{ref}}(t)-\delta).

Putting 6 CBF conditions (for
h_{\overline{x}},h_{\underline{x}},h_{\overline{y}},h_{\underline{y}},h_{\overline{z}},h_{\underline{z}}
), which are all linear constraints on
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}
for fixed
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}
, into a QP, and choosing the objective function such that
\|\Psi(\pi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}))-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}\|_{2}^{2}
is minimized, where
\Psi(\pi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}))
yields the nominal virtual inputs, we have the following CBF-QP:

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}}\quad

\displaystyle\|\Psi(\pi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}))-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}\|_{2}^{2}

(CBF-QP)

s.t.

\displaystyle\phi_{\overline{q}}^{1}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}+\phi_{\overline{q}}^{2}\leq 0,

\displaystyle\phi_{\underline{q}}^{1}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}+\phi_{\underline{q}}^{2}\leq 0,\quad q\in\{x,y,z\},

where the virtual input
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}
is the decision variable,

\displaystyle\phi_{\overline{q}}^{1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{W}^{T},

(55a)

\displaystyle\phi_{\overline{q}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)+a_{1}(\dot{q}-\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t))+a_{2}(q-q^{\text{ref}}(t)-\delta),

(55b)

\displaystyle\phi_{\underline{q}}^{1}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}^{T}_{W},

(55c)

\displaystyle\phi_{\underline{q}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)+a_{1}(\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)-\dot{q})+a_{2}(q^{\text{ref}}(t)-q-\delta),

(55d)

with
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{W}
representing the appropriate world-frame’s axis (see Figure
2
). Note that by the dynamic feasibility of the reference trajectory, we can extract
\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)
from:

\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)=A\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}^{\text{ref}}(t)+B\Psi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}^{\text{ref}}(t)),\quad t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v}).

(56)

The (
CBF-QP
) is convex and can be solved in polynomial-time by QP solvers such as MOSEK
[
39
]
or OSQP
[
47
]
. Letting
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)
be the solution of (
CBF-QP
) for any
(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}})\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
, the safe input to the quadcopter is then given as

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)=\Psi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t),\psi(t)),

(57)

where
\psi(t)
is recovered from
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}(t)=\pi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}(t))
.

The following result shows sufficient conditions that ensure the forward invariance of the set
\mathcal{S}_{h}(t)
, and feasibility of the (
CBF-QP
) with multiple CBF constraints.

Theorem 2
.

Consider the system given in (
51
). If constants
a_{1},a_{2}>0
are chosen such that the roots of
\lambda^{2}+a_{1}\lambda+a_{2}
are negative reals
-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2}<0
,
then (
CBF-QP
) is feasible for all
(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}})\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
. Furthermore, if

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(\tau_{0})-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
,
\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(\tau_{0})-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})+\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(\tau_{0})-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0}))\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{1}\delta
,
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)
from (
CBF-QP
) is locally Lipchitz in
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}
, then the input
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)
will render
\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}(t)\in\mathcal{S}_{h}(t)
for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Proof.

The candidate CBFs defined in (
53
) aim to ensure
q^{\text{ref}}(t)-\delta\triangleq\underline{q}(t)\leq q(t)\leq\overline{q}(t)\triangleq q^{\text{ref}}+\delta
, for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
. Recalling that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}=[\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}]^{T}
, the system model shown in (
51
) is equivalent to
\ddot{x}=\mu_{1}
,
\ddot{y}=\mu_{2}
,
\ddot{z}=\mu_{3}
. Thus, the axes are decoupled which enable us to consider each axis individually. It is easy to see that for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
,
\overline{q}-\underline{q}=2\delta
,
\dot{\overline{q}}-\dot{\underline{q}}=0
, and
\ddot{\overline{q}}-\ddot{\underline{q}}=0
. Since
a_{1},a_{2}>0
, we have
\ddot{\overline{q}}-\ddot{\underline{q}}+a_{1}(\dot{\overline{q}}-\dot{\underline{q}})+a_{2}(\overline{q}-\underline{q})=2a_{2}\delta>0
. Therefore, condition (15) in Theorem 2 of
[
34
]
is satisfied, which implies that
h_{\overline{q}},h_{\underline{q}}
are control sharing barrier functions. It is easy to see that functions
h_{\overline{x}},h_{\underline{x}},h_{\overline{y}},h_{\underline{y}},h_{\overline{z}},h_{\underline{z}}
are all control sharing barrier functions, which means that (
CBF-QP
) is feasible for all
(t,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}})\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
. Since
\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(\tau_{0})-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
,
\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(\tau_{0})-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})+\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(\tau_{0})-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0}))\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{1}\delta
, we have
|q(\tau_{0})-q^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})|\leq\delta
,

|\dot{q}(\tau_{0})-\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})+\lambda_{1}q(\tau_{0})-\lambda_{1}q^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})|\leq\lambda_{1}\delta,\ \forall q\in\{x,y,z\}
, which is equivalent to
h_{\overline{q}}(\tau_{0})\geq 0
,
h_{\underline{q}}(\tau_{0})\geq 0
and
\dot{h}_{\overline{q}}(\tau_{0})+\lambda_{1}h_{\overline{q}}(\tau_{0})\geq 0
,
\dot{h}_{\underline{q}}(\tau_{0})+\lambda_{1}h_{\underline{q}}(\tau_{0})\geq 0
for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
. Therefore,
h_{\overline{q}}(t)\geq 0
,
h_{\underline{q}}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
and
q\in\{x,y,z\}
by
[
34
]
, which implies that
\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
holds for
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. The final conclusion follows by the definition of set
\mathcal{S}_{h}(t)
.
∎

Remark 9
.

The feasibility of (
CBF-QP
) can be also seen from the non-emptiness of its admissible input set.
For
q\in\{x,y,z\}
, define the respective admissible input sets corresponding to CBFs
h_{\overline{q}}
and
h_{\underline{q}}
as
\mathcal{K}_{\overline{q}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}\mid\phi_{\overline{q}}^{1}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}+\phi_{\overline{q}}^{2}\leq 0\},\mathcal{K}_{\underline{q}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}\mid\phi_{\underline{q}}^{1}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}+\phi_{\underline{q}}^{2}\leq 0\}
, respectively.
Note that
-\phi_{\overline{q}}^{2}\geq\phi_{\underline{q}}^{2}
holds
\forall q\in\{x,y,z\}
because
-\phi_{\overline{q}}^{2}-\phi_{\underline{q}}^{2}=2a_{2}\delta>0
. Since
\mathcal{K}_{x}\triangleq\mathcal{K}_{\overline{x}}\cap\mathcal{K}_{\underline{x}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}\mid\phi_{\underline{x}}^{2}\leq\mu_{1}\leq-\phi_{\overline{x}}^{2}\}
, we have
\mathcal{K}_{x}\neq\emptyset
. Similarly,

\mathcal{K}_{y}\triangleq\mathcal{K}_{\overline{y}}\cup\mathcal{K}_{\underline{y}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}\mid\phi_{\underline{y}}^{2}\leq\mu_{2}\leq-\phi_{\overline{y}}^{2}\}\neq\emptyset
, and

\mathcal{K}_{z}\triangleq\mathcal{K}_{\overline{z}}\cup\mathcal{K}_{\underline{z}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}\mid\phi_{\underline{z}}^{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq-\phi_{\overline{z}}^{2}\}\neq\emptyset
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{K}_{x}\cap\mathcal{K}_{y}\cap\mathcal{K}_{z}\neq\emptyset
as

\mathcal{K}_{x},\mathcal{K}_{y},\mathcal{K}_{z}
impose constraints on
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}
, respectively. Therefore, the admissible input set of (
CBF-QP
) is always non-empty, which guarantees the feasibility of (
CBF-QP
).

Remark 10
.

The safety guarantee provided by Theorem
2
is predicated on the assumption that the control input generated by the (
CBF-QP
) can be updated continuously. When a sampled-data controller is implemented in practice, it is still possible to provide the same safety guarantee in continuous time, if we make a slight modification to the CBF conditions of (
CBF-QP
). We refer the readers to our recent work
[
48
]
that proposes sampled-data CBFs suitable for real-time computation, and demonstrates the related algorithms using experiments in real hardware.

For the trajectory obtained from (
CBF-QP
), we can also quantify the upper bounds of
\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}
and
\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}
as shown in the following two corollaries.

Corollary 1
.

If the conditions of Theorem
2
are satisfied and
\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(\tau_{0})-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2\delta a_{2}}{a_{1}}
, then

\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2\delta a_{2}}{a_{1}}

(58)

holds for any
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Proof.

Any feasible solution
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}=(\ddot{x},\ddot{y},\ddot{z})^{T}
of (
CBF-QP
) satisfies:

\displaystyle\ddot{q}

\displaystyle\leq\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}+a_{1}(\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}-\dot{q})+a_{2}(q^{\text{ref}}-q+\delta),

(59a)

\displaystyle\ddot{q}

\displaystyle\geq\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}+a_{1}(\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}-\dot{q})+a_{2}(q^{\text{ref}}-q-\delta),

(59b)

for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
. Since
|q-q^{\text{ref}}|\leq\delta
, (
59a
)-(
59b
) are equivalent to:

\displaystyle\ddot{q}

\displaystyle\leq\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}+a_{1}(\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}-\dot{q})+2a_{2}\delta,

(60a)

\displaystyle\ddot{q}

\displaystyle\geq\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}+a_{1}(\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}-\dot{q})-2a_{2}\delta.

(60b)

Define candidate CBFs
\hat{h}_{\overline{q}}
and
\hat{h}_{\underline{q}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{\overline{q}}(t)

\displaystyle\triangleq\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)-\dot{q}(t)+\frac{2\delta a_{2}}{a_{1}},

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{\underline{q}}(t)

\displaystyle\triangleq\dot{q}(t)-\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)+\frac{2\delta a_{2}}{a_{1}}.

Then (
60a
)-(
60b
) are equivalent to

\dot{\hat{h}}_{\overline{q}}(t)+a_{1}\hat{h}_{\overline{q}}(t)\geq 0,\quad\dot{\hat{h}}_{\underline{q}}(t)+a_{1}\hat{h}_{\underline{q}}(t)\geq 0,

for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
and any
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. Therefore,
\hat{h}_{\overline{q}}
and
\hat{h}_{\underline{q}}
satisfy the CBF condition. Furthermore,
since
\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(\tau_{0})-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(\tau_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2\delta a_{2}}{a_{1}}
, we have
\hat{h}_{\overline{q}}(\tau_{0})\geq 0
and
\hat{h}_{\underline{q}}(\tau_{0})\geq 0
for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
. Thus,
\hat{h}_{\overline{q}}(t)\geq 0
and
\hat{h}_{\underline{q}}(t)\geq 0
for
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
by
[
22
]
, which implies that
|\dot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)-\dot{q}(t)|\leq\frac{2\delta a_{2}}{a_{1}}
for
q\in\{x,y,z\}
and
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. The conclusion (
58
) follows immediately.
∎

Corollary 2
.

If the conditions of Theorem
2
are satisfied, then the feasible solution
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}
of (
CBF-QP
) is bounded by:

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 4\delta a_{2},

(61)

for any
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

Proof.

Recall that
\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}(t)-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
by Theorem
2
and
\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}(t)-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\delta a_{2}/a_{1}

1
.
These bounds allow us to upper and lower bound the feasible set of (
CBF-QP
) by:

\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)-4\delta a_{2}\leq\ddot{q}\leq\ddot{q}^{\text{ref}}(t)+4\delta a_{2},

where

. Noticing that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}=(\ddot{x},\ddot{y},\ddot{z})^{T}

∎

V-C

Input Constraints Satisfaction for the CBF-QP Controller

The thrust
T
, the roll angle
\phi
, and the pitch angle
\theta
are related to the decision variable
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}

in (
CBF-QP
) through the relation shown in (
57
). In this subsection, we will consider how the safety constraints on
T,\phi,\theta
considered in Section
IV
can be respected by the CBF-QP-based tracking controller that filters the nominal controller
\pi(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}})
.
Specifically, we aim to guarantee that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)\in\mathcal{V}
where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)
is given in (
57
) and

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathcal{V}_{T}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\xi},

(62a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{T}

\displaystyle\triangleq\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\mid 0\leq T\leq\overline{T}\},

(62b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\xi}

\displaystyle\triangleq\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:|\phi|,|\theta|\leq\epsilon<\pi/2\},

(62c)

with
\epsilon
the upper bound for
|\phi|
and
|\theta|
, and
\overline{T}
the upper bound for
T
. Note that the constraint
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)\in\mathcal{V}
is important to ensure actuator saturation will not happen.

Proposition 3
.

Consider the system given in (
51
) and suppose that the conditions of Theorem
2
are satisfied. If trajectories of the state
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
and the input
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
are dynamically feasible by satisfying (
1a
) and
the following conditions hold for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
:

\displaystyle 0\leq T^{\text{ref}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\overline{T}-4\sqrt{3}\delta a_{2},

(63a)

\displaystyle\|A_{\epsilon}\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ddot{z}^{\text{ref}}(t)+g-4\delta a_{2}(1+|\cot\epsilon|\sqrt{2}),

(63b)

where
A_{\epsilon}=|\cot\epsilon|\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{W}&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{W}&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{3\times 1}\end{bmatrix}
, then
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)\in\mathcal{V}
with
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)
given in (
57
) and
\mathcal{V}
given in (
62a
).

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)
be the solution of (
CBF-QP
). Note that by norm equivalence, (
61
) implies:

\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{3}\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 4\sqrt{3}\delta a_{2},

(64)

for any
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
.

First, consider the thrust element of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)
given as
T(t)=\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)+g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}\|_{2}
by the map
\Psi^{-1}
. By
(
63a
)
we have

T^{\text{ref}}(t)+4\sqrt{3}\delta a_{2}=\|\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)+g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}\|_{2}+4\sqrt{3}\delta a_{2}\leq\overline{T}.

(65)

Therefore,

\displaystyle T(t)-\overline{T}

\displaystyle=\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)+g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}\|_{2}-\overline{T}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)+g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}\|_{2}-\|\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)+g\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}_{W}\|_{2}-4\sqrt{3}\delta a_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}(t)-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)\|_{2}-4\sqrt{3}\delta a_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 0,

where the first inequality is from (
65
), the second inequality is from the reverse triangle inequality, and the third inequality is from (
64
). Thus,
T(t)\leq\overline{T}
for
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. Note that
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
can be extracted from
\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{z}}}^{\text{ref}}(t)
which is given by dynamic feasibility of the reference trajectory.

Next, we examine the roll
\phi(t)
and pitch
\theta(t)
elements of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{s}(t)
. We drop the time-dependence of terms for convenience. Let
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
be given as in (
27
).
Note that

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\mu^{*}_{1}\\
\mu^{*}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\mu^{*}_{1}\\
\mu^{*}_{2}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\ddot{x}^{\text{ref}}\\
\ddot{y}^{\text{ref}}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\ddot{x}^{\text{ref}}\\
\ddot{y}^{\text{ref}}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{2}\delta a_{2}+\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\ddot{x}^{\text{ref}}\\
\ddot{y}^{\text{ref}}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}

(66)

where the first inequality is from the
triangle inequality and the second inequality is from norm equivalence and Corollary
2
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|A_{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\mu}}^{*}\|_{2}=|\cot\epsilon|\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\mu^{*}_{1}\\
\mu^{*}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4|\cot\epsilon|\sqrt{2}\delta a_{2}+\|A_{\epsilon}\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{r}}}^{\text{ref}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ddot{z}^{\text{ref}}-4\delta a_{2}+g

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{3}^{*}+g

where the first inequality is from (
66
) and the definition of
A_{\epsilon}
, the second inequality is from (
63b
), and the third inequality is from the fact that
\ddot{z}^{\text{ref}}\leq\mu_{3}^{*}+4\delta a_{2}
, which can be derived from

for all
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{v})
. This completes the proof.

VII
Conclusions

We presented a framework based on convex optimization that achieves rigorous continuous-time safety for the planning and tracking of quadcopter trajectories. We also showed several experimental examples that demonstrate the usefulness of the presented framework. We highlight that the presented trajectory optimization method can easily generalize to other differentially flat systems, especially when geometric interpretation of the flat-space constraints can be inferred. The presented safe tracking approach can also generalize to other control-affine mechanical systems. In future work, we will explore customized solvers for the SOCP problem involved, and incorporate high-level “safe corridor” finding algorithms into the proposed framework.

Construction of Matrix
B_{r}

The matrix
B_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)\times(N+r+1)}
is only defined when
0\leq r\leq d
, and can be computed from two matrices
M_{d,d-r}
and
C_{r}
such that
B_{r}=M_{d,d-r}C_{r}
; see
[
31
]
for more details.

The matrix
M_{d,d-r}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)\times(N-r+1)}
can be constructed recursively by:

\displaystyle M_{d,d-r}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}I_{N+1},&r=0,\\
\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{M}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\tau}},d,N,i),&1\leq r\leq d,\end{cases}

where each iteration in the product is a right-multiplication and the matrix-valued function
f_{M}:\mathbb{R}^{v+1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(N-i+2)\times(N-i+1)}
is defined as:

\displaystyle f_{M}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\tau}},d,N,i)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-a_{0}&\dots&0\\
a_{0}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&-a_{N-i}\\
0&\ldots&a_{N-i}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{d-i+1}{\tau_{k+d+1}-\tau_{k+i}}.

The matrix
C_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N-r+1)\times(N+r+1)}
is constructed as

C_{r}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{(N+1-r)\times r}&I_{N+1-r}&\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}_{(N+1-r)\times r}\end{bmatrix}

for
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
C_{0}=I_{N+1}
.

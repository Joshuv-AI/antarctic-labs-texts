Actions of compact and discrete quantum groups on operator systems
Lucas Hataishi

Abstract
We introduce the notion of an action of a discrete or compact quantum group on an operator system, and study equivariant operator system injectivity. We then prove a duality result that relates equivariant injectivity with dual injectivity on associated crossed products. As an application, we give a description of the equivariant injective envelope of the reduced crossed product built from an action of a discrete quantum group on an operator system.

Introduction

The Furstenberg boundary
\partial_{F}\Gamma
associated to a discrete group
\Gamma
fits naturally in Hamana’s theory of
\Gamma
-injective envelopes
[
Ham85
]
by observing that
C(\partial_{F}\Gamma)=I_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{C})
. This key insight has led to important advances in understanding

-simple discrete groups
[

]
, and is very suitable to generalisation to the quantum setting. This was first explored in
[
KKV22
]
and later in
[
HHN22
]
. A significant part of these papers is devoted to understanding the notion of equivariant injectivity of a (unital)
C^{*}
-algebra on which a certain quantum group acts.

In this paper, we continue the study of equivariant injectivity for actions of compact and discrete quantum groups. Notions of (equivariant) injectivity are naturally studied in the context of operator systems, so we first introduce the notion of an action of a compact and a discrete quantum group on an operator system, leading us to the notion of equivariant operator system. This offers a unifying and natural framework in which the notion of equivariant injectivity, and by extension other dynamical properties of compact and discrete quantum groups, can be studied. The choice to work with compact and discrete quantum groups instead of with general locally compact quantum groups was a deliberate one: the proofs of the main results in this paper rely on the specific structure of compact quantum groups and their duals. Nevertheless, some of the definitions in this paper can be immediately given on the level of locally compact quantum groups, but we refrained from doing so to keep things more concrete and less technical.

We now give an overview of the content of this paper.

In

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Tensor products of operator systems

Operator systems.

[
Pau02
]
An operator system
X
is a norm-closed, self-adjoint linear subspace
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
where
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space such that
X
contains the unit of
B(\mathcal{H})
. We write
X_{*}:=\{\omega|_{X}:\omega\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}\}
. We will abbreviate (unital) completely positive map by (u)cp map and (unital) completely isometric map by (u)ci map. If
Y\subseteq X
are operator systems, then a map
\varphi:X\to Y
is called ucp conditional expectation if
\varphi
is a ucp map such that
\varphi(y)=y
for all
y\in Y
. An operator system
I
is called injective if for every ucp map
\varphi:X\to I
and every uci map
\iota:X\to Y
, there is a ucp map
\Phi:Y\to I
such that
\Phi\iota=\phi
. Arveson’s extension theorem says that
B(\mathcal{H})
is an injective operator system for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

Given two operator systems, there are several kinds of tensor products that will be important to us. The usual algebraic tensor product of vector spaces will be denoted by
\odot
. The letters
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
will always denote Hilbert spaces and their Hilbert space tensor product is denoted by
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}
. We assume that inner products are linear in the second factor.

Spatial tensor product
.
[
Pau02
, Chapter 12]
Let
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}),Y\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
be operator systems. We define the
spatial tensor product
of
X
and
Y
to be the closed linear span of
X\odot Y
inside
B(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K})
. If
X,Y
are
C^{*}
-algebras, this agrees with the minimal tensor product of
C^{*}
-algebras. If
\phi_{1}:X_{1}\to Y_{1}
and
\phi_{2}:X_{2}\to Y_{2}
are ucp maps, there is a unique ucp map
\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}:X_{1}\otimes Y_{1}\to X_{2}\otimes Y_{2}
that extends
\phi_{1}\odot\phi_{2}:X_{1}\odot Y_{1}\to X_{2}\odot Y_{2}
.

Fubini tensor product
.
[
Ham82
,
Ham11
]
Let
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}),Y\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
be operator systems. We define the
Fubini tensor product

X\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y=\{z\in B(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}):(\omega\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id})(z)\in Y\text{\ and }(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\chi)(z)\in X\text{\ for\ all }\omega\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*},\chi\in B(\mathcal{K})_{*}\}

which is again an operator system. The Fubini tensor product obeys obvious commutativity, associativity and distributivity (with respect to
\ell^{\infty}
-direct sums of operator systems) properties. If either
X
or
Y
is finite-dimensional, then
X\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y
coincides with the algebraic tensor product
X\odot Y
. If
X,Y
are both von Neumann algebras, then the Fubini tensor product is simply the von Neumann tensor product.

If
X_{i}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{i}),i=1,2
are operator systems and
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a ucp map, there is a unique ucp map

\phi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}:X_{1}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}B(\mathcal{K})\to X_{2}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}B(\mathcal{K})

that extends
\phi\odot\operatorname{id}:X_{1}\odot B(\mathcal{K})\to X_{2}\odot B(\mathcal{K})
. Similarly, if
Y_{i}\subseteq B(\mathcal{K}_{i}),i=1,2
are operator systems and
\psi:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
is ucp, we can define the ucp map
\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi:B(\mathcal{H})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y_{1}\to B(\mathcal{H})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y_{2}
.

To make sure that everything works well, we need to impose continuity assumptions. This is illustrated by the following results, which will be used all the time without further mention.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Ham82
, I, Lemma 3.8]

With notations as above and
Y\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
an operator system, we have
(\phi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id})(X_{1}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y)\subseteq X_{2}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y
if either
Y
is
\sigma
-weakly closed or if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are
\sigma
-weakly closed and
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous. A similar result holds for
\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
Ham82
, I, Lemma 3.9]

With notations as above, if
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are
\sigma
-weakly closed and
\psi:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous, then the compositions

(\operatorname{id}_{B(\mathcal{H}_{2})}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi)\circ(\phi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}_{B(\mathcal{K}_{1})}),\quad(\phi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}_{B(\mathcal{K}_{1})})\circ(\operatorname{id}_{B(\mathcal{H}_{1})}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi)

agree on
X_{1}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y_{1}
and map
X_{1}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y_{1}
into
X_{2}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y_{2}
. In this way, we obtain a canonical map
\phi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi:X_{1}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y_{1}\to X_{2}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y_{2}
. If
\phi,\psi
are complete order isomorphisms, then so is
\phi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi
. Thus, as an abstract operator system, the Fubini tensor product only depends on the isomorphism classes of the factors.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}),Y\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
be operator systems with
Y

\sigma
-weakly closed in
B(\mathcal{K})
. Assume that
f\in X^{*}
and
g\in Y_{*}
. There exist canonical slice maps

f\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}_{Y}:X\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y\to Y
and
\quad\operatorname{id}_{X}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}g:X\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y\to X
that extend
f\odot\operatorname{id}:X\odot Y\to Y
and
\operatorname{id}\odot g:X\odot Y\to X
, are norm-bounded, and satisfy

g\circ(f\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}_{Y})=f\circ(\operatorname{id}_{X}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}g).

This allows us to define the product functional
f\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}g:X\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y\to\mathbb{C}
.

Everything then works as expected. For example:

Proposition 2.4
.

[
Ham11
, Proposition 1.2]
Assume
X_{i}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{i})
and
Y_{i}\subseteq B(\mathcal{K}_{i})
are operator systems.
Assume
\varphi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
and
\psi:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
are ucp maps with
\psi

\sigma
-weakly continuous and
Y_{i}

\sigma
-weakly closed in
B(\mathcal{K}_{i})
. If
f\in X_{2}^{*}
and
g\in(Y_{2})_{*}
, then

\displaystyle(f\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}_{Y_{2}})\circ(\varphi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi)=\psi\circ((f\circ\varphi)\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}_{Y_{1}}),\quad(\operatorname{id}_{X_{2}}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}g)\circ(\varphi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\psi)=\varphi\circ(\operatorname{id}_{X_{1}}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}(g\circ\psi)).

We also mention the following result:

Proposition 2.5
.

[
Ham82
, I. Proposition 3.10]
If
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
is an operator system and
Y\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
is a
\sigma
-weakly closed operator system, then
X\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}Y
is injective as an operator system if and only if both
X
and
Y
are injective as operator systems.

If one encounters a Fubini tensor product (possibly with more than two factors) in this paper, it will always be the case that all factors, except possibly the first one, are von Neumann algebras and if tensor product maps between Fubini tensor products are considered, then there will always be
\sigma
-weakly continuous maps acting on these factors. Hence, we do not have to worry too much about technicalities that come with the Fubini tensor product in this paper.

2.2
Compact and discrete quantum groups

Compact quantum groups.

[
Wor98
,
NT13
,
Tim08
]
A compact quantum group (= CQG)
\mathbb{G}
consists of a pair
(C(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
where
C(\mathbb{G})
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
\Delta:C(\mathbb{G})\to C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
a unital
*
-homomorphism satisfying coassociativity
(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta=(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta
and such that the quantum cancellation rules
[\Delta(C(\mathbb{G}))(1\otimes C(\mathbb{G}))]=C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})=[\Delta(C(\mathbb{G}))(C(\mathbb{G})\otimes 1)]
hold.

Every CQG admits a unique state
\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}:C(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}
, called Haar state, satisfying the following invariance conditions

(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varphi_{\mathbb{G}})\Delta(a)=\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}(a)1=(\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(a),\quad a\in C(\mathbb{G}).

If the Haar state

\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}
is a trace,
\mathbb{G}
is said to be a CQG of Kac type. If the Haar state
\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}
is faithful, then
\mathbb{G}
is called reduced.

With respect to the state
\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}
, we can consider the GNS-triplet
(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),\lambda,\xi_{\mathbb{G}})
where
\lambda:C(\mathbb{G})\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
is a
*
-representation and
\xi_{\mathbb{G}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
the cyclic vector such that
\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}=\langle\xi_{\mathbb{G}},\lambda(-)\xi_{\mathbb{G}}\rangle
. We will also consider the GNS-map
\Lambda:C(\mathbb{G})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G}):a\mapsto\lambda(a)\xi_{\mathbb{G}}
and we define
C_{r}(\mathbb{G})=\lambda(C(\mathbb{G}))
.

A unitary finite-dimensional (= ufd) representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}
consists of a pair
(\mathcal{H}_{\pi},U_{\pi})
where
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and
U_{\pi}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
a unitary such that
(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)(U_{\pi})=U_{\pi,12}U_{\pi,13}
in
B(H)\otimes C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
. Given
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, we define the matrix coefficient

U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta):=(\omega_{\xi,\eta}\otimes\operatorname{id})(U_{\pi})
where
\omega_{\xi,\eta}
is the vector functional
x\mapsto\langle\xi,x\eta\rangle.
We will write
n_{\pi}:=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
. If
\{e_{i}^{\pi}\}_{i=1}^{n_{\pi}}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, then

\Delta(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(\xi,e_{i}^{\pi})\otimes U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},\eta).

If
\pi,\chi
are ufd representations, then a linear map
T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
is called intertwiner if
(T\otimes 1)U_{\pi}=U_{\chi}(T\otimes 1)
and we write
\operatorname{Mor}(\pi,\chi)
for the set of such intertwiners. A ufd representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}
is called irreducible if
\operatorname{Mor}(\pi,\pi)
is one-dimensional. If
\pi,\chi
are ufd representations, then
U_{\pi\boxtimes\chi}:=U_{\pi,13}U_{\chi,23}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\chi})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
defines the tensor product representation
\pi\boxtimes\chi
. We will fix, once and for the remainder of this paper, a maximal family
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})
of pairwise non-equivalent irreducible representations.
The space linearly spanned by all matrix coefficients is denoted by
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. It is norm-dense in
C(\mathbb{G})
,
\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}
is faithful on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
and
(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
forms a Hopf
∗
-algebra. A Hamel basis for
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
is constructed as follows: let
\{e_{i}^{\pi}\}_{i=1}^{n_{\pi}}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
for every
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})
. Then
\{U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},e_{j}^{\pi}):\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G}),1\leq i,j\leq n_{\pi}\}
is a Hamel basis for
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. Counit and antipode act on matrix coefficients by

\epsilon(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle,\quad S(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=U_{\pi}(\eta,\xi)^{*}.

If
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})
, there exists a canonical positive invertible operator
Q_{\pi}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
such that
\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{\pi}^{1/2})=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{\pi}^{-1/2})
. We write
\dim_{q}(\pi)
for the latter value and the following orthogonality relations hold:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta)U_{\chi}(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})^{*})=\delta_{\pi,\chi}\frac{\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\eta^{\prime},Q_{\pi}^{-1/2}\eta\rangle}{\dim_{q}(\pi)},

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta)^{*}U_{\chi}(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))=\delta_{\pi,\chi}\frac{\langle\xi^{\prime},Q_{\pi}^{1/2}\xi\rangle\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle}{\dim_{q}(\pi)}.

Given
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})
, fix once and for all an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}^{\pi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
so that the operator
Q_{\pi}
becomes diagonal, say
Q_{\pi}e_{i}^{\pi}=q_{\pi,i}e_{i}^{\pi}
. We then write
u_{ij}^{\pi}:=U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},e_{j}^{\pi})
and the orthogonality relations take the form

\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{ij}^{\pi}(u_{kl}^{\chi})^{*})=\frac{\delta_{\pi,\chi}\delta_{i,k}\delta_{j,l}q_{\pi,j}^{-1/2}}{\dim_{q}(\pi)},\quad\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}((u_{ij}^{\pi})^{*}u_{kl}^{\chi})=\frac{\delta_{\pi,\chi}\delta_{i,k}\delta_{j,l}q_{\pi,i}^{1/2}}{\dim_{q}(\pi)}.

We also define the family of Woronowicz characters
\{\delta^{z}:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}\}_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
uniquely determined by

(\operatorname{id}\odot\delta^{z})(U_{\pi})=Q_{\pi}^{-z/2},\quad\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G}).

With the CQG
\mathbb{G}
, we can associate the two multiplicative unitaries
V_{\mathbb{G}},W_{\mathbb{G}}\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
given by

V_{\mathbb{G}}(\Lambda(a)\otimes\Lambda(b))=(\Lambda\odot\Lambda)(\Delta(a)(1\otimes b)),\quad W_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}(\Lambda(a)\otimes\Lambda(b))=(\Lambda\odot\Lambda)(\Delta(b)(a\otimes 1))

where
a,b\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. One has the relations

V_{\mathbb{G}}(\lambda(a)\otimes 1)V_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}=(\lambda\odot\lambda)(\Delta(a))=W_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}(1\otimes\lambda(a))W_{\mathbb{G}},\quad a\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})

and the comultiplication

\Delta:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):x\mapsto V_{\mathbb{G}}(x\otimes 1)V_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}

restricts to a comultiplication

\Delta:C_{r}(\mathbb{G})\to C_{r}(\mathbb{G})\otimes C_{r}(\mathbb{G}):x\mapsto V_{\mathbb{G}}(x\otimes 1)V_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}=W_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}(1\otimes x)W_{\mathbb{G}}.

The pair
(C_{r}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
then defines a reduced CQG
\mathbb{G}_{r}
and should be thought of as a ‘reduced version of
\mathbb{G}
’. For most purposes, it suffices to work with the reduced CQG. We have
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{r})=\lambda(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}))
. The counit
\epsilon_{r}
for
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{r})
satisfies
\epsilon_{r}\circ\lambda=\epsilon
and similarly for the antipode
S_{r}
and Haar state
\varphi_{\mathbb{G}_{r}}
. The von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):=\lambda(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}))^{\prime\prime}
carries the coproduct

\Delta:\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):x\mapsto V_{\mathbb{G}}(x\otimes 1)V_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}=W_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}(1\otimes x)W_{\mathbb{G}}

which extends the coproduct on
C_{r}(\mathbb{G})
. The vector state
\omega_{\xi_{\mathbb{G}}}:\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}:x\mapsto\langle\xi_{\mathbb{G}},x\xi_{\mathbb{G}}\rangle
is faithful and satisfies the invariance properties

(\omega_{\xi_{\mathbb{G}}}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id})\Delta=\omega_{\xi_{\mathbb{G}}}(-)1=(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\omega_{\xi_{\mathbb{G}}})\Delta

on
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
.

Discrete quantum groups.
We will introduce discrete quantum groups in the von Neumann algebra setting using duality theory for multiplier Hopf
*
-algebras
[
KD97
]
. The pair
(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
is a multiplier Hopf
∗
-algebra for which the duality theory in
[
Van98
]
applies. We therefore obtain a new dual multiplier Hopf
∗
-algebra
(c_{c}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}),\widetilde{\Delta})
where

c_{c}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}})=\{\varphi_{\mathbb{G}}(-a):a\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})^{\prime}=M(c_{c}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}))

and refer to
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
as a discrete quantum group (= DQG). We define dual GNS maps

3
Actions of quantum groups

In this section, we define actions of compact and discrete quantum groups on operator systems, establish their basic properties and show that these definitions generalise the definitions of actions known in the literature. Then, we define the reduced crossed product and the Fubini crossed product with respect to an operator system on which a DQG acts, and show that these crossed products admit actions of the dual CQG. These actions are then studied.

This holds for all positive invariant functionals

. It then follows that the multiplicative domain of

is

, as desired.∎

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(1\otimes s^{*}\otimes 1)(\alpha_{\pi}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id})((\alpha_{\rho}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id})(x))(1\otimes 1\otimes t)

\mathcal{G}
that converges to
\varphi

in this topology. If
\omega\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}})_{*}
and

, we see that

\displaystyle(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\omega)((\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\hat{\Delta})\varphi(y))

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\in I}(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\omega)((\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\hat{\Delta})(\varphi_{i}(y)))

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\in I}(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\omega)((\varphi_{i}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id})(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\hat{\Delta})(y))

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}((\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\omega)(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\hat{\Delta})(y))

\displaystyle=\varphi((\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\omega)(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\hat{\Delta})(y))

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\omega)((\varphi\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{id})(\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\hat{\Delta})(y))\ ,

so we conclude that

\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant and thus

.

Cyclic matrices and polynomial interpolation over division rings
Vladimir Bolotnikov

Abstract
. As is well known, any complex cyclic matrix A A is similar to the unique companion matrix associated with the minimal polynomial of A A . On the other hand, a cyclic matrix over a division ring 𝔽 \mathbb{F} is similar to a companion matrix of a polynomial which is defined up to polynomial similarity. In this paper we study more rigid canonical forms by embedding a given cyclic matrix over a division ring 𝔽 \mathbb{F} into a controllable or an observable pair. Using the characterization of ideals in 𝔽 ⁡ [ z ] \mathbb{F}[z] in terms of controllable and observable pairs we consider ideal interpolation schemes in 𝔽 ⁡ [ z ] \mathbb{F}[z] which merge into a polynomial interpolation problems containing both left and right interpolation conditions.



1.
Introduction

Given a complex matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and a vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the sets

\mathbb{I}_{A}:=\{p\in\mathbb{C}[z]:\;p(A)=0\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{I}_{A,\bm{v}}:=\{p\in\mathbb{C}[z]:\;p(A)\bm{v}=0\}

are ideals in the ring
\mathbb{C}[z]
of complex polynomials; their respective (monic) generators
\bm{\mu}_{A}
and

\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
are called the
minimal polynomial
of the matrix
A
and the
minimal polynomial
of the pair

(A,\bm{v})
, respectively. As
\mathbb{I}_{A}\subseteq\mathbb{I}_{A,\bm{v}}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}

divides
\bm{\mu}_{A}
.

A matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
cyclic
if there exists a (cyclic) vector

\bm{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
{\rm span}\{\bm{v},A\bm{v},\ldots,A^{n-1}\bm{v}\}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, i.e., the
controllability matrix

\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{v}&A\bm{v}&\ldots&A^{n-1}\bm{v}\end{bmatrix}

is invertible (equivalently,
\deg(\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}})=n
). In this case, we say that the pair
(A,\bm{v})
is

controllable
.
Cyclic matrices and controllable pairs can be characterized in interpolation terms as follows.

Proposition 1.1
.

(1)
The matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is cyclic if and only if for any
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
commuting with
A
,
there is an
f\in\mathbb{C}[z]
such that
f(A)=B
.

(2)
The pair
(A,\bm{v})
with
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is controllable if and only if for any
{\bf b}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, there is an

f\in\mathbb{C}[z]
such that
f(A)\bm{v}={\bf b}
.

The first objective of this paper is to study possible extensions of these results as well as of some other characterizations of
complex cyclic matrices recalled in Proposition
1.2
below to the non-commutative
setting of a division ring
\mathbb{F}
. To fix notation, we let
{\bf e}_{j}
to denote the
j
-th column of the
n\times n
identity matrix

{\bf I}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf e}_{1}&\ldots&{\bf e}_{n}\end{bmatrix}
(occasionally writing

{\bf e}_{j,n}
if the dimension is not clear from the context). We will use notation

\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z):=\begin{bmatrix}1&z&\cdots&z^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}

(1.1)

to identify a polynomial
g(z)=\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z){\bf g}
with the column
{\bf g}
of its coefficients.
In terms of this notation, we recall the
companion matrix

C(f)
of a monic polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[z]
:

C(f)=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf e}_{2}&{\bf e}_{3}&\ldots&{\bf e}_{n}&-{\bf f}\end{bmatrix}\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;f(z)=z^{n}+\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z){\bf f}.

(1.2)

Proposition 1.2
.

Given a matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is cyclic.

(2)

\deg{\bm{\mu}}_{A}=n
, where
\bm{\mu}_{A}
is the minimal polynomial of
A
.

(3)

A
is similar to a (unique) companion matrix (which is
C(\bm{\mu}_{A})
).

(4)

A
is similar to a two-diagonal matrix

\Gamma=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\gamma_{1}&0&\ldots&0\\
1&\gamma_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&&1&\gamma_{n}\end{array}\right]

(1.3)

with diagonal entries equal to zeros (roots) of the polynomial
\bm{\mu}_{A}
.

(5)

The Jordan form of
A
contains only one Jordan block corresponding to each eigenvalue
(which is a zero of
\bm{\mu}_{A}
).

If this is the case, then
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}(z)=\bm{\mu}_{A}(z)=\det(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)
for any cyclic vector
\bm{v}
of
A
.

In the division-ring setting, it is still true that the matrices (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are
cyclic. The rest requires certain adjustments. First, the minimal polynomial
\bm{\mu}_{A}

(more precisely, left and right minimal polynomials; see (
2.4
)) is not similarity invariant;
besides, simple examples show that its degree can be different from the dimension of
A
.
The polynomial
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
seems to be more suitable as it is invariant under
similarity of controllable pairs (see Definition
2.4
below) and
the equality
\deg(\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}})=n
is equivalent to
{\bf v}
be a (right) cyclic vector for
A
.
Although different cyclic vectors
{\bf v},{\bf v}^{\prime}
of
A
may lead to different minimal polynomials
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
and

\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}^{\prime}}
, the latter polynomials are
similar
:
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}\approx\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}^{\prime}}
(see Section 4.1
for the precise definition). Thus, in a context that does not distinguish similar polynomials, we may write
\mathfrak{P}_{A}
rather than
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
.
Then the well-known fact that two cyclic complex matrices are similar if and only if their minimal polynomials are equal, extends
to the non-commutative setting as follows:

cyclic matrices
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
are similar if and only if
\mathfrak{P}_{A}\approx\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime}}
.

However, there is a more rigid extension in terms of controllable pairs:

two controllable pairs
(A,{\bf v})
and
(A^{\prime},{\bf v}^{\prime})
are similar if and only if

\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}=\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},{\bf v}^{\prime}}
(see Theorem
3.3
below).

In a similar manner, part (3) in Proposition
1.2
extends to
\mathbb{F}
-setting in two ways:

(1)

A cyclic matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
is similar to the companion matrix
C(\mathfrak{P}_{A})
.

(2)

For a fixed cyclic
vector
{\bf v}
of
A
, the controllable pair
(A,{\bf v})
is similar to a
unique
pair of the form
(C(f),{\bf e}_{1})

(with
f=\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
).

A related result (Theorem
4.1
) describes the similarity class of a given polynomial

p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
in terms of cyclic vectors of the companion matrix
C(p)
. Similarity of a cyclic matrix to two-diagonal or
block-diagonal matrices is discussed in Section 4.2.

Our second goal is to study interpolation problems of Hermite-Lagrange type in
\mathbb{F}[z]
. In contrast to the commutative case,
polynomials over a division ring can be evaluated on the left and on the right. Simple examples are given by left and right
Lagrange interpolation problems where one is seeking an
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
with prescribed left (right) values at given points.
Solution sets for homogeneous problems are right (left) ideals in
F[z]
, and under mild the assumption that the set of interpolation
nodes is
P
-independent (see Definition
3.8
) the problems have unique low-degree solutions. In Section 5 we consider
more general interpolation problems with interpolation conditions given in terms of left and right tangential evaluation calculi induced
by respectively, controllable and observable pairs. In Section 6 we consider the combined (two-sided) problem containing both left and right
interpolation conditions. The solution set of the homogeneous version of this problem is the intersection of a left and a right ideal in

\mathbb{F}[z]
, while the nonhomogeneous problem may have no solutions as well as multiple low-degree solutions. Two-sided Lagrange
problem is presented in Section 6 as an illustrative particular case.

2.
Preliminaries

Given a division ring
\mathbb{F}
, let
\mathbb{F}[z]
denote the ring of polynomials
in one formal variable
z
which commutes with coefficients from
\mathbb{F}
.
Since the division algorithm holds in
\mathbb{F}[z]
on
either side, any ideal (left or right) in
\mathbb{F}[z]
is principal. We will write
\langle f\rangle_{\bf r}

and
\langle f\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}
for the right and the left ideal generated by
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]

dropping the subscript if the ideal is two-sided (i.e., left and right simultaneously).
The intersection of two left (right) ideals is a left (right)
ideal; the
least right
and
left common multiples

{\bf lrcm}(f,g)
and
{\bf llcm}(f,g)

of two monic polynomials
f,g\in\mathbb{F}[z]
are defined as generators of the respective ideals

\langle f\rangle_{\bf r}\cap\langle g\rangle_{\bf r}=\langle{\bf lrcm}(f,g)\rangle_{\bf r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle f\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}\cap\langle g\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}=\langle{\bf llcm}(f,g)\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}.

(2.1)

If we let
Z_{\mathbb{F}}
to denote the center of
\mathbb{F}
, then

Z_{\mathbb{F}[z]}=Z_{\mathbb{F}}[z]
, and consequently, any ideal generated by an element of
Z_{\mathbb{F}}[z]
is two-sided.
The converse is also true: the generator of any two-sided ideal of
\mathbb{F}[z]
is in
Z_{\mathbb{F}}[z]
. Indeed,
the left and the right (monic) generators of the ideal must be multiples of each other and therefore, they coincide.
On the other hand if
p
is a left and right generator, then it commutes with each
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
which implies that
its coefficients are in
Z_{\mathbb{F}}
; see e.g.,
[
5
, Proposition 2.2.2]
for details.

2.1.
Minimal polynomials

Any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
can be evaluated at a matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}

on the left and on the right (by interpreting
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
as an
\mathbb{F}
-bimodule)
as follows:

f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\sum A^{j}f_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=\sum f_{j}A^{j},\quad\mbox{if}\quad f(z)=\sum f_{j}z^{j}.

(2.2)

Since for any
f,g\in\mathbb{F}[z]
and
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
,

(gf)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\sum A^{j}g^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)f_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(fg)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=\sum f_{j}g^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)A^{j},

(2.3)

it follows that
(gf)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=0
and
(fg)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=0
whenever

g^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=0
and
g^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=0
, respectively. Hence, the sets

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf r}}&:=\{p\in\mathbb{F}[z]:\;p^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=0\}=\langle\bm{\mu}_{A,\bm{\ell}}\rangle_{\bf r},\\
\mathbb{I}_{A,\bm{\ell}}&:=\{p\in\mathbb{F}[z]:\;p^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=0\}=\langle\bm{\mu}_{A,{\bf r}}\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}\end{array}

(2.4)

are respectively, a right and a left ideal in
\mathbb{F}[z]
; their generators
\bm{\mu}_{A,\bm{\ell}}
and

\bm{\mu}_{A,{\bf r}}
will be called the
left
and the
right minimal polynomial
of
A
, respectively.

If
\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf r}}
contains a non-zero polynomial
p\in Z_{\mathbb{F}}[z]
such that
p(A)=0
, then
the set of all such polynomials form the maximal two-sided ideal contained in
\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf r}}\cap\mathbb{I}_{A,\bm{\ell}}

and its generator
\bm{\mu}_{A}\in Z_{\mathbb{F}}[z]
is called the
minimal (central) polynomial of
A
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
A=\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
, then evaluations (
2.2
) amount to left and right “point” evaluations of
f
at
\alpha
:

f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha)=\sum\alpha^{j}f_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(\alpha)=\sum f_{j}\alpha^{j}.

(2.5)

An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
is called a left or right zero of a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
if

f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha)=0
or
f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(\alpha)=0
, respectively. The ideals
\mathbb{I}_{\alpha,{\bf r}}
and
\mathbb{I}_{\alpha,\bm{\ell}}
(
2.4
)
of polynomials vanishing at
\alpha
on the left and on the right respectively, are
generated by the linear monic polynomial
\bm{\mu}_{\alpha,\bm{\ell}}=\bm{\mu}_{\alpha,{\bf r}}=\bm{\rho}_{\alpha}
, where

\bm{\rho}_{\alpha}(z):=z-\alpha\quad(\alpha\in\mathbb{F}).

(2.6)

In other words,

f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha)=0\;\Leftrightarrow\;f\in\langle\bm{\rho}_{\alpha}\rangle_{\bf r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(\alpha)=0\;\Leftrightarrow\;f\in\langle\bm{\rho}_{\alpha}\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}.

The existence of the minimal central polynomial
\bm{\mu}_{\alpha}\in Z_{\mathbb{F}}[z]
is the definition of
\alpha
being algebraic over

Z_{\mathbb{F}}
.

Given a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
and given a matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
and vectors

{\bf v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}
and
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
,
one can apply evaluations (
2.2
) to polynomials
{\bf v}f\in\mathbb{F}[z]^{n\times 1}
and
f\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}[z]^{1\times n}

as follows:

({\bf v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\sum A^{j}{\bf v}f_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=\sum f_{j}\bm{u}A^{j}.

(2.7)

Due to equalities

\displaystyle({\bf v}gf)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum A^{j}\cdot({\bf v}g)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)\cdot f_{j}=\big(({\bf v}g)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)f\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A),

(2.8)

\displaystyle(fg\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum f_{j}\cdot(g\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)\cdot A^{j}=\big(f(g\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A),

holding for all
f,g\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, the sets

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf v}}&:=\{p\in\mathbb{F}[z]:\;({\bf v}p)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=0\}=\langle\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}\rangle_{\bf r},\\
\mathbb{I}_{\bm{u},A}&:=\{p\in\mathbb{F}[z]:\;(p\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=0\}=\langle\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}\end{array}

(2.9)

are respectively a right and a left ideal in
\mathbb{F}[z]
; their generators are called the minimal polynomials of the
input pair

(A,\bf v)
and of the
output pair

(\bm{u},A)
, respectively.

Straightforward calculations show that for any

A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
,
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}
,
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
, and
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\bm{v}f(z)&=(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)\cdot(L_{A}(\bm{v}f))(z)+({\bf v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A),\\[5.69054pt]
f(z)\bm{u}&=(R_{A}(f\bm{u}))(z)\cdot(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)+(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A),\end{array}

(2.10)

where
({\bf v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)
and
(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)
are defined as in (
2.7
)
and where
L_{A}(\bm{v}f)
and
R_{A}(f\bm{u})
are vector polynomials given by

L_{A}(\bm{v}f)=\sum_{j+k=0}^{\deg f-1}A^{j}\bm{v}f_{k+j+1}z^{k},\quad R_{A}(f\bm{u})=\sum_{j+k=0}^{\deg f-1}f_{k+j+1}\bm{u}A^{j}z^{k}.

(2.11)

Remark 2.2
.

Representations (
2.10
) are unique in the following sense: if

\bm{v}f(z)=(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)G(z)+{\bf b}\quad\mbox{for some}\quad G\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}[z],\quad{\bf b}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times 1}n,

then necessarily (by comparing the coefficients in the polynomial identity above),
G=L_{A}(\bm{v}f)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\bf b}=(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)
.
The second representation in (
2.10
) is unique in a similar sense.

Ideals (
2.9
) can be characterized in terms of evaluations (
2.8
) as follows.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
,
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}
,
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
and
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
,

(1)

({\bf v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=0
if and only if
\bm{v}f=(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)\cdot G
for some
G\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}[z]
.

(2)

(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=0
if and only if
f\bm{u}=H\cdot(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)
for some
H\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}[z]
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in the scalar-valued case
[
7
, Theorem 1]
. The “only if” parts follow from equalities
(
2.10
). Conversely, comparing the coefficients
in the polynomial identity

\bm{v}f(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\bm{v}f_{k}z^{k}=(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}G_{i}z^{i}=(z{\bf I}_{n}-A)G(z)

leads us to equalities

\bm{v}f_{0}=-AG_{0},\quad\bm{v}f_{k}=G_{k-1}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\bm{v}f_{j}=G_{j-1}-AG_{j}

for
j=1,\ldots,k-1
, which in turn imply

({\bf v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}A^{j}\bm{v}f_{j}=-AG_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}A^{j}(G_{j-1}-AG_{j})+A^{k}G_{k-1}=0.

The proof of part (2) is similar.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let us say that two input pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
(two output pairs
(\bm{u},A)
and
(\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
)
are
similar
if
A^{\prime}=TAT^{-1}
and
\bm{v}^{\prime}=T\bm{v}
(
\bm{u}^{\prime}=\bm{u}T^{-1}
)
for some invertible matrix
T\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
.

The next observation follows from Definition
2.4
and formulas (
2.7
), (
2.9
).

Remark 2.5
.

(1) If the input pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
are similar with the similarity matrix
T
,
then
(\bm{v}^{\prime}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A^{\prime})=T\cdot(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)
and hence
\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf v}}=\mathbb{I}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}

and
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}=\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},{\bf v}^{\prime}}
.

(2) If the output pairs
(\bm{u},A)
and
(\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
are similar,
then
(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=(f\bm{u}^{\prime})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A^{\prime})\cdot T

and hence
\mathbb{I}_{\bm{u},A}=\mathbb{I}_{\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf u},A}=\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf u}^{\prime},A^{\prime}}
.

2.2.
Explicit formulas for minimal polynomials.

To compute
\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
, we first find the
least integer
d
such that the vectors
{\bf v},A{\bf v},\ldots,A^{d}{\bf v}
are (right) linearly dependent and then conclude from
the relation

A^{d}{\bf v}+A^{d-1}{\bf v}b_{d-1}+\ldots+A{\bf v}b_{1}+{\bf v}b_{0}=0

(2.12)

that

\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}(z)=z^{d}+{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}z^{k}b_{k}}.

The construction of
\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}
is similar.
As for the minimal polynomials
\bm{\mu}_{A,\bm{\ell}}
and
\bm{\mu}_{A,{\bf r}}
in (
2.4
), let us observe that

\bm{\mu}_{A,\bm{\ell}}={\bf lrcm}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf e}_{k}}:\,1\leq k\leq n),\quad\bm{\mu}_{A,{\bf r}}={\bf llcm}(\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}^{\top}_{k},A}:\,1\leq k\leq n),

(2.13)

where
{\bf e}_{1},\ldots,{\bf e}_{n}
denote the columns in the identity matrix
{\mathbf{I}}_{n}
. Indeed, since both
0
and
1
belong to
the center of
\mathbb{F}
, it follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.7
) that

({\bf e}_{k}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A){\bf e}_{k}\quad\mbox{for}\quad k=1,\ldots,n,

so that
\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf r}}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf e}_{k}}
, by (
2.4
) and (
2.9
).
Writing the latter equality in terms of generators we get the first equality in (
2.13
); the second equality follows similarly.
We illustrate the above recipe by computing minimal polynomials of the two-diagonal matrix
\Gamma
as in (
1.3
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
be of the form

\Gamma=\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}}=\big[\delta_{i,j}\gamma_{j}+\delta_{i-1,j}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n},\quad\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n})\subset\mathbb{F}^{n},

(2.14)

(
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker symbol) and let
{\bf e}_{k}
be the
k
-th column of
{\bf I}_{n}
. Then

\mathfrak{P}_{\Gamma,{\bf e}_{k}}=\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k}}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k+1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}\quad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{k}^{\top},\Gamma}=\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{2}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k}}

(2.15)

for all
k=1,\ldots,n
, and consequently, the left and right minimal polynomials of
\Gamma
are given by

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\Gamma,{\bm{\ell}}}

\displaystyle={\bf lrcm}\big(\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}},\;\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{2}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}},\ldots,\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n-1}}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}},\;\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}\big),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\Gamma,{\bf r}}

\displaystyle={\bf llcm}\big(\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}},\;\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n-1}},\ldots,\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{2}},\;\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\big).

Proof.

We first observe from (
2.14
) that

\Gamma{\bf e}_{j}={\bf e}_{j}\gamma_{j}+{\bf e}_{j+1}\quad(j=1,\ldots,n-1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma{\bf e}_{n}={\bf e}_{n}.

(2.17)

Therefore, for any fixed
k\leq n
, the vectors
{\bf e}_{k},\Gamma{\bf e}_{k},\ldots,\Gamma^{n-k}{\bf e}_{k}
are right
linearly independent. By the recipe (
2.12
), it suffices to find a monic polynomial
f
with
\deg f=n-k
subject to
condition
({\bf e}_{k}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)=0
to claim that
\mathfrak{P}_{\Gamma,{\bf e}_{k}}=f
. We next show that

f=\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k}}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k+1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}
is such a polynomial. To this end, we write equalities
(
2.17
) in terms of the left evaluation (
2.8
) as

({\bf e}_{j}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{j}})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)={\bf e}_{j+1}\quad(j=1,\ldots,n-1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad({\bf e}_{n}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)=0.

Upon making use of the first formula in (
2.8
) and taking into account the latter equalities for
j=k,\ldots,n
we get

\displaystyle\big({\bf e}_{k}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k}}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k+1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\big(({\bf e}_{k}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k}})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k+1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\big({\bf e}_{k+1}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{k+1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\ldots=\big({\bf e}_{n}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\Gamma)=0,

which verifies the first part in (
2.15
). The second part is verified similarly via writing relations

{\bf e}_{j}^{\top}\Gamma={\bf e}_{j}\gamma_{j}+{\bf e}_{j-1}\quad(j=2,\ldots,n)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\bf e}_{1}\Gamma=\gamma_{1}{\bf e}_{1}

in terms of the evaluation (
2.8
) as

(\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{j}}{\bf e}_{j}^{\top})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(\Gamma)={\bf e}_{j-1}\quad(j=2,\ldots,n)\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}{\bf e}_{1}^{\top})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(\Gamma)=0

and then making use of the second formula in (
2.8
). Formulas (
2.16
) follow from (
2.15
) by the general principle (
2.13
).
∎

2.3.
Companion matrices

For a monic polynomial
p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, the associated
left
and

right companion matrices
are defined as

C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\ldots&0&-p_{0}\\
1&0&\ldots&0&-p_{1}\\
0&1&\ldots&0&-p_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&1&-p_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}=C_{\bf r}(p)^{\top},\quad p(z)=z^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}z^{k}.

(2.18)

By
[
17
, Theorem A2]
, two matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
are similar over a unital ring
\mathbb{F}
if and only if
the pencils
z{\bf I}_{n}-A
and
z{\bf I}_{n}-B
are
equivalent
over
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}[z]
(i.e., one of them can be
transformed into another by elementary row and column operations). Combining this result with the observation that
the pencils
z{\bf I}_{n}-C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
and
z{\bf I}_{n}-C_{\bf r}(p)
are both equivalent over
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}[z]
to the
diagonal polynomial matrix
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}{\bf I}_{n-1}&0\\
0&p(z)\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, leads to the conclusion (see
[
17
]
) that

C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)\sim C_{\bf r}(p)\quad\mbox{for any monic}\quad p\in\mathbb{F}[z].

(2.19)

Remark 2.7
.

For a monic
p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
and associated companion matrices
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
and
C_{\bf r}(p)
(as in (
2.18
)),

\bm{\mu}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),\bm{\ell}}=\bm{\mu}_{C_{\bf r}(p),{\bf r}}=\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf e}_{1}}=\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{1}^{\top},C_{\bf r}(p)}=p.

(2.20)

Indeed, recalling
{\bf e}_{j}
, the
j
-th column of
{\bf I}_{n}
and observing that

C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{k-1}{\bf e}_{1}={\bf e}_{k}\quad(k=1,\ldots,n)\quad\mbox{and}\quad C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{n}{\bf e}_{1}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\bf e}_{j}p_{j},

(2.21)

we see that the minimal right linearly dependent set
\{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{j}{\bf e}_{1}\}_{j=1}^{d}
occurs for
d=n
, and
the relation

C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{n}{\bf e}_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{n-j}{\bf e}_{1}p_{n-j}=0

means that
\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf e}_{1}}=p
. Multiplying the latter equality by

C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{k}
on the left (
k=1,\ldots,n-1
) we get, on account of (
2.21
),

\displaystyle 0=C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{n}{\bf e}_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{n-j}{\bf e}_{k}p_{n-j}

\displaystyle=\bigg(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{n-j}p_{n-j}\bigg){\bf e}_{k}

\displaystyle=p^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)){\bf e}_{k},

where the second equality holds since
{\bf e}_{k}\in Z_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Since

p^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)){\bf e}_{k}=0
for
k=1,\ldots,n
, it follows that

p^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))=0
, so that
p\in\langle\bm{\mu}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bm{\ell}}}\rangle_{\bf r}
.
Since
p=\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf e}_{1}}
is a divisor of
\bm{\mu}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bm{\ell}}}
, it follows that

\bm{\mu}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bm{\ell}}}=p
. The rest of (
2.19
) is verified similarly
.

Remark 2.8
.

Although in general,
\bm{\mu}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf r}}
and
\bm{\mu}_{C_{\bf r}(p),{\bm{\ell}}}
are not equal to
p

(quite expectedly), they are divisible by
p
on the right and on the left, respectively. The latter follows from the general principle
(
2.13
) and equalities

\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{n}^{\top},C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)}=\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bf r}(p),{\bf e}_{n}}=p

which are verified as in the proof of Proposition (
2.6
).

We next specify formulas (
2.7
) and (
2.11
) for the case where
A
is a companion matrix (we will need them in Section 6). To this end, we recall
the backward-shift operator
R_{0}
acting on
\mathbb{F}[z]
by the rule

R_{0}:\;\sum_{j=0}^{N}f_{j}z^{j}\to\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{j+1}z^{k}.

(2.22)

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
and
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
be defined as in (
2.18
).
Given any
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, let us divide it by
p
on the left:

f(z)=p(z)g(z)+b(z),\quad g\in\mathbb{F}[z],\quad b(z)=b_{0}+b_{1}z+\ldots+b_{n-1}z^{n-1},

(2.23)

and let us define the polynomials

g_{j}=(R_{0}^{j}p)\cdot g+R_{0}^{j}b\quad\mbox{for}\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

(2.24)

Then
g_{n}=g
and

({\bf e}_{1}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))={\bf b}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}b_{0}\\
b_{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{n-1}\end{array}\right],\quad L_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)}({\bf e}_{1}f)=G:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}\\
g_{2}\\
\vdots\\
g\end{array}\right].

(2.25)

Proof.

Since
p
is monic and
\deg p=n
, we have
R_{0}^{n}p=1
. Since
\deg b<n
, we have
R_{0}^{n}b=0
. Then for
j=n
, the formula
(
2.24
) gives
g_{n}=g
. By definitions (
2.24
), we have

\displaystyle zg_{j+1}(z)+p_{j}g(z)+b_{j+1}

\displaystyle=z(R_{0}^{j+1}p)(z)g(z)+(R_{0}^{j}b(z)+p_{j}g(z)+b_{j}

\displaystyle=\big[z(R_{0}^{j+1}p)(z)+p_{j}\big]g(z)+(R_{0}^{j}b)(z)+b_{j}

\displaystyle=(R_{0}^{j}p)(z)g(z)g+R_{0}^{j}b=g_{j}(z)

for all
j=1,\ldots,n-1
. The latter
n-1
equalities along with (
2.23
) can be written in the matrix form as

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}f(z)\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{array}\right]=\begin{bmatrix}z&0&\ldots&0&p_{0}\\
-1&z&\ldots&0&p_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&-1&z+p_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}(z)\\
g_{2}(z)\\
\vdots\\
g_{n}(z)\end{bmatrix}+\left[\begin{array}[]{c}b_{0}\\
b_{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{n-1}\end{array}\right].

Writing this identity in the form

\;{\bf e}_{1}f(z)=(z{\bf I}_{n}-C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))G(z)+{\bf b},\;

we arrive at both formulas in (
2.25
), by Remark
2.2
.
∎

The same arguments lead us to the right-sided analogues of formulas (
2.25
) which are recorded below for
future references.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{F}[z]

(\deg q=k
) and
C_{\bf r}(p)\in\mathbb{F}^{k\times k}
be defined as in (
2.18
).
Given any
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, let us divide it by
q
on the right

f(z)=h(z)q(z)+d(z),\quad h\in\mathbb{F}[z],\quad d(z)=d_{0}+d_{1}z+\ldots+d_{n-1}z^{k-1},

and let us define the polynomials

h_{j}=h\cdot(R_{0}^{j}q)+R_{0}^{j}d\quad\mbox{for}\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

(2.26)

Then
h_{k}=h
and

(f{\bf e}^{\top}_{1})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(C_{\bf r}(q))={\bf d}:=\left[d_{0}\;\ldots\;d_{n-1}\right],\quad L_{C_{\bf r}(q)}(f{\bf e}_{1}^{\top})=\left[h_{1}\;\ldots\;h\right].

3.
Controllable and observable pairs

For
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
and
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}
, the input pair

(A,\bm{v})
is called
controllable
, if its
controllability matrix

\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{v}&A\bm{v}&\ldots&A^{n-1}\bm{v}\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{is
invertible}.

(3.1)

In this case, the vector
\bm{v}
is called a
right cyclic vector
for
A
. For similar input pairs
(A,\bm{v})

and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
(as in Definition
2.4
), we have from (
3.1
)

T\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{v}^{\prime}&TAT^{-1}\bm{v}^{\prime}&\ldots&TA^{n-1}T^{-1}\bm{v}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=\mathfrak{C}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}},

from which we see that controllability is similarity-invariant.

The concept of controllability goes back to
[
10
]
; the discrete time-invariant linear system

x(k+1)=Ax(k)+{\bm{v}}w(k),\quad x(0)=x_{0}

is called controllable, if for any preassigned
x(m)\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, there exists the input sequence
w(0),\ldots,w(m-1)\in\mathbb{F}

transferring the given initial state
x(0)
into
x(m)
. The latter turns out to be equivalent to the controllability matrix (
3.1
)
be invertible.

The concept of observability is dual to that of controllability: given
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
and
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
,
the discrete time-invariant linear system

x(k+1)=Ax(k),\quad y(k)=\bm{v}x(k),\quad x(0)=x_{0}

is called
observable
if any preassigned output sequence
y(0),\ldots,y(n-1)
can be generated by an appropriate initial state
x(0)
.
The latter holds if and only if the observability matrix of the pair
(\bm{u},A)
is invertible which we adopt as the definition
of observability:

Given
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
and a row vector
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
,
the output pair
(\bm{u},A)
is called
observable
if its
observability matrix

\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},A}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\bm{u}\\
\bm{u}A\vskip-2.84526pt\\
\vdots\\
\bm{u}A^{n-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\quad\mbox{is
invertible}.

(3.2)

In this case,
\bm{u}
is called a
left cyclic vector
for
A
. For similar output pairs
(\bm{u},A)

and
(\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
, we have
\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},A}T^{-1}
and hence,
observability is similarity-invariant.

Remark 3.1
.

It follows from the definition (
3.1
) that an input pair
(A,{\bm{v}})
with
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}

is controllable if and only if the least integer
d
for which (
2.12
) holds equals
d=n
, i.e., if and only if

\deg\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}=n
. Similarly, an output pair
({\bf u},A)
is observable if and only if

\deg\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf u},A}=n
.

Explicit formulas for minimal polynomials of controllable and observable pairs are given below.

Proposition 3.2
.

(1)
If the pair
(A,\bm{v})
(with
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
) is controllable, then its minimal polynomial is given by the formula

\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}(z)=z^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}z^{k}b_{k}=z^{n}-\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z)\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A^{n}\bm{v},

(3.3)

(where
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is given by (
1.1
)), and furthermore,

\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}=C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}),\quad\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}{\bf v}={\bf e}_{1},

i.e., the pair
(A,\bm{v})
is similar to the pair
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}),{\bf e}_{1})
.

(2)
If the pair
(\bm{u},A)
(with
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
) is observable, then

\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}(z)=z^{n}-\bm{u}A^{n}\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},A}^{-1}\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z)^{\top},

(3.4)

and furthermore, the pair
(\bm{u},A)
is similar to the pair
({\bf e}_{1}^{\top},C_{\bf r}(\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}))
, as

\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},A}A\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},A}^{-1}=C_{\bf r}(\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A})\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\bf u}\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},A}^{-1}={\bf e}_{1}^{\top}.

Proof.

If the pair
(A,{\bf v})
is controllable, equality (
2.12
) holds with
d=n
and the coefficients
b_{k}

are defined by the formula

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}b_{0}\\
\vdots\\
b_{n-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]=-\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A^{n}\bm{v}.

(3.5)

The formula (
3.3
) is now immediate. By (
3.1
),
\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}{\bf v}={\bf e}_{1}
and furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}A\bm{v}&\ldots&A^{n-1}\bm{v}&A^{n}\bm{v}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf e}_{2}&\ldots&{\bf e}_{n-1}&\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A^{n}\bm{v}\end{bmatrix}=C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}})

(3.6)

where
C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}})
is the left companion matrix of the polynomial (
3.3
).
The second part is verified similarly.
∎

By Remark
2.5
, similar pairs have the same minimal polynomial. For
controllable or observable pairs, we have the converse.

Theorem 3.3
.

Controllable pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
(observable pairs
(\bm{u},A)
and
(\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
) are similar
in the sense of Definition
2.4
if and only if their minimal polynomials

\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}
(
\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime}}
)
are equal.

Proof.

The “only if” part is contained in Remark
2.5
. To justify the “only if part”,
let us assume that the minimal polynomials
\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}
of
two controllable pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
are equal. Then the matrices
A
and
A^{\prime}

have the same dimensions (equal to
\deg\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}=\deg\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}=n
).

The controllability matrices
{\mathfrak{C}}_{A,\bm{v}}
and
{\mathfrak{C}}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}

are both invertible, and we may let
T:={\mathfrak{C}}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{C}}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}
.
We next show that

{A}^{\prime j}\bm{v}^{\prime}=TA^{j}\bm{v}\quad\mbox{for}\quad j=0,\ldots,n.

(3.7)

Indeed, comparing the corresponding columns in the matrix equality

T{\mathfrak{C}}_{A,\bm{v}}={\mathfrak{C}}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}

gives equalities (
3.7
) for
j=0,\ldots,n-1
.
From the formula (
3.5
) for coefficients of
\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}
and from the similar formula for
coefficients of
\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}
we conclude (since

\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}=\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}
) that

{\mathfrak{C}}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A^{n}\bm{v}={\mathfrak{C}}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}^{-1}A^{\prime n}\bm{v}^{\prime}
, which can be written
equivalently as
A^{\prime n}\bm{v}^{\prime}=TA^{n}\bm{v}
thus justifying equality (
3.7
) for
j=n
.
Letting
j=0
in (
3.7
) gives
\bm{v}^{\prime}=T\bm{v}
, while all other equalities in (
3.7
) imply

A^{\prime}{\mathfrak{C}}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}\bm{v}^{\prime}&\ldots&A^{\prime n}\bm{v}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}TA\bm{v}&\ldots&TA^{n}\bm{v}\end{bmatrix}=TA{\mathfrak{C}}_{A,\bm{v}},

which is the same as
A^{\prime}=TAT^{-1}
, by the definition of
T
. Thus, the pairs

(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
are similar. The statement concerning observable pairs follows by similar arguments.
∎

3.1.
{\mathbb{I}}_{A,\bm{v}}
and
{\mathbb{I}}_{\bm{u},A}
as generic right and left ideals

As a consequence of Theorem
3.3
,
it follows that any right or left ideal in
\mathbb{F}[z]
is necessarily of the form (
2.9
), and that under
controllability/observability assumption the representing pair
(A,\bm{v})
or
(\bm{u},A)
is unique up to similarity.

Proposition 3.4
.

(1) Any right ideal
{\mathbb{I}}\subset\mathbb{F}[z]
is of the form

{\mathbb{I}}={\mathbb{I}}_{A,\bm{v}}:=\{p\in\mathbb{F}[z]:\;({\bf v}p)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=0\}=\langle\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}\rangle_{\bf r}

for some controllable pair
(A,\bm{v})
. Controllable pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})

define the same ideal
{\mathbb{I}}_{A,\bm{v}}={\mathbb{I}}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}
if and only if they are similar.

(2) Any left ideal
{\mathbb{I}}\subset\mathbb{F}[z]
is of the form

{\mathbb{I}}=\mathbb{I}_{\bm{u},A}:=\{p\in\mathbb{F}[z]:\;(p\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(A)=0\}=\langle\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}

for some observable pair
(\bm{u},A)
. Observable pairs
(\bm{u},A)
and
(\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime})

define the same ideal
{\mathbb{I}}_{\bm{u},A}={\mathbb{I}}_{\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime}}
if and only if they are similar.

Proof.

Given a right ideal
\mathbb{I}\subset\mathbb{F}[z]
, let
f
be its generator.
The pair
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1})
is controllable and since

\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1}}=f
(by (
2.20
)), we have

\mathbb{I}=\langle f\rangle_{\bf r}=\langle\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1}}\rangle_{\bf r}={\mathbb{I}}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1}}.

By Theorem
3.3
, two controllable pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
are similar
if and only if their minimal polynomials are equal, i.e.,

{\mathbb{I}}_{A,\bm{v}}=\langle\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}\rangle_{\bf r}=\langle\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}\rangle_{\bf r}={\mathbb{I}}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}.

This completes the proof of part (1). Part (2) follows similarly.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Any two-sided ideal
{\mathbb{I}}\subset\mathbb{F}[z]
is of the form
{\mathbb{I}}={\mathbb{I}}_{A,\bm{v}}

for a unique (up to similarity) controllable pair
(A,\bm{v})
such that
\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A^{n}\bm{v}\in Z_{\mathbb{F}}^{n\times 1}
.

Alternatively, any two-sided ideal
{\mathbb{I}}\subset\mathbb{F}[z]
is of the form
{\mathbb{I}}=\mathbb{I}_{\bm{u},A}

for a unique (up to similarity) observable pair
(\bm{u},A)
such that
\bm{u}A^{n}\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},A}^{-1}\in Z_{\mathbb{F}}^{1\times n}
.

By Propositions
3.2
and
3.4
, the latter statement asserts that
any two-sided ideal is a left or right ideal generated by a polynomial in
Z_{\mathbb{F}}[z]
, which is obviously true.

We record several concrete examples of controllable and observable pairs that have already appeared above.

Example 3.6
.

For any monic
p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
of degree
n
, the pairs
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf e}_{1})
and
(C_{\bf r}(p),{\bf e}_{n})

are controllable, while the pairs
({\bf e}_{1},C_{\bf r}(p)
and
({\bf e}_{n},C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
are observable
(since their minimal polynomials equal
p
and
\deg p=n
)
.

Example 3.7
.

If
\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
is of the form (
2.14
), then the pair
(\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}},{\bf e}_{1})
is controllable,
while the pair
({\bf e}_{n}^{\top},\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}})
is observable (since their minimal polynomials are of degree
n
, by formulas (
2.15
)
for
k=1
and
k=n
, respectively).

For the next example, we recall the notion of polynomial independence (
P
-independence) introduced in
[
11
]
;
see also
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

Definition 3.8
.

A set
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{F}
is called
left (right)
P
-independent

if the monic linear polynomials
\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{n}}
are left (right) coprime.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
be diagonal and let
{\bf v}={\bf e}_{1}+\ldots+{\bf e}_{n}
:

A=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}&&0\vskip-2.84526pt\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&\alpha_{n}\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\bf v}=\begin{bmatrix}1\vskip-2.84526pt\\
\vdots\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

(3.8)

The pair
(A,{\bf v})
is controllable (the pair
(\bm{v}^{\top},A)
is observable)
if and only if the set
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
is left (right)
P
-independent.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.7
), that for every polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
,

f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\begin{bmatrix}f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha_{1})&&0\vskip-2.84526pt\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha_{n})\end{bmatrix},\quad({\bf v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\begin{bmatrix}f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha_{1})\vskip-2.84526pt\\
\vdots\\
f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha_{n})\end{bmatrix},

(3.9)

and hence, the ideals
\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf r}}=\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf v}}
consist of all polynomials that vanish on the left at

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
. Thus,

\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf r}}=\mathbb{I}_{A,{\bf v}}={\displaystyle\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{i}}\rangle_{\bf r}}
and subsequently,

\bm{\mu}_{A,\bm{\ell}}=\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}={\bf lrcm}(\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{n}}).

(3.10)

One can see from the definition (
3.1
) that the controllability matrix of the pair (
3.8
) is equal to the
left Vandermonde matrix

\mathfrak{C}_{A,{\bf v}}=V_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{\alpha}):=\left[\alpha_{i}^{j-1}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n},\quad\bm{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}).

This matrix is invertible (i.e., the pair
(A,{\bf v})
is controllable) if and only if
\deg\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}=n

(we recall that
\deg\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
equals the maximal number of leftmost right linearly independent columns in

\mathfrak{C}_{A,{\bf v}}
). Due to (
3.10
),
\deg\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}=n
if and only if the polynomials

\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{n}}
are left coprime, i.e., the set
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
is left
P
-independent.
The statement concerning the pair
(\bm{v}^{\top},A)
is justified similarly.
∎

The polynomials that are (left or right) minimal polynomials of an algebraic set in a division ring
are called
Wedderburn polynomials
; we refer to
[
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
for the thorough account on the subject.
Since Wedderburn polynomials can be characterized as least common multiples of coprime monic linear polynomials,
the formula (
3.10
) asserts that the pair
(A,{\bf v})
is controllable if and only if its minimal polynomial

\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}
is a Wedderburn polynomial.

In the next proposition we will use the minimal polynomial of a controllable pair to get a closed (and fairly explicit)
formula for the least right common multiple of several given left-coprime polynomials.

Proposition 3.10
.

Given left-coprime monic
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, let

C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}):=\begin{bmatrix}C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1})&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{k})\end{bmatrix},\quad E=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf e}_{1,n_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
{\bf e}_{1,n_{k}}\end{bmatrix},

(3.11)

where
n_{j}=\deg f_{j}
, and let
n:=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}
.
Then the pair
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E)
is controllable and its minimal polynomial is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E}

\displaystyle={\bf lrcm}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})

(3.12)

\displaystyle=z^{n}-\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z)\mathfrak{C}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1})^{n}{\bf e}_{1,n_{1}}\vskip-2.84526pt\\
\vdots\\
C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{k})^{n}{\bf e}_{1,n_{k}}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

By the block-diagonal structure of
C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
and due to (
2.20
),

\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E}={\bf lrcm}(\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1}),{\bf e}_{1,n_{1}}},\ldots,\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{k}),{\bf e}_{1,n_{k}}})={\bf lrcm}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}).

Since
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
are left coprime,

\deg({\bf lrcm}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}))=\deg f_{1}+\ldots+\deg f_{k}=n.

Hence, the two last formulas imply
\deg\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E}=n
. Therefore,
the matrix
\mathfrak{C}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E}
is invertible
(i.e., the pair
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E)
is controllable), and we can apply formula (
3.3
) to complete
the proof of (
3.12
).
∎

4.
Cyclic matrices and similarity reduction

A matrix
A
over a division ring
\mathbb{F}
is called
cyclic
if it admits a (left or right) cyclic vector, i.e.,
if it can be embedded into a controllable or an observable pair. Alternatively, cyclic matrices can be defined
as the ones similar to companion matrices or as the matrices having one non-constant invariant factor.
All these equivalent definitions are recorded below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given a matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}
such that the pair
(A,\bm{v})
is controllable.

(2)

There exists
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
such that the pair
(\bm{u},A)
is observable.

(3)

A
is similar to a (left or right) companion matrix.

(4)

The pencil
z{\bf I}_{n}-A
is equivalent to a polynomial matrix
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}{\bf I}_{n-1}&0\\
0&h(z)\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.

If this is the case (i.e., if
A
is cyclic), then

(a)

A\sim C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}})\sim C_{\bf r}(\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A})
for any cyclic vectors
\bm{v},\bm{u}
of
A
.

(b)

If
A=TC_{\bm{\ell}}(f)T^{-1}=SC_{\bf r}(g)S^{-1}
for some
f,g\in\mathbb{F}[z]
,
then the vectors
\bm{v}=T{\bf e}_{1}
and
\bm{u}={\bf e}_{1}^{\top}S^{-1}
are cyclic for
A
and furthermore,

f=\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g=\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}.

(c)

The invariant factor
h
of
A
from part (4) is necessarily of the form
h=\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf w}}
for some
cyclic vector
{\bf w}
of
A
.

Proof.

Each one of the properties (1)-(4) is similarity invariant. By (
3.6
) and (
3.2
),
A
admits
a right (left) cyclic vector if and only if it is similar to a left (right) companion matrix. By (
2.19
), we now conclude that
the statements (1), (2), (3) are equivalent. Since (4) holds (with
h=f
) for any companion matrix
C_{\bm{\ell}}(f)
,
the equivalence
(3)\Leftrightarrow(4)
follows.

The statement (a) follows from Proposition
3.2
. If
A=TC_{\bm{\ell}}(f)T^{-1}
and

\bm{v}=T{\bf e}_{1}
, then the input pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1})
are similar (see
Definition
2.4
) and therefore,
\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}=\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1}}=f
,
by Remark
2.5
and due to (
2.20
). The rest of the part (b) follows from similarity of output pairs
(\bm{u},A)
and

({\bf e}_{1}^{\top},C_{\bf r}(g))
. Finally, being an invariant factor of
A
, the polynomial
h
in (4) is also an invariant factor
for its companion matrix
C_{\bm{\ell}}(h)
. Therefore, the pencils
z{\bf I}_{n}-A
and
z{\bf I}_{n}-C_{\bm{\ell}}(h)

are equivalent and hence,
A\sim C_{\bm{\ell}}(h)
. Now part (c) follows from (b).
∎

4.1.
Similar polynomials

In the contrast to the commutative
setting of Proposition
1.2
, similar companion matrices over a noncommutative division ring do not have to be equal
(for an example, take two similar elements
\alpha\sim\alpha^{\prime}
(i.e.,
\alpha\beta=\beta\alpha^{\prime}
for some
\beta\neq 0
)
and consider the companion matrices
C_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{\rho}_{\alpha})=\alpha
and
C_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{\rho}_{\alpha^{\prime}})=\alpha^{\prime}
).
The polynomials generating similar companion matrices are called
similar
; in notation:
f\approx g
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For polynomials
f,g\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\bf lrcm}(f,h)=hg
for some
h\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that
f,h
are left coprime.

(2)

{\bf llcm}(g,p)=fp
for some
p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that
p,g
are right coprime.

(3)

fp=hg
for some
h,p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that
f,h
are left coprime and
p,g
are right coprime.

(4)

C_{\bm{\ell}}(f)\sim C_{\bm{\ell}}(g)
.

Property (1) is the original definition of similar polynomials that appeared in
[
16
]
.
The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
was shown in
[
16
, Theorem 1.18]
.
If
f,g
satisfy (1), then
fp=hg
for some
p\in\mathbb{H}[z]
which is necessarily right coprime
with
g
(for otherwise,
hg
wouldn’t be the
least
right common multiple of
f
and
h
).
On the other hand, if (3) is in force, then (1) holds with the same
h

(for otherwise, the polynomial
{\bf lrcm}(f,h)=f\widetilde{p}=h\widetilde{g}
would be a proper left divisor
of
fp=hg
implying that
p=\widetilde{p}q
and
g=\widetilde{g}q
for some non-constant
q\in\mathbb{H}[z]

contradicting the right coprimeness of
p
and
g
). Property (3) appears as the definition of polynomial similarity
in
[
6
,
8
,
3
]
in terms of isomorphic cyclic modules. For the equivalence

(3)\Leftrightarrow(4)
, see e.g.,
[
15
, Theorem 4.9]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
4.1
, we have the following relaxed version of Theorem
3.3
(when similarity is imposed on
state space matrices rather then on input or output pairs).

Proposition 4.3
.

(1) The minimal polynomials
\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}
of controllable pairs

(A,\bm{v})
and
(A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime})
are similar if and only if
A\sim A^{\prime}
.

(2) The minimal polynomials
\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime}}

of observable pairs
(\bm{u},A)
and
(\bm{u}^{\prime},A^{\prime})
are similar if and only if
A\sim A^{\prime}
.

Indeed, by Proposition
3.2
,
A\sim C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}})
and
A^{\prime}\sim C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}})
. By Theorem
4.1
, we therefore have

A\sim A^{\prime}\;\Leftrightarrow\,C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}})\sim C_{\bm{\ell}}(\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}})\,\Leftrightarrow\,\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}\approx\mathfrak{P}_{A^{\prime},\bm{v}^{\prime}}.

The second statement follows similarly, due to (
2.19
).

Upon combining Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
3.3
, we arrive at the following parametrization of the similarity
class of a given polynomial.

Theorem 4.4
.

Given a monic
p\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, the formula

\varphi:\,\bm{v}\mapsto\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),\bm{v}}

establishes a map from the set of all cyclic vectors of the companion matrix
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
onto
the similarity class of
p
. Moreover,
\varphi(\bm{v})=\varphi(\bm{v}^{\prime})
if and only if there exists an invertible

T\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
such that

TC_{\bm{\ell}}(p)=C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)T\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{v}^{\prime}=T\bm{v}.

(4.1)

Proof.

The vector
{\bf e}_{1}
is cyclic for
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
and
\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf e}_{1}}=p
.
By Proposition
4.3
(part (1) with
A=A^{\prime}=C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
),

\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),\bm{v}}\approx\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf e}_{1}}=p

for any cyclic vector
{\bf v}
of
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
. Conversely, if
g\approx p
, then

C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)\sim C_{\bm{\ell}}(g)
, i.e.,
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)=T^{-1}C_{\bm{\ell}}(g)T

for some invertible
T\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
. If we let
{\bf v}=T^{-1}{\bf e}_{1}
, then controllable pairs

(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf v})
and
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(g),{\bf e}_{1})
will be similar and hence,

\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),\bm{v}}=\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(g),{\bf e}_{1}}=g,

by Theorem
3.3
. Therefore, the map
\varphi
is onto. Again due to Theorem
3.3
,

\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),\bm{v}}=\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),\bm{v}^{\prime}}
if and only if the pairs

(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf v})
and
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf v^{\prime}}
are similar, which is equivalent to
relations (
4.1
).
∎

4.2.
Similarity reduction

A cyclic matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
does not have to be similar to a two-diagonal matrix.
To address Proposition
1.2
(part (4)) in the non-commutative setting, we put it in the following form:
if the companion
matrix
C(f)
(
f\in\mathbb{C}[z]
) is similar to a matrix
\Gamma
(
1.3
), then necessarily
f=\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}
.
Two noncommutative extensions of the latter statement are given in Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
below.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
be a monic polynomial of degree
n
. Let
\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}}
,
A
and
C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})

be
n\times n
matrices given by (
2.14
), (
3.8
) and (
3.11
), respectively. Then

(1)

C_{\bm{\ell}}(f)\sim\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}}
if and only if
f\approx\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{2}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}
.

(2)

C_{\bm{\ell}}(f)\sim A={\rm diag}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
if and only if

f\approx{\bf lrcm}(\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{n}})
.

(3)

C_{\bm{\ell}}(f)\sim{\rm diag}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1}),\ldots,C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{k}))
if and only if
f\approx{\bf lrcm}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
.

All three statements are known in the more general setting of skew polynomials
[
15
, Section 5]
. In the present context, they
follow from Proposition
4.3
and formulas (
2.15
), (
3.10
), (
3.12
) and (
2.20
).

The main point in part (1) is:
C_{\bm{\ell}}(f)
is similar to a two-diagonal matrix of the form (
2.14
)
if and only if
f
splits into the product of linear factors
.
Part (2) says that
C_{\bm{\ell}}(f)
is similar to a diagonal matrix if and only if
f
is a Wedderburn polynomial
.
Since
\alpha_{i}=C_{\bm{\ell}}(\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{i}})
, part (2) can be interpreted as the extremal particular case of part (3)
when all diagonal blocks in the matrix
C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
are scalars.
The opposite extremal case is the one where
f
cannot be represented as the
lrcm
of its proper left divisors, or equivalently,
the ideal
\langle f\rangle_{\bf r}
is irreducible in the sense that it is not contained into two distinct proper right ideals in

\mathbb{F}[z]
. Following Ore
[
16
]
we will call such polynomials
indecomposable
. By
[
16
, Theorem 13, Part II]
,
any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
admits a representation

f={\bf lrcm}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})

(4.2)

where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
are left coprime indecomposable polynomials, and this representation is unique up to similarity of each component.

We now present a more rigid version of Proposition
4.5
dealing with the fixed controllable pair

(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1})
rather than the companion matrix itself.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
be a monic polynomial of degree
n
, let
\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}}
and the controllable pairs

(A,{\bm{v}})
,
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E)
be defined as in (
2.14
), (
3.8
)
and (
3.11
), respectively. Then

(1)

(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1})\sim(\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}},{\bf e}_{1})
, i.e.,
there exists an invertible
T
such that

TC_{\bm{\ell}}(f)T^{-1}=\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T{\bf e}_{1}={\bf e}_{1}

if and only if
f=\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\bm{\rho}_{\gamma_{n}}
.

(2)

(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1})\sim(A,{\bf v})
if and only if

f={\bf lrcm}(\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\bm{\rho}_{\alpha_{n}})
.

(3)

(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1})\sim(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E)

if and only if
f={\bf lrcm}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
.

Proof.

Since the pairs
(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1})
,
(\Gamma_{\bm{\gamma}},{\bf e}_{1})
,
(A,{\bf v})
and

(C_{\bm{\ell}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),E)
are all controllable, any two of them are similar if and only if their
minimal polynomials are equal. Since
\mathfrak{P}_{C_{\bm{\ell}}(f),{\bf e}_{1}}=f
the statements follow
from the first formula in (
2.15
) (for
k=1
) and formulas (
3.10
) and (
3.12
).
∎

In case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
, the skew field quaternions, some of the previous results
can be elaborated a bit further, due to the facts that any non-real (i.e., non-cetral) element in
\mathbb{H}
is algebraic of
degree two and that
\mathbb{H}
is algebraically closed on the left and on the right and hence any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{H}

splits in
\mathbb{H}
. In this setting,
any
cyclic matrix is similar to a two-diagonal matrix (
1.3
), which is the exact
analog of part (3) in Proposition
1.2
. Part (3) in Proposition
4.5
is worked out to get the
Jordan form of a cyclic matrix
A
(which necessarily contains one block corresponding to each real
eigenvalue and at most two blocks corresponding to each non-real eigenvalue), while part (3) in Proposition
4.6
eventually
establishes similarity of a controllable pair
(A,{\bf v})
to the essentially unique pair
(\mathcal{J},E)
where
\mathcal{J}

is the block-diagonal matrix with diagonal blocks of the form (
1.3
) where this time, all
\gamma_{j}
’s are similar to each other
and
\gamma_{j+1}\neq\overline{\gamma}_{j}
(the quaternion conjugate of
\gamma_{j}
). We omit details.

5.
Ideal interpolation schemes

Characterizations of ideals of
\mathbb{F}[z]
given in Proposition
3.4
and Remark
3.5

in terms of evaluations (
2.7
)
based on controllable and observable pairs suggest to take yet another look at interpolation problems
in
\mathbb{F}[z]
. We start with ideal interpolation schemes that were proposed in
[
1
]
in an attempt to come
up with meaningful multivariate analogues of the Lagrange-Hermite interpolation problem.
The single-variable non-commutative version of this concept is the following: given a ring
\mathbb{F}
,
a finite set
\Phi=\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of linearly independent functionals

\varphi_{i}:\,\mathbb{F}[z]\mapsto\mathbb{F}
is called a
right (left, two-sided) ideal interpolation scheme

if
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\rm Ker}\,\varphi_{i}
is a right (left, two-sided) ideal in
\mathbb{F}[z]
.
Given an ideal interpolation scheme, the associated interpolation problem consists of finding all

f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that
\varphi_{i}(f)=c_{i}
for preassigned
c_{j}\in\mathbb{F}
(
i=1,\ldots,n
).
Since the problem is linear, the answer for a left (or right) scheme is given by the respective
formulas (which are the same if the scheme is two-sided)

f=f_{0}+ph\quad\mbox{or}\quad f=f_{0}+hp,

(5.1)

where
p
is the generator of the ideal
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\rm Ker}\,\varphi_{i}
,
where
f_{0}
is a unique particular solution to the problem with
\deg f_{0}<\deg p
, and where
h\in\mathbb{F}[z]
is a free parameter.
The only remaining question is to find an explicit formula for
f_{0}
in terms of given
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
and
p
.

By Proposition
3.4
, any right (left) ideal interpolation scheme in
\mathbb{F}[z]
can be embedded into the
following left (right) interpolation problem with the interpolation condition given in terms of evaluations
(
2.7
).

Problem

{\bf LP}(A,\bm{v},{\bf b})
:
given a controllable pair
(A,\bm{v})
with
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
,
and given
{\bf b}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}
, find a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that

(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)={\bf b}.

(5.2)

Problem

{\bf RP}(B,\bm{u},{\bf d})
:
given an observable pair
(\bm{u},B)
with
B\in\mathbb{F}^{k\times k}
,
and given
{\bf d}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times k}
, find a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that

(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(B)={\bf d}.

(5.3)

The next two results specifying the parametrization formulas (
5.1
) in terms of
interpolation data can be regarded as left and right noncommutative analogues of Proposition
1.1
(part (2)).

Theorem 5.1
.

The input pair
(A,\bm{v})
with
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
is controllable if and only if the problem
{\bf LP}(A,\bm{v},{\bf b})

has a solution for any
{\bf b}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1}
. In this case, all solutions to the problem
are parametrized by the formula

f={f}_{\bm{\ell}}+\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}h,\quad\mbox{with}\quad{f}_{\bm{\ell}}(z)=\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z)\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}{\bf b},\quad h\in\mathbb{F}[z],

(5.4)

where
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is defined in (
1.1
),
\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}
is the minimal polynomial of the pair
(A,\bm{v})
,
f_{\bm{\ell}}

is the low-degree solution, and
h
is a free parameter.

Proof.

by the division algorithm, the problem
{\bf LP}(A,\bm{v},{\bf b})
has a solution if and only if it has
a low-degree one. To find a polynomial
f_{\bm{\ell}}
with
\deg f<n
and subject to condition (
5.2
), we may take it in the form

f_{\bm{\ell}}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f_{j}z^{j}=\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z)F,\qquad F=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}f_{0}\\
\vdots\\
f_{n-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right],

(5.5)

and then compute, upon making use of (
2.7
) and (
3.1
),

(\bm{v}f_{\bm{\ell}})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)={\bf v}f_{0}+A{\bf v}f_{1}+\ldots+A^{n-1}{\bf v}f_{n-1}={\mathfrak{C}}_{A,\bm{v}}F.

Thus the equation
(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}F={\bf b}
has a solution

F
for any
{\bf b}\in\mathbb{F}
if and only if the controllability matrix
\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}
is invertible, i.e.,
the pair
(A,\bm{v})
is controllable. In this case,
f_{\bm{\ell}}
satisfies condition (
5.2
) if and only if

F={\mathfrak{C}}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}{\bf b}
which being substituted into (
5.5
), gives (
5.4
).
Since the solution set of the homogeneous problem
{\bf LP}(A,\bm{v},0)
is the right ideal
\langle\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf v}}\rangle_{\bf r}
,
(by Proposition
3.4
), the parametrization formula (
5.4
) follows.
∎

The right-sided version of Theorem
5.1
presented below is justified similarly.

Theorem 5.2
.

The output pair
(\bm{u},B)
with
B\in\mathbb{F}^{k\times k}
is observable if and only if the problem
{\bf RP}(B,\bm{u},{\bf d})

has a solution for any
{\bf d}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times k}
. In this case, all solutions to the problem
are given by the formula

f={f}_{\bm{r}}+h\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},B},\quad\mbox{with}\quad{f}_{\bm{r}}(z)={\bf d}\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},B}^{-1}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(z)^{\top},\quad h\in\mathbb{F}[z],

(5.6)

where
\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},A}
is the minimal polynomial of the pair
(\bm{u},A)
,
f_{\bm{r}}
is the low-degree solution,
and
h
is a free parameter.

Remark 5.3
.

The problem (
5.2
) can be efficiently solved for
any
(not necessarily controllable)
input pair
(A,{\bf v})
as follows. Given a pair
(A,{\bf v})
,
we find the smallest integer
d
such that the vectors
{\bf v},A{\bf v},\ldots,A^{d}{\bf v}
are (right) linearly dependent and then
construct the minimal polynomial
\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}
(of degree
d
) as suggested in (
2.12
). The problem (
5.2
)
has a solution if and only if the column
{\bf b}
belongs to the right range space of
\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}
(i.e.,
to the right linear span of
{\bf v},A{\bf v},\ldots,A^{d-1}{\bf v}
), the
controllability space
of the pair

(A,{\bf v})
. If this is the case, we represent
{\bf b}
as

{\bf b}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}A^{j}{\bf v}b_{j},\quad\mbox{and let}\quad f_{\ell}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}b_{j}z^{j}.

It is readily seen that all polynomials
f
subject to the interpolation condition (
5.2
) are parametrized by the
formula (
5.4
).

In conclusion we briefly address the first statement in Proposition
1.1
. In the complex setting,

f(A)
commutes with
A
, so the “only if” part is immediate.
As complex polynomials respect similarity, the matrix
A
can be taken
in the canonical Jordan form, and then the commutativity relation
AB=BA
forces
B
to be of triangular
block Toeplitz structure. Then the Hermite-Lagrange polynomial with prescribed values
(determined by
B
at eigenvalues of
A
(with multiplicities) satisfies
f(A)=B
.

In contrary to this case, polynomials over
\mathbb{F}
do not respect similarity and besides,
f(A)
does not have to commute with
A
.
The solvability of the interpolation problem
f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=B
for every
B\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
does not seem to have much to do
with cyclicity of
A
. However, this problem falls in the left ideal interpolation scheme and its particular solution can be
found recursively as follows. Letting
B=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf b}_{1}&{\bf b}_{2}&\ldots&{\bf b}_{n}\end{bmatrix}
we recall that
{\bf e}_{j}\in Z_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}

and write the interpolation
condition
f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=B
equivalently as

\big({\bf e}_{j}f\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)={\bf b}_{j}\quad\mbox{for}\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

(5.7)

Applying the procedure from Remark
5.2
, we either conclude that the first condition in (
5.7
) is inconsistent (and hence the
problem has no solutions) or we get all polynomials subject to this conditions in the form

f=f_{{\bm{\ell}}}+\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf e}_{1}}h,\qquad h\in\mathbb{F}[z].

(5.8)

Making use of (
2.8
), we see that a polynomial
f
of the form (
5.8
) satisfies conditions (
5.2
) for
j=2,\ldots,n
if and only if

{\bf b}_{j}=\big({\bf e}_{j}f\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)=\big({\bf e}_{j}f_{{\bm{\ell}}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)+\big(\big({\bf e}_{j}\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf e}_{1}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A))h\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)

for
j=2,\ldots,n
, which can be written in terms of the parameter
h
as

\big({\bf c}_{j}h\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)={\bf d}_{j}\quad\mbox{for}\quad j=2,\ldots,n,

(5.9)

where

{\bf c}_{j}=\big({\bf e}_{j}\mathfrak{P}_{A,{\bf e}_{1}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)
and
{\bf d}_{j}={\bf b}_{j}-\big({\bf e}_{j}f_{{\bm{\ell}}}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)
. Thus,
either the problem (
5.7
) is inconsistent or it reduces (via (
5.8
) to a similar problem (
5.9
) with fewer conditions.
Continuing this reduction, we either conclude that the original problem (
5.7
) is inconsistent or will come up (in
n
steps) with a parametrization
of all its solutions.

6.
Two-sided interpolation

Our next goal is to consider the problem which arises by combining left and right ideal interpolation schemes.
We will call this problem
two-sided
as it contains both left and right interpolation conditions.

Problem

{\bf TSP}
:
Given a controllable pair
(A,\bm{v})
and an observable pair
(\bm{u},B)
(with
A\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
,

B\in\mathbb{F}^{k\times k}
), along with the
target vectors
{\bf b}
and
{\bf d}
, find a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
subject to conditions

(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)={\bf b},\quad(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(B)={\bf d},\quad\deg f<n+k.

(6.1)

By Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, the latter problem can be identified with the following one:

given polynomials
p,q,{f}_{\bm{\ell}},{f}_{\bm{r}}\in\mathbb{F}[z]
with
\deg{f}_{\bm{\ell}}<\deg p
and
\deg{f}_{\bm{\ell}}<\deg q
,
find an
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that
\deg f<\deg p+\deg q
and

f-f_{\bm{\ell}}\in\langle p\rangle_{\bm{r}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f-f_{\bm{r}}\in\langle q\rangle_{\bm{\ell}}.

(6.2)

Indeed, if we let

p=\mathfrak{P}_{A,\bm{v}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad q=\mathfrak{P}_{\bm{u},B}

(6.3)

be minimal polynomials of the pairs
(A,\bm{v})
and
(\bm{u},B)
, and then let

f_{\bm{\ell}}=\mathfrak{A}_{n}\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}{\bf b}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\bm{r}}={\bf d}\mathfrak{O}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\top},

then conditions (
6.2
) turn out to be identical to parametrization formulas
(
5.4
), (
5.6
), and hence, they are equivalent to conditions (
5.2
) and (
5.3
).

Remark 6.1
.

The degree constraint in (
6.1
) is not restrictive. By the left and right division algorithms,
any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
can be uniquely represented as

f=\widetilde{f}+phq\quad\mbox{for some}\;\;\widetilde{f},h\in\mathbb{F}[z],\;\;\deg\widetilde{f}<\deg p+\deg q,

(6.4)

and furthermore,
f
satisfies conditions (
6.2
) if and only if
\widetilde{f}
does.

Theorem
6.11
below states that the problem (
6.1
) has a solution if
and only if the Sylvester equation
AY-YB={\bf b}\bm{u}-\bm{v}{\bf d}
has a solution
Y\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
.
The “only if” part of this criterion is verified in the next section via certain “two-sided” evaluation calculus.

6.1.
Two-sided evaluation

Left and right evaluations (
2.7
) based on input
and output pairs evaluate a scalar polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]

at these pairs, rather at matrices (as in (
2.2
)). In formula (
6.1
) below, we introduce a map

\mathbb{F}[z]\to\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
that evaluates
f
at the couple
\{(A,\bm{v}),(\bm{u},B)\}
consisting of an
input pair
(A,\bm{v})
and an output pair
(\bm{u},B)
.

Let us extend the backward-shift operators
L_{A}
and
R_{B}
defined via formulas (
2.11
) on vector polynomials of special form
(polynomial multiples of a constant vector) to matrix polynomials of the form

F=\bm{v}f\bm{u},\quad\mbox{where}\quad\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times 1},\;\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times k},\;f(z)=\sum f_{j}z^{j}\in\mathbb{F}[z],

by the formulas

\displaystyle L_{A}(\bm{v}f\bm{u})

\displaystyle:=L_{A}(\bm{v}f)\cdot\bm{u}=\sum_{j+k=0}^{\deg f-1}A^{j}\bm{v}f_{k+j+1}\bm{u}z^{k},

(6.5)

\displaystyle R_{B}(\bm{v}f\bm{u})

\displaystyle:=\bm{v}\cdot R_{B}(f\bm{u})=\sum_{j+k=0}^{\deg f-1}\bm{v}f_{k+j+1}\bm{u}B^{j}z^{k}.

Evaluating the top formula at
B
on the right and the bottom formula at
A
on the left we get the same outcomes which we will refer to as
the
two-sided evaluation
of
f
at the couple
\{(A,\bm{v}),(\bm{u},B)\}
:

\displaystyle(\bm{v}f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{ts}}}(A,B)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i+j=0}^{\deg f-1}A^{i}\bm{v}f_{i+j+1}\bm{u}B^{j}

(6.6)

\displaystyle=(L_{A}(\bm{u}f\bm{v}))^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(B)=(R_{B}(\bm{u}f\bm{v}))^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A).

Proposition 6.2
.

For any
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, the evaluations (
2.7
) and (
6.1
) satisfy the Sylvester equation

A\cdot(\bm{v}f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{ts}}}(A,B)-(\bm{v}f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{ts}}}(A,B)\cdot B=(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)\cdot\bm{u}-\bm{v}\cdot(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(B).

(6.7)

Indeed, by making use of polynomial expressions in (
2.7
) and (
6.7
) we get

\displaystyle A\cdot(\bm{v}f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{ts}}}(A,B)-(\bm{v}f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{ts}}}(A,B)\cdot B

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=0}^{{\rm degf}-1}A^{i+1}\bm{v}f_{i+j+1}\bm{u}B^{j}-\sum_{i+j=0}^{{\rm degf}-1}A^{i}\bm{v}f_{i+j+1}\bm{u}B^{j+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm degf}}A^{i}\bm{v}f_{i}\bm{u}-\sum_{j=1}^{{\rm degf}}\bm{v}f_{j}\bm{u}B^{j}=(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A)\cdot\bm{u}-\bm{v}\cdot(f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(B).

Corollary 6.3
.

If a polynomial
f
satisfies conditions (
6.1
), then the matrix
Y=(\bm{v}f\bm{u})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{ts}}}(A,B)
solves the
Sylvester equation
AY-YB={\bf b}\bm{u}-\bm{v}{\bf d}
.

For a concrete example, we will compute the two-sided evaluation at
((C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),{\bf e}_{1,n}),({\bf e}_{1,k},C_{\bf r}(q)))
where
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)
and
C_{\bf r}(q)
are companion matrices of given polynomials

p,q\in\mathbb{F}[z]
with
\deg p=n
,
\deg q=k
. We let for short,

\Upsilon^{f}=\left[\Upsilon_{ij}\right]:=({\bf e}_{1,n}f{\bf e}_{1,k}^{\top})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{ts}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),C_{\bf r}(q))

(6.8)

and denote by
\Upsilon_{j}
and
\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{i}
the
j
-th column and the
i
-th row of the matrix
\Upsilon^{f}
.
Upon specifying two last formulas in (
6.1
) to the present setting and combining Propositions
2.25
and
2.10

we see that

\Upsilon_{j}=\big({\bf e}_{1,n}h_{j}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{i}=\big(g_{i}{\bf e}_{1,k}^{\top}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(C_{\bf r}(q)),

(6.9)

where the polynomials
g_{i}
and
h_{j}
are defined in (
2.24
) and (
2.26
), respectively.

Remark 6.4
.

\Upsilon^{f}=0
if and only if
f=\alpha+phq
for some
h\in\mathbb{F}[z]

and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Since
q
is the minimal polynomial of the pair
({\bf e}_{1,k},C_{\bf r}(q))
, it follows from (
6.9
) that

\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{i}=0
if and only if
g_{i}
is a left multiple of
q
.
Given
f=\alpha+phq
, equalities (
2.23
) hold with
b(z)=b_{0}=\alpha
and
g=hq
. Hence,
g_{i}=(R_{0}^{j}p)\cdot g
is a left multiple of
q
.
Therefore,
\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{i}=0
for
i=1,\ldots,k
and hence,
\Upsilon^{f}=0
. Conversely, if
\Upsilon^{f}=0
, then
g_{j}=r_{j}q

for
j=1,\ldots,n
. In particular,
g_{n}=g=r_{n}q
, and now it follows from (
2.24
) that
R_{0}^{j}b=0
for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
.
Since
\deg b<n
, the latter equalities imply that
b=b_{0}
. Then (
2.23
) takes the form
f=pr_{n}q+b_{0}
as desired.
∎

Since any
f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
can be represented as in (
6.4
) and then necessarily
\Upsilon^{f}=\Upsilon^{\widetilde{f}}
, by
Remark
6.4
, it follows that it suffices to compute
\Upsilon^{f}
for
f
with
\deg f<n+k
.
Any such polynomial can be represented as

f=pg+b=hq+d

(6.10)

with
b,h
of degree less than
n
and

of degree less than
k
, i.e.,

\begin{array}[]{ll}b(z)=b_{0}+\ldots+b_{n-1}z^{n-1},&g(z)=g_{0}+\ldots+g_{k-1}z^{k-1},\\[5.69054pt]
d(z)=d_{0}+\ldots+d_{k-1}z^{k-1},&h(z)=h_{0}+\ldots+h_{n-1}z^{n-1}.\end{array}

(6.11)

Below, we compute the matrix
\Upsilon^{f}
in terms of polynomials (
6.11
).

Lemma 6.5
.

If
f
is of the form (
6.10
), (
6.11
), then the columns
\Upsilon_{j}
and the rows
\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{i}

of the matrix (
6.8

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}h_{0}\\
\vdots\\
h_{n-1}\end{bmatrix},\quad\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}g_{0}&\ldots&g_{k-1}\end{bmatrix},

(6.12)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-j}C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{i}\Upsilon_{k}q_{j+i}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-j-1}C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{i}{\bf e}_{1}d_{j+i}\quad(j=1,\ldots,k-1),

(6.13)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}p_{i+j}\Upsilon_{n}C_{\bf r}(q)^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-i-1}b_{i+j}{\bf e}_{1}^{\top}C_{\bf r}(q)^{j}\quad(i=1,\ldots,n-1).

Proof.

By (
6.9
) and (
2.26
) (for
j=k
) ,

\Upsilon_{k}=\big({\bf e}_{1}h\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)),

(6.14)

and since

, the latter column consists of the coefficients of
h

2.25
, which verifies the
first formula in (
6.12
). We next compute

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{j}

\displaystyle=\big({\bf e}_{1}h_{j}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))

\displaystyle=\big({\bf e}_{1}\big(h\cdot(R_{0}^{j}q)+R_{0}^{j}d\big)\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))

\displaystyle=\big({\bf e}_{1}\big(h\cdot(R_{0}^{j}g\big)\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))+\big({\bf e}_{1}(R_{0}^{j}d)\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))

\displaystyle=\big(({\bf e}_{1}h)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))\cdot R_{0}^{j}g\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))+\big({\bf e}_{1}(R_{0}^{j}d)\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))

\displaystyle=\big(\Upsilon_{k}\cdot R_{0}^{j}g\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))+\big({\bf e}_{1}(R_{0}^{j}d)\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)),

where we used (
6.9
) and (
2.26
) for the two first steps, the additivity of evaluation operators and the
multiplicative property (
2.8
) (applied to
h
and
R_{0}^{j}g
) for the next two step, and finally we used (
6.14
)
for the last step. The expression on the right side is the same as in (

), by definition (
2.7
) of left evaluation. Equalities for
the rows
\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{i}
are verified similarly.
∎

6.2.
Sylvester equations

We now consider the Sylvester equation

C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)X-XC_{\bf r}(q)={\bf b}{\bf e}^{\top}_{1,k}-{\bf e}_{1,n}{\bf d}

with given
{\bf b}=\left[b_{0}\;\ldots\;b_{n-1}\right]^{\top}
and
{\bf d}=\left[d_{0}\;\ldots\;d_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle\big({\bf x}q\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{i}{\bf x}q_{i}={\bf b}-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)^{i}{\bf e}_{1,n}d_{i},

\displaystyle\big(p\widetilde{\bf x}\big)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(C_{\bf r}(q))

6.15

6.16
), (
6.17

({\bf e}_{1,n}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))={\bf b},\quad(f{\bf e}_{1,k}^{\top})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(C_{\bf r}(q))={\bf d},\quad\Upsilon^{f}=X,\quad\deg f<n+k,

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle={\bf d}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(z)^{\top}+\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z)X{\bf e}_{k,k}q(z)

(3)

(6.20)

subject to conditions (

to equation (

(4)
If

solves the equation (

f(z)=\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z){\bf b}+p(z)\widetilde{\bf x}\mathfrak{A}_{k}(z)^{\top}

subject to conditions (

\widetilde{X}_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}p_{i+j}\widetilde{\bf x}C_{\bf r}(q)^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-i-1}b_{i+j}{\bf e}_{1}^{\top}C_{\bf r}(q)^{j}\quad(1\leq i\leq n-1)

Proof.

Part (1) follows from Corollary

Making use of the explicit formula

C_{\bf r}(q)=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&\ldots&0\\
0&0&1&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

-q_{0}&-q_{1}&-q_{2}&\ldots&-q_{k-1}\end{bmatrix},

we equate the corresponding columns in (

\displaystyle C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)X_{1}+X_{k}q_{0}

\displaystyle={\bf b}-{\bf e}_{1,n}d_{0},

(6.23)

\displaystyle C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)X_{j}-X_{j-1}+X_{k}q_{j-1}

\displaystyle=-{\bf e}_{1,n}d_{j-1}\quad\mbox{for}\quad j=2,\ldots,k.

(6.24)

From (

6.3.
Quasi-ideals in
\mathbb{F}[z]

An additive subgroup
\bm{\mathcal{Q}}
of an associative ring
\mathcal{A}

\bm{\mathcal{Q}}\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{A}\bm{\mathcal{Q}}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{Q}}

generated by two given polynomials. Any element

is characterized by factorizations

or by homogeneous

({\bf e}_{1,n}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))=0
and

.
By letting
{\bf b}=0
and
{\bf d}=0
throughout Section 6.2 we arrive at the following result.

Proposition 6.7
.

Given polynomials
p,q\in\mathbb{F}[z]
, the formula

X\mapsto\mathfrak{A}_{n}(z)X{\bf e}_{k,k}q(z)=p(z){\bf e}_{n,n}^{\top}X\mathfrak{A}_{k}(z)^{\top}

establishes the one-to-one correspondence between the matrices
X\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
such that
C_{\bm{\ell}}(p)X=XC_{\bf r}(q)

and the polynomials
f\in\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{p,q}
of degree

.

By Remark
6.1
, any
f\in\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{p,q}
can be uniquely represented as in (
6.4
) with
\widetilde{f}\in\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{p,q}
; hence
the description of the whole
\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{p,q}
follows from Proposition
6.7
.

6.4.

, an observable pair

and

\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}A\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}=C_{\bm{\ell}}(p),\;\;\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}\bm{v}={\bf e}_{1},\;\;\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},B}B\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},B}^{-1}=C_{\bf r}(q),\;\;\bm{u}\mathfrak{O}_{\bm{u},B}^{-1}={\bf e}_{1}^{\top}.

and

Remark 6.8
.

Conditions (

) and (

) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle({\bf e}_{1,n}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))

\displaystyle(f{\bf e}_{1,k}^{\top})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(C_{\bf r}(q))

\displaystyle({\bf e}_{1,n}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(C_{\bm{\ell}}(p))

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}_{A,\bm{v}}^{-1}\cdot(\bm{v}f)^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(A),

\displaystyle\quad(f{\bf e}_{1,k}^{\top})^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(C_{\bf r}(q))

)

As we know from Theorem
6.6

6.31

6.33
)) has a solution if and only if

-Y\bm{\mu}_{A}(B)=\bm{\mu}_{A}(A)Y-Y\bm{\mu}_{A}(B)=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\mu_{j}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}A^{i}(AY-YB)B^{j-i-1}.

(6.48)

If

6.42

on the right side of (

{\bf b}\bm{u}-\bm{v}{\bf d}

To verify that

6.42

\bm{\mu}_{A}(B)
commute, we therefore, have

({\bf b}\bm{u}-\bm{v}{\bf d})\bm{\mu}_{A}(B)=AY\bm{\mu}_{A}(B)-YB\bm{\mu}_{A}(B),

which is equivalent to (
6.42
), since
\bm{\mu}_{A}(B)
is invertible.
The rest follows by Theorem
6.11
.
∎

6.5.
Lagrange interpolation

Given interpolation nodes
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}
in
\mathbb{F}

along with target values
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
,
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
, the
two-sided Lagrange interpolation problem
consists of finding an

f\in\mathbb{F}[z]
such that

f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}}}(\alpha_{i})=b_{i}\quad(i=1,\ldots,n)\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{\bm{\mathfrak{e}_{r}}}(\beta_{j})=d_{j}\quad(j=1,\ldots,k).

(6.49)

We refer to
[
2
]
for a detailed treatment of this problem. Here we only show that under the assumption that

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{(a) the set $\Lambda_{\bm{\ell}}=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}$ is left $P$-independent,}\\
\mbox{(b) the set $\Lambda_{\bf r}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\}$ is right $P$-independent,}\end{array}

(6.50)

the problem can be embedded into the scheme of
{\bf TSP}
. To this end, note
that interpolation conditions (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) specified to the case

\begin{array}[]{rlll}A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{1}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&\alpha_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right],&\;B=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\beta_{1}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&\beta_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right],&{\bf v}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1\vskip-2.84526pt\\
\vdots\vskip 2.84526pt\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right],&{\bf b}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}b_{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right],\\
{\bf u}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\ldots&1\end{bmatrix},&\;{\bf d}=\begin{bmatrix}d_{1}&\ldots&d_{k}\end{bmatrix}&&\end{array}

(6.51)

amount to conditions (
6.49
).
By Proposition
3.9
, the assumptions (
6.50
) ensure the pair
(A,\bm{v})
be controllable
and the pair
(\bm{u},B)
be observable and hence, all general results from Section 4.5 apply.
Theorem
6.11
describes all solutions
to the problem (
6.49
) in terms of solutions
Y=\left[y_{ij}\right]
of the Sylvester equation (
6.42
),
which in the present setting breaks up into the system of

nk
scalar equations

\alpha_{i}y_{ij}-y_{ij}\beta_{j}=b_{i}-d_{j}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n,\;1\leq j\leq k).

(6.52)

In the case where
\alpha_{i}
or
\beta_{j}
are algebraic over
Z_{\mathbb{F}}
, the solvability
criterion for the equation (
6.52
) (as well as the parametrization of all solutions in the indeterminate case) known
from
[
9
]
lead to an explicit description of all solutions to the problem (
6.49
).

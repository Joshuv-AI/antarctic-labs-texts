Strong gravitational lensing around Kehagias-Sfetsos compact objects surrounded by plasma
Sudipta Hensh, Jan Schee, Ahmadjon Abdujabbarov, Zdeněk Stuchlík

Abstract
We present the analysis how Hořava gravity and plasma influence the strong lensing phenomena around Kehagias-Sfetsos (KS) black holes. Using the semi-analytical Bozza method of strong lensing limit, we determine the multiple images, namely their separation S, and magnification R. We apply our calculations to the case of supermassive black hole having mass M = 6.5 × 10 9 ​ M ⊙ M=6.5\times 10^{9}\textrm{M}_{\odot} and being at distance d 0 = 16.8 ​ Mpc d_{0}=16.8\textrm{Mpc} from observer corresponding to those observed in M87. We show that the sensitivity of image magnification, image separation, and shadow angular size on KS parameter ω \omega and plasma parameter k k are of order from 1 % 1\% to 10 % 10\% for R R and 16 % ~\,16\% for S S .

On the other hand, in the close vicinity of the black hole horizon, multiple images of radiating objects could be observed due to the effects of strong gravitational lensing, enabling thus an efficient way of testing alternative models of gravity, as in such circumstances the predictions of alternative gravity can differ significantly from those of general relativity.
The strong gravitational lensing is one of the key phenomena, predicted by General Theory of Relativity (GR). It plays the crucial role in our attempt to distinguish among many concurrent theories arising since the advent of GR and determine the parameters of astrophysical compact objects that are responsible for strong gravitational lensing phenomena.

The most direct attempt to calculate strong lensing effects is to numerically solve geodesics equations and determine associate magnitude and angular image separation. The pure numerical solutions encounter its limits when trying to determine the deflection of high order images.

Luckily, there is a semi-analytical method developed by Bozza (
[

,
[

The analytical part represents the integral that diverges as photons approach the photon circular orbit and is expressed as the logarithmic function of radial coordinate, while the numerical part is the regular integral, that can be calculated numerically with required accuracy.

Using Bozza’s Strong Lensing Limit (SLL) we determine strong lensing effects in the vicinity of Kehagias-Sfetsos black-hole surrounded by plasma. The motivation is straightforward.

The Kehagias-Sfetsos (KS) (

Hořava gravity touches the realm of the quantum gravity and strong lensing analysis in the vicinity of KS can give constraints of parameters defining black hole spacetime and corresponding link between GR and quantum gravity.
In fact, there is a series of works treating the astrophysical phenomena in the vicinity of KS black hole

2
Deflection angle in KS with Plasma

The line element for Kehagias–Sfetsos spacetime is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\ ,

(1)

where

\displaystyle f(r)\equiv 1+r^{2}\omega\left(1-\sqrt{1+4/(\omega r^{3})}\right)\ .

(2)

We calculate deflection angle in the field of Kehagias-Sfetsos black hole surrounded by plasma with refractive index
n(r)
.
We consider an isotropic distribution of plasma given by

\displaystyle n^{2}(r)=1-\frac{k}{r}\ ,

(3)

where
k
is the plasma parameter determined by the properties of plasma.
Photons interact with plasma and therefore no longer move along null geodesics of the background spacetime but rather, as can be shown, along null geodesics of effective geometry that in our case read

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\frac{f(r)}{n^{2}}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{1}{f(r)}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}.

(4)

Due to spherical symmetry of the spacetime it is sufficient to consider, without loss of generality, motion in equatorial plane, i.e. there is
\theta=\pi/2
along the geodesics of interest. The metric (
4
) then reads

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\frac{f(r)}{n^{2}(r)}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{1}{f(r)}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}.\ .

(5)

For above effective KS geometry given in (
5
), let us define

A(r)\equiv\frac{f(r)}{n^{2}(r)}\ ,\quad B(r)\equiv\frac{1}{f(r)}\ ,\quad\textrm{and}\quad C(r)\equiv r^{2}\ .

(6)

From the null vector normalization condition

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=0\ ,

(7)

where tilde refers to effective geometry given in (
5
) and from existence of constants of motion
L\equiv k_{\phi}
and
E\equiv-k_{t}
one derives formula for the value of subtend azimuthal angle of photon geodesics in the form

\Delta\phi(r_{0})=2\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{d\phi}{dr}dr=2\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{l\mathrm{d}r}{r^{2}\sqrt{n^{2}(r)-\frac{f(r)l^{2}}{r^{2}}}}.

(8)

Here,
l\equiv L/E
is the impact parameter and
r_{0}
is the distance of closest approach i.e. corresponding turning point in radial coordinate and it is a solution of equation

n^{2}(r)r^{2}-f(r)l^{2}=0.

(9)

The deflection angle
\alpha
is then given by expression

\alpha(r_{0})=\Delta\phi(r_{0})-\pi.

(10)

Integral (
8
) is valid only for
l\geq l_{\gamma}
where
l_{\gamma}
is impact parameter of photon circular orbit in effective geometry, i.e. it is determined from conditions

k^{u}=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{u}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}=0\ ,

(11)

leading to following couple of equations

\displaystyle n^{2}(r_{\gamma})r^{2}-f(r_{\gamma})l_{\gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ ,

(12)

\displaystyle 2n(r_{\gamma})\,n^{\prime}({r_{\gamma}})r_{\gamma}^{3}+2f(r_{\gamma})l_{\gamma}^{2}-2f^{\prime}({r_{\gamma}})r_{\gamma}l_{\gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ ,

(13)

where the prime represents the derivative with respect to
r
and
r_{\gamma}
is the radius of the photon circular orbit.

3
Bozza’s Strong Lensing Limit

Bozza in his paper
[
13
]
developed general method to construct strong lensing limit, i.e. the regime of photon impact parameter is close to impact parameter of photon circular orbit of given spacetime.
Here we summarize the results of this procedure.

Let us define a variable
z
which is mutually connected with
r
by transformation

z\equiv\frac{A-A_{0}}{1-A_{0}}\ ,

(14)

where the index ‘0’ refers to quantities evaluated at turning point
r_{0}
.

We can rewrite the integral (
8
) as

\displaystyle\Delta\phi(r_{0})=\int_{0}^{1}R(z,r_{0})f(z,r_{0})dz\ ,

(15)

where

\displaystyle R(z,r_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{BA}}{CA^{\prime}}(1-A_{0})\sqrt{C_{0}}\ ,

(16)

\displaystyle f(z,r_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{0}-\left((1-A_{0})z+A_{0}\right)C_{0}/C}}\ .

(17)

The function
f(z,r_{0})
diverges for
z\rightarrow 0
but the function
R(z,r_{0})
is regular for all values of
z
. Using Taylor expansion, the function
f(z,r_{0})
can be approximated as

\displaystyle f(z,r_{0})\sim f_{0}(z,r_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\zeta z+\chi z^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\ ,

(18)

with

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-A_{0}}{C_{0}A^{\prime}_{0}}\left(C^{\prime}_{0}A_{0}-C_{0}A^{\prime}_{0}\right),

(19)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-A_{0})^{2}}{2C_{0}^{2}A^{\prime 3}_{0}}\left[2C_{0}C^{\prime}_{0}A^{\prime 2}_{0}+(C0C^{\prime\prime}_{0}-2C^{\prime 2}_{0})A_{0}A^{\prime}_{0}-C_{0}C^{\prime}_{0}A_{0}A^{\prime\prime}_{0}\right]\ .

(20)

The integral (
15
) can be divided into regular (
I_{R}(r_{0})
) and divergent parts (
I_{D}(r_{0})
) as follows,

\Delta\phi(r_{0})=I_{D}(r_{0})+I_{R}(r_{0})\ ,

(21)

where

\displaystyle I_{D}(r_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}R(0,r_{\gamma})f_{0}(z,r_{0})dz\ ,

(22)

\displaystyle I_{R}(r_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[R(z,r_{0})f(z,r_{0})-R(0,r_{\gamma})f_{0}(z,r_{0})]dz\ .

(23)

If we solve regular and divergent part of integral separately and assume the closest approach distance
r_{0}
to be very close to
r_{\gamma}
we can write the expression for deflection angle as

\alpha(\theta)=-\bar{a}\log\left[\frac{\theta\,D_{OL}}{l_{\gamma}}-1\right]+\bar{b}.

(24)

where
D_{OL}
is the distance between observer and lens,
\theta=l/D_{OL}
is the image angular position in the lens system. The parameters that spacetime geometry determines are calculated form formulas

\displaystyle\bar{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R(0,r_{\gamma})}{2\sqrt{\chi_{\gamma}}},

(25)

\displaystyle\bar{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\pi+b_{R}+\bar{a}\log\left(\frac{2\chi_{\gamma}}{A_{\gamma}}\right)

(26)

with following definitions

\displaystyle R(0,r_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2A_{0}}{C_{0}\,A^{\prime}_{0}}(1-A_{\gamma})\sqrt{C_{\gamma}},

(27)

\displaystyle\chi_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\gamma}(1-A_{\gamma})^{2}\left[C^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}A_{\gamma}-C_{\gamma}A^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma}\right]}{2A^{2}_{\gamma}C^{\prime 2}_{\gamma}}\ ,

(28)

where the index ‘
\gamma
’ refers to quantities evaluated at photon orbit, i.e. at
r_{\gamma}
.
The parameter
b_{R}
is calculated by numerical integration of finite definite integral

b_{R}=I_{R}(r_{\gamma})\ .

(29)

When evaluating
b_{R}
, i.e. calculating integral (28) we need expression for
r=r(z)
. Rearranging (13) we get

A(r)=(1-A_{0})z+A_{0}\equiv F(z).

(30)

For given
z
we can solve this equation numerically, but one can convince himself that this function is very steep at
z\sim 1
. Fortunately function
A(r)
is rational and equation (
30
) can be solved as polynomial equation in the case of
n^{2}
being given by (
3
). Equation (
30
) can then be rewritten to read

\displaystyle 2\omega^{2}(1-F(z))r^{7}+(2k\omega^{2}F(z)-4\omega^{2})r^{6}+\omega(1+F^{2}(z)-2F(z))r^{5}+2k\omega(1-F(z))F(z)r^{4}+k^{2}\omega F^{2}(z)r^{3}=0

(31)

3.1
Image separation and magnification

The strong lensing tool becomes very useful when deflection angle is associated with observable quantities giving us connection between observations and parameters of the model. The key equation is the lens equation derived for case of strong deflection limit also by Bozza in
[
13
]
considering the observer, source and lens are almost aligned reads

\beta=\theta-\frac{D_{LS}}{D_{OS}}\Delta\alpha_{n}m\ ,

(32)

where
\beta
is the angular position of the source i.e. angel between the direction of the source and the optical axis,

D_{L}S
and
D_{O}S
are the lens-source and observer-source distance.
The quantity
\Delta\alpha_{m}
is the offset of deflection angle given by

\Delta\alpha_{m}\equiv\alpha(\theta)-2\pi m\ ,

(33)

where
m
indicates the number of loops made by the photon around the black hole.
Angular position of the
m
th order image is

\theta_{m}=\theta_{m}^{0}+\frac{l_{\gamma}\,H_{m}\left(\beta-\theta^{0}_{m}\right)\,D_{OS}}{\bar{a}\,D_{LS}\,D_{OL}}\ ,

(34)

where

H_{m}=\exp\left(\frac{\bar{a}-2n\pi}{\bar{b}}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\theta_{m}^{0}=\frac{l_{\gamma}}{D_{OL}}(1+H_{m})\ ,

(35)

with
\theta_{m}^{0}
is the angular position of the image when
\alpha(\theta_{m}^{0})=2\pi\,m
.

The image magnification is associated with the ratio between image angular position
\theta
and angular position of the source
\beta
, it reads

\mu_{m}=\left(\frac{\beta}{\theta}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial\theta}\right)^{-1}_{\theta_{n}^{0}}.

(36)

Applying strong lensing lens equation, the resulting magnification formula is given by equation

\mu_{m}=H_{m}\frac{l^{2}_{\gamma}\left(1+H_{m}\right)D_{OS}}{\bar{a}\beta\,D^{2}_{OL}\,D_{LS}}.

(37)

3.2
Observable quantities

In order to make comparison between model and observations, potentially observable quantities must be defined. Here we consider the two natural quantities, already introduced by Bozza
[
13
]
, the angular separation among the source images of different order and the magnification. First, let us discuss images angular separation. Due to the fact that the higher is the order of the image the more they are indistinguishable from the limiting case of images of infinite order, the natural choice of the angular separation parameter
S
is

S\equiv\theta_{1}-\theta_{\infty},

(38)

it is the angular separation between first order image and the limiting image order. Second, let us discuss the second parameter,
R
, expressing comparison between different images magnification parameter. The magnification of the images exponentially decreases with increasing order of the image. It is reasonable to define
R
as quantity relating magnification of the first order image with the sum of magnification contributions of second and higher order images, i.e.

R\equiv\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2+}}\ ,

(39)

where

\mu_{2+}\equiv\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\infty}{\mu_{i}}\ .

(40)

Using formulae introduced in previous subsection following from lens equation, the observable parameters read

S=\theta_{\infty}\,\exp\left[\left(\bar{b}-2\pi\right)/\bar{a}\right]\ ,

(41)

and

R=\exp\left(2\pi/\bar{a}\right)\ .

(42)

Conclusions

We applied Bozza’s technique to Kehagias-Sfetsos black hole to analyze the effect of imprinted non-Lorentzian character of the Hořava gravity theory on strong lensing phenomena in the vicinity of the supermassive black-hole similar to one in M87 galaxy, i.e. the black hole mass
6.5\times 10^{9}\,M_{\odot}
and its distance is
16
kpc being surrounded with plasma. We modelled the environment around black hole by a plasma with refractive index
n
(
3
). The model was controlled with two parameters,
\omega
representing Hořava gravity and
k
representing the plasma and we studied the effect of them on astrophysically relevant quantities
S
the angular separation parameter (
38
) and
R
the magnification parameter (
39
). We have found out that for fixed value of parameter
k
the value of
S
parameter monotonically decreases with increasing parameter
\omega
in range of
\omega>\omega_{mbh}
, while the value of
R
parameter is monotonically increasing, on the same interval of
\omega
. Further, we have found out that the relative change of
S
between
k=0
and
k=0.1
is
17.3\%
(
\omega=0.5
),
15.3\%
(
\omega=1.0
),
15.6\%
(
\omega=2.0
). In case of relative magnification
R
, its relative change between
k=0
and
k=0.1
is
0.95\%
(
\omega=0.5
),
8.5\%
(
\omega=1.0
),
10.1\%
(
\omega=2.0
).

We conclude, that the effect of
\omega
on angular separation
S
, in terms of
\Delta S/S_{0}
decreases with increasing
\omega
, but the change is not steep. On the other hand, there is increasing effect on relative magnification with increasing
\omega
from
1\%
for
\omega=0.5
to
10\%
for
\omega=2.0
. We have also calculate the effect of parameter
\omega
on angular size of the black hole shadow. We see, from Table
1
, that increasing value of
\omega
leads to the increase of
\theta_{\infty}
with relative change between
k=0
and
k=0.1
being
\Delta\theta_{\infty}/\theta_{\infty}=2.1\%
(
\omega=0.5
),
1.7\%
(
\omega=1.0
), and
1.7\%
(
\omega=2.0
). The width of
\theta_{\infty}
range is approximately
1.5\mu
arcsec which is too small to be a distinguishable effect today.

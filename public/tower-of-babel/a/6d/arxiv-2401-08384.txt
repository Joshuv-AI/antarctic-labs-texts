Shear and bulk viscosity of quark-gluon plasma with Gribov gluons and quasiparticle quarks
Sadaf Madni a, Arghya Mukherjee b, Amaresh Jaiswal a, Najmul Haque a

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the transport coefficients, specifically the bulk ( ζ \zeta ) and shear ( η \eta ) viscosity coefficients of hot QCD matter at finite temperature and vanishing chemical potential. For gluonic plasma, we consider the Gribov’s prescription for the gluon propagator, which has been proven to be effective in exploring the non-perturbative region. For a system of quark-gluon plasma (QGP), we adopt the quasiparticle prescription for quarks along with the Gribov’s prescription for gluons. We determine the Gribov parameter and quasi-quark mass by utilizing lattice QCD data for the equation-of-state of pure glue and QGP, respectively. Our primary objective is to examine the influence of quasi-quarks on the transport coefficients of QGP. In the end, we observed a significant decrease in the scaled transport coefficients with temperature across the entire relevant temperature range ( 1 ≤ T / T c ≤ 3 1\leq T/T_{c}\leq 3 ).



I
Introduction

The Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN, Geneva, and the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) at the Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL) in New York are two remarkable scientific facilities that serve as vital platforms for studying the primordial form of matter that existed in the universe few microseconds after the Big Bang
Chen:2014kpa
. High-energy nucleus-nucleus collision experiments at these facilities have the potential to convert a portion of the kinetic energies of the colliding nuclei into heating the QCD vacuum within an extremely small volume. This could result in achieving temperatures millions of times hotter than the core of the sun. At temperatures around
\sim
150 MeV, the transition of nuclear matter from the hadronic phase, consisting of protons and neutrons, to the deconfined phase, consisting of quarks and gluons, occurs. This transition from the hadronic phase to the nearly equilibrated deconfined phase leads to the emergence of a new state of matter termed as the Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP)
Pasechnik:2016wkt
.

Analysis of spectra and azimuthal anisotropy of particles produced in relativistic heavy-ion collisions suggested that the QGP produced is strongly coupled
Braun-Munzinger:2007edi
;
Gyulassy:2004zy
;
Shuryak:2008eq
;
Heinz:2005zg
. Application of viscous hydrodynamics to understand the evolution of the fireball produced in heavy-ion collisions gained widespread interest ever since the ratio of shear viscosity to entropy density (
\eta/s
) was estimated to be close to the absolute lower bound for all fluids,
1/4\pi

Kovtun:2004de
. This allows one to investigate the behavior of QGP in the light of viscous hydrodynamics
Jaiswal:2016hex
;
Arnold:2012dqa
;
Florkowski:2015lra
;
Florkowski:2010zz
. Apart from the equation of state, transport coefficients such as shear viscosity and bulk viscosity are major supplements in the hydrodynamic equation, which governs the evolution of the fireball. While the equation of state for QGP is well understood from lattice QCD calculations, the transport coefficients are still not very well constrained. Therefore, it is important to accurately and consistently quantify the transport coefficients of QGP in order to understand the evolution of strongly interacting QCD matter formed in high-energy nucleus-nucleus collisions.

Considering these, there has been a notable endeavor in the past to determine the ratio
\eta/s
as well as the ratio of bulk viscosity to entropy density (
\zeta/s
) of the QGP as a function of the temperature (
T
). The transport coefficients of the hot QCD matter have been an active area of research for more than two decades and have been dealt with various approaches which include the kinetic theory approach, quasiparticle model, Chapman-Enskog method, Gribov plasma, Polyakov-quark-meson model
Jaiswal:2020qmj
;
Sasaki:2008fg
;
Torrieri:2007fb
;
Plumari:2011mk
;
Soloveva:2019xph
;
Berrehrah:2016vzw
;
Mitra:2017sjo
;
Singha:2017jmq
. The transport properties of the QGP have also been extensively studied in the high-temperature regime using resummed perturbation theory
Andersen:2004fp
as well as lattice calculations
HotQCD:2014kol
;
Meyer:2007dy
;
Meyer:2007ic
. In the phenomenologically relevant temperature range, typically
T\sim(1-4)T_{c}
, where
T_{c}
represents the critical temperature, it is evident that the perturbative calculation alone is not an efficient tool due to the limitations that arise when the coupling strength of the system becomes of the order of unity
Su:2015esa
. Due to this, a theoretical model that encapsulates the non-perturbative features is more favored for exploring the medium QGP.

Lattice QCD is a numerical approach that is best suited for reliable computations in the nonperturbative regime. However, lattice QCD is computationally expensive, and it is challenging to calculate dynamical quantities. Moreover, past studies based on lattice calculation have revealed that the results for transport coefficients are plagued by large inconsistencies, particularly in the phenomenologically relevant temperature regime
Meyer:2007dy
;
Meyer:2007ic
and are still inconclusive. Therefore, it is useful to have an alternative approach to incorporate nonperturbative features in the theory. In this work, we adopt the prescription suggested by Gribov in his seminal work Ref.
Gribov:1977wm
. Following this idea, Zwnaziger, in the later year, formulated the local and renormalizable action discussed in detail in Ref.
Zwanziger:1989mf
.

The Gribov’s prescription has been explored quite extensively encompassing a broad spectrum of topics
Bandyopadhyay:2015wua
;
Begun:2016lgx
;
Su:2014rma
;
Dudal:2017kxb
;
Bandyopadhyay:2023yjp
;
Wu:2022nbv
;
Canfora:2015yia
;
Gotsman:2020ryd
;
Debnath:2023dhs
. In
Kharzeev:2015xsa
, the connection between the Gribov parameter and the topological structure of the QCD vacuum has been explored. The results of this study shed light on a profound relationship between the Gribov-Zwanziger (GZ) quantization and the confinement-deconfinement phase transition. Earlier works, such as
Madni:2022bea
;
Fukushima:2013xsa
;
Dudal:2008sp
;
Burgio:2008jr
, have yielded promising agreement with lattice results, particularly in the infrared regime.

These compelling consistencies with lattice findings have motivated us to consider a Gribov-improved gluon propagator in investigating transport coefficients of the hot QCD matter. To consider the effect of quarks on the transport coefficients, we adopt a quasiparticle model
Gorenstein:1995vm
. A similar approach has been explored previously in
Mykhaylova:2020pfk
, where the influence of dynamical quarks on QGP transport coefficients was explored by incorporating the interactions within the effective mass through a temperature-dependent running coupling derived from lattice QCD thermodynamics.

The structure of this article is as follows: In Sec.
II

, we delve into the thermodynamics of gluonic plasma using the Gribov prescription within the kinetic theory. Additionally, we employ a quasiparticle approach for quarks within the framework of kinetic theory to analyze the thermodynamic properties of the Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP). We ensure the thermodynamic consistency is maintained within the desired temperature range by fixing the Gribov parameter and quasiparticle mass appropriately. Following this, we discussed the velocity of sound(

Chakraborty:2010fr

1\leq T/T_{c}\leq 3
). In Sec.
VI
, we explore the behavior of the ratio
\zeta/\eta
for Gribov-modified gluonic plasma as well as for the QGP. In Sec.
VII

II
Gribov Gluons

In order to counter the divergence in the functional integral in the quantization of the non-abelian gauge theory, gauge fixing seemed inevitable. The gauge fixing method in the quantization of the non-abelian gauge field was first proposed by Faddeev and Popov back in 1967, where the gauge fixing was ensured by incorporating the delta function into the path integral which, without the source term reads
Sainapha:2019cfn

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(0)=\int\mathcal{D}\alpha\int\mathcal{D}A\hskip 4.26773pt\delta(G(^{\alpha}\!A))e^{-S_{YM}}\Delta_{FP}\penalty\ ,

(1)

where
\Delta_{FP}
is the Faddeev-Popov determinant given as

\displaystyle\Delta_{FP}=\bigg|\frac{1}{g}{\rm det}\mathcal{M}_{ab}\bigg|=\bigg|{\rm det}\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}^{a}(^{\alpha}\!A))}{\delta\Lambda^{b}}\bigg|_{G(^{\Lambda}\!A)=0}\penalty\ ,

(2)

where
\mathcal{G}(^{\Lambda}\!A)
is the gauge constraint.
\Lambda^{b}
is the infinitesimal parameter of a particular gauge transformation
U=1-\Lambda^{b}T^{b}+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda^{2})
. The Faddeev-Popov operator is given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{ab}=-\partial_{\mu}D_{\mu}^{ab}\penalty\ .

(3)

In Eq. (
3
),
D_{\mu}^{ab}
is the covariant derivative. While the FP quantization has shown considerable success across various aspects, it remains incomplete. The root of the issue lies in our insistence on an ideal gauge fixing condition by stipulating that the gauge orbit must intersect the gauge fixing constraint surface precisely once and only once. However, in practical scenarios, this is not true and is known as Gribov ambiguity. Gribov’s approach, extensively reviewed in Refs
Vandersickel:2012tz
and
Sobreiro:2005ec
, focuses on improving the infrared dynamics of non-abelian gauge theory by fixing the residual gauge transformations that persist even after applying Faddeev-Popov quantization. Mathematically, it was achieved by restricting the path integral to a Gribov region
\Omega
in a way so chosen that for any gauge field configuration
A^{\mu}_{a}
,

\displaystyle\Omega=\{A_{a}^{\mu},\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}_{a}=0;\mathcal{M}_{ab}>0\}\penalty\ ,

(4)

where the gauge field
A^{\mu}_{a}
obeys the Landau gauge condition for which the Faddeev-Popov operator
(\mathcal{M}_{ab}=-\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{D}^{\mu}_{ab})
is strictly greater than 0 to ensure the no-pole condition required the overcome the Gribov ambiguity. Following this restriction on the gauge field configuration, the partition function in the Euclidean space-time reads as
Vandersickel:2012tz

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle=\int[\mathcal{D}c]\int[\mathcal{D}\bar{c}]\int[\mathcal{D}A]\hskip 2.84544ptV(\Omega)\delta(\partial\cdot A)

\displaystyle\times{\rm exp}\hskip 2.84544pt[-S_{YM}-\int d^{4}x\bar{c}^{a}(x)\partial_{\mu}D_{\mu}^{ab}c^{b}(x)]\penalty\ ,

(5)

where
c(x)
and
\bar{c}(x)
are the ghost and anti-ghost fields respectively. The term
V(\Omega)=\theta[1-\sigma_{0}]
is the step function needed to take care of the no-pole condition. Also,
[1-\sigma_{0}]=[1-\sigma(P=0)]
is the inverse of ghost dressing function, defined as
Z_{G}=[1-\sigma(P)]^{-1}
. In order to obtain the gluon two-point function, the constraint on the gauge fields is inserted in the Eq. (
5
) by re-expressing the parameter
V(\Omega)
as

\displaystyle V(\Omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-i\infty+0^{+}}^{i\infty+0^{+}}\frac{ds}{s}\hskip 2.84544pt{\rm exp}[s(1-\sigma_{0})]\penalty\ .

(6)

The Gribov modified gluon propagator in Landau gauge, with the dynamical mass term,
m_{g}
, is given as
Sobreiro:2004us

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{ab}_{\mu\nu}(p)=\delta^{ab}\frac{p^{2}}{p^{4}+m_{g}^{2}p^{2}+\gamma_{G}^{4}}\left[\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}{p^{2}}\right]\penalty\ ,

(7)

where
p=(p_{0},\vec{\textbf{{p}}})
, and the thermal mass
m_{g}
has the form
m_{g}=gT
and
\gamma_{G}
is the Gribov parameter which can be fitted from known lattice data of thermodynamics quantities or is solved self-consistently from a gap equation that is defined to infinite loop orders. In comparison to the normal gluon propagator, the Gribov-modified gluon propagator is infrared suppressed and has complex poles as
p^{2}=\pm i\gamma_{G}^{2}
. This configuration (with imaginary poles) prevents us from ascribing the conventional particle interpretation to the gluons, undermining the notion of gluons as excitations of the physical spectrum. Consequently, gluons experience confinement within the Gribov approach.

At one-loop, the analytical solution at asymptotically high temperature gives us
Zwanziger:2006sc

\displaystyle\gamma_{G}=\left(\frac{d-1}{d}\right)\frac{\rm N_{c}}{4\sqrt{2}\pi}g^{2}T,

(8)

where
d
is the space-time dimension and
\rm N_{c}
is the number of colors, and
g
, the running coupling with
T
being temperature. It is evident from Eq. (
8
) that the Gribov parameter is proportional to the magnetic scale, i.e.
\gamma_{G}\propto g^{2}T
. It is worth noting that the inclusion of the magnetic scale inherently suggests a promising resolution to IR catastrophe caused by the magnetic scale, called the Linde problem (see
Linde:1980ts
for detail). Therefore, the dynamical gluonic mass of the electric scale (
gT
) along with the Gribov parameter of magnetic scale (
g^{2}T
) could be utilized to explore the dynamics of the deconfined medium. A similar approach has been previously studied in the Refs
Bandyopadhyay:2015wua
where dilepton rate and quark number susceptibility have been worked out and also in Ref
Su:2014rma
to examine the quark self-energy at one-loop level at high temperature.

III.1.2
Quasi quarks

In the study of quark-gluon plasma, the quasi-particle concept describes the collective behavior of quarks and gluons within the plasma. The quasi-particles are effective descriptions and provide a useful way to understand and model the complex dynamics of strongly interacting systems under extreme conditions. In the quasi-particle approach, the energy of the particle is taken as
\epsilon_{q}=(\textbf{{p}}^{2}+m_{q}^{2})^{1/2}
, where
m_{q}\equiv m_{q}(T)
denotes the temperature-dependent mass of the quasi-particles, here, quarks. The distribution function of the quark, following the Fermi Dirac statistics, is given as

\displaystyle f_{q}(\epsilon_{q})=\left[{\rm exp}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{q}}{T}\right)+1\right]^{-1}\penalty\ .

(26)

In order to formulate the thermodynamics of quasi-quarks, the pressure and energy density are evaluated using Eq. (
10

\displaystyle\int dp=\frac{g_{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}\textbf{{p}}\int dp_{0}\hskip 2.84544pt2\ \Theta(p_{0})\hskip 2.84544pt\delta(p^{2}-m_{q}^{2})\penalty\ .

This fundamental relationship is essential in understanding the thermodynamic properties of the system under consideration, especially in scenarios where the conformal symmetry is perturbed. Consequently, this insight contributes significantly to comprehending fluid dynamics and thermodynamic behavior in various physical systems, making it paramount in heavy ion collision. V
Transport coefficients

In this section, we have presented the theoretical framework utilized for calculating the transport coefficients, namely the Shear (
\eta
) and Bulk (
\zeta
) viscosities for the hot QGP medium. The centerpiece of our approach lies in solving the Boltzmann equation, which serves as the fundamental equation in kinetic theory and plays a crucial role in describing the transport properties. As a result, the Boltzmann equation holds significant importance in the field of heavy ion collisions. The Boltzmann equation is mathematically expressed as

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-1}p^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}f^{eq}=\frac{\partial f^{eq}}{\partial t}+v\cdot\nabla f^{eq}=\textit{C}_{f}\penalty\ ,

(39)

where
f=f(\textbf{x},t,\textbf{p})
is distribution function and
\textit{C}_{f}
is the collision kernel.
In the relaxation time approximation(RTA), the collision kernel
\textit{C}_{f}
is

\displaystyle\textit{C}_{f}=-\frac{u\cdot p}{\tau_{a}}\delta f\penalty\ ,

(40)

where
\tau_{a}
is the relaxation time and the deviation away from equilibrium manifests itself via
\delta f
. Therefore, in RTA, the Boltzmann equation takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f^{eq}}{dt}+v\cdot\nabla f=-\omega(\epsilon)\delta f\penalty\ ,

(41)

where
\omega(\epsilon)=1/\tau(\epsilon)
.
Following the arguments of
Chakraborty:2010fr
, the expression for bulk (
\zeta
) and shear (
\eta
) viscosity takes the form

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{1}{3}\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\textbf{{p}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\textbf{{p}}^{2}}{\epsilon}f^{eq}(\epsilon/T)\mathcal{A}(\epsilon)\penalty\ .

(42)

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{2}{15}\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\textbf{{p}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\textbf{{p}}^{4}}{\epsilon}f^{eq}(\epsilon/T)\mathcal{C}(\epsilon)\penalty\ .

(43)

As it is evident from the above expression of
\zeta
(Eq. (
42
)) and
\eta
(Eq .(
43
)), we need to find the unknown scalar parameters
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
to find the temperature dependence of these two transport coefficients. These parameters will be determined by solving the Boltzmann equation using the local equilibrium distribution function in Gribov-modified gluonic plasma and then separately for the quasi-quark approach. This is not the end of it since we also have to see the consequence of the Landau-Lifshitz condition on these parameters, which will define the final expression of bulk (
\zeta
) and shear (
\eta
) viscosity.

We confine our analysis to the context of zero chemical potentials. The underlying assumption considers the system to be in a state of approximate local equilibrium, characterized by local temperature distribution,
T(x)
, along with a flow velocity,
u(x)
. Within the Landau-Lifshitz framework,
u(x)
signifies the energy transport velocity.

Bulk to Shear viscosity analysis

In this section, we present an analysis of the ratio bulk to shear viscosity(
\zeta/\eta
) for Gribov-modified gluonic plasma as well as the plasma consisting of Gribov gluons along with quasi-quarks. The importance of the ratio(
\zeta/\eta
) lies in the fact that it carries information about the breaking of conformal symmetry in the medium under consideration. The value of the (
\zeta/\eta
) ratio can have significant consequences for the behavior of the QGP. A low value of (
\zeta/\eta
) implies that shear viscosity dominates over bulk viscosity, indicating that the QGP medium responds more readily to changes in shape rather than volume. This can lead to a more fluid-like behavior and a smaller tendency for the system to develop mechanical instabilities. Conversely, a high value of (
\zeta/\eta
) suggests that bulk viscosity is relatively more significant, implying that volume-changing processes play a more prominent role, which could be seen near the transition region. Physically, it leads to slower equilibration and an increased likelihood of mechanical instabilities within the QGP.

VII
Summary and Outlook

In this study, we have developed a framework to explore the transport coefficients: shear viscosity (
\eta
) and bulk viscosity (
\zeta
) of the medium QGP. While extensive literature exists on the investigation of these coefficients in the perturbative domain, both for gluonic plasma
Czajka:2017wdo
;
Begun:2016lgx
along with the inclusion of quark
Borsanyi:2010cj
;
Mykhaylova:2020pfk
, it is, however, crucial to encompass the non-perturbative feature which has a sound effect near the phase transition region(from hadronic to QGP medium) when examining the transport coefficients of the QGP. Thus, our work aims to fill this gap by incorporating non-perturbative effects and considering the influence of dynamical quarks and Gribov-modified gluons to understand the transport properties near the crossover region comprehensively.

The Gribov idea, known for its efficacy in capturing non-perturbative effects, has been employed to analyze the thermodynamics of gluonic plasma. In the context of quark systems, we have explored the quasi-particle approach, which characterizes the energy as
\epsilon_{q}=(\textbf{{p}}^{2}+m_{q}^{2})^{1/2}
, where the quasi-mass of quark, denoted as
m_{q}
, has been determined by using lattice data of entropy density(
s/T^{3}
) from
Borsanyi:2010cj
. It is worth noting that we have captured the interaction of quarks and gluons in the quasi-mass
m_{q}
itself. The variation of the quasi-mass
m_{q}
as a function of scaled temperature has been depicted in Fig (
3
).

In Fig(
6
), we see the variation of the ratio of bulk to shear(
\zeta/\eta
)viscosity for the plasma consisting of Gribov-modified gluons along with the lattice findings from the mentioned sources. We see a decent match with the lattice findings, specifically near the transition region (
T/T_{c}\sim 1
). However, it should also be noted that the transport coefficients by lattice finding are plagued by large uncertainties near the phase transition region. We find that the ratio
(\zeta/\eta)
tends to zero at high temperatures. This is also termed the Stefan-Boltzmann limit. In other words, the system tends to regain its conformality.

The observations from Fig (
7
) indicate that in the context of strongly interacting matter, the ratio of specific bulk viscosity to entropy density
(\zeta/s\tau_{R}T_{c})
exhibits a peak in the vicinity of the critical temperature (
T_{c}
). This finding suggests the presence of a maximum value for the (
\zeta/s\tau_{R}T_{c}
) near transition temperature, underscoring the nontrivial behavior of the bulk viscosity in relation to temperature. Interestingly, we observed a significant change(suppression) in the behavior of the scaled bulk viscosity(
\zeta/s\tau_{R}T_{c}
) throughout the entire range of scaled temperature (
T/T_{c}
). The small value of the scaled bulk viscosity across the whole temperature range serves as a mitigating factor against the tendency of the fluid to undergo mechanical instability
Torrieri:2008ip
. Near
T\sim T_{c}
, the bulk viscosity of the QGP is relatively high, which implies that the medium is less efficient at restoring equilibrium. However, as the temperature increases beyond
T_{c}
, the specific bulk viscosity diminishes rapidly, converging towards its conformal limit.

In the Fig(
8
), we also see in the proximity of the critical temperature
T_{c}
, the scaled shear viscosity (
\eta/s\tau_{R}T_{c}
) demonstrates a pronounced minimum. The minimum value of shear viscosity near the critical temperature (
T_{c}
) in QGP is a consequence of the collective behavior of the system. The subsequent increase in the temperature can be understood in terms of the changing nature of particle interactions and the transition from a strongly interacting, collective state to a weakly interacting state. The rise in shear viscosity with increasing temperature is attributed to the increasing dominance of particle collisions and scattering processes. These interactions contribute to the transfer of momentum across different fluid regions, resulting in a higher resistance to shear deformation and, therefore, an elevated shear viscosity. It is worth noting that while incorporating the effects of quasi-quarks along with Gribov-modified gluons, the scaled transport coefficients(
\zeta/s\tau_{R}T_{c}
and
\eta/s\tau_{R}T_{c}
) got suppressed for the entire range of temperature(
1\leq T/T_{c}\leq 3
).

In Fig(
10
) and Fig(
11
), we have mapped the variation of the ratio(
\zeta/\eta
) to that on the linear and quadratic dependence on the conformal parameter,
\Delta c_{s}^{2}=(1/3-c_{s}^{2})
for
N_{f}=0
and
N_{f}=3
case. The fit parameters are (
\alpha
and
\beta
for linear dependence) and (
\gamma
and
\delta
for quadratic dependence). In our findings, we notice that both fit well with the quadratic dependence at higher temperatures, while near the transition region, we have a good fit with linear scaling. The similar calculation in
Mykhaylova:2020pfk
shows a good fit with linear scaling for
T/T_{c}\leq 1.3
for
N_{f}=0
and beyond
T/T_{c}\sim 1.4
, the quadratic dependence is observed. A similar nature is observed for the
N_{f}=3
case; however, the shift from linear to quadratic dependence is now observed near
T/T_{c}\sim 2
. In our case, for
N_{f}=0
, the shift from linear to quadratic scaling occurs near
T/T_{c}\sim 1.5
, and for
N_{f}=3
, the same shift occurs near
T/T_{c}\sim 2.5
.

The current approach in exploring the transport coefficients of QGP does not aim to serve as a precise phenomenological model for a thorough investigation of the transport coefficients (
\eta
and
\zeta
). Instead, it could be considered as a starting point of yet another means to incorporate non-perturbative effects by Gribov prescription while studying the transport coefficients of the hot QGP medium. Incorporating non-perturbative effects is crucial, particularly in the vicinity of the transition region where the deconfinement occurs. Such an investigation holds immense significance as it deepens our understanding of the complex connection between non-perturbative physics, transport coefficients, and the phenomenon of deconfinement. By considering these non-perturbative effects, our approach contributes to a more comprehensive analysis of the transport properties of the QGP medium, especially in the critical region where significant changes in the system occur.

In future research, the relaxation time (
\tau_{R}
) could be further investigated using either momentum-dependent or momentum-independent approaches. Additionally, to enhance the reliability of this framework, it would be valuable to consider alternative means of determining the Gribov parameter
\gamma_{G}
, which is not just the analytic form of it in the asymptotic limit as presented in Eq. (
8
). One possibility is by fitting it within a desired temperature range using available lattice data of thermodynamic parameters, running coupling, or solving the one-loop gap equation as demonstrated in
Fukushima:2013xsa
. Apart from these, the flavor dependence of the system to recover the conformal limit could also be explored exclusively. By incorporating these refinements, we can establish a more robust foundation for assessing the validity of our findings, particularly by comparing them to lattice calculations of scaled transport coefficients available in Refs.
Nakamura:2004sy
;
Sakai:2007cm
;
Astrakhantsev:2018oue
;
Meyer:2007dy
. This comparative analysis encompasses both the pure Yang-Mills case and also the case with quark effects incorporated. Such endeavors will contribute to a comprehensive understanding of the transport properties of the system and provide valuable insights into the interplay between non-perturbative effects, thermodynamics, and the role of quasi-quarks in the transport properties of medium QGP.

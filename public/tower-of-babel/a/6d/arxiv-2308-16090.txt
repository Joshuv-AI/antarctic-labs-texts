Tensor-Hom formalism for modules over nonunital rings
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. We say that a ring R R is t-unital if the natural map R ⊗ R R ⟶ R R\otimes_{R}R\longrightarrow R is an isomorphism, and a left R R -module P P is c-unital if the natural map P ⟶ Hom R ⁡ ( R , P ) P\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,P) is an isomorphism. For a t-unital ring R R , the category of t-unital left R R -modules is a unital left module category over an associative, unital monoidal category of t-unital R R - R R -bimodules, while the category of c-unital left R R -modules is opposite to a unital right module category over the same monoidal category. Any left s-unital ring R R , as defined by Tominaga in 1975, is t-unital; and a left R R -module is s-unital if and only if it is t-unital. For any (nonunital) ring R R , the full subcategory of s-unital R R -modules is a hereditary torsion class in the category of nonunital R R -modules; and for rings R R arising from small preadditive categories, the full subcategory of c-unital R R -modules is closed under kernels and cokernels. However, over a t-unital ring R R , the full subcategory of t-unital modules need not be closed under kernels, and the full subcategory of c-unital modules need not be closed under cokernels in the category of nonunital modules. Nevertheless, the categories of t-unital and c-unital left R R -modules are abelian and naturally equivalent to each other; they are also equivalent to the quotient category of the abelian category of nonunital modules by the Serre subcategory of modules with zero action of R R . This is a particular case of the result from a 1996 manuscript of Quillen. We also discuss related flatness, projectivity, and injectivity properties; and study the behavior of t-unitality and c-unitality under the restriction of scalars for a homomorphism of nonunital rings. Our motivation comes from the theory of semialgebras over coalgebras over fields.

\widetilde{R}/R\otimes_{\widetilde{R}}M=0=\Tor_{1}^{\widetilde{R}}(\widetilde{R}/R,M)
.
(This observation is a particular case of the discussion
in
[
22
, Definition 2.3]
.)

Now the mentioned results from
[
9
,
18
]
tell that the full
subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is closed under kernels in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
(and,
consequently, abelian) whenever the flat dimension of the unital right

\widetilde{R}
-module
\widetilde{R}/R
does not exceed
1
, i. e.,
in other words, the unital
\widetilde{R}
-module
R
is flat.
This can be also easily seen directly from the definition: if
the functor
R\otimes_{R}{-}\,\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
is exact (preserves
kernels), then the full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
is
closed under kernels.
The next example shows that this does
not
hold in general.

Example 1.7
.

Let
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
be the commutative ring of polynomials
in two variables
x
,
y
with integer coefficients.
Let
R\subset\widetilde{R}
be the ideal
R=(x,y)\subset\widetilde{R}

spanned by the elements
x
,
y\in~\widetilde{R}
.
Then it is clear that the ring
\widetilde{R}
is the unitalization
of the ring
R
, just as the notation suggests.
The flat dimension of the unital
\widetilde{R}
-module

\mathbb{Z}=\widetilde{R}/R
is equal to
2
, and the flat dimension of
the unital
\widetilde{R}
-module
R
is equal to
1
.
We claim that the full subcategory of t-unital
R
-modules

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is
not
closed under kernels in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}=\widetilde{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.

Indeed, consider the generalized Prüfer module

P_{x}=\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]/\mathbb{Z}[x]
over the ring
\mathbb{Z}[x]
, and the similar
module
P_{y}=\mathbb{Z}[y,y^{-1}]/\mathbb{Z}[y]
over the ring
\mathbb{Z}[y]
.
Furthermore, consider the
\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
-module
P_{x}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P_{y}
.
Then one can easily see that
\Tor^{\mathbb{Z}[x]}_{0}(\mathbb{Z},P_{x})=0

and
\Tor^{\mathbb{Z}[x]}_{1}(\mathbb{Z},P_{x})=\mathbb{Z}
(where
x
acts by zero in
the
\mathbb{Z}[x]
-module
\mathbb{Z}
).
Consequently, by the Künneth formula (see
Lemma
4.4
below),

\Tor_{n}^{\widetilde{R}}(\mathbb{Z},\>P_{x}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P_{y})=0
for
n=0
,
1
,
and
\mathbb{Z}
for
n=2
.
Following Remark
1.6
, we have

P_{x}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P_{y}\in R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
.

On the other hand, denote by
Q_{y}
the
\mathbb{Z}[y]
-module
P_{y}
viewed
as a
\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
-module with the given (Prüfer) action of
y

and the zero action of
x
.
Then
\Tor_{1}^{\widetilde{R}}(\mathbb{Z},Q_{y})=\Tor_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}[y]}(\mathbb{Z},P_{y})=\mathbb{Z}
.
So
Q_{y}\notin R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
.
Nevertheless,
Q_{y}
is the kernel of the (surjective)
R
-module
(or
\widetilde{R}
-module) map
x\colon P_{x}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P_{y}\longrightarrow P_{x}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}P_{y}
.

Notice, however, that the ring
R
in this example is
not

t-unital.
In fact,
R
is not even an idempotent ideal in the unital
ring
\widetilde{R}
, i. e.,
R^{2}\neq R
.
An example of t-unital ring
T
for which the full subcategory

T{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is not closed under kernels in
T{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
will be given
below in Example
4.7
.

For any t-unital ring
R
,
Theorem
5.8
(a) below tells that
the category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is abelian.
On the other hand, again over a t-unital ring
R
, according to
Corollary
7.4
, the full subcategory
of t-unital modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is closed under kernels in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}

if and only if

R
is a flat unital right
\widetilde{R}
-module.

Remark 1.8
.

Speaking of unital modules over a unital ring
A
(such as

A=\widetilde{R}
), we often emphasize that we work in the category
of unital
A
-modules (e. g., that particular modules are flat or
projective
as unital modules
), but it actually does not
matter that much.
Nonunital modules over any ring
R
are the same things as unital
modules over the unitalization
\widetilde{R}
of the ring
R
,
and the unitalization
\widetilde{A}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus A
of a
unital

ring
A
is naturally isomorphic to the Cartesian product of
the rings
\mathbb{Z}
and
A
, that is
\widetilde{A}\simeq\mathbb{Z}\times A
.
The ring isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\oplus A\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\times A
takes a formal
sum
n+a
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a\in A
to the pair
(n,\>ne+a)
,
where
e\in A
is the unit element.
Accordingly, the abelian category of nonunital
A
-modules is just
the Cartesian product of the abelian categories of abelian groups and
unital
A
-modules,

A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\simeq\widetilde{A}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}\times A){\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\times A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}.

So there is not much of a difference whether to consider unital
modules over unital rings as objects of the category of unital or
nonunital modules over such ring.
(Cf. the discussion in
[
22
, §1]
, which emphasizes
a different point.)

In particular, all the groups/modules
\Tor
and
\Ext
in this paper
are taken over unital rings and in the categories of unital modules.

The following definition is due to Tominaga
[

,

,

]
.
The exposition in the survey paper
[

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a (nonunital) ring.
A left
R
-module
M
is said to be
s-unital
if for every
element
m\in M
there exists an element
e\in R
such that
em=m

in
M
.
(Notice that
no
assumption of idempotency of
e
is made here!)
Similarly, a right
R
-module
N
is said to be
s-unital
if
for every element
n\in N
there exists an element
e\in R
such that

ne=n
in
N
.
Given two rings
R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime\prime}
, an
R^{\prime}
-
R^{\prime\prime}
-bimodule is said to be

s-unital
if it is s-unital both as a left
R^{\prime}
-module and
as a right
R^{\prime\prime}
-module.

Given three rings
R
,
R^{\prime}
,
R^{\prime\prime}
, we will denote by
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{s}Mod}}}
,

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{s}--}}}R
, and
R^{\prime}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{s}Bimod^{s}--}}}R^{\prime\prime}
the full subcategories of
s-unital left
R
-modules, s-unital right
R
-modules, and
s-unital
R^{\prime}
-
R^{\prime\prime}
-bimodules in the abelian categories

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
,
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R
, and
R^{\prime}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Bimod^{n}--}}}R^{\prime\prime}
, respectively.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
R
be an arbitrary (nonunital) ring.
Then the full subcategory of s-unital
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{s}Mod}}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
is closed under submodules, quotients, extensions, and all
colimits (including direct sums and direct limits) in the abelian
category of nonunital modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
In other words,
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{s}Mod}}}
is a
hereditary torsion class
in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
in the sense of
[
5
]
,
[
23
, Sections VI.2–3]
.

Proof.

Closedness under submodules, quotients, and direct limits (indexed by
directed posets) is obvious immediately from the definition.
All direct sums are direct limits of finite direct sums, and finite
direct sums are finitely iterated extensions.
So it suffices to prove the closedness under extensions.

Let
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence
of nonunital left
R
-modules.
Assume that the
R
-modules
K
and
M
are s-unital.
Given an element
l\in L
, we need to find an element
g\in R
such
that
gl=l
in
L
.
Since the
R
-module
M=L/K
is s-unital, there exists an element

e\in R
such that
e(l+K)=l+K
in
M
.
So we have
el-l\in K
.
Since the
R
-module
K
is s-unital, there exists an element

f\in R
such that
f(el-l)=el-l
in
K
.
Now we have
(e+f-fe)l=l
in
L
, and it remains to put

g=e+f-ef\in R
.
∎

The following result goes back to Tominaga
[

]
.
It can be also found in

, Proposition 2.8 of the published

Corollary 2.3

(Tominaga
[

)
.

Let

be a ring and

be an s-unital left

-module.
Then, for any finite collection of elements

, …,

there exists an element

such that

for all

Proof.

By Proposition

, the

is s-unital.
By the definition, this means existence of the desired element

for the element

.

Definition 2.5

(Tominaga

,

.

A ring

Similarly,

The following theorem is due to Tominaga
[
25
, Remark (2)
in Section 1 on p. 121–122]
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
25
]
)
.

(a)
Let
R
be a left s-unital ring.
Then, for any s-unital left
R
-module
M
, the natural map

R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M
(
10
) is
an isomorphism.
In other words, any s-unital left
R
-module is t-unital.

(b)
Let
R
be a right s-unital ring.
Then, for any s-unital right
R
-module
N
, the natural map

N\otimes_{R}R\longrightarrow N
(
11
) is
an isomorphism.
In other words, any s-unital right
R
-module is t-unital.

Proof.

Let us prove part (a).
For this purpose, we will construct an inverse map

\phi_{M}\colon M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muR\otimes_{R}M

to the multiplication map (
10
).
Given an element
m\in M
, pick an arbitrary element
e\in R

such that
em=m
in
M
, and put
\phi(m)=e\otimes m\in R\otimes_{R}M
.

The key step is to check that the map
\phi
is well-defined.
The assumption of left s-unitality of the ring
R
is used here.

Let
f\in R
be another element such that
fm=m
in
M
.
Since the left
R
-module
R
is s-unital,
Corollary
2.3
(for
n=2
) tells that
there exists an element
g\in R
such that
ge=e
and
gf=f

in
R
.
Now we have

e\otimes m=ge\otimes m=g\otimes em=g\otimes m=g\otimes fm=gf\otimes m=f\otimes m

in
R\otimes_{R}M
.
Here the first and sixth equations hold by the choice of
the element
g
, the second and fifth equations are provided
by the definition of the tensor product, and the third and fourth
equations hold by the conditions imposed on the elements
e
and
f
.
Thus the map
\phi
is well-defined.

To check that the map
\phi
is additive (i. e., an abelian
group homomorphism), we apply Corollary
2.3

(for
n=2
) to the s-unital
R
-module
M
.
Given two elements
m^{\prime}
and
m^{\prime\prime}\in M
, the corollary tells that
there exists an element
e\in R
such that
em^{\prime}=m^{\prime}
and
em^{\prime\prime}=m^{\prime\prime}

in
M
.
Hence
e(m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime})=m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}
.
Now it is clear that
\phi(m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime})=e\otimes(m^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime})=e\otimes m^{\prime}+e\otimes m^{\prime\prime}=\phi(m^{\prime})+\phi(m^{\prime\prime})
.

One can also check directly that
\phi
is a left
R
-module map
(though we will not need this).
Given any elements
r\in R
and
m\in M
, choose an element
f\in R

such that
fr=r
in
R
.
Then
frm=rm
in
M
.
Also, choose an element
e\in R
such that
em=m
in
M
.
Now we have
\phi(rm)=f\otimes rm=fr\otimes m=r\otimes m=r\otimes em=re\otimes m=r\phi(m)
in
R\otimes_{R}M
.

What we need to check, though, is that both the compositions

M\longrightarrow R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M
and
R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow R\otimes_{R}M

are identity maps.
In the case of the former composition, this is immediate from
the constructions.
Concerning the latter composition, we know that both the maps being
composed are abelian group homomorphisms; so it suffices to compute
the action of the composition in question on decomposable tensors
in
R\otimes_{R}M
.

Once again, given any elements
r\in R
and
m\in M
, let us
choose an element
f\in R
such that
fr=r
in
R
.
Then
frm=m
in
M
.
Finally, we have
r\otimes m\longmapsto rm\longmapsto f\otimes rm=fr\otimes m=r\otimes m
in
R\otimes_{R}M
.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Any left s-unital ring is t-unital.
Similarly, any right s-unital ring is t-unital.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
2.6

and the definition of a t-unital ring.
∎

In Corollary

below, we will show
that, for any left s-unital ring

-module

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
R^{\prime}
and

be arbitrary (nonunital) rings.

(a)
Let

be an
R^{\prime}
-
R^{\prime\prime}
-bimodule and

(b)

Example 2.11
.

Here is an example of a finitely generated free abelian group
R
with
a structure of associative ring which is t-unital but
neither

left
nor
right s-unital.

(0) We start from the observation that any homomorphic image of
a left (right) s-unital ring is left (right) s-unital.
This follows immediately from the definitions.

(1) Another simple observation is that the direct product of any two
t-unital rings is t-unital.
Let
R
and
S
be t-unital rings, and let
T=R\times S
be their
direct product (with componentwise addition and multiplication).
Let us show that the ring
T
is t-unital.
It is clear that
R\otimes_{T}R=R\otimes_{R}R=R
and
S\otimes_{T}S=S\otimes_{S}S=S
.
It remains to check that
R\otimes_{T}S=0
and
S\otimes_{T}R=0
.
Here we notice that any t-unital ring is idempotent, i. e.,
R^{2}=R
.
Now we have
r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{T}s=r^{\prime}\otimes_{T}r^{\prime\prime}s=0
for any
r^{\prime}
,
r^{\prime\prime}\in R

and
s\in S
, and similarly
s\otimes_{T}r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}=0
.

(2) The following example of a left unital, but not right s-unital
associative ring
D
can be found in
[
12
, Solution to
Exercise 1.10(b)]
and
[
16
, Example 2.6 of the published version
or Example 6 of the
arXiv
version]
.
Consider the matrix ring
D=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
Explicitly,
D\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
is the free abelian group with
two generators endowed with the multiplication
(a,b)(c,d)=(ac,ad)

for all
a
,
b
,
c
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the ring
D
has a left unit
(1,0)
, but is not right
s-unital, as
(0,1)D=0
in
D
.

(3) The ring
D
is left s-unital, so it is t-unital by
Corollary
2.7
.
Similarly, the opposite ring
D^{\mathrm{op}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\mathbb{Z}\\
0&\mathbb{Z}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

is right s-unital (hence t-unital), but not left s-unital.
Now the ring
R=D\times D^{\mathrm{op}}
is t-unital by (1).
But both the rings
D
and
D^{\mathrm{op}}
are homomorphic images of
R
,
so
R
is neither left nor right s-unital by (0).

An example of
commutative
associative ring
R
which is
t-unital but not s-unital will be given below in
Example
4.1
.

(13)

Definition 3.1
.

We will say that a left

the natural map

(

What we call “c-unital modules” are called “closed modules”

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B
be an
R^{\prime}

Remark 3.6
.

Similarly to Remark

4.
Counterexamples

In this section we provide some more advanced versions of

,

2.11

All the examples in this section are commutative, associative rings.
We start with an example of commutative t-unital but not

Example 4.1
.

Here is an example of a
commutative
associative ring
R
which
is t-unital but
not
s-unital (see
Example
2.11
for an easier
noncommutative example).
Let
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{Z}[z^{q}\mid q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}]
be the free abelian
group spanned by the formal expressions
z^{q}
, where
z
is a variable
and
q
ranges over the nonnegative rational numbers.
The multiplication given by the obvious rule
z^{q^{\prime}}z^{q^{\prime\prime}}=z^{q^{\prime}+q^{\prime\prime}}

makes
\widetilde{R}
an associative, commutative, unital ring.
Let
R\subset\widetilde{R}
be the subgroup spanned by
z^{q}
with

q>0
.
Then
R
is an ideal in
\widetilde{R}
, and
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus R

is the unitalization of the nonunital ring
R
.
We claim that
R
is a t-unital, but not s-unital ring.

Indeed, for any nonzero element
r\in R
one has
r\notin Rr
, so

R
is not s-unital.
In other words, the ring
R
is not s-unital, since
R\neq 0
and
the ring
\widetilde{R}
has no zero-divisors.
On the other hand, the multiplication map
R\otimes_{R}R\longrightarrow R
is
surjective, since one has
z^{q/2}\otimes z^{q/2}\longmapsto z^{q}
for
every positive rational number
q
.
To show that the multiplication map is also injective, consider
any quadruple of positive rational numbers
a
,
b
,
c
,
d
such
that
a+b=c+d
in
\mathbb{Q}
.
We have to show that
z^{a}\otimes z^{b}=z^{c}\otimes z^{d}
in
R\otimes_{R}R
.
Indeed, if
a=c
and
b=d
, then there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise, assume without loss of generality that
a>c
and
b<d
.
Then we have
z^{a}\otimes z^{b}=z^{c}z^{a-c}\otimes z^{b}=z^{c}\otimes z^{a-c}z^{b}=z^{c}\otimes z^{d}
in
R\otimes_{R}R
, as
a-c+b=d
in
\mathbb{Q}
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}[z^{q}\mid q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}]
be the ring from
Example
4.1
.
Notice that

is a flat
unital
\widetilde{R}
-module.
According to Remark
1.6

that the full subcategory of t-unital

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}

So

is not s-unital).

On the other hand, the unital
\widetilde{R}
-module

is

projective.
Indeed, the telescope construction provides a short exact sequence of

\widetilde{R}
-modules

If

\widetilde{R}
-module, it would follow

The image of the element

Since

-modules,

According to Corollary
7.6

below, we can conclude that the full subcategory

is

not
closed under cokernels in

.
(Still, the category

Example 4.3
.

Here is an example of an associative, commutative ring

Let

Denote by

Put

For any positive rational numbers

Lemma 4.4

(Künneth formula)
.

Let
A
and
B
be associative, unital rings.
Let
K
be a right
A
-module,
N
be a right
B
-module,

L
be a left
A
-module, and
M
be a left
B
-module.
Assume that all the abelian groups
A
,
B
,
K
,
N
,
L
,
M

have no torsion; and assume further that all the abelian groups

\Tor_{i}^{A}(K,L)
and
\Tor_{j}^{B}(N,M)
,
i
,
j\geq 0
, have
no torsion, either.
Then there is a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

\Tor_{n}^{A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B}(K\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N,\>L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M)\simeq\bigoplus\nolimits_{i+j=n}\Tor_{i}^{A}(K,L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Tor_{j}^{B}(N,M)

for every
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Pick a flat resolution
F_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
for the left
A
-module
L

and a flat resolution
G_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
for the left
B
-module
M
.
Then
F_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is a flat resolution for the left

(A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B)
-module
L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M
, and one can compute

(K\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N)\otimes_{A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}B}(F_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})=(K\otimes_{A}F_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(N\otimes_{B}G_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
.
Finally, for any (bounded above) complexes of torsion-free abelian
groups
C_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
and
D_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
with torsion-free homology groups, one has

H_{n}(C_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}D_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}H_{i}(C_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{j}(D_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
for all

.

Remark 4.5
.

Let

As a particular case of Remark

.

5.
The Main Abelian Category Equivalence

Let
R
be a (nonunital) ring.
We will say that a left
R
-module
M
is a
null-module
if
the action of
R
in
M
is zero, i. e.,
rm=0
in
M
for all

r\in R
and
m\in M
.
Right null-modules are defined similarly.
We will denote the full subcategories of null-modules by

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
and
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{0}--}}}R\subset{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R
.
(Cf.
[
22
, Definitions 2.1 and 7.1]
,

[
7
, Section 2.1.3]
.)

Notice that the full subcategory of null-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
is closed
under submodules, quotients, infinite direct sums, and infinite products
(hence under all limits and colimits) in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
The following easy lemma says more.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
R
be an idempotent ring, i. e.,
R^{2}=R
.
Then the full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
is closed under extensions in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.

Proof.

Let
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
with
K
,
M\in R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
.
Given an element
l\in L
, it suffices to show that
r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}l=0
in
L

for all
r^{\prime}
,
r^{\prime\prime}\in R
.
Now we have
r^{\prime\prime}(l+K)=0
in
M
, since
M
is a null-module.
Hence
r^{\prime\prime}l\in K\subset L
.
Finally
r^{\prime}(r^{\prime\prime}l)=0
in
K
, since
K
is a null-module.
∎

Thus, over an idempotent ring
R
, the full subcategory of null-modules
is a thick subcategory, or in a different terminology, a
Serre
subcategory
in the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}

[
10
, Section 1.11]
,
[
8
, Section III.1]
.
Therefore, the abelian Serre quotient category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}

is defined.
Ignoring set-theoretical issues, one can say that the quotient category

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
can be obtained from
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
by inverting all
the morphisms whose kernel and cokernel belong to
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
.
(See
[
10
,
8
]
for a more concrete construction.)

The aim of this section is to explain that, for a t-unital ring
R
,
both the categories of t-unital and c-unital left
R
-modules are
actually abelian, and naturally equivalent to each other and to
the Serre quotient category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
,

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}.

A more general version of this result is due to
Quillen
[
22
, Theorems 4.5 and 5.6]
.

The following lemma can be compared
to
[
1
, Proposition 3.1(b)]
(cf. the paragraph preceding
Remark
4.5
above).
What we call t-unital modules are called “comodules” in
the context of
[

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
Then the functor
R\otimes_{R}{-}\,\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is right adjoint
to the fully faithful inclusion functor
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
For any left
R
-module
M
, the natural map
R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M
is
the adjunction counit.

Proof.

For any left
R
-module
M
, the left
R
-module
R\otimes_{R}M
is
t-unital by Corollary
1.3
(a).
Now let
L
be a t-unital left
R
-module.
We need to construct a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

\Hom_{R}(L,M)\simeq\Hom_{R}(L,\>R\otimes_{R}M)
.
For this purpose, to any
R
-module morphism
f\colon L\longrightarrow M

we assign the morphism
R\otimes_{R}f\colon L\simeq R\otimes_{R}L\longrightarrow R\otimes_{R}M
.
To any
R
-module morphism
g\colon L\longrightarrow R\otimes_{R}M
we assign
the composition
L\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M
.

Let us check that these two maps between the two groups of morphisms
are inverse to each other.
Starting with a morphism
f\colon L\longrightarrow M
, it is clear that
the composition
L\simeq R\otimes_{R}L\longrightarrow R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M

is equal to
f
.
Starting with a morphism
g\colon L\longrightarrow R\otimes_{R}M
, producing
the related morphism
f\colon L\longrightarrow M
, and coming back to a new morphism

g^{\prime}\colon L\longrightarrow R\otimes_{R}M
via the constructions above, in order to check
that
g^{\prime}=g
one needs to observe that the two multiplication maps

R\otimes_{R}R\otimes_{R}M\rightrightarrows R\otimes_{R}M
are equal to each other.
The latter property holds by the definition of the tensor product
of
R
-modules
R\otimes_{R}M
.
∎

[

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

be a t-unital ring.
Then the functor
\Hom_{R}(R,{-})\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
is left
adjoint to the fully faithful inclusion functor

R
-module

, the natural map

Proof.

For any left

.
Now let

\Hom_{R}(P,Q)\simeq\Hom_{R}(\Hom_{R}(R,P),Q)

For this purpose, to any
R
-module morphism

P\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,P)\longrightarrow Q

Lemma 5.4

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
R
be an arbitrary (nonunital) ring. Then

(a)
for any left
R
-module
M
, the kernel and cokernel of
the natural
R
-module morphism
R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M
are null-modules;

(b)
for any left
R
-module
P
, the kernel and cokernel of
the natural
R
-module morphism
P\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,P)
are null-modules.

Proof.

Part (a): the cokernel of the map
R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M
is obviously
the maximal null quotient module of
M
.
Concerning the kernel, let
\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\otimes m_{i}\in R\otimes_{R}M
be
an element annihilated by the map
R\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M
.
Then, for any

Part (b): the kernel of the map

is obviously
the maximal null submodule of
M
.
Concerning the cokernel, let
f\in\Hom_{R}(R,P)
be a morphism and

r\in R
be an element.
Then the
R
-module morphism

(rf)(r^{\prime})=f(r^{\prime}r)=r^{\prime}f(r)
for all

, comes from the element

f(r)\in P
via the natural map

.
So
R
acts by zero on the coset of the element

in the cokernel
of the map
P\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,P)
.
∎

In the proof of part (b) of the next lemma, the following well-known

E
be a right

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring,
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus R
be its
unitalization, and
N
be a left null-module over
R
.
Then

(a)

R\otimes_{R}N=R\otimes_{\widetilde{R}}N=0=\Tor^{\widetilde{R}}_{1}(R,N)
;

(b)

\Hom_{R}(R,N)=\Hom_{\widetilde{R}}(R,N)=0=\Ext_{\widetilde{R}}^{1}(R,N)
.

Proof.

Part (a): one has
R\otimes_{R}N=0
for any null-module
N
over
an idempotent ring
R
, as
r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}\otimes n=r^{\prime}\otimes r^{\prime\prime}n=0
in
R\otimes_{R}N

for any
r^{\prime}
,
r^{\prime\prime}\in R
and
n\in N
.
To prove the
\Tor_{1}
vanishing, notice that the category of (left)
null-modules over
R
is isomorphic to the category of abelian groups:
any abelian group can be uniquely endowed with a null-module structure.
Consider a short exact sequence of abelian groups
0\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
with a free abelian group
F
, and view it as
a short exact sequence of left null-modules over
R
.
We have already seen that
R\otimes_{\widetilde{R}}G=0
.
Following Remark
1.6
, we have

\Tor^{\widetilde{R}}_{1}(R,\mathbb{Z})=\Tor^{\widetilde{R}}_{1}(R,\widetilde{R}/R)=0
, since
R
is a t-unital right
R
-module.
Hence
\Tor^{\widetilde{R}}_{1}(R,F)=0
, and we can conclude that

\Tor^{\widetilde{R}}_{1}(R,N)=0
.

Part (b): one has
\Hom_{R}(R,N)=0
for any null-module
N
over
an idempotent ring
R
, as
f(r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime})=r^{\prime}f(r^{\prime\prime})=0
in
N
for any

r^{\prime}
,
r^{\prime\prime}\in R
and
f\in\Hom_{R}(R,N)
.
To prove the
\Ext^{1}
vanishing, consider a short exact sequence of
abelian groups
0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow 0
, where
J
is
a product of copies of
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, and view it as a short exact
sequence of left null-modules over
R
.
We have already seen that
\Hom_{\widetilde{R}}(R,K)=0
.
By Remark
1.6
, we have

\Tor^{\widetilde{R}}_{1}(\mathbb{Z},R)=\Tor^{\widetilde{R}}_{1}(\widetilde{R}/R,R)=0
, since
R
is a t-unital left
R
-module.
Hence, viewing
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
as an abelian group
and
a left
null-module
{}_{\widetilde{R}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, and viewing
\mathbb{Z}
as a right

\mathbb{Z}_{\widetilde{R}}
, we have

\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{R}}(R,{}_{\widetilde{R}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})=\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{R}}(R,\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\widetilde{R}},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Tor_{1}^{\widetilde{R}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\widetilde{R}},R),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})=0
.
Therefore,
\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{R}}(R,J)=0
, and we can conclude that

\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{R}}(R,N)=0
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and

be a left
R
-module

(a)

(b)
the left

Remark 5.9
.

Notice that the functor

exact in general.

The inclusion functor
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
is also
not

It is only the composition
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
that is exact.

Similarly, the functor

not

The inclusion functor

The full subcategory

in the sense of

Dually, the full subcategory

This terminology means that the inclusion functor

In the proof of the theorem, the following observation is used.
Let
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{D}
be two categories, and let
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{C}

and
\mathsf{T}\subset\mathsf{D}
be two multiplicative subsets of morphisms.
Consider the localizations
\mathsf{C}[\mathsf{S}^{-1}]
and
\mathsf{D}[\mathsf{T}^{-1}]

of the categories
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{D}
.
Let

and

be a pair of adjoint
functors, with the functor

left adjoint to

Assume that the functors

The adjunction unit and counit for the pair

.

6.
Rings Arising from Small Preadditive Categories

Let
R
be a (nonunital) ring.
A
family of orthogonal idempotents

(e_{x}\in R)_{x\in X}
in
R

is a family of elements such that
e_{x}^{2}=e_{x}
for all
x\in X
and

e_{x}e_{y}=0
for all
x\neq y\in X
.
A family of orthogonal idempotents
(e_{x})_{x\in X}
in
R
is said
to be
complete
if

R=\bigoplus_{x\in X}Re_{x}=\bigoplus_{x\in X}e_{x}R
,
or equivalently,
R=\bigoplus_{x,y\in X}e_{y}Re_{x}
.
Following the terminology of
[
16
]
(going back to
[
6
]
),
we will say that
R
is
a ring with enough idempotents
if
there is a chosen complete family of orthogonal idempotents in
R
.

The following lemma is easy and well-known
[
16
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Any ring with enough idempotents in s-unital
(and consequently, t-unital).

Proof.

The point is that for any element
r\in R
one can choose a finite
subset
Z\subset X
such that the (idempotent) element

e=\sum_{z\in Z}e_{z}\in R
satisfies
er=r=re
.
The assertion in parentheses follows by
Corollary
2.7
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
R
be a ring with enough idempotents and
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus R
be its unitalization.
Then
R
is an (infinitely generated) projective unital left

\widetilde{R}
-module and projective unital right

\widetilde{R}
-module.
Specifically, one has

(21)

R=\bigoplus\nolimits_{x\in X}\widetilde{R}e_{x}=\bigoplus\nolimits_{x\in X}e_{x}\widetilde{R},

where, for every
x\in X
, the left
\widetilde{R}
-module

\widetilde{R}e_{x}=Re_{x}
is projective with a single generator
e_{x}
,
and the right
\widetilde{R}
-module
e_{x}\widetilde{R}=e_{x}R
is
projective with a single generator
e_{x}
.

Proof.

Notice that one has
\widetilde{R}r=Rr
and
r\widetilde{R}=rR

for any s-unital ring
R
and element
r\in R
.
With this simple observation, all the assertions of the lemma
follow immediately from the definition of a ring with enough
idempotents.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

(a)
Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
Then the categories of t-unital and c-unital left
R
-modules

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
and
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
are abelian.

(b)
Let
R
be a left s-unital ring (for example,
R
is
a ring with enough idempotents).
Then the full subcategory of t-unital left
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is
closed under submodules, quotients, extensions, and direct sums in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
Hence, in particular, the fully faithful inclusion functor of abelian
categories
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
is exact.

(c)
Let
R
be a ring with enough idempotents.
Then the full subcategory of c-unital
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}

is closed under kernels, cokernels, extensions, and direct products
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
Hence, in particular, the fully faithful inclusion functor of abelian
categories
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
is exact.

Proof.

Part (a) is a part of
Theorem
5.8
(a–b).
Part (b) is Proposition
2.2

together with Corollary
2.9
(a).
Part (c) follows from Lemma
3.5

together with Remark
3.6
and the first
assertion of Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a small preadditive category, i. e., a small category
enriched in abelian groups.
This means that, for every pair of objects
x
,
y\in\mathsf{E}
, there is
the abelian group of morphisms
\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,y)
, and the composition maps

\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(y,z)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,z)

are defined for all objects
x
,
y
,
z\in\mathsf{E}
in such a way that
the usual associativity and unitality axioms (for identity elements

{\mathrm{id}}_{x}\in\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,x)
) are satisfied.

Then the abelian group
R_{\mathsf{E}}=\bigoplus_{x,y\in\mathsf{E}}\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,y)

has a natural structure of (nonunital) ring with the multiplication
map given by the composition of morphisms in
\mathsf{E}
.
The following lemma is certainly standard and well-known.

Lemma 6.4
.

A ring
R
has enough idempotents if and only if it has the form

R=R_{\mathsf{E}}
for some small preadditive category
\mathsf{E}
.

Proof.

Let
R
be a ring with enough idempotents, and let

(e_{x}\in R)_{x\in X}
be its complete family of orthogonal idempotents.
Then
\mathsf{E}
is the category with the set of objects
X
and the groups
of morphisms
\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,y)=e_{y}Re_{x}
.
Conversely, if
R=R_{\mathsf{E}}
for a small preadditive category
\mathsf{E}
, then
the identity elements
{\mathrm{id}}_{x}\in\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,x)
, viewed as elements of

R_{\mathsf{E}}
, form a complete family of orthogonal idempotents in
R
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a small preadditive category.
By a
left
\mathsf{E}
-module
we mean a covariant additive functor

\mathsf{E}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
from
\mathsf{E}
to the category of abelian groups.
In other words, a left
\mathsf{E}
-module
\mathsf{M}
is a rule assigning to every
object
x\in\mathsf{E}
an abelian group
\mathsf{M}(x)
, and to every pair of
objects
x
,
y\in\mathsf{E}
the
action map

\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,y)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{M}(x)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathsf{M}(y)

in such a way that the usual associativity and unitality axioms are
satisfied.

Similarly, a
right
\mathsf{E}
-module

\mathsf{N}
is a contravariant
additive functor
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
.
In other words, it is a rule assigning to every object
x\in\mathsf{E}

an abelian group
\mathsf{N}(x)
, and to every pair of objects
x
,
y\in\mathsf{E}

the action map

\Hom_{\mathsf{E}}(x,y)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{N}(y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathsf{N}(x)

satisfying the usual associativity and unitality axioms.
We denote the abelian categories of left and right
\mathsf{E}
-modules by

\mathsf{E}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
and
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathsf{E}
, respectively.

The next proposition is also well-known.
One relevant reference is
[
14
, Section 7]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a small preadditive category and
R=R_{\mathsf{E}}
be

In this context:

(a)
The category of t-unital left
R
-modules is naturally

\mathsf{E}
-modules,

\mathsf{E}

\mathsf{M}

the t-unital left
R

, with
the action of

\mathsf{E}
in
\mathsf{M}
.

R
-modules is naturally

\mathsf{E}
-modules,

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R_{\mathsf{E}}\simeq{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathsf{E}.

\mathsf{E}
-module
\mathsf{N}

the t-unital right

\mathsf{E}

Let us explain part (a).
By Lemmas

The second equality holds because

When the

.
∎

.

\mathsf{E}

, we have

,

.

∎

7.
More on t-Unital and c-Unital Modules

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
According to
Examples
4.2
,

4.7
,
and
4.8
, the full
subcategory of t-unital modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}

need not
be closed
under kernels in the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
, and the full
subcategory of c-unital modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}

need not
be closed
under cokernels in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
Nevertheless,
Corollary
6.3
(a) tells
that the categories
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
and
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
are abelian; so all kernels
and cokernels exist in them.
The following two lemmas and proposition explain more.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
Then all limits and colimits exist in the category of t-unital
modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
.

Proof.

The abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\simeq\widetilde{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
obviously has
all limits and colimits.
By Lemma
1.5
, the full
subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is closed under colimits in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
So all colimits exist in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
and can be computed as
the colimits in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
On the other hand, Lemma
5.2
tells that
the functor
R\otimes_{R}{-}\,\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is right adjoint
to the inclusion
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
Consequently, the functor
R\otimes_{R}{-}\,\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}

preserves limits.
Now, in order to compute the limit of a diagram
(M_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Xi}

in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
, it suffices to apply the functor
R\otimes_{R}{-}
to
the limit of the same diagram in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
Then all limits and colimits exist in the category of c-unital
modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, the full
subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
is closed under limits in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
So all limits exist in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
and can be computed as
the limits in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
On the other hand, Lemma
5.3
tells that
the functor
\Hom_{R}(R,{-})\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
is left adjoint
to the inclusion
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
Consequently, the functor
\Hom_{R}(R,{-})\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}

preserves colimits.
Now, in order to compute the colimit of a diagram
(P_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Xi}

in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
, it suffices to apply the functor
\Hom_{R}(R,{-})
to
the colimit of the same diagram in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
∎

A study of abelian categories of the form
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
(for
idempotent rings
R
) can be found in the note of Roos
[
20
]

(cf.
[
22
, Section 6.3]
).
The following result is a particular case
of
[
20
, Théorème 1]
.

Proposition 7.3

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
Then the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
is a Grothendieck abelian category with exact functors of
infinite product.

Proof.

The equivalences of abelian categories

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
hold by
Corollary
5.10
.
Furthermore, the Serre subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
is closed under direct
sums and direct products in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
, as mentioned in the beginning
of Section
5
; and the localization functor

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
has adjoints on both sides, as
per Remark
5.9
.

It is well-known that for any Grothendieck abelian category
\mathsf{A}

with a Serre subcategory
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
such that the localization
functor
\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
admits a right adjoint, the quotient
category
\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
is also Grothendieck
[
8
, Proposition 8
in Section III.4]
,
[
23
, Proposition X.1.3]
.
In particular, the direct limit functors in
\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
are exact,
since they are exact in
\mathsf{A}
and the localization functor

\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
, being a left adjoint, preserves direct limits.
Similarly, if the functor
\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
has a left adjoint,
then it preserves direct products; and if the direct products are
exact in
\mathsf{A}
, then it follows that they are also exact in
\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
.

In fact, it is not difficult to prove directly that for any abelian
category
\mathsf{A}
with exact functors of coproduct and a Serre subcategory

\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
closed under coproducts, the localization functor

\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
preserves coproducts (and takes any set of
generators of
\mathsf{A}
to a set of generators of
\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
).
Dually, if an abelian category
\mathsf{A}
has exact products and a Serre
subcategory
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
is closed under products, then
the localization functor
\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}/\mathsf{S}
preserves products.
See, e. g.,
[
2
, Lemma 1.5]
or
[
15
, Lemma 3.2.10]
for
a similar argument in the triangulated category realm.
(Cf. the discussion in
[
22
, Section 6.4 and Proposition 6.5]

for module categories.)
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus R
be its
unitalization.
Then the full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
is closed under kernels in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}

if and only if
the unital right
\widetilde{R}
-module

is flat.

Proof.

The “if” assertion was explained in
Remark
1.6
; it does not depend on
the assumption that
R
is t-unital.
The “only if” claim is a corollary of
Theorem
5.8
(a).
Assume that the full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}

in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}

Thus the composition

But this composition is the tensor product functor

, that is, in other notation, the tensor

R\otimes_{\widetilde{R}}{-}\,\colon\widetilde{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow\widetilde{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
Alternatively, one could refer to Remark
8.14
(3)
and Corollary

below.
∎

8.
t-Flat, c-Projective, and t-Injective Modules

We start with a discussion of c-projectivity, then pass to
t-injectivity, and in the end come to t-flatness.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
We will say that a left
R
-module
Q
is
c-projective
if
the covariant functor
\Hom_{R}(Q,{-})\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
preserves
cokernels (i. e., takes cokernels in the category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
to
cokernels in the category of abelian groups
\mathsf{Ab}
).

Remarks 8.2
.

(0) Notice that, by the definition, a c-projective
R
-module

need not
be c-unital.

(1) Furthermore, a projective nonunital
R
-module (i. e.,
a projective unital
\widetilde{R}
-module)
need not
be
c-projective.
For example, let
T
be the commutative ring from
Example
4.6

and
0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow\widetilde{F}\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0
be
the short exact sequence of
T
-modules from
Example
4.8
.
So
F
and
\widetilde{F}
are c-unital
T
-modules, but
S
is not.
One can easily compute that
\Hom_{T}(T,S)=\Hom_{S}(S,S)=\widetilde{S}
,
where the basis elements
x^{p}y^{q}\in T
act by zero in
\widetilde{S}

when
p>0
.
According to the proof of Lemma
7.2
,

\widetilde{S}
is the cokernel of the morphism
F\longrightarrow\widetilde{F}

in
T{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
.
Since the map
\widetilde{F}\longrightarrow\widetilde{S}
is not surjective
(i. e., not an epimorphism in
T{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
or in
\mathsf{Ab}
), it follows that
the free
\widetilde{T}
-module
\widetilde{T}
is
not

c-projective as a
T
-module.

(2) Quite generally, the free left
\widetilde{R}
-module

\widetilde{R}
is c-projective as an
R
-module
if and only if

the full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
is closed under cokernels in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.
If this is the case (e. g., this holds for all rings
R
with enough
idempotents, see
Corollary
6.3
(c)),
then all projective nonunital
R
-modules are c-projective.

(3) The left
R
-module
R
is c-projective if and only if
the left
R
-module
\widetilde{R}
is (cf. Proposition
8.4
below).
Consequently, the left
R
-module
R
is c-projective if and only if
the full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
is closed under cokernels in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
Q
be a t-unital left
R
-module.
Then the
R
-module
Q
is c-projective if and only if, for every
right exact sequence
L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
with

L
,
M\in R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
, the sequence of abelian groups
\Hom_{R}(Q,L)\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(Q,M)\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(Q,E)\longrightarrow 0
is right exact.

Proof.

Following the proof of Lemma
7.2
,
the c-unital
R
-module
\Hom_{R}(R,E)
is the cokernel of
the morphism
L\longrightarrow M
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
.
By the definition, c-projectivity of
Q
means right exactness of
the sequences of abelian groups
\Hom_{R}(Q,L)\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(Q,M)\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(Q,\Hom_{R}(R,E))\longrightarrow 0
.
It remains to recall the natural isomorphisms of abelian groups

\Hom_{R}(Q,\Hom_{R}(R,E))\simeq\Hom_{R}(R\otimes_{R}Q,\>E)\simeq\Hom_{R}(Q,E)

for a t-unital
R
-module
Q
.
∎

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
Q
be a left
R
-module.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

Q
is a c-projective
R
-module;

(2)

the c-unital
R
-module
\Hom_{R}(R,Q)
is c-projective;

(3)

the t-unital
R
-module
R\otimes_{R}Q
is c-projective.

Proof.

(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(2) follows from
Lemma
5.3
.

(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(3) holds due to the natural isomorphisms

\Hom_{R}(R\otimes_{R}Q,\>P)\simeq\Hom_{R}(Q,\Hom_{R}(R,P))\simeq\Hom_{R}(Q,P)
for
all c-unital left
R
-modules
P
.

Alternatively, both the equivalences follow from
Lemma
5.4
and
Corollary
5.7
(c).
∎

Remark 8.5
.

Clearly, a c-unital left
R
-module is c-projective if and only if
it is a projective object in the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
.
Now, in view of
Corollary

,
Proposition

implies that a t-unital
left

object in the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
.
Furthermore, by Theorem

a nonunital left
R

represents a projective object in the quotient category

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
.

The following corollary is our version of the projective

[

, Proposition 6.2]
.

Corollary 8.6
.

Let

be a t-unital ring and

be a t-unital left

Then

is c-projective if and only if it is projective as
an object of the category of nonunital modules

.

By Remark

,

is c-projective
if and only if it represents a projective object in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
.
As mentioned in Remark

, the inclusion functor

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}

Since the latter functor is exact, the former one takes projectives

This proves the “only if” implication of the corollary.
The “if” implication follows from the “if” assertion of
Lemma

.
∎

The next definition is dual-analogous to
Definition

.

Definition 8.7

Let

\mathsf{Ab}

Remarks 8.8

(0) Notice that, by the definition, a t-injective

(1) Furthermore, an injective nonunital

Lemma 8.9

Let

Following the proof of Lemma

∎

Proposition 8.10
.

Let

.

Clearly, a t-unital left

The next corollary is our version of the injective

∎

Finally, we come to the last main definition of this section.

Definition 8.13
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
We will say that a left

-module

is

the covariant functor
{-}\otimes_{R}F\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
preserves
kernels (i. e., takes kernels in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R
to kernels in
\mathsf{Ab}
).
The definition of a t-flat right

Remarks 8.14
.

(0) Notice that, by the definition, a t-flat
R
-module
need
not
be t-unital.

(1) Furthermore, a flat nonunital
R
-module (i. e., a flat
unital
\widetilde{R}
-module)

be t-flat.
Quite generally, the free left
\widetilde{R}
-module
\widetilde{R}

if and only if

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R

shows that

(2) If the full subcategory
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R
is closed under kernels in

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R

(3) The left
R
-module
R
is t-flat if and only if the left

R
-module
\widetilde{R}

8.16

Consequently, the left
R
-module

is t-flat if and only if
the full subcategory
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R
is closed under kernels in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R
.

Lemma 8.15
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
F
be a t-unital left
R
-module.
Then the
R
-module
F
is t-flat if and only if, for every left
exact sequence
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M
in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R
with

L
,
M\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R
, the sequence of abelian groups

0\longrightarrow K\otimes_{R}F\longrightarrow L\otimes_{R}F\longrightarrow M\otimes_{R}F
is left exact.

Proof.

Following the proof of Lemma
7.1
,
the t-unital right
R
-module
K\otimes_{R}R
is the kernel of
the morphism
L\longrightarrow M
in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R
.
By the definition, t-flatness of

means left exactness of
the sequences of abelian groups
0\longrightarrow(K\otimes_{R}R)\otimes_{R}F\longrightarrow L\otimes_{R}F\longrightarrow M\otimes_{R}F
.
It remains to recall the natural isomorphisms of abelian groups

(K\otimes_{R}R)\otimes_{R}F\simeq K\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{R}F)\simeq K\otimes_{R}F
for
a t-unital
R
-module

.
∎

Proposition 8.16
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
F
be a left
R
-module.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

F
is a t-flat
R
-module;

(2)

the t-unital
R
-module
R\otimes_{R}F
is t-flat;

(3)

the c-unital
R
-module
\Hom_{R}(R,F)
is t-flat.

Proof.

More generally, the claim is that t-flatness of a left
R
-module

only depends on the image of

in the Serre quotient category

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
.
In other words, if

is a morphism of left

-modules
with null kernel and cokernel, that the
R
-module

is t-flat
if and only if the
R
-module
G
is.
This claim follows from
Corollary
5.7
(a),
and it implies the desired equivalences by
Lemma
5.4
.
∎

For any right
R
-module
N
, the
character module

N^{+}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is a left
R
-module.
At the end of this section we collect several simple observations
related to or provable by the passage to the character module.

Lemma 8.17
.

Let
R
be an arbitrary (nonunital) ring.
Then a right
R
-module
N
is t-unital if and only if the left

R
-module
N^{+}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is c-unital.

Proof.

One needs to observe that the passage to the character modules
transforms the natural morphism
N\otimes_{R}R\longrightarrow N
into the natural
morphism
P\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,P)
for the
R
-module
P=N^{+}
.
Notice the natural isomorphism of abelian groups/left
R
-modules

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(N\otimes_{R}R,\>\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\simeq\Hom_{R}(R,\Hom_{R}(N,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}))
.
∎

Lemma 8.18
.

For any t-unital ring
R
, the contravariant functor

N\longmapsto N^{+}\colon\allowbreak({\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R)^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
is exact.

Proof.

The point is that a complex of
R
-modules is exact in the quotient
category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}
if and only if its cohomology modules
computed in the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
are null-modules.
Consequently (in view of
Theorem
5.8
), the same criterion
applies in the abelian categories
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R
and
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
: a complex
in any one of these categories is exact if and only if, viewed as
a complex, respectively, in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R
or
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
,
it has null cohomology modules (cf.
[
22
, Section 4.6]
).

Now it remains to say that the functor

N\longmapsto N^{+}\colon\allowbreak({\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{n}--}}}R)^{\mathsf{op}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
preserves
the cohomology of complexes, and a right
R
-module

is null if
and only if the left
R
-module
N^{+}
is null.
∎

Lemma 8.19
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
F
be a right
R
-module.
Then the left
R
-module
F^{+}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(F,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is
t-injective if and only if the right
R
-module

is t-flat.

Proof.

Let
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M
be a left exact sequence in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}
.
Then the sequence of abelian groups
\Hom_{R}(M,F^{+})\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(L,F^{+})\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(K,F^{+})\longrightarrow 0
can be obtained by applying
the functor
\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}({-},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
to the sequence of abelian
groups
0\longrightarrow F\otimes_{R}K\longrightarrow F\otimes_{R}L\longrightarrow F\otimes_{R}M
.
Hence the former sequence is right exact if and only if the latter
one is left exact.
∎

Corollary 8.20
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
F
be a t-unital
R
-module.
Then
F
is t-flat if and only if it is a flat nonunital
R
-module,
or in other words, if and only if
F
is a flat unital

\widetilde{R}
-module.

Proof.

Let
F
be a t-unital right
R
-module.
By Lemma
8.17
,
F^{+}
is
a c-unital left
R
-module.
Lemma
8.19
tells that

is t-flat if and only if
F^{+}
is t-injective.
According to Corollary
8.12
,
F^{+}
is t-injective
if and only if it is an injective object of
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
, or in other
words, if and only if
F^{+}
is an injective left

\widetilde{R}
-module.
The latter condition is well-known to hold if and only if
F
is
a flat right
\widetilde{R}
-module.
∎

Proposition 8.21
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
Then any c-projective
R
-module is t-flat.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a c-projective left
R
-module.
For any left exact sequence
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M
in

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod^{t}--}}}R
, the sequence of character modules
M^{+}\longrightarrow L^{+}\longrightarrow K^{+}\longrightarrow 0
is right exact in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}
by
Lemmas

and
8.18

Now the sequence of abelian groups
\Hom_{R}(Q,M^{+})\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(Q,L^{+})\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(Q,K^{+})\longrightarrow 0

the functor
\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}({-},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
to the sequence of abelian

.
Since the former sequence is right exact by assumption, it follows

Alternatively, one could assume without loss of generality that
Q

is t-unital (using Propositions
8.4

and
8.16
), and compare Corollary
8.6

with Corollary
8.20
.
∎

Let
R
be a t-unital ring.
Then the abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}/R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{0}Mod}}}\simeq R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{c}Mod}}}

is Grothendieck by Proposition
7.3
.
Hence there are enough injective objects in this abelian category
(cf. a more general result in
[
22
, Lemma 5.4]
).

A beautiful construction of Quillen provides (a more general version
of) the following lemma, claiming existence of enough t-flat
modules.

Lemma 8.22

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
R
be a t-unital ring and
M
be a t-unital
R
-module.
Then
M
is a quotient module of a t-flat t-unital
R
-module.
In fact,
M
is the cokernel of a morphism of t-flat t-unital

R
-modules.

Proof.

Notice that the inclusion
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{t}Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--{}^{n}Mod}}}
preserves
epimorphisms and cokernels; so the assertions are unambiguous.
The first assertion is
[
22
, Lemma 2.4]
; the second one
is
[
22
, Proposition 2.6 (a)
\Rightarrow
(b)]
.
∎

On the other hand, the discussion in
[
22
, Section 2.5]
,
based on Kaplansky’s theorem that all projective modules over
a local ring are free
[

, shows that there

need not
be enough projective objects in

.
The following example confirms that this phenomenon occurs for
t-unital commutative rings

.

Example 8.23
.

Let
R\subset\widetilde{R}
be the rings from
Example
4.1
.
Choose a prime number

, and put
P=p\mathbb{Z}\oplus R\subset\mathbb{Z}\oplus R=\widetilde{R}
.
Then

is a maximal ideal in
\widetilde{R}
, since the quotient
ring
\widetilde{R}/P\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is a field.
The complement
\widetilde{R}\setminus P
consists of all the elements

n+r\in\mathbb{Z}\oplus R
such that

is not divisible by
p
.

Consider the localizations
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}=\widetilde{R}_{(P)}=S^{-1}\widetilde{R}
and
T=R\widetilde{R}_{(P)}=S^{-1}R
.
Then
T
is an ideal in the commutative local ring
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
.
The ring
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
differs from the unitalization
\widetilde{T}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus T
of the ring

; in fact, one has
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\oplus T
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is the localization of the ring
of integers at the maximal ideal
(p)\subset\mathbb{Z}
.
There is a natural injective ring homomorphism
\widetilde{T}\longrightarrow\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
acting by the identity map on

.

Let us show that any c-projective
T
-module is zero.
By Proposition
8.4
, it suffices to check that
any c-projective t-unital
T
-module

is zero.
It is clear from the definition that any t-unital
T
-module is
a
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
-module (since
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
is the localization
of
\widetilde{T}
at

and
T
is
a
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
-module; cf.
[

, Proposition 9.2]
).
By Corollary
8.6
,
F
is a projective

\widetilde{T}
-module.
Hence

is also a projective

\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
-module.
Since
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
is a local ring, Kaplansky’s theorem tells
that

is a free
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
-module.
But a nonzero free
\widetilde{T}^{\prime}
-module cannot be t-unital
over

.

9.
s-Unital and t-Unital Homomorphisms of Rings

We start with a brief discussion of s-unital ring homomorphisms
before passing to the somewhat more complicated t-unital case.

Let
f\colon K\longrightarrow R
be a homomorphism of nonunital rings.
We will say that the homomorphism
f
is
left s-unital

if
R
an s-unital left
K
-module in the module structure
induced by
f
.
Similarly, we say that
f
is
right s-unital
if

f
makes
R
an s-unital right
K
-module.
Finally, we say that
f
is
s-unital
if it is both
left and right s-unital.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
f\colon K\longrightarrow R
be a homomorphism of nonunital rings.
Assume that
f
is s-unital (respectively, left s-unital,
right s-unital).
Then the ring
R
is s-unital (resp., left s-unital,
right s-unital).

Proof.

Let
r\in R
be an element.
The homomorphism
f
being left s-unital means existence of
an element
e\in K
such that
f(e)r=r
in
R
.
The ring
R
being left s-unital means existence of
an element
g\in R
such that
gr=r
.
In order to show that the former implies the latter, it remains
to take
g=f(e)
.
∎

The next lemma is even more obvious.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
K\longrightarrow R
be a homomorphism of nonunital rings.
Then any
R
-module that is s-unital as a
K
-module is
also s-unital as an
R
-module. ∎

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
f\colon K\longrightarrow R
be a left s-unital homomorphism of nonunital
rings.
Then a left
R
-module is s-unital as an
R
-module if and only if
it is s-unital as a
K
-module.

Proof.

The “‘if” implication is provided by
Lemma
9.2
.
To prove the “only if”, let
M
be an s-unital left
R
-module
and
m\in M
be an element.
By assumption, there exist an element
g\in R
such that
gm=m
in
M

and an element
e\in K
such that
f(e)g=g
in
R
.
Now it is clear that
f(e)m=f(e)gm=gm=m
in
M
.
∎

We will say that a ring homomorphism
f\colon K\longrightarrow R
is
left
t-unital
if
R
a t-unital left
K
-module in the module structure
induced by
f
.
Similarly, we say that
f
is
right t-unital
if

f
makes
R
a t-unital right
K
-module.
At last, we say that
f
is
t-unital
if it is both
left and right t-unital.

Lemma 9.4
.

(a)
If a ring
K
is left s-unital, then a ring homomorphism

f\colon K\longrightarrow R
is left t-unital if and only if
f
is left s-unital.

(b)
If a ring
K
is right s-unital, then a ring homomorphism

f\colon K\longrightarrow R
is right t-unital if and only if
f
is
right s-unital.

Proof.

Follows from Corollary
2.9
.
∎

Clearly, if
KR=R
or
RK=R
(which means
f(K)R=R
or
Rf(K)=R
,
respectively), then
R^{2}=R
.
The following proposition, providing a direct analogue of
Lemma
9.1
for t-unitality, is a bit
more involved.

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
f\colon K\longrightarrow R
be a homomorphism of nonunital rings.
Assume that
f
is
either

Then the ring

Proof.

Assuming that the multiplication map
K\otimes_{K}R\longrightarrow R

an isomorphism, we need to prove that so is the multiplication map

.

Indeed, consider the initial fragment of a relative bar-complex

(23)

R\otimes_{K}R\otimes_{K}R\overset{\partial}{\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu}R\otimes_{K}R\overset{\partial}{\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu}R\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu0

with the differentials
\partial(r^{\prime}\otimes r^{\prime\prime}\otimes r^{\prime\prime\prime})=r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}\otimes r^{\prime\prime\prime}-r^{\prime}\otimes r^{\prime\prime}r^{\prime\prime\prime}
and
\partial(r^{\prime}\otimes r^{\prime\prime})=r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}
for all

r^{\prime}
,
r^{\prime\prime}
,
r^{\prime\prime\prime}\in R
.
We have to show that the short sequence (
23
)
is right exact.
This is obviously equivalent to the desired isomorphism

R\otimes_{R}R\simeq R
.

Actually, the short sequence of left

K
-modules (
23
) is split right exact
(and in fact, the whole bar-complex is split exact as a complex
of left
K
-modules).
Let us construct a contracting homotopy

Its component
h_{0}\colon R\longrightarrow R\otimes_{K}R
is defined as the composition

R\simeq K\otimes_{K}R\longrightarrow R\otimes_{K}R
of the inverse map to the natural

K\otimes_{K}R\longrightarrow R

Similarly, the component

is
defined as the composition

of the inverse map to the natural isomorphism

and the map

It is straightforward to check that

the term

.

Alternatively, one could obtain the desired result (for right
t-unital ring homomorphisms

(a).

Proposition 9.6
.

Let
f\colon K\longrightarrow R
be a homomorphism of nonunital rings.
Assume that
RK=R
, or more generally,
KR\subset RK
in
R
.
Then

(a)
any right
R
-module that is t-unital as a
K
-module
is also t-unital as an
R
-module;

(b)
any left
R
-module that is c-unital as a
K
-module
is also c-unital as an
R
-module.

In particular, if the ring
R
is commutative, then
the assertions
(a–b)
hold for any homomorphism
f
.

Proof.

Part (b): Let
P
be a left
R
-module that is c-unital as
a left
K
-module.
Then, first of all, the map
P\longrightarrow\Hom_{K}(K,P)
is injective;
this means that no nonzero element of
P
is annihilated by
K
.
It follows that no nonzero element of
P
is annihilated by
R
,
either; so the map
P\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R,P)
is injective.

To prove surjectivity of the latter map, consider a left

R
-module morphism
g\colon R\longrightarrow P
.
The composition
K\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow P
is a left
K
-module
morphism
gf\colon K\longrightarrow P
.
Since
P
is c-unital over
K
, there exists an element
p\in P

such that
gf(k)=kp
for all
k\in K
.
Consider the
R
-module morphism
h\colon R\longrightarrow P
given by
the formula
h(r)=g(r)-rp
for all
r\in R
.
Then the composition
hf\colon K\longrightarrow P
vanishes; and we have to
prove that the whole map
h
vanishes.

Indeed, for all
r\in R
and
k\in K
we have
h(rf(k))=rh(f(k))=0

in
P
; so
h|_{RK}=0
.
By assumption, we have
KR\subset RK\subset R
; hence
h|_{KR}=0
.
This means that for any
r\in R
and
k\in K
we have
kh(r)=h(f(k)r)=0
in
P
.
Thus the element
h(r)\in P
is annihilated by
K
, and we can
conclude that
h(r)=0
.

Part (a): let
N
be a right
R
-module that is t-unital as
a right
K
-module.
Then, first of all, the map
N\otimes_{K}K\longrightarrow N
is surjective;
so
NK=N
, and it follows that
NR=N
, or in other words, the map

N\otimes_{R}R\longrightarrow N
is surjective.

To prove injectivity of the latter map, consider the composition

N\otimes_{K}K\longrightarrow N\otimes_{R}R\longrightarrow N
.
By assumption, the map
N\otimes_{K}K\longrightarrow N
is an isomorphism.
So it suffices to show that the map
N\otimes_{K}K\longrightarrow N\otimes_{R}R
is
surjective.
We have
NK=N
; so any element
n\in N
has the form
n=\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i}k_{i}
for some
n_{i}\in N
and
k_{i}\in K
.
Hence, for any
r\in R
, we have
n\otimes_{R}r=\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i}k_{i}\otimes_{R}r=\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i}\otimes_{R}k_{i}r
.

By assumption,
KR\subset RK
; so, for every
1\leq i\leq j
, we have

k_{i}r=\sum_{u=1}^{v_{i}}r_{i,u}k^{\prime}_{i,u}
for some
r_{i,u}\in R
and

k^{\prime}_{i,u}\in K
.
Thus
n\otimes_{R}r=\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i}\otimes_{R}k_{i}r=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{u=1}^{v_{i}}n_{i}\otimes_{R}r_{i,u}k^{\prime}_{i,u}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{u=1}^{v_{i}}n_{i}r_{i,u}\otimes_{R}k^{\prime}_{i,u}
, and the latter element belongs to the image of
the map
N\otimes_{K}K\longrightarrow N\otimes_{R}R
, as desired.

Alternatively, one can deduce part (a) from part (b) using
Lemma
8.17
.
∎

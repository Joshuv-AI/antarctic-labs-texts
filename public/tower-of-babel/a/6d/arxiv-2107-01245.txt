Negative thermal expansion and itinerant ferromagnetism in Mn 1.4 Fe 3.6 Si 3
Vikram Singh, R. Nath

Abstract
We report the thermal expansion, critical behavior, magnetocaloric effect (MCE), and magnetoresistance ( M ​ R MR ) on the polycrystalline Mn 1.4 Fe 3.6 Si 3 compound around the ferromagnetic transition. A large negative volume thermal expansion ( α V ∼ − 20 × 10 − 6 \alpha_{\rm V}\sim-20\times 10^{-6} K -1 ) is observed across the transition temperature with a strong anisotropic variation of lattice parameters in the a ​ b ab -plane. The anisotropic magnetoelasticity arises from the competition between magnetic ordering and structural deformation which could be responsible for the large MCE ( Δ ​ S m ≃ − 6 \Delta S_{\rm m}\simeq-6 J/Kg-K) across the magnetic transition in this compound. The large and negative M ​ R MR ( ∼ − 3 % \sim-3\% in 80 kOe) is also observed at the transition temperature which can be attributed to the suppression of spin disorder. Further, the Rhodes-Wolfarth ratio (RWR > 1 >1 ) and identical field dependence of M ​ R MR and MCE isotherms indicate the itinerant character of the 3 ​ d 3d electrons. The critical exponents determined from the analysis of magnetization and MCE are consistent with the quasi-two-dimensional (2D) Ising model with long range exchange interactions which decays as J ⁡ ( r ) ∼ r − 3.41 J(r)\sim r^{-3.41} . This unconventional quasi-2D Ising character with long-range interactions can be ascribed to strong a ​ b ab -plane anisotropy and the delocalized 3 ​ d 3d electrons in the studied compound.

I
Introduction

In the past decades, research on materials with almost zero thermal expansion (ZTE) and negative thermal expansion (NTE) has been significantly enhan ced due to their potential technological applications
Takenaka 2012
.
Recently, the large or giant NTE has been found in a variety of materials including oxides, intermetallics, alloys, antipervoskites, fluorides, and organometallic frameworks
over a wide range of temperature
Mary et al. 1996
;
Guillaume 1897
;
van Schilfgaarde et al. 1999
;
Takenaka and Takagi 2009
;
Song et al. 2011
;
Chen et al. 2011
;
Huang et al. 2013
;
Goodwin et al. 2008
;
Song et al. 2013
;
Hu et al. 2001
;
Greve et al. 2010
;
Chen et al. 2015
. Among these materials, the NTE in compounds with large magnetocaloric effect (MCE) has attracted special attention due to strong magnetoelastic coupling. In these materials, the NTE occurs as a result of the volume expansion accompanied by magnetic transition, known as magnetovolume effect (MVE) which dominates over the conventional phononic thermal expansion
Takenaka 2012
. Here, a disordered magnetic phase with smaller volume transforms to a ordered magnetic structure with larger volume as the temperature decreases, causing a negative thermal expansion
van Schilfgaarde et al. 1999
. Prominent examples include La(Fe,Si)
13
, Tb(Co,Fe)
2
, Mn
3
Ge,
R_{2}
Fe
17
(
R
= rare earth elements), (Hf,Ta)Fe
2
, (Sc,Ti)Fe
2
, MnCoGe etc
Huang et al. 2013
;
Song et al. 2018a
;
Song et al. 2018b
;
Álvarez-Alonso et al. 2012
;
Li et al. 2016
;
Song et al. 2020
;
Ren et al. 2019
. Furthermore, the manipulation or control of NTE via different routes such as chemical substitution, nano-crystallization, introduction of disorder, and application of external pressure and magnetic field provides extra working flexibility and advantage in multifunctional applications in these materials.

Basically, these MCE materials are divided into two categories based on the nature of the magnetic transition: first order transition and second order transition. In the first order, the magnetic transition is accompanied with an abrupt structural deformation which gives rise to the sharp and large MVE at the transition. The colossal NTE in such materials is mainly associated with the abrupt enhancement of unit cell volume at the transition and is being investigated extensively in the recent days
Hu et al. 2001
. On the other hand, the pronounced NTE across a continuous second order transition has been observed only in few selected MCE materials including the Invar alloys and intermetallic compounds La(Fe,Co,Si)
13
due to the itinerant ferromagnetism and magnetoelastic coupling
Guillaume 1897
;
Huang et al. 2013
;
Takenaka 2012
.

MnFe
4
Si
3

4
Si

;
Fujita et al. 2003
;
Dung et al. 2011
;
Zhao et al. 2017
. Therefore, it would be interesting to investigate the thermal expansion behaviour of the Mn
1+x

3

Recently, we have investigated the Mn

Fe
4-x
Si
3

In the present work, we choose another composition

1.4
Fe
3.6
Si

IV
Conclusion

The structural, magnetic, and electronic properties of Mn
1.4
Fe
3.6
Si
3
are investigated in detail by means of the temperature dependent powder XRD, magnetization,
MCE
, and
MR
measurements. An anisotropic structural distortion is observed across the magnetic transition, where the lattice expands in the
ab
-plane and contracts slightly along the
c
-axis. This results in a large negative volume thermal expansion (
\alpha_{\rm V}\sim-20\times 10^{-6}

MCE
data supports the second order nature of the phase transition which can be described in the framework of a quasi-2D Ising model with long-range magnetic interactions. The effective 2D character of the magnetic interactions originates from strong in-plane anisotropy across the transition while the itinerant or delocalized character of the
3d
electrons is responsible for the long-range magnetic interactions in the Mn
1+x
Fe
4-x
Si
3
series.

The critical behaviour analysis across a ferromagnetic transition temperature (

\beta
,
\gamma

Kaul5s

), zero field inverse susceptibility (

through the following expressions.
Stanley336s

M_{\rm S}(T)=M_{0}(-\epsilon)^{\beta},~{\rm for}~\epsilon<0,T<T_{\rm C},

(S1)

\chi_{0}^{-1}(T)=\Gamma(\epsilon)^{\gamma},~{\rm for}~\epsilon>0,T>T_{\rm C},

(S2)

and

M(H)=X(H)^{1/\delta},~{\rm for}~\epsilon=0,T=T_{\rm C}.

(S3)

Here,

H

Widom3898s

\alpha+2\beta+\gamma=2

M(H,\epsilon)\left\lvert\epsilon\right\rvert^{-\beta}=\textit{f}_{\pm}(H\left\lvert\epsilon\right\rvert^{-(\beta+\gamma)}).

and

,

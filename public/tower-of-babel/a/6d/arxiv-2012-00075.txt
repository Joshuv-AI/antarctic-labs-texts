Prescribing the curvature to Killing Graphs
Yunelsy N. Alvarez

Abstract
In this work we prove the existence and uniqueness of Killing graphs with prescribed mean curvature considering functions which are not necessarily constant along the flow lines of the Killing vector field.

Introduction

Let
\overline{M}
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n+1
,
n\geq 2
. Let us suppose that
\overline{M}
is endowed with a non-singular Killing vector field
Y
whose orthogonal distribution is integrable. We fix some integral leaf
M
and we denote by
\Phi:M\times\R\rightarrow\overline{M}
the flow generated by
Y
. The Killing graph of a function
u
defined over a bounded domain
\Omega\subset M
, firstly defined by Dajczer-Hinojosa-Lira in
[
4
]
, is the hypersurface

\Gr u=\{\Phi(x,u(x));\ x\in\Omega\}.

(1)

If
H(\Phi(x,u(x)))
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
at each point
\Phi(x,u(x))
, then
u
necessarily satisfies the equation

W^{2}\Delta u-\Hess u(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\rho^{-2}\left(\rho^{-2}+W^{2}\right){\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla u\right\rangle}=nH(\Phi(x,u))W^{3},

(2)

where

\rho=\rho(x)=\left\|Y\right\|_{\overline{M}},

(3)

W=\sqrt{\rho^{-2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}}.

(4)

The main goal in this work is to study the Dirichlet problem for equation (
2
). Before to state the principal theorem we need to fix the notation.

Notation.

1.

Geometric entities on
\overline{M}
will be marked with a bar and any geometric objects without this superscript will be assumed to lie on
M
.

2.

For an hypersurface
\Gamma
in
M
, we called the hypersurface

\overline{\Gamma}=\{\Phi(y,z),\ y\in\Gamma,\ z\in\R\}\subset\overline{M}

the translation of
\Gamma
along the flow of
Y
.
In particular, for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset M

\mathcal{C}=\{\Phi(y,z);\ y\in\partial\Omega,\ z\in\R\}

(5)

is the cylinder obtained by “translating”
\partial\Omega
along the flow lines,

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\{\Phi(x,z);\ x\in\overline{\Omega},\ z\in\R\}

(6)

is the “solid” cylinder bounded by
\mathcal{C}

and, for
\delta>0
,

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}({\delta})=\{\Phi(x,z);\ x\in\overline{\Omega},\ -{\delta}\leq z\leq{\delta}\}

(7)

is the solid cylinder “between” the hypersurfaces
\Phi_{-\delta}(M)
and
\Phi_{\delta}(M)
.

3.

The set of points in
\Omega
having a unique nearest point to
\partial\Omega
will be denoted by
\Omega_{0}
. Besides, for each
x\in\Omega_{0}
,
y(x)
is the point in
\partial\Omega
realizing the distance
\dd(x):=\dist(x,\partial\Omega)
. Furthermore,
\gamma_{y}(t)
,
0\leq t<\tau(y)
, is the inner normal geodesic to the boundary, being that
\gamma_{y}(\tau(y))
is the point of
\gamma_{y}
in
\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus\partial\Omega
.

4.

For every hypersurface
\Gamma
in
M
we will denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
the mean curvature of
\Gamma
in
M
, and
\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\Gamma}}
will denote the mean curvature of its translation
\overline{\Gamma}
in
\overline{M}
.

5.

Let
\rho_{i}=\inf\limits_{\Omega}\rho
and
\rho_{s}=\sup\limits_{\Omega}\rho
.

6.

Over
\mathscr{C}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
we define the operators

\mathfrak{Q}u:=W^{2}\Delta u-\Hess u(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\rho^{-2}\left(\rho^{-2}+W^{2}\right){\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla u\right\rangle}-nH(\Phi(x,u))W^{3}.

(8)

and

\mathfrak{Q}_{\sigma}u:=W^{2}\Delta u-\Hess u(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\rho^{-2}\left(\rho^{-2}+W^{2}\right){\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla u\right\rangle}-n\sigma H(\Phi(x,u))W^{3}.

(9)

where
\sigma\in[0,1]
.

Having set the notation, we state the principal results of this work:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a
\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}
bounded domain for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
.
Let
H\in\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}})
satisfying

{\left\langle\overline{\nabla}H,Y\right\rangle}_{\overline{M}}\geq 0\mbox{ in }\tilde{\mathcal{C}}

(10)

and

\left\|\overline{\nabla}H\left(\Phi(x,\varphi(y(x)))\right)\right\|_{\overline{M}}\leq\left(H\left(\Phi(x,\varphi(y(x)))\right)\right)^{2}+\frac{\overline{\Ricc}_{x}}{n}\ \ \forall\ x\in\Omega_{0}.

(11)

If

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\geq\sup_{\sigma\in[0,1]}\left|H(\Phi(y,\sigma\varphi(y)))\right|\ \forall\ y\in\partial\Omega,

(12)

then there exists a unique function
u\in\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying
u|_{\partial\Omega}=\varphi
and whose Killing graph has mean curvature
H
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a
\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}
bounded domain for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Let
H\in\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}})
satisfying

{\left\langle\overline{\nabla}H,Y\right\rangle}_{\overline{M}}\geq 0\mbox{ in }\tilde{\mathcal{C}},

(13)

and

\sup_{z\in\R}\left\|\overline{\nabla}H\left(\Phi(x,z)\right)\right\|_{\overline{M}}\leq\inf_{z\in\R}\left(H\left(\Phi(x,z)\right)\right)^{2}+\frac{\overline{\Ricc}_{x}}{n}\ \ \forall\ x\in\Omega_{0}.

(14)

If

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\geq\sup\limits_{z\in\R}\left|H(\Phi(y,z))\right|\ \forall\ y\in\partial\Omega,

(15)

then for every
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
there exists a unique function
u\in\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying
u|_{\partial\Omega}=\varphi
and whose Killing graph has mean curvature
H
.

Existence of graphs with prescribed mean curvature is a subject that has been studied for more than a hundred years. The pioneers were Berstein
[
3
]
, Douglas
[
5
]
, Radó
[
12
]
and Finn
[
6
]
in the three-dimensional Euclidean space. Subsequently, Jenkins-Serrin
[
9
]
and Serrin
[
13
]
studied the problem in higher dimension.

One conclusion we derive from those works is that there exists a sharp geometric condition to be satisfied by
\Omega
in order to have a solution of the Dirichlet problem for the prescribed mean curvature equation for arbitrary boundary values (with further assumption concerning the regularity)
1
1

1

Sharp in the sense that if this condition fails we can construct a
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
function over
\partial\Omega
that can not be the boundary values of any solution of the corresponding mean curvature equation.
.
For example,
\Omega
must be mean convex to construct minimal graphs in
\R^{n+1}
for each smooth boundary
(see
[
9
, Th. 1 p. 171]
). In the more general case,
H=H(x)
, Serrin proved that there exists a graph with mean curvature
H
for arbitrarily given
\mathscr{C}^{2}
boundary values if and only if

(n-1)\mathcal{H}_{\partial\Omega}(y)\geq n\left|H(y)\right|\ \forall\ y\in\partial\Omega,

(16)

provided the function
H\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies an additional (not geometric) hypothesis (see
[
13
, Th. p. 484]
).

The present author and Sa Earp in
[
2
,
1
]
have generalized recently the aforementioned result of Serrin to the Riemannian product
M\times\R
.
We proved that for arbitrary smooth boundary data there exists a vertical graphs in
M\times\R
with prescribed mean curvature
H=H(x,z)
(satisfying some additional hypothesis) when

(n-1)\mathcal{H}_{\partial\Omega}(y)\geq n\sup\limits_{z\in\R}\left|H\left(y,z\right)\right|\ \forall\ y\in\partial\Omega.

(17)

Besides, we proved that (
17
) is sharp also in this context.

Previously, Dajczer-Hinojosa-Lira had already introduced the concept of Killing graphs.
In this setting they proved (see
[
4
, T. 1 p. 232]
) that,
for
H\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfying

\overline{\Ricc}\geq-n\displaystyle\inf_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}.

(18)

and
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
given,
there exists a unique function
u\in\mathscr{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying
u|_{\partial\Omega}=\varphi
whose Killing graph has mean curvature
H
if

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}\left|H\right|\leq\inf_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}.

(19)

Theorem
1
improves this result of Dajczer-Hinojosa-Lira in two senses. First of all because we are considering prescribed mean curvature functions that are not necessarily constant along the flow lines (
H=H(\Phi(x,z))
). Hypothesis (
10
) guarantees that the maximum principle for operator
\mathfrak{Q}
holds.

Secondly, because inequality (
19
) is weakened to a pointwise condition to be satisfied along the boundary. Indeed, if
H=H(x)
inequality (
12
) becomes

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\geq\left|H(y)\right|\ \forall\ y\in\partial\Omega.

(20)

The key is to replace the additional geometric restriction (
18
) on
\Omega
by an additional hypothesis on the function
H
(see (
11
)).

We notice that, (
12
) seems to be the natural generalization of conditions (
16
) and (
17
) because, analogously to the
\R^{n+1}
and
M\times\R
cases, it means that the mean curvature of the Killing cylinder must be greater than the mean curvature of the Killing graph of the function
u
at each point of the intersection between them.
The clues were given to us by two works whose focuses were existence of graphs-like with prescribed mean curvature in the half-space setting of the hyperbolic space
\HH^{n+1}
.

P.-A Nitsche
[
11
]
studied radial graphs over bounded domains in the spherical cap of euclidean radius 1. He proved that there exists a graph over
\Omega
with prescribed mean curvature a function
H(x)
taking values on
[0,1]
for arbitrary continuous boundary values if
\left|H(y)\right|<\mathcal{H}_{C}(y)
everywhere on
\partial\Omega
; while if
\left|H(y)\right|>\mathcal{H}_{C}(y)
for some
y\in\partial\Omega
, it can be constructed a smooth curve that cannot be the boundary of any graph over
\Omega
with curvature
H
.

On the other hand, Guio-Sa Earp
[
8
]
considered a bounded domain
\Omega
contained in a vertical hyperplane and studied the Dirichlet problem for the prescribed mean curvature equation for hypersurfaces which intersect at must in one point the horizontal horocycles orthogonal to
\Omega
. They obtained a sharp result when
\mathcal{H}_{C}(y)\geq\left|H(y)\right|
for each
y\in\partial\Omega
provided again that
\left|H(x)\right|\leq 1
in
\Omega
.

We highlight that the reason why Guio-Sa Earp and Nitsche have assumed that
H
takes values on [-1,1] is to use the horoespheres as barriers in order to obtain a priori height estimates. Theorem (
1
) allows to consider functions taking values in the complement of this set. To see this note that, in this context, condition (
11
) becomes

\left\|\overline{\nabla}H\left(\Phi(x,\varphi(y(x)))\right)\right\|_{\overline{M}}\leq\left(H\left(\Phi(x,\varphi(y(x)))\right)\right)^{2}-1.

2
Transformation formulas

In this section we compute some formulas that will help us throughout the text.
First we establish a proposition that gives us a relation between the mean curvature of an hypersurfaces in
M
and its translations along the flow lines of
Y
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be an embedded and oriented hypersurface in
M
and
N
a unit vector field normal to
\Gamma
. Let
\overline{\Gamma}
be the translation of
\Gamma
along the flow of
Y
.
Then, for every
x\in\Gamma
,
N(x)
is normal to
\overline{\Gamma}
at
x
and

n\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\Gamma}}(x)=(n-1)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(x)+\rho^{-2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,N\right\rangle},

(21)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\Gamma}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
are calculated with respect to
N
.

Proof.

Recall that the translation of
\Gamma
is given by

\overline{\Gamma}=\left\{\Phi(x,z),\ x\in\Gamma,\ z\in\R\right\}.

Therefore, if
x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}
are coordinates in
\Gamma
, then
x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},z
are coordinates in
\overline{\Gamma}
. Consequently,
Y=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z}
is tangent to
\overline{\Gamma}
. For
x\in\Gamma
we obviously have that
N(x)
is orthogonal to
\overline{\Gamma}
at
x
because it is orthogonal to
Y
(since
Y
is normal to
M
) and to the remaining coordinate vectors
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x_{i}}
.

Let us denote by
A_{N}
and
\overline{A}_{N}
the shape operator of
{\Gamma}
and
\overline{\Gamma}
, respectively. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1}
be the principal directions of
\Gamma
. Then, for each
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we have

-\overline{A}_{N}(e_{i})=\left(\overline{\conex}_{{e}_{i}}{N}\right)^{\top\left({\overline{\Gamma}}\right)}=\overline{\conex}_{{e}_{i}}{N}=\conex_{{e}_{i}}{N}+\left(\overline{\conex}_{{e}_{i}}{N}\right)^{\bot(M)}=\left(\conex_{{e}_{i}}{N}\right)^{\top\left({{\Gamma}}\right)}=-A_{N}(e_{i}),

where the the penultimate step is true because
M
is totally geodesic in
\overline{M}
and
N
is a unit vector.
This means that
e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1}
are also principal directions of
\overline{\Gamma}
at
x
.
Since
Y
is a tangent vector to
\overline{\Gamma}
orthogonal to
e_{i},\dots,e_{n-1}
, then
\rho^{-1}Y
is the
n
-th principal direction of
\overline{\Gamma}
, and the corresponding principal curvature is given by

\kappa(x)=\rho^{-2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,N\right\rangle}.

(22)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\kappa(x)={\left\langle\overline{A}_{N}\left(\rho^{-1}Y\right),\rho^{-1}Y\right\rangle}_{\overline{M}}=\rho^{-2}{\left\langle-\overline{\conex}_{Y}\overline{N},Y\right\rangle}_{\overline{M}}=\rho^{-2}\left(-Y{\left\langle\overline{N},Y\right\rangle}_{\overline{M}}+{\left\langle\overline{N},\overline{\conex}_{Y}{Y}\right\rangle}_{\overline{M}}\right).

Expression (
21
) directly follows from the previous explanation.
∎

2.1
Transformation formulas

In an open set in
M
we define
w=\psi\circ\dd+\varphi
, where
\psi\in\mathscr{C}^{2}([a,b])
and
\dd
is a distance function (that is,
\nabla\dd=1
).
We want to compute
\mathfrak{Q}w
, where
\mathfrak{Q}
is the operator defined in (
8
).
We have

\nabla w=\psi^{\prime}(\dd)\nabla\dd+\nabla\varphi,

so

\Hess w=\nabla\nabla w=\psi^{\prime\prime}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\cdot\right\rangle}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\cdot\right\rangle}+\psi^{\prime}\Hess\dd+\Hess\varphi

and

\Delta w=\tr\left(\Hess w\right)=\psi^{\prime\prime}+\psi^{\prime}\Delta\dd+\Delta\varphi.

(23)

We now compute

\displaystyle\Hess w(\nabla w,\nabla w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\nabla w\right\rangle}^{2}+\psi^{\prime}\Hess\dd(\nabla w,\nabla w)+\Hess\varphi(\nabla w,\nabla w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\nabla w\right\rangle}^{2}+\psi^{\prime}\left(\Hess\dd(\psi^{\prime}\nabla\dd+\nabla\varphi,\psi^{\prime}\nabla\dd+\nabla\varphi)\right)+\Hess\varphi(\nabla w,\nabla w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\nabla w\right\rangle}^{2}+\psi^{\prime}\left(\psi^{\prime 2}\Hess\dd(\nabla\dd,\nabla\dd)+2\psi^{\prime}\Hess\dd(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\dd)+\Hess\dd(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi)\right)+\Hess\varphi(\nabla w,\nabla w).

Since

\Hess\dd(X,\nabla\dd)={\left\langle\conex_{X}\nabla\dd,\nabla\dd\right\rangle}=\frac{1}{2}X{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\nabla\dd\right\rangle}=0

for every smooth field
X
over
M
, we conclude

\Hess w(\nabla w,\nabla w)=\psi^{\prime\prime}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\nabla w\right\rangle}^{2}+\psi^{\prime}\Hess\dd(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi)+\Hess\varphi(\nabla w,\nabla w).

(24)

On the other hand,

{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla w\right\rangle}={\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\psi^{\prime}\nabla\dd+\nabla\varphi\right\rangle}=\psi^{\prime}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\dd\right\rangle}+{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\varphi\right\rangle}.

Therefore,

\rho^{-2}\left(\rho^{-2}+W^{2}\right){\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla w\right\rangle}=\rho^{-4}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla w\right\rangle}+\psi^{\prime}\rho^{-2}W^{2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\dd\right\rangle}+\rho^{-2}W^{2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\varphi\right\rangle}.

(25)

Using (
23
), (
24
) and (
25
) we finally get (see (
8
))

\begin{split}\mathfrak{Q}w=\psi^{\prime}W^{2}\Delta\dd-\psi^{\prime}\Hess\dd(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi)+\psi^{\prime\prime}W^{2}-\psi^{\prime\prime}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\nabla w\right\rangle}^{2}+W^{2}\Delta\varphi-\Hess\varphi\left(\nabla w,\nabla w\right)\\
-\rho^{-4}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla w\right\rangle}-\psi^{\prime}\rho^{-2}W^{2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\dd\right\rangle}-\rho^{-2}W^{2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\varphi\right\rangle}-nH\left(\Phi(x,w)\right)W^{3},\end{split}

(26)

where

W=W(x,\nabla w)=\sqrt{\rho^{-2}+\left\|\nabla w\right\|^{2}}=\sqrt{\rho^{-2}+\left\|\psi^{\prime}\nabla\dd+\nabla\varphi\right\|^{2}}

Suppose now that
\dd
is the distance to a compact and embedded hypersurface
\Gamma
in
M
, that is,
\dd(x)=\dist(x,\Gamma)
.
It is well known that
\dd
is a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function over the set

\Sigma=\left\{\exp^{\bot}\left(y,tN(y)\right);\ y\in\Gamma,0\leq{t}<\tau(y)\right\},

where
N
is the unit normal field to
\Gamma
.
Besides, every
x\in\Sigma
is contained in an hypersurface
\Gamma_{\dd}
which is parallel to a portion of
\Gamma
(or even the whole
\Gamma
in case
x
is close enough to
\Gamma
). Furthermore, the laplacian of the function
\dd
and the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{\dd}
with respect to the unit normal field
N=\nabla\dd
are related by the formula

\Delta\dd(x)=-(n-1)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\dd}}(x).

Let
\overline{\Gamma}
the translation of
\Gamma
along the flow lines of
Y
. Proposition
3
then yields

n\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\Gamma}_{\dd}}(x)=(n-1)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\dd}}(x)+\rho^{-2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,N\right\rangle}=-\Delta\dd(x)+\rho^{-2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\dd\right\rangle}.

(27)

Substituting (
27
) in (
26
) we finally get

\begin{split}\mathfrak{Q}w=-n\psi^{\prime}W^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\Gamma}_{\dd}}-\psi^{\prime}\Hess\dd(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi)+\psi^{\prime\prime}W^{2}-\psi^{\prime\prime}{\left\langle\nabla\dd,\nabla w\right\rangle}^{2}+W^{2}\Delta\varphi-\Hess\varphi\left(\nabla w,\nabla w\right)\\
-\rho^{-4}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla w\right\rangle}-\rho^{-2}W^{2}{\left\langle\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y,\nabla\varphi\right\rangle}-nH\left(\Phi(x,w)\right)W^{3},\end{split}

(28)

Furthermore, if
\varphi
is a constant, then

\mathfrak{Q}w=-n\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\Gamma}_{\dd}}\psi^{\prime}\left(\rho^{-2}+\psi^{\prime 2}\right)+\rho^{-2}\psi^{\prime\prime}-\rho^{-2}\psi^{\prime}\kappa(x)-nH\left(\Phi(x,w)\right)\left(\rho^{-2}+\psi^{\prime 2}\right)^{3/2},

(29)

where
\kappa(x)
is the geodesic curvature of
\left\{\Phi_{x}(t),\ t\in\R\right\}
at
x
computed with respect to the normal
\nabla\dd(x)
.

3
The a priori estimates

Following a classical argument, the proof of Theorem
1
depends on a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
a priori estimate for the solutions of related Dirichlet problems. In this section we establish the required estimate.

3.1
Global gradient estimate

We adapt the proof of our Theorem 13 in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 4

(Global gradient estimate)
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded domain. If a function
u\in\mathscr{C}^{3}(\Omega)\cap\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
is a solution of (
2
) for

H\in\mathscr{C}^{1}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\left({\sup\limits_{\overline{\Omega}}\left|u\right|}\right)\right)
satisfying

{\left\langle\overline{\nabla}H,Y\right\rangle}_{\overline{M}}\geq 0\mbox{ in }\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\left({\sup\limits_{\overline{\Omega}}\left|u\right|}\right),

(30)

then

\sup_{\Omega}\left\|\nabla u\right\|\leq C

where
C
is a constant depending on the data

n
,

\sup\limits_{\Omega}\left|\overline{\Ricc}\right|
,

\sup\limits_{\Omega}\rho,\inf\limits_{\Omega}\rho
,

\sup\limits_{\Omega}\left\|\overline{\conex}_{Y}Y\right\|_{\overline{M}}
,

\sup\limits_{\Omega\times\left[-\sup\limits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|,\sup\limits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|\right]}\left\|D\Phi\right\|
,

\sup\limits_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\left({\sup\limits_{\overline{\Omega}}\left|u\right|}\right)}\left\|\overline{\nabla}H\right\|_{\overline{M}}

and on
\sup\limits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|
and

\sup\limits_{\partial\Omega}\left\|\nabla u\right\|
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem 13 in
[
1
]
, we set

w(x)=\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|e^{Au(x)},

(31)

where
A
is a constant greater than 1 to be fixed latter on. We denote by
x_{0}
a point where
w
attains its maximum in
\overline{\Omega}
. Suppose that
\nabla u(x_{0})\neq 0
. If
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, then

\sup_{\Omega}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}\left\|\nabla u\right\|e^{2A\sup\limits_{\Omega}\left|u\right|}.

(32)

Let us assume, instead, that
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Set normal coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
at
x_{0}
in
M
such that
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\big|_{x_{0}}=\frac{\nabla u(x_{0})}{\left\|\nabla u(x_{0})\right\|}
. Then,

\nabla u(x_{0})={\left\|\nabla u(x_{0})\right\|}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}}\big|_{x_{0}}

(33)

and

\partial_{k}u(x_{0})={\left\langle\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}}\big|_{x_{0}},\nabla u(x_{0})\right\rangle}=\left\|\nabla u(x_{0})\right\|\delta_{k1}.

(34)

We denote by
H_{0}=H(\Phi(x_{0},u(x_{0})))
,
\rho_{0}=\rho(x_{0})
e
W_{0}=\sqrt{\rho_{0}^{-2}+\left\|\nabla u(x_{0})\right\|^{2}}
. Differentiating (
2
) with respect to

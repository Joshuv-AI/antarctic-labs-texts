Acceleration in integro-differential combustion equations
Emeric Bouin, Jérôme Coville, Guillaume Legendre

Abstract
We study acceleration phenomena in monostable integro-differential equations with ignition nonlinearity. Our results cover fractional Laplace operators and standard convolutions in a unified way, which is also a contribution of this paper. To achieve this, we construct a sub-solution that captures the expected dynamics of the accelerating solution, and this is here the main difficulty. This study involves the flattening effect occurring in accelerated propagation phenomena.



Keywords:
integro-differential operators, fractional laplacian, acceleration, spreading.

1
Introduction.

In this paper, we are interested in describing quantitatively propagation phenomena in the following (non-local) integro-differential equation:

\displaystyle u_{t}(t,x)={\mathcal{D}}[\,{u}\,](t,x)+f(u(t,x))\quad\text{ for }\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)=\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]},

where

{\mathcal{D}}[\,{u}\,](t,x):=P.V.\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}[u(t,y)-u(t,x)]J(x-y)\,dy\right)

with
J
is a nonnegative function satisfying the following properties.

Hypothesis 1.1
.

Let
s\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. The kernel
J
is symmetric and is such that there exists positive constants
{\mathcal{J}}_{0},{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
and
R_{0}\geq 1
such that

\int_{|z|\leq 1}J(z)|z|^{2}\,dz\leq 2{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{{\mathcal{J}}_{0}}{|z|^{1+2s}}\mathds{1}_{\{|z|\geq 1\}}\geq J(z)\geq\frac{{\mathcal{J}}_{0}^{-1}}{|z|^{1+2s}}\mathds{1}_{\{|z|\geq R_{0}\}}.

The operator
{\mathcal{D}}[\,{}\,]
describes the dispersion process of the individuals. Roughly, the kernel
J
gives the probability of a jump from a position
x
to a position
y
, so that the tails of
J
are of crucial importance to quantify the dynamics of the population. As a matter of fact, the parameter
s
will thus appear in the rates we obtain later. One may readily notice that our hypothesis on
J
allows to cover the two broad types of integro-differential operators
{\mathcal{D}}[\,{u}\,]
usually considered in the literature which are the fractional laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}u
and the standard convolution operators with integrable kernels often written
J\star u-u
. This universality is one main contribution of this paper.

Hypothesis 1.2
.

Take
\theta>0
,

f(1)=0,\qquad f(u)=0\quad\text{if }u\leq\theta\qquad\text{and }f(u)>0\quad\text{in }[\theta,1].

The strong maximum principle implies that the solution to (
1.1
) takes values in
[0,1]
only. Moreover, since the initial data is decreasing, at all times
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the function
x\mapsto u(t,x)
is decreasing over
\mathbb{R}
, from one to zero. To follow the propagation, we may thus follow level sets of height
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

x_{\lambda}(t):=\sup\left\{x\in\mathbb{R},\,u(t,x)\geq\lambda\right\}.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
J
satisfies
1.1
with
s<\frac{1}{2}
and that
f
is an ignition nonlinearity. For any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the level line
x_{\lambda}(t)
accelerates with the following rate,

t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\lesssim x_{\lambda}(t)\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2s}+0}.

The main purpose of this research report is to show the lower bound, the sharp upper bound will come in a later work.

2
Flattening in the linear problem

Finally, the flattening behaviour of linear anomalous diffusions is reminiscent from
[
1
,
10
,
5
,
2
]
, and we shall here recall and extend some principal computations from these papers.

The Fourier symbol of the operator
{\mathcal{D}}[\,{\cdot}\,]
is

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R},\qquad W(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\cos(\xi y)-1\right)J(y)\,dy.

Note that one may recover two typical cases. If
J(y)\propto|y|^{-1-2s}
, that is
{\mathcal{D}}[\,{\cdot}\,]
is a fractional Laplacian, then
W(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}
. If
J
is an integrable function with unit mass, as in convolution models, then
W(\xi)=\hat{J}(\xi)-1
.
The presence of a singularity at
0
for
J
has an influence on large frequencies
\xi
, whereas the tail of
J
influences small frequencies. As a consequence,

For small
\xi
, write,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\cos(\xi y)-1\right)J(y)\,dy=\int_{|y|\leq R_{0}}\left(\cos(\xi y)-1\right)J(y)\,dy+\int_{|y|\leq R_{0}}\left(\cos(\xi y)-1\right)J(y)\,dy

The first integral in the r.h.s is of order
|\xi|^{2}
with explicit constant by a direct Taylor expansion and using the hypothesis on
J
. The second one is estimated as follows. Since

\int_{|y|\leq R_{0}}{\mathcal{J}}_{0}^{-1}\frac{\cos(\xi y)-1}{|y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\leq\int_{|y|\leq R_{0}}\left(\cos(\xi y)-1\right)J(y)\,dy\leq\int_{|y|\leq R_{0}}{\mathcal{J}}_{0}\frac{\cos(\xi y)-1}{|y|^{1+2s}}\,dy,

we have that
\int_{|y|\leq R_{0}}\left(\cos(\xi y)-1\right)J(y)\,dy
is of order
-|\xi|^{2s}
with explicit estimates. As a consequence, since
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
W
is of order
-|\xi|^{2s}
with explicit estimates.

In this section, we discuss the fact that the solution to the following linearised problem

\displaystyle G_{t}={\mathcal{D}}[\,{G}\,]\quad\text{ for }\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.2)

\displaystyle G(0,\cdot)=\delta_{x=0},

flattens, that is, there exists
C_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\exists x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\qquad G(t,x)\geq\frac{C_{0}t}{|x|^{1+2s}}.

The proof takes its spirit in
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
and we explain the arguments below. Note that then the solution to

\displaystyle v_{t}={\mathcal{D}}[\,{v}\,]\quad\text{ for }\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.3)

\displaystyle v(0,\cdot)=\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]},

is then given by

v(t,x)=G(t,\cdot)\star\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,0]}(\cdot)(x)=\int_{x}^{+\infty}G(t,y)\,dy.

From this computation, observe that for any
t
, we have
\lim_{x\to-\infty}v(t,x)=1
. Getting an estimate of
G
for very large
y
will yield an estimate for
v
.

Solving in the Fourier variable, this is

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\hat{G}(t,\xi)=\exp\left(W(\xi)t\right).

Observe that, formally,

G(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(W(\xi)t-ix\xi\right)\,d\xi=2\text{Re}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(W(\xi)t-ix\xi\right)\,d\xi\right).

Compute, for any
x\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\exp\left(W(\xi)t-ix\xi\right)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{Rx}\exp\left(W\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)t-iu\right)\,du

Following the same steps as in
[]
, we shall use a contour integral in the complex plane. Define the holomorphic function
\varphi(z):=\exp\left(W\left(\frac{z}{ix}\right)t-z\right)
that we integrate on the contour … . The Cauchy integral theorem gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{Rx}\exp\left(W\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)t-iu\right)\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{ix}\int_{0}^{Rx}\exp\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t-u\right)\,du+\frac{1}{ix}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\exp\left(W\left(\frac{Rxe^{i\theta}}{ix}\right)t-Rxe^{i\theta}\right)\,iRxe^{i\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{ix}\int_{0}^{Rx}\exp\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t-u\right)\,du+\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\exp\left(W\left(Re^{i\theta-i\frac{\pi}{2}}\right)t-Rxe^{i\theta}\right)\,Re^{i\theta}d\theta

The second integral goes to zero by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. Taking
R\to\infty
, we get that the following integral exists and that

\displaystyle 2\text{Re}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(W(\xi)t-ix\xi\right)\,d\xi\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{x}\text{Re}\left(i\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t-u\right)\,du\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Im}\left(\exp\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t\right)\right)e^{-u}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\text{Im}\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t\right)\right)\sin\left(\text{Im}\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t\right)\right)e^{-u}\,du

From the latter, we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\frac{2x^{1+2s}}{t}\text{Re}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(W(\xi)t-ix\xi\right)\,d\xi\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{2}{t}x^{2s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\text{Im}\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t\right)\right)\sin\left(\text{Im}\left(W\left(\frac{u}{ix}\right)t\right)\right)e^{-u}\,du

\displaystyle\gtrsim 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin(s\pi)u^{2s}e^{-u}\,du

using the scaling of
W
near zero and the dominated convergence theorem.

Auslander conditions and tilting-like cotorsion pairs
Jian Wang

Abstract




Abstract

We study homological behavior of modules satisfying the
Auslander condition. Assume that
\mathcal{AC}
is the class of left
R
-modules satisfying the Auslander condition. It is proved that each cycle of an exact complex with each term in
\mathcal{AC}
belongs to
\mathcal{AC}
for any ring
R
. As a consequence, we show that for any left Noetherian ring
R
,
\mathcal{AC}
is a resolving subcategory of the category of left
R
-modules if and only if
{}_{R}R
satisfies the Auslander condition if and only if each Gorenstein projective left
R
-module belongs to
\mathcal{AC}
. As an application, we prove that, for an Artinian algebra
R
satisfying the Auslander condition,
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{AC}
coincides with the class of Gorenstein projective left
R
-modules if and only if (
{\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},(\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty})^{\bot}
) is a tilting-like cotorsion pair if and only if (
{\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},\mathcal{I}
) is a tilting-like cotorsion pair, where
\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}
is the class of left
R
-modules with finite
\mathcal{AC}
-dimension and
\mathcal{I}
is the class of injective left
R
-modules. This leads to some criteria for the validity of the Auslander and Reiten conjecture which says that an Artinian algebra satisfying the Auslander condition is Gorenstein.

Keywords:
Auslander condition; Tilting-like cotorsion pair; Tilting cotorsion pair; Gorenstein ring; Gorenstein projective module.

1.
Introduction

For a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, Bass proved in
[
7
]
that, it is a Gorenstein ring (that is, the self-injective dimension of
R
is finite) if and only if the flat dimension of the
i
-th term in a minimal injective coresolution of
R
as an
R
-module is at most
i-1
for any
i\geqslant 1
. Auslander showed that this condition is left-right symmetric for a left and right Noetherian ring
R
; see Fossum, Griffith and Reiten
[
16
]
. In this case,
R
is said to satisfy the
Auslander condition
, which may be regarded as a non-commutative version of commutative Gorenstein rings. Moreover, Auslander and Reiten conjectured in
[
4
]
that an Artinian algebra satisfying the Auslander condition is Gorenstein. The conjecture is of particular interest due to its strong connection with several long-standing homological conjectures such as the finitistic dimension conjecture, the Nakayama conjecture and the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture (see
[
4
,
8
,
22
]
).

Cotorsion pairs were invented by Salce
[
28
]
in the category of
abelian groups and have been deeply studied in approximation theory of modules
[
19
]
, especially in the proof of the flat cover conjecture
[
10
]
. Motivated by the fact that finitistic dimensions of an algebra can alternatively be computed by Gorenstein projective dimension, Moradifar and
\check{\rm{S}}
aroch
[
27
]
introduced the notion of tilting-like cotorsion pairs, which is a natural generalization of tilting cotorsion pairs. These cotorsion pairs have been studied in
[
27
,
30
]
, especially providing criteria for the validity of the finitistic dimension conjecture and the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture.

The main aim of this paper is to investigate the homological behavior of modules satisfying the
Auslander condition. More precisely, we first characterize when the class
\mathcal{AC}
of left
R
-modules satisfying the Auslander condition is resolving. As a result, we establish the connection between the Auslander and Reiten conjecture
mentioned above with the cotorsion pair induced by the class
\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}
of left
R
-modules with finite
\mathcal{AC}
-dimension.

To state our results precisely, we first introduce some notation and definitions.
Throughout this paper,
R
is an associative ring with identity and
R
-Mod is the category of left
R
-modules. Recall that a full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
R
-Mod is
resolving
if it contains all projective modules and is closed under direct summands, extensions and kernels of surjective homomorphisms. The notion of a resolving subcategory was introduced by Auslander and Bridger
[
3
]
in the study of totally reflexive modules, which are also called modules of Gorenstein dimension zero or finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules.

Let
M
be a left
R
-module. We use

0\to M\to E^{0}(M)\to E^{1}(M)\to\cdots\to E^{i}(M)\to\cdots

to denote a minimal injective coresolution of
M
.
Following
[
22
]
,
M
is
said to satisfy
the Auslander condition
if the flat dimension of
E^{i}(M)
is at most
i
for any
i\geqslant 0
. Denote by
\mathcal{AC}
the class of left
R
-modules satisfying the Auslander condition.

Recall that a ring
R
is
Gorenstein

[
25
]
if it is left and right Noetherian and
R
has finite self-injective dimension on either side. Moreover, a Gorenstein ring is called
Auslander-Gorenstein

[
11
]
if it satisfies the Auslander condition.
A left and right Noetherian ring
R
is called
Auslander-regular

[
11
]
if
R
satisfies the Auslander condition and each left
R
-module has finite projective dimension.

Our first main result, characterizing when
\mathcal{AC}
is a resolving subcategory of
R
-Mod, can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be a left Noetherian ring. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{AC}
is a resolving subcategory of
R
-
Mod
.

(2)

{}_{R}R
satisfies the Auslander condition.

(3)

Each Gorenstein projective left
R
-module satisfies the Auslander condition.

(4)

Each Ding projective left
R
-module satisfies the Auslander condition.

(5)

Each Gorenstein flat left
R
-module satisfies the Auslander condition.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies heavily on the observation that
each cycle of an exact complex with each term in
\mathcal{AC}
belongs to
\mathcal{AC}
for any ring
R
; see Proposition
3.2
. As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we prove that an Artinian algebra
R
is Auslander-Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{AC}
coincides with the class of Gorenstein projective left
R
-modules, which gives a criterion for the validity of the Auslander and Reiten conjecture mentioned above; see Corollary
3.5
.

Recall that a cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
of left
R
-modules is said to be
tilting-like
if
\mathcal{B}=(T\oplus S)^{\perp_{\infty}}
, where
T
is a tilting left
R
-module and
S
is a strongly Gorenstein projective left
R
-module; see
[
27
, Definition 1.19]
. Needless to say, for
S=0
we recover the tilting cotorsion pair induced by
T
.

Let
\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}
be the class of left
R
-modules with finite
\mathcal{AC}
-dimension, i.e., for each
M
in
\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}
, there is a non-negative integer
m
and an exact sequence
0\rightarrow P_{m}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
of left
R
-modules with each
P_{i}
in
\mathcal{AC}
. Assume that
R
is a left Noetherian ring such that
{}_{R}R
satisfies the Auslander condition. It follows from Theorem
1.1
that
\mathcal{AC}
is a resolving subcategory of
R
-Mod. In combination with a result due to Huang
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
, we obtain that
\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}
is also resolving; see Corollary
3.6
.

Our next main result characterizes when
\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}
can induce tilting or tilting-like cotorsion pairs.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be a left and right Noetherian ring satisfying the Auslander condition. Denote by
\mathcal{I}
the class of injective left
R
-modules and by
\mathcal{GI}
the class of Gorenstein injective left
R
-modules. Then

(1)

R
is a Gorenstein ring if and only if
(
{\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},\mathcal{I}
)
is a tilting-like cotorsion pair.

(2)

The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

R
is an Auslander-regular ring.

(b)

(
{\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},\mathcal{I}
)
is a tilting cotorsion pair.

(c)

(
{\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},\mathcal{GI}
)
is a tilting cotorsion pair.

(d)

(
{\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},\mathcal{GI}
)
is a tilting-like cotorsion pair.

(3)

If
R
is an Artinian algebra, then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
(
{\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},(\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty})^{\bot}
)
is a tilting-like cotorsion pair.

(4)

If
R
is an Artinian algebra, then
R
is Auslander-regular if and only if
({\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty}},(\mathcal{AC}^{<\infty})^{\bot}
)
is a tilting cotorsion pair.

Remark that (1) and (3) in Theorem
1.2
together provide criteria for the validity of the Auslander and Reiten conjecture mentioned above.

This paper is structured as follows. Section
2
contains notation and
definitions for use throughout this paper. In Section
3
, we prove all results mentioned in Introduction. In the course of our proofs, we prove that each cycle of an exact complex with each term in
\mathcal{AC}
belongs to
\mathcal{AC}
for any ring
R
; see Proposition
3.2
.

2.2.
Gorenstein modules

Following
[
14
,
20
]
, a left
R
-module
G
is called
Gorenstein projective
if there is an exact sequence

\mathbf{P}:\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow P^{0}\rightarrow P^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

of projective left
R
-modules such that
G

\cong

\ker(P^{0}\rightarrow P^{1})
and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{P},Q)
is exact for every projective left
R
-module
Q
. In this case, the complex
\mathbf{P}
is also called a totally acyclic complex of projective left
R
-modules. Moreover, a left
R
-module
M
is called
strongly Gorenstein projective

[
9
]
if there is a totally acyclic complex

\cdots\rightarrow P\xrightarrow{f}P\xrightarrow{f}P\xrightarrow{f}P\xrightarrow{f}\cdots

of projective left
R
-modules such that
M

\cong

\ker(f)
.

A left
R
-module
M
is called
Ding projective

[
13
,
18
]
if there is an exact sequence

\mathbf{P}:\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow P^{0}\rightarrow P^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

of projective left
R
-modules with
M\cong\textrm{ker}(P^{0}\rightarrow P^{1})
such that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{P},F)
is exact for every flat left
R
-module
F
.

A left
R
-module
M
is called
Gorenstein flat

[
15
]
if there is an exact sequence

\mathbf{F}:\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow F^{0}\rightarrow F^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

of flat left
R
-modules with
M\cong\textrm{ker}(F^{0}\rightarrow F^{1})
such that
E\otimes_{R}\mathbf{F}
is exact for every injective right
R
-module
E
.

A left
R
-module
M
is called
Gorenstein injective

[
14
]
if there exists an exact sequence

\mathbf{I}:\cdots\rightarrow I_{1}\rightarrow I_{0}\rightarrow I^{0}\rightarrow I^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

of injective left
R
-modules with
M\cong\textrm{ker}(I^{0}\rightarrow I^{1})
such that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(E,\mathbf{I})
is exact for every injective left
R
-module

.

In the following, we use
\mathcal{GP}
,
\mathcal{G}p
,
\mathcal{GF}
and
\mathcal{GI}
to denote the classes of Gorenstein projective, finitely generated Gorenstein projective, Gorenstein flat and Gorenstein injective left

2.3.
Cotorsion pairs

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be classes in
R
-Mod.
A pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is called a
cotorsion pair

[
19
,
28
]
if
\mathcal{A}

=

{}^{\bot}\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}

=

\mathcal{A}^{\bot}
. The class
\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}
is called the
kernel
of the cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

A cotorsion pair (
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
) is said to be

hereditary

[
19
]
if
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(A,B)

=

0
for all
i

\geqslant

1
,
A

\in

\mathcal{A}
and
B

\in

\mathcal{B}
. This definition is equivalent to the condition that if whenever
0\rightarrow A_{3}\rightarrow A_{2}\rightarrow A_{1}\rightarrow 0
is exact with
A_{2}
,
A_{1}

\in

\mathcal{A}
, then
A_{3}
is also in
\mathcal{A}
, or equivalently, if whenever
0\rightarrow B_{1}\rightarrow B_{2}\rightarrow B_{3}\rightarrow 0
is exact with
B_{1}
,
B_{2}

\in

\mathcal{B}
, then
B_{3}
is also in
\mathcal{B}
.

A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
complete

[
19
]
if, for each left
R
-module
M
, there is an exact sequence
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow B\rightarrow L\rightarrow 0
with
B

\in

\mathcal{B}
and
L

\in

\mathcal{A}
. This definition is equivalent to the condition that, for each left
R
-module
M
, there is an exact sequence
0\rightarrow D\rightarrow C\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
with
C

\in

\mathcal{A}
and
D

\in

\mathcal{B}
.

A cotorsion
pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
generated by a set

[
19
]
provided that there is a set
\mathcal{S}
of left
R
-modules such that
\mathcal{S}^{\bot}=\mathcal{B}

(
i.e.,
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

=

(^{\bot}(\mathcal{S}^{\bot}),\mathcal{S}^{\bot})
)
. A cotorsion pair generated by a set
\mathcal{S}
is also called a cotorsion pair
cogenerated
by
\mathcal{S}
in some literature. Here, we use the terminology from
[
19
]
.
If (
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
) is a hereditary cotorsion pair generated by a set
\mathcal{S}
, then there is a single module
M:=\bigoplus_{A\in\mathcal{S}}A
such that
M^{\bot_{\infty}}=\mathcal{B}
.

A left
R
-module
T
is
tilting

[
2
,
12
]
if
\mbox{\rm pd}_{R}T

<

\infty
,
T^{(\lambda)}

\in

T^{\bot_{\infty}}
for every cardinal
\lambda
and there is an exact sequence
0\rightarrow R\rightarrow T_{0}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow T_{r}\rightarrow 0
with
T_{i}

\in

\mbox{\rm Add}T
for all
0

\leqslant

i

\leqslant

r
, where
r
is the projective dimension of
T
and Add
T
is all direct summands of direct sums of copies of
T
.

Following
[
19
]
, a cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
of left
R
-modules is called a
tilting cotorsion pair
if
\mathcal{B}

=

T^{\bot_{\infty}}
, where
T
is a tilting left
R
-module. Recall from
[
27
, Definition 1.19]
that a cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
of left
R
-modules is said to be
tilting-like
if
\mathcal{B}=(T\oplus S)^{\perp_{\infty}}
, where
T
is a tilting left
R
-module and
S
is a strongly Gorenstein projective left
R
-module. It is clear that all tilting cotorsion pairs are tilting-like cotorsion pairs. But the converse is not true in general. For example, the module
M:
=
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
over the
quasi-Frobenius ring
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
is clearly a non-projective strongly Gorenstein
projective module, whence the hereditary cotorsion pair generated by
M
is
tilting-like, but it cannot be tilting since
M
does not have finite projective
dimension.

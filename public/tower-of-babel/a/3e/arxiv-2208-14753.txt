Optimal transport with nonlinear mobilities: a deterministic particle approximation result
S. Di Marino, L. Portinale, E. Radici

Abstract
. We study the discretisation of generalised Wasserstein distances with nonlinear mobilities on the real line via suitable discrete metrics on the cone of N ordered particles, a setting which naturally appears in the framework of deterministic particle approximation of partial differential equations. In particular, we provide a Γ \Gamma -convergence result for the associated discrete metrics as N → ∞ N\to\infty to the continuous one and discuss applications to the approximation of one-dimensional conservation laws (of gradient flow type) via the so-called generalised minimising movements, proving a convergence result of the schemes at any given discrete time step τ > 0 \tau>0 . This the first work of a series aimed at sheding new lights on the interplay between generalised gradient-flow structures, conservation laws, and Wasserstein distances with nonlinear mobilities.



1.
Introduction

The optimal transport theory has been a very active research field in the last twenty years, revealing suprising connections between the classical Monge–Kantorovich transportation model and various other fields of mathematics, including the theory of partial differential equations (PDEs)
[
JKO98
]
, functional and geometric inequalities
[
OV00
]
, and metric measure spaces
[
LV07
,
LV09
,
Stu06
]
.
These connections are fundamentally based on the ideas of the seminal works of Benamou–Brenier
[
BB00
]
and Otto
[
Ott01
]
: one can associate a (formal) infinite-dimensional Riemannian structure to the space of probability measures using the quadratic transport distance (i.e. the
2
-Wasserstein–Kantorovich–Rubenstein distance), whose associated gradient flow of the relative entropy coincides with the heat equation (in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
). This new perspective opened the door to wide range applications, not limited to the continuous setting of Riemannian manifolds, but applicable to discrete and non-commutative frameworks as well
[
Maa11
,
Mie11
,
CM14
]
.

The main focus of this work is the study of generalised optimal transport distances
[
DNS12
]
,
[
LM10
]
on the real line, its spatial discretisation using deterministic particle methods (in the spirit of
[
DFR15
]
), and the applications of these methods to the study of the associated gradient-flow evolutions.

The class of evolutions we are interested in might include local and/or non local effects, possibily including prevention of overcrowding. Mathematically, this translates into considering evolutions with
non-linear mobilities
, possibly with bounded support. To these equations, one can associate a corresponding Wasserstein-like distance that takes into account the nonlinearity.

Our goal is to study suitable discretisations of these problems, both the generalised optimal transport distances and the associated gradient flows, with particular attention to two types of approximations: in space and in time.

In particular, we introduce, in the scalar case, a
space discretisation
in the framework of non-linear mobilities, adopting a
Lagrangian
point of view. The generalised transport distances are approximated using systems of
N
-ordered particles, and a discrete-to-continuum approximation result is provided (Theorem
3.1
). Subsequently, we take advantage of this discrete-in-space approximation to show the stability at the level of the corresponding gradient-flow structures, providing a finite-dimensional approximation
of the associated evolution equation (Theorem
3.2
).

1.1.
Optimal transport with non-linear mobilities

The
generalised Wasserstein distances
were introduced in
[
DNS12
]
,
[
LM10
]
. Given
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
and a mobility
m:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the generalized Wasserstein psuedo-distance is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2}:=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v_{t}|^{2}m(\rho_{t})\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{1}\,\mathrm{d}t\ :\ \partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\nabla\cdot(m(\rho_{t})v_{t})=0,\ \mu_{t=i}=\mu_{i}\right\},

for
i=0,1
, where in the formula above
\rho_{t}
denotes the density of
\mu_{t}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{1}
on
\mathbb{R}
.

One of the motivation for the introduction of
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}
is precisely to be able to give a variational structure to PDEs of the type

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{t}-\nabla\cdot(m(\rho_{t})\nabla(\text{D}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{t}))=0\,,

which indeed can be formally seen as gradient flows of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}
. The linear case
m(\rho)=\rho
corresponds to the classical Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{W}_{2}
.

Following the ideas of Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto, one
rigorous interpretation of (
1.1
) as a gradient flow consists in considering the time-discrete approximation (also known as
JKO-scheme
from the names of the authors, or, more generally,
minimising movements scheme
in the spirit of De Giorgi, see
[
AGS08
,
San17
]
) of parameter
\tau>0
given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{\tau}^{(n)}\in\argmin_{\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{\mathcal{E}(\mu)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu,\mu_{\tau}^{(n-1)})^{2}\right\}\,.

The goal is then to recover a solution (
1.1
) defining a curve
\bm{\mu}^{\tau}:[0,1]\to\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
obtained, for every
\tau>0
, interpolating the family
\left(\mu_{\tau}^{(n)}\right)_{n}
and taking its limit as
\tau\to 0
. This is the content of
[
JKO98
]
in the case of linear mobility and the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

1.2.
Main results

The goal of this work is a particle approximation result for the Wasserstein-like distances with non-linear mobilities
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}
and its applications to the convergence of associated gradient-flow structures, in the scalar case
d=1
.
A natural generalisation of this result to the case of
p
-homogenous energy functionals for
p\neq 2
is also considered. For the sake of simplicity, we here restrict the exposition of the main results to the quadratic case.

The starting point of discussion is the work
[
DFR15
]
(together with several companion works, see Section
1.4
for a more detailed literature review), where the authors propose a deterministic particle approximation result to entropic solutions to scalar conservation laws of the form
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\partial_{x}(f[\mu_{t}])=0
. The procedure is based on a discretisation of the Lagrangian formulation of the evolution, working in terms of pseudo-inverses of cumulative distributions and their approximation. For any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
, one considers the quantile function

\displaystyle X_{\mu}(z):=\inf\left\{a\in\mathbb{R}\ :\ \mu(-\infty,a)>z\right\}\,.

At least formally, the evolution of
x_{t}:=X_{\mu_{t}}
is then given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{t}(z)=v[\mu_{t}(x_{t}(z))]\,,

where
v[\mu]:=f[\mu]/\mu
is the Eulerian velocity.
The above mentioned works consider the discrete model given by the space of
N+1
ordered particles
\mathcal{K}_{N}:=\{x=(x_{0},\dots,x_{N})\ :\ x_{0}<\dots<x_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and space-discrete evolutions obtained via a natural discretisation of (
1.3
).
Folloeing
[
DFR15
]
, for compactly supported measures
\mu
, we set
x_{j}^{N}:=X_{\mu}(j/N)
for
j=0,\dots,N
; then we notice that formally we also have
\mu_{t}(x_{t}(z))=\partial_{z}x_{t}(z)^{-1}
, so that we can use the discrete derivative of
x_{t}
as a recostruction for the density. In the end we consider the system of ODEs

(1.4)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{j}^{N}(t)=v[R_{j}^{N}(t)]\,,\quad R_{j}^{N}(t)=\frac{1}{N(x_{j+1}^{N}(t)-x_{j}^{N}(t))}\,,\quad j=0,\dots,N-1\,,

There is no canonical definition of the rightmost reconstructed density
R_{N}^{N}(t)
, since
x_{N}^{N}(t)
have no particles in front. For example, in
[
DFR15
]
the authors set
R_{N}^{N}(t)=\arg\max v(\cdot)
, the so-called
Follow-The-Leader scheme
. The name comes from the fact that the last particle
x^{N}_{N}
moves with maximum velocity, whilst the other particles "see" the one in front of them, and they choose their velocity accordingly.
It is then showed that, both the empirical and piecewise constant measures

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{N}(x^{N}(t)):=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\delta_{x_{j}^{N}(t)},\quad\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x^{N}(t)):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}R_{j}^{N}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\left[x_{j}^{N}(t),x_{j+1}^{N}(t)\right)}

converge as
N\to+\infty
to the (unique) entropy solution of the associated conservation law.

The main goal of this work is to introduce a suitable transport distance at the discrete level, on the open cone
\mathcal{K}_{N}
, which plays the role of a discrete approximation of the optimal transport distance
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}
on
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
, and prove a convergence result as
N\to\infty
; then we will extend this approach to the gradient flow structure introducing the JKO scheme at the discrete level.
For every given concave mobility
m:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(1.6)

\displaystyle d_{2,m}^{N}(z,y)^{2}:=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{|\dot{x}_{j}(t)|^{2}}{\theta(R_{j}(t))}\,\mathrm{d}t\ :\ x(0)=z,\,x(1)=y\,,\ x(t)\in\mathcal{K}_{N}\right\}\,,

where
\theta(\rho):=m(\rho)/\rho
and
R_{j}(t)
as in (
1.4
). Our first contribution is to show that the discrete (pseudo)distances
d_{2,m}^{N}
are a good approximation of the continuous ones
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}
. We define
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}:\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
as

\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}(\mu,\nu)=\begin{cases}d_{2,m}^{N}(x,y)\qquad&\text{ if }\mu=\mathcal{E}^{N}(x)\text{ and }\nu=\mathcal{E}^{N}(y)\text{ for some }x,y\in\mathcal{K}_{N}\\
+\infty&\text{ if }\mu\notin\mathcal{E}^{N}(\mathcal{K}_{N})\text{ or }\nu\notin\mathcal{E}^{N}(\mathcal{K}_{N})\end{cases}

First main result
: the (pseudo)distances
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}

\Gamma
-converge to
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}
as
N\to+\infty
, with respect to the vague and the classical
\mathbb{W}_{2}
-topology.

Note that in the interesting case when m is assumed to have compact support, the definition of
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}
presents an additional singularity, which in particular makes this convergence less trivial.

The study of the discrete distances opens the doors to possible applications to gradient flows approximation. In particular, at fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, for a given functional on
F_{N}:\mathcal{K}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
and initial value
x^{(0),N}\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
, one can consider the associated JKO-scheme

(1.7)

\displaystyle x_{\tau}^{(n),N}\in\argmin_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{N}}\left\{F_{N}(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}d_{2,m}^{N}\big(x,x_{\tau}^{(n-1),N}\big)^{2}\right\}.

Assume that
F_{N}
suitably converges to some continuous functional
F:\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
and that the empirical measures
\mu^{(0),N}
associated to the initial data
x^{(0),N}
as in (
1.5
) converge to a limit
\mu^{(0)}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
. Similarly, denote by
\mu_{\tau}^{(n),N}=\mathcal{E}^{N}\big(x_{\tau}^{(n),N}\big)
the empirical measures associated to
x_{\tau}^{(n),N}
.

Second main result
: for
\tau>0
, under suitable assumptions on
F_{N}\to F
(see (
A
), (
B
), and (
3.1
)), the steps of the discrete JKO-scheme (
1.7
) suitably converge to the steps of the continuous JKO-scheme (
1.2
),
i.e.
\mu_{\tau}^{(n),N}\to\mu_{\tau}^{(n)}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, as
N\to+\infty
.

In particular, we believe that the variational point of view coming from the gradient-flow structure represents a powerful tool to handle evolution equations that might present deep mathematical difficulties, due to the lack of regularity and uniqueness.

1.3.
Sketch of the proofs

For the convenience of the reader, we include a short sketch of the proofs of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
, avoiding the technical details. In what follows, we denote by
\mathsf{CE}
the set of solutions of the
continuity equation
on
\mathbb{R}
, i.e. the curves
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
which solves in the sense of distribution the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\partial_{x}\nu_{t}=0\,,\quad t\in[0,1]\,.

Sketch of the liminf inequality

Consider a sequence
\big\{\big(x^{N}(0),x^{N}(1)\big)\big\}_{N}
, with
x^{N}(0),x^{N}(1)\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
. Assume that the piecewise-constant measures
\mu_{0}^{N}:=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x^{N}(0))
,
\mu_{1}^{N}:=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x^{N}(1))\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
are vaguely (in duality with
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
) converging to
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
as
N\to\infty
.
We want to show the
liminf inequality

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}d_{2,m}^{N}(x^{N}(0),x^{N}(1))\geq\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\,.

Let
x^{N}(\cdot)
be the discrete geodesic between
x^{N}(0)
and
x^{N}(1)
with respect to
d_{2,m}^{N}
, viz. satisfying

(1.8)

\displaystyle d_{2,m}^{N}(x^{N}(0),x^{N}(1))=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{|\dot{x}_{j}^{N}(t)|^{2}}{\theta(R_{j}^{N}(t))}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{|\dot{x}_{j}^{N}(t)|^{2}}{\theta(R_{j}^{N}(t))}<\infty\,.

We define
\mu_{t}^{N}:=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x^{N}(t))

and consider the momentum fields given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle j_{t}^{N}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\dot{x}_{i}^{N}(t)R_{i}^{N}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{[x_{i}^{N}(t),x_{i+1}^{N}(t))}\,,\quad\nu_{t}^{N}:=j_{t}^{N}\mathscr{L}^{1}\,.

Intuitively, because the particles follows the evolution described by
\dot{x}_{i}^{N}
, we expect
(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})
to be close to a solution to
\mathsf{CE}
. We show indeed, in the limit as
N\rightarrow+\infty
, that
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}^{N}+\partial_{x}j_{t}^{N}\to 0
in the sense of distributions.

The second step is to provide a compactness result for (almost) solutions to
\mathsf{CE}
satisfying energy bounds as (
1.8
), in the same spirit of
[
DNS12
, Lemma 4.5]
: we prove that, up to a non-relabeled subsequence,
(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})\to(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
in a weak topology (Proposition
5.2
).

The final step is the comparison of the discrete and continuous energy functionals: by construction, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|j_{t}^{N}|^{2}}{m(\rho_{t}^{N})}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{N+1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{|\dot{x}_{j}^{N}(t)|^{2}}{\theta(R_{j}^{N}(t))}\,,\quad t\in[0,1]\,.

The proof of the liminf inequality is then concluded by (
1.8
) and by means of lower semicontinuity properties of the continuous action (
I
).

Sketch of the limsup inequality

We fix
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R})
two probability measures with
q
-th moment finite and such that
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})<\infty
. We pick
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
the unique
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}
-geodesic between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
. The goal is to find a sequence
\{(x^{N}(0),x^{N}(1))\}_{N}
with
x^{N}(i)\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
such that we have the
limsup inequality

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}d_{2,m}^{N}(x^{N}(0),x^{N}(1))\leq\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\,.

The idea is to first regularise the curve
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})
in order to obtain a solution of
\mathsf{CE}
with compact support and smooth densities (w.r.t to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
), comparable energy, and whose density does not assume value at the boundary of the support of
m
(which creates singularities in the energy functional). This is the content of Proposition
4.2
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we assume here that
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
has such properties:

i)

for all
t\in[0,1]
,
\mu_{t}
and
\nu_{t}
have compact and connected support,
\mu_{t},\nu_{t}\ll\mathscr{L}^{1}
and their respective densities densities
\rho_{t}
,
j_{t}
are
C^{\infty}
in their support

ii)

there exist a compact set
K
and two constants
0<l\leq\tilde{M}<M
such that

\supp(\rho_{t}),\,\supp(j_{t})\subset K\quad\text{and}\quad l<\rho_{t}(x)<\tilde{M}\quad

for all
x\in\supp(\rho_{t})
and
t\in[0,1]
.

Therefore, we can consider the flow map
\Psi_{v}
associated with
v_{t}:=j_{t}/\rho_{t}
and define for
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle X_{t}(z):=\Psi_{v}(t,X_{0}(z)),\quad X_{0}:=X_{\mu_{0}},\quad X_{0}\in C^{1}([0,1]),

which is nothing but the quantile function
X_{t}=X_{\mu_{t}}
of
\mu_{t}
. The sought recovery sequence can then be obtained as

\displaystyle x_{i}^{N}(t):=X_{t}\left(\frac{i}{N}\right),\quad t\in[0,1],\;i\in\{0,...,N\}\,.

It is not hard to see that
\mu_{t}^{N}:=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x^{N}(t))\to\mu_{t}
in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
as
N\to\infty
. Moreover, by means of Jensen’s inequality and by the regularity assumptions on
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})
, one can show that the discrete energy of
x^{N}(t)
converge to the one of
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})
as
N\to\infty
, which concludes the proof.

Sketch of the JKO-scheme convergence

The convergence of the discrete JKO-schemes relies on the convergence of the distances. Indeed, under suitable assumptions on the functionals
F_{N}
, one can reduce the proof to the simpler case of
F_{N}\equiv 0
. In particular, it suffices to show that, for some
q>1
, the functionals

\displaystyle y^{N}\mapsto\mathcal{J}_{N}(y^{N}):=d_{2,m}^{N}(x^{N},y^{N})^{2}\,,\quad\text{for}\;x^{N}\in\mathcal{K}_{N}\;\text{s.t.}\;\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{N}(x^{N})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{W}_{q}}\mu^{(0)}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}),\\
\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}^{N}(x^{N})}|x|^{2}<\infty,\end{cases}

are
equicoercive
and
\Gamma
-converging
to the continuous one
\mu\mapsto\mathcal{J}(\mu):=\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu^{(0)},\mu)
.

In order to have equicoercivity for
\left(\mathcal{J}_{N}\right)_{N}
, and because we do not want to enforce any coercivity for the functionals
\left(F_{N}\right)_{N}
(due to the applications we have in mind), we work with
q<2
. Indeed, using that balls in
\mathbb{W}_{2}
are pre-compact in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
, for every
q<2
(but not
q=2
!), it is not hard to prove the sought equicoercivity. On the other hand, working with
q<2
makes the
\Gamma
-convergence less trivial.

Let us say we have
\big(x^{N}\big)_{N}
,
\mu^{(0)}
,
\mu
given and we want to find a recovery sequence
\big(y^{N}\big)_{N}
for
\left(\mathcal{J}_{N}\right)_{N}
. A first näive attempt would be to apply Theorem
3.1
, which ensures the existence of a recovery sequence
\{(\tilde{x}^{N},\tilde{y}^{N})\}_{N}
such that
d_{2,m}^{N}(\tilde{x}^{N},\tilde{y}^{N})\to\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu^{(0)},\mu)
, and set
y^{N}:=\tilde{y}^{N}
. Nonetheless, having by assumption that
\mathcal{E}^{N}(x^{N})\to\mu^{(0)}
in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
(
not
in
\mathbb{W}_{2}
), a priori we are not able to show that
d_{2,m}^{N}(x^{N},\tilde{x}^{N})\to 0
.

However, thanks to the fact that
\big(x^{N}\big)_{N}
has uniformly bounded second moments, we are able to provide the existence of the sought recovery sequence in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
, for
q<2
. This relies on an quantitative regularisation result (Proposition
4.2
, in particular
(iii)
) and a careful construction of the recovery sequence, based on Theorem
3.1
together with a delicate localisation argument (see proof of Lemma
6.1
).

1.4.
Related literature

The Wasserstein gradient-flow structure is a common feature of several evolution equations, including Fokker–Planck equations, porous-medium
[
Ott01
]
, fourth-order evolutions
[
MMS09
,
LMZ16
]
, and more generally can describe diffusion, aggregation, and advection effects, see
[
San17
]
for a general discussion about various possible combinations.

The JKO scheme as a numerical tool to approximate PDEs has recently been the focus of several works, such as porous medium equations
[
BCL16
]
and the Patlak–Keller–Segel model
[
BCC08
]
. In the setting of non-linear mobilities, this variational strcture is exploited in a couple of recent works, in the case of scalar Burger equations
[
GO13
]
and thin-films evolutions
[
LMS12
]
.

In the last decade, a lot of work have been devoted to the study of discrete approximation of optimal transport distances
[
GM13
,
GT20
,
GKM20
,
GKMP20
,
GKMP21
]
and of the associated gradient flows
[
DL15
,
FMP22
]
. Nonetheless, these works deal with a discretisation of the underlying space (here
\mathbb{R}
), and do not work with deterministic particle methods.

The notion of generalised Wasserstein distances has been introduced in
[
DNS12
,
LM10
]
and the study of the corresponding gradient-flow structures found applications to the study of non-linear cross-diffusion systems
[
BDFPS10
]
and fourth-order equations, such as Cahn-Hillard and thin film equations
[
LMS12
]
,
[
Zin17
]
. See also

[
CLSS10
]
for the study of convexity properties of functionals on the space of measures with respect to such transport distances.

On the other hand, the validation of the continuum evolutive equations is classically obtained proving the convergence of systems of interacting particles.
The literature involving probabilistic methods is extremely rich and quite well-understood, see e.g.
[
DMP91
]
,
[
SV79
]
.
A first rigorous fully determistic approximation result for non-linear conservation laws has been obtained in
[
DFR15
]
. More in general, many-particle limit results for scalar evolutions with non-local interaction have been obtained for both linear
[
DRR22
,
FR21
]
and non-linear mobilities
[
DFS19
,
DFFR19
,
FR18
,
RS21
,
FT22
]
. In particular, these works concern the validity of the top, blue arrow in Figure
1
.

In all the above mentioned deterministic methods, the particle system is obtained as a discretised version of the Lagrangian formulation of the evolution, using the pseudo-inverse formalism.
We emphasise that
[
FT22
]
shows the convergence of the particle systems to the corresponding entropy solution, working directly at the level of the gradient-flow structures at the continuous-time scale (but with no analysis of the behavior of the discrete distances nor the time-discrete scheme).

Organisation of the paper

In Section
2
and
3

, whereas in Section

Notation

For
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
(resp.
\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
) the set of all signed, locally finite Radon measures (resp. probability measures) on
\Omega
. Also,

denote respectively the space of continuous, bounded, and compactly supported functions.

For a given sequence of measures

(i)

\nu_{n}\to\nu

vaguely
in

if
\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\to\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu
, for every
\varphi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

\nu_{n}\to\nu

narrowly
in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
if
\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\to\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu
, for every
\varphi\in C_{b}(\Omega)
.

We also adopt the following notation:

•

For
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, set
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}|x|^{s}:=\int|x|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
(
s
-th moment).

•

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
p\geq 1
, we write
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\ell_{p,N}}:=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}|x_{i}|^{p}
.

•

\mathscr{L}^{d}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on

,
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

For

,

.

2.
The discrete and continuous settings

In this section, we introduce the general framework of the problem, both in the continuous and discrete setting.

2.1.
Optimal transport in
\mathbb{R}
with nonlinear mobility

We start recalling the definition of solutions of the continuity equation (as in
[
DNS12
]
,
[
LM10
]
), namely the equation

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\partial_{x}\nu_{t}=0,

in
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
,
where
\mu_{t},\nu_{t}
are Borel families of measures in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
respectively, and satisfying
the equation in the sense of distributions, namely,
\forall\xi\in C_{c}^{1}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\xi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\xi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t=0\,.

For a given sequence of measures
\nu_{t}
we consider the corresponding measure
{\bm{\nu}}:=\int_{0}^{1}\nu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathcal{M}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
defined via disintegration

(2.2)

\displaystyle<{\bm{\nu}},\xi>=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}(x)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall\xi\in C_{c}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R}).

Similarly, we write
{\bm{\mu}}:=\int_{0}^{1}\mu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathscr{P}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 2.1

(Solution of continuity equation,
[
LM10
]
)
.

We denote by
\mathsf{CE}
the set of curves
\left(\mu_{t},\nu_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,1]}
such that

(1)

t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is vaguely-continuous in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

(2)

\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t}
is a Borel family of finite Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}
.

(3)

({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\nu}})
satisfies (
2.1
).

In this case we write in short

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\partial_{x}\nu_{t}=0
and

(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
, or
({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\nu}})\in\mathsf{CE}
.
Whenever we want to emphasis the dependence on the initial and final datum, we write
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.

We now fix the notation concerning the action functionals. See
[
LM10
, Section 2.3]
.

Definition 2.2

(Nonlinear mobility)
.

Given a maximal density
M>0
, a
nonlinear mobility
is a continuous concave function
m:[0,M]\to[0,+\infty)
such that
m(0)=0
; sometimes, if needed we extend the domain of
m
to
[0,+\infty)
letting
m(\rho)=-\infty
for
\rho>M
. We moreover define

\displaystyle\theta:[0,M]\to[0,+\infty)\,,\quad\theta(\rho):=\begin{cases}\frac{m(\rho)}{\rho}&\text{if}\ \rho>0,\\
{\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\downarrow 0}}\,\frac{m(\rho)}{\rho}&\text{if}\ \rho=0.\end{cases}

Notice that
\theta
is an (extended) continuous function in
[0,M]
; in this work, we often assume that
\theta
is also bounded. Note that by concavity of
m
, this is equivalent to the superdifferential of
m
being not empty in
\rho=0
.

Remark 2.3

(Monotonicity of
\theta
)
.

Note that by concavity of
m
, it follows that
\theta
is nonincreasing on
(0,M]
. Indeed, for
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}\leq M
, we have that

\displaystyle\theta(\rho_{2})-\theta(\rho_{1})=\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\Big[\big(m(\rho_{2})-m(\rho_{1})\big)-\frac{m(\rho_{1})}{\rho_{1}}(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\Big]\leq 0

by concavity of
m
on
[0,M]
and
m(0)=0
. In particular,
\sup\theta=\theta(0)
.

Definition 2.4

(Action functionals)
.

Fix a maximal density
M>0
and
p>1
. We then consider
\phi_{p,m}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
defined as

\displaystyle\phi_{p,m}(\rho,j)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{|j|^{p}}{m(\rho)^{p-1}},&\mathrm{if}\;\ m(\rho)>0,\\
0&\mathrm{if}\;\ m(\rho)=0=j,\\
+\infty,&\mathrm{if}\;\emph{either}\ m(\rho)=0\,,j\neq 0\emph{or}\rho>M\,,\end{cases}

where
m
is a nonlinear mobility with maximal density
M
. Note that
\phi_{p,m}
is a lower semicontinuous, nonnegative, proper, convex function, with
\mathrm{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\phi_{p,m}))=(0,M)\times\mathbb{R}
.
For every
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the

action functional

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Phi_{p,m}(\mu,\nu):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{p,m}(\rho,j)\,\mathrm{d}x&\text{if}\quad\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\ \,\mathrm{d}\nu=j\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Remark 2.5
.

As observed in
[
LM10
, Proposition 2.3]
, if
\mu
,
\nu
are such that
\Phi_{p,m}(\mu,\nu)<\infty
(in particular they are absolutely continuous w.r.t. d
x
), then
\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\rho\,\mathrm{d}x
with
0\leq\rho(\cdot)\leq M
.

Definition 2.6

(
m
-Wasserstein distances)
.

Let
\phi_{p,m}
,
\Phi_{p,m}
be as in Definition
2.1
.
For any
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
, we define

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}):=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\ :\ (\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\text{CE}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}
is only a pseudo-distance, given that it can assume the value
+\infty
. If
m(\rho)=\rho
, then we recover the usual
p
-Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{W}_{p}
.

Remark 2.7

(Time regularity of finite energy curves)
.

Curves of measures with finite energy enjoy good regularity properties in the time-variable, if
\theta
is bounded. Precisely, if
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
and

\displaystyle E:=\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\,.

Then
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is Lipschitz-continuous with respect to the
\mathbb{W}_{p}
distance on
\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, by the homogeneity of
\phi
in the second variable,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})^{p}\leq\|\theta\|_{\infty}\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})^{p}\leq\|\theta\|_{\infty}|t-s|^{p}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{h},\nu_{h})\,\mathrm{d}h\leq E\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\infty}|t-s|^{p}\,,

as claimed. In particular, by Prokhorov’s theorem, the family of probability measures
\left(\mu_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,1]}
is tight in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

2.1.1.
Auxiliary results

In this section we recall some well-known properties of the actions and the generalised distances. We refer to
[
DNS12
]
and
[
LM10
, Section 3&4]
for details. Let
\phi_{p,m}
be as in Definition
2.1
.

(I)

Lower semicontinuity
. For every
\left(\mu_{n}\right)_{n}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
,
\left(\nu_{n}\right)_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
vaguely converging to
\mu
,
\nu
respectively, then
\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})\geq\Phi_{p,m}(\mu,\nu)
.

(II)

Convolution
. Let
h\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
be a positive function with
\int h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. For
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
a curve of measures, we consider their natural extension on
\mathbb{R}
by setting

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=\mu_{0}\ \ \text{if}\ \ t<0\,,\quad\mu_{t}=\mu_{1}\ \ \text{if}\ \ t>1\,,\quad\nu_{t}=0\ \ \text{if}\ \ t\in(0,1)^{c}\,.

Let
({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\nu}})
as in (
2.2
); define
\hat{\bm{\mu}}:=h*{\bm{\mu}}
and
\hat{\bm{\nu}}:=h*{\bm{\nu}}
, where
h*{\bm{\nu}}
denotes the measure which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
with density
\hat{j}(t,x):=\int h(t-s,x-y)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\nu}}(s,y)
. Note that if
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
, then
(\hat{\mu}_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
as well. Moreover, by
\phi\geq 0
and
\phi(\cdot,0)=0
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\hat{\mu}_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

(III)

\mathbb{W}_{p,m}
-geodesics
. Fix
\mu_{0}
,
\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})<\infty
. Then there exists
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
such that
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t
. Furthermore,
\mu_{t}
is a constant speed geodesic for
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}
, i.e. it satisfies
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\mu_{s})=|t-s|\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, for all
s,t\in[0,1]
.

2.2.
The discrete transport problem

In this section we introduce the discrete setting. Recall that
M\in(0,+\infty)
is a fixed positive number. For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the (closed) cone
\mathcal{K}_{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{N}:=\left\{x=(x_{0},...,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\ :\ x_{i+1}-x_{i}\geq\frac{1}{NM}\,,\;\forall i\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

For simplicity, we omit the dependence on
M
in
\mathcal{K}_{N}
. We consistently adopt the following notation: for
x\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
, we denote by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Delta x_{i}:=x_{i+1}-x_{i},\quad R_{i}^{N}(x):=\frac{1}{N\Delta x_{i}}\leq M\,,

for
i=0,...,N-1
, and
\rho^{*}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
a nonnegative number. The definition of the density reconstruction for
i=N
admits no canonical definition (there is no particle in front of
x_{N}
), and several choice are possible. We define
R_{N}^{N}(x):=\rho_{N}^{*}(x)
, where
\rho_{N}^{*}:\mathcal{K}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a family of continuous functions satisfying

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\|\rho_{N}^{*}\|_{\infty}<M\,.

Remark 2.8

(Examples of
\rho_{N}^{*}
)
.

A simple choice is to set
\rho_{N}^{*}(x)\equiv\rho^{*}:=\arg\max\theta
, then the last particle motion is not influenced by the rest of the particles (
follow-the-leader
scheme, see e.g.
[
DFR15
]
). Another option could be to "look back" to the previous particle, i.e.
\rho_{N}(x)=R_{N-1}^{N}(x)
.

Definition 2.9

(Discrete distances)
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
p>1
,
M\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
\phi_{p,m}
be as in Definition
2.1
. We define the corresponding
discrete action functional

\Phi_{p,m}^{N}:\text{T}\mathcal{K}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x,v):=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(R_{i}^{N}(x))^{p-1}\phi_{p,m}(R_{i}^{N}(x),v_{i}^{N})=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\frac{|v_{i}|^{p}}{\theta(R_{i}^{N}(x))^{p-1}}\,,

for any
x\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
and
v\in\text{T}_{x}\mathcal{K}_{N}
. Here
\text{T}\mathcal{K}_{N}\approx\mathcal{K}_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
denotes the tangent bundle of the cone
\mathcal{K}_{N}
.
Furthermore, we define the
discrete
\phi_{p,m}
-distance
between
x,y\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
as

(2.8)

\displaystyle d_{p,m}^{N}(x,y)^{p}:=\inf_{x\in W^{1,p}(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(t),\dot{x}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\ :\ x(0)=x,\;x(1)=y\right\},

where
W^{1,p}(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})
denotes the space of
p
-Sobolev functions from
(0,1)
to
\mathcal{K}_{N}
.
Note that
d_{p,m}^{N}
is only a pseudo-distance, given that it can assume the value
+\infty
.

Remark 2.10
.

For the sake of simplicity, we omit the dependence of
\Phi_{p,m}^{N}
on
\rho^{*}
. As we are going to see, the exact value chosen plays no role in our main results, due to the fact we consider limits as
N\to\infty
. If
\rho^{*}(x)\geq M
(and thus
\theta(R_{N}^{N}(x))=0
), then
\phi_{p,m}(x,v)<\infty
forces
v_{N}=0
.

Example 2.11

(Riemannian case)
.

In the case
p=2
,
d_{2,m}^{N}
is a Riemannian distance induced by the Riemannian metric on the cone
\mathcal{K}_{N}
given by

(2.9)

\displaystyle g_{N}\left(v,w\right)_{x}:=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\frac{v_{i}w_{i}}{\theta(R_{i}^{N}(x))},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{K}_{N},\;\forall v,w\in\text{T}_{x}\mathcal{K}_{N}\,.

Now we prove the existence of geodesics in
\mathcal{K}_{N}
, which follows by standard compactness arguments, together with a careful analysis close to the singularity of
\Phi_{p,m}^{N}
.

Lemma 2.12

(Existence of discrete geodesics)
.

Let
\theta\in\emph{L}^{\infty}(0,M)
and
p>1
. For every
z,y\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
, there exists
x\in W^{1,p}(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})
such that
x(0)=z
,
x(1)=y
and

\displaystyle d_{p,m}^{N}(z,y)^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(t),\dot{x}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(s),\dot{x}(s))\,,\quad\forall s\in[0,1]\,.

Proof.

For
z,y\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
, pick a minimising sequence
\left(x^{n}\right)_{n}\subset W^{1,p}(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x^{n}(t),\dot{x}^{n}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t=d_{p,m}^{N}(z,y)^{p}\,,\quad x^{n}(0)=z\,,\;x^{n}(1)=y\,.

With no loss of generality, we assume
D:=\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x^{n},\dot{x}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
. In particular

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\dot{x}^{n}(t)\right\rVert_{\ell_{p,N}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq D\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p-1}<\infty\,.

In particular, up to (a non-relabeled) subsequence,
x^{n}\to x
weakly in
W^{1,p}(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})
and strongly in
C(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})
(by Sobolev embedding, thanks to
p>1
) as
n\to\infty
. To show that
x
is indeed a minimiser, we show the lower semicontinuity of the discrete energies with respect to such convergence.

First we assume that
\theta(M)>0
and in particular
\theta
is continuous and bounded from below. Now, since
R_{i}^{N}(x^{n})
is converging uniformly to
R_{i}^{N}(x)
, we also have that
\theta(R_{i}^{N}(x^{n}))^{1-p}
will converge uniformly to
\theta(R_{i}^{N}(x))^{1-p}
. Since
\dot{x}^{n}\rightharpoonup\dot{x}
weakly in
L^{p}
, we then can conclude, by classical semicontinuity arguments, that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\dot{x}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{p}}{\theta(R_{i}^{N}(x^{n}(t)))^{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\dot{x}_{i}(t)|^{p}}{\theta(R_{i}^{N}(x(t)))^{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Summing up over
i=0,\ldots,N
and then dividing by
N+1
we get the actual semicontinuity of the energy
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x^{n},\dot{x}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x,\dot{x})\,\mathrm{d}t
, hence
x
is a minimizer.

In order to deal with the general case we consider for
\lambda>1
a relaxed mobility
m^{\lambda}(\rho)=\lambda m(\rho/\lambda)
. Of course we have
\theta^{\lambda}(\rho)=\theta(\rho/\lambda)
. Using the monotonicity of
\theta
we have that
\Phi_{p,m^{\lambda}}^{N}(x^{n},\dot{x}^{n})\leq\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x^{n},\dot{x}^{n})
. Moreover, since
\theta^{\lambda}(M)=\theta(M/\lambda)>0
we can apply the previous result and we can get

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x^{n},\dot{x}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m^{\lambda}}^{N}(x^{n},\dot{x}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m^{\lambda}}^{N}(x,\dot{x})\,\mathrm{d}t.

By monotone convergence we then conclude since
\Phi_{p,m^{\lambda}}^{N}(x,\dot{x})\uparrow\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x,\dot{x})
.

The fact that
s\mapsto\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(s),\dot{x}(s))
is constant along discrete geodesics follows by classical arguments and is consequence of the
p
-homogeneity of
\phi
. Let us argue by contradiction that it is not true; in particular by Jensen we have
\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(s),\dot{x}(s))\,ds>\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(s),\dot{x}(s))^{1/p}\,ds\right)^{p}
. Now let us consider any
C^{1}
increasing reparametrization
\xi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, and let
y(t)=x(\xi(t))
; we have of course
y(0)=0
and
y(1)=1
. Moreover

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(y(t),\dot{y}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(\xi(t)),\xi^{\prime}(t)\dot{x}(\xi(t)))\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(\xi(t)),\xi^{\prime}(t)\dot{x}(\xi(t)))^{1/p}\,dt\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime}(t)\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(\xi(t)),\dot{x}(\xi(t)))^{1/p}\,dt\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(s),\dot{x}(s))^{1/p}\,dt\right)^{p}.

We can have equality as long as
\xi^{\prime}(\xi^{-1}(s))=(\xi^{-1})^{\prime}(s)
is proportional to
\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(s),\dot{x}(s))^{1/p}
. While this is not guaranteed to happen for
\xi\in C^{1}
we can use a sufficiently good approximation in order to get
\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(y(t),\dot{y}(t))\,dt<\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x(s),\dot{x}(s))\,ds
, which is a contradiction.

∎

In order to compare the discrete and the continuous setting, we introduce suitable embedding maps from the cones
\mathcal{K}_{N}
to the space of continuous measures
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

Embedding via piecewise-constant measures.
For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the map

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{PC}^{N}:\mathcal{K}_{N}\to\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})\,,\quad\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}R_{i}^{N}(x)\mathbbm{1}_{\left[x_{i},x_{i+1}\right]}(\cdot)\mathscr{L}^{1}\,,

where
\mathscr{L}^{1}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Note that, for every
x\in\mathcal{K}_{N}
, the measures
\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x)
are absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{1}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x)
charges every interval
[x_{i},x_{i+1}]
with equal mass
\frac{1}{N}
. We shall use the shorthand notation
\mathcal{PC}^{N}:=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(\mathcal{K}_{N})\subset\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

Embedding via empirical measures.
For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the map

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{N}:\mathcal{K}_{N}\to\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})\,,\quad\mathcal{E}^{N}(x)=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}\,.

Note that the image of
\mathcal{E}^{N}
consists of atomic measures over the real line. In particular,
\mathcal{E}^{N}(x)
is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure. We adopt the shorthand notation
\mathcal{E}^{N}:=\mathcal{E}^{N}(\mathcal{K}_{N})\subset\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

It’s easy to see that
\mathcal{E}^{N}
and
\mathcal{PC}^{N}

On one hand, the

On the other hand, the

Depending on the choice of the embedding, we can define the corresponding embedded distance at the continuous level.

Definition 2.13

(Discrete distances - embedding)

Fix

4.
Approximation of finite-energy solutions of
\mathsf{CE}

In this chapter we provide some approximation results (Proposition
4.2
) for solutions to the continuity equation with bounded action. These techniques allow us to obtain "good" recovery sequences for
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}
, which turn out to be crucial in the proof of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
. We start recalling some basic notions.

Definition 4.1

(Pseudo-inverse)
.

Fix
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
. The
pseudo-inverse
or
quantile function

X_{\mu}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
of
\mu
is the non-decreasing, right-continuous map given by

\displaystyle X_{\mu}(z)=\inf\left\{a\in\mathbb{R}\ :\ \mu(-\infty,a)>z\right\}\,.

Remark 4.2

(Law of the pseudo-inverse)
.

It is easy to check that, by construction, the law of
X_{\mu}
coincides with
\mu
itself, i.e.
(X_{\mu})_{\#}\mathscr{L}^{1}=\mu
. In fact, it is the unique right continuous monotone increasing map from
(0,1)
to
\mathbb{R}
with such property.

Remark 4.3

(Wasserstein distance on the real line)
.

A remarkable property of the one-dimensional optimal transport problem is the possibility of computing the
p
-Wasserstein distance between two probability measures via the
\text{L}^{p}
-distance of the corresponding pseudo-inverses. Precisely, for
p\geq 1
, and every
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\|X_{\mu}-X_{\nu}\|_{\text{L}^{p}(0,1)}\,.

Remark 4.4

(Monotonocity and continuity of the pseudo-inverse)
.

Whenever
\mu
does not charge single points,
X_{\mu}
is an increasing map (and in particular, injective). In general, even for absolute continuous measures (with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{1}
) with smooth densities, the corresponding pseudo-inverse might fail to be continuous. If the support of
\mu
is connected, then
X_{\mu}
is continuous.

4.1.
Reduction to compact support

In this section, we discuss a procedure to reduce measures to compactly supported ones, and how this effects the value of the associated
\phi_{p,m}
-energies.

Definition 4.5

(Compactification of a measure)
.

Fix
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
an atomless probability measure,
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, we define the measure
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\nu|\mu)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
as

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\nu|\mu):=\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}\nu\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]\,,\quad I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]:=\Big[X_{\mu}(\varepsilon),X_{\mu}(1-\varepsilon)\Big]\,.

We use the shorthand notation
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu:=\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu|\mu)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

The next lemma provides a control of the error in applying the operator
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.6

(Compactification error)
.

Fix
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
an atomless probability measure,
\varepsilon>0
. Then for every
q\leq p
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\emph{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\mu])^{p-q}\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu,\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu)^{q}\leq C\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x-x_{\mu}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,,

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
p
, where
x_{\mu}:=X_{\mu}\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)
is the median of
\mu
. If
q=p
, one can take
C=1
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
x_{\mu}=0
, otherwise we change variables
x\mapsto x-x_{\mu}
. By density arguments, we can further assume that
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{1}
.
We split the proof in two steps.

Step 1: the case
q=p
.
Pick any admissible map
\tilde{T}
between
\mu\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}
and
2\varepsilon\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu
(i.e.
\tilde{T}_{\#}(\mu\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c})=2\varepsilon\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu
). Moreover, thanks to
x_{\mu}=0
, we can also assume that
\tilde{T}(x)x\geq 0
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, we define the map

\displaystyle T:\mathbb{R}\to I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]\,,\quad T(x)=\begin{cases}x,&\text{if }x\in I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]\,,\\
\tilde{T}(x),&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Note that
T
is an admissible map between
\mu
and
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu
. Indeed for every measurable map
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\displaystyle\int\varphi(T(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}\varphi(\tilde{T}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=(1-2\varepsilon)\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu+2\varepsilon\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu=\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu\,.

Computing the transport cost with respect to
|\cdot|^{p}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle W_{p}^{p}(\mu,\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu)\leq\int|x-T(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x-\tilde{T}(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,,

where at last we used
\tilde{T}(x)x\geq 0
, which concludes the proof of (
4.3
).

Step 2:
q<p
. Fix
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
q<p
. Then applying the first step of the proof and using that
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R})
we deduce

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu,\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu)^{q}\leq\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,.

Consider any transport map
S:I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}\to I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}
satisfying

(4.5)

\displaystyle S_{\#}(\mu\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c})=\mu\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}\quad\text{and}\quad S(x)x\leq 0\,,\quad x\in I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}\,.

One can for example consider any map that sends the
\varepsilon
mass of
\mu
which lives in
I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}\cap\{x\leq 0\}
into
I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}\cap\{x\geq 0\}
and viceversa. In particular, we observe that
|x|\leq|x-S(x)|
, for
x\in I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}
. Together with (
4.4
), this yields

\displaystyle\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\mu])^{p-q}\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu,\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\mu])^{p-q}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x-S(x)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}\bigg(\frac{\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\mu])}{|x-S(x)|}\bigg)^{p-q}|x-S(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x-S(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,,

where at last we used that for
x\in I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}
we have
|x-S(x)|\geq\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\mu])
. By (
4.5
)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x-S(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}\big(|x|^{p}+|S(x)|^{p}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=2^{p}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,,

we conclude the proof.
∎

The operator
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
is constructed in such a way to preserve solutions to
\mathsf{CE}
.

Lemma 4.7

(Compactification of solutions to
\mathsf{CE}
)
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
with
C^{1}
(space-time) densities
(\rho_{t},j_{t})_{t}
with respect to
\mathscr{L}^{1}
. Assume also that
\rho_{t}>0
for every
t\in[0,1]
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, consider the operator
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
as defined in Definition
4.1
.
Then

\big(\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu_{t},\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\nu_{t}|\mu_{t})\big)\in\mathsf{CE}\,.

Proof.

Fix any
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. For the sake of simplicity, set
a_{t}:=X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon)
,
b_{t}:=X_{\mu_{t}}(1-\varepsilon)
. Firstly, for
t\in(0,1)
, set
F_{\mu}(x):=\mu(-\infty,x)
and note that by definition of quantile function one has that

\displaystyle F_{\mu_{t}}\big(X_{\mu_{t}}(z)\big)=z\,,\quad\forall z\in(0,1)\,.

Using that
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
, it is readily verified that
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}F_{\mu_{t}}(x)=-j_{t}(x)
, for every
t\in[0,1]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that
(t,x)\mapsto F_{\mu_{t}}(x)
is
C^{1}
in space and time. By applying the implicit function theorem, we infer that
t\mapsto X_{\mu_{t}}(z)
is differentiable for every
z\in(0,1)
and it holds

(4.6)

\displaystyle-j_{t}\big(X_{\mu_{t}}(z)\big)+\rho_{t}\big(X_{\mu_{t}}(z)\big)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}X_{\mu_{t}}(z)=0\,.

Hence,
a_{t}
,
b_{t}
both solves the differential equation
\rho_{t}(c_{t})\dot{c}_{t}=j_{t}(c_{t})
.
Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle(1-2\varepsilon)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]}\varphi\dot{\rho}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x+\dot{b}_{t}\varphi(b_{t})\rho_{t}(b_{t})-\dot{a}_{t}\varphi(a_{t})\rho_{t}(a_{t})

\displaystyle=-\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]}\varphi\nabla\cdot j_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x+\varphi j_{t}\big|_{\partial I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]}

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]}\nabla\varphi\cdot j_{t}=(1-2\varepsilon)\int\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\nu_{t}|\mu_{t})\,.\qed

In view of the error estimate of Lemma
4.1
, we also prove the following result.

Lemma 4.8

(Uniform bounds and stability properties)
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
,
\left(\mu_{n}\right)_{n}\subset\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
a sequence of atomless measures.

(i)

If
\left(\mu_{n}\right)_{n}
is tight in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
, then for
s\in(0,1)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\emph{\text{diam}}(I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{n}])<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big|X_{\mu_{n}}(s)\big|<\infty\,.

(ii)

For every
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
atomless measures, it holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\nu]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\right|^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Big\|X_{\mu}-X_{\nu}\Big\|_{\emph{L}^{p}([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]^{c})}.

In particular, if
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in
\mathbb{W}_{p}
, then

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{n}]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,.

Proof.

(
i
): We proceed by contradiction: assume that (up to subsequence)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{n}])=+\infty\,.

By tightness, we know that there exists a compact set
K_{\varepsilon}\Subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\sup_{n}\mu_{n}(K_{\varepsilon}^{c})<\varepsilon
. On the other hand, by definition of
I_{\varepsilon}
, we know that
I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{n}]^{c}
is a disjoint union of unbounded intervals
I_{n}^{-}
and
I_{n}^{+}
, each of
\mu_{n}
-mass equals to
\varepsilon
. From (
4.9
), we deduce that, for
n
sufficiently big, either
I_{n}^{-}
or
I_{n}^{+}
must be subsets of
K_{\varepsilon}^{c}
, which gives us the contradiction.
A similar argument shwos the uniform bound on the quantiles.

(
ii
): Fix
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
. By definition of pseudo-inverse we have

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\Big|X_{\mu}(z)\Big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\Big|X_{\mu}(z)\Big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z\,.

The assumption on the
\mathbb{W}_{p}
convergence on
\mu_{n}
is equivalent to the fact that
X_{\mu_{n}}\to X_{\mu}
in
\text{L}^{p}(0,1)
(see Remark
4
), and thus
(ii)
follows.
∎

4.2.
The approximation procedure

We introduce the following

Definition 4.9

(Regular curves)
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
be a solution of the continuity equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})
is a
regular curve
if the following properties hold

i)

for all
t\in[0,1]
,
\mu_{t}
and
\nu_{t}
have compact and connected support,
\mu_{t},\nu_{t}\ll\mathscr{L}^{1}
and their respective densities densities
\rho_{t}
,
j_{t}
are
C^{\infty}
in their support

ii)

there exist a compact set
K
and two constants
0<l\leq\tilde{M}<M
such that

\supp(\rho_{t}),\,\supp(j_{t})\subset K\quad\text{and}\quad l<\rho_{t}(x)<\tilde{M}\quad\text{for all $x\in\supp(\rho_{t})$ and $t\in[0,1]$.}

We are finally ready to state and prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.10

(Approximation with regular curves)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
M\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\phi_{p,m}
be as in Definition
2.1
. Let

(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
satisfying

(4.10)

\displaystyle E:=\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\,,\quad\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\rho_{t}\|_{\emph{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=:\bar{M}<M\,\,.

Then, for every
\eta>0
, there exists
(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\tilde{\nu}_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}

such that

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\tilde{\nu}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\eta\,.

(ii)

(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\tilde{\nu}_{t})
are regular curves in the sense of Definition
4.2
, for some compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}
and
l>0
depending only on
\eta
and
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
, while
\tilde{M}
only depends on
\bar{M}
.

(iii)

For
q\leq p
and
t\in[0,1]
, we have that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\Big(\emph{diam}(\supp(\tilde{\mu}_{t}))^{p-q}\vee 1\Big)\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},\tilde{\mu}_{t})^{q}\,,\int_{\supp(\tilde{\mu}_{t})^{c}}(|x|^{p}+1)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\right\}\leq\eta\,.

Proof.

We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1: Space-time regularisation
. We extend
\mu_{t},\nu_{t}
as
\mu_{t}=\mu_{0}
for
t<0
,
\mu_{t}=\mu_{1}
for
t>1
, and
\nu_{t}=0
for
t\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,+\infty)
. Given two smooth functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1\,,\quad\int|x|^{p}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\,,\quad f>0\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}\,,

and the same for
g
. We define for
\tau>0
the rescaled kernel on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
by setting
h_{\tau}(t,x):=\tau^{-2}f(\tau^{-1}t)g(\tau^{-1}x)
, for
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. Note that
h_{\tau}
has integral
1
and full support, for every
\tau>0
. We then consider
({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\nu}})
as in (
2.2
) and define

(4.12)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\mu}}:=h_{\tau}*{\bm{\mu}}\,,\quad\hat{\bm{\nu}}:=h_{\tau}*{\bm{\nu}}\,,

where the convolution has to be intended as in Section
2.1.1
(
II
). In particular,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\hat{\mu}_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Moreover,
\hat{\bm{\mu}}
,
\hat{\bm{\nu}}
have densities
\hat{\rho}_{\cdot}(\cdot),\hat{j}_{\cdot}(\cdot)\in C^{\infty}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})
. Thanks to the positivity of
h_{\tau}
and
\int h_{\tau}=1
, we have that

\hat{\rho}_{t}(x)\in(0,\bar{M})
,
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\in[0,1]
.

Step 2: Reduction to compact supports
. Pick
\bar{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(\bar{M})\in(0,1)
so that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\tilde{M}:=(1-2\bar{\varepsilon})^{-1}\bar{M}<M\,.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
to be suitably chosen and
t\in[0,1]
, we define

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{t}:=\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\hat{\mu}_{t}\,,\quad\tilde{\nu}_{t}:=\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\hat{\nu}_{t}|\hat{\mu}_{t})\,,

where
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
is defined in Definition
4.1
. We denote by
\tilde{\rho}_{t}
,
\tilde{j}_{t}
the corresponding densities.

Step 3: Energy estimates
. Thanks to Lemma
4.1
and the fact that
(\hat{\mu}_{t},\hat{\nu}_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
, we know that
(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\tilde{\nu}_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
.
By the convexity and homogeneity of
\phi_{p,m}
, we have that

\displaystyle\phi_{p,m}(\tilde{\rho}_{t},\tilde{j}_{t})=\frac{1}{(1-2\varepsilon)^{p}}\phi_{p,m}\Big(\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}\hat{\rho}_{t},\hat{j}_{t}\Big)\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon\bar{C}}{(1-2\varepsilon)^{p}}\phi_{p,m}(\hat{\rho}_{t},\hat{j}_{t})\,,

for some
\bar{C}=\bar{C}(\bar{M},\phi_{p,m})<\infty
depending only on
\bar{M}
and
\phi_{p,m}
, where at last we used Lemma
7
, thanks to (
4.14
). In particular, by (
4.10
), (
4.13
), and the latter bound,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\tilde{\nu}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon\bar{C}}{(1-2\varepsilon)^{p}}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{C}E\varepsilon\,.

The upper bound (
i
) then follows directly by choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

Step 4: Regularity of the approximation
. By construction and (
4.14
), we have that

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\big\{t\mapsto\tilde{\rho}_{t},\,\tilde{j}_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\supp(\tilde{\mu}_{t}))\big\}\in C^{\infty}([0,1])\,,\quad\supp\tilde{\mu}_{t}=I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}]\subset\mathbb{R}\,,\\
0<l_{\tau}\leq\tilde{\rho}_{s}(x)\leq\tilde{M}<M\,,\quad\forall x\in\supp(\tilde{\mu}_{s})\,,\end{gathered}

for every
s\in[0,1]
, for some
l_{\tau}>0
depending only on
\tau
,
f
, and
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
(but not on
\varepsilon
, since
(1-2\varepsilon)^{-1}>1
). Recall the definition of
I_{\varepsilon}[\cdot]
as given in (
4.2
).
In particular, (
ii
) follows with a compact set
K=K_{\tau,\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}
only depending on
\varepsilon
,
\tau
, and
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
.

Step 5: Estimate of the distance.
We are left with the proof of (
iii
). By triangle inequality, we have that

\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},\tilde{\mu}_{t})\leq\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t})+\mathbb{W}_{q}(\hat{\mu}_{t},\tilde{\mu}_{t})\,.

Set
f_{\tau}:=\tau^{-1}f(\tau^{-1}\cdot)
. By Remark
2.1
with
q\leq p
and (
4.11
), for every
s,t\in[0,1]
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},f_{\tau}*\mu_{s})\leq\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},\mu_{s})+\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{s},f_{\tau}*\mu_{s})\leq E^{\prime}|t-s|+\tau(\mathbb{E}_{f}|x|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,

for some
E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}(E,{\bm{\mu}},\theta)
, where the second inequality follows using the admissible transport plan
\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y):=f_{\tau}(y-x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}(x)
.
Using the convexity of
\mathbb{W}_{q}
and the latter bound, by (
4.11
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t})\leq\int g_{\tau}(t-s)\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},f_{\tau}*\mu_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C\tau\,,

for some
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
depending on
f
,
g
, and
E^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we get that

\displaystyle\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}])^{p-q}\mathbb{W}_{q}(\hat{\mu}_{t},\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}\hat{\mu}_{t})^{q}\leq\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}]^{c}}|x-x_{\hat{\mu}_{t}}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mu}_{t}(x)\,.

Collecting the various estimates, we end up with

\displaystyle\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}])^{p-q}\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},\tilde{\mu}_{t})^{q}\leq\bigg(\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}])^{\frac{p}{q}-1}C\tau+\left(\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}]^{c}}|x-x_{\hat{\mu}_{t}}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mu}_{t}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg)^{q}\,.

Employing Lemma
4.1
, together with Remark
2.1
, and taking the limit as
\tau\to 0
, for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in[0,1]
we find that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\to 0}\text{diam}(I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}])^{p-q}\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{t},\tilde{\mu}_{t})^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\left(\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)+\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\Big|X_{\mu_{t}}(z)\Big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{1-\varepsilon}^{1}\Big|X_{\mu_{t}}(z)\Big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z+\varepsilon\right)\,,

for some
C^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
independent of
\varepsilon
and
t
. The latter term is infinitesimal as
\varepsilon\to 0
uniformly in time, thanks to Remark
2.1
and (
4.1
). Therefore, we reach any threshold
\eta>0
if we suitably choose first
\tau
and then
\varepsilon
small enough.

Step 5: Estimate of the tail.
We are left to prove we can choose
\tau,\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\int_{\supp(\tilde{\mu}_{t})^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\leq\eta\,,

for any given threshold
\eta>0
. We claim that
I_{2\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]\subset I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}]
, for
\tau
sufficiently small. This would allow us to conclude, as in the proof of Step 4, that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\int_{I_{2\varepsilon}[\mu_{t}]^{c}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. More precisely, we shall prove that

(4.18)

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon+\delta_{\tau}}[\mu_{t}]\subset I_{\varepsilon}[\hat{\mu}_{t}],\quad\forall t\in[0,1],

where
\delta_{\tau}
can be explicitly chosen depending only on
\tau
,
p
,
M
, and the curve
{\bm{\mu}}
as

\displaystyle\delta_{\tau}:=\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t})^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\Big)\Big((p+1)M^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}.

Using the fact that
\delta_{\tau}\to 0
as
\tau\to 0
, the claim is proved.
To prove (
4.18
), suppose that
\delta>0
is such that

X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+\delta)<X_{\hat{\mu}_{t}}(\varepsilon)
, for some
t\in[0,1]
. Using the fact that
\rho_{t}\leq M
, we obtain

\displaystyle X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+\delta)>X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+z)+M^{-1}(\delta-z),\quad\forall z\in[0,\delta].

Hence, by assumption on
\delta
, for
z\in[0,\delta]
we have that

X_{\hat{\mu}_{t}}(\varepsilon+z)-X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+z)>X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+\delta)-X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+z)>M^{-1}(\delta-z)\,.

By Remark
4
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t})^{p}\geq\int_{0}^{\delta}\Big|X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+z)-X_{\hat{\mu}_{t}}(\varepsilon+z)\Big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z>\delta^{p+1}(p+1)^{-1}M^{-p},

which shows that
\delta<\delta_{\tau}
. This shows that
X_{\mu_{t}}(\varepsilon+\delta_{\tau})\geq X_{\hat{\mu}_{t}}(\varepsilon)
. In a similar way, one can prove that
X_{\mu_{t}}(1-\varepsilon-\delta_{\tau})\leq X_{\hat{\mu}_{t}}(1-\varepsilon)
, and thus proving (
4.18
).
∎

5.
Proof of the main result:
\Gamma
-convergence.

In this section we prove Theorem
3.1
.
We start showing that for any sequence
\left(x^{N}\in\mathcal{K}^{N}\right)_{N}
, the weak limits of the associated empirical measures coincide with the weak limits of the corresponding piecewise-constant measures.

Lemma 5.1

(Weak limits of empirical and piecewise-constant measures)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\left(x^{N}\in\mathcal{K}_{N}\right)_{N}
. Set

\hat{\mu}^{N}:=\mathcal{E}^{N}(x^{N})
,

\mu^{N}:=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x^{N})
, and fix
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(R)
.

(i)

\hat{\mu}^{N}\to\mu
vaguely if and only if
\mu^{N}\to\mu
vaguely.

(ii)

The
p
th-moments of
\hat{\mu}^{N}
are equi-integrable if and only if the
p
th-moments of
\mu^{N}
are equi-integrable.

(iii)

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\hat{\mu}^{N},\mu)\to 0
if and only if
\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu^{N},\mu)\to 0
.

Proof.

Note that (
iii
) is a direct consequence of (
i
) and (
ii
). For the sake of simplicity, we omit the dependence on
N
and write
x_{i}=x_{i}^{N}
.

(
i
): It suffices to prove that
\hat{\mu}^{N}-\mu^{N}\to 0
vaguely.
In fact, we claim that

\hat{\mu}^{N}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\delta_{x_{i}}\to 0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\delta_{x_{i}}-\mu^{N}\to 0\quad\text{vaguely}\,.

For simplicity of notation, we denote
\bar{\mu}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\delta_{x_{i}}
(note that in general
\bar{\mu}^{N}
does not coincide with
\hat{\mu}^{N-1}
because the set of particles may differ).
For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote by
\supp\varphi
its (compact) support.
On one hand

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\hat{\mu}^{N}-\bar{\mu}^{N})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\varphi(x_{i})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\varphi(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N(N+1)}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}|\varphi(x_{i})|+\frac{\|\varphi(x_{N})\|}{N+1}\leq\frac{2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}{N+1}\to 0\,.

On the other side,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\bar{\mu}^{N}-\mu^{N})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left|\varphi(x_{i})-\fint_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}}{N}\text{diam(spt}\varphi)\to 0\,.

(
ii
): Let us first assume that the
p
th-moments of
\hat{\mu}^{N}
are equi-integrable, namely for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
L_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mu}^{N}(x)<\varepsilon.

We set

i_{\ell}:=\min\{i:x_{i}\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]\}
,
i_{r}:=\max\{i:x_{i}\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]\}.

In particular

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N+1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\ell}-1}|x_{i}|^{p}+\sum_{i=i_{r}+1}^{N}|x_{i}|^{p}\right)<\varepsilon,

from which, by monotonicity of the function
|\cdot|^{p}
separately in
(\infty,-L_{\varepsilon})
and
(L_{\varepsilon},\infty)
, for
N
big enough we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{N}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\ell}-1}\fint_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{i=i_{r}}^{N-1}\fint_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\ell}-1}|x_{i}|^{p}+\sum_{i=i_{r}}^{N-1}|x_{i+1}|^{p}\right)\leq 2\varepsilon\,,

thus providing the equi-integrability of the
p
th-moments of
\mu^{N}
.

Viceversa: assume that for
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
L_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{N}<\varepsilon.

Observe that, calling again
i_{\ell},i_{r}
the first and the last particle inside
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
respectively, for
N
big enough we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[x_{i_{\ell}},x_{i_{r}}]}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{N}+\int_{[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]\setminus[x_{i_{\ell}},x_{i_{r}}]}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{N}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i_{\ell}-1}}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{x_{i_{\ell}}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\frac{1}{\Delta x_{i_{r}}}\int^{L_{\varepsilon}}_{x_{i_{r}}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq\varepsilon+\frac{2|L_{\varepsilon}|^{p}}{N}\leq 2\varepsilon\,.

Therefore

(5.1)

\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\ell}-1}\fint_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}(-x)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{i=i_{r}}^{N-1}\fint_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}x^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)<2\varepsilon.

Since
|x_{i_{\ell}-1}|\leq|x_{i_{\ell}-2}|
and
a^{p+1}-b^{p+1}\geq(a-b)a^{p}
for
0<b<a
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mu}^{N}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\ell}-2}|x_{i}|^{p}+\sum_{i=i_{r}}^{N-1}|x_{i+1}|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2(p+1)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\ell}-1}\fint_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}(-x)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{i=i_{r}}^{N-1}\fint_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}x^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,.

Hence, by (
5.1
) we conclude the equi-integrability of the
p
-moments of
\hat{\mu}^{N}
.
∎

5.1.
Proof of the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality

In the first result, we show the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality for regular curves, in the sense of Definition
4.2
, and we conclude using the approximation result provided by Proposition
4.2
.
We emphasise that, for regular curves, we are able to construct a recovery sequence satisfying a discrete version of Definition
4.2
. In principle, this is not necessary to prove Theorem
3.1
, nonetheless it plays a crucial role in Section
6
for the convergence of the JKO-schemes. Throughout the whole section, we fix
\phi_{p,m}
as in Definition
2.1
, with
1<p<\infty
. Moreover, we denote by
\mathbb{W}_{\infty}
the distance given by
\mathbb{W}_{\infty}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}):=\|X_{\mu_{0}}-X_{\mu_{1}}\|_{\infty}
, for
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
compactly supported.

Proposition 5.2

(Existence of (regular) recovery sequences for regular curves)
.

Let
(\tilde{\bm{\mu}},\tilde{\bm{\nu}})\in\mathsf{CE}
be regular curves in the sense of Definition
4.2
for some compact set
K
and constants
0<l\leq\tilde{M}<M
.
Then there exist discrete curves
\left(\tilde{x}^{N}\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathcal{K}_{N})\right)_{N}
such that

(i)

the measures

\displaystyle\mathcal{PC}^{N}(\tilde{x}^{N}(k))
(and hence
\mathcal{E}^{N}(\tilde{x}^{N}(k))
as well, by Lemma
5
) converge to

\tilde{\mu}_{k}
in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
as
N\to\infty
, for
k=0,1
, for every
q\in(1,+\infty]
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}\big(\tilde{x}^{N}(t),\dot{\tilde{x}}^{N}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\tilde{\mu}_{0},\tilde{\mu}_{1})^{p}
.

(iii)

The following regularity properties holds: for
i=0,\dots,N
,
t\in[0,1]
,
N\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle l\leq\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}(\tilde{x}^{N}(t))\leq\tilde{M},\quad\supp\mathcal{PC}^{N}\big(\tilde{x}^{N}(t)\big)=\supp(\tilde{\mu}_{t})\subset K\,.

Proof.

The main idea is to consider the pseudo-inverse associated with the regular curve
\tilde{\bm{\mu}}
and discretise them in space on a scale
1/N
.

Step 1: Discretisation of
(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\tilde{\nu}_{t})
. Denote by
\tilde{v}_{t}=\tilde{j}_{t}/\tilde{\rho}_{t}
the associated velocity field, and by
I_{t}
its compact and connected support. By construction, we have that
\big\{t\mapsto\tilde{v}_{t}\in C^{\infty}(I_{t})\big\}\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
. In particular, the curve
\tilde{X}_{t}:=X_{\tilde{\mu}_{t}}
solves the differential equation (see (
4.6
))

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{X}}_{t}(z)=\tilde{v}_{t}(\tilde{X}_{t}(z))\,,\quad\tilde{X}_{0}:=X_{\tilde{\mu}_{0}},\quad\tilde{X}_{0}\in C^{1}([0,1])\,,

where the latter regularity follows from the continuity of (the density of)
\mu_{0}
and the fact that
I_{t}
is compact and connected.
Moreover, by the homogeneity of
\phi_{p,m}
, we have that for
t\in[0,1]

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\tilde{\rho}_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t})^{-1}\phi_{p,m}(\tilde{\rho}_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{j}_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{p,m}(\tilde{\rho}_{t},\tilde{j}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}x

We define for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
the discrete curve
\tilde{x}^{N}(t)
as

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{i}^{N}(t):=\tilde{X}_{t}\left(\frac{i}{N}\right),\quad t\in[0,1],\;i\in\{0,...,N\}\,,

and consider the corresponding measures
\tilde{\mu}_{t}^{N}=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(\tilde{x}^{N}(t))\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
with densities

(5.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{t}^{N}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{\left[\tilde{x}_{i}^{N},\tilde{x}_{i+1}^{N}\right)}(x)\,,\quad\tilde{R}_{j}^{N}:=R_{j}^{N}(\tilde{x}^{N})\,,

for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
j=0,\dots,N-1
.
Note that the pseudo-inverse of
\tilde{\mu}_{t}^{N}
is nothing but the piecewise affine interpolation of the
\tilde{X}_{t}
on
[0,1]
.
Thus for
i=0,\dots,N-1

\tilde{X}_{\tilde{\mu}_{t}^{N}}(z)=(Nz-i)\tilde{X}_{t}\left(\frac{i+1}{N}\right)+(i+1-Nz)\tilde{X}_{t}\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)\qquad\text{for }z\in\left[\frac{i}{N},\frac{i+1}{N}\right].

From the continuity of
\tilde{X}_{t}
, it follows that

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\big\|\tilde{X}_{t}-\tilde{X}_{\tilde{\mu}_{t}^{N}}\big\|_{\text{L}^{\infty}(0,1)}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
,
which shows (
i
) (see also Remark
4
).
By construction, for any
t\in[0,1]
the
\supp\tilde{\mu}_{t}^{N}=\supp\tilde{\mu}_{t}
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, in particular
\supp\tilde{\mu}_{t}^{N}\subset K
and also
\tilde{x}_{i}^{N}(t)\in K
for every
i=0,\dots,N
. Also, being
l\leq\tilde{\rho}_{t}\leq\tilde{M}
, it is immediate to see that
l\leq\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}\leq\tilde{M}
, thus proving (
iii
).

Step 2: Energy estimates
. Using that
(\tilde{X}_{t})_{\#}\,\mathrm{d}z=\tilde{\mu}_{t}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\fint_{\frac{i}{N}}^{\frac{i+1}{N}}\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}(\tilde{\rho}_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t})^{-1}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=N\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}\int_{\tilde{X}_{t}(\frac{i}{N})}^{\tilde{X}_{t}(\frac{i+1}{N})}\tilde{\rho}_{t}^{-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{t}(x)=1\,,

by the very definition of
\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}
. This means that the measures
\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{t}^{N,i}:=N\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}(\tilde{\rho}_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t})^{-1}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\in\mathscr{P}\big(\big[\frac{i}{N},\frac{i+1}{N}\big]\big)
.
By Jensen’s inequality and (
5.2
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{p,m}(\tilde{\rho}_{t},\tilde{j}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(\tilde{R}_{i}^{N})^{-1}\phi_{p,m}\bigg(\int_{\frac{i}{N}}^{\frac{i+1}{N}}(\tilde{\rho}_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{j}_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{t}^{N,i}(z)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(\tilde{R}_{i}^{N})^{-1}\phi_{p,m}\bigg(\tilde{R}_{i}^{N},\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}\fint_{\frac{i}{N}}^{\frac{i+1}{N}}v_{t}\circ\tilde{X}_{t}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(\tilde{R}_{i}^{N})^{p-1}\phi_{p,m}\bigg(\tilde{R}_{i}^{N},\fint_{\frac{i}{N}}^{\frac{i+1}{N}}\partial_{t}\tilde{X}_{t}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)\,,

where at last we used the
p
-homogeneity of
\phi_{p,m}
with respect to the second variable. Moreover, by
\tilde{X}\in C^{1}([0,1]\times[0,1])
,
\text{Im}(\tilde{X}_{t})\subset\supp\tilde{\rho}_{t}
, and the boundedness of
\tilde{v}
,

\displaystyle\left\{(\tilde{R}_{i}^{N}\big(\tilde{x}_{t}^{N}\big),\dot{\tilde{X}}_{t}(z))\ :\ t>0\,,\;z\in[0,1]\,,\;N\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subset[l,\tilde{M}]\times[0,\|\tilde{v}\|_{\infty}]\,=K

It is clear that
\phi_{p,m}
is Lipschitz in
K
, therefore, using (
2.7
), (
5.1
) and (
2.6
), we get that there exists a constant
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
which only depends on
l
,
\tilde{M}
,
p
,
\theta
, and
\rho^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\tilde{\rho}_{t},\tilde{j}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(\tilde{x}^{N},\dot{\tilde{x}}^{N})\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{C}{N}\Big(\|\tilde{X}\|_{C^{2}}+\|\tilde{X}\|_{C^{1}}^{p}\Big)\,.

Letting
N\to+\infty
we obtain (
ii
).
∎

We are finally ready to prove the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality in Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
,
\Gamma
-
\limsup
.

Fix
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})<+\infty
. Let
({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\nu}})\in\mathsf{CE}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
be a
\mathbb{W}_{p,m}
-geodesic between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
(see (
III
)).
For every
\eta>0
, we apply Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
5.1
, and find regular curves
\tilde{\mu}_{0}^{\eta}
,
\tilde{\mu}_{1}^{\eta}
(thus belonging to
\mathscr{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R})
) and discrete curves
\tilde{x}^{N,\eta}\in W^{1,p}(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})
such that
\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{k},\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\eta})^{q}\leq\eta
,
\mathcal{E}^{N}(\tilde{x}_{k}^{N,\eta})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{W}_{q}}\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{\eta}
, and

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}\big(\tilde{x}^{N,\eta}(t),\dot{\tilde{x}}^{N,\eta}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\tilde{\mu}_{0}^{\eta},\tilde{\mu}_{1}^{\eta})^{p}\leq\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{p}+\eta\,.

We conclude the proof by choosing a suitable diagonal sequence

.

5.2.
Proof of the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality

In this section we prove the
\Gamma
-
\liminf
in Theorem
3.1
. A crucial tool is a compactness result for "almost" solutions to the continuity equation.
This is the content of the next Proposition, which is inspired by
[
DNS12
, Lemma 4.5]
(see also
[
LM10
, Proposition 2.5]
) and follows similar ideas. For the sake of clarity, we provide a proof.

Definition 5.3

(Asymptotic solutions to
\mathsf{CE}
)
.

We say that a sequence
\left(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N}\right)_{N}
is
asymptotically a solution to
\mathsf{CE}
on
[0,1]
if the same assumptions of Definition
2.1
are satisfied for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, but with
3.
replaced by

3^{\prime}
.

For every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the measurable maps
\mathcal{R}^{N}\varphi:=t\mapsto(\partial_{t}\mu_{t}^{N}+\partial_{x}\nu_{t}^{N},\varphi)
converge to
0
weakly in
\text{L}^{1}(0,1)
as
N\to\infty
.

We use the notation
\mathcal{R}_{t}^{N}\varphi:=(\mathcal{R}^{N}\varphi)(t)
, for
t\in(0,1)
.

Proposition 5.4

(Compactness)
.

Let
\left(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N}\right)_{N}
be asymptotically a solution to
\mathsf{CE}
satisfying

\displaystyle A:=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\,.

Suppose that
\mu_{0}^{N}\to\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
vaguely. Then there exists a (non-relabeled) subsequence and a solution
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
such that
\mu_{t}^{N}\to\mu_{t}
vaguely in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
for all
t\in[0,1]
,
{\bm{\nu}}^{N}\to{\bm{\nu}}
vaguely in
\mathcal{M}((0,1)\times\mathbb{R})
, and

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Proof.

We denote by
\rho_{t}^{N}
,
j_{t}^{N}
the corresponding densities of
\mu_{t}^{N}
and
\nu_{t}^{N}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}
. Then an application of the Hölder inequality yields for every Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}
and
t_{1}<t_{2}\in[0,1]
the bound

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B}|j_{t}^{N}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t&\leq\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B}m(\rho_{t}^{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq\left(\|m\|_{\infty}\mathscr{L}^{1}(B)(t_{2}-t_{1})\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}A^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{aligned}

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, for
p^{\prime}
conjugate of
p
(note that
m\in\text{L}^{\infty}(0,M)
by concavity and
m(0)=0=m(M)
). Consequently there exists
{\bm{\nu}}\in\mathcal{M}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})
and a subsequence (not relabeled) such that
{\bm{\nu}}^{N}\to{\bm{\nu}}
vaguely as
N\rightarrow+\infty
.

Moreover, (
5.5
) shows that the map
t\mapsto|\nu_{t}^{N}|(B)
is equi-integrable in
[0,1]
and uniformly bounded in
L^{1}(0,1)
, for every
B\subset\mathbb{R}
. By Dunford–Pettis
[
Bre11
, Theorem 4.30]
, this in particular implies that
{\bm{\nu}}
satisfies for every
R>0

(5.6)

\displaystyle|{\bm{\nu}}|(B_{R}\times I)=\int_{I}m_{R}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall I\subset([0,1),\,B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R},\quad\text{with }m_{R}\in\text{L}^{1}(0,1),

so that we can write
{\bm{\nu}}=\int_{0}^{1}\nu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
, intended as in (
2.2
), for a Borel family of (signed) finite measures
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\in(0,1)}
(see
[
DNS12
, Lemma 4.5]
for a similar argument).

By assumption we know that
\mu_{0}^{N}\to\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
vaguely (in fact narrowly, by Remark
2.1
).
We now claim that for every time
t\in[0,1]
, the sequence
\mu_{t}^{N}
vaguely converges to some
\mu_{t}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
, as a consequence of
(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})
being asymptotically a solution to
\mathsf{CE}
.

Indeed, consider a function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and define
\phi(\tau,x):=\mathbbm{1}_{I}(\tau)\partial_{x}\varphi(x)
,
I:=[0,t]
. Note that the discontinuity set of
\phi
is concentrated on
D:=\{0,t\}\times\mathbb{R}
and from (
5.6
) one has
|{\bm{\nu}}|(D)=0
. It follows
[
AGS08
, Proposition 5.1.10]
that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\tau}^{N}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{I\times\mathbb{R}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\nu}}^{N}=\int_{I\times\mathbb{R}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\nu}}=\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Moreover, by definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{N}
and integrating over
I
we obtain

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N}&=\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}^{N}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\tau}^{N}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{N}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}+\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\end{aligned}

where we used that
\mu_{0}^{N}\to\mu_{0}
vaguely and that
\mathcal{R}^{N}\varphi\to 0
weakly in
\text{L}^{1}(0,1)
.
Hence, (
5.7
) shows that
\mu_{t}^{N}
is vaguely converging to some
\mu_{t}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
as
N\rightarrow+\infty
for every
t\in[0,1]
, with
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\in\mathsf{CE}
.
Finally, the lower semicontinuity estimate follows from the lower semicontinuity of
\Phi_{p,m}
, see (
I
).
∎

We are ready to prove the
\Gamma
-
\liminf
inequality in Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
,
\Gamma
-
\liminf
.

Let
\mu_{0}^{N}=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x_{0}^{N})
,
\mu_{1}^{N}=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x_{1}^{N})
be two sequences in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R})
converging vaguely to
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
, respectively. We claim that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{W}_{p,m}^{N}(\mu_{0}^{N},\mu_{1}^{N})\geq\mathbb{W}_{p,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

Applying Lemma
2.2
, with no loss of generality we can write

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{W}_{p,m}^{N}(\mu_{0}^{N},\mu_{1}^{N})^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x^{N}(t),\dot{x}^{N}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\ ,\ \ E:=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Phi_{p,m}^{N}(x^{N}(t),\dot{x}^{N}(t))<\infty\,,\end{aligned}

where
x^{N}\in W^{1,p}(0,1;\mathcal{K}_{N})
is the discrete geodesic between
x_{0}^{N}
and
x_{1}^{N}
with respect to
d_{p,m}^{N}
.
We define
\mu_{t}^{N}:=\mathcal{PC}^{N}(x_{t}^{N})
with density (w.r.t the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
)
\rho_{t}^{N}
given by the piecewise-constant map

(5.10)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{N}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}R_{i}^{N}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\left[x_{i}^{N}(t),x_{i+1}^{N}(t)\right)}(x)\,,

and define the momentum fields as

(5.11)

\displaystyle j_{t}^{N}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\dot{x}_{i}^{N}(t)R_{i}^{N}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\left[x_{i}^{N}(t),x_{i+1}^{N}(t)\right)}(x),\quad\nu_{t}^{N}:=j_{t}^{N}\mathscr{L}^{1}.

Step 1: Uniform error estimate.
Let us pick any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. We claim that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\left|\left(\partial_{t}\mu_{t}^{N}+\partial_{x}\nu_{t}^{N},\varphi\right)\right|\leq cN^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\left(\text{diam}(\supp\varphi)+2\right),

for some
c=c(\theta,E,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate of
p
.
For simplicity, in this proof we use the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\Delta x_{i}:=x_{i+1}^{N}(t)-x_{i}^{N}(t),\ \quad\Delta\dot{x}_{i}:=\dot{x}_{i+1}^{N}(t)-\dot{x}_{i}^{N}(t),\quad R_{i}:=R_{i}^{N}(t),\quad I_{i}:=[x_{i},x_{i+1}].

In particular, we have that
\dot{R_{i}}=-NR_{i}^{2}\Delta\dot{x}_{i}\,
, from which we infer

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N}=-\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}R_{i}\Delta\dot{x}_{i}\fint_{I_{i}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x-R_{i}\big(\varphi(x_{i})\dot{x}_{i}-\varphi(x_{i+1})\dot{x}_{i+1}\big).

Using a second order Taylor expansion for
\varphi
, we know there exist a family of points
\left(c_{x}\right)_{x}
such that for every
x\in I_{i}
, we have
c_{x}\in(x,x_{i+1})
and

(\mu_{t}^{\lambda},\nu_{t}^{\lambda})\in\mathsf{CE}
given by

\displaystyle\mu_{t}^{\lambda}:=\big((1+\lambda)\text{id}\big)_{\#}\mu_{t}\,,\quad\nu_{t}^{\lambda}:=(1+\lambda)\big((1+\lambda)\text{id}\big)_{\#}\nu_{t}\,.

Note that by construction, the corresponding densities
\rho_{t}^{\lambda}
,
j_{t}^{\lambda}
are given by

(6.15)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\rho_{t}\Big(\frac{x}{1+\lambda}\Big)\,,\quad j_{t}^{\lambda}(x)=j_{t}\Big(\frac{x}{1+\lambda}\Big)\,,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\,,t\in[0,1]\,.

Furthermore, we have that
\mu_{0}^{N,\lambda}:=\big((1+\lambda)\text{id}\big)_{\#}\mu_{0}^{N}=\mathcal{E}^{N}(w^{N,\lambda})
, where
w^{N,\lambda}=(1+\lambda)w^{N}
. In particular, we note that

(6.16)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{i\leq N}R_{i}^{N}(w^{N,\lambda}),\,\sup_{t}\|\rho_{t}^{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\right\}\leq(1+\lambda)^{-1}M<M\,.

We can then apply the first part of the proof (Case 1) to
\mu_{0}^{N,\lambda}
,
\mu_{0}^{\lambda}
, and
\mu_{1}^{\lambda}
: for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we can find
\mu_{1}^{N,\lambda}\in\mathcal{E}^{N}
,
\bar{\mu}_{1}^{\lambda}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mu_{1}^{N,\lambda}\to\bar{\mu}_{1}^{\lambda}
in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
and

(6.17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{1}^{\lambda},\bar{\mu}_{1}^{\lambda})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}(\mu_{0}^{N,\lambda},\mu_{1}^{N,\lambda})\leq\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu_{0}^{\lambda},\mu_{1}^{\lambda})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

Using similar arguments as in
[
LM10
, Theorem 4.1]
, it is not hard to see that

(6.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}d_{2,m}^{N}(w^{N},w^{N,\lambda})^{2}\leq C\,\lambda\,\sup_{N}\|w^{N}\|_{\ell_{2,N}}^{2}=:\bar{C}^{2}\lambda\,,

where
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
only depends on
\phi
and
\bar{C}<\infty
since
\left(\mu_{0}^{N}\right)_{N}
satisfies (
2.20
).
Moreover, by (
6.15
), we also have that

(6.19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{2,m}(\mu_{t}^{\lambda},\nu_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq(1+\lambda)\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{2,m}((1+\lambda)^{-1}\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq(1+\lambda)\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})^{2}\,,

where at last we used that
\theta
is non-increasing (see Remark
2.1
). Putting (
6.17
), (
6.18
), and (
6.19
) together, we obtain that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}^{N,\lambda})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}(\mu_{0},\mu_{0}^{N,\lambda})+\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}(\mu_{0}^{N,\lambda},\mu_{1}^{N,\lambda})

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{(1+\lambda)}\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

This shows that (
iii
) is satisfied with
\mu_{1}^{N}:=\mu_{1}^{N,\lambda}
if
\lambda>0
is sufficiently small. Finally, by (
6.17
) and the scaling properties of
\mathbb{W}_{q}
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{1},\bar{\mu}_{1}^{\lambda})\leq\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{1},\mu_{1}^{\lambda})+\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu_{1}^{\lambda},\bar{\mu}_{1}^{\lambda})\leq\lambda\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{1}}|x|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

This shows that (
ii
) is satisfied with
\bar{\mu}_{1}:=\bar{\mu}_{1}^{\lambda}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
if
\lambda>0
is sufficiently small. Given that
\mu_{1}^{N}\to\bar{\mu}_{1}
in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
by construction, we also deduce (
i
), which ends the proof.
∎

6.2.
Proof of the convergence of the JKO schemes (Theorem
3.2
)

We are finally ready to prove Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

We start showing (
i
).
With no loss of generality, we assume
\tau=\frac{1}{2}
and
F_{N}\equiv 0
, the general case following trivially by the assumption in (
3.1
). Thus,
\mathcal{J}_{\tau,N}(\cdot)=\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}(\mu_{N}^{(0)},\cdot)^{2}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(\cdot)=\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu^{(0)},\cdot)^{2}
. In this case, the
\Gamma
-
\liminf
inequality easily follows by Theorem
3.1
(LI), hence we shall prove the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
.

Let
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu^{(0)},\mu)<\infty
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, we apply Proposition
6.1
and find a sequence
\left(\mu^{N,\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{E}^{N}\right)_{N}
and
\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mu^{N,\varepsilon}\to\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon}
in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
, with
\mathbb{W}_{q}(\mu,\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon
, and
\limsup_{N}\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}\big(\mu_{N}^{(0)},\mu^{N,\varepsilon}\big)\leq\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu^{(0)},\mu)+\varepsilon
. Hence, we can choose a diagonal sequence
\mu^{N}:=\mu^{N,\varepsilon_{N}}
such that
\mu^{N}\to\mu
in
\mathbb{W}_{q}
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{W}_{2,m}^{N}\big(\mu_{N}^{(0)},\mu^{N}\big)\leq\mathbb{W}_{2,m}(\mu^{(0)},\mu)\,,

which concludes the proof of (
i
).

Finally, the proof of (
ii
) follows from the
\Gamma
-convergence shown in (
i
) and the equicoercivity property proved in Proposition
2.3
, see e.g.
[
Bra02
, Theorem 1.21]
.

7.
Appendix A. Properties of the action densities

The following lemma concerns continuity properties of
\phi
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Fix
\phi_{p,m}
as in Defintion
2.1
and define
g(r):=\phi_{p,m}(r,1)
. Pick
\bar{M}<M
and for every
\lambda\in(1,2)
, consider the set

\displaystyle I_{\bar{M}}(\lambda):=\left\{r\in(0,M)\ :\ \lambda r\leq\bar{M}\right\}\,,\quad I_{\bar{M}}(\lambda)\subset(0,M)\,.

Then we have:

(7.1)

\displaystyle g(\lambda r)\leq\big(1+C_{\bar{M}}(\lambda-1)\big)g(r)\,,\quad\forall r\in I_{\bar{M}}(\lambda)\,,

where
C_{\bar{M}}<\infty
is a constant depending on
g
and
\bar{M}
.

Proof.

Let
r\in I_{\bar{M}}(\lambda)
. Note that
g
is strictly positive convex function with domain
(0,M)
such that
g\to+\infty
at the boundary of this interval. Hence there exists
r_{\min}\in(0,M)
a minimum of
g
.
We split the proof of (
7.1
) into two cases.

Case 1
:
\lambda r\leq r_{\min}
. In this case, having that
\lambda>1
and
g
is non increasing (by convexity) in
(0,r_{\min})
, we conclude that
g(\lambda r)\leq g(r)
.

Case 2
:
\lambda r>r_{\min}
. Using that
\lambda<2
, we obtain that
r>r_{\min}/2
and thus
r,\lambda r\in(r_{\min}/2,\bar{M})
. Over this interval,
g
is a Lipschitz map of Lipschitz constant
\bar{L}=\bar{L}(r_{\min},\bar{M})
. It follows

\displaystyle g(\lambda r)\leq g(r)+\bar{L}(\lambda-1)r\leq\bigg(1+\frac{\bar{L}\bar{M}}{g(r_{\min})}(\lambda-1)\bigg)g(r)\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Hitting sets and colorings of hypergraphs
Balázs Bursics, Bence Csonka, Luca Szepessy

Abstract
. In this paper we study the minimal size of edges in hypergraph families that guarantees the existence of a polychromatic coloring, that is, a k k -coloring of a vertex set such that every hyperedge contains a vertex of all k k color classes. We also investigate the connection of this problem with c c -shallow hitting sets: sets of vertices that intersect each hyperedge in at least one and at most c c vertices. We determine for some hypergraph families the minimal c c for which a c c -shallow hitting set exists. We also study this problem for a special hypergraph family, which is induced by arithmetic progressions with a difference from a given set. We show connections between some geometric hypergraph families and the latter, and prove relations between the set of differences and polychromatic colorability.

A
polychromatic k-coloring
of a hypergraph
H
is a
k
-coloring of its vertex set such that every hyperedge contains a vertex of all
k
color classes. Observe that a polychromatic 2-coloring is the same as the usual proper 2-coloring of hypergraphs, where we require that no edge is monochromatic. By merging
j
color classes of a polychromatic
k
-coloring we get a polychromatic
(k-j+1)
-coloring, so the condition of monochromatic
k
-colorability becomes stricter as
k
increases. A trivial necessary condition for the existence of a polychromatic
k
-coloring is that all edges of
H
must be of size at least
k
.

Definition 1
.

Denote by
H_{\geq m}
or simply
H_{m}
the hypergraph obtained from
H
by deleting all hyperedges of size smaller than
m
, and denote by
H_{=m}
the hypergraph consisting of the hyperedges of
H
with size exactly
m
. Similarly, for a hypergraph family
\mathcal{H}
let
\mathcal{H}_{m}=\mathcal{H}_{\geq m}=\{H_{\geq m}:H\in\mathcal{H}\}
and
\mathcal{H}_{=m}=\{H_{=m}:H\in\mathcal{H}\}
.

One can make statements about the existence of polychromatic
k
-colorings for every member of a hypergraph family
\mathcal{H}
using the following parameter:

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph family. Denote by

the smallest positive integer

such that for every

there exists a polychromatic
k
-coloring of the hypergraph
H_{\geq m}
. If there is no such

, set
m_{\mathcal{H}}(k)=\infty
.

Determining or bounding
m_{\mathcal{H}}(k)
is an interesting problem in itself for some hypergraph families.
Range capturing hypergraph families
are particularly well studied: Suppose
\mathcal{S}
is a family of planar (or higher dimensional) sets, called the

, consider the family of hypergraphs whose members have a finite vertex set
V
, and whose edge set consists of all subsets

such that

for some

, in which case we say that the range
S
captures
e
. Much research has been done on polychromatic colorings and the parameter
m(k)
of such hypergraph families, for example, when the range family consists of halfplanes
[
24
]
, translates of a polygon
[
11
]
, translates of a convex body
[
9
]
, homothets of a polygon
[
10
]
, translates of an octant
[
15
]
, axis-parallel rectangles
[

]
, or axis-parallel strips
[

]
. For a comprehensive summary, see the website
[

]
maintained by Keszegh and Pálvölgyi.

Investigating this problem is also motivated by the connection between polychromatic colorings and the cover-decomposability of planar sets. Suppose that we have a planar polygon

Now we turn to shallow hitting sets, and their role in constructing polychromatic colorings under suitable conditions.

Definition 3
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be a hypergraph, an
U\subset V
vertex set is a c-shallow hitting set for a positive integer
c
if for every hyperedge
e\in E
:
1\leq|e\cap U|\leq c.

Definition 4
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be a hypergraph, and
V^{\prime}\subset V
. Define
E^{\prime}=\{e^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}|\exists e\in E:e^{\prime}=e\cap E^{\prime}\}
, we say that
E^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is the induced subhypergraph of
H
on
V^{\prime}
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph family such that for arbitrary
H=(V,E)\in\mathcal{H}
and
V^{\prime}\subset V
the induced subhypergraph
H^{\prime}
is a member of
\mathcal{H}
. Then
c
-shallow hitting sets can be used to create polychromatic colorings, see e.g.
[
24
,
16
]
.

In these papers
c
-shallow hitting sets are applied through the implicit use of the following lemma, which we restate here (and which also follows from
[
16
, Theorem 2.11.]
):

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that the hypergraph family
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the condition that for arbitrary
H=(V,E)\in\mathcal{H}
and
V^{\prime}\subset V
, the induced subhypergraph of
H
on
V^{\prime}
is an element of
\mathcal{H}
, and that every hyperedge
e\in E
contains a vertex
v\in e
such that for
e^{\prime}=e\setminus\{v\}
and
E^{\prime}=E\setminus\{e\}\cup\{e^{\prime}\}
we have
H^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime})\in\mathcal{H}.

Suppose that for every
m\geq c
and every element of
\mathcal{H}_{=m}
there exists a
c
-shallow hitting set. Then
m_{\mathcal{H}}(k)\leq c\cdot(k-1)+1
.

For range capturing hypergraph families, the first condition of the above statement – that taking an induced subhypergraph does not lead out of the family – suffices automatically.

On the other hand, the existence of
c
-shallow hitting sets is not so evident, for example it is a frequent case for geometric hypergraph families that a hyperedge contains more than
c
pairwise disjoint hyperedges.
Thus we need to restrict to a
Sperner
subfamily of the original hypergraph family (that is, where no two edges are contained one in another), to have a chance for the existence of
c
-shallow hitting sets. However, in some cases, e.g., hypergraphs induced by bottomless rectangles (see below), this is not strong enough either
[
16
]
.

Fortunately, Lemma
1
. is about a special type of Sperner subfamily, the
m
-uniform members of the family, which gives even better chances to obtain
c
-shallow hitting sets. Therefore, for a given hypergraph family
\mathcal{H}
, it is natural to ask whether there is a positive integer
c
such that there exists a
c
-shallow hitting set for all
H_{=m}\in\mathcal{H}_{=m}
.

Since the resulting bound is linear in
k
, Lemma
1
. is also loosely related to an important conjecture of the field:

Conjecture 1

(
[
20
]
)
.

If
m_{\mathcal{H}}(2)<\infty
for a hypergraph family
\mathcal{H}
, then
m_{\mathcal{H}}(k)=O(k)
.

It would also be interesting to see whether Lemma
1
. is reversible, more precisely, does an upper bound of
m_{\mathcal{H}}(k)
, which is linear in
k
, guarantee a constant
c
independent from
k
such that there exists a
c
-shallow hitting set for every member of
\mathcal{H}_{=m}
?

Denote by

1.2.
Axis-parallel strips

We also investigated this question on another geometric hypergraph family and its dual:

Definition 6
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the hypergraph family which consists of those hypergraphs
H=(V,E)
for which
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is finite, and the edges are induced by axis-parallel strips: every
e\in E
edge can be presented in the form
V\cap\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{0}<x<x_{1}\}
or

for some

. Denote by
\mathcal{A}^{*}
its dual: the family of such hypergraphs that the vertices are axis-parallel strips, and the edges are sets consisting of the strips containing a given point
(x,y)
.

The following results are presented in
[

]

2\bigg\lceil\frac{3k}{4}\bigg\rceil-1\leq m_{\mathcal{A}}(k)\leq 2k-1

2\bigg\lceil\frac{k}{2}\bigg\rceil+1\leq m_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}(k)\leq 2k-1

The lower bound for

comes from the more general case of arbitrary dimension, however, in the plane we can improve on this bound:

.

For the hypergraph family

.

The existence of 2-shallow hitting sets on the hypergraph family

would give an alternative proof for the upper bound of

but this is not the case for this problem either.

Theorem 3
.

For sufficiently large
m
there is an element in
\mathcal{A}_{=m}
with no 2-shallow hitting set.

Note that for given
m
and for
k=\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil
colors, taking a color class in the coloring corresponding to the upper bound in
[
2
]
gives a 3-shallow hitting set, so we have determined the smallest possible
c
with a
c
-shallow hitting set for every member of
\mathcal{A}_{=m}
.

This problem motivated the study of the following more general hypergraph families:

Definition 7
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}^{+}
the hypergraph family which consists of such

hypergraphs for which

is finite, and the edges are induced by the union of a horizontal and a vertical axis-parallel strip: every

for some

.

Definition 8
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{s}
the hypergraph family which consists of such

hypergraphs for which

is finite, and the edges are induced by the union of

where

We have the following bounds about their polychromatic colorings:

3\bigg\lceil\frac{3}{4}k\bigg\rceil-2\leq m_{\mathcal{A}^{+}}(k)\leq 4k-3.

We note that

, which implies that the upper bound is sharp in the case of two colors.

m_{\mathcal{A}_{s}}(k)=s\cdot m_{\mathcal{A}}(k)-s+1.

Corollary 1

1.3.
Arithmetic progressions

A famous result of van der Waerden states that for any finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
there exists an arbitrarily long monochromatic arithmetic progression, or in other words, if we take the hypergraph on
\mathbb{N}
with finite arithmetic progressions as edges, then every finite coloring contains a monochromatic hyperedge of arbitrary size.

There are many well-studied related problems, one of them being how does a
ladder
, a set
L\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that all finite colorings of
\mathbb{N}
contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions with difference
d\in L
look like
[
12
,
5
]
. Translated again to the hypergraph terminology, let
H^{L}
be the hypergraph obtained from the previously mentioned hypergraph by keeping only those edges which are derived from arithmetic progressions with difference from some subset
L\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, then
L
is a ladder if there is no
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(H^{L})_{m}
is properly colorable with finitely many colors. Another related and already examined question concerning polychromatic edges is the following: what is the smallest
r
such that every
r
-coloring of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
contains a polychromatic arithmetic progression of size
k
? This is usually referred to as the
anti-van der Waerden number
aw
([n],k)
, which is also studied in the case of Abelian groups instead of
[n]
, see e.g.
[
6
,
4
]
.

We have considered the polychromatic colorability of these, and also of somewhat more general hypergraphs. An additional motivation for this is a connection between the family
\mathcal{B}
of geometric hypergraphs induced by bottomless rectangles and a special case of the arithmetic progression hypergraphs (Theorem
7
. (1),
M=\{0\}
), realized by Keszegh and Pálvölgyi
[
13
]
. In this paper some more connections between geometric hypergraph families and hypergraph families induced by arithmetic progressions are shown, leading to bounds for the parameter
m_{k}
of these hypergraphs.

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of all natural numbers including
0
and
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

Definition 9
.

Let
D,M\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and denote by
\mathcal{A}_{D,M}
the family of hypergraphs
A
such that
S:=V(A)\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a finite set, and

E(A)\subseteq\{\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{k}\cap S:\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{k}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{is a finite arithmetic}\newline
\text{progression with difference}\hskip 5.69046ptd\in D\hskip 5.69046pt\text{such that}\hskip 5.69046pt\exists m\in\mathbb{N}:a_{0}-md\in M\}.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{D,M}^{\infty}
the family of hypergraphs
A
such that
S:=V(A)\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a finite set and
E(A)\subseteq\{\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\cap S:\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{is an infinite arithmetic progression with difference}\hskip 5.69046ptd\in D\hskip 5.69046pt\text{such that}\hskip 5.69046pt\exists m\in\mathbb{N}:a_{0}-md\in M\}.

For the sake of simplicity we use the notations

m_{D,M}(k):=m_{\mathcal{A}_{D,M}}(k)
and

m_{D,M}^{\infty}(k):=m_{\mathcal{A}_{D,M}^{\infty}}(k)
.

If

can be written in the form
\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{j_{i}}:j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\}
for some

integers, we can determine in most cases whether

and the following hypergraph families:

Denote by
\mathcal{R}
the hypergraph family which consists hypergraphs

V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is finite and the edges are induced by axis-parallel rectangles: every

Definition 11

Denote by

the hypergraph family which consists hypergraphs

such that

is finite and the edges are induced by translates of octants: every

Denote by

(in fact, there is not even an integer

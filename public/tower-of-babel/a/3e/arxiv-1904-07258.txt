Boundaries and supercurrent multiplets in 3D Landau-Ginzburg models Preprint: LMU-ASC 16/19
Ilka Brunner, Jonathan Schulz, and Alexander Tabler

Abstract
Theories with 3D 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 bulk supersymmetry may preserve a 2D 𝒩 = ( 0 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(0,2) subalgebra when a boundary is introduced, possibly with localized degrees of freedom. We propose generalized supercurrent multiplets with bulk and boundary parts adapted to such setups. Using their structure, we comment on implications for the Q ¯ + \overline{Q}_{+} -cohomology. As an example, we apply the developed framework to Landau-Ginzburg models. In these models, we study the role of boundary degrees of freedom and matrix factorizations. We verify our results using quantization.



1
Introduction

In this paper we study
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric theories in flat
(2+1)
-dimensional spacetime with (spacelike) boundaries. The boundary necessarily breaks translational invariance and hence can only preserve part of the bulk supersymmetry. Explicitly, the
\mathcal{N}=2
algebra in 3 dimensions is

\{Q_{\pm},\overline{Q}_{\pm}\}=-4P_{\pm},\quad\{Q_{+},\overline{Q}_{-}\}=2P_{\perp},

(1)

where spacetime has coordinates
x^{\pm}
and
x_{\perp}
, see appendix
A
for a summary of our conventions. We want to consider the case with a boundary in
x_{\perp}
-direction, breaking supersymmetry to a subalgebra of (
1
) that does not contain
P_{\perp}
, the generator of translations in that direction.
As has been analyzed before
Dimofte:2017tpi
;
Okazaki:2013kaa
;
Gadde:2013sca
, in the case of
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry in
3
dimensions, there are two types of supersymmetric boundary conditions, referred to as A-type and B-type. Each of them is associated to a subalgebra of the initial bulk supersymmetry algebra, containing two momentum operators and two supersymmetry charges. A-type boundary conditions preserve
(1,1)
supersymmetry, whereas B-type boundary conditions preserve a chiral
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
subalgebra, generated by
Q_{+}
and
\overline{Q}_{+}
.
We will focus on
(0,2)
boundary conditions and analyze them from two points of view: On the one hand, for theories defined by a Lagrangian, we employ a Noether procedure. On the other hand, we discuss the structure of the supercurrent multiplets
Komargodski:2010rb
;
Dumitrescu:2011iu
and formulate boundary multiplets. The two points of view are interrelated, as the (improved) Noether currents form components of the current multiplets.

From a Lagrangian point of view, the supersymmetric bulk Lagrangian transforms under SUSY-variations into a total derivative. In the presence of a boundary, this generically yields a boundary term which must be canceled for the symmetry to be preserved. This can be achieved by choosing boundary conditions on the fields, such that the boundary variation vanishes. Alternatively, and this is the main focus of the present paper, one can cancel the boundary variation by adding a suitable boundary part to the action, such that the action is invariant under symmetry variations without reference to the boundary conditions on the fields. This term can contain extra boundary degrees of freedom that are not inherited from the bulk. The full invariant action thus contains a bulk and boundary term.

S=\int_{M}{\cal L}^{B}+\int_{\partial M}{\cal L}^{\partial}.

(2)

Given an action which is invariant under a symmetry, Noether’s procedure yields a set of conserved charges and currents. In the case of supersymmetry, this includes the (canonical) energy momentum tensor and the supersymmetry currents. After imposing canonical commutation relations between the fields, the Noether charges provide a representation of the symmetry algebra in terms of the fields.

In the case of pure bulk theories, it is very useful to arrange the supercurrents together with other conserved currents into multiplets. This can be done independently of a Lagrangian definition of a theory. The supercurrents form a representation of the supersymmetry algebra, and their most general form has been discussed recently in
Komargodski:2010rb
;
Dumitrescu:2011iu
.
Supercurrent multiplets are indecomposable SUSY multiplets that contain the stress energy tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
and the supercurrents
S_{\alpha\mu}
as part of their components. In addition, there are brane currents, whose integrals yield brane charges. The components of the supercurrent multiplets appear in a local version of the supersymmetry algebra, which very schematically takes the form

\displaystyle\{\overline{Q}_{\alpha},S_{\beta\mu}\}

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}+\dots,

(3)

\displaystyle\{Q_{\alpha},S_{\beta\mu}\}

\displaystyle=\dots,

(4)

where the dots indicate various currents that will be explained later in the text.
As is well-known, the stress tensor for a theory is not unique, but can be modified by improvement transformations. Indeed, the same holds for the supercurrents, and the notion of improvement transformations can be lifted to the full multiplet. In any three-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric theory (and also in other dimensions with the same amount of supersymmetry), there exists a so-called
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet. Under special conditions, the
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet can be decomposable, such that there exist smaller multiplets. Of special interest in the context of the current work is the
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet, which exists in theories which exhibit an
R
-symmetry.

The notion of supercurrent multiplets has been extended to theories with defects in
Drukker:2017xrb
, where a new so-called defect multiplet was constructed. As a consequence of the violation of translation symmetry perpendicular to the defect, the stress tensor is no longer conserved. This violation is encoded in the displacement operator. The defect multiplet contains the displacement operator as one of its components
Gaiotto:2013sma
;
Drukker:2017xrb
.

In the current paper, we consider supermultiplets in situations with boundary, focusing on the
preserved
symmetries. As mentioned above, to formulate boundary conditions means to specify a subalgebra of the SUSY algebra such that the momentum operator in the direction perpendicular to the boundary is not contained. The supercurrent multiplet is in particular a representation of the larger (bulk) algebra and hence also of the smaller algebra. In the case of the
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
subalgebra, we show how the bulk supercurrent multiplets decompose under the smaller algebra. Of course, due to the presence of the boundary, the currents contained in the multiplet are no longer conserved by themselves. To formulate a consistent multiplet for a theory with boundary, we discuss how to add boundary parts to the
(0,2)
-components of the initial bulk multiplet. Our ansatz for a full
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet is

\mathcal{R}_{\mu}^{\text{full}}=\mathcal{R}_{\mu}^{B}+\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\phantom{\mu}{\hat{\mu}}}\delta(x_{\perp})\mathcal{R}^{\partial}_{\hat{\mu}},

(5)

where
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}^{B}
is the bulk part,
\mathcal{R}^{\partial}_{\hat{\mu}}
is the boundary part and
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\phantom{\mu}{\hat{\mu}}}
denotes an embedding. Both parts decompose into
(0,2)
-components. The boundary part is added to the bulk multiplet in such a way that the initial divergence-freeness of the bulk currents is completed to bulk-boundary conservation laws. We do not discuss possible modifications of the bulk currents corresponding to the
broken
symmetries.

One important feature of supercurrent multiplets is that they fall into short representations of the supersymmetry algebra. Therefore, they are protected under RG flow and retain their form
Dumitrescu:2011iu
. The supercurrents of the quantum theory can thus be used to constrain the IR behavior of a theory using the UV information. In the case of
two-dimensional

{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
models, the supercurrent multiplet for theories with an
R
-symmetry was used to study renormalization group invariants in
Dedushenko:2015opz
. In particular, it was shown that an RG invariant chiral algebra exists, extending earlier works
Witten:1993jg
;
Silverstein:1994ih
;
Witten:2005px
. The chiral algebra arises as the cohomology of the supercharge
\overline{Q}_{+}
. Using only the form of the supercurrent multiplet,
Dedushenko:2015opz
shows that there is a half-twisted stress tensor (the original stress tensor modified by the
R
-current) in the cohomology. As a consequence, conformal symmetry is part of the chiral algebra.

Given these findings, we consider the consequences of the supercurrent multiplets for the case that the
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
supersymmetry is the symmetry preserved at the
boundary
of a three-dimensional theory. We do not find a stress-tensor in the cohomology following the steps in
Dedushenko:2015opz
, however, there is a weaker statement. For this, one makes the
(0,2)
-structure manifest by regarding the three-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
theory as a two-dimensional theory living on the boundary
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
. The bosonic fields of this theory are valued in maps from
\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
to the original target. The currents are obtained from the original three-dimensional ones by integrating over the direction perpendicular to the boundary and are preserved in the boundary theory. In this theory, we then do have a stress energy tensor that is part of the cohomology. Formulated in the initial theory, this cohomology element is obtained by an integration in the perpendicular direction from infinity (or a second boundary, which we do not discuss here) to the boundary. Note that the action of any charge computed from the currents applied to an insertion at the boundary would involve an integration over this direction, as well as all other spatial directions. In this sense we can also interpret the partially integrated currents in the original theory.

The integration along the perpendicular direction also gives another perspective on the boundary multiplets. The theory effectively becomes a two-dimensional theory with
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
supersymmetry, and the integrated multiplets are genuine
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
multiplets.

In the second part of the paper, we study a specific example, namely a theory of three-dimensional chiral multiplets with a superpotential. We restrict our explicit discussion to the case of a single chiral field with a monomial superpotential. However, a generalization to more than one superfield and an arbitrary superpotential preserving an
R
-symmetry is straightforward and our discussion applies to this case as well. As has already been shown in
Gadde:2013sca
;
Yoshida:2014ssa
, the condition of preserving
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
supersymmetry leads to a three-dimensional generalization of matrix factorizations
Kapustin:2002bi
;
Brunner:2003dc
. In this case, boundary terms are canceled by adding fermionic degrees of freedom and a potential at the boundary. Using Noether’s procedure, we compute the conserved currents for different boundary conditions. These currents contain pure bulk as well as boundary pieces and only the combination of both is conserved. We also discuss the current multiplets, following
Dumitrescu:2011iu
for the bulk case. Starting from the Noether currents, one needs to apply improvement transformations to symmetrize the stress energy tensor and subsequently organize the currents into supercurrent multiplets. As we consider the case where
R
-symmetry is preserved, the relevant multiplet is the
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet. We spell out all components of the bulk-boundary multiplet in the example following the strategy outlined above.

To complete our understanding of the symmetries in the model, we study the algebra of the supercharges and supercurrents (
3
) in the example. We start with the explicit expressions of the currents in terms of fields and impose canonical commutation relations on the fields. We then verify the relations (
3
) as well as the
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
superalgebra in the specific representation of the example. In the computation, it is essential to make use of the correct factorization properties of the superpotential to recover the correct form of the algebra.

This paper is organized as follows: In section
2
we review and elaborate on various aspects of theories formulated on Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{1,N-1}
with a flat boundary
\mathbb{R}^{1,N-2}
, following and extending
DiPietro:2015zia
. In section
3
we discuss supercurrent multiplets. We review the constraints that have to be satisfied by such a multiplet from
Dumitrescu:2011iu
. As explained there, the most general supercurrent multiplets consists of certain superfields satisfying defining constraint relations. Their solutions are unique up to improvement transformations. In special situations, such improvements can be used to formulate shorter multiplets, in particular the
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet. We decompose the multiplets and constraints according to the
(0,2)
-substructure and formulate consistent bulk-boundary multiplets. Here, current conservation of the combined bulk-boundary system is imposed. We then discuss an integrated structure, where we integrate in the direction perpendicular to the boundary. This provides a two-dimensional version of the conservation equations, taking the familiar form of divergence-freeness of the currents.

We then turn to a discussion of the Landau-Ginzburg example in section
4
. First, in
4.1
, we introduce the bulk model, then introduce a boundary in
4.2
. We distinguish the cases with and without superpotential and show that without specifying boundary conditions,
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
SUSY can be preserved by introducing boundary fermions and matrix factorizations. Boundary conditions do however have to be imposed to make the action stationary and we discuss Dirichlet and Neumann conditions. In particular, there is a possibility to make boundary conditions dynamical, imposing them as equations of motion. We then turn to the formulation of currents in
4.3
. Here, Noether’s procedure is employed to compute the conserved currents in the combined bulk boundary system. We discuss improvements to symmetrize the stress-energy tensor. Section
4.4
contains a discussion of the supercurrent multiplets in the example.

In section
5
we study the realization of the symmetry algebra in the Landau-Ginzburg model in terms of the fields. By imposing canonical commutation relations for the bulk and boundary fields, we verify that the supercharges implement the correct symmetry transformations on the fields and their derivatives, and we compute the brackets between supercharges and supercurrents. Special attention is paid to the contributions from the boundary. We do not impose any explicit boundary conditions on the fields in our computations. In
5.1
we use the action of the supercharge on the derivative of the scalars to verify how the bulk fermions decompose into boundary fermions — a decomposition that was seen in earlier sections from the point of view of the action. In the following subsections
5.2
and
5.3
we verify the brackets between supercharges and currents and finally integrate them to the global supersymmetry algebra. The factorization condition of the superpotential arises as a consistency condition on the SUSY algebra. Finally, section
6
contains some conclusions and outlook.

We summarize various technical points in a number of appendices. Here we also collect some supplementary material for completeness. In particular, we present some results on
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
supersymmetry in appendix
B.2
.

2
Currents and charges in theories with boundaries

In this section we want to study (classical) theories on flat Minkowski half-space with spacelike boundary. In particular, we will consider

M=\Set{x^{\mu}=\big(x^{0},\ldots,x^{n},\ldots,x^{N-1}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{1,N-1}}{x^{n}\leq 0},\quad\partial M=\big\{x^{n}=0\big\}.

(6)

On the flat boundary we will denote the tangential coordinates by
x^{\hat{\mu}}
while the normal coordinate is
x^{n}
, i.e.
{\hat{\mu}}
takes all values except
n
. While our main focus will be
N=3
later on, we will keep the discussion more general in this section.

2.1
Bulk and boundary Lagrangians

We want to study theories that have both bulk and boundary fields with an action of the form

S=S^{B}+S^{\partial}=\int_{M}\mathcal{L}^{B}+\int_{\partial M}\mathcal{L}^{\partial},\quad\mathcal{L}^{B}=\mathcal{L}^{B}[\Phi,\partial_{\mu}\Phi],\quad\mathcal{L}^{\partial}=\mathcal{L}^{\partial}[X,\partial_{\hat{\mu}}X,\Phi|_{\partial},\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\Phi|_{\partial}],

(7)

where
\Phi
and
X
denotes bulk and boundary fields, respectively. Furthermore, one has to impose
boundary conditions
. We follow the similar discussion from
DiPietro:2015zia
, which we generalize here. The most general boundary conditions will be of the form

G(\text{fields}|_{\partial},\text{derivatives of fields}|_{\partial})=0.

(8)

Under a (rigid) variation of the fields and an integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle\delta S

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\delta\mathcal{L}^{B}+\int_{\partial M}\delta\mathcal{L}^{\partial}

(9)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Big[\underset{\text{Bulk }\operatorname{EoM}^{B}[\Phi]}{\underbrace{\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial\Phi}-\partial_{\mu}\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\Phi)}}}\Big]\delta\Phi

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial M}\Big[\big(\underset{\text{Boundary }\operatorname{EoM}^{\partial}[X]}{\underbrace{\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial X}-\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}X)}}}\big)\delta X+\big(\underset{\eqqcolon\mathcal{A}}{\underbrace{\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial\Phi|_{\partial}}-\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\Phi|_{\partial})}+\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial(\partial_{n}\Phi|_{\partial})}}}\big)\delta\Phi|_{\partial}+\partial_{\hat{\mu}}(\cdots)\Big]\mathclap{.}

Stationarity of the action requires that

\big[\mathcal{A}\cdot\delta\Phi|_{\partial}\big]_{G=0}=\partial_{\hat{\mu}}A^{\hat{\mu}},

(10)

for some boundary vector field
A^{\hat{\mu}}
. Note that in any case, the variations
\delta\Phi|_{\partial}
,
\delta X
we consider must be
compatible
with the chosen boundary condition, i.e. we may only consider such variations that satisfy

\delta G|_{G=0}=0.

(11)

A special kind of boundary condition is the
dynamical boundary condition
, which amounts to requiring

G\coloneqq\mathcal{A}\overset{!}{=}0.

(12)

This is equivalent to the paradigm (e.g. found in
Dimofte:2017tpi
;
Bilal:2011gp
) not to impose static boundary conditions, but instead adopt the boundary conditions that are naturally imposed by the tendency of the system to make the action stationary.

2.2
Symmetries, currents and charges in boundary theories

2.2.1
Symmetries in boundary theories

Let us try to understand symmetries in theories with a boundary.
If we want the full theory to be invariant under some symmetry transformation of the fields, the boundary condition must be compatible with the symmetry transformation, as mentioned in
DiPietro:2015zia
:

\delta_{\text{sym}}G|_{G=0}=0.

(13)

If this is satisfied,
G
is called a
symmetric
boundary condition with respect to
\delta_{\text{sym}}
. Conveniently, this requirement is equivalent to demanding that the symmetry variation
\delta_{\text{sym}}
is a
permitted variation
in the sense of equation (
11
).

The definition of a symmetry in the presence of a boundary is analogous to the case of a pure bulk theory: a symmetry is an off-shell transformation of both bulk and boundary fields that leaves the action invariant,
possibly
after using boundary conditions:

0=\delta_{\text{sym}}S|_{G=0}=(\delta_{\text{sym}}S^{B}+\delta_{\text{sym}}S^{\partial})|_{G=0}=0.

(14)

It is natural to restrict to symmetries that arise from a symmetry of the bulk theory, i.e.
\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{B}=\partial_{\mu}V^{\mu}
holds for some bulk vector fields
V^{\mu}
. Noether’s theorem in the bulk then ensures that
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{B}=0
, where
J_{B}^{\mu}=-\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\Phi)}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi+V^{\mu}
still holds.
In terms of Lagrangians, we can then write

0=\delta_{\text{sym}}S|_{G=0}=\int_{M}\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{B}+\int_{\partial M}\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{\partial}=\int_{\partial M}(V^{n}+\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{\partial})|_{G=0}.

(15)

If the above condition holds without imposing any boundary condition
G=0
, one says that the symmetry is preserved
without reference to specific boundary conditions
. As we start from a bulk theory
\mathcal{L}^{B}
with a symmetry, it is interesting to investigate whether a
boundary compensating term

\mathcal{L}^{\partial}
exists, so that (
14
) holds without referring to a boundary condition
DiPietro:2015zia
;
Bilal:2011gp
.
However, in general cases, one must impose specific symmetric boundary conditions
and
add boundary terms so that the full action is stationary (
10
) and symmetric (
14
).

2.2.2
Currents and charges

It is clear that the bulk theory charge
Q_{B}=\int_{\Sigma}J_{B}^{0}
of the aforementioned symmetry is, in general, no longer conserved after introducing a boundary, since the constant-time slice
\Sigma
now has a boundary
\partial\Sigma
. As a physical interpretation, the bulk current “leaks” from the boundary, and this “leakage” must be compensated by a boundary term. More precisely, what we need in addition to the bulk current
J^{\mu}_{B}
is a
boundary current

J_{\partial}^{\hat{\mu}}
which lives on the boundary of the full theory, such that the equations

\partial_{\mu}J_{B}^{\mu}=0,\quad\partial_{\hat{\mu}}J_{\partial}^{\hat{\mu}}=J_{B}^{n}|_{\partial}

(16)

are satisfied. We also introduce the
total
conserved current
2
2

2

See appendix
D
for details on
\delta
-distributions at the boundary.

J^{\mu}_{\text{full}}=J_{B}^{\mu}+\delta(x^{n})\mathcal{P}^{\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}{\hat{\mu}}}J^{\hat{\mu}}_{\partial},

(17)

where
\mathcal{P}^{\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}{\hat{\mu}}}
is a projector/embedding with
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{\phantom{n}{\hat{\mu}}}\!=0
,
\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\nu}}_{\phantom{{\hat{\nu}}}{\hat{\mu}}}\!=\delta^{\hat{\nu}}_{\phantom{{\hat{\nu}}}{\hat{\mu}}}
. The conservation equations (
16
) can be expressed as:

\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{\text{full}}=\delta(x^{n})J^{n}_{\text{full}}.

(18)

Note that the (boundary) conservation equation might only hold modulo boundary conditions.
The full conserved charge of the theory is then given by

Q=\int_{\Sigma}J^{0}_{B}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}J^{0}_{\partial}=\int_{\Sigma}J_{\text{full}}^{0},

(19)

whose conservation is easy to see from (
16
).

Just as in pure bulk theories, there is more than one current leading to the same conserved charge associated to a given symmetry. Transformations of the currents that preserve the conservation equations and the charges are called the
improvements
of the currents.

For a pure bulk theory, an improvement locally takes the form

J_{B}^{\mu}\mapsto\tilde{J}_{B}^{\mu}=J_{B}^{\mu}+\partial_{\nu}M^{[\mu\nu]},

(20)

which preserves the conservation equations and charges:
\partial_{\mu}\tilde{J}_{B}^{\mu}=0
and
\tilde{Q}_{B}=\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{J}_{B}^{0}\equiv Q_{B}
.

For a theory with boundary, an improvement takes the form

\begin{Bmatrix}J_{B}^{\mu}\\
J_{\partial}^{\hat{\mu}}\end{Bmatrix}\mapsto\begin{Bmatrix}\tilde{J}_{B}^{\mu}=J_{B}^{\mu}+\partial_{\nu}M^{[\mu\nu]}\\
\tilde{J}_{\partial}^{\hat{\mu}}=J_{\partial}^{\hat{\mu}}+M^{n{\hat{\mu}}}+\partial_{\hat{\nu}}m^{[{\hat{\mu}}{\hat{\nu}}]}\end{Bmatrix},

(21)

which preserves the conservation equations (
16
) and the charge (
19
). The improvement of the bulk current induces an improvement on the boundary current, and the boundary current may be further improved by a pure boundary improvement.

Note that it is sometimes possible to completely “improve away” the boundary part of the conserved current, in particular if there are no degrees of freedom on the boundary. In that case the bulk charge
Q_{B}
is conserved even in the presence of a boundary, but then it is, in general, sensitive to bulk improvements. This is the approach of the authors of
DiPietro:2015zia
.

2.3
Noether’s theorem on manifolds with boundary

Now that we have discussed the properties of conserved currents and charges in boundary theories, let us investigate how we can compute them in a particular model. We present a modification of Noether’s theorem that yields bulk and boundary currents in the sense we defined above. The special case of a theory with boundary (and boundary terms) but without boundary dynamics is discussed in detail in
DiPietro:2015zia
.

2.3.1
Currents and charges without boundary

For completeness, let us quickly repeat Noether’s theorem in pure bulk theories. A symmetry is an off-shell transformation of the fields that leaves the action invariant:

\delta_{\text{sym}}S=0.

(22)

If the transformation is rigid (i.e. leaves spacetime invariant) and assuming that fields “fall off” at infinity the above condition is equivalent to

\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{B}=\partial_{\mu}V^{\mu}

(23)

for some bulk vector field
V^{\mu}
. On the other hand, a generic variation of the Lagrangian is also given by

\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{B}=\operatorname{EoM}^{B}[\Phi]\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi+\partial_{\mu}\big(\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\Phi|_{\partial})}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi\big)\overset{\text{\tiny on-shell}}{=}\partial_{\mu}\big(\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\Phi|_{\partial})}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi\big).

(24)

We find that on-shell
0=\partial_{\mu}(V^{\mu}-\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\Phi)}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi)
, thus the
bulk Noether current

J_{B}^{\mu}=-\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{B}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\Phi)}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi+V^{\mu},\quad\partial_{\mu}J_{B}^{\mu}=0,

(25)

is divergence-free (on-shell). Since there is no boundary present, divergence-freeness implies the conservation of the charge

Q_{B}=\int_{\Sigma}J_{B}^{0},

(26)

where
\Sigma
is a constant-time slice of
M
, since
\partial_{0}Q_{B}=\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{0}J^{0}=-\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{i}J^{i}=0
.

2.3.2
Currents and charges with boundary

As we restricted to symmetries of boundary theories that come from a bulk theory,
J_{B}^{\mu}
from (
25
) is still a valid divergence-free current by the same argument as above, so we can use it as the bulk part of the full current (
17
). The task at hand is to now find a boundary current which satisfies
\partial_{\hat{\mu}}J_{\partial}^{\hat{\mu}}=J_{B}^{n}|_{\partial}
. We want to apply a similar strategy as in the pure bulk theory: compare the (off-shell) symmetry variation of the action with an on-shell variation. On the off-shell side we get

0=\delta_{\text{sym}}S|_{G=0}=\int_{M}\partial_{\mu}V^{\mu}+\int_{\partial M}\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{\partial}|_{G=0}=\int_{\partial M}[V^{n}+\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{\partial}]_{G=0},

(27)

which implies that

[V^{n}+\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{\partial}]_{G=0}=\partial_{\hat{\mu}}K^{\hat{\mu}}

(28)

for some boundary vector field
K^{\hat{\mu}}
.

On the on-shell side, we now use equations of motion
and
the stationarity condition (
10
). By varying the boundary Lagrangian directly and assuming
G[\ldots]|_{\partial}=0
we get

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{\partial}

\displaystyle=\left[\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial\Phi|_{\partial}}-\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\Phi|_{\partial})}\right]\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi|_{\partial}+\operatorname{EoM}^{\partial}[X]\delta_{\text{sym}}X

(29)

\displaystyle+\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\big(\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}X)}\delta_{\text{sym}}X+\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\Phi|_{\partial})}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi|_{\partial}\big).

To rewrite the first term, let us plug in
\delta=\delta_{\text{sym}}
into (
10
) (still assuming
G[\ldots]=0
) and use the definition of the bulk Noether current (
25
) to rewrite it:

\partial_{\hat{\mu}}A^{\hat{\mu}}\overset{\text{\tiny on-shell}}{=}\left[\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial\Phi|_{\partial}}-\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\Phi|_{\partial})}\right]\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi|_{\partial}+[V^{n}-J_{B}^{n}]_{\partial}.

(30)

Plugging this into the previous equation and going on-shell, we get

\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}^{\partial}\overset{\text{\tiny on-shell}}{=}[J_{B}^{n}-V^{n}]_{\partial}+\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\big(A^{\hat{\mu}}+\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}X)}\delta_{\text{sym}}X+\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\Phi|_{\partial})}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi|_{\partial}\big).

(31)

We can now compare this on-shell variation to the off-shell variation in (
28
) and see

J_{B}^{n}|_{\partial}=\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\big(K^{\hat{\mu}}-A^{\hat{\mu}}-\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}X)}\delta_{\text{sym}}X-\tfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\partial}}{\partial(\partial_{\hat{\mu}}\Phi|_{\partial})}\delta_{\text{sym}}\Phi|_{\partial}).

(32)

3
Boundary supercurrent multiplets in 3D

We want to study supercurrent multiplets of theories on manifolds with boundary. In particular, we consider the special case of bulk theories with 3D
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry, broken to 2D
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
due to the boundary. While our discussion is limited to this particular case, the strategy is expected to work in greater generality.
We start by recalling the definitions and some facts about supercurrent multiplets, following
Dumitrescu:2011iu
(see also
Magro:2001aj
for a connection to a superspace Noether procedure).

The defining properties of a supercurrent multiplet are:

(a)

The energy-momentum tensor
(T^{B})^{\mu\nu}
should be a component of the multiplet. It is also the only component with spin 2.

(b)

The supercurrents, i.e. conserved currents associated to supersymmetry, are components of the multiplet. They are the only components with spin
3/2
. No component other than the supercurrents and the energy-momentum tensor are allowed to have spin larger than
1
.

(c)

The supercurrent multiplet is not unique: it allows for (supersymmetrically complete) improvements of its components.

(d)

The multiplet is
indecomposable
, so it may have non-trivial submultiplets, but it may not be decomposed into two independent decoupled multiplets.

The components of a supercurrent multiplet (in particular, the conserved currents) are only unique up to improvements. However, improving one component and not the others breaks the structure of the multiplet. Hence, to consistently improve the supercurrent multiplet, we must restrict to improvements of all components which are related in a certain way (specifically, the improvement terms have to form a supersymmetry multiplet themselves; details are in
Dumitrescu:2011iu
). In other words, if one is given two components (e.g. a supercurrent and an energy-momentum tensor), one may have to improve one of them such that they can be part of the same supercurrent multiplet. We say two conserved currents which have been improved such that they are part of a consistent supercurrent multiplet are in the same
improvement frame
.

For some theories the supercurrent multiplet may be improved into a smaller multiplet (e.g. to obtain an
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet or a Ferrara-Zumino multiplet). There are still improvements that preserve this smaller multiplet
Dumitrescu:2011iu
;
Komargodski:2010rb
;
Dedushenko:2015opz
. We will recall the case of 3D
\mathcal{N}=2
theories in more detail.

We will focus on three-dimensional theories with two-dimensional boundaries. In this section we recall the defining relations and properties of supercurrent multiplets in three-dimensional bulk theories with

3.2
In theories with boundary

The introduction of a boundary affects supercurrent multiplets in two obvious ways.

First, supersymmetry is broken to a subalgebra, where our main focus will be 2D

Second, a boundary changes the conserved currents of

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{B}+\delta(x^{n})\mathcal{P}^{\hat{\mu}}_{\phantom{{\hat{\mu}}}\mu}\mathcal{S}_{\hat{\mu}}^{\partial},

3.4.1

The supercurrent multiplets that satisfy the bulk (

In the case of a 2D theory with

-multiplet is given by superfields
(\mathcal{S}_{++}^{(0,2)},\mathcal{W}_{-}^{(0,2)},\mathcal{T}_{----}^{(0,2)},C)
and corresponding defining constraints in 2D
{(0,2)}

Dumitrescu:2011iu
;

{(0,2)}

\widetilde{T}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{++}

\displaystyle=T_{++}+\tfrac{i}{2}\partial_{+}j_{+},

(79)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{+-}

\displaystyle=T_{+-}-\tfrac{i}{2}\partial_{-}j_{+},

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{--}

\displaystyle=T_{--}-\tfrac{i}{2}\partial_{-}j_{-},

\displaystyle\{\overline{Q}_{+},\cdots\}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{T}_{+{\hat{\mu}}},

(80)

\displaystyle\{\overline{Q}_{+},\widetilde{T}_{--}\}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{but }\{\overline{Q}_{+},\cdots\}\neq\widetilde{T}_{--}.

In other words, the components of the twisted energy-momentum tensor are
\overline{Q}_{+}
-cohomology elements, and

Secondly, it is well known for

\overline{D}_{+}

\overline{D}_{+}

3.4.3
Integrated currents and multiplets

Equation (
86

-symmetry such that it is a
\overline{Q}_{+}
-cohomology element. However, there is a different point of view which is helpful here: A three-dimensional quantum field theory with a finite number of fields can instead be regarded as a two-dimensional quantum field theory with an infinite number of fields. More precisely, instead of viewing bulk fields, loosely speaking, as maps
\partial M\times\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}\to T
, we view them as maps
\partial M\to\{\text{maps: }\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}\to T\}

Dimofte:2017tpi
;
Bullimore:2016nji
. Now, instead of considering separate bulk and boundary actions, we can write a single Lagrangian for the full theory:

\mathcal{L}^{\text{int.}}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}^{\partial}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}}\mathrm{d}x^{n}\mathcal{L}^{B};\quad S=\int_{\partial M}\mathrm{d}x^{N-1}\mathcal{L}^{\text{int.}}.

(87)

We see that the action is the same as before, but integration along
x^{n}
is now a conceptually different operation: before, it used to be an integral on the spacetime on which the field theory is defined; now it is an operation on the new target space (i.e. a functional). The integration along
x^{n}
also translates to conserved currents: As the theory is now formally two-dimensional, applying Noether’s theorem to the above Lagrangian yields a two-dimensional current of the form

J_{\hat{\mu}}^{\text{int.}}=J_{\hat{\mu}}^{\partial}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}}\hskip-8.5359pt\mathrm{d}x^{n}J_{\hat{\mu}}^{B}.

(88)

We see that its conserved charge

Q=\int_{\partial\Sigma}J_{\text{int.}}^{0}

(89)

is identical to the one belonging to the local current (
19
). This also provides an argument why the integrated currents are “natural” from the point of view of the three-dimensional QFT: To find the conserved charge of a current, one has to integrate all spatial directions, and the integrated current is an “intermediate step” of this integration.
The conservation equations (
33
) now take the familiar form

\partial^{\hat{\mu}}J_{\hat{\mu}}^{\text{int.}}=0,

(90)

where boundary conditions are possibly used.
Extending supersymmetrically, we introduce the
integrated supercurrent multiplets
, in our conventions (recall, mixed indices correspond to the
\perp
-direction, which does not appear here, see (
193

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\alpha}^{\text{int.}}=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\alpha}^{\partial}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}}\hskip-8.5359pt\mathrm{d}x^{\perp}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\alpha}^{B}.

(91)

86

\displaystyle\overline{D}_{+}(\mathcal{R}^{\text{int.}{(2)}}_{--}+2i\partial_{-}\mathcal{R}^{\text{int.}{(0)}}_{--})

\displaystyle=0,

(92)

\displaystyle\overline{D}_{+}(\mathcal{R}^{\text{int.}{(2)}}_{++}+2i\partial_{-}\mathcal{R}^{\text{int.}{(0)}}_{++})

The general arguments from

3.5

We look at three-dimensional theories with
\mathcal{N}=2

{(0,2)}

First we study 3D bulk supercurrent multiplets, in particular the
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet. Its structure remains unchanged, as the bulk parts of conserved currents are still divergence-free; we merely decompose the bulk multiplets into their

4
Landau-Ginzburg model

4.1
Bulk theory

We now study a particular model where the framework we developed above can be applied. Our bulk theory should be a 3D
\mathcal{N}=2
Landau-Ginzburg model which lives on three-dimensional Minkowski space. At a later point, we will introduce a boundary and restrict the theory to the half-space

M=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{1,2}}{x^{\smash{\perp}}\coloneqq x^{1}\leq 0}.

(98)

We will formulate the bulk theory in 3D
\mathcal{N}=2
superspace. A generic chiral field is given by

\Phi_{3D}(x,\theta,\overline{\theta})=\phi(y)+\sqrt{2}\theta\psi(y)+\theta\theta F(y),\qquad y^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-i\theta\gamma^{\mu}\overline{\theta},

(99)

where, as usual,
\phi
is a complex scalar field,
\psi_{\alpha}
is a complex fermion, and
F
is a complex auxiliary field. Under the bulk supersymmetry, the components transform as follows:

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}\phi

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\epsilon\psi,

(100)

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}\psi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\epsilon_{\alpha}F-\sqrt{2}i(\gamma^{\mu}\overline{\epsilon})_{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\phi,

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}F

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}i\overline{\epsilon}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi.

Let us now consider the simplest non-trivial theory: a Landau-Ginzburg model of a single chiral superfield. Its kinetic (Kähler) term is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin.}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}\theta\,\Phi_{3D}\overline{\Phi}_{3D}=-\partial_{\mu}\overline{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi+\tfrac{i}{2}(\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi)-\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)+\overline{F}F+\tfrac{1}{4}\partial^{2}(\overline{\phi}\phi).

(101)

At a later point, the term
\tfrac{1}{4}\partial^{2}(\overline{\phi}\phi)
will be removed; as it is a total derivative, it does not influence the bulk theory, but will be relevant once a boundary is introduced.

The superpotential is of the well-known form

\mathcal{L}_{W}=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta\,W(\Phi_{3D})+\text{cc.}=W^{\prime}(\phi)F-\tfrac{1}{2}W^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\psi\psi+\text{cc.}

(102)

The bulk equations of motion are given by

\overline{D}^{2}\overline{\Phi}=-4W^{\prime}(\Phi)\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{aligned} 0&=\overline{F}+W^{\prime}(\phi)\\
0&=\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\overline{\phi}+W^{\prime\prime}(\phi)F-\tfrac{1}{2}W^{\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)\psi\psi\\
0&=i(\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi})_{\alpha}-W^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\psi_{\alpha}\end{aligned}\right\}.

(103)

4.2
Introducing a boundary and breaking to
{(0,2)}

As we already saw in section
3.3
, the supersymmetry algebra breaks at least to a 2D
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
(or 2D
\mathcal{N}={(1,1)}
, which we do not consider here) subalgebra when we introduce a boundary. Let us study sufficient conditions to preserve exactly
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
.

4.2.1
Decomposition of the bulk fields

Under the
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
subalgebra, the chiral field
\Phi_{3D}
decomposes into a
{(0,2)}
chiral multiplet and a Fermi multiplet. More details of this decomposition are written in appendix
B.1
. The resulting
{(0,2)}
-superfields are

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\phi+\sqrt{2}\theta^{+}\psi_{+}-2i\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{+}\partial_{+}\phi,

(104)

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=\psi_{-}-\sqrt{2}\theta^{+}F-2i\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{+}\partial_{+}\psi_{-}+\sqrt{2}i\overline{\theta}^{+}\partial_{\perp}\phi-2i\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{+}\partial_{\perp}\psi_{+}.

The Fermi superfield satisfies

\overline{D}_{+}\Psi=\sqrt{2}E_{\Psi},\quad E_{\Psi}=-i\partial_{\perp}\Phi,

(105)

and the chirality condition reads

\overline{D}_{+}\Phi=0.

(106)

The supersymmetry variation in the smaller algebra is given by
\delta_{\text{sym}}\coloneqq\epsilon Q_{+}-\overline{\epsilon}\overline{Q}_{+}
(cf. equation (
203
) for the definition of the superspace operators). The component fields now transform as

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}\phi

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\epsilon\psi_{+},

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}\psi_{+}

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{2}i\overline{\epsilon}\partial_{+}\phi,

(107)

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}F

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{2}i\overline{\epsilon}\partial_{+}\psi_{-}+\sqrt{2}i\overline{\epsilon}\partial_{\perp}\psi_{+},

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}\psi_{-}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}\epsilon F+\sqrt{2}i\overline{\epsilon}\partial_{\perp}\phi,

which is precisely the restriction of (
100
) to the
{(0,2)}
-subalgebra, given by choosing
\epsilon^{\alpha}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
\epsilon\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
We may rewrite the Lagrangian in terms of
{(0,2)}
-superspace:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin.}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta^{+}\Big[i\overline{\Phi}\partial_{-}\Phi-i\partial_{-}\overline{\Phi}\Phi+\overline{\Psi}\Psi

(108)

\displaystyle+\partial_{\perp}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{+}\partial_{\perp}(\overline{\Phi}\Phi)+\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\theta^{+}\overline{\Phi}\Psi+\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\overline{\theta}^{+}\overline{\Psi}\Phi\right)\Big],

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{W}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int\mathrm{d}\theta^{+}\Psi W^{\prime}(\Phi)+\text{cc.}

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin.}}
consists of two parts. The first part is invariant under
{(0,2)}
-supersymmetry even in the presence of a boundary, as its
{(0,2)}
-variation is just a total
x^{+}
-derivative. The second term (trivially) transforms into an
x^{\perp}
-derivative, so it breaks
{(0,2)}
-supersymmetry in the presence of a boundary, and hence dictates part of the “boundary compensating term” (cf. discussion at the end of section
2.2.1
).

The equations of motion may again be written as superfield equations, now in
{(0,2)}
-superspace:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=2i\partial_{-}\overline{D}_{+}\overline{\Phi}+\sqrt{2}i\partial_{\perp}\overline{\Psi}-\sqrt{2}W^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)\Psi,

(109)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\overline{D}_{+}\overline{\Psi}+\sqrt{2}W^{\prime}(\Phi).

4.2.2
Recovering partial supersymmetry

As it stands, the pure bulk action (
108
) is not even
{(0,2)}
-supersymmetric in the presence of a boundary:

\delta_{\text{sym}}S=\int_{M}\delta_{\text{sym}}(\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin.}}+\mathcal{L}_{W})=\int_{M}\partial_{\mu}(V^{\mu}_{\text{kin.}}+V^{\mu}_{W})=\int_{\partial M}(V^{\perp}_{\text{kin.}}+V^{\perp}_{W}),

(110)

which, in general, does not vanish.
To recover at least
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
supersymmetry, we must compensate these bulk variations.

For the kinetic term, we can add a boundary compensating term
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\text{kin.}}
to the boundary Lagrangian (in a boundary-condition-independent way) in the spirit of
Brunner:2003dc
;
DiPietro:2015zia
. The boundary term is precisely minus the total
\perp
-derivative from the bulk Lagrangian in
{(0,2)}
-superspace (
108
):

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\text{kin.}}\coloneqq-\tfrac{1}{4}\partial_{\perp}(\overline{\phi}\phi)-\tfrac{i}{2}\overline{\psi}_{+}\psi_{-}+\tfrac{i}{2}\overline{\psi}_{-}\psi_{+}.

(111)

We see that the
-\tfrac{1}{4}\partial_{\perp}(\overline{\phi}\phi)
cancels the bulk total derivative in
x^{\perp}
direction when pulled into the bulk. This means that we can just drop
\tfrac{1}{4}\partial^{2}(\overline{\phi}\phi)
from the bulk and
-\tfrac{1}{4}\partial_{\perp}(\overline{\phi}\phi)
from the boundary simultaneously, leaving us with bulk and boundary Lagrangians:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{B}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\mu}\overline{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi+\tfrac{i}{2}(\psi\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi})-\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\psi\gamma^{\mu}\overline{\psi})

(112)

\displaystyle+\overline{F}F+W^{\prime}(\phi)F+\overline{W}^{\prime}(\overline{\phi})\overline{F}-\tfrac{1}{2}W^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\psi\psi+\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{W}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\phi})\overline{\psi}\overline{\psi},

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{kin.}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{i}{2}\overline{\psi}_{+}\psi_{-}+\tfrac{i}{2}\overline{\psi}_{-}\psi_{+}=-\tfrac{i}{2}\overline{\psi}\psi.

For the bulk superpotential term, the supersymmetry variation yields

\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}_{W}=\partial_{\perp}\left(-i\int\mathrm{d}\theta^{+}\overline{\epsilon}W(\Phi)+\text{cc.}\right)+\partial_{+}(\dots)=\partial_{\perp}(-i\overline{\epsilon}\psi_{+}W^{\prime}(\phi)+\text{cc.})+\partial_{+}(\dots),

(113)

where the right-hand side needs to be compensated. To do this in a boundary-condition-independent way, one can use a bulk R-symmetry (see
DiPietro:2015zia
), or one can add boundary degrees of freedom, which we will discuss in detail.

4.2.3
Boundary Fermi multiplet and factorization

To compensate the superpotential term variation (
113
), we introduce a 2D boundary Fermi multiplet with
E
- and
J
-potential terms, analogously to
Kapustin:2003ga
;
Brunner:2003dc
;
Herbst:2008jq
;
Lazaroiu:2003zi
, where a 1D Fermi multiplet was used to compensate bulk 2D superpotential terms (see also
Yoshida:2014ssa
;
Gadde:2013sca
for equivalent, three-dimensional examples). The general superspace expansion of a 2D Fermi multiplet is given by

H=\eta-\sqrt{2}\theta^{+}G-2i\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{+}\partial_{+}\eta-\sqrt{2}\overline{\theta}^{+}E(\phi)+2\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{+}E^{\prime}(\phi)\psi_{+},

(114)

so it has an
E
-potential of

\overline{D}_{+}H=\sqrt{2}E(\Phi).

(115)

The
{(0,2)}
-supersymmetry variation of the components is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}\eta

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}(\epsilon G+\overline{\epsilon}E),

(116)

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{sym}}G

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\overline{\epsilon}(2i\partial_{+}\eta-E^{\prime}\psi_{+}).

Its (boundary) Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{H}

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta^{+}\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{H}H-\int\mathrm{d}\theta^{+}\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}J(\Phi)H+\int\mathrm{d}\overline{\theta}^{+}\tfrac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\overline{J}(\overline{\Phi})\overline{H}

(117)

\displaystyle=i\overline{\eta}\partial_{+}\eta-i\partial_{+}\overline{\eta}\eta-E^{\prime}\overline{\eta}\psi_{+}-\overline{E}^{\prime}\overline{\psi}_{+}\eta+iJ^{\prime}\eta\psi_{+}-i\overline{J}^{\prime}\overline{\psi}_{+}\overline{\eta}-|E|^{2}-|J|^{2}.

It consists of a kinetic term, two boundary potentials
E
and
J
of the
bulk
chiral field
\phi
, and interactions between the boundary and bulk fermions. The boundary equations of motion are

\overline{D}_{+}\overline{H}+\sqrt{2}iJ(\Phi)=0\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{aligned} G&=i\overline{J}\\
2i\partial_{+}\eta&=E^{\prime}(\phi)\psi_{+}-i\overline{J}^{\prime}(\overline{\phi})\overline{\psi}_{+}\end{aligned}\right\}.

(118)

The supersymmetry variation is

\delta_{\text{sym}}\mathcal{L}_{H}=i\int\mathrm{d}\theta^{+}\overline{\epsilon}J(\Phi)E(\Phi)+\text{cc.}+\partial_{+}(\dots).

(119)

We thus find that in case of a
matrix factorization

W(\Phi)|_{\partial}=E(\Phi)J(\Phi)|_{\partial},

(120)

the bulk term from (
113
) will be compensated precisely, and
{(0,2)}
-supersymmetry is preserved. As stated before
Witten:1993yc
and can be seen from (
119
), a pure 2D
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
theory must fulfill
E\cdot J=0
in order to preserve supersymmetry. However, in our case, the “failure” of the boundary Fermi multiplet to meet this condition cancels the failure of the bulk theory to preserve
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
-supersymmetry at the boundary.

The total action of the factorized Landau-Ginzburg model then reads

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\mathcal{L}^{B}+\int_{\partial M}\mathcal{L}^{\partial}

(121)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\bigg\{\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta^{+}\big[i\overline{\Phi}\partial_{-}\Phi-i\partial_{-}\overline{\Phi}\Phi+\overline{\Psi}\Psi+\partial_{\perp}\Delta\big]-\sqrt{2}\int\mathrm{d}\theta^{+}\Psi W(\Phi)+\text{cc.}\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\bigg\{\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta^{+}\big[-\Delta|_{\partial}+\overline{H}H\big]-\sqrt{2}i\int\mathrm{d}\theta^{+}J(\Phi)H+\text{cc.}\bigg\},

where
\tfrac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta^{+}\Delta=\tfrac{i}{2\sqrt{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta^{+}(\theta^{+}\overline{\Phi}\Psi+\overline{\theta}^{+}\overline{\Psi}\Phi)=\tfrac{i}{2}(\overline{\psi}_{+}\psi_{-}-\overline{\psi}_{-}\psi_{+})
(cf. (
112
)).
After using the algebraic equations of motion, we get the following component expansions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{B}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\mu}\overline{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi+\tfrac{i}{2}(\psi\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi})-\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\psi\gamma^{\mu}\overline{\psi})

(122)

\displaystyle-|W(\phi)|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}W^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\psi\psi+\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{W}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\phi})\overline{\psi}\overline{\psi},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\partial}

\displaystyle=i\overline{\eta}\partial_{+}\eta-i\partial_{+}\overline{\eta}\eta-|J|^{2}-|E|^{2}-\overline{E}^{\prime}\overline{\psi}_{+}\eta-E^{\prime}\overline{\eta}\psi_{+}

\displaystyle-iJ^{\prime}\psi_{+}\eta-i\overline{J}^{\prime}\overline{\psi}_{+}\overline{\eta}-\tfrac{i}{2}(\overline{\psi}_{+}\psi_{-}-\overline{\psi}_{-}\psi_{+})|_{\partial}.

The
{(0,2)}
-variation of the total action is zero, hence
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
supersymmetry is preserved in a boundary-condition-independent way.

4.2.4
Symmetric boundary conditions

In any theory with a boundary it is necessary to introduce boundary conditions such that the action can be made stationary. Requiring the boundary condition to be compatible with the
{\mathcal{N}=(0,2)}
subalgebra in the sense of (
13
) further restricts the number of options. We now discuss some explicit boundary conditions for our LG model. We consider boundary conditions without superpotential (previously discussed in
Dimofte:2017tpi
) and with superpotential separately.

Without superpotential

•

(generalized) Dirichlet
:
\Phi=0
or more generally
\Phi=c
(in components
\phi=c
and
\psi_{+}=0
) is a symmetric boundary condition (the action may require the addition of some boundary terms to be symmetric).

•

Neumann
:
\Psi=0
(in components
\partial_{\perp}\phi=0
and
\psi_{-}=0
) is also symmetric. It is also the dynamical boundary condition in the sense of (
12
) for the action (
112
) without superpotential. Note that one can also obtain the (generalized) Dirichlet as a dynamical boundary condition by adding appropriate boundary terms
Dimofte:2017tpi
.

•

Mixed conditions
: In models with more than one 3D chiral superfield, we may assign Dirichlet conditions to some and Neumann conditions to others
Dimofte:2017tpi
.

With superpotential

•

(generalized) Dirichlet
: Setting
\Phi=c
is symmetric and also statically cancels the supervariation of the potential (
113
) (albeit in a boundary-condition-
dependent
way). However, if
W^{\prime}(c)|_{\partial}\neq 0
, supersymmetry is broken spontaneously, as the vacuum expectation value of
\psi_{-}
then transforms non-trivially under supersymmetry.
11
11

11

We note that if there is a bulk
R
-symmetry, one can also compensate the superpotential variation boundary-condition-independently, see
DiPietro:2015zia
. However, in the case of one chiral field, one can then only impose Dirichlet boundary conditions, as the Neumann condition with
W\neq 0
is not symmetric.

•

Mixed conditions
: Setting
\Psi=0
(Neumann) is only symmetric if
W^{\prime}(\phi)|_{\partial}=0
. For one bosonic field, this holds only if
W=0
, as
\phi
is unconstrained on the boundary. If
W\neq 0
and the theory has more than one chiral superfield, one can assign Dirichlet conditions to some and Neumann conditions to others while maintaining supersymmetry (a requirement the authors in

call “sufficiently Dirichlet”).

•

Factorized Neumann
: If we introduce additional degrees of freedom on the boundary as in section
4.2.3
, we may again choose dynamical boundary conditions. In the case without superpotential this lead to the Neumann boundary condition. For the action (
122
) the dynamical boundary condition is the analog of the Neumann boundary condition, now with superpotential:

\overline{\Psi}=-i\overline{H}E^{\prime}(\Phi)-HJ^{\prime}(\Phi)\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{\psi}_{-}&=-i\overline{\eta}E^{\prime}-\eta J^{\prime},\\
\partial_{\perp}\overline{\phi}&=-\overline{E}E^{\prime}-\overline{J}J^{\prime}-(\overline{\eta}E^{\prime\prime}-i\eta J^{\prime\prime})\psi_{+}\end{aligned}\right\}.

(123)

One can check that it is indeed symmetric if the factorization condition (
120
) is met. We use this boundary condition in our computations for currents and current multiplets. This choice of boundary condition in fact encodes a collection of boundary conditions labeled by the choices of
matrix factorizations
of
W
(since the boundary condition depends explicitly on
E
and
J
).

Similarly to

\displaystyle[\partial_{0}\phi(x),\overline{\phi}(y)]

\displaystyle\{\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}(x),\psi_{\beta}(y)\}

(163)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha},S_{\beta\mu}\}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\overline{C}(\gamma_{\mu})_{\alpha\beta}+i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}\gamma^{\nu}_{\alpha\beta}\overline{Y}^{\rho}.

(164)

In the absence of a boundary, integration of these relations yields the expected supersymmetry algebra. However, this changes under the introduction of a boundary for two reasons: First, there are additional degrees of freedom at the boundary which appear in
\mathcal{Q}
and
S
, and second, there are boundary contributions from pure
bulk
terms as well.

Notice that the half-integrated commutators like
\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha},S_{\alpha\mu}\}
or
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{\alpha},S_{\beta\mu}\}
are affected by improvements, but the fully integrated commutators like
\{\mathcal{Q}_{+},\mathcal{Q}_{+}\}
and
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},\mathcal{Q}_{+}\}
must be invariant under them. This is easy to see in pure bulk theories, but with a boundary, it holds as well. A generic commutator of a charge
\mathcal{Q}
and a current
J^{\mu}
improves as follows:

\{\mathcal{Q},J^{\prime\mu}(x)\}=\{\mathcal{Q},J^{\mu}(x)\}+\{\mathcal{Q},\partial_{\nu}M^{[\mu\nu]}(x)\}+\delta(x^{n})\{\mathcal{Q},M^{n{\hat{\mu}}}(x)\}.

(165)

Notice that
\mathcal{Q}
is a charge and thus invariant under improvements. If
\mathcal{Q}
commutes with
\partial_{\nu}
, we find that the integrated algebra
\int\mathrm{d}x\{\mathcal{Q},J^{\prime\mu}(x)\}
is unchanged.

5.1
General properties of the supercharge

Let us now restrict to the case
\alpha=+
,
\beta=+
as the other supercharge and -current will be broken by the introduction of the boundary. The supercurrent from (
129
), (
131
) (which we repeat for convenience)

S_{+\mu}(x)=-\sqrt{2}i(\gamma_{\mu}\overline{\psi})_{+}\overline{W}^{\prime}(\overline{\phi})+\sqrt{2}(\psi\gamma_{\mu}\gamma^{\nu})_{+}\partial_{\nu}\overline{\phi}-\sqrt{2}\,\delta(x^{\perp})\delta_{\mu}^{-}(\overline{J}\overline{\eta}-i\overline{E}\eta)

(166)

integrates to the full supercharge

\mathcal{Q}_{+}=\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{2}\big(\psi_{-}\partial_{\perp}\overline{\phi}+i\overline{\psi}_{-}\overline{W}^{\prime}(\overline{\phi})-2\psi_{+}\partial_{+}\overline{\phi}\big)+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\sqrt{2}(\overline{J}\overline{\eta}-i\overline{E}\eta),

(167)

where
\int_{\Sigma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,0]}\mathrm{d}x^{2}\mathrm{d}x^{\perp}
and
\int_{\partial\Sigma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x^{2}
. Let us start by studying the action of the bulk part of
\mathcal{Q}_{+}
on component fields. Analogous to
DiPietro:2015zia
, the action of
[\mathcal{Q}_{+,{\text{bulk}}},\boldsymbol{\cdot}]
on bulk component fields (
\phi
,
\overline{\phi}
,
\psi_{\pm}
,
\overline{\psi}_{\pm}
) is the same as in the pure bulk theory with one exception: The commutator with
\partial_{0}\phi
receives an extra boundary term

[\mathcal{Q}_{+,{\text{bulk}}},\partial_{0}\phi(x)]=\sqrt{2}i\partial_{0}\psi_{+}(x)+\sqrt{2}i\delta(x^{\perp})\psi_{-}(x).

(168)

This result can be derived using the quantization conditions (
162
) and the delta distribution rule in appendix
D
. Notice that for
\nu\neq 0
, the identity
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\nu}}[\mathcal{Q}_{+},\phi(x)]=[\mathcal{Q}_{+},\partial_{\nu}\phi(x)]
is only true if the correct delta distribution rules derived in appendix
D
are applied.

The boundary part
\mathcal{Q}_{+,{\text{bdy}}}
has a trivial action on the bulk component fields and on
\partial_{\nu}\phi
,
\nu\neq 0
. The action on the boundary fermions
\eta
,
\overline{\eta}
is as expected. Again, we get extra terms for
\partial_{0}\phi
. Overall, the full charge acts as follows on
\partial_{0}\phi
:

[\mathcal{Q}_{+},\partial_{0}\phi(x)]=\sqrt{2}i\partial_{0}\psi+\sqrt{2}(i\overline{\eta}\overline{J}^{\prime}+\eta\overline{E}^{\prime}+i\psi_{-})\delta(x^{\perp}).

(169)

We see that the boundary contribution vanishes under the symmetric boundary condition (
123
), which is an independent way of verifying this boundary condition. However, for reasons outlined above, we will not impose this condition statically and thus treat this extra term like a genuine new contribution.

5.2
The
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}},S\}
commutator

We now would like to compute
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},S_{+\mu}(x)\}
in the presence of a boundary and verify that it integrates to the expected preserved algebra
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},\mathcal{Q}_{+}\}=-4P_{+}
(see section
3.3
). We expect the known terms (
163
) in the bulk, and extra terms at the boundary. As both
{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+}
and
S_{+\mu}(x)
have bulk and boundary parts, there are four combinations from which new terms may arise: bulk-bulk, bulk-boundary, boundary-bulk, and boundary-boundary.

5.2.1
Boundary contributions from bulk-bulk terms

Let us now check how the changes introduced by the boundary affect the half-integrated algebra: the bulk-bulk term

\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{\alpha,{\text{bulk}}},S_{\beta\mu,{\text{bulk}}}\}=\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{\alpha},-\sqrt{2}i(\gamma_{\mu}\overline{\psi})_{\beta}\overline{W}^{\prime}(\overline{\phi})+\sqrt{2}(\psi\gamma_{\mu}\gamma^{\nu})_{\beta}\partial_{\nu}\overline{\phi}\}

(170)

is affected by the changed relation (
168
) in the term
\partial_{0}\overline{\phi}
, where we get an extra boundary term

\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{\alpha,{\text{bulk}}},S_{\beta\mu,{\text{bulk}}}(x)\}=\text{known bulk terms \eqref{eq:bulk QbS commutator}}+\delta(x^{\perp})\underbrace{2i(\psi\gamma_{\mu}\gamma^{0})_{\beta}(\gamma^{n}\gamma^{0}\overline{\psi}(x))_{\alpha}}_{=:B_{\alpha\beta,\mu}},

(171)

which is also consistent with a similar result in
DiPietro:2015zia
. The component most relevant to us is
B_{++}^{\phantom{+}\phantom{+}0}
, as it appears in the integration of the part of the algebra that is unbroken. It can be rewritten to

B_{++}^{\phantom{+}\phantom{+}0}=2i\psi_{+}\overline{\psi}_{-}=i\overline{\psi}\psi-i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\perp}\psi=i\overline{\psi}\psi-ij^{\perp},

(172)

where we have inserted a bulk component of the supercurrent multiplet in the last equality, see appendix
C.5
. It is noteworthy that this boundary term is neither real nor imaginary.

5.2.2
Contributions from the boundary degrees of freedom

Again using the commutation relations but not boundary conditions, we find

\displaystyle\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+,{\text{bdy}}},S_{+,\mu,{\text{bdy}}}(x)\}

\displaystyle=-2\delta_{\mu}^{-}\delta(x^{\perp})(|J|^{2}+|E|^{2}),

(173a)

\displaystyle\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+,{\text{bulk}}},S_{+,{\hat{\mu}},{\text{bdy}}}(x)\}

\displaystyle=-2i\delta(x^{\perp})\delta_{\hat{\mu}}^{-}\overline{\psi}_{+}(x)\big(\overline{J}^{\prime}(x)\overline{\eta}(x)-i\overline{E}^{\prime}(x)\eta(x)\big),

(173b)

\displaystyle\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+,{\text{bdy}}},S_{+,\mu,{\text{bulk}}}(x)\}

\displaystyle=2i\delta(x^{\perp})\big(J^{\prime}(x)\eta(x)+iE^{\prime}(x)\overline{\eta}(x)\big)(\psi(x)\gamma_{\mu}\gamma^{0})_{+}.

(173c)

It is noteworthy that modulo boundary conditions, the third term cancels the boundary contribution of
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+,{\text{bulk}}},S_{+,\mu,{\text{bulk}}}(x)\}
. This is expected since both extra terms have their origins in the changed relation (
169
) which is identical to the original bulk relation modulo boundary conditions.

5.2.3
Integrating the algebra

As the integral of
S_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}
over a constant time slice yields the supercharge
\mathcal{Q}_{+}
, an integration of the commutator
\int\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},S_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}\}=\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},\mathcal{Q}_{+}\}
is a commutator which appears in the preserved supersymmetry algebra (
53
). We can thus check (
171
) and (
173
) by integrating
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},S_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}\}
and comparing the result to the known algebra. We will plug in the component expansions from appendix
C.5
.

Let us first check the imaginary part, which is zero on the expected right-hand side of the equation. Interesting contributions come only from
\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+,{\text{bulk}}},S_{+,{\text{bulk}}}^{\phantom{+}0}\}
, as all other contributions together are trivially real.

\operatorname{Im}\int_{\Sigma}\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},{S_{+}}^{0}\}=\int_{\Sigma}2(i\partial_{+}j_{0}-i\eta_{+0}\partial^{\rho}j_{\rho})+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\operatorname{Im}(B_{++}^{\phantom{+}\phantom{+}0})=\int_{\Sigma}i\partial_{\perp}j_{\perp}-\int_{\partial\Sigma}ij_{\perp}=0.

(174)

A similar computation in four dimensions was done in
DiPietro:2015zia
.

For the real part we get (as expected from (
163
))

\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},S_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}\}|_{\text{bulk}}=-\gamma_{22}^{\nu}\big(2T_{\nu 0}+\tfrac{1}{4}\epsilon_{\nu 0\rho}H^{\rho}\big)=-4({\widehat{T}}^{B})_{+}^{\phantom{+}0},

(175)

where
{\widehat{T}}^{B}
is the bulk Noether (non-symmetric) energy momentum tensor (
136
).
All boundary terms together (eqs. (
171
) and (
173
)) yield

\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},S_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}\}|_{\partial}=i\overline{\psi}\psi+2|J|^{2}+2|E|^{2}+2i\overline{\psi}_{+}\big(\overline{J}^{\prime}\overline{\eta}-i\overline{E}^{\prime}\eta\big)-2i\big(J^{\prime}\eta+iE^{\prime}\overline{\eta}\big)\psi_{+}=-4({\widehat{T}}^{\partial})_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}

(176)

using an explicit comparison to the non-symmetric boundary energy momentum tensor
{\widehat{T}}^{\partial}
(
137
) which belongs to the bulk Noether energy momentum tensor
{\widehat{T}}^{B}
. Overall, we find that

\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},\mathcal{Q}_{+}\}=\int_{\Sigma}\{{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+},S_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}\}=-4\int_{\Sigma}\big(({\widehat{T}}^{B})_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}+\delta(x^{\perp})({\widehat{T}}^{\partial})_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}\big)=-4P_{+},

(177)

which verifies the algebra. Notice that
P_{+}
is independent of improvements, thus we may use improved versions of the energy-momentum tensor to compute the right-hand side of the equality. However, on the level of the half-integrated algebra, we see that the Noether (
\mathcal{S}
-frame) supercurrent generates the non-symmetric Noether energy momentum tensor, and both are sensitive to improvements.

Let us emphasize again that this argument works
without
explicitly assuming boundary conditions and modifying the bulk fields in the presence of a boundary. Rather, we study the bulk theory without a boundary, then introduce it, and verify that the supersymmetry algebra is preserved by the equal-time commutators. Notice that without assuming any boundary conditions, the charges
\mathcal{Q}_{+}
,
{\overline{\mathcal{Q}}}_{+}
are not conserved. However, no reference to the specific choice of boundary conditions was made in this argument (although in this simple model with one bulk chiral field and one boundary Fermi, (
123
) is the only symmetric boundary condition compatible with stationarity).

5.3
The
\{\mathcal{Q},S\}
commutator

In a similar way, we can also verify that
\{\mathcal{Q}_{+},S_{+\mu}(x)\}
integrates to the expected algebra
\{\mathcal{Q}_{+},\mathcal{Q}_{+}\}=0
. In the bulk, we get from (
164
)

\{\mathcal{Q}_{+,{\text{bulk}}},S_{+\mu,{\text{bulk}}}(x)\}=i\epsilon_{\mu+\rho}(\gamma^{+})_{++}\overline{Y}^{\rho}=-8i\epsilon_{\mu+\rho}\partial^{\rho}\overline{W}.

(178)

From the commutation relations (
162
) we find that

\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha,{\text{bulk}}},S_{\beta\mu,{\text{bdy}}}\}
and
\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha,{\text{bdy}}},S_{\beta\mu,{\text{bulk}}}\}
are zero, but we do get a contribution from

\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha,{\text{bdy}}},S_{\beta\mu,{\text{bdy}}}(x)\}=4i\delta_{\mu}^{-}\delta(x^{\perp})\overline{J}(x)\overline{E}(x).

(179)

Integrating the relation, we find

\{\mathcal{Q}_{+},\mathcal{Q}_{+}\}=\int_{\Sigma}\{\mathcal{Q}_{+},S_{+}^{\phantom{+}0}(x)\}=4i\int_{\partial\Sigma}\big(\overline{W}-\overline{J}(x)\overline{E}(x)\big),

(180)

so if the factorization condition (
120
) is met, the algebra is preserved under the introduction of the boundary.

6
Conclusions and Outlook

In this paper we analyzed three-dimensional theories with
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry from various points of view: We constructed the symmetry currents by developing an extension of Noether’s procedure, we considered the supermultiplets in the boundary situation and we commented on the
\overline{Q}_{+}
-cohomology in a possible half-twisted theory. There are various ways to extend our results. First of all, while we focused on the case of
\mathcal{N}=2
with specific boundary conditions, a similar analysis can be performed for other dimensions and other supersymmetries. Furthermore, our examples were limited to theories of chiral multiplets and it would be interesting to couple these systems to a gauge group.

We considered only flat backgrounds, and it would be very interesting to extend the discussion to three-dimensional theories with boundaries in curved backgrounds. The partition function in such backgrounds has been studied recently in
Yoshida:2014ssa
;
Jockers:2018sfl
;
Cheng:2018vpl
. Matrix factorizations have already been discussed on curved manifolds with boundary in
Yoshida:2014ssa
;
Jockers:2018sfl
, see
Aprile:2016gvn
for a discussion of A-type boundary conditions on curved manifolds. The formulation of boundary conditions must be compatible with the constraints imposed by the bulk supersymmetry on the background geometry. In particular, the manifolds have to admit transversely holomorphic foliations, see
Closset:2012ru
for a discussion of the geometry compatible with supersymmetry.

In theories without boundary, supercurrent multiplets have been very useful to formulate theories in curved space. They also helped to understand the (in-)dependence of the partition function of part of the data
Closset:2013vra
. It would be interesting to see whether boundary supermultiplets can help to draw similar conclusions in situations with boundaries.

In the example of Landau-Ginzburg models, we have exhibited in some detail the symmetries of models involving matrix factorizations. In the case of
two-dimensional
Landau-Ginzburg models, matrix factorizations provided the key to fully solve the theories in situations with boundaries, in the sense that the full bulk and boundary spectrum and all correlation functions
Kapustin:2003ga
were determined. It would be interesting to see to what extend these features have analogs in three dimensions. As the theory cannot be fully twisted, one would expect that a holomorphic dependence has to remain.

Finally, one could generalize boundary conditions to defect gluing conditions, making potentially contact with
Dimofte:2017tpi
;
Drukker:2017dgn
. In the Landau-Ginzburg example it is easy to see that defects between theories with different superpotentials involve factorizations of the difference of the two superpotentials on the two sides of the defect.

Appendix D
Delta distributions on the boundary

The commutators of quantized operators involve some subtleties with respect to delta distributions and boundaries. We define

\int\limits_{\mathclap{(-\infty,0]}}f(x)\delta(x)\coloneqq f(0),

(246)

with the reasoning that we want the entire boundary to be part of the theory, thus anything on the boundary is fully part of the system, and that if the
\delta
-distribution is to be understood as a limit of functions
g_{n}(x)\to\delta(x)
which fulfill
\int_{(-\infty,0]}g_{n}(x)=1
, then the above will follow automatically.
This results in one important subtlety: switching from
\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\delta(x-y)
to
-\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\delta(x-y)
introduces a boundary term, specifically

\int\limits_{\mathclap{(-\infty,0]}}\mathrm{d}y\>\int\limits_{\mathclap{(-\infty,0]}}\mathrm{d}xf(x)g(y)\big(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\delta(x-y)+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\delta(x-y)\big)=f(0)g(0).

(247)

This, in turn, implies

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{\mathclap{(-\infty,0]}}\mathrm{d}yf(x)g(y)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\delta(x-y)

\displaystyle=-f(0)g(0)+\int\limits_{\mathclap{(-\infty,0]}}\mathrm{d}yf(y)g^{\prime}(y),

(248)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathclap{(-\infty,0]}}\mathrm{d}yf(x)g(y)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\delta(x-y)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathclap{(-\infty,0]}}\mathrm{d}yf(y)g^{\prime}(y),

(249)

as both boundary terms cancel in the second equation.

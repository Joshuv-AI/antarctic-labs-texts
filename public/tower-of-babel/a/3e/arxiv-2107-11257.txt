Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations
Alain J. Brizard

Abstract
The Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations is presented in terms of a Hamiltonian functional and a guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket. The bracket, which is shown to satisfy the Jacobi identity exactly, is used to show that the guiding-center momentum and angular-momentum conservation laws can also be expressed in Hamiltonian form.



I
Introduction

The complementary Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations of dissipationless plasma equations have a rich history in plasma physics
Morrison_2005
;
Morrison_2017
. In the Lagrangian formulation for the Vlasov-Maxwell equations, for example, one of several types of variational (Euler, Lagrange, or Euler-Poincaré) principles is used to derive these dissipationless plasma equations
Ye_Morrison_1992
;
Brizard_2000
, while their exact conservation laws can be derived through the Noether method.

In the Hamiltonian formulation of dissipationless plasma equations, on the other hand, the equations for a given set of plasma fields
\psi
are, first, written in Hamiltonian form:

\frac{\partial\psi^{a}}{\partial t}\;=\;{\sf J}^{ab}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\psi^{b}},

(1)

in terms of a Hamiltonian functional
{\mathcal{H}}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
and the antisymmetric Poisson operator
{\sf J}^{ab}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$})
acting (denoted as
\circ
) on the functional derivative
\delta{\mathcal{H}}/\delta\psi^{b}
. Next, the evolution of an arbitrary functional
{\mathcal{F}}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
is expressed in Hamiltonian form

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}=\left\langle\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\psi^{a}}\left|\frac{}{}\right.\frac{\partial\psi^{a}}{\partial t}\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\psi^{a}}\left|\frac{}{}\right.{\sf J}^{ab}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\psi^{b}}\right\rangle\equiv\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{H}}\right],

(2)

which is then used to construct a bracket
[\;,\;]
on functionals, where the inner product
\langle\;|\;\rangle
involves suitable integrations over the domains of the field components.

For example, we consider the Vlasov-Maxwell equations with field components
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}=(f,{\bf E},{\bf B})
, which are expressed in Hamiltonian form (
1
):

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f({\bf x},{\bf p},t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\left\{f,\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}\right\}\;+\;4\pi q\,\left\{f,\;{\bf x}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E}\

(3)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\sf J}^{ff}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}+{\sf J}^{f{\bf E}}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bf E}({\bf x},t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf B}\;+\;4\pi q\int_{\bf p}\left\{{\bf x},\;f\right\}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}

(4)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\sf J}^{{\bf E}{\bf B}}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf B}+{\sf J}^{{\bf E}f}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bf B}({\bf x},t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,4\pi c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E}\equiv{\sf J}^{{\bf B}{\bf E}}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E},

(5)

The purpose of the present paper is to follow a similar construction for the guiding-center Hamiltonian structure directly from the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations. II
Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Equations

The variational formulations of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations were presented by Pfirsch and Morrison
Pfirsch_Morrison_1985
and more recently by Brizard and Tronci
Brizard_Tronci_2016
, whose works also included a derivation of exact conservation laws for energy-momentum and angular momentum through the Noether method. The guiding-center equations of motion considered here are the simplest equations derived from a variational principle
Littlejohn_1983
;
Cary_Brizard_2009
.

First, the guiding-center single-particle Lagrangian for a charged particle (of charge
q
and mass
m
) moving in a reduced phase space, with guiding-center position
{\bf X}
and guiding-center parallel momentum
p_{\|}
, is expressed as

L_{\rm gc}\;=\;\left(\frac{q}{c}\,{\bf A}+p_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\;-\;\left(q\,\Phi\;+\frac{}{}K_{\rm gc}\right),

(11)

where the electromagnetic potentials
(\Phi,{\bf A})
yield the electric field
{\bf E}=-\,\nabla\Phi-c^{-1}\partial{\bf A}/\partial t
and the magnetic field
{\bf B}=\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A}\equiv B\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
, and the guiding-center kinetic energy is
K_{\rm gc}=p_{\|}^{2}/2m+\mu\,B
, where
\mu
denotes the guiding-center magnetic moment (which is a guiding-center invariant). The guiding-center equations of motion are derived from the guiding-center Lagrangian (
11
) as Euler-Lagrange equations, which are expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{{\bf X},\;K_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}\;+\;q\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gc},

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{\|}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{p_{\|},\;K_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}\;+\;q\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},p_{\|}\}_{\rm gc},

(13)

where the guiding-center Poisson bracket
Cary_Brizard_2009

\displaystyle\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\nabla f\;\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}\;-\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{\|}}\;\nabla g\right)

(14)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla f\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla g

is used without the ignorable gyromotion pair
(\mu,\theta)
, and the effective fields are

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\bf E}^{*}&\equiv&{\bf E}\;-\;(p_{\|}/q)\,\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}/\partial t\\
{\bf B}^{*}&\equiv&{\bf B}\;+\;(p_{\|}c/q)\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\\
B_{\|}^{*}&\equiv&\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}\;=\;B\;+\;(p_{\|}c/q)\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\end{array}\right\}.

(15)

We note that the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
14
) satisfies the Jacobi property for arbitrary functions
(f,g,h)
:

\left\{\{f,g\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}h\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{g,h\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}f\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{h,f\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gc}=0,

(16)

subject to the condition

\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}\;=\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}\;=\;0,

(17)

which is satisfied by the definition (
15
). We also note that the guiding-center Jacobian
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\equiv 2\pi\,m\,B_{\|}^{*}
satisfies the guiding-center Liouville theorem

\frac{\partial{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left({\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\,\frac{dp_{\|}}{dt}\right)=0,

(18)

with the guiding-center equations of motion (
12
)-(
13

Next, we introduce the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations

for the guiding-center fields
\mbox{\boldmath$\Psi$}_{\rm gc}=(F_{\rm gc},{\bf E},{\bf B})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(F_{\rm gc}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(F_{\rm gc}\,\frac{dp_{\|}}{dt}\right),

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left({\bf B}-\frac{}{}4\pi\,{\bf M}_{\rm gc}\right)-4\pi q\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}

(20)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\;-\;4\pi\,{\bf J}_{\rm gc},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E},

(21)

where the phase-space density
F_{\rm gc}\equiv F\,{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}
is defined in terms of the guiding-center Vlasov function
F
and the guiding-center Jacobian
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}

excludes the guiding-center Jacobian
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}

F_{\rm gc}
appears. In addition, the guiding-center magnetic field

(22)

Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket is now constructed from the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
25
)-(
27
) and the Hamiltonian functional (
29

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}\frac{\partial F_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}+\int_{\bf X}\left(\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf E}+\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf B}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\Psi^{a}}\left|\frac{}{}\right.\frac{\partial\Psi^{a}}{\partial t}\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\Psi^{a}}\left|\frac{}{}\right.{\sf J}_{\rm gc}^{ab}\circ\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\Psi^{b}}\right\rangle,

where the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket for two arbitrary functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})
of the fields
\mbox{\boldmath$\Psi$}=(F_{\rm gc},{\bf E},{\bf B})
is defined in terms of the Poisson structure:

\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;\left\langle\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\Psi^{a}}\;\left|\frac{}{}\right.{\sf J}_{\rm gc}^{ab}\circ\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\Psi^{b}}\right\rangle.

(35)

Here, the antisymmetric Poisson operator
{\sf J}_{\rm gc}^{ab}(\mbox{\boldmath$\Psi$})
guarantees the antisymmetry property:
[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}=-\,[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}]_{\rm gc}
; and the bilinearity of Eq. (
35
) guarantees the Leibniz property:
[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\,{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gc}=[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}\,{\mathcal{K}}+{\mathcal{G}}\,[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gc}
. The Jacobi property:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}ac}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gc}+\left[[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{F}}\right]_{\rm gc}

(36)

\displaystyle+\left[[{\mathcal{K}},{\mathcal{F}}]_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\rm gc}\;=\;0,

which holds for arbitrary functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
, involves the guiding-center Poisson operator
{\sf J}_{\rm gc}^{ab}(\mbox{\boldmath$\Psi$})
.

From Eq. (
34
), we can now extract the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket expressed in terms of two arbitrary guiding-center functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})
as

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left\{\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

(37)

\displaystyle+\;4\pi q\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{G}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle-\;4\pi q\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle+\;(4\pi q)^{2}\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left(\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;{\bf X}\right\}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{G}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\right)

\displaystyle+\;4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\bf B}-\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf B}\right),

where

is defined in Eq. (
31
). The guiding-center bracket (

) is analogous to the Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (

), where the Maxwell sub-bracket (last term) is identical in both cases, while the guiding-center Vlasov sub-bracket (first term) is connected by guiding-center phase-space transformation of the Vlasov sub-bracket in Eq. (

). The Interaction sub-bracket in Eq. (

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left\{\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle-\;4\pi q\,\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle+\;4\pi q\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle+\;(4\pi q)^{2}\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;{\bf X}\right\}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf E}}

\displaystyle+\;4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf B}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta\bf E}

\displaystyle-\;4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf B}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf E}.

Here, the functional derivatives of the guiding-center Hamiltonian functional (
29
) are given in Eq. (
30
), and the functional derivatives of the
z
-component of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum (
38
) are

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}/\delta F_{\rm gc}\\
4\pi c\,\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}/\delta{\bf E}\\
4\pi c\,\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}/\delta{\bf B}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}p_{\|}\,b_{z}\\
{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\\
\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E}+4\pi q\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{P}_{\|}\end{array}\right),

(40)

and

\displaystyle 4\pi c\,\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left({\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf B}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;(p_{\|}c/q)\;\nabla b_{z}.

In Eq. (
39
), we now evaluate

\displaystyle 4\pi q\;\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},K_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla K_{\rm gc}+\left\{\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}},K_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc},

\displaystyle 4\pi q\;\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;{\bf X}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\sf z}\;-\;\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc},

so that

\displaystyle 4\pi q\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\left\{{\bf X},\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}+4\pi q\,\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\right\}_{\rm gc}\right)

\displaystyle=\;q\,\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf E}\;+\frac{}{}\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E}\right)\;\equiv\;q\,\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}^{*},

and, hence, we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left[\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(q\,{\bf E}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\nabla K_{\rm gc}\right)\right]

(42)

\displaystyle+\;4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf B}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta\bf E}

\displaystyle-\;4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf B}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf E}.

Next, we find

\displaystyle 4\pi c\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf B}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta\bf E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{|{\bf B}|^{2}}{8\pi}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right)+\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla K_{\rm gc},

\displaystyle 4\pi c\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{z}}{\delta{\bf B}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\left(\frac{{\bf E}{\bf E}}{4\pi}\;-\;\frac{|{\bf E}|^{2}}{8\pi}\,{\bf I}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right]

\displaystyle+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;q\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf z},

where we made use of Eq. (
32
), so that Eq. (
39
) yields the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum conservation law
\partial{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}/\partial t=[{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc},{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc}=0
. The derivation of the angular guiding-center momentum conservation law
\partial{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi}/\partial t=[{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi},{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc}=0
, where
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi}=\int_{\bf X}{\bf P}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial{\bf X}/\partial\varphi
follows similar steps.

III.2
Guiding-center Casimir functionals

Casimir functionals
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfy the bracket property
[{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gc}=0
, which holds for an arbitrary functional
{\mathcal{K}}
. A standard example is the guiding-center entropy functional (omitting Boltzmann’s constant)

{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]\;\equiv\;-\;\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\ln\left(F_{\rm gc}/\frac{}{}B_{\|}^{*}\right),

(43)

for which we obtain

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left\{\frac{\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

(44)

\displaystyle+\;4\pi q\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{K}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle-\;4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf B},

where
\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}/\delta F_{\rm gc}\;=\;-1-\ln(F)
, with
F\equiv F_{\rm gc}/B_{\|}^{*}
, and

\frac{\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf B}\;=\;\int_{P}\left[F\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\frac{q}{c}\,\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf B}^{*}\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{q}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla F\right)\right],

which is obtained after using the magnetic variations

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}\delta{\bf B}^{*}&=&\delta{\bf B}+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,(\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B})\\
(c/q)\delta\widehat{{\sf b}}&=&\delta{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial\mathbb{P}_{\|}/\partial p_{\|}\\
\delta B_{\|}^{*}&=&\delta\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}+\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B}^{*}\end{array}\right\}.

(45)

Hence, we find the guiding-center bracket identity

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\int_{\bf Z}B_{\|}^{*}\left\{F,\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle-\;4\pi q\int_{\bf Z}\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{K}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf B}^{*}\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{q}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla F\right)

\displaystyle-\;4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta\bf E}\;\equiv\;0,

where the first term on the right side vanishes since it is an exact phase-space divergence, while the last term cancels out the second term. The expression (
43
) for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell entropy was recently mentioned by Burby and Tronci
Burby_Tronci_2017
and might find applications in the dissipative guiding-center bracket formulation
Kaufman_1984
;
Morrison_1984
;
Morrison_1986
of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau model (e.g., see Ref.
Iorio_Hirvijoki_2021
).

III.3
Jacobi property of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket

We now verify that the guiding-center bracket (
37
) satisfies the Jacobi property (
36
). According to the Bracket theorem
Morrison_2013
, the proof of the Jacobi property involves only the explicit dependence of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
37
) on the guiding-center fields
{\sf J}_{\rm gc}^{ab}(F_{\rm gc},{\bf B})
, where we note that the dependence on the magnetic field
{\bf B}
enters through the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
14
), while the electric field
{\bf E}
is explicitly absent.

Hence, we can write the double-bracket involving three arbitrary guiding-center functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
:

\displaystyle\left[[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gc}^{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left\{\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}},\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

(46)

\displaystyle+4\pi q\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\,\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{K}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left\{{\bf X},\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle-4\pi c\int_{\bf X}\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf B}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta\bf E},

where the terms involving
\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}/\delta{\bf E}
vanish on the basis of the Bracket theorem. Here, the functional derivative
\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}/\delta F_{\rm gc}
involves the explicit dependence on the guiding-center Vlasov distribution
F_{\rm gc}
in the Vlasov and Interaction sub-brackets, while
\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}/\delta{\bf B}
in the Maxwell sub-bracket involves the explicit dependence on the magnetic field
{\bf B}
, which appears through
(\widehat{{\sf b}},{\bf B}^{*},B_{\|}^{*})
in the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
14
) and the dyadic tensor (
24
), where
(\delta\widehat{{\sf b}},\delta{\bf B}^{*},\delta B_{\|}^{*})
are given in Eq. (
45
).

The proof of the Jacobi property for the guiding-center bracket (
37
) involves using several identities derived from the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
14
) leading to an expansion in powers of
\epsilon\equiv 4\pi q
up to third order:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}ac}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left(\left\{\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}k\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{g,\;k\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}f\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{k,\;f\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gc}\right)

(47)

\displaystyle+\,\epsilon\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left[F_{i}^{\star}\left(\left\{\{X^{i},\;g\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}k\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{g,\;k\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}X^{i}\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{k,\;X^{i}\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gc}\right)\;+\;\leftturn\right]

\displaystyle+\,\epsilon^{2}\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left[F_{i}^{\star}\,G_{j}^{\star}\left(\left\{\{X^{i},\;X^{j}\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}k\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{X^{j},\;k\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}X^{i}\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{k,\;X^{i}\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}X^{j}\right\}_{\rm gc}\right)\;+\;\leftturn\right]

\displaystyle+\,\epsilon^{3}\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left[F_{i}^{\star}\,G_{j}^{\star}\,K_{\ell}^{\star}\left(\left\{\{X^{i},X^{j}\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}X^{\ell}\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{X^{j},X^{\ell}\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}X^{i}\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{\{X^{\ell},X^{i}\}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}X^{j}\right\}_{\rm gc}\right)\right],

where
(f,g,k)\equiv(\delta{\mathcal{F}}/\delta F_{\rm gc},\delta{\mathcal{G}}/\delta F_{\rm gc},\delta{\mathcal{K}}/\delta F_{\rm gc})
and
({\bf F}^{\star},{\bf G}^{\star},{\bf K}^{\star})\equiv(\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{F}}/\delta{\bf E},\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{G}}/\delta{\bf E},\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{K}}/\delta{\bf E})
, while all additional terms have cancelled out exactly (details of the proof are presented elsewhere
Brizard_Jac_2021
). Here, summation over the repeated indices
(i,j,\ell)
denoting the components of the vector fields
({\bf F}^{\star},{\bf G}^{\star},{\bf K}^{\star})
is implied, and the symbol
\leftturn
denotes cyclic permutations of the functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
.

In Eq. (
47
), it is clear that the Jacobi property of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
37
) is inherited from the Jacobi property (
16
) of the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
14
), since each term in Eq. (
47
) vanishes identically because of this latter property. Hence, the Jacobi property for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
37
) holds under the condition (
17
).

IV
Discussion

In the present paper, we derived the Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations introduced by Brizard and Tronci
Brizard_Tronci_2016
. The associated guiding-center momentum and angular-momentum conservation laws were also presented in Hamiltonian form in terms of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
37
). Since the guiding-center kinetic energy and Poisson bracket associated with the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell model considered here are both independent of the electric field, the effects of guiding-center polarization only appear through the moving electric-dipole contribution to the guiding-center magnetization (
22
).

Future work will consider the inclusion of guiding-center polarization into our guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations, as well as applications of the guiding-center Hamiltonian structure in gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell theory
Burby_Brizard_2019
;
Brizard_2021
, where the perturbed electromagnetic fields
({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})
appear explicitly in the gyrocenter Lagrangian (e.g., see Ref.
Brizard_gyVM_2021
). We also plan to use the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
37
) to explore extensions of Hamiltonian functional perturbation theory
Brizard_Chandre_2020
.

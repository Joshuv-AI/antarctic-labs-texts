Fast Graph Sampling for Short Video Summarization using Gershgorin Disc Alignment
Sadid Sahami, Gene Cheung, Chia-Wen Lin

Abstract
We study the problem of efficiently summarizing a short video into several keyframes, leveraging recent progress in fast graph sampling. Specifically, we first construct a similarity path graph (SPG) 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} , represented by graph Laplacian matrix 𝐋 {\mathbf{L}} , where the similarities between adjacent frames are encoded as positive edge weights. We show that maximizing the smallest eigenvalue λ min ​ ( 𝐁 ) \lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{B}}) of a coefficient matrix 𝐁 = diag ​ ( 𝐚 ) + μ ​ 𝐋 {\mathbf{B}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}})+\mu{\mathbf{L}} , where 𝐚 {\mathbf{a}} is the binary keyframe selection vector, is equivalent to minimizing a worst-case signal reconstruction error. We prove that, after partitioning 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} into Q Q sub-graphs { 𝒢 q } q = 1 Q \{{\mathcal{G}}^{q}\}^{Q}_{q=1} , the smallest Gershgorin circle theorem (GCT) lower bound of Q Q corresponding coefficient matrices— min q ⁡ λ min − ​ ( 𝐁 q ) \min_{q}\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{B}}^{q}) —is a lower bound for λ min ​ ( 𝐁 ) \lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{B}}) . This inspires a fast graph sampling algorithm to iteratively partition 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} into Q Q sub-graphs using Q Q samples (keyframes), while maximizing λ min − ​ ( 𝐁 q ) \lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{B}}^{q}) for each sub-graph 𝒢 q {\mathcal{G}}^{q} . Experimental results show that our algorithm achieves comparable video summarization performance as state-of-the-art methods, at a substantially reduced complexity.

INTRODUCTION

The ubiquity of mobile phones equipped with high-resolution, high-quality cameras means that video contents (typically of short duration no longer than a handful of minutes) are now generated at an astonishing rate
1
1

1

As of Feb 2020, 500 hours of videos were uploaded to YouTube every minute
https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-at-15-my-personal-journey/
.
. On the other hand, human’s available resources to consume media content, both in time and attention, are far more limited, and thus it is imperative to automatically summarize uploaded videos quickly into several keyframes to facilitate downstream tasks such as a user’s selection, retrieval
[
1
]
, and classification
[
2
]
.

Keyframe selection for short videos is well studied in the literature
[
3
,
4
]
.
One main category of algorithms is clustering-based
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
: grouping frames into clusters, then selecting a representative frame from each cluster.
While some methods have low complexity
[
7
,
6
]
, they are heuristic-based when setting the number of clusters and selecting keyframes.
In contrast, there exist optimization-based methods, such as recent
sparse dictionary selection
methods
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
These methods formulated video summarization as a sparse dictionary learning problem by selecting a subset of frames (keyframes) as the dictionary for minimum-error reconstruction.
The sparse dictionary selection problem is, however, NP-hard
[
13
]
.
To solve the problem, these methods resorted to an approximate solution by selecting dictionary atoms (keyframes) iteratively based on a pre-defined criterion.
This also necessitated an initialization by selecting the first keyframe (
e.g.
, the first frame).
A recent state-of-the-art method
[
10
]
extended the formulation to model blocks of image patches per frame (SBOMP) and further grouped neighboring frame blocks into super-blocks (SBOMPn), where the atoms for the dictionary are greedily selected by a variation of
Orthogonal Matching Pursuit
(OMP).
This complex spatio-temporal representation resulted in better performance at a cost of increased computational complexity.

Keyframe selection bears a strong resemblance to the
graph sampling
problem in
graph signal processing
(GSP)
[
14
,
15
]
:
given a combinatorial graph with edge weights that reflect similarities of connected node pairs, choosing a node subset to collect signal samples, so that the reconstruction quality of an assumed smooth (or low-pass) graph signal is optimized.
In particular, a recent fast graph sampling scheme called
Gershgorin disc alignment sampling
(GDAS)
[
16
]
, based on the well-known
Gershgorin circle theorem
(GCT)
[
17
]
, achieved competitive performance while running in roughly linear time.
Leveraging GDAS, we propose an efficient keyframe extraction algorithm,
the first in the literature to pose video summarization as a graph sampling problem
.

Specifically, we first construct a
similarity path graph
(SPG)
{\mathcal{G}}
for frames in a video, where each positive edge weight
w_{i,i+1}
is inversely proportional to
feature distance
between feature vectors
{\mathbf{f}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}
(computed using GoogLeNet features
[
18
]
pre-trained on ImageNet
[
19
]
) associated with frames
i
and
i+1
, respectively.
Given graph Laplacian matrix
{\mathbf{L}}
corresponding to SPG
{\mathcal{G}}
, we show that maximizing the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{B}})
of a coefficient matrix
{\mathbf{B}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}})+\mu{\mathbf{L}}
, where
{\mathbf{a}}
is the binary keyframe selection vector, is equivalent to minimizing a worst-case signal reconstruction error.
Next, we prove that, after partitioning
{\mathcal{G}}
into
Q
sub-graphs
\{{\mathcal{G}}^{q}\}_{q=1}^{Q}
, the smallest GCT lower bound of
Q
corresponding coefficient matrices—
\min_{q}\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{B}}^{q})
—is a lower bound for
\lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{B}})
.
This inspires a fast graph sampling algorithm similar to GDAS
[
16
]
to partition
{\mathcal{G}}
into
Q
sub-graphs, while maximizing
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{B}}^{q})
for each sub-graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{q}
.
Experimental results show that our algorithm achieves comparable video summarization performance as state-of-the-art methods
[
11
,
20
,
10
]
, at a substantially reduced complexity.

PRELIMINARIES

2.1
Graph Definitions

A positive graph
{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}},{\mathbf{W}})
is defined by a set of
N
nodes
{\mathcal{V}}=\{1,\ldots,N\}
, edges
{\mathcal{E}}=\{(i,j)\}
, and an
adjacency matrix

{\mathbf{W}}
, where
W_{i,j}=w_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the positive edge weight if
(i,j)\in{\mathcal{E}}
, and
W_{i,j}=0
otherwise.
If self-loops exist, then
W_{i,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the self-loop weight for node
i
.

Degree matrix

{\mathbf{D}}
has diagonal entries
D_{i,i}=\sum_{j}W_{i,j},\forall i
.
A
combinatorial graph Laplacian matrix

{\mathbf{L}}
is defined as
{\mathbf{L}}\triangleq{\mathbf{D}}-{\mathbf{W}}

[
14
]
.
One can easily show that
{\mathbf{L}}
is
positive semi-definite
(PSD),
i.e.
, all eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{L}}
are non-negative
[
15
]
.
If self-loops exist, then the
generalized graph Laplacian matrix

{\mathcal{L}}
is
{\mathcal{L}}\triangleq{\mathbf{D}}-{\mathbf{W}}+\text{diag}({\mathbf{W}})
to account for self-loops.

A signal
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is smooth w.r.t. graph
{\mathcal{G}}
(without self-loops) if the
graph Laplacian regularizer
(GLR),
{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{L}}{\mathbf{x}}
, is small
[
21
]
:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{L}}{\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{(i,j)\in{\mathcal{E}}}W_{i,j}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}.

(1)

GLR is used to regularize ill-posed inverse problems such as graph signal denoising, dequantization, and interpolation
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

2.2
Gershgorin Circle Theorem

Given a real symmetric matrix
{\mathbf{M}}
, corresponding to each row
i
is a
Gershgorin disc

i
with center
c_{i}\triangleq M_{i,i}
and radius
r_{i}\triangleq\sum_{j\neq i}|M_{i,j}|
.
GCT
[
17
]
states that each eigenvalue
\lambda
resides in at least one Gershgorin disc. A corollary is that the smallest Gershgorin disc left-end
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{M}})
is a lower bound of the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{M}})
of
{\mathbf{M}}
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{M}})\triangleq\min_{i}c_{i}-r_{i}\leq\lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{M}}).

(2)

Note that a
similarity transform

[
24
]

{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1}
for an invertible matrix
{\mathbf{S}}
has the same set of eigenvalues as
{\mathbf{M}}
.
Thus, a GCT lower bound for
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1}
is also a lower bound for
{\mathbf{M}}
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1})\leq\lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{M}}).

(3)

Because GCT lower bound of the original matrix
{\mathbf{M}}
(
2
) is often loose, typically a suitable similarity transform is first performed to obtain a tighter bound (
3
).

3
PATH GRAPH CONSTRUCTION

To represent video frames, we utilize GoogLeNet, pretrained on ImageNet
[
19
]
.
Each frame
F_{i}
of a video sequence is fed into the pre-trained network, and the activations of
pool5
layer in the network are recorded as a
feature vector

{\mathbf{f}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.

Given extracted feature vectors
{\mathbf{f}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}
for frames
F_{i}
and
F_{i+1}
, we compute edge weight
w_{i,i+1}
of a
similarity path graph
(SPG)
{\mathcal{G}}
connecting nodes
i
and
i+1
as follows.
We first compute
feature distance

\delta_{i,i+1}
between
{\mathbf{f}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}
as

\displaystyle\delta_{i,i+1}=\frac{\|{\mathbf{f}}_{i}-\cos(\theta_{i,i+1}){\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}\|_{2}+\|{\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}-\cos(\theta_{i,i+1}){\mathbf{f}}_{i}\|_{2}}{\|{\mathbf{f}}_{i}\|_{2}+\|{\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}\|_{2}}

(4)

where
\theta_{i,i+1}
is the angle between
{\mathbf{f}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}
.
The distance definition
\delta_{i,i+1}
is symmetric:
i.e.
,
\delta_{i,i+1}=\delta_{i+1,i}
.
Note that if
{\mathbf{f}}_{i}={\mathbf{f}}_{i+1}
,
i.e.
, frames
i
and
i+1
are identical, then
\cos(\theta_{i,i+1})=1
, and
\delta_{i,i+1}=0
.
We define the edge weight connecting frames
i
and
i+1
as
w_{i,i+1}=1-\delta_{i,i+1}
.
Since the number of edges in an
N
-node SPG
{\mathcal{G}}
is
N-1
, the complexity of graph construction is
{\mathcal{O}}(N)
.

4
PATH GRAPH SAMPLING

4.1
Defining Sampling Objective

Given SPG
{\mathcal{G}}
, we next derive an objective to choose sample nodes in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Denote by
{\mathbf{L}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
a combinatorial graph Laplacian matrix corresponding to
{\mathcal{G}}
, and by
{\mathbf{H}}\in\{0,1\}^{C\times N}
a
sampling matrix
— each row being a one-hot vector—that chooses
C
samples from a signal
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
As an example, the following sampling matrix
{\mathbf{H}}
chooses nodes
1
and
3
from a four-node graph
{\mathcal{G}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0\end{array}\right].

Denote by
{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
a vector of
C
observed samples.
Using GLR for regularization
[
21
]
, signal
{\mathbf{x}}
can be estimated by solving the following optimization:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\|{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}_{2}+\mu{\mathbf{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{L}}{\mathbf{x}}

(7)

where
\mu>0
is a weight parameter.

Since (
7
) is quadratic, convex and differentiable, the solution
{\mathbf{x}}^{*}
can be obtained by solving the following linear system:

\displaystyle\left({\mathbf{H}}^{\top}{\mathbf{H}}+\mu{\mathbf{L}}\right){\mathbf{x}}^{*}={\mathbf{H}}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}

(8)

where the
coefficient matrix

{\mathbf{B}}={\mathbf{H}}^{\top}{\mathbf{H}}+\mu{\mathbf{L}}
is
positive definite
(PD) and thus invertible
[
16
]
.

{\mathbf{H}}^{\top}{\mathbf{H}}
is a diagonal matrix with the diagonal entries corresponding to chosen samples equal to
1
.
Thus, one can compactly rewrite
{\mathbf{H}}^{\top}{\mathbf{H}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}})
, where
{\mathbf{a}}\in\{0,1\}^{N}
is a
sample selection
vector with those entries corresponding to chosen samples equal to
1
.

One can show that maximizing the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}({\mathbf{B}})
of coefficient matrix
{\mathbf{B}}
—known as the
E-optimality criterion
in system design
[
25
]
—minimizes the worst-case reconstruction error (Proposition 1 in
[
16
]
).
For ease of computation, we maximize instead its GCT lower bound
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1})
:

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{S}}}\lambda^{-}_{\min}\left({\mathbf{S}}(\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}})+\mu{\mathbf{L}}){\mathbf{S}}^{-1}\right),~~\mbox{s.t.}~\|{\mathbf{a}}\|_{1}\leq C

(9)

where
{\mathbf{a}}\in\{0,1\}^{N}
and
{\mathbf{S}}
is any invertible matrix.
For simplicity, we focus on diagonal matrices,
i.e.
,
{\mathbf{S}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{s}})
, where
s_{i}>0,\forall i
.

4.2
Problem Transformation

Since (
9
) is NP-hard
[
16
]
, for expediency, we swap the roles of the objective and constraint in (
9
) and solve instead its corresponding
dual problem
given threshold
0<T<1

[
16
]
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{S}}}\|{\mathbf{a}}\|_{1},~~\mbox{s.t.}~~\lambda^{-}_{\min}\left({\mathbf{S}}(\text{diag}({\mathbf{a}})+\mu{\mathbf{L}}){\mathbf{S}}^{-1}\right)\geq T.

(10)

where
T
is inversely proportional to
\|{\mathbf{a}}^{*}\|_{1}
of the optimal solution
{\mathbf{a}}^{*}
to (
10
)
[
16
]
.
Thus, to find the dual optimal solution
{\mathbf{a}}^{*}
such that
\|{\mathbf{a}}^{*}\|_{1}=C
—and hence the optimal solution to the primal problem (
9
) also—one can perform binary search on
T
to find an appropriate
T
.
We design a fast algorithm to approximately solve (
10
) for a given
T
next.

We first state the following motivating lemma.
Given a positive graph
{\mathcal{G}}
(possibly with self-loops), suppose we partition it into
Q
sub-graphs
{\mathcal{G}}^{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{G}}^{Q}
, where edges connecting nodes from different sub-graphs are removed.

Lemma 1
.

The smallest GCT lower bound among all generalized graph Laplacians
{\mathcal{L}}^{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{L}}^{Q}
for sub-graphs
{\mathcal{G}}^{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{G}}^{Q}
of graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is a lower bound for
\lambda_{\min}({\mathcal{L}})
of
{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\min_{q\in\{1,\ldots,Q\}}\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathcal{L}}^{q})\leq\lambda_{\min}({\mathcal{L}}).

(11)

Proof.

We prove (
11
) as follows.
By GCT, we know that
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathcal{L}})\leq\lambda_{\min}({\mathcal{L}})
.
Left-end of disc
i
of
{\mathcal{L}}
is the disc center minus the radius,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle c_{i}({\mathcal{L}})-r_{i}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}_{i,i}-\sum_{j\neq i}|{\mathcal{L}}_{i,j}|

(12)

\displaystyle=W_{i,i}+\sum_{j\neq i}W_{i,j}-\sum_{j\neq i}W_{i,j}=W_{i,i}.

Suppose now node
i
is in sub-graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{q}
.
Suppose further that one or more edges
(i,k)
,
k\in{\mathcal{K}}
, are removed during sub-graph partition.
The left-end of row
i
in generalized graph Laplacian matrix
{\mathcal{L}}^{q}
of
{\mathcal{G}}^{q}
is

\displaystyle c_{i}({\mathcal{L}}^{q})-r_{i}({\mathcal{L}}^{q})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{i,i}-\sum_{j\neq i}|{\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{i,j}|

(13)

\displaystyle=W_{i,i}+\!\!\!\!\sum_{j\not\in{\mathcal{K}}\cup\{i\}}\!\!W_{i,j}-\!\!\!\!\sum_{j\not\in{\mathcal{K}}\cup\{i\}}\!\!W_{i,j}=W_{i,i}.

We thus conclude that the left-ends of row
i
in
{\mathcal{L}}
and
{\mathcal{L}}^{q}
are the same.
Since
\min_{q}\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathcal{L}}^{q})=\min_{q}\min_{i}c_{i}({\mathcal{L}}^{q})-r_{i}({\mathcal{L}}^{q})
is the same as
\min_{i}c_{i}({\mathcal{L}})-r_{i}({\mathcal{L}})=\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathcal{L}})
, we conclude (
11
) is true.
∎

The corollary of the lemma is that one can maximize a lower bound of
\lambda_{\min}({\mathcal{L}})
by maximizing individual GCT lower bounds
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathcal{L}}^{q})
of sub-graph Laplacians
{\mathcal{L}}^{q}
’s.

4.3
Line Graph Sampling Algorithm

Given Lemma
1
, we solve (
10
) by dividing graph
{\mathcal{G}}
into sub-graphs, where
each
sub-graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{q}
, bestowed with a single node sample, satisfies
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}^{q}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1})\geq T
for some
{\mathbf{S}}
.
Specifically, given SPG
{\mathcal{G}}
with
N
nodes, we first identify sample
k
solving the following optimization:

\displaystyle\max_{k,{\mathbf{S}}}~d,~~\mbox{s.t.}~~\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{S}}(\text{diag}({\mathbf{e}}_{k})+\mu{\mathbf{L}}^{d}){\mathbf{S}}^{-1})\geq T

(14)

where
{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
is a
canonical vector
of zeros except entry
k
which equals
1
, and
{\mathbf{L}}^{d}
is the graph Laplacian matrix corresponding to the sub-graph of the first
d
nodes.
The idea is to find a sample node
k
which can “cover” the
longest
sub-graph from
1
to
d
,
i.e.
, satisfying
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1})\geq T
, where
{\mathbf{B}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{e}}_{k})+\mu{\mathbf{L}}^{d}
.
This is analogous to selecting a graph node with the largest set in the famous
set cover
(SC) in combinatorial optimization
[
26
]
.
The analogy between graph sampling dual (
10
) and SC is also discussed in
[
16
]
.

To determine optimal
k
, we incrementally test each candidate
k
from
1
, until
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1})<T
.
After sample node
k
is chosen, the first
d
nodes are grouped into a sub-graph and removed from SPG
{\mathcal{G}}
.
The optimization repeats for the remaining nodes until all nodes are partitioned into sub-graphs.

4.4
Gershgorin Disc Alignment on SPG

For a candidate sample node
k
, to compute an appropriate
{\mathbf{S}}=\text{diag}({\mathbf{s}})
efficiently ensuring
\lambda^{-}_{\min}({\mathbf{S}}(\text{diag}({\mathbf{e}}_{k})+\mu{\mathbf{L}}^{d}){\mathbf{S}}^{-1})\geq T
in (
14
) for the largest possible
d
, we perform the following recursion.
Given sample node
k
, the
k
-th diagonal entry of
{\mathbf{B}}
is
B_{k,k}=1+\mu L_{k,k}
.
We first
scale
the radius of disc
k
of
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1}
so that its left-end
c_{k}-r_{k}
is
aligned
at
T
;
i.e.
, choosing scalar
s_{k}
so that

\displaystyle B_{k,k}-s_{k}(|L_{k,k-1}|+|L_{k,k+1}|)=T.

(15)

Graphically, sampling node
k
means shifting disc
k
right by
1
, and scaling the radius of disc
k
with scalar
s_{k}
means shifting its left-end left to
T
.
See Fig.
1
for an illustration.

6
CONCLUSION

We proposed to solve the keyframe selection problem from a graph sampling perspective.
Specifically, our method first represents the frames in a video as nodes in a similarity line graph, where edge weights are computed using features derived from pre-trained deep neural networks.
Given the constructed graph, we posed keyframe selection as a budget-constrained graph sampling problem, and devised an efficient algorithm that iteratively divides the graph into sub-graphs.
Experimental results show that our proposal achieved comparable performance to state-of-the-art methods at reduced complexity.

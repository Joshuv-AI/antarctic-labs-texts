A technical note on finite best-worst random utility model representations Revised 16-09-2024
Hans Colonius

Abstract
This paper investigates the random utility representation of best-worst choice probabilities (picking the best and the worst alternative from an offered set). Doignon [2023] presented a complete characterization of the best-worst-choice polytope on four alternatives. Moreover, using polytope methods he showed that the Block-Marschak inequalities for best-worst choices are not sufficient for a random utility representation of best-worst choices for sets of four or more alternatives. Following the approach of Falmagne [1978] , we construct a probability measure on the set of rankings for a set of four alternatives implying a random utility representation.



Introduction

The purpose of this note is to construct a probability measure on the rankings (permutations) of a finite set
A
that allows one to infer the existence of a random utility representation of best-worst choice probabilities for
A
. For context and related results, we refer to
Marley and Louviere [2005]
,
Colonius [2021]
, and
Doignon [2023]
.

1
Some definitions and basic results

For ease of reference, we keep the notation in the original paper of
Falmagne [1978]
. For a finite set
A
, we write
|A|
for the number of elements in
A
,
\mathscr{P}(A)
for the power set of
A
. For any finite nonempty set
A
let
\Phi(A)
be the set of all finite, nonempty subsets of
A
. Moreover, let
\mathscr{P}(A,\geq c)
(resp.,
\mathscr{P}(A,\leq c)
) be the set of all subsets of
A
containing at least (resp., at most)
c
elements.

Definition 1
.

Let
A
be a nonempty set of elements with at least 2 elements. For any
B\subseteq A
, with
|B|\geq 2
, and any
a,b\in B
,
a\neq b
,
let
BW_{B}(a,b)\mapsto[0,1]
denote the probability that
a
and
b
are respectively chosen as best and worst elements in the subset
B
of
A
. Let
\mathbb{BWP}=\{BW_{B}\,|\,B\subseteq A,BW_{B}(a,b)\mapsto[0,1],a,b\in B,a\neq b\}
be the collection of all those probability measures. Suppose that

\sum_{a,b\,\in B,\,a\neq b}BW_{B}(a,b)=1\hskip 28.45274pt(BW\in\mathbb{BWP}).

Then
(A,\mathbb{BWP})
is called a
finite system of best-worst choice probabilities
, or more briefly, a
system
.

The quantities
BW_{B}(a,b)
will be referred to as
best-worst choice probabilities
indicating that an alternative
a
is judged as best and
b
as worst, when subset
B
is available.

1.1
Best-Worst Block-Marschak polynomials

Suppose
(A,\mathbb{BWP})
is a system. Consider the following expressions:

\displaystyle BW_{A}(a,b),

\displaystyle BW_{A\setminus\{c\}}(a,b)-BW_{A}(a,b),

\displaystyle BW_{A\setminus\{c,d\}}(a,b)-[BW_{A\setminus\{c\}}(a,b)+BW_{A\setminus\{d\}}(a,b)]+BW_{A}(a,b),

\displaystyle BW_{A\setminus\{c,d,e\}}(a,b)-[BW_{A\setminus\{c,d\}}(a,b)+BW_{A\setminus\{c,e\}}(a,b)+BW_{A\setminus\{d,e\}}(a,b)]+

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt[BW_{A\setminus\{c\}}(a,b)+BW_{A\setminus\{d\}}(a,b)+BW_{A\setminus\{e\}}(a,b)]-BW_{A}(a,b),

etc.

We introduce a compact notation.

Definition 2
.

For any
B\subset A
,
B\neq A
,
a,b\in A\setminus B,a\neq b,
in a system
(A,\mathbb{BWP})
, we define

K_{ab,B}=\sum_{i=0}^{|B|}(-1)^{i}\sum_{C\in\mathscr{P}(B,|B|-i)}BW_{A\setminus C}(a,b).

(1)

The
K_{ab,B}
are called
best-worst Block-Marschak polynomials of

(A,\mathbb{BWP})
, or
best-worst BM polynomials
, for short.

Observe that

\displaystyle K_{ab,\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{0}(-1)^{i}\sum_{C\in\mathscr{P}(\emptyset,0-i)}BW_{A\setminus C}(a,b)

\displaystyle=BW_{A}(a,b);

\displaystyle K_{ab,\{c\}}

\displaystyle=BW_{A\setminus\{c\}}(a,b)-BW_{A}(a,b)

\displaystyle=BW_{A\setminus\{c\}}(a,b)-K_{ab,\emptyset};

\displaystyle K_{ab,\{c,d\}}

\displaystyle=BW_{A\setminus\{c,d\}}(a,b)-[BW_{A\setminus\{c\}}(a,b)+BW_{A\setminus\{d\}}(a,b)]+BW_{A}(a,b)

\displaystyle=BW_{A\setminus\{c,d\}}(a,b)-K_{ab,\{c\}}-K_{ab,\{d\}}-K_{ab,\emptyset}.

Similar computations show that

\displaystyle K_{ab,\{c,d,e\}}

\displaystyle=BW_{A\setminus\{c,d,e\}}(a,b)-K_{ab,\{c,d\}}-K_{ab,\{c,e\}}-K_{ab,\{d,e\}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-K_{ab,\{c\}}-K_{ab,\{d\}}-K_{ab,\{e\}}-K_{ab,\emptyset}

These examples suggest the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(A,\mathbb{BWP})
be a system of best-worst choice probabilities. Then, for all
B\subset A,B\neq A
, and
a,b\in A\setminus B
,

BW_{A\setminus B}(a,b)=\sum_{C\in\mathscr{P}(B)}K_{ab,C}.

(2)

For proof see
Colonius [2021]
and the references therein.

Some more definitions

Let
\geq
be an arbitrarily chosen simple order on
A
. As usual, we write, for any
a,b\in A,a<b
iff not
a\geq b
, and
a>b
iff not
b\geq a
. For any
B\subset A
, we write
\Pi_{B}
for the set of
|B|!
permutations (rankings) on
B
. For
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in A
, we write
a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{n}
for the ranking (defined by
>
) corresponding to permutation
\pi
with
\pi(a_{1})>\pi(a_{2})>\cdots>\pi(a_{n})
.

Definition 4
.

Let
B\subset A
with
|A|-|B|\geq 2
; for
a,b\in A\setminus B
define

\mathrm{S}(a~A\setminus B~b)=\{\pi\in\Pi_{A}\,|\,\text{ $a$ is ranked ``best'' and $b$ is ranked ``worst'' in $A\setminus B$}\}.

Lemma 5
.

For any distinct
a,b\in A\setminus B

S(a~A\setminus B~b)=\sum_{C\in\mathscr{P}(B)}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{C}}\sum_{(\pi_{1}\pi_{2})=\pi}S(\pi_{1}a~A\setminus C~b\pi_{2});

(3)

Here, notation
S(\pi_{1}a~A\setminus C~b\pi_{2})
means the set of all rankings of
\Pi_{A}
where
\pi=(\pi_{1}\pi_{2})\in\Pi_{C}
is split into all possible pairs
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
; for example, ranking
cde\in\Pi_{\{c,d,e\}}
is split into

cde/\cdot,cd/e,c/de,\cdot/cde

(using notation
\cdot/\cdot
) so that
cd/e
corresponds to
S(cda~A\setminus C~be)
,
\cdot/cde
to
S(a~A\setminus C~bcde)
, etc. For a proof of Lemma
5
see
Colonius [2021]
.

Example 6
.

Let
A=\{a,b,c,d\}
and
B=\{c,d\}
. Then

\displaystyle S(a~A\setminus B~b)

\displaystyle=S(a~A~b)+S(ca~A~b)+S(a~A~bc)+S(da~A~b)+S(a~A~bd)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle S(cda~A~b)+S(dca~A~b)+S(a~A~bcd)+S(a~A~bdc)

\displaystyle+S(ca~A~bd)+S(da~A~bc)

\displaystyle=\{acdb,adcb\}+\{cadb\}+\{adbc\}+\{dacb\}+\{acbd\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\{cdab\}+\{dcab\}+\{abcd\}+\{abdc\}+\{cabd\}+\{dabc\}.

2
Construction of
\mathrm{P}[\,.\,]

The following lemma is a variant of the well-known result by
Block and Marschak [1960]
(see also
Marley and Louviere [2005]
, section on random ranking models).

Lemma 7
.

(A,\mathbb{BWP})
is a best-worst random utility system if and only if there exists a probability measure
\mathrm{P}[\,.\,]
on
\mathscr{P}(\Pi_{A})
satisfying

BW_{B}(a,b)=\mathrm{P}[S(a~B~b)]

(4)

for all
a.b\in B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\geq 2)
.

In constructing a probability measure
\mathrm{P}[\,.\,]
on
\mathscr{P}(\Pi_{A})
we must show, in addition to Equation (
4
), that
\mathrm{P}[\Pi_{A}]=1
. We first define
\mathrm{P}[\,.\,]
on the points of
\Pi_{A}
and then extend it in the usual way to a probability measure on
\mathscr{P}(\Pi_{A})
.

We define the probability of each point
\pi
in
\Pi_{A}
as follows:

Definition 8
.

For
a,b\in A\setminus B

\mathrm{P}[\pi\in S(a~A\setminus B~b)]=\frac{K_{ab,B}}{|S(a~A\setminus B~b)|}.

(5)

Lemma 9
.

Let
B\subset A
with
|A|-|B|\geq 2
; for
a,b\in A\setminus B
the number of elements in set

\mathrm{S}(a~A\setminus B~b)
is

|\mathrm{S}(a~A\setminus B~b)|=(|B|+1)!\times(|A\setminus B|-2)!

(6)

Indeed, we have
|B|!
permutations for the elements in
B
and there are
|B|+1
ways to partition them into elements ranking above
a
and below
b
; moreover, there are
|A\setminus B|-2)!
permutations of the elements in
|A\setminus B|
while keeping
a
and
b
fixed.

2.1
Construction of
\mathrm{P}[\,.\,]
for
|A|=4

We illustrate the construction for
A=\{a,b,c,d\}
. For pairwise different and arbitrary
i,j,k,l\in A
, Table
1
contains all 12 possible rankings of the elements in
A
where
i
is ranked “best” and
j
is ranked “worst” in
A\setminus B
.

\begin{array}[]{|c||c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr B&\emptyset&\{k\}&\{l\}&\{k,l\}\\
\hline\cr S(i~A\setminus B~j)&S(i~A~j)&S(i~A\setminus\{k\}~j)&S(i~A\setminus\{l\}~j)&S(i~A\setminus\{k,l\}~j)\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr&iklj&kilj&likj&klij\\
&ilkj&iljk&ikjl&lkij\\
&&&&kijl\\
\text{all rankings }&&&&lijk\\
&&&&ijkl\\
&&&&ijlk\\
\hline\cr|S(i~A\setminus B~j)|&2!&2!&2!&3!\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Table 1:
Sets of rankings
S(i~A\setminus B~j)\subset\Pi_{A}
,
i,j\in A\setminus B
. Note that interchanging the positions of
i
and
j
yields all 4! rankings .

Note that

\sum_{B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\leq|A|-2)}(|S(i~A\setminus B~j)|+|S(j~A\setminus B~i)|)=4!=24=|\Pi_{A}|.

Proposition 10
.

Let
A=\{i,j,k,l\}
and
B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\leq 2)
; define for any
i,j\in A\setminus B
(
i\neq j
)

\mathrm{P}[\pi\in S(i~A\setminus B~j)]=\frac{K_{ij,B}}{|S(i~A\setminus B~j)|}.

Then,

(a)

\mathrm{P}(\Pi_{A})=1
;

(b)

BW_{B}(i,j)=\mathrm{P}[S(i~B~j)]
.

First, we have to show that
\mathrm{P}[\Pi_{A}]=1
. From Definition
10
, for each
B\subset A

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in S(i~A\setminus B~j)}\mathrm{P}[\pi\in S(i~A\setminus B~j)]

\displaystyle=K_{ij,B}.

We extend probability measure
\mathrm{P}(A)
to a measure on subsets of
\Pi_{A}
by setting

\mathrm{P}[S(i~A\setminus B~j)]=K_{ij,B}.

From Table
1
(or, Lemma
5
),

\Pi_{A}=\sum_{B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\leq|A|-2)}(S(i~A\setminus B~j)+S(j~A\setminus B~i)),

so that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}[\Pi_{A}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\leq|A|-2)}(K_{ij,B}+K_{ji,B})

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}[\Pi_{A}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\leq|A|-2)}K_{ij,B}+K_{ji,B}

\displaystyle=(K_{ij,\emptyset}+K_{ji,\emptyset})+(K_{ij,\{k\}}+K_{ji,\{k\}})+(K_{ij,\{l\}}+K_{ji,\{l\}})+(K_{ij,\{k,l\}}+K_{ji,\{k,l\}}).

Now, by definition

\displaystyle K_{ij,\emptyset}+K_{ji,\emptyset}=BW_{A}(i,j)+BW_{A}(j,i);

(7)

\displaystyle K_{ij,\{k\}}+K_{ji,\{k\}}=BW_{A-\{k\}}(i,j)-BW_{A}(i,j)+BW_{A-\{k\}}(j,i)-BW_{A}(j,i);

(8)

\displaystyle K_{ij,\{l\}}+K_{ji,\{l\}}=BW_{A-\{l\}}(i,j)-BW_{A}(i,j)+BW_{A-\{l\}}(j,i)-BW_{A}(j,i);

(9)

\displaystyle K_{ij,\{k,l\}}=BW_{\{i,j\}}(i,j)-[BW_{A-\{k\}}(i,j)+BW_{A-\{l\}}(i,j)]+BW_{A}(i,j);

(10)

\displaystyle K_{ji,\{k,l\}}=BW_{\{i,j\}}(j,i)-[BW_{A-\{k\}}(j,i)+BW_{A-\{l\}}(j,i)]+BW_{A}(j,i).

(11)

Abbreviating
BW_{A}(i,j)=(ij),BW_{A-\{k\}}(i,j)=(ij|k),BW_{A-\{l\}}(i,j)=(ij|l)
and
BW_{\{i,j\}}(i,j)=(ij|k,l)
, collecting all terms from Equations 7–11, reordering, and canceling yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}[\Pi_{A}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ij)+(ji)

\displaystyle+(ij|k)-(ij)+(ji|k)-(ji)

\displaystyle+(ij|l)-(ij)+(ji|l)-(ji)

\displaystyle+(ij|k,l)-[(ij|k)+(ij|l)]+(ij)

\displaystyle+(ji|k,l)-[(ji|k)+(ji|l)]+(ji)

\displaystyle=(ij)+(ji)-(ij)-(ji)-(ij)-(ji)+(ij)+(ji)

\displaystyle+(ij|k)+(ji|k)-(ij|k)-(ji|k)+(ij|l)+(ji|l)-(ij|l)-(ji|l)

\displaystyle+(ij|k,l)+(ji|k,l)

\displaystyle=1.

Next, from Lemma
7
, we have to show that

BW_{B}(a,b)=\mathrm{P}[S(a~B~b)]

for all
B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\geq 2)
, and
a,b\in B
. Equivalently, we show that

BW_{A\setminus B}(a,b)=\mathrm{P}[S(a~A\setminus B~b)]

for all
B\in\mathscr{P}(A,\leq|A|-2)
, and
a,b\in A\setminus B
.

Indeed, from Lemma
5
and Theorem
3

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}[S(a~A\setminus B~b)]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P}\left[\sum_{C\in\mathscr{P}(B)}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{C}}\sum_{(\pi_{1}\pi_{2})=\pi}S(\pi_{1}a~A\setminus C~b\pi_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{C\in\mathscr{P}(B)}\mathrm{P}\left[\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{C}}\sum_{(\pi_{1}\pi_{2})=\pi}S(\pi_{1}a~A\setminus C~b\pi_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{C\in\mathscr{P}(B)}K_{ab,C}

\displaystyle=BW_{A\setminus B}(a,b).

This completes the proof of Proposition
10
.

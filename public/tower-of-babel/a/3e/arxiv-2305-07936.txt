Analytic Gelfand-Shilov smoothing effect of fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation
Chao-Jiang Xu and Yan Xu

Abstract
. We study the Cauchy problem of the fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation and show that the solution to the Cauchy problem enjoys an analytic Gelfand-Shilov regularizing effect for positive time.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35B65,76P05,82C40

1.
Introduction

The Cauchy problem of fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation reads

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(1-\Delta_{v})^{s}u+(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}u=f(t,x,v),\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u=u(t,x,v)\geq 0
is the density distribution function of particles depending on time
t\geq 0
, the position variable
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the velocity variable
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\gamma>-3
,
0<s\leq 1
.

The Fokker-Planck equation was introduced by Fokker and Planck, to describe the evolution of the density of particles under the Brownian motion. Recently, the results of the global hypoelliptic estimates were motivated by applications to the kinetic theory of gases. The Kramers-Fokker-Planck operator has been studied in
[
11
,
12
]
.

It is a linear model of kinetic equations such as the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation for
0<s<1
and the Landau equation for
s=1
with moderate soft potential. For
\gamma=0,s=1
, the above equation is the classical Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation. The
C^{\infty}
regularity of weak solution for the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation without angular cutoff has been discussed in
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
22
]
. The Gevrey regularity of the local solution for the initial datum with the same Gevrey regularity has been established in
[
21
]
. The Gevrey regularity results for the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation have given in
[
3
,
17
]
. Regularity results for the spatially homogeneous Landau equation have been studied in
[
14
,
16
,
18
]
.

The regularity problems for the solutions of the kinetic equation have been studied in
[
20
,
4
]
. For the spatially inhomogeneous problems,
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
concerned the
C^{\infty}
regularity problems for linear spatially inhomogeneous Boltzmann equation without angular cutoff. The analytic smoothing effect of a solution for the spatially inhomogeneous Landau equation has been given in
[
19
]
. And
[
5
,
8
,
10
]
also concern the regularity problems for the spatially homogeneous Landau equation.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\sigma>0
, the Gevrey spaces
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of
C^{\infty}
functions
u
satisfying for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2\sigma}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{k+1}k!.

Let
\mu,\nu>0
and
\mu+\nu\geq 1
, the Gelfand-Shilov space
S^{\mu}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of
C^{\infty}
functions
u
satisfying for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist the constants
C>0,\tilde{C}>0
such that

\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2\mu}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{k+1}k!\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\left\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{k}{2\nu}}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\tilde{C}^{k+1}k!.

In this work, we consider the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with
0<s<1
,
\gamma+2s>0
. Set
\tilde{s}=\min\{1/2,s\}
, we would show the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) admits a solution that enjoys the Gevrey regularity and decay estimation. The main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<s<1
,
\gamma+2s>0
and
T>0
. Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})
,
f\in C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{G}^{\frac{1}{2\tilde{s}}}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3};S^{\frac{1}{2\tilde{s}}}_{\frac{1}{\gamma/2+s}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})))
, then the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) admits a solution in
C^{\infty}(]0,T];\mathcal{G}^{\frac{1}{2\tilde{s}}}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3};S^{\frac{1}{2\tilde{s}}}_{\frac{1}{\gamma/2+s}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})))
. In fact, we prove that there exists
C>0
such that for all
0<t\leq T

\left\|(1-\Delta_{v}-t^{2}\Delta_{x})^{\tilde{s}k}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+\left\|\langle v\rangle^{(\gamma/2+s)k}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\leq\frac{C^{k+1}}{t^{k}}k!,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

In
[
23
]
, we show that the solution for the Cauchy problem of the spatially homogenous fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation belongs to the Gelfand-Shilov space
S^{\frac{1}{2\tilde{s}}}_{\frac{1}{\gamma/2+s}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
t>0
. However, the equation of (
1.1
) is a degenerate parabolic type equation, but in this work, we prove that the regularity of solution of the velocity variables transport to the spatial variables with same order
\frac{1}{2\tilde{s}}
for all
t>0
, so that our results is much more strong then the classical hypoelliptic regularity results.

2.
Estimations of Commutator and Interpolation

In the following, the notation
A\lesssim B
means there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, the notation
[T_{1},T_{2}]=T_{1}T_{2}-T_{2}T_{1}
is commutator. For simplicity, we denote
\langle D_{v}\rangle=\sqrt{1-\Delta_{v}}
,
\langle v\rangle=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}
, then (
1.1
) can be written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s}u+\langle v\rangle^{\gamma+2s}u=f,\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

For later use, we need the following estimation of commutator for the operator
\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r}
with
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m,r\in\mathbb{R}
, then there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for all
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x};\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}))
,

\left\|\left[\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x,v}^{6})}\leq C_{1}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r-1}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}u\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})},

(2.1)

with the constant
C_{1}
depends on
m
and
r
,

Proof.

Let
\langle v\rangle^{m}u(x,v)=g(x,v)
, then

\left[\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u=\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r}g-\langle v\rangle^{m}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r}\left(\langle v\rangle^{-m}g\right).

Since
\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r}
can be viewed as a pseudo-differential operator of symbol
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{r}{2}}
, it follows that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\langle v\rangle^{m}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r}\langle v\rangle^{-m}=\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|<N}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\langle v\rangle^{m}D_{v}^{\alpha}\langle v\rangle^{-m}a^{(\alpha)}(D_{v})+\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{N}(v,D_{v}),

where
a^{(\alpha)}(\xi)=\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\langle\xi\rangle^{r}
, and

r_{N}(v,\xi)=N\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\theta)^{N-1}}{\alpha!}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)d\theta,

with
r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)
is the oscillating integral, defined via

r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)=Os-\iint e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}D^{\alpha}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m}a^{(\alpha)}(\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime})\frac{dv^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

Using the identity

e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}=\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}\langle D_{\xi^{\prime}}\rangle^{2l^{\prime}}e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}=\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l}\langle D_{v^{\prime}}\rangle^{2l}e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}},

(2.2)

and the fact
2l^{\prime}>|m|+N+|\beta|+3
,
2l>r+N+|\beta^{\prime}|+3
, then from integration by parts and using the Leibniz formula, we can deduce that

\begin{split}&D^{\beta}_{v}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\xi}(\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi))=\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}C_{\beta}^{\beta_{1}}D^{\beta_{1}}_{v}\langle v\rangle^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle D_{\xi^{\prime}}\rangle^{2l^{\prime}}a^{(\alpha+\beta^{\prime})}(\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime})\frac{G(v,\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}}{\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{2l}(2\pi)^{3}},\end{split}

where

G(v,\xi^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{3}}e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}\langle D_{v^{\prime}}\rangle^{2l}\left(\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}D^{\alpha+\beta_{2}}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m}\right)dv^{\prime}.

Now, we consider the term
G(v,\xi^{\prime})
first. By using the Leibniz formula and the fact
2l^{\prime}>|m|+N+|\beta|+3
, one gets

\begin{split}|G(v,\xi^{\prime})|&\leq C(l)\sum_{|\sigma|\leq 2l}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{3}}\left|\partial^{\sigma}_{v^{\prime}}\left(\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}D^{\alpha+\beta_{2}}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m}\right)\right|dv^{\prime}\\
&\leq C(l,l^{\prime},\alpha,\beta_{2})\int\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m-N-|\beta_{2}|}dv^{\prime}\\
&\leq C(l,l^{\prime},\alpha,\beta_{2},m)\langle v\rangle^{-m-N-|\beta_{2}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{3}}\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}+|m|+N+|\beta_{2}|}dv^{\prime}\\
&\leq\tilde{C}(l,l^{\prime},\alpha,\beta_{2},m)\langle v\rangle^{-m-N-|\beta_{2}|}.\end{split}

Substituting it into
D^{\beta}_{v}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\xi}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)\right)
, since
\theta\in]0,1[
, by using Peetre’s inequality, for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{split}&\left|D^{\beta}_{v}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\xi}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)\right)\right|\\
&\leq C(l,l^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\beta^{\prime},m)\langle v\rangle^{-N-|\beta|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l}\langle\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{r-N-|\beta^{\prime}|}d\xi^{\prime}\\
&\leq C(l,l^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\beta^{\prime},m)\langle v\rangle^{-N-|\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle^{r-N-|\beta^{\prime}|}2^{r+N+|\beta^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l+|r|+N+|\beta^{\prime}|}d\xi^{\prime}\\
&\leq\tilde{C}(l,l^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\beta^{\prime},m)\langle v\rangle^{-N-|\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle^{r-N-|\beta^{\prime}|}\leq\tilde{C}(l,l^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\beta^{\prime},m)\langle\xi\rangle^{r-N-|\beta^{\prime}|},\end{split}

here we use the fact
2l>r+N+|\beta^{\prime}|+3
, hence

\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{N}(v,D_{v})\in\Psi_{1,0}^{r-N},

this implies
\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{N}(v,D_{v})\langle D_{v}\rangle^{N-r}\in\Psi_{1,0}^{0}
. Since
u(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
, it follows that
g(x,\cdot)=\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}u(x,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
, then we have

\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}r_{N}(\cdot,D_{v})\langle D_{v}\rangle^{N-r}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r-N}g(x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})}\leq C_{r,m}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r-N}g(x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})}.

Therefore, taking
N=1
, since
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x};\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}))
, we can obtain that

\begin{split}&\left\|\left[\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}=\left\|\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{1}(v,D_{v})\langle D_{v}\rangle^{1-r}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r-1}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}}\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}r_{1}(\cdot,D_{v})\langle D_{v}\rangle^{1-r}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r-1}g(x,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C_{1}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{r-1}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}u\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})},\end{split}

with
C_{1}
depends on
m
and
r
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Taking
r=2s
in (
2.1
), if
0<s\leq 1/2
, then
2s-1\leq 0
, so that

\begin{split}\left\|\left[\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}&\leq C_{1}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s-1}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}u\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&\leq C_{1}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{m}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})},\end{split}

(2.3)

if
1/2<s<1
, then from (
2.1
) we have

\begin{split}\left\|\left[\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\leq C_{1}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s-1}(\langle v\rangle^{m}u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Now, recall the result from
[
19
]
, we have

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
19
]
)
For any
\alpha>0
, there exists
c_{\alpha}>0,C_{\alpha}>0
such that
\forall t>0

c_{\alpha}t\left(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+|\xi+\rho\eta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}d\rho\leq C_{\alpha}t\left(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

For
0<s<1
, set
\tilde{s}=\min\{1/2,s\}
and

M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,D_{x},D_{v})=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle D_{v}+\rho D_{x}\right\rangle^{2\tilde{s}}d\rho\sim t\left(1-\Delta_{v}-t^{2}\Delta_{x}\right)^{\tilde{s}},

we consider the commutator for the operator
M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,D_{x},D_{v})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<s<1
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
, then there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

\begin{split}&\left\|\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left[M^{k}_{2\tilde{s}},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&\leq\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\left(C_{2}(t+1)\right)^{k-p}C_{k}^{p}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}M^{p}_{2\tilde{s}}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})},\quad\forall u\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x};\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})).\end{split}

(2.5)

where the constant
C_{2}
depends on
m,s
and
C_{k}^{p}=\frac{k!}{p!(k-p)!}
.

Proof.

We show (
2.5
) holds by induction on the index
k
. For
k=1
, set
g(x,v)=\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}u(x,v)
it follows that

\begin{split}\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left[M_{2\tilde{s}},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u&=\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left[M_{2\tilde{s}},\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\right]g-\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\left[M_{2\tilde{s}},\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\right]g\\
&=\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}r_{t,j}(v,D_{x},D_{v})g-\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\tilde{r}_{t,j}(v,D_{x},D_{v})g,\end{split}

here

\begin{split}r_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}r_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)d\theta\quad{\rm and}\quad\tilde{r}_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{r}_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)d\theta,\end{split}

with the oscillating integrals
r_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)
and
\tilde{r}_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)
, defined via

r_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=Os-\iint e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}D_{j}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\partial_{\xi_{j}}M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,\eta,\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime})\frac{dv^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

\tilde{r}_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=Os-\iint e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}D_{j}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\partial_{\xi_{j}}M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,\eta,\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime})\frac{dv^{\prime}d\xi^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

Set

R_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}r_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)\quad{\rm and}\quad\tilde{R}_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\tilde{r}_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi),

R_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}R_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)d\theta\quad{\rm and}\quad\tilde{R}_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{R}_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)d\theta,

then
\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left[M_{2\tilde{s}},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u
can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left[M_{2\tilde{s}},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u&=\sum_{j=1}^{3}R_{t,j}(v,D_{x},D_{v})g-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\tilde{R}_{t,j}(v,D_{x},D_{v})g,\end{split}

we would show that
R_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)
and
\tilde{R}_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)
are the pseudo-differential operators of order
0
.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, by using the identity (
2.2
) and the fact
2l>4+|\beta|-2\tilde{s}
,
2l^{\prime}>\frac{|m|}{2}+|\alpha|+4
, then from integration by part and the Leibniz formula,

\begin{split}&\partial^{\alpha}_{v}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}R_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)=\partial^{\alpha}_{v}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\left(\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}r_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha}C^{\alpha_{1}}_{\alpha}\int\langle D_{\xi^{\prime}}\rangle^{2l^{\prime}}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\partial_{\xi_{j}}M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,\eta,\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime})\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{v}\langle v\rangle^{-\frac{m}{2}}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{v}T(v,\xi^{\prime})\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{2l}(2\pi)^{3}},\end{split}

where

T(v,\xi^{\prime})=\int e^{-iv^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}\langle D_{v^{\prime}}\rangle^{2l}\left(\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}D_{j}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\right)dv^{\prime}.

Now, we consider
\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}T(v,\xi^{\prime})
. For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, noting that

\partial_{v}^{\alpha}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\leq C(m,\alpha)\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}-|\alpha|},

then by using the Leibniz formula and Peetre’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}T(v,\xi^{\prime})\right|&\leq C(l)\sum_{|\sigma|\leq 2l}\sum_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}=\sigma}C_{\sigma}^{\sigma_{1}}\int\left|\partial^{\sigma_{1}}_{v^{\prime}}\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}\partial^{\sigma_{2}}_{v^{\prime}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}D_{j}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|dv^{\prime}\\
&\leq C(l)\sum_{|\sigma|\leq 2l}C(\sigma,\alpha_{2},m,l^{\prime})\int\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}-1-|\alpha_{2}|}dv^{\prime}\\
&\leq 2^{1+|\alpha_{2}|+\frac{|m|}{2}}C(l,l^{\prime},m,\alpha_{2})\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}-1-|\alpha_{2}|}\int\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}+1+|\alpha_{2}|+\frac{|m|}{2}}dv^{\prime}.\end{split}

Since
2l^{\prime}>\frac{|m|}{2}+|\alpha|+4
, it follows that

\left|\partial_{v}^{\alpha_{2}}T(v,\xi^{\prime})\right|\leq C(l,m,\alpha_{2},\beta)\langle v\rangle^{\frac{m}{2}-1-|\alpha_{2}|}.

Plugging it back into
\partial^{\alpha}_{v}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}R_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)
, it follows that

\begin{split}\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{v}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}R_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)\right|&\leq C(l,m,\alpha,\beta)\langle v\rangle^{-|\alpha|-1}\\
&\quad\times\sum_{|\sigma|\leq 2l^{\prime}}\int\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l}\left|\partial^{\sigma}_{\xi^{\prime}}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\partial_{\xi_{j}}M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,\eta,\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime})\right|d\xi^{\prime}.\end{split}

From Lemma
2.3
, it follows that
M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,D_{x},D_{v})\sim t\left(1-\Delta_{v}-t^{2}\Delta_{x}\right)^{\tilde{s}}
, then we have for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
,

\left|\partial^{\sigma}_{\xi}M_{2\tilde{s}}(t,\eta,\xi)\right|\leq C(s,\sigma)t(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{2\tilde{s}-|\sigma|}{2}},

so that using the fact
\theta\in]0,1[
, one has for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{3}

\begin{split}\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{v}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}R_{\theta,t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)\right|&\leq C(l^{\prime},l,m,\alpha,\beta)t\int\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l}(1+|\xi+\theta\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{2\tilde{s}-1-|\beta|}{2}}d\xi^{\prime}\\
&\leq C(s,m,\alpha,\beta)t\int\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l+1+|\beta|-2\tilde{s}}d\xi^{\prime}(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{2\tilde{s}-1-|\beta|}{2}}.\end{split}

Since
2l>4+|\beta|-2\tilde{s}
, one has for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{split}&\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{v}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}R_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)\right|\leq C(s,m,\alpha,\beta)t(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{-|\beta|}{2}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we can prove that

\begin{split}&\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{v}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\tilde{R}_{t,j}(v,\eta,\xi)\right|\leq C(s,m,\alpha,\beta)t(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{-|\beta|}{2}}.\end{split}

Hence
R_{t,j}(v,D_{x},D_{v})
and
\tilde{R}_{t,j}(v,D_{x},D_{v})
are the pseudo-differential operators of order
0
. So that for all
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})
, we have

\left\|R_{t,j}(v,D_{x},D_{v})g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\lesssim t\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})},

and

&\quad+\left(\tilde{C}_{17}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(C_{17}t\right)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{j}u_{\delta}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\right)^{2},\end{split}

with
C_{18}
depends on
C_{17}
and
\tilde{C}_{17}
.

It remains to consider the third term on the left-hand side of (
4.12
). From Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma 2.2 of
[
13
]
,

\begin{split}&\left(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta},\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right)=\left(\langle D_{v}\rangle^{-s}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta},\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right)\\
&=\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left(\langle D_{v}\rangle^{-s}[\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}},\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s}]\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta},\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right),\end{split}

as the arguments in Lemma
2.1
, there exists
C_{19}
depends on
\gamma
,
s
such that

\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{-s}[\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}},\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s}]\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\leq C_{19}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})},

hence from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\left|\left(\langle D_{v}\rangle^{-s}[\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}},\langle D_{v}\rangle^{2s}]\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta},\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right)\right|\\
&\leq C_{19}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&\leq 2(C_{19})^{2}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+\frac{1}{8}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})},\end{split}

for
\gamma>0
, using Lemma
2.6
, and for
-2s<\gamma\leq 0
, using Lemma
2.7
, one has

\begin{split}&2(C_{19})^{2}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tilde{C}_{19}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Plugging these results back into (
4.12
) and using the fact
\gamma+2s>0
, one gets

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&\quad+\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u_{\delta}(t)\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&\leq 2k^{2}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k-1}u_{\delta}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+2\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}f(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&\quad+2(C_{18}+\tilde{C}_{19})(t+1)\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\\
&\quad+2\left(\tilde{C}_{17}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(C_{17}t\right)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{j}u_{\delta}(t)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Integrating from 0 to
t
and using (
4.13
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u_{\delta}(\tau)\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\leq 2\left(B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}f(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\quad+2(C_{18}+\tilde{C}_{19})(t+1)\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\quad+2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\tilde{C}_{17}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(C_{17}\tau\right)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{j}u_{\delta}(\tau)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\right)^{2}d\tau.\end{split}

From the Minkowski inequality and (
4.13
), taking
B_{2}\geq(C_{16}T)^{3}+1
, it follows that for any
t\in[0,T]

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\left(\tilde{C}_{17}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(C_{17}\tau\right)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{j}u_{\delta}(\tau)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\right)^{2}d\tau\\
&\leq\left(\tilde{C}_{17}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(C_{17}T\right)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{j}u_{\delta}(\tau)\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\tilde{C}_{17}\sum_{j=1}^{k-3}\frac{k!\left(C_{17}T\right)^{k-(j-1)}B_{2}^{j+1}}{(k-j)!}+\tilde{C}_{17}(C_{17}T)^{3}B_{2}^{k-1}k!+\tilde{C}_{17}(C_{17}T)^{2}B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\tilde{C}_{17}(4+(C_{17}T)^{2})B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Combining these inequalities, we can get for all
0\leq t\leq T
,

\begin{split}&\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u_{\delta}\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\leq C_{20}\left(B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}f(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau+\tilde{C}_{20}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau,\end{split}

here
C_{20}=2+\left(\tilde{C}_{17}(4+(C_{17}T)^{2})\right)^{2}
and
\tilde{C}_{20}=2(C_{18}+\tilde{C}_{19})(T+1)
. Then by applying Gronwall inequality, one has for all
t\in[0,T]

\begin{split}&\left\|\Phi_{\gamma/2+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\leq 2e^{2\tilde{C}_{20}T}\left(C_{20}\left(B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}f(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\right),\end{split}

plugging it back into the above inequality, and using Lemma
4.1
, one has

\begin{split}&\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}u_{\delta}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle D_{v}\rangle^{s}\left(\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u_{\delta}\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\\
&\leq\left(2T\tilde{C}_{20}e^{2\tilde{C}_{20}T}+1\right)\left(C_{20}\left(B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Phi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+s}^{k}f(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}d\tau\right)\\
&\leq\left(2T\tilde{C}_{20}e^{2\tilde{C}_{20}T}+1\right)\left(C_{20}\left(B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}+2T(T^{k}\tilde{A}^{k+1}k!)^{2}\right).\end{split}

\begin{split}&B_{2}\geq\max\bigg\{B_{0},\tilde{A}T,\sqrt{\left(2\tilde{C}_{20}Te^{2\tilde{C}_{20}T}+1\right)\left(2\tilde{A}^{2}T+C_{20}\right)}\bigg\},\end{split}

it follows that for all

Proof of Theorem
1.2

From (
4.1
), we have

\left\|M^{k}_{2\tilde{s}}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\leq B_{1}^{k+1}k!,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Using the Plancherel theorem and Lemma
2.3
, it follows that

\begin{split}\left\|M^{k}_{2\tilde{s}}u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v}}\left|M^{k}_{2\tilde{s}}(t,\eta,\xi)\hat{u}(t,\eta,\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{3}}\\
&\geq\left(c_{s}t\right)^{2k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v}}\left|\left(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2}\right)^{\tilde{s}k}\hat{u}(t,\eta,\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{3}}.\end{split}

Hence, for any
t>0
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v}}\left|\left(1+|\xi|^{2}+t^{2}|\eta|^{2}\right)^{\tilde{s}k}\hat{u}(t,\eta,\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{3}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{t^{k}}\left(\frac{B_{1}}{c_{s}}\right)^{k+1}k!\right)^{2},\end{split}

by the Plancherel theorem, it follows that for all
t\in]0,T]

\begin{split}&\left\|(1-\Delta_{v}-t^{2}\Delta_{x})^{\tilde{s}k}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\leq\frac{1}{t^{k}}\left(\frac{B_{1}}{c_{s}}\right)^{k+1}k!,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

From (
4.10
), we can get that for all
t\in]0,T]

And therefore, combing these results and taking
C=\frac{B_{1}}{c_{s}}+B_{2}

\left\|(1-\Delta_{v}-t^{2}\Delta_{x})^{\tilde{s}k}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}+\left\|\langle v\rangle^{(\gamma/2+s)k}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}_{x,v})}\leq\frac{C^{k+1}}{t^{k}}k!,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Products of Luzin-type sets with combinatorial properties
Piotr Szewczak, Grzegorz Wiśniewski

Abstract
. We construct Luzin-type subsets of the real line in all finite powers Rothberger, with a non-Menger product. To this end, we use a purely combinatorial approach which allows to weaken assumptions used earlier to construct sets with analogous properties. Our assumptions hold, e.g., in the Random model, where already known category theoretic methods fail.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification

Primary: 54D20; Secondary: 03E17.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Combinatorial covering properties

By
space
we mean a topological space.
A space
X
is
Rothberger
if, for every sequence
\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{2},\allowbreak\dots
of open covers of the space
X
, there are sets
U_{1}\in\mathcal{U}_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{2},\dotsc
such that the family
\{\,U_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\,\}
covers
X
.
In the latter case, Rothberger’s property can be viewed as a topological version of
strong measure zero
.
We restrict our consideration to
sets of reals
, i.e., spaces homeomorphic to subsets of the real line.
In the realm of sets of reals, we have the following implications.

\text{countability }\longrightarrow\text{ Rothberger }\longrightarrow\text{ strong measure zero}

It is consistent that these properties hold only
for countable spaces and are, thus, equivalent, e.g., in the Laver model for the consistency of Borel’s conjecture
[
12
]
.
On the other hand, the Continuum Hypothesis implies that the Rothberger property is strictly in between countablility and strong measure zero.

A space
X
is
Menger
if, for every sequence
\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{2},\allowbreak\dots
of open covers of the space
X
, there are finite subsets
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{2},\dotsc
such that the family
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}_{n}
covers
X
.
Menger’s property is a generalization of Rothberger’s property, and, in the realm of sets of reals, Menger’s property is strictly in between
\sigma
-compactness and the Lindelöf property.

The above covering properties, are central in the
selection principles theory

[
23
]
.

1.2.
Luzin sets

A
Luzin
set is an uncountable set of reals whose intersection with every meager set is at most countable; its existence, is independent with ZFC.
A Luzin set is Rothberger (and thus Menger), and it is not
\sigma
-compact.
Thus, a Luzin set is a counterexample to Menger’s conjecture which says that, in the realm of sets of reals, the Menger property and
\sigma
-compactness, are equivalent
[
13
,
10
]
.
A Luzin set also solves Hurewicz’s problem, whether the Menger property is equivalent to the Hurewicz property, a formally stronger than Menger, a covering property; every Luzin set is Menger, but not Hurewicz
[
10
]
.
A detailed analysis of Luzin sets, gives a good intuition how to proceed, using combinatorics, in order to construct Menger sets of reals, with no any extra assumptions
[
7
,
4
]
.

1.3.
Products of Luzin sets

Let
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
be the minimal cardinality of a family of meager subsets of the real line, that covers the real line.
Let
\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
be the minimal cardinality of a
cofinal
family of meager subsets of the real line, i.e., every meager subset of the real line is contained in a member of the family.
A set of reals
X
is a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin
set if
\left|X\right|\geq\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
and, for every meager set
M
, we have
\left|X\cap M\right|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
Every
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set is hereditary Rothberger and, assuming that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
, a direct construction of such a set is possible.

Luzin sets and
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin sets, play an important role in investigations of products spaces with the Rothberger, or Menger properties.
Just, Miller, Scheepers, and Szeptycki proved that, under the Continuum Hypothesis, there is a Luzin set, whose square is not Menger
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]
; it was the first example showing that products of Rothberger sets, may not be Menger.
Let
\mathfrak{c}
be the cardinality of the real line.
Assuming the Continuum Hypothesis or the equality
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\mathfrak{c}
, independently, many authors proved that there are two
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin sets in all finite powers Rothberger, with a non-Menger product (
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
,
[
9
, Page 205]
,
[
16
, Theorem 13]
,
[
11
, Section 3]
,
[
3
, Theorem 4]
).
Category theoretic methods used there, have substantial limitations, which prevent to weaken assumptions.
A natural question arises, whether the existence of such
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin sets can be proven under
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
.
We show that, assuming that the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular, the answer is positive.
In particular, we prove the existence of such sets, e.g., in the Random model, where
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})=\aleph_{1}<\mathfrak{c}
.
In this model a category theoretic approach fails.

In the second part of the paper, we use already known methods, to construct, for any Luzin-type set
X
, another Luzin type-set
Y
with some additional properties (as being a topological group, or whose all finite powers are Rothberger) such that the product space
X\times Y
is not Menger.

We apply obtained results to products of function spaces with some local properties.

2.
Luzin-type sets via combinatorial approach

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers and
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
be the family of subsets of
\mathbb{N}
.
Identify every element of the set
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
with its characteristic function, an element of the Cantor cube
{\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Using this identification, we introduce a topology in
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
.
Since the Cantor cube is homeomorphic to Cantor’s set, every subspace of
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
is a set of reals.
Let
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
be the set of all infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
.
We identify each set
a\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
with its increasing enumeration, an element of the Baire space
{\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}}
.
For each natural number
n
, by
a(n)
denote the
n
-th smallest element of the set
a
.
Thus, we have
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Relative topologies in the set
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, from the Baire space
{\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}}
and from the Cantor space
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, are equal.
For elements
x,y\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, we write
x\leq^{*}y
, if
x

is dominated
by
y
, i.e., if the set
\{\,n:y(n)<x(n)\,\}
is finite.
A subset of
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
is
dominating
if every element in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
is dominated by an element from this set.
Analogously, we define relation
\leq^{*}
in
{\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}}
, and a dominating subset of
{\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{d}
be the minimal cardinality of a dominating set in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
(equivalently in
{\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}}
).
For elements
x,y\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, define
\max\{x,y\}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that
\max\{x,y\}(n):=\max\{x(n),y(n)\}
, for all natural numbers
n
.
A set
X\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
is
2-dominating
, if the set
\{\,\max\{x,y\}:x,y\in X\,\}
is dominating.

The cardinal numbers
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
and
\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
do not change if, in their definitions, consider families of meager subsets of
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, instead of the real line.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
and the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular.
There are
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin sets
X,Y\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that:

(1)

all finite powers of
X
and
Y
are Rothberger,

(2)

the product space
X\times Y
is not Menger,

(3)

the union
X\cup Y
is
2
-dominating.

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
and later discussion, we need the following auxiliary results and notations.
A set
r\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}

reaps
a family
A\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
if, for each set
a\in A
, both sets
a\cap r
and
a\setminus r
are infinite.
Let
\mathfrak{r}
be the minimal cardinality of a family
A\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
that no set
r
reaps.
For elements
x,y\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, we write
x\leq^{\infty}y
if
y\not\leq^{*}x
, i.e., if the set
\{\,n:x(n)<y(n)\,\}
is infinite.
For a set
Z\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
and an element
x\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, we write
Z\leq^{\infty}x
if
z\leq^{\infty}x
for all elements
z\in Z
.
We use this convention to any binary relation in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.
For a set
x\in{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, define
x^{\text{\tt c}}:=\mathbb{N}\setminus x
.
For an element
z\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
with
z(1)\neq 1
, define
\tilde{z}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that
\tilde{z}(1):=z(1)
, and
\tilde{z}(n+1):=z(\tilde{z}(n))
, for all natural numbers
n
.
For natural numbers
j,k
with
j<k
, let
[j,k):=\{\,j,j+1,\dotsc,k-1\,\}
.
For a set
a\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
and a function
f\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, define

a/f:=\{\,n\in\mathbb{N}:a\cap[f(n),f(n+1))\neq\emptyset\,\}.

The following combinatorial characterization of meager subsets of
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
is an important tool, in our constructions.

Lemma 2.2

(Bartoszyński, Judah
[
2
, Theorem 2.2.4]
)
.

Let
M\subseteq{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
be a meager set.
There are a set
a\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
and a function
f\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that the set
M
is contained in the meager set

\{\,x\in{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}:x\cap[f(n),f(n+1))\neq a\cap[f(n),f(n+1))\text{ for all but finitely many }n\,\}.

Let
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
be the minimal cardinality of a set
A\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that, for each function
b\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, there is a set
a\in A
such that, the set
\{\,n:\left|a\cap[b(n),b(n+1))\right|\geq 2\,\}
is finite.
The following Lemma, used in our main result, is essentially due to Blass
[
6
, Theorem 2.10]
.
We provide a proof, for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(
\mathfrak{d}\leq\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
):
Let
A\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
be a set from the definition of the number
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
, of cardinality
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
, closed under finite modifications of its elements.

The set
A
is dominating in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
:
Fix an element
b\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.
There is a set
a\in A
such that the set
\{\,n:\left|a\cap[b(n),b(n+1))\right|\geq 2\,\}
is finite.
Since the set
A
is closed under finite modifications of its elements, we may assume that, this finite set is empty, and
b(1)<a(1)
.
Fix a natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
b(n)\leq a(n)
.
If
a(n+1)<b(n+1)
, then
b(n)\leq a(n)<a(n+1)<b(n+1)
, and thus
\left|a\cap[b(n),b(n+1))\right|\geq 2
, a contradiction.
It follows that
b(n+1)\leq a(n+1)
.
We have
b\leq^{*}a
, and thus the set
A
is dominating in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.

(
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\leq\mathfrak{d}
):
Let
D\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
be a dominating set, of cardinality
\mathfrak{d}
, closed under finite modifications of its elements.

The set
\{\,\tilde{d}:d\in D\,\}
satisfies the condition from the definition of the number
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
:
Fix an element
b\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.
There exists en element
d\in D
such that
b(n)<d(n)
, for all natural numbers
n
.
Fix a natural number
n
.
If there is a natural number
k
with
b(n)\leq\tilde{d}(k)<\tilde{d}(k+1)<b(n+1)
, then

d(\tilde{d}(k))=\tilde{d}(k+1)<b(n+1)\leq b(\tilde{d}(k)),

a contradiction.
Thus,
\{\,n:|\,\tilde{d}\cap[b(n),b(n+1))\,|\geq 2\,\}=\emptyset
.
∎

We have the following inequalities between considered cardinals
[
6
]
:

\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathfrak{d}\leq\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})\text{ and }\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathfrak{r}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be a family of meager sets in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
with
\left|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, and
Z\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
be a set with
\left|Z\right|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
Let
d\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.
There are elements
x,y\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
such that
Z\leq^{\infty}x,Z\leq^{\infty}y
, and
d\leq^{*}\max\{x^{\text{\tt c}},y^{\text{\tt c}}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{\,M_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}
, for some ordinal number
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
Fix an ordinal number
\beta<\alpha
.
By Lemma
2.2
, there are a set
a_{\beta}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
and a function
f_{\beta}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that the set
M_{\beta}
is contained in the meager set

\{\,y\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}:y\cap[f_{\beta}(n),f_{\beta}(n+1))\neq a_{\beta}\cap[f_{\beta}(n),f_{\beta}(n+1))\text{ for all but finitely many }n\,\}.

Thus, we may assume that the set
M_{\beta}
is equal to the above meager set.
Since
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, there is a set
a\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}\setminus\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}M_{\beta}
.
Then the sets

f^{\prime}_{\beta}:=\{\,f_{\beta}(n):a\cap[f_{\beta}(n),f_{\beta}(n+1))=a_{\beta}\cap[f_{\beta}(n),f_{\beta}(n+1)),n\in\mathbb{N}\,\}

are infinite, for all ordinal numbers
\beta<\alpha
.

Assume that
Z=\{\,z_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}
.
We have
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\mathfrak{d}
.
By Lemma
2.3
, there is a function
b\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that the sets

I_{\beta}:=\{\,n:|\,\tilde{z}_{\beta}/\tilde{d}\cap(b(n),b(n+1))\,|\geq 2\,\},J_{\beta}:=\{\,n:|\,f^{\prime}_{\beta}/\tilde{d}\cap(b(n),b(n+1))\,|\geq 2\,\}

are infinite for all ordinal numbers
\beta<\alpha
.

Since
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathfrak{r}
, there is a decomposition of the set
\mathbb{N}
into infinite sets
r,s,t,u
, each of them reaps the family
\{\,I_{\beta},J_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}
.
Define

\displaystyle x:=\bigcup_{n\in r\cup u}\bigl[\tilde{d}(b(n)),\tilde{d}(b(n+1)+1)\bigr)\cup\bigcup_{n\in s}a\cap\bigl[\tilde{d}(b(n)),\tilde{d}(b(n+1)+1))\bigr),

Fix an ordinal number
\beta<\alpha
and a natural number
n\in s\cap J_{\beta}
.
There is a natural number
i
such that

f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i),f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i+1)\in[\tilde{d}(b(n)),\tilde{d}(b(n+1)))

Thus,

x\cap[f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i),f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i+1))=a\cap[f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i),f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i+1))=a_{\beta}\cap[f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i),f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i+1)).

Let
j
be a natural number such that
f_{\beta}(j)=f^{\prime}_{\beta}(i).

We have

x\cap[f_{\beta}(j),f_{\beta}(j+1))=a_{\beta}\cap[f_{\beta}(j),f_{\beta}(j+1)).

Since the set
s\cap J_{\beta}
is infinite, the set

\{\,n\in\mathbb{N}:x\cap[f_{\beta}(j),f_{\beta}(j+1))=a_{\beta}\cap[f_{\beta}(j),f_{\beta}(j+1))\,\}

is infinite, too.
Thus,
x\notin\bigcup\mathcal{M}^{\prime}.

Analogously, we get

.

Fix an ordinal number
\beta<\alpha
and a natural number
n\in t\cap I_{\beta}
.
Since

|\,\tilde{z}_{\beta}/\tilde{d}\cap(b(n),b(n+1))\,|\geq 2,

\tilde{z}_{\beta}(i),\tilde{z}_{\beta}(i+1)\in[\tilde{d}(b(n)+1),\tilde{d}(b(n+1))).

Since

The set

.
Analogously, we get

.

Fix a natural number
k\geq\tilde{d}(b(1))
.
There is a natural number

such that
k\in[\tilde{d}(b(n)),\tilde{d}(b(n+1)))
.
Assume that

.
Since
x^{\text{\tt c}}\cap[\tilde{d}(b(n)),\tilde{d}(b(n+1)+1))=\emptyset
, we have

d(k)<d(\tilde{d}(b(n+1)))=\tilde{d}(b(n+1)+1)\leq x^{\text{\tt c}}(k).

Analogously, if
n\in t\cup s
, then

.
Thus,

.

A
filter
is a subset of
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
with empty intersection, that is closed under finite intersections and taking supersets.
An
ultrafilter
is a maximal filter.
For elements
x,y\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, let
[x\leq y]:=\{\,n:x(n)\leq y(n)\,\}
.
For a set
F\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
and elements
x,y\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, we write
x\leq_{F}y
, if
[x\leq y]\in F
.
If the above set
F
is a filter, then the relation
\leq_{F}
is transitive.
Let
U
be an ultrafilter.
A set
X\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
is
\leq_{U}
-unbounded
if, for each element
b\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, there is an element
x\in X
with
b\leq_{U}x
.
Let
\mathfrak{b}(U)
be the minimal cardinality of a
\leq_{U}
-unbounded subset of
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.

Definition 2.5

(Szewczak, Tsaban
[
19
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
U
be an ultrafilter.
A set
X\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
with
\left|X\right|\geq\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)
is a

if, for each element

\left|\{\,x\in X:x\leq_{U}b\,\}\right|<\mathfrak{b}(U).

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
and the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})

There are ultrafilters

and sets

(1)

the set
X
is a
U
-scale and a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set,

(2)

the set
Y
is a
\tilde{U}
-scale and a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set,

(3)

the set
\{\,\max\{x^{\text{\tt c}},y^{\text{\tt c}}\}:x\in X,y\in Y\,\}

{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\,d_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}
be a dominating set in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
and
\{\,M_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}

.

Let
F_{0}=\tilde{F}_{0}=\{\mathbb{N}\}
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
be elements such that
d_{0}\leq^{*}\max\{{x}_{0}^{\text{\tt c}},{y}_{0}^{\text{\tt c}}\}
.
Fix an ordinal number
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
We show that there are elements
x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
and sets
F_{\alpha},\tilde{F}_{\alpha}\subseteq{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that:

(i)

the sets
F_{\alpha},\tilde{F}_{\alpha}
are closed under finite intersections,

(ii)

\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}F_{\beta}\subseteq F_{\alpha}
,
\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\tilde{F}_{\beta}\subseteq\tilde{F}_{\alpha}
,

(iii)

\left|F_{\alpha}\right|,|\,\tilde{F}_{\alpha}\,|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
,

(iv)

\{\,d_{\beta},x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\leq_{F_{\alpha}}x_{\alpha},\{\,d_{\beta},y_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\leq_{\tilde{F}_{\alpha}}y_{\alpha}
,

(v)

x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\notin\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}M_{\beta}
,

(vi)

d_{\alpha}\leq^{*}\max\{{x}_{\alpha}^{\text{\tt c}},{y}_{\alpha}^{\text{\tt c}}\}
:

Let

F=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}F_{\beta}\text{ and }\tilde{F}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\tilde{F}_{\beta}.

The cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular, and thus
\left|F\right|,|\,\tilde{F}\,|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})

Define

3.1.
Products of Luzin-type sets in
{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathbb{N}}}

Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of integers.
Let

\displaystyle:=f(n)+g(n),

\displaystyle|f+g|(n)

\displaystyle:=|f(n)+g(n)|,

\displaystyle:=f(n)-g(n),

\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
and

\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})

.
The set

\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})

Assume that

3.2.
Products of Luzin-type groups in
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}

A
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin group
, is a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set that is a topological group.

Let
a,b\in{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
and
A,B\subseteq{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
.
Define

\displaystyle a\oplus b:=

\displaystyle(a\cup b)\setminus(a\cap b),

\displaystyle a\oplus B:=

\displaystyle\{\,a\oplus b:b\in B\,\},

\displaystyle A\oplus B:=

\displaystyle\{\,a\oplus b:a\in A,b\in B\,\}.

The space
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, with the group operation
\oplus
, is a topological group.
For
Y\subseteq{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, and
y\in{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
,
let
\langle Y\rangle
be the group generated by the set
Y
, and
\langle A,x\rangle:=\langle A\cup\{x\}\rangle
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
.
There exist
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin groups
X,Y\subseteq{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
such that the product space
X\times Y
is not Menger.

Proof.

Let
\{\,d_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}
be a dominating set in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.
Let
\{\,M_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}
be a cofinal family of meager sets in
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, and
\tilde{M}_{\alpha}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}M_{\beta}\cup\{\,x^{\text{\tt c}}:x\in\mathrm{Fin}\,\}
, for all ordinal numbers
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.

By transfinite induction on ordinal numbers
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, pick elements
x_{\alpha}
,
y_{\alpha}\in{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, construct increasing groups
X_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha}
with
\left|X_{\alpha}\right|,\left|Y_{\alpha}\right|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
and groups
\tilde{X}_{\alpha}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}X_{\beta}
,
\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}Y_{\beta}
such that

(i)

x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\notin\tilde{M}_{\alpha}\oplus(\tilde{X}_{\alpha}\cup\tilde{Y}_{\alpha})
,

(ii)

d_{\alpha}\leq^{*}\max\{{x}_{\alpha}^{\text{\tt c}},{y}_{\alpha}^{\text{\tt c}}\}
,

(iii)

(X_{\alpha}\setminus\tilde{X}_{\alpha})\cap\tilde{M}_{\alpha}=\emptyset,(Y_{\alpha}\setminus\tilde{Y}_{\alpha})\cap\tilde{M}_{\alpha}=\emptyset
:

Fix an ordinal number
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
By Lemma
2.4
, applied to the set
\tilde{M}_{\alpha}\oplus(\tilde{X}_{\alpha}\cup\tilde{Y}_{\alpha})
, a union of less than
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
meager sets, and to the function
d_{\alpha}
, there are elements
x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
which satisfies
(i)
and
(ii)
.
Let
X_{\alpha}:=\langle\tilde{X}_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}\rangle
and
Y_{\alpha}:=\langle\tilde{Y}_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\rangle
.
We have
(X_{\alpha}\setminus\tilde{X}_{\alpha})\cap\tilde{M}_{\alpha}=\emptyset
:
Suppose not.
Then there are elements
x\in\tilde{X}_{\alpha}
and
m\in\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
such that
x\oplus x_{\alpha}=m
.
We have
x_{\alpha}=x\oplus m
, and thus
x_{\alpha}\in\tilde{X}_{\alpha}\oplus\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
, a contradiction.
Similarly, we have
(Y_{\alpha}\setminus\tilde{Y}_{\alpha})\cap\tilde{M}_{\alpha}=\emptyset
.

By
(iii)
, the sets
X:=\bigcup_{\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})}X_{\alpha}
and
Y:=\bigcup_{\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})}Y_{\alpha}
are
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin groups.

The product space
X\times Y
is not Menger:
By
(iii)
and the definition of the sets
\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
we have
x^{\text{\tt c}}\notin\mathrm{Fin}
, for all elements
x\in X\cup Y
.
Thus, the continuous image of the product space
X\times Y
, under the map
(x,y)\mapsto\max\{{x}^{\text{\tt c}},{y}^{\text{\tt c}}\}
, is in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
.
By
(ii)
, the image contains a dominating set

\{\,\max\{{x_{\alpha}}^{\text{\tt c}},{y_{\alpha}}^{\text{\tt c}}\}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}
.
Thus, the product space
X\times Y
is not Menger
[
14
, Proposition 3]
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
and the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular.
For every
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin group
X\subseteq{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, there is a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin group
Y\subseteq{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
such that the product space
X\times Y
is not Menger.

Proof.

Let
\{\,d_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}
be a dominating set in
{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
such that
d_{\alpha}^{\text{\tt c}}\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}
, for all ordinal numbers
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
The sets
B_{\alpha}:=\{\,b\in{[\mathbb{N}]^{\mbox{\tiny$\infty$}}}:d_{\alpha}\leq^{*}b\,\}
are meager sets of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, for all ordinal numbers
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
Let
\{\,M_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}
be a cofinal family of meager sets in
{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
, and
\tilde{M}_{\alpha}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}M_{\beta}
, for all ordinal numbers
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.

By transfinite induction on ordinal numbers
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, pick elements
b_{\alpha}
,
y_{\alpha}\in{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
and construct increasing groups
Y_{\alpha}
with
\left|Y_{\alpha}\right|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, and
\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}Y_{\beta}
such that:

(i)

b_{\alpha}\in B_{\alpha}\setminus\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{Fin}\oplus X
,

(ii)

y_{\alpha}\in(b_{\alpha}\oplus X)\setminus\bigl(\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\oplus\tilde{M}_{\alpha}\cup\{\,y_{\alpha}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\bigr)

(iii)

(Y_{\alpha}\setminus\tilde{Y}_{\alpha})\cap\tilde{M}_{\alpha}=\emptyset
.

Since the set
B_{0}
is a meager set of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, and
\mathrm{Fin}\oplus X
is a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set, there is an element

b_{0}\in B_{0}\setminus(\mathrm{Fin}\oplus X).

Let

y_{0}\in b_{0}\oplus X,

and
Y_{0}:=\langle y_{0}\rangle
.
Fix an ordinal number
\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
Let
\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}:=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}Y_{\beta}
.
Since the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular, we have
|\,\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\,|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
Thus, the set
\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{Fin}\oplus L
, a union of less than
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
sets that are
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin sets, is a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set.
Since
B_{\alpha}
is a meager set of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, there is an element

b_{\alpha}\in B_{\alpha}\setminus(\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{Fin}\oplus X).

Since the set
b_{\alpha}\oplus X
is a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set and the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular, there is an element

y_{\alpha}\in(b_{\alpha}\oplus X)\setminus\bigl(\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\oplus\tilde{M}_{\alpha}\cup\{\,x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}\bigr).

We have
b_{\alpha}\notin\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{Fin}\oplus X
, the set
X
is a group, and thus
(y_{\alpha}\oplus X)\cap(\tilde{Y}_{\beta}\oplus\mathrm{Fin}\oplus X)=\emptyset
.
Let
Y_{\alpha}:=\langle\tilde{Y}_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\rangle
.

We have
(Y_{\alpha}\setminus\tilde{Y}_{\alpha})\cap\tilde{M}_{\alpha}=\emptyset
:
Suppose not.
Then there are elements
y\in\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}
and
m\in\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
such that
y\oplus y_{\alpha}=m
.
We have
y_{\alpha}=y\oplus m
, and thus
y_{\alpha}\in\tilde{Y}_{\alpha}\oplus\tilde{M}_{\alpha}

By
(iii)

is a
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin set, and since
\{\,Y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})\,\}

The product space

is not Menger:
Let
Z\subseteq{\operatorname{P}(\mathbb{N})}
be the image of the product space

, under the map

.
Suppose that there are elements
x\in X
,

.
Then there is an ordinal number

.

.
Since

∎

4.
Applications to function spaces

For a set of reals
X
, let
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
be the space of all continuous real-valued functions on the spaces
X
with the topology of pointwise convergence.
Properties of the set
X
can describe local properties of the space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
, and vice versa.
A space
Y
has
countable fan tightness

[
1
]
if, for each point
y\in Y
and for every sequence
U_{1},\allowbreak U_{2},\allowbreak\dots
of subsets of the space
Y
with
y\in\bigcap_{n}\overline{U_{n}}
, there are finite sets
F_{1}\subseteq U_{1},F_{2}\subseteq U_{2},\dotsc
such that
y\in\overline{\bigcup_{n}F_{n}}
.
If we request that the above sets
F_{1},\allowbreak F_{2},\allowbreak\dots
are singletons, then the space
Y
has
countable strong fan tightness

[
15
]
.
A space is
M-separable

[
5
]
if, for every sequence
D_{1},\allowbreak D_{2},\allowbreak\dots
of dense subsets of the space, there are finite sets
F_{1}\subseteq D_{1},F_{2}\subseteq D_{2},\dotsc
such that the union
\bigcup_{n}F_{n}
is a dense subset of the space.
If we request that, the above sets
D_{1},\allowbreak D_{2},\allowbreak\dots
are singletons, then the space is
R-separable

[
5
]
.

Let
X
be a set of reals.
By the results of Sakai
[
15
]
, and Scheepers
[
17
, Theorem 13]
, the statements:
all finite powers of the set
X
are Rothberger, the space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
has countable strong fan tightness, the space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
is R-separable, are equivalent.
By the results of Just, Miller, Scheepers, and Szeptycki
[
17
, Theorem 3.9]
, and Scheepers
[
17
, Theorem 35]
, the statements: all finite powers of the set
X
are Menger, the space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
has countable fan tightness, the space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
is M-separable, are equivalent, too.

Let
X
and
Y
be sets of reals, and
X\sqcup Y
be a topological sum of these sets.
The product space
X\times Y
is a closed subspace of the product space
(X\sqcup Y)^{2}
.
Thus, if
X\times Y
is not Menger, then
(X\sqcup Y)^{2}
is not Menger, too.
Since the product space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)\times\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(Y)
is homeomorphic to the space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X\sqcup Y)
, we have the following corollaries from Theorem
2.1
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
and the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular.
There are
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
-Luzin sets
X
and
Y
such that the spaces
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
and
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(Y)
have countable strong fan tightness (they are R-separable) but their product space does not have countable fan tightness (is not M-separable).

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
and the cardinal number
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is regular.
There are hereditary Rothberger sets of reals
X
and
Y
such that the spaces
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)
and
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(Y)
have countable strong fan tightness (they are R-separable), but the product space
\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(X)\times\operatorname{C}_{\mathrm{p}}(Y)
does not have countable fan tightness (is not M-separable).

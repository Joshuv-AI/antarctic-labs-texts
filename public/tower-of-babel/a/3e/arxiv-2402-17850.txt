Relation between the minimal Lorentz surfaces in ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{2} and ℝ 1 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
Krasimir Kanchev, Ognian Kassabov, Velichka Milousheva

Abstract
. In this paper we give Weierstrass-type representation formulas for the null curves and for the minimal Lorentz surfaces in the Minkowski 3-space ℝ 1 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}_{1} using real-valued functions. Applying the Weierstrass-type representations for the null curves, we find a correspondence between the null curves in ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{2} and the pairs of null curves in ℝ 1 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}_{1} . Based on this correspondence, we obtain a relation between the minimal Lorentz surfaces in ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{2} and the pairs of minimal Lorentz surfaces in ℝ 1 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}_{1} .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53B30; Secondary 53A35; 53A10; 53B25

1.
Introduction

The study of minimal surfaces in different ambient spaces is one of the main topics in both classical and modern differential geometry
and has been attracting the attention of many mathematicians for more than two centuries.
In the last years, the geometry of Lorentz surfaces in pseudo-Euclidean spaces has been of wide interest, since pseudo-Riemannian geometry has many
interesting applications in Physics.

One of the instruments in the study of minimal surfaces in the Euclidean space is the generalized Gauss map
[
15
]
.
In
[
10
]
, Gálvez et al. obtained a representation for spacelike surfaces in the Minkowski three-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}

using the Gauss map and the conformal structure given by the second fundamental form. Timelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}

with prescribed Gauss curvature and Gauss map were studied in
[
1
]
, where a Kenmotsu-type representation for these surfaces was given.
In
[
20
]
, M. Magid proved that the Gauss map and the mean curvature of a timelike surface
satisfy a system of partial differential equations and found a Weierstrass representation formula for timelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.

A representation of a minimal Lorentz surface was given by M. Dussan and M. Magid in
[
8
]
, where they solved the Björling problem for timelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
. The Björling problem for timelike surfaces in the Lorentz-Minkowski spaces
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
was solved in
[
4
]
and
[
9
]
, respectively.
Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
were studied in
[
3
]
and
[
21
]
by use of spinors and Lorentz numbers (also known as para-complex, split-complex, double or hyperbolic numbers), and a generalized Weierstrass representation of a minimal Lorentz surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
was given in
[
21
]
.
Many other researches have studied spacelike and timelike surfaces in a 3-dimensional Lorentz space via Weierstrass-type formulas, see e.g.
[
6
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
22
]
.

On the other hand, the class of minimal surfaces in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and the classes of spacelike or timelike surfaces with vanishing mean curvature in the pseudo-Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
are characterized by special systems of PDEs (called natural systems) for the Gauss curvature
K
and the normal curvature
\varkappa
. Explicit solutions to these systems of PDEs can be found by use of a special type of Weierstrass representation (in terms of canonical coordinates), which is called canonical Weierstrass-type representation. This problem is solved for the class of minimal spacelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
in
[
12
]
. In the case of a minimal Lorentz surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, the problem is solved in
[
17
]
.

A correspondence between the solutions of the system of natural PDEs describing the minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}

and the pairs of solutions to the natural PDEs of the minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is
obtained in
[
11
]
.
Using this correspondence one can find also a correspondence between the minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}

and the pairs of minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Applying a similar approach, a relation between the
maximal spacelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and the pairs of maximal spacelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
is found in
[
13
]
.

The main purpose of the present paper is to find a correspondence of this kind between the minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}

and the pairs of minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
As the case of Lorentz surfaces is much more complicated than the case of spacelike surfaces,
we use a different method, applying the theory of the Weierstrass-type representations for the null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, which was developed in
[
16
]
.
First, we use the main definitions and results from
[
16
]
and obtain a correspondence between the null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and the pairs of null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.

It is known, that there exists a Weierstrass-type representation for the minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
,
analogous to the classical Weierstrass representation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, see
[
18
]
.
An analogous natural representation for the surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
exists.
In this paper, we apply the method developed in
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
which is based on the Weierstrass-type representation for the null curves.
This method has the obvious advantage that the surfaces and their invariants are
expressed in terms of real-valued functions.

In Sections 3 and 4, we recall the Weierstrass-type representation for the null curves and for the minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and give similar Weierstrass-type representation formulas for the null curves and for the minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
using real-valued functions. We also define canonical coordinates and give canonical Weierstrass-type representation formulas for minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
. Using the Weierstrass-type representation for the null curves, in Section 5, we give a relation between the non-degenerate null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
In Section 6, we use the canonical isotropic coordinates for the minimal Lorentz surfaces and obtain a relation between the set of minimal Lorentz
surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and the set of ordered pairs of minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
Finally, we give formulas expressing the Gauss curvature
K
and the normal curvature
\varkappa
of a minimal Lorenz surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
in terms of the Gauss curvatures of the corresponding minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.

2.
Preliminaries

We denote by
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
, resp.
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, the standard three-dimensional, resp. four-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space
with the indefinite scalar product

\mathrm{a}\cdot\mathrm{b}=-a_{1}b_{1}+a_{2}b_{2}+a_{3}b_{3},\,

resp.

\mathrm{a}\cdot\mathrm{b}=-a_{1}b_{1}+a_{2}b_{2}-a_{3}b_{3}+a_{4}b_{4}\,.

All considered functions, curves and surfaces in this paper are supposed to be real and smooth.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{D},\mathrm{x})
be a Lorentz surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
\mathrm{x}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
is an immersion.
It is known (see e.g.
[
5
]
) that for every minimal Lorentz surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}

there exists a pair
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
of null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, such that

\alpha^{\prime 2}_{1}=0
,
\alpha^{\prime 2}_{2}=0
,
{\alpha^{\prime}_{1}\cdot\alpha^{\prime}_{2}\neq 0}
and

(2.1)

\mathrm{x}(t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{\alpha_{1}(t_{1})+\alpha_{2}(t_{2})}{2}\,,

where
(t_{1},t_{2})
is a pair of isotropic coordinates for
\mathcal{M}
.
This implies that with respect to such parametrization the coefficients
E
,
F
,
G
of the first fundamental form of
\mathcal{M}
are expressed as follows:

E=G=0\,;\qquad F=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}_{1}\cdot\alpha^{\prime}_{2}}{4}\,.

Hence, the change of the sign of one of the isotropic coordinates
(t_{1},t_{2})
changes the sign of

F
. Consequently, without loss of generality we may suppose that
F>0
(or
F<0
) at each point of
\mathcal{M}
.

On the other hand, if
\mathrm{x}
is parametrized by isothermal coordinates, then
\mathcal{M}
is minimal if and only if

\mathrm{x}
is hyperbolic harmonic (see
[
16
]
).
Then, we can introduce a function
\mathrm{y}
, which is hyperbolic harmonic conjugate to
\mathrm{x}
.
The induced minimal Lorentz surface is called conjugate to the given surface.
In isotropic coordinates
(t_{1},t_{2})
, we have:

(2.2)

\mathrm{y}(t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{\alpha_{1}(t_{1})-\alpha_{2}(t_{2})}{2}\,.

The null curve
\alpha
is called
nondegenerate
if
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}\neq 0
everywhere.
Note that if we make the parameter change
t=t(s),\,t^{\prime}\neq 0
, in the null curve
\alpha(t)

then

{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{s}}^{2}={\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{t}}^{2}{t^{\prime}}^{4}
.
This implies that the degeneracity of a null curve does not depend on the parametrization.
A minimal Lorentz surface
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
is said to be of
general type
, if
its corresponding null curves
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are both nondegenerate

[
14
]
,
[
16
]
.
This definition does not depend on the local coordinates and is invariant
under a motion in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
14
]
,
[
16
]

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a minimal Lorentz surface of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
with corresponding null curves

{\alpha_{1}}
,
{\alpha_{2}}
. We say that the surface
\mathcal{M}
is of

•

first type
, if both vectors
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{1}}
,
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{2}}

are spacelike;

•

second type
, if both vectors
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{1}}
,
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{2}}

are timelike;

•

third type
, if one of the vectors
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{1}}
,
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{2}}

is spacelike and the other one is timelike.

Note that this definition does not depend on the choice of the local coordinates and is
invariant under a motion in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
. Moreover, for a surface ot third type
we may suppose
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{1}}^{2}>0
and
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{2}}^{2}<0

(otherwise we may renumerate the curves and the parameters).

A non-degenerate curve
\alpha
is said to be parametrized by a
natural parameter
,
if
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=\pm 1
. Since this parameter plays a role
similar to the role of an arc-length parameter for non-null curves, it is known in the literature as
pseudo arc-length parameter

[
24
,
7
]
.
If
t
is an arbitrary parameter of
\alpha
, then a natural parameter
s
is given by

s=\int\sqrt[4]{\big|{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}(t)\big|}\>dt\,.

Definition 2.2
.

[
16
]

If
t_{1}
, resp.
t_{2}
is a natural parameter of
\alpha_{1}
, resp.
\alpha_{2}
, then, we say that
(t_{1},t_{2})

are
canonical coordinates
of the corresponding minimal Lorentz surface of general type
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
,
(if
\mathcal{M}
is of third type we suppose
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{1}}^{2}=1
and
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{2}}^{2}=-1
).

If
t
and
s
are two natural parameters of
\alpha
, then
it follows that
t^{\prime}(s)=\pm 1
. Consequently,

(2.3)

t=\pm s+c\,,\qquad c=\textnormal{const}\,.

This shows that the canonical coordinates of a minimal Lorentz surface of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}

are defined up to numeration, sign and additive constants. For the surfaces of third type the numeration is fixed, since

{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{1}}^{2}=1
and
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{2}}^{2}=-1
.

Note that we can consider
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
as a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, defined by
x_{3}=0
.
So, every object in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
can be considered as an object in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
.
Hence, the above remarks about the existence and uniqueness of the natural parameters of curves
and the canonical coordinates of the surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
hold also for
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
Moreover, the minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
considered as surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
are always of first type,
since
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}\geq 0
for the null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.

The main invariants of a minimal Lorentz surface
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
are the
Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection (normal curvature)
\varkappa
.
The following statement holds true.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
14
]

A minimal Lorentz surface
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
is of general type if and only if

K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}\neq 0
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}
is of first or second type (resp. of third type)
if and only if
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
(resp.
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}<0
).

3.
Weierstrass-type representation for null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}

The null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
can be determined by a triple of real functions as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
16
]

Let
\alpha
be a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})
satisfies
\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\neq 0
.
Then,
\alpha^{\prime}
has the form

(3.1)

\alpha^{\prime}=f\big(\,gh+1\,,\,gh-1\,,\,h-g\,,\,h+g\,\big),

where the functions
f\neq 0
,
g
,
h
are uniquely determined by

f=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\,;\qquad g=\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{4}-\xi_{3}}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}\;;\qquad h=\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{4}+\xi_{3}}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}\;.

Conversely, let
(f,g,h)
be a triple of functions,
f\neq 0
.
Then there exists a null curve
\alpha
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
such that (
3.1
) holds and

\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\neq 0
.

Remark 3.1
.

The restriction
\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\neq 0
in the last theorem is not essential for the geometric properties of the curve,
because every null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
with
\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}=0

can be transformed by a proper motion to a curve satisfying the condition
\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\neq 0
.

Now, consider the null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
Note that (
3.1
) implies that
x_{3}=0
is equivelent to
g=h
.
So, we obtain the following analogue of Theorem
3.1
:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
, such that
\alpha^{\prime}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
satisfies
\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\neq 0
.
Then,
\alpha^{\prime}
has the form:

(3.2)

\alpha^{\prime}=f\big(\,g^{2}+1\,,\,g^{2}-1\,,\,2g\,\big),

where
f\neq 0
and
g
are functions, given uniquely by

(3.3)

f=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\,;\qquad g=\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{3}}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}\;.

Conversely, let
(f,g)
be a pair of functions,
f\neq 0
.
Then, there exists a null curve
\alpha
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
, such that

\alpha^{\prime}
has the form (
3.2
) and

\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\neq 0
.

Formulas (
3.2
) and (
3.1
) are analogous to the classical Weierstrass formula for minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
So, we call them the
Weierstrass-type representation
of a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
, resp.
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
,

gererated by the pair of functions
(f,g)
, resp.
the triple of functions
(f,g,h)
.

In what follows, we find the transformation formulas under proper motions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
for the functions that participate in (
3.2
).
First, we consider a proper orthochronous motion.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\hat{\alpha}
and
\alpha
be null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}

having Weierstrass-type representations of the form (
3.2
).
Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\hat{\alpha}
and
\alpha
are related by a proper orthochronous motion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
of the type

\hat{\alpha}(t)=A\alpha(t)+\mathrm{b}
, where
A\in\mathbf{SO}^{+}(2,1,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.

(2)

The functions in the Weierstrass-type representation formulas for

\hat{\alpha}
and
\alpha
are related by

(3.4)

\hat{f}=f(cg+d)^{2}\,;\qquad\hat{g}=\displaystyle\frac{ag+b}{cg+d}\,;\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\,;\;\;ad-bc=1\,.

Proof.

For any vector
\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
we define a
2\times 2
matrix
S
by:

\mathrm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\ \leftrightarrow\ S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-x_{3}&x_{2}+x_{1}\\
x_{2}-x_{1}&x_{3}\end{array}\right).

It is easy to see that the above correspondence gives a linear isomorphism between
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
and the linear space of all
2\times 2

real matrices with vanishing trace. Moreover, this isomorphism satisfies the condition

\det S=-\mathrm{x}^{2}
. So, every linear operator, acting in the space of

2\times 2
real matrices, which preserves the determinant and the trace,
determines an orthogonal operator in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
If
B\in\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
,
then
\det BSB^{-1}=\det S
and
\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}\nolimits BSB^{-1}=\mathop{\mathrm{trace}}\nolimits S
.
For each such matrix
B
there is an orthogonal matrix
A\in\mathbf{O}(2,1,\mathbb{R})
corresponding to
B
.
Consequently, there exists a group homomorphism
B\rightarrow A
:

(3.5)

\hat{S}=BSB^{-1}\ \rightarrow\ \hat{\mathrm{x}}=A\mathrm{x}\,.

This homomorphism from
\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
in
\mathbf{O}(2,1,\mathbb{R})

is a spinor map.
The kernel of this map has two elements (see
[
23
]
):
I
and
-I
,

I
being the unit matrix, and the image coincides with the connected component
of the unit element in
\mathbf{O}(2,1,\mathbb{R})
.
This is the group
\mathbf{SO}^{+}(2,1,\mathbb{R})
of proper orthochronous motions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.
The form of the kernel and the image of (
3.5
) imply that the map (
3.5
)
gives rise to a group isomorphism:

\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\{I,-I\}\ \cong\ \mathbf{SO}^{+}(2,1,\mathbb{R})\,.

Applying this to the null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
,
we derive the assertion.
∎

Every proper non-orthochronous motion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
is a composition
of a proper orthochronous motion with a proper non-orthochronous motion,
obtained with changing the signs of the first two coordinates,
i.e. with changing the signs of
f
and
g
. Hence, the transformation formulas
for a proper non-orthochronous motion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
are:

(3.6)

\hat{f}=-f(cg+d)^{2}\,;\qquad\hat{g}=\displaystyle\frac{ag+b}{cg+d}\,;\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\,;\;\;ad-bc=-1\,.

Analogously, we can see that the transformation formulas for a non-proper orthochronous motion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
are

(3.7)

\hat{f}=f(cg+d)^{2}\,;\qquad\hat{g}=\displaystyle\frac{ag+b}{cg+d}\,;\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\,;\;\;ad-bc=-1\,,

and the transformation formulas for a non-proper non-orthochronous motion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
are

(3.8)

\hat{f}=-f(cg+d)^{2}\,;\qquad\hat{g}=\displaystyle\frac{ag+b}{cg+d}\,;\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\,;\;\;ad-bc=1\,.

Let
\alpha
be a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
parametrized by an arbitrary parameter.

The following statement holds true.

Proposition 3.4
.

[
16
]

Let
\alpha
be a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
with Weierstrass-type representation (
3.1
). Then,
\alpha
is non-degenerate if and only if
g^{\prime}h^{\prime}\neq 0
at each point.

Now, let
\alpha
be parametrized by a natural parameter, i.e.
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=\pm 1
. Using (
3.1
) we get
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=4f^{2}g^{\prime}h^{\prime}
and hence,

4f^{2}g^{\prime}h^{\prime}=\pm 1
,
or equivalently
4f^{2}|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|=1
.
So, we obtain:

(3.9)

f=\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{2\sqrt{|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|}}\,;\qquad\omega=\pm 1\,,

and the sign of
\omega
coinsides with the sign of
f
.
Using (
3.1
) and (
3.9
), we obtain:

Theorem 3.5
.

[
16
]

Let
\alpha
be a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, parametrized by a natural parameter, i.e.
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=\pm 1
.
Then,
\alpha
has the following Weierstrass-type representation:

(3.10)

\alpha^{\prime}=\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{2\sqrt{|g^{\prime}h^{\prime}|}}\left(gh+1,gh-1,h-g,h+g\right),

where

and

are smooth real functions satisfying
g^{\prime}h^{\prime}\neq 0
, and
\omega=\pm 1
.
The functions

and

as well as
\omega
are determined uniquely by
\alpha
in accordance with (
3.1
).

Conversely, if
(g,h)
is a pair of smooth real functions satisfying
g^{\prime}h^{\prime}\neq 0

\omega=\pm 1
, then there exists a null curve
\alpha
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
parametrized by a natural parameter, such that
\alpha^{\prime}
is expressed by the given functions in the form
(
3.10
).

Remark 3.2
.

If we apply (
3.10
) to the pair of functions

, we obtain two null curves

and
\omega=-1
. These curves are related by a proper orthochronous motion in

Note also that, if
\alpha
is a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
, then

{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=4f^{2}g^{\prime 2}
implies the following statement:

Proposition 3.6
.

If
\alpha
is a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
with Weierstrass-type representation (
3.2
),
then
\alpha

If the parameter in (

) is natural, then

{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=4f^{2}g^{\prime 2}

. Hence, we find

(3.11)

f=\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{2|g^{\prime}|}\,;\qquad\omega=\pm 1,\,

where the sign of
\omega
coincides with the sign of
f
.
Now, from (
3.2
) and (
3.11
) we derive

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\alpha
be a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
, parametrized by a natural parameter, i.e.
{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}^{2}=1
.
Then,
\alpha
has the following Weierstrass-type representation:

(3.12)

\alpha^{\prime}=\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{2|g^{\prime}|}\left(\,g^{2}+1\,,\,g^{2}-1\,,\,2g\,\right),

where
g^{\prime}\neq 0
and
\omega=\pm 1
.
The function
g
and the sign of
\omega
are uniquely determined by
\alpha
.

Conversely, if
g
is a function,
g^{\prime}\neq 0
,
\omega=\pm 1
, then there exists a null curve
\alpha

In the previous section, we considered a relation between the non-degenerate null curves in

\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}

In this section, we will find a relation between the minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}

and the pairs of minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}

We will use the canonical isotropic coordinates for the minimal Lorentz surfaces.
In general, the canonical coordinates exist only in a neighbourhood of a point, so our considerations will be local.
To formalize the correspondence, we will consider the pairs

K=\eta\sqrt[4]{|K_{g}K_{h}|}\>\frac{\sqrt{|K_{g}|}-\sqrt{|K_{h}|}}{2}\;;\quad\varkappa=\eta\sqrt[4]{|K_{g}K_{h}|}\>\frac{\sqrt{|K_{g}|}+\sqrt{|K_{h}|}}{2}\;,\;\;\eta=\mathop{\operatorname{sign}}\nolimits\varkappa\,.

Proof.

For simplicity we suppose that the first fundamental form of
\mathcal{M}
satisfies
F<0
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be of first or second type, i.e.
|K|>|\varkappa|
.
Then, (
4.9
) implies that
g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}
and
h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}
have the same sign,
which coincides with the sign of
K
. Denote this sign by
\eta
.
Then, we have
g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}=\eta|g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}|
,
h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}=\eta|h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}|
.
So, equalities (
4.9
) take the following form:

(6.5)

\begin{array}[]{llr}K&=&\displaystyle\frac{8\eta\sqrt{|g^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}h^{\prime}_{2}|}}{|(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})|}\left(\displaystyle\frac{|g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}|}{(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}}+\displaystyle\frac{|h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}|}{(h_{1}-h_{2})^{2}}\right);\\[12.91663pt]
\varkappa&=&\displaystyle\frac{8\eta\sqrt{|g^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}h^{\prime}_{2}|}}{|(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})|}\left(\displaystyle\frac{|g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}|}{(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}}-\displaystyle\frac{|h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}|}{(h_{1}-h_{2})^{2}}\right).\end{array}

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{g},p_{g})
and
(\mathcal{M}_{h},p_{h})
be the minimal Lorentz surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
that correspond to
(\mathcal{M},p)

according to (
6.1
) . Then, by use of
(
4.12
) we obtain that the Gauss curvatures
K_{g}
and
K_{h}
are expressed as follows:

(6.6)

\sqrt{|K_{g}|}=\displaystyle\frac{4|g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}|}{(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}}\,;\qquad\sqrt{|K_{h}|}=\displaystyle\frac{4|h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}|}{(h_{1}-h_{2})^{2}}\,.

Hence, using (
6.5
), we obtain (
6.3
) .

Now, let
\mathcal{M}
be of third type.
According to Theorem
2.1
, this is equivalent to
|\varkappa|>|K|
.
Then, (
4.9
) implies that
g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}
and
h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}
have different signs.
If
\varkappa>0
, we obtain
g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}>0
and
h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}<0
.
If
\varkappa<0
, we get
g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}<0
and
h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}>0
.
Denote
\eta=\mathop{\operatorname{sign}}\nolimits\varkappa
.
Then
g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}=\eta|g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}|
and
h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}=-\eta|h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}|
.
Now, using (
4.9
), we derive the following expressions of
K
and
\varkappa
:

(6.7)

\begin{array}[]{llr}K&=&\displaystyle\frac{8\eta\sqrt{|g^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}h^{\prime}_{2}|}}{|(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})|}\left(\displaystyle\frac{|g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}|}{(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}}-\displaystyle\frac{|h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}|}{(h_{1}-h_{2})^{2}}\right);\\[12.91663pt]
\varkappa&=&\displaystyle\frac{8\eta\sqrt{|g^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}h^{\prime}_{2}|}}{|(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})|}\left(\displaystyle\frac{|g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2}|}{(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}}+\displaystyle\frac{|h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}|}{(h_{1}-h_{2})^{2}}\right).\end{array}

From (
6.6

6.4
) .

So, we proved formulas (

) and (

) in the case

.
But, according to Theorem

The curvatures

Multi-Mixed Fractional Brownian Motions and Orstein–Uhlenbeck Processes
Hamidreza Maleki Almani, Tommi Sottinen

Abstract
. We study the so-called multi-mixed fractional Brownian motions (mmfBm) and multi-mixed fractional Ornstein–Ulhenbeck (mmfOU) processes. These processes are constructed by mixing by superimposing (infinitely many) independent fractional Brownian motions (fBm) and fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes (fOU), respectively. We prove their existence as L 2 L^{2} processes and study their path properties, viz. long-range and short-range dependence, Hölder continuity, p p -variation, and conditional full support.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

The fractional Brownian motion (fBm)
B^{H}
, with parameter
H\in(0,1)
called the Hurst index, is the unique (up to a multiplicative constant) centered
H
-self-similar stationary-increment Gaussian process. The fBm was first studied in
[
15
]
. The name fractional Brownian motion comes from the influential article
[
18
]
. For further information of the fBm, see the monographs
[
5
,
19
]
. The covariance of the fBm with Hurst index
H
is given by

r_{H}(t,s)=\frac{1}{2}\left[t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right].

For
H=1/2
this process is well-known as the Brownian motion (Bm) or the Wiener process.
As a stationary-increment process, the fBm admits the spectral density

(1.1)

f_{H}(x)=\frac{\sin(\pi H)\Gamma(1+2H)}{2\pi}\,|x|^{1-2H},

where
\Gamma
is the complete gamma function

\Gamma(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t,

see
[
22
]
.

Let

\varrho_{H}(\delta;t)=\mathbb{E}\left[(B^{H}_{\delta}-B^{H}_{0})(B^{H}_{t+\delta}-B^{H}_{t})\right]

be the incremental autocovariance (with lag
\delta
) of the fBm.
For
t\to\infty
we have the power decay

\varrho_{H}(\delta;t)\sim H(2H-1)\delta^{2}t^{2H-2}.

This means that the increments of fBm, called the fractional Gaussian noise (fGn), are positively correlated and long-range dependent of
H>\frac{1}{2}
. For
H<\frac{1}{2}
they are negatively correlated and short-range dependent.

In the Bm case
H=\frac{1}{2}
we have independent increments, i.e., no dependence:

\varrho_{\frac{1}{2}}(\delta;t)=0.

A process
X
is (locally) Hölder continuous with exponent
H
if

\sup_{t,s\in[0,T]}\frac{|X_{t}-X_{s}|}{|t-s|^{H}}<\infty

The Hölder index of a process
X
is

\mathrm{Hol}_{T}(X)=\sup\left\{H>0\,;\,\sup_{t,s\in[0,T]}\frac{|X_{t}-X_{s}|}{|t-s|^{H}}<\infty\right\}

The fBm has almost surely locally Hölder continuous paths with any order
H-\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
, but not with order
H
.
This follows, e.g., from Theorem 1 of
[
1
]
.
Consequenlty,
\mathrm{Hol}(B^{H})=H
.

In addition to Hölder continuity, we have the equidistant
p
-variation as a measure of the path regularity. For a process
X
and
p\in[1,\infty)
for the equidistant partitions

\pi_{n}:=\{t_{k}=\frac{k}{n}T:k=0,1,\ldots,n\},

consider the limit in probability

V^{p}_{T}(X):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}|^{p}.

If this limit is finite, it is called the equidistant
p
-variation on
[0,T]
of
X
. The equidistant
p
-variation index of a process
X
is

\mathrm{var}_{T}(X)=\sup\left\{p\,;\,V_{T}^{p}(X)<\infty\right\}.

For the fBm we have

V^{p}_{T}(B^{H})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\infty&;&pH<1\\
T\mu_{p}&;&pH=1\\
0&;&pH>1\end{array}\right.

where
\mu_{p}
is the
p
th moment of a standard Gaussian random variable, see
[
8
,
9
]
. Consequently,
\mathrm{var}_{T}(B^{H})=1/H
.

While the fBm has been proposed as a model for financial time series, modeling with it makes arbitrage possible, see
[
3
]
. To eliminate this problem, a generalization called mixed fractional Brownian motion (mfBm) was introduced in
[
6
]
. This is the mixture model

M^{a,b}=aB+bB^{H},

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
B
is a standard Brownian motion (Bm) independent of the fBm
B^{H}
. If
H>1/2
, the mfBm has the path roughness governed by the Bm part and the long-range dependence governed by the fBm part. Hence, e.g., in pricing of financial derivatives the corresponding mixed Black–Scholes model yields the same option prices as the standard Brownian model, see
[
4
]
.

A natural generalization of the mfBm is to consider two (or
n
) independent fBm mixtures, see
[
17
]
. In this paper, we study an independent infinite-mixture generalization that we call the multi-mixed fractional Brownian motion (mmfBm) with parameters
\sigma_{k}
,
H_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

M=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}B^{H_{k}},

where
B^{H_{k}}
’s are independent fBm’s with Hurst indices
H_{k}\in(0,1)
, and
\sigma_{k}
’s are positive volatility constants satisfying
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}<\infty
.

For other kinds of generalizations of the fBm, see e.g.
[
12
,
16
,
20
,
21
]
.

The fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (fOU)
U^{\lambda,H}
, with parameters
\lambda>0
and
H\in(0,1)
is the stationary solution of the Langevin equation

\mathrm{d}U^{\lambda,H}_{t}=-\lambda U^{\lambda,H}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t},

which is given by

U^{\lambda,H}_{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{s},

where
(B^{H}_{s})_{s\leq 0}
is an independent copy of the fBm
(B^{H}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
, see
[
7
]
.
Note that the Langevin equation and its solution can be understood via integration-by-parts.
As a stationary process, the fOU admits the spectral density

(1.2)

f_{\lambda,H}(x)=\frac{f_{H}(x)}{x^{2}+\lambda^{2}},

where
f_{H}
is the spectral density of the driving fBm (
1.1
), see
[
2
]
.
Denote, for
\alpha\in(-1,0)\cup(0,1)

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{x}s^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{e}^{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{x}^{\infty}s^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{e}^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

and
{\gamma}_{0}(x)=1
,
{\Gamma}_{0}(x)=0
.
The autocovariance function of the fOU process can be written as

(1.5)

\rho_{\lambda,H}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(1+2H)}{4}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda^{2H}}\bigg\{1+{\gamma}_{2H-1}(\lambda t)+\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda t}{\Gamma}_{2H-1}(\lambda t)\bigg\},

see Proposition
2.4
below. For
H=\frac{1}{2}
we recover the well-known Bm case

\rho_{\lambda,\frac{1}{2}}(t)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}}{2\lambda}.

For
t\to\infty
we have the power decay

\rho_{\lambda,H}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda^{-2n}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{2n-1}(2H-j)\right)t^{2H-2n}+O(t^{2H-2N-2}),

for
N=1,2,\ldots
, i.e., the fOU process with
H>\frac{1}{2}
is long-range dependent, and for
H\leq\frac{1}{2}
it is short-range dependent, see
[
7
]
.

The Hölder index and the
p
-variation
p
-variation index of fOU is the same as for the fBm:
\mathrm{Hol}_{T}(U^{\lambda,H})=\mathrm{Hol}_{T}(B^{H})=H
and

\mathrm{var}_{T}(U^{\lambda,H})=\mathrm{var}_{T}(B^{H})=1/H
. These results follow e.g. from our Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
5.1
.

In this paper we study the multi-mixed fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (mmfOU) with parameters
\lambda>0
and
\sigma_{k}
,
H_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, that is defined naturally as the stationary solution of Langevin equation with mmfBm as the driving noise:

\mathrm{d}U_{t}=-\lambda U_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}M_{t},

with

U_{0}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}\,\mathrm{d}M_{s},

where
(M_{s})_{s\leq 0}
is an independent copy of the mmfBm.

2.
Definitions and Basic Properties

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy

(2.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}<\infty,

and let
H_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy

\displaystyle H_{k}\neq H_{l}\mbox{ for }k\neq l,

(2.2)

\displaystyle H_{\inf}=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}H_{k}>0

\displaystyle H_{\sup}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}H_{k}<1.

The multi-mixed fractional Brownian motion (mmfBm) is

M=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}B^{H_{k}},

where
B^{H_{k}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, are independent fBm’s.

The following proposition shows the existence of the mmfBm.

Proposition 2.1
.

The mmfBm
M
exist as a random function taking values in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
for all
T>0
.

Proof.

Let

M^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}B^{H_{k}}.
Clearly
M^{n}
takes values in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
.
Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>m
. Then

\displaystyle{\|M^{n}-M^{m}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[(M_{t}^{n}-M_{t}^{m})^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\sigma_{k}B^{H_{k}}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[(B_{t}^{H_{k}})^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{k}^{2}t^{2H_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{2}\frac{T^{1+2H_{k}}}{1+2H_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\max\left\{1,T^{3}\right\},

which shows that the sequence
(M^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy. Thus
M^{n}\to M
in

L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
showing the existence.
∎

In the same way we see that the mmfBm
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
exist in the sense that
M^{n}_{t}\to M_{t}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for all
t\geq 0
.

The following is now obvious:

Proposition 2.2
.

The mmfBm has stationary increments, its covariance function is

(2.3)

r(t,s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}r_{H_{k}}(s,t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\left[|t|^{2H_{k}}+|s|^{2H_{k}}-|t-s|^{2H_{k}}\right],

and it admits the spectral density

(2.4)

f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}f_{H_{k}}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(\pi H_{k})\Gamma(1+2H_{k})}{2\pi}\sigma_{k}^{2}|x|^{1-2H_{k}}.

Definition 2.2
.

The multi-mixed fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (mmfOU)
U
with parameter
\lambda>0
, is the stationary solution of the Langevin equation

(2.5)

\mathrm{d}U_{t}=-\lambda U_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}M_{t},

where the equation is understood in the integration-by-parts sense.

Proposition 2.3
.

On
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
, the mmfOU can be represented as the integral

U_{t}=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}M_{s},

where the integral is understood in the integration-by-parts sense, and

\xi=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}\,\mathrm{d}M_{s},

where
(M_{s})_{s\leq 0}
is an independent copy of the mmfBm
(M_{s})_{s\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Let
M^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}B^{H_{k}}
. Then, the stationary solution of the Langevin equation

\mathrm{d}U^{n}_{t}=-\lambda U^{n}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}M^{n}_{t},

is given by

U^{n}_{t}=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\xi_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}M^{n}_{s},

where

\xi_{n}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}\,\mathrm{d}M^{n}_{s}.

Then, with integration-by-parts

\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}\,\mathrm{d}M^{n}_{s}=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}M^{n}_{t}-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}M^{n}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\ \to\ \mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}M_{t}-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}M_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}\,\mathrm{d}M_{s},

because
M^{n}\to M
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
. With the same arguments
\xi_{n}\to\xi
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. This yields
U^{n}\to U
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
.
∎

The following technical lemma is used to calculate spectral densities.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
0\neq p\in(-1,1),\,\lambda>0,\,t>0

(2.6)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tx}\frac{|x|^{p}}{\lambda^{2}+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\pi\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}}{2\cos(\frac{p\pi}{2})\lambda^{1-p}}\left\{1+\gamma_{-p}(\lambda t)+\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda t}\Gamma_{-p}(\lambda t)\right\},

where
\gamma_{-p}
and
\Gamma_{-p}
are given by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

Proof.

Recall that for the Fourier transform

\mathscr{F}(f)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tx}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

we have the convolution theorem

(2.7)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tx}\mathscr{F}(f)(x)\mathscr{F}(g)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t-\xi)g(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Moreover, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\left(e^{-\lambda|t|}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+x^{2}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\left(|t|^{\alpha}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\cdot\Gamma(\alpha+1)\cos\left(\frac{(\alpha+1)\pi}{2}\right)|x|^{-(\alpha+1)}.

The first formula (
2.8
) is valid for
\lambda>0
. The second formula (
2.9
) is valid for
-1<\alpha<0
. For
-2<\alpha<-1
, because of the function
|t|^{\alpha}
, some singular terms arise at the origin. Nevertheless, it admits a unique meromorphic extension as a tempered distribution, also denoted
|t|^{\alpha}
as a homogeneous distribution on all real line
\mathbb{R}
including the origin (see
[
11
]
). So, we use that extension and formula (
2.9
) will be valid for all
-1\neq\alpha\in(-2,0)
. So, using
f(t)=e^{-\lambda|t|}
and
g(t)=|t|^{\alpha}
in (
2.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\Gamma(\alpha+1)\cos\left(\frac{(\alpha+1)\pi}{2}\right)\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{itx}\frac{|x|^{-(\alpha+1)}}{\lambda^{2}+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda|t-\xi|}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}(-\xi)^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\xi^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{t}^{\infty}\xi^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(\xi-t)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda^{(\alpha+1)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-u}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda^{(\alpha+1)}}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}u^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{u}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}}{\lambda^{(\alpha+1)}}\int_{\lambda t}^{\infty}u^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-u}\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\lambda^{(\alpha+1)}}\left\{1+\gamma_{(\alpha+1)}(\lambda t)+\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda t}\Gamma_{(\alpha+1)}(\lambda t)\right\}.

Now, choosing
p=-(\alpha+1)
proves (
2.6
).
∎

The covariance function of the fOU is

Proof.

Let
U^{n}
be like in the proof of Proposition

f_{\lambda,n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{2}\frac{\sin(\pi H_{k})\Gamma(1+2H_{k})}{2\pi}\frac{|x|^{1-2H_{k}}}{x^{2}+\lambda^{2}},

and

Remark 2.1
.

Proposition
2.4
represents the covariance function
\rho_{\lambda,H}(t)
in a form involving special functions. However, these special complex functions are usually not suitable for numerical computations. For example,in
[
2
]
, Lemma B.1, the following representation was used for
H>\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda,H}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\Gamma(2H)\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda^{2H}}\left\{\frac{1+e^{2\lambda t}}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(2H-1)}I_{\lambda,H}(t)\right\},

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,H}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\lambda v}e^{2\lambda v}e^{-s}s^{2H-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}v.

the double integral above seems reasonable enough, but yields slow numerical calculation in practice. This can be remedied by calculating the inner integral as follows:

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,H}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\int_{s/{\lambda}}^{t}e^{2\lambda v}e^{-s}s^{2H-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}s^{2H-2}(e^{2\lambda t-s}-e^{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\lambda t}}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}s^{2H-2}\sinh(\lambda t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Consequently,

\rho_{\lambda,H}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)}{2\lambda^{2H}}\bigg\{\cosh(\lambda t)-\frac{1}{\Gamma(2H-1)}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}s^{2H-2}\sinh(\lambda t-s)\,ds\bigg\},

For the case
H<1/2
we use the following developed version of Lemma 5.1 in
[
13
]
for
\alpha>-1
. The proof is similar.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\alpha>-1

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-(x+y)}|x-y|^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=\Gamma(\alpha+1).

Theorem 2.1
.

For the fOU process
U^{\lambda,H}
. We have

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda,H}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)}{2\lambda^{2H}}\,\bigg\{\cosh(\lambda t)-\frac{1}{\Gamma(2H)}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}s^{2H-1}\cosh(\lambda t-s)\,ds\bigg\},

Proof.

For
H=1/2

wich is equal to the outocovariance of the classical Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with respect to the standard Brownian motion. For

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda,H}(t)=

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}[U^{\lambda,H}_{t}U^{\lambda,H}_{0}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{u}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-v)}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{v}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\bigg\{\mathbb{V}ar(U^{\lambda,H}_{0})+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{u}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{v}\bigg]\bigg\}.

To obtain the term

Since

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}ar(U^{\lambda,H}_{0})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}ar\left[-\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}B^{H}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lambda^{2}\mathbb{V}ar\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}B^{H}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}B^{H}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(u+v)}B^{H}_{u}B^{H}_{v}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(u+v)}\cdot\Big\{u^{2H}+v^{2H}-|u-v|^{2H}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\bigg\{2\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}u\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda v}v^{2H}\,\mathrm{d}v\right)-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(u+v)}|u-v|^{2H}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\bigg\},

Now choosing
x=\lambda u,\,y=\lambda v
and Lemma
2.2
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}ar(U^{\lambda,H}_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{-2H}}{2}\bigg\{2\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-y}y^{2H}\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-(x+y)}|x-y|^{2H}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{-2H}}{2}\Big[2\Gamma(2H+1)-\Gamma(2H+1)\Big]

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-2H}H\Gamma(2H).

On the other hand, as in Lemma 2.1 in
[
7
]
and the proof of Lemma B.1 in
[
2
]
, using formula

\gamma_{\ell}(z,x)=\frac{\gamma_{\ell}(z+1,x)+x^{z}\mathrm{e}^{-x}}{z},

where
\gamma_{\ell}
is the well-known lower Gamma function, for
H<1/2
we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{u}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{v}\right]

\displaystyle=H(2H-1)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(u+v)}|u-v|^{2H-2}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}ar(U^{\lambda,H}_{0})\bigg\{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda t}-1}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(2H-1)}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda v}\int_{0}^{\lambda v}\mathrm{e}^{-s}s^{2H-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}v\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}ar(U^{\lambda,H}_{0})\bigg\{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda t}-1}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(2H-1)}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda v}\gamma_{\ell}(2H-1,\lambda v)\,\mathrm{d}v\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}ar(U^{\lambda,H}_{0})\bigg\{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda t}-1}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(2H)}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda v}\gamma_{\ell}(2H,\lambda v)\,\mathrm{d}v-\frac{\lambda^{2H}}{\Gamma(2H)}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}v^{2H-1}\,\mathrm{d}v\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}ar(U^{\lambda,H}_{0})\bigg\{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda t}-1}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\Gamma(2H)}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda v}\int_{0}^{\lambda v}\mathrm{e}^{-s}s^{2H-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}v-\frac{\lambda^{2H}}{\Gamma(2H)}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}v^{2H-1}\,\mathrm{d}v\bigg\}.

Using (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), with similar arguments as we did for (
2.1
) we obtain (
2.1
).
∎

3.
Long-Range Dependence

The increments of fBm, the fGn, is a well-known stationary process, that is long-range dependent (LRD) if
H>1/2
, and short-range dependent (SRD) in
H<\frac{1}{2}

Motivated with this, we consider the LRD of the increments the of mmfBm.

For a lag
\delta
and a process
X
we denote

\Delta_{\delta}X_{t}=X_{t+\delta}-X_{t}.

Then

\Delta_{\delta}M_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}\Delta_{\delta}B^{H_{k}}_{t}

is stationary and its autocovariance function is denoted by

\varrho(\delta;t)=\mathbb{E}\big[\Delta_{\delta}M_{s+t}\Delta_{\delta}M_{s}\big],

Theorem 3.1
.

For
t\to\infty

(3.1)

\varrho(\delta;t)\sim\delta^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)t^{2H_{k}-2}=O(t^{2H_{\sup}-2}).

So the mmfBm increment process
\Delta_{\delta}M_{t}
is LRD if and only if
H_{k}>1/2
for some
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

By using the generalized binomial theorem

(3.2)

\displaystyle\varrho(\delta;t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\Big\{(t+\delta)^{2H_{k}}+(t-\delta)^{2H_{k}}-2t^{2H_{k}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}t^{2H_{k}}\Bigg\{\Big(1+\frac{\delta}{t}\Big)^{2H_{k}}+\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{t}\Big)^{2H_{k}}-2\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}t^{2H_{k}}\Bigg\{\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\binom{2H_{k}}{r}\Big(\frac{\delta}{t}\Big)^{r}+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\binom{2H_{k}}{r}(-1)^{r}\Big(\frac{\delta}{t}\Big)^{r}-2\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)t^{2H_{k}-2}.

Since

\sigma_{k}^{2}H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)t^{2H_{k}-2}\leq\sigma_{k}^{2},

the series (
3.2
) is uniformly convergent. So we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)t^{2H_{k}-2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)t^{2H_{k}-2}.

This yields (
3.1
).
∎

To investigate LRD for the mmfOU process, we first need some lemmas.

The following theorem shows that similar to the mmfBm increment process, the long-range dependence of the mmfOU is governed by the long-range dependence of the largest Hurst index in the driving mmfBm.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
t\to\infty
and each
N=1,2,\ldots

(3.3)

\rho_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma^{2}_{k}\lambda^{-2n}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{2n-1}(2H_{k}-j)\right)t^{2H_{k}-2n}+O(t^{2H_{\sup}-2N-2}).

So the mmfOU process
U
is LRD if and only if
H_{k}>1/2
for some
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

By the proof of Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.3 in
[
7
]

(3.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}M_{u}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(t-v)}\,\mathrm{d}M_{v}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\,\mathrm{d}M_{u}\int_{-\infty}^{1/\lambda}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}\,\mathrm{d}M_{v}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}H_{i}(2H_{i}-1)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\left(\int_{1/\lambda}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}(v-u)^{2H_{i}-2}\,\mathrm{d}v\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}\frac{H_{i}(2H_{i}-1)}{\lambda^{2H_{i}}}\Bigg\{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\int_{1}^{\lambda t}\mathrm{e}^{y}y^{2H_{i}-2}\,\mathrm{d}y+\mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}\int_{\lambda t}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-y}y^{2H_{i}-2}\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma^{2}_{k}\lambda^{-2n}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{2n-1}(2H_{k}-j)\right)t^{2H_{k}-2n}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\frac{\Big|H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)\cdots(2H_{k}-2-2N)\Big|}{\lambda^{2H_{k}}}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}+(1+2^{2H_{k}-2N-3})(\lambda t)^{2H_{k}-2N-3}\right].

Now, for
t\in[1,\infty)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\Big|H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)\cdots(2H_{k}-2-2N)\Big|}{\lambda^{2H_{k}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}<\Lambda_{N}\sigma_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\Big|H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)\cdots(2H_{k}-2-2N)\Big|}{\lambda^{2H_{k}}}\,(1+2^{2H_{k}-2N-3})(\lambda t)^{2H_{k}-2N-3}<\Pi_{N}\sigma_{k}^{2},

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\sup}\frac{\max\Big(|2H_{\inf}-1|,|2H_{\sup}-1|\Big)}{\max\Big(\lambda^{2H_{\inf}},\lambda^{2H_{\sup}}\Big)}\Big|(2H_{\inf}-2)\cdots(2H_{\inf}-2-2N)\Big|,

\displaystyle\Pi_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\sup}\frac{\max\Big(|2H_{\inf}-1|,|2H_{\sup}-1|\Big)}{\lambda^{2N+3}}\Big|(2H_{\inf}-2)\cdots(2H_{\inf}-2-2N)\Big|(1+2^{2H_{\sup}-2N-3}).

So, as
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}<\infty
, the series in the right-hand side of the inequality (
3.4
) is uniformly convergent on
t\in[1,\infty)
. Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\frac{\Big|H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)\cdots(2H_{k}-2-2N)\Big|}{\lambda^{2H_{k}}}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}+(1+2^{2H_{k}-2N-3})(\lambda t)^{2H_{k}-2N-3}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\frac{\Big|H_{k}(2H_{k}-1)\cdots(2H_{k}-2-2N)\Big|}{\lambda^{2H_{k}}}\lim_{t\to\infty}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}+(1+2^{2H_{k}-2N-3})(\lambda t)^{2H_{k}-2N-3}\right].

This proves (
3.3
).
∎

4.
Continuity

Theorem 4.1
.

Both mmfBm and mmfOU have Hölder index
H_{\inf}
.

Proof.

For
\epsilon>0
and
|t-s|<1
, the mmfBm satisfies

\mathbb{E}\Big[(M_{t}-M_{s})^{2}\Big]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}|t-s|^{2H_{k}}\leq\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\bigg)|t-s|^{2H_{\inf}-\epsilon}=C_{0}|t-s|^{2H_{\inf}-\epsilon},

where
C_{0}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}>0
. Thus, Hölder continuity with exponent
H_{\inf}-\varepsilon
follows from Theorem 1 of
[
1
]
. On the other hand, for some
j\geq 1
we have
H_{\inf}\leq H_{j}<H_{\inf}+\epsilon
and so the fBm
B^{H_{j}}
is not
(H_{\inf}+\epsilon)
-Hölder continuous. Hence the process
M=\sigma_{j}B^{H_{j}}+\sum_{k\neq j}\sigma_{k}B^{H_{k}}
is not
(H_{\inf}+\epsilon)
-Hölder continuous. This proves the claim for mmfBm.

For the mmfOU, we apply the Corollary 2 of
[
1
]
. That states that the stationary process
U
is Hölder-continuous with any exponent
0<a<H_{\inf}
if and only if for each
0<\epsilon<2H_{\inf}
, there is some
0<\delta<1
that

(4.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(sx))f_{\lambda}(x)dx<C_{\epsilon}s^{2H_{\inf}-\epsilon},\quad s\in(0,\delta).

This is equivalent to have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\cos(sx))}{s^{2H_{\inf}-\epsilon}}f_{\lambda}(x)dx<C_{\epsilon},\quad s\in(0,\delta).

To show this, here for
s<1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\cos(sx))}{s^{2H_{k}-\epsilon}}f_{\lambda,H_{k}}(x)dx

\displaystyle=s^{\epsilon}c_{H_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(sx))\frac{x\cdot(sx)^{-2H_{k}}}{\lambda^{2}+x^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=s^{\epsilon}c_{H_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos u)\frac{u^{1-2H_{k}}}{s^{2}\lambda^{2}+u^{2}}du\quad(u=sx)

\displaystyle\leq c_{H_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos u)\frac{u^{1-2H_{k}}}{s^{2}\lambda^{2}+u^{2}}du\quad(0<s<1)

\displaystyle\leq c_{H_{k}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}(1-\cos u)\frac{u^{1-2H_{k}}}{u^{2}}du+\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{u^{1-2H_{k}}}{u^{2}}du\Big\}

\displaystyle=c_{H_{k}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{2\sin^{2}(\frac{u}{2})}{u^{2}}u^{1-2H_{k}}du+\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}u^{-1-2H_{k}}du\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq c_{H_{k}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{1}{2}u^{1-2H_{k}}du+\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}u^{-1-2H_{k}}du\Big\}

\displaystyle=c_{H_{k}}\Big\{\frac{\epsilon^{2-2H_{k}}}{4(1-H_{k})}+\frac{\epsilon^{-2H_{k}}}{2H_{k}}\Big\}=:C_{\epsilon,H_{k}}<\infty.

Therefore,

(4.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(sx))f_{\lambda,H_{k}}(x)dx\leq C_{\epsilon,H_{k}}s^{2H_{k}-\epsilon}\leq C_{\epsilon,H_{k}}s^{2H_{\inf}-\epsilon}.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}C_{\epsilon,H_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\frac{\sin(\pi H_{k})\Gamma(1+2H_{k})}{2\pi}\Big\{\frac{\epsilon^{2-2H_{k}}}{4(1-H_{k})}+\frac{\epsilon^{-2H_{k}}}{2H_{k}}\Big\}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(3)}{2\pi}\Big\{\frac{\epsilon^{2-2H_{sup}}}{4(1-H_{sup})}+\frac{\epsilon^{-2H_{inf}}}{2H_{inf}}\Big\}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\Big)=:C_{\epsilon}<\infty,

if and only if
0<H_{\inf}\leq H_{\sup}<1
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}<\infty
. Now, (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) yield (
4.1
). Moreover, for some
j\geq 1
we have
H_{\inf}\leq H_{j}<H_{\inf}+\epsilon
and so the fOU
U^{H_{j}}
is not
(H_{\inf}+\epsilon)
-Hölder continuous. Hence the process
U=\sigma_{j}U^{H_{j}}+\sum_{k\neq j}\sigma_{k}U^{H_{k}}
is not
(H_{\inf}+\epsilon)
-Hölder continuous. This proves the claim for mmfOU.
∎

p

.

p>0
, the equidistant

M

Proof.

For the mmfBm
M
, We have

\displaystyle v^{p}_{\pi_{n}}(M)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{k}\in\pi_{n}}|\Delta M_{t_{k}}|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{k}\in\pi_{n}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}(\Delta t_{k})^{2H_{i}}\right|^{p/2}\left|\frac{\Delta M_{t_{k}}}{\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}(\Delta t_{k})^{2H_{i}}\Big]^{1/2}}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\overset{d}{=}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}T^{2H_{i}}n^{2/p-2H_{i}}\right)^{p/2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|Z_{k}|^{p}.

as
|\pi_{n}|\to 0
or equivalently
n\to\infty
. Here
(Z_{k})
is a stationary Gaussian process and so by the proof of Lemma 3.7 in
[
23
]

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|Z_{k}|^{p}\to\mu_{p},

as

, where

is the

, and so there exists some
j\geq 1
that
H_{j}<1/p
, and so

. Therefore

v^{p}_{\pi_{n}}(M)\geq\left(\sigma_{j}^{2}T^{2H_{j}}n^{2/p-2H_{j}}\right)^{p/2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|Z_{k}|^{p}\to\infty.

On the other hand, if

for

\sigma_{i}^{2}T^{2H_{i}}x^{2/p-2H_{i}}\leq\sigma_{i}^{2}T^{2},

and because

, the

. So for

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}T^{2H_{i}}n^{2/p-2H_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}T^{2H_{i}}n^{2/p-2H_{i}}.

This yields the values mentioned in (
5.1
) are correct for the
p
-variation of
M
. For the mmfOU
U
, as it is stationary we have

v^{p}_{\pi_{n}}(U):=\sum_{t_{k}\in\pi_{n}}|\Delta U_{t_{k}}|^{p}\overset{d}{=}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\mathbb{V}ar[\Delta U_{t_{1}}]\Big)^{p/2}|Z_{k}|^{p}=n\Big(\mathbb{V}ar[U_{\frac{T}{n}}-U_{0}]\Big)^{p/2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|Z_{k}|^{p}.

As
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|Z_{k}|^{p}\to\mu_{p}
for

, problem vanish to find

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\Big(\mathbb{V}ar[U_{\frac{T}{n}}-U_{0}]\Big)^{p/2}.

To find it, again because
U
is stationary, and using the proof of Theorem
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}ar[U_{\frac{T}{n}}-U_{0}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}arU_{\frac{T}{n}}+\mathbb{V}arU_{0}-2\,\mathrm{C}ov\Big(U_{\frac{T}{n}},U_{0}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\mathbb{V}arU_{0}-2\,\mathrm{C}ov\Big(U_{\frac{T}{n}},U_{0}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}\lambda^{-2H_{i}}H_{i}\Gamma(2H_{i})-2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}\frac{\Gamma(2H_{i}+1)}{2\lambda^{2H_{i}}}\bigg\{\cosh\Big(\frac{\lambda T}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Gamma(2H_{i})}\int_{0}^{\frac{\lambda T}{n}}s^{2H_{i}-1}\cosh\Big(\frac{\lambda T}{n}-s\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}\frac{\Gamma(2H_{i}+1)}{\lambda^{2H_{i}}}\bigg\{1-\cosh\Big(\frac{\lambda T}{n}\Big)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(2H_{i})}\int_{0}^{\frac{\lambda T}{n}}s^{2H_{i}-1}\cosh\Big(\frac{\lambda T}{n}-s\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}.

For the large values of
n
the final series in the right hand side above, is uniformly convergent. So, the
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}
could change places. This yields

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n\Big(\mathbb{V}ar[U_{\frac{T}{n}}-U_{0}]\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(n^{2/p}\,\mathbb{V}ar[U_{\frac{T}{n}}-U_{0}]\Big)^{p/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{i}^{2}\frac{\Gamma(2H_{i}+1)}{\lambda^{2H_{i}}}\cdot\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2/p}\bigg\{1-\cosh\Big(\frac{\lambda T}{n}\Big)+\,\frac{1}{\Gamma(2H_{i})}\int_{0}^{\frac{\lambda T}{n}}s^{2H_{i}-1}\cosh\Big(\frac{\lambda T}{n}-s\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}\Bigg)^{p/2}.

Now for
t\to 0
, by the Taylor expansion

1-\cosh t=-\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2r}}{(2r)!},

and via integration by parts

\int_{0}^{t}s^{2H_{i}-1}\cosh(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{t^{2H_{i}}}{2H_{i}}+\frac{1}{2H_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{2H_{i}}\sinh(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Again for
t\to 0

\int_{0}^{t}s^{2H_{i}}\sinh(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{t}t^{2H_{i}}\sinh t\,\mathrm{d}s=t^{2H_{i}+1}\sinh t=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2r+2H_{i}}}{(2r-1)!}.

These yield for

this proves (
5.1
).
∎

6.
Conditional Full Support

As explained in
[
4
]
, in mathematical finance models one of the must require the so-called Conditional Full Support (CFS) to avoid simple kind of arbitrage. This means that, given the information up to any stopping time
\tau\in[0,T]
, the process is inherently free enough to go anywhere after the stopping time
\tau
with positive probability. This motivates us to study the CFS property of the mmfBm and mmfOU processes but first we restate the precise definition of CFS from
[
10
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
be a continuous stochastic process defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
, and
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
be its natural filtration. The process

X
is said to have CFS if, for all
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-stopping times
\tau
, the conditional law of
(X_{u})_{\tau\leq u\leq T}
given
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}
, almost surely has support
C_{X_{\tau}}[\tau,T]
, where
C_{x}[\tau,T]
is the space of continuous functions
f
on
[\tau,T]
satisfying
f(t)=x
. Equivalently, this means that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
f\in C_{0}[\tau,T]
, and
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\tau\leq u\leq T}|X_{u}-X_{\tau}-f(u)|<\varepsilon\bigg|\mathscr{F}_{\tau}\right)>0,

almost surely.

Theorem 6.1
.

Both the mmfBm and the mmfOU have conditional full support.

Proof.

It is easy to check that

f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}f_{H_{k}}(x)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\varepsilon_{H}\Gamma(1)}{2\pi}\biggl(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\biggl)|x|^{1-2H_{\inf}}&:&|x|\leq 1\\
\\
\frac{\varepsilon_{H}\Gamma(1)}{2\pi}\biggl(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\biggl)|x|^{1-2H_{\sup}}&:&|x|\geq 1\end{array}\right.=:h(x),

where

\varepsilon_{H}:=\inf\Big\{\sin(\pi H_{k})\Big\}_{k\geq 1}=\inf\Big\{\sin(\pi H_{\inf}),\sin(\pi H_{\sup})\Big\}

Since
0<H_{\inf}\leq H_{\sup}<1
,
\varepsilon_{H}>0
. Thus
h(x)>0
for
x\neq 0
. Thus, for any

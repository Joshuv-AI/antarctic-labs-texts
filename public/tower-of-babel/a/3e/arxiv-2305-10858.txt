H p H^{p} spaces of separately ( α , β ) − (\alpha,\beta)- harmonic functions in the unit polydisc
Jelena Gajić, Miodrag Mateljević, Miloš Arsenović

Abstract
. We prove existence and uniqueness of a solution of the Dirichlet problem for separately ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) - harmonic functions on the unit polydisc 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} with boundary data in C ⁡ ( 𝕋 n ) C(\mathbb{T}^{n}) using ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) - Poisson kernel P α , β ​ ( z , ζ ) P_{\alpha,\beta}(z,\zeta) . A characterization by hypergeometric functions of separately ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) - harmonic functions which are also m m - homogeneous is given, this characterization is used to obtain series expansion of separately ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) - harmonic functions. Basic H p H^{p} theory of such functions is developed: integral representations by measures and L p L^{p} functions on 𝕋 n \mathbb{T}^{n} , norm and weak ∗ convergence at the distinguished boundary 𝕋 n \mathbb{T}^{n} . Weak ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) - type estimate for a restricted non-tangential maximal function M A , B N ​ T M_{A,B}^{NT} is derived. Slice functions u ⁡ ( z 1 , … , z k , ζ k + 1 , … , ζ n ) u(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}) , where some of the variables are fixed, are shown to belong in the appropriate space of separately ( α ′ , β ′ ) (\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}) - harmonic functions of k k variables. We prove a Fatou type theorem on a. e. existence of restricted non-tangential limits for these functions and a corresponding result for unrestricted limit at a point in 𝕋 n \mathbb{T}^{n} . Our results extend earlier results for ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) - harmonic functions in the disc and for n n - harmonic functions in 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} .

Introduction and notation

The first line of research that motivates our investigations is concerned with
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic functions, i.e. functions

u

where

. Here
\Delta
is the Laplace - Beltrami operator on a domain
\Omega

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are linear differential operators of first order and
Q_{\alpha,\beta}
is a zero order operator. An early paper touching on this topic is
[
5
]
where
\Omega
is the Siegel upper half plane
{\bf U^{n+1}}
.
There
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic functions were used as a tool for obtaining results on
H^{p}
theory on the Heisenberg group

\partial{\bf U^{n+1}}={\bf H^{n}}
.
The case where
\Omega
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
was considered in
[
1
]
where series expansion of

(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic functions was derived and
H^{p}
theory results for such functions were obtained. A detailed
investigation of series expansion of
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic functions in the unit disc can be found in
[
9
]
. Let us
mention that special one parameter cases also appeared, see for example
[
12
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
.
In the cited papers the operators

are uniformly elliptic, but degenerate near the boundary of the domain: the lower order terms
\alpha L_{1},\beta L_{2}
and
Q_{\alpha,\beta}
are not dominated by the principal part unless

\alpha=\beta=0
.

The second line of research relevant for us is the study of
n
- harmonic functions, that is functions defined on the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
which are harmonic in each of the

variables

- harmonic functions can be found in Chapter XVII of

- harmonic functions is given in

, more recent paper on

- harmonic functions is

.

In this paper we merge these two avenues of investigation. We deal with functions

in the unit polydisc which satisfy a system of PDEs:

,

. Each of the operators

acts on the

[

]
,
[

]
and
[

]

to

- harmonic functions.

If

\mathbb{D}^{n}
. We set
{\bf 1}=(1,\ldots,1)
.

For

we set
\overline{z}=(\overline{z_{1}},\ldots,\overline{z_{n}})
. For

z\cdot w=(z_{1}w_{1},\ldots,z_{n}w_{n})
. We also write
z=(z^{\prime},z_{n})
where

z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
is in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and

\mathbb{C}^{n-1}

We will use the normalized Haar measure

on
\mathbb{T}^{n}

. The

. The pairing of duality is given by

\langle\varphi,d\mu\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\varphi d\mu,\qquad\varphi\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

The total variation of
\mu

\|\mu\|
, it is equal to
|\mu|(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

For
z
in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and a function

we define

by

.
If

instead of

. In particular, if
{\bf r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in[0,1)^{n}

. Also, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
we define
a function
\zeta{\bf\cdot}u
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
by
\zeta{\bf\cdot}u(z)=u(\zeta\cdot z)
.

We use notation

and
x^{-}=\max(-x,0)
for a real number
x
. For
m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
in

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we set
m^{+}=(m_{1}^{+},\ldots,m_{n}^{+})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
and similarly

m^{-}=(m_{1}^{-},\ldots,m_{n}^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
.
|m|=|m_{1}|+\cdots+|m_{n}|

is the order of a multiindex
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Also,
m!=m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
.
For
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
and

we set

z^{p}=z_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot z_{n}^{p_{n}}
,
\overline{z}^{p}=\overline{z}_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\overline{z}_{n}^{p_{n}}
.

The order of a multiindex
(p,q)
of length

is
|(p,q)|=|p|+|q|
. We set

\partial^{(p,q)}=\frac{\partial^{p_{1}}}{\partial z_{1}^{p_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{p_{n}}}{\partial z_{n}^{p_{n}}}\frac{\partial^{q_{1}}}{\partial\overline{z}_{1}^{q_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{q_{n}}}{\partial\overline{z}_{n}^{q_{n}}},\quad\partial^{p}=\partial^{(p,0)},\quad{\overline{\partial}}^{q}=\partial^{(0,q)}.

Here

The space

is a locally convex complete metrizable topological vector space, it is endowed with the
following family of seminorms:

\|u\|_{p,q,K}=\max_{z\in K}|\partial^{(p,q)}u(z)|,\qquad K\subset\mathbb{D}^{n}\quad\mbox{is compact},\quad p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}.

For complex numbers

and

such that

hypergeometric function

F(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!},\qquad|z|<1

where

is the Pochhammer symbol:

(a)_{0}=1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1)\quad\mbox{\rm for}\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

If
\Re(a+b-c)<0
then the series in (
1.1
) converges absolutely for
|z|\leq 1
.
More details about the hypergeometric functions can be found in
[
2
]
.

2.
Separately
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic functions in
\mathbb{D}^{n}

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
A function
u
in
C^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is separately
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic if
L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}u=0
for

1\leq j\leq n
, where

L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}=(1-|z_{j}|^{2})\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{j}}}+\alpha_{j}z_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+\beta_{j}\overline{z_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z_{j}}}-\alpha_{j}\beta_{j},\qquad 1\leq j\leq n.

We denote the vector space of all separately
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic functions in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
by

sh_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Note that
L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}
is a second order linear partial differential operator which acts only with respect to

z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
variable, hence the term ”separately”. The first order coefficients
\alpha_{j}z_{j}
and
\beta_{j}\overline{z_{j}}

are not dominated by
1-|z_{j}|^{2}
, unless
\alpha_{j}=\beta_{j}=0
, thus although the operator is uniformly elliptic it
is degenerate. If
\alpha_{j}=\beta_{j}=0
, then
L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}=4^{-1}(1-|z_{j}|^{2})\Delta_{j}
, where

\Delta_{j}
is the Laplace operator with respect to
x_{j}
and
y_{j}
variables. Thus
sh_{0,0}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the space of

n
- harmonic functions. Even in this special case we do not get an algebra: a square of a harmonic function is not a harmonic
function in general.

Lemma 2.2
.

sh_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Proof.

Any separately
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic function
u
is a solution of a linear elliptic
partial differential equation

Lu=0
, where
L=L_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}+\cdots+L_{\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}}
has
C^{\infty}
coefficients. Therefore, by elliptic regularity,
u
is in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(
[
9
]
, Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 6.4.)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. Then the function

(2.1)

u_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+\beta+1}}{(1-z)^{\alpha+1}(1-\overline{z})^{\beta+1}},\qquad|z|<1

is
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
\Re\alpha+\Re\beta>-1

(2.2)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|u_{\alpha,\beta}(re^{i\theta})|d\theta\leq e^{\frac{\pi}{2}|\Im\alpha-\Im\beta|}\frac{\Gamma(\Re\alpha+\Re\beta+1)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{\Re\alpha+\Re\beta}{2}+1\right)},\qquad 0\leq r<1.

We also define, for complex
\alpha,\beta\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}=\{-1,-2,\ldots\}
and
\Re\alpha+\Re\beta>-1
:

(2.3)

c_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+1)},

(2.4)

v_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=c_{\alpha,\beta}u_{\alpha,\beta}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Since, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}

\partial_{z}[u(\zeta z)]=\zeta(\partial_{z}u)(\zeta z)
and
\partial_{\overline{z}}[u(\zeta z)]=\overline{\zeta}(\partial_{\overline{z}}u)(\zeta z)

L_{\alpha,\beta}[u(\zeta z)]=(1-|z|^{2})|\zeta|^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}(\zeta z)+\alpha\zeta z(\partial_{z}u)(\zeta z)+\beta\overline{\zeta z}(\partial_{\overline{z}}u)(\zeta z)-\alpha\beta u(\zeta z).

A consequence of this formula is the following rotational invariance of
L_{\alpha,\beta}
, which appeared in
[
9
]

L_{\alpha,\beta}(\zeta\cdot u)=\zeta\cdot(L_{\alpha,\beta}u)
if
|\zeta|=1
. It follows that

(2.5)

L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}(\zeta\cdot u)=\zeta\cdot(L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}u),\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

Hence, for
|\zeta|=1
, the function
f(z)=u_{\alpha,\beta}(\zeta z)
is
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic in

\mathbb{D}
. On the other hand, taking
\zeta=r
we obtain

(2.6)

L_{\alpha,\beta}[u(rz)]=(L_{\alpha,\beta}u)(rz)-(1-r^{2})\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}(rz),\qquad 0<r<1,

which shows that for
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic function
u
the function

(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic.

From this point on we assume that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfy conditions

(2.7)

\Re\alpha_{j}+\Re\beta_{j}>-1\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}\qquad\mbox{\rm for all}\qquad 1\leq j\leq n.

.

We define

If

We fix a compact

For a given

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{(\gamma,\delta)}u_{m}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cdots\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\partial_{z}^{(\gamma,\delta)}u(e^{i\theta_{1}}z_{1},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{n}}z_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-im_{j}\theta_{j}}\frac{d\theta_{1}\ldots d\theta_{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}

Definition 3.1

Proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
u\in Csh_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
1\leq k<n
and
\zeta_{j}\in\mathbb{T}
,
k<j\leq n
.
Set
\alpha^{\prime}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
,

\beta^{\prime}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k})
. Then the function

u_{\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})=u(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})

belongs to the space
Csh_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
.

Proof.

Choose a sequence of points
w_{l}=(w_{k+1}^{l},\ldots,w_{n}^{l})
in
\mathbb{D}^{n-k}
which converges to

\zeta=(\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})
and set
u_{l}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})=u(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},w_{k+1}^{l},\ldots,w_{n}^{l})
. Then

uniformly converges to

on
\overline{\mathbb{D}^{k}}
. Clearly

u_{\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}}
is continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}^{k}}
. Since
L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}u_{l}=0

\mathbb{D}^{k}
, the mentioned uniform convergence gives us that
L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}u_{\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}}=0

in the weak sense,

. Then we have
Lu_{\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}}=0
in the weak sense as well, where

. Since

is elliptic, elliptic regularity gives us

u_{\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
and therefore

L_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}u_{\zeta_{k+1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}}=0
in the classical sense for

. This implies

.
∎

Of course, any other

could have been fixed, with analogous conclusion.

Theorem 3.4
.

If

, then

u(z)=P_{\alpha,\beta}[u|_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}](z)

Proof.

Let

.
Clearly

is continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}

(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
- harmonic in

.
Then, by Theorem

Since,

((\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}),(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1}))

\displaystyle u(z)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}P_{\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}}(z_{n},\zeta_{n})

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}P_{\alpha_{n-1},\beta_{n-1}}(z_{n-1},\zeta_{n-1})u(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_{n})dm_{1}(\zeta_{n-1})\right)dm_{1}(\zeta_{n}).

Clearly we can iterate this process, using Lemma

Theorem 3.5
.

If

,

The set

of continuous bounded functions on

, with supremum norm. Let

. By

maps

to
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is equal to

. Moreover,

\lim_{{\bf r\to 1}}P_{\alpha,\beta}[u_{\bf r}](z)=u(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{{\bf r\to 1}}P_{\alpha,\beta}[u_{\bf r}](z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{{\bf r\to 1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}P_{\alpha,\beta}(z,\zeta)\sum_{(p,q)\in H}c_{p,q}{\bf r}^{p}{\bf r}^{q}\mathcal{F}_{p,q}({\bf r})\zeta^{p}\overline{\zeta}^{q}dm_{n}(\zeta)

\varphi(\zeta^{0})=0

\displaystyle|u(z)|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\varphi(\zeta)|\prod_{j=1}^{n}|P_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}(r_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}},\zeta_{j})|dm_{n}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cdots\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mid\varphi(e^{it_{1}},\ldots,e^{it_{n}})\mid\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mid v_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}(r_{j}e^{i(\theta_{j}-t_{j})})\mid dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n}

For

Thus we have

\displaystyle I_{0}(z,\delta)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[-\delta,\delta]^{n}}|\varphi(e^{i(\theta_{1}-t_{1})},\ldots,e^{i(\theta_{n}-t_{n})})|\prod_{j=1}^{n}|v_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}(r_{j}e^{it_{j}})|dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{M_{\infty}(\varphi,\theta,\delta)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{[-\delta,\delta]^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|v_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}(r_{j}e^{it_{j}})|dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n}\leq K(\alpha,\beta)M_{\infty}(\varphi,\theta,\delta)

:

\displaystyle I_{j}(z,\delta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}

\displaystyle\int_{S_{j}}|\varphi(e^{i(\theta_{1}-t_{1})},\ldots,e^{i(\theta_{n}-t_{n})})|\prod_{k=1}^{n}|v_{\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}}(r_{k}e^{it_{k}})|dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\prod_{k\not=j}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|v_{\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}}(r_{k}e^{it_{k}})|dt_{k}\cdot\int_{\delta<|t_{j}|\leq\pi}|v_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}(r_{j}e^{it_{j}})|dt_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K_{j}(\alpha,\beta)}{2\pi}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta<|t_{j}|\leq\pi}|v_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}(r_{j}e^{it_{j}})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{j}(\delta)K_{j}(\alpha,\beta)(1-r_{j}^{2})^{\Re\alpha_{j}+\Re\beta_{j}+1}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}.

Hence, setting
C_{\alpha,\beta}(\delta)=\max_{j}C_{j}(\delta)K_{j}(\alpha,\beta)
, we have obtained

(3.9)

|u(z)|\leq K(\alpha,\beta)M_{\infty}(\varphi,\theta,\delta)+C_{\alpha,\beta}(\delta)\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-r_{j}^{2})^{\Re\alpha_{j}+\Re\beta_{j}+1}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
we can choose
\delta>0
such that
\mid\theta_{j}-\theta_{j}^{0}\mid<2\delta
,
1\leq j\leq n
,
implies
\mid\varphi(\zeta)\mid<\varepsilon
, where
\zeta=(e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{n}})
. Therefore, if

\mid\theta_{j}-\theta_{j}^{0}\mid<\delta
,
1\leq j\leq n
, then
M_{\infty}(\varphi,\theta,\delta)\leq\varepsilon
. Now the
result follows from (
3.9
), conditions

and observation that

converges to
\zeta^{0}
if and only if
r_{j}\to 1

and
\theta_{j}\to\theta_{j}^{0}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
.
∎

Definition 3.10

Lemma 3.12

Theorem 3.14

In proving (

(\alpha,\beta)

4.
Maximal functions and Fatou type theorems

In the previous section we studied convergence in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
norm, or in weak
∗
topology, of
u_{\bf r}
for
u
in the appropriate
sh^{p}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
space. In this section we study almost everywhere convergence of

u_{\bf r}
for a separately
(\alpha,\beta)
- harmonic function
u
. The first result of this nature was related to Cesaro summability of double Fourier series in
[
10
]
. In that paper authors noted that their results hold for Poisson kernels as well as for Fejer kernels,
thus obtaining a result for 2 - harmonic functions. Our approach is based on presentation given in
[
13
]
, where
n
- harmonic functions are considered, see Theorem 2.3.1. from
[
13
]
. An important tool is a special maximal function
M_{q}
, it is used to obtain estimates of relevant restricted non-tangential maximal functions which lead to Fatou type theorems. Since the proof of crucial Theorem
4.6
is
rather involved, we split this section into subsections.

4.1.
A maximal function
M_{q}

For
\gamma
in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
we define a
\gamma
box as a subset
Q
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
of the following form:

Q=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n},\quad\mbox{ where}\quad I_{j}=\{e^{i\theta}\mid a_{j}\leq\theta<b_{j}\}

is a semi-open arc on

\mathbb{T}
of length
0<b_{j}-a_{j}\leq 2\pi
and these lengths have the same ratio as
(2^{\gamma_{1}},\ldots,2^{\gamma_{n}})
. The center of such a box is denoted by
c(Q)=(e^{ic_{1}},\ldots,e^{ic_{n}})
, where
c_{j}=(a_{j}+b_{j})/2
.
We denote the family of all
\gamma
boxes by
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}
.
Next we define
\gamma
- maximal function of a complex Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
by the following formula:

(4.1)

M_{\gamma}\mu(\zeta)=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma},c(Q)=\zeta}\frac{|\mu|(Q)}{m_{n}(Q)},\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

By considering suitable concentric boxes one shows the following estimate:

(4.2)

M_{\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(\zeta)\leq 2^{|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|}M_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\zeta),\qquad\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

As in the standard situation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with balls instead of
\gamma
boxes the following basic estimate holds
(see
[
13
]
, pp. 26-27):

(4.3)

m_{n}(\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid M_{\gamma}\mu(\zeta)>\lambda\})\leq 3^{n}\frac{\|\mu\|}{\lambda},\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad\lambda>0.

The fact that the constant
3^{n}
is independent of
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
is of crucial importance. Next, for

0<q<1
, we introduce a maximal function
M_{q}
by

(4.4)

M_{q}\mu(\zeta)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}q^{|\gamma|}M_{\gamma}\mu(\zeta),\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
13
]
, page 26 for
q=1/2
)
.

Let
0<q<1
and let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then

(4.5)

m_{n}(\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid M_{q}\mu(\zeta)>\lambda\})\leq\frac{3^{n}}{(1-\sqrt{q})^{2n}}\frac{\|\mu\|}{\lambda},\quad\lambda>0.

Proof.

Set, for
\lambda>0
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
:

E(\lambda)=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid M_{q}\mu(\zeta)>\lambda\},\qquad E_{\gamma}(\lambda)=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid M_{\gamma}\mu(\zeta)>\lambda\}.

Note that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{\frac{k}{2}}=\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{q}}=\eta(q)
and

\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}q^{\frac{|\gamma|}{2}}=\eta(q)^{n}
for
0<q<1
. Hence, taking
into account (
4.4
), we see that

E(\lambda)\subset\cup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}E_{\gamma}(\eta(q)^{-n}q^{-|\gamma|/2}\lambda).

Therefore, using (
4.3
), we obtain

m_{n}(E(\lambda))\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}3^{n}\frac{\|\mu\|}{\eta(q)^{-n}q^{-|\gamma|/2}\lambda}=3^{n}\eta(q)^{2n}\|\mu\|\lambda^{-1},

which is desired estimate (
4.5
).
∎

4.2.
An estimate of
P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu]
by
P_{\bf t}[d\mu]

For real
t>-1
and
z
in
\mathbb{D}
we set

(4.6)

\ u_{t}(z)=u_{t/2,t/2}(z)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{t+1}}{|1-z|^{t+2}},

(4.7)

v_{t}(z)=v_{t/2,t/2}(z)=\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{t}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(t+1)}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{t+1}}{|1-z|^{t+2}}.

An elementary inequality
|1-z|\geq\pi^{-1}|\theta|
, which is valid for

0\leq r<1
and
-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi
, implies the following estimate:

(4.8)

0<u_{t}(re^{i\theta})\leq 2^{t+1}\pi^{t+2}\frac{(1-r)^{t+1}}{|\theta|^{t+2}},\qquad 0\leq r<1,\quad-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi.

The kernel
v_{t}(z)
appeared in
[
11
]
where the operator
L_{t/2,t/2}
was analysed and a Fatou type theorem
was obtained in the one dimensional setting of
\mathbb{D}
.

For
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in(-1,+\infty)^{n}
we set

(4.9)

U_{\bf t}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}u_{t_{j}}(z_{j}),\quad V_{\bf t}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}v_{t_{j}}(z_{j}),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

Now we have positive kernels
K_{\bf t}(z,\zeta)=V_{\bf t}(z\cdot\overline{\zeta})

and
K^{\prime}_{\bf t}(z,\zeta)=U_{\bf t}(z\cdot\overline{\zeta})
where
z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
Clearly
K_{\bf t}=P_{\alpha,\beta}
with
\alpha=\beta={\bf t}/2
.

The next lemma follows from (
2.11
), (
2.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.6
). It allows us to pass
from complex valued kernels
P_{\alpha,\beta}
to positive ones in various estimates, see (
4.15
) below.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and let
z\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then we have

(4.10)

|P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu](z)|\leq k_{\alpha,\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}U_{\bf t}(z\cdot\overline{\zeta})d|\mu|(\zeta)=\frac{k_{\alpha,\beta}}{k_{\bf t}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}K_{\bf t}(z,\zeta)d|\mu|(\zeta).

Here
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
where
t_{j}=\Re\alpha_{j}+\Re\beta_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
and

k_{\alpha,\beta}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}|c_{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}}|,\qquad k_{\bf t}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{t_{j}}{2}+1\right)}{\Gamma(t_{j}+1)}.

4.3.
Estimates within Stolz cone with fixed
\bf r

We define the full Stolz cone at
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
of aperture
0<A<\infty
by

(4.11)

S_{A}(\zeta)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}S_{A}(\zeta_{j})

and a restricted Stolz cone
S_{A,B}(\zeta)
for
0<A<\infty
,
1\leq B<\infty
at
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
by

(4.12)

S_{A,B}(\zeta)=\left\{(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,r_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}})\in S_{A}(\zeta):\max_{j,k}\frac{1-r_{j}}{1-r_{k}}\leq B\right\}.

It is an elementary fact that there is a constant
\delta_{A}>0
such that

(4.13)

\delta_{A}|1-re^{i\varphi}|\leq\inf_{|\theta|\leq A(1-r)}|1-re^{i(\varphi-\theta)}|\leq|1-re^{i\varphi}|,\quad 0<A<\infty.

Let us set

(4.14)

c({\bf t})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j}+2),\qquad{\bf t}\in(-1,+\infty)^{n}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\eta=(e^{i\psi_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\psi_{n}})
in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
{\bf r}\in[0,1)^{n}
,
{\bf t}\in(-1,+\infty)^{n}

and
0<A<\infty
. Then we have,

(4.15)

\sup_{\xi:\;{\bf r}\cdot\xi\in S_{A}(\eta)}|P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu]({\bf r}\cdot\xi)|\leq\frac{k_{\alpha,\beta}}{k_{\bf t}\delta_{A}^{c({\bf t})}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}K_{\bf t}({\bf r}\cdot\eta,\zeta)d|\mu|(\zeta),\quad\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Here
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
where
t_{j}=\Re\alpha_{j}+\Re\beta_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
.

Proof.

Using (
4.13
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in S_{A}(\eta),|z_{j}|=r_{j}}U_{\bf t}(z\cdot\overline{\zeta})

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in S_{A}(\eta),|z_{j}|=r_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(1-r_{j}^{2})^{t_{j}+1}}{|1-z_{j}\overline{\zeta_{j}}|^{t_{j}+2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{|\theta_{j}|\leq A(1-r_{j})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(1-r_{j}^{2})^{t_{j}+1}}{|1-r_{j}e^{i[(\psi_{j}-\theta_{j})-\varphi_{j}]}|^{t_{j}+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{A}^{-c({\bf t})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(1-r_{j}^{2})^{t_{j}+1}}{|1-r_{j}e^{i(\psi_{j}-\varphi_{j})}|^{t_{j}+2}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle=\delta_{A}^{-c({\bf t})}U_{\bf t}({\bf r}\cdot\eta\cdot\overline{\zeta}),

where
z=(r_{1}e^{i(\psi_{1}-\theta_{1})},\ldots,r_{n}e^{i(\psi_{n}-\theta_{n})})
is in
S_{A}(\eta)
and

\zeta=(e^{i\varphi_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\varphi_{n}})
is in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. This and
Lemma
4.2
give desired estimate.
∎

Note that in (
4.15
) both
\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
{\bf r}\in[0,1)^{n}
are fixed. In order to estimate the
integral on the right hand side of (
4.15
) we need a special partition of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
adapted to given
\bf r
.

4.4.
Partitions
\mathcal{P}_{\bf r}
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and estimates of
|\mu|(Q)
,

Q\in\mathcal{P}_{\bf r}

For a given
0\leq\rho<1
we denote by
\kappa=\kappa(\rho)
the unique
\kappa\in[1,2)
such that

(4.17)

\frac{\pi}{(1-\rho)\kappa(\rho)}=2^{p}\qquad\mbox{where}\quad p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This
p=p(\rho)
is uniquely determined by
\rho
. We set

(4.18)

x_{0}=0,\quad x_{j}=x_{j}(\rho)=2^{j-1}(1-\rho)\kappa(\rho)\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq j\leq p+1.

Clearly
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{p+1}=\pi
and
2x_{j}=x_{j+1}
for
1\leq j\leq p
. Set, for
j=0,\ldots,p
,

J_{j}^{+}=J_{j}^{+}(\rho)=[x_{j},x_{j+1})
and
J_{j}^{-}=J_{j}^{-}(\rho)=[-x_{j+1},-x_{j})
. These

2(p+1)
semi open intervals form a partition
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho}
of
[-\pi,\pi)
and the corresponding
2(p+1)
semi open arcs
I_{j}^{+}=I_{j}^{+}(\rho)
and
I_{j}^{-}=I_{j}^{-}(\rho)
on
\mathbb{T}
form a partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
of

\mathbb{T}
. Note that
m_{1}(I_{j}^{\pm})=2^{j-2}(1-\rho)\kappa(\rho)/\pi
for
1\leq j\leq p
and

m_{1}(I_{1}^{\pm})=m_{1}(I_{0}^{\pm})
.

Let us fix
1\leq B<+\infty
and choose the smallest integer
b\geq 0
such that
B\leq 2^{b}
. Assume
{\bf r}\in[0,1)^{n}

satisfies condition
\max_{j}(1-r_{j})\leq B\min_{j}(1-r_{j})
. We can assume, without loss of generality, that
r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\cdots\leq r_{n}
. The partitions
\mathcal{P}_{r_{k}}
,
1\leq k\leq n
, of
\mathbb{T}
induce a partition of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Namely, choose
\varepsilon
in
\{+,-\}^{n}
,
j
in
\{0,\ldots,p(r_{1})\}\times\cdots\times\{0,\ldots,p(r_{n})\}
and set

Q(\varepsilon,j)=\prod_{l=1}^{n}I_{j_{l}}^{\varepsilon_{l}}
.
These
[2(p(r_{1})+1)]\times\cdots\times[2(p(r_{n})+1)]
boxes form a partition
\mathcal{P}_{\bf r}
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
Moreover, for each choice of
\varepsilon
and
j
there is an increasing sequence
\iota=(\iota_{l})_{l=1}^{n}
of integers such that
\iota_{1}=0
,
\iota_{n}\leq b
and
Q(\varepsilon,j)
is a
(j_{1}+\iota_{1},\ldots,j_{n}+\iota_{n})
box.

Every box
Q(\varepsilon,j)
is contained in a box
R(j)

consisting of all
(e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{n}})
such that
-x_{j_{k}+1}(r_{k})\leq\theta_{k}<x_{j_{k}+1}(r_{k})
for all

1\leq k\leq n
. The box
R(j)
is, being homothetic to
Q(\varepsilon,j)
, also a
j+\iota
box, it is clearly centered at

\bf 1
and
m_{n}(R(j))=4^{n}m_{n}(Q(\varepsilon,j))
. Hence, using (
4.2
) we obtain

(4.19)

\displaystyle|\mu|(Q(\varepsilon,j))

\displaystyle\leq|\mu|(R(j))\leq m_{n}(R(j))(M_{j+\iota}\mu)({\bf 1})

\displaystyle=4^{n}m_{n}(Q(\varepsilon,j))(M_{j+\iota}\mu)({\bf 1})

\displaystyle\leq 4^{n}2^{(n-1)b}m_{n}(Q(\varepsilon,j))(M_{j}\mu)({\bf 1})

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{n}(2B)^{n-1}m_{n}(Q(\varepsilon,j))(M_{j}\mu)({\bf 1})

It easily follows from (
4.18
) and (
4.17
) that

(4.21)

m_{n}(Q(\varepsilon,j))=4^{-n}2^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(1-r_{k})\kappa(r_{k})}{\pi}=4^{-n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}2^{j_{k}-p(r_{k})}

which, combined with the previous inequality, gives the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mu
be a complex Borel measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, let
1\leq B<\infty
, assume
{\bf r}\in[0,1)^{n}
satisfies condition
\max_{j}(1-r_{j})\leq B\min_{j}(1-r_{j})
and let
\mathcal{P}_{\bf r}
be the partition of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
associated with

\bf r
. Then for every
\varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}^{n}
and
j=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
such that
0\leq j_{k}\leq p(r_{k})
for all

1\leq k\leq n
we have:

(4.22)

|\mu|(Q(\varepsilon,j))\leq(2B)^{n-1}2^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(1-r_{k})\kappa(r_{k})}{\pi}(M_{j}\mu)({\bf 1}).

4.5.
Estimate of
P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu]
over
S_{A,B}(\eta)

Let us fix
0\leq\rho<1
and consider points
x_{0},\ldots x_{p+1}
from (
4.18
) which generate partition

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho}
of
[0,\pi)
. We apply (
4.8
) to
\theta=x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}
and obtain,
using (
4.18
) and
\kappa(\rho)\geq 1
, the following inequality for
1\leq j\leq p=p(\rho)
:

u_{t}(\rho e^{ix_{j}})\leq\frac{2^{t+1}\pi^{t+2}}{1-\rho}\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2^{j-1}(1-\rho)\kappa(\rho)}\right)^{t+2}\leq\frac{2^{2t+3}\pi^{t+2}}{1-\rho}2^{-(t+2)j}.

It is valid also for
j=0
by definition
(
4.6
) of
u_{t}
, so we obtained

(4.23)

u_{t}(\rho e^{ix_{j}})\leq\frac{2^{2t+3}\pi^{t+2}}{1-\rho}2^{-(t+2)j},\qquad 0\leq j\leq p=p(\rho).

Note that
u_{t}(\rho e^{i\theta})
is even in
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi)
and decreasing in
\theta\in(0,\pi)
for a fixed

0\leq\rho<1
(see
[
12
]
). Thus (
4.23
) gives, for
{\bf t}
in
(-1,+\infty)^{n}

and
{\bf r}
in
[0,1)^{n}
:

(4.24)

\displaystyle U_{\bf t}({\bf r}\cdot\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\bf t}\prod_{k=1}^{n}2^{-(t_{k}+2)j_{k}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-r_{k}},\qquad\zeta\in Q(\varepsilon,j).

Now we assume
\bf r
satisfies condition
\max_{j}(1-r_{j})\leq B\min_{j}(1-r_{j})
where
B\geq 1
and
estimate the integral appearing in Proposition
4.3
, taking
\eta={\bf 1}
for convenience of notation. Using
(
4.24
) and Proposition
4.4
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}K_{\bf t}({\bf r}\cdot{\bf 1},\zeta)d|\mu|(\zeta)

\displaystyle=k_{\bf t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}U_{\bf t}({\bf r}\cdot\zeta)d|\mu|(\zeta)=k_{\bf t}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{P}_{\bf r}}\int_{Q}U_{\bf t}({\bf r}\cdot\zeta)d|\mu|(\zeta)

(4.25)

\displaystyle\leq k_{\bf t}C_{\bf t}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-r_{k}}\sum_{(\varepsilon,j)}|\mu|(Q(\varepsilon,j))\prod_{k=1}^{n}2^{-(t_{k}+2)j_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2n-1}B^{n-1}k_{\bf t}C_{\bf t}\sum_{j}\prod_{k=1}^{n}2^{-(t_{k}+2)j_{k}}2^{j_{k}}\frac{\kappa(r_{k})}{\pi}(M_{j}\mu)({\bf 1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{3n-1}B^{n-1}}{\pi^{n}}k_{\bf t}C_{\bf t}\sum_{j}\prod_{k=1}^{n}2^{-(t_{k}+1)j_{k}}(M_{j}\mu)({\bf 1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{3n-1}B^{n-1}}{\pi^{n}}k_{\bf t}C_{\bf t}(M_{q}\mu)({\bf 1})

where

\quad q=q({\bf t})=2^{-\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(t_{k}+1)}<1.

Note that the right hand side does not depend on
{\bf r}
. Now we use (
4.15
) to get

(4.26)

\sup_{{\bf r}\cdot\xi\in S_{A,B}({\bf 1})}|P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu]({\bf r}\cdot\xi)|\leq\frac{k_{\alpha,\beta}}{\delta_{A}^{c({\bf t})}}\frac{2^{3n-1}B^{n-1}}{\pi^{n}}C_{\bf t}(M_{q}\mu)({\bf 1}).

Since (
4.26
) holds at any point
\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
we proved the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
0<A<+\infty
and
1\leq B<+\infty
. Set
q=2^{-m(\alpha,\beta)}<1
where

m(\alpha,\beta)=\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(\Re\alpha_{k}+\Re\beta_{k}+1)
,

t_{j}=\Re\alpha_{j}+\Re\beta_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
and
c({\bf t})=\sum(t_{j}+2)
.
Then there are constants
\delta_{A}>0
and
C_{\alpha,\beta}<\infty
such that

(4.27)

\sup_{z\in S_{A,B}(\eta)}|P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu](z)|\leq B^{n-1}\frac{C_{\alpha,\beta}}{\delta^{c({\bf t})}_{A}}(M_{q}\mu)(\eta),\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

4.6.
Restricted non-tangential maximal function

In view of Proposition
4.5
it is natural to introduce a restricted non-tangential maximal function of aperture

0<A<+\infty
and restriction
1\leq B<\infty
of a function
u\in C(\mathbb{D}^{n})

by
(\tilde{M}_{A,B}^{NT}u)(\eta)=\sup_{z\in S_{A,B}(\eta)}|u(z)|
,
\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and of a
complex Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
by

(4.28)

(M_{A,B}^{NT}\mu)(\eta)=\sup_{z\in S_{A,B}(\eta)}|P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu](z)|,\qquad\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Of course,
M_{A,B}^{NT}\mu=\tilde{M}_{A,B}^{NT}(P_{\alpha,\beta}[d\mu])
.
If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
we write simply
M_{A,B}^{NT}f
instead of
M_{A,B}^{NT}fdm_{n}
.

Combining Proposition
4.5
and Lemma
4.1
we obtain the following weak
(1,1)
type estimate for the restricted
non-tangential maximal function
M_{A,B}^{NT}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

For every
0<A<+\infty
and
1\leq B<\infty
there is a constant
C=C_{\alpha,\beta,A,B}
such that

(4.29)

m_{n}(\{\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:(M_{A,B}^{NT}\mu)(\eta)>\lambda\})\leq C\frac{\|\mu\|}{\lambda},\qquad\lambda>0.

4.7.
Applications to Fatou type theorems

Now we turn to Fatou type theorems, Theorem
4.6
will play a key role.

Definition 4.7
.

We write
NT_{A,B}-\lim_{z\to\eta}u(z)=l
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\delta>0
such that

|u(z)-l|<\varepsilon
whenever
|z-\eta|<\delta
and
z\in S_{A,B}(\eta)
. Here
u
is a function on

\mathbb{D}^{n}
,
0<A<\infty
,
1\leq B<\infty
and
\eta
is in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

If
NT_{A,B}-\lim_{z\to\eta}u(z)=l
for every
0<A<\infty
and
1\leq B<\infty
we write

RNT-\lim_{z\to\eta}u(z)=l
(restricted non-tangential limit).

If
u
is real valued we can analogously consider
NT_{A,B}-\limsup_{z\to\eta}u(z)
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
u=P_{\alpha,\beta}[f]
. Then

(4.30)

RNT-\lim_{z\to\eta}u(z)=f(\eta)\quad\mbox{for almost every}\quad\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Proof.

We fix
0<A<\infty
and
1\leq B<\infty
.
Let us define for
\delta>0
,
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}

(4.31)

\Omega^{\delta}(g,\eta)=\sup\{|P_{\alpha,\beta}[g](z)-P_{\alpha,\beta}[g](w)|:z,w\in S_{A,B}(\eta)\cap B(\eta,\delta)\}

and note the following properties of
\Omega^{\delta}(g,\eta)
:

1^{o}
.
\Omega^{\delta}(g,\eta)
is increasing in
\delta>0
and

\Omega^{\delta}(g,\eta)\leq 2(M_{A,B}^{NT}g)(\eta)
.

2^{o}
.
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Omega^{\delta}(g,\eta)=0
if and only if

NT_{A,B}-\lim_{z\to\eta}P_{\alpha,\beta}[g](z)
exists.

3^{o}
.
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Omega^{\delta}(g,\eta)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Omega^{\delta}(g-\varphi,\eta)
for every

\varphi\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

The first two properties are obvious, the third one follows from Theorem
3.5
. Set

E_{\varepsilon}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Omega^{\delta}(f,\zeta)>\varepsilon\}
for
\varepsilon>0
.
By properties
1^{o}
and
3^{o}
we have, for any
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
:

E_{\varepsilon}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Omega^{\delta}(f-\varphi,\zeta)>\varepsilon\}\subset\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:[M_{A,B}^{NT}(f-\varphi)](\zeta)>\varepsilon/2\}.

Using Theorem
4.6
we deduce

m_{n}(\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:[M_{A,B}^{NT}(f-\varphi)](\zeta)>\varepsilon/2\})\leq\frac{2C_{\alpha,\beta,A,B}}{\varepsilon}\|f-\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

Since
C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is dense in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, the set

\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:[M_{A,B}^{NT}(f-\varphi)](\zeta)>\varepsilon/2\}
is a null set and therefore
E_{\varepsilon}
is also a null set. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary it follows that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Omega^{\delta}(f,\eta)=0
for almost every
\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Therefore, using property
2^{o}
,

NT_{A,B}-\lim_{z\to\eta}P_{\alpha,\beta}[f](z)=F(\eta)
exists for almost every
\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
Now we have
F(\eta)=f(\eta)
by Theorem
3.14
, formula (
3.13
).
∎

Lemma 4.9

If a complex Borel measure

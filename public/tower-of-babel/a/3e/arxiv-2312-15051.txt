One-loop five-parton amplitudes in the NMRK limit
Emmet P. Byrne

Abstract
We analyse the real part of one-loop five-parton amplitudes in the next-to-multi-Regge kinematic (NMRK) limit, to leading power, and to finite order in the dimensional regularisation parameter. To leading logarithmic (LL) accuracy, it is known that five-parton amplitudes in this limit are given to all-orders by a single factorised expression, in which the pair of partons which are not well-separated in rapidity are described by a two-parton emission vertex. In this study, we investigate the one-loop amplitudes at next-to-leading logarithmic (NLL) accuracy, and find that is has a more complex structure. In particular, it is found that the purely gluonic amplitudes are compatible with an analogous factorisation of individual colour structures. From the one-loop amplitudes we extract one-loop two-parton emission vertices, which are functions of a subset of the momenta of the amplitude. In the multi-Regge kinematic (MRK) limit, the vertices themselves factorise into the known one-loop single-parton emission vertices and Lipatov vertex, with rapidity dependence governed by the one-loop gluon Regge trajectory, as required by compatibility with the known MRK limit of amplitudes. The one-loop two-parton emission vertices are necessary ingredients for the construction of the next-to-next-to leading order (NNLO) jet impact factors in the BFKL framework.

Introduction

The multi-Regge kinematic (MRK) limit of amplitudes, in which final-state particles are strongly ordered in rapidity, has long been a source of insight into the all-orders structure of amplitudes
Lipatov:1976zz
;
DelDuca:2019tur
. Knowledge of the MRK limit has been used to compute or constrain amplitudes in general kinematics
DelDuca:2009au
;
DelDuca:2010zg
;
Dixon:2014iba
;
Henn:2016jdu
;
Caron-Huot:2016owq
;
Almelid:2017qju
;
Caron-Huot:2019vjl
;
Falcioni:2021buo
;
Caola:2021izf

White:1973ola
;
Brower:1974yv
. The NMRK limit is an intriguing theoretical arena, where on the one hand the simplified kinematics make possible a detailed study of high-multiplicity amplitudes, revealing structures which are obscured in general kinematics
Bartels:2008ce
;
Byrne:2022wzk
, and on the other hand, the NMRK limit captures more of the complexity and richness of amplitudes than does the MRK limit. The NMRK limit and its MRK boundaries provide an ideal test bed for studying which properties of MRK amplitudes generalise to more generic kinematic spaces. In this work, we find that the known MRK limit of amplitudes motivates a highly convenient framework for organising the kinematic structure of the amplitudes in the NMRK limit. In particular, this organisation makes it trivial to take the further MRK limit of NMRK-factorised expressions.

The behaviour of amplitudes in Regge limits is important for determining the asymptotic high-energy limit of cross sections in QCD

;

The Balitsky-Fadin-Kuraev-Lipatov (BFKL) equation

;

;

The leading order (LO) kernel of this equation consists of the square of the Lipatov vertex

The NLO kernel may be computed using building blocks which can be obtained by taking the MRK limit of one-loop amplitudes as well as building blocks which can be obtained by taking the NMRK limit of tree-level amplitudes. At next-to-next-to leading-logarithmic (NNLL) accuracy, the real part of the amplitude is described by a Regge cut in addition to a Regge pole

Caron-Huot:2017fxr

One of the most interesting aspects of Regge limits of amplitudes is the interplay of colour and kinematic structure. In this section we study the colour structure of one-loop five-parton amplitudes in the NMRK limit. We will utilise the one-loop DDM colour decompositions

Byrne:2022wzk
, in
section

3.1
Review of colour structure of one-loop amplitudes

Beyond tree level it is useful to make a distinction between partial and primitive amplitudes. By primitive amplitude we mean a gauge invariant subset of colour-ordered Feynman diagrams. By partial amplitude we mean a kinematic coefficient of a colour structure where the colour ordering is not necessarily fixed.

For the one-loop amplitudes that we analyse in this paper we will use the DDM colour bases introduced in ref.
DelDuca:1999rs
. For
n
-gluon amplitudes the relevant colour decomposition is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{(1)}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})&=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-1}/R}\mbox{tr}\left(F^{a_{\sigma_{1}}}\cdots F^{a_{\sigma_{n}}}\right)A^{(1,\,g)}_{n}\left({\sigma_{1}}^{\lambda_{\sigma_{1}}},\ldots,{\sigma_{n}}^{\lambda_{\sigma_{n}}}\right)\\
&+N_{f}\ g_{\mathrm{s}}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\mbox{tr}\left(T^{a_{\sigma_{1}}}\cdots T^{a_{\sigma_{n}}}\right)A^{(1,\,f)}_{n}\left(\sigma_{1}^{\lambda_{\sigma_{1}}},\ldots,{\sigma_{n}}^{\lambda_{\sigma_{n}}}\right)\,,\end{split}

(39)

where
S_{n-1}=S_{n}/Z_{n}
is the group of non-cyclic permutations and
R
is the reflection operation
R(1,2,\ldots,n-1,n)=R(n,n-1,\ldots,2,1)
. Here,
A_{n}^{(1,\ m)}
are the one-loop primitive amplitudes with
n
external partons, and particle content
m
circulating in the loop. We use the notation
g
or
f
to indicate a gluon or fermion is circulating in the loop. We will later also consider the possibility of a complex scalar,
m{=}s
, circulating in the loop. We note the
n
-gluon partial amplitudes obey the reflection identity

A_{n}^{(1,\,m)}\left(1,2,\ldots,n-1,n\right)=(-1)^{n}A_{n}^{(1,\,m)}\left(n,n-1,\ldots,2,1\right)\,.

(40)

For amplitudes with
(n-2)
gluons and a quark-antiquark pair the analogous colour decomposition is
DelDuca:1999rs

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{(1)}\left(\bar{q}_{1},q_{2},g_{3},\ldots,g_{n}\right)=&g_{\mathrm{s}}^{n}\Bigg[\sum_{p=2}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}}\left(T^{c_{2}}T^{a_{\sigma_{3}}}\cdots T^{a_{\sigma_{p}}}T^{c_{1}}\right)_{\imath_{2}\bar{\imath}_{1}}\left(F^{a_{\sigma_{p+1}}}\cdots F^{a_{\sigma_{n}}}\right)_{c_{1}c_{2}}\\
&\times A^{(1,\,g_{R})}_{n}\left(1_{{\bar{q}}}^{\lambda_{1}},{\sigma_{p+1}}^{\lambda_{\sigma_{p+1}}},\ldots,{\sigma_{n}}^{\lambda_{\sigma_{n}}},2_{q}^{\lambda_{2}},{\sigma_{3}}^{\lambda_{3}},\ldots,{\sigma_{p}}^{\lambda_{p}}\right)\\
&\mkern-180.0mu+\frac{N_{f}}{N_{c}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}/S_{n;j}}\operatorname{Gr}_{n;j}^{(\bar{q}q)}\left(\sigma_{3},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\right)A_{n;j}^{(1,\,f)}\left(1_{\bar{q}}^{\lambda_{1}},2_{q}^{\lambda_{2}};{\sigma_{3}}^{\lambda_{3}},\ldots,{\sigma_{n}}^{\lambda_{n}}\right)\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(41)

For primitive amplitudes with an external quark-antiquark pair, diagrams without closed fermion or scalar loops are assigned to
m{=}g
because these diagrams mix under gauge transformations with diagrams with closed gluon loops.
Following ref.
Bern:1994fz
, we separate primitive amplitudes with an external quark-antiquark pair into individually gauge invariant terms,

A^{(1,\,m)}_{n}=A^{(1,\,m_{R})}_{n}+A^{(1,\,m_{L})}_{n}\,.

(42)

The label
R
or
L
denotes the gauge invariant subset of colour-ordered Feynman diagrams in which the fermion line passes to the right or left of the closed loop (with the diagrammatic convention that colour ordered partons are labeled clockwise). The
L
and
R
primitive amplitudes are related by a reflection identity analogous to
eq.

40
:

A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{R})}\left(1_{\bar{q}},3,4,\ldots,2_{q},\ldots,n-1,n\right)=(-1)^{n}A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{L})}\left(1_{\bar{q}},n,n-1,\ldots,2_{q},\ldots,4,3\right)\,.

(43)

Note that for
m{\in}\{f,s\}
the following primitive amplitudes vanish:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{R})}(1_{\bar{q}},2_{q},3,4,\ldots,n)

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{L})}(1_{\bar{q}},n,\ldots,4,3,2_{q})=0\,,

(44a)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{R})}(1_{\bar{q}},3,2_{q},4,\ldots,n)

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{L})}(1_{\bar{q}},n,\ldots,4,2_{q},3)=0\,.

(44b)

These identities can be understood by considering the Feynman diagrams which contribute to the respective amplitudes. The amplitudes in
eq.

44a
and
eq.

44b
permit only tadpole and bubble diagrams respectively, which vanish in dimensional regularisation. We emphasise that
eq.

44
does not hold for
m{=}g
because, as stated previously, the external fermion line may form part of the gluon loop.

The pure-gluon decomposition,
eq.

39
, is written solely in terms of primitive amplitudes. This is not the case for one-loop amplitudes with an external quark-antiquark pair,
eq.

41
, where partial amplitudes, not primitive amplitudes, multiply the
colour structures
\operatorname{Gr}^{(q{\bar{q}})}_{n;j}
. These colour structures are defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{Gr}_{n;1}^{(\bar{q}q)}(3,\ldots,n)&=N_{c}\left(T^{a_{3}}\cdots T^{a_{n}}\right)_{\imath_{2}\bar{\imath}_{1}}\,,\\
\operatorname{Gr}_{n;2}^{(\bar{q}q)}(3;4,\ldots,n)&=0\,,\\
\operatorname{Gr}_{n;j}^{(\bar{q}q)}(3,\ldots,j+1;j+2,\ldots,n)&=\mbox{tr}\left(T^{a_{3}}\cdots T^{a_{j+1}}\right)\left(T^{a_{j+2}}\cdots T^{a_{n}}\right)_{\imath_{2}\bar{\imath}_{1}},\quad 3\leq j\leq n-2,\\
\operatorname{Gr}_{n;n-1}^{(\bar{q}q)}(3,\ldots,n)&=\mbox{tr}\left(T^{a_{3}}\cdots T^{a_{n}}\right)\delta_{\imath_{2}\bar{\imath}_{1}}\,,\end{split}

(45)

and
S_{n;j}=Z_{j-1}
is the subgroup of
S_{n-2}
that leaves
\operatorname{Gr}^{(q{\bar{q}})}_{n;j}
invariant. The partial amplitudes which multiply these colour structures can be written as a sum of the simpler primitive amplitudes
Bern:1994fz

\displaystyle A_{n;1}^{(1,\,f)}\left(1_{\bar{q}},2_{q},3,\ldots,n\right)

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\,f_{L})}\left(1_{\bar{q}},2_{q},3,\ldots,n\right),

(46a)

\displaystyle A_{n;j>1}^{(1,\,f)}\left(1_{\bar{q}},2_{q};3,\ldots,j{+}1;j{+}2,\ldots,n\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{COP}\{\alpha\}\{\beta\}}A_{n}^{(1,\,f_{R})}\left({\sigma_{1}}_{\bar{q}},{\sigma_{2}}_{q},\sigma_{3},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\right),

(46b)

with
\{\alpha\}=\{j{+}1,j,\ldots,4,3\},\{\beta\}=\{1,2,j{+}2,j{+}3,\ldots,n{-}1,n\}
, and where
\operatorname{COP}\{\alpha\}\{\beta\}
is the set of all permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
with leg 1 held fixed that preserve the cyclic ordering of the elements
\alpha_{i}
within
\{\alpha\}
and the elements
\beta_{i}
within
\{\beta\}
, while allowing for all possible relative orderings of the
\alpha_{i}
with respect to the
\{\beta\}

Bern:1993mq

This concludes our review of the colour structure of one-loop amplitudes. In the next section we show how the colour structure of one-loop five-parton amplitudes simplifies in the NMRK limit.

Let us first consider the five-gluon amplitude for the physical process

4
One-loop primitive vertices for the peripheral emission of two gluons

In this section we investigate the NMRK limit of one-loop, colour-ordered, five-gluon amplitudes. Such amplitudes can be organised along the lines of colour ordering, helicity configuration, and loop content. We have organised this section firstly in terms of loop content, that is, by what particle type, or particle multiplet, is circulating in the loop. Following the organisation of ref.
Bern:1993mq
, we investigate the
\mathcal{N}=4
amplitudes in
section

4.1

\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}

section

4.2

4.3

Bern:1993mq
;
Bern:1994zx

We can decompose one-loop pure-gluon primitive amplitudes in supersymmetric theories into a sum of primitive amplitudes with a spin-1 boson, spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fermion or complex scalar circulating in the loop. In this paper we consider the following supersymmetric multiplets:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\,g)}+4\ A_{n}^{(1,\,f)}+3\ A_{n}^{(1,\,s)}\,,

(63a)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{V})}

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\,g)}+A_{n}^{(1,\,f)}\,,

(63b)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi})}

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\,f)}+A_{n}^{(1,\,s)}\,,

(63c)

where the subscripts
V
and
\chi
denote vector and chiral multiplets respectively. We can then write the primitive amplitudes required for QCD in terms of supersymmetric amplitudes,

\displaystyle A_{n}^{(1,\ g)}

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\ \mathcal{N}=4)}-4\ A_{n}^{(1,\ \mathcal{N}=1_{\chi})}+A_{n}^{(1,\ s)}

(64a)

\displaystyle A_{n}^{(1,\ f)}

\displaystyle=A_{n}^{(1,\ \mathcal{N}=1_{\chi})}-A_{n}^{(1,\ s)}\,.

(64b)

The main benefit of these decompositions is that amplitudes in supersymmetric theories are cut-constructable, that is, they can be computed entirely from generalised unitarity cuts
Bern:1993mq
;
Bern:1994zx
;
Bern:1994cg
. The remaining complex scalar term in
eq.

64a
has a rational term which (in
D=4-2\epsilon
) cannot be computed from taking discontinuities. It is nevertheless simpler to compute than the gluon- or quark-loop amplitudes, because the complex scalar does not propagate spin information around the loop.

After the primary separation of
sections

4.1

4.2
and
4.3

For our purposes, there are two separate points worth emphasising at this stage. Firstly, a given two-gluon peripheral emission vertex, say

In, the following, we choose to present the extraction of the peripheral emission vertex from the simplest starting amplitude (although in practice we have checked each helicity configuration to verify the universality of the factorisation).

vertex

&=\frac{1}{q_{2\perp}}\left(\frac{q_{2\perp}^{*}}{q_{2\perp}}\right)\frac{X(z-1)^{3}\left(X^{3}{\bar{z}}+X^{2}(z+1){\bar{z}}+Xz(z+1)+z^{2}\right)}{(X+1)^{2}z^{2}({\bar{z}}-1)(X+z)^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(135)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{d}^{(1,\,s)}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})&=C_{d}^{(1,\,g)}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})=-C_{d}^{(1,\,f)}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})\\
&=-\frac{1}{q_{2\perp}}\left(\frac{q_{2\perp}^{*}}{q_{2\perp}}\right)\frac{3X(z-1)^{2}(z-{\bar{z}})}{(X+1)z({\bar{z}}-1)(X+z)}\,.\end{split}

(136)

The one-loop amplitude in the NMRK is then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{A}^{(1)[-]}_{5}(2^{\oplus},3^{\oplus},4^{\oplus},5^{-\lambda_{1}},1^{\lambda_{1}})\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}g_{\mathrm{s}}^{5}\,2s_{12}\,R^{(0)}_{g^{*}}(q_{1})(-F^{d}_{a_{5}a_{1}})C^{(0)}_{g^{*}gg}(-q_{2},p_{5}^{-\lambda_{1}},p_{1}^{\lambda_{1}})\\
&\times\Bigg\{\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2}}(F^{a_{\sigma_{3}}}F^{a_{\sigma_{4}}})_{a_{2}c_{2}}\ (N_{c}-N_{f})C_{FF}^{(1,\,s)}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{\sigma_{3}}^{\oplus},p_{\sigma_{4}}^{\oplus},q_{2})\,\\
&\quad+\left(d_{A}-2\,d_{F}\right)^{a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}c_{2}}C_{d}^{(1,\,s)}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})\Bigg\}+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon)\,.\end{split}

(137)

This amplitude is compatible with
eq.

29
, and we can define the one-loop colour-dressed vertex

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{ggg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2}}(F^{a_{\sigma_{3}}}F^{a_{\sigma_{4}}})_{a_{2}c_{2}}\ (N_{c}-N_{f})C_{FF}^{(1,\,s)}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{\sigma_{3}}^{\oplus},p_{\sigma_{4}}^{\oplus},q_{2})\,\\
&\quad+\left(d_{A}-2\,d_{F}\right)^{a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}c_{2}}C_{d}^{(1,\,s)}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})\,.\end{split}

(138)

This colour-dressed vertex has the MRK limit

\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{ggg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{MRK}}}{\to}\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{ggg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{1})\mathcal{R}_{g^{*}}^{(0)}(q_{1})\mathcal{V}_{g^{*}gg^{*}}^{(0)}(-q_{1},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2}),

(139)

as required by the known compatibility of the amplitude with
eq.

23
.

In the remainder of this paper, we perform an analogous investigation of the one-loop amplitudes with an external quark-antiquark pair.

6
One-loop primitive vertices for the peripheral emission of a quark and gluon

In this section we take the NMRK limit of one-loop primitive amplitudes of three gluons and a quark-antiquark pair. We use the amplitudes provided by ref.
Bern:1994fz
. Expressions are given in that paper for the following N
-1
MHV helicity configurations

\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus}\},\quad\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus}\},

which in the NMRK limit only give rise to the one-loop

\{g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{*}\}
vertex discussed in
section

4.3.1
, and the MHV configurations

\displaystyle\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus}\},\quad\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\ominus},g^{\oplus}\},\quad

\displaystyle\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\ominus}\},\quad\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\oplus}\}

\displaystyle\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},q^{\oplus},g^{\ominus},g^{\oplus}\},\quad

\displaystyle\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\ominus}\}.

Let us briefly review how any amplitude can be related to one of the above by discrete transformations. We note that parity conjugation, via complex conjugation, can be used to map to a single-minus or two-minus amplitude, and charge conjugation,

A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{L})}(\cdots,i_{q}^{\lambda},\cdots,j_{{\bar{q}}}^{(-\lambda)},\cdots)=A_{n}^{(1,\,m_{R})}(\cdots,i_{{\bar{q}}}^{\lambda},\cdots,j_{q}^{(-\lambda)},\cdots)\,,

(140)

can be used to map to an amplitude where the negative-helicity fermion is the antiquark. Cyclicity of the amplitude means the first argument can always be set to be the antiquark. Finally, the reflection identity,
eq.

43
, can be used to bring the quark into either the second or third entry.

A supersymmetric decomposition analogous to
eq.

63
can be used to organise one-loop amplitudes with an external quark-antiquark pair. We follow ref.
Bern:1994fz
in relating
g
and
f
amplitudes to a
{\mathcal{N}}{=}1
vector multiplet,

A_{n}^{(1,\ \mathcal{N}=1_{V})}=A_{n}^{(1,\,g_{L})}+A_{n}^{(1,\,g_{R})}+A_{n}^{(1,\,f_{L})}+A_{n}^{(1,\,f_{R})}\,.

(141)

The left-hand side of this equation can be immediately obtained from the corresponding pure-gluon amplitude via a supersymmetric Ward identity, reducing the number of independent amplitudes which we need to analyse. In
section

D.2.2
we explain how a supersymmetric Ward identity leads to the equivalence of the one-loop corrections to
ggg^{*}
and
{\bar{q}}qg^{*}
vertices in
\mathcal{N}=1_{V}
,
eq.

294
. The same reasoning applies here for five-parton amplitudes in the NMRK limit. For example, from the supersymmetric Ward identity

a_{5}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{V})}(2_{\bar{q}},3_{q},4,5,1)=a_{5}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{V})}(2,3,4,5,1)\,,

(142)

it follows that

c_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{V})}(p_{2}^{\lambda_{2}},p_{3}^{\lambda_{3}},p_{4}^{\lambda_{4}},q_{2})=c_{gggg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{V})}(p_{2}^{\lambda_{2}},p_{3}^{\lambda_{3}},p_{4}^{\lambda_{4}},q_{2})\,,

(143)

with analogous relations for different orderings of the external particle species.

For each configuration listed above, explicit amplitudes are provided in ref.
Bern:1994fz
for particle content
\mathcal{N}=1_{V}
,
g_{L}
,
{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L}
and
s_{L}
.
We remind the reader that the
{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{R}
and
s_{R}
amplitudes vanish for these configurations according to
eq.

44a
and
eq.

44b
. The fermion contribution can be obtained via the decomposition

A_{n}^{(1,\,f_{L})}=A_{n}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}-A_{n}^{(1,\,{s}_{L})}\,,

(144)

and the
g_{R}
contribution can be subsequently obtained via
eq.

141
, i.e.

A_{n}^{(1,\,g_{R})}=A_{n}^{(1,\ \mathcal{N}=1_{V})}-A_{n}^{(1,\,g_{L})}-A_{n}^{(1,f_{L})}\,.

(145)

While such organisational principles have been long used in the study of one-loop amplitudes, this work is the first time extensive use has been made these principles to organise factorised building blocks in a high-energy limit. As such, we needed to re-analyse the Regge limit of one-loop
{\bar{q}}qgg
amplitudes in this framework, which is included in
appendix

D
.
We find that a separate treatment of
m_{L}
and
m_{R}
primitive amplitudes, already useful for one-loop amplitudes, becomes even more useful when studying one-loop corrections. This is because requiring the external fermion line to pass to the left or right of the loop essentially limits the loop corrections to affect certain building blocks. We see this already in
eq.

305
, where
g_{R}
amplitudes in the Regge limit only contribute to the
{\bar{q}}qg^{*}
vertex, in line with our intuition.

The organisation of this section is similar to
section

4
, where we first categorise the peripheral-emission vertices by internal particle content, then by helicity configuration, where a minimal set of primitive vertices is given by the three cyclic orderings

\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{*}\}\,,\qquad\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},q^{\oplus},g^{*}\}\,,\qquad\{g^{\oplus},{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{*}\}\,.

6.1
\mathcal{N}=4
contribution

Although in practice we use a
\mathcal{N}=1_{V}
multiplet decomposition,
eq.

145
, to organise the amplitudes and vertices, it is worthwhile to analyse the amplitudes with a circulating
\mathcal{N}=4
multiplet for two reasons. Firstly, ref.
Bern:1994fz
writes the
g_{L}
and
\mathcal{N}=1_{V}
amplitudes in terms of the function
eq.

69
, and this proves convenient also for the factorised expressions. Secondly, it provides us with the simplest context to study the key difference between the pure-gluon case and the case with an external quark-antiquark pair.

Recall the discussion of
eq.

69
in
section

4.1.1
. To be concrete, let us study the physical scattering

q(p_{2})\,g(p_{1})\to q(p_{3})\,g(p_{4})\,g(p_{5}).

The treatment of the ordering
\{p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2}\}
proceeds similarly to the pure gluon case, and by a SUSY Ward identity we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{Re}\left[a_{5}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(2_{\bar{q}},3_{g},4,5,1)\right]\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}&c_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})\\
+&r_{g^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(q_{2};p_{4}^{+}p_{5}^{-})+c_{g^{*}gg}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(-q_{2},p_{5},p_{1})\end{split}

(146)

with primitive vertex

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})=&c_{ggg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{2},p_{3},q_{1})+r_{g^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(q_{1};s_{34})\\
+&v^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(t_{1},\frac{s_{34}\,p_{4}^{+}}{(p_{3}^{+}+p_{4}^{+})},t_{2})\,.\end{split}

(147)

Likewise, for the second colour-ordering in
eq.

61

we write

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{Re}\left[a_{5}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(4,2_{\bar{q}},3_{q},5,1)\right]\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}&c_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{4},p_{2},p_{3},q_{2})\\
+&r_{g^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(q_{2};p_{4}^{+}p_{5}^{-})+c_{g^{*}gg}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(-q_{2},p_{5},p_{1})\,,\end{split}

(148)

with primitive vertex

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{4},p_{2},p_{3},q_{2})=&c_{ggg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{3},p_{2},q_{1})+r_{g^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(q_{1};s_{24})\\
+&v^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(t_{1},\frac{s_{24}\,p_{4}^{+}}{(-p_{2}^{+}+p_{4}^{+})},t_{2})\,.\end{split}

(149)

In this form, it is transparent that the correct large logarithms are generated by the
{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}
and
g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}
vertices in the MRK limit. This follows from the
U(1)
-decoupling identities, e.g.

C^{(0)}_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\ominus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})+C^{(0)}_{{\bar{q}}gqg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\ominus},q_{2})+C^{(0)}_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{4}^{\oplus},p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\ominus},q_{2})=0\,,

(150)

which in the MRK limit becomes

C^{(0)}_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\ominus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})=-C^{(0)}_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{4}^{\oplus},p_{2}^{\oplus},p_{3}^{\ominus},q_{2})\,,

(151)

due to the power-suppression of the second term in
eq.

150
. The one-loop corrections,
eqs.

147
and
149
on the other hand become equal in the MRK limit, due to the limiting behaviour

\frac{s_{24}\,p_{4}^{+}}{(-p_{2}^{+}+p_{4}^{+})}\underset{{\mathrm{MRK}}}{=}\frac{s_{34}\,p_{4}^{+}}{(p_{3}^{+}+p_{4}^{+})}\,.

(152)

Thus the kinematic coefficients of the first two colour-structures in
eq.

61
are equal and opposite, as required by compatibility with
eq.

23
.

We also need to consider the
\delta\Delta
coefficient,
eq.

62
. This must be free from large logarithms in the NMRK and must vanish in the MRK limit. To verify the former condition, we can write, (merely for convenience to separate out a large logarithmic term and a backward
g^{*}gg
vertex)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{Re}\left[a_{5}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(2_{\bar{q}},4,3_{q},5,1)\right]\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}&c_{{\bar{q}}gqg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{2},p_{4},p_{3},q_{2})\\
+&r_{g^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(q_{2};p_{3}^{+}p_{5}^{-})+c_{g^{*}gg}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(-q_{2},p_{5},p_{1})\,,\end{split}

(153)

with vertex

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{{\bar{q}}gqg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{2},p_{4},p_{3},q_{2})=&c_{ggg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(p_{2},p_{4},q_{1}^{\prime})+r_{g^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(q_{1}^{\prime};s_{43})\\
+&v^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}(t_{1}^{\prime},\frac{s_{43}\,p_{3}^{+}}{(p_{4}^{+}+p_{3}^{+})},t_{2})\,.\end{split}

(154)

All large logarithmic behaviour in the
m{=}g
amplitudes can be conveniently written in terms of the
\mathcal{N}=4
function
eq.

69
, and only the
m{=}g_{L}
amplitudes have large logarithms in the NMRK limit. Therefore the LL behaviour of
eq.

62
in the NMRK limit coincides with that of

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathrm{Disp}\left[{A^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}_{\delta\Delta}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},p_{5},p_{1})}\right]}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}C^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}_{\delta\Delta}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})R_{g^{*}}^{(0)}(q_{2})C^{(0)}_{g^{*}gg}(-q_{2},p_{5},p_{1})\,.\end{split}

(155)

Here we have defined the partial vertex

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}_{\delta\Delta}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})=\\
&\quad C^{(0)}_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})\\
&\quad\times\bigg(c^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},q_{2})-c^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}_{{\bar{q}}gqg^{*}}(p_{2},p_{4},p_{3},q_{2})+r^{(1,\,g)}_{g^{*}}(q_{2},p_{4}^{+}p_{5}^{-})-r^{(1,\,g)}_{g^{*}}(q_{2},p_{3}^{+}p_{5}^{-})\bigg)\\
&\quad+C^{(0)}_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{4},p_{2},p_{3},q_{2})\\
&\quad\times\bigg(c^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{4},p_{2},p_{3},q_{2})-c^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=4)}_{{\bar{q}}gqg^{*}}(p_{2},p_{4},p_{3},q_{2})+r^{(1,\,g)}_{g^{*}}(q_{2},p_{4}^{+}p_{5}^{-})-r^{(1,\,g)}_{g^{*}}(q_{2},p_{3}^{+}p_{5}^{-})\bigg)\,.\end{split}

(156)

We note that in
eq.

155
, all dependence on the one-loop corrections to the backward
g^{*}ggg
vertex cancels, so that this partial amplitude only contributes to the forward physics. We note that this partial vertex is free from large logarithms, because the difference of Reggeisation factors

r^{(1,\,g)}_{g^{*}}(q_{2},p_{4}^{+}p_{5}^{-})-r^{(1,\,g)}_{g^{*}}(q_{2},p_{3}^{+}p_{5}^{-})\,,

depends only on the ratio
X=p_{3}^{+}/p_{4}^{+}
which is finite in the NMRK limit. Finally, we note that due to
eq.

151
and
eq.

152
, the entire partial vertex vanishes in the MRK limit as expected.

6.2
\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}
contribution

In this section, we repeat the analysis of
section

4.2
but for amplitudes with an external quark-antiquark pair.

6.2.1
\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{*}\}

The amplitudes we can use to derive these vertices have a very simple form, for example,

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{5}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},4^{\oplus},5^{\oplus},1^{\ominus})=&-\frac{(X+z)(Y({\bar{z}}-1)-1)}{X+XY|z-1|^{2}+|z|^{2}}L_{0}\left(\frac{-s_{23}}{-s_{51}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(157)

This amplitude is finite in
\epsilon
, and has the simple NMRK limit

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{5}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},4^{\oplus},5^{\oplus},1^{\ominus})\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}&-\frac{(X+z)}{X(z-1)}L_{0}\left(\frac{-s_{23}}{-s_{51}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(158)

Unlike the amplitudes in
section

4.2
, the form of this amplitude is not suggestive of a separation into central and peripheral parts. To define a
{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}
vertex, we must therefore massage the amplitude into such a form. We use
eq.

302
to add and subtract

c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{1})+c_{g^{*}gg}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi})}(-q_{2},p_{5}^{\ominus},p_{1}^{\oplus})=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{-s_{23}}{-s_{51}}\right)\,.

(159)

This brings the amplitude into a more familiar form

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{5}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},4^{\oplus},5^{\ominus},1^{\oplus})\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}&c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{1})\\
+&\frac{X(1+z-{\bar{z}})-z({\bar{z}}-2)}{2X|z-1|^{2}}L_{0}\left(\frac{-s_{23}}{-s_{51}}\right)\\
+&c_{g^{*}gg}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(-q_{2},p_{5}^{\ominus},p_{1}^{\oplus})\,.\end{split}

(160)

Now subtracting the
g^{*}gg
vertex, we are left with the two-parton peripheral-emission vertex

\displaystyle\begin{split}c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})=&c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{1})+\frac{X(1+z-{\bar{z}})-z({\bar{z}}-2)}{2X|z-1|^{2}}L_{0}\left(\frac{-t_{1}}{-t_{2}}\right)\,,\end{split}

(161)

which has the trivial MRK limit

\displaystyle\begin{split}c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})\underset{X\to\infty}{\to}&c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{1})+v_{g^{*}gg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi})}(-q_{1},p_{4},q_{2})\,.\end{split}

(162)

As mentioned previously, we cannot use the
{\mathrm{MRK}^{\prime}}
limit to check this function, as the tree-level amplitude which multiplies it is power-suppressed in this limit.

6.2.2
\{{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},q^{\oplus},g^{*}\}

The derivation of the
{\bar{q}}^{\ominus}g^{\oplus}q^{\oplus}g^{*}
vertex is subtle, because the amplitudes from which we start are zero:

A_{5}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}({\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},q^{\oplus},g^{\oplus},g^{\ominus})=A_{5}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}({\bar{q}}^{\ominus},g^{\oplus},q^{\oplus},g^{\ominus},g^{\oplus})=0\,.

(163)

We emphasise that this identity is not a consequence of
eq.

44b
, which only applies to their
{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{R}
counterparts. However, we should proceed as in all previous examples, and define a
c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}gqg^{*}}
vertex by subtracting the known
c^{{}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}})}}_{g^{*}gg}
vertex. This is the same logic we followed in
section

D.2.2
, which led us to re-derive the known one-loop
{\bar{q}}qg^{*}
vertex in QCD,
eq.

314
. In the present case we obtain

\displaystyle c_{{\bar{q}}gqg^{*}}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},p_{4}^{\oplus},q_{2})

\displaystyle=-c_{g^{*}gg}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}})}(-q_{2},p_{5}^{\lambda_{5}},p_{1}^{\lambda_{1}})

(164)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{-s_{234}}\right)^{\epsilon}-1\,,

(165)

where in the last line we have made use of the equality
s_{51}=s_{234}
. We note that the tree-level pre-factor of this one-loop function vanishes in both the MRK and
{\mathrm{MRK}^{\prime}}
limits.

6.2.3
\{g^{\oplus},{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},q^{\oplus},g^{*}\}

As in
section

6.2.1
, the NMRK limits of the relevant amplitudes are again very simple, for example

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{5}^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},5^{\ominus},1^{\oplus},4^{\oplus})\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}&\frac{1}{(z-1)}L_{0}\left(\frac{-s_{23}}{-s_{51}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(166)

We remark that this is simply
X/(X+z)
times the amplitude in
eq.

158
.
Recalling the NMRK behaviour of the tree level amplitudes,

\displaystyle\frac{A^{(0)}(4^{\oplus},2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},5^{\ominus},1^{\oplus})}{A^{(0)}(2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},4^{\oplus},5^{\ominus},1^{\oplus})}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}\frac{X}{X+z}

(167)

we see that

\displaystyle A^{(1,\ {\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(4^{\oplus},2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},5^{\ominus},1^{\oplus})+A^{(1,\ {\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}(2_{\bar{q}}^{\ominus},3_{q}^{\oplus},4^{\oplus},5^{\ominus},1^{\oplus})\underset{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{NMRK}}}{\to}0\,,

(168)

which is reminiscent of the identity
eq.

256
, which followed from Furry’s theorem, but for an off-shell gluon with momentum
p_{5}+p_{1}
.

The vertex resulting from
eq.

166
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{4}^{\oplus},p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{2})=&c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{1})+\frac{X(1+z-{\bar{z}})+|z|^{2}}{2X|z-1|^{2}}L_{0}\left(\frac{-t_{1}}{-t_{2}}\right)\,,\end{split}

(169)

which has the transparent MRK limit

\displaystyle\begin{split}c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{g{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{4}^{\oplus},p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{2})\underset{X\to\infty}{\to}&c^{(1,\,{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L})}_{{\bar{q}}qg^{*}}(p_{2}^{\ominus},p_{3}^{\oplus},q_{1})+v_{g^{*}gg^{*}}^{(1,\,\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi})}(-q_{1},p_{4},q_{2})\,.\end{split}

(170)

Once again, the tree-level coefficient of
eq.

169
is power supressed in the
{\mathrm{MRK}^{\prime}}
limit. This concludes our extraction of the necessary two-parton emission vertices with a circulating
{\mathcal{N}=1_{\chi}}_{L}
multiplet.

6.3
Complex scalar contribution

The procedure to obtain these vertices proceeds similarly to
section

6.2
and we simply collect the primitive vertices here.
The
{\bar{q}}^{\ominus}q^{\oplus}g^{\oplus}g^{*}

8
Conclusions

In this paper we have analysed the dispersive parts of the one-loop
ggggg
and
{\bar{q}}qggg
ampltiudes in the NMRK limit
eq.

212
. The final results are presented in a compact notation in
eq.

133
and
eq.

192
respectively. In both cases, to leading power, the amplitudes comprise solely of the exchange of a colour octet in the
t_{2}
channel.
Equation

192
is compatible with an all-orders factorisation of the form
eq.

29
, which defines the colour dressed one-loop
{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}
vertex
eq.

194
. The pure-gluon case is not quite as simple
eq.

133
, but the one-loop amplitude is compatible with an analogous factorisation of each colour structure, one such possibility being given in
eq.

134
. We have defined a colour-dressed one-loop
gggg^{*}
vertex for each colour structure in
eq.

129
and
eq.

132
.

As reviewed in the introduction, there has been much progress towards extending the BFKL approach to NNLL accuracy. In order to make predictions for jet cross sections at this accuracy, it is necessary to compute the jet impact factors to NNLO. The vertices extracted in this paper are necessary ingredients for the real-virtual contribution to the NNLO impact factors. The new totally symmetric colour structures appearing in the one-loop
gggg^{*}
vertex do not survive interference with the tree-level amplitude and therefore are not relevant for the impact factors. The same is not true for the
{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}
vertex, where the new one-loop colour structure in
eq.

189
has non-zero interference with the tree-level colour structures.

The analysis in this paper was performed to
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{0})
. To obtain the NNLL impact factor for the inclusive production of a jet it will be necessary to obtain the one-loop two-parton emission vertices to higher powers of
\epsilon
in IR singular limits, up to
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{2})
in the soft-collinear limit. The vertices presented here have a more immediate phenomenological application if the two peripheral partons are required to form separate jets.

The present work also sets some groundwork for the extraction of the one-loop two-parton central emission vertices in QCD, which are necessary ingredients for extending the BFKL approach to NNLL accuracy. The colour structure of the
g^{*}ggg^{*}
and
g^{*}{\bar{q}}qg^{*}
central-emission vertices is expected to be the same as the
gggg^{*}
and
{\bar{q}}qgg^{*}
peripheral-emission vertices presented here.

While this analysis of one-loop amplitudes allows for the extraction of one-loop vertices which themselves factorise into known quantities in the further MRK limit, a one-loop analysis cannot provide information on the logarithmic corrections these vertices may receive at higher orders. A particularly interesting question is whether the new colour structures encountered in this one-loop analysis recieve large logarithmic corrections at the next loop order, and if so, whether these large logarithms are simply governed by the one-loop Regge trajectory. The results of this paper therefore provide strong motivation to perform a similar investigation of the NMRK limit of the recently computed two-loop five parton amplitudes.

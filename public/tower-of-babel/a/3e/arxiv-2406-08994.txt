Asymptotic Stability and Strict Passivity of Port-Hamiltonian Descriptor Systems via State Feedback
Delin Chu, Volker Mehrmann

Abstract
While port-Hamiltonian descriptor systems are known to be stable and passive, they may not be asymptotically stable or strictly passive. Necessary and sufficient conditions are presented when these properties as well as the regularity and the index one property can be achieved via state feedback while preserving the port-Hamiltonian structure.



Keywords:
Port-Hamiltonian descriptor system, proportional state feedback, regularity, index reduction, asymptotic stability, strict passivity.

AMS subject classification:
93B05, 93B40, 93B52, 65F35

Introduction

In this paper we consider linear time-invariant port-Hamiltonian (pH) descriptor systems of the form

\displaystyle E{\dot{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(J-R)x+(G-P)u,

(1)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(G+P)^{T}x+(S+N)u,

where
E,J,R\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,

,

N=-N^{T}=\frac{1}{2}(D-D^{T})

are the state, output, and input of the system. The coefficient matrices satisfy

E=E^{T}\geq 0,\ \left[\begin{array}[]{cc}J&G\\
-G^{T}&N\end{array}\right]=-\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}J&G\\
-G^{T}&N\end{array}\right]^{T},

W=W^{T}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}R&P\\
P^{T}&S\end{array}\right]\geq 0,

(2)

where for a matrix

M\geq 0

(M>0)
denotes that
M
is positive semi-definite (definite) and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
denotes the real

matrices.

Note that there are different representations of pH descriptor systems, some include an extra term

in front of the state
x

1

several publications, see
[
11

14
,
15

how the system can be transformed to remove this term, so in the following we use the form (

The class of pH descriptor systems represents an ideal modeling framework for control systems in almost all real world physical systems. While ordinary pH systems are well studied see e. g.
[
8
,
17
,
19
]
, the study of pH descriptor systems has only recently received a lot of attention, see
[
2

13
,
15
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
for a detailed discussion of the many important properties and a large number of applications.
These properties include the stability and passivity of the system. The extension of classical analytical definition of stability for ordinary differential equations, see e. g.
[
7
]
to descriptor systems is well-known, see e. g.
[
10
,
14
]
. The difficulty in this extension is that either the set of perturbations in the initial conditions has to be restricted or the class of solution functions has to be enriched to deal with inconsistent initial conditions, see
[
10
]
for a detailed analysis. For the case of linear time-invariant descriptor systems, however, it is a well established fact, see e. g.
[
6
]
, that the stability analysis can be carried via the eigenstructure of the associated matrix pencil which in our case is the
dissipative Hamiltonian matrix pencil

sE-(J-R)
.

A characteristic property of systems of the form (
1
) is the availability of a
storage function (Hamiltonian)
which is given by
\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Ex\geq 0
and that the following power balance equation holds, as well as the resulting dissipation inequality,
see e. g.
[

Theorem 1
.

Consider a pH descriptor system of the form (
1

power balance equation

{\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}(x)}=-\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\end{bmatrix}^{T}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}R&P\\
P^{T}&S\end{array}\right]\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\end{bmatrix}+y^{T}u

(3)

holds along any solution

.
In particular, the
dissipation inequality

\mathcal{H}(x(t_{2}))-\mathcal{H}(x(t_{1}))\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(y(\tau)^{T}u(\tau))\ d\tau

(4)

holds.

The dissipation inequality (
4
) immediately implies that the system is
passive
, see
[
3

23
]
, but since the inequality in (

Similarly, it is well known, see e. g.
[

, that pH descriptor systems of the form (

stable
, in the sense that all finite eigenvalues of

are in the closed left half plane and those on the imaginary axis are semi-simple. But since purely imaginary eigenvalues may occur, pH descriptor systems are not necessarily
asymptotically stable
.

Furthermore, it is shown in
[
11
]
that the

of a pH descriptor system, i. e. the size of the largest Jordan block associated with the eigenvalue
\infty

sE_{(}J-R)
, can be at most
2
, and it is shown in
[
12
]
that the

of the pencil

, i. e. that

\det(sE-(J-R))
is identically

E,J,R
.

It is well known
[
9
]
that neither (asymptotic) stability nor
(strict) passivity and also neither the regularity and the index of a descriptor system are invariant under feedback. On the contrary, typically feedback is used to make an unstable or non-passive system asymptotically stable, respectively strictly passive and to make a descriptor system regular and of index at most one,
[
5
]
.

In
[
4
]
the difficult question of obtaining a regular, index at most one, and asymptotically stable closed pH system via output feedback has been discussed and a complete characterization has been given. Typically, however, one can achieve a lot better results when one uses state feedback, because for the changes in the system pencil one has a larger class available. On the other hand, state feedback will usually destroy the pH structure.

The goal of this paper, is therefore, to study the question whether a pH descriptor system can be made regular, of index at most one, and furthermore strictly passive, respectively asymptotically stable, by applying a state feedback
u=Fx+v
with
F\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
to (
1
) while preserving the pH structure.

Applying such a state feedback, the closed-loop system has the form

\displaystyle E\dot{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[J-R+(G-P)F]x+(G-P)v=(\tilde{J}-\tilde{R})x+(\tilde{G}-\tilde{P})v,

(5)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(G^{T}+P^{T}+(S+N)F)x+(S+N)u=(\tilde{G}+\tilde{P})^{T}x+(S+N)v,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J+{1\over 2}[(G-P)F-F^{T}(G-P)^{T}],

\displaystyle\tilde{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R-{1\over 2}[(G-P)F+F^{T}(G-P)^{T}],

\displaystyle\tilde{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G+{1\over 2}F^{T}(S+N)^{T},\ \tilde{P}=P+{1\over 2}F^{T}(S+N)^{T}.

We present a complete characterization for the following two questions.

Question 1
:
When does there exist a state feedback matrix
F
such that the closed-loop system (
5
) associated with (
1
) is port-Hamiltonian, regular, of index at most one, and asymptotically stable.

Question 2
: When does there exists a state feedback matrix
F
such that the closed-loop system (
5
) associated with (
1
) is port-Hamiltonian, regular, of index at most one, and strictly passive, i. e., the dissipation matrix of the closed loop system satisfies

\tilde{W}=\tilde{W}^{T}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\tilde{R}&\tilde{P}\\
\tilde{P}^{T}&S\end{array}\right]>0.

(6)

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present some preliminary results. In Section
3
we present the answers to the two questions
and we finish with some conclusions and open problems in Section
4

4
Conclusions and Future Work

We have presented a complete characterization when a pH descriptor system (
1
) can be made asymptotically stable and strictly passive, as well as regular and of index at most one via state feedback while preserving the pH structure.

A complete characterization when a pH descriptor system (
1
) with
D=0
can be made regular, of index at most one and asymptotically stable by output feedback while preserving the pH structure has been presented in
[
4
]
, while the question how to achieve a regular, index at most one and asymptotically stable or strictly passive system via output feedback for a pH descriptor system (
1
) with feedthrough term
D\not=0
is still an open problem.

An interesting question that also remains to be answered is to use the discussed state feedback to obtain a representation that maximizes the distance to instability, respectively non-passivity. For both questions only partial solutions are available, see
[
14
]
and the references therein.

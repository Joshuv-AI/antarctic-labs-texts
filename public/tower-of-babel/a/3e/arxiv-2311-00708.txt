Abstract
We present a new method to determine the best constant of the Sobolev-type embedding in one dimension with a norm including a bounded inhomogeneous potential term. This problem is closely connected to the Green function of the Schrödinger operator with inhomogeneous potential. A minimization problem of a Rayleigh-type quotient in a Sobolev space gives the best constant of the Sobolev embedding. We decompose the minimization problem into two sub-minimization problems and show that the Green function provides the minimizer of the first minimization problem. Then, it enables us to derive a new precise estimate of the best constant and function for inhomogeneous bounded potential cases. As applications, we give some examples of the inhomogeneous potential whose best constant and function of the Sobolev-type embedding are explicitly determined.

Introduction

In this paper,
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
and
W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}})

denote the real-valued Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, and we set
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})=W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}})
.
For every
1\leq p\leq\infty
and every function
u\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}})
,
it is well known to hold the following Sobolev-type inequality:

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}})},

(1.1)

where
\|u\|_{\infty}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}
, and

C=C(p)>0
is a constant independent of
u
;
see
[
1
, Theorem 8.8]
for example.
Indeed,
we can choose
C(p)=p^{1/p}\leq e^{1/e}
from an elementary calculation.
We consider a generalization for the case of
p=2
and
discuss the corresponding best constant.
The Sobolev-type inequality of the form

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}
has
been intensively studied even in higher dimensions with
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

under various settings in connection with applications in PDE problems,
for example,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
]
.
In particular, the best constant
for the case with an inhomogeneous potential is closely connected
with the corresponding Schrödinger operator.
However, there has not been much precise analysis of
the best constant of the Sobolev-type inequality when
there is a general inhomogeneous potential term.

Let
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}V(x)>0

for simplicity. We introduce a norm on
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})=W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}})
such as

\|u\|_{V}^{2}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2})\,dx,

which gives an equivalent norm to the usual
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}})}

from the assumptions on
V(x)
.
We define a Rayleigh-type quotient

R(u;V):=\frac{\|u\|_{V}^{2}}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}},

(1.2)

and consider a minimization problem of
R(u;V)
for
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
More precisely, we define

\displaystyle m(V):=\inf_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\not{\equiv}0}R(u;V),

(1.3)

\displaystyle M(V):=\left\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}});~m(V)=R(u;V),~u\not{\hskip-2.15277pt\equiv}0\right\}.

(1.4)

We note that
m(V)^{-1/2}
gives the best constant of the following
Sobolev-type inequality

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\|u\|_{V},

(1.5)

which is similar to (
1.1
).
In this paper, we call
m(V)^{-1/2}
the Sobolev best constant of
the inequality (
1.5
).

Contrary to the precise and detailed analysis of the Sobolev best constant
and function for the homogeneous case
V\equiv const.
, it is not easy to get the precise
value of the Sobolev best constant and profile of the minimizer, which achieves
the best constant for inhomogeneous potentials.
However, the significance of inhomogeneous potential cases
is increasing in connection with various applications.
In this research, we focus on one-dimensional and
inhomogeneous potential cases. This paper aims to develop
a new two-step minimization approach and establish
new criteria to determine the precise value of the Sobolev
best constant and function for (
1.5
).
We clarify the influence of the potential

V
to
m(V)
and the elements of
M(V)
,
and construct concrete examples using the obtained new criteria.

The main idea that we employed in our analysis is the reduction of
the minimization problem in the whole
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
to two-step minimizations
in
K_{a}
and in
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where
K_{a}:=\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}});~u(a)=\|u\|_{\infty}=1\}
.
In the first minimization in
K_{a}
, the unique minimizer
u_{a}

coincides with the Green function for the Schrödinger operator

-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V(x)
with the potential function
V(x)
.
This fact closely links the Sobolev best constant problem to the estimate of the Green function
and gives one of our strong motivations. In the second minimization
step among the parameter
a
, we give a necessary and sufficient condition
for the global minimizer regarding the
fundamental solutions of the linear ODE
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}u+V(x)u=0
.

The outline of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we
decompose the minimization problem (
1.3
) to the two sub-minimization problems
and prove the equivalency between the original minimization
problem and our two-step minimization problem.
In Section
3
,
we first establish precise properties
of the minimizer of the first minimization problem in
K_{a}

and a relation to the Green function.
Moreover, by extending the right and left tails of the minimizer,
we construct the fundamental solutions of
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}u+V(x)u=0

and give upper and lower estimates for them.
Section
4
is devoted to the second minimization step.
In Theorem
4.1
, we establish some useful variational formulae for the minimum value
obtained in the first minimization step.
Section
5
establishes our main theorem, which
gives three new necessary and sufficient conditions
for the local minimality in the second minimization step.
As an application,
we construct a nontrivial example of the bounded inhomogeneous potential
for which the Sobolev best constant and function are explicitly specified.

Two-step minimization approach

Throughout of this paper, we suppose

\displaystyle V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad 0<v_{0}\leq V(x)\leq v_{1}~\mbox{a.e.}~x\in{\mathbb{R}},

(2.1)

for some constants
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
.
Then, for
u,~v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, we define

\displaystyle(u,v)_{V}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{\prime}(x)v^{\prime}(x)+V(x)u(x)v(x)\right)\,dx,\quad\|u\|_{V}:=(u,u)_{V}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad I(u;V):=\|u\|_{V}^{2},

where we abbreviate
\frac{du}{dx}
as
u^{\prime}
.
We remark that
(u,v)_{V}
defines an inner product on
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
the corresponding norm
\|u\|_{V}
is equivalent to the norm
of
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

In this paper, without loss of generality,
we suppose that
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
(or more generally,
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})

for
p\in[1,\infty]
) always satisfies
u\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
, since
an element of the function space
W^{1,p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
has a continuous representation
(Theorem 8.8 of
[
1
]
). We also remark that

u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfies
u\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0
(Theorem 8.8 and Corollary 8.9 of
[
1
]
).

For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we set

K_{a}:=\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}});~u(a)=\|u\|_{\infty}=1\},

and define

F(a):=\inf_{u\in K_{a}}I(u;V).

The following proposition establishes a decomposition principle
for the minimization problem (
1.3
).
It gives a foundation for the two-step minimization method
and enables us to capture the detail of the inhomogeneous potential
V
.

Proposition 2.1

(decomposition principle)
.

Let
V
satisfy (
2.1
) and set
m(V)
as (
1.3
). Then, we have

\displaystyle m(V)=\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(a).

(2.2)

Proof.

We define
\tilde{m}(V):=\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(a)
.
Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a minimizing sequence to the infimum of (
1.3
).
Without loss of generality, we can assume
u_{n}\in K_{a_{n}}
for some
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}

and

m(V)=\lim_{n\to\infty}I(u_{n};V)
.
Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
in
\tilde{m}(V)\leq F(a_{n})\leq I(u_{n};V)
,
we obtain
\tilde{m}(V)\leq m(V)
.

On the other hand, we choose
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{R}}

and
\{u_{n,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset K_{a_{n}}
such that

\tilde{m}(V)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F(a_{n}),\quad F(a_{n})=\lim_{k\to\infty}I(u_{n,k};V).

Since
m(V)\leq I(u_{n,k};V)
, taking the limit as
k\to\infty
, we have

m(V)\leq F(a_{n})
. Then, taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we obtain
m(V)\leq\tilde{m}(V)
.
∎

Furthermore, they satisfy the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{v_{0}}{v_{1}}}e^{-\max(\sqrt{v_{0}}x,\sqrt{v_{1}}x)}\leq\varphi_{+}(x)\leq\sqrt{\frac{v_{1}}{v_{0}}}e^{-\min(\sqrt{v_{0}}x,\sqrt{v_{1}}x)}\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{v_{0}}{v_{1}}}e^{\min(\sqrt{v_{0}}x,\sqrt{v_{1}}x)}\leq\varphi_{-}(x)\leq\sqrt{\frac{v_{1}}{v_{0}}}e^{\max(\sqrt{v_{0}}x,\sqrt{v_{1}}x)}\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}).

(3.14)

Moreover,

\displaystyle u_{a}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\varphi_{-}(x)}{\varphi_{-}(a)}\qquad(x<a)}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\varphi_{+}(x)}{\varphi_{+}(a)}\qquad(x\geq a)}\end{cases}

(3.15)

holds for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

We set
m_{\pm}:=u_{0}^{\prime}(0_{\pm})
.
We consider the following initial value problem of linear
ordinary differential equation:

\begin{cases}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)=V(x)\varphi(x)\quad\mbox{a.e.}~x\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
\varphi(0)=1,~\varphi^{\prime}(0)=m_{\pm}.\end{cases}

(3.16)

Since the coefficient
V
may not be continuous,
we consider a mild solution for (
3.16
):

\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad\varphi(0)=1,\quad\varphi^{\prime}(x)=m_{\pm}+\int_{0}^{x}V(t)\varphi(t)\,dt~~(x\in{\mathbb{R}}).

(3.17)

It is well known that there exists a unique mild solution
\varphi_{\pm}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})

of (
3.17
) as it is a linear ODE with bounded coefficient
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Moreover, from (
3.17
),
\varphi_{\pm}\in W^{2,\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
also holds.

We can show that the function defined by the right-hand side of (
3.15
) satisfies the
condition (
3.6
) for all
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, similar to the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
So, we conclude (
3.15
). Then, from the estimate (
3.4
), we find that

\varphi_{\pm}
satisfy the initial value problems of (
3.12
).

Next, we prove the estimate (
3.14
). Since
\varphi_{-}(x)=u_{0}(x)
for
x\leq 0
,
(
3.14
) for
x\leq 0
follows from (
3.4
).
For
x>0
, we first remark that
\varphi_{-}(x)>0
holds from (
3.17
).
If
\varphi_{-}(x_{0})=0
and
\varphi_{-}(x)>0
for
0<x<x_{0}
,
then there should be an
x_{1}\in(0,x_{0})
such that
\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x_{1})<0
,
but it is impossible from (
3.17
).
We define
y(x):=\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{0}}\varphi_{-}(x)
for
x\geq 0
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(x)=\varphi_{-}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{0}}\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)=\sqrt{v_{0}}\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+V(x)\varphi_{-}(x)\geq\sqrt{v_{0}}\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+v_{0}\varphi_{-}(x)=\sqrt{v_{0}}y(x).

Solving this differential inequality, we obtain

y(x)\geq y(0)e^{\sqrt{v_{0}}x}
. Since
y(0)=m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{0}}
,
this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{0}}\varphi_{-}(x)\geq(m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{0}})e^{\sqrt{v_{0}}x}.

We solve this differential inequality as follows:

\displaystyle\left(e^{\sqrt{v_{0}}x}\varphi_{-}(x)\right)^{\prime}=e^{\sqrt{v_{0}}x}\left(\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{0}}\varphi_{-}(x)\right)\geq(m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{0}})e^{2\sqrt{v_{0}}x}=\left(\frac{m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{0}}}{2\sqrt{v_{0}}}e^{2\sqrt{v_{0}}x}\right)^{\prime}.

Integrating the above inequality on the interval
(0,x)
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{v_{0}}x}\varphi_{-}(x)-1\geq\frac{m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{0}}}{2\sqrt{v_{0}}}\left(e^{2\sqrt{v_{0}}x}-1\right)\geq\sqrt{\frac{v_{0}}{v_{1}}}\left(e^{2\sqrt{v_{0}}x}-1\right),

(3.18)

where the last inequality follows from
m_{-}\geq v_{0}/\sqrt{v_{1}}
by (
3.5
).
From (
3.18
), we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}(x)\geq\sqrt{\frac{v_{0}}{v_{1}}}e^{\sqrt{v_{0}}x}+\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{v_{0}}{v_{1}}}\right)e^{-\sqrt{v_{0}}x}\geq\sqrt{\frac{v_{0}}{v_{1}}}e^{\sqrt{v_{0}}x},

which gives the lower bound estimate in (
3.14
) for
x>0
.

For the upper bound estimate in (
3.14
),
we define

y(x):=\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{1}}\varphi_{-}(x)
for
x\geq 0
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(x)=\varphi_{-}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{1}}\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)=\sqrt{v_{1}}\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+V(x)\varphi_{-}(x)\leq\sqrt{v_{1}}\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+v_{1}\varphi_{-}(x)=\sqrt{v_{1}}y(x).

Solving this differential inequality, we obtain

y(x)\leq y(0)e^{\sqrt{v_{1}}x}
. Since
y(0)=m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{1}}
,
this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{1}}\varphi_{-}(x)\leq(m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{1}})e^{\sqrt{v_{1}}x}.

We solve this differential inequality as follows:

\displaystyle\left(e^{\sqrt{v_{1}}x}\varphi_{-}(x)\right)^{\prime}=e^{\sqrt{v_{1}}x}\left(\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{v_{1}}\varphi_{-}(x)\right)\leq(m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{1}})e^{2\sqrt{v_{1}}x}=\left(\frac{m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{1}}}{2\sqrt{v_{1}}}e^{2\sqrt{v_{1}}x}\right)^{\prime}.

Integrating the above inequality on the interval
(0,x)
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{v_{1}}x}\varphi_{-}(x)-1\leq\frac{m_{-}+\sqrt{v_{1}}}{2\sqrt{v_{1}}}\left(e^{2\sqrt{v_{1}}x}-1\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{v_{1}}{v_{0}}}\left(e^{2\sqrt{v_{1}}x}-1\right),

(3.19)

where the last inequlity follows from
m_{-}\leq v_{1}/\sqrt{v_{0}}
by (
3.5
).
From (
3.19
), we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}(x)\leq\sqrt{\frac{v_{1}}{v_{0}}}e^{\sqrt{v_{1}}x}+\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{v_{1}}{v_{0}}}\right)e^{-\sqrt{v_{1}}x}\leq\sqrt{\frac{v_{1}}{v_{0}}}e^{\sqrt{v_{1}}x},

which gives the upper bound estimate in (
3.14
) for
x>0
.
The estimate (
3.13
) can be proven smilarly,
and we omit it.

Lastly, we prove the uniqueness of the boundary value problems (
3.12
).
We prove it for
\varphi_{-}
, since we can prove it for
\varphi_{+}
similarly.
Let
\varphi_{-}^{1}
and
\varphi_{-}^{2}
be two solutions of (
3.12
).
Setting
\varphi_{0}:=\varphi_{-}^{1}-\varphi_{-}^{2}
, it satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varphi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)=V(x)\varphi_{0}(x)\qquad(\text{a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}),\\
\varphi_{0}(0)=0,\\
\displaystyle{\lim_{x\to-\infty}\varphi_{0}(x)=0.}\end{cases}

Since a pair of
\varphi_{+}
and
\varphi_{-}
consists a basis
of the solution space of the second order linear ODE
\varphi^{\prime\prime}+V(x)\varphi=0
,
there exist
c_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\varphi_{0}(x)=c_{+}\varphi_{+}(x)+c_{-}\varphi_{-}(x)
holds for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The condition
\lim_{x\to-\infty}\varphi_{0}(x)=0
implies that
c_{+}=0

and
c_{-}=c_{-}\varphi_{-}(0)=\varphi_{0}(0)=0
follows.
Hence, the solution of (
3.12
) is unique.
∎

Second minimization step

Based on (
2.2
), this section considers the second minimization problem

\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(a)
.
The following theorem gives the derivative of the function
F(a)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

We suppose (
2.1
) and define
F(a)
by (
3.1
).
Then,
F\in W^{2,\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
and
the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle F(a)=u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})-u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})\quad(a\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(4.1)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(a)=|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})|^{2}-|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})|^{2}\quad(a\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(a)=2F(a)\left(|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})|^{2}+u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})+|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})|^{2}-V(a)\right)\quad(\mbox{a.e. }a\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(4.3)

where
u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})
and
u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})
are the right and left derivatives of
u_{a}(x)

at
x=a
, respectively.
Moreover, if
V
is continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}
,
F\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
holds.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle F(a)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{a}\left(|u_{a}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{a}^{\infty}\left(|u_{a}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{a}\left(|u_{a}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+u_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)u_{a}(x)\right)dx+\int_{a}^{\infty}\left(|u_{a}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+u_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)u_{a}(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{a}\left(u_{a}(x)u_{a}^{\prime}(x)\right)^{\prime}dx+\int_{a}^{\infty}\left(u_{a}(x)u_{a}^{\prime}(x)\right)^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=\Big[u_{a}(x)u_{a}^{\prime}(x)\Big]_{-\infty}^{a}+\Big[u_{a}(x)u_{a}^{\prime}(x)\Big]_{a}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=u_{a}(a)u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})-u_{a}(a)u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})=u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})-u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+}).

From this formula and (
3.11
), we also obtain
F\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Using Theorem
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{da}\left(u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{\pm})\right)=\frac{d}{da}\left(\frac{\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime}(a)}{\varphi_{\pm}(a)}\right)=\frac{\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(a)\varphi_{\pm}(a)-|\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime}(a)|^{2}}{|\varphi_{\pm}(a)|^{2}}=V(a)-|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{\pm})|^{2},

and (
4.2
) follows.
We also have

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(a)=\left(u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})-u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})\right)\left(u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})+u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})\right)=-F(a)\left(u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})+u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})\right).

This formula implies
F\in W^{2,\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Then, differentiating
F^{\prime}(a)
, we obtain

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=-F^{\prime}(a)\left(u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})+u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})\right)-F(a)\left(2V(a)-|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})|^{2}-|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2F(a)\left(|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})|^{2}+u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{+})u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})+|u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{-})|^{2}-V(a)\right),

(4.4)

for a.e.
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Moreover, if
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
,
F\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
holds from (
3.11
) and (
4.4
).
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the condition (
2.1
), it holds that

\displaystyle 2\frac{v_{0}}{\sqrt{v_{1}}}\leq m(V)\leq 2\frac{v_{1}}{\sqrt{v_{0}}}.

Proof.

From the estimate (
3.5
), we have

v_{0}/\sqrt{v_{1}}\leq\mp u_{a}^{\prime}(a_{\pm})\leq v_{1}/\sqrt{v_{0}}
for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then, from (
4.1
),

2v_{0}/\sqrt{v_{1}}\leq F(a)\leq 2v_{1}/\sqrt{v_{0}}
holds.
Since
m(V)=\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(a)
, we conclude the assertion of the theorem.
∎

When
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
, if
F(x)
has a local minimum at
x=a
, then
the following condition holds:

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(a)=0,\qquad F^{\prime\prime}(a)\geq 0.

(4.5)

So, we define

N(V):=\left\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}\;;\;F^{\prime}(a)=0,F^{\prime\prime}(a)\geq 0\right\}.

Then, as a consequence of Theorem
4.2
, we obtain
the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

We suppose (
2.1
) and
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Then,

\displaystyle m(V)=\min\left(\liminf\limits_{|a|\to\infty}F(a),\inf\limits_{a\in N(V)}F(a)\right),

(4.6)

\displaystyle M(V)=\left\{cu_{a}\;;\;c\in{\mathbb{R}}{\hskip-0.3pt\setminus\hskip-0.3pt}\{0\},~F(a)=m(V)\right\}.

(4.7)

In particular,
M(V)=\emptyset
holds if and only if

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{|a|\to\infty}F(a)<F(b)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad b\in N(V).

(4.8)

Proof.

Since
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
,
F\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
follows from Theorem
4.1
.
Then, (
4.6
) holds from Proposition
2.1
.
We set

\tilde{M}(V):=\left\{cu_{a}\;;\;c\in{\mathbb{R}}{\hskip-0.3pt\setminus\hskip-0.3pt}\{0\},~F(a)=m(V)\right\}.

If
M(V)\neq\emptyset
, for any
u\in M(V)
,
we choose
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|u(a)|=\|u\|_{\infty}

and define
\tilde{u}(x):=u(x)/u(a)
.
Since
\tilde{u}\in K_{a}
, we obtain

m(V)\leq F(a)\leq I(\tilde{u};V)=R(\tilde{u};V)=R(u;V)=m(V).

Hence,
I(\tilde{u};V)=F(a)=m(V)
follows.
From Theorem
3.1
, we have
\tilde{u}=u_{a}
and it implies
M(V)=\tilde{M}(V)
.
It is also obvious that
\tilde{M}(V)=\emptyset
if
M(V)=\emptyset
.
So, we obtain (
4.7
).

When
M(V)=\emptyset
, since
m(V)=\liminf\limits_{|a|\to\infty}F(a)

and
m(V)<F(b)
for all
b\in N(V)
, (
4.8
) holds.
Conversely, (
4.8
) also implies
M(V)=\emptyset
, since

m(V)\leq\liminf\limits_{|a|\to\infty}F(a)
.
∎

We immediately obtain characterizations of
m(V)
and
M(V)
for
the case of constant potential as a corollary of Theorem
4.3
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
V
be a positive constant:

V>0
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle m(V)=2\sqrt{V},~~~~~M(V)=\{cu_{a};~c\in{\mathbb{R}}{\hskip-0.3pt\setminus\hskip-0.3pt}\{0\},~a\in{\mathbb{R}}\},

(4.9)

where
u_{a}(x)=e^{-\sqrt{V}|x-a|}
.

Proof.

Since
v_{0}=v_{1}=V
, from Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.2
,
we obtain
u_{a}(x)=e^{-\sqrt{V}|x-a|}
and
m(V)=2\sqrt{V}
, respectively.
As
F(a)=\|u_{a}\|_{V}^{2}
is constant,
N(V)={\mathbb{R}}
and (
4.9
) follow from
Theorem
4.3
.
∎

We also give characterizations of
m(V)
and
M(V)
for
the case of nondecreasing potential as follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

We suppose (
2.1
) and
V
is a non-decreasing function with

\lim_{x\to-\infty}V(x)=v_{0}>0,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}V(x)=v_{1}.

Then, it holds that

m(V)=\lim_{a\to-\infty}F(a)=2\sqrt{v_{0}}.

Furthermore, if
v_{0}<v_{1}
, then
F
is a strictly increasing function
and
M(V)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

For
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus\{0\}
, since
V(x)\geq v_{0}
, we have

R(u;V)\geq R(u;v_{0})\geq m(v_{0})
.
Taking the infimum of
R(u;V)
with respect to
u
, we obtain
that
m(V)\geq m(v_{0})
.

We put
v_{a}(x):=e^{-\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}
for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
We also obtain

\displaystyle m(v_{0})

\displaystyle\leq m(V)\leq F(a)\leq I(v_{a};V)

\displaystyle=I(v_{a};v_{0})+\left(I(v_{a};V)-I(v_{a};v_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=m(v_{0})+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(V(x)-v_{0})e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}dx.

Using the following estimate for
R>0
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(V(x)-v_{0})e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{a+R}(V(x)-v_{0})e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}dx+\int_{a+R}^{\infty}(V(x)-v_{0})e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\leq a+R}(V(x)-v_{0})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}dx+(v_{1}-v_{0})\int_{a+R}^{\infty}e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{v_{0}}}\sup_{x\leq a+R}(V(x)-v_{0})+\frac{v_{1}-v_{0}}{2\sqrt{v_{0}}}e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}R},

we obtain

\lim_{a\to-\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(V(x)-v_{0})e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}dx=0.

and
m(v_{0})\leq m(V)\leq\lim_{a\to-\infty}F(a)=m(v_{0})
.
Hence,
it holds that

m(V)=\lim_{a\to-\infty}F(a)=m(v_{0})=2\sqrt{v_{0}}.

Furthermore, for
b<a
, we set
u_{a}\in K_{a}
by (
3.2
)
and define
w_{b}\in K_{b}
by
w_{b}(x):=u_{a}(x-b+a)
.
Then, if
v_{0}<v_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle F(b)-F(a)\leq I(w_{b};V)-I(u_{a};V)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(V(x+b-a)-V(x))|u_{a}(x)|^{2}dx<0.

This estimate implies that
M(V)=\emptyset
, if
v_{0}<v_{1}
.
∎

A representation formula of the potential and its application

We study the condition (
4.5
) to investigate
m(V)
and
M(V)
in detail.
Surprisingly enough, we are able to express the
given
potential
V

in terms of
\varphi_{\pm}
,
where
\varphi_{\pm}
are the functions defined by Theorem
3.5
.
In this section, we observe this fact and use it to construct
an example of
V
, for which
m(V)
and
M(V)
are obtained explicitly.
We set

\displaystyle h_{\pm}(x):=\varphi_{\pm}(x)^{2},\quad\ell_{\pm}(x):=\log\varphi_{\pm}(x),

\displaystyle H_{+}(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{1}{h_{+}(\xi)}d\xi,\quad H_{-}(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h_{-}(\xi)}d\xi,

where
H_{\pm}(x)
are well defined from the estimate (
3.13
) and (
3.14
).
Then, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

We suppose (
2.1
) and set
W:=\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(0)-\varphi_{+}^{\prime}(0)>0
.
Then, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle u_{x}^{\prime}(x_{\pm})=\frac{\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi_{\pm}(x)}=\ell_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mp}(x)=W\varphi_{\pm}(x)H_{\pm}(x)\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(5.2)

\displaystyle V(x)=\ell_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\ell_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)^{2}\quad(\mbox{a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(5.3)

\displaystyle F(x)=\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x)-\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(5.4)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)=-F(x)(\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x))=-\frac{WF(x)}{\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)}(h_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)H_{\pm}(x)\pm 1)\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(5.5)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-2F^{\prime}(x)\ell_{\mp}^{\prime}(x)-2F(x)\ell_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x)\quad(\mbox{a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}).

(5.6)

Proof.

The equality (
5.1
) immediately follows from (
3.15
) and the definition of
\ell_{\pm}
.
Then,
(
5.4
) and the first equality of (
5.5
) follow
from Theorem
4.1
and (
5.1
).
Also, from (
5.1
), we have

\displaystyle\ell_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x)\varphi_{\pm}(x)-\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)^{2}}{\varphi_{\pm}(x)^{2}}=V(x)-\ell_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)^{2},

for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
which implies the formula (
5.3
).

We define the Wronskian,

W:=\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)-\varphi_{+}^{\prime}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)
.
Then, since

W^{\prime}(x):=\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}^{\prime\prime}(x)-\varphi_{+}^{\prime\prime}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)=\varphi_{+}(x)(V(x)\varphi_{-}(x))-(V(x)\varphi_{+}(x))\varphi_{-}(x)=0,

holds for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we obtain that
W
is a constant
and
W=\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(0)-\varphi_{+}^{\prime}(0)>0
holds.
Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\varphi_{\mp}(x)}{\varphi_{\pm}(x)}\right)=\frac{\varphi_{\mp}^{\prime}(x)\varphi_{\pm}(x)-\varphi_{\mp}(x)\varphi_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi_{\pm}(x)^{2}}=\frac{\pm W}{h_{\pm}(x)}=WH_{\pm}^{\prime}(x).

Integrating this equality,
we obtain
\varphi_{\mp}(x)/\varphi_{\pm}(x)=WH_{\pm}(x)
,
since
\lim_{x\to\mp\infty}H_{\pm}(x)=0
and

\lim_{x\to\mp\infty}\varphi_{\mp}(x)/\varphi_{\pm}(x)=0
.
Hence, (
5.2
) holds.

From (
5.2
), we obtain
\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)=Wh_{\pm}(x)H_{\pm}(x)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\big(\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)\big)=W\big(h_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)H_{\pm}(x)+h_{\pm}(x)H_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)\big)=W\big(h_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)H_{\pm}(x)\pm 1\big),

(5.7)

where we used the relation
H_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)=\pm h_{\pm}(x)^{-1}
.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\big(\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{+}^{\prime}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)+\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)\left(\frac{\varphi_{+}^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi_{+}(x)}+\frac{\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi_{-}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)\big(\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x)\big).

(5.8)

From (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{W(h_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)H_{\pm}(x)\pm 1)}{\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x)},

which implies the second equality of (
5.5
).

Lastly, we show (
5.6
).
Differentiating (
5.4
), we have

F^{\prime}(x)=\ell_{-}^{\prime\prime}(x)-\ell_{+}^{\prime\prime}(x)
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
So, it is equivalent to

\displaystyle\ell_{+}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime\prime}(x)=2\ell_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x)\pm F^{\prime}(x)\quad(\mbox{a.e.}\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}).

(5.9)

Hence, from the first equality of (
5.5
) and (
5.9
),
we obtain (
5.6
) as follows:

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-F^{\prime}(x)(\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x))-F(x)(\ell_{+}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime\prime}(x))

\displaystyle=-F^{\prime}(x)(\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x))-F(x)(2\ell_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x)\pm F^{\prime}(x))

\displaystyle=-F^{\prime}(x)(\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x)\pm F(x))-2F(x)\ell_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-2F^{\prime}(x)\ell_{\mp}^{\prime}(x)-2F(x)\ell_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}(x).

∎

Thus, the necessary and sufficient condition for satisfying
condition (
4.5
) is obtained as the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

We suppose (
2.1
) and
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Then, each of the following conditions is equivalent to (
4.5
):

1.

-\ell_{+}^{\prime}(a)=\ell_{-}^{\prime}(a)\geq\sqrt{V(a)}.

2.

h_{+}^{\prime}(a)H_{+}(a)=-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ell_{+}^{\prime\prime}(a)\leq 0.

3.

h_{-}^{\prime}(a)H_{-}(a)=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ell_{-}^{\prime\prime}(a)\leq 0.

Proof.

From (
4.3
) and (
5.1
),
we obtain

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(x)=2F(x)\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)(\ell_{+}^{\prime}(x)+\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x))+2F(x)(\ell_{-}^{\prime}(x)^{2}-V(x)).

(5.10)

From (
5.10
) and the first equality of (
5.5
),
we conclude that condition 1 is equivalent to (
4.5
).
The equivalency of condition 2 or 3 to (
4.5
) is
shown from (
5.5
) and (
5.6
).
∎

Conclusion

We developed a new variational approach
to determine the best constant of the
Sobolev-type embedding inequality in one dimension with a bounded inhomogeneous potential term.
We adopted two-step minimization method.
In the first minimization step, the Green function of the differential operator

-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V(x)

was captured as the minimizer.
We studied the fine properties
of the minimizer and the fundamental solutions of the ODE
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}u+V(x)u=0
.
It enabled us to derive new necessary and sufficient conditions
for the local minimality in the second minimization step (Theorem
5.2
).
Furthermore, as applications of our estimates,
we constructed some concrete examples of the inhomogeneous
potential
V(x)
for which the best constant and function of the
Sobolev-type embedding is exactly identified.

Since our approach provides a new tool to study the fine properties
of the best constant and function of the
Sobolev-type embedding with an inhomogeneous potential term,
further extension of our strategies
is expected in future work.
For example, the case the potential
V(x)
is a periodic function
is interested in connection with the periodic crystal lattice, e.g., Kronig-Penney potential

[
7
]
.
Extention of the Bloch theorem
[
6
]
for general
periodic potentials in our framework is also a challenging and worthy topic.
On the other hand, extending our method to the higher dimensional setting seems more complex. Nevertheless, it is a significant and exciting issue that we should address. Our approach in this paper is the first step toward that end.

Interior Estimates for Generalized Forchheimer Flows of Slightly Compressible Fluids
Luan T. Hoang, Thinh T. Kieu

Abstract
The generalized Forchheimer flows are studied for slightly compressible fluids in porous media with time-dependent Dirichlet boundary data for the pressure. No restrictions on the degree of the Forchheimer polynomial are imposed. We derive, for all time, the interior L ∞ L^{\infty} -estimates for the pressure and its partial derivatives, and the interior L 2 L^{2} -estimates for its Hessian. The De Giorgi and Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva iteration techniques are used taking into account the special structures of the equations for both pressure and its gradient. These are combined with the uniform Gronwall-type bounds in establishing the asymptotic estimates when time tends to infinity.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the generalized Forchheimer flows for slightly compressible fluids in porous media.
Forchheimer equations are used to describe the fluids’ dynamics when the ubiquitous Darcy’s law is not applicable.
They are nonlinear relations between the fluid’s velocity and gradient of pressure which were realized by Darcy
[
3
]
and Dupuit
[
7
]
in early works, formulated by Forchheimer
[
9
,
10
]
, and were studied more extensively afterward in physics and engineering, see
[
17
,
24
,
2
,
18
,
21
]
and references therein.
Mathematics of Darcy flows has been studied thoroughly with a vast literature for a long time dated back to the 1960s, see the treaty
[
23
]
for references. In contrast, Forchheimer flows were investigated mathematically much later in the 1990s.
Even less are the mathematical works on compressible Forchheimer flows. The reader is referred to
[
1
,
11
,
12
]
for more information about this topic.

Generalized Forchheimer equations were proposed and studied in our previous works
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
in order to cover a large class of fluid flows formulated from experiments. For compressible fluids, they form a new class of degenerate parabolic equations with their own characteristics compared to other models of porous medium equations. Among a small number of papers on these flows, recent work
[
15
]
is focused on studying the pressure and its time derivative in space
L^{\infty}
, the pressure gradient in
L^{s}
for
s\in[1,\infty)
and the pressure’s Hessian in
L^{2-\delta}
for
\delta\in(0,1)
. However, it requires the so-called Strict Degree Condition (SDC), that is, the degree of the Forchheimer polynomial is less than
4/(n-2)
, where
n
is the spatial dimension. Another related paper
[
13
]
, in contrast, does not require (SDC), but the analysis is mainly for pressure in space
L^{s}
, pressure gradient in space
L^{2-a}
for a specific number
a\in(0,1)
, and pressure’s
time derivative in
L^{2}
.
Our current work is to unite the two approaches and develop them further without any restrictions on the Forchheimer polynomials.
Specifically, we consider the initial boundary value problem (IBVP) for the pressure in a bounded domain with time-dependent Dirichlet boundary data.
The interior
W^{1,\infty}
and
W^{2,2}
norms of the solutions are estimated, particularly for large time.
Such estimates for degenerate parabolic equations usually require much work, see, for e.g.,
[
22
,
19
,
20
,
6
]
.
Improving on
[
15
]
, we adapt and refine techniques by De Giorgi
[
4
]
, Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva
[
16
]
, and also DiBenedetto
[
5
]
for parabolic equations, and combine them with those used for Navier-Stokes equations
[
8
]
. These techniques are utilized successfully here thanks to the special structure of our equation.

The paper is organized as follows.
We recall the model, equations and basic facts in section
2
.
In section
3
, we derive the uniform Gronwall-type estimates that sharpen the previous results in
[
11
,
13
]
especially for large time.
In section
4
, the interior
L^{\infty}
-estimates for pressure are established by using
L^{\alpha}
-based De Giorgi iteration with sufficiently large
\alpha
. This is different from
[
15
]
which is based on the
L^{2}
-estimate and, hence, requires (SDC).
As a result, no restrictions on the Forchheimer polynomials are needed for our estimates.
In section
5
, we first use Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva-type imbedding and iteration to estimate the pressure gradient in
L^{s}
for any
s\geq 1
.
Our estimates only require the initial data to be in the spaces
W^{1,2-a}
and
L^{\alpha}
for a fixed
\alpha
. When
s
is large, this requirement is much less than the
W^{1,s}
condition in
[
15
]
. In subsection
5.2
, we derive the
W^{1,\infty}
estimates for the pressure, which were not previously studied in
[
13
,
15
]
. We make use of the equation (
5.21
) for the pressure gradient, which it is highly nonlinear but has a special structure. Thanks to this and previous
W^{1,s}
(
s<\infty
) bounds, we utilize the De Giorgi technique to obtain the
L^{\infty}
-estimates for the pressure gradient.
Section
6
contains the
L^{\infty}
-estimates for the time derivative of the pressure, while
section
7
contains new
L^{2}
-bounds for the pressure’s Hessian.
It is noteworthy that, thanks to the uniform Gronwall-type inequalities in section
3
, the asymptotic bounds obtained as time goes to infinity depend only on the asymptotic behavior of the boundary data.
This paper is focused primarily on the interior estimates. The issue of the solutions’ estimates on the entire domain is addressed in another work of ours
[
14
]
.

2
Background

Consider a fluid in a porous medium occupying a bounded domain
U
with boundary
\Gamma=\partial U
in space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For physics problem
n=3
, but here we consider any
n\geq 2
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
be the spatial and time variables.
The fluid flow has velocity
v(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, pressure
p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
and density
\rho(x,t)\in[0,\infty)
.

The generalized Forchheimer equations studied in
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
are of the the form:

(2.1)

g(|v|)v=-\nabla p,

where
g(s)\geq 0
is a function defined on
[0,\infty)
.
When
g(s)=\alpha,\alpha+\beta s,\alpha+\beta s+\gamma s^{2},\alpha+\gamma_{m}s^{m-1}
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,m,\gamma_{m}
are empirical constants, we have Darcy’s law, Forchheimer’s two-term, three-term and power laws, respectively.

In this paper, we study the case when the function
g
in (
2.1
) is a generalized polynomial with non-negative coefficients, that is,

(2.2)

g(s)=a_{0}s^{\alpha_{0}}+a_{1}s^{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+a_{N}s^{\alpha_{N}}\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq 0,

where

N\geq 1
, the powers
\alpha_{0}=0<\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{N}
are fixed real numbers (not necessarily integers), the coefficients
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
are non-negative with
a_{0}>0
and
a_{N}>0
.
This function
g(s)
is referred to as the Forchheimer polynomial in equation (
2.1
), and
\alpha_{N}
is the degree of
g
.

From (
2.1
) one can solve
v
implicitly in terms of
\nabla p
and
derives a nonlinear version of Darcy’s equation:

(2.3)

v=-K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p,

where the function
K:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

(2.4)

K(\xi)=\frac{1}{g(s(\xi))},\text{ with }s=s(\xi)\geq 0\text{ satisfying }sg(s)=\xi,\ \text{ for }\xi\geq 0.

In addition to (
2.1
), we have the continuity equation

(2.5)

\phi\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,

where number
\phi\in(0,1)
is the constant porosity.
Also, for slightly compressible fluids, the equation of state is

(2.6)

\frac{d\rho}{dp}=\frac{\rho}{\kappa},\quad\text{with }\kappa=const.>0.

From (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) one derives a scalar equation for the pressure:

(2.7)

\phi\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\kappa\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p)+K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2}.

On the right-hand side of (
2.7
), the constant
\kappa
is very large for most slightly compressible fluids in porous media
[
17
]
, hence we neglect its second term and by scaling the time variable, we study the reduced equation

(2.8)

\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p).

Note that this reduction is commonly used in engineering.

Our aim is to study the IBVP for equation (
2.8
) in a bounded domain.
Here afterward
U
is a bounded open connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3,\ldots
with
C^{2}
boundary
\Gamma=\partial U
.
Throughout, the Forchheimer polynomial
g(s)
is fixed.
The following number is frequently used in our calculations:

(2.9)

a=\frac{\alpha_{N}}{1+\alpha_{N}}\in(0,1).

The function
K(\xi)
in (
2.4
) has the following properties (c.f.
[
1
,
11
]
): it is decreasing in
\xi
mapping
\xi\in[0,\infty)
onto
(0,1/a_{0}]
, and

(2.10)

\frac{C_{1}}{(1+\xi)^{a}}\leq K(\xi)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{(1+\xi)^{a}},

(2.11)

C_{3}(\xi^{2-a}-1)\leq K(\xi)\xi^{2}\leq C_{2}\xi^{2-a},

(2.12)

-aK(\xi)\leq K^{\prime}(\xi)\xi\leq 0,

where
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
are positive constants depending on
g
.
As in previous works, we use the function
H(\xi)
defined by

H(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\xi^{2}}K(\sqrt{s})ds\quad\hbox{for }\xi\geq 0.

It satisfies
K(\xi)\xi^{2}\leq H(\xi)\leq 2K(\xi)\xi^{2}
, thus, by (
2.11
),

(2.13)

C_{3}(\xi^{2-a}-1)\leq H(\xi)\leq 2C_{2}\xi^{2-a}.

The following parabolic Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities are needed for our study.
For each
T>0
, denote
Q_{T}=U\times(0,T)
.
We define a threshold exponent

(2.14)

\alpha_{*}=\frac{an}{2-a}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume

(2.15)

\alpha\geq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha>\alpha_{*}.

Let

(2.16)

p=\alpha\Big(1+\frac{2-a}{n}\Big)-a.

Then

(2.17)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(Q_{T})}\leq C(1+\delta T)^{1/p}[[u]],

where
\delta=1
in general,
\delta=0
in case
u
vanishes on the boundary
\partial U
, and

(2.18)

[[u]]=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\alpha}(U)}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}|u|^{\alpha-2}|\nabla u|^{2-a}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-a}}.

In case
U=B_{R}
– a ball of radius
R
– the inequality (
2.17
) holds with

(2.19)

[[u]]=R^{\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{\alpha}}\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\alpha}(B_{R})}+R^{\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n-(2-a)}{\alpha-a}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{\alpha-2}|\nabla u|^{2-a}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-a}}

and the constant
C
independent of
R
.

The proof of Lemma
2.1
is given in Appendix
A
.
The next is a particular embedding with spatial weights from Lemma 2.4 of
[
15
]
(see inequality (2.28) with
m=2
there).

Lemma 2.2

(cf.
[
15
]
, Lemma 2.4)
.

Given
W(x,t)>0
on
Q_{T}
.
Let
r
be a number that satisfies

(2.20)

\frac{2n}{n+2}<r<2.

Set

(2.21)

\varrho=\varrho(r)\mathbin{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mathbin{=\kern-2.0pt=}}\limits^{\rm def}}}4(1-1/r^{*}).

Then

(2.22)

\|u\|_{L^{\varrho}(Q_{T})}\leq C[[u]]_{2,W;T}\Big\{\delta T^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}+\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{U}W(x,t)^{-\frac{r}{2-r}}\chi_{{\rm supp}\,u}(x,t)dx\Big)^{\frac{2-r}{\varrho r}}\Big\},

where
\delta=1
in general,
\delta=0
in case
u
vanishes on the boundary
\partial U
, and

(2.23)

[[u]]_{2,W;T}=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(U)}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}W(x,t)|\nabla u|^{2}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The following is a generalization of the convergence of fast decay geometry sequences in Lemma 5.6, Chapter II of
[
16
]
. It will be used in the De Giorgi iterations.

Lemma 2.3

(cf.
[
15
]
, Lemma A.2)
.

Let
\{Y_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers satisfying

Y_{i+1}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}B_{k}^{i}Y_{i}^{1+\mu_{k}},\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots,

where
A_{k}>0
,
B_{k}>1
and
\mu_{k}>0
for
k=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Let
B=\max\{B_{k}:1\leq k\leq m\}
and
\mu=\min\{\mu_{k}:1\leq k\leq m\}
.
If
Y_{0}\leq\min\{(m^{-1}A_{k}^{-1}B^{-\frac{1}{\mu}})^{1/\mu_{k}}:1\leq k\leq m\}

then
\lim_{i\to\infty}Y_{i}=0
.

3
Uniform Gronwall-type estimates

We study the following IBVP for
p(x,t)
:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p)&\text{in }U\times(0,\infty),\\
p(x,0)=p_{0}(x)&\text{in }U,\\
p(x,t)=\psi(x,t)&\text{on }\Gamma\times(0,\infty).\end{cases}

In order to deal with the non-homogeneous boundary condition, the data
\psi(x,t)
with
x\in\Gamma
and
t>0
is extended to a function
\Psi(x,t)
with
x\in\bar{U}
and
t\geq 0
. Throughout, our results are stated in terms of
\Psi
instead of
\psi
. Nonetheless, corresponding results in terms of
\psi
can be retrieved as performed in
[
11
]
. The function
\Psi
is always assumed to have adequate regularities for all calculations in this paper.

It is proved in Section 3 of
[
13
]
that (
3.1
) possesses a weak solution
p(x,t)
for all
t>0
.
It, in fact, has more regularity in spatial and time variables, see
[
5
]
.
For the current study, we assume that solution
p(x,t)
has sufficient regularities both in
x
and
t
variables such that our calculations hereafterward can be performed legitimately.

In this section, we obtain improved estimates for solutions of (
3.1
). We emphasize the asymptotic estimates as
t\to\infty
in terms of the asymptotic behavior of
\Psi(x,t)
.

Theorem 3.1

(cf.
[

]

.

Let
\alpha>0

(i)
For all

(ii)
If

(iii)

\displaystyle G_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{U}|\nabla\Psi(x,t)|^{2}dx+\Big[\int_{U}|\Psi_{t}(x,t)|^{r_{0}}dx\Big]^{\frac{2-a}{r_{0}(1-a)}}+\Big[\int_{U}|\Psi_{t}(x,t)|^{r_{0}}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{r_{0}}},

Lemma 3.2

For

(3.18)

\int_{U}H(|\nabla p(x,t)|)dx+\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau ds\leq 2\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{U}H(|\nabla p|)dxd\tau+C\int_{t-1}^{t}G_{2}(\tau)d\tau\\
\leq C\int_{U}\bar{p}^{2}(x,t-1)dx+C\int_{t-1}^{t}G_{1}(\tau)d\tau+C\int_{t-1}^{t}G_{2}(\tau)d\tau.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau ds=\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{t-1}^{\tau}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,\tau)dxdsd\tau

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t-1/2}^{t}\int_{t-1}^{\tau}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,\tau)dxdsd\tau\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-1/2}^{t}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau.

Therefore (
3.13
) follows from (
3.18
).

Proof of (
3.14
).
From (3.37) of Proposition 3.11 in
[

]
we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}dx\leq-C\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}dx+C\int_{U}|\nabla\Psi_{t}|^{2}dx-\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}\Psi_{tt}dx.

Dropping the first term on the right-hand side and using Cauchy’s inequality give

(3.19)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}dx\leq C\int_{U}|\nabla\Psi_{t}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{U}|\bar{p}_{t}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{U}|\Psi_{tt}|^{2}dx.

For

, integrating (

) from
s
to

gives

\displaystyle\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,s)dx+C\int_{s}^{t}\int_{U}|\nabla\Psi_{t}|^{2}+|\Psi_{tt}|^{2}dxd\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{U}|\bar{p}_{t}(x,\tau)|^{2}dxd\tau,

\displaystyle\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,s)dx+C\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{U}|\nabla\Psi_{t}|^{2}+|\Psi_{tt}|^{2}dxd\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-1/2}^{t}\int_{U}|\bar{p}_{t}(x,\tau)|^{2}dxd\tau.

Integrating the last inequality in
s
from

to

\frac{1}{2}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dx\leq\frac{5}{4}\int_{t-1/2}^{t}\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,s)dxds+C\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{U}|\nabla\Psi_{t})|^{2}+|\Psi_{tt}|^{2}dxd\tau.

Using (
3.13
) for the first integral on the right-hand side, we have

(3.20)

\int_{U}\bar{p}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dx\leq C\int_{U}\bar{p}^{2}(x,t-1)dx+C\int_{t-1}^{t}G_{3}(\tau)d\tau+C\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{U}|\nabla\Psi_{t})|^{2}+|\Psi_{tt}|^{2}dxd\tau.

4

In this section we estimate the

-norm of the pressure.
Hereafter,
\alpha
is a number that satisfies (

We use

(4.1)

\kappa_{0}=\alpha(1+(2-a)/n)-a,\quad\kappa_{1}=1/\delta_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa_{2}=1/\delta_{2},

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
U^{\prime}\Subset U
. If
T_{0}\geq 0
,
T>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
then

(4.2)

\sup_{[T_{0}+\theta T,T_{0}+T]}\|{p(t)}\|_{L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})}\leq C(1+T)^{\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{0}}}\Big(1+(\theta T)^{-1}\Big)^{\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\alpha-a}}\Big(1+\|p\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}\Big)^{\kappa_{2}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
T_{0}=0
.
For
k\geq 0
, define
p^{(k)}=\max\{p-k,0\}
and denote by
\chi_{k}(x,t)
the characteristic function of the set
\operatorname{supp}p^{(k)}
.
Let
\phi_{1}(x)
and
\phi_{2}(t)
be cut-off functions with
\phi_{1}=1
on
U^{\prime}
,
\phi_{1}=0
on a neighborhood of
\partial U
, and
\phi_{2}(0)=0
.
Let
\zeta(x,t)=\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(t)
. Multiplying the first equation in (
3.1
) by
|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-1}\zeta^{2}
and integrating over
U
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta^{2}dx+(\alpha-1)\int_{U}K(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)|\nabla p^{(k)}|^{2}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\alpha}\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta\zeta_{t}dx+2\int_{U}K(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)(\nabla p^{(k)}\cdot\nabla\zeta)|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-1}\zeta dx.

By Cauchy’s inequality, we have for the last integral that

\displaystyle 2|K(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)(\nabla p^{(k)}\cdot\nabla\zeta)|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-1}\zeta|\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{2}K(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)|\nabla p^{(k)}|^{2}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta^{2}+CK(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}.

Combining the above, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta^{2}dx+\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)|\nabla p^{(k)}|^{2}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta|\zeta_{t}|dx+C\int_{U}K(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}dx.

Using (
2.11
), we then have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{U}|\nabla p^{(k)}|^{2-a}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta|\zeta_{t}|dx+C\int_{U}K(|\nabla p^{(k)}|)|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}dx+C\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta^{2}dx.

Using the boundedness of
K(\cdot)
in the second to last integral, and applying Young’s inequality to the last terms yield

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{U}|\nabla p^{(k)}|^{2-a}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}(\zeta^{2}+\zeta|\zeta_{t}|+|\nabla\zeta|^{2})dx+C\int_{U}\chi_{k}\zeta^{2}dx.

For
t\in[0,T]
, integrating from
0
to
T
, and taking supremum in
t
, we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T]}\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}|\nabla p^{(k)}|^{2-a}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}|p^{(k)}|^{\alpha}(\zeta^{2}+\zeta|\zeta_{t}|+|\nabla\zeta|^{2})dx+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}\chi_{k}\zeta^{2}dx.

Let
x_{0}
be any given point in
\bar{U}^{\prime}
. Denote
\rho={\rm dist}(\bar{U}^{\prime},\partial U)>0
.
Let
M_{0}>0
be fixed which will be determined later.
For
i\geq 0
, define

(4.4)

k_{i}=M_{0}(1-2^{-i}),\quad t_{i}=\theta T(1-2^{-i}),\quad\rho_{i}=\frac{1}{4}\rho(1+2^{-i}).

Then

t_{0}=0<t_{1}<\ldots<\theta T
and
\rho_{0}=\rho/2>\rho_{1}>\ldots>\rho/4>0.

Note that

(4.5)

\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}=\theta T\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}\rho_{i}=\rho/4.

Let
U_{i}=\{x:\|{x-x_{0}}\|<\rho_{i}\}
then
U_{i+1}\Subset U_{i}
for
i=0,1,2,\ldots
.
For
i,j\geq 0
, we denote

(4.6)

\mathcal{Q}_{i}=U_{i}\times(t_{i},T)\quad\text{ and }\quad A_{i,j}=\{(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{j}:p(x,t)>k_{i}\}.

For each
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
, we use a cut-off function
\zeta_{i}(x,t)
with
\zeta_{i}\equiv 1
in
\mathcal{Q}_{i+1}
and

\zeta_{i}\equiv 0
on
Q_{T}\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{i}
, and

(4.7)

|(\zeta_{i})_{t}|\leq\frac{C}{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}=\frac{C2^{i+1}}{\theta T},\quad\text{ and }\quad|\nabla\zeta_{i}|\leq\frac{C}{\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}}=\frac{C2^{i+1}}{4\rho}.

for some
C>0
.
Applying (
4.3
) with
k=k_{i+1}
and
\zeta=\zeta_{i}
gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T]}\int_{U}|p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{\alpha}\zeta_{i}^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}|\nabla p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{2-a}|p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{\alpha-2}\zeta_{i}^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}|p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{\alpha}(\zeta_{i}^{2}+\zeta_{i}|\zeta_{it}|+|\nabla\zeta_{i}|^{2})dxdt+C\mathcal{\int}_{0}^{T}\int_{U}\chi_{k_{i+1}}\zeta_{i}^{2}dxdt.

Define

F_{i}\mathbin{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mathbin{=\kern-2.0pt=}}\limits^{\rm def}}}\sup_{[t_{i+1},T]}\int_{U_{i+1}}|p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{\alpha}dx+\int_{t_{i+1}}^{T}\int_{U_{i+1}}|\nabla p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{2-a}|p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{\alpha-2}dxdt.

Then (
4.8
) yields

(4.9)

\displaystyle F_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{i}}^{T}\int_{U}|p^{(k_{i+1})}|^{\alpha}(\zeta_{i}^{2}+\zeta_{i}|\zeta_{it}|+|\nabla\zeta_{i}|^{2})dxdt+C\Big(\int_{t_{i}}^{T}\int_{U_{i}}\chi_{k_{i+1}}dxdt\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C4^{i}((\theta T)^{-1}+1)\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i+1,i})}^{\alpha}+C|A_{i+1,i}|.

Since
\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}\geq\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i+1,i})}\geq(k_{i+1}-k_{i})|A_{i+1,i}|^{1/\alpha}
,
we have

(4.10)

|A_{i+1,i}|\leq(k_{i+1}-k_{i})^{-\alpha}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}^{\alpha}\leq C2^{\alpha i}M_{0}^{-\alpha}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}^{\alpha}.

This and (
4.9
) imply

(4.11)

\displaystyle F_{i}

\displaystyle\leq C4^{i}(1+(\theta T)^{-1})\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i+1,i})}^{\alpha}+C2^{\alpha i}M_{0}^{-\alpha}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\alpha i}\Big(1+(\theta T)^{-1}+M_{0}^{-\alpha}\Big)\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}^{\alpha}.

Note that
\kappa_{0}
is the exponent defined in (
2.16
). Applying Lemma
2.1
to domain
U_{i}
, interval
[t_{i+1},T]
and function
p^{(k_{i+1})}
with the use of (
2.19
), we have

(4.12)

\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\kappa_{0}}(A_{i+1,i+1})}=\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\kappa_{0}}(\mathcal{Q}_{i+1})}\leq C(1+T)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}(F_{i}^{1/\alpha}+F_{i}^{1/(\alpha-a)}).

Note that the constant
C
depends on
\rho
.
Holder’s inequality gives

\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i+1,i+1})}\leq\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\kappa_{0}}(A_{i+1,i+1})}|A_{i+1,i+1}|^{1/\alpha-1/\kappa_{0}}\leq\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\kappa_{0}}(A_{i+1,i+1})}|A_{i+1,i}|^{1/\alpha-1/\kappa_{0}}.

It follows from this, (
4.12
), (
4.11
), and (
4.10
) that

\displaystyle\|p^{(k_{i+1})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i+1,i+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+T)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}(F_{i}^{1/\alpha}+F_{i}^{1/(\alpha-a)})|A_{i+1,i}|^{1/\alpha-1/\kappa_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+T)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}B^{i}\Big\{\Big(1+(\theta T)^{-1}+M_{0}^{-\alpha}\Big)^{1/\alpha}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(1+(\theta T)^{-1}+M_{0}^{-\alpha}\Big)^{1/(\alpha-a)}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i})}^{\alpha/(\alpha-a)}\Big\}M_{0}^{-1+\alpha/\kappa_{0}}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{2}(A_{i,i})}^{1-\alpha/\kappa_{0}},

where

. Now selecting

we have

\displaystyle\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i+1,i+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+T)^{\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}}B^{i}\Big\{\Big(1+(\theta T)^{-1}\Big)^{1/\alpha}M_{0}^{-1+\alpha/\kappa_{0}}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}^{2-\alpha/\kappa_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(1+(\theta T)^{-1}\Big)^{1/(\alpha-a)}M_{0}^{-1+\alpha/\kappa_{0}}\|p^{(k_{i})}\|_{L^{\alpha}(A_{i,i})}^{\alpha/(\alpha-a)+1-\alpha/\kappa_{0}}\Big\}.

Then

where

),

Then by Lemma
2.3
,

, consequently,

that is

This, in turn, is satisfied if

with an appropriate positive constant

.

a.e. in
U^{\prime}\times[\theta T,T]
,
and hence (

) follows. The proof is complete.

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|{p(t)}\|_{L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})}\leq C\Big(1+\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(t-1,t))}+\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\Psi\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(t-1,t))}\Big)^{\kappa_{2}}.

(iii) By (

Lemma 5.1

(cf.
[

]
, Lemma 3.2)

For each

\displaystyle\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{\text{\rm supp}\zeta}|p|^{2}\Big[\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2s-2}|\nabla^{2}p|^{2}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2s}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}dx\Big],

We establish the basic step for the Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva iteration.

Lemma 5.2
.

For each
s\geq 0

,
T>0

\zeta(x,t)

(5.1)

\sup_{[T_{0},T_{0}+T]}\int_{U}|\nabla p(x,t)|^{2s+2}\zeta^{2}dx+\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{0}+T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla^{2}p|^{2}|\nabla p|^{2s}\zeta^{2}dxdt\\
\leq C\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{0}+T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2s+2}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}dxdt+C\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{0}+T}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta|\zeta_{t}|dx.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
T_{0}=0

Multiplying the equation (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta^{2}dx=-\int_{U}\partial_{j}(K(|\nabla p|)\partial_{i}p)\,\partial_{i}(|\nabla p|^{2s}\partial_{j}p\zeta^{2})dx+\frac{1}{s+1}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta\zeta_{t}dx.

Calculated as in Lemma 3.1 of
[
15

above equation is rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta^{2}dx=-\int_{U}\Big[\partial_{y_{l}}(K(|y|)y_{i})\Big|_{y=\nabla p}\partial_{j}\partial_{l}p\Big]\partial_{j}\partial_{i}p|\nabla p|^{2s}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{U}\Big[\partial_{y_{l}}(K(|y|)y_{i})\Big|_{y=\nabla p}\partial_{j}\partial_{l}p\Big]\partial_{j}p\,|\nabla p|^{2s}\,\zeta\partial_{i}\zeta dx

\displaystyle\quad-2s\int_{U}\Big[\partial_{y_{l}}(K(|y|)y_{i})\Big|_{y=\nabla p}\partial_{j}\partial_{l}p\Big]\partial_{j}p\,(|\nabla p|^{2s-2}\partial_{i}\partial_{m}p\partial_{m}p)\,\zeta^{2}dx+\frac{1}{s+1}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta\zeta_{t}dx.

We denote the four terms on the right-hand side by

It follows from the calculations in Lemma 3.1 of

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-(1-a)\sum_{j}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla(\partial_{j}p)|^{2}|\nabla p|^{2s}\zeta^{2}dx,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+a)\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla^{2}p||\nabla p|^{2s+1}\zeta|\nabla\zeta|dx,

and

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq-2(1-a)s\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)\Big|\nabla\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla p|^{2}\Big)\Big|^{2}|\nabla p|^{2s-2}\zeta^{2}dx\leq 0.

Combining these estimates with Young’s inequality, we find that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta^{2}dx+\frac{1-a}{2}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla^{2}p|^{2}|\nabla p|^{2s}\zeta^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2s+2}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{s+1}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2s+2}\zeta|\zeta_{t}|dx.

Inequality (

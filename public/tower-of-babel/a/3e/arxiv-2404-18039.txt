Abstract
A simple iterative approach for solving a set of implicit kinetic moment equations is proposed. This implicit solve is a key component in the IMEX discretization of the multi-species Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (M-BGK) model with nontrivial collision frequencies depending on individual species temperatures. We prove that under mild time step restrictions, the iterative method generates a contraction mapping. Numerical simulations are provided to illustrate results of the IMEX scheme using the implicit moment solver.



Keywords:
Multi-species BGK, fixed point iterative solvers, implicit time stepping strategies, moment equations, kinetic theory, rarefied gas dynamics

1
Introduction

Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) models (see e.g.
[
22
]
) are a computationally inexpensive alternative to the Boltzmann equation of rarefied gas dynamics.
They are derived by replacing the sophisticated Boltzmann collision operator with a nonlinear relaxation model.
In the single species case, the target of this relaxation model is a Maxwellian distribution that shares the same mass, momentum, and energy density as the kinetic distribution.
This model was first introduced in
[
4
]
, and rigorous mathematical results about the model can be found in
[
25
,
24
]
.

Like the Boltzmann equation, the single-species BGK model recovers the compressible system of Euler equations in the limit of infinite collisions
[
9
]
.
For simulation purposes, implicit-explicit (IMEX) methods are often employed that treat the advection term explicitly and the stiff collision term implicitly.
The strategy for the implicit evaluation of the BGK operator is to recognize that the moments that characterize the Maxwellian target are conserved by the space-homogeneous problem.
As a result, the implicit part of the relaxation operator is reduced to a simple multiple of the identity.
This trick, introduced in
[
12
]
, is the basis of many semi-implicit schemes.

More recently, multi-species versions of the BGK equation have been derived that satisfy the conservation and entropy dissipation properties of the multi-species Boltzmann equation.
Some models still use a single relaxation operator
[
1
,
8
]
while others use a sum of operators with Maxwellian-like targets for each binary interaction
[
6
,
20
,
16
,
15
]
.
We focus here on the model from
[
16
]
, but the approach and analysis can be extended to the generalizations in
[
6
,
20
]
.
In these models, each relaxation operator is equipped with a collision frequency.
Conservation of the number density in the multi-species setting implies that frequencies that depend only on the number densities will not be affected by the collision process.
However, for general molecular models, collision frequencies may depend on the species temperature, average velocities, and even microscopic velocities
[
5
]
, and removing such dependencies can significantly alter the behavior of solutions
[
15
]
.
In addition, the analysis and simulation of the BGK equations becomes more difficult when the frequencies depend on time and/or phase-space velocity.

The focus of the present paper is the implicit update of the collision term in the multi-species setting when more realistic collision frequencies are employed.
In order to follow the strategy used for the single-species model, one must update an ODE system for the species’ average velocities and temperatures, thereby enabling the evaluation of the Maxwellian target at the next time step.
As in the single species case, this approach effectively linearizes the problem, allowing for a simple update of the collision term.
However, unlike the single species case, the moments of each species are not conserved; rather the individual species densities, total momentum, and total energy are conserved.
As a result, the ODE for the average velocities and temperatures requires an implicit solve.
For collision frequencies that depend only on species densities, such a solve is still trivial to implement because the frequencies are not affected by the collisions.
However, for more complicated collision frequencies, a fully implicit solve must take such variations into account.

We propose here to solve the multi-species BGK collision operator in a fully implicit manner using a nonlinear Gauss-Seidel-type iterative solver.
The focus of the paper is on the numerical analysis of the solver.
In particular, we demonstrate a contraction mapping under mild time step restrictions.
Importantly, we show that, given a user specified time step, there is a stable time step that, at worst, is smaller by an
\mathcal{O}(1)
constant that does not depend on the stiffness of the collision operator.
Thus, in the fluid regime, the method can still take time steps determined by particle advection and not particle collisions.
This result and the underlying analysis can be extended to general relaxation systems with state-dependent relaxation times.

Background

2.1
Models and equations

We denote by
f_{i}
the kinetic distribution of particles of species
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
having mass
m_{i}>0
.
More specifically,
f_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v},t)
is the density of species
i
particles at the point
\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with microscopic velocity
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, at time
t\geq 0,
with respect to the measure
\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{x}
.
Associated to each
f_{i}
are the species number density
n_{i}
, bulk velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
, and temperature
T_{i}
, defined by

n_{i}={\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f_{i}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v},\qquad\boldsymbol{u}_{i}=\frac{1}{n_{i}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\boldsymbol{v}f_{i}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v},\qquad T_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}d}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}f_{i}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}.

(1)

The species mass density
\rho_{i}
, momentum density
\boldsymbol{k}_{i}
, and energy density
E_{i}
are

\rho_{i}=m_{i}n_{i},\qquad\boldsymbol{k}_{i}=\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad E_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{i}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{d}{2}n_{i}T_{i}.

(2)

BGK models are expressed in terms of Maxwellian distributions that depend on these moments.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
n>0
,
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\theta>0
, the Maxwellian
M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}
is a function of the form

M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}(\boldsymbol{v})=\frac{n}{(2\pi\theta)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}}{2\theta}\right).

(3)

Straightforward computations show that the moments of
M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}
satisfy

{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n,\qquad{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\boldsymbol{v}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n\boldsymbol{u},\qquad{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n|\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}+dn\theta.

(4)

We consider in this paper the multi-species BGK equation from
[
16
,
17
]
, given by

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}+\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f_{i}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{i,j}(M_{i,j}-f_{i}),\qquad\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},

(5)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a dimensionless parameter that comes from a rescaling of space and time,
\lambda_{i,j}>0
is the frequency of collisions between species
i
and
j
(independent of
\boldsymbol{v}
), and

M_{i,j}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq M_{n_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j},T_{i,j}/m_{i}}(\boldsymbol{v})

(6)

is a Maxwellian defined by (
3
), using the mixture velocities and temperatures

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}},

(7a)

\displaystyle T_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}T_{i}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}T_{j}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\lambda_{j,i}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}.

(7b)

As in
[
17
]
, we consider a hard spheres collision model for the collision frequencies:

\lambda_{i,j}\coloneqq\lambda_{i,j}^{\textnormal{HS}}(n_{j},T_{i},T_{j})=\frac{32\pi^{2}}{3(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{(m_{i}+m_{j})^{2}}(d_{i}+d_{j})^{2}n_{j}\sqrt{\frac{T_{i}}{m_{i}}+\frac{T_{j}}{m_{j}}},

(8)

where
d_{i}
and
d_{j}
are reference diameters for the particles of species
i
and
j
.
For simplicity, we write
\lambda_{i,j}=c_{i,j}n_{j}\Psi_{i,j},
where

\Psi_{i,j}(T_{i},T_{j})=\sqrt{\frac{T_{i}}{m_{i}}+\frac{T_{j}}{m_{j}}}

(9)

is the only time-dependent term.
The challenging features of
\lambda_{i,j}^{\textnormal{HS}}
are (i) it depends on species temperatures that are not conserved by collision dynamics, and (ii) this temperature dependence is not Lipschitz at zero temperatures.

Going forward, we will abbreviate summation symbols
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
as
\sum_{j}
, when the upper and lower index is understood as
j=1
and
j=N
, respectively.

2.2
IMEX strategy and backward Euler step

Implicit-explicit (IMEX) Runge Kutta methods are a common tool for simulating kinetic equations.
These methods typically treat advection explicitly and collisions implicitly.
A diagonally implicit IMEX Runge-Kutta method for (
5
) with
\nu
stages and time step
\Delta t^{\mathfrak{n}}>0
takes the form
[
26
]

\displaystyle f_{i}^{(s)}

\displaystyle=f_{i}^{(s,\star)}+\frac{\Delta t^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\varepsilon}a_{s,s}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{(s)}(M^{(s)}_{i,j}-f^{(s)}_{i}),\qquad s\in\{1,\cdots,\nu\},

(10a)

\displaystyle f^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i}

\displaystyle=f_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\Delta t^{\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{s=1}^{\nu}\widetilde{b}_{s}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f_{i}^{(s)}+\frac{\Delta t^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\varepsilon}\sum_{s=1}^{\nu}b_{s}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{(s)}(M_{i,j}^{(s)}-f_{i}^{(s)}),

(10b)

where

f_{i}^{(s,\star)}=f_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\Delta t^{\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}\widetilde{a}_{s,r}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f^{(r)}_{i}+\frac{\Delta t^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\varepsilon}\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}a_{s,r}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{(r)}(M^{(r)}_{i,j}-f^{(r)}_{i}),

(11)

and

\lambda_{i,j}^{(r)}=\lambda_{i,j}^{\textnormal{HS}}(n_{j}^{(r)},T^{(r)}_{i},T^{(r)}_{j})
.
The coefficients
a_{s,r}
and
\widetilde{a}_{s,r}
are determined by order conditions and other desired properties of the method
[
23
,
3
,
7
]
.

A prototype for the update in (
10a
), and the focus of this paper, is the Backward Euler update, namely,

f_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=f_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}(M^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i,j}-f^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i}).

(12)

Indeed, given
f_{i}^{(s,\star)}
, solving (
12
) is equivalent to solving (
10
) with slightly modified constants.
The standard approach to solve the nonlinear equation (
12
) is to first solve moment equations for
n^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i}
,
\boldsymbol{u}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i}
, and
T^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i}
, which can then be used to define
M^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i,j}
in an explicit fashion; what remains is a linear solve for
f_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}

[
26
]
.
In the single species case, the update is trivial: due to conservation properties of the collision operator
(n_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1},\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1},T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1})=(n_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}},\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}},T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}})

[
12
]
.
In the multi-species case, taking moments of (
12
) and applying the definitions of
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}
and
T_{i,j}
from (
7
) gives the following algebraic system:

(Backward Euler)
.

If scheme (
12
) is applied for the kinetic densities, then, for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-n_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=0,

(13a)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\frac{\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\left(A_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-D_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\right)\boldsymbol{u}_{j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1},

(13b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{i}\frac{s_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-s_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\Delta t}+dn_{i}\frac{T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\Delta t}&=\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\left(B_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}-F_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\right)T_{j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\\

where

{N\times N}

\displaystyle[A]_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\lambda_{j,i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}},\qquad[B]_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=\frac{n_{i}n_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\lambda_{j,i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}},\qquad[S]_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=\left|\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\right|^{2},

(14b)

Remark 2.3
.

According to (

Proposition 2.4

(Discrete Conservation Laws)
.

The backward Euler scheme in (

) satisfies discrete versions of the conservation of total momentum and conservation of total energy.
Specifically,

\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}.

(15)

∎

.

The quantities

is independent of

When collision frequencies are constant or depend only on the number densities,
\lambda_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}=\lambda_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}}
, and solving (
13
) is straightforward
[
26
]
.
First, (
13b
) requires a linear solve for
\boldsymbol{u}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}_{i}
.
The new velocity can then be used to evaluate implicit source terms in (
13c
), leaving another linear solve to find each
T_{i}
.
However, for more realistic frequencies that depend on
T_{i}
, this approach no longer works.
Two options remain: (i) lag
\lambda_{i,j}
at the previous time step or (ii) solve (
13
) in a fully nonlinear fashion.

Since the first option may lead to loss of accuracy or instability, we focus here on the second option.
In terms of cost, the linear solve is trivial compared to the rest of the kinetic simulation.
The challenge is to establish an iterative algorithm that is provably convergent, even when
\Delta t/\varepsilon
is large.

3
Iterative Solver

3.1
Definition and Main Result

We propose the following Gauss-Seidel type (GST) iterative solver to solve the backward Euler system in (
13
).
We show below that, under a mild time step restriction which is independent of
\varepsilon
, the GST iteration has a fixed point that yields the unique solution to (
13
).

Definition 3.1

(GST method)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,0}=\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}
and
T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,0}=T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}
be given.
For
\ell\in\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
, let
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}
and
T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}
be given by

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\frac{\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\left(A_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell}-D_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell}\right)\boldsymbol{u}_{j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1},

(18a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{i}\frac{s_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}-s_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\Delta t}+dn_{i}\frac{T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}-T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\Delta t}&=\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\left(B_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell}-F_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell}\right)T_{j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}\\
&\hskip-142.26378pt+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}B_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell}\left[m_{j}\left(s_{j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}-S_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}\right)-m_{i}\left(s_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}-S_{i,j}^{\mathfrak{n}+1,\ell+1}\right)\right].\end{split}

(18b)

.

The eigenvalues of the matrices
Z^{\ell}=P^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(D^{\ell}-A^{\ell})P^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
and
\widehat{Z}^{\ell}=Q^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(F^{\ell}-B^{\ell})Q^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
satisfy

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=z_{0}<z_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}\leq z_{1}^{\ell}\leq\cdots\leq z_{N-1}^{\ell}\leq z_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}},

(26a)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\widehat{z}_{0}<\widehat{z}_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}\leq\widehat{z}_{1}^{\ell}\leq\cdots\leq\widehat{z}_{N-1}^{\ell}\leq\widehat{z}_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}},

(26b)

where

z_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{A_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}N}{\rho_{\max}},\qquad z_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{A_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}(N-1)}{\rho_{\min}},\qquad\widehat{z}_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{B_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}N}{n_{\max}},\qquad\widehat{z}_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{B_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}(N-1)}{{n_{\min}}}.

(27)

Proof.

The proof of this result follows as in
[
17
, Appendix B]
, and uses (
33
).
∎

Definition 3.5
.

For any quantity
K
that depends on
\ell
, define the difference operator
\Delta^{\ell}
by
\Delta^{\ell}K\equiv\Delta^{\ell}(K)=K^{\ell+1}-K^{\ell}
.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

The differences
\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}
and
\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{\eta}
, defined according to (
20
), satisfy

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}\|_{\textnormal{F}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2},

(28a)

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2}+C_{1}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{X}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2},

(28b)

where the positive constants
C_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}
,
C_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}}
, and
C_{1}^{\mathfrak{n}}
depend only on
\left\{N,m_{k},d_{k},n_{k},\boldsymbol{W}^{\mathfrak{n}},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathfrak{n}}\right\}
;

\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\Delta t\varepsilon}{(\varepsilon+\Delta tz_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}},\qquad\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\Delta t\varepsilon}{(\varepsilon+\Delta t\widehat{z}_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\max\{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}},\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\};

(29)

and
z_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}
and
\widehat{z}_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}
are defined in
3.4
.

As a corollary, we obtain the following sufficient condition on the time step to ensure the method is convergent.
Importantly, this condition does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Corollary 3.6.1
.

Given
\Delta t_{0}>0
, there exists
\Delta t_{1}
such that (i)
C\Delta t_{0}\leq\Delta t_{1}\leq\Delta t_{0}
for a constant
C\in(0,1]
that is independent of
\varepsilon
, and (ii) for this
\Delta t_{1}
,

C_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}+C_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}+C_{1}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathfrak{n}}<1,

(30)

thus ensuring convergence of the GST method.

Most of the remainder of the paper is dedicated to the proof of
Theorem

3.6
.
In the next subsection, we collect some useful properties of the method.

Section

4
is dedicated to proving the bound in (
28a
), and
Section

5
is dedicated to proving the bound in (
28b
); see
Theorems

4.9
and
5.15
, respectively.
The proof of

3.6.1
is contained in
Section

6
.

3.2
Basic properties of the GST method

Proposition 3.7
.

The iterates of the GST method satisfy the discrete version of the conservation of total momentum and total energy:

\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\ell+1}=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\ell+1}=\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}.

(31)

Proof.

The proof follows as in
2.4
, with
\mathfrak{n}+1
replaced by
\ell
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

The velocity and temperature iterates satisfy the bounds

\displaystyle\min_{j}\{u_{j,k}^{\mathfrak{n}}\}\leq\min_{j}\{u_{j,k}^{\ell+1}\}\leq\max_{j}\{u_{j,k}^{\ell+1}\}\leq\max_{j}\{u_{j,k}^{\mathfrak{n}}\},\qquad k\in\{1,\cdots,d\},

(32)

\displaystyle T_{i}^{\ell+1}\geq\min_{j}\{T_{j}^{\mathfrak{n}}\}\eqqcolon T_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}},\qquad i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},

(33)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\ell+1}\|_{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|_{2}\eqqcolon u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}},\qquad i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},

(34)

where
\left[\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}\right]_{k}=\max_{j}\{|u_{j,k}^{\mathfrak{n}}|\}
, for
k\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
.

Proof.

We first verify the lower bound in (
32
).
The velocity update (
18a
) is equivalent to

u_{i,k}^{\ell+1}=u_{i,k}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}\alpha_{j,i}\left(u_{j,k}^{\ell+1}-u_{i,k}^{\ell+1}\right),

(35)

where
\alpha_{j,i}=\frac{\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell}}
is independent of
\ell
(see
Lemma

C.1
).
Write
\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell}=\min_{j}\{u_{j,k}^{\ell}\}
.
Then, with
c_{i}^{\ell}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}\alpha_{j,i}
,

\displaystyle u_{i,k}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle\geq\underline{u}_{k}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}\alpha_{j,i}\left(\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell+1}\!-u_{i,k}^{\ell+1}\right)=\underline{u}_{k}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}c_{i}^{\ell}\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell+1}-\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}c_{i}^{\ell}u_{i,k}^{\ell+1}

(36a)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad u_{i,k}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon+\Delta tc_{i}^{\ell}}\underline{u}_{k}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta tc_{i}^{\ell}}{\varepsilon+\Delta tc_{i}^{\ell}}\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell+1}.

(36b)

Choose
i_{\star}\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
such that
u_{i_{\star},k}^{\ell+1}=\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell+1}
, and let
c_{\star}^{\ell}=c_{i_{\star}}^{\ell}
.
Then after some simple algebra, (
36b
) implies that

\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell+1}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon+\Delta tc_{\star}^{\ell}}\underline{u}_{k}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta tc_{\star}^{\ell}}{\varepsilon+\Delta tc_{\star}^{\ell}}\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell+1}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\underline{u}_{k}^{\ell+1}\geq\underline{u}_{k}^{\mathfrak{n}}.

(37)

The proof of the upper bound in (
32
) is similar, and
(
34
) follows easily from (
32
).

We next show (
33
).
Rearranging (
18b
) for the temperature gives

\displaystyle T_{i}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}\left(T_{j}^{\ell+1}-T_{i}^{\ell+1}\right)+\frac{m_{i}}{d}(s_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}-s_{i}^{\ell+1})+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon d}\frac{1}{n_{i}}\left[\boldsymbol{s}^{(2),\ell+1,\ell}\right]_{i}

\displaystyle\geq T_{i}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}\left(T_{j}^{\ell+1}-T_{i}^{\ell+1}\right),

(38)

where the inequality in (
38
) follows from
D.3
.
The rest of the proof of (
33
) proceeds in the same way as the velocity lower bound, starting at (
36a
).
∎

4
Analysis of Velocity Iterates

Let
\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ell}
be the null and range space components, respectively, of
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}
with respect to
Z^{\ell}
.
Since
Z^{\ell}
is symmetric, its null space and range space are orthogonal, and
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}=\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell}+\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ell}
.
The following property will be used throughout the section.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
17
]
, Section 4)
.

The null space
\mathcal{N}
and range space
\mathcal{R}
of the matrix
Z^{\ell}
are independent of
\ell
.
The null space has dimension one and is spanned by the vector

P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mathbf{1}
.

It will be convenient for the analysis of velocity terms to use the Frobenius norm

\|\boldsymbol{W}\|_{\textnormal{F}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}w_{i,j}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\boldsymbol{w}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\boldsymbol{W}_{(\cdot),j}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(39)

4.1
Velocity Iterates on Null Space

Lemma 4.2
.

The null space iterates are
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}=P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\boldsymbol{U}^{\infty}
, where
\boldsymbol{U}^{\infty}=\mathbf{1}(\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty})^{\top}
.
In particular, they are independent of
\ell
.
As a result,
\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}\|_{\textnormal{F}}=\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{R}})\|_{\textnormal{F}}
.

Proof.

According to
Lemma

4.1
,
\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell}=P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mathbf{1}(\boldsymbol{c}^{\ell})^{\top}
, for some
\boldsymbol{c}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}\perp\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{R}}
, it follows that
\mathbf{1}^{\top}P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}=\mathbf{1}^{\top}P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbf{1}^{\top}P\mathbf{1}(\boldsymbol{c}^{\ell})^{\top}.

Hence,

\boldsymbol{c}^{\ell}=\frac{(\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell})^{\top}P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}P\mathbf{1}}=\frac{(\boldsymbol{U}^{\ell})^{\top}P\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}P\mathbf{1}}=\frac{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\ell}}{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}}=\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty},

(40)

which is independent of
\ell
by
3.7
.
Thus,
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}=P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mathbf{1}(\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty})^{\top}=P^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\boldsymbol{U}^{\infty}
is independent of
\ell
.
As a consequence,

\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}\|_{\textnormal{F}}^{2}=\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{R}})\|_{\textnormal{F}}^{2}+\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}})\|_{\textnormal{F}}^{2}=\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{R}})\|_{\textnormal{F}}^{2}.

(41)

∎

4.2
Velocity Iterates on Range Space and Initial Norm Bounds

Definition 4.3
.

Let the columns of
V=[\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{v}_{N-1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(N-1)}
form an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{R}
.
For each
\ell
, let
\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell}=[\boldsymbol{a}^{\ell}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{a}^{\ell}_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}^{(N-1)\times d}
be the unique matrix of coefficients such that
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{R}}=V\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Given
V
as in
4.3
and any
\boldsymbol{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
,

\|V\|_{2}=1,\qquad\|V^{\top}\|_{2}\leq 1,\qquad\|V^{\top}\boldsymbol{Y}\|_{\textnormal{F}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{Y}\|_{\textnormal{F}},

(42)

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the induced (operator) 2-norm when the argument is an operator.
In particular, setting
\boldsymbol{Y}=\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{Y}=\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{A}
, respectively, gives

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{R}}\|_{\textnormal{F}}=\|V\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell}\|_{\textnormal{F}}=\|\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell}\|_{\textnormal{F}},

(43a)

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}\|_{\textnormal{F}}=\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{R}})\|_{\textnormal{F}}=\|V\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{A})\|_{\textnormal{F}}=\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{A}\|_{\textnormal{F}}.

(43b)

Based on (
43
), we seek to bound
\|\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell}\|_{\textnormal{F}}
, and the difference
\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{A}\|_{\textnormal{F}}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The matrix iterates
\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell+1}
satisfy

\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell+1}=M^{\ell}V^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}^{\mathfrak{n}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{A}=\Delta^{\ell-1}(M)V^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}^{\mathfrak{n}},

(44)

where
M^{\ell}=\left(I+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}V^{\top}Z^{\ell}V\right)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Write
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell+1}=\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ell+1}+\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell+1}=V\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell+1}+\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell+1}
in (
20a
)
and multiply on the left by
V^{\top}
:

V^{\top}\left(I+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}Z^{\ell}\right)\left(V\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell+1}+\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell+1}\right)=V^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}^{\mathfrak{n}}.

(45)

Since
Z^{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell+1}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and
V^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell+1}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N-1)\times d}
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\gamma^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon+\Delta tz_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}}
, where
z_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}}>0
is a lower bound on the positive eigenvalues of
Z^{\mathfrak{n}}
, defined in
3.4
.
Then

\|M^{\ell}\|_{2}\leq\gamma^{\mathfrak{n}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\|\Delta^{\ell-1}M\|_{2}\leq\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}{Z}\|_{2},

(46)

where
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}(\gamma^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}
.

Proof.

The first bound in (
46
) is verified in
Appendix

A
.
For the second, the identity
X^{-1}-Y^{-1}=X^{-1}(Y-X)Y^{-1}
with
X^{-1}=M^{\ell}
and
Y^{-1}=M^{\ell-1}
implies

\Delta^{\ell-1}M=-\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}M^{\ell}V^{\top}\Delta^{\ell-1}(Z)VM^{\ell-1}.

(47)

The result follows after taking operator norms.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

The matrix iterates
\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{A}^{\ell+1}\|_{\textnormal{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{n}\|\boldsymbol{W}^{n}\|_{\textnormal{F}},

(48a)

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{A}\|_{\textnormal{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|\boldsymbol{W}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|_{\textnormal{F}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}Z\|_{2},

(48b)

where
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}(\gamma^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}
.

Proof.

The result follows using
Lemmas

4.4
,
4.5
and
4.6
.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

The difference of matrices
Z^{\ell}
satisfy

\|\Delta^{\ell-1}Z\|_{2}\leq C_{Z}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2},\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad C_{Z}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{2(N+N^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})C_{A}^{\mathfrak{n}}}{\rho_{\min}n_{\min}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}.

(49)

Proof.

The proof is contained in
Appendix

B
.
∎

The iterations of the velocity terms satisfy the following bound:

where
C_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=C_{Z}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|\boldsymbol{W}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|_{\textnormal{F}}
and
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\Delta t\varepsilon}{(\varepsilon+\Delta tz_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}}
, with
C_{Z}^{\mathfrak{n}}
as in (
49
) and

defined in
3.4
.

Proof.

The result follows using (
43b
), (
48b
), and (
49
).

5
Analysis of Temperature Iterations

In this section, we analyze the temperature iterations; the results are summarized by
Theorem

5.15
.
The basic strategy is similar to that of the previous section, but there are important differences.
Denote the null space and range space of
\widehat{Z}^{\ell}
by
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
, respectively, and let
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell}+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ell}
, where
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell}\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ell}\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
.
As before, the fact that
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\perp\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
implies that
\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{R}}\|_{2}^{2}
.
However, unlike
\boldsymbol{W}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}
, the iterates
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}
depend on
\ell
, and thus
\|\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell+1}_{\mathcal{N}}-\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{2}
must be bounded.
In addition, there are source terms in the update for
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell}
that depend on velocity iterates and require technical bounds.

5.1
Temperature Iterates on Null Space

Lemma 5.1

(
[
17
]
, Sec. 4)
.

The null space
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
and range space
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
of
\widehat{Z}^{\ell}
are independent of
\ell
.
The null space has dimension one and is spanned by the vector
Q^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mathbf{1}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The null space iterations
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{N}}
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}})\right\|_{2}\leq C_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\left\|\Delta^{\ell-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}\right\|_{2},\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad

(51)

\displaystyle C_{0}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}b}{d}\rho_{\max}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}N^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}C_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}},\qquad b=\|Q^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mathbf{1}\|_{2}^{-1},\qquad\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\Delta t\varepsilon}{(\varepsilon+\Delta tz_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}}.

(52)

Proof.

Write
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}}^{\ell+1}=b_{0}^{\ell+1}\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},
where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}=bQ^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mathbf{1}
satisfies
\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}\|_{2}=1
.
Then

\left\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{N}})\right\|_{2}=\left\|\Delta^{\ell}(b_{0})\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}\right\|_{2}=\left|\Delta^{\ell}(b_{0})\right|.

(53)

Now multiply (
20b
) by
\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}^{\top}
.
Since
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\perp\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
, the left-hand side gives

\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}^{\top}\left(I+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\widehat{Z}^{\ell}\right)(b_{0}^{\ell+1}\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\ell+1})=b_{0}^{\ell+1}\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}^{\top}\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}=b_{0}^{\ell+1}.

(54)

Meanwhile, the right-hand side gives

\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathfrak{n}}\right)_{2}+\frac{1}{d}\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},Q^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\ell+1}\right)\right)_{2}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{d}\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},Q^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\boldsymbol{s}^{(2),\ell+1,\ell}\right)_{2}\\
=\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathfrak{n}}\right)_{2}+\frac{1}{d}\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},Q^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\ell+1}\right)\right)_{2},

(55)

where the second source term vanishes due to the symmetry of
B
.
Specifically, using (
22b
) and the definition of
\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0}
,

\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},Q^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\boldsymbol{s}^{(2),\ell+1,\ell}\right)_{2}=b\sum_{i,j}B_{i,j}^{\ell}\left[m_{j}(s_{j}^{\ell+1}-S_{i,j}^{\ell+1})-m_{i}(s_{i}^{\ell+1}-S_{i,j}^{\ell+1})\right]=0.

(56)

Combining (
54
) and (
55
) gives

b_{0}^{\ell+1}=\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{0},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathfrak{n}}\right)_{2}+\frac{b}{d}\left(\mathbf{1},\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\right)_{2}-\frac{b}{d}\left(\mathbf{1},\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\ell+1}\right)_{2}.

(57)

Therefore, with the definition of
\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}
in (
22a
),

\begin{split}\left|\Delta^{\ell}(b_{0})\right|&=\frac{b}{d}\left|\left(\mathbf{1},\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}})\right)_{2}\right|\leq\frac{b}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\;\left\|\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell+1}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell}\right\|_{2}^{2}\;\right|\\
&\leq\frac{b}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{w}_{i})\right\|_{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell+1}+\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{2b}{d}\rho_{\max}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{w}_{i})\right\|_{2}\\
&\leq\frac{2b}{d}\rho_{\max}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}\sqrt{N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{w}_{i})\right\|^{2}_{2}}=\frac{2b}{d}\rho_{\max}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}N^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\Delta^{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}\|_{\textnormal{F}}\\
&\leq\frac{2b}{d}\rho_{\max}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}N^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}C_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\left\|\Delta^{\ell-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}\right\|_{2},\end{split}

(58)

where the last line follows from
Theorem

4.9
.
∎

5.2
Bounding Source Terms in the Temperature Equation

Lemma 5.3
.

The vector
\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\ell}=\left(|\boldsymbol{w}_{1}^{\ell}|^{2},\cdots,|\boldsymbol{w}_{N}^{\ell}|^{2}\right)^{\top}
satisfies the bounds

\|\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\ell}\|_{2}\leq(u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}\|P\mathbf{1}\|_{2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}})\|_{2}\leq C_{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2},

(59)

where
C_{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=2\rho_{\max}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}}C_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}
and
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\frac{\Delta t\varepsilon}{(\varepsilon+\Delta tz_{\min}^{\mathfrak{n}})^{2}}
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from the bound on the velocity terms (
34
) in
3.8
:

\|\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}^{\ell}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i}[\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}}]_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\ell}\|_{2}^{4}\leq(u_{\max}^{\mathfrak{n}})^{4}\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{2}.

(60)

For the second inequality,

\begin{split}\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{W}})\|_{2}^{2}&=\sum_{i}\left|\left(\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{w}_{i}),\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell+1}+\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell}\right)_{2}\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{i}\|\Delta^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{w}_{i})\|_{2}^{2}\;\|\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell+1}+\boldsymbol{w}_{i}^{\ell}\|_{2}^{2}\\

6.2

Denote the zeros of

, defined in (

\Delta t_{\pm}=\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathfrak{n}}-2rz}{2rz^{2}}\varepsilon\pm\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4rz}{C_{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathfrak{n}}}}}{2rz^{2}}\varepsilon.

Since the

are complex conjugates, which implies that,

Theorem 6.4
.

Given
\Delta t_{0}>0

\Delta t_{1}
such that (i)

The only nontrivial case to consider is when

If

or
\Delta t_{+}\leq\Delta t_{0}

8
Conclusions

In this paper, we have presented a simple iterative approach for solving a set of implicit moment equations as part of a larger IMEX-based strategy for computing solutions of a multi-species BGK model.
The main challenge is that the collision frequencies in the moment equations themselves depend on the moments in a complicated way.
The key contribution is a rigorous contraction mapping proof, which shows that the iterative method converges to a unique solution under reasonable time steps that do not degenerate to zero when the collision parameter
\varepsilon\to 0
.
This implicit solve is a key component in the IMEX discretization of the multi-species BGK model, the implementation of which is done in slab geometry but closed in three-dimensional velocity space using a Chu reduction technique
[
11
]
.

In future work, we will examine similar problems for multi-species ellipsoidal statistical models (ES-BGK)
[
19
,
2
]
that, unlike standard BGK models, recover in highly collisional regimes, the correct Prandtl number for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
We will also extend the current code structure used here to generate results for more general problems in multi-dimensional domains.
We will also explore a Fokker-Planck analog of the BGK model, often referred to as the Lenard-Bernstein model
[
21
]
, that is useful in plasma physics simulations
[
13
,
14
,
29
]
.

\|\Delta^{\ell-1}A\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta^{\ell-1}D\|_{2}
, and using
Lemma

B.2
in the second line below gives,

\begin{split}&\|\Delta^{\ell-1}Z\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{\min}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}(D-A)\|_{2}\leq\frac{2}{\rho_{\min}}\|\Delta^{\ell-1}D\|_{2}\\

where the last line follow from the relation

Appendix C
Invariance of
\alpha_{i,j}
and
\beta_{i,j}

Lemma C.1
.

For any
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, let

\alpha_{i,j}^{\ell}=\frac{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\beta_{i,j}^{\ell}=\frac{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell}},

(120)

where (cf. (
8
))
\lambda^{\ell}_{i,j}=c_{i,j}n_{j}\Psi^{\ell}_{i,j}
.
Then

\alpha_{i,j}^{\ell+1}-\alpha_{i,j}^{\ell}=0=\beta_{i,j}^{\ell+1}-\beta_{i,j}^{\ell}.

(121)

Proof.

Only the first equality is shown.
The other case is similar.
Direct calculation gives

\alpha_{i,j}^{\ell+1}-\alpha_{i,j}^{\ell}=\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\left(\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell+1}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell}-\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell+1}\right)}{(\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell+1}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell+1})(\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell})},

(122)

It is easy to verify that the product
\lambda_{i,j}^{\ell+1}\lambda_{j,i}^{\ell}
is symmetric in
i,j
.
Hence the difference in (
122
) is identically zero.
∎

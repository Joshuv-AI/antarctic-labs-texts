Gamma vectors of partitioned permutohedra
Tatsuya Horiguchi, Mikiya Masuda, Takashi Sato, John Shareshian, Jongbaek Song

Abstract
. We determine that γ \gamma -vectors of partitioned permutohedra, thereby generalizing a result of Foata and Schützenberger. Our result is closely related to a result of Athanasiadis on the representation of the symmetric group on the cohomology of the permutohedral variety. We explain how to derive Athanasiadis’ result from ours and vice versa.

1.
Introduction

We continue the study of partitioned permutohedra that was initiated by some of the present authors in
[
14
]
. We examine the
\gamma
-vectors of these polytopes, obtaining a generalization of a result of Foata and Schützenberger found in
[
8
]
and recovering a representation-theoretic result of Athanasiadis found in
[
1
]
.

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by permuting coordinates. This action preserves each affine hyperplane
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
consisting of vectors with constant coordinate sum. Fix
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in E
such that the entries
p_{i}
are pairwise distinct. The
permutohedron

P_{n}
is the convex hull of the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-orbit of
p
. (The combinatorial type of
P_{n}
does not depend on the choice of
p
.)
For
k\in[n-1]
, we define the halfspace

H(k)^{\leq}\coloneqq\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in E\mid x_{k}\leq x_{k+1}\}.

The
partitioned permutohedron
associated to
K\subseteq[n-1]
is

P_{n}(K)\coloneqq P_{n}\cap\bigcap_{k\in K}H(k)^{\leq}.

It was shown in
[
14
]
that the polytopes
P_{n}(K)
are all simple of dimension
n-1
. Therefore, if we write
f_{i}
for the number of
i
-dimensional faces of
P_{n}(K)
and set

h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_{i}t^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{i}(t-1)^{i},

then
h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)
is palindromic, that is,
h_{i}=h_{n-1-i}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
. (This is the content of the
Dehn–Sommerville equations
, see for example
[
12
, Section 9.2]
.) It follows that there exist uniquely determined
\gamma_{n,j,K}\in\mathbb{Z}
(
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
) such that

h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\gamma_{n,j,K}t^{j}(1+t)^{n-1-2j}.

We show here that each
\gamma_{n,j,K}
is non-negative by exhibiting a set
\widehat{W^{K}}(j)\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that
\left|\widehat{W^{K}}(j)\right|=\gamma_{n,j,K}
.

More precisely, let
W_{K}
be the subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
generated by the set of transpositions
\{(k,k+1):k\in K\}
. Each coset of
W_{K}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
contains a unique shortest representative with respect to the usual length function (see Section
2
for definitions of statistics on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
). Write
W^{K}
for the set of such shortest representatives. Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
be the set of all
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with no double descent and no final descent and set

\widehat{W^{K}}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\cap W^{K}.

Write
\des(w)
for the number of descents of
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and for
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
, set

\widehat{W^{K}}(j)\coloneqq\{w\in\widehat{W^{K}}\mid\des(w)=j\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
j\in\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
,

\gamma_{n,j,K}=|\widehat{W^{K}}(j)|.

Equivalently,

h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)=\sum_{w\in\widehat{W^{K}}}t^{\des(w)}(1+t)^{n-1-2\des(w)}.

In the main result of this article (Theorem
3.2
), we also verify that

\gamma_{n,j,K}=|\{w\in\widetilde{W^{K}}\mid\des(w)=j\}|,

where
\widetilde{W^{K}}
denotes the permutations in
W^{K}
with no double descent and no
initial
descent.

Remark 1.2
.

It is known that
h_{P_{n}}(t)
is the Eulerian polynomial,

h_{P_{n}}(t)=A_{n}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}t^{\des(w)}.

When
K=\emptyset
, we have
W_{K}=\{1\}
and
W^{K}=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. In this case, Theorem
1.1
specializes to

A_{n}(t)=\sum_{W\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}}t^{\des(w)}(1+t)^{n-1-2\des(w)},

which was proved by Foata and Schützenberger in
[
8
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

It was shown in
[
14
]
that each
P_{n}(K)
is a simple flag polytope. It was conjectured by Gal in
[
11
]
that when the
h
-polynomial of a simple flag
d
-polytope is expanded in terms of the basis
t^{j}(1+t)^{d-2j}
, each coefficient is nonnegative. Theorem
1.1
confirms that Gal’s Conjecture holds for partitioned permutohedra.

If the chosen point
p
has integer coordinates then
P_{n}(K)
is a simple rational polytope and thus determines a toric variety
X_{n}(K)
of complex dimension
n-1
. By work of Danilov and of Jurkiewicz (see
[
6
, Theorem 10.8 and Remark 10.9]
),

h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\dim H^{2i}(X_{n}(K);\mathbb{C})t^{i}.

By construction,
P_{n}
admits an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
action. With
X_{n}=X_{n}(\emptyset)
, this action determines an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
representation on each
H^{2i}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})
. Such representations have been studied closely, for example by Procesi in
[
22
]
, by Stanley in
[
27
]
, by Stembridge in
[
28
,
29
]
, and by Dolgachev and Lunts in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.1 of
[
14
]
says that the cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(X_{n}(K);\mathbb{C})
is isomorphic (as a graded ring) to the ring
H^{\ast}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})^{W_{K}}
of
W_{K}
-invariant elements of
H^{\ast}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})
. Every
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-module
V
is completely determined by the fixed-point submodules
V^{W_{K}}
as
K
ranges over all subsets of
[n-1]
. (Indeed, one needs only one representative from each
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-conjugacy class of parabolic subgroups.) Since each
h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)
is palindromic, we recover from Theorem
1.1
the already-known isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-modules

H^{2i}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})\cong_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}H^{2(n-1-i)}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})

for
0\leq i\leq n-1
. (See
[
27
, Corollary 4.4]
and
[
29
, Proposition 12]
.) We should therefore hope for a nice expansion of the representation
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
H^{\ast}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})
using the palindromic polynomials
t^{j}(1+t)^{n-1-2j}
. Such an expansion was given by Athanasiadis, who expressed the irreducible decomposition of this representation in the desired form in
[
1
]
. (We remark here that Stembridge gave in
[
29
, Theorem 4.2]
a combinatorial formula for the irreducible decomposition of each
H^{2k}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})
. A different such formula is a special case of
[
24
, Theorem 6.3]
.)

Write
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}
for the set of all standard Young tableaux with
n
boxes and arbitrary shape and
\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}
for the set of all such tableaux having no double descent and no descent at
n-1
. Given a tableau
Q
, write
\lambda(Q)
for the shape of
Q
. (See Section
8
for this and other definitions.) With
R_{n,j}
denoting the
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-module
H^{2j}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})
, Athanasiadis proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

(
Athanasiadis, see
[
1
, Corollary 2.41, Equation (90)]
)
For
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
,

R_{n,j}\cong_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\\
{\des(Q)=j}\end{subarray}}S^{\lambda(Q)}.

Equivalently,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H^{2k}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})t^{k}\cong_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sum_{Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}}S^{\lambda(Q)}t^{\des(Q)}(1+t)^{n-1-2\des(Q)}.

As we have discussed, Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
carry the same information; if one knows the dimensions of the fixed point spaces for all parabolic subgroups, then one knows the representation and the converse is clear. After proving Theorem
1.1
directly (without using Theorem
1.4
) in Section
7
, we show in Section
9
how to derive each of the theorems quickly assuming the other.

2.
Preliminaries

We review here some standard definitions and known facts from algebraic combinatorics. We also introduce some notation. It is reasonable to skip much of this section and refer back as necessary, but note the use of
\widehat{X}
(resp.
\widetilde{X}
) to indicate the set of all elements of
X
having no double descent and no final descent (resp. no initial descent) when
X
is a set on whose elements descents are defined.

2.1.
Words, permutations, and descents

By a
word of length
n
we mean an ordered list
v=v(1)\cdots v(n)
of
n
positive integers. So, the set of words of length
n
is
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}
. A
descent
of
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}
is any
i\in[n-1]
such that
v(i)>v(i+1)
. We write
\Des(v)
for the set of descents of
v
and
\des(v)
for
|\Des(v)|
. We say that
v
has a
double descent
if there is some
i\in[n-2]
such that
\{i,i+1\}\subseteq\Des(v)
and that
v
has an
initial descent
if
1\in\Des(v)
.
Similarly, we say that
v
has a
final descent
if
n-1\in\Des(v)
. The
multiplicity
of
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
in
v
is the number of
j\in[n]
such that
v(j)=i
, which will be denoted by
m_{i}(v)
. For example,
v=4223185
is a word of length seven,
\Des(v)=\{1,4,6\}
,
\des(v)=3
, and (for example)
m_{2}(v)=2
and
m_{8}(v)=1
. In this case,
v
has no double descent but does have an initial descent and a final descent. We write
\widetilde{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}
(resp.
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}
) for the set of all words of length
n
having no double descent and no initial descent (resp. final descent).

For a subset
X
of
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}
, we write
\widetilde{X}
and
\widehat{X}
for
X\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}
and
X\cap\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}
, respectively.
For example, a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, written in one-line notation, can be thought of as a word of length
n
in which
m_{i}(v)=1
for all
i\in[n]
.
Then
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
is the set of permutations with no double descent and no initial descent.

2.2.
Parabolic subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

A subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is
parabolic
if it is generated by transpositions. Every parabolic subgroup is conjugate in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
to a
standard parabolic subgroup
, which is a subgroup generated by some adjacent transpositions
(k,k+1)
. For a subset
K\subseteq[n-1]
, we write
W_{K}
for the standard parabolic subgroup generated by
\{(k,k+1):k\in K\}
. If
1\leq i<j\leq n
then
i,j
lie in the same
W_{K}
-orbit on
[n]
if and only if
\ell\in K
whenever
i\leq\ell<j
.

The
length

\ell(w)
of
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the smallest number of adjacent transpositions whose product is
w
, which is equal to the number of pairs
(i,j)
from
[n]
such that
i<j
and
w(i)>w(j)
(see for example
[
3
, Proposition 1.5.2]
). It is known (see for example
[
3
, Proposition 2.4.4]
) that for
K\subseteq[n-1]
, each left coset
C
of
W_{K}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
contains some
w
such that
\ell(w)<\ell(x)
for all
x\in C\setminus\{w\}
. We compose permutations from left to right.
So, applying
x\in wW_{K}
to
[n]
is the same as applying
w
and then permuting elements within
W_{K}
-orbits. As the length of
w
is minimal in
wW_{K}
, it must be the case that
i
appears to the left of
j
in
w
whenever
i<j
and
i,j
are in the same
W_{K}
-orbit. We write
W^{K}
for the set of all minimal length coset representatives for
W_{K}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
In other words, if we decompose a subset
K\subseteq[n-1]
into maximal consecutive strings
K=[a_{1},b_{1}]\sqcup\cdots\sqcup[a_{m},b_{m}]
, then
W^{K}
is the set of elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
satisfying the following condition:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\textrm{for each $i$ with $1\leq i\leq m$, the numbers $a_{i},a_{i}+1,\cdots,b_{i},b_{i}+1$}\\
&\textrm{appear in the one-line notation of $w$ as a subsequence in increasing order.}\end{split}

2.3.
Fans, polytopes, toric varieties, and
h
-polynomials

We will not discuss at length the rich theory of toric varieties and related combinatorial objects, which can be found in various textbooks, including
[
18
]
. Rather, we record a little more than we need herein. A
d
-dimensional convex polytope
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
simple
if each vertex of
P
is contained in exactly
d
faces of dimension
(d-1)
. Given a simple polytope
P
, we write
f_{i}=f_{i}(P)
for the number of
i
-dimensional faces of
P
(
0\leq i\leq d
). The
f
-polynomial
of
P
is

f_{P}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}t^{i},

and the
h
-polynomial of
P
is

h_{P}(t)\coloneqq f_{P}(t-1).

So, for example, if
P
is a
4
-gon in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
then
f_{P}(t)=4+4t+t^{2}
and
h_{P}(t)=1+2t+t^{2}
.

Given a simple polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with rational vertices, the fan
\Delta_{P}
dual to
P
is simplicial and determines a toric variety
X_{P}
. The key point here, mentioned in the introduction, is the result proved in the projective case by Jurkiewicz and in the general case by Danilov,

\sum_{k=0}^{d}\dim H^{2k}(X_{P};\mathbb{C})t^{k}=h_{P}(t).

We remark that
X_{P}
has trivial rational cohomology in odd degrees, hence
h_{P}(t)
tells us everything about the Betti numbers of
X_{P}
.

2.4.
Partitioned permutohedra

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
be pairwise distinct real numbers. The
permutohedron

P_{n}(p)
associated to
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
is the convex hull of the
n!
points
(p_{w(1)},\ldots,p_{w(n)})
, with
w
running through
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. We note that
P_{n}(p)
lies in the affine hyperplane
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
consisting of vectors whose entries sum to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
. It is also known that
P_{n}(p)
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional simple polytope. Under our assumption that the coordinates in
p
are pairwise distinct, the combinatorial type of
P_{n}(p)
does not depend on the choice of
p
. Therefore, we write
P_{n}
for
P_{n}(p)
for simplicity. The toric variety associated with
P_{n}
is called the
permutohedral variety
.

For each
k\in[n-1]
, we set

H(k)^{\leq}\coloneqq\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in E\mid x_{k}\leq x_{k+1}\}.

The
partitioned permutohedron

P_{n}(K)
associated to
K\subseteq[n-1]
is

P_{n}(K):=P_{n}\cap\bigcap_{k\in K}H(k)^{\leq}.

The polytopes
P_{n}(K)
were first studied in
[
14
]
. It is known that, in addition to being simple,
P_{n}(K)
is a flag polytope (see
[
14
, Proposition 4.6]
). Note that
P_{n}(\emptyset)=P_{n}
. On the other hand,
P_{n}([n-1])
is combinatorially equivalent to a cube. Using work of Precup on Hessenberg varieties in
[
20
]
, the following result was proved in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
14
]
, Proposition 7.4)
.

Given
K\subseteq[n-1]
, set

W(K):=\{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\mid w^{-1}(k)-w^{-1}(k+1)\leq 1\mbox{ for all }k\in K\}.

For each such
K
,

h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)=\sum_{w\in W(K)}t^{\des(w)}.

3.
Main theorem

The main result of this paper gives a combinatorial formula for expanding
h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)
in terms of the gamma basis
\{t^{j}(1+t)^{n-1-2j}\}_{0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}
.
Foata and Schützenberger proved the following result.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

We can write the Eulerian polynomial as

\displaystyle A_{n}(t)=h_{P_{n}}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\gamma_{n,j}\,t^{j}(1+t)^{n-1-2j},

where the coefficient
\gamma_{n,j}
is equal to

\gamma_{n,j}=|\{w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mid\des(w)=j\}|=|\{w\in\widehat{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mid\des(w)=j\}|.

Note that the last equality above follows from the involution
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
sending
w
to the permutation
w^{\ast}
satisfying
w^{\ast}(i)=n+1-w(n+1-i)
for all
i\in[n]
.
Our main theorem generalizes Theorem
3.1
as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

For a subset
K\subseteq[n-1]
, the
h
-polynomial of the partitioned permutohedron
P_{n}(K)
satisfies

\displaystyle h_{P_{n}(K)}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\gamma_{n,j,K}\,t^{j}(1+t)^{n-1-2j},

where for each
j
,

\gamma_{n,j,K}=|\{w\in\widetilde{W^{K}}\mid\des(w)=j\}|=|\{w\in\widehat{W^{K}}\mid\des(w)=j\}|.

8.3.
Modules for
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
, the Frobenius characteristic, and Kostka numbers

The irreducible modules of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
are parameterized by partitions of
n
. We write
S^{\lambda}
for the module associated to the partition
\lambda
. Every
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-module is a direct sum of simple modules, and each
S^{\lambda}
is a summand in some permutation module
M^{\mu}
. Here
\mu=\mu(K)
for some
K\subseteq[n-1]
and
M^{\mu}
has a
\mathbb{C}
-basis indexed by the cosets of
W_{K}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. The elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
permute this basis as they permute the cosets by translation. The
Kostka number

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,\mu}
is the number of semistandard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
and content
\mu
. The first equality in the next theorem is well-known (see for example
[
26
, Theorem 7.18.7]
and the second follows directly from Frobenius reciprocity (see for example
[
16
, Lemma 5.2]
).

Theorem 8.2
.

The Kostka number
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,\mu}
is equal to the number of summands isomorphic to
S^{\lambda}
in a direct sum decomposition of
M^{\mu}
into simple submodules, which is equal in turn to the dimension
\dim(S^{\lambda})^{W_{K}}
of the subspace of
S^{\lambda}
consisting of vectors fixed by a parabolic subgroup
W_{K}
satisfying
\mu(K)=\mu
.

The
Frobenius characteristic

\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}
is a map from the direct sum of the spaces of virtual modules for the group algebras
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
(
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
) to the algebra of symmetric functions. We do not need to know anything about this map other than that for each
n
it determines an isomorphism between the space of virtual
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-modules and the space of homogeneous degree
n
symmetric functions. All of this is described (using character-theoretic language) in
[
26
, Section 7.18]
.

8.4.
Graded
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-representation formula for gamma vectors

Consider the permutohedral variety
X_{n}
, which is the toric variety associated with the normal fan
\Delta_{n}
of a permutohedron
P_{n}
.
Since the fan
\Delta_{n}
is invariant under the reflection action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, this induces an action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on the permutohedral variety
X_{n}
, which determines a representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
H^{\ast}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})
.
This representation is palindromic as noted in
[
27
, Corollary 4.4]
and
[
29
, Proposition 12]
, which implies that there exist virtual modules
R_{n,j}
(
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
) such that

(8.1)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H^{2k}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})t^{k}\cong_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}R_{n,j}t^{j}(1+t)^{n-1-2j},

where
\cong_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
denotes isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-modules.
Athanasiadis proved the following result.

Theorem 8.3
.

(
[
1
, Corollary 2.41]
)

For
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
,

R_{n,j}\cong_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\\
{\des(Q)=j}\end{subarray}}S^{\lambda(Q)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\\
{\des(Q)=j}\end{subarray}}S^{\lambda(Q)}.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H^{2k}(X_{n};\mathbb{C})t^{k}

\displaystyle\cong_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sum_{Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}}S^{\lambda(Q)}t^{\des(Q)}(1+t)^{n-1-2\des(Q)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}}S^{\lambda(Q)}t^{\des(Q)}(1+t)^{n-1-2\des(Q)}.

For the reader’s convenience, we here give a proof of Theorem
8.3
.
We begin with the result of Gessel (cf.
[
25
, Theorem 7.3]
). Given
v\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}
, we define
\mathbf{x}^{v}\coloneqq\prod_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{m_{i}(v)}
. So, the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{v}
tells us how many times each letter
i
appears in the word
v
. As stated in
[
25
]
,

(8.2)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}(H^{2k}(X_{n};\mathbb{C}))t^{k}=\sum_{v\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}}\mathbf{x}^{v}t^{\des(v)}(1+t)^{n-1-2\des(v)}.

It follows from (
8.2
) and the definition of
R_{n,j}
in (
8.1
) that for
j\in[n-1]

(8.3)

\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}(R_{n,j})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}\\
\des(v)=j\end{subarray}}\mathbf{x}^{v}.

Given a Young tableau
T
, we write
\mathbf{x}^{T}
for the monomial
\prod_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{m_{i}(T)}
, where
m_{i}(T)
is the number of boxes in
T
filled with
i
. For any partition
\lambda
of
n
,

\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}(S^{\lambda})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}}\mathbf{x}^{T},

see for example
[
26
, Definition 7.10.1 and (7.86)]
. Therefore, for
Q\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}
,

(8.4)

\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}(S^{\lambda(Q)})=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda(Q)}}\mathbf{x}^{P}.

We conclude now that for
j\in[n-1]

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\\
\des(Q)=j\end{subarray}}\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}(S^{\lambda(Q)})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(P,Q)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}\times\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\\
\lambda(P)=\lambda(Q)\\
\des(Q)=j\end{subarray}}\mathbf{x}^{P}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{n}}\\
\des(v)=j\end{subarray}}\mathbf{x}^{v}=\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}(R_{n,j}),

the first equality following from (
8.4
), the second from Proposition
8.1
and the third from (
8.3
). As
\mathsf{c}\mathsf{h}
is an isomorphism, the first half of Theorem
8.3
follows.
It remains to show the following equality

(8.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\\
{\des(Q)=j}\end{subarray}}S^{\lambda(Q)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\\
{\des(Q)=j}\end{subarray}}S^{\lambda(Q)}.

For this, we consider the evacuation
Q^{\ast}
for
Q\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
We will not describe the evacuation, but we will describe its key consequences.
We refer the reader to
[
10
, Appendix A.1]
for the details.
The evacuation
Q^{\ast}
for
Q\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}
is the standard tableau of entries
i^{\ast}\coloneqq n+1-i\ (i\in[n])
with the same shape
\lambda(Q)
.
For
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, let
w^{\ast}
be the permutation defined by
w^{\ast}(i)=n+1-w(n+1-i)
for all
i\in[n]
.
It is known that if
\mathbf{R}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{K}(w)=(P,Q)
, then
\mathbf{R}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{K}(w^{\ast})=(P^{\ast},Q^{\ast})
.
This property implies that

\displaystyle\Des(Q^{\ast})=\Des(w^{\ast})=\{(i+1)^{\ast}\mid i\in\Des(w)\}=\{(i+1)^{\ast}\mid i\in\Des(Q)\}.

The equality above shows that the assigement by sending
Q
to
Q^{*}
gives a one-to-one correspondence

\displaystyle\{Q\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\mid\des(Q)=j\}\rightarrow\{Q\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}_{n}}\mid\des(Q)=j\},

which yields (
8.5
).

Improved Exponent for Marton’s Conjecture in 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
Jyun-Jie Liao

Abstract
A conjecture of Marton, widely known as the polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa conjecture, was recently proved by Gowers, Green, Manners and Tao for any bounded-torsion Abelian group G G . In this paper we show a few simple modifications that improve their bound in G = 𝔽 2 n G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} . Specifically, for G = 𝔽 2 n G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , they proved that any set A ⊆ G A\subseteq G with | A + A | ≤ K ​ | A | \left|A+A\right|\leq K\left|A\right| can be covered by at most 2 ​ K C 2K^{C} cosets of a subgroup H H of G G of cardinality at most | A | \left|A\right| , with C = 12 C=12 . In this paper we prove the same statement for C = 9 C=9 .

In this section we briefly recap the proof of entropic Marton’s conjecture in
[
GGMT23
]
. The outline of the proof is as follows. Fix two starting random variables
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

\tau_{A}(\cdot),\tau_{B}(\cdot)
be some functions over random variables in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, so that
\tau_{A}(\mathbf{X}),\tau_{B}(\mathbf{X})
denote certain “divergence” of a random variable
\mathbf{X}
from
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, and that
\tau_{A},\tau_{B}
are both invariant under translation. To prove the entropic version (

3
) we should choose
\tau_{A}(\mathbf{X}):=\mathrm{d}[\mathbf{B};\mathbf{X}]
and
\tau_{B}(\mathbf{X}):=\mathrm{d}[\mathbf{A};\mathbf{X}]
. The overall strategy is to start with
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{A},\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{B}
, and keep updating
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
so that
\mathrm{d}[\mathbf{X};\mathbf{Y}]
always becomes strictly smaller than in the previous round. At the same time, we want to make sure that
\tau_{A}(\mathbf{X})
and
\tau_{B}(\mathbf{Y})
do not grow too much in every round. Recall that
\mathrm{d}[\mathbf{X};\mathbf{Y}]
is non-negative, and is
0
if and only if there exists a subspace
V
such that
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
are both uniform over some translates of
V
. (See, e.g.,
[
Tao10
, Theorem 1.11]
.) Therefore, when
\mathrm{d}[\mathbf{X};\mathbf{Y}]
eventually decreases to
0
, if at the same time we can show that
\tau_{A}(\mathbf{X})=\tau_{A}(\mathbf{U}_{V})
and
\tau_{B}(\mathbf{Y})=\tau_{B}(\mathbf{U}_{V})
are not too large, then we are done.

To update
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}

\mathbf{X}_{1},\mathbf{X}_{2}

\mathbf{X}
, and
\mathbf{Y}_{1},\mathbf{Y}_{2}
be independent copies of
\mathbf{Y}
(that are also independent of

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{X}_{1}+\mathbf{Y}_{1},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{T}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{X}_{2}+\mathbf{Y}_{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{X}_{1}+\mathbf{Y}_{2},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{X}_{2}+\mathbf{Y}_{1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{X}_{1}+\mathbf{X}_{2},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{Y}_{1}+\mathbf{Y}_{2}.

The typical next choice of
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})

(\mathbf{T},\overline{\mathbf{T}})
or
(\mathbf{W},\overline{\mathbf{W}})

(\mathbf{X}_{1}|\mathbf{W},\mathbf{Y}_{1}|\overline{\mathbf{W}})
.

In this work, our improvement in

Lemma 9

Lemma 10

Appendix A
Basic Information Measures

In this section we briefly review some basic information measures and some of their properties that we use in this paper. Their proofs are either straightforward or can be found in textbooks of information theory, e.g.
[
CT12
]
.

Shannon Entropy.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be any random variable on a finite set. The (Shannon) entropy of
\mathbf{X}
is defined as
\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}):=\sum_{x\in\Supp(\mathbf{X})}-\Pr\left[\mathbf{X}=x\right]\log(\Pr\left[\mathbf{X}=x\right])
. Note that
\mathrm{H}
only depends on the distribution of
\mathbf{X}
, so we also treat it as a function on distributions. We will use the following simple properties.

•

\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})\leq\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X})+\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{Y})
, and
\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X})+\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{Y})
iff
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
are independent.

•

\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X})\leq\log(\Supp(\mathbf{X}))
.

•

\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X})=\mathrm{H}(f(\mathbf{X}))
for any
f
that is injective on
\Supp(\mathbf{X})
.

•

For distribution
P,Q
over a finite set
S
and any
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
\mathrm{H}(\lambda P+(1-\lambda)Q)\geq\lambda\mathrm{H}(P)+(1-\lambda)\mathrm{H}(Q)
. In other words,
\mathrm{H}
is concave.

Conditional Entropy.

For joint random variables
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z})
we define the conditional entropy
\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Z})
to be
\Ex_{z\sim\mathbf{Z}}\left[\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}|_{\mathbf{Z}=z})\right]
. We need the following properties.

•

\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Z})=\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z})-\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{Z})

•

\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Z})\leq\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X})

•

\mathrm{H}(f(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z})\mid g(\mathbf{Z}))=\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Z})
for any
g
injective on
\Supp(\mathbf{Z})
and any
f(\cdot,z)
that is injective on
\Supp(\mathbf{X})
for every
z\in\Supp(\mathbf{Z})
.

Mutual Information.

The mutual information between two correlated random variables
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
is defined as
\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y}):=\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X})+\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{Y})-\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
. For
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z})
we also define the conditional mutual entropy
\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathbf{Z}):=\Ex_{z\sim\mathbf{Z}}\left[\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}|_{\mathbf{Z}=z},\mathbf{Y}|_{\mathbf{Z}=z})\right]
. We have the following properties.

•

\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y})\geq 0
, and
\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y})=0
iff
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
are independent.

•

\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{Y}:\mathbf{X})

•

\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X})-\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Y})
. Similarly
\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathbf{Z})=\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Z})-\mathrm{H}(\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{Z})
.

•

\mathrm{I}(f(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}):\mathbf{Y}\mid g(\mathbf{Z}))=\mathrm{I}(\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathbf{Z})
for any
g
injective on
\Supp(\mathbf{Z})
and any
f(\cdot,z)
that is injective on
\Supp(\mathbf{X})
for every
z\in\Supp(\mathbf{Z})
.

Kullback-Leibler Divergence.

For distributions
P,Q
over a finite
S
s.t.
\Supp(P)\subseteq\Supp(Q)
, the Kullback-Leibler divergence is defined as
D_{\mathrm{KL}}({P}\;\|\;{Q})=\sum_{x\in\Supp(P)}P(x)\log(P(x)/Q(x))
. In addition, for
\Supp(P)\subsetneq\Supp(Q)
define
D_{\mathrm{KL}}({P}\;\|\;{Q})=\infty
. We also abuse notation and define
D_{\mathrm{KL}}({\mathbf{X}}\;\|\;{\mathbf{Y}})=D_{\mathrm{KL}}({P_{\mathbf{X}}}\;\|\;{P_{\mathbf{Y}}})
for two random variables
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
where
P_{\mathbf{X}},P_{\mathbf{Y}}
denote their distributions. In addition, we write
D_{\mathrm{KL}}({\mathbf{X}\mid\mathbf{Z}}\;\|\;{\mathbf{Y}})=\Ex_{z\sim\mathbf{Z}}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}({\mathbf{X}|_{\mathbf{Z}=z}}\;\|\;{\mathbf{Y}})\right]
. For Kullback-Leibler divergence we have the following properties.

•

D_{\mathrm{KL}}({P}\;\|\;{Q})\geq 0
, and
D_{\mathrm{KL}}({P}\;\|\;{Q})=0
iff
P=Q
.

•

For distributions
P_{1},Q_{1},P_{2},Q_{2}
over a finite set
S
and any
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}({\lambda P_{1}+(1-\lambda)P_{2}}\;\|\;{\lambda Q_{1}+(1-\lambda)Q_{2}})\leq\lambda D_{\mathrm{KL}}({P_{1}}\;\|\;{Q_{1}})+(1-\lambda)D_{\mathrm{KL}}({P_{2}}\;\|\;{Q_{2}}).

In other words,
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
is convex.

•

For any random variables
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
on
S
and any injection
f
,
D_{\mathrm{KL}}({f(\mathbf{X})}\;\|\;{f(\mathbf{Y})})=D_{\mathrm{KL}}({\mathbf{X}}\;\|\;{\mathbf{Y}})
.

Painlevé analysis, Bäcklund transformation, Lax pair and periodic wave solutions for a generalized (2+1)-dimensional Hirota-Satsuma-Ito equation in fluid mechanics
Dong Wang, Yi-Tian Gao, Xin Yu, Gao-Fu Deng, Fei-Yan Liu Ministry-of-Education Key Laboratory of Fluid Mechanics and NationalLaboratory for Computational Fluid Dynamics, Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Beijing 100191, China

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a generalized (2+1)-dimensional Hirota-Satsuma-Ito (HSI) equation in fluid mechanics. Via the Painlevé analysis, we find that the HSI equation is Painlevé integrable under certain condition. Bilinear form, Bell-polynomial-type Bäcklund transformation and Lax pair are constructed with the binary Bell polynomials. One-periodic-wave solutions are derived via the Hirota-Riemann method and displayed graphically.



Keywords:
Fluid mechanics; (2+1)-dimensional Hirota-Satsuma-Ito equation; Painlevé analysis; Bell polynomials; Bäcklund transformation; Lax pair; Periodic wave solutions

2. Painlevé analysis for Eq. (
1
)

Motivated by Ref.
[
27
]
, solutions for Eq. (
1
) can be expanded to the Laurent series,

\displaystyle u=\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}u_{b}\phi^{b+\alpha},

(4)

where
\phi
and
u_{b}
’s are the analytic functions of
x
,
y
and
t
,
b
is an integer, and
\alpha
is a negative integer. The leading order of Expression (
4
) can be assumed as

\displaystyle u\sim u_{0}\phi^{\alpha},

(5)

where
u_{0}
is a nonzero function in the neighborhood of a non-characteristic movable singularity manifold.

Substituting Expression (
5
) into Eq. (
1
) and balancing the highest-order nonlinear and linear terms, we obtain
\alpha=-1
and
u_{0}=2\phi_{x}
. To find the resonance points, we can substitute

\displaystyle u\sim u_{0}\phi^{-1}+u_{b}\phi^{b-1}

(6)

into Eq. (
1
) and let the sum of the terms with the lowest power of
\phi
in Eq. (
1
) vanish, i.e.,

\displaystyle(b^{4}-10b^{3}+23b^{2}+10b-24)\phi^{-5+b}u_{b}\phi_{x}^{3}\phi_{t}=0.

(7)

Solving Eq. (
7
) yields four resonant points at
b=-1,1,4,6
.

Substituting

\displaystyle u=\sum_{b=0}^{4}u_{b}\phi_{b-1}

(8)

into Eq. (
1
) and setting the coefficients of
\phi^{b-5}
’s vanish, we can verify that the compatibility conditions are satisfied at the resonant points
b=1,4
, and that the corresponding
u_{2}
,
u_{3}
and
u_{5}
can be expressed. However, at the resonant point
b=6
, the compatibility condition is written as

\displaystyle 6\delta_{5}^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}h_{1}+3\delta_{1}\phi_{t}(2\delta_{5}\phi_{y}+\delta_{3}\phi_{x})h_{2}+2\delta_{5}h_{3}+\delta_{3}h_{4}=0,

(9)

where
h_{\aleph}=h_{\aleph}(u_{1},u_{4},\phi,\phi_{x},\phi_{y},\phi_{t},\phi_{xx},\phi_{xy},\phi_{xt},\phi_{yt},\phi_{tt},\ldots)
(
\aleph=1,2,3,4
) are the real functions relying on
\phi
. To satisfy Condition (
9
) with whatever
\phi=\phi(x,y,t)
takes, we have to set

\displaystyle\delta_{3}=\delta_{5}=0,

(10)

and consequently Eq. (
1
) becomes Painlevé integrable.

It is worth noting that if we truncate Expression (
8
) into

\displaystyle u=u_{0}\phi^{-1}+u_{1},

(11)

and set
u_{1}=0
, we obtain the aforementioned logarithm transformation
u=2(\ln f)_{x}
, which can be used to derive the bilinear form for Eq. (
1
).

3. Bell polynomials

The multi-dimensional Bell polynomials are defined as
[
28
]

\displaystyle Y_{n_{1}x_{1},n_{2}x_{2},\ldots,n_{l}x_{l}}(\varphi)=Y_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{l}}(\varphi_{r_{1}x_{1},r_{2}x_{2},\ldots,r_{l}x_{l}})=\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi}\partial_{x_{1}}^{n_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots\partial_{x_{l}}^{n_{l}}\mathrm{e}^{\varphi},

(12)

where
\varphi=\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})
is a
C^{\infty}
function with multi-variables,
l
is a nonnegative integer,
\varphi_{r_{1}x_{1},r_{2}x_{2},\ldots,r_{l}x_{l}}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{n_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots\partial_{x_{l}}^{n_{l}}
,
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{l}
are the nonnegative integers, and
r_{1}=0,1,\ldots,n_{1}
;
r_{2}=0,1,\ldots,n_{2}
; …;
r_{l}=0,1,\ldots,n_{l}
. For instance,
Y_{x,t}=\varphi_{xt}+\varphi_{x}\varphi_{t}
,
Y_{2x,t}=\varphi_{xxt}+\varphi_{xx}\varphi_{t}+2\varphi_{xt}\varphi_{x}+\varphi_{x}^{2}\varphi_{t}
.

Based on Expression (
12
), the binary Bell polynomials are written as
[
29
]

\displaystyle\mathscr{Y}_{n_{1}x_{1},n_{2}x_{2},\ldots,n_{l}x_{l}}(v,w)=Y_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{l}}(\varphi)\Big|_{\varphi_{r_{1}x_{1},r_{2}x_{2},\ldots,r_{l}x_{l}}}

(13)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}v_{r_{1}x_{1},r_{2}x_{2},\ldots,r_{l}x_{l}}&\text{if $r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{l}$ is odd},\\
w_{r_{1}x_{1},r_{2}x_{2},\ldots,r_{l}x_{l}}&\text{if $r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{l}$ is even},\end{cases}

where
v
and
w
are the
C^{\infty}
functions of
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}
. For example,
\mathscr{Y}_{x}(v,w)=v_{x},~\mathscr{Y}_{2x}(v,w)=w_{xx}+v_{x}^{2},~\mathscr{Y}_{x,t}(v,w)=v_{x}v_{t}+w_{xt},~\mathscr{Y}_{3x}(v,w)=v_{xxx}+3v_{x}w_{xx}+v_{x}^{3}
. The relationship between the
n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{l}
th order
\mathscr{Y}
-polynomials and the Hirota bilinear operators is expressed as
[
30
]

\displaystyle\mathscr{Y}_{n_{1}x_{1},n_{2}x_{2},\ldots,n_{l}x_{l}}[v=\ln(G/F),w=\ln(GF)]=(G\cdot F)^{-1}D_{x_{1}}^{n_{1}}D_{x_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots D_{x_{l}}^{n_{l}}G\cdot F,

(14)

where
n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{l}\geq 1
,
G
and
F
are the
C^{\infty}
functions of
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}
.

In the particular case of
F=G
, Eq. (
14
) can be written as

\displaystyle F^{-2}D_{x_{1}}^{n_{1}}D_{x_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots D_{x_{l}}^{n_{l}}F\cdot F=\mathscr{Y}_{n_{1}x_{1},n_{2}x_{2},\ldots,n_{l}x_{l}}(0,q=2\ln F).

(15)

Examples for the
P
-polynomials are shown as:

\displaystyle P_{2x}(q)=q_{xx},~P_{x,t}(q)=q_{xt},~P_{4x}(q)=q_{xxxx}+3q_{xx}^{2},~P_{3x,y}(q)=q_{xxxy}+3q_{xx}q_{xy}.

(16)

\mathscr{Y}
-polynomials can be separated into
P
-polynomials and
Y
-polynomials as
[
28
]

\displaystyle(G\cdot F)^{-1}D_{x_{1}}^{n_{1}}D_{x_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots D_{x_{l}}^{n_{l}}G\cdot F

(17)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{Y}_{n_{1}x_{1},n_{2}x_{2},\ldots,n_{l}x_{l}}(v,w)\Big|_{v=\ln(G/F),~w=\ln(GF)}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{Y}_{n_{1}x_{1},n_{2}x_{2},\ldots,n_{l}x_{l}}(v,v+q)\Big|_{v=\ln(G/F),~q=2\ln F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{l}=\text{even}}\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\sum_{r_{2}=0}^{n_{2}}\cdots\sum_{r_{l}=0}^{n_{l}}\prod_{\imath=q}^{l}\begin{pmatrix}n_{\imath}\\
r_{\imath}\end{pmatrix}P_{r_{1}x_{1},r_{2}x_{2},\ldots,r_{l}x_{l}}Y_{(n_{1}-r_{1})x_{1},(n_{2}-r_{2})x_{2},\ldots,(n_{l}-r_{l})x_{l}}(v).

One property of the multi-dimensional Bell polynomials, i.e.,
[
29
]

\displaystyle\mathscr{Y}_{(n_{1}-r_{1})x_{1},(n_{2}-r_{2})x_{2},\ldots,(n_{l}-r_{l})x_{l}}(v)\Big|_{v=\ln\psi}=\frac{\psi_{(n_{1}-r_{1})x_{1},(n_{2}-r_{2})x_{2},\ldots,(n_{l}-r_{l})x_{l}}}{\psi},

(18)

implies that the
\mathscr{Y}
-polynomials can be linearized by means of the Cole-Hopf transformation
v=\ln\psi
, where
\psi
is a real function of
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}
.

\displaystyle-2\partial_{y}(3v_{x}w_{xx}+v_{x}^{3})+6(w_{xx}v_{xy}+v_{xx}w_{xy}),

Introducing the constraint equation,

\displaystyle w_{xy}+v_{x}v_{y}=\lambda(t),

(28)

where
\lambda(t)
is a real function of

\displaystyle E(\tilde{q})-E(\hat{q})=2\partial_{y}\left[\mathscr{Y}_{3x}(v,w)+\delta_{1}\mathscr{Y}_{t}(v,w)+\delta_{3}\mathscr{Y}_{x}(v,w)+\delta_{5}\mathscr{Y}_{y}(v,w)\right]

Therefore, Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathscr{Y}_{3x}(v,w)+\delta_{1}\mathscr{Y}_{t}(v,w)+\delta_{3}\mathscr{Y}_{x}(v,w)+\delta_{5}\mathscr{Y}_{y}(v,w)=0,\\

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(D_{x}^{3}+\delta_{1}D_{t}+\delta_{3}D_{x}+\delta_{5}D_{y}\right)G\cdot F=0,\\

4. Periodic wave solutions for Eq. (
1

In order to study the one-periodic wave solutions for Eq. (
1

[
31
]

\displaystyle\theta(\xi,\tau)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi n^{2}\tau+2\mathrm{i}\pi n\xi},

where
n
is an integer,
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
,

\tau

,
\nu

\gamma

\displaystyle\bar{G}[D_{x},D_{y},D_{t},c]\theta(\xi,\tau)\cdot\theta(\xi,\tau)\triangleq[D_{x}^{3}D_{t}+\delta_{1}D_{y}D_{t}+\delta_{2}D_{x}^{2}+\delta_{3}D_{x}D_{y}

\displaystyle+\delta_{4}D_{x}D_{t}+\delta_{5}D_{y}^{2}+c]\theta(\xi,\tau)\cdot\theta(\xi,\tau)=0,

where
c
is a constant to be known later.

With the following property of the Hitora bilinear operators,

\displaystyle D_{x}^{A_{1}}D_{y}^{A_{2}}D_{t}^{A_{3}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi_{1}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{\xi_{2}}=(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})^{A_{1}}(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})^{A_{2}}(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})^{A_{3}}\mathrm{e}^{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}},

\displaystyle\bar{G}[D_{x},D_{y},D_{t},c]\theta(\xi,\tau)\cdot\theta(\xi,\tau)

In this paper, we have investigated a generalized (2+1)-dimensional HSI equation in fluid mechanics, i.e., Eq. (
1
). Via Painlevé analysis, we have found that Eq. (

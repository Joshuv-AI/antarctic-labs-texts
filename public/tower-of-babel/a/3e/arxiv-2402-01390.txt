Symmetric Petz-Rényi relative entropy uncertainty relation
Domingos S. P. Salazar

Abstract
Holevo introduced a fidelity between quantum states that is symmetric and as effective as the trace norm in evaluating their similarity. This fidelity is bounded by a function of the trace norm, a relationship to which we will refer as Holevo’s inequality. More broadly, Holevo’s fidelity is part of a one-parameter family of symmetric Petz-Rényi relative entropies, which in turn satisfy a Pinsker’s-like inequality with respect to the trace norm. Although Holevo’s inequality is tight, Pinsker’s inequality is loose for this family. We show that the symmetric Petz-Rényi relative entropies satisfy a tight inequality with respect to the trace norm, improving Pinsker’s and reproducing Holevo’s as a specific case. Additionally, we show how this result emerges from a symmetric Petz-Rényi uncertainty relation, a result that encompasses several relations in quantum and stochastic thermodynamics.



Introduction -
Holevo introduced a quantum fidelity
Holevo 1972
between states
\rho
and
\sigma
defined as

F_{H}(\rho,\sigma):=[\text{tr}(\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma})]^{2},

(1)

which is symmetric,
F_{H}(\rho,\sigma)=F_{H}(\sigma,\rho)
, and used under different names in the literature such as affinity and overlap
Wilde 2018
;
Iten et al. 2017
;
Ma et al. 2008
;
Albrecht 1994
;
Luo and Zhang 2004
;
Kim 2013
;
Audenaert 2014
. We have the following inequality for Holevo’s fidelity,

T(\rho,\sigma):=\frac{1}{2}|\rho-\sigma|_{1}\leq\sqrt{1-F_{H}(\rho,\sigma)},

(2)

where
|x|_{1}:=\text{tr}(\sqrt{x^{\dagger}x})
is the trace norm. More generally, Holevo’s fidelity is a member of a one parameter family of symmetric Petz-Rényi relative entropies defined as

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma):=\frac{1}{2}(D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)+D_{\alpha}(\sigma||\rho)),

(3)

such that
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)=\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\sigma,\rho)
, where
D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)
is the Petz-Rényi relative entropy
Petz 1986
defined as

D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\text{tr}(\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}),

(4)

which is non negative for any
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. It has applications in quantum hypothesis testing
Buscemi and Datta 2010
;
Brandao and Datta 2011
and in quantum field theory
Kudler-Flam 2023
. The case
\alpha=1
is understood as
D_{1}(\rho,\sigma):=(D(\rho||\sigma)+D(\sigma||\rho))/2
, where
D(\rho||\sigma)=\text{tr}(\rho\ln\rho)-\text{tr}(\rho\ln\sigma)
is the quantum relative entropy. For the specific case
\alpha=1/2
, one has
\tilde{D}_{1/2}(\rho,\sigma)=D_{1/2}(\rho||\sigma)=-2\ln\text{tr}(\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma})=-\ln F_{H}(\rho,\sigma)
from (
1
) and (
3
). In this case, one could write Holevo’s inequality (
2
) as a lower bound for
\tilde{D}_{1/2}(\rho,\sigma)
as

\tilde{D}_{1/2}(\rho,\sigma)\geq\ln\frac{1}{1-T(\rho,\sigma)^{2}},

(5)

also recently used in the applications of Petz-Rényi relative entropy in quantum field theory
Kudler-Flam 2023
, where it was explored a Pinsker’s-like inequality,

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)\geq 2\min(\alpha,1)T(\rho,\sigma)^{2}.

(6)

Note that, for the case
\alpha=1/2
, Holevo’s inequality improves Pinsker’s inequality for the particular case
\alpha=1/2
,

\tilde{D}_{1/2}(\rho,\sigma)\geq\ln\frac{1}{1-T(\rho,\sigma)^{2}}\geq T(\rho,\sigma)^{2}.

(7)

Motivated by (
7

5
) beyond the case
\alpha=1/2
such that it improves Pinsker’s inequality for any
\alpha
in the symmetric Petz-Rényi family? In this case, we obtain the following result:

(
Generalized Holevo’s inequality
) Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be any density matrices. Then,

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\frac{\cosh[(2\alpha-1)\arctanh(T(\rho,\sigma))]}{\cosh[\arctanh(T(\rho,\sigma))]}.

(8)

For the specific case
\alpha=1/2

\alpha=1
previously appeared in the classic case (
[\rho,\sigma]=0
) as a bound for the symmetric Kullback-Leibler divergence in terms of total variation

;

More generally, we show that (

(

This letter is organized as follows. First, we present the main steps of the proof, then we show how the bound is saturated and the improvement over Pinsker’s inequality. Finally, we discuss the applications of (

Formalism:

The idea behind the proof is a strategy that maps
n
dimensional quantum states
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|p_{i}\rangle\langle p_{i}|
and
\sigma=\sum_{j}q_{j}|q_{j}\rangle\langle q_{j}|
into the following
n^{2}
dimensional classic distributions:
P_{ij}:=p_{i}|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}
and
Q_{ij}:=q_{j}|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}
. This strategy is sometimes called Nussbaum-Szkoła distributions
Nussbaum and Szkoła 2009
and it was successfully used in other contexts
Datta et al. 2013
;
Salazar 2024
, particularly as tool to calculate relative entropies
Androulakis and John 2023a
;
Androulakis and John 2023b
as follows,

\sum_{ij}P_{ij}^{\alpha}Q_{ij}^{1-\alpha}=\sum_{ij}p_{i}^{\alpha}q_{j}^{1-\alpha}|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\text{tr}(\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}),

(11)

which results in
D_{\alpha}(P|Q)=D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)
, where
D_{\alpha}(P|Q):=[1/(1-\alpha)]\ln\sum_{ij}P_{ij}^{\alpha}Q_{ij}^{1-\alpha}
is the Rényi relative entropy, thus we have

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P,Q)=\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma),

(12)

where
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P,Q)=(1/2)[D_{\alpha}(P|Q)+D_{\alpha}(Q|P)]
. Now that we mapped the Petz-Rényi relative entropy into a classic divergence, we import a recent result from information theory
Nishiyama 2022
,

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P,Q)\geq B(\alpha,\sqrt{\delta(P,Q)}),

(13)

where
\delta(P,Q):=\sum_{s}(P_{s}-Q_{s})^{2}/(P_{s}+Q_{s})
is the triangular discrimination and

B(\alpha,x):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\frac{\cosh[(2\alpha-1)\arctanh(x)]}{\cosh[\arctanh(x)]}

(14)

is increasing in the interval
0<x<1
for all
\alpha>0
. Finally, we use the inequality
\sqrt{\delta(P,Q)}\geq s(\rho,\sigma,\hat{\theta})
(see Appendix), which results in

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)=\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P,Q)\geq B(\alpha,\sqrt{\delta(P,Q)})\geq B(\alpha,s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta})),

(15)

which proves our main result (
9
). The generalized Holevo’s inequality (
8
) is obtained as a particular case of (
9
) where
\hat{\theta}=\sum_{k}\sign(w_{k})|w_{k}\rangle\langle w_{k}|
and
\{|w_{k}\rangle\}
are the eigenvalues of
\rho-\sigma
(see Appendix).

As in previous results, the bound (
9
) is saturated for the specific two-level system, where
\rho=[e^{\epsilon/2}|1\rangle\langle 1|+e^{-\epsilon/2}|0\rangle\langle 0|]/(2\cosh(\epsilon/2))
,
\sigma=[e^{-\epsilon/2}|1\rangle\langle 1|+e^{\epsilon/2}|0\rangle\langle 0|]/(2\cosh(\epsilon/2))
and
\hat{\theta}=\phi(|1\rangle\langle 1|-|0\rangle\langle 0|)
. In this case, one has
\text{tr}(\rho\hat{\theta})=\phi\tanh(\epsilon/2)
,
\text{tr}(\sigma\hat{\theta})=-\phi\tanh(\epsilon/2)
and
\text{tr}(\rho\hat{\theta}^{2})=\text{tr}(\sigma\hat{\theta}^{2})=\phi^{2}
, such that

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\ln\frac{\cosh[(2\alpha-1)\epsilon/2]}{\cosh[\epsilon/2]},

(16)

and also
s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta})=|\tanh(\epsilon/2)|
. Therefore, in this minimal system, we have the saturation of (
9
),

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)=B(\alpha,s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta})).

(17)

We also show that (
8
) improves Pinsker’s inequality (
6
) in the Appendix, where we have

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)\geq B(\alpha,T(\rho,\sigma))\geq 2\min(\alpha,1)T(\rho,\sigma)^{2},

(18)

which generalizes Holevo’s case (
7
) for any
\alpha
.

Discussion -
We investigate particular cases of (
9
) and (
8
) in stochastic and quantum thermodynamics. Some of the cases are very well known and some of them are new to our knowledge.

First, turning our attention to relation (
8
), we note that particular cases also appeared in the literature. The case
\alpha=1/2
is obviously the
Holevo’s inequality
(
5
), but we also observe that the case
\lim\alpha\rightarrow 1
results in

\tilde{D}(\rho,\sigma)\geq 2T(\rho,\sigma)\arctanh(T(\rho,\sigma)),

(19)

which was recently used to to analyse fluxes in quantum thermodynamics
Salazar 2023a
. We also note that the classic case (
[\rho,\sigma]=0
) of (
19
) was used in the study of Markov chains in stochastic thermodynamics as well
Dechant 2022
;
Vo et al. 2022
;
Salazar 2022a
, where
\rho
and
\sigma
can be written as classic probabilities
p=(p_{1},...,p_{n})
,
q=(q_{1},...,q_{n})
, yielding

\tilde{D}(p,q)\geq 2\Delta(p,q)\arctanh(\Delta(p,q)),

(20)

where
\Delta(p,q)=(1/2)\sum_{s}|p_{i}-q_{i}|
is the total variation.

Moreover, we note that (
9
) can be inverted to the following expression when
\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}\neq\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma}
,

\frac{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\rho}+\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}}{(1/2)(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}\geq f(\alpha,\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)),

(21)

where
f(\alpha,x):=1/[B^{-1}(\alpha,D)]^{2}-1
, and for a fixed
\alpha
,
B^{-1}(\alpha,x)
is the inverse of
B(\alpha,x)
for
x\geq 0
, such that
B^{-1}(\alpha,B(\alpha,x))=x
. Remarkably, expression (
21
) has the form of a quantum uncertainty relation, which explains the name of the theorem as
symmetric Petz-Rényi uncertainty relation
. As matter of fact, the case
\alpha=1
results in the recently proposed
quantum relative entropy uncertainty relation

Salazar 2024
,

\frac{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\rho}+\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}}{(1/2)(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}\geq f(1,\tilde{D}(\rho,\sigma)),

(22)

where
f(1,x)=1/\sinh^{2}(g(x)/2)
and
g(x)
is the inverse of
h(x)=x\tanh(x/2)
for
x\geq 0
. Analogously, the case
\alpha=1/2
in (
21
) results in the following
Holevo’s uncertainty relation
,

\frac{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\rho}+\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}}{(1/2)(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}\geq\frac{F_{H}(\rho,\sigma)}{1-F_{H}(\rho,\sigma)},

(23)

which takes the usual form of thermodynamic uncertainty relations as a lower bound for some uncertainty in terms of a dissimilarity. In this case, the dissimilarity is not the usual entropy production, but given in terms of the Holevo’s fidelity (
1
) instead.

We also consider the classic situation
[\rho,\sigma]=0
, which represents the absence of coherence between the states. In this particular case, writing again
\rho
and
\sigma
in terms of classic probabilities
p=(p_{1},...,p_{n})
,
q=(q_{1},...,q_{n})
and
\theta=(\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n})
is a random variable. We obtain the classic version of (
21
),

\frac{\langle\langle\theta\rangle\rangle_{p}+\langle\langle\theta\rangle\rangle_{q}}{(1/2)(\langle\theta\rangle_{p}-\langle\theta\rangle_{q})^{2}}\geq f(\alpha,\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(p,q)),

(24)

which can be seen as the
\alpha-
generalized version of the tightest form of the
hysteretic thermodynamic uncertainty relation
(
\alpha=1
)
Proesmans and Horowitz 2019
;
Francica 2022
;
Salazar 2022b
,

\frac{\langle\langle\theta\rangle\rangle_{p}+\langle\langle\theta\rangle\rangle_{q}}{(1/2)(\langle\theta\rangle_{p}-\langle\theta\rangle_{q})^{2}}\geq f(1,\tilde{D}(p,q)),

(25)

while the case
\alpha=1/2
in (
24
)
can be written as (
23
) but in terms of the Bhattacharyya or Hellinger distances.

Finally, in the particular case
p=P(\Gamma)
,
q=P(\Gamma^{\dagger})
, where
\Gamma
typically represents a trajectory and
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is the inverse trajectory, such that
(\Gamma^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=\Gamma
, and
\theta(\Gamma)
is a current with property
\theta(\Gamma^{\dagger})=-\theta(\Gamma)
, relation (
25
) yields a result known as the
thermodynamic uncertainty relation from the exchange fluctuation theorem

Hasegawa and Van Vu 2019a
;
Timpanaro et al. 2019
,

\frac{\langle\langle\theta\rangle\rangle_{p}}{\langle\theta\rangle_{p}^{2}}\geq f(1,\langle\Sigma\rangle),

(26)

where
\langle\Sigma\rangle:=\sum_{\Gamma}P(\Gamma)\ln P(\Gamma)/P(\Gamma^{\dagger})
is the average entropy production.

Conclusions -

We proposed a symmetric Petz-Rényi uncertainty relation (
9
) and studied multiple applications. Our result was obtained using a mapping from quantum to classic systems and exploring recent results from information theory. From our result, we wrote a general uncertainty relation (
21
) and, for the particular case
\alpha=1/2
, we obtained a uncertainty relation in terms of Holevo’s fidelity (
22
), a generalized Holevo’s inequality (
8
) and multiple known results in quantum and stochastic thermodynamics (
20
,
25
,
26
). Our results highlight that different symmetric quantum dissimilarities may also play a role akin to the entropy production in expressions resembling thermodynamic uncertainty relations. This fact does not depend on specific properties of the system (such as the detailed fluctuation theorem), but it is rather a fundamental interplay between quantum uncertainties and symmetric dissimilarities.

\frac{1}{4}|\overline{\Theta}_{P}-\overline{\Theta}_{Q}|^{2}=|\sum_{s\in S^{\prime}}(\Theta(s)-c)\frac{(P(s)-Q(s))}{2}|^{2},

(42)

for any complex
c
. Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we also obtain for any complex
c
,

|\sum_{s\in S^{\prime}}(\Theta(s)-c)\frac{(P(s)-Q(s))}{2}|^{2}\leq\langle|\Theta-c|^{2}\rangle_{\tilde{P}}\langle(\frac{P-Q}{P+Q})^{2}\rangle_{\tilde{P}},

(43)

so that combining (
42
) and (
43
) for
c=\overline{\Theta}_{\tilde{P}}
, it yields

\frac{1}{4}|\overline{\Theta}_{P}-\overline{\Theta}_{Q}|^{2}\leq\langle|\Theta-\overline{\Theta}_{\tilde{P}}|^{2}\rangle_{\tilde{P}}\langle(\frac{P-Q}{P+Q})^{2}\rangle_{\tilde{P}}.

(44)

We note from Definition 1 (
27
) that

\delta(P,Q)=\langle(\frac{P-Q}{P+Q})^{2}\rangle_{\tilde{P}}.

(45)

Now consider the identity

4\langle|\Theta-\overline{\Theta}_{\tilde{P}}|^{2}\rangle_{\tilde{P}}=2(\langle|\Theta|^{2}\rangle_{P}-|\overline{\Theta}_{P}|^{2})+2(\langle|\Theta|^{2}\rangle_{Q}-|\overline{\Theta}_{Q}|^{2})+|\overline{\Theta}_{P}-\overline{\Theta}_{Q}|^{2}.

(46)

Combining (
44
), (
45
) and (
46
) it results in (
41
)
\blacksquare
.

Definition 2.
(
Nussbaum-Szkoła distributions
) Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be density matrices in
n
dimensions with spectral decomposition
\rho=\sum_{i}^{n}p_{i}|p_{i}\rangle\langle p_{i}|
and
\sigma=\sum_{j}^{n}q_{j}|q_{j}\rangle\langle q_{j}|
. We define distributions
P
and
Q
in
S=\{(i,j)|1\leq i\leq n;1\leq j\leq n\}
as

P_{ij}:=|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}p_{i},

(47)

Q_{ij}:=|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}q_{j},

(48)

and we add the definition of the auxiliary complex random variable
\Theta
based on a Hermitian operator
\hat{\theta}
,

\Theta_{ij}:=\frac{\langle p_{i}|\hat{\theta}|q_{j}\rangle}{\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle},

(49)

for
\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle\neq 0
and
\Theta_{ij}:=0
otherwise.

Lemma 3.
For any density matrices
\rho,\sigma
and Hermitian operator
\hat{\theta}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{(1/2)|\langle\Theta\rangle_{P}-\langle\Theta\rangle_{Q}|^{2}}{\langle\langle\Theta\rangle\rangle_{P}+\langle\langle\Theta\rangle\rangle_{Q}+(1/2)|\langle\Theta\rangle_{P}-\langle\Theta\rangle_{Q}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1/2)(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\rho}+\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}+(1/2)(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}},

(50)

with
P,Q,\Theta
given by Definition 2 and
\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}:=\text{tr}\{\rho\hat{\theta}\}
,
\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma}:=\text{tr}\{\sigma\hat{\theta}\}
,
\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\rho}:=\text{tr}\{\rho\hat{\theta}^{2}\}-\text{tr}\{\rho\hat{\theta}\}^{2}
and
\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}:=\text{tr}\{\sigma\hat{\theta}^{2}\}-\text{tr}\{\sigma\hat{\theta}\}^{2}
.

Proof.
This idea was explored in
Salazar 2024
. The expected value of
\hat{\theta}
with respect to
\rho
is

\text{tr}(\rho\hat{\theta})=\sum_{ij}p_{i}\langle p_{i}|\hat{\theta}|q_{j}\rangle\langle q_{j}|p_{i}\rangle=\sum_{ij;\langle q_{j}|p_{i}\rangle\neq 0}p_{i}|\langle q_{j}|p_{i}\rangle|^{2}\frac{\langle p_{i}|\hat{\theta}|q_{j}\rangle}{\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle},

(51)

where we used
\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle=\langle q_{j}|p_{i}\rangle^{*}
. Using Definition 2, in terms of
P
and
\Theta
, we have from (
51
),

\text{tr}(\rho\hat{\theta})=\sum_{ij}P_{ij}\Theta_{ij}:=\langle\Theta\rangle_{P}.

(52)

Similarly, we obtain for the expected value of
\hat{\theta}
with respect to
\sigma
using Definition 2,

\text{tr}(\sigma\hat{\theta})=\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\Theta_{ij}:=\langle\Theta\rangle_{Q}.

(53)

Analogously, we have for the expected value of
\hat{\theta}^{2}
with respect to
\rho
,

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\rho\hat{\theta}^{2})=\sum_{ij}p_{i}|\langle p_{i}|\hat{\theta}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}\geq

(54)

\displaystyle\sum_{ij;\langle q_{j}|p_{i}\rangle\neq 0}p_{i}|\langle p_{i}|\hat{\theta}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{ij}P_{ij}|\Theta_{ij}|^{2},

(55)

where we used
\hat{\theta}=\hat{\theta}^{\dagger}
, which yields

\text{tr}(\rho\hat{\theta}^{2})\geq\sum_{ij}P_{ij}|\Theta_{ij}|^{2}:=\langle|\Theta|^{2}\rangle_{P}.

(56)

We have a similar expression in terms of
\sigma
,

\text{tr}(\sigma\hat{\theta}^{2})\geq\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}|\Theta_{ij}|^{2}:=\langle|\Theta|^{2}\rangle_{Q}.

(57)

Combining expressions (
52
), (
53
), (
56
) and (
57
) completes the proof of Lemma 3 (
Symmetric Petz-Rényi relative entropy uncertainty relation
).
\blacksquare

Definition 3.

Let the trace-norm be defined as

T(\rho,\sigma):=\frac{1}{2}|\rho-\sigma|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\{\sqrt{(\rho-\sigma)^{2}}\},

(58)

and the Petz-Rényi relative entropy

D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\text{tr}\{\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}\},

(59)

for any
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
, with the symmetric version defined as

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{2}(D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)+D_{\alpha}(\sigma||\rho)).

(60)

Theorem 1.

(Symmetric Petz-Rényi uncertainty relation) Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be any density matrices and
\hat{\theta}
be any Hermitian operator. Then,

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\frac{\cosh[(2\alpha-1)\arctanh(s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta}))]}{\cosh[\arctanh(s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta}))]},

(61)

where

s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta}):=\Big[\frac{(1/2)(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}{\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\rho}+\langle\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle\rangle_{\sigma}+(1/2)(\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\theta}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}}\Big]^{1/2}.

(62)

Proof.
Using Definition 2, we have the following identity

\sum_{ij}P_{ij}^{\alpha}Q_{ij}^{1-\alpha}=\sum_{ij}p_{i}^{\alpha}q_{j}^{1-\alpha}|\langle p_{i}|q_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\text{tr}(\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}),

(63)

which results in
D_{\alpha}(P|Q)=D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)
, thus

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P,Q)=\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma).

(64)

We also have from Lemma 2 (
41
) and Lemma 3 (
Symmetric Petz-Rényi relative entropy uncertainty relation
),

\sqrt{\delta(P,Q)}\geq s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta}).

(65)

Finally, note that the function

B(\alpha,x):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\frac{\cosh[(2\alpha-1)\arctanh(x)]}{\cosh[\arctanh(x)]}

(66)

is increasing for all
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
, which combined with (
64
), (
65
) and Lemma 1 (
30
), it results in

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)=\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P,Q)\geq B(\alpha,\sqrt{\delta(P,Q)})\geq B(\alpha,s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\theta})),

(67)

which proves Theorem 1 (
61
).
\blacksquare

Corollary 1.
(
Generalized Holevo’s inequality
) Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be any density matrices and
\hat{\theta}
be any Hermitian operator. Then,

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\frac{\cosh[(2\alpha-1)\arctanh(T(\rho,\sigma))]}{\cosh[\arctanh(T(\rho,\sigma))]}.

(68)

Proof
. We use Theorem 1, we use a specific operator
\hat{\theta}=\hat{\omega}
suggested in
Salazar 2023a
as follows, consider the following spectral decomposition of the operator
\rho-\sigma=\sum_{k}w_{k}|w_{k}\rangle\langle w_{k}|
. Now we define
\hat{\omega}=\hat{\omega}
as

\hat{\omega}:=\sum_{k,w_{k}\neq 0}\sign(w_{k})|w_{k}\rangle\langle w_{k}|,

(69)

where
\sign(x)=1\penalty\ (-1)
, for
x>0\penalty\ (x<0)
. Then, we write the trace-norm
|\rho-\sigma|_{1}
in terms of
\hat{\omega}
from (
69
),

\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\sigma}=\text{tr}[\hat{\omega}(\rho-\sigma)]=\sum_{k}|w_{k}|=|\rho-\sigma|_{1}.

(70)

Then, we observe that

\hat{\omega}^{2}=\sum_{k,w_{k}\neq 0}\sign(w_{k})^{2}|w_{k}\rangle\langle w_{k}|=I-\hat{\epsilon},

(71)

where
I
is the identity operator and
\hat{\epsilon}:=\sum_{k,w_{k}=0}|w_{k}\rangle\langle w_{k}|
, with averages

\langle\hat{\epsilon}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle\hat{\epsilon}\rangle_{\sigma}:=\epsilon,

(72)

obtained from
\langle\hat{\epsilon}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\epsilon}\rangle_{\sigma}=\text{tr}[\hat{\epsilon}(\rho-\sigma)]=\sum_{k,w_{k}=0}w_{k}=0
. We also have
0\leq\epsilon\leq 1
, because
\rho,\sigma
are positive definite and
\text{tr}(\rho)=\text{tr}(\sigma)=1
. From (
71
) and (
72
), we get

\langle\hat{\omega}^{2}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle\hat{\omega}^{2}\rangle_{\sigma}=1-\epsilon.

(73)

Using the averages (
70
) and (
73
) we obtain

s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\omega})^{2}=\frac{(1/2)|\rho-\sigma|_{1}^{2}}{(2-2\epsilon)-\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\rho}-\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\sigma}+(1/2)|\rho-\sigma|_{1}^{2}}.

(74)

Also check that

(1/2)|\rho-\sigma|_{1}^{2}\leq\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\rho}^{2}+\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\sigma}^{2},

(75)

directly from (
70
) and the expression
(1/2)(x-y)^{2}\leq(1/2)(x-y)^{2}+(1/2)(x+y)^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
, for
x=\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\rho}
,
y=\langle\hat{\omega}\rangle_{\sigma}
. In this case, we obtain from (
75
),
s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\omega})^{2}\geq\frac{|\rho-\sigma|_{1}^{2}}{4(1-\epsilon)}\geq\frac{|\rho-\sigma|_{1}^{2}}{4}
, which results in

s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\omega})\geq\frac{|\rho-\sigma|_{1}}{2}=T(\rho,\sigma).

(76)

Now, using Theorem 1 with operator
\hat{\omega}
, we have
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho,\sigma)\geq B(\alpha,s(\rho,\sigma;\hat{\omega}))\geq B(\alpha,T(\rho,\alpha))
, using (
76
) and the fact that
B(\alpha,x)
is increasing, which proves the first inequality in (
68
).
\blacksquare

Lemma 4.

The bound in Corollary 1 improves Pinsker’s inequality.

Proof
.
Let
T\in[0,1]
, then we show that

B(\alpha,T):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\frac{\cosh[(2\alpha-1)\arctanh(T)]}{\cosh[\arctanh(T)]}\geq 2\min(\alpha,1)T^{2}.

(77)

For that purpose, we consider the function
g(\alpha,x)=\ln[\cosh((2\alpha-1)x)/\cosh(x)]
for
x\geq 0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. For a constant
x
, we have
\partial^{2}g(\alpha,x)/\partial^{2}\alpha=4x^{2}\sech^{2}[(x(1-2\alpha)]\geq 0
. Therefore, we could use the property of convex functions,
g(\alpha,x)-g(1,x)\geq g^{\prime}(1,x)(\alpha-1)
, where
g^{\prime}(\alpha,x)=\partial g(\alpha,x)/\partial\alpha
. Replacing
g^{\prime}(1,x)=2x\tanh(x)
and
g(1,x)=0
, we have
g(\alpha,x)\geq 2x\tanh(x)(\alpha-1)
, which yields for
\alpha>1
,

B(\alpha,T)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}g(\alpha,\arctanh(T))\geq 2T\arctanh(T)\geq 2T^{2}.

(78)

Now for
0<\alpha<1
, we use a different strategy. First, note that
g(\alpha,x)
can be conveniently rewritten as
g(\alpha,x)=2\arctanh(\tanh(\alpha x)\tanh((\alpha-1)x))
using
\arctanh(x)=(1/2)\ln[(1+x)/(1-x)]
. Thus we obtain using
-\arctanh(x)\leq-x
,

g(\alpha,x)\leq-2\tanh(\alpha x)\tanh((1-\alpha)x)\leq 2\alpha(\alpha-1)\tanh(x)^{2},

(79)

where we used
\tanh(\epsilon x)\geq\epsilon\tanh(x)
for
0<\epsilon<1
and
x>0
(one can check this showing the function
j(x)=\tanh(\epsilon x)-\epsilon\tanh(x)
is increasing in
x
for any constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
j(0)=0
). Finally, we get from (
79
), using
\alpha-1<0
,

B(\alpha,T)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}g(\alpha,\arctanh(T))\geq 2\alpha T^{2}.

(80)

Combining (
78
) and (
80
), it results in
B(\alpha,T)\geq 2\min(\alpha,1)T^{2}
for all
\alpha\geq 0
, which proves (
77
).

\blacksquare

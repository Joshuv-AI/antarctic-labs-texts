The behavior of essential dimension under specialization
Zinovy Reichstein, Federico Scavia

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
A
be a discrete valuation ring with generic point
\eta
and closed point
s
.
We show that in a family of torsors over
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, the essential dimension of the torsor above
s

is less than or equal to the essential dimension of the torsor above
\eta
. We give two applications of this result, one in mixed
characteristic, the other in equal characteristic.

Keywords.
Algebraic group, torsor, versal torsor, discrete valuation ring, essential dimension

The essential dimension

\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})
such that

.
Essential dimension of

In this paper we will investigate how

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

, fraction field

Set

is contained in the maximal ideal of

\operatorname{ed}_{k_{0}}(\alpha_{K_{0}})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(\alpha_{K}).

Furthermore, if

is defined over

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

.

The assumption that

It is natural to ask whether or not conditions (ii) and (iii) can be replaced by a single (weaker) assumption.

Question 1.3

Does

For our second application of

, we briefly recall the definition of essential dimension of a
G
-variety.
Once again, let

k
. By a

-scheme of finite type endowed with a
G
-action over
k

G
-variety

is primitive if

and

transitively permutes the irreducible components of

. We will say that the
G
-variety

(or equivalently, the

-action on

if there exists a dense open subscheme

such that for every

the scheme-theoretic stabilizer
G_{u}
of

is trivial.

Now suppose that
Y
is a generically free primitive
G
-variety defined over
k
. By a
G
-compression of

we will mean
a dominant
G
-equivariant rational map

, defined over
k
,
where the
G
-action on

is again generically free and primitive. The essential dimension of

, denoted by

is defined as the minimal value of
\dim(X)-\dim(G)
, where the minimum is taken over all

-compressions

.
This notion is closely related to that of essential dimension of a torsor; see

. If the reference to

in place of
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(Y;G)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic

,

be a linear algebraic group defined over

Let

(1)

(2)

(3)

(4)

Here in parts (2) and (3),

denotes the order of the Weyl group

If

is obvious from the definition, since any compression of

Lemma 2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Y
be a generically free primitive
G
-variety defined over
k
, and
k^{\prime}/k
be a field extension.
Then

(a)

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(Y)\geqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(Y_{k^{\prime}})
.

(b)

There exists an intermediate field

such that

\operatorname{ed}_{l}(Y_{l})=\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(Y_{k^{\prime}})

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(Y)=\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(Y_{k^{\prime}})
.

is a generically free primitive

-variety.

Proof.

(a) The inequality
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(Y)\geqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(Y_{k^{\prime}})

every
G
-compression

gives rise to a

-compression

by base-change.

(b) By (a), it suffices to find

\operatorname{ed}_{l}(Y_{l})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(Y_{k^{\prime}})

-compression over

.

such that

, where

as desired.

2.3. Essential dimension at a prime

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group defined over a field
k
and let
q
be a prime integer. Essential
dimension at
q
for
G
-torsors,
G
-varieties, and
G
itself, is defined in a way that parallels
the definitions of essential dimension for these objects. We recall these definitions below.

A field
K
is called
q
-closed if every finite field extension
L/K
is of degree
[L:K]=q^{r}
for some integer
r\geqslant 0
. For every field
K
, there exists a unique algebraic extension
K^{(q)}/K
such that
K^{(q)}
is
q
-closed and the degree
[L:K]
of every every finite subextension
K\subset L\subset K^{(q)}
is prime to
q
. The field
K^{(q)}
is called a
q
-closure of
K
. It is the fixed field of a
q
-Sylow subgroup of the absolute Galois group of
K
.

Let
k
be a field, and let
G
be an algebraic
k
-group. If
K/k
is a field extension and
\tau\colon\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Spec}(K)
is a
G
-torsor,
then
\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(\tau)
is the minimal value of
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(\tau_{L})
, where
L
ranges over
the finite field extensions of
K
whose degree
[L:K]
is prime to
q
. Equivalently,

(2.5)

\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(\tau)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(\tau_{K^{(q)}}),

where
K^{(q)}
is the
q
-closure of
K
.

Recall that a correspondence
X\rightsquigarrow Z
between
G
-varieties
X
and
Z
of
degree
d
is a diagram of
G
-equivariant rational maps of
G
-varieties the form

(2.6)

\textstyle{X^{\prime}\;\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
degree
d
\textstyle{X}
\textstyle{Z\,,}

where the vertical map is dominant of degree
d
. We say that
X\rightsquigarrow Z

is dominant if the rational map
X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow Z
in the above
diagram is so. Dominant correspondences may be composed in an evident way.
Note that sometimes the term “rational correspondence” is used in place of correspondence, to indicate that
the maps in Diagram (
2.6
) are rational maps. All correspondences used in this paper will be rational in this sense.
For notational simplicity we will use the term “correspondence” throughout.

Now let
X
be a generically free primitive
G
-variety over
k
. The essential dimension of
\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(X)
of
X
at
q
is
the minimal value of
\dim(Z)-\dim(G)
, where the minimum is taken over all
G
-equivariant dominant correspondences
X\rightsquigarrow Z
of degree prime to
q
. Equivalently,

(2.7)

\begin{split}\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(X)=\ &\text{maximal value of }\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(X^{\prime}),\ \text{where the maximum is taken over all $G$-equivariant}\\
&\text{dominant rational covers $X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow X$ of degree prime to $q$.}\end{split}

If
\tau_{X}
is the
G
-torsor over
k(X)^{G}
associated to
X
, as in the previous section, then one readily checks that

(2.8)

\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(X)=\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(\tau_{X}).

The essential dimension
\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(G)
of
G
at
q
is the maximal value of
\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(\tau)
, as
K
ranges over
fields containing
k
and
\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Spec}(K)
ranges over
G
-torsors over
\operatorname{Spec}(K)
. Equivalently,

\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(G)
is the maximal value of
\operatorname{ed}_{k,q}(X)
, as
X
ranges over the generically free primitive
G
-varieties.

3. Torsors over complete discrete valuation rings

Let
R
be a complete discrete valuation ring,
F
its fraction field,
F_{0}
its residue field, and
\overline{s}
and
\overline{\eta}
geometric points lying over the closed and generic point of
\operatorname{Spec}R
, respectively. We have a group homomorphism

\operatorname{sp}:\operatorname{Gal}(F)\to\operatorname{Gal}(F_{0}),

called specialization homomorphism. Under the identifications
\operatorname{Gal}(F)=\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Spec}F,\overline{\eta})
and
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{0})=\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Spec}F_{0},\overline{s})
, the homomorphism
\operatorname{sp}
is the composition

(3.1)

\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Spec}F,\overline{\eta})\to\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Spec}R,\overline{\eta})\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Spec}F_{0},\overline{s}),

where the map on the left is induced by the inclusion
\operatorname{Spec}F\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}R
, and the isomorphism on the right by specialization of finite étale covers; see
e.g.

[
Stacks
, Tag 0BUP]
. The homomorphism
\operatorname{sp}
may also be defined Galois-theoretically; see
[
Ser03
, Section 7.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a field,
\pi\in K^{\times}
, and fix an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
. Then

(a) there exists a system
\left\{\pi^{1/m}\right\}_{m\geqslant 1}
of roots of
\pi
in
\overline{K}
such that
(\pi^{1/m_{1}m_{2}})^{m_{2}}=\pi^{1/m_{1}}
for any
m_{1},m_{2}\geqslant 1
.

(b) Moreover, suppose
\alpha\in\overline{K}
satisfies
\alpha^{n}=\pi
for some
n\geqslant 1
. Then the system
\left\{\pi^{1/m}\right\}_{m\geqslant 1}
in part (a)
can be chosen so that
\pi^{1/n}=\alpha
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove part (b). Once (b) is established, we deduce (a) by setting
n=1
and
\alpha=\pi
.

To prove (b), choose
\beta\in\overline{K}
such that
\beta^{(n-1)!}=\alpha
. In particular,
\beta^{n!}=\pi
.
For every integer
e\geqslant d
, we define
\pi^{1/e!}\in\overline{K}
recursively as follows:

\pi^{1/n!}:=\beta
, and if
e>n
then
\pi^{1/e!}
is defined as an arbitrary
e^{\mathrm{th}}
root of
\pi^{1/(e-1)!}
.
Now we define
\pi^{1/m}
for an arbitrary integer
m\geqslant 1
as follows.
Choose
e\geqslant n
such that
m
divides
e!
, and set
\pi^{1/m}:=(\pi^{1/e!})^{e!/m}
.
It is immediate to check that
\pi^{1/m}
does not depend on the choice of
e
, that

(\pi^{1/m_{1}m_{2}})^{m_{2}}=\pi^{1/m_{1}}
for any
m_{1},m_{2}\geqslant 1
and that

\pi^{1/n}=(\pi^{1/n!})^{(n-1)!}=\beta^{(n-1)!}=\alpha
.
∎

Unbounded order convergence on infinitely distributive lattices
Kevin Abela, Emmanuel Chetcuti

Abstract
. We study 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} O-convergence on infinitely distributive lattices, extending key properties known from Riesz spaces. We show that order continuity of 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} O-convergence characterizes infinite distributivity. We examine O-adherence and 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} O-adherence of sublattices and ideals, proving that the 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} O- and O-closures of a sublattice coincide and form a sublattice, and that the first 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} O-adherence of an ideal is an O-closed ideal. We also analyze the Dedekind–MacNeille completion of a sublattice Y Y within that of a lattice L L , identifying conditions (A) and (B) under which the completion of Y Y embeds regularly in that of L L . In this case, we show that the first 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} O-adherence of Y Y covers its O-closure.

Gao and Xanthos showed that every weakly compact

-convergent net is norm convergent in Banach lattices with the positive Schur property. The notion of
\mathfrak{u}O
-Cauchy nets was used to show that every relative weakly compact

-Cauchy net is

-convergent in an order continuous Banach lattice

A pivotal study on

In this article, we investigate

3.
O-convergence and
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergence

In this section, we introduce the notions of O-convergence and
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergence within the framework of lattices. While O-convergence has been widely studied in the broader context of general lattices and partially ordered sets (posets)
[
6
,
13
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
21
]
, the more recent concept of
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergence has predominantly been investigated in Riesz spaces and
\ell
-groups. Given that both Riesz spaces and
\ell
-groups are examples of infinitely distributive lattices, our work not only generalizes existing results on
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergence in these settings but also offers potential insights into the influence of the additive structure on the behavior of O- and
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergence.

Proposition 1
.

[
2
, Proposition 3.10]

(a)

For a lattice
L
the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

L
is distributive.

(ii)

For all
s,t\in L
the function
f_{s,t}:L\to L
defined by
f_{s,t}(x):=(x\wedge t)\vee s
is a lattice homomorphism.

(iii)

For all
s,t\in L
the function
g_{s,t}:L\to L
defined by
g_{s,t}(x):=(x\vee s)\wedge t
is a lattice homomorphism.

(b)

If
L
is distributive, then

f_{s,t}=f_{s,s\vee t}=g_{s,s\vee t}
and
g_{s,t}=g_{s\wedge t,t}=f_{s\wedge t,t}

for every
s,t\in L
.

Definition 1
.

Let
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a net and
x
a point in a lattice
L
.

(i)

(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is said to
order converge
(
O-converge
) to
x\in L
if there exists a directed set
M\subseteq L
and a filtered set
N\subseteq L
satisfying
\bigvee M=\bigwedge N=x
, and such that for every
(m,n)\in M\times N
,
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is eventually contained in
[m,n]
. In this case we write
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
.

(ii)

(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is said to
unbounded order converge
(
\mathfrak{u}
O-converge
) to
x\in L
, if
(x_{\gamma}\wedge t)\vee s\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}(x\wedge t)\vee s
for every
s,t\in L
and
s\leq t
. In this case we write
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}O$}}{\longrightarrow}}x
.

The subsequent remark presents a collection of immediate consequences derived from these definitions.

Remark 1
.

The following assertions are easily verified.

(i)

If
x_{\gamma}\uparrow x
in
L
then
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
. The dual statement for decreasing nets holds as well.

(ii)

If a net O-converges, then the order limit is unique. Let us verify that if a net
\mathfrak{u}
O-converges, then the
\mathfrak{u}
O-limit is unique. Indeed, if
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}O$}}{\longrightarrow}}x
and
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}O$}}{\longrightarrow}}y
, then
(x_{\gamma}\wedge t)\vee s\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
and
(x_{\gamma}\wedge t)\vee s\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}y
, for every
s\leq t
in
L
. This implies that
(x\wedge t)\vee s=(y\wedge t)\vee s
for every
s\leq t
in
L
. In particular, setting
s:=x\wedge y
and
t:=x\vee y
, one gets

x=(x\wedge(x\vee y))\vee(x\wedge y)=(y\wedge(x\vee y))\vee(x\wedge y)=y\,.

(iii)

If
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is O-convergent to
x
, and eventually
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is contained in
(\leftarrow,a]
, then
x\leq a
. The dual statement holds as well.

(iv)

If
L
is bounded,
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergence implies O-convergence.

(v)

In the light of Proposition
1
, if the lattice is distributive, the condition
s\leq t
in the definition of
\mathfrak{u}O
-convergence becomes redundant:
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
iff
(x_{\gamma}\vee s)\wedge t\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}(x\vee s)\wedge t
for every
s,t\in L
.

The following proposition demonstrates that when the lattice is a commutative
\ell
-group (and, in particular, when it is a Riesz space), the unbounded-order convergence defined in Definition
1
coincides with the well-established notion of unbounded convergence on such structures.

Proposition 2
.

[
8
, Prop. 7.2]

For the net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, and the point
x
, in a commutative
\ell
-group
(\mathscr{G},+)
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

|x_{\gamma}-x|\wedge u\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}0
for every
u\in\mathscr{G}_{+}
,

(ii)

(x_{\gamma}\wedge t)\vee s\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}(x\wedge t)\vee s
for every

Proposition 3
.

Let
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a net in a lattice
L
.

(i)

If
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
, and eventually
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is contained in
(\leftarrow,u]
, then
x\leq u
. The dual statement holds as well.

(ii)

If
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is monotonic, the following implication holds:

x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}{\longrightarrow}}x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}x_{\gamma}\uparrow x\text{ (if the net is increasing)},\\
x_{\gamma}\downarrow x\text{ (if the net is decreasing)}.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i) Assume that
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
, and
x_{\gamma}\leq u
for every
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
. Define
a:=u\wedge x
and
b:=u\vee x
. We have
(x_{\gamma}\wedge b)\vee a\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}(x\wedge b)\vee a
, and by Remark
1

x=(x\wedge b)\vee a\leq(u\wedge b)\vee a=u
. The dual statement can be proved similarly.

(ii) Assume that
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is monotonic increasing and
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
. For
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
the net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}}
is contained in
[x_{\gamma_{0}},\rightarrow)
and
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergent to
x
. (i) implies
x\geq x_{\gamma_{0}}
. Therefore

is an upper bound for
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
. Hence,
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
and therefore
x=\bigvee_{\gamma\in\Gamma}x_{\gamma}
.

∎

That is, if a net is order convergent to a point, then it is also

Example 4

Let

Definition 2
.

(i)

A lattice

is said to satisfy the

if for

\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\}\subseteq L

\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}x_{\alpha}
exists in

x\vee\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}x_{\alpha}=\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(x\vee x_{\alpha}).

(ii)

A lattice

is said to satisfy the

if for
x\in L

\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\}\subseteq L
such that
\bigvee_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}x_{\alpha}
exists in

x\wedge\bigvee_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}x_{\alpha}=\bigvee_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}(x\wedge x_{\alpha}).

(iii)

A lattice satisfying both the join and meet-infinite distributive laws is called an

.

For subsets
A
,
B
of a lattice

, we write

Proposition 5
.

Let
L
be an infinitely distributive lattice. If
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
and
(y_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}y
, then the net

\bigl((x_{\gamma}\vee y_{\omega}):(\gamma,\omega)\in\Gamma\times\Omega\bigr)

O-converges to
x\vee y
, and dually,

\bigl((x_{\gamma}\wedge y_{\omega}):(\gamma,\omega)\in\Gamma\times\Omega\bigr)

O-converges to
x\wedge y
.

Proof.

There are directed sets
M^{x}
,
M^{y}
, and filtered sets
N^{x}
,
N^{y}
, such that for
(a^{x},b^{x})\in M^{x}\times N^{x}
, and for
(a^{y},b^{y})\in M^{y}\times N^{y}
, one can find
\gamma(a^{x},b^{x})
,
\omega(a^{y},b^{y})
such that
x_{\gamma}\in[a^{x},b^{x}]
for
\gamma\geq\gamma(a^{x},b^{x})
and
y_{\omega}\in[a^{y},b^{y}]
for
\omega\geq\omega(a^{y},b^{y})
. Define
M:=M^{x}\vee M^{y}
and
N:=N^{x}\vee N^{y}
. Then,
M
is directed,
N
is filtered,
\bigvee M=x\vee y
, and by infinite distributivity,
\bigwedge N=x\vee y
. Furthermore, for
a^{x}\vee a^{y}\in M^{x}\vee M^{y}
and
b^{x}\vee b^{y}\in N^{x}\vee N^{y}
, it holds that
x_{\gamma}\vee y_{\omega}\in[a^{x}\vee a^{y}\,,\,b^{x}\vee b^{y}]
for
(\gamma,\omega)\geq\gamma(a^{x},b^{x})\times\omega(a^{y},b^{y})
. The other assertion can be proved similarly.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
L
be an infinitely distributive lattice. If
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}}{\longrightarrow}}x
and
(y_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathop{\overset{\text{{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}}{\longrightarrow}}y
, then

\bigl((x_{\gamma}\vee y_{\omega}):(\gamma,\omega)\in\Gamma\times\Omega\bigr)

\mathfrak{u}
O-converges to
x\vee y
, and dually,

\bigl((x_{\gamma}\wedge y_{\omega}):(\gamma,\omega)\in\Gamma\times\Omega\bigr)

\mathfrak{u}
O-converges to
x\wedge y
.

Proof.

For any
s,t\in L
we have

\displaystyle(x_{\gamma}\vee s)\wedge t\,

\displaystyle\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}\,(x\vee s)\wedge t,

\displaystyle(y_{\omega}\vee s)\wedge t\,

\displaystyle\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}\,(y\vee s)\wedge t,

and therefore, by Proposition
5
,

((x_{\gamma}\vee s)\wedge t)\vee((y_{\omega}\vee s)\wedge t)\,\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}\,((x\vee s)\wedge t)\vee((y\vee s)\wedge t),

and

((x_{\gamma}\vee s)\wedge t)\wedge((y_{\omega}\vee s)\wedge t)\,\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}\,((x\vee s)\wedge t)\wedge((y\vee s)\wedge t).

Hence,

(x_{\gamma}\vee y_{\omega}\vee s)\wedge t\,\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}\,(x\vee y\vee s)\wedge t,

and

((x_{\gamma}\wedge y_{\omega})\vee s)\wedge t\,\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}\,((x\wedge y)\vee s)\wedge t.

∎

We now show that for a distributive lattice
L
, the order continuity of
\mathfrak{u}
O-convergence is equivalent to
L
being infinitely distributive.

Theorem 7
.

A distributive lattice
L
is infinitely distributive if and only if
\mathfrak{u}O
-convergence is order continuous.

Proof.

If
L
is infinitely distributive, then
\mathfrak{u}O
-convergence is order continuous by Proposition
5
. Conversely, assume that
\mathfrak{u}O
-convergence is order continuous. Let
A\subseteq L
and
y\in L
. We want to show that if
\bigvee A=u
(resp.
\bigwedge A=v
) then
\bigvee\{a\wedge y:a\in A\}=u\wedge y
(resp.
\bigwedge\{a\vee y:a\in A\}=v\vee y
).
Let
\mathscr{A}=\{B\subseteq A:|B|<\aleph_{0}\}
and for every
B\in\mathscr{A}
let
a_{B}=\bigvee B
, to get an increasing net
\{a_{B}:B\in\mathscr{A}\}
in
L
with supremum
u
.
Then
(a_{B})_{B\in\mathscr{A}}
is order convergent to
u
, and the assumed order continuity of
\mathfrak{u}O
-convergence implies that
a_{B}\mathop{\overset{\text{{$\mathfrak{u}\mbox{O}$}}}{\longrightarrow}}u
.

Take
t=y
and
s=a_{0}\wedge y
, where
a_{0}
is an arbitrary point of
A
. Then

(a_{B}\wedge y)\vee(a_{0}\wedge y)\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}(u\wedge y)\vee(a_{0}\wedge y)=u\wedge y.

Observe that the net

\{(a_{B}\wedge y)\vee(a_{0}\wedge y):B\in\mathscr{A}\}

is increasing, and so Proposition
3
implies that

\bigvee\{(a_{B}\wedge y)\vee(a_{0}\wedge y):B\in\mathscr{A}\}=u\wedge y.

Since
(a_{B}\wedge y)\geq(a_{0}\wedge y)
when
a_{0}\in B
, it follows that

\bigvee\{(a_{B}\wedge y)\vee(a_{0}\wedge y):B\in\mathscr{A}\}=\bigvee\{(a_{B}\wedge y):B\in\mathscr{A}\}.

Finally, if
B=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
, then
a_{B}\wedge y=\bigvee_{i\leq n}(a_{i}\wedge y)
, because
L
is distributive. Thus

\bigvee\{a\wedge y:a\in A\}=\bigvee\{(a_{B}\wedge y):B\in\mathscr{A}\}=u\wedge y.

A similar argument can be made to prove the dual.
∎

Proposition 8
.

Let
L
be an infinitely distributive lattice. If
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is an order bounded net, then
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}}{\longrightarrow}}x
if and only if
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x

Proof.

That
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
implies
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}}{\longrightarrow}}x
follows by Theorem

. On the other hand assume that there exists

such that
x_{\gamma}\in[a,b]
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{{$\mathfrak{u}$O}}}{\longrightarrow}}x
. Then
(x_{\alpha}\wedge x_{\gamma})\vee x_{\beta}\in[a,b]
for every
\alpha
,

and
\gamma
in
\Gamma
, and thus
(x\wedge x_{\gamma})\vee x_{\beta}\in[a,b]
. Repeating this argument it follows that

. Hence,
x_{\gamma}=(x_{\gamma}\wedge b)\vee a\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}(x\wedge b)\vee a=x
.
∎

Corollary 9
.

In a bounded, infinitely distributive lattice, O-convergence and
\mathfrak{u}

The O-closure and
\mathfrak{u}
O-closure of sublattices and ideals

For a subset
X
of a lattice
L
let

X^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}

We can define the
\lambda
-O-adherence

and for every ordinal number
\lambda>0

\lambda
-O-adherence

by:

X^{{\scriptscriptstyle{O}}}_{\lambda}:=\left(\bigcup_{\beta<\gamma}X^{{\scriptscriptstyle{O}}}_{\beta}\right)^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}.

It is possible to define the
\lambda

\mathfrak{u}
O-adherence

for every ordinal
\lambda>0
by recursion, in the same way that we defined the
\lambda
-O-adherence

, i.e.

X^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{u}{O}}}_{\lambda}:=\left(\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}X^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{u}{O}}}_{\beta}\right)^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{u}O}}_{1}.

The set

is said to be

(resp.
\mathfrak{u}
O-closed
) if

(resp.

). The set of all O-closed subsets of

, called the

. The same can be said for the
\mathfrak{u}
O-closed sets and one can speak of the

O-topology
as the topology given rise by the

O-closed subsets of

. The O-closure of

is the smallest O-closed subset of

that contains

, i.e. the O-closure is the topological closure w.r.t. the order topology. Note that this will generally be larger than

.
Similarly, the
\mathfrak{u}
O-closure is the smallest
\mathfrak{u}
O-closed subset of

that contains

.

Remark 2
.

(i)

If

is a subset of an infinitely distributive lattice

(ii)

In a lattice, the cuts

(iii)
), and

(iii)

There must exist an ordinal

\displaystyle\alpha:=

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda\geq 0:X^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{\lambda}=X^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{\lambda+1}\}

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda\geq 0:X^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{u}O}}_{\lambda}=X^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{u}O}}_{\lambda+1}\}\,,

We shall only show the proof of (i), as the proof for (ii) follows by a similar argument. It can be seen that
A^{-+}\subseteq A^{-_{Y}+}
. To see the reverse inclusion let
x\in A^{-_{Y}+}
and
z\in A^{-}
. Then there exists
y\in A^{-_{Y}}
such that
z\leq y
. This implies that
z\leq y\leq x
. Hence,
x\in A^{-+}
. This shows that
A^{-+}\supseteq A^{-_{Y}+}
.
∎

Proposition 24
.

Let
L
be a lattice,
Y\subseteq L
a sublattice of
L
and
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\}
a collection of sets in
Y
satisfying
A_{\alpha}^{+_{Y}-_{Y}}=A_{\alpha}
.

(i)

If
Y
satisfies Property (A) then

\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+-}=\left(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+-}\,.

(ii)

If
Y
satisfies Property (B) then

\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+_{Y}-_{Y}+-}=\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+-}\right)^{+-}\,.

Proof.

(i)

We first note that the inclusion
(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha})^{+-}\subseteq\bigcap_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}^{+-}
is trivial. To see the reverse inclusion, first note that
\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+-}=(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+})^{-}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+}\right)^{-}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+}\right)^{-+-}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+_{Y}}\right)^{-+-}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+_{Y}}\right)^{-_{Y}+-}\text{ (By property (A))}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+_{Y}-_{Y}}\right)^{+-}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+-}.

(ii)

To see this equality, we note that

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+_{Y}-_{Y}+-}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+_{Y}-_{Y}+_{Y}-}\text{ (By property (B))}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+_{Y}-}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+-}\text{ (By property (B))}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+-}\right)^{+-}.

∎

Theorem 25
.

Let
L
be a lattice and
Y\subseteq L
be a sublattice. Let
i:\text{DM}(Y)\to\text{DM}(L):A\mapsto A^{+-}
be order-embedding described in Theorem
20
.

(i)

If
Y
satisfies Property (A), then
i
preserves arbitrary meets.

(ii)

If
Y
satisfies Property (B), then
i
preserves arbitrary joins.

Proof.

Let
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\}\subseteq\text{DM}(Y)
. Then Proposition
24
implies that:

\displaystyle i\left(\bigwedge{}^{\!\text{DM}(Y)}_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge{}^{\!\text{DM}(L)}_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\ i(A_{\alpha})\,,

if
Y
satisfies Property (A); and

\displaystyle i\left(\bigvee{}^{\!\text{DM}(Y)}_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}\right)^{+_{Y}-_{Y}+-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}A_{\alpha}^{+-}\right)^{+-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee{}^{\!\text{DM}(L)}_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\ i(A_{\alpha})\,,

if
Y
satisfies Property (B).
∎

Proposition 27
.

Let
L
be a lattice and
Y
be a convex sublattice.

(i)

If
Y
has a maximal element, it satisfies Property (B);

(ii)

If
Y

a_{\max}
be the maximal element of
Y
. Let

and

.
∎

Proposition 28
.

Let
L
be a complete lattice and
Y
a sublattice of
L
. Then
Y
is
O
-closed if and only if
Y
is supremum and infimum closed.

Proof.

Assume that
Y
is O-closed. Let
A\subseteq L
with
\bigvee^{\scriptscriptstyle L}A=x
. Let
\mathscr{F}=\{B\subseteq A:|B|<\omega\}
.
\mathscr{F}
is directed with respect to inclusion. For every
B\in\mathscr{F}
let
x_{B}=\bigvee^{\scriptscriptstyle L}B=\bigvee^{\scriptscriptstyle Y}B
to get an increasing net
\{x_{B}:B\in\mathscr{F}\}
in
Y
with
\bigvee^{\scriptscriptstyle L}_{B\in\mathscr{F}}x_{B}=x
, i.e. a net in
Y
that O-converges to
x
. Since
Y
is O-closed,
x\in Y
. The dual can be proved similarly.

Conversely, assume that for any subset
A
of
Y
, its supremum and infimum in
L
belong to
Y
, and let
x\in Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}
. Then there exists a net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
such that

\bigvee{}^{\!L}_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}\bigwedge{}^{\!L}_{\gamma\geq\gamma^{\prime}}x_{\gamma}=x=\bigwedge{}^{\!L}_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}\bigvee{}^{\!L}_{\gamma\geq\gamma^{\prime}}x_{\gamma}\,.

By hypothesis it follows that
x\in Y
.
∎

Proposition 29

Proof.

The result follows by Proposition
28
because

∎

Theorem 30
.

Let
L
be an infinitely distributive lattice and
Y\subseteq L
a sublattice satisfying Properties (A) and (B). Then
Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathfrak{u}{O}}}_{1}=Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle{O}}}_{1}
, and both are simultaneously O-closed and
\mathfrak{u}
O-closed.

Proof.

Identify
\text{DM}(Y)
with
i[\text{DM}(Y)]\subseteq\text{DM}(L)
. Our hypothesis and Corollary
26
imply that
\text{DM}(Y)
is a complete regular sublattice of
\text{DM}(L)
. In particular, by Proposition
29
,
\text{DM}(Y)
is O-closed (and therefore
\mathfrak{u}
O-closed because
\text{DM}(L)
has maximal and minimal elements). We show that
\text{DM}(Y)\cap L=Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}
. For every
x\in Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}
there exists a net

(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in
Y
such that
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
in
L
. From
[
1
, Theorem 3]
we have that
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O}}{\longrightarrow}}x
in
\text{DM}(L)
. Since
\text{DM}(Y)
is O-closed in
\text{DM}(L)
, it follows that
x\in\text{DM}(Y)\cap L
. To see the converse, take
x\in\text{DM}(Y)\cap L
. Let
A=\{y\in Y:y\leq x\}
. By Theorem
25
,
\text{DM}(Y)
is regular in
\text{DM}(L)
and thus
\bigvee^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{DM}(L)}A=\bigvee^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{DM}{Y}}A=x
. Since
L
is regular in
\text{DM}(L)
, it follows that
x\in Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}
. This shows that
\text{DM}(Y)\cap L=Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}
, and therefore
Y^{{\scriptscriptstyle O}}_{1}
is simultaneously O-closed and
\mathfrak{u}
O-closed.
∎

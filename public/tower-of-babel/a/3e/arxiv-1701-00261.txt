Casimir effect for Dirac lattices
M. Bordag, I.G. Pirozhenko

Abstract
We consider polarizable sheets, which recently received some attention, especially in context of the dispersion interaction of thin sheets like graphene. These sheets are modeled by a collection of delta function potentials and resemble ‘zero range potentials’, known in quantum mechanics. We develop a theoretical description and apply the so-called ‘TGTG’-formula to calculate the interaction of two such lattices. Thereby we make use of the formulation of the scattering of waves off such sheets provided earlier. We consider all limiting cases, providing link to earlier results. Also, we discuss the relation to the pairwise summation method.

I
Introduction

This paper is a contribution to the discussion of van der Waals and Casimir forces between surfaces. Last years, much attention was paid to the interaction between slabs of finite, and especially small, thickness. This triggered by a growing interest to two dimensional structures (sheets) like 2d electron gas, monoatomically layers and graphene, as well as to the interaction between them. As discussed in
Barton 2013
, the situation with sheets having only in-plane polarizability is relatively clear. Here one can formulate a hydrodynamic plasma model and calculate the quantities of interest
Barton 2005
. The same holds for graphene described by the Dirac equation model for the
\pi
-electrons
Bordag et al. 2009a
, which are responsible for the interaction with the electromagnetic field.

The situation with perpendicular polarizability is more complicated. While in
Parashar et al. 2012
no response to the electromagnetic field was found, in
Barton 2015
it was shown that such response takes place. In that paper the sheet was modelled as a lattice of harmonic oscillators (dipoles), vibrating in direction perpendicular to the sheet, and the limit of zero spacing of this lattice was investigated. This was extended in
Bordag 2014
to a sheet, continuous from the very beginning, having parallel or perpendicular polarizabilities. Later, a similar setup was considered using point dipoles
Bordag and Munoz-Castaneda 2015
. These can be represented by Dirac delta functions forming a so-called ’Dirac lattice’. In fact, there represent point scatterers.

Such lattices, taken alone, have a well known internal dynamics, the simplest case being the Kronig-Penney model (’Dirac comb’)
Kronig and Penney 1931
. In quantum mechanics their use is known as ’method of zero-range potential’
Demkov and Ostrovskii 1988
. As well known, in more than one dimension a Hamilton operator with a delta function potential is not self adjoint
Berezin and Faddeev 1961
. In electrodynamics, the self energy of a delta function potential is singular and one needs a renormalization. In terms of quantum field theory this setup was considered in
Jackiw 1995
.

Recently, a sheet of delta function potentials was used in
Bordag and Munoz-Castaneda 2015
to model a polarizable sheet. For instance, scattering off such sheet was investigated and subsequently the transition to a continuous sheet as well.

In the present note we consider a setup of two such sheets, hold in parallel at some separation. This is a typical situation for the Casimir effect.
We use the well known scattering approach, also called ’TGTG’-formula, which can be considered also as a generalization of the Lifshitz formula. Making use of the translational invariance of the lattice, the
T
-matrices can be calculated in momentum representation; for a one dimensional lattice even explicitly. Further, we allow for a displacement of one lattice with respect to the other. Also we study the limiting cases of small and large separation, resp. of large and small lattice spacing. For large separation, the limiting case corresponds to the interaction of two sheets carrying delta function potential, and for small separation the limiting case corresponds to the interaction of two points carrying delta function potential. In this paper we consider only a scalar field. Extensions to the electromagnetic field should be quite straight forward.
It is also an intention of this paper to provide a framework for calculating dispersion forces in realistic, experimentally relevant situations, where the atomistic structure must be accounted for.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we provide the necessary formulas to calculate the vacuum energy for two and one dimensional lattices. In the third section we derive all limiting cases and discuss their interrelations. In the fourth section we compare the results with pairwise summation. Some conclusions and an appendix complete the paper.

II
Vacuum energy for point scatterers

We consider two types of lattices. First, two dimensional lattices, A and B, given by the lattice vectors

\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}+\mathbf{c}\\
b\end{array}\right),\quad\vec{a}^{\rm\,B}_{\mathbf{n}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}\\
0\end{array}\right),

(1)

For these, we use the following notations. A three dimensional vector is denoted by an arrow, a two dimensional vector parallel to the
(x,y)
-plane is denoted in bold, for instance
\vec{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}\\
x_{3}\end{array}\right)
. The lattice sites are given by

\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{n}}=a\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
n_{2}\end{array}\right)
,
where
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
are integers,
a
is the lattice spacing; the lattice is rectangular.
The lattice B is in the
(x,y)
-plane and the lattice A is parallel to B on a separation
b
and shifted within that plane by the displacement vector
\mathbf{c}
.

Second we consider one dimensional lattices (chains), A and B, given by the lattice vectors

\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}an+c\\
0\\
b\end{array}\right),\quad\vec{a}^{\rm\,B}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}an\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right),

(2)

where
n
is a (single) integer. These chains are on the
x
-axis (B) resp. parallel to it (A). Both are in the plane
y=0
. Their separation is
b
as above, the displacement
c
is a number.

With these notations, we consider a scalar field
\phi(\vec{x})
, whose wave equation after Fourier transform in time is

\left(-\omega^{2}-\Delta+g\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\left(\delta(\vec{x}-\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}})+\delta(\vec{x}-\vec{a}^{\rm\,B}_{\mathbf{n}})\right)\right)\phi(\vec{x})=0,

(3)

where
\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
\vec{a}^{\rm\,B}_{\mathbf{n}}
are given by
1
. In this equation, the delta functions are three dimensional, hence the coupling
g
has the dimension of length. As mentioned in the Introduction, one should remember that the above equation is not well defined and

must be viewed as a bare coupling which needs to undergo a renormalization.

The vacuum energy of the interaction of two lattices is given by the mentioned ’TGTG’-formula,

E_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\,{\rm Tr}\ln(1-{\cal M}),

(4)

and
\xi
is the imaginary frequency,
\omega=i\xi
. The kernel
{\cal M}
is the product of two other kernels,

{\cal M}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})=\int d{x^{\prime\prime}}{\cal N}_{A}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime\prime}){\cal N}_{B}(\vec{x}^{\prime\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime}),

(5)

each of which is given by

{\cal N}_{A,B}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})=\int d{x^{\prime\prime}}G_{0}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime\prime})T_{A,B}(\vec{x}^{\prime\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime}),

(6)

in terms of the
T
-operators of the corresponding lattice. Further, the free Green’s function,

\displaystyle G_{0}(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{e^{i\vec{k}(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})}}{\omega^{2}-\mathbf{k}^{2}-k_{3}^{2}+i0},

(7)

enters this formula. We will use below also the following two representations,

G_{0}(\vec{x})=\frac{e^{i\omega|\vec{x}|}}{4\pi|\vec{x}|}=\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime})+i\Gamma|\mathbf{x}_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime}|}}{2i\Gamma(k)},

(8)

with
\Gamma(k)=\sqrt{\omega^{2}-k^{2}+i0}

The
T
-operators can be defined from

\displaystyle G(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})

(9)

\displaystyle=G_{0}(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})-\int dz\,\int dz^{\prime}\,G_{0}(\vec{x}-\vec{z})T(\vec{z},\vec{z}^{\prime})G_{0}(\vec{z}^{\prime}-\vec{x}^{\prime}),

where

is the Green’s function of equation
3
and can be related to the ansatz

(10)

Inserting this ansatz into the equation

\displaystyle\left(-\omega^{2}-\Delta+g\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\left(\delta(\mathbf{x}-\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}})+\delta(\mathbf{x}-\vec{a}^{\rm\,B}_{\mathbf{n}})\right)\right)G(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\delta(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})

(11)

and using the equation

\left(-\omega^{2}-\Delta\right)G_{0}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})=\delta(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})

(12)

-\Phi^{-1}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}+g\delta_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}-g\sum_{\mathbf{m}}G_{0}(\vec{a}_{\mathbf{n}}-\vec{a}_{\mathbf{m}})\Phi^{-1}_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}=0.

(13)

With the definition

\Phi_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{g}\delta_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}-G_{0}(\vec{a}_{\mathbf{n}}-\vec{a}_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}})

(14)

Eq.
13
becomes

\sum_{\mathbf{m}}\Phi_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}\Phi^{-1}_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}=\delta_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}

(15)

and
\Phi^{-1}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}
is the inverse matrix to
\Phi_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}
.

The diagonal elements of
\Phi_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}
contain
G_{0}(0)
which is not well defined. Referring to
Bordag and Munoz-Castaneda 2015
for a discussion, we renormalize the coupling,

\frac{1}{g}-G_{0}(0)\to\frac{1}{g},

(16)

and after that we get

\Phi_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\frac{1}{g},&(\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}^{\prime})\\[5.0pt]
-G_{0}(\vec{a}_{\mathbf{n}}-\vec{a}_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}),&(\mathbf{n}\neq\mathbf{n}^{\prime})\end{array}\right.

(17)

which is well defined.

Now, comparing Eqs.
II
and
10
, we identify the
T
-operator to be

T^{\rm A}(\vec{z},\vec{z}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\delta(\vec{z}-\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}})\Phi^{-1}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\delta(\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}-\vec{z}^{\prime})

(18)

for one lattice and by the same formula with
B
in place of
A
for the other.
Inserting this expression into
6
we get

{\cal N}_{A}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}G_{0}(\vec{x}-\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}})\Phi^{-1}_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\delta(\vec{a}^{\rm\,A}_{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}-\vec{x}^{\prime})

(19)

and, again, the same with
A\to B
. Further, inserting these into

, we get

{\cal M}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})=\int dx^{\prime\prime}\,{\cal N}_{A}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime\prime}){\cal N}_{B}(\vec{x}^{\prime\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime}),

(20)

for the kernel
{\cal M}
. Eqs.

and

represent the general expressions for the kernel entering the ’TGTG’-formula

for a generic lattice of delta functions.
In the following we consider first two dimensional lattices as given by
1
, and, subsequently one dimensional lattices (chains) as given by Eq.
2
.

In this subsection we consider lattices as given by Eq.

In this subsection we consider lattices as given by Eq.

The calculations are mostly in parallel to the preceeding subsection. The Fourier transform is now one dimensional,

III
Limiting cases

In this section we discuss the limiting cases of the interaction lattices of delta functions.
There are two limiting cases for each, two and one dimensional lattices. One limiting case is for large separation, or equivalently for dimensional reasons, for small lattice step. The other is for small separation, or equivalently, large lattice step.

For large separation, the two dimensional lattices turn into planes, carrying a delta function potential. For these the Casimir effect was first investigated in
Bordag et al. 1992
. The one dimensional lattices (chains) turn into lines, carrying a (two dimensional) delta function potential. This case was not considered so far. However, it turns out that that it is equivalent to the finite size cylinders with Dirichlet boundary conditions for turning their radius to zero, as considered in
Emig et al. 2006
and
Bordag 2006
.

For small separation, in the limit, only one delta function for the one lattice interacts with the closest one in the other lattice. This is equivalent to the Casimir-Polder interaction of two points carrying a delta function potential each. This is the scalar version of the well known interaction of two dipoles, which was considered within the scattering approach in
Power and Thirunamachandran 1993
. Also, it can be considered as limiting case of the interaction of two spheres carrying delta function potential for vanishing radius of the spheres. The corresponding calculation is shown in the Appendix.

The considered cases and their interrelations are shown in Fig.
3
.

, i.e., without displacement. For large separation, the lattices turn into continuous plates and the chains into wires. For small separation both turn into the closes pints.
Also shown are the transitions from spheres and cylinders to plates and wires.

IV
Comparison with pairwise summation

A special feature of van der Waals and Casimir forces is their multi particle character. While in certain cases a pairwise summation may give a good approximation, in general it will not. In this section we compare the results of the exact calculation for point scatterers with the result of pairwise summation, i.e., without multi particle forces.

V
Conclusions

We considered
T
-operators for a two-dimensional and a one dimensional lattices of
\delta
-functions. These involve lattice sums, which
can be expressed in terms Hurwitz zeta function. Further we used this
T
-operator to formulate the kernel in the TGTG formula for the dispersion interaction of two such lattices. This can be viewed as a kind of generalized Lifshitz formula and a represents a finite (converging) expression for the interaction energy. We considered the cases of the interaction of two parallel two-dimensional lattices and, of 1-dimensional parallel lattices (chains). The generalization to rotations is left for future work.

We consider in detail to limiting cases and show the transition from lattices to plans for large separation and to the Casimir-Polder interaction of two lattice sites at small separation. These limiting cases are in agreement with earlier results. Our formulas appear to interpolate between these and establish the link between these..

Appendix A
The Casimir effect for two spheres carrying delta function potentials

In this appendix we display basic formulas for the interaction of two spheres carrying delta function potential (’semitransparent’ spheres) using the by now well known scattering approach (’TGTG’-formula). Although such kind of calculations are, in much more general form, contained in a number of papers, for example in
Emig 2008
, specific formulas for one of the most simple special cases may be of use. The first calculation involving delta function potential is
Bordag 2007
, which was focused on the corrections beyond PFA.

The basic setup is given by the equation

\left(-\omega^{2}-\Delta+\frac{g}{4\pi R^{2}}\left(\delta(|\vec{x}|-R)+\delta(|\vec{x}-\vec{d}|-R)\right)\right)\phi(\vec{x})=0,

(76)

where
R
is the radius of the spheres, one at the origin, the other at separation
d=|\vec{d}|
. These delta functions are one dimensional and the coupling
g
does not undergo any renormalization.

Within the chosen approach, we need for the
T
-operator for a single sphere. In this case the equation is

\left(-\omega^{2}-\Delta+\frac{g}{4\pi R^{2}}\delta(|\vec{x}|-R)\right)\phi(\vec{x})=0.

(77)

The scattering problem for a single sphere was considered, for example, in
Bordag et al. 1999
and we use some notations from there. The Green’s function for Eq.
77
, in a spherical basis, is

G(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})=\sum_{lm}Y_{lm}(\Omega)d_{l}(r,r^{\prime})Y_{lm}(\Omega^{\prime})^{*}

(78)

and the free radial Green’s function is

d^{(0)}(r,r^{\prime})=\frac{1}{rr^{\prime}}j_{l}(\omega r_{<})h^{(1)}_{l}(\omega r_{>}),

(79)

where
j_{l}(z)=\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}J_{l+1/2}(x)
and
h^{(1,2)}_{l}(z)=\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}H^{(1,2)}_{l+1/2}(x)
are spherical Bessel functions. Their Wronskian is
j_{l}^{\prime}(z)h_{l}(z)-j_{l}(z)h_{l}^{\prime}(z)=1/iz^{2}
. The delta function in
77
results in matching conditions on the radial function
d_{l}(r,r^{\prime})
, which must be continuous and obey

\partial_{r}d_{l}(r,r^{\prime})_{|}{{}_{r=R+0}}-\partial_{r}d_{l}(r,r^{\prime})_{|}{{}_{r=R-0}}=-\frac{g}{4\pi R^{2}}d_{l}(R,r^{\prime}).

(80)

With the ansatz

d_{l}(r,r^{\prime})=d^{(0)}(r,r^{\prime})-d^{(0)}(r,R)\Phi^{-1}(\omega)d^{(0)}(R,r^{\prime})

(81)

we get

\Phi^{-1}(\omega)=\frac{i\omega g/4\pi}{1+\frac{g}{4\pi R}i\omega j_{l}(\omega R)h_{l}(\omega R)}.

(82)

After Wick rotation, this expression becomes

\Phi^{-1}(i\xi)=\frac{-gR/4\pi}{1+\frac{g}{4\pi R^{2}}I_{l+\frac{1}{2}}(\xi R)K_{l+\frac{1}{2}}(\xi R)}

(83)

with the modified Bessel functions
I_{\nu}(z)
and
K_{\nu}(z)
. Eq.
83
is in agreement with Eq. (23) in
Bordag et al. 1999
.

The ’TGTG’-formula can be written in a spherical basis,

E_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\xi}{\pi}{\rm Tr}\ln(1-{\cal M})

(84)

where the kernel is

{\cal M}_{l,l^{\prime};m}=\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}=|l-l^{\prime}|}^{l+l^{\prime}}{\cal N}_{l,l^{\prime\prime};m}{\cal N}_{l^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime};m}

(85)

and the trace is over the orbital momenta. All expressions are diagonal in the magnetic quantum number
m
. The kernels in
85
are

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{l,l^{\prime};m}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(86)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\xi R}}K_{l^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}(\xi d)H^{l^{\prime\prime}}_{l,l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle I_{l+\frac{1}{2}}(\xi R)I_{l^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}(\xi R)\Phi^{-1}(i\xi)

with the factors
H^{l^{\prime\prime}}_{l,l^{\prime}}
resulting from the matrix elements for the transition between the two centers. These are given, e.g., by Eq. (10.129) in
Bordag et al. 2009b
or Eq. (5) in

Teo et al. 2011
, and correspond to the
\cal U
in
Emig 2008
, Eq. (2.25). For

, this formula turns into that for Dirichlet boundary conditions on the spheres.

For us, the important property is that for
d\to\infty
, the leading order comes from
l=l^{\prime}=l^{\prime\prime}=0
and with
H^{0}_{0,0}=1
we get

{\cal N}_{00}\raisebox{-5.0pt}{${\to\atop d\to\infty}$}\ \frac{g}{4\pi d}E^{-\xi d}.

(87)

The dependence on the radius
R
of the spheres drops out in this limit. By inserting this into
84
we get

E_{0}\raisebox{-5.0pt}{${\to\atop d\to\infty}$}\ \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\xi}{\pi}\ln\left(1-\left(\frac{g}{4\pi d}e^{-\xi d}\right)^{2}\right)

(88)

for the interaction of two spheres carrying delta function potential in the limit of large separation.

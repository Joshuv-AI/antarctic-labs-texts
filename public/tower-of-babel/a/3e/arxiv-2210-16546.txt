On the structure of weak solutions to the Riemann problem for degenerate nonlinear diffusion equation
Evgeny Yu. Panov

Abstract
We find an explicit form of weak solutions to a Riemann problem for a degenerate semilinear parabolic equation with piecewise constant diffusion coefficient. It is demonstrated that the phase transition lines (free boundaries) correspond to the minimum point of some strictly convex function of a finite number of variables. In the limit as number of phases tend to infinity we obtain a variational formulation of self-similar solution with an arbitrary nonnegative diffusion function.



In a half-plane
t>0
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we consider a semilinear parabolic equation

u_{t}=(a^{2}(u)u_{x})_{x}=A(u)_{xx},

(1)

where
A^{\prime}(u)=a^{2}(u)
,
a(u)\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
,
a(u)\geq 0
. The change
U=A(u)
reduces (
1
) to the equation

b(U)_{t}=U_{xx},

with
b(U)=A^{-1}(U)
being a strictly increasing and possibly discontinuous function.

The same reduction was used in
[
2
, Chapter 5,§ 9]
for a Stefan problem. By the similar methods as in
[
2
]
one can establish the existence and uniqueness of a bounded weak solution (understood in the sense of distributions) of the Cauchy problem for equation (
1
). In the case of piecewise smooth weak solution the following conditions should be fulfilled on discontinuity lines
x=x(t)
:

[A(u)]=0,\quad[u]x^{\prime}(t)+[A(u)_{x}]=0,

(2)

where
[v]=v(t,x(t)+)-v(t,x(t)-)
denotes a jump of a function
v=v(t,x)
on the discontinuity line. Conditions (
2
) are derived by the application of the distribution
0=u_{t}-A(u)_{xx}
to an arbitrary test function and integration by parts with the help of Green formula. As is easy to verify, these conditions together with the requirement that
u(t,x)
is a classic solution of (
1
) in the smoothness domains are equivalent to the statement that
u=u(t,x)
is a weak solution of equation (
1
).

We will study the Cauchy problem for equation (
1
) with a Riemann initial data

u(0,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}u_{-},&x<0,\\
u_{+},&x>0.\end{array}\right.

(3)

As was mentioned above, a weak solution of (
1
), (
3
) is unique. By the uniqueness and the invariance of the problem under the transformation group
(t,x)\to(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\lambda\not=0
, a weak solution of problem (
1
), (
3
) is self-similar:
u(t,x)=v(\xi)
,
\xi=x/\sqrt{t}
. In the case of the heat equation
u_{t}=a^{2}u_{xx}
a self-similar solution
u=v(\xi)
must satisfy the linear ODE
a^{2}v^{\prime\prime}=-\xi v^{\prime}/2
, the general solution of which is

v=C_{1}F(\xi/a)+C_{2}
,
C_{1},C_{2}=\mathrm{const}
, where

F(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}e^{-s^{2}/4}ds

is the error function. In particular, the solution of Riemann problem (
1
), (
3
) can be found if we choose
C_{2}=u_{-}
,
C_{1}=u_{+}-u_{-}
. We consider now the nonlinear case assuming that the diffusion coefficient is a piecewise constant function,
a(u)=a_{k}
for
u_{k}<u<u_{k+1}
,
k=0,\ldots,n
, where

u_{-}=u_{0}<u_{1}<\cdots<u_{n}<u_{n+1}=u_{+}

(since equation (
1
) is invariant under the change
x\to-x
, we can suppose that
u_{+}>u_{-}
), and
a_{k+1}\not=a_{k}
,
k=0,\ldots,n-1
. Let us begin with the nondegenerate case
a_{k}>0
,
k=0,\ldots,n
. We are going to find a weak solution
u=v(\xi)
of (
1
), (
3
) as a piecewise smooth function

\displaystyle v(\xi)=u_{k}+\frac{u_{k+1}-u_{k}}{F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k})}(F(\xi/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k})),

(4)

\displaystyle\xi_{k}<\xi<\xi_{k+1},\ k=0,\ldots,n,

where

-\infty=\xi_{0}<\xi_{1}<\cdots<\xi_{n}<\xi_{n+1}=+\infty

and we agree that
F(-\infty)=0
,
F(+\infty)=1
.
The parabolas
\xi=\xi_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
, should be determine by conditions (
2
). Observing that the piecewise linear function
A(u)
is strictly increasing, we conclude that the condition
[A(u)]=0
reduces to the continuity condition
[u]=0
. We see that
u=u_{k}
on the phase transition lines
\xi=\xi_{k}
, and these lines are weak discontinuities of
u(t,x)
. The second condition in (
2
) means that
[A(u)^{\prime}_{\xi}]=0
(i.e.,
A(u)\in C^{1}
). In view of (
4
) these conditions reduce to the equalities

\frac{a_{k}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})F^{\prime}(\xi_{k}/a_{k})}{F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k})}=\frac{a_{k-1}(u_{k}-u_{k-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{k}/a_{k-1})}{F(\xi_{k}/a_{k-1})-F(\xi_{k-1}/a_{k-1})},\quad k=1,\ldots,n.

(5)

To prove that the nonlinear system (
5
) admits a solution, we observe that it is equivalent to the equality

\nabla E(\bar{\xi})=0
, where the function

E(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{k=0}^{n}(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\ln(F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k})),\quad\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\Omega,

(6)

defined in the convex open domain
\Omega
given by the inequalities
\xi_{1}<\cdots<\xi_{n}
. Obviously,
E(\bar{\xi})\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Notice that for all
k=0,\ldots,n

\ln(F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k}))<0.

(7)

In particular,
E(\bar{\xi})>0
. We will call the function
E(\bar{\xi})>0

the entropy
, it depends only on discontinuities of the function
v(\xi)
.

Lemma 1
.

The sets
E(\bar{\xi})\leq c
are compact for each constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

If
E(\bar{\xi})\leq c
then it follows from (
7
) that for all
k=0,\ldots,n

\displaystyle-(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\ln(F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k}))\leq E(\bar{\xi})\leq c,

which implies the estimate

F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k})\geq\delta\doteq\exp(-c/m)>0,

(8)

where
\displaystyle m=\min_{k=0,\ldots,n}(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})>0
. In the particular cases
k=0,n
these inequalities imply that
F(\xi_{1}/a_{0})\geq\delta
,
F(-\xi_{n}/a_{n})=1-F(\xi_{n}/a_{n})\geq\delta
, therefore

-r\leq\xi_{1}<\xi_{n}\leq r
, where
r
is a positive constant satisfying the condition
\max(F(-r/a_{0}),F(-r/a_{n}))\leq\delta
. Since the remaining coordinates of
\bar{\xi}
are situated between
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{n}
, we find that
{\|\bar{\xi}\|_{\infty}\leq r}
. Further, since
F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/4}<1
, the function
F(x)
is Lipschitz with constant
1
and it follows from (
8
) that

(\xi_{k+1}-\xi_{k})/a_{k}\geq F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k})\geq\delta,\quad k=1,\ldots,n-1.

We find that
\xi_{k+1}-\xi_{k}\geq\delta_{1}=\delta\min a_{k}
. We conclude tat the set
E(\bar{\xi})\leq c
lies in the compact set

K=\{\ \bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ |\ \|\bar{\xi}\|_{\infty}\leq r,\ \xi_{k+1}-\xi_{k}\geq\delta_{1}\ \forall k=1,\ldots,n-1\ \}.

By the continuity of
E(\bar{\xi})
the set
E(\bar{\xi})\leq c
is a closed subset of
K
and therefore is compact.
∎

We take
c>N\doteq\inf E(\bar{\xi})
. Then the set
E(\bar{\xi})\leq c
is not empty. By Lemma
1
this set is compact and therefore the continuous function
E(\bar{\xi})
reaches the minimal value on it, which is evidently equal to
N
. We proved the existence of global minimum
E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=\min E(\bar{\xi})
. At the minimum point
\bar{\xi}_{0}
the required condition
\nabla E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=0
holds and we conclude that system (
5
) has a solution.

The uniqueness of this solution follows from the uniqueness of a weak solution of our Riemann problem. Alternatively, this uniqueness can be derived from strict convexity of the entropy function
E(\bar{\xi})
(so that this function can have at most one extremum in
\Omega
). In view of representation (
6
) the strict convexity of the entropy easily follows from the lemma below.

Lemma 2
.

The function
P(x,y)=-\ln(F(x)-F(y))
is strictly convex in the half-plane
x>y
.

Proof.

The function
P(x,y)
is infinitely differentiable in the domain
x>y
. To prove the lemma, we need to establish that the Hessian
D^{2}P
is positive definite at every point. By the direct computation we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}P(x,y)=\frac{(F^{\prime}(x))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}P(x,y)=\frac{(F^{\prime}(y))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(y)(F(y)-F(x))}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}},\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}P(x,y)=-\frac{F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y)}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}}.

We have to prove positive definiteness of the matrix
Q=(F(x)-F(y))^{2}D^{2}P(x,y)
with the components

\displaystyle Q_{11}=(F^{\prime}(x))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y)),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=(F^{\prime}(y))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(y)(F(y)-F(x)),\ Q_{12}=Q_{21}=-F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y).

Since
F^{\prime}(x)=e^{-x^{2}/4}
, then
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{x}{2}F^{\prime}(x)
and the diagonal elements of this matrix can be written in the form

\displaystyle Q_{11}=F^{\prime}(x)(\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-F(y))+F^{\prime}(x))=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)(\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-F(y))+(F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)))+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=F^{\prime}(y)(\frac{y}{2}(F(y)-F(x))+(F^{\prime}(y)-F^{\prime}(x)))+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y).

By Cauchy mean value theorem there exists such a value
z\in(y,x)
that

\frac{F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)}{F(x)-F(y)}=\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(z)}{F^{\prime}(z)}=-z/2.

Therefore,

\displaystyle Q_{11}=F^{\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))(x-z)/2+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=F^{\prime}(y)(F(x)-F(y))(z-y)/2+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

and it follows that
Q=R_{1}+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y)R_{2}
, where
R_{1}
is a diagonal matrix with the positive diagonal elements

F^{\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))(x-z)/2
,
F^{\prime}(y)(F(x)-F(y))(z-y)/2
while
R_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Since
R_{1}>0
,
R_{2}\geq 0
, then the matrix
Q>0
, as was to be proved.
∎

Now we consider the case when some of the coefficients
a_{k}=0
. If
k=0
or
k=n
, the structure (
4
) of the solution remains the same but for
\xi<\xi_{1}
(respectively, for
\xi>\xi_{n}
) the solution becomes constant:
v\equiv u_{-}
(
v\equiv u_{+}
). Moreover, the discontinuity
\xi=\xi_{1}
(
\xi=\xi_{n}
) is now strong, and condition (
2
) implies the following relation of Stefan type

-(u_{1}-u_{-})\xi_{1}/2=\frac{a_{1}(u_{2}-u_{1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{1}/a_{1})}{F(\xi_{2}/a_{1})-F(\xi_{1}/a_{1})},

respectively,

(u_{+}-u_{n})\xi_{n}/2=\frac{a_{n-1}(u_{n}-u_{n-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{n}/a_{n-1})}{F(\xi_{n}/a_{n-1})-F(\xi_{n-1}/a_{n-1})}.

These relations replace, respectively, the first and the last equality in (
5
).
Notice that the first condition
[A(u)]=0
in (
2
) is satisfied because the function
A(u)
is constant on the segment
[u_{-},u_{1}]
(on
[u_{n},u_{+}]
).
Replacing the first term in sum (
6
) by
(u_{1}-u_{-})(\xi_{1})^{2}/4
(the last term by
(u_{+}-u_{n})(\xi_{n})^{2}/4
), we obtain that all the required conditions on the lines
\xi=\xi_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
, reduce again to the condition
\nabla E=0
, and the solution is uniquely determined by the minimum point of the entropy
E(\bar{\xi})
. Remark that after the described reduction the entropy function remains to satisfy the statements of Lemma
1
, Lemma
2
.

The case when the diffusion degenerates in an inner interval, i.e., when
a_{k}=0
for

0<k<n
, is more complicated. In this case two weak discontinuities
\xi=\xi_{k},\xi_{k+1}
merge into one strong discontinuity
\xi=\xi_{k}
with the limit values
v(\xi_{k}-)=u_{k}
,
v(\xi_{k}+)=u_{k+1}
, (one just need to put
\xi_{k+1}=\xi_{k}
in formula (
4
)).
Condition (
2
) on the line
\xi=\xi_{k}
reduces to the equality

-(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\xi_{k}/2=\frac{a_{k+1}(u_{k+2}-u_{k+1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{k}/a_{k+1})}{F(\xi_{k+2}/a_{k+1})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k+1})}-\frac{a_{k-1}(u_{k}-u_{k-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{k}/a_{k-1})}{F(\xi_{k}/a_{k-1})-F(\xi_{k-1}/a_{k-1})}.

This condition replaces two conditions with numbers
k,k+1
in system (
5
), where we also agree that
\xi_{k+1}=\xi_{k}
. Generally, we get the system of
n-l
equations with
n-l
unknowns, where
l
is a number of inner intervals
(u_{k},u_{k+1})
with degenerate diffusion (
a_{k}=0
). As above, we find that solutions of this system coincide with critical points of the entropy function
E(\bar{\xi})
of
n-l
variables defined by the expression

\displaystyle E(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=-\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n,a_{k}>0}(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\ln(F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n,a_{k}=0}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})(\xi_{k})^{2}/4,

(9)

where variables
\xi_{k}
,
\xi_{k+1}
are identified whenever
a_{k}=0
,
0<k<n
.

In the same way as in the nondegenerate case we can easily prove that this function is strictly convex and reaches the unique minimal value
E(\bar{\xi}_{0})
. Coordinates of the minimum point
\bar{\xi}_{0}
determine the unique weak solution of problem (
1
), (
3
).

Remark 1
.

Adding to the entropy (
On the structure of weak solutions to the Riemann problem for degenerate nonlinear diffusion equation
) the constant

\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n,a_{k}>0}(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\ln((u_{k+1}-u_{k})/a_{k}),

we obtain the alternative variant of the entropy

\displaystyle E_{1}(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n,a_{k}>0}(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\ln\left(\frac{F(\xi_{k+1}/a_{k})-F(\xi_{k}/a_{k})}{(u_{k+1}-u_{k})/a_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n,a_{k}=0}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})(\xi_{k})^{2}/4.

(10)

If we consider the values
a_{k}
as a piecewise constant approximation of an arbitrary diffusion function
a(u)\geq 0
then, passing in (
1
) to the limit as
\max(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\to 0
, we find that the entropy
E_{1}(\bar{\xi})
turns into the variational functional

J(\xi)=-\int_{\{u\in[u_{-},u_{+}],a(u)>0\}}(a(u))^{2}\ln(F^{\prime}(\xi(u)/a(u))\xi^{\prime}(u))du+\int_{\{u\in[u_{-},u_{+}],a(u)=0\}}(\xi(u))^{2}/4du,

where
\xi(u)
is an increasing function on
[u_{-},u_{+}]
, which is expected to be the inverse function to a self-similar solution
u=u(\xi)
to problem (
1
), (
3
). Taking into account that

\ln(F^{\prime}(\xi(u)/a(u))\xi^{\prime}(u))=\ln F^{\prime}(\xi(u)/a(u))+\ln\xi^{\prime}(u)=-\frac{(\xi(u))^{2}}{4a^{2}(u)}+\ln\xi^{\prime}(u),

we may simplify the expression for the functional
J(\xi)

J(\xi)=-\int_{u_{-}}^{u_{+}}(a(u))^{2}\ln(\xi^{\prime}(u))du+\frac{1}{4}\int_{u_{-}}^{u_{+}}(\xi(u))^{2}du.

(11)

We see that this functional is strictly convex. As is easy to verify, the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation has the form

\xi(u)/2+((a(u))^{2}/\xi^{\prime}(u))^{\prime}=0.

(12)

Since
u^{\prime}(\xi)=1/\xi^{\prime}(u)
,
u=u(\xi)
, we can transform (
12
) as follows

\xi(u)/2+((a(u))^{2}u^{\prime}(\xi))^{\prime}_{u}=0.

Multiplying this equation by
u^{\prime}(\xi)
, we obtain the equation

(a^{2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}=-\xi u^{\prime}/2,\quad u=u(\xi),

which is exactly our equation (
1
) written in the self-similar variable.
We conclude that minimization of the functional (
11
) gives a variational formulation of self-similar solutions of equation (
1
).

Virasoro Constraint for Uglov Matrix Model
Osama Khlaif, Taro Kimura

Abstract
We study the root of unity limit of ( q , t ) (\textbf{q},\textbf{t}) -deformed Virasoro matrix models, for which we call the resulting model Uglov matrix model. We derive the associated Virasoro constraints on the partition function, and find agreement of the central charge with the expression obtained from the level-rank duality associated with the parafermion CFT.

Introduction

In recent years, matrix models have been playing a crucial role in studying gauge and string theories. Via the supersymmetric localization principle first applied in
[
Pes12
]
, partition functions of several supersymmetric gauge theories were reduced into matrix models that could be studied more easily. See a review article
[
PZ+17
]
for more details. As a result of this reduction, many correspondences have been established between gauge theories and conformal field theories (CFTs) in 2d hence making the calculations of several physical observables, e.g., Wilson loops, more tractable, along the line of the BPS/CFT correspondence
[
Nek16
]
.

One interesting correspondence that has been studied lately is a 3d version of the BPS/CFT correspondence. It was found that the matrix model form of the 3d holomorphic blocks associated with
\mathcal{N}=2
3d supersymmetric Chern-Simons-Yang-Mills matter theory on
\mathbb{D}^{2}\times_{q}\mathbb{S}^{1}
is identified as that associated with the
(\textbf{q},\textbf{t})
-deformed Virasoro algebra
[
Pas12
,
BDP14
,
NPP15
]

[
NZ17
,
NNZ17
,
CLPZ19
,
CLZ20
]
.
See also a review article on this topic
[
Pas17
]
.
Based on this dictionary, some Virasoro limits were taken in the
q
-Virasoro CFT side that lead to matrix models which on the gauge theory side are associated with well-studied partition functions coming from supersymmetric localization.

Such correspondence was established by applying some techniques
[
MM90
,
DVV91
]
to derive the Ward identities (which in the case of Virasoro CFTs we refer to as
Virasoro constraints
). See also
[
Mor94
]
for a review on this direction. These constraints are an (infinite) set of PDEs that are solved by the matrix model under study. When the matrix model under consideration is a Virasoro matrix model, these constraints can be written in the form of modes that satisfy Virasoro algebraic relation with a particular central charge.

On the other hand, one can move in the opposite direction. One can start with a Virasoro algebra with some choice of the central charge and then can follow an algorithm to construct the associated matrix model. In this algorithm one looks for an operator referred to as a “screening current” such that, if integrated along a particular choice of contour, the resulting operator, which we will refer to as the “screening charge” commutes with the Virasoro modes and hence it solves the Virasoro constraints.

The purpose of this paper is to extend the CFT description of the matrix model to a class of models that we call the
Uglov matrix model
.
The partition function of degree
r
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\text{Uglov}}(\underline{\tau};N)=\frac{1}{N!}\int\prod_{j=1}^{N}dx_{j}\,e^{-\frac{N\beta}{2}V(x_{j})}\prod_{i<j}^{N}(x_{i}^{r}-x_{j}^{r})^{2(\beta-1)/r}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\,,

(1.1)

where we denote the potential function depending on the coupling constants
(\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
by
V(x)
.
See (
3.4
) for details.
The Uglov matrix model is obtained from the root of unity limit (Uglov limit) of the

-deformed matrix model,

2.1
(\textbf{q},\textbf{t})
-deformed Virasoro algebra

Take
\textbf{q},\textbf{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}=\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. We start by introducing the notion of
(\textbf{q},\textbf{t})
-deformed Virasoro algebra
which we will denote by
\mathcal{V}ir_{\textbf{q},\textbf{t}}
. This algebra is generated by the modes
\{\textbf{T}_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfying the relations
[
SKAO96
]

\left[\textbf{T}_{n},\textbf{T}_{m}\right]+\sum_{l\geq 1}\textbf{f}_{l}\left(\textbf{T}_{n-l}\textbf{T}_{m+l}-\textbf{T}_{m-l}\textbf{T}_{n+l}\right)=-\frac{(1-\textbf{q})(1-\textbf{t}^{-1})}{1-\textbf{p}}\left(\textbf{p}^{n}-\textbf{p}^{-n}\right)\delta_{n+m,0}.

(2.1)

The expansion coefficients
\textbf{f}_{l}
are defined via the expansion

\sum_{l\geq 0}\textbf{f}_{l}x^{l}:=\exp\left(\sum_{n>0}\frac{(1-\textbf{q}^{n})(1-\textbf{t}^{-n})}{n(1+\textbf{p}^{n})}x^{n}\right),

(2.2)

with the new parameter
p
defined as

\textbf{p}=\textbf{q}\textbf{t}^{-1}.

(2.3)

Free field realization

One interesting representation for the algebra (
2.1
) is the free boson representation. In this case we introduce the following
\mathcal{H}_{q,t}
Heisenberg algebra

\left[\textbf{a}_{n},\textbf{a}_{m}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\left(\textbf{q}^{\frac{n}{2}}-\textbf{q}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\right)\left(\textbf{t}^{\frac{n}{2}}-\textbf{t}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\right)\left(\textbf{p}^{\frac{n}{2}}+\textbf{p}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\right)\delta_{n+m,0},\quad\text{and},\quad\left[\textbf{a}_{n},\textbf{Q}\right]=\delta_{n,0}.

(2.4)

Taking the case where
\textbf{t}=\textbf{q}^{\beta}
, with
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, the modes of the
q
-deformed Virasoro can be written in terms of the Heisenberg modes above as
[
SKAO96
]
,

\textbf{T}(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\textbf{T}_{n}x^{-n}=\textbf{p}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{:\exp\left(-\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{x^{-n}}{(1+\textbf{p}^{n})}\textbf{a}_{n}\right):}\textbf{q}^{-\sqrt{\beta}\textbf{a}_{0}}+\textbf{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}{:\exp\left(\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{x^{-n}}{(1+\textbf{p}^{-n})}\textbf{a}_{n}\right):}\textbf{q}^{\sqrt{\beta}\textbf{a}_{0}}.

(2.5)

By normal ordering we mean putting the negative oscillators to the right of the positive ones and
the momentum operator

\textbf{a}_{0}
to the right of
the charge operator

Q
.

Scaling limit

Being a
q
-deformation of the usual Virasoro algebra, one expects to recover the usual Virasoro modes in the limit where one takes
\textbf{q}=e^{\hbar}
with
\hbar\rightarrow 0
. Applying this limiting case to the algebra (
2.1
) with
t
taking the particular form mentioned above, we end up with

\textbf{T}_{n}=2\delta_{n,0}+\hbar^{2}\beta\left(\textbf{L}_{n}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}^{2}\delta_{n,0}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{4}),

(2.6)

where

\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}:=\sqrt{\beta}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}},

(2.7)

and the generators
(\textbf{L}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy the Virasoro algebra

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{L}_{m}\right]=(n-m)\mathbf{L}_{n+m}+\frac{c}{12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0}\,,

(2.8)

with central charge given by

c=1-6\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}^{2}.

(2.9)

Screening current

Going back to
q
-Virasoro, we define a
screening-current

\mathcal{S}(x)
to be such that

\left[\textbf{T}(x),\mathcal{S}(x)\right]=\frac{\textbf{O}(\textbf{q}x)-\textbf{O}(x)}{x},

(2.10)

with
\textbf{O}(x)
being some function. It has been found
[
SKAO96
]
that there are two operators satisfying the this definition,

\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}(x):={:\exp\left(-\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{x^{-n}}{\textbf{q}^{\frac{n}{2}}-\textbf{q}^{-\frac{n}{2}}}\textbf{a}_{n}\right):}\exp\left(\sqrt{\beta}\textbf{Q}\right)x^{2\sqrt{\beta}\textbf{a}_{0}},

(2.11a)

\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{t}}(x):={:\exp\left(-\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{x^{-n}}{\textbf{t}^{-\frac{n}{2}}-\textbf{t}^{\frac{n}{2}}}\textbf{a}_{n}\right):}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{Q}}{\sqrt{\beta}}\right)x^{-\frac{2\textbf{a}_{0}}{\sqrt{\beta}}}.

(2.11b)

We can see that there is a similarity between these two solutions. Hence, we keep going with our discussion using only the first one.

The reason behind referring to such operators as “currents” is that one can pick a particular contour

(2.12)

2.2
\mathbf{q}
-Virasoro matrix model

As the last object to introduce in this section we define the
q
-Virasoro matrix model
, denoted by
\mathcal{Z}_{\textbf{q}}
to be the solution of

-Virasoro constraints
[

,
NZ17
,
NNZ17
,

,
CLPZ19
,
CLZ20
]

\textbf{T}_{n>0}\mathcal{Z}_{\textbf{q}}=0.

(2.13)

With the construction of the screening currents we made above we note that these equations can be solved by taking

\mathcal{Z}_{\textbf{q}}=\mathcal{J}_{\textbf{q}}^{N},

(2.14)

for some positive integer

To see the matrix model form more explicitly we first need to note that if we take
\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}^{+}(x)
(
\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}^{-}(x)
) to be the part of the exponential containing the positive (negative) Heisenberg oscillators in (

\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}^{+}(x_{i})\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}^{-}(x_{j})=\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}^{-}(x_{j})\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}^{+}(x_{i})\frac{\left(x_{j}x_{i}^{-1};\textbf{q}\right)_{\infty}\left(\textbf{q}\textbf{t}^{-1}x_{j}x_{i}^{-1};\textbf{q}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\textbf{t}x_{j}x_{i}^{-1};\textbf{q}\right)_{\infty}\left(\textbf{q}x_{j}x_{i}^{-1};\textbf{q}\right)_{\infty}}.

(2.15)

Here we used the
q
-Pochhammer symbol

\left(x;\textbf{q}\right)_{\infty}:=\prod_{k\geq 0}\left(1-\textbf{q}^{k}x\right).

(2.16)

With this observation one can deduce the following OPE for the
q

\prod_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}(x_{j})={:\prod_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{S}_{\textbf{q}}(x_{j}):}\Delta_{\beta}(\underline{x};\textbf{q})\textbf{c}_{\beta}(\underline{x};\textbf{q})\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{\beta(N-1)},

(2.17)

where
\Delta_{\beta}(\underline{x};\textbf{q})
is the
q
-Vandermonde determinant

\Delta_{\beta}(\underline{x};\textbf{q}):=\prod_{k\neq j}^{N}\frac{(x_{k}x_{j}^{-1};\textbf{q})_{\infty}}{(\textbf{t}x_{k}x_{j}^{-1};\textbf{q})_{\infty}}

(2.18)

Meanwhile, the other factor is given by

\textbf{c}_{\beta}(\underline{x};\textbf{q}):=\prod_{k<j}(x_{k}x_{j}^{-1})^{\beta}\frac{\Theta(\textbf{t}x_{k}x_{j}^{-1};\textbf{q})}{\Theta(x_{k}x_{j}^{-1};\textbf{q})}.

(2.19)

Here we also introduced the
\Theta
-function defined in terms of
q
-Pochhammer symbol
as

\Theta(x;\textbf{q}):=(x;\textbf{q})_{\infty}\left(\textbf{q}x^{-1};\textbf{q}\right)_{\infty}

(2.20)

Going back to the Heisenberg algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q,t}
in (
2.4
) we define the vacuum state to be annihilated by all the positive oscillators along with the momentum operator. Moreover, due to the non-vanishing commutator of the momentum with the charge operator, one can construct charged-vacuum states which are momentum eigenstates:

\left|\alpha\right>:=\exp\left(\alpha\textbf{Q}\right)\left|0\right>,\quad\textbf{a}_{0}\left|\alpha\right>=\alpha\left|\alpha\right>.

(2.21)

An interesting point about this observation is that now we can pick a representation of the algebra (
2.4
) such that we can explicitly write down the matrix model form of
\mathcal{Z}_{\textbf{q}}
defined in (
2.14
). In order to accomplish this, let’s consider the following representation in terms of the

\underline{\tau}:=(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\cdots)

[
NNZ17
]

\textbf{a}_{-n>0}\sim\left(\textbf{q}^{\frac{n}{2}}-\textbf{q}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\right)\tau_{n},\quad\textbf{a}_{n>0}\sim\frac{1}{n}\left(\textbf{t}^{\frac{n}{2}}-\textbf{t}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\right)\left(\textbf{p}^{\frac{n}{2}}+\textbf{p}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau_{n}}.

(2.22)

As for the momentum and charge operators,

\textbf{Q}\sim\sqrt{\beta}\tau_{0},\quad\textbf{a}_{0}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau_{0}},\quad\text{and,}\quad\left|\alpha\right>\sim\exp\left(\sqrt{\beta}\tau_{0}\right)\cdot 1.

(2.23)

With respect to the representation, the matrix model associated with
q
-Virasoro algebra is given by,

\mathcal{Z}_{\textbf{q}}\left|\alpha\right>\sim\mathcal{Z}_{\textbf{q}}(\underline{\tau})=:\mathcal{N}_{\textbf{q}}\oint_{\cup_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{j}}{2\pi\textbf{i}}\Delta_{\beta}(\underline{x};\textbf{q})\textbf{c}_{\beta}(\underline{x};\textbf{q})\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{\textbf{q}}\left(x_{j}\mid\underline{\tau}\right)\right),

(2.24)

where the normalization factor and the potential function are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\textbf{q}}

\displaystyle:=\exp\left(\kappa_{0}\tau_{0}\right),

(2.25a)

\displaystyle V_{\textbf{q}}(x\mid\underline{\tau})

\displaystyle:=\kappa_{1}\ln x+\sum_{n>0}\tau_{n}x^{n},

(2.25b)

with

\kappa_{0}:=N+{\alpha}\sqrt{\beta},\quad\text{and,}\quad\kappa_{1}:=\sqrt{\beta}\left(\alpha+\sqrt{\beta}N-\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}\right).

(2.26)

The measure of this matrix model is the same as that of the so-called 3d holomorphic blocks
[
Pas12
,
BDP14
,
NPP15
]
coming from localizing the partition function associated with
\mathcal{N}=2

U(N)
supersymmetric Chern-Simon-Yang-Mills theory on
\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
with a matter multiplet in the adjoint representation
[

]

[

]
a correspondence can be established between 3d

q
-Virasoro CFT where the parameters
\kappa_{0}
and
\kappa_{1}
defined above are translated in terms of the Chern-Simons level and FI coupling constants of the 3d theory.

\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\colon\mathcal{A}_{r}(x_{i}\mid\underline{\textbf{a}})\colon}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\left(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\beta}{r}}{\colon\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{A}_{r}(x_{i}\mid\underline{\textbf{a}})\colon}.

(3.10)

Moreover, observing that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}\sum_{n>0}\frac{x^{n+\frac{\ell}{r}}}{n+\frac{\ell}{r}}=\log\frac{1-x}{\left(1-x^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{r}},

(3.11)

one can compute the OPE coming from the
\mathcal{B}_{r}
-factor of the Uglov current to be

\mathcal{B}_{r}^{(+)}(x_{i}\mid\underline{\tilde{\textbf{a}}})\mathcal{B}_{r}^{(-)}(x_{j}\mid\underline{\tilde{\textbf{a}}})=\left(1-\frac{x_{j}^{\frac{1}{r}}}{x_{i}^{\frac{1}{r}}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\right)^{\frac{2}{r}}\mathcal{B}_{r}^{(-)}(x_{j}\mid\underline{\tilde{\textbf{a}}})\mathcal{B}_{r}^{(+)}(x_{i}\mid\underline{\tilde{\textbf{a}}}).

(3.12)

This can be generalized for the case of
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
currents as

\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\colon\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{i}\mid\underline{\tilde{\textbf{a}}})\colon}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\left(1-\frac{x_{j}^{\frac{1}{r}}}{x_{i}^{\frac{1}{r}}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\right)^{\frac{2}{r}}\cdot{\colon\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{i}\mid\underline{\tilde{\textbf{a}}})\colon}.

(3.13)

Putting these results together along with the factor resulting from the non-vanishing commutator of the
\textbf{a}_{0}
with the charge operator
Q
, we end up with the following OPE

\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{S}_{\text{Uglov}}(x_{i})=\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{\beta}(\underline{x}){:\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{S}_{\text{Uglov}}(x_{i}):},

(3.14)

with the OPE factor

\mathcal{D}_{\beta}^{(r)}(\underline{x}):=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\left(x_{i}^{\frac{1}{r}}-x_{j}^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{2}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)^{2\frac{\beta-1}{r}}=\Delta_{\text{Uglov}}^{(r)}\left(\underline{x}^{\frac{1}{r}}\right),

(3.15)

where from the last equality we can see that matching between this measure with that of Uglov matrix model we derived in (
3.3
).

Virasoro Constraint of Uglov Matrix Model

Going back to the model (
3.4
) we now come to extracting what Virasoro algebra is it associated with. Following

MM90
,

we will work out the explicit form of the Virasoro modes where, as we shall see, this can be explicitly done for a particular subset of these modes.

Free field realization

To do this, let us start with the observation that the time-derivative of the integrand of (
3.4
) yields the power sum symmetric polynomial

p_{n}(\underline{x}):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{n}=-\frac{2n}{N\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau_{n}}\log\textbf{Z}_{N}(\underline{x}\mid\underline{\tau}).

(4.1)

Taking the following representation for
n>0

\textbf{a}_{n}\sim\frac{2n}{N\sqrt{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau_{n}},\quad\textbf{a}_{-n}\sim\frac{N\sqrt{\beta}}{2}\tau_{n},

(4.2)

one can see that these modes are Heisenberg oscillators in the sense that

[\textbf{a}_{n},\textbf{a}_{m}]=n\delta_{n+m,0}.

(4.3)

This implies that the integrand of Uglov model is an eigenstate of the positive-Heisenberg oscillators

p_{n}(\underline{x})\textbf{Z}_{N}(\underline{x}\mid\underline{\tau})=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}\textbf{a}_{n}\textbf{Z}_{N}(\underline{x}\mid\underline{\tau}).

(4.4)

Ward identity

The Virasoro constraints are nothing other than the Ward identities resulting from the invariance of our model (
3.4
) under the shift

\delta_{n}x_{i}:=\epsilon_{n}x_{i}^{n+1}.

(4.5)

Under this shift, the Vandermonde-like part of the measure changes as

\delta_{n}\Delta_{\text{Uglov}}^{(r)}(\underline{x})=\Delta_{\text{Uglov}}^{(r)}(\underline{x})\cdot\left(2\sum_{i<j}\frac{x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{j}^{n+1}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}+2kr\sum_{i<j}\frac{x_{i}^{r+n}-x_{j}^{r+n}}{x_{i}^{r}-x_{j}^{r}}\right).

(4.6)

Meanwhile, the other part of the measure changes as

\delta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{N}dx_{j}=\epsilon_{n}(n+1)\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{n}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{N}dx_{j}=\epsilon_{n}(n+1)p_{n}(\underline{x})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{N}dx_{j}.

(4.7)

As for the argument of the exponential,

\delta_{n}\left(-\frac{N\beta}{2}\sum_{m>0}\frac{\tau_{m}}{m}p_{m}(\underline{x})\right)=-\epsilon_{n}\frac{N\beta}{2}\sum_{m>0}\tau_{m}p_{m+n}(\underline{x}).

(4.8)

To re-write (
4.6
) in terms of the polynomials
p_{n}(\underline{x})
we note the identity

\sum_{i\neq j}^{N}\frac{x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{j}^{n+1}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}p_{m}(\underline{x})p_{n-m}(\underline{x})-(n+1)p_{n}(\underline{x}).

(4.9)

This identity works for positive modes
n
. At the time of writing this article we did not have a more generic identity that we could apply for the the second sum in (
4.6
) so we are restricting to the case where
n=r\tilde{n}
for some positive
\tilde{n}
.

.
From this point of view, the mode

is not
\mathbb{Z}_{r}

In this case, noting that
p_{n}(\underline{x^{r}})=p_{rn}(\underline{x})
, we can see that the second sum can be written as

\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{x_{i}^{r\tilde{n}+r}-x_{j}^{r\tilde{n}+r}}{x_{i}^{r}-x_{j}^{r}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\tilde{n}}p_{rm}(\underline{x})p_{r\tilde{n}-rm}(\underline{x})-(\tilde{n}+1)p_{r\tilde{n}}(\underline{x}).

(4.10)

Putting these results together, the corresponding Ward identity is

\delta_{rn}\mathcal{Z}_{\text{Uglov}}(\underline{\tau};N)=0\Rightarrow\\
\left<-\frac{N\beta}{2}\sum_{m>0}\tau_{m}p_{m+rn}(\underline{x})-kr(n+1)p_{rn}(\underline{x})+kr\sum_{m=0}^{n}p_{rm}(\underline{x})p_{rn-rm}(\underline{x})+\sum_{m=0}^{rn}p_{m}(\underline{x})p_{rn-m}(\underline{x})\right>=0,

(4.11)

which, using the representation (
4.2
), can be re-written as the form of the Virasoro constraint

\textbf{L}_{rn}\mathcal{Z}_{\text{Uglov}}(\underline{\tau};N)=0\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 0

(4.12)

The differential operator is now given by

\textbf{L}_{rn}:=\sum_{m>0}\textbf{a}_{-m}\textbf{a}_{m+rn}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{m=0}^{rn}\textbf{a}_{m}\textbf{a}_{rn-m}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}}{\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\textbf{a}_{rm}\textbf{a}_{rn-rm}+\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}(n+1)\textbf{a}_{rn}.

(4.13)

Virasoro generator of degree
r

We have the decomposition

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{rn}\textbf{a}_{m}\textbf{a}_{rn-m}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\textbf{a}_{rm}\textbf{a}_{rn-rm}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\textbf{a}_{rm+\ell}\textbf{a}_{rn-rm-\ell}\,,

(4.14)

which, along with the redefinition
(\textbf{a}_{-n>0},\textbf{a}_{n>0})\mapsto(\sqrt{2}\textbf{a}_{-n>0},1/\sqrt{2}\textbf{a}_{n>0})
, yields the following expression of the modes (

\displaystyle\textbf{L}_{rn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m>0}\textbf{a}_{-m}\textbf{a}_{rn+m}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\textbf{a}_{rm}\textbf{a}_{rn-rm}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}}{\sqrt{2}}(n+1)\textbf{a}_{rn}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}\left[\sum_{m>0}\textbf{a}_{-rm-\ell}\textbf{a}_{rn+rm+\ell}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\textbf{a}_{rm+\ell}\textbf{a}_{rn-rm-\ell}\right]\,.

(4.15)

We see that it obeys the Virasoro algebraic relation,

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{L}_{rn},\mathbf{L}_{rn^{\prime}}\right]=r(n-n^{\prime})\mathbf{L}_{r(n+n^{\prime})}\,,\qquad n,n^{\prime}\geq 0\,.

(4.16)

We define a new operator for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(r,\ell)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\sum_{m>1}\textbf{a}_{-rm}\textbf{a}_{rn+rm}+\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\textbf{a}_{rm}\textbf{a}_{rn-rm}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}}{\sqrt{2}r}(n+1)\textbf{a}_{rn}&(\ell=0)\\

\displaystyle\textbf{L}_{rn}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}rL^{(r,\ell)}_{n}\,.

\displaystyle\left[L^{(r,\ell)}_{n},L^{(r,\ell^{\prime})}_{n^{\prime}}\right]=(n-n^{\prime})\,L^{(r,\ell)}_{n+n^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\,,\qquad n,n^{\prime}\geq 0\,.

\displaystyle\textbf{L}_{n}^{(r,\ell)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}{:\textbf{a}_{-rm}\textbf{a}_{rn+rm}:}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}}{\sqrt{2}r}(n+1)\textbf{a}_{rn}&(\ell=0)\\

5
Discussion

In this paper, we have discussed the root of unity limit of the
q
-Virasoro matrix model, and explored the associated Virasoro constraint.
We have clarified that the corresponding central charge agrees with the conjectural result obtained from the degree
r
generalization of the minimal model CFT perspective.

We address several issues along the direction discussed in this paper.
The first is the origin of the Uglov matrix model itself.
In this paper, we have formally defined the Uglov matrix model from the root of unity limit of the
q
-deformed matrix model.
Since the
q
-matrix model has the interpretation as the 3d gauge theory partition function, it would be possible that the Uglov matrix model also has a similar gauge theory interpretation.
From this point of view, recalling that the Uglov limit of the 5d gauge theory on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
is reduced to the 4d theory on the orbifold
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{r}
(or its resolution
\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{r}}
), it is expected that the Uglov matrix model may have a link with the 2d gauge theory on the orbifold,
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}_{r}
.
It would be interesting to derive the Uglov matrix model from the gauge theory perspective on the 2d orbifold as discussed in
[
KN11
,
FKNO12
]
.

The second is a generalization of the Uglov-Virasoro constraint to the W-constraint.
As is known that the W-constraint is naturally obtained from the multi-matrix model, it seems to be reasonable to study the multi-matrix version of the Uglov matrix model.
In fact, the original conjecture of the central charge given in
[
Kim12b
]
is

\displaystyle c=r(N-1)-\frac{N(N^{2}-1)}{r}\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}^{2}\,,

(5.1)

for the associated W
N
algebra (The Virasoro algebra corresponds to
N=2
).
It would be interesting to reproduce this central charge from the W-constraint of the multi-matrix analog of the Uglov matrix model.

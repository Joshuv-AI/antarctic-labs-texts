Abstract
. In this paper, a new P 2 − P 1 P_{2}-P_{1} finite element pair is proposed for incompressible fluid. For this pair, the discrete inf-sup condition and the discrete Korn’s inequality hold on general triangulations. It yields exactly divergence-free velocity approximations when applied to models of incompressible flows. The robust capacity of the pair for incompressible flows are verified theoretically and numerically.

1.
Introduction

The property of conservation plays a key role in the modeling of many physical systems. For the Stokes problem, for example, if a stable finite element pair can inherit the mass conservation, the approximation of the velocity can be independent of the pressure and the method does not suffer from the locking effect with respect to large Reynolds’ numbers (c.f., e.g.,
[
8
]
). The importance of conservative schemes is also significant in, e.g., the nonlinear mechanics
[
2
,
3
]
and the magnetohydrodynamics
[
26
,
24
,
27
]
. In this paper, we focus on the conservative scheme for the Stokes-type problems for incompressible flows. As Stokes-type problems are applied widely to not only fluid problems but also elastic models such as the earth model with a fluid core
[
13
]
and they are immediately related to many other model problems, their conservative schemes can be relevant and helpful to more other equations.

Most classical stable Stokes pairs relax the divergence-free constraint by enforcing the condition in the weak sense, and the conservation can be preserved strictly only for special examples. Though, during the past decade, the conservative schemes have been recognized more clearly as
pressure robustness
and widely studied and surveyed in, e.g.,
[
18
,
22
,
29
,
36
]
. This conservation is also related to other key features such as “viscosity-independent”
[
40
]
and “gradient-robustness”
[
31
]
for numerical schemes. There have been various successful examples along different technical approaches. Efforts have been devoted to the construction of conforming conservative pairs, and extra structural assumptions are generally needed for the subdivision and finite element functions. Examples include conforming elements designed for special meshes, such as
P_{k}-P_{k-1}
triangular elements for
k\geqslant 4
on singular-vertex-free meshes
[
37
]
and for smaller
k
constructed on composite grids
[
1
,
37
,
35
,
46
,
43
]
, and the pairs given in
[
16
,
22
]
which work for general triangulations but with extra smoothness requirement and more complicated shape function spaces. A natural way to relax the constraints is to use
H({\rm div})
-conforming but
(H^{1})^{2}
-nonconforming finite element functions for the velocity. For example, in
[
33
]
, a reduced cubic polynomial space which is
H({\rm div})
-conforming and
(H^{1})^{2}
-nonconforming is used for the velocity and piecewise constant for the pressure. The pair is both stable and conservative on general triangulations. The velocity space of
[
33
]
can be recognized as a modification of an
H({\rm div})
-conforming space by adding some normal-bubble-like functions to enforce weak continuity of tangential component. Several conservative pairs are constructed subsequently in, e.g.,
[
20
,
39
,
41
]
. Generally, to construct a conservative pair that works on general triangulations without special structures, cubic and higher-degree polynomials are used for the velocity. Besides, For conservative pairs in three-dimension, we refer to, e.g.,
[
23
,
45
,
49
]
where composite grids are required, as well as
[
21
,
48
]
where high degree local polynomials are utilized. We refer to
[
10
,
28
,
47
]
for rectangular grids and
[
34
]
for cubic grids where full advantage of the geometric symmetry of the cells are taken.

In this paper, we propose a new
P_{2}-P_{1}
finite element pair on triangulations; for the velocity field, we use piecewise quadratic
H({\rm div})
functions whose tangential component is continuous in the average sense, and for the pressure, we use discontinuous piecewise linear functions. The pair is stable and immediately strictly conservative on general triangulations. Further, a discrete Korn’s inequality holds for the velocity. The capability of the pair is verified both theoretically and numerically. When applied to the Stokes and the Darcy–Stokes–Brinkman problems, the approximation of the velocity is independent of the small parameters and thus locking-free; numerical experiments verify the validity of the theory. We note that, as the tangential component of the velocity function is continuous only in the average sense, the convergence rate can only be proved to be of
\mathcal{O}(h)
order. However, since the pair is conservatively stable on general triangulations, it plays superior to some
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
schemes numerically in robustness with respect to triangulations and with respect to small parameters. The performance of the pair on the Navier–Stokes equation is also illustrated numerically. More applications in other model problems for both the source problems and the eigenvalue problems may be studied in future.

For the newly designed space for velocity, all the degrees of freedom are located on edges of the triangulation. It is thus impossible to construct a commutative nodal interpolator with a non-constant pressure space. To prove the inf-sup condition, we adopt Stenberg’s macroelement technique
[
38
]
. On every macroelement, the surjection property of the divergence operator is confirmed by figuring out its kernel space. This figures out a structure of discretized Stokes complex on any local macroelements. On the other hand, similar to the study of conservative pairs in
[
16
,
22
]
and the study of biharmonic finite elements in
[
51
,
15
,
50
,
44
]
, the proposed global space will be embedded in a discretized Stokes complex on the whole triangulation; this will be studied in detail in future.

The method given uses an
H({\rm div})
-conforming and
(H^{1})^{2}
-nonconforming finite element for the velocity. Indeed, the space given here is a reduced subspace of the second order Brezzi-Douglas-Marini element
[
7
]
space by enhancing smoothness. This way, the proposed pair is different from most existing
H({\rm div})
-conforming and
(H{}^{1})^{2}
-nonconforming methods which propose to add bubble-like basis functions on some specific
H({\rm div})
finite element space. Moreover, as we use quadratic polynomials only for velocity, to the best of our knowledge, this is the lowest-degree conservative stable pair for the Stokes problem on general triangulations. More stable and conservative pairs may be designed by reducing other
H({\rm div})
-conforming elements. The possible generalization of the proposed pair to three-dimensional case will also be discussed.

Finally we remark that, besides these finite element methods mentioned above, an alternative is to construct specially discrete variational forms onto
H({\rm div})
functions where extra stabilizations may play roles; works such as the discontinuous Galerkin method, the weak Galerkin method, and the virtual element method all fall into this category. There have been many valuable works of these types, but we do not seek to give a complete survey and thus will not discuss them in the present paper. We only note that natural connections between the proposed pair and DG-type methods may be expected under the framework of
[
25
]
; along the lines of
[
14
]
, these connections may be expected helpful for the construction of optimal solvers for the DG schemes.

2.2.3.
Proof of Theorem

By Lemma

The inf-sup stability of
\undertilde{V}{}_{hD}\times Q_{h}
is proved utilizing the technique introduced in
[

by the following four steps.

Step 1.

Given
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
, let
\overline{q}_{h}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}q_{h}\,d\Omega
and
q^{*}_{h}=q_{h}-\overline{q}_{h}
. Then
q^{*}_{h}\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}=\|\overline{q}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|q^{*}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}.

Step 2.

By (
2.5
), there exists some
\undertilde{v}^{*}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h0}
, such that

(2.9)

\displaystyle({\rm div}\,\undertilde{v}{}_{h}^{*},q_{h}^{*})\geqslant C_{1}\|q_{h}^{*}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}^{*}|_{1,h}=\|q_{h}^{*}\|_{0,\Omega},

where

is a generic constant independent of

Step 3.

Let
\Gamma_{N}:=\partial\Omega\backslash\Gamma_{D}
. Notice that
\overline{q}_{h}
is constant in
\Omega
. Let
\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{hD}
satisfy that
\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n_{e}}=C_{0}\overline{q}_{h}|_{e}
for any

, and other degrees of freedom vanish. The value of

C_{2}:=C_{0}\frac{|\Gamma_{N}|}{|\Omega|}

(2.10)

({\rm div}\,\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h},\overline{q}_{h})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}{\rm div}\,\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h}\,\overline{q}_{h}\,dT=\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{N}}\int_{e}\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,\overline{q}_{h}\,ds=C_{2}\|\overline{q}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}.

Step 4.
Let
\undertilde{v}{}_{h}=\undertilde{v}{}_{h}^{*}+\kappa\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h}
with
\kappa=\frac{2C_{1}C_{2}}{(C_{2})^{2}+2}
. By the Schwarz inequality, the elementary inequality, and (

)–(
2.10

\begin{split}({\rm div}\,\undertilde{v}{}_{h},q_{h})&=({\rm div}\,\undertilde{v}{}_{h}^{*},q_{h}^{*})+\kappa({\rm div}\,\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h},\overline{q}_{h})+\kappa({\rm div}\,\overline{\undertilde{v}}{}_{h},q_{h}^{*})\\

This completes the proof of (

.

Lemma 2.11
.

For any
\undertilde{v}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{hD}

\undertilde{z}\in\undertilde{C}{}_{h}^{N}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\nabla\undertilde{v}:{\rm curl}\,\undertilde{z}\,dT=0.

2.3.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

For any
\undertilde{v}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{hD}
,

\epsilon(\undertilde{v})|_{T}=(\nabla\undertilde{v}-\frac{1}{2}{\rm rot}\,\undertilde{v}\ \chi)|_{T}
,
where
\chi=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
. From (
2.11
) and
{\rm rot}\,\undertilde{v}\in Q_{h}
, there exists some
\undertilde{z}\in\undertilde{C}{}_{h}^{N}
, such that

\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}\,\undertilde{z}\,q\,d\Omega=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}{\rm rot}\,\undertilde{v}\,q\,dT,\quad\forall q\in Q_{h}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\undertilde{z}|_{1,h}\lesssim\|{\rm rot}\,\undertilde{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim|\undertilde{v}|_{1,h}.

Therefore,

\|\nabla\undertilde{v}-{\rm curl}\,\undertilde{z}\|_{0,\Omega}\leqslant|\undertilde{v}|_{1,h}+|\undertilde{z}|_{1,h}\lesssim|v|_{1,h},

and

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\epsilon(\undertilde{v})\,:\,(\nabla\undertilde{v}-{\rm curl}\,\undertilde{z})\,dT=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}(\nabla\undertilde{v}-\frac{1}{2}{\rm rot}\,\undertilde{v}\ \chi)\,:\,(\nabla\undertilde{v}-{\rm curl}\,\undertilde{z})\,dT\\

3.
Application to Stokes problems

3.1.
Application to the Stokes equations

Consider the stationary Stokes system:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{split}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\,\undertilde{u}+\nabla\,p&=\undertilde{f}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}&=g\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
\undertilde{u}&=0,\quad\mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{D}.\end{split}\right.

For simplicity of presentation, we only consider the case of
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{D}
herein. Extensions to other boundary conditions follows directly.

The discretization scheme of (
3.1
) reads:
Find
(\undertilde{u}{}_{h},p_{h})\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h0}\times Q_{h*}
,
such that

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{split}\varepsilon^{2}\big(\nabla_{h}\,\undertilde{u}{}_{h},\nabla_{h}\,\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\big)-({\rm div}\,\undertilde{v}{}_{h},p_{h})&=\langle\undertilde{f},\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\rangle\quad\forall\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h},\\
({\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}{}_{h},q_{h})&=\langle g,q_{h}\rangle\quad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}.\end{split}\right.

Based on the discussions in Section
2
, Brezzi’s conditions can be easily verified, and (
3.2
) is uniformly well-posed with respect to
\varepsilon
and
h
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\undertilde{u},p)
and
(\undertilde{u}{}_{h},p_{h})
be the solutions of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), respectively. The following estimates hold with
0<r\leqslant 2

\displaystyle|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r}|\undertilde{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+h|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega},

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r}|p|_{r,\Omega}+\varepsilon^{2}\big(h^{r}|\undertilde{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+h|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}\big).

Proof.

Since the mixed element is inf-sup stable, divergence-free, and
\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}
is continuous, the following estimates are standard
[
6
,
8
,
12
]
:

\displaystyle|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}\lesssim\inf_{\undertilde{w}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h}}|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{w}{}_{h}|_{1,h}+\sup_{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{Z}{}_{h}(0)}\frac{\big|\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{e}(\nabla\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e})\cdot\llbracket{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}}\rrbracket\,ds\big|}{\varepsilon^{2}|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}|_{1,h}},

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}+\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\|p-q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\sup_{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h}}\frac{\big|\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{e}(\nabla\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e})\cdot\llbracket{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}}\rrbracket\,ds\big|}{|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}|_{1,h}}.

The term
\inf_{\undertilde{w}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h}}|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{w}{}_{h}|_{1,h}
is bounded by the interpolation error. Since
\int_{e}\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\,ds
is continuous across interior edges and vanish on
\partial\Omega
, a standard estimate similar to that of the Crouzeix and Raviart element
[
11
, Lemma 3]
leads to

(3.3)

\displaystyle\big|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{e}(\nabla\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e})\cdot\llbracket{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}}\rrbracket\,ds\big|\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}h|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}|_{1,h}.

Hence we derive

|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}\lesssim h^{r}|\undertilde{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+h|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}\ \mbox{ with }\ 0<r\leqslant 2.

The above estimates together with
\inf\limits_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\|p-q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{r}|p|_{r,\Omega}
lead to that

\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{r}|p|_{r,\Omega}+\varepsilon^{2}\big(h^{r}|\undertilde{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+h|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}\big)\ \mbox{ with }\ 0<r\leqslant 2.

The proof is completed.
∎

3.2.
Application to the Darcy–Stokes–Brinkman equations

Consider the Darcy–Stokes–Brinkman equations:

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{split}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\,\undertilde{u}+\undertilde{u}+\nabla p&=\undertilde{f}\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}&=g\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\ \varepsilon\,\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{t}&=0\quad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{split}\right.

where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
is a parameter. When
\varepsilon
is not too small and
g=0
, it is a Stokes problem with an additional lower order term. When
\varepsilon=0
, the first equation becomes the Darcy’s law for porous medium flow. Most classic mixed elements fail to converge uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon
when applied to (
3.4
)
[
33
]
.

The discretization scheme of (
3.4
) reads:
Find
(\undertilde{u}{}_{h},p_{h})\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h0}\times Q_{h*}
,
such that

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{split}\varepsilon^{2}\big(\nabla_{h}\,\undertilde{u}{}_{h},\nabla_{h}\,\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\big)+(\undertilde{u}{}_{h},\undertilde{v}{}_{h})-({\rm div}\,\undertilde{v}{}_{h},p_{h})&=\langle\undertilde{f},\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\rangle\quad\forall\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h},\\
({\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}{}_{h},q_{h})&=\langle g,q_{h}\rangle\quad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}.\end{split}\right.

Since the finite element pair is stable and conservative, Brezzi’s conditions can be easily verified for (
3.5
), and it is uniformly well-posed with respect to
\varepsilon
and
h
, provided
\int_{\Omega}g\,d\Omega=0
. Robust convergence can be obtained both for smooth continuous solutions and for the case that the effect of the
\varepsilon
-dependent boundary layers is taken into account later.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
\undertilde{u}\in\undertilde{H}{}^{r+1}(\Omega)\cap\undertilde{H}{}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
p\in H^{r}(\Omega)\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
with
0<r\leqslant 2
, then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}-{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}{}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{r}|\undertilde{u}|_{r+1,\Omega},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}\lesssim h^{r}(\varepsilon+h)|\undertilde{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+\varepsilon h|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{r}|p|_{r,\Omega}+h^{r}(\varepsilon+h)|\undertilde{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+\varepsilon h|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}.

Proof.

Evidently,
{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}{}_{h}={\rm P}_{Q_{h*}}({\rm div}\,\undertilde{u})
, where
{\rm P}_{Q_{h*}}
represents the
L^{2}
-projection into
Q_{h*}
. Therefore, the first inequality (
3.6
) follows from the estimation of the
L^{2}
-projection. For this conservative pair, the following estimates are standard (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
and
[
33
]
),

(3.9)

\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}\lesssim\\
\inf_{\undertilde{w}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h}}(\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{w}{}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{w}{}_{h}|_{1,h})+\sup_{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{Z}{}_{h}(0)}\frac{\big|\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{e}(\nabla\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e})\cdot\llbracket{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}}\rrbracket\,ds\big|}{\varepsilon|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}|_{1,h}},

(3.10)

\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}\\
+\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\|p-q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\sup_{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\in\undertilde{V}{}_{h}}\frac{\big|\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{e}(\nabla\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e})\cdot\llbracket{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}}\rrbracket\,ds\big|}{\varepsilon|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}|_{1,h}}.

Hence (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) are derived in a similar way as those in Theorem
3.1
.
∎

As is mentioned in
[
33
]
, it may happen that
|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}
and
|\undertilde{u}|_{3,\Omega}
blow up as
\varepsilon
tends to 0. In this case, the convergence
estimates given in Theorem
3.2
will deteriorate, especially when
the solution of (
3.4
) has boundary layers. To derive a uniform convergence analysis of the discrete solutions, we assume that
\Omega
is a convex polygon . Let
\big\{a_{j}:=(x_{j},y_{j})\big\}
denote the set of corner nodes of
\Omega
. Define

\displaystyle H_{+}^{1}(\Omega):=\Big\{g\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega):\ \int_{\Omega}\frac{|g(x,y)|}{(x-x_{j})^{2}+(y-y_{j})^{2}}\,d\Omega<\infty,\ j=1,2,\ldots,l\,\Big\},

with associated norm

\displaystyle\|g\|_{1,+}^{2}:=\|g\|_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|g(x,y)|}{(x-x_{j})^{2}+(y-y_{j})^{2}}\,d\Omega.

Let
(\undertilde{u}{}^{0},p^{0})
solves (
3.4
) in the case of
\varepsilon=0
. Then it is proved in
[
33
]
that

(3.11)

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{2}\|\undertilde{u}\|_{2,\Omega}+\varepsilon\|\undertilde{u}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}^{0}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p-p^{0}\|_{1,\Omega}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\undertilde{u}{}^{0}\|_{1,\Omega}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|p^{0}\|_{1,\Omega}\\
\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}\big),\end{split}

where
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm rot}}:=\|\cdot\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\rm rot}(\cdot)\|_{0,\Omega}
is the norm defined in
\undertilde{H}{}({\rm rot},\Omega)
.
Following the technique in
[
33
]
, we can obtain the following uniform convergence estimate.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(\undertilde{u},p)
be the exact solution of (
3.4
) and
(\undertilde{u}{}_{h},p_{h})
be its approximation in
\undertilde{V}{}_{h0}\times Q_{h*}
.
If
\undertilde{f}\in\undertilde{H}{}({\rm rot},\Omega)
and
g\in H_{+}^{1}(\Omega)
, then

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}-{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}{}_{h}\|\lesssim h\|g\|_{1,\Omega},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}),

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}).

Proof.

The first estimate is direct since
{\rm div}\,\undertilde{u}=g
.
To obtain the second inequality, we first analyze the interpolation error. By (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
3.11
), we have

(3.15)

\begin{split}\|\undertilde{u}-\Pi_{h}\undertilde{u}\|_{0,\Omega}&\leqslant\|({\rm I}-\Pi_{h})(\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}^{0}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\undertilde{u}{}^{0}-\Pi_{h}\undertilde{u}{}^{0}\|_{0,\Omega}\\
&\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}^{0}\|_{0,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}^{0}\|_{1,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\undertilde{u}{}^{0}\|_{1,\Omega}\big)\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}).\end{split}

At the same time,

(3.16)

\begin{split}\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}-\Pi{}_{h}\undertilde{u}|_{1,h}&\lesssim\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}|_{1,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}-\Pi{}_{h}\undertilde{u}|_{1,h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon h^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}|_{1,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}),\end{split}

where we utilize
\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}|_{1,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}|_{1,\Omega}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}\lesssim\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}
.

By the continuity of
\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e}
and
\fint_{e}\undertilde{v}{}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{t}_{e}\,ds
, a standard estimate (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Lemma 5.1]
) yields

\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{e}(\nabla\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e})\cdot\llbracket{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}}\rrbracket\,ds\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}h^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}{}|_{1,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}{}|_{2,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}|_{1,h}.
Then we derive

(3.17)

\frac{\big|\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{e}(\nabla\undertilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e})\cdot\llbracket{\undertilde{v}{}_{h}}\rrbracket\,ds\big|}{\varepsilon|\undertilde{v}{}_{h}|_{1,h}}\lesssim\varepsilon h^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}|_{1,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\undertilde{u}|_{2,\Omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}).

A combination of(
3.9
), (
3.15
), (
3.16
), and (
3.17
) leads to

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\varepsilon|\undertilde{u}-\undertilde{u}{}_{h}|_{1,h}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}).

Again from (
3.11
) and notice that
\varepsilon<1
, we have

(3.19)

\|p-\Pi_{Q_{h*}}p\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h|p|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim h|p-p^{0}|_{1,\Omega}+h|p^{0}|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim h(\|\undertilde{f}\|_{{\rm rot}}+\|g\|_{1,+}).

Hence, by (
3.10
), (
3.18
), and (
3.19
), the last estimate (
3.14
) is derived.
∎

4.
Numerical Experiments

In this section, we carry out numerical experiments to validate the theory and illustrate the capacity of the newly proposed element pair. Examples are given as illustrations from different perspectives.

•

Examples 1 and 2 test the method with the Stokes problem, especially its robustness with respect to the Reynolds’ number and to the triangulations;

•

Examples 3 and 4 test the method with the Darcy–Stokes–Brinkman equation, especially the robustness with respect to the the small parameter, for smooth solutions as well as solutions with sharp layers;

•

Examples 5 and 6 test the method with the incompressible Navier–Stokes equation, regarding evolutionary and steady states.

Three kinds of
P_{2}-P_{1}
pairs are involved in the experiments, namely,

TH:

the Taylor-Hood element pair with continuous
P_{2}
functions for the velocity space and continuous
P_{1}
functions for the pressure space;

SV:

the Scott-Vogelius element pair with continuous
P_{2}
functions for the velocity space and discontinuous
P_{1}
functions for the pressure space;

NPP:

the newly proposed
P_{2}-P_{1}
element pair.

All simulations are performed on uniformly refined grids. For the SV pair, an additional barycentric refinement is applied on each grid to guarantee the stability.

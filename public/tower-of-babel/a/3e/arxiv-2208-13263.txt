On quantitative structure of symplectic groups Thanks: Corresponding author: S.H. Alavi
Seyed Hassan Alavi ∗, Ashraf Daneshkhah, Hosein Parvizi Mosaed

Abstract
. The main aim of this article is to study quantitative structure of projective symplectic groups PSp 4 ​ ( q ) \mathrm{PSp}_{4}(q) with q > 2 q>2 even. Indeed, we prove that the groups PSp 4 ​ ( q ) \mathrm{PSp}_{4}(q) with q > 2 q>2 even are uniquely determined by their orders and the set of the number of elements of the same order. This result links to the well-known J. G. Thompson’s problem (1987) for finite simple groups.

1.
Introduction

For a finite group
G
and a positive integer
n
, let
G_{n}
consist of all elements
x
satisfying
x^{n}=1
. The
type
of
G
is defined to be the function whose value at
n
is the order of
G_{n}
. This notion links to the notion of
same-order type

\tau(G)
of
G
, that is to say,
the set of the number of elements of the same order
in
G
. Indeed, it turns out that if two groups
G
and
H
are of the same type, then
\tau(G)=\tau(H)
and
|G|=|H|
. However, finite groups with the same order and same-order type may not have the same type or even the same
spectrum
, the set of element orders.
The smallest examples of such groups that we have found are
G:=\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times(\mathbb{Z}_{7}:\mathbb{Z}_{3})
and
H:=\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times(\mathbb{Z}_{7}:\mathbb{Z}_{4})
of order
84
in which
\tau(G)=\tau(H)=\{1,2,6,12,14,28\}
, and
G
has an element of order
28
while
H
has no such an element. Moreover,
G
and
H
are not of the same type as
|G_{3}|=15
and
|H_{3}|=3
.

The main aim of this paper is to investigate the structure of groups with the same-order type as finite simple groups. In this direction, Shao et al
[
14
]
studied finite simple groups whose order is divisible by at most four primes. This problem has been studied for several families of simple groups including small Ree groups and Suzuki groups
[
1
,
2
]
. In this paper, we study groups with the same order and the same-order type as
\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(q)
with
q
even:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group such that
\tau(G)=\tau(\mathrm{S})
and
|G|=|\mathrm{S}|
, where
\mathrm{S}=\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(q)
with
q>2
even. Then
G
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{S}
.

In 1987, J. G. Thompson
[
10
, Problem 12.37]
asked
if a group with the same type as a solvable group is still solvable
. One may ask this problem for non-solvable groups, in particular, finite simple groups. It is easy to see that two groups with the same type share the same order and spectrum, and hence the recognition of finite simple groups by their orders and spectra, which is known as Shi’s problem
[
10
, Problem 12.39]
, gives a positive answer to Thompson’s problem. It is worth noting that Vasil
′
ev, Grechkoseeva and Mazurov
[
15
]
finalized a complete solution to Shi’s problem, and consequently Thompson’s problem is true for finite simple groups. As noted above, two groups
G
and
H
with the same type have the same order and same-order type, and hence Theorem
1.1
also gives a positive answer to Thompson’s problem for finite simple groups
\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(q)
with
q
even:

Corollary 1.2
.

If
G
is a finite group of the same type as
\mathrm{S}:=\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(q)
with
q>2
even, then
G
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{S}
.

Our analysis in proving Theorem
1.1
depends on determining
\tau(\mathrm{S})
, where
\mathrm{S}=\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(q)
with
q>2
even, see Proposition
3.3
. Despite of the investigation of the groups which have been previously considered, we have generally no isolated vertex in the prime graph of a group
G
with the same number of elements of the same order as
\mathrm{S}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

be a power of

. Then

(i)

;

(ii)

;

(iii)

, then

;

(iv)

If
3q^{2}+2
divides
q^{4}(q^{4}-1)(q^{2}-1)
, then

;

(v)

does not divide
q^{4}(q^{4}-1)(q^{2}-1)
.

Proof.

We know that
2q^{2}+3
is coprime to
q^{4}(q^{2}+1)
. If
2q^{2}+3
would divide
q^{4}(q^{4}-1)(q^{2}-1)
, then

would divide

. Since

, it would follow that

, and so

which is true when

but in this case
2q^{2}+3=11
does not divide

. This follows part (i). To prove part (ii), since
q^{2}+2
is coprime to
q^{2}+1
, if
q^{2}+2

, we conclude that

has to divide

. We know that
q^{4}(q^{2}-1)^{2}=(q^{6}-4q^{4}+9q^{2}-18)(q^{2}+2)+36
. Then
q^{2}+2
divides

, and this is true if and only if

, as desired. Parts (iii)-(v) can be proved in a same manner.
∎

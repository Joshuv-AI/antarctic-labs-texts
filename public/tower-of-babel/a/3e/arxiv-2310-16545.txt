1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\mathfrak{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold (possibly with totally geodesic boundary) with strictly negative sectional curvatures.
There is a bijection between the closed geodesics on
(M,\mathfrak{g})
and the conjugacy classes
{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
in the fundamental group
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
. Through this bijection we can associate a length to each conjugacy class. That is, there is a function
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\bf conj}(\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
that sends each conjugacy class
[x]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
to the length of the corresponding closed geodesic
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]
. The function
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}
is referred to as the marked length spectrum of
(M,\mathfrak{g})
. The well known marked length spectrum rigidity conjecture predicts that the isometry class of
\mathfrak{g}
is determined by its marked length spectrum. That is, if two negatively curved Riemannian metrics
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}
on
M
have the same marked length spectrum (
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}[x]=\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}[x]
for all
[x]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
) then
(M,\mathfrak{g}_{1})
and
(M,\mathfrak{g}_{2})
are isometric. This conjecture was resolved in dimension
2
by Otal
[
Ota90
]
and independently by Croke
[
Cro90
]
. There has been much interest in the conjecture and related problems with progress made in various directions
[
CD04
]
,
[
DLR08
]
,
[
BC17
]
,
[
GL19
]
,
[
GKL21
]
,
[
Hao22
]
,
[
GH23
]
,
[
CR23
]
,
[
But23
]
.

It is natural to try to identify subsets of conjugacy classes which determine the length spectrum of a negatively curved metric
\mathfrak{g}
. We say that a collection of conjugacy classes
E\subset{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
is spectrally rigid if when
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}[x]=\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}[x]
for
[x]\in E
then we must have that
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}[x]=\ell_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}[x]
for all
[x]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
. Examples of spectrally rigid sets include:

(1)

conjugacy classes with a representative in a normal subgroup
H<\pi_{1}(M)

[
Bon91
]
or more generally a subgroup that has full limit set in the universal cover
[
Hao22
]
;

(2)

conjugacy classes corresponding to a fixed homology class
[
GH23
]
; and,

(3)

subsets of conjugacy classes that have complement with sub-exponential growth
[
Saw20
]
, have positive upper density
[
DR23
]
or more generally have full exponential growth rate for all Riemannian metrics on
M

[
CR23
]
.

Note also that if we restrict to hyperbolic metrics on compact surfaces there are finite spectrally rigid sets coming from a pants decomposition, i.e. its Fenchel Nielsen coordinates. This is not the case in variable negative curvature (as we can perturb negatively curved metrics away from the union of any finite number of closed geodesics).

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a negatively curved Riemannian metric on
M
and
\alpha\subset{\bf conj}(\pi_{1}(M))
is the collection of conjugacy classes lying in a fixed homology class then
\#\{[x]\in\alpha:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}
grows exponentially as
T\to\infty
and in fact has the same exponential growth rate as
\#\{[x]\in{\bf conj}(\pi_{1}(M)):\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}

[
AS87
]
. The spectrally rigid sets listed in
(1),(2)
and
(3)
above are all large in this sense: they have strictly positive exponential growth rate, i.e. for each set
E
mentioned in
(1),(2)
and
(3)
above

(1)

\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}

grows exponentially quickly as
T\to\infty
. This may lead us to suspect that spectrally rigid sets are necessarily large and it is therefore natural to search for small (in terms of the growth rate of (
1
)) spectrally rigid sets. This problem is given more motivation by the fact that there exist large non-spectrally rigid sets. Indeed, suppose that
(V,\mathfrak{g})
is a negatively curved, closed Riemannian surface. Then the number of closed geodesics on
(V,\mathfrak{g})
of length less than
T
has exponential growth rate
h_{\mathfrak{g}}>0
where
h_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the topological entropy of the geodesic flow on
(V,\mathfrak{g})
. For any
\alpha<h_{\mathfrak{g}}
there is a non-spectrally rigid set
E
with exponential growth rate

v_{E}=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}

such that
\alpha\leq v_{E}<h_{\mathfrak{g}}
. To construct such a set, fix a ball of radius
\epsilon>0
in
(S,\mathfrak{g})
and let
E
be the set of conjugacy classes corresponding to closed geodesics that do not enter this ball. The set
E
is not spectrally rigid (as we can perturb the metric
\mathfrak{g}
in this ball to obtain a new metric that is not isometric to
\mathfrak{g}
) and as
\epsilon
tends to
0
the exponential growth rate of
E
approaches
h_{\mathfrak{g}}
. It is likely a difficult question to ascertain whether there is a non-spectrally rigid set
E
such that
\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}
has exponential growth rate
h_{\mathfrak{g}}
. We do not address this question in the current work but it would be interesting to investigate this problem further.

The author is aware of only two results for spectrally rigid sets with sub-exponential growth rates. However, these results are in different settings to that of negatively curved manifolds considered above. The first is a result of Bridgeman and Canary
[
BC17
]
that states that for Kleinien surfaces groups, the collection of simple closed geodesics form a spectrally rigid set. Note that simple closed geodesics on negatively curved surfaces do not form a spectrally rigid set as their union is not dense. The second is a rigidity result of Kapovich for the Culler-Vogtmann Outer Space of free groups
F_{N}
with rank
N\geq 2

[
Kap12
]
. Kapovich showed that typical geodesic rays (in some sense) in
F_{N}
form spectrally rigid sets for Outer Space. There are many other works that study rigidity sets for Outer Space
[
SV92
]
,
[
CFKM12
]
,
[
Ray12
]
,
[
Hor16
]
. In both the results of Bridgeman-Canary and Kapovich the spectrally rigid sets have polynomial growth (i.e. the analogues of
(\ref{eq.gr})
grow polynomially in
T
).

In this work we generalise and improve Kapovich’s result
[
Kap12
]
. In the setting of negatively curved manifolds, we show that, surprisingly, arbitrarily small spectrally rigid sets exist in all directions in
\pi_{1}(M)
.

Before we state this result we recall the following facts.
Let
(M,\mathfrak{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold with strictly negative sectional curvatures. The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)=:\Gamma
of
M
is a hyperbolic group and the metric
\mathfrak{g}
lifts to a metric
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\Gamma
. Indeed, we consider the universal cover
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{d_{\mathfrak{g}}})
of
M
, fix a base point
p\in\widetilde{M}
and define

d_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,y)=\widetilde{d_{\mathfrak{g}}}(x\cdot p,y\cdot p)\ \text{ for $x,y\in\Gamma$.}

The marked length spectrum of
\mathfrak{g}
can be obtained as

\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{\mathfrak{g}}(o,x^{n})}{n}\ \text{ for each $[x]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)$}

where
o\in\Gamma
is the identity element.
There are associated Patterson-Sullivan measures on the Gromov boundary
\partial\Gamma
of
\Gamma
and we label one of these measures
\nu_{\mathfrak{g}}
. The metric
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\Gamma
may not be geodesic but roughly geodesic (see Section
2
).
Recall that for
D\geq 0
a
D
-rough geodesic for
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a (possibly infinite) sequence of group elements
x_{n}\in\Gamma
such that

|m-n|-D\leq d_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{n},x_{m})\leq|m-n|+D\ \text{ for all $m,n\geq 1$.}

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\mathfrak{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold with strictly negative sectional curvatures. Let
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
be the metric introduced above with Patterson-Sullivan measure
\nu_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then there exists
D>0
such that for
\nu_{\mathfrak{g}}
almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
there exists a
D
-rough geodesic ray
(\xi_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
for
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
starting at the identity in
\Gamma
(
\xi_{0}=o\in\Gamma
) and with end point
\xi
such that for any
M\geq 1
the set
\{[\xi_{k}]:k\geq M\}
is spectrally rigid.

Furthermore, for any function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
with
f(T)\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
there exists a subsequence
n_{k}
such that

E=\{[\xi_{n_{k}}]:k\geq 1\}

is spectrally rigid and

\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}\leq f(T)\ \text{ for all }\ T>0.

Remark 1.2
.

i) In fact the sequence
\xi_{k}
constructed in the above theorem will satisfy
|\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[\xi_{k}]-d_{\mathfrak{g}}(o,\xi_{k})|\leq R\ \text{for all}\ k\geq 1
and so
\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[\xi_{k}]
is diverging linearly as
k
increases.

ii) We note that in the case that
(M,\mathfrak{g})
is a compact,
n
dimensional hyperbolic manifold, then the Gromov boundary of
\Gamma
is homeomorphic to the
n-1
dimensional sphere and the Patterson-Sullivan measure
\nu_{\mathfrak{g}}
is in the same class as the Lebesgue measure.

This result shows that there are arbitrarily sparse spectrally rigid sets that exist in
\nu_{\mathfrak{g}}
almost ever every direction in
\Gamma
.
We immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(M,\mathfrak{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold with strictly negative sectional curvatures. Then for any function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
with
f(T)\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
there exists a set
E\subset{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
(where
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
) such that

\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}\leq f(T)\ \text{ for all }\ T>0

and
E
is spectrally rigid.

This shows that although there are no finite spectrally rigid sets, there are arbitrarily sparse (with respect to
\mathfrak{g}
), infinite, spectrally rigid sets.

In the final section we prove an analogue of Theorem
1.1
in which
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
is replaced by a word metric.
This provides a refinement (see Theorem
4.4
) of Kapovich’s aforementioned result
[
Kap12
]
. We also prove a rigidity result for Hitchin representations, Theorem
4.6
. In fact our methods are sufficiently flexible that they apply to a large class of metrics on general hyperbolic groups, see Theorem
4.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperbolic groups and metrics

We will assume that the reader is familiar with hyperbolic groups and metrics and only briefly introduce them here. See
[
GdlH90
]
and
[
CT21
]
for more detailed accounts.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. The Gromov product associated to
d
is defined by

(x,y)_{w,d}=\frac{1}{2}(d(w,x)+d(w,y)-d(x,y))\ \ \text{for $x,y,w\in X$}

and we say that
(X,d)
is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
if

(x,y)_{w,d}\geq\min\left\{(x,z)_{w,d},(y,z)_{w,d}\right\}-\delta\quad\text{for all $x,y,z,w\in X$}.

A metric space is called
hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. For a finite generating set
S
the corresponding word metric
d_{S}
is defined as follows. For
x,y\in\Gamma

d_{S}(x,y)=\min\{k\geq 0:x^{-1}y=s_{1}\cdots s_{k},\text{ for some }s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}\in S\cup S^{-1}\}.

Throughout we will use
o\in\Gamma
to denote the identity element in
\Gamma
. We will also write
|x|_{S}
for
d_{S}(o,x)
.
A hyperbolic group is a finitely generated group
\Gamma
that, when equipped with a word metric
d_{S}
associated to a finite generating set
S
, becomes a hyperbolic metric space.
In this work all hyperbolic groups will assumed to be non-elementary: we assume that they do not contain a cyclic subgroup with finite index.
We will use the notation
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
to denote the Cayley graph of
\Gamma
with respect to
S
.

We say that two metrics
d,d_{\ast}
on
\Gamma
are
quasi-isometric
if there exist constants
L>1
and
C\geq 0
such that

L^{-1}\,d(x,y)-C\leq d_{\ast}(x,y)\leq L\,d(x,y)+C\ \text{ for all $x,y\in\Gamma$}.

Throughout this work,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
will denote the set of metrics on
\Gamma
which are left-invariant, hyperbolic and quasi-isometric to some (equivalently, any) word metric in
\Gamma
. This class contains many interesting examples of metrics, see
[
CT21
]
. Pairs of metrics
d,d_{\ast}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
have coarsely comparable Gromov products:
there exist
\lambda>1,C>0
such that

\lambda^{-1}(x,y)_{o,d}-C\leq(x,y)_{o,d_{\ast}}\leq\lambda(x,y)_{o,d}+C

for all
x,y\in\Gamma

[
GdlH90
, Prop. 15 (i), Ch. 5]
. For
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
we write
\ell_{d}
for the translation distance function for
d
, i.e.
\ell_{d}[x]=\lim_{n\to\infty}d(o,x^{n})/n
where
[x]
is the conjugacy class containing
x
. To simplify notation, if
S
is a finite generating set for
\Gamma
we will write
\ell_{S}
for the translation length function for
d_{S}
. We will write

v_{d}=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\#\{x\in\Gamma:d(o,x)<T\}

for the exponential growth rate of
d
.
For each
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
there exists a constant
C>0
such that if
x\in\Gamma
satisfies
d(o,x)-2(x,x^{-1})_{o,d}\geq C
then

|\ell_{d}[x]-(d(o,x)-2(x,x^{-1})_{o,d}|\leq C,

see
[
CT21
, Lemma 3.2]
.
If two metrics
d,d_{\ast}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
have the property that there exists
\tau,C>0
such that
|\tau d(o,x)-d_{\ast}(o,x)|<C
for all
x\in\Gamma
(i.e. after scaling they agree up to a uniformly bounded error) then we say that
d,d_{\ast}
are
roughly similar
.

Fix a metric
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
Given an interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and constants
L,D>0
we say that a map
\gamma:I\to\Gamma
is an
(L,D)
-quasi-geodesic
if

L^{-1}\,|s-t|-D\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq L\,|s-t|+D\ \text{ for all $s,t\in I$},

and a
D
-rough geodesic
if

|s-t|-D\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq|s-t|+D\ \text{ for all $s,t\in I$}.

Geodesics are
0
-rough geodesics.
A metric space
(\Gamma,d)
is called
D
-
roughly geodesic
if for each pair
x,y\in\Gamma
we can find a
D
-rough geodesic joining
x
to
y
. We say that
(\Gamma,d)
is
roughly geodesic
if it is
D
-roughly geodesic for some
D\geq 0
.
A geodesic metric space is a
0
-roughly geodesic metric space. Every metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is roughly geodesic
[
BS00
]
.
Moreover the Morse Lemma holds for such metrics: if
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is
D_{0}
-roughly geodesic then for every
(L,D)
-quasi-geodesic
\gamma
in
(\Gamma,d)
there is a
D_{0}
-rough geodesic
\gamma_{0}
such that
\gamma
and
\gamma_{0}
are within Hausdorff distance
K
(depending on
L,D,D_{0},\delta
where
\delta
is a hyperbolicity constant for
d
).

The main case that we consider in this work is the following example.

Example 2.1
.

Let
(M,\mathfrak{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold (possibly with totally geodesic boundary) with strictly negative sectional curvatures.
The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)=:\Gamma
of
M
is a hyperbolic group. Further,
\Gamma
acts on the universal cover
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{d}_{\mathfrak{g}})
of
(M,\mathfrak{g})
and through this action the metric
\mathfrak{g}
lifts to a metric
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
:

\text{ for $x,y\in\Gamma$ }d_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,y)=\widetilde{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x\cdot p,y\cdot p)\ \text{ where $p\in\widetilde{M}$ is a fixed basepoint.}

This metric is quasi-isometric to a (equivalently every) word metric
d_{S}
on
\Gamma
by the Švarc-Milnor Lemma. Further it is left invariant and hyperbolic and so belongs to
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.

Hyperbolic groups can be compactified using their ideal boundary
\partial\Gamma
which consists of equivalence classes
of divergent sequences. Fix a reference metric
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. A sequence of group elements
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
diverges
if
(x_{n},x_{m})_{o,d}
diverges as
\min\{n,m\}
tends to infinity. Two divergent sequences
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are
equivalent
if

(x_{n},y_{m})_{o,d}
diverges as
\min\{n,m\}
tends to infinity.
If
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is
D
-roughly geodesic then for each
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
there exists a
D
-rough geodesic beginning at
o
and ending at
\xi
.

The Gromov product extends to points in
\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma
for any
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
. Fix
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and
let

(\xi,\eta)_{o,d}=\sup\left\{\liminf_{n\to\infty}(x_{n},y_{n})_{o,d}\ :\ \xi=\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty},\ \eta=\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\right\},

for
\xi,\eta\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma
,
where if
\xi
or
\eta
is in
\Gamma
, then we take
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
as the constant sequence
x_{n}=\xi
for all
n\geq 0
.
The Busemann function
\beta_{w}(x,\xi)
associated to
d
based at
w
is given by

\beta_{w}(x,\xi)=\sup\left\{\limsup_{n\to\infty}d(x,\xi_{n})-d(w,\xi_{n}):\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\xi\right\}.

Following the Patterson-Sullivan construction one can obtain the following result (see for example Proposition 2.7 in
[
CT21
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

For each
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
there is a measure
\mu_{d}
on
\partial\Gamma
which is ergodic with respect to the action of
\Gamma
on
\partial\Gamma
. Furthermore there exists a constant
C>1
such that for any
x\in\Gamma

C^{-1}e^{-v_{d}\beta_{o}(x,\xi)}\leq\frac{dx_{\ast}\mu_{d}}{d\mu_{d}}\leq Ce^{-v_{d}\beta_{o}(x,\xi)}

where
\beta_{o}
is the Busemann functions for
d
.

Here ergodic means that for any
U,V\subset\Gamma
with
\mu_{d}(U),\mu_{d}(V)>0
there exists
x\in\Gamma
such that
\mu(x(U)\cap V)>0
.

2.2.
Cannon coding and thermodynamic formalism

Fix a finite generating set
S
for
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{G}},w,S)
be a triple where

(1)

S
is a finite generating set for
\Gamma
;

(2)

{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E,\ast)
is a finite directed graph with a vertex
\ast
called the
initial state
; and,

(3)

w:E\to S
is a labelling such that
for a directed edge path
(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
(where
(x_{i},x_{i+1})
corresponds to a directed edge)
there is an associated path in
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
beginning at the identity. This path corresponds to

(o,w(x_{0},x_{1}),w(x_{0},x_{1})w(x_{1},x_{2}),\dots,w(x_{0},x_{1})\cdots w(x_{n-1},x_{n})).

Let
{\bf ev}
denote the map that sends a finite path to the group element obtained by multiplying the labelings along the path, i.e.

{\bf ev}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=w(x_{0},x_{1})\cdots\,w(x_{n-1},x_{n})
.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is a strongly Markov structure for
\Gamma
and
S
if

(1)

for each vertex
v\in V
there exists a directed path from
\ast
to
v
;

(2)

for each directed path in
{\mathcal{G}}
the associated path in
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
is a geodesic; and,

(3)

the map
{\bf ev}
defines a bijection between the set of directed paths from
\ast
in
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\Gamma
.

Cannon proved that some cocompact Kleinian groups and generating sets admit strongly Markov structures
[
Can84
]
. Ghys and de la Harpe extended Cannons result to every hyperbolic group and finite generating set
[
GdlH90
]
. See also
[
Cal13
, Section 3.2]
.

For technical reasons we augment the above strongly Markov structure by introducing an additional vertex labelled
0
. We also add directed edges from every vertex
x\in V\cup\{0\}\backslash\{\ast\}
to
0
and define
\omega(x,0)=o
(the identity in
\Gamma
) for every
x\in V\cup\{0\}\backslash\{\ast\}
. We will assume that every strongly Markov structure has been augmented in this way and will abuse notation by labelling the augmented structure, its edge and vertex set by
\mathcal{G}
,
V
and
E
respectively. This graph
\mathcal{G}
allows us to introduce a subshift of finite type.

We will now recall some of the preliminary results we need from symbolic dynamics. See
[
PP90
]
for a more thorough introduction. Fix a finite directed graph
\mathcal{G}
and let
A
be the
k\times k
(where
k
is the number of vertices in
{\mathcal{G}}
),
0-1
transition matrix describing
{\mathcal{G}}
.
We use the notation
A_{i,j}
to denote the
(i,j)
th entry of
A
. The one-sided subshift of finite type associated to
A
is the space

\Sigma_{A}=\{(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}:x_{n}\in\{1,2,...,k\},A_{x_{n},x_{n+1}}=1,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

When
A
is clear, we will drop
A
from the notation in the definition of the shift spaces and will simply write
\Sigma
. Given
x\in\Sigma_{A}
we write
x_{n}
for the
n
th coordinate of
x
.
The shift map
\sigma:\Sigma_{A}\rightarrow\Sigma_{A}
sends
x
to
y=\sigma(x)
where
y_{n}=x_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Consider a finite ordered string
x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
where
A_{x_{j},x_{j+1}}=1
for each
j=0,\ldots,m-2
. The cylinder set associated to this string is the subset of
\Sigma_{A}
given by

[x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1}]=\left\{(y_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\Sigma_{A}:y_{j}=x_{j}\text{ for }j=0,\ldots,m-1\right\}.

We endow
\Sigma_{A}
with a topology by declaring the set of all cylinder sets to be an open basis.
For each
0<\theta<1
we can define a metric
d_{\theta}
on
\Sigma_{A}
which is compatible with this topology. Let
x,y\in\Sigma_{A}
. If
x_{0}=y_{0}
then we define
d_{\theta}(x,y)=\theta^{N},
where

Now, fix a conjugacy class
[x]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
. By Proposition
2.10
there exists an integer
M\geq 1
such that there is a loop in
B_{i}
that represents
[x^{\pm M}]
. Suppose that this loop is described by the path
r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1}
so that
{\bf ev}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1})\in[x^{\pm M}]
. Since
z
visits every cylinder infinitely often in
B_{i}
(and in particular visits
[r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1}]
infinitely often) we deduce that
z
belongs to a cylinder of the form

[\ast=z_{0},\ldots,z_{k},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1}]

for some path
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
in
B_{i}
.

Since
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is within Hausdorff distance
K>0
of
\{{\bf ev}_{n}(z)\}_{n\geq 1}
we deduce that there are
N,N^{\prime}>0
such that

d_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},g_{N})\leq K\ \text{ and }\ d_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{2},g_{N^{\prime}})\leq K

where
h_{1}={\bf ev}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},r_{1})
and
h_{2}={\bf ev}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1})
.
Now, we lift the metrics
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
to
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
to obtain metrics
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
as in Example
2.1
.
By the Gromov product condition in (
5
) and the fact that
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
have Gromov products comparable with that for
d_{S}
, we also have that for
j=1,2

d_{j}(o,h_{1})=\ell_{j}[g_{N}]+O(1)\ \text{ and }\ d_{j}(o,h_{2})=\ell_{j}[g_{N^{\prime}}]+O(1)

where the implied constants are independent of
[x]
. Also, since
r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1}
corresponds to a loop in the Cannon coding, we have that

({\bf ev}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1}),{\bf ev}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1})^{-1})_{o,d_{S}}=0

and so
d_{j}(o,{\bf ev}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1}))=\ell_{j}[x^{M}]+O(1)
where the error is independent of
[x]
.
Further by the Morse Lemma,

d_{j}(o,h_{2})=d_{j}(o,h_{1})+d_{j}(o,{\bf ev}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{l},r_{1}))+O(1)\ \text{ for $j=1,2$}

where the implied constant is independent of
[x]
. Combining these expressions give that

\ell_{j}[g_{N}]=\ell_{j}[g_{N^{\prime}}]+\ell_{j}[x^{M}]+O(1)\ \text{ for $j=1,2$}

where the implied constant is independent of
[x]
. We also have by assumption that
\ell_{1}[g_{N}]=\ell_{2}[g_{N}]
and
\ell_{1}[g_{N^{\prime}}]=\ell_{2}[g_{N^{\prime}}]
and so we deduce that

\ell_{1}[x^{M}]=\ell_{1}[g_{N}]-\ell_{1}[g_{N^{\prime}}]+O(1)=\ell_{2}[g_{N}]-\ell_{2}[g_{N^{\prime}}]+O(1)=\ell_{2}[x^{M}]+O(1)

where the implied error does not depend on
[x]
. This concludes the proof of the first part of the theorem.

For the furthermore part we note that to deduce that
\ell_{1}[x^{M}]=\ell_{2}[x^{M}]+O(1)
we need only know that
\ell_{1}[g_{N}]=\ell_{2}[g_{N}]
and
\ell_{1}[g_{N^{\prime}}]=\ell_{2}[g_{N^{\prime}}]
. That is for any
[x]\in{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
we can find a pair of elements
g_{N}([x]),g_{N^{\prime}}([x])
such that if
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
agree on these elements then
\ell_{1}[x^{M}]=\ell_{2}[x^{M}]+O(1)
where the error is independent of
[x]
. Since
z
visits every cylinder in
B_{i}
infinitely often we can take
N,N^{\prime}
to be as large as we like. This observation allows us to inductively construct a spectrally rigid set with growth rate slower than any prescribed function. Fix a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
with
f(T)\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
. We begin by listing the conjugacy class in
\Gamma
,
[x_{1}],[x_{2}],\ldots
and for
[x_{1}]
we find a pair
g_{N}[x_{1}],g_{N^{\prime}}[x_{1}]
of group elements with word lengths
N[x_{1}],N^{\prime}[x_{1}]
. We can take
N[x_{1}],N^{\prime}[x_{1}]
sufficiently large so that when we consider the set
E_{1}
containing this pair of elements we have that
\#\{[x]\in E_{1}:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}\leq f(T)
for all
T>0
. We then do the same for
[x_{2}]
but choose
g_{N}[x_{2}],g_{N^{\prime}}[x_{2}]
so that their word lengths
N[x_{2}],N^{\prime}[x_{2}]
are sufficiently large so that the set
E_{2}=E_{1}\cup\{g_{N}[x_{2}],g_{N^{\prime}}[x_{2}]\}
has the property that
\#\{[x]\in E_{2}:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}\leq f(T)
for all
T>0
. Continuing inductively we obtain a set
E
such that
\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{\mathfrak{g}}[x]<T\}\leq f(T)
for all
T>0
and
E
is spectrally rigid.
∎

4.
Word metrics

In this section we show that Theorem
1.1
holds when we swap
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
for a word metric.
In fact we can prove the following more general result. Given a hyperbolic group
\Gamma
, we say that a subset of conjugacy classes
E\subset{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
is spectrally rigid for
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
if when
d,d_{\ast}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
have the same marked length spectrum on
E
then they are roughly similar (or equivalently have the same length marked length spectrum up to scaling
[
CT21
, Theorem 1.2]
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group. Equip
\Gamma
with a finite generating set
S
and write
\nu_{S}
for the corresponding Patterson-Sullivan measure (for the word metric
d_{S}
) on the Gromov boundary
\partial\Gamma
of
\Gamma
. Then there exists
D>0
such that for
\nu_{S}
almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
there exists a
D
-rough geodesic ray
(\xi_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
for
d_{S}
starting at the identity in
\Gamma
with end point
\xi
such that
for any
M\geq 1
the set
\{[\xi_{k}]:k\geq M\}
is spectrally rigid for
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.

Furthermore, for any function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
with
f(T)\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
there exists a subsequence
n_{k}
such that

E=\{[\xi_{n_{k}}]:k\geq 1\}

is spectrally rigid for
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and

\#\{\ell_{S}[x]<T:[x]\in E\}\leq f(T)\ \text{ for all }\ T>0.

Remark 4.2
.

This result also holds if we replace the word metric
d_{S}
with a Green metric associated to an admissible, finitely supported, symmetric random walk. In this case the Patterson-Sullivan measure becomes the hitting measure for the random walk on the boundary of the group. The same proof as that presented in the previous section can be applied since the construction in Section
2.3
can be carried out as discussed in Remark
2.15
.

The proof follows exactly the same line used to prove Theorem
1.1
. The only difference being that we need to show that there is a sufficiently nice measure
\widehat{\nu}_{S}
on
\Sigma_{[\ast]}
that pushes forward under
{\bf ev}_{\ast}
to a Patterson-Sullivan measure (as in Proposition
2.2
)
\nu_{S}
for
d_{S}
on
\partial\Gamma
, i.e. so that
\nu_{S}(U)=\widehat{\nu}_{S}({\bf ev}_{\ast}^{-1}(U))
for
U\subset\partial\Gamma
. The measure
\widehat{\nu}
can be constructed as in Section
4
of
[
CF10
]
(see also
[
Can21
]
). We will not provide the construction here but will instead present the properties of
\widehat{\nu}_{S}
that we require for our proofs. Fix a Cannon coding
\Sigma
for
\Gamma
and a finite generating set
S
.

Proposition 4.3
.

There exists a measure
\mu
on
\Sigma
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sigma_{\ast}^{k}\widehat{\nu}_{S}=\mu.

Further there exist
0<\alpha_{i}<1
for
i=1,...,m
with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}=1
such that

\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\mu_{i},

where each
\mu_{i}
is the measure of maximal entropy for the system
(\Sigma_{B_{i}},\sigma)
.

An important consequence of this is that if
U\subset\Sigma
has
\mu_{i}(U\cap\Sigma_{B_{i}})=1
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
then the set

\Sigma_{[\ast]}\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma^{-j}(U)

has full
\widehat{\nu}_{S}
measure
[
Can21
, Proposition 4.6]
. This is an analogue of Corollary
2.14
for
\widehat{\nu}_{S}
.

Now we can deduce Theorem
4.1
by following the proof of Theorem
1.1
but using the measure
\widehat{\nu}_{S}
. As the argument is almost identical, we leave the details to the reader.

As an immediate corollary we deduce a refinement of Kapovich’s result
[
Kap12
]
for Outer Space. We refer the reader to
[
CV86
]
for an introduction to Outer Space. Let
F_{N}
be the free group on
N\geq 2
generators. We let
\text{cv}_{N}
denote Culler-Vogtmann Outer Space and we recall that points in Outer Space are determined by their length spectrum, i.e. if
T_{1},T_{2}\in\text{cv}_{N}
have the same length spectra then
T_{1}=T_{2}
. We say that a subset of conjugacy classes
E\subset{\bf conj}(F_{N})
is spectrally rigid for Outer Space if when
T_{1},T_{2}\in\text{cv}_{N}
have the same length spectra on
E
then
T_{1}=T_{2}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
F_{N}
be the free group on
N\geq 2
generators. Equip
F_{N}
with a finite generating set
S
and write
\nu_{S}
for the corresponding Patterson-Sullivan measure (for the word metric
d_{S}
) on the Gromov boundary
\partial F_{N}
of
F_{N}
. Then there exist
D>0
such that for
\nu_{S}
almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
there exists a
D
-rough geodesic ray
(\xi_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
for
d_{S}
starting at the identity in
F_{N}
with end point
\xi
such that
for any
M\geq 1
the set
\{[\xi_{k}]:k\geq M\}
is spectrally rigid for Outer Space.

Furthermore, for any function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
with
f(T)\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
there exists a subsequence
n_{k}
such that

E=\{[\xi_{n_{k}}]:k\geq 1\}

is spectrally rigid for Outer Space and

\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{S}[x]<T\}\leq f(T)\ \text{ for all }\ T>0.

Remark 4.5
.

This theorem in fact generalises to small actions of free groups on
\mathbb{R}
-trees. That is, for any fixed word metric
d_{S}
on
F_{N}
there are arbitrarily small length spectrum rigidity sets for small actions of
F_{N}
on
\mathbb{R}
-trees (that exist in
\nu_{S}
almost every direction in
F_{N}
). This provides an interesting example of sparse length spectrum rigidity sets for non-proper actions. The proof follows identically the same argument used to prove Theorem
1.1
along with two additional facts that are discussed in
[
CR22
, Section 6.2]
.
These facts are as follows, if
d
is the orbit metric corresponding to a small action of
F_{N}
on an
\mathbb{R}
-tree then

(1)

there exists
\lambda>1
such that
(x,y)_{o,d}\leq\lambda(x,y)_{o,d_{S}}+\lambda
for all
x,y\in F_{N}
; and,

(2)

d
satisfies the bounded backtracking property.

In the proof, Property
(1)
guarantees that the values of
\ell_{d}
and
d
along a rough geodesic for
d_{S}
are close and Property
(2)
is used in place of the Morse Lemma. We leave the details to the reader.

We can also apply our methods to Hitchin representations. For an introduction to Hitchin representations we refer the reader to
[
Can23
]
. Suppose that
\Gamma
is a surface group (the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic surface) and that
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm PSL}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})
is a Hitchin representations. For
A\in{\rm PSL}_{k}({\mathbb{R}})
let
\lambda_{1}(A)
denote the spectral radius of
A
. In this setting the map
[x]\mapsto\log\lambda_{1}(\rho(x))
from
{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
to
{\mathbb{R}}
plays the role of the marked length spectrum for the
\rho
. We say that a subset
E\subset{\bf conj}(\Gamma)
is a spectrally rigid for Hitchin representations if when two Hitchin representations
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
have the same length spectra on
E
then they have the same length spectra everywhere (or equivalently either
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
or
\rho_{1}
is the contragredient of
\rho_{2}

[
BCLS15
]
). We then have the following.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a surface group. Equip
\Gamma
with a finite generating set
S
and write
\nu_{S}
for the corresponding Patterson-Sullivan measure (for the word metric
d_{S}
) on the Gromov boundary
\partial\Gamma
of
\Gamma
. Then there exists
D>0
such that for
\nu_{S}
almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
there exists a
D
-rough geodesic ray
(\xi_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
for
d_{S}
starting at the identity in
\Gamma
with end point
\xi

such that
for any
M\geq 1
the set
\{[\xi_{k}]:k\geq M\}
is spectrally rigid for Hitchin representations.

Furthermore, for any function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
with
f(T)\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
there exists a subsequence
n_{k}
such that

E=\{[\xi_{n_{k}}]:k\geq 1\}

is spectrally rigid for Hitchin representations and

\#\{[x]\in E:\ell_{S}[x]<T\}\leq f(T)\ \text{ for all }\ T>0.

The proof again follows exactly the same argument used to prove Theorem
1.1
and so we omit it.

A law of large numbers concerning the distribution of critical points of isotropic Gaussian functions
Liviu I. Nicolaescu

Abstract
. We investigate the distribution of critical points of certain isotropic random functions Φ \Phi on ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} . We show that the distribution of critical points of Φ ⁡ ( R ​ x ) \Phi(Rx) , suitably normalized, converges a.s. and L 2 L^{2} to the Lebesgue measure as R → ∞ R\to\infty . We achieve this by producing precise asymptotics of the second moments of these distributions as R → ∞ R\to\infty .



1.
Introduction

Denote by
\Meas({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
the space of finite Borel measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{a}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}

\mathfrak{a}(0)=1

\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}\in\Meas({\mathbb{R}}^{m})

\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}\big[\,d\xi\,\big]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}w_{\mathfrak{a},m}\big(\,\xi\,\big){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,d\xi\,\big],\;\;w_{\mathfrak{a},m}\big(\,\xi\,\big)=\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|\xi|\,\big)^{2},

{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

The characteristic function of

(1.1)

Clearly
{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}})
is an
O(n)
-invariant, real valued Schwartz function. Then
{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}\big(\,{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\big)
is the covariance kernel of a real valued, smooth isotropic Gaussian function
\Phi=\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with spectral measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}
.

For
R>0
we set

\mathfrak{a}_{R}(t):=\mathfrak{a}(t/R\,\big),\;\;\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Consider the finite Borel measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\in\Meas({\mathbb{R}}^{m})

\mu_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\big[\,d\xi\,\big]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}w_{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{R},m}\big(\,\xi\,\big){\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,d\xi\,\big]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|\xi|/R\,\big)^{2}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[\,d\xi\,\big].

Its characteristic function is the nonnegative definite function

\begin{split}{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\big(\,{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\big)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}e^{\boldsymbol{i}\langle\xi,{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle}\mathfrak{a}\big(\,|\xi|/R\,\big)^{2}d\xi=R^{m}{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}\big(\,R{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\big).\end{split}

(1.2)

We deduce that
{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\big(\,{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\big)
is the covariance kernel of the Gaussian function

\Phi^{R}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}}):=R^{m/2}\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}\big(\,R{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\big).

The Fourier inversion formula shows that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}({\boldsymbol{x}})d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\mathfrak{a}(0)^{2}=1.

Since

is
O(m)
-invariant and smooth, it has the form

17

To use a terminology favored by physicists,

{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}\big(\,{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}\,\big)\to\delta({\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{y}}).

In other words, as

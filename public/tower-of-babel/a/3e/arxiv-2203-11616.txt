Deterministic KPZ-type equations with nonlocal “gradient terms”
Boumediene Abdellaoui, Antonio J. Fernández, Tommaso Leonori and Abdelbadie Younes

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to prove existence and non-existence results for deterministic Kardar–Parisi–Zhang type equations involving non-local “gradient terms”. More precisely, let Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} , N ⩾ 2 N\geqslant 2 , be a bounded domain with boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega of class C 2 C^{2} . For s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , we consider problems of the form { ( − Δ ) s ​ u = μ ( x ) | 𝔻 ( u ) | q + λ f ( x ) , in ​ Ω , u = 0 , in ​ ℝ N ∖ Ω , \left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}u&=\mu(x)\,|\mathbb{D}(u)|^{q}+\lambda f(x),\quad&&\textup{ in }\Omega,\\ u&=0,&&\textup{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right. where q > 1 q>1 and λ > 0 \lambda>0 are real parameters, f f belongs to a suitable Lebesgue space, μ ∈ L ∞ ​ ( Ω ) \mu\in L^{\infty}(\Omega) and 𝔻 \mathbb{D} represents a nonlocal “gradient term”. Depending on the size of λ > 0 \lambda>0 , we derive existence and non-existence results. In particular, we solve several open problems posed in [ 4 , Section 6] and [ 2 , Section 7] . Keywords: Fractional Laplacian, nonlocal “gradient terms”, deterministic KPZ–type equations. 2020 MSC: 35R11, 35J60, 26A33.

Introduction

In this paper we analyse the existence and non–existence of solutions for deterministic Kardar–Parisi–Zhang type equations involving non-local “gradient terms”. For
s,t\in(0,1)
, we consider problems of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}u&=\mu(x)\,|\mathbb{D}_{t}(u)|^{q}+\lambda f(x),\quad&&\textup{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0,&&\textup{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

depending on a real parameter
\lambda>0
. Here,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geqslant 2
, is a bounded domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
of class
C^{2}
,
f
belongs to a suitable Lebesgue space,
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
q\in(1,+\infty)
and
\mathbb{D}_{t}
represents one of the following nonlocal “gradient terms” :

(KPZ
1

\displaystyle\circ\quad(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u(x):=a_{N,\frac{t}{2}}\,{\rm{P.V.}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+t}}dy

\displaystyle(\textup{Half $t$--Laplacian}),

(KPZ
2
)

\displaystyle\circ\quad\nabla^{t}u(x):=\mu_{N,t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+t+1}}dy

\displaystyle(\textup{Riesz $t$--Gradient}),

(KPZ
3
)

\displaystyle\circ\quad\mathcal{D}_{t}u(x):=\left(\frac{a_{N,t}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2t}}dy.\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle(\textup{Stein $t$--Functional}).

Note that the previous definitions make sense for any function

. Also, let us point out that

a_{N,\sigma}:=-\,\frac{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}+\sigma\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(-\sigma)}\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad\mu_{N,\sigma}:=\frac{2^{\sigma}\Gamma(\frac{N+\sigma+1}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{1-\sigma}{2})},

are normalization constants and “P.V.” stands for “in the principal value sense”. Since both these constants and the “principal value sense” will not play an important role in our work, we will omit them from now on.

Function spaces and tools

We collect here the definition of the main function spaces involved in our results and some other tools. First of all, recall that, for all
s\in(0,1)
and
1\leqslant p<+\infty
, the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy<+\infty\right\}.

It is a Banach space endowed with the usual norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Also, having at hand
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we define the space
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u\equiv 0\textup{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\},

and recall that, thanks to the Sobolev inequality, it is a Banach space endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}:=\left(\iint_{D_{\Omega}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{1/p},\quad\textup{ where }\quad D_{\Omega}:=(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\cup((\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega).

Next, we remind that, for any
s\in(0,1)
and
1\leqslant p<+\infty

L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :=\ \overline{\big\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big\}}^{\ \boldsymbol{|}\cdot\boldsymbol{|}_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}},

where

\boldsymbol{|}u\boldsymbol{|}_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad\textup{ and }\quad(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{F}u),\quad\forall\ u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Let us stress that, in the case where

Proposition 2.4

Let
0<t<\min\{1,s(1+N^{-1})\}

\mathbb{G}_{s}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to L_{0}^{t,p}(\Omega),\qquad h\mapsto\mathbb{G}_{s}[h]:=\int_{\Omega}G_{s}(x,y)h(y)\,dy,

Proof.

First of all, note that without loss of generality we can assume that
s\leqslant t<\min\{1,s(1+N^{-1})\}
. Indeed, once we have the result in this case, we can infer the result for
t\in(0,s)
interpolating as in the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 3.10]
. Let us also stress that, by Proposition

, the solution map
\mathbb{G}_{s}
is well-defined and continuous for all
1<p<N/(N(1+t-s)-s)

\mathbb{G}_{s}
is compact for the same range of

.

Let
(h_{n})_{n}\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
be a sequence such that
\|h_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leqslant 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
u_{n}=\mathbb{G}_{s}(h_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
By
[
8
, Proposition 2.6]
we know that, up to a subsequence,

is strongly convergent in

for all

. Moreover, combining this strong convergence with Vitali’s convergence theorem, we deduce that, up to a subsequence,
(u_{n}/\delta^{s})_{n}
is strongly convergent in

for all
1\leqslant r<N/(N-s)
. Having at hand the (up to a subsequence) strong convergence of
(u_{n})_{n}
in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for all
1\leqslant q<N/(N-2s)
, to end the proof, we just have to show that, up to a subsequence,

is strongly convergent in

for all
1<p<N/(N(1+t-s)-s)
.

First, for
0<\alpha<2
, let us consider the integral operator

T_{\alpha}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{\gamma}(\Omega),\qquad g\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{g(y)\mathds{1}_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dy.

Note that, by
[
12
, Lemma 7.12]
,

is well-defined and continuous for all
1\leqslant\gamma<N/(N-\alpha)
. Moreover, following step by step the proof of
[
24

, one can prove that

is compact for all
1\leqslant\gamma<N/(N-\alpha)
. We only sketch the proof. Let
(g_{n})_{n}\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
be a sequence such that
\|g_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leqslant 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
v_{n}:=T_{\alpha}(g_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leqslant\gamma<N/(N-\alpha)
. If we prove the existence of a (not relabelled) subsequence
(v_{n})_{n}
that is Cauchy in

, the compactness immediately follows. Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}
be a standard mollifier and let
v_{n}^{\varepsilon}:=v_{n}\star\eta_{\varepsilon}
for all

and all

. Following

, we obtain that, for all

, there exist constants

and
\sigma>0
(independent of
\varepsilon
and

) such that

\|v_{n}^{\varepsilon}-v_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\varepsilon^{\sigma}.

On the other hand, using the standard properties of the mollifiers
\eta_{\varepsilon}
, we get that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the sequence
(v_{n}^{\varepsilon})_{n}
is bounded and equicontinuous in

. Thus, Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem implies the existence of a subsequence uniformly convergent in
\overline{\Omega}
. Combining (
2.10
) with the uniform convergence in

, a standard diagonal argument shows the existence of a (not relabelled) Cauchy subsequence

in

Having at hand the compactness of

and Lemma
2.2
we now prove that, up to a subsequence,

is strongly convergent in

for all

. To that end, we fix

\Omega

and
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)

Combining (
2.4

1.3
), the compactness of
T_{\alpha}
and Hölder inequality, we get that, up to a subsequence,
((-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u_{n})_{n}
is strongly convergent in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
1\leqslant p<N/\big(N(1+t-s)-s\big)
.

Next, we deal with the strong convergence of

((-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u_{n})_{n}
in
L^{p}(B_{R}(0)\setminus\Omega)
for all
1\leqslant p<N/(N(1+t-s)-s)
. Since
|x-y|\geqslant\max\{\delta(y),\delta(x)\}
for all

and

, by (

) we have that, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u_{n}(x)\big|\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+t}}dy\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta^{t-s+\varepsilon}(x)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(y)|}{\delta^{s}(y)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N-\varepsilon}}\quad\textup{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)\,.

Combining this inequality with Lemma
2.3
, Hölder inequality and the (up to a subsequence) strong convergence of
(u_{n}/\delta^{s})_{n}
in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for all
1\leqslant r<N/(N-s)
, we get the desired convergence in
L^{p}(B_{R}(0)\setminus\Omega)
.

Finally, the (up to a subsequence) strong convergence of

((-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u_{n})_{n}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0))
for all

follows from (
2.9
). Indeed, combining (
2.9
) with the (up to a subsequence) strong convergence of
(u_{n})_{n}
in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for all

, we get the desired convergence in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0))
.

in
L_{0}^{\gamma,1+\eta}(\Omega)
and let
u_{n}=T_{2}(\varphi_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
u=T_{2}(\varphi)
. Note that

(3.23)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}(u_{n}-u)&=\mu(x)\Big((\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n})^{q}-(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi)^{q}\Big),\quad&&\textup{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{n}-u&=0,&&\textup{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

If we show that the
L^{1}
–norm of the right hand side in (
3.23
) goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
, the continuity of
T_{2}
immediately follows from Proposition
2.4
.
We actually prove something more general from which the existence part of the result in the case
m>N/s
immediately follows.

Claim:

For all
1<\alpha<r
, it follows that

(3.24)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big|(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{\alpha}-(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{\alpha}\big|dx=0.

Proof of the claim.
First of all, let
2\leqslant\beta<r
be fixed but arbirary. Using the Mean value Theorem and Hölder and triangular inequalities, one can easily get that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{\beta}-(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{\beta}\big|dx=\int_{\Omega}\big|\big((\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{2}\big)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-\big((\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{2}\big)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\big|dx

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big|(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{2}-(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{2}\big|\big((\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{2}+(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{2}\big)^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{D}_{t}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)(x))(\mathcal{D}_{t}(\varphi_{n}+\varphi)(x))\big((\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{2}+(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{2}\big)^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\beta\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{D}_{t}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)(x))\big(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x)+\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x)\big)^{\beta-1}dx\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\beta\|\mathcal{D}_{t}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)}\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}+\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)}^{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C\big(\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)}^{\beta-1}+\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)}^{\beta-1}\big)\|\mathcal{D}_{t}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\big({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L_{0}^{t,\beta}(\Omega)}^{\beta-1}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L_{0}^{t,\beta}(\Omega)}^{\beta-1}\big){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{n}-\varphi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L_{0}^{t,\beta}(\Omega)},

for some
C>0
depending only on
\beta
. Then, arguing exactly as in the proof of (
3.14
), we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big|(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{\beta}-(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{\beta}\big|dx=0.

Since
\beta\in[2,r)
was fixed but arbitrary, we have proved (
3.24
) for all
2\leqslant\alpha<r
.

It remains to deal with the case
1<\alpha<2
. To that end, note that
(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n})_{n}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
is a bounded non-negative sequence such that
\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
as
n\to\infty
. Hence, it follows that
\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}\to\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
. Also, observe that, for any
\alpha\in(1,2)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big|(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{\alpha}-(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{\alpha}\big|dx

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C\,\|\mathcal{D}_{t}(\varphi_{n})-\mathcal{D}_{t}(\varphi)\|_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}\big(\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}^{\alpha-1}+\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}^{\alpha-1}\big)\leqslant\widetilde{C}\,\|\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}-\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

with
C>0
depending only on
\alpha>0
and
\widetilde{C}>0
depending only on
\alpha
,
r
,
|\Omega|
and
\ell
. Combining this chain of inequalities with the fact that
\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}\to\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big|(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi_{n}(x))^{\alpha}-(\mathcal{D}_{t}\varphi(x))^{\alpha}\big|dx=0.

Once the claim is proved, to conclude the proof in the case where
m>N/s
, it just remains to prove the claimed regularity, that follows thanks to Proposition
2.1
,
[
20
, Proposition 1.4 (iii)]
and the definition of
E_{\eta}
.

Case 2:

\overline{m}<m\leqslant N/s
.

First of all, let us fix
r=r(m,s,t,q)>0
such that
\max\{2,qm\}<r<\frac{mN}{(N-ms+mN(\gamma-s))^{+}}
and consider
\lambda_{\star}
and
\ell
as in (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) respectively. Then, let us define

(3.25)

\widetilde{E}_{\eta}:=\Big\{v\in L_{0}^{\gamma,1+\eta}(\Omega):{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L_{0}^{\gamma,1+\eta}(\Omega)}\leqslant M\textup{ and }{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L_{0}^{\gamma,r}(\Omega)}\leqslant\ell^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\},

with
\gamma
and
\eta
as in (
3.20
) and

M:=C\Big(\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{r-q}{r}}\widetilde{k}^{q}\ell+\lambda_{\star}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),

with
C>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
N
,
s
and
t
(cf. Proposition
2.1
). It is easy to check that
\widetilde{E}_{\eta}
is a bounded, closed and convex set of
L_{0}^{\gamma,1+\eta}(\Omega)
. Moreover, arguing as we did in the first case, one can prove that

(3.26)

\widetilde{T}_{2}:\widetilde{E}_{\eta}\to L_{0}^{\gamma,1+\eta}(\Omega),\quad\varphi\mapsto u,

where
u
is the unique weak solution to (
3.22
), is well-defined, continuous, compact and satisfies
\widetilde{T}_{2}(\widetilde{E}_{\eta})\subset\widetilde{E}_{\eta}
. Hence, applying again Schauder’s fixed point Theorem, the existence follows also in this case. Having at hand the existence, the claimed regularity follows again from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The appearance of the upper bound
\overline{q}\in(1,+\infty]
in Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
is natural in this kind of existence results. Even in the local case (
1.1
), one finds an upper bound on
q
depending on the regularity of the data
f
and on the dimension
N
. In our proofs, the upper bound
\overline{q}
naturally appears when applying Proposition
2.1
and depends on the regularity of the datum
f
, on the dimension
N
and on the order of the “nonlocal gradient”. The lower bound
\overline{m}
is again related to the use of Proposition
2.1
but also to the fact that we need
r>2
in the proof of Theorem
3.4
.

4.
Non–existence results

This section is devoted to prove non-existence results for (
KPZ1
) and (
KPZ3
). Let us start analysing (
KPZ1
). As already mentioned in the introduction, the proof of the non-existence result for (
KPZ1
) is completely different from its counterpart for (
KPZ3
). Here, we prove a generalization of Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0<t<\min\{1,2s\}
,
\mu_{2}\geqslant\mu(x)\geqslant\mu_{1}>0
,
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
with
f\gneqq 0
and
q>\frac{2(s+1)}{t+2}
. Then, there exists
\lambda^{\star\star}>0
such that, for all

\lambda>\lambda^{\star\star}
, (
KPZ1
) has no weak solution in
W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to (
KPZ1
) and let
\phi\in W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the unique (energy) solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}\phi&=1,\quad&&\textup{ in }\Omega,\\
\phi&=0,&&\textup{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

First of all, note that
\phi\in\mathbb{X}^{s}(\Omega)
, so that we can use
\phi
as test function in (
KPZ1
) and get that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\mu(x)|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u|^{q}\phi\,dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,dx\geqslant\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u|^{q}\phi\,dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,dx.

On the other hand, let
\psi\in W_{0}^{\frac{t}{2},2}(\Omega)\cap C^{\frac{t}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the unique (energy) solution to the problem

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}\psi&=1,\quad&&\textup{ in }\Omega,\\
\psi&=0,&&\textup{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)\subset W_{0}^{\frac{t}{2},2}(\Omega)
we can test (
4.2
) with
u
and integrate by parts, so that, using (
4.1
), we obtain

(4.3)

\int_{\Omega}\psi(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u\,dx\geqslant\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u|^{q}\phi\,dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,dx.

Moreover, using Young’s inequality, we easily see that

(4.4)

\int_{\Omega}\psi(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u\,dx\leqslant\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}u|^{q}\phi\,dx+{C_{q}}\mu_{1}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\psi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}{\phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}dx.

Thus, combining (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we obtain that

(4.5)

\lambda\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,dx\leqslant{C_{q}}\mu_{1}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\psi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}{\phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}dx.

Finally, note that (see e.g.
[
11
, Eq. (1.15)]
) there exists
C_{0}>0
(depending only on
\Omega
,
s,t
and
N
) such that

(4.6)

C_{0}^{-1}\delta^{s}(x)\leqslant\phi(x)\leqslant C_{0}\delta^{s}(x)\quad\textup{ and }\quad C_{0}^{-1}\delta^{\frac{t}{2}}(x)\leqslant\psi(x)\leqslant C_{0}\delta^{\frac{t}{2}}(x),\quad\textup{ in }\ \overline{\Omega}.

Hence, since
q>\frac{2(s+1)}{t+2}
, the right hand side in (
4.5
) is bounded, an thus necessarily

(4.7)

\lambda\leqslant\frac{C_{q}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\psi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}{\phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}dx.}{\displaystyle{\mu_{1}^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,dx}=:\lambda^{\star\star}.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

The regularity imposed on
f
can be slightly weakened. Indeed, to prove our non–existence result, namely Theorem
4.1
, we only need
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\delta^{s}(x)dx)
.
Moreover, observe that in the proof we only used that
u
is a (weak) supersolution to (
KPZ1
). On the negative side, observe that the bound appearing on the power
q
seems to be technical.

We also prove here a generalization of
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
covering the cases where
q\neq 2
and/or
t\neq s
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
0<t<\min\{1,2s\}
,
\mu_{2}\geqslant\mu(x)\geqslant\mu_{1}>0
,
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
with
f^{+}\not\equiv 0
and
q>1
. Then, there exists
\lambda^{\star\star}>0
such that, for all
\lambda>\lambda^{\star\star}
, (
KPZ3
) has no weak solution in
W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to (
KPZ3
) and let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be an arbitrary non-negative function such that

(4.8)

\int_{\Omega}f\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx>0\quad\textup{ and }\quad\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{D}_{2s-t}\phi\big)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx<+\infty.

Using
\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}
as test function in (
KPZ3
), we get

(4.9)

\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta)^{s}(\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}})\,dx\geqslant\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{D}_{t}u)^{q}\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}f\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx.

On the other hand, using the Mean value Theorem and Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta)^{s}(\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}})\,dx=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(x)-\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C_{q}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)||\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}(\phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}(x)+\phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}(y))\,dydx

\displaystyle\quad=2C_{q}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)||\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}(x)\,dydx\leqslant 2C_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{t}u\big)\,\big(\mathcal{D}_{2s-t}\phi\big)\ \phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=2C_{q}\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{D}_{t}u\big)\,\big(\mathcal{D}_{2s-t}\phi\big)\phi^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\,dx\leqslant\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{D}_{t}u)^{q}\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx+\widetilde{C}_{q,\mu_{1}}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{D}_{2s-t}\phi)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx\,.

Combining the above chain of inequalities with (
4.9
), we get that necessarily

\lambda\int_{\Omega}f\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx\leqslant\widetilde{C}_{q,\mu_{1}}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{D}_{2s-t}\phi)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx.

Hence, defining

\lambda^{\star\star}:=\inf\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{q,\mu_{1}}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{D}_{2s-t}\phi)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\phi^{\frac{q}{q-1}}dx}:\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\textup{ is non-negative and satisfies }\eqref{propphi}\right\},

the result immediately follows.
∎

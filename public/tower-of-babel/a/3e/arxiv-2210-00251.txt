Some unipotent Arthur packets for reductive p-adic Groups
Dan Ciubotaru, Lucas Mason-Brown, and Emile Okada

Abstract
. Let 𝗄 {\mathsf{k}} be a p p -adic field and let 𝐆 ⁡ ( 𝗄 ) \mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}) be the 𝗄 {\mathsf{k}} -points of a connected reductive group, inner to split. The set of Aubert-Zelevinsky duals of the constituents of a tempered L-packet form an Arthur packet for 𝐆 ⁡ ( 𝗄 ) \mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}) . In this paper, we give an alternative characterization of such Arthur packets in terms of the wavefront set, proving in some instances a conjecture of Jiang-Liu and Shahidi. Pursuing an analogy with real and complex groups, we define some special unions of Arthur packets which we call weak Arthur packets and describe their constituents in terms of their Langlands parameters.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic
0
with ring of integers
\mathfrak{o}
, finite residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of cardinality
q
and valuation
\mathsf{val}_{\mathsf{k}}
. Fix an algebraic closure
\bar{\mathsf{k}}
of
\mathsf{k}
and let
{\mathsf{k}^{un}}\subset\bar{\mathsf{k}}
be the maximal unramified extension of
\mathsf{k}
in
\bar{\mathsf{k}}
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over
{\mathsf{k}}
and let
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
be the group of
{\mathsf{k}}
-rational points. We assume throughout that
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
is inner to split.

Let
W_{{\mathsf{k}}}
be the Weil group associated to
{\mathsf{k}}

[
Tat79
, (1.4)]
. Then the Weil-Deligne group is the semidirect product
[
Del73
, §8.3.6]

W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}=W_{{\mathsf{k}}}\ltimes\mathbb{C}

where
W_{{\mathsf{k}}}
acts on
\mathbb{C}
via

wxw^{-1}=\|w\|x,\qquad x\in\mathbb{C},\ w\in W_{k}.

Let
G^{\vee}
denote the complex Langlands dual group associated to
\mathbf{G}
, see
[
Bor79
, §2.1]
.

Definition 1.0.1
.

A
Langlands parameter
is a continuous homomorphism

(1.0.1)

\phi:W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}\rightarrow G^{\vee}

which respects the Jordan decompositions in
W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}
and
G^{\vee}
(
[
Bor79
, §8.1]
).

To each Langlands parameter
\phi
, one hopes to associate an
L
-packet
\Pi^{\mathsf{Lan}}_{\phi}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
of irreducible admissible
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
-representations
[
Bor79
, §10]
(note: these packets have not been defined in full generalality, see
[
Art13
]
or
[
Kal22
]
for a discussion of the status of the local Langlands conjectures).

Definition 1.0.2
.

An
Arthur parameter
is a continuous homomorphism

\psi:W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}

such that

(i)

The restriction of
\psi
to
W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}
is a tempered Langlands parameter.

(ii)

The restriction of
\psi
to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is algebraic.

Arthur parameters are considered modulo the
G^{\vee}
-action on the target.

For each Arthur parameter
\psi
there is an associated Langlands parameter
\phi_{\psi}:W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}
defined by the formula

(1.0.2)

\phi_{\psi}(w)=\psi(w,\begin{pmatrix}\|w\|^{1/2}&0\\
0&\|w\|^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}),\qquad w\in W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime},

where
\|\bullet\|
is the pull-back to
W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}
of the norm map on
W_{{\mathsf{k}}}
. The local version of Arthur’s conjectures can be stated succinctly as follows.

Conjecture 1.0.3

(Section 6,
[
Art89
]
)
.

For each Arthur parameter
\psi
, there is an associated finite set
\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
of irreducible admissible
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
-representations called the
Arthur packet
attached to
\psi
. The set
\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
should satisfy various properties, including:

(i)

\Pi^{\mathsf{Lan}}_{\phi_{\psi}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))\subseteq\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
,

(ii)

\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
consists of unitary representations,

along with several other properties (e.g. stability, endoscopy), see
[
Art89
, Section 4]
or
[
ABV92
, Chapter 1]
.

Although a general definition is lacking, the packets
\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
have been defined in various special cases. For quasisplit orthogonal and symplectic groups, Arthur has defined them using endoscopic transfer and has proved that they satisfy all of the conjectured properties
[
Art13
, Theorems 1.5.1 and 2.2.1]
. His constructions were extended in
[
Mok15
]
to quasisplit unitary groups. Explicit definitions, compatible with Arthur’s, were proposed by Mœglin in
[
Mœg06
,
Mœg14
]
, see also the exposition in
[
Xu17
]
and the references therein. For split
G_{2}
, some Arthur packets were constructed in
[
GG05
]
. Recently, Cunningham and his collaborators have proposed a ‘microlocal’ approach to defining Arthur packets, see
[
CFMMX17
]
.

There is one simple class of Arthur parameters for which a general (i.e. case-free) definition of Arthur packets is available.

Definition 1.0.4
.

An Arthur parameter
\psi
is
basic
if the restriction
\psi|_{W_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{\prime}}
is trivial.

By the Jacobson-Morozov theorem, there is a natural bijection

\displaystyle\{\text{nilpotent adjoint }G^{\vee}\text{-orbits}\}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\text{basic Arthur parameters}\}/G^{\vee}

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}.

For each nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, we choose an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple
(e^{\vee},f^{\vee},h^{\vee})
and consider the semisimple element
q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}\in G^{\vee}
. This element is well-defined modulo conjugation by
G^{\vee}
, and therefore determines an ‘infinitesimal character’, see Section
2.4
. There is a well-known involution defined by Aubert and Zelevinsky on the set of irreducible admissible
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
-representations, see Section
2.5
. We denote this involution by
X\mapsto\mathrm{AZ}(X)
. The Arthur packets attached to basic Arthur parameters can be defined as follows, , see for example
[
Art13
, §7.1]
.

Definition 1.0.5
.

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent adjoint
G^{\vee}
-orbit and let
\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
be the associated basic Arthur parameter. Then
\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is the set of irreducible
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
-representations
X
with unipotent cuspidal support such that

(i)

The infinitesimal character of
X
is
q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}
.

(ii)

\mathsf{AZ}(X)
is tempered.

The
wavefront set
of an admissible
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
-representation is a fundamental invariant coming from the Harish-Chandra-Howe local character expansion. In its classical form, the wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}(X)
of
X
is a collection of nilpotent
{\mathbf{G}}({\mathsf{k}})
-orbits in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathsf{k}})
, namely the
maximal
nilpotent orbits for which the Fourier transforms of the associated orbital integrals contribute to the local character expansion of the distribution character of
X
,
[
Har99
, Theorem 16.2]
. In this paper, we consider two coarser invariants (see Section
2.6
for the precise definitions). The first of these invariants is the
algebraic wavefront set
, denoted
\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
. This is a collection of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}({\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
, see for example
[
Wal18
, p. 1108]
(where it is simply referred to as the ‘wavefront set’ of
X
). The second invariant is
\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X)
, a natural refinement of
{}^{{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
called the
canonical unramified wavefront set
, defined recently in
[
Oka21
]
. This is a collection of nilpotent orbits
\mathfrak{g}({\mathsf{k}^{un}})
(modulo a certain equivalence relation
\sim_{A}
). The relationship between these three invariants is as follows: the algebraic wavefront set
\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
is deducible from the usual wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}(X)
as well as the canonical unramified wavefront set
\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X)
. It is not known whether the canonical unramified wavefront set is deducible from the usual wavefront set, but we expect this to be the case, see
[
Oka21
, Section 5.1]
for a careful discussion of this topic.

For real and complex groups, it is possible to define Arthur packets in terms of the wavefront set, see
[
BV85
]
and
[
ABV92
, Section 27]
. Thus, it is natural to hope for a similar definition in the
p
-adic setting. Our main result (Theorem
3.0.3
) gives an alternative characterization of the Arthur packets attached to basic Arthur parameters in terms of the canonical unramified wavefront set. The most precise version of our result requires two additional ingredients which will be introduced in Section
2
(the classification of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}(\bar{{\mathsf{k}}})
and Achar duality). Here is a simplified version.

Theorem 1.0.6
.

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent adjoint
G^{\vee}
-orbit and let
\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
be the associated basic Arthur parameter. Then
\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is the set of irreducible representations
X
with unipotent cuspidal support such that

(i)

The infinitesimal character of
X
is
q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}
.

(ii)

The canonical unramified wavefront set
\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X)
is minimal subject to (i).

As a consequence of Theorem
1.0.6
, we prove a conjecture of Jiang-Liu and Shahidi in the case of basic Arthur packets (see Corollary
3.0.5
below).

If we replace
\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X)
in condition (ii) with the coarser invariant
{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
, we get a larger set of representations, which we call a
weak Arthur packet
. We conjecture that this set is a union of Arthur packets (and, in particular, that its constituents are unitary).

For

\mathcal{N}_{o}

For brevity we will write
\mathcal{N}(F^{\prime}/F)

\mathcal{N}

2.2.
Duality for nilpotent orbits

Write

(2.2.1)

d:\mathcal{N}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\vee},\qquad d:\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{0}.

for the
Barbasch-Lusztig-Spaltenstein-Vogan duality maps
(see
[
Spa82
, Chapter 3]
or
[
BV85
, Appendix A]
).
Write

(2.2.2)

d_{S}:\mathcal{N}_{o,c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o},\qquad d_{S}:\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o,c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{N}_{o}

for the duality maps defined by Sommers in
[
Som01
, Section 6]
and

(2.2.3)

D:\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o,\bar{c}},\qquad D:\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o,\bar{c}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}

for the duality maps defined by Achar in (
[
Ach03

Since the latter two maps are less well known we give a (slightly non-standard) account of their definitions and basic properties.

The original precursor to the duality map
d
is the involution on the set of two sided cells of the finite Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
attached to
G
with equal parameters apparent in
[
Lus79
]
.
Upon composing this involution with the Springer correspondence (and identifying the two Hecke algebras obtained from
G
and
G^{\vee}
) one obtains the duality map
d

[
Spa82
, Chapter 3]
.
The map
d
is not an involution, but satisfies
d^{3}=d
.
The reason is that the 2-sided cells of
\mathcal{H}
only biject with
\im(d)
(the so called special nilpotent orbits) instead of whole of
\mathcal{N}_{o}
and so
d
is only an involution when restricted to this set.

One drawback of this construction however is that the non-special nilpotent orbits only play a peripheral role.
Here the affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{aff}
of
G^{\vee}
with equal parameters hints at a remedy for the situation.
In particular, the 2-sided cells of
\mathcal{H}_{aff}
are in bijection with
\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}
.
Moreover, for any element
s\in T
, the finite Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{s}
associated to connected centraliser
C_{G}^{\circ}(s)
embeds into
\mathcal{H}_{aff}
.
Along with an induction operation on 2-sided cells defined in
[
Lus89
, Section 6.5]
, we are now in a position to define
d_{S}
.
For a pair
(\mathbb{O},C)\in\mathcal{N}_{o,c}
let
x\in\mathbb{O}
and
s
be a semisimple element of
C_{G}(x)
such that
sC_{G}^{\circ}(x)
lies in
C
.
Conjugating
s
appropriately we may assume that it lies in
T
.
Write
L_{s}=C_{G}^{\circ}(s)
and
d^{L_{s}}
for the duality map
d
for the complex reductive group
L_{s}
.
Then

d_{S}(\mathbb{O},C)=\Ind d^{L_{s}}(L_{s}.x).

Here we interpret
d^{L_{s}}(L_{s}.x)
as a 2-sided cell of
\mathcal{H}_{s}
and the resulting induced cell of
\mathcal{H}_{aff}
as an elements of
\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}
using the bijections outlined in this and the preceeding paragraph.
Unravelling these constructions gives the clean construction given in
[
Som01
]
using Springer theory, and the resulting map
d_{S}:\mathcal{N}_{o,c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}
is well-defined and has image the whole of
\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}
.
Moreover, it is clear that
d_{S}(\mathbb{O},1)=d(\mathbb{O})
and so it extends the usual map
d
.

This finally brings us to the definition of
D
.
In defining
d_{S}
we obtained a map which is no longer symmetric in target and source.
Achar’s duality map rectifies this.
In
[
Som01
, Proposition 15]
, Sommers shows that
d_{S}
factors through
\mathfrak{Q}:\mathcal{N}_{o,c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
, and by
[
Ach03
, Theorem 1]
we have an embedding

\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}\xhookrightarrow{i}\mathcal{N}_{o}\times\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee},\quad(\mathbb{O},C)\mapsto(\mathbb{O},d_{S}(\mathbb{O},C)).

There is also of course the corresponding embedding for the dual object

\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o,\bar{c}}\xhookrightarrow{i^{\vee}}\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}\times\mathcal{N}_{o}

and this suggests a very natural candidate for
D
, namely
D=(i^{\vee})^{-1}\circ\tilde{D}\circ i
where

\tilde{D}:\mathcal{N}_{o}\times\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}\times\mathcal{N}_{o},\quad(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O}^{\vee})\mapsto(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},\mathbb{O}).

Indeed, whenever
\tilde{D}\circ i(\xi)\in\im i^{\vee}
, this is equivalent to the definition for
D(\xi)
given by Achar.
However in general, not all
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
enjoy this property.
Let us call
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}

special
if
\tilde{D}\circ i(\xi)\in\im i^{\vee}
and let us endow
\mathcal{N}_{o}\times\mathcal{N}_{o}^{\vee}
with the partial order

(\mathbb{O}_{1},\mathbb{O}_{1}^{\vee})\leq(\mathbb{O}_{2},\mathbb{O}_{2}^{\vee}),\text{ if }\mathbb{O}_{1}\leq\mathbb{O}_{2},\mathbb{O}_{1}^{\vee}\geq\mathbb{O}_{2}^{\vee}

(which in turn endows
\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
with a partial order via the embedding
i
).
By
[
Ach03
, Theorem 2.4]
, every
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
is dominated by a unique smallest special
\xi^{\prime}
.
We can then define
D(\xi)=(i^{\vee})^{-1}\circ\tilde{D}\circ i(\xi^{\prime})
, and it is easily seen that this is equivalent to Achar’s definition of
D
.
The resulting map
D:\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}^{\vee}
is then a duality map in much that same way that
d
is: it satisfies
D^{3}=D
.
It also extends
d_{S}
in the sense that
\mathrm{pr}_{1}\circ D=d_{S}
.
These results are enough for our purposes, but let us just note that in obvious analogy to the preceding situations one might wonder if
\im D
is in bijection with the 2-sided cells of some Hecke algebra.
This is to the best of out knowledge still unknown.

2.4.
Representations with unipotent cuspidal support

We briefly recall the classification of irreducible representations with unipotent cuspidal support defined in
[
Lus95
, §1.6,§1.21]
.
If
\mathcal{S}
is a subset of
G^{\vee}
, let

Z^{1}_{G^{\vee}_{\mathsf{sc}}}(\mathcal{S})=\text{ preimage of }G^{\vee}(\mathcal{S})/Z(G^{\vee})\text{ under the projection }G^{\vee}_{\mathsf{sc}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}_{\ad},

and let
A^{1}(\mathcal{S})
denote the component group of
Z^{1}_{G^{\vee}_{\mathsf{sc}}}(\mathcal{S})
.

Write
\Phi(G^{\vee})
for the set of
G^{\vee}
-orbits (under conjugation) of triples
(s,n,\rho)
where

•

s\in G^{\vee}
is semisimple,

•

n\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(s)n=qn
,

•

\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(A^{1}_{G^{\vee}}(s,n))
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
s\in T^{\vee}
. Note that
n\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
is necessarily nilpotent. The group
G^{\vee}(s)
acts with finitely many orbits on the
q
-eigenspace of
\Ad(s)

\mathfrak{g}_{q}^{\vee}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\mid\operatorname{Ad}(s)x=qx\}

In particular, there is a unique open
G^{\vee}(s)
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{q}^{\vee}
.

Fix an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple
\{n^{-},h^{\vee},n\}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
with
h^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
and set

s_{0}:=sq^{-\frac{h^{\vee}}{2}}.

Then
\operatorname{Ad}(s_{0})n=n
.

A parameter
(s,n,\rho)\in\Phi(G^{\vee})
is called
discrete
if
Z_{G^{\vee}}(s,n)
does not contain a nontrivial torus.
A discrete parameter is called
cuspidal
if
(u,\rho)
is a cuspidal pair in
Z_{G^{\vee}}(s)
in the sense of Lusztig.

Let
\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathsf{k}))
denote the equivalence classes of irreducible
{\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathsf{k})
-representations with unipotent cuspidal support and

\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}({\mathbf{G}})=\bigsqcup_{\omega\in\Omega_{\mathsf{ad}}}\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathsf{k})).

(In this subsection,
{\mathbf{G}}^{1}(\mathsf{k})
is the split form.)
The following theorem is a combination of several results, namely
[
KL87
, Theorems 7.12, 8.2, 8.3]
for
{\mathbf{G}}
adjoint and Iwahori-spherical representations,
[
Lus95
, Corollary 6.5]
and
[
Lus02
, Theorem 10.5]
for
{\mathbf{G}}
adjoint and representations with unipotent cuspidal support,
[
Ree02
, Theorem 3.5.4]
for
{\mathbf{G}}
arbitrary and Iwahori-spherical representations, and
[
FOS20
,
FO20
,
Sol18
]
for
{\mathbf{G}}
arbitrary and representations with unipotent cuspidal support. See
[
AMS21
, §2.3]
for a discussion of the compatibility between these classifications.

Theorem 2.4.1

(Deligne-Langlands-Lusztig correspondence)
.

There is a bijection

\Phi(G^{\vee})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}({\mathbf{G}}),\qquad(s,n,\rho)\mapsto X(s,n,\rho),

such that

(1)

X(s,n,\rho)
is tempered if and only if
s_{0}\in T_{c}^{\vee}
(in particular,
\overline{G^{\vee}(s)n}=\mathfrak{g}_{q}^{\vee}
),

(2)

X(s,n,\rho)
is square integrable (modulo the center) if and only if it is tempered and
(s,n,\rho)
is discrete.

(3)

X(s,n,\rho)
is supercuspidal if and only if
(s,n,\rho)
is a cuspidal parameter.

(4)

X(s,n,\rho)\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}({\mathbf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathsf{k}))
if and only if
\rho|_{Z(G^{\vee})}
is a multiple of
\zeta_{\omega}
.

This bijection satisfies several natural desiderata (including formal degrees, equivariance with respect to tensoring by weakly unramified characters), see
[
Sol18
, Theorem 1]
and
[
FOS20
, Theorem 2]
.

We denote by
\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}_{s}({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
the set of irreducible
{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})
-representations
X(s,n,\rho)
for a fixed
s\in T^{\vee}
.

2.5.
Aubert-Zelevinsky duality

There is an involution
\mathsf{AZ}
on the Grothendieck group
R(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
of smooth
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
-representation, called
Aubert-Zelevinsky duality

[
Aub95
, §1]
. This involution can defined in the following manner. Let
\mathcal{Q}
denote the set of parabolic subgroups of
{\mathbf{G}}
defined over
\mathsf{k}
and containing
\mathbf{B}
. For every
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}
, let
i_{\mathbf{Q}(\mathsf{k})}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})}
and
\mathsf{r}_{\mathbf{Q}(\mathsf{k})}^{\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})}
denote the normalized parabolic induction and normalized Jacquet functors, respectively. Then
\mathsf{AZ}
is defined by

(2.5.1)

\widetilde{\mathsf{AZ}}:R(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}R(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})),\qquad\widetilde{\mathsf{AZ}}(X)=\sum_{\mathbf{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}}(-1)^{r_{\mathbf{Q}}}~i_{\mathbf{Q}(\mathsf{k})}^{{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})}(\mathsf{r}_{\mathbf{Q}(\mathsf{k})}^{{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})}(X)),

where
r_{\mathbf{Q}}
is the semisimple rank of the reductive quotient of
\mathbf{Q}
.
If a class
X\in R(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is irreducible and
X
occurs as a composition factor of a parabolically induced module
i_{\mathbf{Q}(\mathsf{k})}^{{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})}(\sigma)
, where
\sigma
is a supercuspidal representation, then, by
[
Aub95
, Corollaire 3.9]

(-1)^{r_{\mathbf{Q}}}\widetilde{\mathsf{AZ}}(X)
is the class of an irreducible representation, which we denote
\mathsf{AZ}(X)
.

Moreover, it is known that
\mathsf{AZ}
is an involution on the set of irreducible representations in any Bernstein component, see
[
Aub95
, §3]
, also
[
BBK18
, §3.2]
. In particular, it preserves the
irreducible representations with unipotent cuspidal support, and in fact, it induces an involution on
\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}_{s}({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
, for each
s\in W\backslash T^{\vee}
. In addition, by
[
BM89
, Theorem 1.1]
for Iwahori-spherical representations and
[
BC13
, Theorem 1.2.1]
in general,
\mathsf{AZ}
maps unitary representations to unitary representations in the category of representations with unipotent supercuspidal support.

Let

.

2.7.
Basic Arthur packets

To formulate the relation between
\mathsf{AZ}
and Arthur packets, it will be convenient to recall an alternative formulation of the Langlands classification. A
simplified Langlands parameter
is a continuous homomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}:W_{{\mathsf{k}}}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}
such that
\widetilde{\varphi}(W_{k})
consists of semisimple elements and
\widetilde{\varphi}|_{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})}
is algebraic. A simplified Langlands parameter is tempered if the image of
W_{k}
is compact (the idea of replacing the Weil-Deligne group
W_{k}^{\prime}
with
W_{k}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
in the non-Archimedean case was first proposed by Langlands in
[
Lan79
, p. 209]
). Although there are good reasons for preferring the Weil-Deligne group formulation, there is a bijection between simplified and honest Langlands parameters, see
[
Kna97
, p. 278]
. Similarly, a
simplified Arthur parameter
is a continuous homomorphism
\widetilde{\psi}:W_{k}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Lan}}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Art}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}
such that the restriction of
\widetilde{\psi}
to
W_{k}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Lan}}
is a tempered simplified Langlands parameter and the restriction of
\widetilde{\psi}
to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Art}}
is algebraic. The bijection between simplified and honest Langlands parameters induces a bijection between simplified and honest Arthur parameters.

If
\widetilde{\psi}
is a simplified Arthur parameter, we get a second simplified Arthur parameter
\widetilde{\psi}^{t}
by ‘flipping’ the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
factors,
[
Art13
, p. 390]
, i.e.

\widetilde{\psi}^{t}(w,x,y):=\widetilde{\psi}(w,y,x),\qquad w\in W_{k},\ x,y\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}).

Via the bijection between simplified and honest Arthur parameters, the map
\widetilde{\psi}\mapsto\widetilde{\psi}^{t}
induces an involution on honest Arthur parameters, which we also denote by
\psi\mapsto\psi^{t}
. It is expected that

(2.7.1)

\mathsf{AZ}(\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))=\Pi_{\psi^{t}}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})).

In the case when
\mathbf{G}
is orthogonal or symplectic, this duality is discussed in
[
Art13
, §7.1]
. If the Arthur parameter
\psi
is trivial on the inertia subgroup
I_{k}
, then the associated Langlands parameter
\phi_{\psi}
is unramified. So the representations in the Langlands packet
\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{\mathsf{Lan}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))
, and hence also in the Arthur packet
\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))
, are of unipotent cuspidal support.

If
\widetilde{\psi}
is trivial on
W_{k}\times\SL(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Art}}
, then
\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))=\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{\mathsf{Lan}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))
consists of
tempered
representations with real infinitesimal character. So if
\psi
is basic (cf. Definition
1.0.4
), the desideratum (
2.7.1
) suggests the following definition for the associated Arthur packet
\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
, see
[
Art13
, (7.1.10)]
.

Definition 2.7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent adjoint
G^{\vee}
-orbit and let
\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
be the associated basic Arthur parameter (cf. Definition
1.0.4
). Then

\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))=\{X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}_{q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))\mid\mathsf{AZ}(X)\text{ is tempered}\}.

Fix a nilpotent orbit

-orbit of

The semisimple operator

Write
L^{\vee}
for the connected (Levi) subgroup corresponding to the centralizer
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{0}
of

. If we set

, then

L^{\vee}=G^{\vee}(s),\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]=\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}

where
G^{\vee}(s)
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
are as defined in Section
2.4
. Note that
L^{\vee}
acts by conjugation on each

.We will need the following well-known facts, see
[

or
[

, Prop 4.2]

.

The following are true:

(i)

is an open subset of

(and hence the unique open

-orbit therein).

(ii)

L^{\vee}e=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]
.

(iii)

.

.

.
Then

is tempered if and only if

.

Let
X=X(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n,\rho)
for
n\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]
and
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(A(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n))
. Note that

. So

\displaystyle X\text{ is tempered}

\displaystyle\iff\overline{L^{\vee}n}=g^{\vee}[2]

(Theorem
2.4.1
(i))

\displaystyle\iff n\in L^{\vee}e^{\vee}

(Lemma
3.0.1
(i))

\displaystyle\iff n\in\mathbb{O}^{\vee}

(Lemma
3.0.1
(ii))

\displaystyle\iff\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{X}=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}.

∎

Theorem 3.0.3
.

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent adjoint
G^{\vee}
-orbit and let
\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
be the associated basic Arthur parameter (cf. Definition

). Then

(3.0.1)

Let

But by Theorem

\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X,\mathbb{C})=D(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{AZ}(X)},1).

Therefore

\displaystyle X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))

\displaystyle\iff\mathsf{AZ}(X)\text{ is tempered}

(Definition

)

\displaystyle\iff\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{AZ}(X)}=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\iff D(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{AZ}(X)},1)=D(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)

(
[

, Lemma 2.1.2]
)

\displaystyle\iff\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X,\mathbb{C})=D(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)

(Equation

)

\displaystyle\iff\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X,\mathbb{C})\leq D(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)

There is a well-known conjecture regarding the (geometric) wavefront sets of the constituents of an Arthur packet. The following formulation essentially appears in
[
JL16
, Conjecture 3.2]
(in
[
JL16
]
it is stated only for classical groups. The version below is the natural generalization to arbitrary groups).

Conjecture 3.0.4

(cf.
[
JL16
, Conjecture 3.2]
)
.

Let
\psi
be an Arthur parameter and let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\psi}
be the nilpotent
G^{\vee}
-orbit corresponding to the restriction of
\psi
to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. Then

(i)

For every
X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
, there is a bound

\hphantom{}{}^{{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X,\mathbb{C})\leq d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\psi}).

(ii)

The bound in (i) is achieved for some
X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
.

We note that a global version of this conjecture appears in
[
Sha90
]
. An immediate consequence of Theorem
3.0.3
is that Conjecture
3.0.4
holds for basic Arthur packets. In fact, something stronger is true for this class of packets.

Corollary 3.0.5
.

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent adjoint
G^{\vee}
-orbit and let
\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
be the associated Arthur parameter. Then for every
X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
, there is an equality

\hphantom{}{}^{{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X,\mathbb{C})=d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}).

Proof.

Let
X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
.
By Theorem
3.0.3
we have that
\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X,\mathbb{C})=D(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\psi},1)
.
Recall from the discussion in Section
2.2
that
\mathrm{pr}_{1}\circ D=d_{S}
and that
d_{S}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)=d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee})
.
Moreover, by Proposition
2.6.1
, we have that
{}^{{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X,\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{pr}_{1}(\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X,\mathbb{C}))
.
Therefore

{}^{{\bar{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X,\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{pr}_{1}(\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X,\mathbb{C}))=\mathrm{pr}_{1}(D(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1))=d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}).

∎

3.1.
Weak Arthur packets

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent adjoint
G^{\vee}
-orbit and let
\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
be the associated Arthur parameter. Theorem
3.0.3
gives a characterization of the associated Arthur packet
\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
in terms of the
canonical unramified wavefront set

\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X)
. If we replace
\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X)
with the coarser invariant
\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
, we get a set which we will denote by
\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))

(3.1.1)

\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})):=\{X=X(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n,\rho)\mid\hphantom{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)\leq d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee})\}.

Because of the compatibility between
\underline{\mathrm{WF}}(X)
and
\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
, there is a containment

\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))\subseteq\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})).

In contrast to the set
\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}({\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k}))
, the set
\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is
not
the
\mathsf{AZ}
-dual of a tempered Arthur packet, nor is it parameterized (in any simple way) by representations of
A(\mathbb{O}^{\vee})
. So it is not a reasonable candidate for an Arthur packet. By analogy with the case of real reductive groups (see
[
ABV92
, Chapter 27]
), we call
\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
the
weak Arthur packet
attached to
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
.

Using Theorem
2.6.2
and Proposition
2.6.1
, it is easy to describe the constituents of
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
.

Corollary 3.1.1
.

The weak Arthur packet
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is the set of irreducible representations
\mathsf{AZ}(X(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n,\rho))
, where
n
belongs to the special piece (in the sense of
[
Spa82
]
) of
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}.

Guided by the case of real reductive groups, we conjecture that
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is a union of Arthur packets. Recall the notation of Section
2.7
.

Conjecture 3.1.2
.

The weak Arthur packet
\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is a (possibly non-disjoint) union of several Arthur packets

(3.1.2)

\Pi_{\psi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\Pi_{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}^{\mathsf{Art}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})),

corresponding to a collection of simplified Arthur parameters

(3.1.3)

\{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}:W_{\mathsf{k}}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Lan}}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Art}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}\mid\ 1\leq i\leq n\},

including the anti-tempered parameter
\widetilde{\psi}_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
, but also several others. Each
\widetilde{\psi}_{i}
is trivial on the Weil group
W_{k}
and thus corresponds to an algebraic homomorphism

\widetilde{\psi}_{i}:\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathrm{Lan}}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})_{\mathsf{Art}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}

Via the Jacobson-Morozov theorem, each such
\widetilde{\psi}_{i}
can be identified with a pair of nilpotent adjoint
G^{\vee}
-orbits

(3.1.4)

(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{i,\mathsf{Art}},\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{i,\mathsf{Lan}}),

and a pair of semisimple elements in a single fixed Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}

(3.1.5)

(h^{\vee}_{i,\mathsf{Art}},h^{\vee}_{i,\mathsf{Lan}}).

These elements should satisfy

(3.1.6)

\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}\in G^{\vee}(\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}_{i,\mathsf{Art}}+\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}_{i,\mathsf{Lan}}).

One might be tempted to guess that the set
\{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}\}
of parameters appearing in Conjecture
3.1.2
consists of
all
possible parameters satisfying the infinitesimal character condition (
3.1.6
). But nothing quite so simple is true. The tempered Arthur packet
corresponding to the pair
(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{Art}},\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{Lan}})=(0,\mathbb{O}^{\vee})
is almost never contained in
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
(see Section
4
for a detailed example). Thus, the problem of describing the Arthur packets in
\Pi_{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}^{\mathsf{Weak}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
seems interesting and nontrivial. We note that Conjecture
3.1.2
together with Corollary
3.1.1
implies the following conjecture (noting that
\mathsf{AZ}
preserves unitarity):

Conjecture 3.1.3
.

Let
(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n,\rho)
be a Deligne-Langlands-Lusztig parameter such that
n
belongs to the special piece of
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
. Then the irreducible representation
X(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n,\rho)
is unitary.

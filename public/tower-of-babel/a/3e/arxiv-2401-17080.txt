Assembling algorithm for Green’s tensors and absorbing boundary conditions for Galbrun’s equation in radial symmetry
Ha Pham, Florian Faucher, Damien Fournier, Hélène Barucq, Laurent Gizon

Abstract
Solar oscillations can be modeled by Galbrun’s equation which describes Lagrangian wave displacement in a self-gravitating stratified medium. For spherically symmetric backgrounds, we construct an algorithm to compute efficiently and accurately the coefficients of the Green’s tensor of the time-harmonic equation in vector spherical harmonic basis. With only two resolutions, our algorithm provides values of the kernels for all heights of source and receiver, and prescribes analytically the singularities of the kernels. We also derive absorbing boundary conditions (ABC) to model wave propagation in the atmosphere above the cut-off frequency. The construction of ABC, which contains varying gravity terms, is rendered difficult by the complex behavior of the solar potential in low atmosphere and for frequencies below the Lamb frequency. We carry out extensive numerical investigations to compare and evaluate the efficiency of the ABCs in capturing outgoing solutions. Finally, as an application towards helioseismology, we compute synthetic solar power spectra that contain pressure modes as well as internal-gravity (g-) and surface-gravity (f-) ridges which are missing in simpler approximations of the wave equation. For purpose of validation, the location of the ridges in the synthetic power spectra are compared with observed solar modes.

Vector spherical harmonics

We follow the definition and notation convention employed in

[
42
,
19
]
for the scalar
and vector spherical harmonics (VSH). The latter are constructed from the scalar spherical harmonics
\mathrm{Y}^{m}_{\ell}
together with tangential gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}:=\mathbf{e}_{\theta}\partial_{\theta}+\tfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\mathbf{e}_{\phi}\partial_{\phi}

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
and are given by, cf.
[
42

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{m}_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})=\mathrm{Y}^{m}_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})\,\mathbf{e}_{r},\quad\ell=0,1,\ldots;

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{m}_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})\,=\,\dfrac{\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\mathrm{Y}^{m}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}\,,\qquad\mathbf{C}^{m}_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})\,=-\dfrac{\mathbf{e}_{r}\times\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\mathrm{Y}^{m}_{\ell}}{\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}\,,\quad\ell=1,2,\dots\,.

We introduce the summation notation,

\sum_{(\ell,m)}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\,,\hskip 28.45274pt\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{(\ell,m)}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\,.

Features of the algorithm

Employing
Proposition

2
to compute the Green’s tensor has the following advantages:

1.

The singularity of the Dirac is avoided, thus the need to employ special techniques such as mesh refinement or singularity extraction to maintain accuracy is removed;

2.

Since one works with regular solutions, the singularity at equal height of source and receiver,
r=s
, is exactly described by the Heaviside distribution;

3.

From just two resolutions (the regular-at-0 solution
\phi
and the outgoing-at-infinity
\phi^{+}
), all components of
\mathbb{G}
, i.e., all coefficients
Eq.

3.57
, are given for any
r,s\in(\epsilon,r_{\max})
with
\epsilon\ll 1
and
r_{\max}>r_{\mathrm{a}}
.

Moreover, using first-order formulation discretized with HDG method further brings the following additional benefits:

1.

Without post-processing, each resolution gives both the primal
unknown and its derivative, both of which are needed to compute
the quantities in
Eq.

3.24
and in
Proposition

2
;

2.

With HDG method, the global discretized system is only in terms of one
unknown (despite solving a first-order system).

Remark 4

One can attempt to make the above approach viable by first

Here, we will employ the simple but commonly used assumption that the line-of-sight
\hat{\mathbf{l}}
is purely radial, i.e.

\hat{\mathbf{l}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})=\mathbf{e}_{r}\,.

(6.3)

The power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{l}^{m}
is defined from the spherical harmonic coefficients of
\psi
with

\mathcal{P}_{l}^{m}=\mathbb{E}\left[|\psi_{l}^{m}|^{2}\right],\hskip 14.22636pt\text{where}\hskip 8.5359pt\psi_{l}^{m}=\int\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\overline{Y_{l}^{m}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=-\mathrm{i}\omega a_{l}^{m}\,,

(6.4)

and coefficients
a_{l}^{m}
are the modal solution from
Eq.

3.4
.
The power spectrum can then be computed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{l}^{m}=\omega^{2}R_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\int\int\overline{G_{l}^{PP}(r,s)}G_{l}^{PP}(r,s^{\prime})\mathbb{E}[\overline{f_{l}^{m}(s)}f_{l}^{m}(s^{\prime})]s^{2}{s^{\prime}}^{2}dsds^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\omega^{2}R_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\int\int\overline{G_{l}^{PB}(r,s)}G_{l}^{PB}(r,s^{\prime})\mathbb{E}[\overline{g_{l}^{m}(s)}g_{l}^{m}(s^{\prime})]s^{2}{s^{\prime}}^{2}dsds^{\prime}.

(6.5)

It remains to define the expected values of the
source of excitation of the waves,

\mathbb{E}[\overline{f_{l}^{m}(s)}f_{l}^{m}(s^{\prime})]
and
\mathbb{E}[\overline{g_{l}^{m}(s)}g_{l}^{m}(s^{\prime})]
.
We suppose that the sources are uncorrelated, purely radial and coming from a single
depth
r_{s}
(see, e.g.,
[
11
]
), so that

\mathbb{E}[\overline{f_{l}^{m}(s)}f_{l}^{m}(s^{\prime})]=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\delta(s-s^{\prime})\delta(s-r_{s}),\hskip 14.22636pt\mathbb{E}[\overline{g_{l}^{m}(s)}g_{l}^{m}(s^{\prime})]=0.

(6.6)

In this case, the power spectrum is directly linked
to the Green’s kernel
G^{\mathrm{PP}}_{\ell}
:

\mathcal{P}_{l}^{m}(r_{\rm obs})=\omega^{2}R_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}|G^{\mathrm{PP}}_{\ell}(r_{\rm obs},r_{s})|^{2}.

(6.7)

Remark 9
.

The kernel
G^{\mathrm{PB}}_{\ell}
needs to be used if sources in the horizontal
direction are kept, and the kernels
G^{\mathrm{BBreg}}_{\ell}
and
G^{\mathrm{BP}}_{\ell}
appear if
one uses a general expression for the line-of-sight instead of the purely radial case
Eq.

6.3
.

6.3
Internal gravity modes in the solar spectrum

The propagative region that appears in the interior when including
the gravity (cf.
Remarks

5
and
1
)
corresponds to the region of internal gravity modes (
g

These modes exist for frequencies below the buoyancy frequency
N
,
that is, for frequencies below
0.5
\mathrm{mHz}
.
However, to be visible at the surface, these modes need to travel
from the radiative interior (below
r=0.7
) to the
surface and are thus strongly damped in the convective zone where
the potential is positive.
Contrary to pressure modes, a clear detection of internal gravity modes
in the observations has yet to be achieved due to their very small expected
amplitude at the surface
[
10
]
.

To highlight them in the simulations, we compute the power spectrum for a
source located at
s=0.7
and keeping the observation height at the surface.

eigenfrequencies from
gyre

the locations of maximum power in our power spectrum.
Finally, note that while the pressure modes are present when neglecting gravity,

7
Conclusion

For the Galbrun’s equation under spherical symmetry, we
develop theoretical and numerical tools
to compute efficiently and accurately
the coefficients of the Green’s tensor in VSH basis.
With
Propositions

1
and
2
, we characterize
explicitly the structure of the directional
kernels, and propose
Algorithm

1
to
compute their values for any height of sources and receivers
from two simulations.
Additionally, with the singularity of the directional kernels
prescribed analytically,
Algorithm

1
avoids
dealing with Dirac-type sources, and provides a numerically
stable and accurate way for their computation.

Regarding numerical resolution, we observe that
the original and Schrödinger modal ODE
behave in a complementary manner.
The original form is stable for low attenuation but unable
to handle large computational domains, while
the Schrödinger form is stable for arbitrarily
large intervals but unstable at very low attenuation.
This means that for a resolution up to low
atmosphere (
r_{\max}\sim$1.001$
), the original form
should be used to provide flexibility with low or
vanishing attenuation in parts of the interior region.
On the other hand,
the Schrödinger form is important to obtain ABCs and
is recommended if the background models extend high in the atmosphere.

To approximate outgoing-at-infinity solutions, we have constructed nonlocal and local ABCs which
contain a gravity term,
and we have numerically compared their efficiency.
In the presence of attenuation, our nonlocal condition
\mathcal{Z}_{\textsc{nl},\ell}

can be employed at scaled height
r=1.001
.
For numerical resolution in 2.5D and 3D, one can employ

\mathcal{Z}_{\textsc{SAIG0}}
or
\mathcal{Z}_{\textsc{HFG1}}
as a low-order condition, and

\mathcal{Z}_{\textsc{SAIG2},\ell}
or
\mathcal{Z}_{\textsc{HFG2},\ell}
which contain tangential derivatives.
Finally,
Algorithm

1
is used
to compute solar power spectra with background
model
S
-
AtmoI
, which display
ridges in agreement with computed eigenvalues and observed modes,
and make appear gravity modes missing with the scalar
approximations.

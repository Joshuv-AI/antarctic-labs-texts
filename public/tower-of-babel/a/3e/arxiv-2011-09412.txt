Finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds are almost determined by their finite quotient groups Thanks: Partially supported by NSFC grant 11925101, and National Key R&D Program of China 2020YFA0712800
Yi Liu

Abstract
. For any orientable finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold, this paper proves that the profinite isomorphism type of the fundamental group uniquely determines the isomorphism type of the first integral cohomology, as marked with the Thurston norm and the fibered classes; moreover, up to finite ambiguity, the profinite isomorphism type determines the isomorphism type of the fundamental group, among the class of finitely generated 3-manifold groups.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 57M50; Secondary 57M10, 30F40

1.
Introduction

A pair of finitely generated groups
G_{A}
and
G_{B}
are said to be
profinitely isomorphic
,
if every finite quotient group of
G_{A}
is isomorphic to a finite quotient group of
G_{B}
,
and vice versa.
It is known that being profinitely isomorphic is equivalent to
having isomorphic profinite completions.
For any class of finitely generated residually finite groups
\mathscr{C}
,
a group
G
in
\mathscr{C}
is said to be
profinitely rigid
among
\mathscr{C}

if any group in
\mathscr{C}
that is profinitely isomorphic to
G
is isomorphic to
G
.
We say that
G
is
profinitely almost rigid
among
\mathscr{C}
,
if there exist finitely many groups in
\mathscr{C}
,
such that any group in
\mathscr{C}
that is profinitely isomorphic to
G

is isomorphic to one of those groups.

For example, among all finitely generated residually finite groups,
it is easy to see that finite groups and abelian groups are profinitely rigid;
nilpotent groups are profinitely almost rigid,
but not always profinitely rigid
[
Pic71
,
Pic73
,
Rem67
]
;
and there exist metabelian groups which are not profinitely almost rigid
[
Pic74
]
.
More recently, M. R. Bridson, D. B. McReynolds, A. W. Reid, and R. Spitler
have proved the profinite rigidity of
several arithmetic lattices of small covolume in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, or in
\mathrm{O}^{+}(3,1)
,

[
BriMRS20
]
.
See
[
Aka12a
,
Aka12b
,
BriCR16
]
for more examples
of profinite rigidity or profinite almost rigidity
among various classes of lattices in Lie groups.
In the literature,
the profinite isomorphism class is also called the
genus
of the group,
which reflects the original analogy of this notion with
the genus of an integral quadratic form.
We refer the reader to Reid’s beautiful survey
[
Rei18
]

for an overview of this topic.

Among finitely generated
3
–manifold groups,
the fundamental group of any once-punctured torus bundle
over a circle is profinitely rigid
[
BriRW17
]
;
see also
[
BriR20
]
for the special case with the figure-eight knot group.
The Weeks manifold provides a closed example with profinite rigidity
[
BriMRS20
]
.
However, there exist Anosov torus bundles and periodic closed surface bundles
with non-isomorphic but profinitely isomorphic fundamental groups

[
Fun13
,
Hem14
,
Ste72
]
.
In a series of works,
G. Wilkes shows that orientable closed Seifert fiber spaces and graph manifolds
are profinitely almost rigid, and in fact,
any profinitely isomorphic pair of those manifolds are commensurable

[
Wlk17b
,
Wlk18
,
Wlk19
]
.

For any orientable connected closed
3
–manifold,
H. Wilton and P. A. Zalesskii show that
the profinite isomorphism type of the fundamental group
determines the profinite isomorphism types
of the prime factors and their geometric pieces,
and also determines
the geometric decomposition graphs together with the kinds of vertex geometries

[
WltZ10
,
WltZ17a
,
WltZ17b
]
.
Therefore,
in view of the geometric decomposition,
it remains the most interesting to understand
orientable finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds,
or more specifically,
how much the profinite completion tells about their groups.

The goal of this paper is to prove the following main result.
To emphasize,
throught this paper, we talk about finite hyperbolic
3
–manifolds
referring only to those
complete
ones.

Theorem 1.1
.

Among finitely generated
3
–manifold groups,
every finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifold group is profinitely almost rigid.

An important step toward Theorem
1.1
is
to show, for finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds,
that profinite group isomorphisms
are quite restrictive on the profinite free abelianization level.
To be precise, let
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
be
a pair of finitely generated residually finite groups,
and denote by
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
their abelianizations modulo torsion, respectively.
Denote by
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
the commutative ring of profinite integers,
and
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
its multiplicative group of units.
We say that an isomorphism
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{A}}\to\widehat{\pi_{B}}

between the profinite completions
is
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regular
,
if the induced
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
–linear isomorphism

\Psi_{*}\colon\widehat{H_{A}}\to\widehat{H_{B}}
is
the profinite completion of some isomorphism
H_{A}\to H_{B}

composed with the scalar multiplication by some unit
\mu\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
.
When
\mu
happens to be
\pm 1
,
being
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regular
is the equivalent to being
regular
modulo torsion,
where the latter condition was introduced
by M. Boileau and S. Friedl in
[
BoiF15
]
.

For orientable aspherical compact
3
–manifolds with empty or tori boundary,
Boileau and Friedl show that regular profinite group isomorphisms canonically determine
linear isomorphisms on the first integral cohomology,
preserving the Thurston norm and the fibered classes

[
BoiF15
]
.
In general,
isomorphisms between profinite
3
–manifold groups do not have to be regular.
Irregular examples include those in Hempel’s pairs
[
Hem14
]
,
and almost all automorphisms of the profinite
3
–torus group.
For finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifold groups,
one might suspect that all profinite isomorphisms are regular,
but that conjecture appears hard to approach.
What we are able to show in the present paper
is the
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regularity in the finite-volume hyperbolic case,
which still leads to a profinite invariance result
regarding the Thurston-norm unit balls and the fibered faces.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any pair of finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds,
every isomorphism between their profinite group completions is
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regular.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any pair of orientable finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds,
every isomorphism between their profinite group completions
determines a linear isomorphism on the first integral cohomology,
canonically up to the central symmetric involution,
such that the linear isomorphism and its inverse
both preserve the Thurston norm and the fibered classes.

In particular, Theorem
1.3
addresses
Reid’s Question 7.3 in
[
Rei18
]
affirmatively.
Theorem
1.1
provides positive evidence to
[
Rei15
, Question 9]
,
as it immediately implies that any profinite isomorphism class of lattices in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})

is the disjoint union of at most finitely many conjugacy classes.
It seems that Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

can be combined with former results of Wilkes and Wilton–Zalesskii,
yielding profinite almost rigidity for more general classes of
3
–manifolds,
such as aspherical compact
3
–manifolds of zero Euler characteristic.
However, the techniques of this paper have limitation in telling
if profinitely isomorphic finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds
should have a common finite cover, or equal volume, or same chirality.

The proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

is concluded in Section
6
.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

is concluded in Section
9
.

1.1.
Ingredients

The general idea of studying profinite
3
–manifold groups
is to read off topology from the profinite completion.
For instance, we wish to establish
either the profinite invariance of certain characteristic properties,
or a profinite correspondence of certain characteristic objects.
From that perspective,
our profinite almost rigidity for finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds
is proved with three major steps, as follows.
Technical ingredients of those steps are explained next with more detail.

(1)

We establish a profinite correspondence of the Thurston-norm cones (Theorem
5.1
),
based on a weaker correspondence of the fibered cones (Theorem
4.2
).
This step actually works for
nonpositively curved, virtually fibered
3
–manifolds.

(2)

We establish the
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regularity of profinite group isomorphisms (Theorem
1.2
),
using more virtual properties that are available for finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds.
This quickly leads to the profinite invariance of the Thurston norm (Theorem
1.3
),
and also to a useful profinite correspondence of the fiber subgroups (Corollary
6.2
).

(3)

We establish the invariance of the indexed periodic Nielsen numbers,
for profinitely corresponding fibers and their pseudo-Anosov monodromy (Theorem
8.1
).
This implies the invariance of the pseudo-Anosov stretch factor,
and the profinite almost rigidity (Theorem
1.1
)
follows from classical finiteness results in mapping class group theory.

1.1.1.
Profinite correspondence of Thurston-norm cones

The key ingredient of this step is inspired from a recent result of A. Jaikin-Zapirain,
which shows that topologically fibering (as a surface bundle over a circle)
is a profinite property among finitely generated
3
–manifold groups
[
Jai20
]
.
Taking a closer look,
we realize that Jaikin-Zapirain’s proof
already sets up certain partial correspondence between the fibered cones,
given a profinite isomorphism between
3
–manifold groups,
(Theorem
4.2
and Corollary
4.3
).
For
3
–manifolds satisfying Agol’s RFRS criterion for virtual fibering,
we promote the partial correspondence to a bijective correspondence between the Thurston-norm cones,
preserving fibered cones, (Theorem
5.1
and Corollary
5.3
).
This is done by applying the partial correspondence to some regular finite cover,
where pullback non-fibered classes all lie on faces of fibered cones.

To motivate the partial correspondence,
we revisit Jaikin–Zapirain’s result as follows:
Given an isomorphism
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{A}}\to\widehat{\pi_{B}}

between profinite completions of
3
–manifold groups,
the induced isomorphism
\Psi_{*}\colon\widehat{H_{A}}\to\widehat{H_{B}}

on the profinite free abelianization level
can be written as a linear combination

z_{1}\Phi_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}\Phi_{r}
, where
\Phi_{1},\cdots,\Phi_{r}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{A},H_{B})

are nonzero and where
z_{1},\cdots,z_{r}\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then, for generic
r
–tuples of integers
c=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{r})
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
,
the homomorphism
\Psi_{*}^{c}\colon H_{A}\to H_{B}
defined as
\Psi_{*}^{c}=c_{1}\Phi_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}\Phi_{r}

is nondegenerate, and it induces
a dual homomorphism
\Psi^{*}_{c}\colon H^{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{Z}})

on the first integral cohomology of the
3
–manifolds.
The argument of Jaikin–Zapirain actually implies that
the
\Psi^{*}_{c}
–preimage of any fibered cone for
M_{A}

is contained in a fibered cone for
M_{B}
.
(See Theorem
4.2
with a slightly more general statement,
where
\Psi
is allowed to be any epimorphism.)
This observation motivates us to introduce a
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodule

\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*};H_{A},H_{B})
of
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
which is contained in the
{\mathbb{Z}}
–span of
z_{1},\cdots,z_{r}
,
torsion-free of rank
r
, and uniquely defined.
We call
\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*};H_{A},H_{B})

the
matrix coefficient module
for
\Psi_{*}
with respect to
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
,
thinking of
\Psi_{*}
abstractly as living in

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{A},H_{B})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*};H_{A},H_{B})
,
and thinking of
\Psi_{*}^{c}
as the specialization of
\Psi_{*}

under some
{\mathbb{Z}}
–homomorphism
\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*};H_{A},H_{B})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We study more generally specializations
\Psi_{*}^{\varepsilon}\colon H_{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})\to H_{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{R}})

and their duals
\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}\colon H^{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})
,
where
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*};H_{A},H_{B}),{\mathbb{R}})
.
See Section
3
for the precise definition.
The matrix coefficient module plays a crucial role throughout this paper.
For example, the
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regularity condition on
\Psi

is equivalent to that
\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*};H_{A},H_{B})
has rank one.

1.1.2.
Profinite correspondence of the Thurston-norm unit ball

For any finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
,
any codimension–
0
Thurston-norm cone
\mathcal{C}
in
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

has a compact cross-section,
which is an affine polytope and whose projective structure depends only on
\mathcal{C}
.
If
\mathcal{C}
is a fibered cone, its linear dual cone in
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

can be characterized in terms of Fried’s homology directions.
This relates up fibered cones with the dynamics of
their associated unparametrized pseudo-Anosov flows on
M
,
(see Section
2.2
).
For any isomorphism
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{A}}\to\widehat{\pi_{B}}

between profinite completions of finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds

M_{A}
and
M_{B}
,
and any
\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}
–corresponding pair of
codimension–
0
Thurston-norm cones
\mathcal{C}_{A}
and
\mathcal{C}_{B}
,
the dual linear isomorphism
\Psi_{*}^{\varepsilon}
maps
the extreme rays dual to the codimension–
1
faces of
\mathcal{C}_{A}

bijectively onto those similar rays with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{B}
.
As one may observe from the above description,
those extreme rays already puts many constraints to the structure of
\Psi_{*}
,
since their correspondence cannot vary under small perturbation of
\varepsilon
.

To prove the
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regularity of
\Psi
,
we offer a criterion,
saying that
\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*};H_{A},H_{B})
has rank one
if
\Psi_{*}^{\varepsilon}
witnesses an extra correspondence between
some pair of rays, in the interior of the linear duals of
\mathcal{C}_{A}
and
\mathcal{C}_{B}
,
and if they stay invariant under small perturbation of
\varepsilon
.
In
[
Liu20
]
,
the author develops relevant techniques
for constructing such interior rays as desired.
In fact,
the rays can be constructed as the projection image of some virtual extreme rays,
associated to some finite covers of the
3
–manifolds and their fibered cones.
The construction invokes fillings of quasiconvex subgroups
in virtually special word hyperbolic groups
[
Wis12a
,
Wis12b
,
AGM
]
,
and its currently available version works for closed hyperbolic
3
–manifolds.
We derive the cusped case from the closed case,
using the profinite correspondence of cusp subgroups
due to Wilton and Zalesski
[
WltZ17a
,
WltZ17b
]
.

1.1.3.
Profinite invariance of Nielsen numbers

In
[
Liu19
]
, the author proves a finiteness result
about mapping classes, which is analogous to the profinite almost rigidity.
In particular, the invariance of the indexed periodic Nielsen numbers
is proved in that case,
for pairs of mapping classes
f_{A},f_{B}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S)
of an orientable connected closed surface
S
,
such that the induced profinite group outer automorphisms
[f_{A}],[f_{B}]\in\mathrm{Out}(\widehat{\pi_{1}(S)})

are conjugate.
When the mapping classes
f_{A}
and
f_{B}
are pseudo-Anosov,
one may actually see (with Theorem
1.2
) that their mapping torus groups
are regularly profinitely isomorphic.
With the profinite correspondence of fiber subgroups (Corollary
6.2
),
the strategy of this step is similar with
[
Liu19
]
:
We first establish the profinite invariance of
certain twisted Reidemeister torsions (Theorem
7.2
),
and then prove the invariance of the indexed periodic Nielsen numbers
using twisted Lefshetz numbers.

Some essential modification from the treatment in
[
Liu19
]
is required,
because we only have
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
–regularity
instead of regularity.
The extra ingredient comes from a recent work of J. Ueki,
which shows that profinitely isomorphic knot groups have
identical Alexander polynomials
[
Uek18
]
.
Ueki’s proof relies on an early result of D. Fried
about cyclic resultants of reciprocal polynomials
[
Frd88
]
.
It also relies on a special fact about knot groups,
that is, the elementary ideal of the Alexander module is principal in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t^{\pm 1}]
,
(generated by the Alexander polynomial).
Using some results from
[
Uek18
]
,
we prove the profinite invariance of twisted Reidemeister torsions
with respect to fibered classes
and
{\mathbb{Q}}
–linear representations with finite image.
Note that the
{\mathbb{Q}}
–irreducible characters of a finite group
only distinguish all the conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups.
(By contrast, the
{\mathbb{C}}
–irreducible characters
distinguish all the conjugacy classes of elements.)
For this reason,
we also must strengthen the argument about Nielsen numbers
in
[
Liu19
]
,
using the conjugacy separability of finitely generated
3
–manifold groups

[
HamWZ13
]

and representation theory of finite groups over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

2.1.
Profinite completions of groups

For any group
G
,
the
profinite completion

\widehat{G}
refers to the inverse limit
of (the inverse system of)
all the quotient groups
G/N
, where
N
ranges over all the finite-index normal subgroups of
G
.
The natural homomorphism
G\to\widehat{G}
is injective if and only if
G
is residually finite.

The
profinite topology
on
\widehat{G}
can be characterized as
the unique, coarsest topology,
such that every homomorphism of
\widehat{G}
to any discrete finite group is continuous.
Furnished with the profinite topology,
\widehat{G}
becomes a compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected
topological group.
A subgroup of
\widehat{G}
is open if and only if it is closed of finite index.
When
G
is finitely generated,
every finite-index subgroup of
\widehat{G}
is open,
which is a deep result due to N. Nikolov and D. Segal
[
NikS07
]
.
It follows that
the abstract group structure of
\widehat{G}
determines its profinite topology.
Moreover, in this case,
the isomorphism type of
\widehat{G}

is determined by all the finite quotients of
G
,
or more precisely, by the set of their isomorphism types without duplication,
no matter in the abstract or the topological sense,
(see
[
Rei18
, Theorem 2.2]
).

The
completion group algebra

\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket

is defined as the inverse limit of all the quotient group algebras

[({\mathbb{Z}}/l{\mathbb{Z}})(G/N)]
of the group algebra
[{\mathbb{Z}}G]
, where
l{\mathbb{Z}}

ranges over all the finite-index ideals of
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and where
N
ranges over all the finite-index normal subgroups of
G
.
There is also a natural profinite topology on
\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket
,
which turns
\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket
into
a compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected topological ring.
In general,
for any discrete left
\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket
–module
V
,
the
n
–th profinite group cohomology
H^{n}(\widehat{G};V)

with coefficients in
V
(
n\in{\mathbb{Z}})
is
a functorially defined, discrete
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
–module,
(which is abstractly a torsion abelian group);
for any profinite right
\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket
–module
W
,
the
n
–th profinite group homology
H_{n}(\widehat{G};W)
with coefficients in
W

(
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
) is
a functorially defined, profinite
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
–module,
(which is abstractly a profinite abelian group).
We refer the reader to
[
RibZ10
, Chapter 6]

for detailed account of profinite group cohomology and homology;
compare the parallel theory with abstract groups in
[
Bro82
, Chapter III]
.

A group
G
is said to be
cohomologically good
,
if the natural homomorphism

H^{n}(\widehat{G};V)\to H^{n}(G;V)
is an isomorphism of abelian groups,
for all
n
,
and for all finite left
[{\mathbb{Z}}G]
–modules
V
,
(which are automatically discrete left
\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket
–modules).
This concept is introduced by J. P. Serre in
[
Ser97
, Chapter I, §2, Exercise 1]
.
When
G
is of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
, for each
n
the group homology
H^{n}(G;V)
is finitely generated as an abelian group
if
V
is finitely generated as an abelian group,
so
H^{n}(G;V)
is finite if
V
is finite

[
Bro82
, Chapter VIII, Section 5, cf. Exercise 1]
.
In this case, being cohomologically good
is equivalent to the condition that

H_{n}(G;W)\to H_{n}(\widehat{G};W)

is an isomorphism
for all
n
and for all finite right
[{\mathbb{Z}}G]
–modules
W
,
(which are automatically profinite right
\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket
–modules).
In fact, this follows immediately from
the Pontrjagin duality between group homology and cohomology
[
Bro82
, Chapter VI, Proposition 7.1]
,
and its profinite group version
[
RibZ10
, Chapter 6, Proposition 6.3.6]
.
As pointed out in
[
Jai20
, Proposition 3.1]
,
a group of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
is cohomologically good
if and only if

H_{n}(G;\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket)

vanishes except
H_{0}(G;\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket)\cong\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
or in other words,

H_{*}(G;\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket)\cong H_{*}(\widehat{G};\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{G}\rrbracket)
.

In this paper, a
3
–manifold group
refers to any group
that is isomorphic to the fundamental group of a connected
3
–manifold
(possibly bounded, possibly non-compact).
Any finitely generated
3
–manifold group can be realized
with a connected compact
3
–manifold.
In particular,
finitely generated
3
–manifold groups
are all finitely presented and residually finite
[
AscFW15
, Section 3.2, (C.5) and (C.29)]
.
Every finitely generated
3
–manifold group can be decomposed
as a free product of finitely many freely indecomposable factors.
The infinite factors are all realizable with aspherical compact
3
–manifolds,
which provide finite CW complex models of their classifying spaces;
the finite factors can be realized with spherical geometric
3
–manifolds,
but their classifying spaces
(and more generally, the classifying space of any finite group)
also admit CW complex models with finitely many cells of each dimension,
by standard construction

[
Bro82
, Chapter I, Section 5, cf. Exercise 3]
.
It follows that finitely generated
3
–manifold groups
are all of type
\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}
.
As a consequence of the geometric decomposition
and the virtual fibering of (complete) finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds,
it is known that finitely generated
3
–manifold groups are all
cohomologically good
[
Rei15
, Theorem 7.3]
.

2.2.
Polytopes and cones

For any orientable connected compact
3
-manifold
M
,
the
Thurston norm
for
M
is a seminorm on the real linear space
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
introduced by W. P. Thurston in
[
Thu86
]
.
It is symmetric and takes integral values on the integral lattice
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
If
M
supports a complete hyperbolic structure of finite volume in its interior,
the Thurston norm is actually a norm.
In this paper, we denote the Thurston norm for
M
as

(2.1)

\|\cdot\|_{\mathtt{Th}}\colon H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})\to[0,+\infty).

We recall the definition (
2.1
)
from
[
Thu86
, Section 1]
as follows.
For any orientable compact surface
\Sigma
whose disconnected components are

\Sigma_{1},\cdots,\Sigma_{r}
, the
complexity
of
\Sigma
refers to
the sum of the nonnegative integers
\mathrm{max}\{-\chi(\Sigma_{i}),0\}
,
ranging over all the components
\Sigma_{i}
.
Choose an orientation of
M
.
For any cohomology class
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
the Thurston norm
\|\phi\|_{\mathtt{Th}}
is defined as
the minimum complexity of
\Sigma
,
where
\Sigma
ranges over all the properly embedded, oriented compact subsurfaces of
M
,
such that
\phi
is the Poincaré–Lefschetz dual of
[\Sigma]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Thurston shows

\|\phi+\psi\|_{\mathtt{Th}}\leq\|\phi\|_{\mathtt{Th}}+\|\psi\|_{\mathtt{Th}}

for all
\phi,\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
,
and

\|m\cdot\phi\|_{\mathtt{Th}}=|m|\cdot\|\phi\|_{\mathtt{Th}}

for all
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
It follows that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathtt{Th}}
can be extended linearly over
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})
,
and then continuously over
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
The result is the Thurston norm (
2.1
),
and it is obviously independent of the auxiliary orientation of
M
.

In the above definition,
one may use properly immersed subsurfaces instead of properly embedded ones,
and the resulting seminorm does not change.
This nontrivial fact is proved by D. Gabai in
[
Gab83
]
,
confirming a former conjecture of Thurston
[
Thu86
, Section 5]
.
In particular, the Thurston norm for any finite cover
is proportional to the covering degree

[
Gab83
, Corollary 6.13]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M
be any orientable connected compact
3
–manifold,
and
M^{\prime}\to M
be a finite cover.
Then the following formula holds
for any cohomology class
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
and its pullback
\phi^{\prime}\in H^{1}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
:

\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\mathtt{Th}}=[M^{\prime}:M]\cdot\|\phi\|_{\mathtt{Th}}.

The unit ball
\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M)
of the Thurston norm for
M

is a (possibly noncompact) codimension–
0
convex polyhedron in
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
It is the linear dual of a polytope in
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
which is symmetric about the origin and
whose vertices all lie in the integral lattice
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathtt{free}}
,

[
Thu86
, Section 2]
.
The radial rays in
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

through any closed face of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M)

form a convex polyhedral cone, on which the Thurston norm is linear.
Such cones are called
Thurston-norm cones
for
M
.
Therefore,
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
is the union of the codimension–
0
Thurston-norm cones.
For any finite cover
M^{\prime}\to M
,
the intersection of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M^{\prime})

with the image of induced embedding
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
is
the image of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M)
dilated by the factor
[M^{\prime}:M]
.
This is the geometric interpretation of Theorem
2.1
.

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

In real affine linear spaces, we speak of convexity
and the point-set topology;
(a
real affine linear space

is a homogeneous space which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

furnished with its affine linear transformations).
A (closed)
convex polyhedron
in a real affine linear space
refers to a closed convex subset,
such that every point on the boundary lies in
only finitely many maximal convex subsets of the boundary.
A compact convex polyhedron is a
polytope
.
We speak of
closed
or
open faces

of various dimension or codimension in convex polyhedra, as usual.
For clarity, we avoid saying ‘open convex polyhedra’,
which could be confusing in the positive codimension case.
Instead, we speak of the interior of convex polyhedra.

(2)

In real linear spaces,
a (radial)
cone
refers generally to a subset that is invariant under positive rescaling.
However, we only encounter convex polyhedral cones in this paper.

A cohomology class
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is said to be
fibered

if
\phi
is represented by the free homotopy class of
a continuous map
M\to{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
which is a fiber-bundle projection
onto the standard circle.
The fiber is connected precisely when
\phi
is primitive.
Thurston shows that every fibered class
\phi
lies in
the interior of a unique codimension–
0
Thurston-norm cone

\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M,\phi)
, and moreover,
every cohomology class
\psi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

in the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M,\phi)

is a fibered class; see
[
Thu86
, Theorem 5]
.
Any
\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M,\phi)
arising this way
is called a
fibered cone
for
M
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
M
be an orientable connected compact
3
–manifold.
Suppose that
M
has empty or tori boundary, and
admits a complete Riemannian metric of nonpositive sectional curvature in the interior.
Then, there exists a finite regular cover
M^{\prime}\to M
,
such that for every nontrivial cohomology class
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
,
the pull-back cohomology class
\phi^{\prime}\in H^{1}(M^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})

lies in the closure of a fibered cone for
M^{\prime}
.

Theorem
2.3
follows from
Agol’s RFRS criterion for virtual fibering
[
Ago08
]
,
and the virtual specialization of nonpositively curved
3
–manifold groups

[
Ago13
,
Liu13
,
PrzW14
,
PrzW18
]
.
Note that
\phi^{\prime}
must lie on the boundary of a fibered cone if
\phi
is not fibered,
and such
\phi^{\prime}
is sometimes called
quasi-fibered
in the literature.
See
[
AscFW15
, Sections 4.7, 4.8, and 5.4.3]
.

The cones in
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
dual to the fibered cones
are intimately related to flow structures on
M
transverse to the surface fibers.
We describe the picture below,
focusing on the case of fibered closed hyperbolic
3
–manifolds,
and following Fried’s expository article
[
FatLP12
, Exposé 14]
.

Suppose that
M
is an orientable closed
3
–manifold which admits a hyperbolic metric.
If
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive fibered class,
then
M
can be identified homeomorphically with the mapping torus
of a pseudo-Anosov automorphism, such that
\phi
is identified with
the distinguished cohomology class dual to the surface, (see Remark
2.4
).
D. Fried gives a characterization of
the linear dual in
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
of the fibered cone
\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M,\phi)
,
which we denote as

\mathcal{C}^{\mathtt{Fr}}(M,\phi)=\left\{x\in H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})\colon\psi(x)\geq 0\mbox{ for all }\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M,\phi)\right\}.

Considering all the homology classes represented by the forward periodic trajectories
of the suspension flow,
Fried shows that the radial rays in
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
that pass through those homology classes
form a dense subset of
\mathcal{C}^{\mathtt{Fr}}(M,\phi)
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathtt{Fr}}(M,\phi)

is the smallest convex polyhedral cone in
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

that contains those homology classes,
and it has codimension zero in
H_{1}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
.
Up to isotopy,
the fibered cone
\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M,\phi)
actually determines
the unparametrized flow,
by which we mean
the oriented
1
–foliation of
M
given by the flow trajectories.
Different primitive cohomology classes in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M,\phi)
are
just the Poincaré dual to different cross-sections of the unparametrized flow.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

For any orientable connected compact surface
S
, possibly with boundary,
and for any orientation-preserving self-homeomorphism

f\colon S\to S
,
the
mapping torus
of
(S,f)
refers to the
3
–manifold

M_{f}={S\times{\mathbb{R}}}/\sim
,
such that
{(x,r+1)\sim(f(x),r)}
for all
(x,r)\in S\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
The
suspension flow
on
M_{f}
refers the continuous family of homeomorphisms

\theta_{t}\colon M_{f}\to M_{f}
,
parametrized by
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and determined by
(x,r)\mapsto(x,r+t)
. The distinuished cohomology class of
M_{f}
refers to
the cohomology class
\phi_{f}\in H^{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{Z}})

represented by the distinguished projection
M_{f}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
,
where
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
is identified with
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

and the projection is determined by
(x,r)\mapsto r
.

(2)

Moreover, when
S
is closed,

f
is said to be a
pseudo-Anosov
automorphism
if there exist a constant
\lambda>1
and a pair of measured foliations

(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}},\mu^{\mathtt{s}})
and
(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\mu^{\mathtt{u}})

of
S
,
such that

f\cdot(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\mu^{\mathtt{u}})=(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\lambda\mu^{\mathtt{u}})

and

f\cdot(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}},\mu^{\mathtt{s}})=(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}},\lambda^{-1}\mu^{\mathtt{s}})
.
The constant
\lambda
is called the
stretch factor
of
the pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f
.
The measured foliations
(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}},\mu^{\mathtt{s}})
and
(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\mu^{\mathtt{u}})

are called the
stable
and the
unstable
measured foliations of
f
, respectively.
The unmeasured invariant foliations are transverse to each other,
except at finitely many common singular points.
At each singular point,
both of the invariant foliations have a
k
–prong singularity,
for some positive integer
k\geq 3
.

2.3.
Twisted invariants

Let
M
be a connected compact
3
–manifold.
Denote by
\pi_{1}(M)
the fundamental group of
M

with respect to an implicitly fixed basepoint.
Let
R
be a (commutative) Noetherian unique factorization domain.
Denote by
R[t^{\pm 1}]
the ring of Laurent polynomials over
R
in an indeterminate
t
.
We recall the following twisted invariants from
Friedl and Vidussi’s survey
[
FrlV11
]
.

Let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{GL}(k,R)

be a linear representation of
\pi_{1}(M)

on the free
R
–module
R^{k}
of finite rank
k
.
Let
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
be a cohomology class,
regarded meanwhile as a homomorphism
\phi\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.
For any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
the
n
–th
twisted homology

H_{n}^{\rho,\phi}(M;R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k})

refers to the twisted (singular) homology of
M
with respect to the representation

t^{\phi}\rho\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{GL}(k,R[t^{\pm 1}])
,
such that
g\mapsto t^{\phi(g)}\cdot\rho(g)
.
This is a finitely generated
R[t^{\pm 1}]
–module,
vanishing unless
n=0,1,2,3
.
In this paper,
we sometimes simplify the notation as

H^{\rho,\phi}_{n}
, if
M
and
R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k}
are apparent from the context.

The
n
–th
twisted Alexander polynomial

\Delta_{n}^{\rho,\phi}(M;R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k})
of
M
with respect to
(\rho,\phi)
,
or simply
\Delta_{n}^{\rho,\phi}(t)
,
is defined as the order of the finitely generated
R[t^{\pm 1}]
–module

H_{n}^{\rho,\phi}(M;R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k})
,
(see Remark
2.5
).
We treat
\Delta_{n}^{\rho,\phi}(t)
as a Laurent polynomial in
t
over
R
,
uniquely defined up to monomial factors and units of
R
.

If the rank of
H_{n}^{\rho,\phi}(M;R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k})

over
R[t^{\pm 1}]
is zero for all
n
,
the
twisted Reidemeister torsion

\tau^{\rho,\phi}(M;R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k})

of
M
with respect to
(\rho,\phi)
, or simply
\tau^{\rho,\phi}(t)
,
can be characterized as the alternating product

(2.2)

\tau^{\rho,\phi}(t)\doteq\frac{\prod_{n\mbox{ odd}}\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{n}(t)}{\prod_{n\mbox{ even}}\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{n}(t)},

where the dotted equality symbol
means being equal up to monomial factors with nonzero
R
–fractional coefficients,
in the field of rational functions in
t
over
R
,
(see Remark
2.5
).
Note that the products in (
2.2
)
essentially only involves the factors with
n=0,1,2,3
.
If some
H_{n}^{\rho,\phi}(M;R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k})
has
R[t^{\pm 1}]
–rank nonzero,
it is customary to define
\tau^{\rho,\phi}\left(M;R[t^{\pm 1}]^{k}\right)

to be zero.

Remark 2.5
.

For any (commutative) domain
S
and and any finitely generated
S
–module
V
,
the
rank
of
V
refers to the dimension of
V\otimes_{S}\mathrm{Frac}(S)

over the field of fractions
\mathrm{Frac}(S)
.
When
S
is a Noetherian unique factorization domain,
the
order
of
V
refers to
the greatest common divisor of all the largest-size minors
in a finite-presentation matrix of
V
,
(that is, a matrix over
S

whose cokernel is isomorphic to
V
).
The order of
V
is well-defined up to a unit of
S
,
and it depends only on the isomorphism type of
V
.
Therefore, the order of
V
equals zero
if and only if the rank of
V
is nonzero.
See
[
Tur01
, Chapter I, Section 4]
.

We collect the following facts
from
[
FrlV11
]
,
see Section 3.3.1, Proposition 2 (1), (4), (7) and Section 3.3.4, Propositions 4 and 5
therein.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
M
be an orientable connected compact
3
–manifold with empty or tori boundary.
Let
R
be a Noetherian UFD and
k
be a natural number.
Suppose that
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a nontrivial cohomology class
and
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{GL}(k,R)
is a representation.
If
\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{1}(t)\neq 0
,
then
\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{n}(t)\neq 0
for all
n
.
In this case, the following statements all hold true.

(1)

\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{n}(t)\doteq 1
for all
n
other than
0,1,2
.

(2)

If
\partial M\neq\emptyset
,
then

\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{0}(t)\doteq\Delta^{\bar{\rho},\phi}_{0}(t^{-1})
, and

\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{1}(t)\doteq\Delta^{\bar{\rho},\phi}_{1}(t^{-1})
, and

\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{2}(t)\doteq 1
.

(3)

If
\partial M=\emptyset
, then

\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{0}(t)\doteq\Delta^{\bar{\rho},\phi}_{2}(t^{-1})
, and

\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{1}(t)\doteq\Delta^{\bar{\rho},\phi}_{1}(t^{-1})
, and

\Delta^{\rho,\phi}_{2}(t)\doteq\Delta^{\bar{\rho},\phi}_{0}(t^{-1})
.

Here,
\bar{\rho}
stands for the representation
\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{GL}(k,R)
, such that

\bar{\rho}(g)
is the transpose of
\rho(g)^{-1}
for all
g\in\pi_{1}(M)
;
the dotted equality symbol means an equality up to monomial factors and units of
R
.

With respect to a fibered class, it is possible to interprete
the twisted Reidemeister torsion (
2.2
)
through the dynamics of a fiber-transverse flow.
Again we describe in the case of pseudo-Anosov mapping tori.

Suppose that
M
is an orientable closed
3
–manifold which admits a hyperbolic metric.
If
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a primitive fibered class,
we adopt the notations of Remark
2.4

and identify
(M,\phi)
with
(M_{f},\phi_{f})
,
where
f\colon S\to S
is
a pseudo-Anosov automorphism of a connected closed orientable surface.
In this setting,
periodic orbits of

correspond to periodic trajectories of the suspension flow,
and they have well-defined periodic indices.

For any natural number

,
an
m
–periodic point of

refers to a fixed point

of

,
and it gives rise to an

–periodic trajectory of the suspension flow on

,
namely, the loop

.
The free-homotopy class of an

–periodic trajectory
depends only on the

–iteration orbit of an

–periodic point.
We denote by
\mathrm{Orb}_{m}(f)
the set of

–periodic orbits of

,
and for any
m
–periodic orbit
\mathbf{O}\in\mathrm{Orb}_{m}(f)
,
denote by
\ell_{m}(f;\mathbf{O})

–periodic trajectory
determined by
\mathbf{O}
.
Since the homotopy set
[{\mathbb{R}}/m{\mathbb{Z}},M_{f}]
is naturally identified

,
denoted as
\mathrm{Orb}(\pi_{1}(M_{f}))

we write

(2.3)

\ell_{m}(f;\mathbf{O})\in\mathrm{Orb}(\pi_{1}(M_{f})),

referring to it as an (essential)
m
–periodic trajectory class
.

For any
m
–periodic point
p\in S
of

,
the
m
–periodic index of
f
at

refers to the fixed point index of

at

.
Explicitly, when

–prong singularity of

–periodic index of

at

–periodic index of

at

-1
or

,
according to a similar rule as if

.
Note that
the
m
–periodic index of

–iteration orbit of

–periodic points.
For any
m
–periodic orbit

,
we define the
m
–periodic index
of

at

,
and denote it as

\mathrm{ind}_{m}(f;\mathbf{O})\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}.

For any representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(M_{f})\to\mathrm{GL}(k,R)

the character

.
So we treat

,
we define the

–th
twisted Lefschetz number
with respect to

L_{m}(f;\chi_{\rho})=\sum_{\mathbf{O}\in\mathrm{Orb}_{m}(f)}\chi_{\rho}\left(\ell_{m}(f;\mathbf{O})\right)\cdot\mathrm{ind}_{m}(f;\mathbf{O}).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let

.

3.
Matrix coefficients of profinite linear transformations

In this section, we introduce matrix coefficient modules and their specializations.
For any finitely generated free
{\mathbb{Z}}
–module
H
of finite rank,
denote by
H^{*}
the dual
{\mathbb{Z}}
–module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
We identify the abelian profinite group completion

of

\mathrm{Hom}(\widehat{H_{A}},\widehat{H_{B}})

naturally as the
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
–module

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(H_{A},H_{B})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}

Definition 3.1
.

Let
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
be a pair of finitely generated free
{\mathbb{Z}}
–modules.
Let
\Phi\colon\widehat{H_{A}}\to\widehat{H_{B}}

We define the

for

to be the smallest
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodule

of
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
such that
\Phi(H_{A})
lies in the submodule
H_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L

of
\widehat{H_{B}}=H_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
We denote the matrix coefficient module as

\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})\subset\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}},

or simply
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)
when there is no danger of confusion.
We denote by

\Phi^{\mathrm{MC}}\colon H_{A}\to H_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)

the homomorphism uniquely determined by the restriction of
\Phi
to
H_{A}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
be a pair of finitely generated free
{\mathbb{Z}}
–modules.
Let
\Phi\colon\widehat{H_{A}}\to\widehat{H_{B}}
be a continuous homomorphism.

(1)

With respect to any bases
u_{1},\cdots,u_{a}
of
H_{A}
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{b}
of
H_{B}
,
represent
\Phi
uniquely as a matrix
(\varphi_{ij})_{b\times a}
over
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Then
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
–linearly spanned by all the entries

\varphi_{ij}
in
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
In particular,

\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})

is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodule of
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
of finite rank.

(2)

If
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B}))=1
,
then for each generator
z\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
of
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
,
there is a unique homomorphism
F_{z}\colon H_{A}\to H_{B}
,
such that
\Phi=z\widehat{F_{z}}
holds in

\mathrm{Hom}(\widehat{H_{A}},\widehat{H_{B}})
.
Hence, the other generator
-z
gives rise to
F_{-z}=-F_{z}
.

(3)

For any
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodules
L_{A}
of
H_{A}
and
L_{B}
of
H_{B}
,
such that
\Phi(\widehat{L_{A}})
is contained in
\widehat{L_{B}}
,
restrict
\Phi
to obtain a continuous homomorphism
\widehat{L_{A}}\to\widehat{L_{B}}
,
still denoted as
\Phi
.
Then
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;L_{A},L_{B})
is commensurable with
a
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodule of
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
in
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
In particular,

\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;L_{A},L_{B}))\leq\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})).

Moreover, if
L_{A}
and
L_{B}
both have finite index,
then
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;L_{A},L_{B})
is commensurable with
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
in
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
and the equality is achieved.

Proof.

For any
u\in H_{A}
and
v^{*}\in H^{*}_{B}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{B},{\mathbb{Z}})
,
we obtain an element
\widehat{v^{*}}(\Phi(u))
in
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)
,
where
\widehat{v^{*}}\colon\widehat{H_{B}}\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is the completion of
v^{*}
.
Moreover, all such elements form a
{\mathbb{Z}}
–submodule of
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
so it is by definition
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
.
For any given basis
u_{1},\cdots,u_{a}
of
H_{A}
,
and any basis
v^{*}_{1},\cdots,v^{*}_{b}
of
H_{B}^{*}

dual to a given basis of
H_{B}
,
the elements
u_{i}\otimes v_{j}^{*}
form a basis
of
H_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{B}^{*}
,
so the matrix entries
\varphi_{ij}=\widehat{v^{*}_{j}}(\Phi(u_{i}))

span
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
This proves the assertion (1).

If the rank of
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
equals
1
,
every entry
\varphi_{ij}
is a unique
{\mathbb{Z}}
–multiple

of
any fixed generator
z\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}

of
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi;H_{A},H_{B})
. So the matrix of

takes the form

where

is the matrix with entries

.
This proves the assertion (2).

It remains to prove the assertion (3). In fact, it suffices to prove the finite-index case,
then the general case follows from the assertion (1),
by considering the smallest direct-summand modules

of
H_{A}
that contains
L_{A}
,
and
L^{\prime}_{B}
of
H_{B}
that contains
L_{B}
.
Suppose below that
L_{A}
and
L_{B}
have finite index in
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
, respectively.
Take bases
u_{1},\cdots,u_{a}
of
H_{A}
, and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{b}
of
H_{B}
,
and represent
\Phi\colon\widehat{H_{A}}\to\widehat{H_{B}}

as a
b\times a
–matrix
T
over
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
namely,
(\Phi(u_{1}),\cdots,\Phi(u_{a}))=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{b})T
.
Similarly, take bases
u^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,u^{\prime}_{a}
of
L_{A}
, and
v^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,v^{\prime}_{b}
of

,
and represent
\Phi\colon\widehat{L_{A}}\to\widehat{L_{B}}
as a matrix

.
Let

and

be the square matrices over

such that

We obtain a matrix equation

.
Then

of

Applying the assertion (1), we see that

is contained in

.
Similarly,

4.
Detecting fibered cones with a profinite epimorphism

In this section, we revisit Jaikin-Zapirain’s work on profinite invariance of fiberedness,
with some slight generalization. We reformulate the result in terms of matrix coefficient modules,
(see Theorem
4.2
and Remark
4.4
).
We introduce the following general setting,
which is frequently invoked in subsequent sections.

Convention 4.1
.

By declaring
(M_{A},M_{B},\Psi)
as a
profinite morphism setting of a 3-manifold pair
,
we agree to adopt the following hypotheses and notations.

(1)

Suppose that
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
are
a pair of orientable connected compact
3
–manifolds. Suppose that
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{A}}\to\widehat{\pi_{B}}

is a continuous homomorphism
between the profinite completions of the fundamental groups

\pi_{A}=\pi_{1}(M_{A})
for
M_{A}
, and similarly
\pi_{B}
for
M_{B}
,
with respect to implicitly fixed basepoints.

(2)

Denote by
\Psi_{*}\colon\widehat{H_{A}}\to\widehat{H_{B}}
the induced continuous homomorphism
between the profinite completions of the first integral homology modulo torsion

H_{A}=H_{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathtt{free}}
, and similarly
H_{B}
for
M_{B}
.
Denote by
\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*})
the matrix coefficient module for
\Psi_{*}

with respect to
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
, (see Definition
3.1
).

(3)

For any homomorphism
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*}),{\mathbb{R}})
,
denote by

\Psi_{*}^{\varepsilon}\colon H_{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})\to H_{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{R}})

the
\varepsilon
–specialization of
\Psi_{*}
,
naturally identifying

H_{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})\cong H_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
, and similarly for
M_{B}
;
and denote by

\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}\colon H^{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})

the dual
\varepsilon
–specialization of
\Psi_{*}
,
naturally identifying
H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{A},{\mathbb{R}})
for
M_{A}
,
and similarly for
M_{B}
,
(see Definition
3.3
).

For most of the time, we only discuss profinite isomorphism settings,
and we often put extra assumptions on the
3
–manifolds,
such as nonpositively curved, or hyperbolic.
This section is an exception, where we consider a profinite epimorphism setting.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(M_{A},M_{B},\Psi)
be a profinite morphism setting of a
3
–manifold pair
(Convention
4.1
).
Suppose that
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{A}}\to\widehat{\pi_{B}}
is an epimorphism.
Suppose
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*}),{\mathbb{R}})
.

Then,
with respect to
\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}\colon H^{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})
,
the preimage of any fibered cone for
M_{A}

is either empty or contained in a unique fibered cone for
M_{B}
.
In other words,
a cohomology class
\phi\in H^{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{R}})
lies in a fibered cone for
M_{B}

if
\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}(\phi)
lies in a fibered cone for
M_{A}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.2
,
if
b_{1}(M_{B})
equals
b_{1}(M_{A})
,
and if
M_{A}
fibers over a circle, then
M_{B}
also fibers over a circle.

Remark 4.4
.

(1)

Any epimorphism between
profinite completions of finitely generated groups
is continuous, thanks to Nikolov–Segal
[
NikS07
]
,
(see Section
2.1
).
Our assumption in Theorem
4.2
is partly motivated
by some well-known facts on knot group epimorphisms.
Given an epimorphism between knot groups,
the target knot is fibered if the source knot is fibered.
This is simply because
knot groups all have infinite cyclic abelianization,
and fibered knot groups are precisely those with finitely generated commutator subgroups.
See also
[
KitSW05
,
SilW06
]
.

(2)

Corollary
4.3
implies
[
Jai20
, Corollary 1.2]
as a special case.
Strictly speaking,
the use of rank in
[
Jai20
, Section 2]
relies on a nontrivial fact, that is,
the commutative pro–
p
group ring

\llbracket\mathbb{F}_{p}t^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\rrbracket
is a domain,
(see
[
CrbKL88
, Theorem B]
).
For any prime
p
,
the notations
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

here stand for the finite field of
p
elements
and the additive group of
p
–adic integers, respectively.
For proving Theorem
4.2
,
we need to work with
\llbracket\mathbb{F}t^{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\rrbracket
,
essentially because we need
\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*})
to be defined in
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
in order for some subsequent application,
(especially Theorem
7.2
).
On the other hand, the ground finite field
\mathbb{F}
does not quite matter.
Not to worry about zero divisors in
\llbracket\mathbb{F}t^{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\rrbracket
,
we only speak of rank for finitely generated modules over
the dense subdomain
[\mathbb{F}t^{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}}]
,
(namely, the group algebra over
\mathbb{F}

of the abstract torsion-free abelian group
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
).
This technical difference from
[
Jai20
]
is reflected
on Proposition
4.6
including its proof.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
We invoke the following Theorem
4.5
,
which translates the topological fibering condition
into an algebraic one, in terms of twisted homology over finite fields.
The ‘only if’ direction is due to Friedl and Kim
[
FrlK06
, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.2]
,
and the ‘if’ direction is due to Friedl and Vidussi
[
FrlV13
]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
M
be an orientable connected compact
3
–manifold and
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field.
A nonzero cohomology class
\phi\in H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is fibered if and only if
for every finite dimensional linear representation

\rho\colon\pi\to\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{F})
over
\mathbb{F}
,

H^{\rho,\phi}_{1}(M;[\mathbb{F}t^{{\mathbb{Z}}}]^{k})
has finite dimension over
\mathbb{F}
.

The next proposition prepares all the algebraic gadgets needed for proving Theorem
4.2
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

\widehat{\psi}^{\prime}

Let

\mathbb{F}

5.
Profinite correspondence for Thurston-norm cones

In this section, we show that profinitely isomorphic pairs
of nonpositively curved
3
–manifolds with empty or tori boundary
have bijectively corresponding Thurston-norm cones and fibered cones
(Theorem
5.1
and Corollary
5.3

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M_{A},M_{B},\Psi)
be a profinite morphism setting of a
3
–manifold pair (Convention
4.1
).
Suppose that
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{A}}\to\widehat{\pi_{B}}
is an isomorphism.
Suppose that
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
are both irreducible with empty or tori boundary,
and admit complete metrics of nonpositive curvature in the interior.
Suppose
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{MC}(\Psi_{*}),{\mathbb{R}})
.

If
\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}\colon H^{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})
is nondegenerate,
then
\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}
witnesses
a dimension-preserving bijective correspondence
between the Thurston-norm cones for
M_{A}
and those for
M_{B}
.
Namely,
under the linear isomorphism
\Psi^{*}_{\varepsilon}
,
every Thurston-norm cone for
M_{B}

projects onto a distinct and unique Thurston-norm cone for
M_{A}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
M
be an orientable connected compact
3
–manifold
which is irreducible with empty or tori boundary.
Then,

M
admits a complete (Riemannian) metric of nonpositive sectional curvature
in the interior
if and only if
\pi_{1}(M)
virtually embeds into a right-angled Artin group,
(see
[
AscFW15
, Corollary 4.3.3 and Theorem 4.7.4]
).
This condition is satisfied
if
M
contains a hyperbolic piece in its geometric decomposition,
or if
M
is a graph manifold with nonempty tori boundary,

denotes the real affine

The projective-geometric claim can be proved

6.2.
The closed case

The next two lemmas allow us to construct some
trackable interior point in a projective dual polytope.
In fact, essentially the same construction has been sketched in

[
Liu20
, Problem 1.5 and Outline of the Solution]
,
as an illustration of the techniques thereof.
What we actually need for applying Lemma

is Lemma

.
Lemma
6.6

is separated out from its proof for an expository purpose.

When
M
is closed hyperbolic with a primitive fibered class
\phi
,
we can identify
M
with the mapping-torus
M_{f}
of a pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f\colon S\to S
,
and
\phi
with the distinguished cohomology class
\phi_{f}
, (see Remark

).
Then,
Fried’s dual characterization of the fibered cone
\mathcal{C}_{f}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{Th}}(M_{f},\phi_{f})

is equivalent to saying that
the
1
–subspaces of

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
f\colon S\to S
be a pseudo-Anosov automorphism of an orientable connected closed surface.
Then,
there exist
a finite collection
\mathcal{H}

of infinite-index quasi-convex subgroups of
\pi_{1}(M_{f})
,
such that the following property is satisfied:

If
\gamma
is a periodic trajectory of the suspension flow,
such that the
1
–subspace of
H_{1}(M_{f};{\mathbb{R}})
spanned by its homology class
[\gamma]

is a boundary point of the polytope
\mathcal{D}(M_{f},\mathcal{C}_{f})
,
then some finite cyclic cover of
\gamma
, as a free-homotopy loop,
represents a conjugacy class in
\pi_{1}(M_{f})

which has nonempty intersection with some
H\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

The asserted subgroups
H_{i}
can be constructed
using the techniques in
[
Liu20
, Sections 5 and 6]
.
Those techniques are developed based on Fried’s original ideas in
[
Frd82a
,
Frd82b
]
,
see the notes in
[
Liu20
, Section 5.4]
.
To simply put,
the subgroups
H_{i}
all correspond to some subgraphs of the transition graph
with respect to any fixed Markov partition,
and the dynamical cycles in those subgraphs
all represent periodic trajectories in
M_{f}

whose homology classes are projectively carried by
\partial\mathcal{D}(M_{f},\mathcal{C}_{f})
. Below we present the construction in more detail,
following
[
Liu20
, Sections 5 and 6]
.

Take a Markov partition
\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\cdots,R_{k}\}
of
S
with respect to
the pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f
.
To be precise, each
R_{i}
is a
birectangle
, namely,
it is a subset of
S
, parametrized as
[0,1]\times[0,1]
,
such that any vertical segment
\{x\}\times[0,1]
is contained in the union of
finitely many leaves or singular points of the stable invariant foliation
\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}}
,
and such that any horizontal segment
[0,1]\times\{y\}

is compatible with the unstable invariant foliation
\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}}
similarly.
As one may check,
singular points only occur on boundary of the birectangles,
and the stable or unstable leaves
containing any interior point of a birectangle must be unique.
Moreover,
the birectangles
R_{i}
cover
S
altogether without mutual intersection in their interiors,
and every birectangle
R_{i}
is the vertical juxtaposition of finitely many horizontal blocks,
which are mapped under
f
onto vertical blocks in mutually distinct birectangles in
\mathcal{R}
.
(See
[
Liu20
, Section 5.1]
.)

The
transition graph

T_{f,\mathcal{R}}

is a directed graph, modeled as a CW
1
–complex with oriented
1
–cells,
such that the vertice

of
T_{f,\mathcal{R}}

corresponds bijectively to the birectangle
R_{i}
,
and the edges
e_{ij}
correspond bijectively
to the horizontal blocks
R_{ij}=R_{i}\cap f^{-1}(R_{j})
,
such that
\mathrm{int}(R_{i})\cap f^{-1}(\mathrm{int}(R_{j}))\neq\emptyset
.
The transition graph
T_{f,\mathcal{R}}
is homotopy equivalent
to an
abstract flow-box complex

X_{f,\mathcal{R}}
,
which is obtained from the disjoint union of the birectangles
R_{i}

by attaching boxes
R_{ij}\times[0,1]
,
such that
R_{ij}\times\{0\}
is attached to
R_{i}
by the inclusion
R_{ij}\to R_{i}
,
and
R_{ij}\times\{1\}
is attached to
R_{j}
by
f\colon R_{ij}\to R_{j}
.
The homotopy equivalence is realized by the natural collapsing map

X_{f,\mathcal{R}}\to T_{f,\mathcal{R}}
.
Meanwhile, there is a natural map
q_{f,\mathcal{R}}\colon X_{f,\mathcal{R}}\to M_{f}
,
which puts the building parts of

into

in the obvious way. (See
[
Liu20
, Sections 5.2 and 5.3]
.)

For every primitive periodic trajectory
\gamma
in
M_{f}
,
the preimage
q_{f,\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(\gamma)
in
X_{f,\mathcal{R}}

is a union of mutually disjoint primitive abstract periodic trajectories,
(meaning that its components are all cyclical concatenations of
mutually distinct, directly consecutive segments of the form

),

[
Liu20
, Lemma 5.2]
. Moreover,
the collapsing map witnesses a bijective correspondence between
the primitive abstract periodic trajectories in

and the primitive dynamical cycles in

,

[
Liu20
, Lemma 5.7]
.
Here, a

in a directed graph
refers to a directly immersed finite cyclic directed graph,
and it is

In particular, we see that any primitive periodic trajectory in

admits a finite-cyclic cover which can be represented as
a dynamical cycle in
T_{f,\mathcal{R}}
(among finitely many choices).

For every closed face
E
of
\mathcal{D}(M_{f},\mathcal{C}_{f})
,
there exists a directed subgraph of

,
called the

of

The support graph is characterized by the following property:
A dynamical cycle of

is contained in

if and only if

M_{f}

whose projective homology class in
\mathcal{D}(M_{f},\mathcal{C}_{f})
lies in

.

In fact,
there exists a canonical polytope

,
whose points are the probability measures on the edge set of

balanced at the vertices, in the sense that the total incoming measure
at any vertex equals the total outgoing measure;
there exists a canonical, surjective, linear map

h_{f,\mathcal{R}}\colon\mathcal{P}_{f,\mathcal{R}}\to\mathcal{D}(M_{f},\mathcal{C}_{f})
,
which takes any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{f,\mathcal{R}}

{\mathbb{R}}
–cellular cycle

\sum_{ij}\mu(\{e_{ij}\})\,e_{ij}

(see
[
Liu20
, Definition 5.9 and (5.4)]

The support of any measure

gives rise to a nonwandering subgraph of

and this construction sets up a canonical bijective correspondence between
the faces of
\mathcal{P}_{f,\mathcal{R}}

and the nonwandering subgraphs of

,

[
Liu20
, Lemma 5.10]
.
The support graph

is precisely the nonwandering subgraph of

that corresponds to the closed face

,
(see
[
Liu20
, Definition 6.6]
).
The characterizing property of

follows from the above description,
since any dynamical cycle of

determines a measure in
\mathcal{P}_{f,\mathcal{R}}
,
which is the normalization of the edge-counting measure.

For every codimension–
1
closed face

of
\mathcal{D}(M_{f},\mathcal{C}_{f})

and for every component

,
namely, the union of all

is a vertex of

and all

We obtain a subgroup

of

as the

–image of the restricted map

(choosing a representative among all conjugates).
The construction gives rise to

The above characterizing property of support subgraphs
ensures that

satisfies the asserted property in Lemma

Note that

is empty if

It remains to argue that any

is quasi-convex of infinite-index.
Suppose

for some codimension–

Denote by

,
such that

.
Since the dynamical cycles of the nonwandering subgraph

(see
[

Moreover, since

there is some nonzero cohomology class

6.3.
The cusped case

We derive the cusped case of Theorem

from the closed case (Lemma

)
as follows.

Lemma 6.9
.

Adopt the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
.
If the homomorphism
H_{1}(\partial M_{A};{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{Q}})

induced by the boundary inclusion has nontrivial cokernel,
then the conclusion of Theorem
6.1
holds true.

Proof.

We enumerate the boundary tori of
M_{A}
as
T_{A,i}
, indexing with a finite set
I
,
and for each
T_{A,i}
, fix a conjugate
P_{A,i}\cong\pi_{1}(T_{A,i})

of the associated peripheral subgroup in
\pi_{1}(M_{A})
.
Under the assumption of Lemma
6.9
,

\Psi
determines a bijective correspondence
between the boundary tori of
M_{A}
and those of
M_{B}
.
In fact, this follows from Wilton and Zalesski
[
WltZ17b
, Proposition 3.1]
,
and in particular,
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
have the same number of boundary tori.
To be precise, we can choose peripheral conjugates

P_{B,i}\cong\pi_{1}(T_{B,i})
in
\pi_{1}(M_{B})
, similarly as above,
and the correspondence of the boundary tori says that upon suitable re-indexing,

\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{A}}\to\widehat{\pi_{B}}

maps the closure of each
P_{A,i}

isomorphically onto a conjugate of the closure of
P_{B,i}
,
for all
i\in I
.
Note that any finitely generated subgroup
is separable in a hyperbolic
3
–manifold group,
so its closure in the profinite completion
is naturally isomorphic to its own profinite completion,
(see
[
AscFW15
, Corollary 4.2.3]
).

We enumerate the
1
–dimensional cone carriers for
M_{A}

as
l_{A,j}
, indexing with a finite set
J
,
and there are corresponding cone carriers
l_{B,j}
for
M_{B}
,
(Corollary
5.4
).
Denote by
K_{A,j}=K(l_{A,j})
the lattice kernels in
H_{A}
,
and similarly
K_{B,j}
in
H_{B}
, (see (

)).
By Corollary

,
the closure of each
K_{A,j}
projects isomorphically onto
the closure of
K_{B,j}

under
\Psi_{*}\colon\widehat{H_{A}}\to\widehat{H_{B}}
.
Note that

H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{R}})=\sum_{j\in J}l_{A,j}
and

since the Thurston-norm unit balls for

and
M_{B}
are both polytopes,
and hence,

\cap_{j\in J}K_{A,j}=\{0\}
and
\cap_{j\in J}K_{B,j}=\{0\}
.

We observe some pairs of corresponding boundary slopes as follows.
By a

on a torus, we mean an essential simple closed curve up to isotopy.
For each
i\in I
,
the image of
P_{A,i}
under the free abelianization
\pi_{A}\to H_{A}
has rank either
1
or
2
.
For example, this follows from the well-known fact that
for any orientable compact
3
–manifold

the kernel and the image of the natural homomorphism

both have dimension

,
applying to a Dehn filling of
M_{A}
that leaves only

unfilled.
Since

,
there exists some

, such that

does not contain the image of
P_{A,i}
.
For any such

the preimage of

in

is an infinite cyclic subgroup

,
such that

is infinite cyclic,

represents a unique slope

on the torus

.
With the same

and

on
T_{B,i}
,
which is represented by an infinite cyclic subgroup

of

.
The above profinite correspondences imply that

isomorphically onto a conjugate of the closure of

and in this sense,

We can design corresponding Dehn fillings of

and

for any

For any admissible filling pattern

we obtain a Dehn filling

of

along the slopes

Namely,

by the smallest normal subgroup that contains all

\widehat{\pi_{A}}

Similarly, we obtain the Dehn filling

.

descends
to a profinite isomorphism

Lemma

would apply to

, if

and

To this end, we denote

Let

Suppose that

is an isomorphism.
Suppose that

and

both admit complete hyperbolic metrics of finite volume in the interior.

Take a finite cover of

,
such that

for example, as constructed by Cooper, Long, and Reid
[

This property guarantees that

has nontrivial cokernel,
because

Our proof invokes the following criterion for identifying reciprocal polynomials over

through principal ideals of the completion group algebra

Let

If the principal ideals

Remark 7.4
.

Recall that a polynomial

Theorem

is quoted from
[

In fact, Ueki shows that for any

the fraction
\Phi_{m}(t^{\mu})/\Phi_{m}(t)
is defined and is a unit
in
\llbracket\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}t^{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\rrbracket

where

denotes the
m

(see
[
Uek18
, Lemma 3.4]
).
This allows one to identify the cyclotomic factors
of the compared reciprocal polynomials.
The other factors are identified using a criterion of D. Fried
[
Frd88
]
,
which determines reciprocal polynomials through their cyclic resultants,
(see also
[
Uek18
, Proposition 3.1]
).

In certain situations we are able to obtain polynomial principal ideals of

For any profinite group

\mu\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
.
This is equivalent to saying that the cyclic subgroups
\langle\bar{g}\rangle

and
\langle\bar{g}^{\prime}\rangle
of
\Gamma_{m}
are conjugate to each other.
However, since
\phi_{f}(g)=m
, the cyclic subgroup
\langle\bar{g}\rangle
has order
d
,
and it intersects the coset
(\pi_{1}(S)/K_{m})\bar{t}^{m}
of
\Gamma_{m}
at the unique element
\bar{g}
.
Similarly,
\langle\bar{g}^{\prime}\rangle
intersects
(\pi_{1}(S)/K_{m})\bar{t}^{m}
at
\bar{g}^{\prime}
.
Since
(\pi_{1}(S)/K_{m})\bar{t}^{m}
is invariant under conjugations of
\Gamma_{m}
,
it follows that
\bar{g}
is conjugate to
\bar{g}^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{m}
.
This implies
\mathbf{O}=\mathbf{O}^{\prime}
,
since
\Gamma_{m}
is a finer quotient than
\tilde{\Gamma}_{m}
.
∎

We prove Theorem
8.1
as follows.
Let
(M_{A},M_{B},\Psi)
be a profinite morphism setting (Convention
4.1
)
where
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi}_{A}\to\widehat{\pi}_{B}
is an isomorphism
and where
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
are both closed and hyperbolic.
Let
\mu\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\times}
be a unit as provided in Theorem
6.1
.

For any primitive fibered class
\phi_{B}
in
H^{1}(M_{B};{\mathbb{Z}})
,
the cohomology class
\psi_{A}=\Psi^{*}_{1/\mu}(\phi_{B})
in
H^{1}(M_{A};{\mathbb{Z}})

is also primitive and fibered (Theorem
5.1
).
Moreover, we can identify
(M_{A},\psi_{A})
with the mapping torus and its distinguished cohomology class
for some pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f_{A}\colon S_{A}\to S_{A}
,
and identify
(M_{B},\phi_{B})
similarly for some
f_{B}\colon S_{B}\to S_{B}
.
Then
\widehat{\pi_{1}(S_{A})}
projects isomorphically onto
\widehat{S_{B}}
under
\Psi
,
(Corollary
6.2
).
For any
\Psi
–corresponding pair of finite quotients

\gamma_{A}\colon\pi_{A}\to\Gamma
and
\gamma_{B}\colon\pi_{B}\to\Gamma
,
and for any finite-dimensional representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
,
we obtain the following equality for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

L_{m}(f_{A};\gamma_{A}^{*}\chi_{\rho})=L_{m}(f_{B};\gamma_{B}^{*}\chi_{\rho}),

by Theorems
2.7
,
7.2
and (
2.5
).
Suitable
{\mathbb{Q}}
–linear combinations of the above equalties over all the
{\mathbb{Q}}
–irreducible
\rho

yield the equality

L_{m}(f_{A};\gamma_{A}^{*}\chi_{\omega})=L_{m}(f_{B};\gamma_{B}^{*}\chi_{\omega}),

for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
\omega\in\Omega(\Gamma)
,
by (
8.5
), (
8.6
) and Lemma
8.4
.
Applying Lemmas
8.6
and
8.5
, taking
\Gamma_{m}
with respect to
f_{A}
,
we obtain a comparison

N_{m}(f_{A})=\#\left\{\omega\in\Omega(\Gamma)\colon L_{m}(f_{A};\gamma_{A}^{*}\chi_{\omega})\neq 0\right\}=\#\left\{\omega\in\Omega(\Gamma)\colon L_{m}(f_{B};\gamma_{B}^{*}\chi_{\omega})\neq 0\right\}\leq N_{m}(f_{B})
.
Similarly,
we obtain
N_{m}(f_{B})\leq N_{m}(f_{A})
by taking
\Gamma_{m}
with respect to
f_{B}
.
Therefore, we obtain the equality for the orbit Nielsen numbers

N_{m}(M_{A},\psi_{A})=N_{m}(f_{A})=N_{m}(f_{B})=N_{m}(M_{B},\phi_{B})

for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, as asserted.
Since this forces the equality to be achieved in the above comparisons,
we obtain the equality for the indexed orbit Nielsen numbers

\nu_{m}(M_{A},\psi_{A};i)=\nu_{m}(M_{B},\phi_{B};i)

for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
,
as asserted, by Lemma
8.5
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
8.1
.

9.
Profinite almost rigidity

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
,
and indeed, we prove a slightly more topological statement as follows.

Theorem 9.1
.

For any finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifold
M
,
there exists a finite collection
\mathcal{M}
of finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds,
such that the following property holds:

If
N
is a connected compact
3
–manifold and if the profinite completion
\widehat{\pi_{1}(N)}

is isomorphic to
\widehat{\pi_{1}(M)}
, then,
possibly after capping off all boundary spheres with
3
–balls,
the interior of
N
is homeomorphic to some member from
\mathcal{M}
.

We prove Theorem
9.1
in the rest of this section.
Our argument relies eventually on the following well-known finiteness result in mapping class group theory,
due to Arnoux–Yoccoz
[
ArnY81
]
and Ivanov
[
Iva88
]
,
(see also
[
FarM12
, Chapter 14, Theorem 14.9]
).

Theorem 9.2
.

For any integers
g,p\geq 0
with
2-2g-p<0
, and for any real constant
C>1
,
there are at most finitely many pseudo-Anosov automorphisms

f\colon S\to S
of an orientable surface
S

with genus
g
and
p
punctures, up to topological equivalence,
such that the stretch factor
\lambda(f)
is at most
C
.

We need the punctured case of Theorem
9.2

to prove the cusped case of Theorem
9.1
.
When
S
has punctures, a
pseudo-Anosov
automorphism
f\colon S\to S

is similarly defined as in Remark
2.4
,
preserving a transverse pair of measured foliations
(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{s}},\mu^{\mathtt{s}})

and
(\mathscr{F}^{\mathtt{u}},\mu^{\mathtt{u}})
, shrinking or stretching the measure with
reciprocal factors, and we allow
k
–prong singularities in the interior with
k\geq 3
,
or at the punctures with
k\geq 1
.
A pair of pseudo-Anosov automorphisms
f_{A}\colon S_{A}\to S_{A}
and
f_{B}\colon S_{B}\to S_{B}

are said to be
topologically equivalent
if there exists a homeomorphism
h\colon S_{A}\to S_{B}
,
such that
f_{B}\circ h=h\circ f_{A}
.

The following Lemma
9.3
is stated in the finite-volume hyperbolic case,
which suffices for our reduction.
As kindly pointed out by the anonymous referee,
a similar proof also works for compact
3
–manifolds with empty or tori boundary,
by invoking B. Zimmermann’s result on finite group actions
on Haken
3
–manifolds, (see
[
Zim86
, Corollary 4.2]
).

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
M
be a finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifold.
The conclusion of Theorem
9.1
holds true for
M

if it holds true for some regular finite cover of
M
.

Proof.

Suppose that we have obtained a finite collection
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
as asserted
for some finite cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
. If
N
is any connected compact
3
–manifold
with
\pi_{1}(N)
profinitely isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(M)
,
then there is some regular finite cover
N^{\prime}
of
N
,
such that
\pi_{1}(N^{\prime})
is profinitely isomorphic
\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})
.
By assumption, there is some
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
,
such that the interior of
N^{\prime}
is homeomorphic to
X^{\prime}
with finitely many punctures.
Note that the boundary spheres of
N^{\prime}
compactifying those punctures
must project homeomorphically onto boundary spheres of
N
.
In fact, otherwise
\pi_{1}(N)
would contain torsion,
but
\widehat{\pi_{1}(N)}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}(M)}
is torsion-free,
since
\pi_{1}(M)
is a residually finite, good group of finite cohomological dimension

[
Rei15
, Corollary 7.6]
.
So, possibly after capping of the boundary spheres of
N
and
N^{\prime}
,
we may assume that the interior of
N^{\prime}
is homeomorphic to
X^{\prime}
.
By the Perelman–Thurston geometrization theorem,
we infer that
N
is finite-volume hyperbolic,
then it follows from the Mostow–Prasad rigidity theorem
that the finite cover
N^{\prime}
is isometric to
X^{\prime}
,
(see
[
AscFW15
, Theorems 1.7.1 and 1.7.5]
);
one may also infer the hyperbolicity of
N

as a particular case of
[
WltZ17a
]
.
This means that
N
is an isometric quotient of
N^{\prime}\cong X^{\prime}
.
Therefore, we can form
\mathcal{M}
by taking
all the finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifolds
X
,
such that
X
is some isometric quotient of some
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.
Since the isometry group of any finite-volume hyperbolic
3
–manifold is finite,

\mathcal{M}
is a finite collection as desired.
∎

Lemma 9.4
.

The conclusion of Theorem
9.1
holds if
M
is closed.

Proof.

With Lemma
9.3
, we may assume without loss of generality that
M
is
orientable, and fibers over a circle (Theorem
2.3
).
Identify
M
with the mapping torus of some pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f_{M}\colon S_{M}\to S_{M}
.
Possibly after cap-off, we may assume
N
has no boundary spheres.
Then the assumption
\widehat{\pi_{1}(N)}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}(M)}
implies that

N
is homeomorphic to an orientable closed hyperbolic
3
–manifold
[
WltZ17b
]
.
By Corollaries
6.2
and
8.2
,

N
is homeomorphic to the mapping torus of some pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f_{N}\colon S_{N}\to S_{N}
,
with
\chi(S_{N})=\chi(S_{M})
and
\lambda(f_{N})=\lambda(f_{M})
.
Therefore, Theorem
9.2
implies that

(S_{M},f_{M})
determines the homeomorphism type of

N
up to finitely many possibilities after cap-off.
∎

Lemma 9.5
.

The conclusion of Theorem
9.1
holds if
M
is cusped.

Proof.

The same proof as with Lemma
9.4
will work,
once we show
\chi(S_{N})=\chi(S_{M})
and
\lambda(f_{N})=\lambda(f_{M})
.
To this end,
it suffices to assume that
M
and
N
are the mapping tori of pseudo-Anosov automorphisms

f_{M}\colon S_{M}\to S_{M}
and
f_{N}\colon S_{N}\to S_{N}

of orientable finite-type punctured surfaces,
such that a profinite group isomorphism
\Psi\colon\widehat{\pi_{1}(M)}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}(N)}

maps the closed normal subgroup
\widehat{\pi_{1}(S_{M})}
isomorphically onto
\widehat{\pi_{1}(S_{N})}
.

We construct a pseudo-Anosov automorphism
f^{\dagger}_{M}\colon S^{\dagger}_{M}\to S^{\dagger}_{M}

as follows. The surface
S^{\dagger}_{M}
is a connected finite cover of
S_{M}

associated to a characteristic finite-index subgroup
K^{\dagger}_{M}
of
\pi_{1}(S_{M})
,
such that no simple closed curves of
S_{M}
lift to
S^{\dagger}_{M}
.
For example, we take an intermediate cover
S^{\prime}_{M}

associated to the kernel of the mod 2 abelianization of
\pi_{1}(S_{M})
,
and take
S^{\dagger}_{M}
associated to
the kernel of the mod 2 abelianization of
\pi_{1}(S^{\prime}_{M})
.
The automorphism
f^{\dagger}_{M}\colon S^{\dagger}_{M}\to S^{\dagger}_{M}

is an iterate of a lift of
f_{M}
,
such that
f^{\dagger}_{M}
fixes every puncture of
S^{\dagger}_{M}
.
For example,
we take the iteration power to be

D=[\pi_{1}(S_{M}):K^{\dagger}_{M}]\times\prod_{g}(2-2g-\chi(S_{M}))!
,
where
g
ranges over
0,1,\cdots,\lfloor(2-\chi(S_{M}))/2\rfloor
,
then
f^{\dagger}_{M}
does not depend on the choice of the lift,
and
f^{\dagger}_{M}
fixes every puncture of
S^{\dagger}_{M}
,
regardless of the particular genus or puncture number of
S_{M}
.
Moreover,
we obtain a pseudo-Anosov automorphism
\bar{f}^{\dagger}_{M}\colon\bar{S}^{\dagger}_{M}\to\bar{S}^{\dagger}_{M}

of an orientable closed surface
\bar{S}^{\dagger}_{M}
by filling the punctures of
S^{\dagger}_{M}
with points.
Since the stable/unstable measured foliations on
S_{M}
pull back to
S^{\dagger}_{M}
,
our construction ensures that there are no
1
–prong singularities
at any punctures of
S^{\dagger}_{M}
,
so
\lambda(\bar{f}^{\dagger}_{M})=\lambda(f^{\dagger}_{M})=\lambda(f_{M})^{D}
.

We construct similarly
f^{\dagger}_{N}\colon S^{\dagger}_{N}\to S^{\dagger}_{N}

with respect to
N
, using the
\Psi
–corresponding characteristic finite-index subgroup
K^{\dagger}_{N}
of
\pi_{1}(N)

and the same iteration power
D
.
So
\lambda(\bar{f}^{\dagger}_{N})=\lambda(f^{\dagger}_{N})=\lambda(f_{N})^{D}
.
The isomorphism
\Psi
determines an isomorphism
\Psi^{\dagger}
between
the profinite completions of
the cusped mapping-torus groups

\pi_{1}(M^{\dagger})
and
\pi_{1}(N^{\dagger})
,
associated to
f^{\dagger}_{M}
and
f^{\dagger}_{N}
respectively.
Moreover,
\Psi^{\dagger}
descends to an isomorphism
\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}
between the profinite completions
of the closed mapping-torus groups

\pi_{1}(\bar{M}^{\dagger})
and
\pi_{1}(\bar{N}^{\dagger})
,
associated to
\bar{f}^{\dagger}_{M}
and
\bar{f}^{\dagger}_{N}
respectively.
This follows from the fiber surface correspondence (Corollary
6.2
)
and the profinite cusp correspondence
[
WltZ17b
, Proposition 3.1]
,
(as aforementioned in the proof of Lemma
6.9
).

In fact,
S_{M}^{\dagger}
and
S_{N}^{\dagger}
has bijectively corresponding punctures,
since
M^{\dagger}
and
N^{\dagger}
has bijectively corresponding peripheral tori,
as witnessed by
\Psi^{\dagger}

[
WltZ17b
, Proposition 3.1]
.
More precisely, for every peripheral torus
T^{\dagger}_{M}
of
M^{\dagger}

there is a unique peripheral torus
T^{\dagger}_{N}
of
N^{\dagger}
,
such that
\Psi^{\dagger}
maps the closure of
\pi_{1}(T^{\dagger}_{M})
onto
a conjugate of the closure of
\pi_{1}(T^{\dagger}_{N})
.
The intersection of
\mathrm{clos}(\pi_{1}(T^{\dagger}_{M}))
with
the normal subgroup
\mathrm{clos}(\pi_{1}(S^{\dagger}_{M}))

in the profinite completion of
\pi_{1}(M^{\dagger})

is exactly
\mathrm{clos}(\pi_{1}(c^{\dagger}_{M}))
,
where
c^{\dagger}_{M}
is the unique slope on
T^{\dagger}

which is freely homotopic in
M^{\dagger}
to a peripheral simple closed curve on
S^{\dagger}_{M}
.
The profinite completion of
\pi_{1}(\bar{M}^{\dagger})

is the quotient of the profinite completion of
\pi_{1}(M^{\dagger})

by the smallest closed normal subgroup
which contains all the peripheral subgroups

\mathrm{clos}(\pi_{1}(c^{\dagger}_{M}))
as above.
Since
\Psi^{\dagger}
carries the similar description to
the profinite completion of
\pi_{1}(\bar{N}^{\dagger})
,
it desends to an isomorphism
\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}

between the profinite completions of
\pi_{1}(\bar{M}^{\dagger})
and
\pi_{1}(\bar{N}^{\dagger})
.

Since
\pi_{1}(S_{M})
and
\pi_{1}(S_{N})
are profinitely isomorphic,
we obtain
\chi(S_{M})=\chi(S_{N})
. Applying Corollary
8.2
to
\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}
,
we obtain
\lambda(\bar{f}^{\dagger}_{M})=\lambda(\bar{f}^{\dagger}_{N})
,
and hence,
\lambda(f_{M})=\lambda(f_{N})
. Supplied with these equalities,
the argument with the closed case (Lemma
9.4
)
works for the cusped case (Lemma
9.5
), as desired.
∎

Theorem
9.1
follows from Lemmas
9.4
and
9.5
.

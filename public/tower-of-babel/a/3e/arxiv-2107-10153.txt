1.
Introduction

Recently, the theory of ordinary Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}n^{-s}=\sum a_{n}e^{-s\log n}
and that of general Dirichlet series

\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, where the frequency
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})
is a strictly increasing, non-negative real sequence,
saw a remarkable renaissance.

General Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
converge on maximal half-planes
[\re>\penalty\ \sigma]
in
\mathbb{C}
, where they define holomorphic functions
f
, and even though in concrete situations these half-planes might be small, the holomorphic functions
f
often extend to holomorphic functions on larger half-planes.

The problem of determining whether a holomorphic
f
, defined on some half-plane, is or is not representable in the form of a

\lambda
-Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, is in general difficult.

An important criterion is that the limit function of a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
has finite order in each closed half-plane of its domain of convergence. Moreover, if this limit function can be holomorphically extended
to a larger half-plane, it may happen that its continuation still has finite order in a larger domain.

Recall that, given a holomorphic function
f:[\re>\sigma_{0}]\to\mathbb{C},\,\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, its order
\mu_{f}(\sigma)\in[\sigma_{0},\infty]
on the line

[\re\penalty\ =\penalty\ \sigma],\,\sigma>\sigma_{0}
is the infimum over all
\ell\in[0,\infty]

such that

(1)

\exists\penalty\ C,t_{0}>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ |t|>t_{0}\colon\penalty\ |f(\sigma+it)|\leq C|t|^{\ell}\,.

The function
f
is said to be of finite order on
[\re>\sigma_{0}]
whenever
\mu_{f}<\infty
for all
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
, and in this case
\mu_{f}:]\sigma_{0},\infty[\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is continuous and convex.

Similarly, the uniform order
\nu_{f}\in[0,\infty]
of
f
on the half-plane
[\re>\sigma_{0}]
is given by the infimum over all
\ell\in[0,\infty]
such that
the above condition holds uniformly, i.e.,

(2)

\exists\penalty\ C,t_{0}>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ \sigma>\sigma_{0},|t|>t_{0}\colon\penalty\ |f(\sigma+it)|\leq C|t|^{\ell}.

Moreover, the function
f
is said to have finite uniform order on
[\re>\sigma_{0}]
whenever
\nu_{f}<\infty
.

To see a famous example, denote by
\zeta:\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}\to\mathbb{C}
the zeta-function, which
is holomorphic with a simple pole in
s=1
, and which
on
[\re>1]
is the pointwise limit of the zeta-Dirichlet series
\sum n^{-s}
. The famous Lindelöf conjecture asks whether the order of
\zeta

on the critical line
[\re=\frac{1}{2}]
equals
0
. Equivalently, consider the entire function

\eta:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\,,\,\,\,\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)\,,

which on
[\re>0]
is nothing else then the pointwise limit of the
\eta
-Dirichlet series
\sum(-1)^{n+1}n^{-s}
.
It is known that

\mu_{\eta}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}-\sigma
for
\sigma<0
and
\mu_{\eta}(\sigma)=0
for
\sigma>1
. Hence
Lindelöf’s conjecture is equivalent to the question whether or not we have that

\mu_{\eta}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}-\sigma&\,\,\,\,0\leq\sigma<\frac{1}{2}\\
0&\,\,\,\,\frac{1}{2}<\sigma\leq 1.\end{cases}

The main aim here is to study holomorphic functions of finite uniform order generated by Dirichlet series from the point of view of functional analysis – and our main inspiration comes from the classical monograph
[
13
]
of Hardy and Riesz on so-called Riesz summation of
general Dirichlet series.

Given a frequency
\lambda
and a holomorphic function
f:[\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
, we say that the

\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}(D)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

is a
\lambda
-Riesz germ of

whenever
D
for some
m\geq 0
and some

satisfies that

The trigonometric polynomials

R_{x}^{\lambda,m}(f)(s)=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}(D)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{m}\,,\,\,x>0

are said to be the
(\lambda,m)
-Riesz means of

of order

.

An important fact will be that such
\lambda
-Riesz germs

of

, whenever they exist (!), are unique (Corollary
2.15
) –
and so in this situation
the coefficients

uniquely determine the function
f
.

We study a graduated scale of Banach spaces that consist of holomorphic functions on the right half-plane, which are of finite uniform order on
[\re>0]
and are generated by
\lambda
-Riesz germs. More precisely, given a frequency
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})
and
\ell\geq 0
, the linear space

(3)

H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]

consists of all
holomorphic functions
f:\penalty\ [\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
, which are generated by a
\lambda
-Riesz germ and satisfy the growth condition

(4)

\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}=\sup_{\re s>0}\frac{|f(s)|}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}<\infty\,.

This defines a scale of Banach spaces (a non-trivial fact from Theorem
3.16
),

\big(H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0],\|\boldsymbol{\cdot}\|_{\infty,\ell}\big)_{\ell\geq 0}\,,

which is intimately connected with the notion of finite uniform order (Theorem

):

\bullet

Each

has finite uniform order
\nu_{f}\leq\ell
on
[\re>0]
,
and conversely,

\bullet

if

is a holomorphic function on
[\re>0]
, which is generated by a
\lambda
-Riesz germ and is of finite uniform order on
[\re\penalty\ >\penalty\ 0]
, then for every

all translations
f_{\sigma}=f(\sigma+\cdot)\in H_{\infty,k}^{\lambda}[\re>0]\,,\,\sigma>0

Outlook.

The classical Carleson-Hunt result implies that the Taylor series of
bounded holomorphic functions on the open unit disk converge almost everywhere on the boundary. In contrast, there exists an ordinary Dirichlet series, which on the open right half-plane converges to a bounded, holomorphic function – but diverges at each point of the imaginary line, although its limit function extends continuously to the closed right half plane. As a continuation of the present article we in the forthcoming
work
[

study Riesz summability of functions in the spaces
H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0],\,\ell\geq 0

axis. Again our inspiration comes from an important, surprisingly deep result of M. Riesz
published (without proof) in
[
13

2.1.
Ordinary summation

Every presentation of the classical convergence theory of general Dirichlet series necessarily starts with the following theorem, and for the sake of completeness we repeat the standard proof based on Abel smmation (see e.g.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

be a

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that

But with

|e^{-\lambda_{\nu}s}-e^{-\lambda_{\nu+1}s}|\leq\frac{|s|}{\re s}(e^{-\lambda_{\nu}\sigma}-e^{-\lambda_{\nu+1}\sigma})\leq\sec{\gamma}\,(e^{-\lambda_{\nu}\sigma}-e^{-\lambda_{\nu+1}\sigma})\,,

and hence, for

\Big|\sum_{\nu=n}^{m}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\Big|\leq\varepsilon

uniformly on the cone

∎

Lemma 2.4

Let

, and

Proof.

S_{x}^{\lambda}(D)(0)=S_{x}^{\lambda}(D)(-s_{0}+s_{0})=e^{s_{0}x}S^{\lambda}_{x}(D)(s_{0})-\int_{0}^{x}S^{\lambda}_{t}(D)(s_{0})(-s_{0})e^{s_{0}t}dt.

2.2.

All definitions following are inspired by

2.4.
Perron formula

We prove Perron’s formula – an integral formula for the summatory function of a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
in terms of its limit function. The result was first presented in
[
13
, Theorem 39]
. In fact, it turns out that it follows by Fourier inversion from
the integral formula in Theorem
2.9
. The original proof of Perron’s formula for general Dirichlet series as given in

[
13
]
is mainly a consequence of Cauchy’s integral theorem. Our approach, deeply inspired by ideas used in
[
17
]
to cover
the case

, differs considerable.

Theorem 2.13
.

For
k\geq 0
let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
be somewhere
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable and

f:[\re>\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)]\to\mathbb{C}
its limit function. Then
for each
x>0
and
c>\max\{\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D),0\}
we have

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s)}{s^{1+k}}e^{xs}ds.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{F}_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}
the Fourier transform on
L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
(with respect to the ordinary Lebesgue measure). Then for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
by Theorem
2.9

\displaystyle\frac{f(c+iy)}{(c+iy)^{1+k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[S_{t}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)e^{-ct}\big]e^{-iyt}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+k)}\mathcal{F}_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\big(t\mapsto S_{t}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)e^{-ct}\big)(y)\,.

Since the function
g(t)=S_{t}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)e^{-ct}
satisfies a Lipschitz condition at all
t\notin\{\lambda_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, we by
[
15
, Corollary on p.9]
for all such

obtain

S_{t}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)e^{-ct}=\lim_{A\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-A}^{A}\Gamma(1+k)\frac{f(c+iy)}{(c+iy)^{1+k}}e^{iyt}dy.

Hence,

\displaystyle S_{t}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi}\lim_{A\to\infty}\int_{-A}^{A}\frac{f(c+iy)}{(c+iy)^{1+k}}e^{t(c+iy)}dy\,,

which is the equality we intended to prove.
∎

It is important for our later purposes that the preceding integral formula for the summatory function of a given
\lambda
-Dirichlet series

, can be extended considerably, whenever the limit function of
f
extends holomorphically to the full right half-plane – still satisfying a
’finite order type-condition’ (a result sketched in
[
13
, p.51]
).

Corollary 2.14
.

For
k\geq 0
let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
be
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable for some
s_{0}\in[\re>0]
. Assume that the limit function
f
of
D
extends holomorphically to
[\re>0]
such that

\forall\varepsilon,\delta>0\,\,\,\exists t_{0}>0\,\,\,\forall|t|\geq t_{0},\sigma>\delta\colon|f(\sigma+it)|\leq\varepsilon|\sigma+it|^{1+k}.

Then
for each
x>0
and
c>0

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s)}{s^{1+k}}e^{xs}ds.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.13
we know that that for
x>0
and
\sigma>\re s_{0}

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\frac{f(s)}{s^{1+k}}e^{xs}ds.

We claim that for each
x>0
and
c>0

(17)

\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s)}{s^{1+k}}e^{xs}ds=\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\frac{f(s)}{s^{1+k}}e^{xs}ds.

Indeed, an application of Cauchy’s integral theorem shows that (
17
) follows, once we prove that

(18)

\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{c}^{\sigma}\frac{f(y\pm iT)}{(y\pm iT)^{1+k}}e^{x(y\pm iT)}dy=0.

To see this, choose, given
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta=c
, some
t_{0}>0
according to the assumption. Then for all

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{c}^{\sigma}\frac{f(y\pm iT)}{(y\pm iT)^{1+k}}e^{x(y\pm iT)}dy\Big|\leq\varepsilon\int_{c}^{\sigma}e^{xy}dy=\varepsilon(e^{x\sigma}-e^{xc})x^{-1},

which implies (
18
).
∎

Finally, we show that the coefficients of a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series are uniquely determined by the values of its limit function on some abscissa - another crucial point for our coming purposes.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
be
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable at
s_{0}=\sigma_{0}+i\tau_{0}
for some

. If the limit function
f
of
D
vanishes on some vertical line
[\re=\sigma]
, where
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
, then
a_{n}=0
for all
n
.

Proof.

Let first
\lambda_{1}<x<\lambda_{2}
. Then by Theorem
2.13
and our assumption

a_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}s_{0}}(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{x})^{k}=S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\frac{f(s)e^{xs}}{s^{1+k}}ds=0,

which implies

. Proceeding successively by considering a sequence

.
∎

2.5.
Bohr-Cahen formula

The following formula for the abscissa of convergence is again due to Hardy and Riesz
[

, Theorem 31]
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
and

. Then

\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(|R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)|)}{x},

with equality whenever
\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)

The proof will be given in Section

.
Note that

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(|R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)|)}{x}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(|S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(0)|)}{x}.

Recall that Theorem
2.16
for the special case

was already presented in Corollary

, and that we in Corollary

Again a simple analysis shows that the proof of Theorem

Hence we exactly as in Section

(note that for

.

Let

3.
A new scale of Banach spaces

We start repeating some of our basic definitions from the introduction.
Given a frequency
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})
, we call a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
a
\lambda
-Riesz germ of the holomorphic function

f:\penalty\ [\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
,
whenever
\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,m}(D)<\infty
for some
m\geq 0
and
f
is the holomorphic extension
of the limit function of
D
to all of
[\re>0]
.

Two remarks are in order.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
f:\penalty\ [\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function generated by the
\lambda
-Riesz germ

D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
.
Then
D
by Corollary
2.15
is unique, and as a consequence we may assign to every such
f
the unique sequence

(a_{n}(f))_{n}=(a_{n})_{n}
, which we (like for Dirichlet series) again call the ’sequence of Bohr coefficients of
f
’.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
f:\penalty\ [\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic. Then
f
has a
\lambda
-Riesz germ if and only if
f
on some half-plane and for some
m
equals the limit function of some
\lambda
-Dirichlet series under
(e^{\lambda},m)
-Riesz summation.
This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.8
.

Moreover, given a holomorphic function
f:\penalty\ [\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
generated by the
\lambda
-Riesz germ

D
, the
x
th Riesz mean of order
k\geq 0

of
f
in
s\in\mathbb{C}
is given by

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)=\sum_{\lambda<x}a_{n}(f)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}\,,

and its summatory function in
s\in\mathbb{C}
by

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)=\sum_{\lambda<x}a_{n}(f)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}(x-\lambda_{n})^{k}\,,\,\,x>0\,.

As explained above the spaces

H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re\penalty\ >\penalty\ 0]\,,\,\,\,\,\ell\geq 0,

accumulate all
holomorphic functions
f:\penalty\ [\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
, which are generated by a
\lambda
-Riesz germ and satisfy
the growth condition

(19)

\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}=\sup_{\re s>0}\frac{|f(s)|}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}<\infty\,.

For each such
f

\sup_{\re s>0}\frac{|f(s)|}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}\leq\sup_{\re s>0}\frac{|f(s)|}{|(1+s)^{\ell}|}\leq 2^{\ell}\sup_{\re s>0}\frac{|f(s)|}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}\,,

since
|1+s|\leq 1+|s|\leq 2|1+s|
for all
s\in[\re>0]
.
Obviously, the pairs

(H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0],\|\cdot\|_{\infty,\ell})

form an increasing scale
of normed spaces. That all these spaces in fact are complete, so Banach spaces, is a non-trivial fact which is given in Theorem
3.16
.

Finally, we remark that a holomorphic function satisfying the growth condition (
19
), by Remark
3.2

(see also (
13
)) belongs to
H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
if and only
if there is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
such that for some
m,\sigma\geq 0
and for every
s\in[\re>\sigma]

(20)

f(s)=\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{e^{\lambda},m}(D)=\lim_{x\to\infty}T_{x}^{\lambda,m}(D)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\Big(1-\frac{e^{\lambda_{n}}}{e^{x}}\Big)^{m}.

3.1.
Ordinary and power case

We start considering the power case
\lambda=(n)
. Then, as already announced in the introduction, the scale
H_{\infty,\ell}^{(n)}[\re>0]\,,\,\ell\geq 0,
collapses to the case
\ell=0
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every
\ell\geq 0

H_{\infty}^{(n)}[\re>0]=H_{\infty,\ell}^{(n)}[\re>0]\,.

Proof.

Take
f\in H_{\infty,\ell}^{(n)}[\re>0]
, let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-ns}
be its associated Dirichlet series, and
choose any
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\ell<m
. Then we deduce from Corollary
3.9
(proved below) and Theorem
2.8
(for which we give a complete proof in in our appendix from
Section
4
) that
D
is
((e^{n}),m)
-Riesz summmable on
[\re>0]
with limit
f
. Then by
[
13
, Theorem 21]

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big(\frac{e^{N+1}-e^{N}}{e^{N+1}}\Big)^{m}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\Big)=0\,,

i.e.

D
on
[\re>0]
converges pointwise to
f
.

We remark that
[
13
, Theorem 21]
is a result on arbitray frequencies
\lambda
and arbitrary orders
k>0
, and we here only use this result for the integer case
k=m\in\mathbb{N}
, which according to
[
13
, p.36]
has an ’extremely simple’ proof.

Continuing the proof, we see that
D
converges uniformly on
[\re>\varepsilon]
for every
\varepsilon>0
(indeed, for the frequency
\lambda=(n)
the abscissas of convergence and absolutely convergence coincide – see
[
4
, 12, §3, Hilfssatz 2,3]
).
So in particular
f
is bounded on
[\re>1]
. Moreover,
f(\sigma+i\boldsymbol{\cdot}):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is
2\pi
-periodic for every
\sigma>0
,
which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\sigma<2\\
t\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}|f(\sigma+it)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\sigma<2\\
t\in[0,2\pi]\end{subarray}}|f(\sigma+it)|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\sigma<2\\
t\in[0,2\pi]\end{subarray}}(1+|\sigma+it|)^{\ell}\leq\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}(1+\sqrt{4+4\pi^{2}})^{\ell}\,.

All in all we conclude that
f\in H_{\infty}^{(n)}[\re>0]
.
∎

Next we consider the frequency
\lambda=(\log n)
, which generates ordinary Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
.
Looking at (
20
) (with
x=\log y
), we see that a holomorphic function
f\colon[\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to the Banach space
\mathcal{H}^{(\log n)}_{\infty,1}[\re>0]
if only if the growth condition (
19
) holds,
and there exists an ordinary Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
such that for some
m,\sigma\geq 0

f(s)=\lim_{y\to\infty}\sum_{n<y}a_{n}n^{-s}\Big(1-\frac{n}{y}\Big)^{m},\penalty\ \penalty\ s\in[\re>\sigma].

Actually, we will see in Corollary
3.26
that Theorem
3.10
in the special case
\lambda=(\log n)
is valid for
k=\ell
, and therefore in the particular case
\ell=1

f(s)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}k^{-s},\penalty\ \penalty\ s\in[\re>0],

so that eventually we arrive at the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f:[\re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic. Then the growth condition (
19
) holds
and there exists an ordinary Dirichlet series which on
[\re>0]
is Cesàro summable with limit
f,
if and only if
f\in H_{\infty,1}^{(\log n)}[\re>0]
.

Note that the space
H^{(\log n)}_{\infty,\ell}[\re>0]
is strictly larger than
H^{(\log n)}_{\infty}[\re>0]
. To see an example, we again
look at the eta-function
\eta
(defined in the introduction), for which

\ell>\dfrac{1}{2}\,\,\,\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\,\,\,\eta\in H^{(\log n)}_{\infty,\ell}[\re>0]\,\,\,\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\,\,\,\ell\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\,.

In particular,
\eta\notin H^{(\log n)}_{\infty}[\re>0]
. Alternatively, this may also be seen as a consequence of Bohr’s inequality (see
[
7
, Corollary 4.3]
or
[
21
, Theorem 4.4.1]
): Assuming
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}((\log n))
implies that

N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}|(-1)^{p_{n}}|\leq\|D\|_{\infty}<\infty
for all
N
,
which clearly is a contradiction.

This example in particular shows that, although we in general have

\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\infty,\ell_{1}}[\re>0]\subset\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\infty,\ell_{2}}[\re>0]\,\,\,\text{ for all $0\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}$ }\,,

this inclusion for certain
\lambda
’s may be strict whereas for other
\lambda
’s it actually may be an equality (of sets).

3.2.
Perron formula – a variant

We now for the new scale of Banach spaces prove an important variant of Perron’s formula from Theorem
2.13
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f\in H^{\lambda}_{\infty,\ell}[\re>0]
and
k>\ell\geq 0
.
Then for all
s_{0}\in[\re\geq 0]
,
x>0
and
c>0

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s_{0})=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s+s_{0})}{s^{1+k}}e^{xs}ds.

Proof.

After translation we may assume that
s_{0}=0.
By assumption the Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
is
(\lambda,m)
-Riesz summable at some
s_{1}\in[\re>0]
for some
m\geq 0
. Hence
D
is
(\lambda,m+k)
-Riesz summable at
s_{1}

(Theorem
2.7
,(i)), and for every
\varepsilon,\sigma>0
and
|t|\geq t_{0}>0

\frac{|f(\sigma+it)|}{|\sigma+it|^{1+m+k}}\leq\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}|\sigma+it|^{-(1+m+k-\ell)}\leq t_{0}^{-(1+m+k-\ell)}\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}\leq\varepsilon\,,

whenever
t_{0}
is large enough. Corollary
2.14
then implies that for all
c,x>0

\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}(x-\lambda_{n})^{m+k}=\frac{\Gamma(1+m+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s)e^{xs}}{s^{1+m+k}}ds.

Now we differentiate
m
-times obtaining

\frac{d^{m}}{dx}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s)e^{xs}}{s^{1+k+m}}ds=\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s)e^{xs}}{s^{1+k}}ds

and

\frac{d^{m}}{dx}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}(x-\lambda_{n})^{m+k}=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(m+k-j)\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}(x-\lambda_{n})^{k}\,;

for the second equality see (
33
), and for the first note that the differentiation under the integral is legit, since for every
j=0,\ldots,m-1

\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\Big|\frac{f(s)e^{xs}}{s^{1+k+j}}\Big|ds\leq e^{xc}\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}|s|^{-(1+k+j-\ell)}ds<\infty.

Additionally by the functional equation of the Gamma function we have

\Gamma(1+k)\Gamma(1+m+k)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(m+k-j),

so that altogether we obtain

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(0)=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}(x-\lambda_{n})^{k}=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{f(s)e^{xs}}{s^{1+k}}ds.\qed

3.3.
A maximal inequality for Riesz means

The following maximal inequality is going to be our major tool to perform a substantial
structure theory of the spaces
H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
. It shows that for
k>\ell
the linear projections

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}:H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]\to H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]\,,\,\,\,\,f\to R_{x}^{\lambda,k}f\,,\,\,\,\,x>0,

are uniformly bounded.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
k>\ell\geq 0
and
\lambda
a frequency. Then there is a constant

C=C(k,\ell,\lambda)>0
such that for all

f\in H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]

\sup_{x>0}\|R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)\|_{\infty,\ell}\leq C\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}\,.

In particular,
for every
\sigma\geq 0
there is a constant
C=C(\ell,k,\lambda,\sigma)>0
such that for every
f\in H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]

and
t\in\mathbb{R}

\sup_{x>0}|R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(\sigma+it)|\leq C(1+|t|)^{\ell}\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}\,.

Proof.

We assume first that
\lambda_{1}>0
, and take
s=\sigma+it\in[\re>0]
and
y\geq\lambda_{1}
. Then by Theorem
3.5
with
c=y^{-1}

\displaystyle|R_{y}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s)|\leq\frac{e\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi}y^{-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(s+\frac{1}{y}+ix)|}{|\frac{1}{y}+ix|^{1+k}}dx\,,

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}^{-1}y^{-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(s+\frac{1}{y}+ix)|}{|\frac{1}{y}+ix|^{1+k}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y|1+s+\frac{1}{y}+ix|^{\ell}}{|1+ixy|^{1+k}}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\max\{0,\ell-1\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y(|1+s|^{\ell}+|\frac{1}{y}+ix|^{\ell})}{|1+ixy|^{1+k}}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\max\{0,\ell-1\}}\Big((1+|s|)^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y}{|1+ixy|^{1+k}}dx+y^{-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y}{|1+ixy|^{1+k-\ell}}dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\max\{0,\ell-1\}}\Big((1+|s|)^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|1+iv|^{1+k}}dv+\lambda_{1}^{-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|1+iv|^{1+k-\ell}}dv\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\max\{0,\ell-1\}}\Big((1+|s|)^{\ell}\big(1+\frac{1}{k}\big)+\lambda_{1}^{-\ell}\big(1+\frac{1}{k-\ell}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\max\{0,\ell-1\}}\Big(\big(1+\frac{1}{k}\big)+\lambda_{1}^{-\ell}\big(1+\frac{1}{k-\ell}\big)\Big)(1+|s|)^{\ell}\,.

Assume now that
\lambda_{1}=0
and choose
0<y<\lambda_{2}
. Then by the previous calculation

|a_{0}|\leq\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}2^{\max\{0,\ell-1\}}\Big(\big(1+\frac{1}{k}\big)+y^{-\ell}\big(1+\frac{1}{k-\ell}\big)\Big)(1+|s|)^{\ell}.

Tending
y\to\lambda_{2}
we obtain

|a_{0}|\leq C(\lambda,k,\ell)(1+|s|)^{\ell}\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}.

The ’in particular part’ is then a straight forward consequence, since

1+|s|\leq(1+\sigma)\big(1+\frac{|t|}{1+\sigma}\big)\leq(1+\sigma)(1+|t|)\,.\qed

3.4.
Uniform Riesz approximation

Next we give the main application of the preceding maximal theorem. We prove that for
k>\ell
all functions in a bounded subset of
H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
, after translation by some arbitrary
u>0
,
are uniformly
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz approximable in the norm of
H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency and
k>\ell\geq 0
. Then for every bounded set
M\subset H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
and every choice of
\varepsilon,u>0
there is
x_{0}>0
such that

\sup_{x>x_{0}}\sup_{f\in M}\|f(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})-R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})\|_{\infty,\ell}\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
m\in N_{0}
such that
m<k\leq m+1
, and
C=\sup_{f\in M}\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}<\infty
. Fix
f\in M
and
u>0
. Then by Lemma
4.11
and Theorem
3.6
we for every
s\in[\re>0]
have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{x^{-k}}{\Gamma(m+2)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{S_{t}^{\lambda,m+1}(f)(s)}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}u^{m+2}(x-t)^{k}e^{-ut}dt-\frac{R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s+u)}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-ux}\frac{|R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)|}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}+C_{1}(m,k)x^{-k}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{|S_{y}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)|}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}u^{j}e^{-uy}(x-y)^{j-1}dy

\displaystyle\leq e^{-ux}C+C_{2}(m,k,u)C\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}x^{-k}\int_{0}^{x}y^{k}(x-y)^{j-1}e^{-uy}dy,

where

\displaystyle x^{-k}\int_{0}^{x}y^{k}(x-y)^{j-1}e^{-uy}dy=x^{j}\int_{0}^{x}x^{-1}(\frac{y}{x})^{k}(1-\frac{y}{x})^{j-1}e^{-uy}dy

\displaystyle=x^{j}e^{-ux}\int_{0}^{1}\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha)^{j-1}d\alpha=x^{j}e^{-ux}\frac{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(j)}{\Gamma(k+1-j)}.

Hence for
x\geq 1
and
f\in M

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[\re>0]}\Big|\frac{x^{-k}}{\Gamma(m+2)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{S_{t}^{\lambda,m+1}(f)(s)}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}u^{m+2}(x-t)^{k}e^{-ut}dt-\frac{R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s+u)}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-ux}(1+C_{3}(m,k,u)x^{m+1}),

which vanishes uniformly for all
f\in M
as
x\to\infty
. Additionally, following the proof of Lemma
4.14
and Lemma
4.5

the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^{-k}}{\Gamma(m+2)}\int_{0}^{x}

\displaystyle\frac{S_{t}^{\lambda,m+1}(f)(s)}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}u^{m+2}(x-t)^{k}e^{-ut}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{S_{t}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}u^{k+1}e^{-ut}dt=\frac{f(s+u)}{(1+|s|)^{\ell}},

exists uniformly on
[\re>0]
and
f\in M
, where the last equality holds by Theorem
2.9
(or more precisely Remark
2.11
). This proves the claim.
∎

The following corollary is an immediate consequence considering singleton sets.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
f\in H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
and
k>\ell\geq 0
. Then
for every
u>0

\text{$\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})=f(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})$ \,\,\,in $H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]$}\,.

In particular, for every
u>0

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{s\in[\re>u]}\Big|\frac{f(s)-R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)}{(1+s)^{\ell}}\Big|=0.

To check the ’in particular’ part fix
u>0
and let
s=w+u
, where
w\in[\re>0]
. Then for large
x
by the first part

\displaystyle\frac{|f(s)-R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)|}{|1+s|^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|f(u+w)-R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(u+w)|}{|1+w|^{\ell}}\frac{|1+w|^{\ell}}{|1+u+w|^{\ell}}\leq\varepsilon\frac{|1+u+w|^{\ell}}{|1+u+w|^{\ell}}=\varepsilon.

3.5.
Theorem 41 of M. Riesz revisted

Corollary
3.8
immediately implies the following reformulation
of an important result of M. Riesz published in
[

, Theorem 41]
. Actually, this result motivated much of the content of this article.

Corollary 3.9
.

If
f\in H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
, then for every
k>\ell\geq 0
and
s\in[\re>0]

f(s)=\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s).

Let us point out that the mentioned result of Riesz actually is formulated in terms of
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz
summability (recall the definition of
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz means of second kind from (

[

it reads as follows (using our terminology of having a Riesz germ).

Theorem 3.10
.

Let

be holomorphic with a
\lambda
-Riesz germ
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
.
Assume that there is
\ell\geq 0
such that

\forall\penalty\ \varepsilon>0\penalty\ \exists\penalty\ C>0\colon|f(s)|\leq C|s|^{\ell},\penalty\ \penalty\ s\in[\re>\varepsilon].

Then for every

and

Let us explain why Theorem
3.10
and Corollary
3.9
in fact are equivalent. Recall first, that by Theorem
2.8

every
\lambda
-Dirichlet series is
(\lambda,k
)-Riesz summable
on all half-planes
[\re>\sigma],\,\sigma>0

if and only if this series is
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable on all such half-planes.
So it is obvious that Theorem
3.10
is applicable to every
f\in H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
;
indeed, given
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\sup_{\re s>\varepsilon}\frac{|f(s)|}{|s|^{\ell}}\leq\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}\sup_{\re s>\varepsilon}\frac{(1+|s|)^{\ell}}{|s|^{\ell}}<\infty\,.

Conversely, if a holomorphic function
f
on
[\re>0]
has a
\lambda
-Riesz germ and satisfies (

f_{\varepsilon}=f(\varepsilon+\cdot)\in H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
for every

,
and consequently we may apply Corollary

.

The decision on why we focus on
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz means instead of
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz means is based on the integral representation

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s_{0})=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}x^{-k}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}f(s+s_{0})\frac{e^{xs}}{s^{1+k}}ds

from Theorem
2.13

of the
x
th Riesz means of first kind of a Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
in terms of its limit function

whereas by
[

for Riesz means of second kind we have

T_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s_{0})=\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}f(s+s_{0})\frac{\Gamma(s)e^{xs}}{\Gamma(1+k+s)}ds.

In fact, we believe that the handling of the kernels
e^{xs}/s^{1+k}
is more simple than that of the kernels
\Gamma(s)e^{xs}/\Gamma(1+k+s)
.

3.6.
The bounded case

For

3.7.
Boundedness of coefficient functionals

We explained above that every function in

is uniquely determined by its

Theorem 3.12

\|\mathcal{C}_{N}\|\leq C(k,\ell,\lambda_{1})\left(\big(\frac{\lambda_{N+1}}{\lambda_{N+1}-\lambda_{N}}\big)^{k}+\big(\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{N-1}}\big)^{k}\right)\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ N\in\mathbb{N}.

Since

Lemma 3.13

Let

and

Proof.

From
[

we know that for every

\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{M}a_{\lambda_{n}}(f)\Big|\leq 3\big(\frac{\lambda_{M+1}}{\lambda_{M+1}-\lambda_{M}}\big)^{k}\sup_{\lambda_{1}<x<\lambda_{M+1}}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{\lambda_{n}}(f)(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x})^{k}\Big|.

Then the conclusion follows from Theorem

∎

3.8.
A Montel theorem

According to

The aim now is to extend this result from

to

Recall from (

The important special case

was first proved by Bayart in
[
1
]
, and within the setting of Hardy space of general Dirichlet series it was substantially extended in
[
8
]
,
[

]
, and
[

Theorem 3.14

\text{$f_{N_{j}}(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})\to f(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})$ \,\,\,\,in \,\,$H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]$}\,.

Theorem 3.15

be a bounded sequence in

\text{$f_{N}(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})\to f(u+\boldsymbol{\cdot})$ \,\,\,in\,\, $H_{\infty,\ell}^{\lambda}[\re>0]$}\,.

3.9.
Completeness

As we have already announced several times, all spaces

Theorem 3.16
.

For each
\ell\geq 0

Proof.

Given a Cauchy sequence
(f_{N})
, Theorem

proves the existence of a subsequence
(N_{j})
and

Hence for large

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{f(s)-f_{N}(s)}{(1+s)^{\ell}}\Big|\leq\Big|\frac{f(s)-f_{N_{j}}(s)}{(1+s)^{\ell}}\Big|+\|f_{N_{j}}-f_{N}\|_{\infty,\ell}\leq|f(s)-f_{N_{j}}(s)|+\varepsilon,

∎

3.10.
Behavior far left

The following result shows that the norm of any
f\in H^{\lambda}_{\infty,\ell}[\re>0]
determines
the growth of
f
on vertical abscissas close to the imaginary axis.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
f\in H^{\lambda}_{\infty,\ell}[\re>0]
with
\ell\geq 0
. Then

\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}=\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\frac{f(\sigma+it)}{(1+(\sigma+it))^{\ell}}\Big|\,.

The proof needs a well-known Phragmen-Lindelöf type lemma (see e.g.
[
3
, p. 138]

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
g
be a bounded function on the strip
[\sigma_{1}\leq\re\leq\sigma_{1}]
, which is holomorphic on

[\sigma_{1}<\re<\sigma_{1}]
. Then the function

L_{g}(\sigma)=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|g(\sigma+it)\big|\,,\,\,\,\sigma\in[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]\,.

is logarithmically convex on
[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]
, i.e.
\log L_{g}(\sigma)
is convex on
[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]

Proof of Theorem
3.17
.

Define the function

g(s)=\frac{f(s+\varepsilon)}{(1+s)^{\ell}}\,,

and for each

g_{x}(s)=\frac{\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\big)^{k}e^{-s\lambda_{n}}}{(1+s)^{\ell}}=\frac{R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(s)}{(1+s)^{\ell}}\,,

which are both obviously holomorphic and bounded on
[\re>0]
.
Note now that
L_{g_{x}}(\sigma)
for each
x>0
is non-increasing for
\sigma>0
; indeed,
by Lemma
3.18
we know that
\log L_{g_{x}}(\sigma)
is convex and moreover
\log L_{g_{x}}(\infty)=-\infty
.
But since Corollary
3.8
implies that for every

g_{x}\to g\,\,\,\text{ uniformly on $[\re>u]$ as $x\to\infty$}\,,

we see that
L_{g}(\sigma)
is non-increasing for
\sigma>0
. This gives

\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}=\sup_{\sigma>0}\big|L_{g_{x}}(\sigma)\big|=\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\big|L_{g_{x}}(\sigma)\big|\,,

which is exactly what we were looking for.
∎

3.11.
Behavior far right

In contrast to what we did in Section

3.13.
Equivalence

Recall that
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
converge on half-planes in the complex plane, where they define holomorphic functions, and in general the largest possible half-planes of convergence, uniform convergence, or absolute convergence differ.

In the early days of the theory, a very prominent research problem was to characterize the class of those frequencies
\lambda
for which boundedness of the limit function of
D
on
[\re>0]

implies uniform convergence of
D
on every smaller half plane
[\re>\varepsilon]
. In other words, if
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
stands for all
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
that on
[\re>0]
converges to a bounded
(holomorphic) function, then the question is whether all these series even converges uniformly on all smaller half-planes
[\re>\varepsilon]
.

Bohr was the first who in
[
5
]
managed to isolate a prominent class of such
\lambda
’s, namely those for which there is some
\beta>0
such that

(BC)

\frac{1}{\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}}=O(e^{\beta\lambda_{n}})\,;

slightly more precise, we in this case say that
\lambda
satisfies (
BC
) with constant
\beta
.

We say that ’Bohr’s theorem holds for
\lambda
’, whenever every
D\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
converges uniformly on
[\re>\varepsilon]
for every
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, frequencies
\lambda
with (
BC
) satisfy Bohr’s theorem.
Later this result was extended by Landau in
[
18
]

assuming the less restrictive assumption

(LC)

\forall\penalty\ \delta>0\penalty\ \exists\penalty\ C>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ n\colon\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\geq Ce^{-e^{\delta\lambda_{n}}}\,,

and very recently, Bayart in
[
2
]
added a further interesting condition, which provides a nontrivial extension of (
LC
): A frequency

\lambda
satisfies
(NC)
if

(NC)

In this context, an important recent achievement is that the property ’
\lambda
satisfies Bohr’s theorem’ in fact
is equivalent to a number of facts which seem absolutely unavoidable for a reasonable abstract theory
of general Dirichlet series. The following so-called ’equivalence theorem’ is taken from
[

[

[

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\lambda
satisfies Bohr’s theorem.

(2)

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is a Banach space.

(3)

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(\lambda)
isometrically and coefficient preserving.

(4)

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=H_{\infty}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
isometrically and coefficient preserving.

(5)

\lambda
satisfies Montel’s theorem in
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
.

Let us take some time to explain this result in more detail.
Note first that
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)

endowed with the sup norm
\|D\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\re>0}|f(s)|
, where
f
denotes the limit function of
D
, forms a normed space. But unfortunately
this space in general is not complete, and so the theorem ensures that this only holds true if
\lambda
satisfies Bohr’s theorem – in particular under each of the conditions of Bayart, Bohr, or Landau.

To see a non-trivial example,
(\sqrt{\log n})
doesn’t satisfy
(BC)
but
(LC)
. Hence, Bohr’s theorem holds for
(\sqrt{\log n})
, and so
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((\sqrt{\log n}))
forms a Banach space – a seemingly non-trivial fact.

An important step is to understand that the Banach space

H_{\infty}^{\lambda}[\re>0]
always contains the (in general non-complete) space
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
isometrically
(see
[
20
, Proposition 3.4]
). Hence both spaces in general differ, but in view of the theorem they coincide iff Bohr’s theorem holds true for
\lambda
.

Moreover, as already mentioned in Section
3.8
, Bayart proved a ’Montel type theorem’ for
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((\log n))=\mathcal{H}_{\infty}((\log n))
, which turned out to be a corner stone of the ordinary theory. The
equivalence theorem shows that the analog result holds true for
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)

if and only if
\lambda
satisfies Bohr’s theorem.

The idea now is to extend parts of Theorem
3.23
to the new setting.
Given a frequency
\lambda
and
\ell\geq 0
, let

denote the space of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet series

(\lambda,\ell)
-Riesz summable on all of

and have limit functions

. Together with the norm
\|D\|_{\infty,\ell}=\|f\|_{\infty,\ell}

of normed spaces of
\lambda
-Dirichlet series.

Recall again from Corollary
3.9
that the
\lambda
-Riesz germ of each

is
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable for each

even holds for

As mentioned above,

is always an isometric subspace
of

Together with Corollary

settles the case

For

Let

Lemma 4.1

Let

4.2.

Given a frequency

We begin collecting a few basic fact about the classical Gamma function given by

Recall that for every

Moreover, the Beta function is given by

This shows that for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}y^{p}(x-y)^{q-p-1}dy

\displaystyle=x^{q}\int_{0}^{x}x^{-1}(\frac{y}{x})^{p}(1-\frac{y}{x})^{q-p-1}dy

\displaystyle=x^{q}\int_{0}^{1}\alpha^{p}(1-\alpha)^{q-p-1}d\alpha=x^{q}\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(q-p)}{\Gamma(q+1)}.

We several times need (

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{x}e^{-wt}(t-y)^{c}(x-t)^{d}dt

\displaystyle=(x-y)^{c+d+1}e^{-wy}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-w\beta(x-y)}\beta^{c}(1-\beta)^{d}d\beta;

indeed,

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{x}e^{-wt}(t-y)^{c}(x-t)^{d}dt

\displaystyle=(x-y)^{c+d+1}e^{-wy}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-w\beta(x-y)}\beta^{c}(1-\beta)^{d}d\beta.

Lemma 4.4

Let

\lambda

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}S_{u}^{\lambda,k}(C)(x-u)^{\mu-1}du

Lemma 4.5

Lemma 4.7

(39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial^{m+2}h_{-s_{0}}(t)=s_{0}^{m+2}e^{s_{0}t}(x-t)^{k}&+\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}s_{0}^{j}e^{s_{0}t}c_{k,j}(x-t)^{k-(m+2-j)}(t)\\

|e^{s_{0}t}-e^{s_{0}x}|\leq|e^{\sigma t}-e^{\sigma x}|\frac{|s_{0}|}{\sigma}\leq(x-t)\sigma e^{\sigma t}\frac{|s_{0}|}{\sigma}=(x-t)e^{\sigma t}|s_{0}|\,,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}y^{m+1}(1-y)^{k-(m+2-j)}dy=\frac{\Gamma(m+2)\Gamma(k+j-m-1)}{\Gamma(k+j+1)}.

Lemma 4.9

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{y}^{x}(t-y)^{m-k}\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\binom{m+2}{j}(-w)^{j}e^{-wt}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2-j}c_{\ell}(k)(e^{x}-e^{t})^{k-\ell}e^{\ell t}dt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\binom{m+2}{j}|w|^{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2-j}c_{\ell}(k)\int_{y}^{x}(t-y)^{m-k}(x-t)^{k-\ell}e^{(k-\ell)x}e^{-\sigma t}e^{\ell t}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\binom{m+2}{j}|w|^{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2-j}c_{\ell}(k)\int_{y}^{x}(t-y)^{m-k}(x-t)^{k-\ell}e^{(k-\ell)x}e^{-\sigma t}e^{(1-\varepsilon)\ell t}e^{\varepsilon\ell t}dt

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\binom{m+2}{j}|w|^{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2-j}c_{\ell}(k)e^{(k-\varepsilon\ell)x}e^{-(\sigma-\varepsilon\ell)y}(x-y)^{m-\ell+1}c(k)\,,

where

c(k)=\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{m-k}(1-\beta)^{m-\ell+1}d\beta=\frac{\Gamma(m-k+1)\Gamma(m-\ell+2)}{\Gamma(2(m+1)-k-\ell)}\,.

And similarly, using (
43
), we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{y}^{x}(t-y)^{m-k}g_{2}(t)dt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{y}^{x}(t-y)^{m-k}(x-t)e^{-\sigma t}|w|\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2}(x-t)^{k-\ell}e^{(k-\ell)x}e^{\ell t}dt

\displaystyle=|w|\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2}\int_{y}^{x}(t-y)^{m-k}(x-t)^{k-\ell+1}e^{(k-\ell)x}e^{-\sigma t}e^{\ell t}dt

\displaystyle\leq|w|\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2}e^{(k-\varepsilon\ell)x}\int_{y}^{x}(t-y)^{m-k}(x-t)^{k-\ell+1}e^{-(\sigma-\varepsilon)\ell)t}dt

\displaystyle\leq|w|\sum_{\ell=1}^{m+2}e^{(k-\varepsilon\ell)x}(x-y)^{m+2-\ell}e^{-(\sigma-\varepsilon\ell)y}c(k)\,.

Implementing the preceding two estimates into (
54
), gives exactly what we were aiming at.

k=m+1
. This case follows similarly – but it is more simple.
We start replacing (
54
) by (
34
), implement the derivatives of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
from (
55
), and finish as above (using again the mean value theorem, the estimate (

), and the splitting

e^{\ell t}=e^{(1-\varepsilon)\ell t}e^{\varepsilon\ell t}
).
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
,
k,q>0
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with

.
Then for all

and
\varepsilon>0

\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_{0}^{x}S_{t}^{\lambda,q}(D)(s)\Big(1-\frac{e^{t}}{e^{x}}\Big)^{k}w^{q+1}e^{-wt}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{y}^{\lambda,q}(D)(s)w^{q+1}e^{-wy}dy\,.

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
s=0
. In order to prove (
56
), we fix
w
and
\varepsilon
, and put
\sigma=\re w-\varepsilon
. Then for all

\displaystyle\Big|S_{t}^{\lambda,q}(D)(0)\Big(1-\frac{e^{t}}{e^{x}}\Big)^{k}w^{q+1}e^{-wt}\chi_{[0,x]}(t)\Big|\leq C(q,\varepsilon)|w|^{q+1}e^{-\sigma t}t^{q}\,,

and hence (
56
) is an immediate consequence of the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

The final argument for Theorem

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Let
k>0
and

m<k\leq m+1
. If

(\lambda,k)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{e^{-kx}}{\Gamma(m+2)}

An Escape from Vardanyan’s Theorem
Ana de Almeida Borges, Joost J. Joosten

Abstract
Vardanyan’s Theorems [ 36 , 37 ] state that 𝖰𝖯𝖫 ⁡ ( 𝖯𝖠 ) \mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA}) – the quantified provability logic of Peano Arithmetic – is Π 2 0 \Pi^{0}_{2} complete, and in particular that this already holds when the language is restricted to a single unary predicate. Moreover, Visser and de Jonge [ 38 ] generalized this result to conclude that it is impossible to computably axiomatize the quantified provability logic of a wide class of theories. However, the proof of this fact cannot be performed in a strictly positive signature. The system 𝖰𝖱𝖢 𝟣 \mathsf{QRC_{1}} was previously introduced by the authors [ 1 ] as a candidate first-order provability logic. Here we generalize the previously available Kripke soundness and completeness proofs, obtaining constant domain completeness. Then we show that 𝖰𝖱𝖢 𝟣 \mathsf{QRC_{1}} is indeed complete with respect to arithmetical semantics. This is achieved via a Solovay-type construction applied to constant domain Kripke models. As corollaries, we see that 𝖰𝖱𝖢 𝟣 \mathsf{QRC_{1}} is the strictly positive fragment of 𝖰𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{QGL} and a fragment of 𝖰𝖯𝖫 ⁡ ( 𝖯𝖠 ) \mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA}) .



Keywords:

Modal logic,
provability logic,
strictly positive logics,
quantified modal logic,
arithmetic interpretations,
feasible fragments.

Introduction

Provability is a fundamental concept in mathematics, logic and philosophy alike. Gödel proved his famous incompleteness results in
[
22

by formalizing provability. Thus, for formal theories like Peano Arithmetic (
\mathsf{PA}

\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}(\cdot)
that is true exactly for the Gödel numbers of
\mathsf{PA}

\mathsf{PA}\vdash A\iff\mathbb{N}\vDash\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}(\ulcorner A\urcorner).

(1)

For readability we shall not distinguish formulas from their Gödel numbers or from syntactic terms (numerals) denoting these Gödel numbers in the future.

Gödel observed various provable structural properties of the provability predicate. For example, for any formula
A

\mathsf{PA}\vdash A
then
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}A
. Moreover, this can be formalized itself: for any formula

\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}A\to\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}A

can neither be proved nor refuted in
\mathsf{PA}
, provided that
\mathsf{PA}
only proves true theorems.

In light of this, it makes sense to design a system that collects all provable structural properties of the provability predicate. The language
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
of propositional modal logic is optimally suited for this purpose. The formulas of this language are as the ones of propositional logic together with a unary modality
\Box
that is syntactically treated as negation. Thus we can write
{\sf Form}_{\Box}::=\bot\mid{\sf Prop}\mid{\sf Form}_{\Box}\to{\sf Form}_{\Box}\mid\Box{\sf Form}_{\Box}

\mathsf{Prop}
represents propositional symbols.

The modal logical formulas are linked to arithmetic using so-called

maps propositional variables to sentences in the language of
\mathsf{PA}
and this map is extended to any formula by stipulating that it commutes with implication,
\bot
is mapped to
(0=1)
, and the modal operator is mapped to formalized provability, i.e.
(\Box A)^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}=\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}A^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}
.

We can now express what it means to be a provable structural property. For example, for any realization

Strictly positive logics

A key feature of our escape is given by restricting the language. Given variables
x,x_{i},\ldots
and a signature
\Sigma
fixing the constants
c,c_{i},\ldots
and relation symbols
S,S_{i},\ldots
, the formulas of
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
are built up from
\top
,
n
-ary relation symbols applied to
n
terms (which are either variables or constants), the binary
\land
, the unary
\Diamond
and the quantifier
\forall x
. The provable judgments in
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
are all of the form
\varphi\vdash\psi
with
\varphi
and
\psi
in the above language.

Our formulas are related to arithmetic through realizations
\cdot^{\circ}
that map
n
-ary predicate symbols to c.e. axiomatizations of theories indexed by
n
parameters (details can be found in Section
7
). Given a
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
axiomatization
\tau
of some theory
T
, we extend
\cdot^{\circ}
to non-predicate formulas such that conjunctions are interpreted as the unions of the corresponding axiom sets (
(\varphi\land\psi)^{\circ_{\tau}}:=\varphi^{\circ_{\tau}}\lor\psi^{\circ_{\tau}}
), and universal quantification corresponds to an infinite union (
(\Foralla x\mskip 3.0mu\varphi)^{\circ_{\tau}}:=\Existsa y\mskip 3.0mu\varphi^{\circ_{\tau}}
, where
y
is the arithmetical variable corresponding to the modal variable
x
).

This interpretation is common in the study of reflection calculi
[
7
]
and is more general than the usual, finitary one, mentioned in the previous section as
\cdot^{*}
. In the latter case, conjunctions of modal formulas are simply interpreted as conjunctions of their arithmetical counterparts. We further define and make use of the finitary notion in Section
8
.
While it is possible to represent finite extensions of a base theory through the finitary
\cdot^{*}
-style realizations, the
\cdot^{\circ}
approach allows for the possibility of infinitary extensions.

Using this restricted fragment and infinitary arithmetical interpretation, we define the
Strictly Positive Quantified Provability Logic
of a theory
T
as follows.

\mathsf{QPL}^{\sf SP}(T):=\{\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle\mid\Foralla\cdot^{\circ}\mskip 3.0muT\vdash\Foralla\theta\mskip 3.0mu(\Box_{\psi^{\circ_{\tau}}}\theta\to\Box_{\varphi^{\circ_{\tau}}}\theta)\}.

The main result of this paper is that, for a large class of theories
T
, the logic
\mathsf{QPL}^{\sf SP}(T)
is decidable and given by the system
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
as introduced in
[
1
]
. As such our paper was inspired by and contributes to three recent developments in the literature: strictly positive logics, reflection calculi, and polymodal logics.

The quintessential polymodal provability logic is
\mathsf{GLP}
, introduced by Japaridze in 1986
[
26
]
.
The Reflection Calculus,
\mathsf{RC}
, was first introduced by Dashkov
[
16
]
as the set of

-equivalences between strictly positive formulas. It was then axiomatized by Beklemishev
[
6

, and it is the latter formulation that appears in most of the ensuing literature.

Even though

has a strictly positive language, it is remarkably expressive, giving rise to an ordinal notation system
[
19
]
and being an appropriate tool for
\Pi^{0}_{1}

[
9
]

This is remarkable because, while

is

[

Thus, at least in the case of

\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
was inspired by
\mathsf{RC}_{1}
, which is the unimodal fragment of

However, the main expressibility tool available in

5
Constant domain completeness

In
[
1
]
we proved relational completeness by building a term model that satisfies
\varphi
and doesn’t satisfy
\psi
when
\varphi\not\vdash\psi
. That construction provides a finite, irreflexive, and rooted model with increasing domains. Here we show that it is possible to build a constant domain model instead, that is, a model where the domain of every world is exactly the same. This is extremely useful to prove the arithmetical completeness theorem in Section
8
.

Before starting the formal proof, we briefly describe the main idea. The term models we build are such that each world is a pair of sets of closed formulas
p=\langle p^{+},p^{-}\rangle
. The first set,
p^{+}
, is the set of formulas that will be satisfied at that world, or the

. The second set,
p^{-}
, is the set of formulas that will not be satisfied at that world, or the
negative part
. All worlds must be well-formed with respect to some set of closed formulas
\Phi
, which means that:

•

p
is closed: every formula in
p^{+}
and every formula in
p^{-}
is closed;

•

p
is
\Phi
-maximal: every formula of
\Phi
is in either
p^{+}
or
p^{-}
(and there are no formulas in

but not in
\Phi
);

•

p
is consistent: if
\delta\in p^{-}
then
\bigwedge p^{+}\not\vdash\delta

•

is fully-witnessed: if
\Foralla x\mskip 3.0mu\varphi\in p^{-}
then there is a constant

such that
\varphi[x{\leftarrow}c]\in p^{-}
.

In that case we say that

is
\Phi
-maximal consistent and fully witnessed, or
\Phi
-MCW for short (the closeness condition is included in the concept, although in practice almost every formula in this section will be closed). If

and

are pairs, we write

when

and

instead of
p^{+}\cup p^{-}\subseteq\Gamma
.

We want to have
\Phi
-MCW pairs where
\Phi
is closed under subformulas. However, the naive subformulas of
\Foralla x\mskip 3.0mu\varphi
might be open. In order to avoid that, we use the notion of closure with respect to a set of constants

defined in
[
1
]

is a valid subformula of

Definition 5.1

(\leftarrow)

The

(\to)

If

A decidable fragment of
\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA})

As we mentioned before, Vardanyan’s results are very robust and
\Pi^{0}_{2}
completeness can already be obtained for the language with just one unary predicate symbol and no nested occurrences of
\Box
, as shown in
[
37
]
. However, Artemov and Japaridze
[
4
]
managed to carve out a non-trivial decidable fragment of
\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA})
: all formulas that are decidable on finite Kripke frames correspond directly to a fragment of
\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA})
.
They further observed that as a corollary one can conclude the decidability of the one variable fragment of
\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA})
.

The above may seem contradictory with Vardanyan’s result on
\Pi^{0}_{2}
completeness of the fragment of
\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA})
with just one unary predicate symbol. However, although it is easy to see that in predicate logic any sentence with just one unary predicate is equivalent to one in the one-variable fragment, this does not hold when modalities are involved, as exhibited by the formula
\Foralla x\mskip 3.0mu\Foralla y\mskip 3.0mu\Box(P(x)\lor\neg P(y))
.

In this section we shall see that
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
gives rise to a new decidable fragment of
\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA})
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\Box,\forall}
be the language of full quantified modal logic, based on the same signatures as the language of
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
and extending the latter with
\to
,
\bot
and the usual abbreviations. We extend the notion of finite arithmetical realization to
\mathcal{L}_{\Box,\forall}
as follows:

•

\bot^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}:=\bot
;

•

(A\to B)^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}:=A^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to B^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}
.

We now define the quantified provability logic of
\mathsf{PA}
as the set of always provable
\mathcal{L}_{\Box,\forall}
formulas:

\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA}):=\{A(\bm{x},\bm{c})\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box,\forall}\mid\text{for any }\cdot^{*},\text{ we have }\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0muA^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\},

where
\bm{y}
are the arithmetical counterpart of
\bm{x}
and
\bm{z}
the arithmetical counterpart of
\bm{c}
, as before.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
formulas. Then

\varphi\vdash\psi\iff\varphi\to\psi\in\mathsf{QPL}(\mathsf{PA}).

Proof.

We begin with the left-to-right implication, which we prove by induction on the proof of
\varphi\vdash\psi
.

Consider
\varphi\vdash\top
with
\bm{x},\bm{c}
the free variables and constants appearing in
\varphi
, respectively. Let
\cdot^{*}
be any realization. We wish to show that
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\top)
, which is clearly the case.
We are equally easily convinced that
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
and that
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{y^{\prime}},\bm{z},\bm{z^{\prime}}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\land\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
(where
\bm{x^{\prime}}
and
\bm{c^{\prime}}
are the variables and constants of
\psi
).

Let
\bm{x}
and
\bm{c}
be the constants appearing in either
\varphi
,
\psi
, or
\chi
.

The conjunction introduction rule states that from
\varphi\vdash\psi
and
\varphi\vdash\chi
we can obtain
\varphi\vdash\psi\land\chi
. Let
\cdot^{*}
be any realization. We wish to prove
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\land\chi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. By the induction hypothesis we know that
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
and
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\chi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
, which easily allows us to prove the desired goal.

The cut rule states that if
\varphi\vdash\psi
and
\psi\vdash\chi
, then
\varphi\vdash\chi
. It is enough to show
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\chi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. By the induction hypothesis, we know both
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
and
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\chi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. The result follows handily.

The necessitation rule states that if
\varphi\vdash\psi
, then
\Diamond\varphi\vdash\Diamond\psi
. We work towards showing
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\Diamond_{\mathsf{PA}}\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\Diamond_{\mathsf{PA}}\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
.
By the induction hypothesis, we know that
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. Taking this under the box, we obtain
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. This implies that
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu\Box_{\mathsf{PA}}(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
by the formalized converse Barcan Formula, which in turn implies our desired goal.

Consider now
\Diamond\Diamond\varphi\to\Diamond\varphi
. We wish to show
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\Diamond_{\mathsf{PA}}\Diamond_{\mathsf{PA}}\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\Diamond_{\mathsf{PA}}\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. This holds by provable
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
completeness.

We turn to the
\forall
-introduction rule on the right, that if
\varphi\vdash\psi
then
\varphi\vdash\Foralla x^{\prime}\mskip 3.0mu\psi
, as long as

. Let
\bm{x}
be the free variables appearing in either
\varphi
or
\Foralla x^{\prime}\mskip 3.0mu\psi
. We wish to show
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\Foralla y^{\prime}\mskip 3.0mu\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},y^{\prime},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
. The goal follows from the observation that

.

For the
\forall
-introduction rule on the left, that if
\varphi[x^{\prime}{\leftarrow}t]\vdash\psi
then
\Foralla x^{\prime}\mskip 3.0mu\varphi\vdash\psi
(
t
free for

in
\varphi
), we want to show
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\Foralla y^{\prime}\mskip 3.0mu\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})

Let

be the

is

and the induction hypothesis tells us that
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z},w\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}[y^{\prime}{\leftarrow}w]\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
, which readily implies the desired result.

For the term instantiation rule, that if
\varphi\vdash\psi
then
\varphi[x^{\prime}{\leftarrow}t]\vdash\psi[x^{\prime}{\leftarrow}t]
, let

be the
\mathsf{PA}
variable corresponding to

. We wish to show
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z},w\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}[y^{\prime}{\leftarrow}w]\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}[y^{\prime}{\leftarrow}w])
. The induction hypothesis is
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},y^{\prime},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})

Finally, consider the constant elimination rule, that if
\varphi[x^{\prime}{\leftarrow}c^{\prime}]\vdash\psi[x^{\prime}{\leftarrow}c^{\prime}]
then
\varphi\vdash\psi
, with

not appearing in either
\varphi
nor
\psi
.
We wish to show
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},y^{\prime},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}})
.
We know
\mathsf{PA}\vdash\Foralla\bm{y},\bm{z},z^{\prime}\mskip 3.0mu(\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}[y^{\prime}{\leftarrow}z^{\prime}]\to\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}[y^{\prime}{\leftarrow}z^{\prime}])
by the induction hypothesis. The result follows by renaming

to

in the induction hypothesis, noting that

does not appear in either
\varphi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}
nor
\psi^{*_{\mathsf{PA}}}

We turn now to the right-to-left implication, which we address by taking the contrapositive. Thus, let
\varphi
and

be strictly positive formulas with free variables

and constants

such that

. By Theorem

, we have

\mathsf{PA}\not\vdash(\varphi^{\circledast_{\mathsf{PA}}}\to\psi^{\circledast_{\mathsf{PA}}})[\bm{y}{\leftarrow}\bm{\lcorners g_{\varphi,\psi}(x)\rcorners}][\bm{z}{\leftarrow}\bm{\lcorners c^{I_{0}}\rcorners}],

∎

be arbitrary, where

\Box_{\Existsa y_{0}\mskip 3.0mu\psi^{\circ_{\eta}}}\theta

\Box_{\eta}(\Foralla i\in I\mskip 3.0mu\delta_{i}\to\theta)

Then for each

is an axiom of
\psi^{\circ_{\eta}}[y_{0}{\leftarrow}k_{i}]
, so in particular
\Box_{\psi^{\circ_{\eta}}[y_{0}{\leftarrow}k_{i}]}\delta_{i}
. We can use the induction hypothesis for each

\Box_{\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}}[y_{0}{\leftarrow}k_{i}]}\delta_{i}

, we also know that
y_{0}\not\in{\text{fv}}(\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}})
, and thus we obtain
\Foralla i\in I\mskip 3.0mu\Box_{\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}}}\delta_{i}
. We now use collection to obtain
\Box_{\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}}}\Foralla i\in I\mskip 3.0mu\delta_{i}

\Box_{\eta}(\Foralla i\in I\mskip 3.0mu\delta_{i}\to\theta)
, noting that
\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}}
extends
\mathsf{HA}
.

The
\forall
introduction on the left states that if
\varphi[x_{0}{\leftarrow}t]\vdash\psi
, then
\Foralla x_{0}\mskip 3.0mu\varphi\vdash\psi
. Let
w
be the arithmetical counterpart of
t
(so if
t
is

then

and if
t
is

then

). We have
\varphi[x_{0}{\leftarrow}t]^{\circ_{\eta}}=\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}}[y_{0}{\leftarrow}w]

\theta,\bm{y}
and
\bm{z}
be arbitrary, where
y_{0}
appears in
\bm{y}
if and only if

\psi

\Box_{\psi^{\circ_{\eta}}}\theta
. If
t

\varphi
or in
\psi

\bm{y}
and
\bm{z}

\Box_{\Existsa y_{0}\mskip 3.0mu\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}}}\theta

The term instantiation rule states that if
\varphi\vdash\psi

\varphi[x_{0}{\leftarrow}t]\vdash\psi[x_{0}{\leftarrow}t]

w

\mathsf{HA}\vdash\Foralla\theta,y_{0},\bm{y},\bm{z}\mskip 3.0mu(\Box_{\psi^{\circ_{\eta}}}\theta\to\Box_{\varphi^{\circ_{\eta}}}\theta)

The constant elimination rule states that if
c_{0}
does not appear in either
\varphi
nor
\psi
and

11
Future work

There are many unexplored paths surrounding
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
. It would be worthwhile to extend it to a polymodal language (in analogy with
\mathsf{RC}
, as in
[
6
]
), and to the positive setting (as in
[
17
,
13
]
). Whether this is possible without loosing decidability remains to be seen.
A hypothetical
\mathsf{QRC}_{\Lambda}
would presumably lead to some interesting applications to
\Pi^{0}_{1}
ordinal analysis and ordinal notation systems.

There are several proof theoretical properties of interest, such as interpolation, cut-free proofs, fixpoints, etc.
One could also strive for uniform completeness.

Moreover, the set of always true
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
sequents should be a productive avenue of study.

The completeness of
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
with respect to
\mathsf{HA}
remains an open question.

Finally, the current approximation for the computational complexity of
\mathsf{QRC_{1}}
is super-exponential space, since the canonical model grows quite large. A more dedicated study might lead to a significant reduction in complexity.

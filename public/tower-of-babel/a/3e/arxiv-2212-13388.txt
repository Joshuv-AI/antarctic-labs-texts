Superstrata on Orbifolded Backgrounds
Masaki Shigemori

Abstract




YITP-22-162

Some microstates of the Strominger-Vafa black hole are represented by
smooth horizonless geometries called superstrata. The standard
superstrata are deformations of
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
, but there are
also generalizations of superstrata on the orbifold
({\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3})/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. In this paper, we discuss aspects of such
orbifolded superstrata. We present a CFT perspective on the structure
of orbifolded superstrata, showing that they can be constructed in a

p
-covering space of the orbifold CFT just as the standard superstrata.
We also explicitly write down and study the geometry of the orbifolded
superstrata, focusing on the difference from the non-orbifold case,

p=1
. We discuss further generalization of superstrata to the ones on
a fractional spectral flow of
({\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3})/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
This generalization involves new fractional mode excitations of the CFT
side. We estimate the number of those generalized superstrata, and show
that their entropy is too small to account for the Strominger-Vafa
entropy. We will discuss some implications of this result, related to
the typical microstates of the black hole and the relevant supersymmetry
index.

In this paper, we will discuss various aspects of
superstrata on orbifolded
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
backgrounds.
First, we will explain the construction in CFT, showing that the
procedure of constructing superstrata – starting with chiral primaries and
acting on it with symmetry generators, which worked for the non-orbifold
case – can be extended to the orbifold case. More specifically, by going
to a covering space in the D1-D5 symmetric orbifold CFT, we can consider a
covering-space version of chiral primaries. By acting on them with a
covering-space version of symmetry generators, which are fractional
generators in the base space, we can generate descendants. Coherent
superpositions of those generalized descendants are the superstrata on

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. This procedure is completely in parallel with the bulk
where we construct superstrata in
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
, which is the covering space,
and then divide the solution by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
to go to the “base space”

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. In both CFT and the bulk, the generators in the covering
space become fractional generators in the real, base space, such as

L_{-{1\over p}},J^{+}_{-{1\over p}}
.

We write down the explicit form of some superstrata on the orbifold

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and analyze their geometries. As an example, we will
study the orbifold version of the so-called
(1,0,n)
superstratum,
which we call
(1,0,n)_{p}
. This has a long throat that starts deeper
inside the geometry than the non-orbifold version. The throat is
narrower than the non-orbifold version but its length is the same. At
the bottom of the throat, the geometry ends with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
conical
singularity. Because the form of the
(1,0,n)_{p}
geometry is as simple
as
(1,0,n)
, it would be interesting to study its physical properties,
focusing on the effect of
p>1
as compared to the
p=1

[

Furthermore, we generalize superstrata on the
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
background
to superstrata on the fractional spectral flow of
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, which
we call
(\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})_{[{s\over p}]}
,
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. These superstrata
now involve more general fractional modes such as
J^{+}_{-{1+2s\over p}}
. Actually, this background
(\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})_{[{s\over p}]}
is
nothing but the so-called GLMT geometry
[
23

, the most
general 2-center bubbled geometry. The original superstrata are
coherent supergravitons on a very special 2-center bubbled geometry, but
superstrata on
(\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})_{[{s\over p}]}
are coherent
supergravitons on the most general 2-center bubbled geometry.

Because we thus have generalized superstrata that involve various
fractional modes, it is natural to ask if their entropy reproduces the
Strominger-Vafa entropy. We will estimate the entropy of superstrata on

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, and find that its entropy is actually even smaller than
the entropy of the original superstrata. The fractional modes that
those generalized superstrata contain are very special ones and do not
lead to enhancement of entropy. However, they are expected to
contribute to supersymmetry indices. We will discuss some of its
interesting implications.

2.3
Spectral flow

CFTs with
{\cal N}\geq 2
supersymmetry has spectral flow symmetry, which
maps a state with
h,j
to a state with

\displaystyle h^{\prime}

\displaystyle=h+2\eta j+{c\over 6}\eta^{2},\qquad j^{\prime}=j+{c\over 6}\eta.

(2.8)

This is for the left-moving sector. We can do spectral flow
transformations in the right-moving sector, with an independent parameter

{\bar{\eta}}
.

We can interpret spectral flow in two ways. (i)
Passive:
By
spectral flow, we can look at the same state in different
sectors. Namely, it gives different viewpoints on the same state. For
example, we can take a state in the NS sector and flow it to a state in the R sector. (ii)
Active:
We
can use spectral flow to transform a state in some sector into a
different state in the same sector. For this to be possible, the
transformed state must be an allowed state in the sector that we are in.

As a concrete example of “passive” spectral flow, by taking take

\eta={\bar{\eta}}={\frac{1}{2}}
, we can map states in the NS-NS sector to ones in the
R-R sector. Anti-chiral primaries flow to RR ground states, and the
descendants such as (
2.6
) go to the RR state

\displaystyle\left[{|{++}\rangle}_{1}^{\rm R}\right]^{N_{0}}\prod_{k,m,n,q}\left[(J_{-1}^{+})^{m}(L_{-1}-J_{-1}^{3})^{n}\Bigl(G_{-1}^{+,1}G_{-1}^{+,2}+\tfrac{1}{2h^{\rm NS}}(L_{-1}-J^{3}_{-1})J_{-1}^{+}\Bigr)^{\!q\,}{|{00}\rangle}_{k}^{\rm R}\right]^{N_{kmnq}}

(2.9)

where the superscript “R” means Ramond sector states. It is such
Ramond states that are in direct correspondence with the superstrata in
the bulk.

2.4
The structure of state construction

There is a key aspect of the structure of the superstratum states
reviewed above
[
66
,
67
,
25
]
.

On each of the
N
copies of
{\cal M}
live the generators
of
SU(1,1|2)^{2}
, and the “total” generator is the sum of such
individual generators. For example,

\displaystyle L_{-1}=\sum_{r=1}^{N}(L_{-1})_{r},

(2.10)

where
r
is the copy number. When the copies are grouped into
strands, we can write this also as

\displaystyle L_{-1}=\sum_{C}(L_{-1})_{C},

(2.11)

where
C
is the strand number and
(L_{-1})_{C}
is the sum of

(L_{-1})_{r}
that belong to the
C
-th strand.

The ground state

\displaystyle({|{++}\rangle}_{1})^{N}

(2.12)

is annihilated by
L_{-1}
because each individual generator
(L_{-1})_{r}

kills the NS vacuum
{|{++}\rangle}_{1}
on the
r
th copy. This corresponds to
the
SU(1,1|2)^{2}
isometry of the dual
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
background. Namely, if

{|{\rm vac}\rangle}
is the bulk
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
vacuum, it is killed by the bulk
isometry generator:

\displaystyle L_{-1}{|{\rm vac}\rangle}=0.

(2.13)

Now consider including a one-particle anti-chiral primary that is not

{|{++}\rangle}_{1}
, for example,

\displaystyle{|{00}\rangle}_{k}\,({|{++}\rangle}_{1})^{N-k}.

(2.14)

If we act on this state with the total generator
L_{-1}
, all the
generators
(L_{-1})_{C}
vanish on

{|{++}\rangle}_{1}
, except for the
(L_{-1})_{C}
acting on the non-trivial
strand
{|{00}\rangle}_{k}
. Namely,

\displaystyle L_{-1}\Bigl[{|{00}\rangle}_{k}\,({|{++}\rangle}_{1})^{N-k}\Bigr]=(L_{-1}{|{00}\rangle}_{k})\,({|{++}\rangle}_{1})^{N-k}.

(2.15)

Here
L_{-1}{|{00}\rangle}_{k}
means
{|{00}\rangle}_{k}
acted upon by
(L_{-1})_{C}

that lives on that strand, but we omitted the label
C
.
The bulk dual statement is the following. If one excites one
supergraviton dual to
{|{00}\rangle}_{k}
in the empty
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
, the bulk state
is
a_{k}^{\dagger}{|{\rm vac}\rangle}
, where
a_{k}^{\dagger}
is the creation
operator of the supergraviton. If we act with the bulk isometry
generator on this state, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{-1}(a_{k}^{\dagger}{|{\rm vac}\rangle})=[L_{-1},a_{k}^{\dagger}]\,{|{\rm vac}\rangle},

(2.16)

because of (
2.13
). So, the total generator
L_{-1}
acts
only on the supergraviton, changing its wavefunction according to the
particle’s
SU(1,1|2)
representation.

So, what we are doing in (
2.5
)–(
2.6
) can
be stated as follows. Consider one supergraviton in the
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
vacuum,
represented by an anti-chiral primary
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{k}
. Then we act upon
the state with the total generators to generate descendants like

(J_{0}^{-})^{m}(L_{-1})^{n}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{k}
. Note that, in the bulk, the

only
available generators are total generators — they are
isometries. We simply do not know the bulk dual of individual generators
in CFT. However, when there is only one particle is present, the
total generators act only of the strand. If we have single-particle
states such as
(J_{0}^{-})^{m}(L_{-1})^{n}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{k}
, we can start exciting
many such particles – even a macroscopic number of them. They are
superstrata.

2.5
The orbifold background
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

The orbifold
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
geometry plays an important role in the
current paper. The construction of the bulk
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
geometry
starts with the Lunin-Mathur geometry
[
68
,
69
,
70
,
71
]
with a circular,
p
-wound profile
function (see Appendix
A.1.1
). The metric of the
Lunin-Mathur geometry is

\displaystyle ds_{6E}^{2}={\Sigma\over\sqrt{Q_{1}Q_{5}}}\Bigl[-(dt+{pR_{y}a^{2}\over\Sigma}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi)^{2}+(dy-{pR_{y}a^{2}\over\Sigma}\cos^{2}\theta\,d\psi)^{2}\Bigr]+{\sqrt{Q_{1}Q_{5}}\over\Sigma}ds^{2}({\cal B})

(2.17)

where
ds^{2}({\cal B}),\Sigma
are defined in
(
A.5
), and the parameters

Q_{1},Q_{5},a,p,R_{y}
are related to each other by (
A.15
). If we
write this in terms of the new variables

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\phi}}=\phi-{t\over pR_{y}},\qquad{\widetilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\psi-{y\over pR_{y}},

(2.18)

we obtain the six-dimensional metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{6}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{Q_{1}Q_{5}}\left(-{r^{2}+a^{2}\over a^{2}(pR_{y})^{2}}dt^{2}+{r^{2}\over a^{2}(pR_{y})^{2}}dy^{2}+{dr^{2}\over r^{2}+a^{2}}+d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d{\widetilde{\phi}}^{2}+\cos^{2}\theta\,d{\widetilde{\psi}}^{2}\right)

(2.19)

which is manifestly locally
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
with radius
{\cal R}=(Q_{1}Q_{5})^{1/4}
.
The periodic identifications of the original variables,

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi,y)\cong(\phi+2\pi,\psi,y)\cong(\phi,\psi+2\pi,y)\cong(\phi,\psi,y+2\pi R_{y}),

(2.20)

means the following identifications for the new variables,

\displaystyle({\widetilde{\phi}},{\widetilde{\psi}},y)\cong({\widetilde{\phi}}+2\pi,{\widetilde{\psi}},y)\cong({\widetilde{\phi}},{\widetilde{\psi}}+2\pi,y)\cong\left({\widetilde{\phi}},{\widetilde{\psi}}-{2\pi\over p},y+2\pi R_{y}\right).

(2.21)

So, the metric (
2.19
) represents a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
orbifold
of
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
space. If
p=1
, the space (
2.19
) is the
non-orbifold
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
. The
p>1
case can be obtained from the

p=1
case by the replacement
R_{y}\to pR_{y}
, which amounts to dividing
the radius of the
y
circle by
p
.

The non-orbifold
{\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}
has the isometry group
SO(2,2)\times SO(4)\cong SL(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{L}\times SL(2)_{R}\times SU(2)_{R}
. If we take
the Killing vectors for this isometry group and set
R_{y}\to pR_{y}
, we
find the following “Killing vectors” in the orbifold
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{0}&={ipR_{y}\over 2}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{y}),\\
L_{\pm{1\over p}}&=ie^{\pm{i\over pR_{y}}(t+y)}\biggl[-{pR_{y}\over 2}\biggl({r\over\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}}\partial_{t}+{\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}\over r}\partial_{y}\biggr)\pm{i\over 2}\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}\,\partial_{r}\biggr],\\
J_{0}^{3}&=-{i\over 2}(\partial_{{\widetilde{\phi}}}+\partial_{{\widetilde{\psi}}}),\quad J_{0}^{\pm}={i\over 2}e^{\pm i({\widetilde{\phi}}+{\widetilde{\psi}})}(\mp i\partial_{\theta}+\cot\theta\,\partial_{{\widetilde{\phi}}}-\tan\theta\,\partial_{{\widetilde{\psi}}}),\end{split}

(2.22)

along with right-moving counterparts. These satisfy the
sl(2)
and

su(2)
algebra relations

\displaystyle\begin{split}[L_{0},L_{\pm{1\over p}}]&=\mp L_{\pm{1\over p}},\qquad[L_{1\over p},L_{-{1\over p}}]=2L_{0},\\
[J^{3}_{0},J^{\pm}_{0}]&=\pm J^{\pm}_{0},\qquad[J^{+}_{0},J^{-}_{0}]=2J^{3}_{0}\end{split}

(2.23)

and leave invariant the local form of the metric. However, these are
not valid Killing vectors of
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, because they are not
single-valued under the last identification in (
2.21
).
They are single-valued only in the
p
-covering space where we make the
radius of the
y
-circle
p
times larger.

When there is a supergraviton in
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, if we act with

L_{-{1\over p}},J_{0}^{+}
on its wavefunction of a particle, the result
will not be single-valued, because of the multi-valuedness mentioned
above. However, if we act
n
times with
L_{-1}
and
m
times with

J_{0}^{+}
, then
L_{-{1\over p}}^{n}(J_{0}^{+})^{m}\propto\exp(-i{m+n\over p}{y\over R_{y}})
and the resulting wavefunction is single-valued if

{m+n\over p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. This suggests that, even for the orbifold

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, the procedure around (
2.16
) of
generating descendants goes through if we use the Killing vectors of the
covering space, (
2.22
). We will discuss the CFT counterpart
of this statement in section

3
.

2.6
Bulk spectral flow

The bulk version of spectral flow transformation discussed in section

2.3
is

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\phi}}

\displaystyle=\phi-(\eta+{\bar{\eta}}){t\over R_{y}}-(\eta-{\bar{\eta}}){y\over R_{y}},

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\psi-(\eta-{\bar{\eta}}){t\over R_{y}}-(\eta+{\bar{\eta}}){y\over R_{y}},

(2.24a)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\phi}}+{\widetilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\phi+\psi-2\eta\,{t+y\over R_{y}},

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\phi}}-{\widetilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle=\phi-\psi-2{\bar{\eta}}\,{t-y\over R_{y}}.

(2.24b)

The Lunin-Mathur geometries are in the RR sector. The CFT dual of the
geometry (
2.17
) is the RR ground state

\displaystyle\bigl(\,{|{++}\rangle}_{p}^{\rm R}\,\bigr)^{N\over p},\qquad h_{\rm R}={N\over 4},\qquad j_{\rm R}={N\over 2p}.

(2.25)

The transformation (
2.18
) to bring the geometry into
(
2.19
) is spectral flow with
\eta={\bar{\eta}}=-{1\over 2p}
. So, the solution (
2.19
) is not in the NS
sector (as we would get if we took
\eta={\bar{\eta}}=-{1\over 2}
) but is in a
different sector, which we dub the NS
′
sector. The dual CFT state is

\displaystyle\bigl(\,{|{++}\rangle}_{p}^{\rm NS^{\prime}}\,\bigr)^{N\over p},\qquad h_{\rm NS^{\prime}}={N\over 4}\left(1-{1\over p^{2}}\right),\qquad j_{\rm NS^{\prime}}=0.

(2.26)

In passing, we mention that, in the R coordinates
(\phi,\psi,y)
, the
generators (
2.22
) have

\displaystyle L_{\pm{1\over p}}\propto e^{\pm{i\over pR_{y}}(t+y)},\qquad J_{0}^{\pm}\propto e^{\pm i\left(\phi+\psi-{t+y\over pR_{y}}\right)}

(2.27)

So, the non-single-valuedness of those “Killing vectors” is related to
the fractional energy and
y
-momentum in the R sector.

The spectral flow transformation (
2.24
) discussed above is
“passive” since we take a particular solution and map it into a
solution in a different sector. The periodicity of the new variables

({\widetilde{\phi}},{\widetilde{\psi}},y)
is derived from and different from that of

(\phi,\psi,y)
. On the other hand, “active” spectral flow
transformations take a solution in a sector and transform it into a
different solution in the same sector. This is executed by replacing

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\to\phi-(\eta+\bar{\eta}){t\over R_{y}}-(\eta-\bar{\eta}){y\over R_{y}},

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\to\psi-(\eta-\bar{\eta}){t\over R_{y}}-(\eta+\bar{\eta}){y\over R_{y}}

(2.28)

in the solution, with the periodicity of
(\phi,\psi)
unchanged. If

\eta,{\bar{\eta}}
are integers, this transformation is an
SL(2,{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}

basis change of coordinate identifications. However, if
\eta,{\bar{\eta}}
are
rational numbers, as we do in section
5
,
this is an
SL(2,\mathbb{Q})^{2}
transformation that changes the physical
solution by changing the orbifold singularity structure.

3
CFT view on superstrata on
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

In the construction reviewed in section

This observation leads to an interesting view on the construction of

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

3.2
Lifting anti-chiral primaries to the covering space

The construction of supergraviton states was based on the following: if
we have an anti-chiral primary with
h=-j
in the NS sector, we can
generate the spectrum of the
SU(1,1|2)
multiplet by repeatedly acting
on it with the modes of
T,G,J
, namely
J^{+}_{0},G^{+,A}_{-1/2},L_{-1}
.
Here we show that, for an anti-chiral primary on a strand of length
kp
,
there is a different way to generate a different “spectrum”. Namely,
if we go to an “NS sector” in the covering
t
space, the state is an anti-chiral primary with respect to
{\widetilde{T}},{\widetilde{G}},{\widetilde{J}}
and we can generate
descendants by the action of their coefficients.

Let us start with a state with
(L^{(z)}_{0},J^{(z)}_{0})=(h,j)
in the
NS sector. Note that we are using the modes of the total currents,
(
3.15
), which define the standard charges of CFT. To be
very clear (although it’s a lot of scripts), we can display the sector

Gravity side

In the previous section, we explained in detail the CFT understanding of

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

will turn to the gravity aspects of superstrata on
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

will write down some simple examples of superstrata on
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

and analyze their geometries. Because the bulk superstrata are

4.1
The
(k,0,0)_{p}
superstratum

First, although this is not really a superstratum, consider the 1/4-BPS
state

\displaystyle({|{++}\rangle}_{p})^{N_{0}}({|{00}\rangle}_{kp})^{N^{00}_{kp}},\qquad N_{0}+kN^{00}_{kp}={N\over p}.

(4.1)

We can call this a
(k,0,0)_{p}
superstratum. The gravity solution dual

, and it is a
deformation of the
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
solution, (
2.19
),
by a parameter

We can write the 6D metric as

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{6E}

\displaystyle=G_{tt}dt^{2}+G_{yy}dy^{2}+G_{rr}dr^{2}+G_{\theta\theta}d\theta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+G_{\phi\phi}(d\phi+A^{\phi}_{t}dt)^{2}+G_{\psi\psi}(d\psi+A^{\psi}_{y}dy)^{2},

(4.2)

where

\displaystyle G_{tt}

\displaystyle=-{(r^{2}+a^{2})\Sigma\sqrt{f}\over pR_{y}((r^{2}+a^{2})f-a^{4}\sin^{2}\theta)},\quad G_{yy}={r^{2}\Sigma\sqrt{f}\over pR_{y}(r^{2}f+a^{4}\cos^{2}\theta)},\quad G_{rr}={pR_{y}\sqrt{f}\over r^{2}+a^{2}},

\displaystyle A_{\phi}

\displaystyle=-{a^{2}\Sigma\over pR_{y}((r^{2}+a^{2})f-a^{4}\sin^{2}\theta)},\quad A_{\psi}=-{a^{2}\Sigma\over pR_{y}(r^{2}f+a^{4}\cos^{2}\theta)},

(4.3)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\equiv a^{2}+{b^{2}\over 2}-{b^{2}a^{2k}\sin^{2k}\theta\over 2(r^{2}+a^{2})^{k}}.

Let us assume that
a\sim b
. At large distance

, we have

\displaystyle f\approx a^{2}+{b^{2}\over 2}={{\cal R}^{4}\over(pR_{y})^{2}},\qquad{\cal R}\equiv(Q_{1}Q_{5})^{1/4},

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle G_{yy}\approx{r^{2}\over{\cal R}^{2}},\qquad G_{tt}\approx-{r^{2}\over{\cal R}^{2}},\qquad G_{rr}\approx{{\cal R}^{2}\over r^{2}}.

(4.5)

So, the
(t,r,y)
part is AdS
3

The undeformed
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
singularity at

. In the present solution, near that point, we find
that

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}G_{yy}\approx{r^{2}\over(pR_{y})a},\quad G_{tt}\approx-{r^{2}\over(pR_{y})a},\quad G_{rr}\approx{pR_{y}\over a},\quad A_{\phi}\approx-{dt\over pR_{y}},\quad A_{\psi}\approx-{dy\over pR_{y}},\quad\end{gathered}

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

can see this by the spectral flow (

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
background.

More generally, we can see that superstratum fluctuations do not change

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
orbifold.

By setting

[

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{4}&=pR_{y}{\Delta_{kmn}\over\Sigma}e^{iv_{kmn}},\\
\Theta_{4}&=-\sqrt{2}\,\Delta_{kmn}\biggl[i\left(-(m+n)r\sin\theta+n\left(1-{m\over k}\right){\Sigma\over r\sin\theta}\right)\Omega^{(1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+m\left(1+{n\over k}\right)\Omega^{(2)}+\left({m\over k}-1\right)n\,\Omega^{(3)}\biggr]e^{iv_{kmn}^{\prime}}\end{split}

(4.7)

where

. The quantities

are defined

A.7
). We have the phase factor

\displaystyle e^{iv_{kmn}^{\prime}}=\exp\Bigl[i\Bigl((m+n){t+y\over pR_{y}}+(k-m)\phi-m\psi\Bigr)\Bigr].

(4.8)

For this to be single-valued as

To study the behavior of fields near the singularity

\displaystyle C_{0}={Z_{4}\over Z_{1}}\propto\Delta_{kmn}\,e^{iv_{kmn}^{\prime}}\sim r^{n}{\sin^{k-m}\!\theta\,\cos^{m}\!\theta}\,e^{iv_{kmn}}.

Because the

\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

6
Discussion

In this paper, we gave a CFT perspective on the construction of the
superstrata on the orbifold background
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and provided some
analysis of the explicit geometry in the bulk. We also discussed
generalization to superstrata on the fractional spectral flowed
background
(\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})_{[{s\over p}]}
.

Given these generalizations of superstratum solutions, which is known to
have a large entropy, one immediate question is what the entropy of
these new solutions is. In particular, because these generalized
superstrata involve fractional modes that have been argued to be
crucial for reproducing the Strominger-Vafa entropy, one naturally
wonders whether they reproduce the Strominger-Vafa entropy. The answer
turns out to be negative.

The problem of counting superstrata is roughly (ignoring angular
momentum and degeneracies coming from different species of anti-chiral
primaries based on which these solutions are built) the problem of
counting
\{N_{k,m,n}\}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{k,m,n}kN_{k,m,n}=N,\qquad\sum_{k,m,n}(m+n)N_{k,m,n}=N_{P}.

(6.1)

The resulting entropy is
[
60
,
61
]

\displaystyle S\sim N^{1/2}N_{P}^{1/4}.

(6.2)

The entropy for superstrata on
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
can roughly be evaluated
by counting
\{N_{k,m,n}\}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{k,m,n}kpN_{k,m,n}=N,\qquad\sum_{k,m,n}{m+n\over p}N_{k,m,n}=N_{P}.

(6.3)

The first one is because now all strand lengths are integer multiples of

p
, and the second one is because all momenta are now
1\over p
-moded. By moving
p
in (
6.3
)
to the right-hand side, one sees that this
changes the entropy (
6.2
) to

\displaystyle S\sim\left({N\over p}\right)^{1/2}(pN_{P})^{1/4}=p^{-1/4}N^{1/2}N_{P}^{1/4}.

(6.4)

Namely, fractionation reduces entropy. Having fractional momenta
enhances entropy, but that is more than canceled by the strand-length
budget getting tighter. To be more precise, the sum in
(
6.3
) must be restricted with the condition
{m+n\over p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, but that is a minor condition for large
N,N_{P}
and should
not change the estimate (
6.4
).

One can also consider the fractional spectral flow of superstrata, or
superstrata on GLMT solutions, discussed in section

5
. We have not estimated their entropy, one
technical issue being that states in different
(p,[{s\over p}])

sectors are not necessarily independent. However, but one extra parameter

(s)
is very unlikely to lead to a parametrically larger entropy.

The fact that superstrata on
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and GLMT backgrounds do not
have the Strominger-Vafa entropy has interesting implications. In

[
75
]
, it was shown that the supersymmetric microstate
geometries in six dimensions that contribute to supersymmetry indices
must have only one compact 3-cycle. However,
\mbox{${\rm AdS}_{3}\times S^{3}$}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and GLMT
backgrounds are the most general 3-cycles that can be written as
bubbling multi-center solutions. Therefore, unless there are more
exotic possibilities for 3-cycles in six dimensions and excitations on
them, the generalized superstrata considered in the current paper are
the most general microstate geometries that are counted by supersymmetry
indices. The fact that they do not reproduce the Strominger-Vafa
entropy likely means that six-dimensional microstate geometries are
simply not enough for reproducing the entropy. One either have to
consider stringy degrees of freedom (see
[
76
,
77
,
78
,
79
,
80
,
81
,
82
,
65
]
for recent
developments in the worldsheet theory for the relevant systems), or
remain in supergravity but look for microstate geometries in
d>6

dimensions, such as backreacted MSW M5-brane waves

[
73
]
, or more recently proposed super-maze

[
83
]
and hyperstrata
[
74
]
.

Based on the above argument, one naturally expects that generalized
superstrata considered in the current paper are the most general
supergravity solutions that contribute to supersymmetry indices. In

[
84
]
(see also
[
72
]
), it was shown
that the K3 elliptic genus for
L_{0}^{\rm NS}\leq{N+1\over 4}
can be
completely accounted for by supergravitons, i.e., superstrata. It is
possible that, above this bound and below the black-hole bound,

L_{0}^{\rm NS}={J^{2}\over 4N}+{N\over 4}
where new states come in, the
elliptic genus can be accounted for by counting the generalized
superstrata. It would be quite interesting to check if this is correct
or not. One technical obstacle is that states in different
(p,[{s\over p}])
sectors are not necessarily independent. Counting supergravitons
for different values of
p,s
separately would be overcounting. One
needs to develop technical tools to properly count the supergraviton
states on those backgrounds.

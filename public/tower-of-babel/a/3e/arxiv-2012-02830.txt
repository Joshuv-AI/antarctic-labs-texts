Simultaneous averaging to zero by unitary mixing operators
Abhinav Chand, Leonel Robert, Arindam Sutradhar

Abstract
. We show that if every element a vector subspace of a C*-algebra can be averaged to zero by means of unitary mixing operators, then all the elements of the subspace can be simultaneously averaged to zero by a net of unitary mixing operators. Moreover, such subspaces admit a simple description in terms of commutators and kernels of states on the C*-algebra. We apply this result to center-valued expectations in C*-algebras with the Dixmier property.



1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
U(A)
denote its unitary group. We call a linear operator
T\colon A\to A
a unitary mixing (or averaging) operator if it is a convex combination of inner automorphisms of
A
. That is,

T=\sum^{n}_{j=1}t_{j}\mathrm{Ad}_{u_{j}},

where
u_{j}\in U(A)
and
t_{j}\geqslant 0
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
,
\sum^{n}_{j=1}t_{j}=1
, and where
\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(x)=uxu^{*}
for all
x\in A
. There is a large literature, going back to Dixmier’s approximation theorem for von Neumann algebras, around the averaging of elements of a C*-algebra by means of unitary mixing operators. The question that we investigate here is that of simultaneously averaging a collection of elements of a C*-algebra towards the zero element.

Let
\mathrm{S}(A)
denote the states space of
A
. Let
\mathrm{Max}(A)
denote the maximal (two-sided) ideals space of
A
. We prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
V\subseteq A
be a linear subspace.
The following are equivalent

(1)

V\subseteq\overline{[A,A]}
and for each maximal ideal
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
there exists
\rho_{M}\in\mathrm{S}(A)
that factors through
A/M
and such that
V\subseteq\ker\rho_{M}
.

(2)

For each
v\in V
there exists a sequence of unitary mixing operators
(T_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that

T_{n}v\to 0
.

(3)

There exists a net of unitary mixing operators
(T_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
such that
T_{\lambda}v\to 0
for all
v\in V
.

It is not difficult to derive the equivalence of (i) and (ii) from
[
7
, Theorem 4.7]
. Our main contribution here is that (i) implies (iii). Observe that in (i), the selection of states
M\mapsto\rho_{M}
may be done only
on the maximal ideals
M
such that
A/M
has no bounded traces, for if
A/M
has a trace, then we are guaranteed the
existence of a
\rho_{M}
vanishing on
V
by the fact that
V\subseteq\overline{[A,A]}
.

Recall that a C*-algebra is said to have the Dixmier property if for each
a\in A
there exist unitary mixing operators
(T_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
T_{n}a\to b\in Z(A)
, where
Z(A)
denotes the center of
A
. The above mentioned approximation theorem of Dixmier states that every von Neumann algebra has the Dixmier property. This is not the case, however, for every C*-algebra (see
[
2
]
). As an immediate corollary of the previous theorem, we obtain the following:

Corollary 2
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra with the Dixmier property. Let
H\colon A\to Z(A)
be a
Z(A)
-linear, positive, unital map such that

(1)

\tau\circ H=\tau
for all bounded traces
\tau
on
A
,

(2)

H(M)\subseteq M\cap Z(A)
for all
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
.

Then there exists a net of unitary mixing operators
(T_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
such that
T_{\lambda}\to H
in the point-norm topology.

This result is possibly well known when
A
is a von Neumann algebra, although we are not aware of a reference for this case. A related theorem of Magajna for weakly central C*-algebras replaces unitary mixing operators by C*-convex combinations (
[
6
, Corollary 1.2]
). The special case of Corollary
2
when
A
is simple and traceless is obtained by Zsido in
[
8
]
. We rely on Zsido’s result to prove Theorem
1
above.

2.
Preliminaries on mixing operators and Dixmier sets

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the C*-algebra
A^{n}
, i.e., the direct sum of
n
copies of
A
. (The norm in
A^{n}
is the maximum norm:
\|\bm{a}\|=\max\|a_{j}\|
.) We refer to the elements of
A^{n}
as
n
-tuples. We regard
A
embedded in
A^{n}
as the constant
n
-tuples.

Let us denote by
\mathrm{Mix}(A)
the set of unitary mixing operators
T\colon A\to A
, as defined in the introduction.
We consider unitary mixing operators in
\mathrm{Mix}(A)
acting on
n
-tuples coordinatewise: given
T\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
and

\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
, we write

T(\bm{a})=(Ta_{1},\ldots,Ta_{n}).

Given an
n
-tuple
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
in
A^{n}
, let us define

\displaystyle D_{A}(\bm{a})=\overline{\{T\bm{a}:T\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)\}}^{\|\cdot\|},

which we call the Dixmier set generated by
\bm{a}
relative to
A
.

The next two lemmas are essentially obtained in
[
1
]
, but we establish them here in the form that will be needed later on.

Lemma 3

(Cf.
[
1
, Proposition 2.4]
)
.

Each operator in
\mathrm{Mix}(A)
is a limit in the point-norm topology of a net of unitary mixing operators whose unitaries are exponentials, i.e., operators of the form

(1)

\sum^{n}_{j=1}t_{j}\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{ih_{j}}},

where
h_{j}\in A
is selfadjoint for all
j
.

Proof.

It suffices to approximate
\mathrm{Ad}_{u}
, with
u\in U(A)
, in the point-norm topology by unitary mixing operators of the form (
1
).
Let
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subseteq A
. By the Borel functional calculus in
A^{**}
, there exists a selfadjoint
h\in A^{**}
such that
u=e^{ih}
. By Kaplansky’s density theorem, there exists a bounded net
(h_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
of selfadjoint elements in
A
such that
h_{\lambda}\to h
in the ultrastrong* topology. Set
u_{\lambda}=e^{ih\lambda}
. Then

u_{\lambda}x_{i}u_{\lambda}^{*}\to ux_{i}u^{*}
in the ultrastrong* topology, and thus also in the
\sigma(A^{**},A^{*})
topology for all
i
. Thus,
u_{\lambda}\bm{x}u_{\lambda}^{*}\to u\bm{x}u^{*}
in the
\sigma((A^{n})^{**},(A^{n})^{*})
-topology, where we have set
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. It follows, by a standard application of the Hahn-Banach Theorem, that
u\bm{x}u^{*}
belongs to the norm closure of
\mathrm{co}\{u_{\lambda}\bm{x}u_{\lambda}^{*}:\lambda\}
. This yields, for each
\varepsilon>0
, an operator
T\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
of the form (
1
) and such that
\|T(\bm{x})-u\bm{x}u^{*}\|<\varepsilon
, as desired.
∎

Recall that
A
is a said to have the Dixmier property if
D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
for all
a\in A
, where
Z(A)
denotes the center of
A
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra with the Dixmier property. Let
I
be a closed two-sided ideal of
A
. Let
\bm{a}\in A^{n}
. Then
D_{A}(\bm{a})\cap Z(A)^{n}
is mapped densely onto
D_{A/I}(\pi(\bm{a}))\cap Z(A/I)^{n}
by the quotient map
\pi\colon A\mapsto A/I
.

Proof.

We follow arguments from
[
1
]
adapted to
n
-tuples.

Let
\bm{\tilde{z}}\in D_{A/I}(\pi(\bm{a}))\cap Z(A/I)^{n}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then
\|\tilde{T}\bm{a}-\bm{\tilde{z}}\|<\varepsilon

for some
\tilde{T}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A/I)
. Moreover, by the previous lemma, we may choose
\tilde{T}
of the form (
1
).
Since the unitaries in
\tilde{T}
are exponentials, they lift to unitaries in
A
. In this way we get
T\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
such that
\pi T=\tilde{T}
, where
\pi\colon A\to A/I
denotes the quotient map. Set
\bm{b}=T(\bm{a})
. Then
\|\pi(\bm{b})-\bm{z}\|<\varepsilon
. By a process of successive averagings by unitary mixing operators, we can find
T_{n}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
such that
T_{n}\bm{b}\to\bm{z}\in D_{A}(\bm{b})\cap Z(A)^{n}
. This is
[
5
, Lemma 8.3.4]
, stated for the von Neumann algebra case, but the proof applies without change to any C*-algebra with the Dixmier property. Then
\bm{z}\in D_{A}(\bm{a})
and
\|\pi(\bm{z})-\bm{\tilde{z}}\|<\varepsilon
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
A
be a von Neumann algebra. Then
D_{A}(\bm{a})
has the following central convexity property:
z\bm{b}+(1-z)\bm{c}\in D_{A}(\bm{a})
for all
\bm{b},\bm{c}\in D_{A}(\bm{a})
and
0\leqslant z\leqslant 1
in
Z(A)
.

Note: This lemma is true in any C*-algebra, but we only prove here the von Neumann algebra case, as it is all that will be needed later on.

Proof.

For
n=1
and
a
selfadjoint, this is
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
. The same proof holds in this case with the obvious modifications.

Let
0\leqslant z\leqslant 1
be a central. Using Borel functional calculus, we can write
z
as a norm limit of elements of the form
\sum_{k=1}^{N}t_{k}e_{k}
, where the
(e_{k})_{k=1}^{N}
are central pairwise orthogonal projections adding up to 1, and
t_{k}\in[0,1]
for all
k
. It thus suffices to assume that
z
is exactly of this form.

We have
\bm{b}\in D_{A}(\bm{a})
if and only
e_{k}\bm{b}\in D_{e_{k}A}(e_{k}\bm{a})
for all
k
. This holds since
unitary mixing operators on
A\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N}e_{k}A
are of the form
\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N}T_{k}
, with

T_{k}\in\mathrm{Mix}(e_{k}A)
for all
k
. Since
e_{k}z
is a scalar multiple of
e_{k}
(the unit of
e_{k}A
), cutting down by each
e_{k}
reduces the proof to the case that
z
is a scalar. In this case, the result is simply the convexity of
D_{A}(\bm{a})
.
∎

In the next section we make essential use of the Zsido’s Approximation Lemma from
[
8
]
:

Lemma 6
.

Let
A
be a simple C*-algebra without bounded traces. Let
\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)
. Then the operator
A\ni a\mapsto\rho(a)1\in A
is in the point-norm closure of
\mathrm{Mix}(A)
.

3.
Proofs of Theorem
1
and Corollary
2

Let us introduce some notation: We denote by
\mathrm{T}(A)
the set of tracial states of
A
. Given an ideal
I\subseteq A
, we denote by
\mathrm{S}(A)_{I}
the states on
A
that vanish on
I
, i.e., that factor through
A/I
. Given a state
\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)
and
\bm{a}\in A^{n}
we evaluate
\rho
on
\bm{a}
coordinatewise:

\rho(\bm{a})=(\rho(a_{1}),\ldots,\rho(a_{n}))\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

We regard
\mathbb{C}^{n}
endowed with the maximum norm. In this way,
A^{n}\ni\bm{a}\mapsto\rho(\bm{a})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
has norm
1
.

We shall deduce the results stated in the introduction from the following result, of independent interest:

Theorem 7
.

Let
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}\in A^{n}
. Let
r\geqslant 0
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\inf\{\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})\|:T_{i}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)\}\leqslant r
.

(2)

The following conditions hold:

(a)

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tau(\bm{a}_{i})\|\leqslant r
for all
\tau\in\mathrm{T}(A)
,

(b)

for each ideal
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
there exist states
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}
such that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})\|\leqslant r
.

Proof of (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).

Let
\tau\in\mathrm{T}(A)
. Since
\tau\circ T=\tau
for all
T\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
, it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tau(\bm{a}_{i})=\tau(\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}(\bm{a}_{i}))

for all
T_{i}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
. Since
\tau
is a state, it is clear that (i) implies
that the left hand side of the equation above has norm at most
r
. (Recall that
we’ve endowed
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the maximum norm.) This proves (a).

Let
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
and
\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}
. Then
\rho\circ T\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}
for all
T\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
.
Also,

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\rho\circ T_{i})(\bm{a}_{i})=\rho(\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})).

Passing to the infimum over all
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
and using (i), we deduce that

\inf\{\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})\|:\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}\}\leqslant r.

By the compactness of
\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}
in the weak* topology, this infimum is attained. This proves
(b).
∎

Before proving (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) of Theorem
7
, we use a standard Hahn-Banach/Kaplansky density argument to reduce the proof to the von Neumann algebra case.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots\bm{a}_{m}\in A^{n}
. Regard
A
as a C*-subalgebra of its bidual
A^{**}
. Then

\inf\{\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})\|:T_{i}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)\}=\inf\{\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})\|:T_{i}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A^{**})\}.

Proof.

Clearly, the right side is dominated by the left side. Let
r
denote the number on the left side. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose that
\bm{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bm{b}_{i}
is such that
\|\bm{b}\|<r+\varepsilon
, with

\bm{b}_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}t_{i,k}u_{i,k}\bm{a}_{i}u_{i,k}^{*},

where the sum is a convex combination, and where
u_{i,k}\in U(A^{**})
for all
i,k
. By Kaplansky’s density theorem for unitaries (
[
4
, Theorem 2]
), there exist (commonly indexed) nets of unitaries
(u_{i,k,\lambda})_{\lambda}\in U(A)
such that
u_{i,k,\lambda}\to u_{i,k}
in the ultrastrong* topology for
i=1,\ldots,m
. For each
i=1,\ldots,m
and
\lambda
, set

\bm{b}_{i,\lambda}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}t_{i}u_{i,k,\lambda}\bm{a}_{i}u_{i,k,\lambda}^{*}\in D_{A}(\bm{a}_{i}).

Then
\bm{b}_{i,\lambda}\to\bm{b}_{i}
coordinatewise in the weak* topology
\sigma(A^{**},A^{*})
, equivalently, in the
\sigma((A^{n})^{**},(A^{n})^{*})
topology. Consider the set

S=\mathrm{co}\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bm{b}_{i,\lambda}:\lambda\}.

This is a convex subset of
A^{n}
whose
\sigma((A^{n})^{**},(A^{n})^{*})
closure in
(A^{**})^{n}
contains
\bm{b}
. It follows that
S
must intersect the ball
\{\bm{x}\in A^{n}:\|\bm{x}\|<r+\varepsilon\}
. For suppose that this is not the case. Then, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists
\bm{\rho}\in(A^{n})^{*}
such that
\mathrm{Re}(\bm{\rho},\bm{x})<r+\varepsilon
for all
\|\bm{x}\|<r+\varepsilon
,
while
\mathrm{Re}(\bm{\rho},\bm{y})>r+\varepsilon
for all
\bm{y}\in S
. The first inequality implies that
\|\bm{\rho}\|\leqslant 1
and the second one that
\mathrm{Re}(\bm{\rho},\bm{b})\geqslant r+\varepsilon
. This contradicts that
\|\bm{b}\|<r+\varepsilon
. Thus, there exists a convex combination of sums of the form
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bm{b}_{i,\lambda}

with norm
<r+\varepsilon
. This yields an element of norm
<r+\varepsilon
and of the desired form.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).

The proof proceeds in stages: We first obtain the result in the case that
A
is a simple C*-algebra without bounded traces. Next, we deal with the case that
A
is a von Neumann algebra, which we break-up into the finite and the properly infinite case. Finally, relying on the previous lemma, we deal with the general case.

A
is simple and traceless
. Let
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}\in A^{n}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. By assumption, there exist
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}\in\mathrm{S}(A)

such that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})\|\leqslant r.

By Lemma
6
, for each
i
the map
A\ni a\mapsto\rho_{i}(a)1\in A
is a point-norm limit of unitary mixing operators. Then, letting
T_{i,\lambda}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
be such that
\lim_{\lambda}T_{i,\lambda}=\rho_{i}1
,
we deduce at once that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}T_{i,\lambda}(\bm{a}_{i})\|<r+\varepsilon,

for some
\lambda
. This, (ii) implies (i) in this case.

A
is a properly infinite von Neumann algebra
. Let
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}\in A^{n}
be
n
-tuples satisfying condition (b) of Theorem
7
for a given
r\geqslant 0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
M
be a maximal ideal of
A
. Passing to the quotient
A/M
, which is simple and traceless, we know, as established in the previous paragraph, that

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{A/M}(\pi_{M}(\bm{a}_{i}))\Big)\cap Z(A/M)^{n}

contains an element of norm less than
r+\varepsilon
(where
\pi_{M}\colon A\to A/M
denotes the quotient map). Since
D_{A}(\bm{a}_{i})\cap Z(A)^{n}
maps densely onto
D_{A/M}(\pi_{M}(\bm{a}_{i}))\cap Z(A/M)
(Lemma
4
), there exist
\bm{z}_{i}\in D_{A}(\bm{a}_{i})\cap Z(A)^{n}
such that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{M}(\bm{z}_{i})\|<r+\varepsilon.

Recall that von Neumann algebras are weakly central, i.e.,
\mathrm{Max}(A)
is homeomorphic to the spectrum of
Z(A)

via the map
\mathrm{Max}(A)\ni M\mapsto M\cap Z(A)\in\widehat{Z(A)}
. Identifying in this way
\mathrm{Max}(A)
with

\widehat{Z(A)}
, we can rephrase the inequality above as

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{\bm{z}}_{i}(M)\|<r+\varepsilon,

where
\widehat{z}\colon\widehat{Z(A)}\to\mathbb{C}
denotes the Gelfand transform of
z\in Z(A)
. This inequality is valid in neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{M}
of
M
:

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{\bm{z}}_{i}(M^{\prime})\|<r+\varepsilon,

for all
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{M}
. Since the spectrum of
Z(A)
is totally disconnected, we can refine the cover
(\mathcal{U}_{M})_{M}
to a finite partition of
\widehat{Z(A)}
by clopen sets. We thus obtain central projections
(e_{k})_{k=1}^{N}
, corresponding to these clopen sets, such that
\sum_{k=1}^{N}e_{k}=1
, and for each
k=1,\ldots,N
there exists
\bm{z}_{i,k}\in D_{A}(\bm{a}_{i})\cap Z(A)^{n}
such that

(2)

\|e_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bm{z}_{i,k}\|<r+\varepsilon.

Now let

\bm{z}_{i}=e_{1}\bm{z}_{i,1}+\ldots+e_{N}\bm{z}_{i,N}\hbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,m.

By Lemma
5
,
\bm{z}_{i}\in D_{A}(\bm{a}_{i})\cap Z(A)^{n}
. Further,

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bm{z}_{i}\|=\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant N}\|e_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bm{z}_{i,k}\|<r+\varepsilon.

This proves that (ii) implies (i) in the case that
A
is a properly infinite von Neumann algebra.

A
is a finite von Neumann algebra
. Suppose that
A
is a finite von Neumann algebra. This case follows using standard results on the center-valued trace.
Let
E\colon A\to Z(A)
denote the center-valued trace. Let
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}\in A^{n}
. Suppose that
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}
satisfy condition (a) of Theorem
7
for a given
r\geqslant 0
. For each point evaluation

\mathrm{ev}_{M}\colon Z(A)\to\mathbb{C}
, with
M\in\widehat{Z(A)}
, the map
\mathrm{ev}_{M}\circ E
is a trace on
A
. We deduce at once from condition (a)
that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}E(\bm{a}_{i})\|\leqslant r.

It thus suffices to show that
E
is a point-norm limit of unitary mixing operators.
Indeed, by
[
5
, Lemma 8.3.4]
, there exists a net
(T_{\lambda})_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
such that
T_{\lambda}a\in D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)
for all
a\in A
. Moreover, since
A
is a finite von Neumann algebra, it has the singleton Dixmier property, i.e.,
D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)=\{E(a)\}
for all
a\in A
.

A
is an arbitrary von Neumann algebra
. Suppose now that
A
is an arbitrary von Neumann algebra.
Then there exists a central projection
e
such that
eA
is a finite von Neumann algebra, while
(1-e)A
is properly infinite. Let
\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}\in A^{n}
be
n
-tuples satisfying (a) and (b) of Theorem
7
. Then
e\bm{a_{1}},\ldots,e\bm{a}_{m}
satisfy (a) relative to
eA
, and as demostrated above this implies that
\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}\bm{a}_{i}\|<r+\varepsilon
for some

T_{1},\ldots T_{m}\in\mathrm{Mix}(eA)
. On the other hand,
(1-e)\bm{a_{1}},\ldots,(1-e)\bm{a_{m}}
satisfy condition (b) on
(1-e)A
, and as demonstrated above,
\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}^{\prime}(\bm{a}_{i})\|<r+\varepsilon
form some
T_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Mix}((1-e)A)
. Since the operators
a\mapsto T_{i}(ea)+T_{i}^{\prime}((1-e)a)
are unitary mixing operator on
A
for all
i
, we again deduce that (ii) implies (i) for an arbitrary von Neumann algebra.

Case of an arbitrary C*-algebra
.
Let us argue that if
A
is unitally embedded in some C*-algebra
B
, then conditions (a) and (b) are verified relative to
B
as well. Since every bounded trace on

restricts to a trace on
A
, we immediately deduce that (a)
holds in
B
. Let

be a maximal ideal of
B
. By condition (b) applied in

, there exist states

\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}\in\mathrm{S}(A)
that vanish on

and such that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})\|\leqslant r.

By the Hahn-Banach theorem, the state induced by
\rho_{i}
on

extends to a state on
B/M
. We thus obtain

\tilde{\rho}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\rho}_{m}\in\mathrm{S}(B)_{M}
extending
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
. This yields condition (b)
relative to
B
. Let us now regard
A
as a C*-subalgebra of

. Since the latter is a von Neumann algebra,

, we deduce (i).
∎

Let us now prove the results stated in the introduction:

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let us prove that (i) implies (iii). Let
F=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
be a finite subset of
V
, and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Set
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. Then
\bm{a}
satisfies conditions (a) and (b) of Theorem
7
(ii), with
m=1
and
r=0
. We conclude that
\bm{0}\in D_{A}(\bm{a})
. Thus, there exists
T_{F,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
such that
\|T_{F,\varepsilon}a_{i}\|<\varepsilon
for all
i
. The net of operators
(F,\varepsilon)\mapsto T_{F,\varepsilon}
is then as desired.

It is clear that (iii) implies (ii).

Let us prove that (ii) implies (i). Let
V
be as in (ii). Observe first that
a-Ta\in[A,A]
for any
T\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
. Hence, if
T_{n}a\to 0
for some sequence
T_{n}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
, then
a\in\overline{[A,A]}
. This shows that
V\subseteq\overline{[A,A]}
.

Let
M
be a maximal ideal. Let
F=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
be a finite subset of
V
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
\bm{0}\notin\{\rho(\bm{a}):\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}\}
. Then, by the Hahn-Banach Theorem applied to the compact convex set
\{\rho(\bm{a}):\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}\}
and
\bm{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, there exist

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
c>0
such that

\mathrm{Re}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})\geqslant c\hbox{ for all }(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\{\rho(\bm{a}):\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}\}.

Let
a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}a_{i}
.
Then,
0\notin\{\rho(a):\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}\}
, which in turn implies that
0\notin D_{A}(a)
, by the equivalence
of (i) and (iii) for the case
n=1
(
[
7
, Theorem 4.7]
). This contradicts (ii). We have shown that for every finite set

F\subseteq V
the set
\{\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}:\rho(F)=\{0\}\}
is non-empty. By the compactness of
\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}
in the weak* topology, we obtain
\rho\in\mathrm{S}(A)_{M}
such that
\rho(V)=\{0\}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 9
.

If every quotient of
A
has a bounded trace, then Theorem
1
(i) simply asserts that
V\subseteq\overline{[A,A]}
. In this case, it is not difficult to go from (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) to (iii) by a process of successive averagings towards zero of a given collection of elements: starting with
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
in
\overline{[A,A]}
, we argue by the equivalence of (i) and (ii) that there exists
T_{1}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
such that
\|T_{1}a_{1}\|<1/2
. Since
T_{1}a_{2}\in\overline{[A,A]}
, we can choose
T_{2}\in\mathrm{Mix}(A)
such that
\|T_{2}T_{1}a\|<\frac{1}{2^{2}}
, etc. This simple strategy breaks down when the C*-algebra
A
has traceless quotients, since after the first step there is no guarantee that
0\in D_{A}(T_{1}a_{2})
.

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Let
V=\{a-H(a):a\in A\}
. By the assumption that
H
preserves traces,
\tau|_{V}=0
for any bounded trace
\tau
.
Since
\overline{[A,A]}=\bigcap_{\tau\in\mathrm{T}(A)}\ker\tau
(see
[
3
]
), it follows that
V\subseteq\overline{[A,A]}
.

Let
M
be a maximal ideal and denote by
\pi_{M}\colon A\to A/M
the quotient map. Since
A/M
is simple and unital, its center is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
. We may thus regard the restriction of
\pi_{M}
to
Z(A)
as a homomorphism onto
\mathbb{C}
. Set
\rho_{M}=\pi_{M}\circ H
. Then
\rho_{M}
is a state factoring through
A/M
and
such that
\rho_{M}(a-H(a))=0
for all
a\in A
, i.e.,
V\subseteq\ker\rho_{M}
. It follows by Theorem
1

that there exists a net of unitary mixing operators
(T_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
such that
T_{\lambda}(a-H(a))\to 0

for all
a\in A
. Since the
T_{\lambda}
s fix the center, we obtain that
T_{\lambda}a\to Ha
for all
a\in A
, as desired.
∎

On the Read-Shockley energy for grain boundaries in poly-crystals
Martino Fortuna, Adriana Garroni, Emanuele Spadaro

Abstract
. In the 50’s Read and Shockley proposed a formula for the energy of small angle grain boundaries in polycrystals based on linearised elasticity and an ansazt on the distribution of incompatibilities of the lattice at the interface. In this paper we derive a sharp interface limiting functional starting from a nonlinear semidiscrete model for dislocations proposed by Lauteri–Luckhaus in [ 11 ] . Building upon their analysis we obtain, via Γ \Gamma -convergence, an interfacial energy depending on the rotations of the grains and the relative orientation of the interface which agrees for small angle grain boundaries with the Read and Shockley logarithmic scaling.



1.
Introduction

In
[
11
]
Lauteri and Luckhaus undertake the analysis of a very general geometric functional for the description of dislocations’ configurations in metal plasticity.
In fact, metals are actually crystals and their mechanic properties are strongly effected by the underlying ordered disposition of atoms. Precisely, metals exhibit a local order in regions (called
grains
) where the underlying structure is closed to a rotated reference lattice. These grains are separated by boundaries and the incompatibility of the crystal lattice along these boundaries induces an elastic distortion, which in certain scales can be modeled with an energies concentrated on these boundaries. The understanding of the dependence of this
surface tension
upon the relative orientations of the grains is crucial in order to clarify the mechanical properties of poly-crystalline materials.
It is understood that the incompatibilities of the crystal lattices at the interface are resolved by means of the presence of defects, such as dislocations, whose density depends on the discrepancy at the interface.

Dislocations are, roughly speaking, the effect of locally lattice invariant slips along crystallographic planes and at the equilibrium may be identified with the lines separating the regions undergoing different slips in such crystallographic planes. Outside a neighborhood of these one-dimensional objects, at a distance comparable with the lattice spacing (the so called
core regions
of the dislocations), the material is a local distortion of a perfect lattice.
For this reason, the typical models for dislocations are somehow hybrid: they show a cohexistence of continuum variables for the bulk elastic deformation and a microscopic parameter
\varepsilon>0
for the size of the core region of the dislocations (proportional to the lattice spacing, essentially the range where the continuum approximation fails). These kind of models are often referred to as
semi-discrete models
and their validity has been confirmed by the asymptotic analysis of fully discrete models (see, e.g,
[
14
,
2
,
5
,
12
]
).

The model proposed by Lauteri and Luckhaus integrates perfectly into this framework.
They consider the problem in a two-dimensional section of a crystal
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the state space is described by couples
(A,S)
, where
A:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is a matrix fields and
S\subset\Omega
is a closed set.
Here
S
is assumed to contain the defects of the materials, whereas
A
outside of
S
represents the local elastic deformation of the material, and hence
A
is locally a gradient in
\Omega\setminus S
.
In the presence of defects,
A
is not globally a gradient and its circulation on closed loops around the connected components of
S
should be a vector of the underlying lattice, the so called
Burgers vectors
.

The energy introduced by Lauteri and Luckhaus has two components: a core energy proportional to the area of the core region (the tubular neighborhood of
S
at distance
\lambda\varepsilon
, with
\lambda\geq 1
a fixed constant and
\varepsilon>0
representing the lattice spacing)

|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)|,

and an elastic energy outside the core region of the form

\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}W(A)\,dx,

with
W
a suitable energy density.

Clearly, since
S
contains the incompatibilities of the elastic deformation, we have that
A
is
\mathrm{Curl}\,
-free away from
S
, and moreover
A
and
S
are further linked by the extra-condition related to the quantization of the circulations in terms of the Burgers vectors.

The main objective of the present paper is the derivation of a sharp interface energy for grain boundaries in dimension two starting from the Lauteri–Luckhaus’ model. Roughly speaking, the energy is defined for fields which are locally constant rotations.
Given two grains
E_{i},E_{j}\subseteq\Omega
and the corresponding constant matrix fields
R_{i},R_{j}\in SO(2)
, representing the rotations of the underlying crystal structure with respect to a reference lattice, we would like to measure the line tension of the boundary between the grains as

\Phi(R_{i},R_{j},\nu_{i,j})\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{i,j}),

where
\Gamma_{i,j}=\partial E_{i}\cap\partial E_{j}
and
\Phi
is the energy per unit length.

In the last decades dislocations have been treated in mathematical terms giving rise to a large literature that deals with microscopic, mesoscopic and macroscopic scales (see …. and the references therein).
The paper of Lauteri–Luckhaus
[
11
]
is a milestone in this theory, because for the first time the energetic regime which leads to the formation of grain boundaries is investigated without ad hoc assumptions on the geometric distribution of defects.
In
[
11
]
the authors consider the problem of interpolating between two small symmetric rotations of angle
\pm\alpha
imposed on the lateral sides of a square and they prove three kinds of results:

1.

an
upper bound
, consisting in the construction of a configuration matching the right boundary conditions in agreement with the
Read and Shockley ansatz
on the energy of the interface (see
[
15
,
10
]
):

\textup{energy per unit length}\sim\varepsilon\;\alpha\;|\log\alpha|,\qquad 0<\alpha<<1;

2.

the convergence of suitable sequences of configurations with finite energy to
micro-rotations
(see Definition
2.5
below), modelling the grains with constant oriented lattices;

3.

a
lower bound
of the energy matching (up to a constant) the
Read and Shockley ansatz
for grain boundary between small rotations.

One of the main contributions of
[
11
]
is the introduction of the variable
S
, which adds flexibility to the model and allows to treat scalings at which geometric necessary dislocations emerge. In doing this, the authors introduce and develop a wealth of innovative ideas and tools, beyond the existing literature on related problems with topological singularities.
It is also worth noticing that the functional proposed by Lauteri and Luckhaus has an essential geometric structure: potentially it is a lower bound that can be used as a comparison energy for several other interesting models, as optimal partition problems, image segmentations etc….

In this paper we build upon the analysis in
[
11
]
and complete the picture for the asymptotics of the Lauteri–Luckhaus energies, proving a full
\Gamma
-convergence result to a limiting energy of the form

(1.1)

\int_{J_{A}}\Phi\big(A^{+},A^{-},\nu_{A}\big)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1},

for
A
a
SBV
-field with values in
SO(2)
and whose derivative has only jump part (
J_{A}
is the jump set,
\nu_{A}
its normal vector, and
A^{+},A^{-}
the traces from both sides of the jump).
In particular, we extend the results in
[
11
]
in four directions:

1.

we prove a
compactness
result in the natural topology for sequences of fields with bounded energies (whereas the compactness of Lauteri–Luckhaus was valid only for distinguished competitors);

2.

we
extend the model
incorporating the anisotropy of the underlying crystalline structure;

3.

we find an
asymptotic formula
for the energy density
\Phi
, showing that the lower and upper bound in
[
11
]
can be upgraded into an interfacial energy;

4.

we find an
improved construction for the upper bound
which depends on the relative orientations of the grains (not necessarily symmetric with respect to the interface), matching the Read–Shockley logarithmic scaling. Moreover, we are able to treat the case of interfaces between two incompatible lattices as studied by Van der Merwe
[
18
]
.

In order to show such results, we need to use the entire machinary introduced and developed by Lauteri and Luckhaus, most notably their
logaritmic estimate
(see Theorem
4.8
below) and the set of ideas in the modification of the matrix field
A
in order to achieve good bounds and compactness properties.
Concerning this last point, for our asymptotic analysis it is necessary to revisit some of the constructions done in
[
11
]
, although we do not introduce any essentially new ideas.

The proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence, passes through an appropriate cell problem formula and the analysis of the thermodynamic limit for the rescaled functionals. A crucial step in this strategy is to show that the thermodynamic limit actually gives the optimal energy per unit length of a straight interface. In doing this we prove that optimal sequences concentrates around the interface and exhibit a sort of equi-distribution of defects (in the spirit of
[
1
,
16
]
in agreement with the ideas behind the explicit upper bound constructions and the computations in
[
15
,
18
,
6
]
).

2.
Setting and main result

2.1.
Parameters and classes of competitors

In the sequel
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded open set which represents a (possibly defected) material body, and
\lambda,\tau>0
are two fixed parameters of the energy functionals.
For every
\varepsilon>0
we define the class of admissible pairs
(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, where
A\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
and
S\subset\Omega
is a relatively closed set satisfying the following conditions:

(H1)

S\supseteq\mathrm{supp}\,(\mathrm{Curl}\,A)
;

(H2)

for every simple rectifiable closed curve

\gamma
in
\Omega\setminus S

one has that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}A\in\tau\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

For a continuous field
A
and a rectifiable curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
the line integral
\int_{\gamma}A
is

\int_{\gamma}A:=\int_{0}^{1}A(\gamma(t))\,\dot{\gamma}(t)\,dt,

whereas for
A\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
one should understand it in the weak sense:

(2.1)

\int_{\gamma}A:=-\textup{sgn}(\gamma)\int_{\Omega}A(x)\,\nabla^{\perp}\varphi(x)\,dx,

with
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
any function such that

\varphi=1\quad\textup{on}\quad D\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\big({\rm supp}\,\varphi\big)\setminus D\subset\Omega\setminus S,

where
D\subset\Omega
is the open set such that
\gamma=\partial D
and
\textup{sgn}(\gamma)=\pm 1
according to the orientation of
\gamma
(anticlockwise
+1
, clokwise
-1
).
Recall that

\nabla^{\perp}\varphi:=J\nabla\varphi,\qquad J:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

This definition reduces to the classical circulation if
A
is smooth, and it is well posed, since the right hand side of (
2.1
) does not depend on the precise choice of
\varphi
: indeed, if we take a second admissible test
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\psi=1
on
D
, and

({\rm supp}\,\psi)\setminus D\subseteq\Omega\setminus S,

then
\varphi-\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

{\rm supp}(\varphi-\psi)\subseteq\Omega\setminus\mathrm{supp}\,(\mathrm{Curl}\,A),

and hence
\int A\,\nabla^{\perp}(\varphi-\psi)=0
.

In this model
A
represents a field that locally (outside the incompatibilities contained in the set
S
) is a gradient of a function that maps the material body
\Omega
in a reference configuration which is given by a perfect crystal, here the square lattice
\varepsilon\tau\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. This property is expressed by the circulation condition (H2). A different Bravais lattice (e.g., triangular lattice) can be also considered without changing our analysis.

Remark 2.1
.

In view of the above interpretation for
A
, we remark that a change of variable in the configuration
\Omega
should not change the circulation. Therefore, changing the domain
\Omega
with a rotation
R\Omega
, we must change the field accordingly. Namelly,
A_{R}:R\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
given by
A_{R}(y)=A(R^{-1}y)R^{-1}
(which is clearly what happens if
A=\nabla u
and
A_{R}=\nabla u_{R}
with
u_{R}(y)=u(R^{-1}y)
). Condition (H2) does not change, indeed

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}A=\int_{R\gamma}A_{R}.

2.2.
The energy functional

For any
(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
and for any open subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, we define

•

the
elastic energy

\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S;\Omega^{\prime}):=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A(x),SO(2))\,dx,

•

the
core energy

\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S;\Omega^{\prime}):=|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)\cap\Omega^{\prime}|\,,

where
|E|
is the
2
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and
B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(E)
is the
\lambda\varepsilon
-neighborhood in
\Omega
of any subset
E\subset\Omega
,

B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(E):=\Big\{x\in\Omega:\ \mathrm{dist}(x,E)<\lambda\varepsilon\Big\}.

The energy functional introduced by Lauteri–Luckhaus in
[
11
]
is given by the sum of the two terms:

(2.2)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S;\Omega^{\prime}):=\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S;\Omega^{\prime})+\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S;\Omega^{\prime}).

The energy of a matrix valued field
A\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
is then defined by optimazing in the set
S\supset\mathrm{supp}\,(\mathrm{Curl}\,A)
and rescaling in
\varepsilon
the Lauteri–Luckhaus energy:

F_{\varepsilon}(A;\Omega^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\inf\Big\{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S;\Omega^{\prime})\,:\,(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\Big\}.

If
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega
, we drop the dependence on the set in the notation.
Our model deviates from the one used in
[
11
]
, because of condition (H2) for the circulation of the admissible fields
A
, which makes the model accounting for the anisotropy due to the underlying crystalline structure.

Remark 2.2
.

In what follows, for every
A\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
we will denote with
S_{A}
any relatively closed set of
\Omega
such that

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S_{A})\leq F_{\varepsilon}(A)+\varepsilon.

Clearly,
S_{A}
is not uniquely determined.

Remark 2.3
.

The energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
does not depend on the values of the field
A
in the
\lambda\varepsilon
-neighbor-hood of the set
S
. Indeed, for every
u\in W_{0}^{1,1}(B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S))
,
(A+\nabla u,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
and its energy remains unchanged

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A+\nabla u,S)=\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S).

Remark 2.4
.

The energy
F_{\varepsilon}
is rotationally invariant, while condition (H2) (in view of Remark
2.1
) for the circulation of the admissible competitor is invariant under right multiplication by rotations.

2.3.
Main results

We aim at proving a
\Gamma
-convergence result for the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}
, thus deriving an interfacial energy for grain boundaries which accounts for the presence of crystal defects that may resolve incompatibilities. In this limiting model the polycrystalline configurations will be represented by fields
A
that takes values in
SO(2)
, called
micro-rotations
(see the definition below), which describe the locally constant orientation of the underlying lattice.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a matrix field
A\in SBV(\Omega,SO(2))
is a
micro-rotation

if

DA^{i}=\left(A^{i,+}-A^{i,-}\right)\otimes\nu_{A}\mathcal{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{A},\qquad i=1,2,

where
J_{A}
is the jump set of
A
,
A^{1}
and
A^{2}
the rows of
A
, and
\nu_{A}
the normal vector to
J_{A}
(pointing towards the value
A^{+}
).

Observe that a micro-rotation is not necessarily a piecewise constant field on a
finite
Caccioppoli partition.
Nevertheless it can be proved (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
) that a micro-rotation can be approximated in energy by polyhedral partitions (i.e., fields which are piecewise constant on finite Caccioppoli partitions of subdomains with polyehdral boundaries).

The main results of the paper are the following.

Theorem 2.6
.

For any sequence
(A_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
such that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon})<+\infty,

there exist a subsequence (not relabeled) and
a micro-rotation
A
such that

(2.3)

A_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{A_{\varepsilon}})}\to A\qquad\textup{in }\quad L^{1}(\Omega).

Moreover, the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the topology induced by the convergence in (
2.3
) to

(2.4)

F_{0}(A)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\int_{J_{A}}\Phi(A^{+},A^{-},\nu_{A})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}},\qquad&\hbox{ if $A$ is a microtation},\\
+\infty&otherwise,\end{cases}

for a suitable continuous energy density
\Phi:SO(2)\times SO(2)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfying for all
R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2)
, and
n\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the bounds

(2.5)

C_{1}|R^{-}-R^{+}||\log|R^{-}-R^{+}||\leq\Phi(R^{-},R^{+},n)\leq C_{2}|R^{-}-R^{+}|(|\log|R^{-}-R^{+}||+1),

with
C_{1},C_{2}>0
dimensional constants.

Remark 2.7
.

The convergence in (
2.3
) cannot be upgraded to a
L^{1}
convergence for the matrix field
A_{\varepsilon}
. Indeed, as noticed in Remark
2.3
, the energy
F_{\varepsilon}
remains unchanged if we pass to
A^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}:=A_{\varepsilon}+\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
, with
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S))
, arbitrary (note that, since
\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{Curl}\,A_{\varepsilon}
, the circulations of
A^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
remain the same).

Remark 2.8
.

We remark that the above result is also true under more general assumptions for the energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
. In particular we can replace the elastic energy
\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}
defined above with

\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S;\Omega^{\prime}):=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}W(A(x))\,dx,

where the energy density
W
satisfies the following properties:

i)

W(AR)=W(A)
for all
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
and for all
R\in SO(2)
;

ii)

There are constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\mathrm{dist}(A,SO(2))\leq W(A)\leq c_{2}\mathrm{dist}(A,SO(2))\qquad\forall A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}.

Clearly with this choice the limiting grain boundary energy density
\Phi
will depend on
W
.
Note that condition i) expresses the invariance of the model under change of variable for the material body represented by the domain
\Omega
(see also Remark
2.4
).

2.4.
Scheme of the proof and structure of the paper

The proof of Theorem
2.6
exploits the many deep ideas introduced by Lauteri and Luckhaus in
[
11
]
. The key point of our proof is the asymptotic analysis of a cell-problem formula, which allows to characterize the
\Gamma
-limit of the generalised Lauteri-Luckhaus energies.

The proof of Theorem
2.6
is divided in different sections and is made of several steps.
In Section 3 and 4 we prove the compactness part of the statement. In particular, the first section is devoted to a detailed revision of the refinement strategy proposed in
[
11
]
, making sure that the resulting field
A
remains close in
L^{1}
to the original one, with local estimates in energy.
In Section 4 we prove the compactness, first in
BV
, by using the rigidity of incompatible fields by Müller-Scardia-Zeppieri
[
13
]
and then in
SBV
deducing the structure of micro-rotation by the optimal lower bound estimate by Lauteri-Luckhaus
[
11
]
.

Section 5 is devoted to the cell-problem formula.
First by means of a classical monotonicity argument we show that existence of the thermodynamic limit of the optimal interfacial energy with fixed boundary conditions.
Then we show, with a construction inspired to the one proposed by Lauteri and Luckhaus in the case of symmetric small angle grain boundary, an upper bound for this thermodynamic limit for any pair of rotations
R^{-}
and
R^{+}
.

Section 6 is devoted to show that the thermodynamic limit agrees with the
\Gamma
-liminf of a straight interface. This requires to show that optimal sequences can be modified in order to achieve the boundary conditions in the cell problem formula.
Here the main difficulty is due to the curl-constraint. Indeed, the use of cut-off functions is not allowed. We overcome this obstruction showing that incompatibilities concentrate along the interface and interpolating the boundary conditions with an explicit construction that uses the rigidity estimates for incompatible fields.

Finally, in Section 7 we show the
\Gamma
-convergence result. The cell-problem formula is the basis for the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality, via a blowup argument, and for the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality, via a density argument.

In two appendices, we recall some simple covering arguments and the Whitney-extension theorem needed in the first part of the paper.

3.
Asymptotically equivalent matrix fields

We start with the modification procedure of compatible matrix fields
A
made by Luckhaus-Lauteri
[
11
]
. We do it in the following three lemmas, producing first bounded fields with controlled Curl, and then harmonic fields.
In what follows
C>0
will denote constants that may change from line to line and can depend on the parameters
\tau,\lambda
of the energy. Moreover,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded connected open set and for every
\sigma>0
we set

\Omega_{\sigma}:=\big\{x\in\Omega:\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>\sigma\big\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every open set
\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
, if
0<\varepsilon<C^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_{0},\partial\Omega)
and
(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, then there exists
(A^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{0})
satisfying

i)

\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime}=\mathrm{Curl}\,A
in
\Omega_{0}
,

ii)

\|A^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0}\setminus S^{\prime})}\leq C
,

iii)

\|A^{\prime}-A\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime}))}^{2}+\|A^{\prime}-A\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{0})}+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{0})\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S)
.

Proof.

We first show that for every
\eta>0
there exists a pair
(A_{\eta},S_{\eta})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{1})
with
A_{\eta}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{1};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
, for some
\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega
, such that

(3.1)

\|A-A_{\eta}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{1})}+\|A-A_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}\setminus S_{\eta})}^{2}+\left|\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S;\Omega_{1})-\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\eta},S_{\eta};\Omega_{1})\right|\leq\eta.

Indeed we can regularise
A
by convolution and set

A_{\eta}(x):=(A\chi_{\Omega_{1}})\star\rho_{\delta}(x),\qquad S_{\eta}:=\overline{B_{\delta}(S)}\cap\Omega_{1},

where
\rho_{\delta}
is a standard radial symmetric convolution kernel with support in
B_{\delta}
.
By the continuity of the convolution it is easy to see that for
\delta
small enough (
3.1
) is satisfied and by the linearity of the convolution
(A_{\eta},S_{\eta})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{1})
.
With little abuse of notation we therefore assume that
A
is continuous.

We consider the sets

\displaystyle E:=\left\{x\in\Omega_{3\varepsilon}:\;\sup_{r\in(0,3\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A(y),SO(2))\,dy>2\right\},

\displaystyle E^{\prime}:=\left\{\bigcup_{x\in\Omega_{3\varepsilon}}B_{\rho}(x):B_{\rho}(x)\subset E,\;\rho\geq\varepsilon\right\}\subset E,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}:=\Big\{x\in\Omega_{0}\cap E:\mathrm{dist}(x,E^{\prime})>6\varepsilon,\;\mathrm{dist}(x,S)>(\lambda+6)\varepsilon\Big\}\subset E.

These three sets are open, because
E
is the strict super-level set of a lower semi-continuous function.
Moreover, by the maximal function estimate (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
) we have that

(3.2)

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{3\varepsilon}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A(x),SO(2))\;dx\leq C\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S).

We set
W:=E\setminus\overline{E}^{\prime}
and redefine
A
in
U:=W\cap\tilde{\Omega}
by a Whitney-type extension lemma, as a consequence of the following three facts:

(W1)

B_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus W\supset B_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus E\neq\emptyset
for every
x\in U
, because otherwise
B_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset E
would imply
x\in E^{\prime}
, against
U\cap E^{\prime}=\emptyset
,

(W2)

\mathrm{Curl}\,A=0
in the sense of distributions in
B_{6\varepsilon}(U)
, because
\mathrm{dist}(x,S)>6\varepsilon
for every
x\in U
,

(W3)

for every
x\in B_{6\varepsilon}(U)\setminus W
we have that
x\notin E^{\prime}
and
x\notin E\setminus\overline{E}^{\prime}
, so that
x\not\in E
and
B_{3\varepsilon}(x)\cap B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)=\emptyset
, thus for every
r\in(0,3\varepsilon)
we have

\displaystyle-\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|A(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\mathrm{dist}(A(y),SO(2))dy+\sqrt{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A(y),SO(2))dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{2}\leq 2\sqrt{2}.

From (W2) for every
x\in U
we find a potential
u
in
B_{6\varepsilon}(x)
such that
\nabla u=A
; from (W1) we know that the complementary set of
W
intersects
B_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
u|_{B_{6\varepsilon}(x)\setminus W}
is Lipschitz continuous by (W3). Then, the Whitney-extension of
u|_{B_{6\varepsilon}(x)\setminus W}
to the whole
B_{\varepsilon}(x)
(suitably patched via a partion of unity) will provide a new field

A^{\prime}:\Omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},\qquad\big\{A^{\prime}\neq A\big\}\subset B_{\varepsilon}(U)\subset E,

with the following properties (the details of the Whitney-type extension argument are given in Lemma
B.1
):

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Curl}\,(A^{\prime}-A)=0\qquad\textup{in }\Omega_{0}\qquad\textup{and}\qquad|A^{\prime}(x)|\leq C\qquad\forall\;x\in B_{\varepsilon}(U).

Conclusion i) is then verified.
Moreover, note that

\displaystyle\|A^{\prime}-A\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(U)}|A^{\prime}(x)-A(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(U)}|A(x)|^{2}\,dx+C\|A^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(U))}|B_{\varepsilon}(U)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A(x),SO(2))\,dx+C(\|A^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(U))}+1)\,|U|

(3.4)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq-misura-E}}}{{\leq}}C\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S).

We then set

(3.5)

S^{\prime}:=\overline{B_{(\lambda+6)\varepsilon}(S)\cup B_{\varepsilon}(E^{\prime})}\cap\Omega_{0}.

Note that

(A^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{0})
, because from
\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime}=\mathrm{Curl}\,A
we deduce that

\int_{\gamma}A^{\prime}=\int_{\gamma}A\in\tau\varepsilon\;\mathbb{Z}^{2}\qquad\forall\;\gamma\subset\Omega_{0}\setminus S^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{0}\setminus S,

with
\gamma
closed simple rectifiable curve.
Moreover, ii) holds true as well, because

\displaystyle x\in\Omega_{0}\setminus S^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\begin{cases}\textup{either }\;x\in\Omega_{0}\setminus(E\cup B_{(\lambda+3)\varepsilon}(S))&\Longrightarrow\quad|A^{\prime}(x)|=|A(x)|\leq 2\sqrt{2},\\
\textup{or }\;x\in(E\setminus S^{\prime})\cap\Omega_{0}\subset U&\Longrightarrow\quad|A^{\prime}(x)|=|A(x)|\leq C,\end{cases}

where in the first instance we have used that
if
x\in\Omega_{3\varepsilon}\setminus(E\cap B_{(\lambda+3)\varepsilon}(S))
, then by definition of
E

\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A(x),SO(2))\leq 2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|A(x)|\leq\mathrm{dist}(A(x),SO(2))+|I|\leq 2\sqrt{2}.

Finally, in order to estimate the energy, we start noticing that

(3.6)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})&\leq C|B_{(\lambda+3)\varepsilon}(S)|+C|B_{\varepsilon}(E^{\prime})|\leq C\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S)+C|B_{\varepsilon}(E^{\prime})|\\
&\leq C\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S)+C\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S),\end{split}

where we used Lemma
A.2
: namely, for every
\kappa>0
there exists a constant
C(\kappa)>0
such that

|B_{\kappa\varepsilon}(S)|\leq C(\kappa)|B_{\varepsilon}(S)|;

in addition we used the following argument: by Vitali covering theorem, there exists a finite family of disjoint balls
B_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i})
, with
B_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i})\subset E
and
\rho_{i}\geq\varepsilon
, such that

E^{\prime}\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{5\rho_{i}}(x_{i}),

which by (
3.2
) leads to

\displaystyle|B_{\varepsilon}(E^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}|B_{\varepsilon+5\rho_{i}}(x_{i})|\leq\sum_{i}|B_{6\rho_{i}}(x_{i})|\leq 6^{2}\sum_{i}|B_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i})|\leq 6^{2}|E|\leq C\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S).

For the elastic energy, since
S\subset S^{\prime}
, in view of (
3
) we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon(S^{\prime})}}\mathrm{dist}(A(x),SO(2))^{2}\,dx+C\|A^{\prime}-A\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.distanza L1 prima}}}{{\leq}}C\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A,S).

Putting these estimates together we conclude iii).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a dimensional constant
C>0
such that for every open set
\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega
, if
0<\varepsilon<C^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_{1},\partial\Omega)
and
(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, then there exists
(A^{\prime\prime},S^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{1})
satisfying

i)

\|A^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C
,

ii)

\|A^{\prime\prime}-A\chi_{\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}^{2}+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A^{\prime\prime},S^{\prime\prime};\Omega_{1})\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S)
,

iii)

(\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime\prime})|_{\Omega_{1}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
with

|\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\varepsilon}\chi_{S^{\prime\prime}}(x)\qquad\forall\;x\in\Omega_{1}.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be any open set such that
\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
and let
(A^{\prime},S^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{0})
be the pair of Lemma
3.1
.
We set
\hat{A}:=A^{\prime}\chi_{\Omega_{0}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})}+I\chi_{B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})\cap\Omega_{0}}
and

(3.7)

A^{\prime\prime}:=(1-\zeta)\hat{A}+\zeta\hat{A}\star\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}\quad\textup{in }\Omega_{1},\qquad S^{\prime\prime}:=\overline{B_{2\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})}\cap\Omega_{1},

where
\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}
is a mollifier with compact support on
B_{\lambda\varepsilon}
and
\zeta
is a cut-off function with

\zeta=1\quad\textup{in }B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{1}),\quad\zeta=0\quad\textup{in}\quad\Omega_{0}\setminus B_{2\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{1})\quad\textup{and}\quad|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\varepsilon},

the last condition being admissible as soon as
\varepsilon<\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_{1},\partial\Omega_{0})
.
We start noticing that i) holds, because
\hat{A}
and, hence,
A^{\prime\prime}
are bounded as a consequence of Lemma
3.1
ii).

Next observe that

\displaystyle\|A^{\prime\prime}-\hat{A}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\zeta(\hat{A}\star\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}-\hat{A})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}^{2}\leq C\|\hat{A}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\,|B_{2\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})\cap\Omega_{0}|\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{0}).

Therefore, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime}\chi_{\Omega_{1}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{\prime\prime}-\hat{A}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}+\|\hat{A}-A^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime}))}+\|\hat{A}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cap B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime}))}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{0})+\sqrt{2}\,|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})\cap\Omega_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{0}),

and

\displaystyle\|A^{\prime\prime}-A\chi_{\Omega_{1}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime}\chi_{\Omega_{1}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}+\|A^{\prime}-A\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime}))}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|A\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cap(B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)))}

\displaystyle=C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{0})+\|A^{\prime}-A\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}+

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(C\int_{B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\big(\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A,SO(2))+1\big)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{0}).

In particular, we infer ii), since

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A^{\prime\prime},S^{\prime\prime};\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{1}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon(S^{\prime})}}\mathrm{dist}(A^{\prime}(x),SO(2))^{2}\,dx+C\|A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime}\chi_{\Omega_{1}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A^{\prime},S^{\prime};\Omega_{1}),

and from Lemma
A.2
also

\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S^{\prime\prime})\leq C\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S).

Finally, we can compute the Curl of
A^{\prime\prime}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\big(\hat{A}\star\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}-\hat{A}\big)\nabla\zeta^{\perp}+\zeta\hat{A}\star\nabla^{\perp}\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}+(1-\zeta)\mathrm{Curl}\,\hat{A}

\displaystyle=(A^{\prime}\star\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}-A^{\prime})\nabla\zeta^{\perp}+\zeta\hat{A}\star\nabla^{\perp}\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}+(1-\zeta)\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(A^{\prime}\star\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon}-A^{\prime})\nabla\zeta^{\perp}+\zeta\hat{A}\star\nabla^{\perp}\varphi_{\lambda\varepsilon},

where we used that
A^{\prime}
is Curl-free outside
S^{\prime}
.
We infer in particular that

\mathrm{supp}\,\big(\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime\prime}\big)\subset\mathrm{supp}\,(\zeta)\cap\Omega_{1}\subset\overline{B_{2\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime})}\cap\Omega_{1}=S^{\prime\prime}.

Moreover, since
\|\hat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C
, we conclude that

|\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\varepsilon}\|\hat{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\,\chi_{S^{\prime\prime}}(x)\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\varepsilon}\chi_{S^{\prime\prime}}(x)\qquad\forall\;x\in\Omega_{1}.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a dimensional constant
C>0
such that for every open set
\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega
, if
0<\varepsilon<C^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_{1},\partial\Omega)
and
(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, then there exists
(A_{\textup{harm}},S_{\textup{harm}})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{1})
satisfying

i)

\|A_{\textup{harm}}-A\chi_{\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}^{2}+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\textup{harm}},S_{\textup{harm}};\Omega_{1})\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S)
;

ii)

A_{\textup{harm}}
is harmonic in
\Omega_{1}\setminus S_{\textup{harm}}
;

iii)

(\mathrm{Curl}\,A_{\textup{harm}})|_{\Omega_{1}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
with

|\mathrm{Curl}\,A_{\textup{harm}}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\varepsilon}\chi_{S_{\textup{harm}}}(x)\qquad\forall\;x\in\Omega_{1}.

Proof.

Let
(A^{\prime\prime},S^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega_{1})
be the pair of Lemma
3.2
.
Recall that, since
\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime\prime}=0
in
\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}
, then
\mathrm{Div}({\rm Cof}A^{\prime\prime})=0
in
\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}
.
We consider the solution
z:\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the following problem:

(3.9)

\begin{cases}\Delta z=-\mathrm{Div}({\rm Cof}A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime\prime})=\mathrm{Div}A^{\prime\prime}\quad&\hbox{in }\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime},\\
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R}^{2}),\end{cases}

and we set

A_{\textup{harm}}:=A^{\prime\prime}-\nabla z,\qquad S_{\textup{harm}}=S^{\prime\prime}.

Clearly, by the definition of
z
we have that
\mathrm{Div}A_{\textup{harm}}=0
in
\Omega_{1}\setminus S_{\textup{harm}}
and
\mathrm{Curl}\,A_{\textup{harm}}=\mathrm{Curl}\,A^{\prime\prime}=0
in
\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}
,
thus implying ii) and iii) (in view of Lemma
3.2
).
Moreover, we can estimate the
L^{2}
distance between
A^{\prime\prime}
and
A_{\textup{harm}}
:

(3.10)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{1}\setminus S_{\textup{harm}}}|A_{\textup{harm}}-A^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dx&=\int_{\Omega_{1}\setminus S_{\textup{harm}}}|\nabla z|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}}|{\rm Cof}A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dx\\
&\leq 4\int_{\Omega_{1}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A^{\prime\prime},SO(2))\,dx,\end{split}

where we used that

|{\rm Cof}M-M|^{2}\leq 4\mathrm{dist}^{2}(M,SO(2))\qquad\forall\;M\in R^{2\times 2}.

Indeed, we can write
M=R+B
with
R\in SO(2)
and
|B|=\mathrm{dist}(M,SO(2))
and, since
{\rm Cof}M=R+{\rm Cof}B
, we get

|M-{\rm Cof}M|^{2}=|B-{\rm Cof}B|^{2}\leq 4|B|^{2}.

Finally, for i) we notice that
|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\textup{harm}})|=|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime\prime})|
and

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{dist}(A_{\textup{harm}},SO(2))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\textup{harm}}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathrm{dist}(A^{\prime\prime},SO(2))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime\prime}))}+

\displaystyle\qquad+2\|A_{\textup{harm}}-A^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S^{\prime\prime}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S).\qed

4.
Compactness

In this section we prove the compactness part of the
\Gamma
-convergence result in Theorem
2.6
. To this aim we recall the notation
S_{A}
introduced in Remark
2.2
for any matrix field
A\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every sequence

(A_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
such that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon})<+\infty,

there
exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0^{+}
such that
A_{{\varepsilon_{n}}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon_{n}}(S_{A_{\varepsilon_{n}}})}
converge to
A
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
A
is a micro-rotation, and for every
\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
it holds

\int_{J_{A}\cap\Omega_{0}}|A^{+}-A^{-}|\;\big|\log|A^{+}-A^{-}|\big|\;d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq C\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon}),

where
C=C(\Omega_{0},\Omega)>0
.

4.1.
Rigidity and BV-compactness

A first step towards the proof of Theorem
4.1
is given by providing the compactness in
BV
.
To this aim we start by recalling some simple results for functions of bounded variations.
In the following we denote the closed (coordinate) cubes with

Q(x_{0},r):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x-x_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq r\right\},

where the sup-norm is

\displaystyle\|x-x_{0}\|_{\infty}:=\sup\big\{|(x-x_{0})\cdot e_{i}|:i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\big\},

with
e_{i}
the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
in
L^{1}
and let
\lambda
be a finite positive Borel measure in
\Omega
such that for all cubes
Q(x,r)\subset\Omega

there exists a value
u_{Q(x,r)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\int_{{Q(x,r)}}|u-u_{Q(x,r)}|\,dx\leq r\lambda({Q(x,r)}).

Then
u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|Du|\leq C\lambda
for a dimensional constant
C>0
.

Proof.

For every open subsets
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
, we consider
0<r<\frac{1}{10}\mathrm{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega^{\prime\prime}))
and a grid of cubes
Q_{i}=Q(x_{i},r)\subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}
with pairwise disjoint interiors such that

\Omega^{\prime}\subset\bigcup_{i}Q_{i}.

We define the piecewise constant function
u_{r}:\Omega^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

u_{r}(x):=\sum_{i}u_{Q_{i}}\chi_{Q_{i}}(x)\qquad x\in\Omega^{\prime}.

By assumption we have

(4.1)

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|u-u_{r}|\,dx\leq r\lambda(\Omega^{\prime\prime}).

In particular
u_{r}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
, because
\lambda(\Omega)<+\infty
.
Now for every pair of cubes
Q_{i}
and
Q_{j}
with a common face, if we consider a cube
Q
of side
2r
containing both
Q_{i}
and
Q_{j}
(notice that by the choice of
r
necessarily we have that
Q\subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}
), then

|u_{Q}-u_{Q_{i}}|\leq\frac{1}{r^{n}}\left[\int_{Q}|u-u_{Q}|\,dx+\int_{Q_{i}}|u-u_{Q_{i}}|\,dx\right]\leq\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\left[2\lambda(Q)+\lambda({Q_{i}})\right].

Therefore, if
S_{ij}=Q_{i}\cap Q_{j}
is the face in common between
Q_{i}
and
Q_{j}
, then

|Du_{r}|(S_{ij})\leq r^{n-1}|u_{Q_{j}}-u_{Q_{i}}|\leq 4\lambda(Q)+\lambda({Q_{i}})+\lambda({Q_{j}}).

From this we deduce that there exists a dimensional constant
C>0
such that

(4.2)

|Du_{r}|(\Omega^{\prime})\leq C\lambda(\Omega^{\prime\prime}).

Considering the
L^{1}
-convergence of
u_{r}
to
u
in
\Omega^{\prime}
, we infer that
u\in BV(\Omega^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and by passing to the limit in (
4.2
) and using the lower-semicontinuity of the total variation we infer that

|Du|(\Omega^{\prime})\leq C\lambda(\Omega^{\prime\prime})
.
By the arbitrariness of
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}
we conclude that
u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
|Du|\leq C\lambda
.
∎

A corollary of the previous lemma is the following perturbed version.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set and
K\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Consider a sequence of functions

u_{j}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, a sequence of
finite positive measures
\lambda_{j}
with
\lambda_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\lambda
and a sequense of positive numbers
\gamma_{j}\downarrow 0
.
If for all cubes
Q(x,r)\subset\Omega
there exists a value
u_{j,Q(x,r)}\in K
such that

\int_{Q(x,r)}|u_{j}-u_{j,Q(x,r)}|\,dx\leq r\lambda_{j}(Q(x,r))+\gamma_{j},

then
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u\in BV(\Omega;K)
and
|Du|\leq C\lambda
for a dimensional constant
C>0
.

Proof.

The proof is an immediate consequence of Lemma
4.2
. Indeed, up to passing to a subsequence, for every
Q(x,r)\subset\Omega
with center
x
with rational coordinates and rational side-length
r
, there exists
u_{Q(x,r)}\in K
such that
u_{j,Q(x,r)}\to u_{Q(x,r)}
and

\displaystyle\int_{Q(x,r)}|u-u_{Q(x,r)}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{Q(x,r)}|u_{j}-u_{j,Q(x,r)}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left(r\lambda_{j}(Q(x,r))+\gamma_{j}\right)\leq r\lambda(Q(x,r)).

Therefore, we are in position to apply Lemma
4.2
. Moreover, for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and for any rational
r<{\rm dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
, the piecewise constant approximations of
u
(done on cubes with rational coordinates) given by
u_{r}:=\sum_{i}u_{Q_{i}}\chi_{Q_{i}}

take values in
K
, we conclude also that
u
itself takes its values almost everywhere in
K
. Finally, considering the corresponding approximations for
u_{j}
,
u_{j,r}:=\sum_{i}u_{j,Q_{i}}\chi_{Q_{i}}
, we have that

\|u-u_{r}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|u_{j}-u_{j,r}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq Cr+\gamma_{j},

and,
since clearly
u_{j,r}\to u_{r}
in
L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
, we also infer the strong convergence of
u_{j}
to
u
in
L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

For the proof of the
BV
-compactness we need to combine the previous corollary together with the rigidity estimate for incompatible fields proved by Müller, Scardia and Zeppieri
[
13
]
(see also
[
8
]
for the linear version of this estimate, and
[
4
]
for the general
n
-dimensional case).

Theorem 4.4

(Müller, Scardia and Zeppieri
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, simply connected
and Lipschitz set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega)>0
with the following property: for every

A\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
with
\mathrm{Curl}\,A
a vector valued measure, there exists a rotation
R\in SO(2)
such that

(4.3)

\|A-R\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\mathrm{dist}(A,SO(2))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\mathrm{Curl}\,A|(\Omega)\right).

Remark 4.5
.

Note that (
4.3
) is invariant under homotethic scalings; therefore, the constant
C(\Omega)
depends only on the geometric shape of
\Omega
, not on its size: for examples, it is the same for all cubes.

The following lemma is the core of the
BV
-compactness as shown in
[
11
]
. For the readers convenience we give the details of the proof.

Lemma 4.6

(
[
11
, Proposition 3]
)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be bounded and open, and let
A_{j}\in L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
be a sequence such that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\|\mathrm{dist}(A_{j},SO(2))\|_{L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=0\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\sup_{j}|\mathrm{Curl}\,(A_{j})|(U)<\infty.

Then, there exists a subsequence
A_{j^{\prime}}
strongly converging in
L^{2}
to a field
A\in BV(U;SO(2))

and

(4.4)

|DA|\leq C|\mathrm{Curl}\,A|\,.

Proof.

We want to apply Corollary
4.3
with
n=2
,
N=2\times 2
,
u_{j}=A_{j}
,
u=A
a
L^{1}
-weak limit of a subsequence of
A_{j}
, and
K=SO(2)
.
First notice that, since

(4.5)

|A_{j}|\leq\mathrm{dist}(A_{j},SO(2))+\sqrt{2},

we have that

A_{j}
is bounded in
L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
and therefore, up to a subsequence, it converges to some
A
weakly in
L^{1}
.
From the rigidity estimate (
4.3
), we obtain that for every cube
Q=Q(x,r)\subset\Omega
there exists a rotation
R^{j}_{Q}\in SO(2)
such that

\|A_{j}-R^{j}_{Q}\|_{L^{2}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\leq C\left(\|\mathrm{dist}(A_{j},SO(2))\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+|\mathrm{Curl}\,A_{j}|(Q)\right).

Then, using Hölder inequality,

(4.6)

\int_{Q}|A_{j}-R^{j}_{Q}|\,dx\leq r\,C\left(\|\mathrm{dist}(A_{j},SO(2))\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+|\mathrm{Curl}\,A_{j}|(Q)\right).

This implies that we can apply Corollary
4.3
with

\gamma_{j}=C\|\mathrm{dist}(A_{j},SO(2))\|_{L^{2}(U)},\qquad\lambda_{j}=C|\mathrm{Curl}\,A_{j}|,

and
\lambda

5.
The cell problem formula

In this section we define the energy per unit of length of a straight interface with normal vector
n\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

separating two grains with constant rotations
R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2)
.
To this aim we need to study two sorts of cell problems, suited for the
\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup
and the
\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf
inequalities, respectively: the former is a thermodynamic limit for minimizers with fixed boundary conditions; the latter accounts for the computation of the asymptotic optimal energy of
a single straight interface.

In view of Remark
2.4
it is enough to consider

, the general line tension will be obtained by a change of variable.

For the purpose of this section, we introduce the following notation:

\displaystyle I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(x):=R^{-}\chi_{\{x_{1}<0\}}(x)+R^{+}\chi_{\{x_{1}\geq 0\}}(x).

Furthermore, we recall the following scaling property:
consider a pair
(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{r})
, where
Q_{r}
is the open square centered at
0
of side
2r
, i.e.,

Q_{r}=\Big(-r,r\Big)\times\Big(-r,r\Big),

then

(5.1)

\begin{split}&(\tilde{A},\tilde{S})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon/\rho}(Q_{r/\rho}),\qquad\tilde{A}(y):=A(\rho y),\quad\tilde{S}:=\rho^{-1}S,\quad\rho>0,\\
&B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)=\rho B_{\frac{\lambda\varepsilon}{\rho}}(\tilde{S})\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S,Q_{r})=\rho^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon/\rho}(\tilde{A},\tilde{S},Q_{r/\rho}).\end{split}

In particular, if

and

, we obtain that

(5.2)

\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A,S,Q_{1})=\varepsilon\mathcal{F}_{1}(\tilde{A},\tilde{S},Q_{1/\varepsilon})

F_{\varepsilon}(A,Q_{1})=\varepsilon F_{1}(\tilde{A},Q_{1/\varepsilon}).

5.1.
The thermodynamic limit

We define the optimal transition energy at scale
L>0
as follows

(5.4)

\begin{split}\psi(R^{-},R^{+},L):=\inf\Big\{F_{1}(A,Q_{L})\,:\ &A\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})\,,\ \\
&A=I_{R^{-},R^{+}}\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus Q_{L^{\prime}},\hbox{for some }L^{\prime}<L\Big\}.\end{split}

The next proposition shows that there exists the thermodynamic limit of the optimal transition functions as the size of the square tends to infinity.

Proposition 5.1
.

For every

For every

\psi(R^{-},R^{+},L)\geq F_{1}(A_{L},Q_{L})-2\eta\,L.

Let

\begin{split}F_{1}(A_{M,L},{Q_{M}})&=F_{1}(A_{M,L},(-L,L)\times(-M,M))\\

\Delta_{4}

\displaystyle v(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\Big(x_{1}+\frac{hx_{1}}{2l}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)-\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)+\frac{(\tilde{\mu}+\mu)}{2}(x_{2}-h)\Big)e_{1}+

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{hx_{1}}{2l}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)-\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)+\frac{(\tilde{\eta}+\eta)}{2}(x_{2}-h)+h\Big)e_{2},\qquad x\in\Delta_{3},

\displaystyle v(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\Big(x_{1}-\frac{hx_{1}}{2l}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)-\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)+\frac{(\tilde{\mu}+\mu)}{2}(x_{2}+h)\Big)e_{1}+

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{hx_{1}}{2l}(\eta-\tilde{\eta})+\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}}(\eta-\tilde{\eta})+(x_{2}+h)\frac{(\eta+\tilde{\eta})}{2}-h\Big)e_{2},\qquad x\in\Delta_{4}.

The field
\hat{D}
is then defined as
\nabla v
, precisley:

(5.15)

\begin{split}&\hat{D}(x)=\hat{D}_{i}(x):=D_{\eta,\mu},\quad x\in\Delta_{i},\quad i\in\{1,6,7\},\\
&\hat{D}(x)=\hat{D}_{8}(x):=D_{\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\mu}},\quad x\in\Delta_{8},\\
&\hat{D}(x)=\hat{D}_{2}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1+\frac{h}{2l}(\mu-\tilde{\mu})&\frac{1}{2}(3\tilde{\mu}-\mu)\\
\frac{h}{2l}(\eta-\tilde{\eta})&\frac{1}{2}(3\tilde{\eta}-\eta)\end{matrix}\right),\quad x\in\Delta_{2},\\
&\hat{D}(x)=\hat{D}_{3}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1+\frac{h}{2l}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)-\frac{h^{2}}{2lx_{2}}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)&\frac{1}{2}(\mu+\tilde{\mu})+\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}^{2}}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)\\
\frac{h}{2l}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)-\frac{h^{2}}{2lx_{2}}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)&\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\tilde{\eta})+\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}^{2}}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)\end{matrix}\right),\quad x\in\Delta_{3},\\
&\hat{D}(x)=\hat{D}_{4}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1-\frac{h}{2l}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)-\frac{h^{2}}{2lx_{2}}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)&\frac{1}{2}(\mu+\tilde{\mu})+\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}^{2}}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)\\
-\frac{h}{2l}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)-\frac{h^{2}}{2lx_{2}}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)&\frac{1}{2}(\eta+\tilde{\eta})+\frac{h^{2}x_{1}}{2lx_{2}^{2}}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)\end{matrix}\right),\quad x\in\Delta_{4},\\
&\hat{D}(x)=\hat{D}_{5}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1+\frac{h}{2l}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)&\frac{1}{2}(3\tilde{\mu}-\mu)\\
\frac{h}{2l}(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)&\frac{1}{2}(3\tilde{\eta}-\eta)\end{matrix}\right),\quad x\in\Delta_{5},\\
&\hat{D}(x)=I,\quad x\in Q^{\prime}.\\
\end{split}

We observe that the following boundary conditions are satisfied

(5.16)

\hat{D}_{7}=D_{\eta,\mu},\qquad\hat{D}_{1}e_{1}=\hat{D}_{6}e_{1}=e_{1},\qquad\hat{D}_{8}=D_{\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\mu}}.

Being the gradient of a function with constant jump on the segments
(-l,-r)\times\{0\}
and
(r,l)\times\{0\}
, the support of
\operatorname{Curl}\hat{D}
is contained in
Q^{\prime}
, and
\hat{D}
has constant but non trivial circulation around
Q^{\prime}
given by

(5.17)

\int_{\partial Q^{\prime}}\hat{D}=\int_{\partial Q}\hat{D}=2h(\mu-\tilde{\mu},\eta-\tilde{\eta}).

We now extend
\hat{D}
to the infinite vertical stripe
(-l,l)\times\mathbb{R}
by repeating its construction periodically in the vertical direction:

\hat{D}(x_{1},x_{2}+2kh)=\hat{D}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad(x_{1},x_{2})\in Q,\quad\forall\;k\in\mathbb{Z},

this field still having compatible circulation since
\hat{D}_{1}e_{1}=\hat{D}_{6}e_{1}
, hence no curl was created in gluing the construction vertically.

In order to extend the construction to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we can now simply set

(5.18)

D(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{cases}D_{\eta,\mu}\,x,\qquad x_{1}<-l,\\
\hat{D}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad|x_{1}|\leq l,\\
D_{\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\mu}}\,x,\qquad x_{1}>l.\end{cases}

We observe that

(5.19)

\operatorname{supp}\mathrm{Curl}\,(D)\subseteq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[-r,r]\times[-\rho+2kh,\rho+2kh],

since again, thanks to the boundary condition
D_{7}=D_{\eta,\mu}
and
D_{8}=D_{\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\mu}}
, no curl was created in extending
\hat{D}
outside
(-l,l)\times\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, since the circulation of
D
around each rectangle
[-r,r]\times[-\rho+2kh,\rho+2kh]
is given by (
5.17
), from (
5.19
) we infer that

(5.20)

\int_{\gamma}D\in h(\mu-\tilde{\mu},\eta-\tilde{\eta})\mathbb{Z},

for every
1
-rectifiable closed loop
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{curl}D
.

We now estimate the energy of
D
in
Q
.
We start with the elastic energy in
\Delta_{3}
and
\Delta_{4}
: for
i=3,4
it holds that

(5.21)

\begin{split}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{D}_{i},SO(2))\leq|\hat{D}_{i}-I|^{2}&\leq C\frac{h^{4}}{l^{2}}[(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)^{2}+(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)^{2}](\frac{1}{x_{2}^{2}}+\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{x_{2}^{4}})\\
&+C\frac{h^{2}}{l^{2}}[(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)^{2}+(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)^{2}]+(\eta+\tilde{\eta}-2),\end{split}

hence

(5.22)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Delta_{i}}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{D}_{i}(x),SO(2))\leq\frac{h^{4}}{l^{2}}[(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)^{2}+(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)^{2}]\int_{\rho}^{h/2}dx_{2}\int_{-\frac{l}{h}x_{2}}^{\frac{l}{h}x_{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{x_{2}^{2}}+\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{x_{2}^{4}}\Big)dx_{1}\\
&+C\frac{h^{3}}{l}[(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)^{2}+(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)^{2}]+lh(\eta+\tilde{\eta}-2)\\
&\leq C\Big(\frac{h^{3}}{l}+hl\Big)[(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)^{2}+(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)^{2}]\Big|\log\Big(\frac{h}{2\rho}\Big)\Big|\\
&+C\frac{h^{3}}{l}[(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)^{2}+(\tilde{\eta}-\eta)^{2}]+hl(\eta+\tilde{\eta}-2).\end{split}

For
\Delta_{i}
with
i=2,5
we have instead

(5.23)

\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{D}_{i},SO(2))\leq|\hat{D}_{i}-I|^{2}=\frac{h^{2}}{4l^{2}}|\mu-\tilde{\mu}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\mu-3\tilde{\mu}|^{2}+\frac{h^{2}}{4l^{2}}|\eta-\tilde{\eta}|^{2}+|1-\frac{1}{2}(3\tilde{\eta}-\eta)|^{2},

hence

(5.24)

\int_{\Delta_{i}}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{D}_{i},SO(2))\leq\frac{h^{3}}{4l}|\mu-\tilde{\mu}|^{2}+\frac{hl}{4}|\mu-3\tilde{\mu}|^{2}+\frac{h^{3}}{4l}|\eta-\tilde{\eta}|^{2}+hl|1-\frac{1}{2}(3\tilde{\eta}-\eta)|^{2}.

Finally for
i=1,6,7
we have

(5.25)

\int_{\Delta_{i}}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(D_{\eta,\mu},SO(2))\leq\int_{\Delta_{i}}|I-D_{\eta,\mu}|^{2}=hl\Big((1-\eta)^{2}+\mu^{2})\Big),

and a similar estimate also holds for
\Delta_{8}
.

To estimate the core energy of
\hat{D}
we recall that support of
\operatorname{Curl}\hat{D}
is contained in
Q^{\prime}
. Hence setting
S=Q^{\prime}
we have

(5.26)

|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)|=|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)|\leq C(r+\lambda\varepsilon)(\rho+\lambda\varepsilon).

5.2.2.
Rotation to compression

Consider as above the rectangles
Q:=Q_{l,h}
,
Q^{\prime}:=Q_{r,\rho}
with
r,\rho<\operatorname{min}\{h,l\}
, and let
\eta,\mu,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be given parameters. We now divide
Q\smallsetminus Q^{\prime}
in
4
regions
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
, with
i=1,\dots,4
, as in Figure
2
.

Figure 2.

In the same spirit of the
compression to compression
construction, the aim is now to define a matrix field
B\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
connecting a rotation
R_{\beta}
and a shear field
D_{\eta,\mu}
via a construction mimicking horizontal dislocations. A fundamental aspect of the construction is the fact that we obtain
\operatorname{curl}B
as a function of the parameters
\eta,\mu,\beta
and
h
.

We start defining
\hat{B}
in a single
Q
. Consider the function

v:Q\setminus(Q^{\prime}\cup\{x_{1}=0,x_{2}>0\})\to\mathbb{R}^{2},

defined as follows:

i)

In the region
\Delta_{4}
we have

v(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{cases}D_{\eta,\mu}\,x\qquad&\hbox{if }x\in\Delta^{\prime}_{4}\setminus\{x_{1}<0,x_{2}>0\}\\
D_{\eta,\mu}[x_{1}+\xi_{1},x_{2}+\xi_{2}]\qquad&\hbox{if }x\in\Delta^{\prime}_{4}\cap\{x_{1}<0,x_{2}>0\}\end{cases}

where the vector
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):=(-2h\frac{\mu}{\eta}\cos\beta-2h\sin\beta,\frac{2h}{\eta}\cos\beta-2h)
is obtained by solving
D_{\eta,\mu}[-h+\xi_{1},h+\xi_{2}]-D_{\eta,\mu}[-h,-h]=R_{\beta}2he_{2}
;

ii)

In
\Delta^{\prime}_{1}
,
\Delta^{\prime}_{2}
, and the function
v
is the linear interpolation over the vertices of the
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
’s;

iii)

In
\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
we interpolate linearly along the vertical direction

\displaystyle v(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\Big((\mu+\frac{l}{x_{1}}(\mu+\sin\beta))x_{2}+x_{1}-h(\mu+\sin\beta)\Big)e_{1}+

\displaystyle\Big((\eta+\frac{l}{x_{1}}(\eta-\cos\beta))x_{2}+h(\cos\beta-\eta)\Big)e_{2},\qquad x\in\Delta^{\prime}_{3}.

We define the field
B=\nabla v
, which is then given by

(5.27)

\begin{split}&\hat{B}(x)=I,\quad x\in Q^{\prime},\\
&\hat{B}(x)=\hat{B}_{1}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1+\frac{h}{l}\mu+\frac{h}{l}\sin\beta&-\sin\beta\\
\frac{h}{l}\eta-\frac{h}{l}\cos\beta&\cos\beta\end{matrix}\right),\quad x\in\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\\
&\hat{B}(x)=\hat{B}_{2}:=\left(\begin{matrix}-2\frac{h}{l}\sin\beta-2\frac{h}{l}\mu+1&-2\sin\beta-\mu\\
2\frac{h}{l}\cos\beta-2\frac{h}{l}\eta&2\cos\beta-\eta\end{matrix}\right),\quad x\in\Delta^{\prime}_{2},\\
&\hat{B}(x)=\hat{B}_{3}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1-\frac{lx_{2}}{x_{1}^{2}}(\mu+\sin\beta)&\mu+\frac{l}{x_{1}}(\mu+\sin\beta)\\
-\frac{lx_{2}}{x_{1}^{2}}(\eta-\cos\beta)&\eta+\frac{l}{x_{1}}(\eta-\cos\beta)\end{matrix}\right),\quad x\in\Delta^{\prime}_{3},\\
&\hat{B}(x)=\hat{B}_{4}(x):=D_{\eta,\mu},\quad x\in\Delta^{\prime}_{4}.\\
\end{split}

We observe that the following boundary conditions are satisfied:

(5.28)

\hat{B}_{1}e_{2}=R_{\beta}e_{2},\qquad\hat{B}_{4}=D_{\eta,\mu}.

As before we compute the circulation of
\hat{B}
around
Q^{\prime}
following
\partial Q
as a path. With a rapid calculation we find that the clockwise circulation of
\hat{B}
is given by

(5.29)

\int_{\partial Q^{\prime}}\hat{B}=\int_{\partial Q}\hat{B}=2h(-\mu-\sin\beta,\cos\beta-\eta).

We now extend
\hat{B}
to the infinite vertical stripe
(-l,l)\times\mathbb{R}
by repeating its construction periodically in the vertical direction:

\hat{B}(x_{1},x_{2}+2kh)=\hat{B}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad(x_{1},x_{2})\in Q,\quad\forall\;k\in\mathbb{Z},

this field still having compatible circulation because of the boundary conditions (
5.28
), hence no curl was created in gluing the construction vertically.

To extend the construction to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we can now simply set

(5.30)

B(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{cases}R_{\beta}\,x,\qquad x_{1}<-l,\\
\hat{B}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad|x_{1}|\leq l,\\
D_{\eta,\mu}\,x,\qquad x_{1}>l.\end{cases}

We observe that

(5.31)

\operatorname{supp}\mathrm{Curl}\,(B)\subseteq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[-r,r]\times[-\rho+2kh,\rho+2kh],

since again, thanks to the boundary condition
B_{1}e_{2}=R_{\beta}e_{2}
and
B_{4}=D_{\eta,\mu}
, no curl was created in extending
\hat{B}
outside
(-l,l)\times\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, since the circulation of
B
around each rectangle
[-r,r]\times[-\rho+2kh,\rho+2kh]
is given by (
5.29
), from (
5.31
) we infer that

(5.32)

\int_{\gamma}B\in h(-\mu-\sin\beta,\cos\beta-\eta)\mathbb{Z},

for every
1
-rectifiable closed loop
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{curl}B
.

We now estimate the energy of
B
in
Q
.
Similarly to the
compression to compression
construction, via a direct computation, we use the fact that
\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{B}_{i},SO(2))\leq|\hat{B}_{i}-Id|^{2}
for
i=1,2,3
to find the following estimates

(5.33)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Delta_{1}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{B}_{1},SO(2))\leq\frac{h^{3}}{l}|\mu+\sin\beta|^{2}+hl|\sin\beta|^{2}+\frac{h^{3}}{l}|\eta-\cos\beta|^{2}+hl|1-\cos\beta|^{2},\\
&\int_{\Delta_{2}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{B}_{2},SO(2))\leq 4\frac{h^{3}}{l}|\mu+\sin\beta|^{2}+hl|\mu+2\sin\beta|^{2}+4\frac{h^{3}}{l}|\cos\beta-\eta|^{2}+hl|2\cos\beta-\eta-1|^{2},\\
&\int_{\Delta_{3}^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(\hat{B}_{3},SO(2))\leq C\Big(\frac{h^{3}}{l}+hl\Big)[(\mu+\sin\beta)^{2}+(\eta-\cos\beta)^{2}]\Big|\log\Big(\frac{2r}{l}\Big)\Big|+2hl(\mu^{2}+(1-\eta)^{2}).\end{split}

The elastic energy in
\Delta^{\prime}_{4}
is in turn given in (
5.25
).

To estimate the core energy of
\hat{B}
we recall that the support of
\operatorname{Curl}(\hat{B})
is contained in
Q^{\prime}
. Hence setting
S=Q^{\prime}
we have

(5.34)

|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)|\leq C(r+\lambda\varepsilon)(\rho+\lambda\varepsilon).

5.2.3.
Rotation to rotation

We now combine the constructions above in order to obtain the interface between two different rotations
R^{-}
and
R^{+}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For every
R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2)
and for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
l=l(R^{-},R^{+}),

h=h(R^{-},R^{+})>0
and a field
A_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})

such that

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}(x)=I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(x)\qquad\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left({(-l/2,l/2)\times\mathbb{R}}\right)

and

(5.35)

F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},Q_{L})\leq CL|R^{-}-R^{+}|\big(\big|\log\big|R^{-}-R^{+}\big|\big|+1\big)\qquad\forall\;L>h,

where
C>0
is a dimensional constant. In particular,

(5.36)

\psi_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})\leq C|R^{-}-R^{+}|\big(\big|\log\big|R^{-}-R^{+}\big|\big|+1\big)\qquad\forall\;R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2).

Proof.

Since
F_{\varepsilon}(I_{R^{-},R^{+}},Q_{L})=2L\lambda
, we need to show (
5.35
) only in the case
|R^{-}-R^{+}|
is sufficiently small.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
R^{-}=R_{\theta-\alpha}
and
R^{+}=R_{\theta+\alpha}
for some
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
(we are considering the square lattice that has a
\pi/2
invariance) and small
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, so that
|R^{-}-R^{+}|=2\sqrt{2}\sin\alpha
and
\sin\alpha<1/8
.
Set

(5.37)

\begin{split}&\eta=\cos\alpha+\cos\theta\sin\theta\sin\alpha,\quad\tilde{\eta}=\cos\alpha-\cos\theta\sin\theta\sin\alpha,\quad\mu=\sin^{2}\theta\sin\alpha\quad\tilde{\mu}=-\mu,\\
&h_{D}=\frac{\varepsilon\tau}{\sin\theta\sin\alpha},\quad l_{D}=\frac{\varepsilon\tau}{\sin\alpha},\quad\rho_{D}=\frac{\varepsilon\tau}{\sin\theta},\quad r_{D}=\varepsilon\tau,\\
&h_{B}=h_{C}=\frac{\varepsilon\tau}{\cos\theta\sin\alpha},\quad l_{B}=\frac{\varepsilon\tau}{\sin\alpha},\quad r_{B}=r_{C}=\frac{\varepsilon\tau}{\cos\theta},\quad\rho_{B}=\rho_{C}=\varepsilon\tau.\end{split}

We apply the
compression to compression
construction with the above choice of the parameters
\eta,\tilde{\eta},\mu,\tilde{\mu},h_{D},l_{D},r_{D},\rho_{D}
to obtain a matrix field
D_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
that we will call simply
D
dropping the dependence in
\varepsilon
for ease of notation. We have

(5.38)

S_{D}:=\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{curl}D\subseteq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[-r_{D},r_{D}]\times[-\rho_{D}+2kh_{D},\rho_{D}+2kh_{D}]

and in view of (
5.20
) it holds

(5.39)

\int_{\gamma}D\in\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon\tau R_{-\theta}e_{2},

for all closed loop
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus S_{D}
.

Consider now the
rotated to compression
construction with parameters
\beta=-\alpha
and
h_{B},l_{B},

r_{B},\rho_{B},\eta,\mu
as in (
5.37
) to obtain a field
B\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
. We have

(5.40)

S_{B}:=\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{curl}B\subseteq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[-r_{B},r_{B}]\times[-\rho_{B}+2kh_{B},\rho_{B}+2kh_{B}]

and in view of (
5.32
) we have

(5.41)

\int_{\gamma}B\in\varepsilon\tau\mathbb{Z}R_{-\theta}e_{1},

for all closed loop
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus S_{B}
.

The last element we need is a rotated version of the
rotation to compression
construction. Precisely, consider the construction of Section
5.2.2
with parameters
\beta=\alpha
and
h_{C},l_{C},r_{C},\rho_{C},\tilde{\eta},\tilde{\mu}
as in (
5.37
) to obtain a field
\tilde{C}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
.
Now we set

\displaystyle C(x_{1},x_{2})=\tilde{C}(-x_{1},-x_{2})\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

see Figure (
3
). Clearly, in view of (
5.32
),
C
is such that its circulation satisfies

(5.42)

\int_{\gamma}C\in\varepsilon\tau\mathbb{Z}R_{-\theta}e_{1},

for all closed loop
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus S_{C}
where
S_{C}=S_{B}
.

Figure 3.
The matrix fields
B
,

, and

We can now define a field on the whole space by simply gluing together the three constructions, precisely we define the field
A_{\theta}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
as follows:

(5.43)

A_{\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}B(x+l_{D}+l_{B})\quad&x\in(-\infty,-l_{D})\times\mathbb{R},\\
D(x)&x\in(-l_{D},l_{D})\times\mathbb{R},\\
C(x-l_{D}-l_{C})&x\in(l_{D},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Observe that,
because of the boundary conditions imposed to
B,D
and
C
, and from (
5.31
) and (
5.19
), no incompatibilities arise in gluing them together. Hence
\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{curl}A_{\theta}
is given by the disjoint union of the (appropriately translated) supports of the curl of
B,C
and
D
:

(5.44)

S_{A_{\theta}}:=\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{curl}A_{\theta}\subseteq\big(S_{B}-(l_{D}+l_{B},0)\big)\cup S_{D}\cup\big(S_{B}+(l_{D}+l_{B},0)\big).

Therefore, if we set
A:=R_{\theta}A_{\theta}
, then
in view of (
5.39
), (
5.41
) and (
5.42
), we have that
A
satisfies
(H2)
. Furthermore,
since
R^{+}=R_{\theta+\alpha}
and

, we have that

in
\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus(-2l_{B}-l_{D},l_{D}+2l_{C})\times\mathbb{R}
.

We now prove the upper bound
(
5.35
).
We set

(5.45)

h=4h_{B}+2h_{D},\qquad l=4l_{B}+2l_{D},

we observe that
l\leq h
and estimate the energy of
A
in the set
Q_{L}
, with

. Set
N_{D}=\lfloor\frac{L}{2h_{D}}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{L\sin\alpha\sin\theta}{2\tau\varepsilon}\rfloor
and

.

in the set

From (
5.34
), (
5.26
) and (
5.44
)
we have that
S_{A}\cap Q_{L}
is contained in the set
S
which is the union of at most

rectangles of area either

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{core}^{\varepsilon}(S):=|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)|&\leq 2(N_{B}+2)\varepsilon^{2}(\lambda+\frac{2\tau}{\cos\theta})(\lambda+2\tau)\\

the constant

depending on
\lambda
and
\tau
.
Moreover, since the energy is invariant under left multiplication by rotations, we have that the elastic energy of

is the same of
A
. Hence we found that

(5.47)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{el}^{\varepsilon}(A_{\theta},S)&\leq(N_{B}+2)\int_{Q_{l_{B},h_{B}}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(Q_{r_{B},\rho_{B}})}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(B,SO(2))\,dx\\
&+(N_{C}+2)\int_{Q_{l_{C},h_{C}}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(Q_{r_{C},\rho_{C}}))}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(C,SO(2))\,dx\\
&+(N_{D}+2)\int_{Q_{l_{D},h_{D}}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(Q_{r_{D},\rho_{D}}))}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(D,SO(2))\,dx.\end{split}

We now estimate the elastic energy due to
B
and
C
. We present only the estimates for
B
, the ones for
C
being identical.
Given the choice of the parameters in (
5.37
), from (
5.33
) and (
5.25
)
we infer that

(5.48)

\begin{split}&(N_{B}+2)\int_{Q_{l_{B},h_{B}}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(Q_{r_{B},\rho_{B}})}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(B,SO(2))\,dx\\
&\leq C(N_{B}+2)(h_{B}\varepsilon\tau\sin\alpha\left(\big|\log(\sin\alpha)\big|+1\right)+h_{B}\tau\varepsilon\sin\alpha)\\
&\leq CL\varepsilon\tau\sin\alpha\left(\big|\log(\sin\alpha)\big|+1\right)+CL\tau\varepsilon\sin\alpha.\end{split}

In the same way, from (
5.22
), (
5.24
) and (
5.25
) we infer that the elastic energy due to
D
can be estimated as follows

(5.49)

\begin{split}&(N_{D}+2)\int_{Q_{l_{D},h_{D}}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(Q_{r_{D},\rho_{D}}))}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(D,SO(2))\,dx\\
&\leq CL\varepsilon\tau\sin\alpha\left(\big|\log(\sin\alpha)\big|+1\right)+CL\tau\varepsilon\sin\alpha.\end{split}

Then, combining the estimates above we obtain

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(A,Q_{L})&\leq CL\sin\alpha\left(\big|\log(\sin\alpha)\big|+1\right)+CL\sin\alpha\\
&\leq CL\sin\alpha\left(\big|\log(\sin\alpha)\big|+1\right),\end{split}

with the constant
C
depending on
\tau
and
\lambda
.
Finally, to infer (
5.36
) we set

\tilde{A}(x)=\begin{cases}A(x)\quad&x\in Q_{L^{\prime}},\\
I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus Q_{L^{\prime}},\end{cases}

with
L^{\prime}=h(\lfloor\frac{L}{h}\rfloor-2)
, and notice that
(A,S)\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{L})
with
S=\mathrm{supp}\,\mathrm{Curl}\,A
and

(5.50)

F_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{A},Q_{L})\leq CL\sin\alpha\left(|\log(\sin\alpha)|+1\right)+Ch,

for a dimensional constant
C>0
, thus implying (
5.36
) by taking the limit as
L\to+\infty
.
∎

5.3.
The optimal grain boundary energy

Here we give an expression for the asymptotic energy, namely the
\Gamma
-liminf, per unit length of a straight interface between two different grains. Again thanks to the invariance under right rotation we only consider the case
n=e_{1}
.

Given two rotations
R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2)
we define the energy density

(5.51)

\begin{split}\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+},Q):=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},Q)\,:&(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(Q)\,,\\
&A_{\varepsilon}\chi_{B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}\to I_{R^{-},R^{+}}\ \hbox{in }L^{1}(Q)\Big\}.\end{split}

If
Q=Q_{\frac{1}{2}}
is the square of side
1
, then we use the simplified notation

\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+}):=\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+},Q_{\frac{1}{2}}).

The main fact leading to the identification of the optimal limiting energy with the thermodynamic limit is the proof that the optimal interfacial energy can be achieved by concentrating defects in a stripe near the interface.

Theorem 5.3
.

For every
R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2)
there exists a sequence
A_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
such that

(5.52)

A_{\varepsilon}\to I_{R^{-},R^{+}}\quad\textup{in $L^{1}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})$}\quad\textup{and}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})=\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+}),

and for every
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})

(5.53)

A_{\varepsilon}(x)=I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(x)\qquad\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ :\ |x_{1}|>\delta,

definitively as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

An immediate consequence of the theorem is the following.

Corollary 5.4
.

The optimal interfacial energy equals the thermodynamic limit

\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+})=\psi_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})\qquad\forall\;R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2).

Proof.

We show separately the two inequalities
\psi_{\infty}\leq\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{0}\leq\psi_{\infty}
.

For the first inequality, consider the sequence
A_{\varepsilon}
, whose existence is guaranteed by Theorem
5.3
such that (
5.52
) and (
5.53
) hold.
For any fixed
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
, we set

(5.54)

\begin{split}&\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}=A_{\varepsilon}\chi_{Q_{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}+I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(1-\chi_{Q_{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}),\\
\hat{S}_{\varepsilon}=S_{A_{\varepsilon}}\cup\{x_{1}=0,\,&1/2-\sigma\leq|x_{2}|<1/2\}\cup\{|x_{1}|\leq\delta,x_{2}=\pm(1/2-\sigma)\}.\end{split}

Since (
5.53
) holds definitively,
(\hat{A}_{\varepsilon},\hat{S}_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}})
(indeed, points which are added to
S_{A_{\varepsilon}}
are connected to the boundary of
Q_{\frac{1}{2}}
and are not considered to determine the compatibility condition (H2)).
Moreover,

F_{\varepsilon}(\hat{A}_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})\leq F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})+C(\delta+\sigma),

for a costant
C>0
depending on
\lambda
. Therefore, by the scaling properties of the energy (
5.3
), we infer that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\psi\big(R^{-},R^{+},\varepsilon^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\inf\Big\{F_{\varepsilon}(A,Q_{\frac{1}{2}}):A=I_{R^{-},R^{+}}\quad\textup{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus Q_{{\frac{1}{2}}-\eta},\ \eta>0\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq F_{\varepsilon}(\hat{A}_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})\leq F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})+C(\delta+\sigma).

Sending
\varepsilon
to zero, we get

\displaystyle\psi_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varepsilon\psi\big(R^{-},R^{+},\varepsilon^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})+C(\delta+\sigma)

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+})+C(\delta+\sigma).

By the arbitrariness of
\delta,\sigma>0
the conclusion follows.

As far as the reverse inequality
\psi_{0}\leq\psi_{\infty}
is concerned, for every
\eta>0
we find
L>0
and
A_{L}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
be a matrix field such that
A_{L}=I_{R^{-},R^{+}}
outside
Q_{L^{\prime}}
, for some
L^{\prime}<L
, and

(5.55)

\psi_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})\geq\frac{1}{2L}\psi(R^{-},R^{+},L)-\eta\geq\frac{1}{2L}F_{1}(A_{L},Q_{L})-2\eta.

We consider the field obtained from
A_{L}
extending it periodically in the vertical direction:

\hat{A}_{L}(x_{1},x_{2}+2kL):=A_{L}(x_{1},x_{2})\qquad\forall\;(x_{1},x_{2})\in Q_{L},\quad\forall\;k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then, the fields

A_{\varepsilon}(x):=\hat{A}_{L}(\varepsilon^{-1}x)\qquad\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

belong to
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
and, considering the periodicity of
A_{\varepsilon}
in the vertical direction with period
2L\varepsilon
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1}}|A_{\varepsilon}(x)-I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{2L\varepsilon}\Big\rfloor+2\right)\int_{Q_{L\varepsilon}}|A_{\varepsilon}(x)-I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle=\left(\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{2L\varepsilon}\Big\rfloor+2\right)\varepsilon^{2}\int_{Q_{L}}|A_{L}(x)-I_{R^{-},R^{+}}(x)|\,dx\to 0\quad\textup{as }\;\varepsilon\to 0^{+},

where
\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{2L\varepsilon}\Big\rfloor+2
is an upper bound for the number of disjoint squares of side-length
2L\varepsilon
with center in the
x_{2}
-axis contained in
Q_{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Analogously, the energy of
A_{\varepsilon}
is estimated as

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})\leq\left(\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{2L\varepsilon}\Big\rfloor+2\right)\varepsilon F_{1}(A_{L},Q_{L}).

Therefore, using the sequence
A_{\varepsilon}
to test
\psi_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}F_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{2L\varepsilon}\Big\rfloor+2\right)\varepsilon F_{1}(A_{L},Q_{L})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}F_{1}(A_{L},Q_{L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.scelta}}}{{\leq}}\psi_{\infty}(R^{-},R^{+})+2\eta,

and by the arbitrariness of
\eta>0
the conclusion follows.
∎

The proof of Theorem
5.3
is postponed to the next sections.

6.
Optimal recovery sequences

For the proof of Theorem
5.3
we need to construct a recovery sequence for the optimal grain boundary energy which is constantly equal to two rotations on the two sides of the interface.
This will be achieved in several steps in what follows.

6.1.
Energy concentration

In the first proposition we show that the recovery sequences of matrix fields
A_{\varepsilon}
for computing the optimal grain boundary energy concentrate the energy in a neighborhood of the limiting interface.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
R^{-},R^{+}\in SO(2)
and
(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}})
be such that

A_{\varepsilon}\chi_{B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}\to I_{R^{-},R^{+}}\quad\textup{in $L^{1}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})$}\quad\textup{and}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}})=\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+}).

Then, for every
\delta\in\big(0,\frac{1}{4}\big)
, we have that

(6.1)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},Q_{\frac{1}{2}}\setminus Q_{\delta,\frac{1}{2}})=0,

where
Q_{\delta,{\frac{1}{2}}}=(-\delta,\delta)\times(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0,1\}
we consider the rectangles

E_{k,i}=\Big(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\Big)\times\Big(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{k},-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i+1}{k}\Big)\qquad i=0,..,k-1,

and let
Q^{k,i}
the square of side-length
\frac{1}{k}
centered at
(0,-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{k}+\frac{1}{2k})
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
we choose
i_{0}(\varepsilon)\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
so that

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},E_{k,i_{0}(\varepsilon)})=\min_{i}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},E_{k,i}).

By the scaling property (
5.1
) we then have that

(6.2)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{k}\,\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+})&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},E_{k,i})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},E_{k,i_{0}(\varepsilon)})\\
&\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},Q^{k,i_{0}(\varepsilon)})\geq\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+},Q_{\frac{1}{2k}})=\frac{1}{k}\,\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+}).\end{split}

This shows also that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},E_{k,i})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},Q^{k,i})=\psi_{0}(R^{-},R^{+},Q_{\frac{1}{2k}})\qquad\forall\;i\in\{0,...,k-1\}.

Passing to the complementary set of
T_{\delta}
, we conclude (
6.1
) for
\delta=\frac{1}{2k}
. The conclusion for every
\delta\leq\frac{1}{4}
is an immediate consequence of the monotonicity of
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},Q_{\delta,\frac{1}{2}})
with respect to
\delta
.
∎

6.2.
Clearing out

If a matrix field is
L^{2}
closed to a fixed rotation and its energy is infinitesimal, then incompatibilities can be actually removed on a large part of the domain replacing the field with the constant rotation. This fact is used to change the boundary conditions of an optimal sequence for
\psi_{0}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
R\in SO(2)
and let
(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}((a,b)\times(c,d))
and

(6.3)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},(a,b)\times(c,d))+\int_{(0,1)^{2}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}|A_{\varepsilon}-R|^{2}\,dx\to 0\quad\hbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Then, for every
\sigma>0
there exists a sequence
\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}((a,b)\times(c,d);\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
converging to
R
in
L^{2}
such that

(6.4)

\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}=A_{\varepsilon}\quad\hbox{in }(a,a+\sigma)\times(c,d),\qquad\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon}=R\quad\hbox{in }(a+2\sigma,b)\times(c,d),

and

(6.5)

F_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon},(a,b)\times(c,d))\to 0\quad\hbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

The proof of Proposition
6.2
requires the interpolation result below.

6.2.1.
Elastic interpolation with incompatibilities

In the following, for every
\ell,L>0
we consider the rectangle of sides
\ell
and
L

Q^{0}_{\ell,L}:=(0,\ell)\times(0,L).

Moreover, given any vector valued function
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we set

(6.6)

u(x_{1},x_{2})=\left(1-\frac{x_{1}}{\ell}\right)\int_{0}^{x_{2}}g(t)\,dt\qquad(x_{1},x_{2})\in Q^{0}_{\ell,L}.

Clearly,
u
is Lipschitz continuous and

(6.7)

\nabla u(x_{1},x_{2})e_{1}=-\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{x_{2}}g(t)dt,\qquad\nabla u(x_{1},x_{2})e_{2}=\left(1-\frac{x_{1}}{\ell}\right)g(x_{2}).

In particular,
\nabla ue_{1}
is a continuous vector field, which extends continuously to
\partial Q^{0}_{\ell,L}
. Whereas,
\nabla ue_{2}
is the tensor product of a continuous function in
x_{1}
and a bounded function in
x_{2}
. Note that, if
g
is piecewise continuous, the same holds true for
\nabla ue_{2}
, thus allowing to define the boundary trace of
\nabla ue_{2}
(this will be always the case in the applications below).
Moreover, by direct computation

(6.8)

\begin{split}\int_{Q^{0}_{\ell,L}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx&=\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{L}\left|\int_{0}^{x_{2}}g(t)dt\right|^{2}dx_{2}+\frac{\ell}{3}\int_{0}^{L}|g(t)|^{2}dt\\
&\leq\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}\right)\ell\int_{0}^{L}|g(t)|^{2}dt.\end{split}

The discussion above is the building block of the construction of an elastic interpolation with incompatibilities, which will be used in the proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
L,\ell>0
with
\ell\geq\varepsilon
and
g\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
. There exists a field
A\in L^{2}(Q^{0}_{\ell,L},\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
such that

(6.9)

Ae_{2}=\left(1-\frac{x_{1}}{\ell}\right)g(x_{2})

and
Ae_{1}
is piecewise continuous and
satisfies

(6.10)

Ae_{1}=\begin{cases}0&\hbox{on }(0,\ell)\times\{0\},\\
a_{0}&\hbox{on }(0,\ell)\times\{L\},\end{cases}

for some
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover if
\ell\int_{0}^{L}|g|^{2}dt<\varepsilon L
, then

(6.11)

\int_{Q^{0}_{\ell,L}}|A|^{2}dx+|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)|\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}L^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\ell\int_{0}^{L}|g|^{2}(t)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}+C\frac{\varepsilon^{2}L}{\ell},

where
C>0
is a dimensional constant,
S:=\mathrm{supp}\,\mathrm{Curl}\,A
is a union of finitely many horizontal segments of lenght
\ell
,
and
Ae_{1}
jumps between across these horizontal segments by a vector in
\frac{1}{\ell}\varepsilon\tau\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
g\not\equiv 0
, otherwise
A=0
satisfies the conclusions.
We set

(6.12)

M:=\left(\frac{L\varepsilon}{\ell\int_{0}^{L}|g(t)|^{2}dt}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

We decompose the rectangle
Q^{0}_{\ell,L}
into the
N=\lfloor\frac{L}{M\ell}\rfloor+1
rectangles

E_{j}=(0,\ell)\times((j-1)M\ell,jM\ell)\qquad\hbox{with }j=1,\ldots,N-1;\ \ E_{N}=(0,\ell)\times((N-1)M\ell,L).

In each of these rectangles
E_{i}
we consider the function
u_{i}
provided
in (
6.6
) and define
A_{i}=\nabla u_{i}
. By construction the field
A_{i}
has a constant tangential component at the horizontal sides of
E_{i}
. Precisely for every
i=1,\ldots,N-1

A_{i}(x_{1},(i-1)M\ell)e_{1}=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\ A_{i}(x_{1},iM\ell)e_{1}=\bar{g}_{i}:=-\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{(i-1)M\ell}^{iM\ell}g(t)\,dt.

We then project on the lattice
\tau\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the vector
\ell\bar{g}_{i}
and get

g_{i}^{\varepsilon}:=\frac{P_{\tau\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(\ell\bar{g}_{i})}{\ell},

where
P_{\varepsilon\tau\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(w)=(i\varepsilon\tau,j\varepsilon\tau)
for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
w\in[i\varepsilon\tau,(i+1)\varepsilon\tau)\times[j\varepsilon\tau,(j+1)\varepsilon\tau)
and
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. So that in particular
|\ell(g_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{g}_{i})|<\sqrt{2}\tau\varepsilon
. Then we define the field as follows

A(x)=A_{1}\chi_{E_{1}}(x)+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\chi_{E_{i}}(x)\left[A_{i}(x)+(g_{i-1}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{g}_{i-1})\otimes e_{1}\right].

Note that the field
Ae_{1}
jumps between two neighbouring rectangles by a vector in
\frac{1}{\ell}\varepsilon\tau\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and

(6.13)

\mathrm{supp}\,\mathrm{Curl}\,A=\bigcup_{j\in J}(0,\ell)\times\{jM\ell\},\qquad\hbox{with }J\subset\{1,\ldots,N-1\}.

It remains to compute the energy of
A
.
By direct computation we have that

(6.14)

\begin{split}\int_{Q^{0}_{\ell,L}}|A|^{2}dx&\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\int_{E_{j}}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx+M\ell^{2}|g_{j}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{g}_{j}|^{2}\right)\\
&\leq 2\left(M^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\right)\ell\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{(j-1)M\ell}^{jM\ell}|g(t)|^{2}dt+2\sum_{j=1}^{N}M\ell^{2}|g_{j}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{g}_{j}|^{2}\\
&\leq 2\left(M^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\right)\ell\int_{0}^{L}|g(t)|^{2}dt+C\frac{L}{\ell}\varepsilon^{2}.\end{split}

Combining this estimate with the information on the support
S
of the
\mathrm{Curl}\,A
in (
6.13
) and the definition of
M
we get

(6.15)

\begin{split}\int_{Q^{0}_{\ell,L}}|A|^{2}dx+|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S)|&\leq C\,\frac{L}{\ell}\varepsilon^{2}+CM^{2}\ell\,\int_{0}^{L}|g(t)|^{2}dt+(N-1)\big(\lambda\varepsilon\ell+\pi\lambda^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\big)\\
&\leq C\frac{L}{\ell}\varepsilon^{2}+CM^{2}\ell\,\int_{0}^{L}|g(t)|^{2}dt+C\frac{L}{M}\varepsilon+C\frac{L\varepsilon^{2}}{M\ell}\\
&\leq C\frac{L}{\ell}\varepsilon^{2}+C\,\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}L^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\ell\,\int_{0}^{L}|g(t)|^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},\end{split}

where we used that
\varepsilon\leq\ell
to bound the last term.
∎

6.2.2.
Proof of Proposition
6.2

Without loss of generality we prove the result in the case
(a,b)\times(c,d)=(0,1)^{2}
.

Fix
\sigma>0
and denote
T^{+}_{\sigma}=(\sigma,2\sigma)\times(0,1)
. We then set

(6.16)

\omega_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T^{+}_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}\mathrm{dist}^{2}(A_{\varepsilon},SO(2))dx+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})|+\int_{T^{+}_{\sigma}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}|A_{\varepsilon}-R|^{2}\,dx,

and using (
6.3
), we have that
\omega_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
Let
\rho_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon M_{\varepsilon}
, with
M_{\varepsilon}=1/\sqrt{\varepsilon\vee\omega_{\varepsilon}}
, and
N_{\varepsilon}:=\lfloor\sigma/\rho_{\varepsilon}\rfloor
. Note that
\rho_{\varepsilon}\to 0
and
\omega_{\varepsilon}M_{\varepsilon}\to 0
.

We consider the
N_{\varepsilon}
rectangles
T_{i}=(\sigma+i\rho_{\varepsilon},\sigma+(i+1)\rho_{\varepsilon})\times(0,1)\subset T^{+}_{\sigma}
, with

.

) we have that

\sum_{i=0}^{N_{\varepsilon}-1}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},T_{i})+\int_{T_{i}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}|A_{\varepsilon}-R|^{2}\,dx\leq\omega_{\varepsilon},

we can find an index

(6.17)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(A_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon},T_{i_{0}})+\int_{T_{i_{0}}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}|A_{\varepsilon}-R|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{N_{\varepsilon}}\omega_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\omega_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}.

In particular, from (

), we infer that

|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})\cap T_{i_{0}}|\leq C\varepsilon\,\omega_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon},

and a simple covering argument implies that for

|B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(p(S_{\varepsilon}))\cap(\sigma+i_{0}\rho_{\varepsilon},\sigma+(i_{0}+1)\rho_{\varepsilon})|\leq C\omega_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}}{4},

Therefore, we can choose a vertical section
\gamma^{-}=\{t_{0}\}\times(0,1)
for some

(6.18)

\gamma^{-}\cap B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})=\emptyset,

and

(6.19)

\rho_{\varepsilon}\int_{\gamma^{-}}|A_{\varepsilon}(x)-R|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\leq 2\int_{T_{i_{0}}\setminus B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(S_{\varepsilon})}|A_{\varepsilon}(x)-R|^{2}dx\leq C\omega_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}=C\varepsilon\omega_{\varepsilon}M_{\varepsilon}.

Moreover we can choose
\gamma^{-}
so that the tangential component of
A_{\varepsilon}
on
\gamma^{-}
is well defined in
L^{2}(\gamma^{-})
.
We apply then Lemma
6.3
in the rectangle

\hat{T}:=(t_{0},\sigma+(i_{0}+1)\rho_{\varepsilon})\times(0,1)
with

g(t)=\big(A_{\varepsilon}(t_{0},t)-R\big)e_{2},

getting a field
\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}:[t_{0},\sigma+(i_{0}+1)\rho_{\varepsilon}]\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
, with
\hat{S}_{\varepsilon}:=\mathrm{supp}\,\mathrm{Curl}\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}
, satisfying

Lemma A.1
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set, let
\sigma>0
and

,
h>1
. Then there exists a constant

(depending only on

and

Proof.

Let

for every

,
i\neq j
(maximal for inclusion: for every

there exists

such that

Note that, since

is bounded, the set

By construction, for every point in

Then, using the fact that the balls

\displaystyle|B_{h\sigma}(S)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J}|B_{(h+2)\sigma}(x_{j})|=(h+2)^{n}\sum_{j\in J}|B_{\sigma}(x_{j})|

∎

Lemma A.2
.

\mu
be a measure whose support is contained in the union of balls
\{B_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i})\}_{i\in I}

\hat{Q}_{j}=x_{Q_{j}}+\frac{5}{4}(Q_{j}-x_{Q_{j}})
.
By the property of the Whitney decomposition
\hat{Q}_{j}\subset W
.
Then for every
\ell
we set

\tilde{u}_{\ell}(x):=\begin{cases}\sum_{j:\ \hat{Q}_{j}\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\neq\emptyset}\varphi_{j}(x)[u_{\ell}(z_{j})+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})]&\textrm{if }x\in B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\cap U,\\
u_{\ell}(x)&\textrm{if }x\in B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\setminus U.\end{cases}

Note that, if
\hat{Q}_{j}\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\neq\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle|x_{Q_{j}}-p_{\ell}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{diam}\,(\hat{Q}_{j})}{2}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{5\mathrm{diam}\,(Q_{j})}{8}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{5\mathrm{dist}(Q_{j},K)}{8C}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{15\varepsilon}{8C}<\varepsilon.

This implies that

|z_{j}-p_{\ell}|\leq|z_{j}-x_{Q_{j}}|+|x_{Q_{j}}-p_{\ell}|<4\varepsilon.

Thus, the definition of
\tilde{u}_{\ell}
is well-posed because
u_{\ell}(z_{j})
is defined.
By Whitney Extension Theorem,
\tilde{u}_{\ell}
is a
C^{1}
extension of
{u_{\ell}}_{|_{B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\setminus W}}
in
B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})
.

If
x\in B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{m})
, then there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{\ell}=u_{m}+c
in
B_{6\varepsilon}(p_{\ell})\cap B_{6\varepsilon}(p_{m})
because
\nabla u_{\ell}=\nabla u_{m}=A
in
B_{6\varepsilon}(p_{\ell})\cap B_{6\varepsilon}(p_{m})
. In this case it is simple to verify that also the gradients of the extensions coincide.
Indeed, for every
x\in U
let
\mathcal{I}_{x}
be the set of indices of squares
Q_{i}
such that
\varphi_{i}(x)\neq 0
(note that
\mathcal{I}_{x}
is made at most of
4
squares); then
\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\nabla\varphi_{i}(x)=0
and

\displaystyle\nabla\tilde{u}_{\ell}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j:\ \hat{Q}_{j}\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\neq\emptyset}\varphi_{j}(x)A(z_{j})+\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)[u_{\ell}(z_{j})+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\varphi_{j}(x)A(z_{j})+\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)[u_{m}(z_{j})+c+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j:\ Q_{j}\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{m})\neq\emptyset}\varphi_{j}(x)A(z_{j})+\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)[u_{m}(z_{j})+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})]

\displaystyle=\nabla\tilde{u}_{m}(x).

This implies that

\tilde{A}(x):=\begin{cases}\nabla\tilde{u}_{\ell}(x)&\textup{if }x\in B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\cap\tilde{\Omega},\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,N,\\
A(x)&\textup{if }x\in\tilde{\Omega}\setminus\bigcup_{\ell}B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell}),\end{cases}

is well-defined. Moreover, by construction
\mathrm{Curl}\,\tilde{A}=\mathrm{Curl}\,A=0
in
\bigcup_{\ell}B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\cap\tilde{\Omega}
, thus implying (
B.1
) and (
B.2
).
Moreover, for every
x\in B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\cap U
we have that

(B.4)

\begin{split}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\ell}(x)|\leq&\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(U)\setminus W)}+\left|\sum_{j:\ \hat{Q}_{j}\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\neq\emptyset}\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)[u_{\ell}(z_{j})+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})]\right|.\end{split}

For
i,j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}
such that
\hat{Q}_{j}\cap\hat{Q}_{i}\neq\emptyset
we have that

\displaystyle\left|u_{\ell}(z_{j})+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})-u_{\ell}(z_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|u_{\ell}(z_{j})-u_{\ell}(z_{i})\right|+\left|A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq CM|z_{j}-z_{i}|+C|A(z_{j})||x-z_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq C(M+\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(p_{\ell})\setminus W)})\,\mathrm{diam}\,(Q_{j})\qquad\forall\>x\in\hat{Q}_{j},

where we used that

\displaystyle|z_{j}-z_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq|z_{j}-x_{Q_{j}}|+|x_{Q_{j}}-x_{Q_{i}}|+|x_{Q_{i}}-z_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\mathrm{diam}\,(Q_{j})+\mathrm{diam}\,(Q_{i})\right)\leq C\mathrm{diam}\,(Q_{j}),

and

\displaystyle|x-z_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{diam}\,(\hat{Q}_{j})/2+|z_{j}-x_{Q_{j}}|\leq C\mathrm{diam}\,(Q_{j}),\qquad\forall\>x\in\hat{Q}_{j},

since any two intersecting squares have comparable diameters.
Therefore, for every
x\in W\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})
, let
i\in\mathcal{I}_{x}
be fixed and,
using that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)=0

because
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\varphi_{j}=1
in a neighborhood of
x
,
we can estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)[u_{\ell}(z_{j})+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)[u_{\ell}(z_{j})+A(z_{j})(x-z_{j})-u_{\ell}(z_{i})]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{x}}\|\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)\|_{\infty}C(M+\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(U)\setminus W)})\mathrm{diam}\,(Q_{j})\leq C(M+\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(U)\setminus W)}).

Joining the two estimate above we conclude that

\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\cap W)}\leq C(M+\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(U)\setminus W)})\leq CM.

Therefore, since
u_{\ell}|_{B_{\varepsilon/2}(p_{\ell})\setminus W}
is
CM
-Lipschitz, we conclude that the
C^{1}
function
\tilde{u}_{\ell}
is Lipschitz with constant
CM
and therefore

\|\tilde{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{6\varepsilon}(U)\cap\tilde{\Omega})}\leq CM.

∎

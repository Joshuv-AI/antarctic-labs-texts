Initial state radiation in e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} annihilation
Göran Fäldt

Abstract
In two earlier papers it was demonstrated that Lorentzian and Galilean symmetries could both be useful in the analysis of the annihilation reaction e + e − → γ Λ ( → p π − ) Λ ¯ ( → p ¯ π + ) e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\gamma\Lambda(\rightarrow p\pi^{-})\bar{\Lambda}(\rightarrow\bar{p}\pi^{+}) . It was also demonstrated that any pair of hyperon form factors would be acceptable, but that the { G E , G M } \{G_{E},G_{M}\} pair would probably be the simplest one to handle.

VII
Phase space

Since the intermediate-state hyperons represent states whose masses
may be considered fixed,
it is useful to rewrite the phase-space differential making this fact explicit, by using the
nesting formula
Pil

\displaystyle\textrm{dLips}(k_{1}+k_{2};q,l_{1},l_{2},q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\textrm{d}s_{g}\textrm{d}s_{h}\textrm{dLips}(k_{1}+k_{2};q,p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle\times\textrm{dLips}(s_{g};l_{1},q_{1})\,\textrm{dLips}(s_{h};l_{2},q_{2}),

,

(38)

where by definition

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{1}+q_{1},

(39)

\displaystyle p_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{2}+q_{2},

(40)

with
p_{1}^{2}=s_{g}
and
p_{2}^{2}=s_{h}
.

Now, we know the analytic expressions for the two- and three-body phase-space
differentials of eq.(
38
) as given in ref.
Pil
. For the two-body differential

\textrm{dLips}(s_{g};l_{1},q_{1})=\frac{p_{g}\textrm{d}\Omega_{g}}{16\pi^{2}\sqrt{s_{g}}},

(41)

where index
g
reminds us we are in the cms of the

N\pi
pair, where each particle has momentum

p_{g}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{s_{g}}}\sqrt{\lambda(s_{g},m_{N}^{2},m_{\pi}^{2})}.

(42)

A corresponding differential, but with index
h
, refers
to the contribution from the
\bar{N}\bar{\pi}
pair.

The three-body differential of eq.(
38
) is calculated after having been
reduced to a product of two-body differentials,

\displaystyle\textrm{dLips}(s;q,p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}{\textrm{d}s_{c}}\textrm{dLips}(s;p_{c},q)\textrm{dLips}(s_{c};p_{1},p_{2}),

(43)

with
p_{c}=p_{1}+p_{2}
and
s_{c}=p_{c}^{2}
. Furthermore,

s_{c}=s-2\omega\sqrt{s},

(44)

so that
\textrm{d}s_{c}=-2\sqrt{s}\,\textrm{d}\omega
.

A straightforward evaluation of the expressions for the two-particle differentials leads to

\displaystyle\textrm{dLips}(s;p_{c},q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\textrm{d}\Omega_{q}}{16\pi^{2}\sqrt{s}},

(45)

\displaystyle\textrm{dLips}(s_{c};p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p_{d}\textrm{d}\Omega_{d}}{16\pi^{2}\sqrt{s_{c}}}.

(46)

Consequently, the cms momentum in each of these two-body cases equals

\displaystyle p_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{s}}\sqrt{\lambda(s,s_{c},0)}=\left|\mathbf{q}\right|,

(47)

\displaystyle p_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{s_{c}}}\sqrt{\lambda(s_{c},s_{g},s_{h})}.

(48)

There are four two-body states to be reckoned with.
They are characterized by the following energies and momenta:
for the energies

\begin{array}[]{rlrl}\sqrt{s}&=2\epsilon&\sqrt{s_{c}}=2E_{\Lambda}&\\
\sqrt{s_{g}}&=M_{\Lambda}&\sqrt{s_{h}}=M_{\Lambda},\end{array}

and for the momenta

\begin{array}[]{rlrl}p_{g}&=p_{\lambda}&p_{q}&=\|\mathbf{q}\|\\
p_{h}&=p_{\lambda}&\qquad p_{d}&=p_{\Lambda}.\end{array}

with
p_{\lambda}
defined as the

p_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2M_{\Lambda}}\sqrt{\lambda(M_{\Lambda}^{2},m_{N}^{2},m_{\pi}^{2})}.

(49)

Collecting all the information on the phase-space differential
of eq.(
38
) gives,

\displaystyle\textrm{dLips}(k_{1}+k_{2};q,l_{1},l_{2},q_{1},q_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\textrm{d}s_{c}\textrm{d}s_{g}\textrm{d}s_{h}\,\cdot\frac{p_{q}\textrm{d}\Omega_{q}}{(4\pi)^{2}\sqrt{s}}\,\times

\displaystyle\frac{p_{g}\textrm{d}\Omega_{g}}{(4\pi)^{2}\sqrt{s_{g}}}\frac{p_{h}\textrm{d}\Omega_{h}}{(4\pi)^{2}\sqrt{s_{h}}}\frac{p_{d}\textrm{d}\Omega_{d}}{(4\pi)^{2}\sqrt{s_{c}}}.

(50)

We end this section with a remark. In the narrow
width approximation
the
\cal{K}
factor of eq.(
2
) contains
two delta-function factors,

\delta(s_{g}-M_{\Lambda}^{2})\delta(s_{h}-M_{\Lambda}^{2}),

(51)

which cancel against the
\textrm{d}s_{g}

and
\textrm{d}s_{h}
integrations of eq.(
50
).
The delta functions arise from the identity

\frac{1}{(s-M^{2})^{2}+M^{2}\Gamma^{2}(\sqrt{s})}=\frac{\pi}{M\Gamma(M)}\delta(s-M^{2}).

VIII
Discussion

In this paper we describe and analyze the structure of the differential-cross-section
distribution in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation for a
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}

final state, which is accompanied by an initial-state-radiation gamma.
To be specific,
the final state is fixed by the directional angles in the
decays

\Lambda\rightarrow N\pi
and
\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow\bar{N}\pi
,
denoted
\Omega_{g}
and
\Omega_{h}
, and the directional angle
\Omega_{d}

in the
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}

final state. Finally, there is the
gamma three-momentum-directional angle
\Omega_{\omega}
, and the radiated gamma energy
\omega
.

We start by rewriting
the cross-section-distribution
function
\textrm{d}\sigma

of eq.(
1
) as a product of three factors.
The first factor is

{\cal{K}}=\frac{1}{2s}\frac{(4\pi\alpha)^{3}}{(P^{2})^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\pi}{M_{\Lambda}\Gamma_{\Lambda}(M_{\Lambda})}\Bigg]^{2}.

(52)

This is what remains when the delta functions

\delta(s_{g}-M_{\Lambda}^{2})\delta(s_{h}-M_{\Lambda}^{2})
hidden
in the
{\cal{K}}
factor of eq.(
2
) are removed.

The second factor is the phase-space-density-distribution
function. After having absorbed the abovementioned delta functions
this factor reads

dLips

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ 4\epsilon\textrm{d}\omega\frac{\omega\textrm{d}\Omega_{\omega}}{(4\pi)^{2}2\epsilon}\frac{p_{\Lambda}\textrm{d}\Omega_{d}}{(4\pi)^{2}2E_{\Lambda}}

(53)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\lambda}\textrm{d}\Omega_{g}}{(4\pi)^{2}2M_{\Lambda}}\frac{p_{\lambda}\textrm{d}\Omega_{h}}{(4\pi)^{2}2M_{\Lambda}},

where
p_{\lambda}=\sqrt{\lambda(M_{\Lambda}^{2},m_{N}^{2},m_{\pi}^{2})/4M_{\Lambda}^{2}}
.

The third factor in the cross-section-distribution function is the squared matrix element

\overline{|{\cal{M}}_{red}|^{2}}
of eq.(
5
),
to which we now turn our attention.

We expand

\overline{|{\cal{M}}_{red}|^{2}}
on a set of functions,

A^{XY}
and
B^{XY}
, which are bilinear forms
in the form factors
G_{M}
and
G_{E}
,

\displaystyle A^{XY}(G_{M},G_{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|G_{M}|^{2}{\cal{L}}^{AXY}_{1}+|G_{E}|^{2}{\cal{L}}^{AXY}_{2}

(54)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\Re(G_{M}G_{E}^{\star}){\cal{L}}^{AXY}_{3}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\Im(G_{M}G_{E}^{\star}){\cal{L}}^{AXY}_{4},

and similarly for
B^{XY}
. We refer to the set of functions
{
{\cal{L}}
} as co-factors. Each of the suffixes
X
and
Y

stand for
R
or
S
, where suffix
R
represents parity-conserving
hyperon decay, and
S
parity-violating hyperon decay.
Several co-factors vanish;
{\cal{L}}^{ARR}_{3}={\cal{L}}^{BRR}_{3}={\cal{L}}^{BSS}_{3}=0
.

Co-factors suffixed

\Omega_{g}
and
\Omega_{h}

and

\Omega_{g}
or
\Omega_{h}

\Omega_{g}

{\cal L}^{ARS}_{4}.

1.
Introduction

The following open problem was raised by Alon in 2006 in the article
[
1
]
on digraph splitting.

Problem 1

(cf.
[
1
]
, Problem 4.1)
.

Does there exist an absolute constant
c>0
such that the following holds for all pairs of natural numbers
(n,s)
?

Every
2n
-vertex digraph
D
of minimum out-degree at least
s
contains an
n
-vertex subdigraph of minimum out-degree at least
\frac{s}{2}-c
.

Using an elegant probabilistic argument, Alon proved in
[
1
]
that a weaker bound holds, as follows: Every
2n
-vertex digraph of minimum out-degree at least
s
contains an
n
-vertex subdigraph of minimum out-degree at least
\frac{s}{2}-O(\sqrt{s}\sqrt{\log s})
.

In this short note, we resolve Problem 1 in the negative, by proving the following result via an explicit construction.

Theorem 1
.

For an infinite sequence of numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a
2n
-vertex tournament whose minimum out-degree is
s=n-1
, while the minimum out-degree of every
n
-vertex subdigraph is at most
\frac{s}{2}-(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))\log_{3}(s)
.

Following the notation from
[
1
]
, for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
denote by
d(s)
the largest integer such that every
2n
-vertex digraph of minimum out-degree at least
s
contains an
n
-vertex subdigraph of minimum out-degree at least
d(s)
. Alon’s result in
[
1
]
and Theorem
1
yield

\Omega(\log s)<\frac{s}{2}-d(s)<O(\sqrt{s}\sqrt{\log s}).

It remains an interesting open problem to close the gap between the lower and upper bounds.

Terminology

All digraphs considered in this paper have no loops or parallel arcs. For a digraph
D
we denote by
V(D)
its vertex set and by
A(D)\subseteq\{(u,v)\in V(D)^{2}|u\neq v\}
its arc set. For a subset
X\subseteq V(D)
of vertices we denote by
D[X]
the subdigraph of
D
induced by
X
, i.e., consisting of the vertex set
X
and all arcs of
D
going between vertices of
X
. By
\delta^{+}(D)
, we denote the
minimum out-degree
of
D
, which is the smallest out-degree occurring in
D
if
D
is non-empty, and defined as
0
when
D
is the empty digraph.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

Theorem
1
is an immediate consequence of the following result.

Theorem 2
.

For every integer
k\geq 0
there exists a tournament
T_{k}
on
3^{k}
vertices in which every vertex has out-degree
\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
, and such that the following holds: For every set
X\subseteq V(T_{k})
of size
|X|\leq\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
we have
\delta^{+}(T_{k}[X])\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}
.

To deduce Theorem
2
from Theorem
1
, pick
k\geq 0
arbitrarily, let
n:=\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
, and let
D_{k}
be the
2n
-vertex tournament obtained from
T_{k}
by deleting an (arbitrarily selected) vertex. Since deleting a vertex can lower the out-degree by at most
1
, we have that
D_{k}
is of minimum out-degree
s:=n-1=\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-1
. Furthermore, for every subdigraph
D^{\prime}\subseteq D_{k}
on
n=\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
vertices, denoting by
X
the set of vertices in
D^{\prime}
, we have that
\delta^{+}(D^{\prime})\leq\delta^{+}(D_{k}[X])=\delta^{+}(T_{k}[X])\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}=\frac{s}{2}-(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))\log_{3}(s)
. Thus, the statement of Theorem
1
follows from Theorem
2
.

Let us now go about proving Theorem
2
.
The tournaments
(T_{k})_{k\geq 0}
have the following simple recursive definition:

•

T_{0}
is the one-vertex-tournament.

•

For every integer
k\geq 0
, the tournament
T_{k+1}
is obtained from the disjoint union of
3
isomorphic copies of
T_{k}
on vertex sets
A_{k},B_{k},C_{k}
by adding all possible arcs from
A_{k}
to
B_{k}
, from
B_{k}
to
C_{k}
and from
C_{k}
to
A_{k}
.

It is immediate from this definition that for every
k\geq 0
the digraph
T_{k}
is a tournament on
3^{k}
vertices and that every vertex has the same in- and out-degree, hence, the out-degree of each vertex equals
\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
. We now prove the statement of Theorem
2
by induction on
k
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

For
k=0
, every induced subdigraph of
T_{1}
has minimum out-degree
0
by definition, which is equal to
\frac{\frac{3^{0}-1}{2}-0}{2}
, and thus the induction basis holds. For the induction step, assume that for some integer
k\geq 0
we have established that
\delta^{+}(T_{k}[X])\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}
for every
X\subseteq V(T_{k})
of size at most
\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
, and let us show the corresponding statement for
T_{k+1}
.

So let
X\subseteq V(T_{k+1})=A_{k}\cup B_{k}\cup C_{k}
be given arbitrarily such that
|X|\leq\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}
. W.l.o.g assume
X\neq\emptyset
. Denote
X_{A}:=X\cap A_{k},X_{B}:=X\cap B_{k},X_{C}:=X\cap C_{k}
, and let
x_{A}:=|X_{A}|,x_{B}:=|X_{B}|,x_{C}:=|X_{C}|
, so that
x_{A}+x_{B}+x_{C}=|X|\leq\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}
.

Consider first the case that
\min\{x_{A},x_{B},x_{C}\}=0
. Then possibly after relabeling, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
X_{B}=\emptyset
and
X_{A}\neq\emptyset
. Pick a vertex
x\in X_{A}
. Since all its out-neighbors within
X
are also contained in
A_{k}
, and since the copy of
T_{k}
induced on
A_{k}
is
\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
-out-regular, this certifies that
\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X])\leq\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}-(k+1)}{2}
, as desired.

Next, suppose that
x_{A},x_{B},x_{C}>0
. We then have, by construction of
T_{k+1}
, that

\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X])=\min\{\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X_{A}])+x_{B},\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X_{B}])+x_{C},\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X_{C}])+x_{A}\}.

Among the three numbers
x_{A},x_{B}
and
x_{C}
, at least two are of size at least
\frac{3^{k}+1}{2}
, or at least two are of size at most
\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
. Without loss of generality (possibly after relabeling), we may assume that
x_{A}
and
x_{B}
have this property. We now proceed according to the two possible cases.

Case 1.

x_{A},x_{B}\geq\frac{3^{k}+1}{2}
.

We want to estimate as follows:

\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X])\leq\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X_{B}])+x_{C}.

In order to estimate the minimum out-degree of
T_{k+1}[X_{B}]
, pick a proper subset
S\subseteq X_{B}
of size exactly
\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
. By inductive assumption (applicable since
T_{k+1}[B_{k}]
is isomorphic to
T_{k}
), we have
\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[S])\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}
. Since adding a single vertex can increase the minimum out-degree by at most
1
, we conclude that
\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X_{B}])\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}+\left(x_{B}-\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}\right)
. Putting things together, we obtain:

\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X])\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}+\left(x_{B}-\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}\right)+x_{C}

=\underbrace{(x_{A}+x_{B}+x_{C})}_{\leq\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}}-\underbrace{x_{A}}_{\geq\frac{3^{k}+1}{2}}-\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}+k}{2}

\leq\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}-\frac{3^{k}+1}{2}-\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}+k}{2}

=\frac{\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}-(k+1)}{2},

as desired, and this concludes the proof in the first case.

Case 2.

x_{A},x_{B}\leq\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
.

In particular,
|X_{A}|\leq\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
, so that we can apply the inductive assumption to the set
X_{A}
in the tournament
T_{k+1}[A_{k}]
, which is isomorphic to
T_{k}
. We conclude that
\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X_{A}])\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}
. We can now estimate the minimum out-degree in
X
via

\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X])\leq\delta^{+}(T_{k+1}[X_{A}])+x_{B}

\leq\frac{\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}-k}{2}+\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}

=\frac{\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}-(k+1)}{2},

thus proving the desired bound on the minimum degree also in the second case. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

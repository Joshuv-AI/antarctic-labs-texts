Stability and interpolation properties for Stokes-like virtual element spaces
J. Meng, L. Beirão da Veiga, L. Mascotto

Abstract
We prove stability bounds for Stokes-like virtual element spaces in two and three dimensions. Such bounds are also instrumental in deriving optimal interpolation estimates. Furthermore, we develop some numerical tests in order to investigate the behaviour of the stability constants also from the practical side. AMS subject classification : 65N12; 65N30; 65N50. Keywords : virtual element method; optimal convergence; stability; Stokes problem.

The analysis of the VEM is based on showing optimal a priori error estimates,
which are proved by means of certain

stability

Optimal convergence is then derived based on using best polynomial and interpolation estimates.

All in all, the main difference with respect to the finite element setting resides in further employing

•

interpolation estimates in virtual element spaces;

•

stability properties on a discrete bilinear form.

Several interpolation estimates for Stokes-type virtual element spaces are available in the literature;
see, e.g.,
[

4.2

Such interpolation estimates are rather technical to prove,
strongly hinge upon the definition of the local virtual element spaces,
and have been proved in two dimensions only.

On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, stability properties for Stokes-like virtual element spaces have never been explicitly proved.
We point out that several works coping with explicit stability estimates for standard Poisson-like virtual element spaces are available;
see, e.g.,
[
17

This paper aims at closing this theoretical gap.
Notably, we contribute to the current state of the art along the three following avenues:

1.

We prove explicit stability properties for Stokes-like virtual element spaces in two and three dimensions.
To this aim, we employ two different stabilizations, namely
one given by the inner product of the degrees of freedom
and one in integral form,
which is independent of the chosen degrees of freedom;

2.

Based on such stability estimates, we prove novel interpolation estimates, which deliver
the same convergence as those already available in the literature but are much easier to prove.

3.

The above achievements are proven for regular polytopal meshes.
Thus, we also exhibit numerical results investigating the stability constants for sequences of elements with degenerating geometry
as well as with respect to the degree of accuracy of the method.

The model problem.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, be an open domain
and
\mathbf{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
.

As a model problem, we consider the Stokes problem

\begin{cases}\text{find }(\mathbf{u},s)\text{ such that}\\
-\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{u}-\nabla s=\mathbf{f}&\text{in }\Omega\\
-\div\mathbf{u}=0&\text{in }\Omega\\
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

which in weak formulation reads as follows:

\begin{cases}\text{find }(\mathbf{u},s)\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{d}\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega):=\mathbf{V}\times Q\text{ such that}\\
(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v})_{0,\Omega}+(\div\mathbf{v},s)_{0,\Omega}=(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v})_{0,\Omega}&\forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}\\
(\div\mathbf{u},t)_{0,\Omega}=0&\forall t\in Q.\end{cases}

The well posedness of this problem is standard
[
15
]
.

2.1
Virtual element spaces in two dimensions

Given a polygon
K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
,
we introduce the space

\mathbf{V}_{n}(K):=\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\in[H^{1}(K)]^{2}\mid\mathbf{v}_{n}\text{ satisfies }\eqref{local-auxiliary-problem}\},

where, for some

\begin{cases}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n}-\nabla s=\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}&q_{p-3}\in\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K)\\[4.0pt]
\div\mathbf{v}_{n}=q_{p-1}&q_{p-1}\in\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(K)\\[4.0pt]
\mathbf{v}_{n}{}_{|\partial K}\in[\mathcal{C}^{0}(\partial K)]^{2},\quad\mathbf{v}_{n}{}_{|e}\in[\mathbb{P}_{p}(e)]^{2}&\forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{K},\end{cases}

(1)

with all equations to be intended in a weak sense.

We endow the space
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
with the following set of unisolvent degrees of freedom (DoFs)
[
11
]
:
given

•

the vector values
Dv
1
(
\mathbf{v}_{n}
) at the vertices of
K
;

•

the vector values
Dv
2
(
\mathbf{v}_{n}
) at the
p-1

\mathcal{E}^{K}
;

•

for

, given
\{\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}
a basis of
\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K)
, the “orthogonal” moments

\textbf{Dv}_{3}(\mathbf{v}_{n}):=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}\mathbf{v}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};

(2)

•

given
\{m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}
a basis of
\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(K)\setminus\mathbb{R}
, the “divergence” moments

\textbf{Dv}_{4}(\mathbf{v}_{n}):=\frac{h_{K}}{|K|}\int_{K}\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\ m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.

(3)

As usual, we require that the bases

and

are invariant with respect to translations and dilations;
see
[
3
,

]
.
More precisely, using the standard multi-index notation,
for given real coefficients

such polynomials have the form

m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{K}}{h_{K}}\right)^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\qquad\qquad\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:=\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.

It is known
[
11
]
that, given
\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
with known DoFs,

\div\ \mathbf{v}_{n}
is explicitly computable.
Further, we can compute the two orthogonal projectors
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{p-2}:[L^{2}(K)]^{2}\to[\mathbb{P}_{p-2}(K)]^{2}

and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}:[L^{2}(K)]^{2}\to\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K)
defined as follows:
for all
\mathbf{v}_{n}
in
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
,

\begin{split}&(\mathbf{q}_{p-2},\mathbf{v}_{n}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{p-2}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}=0\qquad\quad\;\;\;\forall\mathbf{q}_{p-2}\in[\mathbb{P}_{p-2}(K)]^{2},\\[4.0pt]
&(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3},\mathbf{v}_{n}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}=0\qquad\forall q_{p-3}\in\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K).\end{split}

(4)

We can also compute the
H^{1}
projector
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}:[H^{1}(K)]^{2}\to[\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)]^{2}
defined as

(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{q}_{p},\boldsymbol{\nabla}(\mathbf{v}_{n}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}\mathbf{v}_{n}))_{0,K}=0,\qquad\int_{\partial K}(\mathbf{v}_{n}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}\mathbf{v}_{n})=\mathbf{0}\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K),\;\forall\mathbf{q}_{p}\in[\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)]^{2}.

(5)

Functions in the virtual element space
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
, as well as their gradients,
are not available in closed form.
For this reason, following the virtual element gospel
[
5
]
,
we discretize the bilinear form

as follows:
for all

,

\begin{split}a_{h}^{K}(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})&:=a^{K}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}\mathbf{u}_{n},\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}\mathbf{v}_{n})+S^{K}((\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p})\mathbf{u}_{n},(\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p})\mathbf{v}_{n})\\
&:=(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}\mathbf{u}_{n},\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}+S^{K}((\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p})\mathbf{u}_{n},(\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p})\mathbf{v}_{n}).\end{split}

(6)

The bilinear form

is required to be coercive and continuous on

uniformly in the mesh elements.
More precisely, on this space,
we require

.
Proving such an equivalence is our goal in the forthcoming sections.

The global counterpart of the space

is constructed by a standard

-conforming DoFs-coupling.

Remark 1
.

Due to the mesh regularity assumptions,
each

can be split into the union of shape regular simplices

This applies both in the two and the three dimensional cases.

2.2
Stability estimates for a projection based stabilization

Given
K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, we consider the local stabilization

S^{K}(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}):=h_{K}^{-2}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{u}_{n},\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}+(\div\mathbf{u}_{n},\div\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}+h_{K}^{-1}(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,\partial K}.

(7)

We show stability estimates for the bilinear form
S^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The following stability bounds are valid:
there exist
0<\alpha_{*}<\alpha^{*}
independent of
h_{K}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{*}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\leq S^{K}(\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K),

(8)

\displaystyle S^{K}(\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})\leq\alpha^{*}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in[H^{1}(K)]^{2}\text{ such that }\int_{\partial K}\mathbf{v}_{n}=\mathbf{0}.

(9)

Bounds (
8
) and (
9
) are valid for functions in
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)\cap\ker(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p})
.

Proof.

We begin by proving (
8
)
splitting its proof into four steps.
Let
\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
solve (
1
) and

be the associated auxiliary pressure.

Preliminary fact
1
.

We first observe that an integration by parts yields

\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{-1,K}:=\sup_{\mathbf{0}\neq\boldsymbol{\Phi}\in H^{1}_{0}(K)}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n},\boldsymbol{\Phi})_{0,K}}{\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}\|_{1,K}}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}.

(10)

Preliminary fact
2
.

If
\rot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3})=0
, then
\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}=\mathbf{0}
.
To see this, we first recall the Helmholtz-type decomposition for polynomials in two dimensions

\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(K)=\nabla\mathbb{P}_{\ell+1}(K)\oplus\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}\mathbb{P}_{\ell-1}(K)\qquad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Using next
[
BeiraoDaVeiga-Brezzi-Marini-Russo:2016

, we have the property

\left\{\mathbf{v}\in[\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(K)]^{2}\right\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\{\rot\mathbf{v}=0\Longleftrightarrow\mathbf{v}=\nabla q_{\ell+1}\text{ for some }q_{\ell+1}\in\mathbb{P}_{\ell+1}(K)\right\}.

This proves that
\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}=0
.

From the regularity assumptions on the mesh,
we know that there exists a ball
B_{K}
inside
K

with diameter comparable to
h_{K}
.
Thus, using
[
10

6.1
]

and equivalence of Sobolev norms for spaces of polynomials with finite maximum degree on a ball,
we can write

\|\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\|_{0,K}\lesssim\|\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\|_{0,B_{K}}\lesssim h_{K}\|\rot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3})\|_{0,B_{K}}\leq h_{K}\|\rot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3})\|_{0,K}.

(11)

In light of this, we show an auxiliary bound on the

norm of the right-hand side in (
1
).
Using (
11
) and the first line in (
1
), we write

\|\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}\|\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n})\|_{0,K}.

Next, let
b^{K}
denote the piecewise cubic bubble function over the sub tessellation
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n}^{K}
of

introduced in Remark
1

\|b^{K}\|_{\infty,\widetilde{T}}=1
for all

.
Since

is a polynomial,
the following polynomial inverse estimate involving bubbles holds true:

\|\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n})\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim\|b^{K}\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n})\|_{0,K}^{2}.

Integrating by parts twice,
observing that

and its normal derivative are zero over the boundary of each

and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a polynomial inverse inequality twice,

\begin{split}\|\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n})\|_{0,K}^{2}&\lesssim\left(\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n}),(b^{K})^{2}\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n})\right)_{0,K}=\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n},\boldsymbol{\nabla}\curlbold((b^{K})^{2}\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n}))\right)_{0,K}\\[4.0pt]
&\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}\;h_{K}^{-2}\|(b^{K})^{2}\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n})\|_{0,K}\leq|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}\;h_{K}^{-2}\|\rot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n})\|_{0,K}.\end{split}

Combining the two above bounds yields

\|\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{-1}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}.

(12)

(14)

Proving the lower bound (

\begin{split}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}&=(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n},\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}=(\mathbf{v}_{n},(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n})\mathbf{n}_{K})_{0,\partial K}+(\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}+\nabla s)_{0,K}\\[4.0pt]
&=(\mathbf{v}_{n},(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n}+\mathbf{I}s)\mathbf{n}_{K})_{0,\partial K}+(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3})_{0,K}-(\div\mathbf{v}_{n},s)_{0,K}.\end{split}

(15)

Introduce
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n}+\mathbf{I}s
.
Due to the first equation in (
1
),
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}=-\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}
.

Using (
12
) and (
14
) in (
15
) yields

|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\lesssim\left(h_{K}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}+\|\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}\right)|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}+\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{\frac{1}{2},\partial K}\|(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\mathbf{n}_{K}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\partial K}.

Applying the divergence trace inequality
[
36
, Section
3.5.2
]

and a polynomial inverse inequality on
\partial K

(recall that
\mathbf{v}_{n}
is a piecewise polynomial over
\partial K
)
gives

\begin{split}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}&\lesssim\left(h_{K}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}+\|\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}\right)|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}\!+\!h_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial K}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}\|\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{0,K}\right)\\[4.0pt]
&\lesssim\left(h_{K}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}\!+\!\|\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}\right)|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}\!+\!h_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial K}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{0,K}\!+\!h_{K}\|\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\|_{0,K}\right)\!.\end{split}

On the other hand, the triangle inequality and (
14
) entail

\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{0,K}\leq|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}+\|s\|_{0,K}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}.

Combining the two above estimates and recalling (
12
) leads to (
8
).

Next, we prove the upper bound (
9
).
Let
\mathbf{v}_{n}\in[H^{1}(K)]^{2}
with zero average over
\partial K
.
We have to show an upper bound of the three terms on the right-hand side of (
7
) in terms of
|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}
.

As for
h_{K}^{-2}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}^{2}
,
it suffices to use the stability of the
L^{2}
projector and the scaled Poincaré inequality;
we can estimate
\|\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}^{2}
by
|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}
directly;
we control
h_{K}^{-1}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}
by using the trace and the Poincaré inequalities.
∎

Remark 2
.

Let
\{\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}\}
denote the set of boundary DoFs in
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
,
i.e., the DoFs of type
Dv
1
and
Dv
2
.
Following, e.g.,
[

, eq.
(2.14)
with

2.3
Stability estimates for the “dofi-dofi” stabilization

In this section, we prove stability estimates for the classical “dofi-dofi” stabilization

S^{K}_{D}(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim(\mathbf{V}_{n}(K))}\text{dof}_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{n})\text{dof}_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{n}),

(16)

where the set
\{\text{dof}_{j}\}
collects
the sets
\{\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}\}
,
\{\text{dof}^{\perp}_{j}\}
, and
\{\text{dof}^{\text{div}}_{j}\}

of boundary (
\textbf{Dv}_{1}
and
\textbf{Dv}_{2}
),
“orthogonal” (
\textbf{Dv}_{3}
),
and divergence (
\textbf{Dv}_{4}
) DoFs, respectively.

We recall the following technical result; see, e.g.,
[
24
, Lemma
4.1
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{\mathbf{m}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}
be a set of linearly independent polynomials of maximum degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
over a polygon
K
, that are invariant with respect to translation and dilation.
For every polynomial
\mathbf{q}_{p}\in[\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)]^{d}
,
d=2,3
,
consider the decomposition
\mathbf{q}_{p}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{q}_{p}})_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\mathbf{m}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
,
where
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{q}_{p}}
denotes the coefficient vector of
\mathbf{q}_{p}
with respect to the basis
\{\mathbf{m}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}
.
Then, the following equivalence of norms is valid:

h_{K}\|\overrightarrow{\mathbf{q}_{p}}\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{q}_{p}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}\|\overrightarrow{\mathbf{q}_{p}}\|_{\ell^{2}}.

(17)

Under the mesh regularity assumption in Section
1
,
the hidden constant in (
17
)
are uniform with respect to the element
K
.

Next, we prove the following stability result, based on the techniques developed in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The following stability bounds are valid:
there exist
0<\alpha_{*}<\alpha^{*}
independent of
h_{K}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{*}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\leq S^{K}_{D}(\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K),

(18)

\displaystyle S^{K}_{D}(\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})\leq\alpha^{*}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)\text{ such that }\int_{\partial K}\mathbf{v}_{n}=\mathbf{0}.

(19)

Bounds (
18
) and (
19
) are valid for functions in
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)\cap\ker(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p})
.

Proof.

We begin by proving the lower bound (
18
).
Throughout, we use the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We have

\begin{split}&|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\\
&=-\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}+\int_{\partial K}(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n})\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}=\int_{K}(\nabla s+\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}+\int_{\partial K}(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n})\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}\\
&=\int_{K}\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}-\int_{K}s\ \div\mathbf{v}_{n}+\int_{\partial K}(\mathbf{I}s+\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{n})\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}=A+B+C.\end{split}

(20)

We estimate the three terms on the right-hand side separately.

Denote
\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}
by
\mathbf{g}
and consider the expansion

\mathbf{g}:=\sum_{\!\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{g}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},

where
\{\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}
is any basis of
\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K)
as in Lemma
2.2
,
and
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{g}}
is the vector of the coefficients of
\mathbf{g}
with respect to the basis
\{\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}
.

We obtain

\begin{split}A&=\int_{K}\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{g}}_{\!\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{g}}_{\!\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|K|\text{dof}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\\
&\lesssim h_{K}^{2}\|\overrightarrow{\mathbf{g}}\|_{\ell^{2}}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\text{dof}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\overset{\eqref{equivalence:polynomials:l2}}{\lesssim}h_{K}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,K}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\text{dof}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Using the definition of
\mathbf{g}
,
bound (
12
) gives

h_{K}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,K}=h_{K}\|\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}q_{p-3}\|_{0,K}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}.

Combining the two above bounds yields

A\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\text{dof}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(21)

Next, we focus on the term
B
.
Recall that
\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(K)\setminus\mathbb{R}
and set

\Pi^{0}_{p-1}s=:g=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\overrightarrow{g}_{\!\boldsymbol{\beta}}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}},

where
\{m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\}
is any basis of
\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(K)\setminus\mathbb{R}
as in (the scalar version of) Lemma
2.2
.

Using (
17
) and (
14
),
we deduce

\begin{split}B&=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\overrightarrow{g}_{\!\boldsymbol{\beta}}\int_{K}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\approx h_{K}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\overrightarrow{g}_{\!\boldsymbol{\beta}}\text{dof}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\leq h_{K}\|\overrightarrow{g}\|_{\ell^{2}}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\text{dof}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\Pi^{0}_{p-1}s\|_{0,K}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\text{dof}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\text{dof}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(22)

The term
C
can be estimated as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
:

C\lesssim h_{K}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial K}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}.

(23)

Inserting (
21
), (
22
), and (
23
) in (
20
), we deduce

|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\text{dof}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\text{dof}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\mathbf{v}_{n})^{2}+h_{K}^{-1}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}.

Finally, the boundary contribution is spectrally equivalent to the sum of the boundary degrees of freedom squared;
see Remark
2
.

Next, we prove the upper bound (
19
).
Notably, we need to estimate three types of degrees of freedom.
Lemma
2.2
easily implies
\|\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}

and
\|m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}
.
Since
\mathbf{v}_{n}
has zero average over
\partial K

and we consider scaled polynomial functions in the definition of the DoFs (
2
) and (
3
),
a scaled Poincaré inequality entails
a bound on the DoFs of type
\textbf{Dv}_{3}
and
\textbf{Dv}_{4}
:

\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}\mathbf{v}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\lesssim h_{K}^{-2}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}\|\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\|_{0,K}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}

and

\frac{h_{K}}{|K|}\int_{K}\div\mathbf{v}_{n}m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\lesssim h_{K}^{-1}\|\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}\|m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\|_{0,K}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}.

As for the boundary DoFs contribution
\textbf{Dv}_{1}
and
\textbf{Dv}_{2}
,
we resort to Remark
2
,
write the sum of the boundary DoFs equivalently as
\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial K}
,
and use a trace inequality and a Poincaré inequality.
∎

2.4
Interpolation estimates

Interpolation estimates for Stokes-type virtual element functions are well-known;
see
[
11
, Proposition
4.2
]
for the standard 2D case
and
[
12
, Theorem
4.1
]
for the enhanced 2D case.
To the best of our knowledge, no explicit interpolation estimates are available for 3D Stokes-type virtual element functions.

In this section, we prove interpolation estimates undertaking a different avenue,
notably using the stability estimates in Theorem
2.1
.
This novel approach is interesting
per se
.
In fact, it can be easily generalized to derive interpolation properties for other virtual element spaces,
once stability estimates are available.

For all
\mathbf{u}\in H^{1+\varepsilon}(K)
,

\varepsilon>0
,
we define
\mathbf{u}_{I}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
as the only function satisfying

\text{dof}_{j}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I})=0\qquad\qquad\forall j=1,\dots,\dim(\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)).

(24)

We have the following interpolation estimates.

Theorem 2.4
.

Given
\mathbf{u}\in[H^{s+1}(K)]^{2}
,
0<s\leq p
, and
\mathbf{u}_{I}
its DoFs interpolant as in (
24
),
the following bound is valid:

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I}|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{s+1}|\mathbf{u}|_{s+1,K}.

The hidden constant depends on the shape-regularity of the mesh and the degree of accuracy
p
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{\pi}
be
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}\mathbf{u}
, being
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}_{p}
defined in (
5
).
The triangle inequality gives

|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I}|_{1,K}\leq|\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I}|_{1,K}+|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\pi}|_{1,K}.

Thanks to standard polynomial approximation results, we only need to bound the first term on the right-hand side.
Observe that
\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I}
belongs to
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
.
Let
S^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
be defined in (
7
).
Then, we use bound (
8
)
and Remark
2
,
and write

\begin{split}|\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I}|_{1,K}^{2}&\lesssim S^{K}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I},\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})\\[4.0pt]
&\lesssim h_{K}^{-2}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})\|_{0,K}^{2}+\|\div(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{j}\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})^{2}.\end{split}

We prove that each of the three terms above can be bounded by an error term involving the difference
\mathbf{u}\!-\!\mathbf{u}_{\pi}
.
To this aim, we preliminary observe that

\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\perp,p-3}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I})=0,\qquad\div\mathbf{u}_{I}=\Pi^{0}_{p-1}(\div\mathbf{u}),\qquad\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I})=0,

(25)

where
\Pi^{0}_{p-1}:L^{2}(K)\to\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(K)
is the
L^{2}
scalar orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(K)
.

Thus, as for the bulk
L^{2}
term, we use (
25
), the stability of orthogonal projections,
the fact that
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\pi}
has zero average over
K
, and the Poincaré inequality.
Next, we estimate the divergence contribution by using (
25
) and bounding the
L^{2}
norm of the divergence by the
H^{1}
seminorm.
Eventually, the boundary contribution is bounded using (
25
) again
and the Sobolev embedding
[H^{1+\varepsilon}(K)]^{2}\hookrightarrow[L^{\infty}(K)]^{2}
,

\varepsilon>0
, as follows: for each boundary degree of freedom
\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}
,

\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})^{2}=\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\pi})^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\pi}\|_{\infty,K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{-2}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\pi}\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}^{2\varepsilon}|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\pi}|_{1+\varepsilon,K}^{2}.

Estimates in the
H^{1}
norm follow using standard polynomial approximation properties as in
[
41
]
.
Instead, estimates in the
L^{2}
norm are a simple consequence of Poincaré-type arguments
and the estimates in the
H^{1}
seminorm.
∎

Remark 4
.

The present analysis assumes that the length of each edge is comparable to the diameter of the parent element;
see the second mesh condition (
d=2
) at the end of Section
1
.
Nevertheless, our results could be generalized to the “small edges” case
by combining the present analysis with the ideas in
[
10
,
17
]
.

defined as

\begin{split}(\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\mathbf{v}_{n}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\wedge,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}=0\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K),\quad\forall\mathbf{q}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbf{x}\wedge[\mathbb{P}_{p-3}(K)]^{3}.\end{split}

We consider the local stabilization

\begin{split}S^{K}(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})&:=h_{K}^{-2}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\wedge,p-3}\mathbf{u}_{n},\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\wedge,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}+(\div\mathbf{u}_{n},\div\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,K}\\[4.0pt]
&\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{K}}\left[h_{F}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,F}_{p}\mathbf{u}_{n},\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,F}_{p}\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,F}+(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})_{0,\partial F}\right].\end{split}

(33)

We prove the following stability estimates.

Theorem 3.1
.

The following stability bounds are valid:
there exists
0<\alpha_{*}<\alpha^{*}
independent of
h_{K}
such that, for all
\mathbf{v}_{n}
in
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)

such that
\int_{\partial K}\mathbf{v}_{n}=\mathbf{0}
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{*}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}\leq S^{K}(\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}),

(34)

\displaystyle S^{K}(\mathbf{v}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})\leq\alpha^{*}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}.

(35)

Proof.

We begin by proving the lower bound (
34
).
As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we can prove the following bounds:
for all
\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
, with “auxiliary pressure”
s
and right-hand side
\mathbf{x}\wedge\mathbf{q}_{p-3}
in (
29
),

|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{-1,K}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K},\qquad\|\mathbf{x}\wedge\mathbf{q}_{p-3}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{-1}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K},\qquad\|s\|_{0,K}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}.

With this at hand, as in the 2D case, we deduce

|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\wedge,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}+\|\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}+\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{\frac{1}{2},\partial K}.

Thus, we only have to estimate the last term on the right-hand side.
After recalling that
\mathbf{v}_{n}
has zero vector average over
\partial K
,
we observe the bound

[
17
, eq. (2.16)]

\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{\frac{1}{2},\partial K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{K}}\|\nabla_{F}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,F}^{2}.

We can estimate each face contribution by means of standard nodal virtual element inverse estimates;
see, e.g.,
[
7
, Theorem
2
]
and
[
24
, Section
3
]
:

\|\nabla_{F}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,F}^{2}\lesssim h_{F}^{-2}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,F}_{p}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,F}^{2}+h_{F}^{-1}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial F}^{2}.

The assertion follows summing over all the faces and collecting the above estimates.

Next, we focus on the upper bound (
35
).
Notably, we estimate from above the four terms on the right-hand side of (
33
).
Since
\mathbf{v}_{n}
has zero average over
\partial K
, we readily have

h_{K}^{-2}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\wedge,p-3}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\|\div\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}.

The trace and the Poincaré inequalities also yield

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{K}}h_{F}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,F}_{p}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,F}^{2}\lesssim|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,K}^{2}.

We are left with estimating the fourth term on the right-hand side of (
33
).
Using a trace inequality on each face
F
yields

\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial F}\lesssim h_{F}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,F}+h_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\mathbf{v}_{n}|_{1,F}.

Inverse estimates for nodal virtual element functions,
see, e.g.,
[
24
, Theorem
3.6
]
, entail

\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,\partial F}\lesssim h_{F}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\|_{0,F}.

We can apply this inverse estimate
as the restriction of
\mathbf{v}_{n}
on each face
F

belongs to a two dimensional nodal virtual element space.

Taking the square on both sides and summing over the faces,
then using another trace inequality,
and eventually a Poincaré inequality on
K

give the assertion.
∎

S^{K}_{D}(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim(\mathbf{V}_{n}(K))}\text{dof}_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{n})\text{dof}_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{n})

3.3
Interpolation estimates

In this section, we prove interpolation estimates for 3D Stokes-like virtual element spaces.

For all
\mathbf{u}\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(K)
,

\varepsilon>0
,
we define
\mathbf{u}_{I}
as the only function in
\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)
satisfying

\text{dof}_{j}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I})=0\qquad\qquad\forall j=1,\dots,\dim(\mathbf{V}_{n}(K)).

(36)

We have the following interpolation estimates.

Theorem 3.2
.

Given
\mathbf{u}\in[H^{s+1}(K)]^{3}
,
1/2<s\leq p
,
and
\mathbf{u}_{I}
its DoFs interpolant as in (
36
),
the following bound is valid:

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I}|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{s+1}|\mathbf{u}|_{s+1,K}.

The hidden constant depends on the shape-regularity of the mesh and the degree of accuracy
p
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{\pi}
the best vector polynomial approximation of
\mathbf{u}
in
[H^{1}(K)]^{3}
; see, e.g.,
[
41
]
.
As in the proof of Theorem
2.4
, we only need to estimate the energy of
\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I}
.

In addition to the three dimensional counterpart of (
25
), we also have

\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,F}_{p}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I})=0\qquad\forall F\in\mathcal{E}^{K}.

(37)

Let
S^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
be defined in (
33
).
Using (
34
), we write

\begin{split}|\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I}|_{1,K}^{2}&\lesssim S^{K}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I},\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})\\
&=h_{K}^{-2}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\wedge,p-3}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})\|_{0,K}^{2}+\|\div(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})\|_{0,K}^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{K}}\left[h_{F}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,F}_{p}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})\|_{0,F}^{2}\right]+\sum_{j}\text{dof}^{\text{B}}_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I})^{2}.\end{split}

We have to bound the four terms on the right-hand side by
|\mathbf{u}_{\pi}-\mathbf{u}_{I}|_{1,K}
.
The first, second, and fourth are dealt with by using (
25
), as in the proof of Theorem
2.4
.
As for the “new” third term, it suffices to resort to (
37
),
the continuity of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0,F}_{p}
,
the trace inequality,
and standard polynomial approximation properties.

Estimates in the
L^{2}
norm are a consequence of Poincaré type estimates on
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I}

and the energy estimates.
∎

Remark 6
.

So far, we derived stability and interpolation properties for standard Stokes-like spaces.
Following, e.g.,
[
12
]
,
we may also consider the enhanced version of such spaces,
in the same spirit as we defined the nodal virtual elements spaces in (
27
).
It is apparent that no essential modifications take place in the proof of the stability estimates.
The only difference between our setting and the enhanced one
is that in the latter we should employ polynomial inverse estimates for slightly larger polynomial degrees.

In the foregoing sections, we proved stability bounds for fixed degree of accuracy and regular polytopal meshes.

The elements of this preliminary basis are constructed so that they solve Stokes problems with given polynomial data,

We define the three type of basis functions as follows:
given
\{m_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}-\nabla s=\mathbf{0}&\text{in }K\\

\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{B}_{i}&\text{on }\partial K\end{cases}

\displaystyle\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,2p\cdot(\#\text{edges of~$K$}),

\displaystyle\qquad\forall|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=0,\dots,p-3,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}-\nabla s=\mathbf{0}&\text{in }K\\
\div\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=m_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}&\text{in }K\\

4.1
Expanding the boundary-type functions

For all
i=1,\dots,2p\cdot(\#\text{edges of~$K$})
,
we write

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{B}_{i}=\sum_{j}A^{(i)}_{j}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{j}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}B^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}.

We have to determine the A, B, and C-type coefficients by imposing the DoFs definition.

First, we use the boundary DoFs.
For any vertex or Gauß-Lobatto node
N_{K}
on any edge
e
of
K
, we have

\mathcal{\delta}_{i,k}=\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{B}_{i}(N_{k})=\sum_{j}A^{(i)}_{j}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{j}(N_{k})=A^{(i)}_{k}\qquad\forall k=1,\dots,2p\cdot(\#\text{edges of~$K$}).

Therefore, we get the simplified expression

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{B}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}B^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}.

To find the B and C-type coefficients, we impose the divergence and “orthogonal” DoFs definition.
First, we impose the “orthogonal” moments definition and write

0=\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{B}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})=\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}B^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}),

whence we deduce the conditions, for all
|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}|=0,\dots,p-3
,

\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\left(\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})\right)B^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\left(\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})\right)C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}=-\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}).

Next, we impose the divergence moments definition,
recall that the test polynomial
m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}
has zero average over
K
, and get

\begin{split}0&=\int_{K}\div\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{B}_{i}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}=\int_{K}\div\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\int_{K}\div\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\text{div}}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}\\
&=|K|^{-1}\int_{\partial K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{K}\int_{K}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\int_{K}m_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\ m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\int_{K}m_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\ m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}},\end{split}

whence we deduce the conditions, for all
|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}|=1,\dots,p-1
,

\sum_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\left(\int_{K}m_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\ m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}\right)C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}=0.

Using the coercivity of any polynomial mass matrix,
we deduce
C^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}=0
for all
i
and
\boldsymbol{\delta}
.

Thus, the B-type coefficients are obtained by solving the linear system

\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\left(\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\perp}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}})\right)B^{(i)}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=-\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\psi}^{B}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\!\perp}m_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}).

5
Conclusions

We investigated some open issues in the analysis of Stokes-like virtual element spaces.
Notably, we proved stability properties in two and three dimensions,
and furthermore derived interpolation estimates by simplifying the current state-of-the-art proofs.
Numerical experiments seem to indicate that the stability constants
only depend moderately on
p
;
however, they can degenerate more rapidly for nonregular element geometries.
On the other hand, such a discrepancy of the discrete form
with respect to the “exact” one
may be beneficial and explain why in this degenerate mesh conditions
the VEM often performs remarkably well.

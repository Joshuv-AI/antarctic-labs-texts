A lower bound for the genus of a knot using the Links-Gould invariant
Ben-Michael Kohli, Guillaume Tahar

Abstract
. The Links-Gould invariant of links L ​ G 2 , 1 LG^{2,1} is a two-variable generalization of the Alexander-Conway polynomial. Using representation theory of U q ​ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 | 1 ) U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1) , we prove that the degree of the Links-Gould polynomial provides a lower bound on the Seifert genus of any knot, therefore improving the bound known as the Seifert inequality in the case of the Alexander polynomial. As an example, unlike some classical tools such as the Alexander polynomial and Levine-Tristram signature, this new genus bound detects the fact that the Kinoshita-Terasaka and Conway knots have genus greater or equal to 2.

Organization of the paper

Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
3
. The genus bound is seen as a consequence of a “factorization” of the diagram of a knot
K
through the Reshetikhin-Turaev functor in a way that is controlled by the genus of

. For that we use the so called bottom tangle presentation of a Seifert surface of a knot
[
17
]
. This construction follows in spirit the recent work by López Neumann and Van der Veen (see
[

]
).

One crucial step to use that factorization is a representation-theoretic lemma obtained in Section

. The Links-Gould invariant is associated to a family of highest weight supermodules over
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
. Equivalently, it can be obtained from a family of modules

over the bosonization

of the superalgebra

. In Theorem

, we prove that the tensor product representation

does not depend on the parameter
\alpha
. For that purpose we provide an explicit change of basis between

-modules. We also need to understand the dual action of

which we do at the end of Section

Some topological consequences of this new genus bound are presented in Section

Conventions

Representations of Hopf superalgebra

But let us first introduce the objects at hand. A

is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
. The parity of a homogeneous element

is denoted

is the tensor product of the underlying vector spaces with
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading given by

\displaystyle(V\otimes W)_{\bar{p}}=\bigoplus_{\bar{a}+\bar{b}=\bar{p}}V_{\bar{a}}\otimes W_{\bar{b}}.

A (left) module over a (associative) superalgebra

is a super vector space
M
with an
A
-module structure for which the action map
A\otimes M\rightarrow M
is a morphism of super vector spaces. Given homogeneous elements

[a,b]=ab-(-1)^{[a][b]}ba

2.1.
Generators and relations for Hopf superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)

2.1.1.
Generators

Superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
is a
\mathbb{C}
-superalgebra defined by the following seven elementary generators:

•

Cartan generators
q^{E_{1}^{1}}
,
q^{E_{2}^{2}}
and
q^{E_{3}^{3}}
;

•

Raising generators
E_{2}^{1}
and
E_{3}^{2}
;

•

Lowering generators
E_{1}^{2}
and
E_{2}^{3}
.

Elements

are chosen to be invertible and we refer to their inverses as
q^{-E_{i}^{i}}

some choice of a square root for

In addition to the 7 generators, we will use two other elements defined in terms of them:

•

E_{3}^{1}=E_{2}^{1}E_{3}^{2}-q^{-1}E_{3}^{2}E_{2}^{1}
;

•

E_{1}^{3}=E_{2}^{3}E_{1}^{2}-qE_{1}^{2}E_{2}^{3}
.

Remark 2.1
.

This choice of variables is coherent with
[
36
]
up to the fact that what Zhang calls

corresponds to what we denote

in the following paragraph.

2.1.2.
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading

We define a

-grading on

2.1.3.
Relations

Definition 2.2
.

Whenever it makes sense, we will use the following q-bracket notation:

[X]_{q}=\frac{q^{X}-q^{-X}}{q-q^{-1}}.

That being said, the relations defining superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
are:

•

The Cartan generators
q^{E_{1}^{1}}
,
q^{E_{2}^{2}}
,
q^{E_{3}^{3}}
all commute;

•

The raising and lowering generators commute with the Cartan genarators as follows:

q^{(-1)^{|a|}E^{a}_{a}}E^{b}_{b\pm 1}q^{-(-1)^{|a|}E^{a}_{a}}=q^{(-1)^{|a|}(\delta^{a}_{b}-\delta^{a}_{b\pm 1})}E^{b}_{b\pm 1},

\text{where }|1|=|2|=0\text{, }|3|=1\text{ and }\delta^{i}_{j}=1\text{ if and only if }i=j;

•

(E_{2}^{3})^{2}=(E_{3}^{2})^{2}=0
(the squares of odd generators are zero);

•

E^{1}_{2}
commutes with
E_{2}^{3}
while
E_{1}^{2}
commutes with
E_{3}^{2}
;

•

The non Cartan generators also satisfy the following interchange rules:

E_{2}^{1}E_{1}^{2}=[E_{1}^{1}-E_{2}^{2}]_{q}+E_{1}^{2}E_{2}^{1},

E_{3}^{2}E_{2}^{3}=[E_{2}^{2}+E_{3}^{3}]_{q}-E_{2}^{3}E_{3}^{2}.

The Serre relations can be written in terms of generators, but are more easily understood when expressed using
E^{3}_{1}
and
E^{1}_{3}
:

E_{2}^{1}E_{3}^{1}-qE_{3}^{1}E_{2}^{1}=0;

E_{1}^{3}E_{1}^{2}-q^{-1}E_{1}^{2}E_{1}^{3}=0.

Other interesting relations can be deduced from the defining ones. They are all summed up in
[
4
]
, Sections 4.2.3 and 4.2.4.

2.2.
A Hopf superalgebra structure on
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)

Superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
is equipped with a coproduct
\Delta
, a co-unit
\epsilon
and an antipode
S
, turning it into a Hopf superalgebra.

2.2.1.
Coproduct

Coproduct
\Delta:U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)\rightarrow U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)\otimes U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
is an algebra homomorphism defined by
\Delta(xy)=\Delta(x)\Delta(y)\text{ for any }x,y\in U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)\text{, by }

\Delta(1)=1\otimes 1
and by:

\text{ for any }i\text{, }\Delta(q^{E_{i}^{i}})=q^{E_{i}^{i}}\otimes q^{E_{i}^{i}};

\Delta(E_{2}^{1})=E_{2}^{1}\otimes q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}-E_{2}^{2})}+q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}-E_{2}^{2})}\otimes E_{2}^{1};

\Delta(E_{1}^{2})=E_{1}^{2}\otimes q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}-E_{2}^{2})}+q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}-E_{2}^{2})}\otimes E_{1}^{2};

\Delta(E_{3}^{2})=E_{3}^{2}\otimes q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{2}^{2}+E_{3}^{3})}+q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{2}^{2}+E_{3}^{3})}\otimes E_{3}^{2};

\Delta(E_{2}^{3})=E_{2}^{3}\otimes q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{2}^{2}+E_{3}^{3})}+q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{2}^{2}+E_{3}^{3})}\otimes E_{2}^{3}.

Remark 2.3
.

Observe that coproduct
\Delta
preserves grading: for any
x\in U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
,
[\Delta(x)]=[x]
.

It will be necessary to know the expression for coproducts
\Delta(E_{1}^{3})
and
\Delta(E_{3}^{1})
. They have been computed in
[
4
]
, Section 4.5.3:

•

\Delta(E_{1}^{3})=E_{1}^{3}\otimes q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}+E_{3}^{3})}+q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}+E_{3}^{3})}E_{1}^{3}-(q-q^{-1})q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}-E_{2}^{2})}E_{2}^{3}\otimes E_{1}^{2}q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{2}^{2}+E_{3}^{3})}
;

•

\Delta(E_{3}^{1})=E_{3}^{1}\otimes q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}+E_{3}^{3})}+q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}+E_{3}^{3})}E_{3}^{1}+(q-q^{-1})q^{\frac{1}{2}(E_{2}^{2}+E_{3}^{3})}E_{2}^{1}\otimes E_{3}^{2}q^{-\frac{1}{2}(E_{1}^{1}-E_{2}^{2})}
.

2.2.2.
Co-unit

Co-unit
\epsilon:U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an algebra homomorphism satisfying:

\epsilon(q^{E_{1}^{1}})=\epsilon(q^{E_{2}^{2}})=\epsilon(q^{E_{3}^{3}})=\epsilon(1)=1;

\epsilon(E_{2}^{1})=\epsilon(E_{1}^{2})=\epsilon(E_{3}^{2})=\epsilon(E_{2}^{3})=0.

2.2.3.
Antipode

Antipode
S:U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)\rightarrow U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
is a graded algebra antihomomorphism. It satisfies
S(1)=1
and
S(xy)=(-1)^{[x][y]}S(y)S(x)
for any
x,y\in U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
. Also:

S(q^{E_{i}^{i}})=q^{-E_{i}^{i}}\text{ for }i=1,2,3;

S(E_{2}^{1})=-q^{-1}E_{2}^{1}\text{ and }S(E_{1}^{2})=-qE_{1}^{2};

S(E_{3}^{2})=-E_{3}^{2}\text{ and }S(E_{2}^{3})=-E_{2}^{3}.

Superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
equipped with
\Delta,\epsilon,S
satisfies the standard axioms for Hopf superalgebras, see
[
35
]
.

2.4.
Bosonization of Hopf superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)

In order to make computations a bit more straightforward, we will not in practice use Hopf superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
, but its non super counterpart
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
. This transformation, known as bosonization, is due to Majid, see
[

]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Set
(H,\Delta,\epsilon,S)
a Hopf superalgebra. One can define an ordinary Hopf algebra
H^{\sigma}
as follows. As an algebra,
H^{\sigma}
is an extension of
H
by adjoining an element
{\sigma}
subject to the following commutation relations:

\forall x\in H\text{ homogeneous, }\sigma x=(-1)^{[x]}x\sigma.

The coproduct
\Delta^{\sigma}
on
H^{\sigma}
is given by
\Delta^{\sigma}(\sigma)=\sigma\otimes\sigma
and

\forall x\in H\text{, }\Delta^{\sigma}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i}x_{i}\sigma^{[y_{i}]}\otimes y_{i}\text{ where }\Delta(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}.

The counit
\epsilon^{\sigma}
satisfies
\epsilon^{\sigma}(\sigma)=1
and
\epsilon^{\sigma}(x)=\epsilon(x)
otherwise.

The antipode
S^{\sigma}
is given by
S^{\sigma}(\sigma)=\sigma
and

S^{\sigma}(x)=\sigma^{[x]}S(x)\text{ }\forall x\in H\text{ homogeneous.}

Note that
S
is a graded algebra antihomomorphism and therefore
S^{\sigma}
is an ordinary algebra antihomomorphism.

Theorem 2.10
.

For any superalgebra
H

H

H^{\sigma}
-modules.

The previous result also follows from Majid’s work, see

]

, then you get a representation of

by setting
\sigma_{V}=\text{Id}_{V_{even}}\oplus-\text{Id}_{V_{odd}}
. Conversely, since
\sigma^{2}=1
, every

-module inherits a natural

grading: any

splits into

where
V_{even}=\ker(\sigma-1)
and
V_{odd}=\ker(\sigma+1)
.

That is why in the following we will consider Hopf algebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
rather than Hopf superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
. They tell the same story representation-wise.

2.7.
Change of basis

In this section we prove that all representations

are isomorphic to the same representation

. To prove this we build a special basis for

using the action of some generators of
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
on a specific elementary tensor.

Lemma 2.12
.

Set

. We denote by

the family consisting of the following 16 vectors:

is a basis for vector space

.

We can number the 16 vectors we are considering:

,

, … ,

in the same order that was used to write them in Lemma
2.12
, and we will do so in the following.

.

For any element

belong to
\mathbb{C}(q)

2.13
proves that for any
\alpha

and

are isomorphic, and that

.

Lemma
2.12

are proved through explicit computations in Appendix

.

the highest weight vector in

with weight

. Let

denote the lowest weight vector in

. Then

cyclically generates the module

. That vector has weight

. Since the weight of this vector is independent from

, the module that it cyclically generates is independent from

as well: lowering operations move

through a set of basis vectors of

, while raising operators (acting via coproduct) move

through all dual basis vectors, so together the whole tensor space is generated. That is, there exists a basis of weight vectors with weights that do not depend on

3.
Proof of the genus bound

Here we will explain how a special type of (1-1)-tangles will allow us to compute the
LG
invariant through the Reshetikhin-Turaev functor in a way that lets us control the degree of the polynomial in terms of the genus of the knot. But first let us define the Links-Gould invariant of links
LG^{2,1}=LG
.

3.1.
Definition of the Link-Gould invariant of links

The Links-Gould invariant of links
LG
was first defined by Jon Links and Mark Gould in
[
25
]
. We will not exactly follow their approach here to define and compute the invariant. We will stick more closely to the approach taken by De Wit, Kauffman and Links in
[
7
]
where they obtain
LG
from an operator invariant built using the Reshetikhin-Turaev construction applied to the Hopf superalgebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
. In that setting,
LG
is part of a larger family of invariants
LG^{m,n}
where
LG=LG^{2,1}
, see
[
5
]
. In the De Wit-Kauffman-Links approach,
LG
is obtained from an
R
-matrix and cups, caps that define an operator invariant of oriented tangles. Indeed, Hopf algebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
can be decorated with a ribbon structure (see for example
[
21
]
). Then for
L
a link in
S^{3}
,
LG(L,q,q^{\alpha})
is obtained from a (1-1)-tangle
T
with closure
L
and the operator invariant

Q^{U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma},V_{\alpha}}(T)

derived from ribbon Hopf algebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
via the irreducible representation
V_{\alpha}
, in the following way:
Q^{U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma},V_{\alpha}}(T)
is a
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
-linear map from the irreducible
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
- module
V_{\alpha}
to
V_{\alpha}
. Using the fact that
V_{\alpha}
is irreducible, Schur’s Lemma states that such a map acts as a scalar on
V_{\alpha}
, see e.g.
[
2
]
.

Definition 3.1

(The Links-Gould invariant of links
LG
)
.

Set
L
an oriented link in
S^{3}
and
T
an oriented (1-1)-tangle such that the closure of
T
is
L
. Then the operator invariant derived from the ribbon Hopf algebra
U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma}
and irreducible representation
V_{\alpha}
is such that

Q^{U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)^{\sigma},V_{\alpha}}(T)=c.id_{V_{\alpha}}

for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, the value of
c
does not depend on the choice of
T
, but only on the isotopy class of
L
, because
V_{\alpha}
is an ambidextrous object in the sense of
[
15
]
. Constant c therefore is a link invariant and it is by definition the value of the Links-Gould polynomial
LG(L,q,q^{\alpha})
for a given choice of parameters
q,q^{\alpha}
.

To compute
LG
in the following, we will continue using De Wit’s thesis manuscript
[
4
]
as a source of coherent content, as we have done in the previous section. De Wit’s approach to defining
LG
is parallel to that of De Wit-Kauffman-Links in
[
7
]
since
LG
is presented as derived from an operator invariant in both cases, although the notations are not always completely similar in the two presentations.

3.2.

Recall that any

g(\Sigma)=\frac{2-\#\text{ disks}+\#\text{ strips}-\#\text{ boundary components}}{2}.

Such surfaces play an important role in classical knot theory, for example when studying the Alexander polynomial.

Proposition 3.7
.

The contribution of the negative cap
\Omega^{-}:V_{\alpha}\otimes V_{\alpha}^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbb{\mathbb{}}{C}(q,q^{\alpha})
to the span of

can be ignored.

Proof.

In terms of span,

\Omega^{-}=\begin{pmatrix}q^{2\alpha}&0&0&0\\
0&-q^{2\alpha}&0&0\\
0&0&-q^{2(\alpha+1)}&0\\
0&0&0&q^{2(\alpha+1)}\end{pmatrix}

can be replaced by

\Omega^{-}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\

0&0&-q^{2}&0\\

in the computation of

Definition 3.9

Proposition 3.11
.

The following
z
and
t
degrees can be computed, and will be useful later in the discussion:

deg_{z}(q^{\alpha})=deg_{z}([\alpha]_{q})=deg_{z}([\alpha+1]_{q})=1;

deg_{z}(q^{-\alpha})=deg_{z}([\alpha]_{q}^{-1})=deg_{z}([\alpha+1]_{q}^{-1})=-1;

deg_{t}(q^{-\alpha})=deg_{t}([\alpha]_{q})=deg_{t}([\alpha+1]_{q})=1;

deg_{t}(q^{\alpha})=deg_{t}([\alpha]_{q}^{-1})=deg_{t}([\alpha+1]_{q}^{-1})=-1.

Proof.

Let us for example compute
deg_{z}([\alpha]_{q}^{-1})
. The others follow similarly or more easily.

\displaystyle[\alpha]_{q}^{-1}=

\displaystyle\text{ }\frac{q-q^{-1}}{q^{\alpha}-q^{-\alpha}}=\frac{q-q^{-1}}{z-z^{-1}}=z^{-1}\frac{q-q^{-1}}{1-z^{-2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ }(q-q^{-1})z^{-1}(1+z^{-2}+z^{-4}+z^{-6}+\ldots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ }(q-q^{-1})(z^{-1}+z^{-3}+z^{-5}+z^{-7}+\ldots),

so using the definition of the
z

deg_{z}([\alpha]_{q}^{-1})=-1
.
∎

4.
Some interesting topological consequences

The first question that comes to mind is whether this new genus bound actually is better than the one provided by the Alexander polynomial

4.1.

For prime knots of 10 or fewer crossings, the span of the Alexander invariant is always equal to the Seifert genus of the knot. There are seven prime knots with 11 crossings for which the Alexander polynomial fails to recover the 3-genus. When listed with respect to the HTW ordering for tables of prime knots of up to 16 crossings (

11^{N}_{34}\text{ (the Conway knot)},11^{N}_{42}\text{ (the Kinoshita\textendash Terasaka knot)},11^{N}_{67}\text{ and }11^{N}_{97}.

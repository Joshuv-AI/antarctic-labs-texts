Symplectic Leibniz algebras as a non-commutative version of symplectic Lie algebras
Fatima-Ezzahrae Abid, Mohamed Boucetta

Abstract
We introduce symplectic left Leibniz algebras and symplectic right Leibniz algebras as generalizations of symplectic Lie algebras. These algebras possess a left symmetric product and are Lie-admissible. We describe completely symmetric Leibniz algebras that are symplectic as both left and right Leibniz algebras. Additionally, we show that symplectic left or right Leibniz algebras can be constructed from a symplectic Lie algebra and a vector space through a method that combines the double extension process and the T ∗ T^{*} -extension. This approach allows us to generate a broad class of examples.



Keywords: Leibniz algebras, symplectic Lie algebras, double extension.

Introduction

Symplectic Lie algebras and symplectic Lie groups are fundamental concepts in symplectic geometry, an important area in mathematics and theoretical physics. Symplectic Lie algebras were studied intensively by many authors and it will be tedious to cite all the papers on this topic and so one can consult the survey

A symplectic Lie algebra is a Lie algebra

with respect to

{\mathrm{ad}}_{[X,Y]}=[{\mathrm{ad}}_{X},{\mathrm{ad}}_{Y}]
is equivalent to

is a left symmetric product, i.e.,

({\cal A},\bullet)

The following proposition is the starting point of this work.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
({\cal A},\bullet)
be an algebra and
\omega
is nondegenerate bilinear skew-symmetric form such that

u\star v-v\star u=[u,v]

u\star v=-{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}v

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}

\star

Proof.

We denote by
\mathrm{K}
the left multiplication of
\star

u,v\in{\cal A}

\displaystyle{\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}-[{\mathrm{L}}_{u},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}]=({\mathrm{L}}_{u\bullet v}^{*})^{*}+[{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{*}]^{*}

\displaystyle=({\mathrm{L}}_{u\star v-v\star u}^{*})^{*}+({\mathrm{L}}_{u\diamond v}^{*})^{*}+[{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{*}]^{*}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{K}_{[u,v]_{\star}}^{*}+[\mathrm{K}_{u}^{*},\mathrm{K}_{v}^{*}]^{*}+({\mathrm{L}}_{u\diamond v}^{*})^{*}

\displaystyle=([\mathrm{K}_{u}^{*},\mathrm{K}_{v}^{*}]-\mathrm{K}_{[u,v]_{\star}})^{*}+({\mathrm{L}}_{u\diamond v}^{*})^{*}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.4

1.

Let

\displaystyle\omega(u,v\bullet w)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\omega(v\bullet u,w)=\frac{1}{2}\omega(w,v\bullet u)=-\frac{1}{4}\omega(v\bullet w,u).

Bi-symplectic symmetric Leibniz algebras

In this section, we give a complete description of symmetric Leibniz algebras endowed with a skew-symmetric bilinear form which is symplectic for both the right and the left Leibniz structure.

Let
({\cal A},\bullet)
be a symmetric Leibniz algebra and
\omega\in\wedge^{2}{\cal A}^{*}

\omega

({\cal A},\bullet,\omega)
is a symplectic left Leibniz algebra as well as a symplectic right Leibniz algebra. We call

Proposition 3.1

Let

be a symmetric Leibniz algebra and

\omega([u,v],w)+\omega([v,w],u)+\omega([w,u],v)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\omega(u\diamond w,v)=\omega(v\diamond w,u).

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of Proposition

(\mathrm{l}2)

∎

On the other hand, according to

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
({\cal A},\bullet,\omega)

be a bi-symplectic commutative symmetric Leibniz algebra. Then there exists a symplectic vector space

\begin{cases}{\mathrm{L}}_{a}={\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}=0,\;{\mathfrak{h}}\bullet{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\;\prec X\bullet Y,Z\succ=T(X,Y,Z),\\

X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{h}},a,b\in B,\alpha,\beta\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.\end{cases}

Moreover, the associated left symmetric product is given by
u\star v=-u\bullet v
.

Proof.

From the description of symmetric Leibniz algebras aforementioned, a commutative symmetric Leibniz algebra is a commutative algebra
({\cal A},\bullet)
such that for any
u,v\in{\cal A}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}=0
. Put

Suppose that
\omega
is a bi-symplectic form on
{\cal A}
. By virtue of Proposition
3.1

,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
is skew-symmetric with respect to
\omega

I^{\perp}=\bigcap_{u\in{\cal A}}\ker{\mathrm{L}}_{u}\quad\mbox{and}\quad I\subset I^{\perp}.

Choose a complement
B
of
I

I^{\perp}

I^{\perp}=I\oplus B

\omega_{B}
of

to

With this identification in mind, the bracket

We give now a complete description of bi-symplectic symmetric Leibniz algebras.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
({\cal A},\bullet)
be a symmetric Leibniz algebra obtained from
({\cal A},[\;,\;])

\rho

\omega\in\wedge^{2}{\cal A}^{*}

T(I^{\perp},\cdot,\cdot)=0

\omega(\rho(u,v),w)=T(u,v,w),\quad u,v,w\in{\cal A}.

Proof.

According to Proposition
3.1
and the description of symmetric Leibniz algebras aforementioned, if
\omega
is bi-symplectic

({\cal A},[\;,\;],\omega)
is a symplectic Lie algebra,
\rho

\rho([u,v],w)=\rho(\rho(u,v),w)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\omega(\rho(u,v),w)=\omega(\rho(u,w),v),\quad u,v,w\in{\cal A}.

For any
u,v,w,z\in\mathfrak{g}

\omega(\rho(\rho(u,v),w),z)=\omega(\rho(w,z),\rho(u,v))=0

and hence
I
is totally isotropic.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\psi(X,Y)+\psi(X,Y))+\frac{1}{2}(\xi(X,Y)+\xi(Y,X)).

\psi(X,Y)+\psi(X,Y)=-(\xi(X,Y)+\xi(Y,X))+2(\theta(X,Y)+\theta(Y,X)).

By adding this relation and (
14
) we get

\psi(X,Y)+\xi(X,Y)=\theta(X,Y)+\theta(Y,X)

and the first relation follows. To show the second relation, put

Q=F(X)\theta(Y,Z)-F(Y)\theta(X,Z)+F(Z)\theta(X,Y).

Having in mind that
F
takes its values in
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
and the relation
{\mathrm{ad}}_{\theta(X,Y)}^{\mathfrak{g}}=[F(X),F(Y)]
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{ad}}_{Q}^{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=[F(X),{\mathrm{ad}}_{\theta(Y,Z)}^{\mathfrak{g}}]-[F(Y),{\mathrm{ad}}_{\theta(X,Z)}^{\mathfrak{g}}]+[F(Z),{\mathrm{ad}}_{\theta(X,Y)}^{\mathfrak{g}}]

\displaystyle=\oint_{X,Y,Z}[F(X),[F(Y),F(Z)]]=0

and we get the second relation.
Finally, by using the equations 10 and 11 in (
13
), we get

\frac{1}{2}S(Y)S(X)-F(Y)S(X)-F^{*}(Y)S(X)=(S(Y)-F(Y))S(X)=0

and hence

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}S(Y)S(X)-F(Y)^{*}S(X)=0.

We have
S(X)^{*}=-S(X)
and, from the equation 14,
[F(X),F(Y)]=-[F(X),S(Y)-F(Y)]=0
and hence
[F(X),S(Y)]=0
. So, by taking the adjoint and the equation 14 ,we get

S(X)F(Y)=\frac{1}{2}S(X)S(Y)=F(Y)S(X)=S(Y)S(X).

So
S(X)S(Y)=2S(Y)S(X)=4S(X)S(Y)
. Thus
S(X)S(Y)=F(X)S(Y)=S(Y)F(X)=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a symplectic Lie algebra,
\star
the associated left symmetric product and
D
a derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then, for any
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(D+D^{*})([a,b])=a\star(D+D^{*})b-b\star(D+D^{*})a.

Proof.

Recall that the left multiplication associated to
\star
is given by
{\mathrm{L}}=-{\mathrm{ad}}^{*}
. Since
D
is a derivation, we have
[D,{\mathrm{ad}}_{a}]={\mathrm{ad}}_{D(a)}
for any
a\in\mathfrak{g}
, and hence
[{\mathrm{L}}_{a},D^{*}]={\mathrm{L}}_{D(a)}
. Thus, for any
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
,

D^{*}(a\star b)=a\star D^{*}b-D(a)\star b

and

\displaystyle D^{*}(a\star b)

\displaystyle=a\star D^{*}b-D(a)\star b,

\displaystyle(D+D^{*})([a,b])

\displaystyle=[Da,b]+[a,Db]+a\star D^{*}b-D(a)\star b-b\star D^{*}a+D(b)\star a,

\displaystyle=-b\star Da+a\star Db+a\star_{\mathfrak{g}}D^{*}b-b\star D^{*}a,

\displaystyle=a\star(D+D^{*})b-b\star(D+D^{*})a

and we get the desired relation.
∎

Let us show now that the last equation in (
13
) is redundant. Indeed, since
S(X)^{*}=-S(X)
the relation
{\mathrm{ad}}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{S(X)a}=0
is equivalent to
S(X)(a\star_{\mathfrak{g}}b)=0

{\mathrm{R}}_{S(X)a}^{\star_{\mathfrak{g}}}=0

(F(X)+F(X)^{*})([a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}})=a\star_{\mathfrak{g}}(F(X)+F(X)^{*})b-b\star_{\mathfrak{g}}(F(X)+F(X)^{*})a

which is always valid since

is a derivation (see Lemma
4.1

u,v,w\in{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
.
Note that this relation holds if
u
,
v
or
w
belongs to
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
.

1.

For any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}(X,Y\circ Z)-\omega_{n}(Y,X\circ Z)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(X\circ Y,Z)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(Y\circ X,Z)

\displaystyle=-\Omega(Y)(Z,X)+\Omega(X)(Z,Y)-\frac{1}{2}\Omega(X)(Y,Z)+\frac{1}{2}\Omega(Y)(X,Z).

We find the first relation in (
13

2.

For
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}(X,Y\circ a)-\omega_{n}(Y,X\circ a)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(X\circ Y,a)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(Y\circ X,a)

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi(Y,X),a)+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi(X,Y),a)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\theta(X,Y),a)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\theta(Y,X),a).

We find the second relation in (
13
).

3.

For
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and
a\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}(X,a\circ Y)-\omega_{n}(a,X\circ Y)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(X\circ a,Y)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(a\circ X,Y)

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\xi(Y,X),a)-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,\theta(X,Y))-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi(X,Y),a))+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\xi(X,Y),a).

We find the third relation in (
13
).

4.

For
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}(X,a\circ b)-\omega_{n}(a,X\circ b)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(X\circ a,b)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(a\circ X,b)

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(K(X)a,b)-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,F(X)b)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(F(X)a,b)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(G(X)a,b),

thus
K(X)=\frac{1}{2}G(X)-\frac{1}{2}F(X)-F(X)^{*}
.

5.

For
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
and
X\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}(a,b\circ X)-\omega_{n}(b,a\circ X)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(a\circ b,X)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}(b\circ a,X)

\displaystyle=\omega_{G}(a,G(X)b)-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(b,G(X)a)-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(K(X)a,b)+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(K(X)b,a).

So

G(X)^{*}+G(X)=\frac{1}{2}(K(X)+K(X)^{*}).

Or

K(X)+K(X)^{*}=\frac{1}{2}G(X)-\frac{1}{2}F(X)-F(X)^{*}+\frac{1}{2}G(X)^{*}-\frac{1}{2}F(X)^{*}-F(X).

Thus

K(X)+K(X)^{*}=\frac{1}{2}(G(X)+G(X)^{*})-\frac{3}{2}(F(X)+F(X)^{*}).

So

F(X)^{*}+G(X)^{*}=-(F(X)+G(X))

and we recover the
the ninth relation in (
13
).

6.

For
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}
the relation is equivalent to
(\mathfrak{g},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a symplectic Lie algebra.

Let us find now the condition for the product
\circ

\begin{cases}X\circ Y=\theta(X,Y)+\Omega(X)(Y,\;\centerdot\;),\;a\circ b=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(K(\;\centerdot\;)a,b),\;\\

{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}^{\circ}={\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}^{\circ}=0,\end{cases}

This product defines a left Leibniz structure if and only if, for any
u,v,w\in{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}

Q(u,v,w):=u\circ(v\circ w)-(u\circ v)\circ w-v\circ(u\circ w)=0.

Since, for any
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}

{\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}={\mathrm{R}}_{\alpha}=0
, we have
Q(\alpha,.,.)=Q(.,\alpha,.)=Q(.,.,\alpha)=0

\begin{cases}Q(X,Y,Z)=Q(X,Y,a)=Q(X,a,Y)=Q(X,a,b)=0,\\
Q(a,b,c)=Q(a,b,X)=Q(a,X,b)=Q(a,X,Y)=0,\end{cases}

X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}

1.

We have

\displaystyle X\circ(Y\circ Z)

\displaystyle=X\circ\theta(Y,Z)=F(X)\theta(Y,Z)+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\psi(X,\;\centerdot\;),\theta(Y,Z))

\displaystyle(X\circ Y)\circ Z

\displaystyle=\theta(X,Y)\circ Z=G(Z)\theta(X,Y)+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\xi(Z,\;\centerdot\;),\theta(X,Y))

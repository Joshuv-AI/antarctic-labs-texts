Willmore-type inequality for closed hypersurfaces in complete manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below
Xiaoshang Jin Jiabin Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we establish a Willmore-type inequality for closed hypersurfaces in a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension n + 1 n+1 with Ric ≥ − n ​ g {\rm Ric}\geq-ng . It extends the classic result of Argostianiani, Fogagnolo and Mazzieri in [ 1 ] to the Riemannian manifold of negative curvature. As an application, we construct a Willmore-type inequality for closed hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space and obtain the characterization of geodesic sphere.

Introduction

The classical Willmore inequality
[
15
]
for a bounded domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth boundary
says that

\int_{\partial\Omega}H^{2}\geq 16\pi,

where
H
is mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
. Such an inequality has been extended in
[
5
]
to submanifolds of any co-dimension in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. In particular, for a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
smooth boundary there holds

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\frac{H}{n-1}|^{n-1}\geq|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|,

with equality attained if and only if
\Omega
is a ball.

In 2020, Agostiniani-Fogagnolo-Mazzieri
[
1
]
used a powerful and elegant
approach based on nonlinear potential theory to establish Willmore equality for a bounded domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary in complete Riemannian manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\frac{H}{n-1}|^{n-1}\geq{\rm AVR}(g)|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|,

(1.1)

where AVR(g) is asymptotic volume ratio defined by

{\rm AVR}(g)=\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n\cdot{\rm Vol}\ B(p,r)}{r^{n}\cdot{\rm Vol}\ \mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

Whereafter, Wang
[
14
]
provides a standard comparison methods in Riemannian geometry to prove Willmore equality (
1.1
) which is very beautiful and concise.

Inspired by these previous results, we can inquire as to whether exist similar Willmore equality in complete Riemannian manifolds with negative Ricci curvature below bounded.

In this paper, we employ the methodology presented in
[
14
]
and offer a positive answer as following. We need to present a few notions in order to effectively articulate our theorem.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
(M,g)
is an
n+1
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifolds with bounded Ricci curvature from below:

Ric\geq-ng.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
M
with smooth boundary, then we call

{\rm RV}(\Omega)=\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,\Omega)\leq R\}}{\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{R}\sinh^{n}sds}

(1.2)

the relative volume of
\Omega.
Here
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Remark 1.2
.

•

(1)
{\rm RV}(\Omega)
is well defined (see Lemma
2.3
.)

•

(2) The formula (
1.2
) can also be written as:

\displaystyle{\rm RV}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,\Omega)\leq R\}}{e^{nR}}

(1.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,\Omega)=R\}}{e^{nR}}

•

(3) It is easy to see by definition that If
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega_{2},
then
{\rm RV}(\Omega_{1})\leq{\rm RV}(\Omega_{2}).

•

(4) If
\Omega=B(p,r)
is a geodesic ball, then

{\rm RV}(B(p,r))=e^{nr}\cdot{\rm RV}(p)

(1.4)

where

{\rm RV}(p)=\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}\ B(p,R)}{\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{R}\sinh^{n}sds}

is well-defined by Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem.

Here is the first main result in this paper:

.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold of
n+1
-dimension
(n\geq 2)
with

and

Then

\int_{\partial\Omega\bigcap\{H\geq-n\}}(1+\frac{H}{n})^{n}d\sigma\geq{\rm RV}(\Omega)\cdot\omega_{n}.

(1.5)

where

is the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
with respect to the outer normal. Moreover, if
H>-n
and
K=(\frac{{\rm RV}(\Omega)\cdot\omega_{n}}{V(\partial\Omega)})^{\frac{1}{n}}-1>-1,
then the equality holds if and only if one of the following cases occurs:

•

(1) If

for some

then
\partial\Omega
is connected,
H_{\partial\Omega}\equiv nK>n
and

is isometric to

Let

\partial M

The defining function is smooth on

(1.6)

Suppose that
(M,g)
is a conformally compact Riemannian manifold and

is a compactification, we call the induced metric

A conformally compact Riemannian manifold is said to be asymptotically hyperbolic (AH) if its sectional curvature goes to

Let’s look back on the three cases in Theorem 1.3. if we set

\displaystyle\geq{\rm RV}(\Omega)\cdot\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\geq{\rm RV}(B(p,I(\Omega))\cdot\omega_{n}

Let

2
A preliminary analysis in Riemannian geometry

Let
\Omega\subseteq M
be the open bounded set with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
H
be the mean curvature. For any
p\in\partial\Omega,
we set
\sigma_{p}:[0,T)\rightarrow M\setminus\Omega
to be the normal geodesic satisfying
\sigma_{p}(0)=p
and
\sigma_{p}^{\prime}(0)\bot T_{p}\partial\Omega.

We define the function
\tau:\partial\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\bigcup\{+\infty\}
by

\tau(p)=\sup\{t>0:d_{g}(\sigma_{p}(t),\partial\Omega)=t\}.

(2.1)

We call
Cut(\partial\Omega)=\{\sigma_{p}(\tau(p)):p\in\partial\Omega\}
the cut
locus for the boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
in
M\setminus\Omega.
The classic Riemannian geometry theory tells us that
Cut(\partial\Omega)
is a closed subset of measure zero and the distance function
d_{g}(\partial\Omega,\cdot)
is smooth in
(M\setminus\Omega)\setminus Cut(\partial\Omega).
Furthermore, if
q=\sigma_{p}(\tau(p))\in Cut(\partial\Omega),
then either
q
is the first focal point of
\partial\Omega
along
\sigma_{p},
or
there exists another focal point
p^{\prime}
on
\partial\Omega
such that
q=\sigma_{p^{\prime}}(\tau(p^{\prime})).

Let
E=\{(p,r)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,+\infty):r<(\tau(p))\}
and we have the diffeomorphism

\Phi:E\rightarrow(M\setminus\Omega)\setminus Cut(\partial\Omega),\ \ \ (p,r)\mapsto\sigma_{p}(r).

(2.2)

We use
d\mu=J(p,r)drd\sigma(p)
denote the volume form and
H(p,r)
to denote the mean curvature of the level set of the distance function to
\partial\Omega
in
M\setminus\Omega.
For any fixed
p\in\partial\Omega,
we have the following inequality by the Bochner formula and Riccati equation:

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla r|^{2}=

\displaystyle|D^{2}r|^{2}+\langle\nabla r,\,\nabla\Delta r\rangle+Ric(\nabla r,\nabla r)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(\Delta r)^{2}}{n}+\langle\nabla r,\,\nabla\Delta r\rangle-n,

that is

H^{\prime}(r)+\frac{H^{2}(r)}{n}\leq n,\ \ \ \ \ r\in[0,\tau(p)),

(2.3)

where
H(r)
is mean curvature of level set and
\Delta r|_{r=0}=H_{p}(0)=H(p)
.

Let’s study the solution
f_{p}(r)
to the following ODE:

f_{p}^{\prime}(r)+f_{p}^{2}(r)=1,\ \ \ \ \ \ f_{p}(0)=\frac{H(p)}{n},\ \ r\geq 0.

(2.4)

It is easy to find that:

(1)

If
H(p)=\pm n,
then
f_{p}(r)=\pm 1.

(2)

If
|H(p)|<n,
then
f_{p}(r)=\tanh(r+r_{1})
where
r_{1}={\rm arctanh}\frac{H(p)}{n}.

(3)

If
|H(p)|>n,
then
f_{p}(r)=\coth(r+r_{2})
where
r_{2}={\rm arccoth}\frac{H(p)}{n}.

Moreover, if
H(p)<-n,
then
r\in[0,-r_{2}).

Furthermore, we have

\frac{J^{\prime}(p,r)}{J(p,r)}=H(r)\leq nf_{p}(r).

(2.5)

By the above facts, we could obtain the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
r<\tau(p),
we have that

\frac{J(p,r)}{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}

is non-increasing. Moreover,

J(p,r)\leq{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}.

(2.6)

Proof.

We will divide it into three cases:

Case 1
:
H(p)=\pm n
;

Case 2
:
|H(p)|<n
;

Case 3
:
|H(p)|>n
.

For the
Case 1

H(p)=\pm n
, we see that
f_{p}(r)=\pm 1
. Moreover
\frac{J^{\prime}}{J}\leq\pm n.
It is easy to see that
\frac{J(p,r)}{e^{\pm nr}}
is non-increasing for

r<\tau(p)
as
(\frac{J(p,r)}{e^{\pm nr}})^{\prime}\leq 0.
Therefore
\frac{J(p,r)}{e^{\pm nr}}\leq\frac{J(p,0)}{e^{0}}=1
which would implies (
2.6
).

For the
Case 2

|H(p)|<n
, we have
f_{p}(r)=\tanh(r+r_{1})
. Moreover

J^{\prime}\leq nJ\frac{\tanh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}}{1+\frac{H(p)}{n}\tanh r}.

Then we can see that

\frac{J}{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}

is non-increasing. Therefore
J(p,r)\leq{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}
.

For the
Case 3

|H(p)|>n
, we know that
f_{p}(r)=\coth(r+r_{2})
. Moreover

J^{\prime}\leq nJ\frac{\coth r+\frac{H(p)}{n}}{1+\frac{H(p)}{n}\coth r}.

Then we can see that

\frac{J}{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}

is non-increasing. Therefore
J(p,r)\leq{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}
.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Set
\bar{J}(p,r)=J(p,r)
for
r<\tau(p)
and
\bar{J}(p,r)=0
for
r\geq\tau(p).
Then

\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\bar{J}(p,r)}{e^{nr}}
exists.

Proof.

For
r\geq\tau(p)
, it is obvious. We only need to consider the
r<\tau(p)
. On the one hand, from Lemma 2.1, we can see that

\tfrac{J}{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}
is non-increasing and bounded which implies that

\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\tfrac{J}{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}
exists.
On the other hand,

\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\tfrac{(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}}{e^{nr}}
exists.

Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma
2.2
.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
(M,g)
is an
n+1
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with

Ric\geq-ng.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
M
with smooth boundary. Then

\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,\Omega)\leq R\}}{e^{nR}}

exists. Hence the relative volume

{\rm RV}(\Omega)
is well-defined.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that
\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{R}\bar{J}(p,r)dr}{e^{nR}}=j(p)
and there exists a number
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\bar{J}(p,r)\leq Ce^{nr}
. Then

\frac{\int_{0}^{R}\bar{J}(p,r)dr}{e^{nR}}\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{R}Ce^{nr}dr}{e^{nR}}\leq C_{1}.

By Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,\Omega)\leq R\}}{e^{nR}}=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{R}\bar{J}(p,r)drd\sigma(p)}{e^{nR}}

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}j(p)d\sigma(p).

This completes the proof Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Proof of the main theorem

Under the conditions of Theorem 1.3, we set
\Sigma_{1}=\partial\Omega\bigcap\{H\geq-n\}
and
\Sigma_{2}=\partial\Omega\bigcap\{H<-n\}.

Then

\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\bar{J}(p,r)drd\sigma(p)=0

(3.1)

as
\bar{J}(p,r)=0
for
r
large enough. Hence
\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\bar{J}(p,r)drd\sigma(p)=o(R).

For
R>0
big enough,

\displaystyle{\rm Vol}

\displaystyle(x\in M:d_{g}(x,\Omega)\leq R)-{\rm Vol}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{R}\bar{J}(p,r)drd\sigma(p)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\int_{0}^{R}(\cosh r+\frac{H(p)}{n}\sinh r)^{n}drd\sigma(p)+\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\bar{J}(p,r)drd\sigma(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\int_{0}^{R}[1+\frac{H(p)}{n}+(1-\frac{H(p)}{n})e^{-2r}]^{n}\cdot(\frac{e^{r}}{2})^{n}drd\sigma(p)+o(R)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\int_{0}^{R}[(1+\frac{H(p)}{n})^{n}(\frac{e^{r}}{2})^{n}+O(e^{(n-2)r})]drd\sigma(p)+o(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{nR}}{n\cdot 2^{n}}\int_{\Sigma_{1}}(1+\frac{H(p)}{n})^{n}d\sigma(p)+O(e^{(n-2)R})+o(R)

Multiply both sides by
\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}}{e^{nR}}
and let
R\rightarrow+\infty
, we have

\int_{\partial\Omega\bigcap\{H\geq-n\}}(1+\frac{H(p)}{n})^{n}d\sigma\geq{\rm RV}(\Omega)\cdot\omega_{n}.

3.1
Rigidity

We are going to prove the rigidity result in Theorem 1.3. The “if” part is easy from a direct computation. Here we only provide the procedure of case three, i.e. if
M\setminus\Omega
is isometric to

\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}([s_{j},+\infty)\times\Sigma_{j},dr^{2}+\frac{\cosh^{2}r}{\cosh^{2}s_{j}}g_{\Sigma_{j}})

for some constants
\{s_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},
then
\int_{\partial\Omega}(1+\frac{H(p)}{n})^{n}d\sigma={\rm RV}(\Omega)\cdot\omega_{n}.
Notice that
the mean curvature
H
on
\Sigma_{j}
is just
n\tanh s_{j}\in(-n,n).
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(1+\frac{H(p)}{n})^{n}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Sigma_{j}}(1+\frac{H(p)}{n})^{n}d\sigma=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Sigma_{j}}(1+\tanh s_{j})^{n}d\sigma

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\frac{e^{s_{j}}}{\cosh s_{j}})^{n}\cdot{\rm Vol}(\Sigma_{j})

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\rm RV}(\Omega)\cdot\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=n\cdot 2^{n}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,\Omega)\leq R\}}{e^{nR}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=n\cdot 2^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{e^{nR}}\int_{\Sigma_{j}}\int_{s_{j}}^{s_{j}+R}\frac{\cosh^{n}r}{\cosh^{n}s_{j}}drd\sigma

4
A Willmore typer inequality in hyperbolic space

In this section, we prove Theorem 1.7. The inequality (
1.8
) is an easy corollary of Theorem 1.3 and the monotonicity of relative volume. In order to prove the rigidity result, we firstly recall that the relative volume is defined by the limit of the quotient in (1.2) and it is well-defined since the Ricci curvature is bounded from below. In the following, we introduce another method to calculate the relative volume on AH manifold of order 2 which was introduced in
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n+1
-dimensional conformally compact Riemannian manifold and its sectional curvature satisfies that
|K_{g}+1|=O(x^{2})
where
x
is the geodesic defining function and
\bar{g}=x^{2}g
is the geodesic compactification with boundary metric
\hat{g}=\bar{g}|_{\partial M}.
Let
E\subset M
be a compact subset or bounded open set, we use
s_{E}
to denote the distance function of
E,
i.e.

\forall q\in M,\ \ s_{E}(q)=d_{g}(E,q)=\inf\limits_{y\in E}d_{g}(y,q).

(4.1)

Let
r=-\ln x
near the boundary and it is a distance function of
(M,g).
We set the conformal change
g^{E}=e^{-2s_{E}}g
on
M
and could prove that
|d(s_{E}-r)|_{\bar{g}}
is bounded for sufficiently large
r
almost everywhere (Lemma 4.1 in
[
11
]
, Lemma
3.1 in
[
7
]
).
Hence
g^{E}=e^{-2s_{E}}g=e^{2(r-s_{E})}\bar{g}
is Lipschitz continuous to the boundary.
Then

\displaystyle{\rm RV}(E)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}_{g}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,E)=R\}}{e^{nR}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Vol}_{g^{E}}\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,E)=R\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\cdot{\rm Vol}(\partial M,\hat{g}^{E})

where
\hat{g}^{E}=g^{E}|_{\partial X}.

Now we consider the
n+1
-dimentional hyperbolic space. Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be an open bounded set and
B(p,I(\Omega))\subseteq\Omega
is the inscribed ball.
Set
s_{p}(\cdot)=d_{g}(p,\cdot)
and
s_{\Omega}(\cdot)=d_{g}(\Omega,\cdot)
be the distance functions and consider the conformal compact metric
g^{p}=e^{-2s_{p}}g
and
g^{\Omega}=e^{-2s_{\Omega}}g
with boundary metrics
\hat{g}^{p}
and
\hat{g}^{\Omega}
on the boundary
\mathbb{S}^{n}.
We have that

1={\rm RV}(p)=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\cdot{\rm Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\hat{g}^{p}).

As it is showed above,
s_{p}-s_{\Omega}\geq I(\Omega)
and it is Lipschitz continuous
to the boundary. Then
(s_{p}-s_{\Omega})|_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\geq I(\Omega)
.
Hence

\displaystyle{\rm RV}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}{\rm Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\hat{g}_{\Omega})=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}e^{n(s_{p}(q)-s_{\Omega}(q))}dV_{\hat{g}^{p}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}e^{nI(\Omega)}dV_{\hat{g}^{p}}=e^{nI(\Omega)}.

If the equality in (
1.8
) holds, then equality
{\rm RV}(\Omega)=e^{nI(\Omega)}
also holds and from (
4.3
), we get that

(s_{p}-s_{\Omega})|_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\equiv I(\Omega).

(4.4)

by the continuous of
s_{p}-s_{\Omega}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Let
\sigma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be any geodesic ray from
p=\sigma(0)
to
\hat{q}=\sigma(+\infty)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Let
q\in\partial\Omega\bigcap\sigma
be any point when
\sigma
meets
\partial\Omega.
Then

d(p,q)\geq I(\Omega)
by the definition of inscribed radius. On the other hand,

\displaystyle I(\Omega)

\displaystyle=(s_{p}-s_{\Omega})(\hat{q})=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}(d(p,\sigma(t)-d(\Omega,\sigma(t)))

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}(d(p,q)+d(q,\sigma(t))-d(\Omega,\sigma(t)))

\displaystyle\geq\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}d(p,q)=d(p,q).

Then
d(p,q)=I(\Omega)
and hence
q
is unique. We get that
q\in\partial B(p,I(\Omega)).
Since the direction of
\sigma
is arbitrarily selected, we finally obtain that

\Omega=B(p,I(\Omega)).

Example 5.1
.

(M,g)=(\mathbb{R}\times N,dr^{2}+e^{2r}g_{N})

N

n

\partial\Omega

It is easy to calculate that the mean curvature

Then

\displaystyle{\rm RV}(\Omega)\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=n\cdot 2^{n}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}e^{-nR}[V(\Omega)+\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\int_{0}^{R}e^{nr}drd\sigma+\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\int_{-R}^{0}e^{nr}drd\sigma]

\displaystyle=2^{n}\cdot{\rm Vol}(\Sigma_{2})=\int_{\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}}(1+\frac{H}{n})^{n}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}(1+\frac{H}{n})^{n}d\sigma.

In this case,

has two ends and one of them is a cusp. Then
M
dose not admit a conformal compactification. The condition

Example 5.2
.

(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},g)=(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},dr^{2}+\phi^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})
, where

\phi(r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &3e^{r-2}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ r\geq 2,\\
&r+\frac{1}{16}r^{4}\ \ \ r\in[0,2).\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

Then we have the following facts.

Lemma 5.3
.

The metric
g
is
C^{2}
and
Ric\geq-ng
.

Proof.

It is trivial that
g\in C^{2}
because

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi(0)=0,\ \ \phi^{\prime}(0)=1,\ \ \phi^{\prime\prime}(0)=0,\\
&\phi(2)=\phi^{\prime}(2)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(2)=3.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.3)

For
X\bot\nabla r
and
|X|=1

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Ric(X,X)=(n-1)\frac{1-\phi^{\prime 2}}{\phi^{2}}-\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi},\\
&Ric(\nabla r,\nabla r)=-n\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.4)

Hence, we only need to check
\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\leq 1

\frac{\phi^{\prime 2}-1}{\phi^{2}}\leq 1

If

If

.

\displaystyle\phi^{2}-\phi^{\prime 2}+1

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{256}(r^{6}-16r^{4}+32r^{3}-128r+256)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{256}\left((r^{3}-8r+8)^{2}+16(r^{3}-4r^{2}+12)\right)\geq 0.

Abstract
. The algebraic form of Hilbert’s 13th Problem asks for the resolvent degree rd ⁡ ( n ) \operatorname{rd}(n) of the general polynomial f ⁡ ( x ) = x n + a 1 ​ x n − 1 + … + a n f(x)=x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{n} of degree n n , where a 1 , … , a n a_{1},\ldots,a_{n} are independent variables. The resolvent degree is the minimal integer d d such that every root of f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) can be obtained in a finite number of steps, starting with ℂ ⁡ ( a 1 , … , a n ) \mathbb{C}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}) and adjoining algebraic functions in ⩽ d \leqslant d variables at each step. Recently Farb and Wolfson defined the resolvent degree rd k ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{rd}_{k}(G) of any finite group G G and any base field k k of characteristic 0 0 . In this setting rd ⁡ ( n ) = rd ℂ ⁡ ( S n ) \operatorname{rd}(n)=\operatorname{rd}_{\mathbb{C}}(\operatorname{S}_{n}) , where S n \operatorname{S}_{n} denotes the symmetric group. In this paper we define rd k ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{rd}_{k}(G) for every algebraic group G G over an arbitrary field k k , investigate the dependency of this quantity on k k and show that rd k ⁡ ( G ) ⩽ 5 \operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 5 for any field k k and any connected group G G . The question of whether rd k ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{rd}_{k}(G) can be bigger than 1 1 for any field k k and any algebraic group G G over k k (not necessarily connected) remains open.

1.
Introduction

The algebraic forms of Hilbert’s 13th Problem asks for the resolvent degree
\operatorname{rd}(n)
, which is the smallest integer

d
such that a root of the general polynomial

f(x)=x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{n}

can be expressed as a composition of algebraic functions of
d
variables with complex coefficients.
It is known that
\operatorname{rd}(n)=1
when
n\leqslant 5
, and that
1\leqslant\operatorname{rd}(n)\leqslant n-\alpha(n)
.
where
\alpha(n)
is an unbounded but very slow growing function of
n
. Classical upper bounds of this form have been recently
sharpened by Wolfson
[
45
]
, Sutherland
[
39
]
and Heberle-Sutherland
[
22
]
. On the other hand,
it is not known whether or not
\operatorname{rd}(n)>1
for any

n\geqslant 6
. For a brief informal introduction to Hilbert’s 13th Problem, see
[
28
, Section 1]
.
For a more detailed discussion, see
[
14
,
16
]
.

Farb and Wolfson
[
16
]
defined the resolvent degree
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
for every finite group
G
over an arbitrary base field
k
of characteristic
0
. In this setting
\operatorname{rd}(n)=\operatorname{rd}_{\mathbb{C}}(\operatorname{S}_{n})
, and it is not known whether or not
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
can ever be
>1
.

In this paper we extend their definition of
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
to an arbitrary algebraic group
G
(not necesarily finite, affine or smooth) defined over an arbitrary field
k
. Our definition proceeds in three steps. First we define the level of a finite field extension (Definition
4.1
), then the resolvent degree of a functor (Definition
7.3
), then the resolvent degree of a algebraic group (Definition
10.1
). Out first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a connected algebraic group over a field
k
.
Then

(a)
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 5
.

(b) Moreover, if
G
has no simple components of type
E_{8}
, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 1
.

Note that Theorem
1.1
was announced (in a weaker form and without proof) in Section 8 of my survey
[
28
]
.
We will also investigate the dependence of
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
on the base field
k
. Our main results in this direction are Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
below.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group defined over
k
. Then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)=\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(G_{k^{\prime}})

for any field extension
k^{\prime}/k
.

The case where
k^{\prime}
is algebraic over
k
is fairly straightforward (see Proposition
8.3
(b));
the main point here is that the extension
k^{\prime}/k
can be arbitrary. In particular, if
G
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
, when
Theorem
1.2
tells us that
\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(G_{k^{\prime}})=\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{k})
for any two fields
k
and
k^{\prime}
of the same characteristic.
In arbitrary characteristic, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a smooth affine group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
. Denote the connected component
of
G
by
G^{0}
. Assume that
G^{0}
is split reductive and
G/G^{0}
is finite over
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{k})\geqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{k_{0}})
for any other field
k_{0}
.

We will deduce Theorem
1.3
from a more general result, Proposition
13.1
, which compares the resolvent degrees of
the general and the special fibers of a group scheme over a discrete valuation ring.
Note that both Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
apply in the classical case, where
G
is an abstract
finite group viewed as a group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
, in particular to
G=\operatorname{S}_{n}
.

A key role in our proof of Theorem
1.1
(b) will be played by a theorem of Tits, which asserts that
if
G
is a simple group over
k
of any type other
than
E_{8}
and
T\to\operatorname{Spec}(K)
is a
G
-torsor, then
T
can be “split by radicals”, i.e.,
T
splits over some
radical extension of
K
; see Section
16
. Tits asked whether or not the same is true for simple groups
of type
E_{8}
. Using the arguments in Section
16
, one readily sees that a positive answer to this question
would imply the following.

Conjecture 1.4
.

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 1
for any connected algebraic group
G
over any field
k
.

Theorem
1.1
(a) (or more precisely, Proposition
16.1
(a)),
may thus be viewed as a partial answer to Tits’ question. Note that it is not known whether
or not
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
can be
>1
for any field
k
and any algebraic group
G
defined
over
k
(not necessarily connected).

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Sections
2
and
3

are devoted to preliminary material on essential dimension
of finite-dimensional algebras and field extensions. In Section
4
defines

Section

Section

introduces the level

.
This notion parallels the notion

]
but the type of functor we allow is more restrictive.
Much of the work towards proving
Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
is, in fact, done in the general setting of functors
in Sections
8
and
9
. Section
10

introduces the notion of resolvent degree of an algebraic group. In Section
11

we study the resolvent degree of infinitesimal groups and abelian varieties. The proof of Theorem
1.2

is completed in Section
12
, the proof of Theorem
1.3
in Section

, and the proof of Theorem

in Sections
14
-
16
.
In the last section we show that Conjecture
1.4

follows from a positive answer to a long-standing open question of Serre (Question

).

The main focus of this paper is on the aspects of the subject which have not been previously investigated: resolvent degree of
connected groups and dependence of resolvent degree on the base field. However, many of the preliminary results
overlap with existing literature and some have classical roots. In particular, Section

overlaps
with
[

, Section

.

2.
Preliminaries on finite-dimensional algebras

Let
K
be a field and
A
be finite-dimensional
K
-algebra. We will say that
A
descends to a subfield
K_{0}
of
K
if there exists
a
K_{0}
-algebra
A_{0}
such that
A\simeq_{K}A_{0}\otimes_{K_{0}}K
. Here
\simeq_{K}
stands for isomorphism of algebras over
K
.
We will sometimes say that
A/K
descends to
A_{0}/K_{0}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\subset K
be a field extension,
A
a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra, and

S
a finite subset of
A
. Then
A/K
descends to
A_{0}/K_{0}
such that
K_{0}
is finitely
generated over
k
,
A_{0}
is a
K_{0}
-subalgebra of
A
, and
S\subset A_{0}
.

Proof.

Choose a
K
-vector space basis
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
in
A
. Write

\displaystyle b_{i}\cdot b_{j}=\sum_{h=1}^{n}c_{ij}^{h}b_{h}

for every
i
,
j=1,\ldots,n
and

\displaystyle s=\sum_{h=1}^{n}\alpha_{s}^{h}b_{h}

for every
s\in S
. Let

K_{0}=k(c_{ij}^{h},\;\alpha_{s}^{h}\;|\;i,j,h=1,\ldots,n\;\;\text{and}\;\;s\in S)

and
L_{0}
be the
K_{0}
-subsalgebra of
A
generated by
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
. Then one readily sees that

K_{0}
is finitely generated over
k
, the natural map

A_{0}\otimes_{K_{0}}K\to A

is an isomorphism over
K
, and
S\subset A_{0}
.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Let
K
be a field containing
k
and
A
be a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra. The essential dimension
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A/K)
is
the minimal value of
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})
, where the minimum is taken over all intermediate fields
k\subset K_{0}\subset K
such that

L/K
descends to
K_{0}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a field containing
k
and
A
a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra. Then
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)<\infty
.
Moreover,
A/K
descends to some
A_{0}/K_{0}
such that
K_{0}
is finitely generated over
k
and
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A_{0})=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})
.

Proof.

Descend
A/K
to
A_{1}/K_{1}
so that
d=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{1})
is the smallest possible, i.e.,
d=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
.
Note that a priori
d
is a non-negative integer or
\infty
.
By Lemma
2.1
,
A_{1}/K_{1}
further descends to
A_{0}/K_{0}
, where
k\subset K_{0}\subset K_{1}
and
K_{0}
is finitely generated over

k
. By the minimality of
d
,
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A_{1})=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A_{0})=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=d
. Moreover, since
K_{0}
is finitely generated over
k
,

d<\infty
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
k\subset k^{\prime}\subset K
be fields and
A
be a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra.
Then

(a)
\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
.

(b) If
k^{\prime}
is algebraic over
k
, then
\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
.

(c) There exists an intermediate field
k\subset l_{0}\subset k^{\prime}
such that
l_{0}
is finitely generated over
k
and

\operatorname{ed}_{l}(A)=\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)
for any
l_{0}\subset l\subset k^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose
A
descends to a subfield
K_{0}\subset K
containing
k
such that
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})
as small
as possible, i.e.,
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
. Then
A
also descends to
k^{\prime}K_{0}
, where the compositum is
taken in
K
. Now
\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)\leqslant\operatorname{trdeg}_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime}K_{0})\leqslant\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
.

(b) In view of part (a), it suffices to show that
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)
.
Indeed,
A
descends to some intermediate field
k^{\prime}\subset K_{0}^{\prime}\subset K
such that

\operatorname{trdeg}_{k^{\prime}}(K_{0}^{\prime})=\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)
. If
k^{\prime}
is algebraic over
k
, then

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)\leqslant\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0}^{\prime})=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k^{\prime}}(K_{0})=\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)
.

(c) By Lemma
2.3
,
A/K
descends to some
A_{0}/K_{0}
such that

k^{\prime}\subset K_{0}\subset K
,
\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)=\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A_{0})=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k^{\prime}}(K_{0})
and
K_{0}
is is generated by finitely many elements
over
k^{\prime}
, say
K_{0}=k^{\prime}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
be independent variables over
k^{\prime}
.
For each subset
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}\subset\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, such that the elements

a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{r}}
are algebraically dependent over
k^{\prime}
, choose a polynomial
0\neq p_{I}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}})\in k^{\prime}[x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}}]
such that
p_{I}(a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{r}})=0
.
Now choose an intermediate field
k\subset l_{0}\subset k^{\prime}
such that
l_{0}
is generated by the coefficients of
the polynomials
p_{I}
for every such
I
.
With this choice of
l_{0}
, any subset of
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
which is algebraically dependent over
k^{\prime}

remains algebraically dependent in
l_{0}
. In other words,
\operatorname{trdeg}_{l_{0}}(K_{0})=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k^{\prime}}(K_{0})
and thus

(1)

\operatorname{ed}_{l_{0}}(A)\leqslant\operatorname{trdeg}_{l_{0}}(K_{0})=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k^{\prime}}(L_{0})=\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A).

By part (a),
\operatorname{ed}_{l_{0}}(A)\geqslant\operatorname{ed}_{l}(A)\geqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k^{\prime}}(A)
for any intermediate field
l_{0}\subset l\subset k^{\prime}
.
Now (
1
) tells us that both of these inequalities are, in fact, equalities, as desired.
∎

3.
Preliminaries on field extensions

We will be particularly interested in the case where the finite-dimensional
K
-algebra
A
is itself a field.
In this case we will usually use the letter
L
in place of
A
and
write
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
in place of
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\subset K\subset L
be field extensions such that
[L:K]<\infty
.

(a) If
K^{\rm sep}
is the separable closure of
K
in
L
, then
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K^{\rm sep}/K)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
and

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K^{\rm sep})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
.

(b) If
L
is separable over
K
, and
L^{\rm norm}
is the normal closure of
L
, then
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L^{\rm norm}/K)
.

(c) Suppose
K\subset E\subset L
is an intermediate extension. If
E
is separable over
K
, then
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(E/K)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose
L/K
descends to
L_{0}/K_{0}
. Denote the separable closure of
K_{0}
in
L_{0}
by
(K_{0})^{\rm sep}
.
Then
K^{\rm sep}/K
descends to
K_{0}^{\rm sep}/K_{0}
,
L/K^{\rm sep}
descends to
L_{0}/(K_{0})^{\rm sep}
and part (a) follows.

(b) is proved in
[
7
, Lemma 2.3]
.

(c) In view of part (a), it suffices to show that
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(E/K)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K^{\rm sep}/K)
.
In other words, we may replace
L
by
K^{\rm sep}
and thus assume without loss of generality that
L
is separable over
K
. By (b), we may further replace
L
by its normal
closure over
K
and thus assume that
L
is Galois over
K
. Then
E=L^{H}
, where
H
is a subgroup of
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
.
By
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
,
L/K
descends to some
L_{0}/K_{0}
, where
k\subset K_{0}\subset K
,
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
, and

L_{0}
is a
G
-invariant subfield of
L
. Then
E/K
descends to
L_{0}^{H}/K_{0}
. This tells us that
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(E/K)\leqslant\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
, as desired.
∎

We will say that a field extension
L/K
is simple if
[L:K]<\infty
and
L
is generated by one element over
K
.
In other words,
L\simeq K[x]/(f(x))
, where
f(x)\in K[x]
is an irreducible polynomial over
K
.
By the Primitive Element Theorem, every finite separable extension is simple.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose a finite field extension
L/K
descends to
L_{0}/K_{0}
. Then
L/K
is simple if and only if
L_{0}/K_{0}
is simple.

Proof.

One direction is obvious: if
L_{0}=K_{0}(a)
is simple, then
L=K(a)
is also simple.

To prove the converse, assume that
L/K
is simple and set
n=[L:K]=[L_{0}:K_{0}]
.
If
K_{0}
is a finite field, then so is
L_{0}
. In this case
L_{0}/K_{0}
is separable and hence, simple.
Thus we may assume that
K_{0}
is infinite. It suffices to show that
L_{0}
contains an element of degree
n
over
K_{0}
.
View
L_{0}
as the set of
K_{0}
-points of the
n
-dimensional affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
, and

L
as the set of
K
-points of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
. Let
X\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}
be the subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
determined by the condition that
for
x\in\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K_{0}}
,
1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}
are linearly dependent. Here multiplication in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K_{0}}
comes from identifying

\mathbb{A}^{n}(K_{0})
with
L_{0}
. It is easy to see that

X
is a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
(given by the vanishing of a single
n\times n
determinant) defined over
K_{0}
.
Then
X(K_{0})
is the set of elements of
L_{0}
of degree
\leqslant n-1
over
K_{0}

and
X(K)
is the set of elements of
L
of degree
\leqslant n-1
over
K
.
We know that
L/K
is simple; hence,
X\subsetneq\mathbb{A}^{n}
. That is,
U=\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus X
is a non-empty Zariski
open subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
defined over
K_{0}
. Since
K_{0}
is an infinite field, we conclude that

U(K_{0})\neq\emptyset
. In other words,
L_{0}/K_{0}
is simple, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k\subset K\subset L
be fields such that
L/K
is simple.
Assume
K^{\prime}/K
is another field extension (not necessarily finite),
and
L^{\prime}=K^{\prime}L
be a compositum of
K^{\prime}
and
L
over
K
.
Then
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L^{\prime}/K^{\prime})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
.

Note that Lemma
3.3
is immediate from the definition of
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/L)

in the case, where
L^{\prime}\simeq L\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}
or equivalently,
[L^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=[L:K]
.
The only (slight) complication arises from the fact that
[L^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
may be smaller than
[L:K]
.

Proof.

Set
n=[L:K]
and
d=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
. Then
L/K
descends to some intermediate field
k\subset K_{0}\subset K

such that
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=d
. That is, there exists a field extension
L_{0}/K_{0}
such that
L\simeq_{K}L_{0}\otimes_{K_{0}}K
,
where
\simeq_{K_{0}}
denotes an isomorphism of fields over
K_{0}
.

By Lemma
3.2
,
L_{0}/K_{0}
is simple. That is,

L_{0}\simeq_{K_{0}}K_{0}[x]/(f(x))
, where
f(x)\in K_{0}[x]
is a polynomial of degree
n
, irreducible over
K_{0}
.
Then
L\,\simeq_{K}\,K[x]/(f(x))
.
Now let
f(x)=f_{1}(x)\ldots f_{r}(x)
be an irreducible decomposition of
f(x)
over
K^{\prime}
. A compositum
L^{\prime}
of
L
and
K^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
K^{\prime}[x]/(f_{i}(x))
for some
i
, say,
L^{\prime}\,\simeq_{K^{\prime}}\,K^{\prime}[x]/(f_{1}(x))
. Denote the degree of
f_{1}(x)
by
n_{1}=[L^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
and the roots of

f_{1}
in the algebraic closure of
K
by
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n_{1}}
. Since each
\alpha_{i}
is a root of
f(x)\in K_{0}[x]
, each
\alpha_{i}

algebraic over
K_{0}
. Hence, the coefficients of
f_{1}(x)
, being elementary
symmetric polynomials in
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n_{1}}
, are also algebraic over
K_{0}
. This shows that

f_{1}(x)\in K_{0}^{\rm alg}[x]
, where
K_{0}^{alg}
is the algebraic closure of
K_{0}
in
K
. In other words,
L^{\prime}/K^{\prime}
descends to

K_{0}^{\rm alg}
. Consequently,

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L^{\prime}/K^{\prime})\leqslant\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0}^{\rm alg})=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=d=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K),

as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k\subset K\subset L
be fields such that
[L:K]<\infty
. Then there exist
intermediate extensions
K=K^{(0)}\subset K^{(1)}\subset\ldots\subset K^{(r)}=L
such that

K^{(i)}/K^{(i-1)}
is simple and
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K^{(i)}/K^{(i-1)})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)

for every
i=1,\ldots,r
.

Proof.

Set
d=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
. By definition,
L/K
descends to
L_{0}/K_{0}
, where
k\subset K_{0}\subset K
and
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=d
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
be
generators for
L_{0}
over
K_{0}
and set

We obtain the following diagram, where

\textstyle{K=K^{(0)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\;\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K^{(1)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\;\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

4.
The level of a finite field extension

We now define the level of a field extension, following Dixmier
[
14
, Section 2]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
k
be a base field,
K
be a field containing
k
, and
L/K
be a field extension of finite degree.
I will say that
L/K
is of level
\leqslant d
if there exists a diagram of field extensions

(2)

\textstyle{K_{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\vdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

such that

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})\leqslant d
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
.
The level of
L/K
is the smallest such

; I will denote it by
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)
.

Remarks 4.2
.

(1) The same notion was introduced by Brauer
[
2
]
(in characteristic

) under the name of resolvent degree. In this paper we will reserve the term “resolvent degree” for the resolvent degree of an object of a functor; see Definition
7.3

If

is a finite separable extension, then we may view

as an object of the functor of étale algebras, and the two notions coincide; see Example
7.5

(2) (cf.
[

, Lemma 2.5.3]
) If

and

, then
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L^{\prime}/K)\leqslant\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)

Indeed, any tower (

\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L^{\prime}/K)\leqslant d
.

(3) (cf.
[
16
, Lemma 2.5.1]
) Taking

in (

\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
.

(iv) We may assume without loss of generality that each extension

in the tower (
2
)
is simple. Indeed, by Lemma
3.4
, we may replace
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
by a sequence of simple extensions without
increasing the essential dimension.

(5) Definition
4.1
formalizes the classical notion of composition of algebraic functions.
If

is a field of rational functions on some algebraic variety

defined over

, then it
is natural to think of

as being generated by algebraic (multi-valued) functions on

\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{1}/K)

and

as being generated by compositions of

in
\leqslant\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)

(6) No examples where

are known.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
k\subset k^{\prime}\subset K\subset L

are fields and
[L:K]<\infty
. Then

(a) (cf.
[
16
, Lemma 2.5.2]
)
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\geqslant\operatorname{lev}_{k^{\prime}}(L/K)

(b) Moreover, equality holds if

(c) Furthermore, there always exists an intermediate field

\operatorname{lev}_{l}(L/K)=\operatorname{lev}_{k^{\prime}}(L/K)

Proof.

Choose a tower
K=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\ldots\subset K_{m}
, as in Definition
4.1

and apply parts (a), (b) and (c) of Lemma

, respectively,
to each intermediate extension
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
. In part (c), let

be a finitely generated field extension
obtained by applying Lemma

(c) to

to be the compositum of
l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}
in

over

.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
k\subset K\subset L
are fields and

and let

be another field extension (not necessarily finite).
Then
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(K^{\prime}L/K^{\prime})\leqslant\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)

K^{\prime}L
denotes an arbitrary compositum of
K^{\prime}
and
L

K
.

Proof.

Set

and choose
a tower
K=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\ldots\subset K_{m}
as in Definition
4.1
. By Remark
4.2
(4)
we may assume that each intermediate extension
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is simple. Now consider the tower

K^{\prime}=K_{0}^{\prime}\subset K_{1}^{\prime}\subset\ldots\subset K_{m}^{\prime},

Here
K_{m}^{\prime}=K^{\prime}K_{m}
is some compositum of
K^{\prime}
and
K_{m}
, and for

is the compositum of

and

in

over

. Since

\operatorname{lev}_{k}(K^{\prime}L/K^{\prime})\leqslant d
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that
k\subset K\subset L
are fields and
[L:K]<\infty
.
Then
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)=0
if and only if
L
embeds in a compositum
\overline{k}K
over
k
.
In particular, if
k
is algebraically closed, then
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)=0
if and only if
L=K
.

Proof.

The second assertion is an immediate consequence of the first.

To prove the first assertion, suppose
L\subset\overline{k}K
. In other words,
L/K

is generated by elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in L
which are algebraic over
k
.
Consider the tower of simple extensions

k=k_{0}\subset k_{1}\subset\ldots\subset k_{m},

where
k_{i}=k(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})
. Since
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(k_{i})=0
,
we have
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(k_{i}/k_{i-1})=0
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
.
Now consider the tower

K=k_{0}K\subset k_{1}K\subset\ldots\subset k_{m}K=L.

By Lemma
4.4
,
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(k_{i}K/k_{i-1}K)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(k_{i}/k_{i-1})=0
.
Thus
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)=0
.

Conversely, suppose
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)=0
. Then there exists a tower (
2
) of field extensions such that

L\subset K_{m}
(over
K
) and
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})=0
for each

. Consequently,
K_{i}
is generated over

K_{i-1}
by elements that are algebraic over
k
. This implies that
K_{m}
embeds in
\overline{k}K
over
K
, and hence, so does

.
∎

Recall that a finite field extension

is called radical if there exists a tower (
2
)
such that

, where
\lambda^{n_{i}}\in K
for some

,
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
K
be a field containing
k
and
L/K
be a finite field extension.
Assume that
L/K
is (a) solvable, (b) radical, (c) purely inseparable. Then
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\leqslant 1
.

Proof.

By definition
L/K
is solvable if there exists a tower
K=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\ldots\subset K_{m}
,
as in (
2
), such that
L
embeds into
K_{m}
over
K
and

K_{i}
is of the form
K_{i-1}(\lambda_{i})
for each

, where

\lambda_{i}
is a root of a polynomial of the form

(i)
x^{n_{i}}-a_{i}
or
(ii)

for some positive integer

and

Note that (i) covers the case, where
\lambda_{i}
is a root of unity (

),
and (ii) is only needed when

In both cases

and thus

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(k(\lambda)/k(a))\leqslant 1.

Here the first inequality follows from Lemma

. The second inequality is
obvious, since
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(k(a))\leqslant 1
. Thus
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\leqslant 1
. This proves (a).

(b) is proved by the same argument, except that case (ii) does not occur.

(c) follows from (b) because every purely inseparable extension is radical.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

(cf.
[
16
, Lemma 2.7]
) Let

be a field containing

Proof.

Choose a tower

for

over

By Remark

Let

Fix embeddings

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

(cf.
[
16
, Lemma 2.11]
)
Let
k\subset K\subset L
be fields. Assume that the field extension
L/K
is finite and separable.
Denote the normal closure of
L
over
K
by

. Then
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)=\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L^{\rm norm}/K)
.

Proof.

By Remark
4.2
(2),
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\leqslant\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L^{\rm norm}/K)
. We will thus focus on proving the opposite inequality.

Set

. By the Primitive Element Theorem,
L^{\rm norm}\simeq_{K}K[x]/f(x)
for some irreducible polynomial

.
Then
f(x)
splits into a product of linear factors over

. Denote its roots in

by
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
.
Set

. We claim that
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L_{i}/L_{i-1})\leqslant d
for each

If we can prove this claim, then applying Lemma

\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L^{\rm norm}/K)=\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L_{n}/K)\leqslant d=\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K).

It thus remains to prove the claim. Since

is a composite of

and

tells us that

\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L_{i}/L_{i-1})=\operatorname{lev}(L_{i-1}K(\alpha_{i})/L_{i-1})\leqslant\operatorname{lev}_{k}(K(\alpha_{i})/K)=\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)=d,

∎

Proposition 4.9
.

(cf.
[
16
, Lemma 2.12]
)
Let
k\subset K\subset L
be fields, where
[L:K]<\infty
. Assume that

\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\leqslant d
. Then the tower

K=K_{0}\subset\ldots\subset K_{m}

of field extensions
in Definition
4.1
can be chosen to have the following additional properties.

(a) Each field extension
K_{i}/K_{i+1}
is simple and either separable or purely inseparable.

(b) If
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is separable, then it is Galois.

(c) If
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is Galois, then
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})
is a finite simple group.

Proof.

We will start with a tower
K=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\ldots\subset K_{m}
of Definition
4.1
.
By Remark
4.2
(4), we may assume that each intermediate extension
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is simple.
We will now modify this tower in three steps (a), (b) and (c),
so that it acquires properties (a), (b), and (c) from the statement of the proposition, respectively. At each stage
m

may increase and the fields
K_{i}
may change, but every
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
will remain simple, the largest field
K_{m}
will either
get larger or stay the same (and in particular, it will continue to contain
L
), and the maximal value of
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})
will
not increase (so that it will remains
\leqslant d
).

(a) Let
K^{\rm sep}_{i-1}
be the separable closure of
K_{i-1}
in
K_{i}
. If
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is neither separable nor
purely inseparable, i.e.,
K_{i-1}\subsetneq K^{\rm sep}_{i-1}\subsetneq K_{i}
, we insert
K^{\rm sep}_{i-1}

between
K_{i-1}
and
K_{i}
. Note that
K_{i}/K_{i-1}^{\rm sep}
is simple because
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is, and
K^{\rm sep}_{i-1}/K_{i-1}

is simple by the Primitive Element Theorem. Now relabel
K_{0},K_{1},\ldots
to absorb the newly inserted fields. By our construction
each
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is simple and either separable or purely inseparable.
The maximal value of
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})
does not increase by Lemma
3.1
(a).

(b) If
K_{1}/K_{0}
is purely inseparable, do nothing. If
K_{1}/K_{0}
is separable, replace
K_{1}
by its normal closure

K^{\rm norm}_{1}
and
K_{i}
by
K_{1}^{\rm norm}K_{i}
for each
i\geqslant 2
. All newly created extensions
K_{1}^{\rm norm}/K_{0}

and
K_{1}^{\rm norm}K_{i}/K_{1}^{\rm norm}K_{i-1}
(

), remain simple and either separable or purely inseparable.
Moreover, for every
i\geqslant 2
,
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K^{\rm norm}_{1}/K_{0})=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{1}/K_{0})
by Lemma

(b) and

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{1}^{\rm norm}K_{i}/K_{1}^{\rm norm}K_{i-1})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})
by Lemma

.

Now relabel
K_{0},K_{1},\ldots
and do the same for the extension
K_{2}/K_{1}
.
That is, if
K_{2}/K_{1}
is purely inseparable, then do nothing. If
K_{2}/K_{1}
is separable,
replace
K_{2}
by its normal closure
K^{\rm norm}_{2}
, and
K_{i}
by
K_{2}^{\rm norm}K_{i}
for every
i\geqslant 3
.
Proceed recursively: do the same thing for the extension
K_{3}/K_{2}
, then (after suitably modifying
K_{3},\ldots,K_{m}
)
for the extension
K_{4}/K_{3}
, etc. When all of these modifications are completed, the resulting tower

will have properties (a) and (b).

(c) If
K_{i}/K_{i-1}
is purely inseparable, do nothing. If it is Galois and

is simple, again do nothing.
If not - say if

has a proper normal subgroup

- insert

between

and

. By Lemma

(c),

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i}^{N})
and
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}^{N}/K_{i-1})
are both
\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})
. Thus the maximal value of
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(K_{i}/K_{i-1})

does not increase. Proceeding recursively, we arrive at a tower of field extensions satisfying (a), (b) and (c).
∎

5.
Extensions of valued fields

Throughout this section we will assume that

(1)
k\subset K\subset L
are fields and
[L:K]<\infty
,

(2)
K
and
L
are complete relative to a discrete valuation
\nu\colon L^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
.

(3) We will denote the residue fields of
k
,
K
and
L
by
k_{\nu}
,
K_{\nu}
and
L_{\nu}
, respectively.

Note that we do not require
k
to be complete.
Our goal is to compare
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)
to
\operatorname{lev}_{k_{\nu}}(L_{\nu}/K_{\nu})
. Our main result is as follows.

Proposition 5.1
.

In addition to notational convention (1) - (3), assume that the residue field
K_{\nu}
is perfect.
Then
\operatorname{lev}_{k_{\nu}}(L_{\nu}/K_{\nu})\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K),1\}
.

Our proof of Proposition
5.1
will rely on the following lemma comparing the essential dimensions of

L/K
and
L_{\nu}/K_{\nu}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

In addition to notational conventions (1), (2), (3), assume that

L/K
is a Galois extension,
H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
is a non-abelian finite simple group and

L_{\nu}
is separable over
K_{\nu}
.
Then
\operatorname{ed}_{k_{\nu}}(L_{\nu}/K_{\nu})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

First note that since
K
is complete,
\nu
is the unique valuation of
L
lying over
\nu_{|\,K^{*}}
;
see
[
32
, Theorem II.3.1(ii)]
.
Thus
\nu
remains invariant under the action of
H
, and this action descends to
L_{\nu}
.

Let
K_{\rm un}/K
be the largest unramified subextension of
L/K
. Then
I=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K_{\rm un})
is the inertia
subgroup of
H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, i.e., the kernel of the
H
-action on
L_{\nu}
.
In particular,
I
is normal in
H
, and
L_{\nu}/K_{\nu}
is an
(H/I)
-Galois extension; see
[
32
, Corollary III.5.1]
.
Since
H
is simple, there are only two possibilities: either

(i)
I=G
and
K_{\rm un}=K
, i.e.,
L/K
is totally ramified, or

(ii)
I=1
and
K_{\rm un}=L
, i.e.,
L/K
is unramified.

Let us consider these possibilities separately. Case (i) is straightforward. Here
L_{\nu}=K_{\nu}
, so that

\operatorname{ed}_{k_{\nu}}(L_{\nu}/K_{\nu})=0
and hence,
\operatorname{ed}_{k_{\nu}}(L_{\nu}/K_{\nu})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
, as desired.

In case (ii),
L_{\nu}/K_{\nu}
is an
H
-Galois extension.
Let
d=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(L/K)
. By definition,
L/K
descends to
L_{0}/K_{0}
, where
k\subset K_{0}\subset K
and
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})=d
.
By
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
we may assume that
L_{0}
is invariant under
H
. Recall that
L=L_{0}\otimes_{K_{0}}K
. Since
H
acts faithfully on
L

and trivially on
K
, it acts faithfully on
L_{0}
. The valuation
\nu
restricts to an
H
-invariant discrete valuation
on
L_{0}
, and the
H
-action on
L_{0}
descends to an
H
-action on the residue field
(L_{0})_{\nu}
.
Note however that a priori
L_{0}
and
K_{0}
may not be complete, and
L_{0}/K_{0}
may be ramified.

We claim that
H
acts faithfully of
(L_{0})_{\nu}
. Let us assume for a moment that this claim has been established.
Then
L_{\nu}/K_{\nu}
descends to
(L_{0})_{\nu}/(K_{0})_{\nu}
, where
(K_{0})_{\nu}
denotes the residue field of
K_{0}
.
Indeed, the image of the natural map
(L_{0})_{\nu}\otimes_{(K_{0}){\nu}}K_{\nu}\to L_{\nu}

is surjective by the Galois correspondence, and hence, is an isomorphism, because
[(L_{0})_{\nu}:(K_{0})_{\nu}]=|H|=[L_{\nu}:K_{\nu}]
.
We thus conclude, that

as desired. Here the second inequality follows from
[

, which is a special case of Abhyankar’s Lemma.

It remains to prove the claim. For each

, let

.
In particular,
L_{0}^{\geqslant 0}
is the valuation ring of
\nu
in
L_{0}
,
L_{0}^{\geqslant 1}
is the maximal ideal, and

L_{0}^{\geqslant 0}/L_{0}^{\geqslant 1}
is, by definition, the residue field
(L_{0})_{\nu}
. Let

be the kernel of the
H
-action on

.
Then

is a decreasing sequence of normal subgroups of

.
Since

is simple, each

is either all of

or

. Our goal is to show that

. Assume the contrary:

.
Consider two cases.

Case 1:
\operatorname{char}((L_{0})_{\nu})=0
. In this case

is a cyclic group; see
[
32
, Corollary IV.2.2]
or
[
5
, Lemma 2.2(a)]
. This contradicts our assumption that

is non-abelian.

Case 2:
\operatorname{char}((L_{0})_{\nu})=p>0

is of the form

, where

is a
p

is a cyclic group of

; see
[

, Corollary IV.2.4]

is simple and non-abelian.
This completes the proof of the claim and thus of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

6.
The level
d
closure of a field

Definition 6.1
.

Let
K
be a field containing
k

and
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
and
d\geqslant 1
be an integer. We define the level
d
closure
K^{(d)}

of
K
in
\overline{K}
to be the compositum of all intermediate extensions
K\subset L\subset\overline{K}
such that

[L:K]<\infty
and
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\leqslant d
.
Clearly
K^{(1)}\subset K^{(2)}\subset K^{(3)}\subset\ldots
. Up to isomorphism
(over
K
) the level
d
closure
K^{(d)}
depends only on
K
and not on the choice of
\overline{K}
. We will say that

K
is closed at level
d
if
K=K^{(d)}
, i.e., if
K
has no non-trivial extensions of level
\leqslant d
.

Remark 6.2
.

If
d=0
, then
K^{(0)}=\overline{k}K
, where
\overline{k}
denotes the algebraic closure of
k
and
the compositum is taken in
\overline{K}
. In particular,
K
is closed at level
0
if and only if
K
contains an algebraic closure of
k
.
This follows directly from Lemma
4.5
.

In the case, where
k
is an algebraically closed field and
K=k(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a purely transcendental extension,
Definition
6.1
appeared in the short note of Arnold and Shimura in
[
6
]
, pp. 45-46.
In this section we will prove the following properties of level

closure. It seems likely that Arnold and Shimura
had something like this in mind, through I have not encountered any explicit statements along these lines in the literature.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
k\subset K\subset E
be fields and
d\geqslant 0
be an integer.

(a) Consider an intermediate field
K\subset L\subset\overline{K}
such that
[L:K]<\infty
.
Then
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)\leqslant d
if and only if

.

(b)

. Moreover, if

is a finite extension of
K
and

, then equality holds,

K^{(d)}=E^{(d)}
.

(c)

, where the union is taken over
the intermediate fields

with

finitely generated over
K
.

(d)

for every

. In particular,
K^{(d)}
is closed at level
d
.

Our proof of Proposition

will rely on the following lemma.

7.
The resolvent degree of a functor

Following Merkurjev, Berhuy and Favi
[
1
]
, we will now define essential dimension for a broader class of objects, beyond finite
field extensions. Let
k
be a base field, and
\mathcal{F}\colon\operatorname{Fields}_{k}\to\operatorname{Sets}
be a functor from the category
of field extensions
K/k
to the category of sets. All functors in this paper will be assumed to be covariant.
We think of
\mathcal{F}
as specifying the type of object we are considering, and

\mathcal{F}(K)
as the set of objects of this type defined over
K
. Given a field extension
k\subset K\subset K^{\prime}
, we think of
the natural map
\mathcal{F}(K)\to\mathcal{F}(K^{\prime})
as base change. The image of
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
under this map will be denoted by
\alpha_{K^{\prime}}
.

Definition 7.1
.

Any object
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
in the image of the natural map
\mathcal{F}(K_{0})\to\mathcal{F}(K)
is
said to
descend
to
K_{0}
. The essential dimension
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(\alpha)
is defined as the minimal value of
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})
,
where the minimum is taken over all intermediate fields
k\subset K_{0}\subset K
such that
\alpha
descends to
K_{0}
.

Example 7.2
.

Consider the functor
\operatorname{Alg}\colon\operatorname{Fields}_{k}\to\operatorname{Sets}^{\prime}
, where

\operatorname{Alg}(K)
is the set of isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional
K
-algebras.
Here natural map
\operatorname{Alg}(K)\to\operatorname{Alg}(K^{\prime})
takes a
K
-algebra
A

to the
K^{\prime}
-algebra
K^{\prime}\otimes_{K}A
.
Let
K\in\operatorname{Fields}_{k}
and
A
be a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra.
If we view
A
as an object in
\mathcal{F}(K)
, then

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
given by Definition
7.1
is the same as
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A)
given by Definition
2.2
.

Now let
\mathcal{F}
be a functor from the category
\operatorname{Fields}_{k}
of field extensions
K/k
to the category
\operatorname{Sets}^{\prime}

of sets with a marked element. We will denote the marked element in
\mathcal{F}(K)
by
1
and will refer to it as being “split”.
We will say that a field extension
L/K
splits an object
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)

if
\alpha_{L}=1
. Let us assume that

(6)

for every field
K/k
and every
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
,
\alpha
can be

split by a field extension
L/K
of finite degree.

This is a strong condition on
\mathcal{F}
; in particular, it implies that

\mathcal{F}(K)=\{1\}
whenever
K
is algebraically closed.

Definition 7.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\colon\operatorname{Fields}_{k}\to\operatorname{Sets}^{\prime}
be a functor satisfying condition (
6
),
K/k
be a field extension and

\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
.

(a) The resolvent degree
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)
is the minimal integer
d\geqslant 0
such that
\alpha
is split by a field extension
L/K
of level
d

(or equivalently, of level
\leqslant d
).

(b) The resolvent degree
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F})
of the functor
\mathcal{F}
is the maximal value of
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)
, as
K
ranges over all fields containing
k
and
\alpha
ranges over
\mathcal{F}(K)
.

Remarks 7.4
.

(1) Note that the level
\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K)
plays a similar role in Definition
7.3
to the role played by the transcendence degree
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K_{0})

in Definition
7.1
.

(2) Condition (

) ensures that
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)
is finite for every
K\in\operatorname{Fields}_{k}
and every
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
. On the other hand,

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F})
can a priori be infinite, even though no examples where
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F})>1
are known.

Example 7.5
.

Consider the functor
\text{\'{E}t}_{n}\colon\operatorname{Fields}_{k}\to\operatorname{Sets}^{\prime}
, where
\text{\'{E}t}(K)
is the set of isomorphism

-dimensional étale algebras

. Recall that
an

,

.

(a) If
L/K
is a separable field extension of degree

Proof.

(a) By the Primitive Element Theorem,

On the other hand, by Lemma

(b) A field extension

Proposition 7.10
.

Let

be a diagonalizable group (i.e., a closed subgroup of the split torus

)

) and

First let us consider the special case, where

. Thus it suffices to show that every central simple algebra

over every

can be split by
a solvable extension of

is a prime power,

.
If

can be split by a solvable extension of

[

, Corollary 2.5.9]
. If

, then by a theorem of Albert,

is Brauer-equivalent to a cyclic algebra and thus can be split by a cyclic (and hence, once again, solvable) field extension of

.
This completes the proof in the case where

.

If

H^{2}(\ast,\mu_{n})\simeq{\,}_{n}\operatorname{Br}(K)
, where
{\,}{}_{n}\operatorname{Br}(K)

, and the same argument applies.

In general we write

, where each

is

-isomorphic to

or

∎

8.

In this section we will assume that our functor

Examples include Galois cohomology functors

Proposition 8.1

Assume that a functor

) and respects

(a)

(b)
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F})\leqslant d

\mathcal{F}(K)=1
for every field

.

(c) Suppose
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha_{K^{(d)}})\leqslant m

(d) Suppose
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\beta)\leqslant m

(a)
Suppose
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant d

\alpha
splits over a finite extension

.

(b) Suppose

Conversely, assume

be an arbitrary field containing

and

.

(c) Let

,

or

), then no field containing
\overline{k}
can be finitely generated over
k
. This tells us that
the truncated functor
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\overline{k}}
is the trivial functor and consequently,

\operatorname{rd}_{\overline{k}}\,(\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{\overline{k}})=0
. We conclude that
Proposition
8.3
(b) fails for
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
if
k^{\prime}=\overline{k}
.

(b) Set
m=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K)
and consider the truncated functor
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
, where

\Lambda=\{K/k\;\big|\;\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(K)\leqslant m\}.

The functor
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
continues to satisfy condition (
6
) and to respect direct limits.
If
\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(k^{\prime})>m
, then
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{k^{\prime}}
is trivial and
thus
\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda})=0
. On the other hand,
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\geqslant 1
whether we view
\alpha
as an object in
\mathcal{F}
or

\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
. In summary,
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda})\geqslant 1
,
\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}_{k^{\prime}})=0
, and
Proposition
8.3
(b) fails for
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
.
∎

9.
Change of base field

As we saw in Remark
8.5
(b), Proposition
8.3
(b) fails if
k^{\prime}
is not assumed to be algebraic over
k
.
In this section we will show that under an additional condition on the functor
\mathcal{F}
, the equality of
Proposition
8.3
(b) can be (largely) salvaged for an arbitrary field
extension
k^{\prime}/k
. The condition we will impose on
\mathcal{F}
is as follows:

(10)

the natural map
\mathcal{F}(E)\to\mathcal{F}\big(E((t))\big)
has trivial

kernel for every perfect field
E
containing
k
.

Note that this is only slightly weaker than Condition (*) considered by Merkurjev in
[
25
, Section 3]
. The only difference between the two
is that in
[
25
]
,
E
is not required to be perfect. As is pointed out in
[
25
]
, this is a natural condition,
which is often satisfied.

Proposition 9.1
.

Assume that a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\operatorname{Fields}_{k}\to\operatorname{Sets}^{\prime}
satisfies conditions (
6
) and (
10
)
and respects direct limits. Then

\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}})\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F})\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}),\,1\}

for any field extension
k^{\prime}/k
.

The remainder of this section will be devoted to proving Proposition
9.1
.
We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 9.2
.

Assume that a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\operatorname{Fields}_{k}\to\operatorname{Sets}^{\prime}
satisfies condition (
6
) and
respects direct limits. Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a field extension,
K\in\operatorname{Fields}_{k}
and

\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
.
Then there exists an intermediate field
k\subset l\subset k^{\prime}
such that

l
is finitely generated over
k
and
\operatorname{rd}_{l}(\alpha_{lK})=\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K})
.
Here
k^{\prime}K
is some compositum of
k^{\prime}
and
K
over
k
. The compositum
lK
is taken in
k^{\prime}K
.

Proof.

For any intermediate field
k\subset l\subset k^{\prime}
, we have

\operatorname{rd}_{l}(\alpha_{lK})\geqslant\operatorname{rd}_{l}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K})\geqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K});

see (
7
). Our goal is to show that the opposite inequality holds for a suitably
chosen intermediate field
k\subset l\subset k^{\prime}
, where
l
is finitely generated over
k
.

Set
d=\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K})
. By Lemma
7.7
(c) there exists an intermediate extension

k\subset l_{0}\subset k^{\prime}
such that
l_{0}
is finitely generated over
k
,
and
d=\operatorname{rd}_{l}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K})
for any intermediate field
l_{0}\subset l\subset k^{\prime}
.
After replacing
k
by
l_{0}
and
\alpha
by
\alpha_{l_{0}K}
, we may assume without loss of generality
that
k=l_{0}
. In particular,
d=\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K})
. By Proposition
8.1
(a),
\alpha
splits over
(k^{\prime}K)^{(d)}
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
preserves direct limits,
\alpha
splits over
L^{(d)}
for some intermediate extension

K\subset L\subset(k^{\prime}K)
such that
L
is finitely generated over
K
. Any such
L
is contained in
lK
for
some intermediate field
k\subset l\subset k^{\prime}
, where
l
is finitely generated over
k
. Thus
\alpha
splits over
(lK)^{(d)}
.
By Proposition
8.1
(a) this implies that
\operatorname{rd}_{l}(\alpha_{lK})\leqslant d
, as claimed.
∎

Proof of Proposition
9.1
.

The first inequality
\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}})\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F})
is proved in Proposition
8.3
(a).
We will thus focus on proving the second inequality.
Let
K
be a field containing
k
and

\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
.
Our goal is to show that

(11)

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K}),\,1\}.

If we can prove this, then taking the maximum over all
K\in\operatorname{Fields}_{k}
and all
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(K)
,
we will obtain the desired inequality
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathcal{F})\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}),\,1\}
.

We begin by reducing to the case where
k^{\prime}
is finitely generated over
k
.
Indeed, choose
l
as in Lemma
9.2
. That is,
l
is finitely generated over
k
and

\operatorname{rd}_{l}(\alpha_{lK})=\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K})
. For the purpose of
proving (
11
) we may now replace
k^{\prime}
by
l
.

From now on we will assume
k^{\prime}
is finitely generated over
k
.
Choose a transcendence basis
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
for
k^{\prime}/k
and set

k_{i}=k(t_{1},\ldots,t_{i})
, so that
k^{\prime}
is algebraic over
k_{n}
. By (
8
),

\operatorname{rd}_{k_{n}}(\alpha_{k_{n}K})=\operatorname{rd}_{k_{n}}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K})=\operatorname{rd}_{k^{\prime}}(\alpha_{k^{\prime}K}).

Thus we may further replace
k^{\prime}
by
k_{n}
. It remains to show that

(12)

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k(t)}(\alpha_{K(t)}),\,1\},

where
t
is a variable. Indeed, applying this inequality recursively, we readily deduce (
11
):

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k_{1}}(\alpha_{k_{1}K}),\,1\}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k_{n}}(\alpha_{k_{n}K}),\,1\}.

(Recall that here
k^{\prime}=k_{n}
.)

The remainder of the proof will be devoted to establishing the inequality (
12
). First observe that we
may assume without loss of generality that
K
is closed at level
1
.
Indeed, let
K^{(1)}
be the level
1
closure of
K
. By Proposition
8.1
(c),

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha_{K^{(1)}}),\,1\}

and by Lemma
7.6
(a),

\operatorname{rd}_{k(t)}(\alpha_{K^{(1)}(t)})\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k(t)}(\alpha_{K(t)}),

where
K^{(1)}
is the level
1
closure of
K
. These inequalities show
that in the course of proving (
12
), we may replace
K
by
K^{(1)}
and
\alpha
by
\alpha_{K^{(1)}}
.
In other words, for the purpose of proving (
12
), we may assume that
K
is closed at level
1
. In particular, we may assume that

K
is a perfect field; see Corollary
6.5
.

We now proceed with the proof of (
12
) under the assumption that
K
is a perfect field.
First we observe that by Lemma
7.6
(a),
\operatorname{rd}_{k(t)}(\alpha_{K(t)})\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k(t)}(\alpha_{K((t))})
.
Thus we only need to show that

(13)

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k(t)}(\alpha_{K((t))}),\,1\},

Set
d=\operatorname{rd}_{k(t)}(\alpha_{K((t))})
. By definition there exists
a finite field extension
L/K((t))
such that
\alpha_{L}=1
and
\operatorname{lev}_{k(t)}\big(L/K((t))\big)=d
.

The field
K((t))
carries a natural discrete valuation
\nu\colon K((t))^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}

with uniformizer
t
, trivial on
K
. Lift
\nu
to a discrete
valuation
L^{*}\to\dfrac{1}{e}\mathbb{Z}
, where
e
is the ramification index.
By abuse of notation I will continue to denote this lifted valuation by
\nu
.
I will denote the residue field of
L
relative to this valuation by
L_{\nu}
.

Note that since
K((t))
is complete with respect to
\nu
, so is
L
.
Moreover, since
K
is perfect, so is
L_{\nu}
.
Note also that we are in equal characteristic situation here:

\operatorname{char}(L_{\nu})=\operatorname{char}(K)=\operatorname{char}(k)=\operatorname{char}(k(t))=\operatorname{char}(K((t)))=\operatorname{char}(L).

By the Cohen Structure Theorem,
L
is isomorphic to the field of Laurent series
{L}_{\nu}((s))

in one variable over
L_{\nu}
.
Since
\alpha_{L}=1
and
L_{\nu}
is perfect, the natural map

\mathcal{F}({L}_{\nu})\to\mathcal{F}(L_{\nu}((s)))=\mathcal{F}({L})

has trivial kernel, by our assumption (

).
We conclude that
\alpha_{L_{\nu}}=1
. In other words,
{L}_{\nu}/K
is a splitting extension for
\alpha
.
By Proposition
5.1
,

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L_{\nu}/K)\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K((t))),\,1\}=\max\{d,\,1\},

where

is the residue field of

.
Proposition

applies here because we are assuming that the residue field

is perfect. This completes the proof of Proposition

.
∎

10.
The resolvent degree of an algebraic group

Let
G
be an algebraic group over
k
, not necessarily affine, smooth or connected.
Of particular interest to us will be the functor
H^{1}(*,G)
whose objects over
K
are isomorphism classes
of
G
-torsors over
\operatorname{Spec}(K)
. Here torsors are assumed to be locally trivial in the flat (fppf) topology. If
G
is
smooth over
k
, this is equivalent to being trivial in the étale topology.
For every field
K
containing
k
, the set
H^{1}(K,G)
has a marked element, represented by the split
G
-torsor
G_{K}\to\operatorname{Spec}(K)
,
where
G_{K}=G\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}\operatorname{Spec}(K)
.
The functor
H^{1}(\ast,G)
satisfies condition (
6
).

Definition 10.1
.

We define
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H^{1}(\ast,G))
and

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H^{1}(\ast,G))
.

The essential dimension
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)
of an algebraic group
G/k
has been much studied; see
[
26
,
27
]
.
If
G
is an abstract finite group (viewed as an algebraic group over
k
)
and
\operatorname{char}(k)=0
, our definition of
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
above coincides with the definition given by Farb and Wolfson
[
16
]
.
To the best of my knowledge,
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
has not been previously investigated for other algebraic groups
G/k
.

In view of Proposition
8.3
(b), passing from
G
to
G_{\overline{k}}

does not change the resolvent degree. Thus from now on we will assume that
k
is algebraically closed.

Example 10.2
.

The functor
\text{\'{E}t}_{n}
introduced in Example
7.5

is isomorphic to
H^{1}(\ast,\operatorname{S}_{n})
and thus
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\text{\'{E}t}_{n})=\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\operatorname{S}_{n})
. By Example
7.5
,

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\operatorname{S}_{n})=\max\,\operatorname{lev}_{k}(L/K).

The algebraic form of Hilbert’s 13th Problem asks for the value of
\operatorname{rd}(n)=\operatorname{rd}_{\mathbb{C}}(\operatorname{S}_{n})
.

Remark 10.3
.

Recall that the classical definition of
\operatorname{rd}(n)
is motivated by
wanting to express a root of a general polynomial
f(x)=x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{n}
as a composition
of algebraic functions in
\leqslant d
variables. This is equivalent to finding the smallest integer
d
such
that the
0
-cycle in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
given by
f(x)=0
has an
L
-point,
for some field extension
L/K
of level
\leqslant d
. If
G
is an algebraic group over
k
,
K
is a field containing
k

and
T\to\operatorname{Spec}(K)
is a
G
-torsor, then our definition of
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(T)
retains this flavor.
Indeed, saying that
T
is split by
L
is equivalent to saying that
T
has an
L
-point.

Remark 10.4
.

(cf.
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
)
Let
G
be an algebraic group defined over a field
k
,
K
be a field containing
k
, and

\alpha\colon T\to\operatorname{Spec}(K)
be a
G
-torsor. Setting
\mathcal{F}=H^{1}(\ast,G)
in Lemma
7.6
, we obtain
the inequalities
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(\alpha)
and
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)
.

Remark 10.5
.

(cf.
[
16
, Lemma 3.13]
)
Let
G
be a finite group and
H
be a subgroup. We will view
G
and
H
as algebraic groups over
k
.
The long exact sequence in Galois cohomology associated to
1\longrightarrow H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}G

(see
[
34
, Section I.5.4]
) readily show that the induced morphism

i_{*}\colon H^{1}(\ast,H)\to H^{1}(\ast,G)
has trivial kernel. By Lemma
7.9
(b), we conclude that

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(H)\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)
.

Example 10.6
.

(cf.
[
16
, Corollary 3.4]
)
If
G
is a solvable finite group, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 1
. Indeed, every element of
H^{1}(K,G)
can be split by a solvable extension
L/K
, and a solvable extension has level
\leqslant 1
by Lemma
4.6
(a).
Moreover, if we further assume that
G\neq 1
, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)=1
. This follows from Lemma
7.8
and Proposition
8.3
(b).

Recall that an algebraic group
G
defined over a field
k
is called special if
H^{1}(\ast,G)
is the trivial functor, i.e.,

H^{1}(K,G)=1
for every field
K
containing
k
. This notion
is due to Serre
[
31
]
. (Note that
[
31
]
is reprinted in
[
35
]
.)

Lemma 10.7
.

(a) Let
G
be an algebraic group over an algebraically closed field
k
. Then

(a)
G
is special if and only if
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)=0
.

(b) If
G
is connected and solvable, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)=0
.

(c) If
G
is the special linear group
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
or the symplectic group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)=0
for any

.

(a) By Lemma
7.8
,
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)=0
if and only if
H^{1}(\ast,G)
is the trivial functor, i.e., if and only
if

is special.

(b) Every connected solvable group is special; see
[

, Section 4.4(a)]
.

(c)
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
are special; see
[

.
∎

We now record several simple but useful observations about the behavior of resolvent degree in exact sequences of groups.

Proposition 10.8
.

Consider a short exact sequence of algebraic groups

(14)

1\to A\to B\to C\to 1

defined over a field
k
. Then

(a)
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(B)\leqslant\max\,\{\operatorname{rd}_{k}(A),\,\operatorname{rd}_{k}(C)\}
.

(b) If
B
is isomorphic to the direct product
A\times C
, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(B)=\max\,\{\operatorname{rd}_{k}(A),\,\operatorname{rd}_{k}(C)\}
.

(c) If
G
is a diagonalizable algebraic group over
k
, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 1
.

(d) Suppose (
14
) is a central short exact sequence and
A
is diagonalizable over
k
.
Then

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(B)\leqslant\max\,\{\operatorname{rd}_{k}(C),1\}
and
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(C)\leqslant\max\,\{\operatorname{rd}_{k}(B),1\}
.

Proof.

(a) follows from Lemma
7.9
(a) applied to the exact sequence of functors

H^{1}(\ast,A)\to H^{1}(\ast,B)\to H^{1}(\ast,C)

induced by (
14
).

(b) follows from Lemma
7.9
(d), since in this case
the functor
H^{1}(\ast,B)
is isomorphic to
H^{1}(\ast,A)\times H^{1}(\ast,C)
.

(c) Write
G
as a product
G_{1}\times\ldots\times G_{r}
, where each
G_{i}
is isomorphic
either to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
or
\mu_{n}
for some
n\geqslant 2
. By part (b), it suffices to show that
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{i})\leqslant 1

for each
i
. Now recall that
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
by Lemma
10.7
(b). On the other hand,

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\mu_{n})\leqslant\operatorname{ed}_{k}(\mu_{n})
by Remark
10.4
, and
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(\mu_{n})=1
for every
n\geqslant 2
; see,
e.g.,
[
26
, Example 3.5]
.

(d) To prove the first inequality, combine parts (a) and (c).
The second inequality follows from Lemma
7.9
(a) applied to the exact sequences of functors

H^{1}(\ast,B)\to H^{1}(\ast,C)\to H^{2}(\ast,A)

induced by (
14
). Recall that
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(H^{2}(*,A))\leqslant 1
by Proposition
7.10
.
∎

Corollary 10.9
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive affine algebraic group,
T
be a split maximal torus of
G
,

N
be the normalizer of
T
in
G
, and
W=N/T
be the Weyl group. Then

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(W)\geqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(N)\geqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G).

Proof.

By
[
10
, Corollary 5.3]

the natural morphism
H^{1}(K,N)\to H^{1}(K,G)
is surjective;
see also
[
34
, Lemma III.4.3.6]
.
Hence,
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(N)=\operatorname{rd}(H^{1}(\ast,N))\geqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(H^{1}(\ast,G))=\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)

by Lemma
7.9
(c).

The inequality
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(W)\geqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(N)
follows from Proposition
10.8
(a)
applied to the exact sequence
1\to T\to N\to W\to 1
. Note that by Lemma
10.7
(b),

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(T)=0
.
∎

11.
The resolvent degree of an abelian variety

In this section we will assume that the base field
k
is algebraically closed.
This assumption is harmless in view of Proposition
8.3
(b).

Recall that for every algebraic group
G
defined over
k
, there exists a smooth (i.e., reduced)
subgroup
G_{\rm red}
such that
G(k)=G_{\rm red}(k)
; see
[
18
, Exp.VI
A
, Section 0.2]
.

Lemma 11.1
.

Let
K
be a field containing
k
and let
i\colon G_{\rm red}\hookrightarrow G

be the natural inclusion and
i_{*}\colon H^{1}(K,G_{\rm red})\to H^{1}(K,G)
be the induced map in cohomology. Then

(a)
i_{*}
is injective.

(b) If
K
is a perfect field, then
i_{*}
is bijective.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in H^{1}(K,G)
.
By
[
34
, I.5.4, Corollary 2]
, the fiber of
(i_{*})^{-1}(\gamma)
may be identified with the set of orbits of

{\,}{}_{\gamma}G(K)
in
({\,}_{\gamma}G/{\,}_{\gamma}G_{\rm red})(K)
.
2
2

2

In
[
34
]

only étale cohomology is considered. The same argument works for flat cohomology.

Here
{\,}{}_{\gamma}G
denotes the twist of
G
by a cocycle representing
\gamma
, and similarly for
G_{\rm red}
.
Since the homogeneous space
G/G_{\rm red}
is purely inseparable over
\operatorname{Spec}(k)
,
the homogeneous space
{\,}{}_{\gamma}G/{\,}_{\gamma}G_{\rm red}

is purely inseparable over
\operatorname{Spec}(K)
. (To see this, pass to a splitting field of
\gamma
.)
Thus
({\,}_{\gamma}G/{\,}_{\gamma}G_{\rm red})

can have at most one
\operatorname{Spec}(K)
-point. This shows that the fiber of
(i_{*})^{-1}(\gamma)
has at most one element, proving (a).
If
K
is perfect, then
{\,}{}_{\gamma}G(K)
in
({\,}_{\gamma}G/{\,}_{\gamma}G_{\rm red})
has exactly one
K
-point. In this case
the fiber of
(i_{*})^{-1}(\gamma)
has exactly one element for every
\gamma\in H^{1}(K,G_{\rm red})
. This proves (b).
∎

Recall that an infinitesimal group is a connected
0
-dimensional group. Non-trivial infinitesimal groups exist only in prime characteristic.

Proposition 11.2
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group over
k
.
Then

(a)
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant\max\{\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{\rm red}),1\}
.

(b) If
G
be an infinitesimal group over
k
, then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 1
.

(c) Let
G
be a
0
-dimensional abelian group over
k
(not necessarily smooth or connected). Then
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 1
.

Proof.

(a) In view of Proposition
8.1
(d), it suffices to show that
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{\rm red})

for every field
K/k
such that
K
is closed at level
1
and every
\alpha\in H^{1}(K,G)
. Indeed, every such field
K
is perfect;
see Corollary
6.5
. Thus by Lemma
11.1
,

\alpha
is the image of some
\beta\in H^{1}(K,G_{\rm red})
.
Every field extension of
K
which splits
\beta
also splits
\alpha
. This tells us that

\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\alpha)\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(\beta)\leqslant\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{\rm red}),

as claimed.

(b) If
G
is infinitesimal, then
G_{\rm red}=1
. Thus
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G_{\rm red})=0
, and
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G)\leqslant 1
by part (a).

(c) Consider the exact sequence

1\to G^{0}\to G\to G/G^{0}\to 1
.
The group
G^{0}
is infinitesimal; thus
\operatorname{rd}_{k}(G^{0})\leqslant 1

by part (b). On the other hand, by
[
18
, Exp. VI
A
, Proposition 5.5.1]
,
G/G^{0}
is étale. Since
k
is algebraically closed,
this tells us that
G/G^{0}
is constant, i.e., is isomorphic to an abstract finite abelian group, viewed as an algebraic group over
k

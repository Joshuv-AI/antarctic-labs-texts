Quantitative comparison results for first-order Hamilton-Jacobi equations
Vincenzo Amato, Luca Barbato

Abstract
In this paper, we study a quantitative refinement of a classical symmetrisation result for first-order Hamilton–Jacobi equations. We prove that the deficit in the comparison result, established by Giarrusso and Nunziante, controls both the asymmetry of the domain and the deviation of the solution and data from radial symmetry. This yields a stability version of the Giarrusso–Nunziante inequality. Keywords: Hamilton-Jacobi equations, stability, comparison results. MSC 2020: 35F21, 35B35, 35B51.



1
Introduction

In this work, we address a classical question in shape optimisation: how the shape of a domain influences the solutions to certain partial differential equations. We focus on first-order Hamilton-Jacobi equations, a class of PDEs that arise in a wide range of applications, including control theory, differential geometry, and classical mechanics.

Our main tool is symmetrisation: the process of comparing a given function (or domain) with a more symmetric one, typically a ball, and analysing how this affects the solutions of the equation.

More precisely, given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a non-negative function
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
defined on it, we consider the equation

|\nabla u|=f(x)\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

(1)

We then study its symmetrised counterpart, where the domain
\Omega
is replaced by a ball
\Omega^{\sharp}
(with the same volume as
\Omega
), and the function
f
is replaced by its increasing rearrangement
f_{\sharp}
(see Section
2
for its definition). The unique radially symmetric and decreasing solution to this symmetrised problem is denoted by
u_{G}
.

A classical result by Giarrusso and Nunziante,
[
22
]
, shows that the
L^{1}
-norm of the original solution
u
is always less than or equal to that of the symmetric solution
u_{G}
:

\big\lVert u\big\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\big\lVert u^{\text{G}}\big\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}.

(2)

For completeness, we recall that a (
generalized
) solution

u
to (
1
) is any function
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
p\geq 1
, satisfying
{\left|\nabla u\right|}=f(x)
almost everywhere in
\Omega
.

However, uniqueness is not guaranteed: for instance, in the case
\Omega=[0,1]
, and
f(x)=1
, infinitely many solutions exist. To ensure uniqueness, one may refer to the theory of viscosity solutions (see
[
33
]
); however, for the purposes of
[
22
]
and of this paper, the existence of at least one such

u
is sufficient, as every solution satisfies (
2
).

An extension of this result to more general equations, involving terms such as
f(x)-\lambda u
, was given in
[
23
]
.

A natural question is whether other norms (besides the
L^{1}
-norm) of the solutions to (
1
) and the symmetrised version can also be compared. In
[
1
,
15
]
, the authors provide a rearrangement of the gradient of
u
that allows such comparisons in the
L^{q}
-norm. A further characterisation of these rearrangements appears in
[
10
]
, where it is shown that they coincide with the increasing rearrangement for
q=1
and the decreasing one for
q=\infty
.

In
[
7
,
30
]
, the rigidity of inequality (
2
) is examined. In particular, the authors show that if equality holds in (
2
), then
\Omega
, up to translation, must be a ball, and both
u
and
f
must be radially symmetric.

This naturally raises the question: can inequality (
2
) be quantified? More precisely, if the deficit

{\big\lVert u^{\text{G}}\big\rVert}_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}-{\big\lVert u\big\rVert}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

(3)

is small, does this imply that
\Omega
is close to a ball, and that
u
and
f
are approximately radially symmetric?

To measure how far a domain
\Omega
is from being a ball, we use the Fraenkel asymmetry (see
[
20
]
):

\alpha(\Omega):=\min_{x\in\R^{n}}\bigg\{\dfrac{|\Omega\triangle B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}\;,\;|B_{r}(x)|=|\Omega|\bigg\},

where
\triangle
denotes the symmetric difference.

Measuring how far a function is from being radial is more subtle. In related works on quantitative versions of the Pólya–Szegő, Hardy–Littlewood, and Talenti inequalities (see
[
3
,
11
,
12
]
), the distance of a function
g
from radial symmetry is often measured by the
L^{p}
-norm of the difference between
g
and its decreasing rearrangement:

\lVert g-g^{\sharp}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
g
is extended by zero outside
\Omega
. We adopt this convention as well.

Notation and preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
|\Omega|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
P(E,\Omega)
denotes the perimeter of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
\Omega
, defined by

P(E,\Omega):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}\mathrm{div}\,\varphi\,dx:\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),\ \|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

We write
P(E):=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the total perimeter of
E
.

For every function
f\in W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
coarea formula
holds, see for instance
[
6
,
18
,
29
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Coarea formula)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. Let
f\in W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function. Then,

{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(x)|\nabla f(x)|dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\int_{\Omega\cap f^{-1}(t)}u(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)}.

(8)

Moreover, if
f\in\text{BV}(\Omega)
, then the Fleming-Rishel formula holds, i.e.

{\left|Df\right|}(\Omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P(\Set{f>t},\Omega)\,dt.

(9)

In the following, we discuss rearrangements of functions, emphasizing both their qualitative and quantitative aspects. For convenience in the proofs, we also outline several rescaling properties.

2.1
Distribution function and rearrangements

In this subsection, we introduce several definitions and properties related to function rearrangements. For a general overview of the basic results in this area, see, for instance,
[
27
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
g
be a non-negative measurable function on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
distribution function
of
g
is defined as

\mu_{g}(t):=|\{x\in\Omega:g(x)>t\}|,\qquad t\geq 0.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
g:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
decreasing rearrangement
of
g
, denoted by
g^{\ast}(\cdot)
, is defined as

g^{*}(s)=\inf\{t\geq 0:\mu_{g}(t)<s\}.

and the
increasing rearrangement
of
g
, denoted by
g_{\ast}
, is defined as

g_{*}\colon[0,{\left|\Omega\right|}]\to\R^{+}\qquad g_{*}(s)=g^{*}({\left|\Omega\right|}-s)

Definition 2.3
.

The
spherically decreasing rearrangement
(or
Schwarz symmetrisation
) of
g
is the function
g^{\sharp}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
whose superlevel sets are centred balls having the same measure as those of
g
. Equivalently,

g^{\sharp}(x):=g^{*}(\omega_{n}|x|^{n}),

where
\omega_{n}
denotes the measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The
spherically increasing rearrangement
of
g
is the function
g_{\sharp}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
whose superlevel sets are centred annuli having the same measure as those of
g
. Equivalently,

g_{\sharp}(x):=g_{*}(\omega_{n}|x|^{n}).

These rearrangements are all
equimeasurable
, i.e. they preserve the distribution function of
g
, hence, by the Cavalieri principle, it holds

\displaystyle{{\left\|g\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}={\left\|g^{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}[0,{\left|\Omega\right|}]}=\lVert{g^{\sharp}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}={\left\|g_{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}[0,{\left|\Omega\right|}]}=\lVert{g_{\sharp}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}},\quad\text{for all }p\geq 1.

We summarize below some classical results on function rearrangements: the condition ensuring the absolute continuity of
\mu_{g}
and two fundamental inequalities (see, for instance,
[
9
,
13
,
26
,
27
,
31
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g\in W^{1,p}(\R^{n})
, with
p\in(1,+\infty)
.
The following results hold:

1.

(Absolute continuity of
\mu_{g}
)

the distribution function
\mu_{g}
of
g
is absolutely continuous if and only if

{\left|\{0<g^{\sharp}<||g||_{\infty}\}\cap\{|\nabla g^{\sharp}|=0\}\right|}=0.

2.

(Hardy–Littlewood inequality)

if
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then

\int_{\Omega}{\left|h(x)g(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}h^{*}(s)g^{*}(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}h_{*}(s)g_{*}(s)\,ds.

(10)

3.

(Pólya–Szegő inequality)

if
g\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
g^{\sharp}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\sharp})
and

\lVert\nabla g^{\sharp}\rVert_{p}\leq{\left\|\nabla g\right\|}_{p}.

The inequality also holds for
p=\infty
, that is,

\|\nabla g^{\sharp}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\nabla g\|_{\infty}.

2.2
Pseudo-rearrangements

We now introduce the notion of pseudo-rearrangements, which generalizes classical rearrangements and was first studied in
[
2
]
.
These constructions are useful when one wants to compare integrals over level sets of different functions.

Definition 2.4

(Pseudo-rearrangement)
.

Let
g
be a function in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
\forall s\in[0,|\Omega|]
we can find a subset
D(s)
such that:

1.

|D(s)|=s
;

2.

s_{1}\leq s_{2}\implies D(s_{1})\subset D(s_{2})
;

3.

D(s)=\{x\in\Omega:\,|g(x)|>t\}
if
s=\mu_{g}(t)
.

Then, for any
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, the function

\int_{D(s)}f(x)\,dx

is an absolutely continuous function of
s
, so there exists a function
F
, that we call
pseudo-rearrangement
of
f
with respect to
g
, such that

\int_{0}^{s}F(t)\,dt=\int_{D(s)}f(x)\,dx.

The following lemma describes a fundamental property of this rearrangement.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega),p>1
. There exists a sequence
\{F_{k}(s)\}
of functions which have the same rearrangement
f^{*}
of
f
, such that

F_{k}(s)\rightharpoonup F(s)\quad\text{in }L^{p}(0,|\Omega|)

If
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, it follows that:

\lim_{k}\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}F_{k}(s)g(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}F(s)g(s)\,ds

for every function
g
belonging to the space
BV(0,|\Omega|)
.

In the special case where
g^{*}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,|\Omega|)
, the rearrangement can be constructed as in
[
12
]
, where further details are provided.

Definition 2.5
.

Assume that
g^{\ast}
is strictly decreasing in
(0,{\left|\Omega\right|})
. We define
f_{g}:\Omega\to[0,+\infty)
as

f_{g}(x)=f^{\ast}(\mu_{g}(g(x)))\qquad\forall x\in\Omega.

For every
t>0
, one easily verifies that

\Set{x\in\Omega:\,f_{g}(x)>t}=\Set{x\in\Omega:{\left|g(x)\right|}>g^{\ast}(\mu_{f}(t))},

(11)

up to a set of measure zero, and that

(f_{g})^{\ast}(s)=f^{\ast}(s)\qquad a.e.\,s\in(0,{\left|\Omega\right|}).

Remark 2.1
.

Recalling that equality in the Hardy–Littlewood inequality holds when condition (
11
) is satisfied, we obtain:

\int_{\Omega}f_{g}(x){\left|g(x)\right|}\,dt=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{\ast}(s)g^{\ast}(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f_{\ast}(s)g_{\ast}(s)\,ds.

2.3
Rearrangement of the gradient

This section recalls classical results on the rearrangement of the gradient, which are instrumental in deriving quantitative versions of (

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set of
\R^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and let
\Omega^{\sharp}
be the centered ball.

Let
f\colon\Omega\times\R\to\R
be a measurable function with

, and let

be the increasing rearrangement of

. Let

be a measurable, non-negative function and let

Let

be a solution to

\begin{cases}H(\nabla v)=f(x,u)&\text{a.e. in }\Omega\\

and let

\begin{cases}K({\left|\nabla z\right|})=f_{\sharp}(x,0)&\text{a.e. in }\Omega^{\sharp}\\

then, it holds

{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|z\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}.

Moreover, the following rigidity result is proved in
[
7
,
30
]
in the case
H(\cdot)=K(\cdot)=|\cdot|
and

:

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded open set, and let

be a non-negative function. Denote by

and by

the spherically decreasing solution to

{\left|\nabla z\right|}(x)=f_{\sharp}(x)\quad\text{in }\Omega^{\sharp}.

If
{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{1}}={\left\|z\right\|}_{L^{1}}
then there exists

2.4
Some quantitative inequalities

To prove our main theorem, we first recall several quantitative results that will be used throughout the article.
The starting point is the quantitative isoperimetric inequality, established in
[
21
]
(see also
[
14
,
19
,
24
]
).

We recall that the
Fraenkel asymmetry
, denoted by
\alpha(\Omega)
, is a scaling-invariant functional depending only on the shape of
\Omega
. It takes values in
[0,2]
and will serve as our asymmetry index: it is zero if and only if
\Omega
is a ball. Hence, the sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality can be stated as follows:

Theorem 2.6

(Quantitative isoperimetric inequality)
.

There exists a constant
\gamma_{n}
such that, for any measurable set
\Omega
of finite measure

P(\Omega)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(1+\dfrac{\alpha^{2}(\Omega)}{\gamma_{n}}\right).

(12)

The dimensional constant
\gamma_{n}
is explicitly computed in
[
17
]
.

When analyzing the asymmetry of our domain in Section
3
, we will apply the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (
12
) to the superlevel sets of the modulus of a solution to (
4a
).
The main difficulty lies in estimating how the asymmetry changes from the whole domain to the superlevel sets, for which we use the following result (see also
[
8
, Lemma 2.8]
).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be an open set with finite measure and

a subset of positive measure such that

\frac{|\Omega\backslash U|}{|\Omega|}\leq\frac{1}{4}\alpha(\Omega)

then holds

\alpha(U)\geq\frac{1}{2}\alpha(\Omega)

Another key ingredient is the quantitative version of Pólya-Szegő
inequality, proved in
[
11
]

Theorem 2.8

(Quantitative Pólya-Szegő
inequality)
.

Let

,

. Then there exist positive constants

,

and

, depending only on

, such that

\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}\dfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{\R^{n}}{\left|u(x)\pm u^{\sharp}(x+x_{0})\right|}\,dx}}{{\left|\{{\left|u\right|}>0\}\right|}^{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|\nabla u^{\sharp}\right\|}_{2}}\leq C(n)\left[M_{u}(E(u)^{r})+E(u)\right]^{s},

where

E(u)=\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{\R^{n}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{2}}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\R^{n}}|\nabla u^{\sharp}|^{2}}}-1\qquad\text{ and }\qquad M_{u}(\delta)=\dfrac{{\left|\left\{|\nabla u|<\delta\right\}\cap\left\{0<{\left|u\right|}<||u||_{\infty}\right\}\right|}}{{\left|\{{\left|u\right|}>0\}\right|}}.

Finally, the main tool to obtain (

Let

\int_{\Omega}h(x)g(x)\;dx+\dfrac{1}{2^{2}e}{\left\|g\right\|}^{-1}_{\Lambda^{1}_{1}(\Omega)}{\left\|g-g_{h}\right\|}^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}h^{\ast}(s)g^{\ast}(s)\;ds.

2.5
Rescaling properties

To simplify the proofs, we make the following two assumptions:

\displaystyle{\left|\Omega\right|}=1,\qquad\qquad{\left\|f\right\|}_{\infty}=1.

Even in the general case, we can recover this assumption by setting

a=\dfrac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{-\frac{1}{n}}}{{\left\|f\right\|}_{\infty}},\quad\quad b={\left|\Omega\right|}^{-\frac{1}{n}}.

If

z=0&\text{on }\partial\tilde{\Omega}^{\sharp},\end{cases}

with

{\left\|g\right\|}_{\infty}=1

Furthermore, the following holds

\displaystyle\alpha(\tilde{\Omega})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\Omega);

\displaystyle{\left\|z\right\|}_{1}-{\left\|w\right\|}_{1}

Thanks to the rescaling properties in Subsection

Our first step in proving Theorem

In

The validity of the pointwise inequality is way stronger than the

to

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set of
\R^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and let

be a non-negative function. Suppose that

is a solution to (

). Then, it holds

{\big\lVert g\big\rVert}_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}-{\big\lVert u\big\rVert}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\geq s_{\Omega}\frac{\alpha(\Omega)^{2}}{8\gamma_{n}},

where
\gamma_{n}
is the constant appearing in Theorem
2.6
and
g
is the function defined in (

).

Proof.

Firstly, we observe that, since

is spherically decreasing, we have the following explicit expression

g(s)=\int_{s}^{|\Omega|}\frac{F(t)}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}dt.

By Fleming-Rishel Formula (
9
) and the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (

), we obtain

-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\left|u\right|}>t}|\nabla u|dx=P({\left|u\right|}>t)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu_{u}(t)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha({{\left|u\right|}>t})^{2}}{\gamma_{n}}\right),

(15)

and since

is a solution to (
4a
), we have

-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\left|u\right|}>t}|\nabla u|dx=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\left|u\right|}>t}f(x)dx=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\mu_{u}(t)}F(\tau)d\tau=F(\mu_{u}(t))(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t)).

(16)

By combining (
15
) and (
16
), it follows

\frac{\alpha({{\left|u\right|}>t})^{2}}{\gamma_{n}}\leq\frac{F(\mu_{u}(t))(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(t))}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu_{u}(t)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}-1.

Integrating both sides from
0
to

, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{F(\mu_{u}(\tau))(-\mu_{u}^{\prime}(\tau))}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu_{u}(\tau)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}d\tau-t\geq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\alpha({{\left|u\right|}>\tau})^{2}}{\gamma_{n}}d\tau,

which gives, by the definition of rearrangement

g(s)-u^{*}(s)=\int_{s}^{|\Omega|}\frac{F(t)}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}dt-u^{*}(s)\geq\int_{0}^{u^{\ast}(s)}\frac{\alpha({{\left|u\right|}>t})^{2}}{\gamma_{n}}dt.

(17)

In order to apply the Lemma
2.7
to the integrand on the right hand side of (

A=\left\{t\geq 0:\mu_{u}(t)\geq 1-\frac{\alpha({\Omega})}{4}\right\}.

The set

is not empty if

, and it is in fact an interval. Moreover, for every

\frac{|\Omega\backslash\{{\left|u\right|}>t\}|}{|\Omega|}=|\Omega\backslash\{{\left|u\right|}>t\}|=1-\mu_{u}(t)\leq\frac{\alpha({\Omega})}{4},

and by Lemma

, we have

\alpha({{\left|u\right|}>t})\geq\frac{\alpha({\Omega})}{2}.

Thus, since

, for

\mu_{u}(t)\geq 1-\frac{\alpha({\Omega})}{4}\implies\alpha({\left|u\right|}>t)<\frac{\alpha(\Omega)}{2},

hence the integral on the right-hand side of (

\int_{0}^{u^{\ast}(s)}\frac{\alpha({{\left|u\right|}>t})^{2}}{\gamma_{n}}dt\geq\int_{0}^{\min\{s_{\Omega},u^{*}(s)\}}\frac{\alpha({{\left|u\right|}>t})^{2}}{\gamma_{n}}dt\geq\min\{s_{\Omega},u^{*}(s)\}\frac{\alpha({\Omega})^{2}}{4\gamma_{n}}.

Last, the integral of the minimum can be estimated as follows

Proposition 3.2
.

\Omega

Let us recall that if

with respect to

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{D(s+h)\backslash D(s)}m\,dx

Let

be the unique spherically decreasing solution to (

)
and let

denote its distribution function.

By (

), and in view of (

)

.

Hence, for

1=\frac{F(\mu_{g}(t))(-\mu_{g}^{\prime}(t))}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu_{g}(t)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\Rightarrow-\mu_{g}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu_{g}(t)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}{F(\mu_{g}(t))}.

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(\Omega)}{8}

\displaystyle=1-\left(1-\frac{\alpha(\Omega)}{8}\right)=\mu_{g}(0)-\mu_{g}(2t_{1})=

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{2t_{1}}\mu_{g}^{\prime}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{2t_{1}}\frac{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu_{g}(s)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}{F(\mu_{g}(s))}ds\leq\frac{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}2t_{1}}{m}.

1.3
, we show that

\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}f^{*}(t)(1-t)^{\frac{1}{n}}\,dt-\int_{\Omega}fh_{u}(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}|\nabla u|_{*}t^{\frac{1}{n}}\,dt-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|h_{u}(x)\,dx\leq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\varepsilon.

Applying the quantitative Hardy-Littlewood inequality
2.9
, we have

{\left\|f-f_{u}\right\|}_{L^{1}(\R^{n})}\leq\left[4\,e\,{\left\|f\right\|}_{\Lambda_{1}^{1}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}f^{*}(t)(1-t)^{\frac{1}{n}}\,dt-\int_{\Omega}fh_{u}(x)\,dx\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By direct calculations, we have that

{\left\|f\right\|}_{\Lambda_{1}^{1}(\Omega)}=n{\big\lVert f\big\rVert}_{\infty}=n.

Hence, we have

\frac{1}{4en^{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\left\|f-f_{u}\right\|}_{1}^{2}\leq\varepsilon.

Considering the rescaling properties in Section
2.5
, we get the thesis.

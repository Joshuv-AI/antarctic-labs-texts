Optimal Extended Neighbourhood Rule k k Nearest Neighbours Ensemble
Amjad Ali, Zardad Khan, Dost Muhammad Khan Ali, Saeed Aldahmani

Abstract
The traditional k k nearest neighbor ( k k NN) approach uses a distance formula within a spherical region to determine the k k closest training observations to a test sample point. However, this approach may not work well when test point is located outside this region. Moreover, aggregating many base k k NN learners can result in poor ensemble performance due to high classification errors. To address these issues, a new optimal extended neighborhood rule based ensemble method is proposed in this paper. This rule determines neighbors in k k steps starting from the closest sample point to the unseen observation and selecting subsequent nearest data points until the required number of observations is reached. Each base model is constructed on a bootstrap sample with a random subset of features, and optimal models are selected based on out-of-bag performance after building a sufficient number of models. The proposed ensemble is compared with state-of-the-art methods on 17 benchmark datasets using accuracy, Cohen’s kappa, and Brier score (BS). The performance of the proposed method is also assessed by adding contrived features in the original data.

Introduction

Classification is a supervised learning problem dealing with the prediction of class labels for unseen observations using a set of independent features according to training data
[
3
,
11
]
. Machine learning methods are extensively used for dealing with classification problems
[
34
]
.
k
nearest neighbours (
k
NN) is one of the top-ranked procedures used for classification problems
[
31
,
43
]
. It is a simple, easy to understand and non-parametric procedure which gives effective results in many cases, especially in large datasets
[
38
]
. The
k
NN procedure determines
k
nearest observations in the neighbourhood of a test point using a distance formula and predicts its class by using majority voting. As the
k
NN method is simple, easy and outperforms other complex classifiers in many cases, however,
k
NN is affected by many data-related problems, such as noise and irrelevant features in the data.

To improve the classification accuracy of the
k
NN based classifiers, various ensemble procedures have been proposed. For example, bootstrap aggregated
k
NN
[
37
]
, ensemble of subset of
k
NN
[
16
]
, random
k
NN
[
25
]
, weighted heterogeneous distance Metric
[
41
]
, optimal
k
NN ensemble
[
2
]
, ensemble of random subspace
k
NN
[
32
]
, etc. These methods try to make the base
k
NN learners more diverse and accurate to reduce the chance of the same errors repetition
[
13
]
. Almost all these techniques use majority voting based on the response values of the observations in the neighbourhood of the unseen sample point. To estimate the final prediction of the query point, the second round of majority voting is used based on the results given by the base learners. However, the prediction accuracy of these methods might be affected in the case of datasets with a majority of features as irrelevant and/or observations outside the general pattern of the data. This increases the likelihood of the ensemble being adversely affected by the noisy features and/or outliers. Therefore, modifying the nearest neighbourhood rule and selecting optimal models in such situations are needed to maintain the prediction accuracy. Several studies in literature are devoted to model selection for ensemble creation
[
45
,
21
]
. Similarly, to mitigate the effect of an outlier on the nearest neighbours, several studies have proposed modified neighbourhood identification methods. Examples of such procedures could be found in
[
9
,
10
]
.

The current paper proposes an optimal ensemble procedure using the extended neighbourhood rule (ExNRule) for
k
NN
[
1
]
as a merger of modified neighbourhood identification and model selection. The method is named optimal extended neighbourhood rule ensemble (OExNRule). A large number of base ExNRule models are constructed each on a bootstrap sample with a subset of features and the error rate of each model is computed on out-of-bag data points. After building a large number of
k
NN models via the ExNRule, a proportion of best performing models is selected based on their out-of-bag errors for the final ensemble. To assess the performance of the proposed procedure, 17 benchmark datasets are considered using classification accuracy, Cohen’s kappa and Brier score (BS) as performance metrics. The performance is compared with state-of-the-art procedures i.e.
k
nearest neighbour (
k
NN), weighted
k
nearest neighbours (W
k
NN), random
k
nearest neighbour (R
k
NN), random forest (RF), optimal trees ensemble (OTE) and support vector machine (SVM). Boxplots have also been constructed for further assessment of the results of the proposed OExNRule and the other competitors. The rest of the article is organized as follows:

Related work is summarized in Section

Several techniques have been proposed to enhance the performance of the classical
k

k

. Authors proposed a model based

. Additionally, a study introduced clustered

]
. Another study proposed modified

[

, and a different approach suggested adaptive metric nearest neighbor algorithms (ADAMENN) for bias reduction

. A

]
. Further advancements in

[

,
5
]
.

In addition to the above studies, there are several
k
NN based ensemble techniques proposed to improve the model accuracy and make the models more diverse. Breiman in
[
8
]
suggested a bootstrap aggregation (or simply bagging) procedure. It is one of the basic ensemble methods and develops a novel model to estimate the exact bootstrap average in the long run of the model
[
44
]
. Bagging is the fundamental block of many classical ensemble procedures. Methods based on the bagging technique draw a large number of random bootstrap samples of the same size and construct a base classifier on each sample. The final estimated class of a test point is the majority vote in the results given by all base learners
[
8
]
. The exact bagging technique is further extended by Steel
[
37
]
to sub-sampling with and without replacement aggregation. Several ensemble procedures have been proposed that use bagging and features subsets for constructing base
k
NN learners
[
16
,
15
]
. Moreover,
[
40
]
has proposed
k
value optimization in base
k
NN classifiers to construct ensemble methods. The idea of boosting
k
NN is given by Pedrajas and Boyer
[
13
]
, it is a combination of two methodologies to improve the performance of the ensemble. The first methodology is based on feature subset while the second is based on the transformation of the feature space using a non-linear projection. Other studies can be seen in
[
29
,
42
]
to boost up the
k
NN procedure and get improved results.

Ensemble procedures have a large number of base models, therefore, they are computationally complex and time-consuming. To reduce this issue, several authors have proposed algorithms such as the ensemble of a subset of
k
NN models (ES
k
NN)
[
16
]
for model selection. It is an ensemble procedure based on optimal models selected based on out-of-bag errors. It gives efficient results particularly in the case of datasets which have non-informative features. Further examples of similar procedures can be found in
[
21
,
22
]
. Although these procedures have shown promising results in many situations, however, they might give poor results due to mixed patterns in the data. Therefore, these methods need further improvement.

This paper proposes an optimal
k
NN ensemble based on a novel extended neighbourhood rule (OExNRule). This method takes a large number
B
bootstrap samples from training data at first stage, each with a subsets of features. The method applies ExNRule on each bootstrap sample and compute out-of-bag error. It then orders the models based on the out-of-bag errors given by all the
B
models and selects a proportion of top ranked ExNRule learners with minimum errors. Final ensemble is constructed by combining the selected models to predict the test data. This procedure improves the performance in the following ways.

1.

The
k
nearest instances are chosen in a step-wise manner to determine the true representative pattern of the unseen observation in the training data.

2.

The base models are diverse as each model is fitted on a random bootstrap sample in conjunction with a random subset of features for each model. Furthermore, it also avoids over-fitting.

3.

To ensure accuracy for the base learners, models are selected from a large number of
B
ExNRule learners on the basis of their out-of-bag errors.

3
The Optimal Extended Neighbourhood Rule (OExNRule) Ensemble

Consider
\mathcal{L}=(X,Y)_{n\times(p+1)}
be a training dataset, where
X_{n\times p}
represents feature matrix with
n
observations and
p
features while
Y
stands for the binary response, i.e.,
Y\in\{0,1\}
. Draw
B
bootstrap sample from the training data
\mathcal{L}
each with a random subset of
p^{\prime}\leq p
features, i.e.,
S_{n\times(p^{\prime}+1)}^{b}
, where,
b=1,2,3,\ldots,B
. Then apply the ExNRule
[
1
]
on each bootstrap sample, i.e.,
S_{n\times(p^{\prime}+1)}^{b}
and calculate the errors on out-of-bag data points, i.e.,
E^{b}
, where,
b=1,2,3,\ldots,B
. Rank the base models with respect to the out-of-bag errors (
E^{b}
) in ascending order and choose a portion of the top-ranked
B^{\prime}
learners. Based on the selected models, determine the predictions of a new observation, i.e.,
\hat{Y}^{1},\hat{Y}^{2},\hat{Y}^{3},\ldots,\hat{Y}^{B^{\prime}}
. The final estimated class of the unseen observation is the majority vote of the results obtained from the selected models.

3.1
Mathematical Description

Extended neighbourhood rule determines
k
nearest observations in a sequence to a test point
X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{0}
in
b^{th}
bootstrap sample using a distance formula as follows:

\delta_{b}(X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{i-1},X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{i})_{min}=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{p^{\prime}}|x_{j}^{i-1}-x_{j}^{i}|^{q}\right]^{1/q},

(1)

where,
i=1,2,\ldots,k
and
b=1,2,\ldots,B
.

Equation
1
gives the required sequence of the distances as bellows:

\delta_{b}(X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{0},X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{1})_{min},\delta_{b}(X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{1},X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{2})_{min},

\delta_{b}(X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{2},X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{3})_{min},\ldots,\delta_{b}(X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{k-1},X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{k})_{min}.

The above sequence indicates that, the nearest data point to
X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{i-1}
is
X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{i}
, where,
i=1,2,3,\ldots,k
. This gives the required
k
observations in the pattern i.e.
X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{1},X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{2},X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{3},\ldots,X_{1\times p^{\prime}}^{k}
and their corresponding classes are
y^{1},y^{2},y^{3},\ldots,y^{k}
respectively. The estimated class label of
X_{1\times p}^{0}
from the
b^{th}
learner is determine by the majority voting i.e.
\hat{Y}^{b}=
majority vote of
(y^{1},y^{2},y^{3},\ldots,y^{k})
.

The
b^{th}
base model
H^{b}(.)
, uses the above rule to compute the out-of-bag error
E^{b}
. In this way, a total of
B
base models are constructed and their out-of-bag errors are also computed. These models are ranked in ascending order according to their performance i.e.
H^{1}(.),H^{2}(.),H^{3}(.),\ldots,H^{B}(.)
. A proportion of top ranked
B^{\prime}
models is selected i.e.
H^{1}(.),H^{2}(.),H^{3}(.),\ldots,H^{B^{\prime}}(.)
, which comprise the final ensemble to predict the test data.

3.2
Algorithm

The proposed OExNRule based ensemble takes the following steps.

1.

Draw
B
random bootstrap samples from training observations in conjunction with a random subset of
p^{\prime}<p
features without replacement.

2.

Using the suggested ExNRule in each sample drawn in Step 1, construct base models.

3.

Compute out-of-bag errors
E^{b}
for each base model constructed in Step 2.

4.

Rank the models according to their out-of-bag errors i.e.,
E^{b}
in ascending order.

5.

Select
B^{\prime}
models based on individual
E^{b}
values and combine them in an ensemble.

6.

Use the ensemble formed in Step 5 for prediction purposes.

4.3
Results

Table
2
presents a detailed comparison of the classification performance of the OExNRule method with several classical algorithms on 17 benchmark datasets. The results show that different methods have different levels of efficacy across different evaluation metrics.

The OExNRule method outperforms most classical algorithms in terms of accuracy on majority of datasets. In particular, OExNRule exhibits optimal accuracy on 10 datasets, highlighting its effectiveness and robustness in classification tasks. Notably, it performs equally well as the R
k
NN method on 2 datasets, indicating its competitive performance against established algorithms. However, it is worth noting that the
k
NN method outperforms OExNRule on only 1 dataset, while the W
k
NN method does not yield satisfactory results across any dataset. Moreover, while the R
k
NN, RF, and OTE methods achieve maximum accuracy on a few datasets, none of them outperforms the OExNRule method in terms of overall accuracy.

According to the evaluation based on Cohen’s kappa, it has been found that the OExNRule method performs better than other methods on most of the datasets. In fact, the OExNRule method has the highest score of Cohen’s kappa on 10 datasets, which indicates a high level of agreement between the predicted and actual classifications. On the other hand, the
k
NN method does not perform well on any dataset, highlighting its limitations in capturing agreement beyond chance. Although the W
k
NN, R
k
NN, and RF methods show optimal performance on a few datasets, they do not perform as well as the OExNRule method across all datasets. Similarly, the OTE method shows subpar performance across all datasets, while the SVM method outperforms others on only 3 datasets.

The analysis of the BS metric provides strong evidence for the superior predictive accuracy and reliability of the OExNRule method. When compared to classical algorithms such as
k
NN, W
k
NN, RF, and even R
k
NN, OExNRule consistently achieves the lowest BS across different datasets. This indicates that it has better calibration and accuracy in its probabilistic predictions. On the other hand, classical methods show higher BS values, which suggests that they may have less precision and reliability in their probability estimates, leading to less accurate predictions. The consistently superior performance of OExNRule in terms of BS highlights its effectiveness in providing both accurate classifications and reliable probability estimates. This makes it an excellent choice for classification tasks that require robust and well-calibrated predictive models.

5
Conclusion

The method proposed in this paper selects a proportion of optimal extended neighbourhood rule (ExNRule) based models using out-of-bag error from a total of
B
learners. The selected base ExNRule models are combined to predict the class labels of test data using majority voting. The results given in the paper suggest that the proposed method outperforms the other classical procedures considered in this paper and demonstrates high prediction accuracy when data have irrelevant features.

Furthermore, each ExNRule based model is constructed on a random bootstrap sample in conjunction with a random subset of features, brining diversity to the ensemble. The proposed method ensures high prediction performance due to randomization, selection of neighbours in
k
steps pattern and model selection based on out-of-bag error.

The performance of the proposed procedure could further be improved by using a more proper distance formula and weighting schemes for choosing the nearest neighbours. Moreover, improvement in the performance can be maximized by using the feature selection
[
18
,
35
]
during base model construction. These methods select top ranked features from the features space in the dataset and use them to fit the base learners.

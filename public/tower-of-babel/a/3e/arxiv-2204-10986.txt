Regrets of Proximal Method of Multipliers for Online Non-convex Optimization with Long Term Constraints
Liwei Zhang, Haoyang Liu, Xiantao Xiao

Abstract
The online optimization problem with non-convex loss functions over a closed convex set, coupled with a set of inequality (possibly non-convex) constraints is a challenging online learning problem. A proximal method of multipliers with quadratic approximations (named as OPMM) is presented to solve this online non-convex optimization with long term constraints. Regrets of the violation of Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions of OPMM for solving online non-convex optimization problems are analyzed. Under mild conditions, it is shown that this algorithm exhibits 𝒪 ( T − 1 / 8 ) {\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/8}) Lagrangian gradient violation regret, 𝒪 ( T − 1 / 8 ) {\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/8}) constraint violation regret and 𝒪 ( T − 1 / 4 ) {\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/4}) complementarity residual regret if parameters in the algorithm are properly chosen, where T T denotes the number of time periods. For the case that the objective is a convex quadratic function, we demonstrate that the regret of the objective reduction can be established even the feasible set is non-convex. For the case when the constraint functions are convex, if the solution of the subproblem in OPMM is obtained by solving its dual, OPMM is proved to be an implementable projection method for solving the online non-convex optimization problem.

Introduction

In recent years, a number of efficient algorithms have
been developed for online optimization. Convexity of the loss functions and the constraint sets has played a central
role in the development of many of these algorithms. In this paper, we consider a more
general setting, where the sequence of loss functions encountered by the learner could be
non-convex and the constraint set is defined by a set of (possibly non-convex) inequalities.
Such a setting has various applications in machine learning
MSF2017
;
NPM2019
;
CJGWNYS2019
, especially in
adversarial training
SZSEEGF2014
and training of Generative
Adversarial Networks (GANs)
Goodfellow2014
.

Most of the existing works about online optimization have focused
on convex loss functions. A number of computationally efficient approaches
have been proposed for regret minimization in this setting. Among them the famous ones include Follow-the-leader
Kalai2005
, Follow-the-Regularized-Leader
Shai2007a
;
Shai2007b
, Exponentiated Online Gradient
Kiv1997
, Online Mirror Descent, Perceptron
Rosenblatt1958
and Winnow
Littlestone1988
. There are also a lot of publications concerning algorithms for online convex optimization, see
(
MRTal2012
, Chapter 7)
,
(
SSS2014
, Chapter 21)
, and survey papers
Shai2011
;
Hazan2015
and references cited in these two papers.

However, when the loss functions are
non-convex or the constraint sets are non-convex, minimizing the regret is computationally prohibitive. In the last years, there have been several papers about learning with
non-convex losses over simple convex constraint sets. A few heuristic algorithms have been
proposed in
EBG2011
;
GPSB2011

without establishing the regret bounds.
In
GaoZZhang2018
, the regret of online projection gradient method for a restricted
class of loss functions is analyzed.
The notion of local regret and the regret of online gradient method for a class of continuously differentiable non-convex loss functions are presented in
HSZ2017
. DC (difference of convex functions) programming and
DCA method for online learning problems with non-convex loss functions are investigated in
LH2020
. In
YDHTW2018
, a recursive exponential weighted algorithm that attains a regret of
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2})
for non-convex Lipschitz continuous loss functions is proposed.
It is shown in
AgarwalHazn2019
that the classical Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader (FTPL) algorithm achieves
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/3})
regret for general non-convex losses which are
Lipschitz continuous. Moreover, in
SN2019
, it is proved that FTPL achieves optimal regret rate
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2})
for the problem of online learning with non-convex losses. An online
cubic-regularized Newton method for non-convex online optimization is studied in
RBGM2019
.

In this paper, we consider a more complicated non-convex online optimization problem, which has a complex constraint set defined by

\Phi=\{x\in\mathcal{C}:g_{i}(x)\leq 0,\ i=1,\ldots,p\}.

(1)

Here,
\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a nonempty convex compact set with diameter
D_{0}:=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}\|x-x^{\prime}\|
and
g_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=1,\ldots,p
are continuous (possibly non-convex) functions.

In order to alleviate the computational challenge of the projection
\Pi_{\Phi}(\cdot)
with
\Phi
defined by (
1
), in
MJY2012
the authors considered to relax the constraints
g_{i}(x)\leq 0
to be long term constraints. That is, the decision
x^{t}
is not required to satisfy
g_{i}(x_{t})\leq 0
at each round, but only require that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{i}(x^{t})\leq 0
.
There are some recent works related to online convex optimization with long term constraints. In
MJY2012
,
a gradient based algorithm is designed to achieve
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2})
regret bound and
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/4})
violation of constraints for an online optimization problem whose constraint set is defined by a set of inequalities of smooth convex functions. In

JHA2016
;
YNeely2016
new algorithms are developed to improve the performance in comparison with
MJY2012
. However, for non-convex online optimization problems with non-convex loss functions and constraint sets of the form (
1
), the research has been very limited until recently.

At round
t
, we consider the following non-convex optimization problem

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\limits_{x\in\mathcal{C}}&f_{t}(x)\\[4.0pt]
{\rm s.t.}&g(x)\leq 0,\end{array}

(2)

where
g(x):=(g_{1}(x),\ldots,g_{p}(x))^{T}
. Since Problem (
2
) is non-convex, it is unrealistic to analyze the regrets in both objective reduction and constraint violation. Just like the offline non-convex optimization, it is natural to consider the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions which are given by

\begin{array}[]{l}0\in\nabla_{x}L^{t}(x,\lambda)+N_{\mathcal{C}}(x),\\[10.0pt]
0\geq g(x)\perp\lambda\geq 0,\end{array}

(3)

where
L^{t}(x,\lambda)=f_{t}(x)+\lambda^{T}g(x)
and
N_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
is the normal cone of
\mathcal{C}
at
x
.
Conditions (
3
) are equivalent to the following equalities:

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm dist}\,\Big(0,\nabla_{x}L^{t}(x,\lambda)+N_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\Big)=0,\\[10.0pt]
\lambda-[\lambda+g(x)]_{+}=0,\end{array}

(4)

where
[\cdot]_{+}:=\max\{0,\cdot\}
.
Therefore, it is reasonable to consider the regret of violation for the equalities in (
4
).

In this paper, we extend the proximal method of multipliers, a classical algorithm proposed in
Rockafellar76A
to solve convex programming, for online non-convex optimization problem, and analyze its regret bounds for KKT violation consisting of Lagrangian residual, constraint violation and complementarity residual. Let

q^{t}_{i}(x)
,
i=0,1,\ldots,p
be the quadratic approximations of
f_{t}
and
g_{i},i=1,\ldots,p
at
x^{t}
, respectively, defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}q^{t}_{0}(x):=f_{t}(x^{t})+\langle\nabla f_{t}(x^{t}),x-x^{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Theta^{t}_{0}(x-x^{t}),x-x^{t}\right\rangle,\\[10.0pt]
q^{t}_{i}(x):=g_{i}(x^{t})+\langle\nabla g_{i}(x^{t}),x-x^{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Theta^{t}_{i}(x-x^{t}),x-x^{t}\right\rangle,\ i=1,\ldots,p,\end{array}

where
\Theta^{t}_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\Theta^{t}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
are properly selected symmetric

The corresponding augmented Lagrangian function is defined by

\mathcal{L}^{t}_{\sigma}(x,\lambda):=q_{0}^{t}(x)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}[\lambda_{i}+\sigma q^{t}_{i}(x)]_{+}^{2}-\|\lambda\|^{2}\right]

(5)

for
(x,\lambda)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
and
\sigma>0
. At each round

be the optimal solution of the following problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\,\mathcal{L}^{t}_{\sigma}(x,\lambda^{t})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x-x^{t}\|^{2},

and update the multipliers by
\lambda^{t+1}_{i}=[\lambda^{t}_{i}+\sigma q_{i}^{t}(x^{t+1})]_{+},\ i=1,\ldots,p
,
where
\alpha>0
is some parameter. Let

q^{t}(x):=(q_{1}^{t}(x),\ldots,q_{p}^{t}(x))^{T}
,
then

\lambda^{t+1}=[\lambda^{t}+\sigma q^{t}(x^{t+1})]_{+}
.

The main results of this paper can be summarized as follows.

•

When we choose
\sigma=T^{-1/4}
and
\alpha=T^{1/4}
, under mild assumptions, there exists
w^{t+1}\in N_{\mathcal{C}}(x^{t+1})
for any
t=1,\ldots,T
such that the regret of Lagrangian residual is

\left\|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\nabla f_{t+1}(x^{t+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda^{t+1}_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{t+1})+w^{t+1}\right]\right\|\\
\leq\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/8}),

the regret of constraint violation is

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{i}(x^{t})\leq\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/8}),\quad i=1,\ldots,p,

and the regret of complementarity residual is

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\lambda^{t+1}-[\lambda^{t+1}+\sigma g(x^{t+1})]_{+}\|\leq\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4}).

•

For the case that the objective function
f_{t}
is convex quadratic, if
\sigma=T^{-1/2}
and
\alpha=T^{1/2}
, the regret of objective reduction is

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(x^{t})-\inf_{z\in\Phi}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(z)\leq\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2}).

•

For the case that
g_{1},\ldots,g_{p}
are convex functions, if the solution of the subproblem in OPMM is obtained by solving the dual of the subproblem, OPMM can be reformulated as an implementable projection method.

2

2
Auxiliary Properties of OPMM

In this section, we focus on establishing a variety of auxiliary properties of OPMM under some reasonable assumptions. We begin by introducing two classes of assumptions, in which the first class is about the structure of Problem (
2
) and the second class is to ensure that the quadratic approximations

,

Assumption A1
.

There exist constants
\kappa_{f}>0
,
\kappa_{g}>0

\nu_{g}>0

\|g(x)\|\leq\nu_{g}
.

Assumption A2
.

The functions

and
g_{i}
,

.
There exist constants

Assumption A3
.

The Slater condition holds, that is,
there exist a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a vector

Note that the set

holds, it follows that

Assumption B1

The matrix

It holds that

There exists a constant

The augmented Lagrangian function

Let Assumptions

\quad\quad+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\|\widehat{x}-x^{l}\|^{2}-\|\widehat{x}-x^{l+1}\|^{2}\right],\end{array}

in which
\widehat{x}
is given in Assumption
A3
and the second inequality above is obtained from Assumption
B2
.
Reorganizing terms and using Assumptions
A1
,
B1
,
B3
,
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left[\|\lambda^{l+1}\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{l}\|^{2}\right]\\[10.0pt]
\leq\langle\nabla f_{l}(x^{l}),\widehat{x}-x^{l+1}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Theta^{l}_{0}(\widehat{x}-x^{l}),\widehat{x}-x^{l}\right\rangle+\langle\lambda^{l},g(\widehat{x})\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|g(\widehat{x})\|^{2}\\[10.0pt]
\quad\quad+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\|\widehat{x}-x^{l}\|^{2}-\|\widehat{x}-x^{l+1}\|^{2}\right]\\[10.0pt]
\leq\kappa_{f}D_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{q}D_{0}^{2}+\langle\lambda^{l},g(\widehat{x})\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\|\widehat{x}-x^{l}\|^{2}-\|\widehat{x}-x^{l+1}\|^{2}\right].\end{array}

(11)

Noting that for
l\in\{t,t+1,\ldots,t+s-1\}
, one has from Assumption A3 that

\langle\lambda^{l},g(\widehat{x})\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda^{l}_{j}g_{j}(\widehat{x})\leq-\varepsilon_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda^{l}_{j}\leq-\varepsilon_{0}\|\lambda^{l}\|.

(12)

Thus, making a summation of (
11
) over
\{t,t+1,\ldots,t+s-1\}
, noticing
(
12
) and the fact that
\|\lambda^{t+l}\|\geq\|\lambda^{t}\|-\sigma\beta_{0}l
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left[\|\lambda^{t+s}\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}\right]\\[10.0pt]
\leq\left(\kappa_{f}D_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{q}D_{0}^{2}\right)s+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}s+\sum_{l=t}^{t+s-1}\langle\lambda^{l},g(\widehat{x})\rangle+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\left(\|\widehat{x}-x^{t}\|^{2}-\|\widehat{x}-x^{t+s}\|^{2}\right)\right]\\[10.0pt]
\leq\left(\kappa_{f}D_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{q}D_{0}^{2}\right)s+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}s-\varepsilon_{0}\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}\|\lambda^{t+l}\|+\frac{\alpha}{2}D_{0}^{2}\\[10.0pt]
\leq\left(\kappa_{f}D_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{q}D_{0}^{2}\right)s+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}s+\frac{\alpha}{2}D_{0}^{2}-\varepsilon_{0}\sum_{l=0}^{s-1}\left[\|\lambda^{t}\|-\sigma\beta_{0}l\right]\\[10.0pt]
\leq\left(\kappa_{f}D_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{q}D_{0}^{2}\right)s+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}s+\frac{\alpha}{2}D_{0}^{2}+\varepsilon_{0}\sigma\beta_{0}\frac{s(s-1)}{2}-\varepsilon_{0}s\|\lambda^{t}\|,\end{array}

which, together with
\|\lambda^{t}\|\geq\vartheta(\sigma,\alpha,s)
, further implies that

\begin{array}[]{l}\|\lambda^{t+s}\|^{2}\\[10.0pt]
\leq\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}+2\sigma\left(\kappa_{f}D_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{q}D_{0}^{2}\right)s+\sigma^{2}\nu_{g}^{2}s+\alpha\sigma D_{0}^{2}+\varepsilon_{0}\sigma^{2}\beta_{0}s(s-1)-2\varepsilon_{0}\sigma s\|\lambda^{t}\|\\[10.0pt]
=\left(\|\lambda^{t}\|-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\sigma}{2}s\right)^{2}-\frac{3\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{4}s^{2}+\varepsilon_{0}\sigma s\vartheta(\sigma,\alpha,s)-\varepsilon_{0}\sigma s\|\lambda^{t}\|\\[10.0pt]
\leq\left(\|\lambda^{t}\|-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\sigma}{2}s\right)^{2}.\end{array}

Noticing that
\|\lambda^{t}\|\geq\vartheta(\sigma,\alpha,s)\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\sigma}{2}s
, we have

\|\lambda^{t+s}\|\leq\|\lambda^{t}\|-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\sigma}{2}s.

The proof is completed.
∎

Regret Analysis of OPMM

In this section, we establish the regret bounds of the proposed algorithm. In particular, we focus on estimating the following three regrets: regret of Lagrangian residual, regret of constraint violation and regret of complementarity residual.
The following proposition establishes an upper bound of the so-called Lagrangian residual.

Proposition 8
.

Let Assumptions
A1
,
A2
,
B3
be satisfied. Then, there exists a vector

(16)

where
\beta>0
is an arbitrary scalar and

\mathcal{H}_{t}:=\nabla f_{t+1}(x^{t+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda^{t+1}_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{t+1})+w^{t+1}.

Proof.

It follows from the optimality conditions of (
6

0\in\nabla q^{t}_{0}(x^{t+1})+{\cal J}q^{t}(x^{t+1})^{T}\lambda^{t+1}+\alpha(x^{t+1}-x^{t})+N_{\mathcal{C}}(x^{t+1}).

Equivalently, from the definitions of
q_{0}^{t}
and
q^{t}
, there exists
w^{t+1}\in N_{\mathcal{C}}(x^{t+1})
such that

0=\nabla f_{t}(x^{t})+\Theta_{0}^{t}(x^{t+1}-x^{t})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}^{t+1}[\nabla g_{i}(x^{t})+\Theta_{i}^{t}(x^{t+1}-x^{t})]+\alpha(x^{t+1}-x^{t})+w^{t+1}.

(17)

In view of definitions of
q^{t}_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,p
and
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, we may rewrite (

\begin{array}[]{ll}0=&\mathcal{H}_{t}+[\nabla f_{t}(x^{t})-\nabla f_{t+1}(x^{t+1})]+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda^{t+1}_{i}(\nabla g_{i}(x^{t})-\nabla g_{i}(x^{t+1}))\\[10.0pt]
&\quad+\Theta^{t}_{0}(x^{t+1}-x^{t})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda^{t+1}_{i}\Theta^{t}_{i}(x^{t+1}-x^{t})+\alpha(x^{t+1}-x^{t}).\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}&-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{t}\\[10.0pt]

,

,

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\|\\[10.0pt]
\leq[\|\nabla f_{1}(x^{1})\|+\|\nabla f_{T+1}(x^{T+1})\|]+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda^{t+1}_{i}\|\nabla g_{i}(x^{t})-\nabla g_{i}(x^{t+1})\|\right]\\[10.0pt]
\quad+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\Theta^{t}_{0}\|\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda^{t+1}_{i}\|\Theta^{t}_{i}\|\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|\right]+\alpha D_{0}\\[10.0pt]

\quad+\frac{p\beta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}+\alpha D_{0}\\[10.0pt]
=2\kappa_{f}+\frac{\kappa_{q}^{2}}{2\beta}T+\frac{(1+p)\beta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{(L_{g}+\kappa_{q})^{2}}{2\beta}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\lambda^{t+1}\|^{2}+\alpha D_{0},\end{array}

which proves (

Theorem 9
.

Let Assumptions

,

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
7
that

\|\lambda^{t}\|\leq\psi(\sigma,\alpha,s)=\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}\frac{\alpha}{s}+\kappa_{2}\sigma+\kappa_{3}\sigma s,

where
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3}

For
\sigma=T^{-1/4}
and
\alpha=T^{1/4}

(18)

8

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\|&\leq\frac{2\kappa_{f}}{T}+\frac{\kappa_{q}^{2}}{2\beta}+\frac{2(1+p)\beta}{\alpha T}\left[\frac{T}{\alpha}\kappa_{f}^{2}+\nu_{g}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\lambda^{t}\|+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}T\right]\\[10.0pt]
&\quad\quad+\frac{(L_{g}+\kappa_{q})^{2}}{2\beta T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\lambda^{t+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha D_{0}}{T}.\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\|\\[8.0pt]

which yields item (i).

From Proposition
3

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{i}(x^{t})\leq\frac{1}{\sigma T}\lambda^{T+1}_{i}+\gamma\kappa_{g}^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha T}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma}+2\kappa_{q}\right]\left[\frac{\kappa_{f}^{2}}{\alpha}T+\nu_{g}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\lambda^{t}\|+\frac{\sigma}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}T\right].

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{i}(x^{t})\\[10.0pt]

&\quad\quad+T^{-3/4}(\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{3}+\kappa_{2}T^{-1/4})+\kappa_{g}^{2}T^{-1/8},\end{array}

which proves item (ii).

\|g(x^{t+1})-q^{t}(x^{t+1})\|

A2

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|g(x^{t+1})-q^{t}(x^{t+1})\|&=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}(g_{i}(x^{t+1})-q^{t}_{i}(x^{t+1}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\[10.0pt]

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\lambda^{t+1}-[\lambda^{t+1}+\sigma g(x^{t+1})]_{+}\|\\[10.0pt]

Proposition 10
.

Let Assumptions
A1
,

,

be satisfied and

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(x^{t})-\inf_{z\in\Phi}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(z)\leq\left(\kappa_{f}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{g}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}{\rm dist}^{2}\,(x^{1},S^{*})\right)T^{-1/2},

where
S^{*}
is the set of optimal solutions given by
S^{*}:=\argmin_{x\in\Phi}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(x)
.

Proof.

In view of (
6
), we have from Assumption
B2
that, for all
z\in\mathcal{C}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}q_{0}^{t}(x^{t+1})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}\\[8.0pt]
\leq q_{0}^{t}(z)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left[\|[\lambda^{t}+\sigma q^{t}(z)]_{+}\|^{2}-\|[\lambda^{t}+\sigma q^{t}(x^{t+1})]_{+}\|^{2}\right]\\[8.0pt]
\quad\quad+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\|z-x^{t}\|^{2}-\|z-x^{t+1}\|^{2}\right]\\[10.0pt]
\leq q_{0}^{t}(z)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left[\|[\lambda^{t}+\sigma g(z)]_{+}\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{t+1}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\|z-x^{t}\|^{2}-\|z-x^{t+1}\|^{2}\right].\end{array}

Rearranging terms and noticing

we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{t}(x^{t})+\frac{\alpha}{4}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}\\[8.0pt]
\leq f_{t}(z)+\left(f_{t}(x^{t})-q_{0}^{t}(x^{t+1})-\frac{\alpha}{4}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}(\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}-\|\lambda^{t+1}\|^{2})\\[10.0pt]
\quad\quad+\langle\lambda^{t},g(z)\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|g(z)\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\|z-x^{t}\|^{2}-\|z-x^{t+1}\|^{2}\right].\end{array}

(19)

From Assumption
B1
and Assumption
A1
one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{t}(x^{t})-q_{0}^{t}(x^{t+1})-\frac{\alpha}{4}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}\\[8.0pt]

Making a summation, we obtain

(20)

\mathcal{C}
, which is defined by

{\rm dist}^{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(x):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{C}}\|x-u\|_{G},

where
\|x\|_{G}=\sqrt{x^{T}Gx}
is the
G
-weighted norm of
x
. The
G
-weighted projection of
x
onto
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by

\Pi^{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
, is defined by

\Pi^{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(x):=\argmin\limits_{u\in\mathcal{C}}\|x-u\|_{G}.

The following lemma is well-known, see, e.g.,
(
Beck2017
, Example 3.31)
.

Lemma 11
.

For a closed convex set
\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a positively definite matrix
G\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
, let

\pi(x):=\frac{1}{2}{\rm dist}^{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)^{2}
.
Then,
\pi
is continuously differentiable and

\nabla\pi(x)=G(x-\Pi^{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)).

By introducing artificial vectors
z
and
w
, we can express Problem (
20
) as the following equivalent convex quadratic programming problem

\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\limits_{x,z,w}&q_{0}^{t}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x-x^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|z\|^{2}\\[10.0pt]
{\rm s.t.}&\lambda^{t}+\sigma g(x^{t})+\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})(x-x^{t})-z+w=0,\\[10.0pt]
&x\in\mathcal{C},\ z\geq 0,\ w\geq 0.\end{array}

(21)

The Lagrangian function of Problem (
21
) is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}L^{t}(x,z,w,y)\\[10.0pt]
=q_{0}^{t}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x-x^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|z\|^{2}+\langle y,\lambda^{t}+\sigma g(x^{t})+\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})(x-x^{t})-z+w\rangle.\end{array}

Then, the dual of Problem (
21
) is expressed as

\begin{array}[]{l}\max\limits_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}}\inf\limits_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\inf\limits_{z\geq 0}\inf\limits_{w\geq 0}L^{t}(x,z,w,y)\\[8.0pt]
=\max\limits_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}}\left\{\langle y,\lambda^{t}+\sigma g(x^{t})\rangle+\inf\limits_{x\in\mathcal{C}}[q_{0}^{t}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x-x^{t}\|^{2}+\langle y,\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})(x-x^{t})\rangle]\right.\\[10.0pt]
\quad\quad\quad\quad+\inf\limits_{w\geq 0}y^{T}w+\left.\inf\limits_{z\geq 0}\left[\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|z\|^{2}-\langle y,z\rangle\right]\right\}\\[10.0pt]
=\max\limits_{y\geq 0}\left\{\langle y,\lambda^{t}+\sigma g(x^{t})\rangle-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y\|^{2}+f_{t}(x^{t})-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f_{t}(x^{t})+\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})^{T}y\|^{2}_{[H^{t}]^{-1}}\right.\\[10.0pt]
\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left.\inf_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\|x-\Big(x^{t}-[H^{t}]^{-1}(\nabla f_{t}(x^{t})+\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})^{T}y)\Big)\right\|^{2}_{H^{t}}\right]\right\}\\[10.0pt]
=\max\limits_{y\geq 0}\left\{\langle y,\lambda^{t}+\sigma g(x^{t})\rangle-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y\|^{2}+f_{t}(x^{t})-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f_{t}(x^{t})+\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})^{T}y\|^{2}_{[H^{t}]^{-1}}\right.\\[10.0pt]
\quad\quad+\left.\left[\frac{1}{2}{\rm dist}_{\mathcal{C}}^{H^{t}}\Big(x^{t}-[H^{t}]^{-1}(\nabla f_{t}(x^{t})+\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})^{T}y)\Big)^{2}\right]\right\},\end{array}

where
H^{t}:=\Theta^{t}_{0}+\alpha I
.
Therefore, when we derive the optimal solution
y^{t}
by solving the dual problem, from the duality theory, the solution of the subproblem (
6

x^{t+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}^{H^{t}}\Big(x^{t}-[H^{t}]^{-1}(\nabla f_{t}(x^{t})+\sigma{\cal J}g(x^{t})^{T}y^{t})\Big).

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we present a proximal method of multipliers with quadratic approximations (OPMM) for solving an online non-convex optimization with (possibly non-convex) inequality constraints. We show that, this algorithm exhibits
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/8})
Lagrangian residual regret,
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/8})
regret of constraint violation and
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/4})
complementarity residual regret if parameters in the algorithm are properly chosen, where
T
denotes the number of iterations.
We also show that, for the case when the constraint functions are all convex, the projection version of OPMM provides a practical way for finding a decision sequence
\{x^{1},x^{2},\ldots,x^{T}\}
. To the best of our knowledge, the regret analysis of numerical methods for online non-convex optimization with long term constraints has not been studied in the literature yet.

We note that, even for the simple bounded box set
\mathcal{C}=[a,b]\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the analysis of regrets requires the exact solution to the subproblem (
25
). How to obtain the regret bounds when the subproblem is inexactly solved is an important future research topic worth considering.

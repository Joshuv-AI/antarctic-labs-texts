Count and cofactor matroids of highly connected graphs
Dániel Garamvölgyi, Tibor Jordán, Csaba Király

Abstract
We consider two types of matroids defined on the edge set of a graph G G : count matroids ℳ k , ℓ ​ ( G ) {\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G) , in which independence is defined by a sparsity count involving the parameters k k and ℓ \ell , and the C 2 1 C_{2}^{1} -cofactor matroid 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{C}(G) , in which independence is defined by linear independence in the cofactor matrix of G G . We show, for each pair ( k , ℓ ) (k,\ell) , that if G G is sufficiently highly connected, then G − e G-e has maximum rank for all e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G) , and the matroid ℳ k , ℓ ​ ( G ) {\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G) is connected. These results unify and extend several previous results, including theorems of Nash-Williams and Tutte ( k = ℓ = 1 k=\ell=1 ), and Lovász and Yemini ( k = 2 , ℓ = 3 k=2,\ell=3 ). We also prove that if G G is highly connected, then the vertical connectivity of 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{C}(G) is also high. We use these results to generalize Whitney’s celebrated result on the graphic matroid of G G (which corresponds to ℳ 1 , 1 ​ ( G ) {\cal M}_{1,1}(G) ) to all count matroids and to the C 2 1 C_{2}^{1} -cofactor matroid: if G G is highly connected, depending on k k and ℓ \ell , then the count matroid ℳ k , ℓ ​ ( G ) {\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G) uniquely determines G G ; and similarly, if G G is 14 14 -connected, then its C 2 1 C_{2}^{1} -cofactor matroid 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{C}(G) uniquely determines G G . We also derive similar results for the t t -fold union of the C 2 1 C_{2}^{1} -cofactor matroid, and use them to prove that every 24 24 -connected graph has a spanning tree T T for which G − E ⁡ ( T ) G-E(T) is 3 3 -connected, which verifies a case of a conjecture of Kriesell.

2.1
Union and vertical connectivity of matroids

Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}=(E,\mathcal{I}_{i}),i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
be a collection of matroids
on a common ground set
E
, where
\mathcal{I}_{i}
is the family of independent sets in matroid
\mathcal{M}_{i}
.
The
union
of
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{t}
is the matroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
whose independent sets are defined by

\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1}\cup\ldots\cup I_{t}:I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{1},\ldots,I_{t}\in\mathcal{I}_{t}\}.

Nash-Williams
[
17
]
and Edmonds
[
4
]
gave the following characterization of the rank function
r
of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
r_{i}
denote the rank function of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. Then for all
E^{\prime}\subseteq E

we have

r(E^{\prime})=\min_{F\subseteq E^{\prime}}\bigl(|F|+\sum_{i=1}^{t}r_{i}(E^{\prime}-F)\bigr).

(1)

Let
{\cal M}=(E,r)
be a matroid with rank function
r

and let
k
be a positive integer.
We say that a bipartition
(E_{1},E_{2})
of
E
is a
vertical
k
-separation
of
\mathcal{M}
if
r(E_{1}),r(E_{2})\geq k
and

r(E_{1})+r(E_{2})\leq r(E)+k-1

holds. In this case
r(E_{i})<r(E)
for
i=1,2
.
The
vertical connectivity
of
\mathcal{M}

is defined to be the smallest integer
k
for which

{\cal M}
has a vertical
k
-separation. If
{\cal M}
has no vertical
separations at all, then we define its vertical connectivity to be
r(E)
.
We say that
{\cal M}
is

vertically
k
-connected
if its vertical connectivity is at least
k
.

An element
e\in E
is a
bridge
in
{\cal M}
if
r(E-\{e\})=r(E)-1
holds.
We say that a matroid
{\cal M}
on ground set
E
is
connected
if
|E|\geq 2
and
{\cal M}
is loopless and
vertically
2
-connected. In particular, a connected matroid cannot contain any bridges, since if
f\in E
is a bridge, then
(\{f\},E-\{f\})
forms a vertical
1
-separation.
The maximal connected submatroids
of a matroid
{\cal M}

are pairwise disjoint and the sum of their ranks is
equal to the rank of
{\cal M}
.
They are called the
components
of
{\cal M}
. We say that a component is
trivial
if it has a single element (which is necessarily a bridge), and
nontrivial
otherwise.

2.2
Count matroids

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
k
and
\ell
be two integers with
k\geq 1
and
\ell\leq 2k-1
.
The
(k,\ell)
-count matroid
of
G
is the matroid
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G)=(E,{\cal I}_{k,\ell})
on the edge set of
G
in which the family of independent sets is defined by the sparsity condition

{\cal I}_{k,\ell}=\{I\subseteq E:|I^{\prime}|\leq k|V(I^{\prime})|-\ell\ \hbox{for all}\ \varnothing\not=I^{\prime}\subseteq I\}.

It is known that
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G)
is indeed a matroid
[
12
,
21
]
, whose rank function
r_{k,\ell}
is given by

r_{k,\ell}(E^{\prime})=\min\{|F|+\sum_{Y\in{\cal Y}}(k|V(Y)|-\ell):F\subseteq E^{\prime},\ {\cal Y}\ \hbox{is a partition of}\ E^{\prime}-F\},

for
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
; see
[
5
, Sections 13.4, 13.5]
.

We can give an alternative formula for
r_{k,\ell}
using vertex partitions instead of edge partitions.
A
cover
of
G=(V,E)
is a collection
{\cal X}=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots X_{t}\}
of subsets of

V
of size at least two for which every edge in
E
is induced by some
X_{i}
.
When
G
is clear from the context we shall also say that
{\cal X}
is a cover of an edge set
J\subseteq E
to mean that
it is a cover of the subgraph
of
G
induced by
J
.
The cover is said to be
k
-thin
, for some integer
k
, if
|X_{i}\cap X_{j}|\leq k
for all

1\leq i<j\leq t
.
For a cover

{\cal X}=\{X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{t}\}
, we define its
value
(with respect to
k
and
\ell
) to be

\val_{k,\ell}({\cal X})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(k|X_{i}|-\ell).

The proof of the following result can be found in the Appendix.

Theorem 2.1
.

The rank of a set
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
of edges in
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G)

is given by

r_{k,\ell}(E^{\prime})=\min\{|F|+\val_{k,\ell}({\cal X})\},

where the minimum is taken over all subsets
F\subseteq E^{\prime}
and all
1
-thin covers
{\cal X}

of
(V,E^{\prime}-F)
.
Furthermore, if
0<\ell\leq k,
then the minimum is attained on a
0
-thin cover of
(V,E^{\prime}-F)
.
If
\ell\leq 0,
then
the minimum is attained on
{\cal X}=\{V(E^{\prime}-F)\}
.

It is clear from the definitions that for any graph
G=(V,E)
on at least two vertices, the rank of
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G)
is at most
k|V|-\ell
.
For convenience, we introduce the following notions. We say that
G
is

•

(k,\ell)
-rigid
if
r_{k,\ell}(E)=k|V|-\ell
;

•

(k,\ell)
-redundant
if
G-e
is
(k,\ell)
-rigid for all
e\in E
;

•

(k,\ell)
-sparse
if
r_{k,\ell}(E)=|E|
, or equivalently, if for any set of vertices
X\subseteq V
of size at least two, the number of edges in
G[X]
is at most
k|X|-\ell
;

•

(k,\ell)
-tight
if it is both
(k,\ell)
-rigid and
(k,\ell)
-sparse, or equivalently, if it is
(k,\ell)
-rigid and
|E|=k|V|-\ell
.

We say that
G
is an
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-circuit
if
E
is a circuit in
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G)
.
Similarly,
G
is said to be

\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected
if
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G)
is connected. The subgraphs induced by the components of
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}(G)
are the
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}
-components
of
G
.

We record the following facts about the behaviour of count matroids under vertex and edge additions, which are straightforward to deduce from the definitions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be integers with
k\geq 1
and
\ell\leq 2k-1
, and let
G
be a graph.

(a)

Let
G^{\prime}
be obtained from
G
by the addition of a vertex incident to
k
edges in such a way that we add no more than
2k-\ell
parallel edges between any two vertices. If
G
is
(k,\ell)
-sparse (
(k,\ell)
-tight, respectively) then
G^{\prime}
is
(k,\ell)
-sparse (
(k,\ell)
-tight, respectively).

(b)

Let
\ell^{\prime}<\ell
be another integer and let
G^{\prime}
be obtained from
G
by the addition of
\ell-\ell^{\prime}
edges (on the same vertex set). If
G
is
(k,\ell)
-tight, then
G^{\prime}
is
(k,\ell^{\prime})
-tight.

The following lemma collects some observations that can be proved by elementary counting arguments.
The complete graph on
n
vertices is denoted by
K_{n}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be integers with
k\geq 1
and
\ell\leq 2k-1
. Then

(a)

if
G=(V,E)
is a simple graph with
2\leq|V|\leq 2k-1
, then
|E|\leq k|V|-\ell
;

(b)

if
k+1\leq\ell\leq 2k-1
, then
K_{n}
is
(k,\ell)
-redundant for all
n\geq 2k
;

(c)

if
C=(V_{C},E_{C})
is an
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-circuit, then
|V_{C}|=2
or
|V_{C}|>k/(2k-\ell)
.

Proof.

(a)

G
is simple, so

|E|\leq|V|(|V|-1)/2
. Moreover, from
\ell\leq 2k-1
we have
k|V|-(2k-1)\leq k|V|-\ell
. Since for fixed
k
,
|V|(|V|-1)/2\leq k|V|-(2k-1)
is a quadratic inequality in
|V|
that holds for
|V|=2
and
|V|=2k-1
, it holds for every
2\leq|V|\leq 2k-1
.

(b)
Suppose that
n\geq 2k
.
Let us first consider
K_{2k-1}
. It is
(k,2k-1)
-sparse by
(a)
, and it has
k(2k-1)-(2k-1)
edges, so it is
(k,2k-1)
-tight. Adding a new vertex of degree
k+(2k-1-\ell)\leq 2k-2
results in a
(k,\ell)
-tight graph on
2k
vertices by Lemma
2.2
(b), since this is the same as adding a new vertex of degree
k
, which results in a
(k,2k-1)
-tight graph, and then adding
2k-1-\ell
additional edges. Note that it is a proper subgraph of
K_{2k}
. By adding

n-2k
additional vertices of degree
k
, we obtain a
(k,\ell)
-tight spanning (proper) subgraph of
K_{n}
,
c.f. Lemma
2.2
(a).
By symmetry, this shows that
K_{n}-e
is
(k,\ell)
-rigid for any edge
e\in E(K_{n})
, and consequently
K_{n}
is
(k,\ell)
-redundant.

(c)
If
|V_{C}|\leq 2
, then we are done (recall that we do not allow loops in our graphs), so we may suppose that
|V_{C}|\geq 3
. If
k/(2k-\ell)\leq 2
, then, again, we are done, so let us suppose that
k/(2k-\ell)>2
, which is equivalent to
\ell>\frac{3}{2}k
. First, observe that a pair of vertices with
2k-\ell+1
parallel edges between them form an
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-circuit. It follows that between any pair of vertices in
C
there are at most
2k-\ell
parallel edges. Thus, it suffices to show that the graph
(2k-\ell)K_{V_{C}}
, consisting of the vertex set
V_{C}
and
2k-\ell
parallel edges between each pair of vertices in
V_{C}
, is
(k,\ell)
-sparse whenever
|V_{C}|\leq k/(2k-\ell)
. This follows from a similar calculation as in part
(a)
, as follows. Let
(V_{0},E_{0})
be a subgraph of
(2k-\ell)K_{V_{C}}
. We have
|E_{0}|\leq(2k-\ell)|V_{0}|(|V_{0}|-1)/2
, so it suffices to prove

(2k-\ell)\frac{|V_{0}|(|V_{0}|-1)}{2}\leq k|V_{0}|-\ell.

This is a quadratic inequality in
|V_{0}|
, so it is enough to show that it holds for
|V_{0}|=2
and
|V_{0}|=k/(2k-\ell)
. The former is immediate. For the latter, observe that after substitution, rearranging, and multiplying by two we obtain the inequality

(2\ell-k)(2k-\ell)\leq k^{2}.

Since the mapping

f:x\mapsto(2x-k)(2k-x)

is a quadratic function with
f(\frac{3}{2}k)=k^{2}
and
f^{\prime}(x)<0
for all
x>\frac{5}{4}k
,
f(\ell)\leq k^{2}
holds whenever
\ell\geq\frac{3}{2}k
, as desired.
∎

Finally, the following lemma captures an important property of count matroids. Since we could not find a proof in the literature, we provide one.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a nontrivial

{\cal M}_{k,\ell}
-component of a graph
G
.
Then
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
and
H
is
(k,\ell)
-redundant.

Proof.

We start by showing that any
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-circuit
C=(V_{C},E_{C})
is
(k,\ell)
-rigid. Indeed, since
C
is not
(k,\ell)
-sparse, there must be a subset of vertices
X\subseteq V_{C}
of size at least two such that
C[X]
has at least
k|X|-\ell+1
edges. Deleting any edge of
C
results in a
(k,\ell)
-sparse graph, so we must have
X=V_{C}
and
|E_{C}|=k|V_{C}|-\ell+1
. Since
r_{k,\ell}(C)=|E_{C}|-1
,
C
is
(k,\ell)
-rigid, as claimed. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(C)
does not contain bridges, so
C
is, in fact,
(k,\ell)
-redundant.

Next, we show that if a graph
G_{0}
with at least two edges is
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected, then adding any edge
e
with end vertices
u
and
v
to
G_{0}
(possibly parallel to an existing edge of
G_{0}
) also results in an
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected graph; in other words,
e
is not a bridge of
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G_{0}+e)
. Indeed, let
f,f^{\prime}
be (not necessarily distinct) edges of
G_{0}
incident to
u
and
v
, respectively. Since
G_{0}
is
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected, we can find an
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-circuit
C
in
G_{0}
that contains
f
and
f^{\prime}
. In particular,
C
spans
u
and
v
. Now
C
is
(k,\ell)
-rigid, so we must have
r_{k,\ell}(C)=r_{k,\ell}(C+e)
. It follows that
e
is contained in an
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-circuit
C_{0}
of
C+e
. Since
C_{0}
is also an
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-circuit of
G_{0}+e
,
e
is not a bridge in
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G_{0}+e)
.

Now if
H=(V_{H},E_{H})
is a nontrivial
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-component of a graph
G
, then the above argument shows that
G[V_{H}]
is
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected. By the maximality of
H
, we must have
H=G[V_{H}]
, so
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
. We would like to show that it is also
(k,\ell)
-redundant. Again, it is enough to show that
H
is
(k,\ell)
-rigid, since combined with
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connectivity this implies that
H
is
(k,\ell)
-redundant.
Moreover, it follows from the argument used in the first part of the proof that adding edges to
H
does not increase its rank. Thus, it suffices to show that there is a
(k,\ell)
-rigid supergraph of
H
on the vertex set
V_{H}
, which, in turn, is equivalent to showing that there exists a
(k,\ell)
-tight graph on
V_{H}
.

Now if
|V_{H}|=2
, then
2k-\ell
parallel edges between the two vertices form a
(k,\ell)
-tight graph, and we are done. If

Let

As the definition of this matroid is rather technical and not directly relevant for our purposes,

Throughout the rest of the paper we shall adopt the simpler notation

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let

2
-thin cover of

The family

Theorem 2.5
.

[

Let

-thin covers

Theorem 2.6

Let

Since we know that

3
Count matroids of highly connected graphs

In this section we give sufficient conditions for a graph to be
(k,\ell)
-rigid. There are three distinct subcases depending on the value of
\ell
in relation to
k
, the third of which is significantly more difficult than the other two. First, when
\ell\leq 0
, then we only need a bound on the minimum degree to ensure
(k,\ell)
-rigidity, and a slightly higher bound also gives
(k,\ell)
-redundancy. In the
0<\ell\leq k
case we show that if a graph is
2k
-edge-connected, then it is
(k,\ell)
-redundant. As we shall see, this follows quickly from (a slight extension of) the result of Tutte and Nash-Williams that
2k
-edge-connected graphs contain
k
edge-disjoint spanning trees, that is, are
(k,k)
-rigid. Finally, for
k<\ell\leq 2k-1
we show that
2\ell
-connected graphs are
(k,\ell)
-redundant, an extension of the Lovász–Yemini-theorem. In all three cases we also give conditions that ensure
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connectivity.

3.1
The

\ell\leq 0

and

0<\ell\leq k

cases

For the
\ell\leq 0
case, we shall give an argument using orientations of graphs. We say that an orientation
\vec{G}
of

is

if for each vertex
v

differ by at most
1
. It is well-known (see, for example,
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.8]
)
that every graph has a smooth orientation, which can be obtained by adding a perfect matching between the vertices of odd degree, taking an Eulerian orientation of the resulting graph and restricting this orientation to the original edges of the graph.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

and
\ell
be integers such that

and
\ell\leq 0

is
(k,\ell)
-rigid. Moreover, if each degree is at least
2k-\frac{2\ell-2}{|V|}

is
(k,\ell)
-redundant.

Proof.

We first show that
G
has a
(k,0)
-tight spanning subgraph

. Take a smooth orientation

of

. Since the degree of each vertex in
G
is at least

\vec{G}
is at least
k
. It follows that we can find a spanning subdigraph

k
. The underlying undirected graph
G_{0}
of

is
(k,0)
-sparse and has
k|V|
edges, so it is
(k,0)
-tight.

By adding up the degree of each vertex in
G
we have
2|E|\geq 2k|V|-2\ell

. Now we can add
-\ell

to

to obtain a spanning
(k,\ell)
-tight subgraph of

, which shows that

is
(k,\ell)
-rigid (c.f. Lemma
2.2
(b)).

Furthermore, if the degree of each vertex in

is at least

of

and

(k,\ell)
-rigid for each edge

(k,\ell)
-redundant in this case.
∎

|F|+\val_{k,\ell}({\cal X})\leq k|V|-\ell-1,

which implies, by Theorem
2.1
, that
G
is not
(k,\ell)
-rigid (and hence not
(k,\ell)
-redundant).

We can also prove the following strenghtening of Theorem
3.7
using standard methods, see e.g.,
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
2\leq k<\ell\leq 2k-1
be two positive integers and let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. If
G
is
2\ell
-connected, then
G
is
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}
-connected.

Proof.

It is enough to prove for simple graphs, since passing to the underlying simple graph preserves vertex-connectivity, while it follows from Lemma
2.4
that adding parallel edges preserves the property of being
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
G
is not
{\cal M}_{k,\ell}
-connected and let
H_{1},\dots,H_{q}
be the

{\cal M}_{k,\ell}
-components of
G
.
Each
H_{i}
is nontrivial, since
G
is
(k,\ell)
-redundant by Theorem
3.7
.
Let
X_{i}=V(H_{i})-\bigcup_{j\neq i}V(H_{j})
and
let
Y_{i}=V(H_{i})-X_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq q
. We have
|V|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|X_{i}|+|\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{q}Y_{i}|
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|Y_{i}|\geq 2|\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{q}Y_{i}|
, which gives
|V|\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|X_{i}|+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|Y_{i}|
.

It follows from Lemma
2.3
(a)
that

|V(H_{i})|\geq 2k
for
1\leq i\leq q
.
The
2\ell
-connectivity of
G
implies that
|Y_{i}|\geq 2\ell
or
X_{i}=\varnothing
must hold for each
Y_{i}
; thus in both cases we have

|Y_{i}|\geq 2k
for every
1\leq i\leq q
.

Since
G
is
(k,\ell)
-rigid, it has a spanning
(k,\ell)
-tight subgraph
(V,B)
.
Let
B_{i}=B\cap E(H_{i})
,
for
i=1,\dots,q
. Thus
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{q}B_{i}=B
.
Note that
B_{i}
is a base of
H_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq q
.
By using the above inequalities and Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle k|V|-\ell

\displaystyle=|\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{q}B_{i}|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|B_{i}|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}(k|V(H_{i})|-\ell)=k\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|X_{i}|+k\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|Y_{i}|-q\ell

\displaystyle=k\Bigl(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|X_{i}|+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|Y_{i}|\Bigr)+\frac{k}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|Y_{i}|-q\ell\geq k|V|+\frac{k}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}|Y_{i}|-q\ell

\displaystyle\geq k|V|+\frac{k\cdot q\cdot 2k}{2}-q\ell>k|V|,

where the last inequality follows from
2\leq k<\ell\leq 2k-1
.
This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

We close this section by highlighting the following “basis packing” reformulation of Theorem
3.7
. The
(k,\ell)=(2,3)
case of this result can be found in
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
k,\ell
and
t
be positive integers with
2\leq k<\ell\leq 2k-1
and let
G
be a graph. If
G
is
(2\ell\cdot t)
-connected, then it contains
t
edge-disjoint
(k,\ell)
-rigid spanning subgraphs.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.7
,
G
is
(kt,\ell t)
-rigid. The key observation is that
\mathcal{M}_{kt,\ell t}(G)
is precisely the
t
-fold union of
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
. This follows from Theorem
2.1
and (
1
); since the proof is simple and analogous to that of

2.6
, we omit it. It follows that

contains
t
edge-disjoint

-tight (and thus

-rigid) spanning subgraphs.
∎

4
Cofactor matroids of highly connected graphs

In this section we consider properties of
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
, the
t
-fold union of the generic three-dimensional cofactor matroid of
G
. In particular, we show that if
G=(V,E)
is
12t
-connected, then
r_{t}(E)=3t|V|-6t
(i.e.,
G
is “
\mathcal{C}^{t}
-rigid”). We also show that if
G
is sufficiently highly connected, then
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
has high vertical connectivity, and conversely, if
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
is sufficiently highly vertically connected, then
G
has high vertex-connectivity.
Throughout this section we only consider simple graphs.

We say that a bipartition
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
of
E
is
essential
(with respect to
t
) if

\max\{r_{t}(E_{1}),r_{t}(E_{2})\}<3t|V|-6t.

The following lemma is our main technical result in this section. The proof uses ideas from
[
10
, Lemma 5.2.]
and
[
3
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
t
be a positive integer and let
G=(V,E)
be a
12t
-connected graph.
Then for every essential partition

\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
of
E
we have

r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})\geq 3t|V|.

Proof.

Let us suppose, for a contradiction, that the lemma does not hold and
let
G=(V,E)
be a counterexample for which
|V|
is as small as possible and, with respect to this,
|E|
is as large as possible.
Let
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
be an essential partition of
E
for which

r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})<3t|V|.

By Theorem
2.6
, there exist sets
F_{i}\subseteq E_{i}
and

4
-shellable
2
-thin covers
{\cal X}_{i}
of
E_{i}-F_{i}
, consisting of sets of size at least five,
with hinge sets
H_{i}
, for
i=1,2
,
such that

r_{t}(E_{i})=|F_{i}|+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}_{i}}(3t|X|-6t)-t\sum_{h\in H_{i}}(\deg_{{\cal X}_{i}}(h)-1).

Let
{\cal X}={\cal X}_{1}\cup{\cal X}_{2}
and
F=F_{1}\cup F_{2}
.

It follows from the maximality of
|E|
that
G[X]
is complete for every set
X\in{\cal X}
.
This also implies that
F_{i}=E_{i}-\bigcup_{X\in{\cal X}_{i}}K_{X}
, for
i=1,2
, where
K_{X}
denotes
the complete graph on vertex set
X
.

Claim 4.2
.

Each vertex
v\in V
satisfies exactly one of the following.

(i)
v
is contained by at least two sets
X,Y\in{\cal X}
,

(ii)
v
is contained by exactly one set
X\in{\cal X}
, and
v
is incident with at least one edge in
F
,

(iii) no set in
{\cal X}
contains
v
, and
v
is incident with at least
12t
edges in
F
.

Proof.

Since
G
is
12t
-connected, we have
d(v)\geq 12t
. Suppose that
no set in
{\cal X}
contains
v
. Then every edge incident with
v
belongs to
F

and (iii) holds. If
v
is contained by some set in
{\cal X}
then either
(i) holds or
v
is contained by exactly one set, say
X_{0}\in{\cal X}
.
It remains to show that in the latter case

d_{F}(v)\geq 1
, where
d_{F}(v)
is the number of edges in
F
incident with
v
.

For a contradiction suppose
that
d_{F}(v)=0
. By symmetry we may assume that
X_{0}\in{\cal X}_{1}
. This also implies that there is no edge in
E_{2}

incident with
v
. Since
{\cal X}_{1}
covers
E_{1}
and no edge in
F_{1}
is incident with
v
,
we have
|X_{0}|\geq 12t+1
as
X_{0}
must contain
v
and all (at least
12t
) neighbors of
v
in
G
.

Let us consider the case when
G
is a complete graph. In this case
X_{0}=V
must hold and hence
r_{t}(E_{1})=3t|V|-6t
follows by Lemma
2.8
,
which
contradicts the fact that
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
is essential. Hence we may assume that
G
is not complete.
This implies
|V|\geq 12t+2
.

We next show that
\{E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v),E_{2}\}
is an essential partition of the edge set
E-\partial_{G}(v)

of
G-v
. If this is not the case, then one of two possibilities must hold. The first one is that

r_{t}(E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v))=3t|V(G-v)|-6t=3t|V|-6t-3t.

In this case we can use Lemma
2.7
to deduce
that
r_{t}(E_{1})=3t|V|-6t
, a contradiction.
The second possibility is that

r_{t}(E_{2}-\partial_{G}(v))=r_{t}(E_{2})=3t|V(G-v)|-6t.

Since
X_{0}
induces a complete graph on at least
12t+1
vertices in
E_{1}
, Lemma
2.8
implies
r_{t}(E_{1})\geq 3t(12t+1)-6t
. It follows that

r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})\geq 36t^{2}-3t+3t|V|-9t>3t|V|,

a contradiction. This proves that the partition is indeed essential in
G-v
.

We can now complete the proof of the claim.
Since
{\cal X}^{\prime}={\cal X}-\{X_{0}\}\cup\{X_{0}-v\}
covers
E-\partial_{G}(v)-F
, and no hinge of
{\cal X}_{1}
or
{\cal X}_{2}
contains
v
(as
v
is contained by
X_{0}
only),
we have

r_{t}(E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v))+r_{t}(E_{2})\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq|F|+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}^{\prime}}(3t|X|-6t)-t\Bigl(\sum_{h\in H_{1}}(\deg_{{\cal X}_{1}}(h)-1)+\sum_{h\in H_{2}}(\deg_{{\cal X}_{2}}(h)-1)\Bigr)\\
&\leq|F|+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}}(3t|X|-6t)-3t-t\Bigl(\sum_{h\in H_{1}}(\deg_{{\cal X}_{1}}(h)-1)+\sum_{h\in H_{2}}(\deg_{{\cal X}_{2}}(h)-1)\Bigr)\\
&=r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})-3t<3t|V(G-v)|.\end{aligned}

By the minimality of
|V|
this implies that
G-v
is not
12t
-connected. Since
|V|\geq 12t+2
, this implies that
G-v
has a set of vertices
U
of cardinality
at most
12t-1
for which
G-v-U
is disconnected. The graph
G-U
must be connected by the
12t
-connectivity of
G
, which implies that
each connected component of
G-v-U
contains
at least one vertex which is a neighbor of
v
in
G
. But the neighbors of
v
in
G
are all contained in
X
, and
G[X]
is complete,
so there is an edge between each pair of connected components of
G-v-U
, a contradiction.
∎

If a vertex
v\in V
satisfies
(i) (resp. (ii), (iii)) of Claim
4.2
, then we shall say that
v
is of
type
(i) (resp. (ii), (iii)).
Let
H_{i}^{v}
denote those hinges in
H_{i}
which contain
v
, for
i=1,2
.

Claim 4.3
.

Each vertex
v\in V
satisfies

\frac{d_{F}(v)}{2t}+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}:v\in X}(3-\frac{6}{|X|})-\sum_{h\in H_{1}^{v}}\frac{\deg_{{\cal X}_{1}}(h)-1}{2}-\sum_{h\in H_{2}^{v}}\frac{\deg_{{\cal X}_{2}}(h)-1}{2}\geq 3.

Proof.

We have three cases depending on the type of
v
.
Suppose that
v
is of type (i). In this case at least two members of
{\cal X}
contain
v
.
Since
{\cal X}_{1}
and
{\cal X}_{2}
are
4
-shellable, there is an ordering
(X^{1}_{1},\dots,X^{1}_{q_{1}})
of
{\cal X}_{1}

and an ordering
(X^{2}_{1},\dots,X^{2}_{q_{2}})
of
{\cal X}_{2}
for which

|X^{1}_{i_{1}}\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{i_{1}-1}X^{1}_{j}|\leq 4
and
|X^{2}_{i_{2}}\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{i_{2}-1}X^{2}_{j}|\leq 4
hold for
2\leq i_{1}\leq q_{1}
and
2\leq i_{2}\leq q_{2}
.
Let
(Y^{1}_{1},\dots,Y^{1}_{p_{1}})
and
(Y^{2}_{1},\dots,Y^{2}_{p_{2}})
be the inherited ordering of those sets in
{\cal X}_{1}
and
{\cal X}_{2}
, respectively,
that contain
v
.
We may have
p_{i}=0
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
, but
p_{1}+p_{2}\geq 2
follows from the fact that
v
is of type (i).

We assign a non-negative integer
c_{j}^{i}
to each set
Y^{i}_{j}
,
i=1,2
,
1\leq j\leq p_{i}
.
If
Y^{i}_{1}
exists then we put
c^{i}_{1}=0
,
i=1,2
, and we define
c^{i}_{j}=|Y^{i}_{j}\cap\bigcup_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j-1}Y^{i}_{j^{\prime}}|-1
, for
i=1,2
and
2\leq j\leq p_{i}
.
Since the covers are
4
-shellable and
2
-thin, we have
c^{i}_{j}\leq 3
and
c^{i}_{2}\leq 1
, whenever it is defined.
Furthermore, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{p_{i}}c^{i}_{j}=\sum_{h\in H_{i}^{v}}({\deg_{{\cal X}_{i}}(h)-1})

for
i=1,2
. We can now deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{d_{F}(v)}{2t}+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}:v\in X}\bigl(3-\frac{6}{|X|}\bigr)-\sum_{h\in H_{1}^{v}}\frac{\deg_{{\cal X}_{1}}(h)-1}{2}-\sum_{h\in H_{2}^{v}}\frac{\deg_{{\cal X}_{2}}(h)-1}{2}

(5)

\displaystyle\geq 0+\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}}\bigl(3-\frac{6}{|Y_{j}^{1}|}-\frac{c_{j}^{1}}{2}\bigr)+\sum_{j=1}^{p_{2}}\bigl(3-\frac{6}{|Y_{j}^{2}|}-\frac{c_{j}^{2}}{2}\bigr).

Observe that there is at least one member of
{\cal X}
that contains
v
for which the corresponding
c
-value is
0
(since either
Y_{1}^{1}
or
Y_{1}^{2}
must exist) and at least one other for which the corresponding
c
-value is at most
1
(since
p_{1}+p_{2}\geq 2
). For these two sets, call them
X
and
Y
,
the sum of the corresponding terms in (
5
) is at least

(3-\frac{6}{|X|})+(3-\frac{6}{|Y|}-\frac{1}{2})\geq 3-\frac{6}{5}+3-\frac{6}{5}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{31}{10}>3.

For any other set
Z
, the corresponding
c
-value is at most three, and hence the corresponding term in (
5
) is at least

3-\frac{6}{|Z|}-\frac{3}{2}\geq 3-\frac{6}{5}-\frac{3}{2}=\frac{3}{10}>0.

These inequalities imply the claim in the case when
v
is of type (i).

Next suppose that

v
is of type (ii). Then
H_{1}^{v}=H_{2}^{v}=\varnothing
and we have exactly one member of
X\in{\cal X}
that contains
v
. As the degree of
v
is at least
12t
by
the
12t
-connectivity of
G
, we also have
d_{F}(v)+|X|\geq 12t+1
, where
d_{F}(v)\geq 1
and
|X|\geq 5
must hold.
If
|X|\geq 12t
, then we have
\frac{d_{F}(v)}{2t}+(3-\frac{6}{|X|})\geq\frac{1}{2t}+(3-\frac{6}{12t})\geq 3
, as required.
If
5\leq|X|\leq 12t
then we have

\frac{d_{F}(v)}{2t}+(3-\frac{6}{|X|})\geq\frac{12t+1-|X|}{2t}+(3-\frac{6}{|X|})\geq 3,

where the second inequality follows from the fact that this is a quadratic inequality in
|X|
that is satisfied at the two extremes
|X|=5
and
|X|=12t
of the domain, as can be verified by a simple computation.

Finally, if

v
is of type (iii), then we have
\frac{d_{F}(v)}{2t}\geq\frac{12t}{2t}=6>3
, as claimed.
∎

By using Claim
4.3
we can now deduce that

\displaystyle r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})=\begin{aligned} &|F|+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}_{1}}(3t|X|-6t)-t\sum_{h\in H_{1}}(\deg_{{\cal X}_{1}}(h)-1)\\
&+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}_{2}}(3t|X|-6t)-t\sum_{h\in H_{2}}(\deg_{{\cal X}_{2}}(h)-1)\end{aligned}

\displaystyle=t\sum_{v\in V}\Big(\frac{d_{F}(v)}{2t}+\sum_{X\in{\cal X}:v\in X}\big(3-\frac{6}{|X|}\big)-\sum_{h\in H_{1}^{v}}\frac{\deg_{{\cal X}_{1}}(h)-1}{2}-\sum_{h\in H_{2}^{v}}\frac{\deg_{{\cal X}_{2}}(h)-1}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq 3t|V|,

a contradiction. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We have the following corollary, which extends
[
3
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Corollary 4.4
.

If
G=(V,E)
is
12t
-connected for some positive integer
t
then

r_{t}(E)=3t|V|-6t
.

Proof.

Suppose that
r_{t}(E)<3t|V|-6t
. Then
\{E,\varnothing\}
is an essential partition and hence Lemma
4.1
implies

r_{t}(E)=r_{t}(E)+r_{t}(\varnothing)\geq 3t|V|
, a contradiction.
∎

We can now deduce a strengthening of Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
t
be a positive integer and let
G=(V,E)
be a
k
-connected graph with
k\geq 12t
.
Then for every essential partition

\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
of
E
we have

r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})\geq 3t|V|+k-12t.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on
k
.
By Lemma
4.1
the statement holds for
k=12t
, so we may assume that

k\geq 12t+1
. Let
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
be an essential partition of
E
.
Since
r_{t}(E)=3t|V|-6t
by Corollary
4.4
, we have
E_{1},E_{2}\neq\varnothing
.
Hence there exists a vertex

v\in V(E_{1})\cap V(E_{2})
.

Suppose first that
\{E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v),E_{2}-\partial_{G}(v)\}
is not an essential partition of
G-v
. This means that either
r_{t}(E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v))
or
r_{t}(E_{2}-\partial_{G}(v))
is equal to
3t|V(G-v)|-6t
; by symmetry, we may assume that it is the former. It follows that
d_{E_{1}}(v)<3t
, for otherwise Lemma
2.7
would imply that

r_{t}(E_{1})\geq r_{t}(E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v))+3t=3t|V|-6t
, contradicting our assumption that
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
is essential.
Observe that
r_{t}(\partial_{G}(v))=d_{G}(v)
by Lemma
2.7
.
Using Lemma
2.7
again we obtain

r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})\geq r_{t}(E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v))+d_{E_{1}}(v)+d_{E_{2}}(v)\geq 3t|V(G-v)|-6t+k=3t|V|-9t+k,

which is stronger than the bound we set out to prove.

Now let us consider the case when
\{E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v),E_{2}-\partial_{G}(v)\}
is essential.
Then by the induction hypothesis we have

r_{t}(E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v))+r_{t}(E_{2}-\partial_{G}(v))\geq 3t|V(G-v)|+k-1-12t.

Note that
d_{G}(v)\geq k\geq 3t+1
, and that by the choice of
v
,
d_{E_{1}}(v)
and
d_{E_{2}}(v)
are both non-zero. These observations together with Lemma
2.7
imply that

r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})\geq r_{t}(E_{1}-\partial_{G}(v))+r_{t}(E_{2}-\partial_{G}(v))+3t+1\geq 3t|V|+k-12t,

as desired.
∎

We are ready to prove the main results of this section.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
k
-connected graph with

. Then

\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)

is vertically
(k-6t+1)
-connected.

Proof.

Suppose that
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
is a vertical
c
-separation of
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
for some
c\leq k-6t
.
Then

\max\{r_{t}(E_{1}),r_{t}(E_{2})\}<r_{t}(E)=3t|V|-6t,

where the equality follows from
Corollary
4.4
.
Hence this partition of
E
is essential.
Therefore, Lemma
4.5
yields
r_{t}(E_{1})+r_{t}(E_{2})\geq 3t|V|+k-12t\geq 3t|V|-6t+c
which contradicts
the definition of a
c
-separation.
∎

Applying Theorem

Theorem 4.7

Let

\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)

(6t+2)

By Theorem
2.6

-thin cover
{\cal X}
of

It follows that if

{\cal X}=\{V\}

r_{t}(E)=3t|V|-6t
, as required.

Let us assume, for a contradiction, that
|{\cal X}|\geq 2
. Consider the last set
X_{q}
of a
4

{\cal X}
. Since
|X_{q}\cap\bigcup({\cal X}-\{X_{q}\}|\leq 4
, at most
\binom{4}{2}=6
hinges are
incident with
X_{q}
. Therefore

t\sum_{h\in H({\cal X}-\{X_{q}\})}(\deg_{{\cal X}-\{X_{q}\}}(h)-1)\geq t\sum_{h\in H({\cal X})}(\deg_{{\cal X}}(h)-1)-6t.

Thus we obtain, by considering
\mathcal{X}-X_{q}
and using Theorem
2.6
, that

\displaystyle r_{t}(E

\displaystyle-E(X_{q}))\leq\sum_{X\in{\cal X}-\{X_{q}\}}(3t|X|-6t)-t\sum_{h\in H({\cal X}-\{X_{q}\})}(\deg_{{\cal X}-\{X_{q}\}}(h)-1)

(6)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{X\in{\cal X}}(3t|X|-6t)-t\sum_{h\in H({\cal X})}(\deg_{{\cal X}}(h)-1)+6t-(3t|X_{q}|-6t)

\displaystyle\leq r_{t}(E)+6t-(3t|X_{q}|-6t).

On the other hand,
r_{t}(E(X_{q}))\leq 3t|X_{q}|-6t
. Hence
r_{t}(E-E(X_{q}))+r_{t}(E(X_{q}))\leq r_{t}(E)+6t
.
It follows that if
\min(r_{t}(E-E(X_{q})),r_{t}(E(X_{q})))\geq 6t+1
then

\{E-E(X_{q}),E(X_{q})\}
is a
(6t+1)
-separation
of
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
, contradicting the fact that
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)

is vertically
(6t+2)
-connected.
So in the rest of the proof we may assume that at least
one of
r_{t}(E-E(X_{q}))
and
r_{t}(E(X_{q}))
is at most
6t

Since
|{\cal X}|\geq 2
, there is a set
Y\in{\cal X}-X_{q}
. We have
|Y|\geq 5
and
|X_{q}\cap Y|\leq 2

Thus
|X_{q}|\leq|V|-3
. Also, from Lemma

and the fact that

This implies, using Lemma

contains

\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
. Hence

r_{t}(E-E(X_{q}))\geq 9t-1\geq 6t+1
. Therefore we must have
r_{t}(E(X_{q}))\leq 6t
.

If
3t+1\leq r_{t}(E(X_{q}))\leq 6t
, then we have, by using the upper bound on
r_{t}(E-E(X_{q}))
deduced earlier,
that

r_{t}(E-E(X_{q}))+r_{t}(E(X_{q}))\leq r_{t}(E)+6t-(3t|X_{q}|-6t)+6t\leq r_{t}(E)+3t,

which implies that

If

r_{t}(E-E(X_{q}))+r_{t}(E(X_{q}))\leq r_{t}(E)+6t-(3t|X_{q}|-6t)+3t\leq r_{t}(E).

Note that

5
Applications

In this section we use the results of the previous sections to obtain analogues of Whitney’s theorem for
(k,\ell)
-count matroids and for the
C_{2}^{1}
-cofactor matroid. Our proofs follow the basic strategy of the proof of Whitney’s theorem due to Edmonds, see
[
18
, Lemma 5.3.2]
. We also pose a strengthening of a conjecture of Kriesell, and prove a special case of both of these conjectures.

5.1
Recovering graphs from count matroids

Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be graphs and let
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
be a function. We say that
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism
if there is a graph isomorphism
\varphi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
such that for every edge
uv\in E,
the endvertices of
\psi(uv)
in
H
are
\varphi(u)
and
\varphi(v)
.
The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G=(V,E),H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be graphs without isolated vertices and let
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
be a bijection that “sends stars to stars”, that is, for every
v\in V
there is a vertex
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
such that
\psi(\partial_{G}(v))=\partial_{H}(v^{\prime})
. Then
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism.

For a matroid
{\cal M}=(E,r)
, we say that a subset
F\subseteq E
is a
k
-hyperplane
, for some integer
k\geq 0
, if
r(F)=r(E)-k
and
F
is closed, i.e.,
r(F+e)=r(F)+1
for every
e\in E-F
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
k,\ell
be integers with
k\geq 1
and
\ell\leq 2k-1
, and let
c_{k,\ell}\geq 2
be an integer such that every
c_{k,\ell}
-connected graph is
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected. Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be graphs and
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
an isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(H)
. If
G
is
(c_{k,\ell}+1)
-connected and
H
is without isolated vertices, then
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
we have
r(E)=k|V|-\ell
, and similarly,
r(E^{\prime})=k|V^{\prime}|-\ell
. Hence
|V|=|V^{\prime}|
.
Fix
v\in V
and consider
F=E-\partial_{G}(v)
. Since
F
is the edge set of an induced subgraph of
G
,
F
is closed in
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G)
. Moreover, since
G-v
is
c_{k,\ell}
-connected,
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G-v)
is connected, so by Lemma
2.4
we have
r(F)=k(|V|-1)-\ell=r(E)-k
. This shows that
F
is a connected
k
-hyperplane in
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G)
, and consequently
\psi(F)
is a connected
k
-hyperplane in
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(H)
. By Lemma
2.4
again this means that

k|V(\psi(F))|-\ell=r(\psi(F))=r(E^{\prime})-k=r(E)-k=k(|V|-1)-\ell,

so
\psi(F)
is the edge set of an induced subgraph of
H
on
|V^{\prime}|-1
vertices, and hence it is the complement of a vertex star. This shows that
\psi
maps complements of vertex stars to complements of vertex stars. Since
\psi
is a bijection, it also follows that it maps vertex stars to vertex stars. Now Lemma
5.1
implies that
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism, as desired.
∎

In the case of the graphic matroid we can choose
c_{1,1}=2
, and Theorem
5.2
reduces to Whitney’s theorem.
Similarly, by putting
c_{2,3}=6
, we can reproduce
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
.
Combining Theorem
5.2
with Corollaries
3.5
,
3.6
and
3.11
, we obtain the following generalization.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
k,\ell
be integers with
k\geq 1
and
\ell\leq 2k-1
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and suppose that one of the following holds:

(a)

\ell\leq 0
and
G
is
(c+1)
-connected, where
c=\lceil 2k-(2\ell-2)/|V|\rceil
;

(b)

k\geq\ell>0
and
G
is
(2k+1)
-connected;

(c)

2\leq k<\ell
and
G
is
(2\ell+1)
-connected.

Let
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be a graph without isolated vertices. If a function
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
is an isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(H)
, then it is induced by a graph isomorphism.

We note that in the
\ell\leq 0
case of Corollary
5.3
, it would be enough to suppose that
G
is
2
-connected and the degree of each vertex is at
least
2k+1-(2\ell-2)/|V|
, provided that
G
is simple. The proof, which we omit, is analogous to that of Theorem
5.2
.

For the bicircular matroid
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G)
, Corollary
5.3
implies that if
G
is
4
-connected, then
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G)
uniquely determines
G
. Note that
2
-connectivity does not suffice: for example, a cycle and a path with the same number of edges are non-isomorphic graphs with isomorphic bicircular matroids. In fact, a result of Wagner
[
20
]
implies that
3
-connectivity suffices. We give an alternative proof that is similar to our proof of Theorem
5.2
. We shall rely on the following result.

Lemma 5.4
.

[
14
, Proposition 2.4]

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected graph. Then
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G)
is connected if and only if
G
is not a cycle and does not contain vertices of degree one.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be graphs and
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
an isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(H)
. If
G
is
3
-connected with at least five vertices and
H
is without isolated vertices, then
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.2
, we must have
|V|=|V^{\prime}|
, and to each vertex
v\in V
such that
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G-v)
is connected we can associate a vertex
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
such that
\psi(\partial_{G}(v))=\partial_{H}(v^{\prime})
. If
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G-v)
is connected for every
v\in V
, then we are done by Lemma
5.1
. Thus we may assume that there is a vertex
v\in V
for which
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G-v)
is not connected. By Lemma
5.4
and the assumption that
G
is
3
-connected,
G-v
must be a cycle. Therefore
G
is isomorphic to the wheel graph
W_{n}
for
n=|V|\geq 5
. It follows from Lemma
5.4
that
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}(G-u)
is connected for every vertex
u\in(V-v)
. As before, this implies that
\psi
sends
\partial_{G}(u)
to some vertex star
\partial_{H}(\varphi(u))
for all
u\in(V-v)
. The function
\varphi:(V-v)\rightarrow V^{\prime}
defined in this way is injective, so there is a unique vertex
v^{\prime}\in(V^{\prime}-\text{im}(\varphi))
and we have
\psi(\partial_{G}(v))=\partial_{H}(v^{\prime})
. Now Lemma
5.1
implies, again, that
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism.
∎

In the
k<\ell
case the bound given by Corollary
5.3
(c) is almost tight, in the sense that there exist
(2\ell-1)
-connected graphs that are not uniquely determined by their count matroids. Indeed, let
G
be a
(2\ell-1)
-connected graph that is not
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-connected (we gave a construction for such a graph in Section
3
), and let
(E_{1},E_{2})
be a vertical
1
-separation of
G
. Consider the graph
H
obtained as the disjoint union of
G[E_{1}]
and
G[E_{2}]
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(H)
are isomorphic under the natural edge bijection between
G
and
H
, but the graphs are nonisomorphic. Similar examples can be constructed for the cases when
\ell\leq k
; we omit the details.

5.2
Recovering graphs from cofactor matroids

We can also prove an analogue of Whitney’s theorem for the
t
-fold union of the
C_{2}^{1}
-cofactor matroid.
Again, we stress that the analogue of Lemma
2.4
does not hold for this matroid:
in particular, the connectivity of
\mathcal{C}(G)
does not imply the “
\mathcal{C}
-rigidity” of
G
. To overcome this difficulty,
we shall use our results on (unions of) cofactor matroids with high vertical connectivity.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
t
be a positive integer and
let
G=(V,E),H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be graphs and
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
an isomorphism between
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
and
\mathcal{C}^{t}(H)
. If
G
is
(12t+2)
-connected and
H
is without isolated vertices, then
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism.

Proof.

Fix
v\in V
and consider
F=E-\partial_{G}(v)
. Let
H_{0}
denote the subgraph of
H
induced by
\psi(F)
; note that
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G-v)
and
\mathcal{C}^{t}(H_{0})
are isomorphic. Since
G
is
(12t+2)
-connected and
G-v
is
(12t+1)
-connected,
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
is vertically
(6t+3)
-connected and
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G-v)
is vertically
(6t+2)
-connected by Theorem
4.6
, which imply
r_{t}(E)=3t|V|-6t
and
r_{t}(F)=3t(|V|-1)-6t=r_{t}(E)-3t
by Theorem
4.7
. It follows that

3t|V(H_{0})|-6t=r_{t}(\psi(F))=r_{t}(F)=r_{t}(E)-3t=r_{t}(E^{\prime})-3t=3t|V^{\prime}|-6t-3t,

where the first and last equalities follow from Theorem
4.7
, applied to
\mathcal{C}^{t}(H_{0})
and to
\mathcal{C}^{t}(H)
, respectively. Thus, we have
|V(H_{0})|=|V^{\prime}|-1
.
Also, since
F
is the edge set of an induced subgraph of
G
, it is closed in
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
, and thus
\psi(F)
is closed in
\mathcal{C}^{t}(H)
. It follows that
H_{0}
is an induced subgraph of
H
, since
r_{t}(\psi(F))=3t|V(H_{0})|-6t
implies that adding edges induced by
V(H_{0})
to
\psi(F)
cannot increase the rank of the latter.

To summarize,
\psi(F)
is the edge set of an induced subgraph of
H
on
|V^{\prime}|-1
vertices, which implies that it is the complement of a vertex star. This shows that
\psi
maps complements of vertex stars to complements of vertex stars. Since
\psi
is a bijection, it also follows that it maps vertex stars to vertex stars. Now Lemma
5.1
implies that
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism, as desired.
∎

Again, the bound given by Theorem
5.6
is not far from being tight, at least for
t=1
: there are examples of
11
-connected graphs that are not determined by their
C_{2}^{1}
-cofactor matroids. As in the case of count matroids, we can take an
11
-connected graph
G
for which
\mathcal{C}(G)
is not connected. An example of such a graph was constructed by Lovász and Yemini
[
13
]
. Now consider a
1
-separation
(E_{1},E_{2})
of
\mathcal{C}(G)
and observe that the disjoint union of
G[E_{1}]
and
G[E_{2}]
has the same cofactor matroid as
G
, but is not isomorphic to
G
.

In order to deduce a Whitney-type result in terms of the vertical connectivity of
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
, rather than the connectivity of
G
, we
need the converse of Theorem
4.6
. Since the proof of the corresponding result for count matroids (the converse of
Theorem
5.2
) is very similar,
we prove the two statements simultaneously.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
c\geq 5
be an integer and let
G=(V,E)
be a graph on at least
c+2
vertices, without isolated vertices.
Let
{\cal M}=(E,r)
be either
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}(G)
, for some integers
k\geq 1
and
\ell\leq 2k-1
, or
\mathcal{C}^{t}(G)
, for some integer
t\geq 1
.
In the latter case put

k=3t
and
\ell=6t
.
If
{\cal M}

is vertically
(k(c-1)-\ell+2)
-connected, then
G
is
c
-connected.

Proof.

We have
r(E)=k|V|-\ell
by Lemma
2.4
or Theorem
4.7
.
For a contradiction suppose that there is a set

S\subset V
with
|S|\leq k-1
for which

G-S
is disconnected. By adding vertices to
S
we may suppose that
|S|=c-1
. Let
C
be the vertex set of some
connected component of
G-S
.
We define a partition
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
of
E
by letting

E_{1}=E(G[C])\cup\partial_{G}(C)
and
E_{2}=E(G[V-C])
.

Since
E_{1}\subseteq G[C\cup S]
and
|C\cup S|,|V-C|\geq c\geq 3
, we have

r(E_{1})\leq k|C\cup S|-\ell
and
r(E_{2})\leq k|V-C|-\ell
. Hence

r(E_{1})+r(E_{2})\leq k|V|-\ell+k|S|-\ell=r(E)+k|S|-\ell.

(7)

Let
w=\min\{r(E_{1}),r(E_{2})\}
.
First suppose that
w\geq k|S|-\ell+1
.
Then
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
is a
(k|S|-\ell+1)
-separation by
(
7
). Since

k|S|-\ell+1=k(c-1)-\ell+1
, this contradicts the assumption
on the vertical connectivity of
{\cal M}
.

Thus we may suppose that there is an integer
z
with
1\leq z\leq|S|
for which

k(|S|-z+1)-\ell\geq w\geq k(|S|-z)-\ell+1

holds. By definition, we have

or
w=r(E_{2})
. In the
latter case we obtain

\displaystyle r(E_{1})+r(E_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k|C\cup S|-\ell+k(|S|-z+1)-\ell

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k|V|-\ell+k(|S|-z+1)-\ell-k|V-C-S|\leq r(E)+k(|S|-z)-\ell,

using that
|V-C-S|\geq 1
.
Therefore
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
is a vertical
(k(c-z)-\ell+1)
-separation,
contradicting our assumption on the vertical connectivity of
{\cal M}
.
In the former case a similar count (using
r(E_{2})\leq k|V-C|-\ell

and

) leads to the same contradiction.
∎

In the proof of Theorem

5.3
Removable spanning trees

The following conjecture is due to Matthias Kriesell (see
[
15
, Problem 444]
).

Conjecture 5.9
.

For every positive integer
k
there exists a (smallest) integer
f(k)
such that every
f(k)
-connected
graph
G
contains a spanning tree
T
for which
G-E(T)
is
k
-connected.

It follows from Theorem
3.2
that
f(1)=4
.
The bound
f(2)\leq 12
was obtained in

[
9
]
, which was subsequently improved to
f(2)\leq 8
in
[
2
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, the conjecture is still open for
k\geq 3
.
In fact, in both the
k=1
and
k=2
cases a much stronger statement is true, which we put forward as a conjecture for general
k
.

Conjecture 5.10
.

For every positive integer
k
there exists a (smallest) integer
f^{*}(k)
such that for every positive integer
t
, every
(t\cdot f^{*}(k))
-connected graph contains
t
edge-disjoint
k
-connected spanning subgraphs.

With this notation, we have
f^{*}(1)=2
, while
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
asserts that
f^{*}(2)\leq 6
. By using our results on cofactor matroids we now settle the
k=3
case of Conjecture
5.10
, and thus of Conjecture
5.9
. The proof is analogous to the proof of the
k=2
case in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 5.11
.

Every
12t
-connected graph contains
t
edge-disjoint
3
-connected spanning subgraphs. In other words,
f^{*}(3)\leq 12
. It follows that
f(3)\leq 24
.

Proof.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
12t
-connected graph. Corollary
4.4
implies that
r_{t}(E)=3t|V|-6t
. This means that
G
contains
t
edge-disjoint subgraphs
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
with
r_{1}(E(H_{i}))=3|V|-6
for
i=1,\ldots,t
. Since
r_{1}(E(H_{i}))\leq 3|V(H_{i})|-6
also holds, we must have
V=V(H_{i})
, that is,
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
are spanning subgraphs. Finally, it is known
that
r_{1}(E(H_{i}))=3|V(H_{i})|-6
implies that
H_{i}
is

-connected (it follows from
[

, Lemma 10.2.4]
, or it
can also be verified using Theorem
2.5
).
∎

Extrapolating from the above bounds on
f^{*}(k)
for
k\leq 3
, perhaps

holds. A natural strategy to prove Conjecture
5.10
is to prove a “basis packing result” similar to Theorem
4.4
for some matroid
\mathcal{M}(K_{n})
on the edge set of the complete graph

in which spanning sets are

-connected.
The family of
abstract

-rigidity matroids
has this property. However, for

there is no known example of an abstract

-rigidity matroid where a good characterization of the rank function is available. In this context we wish to highlight
[

, Conjecture 8.1]
, which, if true, would give such an example. It is conceivable that a proof of this conjecture, combined with the methods of
[
3
]
and this paper, would lead to a proof of Conjecture

.

Lemma A.

Let

be integers with

\ell\leq 2k-1
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
suppose that

m=\min\{\sum_{Y\in{\cal Y}}(k|V(Y)|-\ell):{\cal Y}\ \hbox{is a partition of}\ E\}.

Then

(a)

there exists a
1
-thin cover
\mathcal{X}
of
G
for which

\val_{k,\ell}({\cal X})=m
holds. Furthermore,

(b)

if
0<\ell\leq k
, then there exists a
0
-thin cover
{\cal X}
of
G
with
\val_{k,\ell}({\cal X})=m
, and

(c)

if
\ell\leq 0
, then we have
m=k|V(E)|-\ell
, that is,
\mathcal{X}=\{V(E)\}
is a minimizing cover.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a partition of
E
and suppose that there are members
Y_{i},Y_{j}\in\mathcal{Y}
with
|V(Y_{i})\cap V(Y_{j})|\geq 2
. Then we have

k|V(Y_{i})|-\ell+k|V(Y_{j})|-\ell=k|V(Y_{i})\cup V(Y_{j})|-\ell+k|V(Y_{i})\cap V(Y_{j})|-\ell=

k|V(Y_{i}\cup Y_{j})|-\ell+k|V(Y_{i})\cap V(Y_{j})|-\ell>k|V(Y_{i}\cup Y_{j})|-\ell,

where the last inequality follows from
\ell<2k
. Thus for
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Y}-Y_{i}-Y_{j}+(Y_{i}\cup Y_{j})
we have

\sum_{Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}(k|V(Y^{\prime})|-\ell<\sum_{Y\in{\cal Y}}(k|V(Y)|-\ell).

It follows that if
\mathcal{Y}
is a minimizing partition, then
|V(Y_{i})\cap V(Y_{j})|\leq 1
holds for every pair of members
Y_{i},Y_{j}\in\mathcal{Y}
, and thus
\mathcal{X}=\{V(Y):Y\in\mathcal{Y}\}
is a
1
-thin cover of
G
with
m=\val_{k,\ell}(\mathcal{X})
.

Parts
(b)
and
(c)
can be deduced similarly by observing that in the
\ell\leq k
case, we have

k|V(Y_{i}\cup Y_{j})|-\ell+k|V(Y_{i})\cap V(Y_{j})|-\ell\geq k|V(Y_{i}\cup Y_{j})|-\ell

for
Y_{i},Y_{j}\in\mathcal{Y}
whenever
|V(Y_{i})\cap V(Y_{j})|\geq 1
, and in the
\ell\leq 0
case the same inequality holds for all pairs
Y_{i},Y_{j}\in\mathcal{Y}
.
∎

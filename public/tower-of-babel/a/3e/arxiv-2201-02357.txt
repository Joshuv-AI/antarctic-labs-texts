Ghost instability in the teleparallel gravity model with parity violations
Mingzhe Li, Zhihao Li, Haomin Rao

Abstract
In this paper we consider the parity violating gravity model within the framework of teleparallel gravity. The parity violations are caused by the couplings of a scalar field to the scalar invariants which are parity-odd and quadratic in the torsion tensor. Totally there are two such type independent invariants, and one of them is the Nieh-Yan density. Through investigations on the cosmological perturbations of this model, we find that in general it suffers from the difficulties of ghost instability in the scalar and vector perturbations. But in the special case only the coupling to the Nieh-Yan density exists, this model is ghost free and reduces to the Nieh-Yan modified Teleparallel Gravity model. We also analyze the severity of the ghost instability by studying the perturbations around the Minkowski background.

II
The Parity Violating gravity model as an extension of NYTG model

The TG theory can be considered as a constrained metric-affine theory.
It is formulated in a spacetime endowed with a metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and an affine connection
\hat{\Gamma}^{\rho}_{~\mu\nu}
,
which is curvature free and metric compatible,

\hat{R}^{\sigma}_{~\rho\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\hat{\Gamma}^{\sigma}_{~\nu\rho}-\partial_{\nu}\hat{\Gamma}^{\sigma}_{~\mu\rho}+\hat{\Gamma}^{\sigma}_{~\mu\lambda}\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{~\nu\rho}-\hat{\Gamma}^{\sigma}_{~\nu\lambda}\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{~\mu\rho}=0~,~\hat{\nabla}_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}-\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{~\rho\mu}g_{\lambda\nu}-\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{~\rho\nu}g_{\mu\lambda}=0~.

(1)

Without curvature and nonmetricity in the TG theory,
the gravity is identified with spacetime torsion
\mathcal{T}^{\rho}_{~\mu\nu}=2\hat{\Gamma}_{\,[\mu\nu]}^{\rho}

In terms of the tetrad
e^{A}_{~\mu}
and spin connection

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{AB}e^{A}_{~\mu}e^{B}_{~\nu}

\hat{\Gamma}^{\rho}_{~\mu\nu}=e_{A}^{~\,\rho}(\partial_{\mu}e^{A}_{~\nu}+\omega^{A}_{~B\mu}e^{B}_{~\nu})

the torsion tensor can be expressed as

\mathcal{T}^{\rho}_{~\mu\nu}=2e_{A}^{~\,\rho}(\partial_{[\mu}e^{A}_{~\nu]}+\omega^{A}_{~B[\mu}e^{B}_{~\nu]})~.

(2)

The teleparallel constraints (
1

\omega_{~B\mu}^{A}=(\Lambda^{-1})^{A}_{~C}\partial_{\mu}\Lambda_{~B}^{C}~,

(3)

Therefore, the tetrad

and the Lorentz matrix

The TGR model, as the GR equivalent TG model, has the following action,

\displaystyle S_{TGR}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x~{\|e\|}\mathbb{T}\equiv\int d^{4}x~{\|e\|}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}\mathcal{T}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha\beta\mu}\mathcal{T}^{\alpha\beta\mu}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha\beta\mu}\mathcal{T}^{\beta\alpha\mu}\right)~,

As mentioned before, the NYTG model

;

\displaystyle S_{NYTG}=\int d^{4}x~{\|e\|}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{T}+\frac{c}{4}\,\theta\,\mathcal{T}_{\lambda\mu\nu}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\lambda\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\theta\nabla^{\mu}\theta-V(\theta)\right]+S_{m}~,

Detailed analyses on the cosmological perturbations

Besides the Nieh-Yan density, there are other scalar invariants which are parity-odd and quadratic in the torsion tensor within the TG framework.
Totally we have the following four invariants of this type:

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathcal{T}^{\lambda}_{~\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda\rho\sigma}~,~\mathcal{P}_{2}=\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}\mathcal{T}_{\nu\rho\sigma}~,~\mathcal{P}_{3}=\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathcal{T}^{\lambda}_{~\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}_{\rho\sigma\lambda}~,~\mathcal{P}_{4}=\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}^{~~\lambda}\mathcal{T}_{\rho\sigma\lambda}~,

In order to explicitly see how many dynamical degrees of freedom and how many ghost modes there are,

\gamma_{i}
with

\zeta
,
\beta

\lambda

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\frac{a\sqrt{|3+3\tau_{1}-\tau_{1}^{2}}|}{\sqrt{2}\mathcal{H}\theta^{\prime}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})(\tau_{1}-\tau_{3})}\left[(1-\tau_{2}-\tau_{3})\theta^{\prime 2}\zeta+\mathcal{H}\theta^{\prime 2}(\tau_{2}+\tau_{3})\beta-c_{2}\mathcal{H}(2+\tau_{2}\tau_{3})k^{2}\lambda\right]~,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\frac{a\sqrt{|3+3\tau_{2}-\tau_{2}^{2}}|}{\sqrt{2}\mathcal{H}\theta^{\prime}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})(\tau_{2}-\tau_{3})}\left[(1-\tau_{1}-\tau_{3})\theta^{\prime 2}\zeta+\mathcal{H}\theta^{\prime 2}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{3})\beta-c_{2}\mathcal{H}(2+\tau_{1}\tau_{3})k^{2}\lambda\right]~,

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}=\frac{a\sqrt{|3+3\tau_{3}-\tau_{3}^{2}}|}{\sqrt{2}\mathcal{H}\theta^{\prime}(\tau_{3}-\tau_{1})(\tau_{3}-\tau_{2})}\left[(1-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\theta^{\prime 2}\zeta+\mathcal{H}\theta^{\prime 2}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\beta-c_{2}\mathcal{H}(2+\tau_{1}\tau_{2})k^{2}\lambda\right]~,

(20)

where
\tau_{1}
,

\tau_{3}
are the three solutions of the equation

\tau_{1}\approx 0.445

\tau_{3}\approx-1.247

It can be verified that the transformation (

Then we can express the action (

S^{(2)}_{S}=\int d\eta d^{3}k\,\Big\{{\gamma_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+{\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}^{2}-{\gamma_{3}^{\prime}}^{2}+\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{12}\gamma^{\prime}_{1}\gamma_{2}+\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{13}\gamma^{\prime}_{1}\gamma_{3}+\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{23}\gamma^{\prime}_{2}\gamma_{3}+\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\gamma_{1}^{2}+\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}+\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{3}\gamma_{3}^{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{12}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{13}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{3}+\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{23}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\Big\}~,

(21)

where

\displaystyle\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{ij}=\frac{2(\tau_{i}-\tau_{j})}{\sqrt{|3+3\tau_{i}-\tau_{i}^{2}||3+3\tau_{j}-\tau_{j}^{2}|}}\left(2\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\theta^{\prime}}-\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}\right)~,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=(i^{2}-3i+1)k^{2}+\frac{1}{|3+3\tau_{i}-\tau_{i}^{2}|}\Bigg\{4\tau_{i}\mathcal{H}^{2}+2\left[6+\tau_{i}-3\tau_{i}^{2}+2\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}(2-\tau_{i}^{2})\right]\mathcal{H}\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\tau_{i}^{2}-3\tau_{i}-5-\frac{2\tau_{i}}{\theta^{2}}\right)\theta^{\prime 2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{\tau_{i}(\tau_{i}+1)}{2}\frac{\theta^{\prime 4}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+3\tau_{i}\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\theta}-2\tau_{i}\left(\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+(\tau_{i}^{2}-1)\frac{\theta^{\prime 3}}{\mathcal{H}\theta}+2(\tau_{i}+1)\frac{\theta^{\prime}\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{H}}+(3+\tau_{i}-\tau_{i}^{2})\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\theta^{\prime}}\Bigg\}~,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|3+3\tau_{i}-\tau_{i}^{2}||3+3\tau_{j}-\tau_{j}^{2}|}}\Bigg\{4(\tau_{i}+\tau_{j})\mathcal{H}^{2}-2\theta^{\prime 2}+2\left[12+\tau_{i}+\tau_{j}-3\tau_{i}^{2}-3\tau_{j}^{2}+2\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}(4-\tau_{i}^{2}-\tau_{j}^{2})\right]\mathcal{H}\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-2\tau_{j}\left(\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}\right)^{2}+(\tau_{i}^{2}+\tau_{j}^{2}-\tau_{i}-\tau_{j}-3)\frac{\theta^{\prime 4}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+2(\tau_{i}+2\tau_{j})\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\theta}-4\tau_{i}\left(\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+(\tau_{i}^{2}+\tau_{j}^{2}-2)\frac{\theta^{\prime 3}}{\mathcal{H}\theta}+2(\tau_{i}^{2}+\tau_{j}^{2}-2\tau_{i}-2\tau_{j}-4)\frac{\theta^{\prime}\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{H}}-4\tau_{j}\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\theta^{\prime}}\Bigg\}~.

This quadratic action (
21
) for the scalar perturbations shows that all three
\gamma_{i}
s represent dynamical degrees of freedom. That means the model (
7
) with
c_{2}\neq 0
has two more dynamical degrees of freedom than the model of GR with a minimally coupled scalar field. At the same time,
the quadratic action (
21
) clearly shows that
\gamma_{3}
is a ghost mode because its kinetic term has a wrong sign.
This will cause the vacuum instability. This ghost mode in the scalar perturbations is completely brought by the PV coupling term
c_{2}\theta\mathcal{P}_{2}
in the action (
7
) and independent of the scale.

III.2
Quadratic action for vector perturbations

For vector perturbations, direct calculation shows that the quadratic action of the model (
7
) is

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{V}=\int d^{4}x\Bigg\{-\frac{a^{2}}{4}B^{V}_{i}\nabla^{2}B^{V}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}c_{1}a^{2}\theta^{\prime}\epsilon_{ijk}\left(\beta^{V}_{i}\beta^{V}_{j,k}-\lambda^{V}_{i}\lambda^{V}_{j,k}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+c_{2}a^{2}\theta\bigg[\beta_{i}^{V\prime}\lambda_{i}^{V\prime}+\lambda_{i}^{V}\nabla^{2}\beta_{i}^{V}+\frac{1}{2}B_{i}^{V}(\nabla^{2}\lambda^{V}_{i}-\epsilon_{ijk}\beta^{V\prime}_{j,k})+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}-\mathcal{H}\Big)\epsilon_{ijk}\left(\beta^{V}_{i}\beta^{V}_{j,k}-\lambda^{V}_{i}\lambda^{V}_{j,k}\right)\bigg]\Bigg\}~.

(22)

where
\nabla^{2}=\partial_{i}\partial_{i}
is Laplacian.
Whatever the values the coefficients
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
take, the variable
B^{V}_{i}
is a non-dynamical field.
It induces the constraint,

\nabla^{2}B^{V}_{i}+c_{2}\theta\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\beta^{V\prime}_{j,k}-\nabla^{2}\lambda^{V}_{i}\right)=0~.

(23)

This constraint equation just solves the variable
B_{i}^{V}
.
For further simplification, we use the circular polarization bases
\hat{e}^{L}_{i}
,
\hat{e}^{R}_{i}
,
so that
B_{i}^{V}
is expanded as,

B^{V}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})=\sum_{A}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}B_{A}(\eta,\vec{k})\hat{e}^{A}_{i}(\vec{k})e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}~.

(24)

The bases satisfy the relation:
\epsilon_{ijk}n^{j}\hat{e}_{k}^{A}=i\kappa_{A}\hat{e}_{ij}^{A}
,
here
A=L,R
and
\kappa_{L}=-1
,
\kappa_{R}=1
for the left- and right-handed polarized components respectively,
\vec{n}
is the unit vector of
\vec{k}
.
We also expand the variables
{\lambda}^{V}_{i}
and
{\beta}^{V}_{i}
in the same way.
Substituting the constraint (
23
) back into the action (
22
),
the quadratic action for vector perturbations can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{V}=\sum_{A}\int d\eta d^{3}ka^{2}\,\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\kappa_{A}\theta^{\prime}k\left(\beta^{*}_{A}\beta_{A}-\lambda^{*}_{A}\lambda_{A}\right)+

\displaystyle\quad\quad c_{2}\theta\bigg[{\beta^{*}_{A}}^{\prime}\lambda_{A}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{4}c_{2}\theta{\beta^{*}_{A}}^{\prime}\beta_{A}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}c_{2}\kappa_{A}\theta k{\beta^{*}_{A}}^{\prime}\lambda_{A}-k^{2}{\beta^{*}_{A}}\lambda_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{A}k\Big(\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}-\mathcal{H}\Big)\left(\beta^{*}_{A}\beta_{A}-\lambda^{*}_{A}\lambda_{A}\right)-\frac{1}{4}c_{2}\theta k^{2}\lambda^{*}_{A}\lambda_{A}\bigg]\Bigg\}~.

Again, from this action, one will see that
whether the coefficient
c_{2}
vanishes or not determines whether the vector perturbations of the model (
7
) contain ghost modes.

In the case where
c_{2}=0
, the model (
7
) reduces to the NYTG model, the quadratic action (
III.2
) reduces to the following one,

S^{(2)}_{V}=\sum_{A}\int d\eta d^{3}k\,\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\kappa_{A}a^{2}\theta^{\prime}k(\beta^{*}_{A}\beta_{A}-\lambda^{*}_{A}\lambda_{A})~.

(26)

Evidently, the vector perturbations
\beta_{A}^{V}
and
\lambda_{A}^{V}
are non-dynamical fields. There is no propagating ghost mode in this action.
In fact, without source to the vector perturbations, all of them vanish in the expanding universe. This is what happens in the NYTG model
PVtele1
;
PVtele2
.

In the more general case with
c_{2}\neq 0
,
the mixing terms
{\beta^{*}_{A}}^{\prime}\lambda_{A}^{\prime}
and
-{\beta^{*}_{A}}^{\prime}\beta_{A}^{\prime}
led by
c_{2}
in the action (
III.2
) generally indicate the existences of ghost modes.
In order to explicitly see how many dynamical degrees of freedom and how many ghost modes there are,
we redefine the following independent vector perturbation variables through
\beta^{V}_{A}
and
\lambda^{V}_{A}
:

\hat{\beta}_{A}=\frac{1}{2}c_{2}a\theta\beta_{A}-a\lambda_{A}~~,~~\hat{\lambda}_{A}=a\lambda_{A}~.

(27)

The above transformation from
(\beta_{A},\lambda_{A})
to
(\hat{\beta}_{A},\hat{\lambda}_{A})
is reversible.
Then we can rewrite the quadratic action (
III.2
) as

S^{(2)}_{V}=\sum_{A}\int d\eta d^{3}k\left(\hat{\lambda}_{A}^{*\prime}\hat{\lambda}_{A}^{\prime}-\hat{\beta}_{A}^{*\prime}\hat{\beta}_{A}^{\prime}+\mathcal{C}_{1}\hat{\beta}_{A}^{*}\hat{\lambda}_{A}^{\prime}+\mathcal{C}_{2}\hat{\beta}_{A}^{*}\hat{\lambda}_{A}+\mathcal{C}_{3}\hat{\lambda}_{A}^{*}\hat{\lambda}_{A}+\mathcal{C}_{4}\hat{\beta}_{A}^{*}\hat{\beta}_{A}\right)~,

(28)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}=\kappa_{A}c_{2}\theta k-2\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}=-2k^{2}-2\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\theta}-2\mathcal{H}\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}+\kappa_{A}c_{2}k(2\theta^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}\theta)+\frac{4\kappa_{A}k}{(c_{2}\theta)^{2}}\left[(c_{1}+c_{2})\theta^{\prime}-c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta\right]~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}=-2k^{2}+2\mathcal{H}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime 2}-\frac{\theta^{\prime 2}}{\theta^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{2}^{2}\theta^{2}k^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{A}k}{2}\left(3c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta+2c_{2}\theta^{\prime}-c_{1}\theta^{\prime}\right)+\frac{2\kappa_{A}k}{(c_{2}\theta)^{2}}\left[(c_{1}+c_{2})\theta^{\prime}-c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta\right]~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{4}=-2\mathcal{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime 2}-\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\theta}-2\mathcal{H}\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}+\frac{2\kappa_{A}k}{(c_{2}\theta)^{2}}\left[(c_{1}+c_{2})\theta^{\prime}-c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta\right]~.

The quadratic action (
28
) shows that all the four components of vector perturbations,
\hat{\beta}_{A}
and
\hat{\lambda}_{A}
with
A=L~{\rm and}~R
, are dynamical modes.
It also clearly shows that both components of
\hat{\beta}_{A}
are ghost modes because their kinetic terms have wrong signs.
Again, this will cause the vacuum instability and such instability in the vector perturbations is completely brought by the PV coupling term
c_{2}\theta\mathcal{P}_{2}
in the action (
7
) and is scale independent.

As a comparison, the model of GR with a scalar field or the NYTG model have no propagating dynamical degrees of freedom in the vector perturbations. Combining with the analysis on the scalar perturbations in the above subsection, the model (
7
) with
c_{2}\neq 0
brings totally six more dynamical degrees of freedom than the model of GR with a minimally coupled scalar field.
This confirms the judgement in Sec.
II
. Furthermore, three of these extra dynamical degrees of freedom represent ghost modes.

III.3
Quadratic action for tensor perturbations

Finally, we have the quadratic action for tensor perturbations in the model (
7
),

S^{(2)}_{T}=\int d^{4}x\ \frac{a^{2}}{8}\left[{h^{T}_{ij}}^{\prime}{h^{T}_{ij}}^{\prime}-h^{T}_{ij}\nabla^{2}h^{T}_{ij}-(c_{1}\theta^{\prime}-3c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta)\epsilon_{ijk}h^{T}_{il}h^{T}_{lj,k}\right]~.

(29)

In terms of the circular polarization bases
\hat{e}^{L}_{ij}
and
\hat{e}^{R}_{ij}
, where
n^{l}\epsilon_{lik}\hat{e}_{jk}^{A}=i\kappa_{A}\hat{e}_{ij}^{A}
with
A=L,R
and
\kappa_{L}=-1
,
\kappa_{R}=1
, the tensor perturbations are expanded as

h_{ij}^{T}(\eta,\vec{x})=\sum_{A}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\,h_{A}(\eta,\vec{k})\,\hat{e}_{ij}^{A}(\vec{k})\,e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}~.

(30)

The final form of the quadratic action for tensor perturbations is

S^{(2)}_{T}=\sum_{A}\int d\eta d^{3}k~\frac{a^{2}}{4}\left[h_{A}^{*\prime}h_{A}^{\prime}-k^{2}\left(1+\kappa_{A}\frac{c_{1}\theta^{\prime}-3c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta}{k}\right)h_{A}^{*}h_{A}\right]~.

(31)

First, we see that the tensor perturbations are ghost free.
Second, the modified dispersion relation

\omega_{A}^{2}=k^{2}\left[1+\kappa_{A}(c_{1}\theta^{\prime}-3c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta)/k\right]
is helicity dependent.
Consider small coupling and slow evolution of
\theta
, one can find that GWs with different helicities will
have different phase velocities
v_{p}^{A}=\omega_{A}/k\simeq 1+\kappa_{A}(c_{1}\theta^{\prime}-3c_{2}\mathcal{H}\theta)/(2k)
, i.e., the velocity birefringence phenomenon.
This is the explicit signal of parity violation in this model.
We can also see that the phase velocity difference become important only at the region of small
k
(large scales), so this is an infrared effect.
Besides the velocity birefringence, the amplitudes for both components of GWs are the same.
When
c_{2}=0
, these results go back to those obtained in the NYTG model
PVtele1
;
PVtele2
.

IV
Perturbations around the Minkowski background

To further explore the severity of the ghost instability in the model (
7
), we study the perturbations around the Minkowski background in this section.
We still take the Weitzenböck gauge
\omega^{A}_{~B\mu}=0
,
and still use Eq. (
III
) to parameterize the perturbations of the tetrad, but the scale factor
a=1
in the Minkowski background.
In addition, the scalar field
\theta
is decomposed as
\theta=\theta_{0}+\delta\theta
,
where the constant
\theta_{0}
is the lowest point of potential
V(\theta)
, satisfying
V(\theta_{0})=0
and
V_{\theta}(\theta_{0})=0
.
Note that
\delta\theta
is gauge invariant in this case, so we can no longer take the unitary gauge in this section.
Instead we take the conformal Newton gauge:
B=0,~\alpha=0,~\alpha_{i}^{V}=0
,
and we choose
A
,
\psi
,
\delta\theta
,
\beta
,
\lambda
,
B_{i}^{V}
,
\beta_{i}^{V}
,
\lambda_{i}^{V}
and
h^{T}_{ij}
as independent variables.
Since the purpose of this section is to explore the severity of the ghost instability in the model (
7
), for simplicity,
we only consider the case of
S_{m}=0
,
c_{1}\neq 0
,
c_{2}\neq 0
and
\theta_{0}\neq 0
in the following.

For scalar perturbations, direct calculation shows that the quadratic action of the model (
7
) is

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{S}=\int dtd^{3}k~\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big[\delta{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}-(k^{2}+m_{\theta}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}\Big]-3{\psi^{\prime}}^{2}+k^{2}\psi^{2}-2k^{2}A\psi+c_{2}\theta_{0}\Big[k^{2}(\beta^{\prime}-A-\psi)\lambda^{\prime}-k^{4}\beta\lambda\Big]\bigg\}~,

(32)

where
m_{\theta}^{2}=d^{2}V(\theta)/d\theta^{2}|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}
.
We have changed to the momentum space in terms of Fourier transformations and
we also simply mark
A^{*}\psi
as
A\psi
,
\psi^{*}\psi
as
\psi^{2}
, and so on.
The variable
A
is non-dynamical field and it induces the constraint

2\psi+c_{2}\theta_{0}\lambda^{\prime}=0~.

(33)

Substituting the constraint (
33
) back into the action (
32
),
the quadratic action for scalar perturbations can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{S}=\int dtd^{3}k~\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big[\delta{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}-(k^{2}+m_{\theta}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}\Big]+\frac{1}{4}\Big[3c_{2}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}(-{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}^{2}+k^{2}{\lambda^{\prime}}^{2})-4c_{2}\theta_{0}k^{2}(\beta\lambda^{\prime\prime}+k^{2}\beta\lambda)\Big]\bigg\}~.

(34)

The action (
34
) is equivalent to the following action,

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{S}=\int dtd^{3}k~\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big[\delta{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}-(k^{2}+m_{\theta}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}\Big]+\frac{1}{4}\Big[c_{2}\theta_{0}\phi\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{12}\phi^{2}+\frac{2}{3}k^{2}\beta\phi+\frac{4}{3}k^{4}\beta^{2}+3c_{2}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}k^{2}{\lambda^{\prime}}^{2}-4c_{2}\theta_{0}k^{4}\beta\lambda\Big]\bigg\}~.

(35)

The variation of the action (
35
) on
\phi
leads to constraint

\phi=-2(2k^{2}\beta+3c_{2}\theta_{0}\lambda^{\prime\prime})~.

(36)

It can be verified that the action (
34
) can be obtained by substituting the constraint (
36
)
back into the action (
35
), so the action (
34
) and the action (
35
) are equivalent.
On the other hand, the variation of the action (
35
) on
\beta
leads to the constraint

\beta=\frac{3}{2}c_{2}\theta_{0}\lambda-\frac{1}{4k^{2}}\phi~.

(37)

Substituting the constraint (
37
) back into the action (
35
),
the quadratic action for scalar perturbations can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{S}=\int dtd^{3}k~\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big[\delta{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}-(k^{2}+m_{\theta}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}\Big]+\frac{c_{2}\theta_{0}}{4}\Big[3c_{2}\theta_{0}k^{2}{\lambda^{\prime}}^{2}-\phi^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}+k^{2}\lambda\phi-3c_{2}\theta_{0}k^{4}\lambda^{2}\Big]\bigg\}~.

(38)

In order to further simplify the quadratic action,
we define new perturbation variables
q_{1},q_{2}
in terms of the old variables
\lambda,\phi
:

\displaystyle q_{1}=2^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1+9c_{2}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}k^{4}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left[\phi-\left(3c_{2}\theta_{0}k^{2}+\sqrt{1+9c_{2}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}k^{4}}\right)\lambda\right]~,

\displaystyle q_{2}=2^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1+9c_{2}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}k^{4}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left[\phi-\left(3c_{2}\theta_{0}k^{2}-\sqrt{1+9c_{2}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}k^{4}}\right)\lambda\right]~.

(39)

Then we can express the action (
38
) in terms of the new perturbation variables as,

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{S}=\int dtd^{3}k~\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big[\delta{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}-(k^{2}+m_{\theta}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}\Big]+c_{2}\theta_{0}\Big[\frac{1}{2}({q_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}-k^{2}q_{1}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}({q_{2}^{\prime}}^{2}-k^{2}q_{2}^{2})\Big]\bigg\}~.

(40)

The quadratic action (
40
) for the scalar perturbations shows that
\delta\theta,q_{1},q_{2}

are all dynamical degrees of freedom and
q_{2}
is a ghost mode because its kinetic term has a wrong sign.
Such a result is consistent with the results in Sec.
III.1
.
Furthermore, the action (
40
) shows that the mass of the ghost mode
q_{2}
is zero.
This means that no matter how low the energy scale of the effective field theory is, the ghost mode
q_{2}
can always be excited.
Therefore, even from the point of view of effective field theory, the model (
7
) still suffers from the difficulty of ghost instability.

For vector perturbations,
its quadratic action can be obtained through the same process as in Sec.
III.2
,
and the result is still the action (
28
),
but here
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\kappa_{A}c_{2}\theta_{0}k
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}=-2k^{2}
,

\mathcal{C}_{3}=-(2+c_{2}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}/4)k^{2}
and
\mathcal{C}_{4}=0
.
The action (
28
) shows that
\hat{\beta}_{A}
and
\hat{\lambda}_{A}
are all dynamical modes but
\hat{\beta}_{A}
are ghost modes.
The fact that the mass term coefficient
\mathcal{C}_{4}
of the ghost modes
\hat{\beta}_{A}
vanish
implies that the ghost modes
\hat{\beta}_{A}
can always be excited no matter how low the energy scale of the effective field theory is.
Such results again illustrate that even from the point of view of effective field theory,
the model (
7
) suffers from the difficulty of ghost instability.

For tensor perturbations, its quadratic action is exactly the same as that of GR, so we won’t discuss it anymore.

V
Conclusion

We studied in this paper a parity violating gravity model within the framework of teleparallel gravity. This model modifies the gravity by the couplings of a scalar field to the scalar invariants which are parity-odd and quadratic in the torsion tensor. Totally there are two such type independent invariants, and one of them is the Nieh-Yan density. Through investigations on its cosmological perturbations, we found that in general this model has six more dynamical degrees of freedom compared with model of GR with a minimally coupled scalar field. Among these extra dynamical degrees of freedom, two of them are scalar perturbations and the rest four are vector perturbations. Furthermore, half of them are ghost modes and cause instabilities at all scales. However, in the special case only the coupling to the Nieh-Yan density exists, this model is ghost free and reduces to the NYTG model.
Finally, by investigating the properties of the ghost modes in Minkowski spacetime,
we find that it is almost impossible to avoid ghost instability in the model (
7
) by adjusting the energy scale of the effective field theory.

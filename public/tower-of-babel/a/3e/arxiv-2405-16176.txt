Bi-reachability in Petri nets with data
Łukasz Kamiński, Sławomir Lasota

Abstract
We investigate Petri nets with data, an extension of plain Petri nets where tokens carry values from an infinite data domain, and executability of transitions is conditioned by equalities between data values. We provide a decision procedure for the bi-reachability problem: given a Petri net and its two configurations, we ask if each of the configurations is reachable from the other. This pushes forward the decidability borderline, as the bi-reachability problem subsumes the coverability problem (which is known to be decidable) and is subsumed by the reachability problem (whose decidability status is unknown).

The most fundamental decision problem for Petri nets, the

given a net together with source and target configurations, if there is a run from source to target.
It is well known that the reachability problem is undecidable for Petri nets with ordered data
[
22
]
,
while the decidability status of this problem for equality data still remains an intriguing open question.
The same applies to two other major extensions of plain Petri nets, namely pushdown Petri nets

[

and branching Petri nets
[

On the other hand, the

that possibly extends target by some extra tokens)
is decidable for both equality and ordered data

As widely known, coverability easily reduces to reachability.
Furthermore, the reachability problem is decidable, also for equality and ordered data,
in the special case of

as recently shown in
[
15

In this paper we do a step towards decidability of reachability in Petri nets with equality data, and
study a relevant decision problem sandwiched between reachability and
the two latter decidable problems: the

(also called

or

[

It asks, for a net and two its configurations, if each of the configurations is reachable from the other one.
In other words, the problem asks if two given configurations are in the same bi-reachability equivalence class.
Here are all know reductions, valid for Petri nets with either equality or ordered data,
as well as for plain Petri nets (without data):

bi-reachability

reachability in

reversible Petri nets

As our main result we prove decidability of this problem for equality data domain.
This result pushes further the decidability border, subsuming decidability
of coverability, and of reachability in reversible Petri nets with equality data.
Our approach is specific to equality data, and thus we leave unresolved the status of bi-reachability
in case of ordered data.

The decision procedure for bi-reachability
is inspired by the classical decomposition approach used to decide reachability

There, it is often more convenient to work with

Following this line, for technical convenience we prefer to work with the model of

In short, our approach consists of two ingredients.
First, we provide a sufficient condition for a

Second, in case the condition fails, we provide an effective way of reducing a

As ranks are well founded, the reduction step guarantees correctness and termination.
Importantly, the decision procedure manipulates

This allows us to avoid similar generalisations in the data setting, and
allows to keep the algorithm relatively simple.

Our work leaves two exciting open questions:
can one extend our approach to bi-reachability in case of ordered data,
or to reachability in case of equality data.
Clearly, if attempting to solve the latter problem, one unavoidably will be faced with some
generalisation of the above-mentioned structures to the data setting.

Related research.

Petri nets with equality data are a well established and widely studied model of concurrent systems,
as data allow to model important aspects of such systems not captured by plain Petri nets,
e.g. process identity

The model can be also seen as a reinterpretation of the classical definition of Petri nets
with a relaxed notion of finiteness, namely orbit-finiteness, where one allows for orbit-finite
sets of places and transitions instead of just finite ones; this is along the lines of
[
3

4

Similar net models have been proposed already in the early 80ies:

[

[

Since then, similar formalisms seem to have been rediscovered, for instance constraint multiset rewriting
[

8

In plain Petri nets, bi-reachability is decidable as a consequence of decidability of reachability

[
18
,
19
,
27

Later, exact

[

]

In our setting, the problem is

[

In pushdown Petri nets, decidability of reachability

Indeed, it is known that reachability in pushdown Petri nets

and the latter problem reduces to bi-reachability in pushdown Petri nets.
Hence, decidability of bi-reachability would imply decidability of reachability

In the sequel, let

By

For a subset

Permutations in

we call

.

Orbit-finite sets.

In the following we study actions of the group

An action of

We write

and

.
In the sequel we always use the natural action of

that,

but leaves other elements intact.
Here are two specific examples of such action that will serve later as building blocks in the definition of
our model:

Example 1
.

Let

.
For any finite sets

and
\mathcal{R}
of

and

acts naturally on the set of

Furthermore, for any finite sets

A subset

Clearly all orbits, and hence also all finite unions thereof, are

Orbit-finite sets are closed under finite unions and products

Vectors.

Data vector addition systems with states

Classical Petri nets are equivalent, with respect to most decision problems, to vector addition systems
(
vass
).
Likewise, we introduce here a formalism equivalent to Petri nets with data, called data vector addition systems
with states (

).
It is an extension of (stateless) data vector addition systems (
dvas
) studied in
[
16
]
.

Data
vass
.

A data
vass
(
dvass
)
\mathcal{V}=(L,\mathcal{R},H,P,T)
consists of
pairwise disjoint finite sets of locations

, register names
\mathcal{R}
,

H
, atom places

, and an

set

of

,
where
Q=L\times\left(\mathcal{R}\to(\mathbb{A}\cup\{\bot\})\right)
is the set of
states
.
The set

is thus assumed to be a finite union of orbits, under
the natural action of
\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
on transitions that extends
the action on vectors and states given in Example
1
:
for

we put
\sigma(t):=(\sigma(q),\sigma(\mathbf{v}),\sigma(q^{\prime}))
.
In particular,

is closed under the action of
\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
.
Given a state

, the function

is called

.
Intuitively,

means that register

contains atom

,
while

means that

is empty.
The vector
\mathbf{v}
is called the

.

The model of (plain)

, i.e.,

without registers and atom places.
In this case the set

The model of

corresponds to the special case when

is a singleton and

without locations, registers and plain places.

A

\pi=q_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{0})\,q_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{1})\,\dots\,q_{k}(\mathbf{v}_{k})

The support of a state

The support of a transition

.
We also define the support of a pseudo-run as the union of supports of all its pseudo-configurations:

\text{\sc supp}(\pi)=\text{\sc supp}(q_{0})\cup\text{\sc supp}(\mathbf{v}_{0})\cup\text{\sc supp}(q_{1})\cup\ldots\cup\text{\sc supp}(q_{k})\cup\text{\sc supp}(\mathbf{v}_{k})

\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
.

Configurations
are those
pseudo-configurations

where the vector
\mathbf{v}
is nonnegative, i.e.,
\mathbf{v}:{(H\cup P{\times}\mathbb{A})}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{N}
.
Let

A

is a pseudo-run where every pseudo-configuration

is actually a configuration.
We write

(resp.

) if there is a pseudo-run (resp. a run)
from

to

.

-fold concatenation of
\delta
or
\delta^{\prime}
yields a cyclic pseudo-run that
uses every transition from
T_{S}
at least
m
times.
We thus have two pseudo-runs

\displaystyle\delta^{m};\pi\ :\ q(\mathbf{0})\dashrightarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})\qquad\qquad\qquad(\delta^{\prime})^{m};\pi^{\prime}\ :\ q(\mathbf{0})\dashleftarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})

each of them using every transition from
T_{S}
at least
m
times. Thus the
vass

\mathcal{V}_{S}
satisfies
two instances of

\Theta_{1}
, one towards a run
q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
and
the other one towards
a run
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{0})
.

Now we concentrate on
\Theta_{2}
.
We proceed similarly as before, namely apply all automorphisms

\sigma\in\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{A}\setminus S}(\mathbb{A})
to
\mathbf{\Delta}
, and sum up all the resulting vectors:

\mathbf{\Delta}_{S}\ :=\ \oplus\left\{\sigma(\mathbf{\Delta})\,\middle|\,\sigma\in\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{A}\setminus S}(\mathbb{A})\right\}.

Let
\overline{\mathbf{\Delta}}_{S}:{H\cup P{\times}S}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{N}
be the restriction of

\mathbf{\Delta}_{S}
to
H\cup P{\times S}
.
Knowing that
\mathbf{\Delta}\gg\mathbf{0}
, we deduce that

\mathbf{\Delta}_{S}(h)>0
for every
h\in H
, and

\mathbf{\Delta}_{S}(p,a)>0
for every
(p,a)\in P\times S
.
In other words,
\overline{\mathbf{\Delta}}_{S}\gg\mathbf{0}
.
By applying all automorphisms
\sigma\in\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{A}\setminus S}(\mathbb{A})
to the run
q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{\Delta})

in
\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
, and concatenating
all the resulting runs, we get a run

q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{\Delta}_{S})
in
\mathcal{V}
.
Clearly, only atoms from
S
appear in this run, and therefore it is also a run

in
\mathcal{V}_{S}
.
In a similar way we define vectors
\overline{\mathbf{\Delta}}_{S}^{\prime}
,
\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}_{S}
and
\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}_{S}^{\prime}
,
and the corresponding runs in
\mathcal{V}_{S}
:

\displaystyle q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\overline{\mathbf{\Delta}}_{S})\qquad\qquad q^{\prime}(\overline{\mathbf{\Delta}}^{\prime}_{S})\longrightarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})

(18)

\displaystyle q(\mathbf{0})\longleftarrow q(\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}_{S})\qquad\qquad q^{\prime}(\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{\prime}_{S})\longleftarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0}).

(19)

Therefore, the
vass

\mathcal{V}_{S}
satisfies two instances of
\Theta_{2}
,
one towards a run
q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
and the other one
towards a run
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{0})
.

Finally, using Lemma
9
we deduce two runs in
\mathcal{V}_{S}
, which
are automatically also runs in
\mathcal{V}
. This completes the proof.
∎

6
Reduction algorithm

As the
rank
of a
dvass

\mathcal{V}=(L,\mathcal{R},H,P,T)

we take the triple
\text{\sc Rank}(\mathcal{V})=(|P|,|H|,||T||)
,
consisting of the number of atom places, the number of plain places,
and the number of orbits
||T||
the set
T
partitions into.
Ranks are compared lexicographically.

Given a
dvass

\mathcal{V}
, the algorithm verifies the conditions
\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}
and
\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
.
If they are all satisfied, it answers positively, relying on Lemma
17
.
Otherwise, depending on which of the conditions is violated, the algorithm either immediately answers negatively,
or applies a reduction step, as outlined below in Sections
6.1
and
6.2
.
Each of the steps produces a new
dvass

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
of strictly smaller rank, which guarantees termination.
Finally, when both
P
and
H
are empty,
the problem reduces to reachability from
q
to
q^{\prime}
in state graph
\text{\sc Graph}(\mathcal{V})
, which
is decidable due to:

Lemma 19
.

For a set
E\subseteq Q\times Q
of edges between states, given as a finite union of orbits,
and a pair
(s,s^{\prime})\in Q\times Q
,
it is decidable if there is a path from
s
to
s^{\prime}
in the graph
(Q,E)
.

Proof.

The orbit of an edge
((\ell,\nu),(\ell^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}))\in E
is determined by the following data:
locations
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
;
the inverse images

\nu^{-1}(\bot),\ (\nu^{\prime})^{-1}(\bot);

and the equality type of the remaining entries in
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
, that is:

\left\{(r,r^{\prime})\,\middle|\,\nu(r)=\nu(r^{\prime})\neq\bot\right\}\quad\left\{(r,r^{\prime})\,\middle|\,\nu^{\prime}(r)=\nu^{\prime}(r^{\prime})\neq\bot\right\}\quad\left\{(r,r^{\prime})\,\middle|\,\nu(r)=\nu^{\prime}(r^{\prime})\neq\bot\right\}.

Using equational reasoning, one computes the transitive closure
E^{*}
of
E
, by consecutively
adding to
E^{*}
every new orbit which is forced to be included in
E^{*}

by some two orbits already included in
E^{*}
, until saturation.
Termination is guaranteed as
Q\times Q
is orbit-finite.
The transitive closure is thus forcedly a finite union of orbits.
Finally, one tests if the orbit of
(s,s^{\prime})
is included in
E^{*}
.
∎

For future use we note an immediate consequence of the above proof:
for every pair of states, if there is a path from one to the other, then there is also a path of bounded length.
This implies a bound on the number of atoms involved:

Corollary 20
.

There is an effective bound
b(Q)\in\mathbb{N}
such that whenever there is a path from
s\in Q
to
s^{\prime}\in Q

in the graph
(Q,E)
,
then there is such a path
\pi
with
|\text{\sc supp}(\pi)|\leq b(Q)
.

Below we describe the two reduction steps, proving their progress property (decreasing rank),
correctness and effectiveness.

6.1
Violation of
\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}

Suppose
\mathcal{V}
violates
\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}
.
If states
q,q^{\prime}
are not in the same strongly connected component of
\text{\sc Graph}(T)
,
which is testable using Lemma
19
,
the configurations
q(\mathbf{0})
,
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
are clearly not bi-reachable and the algorithm answers negatively.
Otherwise,
the algorithm constructs a
dvass

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
of smaller rank, as defined below,
such that bi-reachability of
q(\mathbf{0})
and
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})

in
\mathcal{V}
is equivalent to their bi-reachability in
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
.

Let
\text{\sc Graph}(T)=(Q,E)
.
Transitions witnessing bi-reachability of
q(\mathbf{0})
and
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
, namely used in some cyclic run

q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{0})
,
form a cycle in
\text{\sc Graph}(T)
.
As a consequence, a transition
(s,\mathbf{v},s^{\prime})\in T
may be useful for bi-reachability
only if the edge
(s,s^{\prime})
belongs to the strongly connected component of
\text{\sc Graph}(T)
containing
q
and
q^{\prime}
.
Therefore, bi-reachability of
q(\mathbf{0})
,
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
in
\mathcal{V}
reduces to bi-reachability of
q(\mathbf{0})
,
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})

in
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
obtained by restriction to the strongly connected component of
q
and
q^{\prime}
.
This component is computed by enumerating all orbits included in
E
.
For every orbit
o\subseteq E
one chooses a representative
(s,s^{\prime})\in o
,
and uses Lemma
19
to test reachability, in
\text{\sc Graph}(T)
, for
the four pairs:
(q,s)
,
(s^{\prime},q)
,
(q^{\prime},s)
or
(s^{\prime},q^{\prime})
.
Then one removes from
T
all orbits of transitions
(s,\mathbf{v},s^{\prime})
such that
reachability test fails for any of the four pairs above.
The resulting set of transitions is still a finite union of orbits.
Consequently, from now on we may assume, w.l.o.g., that

\text{\sc Graph}(T)
is strongly connected (we ignore isolated vertices).

Useful transitions.

By a finite multiset of transitions we mean a nonnegative vector
\mathbf{f}:{T}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{N}
.
Given such a finite multiset,
let
\text{\sc State-Eq}(q,q^{\prime})
denote conjunction of the following conditions:

(a)

the sum of effects of all transitions in the multiset is
\mathbf{0}
,

(b)

for every state
s\notin\left\{q,q^{\prime}\right\}
,
the number of transitions incoming to
s
equals
the number of ones outgoing from
s
,

(c)

the number of transitions outgoing from
q
exceeds by one
the number of incoming ones,

(d)

the number of transitions incoming to
q^{\prime}
exceeds by one
the number of outgoing ones;

Symmetrically, let
\text{\sc State-Eq}(q^{\prime},q)
denote the conjunction of (a), (b) and the symmetric versions of (c) and (d)
with

q
and
q^{\prime}
swapped.
Let
O=\left\{\text{\sc Orbit}(t)\,\middle|\,t\in T\right\}
be the set of all orbits in
T
.
We call an orbit
o\in O

useful
if there are two finite multisets of transitions
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}^{\prime}

satisfying
\text{\sc State-Eq}(q,q^{\prime})
and
\text{\sc State-Eq}(q^{\prime},q)
, respectively, each of them containing some transition from
o
.

Lemma 21
.

\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}
holds if and only if all orbits of transitions are useful.

Proof.

The only if direction of the characterisation is immediate, as the multiset of transitions used in a pseudo-run

q(\mathbf{0})\dashrightarrow q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})

necessarily contains some transition from every orbit and satisfies all the conditions (a)–(d),
and likewise for a pseudo-run
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})\dashrightarrow q(\mathbf{0})
.
For the opposite direction, suppose that for every orbit
o\in O
there are finite multisets
\mathbf{f}_{o},\mathbf{f}^{\prime}_{o}

satisfying
\text{\sc State-Eq}(q,q^{\prime})

and
\text{\sc State-Eq}(q^{\prime},q)
, respectively, each of them containing some transition from
o
.
Let

\mathbf{f}\ :=\ \oplus\left\{\mathbf{f}_{o}\,\middle|\,o\in O\right\}\qquad\qquad\mathbf{f}^{\prime}\ :=\ \oplus\left\{\mathbf{f}^{\prime}_{o}\,\middle|\,o\in O\right\}\qquad\qquad S\ :=\ \text{\sc supp}(\mathbf{f})\cup\text{\sc supp}(\mathbf{f}^{\prime}),

where
\oplus
denotes the multiset sum operator.
Thus
S
is the (finite) set of atoms used in all the transitions appearing in
\mathbf{f}
or
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
.
Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
17
we use the subgroup

\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{A}\setminus S}(\mathbb{A})\subseteq\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
of automorphisms
\sigma
of
\mathbb{A}
that
are identity outside
S
, and
define a plain
vass

\mathcal{V}_{S}=(L_{S},\emptyset,H_{S},\emptyset,T_{S})

by restricting the set of atoms to
S
.
Locations of
\mathcal{V}_{S}
are
L_{S}:=L\times(\mathcal{R}\to S\cup\{\bot\})
, its places are

H_{S}:=H\cup P{\times}S
,
and its transitions
T_{S}\subseteq T
are all transitions of
\mathcal{V}
that use only atoms from
S
:

T_{S}=\left\{t\in T\,\middle|\,\text{\sc supp}(t)\subseteq S\right\}.

The state graph
\text{\sc Graph}(T_{S})
is a subgraph of
\text{\sc Graph}(T)
.
As
\text{\sc Graph}(T)
is strongly connected,
we use Corollary
20
to deduce that, for sufficiently large
S
, the state graph
\text{\sc Graph}(T_{S})

is also strongly connected.
Therefore we enlarge
S
, if necessary, to assure that
\text{\sc Graph}(T_{S})
is strongly connected.

As finite multisets
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
are just nonnegative vectors
{T}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{N}
,
they inherit the natural (pointwise) action of
\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
.
Basing on
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
we define two larger multisets of transitions
by applying all automorphisms from
\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{A}\setminus S}(\mathbb{A})
to
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
, respectively, and summing up
all the resulting multisets:

\mathbf{g}\ :=\ \oplus\left\{\sigma(\mathbf{f})\,\middle|\,\sigma\in\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{A}\setminus S}(\mathbb{A})\right\}\qquad\qquad\mathbf{g}^{\prime}\ :=\ \oplus\left\{\sigma(\mathbf{f}^{\prime})\,\middle|\,\sigma\in\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{A}\setminus S}(\mathbb{A})\right\}.

By Claim
18
,
each of
\mathbf{g},\mathbf{g}^{\prime}
contains all transitions from
T_{S}
.
Furthermore, the multiset
\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{f}\oplus\mathbf{g}\oplus\mathbf{g}^{\prime}
satisfies

\text{\sc State-Eq}(\widehat{q},\widehat{q}^{\prime})
,
where

\widehat{q}
,
\widehat{q}^{\prime}
are locations (=states) of
\mathcal{V}_{S}
corresponding to
q
and
q^{\prime}
respectively.
Likewise, the multiset
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}=\mathbf{f}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbf{g}\oplus\mathbf{g}^{\prime}
satisfies

\text{\sc State-Eq}(\widehat{q}^{\prime},\widehat{q})
.
Using the standard Euler argument in the (strongly) connected graph
\text{\sc Graph}(T_{S})
,
and relying on conditions (b)–(d),
we deduce existence of a pseudo-run in
\mathcal{V}_{S}

that uses exactly transitions

\mathbf{h}
.
Due to condition (a), this is a pseudo-run
\widehat{q}(\mathbf{0})\dashrightarrow\widehat{q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})

in
\mathcal{V}_{S}
.
Likewise we deduce a pseudo-run
\widehat{q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})\dashrightarrow\widehat{q}(\mathbf{0})

in
\mathcal{V}_{S}
.
The pseudo-runs are essentially also pseudo-runs in
\mathcal{V}
, both supported by
S
,
and both using some transition from every orbit in
T
.
This completes the proof of the characterisation. ∎

Reduction step.

We define a
dvass

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
by removing some useless orbit of transitions,

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}=(L,\mathcal{R},H,P,\widehat{T})
.
It has the same locations, registers and places as
\mathcal{V}
.

Lemma 22

(Progress)
.

||\widehat{T}||<||T||
, and hence
\text{\sc Rank}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})<\text{\sc Rank}(\mathcal{V})
.

Proof.

As some useless orbit of transitions is removed from
\widehat{T}
,
we have
||\widehat{T}||<||T||
, and therefore

\text{\sc Rank}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})=(|P|,|H|,||\widehat{T}||)<(|P|,|H|,||T||)=\text{\sc Rank}(\mathcal{V})
.
∎

Lemma 23

(Correctness)
.

The configurations

q(\mathbf{0})
,
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
are bi-reachable
in
\mathcal{V}
if and only if they are bi-reachable
in
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proof.

Indeed, useless transitions can not be used in runs between
q(\mathbf{0})
and
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
.
∎

Lemma 24

(Effectiveness)
.

The condition
\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}
is decidable.
When it fails, some useless orbit of transitions is computable.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that it is decidable if a given orbit
o\in O
is useful.
We show decidability by reduction to
Multiset sum
(recall Lemma
4
), i.e., we use
the algorithm for
Multiset Sum
to
check existence of a finite multiset
\mathbf{f}
that satisfies conditions (a)–(d) and contains a transition from
o

(and likewise to check existence of
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
).
Let
X=H\cup P{\times}\mathbb{A}
and
Y=X\cup Q\cup\left\{*\right\}
.
The set
Y
is orbit-finite.
Let every transition
t=(s,\mathbf{v},s^{\prime})\in T
determine a vector
\mathbf{y}_{t}:{Y}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{Z}
, and let every transition

t=(s,\mathbf{v},s^{\prime})\in o
determine additionally a vector
\mathbf{x}_{t}:{Y}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{Z}
, each of them extending
the vector
\mathbf{v}:{X}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{Z}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{x}_{t}(s)=-1\qquad\qquad\mathbf{x}_{t}(s^{\prime})=1\qquad\qquad\mathbf{x}_{t}(*)=1,\\
&\mathbf{y}_{t}(s)=-1\qquad\qquad\mathbf{y}_{t}(s^{\prime})=1\qquad\qquad\mathbf{y}_{t}(*)=0,\end{aligned}

(20)

and
\mathbf{x}_{t}(r)=\mathbf{y}_{t}(r)=0
for other states
r\in Q\setminus\left\{s,s^{\prime}\right\}
.
Intuitively,
the first two columns track contribution of
t
to the number of transitions
outgoing from
s
, or incoming to
s^{\prime}
,
while the latter column tracks the number of usages of transitions from
o
.
Likewise, let the target vector
\mathbf{b}:{Y}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{Z}
extend
\mathbf{0}:{X}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{Z}
by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbf{b}(q)=-1\qquad\qquad\mathbf{b}(q^{\prime})=1\qquad\qquad\mathbf{b}(*)=1,\end{aligned}

(21)

and
\mathbf{b}(s)=0
for other states
s\in Q\setminus\left\{q,q^{\prime}\right\}
.
Let
M=\left\{\mathbf{y}_{t}\,\middle|\,t\in T\right\}\cup\left\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\,\middle|\,t\in o\right\}
, and consider the instance
let
(M,\mathbf{b})
of
Multiset Sum
.
Observe that solutions of
(M,\mathbf{b})
necessarily use exactly one vector
\mathbf{x}_{t}
exactly once,
as in (
22
) below.
It remains to prove that some finite multiset
\mathbf{f}=\left\{t,u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right\}
of transitions from
T
,
where
t\in o
,
satisfies the conditions (a)–(d) exactly when

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}\ =\ \mathbf{x}_{t}+\mathbf{y}_{u_{1}}+\ldots+\mathbf{y}_{u_{m}}.

(22)

As
\mathbf{b}(c)=0
for all

, condition (a) is equivalent to
the equality (
22
) restricted to
H\cup P{\times}\mathbb{A}
.
By the first two columns in (
20
) and (
21
), and since
\mathbf{b}(s)=0
for

,
condition (b) is equivalent to
the equality (
22
) restricted to
Q\setminus\left\{q,q^{\prime}\right\}
, and
the conditions (c) and (d) are equivalent to
the equality (
22
) restricted to
\left\{q,q^{\prime}\right\}
.
∎

6.2
Violation of
\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}

Suppose
\mathcal{V}
violates
\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
.
We define a
dvass

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
of smaller rank and two its states
\widehat{q},\widehat{q}^{\prime}

such that bi-reachability of
q(\mathbf{0})
and
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
in
\mathcal{V}
is equivalent to bi-reachability
of
\widehat{q}(\mathbf{0})
and
\widehat{q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
.

Pumpability and boundedness.

Any run of the form
q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{\Delta})
(resp.
q(\mathbf{\Delta})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{0})
)
we call
forward
(resp.
backward
)
pump
from
q
.
Likewise we define forward (resp. backward) pumps from
q^{\prime}
.

A plain place
h\in H
(resp. an atom place
p\in P
) we call
forward pumpable
from
q
if there is a pump

q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{\Delta})
such that
\mathbf{\Delta}(h)>0

(resp.
\mathbf{\Delta}(p,a)>0
for some atom
a\in\mathbb{A}
).
Symmetrically,
a place
h\in H
(resp.
p\in P
) we call

backward pumpable
from
q
if there is a pump

q(\mathbf{\Delta})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{0})
such that
\mathbf{\Delta}(h)>0

(resp.
\mathbf{\Delta}(p,a)>0
for some atom
a\in\mathbb{A}
).
Likewise we define places forward (resp. backward) pumpable from
q^{\prime}
.
Finally, a (plain or atom)

if it is forward and backward pumpable both from
q
and
from
q^{\prime}
.
Otherwise, the place is called
unpumpable
.

Lemma 25
.

\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
holds if and only if all places are pumpable.

Proof.

In one direction,

\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
amounts to
simultaneous
(= using one pump)
forward and backward pumpability of all places, from both
q
and
q^{\prime}
.
In the converse direction, suppose all places are forward and backward pumpable
from both
q
and
q^{\prime}
.
We observe that
forward (resp. backward) pumps from
q
compose, and hence all places are
simultaneously

forward (resp. backward) pumpable from
q
. Likewise for
q^{\prime}
.
∎

We now introduce a suitable version of boundedness.
In case of plain places
h\in H
boundedness applies, as expected, to the value
\mathbf{v}(h)

in a configuration
s(\mathbf{v})
.
On the other hand in case of atom places
p\in P
, boundedness applies to the number of tokens on a place.
For uniformity we define the size of a place
p\in P
in a vector
\mathbf{v}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(p)=\sum\left\{\mathbf{v}(p,a)\,\middle|\,a\in\mathbb{A},\ \mathbf{v}(p,a)>0\right\}\in\mathbb{N}.

(23)

For a family
\mathcal{F}
of runs,
we say that a place
c\in H\cup P
is
bounded on
\mathcal{F}
by
B\in\mathbb{N}

if for every configuration
s(\mathbf{v})
appearing in every run in the family
\mathcal{F}
,

\mathbf{v}(c)\leq B
.
A place is
bounded on

\mathcal{F}
, if it is bounded on
\mathcal{F}
by some

.
Otherwise, the place is called
unbounded
on
\mathcal{F}
.

As forward (resp. backward) pumps compose,
every place which is forward (resp. backward) pumpable from
q
(resp.
q^{\prime}
) is necessarily unbounded
on respective pumps.
We show the opposite implication, namely unpumpable places are bounded on respective pumps:

Lemma 26
.

A place which is not forward (resp. backward) pumpable from

forward (resp. backward) pumps from
q
. Likewise for
q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

W.l.o.g. we focus on forward pumps from
q
only.
(Backward pumps are tackled similarly as forward ones, but in the
reversed

dvass
,
whose transitions
\left\{(s^{\prime},-\mathbf{v},s)\,\middle|\,(s,\mathbf{v},s^{\prime})\in T\right\}
are inverses of transitions of
\mathcal{V}
.)
Assuming that a place
c\in H\cup P
is unbounded on

q
, we
show that
c
is forward pumpable from
q
.
Unboundedness of

means that for every

there is a run

\displaystyle q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow s_{i}(\mathbf{v}_{i})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{\Delta}_{i})

(24)

such that

n_{i}:=\mathbf{v}_{i}(c)>i
.
By choosing a subsequence of
(n_{i})_{i}
we may assume w.l.o.g. that this sequence is strictly increasing.
As
\sqsubseteq
is a
wqo
, for some
j<i
we have
s_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{j})\sqsubseteq s_{i}(\mathbf{v}_{i})
, i.e.,
there is some
\sigma\in\textnormal{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\sigma(s_{j})=s_{i}
and

\sigma(\mathbf{v}_{j})\leq\mathbf{v}_{i}
.
Equivalently,
\sigma(\mathbf{v}_{j})+\mathbf{\Delta}=\mathbf{v}_{i}
for some nonnegative vector
\mathbf{\Delta}
.
Recall that
q=(\ell,\bot\!\!\!\!\bot)
and hence
\sigma(q)=q
.
Therefore, we can construct a new run, by first using the first half of
(

p(\mathbf{0})
to

, and then applying
\sigma
to
the second half
s_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{j})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{\Delta}_{j})
to return from

\sigma(s_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{j}))=\sigma(s_{j})(\sigma(\mathbf{v}_{j}))
:

q(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow s_{i}(\mathbf{v}_{i})=\sigma(s_{j})(\sigma(\mathbf{v}_{j})+\mathbf{\Delta})\longrightarrow q(\sigma(\mathbf{\Delta}_{j})+\mathbf{\Delta}).

By strict monotonicity of
(n_{i})_{i}
we have
n_{j}<n_{i}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{v}_{j}(c)<\mathbf{v}_{i}(c)
, which implies

\mathbf{\Delta}(c)>0
.
The place
c
is thus forward pumpable from
q
, as required.
∎

Reduction step.

Let
B\in\mathbb{N}
be the universal bound for all unpumpable places on all respective pumps.
Formally, let’s assume that
B
bounds every place which is not forward (resp. backward) pumpable
from
q
, on all forward (resp. backward) pumps from
q
, and

B
also bounds every place which is not forward (resp. backward) pumpable
from
q^{\prime}
, on all forward (resp. backward) pumps from
q^{\prime}
.
As
\mathcal{V}
violates
\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
, we know by Lemma
25
that there are some unpumpable
places.
We define a
dvass

\widehat{\mathcal{V}}=(\widehat{L},\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\widehat{H},\widehat{P},\widehat{T})

by, intuitively speaking, removing some such place.
Let
X=H\cup P{\times}\mathbb{A}
.

Case 1: some plain place is unpumpable.

Let
\widehat{P}:=P
and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{R}
.
We choose an arbitrary unpumpable
h\in H
,
and let
\widehat{H}:=H\setminus\left\{h\right\}
.
We keep track of values on
h
by extending the locations
\widehat{L}:=L\times\left\{0\ldots B\right\}
.
Finally, transitions
\widehat{T}
are obtained from transitions
T
by replacing each

t\ =\ ((\ell,\mu),\mathbf{v},(\ell^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}))\in T

by all the transitions of the form

\widehat{t}\ =\ ((\langle\ell,n\rangle,\mu),\ \mathbf{w},(\langle\ell^{\prime},n+\mathbf{v}(h)\rangle,\mu^{\prime})),

where

\mathbf{w}
is the restriction of
\mathbf{v}:{(H\cup P{\times}\mathbb{A})}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{Z}
to

\widehat{H}\cup P{\times}\mathbb{A}
.
Let

\widehat{q}=(\langle\ell,0\rangle,\bot\!\!\!\!\bot)
and
\widehat{q}^{\prime}=(\langle\ell^{\prime},0\rangle,\bot\!\!\!\!\bot)
.

Case 2: some atom place is unpumpable.

Let
\widehat{L}:=L
and
\widehat{H}:=H
.
We choose an arbitrary unpumpable
p\in P
,
remove place
p
, namely
\widehat{P}:=P\setminus\left\{p\right\}
, and
add
B
new registers, namely
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{R}\cup\{r_{1},\dots,r_{B}\}
, which store,
intuitively speaking, every possible content of the place
p
.
Using the new registers
the transitions
\widehat{T}
track, intuitively speaking,
the effect of transitions from
T
on the removed place
p
.
Every register valuation
\mu
naturally induces a vector
\widehat{\mu}:{\left\{p\right\}{\times}\mathbb{A}}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{N}
,
namely

\widehat{\mu}\ :=\ \oplus\left\{(p,a)\,\middle|\,\mu(r_{i})=a\neq\bot,\ i\in\left\{1\ldots B\right\}\right\}.

Using this notation we define the transitions
\widehat{T}
by replacing every transition

((\ell,\nu),\mathbf{v},(\ell^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}))\in T
with all the transitions of the form

\big((\ell,\nu\oplus\mu),\ \mathbf{w},\ (\ell^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}\oplus\mu^{\prime})\big),

where
\mathbf{w}
is the restriction of

\mathbf{v}:{(H\cup P{\times}\mathbb{A})}\to_{\text{\tiny fin}}\mathbb{Z}
to

H\cup\widehat{P}{\times}\mathbb{A}
, and

\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}\oplus\widehat{\mu}^{\prime}\ominus\widehat{\mu}
.
Let
\widehat{q},\widehat{q}^{\prime}
be extensions of the states
q,q^{\prime}
, respectively, by
empty valuation of all the new registers

.

Lemma 27

(Progress)
.

\text{\sc Rank}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})<\text{\sc Rank}(\mathcal{V})
.

Proof.

In the former case
\widehat{P}=P

|\widehat{H}|<|H|
, while in the latter case

\text{\sc Rank}(\widehat{\mathcal{V}})=(|\widehat{P}|,|\widehat{H}|,||\widehat{T}||)<(|P|,|H|,||T||)=\text{\sc Rank}(\mathcal{V})
.
∎

Lemma 28

(Correctness)
.

The configurations

q(\mathbf{0})
,
q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})
are bi-reachable
in
\mathcal{V}
if and only if
\widehat{q}(\mathbf{0})
,
\widehat{q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})

in
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proof.

As
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
is obtained from
\mathcal{V}
by restricting values in configurations on some places,
each run in
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
is automatically a run in
\mathcal{V}
, and bi-reachability in
\widehat{\mathcal{V}}
implies

\mathcal{V}
.

For the converse implication, suppose

,

are bi-reachable
in
\mathcal{V}
, and fix two arbitrary runs

and

\pi^{\prime}:q^{\prime}(\mathbf{0})\longrightarrow q(\mathbf{0})

Consider any unpumpable place

q

By Lemma
26
the place is bounded by

on all forward pumps from
q
.
In particular, it is bounded by

\pi^{\prime}

Therefore

If

if

we keep track of all tokens on

.
∎

(Effectiveness)

The set of pumpable places and the universal bound

Proof.

For a simple
\omega

Relying on Lemma

Specifically, to test if a place is forward pumpable from

Likewise we test if a place is backward pumpable from

(using the reverse

), or forward (resp. backward) pumpable from

.

Suppose there is some unpumpable place

By Lemma

Consider an atom place

7
Final remarks

We show decidability of the bi-reachability problem for Petri nets with equality data.
The problem subsumes coverability, and reachability in the reversible subclass, and therefore
the result pushes further the decidability border towards the reachability problem.
The latter problem (which we believe to be decidable) is still beyond our reach, and
development of this paper is not sufficient.
For instance, the approach of proving of Lemma
17
would fail for reachability, as we rely on the fact that
bi-reachability implies a cycle.
Moreover,
\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}
reduction step would fail as well, as it assumes that
a transition (orbit) is either unusable, or usable unboundedly,
while in case of reachability a transition can be usable only
boundedly
.

Our approach is specific to equality data, and thus we leave unresolved the status of bi-reachability
in case of ordered data.
In case of ordered data domain the approach of proving Lemma
17
would fail again,
as the trick of applying all permutations of
S
would be impossible.
Moreover, it is not clear how to implement
\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}
reduction step, as no procedure computing
coverability sets is known.

Maffei’s action and symplectic Springer action for quiver varieties
Yaochen Wu

Abstract
. We examine the relationship between the actions of two Weyl groups on the cohomology of a smooth quiver variety: the Maffei’s action of the Weyl group associated to the quiver, defined in [ Maf02 ] , and the symplectic Springer action of the Namikawa-Weyl group of the affine quiver variety, defined in [ MN19 ] . We show there is a natural map from the former group to the latter, which is an embedding in favorable situations, and this map intertwines their actions on the cohomology. This answers a question raised in [ MN19 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Quiver varieties

We recall the construction of quiver varieties following
[
Nak98
]
.
Let
Q
be a quiver without edge loops. Let
Q_{0},Q_{1}
be its set of vertices and arrows respectively.
For
i\neq j\in Q_{0}
, denote by
n_{ij}
the number of arrows between
i
and
j
, regardless of the orientation.
Set
c_{ij}=-n_{ij}
for
i\neq j
, and
c_{ii}=2
.
Let
\alpha_{i}
denote the simple real root attached to the vertex
i
. Denote
\mathfrak{h}_{Q}
to be the complex vector space spanned by the basis
\{\alpha_{i}|i\in Q_{0}\}
. Define the Tits form on
\mathfrak{h}_{Q}
by

(1.1)

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=c_{ij}.

The matrix
(c_{ij})
is a symmetric Cartan matrix. The
roots
of
Q
, real or imaginary, are defined to be the roots of the corresponding Kac-Moody algebra. If
\alpha
is a real root then
(\alpha,\alpha)=2
. If
\alpha
is an imaginary root, we say
\alpha
is
isotropic
(respectively,
non-isotropic
) if
(\alpha,\alpha)=0
(respectively, if
(\alpha,\alpha)<0
).

Let
v=(v_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{Q_{0}}
be a dimension vector, and
w=(w_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
be a framing vector.
A
framed representation
of
Q
with dimension
v
and framing
w
consists of the following data:

(1)

To each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
we assign a vector space
V_{i}
that has dimension
v_{i}
, and a morphism
p_{i}\in Hom(W_{i},V_{i})
, where
W_{i}
is a fixed
w_{i}
-dimensional vector space.

(2)

To each arrow
a\in Q_{1}
we assign an element
x_{a}\in\Hom(V_{t(a)},V_{h(a)})
.

Define

(1.2)

{}R=R(Q,v,w):=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in Q_{1}\end{subarray}}\Hom(V_{t(a)},V_{h(a)})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\Hom(W_{i},V_{i}).

This is the space of representations of
Q
with dimension vector
v
and framing
w
.
We identify
T^{*}R
with the space of representations of
\overline{Q}
, the
double quiver
of
Q
, i.e. for each
a\in Q_{1}
we add an arrow
\overline{a}
so that
h(\overline{a})=t(a),t(\overline{a})=h(a)
, and for each
i\in Q_{0}
we add a coframing in
\Hom(V_{i},W_{i})
.
For an element
r\in T^{*}R
, we write

(1.3)

r=(x_{a},y_{a},p_{i},q_{i})_{a\in Q_{1},i\in Q_{0}},

where
x_{a}\in\Hom(V_{t(a)},V_{h(a)}),y_{a}\in\Hom(V_{h(a)},V_{t(a)}),p_{i}\in\Hom(W_{i},V_{i})
and
q_{i}\in\Hom(V_{i},W_{i})
.

Write
G=GL(v):=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL(V_{i})
. This group acts naturally on
R
, and therefore we have an induced Hamiltonian
G
-action on
T^{*}R
. Let
\mu:T^{*}R\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the standard moment map.

We can identify
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\cong\mathbb{C}^{Q_{0}}
, and write
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathbb{C}^{Q_{0}}
. Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{p}
. Let
\theta
be a character of
G
; we can view
\theta
as an element of
\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
.
We view
\theta
and
\lambda
as elements of
\mathfrak{h}_{Q}^{*}
.
By definition, the Nakajima quiver variety
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(Q,v,w)
is the GIT quotient

(1.4)

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(Q,v,w):=\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta-ss}/\!/G.

When the quiver
Q
is clear from the context, we will write
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)
for the quiver variety. Note that if
\theta=0
, then
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta-ss}=\mu^{-1}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{0}(v,w)
is affine.
We also define

(1.5)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\theta}(v,w):=\mu^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})^{\theta-ss}/\!/G.

There is a natural map
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\theta}(v,w)\to\mathfrak{p}
induced from
\mu
, and
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(v,w)
is the fiber of this map over
\lambda\in\mathfrak{p}
.

We introduce the following notations.

(1)

We denote by
\rho
the natural projective morphism
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)\to\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{0}(v,w)
.

(2)

If
r\in\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta-ss}
, then we denote its image in
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)
by
[r]^{\theta}
.

(3)

If
r\in\mu^{-1}(\lambda)
, then we denote its semisimplification as a
\overline{Q}
-representation by
r^{ss}
.

We say the pair
(\theta,\lambda)
is
generic
if for any
v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{Q_{0}}
,
v^{\prime}\leq v
, we have
(v^{\prime}\cdot\theta,v^{\prime}\cdot\lambda)\neq(0,0)
. We say
\theta
(resp.
\lambda
) is generic if
(\theta,0)
(resp.
(0,\lambda)
) is generic.

We collect a few known results about quiver varieties.

(1)

If
r\in\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta-ss}
then
\rho([r]^{\theta})=[r^{ss}]^{0}
. See
[
Kir16
, Theorem 2.10, Section 10.1]
.

(2)

If
(\theta,\lambda)
is generic, then the
G
-action on
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta-ss}
is free and
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)
is smooth. See
[
Nak94
, Theorem 2.8]
and its proof, and
[
Nak98
, Remark 3.16]
.
In particular, if
\lambda
is generic, then
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)=\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta-ss}
for any
\theta
, and the projective map
\rho:\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)\to\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w)
is an isomorphism.

(3)

If
\theta
is generic, then the affinization
\Spec\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w)]
is independent of the choice of
\theta
. We denote it by
\mathcal{M}_{0}(v,w)
. See
[
BL21
, Corollary 2.4]
.

(4)

If the moment map
\mu
is flat, then
\mathcal{M}_{0}(v,w)\cong\mathcal{M}_{0}^{0}(v,w)
. In this case,
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w)\to\mathcal{M}_{0}^{0}(v,w)
is a conical symplectic resolution of singularities. See
[
BL21
, Proposition 2.3, Proposition 2.5]
.

It will be useful to identify
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)
with a quiver variety without framing by the method in
[
CB01
, Section 1, Remarks]
. Namely, we define a new quiver
{Q^{\infty}}
as follows. We set
{Q^{\infty}_{0}}=Q_{0}\coprod\{\infty\}
. The arrows between the vertices in
Q_{0}
are the same as those in
Q_{1}
. In addition, for each
i\in Q_{0}
, there are
w_{i}
arrows from the vertex
i
to the vertex
\infty
.
Denote the new simple root associated to the vertex
\infty
by
\alpha_{\infty}
. Define the extended dimension vector
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{{Q^{\infty}_{0}}}
by
\tilde{v}_{\infty}=1
and
\tilde{v}_{i}=v_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
.
It is clear that
R(Q,v,w)=R({Q^{\infty}},\tilde{v})
, so
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,v,w)=\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{\tilde{\theta}}({Q^{\infty}},\tilde{v})
,
where
\tilde{\lambda}_{\infty}=-v\cdot\lambda
,
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
;
\tilde{\theta}_{\infty}=-\theta\cdot v,\tilde{\theta}_{i}=\theta_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
. To simplify notations, we will write
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(\tilde{v})
for
\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{\tilde{\theta}}({Q^{\infty}},\tilde{v})
when there is no danger of confusion.

1.2.
Quiver Weyl group

We define the following Coxeter group associated to
Q
.

Definition 1.1
.

The
quiver Weyl group

W_{Q}
of
Q
is generated by
\{s_{i}|i\in Q_{0}\}
, subject to the relations:

(1)

s_{i}^{2}=1
;

(2)

s_{i}s_{j}=s_{j}s_{i}
if
n_{ij}=0
;

(3)

(s_{i}s_{j})^{3}=1
if
n_{ij}=1
.

We remark that we impose no relations between
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
when
n_{ij}\geq 2
.
The group
W_{Q}
is the same as the Weyl group associated to the Kac-Moody algebra defined by the symmetric Cartan matrix
(c_{ij})
.

Similarly we can define
W_{Q^{\infty}}
. We view
W_{Q}
as a subgroup of
W_{Q^{\infty}}
.

There is a natural representation of
W_{Q}
in
\mathfrak{h}_{Q}
given by

(1.6)

s_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\alpha_{j}-c_{ij}\alpha_{i}.

The dual representation is described as follows. If
\chi=\sum\chi_{i}\alpha_{i}^{*}\in\mathfrak{h}_{Q}^{*}
where
\alpha_{i}^{*}
is the dual basis to
\alpha_{i}
, then

(1.7)

s_{i}^{*}(\chi)=\sum_{j\in Q_{0}}(\chi_{j}-c_{ij}\chi_{i})\alpha_{j}^{*}.

Similarly, we can define the natural representations of
W_{Q^{\infty}}
in
\mathfrak{h}_{Q^{\infty}}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{Q^{\infty}}^{*}
. We have a representation of
W_{Q}
on
\mathfrak{h}_{Q^{\infty}}
by the same formula as (
1.6
), and allow
j=\infty
.

Definition 1.2
.

We denote the stabilizer of
\tilde{v}
in
W_{Q}
by
W(v,w)
.

1.3.
Poisson deformations and Namikawa-Weyl groups

We recall the definition of the Namikawa-Weyl group associated to a conical symplectic singularity.

Definition 1.3

(
[
Bea00
]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal affine Poisson variety.

(1)

We say
X

has symplectic singularities
if the Poisson structure on its smooth locus
X^{\reg}
is nondegenerate, thus inducing a symplectic form
\omega
, and there is a projective resolution of singularities
\rho:Y\to X
such that
\rho^{*}\omega
extends to a regular 2-form on
Y
. Note that
\rho^{*}\omega
need not be nondegenerate.

(2)

We say
X
is
conical
if there is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
X
that contracts
X
to a point, and
\omega
has positive weight under this action.

(3)

We say
\rho
is a
symplectic resolution of singularities
if
\rho^{*}\omega
extends to a symplectic form on
Y
; we say
\rho
is
conical
if
Y
is equipped with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action so that
\rho
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
X
be a normal Poisson variety equipped with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action, and the Poisson bracket has degree
-d
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. A
graded Poisson deformation
of
X
is the data
(\mathcal{X},B,j)
, where:

(1)

B=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}B_{i}
is a finitely generated graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, such that the degree 0 piece
B_{0}=\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

\mathcal{X}
is a Poisson
B
-variety equipped with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action, the Poisson structure on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
is
B
-linear, and the structure morphism
m_{X}:\mathcal{X}\to\Spec(B)
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant and flat.

(3)

j:X\xrightarrow{\sim}m_{X}^{-1}(0)
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant isomorphism of Poisson varieties, where
0\in\Spec(B)
corresponds to the maximal ideal
\bigoplus_{i>0}B_{i}
.

Let
X
be as in
Definition

1.4
and
(\mathcal{X},B,j),(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},B^{\prime},j^{\prime})
be two graded Poisson deformations.
A
morphism
of graded Poisson deformations from
(\mathcal{X},B,j)
to
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},B^{\prime},j^{\prime})
consists of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant morphisms
\Phi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and

, such that the following diagram is Cartesian

1.4.
Goal and structure of the paper

Suppose
Y\to X
is a conical symplectic resolution, and
W^{\mathrm{N}}
is the Namikawa-Weyl group of
X
.
In
[
MN19
]
, the authors construct the
symplectic Springer
action of
W^{\mathrm{N}}
on
H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{C})
. We will recall this construction in
Section

3
. In particular, there is a symplectic Springer action of
W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
on
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{C})
. On the other hand,
[
Maf02
]
constructed an action of
W(v,w)
on
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(v,w),\mathbb{C})
, which we call
Maffei’s action
, and we will recall it in
Section

4
.

When
Q
is a Dynkin quiver of type
ADE
and
(\tilde{v},\alpha_{i})\leq 0
for all
i\in Q_{0}
,
W(v,w)=W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
by
[
MN19
, Theorem 5.4]
.
In
[
MN19
, Remark 5.9]
, the authors raise questions on the relations between
W(v,w)
and
W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
, and the Maffei and symplectic Springer actions on

for a general quiver. We aim to answer these questions.

For any quiver
Q
without edge loops denote the
fundamental region
of
Q
to be

(1.11)

\mathcal{F}_{Q}=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}_{Q}|(\alpha,\alpha_{i})\leq 0\ \forall i\in Q_{0}\}.

In
[
CB01
]
the author defined an important set
\Sigma_{0}
of positive roots of a quiver, which we recall in
2.3
. The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
Q
be a quiver without edge loops,
\theta
be generic,
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q^{\infty}}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w)
be nonempty.
Then there is a direct product decomposition
W(v,w)=W(v,w)_{\Irr}\times W(v,w)_{\Ess}
, satisfying the following properties.

(1)

The subgroup
W(v,w)_{\Irr}
is either trivial or a direct product of type

Weyl groups. When

,

(2)

The restriction of Maffei’s action on

to

is trivial.

(3)

There is an embedding
\iota:W(v,w)_{\Ess}\hookrightarrow W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
, and the natural action of
W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
on
H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{C})
and the Maffei’s action of

on

.

(4)

The map

intertwines the Maffei’s action of

and the symplectic Springer action of

on

.

In

we present some preliminary results on

4.
Maffei’s action and tautological line bundles

In this section we recall the construction of the Maffei’s isomorphisms, and examine the Maffei’s action of
W(v,w)
on
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{C})
. We will compare the Maffei’s action with the natural

action on
H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{C})
.

Theorem-Definition 4.1
.

Suppose
(\theta,\lambda)
is generic, and let
\sigma\in W_{Q}
.

(1)

(
[
Maf02
, Section 3]
) There is an isomorphism of varieties

(4.1)

\Phi_{\sigma}:\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{*}\lambda}^{\sigma^{*}\theta}(\sigma\bullet v,w).

Here
\sigma\bullet v
is defined by
\sigma\bullet v+\alpha_{\infty}=\sigma\tilde{v}
.
Moreover, if
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in W_{Q}
then
\Phi_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}=\Phi_{\sigma_{1}}\circ\Phi_{\sigma_{2}}
.

(2)

(
[
Maf02
, Corollary 49]
and
[
Nak94
, Corollary 9.3]
) There is a
W(v,w)
-action on
H^{n}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{Z})
, and hence on
H^{n}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{C})
.

We call
\Phi_{\sigma}
the
Maffei’s isomorphism
and the above action on
H^{n}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{Z})
the
Maffei’s action
.

We will focus on the case where
\theta
is generic,
\lambda=0
, and

.

4.1.

The construction of Maffei’s isomorphism will be useful later, so let us recall it in the case of a simple reflection, following

, Section 3.1]
. Fix

, pick

Write

and
\tilde{v}^{\prime}=s_{j}\tilde{v}

\Phi_{s_{j}}:\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\theta}(\tilde{v})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\theta^{\prime}}(\tilde{v}^{\prime})

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(\tilde{v})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{\theta^{\prime}}(\tilde{v}^{\prime})

Without loss of generality assume

is a source.
For

For an element

(2)

A_{j}^{\prime}B_{j}^{\prime}=A_{j}B_{j}-\lambda_{j}\id_{T_{j}}

For
(r,r^{\prime})\in Z_{0}^{ss}
, define

(4.15)

F(r,r^{\prime})=\det(\alpha_{j}^{\prime})\det(\beta_{j})\det(g)^{-1}.

Note that
F(r,r^{\prime})
is independent of the choice of
A_{j,0}^{\prime},B_{j,0}
and

.

Now let
\chi=(\chi_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
be a character of
\GL(v)
and
s_{j}^{*}\chi=(\chi_{i}-c_{ij}\chi_{j})_{i\in Q_{0}}
be the corresponding character of
\GL(v^{\prime})
. Define a character
\tilde{\chi}=\chi\times 1

, i.e.

(4.16)

G_{j,v}=\GL(V_{j})\times\GL(V_{j}^{\prime})\times\prod_{i\neq j}\GL(V_{i})\ni(g_{j},g_{j}^{\prime},g_{i})_{i\neq j}\mapsto\chi(g_{j},g_{i})_{i\neq j}.

\begin{split}\tilde{p}:Z_{0}^{ss}\times\mathbb{C}_{\tilde{\chi}}&\to\mu^{-1}(0)^{\theta-ss}\times\mathbb{C}_{\chi}\\

4.3.
Maffei’s action on the cohomology

Let
\star
denote the Maffei’s action of
W(v,w)
on
H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{C})
. Let us recall its construction, following
[
Maf02
, Section 5]
. We need the hyper-Kähler construction of quiver varieties, see
[
Nak94
, Section 2]
for details.
Namely, the vector space
T^{*}R
has a natural hyper-Kähler structure. Let
U=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}U(v_{i})
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
,
\mathfrak{u}
be its Lie algebra;
let
\mathfrak{p}_{hk}:=(\mathfrak{u}^{*})^{U}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^{3}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
.
The group
U
acts on
T^{*}R
and respects the hyper-Kähler structure.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg}
be the open subset of
\mathfrak{p}_{hk}
containing all
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
such that
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot u\neq 0
for all
0<u\leq v
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}:T^{*}R\to\mathfrak{p}_{hk}
denote the hyperkahler moment map. Then by
[
Maf02
, Lemma 48]
, the natural morphism

(4.20)

\underline{\boldsymbol{\mu}}:\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg})/U\to\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg}

is a locally trivial fibration.
Recall
\underline{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{*}
denotes the pushforward functor for the category of constructible sheaves with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients.
The base
\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg}
is simply-connected. Therefore, the local system

(4.21)

\underline{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg})/U}

on
\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg}
has trivial monodromy, see e.g.
[
Ach21
, Theorem 1.7.9]
.
In particular, for any
\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg}
, the cohomology groups

H^{n}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})/U,\mathbb{Z})
and
H^{n}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime})/U,\mathbb{Z})
are naturally identified.
By
[
Nak01
, Proposition 2.4.1]
, for generic
\theta\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}},\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{Q_{0}}
, there is an inclusion
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}((i\theta,\lambda))\hookrightarrow\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta-ss}
, inducing a homeomorphism
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}((i\theta,\lambda))/U\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w)
.
Therefore, for any generic
(\theta,\lambda)
and
(\theta^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
, and for any
n\geq 0
, there is a natural isomorphism

(4.22)

\mathrm{top}:H^{n}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{n}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{\theta^{\prime}}(v,w),\mathbb{Z})

where top stands for “topological”.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\theta
be generic and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{p}
be generic.
For any
\sigma\in W(v,w)
, the Maffei’s action
\sigma\star
is defined by the composition

(4.23)

\sigma\star:H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{top}}H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{*}\lambda}^{\sigma^{*}\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\Phi_{\sigma}^{*}}H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{top}}H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}(v,w),\mathbb{Z}).

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\chi
be a character of
G
and
\sigma\in W(v,w)
. Then

(4.24)

\sigma\star\kappa(\chi)=\kappa(\sigma^{*}\chi).

Proof.

Under the hyper-Kähler setup, the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\theta}(v,w),\chi)
is the restriction of the line bundle

(4.25)

\mathcal{O}_{hk}(\chi):=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg})\times^{U}\mathbb{C}_{\chi|_{U}}

to the fiber over
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=(i\theta,\lambda)
.
Here
\chi|_{U}
denotes the restriction of
\chi
to
U
.
The first Chern class
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{hk}(\chi))
gives a global section to the degree 2 part of

\underline{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{hk}^{\reg})/U}

Corollary 4.9
.

Suppose
\tilde{v}\in\Sigma_{0}
. Then the map
\iota:Gen_{\Ess}\to W^{\mathrm{N}}(v_{i})
extends to a group embedding
W(v,w)\hookrightarrow W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
.

Proof.

The group
W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
is naturally identified with a subgroup of
\GL(\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{h}_{i})
. By
Theorem

4.8
, the Maffei’s action restricts to a representation of
W(v,w)
in
\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{h}_{i}
as well, and the images of the generators in
\GL(\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{h}_{i})
lie in
W^{\mathrm{N}}(v,w)
. Therefore, it suffices to show that the
W(v,w)
-representation in
\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is faithful.

If
\sigma\in W(v,w)
acts trivially on
\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{h}_{i}
then
p_{i}(\sigma\star\kappa(\chi))=p_{i}\kappa(\chi)
for all
i
and
\chi
.
By
Proposition

4.6
, this is equivalent to
p_{i}(\kappa(\sigma^{*}\chi-\chi))=0
for all
i
and
\chi
.
Assume
j\in Q_{0}
and
(\tilde{v},\alpha_{j})=0
. By
Proposition

2.7
, the representation type of some codimension 2 leaf
\mathcal{L}_{i}
contains
\alpha_{j}
.
Then by
Proposition

4.7
,
\alpha_{j}\cdot(\chi-\sigma^{*}\chi)=0
for any
\chi
. It follows that
\sigma\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j}
for all
j
such that
(\tilde{v},\alpha_{j})=0
. We claim
\sigma=1
, thus proving faithfulness. In fact, assume
\sigma\neq 1
, and let
\sigma=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
be a reduced expression, where each
s_{i_{j}}
is a generator of

in

2.1
.
Then
\ell(ws_{i_{k}})=\ell(w)-1
, so by
[

∎

Proposition 4.12

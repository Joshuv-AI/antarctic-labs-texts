On a super - analog of the Schur - Weyl Duality
Alexei Borodin, Natasha Rozhkovskaya

Abstract
. Two super-analogs of the Schur - Weyl duality are considered: the duality of actions in ( ℂ m | n ) ⊗ N ({\mathbb{C}}^{m|n})^{\otimes N} of the Lie superalgebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m , n ) \mathfrak{gl}(m,n) and the symmetric group S N S_{N} , and the duality of actions of the Lie superalgebra Q ⁡ ( n ) Q(n) and a certain finite group S ​ e ​ ( N ) Se(N) in ( ℂ n | n ) ⊗ N ({\mathbb{C}}^{n|n})^{\otimes N} . We construct an isomorphism of symmetric and universal enveloping algebras of these Lie superalgebras called special symmetrization. Using this isomorphism of vector spaces we describe explicitly the duality between the centers of the corresponding universal enveloping algebras and the group algebras.

Erwin Schödinger Institute in Vienna. They were inspired by the work of G.I. Olshanski
[

]

on the so-called
special symmetrization
. Further results on special symmetrization
were obtained in
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
; however, the case of Lie superalgebras that we dealt with seems to have so far
remained uncovered by the existing literature. For that reason, and also because of the continuing interest of researchers to the structure
of centers of universal enveloping algebras of Lie superalgebras (see, e.g.,

[
1
,

,

,
9
,

]
), we have decided to make our work publicly available.
The text below is a lightly edited version of the 1995 preprint.

Let
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
be the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
, and let
{\mathbb{C}}[S_{N}]
be the group algebra of the symmetric group
S_{N}
. These two associative superalgebras (all the elements of the second one are even) act on the tensor space

({\mathbb{C}}^{m|n})^{\otimes N}
. For
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
it is the
N
-th tensor power of the vector representation, and
{\mathbb{C}}[S_{N}]
permutes the components
({\mathbb{C}}^{m|n})
of the tensor product. It was proved in
[
11
]
,
see also
[
2
]
in the case of
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
,
that these two actions are dual in the following sense: the commutant of the image of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
in
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{C}}^{m|n})^{\otimes N}
coincides with the image of
{\mathbb{C}}[S_{N}]
, and
vice versa
. This duality is a super-analog of the well-known Schur - Weyl duality for
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
and
S_{N}
actions on
({\mathbb{C}}^{n})^{\otimes N}
.

The matrix Lie superalgebra

\displaystyle Q(n)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
B&A\end{pmatrix}|\,A,B\in\text{Mat}\,(n,{\mathbb{C}})\right\}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(n,n)

(0.1)

has an irreducible vector representation. A dual group for the action of this Lie superalgebra in
({\mathbb{C}}^{n|n})^{\otimes N}

is a double covering of the wreath product of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{N}
and
S_{N}
with the natural action of
S_{N}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{N}

[
11
]
. We refer to this group as the
Sergeev group
and denote by
Se(N)
(see a precise definition in Section
2
).
We refer to
[
3
]
for a detailed exposition on the Schur-Sergeev duality.

The above dualities imply that the centers of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
and
{\mathbb{C}}[S_{N}]
, denoted by
Z(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
and
Z(S_{N})
respectively, as well as the centers of
U(Q(n))
and
{\mathbb{C}}[Se(N)]
, denoted by
Z(Q(n))
and
Z(Se(N))
respectively, have the same images in the automorphisms of the corresponding tensor spaces. The problem is to find explicitly an element of
Z(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
(or
Z(Q(n))
) that acts on a tensor space identically with a given central element from the group algebra of the dual group. This problem was solved in
[
4
]
for the classical Schur - Weyl duality. The result has important applications to the theory of symmetric functions and to the classification of characters of the infinite symmetric group. The key to solving the “classical” problem is in introducing a suitable map called
special symmetrization
that establishes an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
-modules
S(\mathfrak{gl}(n))
(symmetric algebra of the vector space
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
) and

U(\mathfrak{gl}(n))
. The special symmetrization differs from the usual symmetrization, and for the first time it appeared in
[
7
]

in the study of classical infinite-dimensional Lie groups and corresponding analogues of universal enveloping algebras. Generalizations of special symmetrization and its versions for classical Lie algebras were constructed in
[
6
,
8
]
.

In this note we define the special symmetrization in the super-case for
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
and
Q(n)
. Moreover, using this map we define elements of
Z(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
and
Z(Q(n))
that coincide in
\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{C}}^{m|n})^{\otimes N}
with the image of natural basis elements
of
Z(S_{N})
and
Z(Se(N))
. Our main results are Theorems
2.4
and
2.5
below.

1.
Special Symmetrization

Let
S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
denote the symmetric algebra, let
T(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
be the tensor algebra, and let
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
be the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
. Define a linear map
S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))\otimes\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)\,\to\,S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
, sending
A\otimes X
to an element
A*X\in S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
as follows. For homogeneous elements
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}
and
X
we set

\displaystyle(Y_{1}\dots Y_{k})*X=Y_{1}\dots Y_{k}X+\sum_{r=1}^{k}(-1)^{P(r)}Y_{1}\dots Y_{r-1}\langle XY_{r}\rangle Y_{r+1}\dots Y_{k}.

(1.1)

Here
\langle XY\rangle
denotes the product in the associative matrix algebra and

P(r)=\sum_{s=r+1}^{k}p(Y_{s})\,p(X),

where
p
is the parity function on the super space
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
.

Consider the linear map

\displaystyle\begin{split}T(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))\,&\to\,S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)),\\
X_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes X_{k}\,&\mapsto\,((\dots(X_{1}*X_{2})*\dots)*X_{k-1})*X_{k}.\end{split}

(1.2)

Lemma 1.1
.

The map (
1.2
) factors through to a linear map
\tilde{\sigma}:U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))\to S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
.

Proof.

We have to check that for any monomials
A,B
and homogeneous elements
X,Y\in\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
the image of
A\otimes(X\otimes Y-(-1)^{p(X)p(Y)}Y\otimes X)\otimes B
is equal to the image of
A\otimes[X,Y]\otimes B
. This is verified by a direct calculation using the identity
\langle XY\rangle-(-1)^{p(X)p(Y)}\langle YX\rangle=[X,Y]
.
∎

Proposition 1.2
.

The map
\tilde{\sigma}
from Lemma
1.1
is invertible.

Proof.

Let
\{E_{ij}\}
be the standard matrix units, and let
U_{k}
(or
V_{k}
) be a linear span of monomials
\{E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{m}j_{m}},m\leq k\}
in
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
(or in
S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
). From (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) we obtain

\tilde{\sigma}(E_{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{m}j_{m}})=E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{m}j_{m}}+A,\quad A\in V_{m-1}.

Hence
\tilde{\sigma}(U_{k})\subseteq V_{k}
, and in the basis
\{E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{m}j_{m}},m\leq k\}
the map
\tilde{\sigma}|_{U_{k}}
is represented by a triangular matrix with ones on the main diagonal. This means that for every
k=0,1,\dots,
the map
\tilde{\sigma}|_{U_{k}}
is invertible, and therefore
\tilde{\sigma}
is invertible on the whole space
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
.
∎

Thus,
\tilde{\sigma}
defines a linear isomorphism
\sigma=\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}:S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))\to U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
. This isomorphism can be regarded as an analog of the special symmetrization introduced in
[
4
]
, see also
[
7
]
.

We shall obtain an explicit formula for the map
\tilde{\sigma}
.
The image of a monomial
E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M}j_{M}}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
under
\tilde{\sigma}
is equal to the sum of all monomials in
S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
that can be obtained as follows. Fix a partition of the set
\{1,\dots,M\}
into disjoint subsets. Let us group matrix units corresponding to each subset together and multiply matrices in each subset. The result of application of
\tilde{\sigma}
is a sum of basis monomials in
S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
, each supplied with a coefficient
\pm 1
.

To formalize the above description we need some notation.
Let
\alpha
be a partition of the set
\{1,\dots,M\}
into subsets (blocks):

\alpha=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}.

A partition
\alpha
is called
regular
if elements in every block are ordered as

m_{q}<\dots<l_{q},\,q=1,\dots,r
, and if the blocks are listed in the ascending order of their first elements:

m_{1}<\dots<m_{s}<\dots<m_{r},\,1<\dots<s<\dots<r
.

Consider a regular partition
\alpha=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}
. For
t=1,\dots,r
, set

\displaystyle\delta_{t}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\prod_{k=1}^{d_{t}-1}\delta_{j_{s_{k}}}^{i_{s_{k+1}}},&\text{if}\quad d_{t}>1,\\
1,&\text{if}\quad d_{t}=1,\end{cases}

where
\delta^{i}_{j}
is the Kronecker delta,
\{s_{1}=m_{t}\dots s_{d_{t}}=l_{t}\}
is the block number
t
in the partition
\alpha
, and
d_{t}
is the length of this block. Set

\displaystyle\delta(\alpha)=\prod_{t=1}^{r}\delta_{t}(\alpha).

Introduce functions
\theta_{k}(s),\eta_{k}(s)
,
k=1,\dots,r
, such that for
s\in\{1,\dots,M\}
we have

\displaystyle\theta_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\max\{u<s,\,u\in\{m_{k},\dots,l_{k}\}\},&\text{if the $k$-th block contains a number less than $s$};\\
0,&\text{otherwise;}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}m_{k},&\text{if}\quad m_{k}<s;\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

With
p
being the parity function on
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
, we now set
p_{ij}=p(E_{ij})
and assume that
p_{i_{m}j_{k}}=0
for
k=0
or
m=0
. Define

\displaystyle q_{s}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=p_{i_{s}j_{s}}\sum_{k>t}p_{i_{\eta_{k}(s)}j_{\theta_{k}(s)}},\quad\text{for}\quad s\in\{m_{t},\dots,l_{t}\},

\displaystyle q(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{M}q_{s}(\alpha).

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
E_{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M}j_{M}}
be a basis element of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}(E_{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M}j_{M}})=\sum_{\alpha}(-1)^{q(\alpha)}\delta(\alpha)E_{i_{m_{1}}j_{l_{1}}}\dots E_{i_{m_{r}}j_{l_{r}}},

(1.3)

where the summation is taken over the set of all regular partitions
\alpha=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}
of

\{1,\dots,M\}
.

Proof.

Induction on
M
.

(1)

For
M=2

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}E_{i_{2}j_{2}})=E_{i_{1},j_{1}}*E_{i_{2}j_{2}}=E_{i_{1},j_{1}}E_{i_{2}j_{2}}+\delta_{j_{1}}^{i_{2}}E_{i_{1}j_{2}}.

(2)

By the inductive hypothesis we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M}j_{M}})=\sum_{\beta}(-1)^{q(\beta)}\delta(\beta)E_{i_{m_{1}}j_{l_{1}}}\dots E_{i_{m_{r}}j_{l_{r}}},

where
\beta=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}
is a regular partition of
\{1,\dots,M\}
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\sigma}&(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}})=\tilde{\sigma}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M}j_{M}})*E_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}}\\
&=\sum_{\beta}(-1)^{q(\beta)}\delta(\beta)E_{i_{m_{1}}j_{l_{1}}}\dots E_{i_{m_{r}}j_{l_{r}}}E_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\beta}\sum_{t=1}^{r}(-1)^{q(\beta)+\tilde{p}(t)}\delta(\beta)\,\delta_{j_{l_{t}}}^{i_{M+1}}\,E_{i_{m_{1}}j_{l_{1}}}\dots E_{i_{m_{t}}j_{M+1}}E_{i_{m_{r}}j_{l_{r}}},\end{split}

(1.4)

with
\tilde{p}(t)=\sum_{k=t+1}^{r}p_{i_{m_{k}}j_{l_{k}}}p_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}}
.

Every regular partition of the set
\{1,\dots,M+1\}
can be obtained from a regular partition of the set
\{1,\dots,M\}

by inserting
M+1
at the end of one of the blocks. Thus, (
1.4
) is a sum of monomials
E_{i_{m_{1}^{\prime}}\,j_{l_{1}^{\prime}}}\dots E_{i_{m^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}}\,j_{{}_{l^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}}}}
taken with some coefficients, where the summation is over all regular partitions

\{m^{\prime}_{1}\dots l^{\prime}_{1}\}\dots\{m^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}\dots l^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}\}
of the set
\{1,\dots,M+1\}
.

Let us compute the coefficients of the monomials. For

\alpha=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}\{M+1\}

and

\beta=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}q(\alpha)&=q_{M+1}(\alpha)+\sum_{k=1}^{M}q_{k}(\alpha)=q(\beta),\\
\delta(\alpha)&=\delta_{r+1}(\alpha)\prod_{k=1}^{r}\delta_{k}(\alpha)=\delta_{r+1}(\alpha)\delta(\beta)=\delta(\beta).\end{split}

(1.5)

For

\alpha=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{t}\dots l_{t},M+1\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}\

and

\beta=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}

we have

\displaystyle q_{M+1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=t+1}^{r}p_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}}p_{i_{m_{k}}j_{l_{k}}},

\displaystyle q_{s}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=q_{s}(\beta)\quad\text{ for every $s\in\{1,\dots,M\}$.}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}q(\alpha)&=q(\beta)+q_{M+1}(\alpha)=q(\beta)+\tilde{p}(t),\\
\delta(\alpha)&=\delta^{i_{M+1}}_{j_{l_{t}}}\delta(\beta).\end{split}

(1.6)

The claim of the proposition follows from (
1.4
), (
1.5
), and (
1.6
) immediately.

∎

From Proposition
1.3
it is easy to deduce that
\tilde{\sigma}
is an isomorphism not only of vector spaces, but of
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
-modules
U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
and
S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
.

Let us consider the space of polynomials in
(m+n)^{2}
variables
\{x_{ij}\}
. The parity of
x_{ij}
is set to be equal to the parity of
E_{ij}
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}
the algebra of polynomial differential operators acting on this space of polynomials. Define a map
\partial
from

\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
to
\mathcal{D}
by
\partial:E_{ij}\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{m+n}x_{ki}\partial_{kj}
. One can easily check that the map
\partial
is a homomorphism of Lie superalgebras. Therefore, it can be extended to a homomorphism
\partial:U(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))\to\mathcal{D}
. It can be proved that
Ker\,\partial=\{0\}
. The construction is similar to the realization of a universal enveloping algebra by left-invariant differential operators on a Lie group. Thus,

\displaystyle\partial(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{l}j_{l}})=\left(\sum_{k_{1}}x_{k_{1}i_{1}}\partial_{k_{1}j_{1}}\right)\dots\left(\sum_{k_{l}}x_{k_{l}i_{l}}\partial_{k_{l}j_{l}}\right).

(1.7)

Now we shall give a formula for the special symmetrization in terms of differential operators.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\tilde{\partial}=\partial\circ\sigma
. Then for
E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M}j_{M}}\in S(\mathfrak{gl}(m,n))
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots E_{i_{M}j_{M}})=\sum_{\overline{k}=(k_{1}\dots k_{M})}(-1)^{p(\overline{k})}x_{k_{1}i_{1}}\dots x_{k_{M}i_{M}}\partial_{k_{1}j_{1}}\dots\partial_{k_{M}j_{M}},

(1.8)

where
p(\overline{k})=\sum_{1\leq q<t\leq M}p_{k_{q}j_{q}}p_{k_{t}i_{t}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to verify the equality
\tilde{\partial}\circ\tilde{\sigma}=\partial
for the map
\tilde{\partial}
defined by (
1.8
). We now prove that

\displaystyle\sum_{\overline{k}}x_{k_{1}i_{1}}\partial_{k_{1}j_{1}}\dots x_{k_{M}i_{M}}\partial_{k_{M}j_{M}}=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\overline{k}}(-1)^{p_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)+q(\alpha)}\delta(\alpha)D_{\overline{k}}(\alpha),

(1.9)

where

D_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)=D_{\overline{k};i_{m_{1}}\dots i_{m_{r}}j_{l_{1}}\dots j_{l_{r}}}=x_{k_{1}i_{m_{1}}}\dots x_{k_{r}i_{m_{r}}}\partial_{k_{1}j_{l_{1}}}\dots\partial_{k_{r}j_{l_{r}}},

\alpha=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}
is a regular partition of the set
\{1,\dots,M\}
,
\overline{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})
, and

p_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)=\sum_{1\leq q<t\leq r}p_{k_{q}j_{l_{q}}}p_{k_{t}i_{m_{t}}}.

The conclusion of Proposition
1.4
follows from (
1.9
) and Proposition
1.3
. It is easy to check (
1.9
) for
M=2
, hence

\partial(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}E_{i_{2}j_{2}})=\tilde{\partial}\left(\delta_{j_{1}}^{i_{2}}E_{i_{1}j_{2}}+E_{i_{1}j_{1}}E_{i_{2}j_{2}}\right)=\tilde{\partial}\circ\tilde{\sigma}(E_{i_{1}j_{1}}E_{i_{2}j_{2}}).

Using induction on
M
again and applying
\sum_{\overline{k}}D_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)
to
\sum_{k_{M+1}}x_{k_{M+1}i_{M+1}}\partial_{k_{M+1}j_{M+1}}
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\overline{k}}D_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)\,\sum_{k_{r+1}}x_{k_{r+1}i_{M+1}}\partial_{k_{r+1}j_{M+1}}&=\sum_{\overline{k}}\sum_{t=1}^{r}\delta_{j_{l_{t}}}^{i_{M+1}}(-1)^{p_{\overline{k}}^{\prime}}D_{\overline{k};i_{m_{1}}\dots i_{m_{r}}j_{l_{1}}\dots j_{l_{t-1}}j_{M+1}j_{l_{t+1}}\dots j_{l_{r}}}\\
&+\sum_{(\overline{k},k_{r+1})}(-1)^{p^{\prime\prime}_{(\overline{k},k_{r+1})}}D_{(\overline{k},k_{r+1});i_{m_{1}}\dots i_{m_{r}}i_{M+1}j_{l_{1}}\dots j_{l_{r}}j_{M+1}},\end{split}

(1.10)

where

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}=\sum_{s=t+1}^{r}p_{k_{s}j_{l_{s}}}p_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}},\quad p^{\prime\prime}_{(\overline{k},k_{r+1})}=\sum_{t=1}^{r}p_{k_{t}j_{l_{t}}}p_{k_{r+1}i_{M+1}}.

For the same reason as in the proof of Proposition
1.3
the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\overline{k}}(-1)^{p_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)+q(\alpha)}\delta(\alpha)D_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)\sum_{k_{M+1}}x_{k_{M+1}i_{M+1}}\partial_{k_{M+1}j_{M+1}}

is the sum of monomials
D_{\overline{k}}(\beta)
with some coefficients over all regular partitions
\beta
of
\{1,\dots,M+1\}
.
Note that for

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}\{M+1\},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta(\beta)&=\delta(\alpha),\quad q(\beta)=q(\alpha),\\
p_{\overline{k}}(\beta)&=p_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)+\sum_{s=1}^{r}p_{k_{s}j_{l_{s}}}p_{k_{r+1}i_{M+1}},\end{split}

(1.11)

and for

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{t}\dots l_{t},M+1\}\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\{m_{1}\dots l_{1}\}\dots\{m_{r}\dots l_{r}\}

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta(\beta)&=\delta^{i_{M+1}}_{j_{l_{t}}}\delta(\alpha),\\
q(\beta)&=q(\alpha)+\sum_{s=t+1}^{r}p_{i_{m_{s}}j_{l_{s}}}p_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}}\\
&=q(\alpha)+\sum_{s=t+1}^{r}p_{k_{s}i_{m_{s}}}p_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}}+\sum_{s=t+1}^{r}p_{k_{s}j_{l_{s}}}p_{i_{M+1}j_{M+1}},\\
p_{\overline{k}}(\beta)&=p_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)+\sum_{s=t+1}^{r}p_{k_{s}i_{m_{s}}}p_{k_{t}j_{M+1}}-\sum_{s=t+1}^{r}p_{k_{s}i_{m_{s}}}p_{k_{t}j_{l_{t}}}\\
&=p_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)+\sum_{s=t+1}^{r}p_{k_{s}i_{m_{s}}}p_{j_{M+1}j_{l_{t}}}\end{split}

(1.12)

in view of the definition of
q(\alpha)
,
p_{\overline{k}}(\beta)
and
\delta(\beta)
. The presence of the Kronecker delta

\delta_{j_{l_{t}}}^{i_{M+1}}
in the coefficient of the corresponding term implies that either this coefficient is zero, or

q(\beta)+p_{\overline{k}}(\beta)=p_{\overline{k}}(\alpha)+q(\alpha)+p^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}.

∎

Note that the image of the restriction of
\tilde{\sigma}
to the subalgebra
U(Q(n))
of
U(\mathfrak{gl}(n,n))
lies in
S(Q(n))
due to (
1.3
) and the fact that
Q(n)
is the associative sublagebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,n)
. Thus, the map
\tilde{\sigma}
also establishes the isomorphism of vector spaces
U(Q(n))
and
S(Q(n))
.

2.
Duality of Centers

Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{k}
the group generated by
a_{1},\dots,a_{k},\varepsilon
subject to relations
a^{2}_{1}=a^{2}_{2}=\dots=a_{n}^{2}=\varepsilon
,

\varepsilon^{2}=1
,
a_{p}a_{q}=\varepsilon\,a_{q}a_{p}
for
p,q=1,\dots,k
,
p\neq q
. Note that
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is a double cover of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}_{2}
. Symmetric group
S_{k}
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{k}
by
\tau a_{p}=a_{\tau(p)}
,
\tau\varepsilon=\varepsilon
. The
Sergeev group of order
k
is the semidirect product of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
S_{k}
defined by the above action.
Following
[
11
]
we consider natural representations of the groups
Se(N)
and
S_{N}
in the tensor space
({\mathbb{C}}^{m|n})^{\otimes N}
. Namely, let
\mathfrak{A}
be the free associative commutative superalgebra with homogeneous generators
\{y_{i}\}
. We introduce a function
c:{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}_{2}\times S_{k}\to\{\pm 1\}
by

\displaystyle=l_{r+1-s}\dots k_{t_{M}}=m_{r+1-s}\dots k_{N}),

Any

The assertion follows from Lemmas

.

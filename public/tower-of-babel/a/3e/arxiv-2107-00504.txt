Abstract
We propose a new Lagrange multiplier approach to construct positivity preserving schemes for parabolic type equations. The new approach introduces a space-time Lagrange multiplier to enforce the positivity with the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions. We then use a predictor-corrector approach to construct a class of positivity schemes: with a generic semi-implicit or implicit scheme as the prediction step, and the correction step, which enforces the positivity, can be implemented with negligible cost. We also present a modification which allows us to construct schemes which, in addition to positivity preserving, is also mass conserving. This new approach is not restricted to any particular spatial discretization and can be combined with various time discretization schemes. We establish stability results for our first- and second-order schemes under a general setting, and present ample numerical results to validate the new approach.

Introduction

Solutions for a large class of partial differential equations (PDEs) arising from sciences and engineering applications, e.g., solutions for physical variables such as density, concentration, height, population, etc., are required to be positive. It is of critical importance that their numerical approximations preserve the positivity of these variables at the discrete level, as violation of the positivity may render the discrete problems ill posed, although the original problems are well posed.

In recent years, a large effort has been devoted to construct positivity preserving schemes for various problems.
The existing approaches can be roughly classified into the following categories:

•

Cut-off approach: an ad-hoc approach which simply cuts off the values outside of the desired range. This approach is perhaps used in many simulations without being explicitly mentioned, and it is recently analyzed in
[
22
,
17
]
for certain class of time discretization schemes. The main advantages of the cut-off approach is (i) simple to implement, and (ii) it is able to preserve the accuracy of the underlying numerical schemes for problems with smooth solutions (cf.
[
17
]
). A disadvantage is that it does not preserve mass.

•

Discrete maximum principle preserving schemes (see, for instance,
[
6
]
and the references therein): these schemes are usually based on second-order finite differences or piecewise linear finite elements so they are limited in accuracy, see however some recent work on fourth-order finite differences
[
18
,
19
]
applied to second-order elliptic or parabolic equations.

•

Post-processing approach: sophisticated procedures are designed in
[
29
,
28
]
for hyperbolic systems: these are explicit schemes which are not quite suitable for parabolic systems.

•

Convex splitting approach: for examples, see
[
4
]
for Cahn-Hilliard equations with logarithmic potential,
[
20
,
14
]
for PNP and Keller-Segel equations. The drawback of this approach is that a nonlinear system has to be solved at each time step.

•

Reformulation approach: reformulate the problem so that solution of the corresponding discrete problem is always positive, see, for instance,
[
20
,
15
]
).

•

Lagrange multiplier approach:
regarding the positivity as a constraint and introducing a Lagrange multiplier to enforce the positivity constraint. This can be regarded as a special case of the constrainted minimization
[
13
]
. The main difficulty of this approach is that the resulting problem is only semi-smooth which excludes the use of standard Newton method. Hence, special, and often expensive, iterative methods such as semi-smooth Newton method, have to be used
[
23
,
26
,
12
]
.

We consider in this paper a class of linear or nonlinear parabolic equations with positive solutions in the following form:

u_{t}+\mathcal{L}u=0,

(1.1)

with suitable initial and boundary conditions, where
\mathcal{L}
could be
\mathcal{L}u=Au+f(u)
with
A
being a linear or nonlinear positive operator and
f(u)
a semi-linear or quasi-linear operator.
Consider a generic spatial discretization of (
1.1
):

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{h}u_{h}=0,\end{split}

(1.2)

where
u_{h}
is in certain finite dimensional approximation space
X_{h}
and
\mathcal{L}_{h}
is a certain approximation of
\mathcal{L}
. In general,
u_{h}
, if it exists, may not preserve positivity. Oftentimes, (
1.2
) may not be well posed if
u_{h}
can not preserve positivity, e.g., a direct finite elements or spectral approximation to the porous media equation
[
27
]

u_{t}-m\nabla\cdot(u^{m-1}\nabla u)=0
with
(m>1)
can not preserve positivity so it is not well posed. Hence, special efforts have to be devoted to construct spatial discretization such that (
1.2
) is positivity preserving.

Alternatively, we can introduce a Lagrange multiplier function
\lambda_{h}(x,t)
and solve the following expanded system:

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{h}u_{h}=\lambda_{h},\\
&\lambda_{h}\geq 0,\;u_{h}\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}u_{h}=0.\end{split}

(1.3)

Note that the second equation in the above represents the well-known Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions
[
16
,
8
,
10
,
2
]
for constrained minimizations. In the absence of time variable, the problem (
1.3
) has been well studied mathematically and numerically. However, how to efficiently solve the time dependent (
1.3
) is a completely different issue which has not received much attention. One can of course use an implicit discretization scheme such as backward-Euler or BDF schemes so that at each time step, the nonlinear system can still be interpreted as a constrained minimization and apply a suitable iterative procedure. But since these constrained minimization problems are only semi-smooth, a delicate and costly iterative method has to be used. We refer to
[
26
]
for such an attempt with a diagonally implicit Runge-Kutta discretization.

We propose in this paper an operator splitting approach for (
1.3
)
to construct efficient and accurate schemes for (
1.3
). This approach enjoys the following advantages:

•

It allows us to construct positivity preserving schemes for a large class of linear or nonlinear parabolic equations with positive solutions, and the schemes can also be made mass conservative if the PDE is mass conserving;

•

It can be applied directly to any finite difference discretization or other spatial discretization with a Lagrangian basis;

•

It has essentially the same computational cost as the corresponding semi-implicit or implicit scheme with the same spatial discretization;

•

It has good stability property: the first- and second-order versions of our scheme are proven to be unconditionally stable for a large class of problems.

Moreover, we show that schemes based on the ad-hoc cut-off approach can be interpreted as special cases of our approach. Thus, this approach allows us to construct mass conserving schemes based on the cut-off approach, and our analysis leads to new stability results for the cut-off approach.
We shall apply our new schemes to a variety of problems with positive solutions, including the challenging porous media equation
[
27
,
21
]
and the very challenging Lubrication equation
[
30
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the positivity and mass preserving schemes with Lagrange multiplier. In Section 3, we carry out stability analysis for the proposed positivity preserving schemes. In Section 4, we present numerical results for a variety of problems to validate our schemes. Some concluding remarks are given in Section 5.

2
Positivity and mass preserving schemes with Lagrange multiplier

We start with a general description of the spatial discretization, followed by the construction of positivity preserving time discretization schemes without and with mass conservation.

2.1
Spatial discretization

We now give a more precise description on the generic spatial discretization in (
1.3
). Let
\Sigma_{h}
be a set of mesh points or collocation ponts in
\bar{\Omega}
. Note that
\Sigma_{h}
should not include the points at the part of the boundary where a Dirichlet (or essential) boundary condition is prescribed, while it should include the points at the part of the boundary where a Neumann or mixed (or non-essential) boundary condition is prescribed.

We consider a Galerkin type discretization with finite-elements or spectral methods or finite-differences with summation-by-parts in a subspace
X_{h}\subset X
, and
define a discrete inner product, i.e. numerical integration, on
\Sigma_{h}=\{\boldsymbol{z}\}
in
\bar{\Omega}
:

[u,v]=\sum_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Sigma_{h}}\omega_{\boldsymbol{z}}u(\boldsymbol{z})v(\boldsymbol{z}),

(2.4)

where we require that the weights
\omega_{\boldsymbol{z}}>0
. We also denote the induced norm by
\|u\|=[u,u]^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For finite element methods, the sum should be understood as
\sum_{K\subset\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{z}\in Z(K)}
where
\mathcal{T}
is a given triangulation.
And we assume that there is a unique function
\psi_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\boldsymbol{x})
in
X_{h}
satisfying
\psi_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\boldsymbol{z^{\prime}})=\delta_{\boldsymbol{z}\boldsymbol{z^{\prime}}}
for
\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z^{\prime}}\in\Sigma_{h}
.
Then, (
1.3
) is interpreted as follows: Find
u_{h}\in X_{h}
such that

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u_{h}(\boldsymbol{z},t)+\mathcal{L}_{h}u_{h}(\boldsymbol{z},t)=\lambda_{h}(\boldsymbol{z},t),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},\\
&\lambda_{h}(\boldsymbol{z},t)\geq 0,\;u_{h}(\boldsymbol{z},t)\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}(\boldsymbol{z},t)u_{h}(\boldsymbol{z},t)=0,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},\end{split}

(2.5)

with the Dirichlet boundary condition to be satisfied pointwisely if the original problem includes Dirichlet boundary condition at part or all of boundary.

2.2
Time discretization

Let
\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}
be an approximate operator of
\mathcal{L}
at
t_{n}
. For examples,
if
\mathcal{L}u=-\nabla\cdot(f(u)\nabla u)
,
\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}
could be a lagged linear approximation

\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}:=-\nabla\cdot(f(u_{h}^{n})\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}),

(2.6)

and for
\mathcal{L}u=Au+f(u)
,
\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}
could be a fully implicit discretization

\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}:=A\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}+f(\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}),

(2.7)

or an implicit-explicit (IMEX) discretization,

\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}:=A\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}+f(\tilde{u}^{n}_{h}),

(2.8)

or some other type of discretization such as the convex splitting
[
7
]
or the SAV approach
[
25
,
5
]
.

2.2.1
First-order operator splitting scheme

Let
\lambda_{h}^{0}\equiv 0
, for
n\geq 0
, we proceed as follows.

Step 1
: solve
\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}
from

\frac{\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-u_{h}^{n}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h};

(2.9)

Step 2
: solve
(u_{h}^{n+1},\lambda_{h}^{n+1})
from

\begin{split}&\frac{u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},\\
&\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h}.\end{split}

(2.10)

The above scheme can be viewed as an operator splitting method. The first step is just a usual time stepping scheme and can be implemented as usual. However,
\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}
may not be positive. In the second step, we use the KKT conditions to enforce the positivity of
u^{n+1}_{h}
.

A remarkable property of (
2.10
) is that it can be solved pointwisely as follows:

(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}),\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}),0)&&{\mbox{if}\quad 0<\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})}\\
(0,-\frac{\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\delta t})&&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{array},\right.\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h}.

(2.11)

Remark 2.1
.

The second step in the above scheme is equivalent to the simple cut-off approach
[
22
,
17
]
:

u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=\begin{cases}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})&\text{ if }\;\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})>0\\
0&\text{ if }\;\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\leq 0\end{cases},\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h}.

(2.12)

Hence, the cut-off approach can also be understood from an operator splitting point of view which opens new avenue for analysis and further algorithm improvement.

2.2.2
Higher-order schemes

We can construct higher-order schemes by using a predictor-corrector approach. More precisely, a
k
th-order IMEX scheme based on BDF and Adam-Bashforth can be constructed as follows:

Step 1
(prediction): solve
\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}
from

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{k}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}-A_{k}(u_{h}^{n})}{\delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}=B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n});

(2.13)

Step 2
(correction): solve
(u_{h}^{n+1},\lambda_{h}^{n+1})
from

\begin{split}&\frac{\alpha_{k}(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}))}{\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n}),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},\\
&\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},\end{split}

(2.14)

where
\alpha_{k},
the operators
A_{k}
and
B_{k-1}

(k=2,3,4)
are given by:

First-order:

\alpha_{1}=1,\quad A_{1}(u_{h}^{n})=u_{h}^{n},\quad B_{0}(\lambda_{h}^{n})=0;

(2.15)

Second-order:

\alpha_{2}=\frac{3}{2},\quad A_{2}(u_{h}^{n})=2u_{h}^{n}-\frac{1}{2}u_{h}^{n-1},\quad B_{1}(\lambda_{h}^{n})=\lambda_{h}^{n};

(2.16)

Third-order:

\alpha_{3}=\frac{11}{6},\quad A_{3}(u_{h}^{n})=3u_{h}^{n}-\frac{3}{2}u_{h}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{3}u_{h}^{n-2},\quad B_{2}(\lambda_{h}^{n})=2\lambda_{h}^{n}-\lambda_{h}^{n-1};

(2.17)

Fourth-order:

\alpha_{4}=\frac{25}{12},\;A_{4}(u_{h}^{n})=4u_{h}^{n}-3u_{h}^{n-1}+\frac{4}{3}u_{h}^{n-2}-\frac{1}{4}u_{h}^{n-3},\;B_{3}(\lambda_{h}^{n})=3\lambda_{h}^{n}-3\lambda_{h}^{n-1}+\lambda_{h}^{n-2}.

(2.18)

The formulae for
k=5,6
can be derived similarly with Taylor expansions. For the sake of simplicity and with a slight abuse of notations, we used
A_{k}(u_{h}^{n})
and
B_{k}(u_{h}^{n})
to denote
A_{k}(u_{h}^{n},\cdots,u_{h}^{n-k+1})
and

B_{k}(u_{h}^{n},\cdots,u_{h}^{n-k+1})
, respectively.
Note that for
k=1
, the above scheme is exactly (
2.9
)-(
2.10
).

The first-step is a usual
k
th-order IMEX scheme. The second step (
2.14
) can be viewed as a correction step in which
\lambda_{h}^{n+1}
is introduced to enforce the pointwise positivity of
u_{h}^{n+1}
, and can be efficiently solved as follows:

(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}),\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-\frac{\delta t}{\alpha_{k}}B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n}),0)&{\mbox{if}\quad 0<\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})}-\frac{\delta t}{\alpha_{k}}B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n})\\
(0,B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n})-\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\delta t}{\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})})&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{array},\right.\;\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h}.

(2.19)

Remark 2.2
.

Since
\lambda_{h}^{n}
is an approximation to
\lambda_{h}
which tends to zero as
h\rightarrow 0
, an alternative is to replace
B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n})
by zero, i.e., leading to the scheme:

Step 1
: solve
\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}
from

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{k}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}-A_{k}(u_{h}^{n})}{\delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}=0;

(2.20)

Step 2
: solve
(u_{h}^{n+1},\lambda_{h}^{n+1})
from

\begin{split}&\frac{\alpha_{k}(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1)}(\boldsymbol{z}))}{\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},\\
&\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h}.\end{split}

(2.21)

Since the second step is once again equivalent to the cut-off approach (
2.12
), the above scheme can be interpreted as a
k
th-order cut-off scheme.

2.2.3
Positivity preserving schemes with mass conservation

A drawback of the scheme (
2.13
)-(
2.14
) is that it does not preserve mass if the original equation does. For clarity, we consider first the first-order scheme (
2.9
)-(
2.10
).

Let
<\mathcal{L}\cdot,\cdot>
(resp.
<\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}\cdot,\cdot>
) denotes the continuous (resp. discrete) bilinear form after proper integration by parts, e.g., if

\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}u_{h}:=-\nabla\cdot(f(u_{h}^{n})\nabla u_{h})
, then

<\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}u_{h},v_{h}>:=[f(u_{h}^{n})\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h}]
.
Assuming
<\mathcal{L}u,1>=0
, we find from (
1.1
) that
\partial_{t}(u,1)=0
, i.e., the mass is conserved. But assuming
<\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}v_{h},1>=0
for any
v_{h}\in X_{h}
, we derive from (
2.9
)-(
2.10
) that

[u_{h}^{n+1},1]-[u_{h}^{n},1]=\delta t[\lambda_{h}^{n+1},1].

Since
\lambda_{h}^{n+1}\geq 0
, we find that the mass is not conserved, in fact it is increasing with
n
.

We present below a simple modification which enables mass conservation. More precisely, we introduce another Lagrange multiplier
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
, which is independent of spatial variables, to enforce the mass conservation in the correction step.

Step 1
: solve
\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}
from

\frac{\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-u_{h}^{n}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h};

(2.22)

Step 2
: solve
(u_{h}^{n+1},\lambda_{h}^{n+1})
from

\displaystyle\frac{u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\xi^{n+1}_{h},\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},

(2.23a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},

(2.23b)

\displaystyle[u_{h}^{n+1},1]=[u_{h}^{n},1].

(2.23c)

In order to solve (
2.23
), we rewrite (
2.23a
) in the following equivalent form

\frac{u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi^{n+1}_{h})}{\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}).

(2.24)

Hence, assuming
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
is known, (
2.24
) and (
2.23b
) can be solved pointwisely as follows:

(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}),\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi^{n+1}_{h},0)&&{\mbox{if}\quad 0<\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi^{n+1}_{h}}\\
(0,-\frac{\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi^{n+1}_{h}}{\delta t})&&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{array},\right.\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h}.

(2.25)

It remains to determine
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
. We find from (
2.23c
) and (
2.24
) that

[\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}+\delta t\xi^{n+1}_{h},1]=[u_{h}^{n},1]-\delta t[\lambda_{h}^{n+1},1],

which, thanks to (
2.25
), can be rewritten as

\sum_{z\in\Sigma_{h}\,s.t.\,0<\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi^{n+1}_{h}}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi^{n+1}_{h})\omega_{z}=[u_{h}^{n},1].

Hence,
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
is a solution to the nonlinear algebraic equation

F(\xi)=\sum_{z\in\Sigma_{h}\,s.t.\,0<\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\delta t\xi)\omega_{z}-[u_{h}^{n},1]=0.

(2.26)

Since
F^{\prime}(\xi)
may not exist and difficult to compute if it exists, instead of the Newton iteration, we can use the following secant method:

\xi_{k+1}=\xi_{k}-\frac{F(\xi_{k})(\xi_{k}-\xi_{k-1})}{F(\xi_{k})-F(\xi_{k-1})}.

(2.27)

Since
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
is an approximation to zero and it will be shown below that
\xi^{n+1}_{h}\leq 0
, we can choose
\xi_{0}=0
and
\xi_{1}=-O(\delta t)
. In all our experiments, (
2.27
) converges in a few iterations so that the cost is negligible.

Once
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
is known, we can update
(u_{h}^{n+1},\lambda^{n+1}_{h})
with (
2.25
).

Similarly, the higher-order scheme (
2.13
)-(
2.14
) can be modified to preserve mass as follows:

Step 1
(prediction): solve
\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}
from

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{k}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}-A_{k}(u_{h}^{n})}{\delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}=B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n})+B_{k-1}(\xi_{h}^{n});

(2.28)

Step 2
(correction): solve
(u_{h}^{n+1},\lambda_{h}^{n+1})
from

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{k}(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}))}{\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n})+\xi^{n+1}_{h}-B_{k-1}(\xi_{h}^{n}),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},

(2.29a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\;\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\Sigma_{h},

(2.29b)

\displaystyle[u_{h}^{n+1},1]=[u_{h}^{n},1].

(2.29c)

In order to solve the above system, we denote

\eta_{h}^{n+1}:=\frac{\delta t}{\alpha_{k}}(\xi^{n+1}_{h}-B_{k-1}(\xi_{h}^{n})-B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n}))
and
rewrite (
2.29a
) as

\frac{\alpha_{k}(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\eta^{n+1}_{h}(\boldsymbol{z})))}{\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}).

(2.30)

Assuming
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
is known, we find from (
2.29a
) and (
2.30
) that

(u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}),\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\eta_{h}^{n+1},0)&&\text{if}\quad 0<\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\eta_{h}^{n+1},\\
(0,-\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\delta t}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\eta_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})))&&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.31)

Finally, we can determine
\xi^{n+1}_{h}
by solving the nonlinear algebraic equation

F(\xi^{n+1}_{h})=\sum_{z\in\Sigma_{h}\,s.t.\,0<\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\eta_{h}^{n+1(\boldsymbol{z})}}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})+\eta_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z}))\omega_{z}-[u_{h}^{n},1]=0.

(2.32)

Remark 2.3
.

Replacing
B_{k-1}(\lambda_{h}^{n})
in (
2.28
)-(
2.29
) by zero, we obtain a mass conserved
k
th-order cut-off scheme.

3
Stability results

We prove in this section that the first- and second-order positivity preserving schemes with or without mass conservation are dissipative and unconditionally stable if
<\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}{v}_{h},{v}_{h}>\;\geq 0\;\forall v_{h}\in X_{h}
for all
n
.

3.1

3.42

Remark 3.1
.

The results in the previous theorems are derived for a general approximate operator
\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}
. They imply in particular:

•

If
\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}
is non-negative, e.g., as in (
2.6
) with application to Porous Media equation, then the first- and second-order positivity preserving schemes with Lagrange multiplier are unconditionally energy stable.

•

If one can show, perhaps under certain condition
\Delta t\leq c_{0}h^{\alpha}
with a semi-implicit discretization (where
\alpha>0
depending on the problem and discretization), that for the usual schemes, i.e., by setting
\lambda^{n}_{h}\equiv 0
for all
n
,
we have

\delta t\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m-1}<\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1},\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}>\;\geq\;\beta\delta t\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m-1}a_{h}(\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1},\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1})-C_{1},\;\forall m\leq T/{\Delta t}-1,

where
\beta
is some positive constant in
(0,1]
and
T
is the final time,
then we derive from the above and (
3.33
) that the solutions of the corresponding schemes (
2.13
)-(
2.14
) and (
2.28
)-(

) with

are bounded in the sense that

-th degree, and in the multi-dimensional case,
\Sigma_{h}
is obtained by the tensor product of one-dimensional set. We set
X_{h}=\{v_{h}\in P_{N}:v_{h}|_{\partial\Omega=0}\}
, and use the scheme (
2.13
)- (
2.14
) with
k=2
and
\mathcal{L}^{n}_{h}(v_{h})=-\nabla\cdot(m(u^{n+1,*})^{m-1}\nabla v_{h})
. For the reader’s convenience,
it is explicitly described below:

Find
u_{h}^{n+1}\in X_{h}
such that

\displaystyle[\frac{3\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}-4u_{h}^{n}+u_{h}^{n-1}}{2\delta t},v_{h}]+m[(u_{h}^{n+1,*})^{m-1}\nabla u_{h}^{n+1},\nabla v_{h}]=[\lambda_{h}^{n},v_{h}],\quad\forall v_{h}\in X_{h},

(4.65)

\displaystyle\frac{3u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-3\tilde{u}_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})}{2\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})-\lambda_{h}^{n}(\boldsymbol{z}),\quad\forall z\in\Sigma_{h},

(4.66)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})\geq 0,\;\lambda_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})u_{h}^{n+1}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,

(4.67)

where

u_{h}^{n+1,*}=\begin{cases}2u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n-1}\quad if\;u_{h}^{n}\geq u_{h}^{n-1},\\
\frac{1}{2/u_{h}^{n}-1/u_{h}^{n-1}}\quad if\;u_{h}^{n}<u_{h}^{n-1}.\end{cases}

(4.68)

At each time step, we need to solve an elliptic equation with variable coefficients in (
4.65
), which can be efficiently solved by a preconditioned conjugate gradient iteration with a constant coefficient problem as the preconditioner.

4.3
Poisson-Nernst-Planck equations

We consider the followng Poisson-Nernst-Planck (PNP) system
[
14
,
11
]
which describes the dynamics of ion transport in ion channels:

\displaystyle-\epsilon^{2}\Delta\phi=p-n,\mbox{in}~\Omega_{T}:=(0,T]\times\Omega.

(4.72)

\displaystyle p_{t}=\nabla\cdot(\nabla p+p\nabla\phi),

(4.73)

\displaystyle n_{t}=\nabla\cdot(\nabla n-n\nabla\phi),

(4.74)

with initial conditions

p(0,x)=p_{0}(x)\geq 0,\quad n(0,x)=n_{0}(x)\geq 0,\quad\mbox{in}~\Omega,

(4.75)

and homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

\frac{\partial p}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=\frac{\partial n}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{T}:=(0,T]\times\partial\Omega.

(4.76)

In the above,
p
and
n
are concentration of positive and negative ions with valence
+1
and
-1
, respectively,
\phi
is the electrical potential,
\epsilon
is a small positive dimensionless number representing the ratio of the Debye length to the physical characteristic length. The unknown functions
p
and
n
have to be positive for the problem to be well posed. Below we use the general approach presented in the last section to construct a positivity preserving scheme for the PNP equations. Since we need to keep both
p
and
n
positive, two Lagrange multipliers
\lambda
and
\eta
are needed . Lagrange multipliers
\xi
and
\gamma
are used to preserve mass.

We set
X_{h}=P_{N}\times P_{N}
, and
\Sigma_{h}=\{(x_{i},x_{j}),\,1\leq i,j\leq N-1\}
, where
\{x_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N}
are the roots of
(1-x^{2})L^{\prime}_{N}(x)
with
L_{N}
being the Legendre polynomial of
N
-th degree.
And we use the Legendre-Galerkin method with numerical integration in space
[
24
]
. Then a second-order positivity preserving scheme based on the scheme (
2.28
)-(
2.29c
) with
k=2
is as follows: for
\forall q_{h},m_{h}\in X_{h}

\displaystyle[\frac{3\tilde{p}_{h}^{n+1}-4p_{h}^{n}+p_{h}^{n-1}}{2\delta t},q_{h}]=[\nabla p_{h}^{n+1}+p_{h}^{n+1,\star}\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1,\star},\nabla q_{h}]+[\lambda_{h}^{n}+\xi_{h}^{n},q_{h}],

(4.77)

\displaystyle\frac{3p_{h}^{n+1}-\tilde{p}_{h}^{n+1}}{2\delta t}=\lambda_{h}^{n+1}-\lambda_{h}^{n}+\xi_{h}^{n+1}-\xi_{h}^{n},

(4.78)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{n+1}\geq 0,\;p_{h}^{n+1}\geq,\;\lambda_{h}^{n+1}p_{h}^{n+1}=0,\;[p_{h}^{n+1},1]=[p_{h}^{n},1];

(4.79)

\displaystyle[\frac{3\tilde{n}_{h}^{n+1}-4n_{h}^{n}+n_{h}^{n-1}}{2\delta t},m_{h}]=[\nabla n_{h}^{n+1}-n_{h}^{n+1,\star}\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1,\star},\nabla m_{h}]+[\eta_{h}^{n}+\gamma_{h}^{n},m_{h}],

(4.80)

\displaystyle\frac{3n_{h}^{n+1}-3\tilde{n}_{h}^{n+1}}{2\delta t}=\eta_{h}^{n+1}-\eta_{h}^{n}+\gamma_{h}^{n+1}-\gamma_{h}^{n},

(4.81)

\displaystyle\eta_{h}^{n+1}\geq 0,\;n_{h}^{n+1}\geq 0,\;\eta_{h}^{n+1}n_{h}^{n+1}=0,\;[n_{h}^{n+1},1]=[n_{h}^{n},1];

(4.82)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}[\nabla\phi_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\psi_{h}]=[p_{h}^{n+1}-n_{h}^{n+1},\psi_{h}],\quad\forall\psi_{h}\in X_{h};

(4.83)

where

&[u_{h}^{n+1},1]=[u_{h}^{n},1],\end{split}

•

5
Concluding remarks

If a PDE requires its solution to be positive, a generic numerical scheme for the PDE usually can not preserve the positivity.
We presented in this paper a new approach to construct positivity preserving schemes for parabolic type equations by a simple modification to generic numerical schemes.
More precisely, we introduce a space-time Lagrange multiplier function to enforce the positivity, and expand the underlying PDE using the KKT conditions. The key question is how to solve the expanded system efficiently with
essentially the same cost as the generic numerical scheme.

We constructed a new class of positivity preserving schemes by using the predictor-corrector approach to the expanded system: the prediction step can be a generic semi-implicit or implicit scheme, while the correction step
is used to enforce the positivity and can be implemented as a simple pointwise update with negligible cost. This new approach is not restricted to any particular spatial discretization and can be combined with various time discretization schemes. It can be
applied to a large class of parabolic PDEs which require solutions to be positive. It is also non intrusive as you can easily modify your non-positivity preserving schemes for them to become positivity preserving.
In addition, we also presented a modification to the above approach so that the schemes can also preserve mass
if the underlying PDE is mass conserving.

An interesting and useful observation is that the ad-hoc cut-off approach can be interpreted as a special case of our predictor-corrector approach. Hence, it provides a different justification for the cut-off approach, moreover allows us to modify the cut-off approach so that it becomes mass conserving, and opens new avenue for further exploration.

We established stability results for the first- and second-order schemes based on the new approach under a general setting, and presented ample numerical experiments to validate the new approach. Our numerical results indicate that the new approach is very effective for the variety of problems that we tested.

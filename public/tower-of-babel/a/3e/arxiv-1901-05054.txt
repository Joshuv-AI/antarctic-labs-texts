Solution of the equation y ′ = f ⁡ ( y ) y^{\prime}=f(y) and Bell Polynomials
Ronald Orozco López

Abstract
In this paper we use Faà di Bruno’s formula to associate Bell polynomial values to differential equations of the form y ′ = f ⁡ ( y ) y^{\prime}=f(y) . That is, we use partial Bell polynomials to represent the solution of such an equation and use the solution to compute special values of partial Bell polynomials.

1
Introduction

It is a known fact that Bell polynomials are closely related to the derivatives of the composition of functions. For example, Faà di Bruno
[
Faa
]
, Foissy
[
Foissy
]
, and Riordan
[
Riordan_j
]
proved that Bell polynomials are a very useful tool in mathematics to represent the
n
-th derivative of the composition of functions. Also, Bernardini and Ricci
[
Bernardini
]
, Yildiz et al.
[
Yildiz
]
, Caley
[
Caley
]
, and Wang
[
Wang
]
showed the relationship between Bell polynomials and differential equations. On the other hand, Orozco
[
Orozco
]
studied the convergence of the analytic solution of the autonomous differential equation
y^{(k)}=f(y)
using Faà di Bruno’s formula. We can then consider differential equations as a source for researching special values of Bell polynomials.
In this paper we consider the solution
y(t,x)=g(t+g^{-1}(x))
of the autonomous differential equation
y^{\prime}=f(y)
and show how to express this solution by means of Bell polynomials. This will then be used to find special values of partial Bell polynomials. Here we will not consider convergence issues, but formal solutions of such a differential equation.
This paper is organized as follows. We start with basic results on partial and complete Bell polynomials and special values of these. In the third section we show what condition
g(x)
must satisfy for
g(t+g^{-1}(x))
to be a solution of
y^{\prime}=f(y)
. We conclude by showing the relationship between Bell polynomials and the solution of the differential equation
y^{\prime}=f(y)
when
f(x)=ax
,
f(x)=e^{x}
,
f(x)=\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
,
f(x)=1+x^{2}
,
f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}\pm 1}
,
f(x)=1-x^{2}
and
f(x)=\frac{1}{(x-1)^{\alpha-1}}
, where
a,\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.

2
Preliminaries

The following basic results can be found in Comtet
[
Comtent
]
, and Riordan
[
Riordan_b
]
. Exponential Bell polynomials are used to encode information about the ways in which a set can be partitioned, making them a very useful tool in combinatorial analysis. Bell polynomials are obtained from the derivatives of composite functions and are given by the formula of Faà Di Bruno
[
Faa
]
. Bell
[
Bell
]
, Gould
[
Gould_Q
]
, Mihoubi
[
Mihoubi
]
, Wang
[
Wang2
]
and Feng Qi
[
FengQi1
]
,
[
FengQi2
]
,
[
FengQi3
]
(among many others) provided important results on these polynomials. We start with the definition of the partial Bell polynomials.

Definition 2.1
.

The exponential partial Bell polynomials are the polynomials

B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{n},\ldots,x_{n-k+1})

in the infinite variables
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots
defined by the series expansion

\exp\left(u\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\right)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=1}^{n}u^{k}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-k+1}).

(1)

or equivalently defined by the series expansion of the
k
-th power

\frac{1}{k!}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\right)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(2)

The following result gives the explicit way to calculate the partial Bell polynomials

Theorem 2.2
.

The partial or incomplete exponential Bell polynomials are given by

B_{n,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-k+1})=\sum\frac{n!}{c_{1}!c_{2}!\cdots c_{n-k+1}!}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\right)^{c_{1}}\cdots\left(\frac{x_{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}\right)^{c_{n-k+1}},

where the summation takes place over all integers
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n-k+1}\geq 0
, such that

\displaystyle c_{1}+2c_{2}+\cdots+(n-k+1)c_{n-k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n,

\displaystyle c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{n-k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k.

Some values of partial Bell polynomials are

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(0!,1!,\ldots,(n-k)!)

\displaystyle=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}\ \ (\textit{Unsigned Stirling number of first kind}),

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(1!,\ldots,(n-k)!)

\displaystyle=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{k!}\ \ (\textit{Lah number}),

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(1,1,\ldots,1)

\displaystyle=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}\ \ (\textit{Stirling number of second kind}),

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(1,2,\ldots,n-k+1)

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k}k^{n-k}\ \ (\textit{Idempotent number}).

Then we can see the beautiful relationship that exists between Bell polynomials and numbers like the above.
Feng Qi
[
FengQi3
]
deduced the following identity that will be very useful to us

B_{n,k}(x,1,0,0,\ldots,0)=\frac{1}{2^{n-k}}\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{k}{n-k}x^{2k-n},

that together with the identity

B_{n,k}(abx_{1},ab^{2}x_{2},\ldots,ab^{n-k+1}x_{n-k+1})=a^{k}b^{n}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-k+1})

(3)

leads us to

B_{n,k}(ax,a,0,0,\ldots,0)=\frac{a^{k}}{2^{n-k}}\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{k}{n-k}x^{2k-n}.

(4)

Finally we show Faà di Bruno’s formula. Let
f
and
g
be functions with exponential generating functions
\sum a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
and
\sum b_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

respectively, with
a_{n},b_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

f(g(x))=f(b_{0})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f^{(k)}(b_{0})B_{n,k}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-k+1})\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

(5)

3
Differential equation
y^{\prime}=f(y)
and Bell polynomials

This section contains the general results of this paper. Here we show the condition that the function
g(x)
must satisfy for
g(t+g^{-1}(x))
to be a solution of the differential equation
y^{\prime}=f(y)
. Then we will give a representation of
g(t+g^{-1}(x))
in power series using partial Bell polynomials and finally we use the solution of the equation
y^{\prime}=f(y)
to find special values of partial Bell polynomials.

Theorem 3.1
.

The function
y(t,x)=g(t+g^{-1}(x))
is solution of the differential equation
y^{\prime}=f(y)
with initial value problem
y(0)=x
, where
f(x)=\frac{1}{(g^{-1})^{\prime}(x)}
.

Proof 3.2
.

Using the method of separation of variables and the value of the function
f(x)
given in the hypothesis, we find that

\displaystyle\int\frac{dy}{f(y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int(g^{-1})^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{-1}(y)=t+C.

As
y(0)=x
, then
y(t,x)=g(t+g^{-1}(x))
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The function
g(t+g^{-1}(x))
has the following representation using Bell polynomials

g(t+g^{-1}(x))=x+g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)]t\\
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f^{(k)}(x)B_{n,k}(g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)],\ldots,g^{(n-k+1)}[g^{-1}(x)])\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!},

(6)

where
g^{(n)}[g^{-1}(x)]
satisfies

g^{(n+1)}[g^{-1}(x)]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f^{(k)}(x)B_{n,k}(g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)],\ldots,g^{(n-k+1)}[g^{-1}(x)]),\ \ \ n\geq 1

(7)

with
g^{-1}(y)=\int\frac{dy}{f(y)}
.

Proof 3.4
.

Applying Taylor formula to
g(t+g^{-1}(x))
we get

\displaystyle g(t+g^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g^{(n)}(g^{-1}(x))\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)]t+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g^{(n+1)}(g^{-1}(x))\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}.

Since
g(t+g^{-1}(x))
is solution of
y^{\prime}=f(y)
with initial value problem
y(0)=x
, then by directly applying Faà di Bruno’s formula (
5
) to
y^{\prime}=f(y)
we obtain the desired result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
y^{\prime}=f(y)
be the autonomous differential equation with initial value problem
y(0)=x
. For
n\geq k\geq 1
we have

B_{n,k}(g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)],g^{\prime\prime}[g^{-1}(x)],\ldots,g^{(n-k+1)}[g^{-1}(x)])\\
=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}x^{k-i}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}g^{i}(t+g^{-1}(x))\bigg|_{t=0}.

(8)

Proof 3.6
.

Making
x_{m}=g^{(m)}[g^{-1}(x)]
in the equation (
2
) leads us to

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)],\ldots,g^{(n-k+1)}[g^{-1}(x)])\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g^{(m)}[g^{-1}(x)]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\right)^{k}\\
=\frac{1}{k!}(g(t+g^{-1}(x))-x)^{k}\\
=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}x^{k-i}g^{i}(t+g^{-1}(x)).

Differentiating
m\geq k\geq 0
times with respect to
t

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)],\ldots,g^{(n-k+1)}[g^{-1}(x)])\frac{t^{n-m}}{(n-m)!}\\
=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}x^{k-i}\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}g^{i}(t+g^{-1}(x))

and then by making
t=0
, we obtain the desired result

B_{m,k}(g^{\prime}[g^{-1}(x)],\ldots,g^{(m-k+1)}[g^{-1}(x)])=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}x^{k-i}\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}g^{i}(t+g^{-1}(x))\bigg|_{t=0}.

is any of the following functions:

f(x)=\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
,
f(x)=1+x^{2}
,
f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}\pm 1}
,
f(x)=1-x^{2}
and
f(x)=\frac{1}{(x-1)^{\alpha-1}}
, where
a,\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. In addition by using the Theorem
3.5
we will find identities for Bell polynomials, some of which were constructed by Feng Qi et al in
[
FengQi1
]
,
[

,

4.1
Equation
y^{\prime}=ay

The solution to this equation is
y(t,x)=xe^{at}
where
f(x)=ax
,
f^{(k)}(x)=0
for
k\geq 2
, and
g^{(n)}[g^{-1}(x)]=a^{n}x
. By the Theorem
3.3
, we have the representation of
xe^{at}
using Bell polynomials

\displaystyle xe^{ax}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+axt+a\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f^{(k)}(x)B_{n,k}(ax,a^{2}x,\ldots,a^{n-k+1}x)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+axt+a\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n,1}(ax,a^{2}x,\ldots,a^{n}x)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

and by the Theorem
3.5
we find the following value of Bell polynomial using the solution
y(t,x)=xe^{at}

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(ax,a^{2}x,\ldots,a^{n-k+1}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}x^{k-i}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}x^{i}e^{ait}\bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{n}x^{k}}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}i^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{n}x^{k}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k},

where we have used that

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}i^{n}.

We then relate the Stirling numbers of the second kind to the differential equation
y^{\prime}=ay
. When we make
x=1
,
a=1
, the above provides another proof for the known result
B_{n,k}(1,1,\ldots,1)=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}
.

4.2
Equation
y^{\prime}=e^{y}

The solution to this equation is
y(t,x)=-\ln(-t+e^{-x})
. Then by the Theorem
3.3
, with
f(x)=e^{x}
and
g^{(n)}[g^{-1}(x)]=(n-1)!e^{nx}
, we have the representation of the function
-\ln(-t+e^{-x})
, that is,

-\ln(-t+e^{-x})=x+e^{x}t\\
+e^{x}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{n,k}\left(0!e^{x},1!e^{2x},\ldots,(n-k)!e^{(n-k+1)x}\right)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\\
=x+e^{x}t+e^{x}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{nx}\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{n,k}\left(0!,1!,\ldots,(n-k)!\right)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\\
=x+e^{x}t+e^{x}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{nx}B_{n}\left(0!,1!,\ldots,(n-1)!\right)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}.

By the Theorem
3.5
we find another special value of Bell polynomials, i.e.

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\left(0!e^{x},1!e^{2x},\ldots,(n-k+1)!e^{(n-k+1)x}\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
=\frac{(-\ln(-t+e^{-x})-x)^{k}}{k!}=\frac{(-\ln(-te^{x}+1))^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}e^{nx}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

and by comparing the coefficients of the two sums we get

B_{n,k}\left(0!e^{x},1!e^{2x},\ldots,(n-k+1)!e^{(n-k+1)x}\right)=e^{nx}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}.

Clearly the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind are related to the differential equation
y^{\prime}=e^{y}
.

The solution of the equation with initial value problem

\displaystyle y(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin(t+\arcsin(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\cos(t)+\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\sin(t).

Then, due to the Theorem
3.3

x\cos(t)+\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\sin(t)=x+\sqrt{1-x^{2}}t\\

f^{(k)}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}2^{2i-k}k!\binom{1/2}{i}\binom{i}{k-i}(1-x^{2})^{1/2-i}x^{2i-k}

A simple application of the previous result with

4.5
Equation
y^{\prime}=\sqrt{y^{2}\pm 1}

The solution of the equation
y^{\prime}=\sqrt{y^{2}+1}
with initial value problem
y(0)=x
is

\displaystyle y(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sinh(t+\mathrm{arcsinh}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\cosh(t)+\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\sinh(t).

For
n\geq 1
denote
\phi_{n}(x)
the function

\phi_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}x,&\text{ if $n$ is an even number;}\\
\sqrt{1+x^{2}},&\text{ if $n$ is an odd number.}\end{cases}

(12)

By the Theorem
3.3
we have

x\cosh(t)+\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\sinh(t)=x+\sqrt{1+x^{2}}t\\
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f^{(k)}(x)B_{n,k}\left(\phi_{1}(x),\phi_{2}(x),\ldots,\phi_{n-k+1}(x)\right)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!},

where

f^{(k)}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{2i-k}k!\binom{1/2}{i}\binom{i}{k-i}(1+x^{2})^{1/2-i}x^{2i-k}.

has been obtained by applying Faà di Bruno’s formula to
f(x)=g(h(x))
with
g(x)=\sqrt{x}
and
h(x)=1+x^{2}
and by the equation (
4

B_{n,k}(2x,2,0,\ldots,0)=2^{2k-n}\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{k}{n-k}x^{2k-n}.

Theorem 4.4
.

For
n\geq k\geq 1
we have

B_{n,k}(\phi_{1}(x),\phi_{2}(x),\ldots,\phi_{n-k+1}(x))\\
=\frac{(-1)^{k}x^{k}}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\binom{i}{l}(-1)^{l}(2l-i)^{n}\left(\sqrt{1+x^{2}}+x\right)^{2l-i}.

(13)

Proof 4.5
.

Using
\sinh(t)=\frac{e^{t}-e^{-t}}{2}
we will obtain
\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\sinh^{i}(t)
, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}\sinh^{i}(t)}{dt^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\frac{(e^{t}-e^{-t})^{i}}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\binom{i}{l}(-1)^{i-l}e^{(2l-i)t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{i}}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\binom{i}{l}(-1)^{i-l}(2l-i)^{n}e^{(2l-i)t}.

Then by the Theorem
3.5

B_{n,k}(\phi_{1}(x),\phi_{2}(x),\ldots,\phi_{n-k+1}(x))\\
=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}x^{k-i}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\binom{i}{l}(-1)^{i-l}(2l-i)^{n}e^{(2l-i)\mathrm{arcsinh}(x)}\\
=\frac{(-1)^{k}x^{k}}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\binom{i}{l}(-1)^{l}(2l-i)^{n}\left(\sqrt{1+x^{2}}+x\right)^{2l-i}

and the theorem follows.

Analogously, we can find similar results to the previous ones for the solution

Conclusion

As noted, Faà di Bruno’s formula allows us to connect Bell polynomials with autonomous differential equations of order one.

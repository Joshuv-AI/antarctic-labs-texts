Substrate enhanced superconductivity in Li-decorated graphene
T. P. Kaloni 1 , A. V. Balatsky 2,3,4 , and U. Schwingenschlögl 1

Abstract
We investigate the role of the substrate for the strength of the electon phonon coupling in Li-decorated graphene. We find that the interaction with a h h -BN substrate leads to a significant enhancement from λ 0 = 0.62 \lambda_{0}=0.62 to λ 1 = 0.67 \lambda_{1}=0.67 , which corresponds to a 25% increase of the transition temperature from T c ​ 0 = 10.33 T_{c0}=10.33 K to T c ​ 1 = 12.98 T_{c1}=12.98 K. The superconducting gaps amount to 1.56 meV (suspended) and 1.98 meV (supported). These findings open up a new route to enhanced superconducting transition temperatures in graphene-based materials by substrate engineering.

significantly enhanced by the coupling to a properly chosen substrate.

III
conclusion

In conclusion, using density functional theory we have investigated the role of the substrate
for the electron-phonon coupling in Li-decorated suspended and supported graphene. We find
that the interaction with a
h
-BN substrate significantly enhances the electron-phonon coupling
to
\lambda=0.67
as compared to
\lambda=0.62
in the suspended case. The transition
temperature thus is enhanced by 25% to 12.98 K. The superconducting gap for the suspended
and supported systems is found to be 1.56 meV and 1.98 meV, respectively. Our results show
that graphene-based nanomaterials can be tailored by properly choosing the substrate to
robustly increase the superconducting transition temperature.

Computational details

All the results are obtained from density functional theory in the local density approximation.
The van der Waals interaction is taken into account via Grimme’s scheme

the Quantum-ESPRESSO code

optimization of the lattice parameters and the ionic relaxation and a

k-mesh for refining the electronic structure. We achieve an energy convergence of

\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}R30{{}^{\circ}}
supercell with

to that a Li atom
is added on each third hollow site. Phonon frequencies are determined by density functional

the scheme described in Ref.
Mod

24\times 24\times 1
k-mesh. We study the effect of the substrate on the strength of the

Å and
